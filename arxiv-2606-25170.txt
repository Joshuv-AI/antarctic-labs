Minimax PAC Bounds for Learning in Exogenous Contextual MDPs
Corentin Pla Hugo Richard Marc Abeille Vianney Perchet CREST, ENSAE Criteo AI Lab FairPlay Joint Team Paris, France

Abstract
We study PAC learning in tabular discounted Markov decision processes with exogenous i.i.d. contexts, with discount factor γ \gamma , finite state space 𝒳 \mathcal{X} , action space 𝒜 \mathcal{A} , and context space 𝒵 \mathcal{Z} . At each time step, a context is drawn independently from an unknown distribution μ \mu and revealed before the agent acts. This context may affect both rewards and transitions, while remaining uncontrolled by the agent. Depending on the regime, the learner has access either to a sampling oracle for μ \mu , to a sampling oracle for the transition kernel conditioned on state-context-action tuples, or to both. Oracles can be accessed before and during policy execution. The sample complexity is measured by a couple ( n , m ) (n,m) , where n n is the number of calls to the sampling oracles before execution and m m is the number of calls to the sampling oracles during execution. When rewards and transitions are known and only the context distribution μ \mu is sampled, we give a variance-reduced algorithm that solves policy evaluation (PE), best-value estimation (BVE), and best-policy extraction (BPE) with ( O ~ ​ ( 1 / ( ( 1 − γ ) 3 ​ ε 2 ) ) , 0 ) \left(\widetilde{O}\left(1/((1-\gamma)^{3}\varepsilon^{2})\right),0\right) sample complexity. The rate is independent of | 𝒵 | |\mathcal{Z}| and minimax optimal up to logarithmic factors. As a corollary, we also obtain tight rates in the case of one-step perfect look-ahead, improving upon the existing guarantees. In the fully unknown regime, where both μ \mu and P P must be learned, we show that PE remains | 𝒵 | |\mathcal{Z}| -free, with matching upper and lower bounds ( O ~ ​ ( | 𝒳 | / ( ( 1 − γ ) 3 ​ ε 2 ) ) , O ~ ​ ( 1 / ( ( 1 − γ ) 2 ​ ε 2 ) ) ) \bigl(\widetilde{O}(|\mathcal{X}|/((1-\gamma)^{3}\varepsilon^{2})),\,\widetilde{O}(1/((1-\gamma)^{2}\varepsilon^{2}))\bigr) .

12

31

), the controlled state includes inventory or platform dynamics, while the context is the customer or session information observed before pricing. The action is the posted price and the reward is the resulting revenue or profit.

Contribution.

2.1
Exogenous contextual Markov decision process.

Model.

We consider finite discounted Markov decision processes with exogenous
i.i.d. contexts. A model is a tuple
\mathcal{M}=(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Z},\mathcal{A},P,r,\mu,\gamma)
, where
\mathcal{X}
is the finite controlled state space,
\mathcal{Z}
is the context
space,
\mathcal{A}
is the finite action space,
P
is a controlled transition kernel,
r
is a reward function,
\mu
is the context distribution, and

\gamma\in[0,1)
is the discount factor. The transition kernel is

P:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{Z}\to\Delta(\mathcal{X}),

so that
P(\cdot\mid x,a,z)
is the law of the next controlled state after
taking action
a
in controlled state
x
under context
z
. The reward
function is
r:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{Z}\to[0,1]

Throughout the paper, for simplicity,
r
is deterministic and known. Interactions are as follows: at each time
t\geq 0
, the learner observes the augmented state
(X_{t},Z_{t})\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Z}
, chooses an action
A_{t}\in\mathcal{A}
, receives reward

r(X_{t},A_{t},Z_{t})
, and the next state-context is generated according to
X_{t+1}\sim P(\cdot\mid X_{t},A_{t},Z_{t}),Z_{t+1}\sim\mu(\cdot),

where
Z_{t+1}
is independent of the past.

Context-aware policies and value function.

A deterministic stationary context-aware policy is a map

\pi:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Z}\to\mathcal{A}
, under which the action at time
t
is
A_{t}=\pi(X_{t},Z_{t})
. Restricting to policies
that ignore
Z_{t}
would be suboptimal and would not capture the
decision problem of interest.
For a policy
\pi
, define its value function at state-context
(x,z)
by

V^{\pi}(x,z):=\mathbb{E}^{\pi}\!\left[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}r(X_{t},A_{t},Z_{t})\,\middle|\,X_{0}=x,\ Z_{0}=z\right].

The optimal value is
V^{\star}(x,z):=\sup_{\pi}V^{\pi}(x,z)

where the supremum is over deterministic stationary context-aware policies.

The optimal value satisfies the following Bellman optimality equation :

V^{\star}(x,z)=\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\{r(x,a,z)+\gamma\sum_{x^{\prime},z^{\prime}}P(x^{\prime}\mid x,a,z)\mu(z^{\prime})V^{\star}(x^{\prime},z^{\prime})\}
. Since the next context
is drawn independently from
\mu
, the dependence on
z^{\prime}
enters only
through the
averaged value

\bar{V}^{\star}(x^{\prime}):=\mathbb{E}_{Z^{\prime}\sim\mu}[V^{\star}(x^{\prime},Z^{\prime})]
. Hence

V^{\star}(x,z)=\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\{r(x,a,z)+\gamma\sum_{x^{\prime}}P(x^{\prime}\mid x,a,z)\bar{V}^{\star}(x^{\prime})\}
, and averaging over

z\sim\mu
on both sides gives the following Bellman optimality equation for the averaged value :

\bar{V}^{\star}(x)=\mathbb{E}_{Z\sim\mu}\left[\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\left\{r(x,a,Z)+\gamma\sum_{x^{\prime}}P(x^{\prime}\mid x,a,Z)\bar{V}^{\star}(x^{\prime})\right\}\right].

For any context-aware policy
\pi
, the same argument gives

V^{\pi}(x,z)=r(x,\pi(x,z),z)+\gamma\sum_{x^{\prime}}P(x^{\prime}\mid x,\pi(x,z),z)\bar{V}^{\pi}(x^{\prime})
, where

\bar{V}^{\pi}(x):=\mathbb{E}_{Z\sim\mu}[V^{\pi}(x,Z)]
.
\bar{V}^{\pi}
and
\bar{V}^{\star}
will be the quantities learned by our
algorithms: they summarize future exogenous randomness in dimension

|\mathcal{X}|
.

For any function
\bar{v}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}
, define

g_{x,\bar{v}}^{\star}(z):=\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\left\{r(x,a,z)+\gamma\sum_{x^{\prime}}P(x^{\prime}\mid x,a,z)\bar{v}(x^{\prime})\right\},

and, for any policy
\pi
,

g_{x,\bar{v}}^{\pi}(z):=r(x,\pi(x,z),z)+\gamma\sum_{x^{\prime}}P(x^{\prime}\mid x,\pi(x,z),z)\bar{v}(x^{\prime}).

These are the one-step Bellman expressions at state
x
and context

z
, with
\bar{v}
used to evaluate the next state. The corresponding
context-averaged operators are

(T\bar{v})(x):=\mathbb{E}_{Z\sim\mu}[g_{x,\bar{v}}^{\star}(Z)],\text{ and }(T^{\pi}\bar{v})(x):=\mathbb{E}_{Z\sim\mu}[g_{x,\bar{v}}^{\pi}(Z)].

2.2
Learning objectives

Our objective is to determine the sample complexity of policy evaluation (PE), best value estimation (BVE), and best-policy extraction (BPE), which are the tasks of estimating
V^{\pi}(x,z)
,
V^{*}(x,z)
, and
\pi^{*}(x,z)
, respectively. The learner has access to the generative oracle : depending on the regime, an oracle call returns either a sample from the context distribution
\mu
, or a transition sample generated from
P
at a chosen state-context-action triplet. Oracle calls may be made before the query point
(x,z)
is known, during an offline learning phase, or after
(x,z)
is revealed, when answering that query.

Formally, a learning algorithm is a pair
(\mathcal{A}_{\rm off},\mathcal{O})
. The offline algorithm
\mathcal{A}_{\rm off}
, given access to the prescribed generative oracles, makes at most
n_{\rm learn}
oracle calls and outputs a query mechanism
\mathcal{O}
. Given a state-context couple
(x,z)
, the query mechanism
\mathcal{O}(x,z)
may make at most
m_{\rm query}
new generative-oracle calls to produce its output.
Depending on the objective, the output of
\mathcal{O}
when evaluated at state-context couple
(x,z)
is denoted by

\widehat{\mathcal{V}}(x,z)\in\mathbb{R}
for value-estimation tasks, and by

\widehat{\Pi}(x,z)\in\mathcal{A}
for policy-extraction tasks.

Definition 1

(Algorithmic complexity)
.

A learning algorithm
(\mathcal{A}_{\rm off},\mathcal{O})
has complexity

(n_{\rm learn},m_{\rm query})

if the offline algorithm
\mathcal{A}_{\rm off}
uses at most

n_{\rm learn}
generative-oracle calls, and if, for every query point

(x,z)
, the query-time procedure
\mathcal{O}
uses at most

m_{\rm query}
additional generative-oracle calls.

Definition 2

(Decision-time PAC objectives)
.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
and
\delta\in(0,1)
.
It satisfies the following objectives if, for every query point

(x,z)
, the probability is taken jointly over the offline randomness
of
\mathcal{A}_{\rm off}
, the samples obtained in the offline phase, and
the fresh randomness used by
\mathcal{O}
at query time:

(i)

Policy evaluation (PE).

\ \mathbb{P}\!\left(\bigl|\widehat{\mathcal{V}}^{\pi}(x,z)-V^{\pi}(x,z)\bigr|>\varepsilon\right)\leq\delta.

(ii)

Best-value estimation (BVE).

\ \mathbb{P}\!\left(\bigl|\widehat{\mathcal{V}}^{\star}(x,z)-V^{\star}(x,z)\bigr|>\varepsilon\right)\leq\delta.

(iii)

Best-policy extraction (BPE).

\ \mathbb{P}\!\left(|V^{\star}(x,z)-V^{\widehat{\Pi}}(x,z)|>\varepsilon\right)\leq\delta.

3
Learning the Context Distribution with Known Dynamics

Assume that
P
and
r
are known, and that only the context distribution

\mu
is learned from i.i.d. samples. The key point is to learn only the
averaged values

\bar{V}^{\pi}(x):=\mathbb{E}_{Z\sim\mu}[V^{\pi}(x,Z)],\text{ and }\bar{V}^{\star}(x):=\mathbb{E}_{Z\sim\mu}[V^{\star}(x,Z)],

rather than a value for every
(x,z)
. Once an estimate

\hat{\bar{V}}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{X}|}
has been computed offline, the value at
a realized query
(x,z)
is obtained by a single Bellman backup using the
known model:

\mathcal{O}:(x,z)\rightarrow\widehat{\mathcal{V}}^{\star}(x,z)\;:=\;\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\Bigl\{r(x,a,z)+\gamma\sum_{x^{\prime}}P(x^{\prime}\mid x,a,z)\,\hat{\bar{V}}^{\star}(x^{\prime})\Bigr\},

(1)

Similarly, for a fixed policy
\pi
, an averaged estimate
\hat{\bar{V}}^{\pi}

induces the policy-evaluation query mechanism

\mathcal{O}:(x,z)\rightarrow\widehat{\mathcal{V}}^{\pi}(x,z)\;:=\;\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi(\cdot\mid x,z)}\Bigl[r(x,a,z)+\gamma\sum_{x^{\prime}}P(x^{\prime}\mid x,a,z)\,\hat{\bar{V}}^{\pi}(x^{\prime})\Bigr].

(2)

and the policy query mechanism as the greedy selector

\mathcal{O}:(x,z)\rightarrow\widehat{\Pi}(x,z)\;\in\;\arg\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\Bigl\{r(x,a,z)+\gamma\sum_{x^{\prime}}P(x^{\prime}\mid x,a,z)\,\hat{\bar{V}}^{\star}(x^{\prime})\Bigr\}.

(3)

Since
P
and
r
are known, these algorithms use no additional sampling at query time. Thus
m_{\mathrm{query}}=0
, and the only cost to report is the offline learning budget
n_{\text{learn}}
.

3.1
Main results

We now state the guarantee for the algorithms defined above.

Proposition 1

(Minimax lower bound)
.

Fix
\gamma\in[1/2,1)
. There exists an absolute constant
c>0
such
that, for every
\varepsilon\in(0,\beta/18)
and every PAC objective

\mathcal{P}\in\{\mathrm{PE},\mathrm{BVE},\mathrm{BPE}\}
from
Definition
2
, no algorithm

(\mathcal{A}_{\rm off},\mathcal{O})
with total budget

n_{\rm learn}+m_{\rm query}<c\beta^{3}/\varepsilon^{2}
can satisfy

\mathcal{P}
with confidence
\delta=1/6
uniformly over a family of
contextual MDPs in which only the context distribution
\mu
varies.

The proof constructs, for each objective
\mathcal{P}
, a hard family

\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{P}}
of contextual MDPs sharing the same transition
kernel and reward function, and differing only through the context
distribution
\mu
.

Proof sketch.

We sketch the argument for PE and BVE; the full proof, including the
BPE construction, is given in Appendix
A.1
.

Since there is only one action, PE and BVE coincide. The hard instance has two states: a state
x_{0}
with reward one and an absorbing zero-reward state
x_{T}
. The next state is determined by the context:
z=1
keeps the
agent at
x_{0}
, while
z=2
sends it to
x_{T}
. Thus the value at

(x_{\star},z_{\star})=(x_{0},1)
depends only on the probability

\mu_{1}=\mu(z=1)
, namely

V_{\mu}(x_{0},1)=1+\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma\mu_{1}}.

We compare two nearby context distributions with

\mu_{1}=1-\rho
and
\mu_{1}=1-\rho+\Delta
, where

\rho=1/(2\gamma\beta)
. Around this point, the exit context is rare,
so the effective horizon is of order
\beta
, and a perturbation

\Delta
in
\mu_{1}
changes the value by order
\beta^{2}\Delta
.
Hence
\varepsilon
-accurate estimation requires distinguishing alternatives
with
\Delta\asymp\varepsilon/\beta^{2}
. But each oracle call only gives one
i.i.d. sample from
\mu
, and the two alternatives have per-sample
divergence of order
\beta\Delta^{2}
. Le Cam’s two-point method
therefore forces

n_{\rm learn}+m_{\rm query}\gtrsim\frac{1}{\beta\Delta^{2}}\asymp\frac{\beta^{3}}{\varepsilon^{2}}.

∎

3.2
The algorithm

We now describe the algorithm behind Theorem
1
.
It learns
\bar{V}^{\star}
, the unique fixed point of the
context-averaged Bellman operator
T
introduced in Section
2.1
. Since
P
and
r
are known, this quantity can be computed exactly on every sampled context. Thus the only statistical task is to approximate the expectation over
Z\sim\mu
. Given samples
Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n}\sim\mu
, the empirical operator is

(\hat{T}\bar{v})(x):=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}g_{x,\bar{v}}^{\star}(Z_{i}).

The algorithm below is designed to approximate this operator without recomputing a full empirical Bellman backup from a large new batch of samples at every iteration.

Algorithm outline.

Our algorithm is a contextual adaptation of the variance-reduced value
iteration scheme of
(
25
)
.
The idea is to estimate one reference Bellman backup accurately, and
then update it through cheaper estimates of small differences. The
routine
HalfErr
takes as input an initial value
\bar{v}^{(0)}
which is a
u
-subsolution meaning it satisfies

\bar{v}^{(0)}\leq T\bar{v}^{(0)}

and
\|\bar{V}^{\star}-\bar{v}^{(0)}\|_{\infty}\leq u
.
HalfErr
then outputs
\bar{v}^{R}
which is with high probability a
\frac{u}{2}
-subsolution. Applying the procedure
\log(\frac{\beta}{\epsilon})
times yield
\hat{\bar{V}}
which is with high probability a
\epsilon
-subsolution. Having a
\epsilon
-subsolution is interesting because the policy
\hat{\Pi}
greedy with respect to
\hat{\bar{V}}
then satisfies
\|V^{\Pi}-V^{*}\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon
.
Indeed
\hat{\bar{V}}\leq T\hat{\bar{V}}=T^{\Pi}\hat{\bar{V}}
so
\hat{\bar{V}}\leq\bar{V}^{\Pi}
and we know
\hat{\bar{V}}\geq V^{*}-\epsilon
. With this algorithm, best-policy extraction is therefore no more costly than best-value estimation.

We now describe the
HalfErr
procedure. First, the algorithm computes a high probability lower bound
w
of
T\bar{v}^{(0)}
using
n_{1}
samples. Then, for
i\in 1\ldots R
, it computes sequentially
\Delta^{(i)}
a lower bound of
T\bar{v}^{(i-1)}-T\bar{v}^{(0)}
using
n_{2}
samples and sets
\bar{v}^{(i)}=\max(\bar{v}^{(i-1)},w(x)+\Delta^{i}(x))
. The algorithm then returns
\bar{v}^{(R)}
.

Let us now describe the intuition behind the choice of
n_{1},n_{2}
and
R
.
Ignoring logs, constants and lower order terms, the lower bounds satisfy with high probability, by Lemmas
14
and
16
, combined in Lemma
17

\bigg(T\bar{v}^{(i-1)}-(w+\Delta^{i})\bigg)(x)\leq\xi(x):=\sqrt{\frac{\Var(g^{*}_{x,\bar{v}^{(0)}}(Z))}{n_{1}}}+\gamma\|\bar{v}^{(i-1)}-\bar{v}^{(0)}\|_{\infty}\sqrt{\frac{1}{n_{2}}}.

By definition of
\bar{v}^{i}
we have
\bar{v}^{i}\geq w+\Delta^{i}\geq T\bar{v}^{(i-1)}-\xi
. by Lemma
18
(iv) that

\bar{V}^{*}-\bar{v}^{(i)}\leq\gamma\bar{P}^{\pi^{*}}(\bar{V}^{*}-\bar{v}^{(i-1)})+\xi.

Iterating the inequality gives
\bar{V^{*}}-\bar{v}^{(R)}\leq\gamma^{R}(\bar{P}^{\pi^{*}})^{R}(V^{*}-\bar{v}^{(0)})+\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{i}(\bar{P}^{\pi^{*}})^{i}\xi
.
Then, Lemma
15
together with Corollary
2
shows that

\|(I-\gamma\bar{P}^{\pi^{*}})^{-1}\sqrt{\Var(g^{*}_{\cdot,\bar{v}^{(0)}}(Z))}\|_{\infty}\lesssim\beta^{3/2}+\beta u.

It follows that choosing
R
on the order of
\beta
,
n_{1}
on the order of
\frac{\beta^{3}}{u^{2}}
and
n_{2}
on the order of
\beta^{2}
guarantees

\|\bar{V}^{*}-\bar{v}^{(R)}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{u}{2}
.
The sample complexity of
HalfErr
is dominated by
n_{1}
which is always smaller than
\frac{\beta^{3}}{\epsilon^{2}}
.

4.2
The algorithm

We describe the algorithm behind Theorem
2
. As in the
known-dynamics case, the offline phase learns the averaged value

\bar{V}^{\pi}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{X}|}
, defined as the fixed point of

(T^{\pi}\bar{v})(x)=\mathbb{E}_{Z\sim\mu}[g^{\pi}_{x,\bar{v}}(Z)]
. The only difference is
that, once a context
Z
is sampled,
g^{\pi}_{x,\bar{v}}(Z)
is no longer
directly observable: the transition kernel

P(\cdot\mid x,\pi(x,Z),Z)
must also be sampled.

The algorithm is therefore the policy-evaluation analogue of the
variance-reduced halving scheme of Section
3
, with
exact Bellman evaluations replaced by sampled ones. For each sampled context

Z_{i}
and each state
x
, it draws

X_{i,x}\sim P(\cdot\mid x,\pi(x,Z_{i}),Z_{i})
and forms

\widehat{g}^{\pi}_{x,\bar{v}}(Z_{i}):=r(x,\pi(x,Z_{i}),Z_{i})+\gamma\bar{v}(X_{i,x})
. This is an unbiased estimate of

(T^{\pi}\bar{v})(x)
. Thus each context sample is shared across all states, but
must be completed by one transition sample per state, which accounts for the
additional factor
|\mathcal{X}|
in the offline transition budget.

Variance reduction is unchanged. The anchor phase estimates

T^{\pi}\bar{v}^{(0)}
, and inner iterations estimate only the correction

T^{\pi}\bar{v}^{(i-1)}-T^{\pi}\bar{v}^{(0)}
. They use the same transition draw in
both terms, producing

\gamma(\bar{v}^{(i-1)}(X_{i,x})-\bar{v}^{(0)}(X_{i,x}))
. If the current
iterate is within distance
u
of the anchor, this term is bounded by

\gamma u
. As before, the algorithm estimates small Bellman differences
rather than full backups.

The analysis needs one extra variance term. Besides the contextual
variance

v^{\pi}(x):=\Var_{Z\sim\mu}(g^{\pi}_{x,\bar{V}^{\pi}}(Z))
, the sampled transition
contributes

\sigma^{\pi}(x):=\gamma^{2}\mathbb{E}_{Z\sim\mu}[\Var_{X^{\prime}\sim P(\cdot\mid x,\pi(x,Z),Z)}(\bar{V}^{\pi}(X^{\prime}))]
. Appendix
C.2

shows that the key resolvent bound survives with

, namely

\|(I-\gamma\bar{P}^{\pi})^{-1}\sqrt{v^{\pi}+\sigma^{\pi}}\|_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{2}\,\beta^{3/2}
. Thus transition sampling does not change the

\beta^{3}/\varepsilon^{2}
rate per state. It only makes each Bellman sample cost

|\mathcal{X}|
transitions, yielding the offline complexity of
Theorem
2
, with no dependence on
|\mathcal{Z}|
.

4.3
Decision-time mechanism

The previous subsection describes only the offline computation of
\hat{\bar{V}}^{\pi}
, an estimate of the averaged value on
\mathcal{X}
. To complete the decision-time mechanism of Theorem
2
, we must explain how to answer a realized query
(x,z)
, namely how to estimate the pointwise value
V^{\pi}(x,z)
. The query procedure therefore draws

X_{1},\ldots,X_{m_{\rm query}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{iid}}}{{\sim}}P(\cdot\mid x,\pi(x,z),z)
, and returns

\widehat{\mathcal{V}}^{\pi}(x,z):=r(x,\pi(x,z),z)+\frac{\gamma}{m_{\rm query}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m_{\rm query}}\hat{\bar{V}}^{\pi}(X_{\ell}).

(4)

This separates the two roles of the algorithm. The offline phase learns the
future averaged value
\bar{V}^{\pi}
, which summarizes all future context
randomness. The query phase only estimates the current one-step transition
expectation at the realized triplet
(x,\pi(x,z),z)
. Since

\hat{\bar{V}}^{\pi}\in[0,\beta]^{|\mathcal{X}|}
, Hoeffding’s inequality gives a
query-time sampling error of order
\varepsilon
with
m_{\rm query}=\widetilde{O}\!\left(\beta^{2}/\varepsilon^{2}\right)

fresh transition samples. The offline error

\|\hat{\bar{V}}^{\pi}-\bar{V}^{\pi}\|_{\infty}=O(\varepsilon)
contributes another
O(\varepsilon)
through the same one-step identity. Therefore the resulting estimate is
\varepsilon
-accurate at the queried pair
(x,z)
, which proves the PE guarantee of Theorem
2
.

5
Conclusion

We introduce a PAC learning framework for discounted MDPs with exogenous i.i.d. contexts, in which sample complexity is decomposed into an offline learning budget
n_{\rm learn}
and a cost-per-query
m_{\rm query}
. The central question we addressed is whether the statistical price of large context spaces is unavoidable, and our results give a negative answer in two distinct regimes. When the transition kernel and the reward are known and only the context distribution must be learned, we proved that PE, BVE, and BPE all admit decision-time mechanism with complexity
(n_{\rm learn},m_{\rm query})=(\widetilde{O}(\beta^{3}/\varepsilon^{2}),0)
. The rate is independent of
|\mathcal{Z}|
and minimax optimal up to logarithmic factors. Specializing to the perfect one-step transition lookahead, we obtained
(\widetilde{O}(\beta^{3}/\varepsilon^{2}),0)
lookahead samples, which corresponds to
\widetilde{O}(\beta^{3}|\mathcal{X}||\mathcal{A}|/\varepsilon^{2})
tabular transitions and tightens by a factor
\beta
the bound of
17
. When both
\mu
and
P
are unknown, we focused on PE and showed that the sample complexity remains independent of the cardinality of the context space. The algorithm learns an averaged value
\bar{V}^{\pi}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{X}|}
offline, and lifts it at query time by sampling the unknown transition kernel at the realized
(x,z)
. This gives the tight complexity

(n_{\rm learn},m_{\rm query})=\left(\widetilde{O}\!\left(|\mathcal{X}|\beta^{3}/\varepsilon^{2}\right),\widetilde{O}\!\left(\beta^{2}/\varepsilon^{2}\right)\right),

with no dependence on
|\mathcal{Z}|
. Thus, unknown dynamics do not force the
learner to estimate values on the augmented space
\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Z}
; they
only make the decision-time Bellman lift stochastic. Several questions remain open. A first direction is to understand the full offline/online tradeoff when both
\mu
and
P
are unknown. Our lower bound is matched, up to logarithmic factors, at two points: the purely online rollout point
n_{\rm learn}=0
, and the query-efficient point obtained by our reusable evaluator. Whether the same lower bound is tight for intermediate values of
n_{\rm learn}
remains open. A second direction is to extend the fully unknown analysis to BVE and BPE. The main difficulty is that the contextual Bellman maximum must then be estimated from transition samples, which creates a selection bias when several actions are nearly tied. An other direction is to relax the i.i.d. assumption on the contexts and allow the exogenous process to be Markovian, or more generally mixing. In that case, the averaged Bellman operator remains meaningful, but concentration and variance propagation must account for temporal dependence. Finally, it would be interesting to move beyond tabular controlled state spaces. For instance, in linear or otherwise structured MDPs, one may hope to learn an averaged value representation whose complexity scales with the dimension of the controlled state space, rather than with the size of the augmented space
\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Z}
.

\widehat{\Pi}(x,z)\;\in\;\arg\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\Bigl\{r(x,a,z)+\gamma\sum_{x^{\prime}}P(x^{\prime}\mid x,a,z)\,\hat{\bar{V}}^{\star}(x^{\prime})\Bigr\}.

Martingale variance bound.

This section establishes the contextual analogue of Lemma C.1 of

25
. The result holds for
every
context-aware policy
\pi
, and is therefore reused as-is in the analysis of PE, BVE, and BPE.

Lemma 12

(Averaged variance bound)
.

For every context-aware policy
\pi
,

\sum_{t\geq 0}\gamma^{2t}(\bar{P}^{\pi})^{t}v^{\pi}\;\preceq\;\beta^{2}\,\mathbf{1}\qquad\text{(coordinate-wise).}

(31)

Proof.

Fix
x\in\mathcal{X}
and consider the process started at
X_{0}=x
. Define

M_{t}:=\mathbb{E}_{x}[G(x)\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]
. Since
G(x)
is integrable,
(M_{t})_{t\geq 0}

is a martingale w.r.t.
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, with
M_{0}=\bar{V}^{\pi}(x)
and

M_{t}\xrightarrow[t\to\infty]{}G(x)
a.s. and in
L^{2}
(martingale convergence;
|M_{t}|\leq\beta
). In
particular, by orthogonality of martingale increments

\Var_{x}(G(x))\;=\;\sum_{t\geq 0}\mathbb{E}_{x}[(M_{t+1}-M_{t})^{2}].

(32)

Inserting
\mathcal{G}_{t}
between

\mathcal{F}_{t}
and
\mathcal{F}_{t+1}
,

M_{t+1}-M_{t}\;=\;\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{x}[G(x)\mid\mathcal{F}_{t+1}]-\mathbb{E}_{x}[G(x)\mid\mathcal{G}_{t}]}_{=:D_{t}^{X}}+\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{x}[G(x)\mid\mathcal{G}_{t}]-\mathbb{E}_{x}[G(x)\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]}_{=:D_{t}^{Z}}\;

(33)

By construction
D_{t}^{Z}
is
\mathcal{G}_{t}
-measurable with
\mathbb{E}_{x}[D_{t}^{Z}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]=0
,
and
D_{t}^{X}
is
\mathcal{F}_{t+1}
-measurable with
\mathbb{E}_{x}[D_{t}^{X}\mid\mathcal{G}_{t}]=0
. Since

\mathcal{F}_{t}\subset\mathcal{G}_{t}
, the tower property gives

\mathbb{E}_{x}[D_{t}^{Z}D_{t}^{X}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]=\mathbb{E}_{x}[D_{t}^{Z}\mathbb{E}_{x}[D_{t}^{X}\mid\mathcal{G}_{t}]\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]=0
,
so

\mathbb{E}_{x}[(M_{t+1}-M_{t})^{2}]\;=\;\mathbb{E}_{x}[(D_{t}^{Z})^{2}]+\mathbb{E}_{x}[(D_{t}^{X})^{2}].

(34)

We claim

\mathbb{E}_{x}[G(x)\mid\mathcal{G}_{t}]\;=\;\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}\gamma^{s}r(X_{s},A_{s},Z_{s})\;+\;\gamma^{t}g_{X_{t}}^{\pi}(Z_{t})\qquad\text{a.s.}

(35)

Indeed, the first
t
rewards are
\mathcal{G}_{t}
-measurable. Moreover

A_{t}=\pi(X_{t},Z_{t})
is
\mathcal{G}_{t}
-measurable, and conditionally on
(X_{t},A_{t},Z_{t})

the future
\sum_{s\geq t}\gamma^{s-t}r(X_{s},A_{s},Z_{s})
has expectation

r(X_{t},A_{t},Z_{t})+\gamma\sum_{x^{\prime}}P(x^{\prime}\mid X_{t},A_{t},Z_{t})\bar{V}^{\pi}(x^{\prime})=g_{X_{t}}^{\pi}(Z_{t})

by the Markov property and the definition of
\bar{V}^{\pi}
; here we use
crucially that
(Z_{s})_{s>t}
is independent of
\mathcal{G}_{t}
, since
\mu
is i.i.d.

By exogeneity of
Z_{t}
relative to
\mathcal{F}_{t}
(i.e.
Z_{t}
is independent of

\mathcal{F}_{t}
, which depends only on
Z_{0},\dots,Z_{t-1}
and the associated
transitions), integrating (
35
) against
Z_{t}\sim\mu
yields

\mathbb{E}_{x}[G(x)\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]\;=\;\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}\gamma^{s}r(X_{s},A_{s},Z_{s})\;+\;\gamma^{t}\bar{V}^{\pi}(X_{t}).

(36)

Subtracting (
36
) from (
35
),

D_{t}^{Z}\;=\;\gamma^{t}\bigl(g_{X_{t}}^{\pi}(Z_{t})-\bar{V}^{\pi}(X_{t})\bigr).

Computation of
\mathbb{E}_{x}[(D_{t}^{Z})^{2}]
.
By exogeneity of

Z_{t}
once more,

\mathbb{E}_{x}[(D_{t}^{Z})^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]\;=\;\gamma^{2t}\mathbb{E}_{x}\bigl[(g_{X_{t}}^{\pi}(Z_{t})-\bar{V}^{\pi}(X_{t}))^{2}\,\big|\,\mathcal{F}_{t}\bigr]\;=\;\gamma^{2t}v^{\pi}(X_{t}),

since
X_{t}
is
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable,
Z_{t}\sim\mu
independently of
\mathcal{F}_{t}
,
and
\bar{V}^{\pi}(X_{t})=\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[g_{X_{t}}^{\pi}(Z)]
. Taking expectations,

\mathbb{E}_{x}[(D_{t}^{Z})^{2}]\;=\;\gamma^{2t}\mathbb{E}_{x}[v^{\pi}(X_{t})].

(37)

Conclusion.
Combining
(
32
), (
34
),
(
37
), and (
30
),

\sum_{t\geq 0}\gamma^{2t}\mathbb{E}_{x}[v^{\pi}(X_{t})]\;\leq\;\sum_{t\geq 0}\mathbb{E}_{x}[(D_{t}^{Z})^{2}]+\sum_{t\geq 0}\mathbb{E}_{x}[(D_{t}^{X})^{2}]\;=\;\Var_{x}(G(x))\;\leq\;\beta^{2}.

It remains to identify
\mathbb{E}_{x}[v^{\pi}(X_{t})]
with
(\bar{P}^{\pi})^{t}v^{\pi}(x)
.
Under
\pi
, the controlled process
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is Markov on
\mathcal{X}
:

\mathbb{P}(X_{t+1}=x^{\prime}\mid X_{t}=x,\dots)\;=\;\mathbb{E}_{Z\sim\mu}[P(x^{\prime}\mid x,\pi(x,Z),Z)]\;=\;\bar{P}^{\pi}(x^{\prime}\mid x),

again by independence of
Z_{t}
from the controlled past. Hence

\mathbb{E}_{x}[v^{\pi}(X_{t})]=(\bar{P}^{\pi})^{t}v^{\pi}(x)
, and the previous display becomes

\sum_{t\geq 0}\gamma^{2t}(\bar{P}^{\pi})^{t}v^{\pi}(x)\;\leq\;\beta^{2}.

Since
x\in\mathcal{X}
is arbitrary, this concludes the proof.
∎

Corollary 2

(Bound on the complexity functional)
.

For every context-aware policy
\pi
,

\mathcal{V}(P,\mu,\pi)\;:=\;\bigl\|(I-\gamma\bar{P}^{\pi})^{-1}\sqrt{v^{\pi}}\bigr\|_{\infty}\;\leq\;\sqrt{\frac{1+\gamma}{1-\gamma}}\cdot\beta\;\leq\;\sqrt{2}\,\beta^{3/2}.

(38)

Proof.

The matrix
\bar{P}^{\pi}
is row-stochastic, hence

\|\bar{P}^{\pi}\|_{\ell_{\infty}\to\ell_{\infty}}\leq 1
. Applying Lemma C.3 of

25
,

\bigl\|(I-\gamma\bar{P}^{\pi})^{-1}\sqrt{v^{\pi}}\bigr\|_{\infty}\;\leq\;\sqrt{\frac{1+\gamma}{1-\gamma}}\,\bigl\|(I-\gamma^{2}\bar{P}^{\pi})^{-1}v^{\pi}\bigr\|_{\infty}^{1/2}.

(39)

By Lemma
12
,

(I-\gamma^{2}\bar{P}^{\pi})^{-1}v^{\pi}\;=\;\sum_{t\geq 0}\gamma^{2t}(\bar{P}^{\pi})^{t}v^{\pi}\;\preceq\;\beta^{2}\,\mathbf{1},

(40)

so
\|(I-\gamma^{2}\bar{P}^{\pi})^{-1}v^{\pi}\|_{\infty}\leq\beta^{2}
. Combining with

\sqrt{(1+\gamma)/(1-\gamma)}\leq\sqrt{2/(1-\gamma)}=\sqrt{2\beta}
gives the
claim.
∎

The HalfErr algorithm

We now introduce
HalfErr
, the variance-reduced subroutine underlying Theorem
1
. The same algorithm serves both
BVE and BPE. In both cases the relevant operator is the optimality operator

(T\bar{v})(x)\;:=\;\mathbb{E}_{Z\sim\mu}\Bigl[\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\Bigl\{r(x,a,Z)+\gamma\sum_{x^{\prime}}P(x^{\prime}\mid x,a,Z)\,\bar{v}(x^{\prime})\Bigr\}\Bigr],

(41)

and
HalfErr
estimates its fixed point
\bar{V}^{\star}
. The BVE
guarantee is read off directly from the output
\hat{\bar{V}}^{\star}
; the BPE
guarantee follows by greedy extraction at no additional sample cost, thanks to the sub-solution invariant

\hat{\bar{V}}^{\star}\leq T\hat{\bar{V}}^{\star}
enforced throughout the iterations.
The PE case is recovered by replacing the inner
\max_{a}
by the fixed action

\pi(x,Z)
throughout, i.e. by running
HalfErr
on the
policy-evaluation operator
T^{\pi}
instead; the analysis below carries over
verbatim and yields the same sample complexity.

For any value vector

\bar{v}
and context
z
, the optimal one-step Bellman backup at
x
is

g_{x,\bar{v}}^{\star}(z)\;:=\;\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\Bigl\{r(x,a,z)+\gamma\sum_{x^{\prime}}P(x^{\prime}\mid x,a,z)\,\bar{v}(x^{\prime})\Bigr\},

which is computable in closed form since
P
and
r
are known. The empirical
operator
\hat{T}\bar{v}(x):=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i}g_{x,\bar{v}}^{\star}(Z_{i})
is the

\hat{\mu}
-plug-in operator, with
\hat{\mu}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i}\delta_{Z_{i}}
.

HalfErr
takes as input a
sub-solution
\bar{v}^{(0)}\leq T\bar{v}^{(0)}
within
L^{\infty}
-distance
u
of

\bar{V}^{\star}
, and produces an output within distance
u/2
. The strategy
mirrors the variance-reduced QVI of

25
:

•

Phase 1 (anchor).
Estimate
T\bar{v}^{(0)}
once, using

n_{1}=\widetilde{\Theta}(\beta^{3}/u^{2})
samples. The empirical mean is corrected
downward by an empirical Bernstein-type margin so that the resulting anchor

w
satisfies
w\leq T\bar{v}^{(0)}
pointwise on a high-probability event.

•

Phase 2 (low-variance increments).
Run
R=O(\beta\log(\beta/u))

inner iterations. At each iteration
i
, draw a fresh batch of size

n_{2}=\widetilde{\Theta}(\beta^{2})
, estimate the increment

T\bar{v}^{(i-1)}-T\bar{v}^{(0)}
, and update the iterate by

\bar{v}^{(i)}:=\max(\bar{v}^{(i-1)},\,w+\Delta^{(i)})
. Because the
iterates remain within distance
u
of
\bar{v}^{(0)}
, the increment

g_{x,\bar{v}^{(i-1)}}^{\star}-g_{x,\bar{v}^{(0)}}^{\star}
has range

rather than
O(\beta)

\widetilde{O}(\beta^{2})
, independent of
u^{-2}
. The
\max
with the
previous iterate enforces monotonicity.

The total sample cost is
n_{1}+R\,n_{2}=\widetilde{O}(\beta^{3}/u^{2})
, dominated
by Phase 1.

|\hat{\sigma}(x)-v_{0}(x)|\;\leq\;|\widehat{S_{2}}(x)-S_{2}(x)|+|\tilde{w}(x)^{2}-(T\bar{v}^{(0)})(x)^{2}|\;\leq\;3\beta^{2}\sqrt{n_{1}^{-1}L/2}\;\leq\;4\beta^{2}\sqrt{2\alpha_{1}},

(55)

with total failure probability
\leq\delta/2

Lemma 15

(Variance perturbation)
.

For any
\bar{v}
with
\|\bar{v}-\bar{V}^{\star}\|_{\infty}\leq u
,

\sqrt{v_{\bar{v}}}\;\leq\;\sqrt{v_{\star}}+u\,\mathbf{1},\qquad\sqrt{v_{\star}}\;\leq\;\sqrt{v_{\bar{v}}}+u\,\mathbf{1},

where
v_{\bar{v}}(x):=\Var_{\mu}\bigl(g_{x,\bar{v}}^{\star}(Z)\bigr)
and the
inequalities are coordinate-wise.

Proof.

Fix
x\in\mathcal{X}
. By the triangle inequality for the

semi-norm

\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}:=\sqrt{\Var_{\mu}(f)}
,

\bigl|\sqrt{v_{\bar{v}}(x)}-\sqrt{v_{\star}(x)}\bigr|\;\leq\;\sqrt{\Var_{\mu}\bigl(g_{x,\bar{v}}^{\star}(Z)-g_{x,\bar{V}^{\star}}^{\star}(Z)\bigr)}.

By Lipschitzness of
\max
and
\sum_{x^{\prime}}P(x^{\prime}\mid x,a,z)=1
,

\bigl|g_{x,\bar{v}}^{\star}(z)-g_{x,\bar{V}^{\star}}^{\star}(z)\bigr|\;\leq\;\gamma\,\|\bar{v}-\bar{V}^{\star}\|_{\infty}\;\leq\;\gamma u\;\leq\;u\quad\text{pointwise in }z,

so the right-hand side is at most

.
∎

Lemma 16

(Inner-loop estimation error, conditional version)
.

Fix

, and define the event

\mathcal{E}_{i}\;:=\;\Bigl\{\;\forall x\in\mathcal{X}:\;\bigl(\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[g_{\cdot,\bar{v}^{(i-1)}}^{\star}-g_{\cdot,\bar{v}^{(0)}}^{\star}]\bigr)(x)-\tfrac{u}{8\beta}\;\leq\;\Delta^{(i)}(x)\;\leq\;\bigl(\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[g_{\cdot,\bar{v}^{(i-1)}}^{\star}-g_{\cdot,\bar{v}^{(0)}}^{\star}]\bigr)(x)\;\Bigr\}.

On the event
\bigl\{\|\bar{v}^{(i-1)}-\bar{v}^{(0)}\|_{\infty}\leq u\bigr\}\in\mathcal{H}_{i-1}

\Pr\!\bigl(\mathcal{E}_{i}\mid\mathcal{H}_{i-1}\bigr)\;\geq\;1-\delta/R.

Proof.

Conditional on
\mathcal{H}_{i-1}
, the iterate
\bar{v}^{(i-1)}
is deterministic
(measurable w.r.t.
\mathcal{H}_{i-1}
), and the batch

\tilde{Z}^{(i)}_{1},\dots,\tilde{Z}^{(i)}_{n_{2}}
is i.i.d.
\mu
, drawn fresh and
independent of
\mathcal{H}_{i-1}
. Define, for each
x\in\mathcal{X}
and
j=1,\dots,n_{2}
,

\Phi_{j}^{(x)}\;:=\;g_{x,\bar{v}^{(i-1)}}^{\star}(\tilde{Z}_{j}^{(i)})-g_{x,\bar{v}^{(0)}}^{\star}(\tilde{Z}_{j}^{(i)}).

By Lipschitzness of
g^{\star}
in its value-function argument, on the event

\{\|\bar{v}^{(i-1)}-\bar{v}^{(0)}\|_{\infty}\leq u\}
,

\bigl|\Phi_{j}^{(x)}\bigr|\;\leq\;\gamma\,\|\bar{v}^{(i-1)}-\bar{v}^{(0)}\|_{\infty}\;\leq\;\gamma u\quad\text{pointwise.}

The range of

is therefore at most
2\gamma u
. Hoeffding’s

give, conditional on

\mathcal{H}_{i-1}

\Bigl|\tfrac{1}{n_{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{2}}\Phi_{j}^{(x)}-\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[\Phi^{(x)}\mid\mathcal{H}_{i-1}]\Bigr|\;\leq\;\gamma u\sqrt{\frac{2\log(2R|\mathcal{X}|/\delta)}{n_{2}}}.

.
With

(using

and

). The shift by

in

of width

into the claimed one-sided bound: the shifted estimate

below it.
∎

Lemma 18

(Per-iteration contraction)
.

Define the global success event
\mathcal{E}:=\bigcap_{i=0}^{R}\mathcal{E}_{i}
, where

\mathcal{E}_{0}
is the event of Lemma
14
and
\mathcal{E}_{i}
for

i\geq 1
is the event of Lemma
16
. Then

\Pr(\mathcal{E})\;\geq\;1-2\delta,

and on
\mathcal{E}
, for every
i\in\{1,\dots,R\}
:

(i)

(monotonicity and sandwich)

\bar{v}^{(0)}\leq\bar{v}^{(i-1)}\leq\bar{v}^{(i)}\leq\bar{V}^{\star}
;

(ii)

(sub-solution)

\bar{v}^{(i)}\leq T\bar{v}^{(i)}
;

(iii)

(distance from anchor)

\|\bar{v}^{(i)}-\bar{v}^{(0)}\|_{\infty}\leq u
;

(iv)

(contraction recursion)

\bar{V}^{\star}-\bar{v}^{(i)}\;\leq\;\gamma\,\bar{P}^{\pi^{\star}}\bigl(\bar{V}^{\star}-\bar{v}^{(i-1)}\bigr)+\xi,

for any optimal context-aware policy
\pi^{\star}
, where
\bar{P}^{\pi^{\star}}

is the corresponding averaged transition kernel.

Proof.

Probability bound.
The event
\mathcal{E}_{0}
holds with probability

\geq 1-\delta
unconditionally (Lemma
14
). The events

\mathcal{E}_{i}
for
i\geq 1
are conditional, requiring

\|\bar{v}^{(i-1)}-\bar{v}^{(0)}\|_{\infty}\leq u
; we will show by induction
that this hypothesis is satisfied on
\mathcal{E}_{0}\cap\dots\cap\mathcal{E}_{i-1}
, so
that Lemma
16
gives
\Pr(\mathcal{E}_{i}\mid\mathcal{H}_{i-1})\geq 1-\delta/R

on this event. The tower property and a union bound over
i=1,\dots,R
then
yield

\Pr(\mathcal{E})\;\geq\;\Pr(\mathcal{E}_{0})\bigl(1-R\cdot\delta/R\bigr)\;\geq\;1-\delta-\delta\;=\;1-2\delta.

Inductive verification of (i)–(iv).
We argue by induction on
i
. The
base case
i=0
holds by the input hypothesis:
\bar{v}^{(0)}\leq T\bar{v}^{(0)}
,

\bar{V}^{\star}-\bar{v}^{(0)}\leq u\mathbf{1}
, hence
\bar{v}^{(0)}\leq\bar{V}^{\star}

and
\|\bar{v}^{(0)}-\bar{v}^{(0)}\|_{\infty}=0\leq u
.

Assume (i)–(iii) hold up to index
i-1
. By (iii) at
i-1
,

\|\bar{v}^{(i-1)}-\bar{v}^{(0)}\|_{\infty}\leq u
, so the hypothesis of
Lemma
17
is satisfied for iteration
i
, yielding
(
56
) and (
57
).

Proof of (i).
The line 12 update is

\bar{v}^{(i)}(x)=\max(\tilde{v}^{(i)}(x),\bar{v}^{(i-1)}(x))
, so

\bar{v}^{(i)}\geq\bar{v}^{(i-1)}
pointwise, and by induction

\bar{v}^{(i)}\geq\bar{v}^{(0)}
. For the upper bound
\bar{v}^{(i)}\leq\bar{V}^{\star}
,
use (
56
) together with monotonicity of
T
(which
follows from
g_{x,\bar{v}}^{\star}(z)
being non-decreasing in
\bar{v}
):

\tilde{v}^{(i)}(x)=w(x)+\Delta^{(i)}(x)\;\leq\;(T\bar{v}^{(i-1)})(x)\;\leq\;(T\bar{V}^{\star})(x)=\bar{V}^{\star}(x),

where the second inequality uses
\bar{v}^{(i-1)}\leq\bar{V}^{\star}
from the
inductive hypothesis. Hence
\tilde{v}^{(i)}\leq\bar{V}^{\star}
, and therefore

\bar{v}^{(i)}=\max(\tilde{v}^{(i)},\bar{v}^{(i-1)})\leq\bar{V}^{\star}
.

Proof of (ii).
We do a case analysis on the line 12

\max
.

Case A:
\bar{v}^{(i)}(x)=\tilde{v}^{(i)}(x)
.
By
(
56
) and monotonicity of
T
,

\bar{v}^{(i)}(x)=\tilde{v}^{(i)}(x)\;\leq\;(T\bar{v}^{(i-1)})(x)\;\leq\;(T\bar{v}^{(i)})(x),

since
\bar{v}^{(i-1)}\leq\bar{v}^{(i)}
by (i).

Case B:
\bar{v}^{(i)}(x)=\bar{v}^{(i-1)}(x)
.
By the inductive
hypothesis (ii) and monotonicity of
T
,

\bar{v}^{(i)}(x)=\bar{v}^{(i-1)}(x)\;\leq\;(T\bar{v}^{(i-1)})(x)\;\leq\;(T\bar{v}^{(i)})(x).

Proof of (iii).
By (i),

\bar{v}^{(0)}\leq\bar{v}^{(i)}\leq\bar{V}^{\star}
, so

0\leq\bar{v}^{(i)}-\bar{v}^{(0)}\leq\bar{V}^{\star}-\bar{v}^{(0)}\leq u\mathbf{1}
.

Proof of (iv).
By the line 12 update and
(
57
),

\bar{v}^{(i)}(x)\;\geq\;\tilde{v}^{(i)}(x)=w(x)+\Delta^{(i)}(x)\;\geq\;(T\bar{v}^{(i-1)})(x)-\xi(x).

For any optimal policy
\pi^{\star}
,

(T\bar{v}^{(i-1)})(x)\;\geq\;\mathbb{E}_{Z\sim\mu}\Bigl[r(x,\pi^{\star}(x,Z),Z)+\gamma\sum_{x^{\prime}}P(x^{\prime}\mid x,\pi^{\star}(x,Z),Z)\,\bar{v}^{(i-1)}(x^{\prime})\Bigr],

since the inner
\max_{a}
is at least the value under
\pi^{\star}
.
Subtracting from

\bar{V}^{\star}(x)=\mathbb{E}_{Z\sim\mu}\bigl[r(x,\pi^{\star}(x,Z),Z)+\gamma\sum_{x^{\prime}}P(x^{\prime}\mid x,\pi^{\star}(x,Z),Z)\,\bar{V}^{\star}(x^{\prime})\bigr]

\displaystyle\bar{V}^{\star}(x)-(T\bar{v}^{(i-1)})(x)\;

\displaystyle\leq\;\gamma\,\mathbb{E}_{Z\sim\mu}\Bigl[\sum_{x^{\prime}}P(x^{\prime}\mid x,\pi^{\star}(x,Z),Z)\bigl(\bar{V}^{\star}(x^{\prime})-\bar{v}^{(i-1)}(x^{\prime})\bigr)\Bigr]\;

(66)

\displaystyle=\;\gamma\,\bar{P}^{\pi^{\star}}\bigl(\bar{V}^{\star}-\bar{v}^{(i-1)}\bigr)(x).

(67)

Combining,

\bar{V}^{\star}(x)-\bar{v}^{(i)}(x)\;\leq\;\bar{V}^{\star}(x)-(T\bar{v}^{(i-1)})(x)+\xi(x)\;\leq\;\gamma\,\bar{P}^{\pi^{\star}}\bigl(\bar{V}^{\star}-\bar{v}^{(i-1)}\bigr)(x)+\xi(x).\qed

The contraction recursion of Lemma
18
(iv) iterates into a
geometric decay of the error, plus a residual statistical error governed by

\xi
. Proposition
4
balances the two contributions and
yields the halving guarantee.

Proposition 4

(Halving guarantee)
.

With absolute constants
c_{1}\geq 4
,
c_{2}\geq 2^{16}
,
c_{3}\geq 512
,
Algorithm
2
outputs
\bar{v}^{(R)}
satisfying

\bar{v}^{(R)}\leq T\bar{v}^{(R)}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\bar{V}^{\star}-\bar{v}^{(R)}\leq\tfrac{u}{2}\,\mathbf{1}

with probability at least
1-2\delta
, using

n_{1}+R\,n_{2}\;=\;O\!\left(\frac{\beta^{3}\log(|\mathcal{X}|/\delta)}{u^{2}}+\beta^{3}\log(\beta/u)\log(|\mathcal{X}|R/\delta)\right)\;=\;\widetilde{O}\!\left(\frac{\beta^{3}}{u^{2}}\right)

samples from
\mu
.

Proof.

We work on the event
\mathcal{E}
of Lemma
18
, of probability

\geq 1-2\delta
. Iterating the contraction recursion
Lemma
18
(iv) for
R
rounds,

\bar{V}^{\star}-\bar{v}^{(R)}\;\leq\;\gamma^{R}(\bar{P}^{\pi^{\star}})^{R}(\bar{V}^{\star}-\bar{v}^{(0)})+\sum_{i=0}^{R-1}\gamma^{i}(\bar{P}^{\pi^{\star}})^{i}\,\xi\;\leq\;\gamma^{R}u\,\mathbf{1}+(I-\gamma\bar{P}^{\pi^{\star}})^{-1}\xi,

using
\bar{V}^{\star}-\bar{v}^{(0)}\leq u\mathbf{1}
and

\|(\bar{P}^{\pi^{\star}})^{R}\mathbf{1}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
(row-stochasticity).

Step 1: contraction term.
With

R=\lceil c_{1}\beta\ln(4\beta/u)\rceil
and
c_{1}\geq 4
, using

-\ln\gamma\geq 1-\gamma=1/\beta
,

\gamma^{R}u\;\leq\;e^{-R/\beta}u\;\leq\;e^{-c_{1}\ln(4\beta/u)}u\;=\;u\cdot\bigl(u/(4\beta)\bigr)^{c_{1}}\;\leq\;u\cdot 4^{-c_{1}}\;\leq\;\tfrac{u}{4},

where the penultimate inequality uses
u\leq\beta
.

Step 2: statistical term.
Decompose
\xi
from
(
58
):

(I-\gamma\bar{P}^{\pi^{\star}})^{-1}\xi\;\leq\;\underbrace{\tfrac{u}{8\beta}\,(I-\gamma\bar{P}^{\pi^{\star}})^{-1}\mathbf{1}}_{(\textsc{A})}+\underbrace{2(I-\gamma\bar{P}^{\pi^{\star}})^{-1}\sqrt{2\alpha_{1}\,v_{0}}}_{(\textsc{B})}+\underbrace{(8\alpha_{1}^{3/4}\beta+\tfrac{4}{3}\alpha_{1}\beta)(I-\gamma\bar{P}^{\pi^{\star}})^{-1}\mathbf{1}}_{(\textsc{C})}.

Using
\|(I-\gamma\bar{P}^{\pi^{\star}})^{-1}\mathbf{1}\|_{\infty}\leq\beta
:

\|(\textsc{A})\|_{\infty}\leq\tfrac{u}{8},\qquad\|(\textsc{C})\|_{\infty}\leq 8\alpha_{1}^{3/4}\beta^{2}+\tfrac{4}{3}\alpha_{1}\beta^{2}.

For
(\textsc{B})
, we pass from
v_{0}
to
v_{\star}
via
Lemma
15
(using
\|\bar{v}^{(0)}-\bar{V}^{\star}\|_{\infty}\leq u
):

\sqrt{v_{0}}\leq\sqrt{v_{\star}}+u\mathbf{1}
, hence

\|(\textsc{B})\|_{\infty}\;\leq\;2\sqrt{2\alpha_{1}}\bigl(\|(I-\gamma\bar{P}^{\pi^{\star}})^{-1}\sqrt{v_{\star}}\|_{\infty}+u\beta\bigr).

Corollary
2
applied to
\pi=\pi^{\star}
gives

\|(I-\gamma\bar{P}^{\pi^{\star}})^{-1}\sqrt{v_{\star}}\|_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{2}\beta^{3/2}

— note that
v_{\star}=v^{\pi^{\star}}
, since
g_{x,\bar{V}^{\star}}^{\star}=g_{x}^{\pi^{\star}}

(the inner
\max
is attained at
\pi^{\star}(x,z)
). Therefore

\|(\textsc{B})\|_{\infty}\;\leq\;4\sqrt{\alpha_{1}}\,\beta^{3/2}+2\sqrt{2\alpha_{1}}\,u\beta.

Substitution of
\alpha_{1}
.
With
\alpha_{1}=L/n_{1}
and

n_{1}=c_{2}\beta^{3}u^{-2}L
, we have
\alpha_{1}=u^{2}/(c_{2}\beta^{3})
, so:

\sqrt{\alpha_{1}}\,\beta^{3/2}=\frac{u}{\sqrt{c_{2}}},\quad\sqrt{\alpha_{1}}\,u\beta=\frac{u^{2}}{\sqrt{c_{2}\beta}}\leq\frac{u}{\sqrt{c_{2}}},\quad\alpha_{1}^{3/4}\beta^{2}=\frac{u^{3/2}}{c_{2}^{3/4}\beta^{1/4}}\leq\frac{u}{c_{2}^{3/4}},\quad\alpha_{1}\beta^{2}=\frac{u^{2}}{c_{2}\beta}\leq\frac{u}{c_{2}},

where the last three inequalities use
u\leq\beta
.

Combining,

\|(I-\gamma\bar{P}^{\pi^{\star}})^{-1}\xi\|_{\infty}\;\leq\;\tfrac{u}{8}+\tfrac{4u}{\sqrt{c_{2}}}+\tfrac{2\sqrt{2}\,u}{\sqrt{c_{2}}}+\tfrac{8u}{c_{2}^{3/4}}+\tfrac{4u}{3c_{2}}.

With
c_{2}\geq 2^{16}
,
\sqrt{c_{2}}\geq 256
and
c_{2}^{3/4}\geq 4096
, so the
last four terms sum to at most

\tfrac{u}{32}+\tfrac{u}{64}+\tfrac{u}{512}+\tfrac{u}{49152}\;\leq\;\tfrac{u}{16}.

Therefore

\|(I-\gamma\bar{P}^{\pi^{\star}})^{-1}\xi\|_{\infty}\;\leq\;\tfrac{u}{8}+\tfrac{u}{16}\;\leq\;\tfrac{u}{4}.

(68)

Step 3: conclusion.
Combining Steps 1 and 2,

\|\bar{V}^{\star}-\bar{v}^{(R)}\|_{\infty}\;\leq\;\tfrac{u}{4}+\tfrac{u}{4}\;=\;\tfrac{u}{2}.

The sub-solution property
\bar{v}^{(R)}\leq T\bar{v}^{(R)}
is
Lemma
18
(ii) at
i=R
.

Sample count.
The Phase-1 cost is

n_{1}=c_{2}\beta^{3}u^{-2}L=O(\beta^{3}u^{-2}\log(|\mathcal{X}|/\delta))
, and the
Phase-2 cost is

R\,n_{2}=O(\beta\log(\beta/u))\cdot O(\beta^{2}\log(R|\mathcal{X}|/\delta))=O(\beta^{3}\log(\beta/u)\log(|\mathcal{X}|R/\delta))
. The sum is the claimed

\widetilde{O}(\beta^{3}/u^{2})
.
∎

Theorem 3

(BVE upper bound)
.

There exists an absolute constant

and

i.i.d. samples from

Proof.

Independence and probability.
The

Proposition 5

(Lift from averaged to augmented)
.

Let
\hat{\bar{V}}^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{X}|}
satisfy

\|\hat{\bar{V}}^{\star}-\bar{V}^{\star}\|_{\infty}\;\leq\;\varepsilon\qquad\text{and}\qquad\hat{\bar{V}}^{\star}\;\leq\;T\hat{\bar{V}}^{\star},

and define

\hat{V}^{\star}(x,z)\;:=\;\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\Bigl\{r(x,a,z)+\gamma\sum_{x^{\prime}}P(x^{\prime}\mid x,a,z)\,\hat{\bar{V}}^{\star}(x^{\prime})\Bigr\}

for all
(x,z)\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Z}
. Then:

(i)

(BVE)

\|\hat{V}^{\star}-V^{\star}\|_{\infty}\;\leq\;\gamma\varepsilon
.

(ii)

(BPE)
The greedy context-aware policy
extracted from
\hat{\bar{V}}^{\star}

satisfies, uniformly in
(x,z)
,

V^{\star}(x,z)-V^{\hat{\pi}}(x,z)\;\leq\;\gamma\varepsilon.

(iii)

(PE)
For any context-aware policy
\pi
and any
\hat{\bar{V}}^{\pi}

satisfying
\|\hat{\bar{V}}^{\pi}-\bar{V}^{\pi}\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon
, the lifted
estimate

\hat{V}^{\pi}(x,z)\;:=\;r(x,\pi(x,z),z)+\gamma\sum_{x^{\prime}}P(x^{\prime}\mid x,\pi(x,z),z)\,\hat{\bar{V}}^{\pi}(x^{\prime})

satisfies
\|\hat{V}^{\pi}-V^{\pi}\|_{\infty}\leq\gamma\varepsilon
.

Proof.

(i) BVE.
The augmented optimal value satisfies the Bellman identity

V^{\star}(x,z)\;=\;\max_{a}\Bigl\{r(x,a,z)+\gamma\sum_{x^{\prime}}P(x^{\prime}\mid x,a,z)\,\bar{V}^{\star}(x^{\prime})\Bigr\},

which follows from the augmented Bellman optimality equation

V^{\star}(x,z)=\max_{a}\bigl\{r(x,a,z)+\gamma\sum_{x^{\prime},z^{\prime}}P(x^{\prime}\mid x,a,z)\mu(z^{\prime})V^{\star}(x^{\prime},z^{\prime})\bigr\}

and the definition
\bar{V}^{\star}(x^{\prime})=\mathbb{E}_{Z\sim\mu}[V^{\star}(x^{\prime},Z)]
. Hence

V^{\star}
and
\hat{V}^{\star}
are obtained from
\bar{V}^{\star}
and

\hat{\bar{V}}^{\star}
by the same Bellman backup applied at
(x,z)
. By
Lipschitzness of
\max
and
\sum_{x^{\prime}}P(x^{\prime}\mid x,a,z)=1
,

|\hat{V}^{\star}(x,z)-V^{\star}(x,z)|\;\leq\;\gamma\,\|\hat{\bar{V}}^{\star}-\bar{V}^{\star}\|_{\infty}\;\leq\;\gamma\varepsilon.

(ii) BPE.
We first establish the augmented sub-solution invariant

\hat{V}^{\star}(x,z)\;\leq\;V^{\hat{\pi}}(x,z)\qquad\forall(x,z)\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Z}.

(69)

The hypothesis
\hat{\bar{V}}^{\star}\leq T\hat{\bar{V}}^{\star}
, combined with

T\hat{\bar{V}}^{\star}=T^{\hat{\pi}}\hat{\bar{V}}^{\star}
(definition of

\hat{\pi}
as greedy with respect to
\hat{\bar{V}}^{\star}
), gives

\hat{\bar{V}}^{\star}\leq T^{\hat{\pi}}\hat{\bar{V}}^{\star}
. Iterating monotonically
under the contraction
T^{\hat{\pi}}
with fixed point
\bar{V}^{\hat{\pi}}
,

\hat{\bar{V}}^{\star}\;\leq\;\bar{V}^{\hat{\pi}}.

Now, by definition of
\hat{\pi}
and the lift,

\hat{V}^{\star}(x,z)\;=\;r(x,\hat{\pi}(x,z),z)+\gamma\sum_{x^{\prime}}P(x^{\prime}\mid x,\hat{\pi}(x,z),z)\,\hat{\bar{V}}^{\star}(x^{\prime}),

and using
\hat{\bar{V}}^{\star}\leq\bar{V}^{\hat{\pi}}
together with the
augmented Bellman expectation equation

V^{\hat{\pi}}(x,z)=r(x,\hat{\pi}(x,z),z)+\gamma\sum_{x^{\prime}}P(x^{\prime}\mid x,\hat{\pi}(x,z),z)\bar{V}^{\hat{\pi}}(x^{\prime})
,

\hat{V}^{\star}(x,z)\;\leq\;r(x,\hat{\pi}(x,z),z)+\gamma\sum_{x^{\prime}}P(x^{\prime}\mid x,\hat{\pi}(x,z),z)\,\bar{V}^{\hat{\pi}}(x^{\prime})\;=\;V^{\hat{\pi}}(x,z),

which is (
69
). Combining with (i),

V^{\star}(x,z)-V^{\hat{\pi}}(x,z)\;\leq\;V^{\star}(x,z)-\hat{V}^{\star}(x,z)\;\leq\;\gamma\varepsilon.

(iii) PE.
Identical to (i), without the
\max_{a}
: the augmented
Bellman expectation equation gives

V^{\pi}(x,z)=r(x,\pi(x,z),z)+\gamma\sum_{x^{\prime}}P(x^{\prime}\mid x,\pi(x,z),z)\bar{V}^{\pi}(x^{\prime})
,
and the same Lipschitz argument yields

|\hat{V}^{\pi}(x,z)-V^{\pi}(x,z)|\leq\gamma\|\hat{\bar{V}}^{\pi}-\bar{V}^{\pi}\|_{\infty}\leq\gamma\varepsilon
.
∎

This completes the proof of Theorem
1
.

Appendix B
Proof of Proposition
2

(i) Contextual-MDP structure.

Let

\mathcal{Z}:=\mathcal{X}^{\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{A}},

the space of functions assigning a next state to every controlled

(x,a)
pair. A draw
Z\sim\mu
is a tuple

\bigl(Z(x,a)\bigr)_{(x,a)\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{A}}
with independent
coordinates, the
(x,a)
-th of which has law
P_{0}(\cdot\mid x,a)
.
Define the transition kernel
P
and reward
r
of the contextual MDP
by

P(x^{\prime}\mid x,a,z)\;=\;\mathbf{1}\{x^{\prime}=z(x,a)\},\qquad r(x,a,z)\;=\;r_{0}(x,a).

Both are deterministic functions of
(x,a,z)
, so the contextual-MDP
axioms of Section
2
are satisfied: at each step the agent
observes
(X_{t},Z_{t})
, selects
A_{t}
, receives reward
r(X_{t},A_{t},Z_{t})
,
and transitions to
X_{t+1}\sim P(\cdot\mid X_{t},A_{t},Z_{t})
.

(ii) Marginal consistency with the original MDP.

The lookahead pair
(P,\mu)
reproduces the original kernel
P_{0}
under
contextual averaging: for any
(x,a,x^{\prime})
,

\bar{P}(x^{\prime}\mid x,a)\;:=\;\mathbb{E}_{Z\sim\mu}[P(x^{\prime}\mid x,a,Z)]\;=\;\Pr_{Z\sim\mu}\bigl(Z(x,a)=x^{\prime}\bigr)\;=\;P_{0}(x^{\prime}\mid x,a),

where the last equality uses that the
(x,a)
-coordinate of
Z
has
marginal law
P_{0}(\cdot\mid x,a)
by definition of
\mu
. Likewise,

\mathbb{E}_{Z\sim\mu}[r(x,a,Z)]=r_{0}(x,a)
. The induced averaged value

\bar{V}^{\star}
of the contextual MDP therefore coincides with the
optimal value
V_{0}^{\star}
of the original MDP, and any guarantee on

\bar{V}^{\star}
transfers verbatim.

(iii) Information regime.

The sampling oracle for
\mu
is realized by drawing one lookahead
sample: a single call returns the full tuple

\bigl(Z(x,a)\bigr)_{(x,a)}
, one independent next-state per

(x,a)
pair, all sharing the same realization
Z
. This is exactly
one i.i.d. draw from
\mu
, so
n
lookahead calls produce

Z_{1},\dots,Z_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{iid}}}{{\sim}}\mu
as required by
Section
3
. The reward
r(x,a,z)=r_{0}(x,a)
is
known in closed form by assumption on
r_{0}
. The kernel

P(\cdot\mid x,a,z)=\delta_{z(x,a)}
is also known in closed form

once
z
is observed
: evaluating

\sum_{x^{\prime}}P(x^{\prime}\mid x,a,z)\bar{v}(x^{\prime})=\bar{v}(z(x,a))
requires only
table lookup, no further sampling. Both the offline phase and the
decision-time lift of Section
3
therefore apply
without modification.

This concludes the embedding: the lookahead problem is an instance of the

\mu
-unknown /
P
-known regime of
Section
3
, and Theorem
1

applies as a black box, yielding the rate announced in the main text.
∎

Throughout, fix

V^{\pi}_{\bm{\theta}}(x^{(k)},z_{0})=1+\gamma(p+\theta_{k}\alpha)\,\bar{V}^{\pi}_{\bm{\theta}}(x^{(k)}).

A direct computation gives

\left|V^{\pi}_{\theta_{k}=1}(x^{(k)},z_{0})-V^{\pi}_{\theta_{k}=0}(x^{(k)},z_{0})\right|\geq\frac{\gamma\alpha\beta^{2}}{4}.

With
c_{\alpha}=18\gamma
, this lower bound is at least
2\varepsilon
.

We now compute the chi-square divergence between the laws of one informative oracle call at
(x^{(k)},a_{0},z_{0})
under
\theta_{k}=0
and
\theta_{k}=1
. Such a call returns
x^{(k)}
with probability
p+\theta_{k}\alpha
and
x_{T}
otherwise. Let
\mathcal{P}_{b}
denote the one-sample law under
\theta_{k}=b
. Then

\displaystyle\chi^{2}(\mathcal{P}_{1},\mathcal{P}_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{p}+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{1-p}=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{p(1-p)}.

(72)

Since
p\geq 1/3
and

1-p=\frac{2}{3\gamma\beta},

we have

p(1-p)\geq\frac{2}{9\gamma\beta}.

Using

\alpha=\frac{32}{\gamma\beta^{2}}\varepsilon,

we obtain

\chi^{2}(\mathcal{P}_{1},\mathcal{P}_{0})\leq\frac{c_{3}\varepsilon^{2}}{\beta^{3}}

(73)

for a universal constant
c_{3}>0
.

Let

\delta_{0}:=\delta_{\rm off}+\delta_{\rm q}\leq\frac{1}{12}.

The PE guarantee implies that for every model in the family and every
query point
(x,z)
,

\mathbb{P}\!\left(\left|\widehat{\mathcal{V}}^{\pi}(x,z)-V^{\pi}(x,z)\right|>\varepsilon\right)\leq\delta_{0}.

(74)

Fix
k\in\{1,\dots,d\}
. Compare two models that differ only in
\theta_{k}
, with all other parameters fixed. Since the pointwise values at
(x^{(k)},z_{0})
are separated by at least
2\varepsilon
, the estimate
\widehat{\mathcal{V}}^{\pi}(x^{(k)},z_{0})
induces a test between
\theta_{k}=0
and
\theta_{k}=1
with total error at most
2\delta_{0}\leq 1/6
. Hence the total variation distance between the two full transcript laws is at least
5/6
.

Let
T_{k}
be the expected number of informative samples drawn from the triplet
(x^{(k)},a_{0},z_{0})
over the offline phase and the query phase for the query
(x^{(k)},z_{0})
, under the
\theta_{k}=0
model. By the chain rule for KL on adaptive experiments and by
(
73
),

\mathrm{KL}(\mathbb{P}_{\theta_{k}=0}\,\|\,\mathbb{P}_{\theta_{k}=1})\leq T_{k}\cdot\frac{c_{3}\varepsilon^{2}}{\beta^{3}}.

Pinsker’s inequality and the total-variation lower bound give

\frac{5}{6}\leq d_{\rm TV}(\mathbb{P}_{\theta_{k}=0},\mathbb{P}_{\theta_{k}=1})\leq\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{KL}(\mathbb{P}_{\theta_{k}=0}\,\|\,\mathbb{P}_{\theta_{k}=1})}.

Therefore

T_{k}\geq c_{\rm off}\frac{\beta^{3}}{\varepsilon^{2}}

for a universal constant
c_{\rm off}>0
.

Summing this lower bound over
k=1,\dots,d
gives

\sum_{k=1}^{d}T_{k}\geq c_{\rm off}\frac{d\beta^{3}}{\varepsilon^{2}}.

On the other hand, over the
d
branch queries, the offline phase can
contribute at most
n_{\rm learn}
informative branch samples in total,
and each query phase can use at most
m_{\rm query}
samples. Hence

\sum_{k=1}^{d}T_{k}\leq n_{\rm learn}+d\,m_{\rm query}.

Thus

n_{\rm learn}+d\,m_{\rm query}\geq c_{\rm off}\frac{d\beta^{3}}{\varepsilon^{2}}.

(75)

Since
d=S-3
and
S\geq 4
, we have
d\geq S/4
after adjusting the
universal constant. Also
d\leq S
, so

n_{\rm learn}+S\,m_{\rm query}\geq n_{\rm learn}+d\,m_{\rm query}\geq c_{2}\frac{S\beta^{3}}{\varepsilon^{2}}

for a universal constant
c_{2}>0
.

Query budget computed on the query state.

We now prove the lower bound on
m_{\rm query}
. By causal disjointness of the offline and query gadgets, fix
\bm{\theta}
arbitrarily and analyze the query test in isolation. The value
V^{\pi}(x_{\eta},z^{(i)})
depends only on
(\eta,i)
.

Since
x_{\eta}
transitions only to the absorbing states
x_{\beta}
and
x_{T}
, a one-step Bellman backup at
(x_{\eta},z^{(i)})
gives

V^{\pi}_{\eta}(x_{\eta},z^{(i)})=\gamma\beta\bigl(q+(-1)^{\eta}\Delta_{\rm query}\bigr).

Therefore

\left|V^{\pi}_{\eta=1}(x_{\eta},z^{(i)})-V^{\pi}_{\eta=0}(x_{\eta},z^{(i)})\right|=2\gamma\beta\Delta_{\rm query}=2\varepsilon.

At the planted triplet
(x_{\eta},a_{0},z^{(i)})
, the one-sample laws under
\eta=0
and
\eta=1
are Bernoulli laws with parameters
q+\Delta_{\rm query}
and
q-\Delta_{\rm query}
. Since
q=1/2
and
\Delta_{\rm query}\leq 1/4
, there is a universal constant
c_{4}>0
such that

\chi^{2}(\mathcal{P}_{1},\mathcal{P}_{0})\leq c_{4}\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\beta^{2}}.

(76)

At every other triplet, the conditional law is identical under
\eta=0
and
\eta=1
.

For a fixed planted index
i
, let
\mathbb{P}_{i}^{\eta}
denote the full transcript law under
\eta
. Let
M_{\rm off}^{(i)}
be the number of offline samples drawn at the planted triplet
(x_{\eta},a_{0},z^{(i)})
, and let
M_{\rm q}^{(i)}
be the number of query-time samples drawn at this same triplet when the query is
(x_{\eta},z^{(i)})
. The chain rule for KL on adaptive sample paths, together with (
76
), yields

\mathrm{KL}(\mathbb{P}_{i}^{0}\,\|\,\mathbb{P}_{i}^{1})\leq\left(\mathbb{E}_{i}^{0}[M_{\rm off}^{(i)}]+\mathbb{E}_{i}^{0}[M_{\rm q}^{(i)}]\right)\frac{c_{4}\varepsilon^{2}}{\beta^{2}}.

(77)

Since the query-time budget is pointwise bounded,

M_{\rm q}^{(i)}\leq m_{\rm query}\quad\text{almost surely}.

The planted index is chosen among
M
possible contexts. The offline phase does not know in advance which context is planted, and

\sum_{j=1}^{M}M_{\rm off}^{(j)}\leq n_{\rm learn}.

Thus, averaging over
i\sim\mathrm{Unif}\{1,\dots,M\}
,

\mathbb{E}_{i}\mathbb{E}_{i}^{0}[M_{\rm off}^{(i)}]\leq\frac{n_{\rm learn}}{M}\leq\frac{\bar{n}}{M}.

Choose
c_{Z}:=18c_{4}
. Since

M=\left\lceil 18c_{4}\bar{n}\varepsilon^{2}/\beta^{2}\right\rceil+1,

we obtain

\mathbb{E}_{i}\mathbb{E}_{i}^{0}[M_{\rm off}^{(i)}]\,\frac{c_{4}\varepsilon^{2}}{\beta^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{18}.

(78)

Combining (
77
) and (
78
), averaging over
i
, and applying Pinsker’s inequality gives

\mathbb{E}_{i}\,d_{\rm TV}(\mathbb{P}_{i}^{0},\mathbb{P}_{i}^{1})\leq\sqrt{\frac{1}{36}+\frac{1}{2}\,m_{\rm query}\,\frac{c_{4}\varepsilon^{2}}{\beta^{2}}}.

On the other hand, for every
i
, the two values
V^{\pi}_{\eta=0}(x_{\eta},z^{(i)})
and
V^{\pi}_{\eta=1}(x_{\eta},z^{(i)})
are separated by
2\varepsilon
. Therefore the PE guarantee at the query point
(x_{\eta},z^{(i)})
induces a test between
\eta=0
and
\eta=1
with total error at most

2\delta_{0}\leq 1/6
. Hence

d_{\rm TV}(\mathbb{P}_{i}^{0},\mathbb{P}_{i}^{1})\geq 1-2\delta_{0}\geq\frac{5}{6}\qquad\text{for every }i.

Averaging over
i
gives

\frac{5}{6}\leq\sqrt{\frac{1}{36}+\frac{1}{2}\,m_{\rm query}\,\frac{c_{4}\varepsilon^{2}}{\beta^{2}}}.

Squaring and rearranging yields

m_{\rm query}\geq\frac{4}{3c_{4}}\frac{\beta^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}.

Thus there is a universal constant
c_{1}>0
such that

m_{\rm query}\geq c_{1}\frac{\beta^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}.

Combining this query lower bound with (
75
) proves

m_{\rm query}\geq c_{1}\frac{\beta^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}},\qquad n_{\rm learn}+S\,m_{\rm query}\geq c_{2}\frac{S\beta^{3}}{\varepsilon^{2}}.

Finally, if
m_{\rm query}=O(\beta^{2}/\varepsilon^{2})
, the second inequality implies, after adjusting constants,

n_{\rm learn}=\Omega\!\left(\frac{S\beta^{3}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right).

This completes the proof.

C.2
Upper bound

This section proves Theorem
6
: in the regime where both

\mu
and
P
are accessed only through generative oracles, we construct a
decision-time mechanism for PE with complexity

(n_{\rm learn},m_{\rm query})=\left(\widetilde{O}\!\left(\frac{|\mathcal{X}|\beta^{3}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right),\widetilde{O}\!\left(\frac{\beta^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\right).

(79)

The policy
\pi:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Z}\to\mathcal{A}
is fixed and supplied through an
oracle, and the reward
r
is known.

The offline phase produces an
\ell_{\infty}
-accurate estimate

\hat{\bar{V}}^{\pi}
of the averaged value
\bar{V}^{\pi}
, via the
variance-reduced halving scheme detailed below. Since
P
is unknown, each
sampled context used in a Bellman backup must be paired with transition
samples: for every sampled
Z
and every
x\in\mathcal{X}
, the algorithm draws

X^{\prime}\sim P(\cdot\mid x,\pi(x,Z),Z)
and uses the unbiased sampled backup

\widehat{g}^{\pi}_{x,\bar{v}}(Z):=r(x,\pi(x,Z),Z)+\gamma\bar{v}(X^{\prime}).

(80)

This is the source of the extra factor
|\mathcal{X}|
in the offline transition
budget.

At query time, given a realized pair
(x,z)
, the mechanism estimates only
the remaining one-step expectation under the unknown kernel. It draws

X_{1},\dots,X_{m_{\rm query}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{iid}}}{{\sim}}P(\cdot\mid x,\pi(x,z),z)

(81)

and returns

\widehat{\mathcal{V}}^{\pi}(x,z):=r(x,\pi(x,z),z)+\frac{\gamma}{m_{\rm query}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m_{\rm query}}\hat{\bar{V}}^{\pi}(X_{\ell}).

(82)

Hoeffding’s inequality gives the query cost

m_{\rm query}=\widetilde{O}(\beta^{2}/\varepsilon^{2})
, while the offline guarantee
on
\hat{\bar{V}}^{\pi}
gives the desired pointwise PE guarantee.

Notation

Throughout,

\beta:=(1-\gamma)^{-1}.

(83)

Under the policy
\pi
, starting from
X_{0}=x
, the process evolves as

Z_{t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{iid}}}{{\sim}}\mu,\qquad A_{t}=\pi(X_{t},Z_{t}),\qquad X_{t+1}\sim P(\cdot\mid X_{t},A_{t},Z_{t}).

(84)

For any
\bar{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{X}}
, recall the fixed-policy one-step expression

g^{\pi}_{x,\bar{v}}(z):=r(x,\pi(x,z),z)+\gamma\sum_{x^{\prime}}P(x^{\prime}\mid x,\pi(x,z),z)\bar{v}(x^{\prime}).

(85)

We use the shorthand

g_{x}^{\pi}(z):=g^{\pi}_{x,\bar{V}^{\pi}}(z).

(86)

Define

\displaystyle v^{\pi}(x)

\displaystyle:=\Var_{Z\sim\mu}\bigl[g_{x}^{\pi}(Z)\bigr],

(87)

\displaystyle\sigma^{\pi}(x)

\displaystyle:=\gamma^{2}\mathbb{E}_{Z\sim\mu}\left[\Var_{X^{\prime}\sim P(\cdot\mid x,\pi(x,Z),Z)}\bigl(\bar{V}^{\pi}(X^{\prime})\bigr)\right],

(88)

\displaystyle\bar{P}^{\pi}(x^{\prime}\mid x)

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}_{Z\sim\mu}\left[P(x^{\prime}\mid x,\pi(x,Z),Z)\right].

(89)

The averaged value
\bar{V}^{\pi}
is the unique fixed point of

(T^{\pi}\bar{v})(x):=\mathbb{E}_{Z\sim\mu}\left[r(x,\pi(x,Z),Z)+\gamma\sum_{x^{\prime}}P(x^{\prime}\mid x,\pi(x,Z),Z)\bar{v}(x^{\prime})\right].

(90)

Let

G(x):=\sum_{t\geq 0}\gamma^{t}r(X_{t},A_{t},Z_{t}).

(91)

Then
G(x)\in[0,\beta]
almost surely, and hence

\Var_{x}(G(x))\leq\beta^{2}.

(92)

For
t\geq 0
, define

\mathcal{F}_{t}:=\sigma(X_{0},Z_{0},A_{0},\ldots,X_{t-1},Z_{t-1},A_{t-1},X_{t}),\qquad\mathcal{G}_{t}:=\sigma(\mathcal{F}_{t},Z_{t}).

(93)

Thus
\mathcal{F}_{t}\subset\mathcal{G}_{t}\subset\mathcal{F}_{t+1}
.

Martingale variance bound.

The following lemma is the only modification of the martingale variance bound
from the known-dynamics case. The context contribution is the same as in
Lemma
12
; the new term
\sigma^{\pi}
appears because,
after observing
Z_{t}
, the transition
X_{t+1}
is still sampled from the
unknown kernel.

Lemma 19

(Joint contextual–transitional variance bound)
.

For every context-aware policy
\pi
,

\sum_{t\geq 0}\gamma^{2t}(\bar{P}^{\pi})^{t}\bigl(v^{\pi}+\sigma^{\pi}\bigr)\preceq\beta^{2}\mathbf{1}.

(94)

Proof.

Fix
x\in\mathcal{X}
and consider the process started at
X_{0}=x
. We use the
same martingale decomposition as in the proof of
Lemma
12
. Namely, with

M_{t}:=\mathbb{E}_{x}[G(x)\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}],

(95)

(M_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a bounded martingale converging to
G(x)
, and

\Var_{x}(G(x))=\sum_{t\geq 0}\mathbb{E}_{x}[(M_{t+1}-M_{t})^{2}].

(96)

Insert the intermediate
\sigma
-field

\mathcal{G}_{t}=\sigma(\mathcal{F}_{t},Z_{t})
and write

M_{t+1}-M_{t}=D_{t}^{Z}+D_{t}^{X},

(97)

where

D_{t}^{Z}:=\mathbb{E}_{x}[G(x)\mid\mathcal{G}_{t}]-\mathbb{E}_{x}[G(x)\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}],\qquad D_{t}^{X}:=\mathbb{E}_{x}[G(x)\mid\mathcal{F}_{t+1}]-\mathbb{E}_{x}[G(x)\mid\mathcal{G}_{t}].

(98)

As in Lemma
12
, the two increments are orthogonal
conditionally on
\mathcal{F}_{t}
, so

\mathbb{E}_{x}[(M_{t+1}-M_{t})^{2}]=\mathbb{E}_{x}[(D_{t}^{Z})^{2}]+\mathbb{E}_{x}[(D_{t}^{X})^{2}].

(99)

The context increment is exactly the one treated in
Lemma
12
. It gives

D_{t}^{Z}=\gamma^{t}\bigl(g_{X_{t}}^{\pi}(Z_{t})-\bar{V}^{\pi}(X_{t})\bigr),

(100)

and therefore

\mathbb{E}_{x}[(D_{t}^{Z})^{2}]=\gamma^{2t}\mathbb{E}_{x}[v^{\pi}(X_{t})].

(101)

It remains to identify the additional contribution induced by the transition
draw. Conditional on
\mathcal{G}_{t}
, the variables
X_{t}
,
Z_{t}
, and

A_{t}=\pi(X_{t},Z_{t})
are fixed, and the only remaining randomness before

\mathcal{F}_{t+1}
is

X_{t+1}\sim P(\cdot\mid X_{t},A_{t},Z_{t}).

(102)

Thus

D_{t}^{X}=\gamma^{t+1}\left(\bar{V}^{\pi}(X_{t+1})-\sum_{x^{\prime}}P(x^{\prime}\mid X_{t},A_{t},Z_{t})\bar{V}^{\pi}(x^{\prime})\right).

(103)

Taking conditional variance given
\mathcal{G}_{t}
, then averaging, yields

\mathbb{E}_{x}[(D_{t}^{X})^{2}]=\gamma^{2t}\mathbb{E}_{x}[\sigma^{\pi}(X_{t})].

(104)

Here the factor
\gamma^{2t}
appears because
\sigma^{\pi}
already
contains the additional factor
\gamma^{2}
.

Combining the two contributions gives

\sum_{t\geq 0}\gamma^{2t}\mathbb{E}_{x}[v^{\pi}(X_{t})+\sigma^{\pi}(X_{t})]\leq\Var_{x}(G(x))\leq\beta^{2}.

(105)

Finally, as in Lemma
12
, the marginal controlled
process
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is Markov on
\mathcal{X}
with transition kernel

\bar{P}^{\pi}
. Hence

\mathbb{E}_{x}[v^{\pi}(X_{t})+\sigma^{\pi}(X_{t})]=(\bar{P}^{\pi})^{t}(v^{\pi}+\sigma^{\pi})(x),

(106)

and the desired coordinate-wise bound follows.
∎

Corollary 3

(Complexity functional for policy evaluation)
.

For every context-aware policy
\pi
,

\left\|(I-\gamma\bar{P}^{\pi})^{-1}\sqrt{v^{\pi}+\sigma^{\pi}}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{2}\,\beta^{3/2}.

(107)

Proof.

This is the same resolvent step as in Corollary
2
.
Applying Lemma C.3 of
25

with
K=\bar{P}^{\pi}
and
w=v^{\pi}+\sigma^{\pi}
, and using
Lemma
19
, gives

\left\|(I-\gamma\bar{P}^{\pi})^{-1}\sqrt{v^{\pi}+\sigma^{\pi}}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{\frac{1+\gamma}{1-\gamma}}\,\left\|(I-\gamma^{2}\bar{P}^{\pi})^{-1}(v^{\pi}+\sigma^{\pi})\right\|_{\infty}^{1/2}\leq\sqrt{2}\,\beta^{3/2}.

(108)

∎

The new difficulty, compared with the known-dynamics case, is that the
variance of one sampled backup depends on the current value vector

\bar{v}
through two sources of randomness: the sampled context and the
sampled transition. In the halving analysis, the anchor is built at the
current input
\bar{v}^{(0)}
, whereas the variance propagation bound above
is naturally stated at the true value
\bar{V}^{\pi}
. We therefore need a
stability estimate showing that the sampled-backup variance at

\bar{v}^{(0)}
remains controlled whenever
\bar{v}^{(0)}
is close to

\bar{V}^{\pi}
.

For
\bar{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{X}}
, define

v^{\pi}_{\bar{v}}(x):=\Var_{Z\sim\mu}\bigl(g^{\pi}_{x,\bar{v}}(Z)\bigr),\qquad\sigma^{\pi}_{\bar{v}}(x):=\gamma^{2}\mathbb{E}_{Z\sim\mu}\left[\Var_{X^{\prime}\sim P(\cdot\mid x,\pi(x,Z),Z)}(\bar{v}(X^{\prime}))\right].

(109)

The quantity
v^{\pi}_{\bar{v}}(x)+\sigma^{\pi}_{\bar{v}}(x)
is the variance of
one sampled Bellman backup at state
x
when the continuation value is

\bar{v}
.

Lemma 20

(Variance perturbation under a fixed action map)
.

Let
\bar{v}_{1},\bar{v}_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{X}}
satisfy

\|\bar{v}_{1}-\bar{v}_{2}\|_{\infty}\leq u.

(110)

Then, coordinate-wise on
\mathcal{X}
,

\left|(v^{\pi}_{\bar{v}_{1}}+\sigma^{\pi}_{\bar{v}_{1}})(x)-(v^{\pi}_{\bar{v}_{2}}+\sigma^{\pi}_{\bar{v}_{2}})(x)\right|\leq 2\gamma^{2}u^{2}+2\sqrt{2}\,\gamma u\sqrt{(v^{\pi}_{\bar{v}_{2}}+\sigma^{\pi}_{\bar{v}_{2}})(x)}.

(111)

Proof.

Fix
x
. Let

f(z):=g^{\pi}_{x,\bar{v}_{1}}(z),\qquad h(z):=g^{\pi}_{x,\bar{v}_{2}}(z).

(112)

Since the action map is fixed,

|f(z)-h(z)|\leq\gamma u\qquad\text{for every }z.

(113)

For the contextual part,

\Var_{\mu}(f)-\Var_{\mu}(h)=\Var_{\mu}(f-h)+2\Cov_{\mu}(h,f-h).

(114)

By Cauchy–Schwarz,

|\Cov_{\mu}(h,f-h)|\leq\sqrt{\Var_{\mu}(h)\Var_{\mu}(f-h)}\leq\gamma u\sqrt{v^{\pi}_{\bar{v}_{2}}(x)}.

(115)

Moreover,
\Var_{\mu}(f-h)\leq\gamma^{2}u^{2}
. Hence

|v^{\pi}_{\bar{v}_{1}}(x)-v^{\pi}_{\bar{v}_{2}}(x)|\leq\gamma^{2}u^{2}+2\gamma u\sqrt{v^{\pi}_{\bar{v}_{2}}(x)}.

(116)

For the transition part, let
D:=\bar{v}_{1}-\bar{v}_{2}
. Pointwise in
z
,

\left|\Var_{X^{\prime}}(\bar{v}_{1}(X^{\prime}))-\Var_{X^{\prime}}(\bar{v}_{2}(X^{\prime}))\right|\leq u^{2}+2u\sqrt{\Var_{X^{\prime}}(\bar{v}_{2}(X^{\prime}))}.

(117)

Multiplying by
\gamma^{2}
, integrating over
Z\sim\mu
, and using
Jensen’s inequality gives

|\sigma^{\pi}_{\bar{v}_{1}}(x)-\sigma^{\pi}_{\bar{v}_{2}}(x)|\leq\gamma^{2}u^{2}+2\gamma u\sqrt{\sigma^{\pi}_{\bar{v}_{2}}(x)}.

(118)

Summing the contextual and transition bounds and using

\sqrt{a}+\sqrt{b}\leq\sqrt{2(a+b)}
proves the result.
∎

We now record the basic fact that replaces the exact Bellman backup used in
the known-dynamics proof. In the known-
P
case, a sampled context
Z
was
enough to compute
g^{\pi}_{x,\bar{v}}(Z)
. Here the algorithm uses one
transition sample to obtain an unbiased estimate of this quantity, and the
variance decomposes exactly into the two terms controlled above.

Lemma 21

(Sampled fixed-policy backup)
.

Fix
\bar{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{X}}
and
x\in\mathcal{X}
. Let
Z\sim\mu
, and,
conditionally on
Z
, draw

X^{x,Z}_{1}\sim P(\cdot\mid x,\pi(x,Z),Z).

(119)

Define

\hat{Y}_{\bar{v}}(x):=r(x,\pi(x,Z),Z)+\gamma\bar{v}(X^{x,Z}_{1}).

(120)

Then

\mathbb{E}[\hat{Y}_{\bar{v}}(x)]=(T^{\pi}\bar{v})(x),

(121)

and

\Var(\hat{Y}_{\bar{v}}(x))=v^{\pi}_{\bar{v}}(x)+\sigma^{\pi}_{\bar{v}}(x).

(122)

Proof.

Conditioning on
Z
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\hat{Y}_{\bar{v}}(x)\mid Z]

\displaystyle=r(x,\pi(x,Z),Z)+\gamma\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bar{v}(X^{x,Z}_{1})\mid Z\right]

\displaystyle=r(x,\pi(x,Z),Z)+\gamma\sum_{x^{\prime}}P(x^{\prime}\mid x,\pi(x,Z),Z)\bar{v}(x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=g^{\pi}_{x,\bar{v}}(Z).

(123)

Taking expectation over
Z\sim\mu
yields

\mathbb{E}[\hat{Y}_{\bar{v}}(x)]=\mathbb{E}_{Z\sim\mu}[g^{\pi}_{x,\bar{v}}(Z)]=(T^{\pi}\bar{v})(x).

(124)

For the variance identity, use the law of total variance:

\Var(\hat{Y}_{\bar{v}}(x))=\Var_{Z}\!\left(\mathbb{E}[\hat{Y}_{\bar{v}}(x)\mid Z]\right)+\mathbb{E}_{Z}\!\left[\Var(\hat{Y}_{\bar{v}}(x)\mid Z)\right].

(125)

The first term is

\Var_{Z}\!\left(\mathbb{E}[\hat{Y}_{\bar{v}}(x)\mid Z]\right)=\Var_{Z\sim\mu}\bigl(g^{\pi}_{x,\bar{v}}(Z)\bigr)=v^{\pi}_{\bar{v}}(x).

(126)

For the second term, conditionally on
Z
, only the transition draw

X^{x,Z}_{1}
is random, and the reward term is fixed. Hence

\Var(\hat{Y}_{\bar{v}}(x)\mid Z)=\gamma^{2}\Var_{X^{\prime}\sim P(\cdot\mid x,\pi(x,Z),Z)}\bigl(\bar{v}(X^{\prime})\bigr).

(127)

Averaging over
Z\sim\mu
gives

\mathbb{E}_{Z}[\Var(\hat{Y}_{\bar{v}}(x)\mid Z)]=\sigma^{\pi}_{\bar{v}}(x).

(128)

Combining the two terms proves the result.
∎

The halving scheme is the same as in the known-dynamics proof, except that
exact backups are replaced by the sampled backups of
Lemma
21
. There is one additional technical point:
since the initial radius of the meta-algorithm is
u_{0}=\beta
, the proof
must close uniformly for radii
u\in(0,\beta]
. The perturbation lemma gives
a variance bound at radius
u
with a factor
1\vee u
; hence the anchor
batch must contain the same factor.

For a radius
u\in(0,\beta]
, set

\rho(u):=1\vee u.

(129)

The anchor batch size is chosen as

n_{1}=\left\lceil c_{2}\,\rho(u)\,\beta^{3}u^{-2}L\right\rceil,

(130)

where
c_{2}
is an absolute numerical constant. This distinction is needed
for the present proof: without the factor
\rho(u)
, the variance term in
the one-call halving argument would scale as
u\sqrt{1\vee u}
rather than
as
u
, and the halving step would not close uniformly for large radii.

Equivalently,

n_{1}=\widetilde{\Theta}(\beta^{3}/u)\quad\text{when }u>1,\qquad n_{1}=\widetilde{\Theta}(\beta^{3}/u^{2})\quad\text{when }u\leq 1.

(131)

Thus the expensive
u^{-2}
scaling is paid only once the target radius is
below
1
, while the coarse-radius calls cost only

\widetilde{O}(\beta^{3}/u)
.

All logarithmic factors below are absorbed in

L:=\log(64|\mathcal{X}|R\,n_{1}\,n_{2}/\delta),

(132)

with constants chosen large enough to make rounding effects immaterial.
