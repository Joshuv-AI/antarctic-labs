Scattering Amplitudes and Resonant Processes in QED with Chiral Chemical Potential and Chiral Magnetic Conductivity
Jonathan D. Kroth, Kirill Tuchin

Abstract
The QED scattering amplitude in a chiral medium characterized by a constant chiral chemical potential μ 5 \mu_{5} and chiral magnetic conductivity b 0 b_{0} is analyzed. We show the emergence of the resonant behavior in 1 → 2 1\to 2 , 2 → 2 2\to 2 , and 2 → 3 2\to 3 processes. We compute the rates of paradigm 1 → 2 1\to 2 processes that determine the widths of quasi-stationary fermion and photon states in the medium. We elucidate the origin of these resonances, the conditions of their emergence, and the physical principles of their regularization.



I
Introduction

A medium containing chiral fermions may be chiraly imbalanced. The chiral imbalance can be expressed in terms of the
P
and
CP
-odd terms in the Lagrangian. In quark-gluon plasma, the net chirality is reflected in the chiral chemical potential
\mu_{5}
induced by the sphaleron transitions. In Weyl semimetals, the chirality is manifested as a displacement
{\bm{\Delta}}
of the Weyl nodes in momentum space. Many phenomenological models describe the chirality by introducing a term into Lagrangians that couples the four vector
(\mu_{5},{\bm{\Delta}})
to the axial vector current
j^{\mu 5}=\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{5}\psi
.

In the presence of a chiral imbalance, two new currents are induced in the medium due to the chiral anomaly
Adler 1969
;
Bell and Jackiw 1969
. These currents are the chiral magnetic current
{\bm{j}}=b_{0}{\bm{B}}

Kharzeev 2006
;
Kharzeev and Zhitnitsky 2007
;
Fukushima et al. 2008
;
Kharzeev 2010
;
Kharzeev et al. 2008
and the anomalous Hall current
{\bm{j}}={\bm{b}}\times{\bm{E}}
, where
b_{0}=c_{A}\mu_{5}
is called the chiral magnetic conductivity and
{\bm{b}}=c_{A}{\bm{\Delta}}/2
. A medium with the
P
and
CP
-odd responses defined by the chiral magnetic and anomalous Hall currents can be described employing constitutive relations. These relations are linear when
\mu_{5}
and
{\bm{\Delta}}
are constant, and they become isotropic when
{\bm{\Delta}}
vanishes. From the field-theoretic perspective, the anomalous currents can be represented as the Chern-Simons term
\tilde{F}F
in the Lagrangian, where
\tilde{F}
is the dual electromagnetic field tensor.

To obtain an effective Lagrangian for electrodynamics with chiral imbalance, perform a chiral transformation
\psi\to e^{i\theta\gamma^{5}}\psi
of the fermionic functional integral. This transformation produces the following new terms in the Lagrangian
Fujikawa and Suzuki 2004
:

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{L}=\theta(x)\left\{\partial_{\mu}j^{\mu 5}-c_{A}\left(-\frac{1}{4}\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu}\right)\right\}\,,

(1)

where

\displaystyle c_{A}=\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\sum_{a}e_{a}^{2}

(2)

is the chiral anomaly coefficient, with
e_{a}
being the electric charge of fermion
a
and sum running over all fermion species. The expression in the curly brackets in (
1
) is the total derivative, and the Lagrangian (
1
) can be cast in the following form:

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{L}=\partial_{\mu}\theta\left\{-j^{\mu 5}+\frac{c_{A}}{2}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}A_{\nu}\partial_{\lambda}A_{\rho}\right\}\,.

(3)

Therefore, the equations of motion depend not on
\theta
directly, but on its derivative
\partial_{\mu}\theta
. It is a simple exercise to verify that
b_{\mu}=c_{A}\partial_{\mu}\theta
, with
b_{0}
and
{\bm{b}}
being its temporary and spacial components respectively.

With the addition of
\Delta\mathcal{L}
to the Lagrangian of QED, we obtain an effective theory that is extensively used in applications.
The chiral magnetic effect, its theory, and its applications are discussed in
Babaev et al. 2023
. A review focusing on the phenomenology of relativistic heavy-ion collisions is presented in
Kharzeev et al. 2016
;
Kharzeev et al. 2024
, a review on topological materials is presented in
Sekine and Nomura 2021
;
Ong and Liang 2021
while reference
Sikivie 2021
delves into axion phenomenology.

The goal of this article is to explore the role of the chiral imbalance played in scattering processes using the effective theory presented above. In particular, we argue that scattering amplitudes often exhibit resonant behavior in certain chiral modes. A typical example is the chiral (or vacuum) Cherenkov radiation whose rate is characteristically proportional to the delta function of the emitted transverse momentum. We will elucidate the origin of these resonances, the conditions of their emergence and their regularization procedure.

Most applications we have in mind, pertain to high energy nuclear and particle physics where it is usually assumed that chirality distribution is uniform, i.e.
{\bm{\Delta}}=0
. We adopt this approximation as it allows us to present our arguments in the clearest form. However, all conclusions can be readily generalized to any
{\bm{\Delta}}
. Furthermore, we work in the high energy approximation for the same reason.

The paper is structured as follows. Sec.
II
and Sec.
III
review the solutions to the Maxwell equations in a medium with a chiral magnetic current and the Dirac equation at finite chiral chemical potential. In each case, we derive the chiral plane waves and propagators. The detailed derivations are presented in Appendices
A
and
B
. Sec.
IV
deals with the chiral Cherenkov radiation and its cross-channel, pair production. We compute the production rates, show the emergence of the resonant behavior, and study its dependence on
\mu_{5}
and
b_{0}
. Sec.
V
investigates the resonances in
2\to 2
scattering, and section Sec.
VI
does the same for bremsstrahlung. A summary is presented in Sec.
VII
.

We use natural units
c=\hbar=1
and convention of
Peskin and Schroeder 1995
for Dirac matrices.

II
Solution to Maxwell equations in the presence of chiral magnetic current

The chiral magnetic current is a component of the medium’s linear response to an external electromagnetic field. Qiu, Cao and Huang incorporate it into the constitutive relationship between the displacement and electric fields
Qiu et al. 2017
. The primary concern in this section is the electromagnetic field in a medium exhibiting such anomalous constitutive relationship. We ignore all medium effects and sources apart from the chiral magnetic current.

II.1
Maxwell equations with chiral magnetic current

Maxwell’s equations, obtained from (
3
) read:

\displaystyle{\bm{\nabla}}\times{\bm{B}}=\dot{{\bm{E}}}+b_{0}{\bm{B}}\,,

(4a)

\displaystyle{\bm{\nabla}}\cdot{\bm{E}}=0\,,

(4b)

\displaystyle{\bm{\nabla}}\times{\bm{E}}=-\dot{{\bm{B}}}\,,

(4c)

\displaystyle{\bm{\nabla}}\cdot{\bm{B}}=0\,.

(4d)

In the radiation gauge
A^{0}=0
,
{\bm{\nabla}}\cdot A=0
(
4a
) yields the modified wave equation for the vector potential:

\displaystyle-\ddot{{\bm{A}}}+\nabla^{2}{\bm{A}}+b_{0}{\bm{\nabla}}\times{\bm{A}}=0\,.

(5)

The positive energy solutions of (
5
) have the form

\displaystyle{\bm{A}}_{{\bm{p}},\lambda}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\omega_{{\bm{p}},\lambda}}}{\bm{\epsilon}}_{{\bm{p}},\lambda}e^{-ip\cdot x}\,,

(6)

where
p^{\mu}=(\omega_{{\bm{p}},\lambda},{\bm{p}})
, and
{\bm{\epsilon}}_{{\bm{p}},\lambda}
is the polarization vector corresponding to the polarization
\lambda
. Substituting (
6
) into (
5
) we readily find

\displaystyle\left(\omega_{{\bm{p}},\lambda}^{2}-{\bm{p}}^{2}\right){\bm{\epsilon}}_{{\bm{p}},\lambda}+b_{0}i{\bm{p}}\times{\bm{\epsilon}}_{{\bm{p}},\lambda}=0\,.

(7)

It order to diagonalize this equation, we must choose the polarization vectors to represent the right and left circular polarizations corresponding to
\lambda=\pm 1
. This is because such vectors obey the identity:

\displaystyle i\hat{{\bm{p}}}\times{\bm{\epsilon}}_{{\bm{p}},\lambda}=\lambda{\bm{\epsilon}}_{{\bm{p}},\lambda}\,.

(8)

Using (
8
) in (
7
) yields the dispersion relation:

\displaystyle\omega_{{\bm{p}},\lambda}=\sqrt{{\bm{p}}^{2}-\lambda b_{0}|{\bm{p}}|}\,.

(9)

The negative energy solutions read:

\displaystyle{\bm{A}}^{\prime}_{{\bm{p}},\lambda}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\omega^{\prime}_{{\bm{p}},\lambda}}}{\bm{\epsilon}}^{\prime}_{{\bm{p}},\lambda}e^{ip\cdot x}\,,

(10)

The dispersion of the negative energy solutions is found by plugging (
10
) into (
5
), which yields

\displaystyle\left({\omega^{\prime}}^{2}_{{\bm{p}},\lambda}-{\bm{p}}^{2}\right){\bm{\epsilon}}^{\prime}_{{\bm{p}},\lambda}-b_{0}i{\bm{p}}\times{\bm{\epsilon}}^{\prime}_{{\bm{p}},\lambda}=0\,.

(11)

Choosing
{\bm{\epsilon}}^{\prime}_{{\bm{p}},\lambda}={\bm{\epsilon}}^{*}_{{\bm{p}},\lambda}
and using the complex conjugate of (
8
) we find that
\omega^{\prime}_{{\bm{p}},\lambda}=\omega_{{\bm{p}},\lambda}
. As a result,

\displaystyle{\bm{A}}^{\prime}_{{\bm{p}},\lambda}(x)={\bm{A}}^{*}_{{\bm{p}},\lambda}(x)\,.

(12)

Therefore, the total vector potential, which is the sum of (
6
) and (
10
) is real, as required.

The quantized electromagnetic field reads:

\displaystyle{\bm{A}}(x)=\sum_{\lambda}\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\omega_{{\bm{p}},\lambda}}}\left[{\bm{\epsilon}}_{{\bm{p}},\lambda}e^{-ip\cdot x}a_{{\bm{p}},\lambda}+{\bm{\epsilon}}^{*}_{{\bm{p}},\lambda}e^{ip\cdot x}a^{\dagger}_{{\bm{p}},\lambda}\right]\,,

(13)

with the commutation relations given by

\displaystyle[a_{{\bm{p}},\lambda},a^{\dagger}_{{\bm{p}}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}]=(2\pi)^{3}\delta({\bm{p}}-{\bm{p}}^{\prime})\delta_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}\,.

(14)

The energy and the Hamiltonian of electromagnetic field is discussed in Appendix
A
. The quantization of the Maxwell theory with the Chern-Simons term was discussed in
Colladay and Kostelecky 1998
;
Adam and Klinkhamer 2001
.

The photon’s Feynman propagator in a covariant gauge for an arbitrary
b_{\mu}
is given by
Carroll et al. 1990
;
Lehnert and Potting 2004a
:

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{F}^{\mu\nu}(k)=\frac{-ik^{2}g^{\mu\nu}+\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}b_{\rho}k_{\sigma}-ib^{\mu}b^{\nu}}{k^{4}+k^{2}b^{2}-(b\cdot k)^{2}+i\epsilon}\,.

(15)

In Appendix
A
we present a detailed derivation of the photon propagator in the Coulomb gauge, starting from the corresponding correlation function.

Let us now examine the poles of the photon propagator (
15
). At
{\bm{b}}=0
, the propagator has poles at

\displaystyle(k^{0})^{2}={\bm{k}}^{2}+\lambda b_{0}|{\bm{k}}|\,.

(16)

In particular, in static limit
k^{0}=0
, besides the familiar Coulomb pole at
|{\bm{k}}|=0
, there is a pole at
|{\bm{k}}|=b_{0}
indicating the instability of the magnetic field in the chiral matter
Carroll et al. 1990
;
Joyce and Shaposhnikov 1997
;
Adam and Klinkhamer 2001
. This instability is not the subject of this study, although it is related to the resonances we discuss in the subsequent sections. We elaborate on this connection in Sec.
VII
.

At
b_{0}=0
, the denominator of (
15
) vanishes when

\displaystyle{\bm{k}}^{4}+{\bm{k}}^{2}{\bm{b}}^{2}-({\bm{b}}\cdot{\bm{k}})^{2}=0\,.

(17)

This implies the presence of only one pole at
|{\bm{k}}|=0
, in contrast to the previous case. Consequently, the magnetic field is stable in this situation. This is further discussed in Sec.
VII
.

III
Solution to Dirac equation at finite chiral chemical potential

The Dirac equation at finite
\mu_{5}
reads:

\displaystyle(i\not{\partial}-\mu_{5}\gamma^{5}\gamma^{0}-m)\psi=0\,.

(18)

Its solutions were obtained and discussed in
Colladay and Kostelecky 1997
;
Kostelecky and Pickering 2003
;
Kharzeev et al. 2008
;
Sukhachov et al. 2018
;
Sheng et al. 2018
. More general cases involving finite

were explored in

;
Kroth and Tuchin 2026
.

The solutions of (
18
) are derived in Appendix
B
. The positive and negative energy spinors read:

\displaystyle u_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\sqrt{E_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}-\sigma|{\bm{p}}|+\mu_{5}}\xi_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}\\
\sqrt{E_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}+\sigma|{\bm{p}}|-\mu_{5}}\xi_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}\end{array}\right)\,,

with the dispersion

\displaystyle E_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}=\sqrt{(\sigma|{\bm{p}}|-\mu_{5})^{2}+m^{2}}\,,

(21)

and

\displaystyle v_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\sqrt{E^{\prime}_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}-\sigma|{\bm{p}}|-\mu_{5}}\xi_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}\\
-\sqrt{E^{\prime}_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}+\sigma|{\bm{p}}|+\mu_{5}}\xi_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}\end{array}\right)\,,

with the dispersion
E^{\prime}_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}=E_{{\bm{p}},-\sigma}
respectively.

Employing the identities

\displaystyle\left(E_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}+m\mp|{\bm{p}}|\sigma\pm\mu_{5}\right)^{2}=2(E_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}+m)\left(E_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}\mp|{\bm{p}}|\sigma\pm\mu_{5}\right)\,,

(24)

(
III
) can be cast in a different form:

\displaystyle u_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2(E_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}+m)}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\left(E_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}-\sigma|{\bm{p}}|+m+\mu_{5}\right)\xi_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}\\
\left(E_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}+\sigma|{\bm{p}}|+m-\mu_{5}\right)\xi_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}\end{array}\right)\,.

Similarly, the anti-particle spinors can be represented as:

\displaystyle v_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2(E^{\prime}_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}+m)}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\left(E^{\prime}_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}-\sigma|{\bm{p}}|+m-\mu_{5}\right)\xi_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}\\
-\left(E^{\prime}_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}+\sigma|{\bm{p}}|+m+\mu_{5}\right)\xi_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}\end{array}\right)\,.

The spinors in the form (
III
) and (
III
) are convenient for the high-energy expansion.

A derivation of the fermion propagator is presented in Appendix
B
. In momentum space it reads

\displaystyle\tilde{G}(p)

\displaystyle=\frac{i\left(\not{p}-\gamma^{5}\gamma^{0}\mu_{5}+m\right)\left(p^{2}-\mu_{5}^{2}-m^{2}-2\mu_{5}{\bm{\Sigma}}\cdot{\bm{p}}\right)}{\left(p^{2}-\mu_{5}^{2}-m^{2}\right)^{2}-4\mu_{5}^{2}{\bm{p}}^{2}}\,.

(29)

A more general expression can be found in
Miransky and Shovkovy 2015
.

\tilde{G}(p)
has four simple poles at
p^{0}=\pm E_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}
, where
E_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}
are given by (
21
). Adding the
+i\epsilon
prescription to the denominator transforms (
29
) into the Feynman propagator.

At high energies, where
|{\bm{p}}|\gg\mu_{5},m
, the dispersion relation (
21
) reads

\displaystyle E_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}\approx|{\bm{p}}|-\sigma\mu_{5}\,.

(30)

Substituting this into (
III
) and expanding yields the high-energy approximation for the particle spinor:

\displaystyle u_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}\approx\frac{1}{\sqrt{2p_{z}}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\left[|{\bm{p}}|(1-\sigma)+\frac{1}{2}\mu_{5}(1-\sigma)+\frac{1}{2}m(1+\sigma)\right]\xi_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}\\[2.84526pt]
\left[|{\bm{p}}|(1+\sigma)-\frac{1}{2}\mu_{5}(1+\sigma)+\frac{1}{2}m(1-\sigma)\right]\xi_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}\end{array}\right)\,.

This formula can be directly derived from (
III
), but it requires keeping the next term, proportional to
m^{2}
, in the expansion of (
30
).

By the same token, expanding (
III
) yields the high-energy approximation of the anti-particle spinor:

\displaystyle v_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}\approx\frac{1}{\sqrt{2p_{z}}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\left[|{\bm{p}}|(1-\sigma)-\frac{1}{2}\mu_{5}(1-\sigma)+\frac{1}{2}m(1+\sigma)\right]\xi_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}\\[2.84526pt]
-\left[|{\bm{p}}|(1+\sigma)+\frac{1}{2}\mu_{5}(1+\sigma)+\frac{1}{2}m(1-\sigma)\right]\xi_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}\end{array}\right)\,.

Finally, assuming that
|{\bm{p}}|\approx p_{z}
, the helicity eigenstates read:

\displaystyle\xi_{{\bm{p}},+}\approx\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
\frac{p_{x}+ip_{y}}{2p_{z}}\end{array}\right)\,,\qquad\xi_{{\bm{p}},-}\approx\left(\begin{array}[]{c}-\frac{p_{x}-ip_{y}}{2p_{z}}\\
1\end{array}\right)\,.

IV.1
Chiral Cherenkov radiation

Consider the reaction

in the frame where the incident quark moves along the

-axis:

*

In this section, the prime denotes the quantum numbers of the final fermion, unlike in Sec.

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle=(E_{{\bm{p}},\sigma},0,0,p_{z})\,,

(39a)

\displaystyle k

\displaystyle=(\omega_{{\bm{k}},\lambda},k_{\bot},0,xp_{z})\,,

(39b)

\displaystyle p^{\prime}

\displaystyle=(E^{\prime}_{{\bm{p}}^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}},-k_{\bot},0,(1-x)p_{z})\,.

\displaystyle\frac{d\dot{w}}{dx}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{2}}{4\pi}\frac{1}{4E}\left[K_{0}^{2}\frac{x^{2}-2x+2}{(1-x)x^{2}}\theta\left(K_{0}^{2}\right)+\frac{m^{2}x^{2}}{1-x}\theta\left(K^{2}\right)\right]\,.

(52)

The total rate has the familiar QED infrared divergence at
x\to 0
. By parameterizing it with a cutoff
\omega_{c}
, we obtain the rate, with the logarithmic accuracy,

\displaystyle\dot{w}=\frac{e^{2}}{4\pi}\frac{\lambda b_{0}}{2}\log\left[\frac{\lambda b_{0}E^{2}}{(\lambda b_{0}E+m^{2})\omega_{c}}\right]\delta_{\lambda,\sgn b_{0}}\,.

(53)

The total radiated energy, which can be computed by integrating over
x
the product of (
52

xE

\omega_{c}
.

In the chiral limit, only the helicity-preserving channel contributes to the rate. In a model with
\mu_{5}=0
and finite

,
K
coincides with
K_{0}
, and (

\displaystyle\frac{d\dot{w}}{dx}\Big|_{\mu_{5}=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{2}}{4\pi}\frac{1}{4E}\left[\lambda b_{0}E\frac{(1-x)^{2}+1}{x}-2m^{2}\right]\theta\left(K_{0}^{2}\right)\,.

(54)

This result was previously derived in
Tuchin 2018a
.

IV.2
Pair production

The cross channel of the chiral Cherenkov radiation is the pair production. Assuming that the incident photon moves in the
z
-direction, and
x
is the quark’s longitudinal momentum momentum fraction, the differential quark production rate is given by:

\displaystyle d\dot{w}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\sum_{\lambda,\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\frac{k_{z}}{8\omega^{3}x(1-x)}\delta\left(E_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}+E^{\prime}_{{\bm{p}}^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}}-\omega_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}\right)|i\mathcal{M}|^{2}d^{2}p_{\bot}dx\,,

(55)

where now

is the fraction of the initial momentum carried by the quark. The amplitude is given by:

\displaystyle i\mathcal{M}

\displaystyle=\frac{ie}{\sqrt{2x(1-x)}}\left\{(2x-1-\lambda\sigma)p_{\bot}\delta_{\sigma^{\prime},-\sigma}+m(\sigma+\lambda)\delta_{\sigma^{\prime},\sigma}\right\}\,.

The energy conservation is expressed by the equation:

\displaystyle E_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}+E^{\prime}_{{\bm{p}}^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}}-\omega_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}\approx-\mu_{5}(\sigma+\sigma^{\prime})+\frac{1}{2}\lambda b_{0}+\frac{m^{2}+p_{\bot}^{2}}{2x(1-x)\omega}\,,

(57)

were we used (
42a
)–(
42c

In the channel where both quark and anti-quark have the same helicity
\sigma=\sigma^{\prime}
, the quark production rate reads:

\displaystyle\frac{d\dot{w}(\sigma=\sigma^{\prime})}{d^{2}p_{\bot}dx}=\frac{e^{2}}{4\pi}\frac{m^{2}}{4\pi\omega}\frac{1}{x(1-x)}\delta_{\lambda,\sigma}\delta(p_{\bot}^{2}-P^{2})\theta(P^{2})\,,

(58)

where

\displaystyle P^{2}=(4\mu_{5}\sigma-\lambda b_{0})x(1-x)\omega-m^{2}\,.

(59)

Evidently,

is positive only if

. In particular, at finite
\mu_{5}
,

is positive only for the helicity
\sigma=\sgn\mu_{5}
.

In the channel where the quark and anti-quark have opposite helicities
\sigma=-\sigma^{\prime}
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\frac{d\dot{w}(\sigma\neq\sigma^{\prime})}{d^{2}p_{\bot}dx}=\frac{e^{2}}{4\pi}\frac{p_{\bot}^{2}}{4\pi\omega}\frac{(1-x)^{2}+x^{2}}{x(1-x)}\delta(p_{\bot}^{2}-P_{0}^{2})\theta(P_{0}^{2})\,,

(60)

where

\displaystyle P_{0}^{2}=-\lambda b_{0}x(1-x)\omega-m^{2}\,.

(61)

In this case only the photon polarization
\lambda=-\sgn b_{0}
contributes regardless of the quark and anti-quark helicities.

Adding (
58
) and (
61
) and integrating over
p_{\bot}
and
x
yields the total rate:

\displaystyle\dot{w}=\frac{e^{2}}{4\pi}\frac{1}{6\omega}\left\{\left[\sqrt{|\lambda b_{0}|\omega(|\lambda b_{0}|\omega-4m^{2})}-6m^{2}\arctanh\sqrt{1-\frac{4m^{2}}{|b_{0}\lambda|\omega}}+2m^{2}\sqrt{1-\frac{4m^{2}}{|b_{0}\lambda|\omega}}\right]\delta_{\lambda,-\sgn b_{0}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+6m^{2}\arctanh\sqrt{1-\frac{4m^{2}}{(4\mu_{5}\sigma-b_{0}\lambda)\omega}}\right\}\,.

(62)

In the limit of very high energies
\omega\gg 4m^{2}/b_{0}

) is independent of energy and
\mu_{5}

\displaystyle\dot{w}=\frac{e^{2}}{4\pi}\frac{|\lambda b_{0}|}{6}\delta_{\lambda,-\sgn b_{0}}\,.

(63)

In a model with
\mu_{5}=0
and

,

and one obtains after integrating over the quark’s transverse momentum

\displaystyle\frac{d\dot{w}}{dx}\Big|_{\mu_{5}=0}=\frac{e^{2}}{4\pi}\frac{|\lambda b_{0}|}{4}\left[(1-x)^{2}+x^{2}+\frac{2m^{2}}{|\lambda b_{0}|\omega}\right]\theta\left(P_{0}^{2}\right)\,.

(64)

We only considered
1\to 2
processes, however
2\to 1
processes are also allowed. The corresponding cross sections can be obtained by crossing one of the final particles to the initial state. However, the phase space of such scattering is quite small, as it is proportional to a
\delta

V
2\to 2
scattering

Consider a generic
2\to 2
scattering:
p_{1}p_{2}\to p_{3}p_{4}
. The anomalous terms give a small contribution unless the scattering amplitude has a resonance, which occurs when the denominator of a propagator vanishes. In this section, we will study the tree-level amplitudes at high energies and derive the conditions that allow the emergence of resonances.

Choose the center-of-mass frame:

\displaystyle p_{1}=(E_{1},k\hat{{\bm{z}}})\,,\qquad p_{2}=(E_{2},-k\hat{{\bm{z}}})\,,\qquad p_{3}=(E_{3},k^{\prime}\hat{{\bm{n}}})\,,\qquad p_{4}=(E_{4},-k^{\prime}\hat{{\bm{n}}})\,.

(65)

where
a=1,\ldots,4
,

\displaystyle E_{a}=\sqrt{{\bm{p}}_{a}^{2}-2\Lambda_{a}|{\bm{p}}_{a}|+M^{2}_{a}}\,.

(66)

We introduced notations:

\displaystyle\Lambda_{a}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\sigma_{a}\mu_{5}\,,&q,\,\bar{q}\\
\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{a}b_{0}\,,&\gamma\end{array}\right.\,,\qquad M^{2}_{a}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mu_{5}^{2}+m_{a}^{2}\,,&q,\,\bar{q}\\
0\,,&\gamma\end{array}\right.\,.

In the high-energy approximation:

\displaystyle E_{a}\approx|{\bm{p}}_{a}|-\Lambda_{a}+\frac{M_{a}^{2}-\Lambda^{2}_{a}}{2|{\bm{p}}_{a}|}\,.

(71)

The magnitude of the final momentum
k^{\prime}
can be determined by the energy conservation:

\displaystyle k^{\prime}\approx k+\frac{1}{2}(\Lambda_{3}+\Lambda_{4}-\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{2})+\frac{1}{4k}\left(M_{1}^{2}+M_{2}^{2}-M_{3}^{2}-M_{4}^{2}+\Lambda_{3}^{2}+\Lambda_{4}^{2}-\Lambda_{1}^{2}-\Lambda_{2}^{2}\right)\,.

(72)

Inspection of (
98
) and (
29
) reveals that the resonance emerges when

\displaystyle q^{2}-M^{2}+2\Lambda|{\bm{q}}|=0\,,

(73)

where
q
is the momentum flowing through the propagator. The parameters
M
and
\Lambda
, without a subscript, refer to the virtual state represented by the propagator. Denote
\cos\theta=\hat{{\bm{z}}}\cdot\hat{{\bm{n}}}
. In the
t
-channel, when
q=p_{1}-p_{3}
, the expression (
73
) vanishes when

\displaystyle(\theta k)_{0}=-\Lambda\pm\sqrt{\Lambda^{2}-M^{2}-(\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{3})(\Lambda_{2}-\Lambda_{4})}\,,

(74)

whereas in the
u
-channel, when
q=p_{1}-p_{4}
, it vanishes when

\displaystyle(\chi k)_{0}=-\Lambda\pm\sqrt{\Lambda^{2}-M^{2}-(\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{4})(\Lambda_{2}-\Lambda_{3})}\,,

(75)

where we introduced
\chi=\pi-\theta
. We took account of the fact that at high-energies, the scattering cross sections peak at either small
\theta
or small
\chi
. Eqs. (
74
) and (
75
) represent possible resonances in the
t
and
u
channels, respectively. A resonance occurs when there exists a set of helicities and polarizations such that
(\theta k)_{0}
or
(\chi k)_{0}
are physical, meaning they are real and positive.

Consider two specific examples.

Fermion-fermion scattering

q(p_{1})q(p_{2})\to q(p_{3})q(p_{4})
. At high energies, the leading contribution to the cross section stems from the exchange of a virtual photon in the
t
-channel. Moreover, the fermion helicities do not change. As a result, (
74
) becomes:

\displaystyle(\theta k)_{0}=-\frac{\lambda b_{0}}{2}\pm\frac{|b_{0}|}{2}=|\lambda b_{0}|\delta_{\sgn(\lambda b_{0}),-1}\,.

(76)

A resonance emerges for the virtual photon’s polarization
\lambda=-\sgn(b_{0})
at
\sqrt{-t}=\theta k=b_{0}
. The impact of this resonance on the transport properties of chiral media was explored in
Tuchin 2020
.

Compton scattering

q(p_{1})\gamma(p_{2})\to\gamma(p_{3})q(p_{4})
. In this case, the main contribution arises from the exchange of a virtual fermion in the
u
-channel. In view of (approximate) helicity conservation (
75
) yields the following position of the resonance:

\displaystyle(\chi k)_{0}=-\sigma\mu_{5}\pm\sqrt{-m^{2}}\,.

(77)

Since this expression is always complex, there is no resonance in the leading high-energy term.

Apparently, only processes involving virtual photons exhibit resonant behavior at the leading order. We verified numerically that this
conclusion holds at any energy.
At lower energies, spin-flip transitions play a more important role and, as a result, resonances exhibit stronger dependence on
\mu_{5}
.

We have not investigated whether the resonances can appear in virtual fermion states at higher orders of the perturbative expansion. However, in the next section we do demonstrate that both photon and fermion propagators can become resonant in a
2\to 3
process at the leading order.

VI
Bremsstrahlung

VI.1
Resonances in fermion and photon propagators

Consider now photon radiation in an external Coulomb field in chiral medium:
e(p_{1})\to e(p_{2})+\gamma(k)
. Denote the momentum transfer as
{\bm{q}}
. According to (
100
), the photon propagator has a familiar infrared Coulomb pole at
{\bm{q}}^{2}=0
. The other two poles correspond to zeros of denominators of fermion propagators
\tilde{G}(p_{1}-k)
and
\tilde{G}(p_{2}+k)
. The denominator of the former propagator can be written as:

\displaystyle\prod_{\sigma=\pm 1}\left[(p_{1}-k)^{2}-\mu_{5}^{2}-m^{2}-2\sigma\mu_{5}|{\bm{p}}_{1}-{\bm{k}}|\right]\,.

(78)

Using (
66
), denoting by
\theta
the angle between
{\bm{p}}
and
{\bm{k}}
, and expanding in inverse powers of momentum, we obtain for each of the two factors in (
78
):

\displaystyle(p_{1}-k)^{2}-\mu_{5}^{2}-m^{2}-2\mu_{5}|{\bm{p}}_{1}-{\bm{k}}|\approx-\theta^{2}E\omega+2(1-x)[\lambda b_{0}-\mu_{5}(\sigma+\sigma_{1})]E+\frac{b_{0}^{2}-2\lambda\mu_{5}\sigma_{1}b_{0}x-m^{2}x^{2}}{x}\,,

(79)

where
x=\omega/E
. At finite values of
\mu_{5}
and
b_{0}
, we can neglect the last term, which is small. Clearly, this expression vanishes at the photon emission angle:

\displaystyle\theta_{0}=\sqrt{\frac{2(1-x)[\lambda b_{0}-\mu_{5}(\sigma+\sigma_{1})]}{\omega}}\,,

(80)

provided that
\lambda b_{0}-\mu_{5}(\sigma+\sigma_{1})>0
. In the case where
b_{0}
and
\mu_{5}
vanish, the last term in (
79
), because of its negative sign, prevents (
79
) from vanishing at any
\theta
.

A similar analysis applies to the other fermion propagator. Moreover, even the time-time component of the photon propagator
\tilde{D}^{00}(q)
, given by (
100
), that mediates between the fermion current and the Coulomb field, has a resonance. This is because the momentum transfer vanishes at a finite momentum transfer
{\bm{q}}={\bm{p}}_{2}+{\bm{k}}-{\bm{p}}_{1}
. This can be seen by considering the smallest momentum transfer, which occurs when all three momenta are aligned. In this case:

\displaystyle q_{\text{min}}=|{\bm{p}}_{1}|-|{\bm{p}}_{2}|-|{\bm{k}}|\approx E_{1}-\frac{m^{2}}{2E_{1}}+\sigma_{1}\mu_{5}-E_{2}+\frac{m^{2}}{2E_{2}}-\sigma_{2}\mu_{5}-\omega-\frac{\lambda b_{0}}{2}=\frac{m^{2}\omega}{2E_{1}E_{2}}+(\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2})\mu_{5}-\frac{1}{2}\lambda b_{0}\,.

(81)

Clearly,
q_{\text{min}}
can be negative indicating that
|{\bm{q}}|
can vanish.

These conclusions are in agreement with the analysis of
Hansen and Tuchin 2023
, which investigated bremsstrahlung at
\mu_{5}=0
. When an external field source possesses a magnetic moment (e.g. a heavy nucleus), the incoming fermion couples to this magnetic moment through the components
D^{ij}(q)
of the photon propagator, as shown in (
98
). An apparent resonance appears at
{\bm{q}}^{2}=b_{0}^{2}
. This anomalous contribution to bremsstrahlung was computed in
Hansen and Tuchin 2022
under the condition
\mu_{5}=0
.

VI.2
Regularization of resonances

The emergence of resonances indicates instability of the fermion and boson states in the chiral medium. Indeed this is precisely why the splitting processes discussed in Sec.
IV
are possible at all. Since the fermion and boson states in the chiral medium are quasi-stationary they possess a finite width that is proportional to their total decay rate
\dot{w}
, which is given by (
53
) for photons and (
IV.2
) for fermions.
The finite width
\dot{w}
of the propagator screens the poles of both the photon and fermion propagators. However, in conductors, a more effective screening of the resonances of the photon propagator is achieved through conventional plasma polarization effects, which are characterized by the Debye mass at short distances.

The resonances that occur in the fermion propagators, discussed in this section, reflect instability of fermion states in chiral matter with respect to spontaneous photon emission. The width of a quasi-static state is given by the total decay rate as measured in the medium rest frame. Its inverse is the relaxation time
\tau=1/\dot{w}
. In the fermion rest frame the relaxation rate is
(E/m)\tau^{-1}
. With the account of the finite width, the fermion propagators in (
78
) are regulated as follows:

\displaystyle\prod_{\sigma=\pm}\left[(p_{1}-k)^{2}-\mu_{5}^{2}-m^{2}-2\sigma\mu_{5}|{\bm{p}}_{1}-{\bm{k}}|-iE_{{\bm{p}}_{1},\sigma_{1}}/\tau_{{\bm{p}}_{1},\sigma_{1}}\right]\,.

(82)

Due to the resonances, the bremsstrahlung total cross section is very sensitive to
1\to 2
rates.

This regularization procedure can be employed to regularize photon and fermion propagators in other processes.

VII
Summary

We considered an effective quantum electrodynamics in a chiral medium characterized by a constant chiral chemical potential
\mu_{5}
and a chiral magnetic conductivity
b_{0}
. The chiral chemical potential signifies the chiral imbalance of the medium, whereas the chiral magnetic conductivity quantifies the strength of the electric current induced by a magnetic field. The latter is a non-perturbative chiral magnetic effect, which we regard as a constitutive relation incorporated at the classical level of the effective theory. We employed this effective theory to analyze the scattering amplitude in a chiral medium and observed the emergence of resonances in certain chiral channels in
1\to 2
,
2\to 2
and
2\to 3
processes.

We computed the paradigm
1\to 2
processes, the chiral Cherenkov radiation and the pair production, that determine the width of quasi-stationary fermion and photon states in a chiral medium. Kinematic considerations require that
\mu_{5}
enter the expressions for the resonances multiplied by the difference of incoming and outgoing quark helicities. Since at high energies, helicity flip is suppressed, the role of
\mu_{5}
is also suppressed, while
b_{0}
plays the leading role.

Similar patterns are observed in processes involving a larger number of external particles. The widths of the resonances are determined by the
1\to 2
decay rates, which in turn strongly influence the cross section of the resonant channels. A number of such processes were considered in the literature. In this work, we derived general conditions for the emergence of resonances and outlines the physical principles of their regularization. Generalization of our results to QCD is quite straightforward.

We concentrated on chiraly isotropic systems with
{\bm{b}}=0
, which have a simpler mathematical description. This by no means indicate that the results of this paper are restricted to chiraly isotropic systems. For example, the chiral Cherenkov radiation is emitted at finite
{\bm{b}}
and shows a very similar resonant behavior. The primary distinction of an anisotropic chiral medium lies in the fact that the vector
{\bm{b}}
breaks the spherical symmetry and, as a result, the resonance structure depends on the relative angle between the photon direction and
{\bm{b}}

Lehnert and Potting 2004b
;
Huang and Tuchin 2018
;
Tuchin 2018b
;
Hansen et al. 2025
;
Tuchin 2025
.

The instability of electrodynamics with the Chern-Simons term appearing in (
1
) is well-known
Carroll et al. 1990
. This effect, referred to as the chiral magnetic instability, is triggered by the runaway modes
k<b_{0}
of the electromagnetic field and is reflected in the pole at
k^{2}=b_{0}|{\bm{k}}|
in the photon propagator (
15
). It causes the transfer of chirality from the medium to the magnetic field through the inverse cascade process
Carroll et al. 1990
;
Joyce and Shaposhnikov 1997
;
Boyarsky et al. 2012
;
Kharzeev 2014
;
Khaidukov et al. 2018
;
Kirilin et al. 2014
;
Akamatsu and Yamamoto 2013
;
Avdoshkin et al. 2016
;
Dvornikov and Semikoz 2015
;
Tuchin 2015
;
Manuel and Torres-Rincon 2015
;
Buividovich and Ulybyshev 2016
;
Sigl and Leite 2016
;
Xia et al. 2016
;
Kaplan et al. 2017
;
Kirilin and Sadofyev 2017
;
Tuchin 2018a
;
Mace et al. 2020
. While the chiral magnetic instability is indeed caused by the chiral magnetic current, it should not be confused with the instabilities in the scattering amplitude, which are the focus of this paper. The distinction between these instabilities becomes most apparent when
{\bm{b}}
is finite, while
b_{0}=0
. In this case, the scattering amplitudes are resonant, whereas the chiral magnetic instability is absent.

In practical applications, the parameter
b_{0}
may vary with time. A careful analysis performed in
Tuchin 2025
;
Tuchin 2026a
;
Tuchin 2026b
reveals that when
b_{0}
depends on time, the number of resonances can double depending on whether the asymptotic values of
b_{0}
are finite or vanish.

It is noteworthy that this paper makes no mention of experimental measurements. This is because there are none, even though such measurements are technically possible. For instance, one can shoot an energetic electron through a Weyl semimetal to observe polarized THz radiation
Hansen et al. 2025
, or measure the Brewster’s angle in topological insulators
Stewart and Tuchin 2019
. In relativistic heavy-ion collisions, one can study circularly polarized photons originating from the quark-gluon plasma
Tuchin 2019
. Similarly, cosmic rays may induce chiral Cherenkov radiation in the presence of a slowly varying axion field

.

B.3
Gordon’s identity

Consider the Dirac equation at finite chiral chemical potential (
18
) for the positive energy solutions:

\displaystyle\left(p_{\mu}\gamma^{\mu}-\gamma^{5}\gamma^{\mu}a_{\mu}-m\right)u_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}=0\,.

(127)

The adjoint spinor obeys the equation

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}\left(p_{\mu}\gamma^{\mu}-\gamma^{5}\gamma^{\mu}a_{\mu}-m\right)=0\,.

(128)

It can be shown that the spinors satisfy the Gordon’s identity:

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{{\bm{p}}^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}}\gamma^{\mu}u_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}=\frac{1}{2m}\bar{u}_{{\bm{p}}^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}}\left\{p^{\mu}+{p^{\prime}}^{\mu}+i\sigma^{\mu\nu}({p^{\prime}}_{\nu}-p_{\nu})-2i\sigma^{\mu\nu}\gamma^{5}a_{\nu}\right\}u_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}\,.

(129)

It is straightforward to verify that

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{{\bm{p}}^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}}(\not{p}-\not{p}^{\prime})u_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}=0\,,

(130)

which is consistent with the charge conservation requirement.

Using the Gordon identity, the current density is:

\displaystyle j^{\mu}=\frac{1}{2E_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}}\bar{u}_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}\gamma^{\mu}u_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}=\frac{1}{2E_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}}\frac{1}{2m}\bar{u}_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}\left(2p^{\mu}-2i\sigma^{\mu\nu}\gamma^{5}a_{\nu}\right)u_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}=\frac{1}{E_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}}\left(E_{{\bm{p}},\sigma},{\bm{p}}-\mu_{5}\langle{\bm{\sigma}}\rangle\right)\,.

(131)

where
\langle{\bm{\sigma}}\rangle=\xi^{\dagger}_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}{\bm{\sigma}}\xi_{{\bm{p}},\sigma}
.

Given the momentum
{\bm{p}}
with the polar and azimuthal angles
\theta
and
\phi
, the explicit helicity eigenstates read

\displaystyle\xi_{{\bm{p}},+}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\\
e^{i\phi}\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\end{array}\right)\,,\qquad\xi_{{\bm{p}},-}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}-e^{-i\phi}\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\\
\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\end{array}\right)\,.

Using these we compute:

\displaystyle\langle{\bm{\sigma}}\rangle=\sigma\frac{{\bm{p}}}{|{\bm{p}}|}\,.

(136)

It is remarkable that even a particle at rest have non-vanishing current

\displaystyle\lim_{{\bm{p}}\to 0}{\bm{j}}=-\frac{\mu_{5}\sigma\hat{{\bm{p}}}}{\sqrt{m^{2}+\mu_{5}^{2}}}\,,

(137)

which is a reflection of non-zero Berry curvature
