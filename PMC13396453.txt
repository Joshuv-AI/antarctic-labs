Integration of hierarchical clustering in hematologic biomarkers prediction for endocrine toxicity during immunotherapy in lung cancer patients
Authors: Simona Coniac, Radu Tudor Ionescu, Mariana Cristina Costache-Outas, Ionuţ-Lucian Antone-Iordache, Ana-Maria Barbu, Victor Teodor Bardan, Andreea Zamfir, Andreea-Iuliana Ionescu, Corin Badiu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractImmune check-point inhibitors (ICIs) have outstandingly changed the treatment for lung cancer patients providing longer progression-free (PFS) and overall survival (OS). Immune-related adverse events (irAEs) counteract this success, and different biomarkers are broadly investigated to predict their appearance. Hematologic predictive biomarkers for irAEs are extensively studied to better prevent, monitor and treat irAEs. This is a retrospective exploratory-in-nature cohort study of lung cancer patients treated with ICIs in a tertiary level hospital in Romania, from 1 November 2017 until 1 August 2024. We evaluate 173 non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) patients treated with at least three cycles of ICIs and we investigate the association between Endocrine-irAEs and hematologic variables, such as neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), absolute neutrophils, lymphocytes and eosinophils count. We also revise the literature and use an Artificial Intelligence (AI) technique to interpret our endpoints. We identify a statistically significant correspondence between NLR value before ICIs initiation and all-irAEs [odds ratio (OR) 0.877, 95% confidence interval (CI) 0.763–0.979, p = 0.036], but not for Endocrine-irAEs (OR 0.898, 95% CI 0.781–1.031, p = 0.081). While significant barriers for identifying the patients at risk of developing irAEs are still present, the implementation of truly relevant and useful biomarkers remains challenging. Introduction

In recent years, the landscape of non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC) treatment has constantly shifted, but immunotherapy continued to be the main standard therapy for non-oncogene-addicted metastatic1, stage III unresectable NSCLC patients2,3, and even in lately approved neoadjuvant and adjuvant settings4. The revolutionary discovery of immune-check-points inhibitors (ICIs), that disrupt the cancer immune tolerance, restore T-cell recognition against tumor cells and confine the cytotoxic T-lymphocyte-associated antigen 4 (CTLA-4) and the programmed cell death 1 (PD-1)/ligand 1 (PD-L1) pathways, has provided unforeseen durable clinical response, progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) for NSCLC patients. Nevertheless, even if ICIs can offer both long remissions or cure in a subset of patients and short-lasting responses in others, over time, most of NSCLC patients develop resistance to immunotherapy5.

In addition, successful immunotherapy in cancer patients can trigger autoimmune and inflammatory.

toxicities, causing highly unpredictable adverse events. Immune-related adverse events (irAEs) ascribed to CTLA-4 or PD-1 or CTLA-4/PD-1 combination blockade may distress numerous organs and tissues, with high frequency for the skin, gastrointestinal tract and endocrine system6. Utmost frequently reported in randomized clinical trials (RCTs)7–10 and real-world evidence (RWE)11,12, endocrine-immune related adverse events (Endocrine-irAEs) have brought a new scientific interest to the medical community as a non-severe, but mostly irreversible group of irAEs13, in need for life-long hormonal replacement therapy. Thyroid disorders, typically associated with anti-PD-1 antibodies, and hypophysitis, commonly related to anti-CTLA-4 therapy, are the most frequent endocrine toxicities14. ICI-induced insulin-deficient diabetes mellitus and primary adrenal insufficiency, though rare, can be life-threatening if not promptly diagnosed and treated. The combination of anti-CTLA-4 and anti-PD-1 treatment has generally been associated with the highest incidence and severity of ICI-induced endocrinopathies. Furthermore, autoimmune endocrinopathies have been investigated as a possible class of predictive and prognostic biomarkers for ICI’ efficacy and overall survival15–17. As contradictory data counteracted this positive impact on ICI’s response18,19, the research interest remains vivid and actual.

In real-world medical practice, during the course of treatment, metastatic NSCLC patients face complex challenging conditions and need co-medications with proved negative impact on ICI’s response, such as steroids20, proton-pump inhibitors (PPIs)21, antibiotics22 or opioids for pain management23. Reported co-medication from RWE and retrospective studies could bring more insightful information about medical needs hardly influencing the outcomes of cancer patients, even if secondary endpoints.

Predictive biomarkers have been intensively investigated to clearly identify which patients truly benefit from immunotherapy. PD-L1 was the first approved predictive biomarker for NSCLC patients and international guidelines recommend monotherapy ICI or chemotherapy combination according to PD-L1 expression1,4. In newly approved ICI’s indications in earlier stages, such as neoadjuvant or perioperative settings24–27, liquid biopsy circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) might become a strong novel biomarker28,29, not for efficacy, but for relapse or residual disease.

Thus, predictive biomarkers for both efficacy and toxicity are still intensively studied. Despite the abundant literature data, there still exists a knowledge gap to clearly outline the most pertinent and reasonable biomarkers. In routine clinical practice, easy-to-use, affordable and clinically relevant biomarkers are required to support physicians in monitoring the effectiveness of subsequent therapies. Hematologic biomarkers, such as neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), absolute neutrophils count (ANC), absolute lymphocytes count (ALC) and absolute eosinophiles count (AEC) have been extensively investigated for their potential predictive role for irAEs in NSCLC patients treated with immunotherapy28. Conflicting results of positive11,30–32 and negative33–35 predictive correlation between hematologic biomarkers and irAEs inspired enhanced attention for this particularly dynamic research topic. Besides hematologic biomarkers, diverse inflammatory or nutritional indexes, such as Lung Immune Prognostic Index (LIPI)36,37, Systemic Immune-inflammation Index (SII)38, Prognostic Nutritional Index (PNI)39, C-PLAN Index40 or HALP Index41 have been considered in mostly retrospective trials to validate their substantial power to predict immune response, PFS, OS and irAEs. Nevertheless, all these various combined indexes mainly assess hematological biomarkers, especially NLR. Essentially, NLR and absolute count of hematologic biomarkers reveal themselves as the most intriguing, inexpensive and reasonable, but mandatory to be assessed for real clinical practice. The dynamic assessment during ICIs to evaluate both response and toxicity becomes crucial to better foresee cancer patient progress and to prevent the onset of irAEs. As co-medication during immunotherapy majorly influence NLR dynamics, an accurate correlation between all these variables is difficult yet necessary to help physicians in treating cancer patients. Our RWE retrospective exploratory-in-nature research analyzed multiple clinical variables of NSCLC patients treated with ICIs in a tertiary-level hospital in Romania. Hematologic biomarkers, meaning NLR, ANC, ALC and AEC were dynamically investigated, at baseline and during immunotherapy treatment for their greatest possible value in Endocrine-irAEs prediction. To better integrate our results in the context of abundant literature research, Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools were applied to obtain a hierarchical clustering of relevant scientific articles.

Statistical Results

The primary endpoint of the study was the influence of NLR at baseline, before the initiation of immunotherapy in NSCLC patients who experienced Endocrine-irAEs. We performed a logistic regression in order to assess the influence of NLR on developing all irAEs before starting ICI treatment. While the likelihood ratio of the model was statistically significant, the initial odds ratio was crossing 1. This happened because the odds ratio was calculated based on an insignificant Wald statistic. As likelihood ratio and Wald statistic tests should be asymptotically equivalent and we had a finite sample, we performed an empirical bootstrapped model (10000 trials) in order to re-calculate the confidence intervals (Table 5). Therefore, by increasing the NLR with one unit, the probability of developing an irAE was reduced to 87.5%. We identify a statistically significant correspondence between NLR value before ICIs initiation and all-irAEs [odds ratio (OR) 0.877, 95% confidence interval (CI) 0.763–0.979, p = 0.036], but not for Endocrine-irAEs (OR 0.898, 95% CI 0.781–1.031, p = 0.081).

Dendrogram analysis

The employed hierarchical clustering method organized the abstracts of the retrieved references into distinct categories merging them into larger groups, based on the matches observed between the abstracts. This is illustrated in the original BERT-based dendrogram in Figure A1 from the Appendix Section. This analysis was performed based on the titles and abstracts of the articles, not on the full version of the papers. This BERT model limitation42 allowed only 512 tokens (words) per article as input. We highlight that this limitation did not majorly impact the results, as abstracts should accurately represent the content of articles, as Soviany et al.43 showed in their survey.

The BERT-based dendrogram shown in Figure A1 is the result of an unsupervised algorithm that clustered the abstracts based on the similarity among BERT embeddings. Since the BERT model is a pre-trained language model, it can accurately represent the global similarity of any two text samples. Meanwhile, the BERT model was not particularly trained on medical data, and this analysis might generate non-specialized clusters of the studied domain. To overcome this limitation, the validity of the BERT-based dendrogram was manually verified by all authors. Subsequently, three independent authors conducted a manual analysis of all the abstracts, in the specific order given by the dendrogram. We identified the keywords used by the AI algorithm to cluster the abstracts, and we manually exemplified them in Fig. 3. We observed that NLR was the main biomarker identified in all the abstracts. The algorithm clustered the abstracts according to other hematologic, inflammatory or nutritional scores. For example, abstracts were related to other hematologic scores, platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR), lymphocyte-to-monocyte ratio (LMR), ANC, ALC, AEC or inflammatory indexes, such as systemic immune-inflammation index (SII), inflammatory index (II), lung immune prognostic index (LIPI), CPLAN or nutritional scores, such as prognostic nutritional index (PNI), body mass index (BMI), Glasgow prognostic score (GPS), HALP as shown in Fig. 3. NLR is the keyword that characterizes the first (light blue) cluster40,44–48 and NLR associated with SII and PNI are grouped in the second (yellow) one49–55. The third (grey) group matched LIPI index with NLR36,37,56–58. The fourth (pink) cluster comprised several other prognostic scores. The first three articles in the pink cluster investigated how fluorodeoxyglucose Positron Emission Tomography and Computed Tomography (FDG-PET/CT) scan standardized uptake values (SUV) might correlate with NLR59–61. The second subgroup in the pink cluster gathers abstracts that examined NLR and PLR values62–64. The following ten abstracts inside the pink cluster include articles about the correlation between NLR and nutritional indexes39,41,65–72. The last two articles in the pink cluster evaluated ANC and AEC values besides NLR73,74. The fifth (brown) cluster connected abstracts whose keywords are NLR, LMR and PLR75–80. The clustering algorithm separated an article combining hematologic biomarkers, ANC, ALC and absolute platelet count (APT) values81. In the first high-level branch, the AI system mostly gathered hematologic prognostic scores and combined them into a larger cluster. The seventh (purple) set of abstracts paired NLR with LIPI or PLR82–86. The eight (red) group combines NLR with nutritional indexes, such as BMI or GPS87–92. The large nineth (green) cluster mostly gathered abstracts based on CPLAN index (constructed on C-reactive protein, performance status, lactate dehydrogenase, albumin, and derived neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio)40,93–105. The second high-level branch (comprising purple, red and green clusters) covers a larger collection of abstracts which represented combined systemic inflammatory scores. Finally, the last (orange) group harmonizes assorted immune inflammatory scores38,106–119.

For more trustworthy and substantial understanding of the AI dendrogram, we thoroughly reviewed the 83 abstracts of the retrieved references. It is imperative to notice the hierarchical structure of the retrieved abstracts. The authors would like to emphasize that 56 abstracts (67,5%) were retrospective studies37,39,40,45,49,50,52–55,59,60,63,65–70,72,75–80,82,83,87–92,94–97,99–104,106,108,109,111–116,118,119, only 15 articles were prospective41,44,46–48,51,61,62,71,73,74,98,107,110,117, and the remaining 12 were reviews or meta-analysis36,38,56–58,64,81,84–86,93,105. The retrospective cohorts rarely exceeded 200 patients, and just 31 studies (37.3%) investigated NLR and other indexes in first line ICI40,49–56,59,60,62–64,76–79,89–92,97,101,106,107,109–111,116. All the studies examined hematologic, inflammatory or nutritional scores at baseline and just 34 studies (41%) dynamically analyzed the same indexes, but in fact a short period of at least three different values for at longest three months of immunotherapy was truly investigated39–41,44–50,55,61,62,64,66–69,75,78,80,84,85,89,98,104,112–119. Just 12 studies investigated the correlation between NLR and the onset and severity of irAEs39,40,44,45,47–49,67,99–101,116 revealing a thought-provoking future research approach as predictive biomarker for irAEs. No more than four studies reported data about co-medication, meaning the use of antibiotics and steroids during ICIs50,57,87,97 and highlighted their negative impact upon efficacy and how NLR values have been influenced. Experienced physicians revealed deeper significances of the article’s clusters. The main deduction of this revision was that NLR might be the most crucial predictive factor for efficacy, converted into PFS and OS. Additionally, NLR might anticipate the onset of irAEs, and correlate with their severity. Index dynamics were better predictors than baseline values in foreseeing survival, onset of irAEs, especially in first line immunotherapy.

Discussion

Indisputably, innovative state-of-the-art immunotherapy provided durable clinical response, increased PFS and prolonged OS in NSCLC patients, and became unequivocally standard of treatment worldwide1,3,4. Nonetheless, the majority of patients progressed over time, and the combined internal mechanisms of innate or acquired resistance to ICIs5 with external environmental factors, such as co-medication20–23, diet120,121 with impact on microbiota122,123 still challenged the medical professionals in their objective of treating cancer patients. From this perspective, the constant pursuit for reliable, easy-to-use and inexpensive predictive biomarkers signified a restless concern for medical researchers.

Because immune-targeted therapies break the cancer immune tolerance, restoring T-cell recognition against tumor cells, immune system cells were distinctly studied, especially NLR, ANC, ALC, and AEC. Diverse combinations of these immune cells have been developed and intensely investigated, but all of them are based on simple hematologic values as core foundation.

IrAEs counteracted the successfully appraised ICI’s efficacy and even if the frequency and predilection of irAEs for each ICI had been reported, time of onset and severity of irAEs were often unpredictable. Autoimmune endocrinopathies have been deliberately and independently explored for their predictive value to increase PFS and OS15–17,124,125, and for preserving a high quality of life126, especially in metastatic NSCLC patients treated with ICIs. Even though inconsistent results had been similarly published18,19, the interest to study Endocrine-irAEs and more interestingly the predictive biomarkers that could point out the onset and severity of Endocrine-irAEs was still actual.

The main endpoint of this real-world retrospective exploratory-in-nature study was to delve into the power of hematologic biomarkers, meaning NLR, ANC, ALC and AEC values to predict Endocrine-irAEs in real-life NSCLC patients. In our research, we identified a greatly-pretreated cohort of mostly mNSCLC patients, in a good performance status, but with a substantial tumor burden, as 40% of patients had brain or liver metastases before ICI initiation. Before exploring the first objective, our analysis showed that any previous surgery or radiotherapy or chemotherapy treatment before first line ICI was linked with lower baseline ALC, ANC and AEC levels, while NLR was considerably higher, meaning a possible poor outcome. On the other hand, in first-line ICI treated NSCLC patients, meaning 65.3% of patients, ALC and ANC values were significantly higher, while NLR was not influenced. As evidenced by our dendrogram analysis, high NLR was consistently associated with a worse response to ICIs, low PFS and OS, while low NLR predicted an improved effectiveness. However, ANC, ALC and AEC values were not yet validated as reliable biomarkers and their usefulness still needs more research and understanding69,73,81. From this point of view, we drew attention towards the retrospective nature of our study, the large cohort of first-line NSCLC patients treated with ICIs, and we considered that our trial was in line with the most recent research analyzed by AI tools. Nevertheless, NSCLC patients treated with ICIs in our study proved an enhanced efficacy of immunotherapy, evidenced by partial response in almost 65% of patients.

Comparing the average time on ICI therapy between patients with irAEs and patients not experiencing toxicities, we acknowledged an increased range of 15.2 months versus 10.9 months, respectively. Among fifty NSCLC patients experiencing irAEs, autoimmune endocrinopathies occurred in 46 patients, with an average onset time of 6.3 months. Except one case of PAI, all Endocrine-irAEs were classified as grade ≤ 2 CTCAE127, and most of them needed life-long hormonal substitution therapy. Autoimmune thyroiditis was the most frequently reported Endocrine-irAEs, in 32 patients. Because five patients developed both Endocrine and non-endocrine irAEs, and four patients experienced more than one affected gland, 50 NSCLC patients lived through 59 irAEs. Nine NSCLC patients developed non-Endocrine irAEs, four of them being graded ≥ 3 CTCAE. All irAES were diagnosed, treated, monitored and followed up according to the international guidelines128,129. For statistical purposes, composite risk models based on blood parameters featuring the tumor-host interaction might provide accurate prognostic scores able to predict ICI’s response in NSCLC patients. As thoroughly detailed in Methods and Statistics Analysis Section, the main endpoint of this research was to investigate the power of hematologic biomarkers to predict Endocrine-irAEs in heavily-treated real-life lung cancer patients. We ascertained a statistically significant equivalence between NLR value before ICIs initiation and all-irAEs [odds ratio (OR) 0.877, 95% confidence interval (CI) 0.763–0.979, p = 0.036], but not for Endocrine-irAEs (OR 0.898, 95% CI 0.781–1.031, p = 0.081). In conclusion, our study did not definitively link the NLR value as predictive biomarker for Endocrine-irAE onset, and might be considered a negative study. On the other hand, the small sample study might as well not have the power to prove numerical magnitude. As mentioned before, only twelve retrieved references39,40,44,45,47–49,67,99–101,116 analyzed by the AI tool to deliver the dendrogram investigated the power of NLR in predicting irAEs. Therefore, our study analysis brought another statistically significant proof of baseline NLR evidence in predicting irAEs. This actual research did not have the authority to demonstrate a noteworthy correlation between NLR and Endocrine-irAEs, and larger prospective trials should be implemented to deliver this critical endpoint. We wisely reflect on this purpose because Endocrine-irAEs has already provided fascinating suggestions about their predictive role in ICIs efficacy, stated by persistent PFS and OS in NSCLC patients treated with ICIs15–17,130,131.

This study secondary endpoints evaluated the influence of particular clinical conditions with potential impact on ICI’s response. An impressive share of the patients experienced miscellaneous clinical conditions and needed co-medications, such as antibiotics for treated infections, steroids for brain metastasis, radiotherapy or chemotherapy emesis prevention, PPIs and opioids for pain symptomatology control. All these particular but real-life clinical settings were critical to be registered and reported, since they might influence hematologic biomarker dynamics and overall study results. The negative impact of cumulative use of multiple or prolonged antibiotics on response to immunotherapy was already proved22,132. Equally, long-term use of proton-pump inhibitors (PPIs) before ICI therapy seemed to have a dismal impact on overall survival17,21,132. Steroids sustained inherent immunosuppressive properties and their use before and soon after ICI initiation could be associated with poorer clinical outcomes17,20. The influence of the investigated hematological biomarkers at baseline, before ICI’s initiation on steroids use during therapy, on antibiotics treated infections during ICI, and on response to ICI were assessed in our study. ANC levels were substantial in predicting an increase in steroid use and AEC levels were significant in foreseeing a greater rate of infections. In our dendrogram, we identified only four articles50,57,87,97 suggesting the influence of co-medication, specifically steroids and antibiotics and their harmful effect on ICI’s response and their influence on NLR values. Even if our research did not bring clear cut results about the impact of co-medication on hematologic biomarkers in NSCLC patients treated with ICIs, it underlined the imperative need of reporting and publishing data from real-life challenging cancer patients’ populations. Co-medication in immunotherapy treated NSCLC patients had a vital power on treatment outcomes, such as response, PFS and OS, and prospective monitoring and follow-up might better integrate mixed influences upon overall efficacy and toxicities.

Our retrospective cohort of NSCLC patients treated with ICIs and experiencing irAEs comprised 50 patients, representing a small cohort for in-depth statistical analysis. Our reported results are a base for further larger prospective studies, with clear-defined independent variables for investigation. Thus, to better convene and put our research results into perspective, we additionally delved into recent published literature using an agglomerative clustering technique. The BERT-based dendrogram organized the abstracts according to the keywords, that were later identified by experienced physicians. The AI system clustered the abstracts by similarity into several groups, and finally in larger groups, combining hematologic prognostic scores, and systemic and immune inflammatory indexes. Eventually, physicians extracted more meaningful correlations between the retrieved abstracts, with potential to generate premises for future research. To summarize, NLR baseline, but more appealing dynamic evaluation might be established as a reliable predictive biomarker for response to ICIs, improved efficacy, PFS and OS. Furthermore, NLR might have the power to foresee the onset of irAEs and to point out the severity of autoimmune toxicities, which might be vitally important in moderate-to-severe non-Endocrine irAEs. Equally imperative, the influence of co-medication on NLR values should be registered and reported to better monitor the dismal impact of environmental factors on ICI’s response. We profoundly appreciate the support provided by the AI system for clear directions and correlations and for making our medical work easier, but we deemed that medically trained professionals’ intelligence was the one skilled to integrate all the medical knowledge. Following our statistical results, the dendrogram analysis underscored the actual and not resolved knowledge gap of defining precise predictive biomarkers for both efficacy and immune toxicity in NSCLC patients treated with immunotherapy.

The asserted limitations of our research are listed below. Nonetheless, this study cohort was a transparent piece of real-life data, unveiling the challenges that oncologists faced in daily practice. First of all, our retrospective, exploratory-in-nature observational study did not a have a priori-defined protocol and used registered data, resulting in potential selection bias. Secondly, we did not extract hematologic biomarkers in dynamic evaluation for patients not experiencing irAEs. This limitation implied both missing data and selection bias. Moreover, only fifty NSCLC patients were dynamically evaluated for the investigated biomarkers, meaning a small cohort and the possible confusion bias when generalizing the results to larger populations. Thirdly, multiple and varied types of cancer therapies during the study might have induced an immortal-time bias. Although real-life studies are more expected to present all these biases compared with clinical trials, this research might generate evidence from sub-populations under-represented in randomized clinical trials. Finally, the limitation of the BERT model, which accepted only a limited number of characters words per abstract. This restriction did not have a central effect on the resulting dendrogram since the abstracts were projected to truthfully characterize the content of the surveyed articles.

Though substantial impediments for identifying the patients at risk of developing Endocrine-irAEs are still current, the enactment of accurately pertinent and convenient biomarkers remains challenging. NLR might be a effective and easy to assess instrument in real-life clinical settings, but more research is needed to validate its prediction value in Endocrine-irAEs onset. Prospective observational phase IV large cohort studies should be developed to better monitor all the hematologic and predictive biomarkers. Taking into consideration that non-oncogene-addicted NSCLC treatment was based on immunotherapy and overall survival overreached five years, and almost all approved ICIs have indications in NSCLC cancer, this specific cancer site could be the ideal situation to dynamically investigate predictive biomarkers for PFS, OS and irAEs. The prolonged survival might offer enough time to accurately investigate diverse biomarkers and multiple therapy options could help physicians to learn more about the differences between ICIs and their toxicities in real-world setting.

Materials and methods

Study population

This real-life observational, non-interventional, exploratory-in-nature retrospective cohort study analyzed adult lung cancer patients treated with immunotherapy in a tertiary-level hospital, Colțea Clinical Hospital, in Bucharest, Romania. The evaluation period comprised almost seven years, between 1 November 2017 until 1 August 2024. Ethical Institutional Review Board approval of the study was obtained before the study was conducted. This study was managed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee of Colțea Clinical Hospital (protocol code 21.785/OSCC and date of approval 11 September 2021, according to the minutes: 24/11/08/2021). All patients had signed in the registered archive an informed consent to be treated in our clinic and to allow clinicians to use their medical data for educational scientific purposes. Being a retrospective study, actual informed consent was redundant. We utilized registered data and acknowledged several inclusion and exclusion criteria. Inclusion criteria involved adult non-oncogene-addicted advanced or metastatic NSCLC patients, who agreed to be treated with ICI according to Romanian National Protocols133. Exclusion criteria identified lung cancer patients with the following baseline characteristics, before the start of immunotherapy: small-cell lung cancer (SCLC), pregnancy, lactation, renal or liver failure, untreated oncogenic driver mutations, active Hepatitis B, Hepatitis C or Human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) infection, active autoimmune diseases, active immunosuppressive treatment, less than two cycles of ICI, less than three hematologic biomarkers available.

NSCLC patients treated with ICIs were evaluated in two-step process. Firstly, throughout the study, advanced or metastatic 173 NSCLC patients were monitored as routine oncological clinical practice and hospital visits respected specific ICI’s cycle, once every two weeks (q2w) or every three weeks (q3w) or as needed. Baseline demographic characteristics of NSCLC patients, clinical features, such as co-medication or treated infections during ICIs and treatment data, such as type of ICI, response to treatment were documented using registered data. ICI’s efficacy was evaluated by imaging techniques following response evaluation criteria in solid tumors (RECIST) v1.1134. We identified irAEs in the entire cohort and divided immune toxicities in two groups, endocrine-irAEs and other-irAEs, respectively. Endocrine-irAEs were defined as any occurring autoimmune endocrinopathies during ICIs, such as thyroiditis, hypophysitis, primary adrenal insufficiency and Diabetes Mellitus. Other-irAEs were defined as all other encountering toxicity during ICIs and caused by ICI, such as hepatitis, colitis, myocarditis, myositis, nephritis or hemolytic anemia. All irAEs were diagnosed, graded, treated and followed by the hospital endocrinologist or organ-specific specialist, respectively, in multidisciplinary teams. For grading irAEs, we referred to Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) v5.0127. Management of all irAEs followed international guidelines128,129.

Secondly, the research study focused on investigating basic hematologic biomarkers in the cohort of NSCLC patients experiencing irAEs, comprising fifty cancer patients. Hematologic biomarkers, meaning ANC, ALC, and AEC values were collected for the entire cohort at baseline and for lung cancer patients experiencing irAEs at three different moments in time, as following: Time 1 represented the baseline hematological evaluation before ICI’s initiation, Time 2 meant the assessment at the onset of irAEs and Time 3 signified the last available hematological blood sampling. Hospital laboratory’s references range for ANC were 2.2-5*103/µL, 1.3-3*103/µL for ALC and 0-0.4*103/µL for AEC, respectively. Neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) was calculated using registered data.

The main endpoint of the research was to investigate the power of hematologic biomarkers to predict endocrine-irAEs in heavily-treated real-life lung cancer patients. The secondary endpoints of the study were to evaluate the influence of particular clinical conditions with potential impact on ICI’s response, explore which baseline and treatment characteristics affect the absolute values of hematologic biomarkers and their evolution in patients with endocrine-irAEs. Since our study reported findings from a small cohort of NSCLC patients treated with ICIs and concomitantly experiencing irAEs, we developed further research into up-to-date literature to better integrate our outcomes. Additionally, we delve into recently published literature and structure it using a hierarchical clustering technique, to convene our study results.

Statistical methods

For statistical analyses, JASP Version 0.19.3 was used (JASP Team (2024). JASP (Version 0.19.3)135. Categorical variables were presented as number of patients and frequencies (percentages), while continuous variables were presented as mean and standard deviation and median and interquartile range (IQR).

We performed univariate logistic regressions to assess if hematological predictors influenced the appearance of irAEs and Endocrine-irAEs, the response to ICI treatment, steroid administration and infections during immunotherapy. Odds ratios were calculated. In order to improve the accuracy of odds ratio confidence intervals, we employed an empirical bootstrapped method of 10,000 trials.

We further used Mann-Whitney U-tests to determine whether tumor histopathology, PD-L1 value, prior treatment and administering immunotherapy as first line for advanced disease influence the distribution of hematological markers absolute values.

In patients with irAEs, NLR, ANC, ALC and AEC, values were compared at three different moments in time during ICI therapy, T1, T2 and T3, as described above, using the Friedman test for repeated measures. Post-hoc Conover tests were run, where appropriate.

We did not adjust for multiple comparisons in order to control the probability of type II error, taking into account the exploratory nature of our study. A p-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Clustering method

We performed research of literature using databases such as PubMed, Google Scholar, Scopus, for articles published in the last five years, operating pre-specified keywords in March 2025. “Neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio”, “neutrophil-to-eosinophil ratio”, “absolute neutrophil count”, “absolute lymphocyte count”, “absolute eosinophil count”, “lung cancer”, “hematologic predictive biomarker”, “immunotherapy”, “immune check-point inhibitors” were employed as keywords in the abovementioned search engines. Supplementary references were extracted from the returned articles. As this study was not designed as a systematic review, PRISMA principles were not applicable. From a total of 212 references, we excluded research investigating pre-specified hematologic biomarkers in other sites than lung cancer and duplicate records. The remaining 83 articles were screened for eligibility by title and abstract by three independent authors. For each study, we extracted the title and the abstract as input for the clustering technique. To reduce data clutter, we did not used the full version of the articles in clustering technique.

Hierarchical clustering represents a family of cluster analysis methods that seek to build a hierarchy of clusters, also known as dendrogram. We employ agglomerative clustering, which is a bottom-up hierarchical clustering method where each point starts as its own cluster. The, the algorithm repeatedly merges the two most similar clusters based on a chosen linkage rule. The algorithm is executed until all points are merged into one big cluster. The result of this process is a dendrogram, a tree-like structure representing the nested sequence of cluster merges. For the criterion used to choose which clusters to merge at every step, we opt for Ward linkage. In Ward linkage, the two clusters chosen to be merged are those that result in the minimum increase in total within-cluster variance after merging. The clustering algorithm is usually applied on data samples represented as vectors from . To represent the articles as vectors, we alternatively employ two representations, one based on TF-IDF and one based on BERT42. TF-IDF stands for Term Frequency–Inverse Document Frequency, which represents is a traditional method for representing text as numerical vectors. It works by assigning a weight to each word, based on how frequently it appears in a document (term frequency) and how rare it occurs across all documents in a corpus (inverse document frequency). The intuition behind TF-IDF is that common words like “the” or “and” get lower weights, while more informative, rare words get higher weights. The resulting representation is a sparse, high-dimensional vector, where each dimension corresponds to a term in the vocabulary. Although simple and effective in many classical information retrieval tasks, TF-IDF treats words as independent tokens and does not capture word order or semantic relationships.

BERT stands for Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers42, a modern language representation model based on deep learning. BERT generates dense embeddings by using a transformer architecture trained on massive text corpora, in a self-supervised manner. Unlike TF-IDF, BERT considers the surrounding words in both directions, enabling it to understand the meaning of words in context. This enables the model to capture complex linguistic patterns such as syntax and semantics. This usually results in representations that are far richer and more informative, supporting various natural language processing tasks, including question answering, sentiment analysis, and text similarity. The latter task is relevant to our agglomerative clustering technique, which requires computing the similarity between articles. To generate the BERT-based representation of an article, its title and abstract are concatenated with a space and provided as input to the pre-trained BERT base model. We then extract the [CLS] token returned by the last transformer block, which gives a 768-dimensional vector for each scientific article in the database.

We emphasize that the main advantage of BERT over TF-IDF is its ability to understand context and semantics, making it far more powerful for downstream natural language processing applications. Nevertheless, we employed both representations and provided the resulting dendrograms to physicians for manual inspection. The physicians unanimously voted for the dendrogram based on the BERT-based representation, as it provided a more interpretable clustering of the scientific articles.

The dendrogram is comprised of two axes. The horizontal X-axis represents the distance or dissimilarity between different clusters of articles. From 8 to 0 along the X-axis, the similarity between clusters increases, which means that the AI method has found deep similarities between articles. The vertical Y-axis represents the data points (articles) in our dendrogram. Each data point is positioned vertically based on its cluster or subgroup. Clusters: articles that are close to each other in the dendrogram are more similar with each other, while articles that are farther apart are less similar. The clusters formed in the dendrogram indicate groups of articles with similar characteristics or attributes. In the dendrogram, the references are arranged in a descending order from top to bottom.

The dendrogram exposed different systemic and inflammatory indexes, such as: SII, systemic immune-inflammation index [SII = neutrophil count (109/L) × platelet count (109/L) / lymphocyte count (109/L)]; LIPI, lung immune prognostic index [derived neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (dNLR) and lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) levels]; II, inflammatory index (calculated based upon NLR, PLR, SII); Nutritional Index, calculated based on PNI and HALP (hemoglobin, albumin, ALC, APC); CPLAN, index based on C-reactive protein, performance status, lactate dehydrogenase, albumin, and derived neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio.

Finally, the three independent physicians reviewed all 83 abstracts, according to the clusters offered by hierarchical clustering technique, to deeper correlate and integrate the similarities between them. The final dendrogram represents the joint venture among human and artificial intelligence, as it combines cluster annotations from experienced physicians with a hierarchical organization of clusters provided by an AI-based method.
