Complete entanglement detection using polynomial invariants
Thomas C. Fraser, Vjosa Blakaj, Roberto Rubboli, Felix Huber, Marco Fanizza

Abstract
Existing methods for deciding whether a bipartite quantum state is separable or entangled typically fall into one of two categories: they are either complete but require access to an explicit density matrix followed by numerical optimization, or they can be evaluated directly by measuring the quantum system but are incomplete, in the sense that they cannot detect all forms of entanglement. In this work, we overcome both limitations in a unified framework. First, we bypass numerical optimization by deriving separability criteria in the form of universal bounds on tensor powers of separable states. We prove that these bounds are complete: every entangled state violates them for sufficiently large tensor powers. Second, we explicitly construct a corresponding complete family of nonlinear entanglement witnesses, which can detect all forms of entanglement without requiring an explicit density matrix. The witnesses we construct are moreover basis-independent, in the sense that they are invariant under conjugation by local unitaries. Altogether, our results expand the toolbox for entanglement detection in arbitrary local dimensions in a manifestly invariant way.



keywords
entanglement, separability, non-linear witnesses, local-unitary invariants

Introduction

Determining whether a quantum state
\varrho_{AB}
is entangled or separable is a fundamental task in quantum theory.
Despite being an intrinsically challenging task, from both complexity-theoretic
Gurvits 2004
and geometric perspectives
Aubrun and Szarek 2015
,
today there exist a myriad of techniques for certifying entanglement in bipartite systems.
When an explicit density matrix
\varrho_{AB}
is available, entanglement can be certified, for example, using the PPT criterion
Peres 1996
or
k
-symmetric-extension criteria

. They give rise to complete hierarchies of feasibility problems that can, in principle, be tackled using semidefinite programming
Doherty et al. 2004
;
Navascués et al. 2009
;
Harrow et al. 2017
;
Pena et al. 2025
.
On the other hand, when provided with a physical quantum state shared between two subsystems, as in an experimental setting, approaches based on linear and nonlinear entanglement witnesses are more suitable

;
Chruściński and Sarbicki 2014
;
Rico and Huber 2024
;
Elben et al. 2020
;
Neven et al. 2021
;
Miller and Eisert 2026
;
Gulati et al. 2024

Throughout this paper, we denote by
{\mathrm{SEP}(A\!:\!B)}
the set of separable states on a bipartite Hilbert space
A\otimes B
of finite dimension
d_{AB}=\dim(A\otimes B)
.
A
(polynomial) entanglement witness
is a polynomial
p(\varrho_{AB})
of degree
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
specified by a Hermitian observable
W_{A^{\ell}B^{\ell}}
on
\ell
-copies of the bipartite quantum system, whose expectation value

p(\varrho_{AB})=\Tr(W_{A^{\ell}B^{\ell}}\varrho_{AB}^{\otimes\ell}),

(1)

is a polynomial in
\varrho_{AB}
that is non-negative on all separable states:

\forall\varrho_{AB}\in{\mathrm{SEP}(A\!:\!B)}\implies p(\varrho_{AB})\geq 0.

(2)

If
p(\varrho_{AB})<0
holds for some bipartite state
\varrho_{AB}
, then
\varrho_{AB}
is entangled.
In addition to being naturally more probative than linear entanglement witnesses, a polynomial entanglement witness can have the additional desirable property of being local-unitary invariant, i.e., for all unitaries
U_{A}
on
A
and
V_{B}
on
B
,

p(\varrho_{AB})=p((U_{A}\otimes V_{B})\varrho_{AB}(U_{A}^{\dagger}\otimes V_{B}^{\dagger})).

(3)

Since the entanglement of a state

is unchanged under local reversible operations, the property of being local-unitary invariant is a minimal prerequisite for any sensible measure of entanglement

;
Leifer et al. 2004
;
Schmid et al. 2023
.

A set of entanglement witnesses,
\mathcal{P}
, is said to be
complete
for local dimensions
(d_{A},d_{B})
if for every entangled state
\varrho_{AB}
, there exists some witness
p\in\mathcal{P}

\varrho_{AB}
, i.e.,
p(\varrho_{AB})<0
.
For example, for local dimensions
(d_{A},d_{B})

d_{A}d_{B}\leq 6
, the PPT criterion

is both a necessary and sufficient criteria for separability

;
Neven et al. 2021
;
Miller and Eisert 2026
.

Finding a complete collection of entanglement witnesses for quantum systems of larger dimensions has remained an open challenge.
In this work, we address this fundamental limitation by developing a complete family of entanglement witnesses for any finite local dimensions
(d_{A},d_{B})
.

1.1
Contributions

This paper provides characterizations of the set of separable states, formulated both as a family of operator bounds on tensor powers and as a family of polynomial entanglement witnesses.
These characterizations are complete, in the sense that every entangled state violates at least one bound in the family.

Our central contribution is to identify a specific probability measure
\mu
on the set of separable states
{\mathrm{SEP}(A\!:\!B)}
such that, for each positive integer

, the corresponding de Finetti state

\Omega_{A^{n}B^{n}}=\int_{\mathrm{SEP}}\!\!d\mu(\sigma_{AB})\sigma_{AB}^{\otimes n}

(4)

serves as a universal upper bound on tensor powers of separable states. More precisely, for every

\varrho_{AB}\in{\mathrm{SEP}(A\!:\!B)}

\varrho_{AB}^{\otimes n}
is bounded from above by

and

.
Throughout this paper, operator inequalities are understood in the Loewner order, i.e.,

Theorem 1
.

If

\varrho_{AB}^{\otimes n}\preceq f_{AB}(n)\Omega_{A^{n}B^{n}},

where

The immediate application of

For this purpose, we prove a slightly stronger statement than

such that, for all

\varrho_{AB}^{\otimes n}\preceq\Lambda_{A^{n}B^{n}}\preceq f_{AB}(n)\Omega_{A^{n}B^{n}}

Another application of

-copies of separable states.
In particular,

Corollary 2

Let

\Tr[M_{A^{n}B^{n}}\Omega_{A^{n}B^{n}}]\leq\max_{\sigma_{AB}\in\mathrm{SEP}}\Tr[M_{A^{n}B^{n}}\sigma_{AB}^{\otimes n}]\leq f_{AB}(n)\Tr[M_{A^{n}B^{n}}\Omega_{A^{n}B^{n}}].

From the perspective of entanglement detection,

Using this operational perspective, together with techniques from quantum state tomography, we also prove the

In other words, if a state

satisfies

In fact, we formulate the converse statement in a quantitative manner using the

G(\varrho_{AB})\coloneqq-\log F_{\mathrm{s}}(\varrho_{AB}),\quad\text{with}\quad F_{\mathrm{s}}(\varrho_{AB})\coloneqq\max_{\sigma_{AB}\in\mathrm{SEP}}F(\varrho_{AB},\sigma_{AB}),

(7)

where
F(\varrho,\sigma)\coloneqq\Tr(\left|\sqrt{\varrho}\sqrt{\sigma}\right|)^{2}
is the (squared) fidelity between
\varrho
and
\sigma
.
The quantity
G(\varrho_{AB})
is faithful, in the sense that
G(\varrho_{AB})\geq 0
for all states
\varrho_{AB}
, and
G(\varrho_{AB})=0
if and only if
\varrho_{AB}\in{\mathrm{SEP}(A\!:\!B)}
. We
now state our quantitative converse to
1

Theorem 3
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
be such that

and suppose that

satisfies
\varrho_{AB}^{\otimes n}\preceq f_{AB}(n)\Omega_{A^{n}B^{n}}

where

, and

This result shows that the operator inequalities in

Indeed, since

Hence, if

.
In other words,
3
states that every entangled state
\varrho_{AB}
must violate the criterion in
1

\varrho_{AB}^{\otimes n}\not\preceq f_{AB}(n)\Omega_{A^{n}B^{n}}
for all sufficiently large

Building on this complete characterization of
\mathrm{SEP}(A\!:\!B)
in terms of operator inequalities, we additionally construct a countable family of polynomial entanglement witnesses that is complete.
These entanglement witnesses arise from a technical lemma,

We additionally show that

(10)

An explicit formula for

, is given in

Moreover, we show that these polynomial entanglement witnesses are local-unitary invariant, thereby respecting a fundamental symmetry of the set of separable states.

Using these entanglement witnesses, we obtain the following complete characterization of

:

Corollary 4
.

A state
\varrho_{AB}
is separable if and only if, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and all

p_{n,m}(\varrho_{AB})\geq 0.

(11)

The advantage of

In principle,

, can only be maximized when the components of
\kappa=(k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{r})
differ by at most one, i.e.,
\left|k_{i}-k_{j}\right|\leq 1
.
In turn, this implies that
D_{H}(\kappa)
is maximized when each block has size
k_{i}\in\{\lfloor\frac{n}{r}\rfloor,\lceil\frac{n}{r}\rceil\}
, and therefore

\max_{\kappa\in\mathcal{C}(n,r)}D_{H}(\kappa)\leq D_{H}\left(\left\lceil\frac{n}{r}\right\rceil\right)^{r}.

(33)

∎

2.4
Likelihood-ratios and state fidelity

Essential to the proof of
3
is the idea that one can always distinguish
n
-copies of a state
\varrho
from
n
-copies of any different state
\sigma\neq\varrho
by performing suitable measurements.
Specifically, there must exist sequences of measurement effects
M_{n}\succeq 0
such that the probability
\Tr(M_{n}\varrho^{\otimes n})
is large, while the probability
\Tr(M_{n}\sigma^{\otimes n})
is small whenever
\sigma\neq\varrho
.
To quantify this distinction, we consider the associated likelihood-ratio.

Definition 4
.

Given
\varrho,\sigma\in\mathcal{S}(H)
and measurement effect
0\preceq M_{n}\preceq I_{H}^{\otimes n}
acting on
H^{\otimes n}
, we define the
likelihood-ratio
by

\mathcal{R}_{\sigma/\varrho}(M_{n})\coloneqq\frac{\Tr(M_{n}\sigma^{\otimes n})}{\Tr(M_{n}\varrho^{\otimes n})}.

(34)

To quantify the difference between two quantum states
\varrho,\sigma\in\mathcal{S}(H)
, we use the quantum state fidelity.

Definition 5
.

The fidelity between two quantum states
\varrho,\sigma\in\mathcal{S}(H)
is defined by

F(\varrho,\sigma)\coloneqq\Tr(\left|\sqrt{\varrho}\sqrt{\sigma}\right|)^{2}.

(35)

In general,
0\leq F(\varrho,\sigma)\leq 1
with

F(\varrho,\sigma)=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\varrho=\sigma.

(36)

For our purposes, we will use the following likelihood-ratio bound in terms of the fidelity between
\varrho
and
\sigma

Fraser 2022
.

Lemma 7
.

Let
\varrho\in\mathcal{S}(H)
be a
d_{H}
-dimensional quantum state.
Then, there exists a sequence of measurements
M_{n}\succeq 0
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}(H)

\mathcal{R}_{\sigma/\varrho}(M_{n})=\frac{\Tr(M_{n}\sigma^{\otimes n})}{\Tr(M_{n}\varrho^{\otimes n})}\leq C(\varrho)^{-1}F(\varrho,\sigma)^{n-d_{H}^{2}},

(37)

where
C(\varrho)\in(0,1]
is given by

C(\varrho)\coloneqq x_{1}^{s}x_{2}^{s-1}\cdots x_{s-1}^{2}x_{s},

(38)

with
(x_{1}\geq\cdots\geq x_{d_{H}})
being the eigenvalues of
\varrho
, and
s=\mathrm{rank}(\varrho)
.

Proof.

An explicit construction for the sequence of measurements
M_{n}\succeq 0
which satisfies the claim was given in
(
Fraser 2022
, Appendix J, Theorem 15)
.
The proof is also reproduced in
Section

9
, with minor improvements.
∎

The statement of
7
admits the following interpretation. If
\sigma=\varrho
, then
\mathcal{R}_{\varrho/\varrho}(M_{n})=1
and
F(\varrho,\varrho)=1
, so the bound is trivial because
C(\varrho)^{-1}\geq 1
.
On the other hand, if
\sigma\neq\varrho
, then
F(\varrho,\sigma)<1
, and
7
shows that the likelihood ratio
\mathcal{R}_{\sigma/\varrho}(M_{n})
decays to zero as
n\to\infty
at an exponential rate determined by
-\log F(\varrho,\sigma)
. Beyond this exponential decay, the essential feature of the
7
is that the measurements
M_{n}
are
independent
of
\sigma
.

3
Complete entanglement detection

3.1
Random separable states

This section introduces a probability measure
\mu
on the set of separable states
{\mathrm{SEP}(A\!:\!B)}
. We later obtain an analytic formula for the associated de Finetti state
\Omega_{A^{n}B^{n}}
; see
8
. The state is given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Omega_{A^{n}B^{n}}&=\int d\mu(\sigma_{AB})\sigma_{AB}^{\otimes n}.\end{split}

(39)

To define the measure
\mu
, we introduce an auxiliary register Hilbert space
R
with dimension

r=d_{R}=d_{AB}^{2}.

(40)

The purpose of this register is to index the summands in the separable decomposition of a generic separable state
\varrho_{AB}
.

Now let
\ket{\alpha_{1}}_{A},\ldots,\ket{\alpha_{r}}_{A}\in A
,
\ket{\beta_{1}}_{B},\ldots,\ket{\beta_{r}}_{B}\in B
, and
\ket{\gamma}_{R}\in R
be independent uniformly random pure states in their respective Hilbert spaces.
Using these random pure states, the measure
\mu
is induced by constructing the following separable state
\sigma_{AB}
according to the formula

\sigma_{AB}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left|\braket{i|\gamma}_{R}\right|^{2}\ket{\alpha_{i}}\hskip-3.99994pt\bra{\alpha_{i}}_{A}\otimes\ket{\beta_{i}}\hskip-3.99994pt\bra{\beta_{i}}_{B},

(41)

where the weights
p_{i}=\left|\braket{i|\gamma}_{R}\right|^{2}
are given by the squared coordinates of the random pure state
\ket{\gamma}\in R
taken with respect to any orthonormal basis
\{\ket{1}_{R},\ldots,\ket{r}_{R}\}
for
R=\mathbb{C}^{r}
.
This choice of weights is equivalent to letting the distribution
p=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{r})
be distributed according to the uniform Dirichlet measure
Życzkowski and Sommers 2001
.

As we shall demonstrate momentarily, this construction gives rise to a tractable formula for the average
n
-th tensor power of
\sigma_{AB}
distributed according to
\mu
, analogous to
Eq.

24
.
To state our result, first recall from the multinomial expansion formula (
5
), that
\mathcal{C}(n,r)
denotes the set of weak compositions
\kappa=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
of
n
(
Eq.

19
), and
\mathcal{T}_{S_{n}}
denotes the symmetric group twirling operator (
Eq.

17
).

Lemma 8
.

The de Finetti state
\Omega_{A^{n}B^{n}}
in
Eq.

39

admits the representation

\Omega_{A^{n}B^{n}}=\frac{1}{D_{R}(n)}\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal{C}(n,r)}\mathcal{T}_{S_{n}}\left(\frac{\Pi_{\mathrm{Sym}^{\kappa}(A)}}{D_{A}(\kappa)}\otimes\frac{\Pi_{\mathrm{Sym}^{\kappa}(B)}}{D_{B}(\kappa)}\right),

(42)

where
D_{H}(\kappa)
is the dimension of the block-wise symmetric subspace
\mathrm{Sym}^{\kappa}(H)
of a Hilbert space
H
and
r=d_{R}=d_{AB}^{2}
.

Proof.

First apply the multinomial expansion from
5
to the integrand in
Eq.

39
, with
p_{i}=\left|\braket{i|\gamma}_{R}\right|^{2}
and
\sigma_{i}=\ket{\alpha_{i}}\hskip-3.99994pt\bra{\alpha_{i}}_{A}\otimes\ket{\beta_{i}}\hskip-3.99994pt\bra{\beta_{i}}_{B}
, to obtain

\Omega_{A^{n}B^{n}}=\int d\gamma\int\prod_{i=1}^{r}d\alpha_{i}d\beta_{i}\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal{C}(n,r)}M_{p}(\kappa)\mathcal{T}_{S_{n}}\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{r}\ket{\alpha_{i}}\hskip-3.99994pt\bra{\alpha_{i}}_{A}^{\otimes k_{i}}\otimes\ket{\beta_{i}}\hskip-3.99994pt\bra{\beta_{i}}_{B}^{\otimes k_{i}}\right).\\

(43)

The claimed formula follows by evaluating this integral through repeated applications of
Eq.

24
.
The resulting expression involves integrals over the pure states
\ket{\gamma}_{R}
,
\ket{\alpha_{i}}_{A}
, and
\ket{\beta_{i}}_{B}
, which we address separately.
For each
\kappa=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})\in\mathcal{C}(n,r)
and each
i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
, the integral over
\ket{\alpha_{i}}_{A}
of
\ket{\alpha_{i}}\hskip-3.99994pt\bra{\alpha_{i}}_{A}^{\otimes k_{i}}
is a direct application of
Eq.

24
, yielding the term
\frac{\Pi_{\mathrm{Sym}^{k_{i}}(A)}}{D_{A}(k_{i})}
.
The same argument applies to the integrals over
\ket{\beta_{i}}_{B}
, giving
\frac{\Pi_{\mathrm{Sym}^{k_{i}}(B)}}{D_{B}(k_{i})}
.
It remains to evaluate the integral over
\ket{\gamma}_{R}
of the multinomial distribution
M_{p}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
, with probability distribution
p_{i}=\left|\braket{i|\gamma}_{R}\right|^{2}
. For this, consider the state

\ket{\Psi_{k}}_{R^{n}}\coloneqq\ket{1}_{R}^{\otimes k_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\ket{r}_{R}^{\otimes k_{r}}\in R^{\otimes n},

(44)

so that

p_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots p_{r}^{k_{r}}=\left|\braket{\Psi_{k}|\gamma}^{\otimes n}_{R}\right|^{2}.

(45)

Although
\ket{\Psi_{k}}
is not, in general,
S_{n}
-symmetric, it is invariant under
S_{k_{1}}\times\cdots\times S_{k_{r}}
.
Further, as
\{\ket{1}_{R},\ldots,\ket{r}_{R}\}\subseteq R
was assumed orthonormal, we obtain

\bra{\Psi_{k}}\Pi_{\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(R)}\ket{\Psi_{k}}_{R^{n}}=\frac{k_{1}!\cdots k_{r}!}{n!}.

(46)

Using this, we compute the integral over
\ket{\gamma}_{R}
and obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int d\gamma M_{p}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})&=\frac{n!}{k_{1}!\cdots k_{r}!}\int d\gamma p_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots p_{r}^{k_{r}}\\
&=\frac{n!}{k_{1}!\cdots k_{r}!}\int d\gamma\bra{\Psi_{k}}(\ket{\gamma}\hskip-3.99994pt\bra{\gamma}_{R})^{\otimes n}\ket{\Psi_{k}}\\
&=\frac{n!}{k_{1}!\cdots k_{r}!}\bra{\Psi_{k}}\frac{\Pi_{(n)}^{r}}{D_{R}(n)}\ket{\Psi_{k}}\\
&=\frac{1}{D_{R}(n)},\end{split}

(47)

which completes the proof.
∎

3.2
Separable state inequalities

We now prove
1
, which establishes a universal operator upper bound on
\varrho_{AB}^{\otimes n}
for all separable states
\varrho_{AB}\in{\mathrm{SEP}(A\!:\!B)}
and all positive integers
n
.

To illustrate our technique, we begin by describing how the method works for the case of
pure
product states
\varrho_{AB}=\ket{\Psi}\hskip-3.99994pt\bra{\Psi}_{AB}
where
\ket{\Psi}_{AB}=\ket{\alpha}_{A}\otimes\ket{\beta}_{B}
.
The core intuition of our approach is to notice that pure product states
\ket{\Psi}_{AB}=\ket{\psi}_{A}\otimes\ket{\phi}_{B}
have more
symmetry
than pure entangled states;
specifically, while the
n
-th tensor power of a pure state,
\ket{\Psi}_{AB}^{\otimes n}
, lies in the symmetric subspace
\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(A\otimes B)
, the
n
-th tensor power of a pure
product
state,
\ket{\Psi}_{AB}^{\otimes n}=\ket{\alpha}_{A}^{\otimes n}\otimes\ket{\beta}_{B}^{\otimes n}
, lies in the smaller
locally-symmetric subspace

\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(A)\otimes\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(B)
.

This extra symmetry of pure product states can be encoded into an inequality as follows.

First, let
\Pi_{\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(H)}
denote the projection operator onto the symmetric subspace
\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(H)
, so that
\Pi_{\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(A)}\otimes\Pi_{\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(B)}
denotes the projection operator onto the corresponding locally-symmetric subspace,
\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(A)\otimes\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(B)
.
Then, the vector
\ket{\Psi}_{AB}^{\otimes n}
lies in the locally-symmetric subspace if and only if for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\ket{\Psi}\hskip-3.99994pt\bra{\Psi}_{AB}^{\otimes n}\preceq\Pi_{\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(A)}\otimes\Pi_{\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(B)}.

(48)

Note that for this case, it actually suffices to check
n=2
.

To extend this intuition from pure states
\ket{\Psi}_{AB}
to mixed states
\varrho_{AB}
, we use the multinomial expansion given by
5
and obtain the following result.

Theorem 9
.

If
\varrho_{AB}\in{\mathrm{SEP}(A\!:\!B)}
, then for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\varrho_{AB}^{\otimes n}\preceq\Lambda_{A^{n}B^{n}},

(49)

where
\Lambda_{A^{n}B^{n}}
is given by

\Lambda_{A^{n}B^{n}}=\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal{C}(n,r)}\mathcal{T}_{S_{n}}\left(\Pi_{\mathrm{Sym}^{\kappa}(A)}\otimes\Pi_{\mathrm{Sym}^{\kappa}(B)}\right),

(50)

with
r=d_{AB}^{2}
.

Proof.

Let
\varrho_{AB}\in{\mathrm{SEP}(A\!:\!B)}
be a separable state with a decomposition as in
Eq.

13
, namely

\varrho_{AB}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}p_{i}\ket{\alpha_{i}}\hskip-3.99994pt\bra{\alpha_{i}}_{A}\otimes\ket{\beta_{i}}\hskip-3.99994pt\bra{\beta_{i}}_{B}.

(51)

By
Eq.

14
, we may take
r=d_{AB}^{2}
without loss of generality.
By the multinomial expansion formula from
5
, the
n
-th tensor power of
\varrho_{AB}
can be written as

\varrho_{AB}^{\otimes n}=\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal{C}(n,r)}M_{p}(\kappa)\mathcal{T}_{S_{n}}\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{r}\ket{\alpha_{i}}\hskip-3.99994pt\bra{\alpha_{i}}_{A}^{\otimes k_{i}}\otimes\ket{\beta_{i}}\hskip-3.99994pt\bra{\beta_{i}}_{B}^{\otimes k_{i}}\right),\\

(52)

where
M_{p}(\kappa)
denotes the multinomial probability associated with the probability vector
p=(p_{1},\dots,p_{r})
. We now use the operator bound

\bigotimes_{i=1}^{r}\ket{\alpha_{i}}\hskip-3.99994pt\bra{\alpha_{i}}_{A}^{\otimes k_{i}}\preceq\bigotimes_{i=1}^{r}\Pi_{\mathrm{Sym}^{k_{i}}(A)}=\Pi_{\mathrm{Sym}^{\kappa}(A)}.

(53)

An analogously bound holds for the for the
\ket{\beta_{i}}_{B}
factors.
Therefore, we obtain

\varrho_{AB}^{\otimes n}\preceq\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal{C}(n,r)}M_{p}(\kappa)\mathcal{T}_{S_{n}}\left(\Pi_{\mathrm{Sym}^{\kappa}(A)}\otimes\Pi_{\mathrm{Sym}^{\kappa}(B)}\right).

(54)

Finally, since the multinomial distribution probability satisfies

M_{p}(\kappa)\leq 1,

(55)

for every
\kappa\in\mathcal{C}(n,r)
, and
r=d_{AB}^{2}
, we obtain the operator upper bound
\Lambda_{A^{n}B^{n}}
as claimed.
∎

Before proceeding, we remark the bound in
9
could be improved further.
For example, in
Eq.

55
we bounded the multinomial probability
M_{p}(\kappa)
by one.
Alternatively, one could also bound
M_{p}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
by the maximum likelihood probability,
M_{q^{*}}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
where
q^{*}=(\frac{k_{1}}{n},\ldots,\frac{k_{r}}{n})
, or even group the weak compositions
\kappa
related by permutation to obtain a tighter version of
9
.

Nevertheless, the operator
\Lambda_{A^{n}B^{n}}
admits a straightforward comparison with the de Finetti operator
\Omega_{A^{n}B^{n}}
from
1
in
Section

3.1
, which is sufficient for our purposes.
By direct comparison of their formulas, we see that
\Lambda_{A^{n}B^{n}}
and
\Omega_{A^{n}B^{n}}
differ only by normalization factors involving dimensions of the symmetric subspaces.

Lemma 10
.

For all
n
, the operators
\Omega_{A^{n}B^{n}}
and
\Lambda_{A^{n}B^{n}}
satisfy

\Omega_{A^{n}B^{n}}\preceq\Lambda_{A^{n}B^{n}}\preceq f_{AB}(n)\Omega_{A^{n}B^{n}},

(56)

where

f_{AB}(n)=D_{R}(n)D_{A}\left(\left\lceil\frac{n}{d_{R}}\right\rceil\right)^{d_{R}}D_{B}\left(\left\lceil\frac{n}{d_{R}}\right\rceil\right)^{d_{R}},

(57)

with
d_{R}=d_{AB}^{2}
and
D_{H}(n)=\dim\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(H)
.

Proof.

These inequalities follow by noting that the definition for
\Lambda_{A^{n}B^{n}}
in
Eq.

50
and the formula for
\Omega_{A^{n}B^{n}}
in
Eq.

42

differ only by factors involving dimensions of symmetric subspaces.
The first inequality holds because each of these dimension factors are at least one, i.e.,
D_{R}(n),D_{A}(\kappa),D_{B}(\kappa)\geq 1
.

For the second inequality,
6
implies, for any
\kappa=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})\in\mathcal{C}(n,r)
, that

D_{H}(\kappa)\leq D_{H}\left(\left\lceil\frac{n}{r}\right\rceil\right)^{r}.

(58)

Therefore, by letting
f_{AB}(n)
be as in
Eq.

57
with
r=d_{R}=d_{AB}^{2}
, we see

\displaystyle\begin{split}f_{AB}(n)\Omega_{A^{n}B^{n}}&=\underbrace{\frac{D_{R}(n)}{D_{R}(n)}}_{=1}\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal{C}(n,r)}\underbrace{\frac{D_{A}\left(\left\lceil\frac{n}{r}\right\rceil\right)^{r}}{D_{A}(\kappa)}}_{\geq 1}\underbrace{\frac{D_{B}\left(\left\lceil\frac{n}{r}\right\rceil\right)^{r}}{D_{B}(\kappa)}}_{\geq 1}\mathcal{T}_{S_{n}}\left(\Pi_{\mathrm{Sym}^{\kappa}(A)}\otimes\Pi_{\mathrm{Sym}^{\kappa}(B)}\right)\\
&\succeq\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal{C}(n,r)}\mathcal{T}_{S_{n}}\left(\Pi_{\mathrm{Sym}^{\kappa}(A)}\otimes\Pi_{\mathrm{Sym}^{\kappa}(B)}\right)=\Lambda_{A^{n}B^{n}}.\end{split}

(59)

This proves the second inequality.
∎

To prove completeness of our criteria in subsequent sections, we will additionally exploit that the prefactor
f_{AB}(n)
in
10
grows at most polynomially in
n
.
To see this clearly, it suffices to consider the following crude bound:

\displaystyle\begin{split}f_{AB}(n)&=D_{R}(n)D_{A}\left(\left\lceil\frac{n}{d_{R}}\right\rceil\right)^{d_{R}}D_{B}\left(\left\lceil\frac{n}{d_{R}}\right\rceil\right)^{d_{R}},\\
&\leq D_{R}(n)D_{A}\left(n\right)^{d_{R}}D_{B}\left(n\right)^{d_{R}},\\
&\leq D_{R}(n)D_{AB}\left(n\right)^{d_{R}},\\
&\leq(n+1)^{d_{R}-1}(n+1)^{d_{R}(d_{AB}-1)},\\
&=(n+1)^{d_{AB}^{3}-1},\end{split}

(60)

where we again use that
d_{R}=d_{AB}^{2}
together with the simple bound
D_{H}(n)\leq(n+1)^{d_{H}-1}

Harrow 2013
;
Christandl et al. 2009
.
Of course, in practice, the prefactor
f_{AB}(n)
is typically smaller than the bound provided by
Eq.

60
.

With
10
established, we now observe that
9
implies
1
, which we restate and prove now.
See
1

Proof.

If
\varrho_{AB}
is separable, then
9
implies
\varrho_{AB}^{\otimes n}\preceq\Lambda_{A^{n}B^{n}}
and therefore
10
implies,

\varrho_{AB}^{\otimes n}\preceq\Lambda_{A^{n}B^{n}}\preceq f_{AB}(n)\Omega_{A^{n}B^{n}}.

(61)

This observation, combined with the bound on
f_{AB}(n)
in
Eq.

60
is the statement of
1
.
∎

3.3
Bounding fidelity of separability

In this section, we establish
3
which provides a bound on the logarithmic fidelity of separability,
G(\varrho_{AB})=-\log F_{s}(\varrho_{AB})
, whenever
\varrho_{AB}
satisfies the operator inequality
\varrho_{AB}^{\otimes n}\leq f_{AB}(n)\Omega_{A^{n}B^{n}}
in
1
.
The proof relies on
7
, which compares the fidelity between any two states
\varrho
and
\sigma
using a sequence of measurement effects
M_{n}
depending only on
\varrho
.
For convenience, we restate
3
below.
See
3

Proof.

For any state
\varrho_{AB}\in\mathcal{S}(A\otimes B)
, by
7
there exists a sequence of measurements
M_{A^{n}B^{n}}\succeq 0
such that, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and all
\sigma_{AB}\in\mathcal{S}(A\otimes B)
,

\mathcal{R}_{\sigma_{AB}/\varrho_{AB}}(M_{A^{n}B^{n}})=\frac{\Tr(M_{A^{n}B^{n}}\sigma_{AB}^{\otimes n})}{\Tr(M_{A^{n}B^{n}}\varrho_{AB}^{\otimes n})}\leq C(\varrho_{AB})^{-1}F(\varrho_{AB},\sigma_{AB})^{n-d_{AB}^{2}},

(62)

where
C(\varrho_{AB})
is the constant defined in
Eq.

38
.
Maximizing over all separable states
\sigma_{AB}\in{\mathrm{SEP}(A\!:\!B)}
gives

\sup_{\sigma_{AB}\in\mathrm{SEP}}\mathcal{R}_{\sigma_{AB}/\varrho_{AB}}(M_{A^{n}B^{n}})\leq C(\varrho_{AB})^{-1}F_{s}(\varrho_{AB})^{n-d_{AB}^{2}},

(63)

where
F_{s}(\varrho_{AB})=\sup_{\sigma_{AB}\in\mathrm{SEP}}F(\varrho_{AB},\sigma_{AB})
is the fidelity of separability of
\varrho_{AB}
.
Now assume that
\varrho_{AB}
satisfies the operator inequality
\varrho_{AB}^{\otimes n}\leq f_{AB}(n)\Omega_{A^{n}B^{n}}
for some
n>d_{AB}^{2}
.
Then, applying the measurement effect
M_{A^{n}B^{n}}
yields

\Tr(M_{A^{n}B^{n}}\varrho_{AB}^{\otimes n})\leq f_{AB}(n)\Tr(M_{A^{n}B^{n}}\Omega_{A^{n}B^{n}})\leq f_{AB}(n)\sup_{\sigma_{AB}\in\mathrm{SEP}}\Tr(M_{A^{n}B^{n}}\sigma_{AB}^{\otimes n}).

(64)

This implies the lower bound

\sup_{\sigma_{AB}\in\mathrm{SEP}}\mathcal{R}_{\sigma_{AB}/\varrho_{AB}}(M_{A^{n}B^{n}})\geq\frac{1}{f_{AB}(n)}.

(65)

Combining these two bounds on the maximum likelihood ratio, and noting
n-d_{AB}^{2}>0
, yields

F_{s}(\varrho_{AB})\geq\left(\frac{1}{C(\varrho_{AB})^{-1}f_{AB}(n)}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-d_{AB}^{2}}},

(66)

and therefore

G(\varrho_{AB})=-\log F_{s}(\varrho_{AB})\leq\frac{\log(C(\varrho_{AB})^{-1}f_{AB}(n))}{n-d_{AB}^{2}}.

(67)

∎

From
3
, we conclude that the criteria in
1
is complete: any state satisfying the tensor-power bound for arbitrarily large
n
must have vanishing logarithmic fidelity of separability, and hence must be separable.

Corollary 11
.

If
\varrho_{AB}\in\mathcal{S}(A\otimes B)
satisfies
\varrho_{AB}^{\otimes n}\preceq f_{AB}(n)\Omega_{A^{n}B^{n}}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then
\varrho_{AB}\in{\mathrm{SEP}(A\!:\!B)}
.

Proof.

By
3
, we have
G(\varrho_{AB})\leq O(\log n/n)
, since the pre-factor
f_{AB}(n)
grows polynomially in
n
by
Eq.

60
.
Taking the limit
n\to\infty
, we obtain
G(\varrho_{AB})=0
. Since
G(\varrho_{AB})
is faithful, this implies that
\varrho_{AB}\in{\mathrm{SEP}(A\!:\!B)}
.
∎

3.4
Entanglement witnesses

The main application of our results is the construction of a complete family of polynomial entanglement witnesses that are, in addition, invariant under local unitaries. This establishes
4
.

We begin by observing that the entanglement criteria provided by
9
are operator inequalities of the form
\varrho_{AB}^{\otimes n}\preceq\Lambda_{A^{n}B^{n}}
, for some operator
\Lambda_{A^{n}B^{n}}
. As such, they are
not
polynomial entanglement witnesses, which instead correspond to polynomial inequalities of the form

p(\varrho_{AB})=\Tr(W_{A^{\ell}B^{\ell}}\varrho_{AB}^{\otimes\ell})\geq 0,

(68)

where
W_{A^{\ell}B^{\ell}}
is a Hermitian operator on
(A\otimes B)^{\otimes\ell}
for some
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
.
However, it is possible to extract polynomial entanglement witnesses from operator inequalities in several ways.
The simplest construction is to take any positive semidefinite operator
M_{A^{n}B^{n}}\succeq 0
on
(A\otimes B)^{\otimes n}
, and then to let

W_{A^{n}B^{n}}=\Tr(M_{A^{n}B^{n}}\Lambda_{A^{n}B^{n}})I_{AB}^{\otimes n}-M_{A^{n}B^{n}}.

(69)

The operator inequality
\varrho_{AB}^{\otimes n}\preceq\Lambda_{A^{n}B^{n}}
for
\varrho_{AB}\in{\mathrm{SEP}(A\!:\!B)}
then implies that
\Tr(W_{A^{n}B^{n}}\varrho_{AB}^{\otimes n})
is non-negative, because

\Tr(W_{A^{n}B^{n}}\varrho_{AB}^{\otimes n})=\Tr(M_{A^{n}B^{n}}(\Lambda_{A^{n}B^{n}}-\varrho_{AB}^{\otimes n}))\geq 0.

(70)

One consequence of
9
and
3
is that
every
entangled state
\varrho_{AB}\not\in{\mathrm{SEP}(A\!:\!B)}
can be detected by a witness of this form, for some sufficiently large
n
and some operator
M_{A^{n}B^{n}}\succeq 0
.
Indeed, it suffices to choose
M_{A^{n}B^{n}}
to be any measurement effect capable of tomographically distinguishing
\varrho_{AB}
from other states
\sigma_{AB}
, for instance, the
\varrho_{AB}
-dependent measurement effects provided by
7
.
For the purposes of
experimental
entanglement detection, however, this approach has a drawback: when
\varrho_{AB}
is unknown, it is unclear which measurement operator
M_{A^{n}B^{n}}
and thus which witnesses
W_{A^{n}B^{n}}
suffices to certify the entanglement of
\varrho_{AB}
.

To address this issue, we adopt a different approach based on Sylvester’s criterion for operator positivity, which leads to a family of entanglement witnesses that is both independent of the state
\varrho_{AB}
and also complete.
This criterion states that an
\ell\times\ell
Hermitian matrix
X
is positive semidefinite,
X\succeq 0
, if and only if, for every
m\in\{1,\ldots,\ell\}
the polynomial

E_{m}(X)=\Tr(P_{\wedge^{m}\mathbb{C}^{\ell}}X^{\otimes m})

(71)

is nonnegative,
E_{m}(X)\geq 0
. Here,
P_{\wedge^{m}\mathbb{C}^{\ell}}

denotes the projection onto the
antisymmetric
subspace
\wedge^{m}\mathbb{C}^{\ell}
of
(\mathbb{C}^{\ell})^{\otimes m}
(see
17
in
Section

6
for a derivation).

The key to converting the operator inequality in
9
into polynomial entanglement witnesses is to evaluate
E_{m}
on
X=\Lambda_{A^{n}B^{n}}-\varrho_{AB}^{\otimes n}
. Thus, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
m\in\{1,\ldots,d_{AB}^{n}\}
, we define the degree-
nm
polynomial

p_{n,m}(\varrho_{AB})\coloneqq E_{m}(\Lambda_{A^{n}B^{n}}-\varrho_{AB}^{\otimes n}).

(72)

Since
E_{m}(\Lambda_{A^{n}B^{n}}-\varrho_{AB}^{\otimes n})
is a degree-
nm
polynomial in the coefficients of
\varrho_{AB}
, there necessarily exists a Hermitian operator
W_{A^{nm}B^{nm}}^{n,m}
on
(A\otimes B)^{\otimes nm}
, independent of
\varrho_{AB}
, such that for all
\varrho_{AB}
we have,

p_{n,m}(\varrho_{AB})=\Tr(W_{A^{nm}B^{nm}}^{n,m}\varrho_{AB}^{\otimes nm}).

(73)

For an explicit formula for
W_{A^{nm}B^{nm}}^{n,m}
as a function of
\Lambda_{A^{n}B^{n}}
and
m
, we refer the reader to

18
in
Section

6
.
This method of converting operator inequalities into polynomial inequalities is inspired by its application to
X=\varrho_{AB}^{T_{B}}
, where it is used to characterize PPT-ness via polynomial entanglement witnesses
Elben et al. 2020
;
Neven et al. 2021
.

In any case, we obtain another characterization of
{\mathrm{SEP}(A\!:\!B)}
, now in terms of a countable family of polynomial entanglement witnesses.

Corollary 12
.

A state
\varrho_{AB}
belongs to
{\mathrm{SEP}(A\!:\!B)}
if and only if, for all
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
with
m\leq d_{AB}^{n}
,

p_{n,m}(\varrho_{AB})\geq 0,

(74)

where
p_{n,m}(\varrho_{AB})=E_{m}(\Lambda_{A^{n}B^{n}}-\varrho_{AB}^{\otimes n})
.

Moreover, the polynomial entanglement witnesses in
12
are local-unitary invariant. Indeed, this follows from the fact that the operator
\Lambda_{A^{n}B^{n}}
from
9
is invariant under local unitaries, and the polynomial
E_{m}
is
U(d_{AB})
-invariant.

4
Discussion

In this work, we provided a systematic characterization of the set of separable states in arbitrary local dimensions
(d_{A},d_{B})
, both in terms of operator inequalities (
9
) and in terms of entanglement witnesses (
12
). We now address some of the advantages and limitations of our results, and outline directions for future investigation.

First and foremost,
12
involves a countably-infinite family of polynomials of arbitrarily high degree in
\varrho_{AB}
.
As there are intrinsic difficulties in evaluating high-degree polynomials or in measuring many copies of a quantum state, evaluating the entanglement witnesses in
12
, or the operator inequalities in
9
, becomes infeasible for larger and larger values of
n
.
Indeed, from the definition of
\Lambda_{A^{n}B^{n}}
in
Eq.

50
it can be seen that the operator inequality in
9
becomes a
trivial
inequality when
n\leq d_{AB}^{2}
, which implies that the number of copies
n
must always be taken larger than the separable rank
r
which, in turn, can be as large as
d_{AB}^{2}
.
4
4

4

If
n,r\in\mathbb{N}
are such that
n\leq r
then
\mathcal{C}(n,r)
contains the weak composition
\kappa=(\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{n},\underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{r-n})
for which
\mathrm{Sym}^{\kappa}(H)\equiv H^{\otimes n}
and then
\Lambda_{A^{n}B^{n}}\succeq I_{AB}^{\otimes n}\succeq\varrho_{AB}^{\otimes n}
holds for
all
bipartite states
\varrho_{AB}
.

Consequently, we anticipate that families of low-degree entanglement witnesses, such as in
Miller and Eisert 2026
, will remain more efficient and practically relevant for experimental entanglement detection, despite their incompleteness in higher dimensions.

Fortunately, for fixed dimensions
(d_{A},d_{B})
the set of separable states is
semialgebraic
, meaning the set
{\mathrm{SEP}(A\!:\!B)}
can, in theory, be expressed using a
finite
number of polynomial inequalities in the coefficients of
\varrho_{AB}
.
In other words, for fixed local dimensions
(d_{A},d_{B})
, theoretically there exists a
finite
collection of polynomial entanglement witnesses which is complete for detecting entanglement.
In
Section

5
, we additionally prove that this semialgebraic property of
{\mathrm{SEP}(A\!:\!B)}
extends to the orbit space
{\mathrm{SEP}(A\!:\!B)}/(U(d_{A})\times U(d_{B}))
, meaning that there also exists a finite and complete family of entanglement witnesses consisting entirely of local-unitary invariant
polynomials.
Unfortunately, while algorithms for finding such polynomials exist
Jirstrand 1995
, they are computationally prohibitive – so finding a complete family of entanglement witnesses that is
finite
, even for
(d_{A},d_{B})=(3,3)
or
(2,4)
, remains a challenging open problem.

Third, while we have only stated our results in terms of bipartite separability, our techniques can be readily extended to address the case of
fully-separable
multipartite states.
For example, using the techniques in this paper one can see that a tripartite state
\varrho_{ABC}
is fully-separable,

\varrho_{ABC}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}p_{i}\ket{\alpha_{i}}\hskip-3.99994pt\bra{\alpha_{i}}_{A}\otimes\ket{\beta_{i}}\hskip-3.99994pt\bra{\beta_{i}}_{B}\otimes\ket{\gamma_{i}}\hskip-3.99994pt\bra{\gamma_{i}}_{C},

(75)

with
r=d_{ABC}^{2}
, if and only if, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, it satisfies
\varrho_{ABC}^{\otimes n}\preceq\Lambda_{A^{n}B^{n}C^{n}}
, where

\Lambda_{A^{n}B^{n}C^{n}}=\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal{C}(n,r)}\mathcal{T}_{S_{n}}\left(\Pi_{\mathrm{Sym}^{\kappa}(A)}\otimes\Pi_{\mathrm{Sym}^{\kappa}(B)}\otimes\Pi_{\mathrm{Sym}^{\kappa}(C)}\right).

(76)

While our methods can also be adapted to handle some additionally more nuanced notions of multipartite entanglement such as genuine multipartite entanglement
Dür et al. 1999
;
Dür et al. 2000
;
Gühne and Seevinck 2010
, it is presently unclear how to extend the techniques of this paper to derive an analogous complete characterization for the set of bi-separable states
Gühne and Tóth 2009
, or
k
-partite states which are accessible via local operations, global shared randomness, and
(k-1)
-shared entanglement (LOSRSE) as proposed in
Schmid et al. 2023
5
5

5

Although, a promising approach would be to combine the techniques developed here with both the fully quantum inflation technique
Smith et al. 2026
and the quantum marginal inequalities from
Fraser 2022
.
.

Finally, one may be concerned that the inequalities in
9
and
12
involve
(d_{AB})^{n}
-dimensional matrices, which grow rapidly with increasing
n
.
To address this issue, in
Section

8
, we show how to block-diagonalize
9
into a polynomial number of blocks, of polynomial size, using the
S_{n}
-invariance of
9
.
Moreover,
Section

8
provides a manifestly local-unitary invariant formulation of
9
, in the form of

5
Semialgebraicity of separable states

In
Section

4
, it was mentioned that the set of bipartite separable states for fixed local dimensions, is semialgebraic.
In other words,
{\mathrm{SEP}(A\!:\!B)}
can be described by finitely
many polynomial (in)equalities in the coefficients of
\varrho_{AB}
, viewed as element of the real vector space of local dimension
(d_{A},d_{B})
Hermitian matrices, i.e.,
{\mathrm{SEP}(A\!:\!B)}
can be characterized using finitely many polynomial entanglement witnesses.
This property of
{\mathrm{SEP}(A\!:\!B)}
follows from a combination of Sylvester’s criterion (
Section

6
), Carathéodory’s theorem, and the Tarski-Seidenberg theorem
Basu et al. 2007
.
In this section, we further show that
{\mathrm{SEP}(A\!:\!B)}
admits a semialgebraic description in terms of finitely many local-unitary invariant polynomial entanglement witnesses.

The orbit space

\widetilde{\mathrm{SEP}}(A\!:\!B)\coloneq{\mathrm{SEP}(A\!:\!B)}/\bigl(U(d_{A})\times U(d_{B})\bigr),

(77)

(equipped with the quotient topology), is an
abstract
quotient, and thus it is not
a priori
a subset of some
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
To say that
\widetilde{\mathrm{SEP}}(A\!:\!B)

admits a semialgebraic model

means that there exist an
m\in\mathbb{N}
and a semialgebraic set
Y\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
together with a homeomorphism, such that
\widetilde{\mathrm{SEP}}(A\!:\!B)\ \cong\ Y
.
Although we can show that such a finite characterization exists, we are currently unable
to provide such a finite characterization of
{\mathrm{SEP}(A\!:\!B)}
for all local dimensions
(d_{A},d_{B})
where
d_{A}d_{B}>6
6
6

6

For local dimensions
(d_{A},d_{B})
where
d_{A}d_{B}\leq 6
, the PPT criterion is necessary and sufficient
Horodecki et al. 2001
;
Elben et al. 2020
.
.

Let us start by recalling fundamental results concerning the action of a compact Lie group
K
, on a finite dimensional real vector spaces
W
.
In our setting, we consider the local unitary group
K=U(d_{A})\times U(d_{B})
, acting on the real vector space
W=\mathrm{Herm}(A\!:\!B)
of Hermitian matrices on
\mathbb{C}^{d_{A}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{B}}
by conjugation, i.e., for all
(U,V)\in U(d_{A})\times U(d_{B})
and
\varrho_{AB}\in\mathrm{Herm}(A\!:\!B)

\varrho_{AB}\mapsto(U\otimes V)\varrho_{AB}(U\otimes V)^{\dagger}.

(78)

Note that this action is orthogonal with respect to the Hilbert-Schmidt norm.

In general, for any real vector space
W
and group
K
by
\mathbb{R}[W]
is denoted the ring of real polynomial functions on
W
, and by
\mathbb{R}[W]^{K}
the subring of
K
-invariant polynomial functions on
W
:

\mathbb{R}[W]^{K}=\{f\in\mathbb{R}[W]\mid\forall k\in K,w\in W:f(k\cdot w)=f(w)\}.

(79)

By the results of Hilbert and Hurwitz (see
(
Weyl 1946
, Theorem 8.14.A, p. 274)
) the ring
\mathbb{R}[W]^{K}
is
finitely
generated when
K
is compact.
Let
p_{1},\dots,p_{m}\in\mathbb{R}[W]^{K}
be a finite list of generators.
We define the map
P:W\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
by

\displaystyle P(w)=(p_{1}(w),\dots,p_{m}(w)).

(80)

By the Tarski-Seidenberg theorem
Basu et al. 2007
, the image
X\coloneq\mathrm{im}(P)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
of the polynomial map
P
is semialgebraic and homeomorphic to the quotient
W/K
by the following proposition.

Proposition 13

(
Procesi and Schwarz 1985
, Proposition 0.4)
.

The map
P:W\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is proper and induces a homeomorphism
\bar{P}:W/K\to X
between the quotient
W/K
and its image
X=\mathrm{im}(P)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

Moreover, the image of
P
restricted to any semialgebraic subset
S
of
W
is also semialgebraic by the following.

Proposition 14

(
Blakaj 2025
, Chapter 4)
.

If
S\subseteq W
is a semialgebraic subset of
W
, then its image,
P(S)=\mathrm{im}(P|_{S})
, under a polynomial map
P:W\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is also semialgebraic.

Building on the above, we prove the following.

Theorem 15
.

If
S\subseteq W
is a semialgebraic subset of
W
that is
K
-invariant, then the orbit space
S/K
is homeomorphic to the semialgebraic set
P(S)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

Proof.

Let
\pi_{S}:S\to S/K
be the orbit map restricted to
S
, i.e., for
x\in S
we have
\pi_{S}(x)=K\cdot x=[x]
.
Since each component polynomial of
P=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{m})
is
K
-invariant on
W
,
P
is constant on each
K
-orbit
K\cdot x=[x]
of an element
x\in S
.
From here we obtain a well-defined continuous map
\bar{P}_{S}:S/K\to Y
that is the restriction of the homeomorphism
\bar{P}
from
13
and
Y=P(S)=\mathrm{im}(P|_{S})=\mathrm{im}(\bar{P}_{S})=\mathrm{im}(\bar{P}|_{S/K})
. Finally,
by
14
the set
Y
is semialgebraic, which proves the claim.
∎

In our setting, the set
S={\mathrm{SEP}(A\!:\!B)}
is a
K=U(d_{A})\times U(d_{B})
-invariant semialgebraic subset of
W=\mathrm{Herm}(A\!:\!B)
. Therefore,
15
yields the following result.

Corollary 16
.

The orbit space

\widetilde{\mathrm{SEP}}(A\!:\!B)={\mathrm{SEP}(A\!:\!B)}/(U(d_{A})\times U(d_{B}))

(81)

is homeomorphic to the semialgebraic set
P({\mathrm{SEP}(A\!:\!B)})\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
In particular,
{\mathrm{SEP}(A\!:\!B)}
admits a semialgebraic model consisting entirely of
U(d_{A})\times U(d_{B})
-invariant polynomials.

Sylvester’s criterion

A necessary and sufficient condition for the positive semidefiniteness of a Hermitian matrix is given by the non-negativity of all its principal minors; this is known as Sylvester’s criterion
(
Horn and Johnson 1985
, Theorem 7.2.5 (a))
.
In what follows, we present a well-known variation of this criterion, which we later use to convert operator inequalities into polynomial entanglement witnesses.

Proposition 17
.

Let
X
be an
\ell\times\ell
Hermitian matrix acting on a Hilbert space
L=\mathbb{C}^{\ell}
.
Then,
X\succeq 0
if and only if for all
m\in\{1,\ldots,\ell\}

E_{m}(X)\geq 0,

(82)

where
E_{m}(X)=\Tr(P_{\wedge^{m}L}X^{\otimes m})
and
P_{\wedge^{m}L}
is the projection operator onto the skew-symmetric subspace
\wedge^{m}L\subseteq L^{\otimes m}
.

Note that
E_{m}(X)
is also equivalent to both
(i)
the sum over all principal minors of
X
of degree
m
, and
(ii)
the elementary symmetric polynomial in the eigenvalues of
X
.
We now provide the proof of
17
.

Proof of
17
.

The characteristic polynomial of
X
,

p_{X}(t)=\det(X-tI)=\Tr(P_{\wedge^{\ell}L}(X-tI)^{\otimes\ell}),

(83)

is related to the quantities
E_{m}(X)
for
m\in\{1,\ldots,\ell\}
by the identity

\displaystyle\begin{split}p_{X}(t)&=(-t)^{\ell}+E_{1}(X)(-t)^{\ell-1}+\cdots+E_{\ell-1}(X)(-t)+E_{\ell}(X).\end{split}

(84)

Since the eigenvalues of
X
are the roots of
p_{X}(t)
, the number of strictly negative eigenvalues of
X
is equal to the number of strictly positive roots of the polynomial
q_{X}(t)=p_{X}(-t)
(both counted with multiplicities), where

q_{X}(t)=t^{\ell}+E_{1}(X)t^{\ell-1}+\cdots+E_{\ell-1}(X)t+E_{\ell}(X).

(85)

The assumption that
E_{m}(X)\geq 0
for all
m\in\{1,\ldots,\ell\}
implies that all coefficients of
q_{X}(t)
are non-negative.
Therefore, the number of strictly positive roots of
q_{X}(t)
is zero as
q_{X}(t)>0
, whenever
t>0
.
This establishes that if
E_{m}(X)\geq 0
for all
m\in\{1,\ldots,\ell\}
, then
X\succeq 0
.
The converse statement follows because
X\succeq 0
implies
X^{\otimes m}\succeq 0
, and hence
E_{m}(X)\geq 0
, as
P_{\wedge^{m}L}
is positive semidefinite.
∎

In the main text, we applied
17
to the positive semidefinite operator
X=\Lambda_{A^{n}B^{n}}-\varrho_{AB}^{\otimes n}
from
9
to obtain an polynomial entanglement witnesses
W_{A^{nm}B^{nm}}^{n,m}
for
\varrho_{AB}
as in
Eq.

73
.
The following lemma (after making the substitutions
\ell=d_{AB}^{n}
,
\omega=\varrho_{AB}^{\otimes n}
and
\Lambda=\Lambda_{A^{n}B^{n}}
) provides a formula for computing the witness operator
W_{A^{nm}B^{nm}}^{n,m}
from
\Lambda_{A^{n}B^{n}}
for each value of
m
using

W_{A^{nm}B^{nm}}^{n,m}\coloneqq\Gamma_{m}[\Lambda_{A^{n}B^{n}}].

(86)

Lemma 18
.

Let
\Lambda,\omega
be
\ell\times\ell
Hermitian matrices with
\Tr(\omega)=1
.
Then
\omega\preceq\Lambda
if and only if, for all
m\in\{1,\ldots,\ell\}

\Tr[\Gamma_{m}[\Lambda]\omega^{\otimes m}]\geq 0

(87)

where
\Gamma_{m}[\Lambda]
is the Hermitian operator on
L^{\otimes m}
defined by

\Gamma_{m}[\Lambda]=\sum_{j=0}^{m}(-1)^{j}\binom{m}{j}\Tr_{1,\ldots,m-j}[P_{\wedge^{m}L}(\Lambda^{\otimes m-j}\otimes I^{\otimes j})]\otimes I^{\otimes m-j}.

(88)

Proof.

From Sylvester’s criterion we have
\omega\preceq\Lambda
if and only if for all
m\in\{1,\ldots,\ell\}

E_{m}(\Lambda-\omega)=\Tr(P_{\wedge^{m}L}(\Lambda-\omega)^{\otimes m})\geq 0.

(89)

Now we apply the binomial expansion to
(\Lambda-\omega)^{\otimes m}
and note that
P_{\wedge^{m}L}
is invariant under conjugation by permutations in
S_{m}
so we obtain

\displaystyle E_{m}(\Lambda-\omega)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{m}(-1)^{j}\binom{m}{j}\Tr(P_{\wedge^{m}L}\Lambda^{\otimes m-j}\otimes\omega^{\otimes j}).

(90)

From here we see that the definition of
\Gamma_{m}
in
Eq.

88
ensures

E_{m}(\Lambda-\omega)=\Tr(\Gamma_{m}[\Lambda]\omega^{\otimes m}),

(91)

provided that
\Tr(\omega)=1
.
∎

7
Twirled pinching inequalities

In this section, we establishes an operator inequality relating a positive semidefinite operator
X
to its image
\mathcal{T}_{G}(X)
under
twirling
, or equivalently
pinching
, by a compact group
G
.

Throughout, let
\pi:G\to\mathrm{GL}(H)
be a unitary representation of
G
on a finite-dimensional complex vector space
H=\mathbb{C}^{D}
. We assume that
H
decomposes into irreducible subrepresentations
V_{\lambda}
as

H=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\hat{G}}V_{\lambda}\otimes M_{\lambda},

(92)

with
M_{\lambda}
denoting the corresponding multiplicity spaces.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{G}
denote the twirling map with respect to
G
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{G}(X)

\displaystyle=\int d\mu(g)\pi(g)X\pi(g^{-1}),

(93)

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\hat{G}}\frac{I_{V_{\lambda}}}{d_{\lambda}}\otimes\Tr_{V_{\lambda}}(P_{\lambda}XP_{\lambda}).

(94)

Here
d_{\lambda}=\dim V_{\lambda}
and
P_{\lambda}
denotes the projection onto the
\lambda
-isotypic subspace
V_{\lambda}\otimes M_{\lambda}\subseteq H
.

We now prove an operator bound between any positive semidefinite operator
X\succeq 0
and its image under twirling
\mathcal{T}_{G}(X)
. This bound is related to Hayashi’s pinching inequality
(
Hayashi 2002
, Lemma 9)
, adapted to the subspaces of
H
in
Eq.

92
.

Theorem 19
.

For every positive semidefinite operator
X\succeq 0
, we have

X\preceq\Gamma_{G}\mathcal{T}_{G}(X),

(95)

where

\Gamma_{G}=\sum_{\lambda\in\hat{G}}d_{\lambda}\min\{d_{\lambda},m_{\lambda}\},

(96)

with
d_{\lambda}=\dim V_{\lambda}
and
m_{\lambda}=\dim M_{\lambda}
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove the inequality for rank-one operators
X=\ket{\Psi}\hskip-3.99994pt\bra{\Psi}
, since the general case follows by spectral decomposition: if
X=\sum_{i}x_{i}\ket{\Psi_{i}}\hskip-3.99994pt\bra{\Psi_{i}}
and
x_{i}\geq 0
then the desired inequality for each
\ket{\Psi_{i}}\hskip-3.99994pt\bra{\Psi_{i}}
implies it for
X
.
Let
\ket{\Psi}=\bigoplus_{\lambda}\ket{\psi_{\lambda}}
be the decomposition of
\ket{\Psi}\in H
into isotypic blocks, where
\ket{\psi_{\lambda}}\in V_{\lambda}\otimes M_{\lambda}
and note that

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{G}(\ket{\Psi}\hskip-3.99994pt\bra{\Psi})

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\hat{G}}\frac{I_{V_{\lambda}}}{d_{\lambda}}\otimes\Tr_{V_{\lambda}}(\ket{\psi_{\lambda}}\hskip-3.99994pt\bra{\psi_{\lambda}}).

(97)

Now, for any
\ket{\psi_{\lambda}}\in V_{\lambda}\otimes M_{\lambda}
with Schmidt rank
s\leq\min\{d_{\lambda},m_{\lambda}\}
, one always has

\ket{\psi_{\lambda}}\hskip-3.99994pt\bra{\psi_{\lambda}}\preceq sI_{V_{\lambda}}\otimes\Tr_{V_{\lambda}}(\ket{\psi_{\lambda}}\hskip-3.99994pt\bra{\psi_{\lambda}}).

(98)

Hence

\ket{\psi_{\lambda}}\hskip-3.99994pt\bra{\psi_{\lambda}}\preceq\gamma_{\lambda}\left(\frac{I_{V_{\lambda}}}{d_{\lambda}}\otimes\Tr_{V_{\lambda}}(\ket{\psi_{\lambda}}\hskip-3.99994pt\bra{\psi_{\lambda}})\right),

(99)

where
\gamma_{\lambda}\coloneq d_{\lambda}\min\{d_{\lambda},m_{\lambda}\}
.
Let
\ket{\Phi}\in H
be an arbitrary vector with block decomposition
\ket{\Phi}=\bigoplus_{\lambda}\ket{\phi_{\lambda}}
.
Applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\braket{\Phi,\Psi}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{\lambda}\braket{\phi_{\lambda},\psi_{\lambda}}\right|^{2}

(100)

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{\lambda}\sqrt{\gamma}_{\lambda}\frac{\braket{\phi_{\lambda},\psi_{\lambda}}}{\sqrt{\gamma}_{\lambda}}\right|^{2}

(101)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{\mu}\gamma_{\mu}\right)\left(\sum_{\lambda}\frac{\left|\braket{\phi_{\lambda},\psi_{\lambda}}\right|^{2}}{\gamma_{\lambda}}\right)

(102)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{\mu}\gamma_{\mu}\right)\sum_{\lambda}\bra{\Phi}\left(\frac{I_{V_{\lambda}}}{d_{\lambda}}\otimes\Tr_{V_{\lambda}}(\ket{\psi_{\lambda}}\hskip-3.99994pt\bra{\psi_{\lambda}})\right)\ket{\Phi}

(103)

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{G}\bra{\Phi}\mathcal{T}_{G}(\ket{\Psi}\hskip-3.99994pt\bra{\Psi})\ket{\Phi}.

(104)

This proves the claim for
X=\ket{\Psi}\hskip-3.99994pt\bra{\Psi}
, and hence for all
X\succeq 0
.
∎

8
The local unitary Fourier transform

We now exploit the symmetries of our operator inequalities to block-diagonalize the characterization of
9
. This yields an equivalent formulation of separability in terms of the Fourier components of
\varrho_{AB}
with respect to the local unitary group
G=U(d_{A})\times U(d_{B})
.
To begin, we recall Schur–Weyl duality asserts that the Hilbert space
H^{\otimes n}=(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes n}
, viewed as a representation of
S_{n}\times U(d)
, can be decomposed into sectors indexed by (sorted) integer partitions
\lambda=(\lambda_{1}\ldots,\lambda_{d})
of
n
, with at most
d
nonzero parts:

H^{\otimes n}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\vdash n}V_{\lambda}\otimes U_{\lambda}^{d}.

(105)

Here
V_{\lambda}
carries an irreducible representation of the symmetric group
S_{n}
, while
U_{\lambda}^{d}
carries an irreducible representation of the unitary group
U(d)
where
d=\dim(H)
.
Implicit in this decomposition is the condition that
\lambda
has at most
d
nonzero parts, since otherwise
U_{\lambda}^{d}=\{0\}
.

With respect to this decomposition, the
S_{n}
-twirling channel acts on an operator
X
on

as

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{S_{n}}(X)=\bigoplus_{\lambda\vdash n}\frac{I_{V_{\lambda}}}{\dim V_{\lambda}}\otimes\Tr_{V_{\lambda}}[P_{\lambda}XP_{\lambda}],

where
P_{\lambda}
denotes the orthogonal projection onto the subspace
V_{\lambda}\otimes U_{\lambda}^{d}\subseteq(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes n}
.
Similarly, the bipartite space
(A\otimes B)^{\otimes n}
decomposes, as a representation of
S_{n}\times U(d_{A})\times U(d_{B})

(A\otimes B)^{\otimes n}=\bigoplus_{\alpha,\beta,\lambda\vdash n}V_{\lambda}\otimes U_{\alpha}^{a}\otimes U_{\beta}^{b}\otimes M_{\alpha\beta}^{\lambda}.

(107)

Here
\alpha,\beta
, and
\lambda
are (sorted) integer partitions of
n
with at most
d_{A},d_{B}
, and
d_{AB}
nonzero parts, respectively.
The multiplicity space is given by

M_{\alpha\beta}^{\lambda}=\mathrm{Hom}_{U(d_{A})\times U(d_{B})}(U_{\alpha}^{d_{A}}\otimes U_{\beta}^{d_{B}},U_{\lambda}^{d_{AB}}),

(108)

and its dimension

g_{\alpha\beta}^{\lambda}\coloneqq\dim M_{\alpha\beta}^{\lambda}

(109)

is the corresponding

.

Given an operator
X
on
(A\otimes B)^{\otimes n}
, twirling with respect to the group
S_{n}\times U(d_{A})\times U(d_{B})
gives

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{T}_{S_{n}\times U(d_{A})\times U(d_{B})}(X)&=\bigoplus_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta\vdash n}\frac{I_{V_{\lambda}}}{\dim V_{\lambda}}\otimes\frac{I_{U_{\alpha}^{d_{A}}}}{\dim U_{\alpha}^{d_{A}}}\otimes\frac{I_{U_{\beta}^{d_{B}}}}{\dim U_{\beta}^{d_{B}}}\otimes K_{\alpha\beta}^{\lambda}(X),\end{split}

(110)

where
K_{\alpha\beta}^{\lambda}(X)
is the operator on

defined by

K_{\alpha\beta}^{\lambda}(X)\coloneqq\Tr_{V_{\lambda}\otimes U_{\alpha}^{d_{A}}\otimes U_{\beta}^{d_{B}}}[P_{\alpha\beta}^{\lambda}XP_{\alpha\beta}^{\lambda}].

(111)

Here,
P_{\alpha\beta}^{\lambda}=P_{\lambda}(P_{\alpha}\otimes P_{\beta})
denotes the orthogonal projection onto the
(\alpha,\beta,\lambda)
-isotypic subspace of
(A\otimes B)^{\otimes n}
.
By construction,
K_{\alpha\beta}^{\lambda}
is
S_{n}\times U(d_{A})\times U(d_{B})
-invariant, in the sense that

K_{\alpha\beta}^{\lambda}\circ\mathcal{T}_{S_{n}\times U(d_{A})\times U(d_{B})}=K_{\alpha\beta}^{\lambda}.

(112)

The trace of
K_{\alpha\beta}^{\lambda}(\varrho_{AB}^{\otimes n})
, namely

q_{\varrho}(\alpha,\beta,\lambda)\coloneqq\Tr(P_{\alpha\beta}^{\lambda}\varrho_{AB}^{\otimes n}),

(113)

defines a probability distribution over triples of partitions
\alpha,\beta,\lambda\vdash n
.
This distribution was studied by Christandl & Mitchison
Christandl and Mitchison 2006
, who showed, using spectrum-estimation techniques
Keyl and Werner 2001
, that it concentrates around the spectra of
\varrho_{AB}
and of its marginals
\varrho_{A}
and
\varrho_{B}
.

More generally, by the Peter–Weyl theorem
(
Folland 2016
, Chapter 5)
, every polynomial function of
\varrho_{AB}
that is invariant under
(U(d_{A})\times U(d_{B}))
can be expanded as a linear combination of matrix coefficients of the operators
K_{\alpha\beta}^{\lambda}(\varrho_{AB}^{\otimes n})
, as
\alpha,\beta,\lambda
range over partitions of
n\in\mathbb{N}
. In this sense, the operators
K_{\alpha\beta}^{\lambda}(\varrho_{AB}^{\otimes n})
, together with their matrix coefficients, are precisely the Fourier components of
\varrho_{AB}
with respect to the non-abelian local-unitary Fourier transform.

In this language, the Fourier-transform version of our main result takes the following form.

Corollary 20
.

A state
\varrho_{AB}
belongs to
{\mathrm{SEP}(A\!:\!B)}

\Longleftrightarrow
for all
n
and all
\alpha,\beta,\lambda\vdash n
,

K_{\alpha\beta}^{\lambda}(\varrho_{AB}^{\otimes n})\preceq K_{\alpha\beta}^{\lambda}(\Lambda_{A^{n}B^{n}}).

(114)

Proof.

(
\boldsymbol{\Longrightarrow}
)

The forward implication follows from the
U(d_{A})\times U(d_{B})
-invariance of
{\mathrm{SEP}(A\!:\!B)}
, together with
9
. Indeed,
by definition,

\mathcal{T}_{U(d_{A})\times U(d_{B})}(\varrho_{AB}^{\otimes n})=\iint dUdV[(U\otimes V)\varrho_{AB}(U^{\dagger}\otimes V^{\dagger})]^{\otimes n}.

(115)

Since every state appearing in the integrand above belongs to
{\mathrm{SEP}(A\!:\!B)}
, the forward direction of
9
applies pointwise, and the claimed inequality follows.

(
\boldsymbol{\Longleftarrow}
)

The reverse implication is not immediate from
3
, since in general
\varrho_{AB}^{\otimes n}\neq\mathcal{T}_{U(d_{A})\times U(d_{B})}(\varrho_{AB}^{\otimes n})
.
To address this, we apply the pinching inequality from
19
, yielding

\varrho_{AB}^{\otimes n}=\mathcal{T}_{S_{n}}(\varrho_{AB}^{\otimes n})\preceq\Gamma(n)\mathcal{T}_{U(d_{A})\times U(d_{B})}(\varrho_{AB}^{\otimes n}),

(116)

where
\Gamma_{U(d_{A})\times U(d_{B})}(n)\in\mathrm{poly}(n)
may be chosen as

\Gamma_{U(d_{A})\times U(d_{B})}(n)=\sum_{\alpha,\beta\vdash n}\dim(U_{\alpha}^{d_{A}})^{2}\dim(U_{\beta}^{d_{B}})^{2}.

(117)

Finally, the proof of the converse direction in
3
is unaffected by multiplication with polynomial factors in
n
.
The same argument therefore applies here, with
f_{AB}(n)
replaced by
f_{AB}(n)\Gamma_{U(d_{A})\times U(d_{B})}(n)
.
∎

The advantage of
20
over
1
is that it involves only operators acting on the multiplicity spaces

, whose dimensions grow polynomially in

when the numbers of parts of
\alpha,\beta
, and

are bounded

1

.
This reduction in complexity essentially mimics the symmetry-reductions from exponentially-sized to polynomially-sized matrices, required to perform algorithms for entanglement detection based on hierarchies of semidefinite programs
Doherty et al. 2004
;
Harrow et al. 2017
;
Pena et al. 2025
.

The goal of this section is to provide a proof of

Although this result was already established in
(
Fraser 2022
, Appendix J, Theorem 15)
, we reproduce the proof details here for completeness.
We being by reviewing the essential representation-theoretic features of this estimation scheme before turning to the proof of
7
.

Let
H=\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a
d
-dimensional complex Hilbert space equipped with its standard inner product. Its
n
-th tensor power
H^{\otimes n}
supports a representation of
U(d)\times S_{n}
: the symmetric group
S_{n}
acts by permuting the tensor factors, as in
Eq.

16

U(d)

Schur-Weyl duality states that

decomposes into irreducible subrepresentations indexed by sorted integer partitions

of
n
, or Young diagrams, with at most
d
parts, denoted by
\lambda\vdash_{d}n

H^{\otimes n}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\vdash_{d}n}V_{\lambda}\otimes U_{\lambda}^{d}.

(118)

Here,
V_{\lambda}
carries an irreducible representation of the symmetric group
S_{n}
, while
U_{\lambda}^{d}
carries the irreducible representation
\pi_{\lambda}
of the unitary group
U(d)
with highest weight
\lambda

.
In particular, there exists a unique normalized highest-weight vector
\ket{\phi_{\lambda}}\in U_{\lambda}^{d}
satisfying

\pi_{\lambda}(\mathrm{diag}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}))\ket{\phi_{\lambda}}=x_{1}^{\lambda_{1}}\cdots x_{d}^{\lambda_{d}}\ket{\phi_{\lambda}}.

(119)

Following the notation of
Fraser 2022
, for each sorted integer partition
\lambda\vdash_{d}n
and each unitary

, let
\Phi_{\lambda}^{U}
denote the projection operator on

given by

\Phi_{\lambda}^{U}=I_{V_{\lambda}}\otimes\ket{\phi_{\lambda}^{U}}\hskip-3.99994pt\bra{\phi_{\lambda}^{U}},

(120)

where
\ket{\phi_{\lambda}^{U}}
is the normalized
U
-twirled highest-weight vector
\ket{\phi_{\lambda}^{U}}=\pi_{\lambda}(U)\ket{\phi_{\lambda}}
.
For any state
\sigma

(

(

, Section 49)

\Tr(\Phi_{\lambda}^{U}\sigma^{\otimes n})=\dim(V_{\lambda})\Delta_{\lambda}(U^{\dagger}\sigma U).

(121)

Here, for any vector
r=(r_{1}\geq r_{2}\geq\cdots\geq r_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{\geq 0}
, the function
\Delta_{r}(X)
is the

\Delta_{r}(X)=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\mathrm{pm}_{j}(X)^{r_{j}-r_{j+1}},

(122)

denoting the leading
j\times j

X
.
The notational convention is that
r_{d+1}=0
.
See also the approach based on the

(

.

Moreover, for each
\lambda\vdash_{d}n
and unitary

\eta_{\lambda}^{U}
denote the density matrix with rational eigenvalues

\eta_{\lambda}^{U}=U\mathrm{diag}\left(\frac{\lambda_{1}}{n},\ldots,\frac{\lambda_{d}}{n}\right)U^{\dagger}.

(123)

Using this notation, the state-estimation POVM introduced in
Keyl 2006
assigns to each measurable subset
C\subseteq\mathcal{S}(H)
the operator

E_{n}(C)=\sum_{\lambda\vdash_{d}n}\dim(U_{\lambda}^{d})\int_{U(d)}dU\delta_{C}(\eta_{\lambda}^{U})\Phi_{\lambda}^{U},

(124)

dU
denotes the Haar measure on

In

D^{*}(\varrho\|\sigma)\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{d}[x_{j}\ln x_{j}-(x_{j}-x_{j+1})\log\mathrm{pm}_{j}(U^{\dagger}\sigma U)].

Here,
x_{1}\geq\cdots\geq x_{d}
are the eigenvalues of
\varrho

x_{d+1}=0
, and
U
is a unitary diagonalizing

\varrho=U\mathrm{diag}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})U^{\dagger}

Franks and Walter 2020
;
Botero et al. 2021
;
Fraser 2022
;
Fraser 2023
.
7
7

7

Proof of

The sequence of measurements depends on the projection operators
\Phi_{\lambda}^{U}

Considering the ratio of

\frac{\Tr(\Phi_{\lambda}^{U}\sigma^{\otimes n})}{\Tr(\Phi_{\lambda}^{U}\varrho^{\otimes n})}=\frac{\Delta_{\lambda}(U^{\dagger}\sigma U)}{\Delta_{\lambda}(\mathrm{diag}(x))}.

(133)

Our goal is to choose a Young diagram
\lambda
, whose normalized shape approximates the eigenvalues
x

\varrho
.
For this, we fix a positive integer
k

\mu

\mu_{j}-\mu_{j+1}\coloneq\lceil k(x_{j}-x_{j+1})\rceil.

Letting

(135)

Since

(137)

The ratio we are interested in, is

\frac{\Delta_{kx}(U^{\dagger}\sigma U)}{\Delta_{kx}(\mathrm{diag}(x))}=\exp(-kD^{*}(\varrho\|\sigma)).

The remaining term, depending on

\displaystyle\Delta_{\delta}(\mathrm{diag}(x))=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\mathrm{pm}_{j}(\mathrm{diag}(x))^{\delta_{j}-\delta_{j+1}}\geq\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\

\frac{\Tr(\Phi_{\mu}^{U}\sigma^{\otimes\left|\mu\right|})}{\Tr(\Phi_{\mu}^{U}\varrho^{\otimes\left|\mu\right|})}\leq C(\varrho)^{-1}\exp(-kD^{*}(\varrho\|\sigma)).
