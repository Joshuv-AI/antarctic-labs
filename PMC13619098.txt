A unified fixed point approach for enriched bivariate mappings with applications to volterra systems and fractional-order chaos

Khaleel Ahmad, Sahib Yar, Abdul Rahim Khan, Nida Najeeb, Adil Jhangeer, Walid Abdelfattah, Vikash Kumar Sinha

Abstract

We develop a unified fixed point theory for enriched bivariate contractions in ordered Banach spaces and in ordered convex metric spaces. Two classes of mappings are introduced and analysed: enriched bivariate Ciric-Reich-Rus contractions ([pone.0358480.e001.webp]CBCRRCe) and enriched bivariate interpolative Ciric-Reich-Rus contractions ([pone.0358480.e002.webp]CBICRRCe). For each class we prove the existence and uniqueness of coupled fixed points and establish the geometric convergence of the coupled Krasnoselskij iteration. The methodological contribution is a transparent product space reduction: the coupled averaged operator associated with an enriched bivariate map is shown to be an ordinary (single variable) Ciric-Reich-Rus operator on the product space, so that the classical theory applies directly and every algebraic step is explicit. This reduction yields a sharp sufficient condition for the existence of a coupled fixed point, namely [pone.0358480.e003.webp](2a+k2)/(k1+1)+2b<1, where a,b are the contraction constants and [pone.0358480.e004.webp]k1,k2 are the enrichment constants. In particular the coupling doubles the coefficient of the point distance term relative to the univariate enriched theory, so a strictly stronger requirement on a is unavoidable. We further show that the condition 2a + 3b < 1 stated in earlier formulations is sufficient but not necessary, and we exhibit an explicit map for which a coupled fixed point exists while 2a + 3b < 1 fails. The abstract results are applied first to a Volterra type coupled integral system, and then to the fractional order Thomas cyclically symmetric attractor formulated with the Caputo-Fabrizio derivative, where an averaged mapping yields local in time existence and uniqueness even though the long time dynamics are chaotic. All numerical results are produced by direct computation. A bifurcation diagram and a largest Lyapunov exponent spectrum, computed independently, agree window for window and confirm a period doubling route to chaos with intermediate periodic windows, while the measured convergence factor of the coupled iteration is geometric.

1 Introduction

The Banach contraction principle [] is the cornerstone of metric fixed point theory, and its many refinements continue to drive progress in nonlinear analysis. Two directions are particularly relevant to the present work. The first is the family of Kannan and Ciric-Reich-Rus contractions, in which the displacement terms [pone.0358480.e005.webp]‖I−ΥI‖ and [pone.0358480.e006.webp]‖u−Υu‖ enter the contractive inequality alongside the point distance [pone.0358480.e007.webp]‖I−u‖, so that the class includes mappings that need not be continuous. Kannan introduced this idea in [,], and Karapinar revisited it through interpolation, producing the interpolative Kannan and interpolative Ciric-Reich-Rus contractions [,]. The second direction is the enriched contraction programme of Berinde and Pacurar, in which a possibly nonexpansive or even expansive mapping is first averaged with the identity and the averaged mapping is then shown to be contractive. This Krasnoselskij averaging recovers fixed points of mappings far outside the Banach class, and it has produced enriched Kannan, enriched Ciric-Reich-Rus, enriched Chatterjea and enriched Hardy-Rogers operators in Banach spaces and in convex metric spaces [–]. Interpolative and enriched ideas have since been combined in several settings [–].

A parallel and equally active strand concerns coupled and bivariate fixed points. Bhaskar and Lakshmikantham established coupled fixed point theorems in partially ordered metric spaces under a mixed monotone hypothesis [], and the theory was extended to nonlinear contractions and to tripled and higher order fixed points [,]. Coupled solutions of operator equations were approximated by iterative methods for weakly contractive bivariate operators [], and coupled fixed point results were obtained on quasi Banach spaces and in related abstract settings, with applications to systems of integral equations [–]. The convex metric space framework of Takahashi [] provides a natural home for the averaging step when no linear structure is available, and enriched contractions have been studied there as well [–]. The bivariate enriched programme was initiated by Wahab and Rauf, who studied an enriched bivariate contraction map for approximating coupled operators [,].

Fixed point methods are also a standard tool for the qualitative analysis of fractional order models, where the existence and uniqueness of a solution is obtained by reformulating the model as a fixed point problem for an integral operator. This approach is now routine across a wide range of fractional epidemic and dynamical models, including optimal control studies of monkeypox in a contaminated environment [], fractional models of hepatitis B transmission and their well posedness [,], malaria control with the Atangana-Baleanu derivative [], co-infection control problems [], onchocerciasis control [], a fractional model of chicken pox [], and further recent fractional modelling studies []. These works rely on Banach type arguments [] and therefore require the underlying integral operator to be a strict contraction. When the model is chaotic, the relevant operator is expansive on long horizons and the classical argument no longer applies directly. The enriched and bivariate machinery is precisely what is needed to restore contractivity through averaging, which motivates the unified treatment developed here.

Against this background the present paper makes the following contributions. We formulate two classes of enriched bivariate mappings on a product space, the enriched bivariate Ciric-Reich-Rus contractions [pone.0358480.e008.webp]CBCRRCe and their interpolative counterpart [pone.0358480.e009.webp]CBICRRCe, and we give a single, transparent proof technique for both. The technique is a product space reduction: the coupled averaged operator S associated with an enriched bivariate map is shown to be an ordinary Ciric-Reich-Rus operator, respectively an ordinary interpolative Ciric-Reich-Rus operator, on the product space C2 endowed with the additive product norm. Every constant is tracked explicitly, which removes the unspecified algebraic steps present in earlier treatments and answers a direct concern about rigour. The reduction produces a sharp sufficient condition for the existence of a coupled fixed point, and it shows clearly why the bivariate case is genuinely harder than the univariate one: passing to the coupled operator sums the contractive inequality over a pair, which doubles the coefficient of the point distance term. We also clarify the status of the condition 2a + 3b < 1 that appeared in earlier work. We prove that it is sufficient but not necessary, we identify the exact threshold, and we give a concrete map for which a coupled fixed point exists while 2a + 3b < 1 is violated. Existence and uniqueness are obtained without any symmetry assumption on the mapping. The theory is then illustrated on a Volterra type coupled integral system, where the bivariate framework strictly enlarges the class of systems that can be treated, and on the fractional order Thomas attractor, where averaging gives local existence and uniqueness on a horizon on which the integral operator is contractive, consistent with chaotic long time behaviour. Finally, all reported numerical quantities, including a bifurcation diagram, a largest Lyapunov exponent spectrum and the convergence factor of the coupled iteration, are generated by direct computation from a single reproducible script provided as Supporting Information.

The rest of the paper is organised as follows. Section fixes notation and recalls the background notions. Section develops the theory in ordered Banach spaces, including the corrected main theorem, the corrected interpolative theorem, a worked nonsymmetric example, and the consequence for the threshold condition. Section transfers the results to ordered convex metric spaces. Sections and present the Volterra and fractional Thomas applications. Section reports the numerical study, and Section concludes.

6.2 Fixed point well posedness and chaos are compatible

Theorem 6.3 is a local in time statement. The constant K in (26) is an increasing function of the horizon [pone.0358480.e301.webp]Tmax, and the contraction requirement 3KL < 1 holds precisely when [pone.0358480.e302.webp]Tmax is small enough. On such a horizon the integral operator is a strict contraction, so for each fixed initial condition the solution exists and is unique. This is entirely consistent with the chaotic behaviour observed on long horizons. Chaos is a statement about how solutions issued from nearby but distinct initial conditions separate as time grows, quantified by a positive largest Lyapunov exponent and by sensitive dependence on initial data. Uniqueness, by contrast, fixes the initial condition and asserts a single solution curve. The two notions act on different objects, the spread of an ensemble versus a single trajectory, and they do not conflict. The Krasnoselskij averaging contributes a contractive reformulation that is valid step by step and underlies the convergence of the numerical scheme used below, while the long time dynamics generated by repeated application of the flow remain free to be chaotic.

8 Conclusion

We established a unified fixed point theory for two classes of enriched bivariate mappings, the enriched bivariate Ciric-Reich-Rus contractions and their interpolative counterpart, in ordered Banach spaces and in ordered convex metric spaces. The central device is a product space reduction in which the coupled averaged operator is shown to be an ordinary, single variable contraction of the appropriate type on the product space, so that the classical theorems apply with every constant tracked explicitly. This reduction yields the sharp sufficient condition [pone.0358480.e369.webp](2a+k2)/(k1+1)+2b<1 for the existence of a coupled fixed point, it exhibits the doubling of the point distance coefficient as the genuine source of difficulty in the bivariate case, and it clarifies that the previously stated condition 2a + 3b < 1 is sufficient but not necessary, as confirmed by an explicit map. Existence and uniqueness were obtained with no symmetry assumption. The theory was applied to a coupled Volterra system, where the bivariate framework strictly enlarges the treatable class, and to the fractional order Thomas attractor, where averaging gives local in time well posedness that coexists with chaotic long time behaviour. All numerical results, including a bifurcation diagram and a Lyapunov spectrum that agree window for window, and a measured geometric convergence factor, were produced by direct computation from a single reproducible script.

Several directions remain open. The common coupled fixed point problem for pairs of enriched bivariate contractions in convex metric spaces, in the spirit of [,], is natural to pursue. The averaging approach can be applied to other fractional order chaotic systems such as the Lorenz, Rossler and Aizawa systems. Finally, the convex metric results may be extended to b metric spaces and to spaces with weaker convex structure.
