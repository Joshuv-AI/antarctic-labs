3.​ Executive Summary:

Steel production is responsible for approximately 8% of global CO2 emissions, with over
75% of this footprint originating from the reduction of iron ore to metallic iron. This
project demonstrates a transformative pathway for American steelmaking by integrating
Molten Industries’ proprietary methane pyrolysis technology with a direct reduced iron
(DRI) shaft furnace. By thermally decomposing natural gas into solid carbon (graphite)
and hydrogen using renewable electricity, this process avoids the massive CO2
emissions inherent in traditional steam methane reforming.

The project aims to achieve an 85% reduction in CO2 emissions compared to current
blast furnace methods. Beyond environmental impact, this approach ensures U.S.
economic competitiveness by leveraging the nation's abundant, low-cost natural gas
resources. The dual-product nature of the process—producing high-purity hydrogen for
iron reduction and valuable solid graphite—enables a cost-effective transition to green
steel. This technology requires 5x less energy than water electrolysis, significantly
reducing the burden on the electrical grid. Key outcomes include the successful
validation of high-temperature carbon separation (>99% efficiency) and advanced 3D
modeling that optimized hydrogen-driven iron reduction, achieving >99% metallization at
7 bar pressure. This project provides a scalable, mine-to-mill solution for carbon-neutral
American manufacturing.

4.​ Background:

The global steel industry primarily relies on the Blast Furnace (BF) + Basic Oxygen
Furnace (BOF) route, which emits 1.9 kg CO2 per kg of steel. While state-of-the-art
Natural Gas DRI (NG DRI) processes reduce emissions by 30-50%, they still produce
significant CO2 during reforming and heating. Alternative electrolytic hydrogen (H2 DRI)
pathways can achieve deeper decarbonization but are hindered by extreme energy
intensity (>3 MWhe/ton DRI) and the need for massive new infrastructure for hydrogen
storage and compression.

Molten Industries’ methane pyrolysis technology presents a distinct advantage by
producing hydrogen at costs potentially <$1/kg while generating a valuable solid carbon
byproduct in graphite. This project leverages the Barracuda Virtual Reactor software
from CPFD to simulate complex high-temperature fluid processes, building upon
foundational work in ironmaking.

Differentiation from Existing Research:
 ●​ Direct High-Temperature Integration: Unlike standard systems that cool and then
 reheat hydrogen, Molten aims to deliver H2 at >700°C directly to the shaft
 furnace, targeting a 36% overall energy saving.
 ●​ Solid Carbon Management: This project pioneered modeling for
 hydrogen/methane/solid carbon mixtures in the reduction zone, a first in the field.
 ●​ Granular Phase Modeling: While most academic literature uses fixed granular
 velocities, this project utilized ambitious particle-fluid interaction modeling to
 accurately predict iron ore reduction.

5.​ Project Objectives:

This project supports national goals for clean energy by providing a cost-effective
pathway to eliminate up to 60 million tons of CO2 annually in the U.S. steel sector. It
enhances economic competitiveness by decreasing dependence on foreign steel
imports and utilizing domestic natural gas to produce green products at commercial
scale.

Project Goals and Significance
 i.​ Pilot Scale: Develop an integrated system producing 400 kg/day of direct reduced
 iron.
 j.​ Energy Savings: Achieve ~36% energy savings through heat integration, delivering
 H2 at >700°C directly to the shaft furnace.
 k.​ Carbon Separation: Separate >75% of solid carbon in a high-temperature cyclone
 to maintain low CO2 emissions.
 l.​ Innovation: This body of work represents a fundamental advance in fluid-particle
 modeling, being the first to explore 3D transient CFD simulations of iron reduction
 using hydrogen/methane/solid carbon mixtures.​

7.​ Significant Accomplishments and Conclusions:

This section highlights the key technical and economic milestones achieved during
Budget Period 1, which have successfully mitigated major risks associated with
integrating methane pyrolysis and iron reduction.

1. Economic Competitiveness of Hydrogen-Driven DRI
One of the project's most significant accomplishments is the validation of the
techno-economic viability of pure hydrogen ironmaking.

 ●​ Accomplishment: Detailed TEA modeling (Milestone 2.2.1) demonstrated that
 reheating and recompressing pure hydrogen adds approximately $17.36/ton to
 DRI production costs (assuming an electricity price of $70/MWh).
 ●​ Significance: This cost is only $9.16/ton higher than the traditional Midrex
 synthesis gas baseline ($8.2/ton), placing the technology well within the
 competitive target of $100/ton-Fe. This finding dispels the industry assumption
 that switching to pure hydrogen necessarily leads to prohibitive cost increases.
 ●​ Carbon Valorization: The economic outlook is further enhanced by the
 production of high-purity solid carbon. By valorizing this byproduct as
 battery-grade graphite rather than treating it as a waste stream, Molten Industries
 can potentially achieve a lower net cost of iron production than current blast
 furnace methods.

2. Validating the Path to 85% CO2 Reduction
The preliminary Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) and energy balance work provided a
transparent roadmap for industrial decarbonization.

 ●​ Accomplishment: Energy balance modeling (Milestone 1.7.2) currently shows a
 realized minimum energy intensity of ~0.89 MWh/ton-DRI.

 Page 8 of 11

 ●​ Significance: While this is slightly above the aggressive project target of 0.7
 MWh/ton-DRI, it represents a 70% reduction in energy intensity compared to the
 ~3 MWh/ton required for electrolytic hydrogen (eH2) pathways.
 ●​ Lesson Learned: The modeling revealed that achieving the stretch goal of 0.7
 MWh/ton-DRI requires a heat recovery efficiency of >63%. This identified heat
 integration as the "greatest differentiator" and the primary technical hurdle for
 commercial scale-up, focusing the team's engineering efforts for Budget Period
 2.

3. Overcoming Hydrogen Reduction Kinetics (7-Bar Innovation)
Transitioning from a carbon-monoxide (CO) based reduction to a pure hydrogen (H2)
based reduction presented a significant technical challenge due to the endothermic
nature of H2 reduction.

 ●​ Accomplishment: Through 3D transient CFD modeling, the team discovered
 that increasing reactor pressure to 7 bar and utilizing internal baffles could
 overcome "channeling" and poor metallization.
 ●​ Significance: These innovations increased metallization from a baseline of 63%
 to >99%. This provides a critical design template for future commercial reactors,
 ensuring high-quality iron ore reduction without the need for massive excess
 hydrogen.

4. High-Efficiency Carbon Separation
Maintaining a high-temperature hydrogen feed requires the near-total removal of solid
carbon particulates to prevent fouling and downstream CO2 formation.

 ●​ Accomplishment: The project exceeded its milestone of 75% carbon separation
 by achieving >99% efficiency in experimental quartz cyclone tests.
 ●​ Significance: This result validates the "integrated" nature of the process, proving
 that hydrogen can be delivered at >1000°C while simultaneously capturing the
 graphite byproduct in a clean, usable form.

Strategic Pivots

 ●​ Strategic Pivot (Hazen Research Center): During Q5, the team identified a
 significant opportunity to pivot the experimental scope. Rather than building a
 redundant pilot-scale furnace in Oakland, Molten proposes to collaborate with the
 DOE-funded GISH project at the Hazen Research Center. This move could
 reduce the capital expenditure by over $200k, mitigating the technical risk of
 building a new pressure vessel from scratch.

8.​ Path Forward:

Future Research and Development:
The primary R&D path forward is to shift from computational modeling to physical
demonstration in a direct reduced iron shaft furnace.

 ●​ Experimental Validation of 3D Models: Test the high-temperature injection of
 hydrogen and methane mixtures. This will provide the critical real-world data
 needed to validate the 7-bar pressure and internal baffle configurations identified
 in our CFD simulations.
 ●​ Heat Integration Hardware: Prove that delivering H2 at >1000°C is technically
 feasible without damaging reactor internals or compromising gas purity.
 ●​ Emission Characterization (Task 4): Future work will include measuring
 top-gas exhaust to verify air quality standards and model process emissions for a
 commercial-scale (1 million ton per year) plant, ensuring the technology meets
 stringent environmental regulations.

Technology Transfer and Commercialization
Molten Industries is pursuing a dual-product commercialization strategy that leverages
the high value of both carbon-neutral iron and battery-grade graphite.
 ●​ Market Transformation: Integrating Molten’s methane pyrolysis technology with
 existing steel producers looking to replace traditional natural gas reformers could
 enable economically competitive U.S. steel production with significantly reduced
 emissions.
 ●​ The Graphite Revenue Stream: By valorizing the solid carbon byproduct as a
 high-purity material for the EV battery supply chain, the technology can offset the
 costs of hydrogen production. This makes this economically superior to
 electrolytic hydrogen methods, which produce no valuable byproducts.
 ●​ Industrial Partnerships: Continued collaboration with U.S. Steel or Cleveland
 Cliffs would focus on evaluating the DRI produced in the pilot for use in Electric
 Arc Furnaces (EAFs).

Remaining Technical Risks and Barriers
While the project has de-risked the integration of pyrolysis and ironmaking, several
barriers to immediate commercialization remain:
 ●​ Heat Recovery Efficiency: Our current optimized models show an energy
 intensity of ~0.89 MWh/ton-DRI. To reach the project goal of 0.7 MWh/ton-DRI,
 the system must achieve a thermal recovery efficiency of >63%. Engineering a
 heat exchanger that can withstand high-temperature, hydrogen-rich, and
 potentially carbon-laden environments remains a primary technical risk.
 ●​ Scaling Kinetics: Translating the >99% metallization yields from 3D models to a
 commercial-scale shaft furnace (producing millions of tons per year) requires
 further study of iron ore "channeling" and gas distribution at massive scales.
