Poster 241. Outcomes of Operative and Non-Operative Midshaft Clavicle Fractures in a Young and Athletic Population
Authors: Houston Dove, Campbell Aiken, Eva Dreiling, Morgan Russell, Gabby West, Alex Yates, Matthew P. Ithurburn, Christopher H. Garrett, Marcus A. Rothermich, Benton A. Emblom, E. Lyle Cain, Jeffrey R. Dugas
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Objective:Midshaft clavicle fractures are common in active adolescents. Currently, there is no strong consensus on the ideal management of these injuries, as several studies have shown that nonoperative treatment leads to similar outcomes as operative treatment in long-term follow-up. However, despite the risks of operative treatment, including reoperation for hardware removal, evidence has shown that operative treatment can lead to improved rates of return-to-sport (RTS). In this study, we investigated differences in mid-term patient-reported and RTS outcomes between operative and non-operative treatment of midshaft clavicle fractures in adolescents, including a large proportion of competitive athletes.Methods:We conducted a retrospective cohort study including adolescents (10-18 years old at time of injury) with acute mid-shaft clavicular fractures. Included patients were treated either operatively or with nonoperative management and were followed until healing of their fracture. Relevant demographic, injury, treatment, and revision surgery data were collected via electronic health record review. At follow-up (minimum of 2 years post-treatment), we collected patient-reported outcomes including the American Shoulder and Elbow Surgeons (ASES) score and the Kerlan-Jobe Orthopaedic Clinic (KJOC) Shoulder and Elbow score. Specific to competitive athletes, we also collected information regarding return to preinjury sport and relevant contextual factors related to RTS. Linear regression modeling, controlling for relevant covariates, was used to compare PRO scores between groups. Chi-square tests were used to compare proportions of competitive athletes who successfully returned to preinjury sport and the proportions of subsequent or revision clavicle-related procedures.Results:Eighty-four patients, aged 10-18, with complete clinical and outcome data, were included. For the entire cohort, the mean age at surgery or non-operative treatment visit was 15.5 years, and the cohort was 89% male and 87% competitive athletes. The operative group had significantly shorter follow-up time than the non-operative group, and the operative group had a significantly larger proportion of competitive athletes than the non-operative group (Table 1). Injury severity, as measured by all radiographic assessments (fracture displacement, shortening, angulation, and comminution), was higher/worse in the operative group versus the non-operative group (Table 2). After controlling for differences in follow-up time, the groups' ASES and KJOC scores at follow-up did not differ (Table 3). However, there was a trend toward higher KJOC scores in the operative group (Table 3; p=0.09). There was no statistical difference in the proportions that returned to preinjury sport between the operative and non-operative group (p=0.53). Of the 59 athletes in the operative group, 51 attempted to return to their preinjury sport, and 50 (98%) were able to RTS (Table 4). Of the 12 athletes in the non-operative group, 11 attempted to return to their preinjury sport, and all 11 (100%) were able to successfully RTS (Table 4). Lastly, there was no significant difference in the proportions of revision clavicle surgical procedures for the operative group (16%) versus subsequent clavicle surgical procedures for the non-operative group (10%; p=0.47).Conclusions:At our institution, we had a high rate of adolescent patients with mid-shaft clavicle fractures successfully return to preinjury level of sport, in both operative and nonoperative groups. Even with notably higher severity of injury in the operative group, outcomes between both groups were similar at mid-term follow-up. Interestingly, there was no statistically-significant difference in subsequent clavicle surgery between initial operative and nonoperative management.
