Asymptotic stability of Stationary solutions to 3D incompressible flow in porous media with diffusion Thanks: The author was supported by FAPERJ Nota 10 grant 203.710/2025, and CAPES PROEX grant 88887.901672/2023-00
Juan Sebastián Ángel-Echeverry

Abstract
. Under appropiate hypotheses on the external force acting on an incompressible flow diffusing through a porous medium, we show that there is a unique stationary solution to the diffusive porous media equation. Moreover, we show that this solution is asymptotically stable and estimate the decay rate of any perturbation towards this steady state.



1.
Introduction

We are interested in investigating the asymptotic behavior of stationary solutions to the Diffusive Porous Media Equation, or DPME, on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
,

(1.1)

\begin{cases}&\partial_{t}\theta+V_{\theta}\cdot\nabla\theta=\Delta\theta+f,\\
&V_{\theta}=-(\nabla P_{\theta}+\theta\ e_{3}),\\
&\nabla\cdot V_{\theta}=0,\\
&\theta(x,0)=\theta_{0}(x),\end{cases}

where
\theta
is flow temperature,
V_{\theta}
is flow velocity and
f
is an external force. This type of equation models phenomena such as groundwater flows and oil recovery processes, see Whitaker
[
20
]
.

The first studies regarding flow in porous media were performed by Henry Darcy in the mid 1850s as part of his studies in Hydrology. He discovered that there was a proportional relation between flow rate and the applied pressure gradient, which can be expressed as follows

V_{\theta}=-\kappa(\nabla P_{\theta}+g\theta\,e_{3}),

where
\kappa
is the matrix of medium permeabilities in the different directions divided by the viscosity. Here
g
is the acceleration due to gravity and
e_{3}=(0,0,1)
. In our study, we choose
g=1,\kappa=Id
. Regarding heat transfer,
\Delta\theta
represents the net rate of heat conduction, while
V_{\theta}\cdot\nabla\theta
represents the net rate of change of temperature due to convection. For further details on the physical behavior of this phenomenon see Bejan and Nield
[
14
]

Different aspects of equation (
1.1
) have been studied. Castro, Córdoba, Gancedo and Orive
[
6
]
established the existence of strong and weak solutions to the equation with general fractional diffusion
\Lambda^{\alpha}=(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}
, obtained the decay of solution in
L^{p}
spaces for
p\geq 2
and investigated their asymptotic behavior; also they proved the existence of attractors. Meanwhile, Niche and Orive
[
13
]
extended the results of Castro, Córdoba, Gancedo and Orive
[
6
]
regarding the decay of the
L^{2}
norm of weak solutions to (
1.1
). They also obtained the decay of the
L^{p}
norm of the solution and its derivatives for
1\leq p\leq\infty
, as well as the first order decay of the solutions. Xue
[
22
]
proved, for
0<\alpha<1
, global well-posedness for small initial data in a given Besov space. For other results about (
1.1
) see Yuan and Yuan
[
27
]
; Yamazaki
[
23
,
24
]
; Bie, Wang, and Yao
[
19
]
; Guo and Haifeng
[
10
]
; Yu, He, Tong and Wu
[
25
]
; and Wu, Yu and Tang
[
21
]
. Regarding the inviscid case, see Córdoba, Gancedo and Orive
[
7
]
and Yu and He
[
26
]
.

Our first main result concerns the existence and uniqueness of stationary solutions to

(1.2)

\begin{cases}&V\cdot\nabla\theta_{st}=\Delta\theta_{st}+f,\\
&V=-(\nabla P_{\theta_{st}}+\theta_{st}\ e_{3}),\\
&\nabla\cdot V=0,\end{cases}

under the hypothesis that the external force
f
has finite
L^{2}
energy and certain behavior at the small frequencies level. We recall that the decay character
r^{\ast}=r^{\ast}(g)
of
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
is a real number that describes the behavior of
g
near the origin in frequency space, namely
r^{\ast}(g)=r^{\ast}
provided
|\widehat{g}(\xi)|\sim|\xi|^{r^{\ast}}
near
\xi=0
, for details see Section
2.1
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, with decay character
\gamma^{*}=\gamma^{*}(f)>2
. Let

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}=\max\left\{\|f\|_{L^{2}},\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}}\right\}.

Then, there exists a constant
\mu_{\gamma^{*}}>0
such that for any
M>\mu_{\gamma^{*}}
, there is a constant
\mathcal{C}_{M}=\mathcal{C}(M,\gamma^{*})>0
such that if
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}\leq\mathcal{C}_{M}
, then there is a weak solution
\theta_{st}\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
to (
1.2
). Moreover,
\lVert\theta_{st}\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq M
, and
\theta_{st}
is unique amongst all solutions satisfying

\left\lVert\theta_{st}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}}.

In order to solve this problem, we use the approach developed by Bjorland and Schonbek in
[
2
]
to prove existence of a stationary weak solutions to the Navier-Stokes equations. This method consists of first constructing a linear parabolic PDE with a structure analogous to (
1.1
). Then, integrating the solution to this linear parabolic PDE in time, we obtain a weak solution to a linear equation with a structure analogous to (
1.2
). We repeat this process iteratively to obtain a sequence that converges to a weak solution to the nonlinear equation (
1.2
). This method is motivated by the well known observation that integrating in time the solution to the heat equation yields a solution to the Poisson equation.

In our second main result, we deal with the asymptotic stability of
\theta_{st}
, in the sense that, for large times, the solution
\theta
to (
1.1
) converges to
\theta_{st}
. To that end, we set
\omega=\theta-\theta_{st}
, which satisfies

(1.3)

\begin{cases}&\partial_{t}\omega+V_{\theta}\cdot\nabla\omega+V_{\omega}\cdot\nabla\theta_{st}=\Delta\omega,\\
&V_{\omega}=V_{\theta}-V_{\theta_{st}},\\
&\nabla\cdot V_{\omega}=0,\\
&\omega(x,0)=\omega_{0}=\theta_{0}-\theta_{st}.\end{cases}

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\theta
be a weak solution to (
1.1
) with initial data
\theta_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
\theta_{st}
the stationary solution obtained in Theorem
1.1
. Then, if
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}}
is small enough, the weak solution
\omega
to (
1.3
) with initial data
\omega_{0}=\theta_{0}-\theta_{st}
is such that

\left\lVert\omega(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C(1+t)^{-\min\left\{\frac{3}{2},\frac{3}{2}+\min\left\{r^{*}(\theta_{0}),r^{*}(\theta_{st})\right\}\right\}},

where
C=C(\left\lVert\omega_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}})
and
r^{*}(\theta_{0})
,
r^{*}(\theta_{st})
are the decay characters of
\theta_{0}
and
\theta_{st}
.

Notice that the initial perturbation
\omega_{0}
need not be small. We prove this Theorem using the Fourier Splitting method developed by Schonbek
[
17
,
18
]
. The method consists of obtaining a differential inequality for the
L^{2}
norm of
\omega
, such that, after considering a time-dependent ball in frequency space, its behavior will depend on the small frequencies of
\widehat{\omega}
for large t. We prove existence of weak solutions to (
1.3
) in Theorem
4.5
.

The structure of this article is the following. In Section 2, we introduce tools important for the development of the proofs, such as the definition of the Decay Character and the Fourier Splitting method. In Section 3, we focus on proving the existence of weak solutions to (
1.2
), following the method developed by Bjorland and Schonbek
[
2
]
. Finally, in Section 4, we use the retarded mollifiers method developed by Caffarelli, Kohn and Nirenberg
[
5
]
to prove the existence of weak solutions to (
1.3
). Then, we address the asymptotic behavior of (
1.1
),
applying the Fourier splitting method to prove Theorem
1.2
.

2.
Preliminaries

We adopt the following notations,

\displaystyle L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})=\left\{u:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}\mid\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{p}}<\infty\right\},\ \text{where}\ \left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{p}}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,dx,\,\,\text{for}\ 1\leq p<\infty,

\displaystyle L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\ \text{is the Hilbert space with inner product}\ \big\langle u,v\big\rangle_{L^{2}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}uv\ dx,

\displaystyle\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\ \text{is the closure of the space $C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{3})$ in the norm $\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}$},

\displaystyle\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})=(\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))^{{}^{\prime}},

\displaystyle\big\langle g,u\big\rangle_{\dot{H}^{-1},\dot{H}^{1}}\ \text{is the action of the functional $g\in H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})$ over the element $u\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})$},

\displaystyle\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}=\max\left\{\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{2}},\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}}\right\}\ \forall f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),

where
V^{{}^{\prime}}
denotes the dual space of
V
. Also,
C
denotes general positive constants that may change from line to line.

2.1.
Decay Character

Given a function
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
the decay character associates to
u_{0}
a number
\gamma=\gamma(u_{0})
which measures the algebraic order of
u_{0}
near the origin, in the sense that
\gamma=\gamma(u_{0})
if
|\widehat{u_{0}}(\xi)|\approx|\xi|^{\gamma}
, for
|\xi|\approx 0
. This concept was first introduce Bjorland and Schonbek in
[
3
]
; it was later studied in more detail by Niche and Schonbek
[
12
]
, and Brandolese
[
4
]
. We now introduce some important definitions and properties regarding the decay character.

Definition 2.1
.

The decay indicator of
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is
\gamma\in\left(-\frac{n}{2},\infty\right)
such that

(2.1)

P_{\gamma}(u_{0})=\lim_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\rho^{-2\gamma}\rho^{-n}\int_{B(\rho)}\left\lvert\widehat{u_{0}}(\rho)\right\rvert^{2}d\rho,

provided the limit exists. Here,
B(\rho)
is the ball of radius
\rho
centered at the origin.

Definition 2.2
.

The decay character of
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, denoted by
\gamma^{\ast}=\gamma^{*}(u_{0})
, is the only number
\gamma\in\left(-\frac{n}{2},\infty\right)
such that
0<P_{\gamma}(u_{0})<\infty
.

Remark 1
.

It is possible to explicitly calculate the decay character when
u_{0}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
1\leq p\leq 2
, see Ferreira, Niche and Planas
[
9
]
.

A useful application concerning estimates for the decay of solutions to the heat equation through the use of the decay character concept was established by Bjorland and Schonbek
[
3
]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with decay character
\gamma^{*}=\gamma^{*}(u_{0})
and
u
be a solution to the heat equation with initial condition
u_{0}
. Then, there exist constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

C_{1}(1+t)^{-\left(\frac{n}{2}+\gamma^{*}\right)}\leq\left\lVert u(t)\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq C_{2}(1+t)^{-\left(\frac{n}{2}+\gamma^{*}\right)}.

The following Lemma is an immediate consequence of Theorem
2.3
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\gamma^{*}(f)>1-\frac{n}{2}
and let
\Phi=e^{\Delta t}f
. Then

(2.2)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\lVert\Phi(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\leq\mu_{\gamma^{*}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\mu_{\gamma^{*}}(1+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2})=\mathcal{M}_{1},

where
\mu_{\gamma^{*}}>0
is a constant that depends only on
\gamma^{*}
and
n
.

Niche and Schonbek
[
12
]
generalized Theorem
2.3
as follows. Let
X
be a Hilbert space and
\mathcal{L}
be a linear diagonalizable pseudodifferential operator
\mathcal{L}:X^{n}\to(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))^{n}
with symbol
\mathcal{M}(\xi)
such that

\mathcal{M}(\xi)=P^{-1}(\xi)D(\xi)P(\xi),\hskip 14.22636pt\xi-\text{a.e.},

where
P(\xi)\in O(n)
and
D(\xi)=-c_{i}\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2\alpha}\delta_{ij}
with
c_{i}>C>0
and
0<\alpha\leq 1
. Then, the following holds.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with decay character
\gamma^{*}=\gamma^{*}(u_{0})
and
u
a solution to

(2.3)

u_{t}=\mathcal{L}u,

with initial condition
u_{0}
. Then there exist constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

C_{1}(1+t)^{-\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)\left(\frac{n}{2}+\gamma^{*}\right)}\leq\left\lVert u(t)\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq C_{2}(1+t)^{-\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)\left(\frac{n}{2}+\gamma^{*}\right)}.

In Definitions
2.1
and
2.2
, we assumed that the limit (
2.1
) exists. However, Brandolese
[
4
]
proved that there exist functions in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that (
2.1
) is not well-defined. Such functions can be constructed by introducing fast oscillations around the origin in the frequency space, see Example 6.1 in Brandolese
[
4
]
. To circumvent this problem, he gave generalized definitions of upper and lower decay character for functions such that (
2.1
) does not exist. These definitions are characterized in terms of subsets of homogeneous Besov spaces
{\dot{B}}_{2,\infty}^{-\sigma}
, see Brandolese
[
4
]
.

We now prove a simple, but important, Lemma concerning the decay character of
f\pm g
, where
f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, with decay characters
r^{\ast}(f),r^{\ast}(g)
respectively. Then

r^{\ast}(f\pm g)\leq\min\left\{r^{\ast}(f),r^{\ast}(g)\right\}.

Proof.

We have

\rho^{-2\gamma}\rho^{-n}\int_{B(\rho)}\left\lvert\widehat{(f\pm g)}(\rho)\right\rvert^{2}d\rho\leq C\rho^{-2\gamma}\rho^{-n}\int_{B(\rho)}\left\lvert\widehat{f}(\rho)\right\rvert^{2}d\rho+C\rho^{-2\gamma}\rho^{-n}\int_{B(\rho)}\left\lvert\widehat{g}(\rho)\right\rvert^{2}d\rho.

If
\gamma=\min\left\{r^{\ast}(f),r^{\ast}(g)\right\}
, the result follows after taking limit when
\rho
goes to zero and using the definition of decay character.
∎

3.
Existence of solution to the Stationary DPM equation (
1.2
)

In this section we will prove the existence of a unique weak solution to (
1.2
) under some constraints related to the size of the external force
f
. To address this problem, we first construct the following linear parabolic PDE, whose structure is analogous to (
1.1
)

(3.1)

\begin{split}u_{t}^{i+1}(t)+V^{i}\cdot\nabla u^{i+1}(t)&=\Delta u^{i+1}(t)-V^{i}\cdot\nabla\Phi(t),\\
V^{i}&=-(\nabla P+\theta_{st}^{i}\ e_{3}),\\
u^{i+1}(0)&=0,\end{split}

for given stationary scalar
\theta_{st}^{i}
and vector field
V^{i}
. In Theorem
3.2
, we prove existence of weak solutions to (
3.1
).

We also consider the following PDE

(3.2)

\begin{cases}&V^{i}\cdot\nabla\theta_{st}^{i+1}=\Delta\theta_{st}^{i+1}+f,\\
&V^{i}=-(\nabla P+\theta_{st}^{i}\ e_{3}),\\
&\nabla\cdot V^{i}=0,\end{cases}

where again
\theta_{st}^{i}
is a known stationary scalar. In Theorem
3.1
, we prove the existence of a weak solution to (
3.2
). Then, to the solution to (
3.1
) we add a corrector term given by the solution to the heat equation with initial datum
f
, which integrated in time provide another solution to (
3.2
). Uniqueness of solution given by Theorem
3.1
implies that the solution to (
3.2
) is actually this integral.
Therefore, for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
we obtain a solution
\theta_{st}^{i+1}
to (
3.2
) and with this we construct a sequence
{\theta_{st}^{i+1}}
that converges to a solution to (
1.2
). This method is motivated by the well known observation that integrating in time the solution to the heat equation yields a solution to the Poisson equation.

A useful result regarding the
L^{p}
norm of the term
V_{\theta}
, proved by Niche and Orive
[
13
]
, states that

(3.3)

\left\lVert V_{\theta}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\leq C\left\lVert\theta(t)\right\rVert_{L^{p}},\hskip 14.22636pt1<p<\infty.

Analogously,

\left\lVert V^{i}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\leq C\left\lVert\theta^{i}_{st}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{p}},\hskip 14.22636pt1<p<\infty.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
f\in\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
\theta_{st}^{i}\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
be a fixed scalar field. Then there exists a unique weak solution
\theta_{st}^{i+1}\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
to (
3.2
), in the sense that for any
\varphi\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

\big\langle V^{i}\cdot\nabla{\theta_{st}^{i+1}},\varphi\big\rangle=-\big\langle\nabla\theta_{st}^{i+1},\nabla\varphi\big\rangle+\big\langle f,\varphi\big\rangle.

Moreover, this solution satisfies

(3.4)

\left\lVert\nabla\theta_{st}^{i+1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}.

Proof.

We use the Galerkin approximation method to prove the existence of weak solutions in smooth bounded domains
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
with Dirichlet boundary conditions. In order to prove the existence of weak solutions in the whole space we construct a sequence of solutions defined on balls with increasing radii and show its convergence.

Let
\mathcal{W}=\left\{w_{k}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be the set of eigenfunction associated to the Laplace Operator in
L^{2}(\Omega)
with Dirichlet boundary conditions. We recall it is an orthonormal basis for
L^{2}(\Omega)
; an orthogonal basis for
\dot{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and
w_{k}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. For every
N\in\mathbb{N}
we define

W_{N}=span\left\{w_{k}\right\}_{k=1,\dots,N},

and consider the projection of (
3.2
) onto the subspace
W_{N}
. Let us define, for fixed
N\in\mathbb{N}
,

(\theta_{st})^{i+1}_{N}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}c_{k}w_{k}(x),

then for each
s=1,\dots,N
define

(3.5)

\big\langle V^{i}\cdot\nabla{(\theta_{st})^{i+1}_{N},w_{s}}\big\rangle+\big\langle\nabla\left(\theta_{st}\right)_{N}^{i+1},\nabla w_{s}\big\rangle=\big\langle f,w_{s}\big\rangle.

This is a system of ordinary differential equations in the finite-dimensional space
W_{N}
which has a unique solution
(\theta_{st})^{i+1}_{N}
. Since
\nabla\cdot V^{i}=0
it follows that sequence
\left\{(\theta_{st})^{i+1}_{N}\right\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies the following inequality

(3.6)

\left\lVert\nabla(\theta_{st})^{i+1}_{N}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq C\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}.

Then
\left\{(\theta_{st})^{i+1}_{N}\right\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
is uniformly bounded in
\dot{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, and we obtain a subsequence
\left\{(\theta_{st})^{i+1}_{N}\right\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
, that we continue denoting in the same way
1
1

1

From now on we omit saying that we rename the subsequence.
, such that

(3.7)

(\theta_{st})^{i+1}_{N}\to\theta_{st}^{i+1}\hskip 14.22636pt\text{strongly in $L^{2}(\Omega)$},\hskip 28.45274pt(\theta_{st})^{i+1}_{N}\to\theta_{st}^{i+1}\hskip 14.22636pt\text{weakly in $\dot{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)$}.

Notice that
(\theta_{st})^{i+1}_{N}
satisfies

\big\langle V^{i}\cdot\nabla{(\theta_{st})_{N}^{i+1}},\varphi_{m}\big\rangle+\big\langle\nabla(\theta_{st})_{N}^{i+1},\nabla\varphi_{m}\big\rangle=\big\langle f,\varphi_{m}\big\rangle

for all
\varphi_{m}\in W_{m}
. Then, due to (
3.7
), we have that

(3.8)

\big\langle V^{i}\cdot\nabla\theta_{st}^{i+1},\varphi_{m}\big\rangle+\big\langle\nabla\theta_{st}^{i+1},\nabla\varphi_{m}\big\rangle=\big\langle f,\varphi_{m}\big\rangle

for all
\varphi_{m}\in W_{m}
.

Since
\mathcal{W}
is an orthogonal basis for
\dot{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, each
\varphi\in\dot{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
can be written as

\varphi(x)=\lim_{m\to\infty}\sum_{s=1}^{m}b_{s}w_{s}

Using this strong convergence in (
3.8
) we obtain

\big\langle V^{i}\cdot\nabla\theta_{st}^{i+1},\varphi\big\rangle+\big\langle\nabla\theta_{st}^{i+1},\nabla\varphi\big\rangle=\big\langle f,\varphi\big\rangle

for all
\varphi\in\dot{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.

We now extend the result to the whole space as follows. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
define
B_{n}=B(0,R_{n})
, where
\left\{R_{n}\right\}
is an increasing sequence of positive real numbers such that
R_{n}\to\infty
, when
n\to\infty
.

Since
B_{n}
is a smooth bounded domain of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
there is a unique solution
(\hat{{\theta}}_{st})^{i+1}_{n}
of the Dirichlet problem over
B_{n}
. We can extend this function to all
\mathbb{R}^{3}
as follows

(\theta_{st})^{i+1}_{n}(x)=\begin{cases}(\hat{\theta}_{st})^{i+1}_{n}(x),\hskip 28.45274pt&\text{in}\hskip 2.84544ptB_{n},\\
0,\hskip 28.45274pt&\text{elsewhere}.\end{cases}

Since we have Dirichlet boundary conditions, it follows that

\begin{split}\left\lVert(\theta_{st})^{i+1}_{n}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}=\left\lVert(\hat{\theta}_{st})^{i+1}_{n}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}(B_{n})}^{2},\end{split}

then
(\theta_{st})^{i+1}_{n}\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. As a result

(3.9)

\left\lVert\nabla(\theta_{st})^{i+1}_{n}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=\left\lVert(\nabla\hat{\theta}_{st})^{i+1}_{n}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(B_{n})}^{2}\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X},\hskip 14.22636ptn\in\mathbb{N}.

So
\left\{(\theta_{st})^{i+1}_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a uniformly bounded sequence in
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and there is a subsequence such that

(3.10)

(\theta_{st})^{i+1}_{n}\rightharpoonup(\theta_{st})^{i+1}\hskip 2.84544pt\text{weakly in }\hskip 2.84544pt\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}).

Now, for each
M\in\mathbb{N}

\left\{(\theta_{st})^{i+1}_{n}|_{B_{M}}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is uniformly bounded. Then, there is a subsequence such that

(\theta_{st})^{i+1}_{n}\to\theta_{st}^{i+1}\hskip 2.84544pt\text{strongly in }\hskip 2.84544ptL^{2}(B_{M}).

So, for
M=1
there is a subsequence
\left\{(\theta_{st})_{n,1}^{i+1}|_{B_{1}}\right\}
of
\left\{(\theta_{st})^{i+1}_{n}|_{B_{1}}\right\}
such that

(\theta_{st})^{i+1}_{n,1}\to(\theta_{st})_{1}^{i+1}\hskip 2.84544pt\text{strongly in }\hskip 2.84544ptL^{2}(B_{1}).

Using a diagonal argument we have that there is a subsequence
\left\{(\theta_{st})_{n}^{i+1}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
that converges strongly in
L^{2}(B_{M})
, for all
M\in\mathbb{N}
. From (
3.10
) it also follows, taking a subsequence if necessary, that
\left\{(\theta_{st})_{n}^{i+1}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges weakly in
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
to
(\theta_{st})^{i+1}
.

Now, let
\varphi_{n_{0}}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
such that
\text{supp}(\varphi_{n_{0}})\subset B_{n_{0}}
for some sufficiently large
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
. For
m>n_{0}
we have that
B_{n_{0}}\subset B_{m}
, so
\varphi_{n_{0}}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{m})
. Then, since
(\theta_{st})^{i+1}_{n}
is a weak solution to the equation in
B_{m}
, we get

\big\langle V^{i}\cdot\nabla{(\theta_{st})^{i+1}_{n}},\varphi_{n_{0}}\big\rangle+\big\langle\nabla(\theta_{st})^{i+1}_{n},\nabla\varphi_{n_{0}}\big\rangle=\big\langle f,\varphi_{n_{0}}\big\rangle.

Now, since
\left\{(\theta_{st})^{i+1}_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges weakly to
(\theta_{st})^{i+1}
in
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
\varphi_{n_{0}}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, we obtain

\big\langle\nabla(\theta_{st})^{i+1}_{n},\nabla\varphi_{n_{0}}\big\rangle\to\big\langle\nabla(\theta_{st})^{i+1},\nabla\varphi_{n_{0}}\big\rangle,\hskip 5.69046pt\text{when}\hskip 5.69046ptn\to\infty.

On the other hand, we notice that

\displaystyle\left\lvert\big\langle V^{i}\cdot\nabla\left((\theta_{st})^{i+1}_{n}-(\theta_{st})^{i+1}\right),\varphi_{n_{0}}\big\rangle\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\big\langle V^{i}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{n_{0}},(\theta_{st})^{i+1}_{n}-(\theta_{st})^{i+1}\big\rangle\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert V^{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(B_{m})}\left\lVert(\theta_{st})^{i+1}_{n}-(\theta_{st})^{i+1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(B_{m})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert(\theta_{st})^{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(B_{m})}\left\lVert(\theta_{st})^{i+1}_{n}-(\theta_{st})^{i+1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(B_{m})},

where
C=\sup_{\text{supp}(\varphi_{n_{0}})}\left\lvert\nabla\varphi_{n_{0}}\right\rvert
. Since
\left\{(\theta_{st})^{i+1}_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges strongly to
(\theta_{st})^{i+1}
in
L^{2}(B_{m})
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
, it follows that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\big\langle V^{i}\cdot\nabla\left((\theta_{st})^{i+1}_{n}-(\theta_{st})^{i+1}\right),\varphi_{n_{0}}\big\rangle_{L^{2}}=0\hskip 14.22636pt\text{in $B_{m}$},

for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
. So we proved that for any
\varphi_{n_{0}}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

\begin{split}\big\langle V^{i}\cdot\nabla{(\theta_{st})^{i+1}},\varphi_{n_{0}}\big\rangle+\big\langle\nabla(\theta_{st})^{i+1},\nabla\varphi_{n_{0}}\big\rangle&=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\big\langle V^{i}\cdot\nabla{(\theta_{st})^{i+1}_{n}},\varphi_{n_{0}}\big\rangle+\big\langle\nabla(\theta_{st})^{i+1}_{n},\nabla\varphi_{n_{0}}\big\rangle\right)\\
&=\big\langle f,\varphi_{n_{0}}\big\rangle.\end{split}

Now, since
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})=\overline{C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}
, for any
\varphi\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
we have that

(3.11)

\big\langle V^{i}\cdot\nabla{(\theta_{st})^{i+1}},\varphi\big\rangle+\big\langle\nabla(\theta_{st})^{i+1},\nabla\varphi\big\rangle=\big\langle f,\varphi\big\rangle.

Then
(\theta_{st})^{i+1}
is a weak solution.

Now, we prove uniqueness of this solution. Let us suppose that there are two weak solutions
(\theta_{st})_{1}
and
(\theta_{st})_{2}
and define
\tilde{\theta}_{st}=(\theta_{st})_{1}-(\theta_{st})_{2}
. Since
\tilde{\theta}_{st}\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, from (
3.11
) we have

\big\langle V^{i}\cdot\nabla{\tilde{\theta}_{st}},\tilde{\theta}_{st}\big\rangle+\big\langle\nabla\tilde{\theta}_{st},\nabla\tilde{\theta}_{st}\big\rangle=0,

and, since
\big\langle V^{i}\cdot\nabla{\tilde{\theta}_{st}},\tilde{\theta}_{st}\big\rangle=0
, it follows

\left\lVert\tilde{\theta}_{st}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=\left\lVert\nabla\tilde{\theta}_{st}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}=\big\langle\nabla\tilde{\theta}_{st},\nabla\tilde{\theta}_{st}\big\rangle=0.

This implies
\tilde{\theta}_{st}=0
and
(\theta_{st})_{1}=(\theta_{st})_{2}
.

To prove the estimate (
3.4
) we take
\liminf
in (
3.9
) and by the semi-continuity of the norm we get

\left\lVert\nabla(\theta_{st})^{i+1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}.

∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\theta_{st}^{i}\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, satisfy

\left\lVert\nabla\theta_{st}^{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X},

where
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
,
\gamma^{*}=\gamma^{*}(f)>2
, and
\Phi(t)=e^{\Delta t}f
. There exist a unique weak solution
u^{i+1}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+},L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
to (
3.1
), in the sense that for any
\varphi\in\dot{H}^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and a.e
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
,

\big\langle u_{t}^{i+1}(t),\varphi\big\rangle_{L^{2}}+\big\langle V^{i}\cdot\nabla u^{i+1}(t),\varphi\big\rangle_{L^{2}}=-\big\langle\nabla u^{i+1}(t),\nabla\varphi\big\rangle_{L^{2}}-\big\langle V^{i}\cdot\nabla\Phi(t),\varphi\big\rangle_{L^{2}}.

Also, this solution verifies

(3.12)

\sup_{t}\left\lVert u^{i+1}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\lVert\nabla u^{i+1}(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}ds\leq C\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{3}\left[\mu_{\gamma^{*}}(1+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{2})\right]^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
\mu_{\gamma^{*}}>0
is a constant.

Proof.

The proof is based on the Galerkin approximation method and is similar to the proof of Theorem
3.1
with some modifications due to the parabolic nature of the equation, for details we refer the reader to Chapter 9, Salsa and Verzini
[
16
]
.

∎

Having proved the existence of weak solutions to (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), we now will describe and prove some decay properties of
u^{i+1}
that will be useful when we study the relation between them.

3.1.
Decay properties

Our goal now is to prove that the decay of the solution
u^{i+1}
to (
3.1
) is given by

\left\lVert u^{i+1}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\overline{C}\left(\left\lVert\theta_{st}^{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+1\right)^{5}\left(1+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{2}\right)^{2}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{3}(1+t)^{-\frac{5}{2}},

where
\overline{C}=\overline{C}(\gamma^{*}(f))
. To this end, we use the Fourier Splitting Method. We first prove some Lemmas.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
u^{i+1}
be the solution to equation (
3.1
) given by Theorem
3.2
. Then,

\left\lvert\widehat{u^{i+1}}(\xi,t)\right\rvert\leq C\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert\left\lVert\theta_{st}^{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert u^{i+1}(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}ds+\mu_{\gamma^{*}}(1+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X})\right).

Proof.

Taking Fourier Transform in (
3.1
) we get

\frac{d}{dt}\widehat{u^{i+1}}(\xi,t)+\xi\widehat{V^{i}(\xi)u^{i+1}(\xi,t)}=\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2}\widehat{u^{i+1}}(\xi,t)-\xi\widehat{V^{i}(\xi)\Phi(\xi,t)}.

Solving this ordinary differential equation, and taking into account that
u^{i+1}(0)=0
, we obtain

\begin{split}\widehat{u^{i+1}}(\xi,t)&=e^{-\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2}t}\widehat{u^{i+1}}(\xi,0)-\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2}(t-s)}(\xi\widehat{V^{i}(\xi)u^{i+1}(\xi,s)}+\xi\widehat{V^{i}(\xi)\Phi(\xi,s)})ds\\
&=-\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2}(t-s)}(\xi\widehat{V^{i}(\xi)u^{i+1}(\xi,s)}+\xi\widehat{V^{i}(\xi)\Phi(\xi,s)})\ ds.\\
\end{split}

Then,

\displaystyle\left\lvert\widehat{u^{i+1}}(\xi,t)\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert\int_{0}^{t}\left\lvert\widehat{V^{i}(\xi)u^{i+1}(\xi,s)}+\widehat{V^{i}(\xi)\Phi(\xi,s)}\right\rvert ds\leq\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert\int_{0}^{t}\left(\left\lvert\widehat{V^{i}(\xi)u^{i+1}(\xi,s)}\right\rvert+\left\lvert\widehat{V^{i}(\xi)\Phi(\xi,s)}\right\rvert\right)ds.

Estimating each term of the right hand side in the last inequality using that
\left\lVert\widehat{\varphi}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{1}}
, together with the fact that
\left\lVert V^{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq C\left\lVert\theta_{st}^{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}
(see (
3.3
)) and the Hölder Inequality we get

\begin{split}\left\lvert\widehat{V^{i}(\xi)u^{i+1}(\xi,s)}\right\rvert&\leq\left\lVert\widehat{V^{i}(\xi)u^{i+1}(\xi,s)}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq\left\lVert V^{i}u^{i+1}(s)\right\rVert_{L^{1}}\\
&\leq\left\lVert V^{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert u^{i+1}(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq C\left\lVert\theta_{st}^{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert u^{i+1}(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}},\end{split}

and

\left\lvert\widehat{V^{i}(\xi)\Phi(\xi,s)}\right\rvert\leq\left\lVert\widehat{V^{i}(\xi)\Phi(\xi,s)}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq\left\lVert V^{i}\Phi(s)\right\rVert_{L^{1}}\leq\left\lVert V^{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\Phi(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq C\left\lVert\theta_{st}^{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\Phi(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}.

Now, from Lemma
2.4
and
\gamma^{*}>2
, we have that

\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\lVert\Phi(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}dt\leq\mu_{\gamma^{*}}(1+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{2}}).

Then

\displaystyle\left\lvert\widehat{u^{i+1}}(\xi,t)\right\rvert\leq C\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert\left\lVert\theta_{st}^{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert u^{i+1}(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}ds+\mu_{\gamma^{*}}(1+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X})\right)\ ds.

∎

Now, using Lemma
3.3
, we establish a differential inequality for
\left\lVert u^{i+1}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
u^{i+1}
be the solution to equation (
3.1
) given by Theorem
3.2
. Then, for
m\geq 3
we have that

(3.13)

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\left\lVert u^{i+1}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}(1+t)^{m}\right)&\leq C\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{2}\left\lVert\Phi(t)\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{2}(1+t)^{m}\\
&+C\left\lVert\theta_{st}^{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert u^{i+1}(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}ds+\mu_{\gamma^{*}}(1+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X})\right)^{2}(1+t)^{m-\frac{7}{2}}.\end{split}

Proof.

We start by multiplying equation (
3.1
) by
u^{i+1}
. Then, we obtain

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert u^{i+1}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla u^{i+1}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}=\big\langle V^{i}\cdot\nabla u^{i+1}(t),\Phi(t)\big\rangle.

By Hölder inequality, Sobolev Embedding Theorem and our hypothesis on
\theta_{st}^{i}
, we have that

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert u^{i+1}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla u^{i+1}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{C}{2}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{2}\left\lVert\Phi(t)\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\nabla u^{i+1}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Rewriting this last inequality we get

(3.14)

\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert u^{i+1}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla u^{i+1}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{2}\left\lVert\Phi(t)\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{2}.

At this point we are going to use the Fourier Splitting Method. We start as usual by using Plancherel Theorem and splitting the space into a ball
B(R)
and its complement, where
R=R(t)
will be a time dependent radius. Namely,

\displaystyle-\left\lVert\nabla u^{i+1}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla u^{i+1}(t)}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}=-\left\lVert\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert\widehat{u^{i+1}}(\xi,t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\int_{B(R)}\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert\widehat{u^{i+1}}(\xi,t)\right\rvert^{2}\hskip 2.84544ptd\xi-\int_{B(R)^{c}}\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert\widehat{u^{i+1}}(\xi,t)\right\rvert^{2}\hskip 2.84544ptd\xi

\displaystyle\leq-R(t)^{2}\int_{B(R)^{c}}\left\lvert\widehat{u^{i+1}}(\xi,t)\right\rvert^{2}\hskip 2.84544ptd\xi.

Then,

-\left\lVert\nabla u^{i+1}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+R(t)^{2}\left\lVert\widehat{u^{i+1}(t)}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq R(t)^{2}\int_{B(R)}\left\lvert\widehat{u^{i+1}}(\xi,t)\right\rvert^{2}\hskip 2.84544ptd\xi.

Using this last inequality in (
3.14
) we obtain

\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert u^{i+1}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+R(t)^{2}\left\lVert u^{i+1}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{2}\left\lVert\Phi(t)\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{2}+R(t)^{2}\int_{B(R)}\left\lvert\widehat{u^{i+1}}(\xi,t)\right\rvert^{2}\hskip 2.84544ptd\xi.

Now, using Lemma
3.3
in the second term of the right hand side we have that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}

\displaystyle\left\lVert u^{i+1}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+R(t)^{2}\left\lVert u^{i+1}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{2}\left\lVert\Phi(t)\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{2}+C\left\lVert\theta_{st}^{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}R(t)^{2}\int_{B(R)}\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert u^{i+1}(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}ds+\mu_{\gamma^{*}}(1+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X})\right)^{2}\hskip 2.84544ptd\xi

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{2}\left\lVert\Phi(t)\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{2}+CR(t)^{7}\left\lVert\theta_{st}^{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert u^{i+1}(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}ds+\mu_{\gamma^{*}}(1+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X})\right)^{2}.

Finally, setting
R(t)^{2}=\dfrac{m}{(1+t)}
and using
h(t)=(1+t)^{m}
as an integrating factor it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\left\lVert u^{i+1}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}(1+t)^{m}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{2}\left\lVert\Phi(t)\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{2}(1+t)^{m}

\displaystyle+C\left\lVert\theta_{st}^{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert u^{i+1}(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}ds+\mu_{\gamma^{*}}(1+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X})\right)^{2}(1+t)^{m-\frac{7}{2}},

where
C=C(m)>0
.
∎

In the following Theorem we will use Lemma
3.4
to estimate the decay of
u^{i+1}
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
u^{i+1}
be the solution to (
3.1
) given by Theorem
3.2
. Then
u^{i+1}
satisfies the following bound

(3.15)

\left\lVert u^{i+1}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\overline{C}\left(\left\lVert\theta_{st}^{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+1\right)^{5}\left(1+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{2}\right)^{2}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{3}(1+t)^{-\frac{5}{2}},

where
\overline{C}=\overline{C}(m,\gamma^{*})>0
.

Proof.

Let us recall that by (
3.12
), we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert u^{i+1}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{3}\left[\mu_{\gamma^{*}}(1+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{2})\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{3}(1+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{2}),

so

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert u^{i+1}(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}ds+\mu_{\gamma^{*}}(1+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X})\right)^{2}\leq C\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{3}(1+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{2})(1+t)^{2}.

Using this last inequality in (
3.13
) we get

(3.16)

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}&\left(\left\lVert u^{i+1}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}(1+t)^{m}\right)\leq C\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{2}\left\lVert\Phi(t)\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{2}(1+t)^{m}\\
&\hskip 99.58464pt+C\left\lVert\theta_{st}^{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert u^{i+1}(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}ds+\mu_{\gamma^{*}}(1+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X})\right)^{2}(1+t)^{m-\frac{7}{2}}\\
&\leq C\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{2}\left\lVert\Phi(t)\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{2}(1+t)^{m}+\overline{C}\left\lVert\theta_{st}^{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{3}(1+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{2})(1+t)^{m-\frac{3}{2}}\\
&\leq C\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{2}\left\lVert\Phi(t)\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{2}(1+t)^{m}+\overline{C}\left(\left\lVert\theta_{st}^{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+1\right)\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{3}(1+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{2})(1+t)^{m-\frac{3}{2}}.\end{split}

We now integrate (
3.16
) in time. First, using the decay of the
L^{2}
norm of the heat equation and its gradient we get

\left\lVert\Phi(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\Phi(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq(1+t)^{-\left(\frac{3}{2}+\gamma^{*}+\frac{1}{2}\right)}=(1+t)^{-\left(2+\gamma^{*}\right)}

Then, we can use this estimate in combination with Lebesgue Interpolation and Sobolev Embedding Theorem in the first term on the right hand side of (
3.16
) to get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}C\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{2}\left\lVert\Phi(s)\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{2}(1+s)^{m}ds

\displaystyle=C\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\Phi(s)\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{2}(1+s)^{m}ds

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\Phi(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\Phi(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}(1+s)^{m}ds

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)^{m-\left(2+\gamma^{*}\right)}ds\leq\ \overline{C}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{2}(1+t)^{m-\left(1+\gamma^{*}\right)}.

Now, integrating directly the second term in the right hand side of (
3.16
) we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\overline{C}\left(\left\lVert\theta_{st}^{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+1\right)

\displaystyle\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{3}(1+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{2})(1+s)^{m-\frac{3}{2}}\hskip 2.84544ptds

\displaystyle\leq\overline{C}\left(\left\lVert\theta_{st}^{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+1\right)\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{3}(1+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{2})(1+t)^{m-\frac{1}{2}}.

Then,

\displaystyle\left\lVert u^{i+1}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\overline{C}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{2}(1+t)^{-\left(1+\gamma^{*}\right)}+\overline{C}\left(\left\lVert\theta_{st}^{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+1\right)\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{3}(1+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{2})(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\overline{C}\left(\left\lVert\theta_{st}^{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+1\right)\left(1+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{2}\right)\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{3}(1+t)^{-\min\left\{1+\gamma^{*},\frac{1}{2}\right\}}

\displaystyle=\overline{C}\left(\left\lVert\theta_{st}^{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+1\right)\left(1+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{2}\right)\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{3}(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

From this

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert u^{i+1}(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}ds\leq\left[\overline{C}\left(\left\lVert\theta_{st}^{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+1\right)\left(1+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{2}\right)\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{3}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+t)^{\frac{3}{4}},

then

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\left\lVert u^{i+1}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}(1+t)^{m}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{2}\left\lVert\Phi(t)\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{2}(1+t)^{m}+\overline{C}\left\lVert\theta_{st}^{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert u^{i+1}(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}ds+\mu_{\gamma^{*}}(1+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X})\right)^{2}(1+t)^{m-\frac{7}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{2}\left\lVert\Phi(t)\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{2}(1+t)^{m}+\overline{C}\left(\left\lVert\theta_{st}^{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+1\right)^{2}\left(1+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{2}\right)^{2}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{3}(1+t)^{m-2}.

As
\gamma^{*}>2

\displaystyle\left\lVert u^{i+1}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\overline{C}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{2}(1+\theta_{st})^{-\left(1+\gamma^{*}\right)}+\overline{C}\left(\left\lVert\theta_{st}^{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+1\right)^{2}\left(1+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{2}\right)^{2}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{3}(1+t)^{-1}

\displaystyle\leq\overline{C}\left(\left\lVert\theta_{st}^{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+1\right)^{2}\left(1+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{2}\right)^{2}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{3}(1+t)^{-\min{\left\{1+\gamma^{*},1\right\}}}.

This same process, repeated three times, produces the optimal decay rate

\left\lVert u^{i+1}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\overline{C}\left(\left\lVert\theta_{st}^{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+1\right)^{5}\left(1+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{2}\right)^{2}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{3}(1+t)^{-\frac{5}{2}}.

∎

3.2.
The relation between the solutions to the auxiliary equations (
3.1
) and (
3.2
)

Having proved the decay properties of the
L^{2}
norm of the solution
u^{i+1}
to (
3.1
), we are now in position to study the relation between this solution and the solution
\theta_{st}^{i+1}
to (
3.2
). The main goal is prove that

\theta_{st}^{i+1}(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{i+1}(x,t)+\Phi(x,t)\hskip 2.84544ptdt,

where
\Phi(t)=e^{\Delta t}f
. The path to accomplishing this end consists of the following. First, we define
z^{i+1}(x,t)=u^{i+1}(x,t)+\Phi(x,t)
and prove that its integral in time is the limit of a Cauchy sequence in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Next, we prove that this integral is, in fact, a weak solution to (
3.2
), thus, by the uniqueness arising from Theorem
3.1
, we arrive at the relation we are looking for.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
u^{i+1}
be the solution to (
3.1
) given by Theorem
3.2
and
\Phi(t)=e^{\Delta t}f
. Then,
z^{i+1}(t)=u^{i+1}(t)+\Phi(t)
is such that

\int_{0}^{\infty}z^{i+1}(t)\hskip 2.84544ptdt\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

Proof.

Fix
i
and define

Z^{i+1}_{n}=\int_{0}^{n}z^{i+1}(t)\hskip 2.84544ptdt.

Let us prove that
Z^{i+1}_{n}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. We have that

\begin{split}\left\lVert Z^{i+1}_{n}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\int_{0}^{n}\left\lVert z^{i+1}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\hskip 2.84544ptdt&\leq\int_{0}^{n}\left\lVert u^{i+1}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\hskip 2.84544ptdt+\int_{0}^{n}\left\lVert\Phi(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\hskip 2.84544ptdt\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{n}\left\lVert u^{i+1}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\hskip 2.84544ptdt+\mu_{\gamma^{*}}(1+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2})\\
&\leq\overline{C}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\left\lVert\theta_{st}^{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+1\right)^{\frac{5}{2}}\left(1+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{2}\right)\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{\frac{3}{2}}+\mu_{\gamma^{*}}(1+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}),\end{split}

where in the second line we used Lemma
2.4
and in the third line we used (
3.15
). So
Z^{i+1}_{n}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and the sequence
\left\{Z^{i+1}_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is uniformly bounded.

Now, we will prove that the sequence is Cauchy. For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
define

a_{n}=\int_{0}^{n}\left\lVert z^{i+1}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\hskip 2.84544ptdt.

As before we have that

\displaystyle a_{n}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{n}\left\lVert u^{i+1}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\hskip 2.84544ptdt+\int_{0}^{n}\left\lVert\Phi(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\hskip 2.84544ptdt

\displaystyle\leq\overline{C}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\left\lVert\theta_{st}^{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+1\right)^{\frac{5}{2}}\left(1+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{2}\right)\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{\frac{3}{2}}(1+n)^{-\frac{1}{4}}+\frac{1}{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\gamma^{*}}{2}}(1+n)^{-\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\gamma^{*}}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\overline{C}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\left\lVert\theta_{st}^{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+1\right)^{\frac{5}{2}}\left(1+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{2}\right)\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)(1+n)^{-\frac{1}{4}}.

Therefore
a_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
, so is a Cauchy sequence. Notice that, for all
\varepsilon>0

\left\lVert Z^{i+1}_{m}-Z^{i+1}_{n}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\int_{0}^{m}\left\lVert z^{i+1}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\hskip 2.84544ptdt-\int_{0}^{n}\left\lVert z^{i+1}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\hskip 2.84544ptdt\leq\left\lvert a_{m}-a_{n}\right\rvert<\varepsilon,

if
m>n>n_{0}
. Then
\left\{Z^{i+1}_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a convergent sequence in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
u^{i+1}
be the solution to (
3.1
) given by Theorem
3.2
. Then the function
z^{i+1}(t)=u^{i+1}(t)+\Phi(t)
satisfies

\theta_{st}^{i+1}(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}z^{i+1}(x,t)\hskip 2.84544ptdt.

Proof.

Let

Z=\int_{0}^{\infty}z^{i+1}(x,t)\hskip 2.84544ptdt.

In order to prove that
Z
is a weak solution to (
3.2
), let
\left\{Z^{i+1}_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be as in the previous Lemma. As
u^{i+1}
is a weak solution to (
3.1
) and
\Phi(t)
is a classical solution to the Heat Equation, we have that

\big\langle z_{t}^{i+1},\varphi\big\rangle+\big\langle V^{i}\cdot\nabla z^{i+1},\varphi\big\rangle=-\big\langle\nabla z^{i+1},\nabla\varphi\big\rangle,

for all
\varphi\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and almost every
t\in[0,\tau]
for
\tau>0
. Integrating over
(0,n)
in time we get

(3.17)

\big\langle u^{i+1}(n)+\Phi(n),\varphi\big\rangle+\big\langle V^{i}\cdot\nabla Z^{i+1}_{n},\varphi\big\rangle_{L^{2}}=-\big\langle\nabla Z_{n}^{i+1},\nabla\varphi\big\rangle_{L^{2}}+\big\langle f,\varphi\big\rangle.

As
\Phi(t)\to 0
as
t\to\infty
and by (
3.15
), the first term on the left hand side in (
3.17
) goes to
0
as
n\to\infty
.

Notice that for
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, we have that
\Delta\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
\nabla\varphi\in L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Then, by Hölder inequality and the Sobolev Embedding Theorem, we get

\displaystyle\big\langle V^{i}\cdot\nabla(Z^{i+1}_{n}-Z)\big\rangle\leq C\left\lVert\nabla V^{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert Z^{i+1}_{n}-Z\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{3}}\to 0,

due to strong convergence of
\left\{Z^{i+1}_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
as in Lemma
3.6
and
\nabla V^{i}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

Analogously,

-\big\langle\nabla(Z^{i+1}_{n}-Z),\nabla\varphi\big\rangle_{L^{2}}\to 0\hskip 14.22636pt\text{as}\hskip 14.22636ptn\to\infty.

So far we have that

\big\langle V^{i}\cdot\nabla Z,\varphi\big\rangle_{L^{2}}=-\big\langle\nabla Z,\nabla\varphi\big\rangle_{L^{2}}+\big\langle f,\varphi\big\rangle

for all
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Using the fact that
\overline{C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
we get

\big\langle V^{i}\cdot\nabla Z,\varphi\big\rangle_{L^{2}}=-\big\langle\nabla Z,\nabla\varphi\big\rangle_{L^{2}}+\big\langle f,\varphi\big\rangle

for all
\varphi\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Then
Z
is a weak solution to (
3.2
), and by uniqueness from Theorem
3.2
, we obtain the desired result. ∎

Finally, we prove that

is bounded by a term that depends on the

Lemma 3.8
.

Let

3.1

Let

\left\lVert\theta_{st}^{i+1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\lVert u^{i+1}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\hskip 2.84544ptdt+\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\lVert\Phi(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\hskip 2.84544ptdt,

3.3.
Weak Solution to the stationary equation

At this point, we have developed all the tools needed to prove the existence of weak solutions to equation (
1.2
). To this end, we first prove a Lemma establishing that, under certain conditions on
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}
, the
L^{2}
norm of
\theta_{st}^{i}
is bounded by a constant
M
. Following this, we prove the main Theorem of this section regarding the existence of weak solutions, and the uniqueness under specific conditions.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
\theta_{st}^{i+1}
be the solution to (
3.2
) given by Theorem
3.1
. Also, let
M
a positive constant such that
M>\mu_{\gamma^{*}}
. Additionally suppose that
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
\gamma^{*}(f)>2
. Then there is a constant
\mathcal{K}_{M}
such that if
\left\lVert\theta_{st}^{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq M
and

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}\leq\max\left\{\mathcal{K}_{M},\left(\frac{M-\mu_{\gamma^{*}}}{\mu_{\gamma^{*}}}\right)\right\},

then

\left\lVert\theta_{st}^{i+1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq M.

Proof.

By hypothesis we have that

\left(\left\lVert\theta_{st}^{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+1\right)^{5}\leq\left(M^{2}+1\right)^{5}.

Then, from (
3.18
) we obtain

(3.19)

\left\lVert\theta_{st}^{i+1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\overline{C}\left(M^{2}+1\right)^{5}\left(1+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{2}\right)^{2}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{3}+\mathcal{M}_{1}^{2}.

Let
Y=\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}
and define the polynomial in
Y
,

\left(1+Y^{2}\right)^{2}Y^{3}=\frac{M^{2}-\mathcal{M}_{1}^{2}}{\overline{C}\left(M^{2}+1\right)^{5}}.

If this polynomial has a positive root
\mathcal{K}_{M}
we will have that, when
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}\leq\mathcal{K}_{M}
,

\left\lVert\theta_{st}^{i+1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}<M^{2}.

In order to prove this, notice that the polynomial has a positive root when the term in the right hand side is positive. As
\overline{C}\left(M^{2}+1\right)^{5}\geq 0
we only need that
M^{2}-\mathcal{M}_{1}^{2}\geq 0
. In fact, by hypothesis

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{2}<\frac{M-\mu_{\gamma^{*}}}{\mu_{\gamma^{*}}}

then

\mathcal{M}_{1}=\mu_{\gamma^{*}}(1+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}^{2})<M.

So
M^{2}-\mathcal{M}_{1}^{2}\geq 0
, and we have proved that there exists a root
\mathcal{K}_{M}>0
.
∎

We are now able to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
M
and
f
as in Lemma
3.9
. Our goal is to find a unique
\theta_{st}\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
such that

(3.20)

\big\langle V\cdot\nabla\theta_{st},\varphi\big\rangle_{L^{2}}=\big\langle\nabla\theta_{st},\nabla\varphi\big\rangle_{L^{2}}+\big\langle f,\varphi\big\rangle_{L^{2}},\hskip 14.22636pt\forall\varphi\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}).

Existence:

Our first objective is to find a convergent sequence
\left\{\theta_{st}^{i}\right\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
such that its limit satisfies (
3.20
). Let us start by choosing
\theta_{st}^{0}\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
such that

\left\lVert\theta_{st}^{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq M,\hskip 28.45274pt\left\lVert\nabla\theta_{st}^{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}}^{2}.

By Theorem
3.1
there exists a weak solution
\theta_{st}^{1}
to

\begin{cases}&V^{0}\cdot\nabla{\theta_{st}^{1}}=\Delta\theta_{st}^{1}+f\\
&V^{0}=(\nabla P+\theta_{st}^{0}\ e_{3})\\
&\nabla\cdot V^{0}=0,\end{cases}

such that
\theta_{st}^{1}\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
\left\lVert\nabla\theta_{st}^{1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}}^{2}
, such that by Lemma
3.9

\left\lVert\theta_{st}^{1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq M
.
Repeating this process recursively we obtain a sequence
\left\{\theta_{st}^{i}\right\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of functions in
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
such that
\theta_{st}^{i+1}
is a weak solution to (
3.2
) and

\left\lVert\theta_{st}^{i+1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq M,\hskip 28.45274pt\left\lVert\nabla\theta_{st}^{i+1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}}^{2},\hskip 28.45274pt\forall i\in\mathbb{N}.

Now, defining
Z^{i+1}=\theta_{st}^{i+1}-\theta_{st}^{i}
, we notice that it satisfies

(3.21)

V^{i}\cdot\nabla Z^{i+1}+(V^{i}-V^{i-1})\cdot\nabla\theta_{st}^{i}=\Delta Z^{i+1}.

Multiplying (
3.21
) by
Z^{i+1}
, integrating by parts over
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and using the skew-symmetry property of the convective term we have that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla Z^{i+1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\left\lVert\nabla Z^{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\nabla Z^{i+1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\theta_{st}^{i}\right\rVert_{L^{3}}\leq\frac{1}{2}C\left\lVert\nabla Z^{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\left\lVert\theta_{st}^{i}\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\nabla Z^{i+1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2},

where we used the 2-3-6 Hölder Inequality, Sobolev Embedding Theorem and Young inequality for products. Now, by Lebesgue Interpolation, Lemma
3.9
and
\left\lVert\nabla\theta_{st}^{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}<\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}
, we have

(3.22)

\left\lVert\nabla Z^{i+1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq CM\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}\left\lVert\nabla Z^{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\hskip 28.45274pt\forall i\in\mathbb{N}.

For
Z^{i}
, we also have that

(3.23)

\left\lVert\nabla Z^{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq CM\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}\left\lVert\nabla Z^{i-1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Plugging (
3.23
) into (
3.22
) and using a recursive argument we obtain

(3.24)

\left\lVert\nabla Z^{i+1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq(CM\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X})^{i+1}\left\lVert\nabla Z^{1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 2\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}(CM\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X})^{i+1}.

Now, we choose the constant
\mathcal{C}_{M}
such that

\mathcal{C}_{M}=\min\left\{\frac{1}{CM},\mathcal{K}_{M}\right\}

where
\mathcal{K}_{M}
is given by the Lemma
3.9
. By hypothesis we have that

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}\leq\mathcal{C}_{M}\leq\frac{1}{CM},

then

CM\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}<1.

Taking
i\to\infty
in equation (
3.24
), we obtain

\left\lVert\nabla(\theta_{st}^{i+1}-\theta_{st}^{i})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}=\left\lVert\nabla Z^{i+1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\to 0.

Then
\left\{\theta_{st}^{i}\right\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, so
\theta_{st}^{i}\to\theta_{st}
strongly in
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
\left\lVert\nabla\theta_{st}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq M
.

It remains to be shown that
\theta_{st}
is in fact a weak solution to (
1.2
). By Theorem
3.1
we have that

(3.25)

\big\langle V^{i}\cdot\nabla\theta_{st}^{i+1},\varphi\big\rangle_{L^{2}}=\big\langle\nabla\theta_{st}^{i+1},\nabla\varphi\big\rangle_{L^{2}}+\big\langle f,\varphi\big\rangle_{L^{2}}\hskip 14.22636pt\forall\varphi\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}).

We will prove the convergence of the non-linear terms, the process for the other terms is analogous. Notice that

\displaystyle\left\lvert\big\langle V\cdot\nabla\theta_{st},\varphi\big\rangle_{L^{2}}-\big\langle V^{i}\cdot\nabla\theta_{st}^{i+1},\varphi\big\rangle_{L^{2}}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\left\lvert\big\langle V\cdot\nabla(\theta_{st}-\theta_{st}^{i+1}),\varphi\big\rangle_{L^{2}}\right\rvert+\left\lvert\big\langle(V^{i}-V)\cdot\nabla\theta_{st}^{i+1},\varphi\big\rangle_{L^{2}}\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\Psi_{1}+\Psi_{2}.

Recall that
V=\nabla P-\theta_{st}\ e_{3}
and
V^{i}=\nabla P-\theta_{st}^{i}\ e_{3}
. By the strong convergence of
\theta_{st}^{i}
to
\theta_{st}
in
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert V^{i}-V\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}\leq\left\lVert\nabla(\theta_{st}^{i}-\theta_{st})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\to 0,

as
i\to\infty
. Thus,
V^{i}\to V
strongly in
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
V\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

By Hölder Inequality, Sobolev Embedding Theorem and Lebesgue Interpolation we have that
\left\lVert\theta_{st}\right\rVert_{L^{2}},\left\lVert\nabla\theta_{st}\right\rVert_{L^{2}},\left\lVert\nabla\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}<\infty
. Then

(3.26)

\begin{split}\Psi_{1}\leq C\left\lVert\nabla(\theta_{st}-\theta_{st}^{i+1})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\theta_{st}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\theta_{st}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\to 0,\end{split}

because of the strong convergence of
\theta_{st}^{i}
to
\theta_{st}
.

Analogously for
\Psi_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\Psi_{2}\leq\left\lVert V^{i}-V\right\rVert_{L^{6}}\left\lVert\nabla\theta_{st}^{i+1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{3}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert\nabla(V^{i}-V)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\theta_{st}^{i+1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{3}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert\nabla(V^{i}-V)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\theta_{st}^{i+1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Since
V^{i}\to V
strongly in
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
it follows
\Psi_{2}\to 0
as
i\to\infty
.

We can conclude that

\left\lvert\big\langle V\cdot\nabla\theta_{st},\varphi\big\rangle_{L^{2}}-\big\langle V^{i}\cdot\nabla\theta_{st}^{i+1},\varphi\big\rangle_{L^{2}}\right\rvert\to 0\hskip 14.22636pt\text{as}\hskip 14.22636pti\to\infty.

We have obtained the following

\displaystyle\big\langle V\cdot\nabla\theta_{st},\varphi\big\rangle_{L^{2}}-\big\langle\nabla\theta_{st},\nabla\varphi\big\rangle_{L^{2}}=\lim_{i\to\infty}\big\langle V^{i}\cdot\nabla\theta_{st}^{i+1},\varphi\big\rangle_{L^{2}}-\big\langle\nabla\theta_{st}^{i+1},\nabla\varphi\big\rangle_{L^{2}}=\big\langle f,\varphi\big\rangle_{L^{2}},

\forall\varphi\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Thus,
\theta_{st}
is a weak solution to (
1.2
).

Uniqueness:
Let
\theta_{st,1}
and
\theta_{st,2}
two weak solutions to (
1.2
) such that

\left\lVert\nabla\theta_{st,i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}\hskip 14.22636pt\text{and}\hskip 14.22636pt\left\lVert(\theta_{st})_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}<\infty\hskip 14.22636pt\text{for}\hskip 5.69046pti=1,2.

Define
Z=\theta_{st,1}-\theta_{st,2}
and notice that it solves the following equation

\displaystyle V_{2}\cdot\nabla Z+(V_{1}-V_{2})\cdot\nabla\theta_{st,1}=\Delta Z,

where
V_{i}=\nabla P-\theta_{st,i}\ e_{3}
. Multiplying this equation by
Z
, integrating by parts, and using the skew-symmetry property of the convective term, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla Z\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{C}{2}\left\lVert\nabla Z\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\left\lVert\theta_{st,1}\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\nabla Z\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2},

where we used the Hölder Inequality, Sobolev Embedding and Young Inequality for products. By Lebesgue Interpolation for
\left\lVert(\theta_{st})_{1}\right\rVert_{L^{3}}
, Lemma
3.9
and

\left\lVert\nabla\theta_{st,i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}
we obtain

\left\lVert\nabla Z\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\left\lVert\nabla Z\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\left\lVert(\theta_{st})_{1}\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{2}\leq CM\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}\left\lVert\nabla Z\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Given that
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}<\dfrac{1}{CM}
, there exists
\delta>0
such that

\left\lVert\nabla Z\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}=\left\lVert\nabla Z\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\delta,

which is a contradiction. Thus, the solution must be unique.
∎

4.
Asymptotic Behavior of Solutions to (
1.1
)

In this last section, we are interested in the asymptotic behavior of the solutions to (
1.1
) that are close to the stationary
\theta_{st}
. To this end, we consider equation (
1.1
) with initial data
\theta_{0}=\theta_{st}+\omega_{0}
, that is, a perturbation to
\theta_{st}
from (
1.2
). We want to establish whether such initial datum leads to a solution
\theta
which tends to
\theta_{st}
when time goes to infinity. This can be formally stated as follows: let
\omega=\theta-\theta_{st}
, where
\theta
and
\theta_{st}
are solutions to (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) respectively. Then
\omega
solves

(4.1)

\begin{cases}&\partial_{t}\omega(t)+V_{\theta}\cdot\nabla\omega+V_{\omega}\cdot\nabla\theta_{st}=\Delta\omega,\\
&V_{\omega}=V_{\theta}-V_{\theta_{st}},\\
&\nabla\cdot V_{\omega}=0,\\
&\omega(x,0)=\omega_{0}.\end{cases}

If we prove that the solution to (
4.1
) goes to zero as
t\to\infty
we will have shown that
\theta
goes to
\theta_{st}
.

This section is organized as follows. We first recall, in Theorem
4.1
, the existence result for solutions to (
1.1
) by Castro, Córdoba, Gancedo and Orive
[
6
]
. Regarding the existence of a weak solution to (
4.1
), we will follow the retarded mollifiers method developed by Caffarelli, Kohn, and Nirenberg
[
5
]
. We introduce a mollifier in time,
\psi_{\delta}
, to regularize the terms
V_{\theta}\cdot\nabla\omega
and
V_{\omega}\cdot\nabla\theta_{st}
. This ensures that
\psi_{\delta}[\omega]
and
\psi_{\delta}[V_{\omega}]
will depend on
\omega
and
V_{\omega}
, respectively, only on the past time interval
t-2\delta<t-s<t-\delta
. Therefore, we will obtain a linear equation over each strip
I_{k}=[k\delta,(k+1)\delta]\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
, where
\delta=\frac{\tau}{m}
and
k=0,\dots,m-1
. By solving these equations iteratively and gluing the results, we obtain a solution
\omega_{m}
to the regularized equation over
[0,\tau]\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Finally, these solutions form a sequence
\left\{\omega_{m}\right\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
that, via energy inequalities, converge to a weak solution to (
4.1
). To this end, in Lemma
4.3
we prove existence of solutions to these linear equations and we then, in Theorem
4.5
prove convergence of
\left\{\omega_{m}\right\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
to a solution. With respect to the decay of
\omega
, in Definition
4.6
we explicitly define the concept of asymptotically stable solution. Then we demonstrate the decay of the
L^{2}
norm of
\omega
using the Fourier splitting method.

4.1.
Existence of weak solutions

In
[
6
]
, Castro, Córdoba, Gancedo, and Orive established the existence of a weak solution to (
1.1
). More precisely, their result is stated as follows.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\theta_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then, for any
\tau>0
, there exist at least one weak solution
\theta\in C([0,\tau];L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\cap L^{2}([0,\tau];H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
to (
1.1
).

Let us introduce the definition of weak solution to (
4.1
).

Definition 4.2
.

We say that a function

\omega\in L^{\infty}(0,\tau;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap L^{2}(0,\tau;\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),\hskip 5.69046pt\partial_{t}\omega(t)\in L^{2}(0,\tau;\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),

is a weak solution to (
4.1
) if for any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R})

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}&\big\langle\partial_{t}\omega(t),\varphi(t)\big\rangle_{\dot{H}^{-1},\dot{H}^{1}}\ dt+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(V_{\theta}\cdot\nabla\omega(t))\varphi(t)\ dx\ dt\\
&+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(V_{\omega}\cdot\nabla\theta_{st})\varphi(t)\ dx\ dt=-\int_{0}^{\tau}\big\langle\nabla\omega(t),\nabla\varphi(t)\big\rangle_{L^{2}}\ dt.\end{split}

Following Niche and Orive
[
13
]
closely, we find an expression for
V_{\omega}
in terms of
\omega
. Since
\nabla\cdot V_{\omega}=0
, we have that

-\Delta P_{\omega}=\partial_{x_{3}}\omega.

where
P_{\omega}=P_{\theta}-P_{\theta_{st}}
and
P_{\theta}
,
P_{\theta_{st}}
are the pressures associated to the parabolic and stationary problems respectively. Notice that this last equation correspond to a Poisson equation with external force
\partial_{x_{3}}\omega
and whose solution in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
is given by

(4.2)

P_{\omega}(x)=-\frac{1}{3S_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{\left\lvert y\right\rvert}\ \partial_{y_{3}}\omega(x-y)\ dy,

where
S_{2}
is the surface area of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Integrating by parts over
\mathbb{R}^{3}
we get

(4.3)

\begin{split}P_{\omega}(x)&=\frac{1}{3S_{2}}\text{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{x_{3}-y_{3}}{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{3}}\ \omega(y)\ dy.\\
\end{split}

Applying gradient in (
4.3
) it follows

\begin{split}\nabla P_{\omega}(x)=\frac{1}{3S_{2}}\text{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathcal{K}(x-y)\omega(y)\ dy,\end{split}

where

\mathcal{K}(x)=\left(\frac{-3x_{1}x_{3}}{\left\lvert x\right\rvert^{5}},\frac{-3x_{2}x_{3}}{\left\lvert x\right\rvert^{5}},\frac{\left\lvert x\right\rvert^{2}-3x_{3}^{2}}{\left\lvert x\right\rvert^{5}}\right).

Thus,

(4.4)

V_{\omega}(x)=H_{\varepsilon}[\omega](x)=-\frac{1}{3S_{2}}\text{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathcal{K}(x-y)\omega(y)\ dy-\omega(x)e_{3}.

By Calderón-Zygmund Theorem (see Theorem 5.1, Chapter 5 in Duoandikoetxea
[
8
]
)

\left\lVert\nabla P_{\omega}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\leq C\left\lVert\omega\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\hskip 14.22636pt1<p<\infty,

where
C
is a constant that depends only on
p
. Therefore,

(4.5)

\left\lVert V_{\omega}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\leq\left\lVert\nabla P_{\omega}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}+\left\lVert\omega\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\leq(C+1)\left\lVert\omega\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\leq C\left\lVert\omega\right\rVert_{L^{p}},\hskip 14.22636pt1<p<\infty.

This also implies that

(4.6)

\left\lVert\nabla V_{\omega}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq C\left\lVert\nabla\omega\right\rVert_{L^{2}}.

Having established the initial properties of the
L^{p}
norm of
V_{\omega}
, we now continue on the path to prove the existence of weak solutions to (
4.1
). The following lemma establishes that there is a weak solution to the associated linear problem.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\tau>0
and
v_{1},v_{2}\in L^{\infty}(0,\tau;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap L^{2}(0,\tau;\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
with
\nabla\cdot v_{2}=0
. Furthermore, let
\omega_{0},\theta_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
,
\theta
a weak solution to (
1.1
) with initial datum
\theta_{0}
given by Theorem
4.1
, and
\theta_{st}
as in Theorem
1.1
. Then, there exists a unique

\omega\in L^{\infty}(0,\tau;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap L^{2}(0,\tau;\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),\hskip 5.69046pt\partial_{t}\omega\in L^{2}(0,\tau;\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),

which is a weak solution to

(4.7)

\begin{cases}&\partial_{t}\omega(t)+V_{\theta}\cdot\nabla v_{1}+v_{2}\cdot\nabla\theta_{st}=\Delta\omega,\\
&\omega(x,0)=\omega_{0}.\end{cases}

Proof.

We will first prove the existence of a weak solution to (
4.7
) on a bounded smooth domain
\Omega
with Dirichlet boundary conditions. Then, as in Theorem
3.1
, we will use this to obtain a weak solution in the whole space from a sequence of solutions defined on balls with increasing radii.

Notice that the terms
V_{\theta}\cdot\nabla v_{1}
and
v_{2}\cdot\nabla\theta_{st}
do not depend on
\omega
, therefore, we can consider them as an external force
f
in the equation and rewrite it as

(4.8)

\begin{cases}&\partial_{t}\omega(t)-\Delta\omega(t)=h\hskip 14.22636pt\text{in}\hskip 5.69046pt\Omega\times(0,\tau],\\
&\omega(x,t)=0\hskip 22.76228pt\text{on}\hskip 5.69046pt\partial\Omega,\\
&\omega(x,0)=\omega_{0},\hskip 14.22636pt\text{in}\hskip 5.69046pt\Omega,\end{cases}

where
h=-V_{\theta}\cdot\nabla v_{1}-v_{2}\cdot\nabla\theta_{st}
.

Let us prove that
h\in L^{2}(0,\tau;H^{-1}(\Omega))
. For
\varphi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, we take

\big\langle h,\varphi\big\rangle_{\dot{H}^{-1},\dot{H}^{1}}=-\int_{\Omega}(V_{\theta}\cdot\nabla v_{1}+v_{2}\cdot\nabla\theta_{st})\varphi\ dx.

Since
\nabla\cdot V_{\theta}=0
we can use the skew-symmetry property of the convective term to obtain

\big\langle h,\varphi\big\rangle_{\dot{H}^{-1},\dot{H}^{1}}=\int_{\Omega}(V_{\theta}\cdot\nabla\varphi)v_{1}-(v_{2}\cdot\nabla\theta_{st})\varphi\ dx.

By Hölder’s inequality, Sobolev Embedding, Lebesgue interpolation and (
3.3
) we have

\begin{split}\left\lVert h(t)\right\rVert_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}&\leq C\left(\left\lVert V_{\theta}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{6}(\Omega)}\left\lVert v_{1}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{3}(\Omega)}+\left\lVert v_{2}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{3}(\Omega)}\left\lVert\nabla\theta_{st}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)\\
&\leq C\left(\left\lVert V_{\theta}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\left\lVert v_{1}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\left\lVert v_{2}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\left\lVert\nabla\theta_{st}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\right)\\
&\leq C\left\lVert\theta(t)\right\rVert_{L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\left\lVert v_{1}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert\nabla v_{1}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\hskip 85.35826pt+C\left\lVert v_{2}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert\nabla v_{2}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\theta_{st}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\\
&\leq C\left\lVert\nabla\theta(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\left\lVert v_{1}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert\nabla v_{1}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\hskip 85.35826pt+C\left\lVert v_{2}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert\nabla v_{2}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\theta_{st}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.\\
\end{split}

Squaring on both sides, using Young inequality and integrating over
(0,\tau)
we obtain

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\tau}\left\lVert h(t)\right\rVert_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}^{2}\ dt&\leq C\int_{0}^{\tau}\left\lVert\nabla\theta(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\left\lVert v_{1}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\left\lVert\nabla v_{1}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\ dt\\
&\hskip 71.13188pt+C\int_{0}^{\tau}\left\lVert v_{2}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\left\lVert\nabla v_{2}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\left\lVert\nabla\theta_{st}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\ dt.\\
\end{split}

Since
\theta\in L^{2}(0,\tau;\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
and
v_{1},v_{2}\in L^{\infty}(0,\tau;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap L^{2}(0,\tau;\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
it follows that

\left\lVert v_{i}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\left\lVert\nabla v_{i}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}<C,\hskip 14.22636pt\text{for a.e $t\in(0,\tau]$, with $i=1,2$}

and

\int_{0}^{\tau}\left\lVert\nabla\theta(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\ dt<\infty.

Since Theorem
1.1
implies that
\|\nabla\theta_{st}\|_{L^{2}}<M
, it follows that

(4.9)

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\tau}\left\lVert h(t)\right\rVert_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}^{2}\ dt\leq C\int_{0}^{\tau}\left\lVert\nabla\theta(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\ dt+CM^{2}\tau.\end{split}

Therefore
h\in L^{2}(0,\tau;H^{-1}(\Omega))
.

By the arguments in Theorem 9.6 in Section 9.3 and in Section 9.4 in Salsa and Verzini
[
16
]
, there is a unique weak solution
\omega
to (
4.8
), and the following energy estimates hold, for every
t\in[0,\tau]

(4.10)

\left\lVert\omega(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\nabla\omega(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}ds\leq\left\lVert\omega_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert h(s)\right\rVert_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}ds,

and

(4.11)

\ \int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\partial_{s}\omega(s)\right\rVert_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}^{2}ds\leq 2\left\lVert\omega_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+4\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert h(s)\right\rVert_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}ds.

We proved existence of a solution in a smooth bounded domain
\Omega
. To extend the result to the whole space we proceed as in Theorem
3.1
, by taking a sequence of balls with radii
R_{n}
going to infinity and using a diagonal argument to show that a global solution exists. We omit the details.

Finally by the lower semi-continuity of the norm, together with (
4.10
) and (
4.11
) it follows that

\left\lVert\omega(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\nabla\omega(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}ds\leq\left\lVert\omega_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert h(s)\right\rVert_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}ds

and

\ \int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\partial_{s}\omega(s)\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}ds\leq 2\left\lVert\omega_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}+4\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert h(s)\right\rVert_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}ds.

∎

Having established the existence of a weak solution to the associated linear problem, we now introduce the mollifier
\psi_{\delta}
that we will use to turn (
4.1
) into a suitable form to apply Lemma
4.3
. This is important because the regularity of solutions obtained in Theorem
4.5
will allow us to apply the Fourier Splitting Method rigorously to prove decay rates. We choose
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that

\psi\geq 0,\hskip 14.22636pt\text{supp $\psi$}\subset[1,2],\hskip 14.22636pt\text{and}\hskip 14.22636pt\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(y,s)ds=1.

For a given function
g=g(x,t)
with
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
t\in[0,\tau]
, we define

\tilde{g}(x,t)=\begin{cases}g(x,t),\hskip 28.45274pt&x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},t\in[0,\tau],\\
0,\hskip 28.45274pt&x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},t\in(\tau,\infty).\end{cases}

Now, we set

(4.12)

\psi_{\delta}[g](x,t)=\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi\left(\frac{s}{\delta}\right)\tilde{g}(x,t-s)\ ds,\hskip 14.22636ptt>0.

Using the change of variable
s^{\prime}=\dfrac{s}{\delta}
in (
4.12
) we obtain

\psi_{\delta}[g](x,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi\left(s^{\prime}\right)\tilde{g}(x,t-\delta s^{\prime})\ dy\ ds^{\prime}.

Since
\text{supp $\psi$}\subset[1,2]
, it follows that if
s^{\prime}\in(0,1)\cup(2,\infty)
, then
\psi_{\delta}(g)(x,t)=0
. On the other hand, when
t-2\delta<t-s^{\prime}<t-\delta
, given that
\tilde{g}
vanishes when
t-s<0
, it follows that
\psi_{\delta}(g)(x,t)=0
for
\psi\neq 0
. Therefore, the values of
\psi_{\delta}[g](x,t)
are determined by the past times
t-s
of
\tilde{g}(x,s)
, that satisfy
t-2\delta<t-s<t-\delta
.

The following Lemma, from Caffarelli, Kohn and Nirenberg
[
5
]
, provides some properties of
\psi_{\delta}
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
v\in L^{\infty}(0,\tau;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap L^{2}(0,\tau;\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
such that
\nabla\cdot v=0
, then

\nabla\cdot\psi_{\delta}[v]=0,

(4.13)

\sup_{0<t<\tau}\left\lVert\psi_{\delta}[v](t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq C\sup_{0<t<\tau}\left\lVert v(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}},

(4.14)

\int_{0}^{\tau}\left\lVert\nabla\psi_{\delta}[v](t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\ dt\leq C\int_{0}^{\tau}\left\lVert\nabla v(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\ dt.

Furthermore,
\psi_{\delta}[v]\in L^{\infty}(0,\tau;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap L^{2}(0,\tau;\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
.

Using (
4.4
) we now define

\psi_{\delta}[V_{\omega_{m}}]=\psi_{\delta}[H_{\varepsilon}(\omega_{m})],

which leads to the linear version of equation (
4.1
) in an interval
I_{k}=\mathbb{R}^{3}\times[k\delta,(k+1)\delta]

(4.15)

\partial_{t}\omega_{m}+V_{\theta}\cdot\nabla\psi_{\delta}[\omega_{m}]+\psi_{\delta}[V_{\omega_{m}}]\cdot\nabla\theta_{st}=\Delta\omega_{m},\hskip 14.22636pt\omega_{m}(x,0)=\omega_{0}(x).

By Lemma
4.4
,

\psi_{\delta}[V_{\omega}],\psi_{\delta}[\omega]\in L^{\infty}(0,\tau;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap L^{2}(0,\tau;\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))

provided that
\omega\in L^{\infty}(0,\tau;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap L^{2}(0,\tau;\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
. Therefore, we can use Lemma
4.3
to obtain a weak solution
\omega_{m,0}=\omega_{m}\big\rvert_{I_{0}}
to

\begin{cases}&\partial_{t}\omega_{m,0}+V_{\theta}\cdot\nabla\psi_{\delta}[\omega_{m,0}]+\psi_{\delta}[V_{\omega_{m,0}}]\cdot\nabla\theta_{st}=\Delta\omega_{m,0},\hskip 14.22636pt\text{in}\hskip 14.22636ptI_{0}=\mathbb{R}^{3}\times[0,\delta],\\
&\omega_{m,0}(x,0)=\omega_{0}(x).\end{cases}

Applying Lemma
4.3
again, but now with
\omega_{m,0}
as initial data, we obtain a weak solution
\omega_{m,1}=\omega_{m}\big\rvert_{I_{1}}
to

\begin{cases}&\partial_{t}\omega_{m,1}+V_{\theta}\cdot\nabla\psi_{\delta}[\omega_{m,1}]+\psi_{\delta}[V_{\omega_{m,1}}]\cdot\nabla\theta_{st}=\Delta\omega_{m,1},\hskip 14.22636pt\text{in}\hskip 14.22636ptI_{1}=\mathbb{R}^{3}\times[\delta,2\delta],\\
&\omega_{m,1}(x,0)=\omega_{m,0}(x,\delta).\end{cases}

Proceeding inductively, we obtain a unique weak solution to (
4.15
) given by

(4.16)

\begin{split}&\omega_{m}\in L^{\infty}(0,\tau;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap L^{2}(0,\tau;\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),\\
&\omega_{m}(x,t)=\omega_{m,k}(x,t)\hskip 5.69046pt\text{if}\hskip 5.69046pt(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times[k\delta,(k+1)\delta],\end{split}

over
\mathbb{R}^{3}\times[0,\tau]
, with
\delta=\frac{\tau}{m}
,
k=0,\dots,m-1
, and for
\tau>0
.

In the next Lemma we establish the existence of a weak solution to (
4.1
), based on the previously described construction.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\theta_{0},\omega_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, and let
f
and
\theta_{st}
be as in Theorem
1.1
. Then there is a weak solution to (
4.1
).

Proof.

Let
\left\{\omega_{m}\right\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
be the sequence of solutions defined in (
4.16
). Multiplying (
4.15
) by
\omega_{m}
, integrating by parts over space, and integrating in time we obtain

(4.17)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\omega_{m}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\nabla\omega_{m}(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}ds&=\left\lVert\omega_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\psi_{\delta}[V_{\omega_{m}}]\cdot\nabla\theta_{st})\omega_{m}\ dx\ ds\\
&\hskip 42.67912pt-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(V_{\theta}\cdot\nabla\psi_{\delta}[\omega_{m}])\omega_{m}\ dx\ ds.\end{split}

By Hölder Inequality, Sobolev Embedding and Lebesgue Interpolation we have

(4.18)

\begin{split}-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\psi_{\delta}[V_{\omega_{m}}]\cdot\nabla\theta_{st})\omega_{m}\ dx\ ds&\leq\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\psi_{\delta}[V_{\omega_{m}}]\right\rVert_{L^{6}}\left\lVert\theta_{st}\right\rVert_{L^{3}}\left\lVert\nabla\omega_{m}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{t}C\left\lVert\nabla\psi_{\delta}[V_{\omega_{m}}]\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\theta_{st}\right\rVert_{L^{3}}\left\lVert\nabla\omega_{m}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}ds\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{t}C\left\lVert\nabla\psi_{\delta}[V_{\omega_{m}}]\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\theta_{st}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\theta_{st}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\omega_{m}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}ds\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{t}CM\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}}\left\lVert\nabla\psi_{\delta}[V_{\omega_{m}}]\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\omega_{m}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}ds,\\
\end{split}

where we have used Theorem
1.1
. Analogously, by (
3.3
) we have that
\left\lVert V_{\theta}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\leq C\left\lVert\theta(t)\right\rVert_{L^{p}}
for
1<p<\infty
, then

\begin{split}-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(V_{\theta}\cdot\nabla\psi_{\delta}[\omega_{m}])\omega_{m}&\ dx\ ds\leq\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert V_{\theta}\right\rVert_{L^{3}}\left\lVert\nabla\psi_{\delta}[\omega_{m}]\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\omega_{m}(s)\right\rVert_{L^{6}}\ ds\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{t}C\left\lVert\theta(s)\right\rVert_{L^{3}}\left\lVert\nabla\psi_{\delta}[\omega_{m}]\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\omega_{m}(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\ ds\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{t}C\left\lVert\theta(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\theta(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\psi_{\delta}[\omega_{m}]\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\omega_{m}(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\ ds.\end{split}

Given that
\theta\in C([0,\tau];L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap L^{2}([0,\tau];H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
for
\tau>0
, there exists
t_{0}\in(0,t)
that depends on
\theta_{0}
such that

(4.19)

\left\lVert\theta(s)\right\rVert_{L^{3}}\leq C\left\lVert\nabla\theta(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert\theta(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}<C\hskip 14.22636pt\forall\ s\in(t_{0},t),

which implies that

(4.20)

-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(V_{\theta}\cdot\nabla\psi_{\delta}[\omega_{m}])\omega_{m}\ dx\ ds\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\nabla\psi_{\delta}[\omega_{m}]\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\omega_{m}(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\ ds.

From (
4.18
), (
4.20
) and by Young inequality for products it follows that we estimate the right hand of (
4.17
) through

(4.21)

\begin{split}&-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\psi_{\delta}[V_{\omega_{m}}]\cdot\nabla\theta_{st})\omega_{m}\ dx\ ds-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(V_{\theta}\cdot\nabla\psi_{\delta}[\omega_{m}])\omega_{m}\ dx\ ds\\

Plugging (

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\omega_{m}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\nabla\omega_{m}(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}ds\leq\left\lVert\omega_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}&+CM^{2}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\nabla\psi_{\delta}[V_{\omega_{m}}]\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}ds\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt+C\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\nabla\psi_{\delta}[\omega_{m}]\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\ ds.\\
\end{split}

By Lemma

Therefore,

\left\lVert\omega_{m}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\nabla\omega_{m}(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}ds\leq 2\left\lVert\omega_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+2(CM^{2}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}}^{2}+C)\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\nabla\omega_{m}(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}ds.

Let us choose

for all

and any

From the weak formulation of (

\begin{split}\left\lvert\big\langle\partial_{t}\omega_{m}(t),\varphi\big\rangle_{\dot{H}^{-1},{\dot{H}^{1}}}\right\rvert\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left\lvert(V_{\theta}\cdot\nabla\varphi)\psi_{\delta}[\omega_{m}](t)\right\rvert\ dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left\lvert(\psi_{\delta}[V_{\omega_{m}}(t)]\cdot\nabla\varphi)\theta_{st}\right\rvert\ dx\\

for

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}&\left\lvert(V_{\theta}\cdot\nabla\varphi)\psi_{\delta}[\omega_{m}(t)]\right\rvert\ dx\leq\left\lVert V_{\theta}\right\rVert_{L^{3}}\left\lVert\nabla\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\psi_{\delta}d[\omega_{m}(t)]\right\rVert_{L^{6}}\\

for all

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left\lvert(\psi_{\delta}[V_{\omega_{m}}]\cdot\nabla\varphi)\theta_{st}\right\rvert\ dx\leq C\left\lVert\nabla\psi_{\delta}[V_{\omega_{m}}]\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\theta_{st}\right\rVert_{L^{3}}\left\lVert\nabla\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}.

By the Lebesgue Interpolation Theorem and Theorem
1.1

(4.26)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left\lvert(\psi_{\delta}[V_{\omega_{m}}]\cdot\nabla\varphi)\theta_{st}\right\rvert\ dx\leq CM\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}}\left\lVert\nabla\psi_{\delta}[V_{\omega_{m}}]\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}.

From Cauchy-Schwarz Inequality we also have

(4.27)

Substituting (

4.26
) and (
4.27

\left\lVert\partial_{t}\omega_{m}(t)\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}}\leq C\left\lVert\nabla\psi_{\delta}[\omega_{m}](t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+CM\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}}\left\lVert\nabla\psi_{\delta}[V_{\omega_{m}}](t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\nabla\omega_{m}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}},

and then

(4.29)

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\tau}\left\lVert\partial_{s}\omega_{m}(s)\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}}^{2}ds&\leq\frac{C}{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}\left\lVert\nabla\psi_{\delta}[\omega_{m}](s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\ ds\\
&+CM^{2}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}}^{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}\left\lVert\nabla\psi_{\delta}[V_{\omega_{m}}](s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}ds+\int_{0}^{\tau}\left\lVert\nabla\omega_{m}(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\ ds.\end{split}

Substituting (
4.23
) into (

(4.30)

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\tau}\left\lVert\partial_{s}\omega_{m}(s)\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}}^{2}ds\leq CM^{2}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}}^{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}\left\lVert\nabla\omega_{m}(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\ ds+\frac{2}{C}\left\lVert\omega_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\left\lVert\omega_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2},\end{split}

for any
\tau>0
. Inequality (

. It follows that there is a subsequence of

\displaystyle\omega_{m}\rightharpoonup\omega\ \text{weakly in }\ L^{2}(0,\tau;\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),\hskip 28.45274pt\partial_{s}\omega_{m}\rightharpoonup\partial_{t}\omega\ \text{weakly in }\ L^{2}(0,\tau;\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),

\tau>0

\omega_{m}\to\omega\hskip 14.22636pt\text{strongly in }\ L^{2}(0,\tau;L_{loc}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})).

Particularly, given

. Since strong convergence implies weak convergence, we have that

\omega_{m}\rightharpoonup\omega\ \text{weakly in }\ L^{2}(0,\tau;L^{2}(\hat{\Omega})),

for all
\tau>0
.

We now pass to the limit in

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\tau}\big\langle\partial_{t}\omega_{m}(t),\varphi(t)\big\rangle_{\dot{H}^{-1},\dot{H}^{1}}&\ dt+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(V_{\theta}\cdot\nabla\psi_{\delta}[\omega_{m}](t))\varphi(t)\ dx\ dt\\

\end{split}

for

, to prove that the limit function
\omega
is in fact a weak solution to (
4.1
). We focus on the terms containing the mollified functions, as the convergence of the other terms follows from standard arguments. Let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}([0,\tau)\times\mathbb{R}^{3})
, by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we have

(4.31)

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left\lvert((\psi_{\delta}[V_{\omega_{m}}](t)-V_{\omega}(t))\cdot\nabla\theta_{st})\varphi(t)\right\rvert dx\ dt\leq C_{\varphi}\left\lVert\nabla\theta_{st}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\int_{0}^{\tau}\left\lVert(\psi_{\delta}[V_{\omega_{m}}]-V_{\omega})(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\ dt,\end{split}

where
\left\lVert\nabla\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,\tau;L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}<C_{\varphi}
. Notice that

\begin{split}\psi_{\delta}[V_{\omega_{m}}](t)-V_{\omega}(t)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(s)H_{\varepsilon}[\omega_{m}](x,t-\delta s)\ ds-H_{\varepsilon}[\omega](x,t)\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(s)H_{\varepsilon}[\omega_{m}](x,t-\delta s)\ ds-H_{\varepsilon}[\omega](x,t)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(s)\ ds\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(s)\left(H_{\varepsilon}[\omega_{m}](x,t-\delta s)-H_{\varepsilon}[\omega](x,t)\right)\ ds.\end{split}

Then, by the Minkowski inequality for integrals and the linearity of the operator
H_{\varepsilon}
follows that

\left\lVert\psi_{\delta}[V_{\omega_{m}}](t)-V_{\omega}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(s)\left\lVert H_{\varepsilon}[\omega_{m}(x,t-\delta s)-\omega(x,t)]\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\ ds.

By (
4.5

\left\lVert\psi_{\delta}[V_{\omega_{m}}](t)-V_{\omega}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(s)\left\lVert\omega_{m}(t-\delta s)-\omega(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}ds.

Plugging (

), Fubini’s Theorem implies that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left\lvert((\psi_{\delta}[V_{\omega_{m}}]-V_{\omega})\cdot\nabla\theta_{st})\varphi(t)\right\rvert dx\

\displaystyle ds\leq C_{\varphi}\left\lVert\nabla\theta_{st}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(s)\left\lVert\omega_{m}(t-\delta s)-\omega(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}ds\ dt

\displaystyle\leq C_{\varphi}\left\lVert\nabla\theta_{st}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(s)\left\lVert\omega_{m}(t-\delta s)-\omega(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}ds\ dt

\displaystyle\leq C_{\varphi}\left\lVert\nabla\theta_{st}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(s)\int_{0}^{\tau}\left\lVert\omega_{m}(t-\delta s)-\omega(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}dt\ ds,

where
C_{\varphi}
is a constant depending on
\varphi
and on the constant given by (
4.5
). Then

(4.33)

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}&\left\lvert((\psi_{\delta}[V_{\omega_{m}}](t)-V_{\omega}(t))\cdot\nabla\theta_{st})\varphi(t)\right\rvert dx\ ds\\
&\leq C\left\lVert\nabla\theta_{st}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(s)\int_{0}^{\tau}\left\lVert\omega_{m}(t-\delta s)-\omega(t-\delta s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\omega(t-\delta s)-\omega(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}dt\ ds\\
&=C\left\lVert\nabla\theta_{st}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(s)\int_{0}^{\tau}\left\lVert\omega_{m}(t-\delta s)-\omega(t-\delta s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}dt\ ds\\
&\hskip 142.26378pt+C\left\lVert\nabla\theta_{st}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(s)\int_{0}^{\tau}\left\lVert\omega(t-\delta s)-\omega(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}dt\ ds.\end{split}

Recalling that

, let us define the following sequences

\begin{split}\Gamma_{m,1}&=\psi(s)\int_{0}^{\tau}\left\lVert\omega_{m}(t-\delta s)-\omega(t-\delta s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}dt,\\

Since

where

. Thus

as

On the other hand, since the norm and the translations are continuous in

, the limit

, as

By Hölder Inequality it follows that

Thus,
\Gamma_{m,2}\to 0
as
m\to\infty

Given that
\Gamma_{m,2}
is bounded in
L^{\infty}(0,\tau;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
, by the Dominated Convergence Theorem we have that

(4.35)

\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(s)\int_{0}^{\tau}\left\lVert\omega\left(t-\frac{\tau}{m}s\right)-\omega(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\hat{\Omega})}dt=0.

By (

Proceeding analogously, we have that

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\tau}&\big\langle\partial_{t}\omega(t),\varphi(t)\big\rangle_{\dot{H}^{-1},\dot{H}^{1}}dt+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(V_{\theta}\cdot\nabla\omega(t))\varphi(t)dx\ dt+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(V_{\omega}\cdot\nabla\theta_{st})\varphi(t)dx\ dt\\

for all

. We conclude that there exists a weak solution

By the weak lower semi-continuity of the norm in Banach spaces, together with the weak convergence of the sequence

, it follows from (

Consequently,

\omega\in L^{\infty}(0,\tau;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap L^{2}(0,\tau;\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),\hskip 5.69046pt\partial_{t}\omega\in L^{2}(0,\tau;\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),

for

.

[

Definition 4.6

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem
4.5
implies that

(4.36)

\omega\in L^{\infty}(0,\tau;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap L^{2}(0,\tau;\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),\hskip 5.69046pt\partial_{t}\omega\in L^{2}(0,\tau;\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})).

Thus,
\partial_{t}\omega(t),\nabla\omega(t)\in\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for a.e.

and, since
\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\subset\mathcal{D^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, we can interpret these linear functionals as distributions. In particular, we observe that

\big\langle\Delta\omega(t),\varphi\big\rangle_{\dot{H}^{-1},\dot{H}^{1}}=\big\langle\nabla\cdot\nabla\omega(t),\varphi\big\rangle_{\dot{H}^{-1},\dot{H}^{1}}=-\big\langle\nabla\omega(t),\nabla\varphi\big\rangle_{\dot{H}^{-1},\dot{H}^{1}}.

It follows that
\Delta\omega(t)\in\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for a.e.

. Moreover, since
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
is a Hilbert space, by the Riesz-Fréchet Theorem we can make the following identification

for a.e

and for all
\varphi\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

As

we have that
V_{\theta}(t)\cdot\nabla\omega+V_{\omega}(t)\cdot\nabla\theta_{st}\in\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for a.e

, where

\big\langle V_{\theta}(t)\cdot\nabla\omega+V_{\omega}(t)\cdot\nabla\theta_{st},\varphi\big\rangle_{\dot{H}^{-1},\dot{H}^{1}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(V_{\theta}(t)\cdot\nabla\omega(t))\varphi\ dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(V_{\omega}(t)\cdot\nabla\theta_{st})\varphi\ dx.

Now, as
\omega\in L^{2}(0,\tau;\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
, we have

(4.37)

\begin{split}\big\langle\partial_{t}\omega(t),\omega(t)\big\rangle_{\dot{H}^{-1},\dot{H}^{1}}=-\big\langle\nabla\omega(t),\nabla\omega(t)\big\rangle_{L^{2}}&-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(V_{\theta}(t)\cdot\nabla\omega(t))\omega(t)\ dx\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(V_{\omega}(t)\cdot\nabla\theta_{st})\omega(t)\ dx,\end{split}

for a.e
t\in(0,\tau]
and any
\tau>0
.

Let us recall that the spaces
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
,
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
form a Hilbert triplet. From (
4.36
) and Theorem 100 in section 7.12 of Salsa and Verzini
[
16
]
we obtain

(4.38)

\int_{s}^{t}\big\langle\partial_{r}\omega(r),v(r)\big\rangle_{\dot{H}^{-1},\dot{H}^{1}}+\big\langle\partial_{r}v(r),\omega(r)\big\rangle_{\dot{H}^{-1},\dot{H}^{1}}\ dr=\big\langle\omega(t),v(t)\big\rangle_{L^{2}}-\big\langle\omega(s),v(s)\big\rangle_{L^{2}},

for any
v\in L^{2}(0,\tau;\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),\hskip 5.69046pt\partial_{t}v\in L^{2}(0,\tau;\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
.
Notice that, by Hölder Inequality it follows

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\tau}&\left\lvert\big\langle\partial_{r}\omega(r),v(r)\big\rangle_{\dot{H}^{-1},\dot{H}^{1}}+\big\langle\partial_{r}v(r),\omega(r)\big\rangle_{\dot{H}^{-1},\dot{H}^{1}}\right\rvert\ dr\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}\left\lVert\partial_{r}\omega(r)\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}}\left\lVert v(r)\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}+\left\lVert\partial_{r}v(r)\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}}\left\lVert\omega(r)\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}\ dr\\
&\leq\left(\int_{0}^{\tau}\ \left\lVert\partial_{r}\omega(r)\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}}^{2}dr\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{\tau}\ \left\lVert v(r)\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}dr\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\left(\int_{0}^{\tau}\ \left\lVert\partial_{r}v(r)\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}}^{2}dr\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{\tau}\ \left\lVert\omega(r)\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}dr\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.\\
\end{split}

Then,
\big\langle\partial_{r}\omega(r),v(r)\big\rangle_{\dot{H}^{-1},\dot{H}^{1}}+\big\langle\partial_{r}v(r),\omega(r)\big\rangle_{\dot{H}^{-1},\dot{H}^{1}}\in L^{1}(0,\tau)
. Therefore, we can apply the Lebesgue Differentiation Theorem to (
4.38
) to obtain

(4.39)

\frac{d}{dt}\big\langle\omega(t),v(t)\big\rangle_{L^{2}}=\big\langle\partial_{t}\omega(t),v(t)\big\rangle_{\dot{H}^{-1},\dot{H}^{1}}+\big\langle\partial_{t}v(t),\omega(t)\big\rangle_{\dot{H}^{-1},\dot{H}^{1}},

for a.e
t\in[0,\tau]
and any
\tau>0
. Taking
v(t)=\omega(t)
leads to

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert\omega(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}=\big\langle\partial_{t}\omega(t),\omega(t)\big\rangle_{\dot{H}^{-1},\dot{H}^{1}},

for a.e
t\in[0,\tau]
and any
\tau>0
. Consequently, we can write (
4.37
) as

(4.40)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert\omega(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}=-\left\lVert\nabla\omega(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(V_{\theta}(t)\cdot\nabla\omega(t))\,\omega(t)dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(V_{\omega}(t)\cdot\nabla\theta_{st})\,\omega(t)dx.

By Hölder Inequality, Sobolev Embedding Theorem, and (
3.3
) we have

(4.41)

-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(V_{\theta}(t)\cdot\nabla\omega(t))\,\omega(t)dx\leq C\left\lVert\theta(t)\right\rVert_{L^{3}}\left\lVert\nabla\omega(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.

To estimate the third term in the right hand side, we use the skew-symmetry property of the convective term, to obtain

(4.42)

\begin{split}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(V_{\omega}(t)\cdot\nabla\theta_{st})\,\omega(t)\,dx&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(V_{\omega}(t)\cdot\nabla\omega(t))\,\theta_{st}\,dx\leq C\left\lVert\theta_{st}\right\rVert_{L^{3}}\left\lVert\omega(t)\right\rVert_{L^{6}}\left\lVert\nabla\omega(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq C\left\lVert\nabla\theta_{st}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert\theta_{st}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\omega(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq CM^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\omega(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2},\end{split}

where we used Hölder Inequality, (
4.5
), Sobolev Embedding Theorem, Lebesgue Interpolation and Theorem
1.1
.
Replacing (
4.41
) and (
4.42
) into (
4.40
) we have that

(4.43)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert\omega(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq-(1-C\left\lVert\theta(t)\right\rVert_{L^{3}}-CM^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}}^{\frac{1}{2}})\left\lVert\nabla\omega\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.

As
\theta\in C([0,\tau];L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap L^{2}([0,\tau];\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
, there is
t_{0}\in(0,\tau)
, that depends on
\theta_{0}
, such that

\left\lVert\theta(t)\right\rVert_{L^{3}}\leq C\left\lVert\nabla\theta(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert\theta(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}<\infty\hskip 14.22636pt\forall\hskip 5.69046ptt\in(t_{0},\tau).

Then we can choose
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}
small enough such that

A=1-C\left\lVert\theta(t)\right\rVert_{L^{3}}-CM^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}>0.

We now apply the Fourier Splitting Method. Let
B(t)
a ball centered at the origin and with a time-dependent radius
R(t)
. By Plancherel Theorem we have that

\displaystyle-A

\displaystyle\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla\omega(t)}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}=-A\int_{B(t)\cup B(t)^{c}}\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert\widehat{\omega}(\xi,t)\right\rvert^{2}\ d\xi

\displaystyle=-A\int_{B(t)}\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert\widehat{\omega}(\xi,t)\right\rvert^{2}\ d\xi-A\int_{B(t)^{c}}\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert\widehat{\omega}(\xi,t)\right\rvert^{2}\ d\xi\leq-A\int_{B(t)^{c}}\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert\widehat{\omega}(\xi,t)\right\rvert^{2}\ d\xi

\displaystyle\leq-AR^{2}\int_{B(t)^{c}}\left\lvert\widehat{\omega}(\xi,t)\right\rvert^{2}\ d\xi\ \leq-AR^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left\lvert\widehat{\omega}(\xi,t)\right\rvert^{2}\ d\xi+AR^{2}\int_{B(t)}\left\lvert\widehat{\omega}(\xi,t)\right\rvert^{2}\ d\xi.

So

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert\omega(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+AR^{2}\left\lVert\omega(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq AR^{2}\int_{B(t)}\left\lvert\widehat{\omega}(\xi,t)\right\rvert^{2}\ d\xi.

We now define the following time-dependent radius

R(t)=\left(\frac{1}{A(1+t)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

and obtain

(4.44)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert\omega(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{(1+t)}\left\lVert\omega(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{(1+t)}\int_{B(t)}\left\lvert\widehat{\omega}(\xi,t)\right\rvert^{2}\ d\xi.

From

\frac{d}{dt}\widehat{\omega}(\xi,t)+i\xi\cdot\widehat{V_{\theta}(t)\omega(t)}+i\xi\cdot\widehat{V_{\omega}(t)\theta_{st}}=\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2}\widehat{\omega(t)},

We have

(4.45)

\widehat{\omega}(\xi,t)=e^{-\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2}t}\widehat{\omega_{0}}(\xi)+\int_{0}^{t}i\xi\cdot\widehat{V_{\theta}(s)\omega(s)}+i\xi\cdot\widehat{V_{\omega}(s)\theta_{st}}\ ds,

and then

(4.46)

\widehat{\omega}(\xi,t)^{2}\leq 2\left(e^{-\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2}t}\widehat{\omega_{0}}(\xi)\right)^{2}+2\left(\int_{0}^{t}i\xi\cdot\widehat{V_{\theta}(s)\omega(s)}+i\xi\cdot\widehat{V_{\omega}(s)\theta_{st}}\ ds\right)^{2}.

Integrating (
4.46
) over
B(t)
yields

(4.47)

\begin{split}\int_{B(t)}\left\lvert\widehat{\omega}(\xi,t)\right\rvert^{2}\ d\xi&\leq 2\int_{B(t)}\left\lvert e^{-\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2}t}\widehat{\omega_{0}}(\xi)\right\rvert^{2}\ d\xi\\
&\ \ \ \ +2\int_{B(t)}\left\lvert\int_{0}^{t}i\xi\cdot\widehat{V_{\theta}(s)\omega(s)}+i\xi\cdot\widehat{V_{\omega}(s)\theta_{st}}\ ds\right\rvert^{2}d\xi.\end{split}

To estimate the second term on the right hand side of (
4.47
), we use Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and (
3.3
) to obtain

\displaystyle\left\lvert i\xi\cdot\widehat{V_{\theta}(s)\omega(s)}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert\left\lvert\widehat{V_{\theta}(s)\omega(s)}\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert\left\lVert\widehat{V_{\theta}(s)\omega(s)}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert\left\lVert V_{\theta}(s)\omega(s)\right\rVert_{L^{1}}

(4.48)

\displaystyle\leq\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert\left\lVert V_{\theta}(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\omega(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq C\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert\left\lVert\theta(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\omega(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq C\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert\left\lVert\omega(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}.

Analogously, by Theorem
1.1
it follows that

(4.49)

\left\lvert i\xi\cdot\widehat{V_{\omega}(s)\theta_{st}}\right\rvert\leq C\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert\left\lVert\omega(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\theta_{st}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq CM\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert\left\lVert\omega(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}.

By (
4.2
) and (
4.49
), we obtain

(4.50)

\left\lvert i\xi\cdot\widehat{V_{\theta}(s)\omega(s)}+i\xi\cdot\widehat{V_{\omega}(s)\theta_{st}}\right\rvert^{2}\leq C\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2}\left\lVert\omega(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Applying Jensen’s inequality together with (
4.50
) to the second term on the right hand side of (
4.47
) we obtain

\begin{split}\left\lvert\int_{0}^{t}i\xi\cdot\widehat{V_{\theta}(s)\omega(s)}+i\xi\cdot\widehat{V_{\omega}(s)\theta_{st}}\ ds\right\rvert^{2}&\leq t\int_{0}^{t}\left\lvert i\xi\cdot\widehat{V_{\theta}(s)\omega(s)}+i\xi\cdot\widehat{V_{\omega}(s)\theta_{st}}\right\rvert^{2}\ ds\\
&\leq C\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2}t\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\omega(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\ ds.\end{split}

This yields

(4.51)

\int_{B(t)}\left\lvert\int_{0}^{t}i\xi\cdot\widehat{V_{\theta}(s)\omega(s)}+i\xi\cdot\widehat{V_{\omega}(s)\theta_{st}}\ ds\right\rvert^{2}d\xi\leq C\left(\int_{B(t)}\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2}\ d\xi\right)t\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\omega(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\ ds.

From Theorem
2.5
it follows that

(4.52)

\int_{B(t)}e^{-2\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2}t}\left\lvert\widehat{\omega_{0}}(\xi)\right\rvert^{2}d\xi\leq\left\lVert e^{-\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2}t}\widehat{\omega_{0}}(\xi)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}-r^{*}}.

Using (
4.51
) and (
4.52
) in (
4.47
) we get

(4.53)

\int_{B(t)}\left\lvert\widehat{\omega}(\xi,t)\right\rvert^{2}\ d\xi\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}-r^{*}}+C\left(\int_{B(t)}\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2}\ d\xi\right)t\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\omega(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\ ds.

Now we choose
\beta>\max\left\{\frac{5}{2}+r^{*},\frac{5}{2}\right\}
and use the integrating factor
h(t)=(1+t)^{\beta}
to turn (
4.44
) into

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\left\lVert\omega(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}(1+t)^{\beta}\right)\leq C(1+t)^{\beta-1}\left((1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}-r^{*}}+(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\omega(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\ ds\right).

From this we obtain

(4.54)

\left\lVert\omega(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}(1+t)^{\frac{3}{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-r^{*}}+C\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\left\lVert\omega(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}(1+s)^{\frac{3}{2}}\ ds.

We can re-write (
4.54
) as

X(t)\leq a(t)+\int_{0}^{t}b(s)X(s)\ ds,

where

\begin{split}&X(t)=\left\lVert\omega(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}(1+t)^{\frac{3}{2}},\hskip 14.22636pta(t)=C(1+t)^{-r^{*}},\hskip 14.22636ptb(t)=C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}}.\end{split}

We consider two cases: first, we focus on the case where
r^{*}\leq 0
, which implies that
a(t)
is a non-decreasing function of
t
. Given that
a(t),b(t),X(t)
are continuous on
[0,t]
, it follows from Corollary 1.2. on page 4 of Bainov and Simeonov
[
1
]
that

X(t)\leq a(t)\exp{\int_{0}^{t}b(s)ds}.

Notice that

\int_{0}^{t}b(s)\ ds=\int_{0}^{t}C(1+s)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\ ds<\infty,

Therefore,

\left\lVert\omega(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}-r^{*}}.

Now suppose
r^{*}>0
. From (
4.54
) we obtain

(4.55)

\left\lVert\omega(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C(1+t)^{-\left(\frac{3}{2}+r^{*}\right)}+C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\omega(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\ ds.

Then let

\begin{split}X(t)=\left\lVert\omega(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2},\hskip 5.69046pta(t)=C(1+t)^{-\left(\frac{3}{2}+r^{*}\right)},\hskip 5.69046ptb(t)=(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}},\hskip 5.69046ptk(t)=1.\end{split}

From Theorem 1, page 356 in Mitrinović, Pečarić, and Fink
[
11
]
we obtain

X(t)\leq a(t)+b(t)\int_{0}^{t}a(s)k(s)\exp\left(\int_{s}^{t}b(r)k(r)\ dr\right)\ ds,

which leads to

\begin{split}\left\lVert\omega(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C(1+t)^{-\left(\frac{3}{2}+r^{*}\right)}+C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)^{-\left(\frac{3}{2}+r^{*}\right)}\ ds\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}}.\end{split}

Then

\left\lVert\omega(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C(1+t)^{-\min\left\{\frac{3}{2},\frac{3}{2}+r^{*}\right\}}.

As, from Lemma
2.6
we have that
r^{*}(\omega_{0})\leq\min\left\{r^{*}(\theta_{0}),r^{*}(\theta_{st})\right\}
, we proved our result.

∎
