Research on the BiSpikeNet classification model for tea plant pests and diseases

Yalin Hu, Shengbing Che, Mengge Yin, Jingqian Tang, Yuqi Li, Fangping Yin, Yongmin Liu

Abstract

BackgroundFast and accurate identification of tea pests and diseases with lightweight, efficient, and resource-friendly computation is a key challenge in smart tea gardens. While deep learning models like ResNet extract strong features, their high computational cost limits application. Conversely, lightweight models like BiRealNet reduce complexity but suffer from unclear intermediate features and weak discrimination.MethodsTo address these limitations, we propose BiSpikeNet, a lightweight, high-efficiency classification model integrating Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) under a “Diagnostic” design paradigm. We introduced an attention mechanism to enhance key feature representation and optimized residual structures. Furthermore, a cross-modal fusion framework was designed, combining a binary SNN meta-network (BiSNN) with a pulse-based residual block (SNNMultiShortcutBlock) to capture temporal and spatial features.ResultsThe proposed model achieved 92.30% accuracy with fewer parameters. With the cross-modal fusion framework, the accuracy improved to 99.92%. Compared with existing lightweight models such as TLDDM and LiSA-MobileNetV2, our method demonstrates superior performance with lower computational costs.ConclusionExperimental results demonstrate that BiSpikeNet provides an effective balance between accuracy and efficiency, offering a robust solution for lightweight, resource-efficient tea pest and disease detection. Code is available at https://github.com/huyalin070-svg/BiSpikeNet.

Introduction

Tea is one of the world’s most significant cash crops, particularly in Asia and many regions of China. It serves not only as the primary ingredient for residents’ beverages but also as a source of income for hundreds of millions of people (). However, during tea cultivation, diseases such as tea grey leaf spot, tea brown leaf spot, tea red leaf spot, and tea leaf spot, alongside pests including tea aphids, tea green leafhoppers, and tea leafhoppers, severely threaten both tea yield and quality (). These pests and diseases not only cause yield reductions but also inflict substantial economic losses, disrupting the tea industry chain and undermining sustainable agricultural development (). Consequently, rapid and accurate detection of tea pests and diseases is crucial for effective prevention and control, safeguarding tea yield and quality, and advancing agricultural modernization.

Traditional tea leaf inspection methods primarily encompass manual inspection and mechanical screening techniques (). Manual inspection typically relies on experienced workers visually examining tea leaves to determine their type and quality (). Mechanical screening separates tea leaves of different sizes based on mesh aperture dimensions (). Furthermore, image processing techniques such as edge detection, threshold segmentation, and colour analysis are widely employed in tea leaf identification projects. These technologies enable automated recognition of tea leaf images to a certain extent (). Such approaches rely on image pre-processing, feature extraction, and classification algorithms, enhancing detection efficacy under specific conditions (). However, their dependence on manually designed features and limited generalisation capability constrain practical application (). For instance, conventional machine learning methods typically require complex, disease-specific pre-processing and segmentation steps, making it difficult to capture deep image patterns and thus limiting their scalability in agricultural production ().

In recent years, the rapid advancement of artificial intelligence and computer vision technologies has propelled the application of deep learning in crop pest and disease identification. Compared to traditional methods, deep learning models—particularly convolutional neural networks (CNNs)—can autonomously learn high-level semantic features from large-scale image datasets, thereby significantly enhancing the accuracy and efficiency of tea pest and disease classification (). CNNs possess robust feature representation and generalisation capabilities, enabling them to process complex agricultural scenarios and accurately identify disease patterns ().Beyond agricultural tasks, deep learning has also been extensively explored for visual enhancement and complex data association in challenging environments, including underwater image enhancement via visual–textual fusion, self- organized strategies, and cascaded enhancement protocols, as well as graph-based representation learning for radar signal processing (, , ; ).

To further enhance the model’s performance in complex environments, attention mechanisms have been incorporated. This mechanism assists the model in focusing on disease-related areas, thereby improving classification accuracy (). For instance, spatial attention emphasises key regions within images to improve localisation and recognition (); channel attention dynamically adjusts the importance of different feature channels to better represent disease characteristics ().

Moreover, the Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP), as a typical feedforward neural network architecture, demonstrates robust performance in feature mapping and non-linear modelling. Models such as the MLP- Mixer, proposed in recent years for visual tasks, have also introduced novel approaches to image feature modelling (). Concurrently, Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs), representing the third generation of artificial neural networks, have garnered increasing attention. SNNs transmit information by simulating the spiking mechanism of biological neurons (), offering advantages such as event-driven computation and low power consumption. They demonstrate significant application potential in embedded and edge computing scenarios, while also providing a novel research direction for designing lightweight models for identifying tea leaf pests and diseases.

Despite significant advances in deep learning for tea leaf disease detection, numerous challenges persist in practical deployment. Factors such as symptom overlap, lighting variations, and background interference frequently cause recognition bias (). Concurrently, excessive computational demands severely constrain the efficient and lightweight application of models. The core task of image detectionlies in accurately assigning pixels to target objects, achieving precise pixel-level segmentation (). To balance accuracy and efficiency, lightweight models have emerged as a research focus, with lightweight binary neural networks becoming the preferred solution (). These models substantially reduce computational overhead by constraining weights to either +1 or -1. A representative example is BiRealNet (), which enhances representational capacity while maintaining the efficiency inherent in binary networks. However, BiRealNet exhibits notable limitations in complex real-world environments. Firstly, despite its lightweight design, the second residual block captures features inaccurately, while the third residual block fails to extract intermediate-level features of tea leaf pests and diseases with sufficient clarity. This results in progressively blurred features obtained by subsequent residual blocks, thereby weakening the model’s overall feature representation capability. On the other hand, during the binarisation feature extraction process, the model exhibits a tendency to prioritise local information such as edge textures, making it challenging to effectively capture subtle disease characteristics and their temporal variations. This constraint hinders improvements in the model’s recognition performance.

To address the aforementioned challenges, this paper proposes a novel binary spike network model, BiSpikeNet, designed to enhance perception capabilities regarding the overall structure of tea plants and diseased areas while retaining its lightweight advantages. This enables precise and robust disease identification in complex environments.

The main contributions of this paper may be summarised as follows:

- A dataset on pests and diseases from the Anhua Tea Plantation in Hunan comprising seven categories of tea plant states and 8,099 images was constructed and adopted. A standardised training, validation, and testing workflow was established through stratified random partitioning to ensure the scientific rigour and reliability of model evaluation.
- The core concept of this paper lies in proposing a “Diagnostic” model design paradigm, where in the visualisation and quantitative analysis of feature maps serve as the direct driving force for model structural optimization. Through in-depth analysis of the semantic distribution, response intensity, and information flow pathways within each module’s feature maps, this study precisely identifies critical bottlenecks and redundant regions within the architecture, thereby enabling targeted design adjustments. Attention mechanisms are introduced in critical regions to enhance feature selectivity, while computational units are streamlined along redundant pathways to improve information transmission efficiency. This closed-loop process of “observation-diagnosis-design” ensures that every structural adjustment is grounded in interpretable visual evidence, achieving a synergistic evolution between lightweight design and feature representation capability.
- This paper implements lightweight optimization of the model architecture. Firstly, an attention mechanism is introduced within the second residual block to enhance the model’s selective expression capability for key characteristics of tea leaf pests and diseases. Secondly, by reducing the number of iterations in the third residual block, the feature propagation pathway is optimised, thereby improving the clarity of mid-level feature expression. This achieves an effective balance between a lightweight structure and robust feature extraction capabilities.
- The BiSpikeNet model presented herein resolves the core challenge of spiking neural networks under ultra-low-bit quantisation through two key structural innovations: Firstly, the BiSNN module was designed to achieve efficient spiking-to-binary computation at the meta-network architecture level, substantially reducing computational and memory overhead; Secondly, it innovatively proposes theSNNMultiShortcutBlock module. By establishing multiple temporal shortcut connections, it provides stable pathways for spike information propagation across the temporal dimension, effectively mitigating the gradient decay and temporal instability issues inherent in deep binary SNN training. Building upon this foundation, the model undergoes further lightweight optimization and incorporates Adaptive-PLIF neurons alongside SpikeNorm to enhance dynamic adaptability, while employing PReLU activation to amplify temporal nonlinearity. This approach reduces computational complexity, with Params decreasing by 50% and 43.84% relative to ResNet and BiRealNet respectively. Exploration of ultra-low power technology integration at the foundational level within crop pest and disease recognition has provided novel insights for a comprehensive, integrated, and highly efficient intelligent paradigm.

Relevant work

In recent years, with the continuous advancement of deep learning models in the field of visual recognition, lightweight and high-efficiency approaches have become key research directions in model design (). Particularly in agricultural intelligent monitoring and edge computing scenarios, the key to solving the problem lies in reducing model complexity while maintaining recognition accuracy. Although traditional deep convolutional neural networks possess powerful feature representation capabilities, their substantial floating-point parameters and high computational overhead limit practical deployment on resource-constrained devices. Therefore, structurally optimized binary neural network design for lightweight, efficient, and redundancy-free computation has become a major research focus and been widely adopted, owing to its ability to significantly reduce parameters and computational overhead while retaining strong feature representation capability.

Lightweight design

In the design of deep vision models, lightweight architecture has emerged as a significant research focus. ResNet achieves outstanding performance through the stacking of numerous convolutional layers with residual connections. However, its full-precision convolutional operations and high-dimensional feature channels result in substantial parameter scales and computational complexity, making it challenging to run in lightweight, resource-saving systems. To reduce computational burden, researchers typically pursue lightweight structural designs by decreasing network depth, compressing channel counts, or introducing convolutional or depthwise separable convolutions (). BiRealNet substantially reduces model parameters and inference power consumption while preserving the residual learning mechanism (). However, relying solely on structural compression may compromise feature representation capabilities. Particularly in multi-class fine-grained recognition tasks, excessive lightweighting can lead to insufficient mid-level semantic expression, resulting in issues such as indistinct subject-background separation and lacklustre edge responses. To mitigate feature degradation caused by lightweighting, attention mechanisms are widely applied in lightweight models. Channel attention mechanisms assign differential weights to distinct feature channels, thereby enhancing key discriminative features. Spatial attention mechanisms, meanwhile, help highlight target regions while suppressing background noise. However, attention modules typically introduce additional parameters and computational overhead. If poorly designed, they may undermine the efficiency advantages of lightweight models. Furthermore, within the binary network framework, feature representations are inherently compressed, potentially limiting the modulation capabilities of attention mechanisms. Consequently, achieving an effective balance between lightweight design and attention enhancement—ensuring models maintain stable and clear featurerepresentation capabilities while preserving low computational complexity—remains a critical challenge in current lightweight binary network design.

Binarization for metaverse applications

Neural network binarisation constitutes an extreme quantisation strategy, whose core principle involves compressing weights and activations into the representation {-1, +1} to achieve exponential compression in both storage and computation (). Conventional binarisation methods typically employ the sign function for hard threshold mapping; however, this approach suffers from gradient discontinuity and optimization difficulties during training, readily leading to performance degradation (). To address this issue, BiRealNet introduces a meta-network-based quantisation mechanism utilising a Multi- Layer Perceptron (MLP). This approach models the weight binarisation process as a learnable function. Compared to fixed-threshold quantisation, the meta-network dynamically learns optimal quantisation boundaries based on data distribution, thereby enhancing the adaptive capability of the quantisation process. This method mitigates the training instability inherent in traditional binarised networks to some extent and improves model recognition performance in complex visual scenarios. However, binarisation inherently constitutes a strong information compression process, retaining only sign information while significantly reducing numerical expressiveness. Even with the adaptive learning provided by the meta- network, it remains challenging to fully compensate for the fine-grained expressive capabilities inherent in floating-point weights. In complex backgrounds or regions with subtle textural variations, binary weights may struggle to generate highly discriminative feature responses. Furthermore, the meta-network itself introduces additional structure. Although its parameter scale is relatively small, it still introduces a degree of structural complexity in extreme lightweight scenarios. Simultaneously, the gradient approximation of the quantisation function remains reliant on surrogate gradient strategies. If poorly designed, this may lead to training oscillations or reduced convergence speed. Therefore, how to further enhance feature expressiveness and training stability while preserving the efficient inference advantages of binarisation remains a crucial direction for the continuous optimization of binary networks.

Residual block

The residual learning framework, proposed by He et al., centres on mitigating gradient vanishing and decay in deep networks through identity mappings, enabling networks to learn residual representations rather than full mappings (). Within floating-point networks, residual structures have been demonstrated to significantly enhance training stability and feature representation capabilities in deep models.

BiRealNet constructs an enhanced binary residual block. This architecture integrates binary input streams, meta-network weight quantisation mechanisms, and efficient binary convolutional operations to achieve feature propagation and representation within a lightweight inference framework. However, within a binary environment, the effectiveness of residual structures remains constrained by quantisation precision limitations. Firstly, both the main branch and shortcut branch employ binary feature representations, whose expressive power is markedly restricted compared to floating-point networks. When intermediate features fail to sufficiently separate subjects from backgrounds, residual stacking may inadequately enhance discriminative information, potentially preserving redundant responses. Secondly, during multi- layer residual stacking, limited feature expressiveness across layers may yield suboptimal information accumulation, leading to edge blurring or response confusion in certain intermediate feature maps. Moreover, while reducing residual block counts for further lightweighting lowers computational costs, it may compromise feature hierarchical progression and semantic refinement capabilities, thereby affectingthe model’s robustness in complex scenarios. Overall, binary residual blocks inherit the advantages of residual learning while achieving efficient inference and low-power operation. However, there remains room for improvement in terms of fine-grained feature representation and background suppression capabilities.

Methodology

Data collection

The dataset utilised in this study was collected from multiple tea gardens within the Anhua region, Hunan Province, China, comprising 8,099 RGB images (2670 × 1200 pixels) categorised into seven tea leaf conditions: damaged tea leaves, tea mosquito blight, tea leaf brown spot disease, tea leaf red spot disease, tea leaf grey spot disease, tea leaf spot disease, and healthy tea leaves, as illustrated in . These categories were defined based on actual field conditions in local tea production and reflect typical pest and disease patterns observed in Chinese tea plantations.

To address class imbalance, a random oversampling strategy was employed by directly replicating samples from minority classes according to predefined coefficients, thereby ensuring a more balanced distribution among categories during model training. It is important to emphasise that tea pest and disease datasets inherently exhibit strong regional characteristics. For example, both the publicly available Tea Leaf Disease Dataset and TeaLeafBD, which were collected in Bangladesh and released on the Mendeley Data platform, define disease categories according to local tea cultivation conditions and regional disease characteristics. To further evaluate the robustness and generalisation capability of the proposed model, additional experiments were conducted by training and testing on the publicly available Tea Leaf Disease Dataset. The model achieved an average classification accuracy of 98%, demonstrating that the proposed approach maintains strong performance on independent public datasets and therefore possesses a certain degree of generalisation ability.

In contrast, the present study focuses specifically on tea gardens in China, and the category definitions are tailored to the regional agronomic environment of Anhua. The dataset contains samples collected under different lighting conditions and complex background scenarios, thereby reflecting the diversity and variability encountered in real tea plantation environments. As one of the representative tea-producing regions in China, Anhua provides highly representative field conditions for tea pest and disease recognition research. The classification system was developed based on extensive field experience and further validated by professional tea masters, nationally recognised tea plantation experts, and senior horticulturists with more than 20 years of experience. In addition, the study is supported by a national-level tea plantation and a university-affiliated training base.

For data partitioning, a stratified random sampling strategy was adopted, with 10% of the dataset used as the test set for final performance evaluation. The remaining 90% was further split into a training subset (80%) and a validation subset (20%) for model development and evaluation. The training subset facilitatedmodel fitting and parameter optimization, while the validation subset guided hyperparameter tuning and monitored training progress. Dataset

As shown in , the dataset employed in this study exhibits significant category imbalance. Certain disease categories possess substantially more training samples than others: tea leaf red spot disease, tea leafgrey spot disease, tea mosquito blight, and tea leaf spot disease comprise 1,143, 1,000, 1,000, and 1,113 images respectively, constituting the majority categories with larger sample sizes. In contrast, the damaged tea leaves category possesses the smallest sample size, comprising merely 439 images; the brown spot disease category also exhibits relatively limited samples, totalling 980 images. Both categories fall markedly below the overall average sample size. Such imbalanced distribution poses potential challenges during model training. Minority categories may fail to acquire sufficient feature representations during training, leading to diminished model recognition capabilities for these categories and consequently impacting overall classification performance and generalisation ability.

To mitigate the adverse effects of class imbalance, this study employs an oversampling-based class balancing strategy. Oversampling is one of the commonly used techniques for addressing class imbalance, with primary methods including random oversampling () and synthetic oversampling techniques (). In this work, we replicate minority class samples according to a predefined ratio to achieve balanced distribution with the majority class within the training set (see for details). By increasing the frequency of minority class samples in training batches, this strategy enables the model to learn their discriminative features more thoroughly during optimization, thereby enhancing classification sensitivity towards the minority class.

To further enhance the diversity of oversampled training samples and bolster model robustness, we concurrently introduce multiple geometric spatial transformations during oversampling to constitute a data augmentation pipeline. Specific augmentation operations comprise: random rotation (within an angular range of ±10°), translation (with amplitude of ±10% relative to image dimensions), scaling (with proportional variation of ±5%), and horizontal and vertical mirror filling. These enhancements are generated in real-time during training, effectively simulating perspective variations, scale discrepancies, and orientation uncertainties inherent in real-world agricultural image acquisition. This guides the model to learn key feature representations exhibiting rotational and scale invariance. This approach not only mitigates overfitting risks stemming from class imbalance to a certain extent but also enhances the model’s adaptability within complex field environments. Importantly, dataset splitting into training, validation, and test sets was performed before oversampling and augmentation, and these operations were applied exclusively to the training set to prevent data leakage.

Development of the BiSpikeNet model

Research indicates that binarisation strategies can significantly reduce model parameter size and computational complexity, serving as an effective approach for achieving efficient edge deployment. However, in practical applications, relying solely on weight and activation binarisation often diminishes feature representation capabilities. This is particularly evident in complex backgrounds or fine-grained category recognition tasks, where models may struggle with inadequate subject-background separation and insufficient edge information representation. Consequently, the core challenge addressed by this paper’s methodology is how to enhance feature modelling capabilities while preserving the efficiency of binary networks.

Among existing lightweight binary networks, BiRealNet achieves high inference efficiency by combining a residual learning framework with binary convolution operations. This architecture utilizes a binary input stream, an MLP-driven adaptive weight quantization mechanism, and XNOR-bitcount computation units to maintain reasonable recognition accuracy while ensuring low-power operation. However, compressing feature representations into the {-1, +1} format limits the network’s semantic expressiveness in intermediate layers. This may result in scattered object responses and residual background noise in feature maps.Furthermore, reducing the number of layers to meet lightweight requirements further constrains the feature abstraction capability at each layer.

To address the aforementioned issues, this paper proposes a novel binary spike neural network model, named BiSpikeNet. Building upon the principles of residual learning frameworks and efficient binary convolutions, it reconstructs and streamlines key residual modules and feature propagation pathways. This approach enhances mid-level semantic representation capabilities while maintaining an overall lightweight structure. Concurrently, a novel meta-network SNN-driven binarisation and feature modulation mechanism amplifies response intensity in critical channels and primary regions while suppressing background interference. This collaborative design effectively mitigates limitations arising from lightweight structures, information compression in meta-network binarisation, and insufficient feature aggregation in residual blocks. It achieves stable and comprehensive tea leaf structure recognition while preserving high-efficiency inference capabilities.

Lightweight module LB-BiRealNet

To more effectively extract key features from images of tea plant pests and diseases, this paper undertakes targeted module optimization and structural adjustments to the BiRealNet model. The core design comprises the following two aspects.

Firstly, given that tea disease images typically exhibit locally prominent features and moderate complexity, this paper streamlines the number of residual blocks within the model. The total residual blocks are reduced from 16 to 14, achieved specifically by decreasing the iteration count of the third residual block by two cycles. This adjustment significantly reduces the parameter size and computational burden while preserving the model’s representational capacity. This characteristic renders the model more suitable for lightweight, resource-efficient practical scenarios with strict computation limits.

Secondly, to enhance the second residual block’s ability to focus on key disease-affected regions, this study introduces a lightweight and efficient channel attention mechanism: the ECA module (). This module is embedded within the residual network architecture. The ECA module can adaptively calibrate channel feature responses with minimal computational overhead. This mechanism effectively improves the second residual block’s perception of subtle disease features and its capacity for discriminative representation learning.

Design of the BiSNN metanetwork

In this paper, we design the meta-network for convolutional operations based on a binary-based strategy for SNNs, as illustrated in .

The traditional meta-network MLP binarisation module depicted in employs a fully connected network to perform binary classification predictions on weights. Whilst this mechanism mitigates the gradient discontinuity issue introduced by the sign function to some extent, its binarisation process remains reliant on static mapping, necessitating a discretisation step to convert the binarised weights. This approach lacks dynamic expression across the temporal dimension. Furthermore, the hard-boundary classification inherent to MLP tends to cause excessive concentration of weight gradients during training, thereby constraining the model’s ability to distinguish complex features ().

To overcome this limitation, this paper proposes a novel design approach employing SNN pulse dynamics for weight quantisation. An Adaptive Parametric LIF (Adaptive-PLIF) neuron is introduced into the meta- network BiSNN as the core neuron for SNN binarisation. The membrane potential evolution of the Adaptive- PLIF neuron is described as follows: the membrane potential accumulates over time upon receiving input pulses. Upon reaching a preset threshold, the neuron fires a pulse and resets the membrane potential. Concurrently, adaptive parameters regulate the leakage and recovery rates of the membrane potential, enabling the neuron to dynamically adjust its firing characteristics based on input. This mechanism endows the weight quantisation process with temporal dynamic expression capabilities, effectively mitigating the issue of gradient convergence in binarisation while enhancing the model’s discriminative learning ability for subtle features of tea leaf pests and diseases.

Additionally, three key enhancement mechanisms have been incorporated into the SNNMultiShortcutBlock:

- Adaptive-PLIF Pulse Neuron: Used for temporal pulse integration of multi-branch outputs, enabling residual mapping to exhibit dynamic temporal characteristics.
- SpikeNorm: Performs normalization and steady-state adjustment of spikes along the temporal dimension to enhance the balance of spike firing distributions, preventing overly sparse or dense binary activations.
- Attention: Used to capture positional dependencies, it restores more fine-grained spatial features under binary conditions, helping to reduce representation loss caused by binarization.

Ultimately, the residual between the fused output and the initial output is added, followed by a nonlinear transformation via PReLU to achieve enhanced nonlinear modelling capabilities. Model design overview

To systematically evaluate the individual contributions and combined effects of reconstruction via binarization, pulse dynamics enhancement, and attention mechanisms within a binary convolutional architecture, this paper designs and compares four variants. Each variant is described as follows.

LB-BiRealNet

This variant adopts a simplified BiRealNet architecture, reducing the number of residual blocks while incorporating attention mechanisms on appropriate residual blocks. It retains the original MLP binarization and binarized convolutional functions.

BiSNNNet

This model builds upon LB-BiRealNet by designing the meta-network as an SNN temporal binarization mechanism. It employs Adaptive-PLIF neurons to achieve dynamic binary classification across timesteps, eliminating the need for subsequent discretization after binarization, unlike MLP approaches. This modification aims to mitigate the gradient clipping issues inherent in static binarization. Since spike activation is non-differentiable, a surrogate gradient strategy is adopted during backpropagation while preserving binary spike generation in the forward pass, thereby enabling smoother and more stable weight binarization. Consequently, it improves convergence speed and generalization performance.

BiMultiNet

In this variant, the SNNMultiShortcutBlock pulse residual block was designed. This module achieves multi-path feature fusion through three-branch binary convolutions. Simultaneously, it enhances the feature expression capability of the binary network under low-bit conditions via cross-temporal processing by Adaptive-PLIF neurons and SpikeNorm for pulse steady-state regulation. While maintaining other structures unchanged, it evaluates the improvement effect brought by “replacing only the residual structure” to the network.

BiSpikeNet

This model integrates the three aforementioned design categories:

- Structure-level lightweight design to reduce computational complexity while preserving expressive power.
- The appropriate incorporation of attention mechanisms serves to enhance the spatial representation of binary features.
- SNN temporal-based binarization replaces MLP binarization using Adaptive-PLIF neurons.
- SNNMultiShortcutBlock has emerged as the new residual architecture, enabling multi-path feature fusion through ternary binary convolutions.

This variant combines the advantages of structural lightweighting, meta-network binarisation, and pulse residual block stabilisation. It constitutes the complete design version proposed in this study, aiming to maximise the feature representation capability and overall accuracy of the binary network.

Model evaluation

To evaluate the performance of the BiSpikeNet model in tea pest and disease identification tasks, this study employs several commonly used evaluation metrics. These metrics include accuracy, precision, recall, F1 score, floating-point operations per second (FLOPs), and number of parameters. Each metric assesses model performance from different dimensions.

Experimental results and analysis

Experimental environment

All experiments were conducted on a system running Microsoft Windows 11, equipped with an Intel Core i5-12400F CPU and an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4070 graphics processing unit (12 GB VRAM). The software environment comprised Python 3.11, PyTorch 2.7.0, CUDA 12.6, and cuDNN 9.1.

Training was performed for 150 epochs with a batch size of 32 using the Adam optimiser with an initial learning rate of 2.5 × 10ˆ-3. A linear learning rate decay strategy was adopted using a LambdaLR scheduler to gradually reduce the learning rate over the training process. A knowledge distillation strategy was employed to improve the performance of the lightweight spiking neural network. A pretrained ResNet- 18 model was adopted as the teacher network, while BiSpikeNet served as the student network in the knowledge distillation framework. The overall training objective consists of a weighted combination of the standard cross-entropy loss and the Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence between the softened output distributions of the teacher and student networks. A distillation temperature of T = 3.0 is used to soften the probability distribution and facilitate more informative guidance from the teacher model. During training, several data augmentation strategies are applied, including random resized cropping, random horizontal flipping, and lighting augmentation, in order to improve model generalisation and robustness. The model with the highest validation accuracy is selected and saved as the final checkpoint for evaluation. The implementation of the proposed BiSpikeNet is carried out in Python using the PyTorch framework, and the corresponding code is made publicly available on GitHub for reproducibility.

BiSpikeNet classification results

To comprehensively evaluate the performance of the BiSpikeNet model in the task of identifying tea leaf pests and diseases, this study generated detailed classification reports on the test dataset. Overall, the model demonstrated stable and high classification performance across all categories. Its F1 score consistentlyremained above 0.99, exhibiting strong discriminative capability and robust generalisation ability, as shown in .

Specifically, this model achieved optimal performance in the tea leaf spot disease category. Its precision, recall, and F1 score all reached 0.9992, indicating near-perfect classification results. Similarly, it demonstrated outstanding performance across categories including damaged tea leaves, tea mosquito blight, and tea grey spot disease. F1 scores nearing 0.99 reflect how the dual design—combining lightweight architecture with SNN temporal augmentation and pulse residuals—endows the model with exceptional capability in both pest and disease identification tasks.

For the majority of categories with larger sample sizes, such as damaged tea leaves, the model maintains an F1 score of 0.9875. This indicates that even under conditions of high intra-class variation, the model remains capable of effective feature learning and generalisation.

However, the model exhibits a relatively low recall rate of 0.9655 for the tea leaf red spot disease category. Overall, the model achieved F1 scores exceeding 0.97 across all seven categories, with two categories surpassing 0.99. These results fully validate the effectiveness of structural designs including lightweight architecture, the SNNMultiShortcutBlock pulse residual block, BiSNN temporal binarisation, and attention mechanisms.

Given the complex morphology of disease lesions, the notable scale variations, and the substantial background interference present in tea-disease images, the network depth and receptive field configurations of the backbone have been deliberately tailored to facilitate more effective extraction of discriminative, fine-grained disease phenotypic features, thereby accommodating the specific demands of the real-world agricultural scenario. In summary, the CIFAR-10 experiments are primarily oriented towards algorithmic fairness verification under standardised benchmarks, whereas the tea-disease experiments are centred on feature adaptation for a specific domain task. The discrepancy in backbone structures between the two experimental setups is therefore justified by their distinct validation objectives and the inherent differences in data characteristics.

Classification accuracy and computational efficiency of ablation experiments

To systematically evaluate the independent contribution of each structural optimization scheme to the overall model performance, a series of ablation experiments were designed and executed. These experiments progressively validated the effectiveness of three design aspects: model lightweighting, the BiSNN module, and the introduction of the SNNMultiShortcutBlock pulse residual structure, as shown in . To ensure statistical power and reproducibility of the experimental results, each model configuration underwent a complete training and testing process under five independent random seeds. The final report presents the average classification accuracy and its standard deviation across the five repeated experiments. This experimental design effectively mitigates random fluctuations caused by parameter random initialisation, thereby more reliably revealing the true impact of each structural modification on model performance.

Experimental results demonstrate that the lightweight architecture LB-BiRealNet achieves an average classification accuracy of 92.30 ± 0.40%, while largely preserving the model’s representational capacity.This outcome represents a 1.28 percentage point improvement over the original BiRealNet baseline model’s 91.02%. This outcome demonstrates that rational module pruning and structural reorganisation can significantly enhance the efficiency of internal information propagation within the network. This design establishes a robust foundation for subsequent architecture enhancements.

This study employs the spiking neural network binary module BiSNN, which incorporates spiking dynamics, forming the BiSNNNet. The model achieves an average accuracy of 95.82 ± 0.38% and a precision rate of 94.23 ± 0.38%. The results validate The key role of BiSNNNet in enhancing feature discriminative power. While SNNs bolster temporal modelling capabilities and enrich representational space at low energy consumption, BiSNNNet occasionally exhibits abnormal accuracy loss during training.

Building upon this foundation, this study introduces a three-branch SNNMultiShortcutBlock and Adaptive-PLIF neurons into LB-BiRealNet to construct BiMultiNet. Five independent experiments demonstrate an accuracy of 95.78 ± 0.21% and a precision of 94.64 ± 0.20%, significantly surpassing LB- BiRealNet and baseline methods. This indicates that structural optimization and enhanced neural dynamics jointly improve feature representation capability, learning stability, and generalisation performance at both macro and micro levels.

To further enhance representation learning, a knowledge distillation strategy is incorporated during training, where a pretrained ResNet-18 teacher provides soft supervision through KL divergence with temperature scaling. An ablation study is conducted to evaluate its effect. The results show that BiSpikeNet achieves 99.13% accuracy without knowledge distillation, and 99.92% with knowledge distillation, indicating that knowledge distillation acts as a complementary optimization strategy that improves decision boundary refinement without changing the fundamental contribution of the proposed architecture.

Further analysis shows that BiSNNNet, which only includes the BiSNN module, and BiMultiNet, which includes the SNNMultiShortcutBlock but excludes BiSNN, exhibit complementary limitations. Specifically, BiSNNNet lacks sufficient structural feature aggregation capability, while BiMultiNet cannot fully exploit temporal spiking dynamics for discriminative representation. These observations indicate that neither component alone is sufficient to construct the complete BiSpikeNet framework.

The full BiSpikeNet integrates Attention, BiSNN, and SNNMultiShortcutBlock, achieving a final performance of 99.92% accuracy and 99.59% precision with consistent results across repeated experiments. In addition, the model maintains a low computational cost of 22.63M FLOPs and 117.75K parameters. Overall, the results demonstrate that the proposed architecture achieves a balanced improvement in representation capability and classification performance.

Conclusion

This paper proposes a novel binary spike neural network architecture termed BiSpikeNet. The core innovation of this model lies in the introduction and implementation of a “Diagnostic” design methodology, wherein the visualisation and quantitative analysis of internal feature maps serve as the fundamental basis for guiding its overall architectural design. Through systematic diagnostics of intermediate feature maps’ semantic distribution, response intensity, and information flow pathways, we precisely pinpointed critical bottlenecks in capturing temporal patterns and spatial details. This led to the original design of novel binary spiking information processing units and a multi-temporal short-circuit connection structure. The former directly stems from analyses of feature response sparsity and event-driven efficiency, while the latter constitutes a targeted solution to optimise deep feature flow propagation pathways. Concurrently, the model incorporates a dynamic feature calibration mechanism, wherein its weight parameters are likewise learned from spatial attention heatmaps of feature maps. This closed-loop paradigm of “observation- diagnosis-design” ensures that every structural innovation directly addresses specific challenges in feature representation. Consequently, it achieves profound synergy between the biomimetic dynamics of spiking neural networks, the ultimate efficiency of binarisation, and the visual demands of the task. Ultimately, this approach constructs a unified architecture that simultaneously delivers high accuracy, low computation waste, efficient resource usage, and robust temporal modelling capabilities.

Experimental evaluations thoroughly validate the efficacy of the original BiSpikeNet architecture. On specialised tea leaf image test datasets encompassing diverse complex scenarios, the model achieves state-of-the-art average detection accuracy, significantly outperforming multiple mainstream baseline models. In terms of efficiency, BiSpikeNet demonstrates notable advantages in key metrics such as parameter count and inference latency, successfully achieving the design objective of “high accuracy with low computational overhead”. Systematic comparative analysis further indicates that our proposed pulse- residual collaborative mechanism can more precisely capture the microscopic morphological variations and spatio-temporal evolution characteristics of tea leaf lesions. Consequently, it learns more discriminative feature representations under stringent lightweight constraints. Although the current work validates the effectiveness of BiSpikeNet through comprehensive software simulations, deployment on edge-computing and neuromorphic hardware platforms remains ongoing work. Future studies will focus on real-device implementation and optimization to further verify the practical advantages of the proposed architecture in terms of latency, power consumption, and deployment efficiency.

Despite the positive progress achieved by BiSpikeNet, future work will be pursued in the following directions to address current limitations and practical application requirements:

Firstly, enhance the model’s adaptability in extreme and unstructured environments. To address complex scenarios involving variable lighting, diverse crop varieties, and varying planting densities, future research will focus on open-world adaptive perception technologies. This includes developing agricultural scenario simulation engines to generate diverse synthetic data. Designing test-time adaptation mechanisms will enable models to rapidly adjust using online imagery. Explore domain-invariant feature learning methods to enhance intrinsic robustness.

Secondly, establish a large-scale, fine-grained, open dataset on tea plant growth and health. To overcome data limitations, we shall promote the collaborative development of a community-driven, standardised benchmark dataset. This will encompass a broader range of varieties, more detailed annotations of disease progression, and richer environmental context, thereby providing a foundation for model refinement and sector advancement.

Thirdly, explore further model compression, hardware deployment, and resource-efficient optimization to support extremely lightweight inference. Although the proposed BiSpikeNet has demonstrated promising performance in software-based simulations, practical deployment on edge-computing and neuromorphic hardware remains an important next step. The current study primarily focuses on the theoretical design and algorithmic validation of the proposed architecture. Future research will investigate the deployment of BiSpikeNet on resource-constrained edge platforms and neuromorphic processors to evaluate real-world inference latency, power efficiency, and hardware compatibility. In addition, hierarchical knowledge distillation, mixed-precision computation, and dynamic sparsification strategies will be explored to further improve computational economy and energy efficiency. Such efforts will help bridge the gap between simulation-based evaluation and practical intelligent agricultural applications.

Furthermore, we shall explore intelligent agricultural systems featuring closed-loop multimodal perception and decision-making. Future endeavours will focus on deeply integrating BiSpikeNet’s temporal visual capabilities with multispectral/hyperspectral imaging, near-surface remote sensing, meteorological and soil data, and agronomic knowledge graphs. This will establish a closed-loop system encompassing “perception-diagnosis-decision-control”, enabling a leap from pest and disease identification to trend forecasting and precision regulation. Such advancements will serve precision agriculture and sustainable development.
