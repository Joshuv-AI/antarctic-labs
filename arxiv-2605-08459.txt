Quantum hypergroups arising from ergodic coactions
Joeri De Ro

Abstract
. Given a locally compact quantum group G \mathbb{G} and an ergodic, integrable action L ∞ ​ ( X ) ↶ α G L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\curvearrowleft}}\mathbb{G} , the von Neumann algebra L ∞ ​ ( X × G ​ X ¯ ) := L ∞ ​ ( X ) □ L ∞ ​ ( X ) ¯ L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}}):=L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X})\square\overline{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X})} is shown to carry a natural normal ucp coassociative map Δ X × G ​ X ¯ : L ∞ ​ ( X × G ​ X ¯ ) → L ∞ ​ ( X × G ​ X ¯ ) ​ ⊗ ¯ ​ L ∞ ​ ( X × G ​ X ¯ ) \Delta_{\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}}}:L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}})\to L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}})\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}}) . Restricting to the class of compact quantum groups, this provides a large class of new examples of (analytical) compact quantum hypergroups. We provide characterizations of coamenability for these compact quantum hypergroups, making use of the theory of equivariant correspondences.

1.
Introduction and preliminaries

Compact quantum hypergroups are objects that simultaneously generalize classical (compact) hypergroups (see the book
[
HB95
]
and many references therein) and compact quantum groups
[
Wor87a
,
Wor87b
,
Wor98
]
. Comparing with the definition of a compact quantum group, the main difference is that the coassociative map
\Delta
of the quantum hypergroup is no longer required to be multiplicative. In the analytical setting (i.e.
C^{*}
-algebraic or
W^{*}
-algebraic framework), it is most natural to ask that
\Delta
is a unital completely positive (= ucp) map instead. Compact quantum hypergroups have been systematically studied from the purely algebraic point of view in
[
DVD11a
,
DVD11b
]
and from the
C^{*}
-algebraic point of view in
[
CV99
]
.

The first non-trivial examples of (analytical) compact quantum hypergroups that were constructed and investigated are the double coset spaces
\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H}
, arising from an inclusion
\mathbb{H}\leq\mathbb{G}
of compact quantum groups
[
CV92
,
Vai95a
,
Vai95b
,
PV99
,
CV99
,
FS00
]
. This was later generalized and studied in the case where
\mathbb{H}
is a compact quasi-subgroup
[
KS20
]
of
\mathbb{G}

[
Ka01
,
FS09
,
Zh20
,
DFW21
]
. To the best of our knowledge, no other examples of (analytical) compact quantum hypergroups have been considered in the literature. In the present paper, we provide a large class of new examples of compact quantum hypergroups, encompassing all known examples. More specifically, we will construct a compact quantum hypergroup from an arbitrary ergodic action
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\curvearrowleft}}\mathbb{G}
, where
\mathbb{G}
is a compact quantum group. This construction is inspired by the technique where a new compact quantum group is obtained by ‘reflection around a Galois object’
[
DC09a
,
DC11
,
DC17
]
. If
\mathbb{H}
is a compact quasi-subgroup of
\mathbb{G}
, our construction for the action
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Delta{G}}}{{\curvearrowleft}}\mathbb{G}
recovers the known examples of compact quantum hypergroups arising from compact quasi-subgroups.

Here is the concrete plan for this paper: In Section
2
, we start within the general setting where a locally compact quantum group
\mathbb{G}
and an integrable, ergodic right action
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\curvearrowleft}}\mathbb{G}
are given. Considering the ‘mirrored’ left action
\mathbb{G}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bar{\alpha}}}{{\curvearrowright}}L^{\infty}(\bar{\mathbb{X}})
, where
L^{\infty}(\bar{\mathbb{X}}):=\overline{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X})}
is the conjugate von Neumann algebra, we then define the von Neumann algebra

L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}}):=L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{G}}}{{\square}}L^{\infty}(\bar{\mathbb{X}})=\{z\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X})\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\bar{\mathbb{X}}):(\alpha\otimes\id)(z)=(\id\otimes\bar{\alpha})(z)\}.

Using the Galois map associated to the action
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\curvearrowleft}}\mathbb{G}
, the main result of this section (Theorem
2.4
) is the construction of a natural ucp normal coassociative map

\Delta_{\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}}}:L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}})\to L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}})\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}}).

(1.1)

However,
\Delta_{\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}}}
needs not be multiplicative, which stems from the fact that the Galois map is an isometry which is not necessarily surjective.

In Section
3
, we restrict our attention to the case where
\mathbb{G}
is a compact quantum group. We start by reviewing some basic theory of ergodic compact quantum group actions. Writing
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X})
for the algebraic core of the ergodic action
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X})\curvearrowleft\mathbb{G}
, we also consider the algebraic version

\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}}):=\{z\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X})\odot\mathcal{O}(\bar{\mathbb{X}}):(\alpha\odot\id)(z)=(\id\odot\bar{\alpha})(z)\}\subseteq L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}}),

which is shown to admit a linearly generating set of ‘matrix coefficients’
Z_{\pi}(\mu,\nu)
indexed by
\pi\in\Irr(\mathbb{G})
and
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{G}_{\pi}
, where
\mathcal{G}_{\pi}
is a canonical finite-dimensional Hilbert space associated to the action
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\curvearrowleft}}\mathbb{G}
. The map (
1.1
) is then shown to satisfy the expected formula

\Delta_{\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}}}(Z_{\pi}(\mu,\nu))=\sum_{j=1}^{m_{\pi}}Z_{\pi}(\mu,f_{j}^{\pi})\otimes Z_{\pi}(f_{j}^{\pi},\nu),

where
\{f_{j}^{\pi}\}_{j=1}^{m_{\pi}}
is an orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{G}_{\pi}
. In particular,
\Delta_{\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}}}(\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}}))\subseteq\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}})\odot\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}})
. Further, the matrix coefficients for
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}})
are used to define linear maps

\epsilon_{\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}}}:\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}})\to\mathbb{C}:Z_{\pi}(\mu,\nu)\mapsto\langle\mu,\nu\rangle,\quad S_{\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}}}:\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}})\to\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}}):Z_{\pi}(\mu,\nu)\mapsto Z_{\pi}(\nu,\mu)^{*},

which endow
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}})
with the structure of an
algebraic compact quantum hypergroup
in the sense of
[
DVD11a
]
(see Theorem
3.10
). This construction generalizes (and is strongly inspired by) the well-known case where the action
C(\mathbb{X})\curvearrowleft\mathbb{G}
is free, in which case we obtain a genuine compact quantum group as opposed to a compact quantum hypergroup. We then discuss that the norm-closure (resp.
\sigma
-weak closure) of
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}})
inside
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}})
carries the structure of a
C^{*}
-algebraic (resp.
W^{*}
-algebraic) compact quantum hypergroup.

In Section
4
, we work out two examples of general nature. In the first example, we look at the general class of coideal von Neumann algebras
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X})\curvearrowleft\mathbb{G}
, so that
\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G}
where
\mathbb{H}
is a ‘generalized quantum subgroup of
\mathbb{G}
’. We provide a more concrete description of the associated compact quantum hypergroup structure using the algebraic theory of coideals developed in
[
Ch18
,
DCDT24
]
. Special attention is given to the case where
\mathbb{H}
is a compact quasi-subgroup of
\mathbb{G}
. In the second example, we consider the natural ergodic action
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})\curvearrowleft\mathbb{G}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathbb{G}
. We make a careful analysis of the spectral data of this action (Proposition
4.4
). The
*
-algebra of the associated algebraic compact quantum hypergroup can be identified with the fusion
*
-algebra
\mathcal{O}(\operatorname{Fus}(\mathbb{G}))
. Under this identification, we recover the natural compact quantum hypergroup structure on
\mathcal{O}(\operatorname{Fus}(\mathbb{G}))
for which the (normalized) irreducible characters become group-like. This example of an algebraic compact quantum hypergroup is certainly not new. Rather, the interesting part is that the quantum hypergroup structure carries over to the
C^{*}
/
W^{*}
-closure of
\mathcal{O}(\operatorname{Fus}(\mathbb{G}))
inside
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
, which appears to be non-obvious if one does not pass through the dynamical formalism developed in this paper.

In fact, our main motivation for studying the space
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}})
comes from the theory of equivariant correspondences
[
DCDR24
,
DCDR25
,
DR25a
]
. More precisely, in
[
DCDR25
]
, a natural functor

\mathrm{Corr}{G}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X}),L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X}))\to\Rep_{*}(\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}}))

was constructed. This allows the study of certain dynamical properties of the
\mathbb{G}
-
W^{*}
-algebra
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X})
via the representation theory of the
*
-algebra
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}})
. Using this connection, we prove (Theorem
5.2
) that the counit
\epsilon_{\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}}}:\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}})\to\mathbb{C}
is bounded (for the norm coming from the inclusion
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}})\subseteq L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X})\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\bar{\mathbb{X}})
) if and only if
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X})
is
\mathbb{G}
-injective. This gives a characterization of ‘coamenability’ for the compact quantum hypergroup
\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}}
. Recalling that
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
is
\mathbb{G}
-injective if and only if
\hat{\mathbb{G}}
is amenable
[
Cr17
,
DH24
,
DR26
]
, this can be seen as a generalization of Tomatsu’s celebrated result on the equivalence between amenability and strong amenability for discrete quantum groups
[
Tom06
]
.

1.1.
Conventions and notations

All vector spaces in this paper are defined over the field
\mathbb{C}
. The symbol
\odot
denotes the tensor product (over
\mathbb{C}
). We assume that inner products of Hilbert spaces are anti-linear in the first variable. Given a subset
S
of a normed linear space
V
, we write
[S]
for the norm-closure of the linear span of
S
. More generally, if
(V,\tau)
is a topological vector space and
S\subseteq V
, we write
[S]^{\tau}
for the
\tau
-closure of the linear span of
S
inside
V
. Given
C^{*}
-algebras
C,D
, their minimal tensor product is denoted by
C\otimes D
. The multiplier
C^{*}
-algebra of
C
is denoted by
M(C)
. Given von Neumann algebras
A
and
B
, their von Neumann algebra tensor product is denoted by
A\bar{\otimes}B
. Given Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K}
, their Hilbert space tensor product is written by
\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{K}
, and we write
\Sigma=\Sigma_{\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K}}:\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{K}\to\mathcal{K}\otimes\mathcal{H}
for the switch map.

1.2.
Locally compact quantum groups

[
KV00
,
KV03
,
VV03
]
. A
von Neumann bialgebra
is a pair
(M,\Delta)
where
M
is a von Neumann algebra and
\Delta:M\to M\bar{\otimes}M
is a unital, normal, isometric
*
-homomorphism such that
(\Delta\otimes\id)\circ\Delta=(\id\otimes\Delta)\circ\Delta
. A
locally compact quantum group

\mathbb{G}
is a von Neumann bialgebra
(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}),\Delta{G})
for which there exist normal, semifinite, faithful weights
\varphi{G},\psi{G}:L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})_{+}\to[0,\infty]
such that
(\id\otimes\varphi{G})\Delta{G}(x)=\varphi{G}(x)1
for all
x\in\mathscr{M}_{\varphi}^{+}
and
(\psi{G}\otimes\id)\Delta{G}(x)=\psi{G}(x)1
for all
x\in\mathscr{M}_{\psi}^{+}
. These are called the
left Haar weight
and the
right Haar weight
, and they can be shown to be unique up to a non-zero positive scalar multiple. We denote the standard predual of the von Neumann algebra
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
by
L^{1}(\mathbb{G})
, so that
L^{1}(\mathbb{G})^{*}\cong L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
.

We denote the GNS-Hilbert space of
\varphi{G}
by
L^{2}(\mathbb{G})
. We use it to view
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})\subseteq B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))
. One can canonically identify the GNS-Hilbert space of
\psi{G}
with
L^{2}(\mathbb{G})
. Using the GNS-maps

\Lambda_{\varphi{G}}:\mathscr{N}_{\varphi{G}}\to L^{2}(\mathbb{G})
and
\Lambda_{\psi{G}}:\mathscr{N}_{\psi{G}}\to L^{2}(\mathbb{G})

we can then define the unitaries

V{G}\in B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
and
W{G}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))
,
called respectively
right
and
left
regular unitary representation. They are uniquely determined by

\displaystyle(\id\otimes\omega)(V{G})\Lambda_{\psi{G}}(x)

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{\psi{G}}((\id\otimes\omega)\Delta{G}(x)),\qquad\omega\in L^{1}(\mathbb{G}),\quad x\in\mathscr{N}_{\psi{G}},

\displaystyle(\omega\otimes\id)(W_{\mathbb{G}}^{*})\Lambda_{\varphi{G}}(x)

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{\varphi{G}}((\omega\otimes\id)\Delta{G}(x)),\qquad\omega\in L^{1}(\mathbb{G}),\quad x\in\mathscr{N}_{\varphi{G}}.

They are
multiplicative unitaries
meaning that

V_{\mathbb{G},12}V_{\mathbb{G},13}V_{\mathbb{G},23}=V_{\mathbb{G},23}V_{\mathbb{G},12},\qquad W_{\mathbb{G},12}W_{\mathbb{G},13}W_{\mathbb{G},23}=W_{\mathbb{G},23}W_{\mathbb{G},12},

and they implement the coproduct of
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
in the sense that

W{G}^{*}(1\otimes x)W{G}=\Delta{G}(x)=V{G}(x\otimes 1)V{G}^{*},\qquad x\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}).

Moreover, we have

C_{0}(\mathbb{G}):=[(\omega\otimes\id)(V{G})\mid\omega\in B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))_{*}]=[(\id\otimes\omega)(W{G})\mid\omega\in B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))_{*}],

which is a
\sigma
-weakly dense C
∗
-subalgebra of
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
. Then
\Delta{G}(C_{0}(\mathbb{G}))\subseteq M(C_{0}(\mathbb{G})\otimes C_{0}(\mathbb{G}))
.

We also define the von Neumann algebra

L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{G}}):=[(\omega\otimes\id)(W{G})\mid\omega\in L^{1}(\mathbb{G})]^{\sigma\textrm{-weak}}
together with the coproduct

\Delta_{\hat{\mathbb{G}}}:L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{G}})\to L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{G}})\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{G}})
given by
\Delta_{\hat{\mathbb{G}}}(\hat{x})=\Sigma W{G}(\hat{x}\otimes 1)W{G}^{*}\Sigma.

The pair
(L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{G}}),\Delta_{\hat{\mathbb{G}}})
then defines the dual locally compact quantum group
\hat{\mathbb{G}}
. The left invariant weight
\varphi_{\hat{\mathbb{G}}}
is constructed in a way that ensures the canonical identification
L^{2}(\mathbb{G})=L^{2}(\hat{\mathbb{G}})
. Pontryagin biduality then holds:
\hat{\hat{\mathbb{G}}}=\mathbb{G}
.

We write
J{G}
for the modular conjugation on
L^{2}(\mathbb{G})
associated to the weight
\varphi{G}
and we write
J_{\hat{\mathbb{G}}}
for the modular conjugation on
L^{2}(\mathbb{G})
associated with the weight
\varphi_{\hat{\mathbb{G}}}
. We have

J_{\hat{\mathbb{G}}}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})J_{\hat{\mathbb{G}}}=L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}),

so we obtain the anti-
*
-homomorphism

R{G}:L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})\to L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}):x\mapsto J_{\hat{\mathbb{G}}}x^{*}J_{\hat{\mathbb{G}}}.

We call
R{G}
the
unitary antipode
of
\mathbb{G}
. There is a canonical unimodular complex number
c\in\mathbb{C}
(cf.
[
KV03
]
*Corollary 2.12) satisfying

cJ_{\hat{\mathbb{G}}}J{G}=\overline{c}J{G}J_{\hat{\mathbb{G}}}.

We write
u{G}:=cJ_{\hat{\mathbb{G}}}J{G}
for the associated self-adjoint unitary. The following identities will be useful:

\displaystyle(J_{\hat{\mathbb{G}}}\otimes J{G})W{G}(J_{\hat{\mathbb{G}}}\otimes J{G})=W{G}^{*},\quad(J{G}\otimes J_{\hat{\mathbb{G}}})V{G}(J{G}\otimes J_{\hat{\mathbb{G}}})=V{G}^{*},\quad(u{G}\otimes 1)V{G}(u{G}\otimes 1)=W_{\mathbb{G},21}.

1.3.
Compact quantum groups

A locally compact quantum group
\mathbb{G}
is called
compact
if the
C^{*}
-algebra
C_{0}(\mathbb{G})
is unital. In that case, we denote it simply by
C(\mathbb{G})
. For the basic theory of compact quantum groups, we refer the reader to
[
NT14
]
*Chapter 1. We collect here some notations, conventions and useful facts. Let
\mathbb{G}
be a compact quantum group with (normal, faithful) Haar state
\varphi{G}:L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})\to\mathbb{C}
so that

(\varphi{G}\otimes\id)\Delta{G}(x)=(\id\otimes\varphi{G})\Delta{G}(x)=\varphi{G}(x)1,\quad x\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}).

We call
\mathbb{G}
of
Kac type
if
\varphi{G}
is tracial. We write
\Rep(\mathbb{G})
for the
W^{*}
-category of finite-dimensional unitary
\mathbb{G}
-representations. Its objects consist of pairs
\pi=(\mathcal{H}_{\pi},U_{\pi})
where
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
is a finite-dimensional Hilbert space and
U_{\pi}\in B(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})\odot C(\mathbb{G})
is a unitary satisfying
(\id\otimes\Delta{G})(U_{\pi})=U_{\pi,12}U_{\pi,13}
. We write
d_{\pi}:=\dim(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})
.
If
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\in\Rep(\mathbb{G})
, we write
\pi_{1}\oplus\pi_{2}\in\Rep(\mathbb{G})
for their direct sum (on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\pi_{1}}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{\pi_{2}}
) and
\pi_{1}\otimes\pi_{2}=(\mathcal{H}_{\pi_{1}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\pi_{2}},U_{\pi_{1},13}U_{\pi_{2},23})\in\Rep(\mathbb{G})
for their tensor product.
Given
\pi\in\Rep(\mathbb{G})
and
\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
, write

U_{\pi}(\xi,\eta):=(\omega_{\xi,\eta}\otimes\id)(U_{\pi})\in C(\mathbb{G})
. The linear subspace of
C(\mathbb{G})
generated by the matrix coefficients
\{U_{\pi}(\xi,\eta):\pi\in\Rep(\mathbb{G}),\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}_{\pi}\}
, will be denoted by
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})
. We have an associated Hopf
∗
-algebraic coaction

\delta_{\pi}:\mathcal{H}_{\pi}\to\mathcal{H}_{\pi}\odot\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G}):\xi\mapsto U_{\pi}(\xi\otimes 1).

The space
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})
is a norm-dense unital
*
-subalgebra of
C(\mathbb{G})
. If
\pi\in\Rep(\mathbb{G})
and if
\{e_{j}^{\pi}\}_{j=1}^{d_{\pi}}
is an orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
, then

\displaystyle\Delta{G}(U_{\pi}(\xi,\eta))=\sum_{j=1}^{d_{\pi}}U_{\pi}(\xi,e_{j}^{\pi})\otimes U_{\pi}(e_{j}^{\pi},\eta),\quad\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}_{\pi}.

In particular,
\Delta{G}(\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G}))\subseteq\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})\odot\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})
. The pair
(\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G}),\Delta{G})
has the structure of a Hopf
*
-algebra. Concretely, the counit
\epsilon{G}:\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})\to\mathbb{C}
and the antipode
S{G}:\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})\to\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})
satisfy

\epsilon{G}(U_{\pi}(\xi,\eta))=\langle\xi,\eta\rangle,\quad S{G}(U_{\pi}(\xi,\eta))=U_{\pi}(\eta,\xi)^{*},\quad\pi\in\Rep(\mathbb{G}),\quad\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}_{\pi}.

(1.2)

We fix a maximal collection
\Irr(\mathbb{G})
of irreducible
\mathbb{G}
-representations
\{U_{\pi}\in B(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})\odot\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})\}_{\pi\in\Irr(\mathbb{G})}
that are pairwise non-isomorphic. Then

\{U_{\pi}(e_{i}^{\pi},e_{j}^{\pi}):\pi\in\Irr(\mathbb{G}),1\leq i,j\leq d_{\pi}\}

forms a Hamel basis for
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})
.

Let us also write
\mathscr{U}{G}
for the algebraic linear dual of
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})
. We then have the isomorphism

\mathscr{U}{G}\cong\prod_{\pi\in\Irr(\mathbb{G})}B(\mathcal{H}_{\pi}):\omega\mapsto((\id\odot\omega)(U_{\pi}))_{\pi\in\Irr(\mathbb{G})}

(1.3)

of
*
-algebras (here
\prod
denotes the algebraic direct product), where
\mathscr{U}{G}
becomes a
*
-algebra for the product and involution given by

(\omega\star\omega^{\prime})(z)=(\omega\otimes\omega^{\prime})\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}(z),\quad\omega^{*}(z)=\overline{\omega(S_{\mathbb{G}}(z)^{*})},\quad\omega,\omega^{\prime}\in\mathscr{U}_{\mathbb{G}},\quad z\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G}).

With respect to the Haar state
\varphi{G}
on
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
, we can consider the modular one-parametergroup

\{\sigma_{t}{G}:=\sigma_{t}^{\varphi{G}}:L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})\to L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}.

The elements of
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})
are analytic for
\{\sigma_{t}{G}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
and
\sigma_{z}{G}(\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G}))\subseteq\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})
for all
z\in\mathbb{C}
. This leads to the
Woronowicz characters

\delta^{z}{G}:=\varepsilon{G}\circ\sigma_{iz}{G}\in\mathscr{U}{G}.

(1.4)

We have the following formulas for the modular automorphism group and
scaling group
of
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})
:

\sigma_{z}{G}(x)=\delta^{-iz/2}{G}(x_{(1)})x_{(2)}\delta^{-iz/2}{G}(x_{(3)}),\qquad\tau_{z}{G}(x)=\delta^{-iz/2}{G}(x_{(1)})x_{(2)}\delta^{iz/2}{G}(x_{(3)})\qquad x\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G}),

(1.5)

where the (sumless) Sweedler notation was used. Also, with
R{G}:L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})\to L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
the unitary antipode, we have
R{G}(\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G}))=\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})
and

S{G}=\tau_{-i/2}{G}\circ R{G}=R{G}\circ\tau_{-i/2}{G},\qquad S{G}^{2}=\tau_{-i}{G}.

(1.6)

We will often view

\delta{G}\in B(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})
for
\pi\in\Irr(\mathbb{G})
using the identification (
1.3
). If we wish to emphasize the dependency on
\pi
, we will sometimes also write
\delta{G}^{\pi}
. We have
\operatorname{Tr}(\delta{G}^{1/2})=\operatorname{Tr}(\delta{G}^{-1/2})
and we call this common value the
quantum dimension of
\pi
and we denote it by
\dim_{q}(\pi)
.

Given
\pi\in\Rep(\mathbb{G})
, define
\bar{\pi}=(\overline{\mathcal{H}_{\pi}},\overline{U_{\pi}})\in\Rep(\mathbb{G})
by

\overline{U_{\pi}}:=(j_{\mathcal{H}_{\pi}}\odot R{G})(U_{\pi})\in B(\overline{\mathcal{H}_{\pi}})\odot\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G}),
where
j_{\mathcal{H}_{\pi}}:B(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})\to B(\overline{\mathcal{H}_{\pi}})
is given by
j_{\mathcal{H}_{\pi}}(x)\bar{\xi}=\overline{x^{*}\xi}
for
x\in B(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})
and
\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
. We have the following useful formulas, for every
\pi\in\Rep(\mathbb{G})
and
\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}_{\pi}

3.

3.1.
Ergodic compact quantum group actions

In this subsection, we recall some theory of ergodic compact quantum group actions, following
[
Boc95
,
BDRV06
,
DC17
]
.

Fix a compact quantum group
\mathbb{G}
and suppose that an ergodic action
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\curvearrowleft}}\mathbb{G}
is given. If
\pi\in\Rep(\mathbb{G})
, we define

\operatorname{Mor}(\pi,\mathbb{X})
to be the space of linear maps
T:\mathcal{H}_{\pi}\to L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X})
satisfying
(T\odot\id)\delta_{\pi}(\xi)=\alpha(T(\xi))
for all
\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
, and we define the
\pi
-spectral subspace

\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X})_{\pi}:=\operatorname{span}\{T\xi:T\in\operatorname{Mor}(\pi,\mathbb{X}),\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{\pi}\}.

We then put
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X}):=\bigoplus_{\pi\in\Irr(\mathbb{G})}\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X})_{\pi}
and we call
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X})
the
algebraic core
of the action. It is a
\sigma
-weakly dense unital
*
-subalgebra of
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X})
. Its norm-closure inside
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X})
will be denoted by
C(\mathbb{X})
. We then have
\alpha(\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X}))\subseteq\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X})\odot\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})
and
\alpha(C(\mathbb{X}))\subseteq C(\mathbb{X})\otimes C(\mathbb{G})
, so that
\alpha
defines a coaction of the Hopf
*
-algebra
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})
on
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X})
and so that
C(\mathbb{X})
becomes a
\mathbb{G}
-
C^{*}
-algebra.

Given
\pi\in\Rep(\mathbb{G})
, we define the space

\mathcal{G}_{\pi}:=\{\mu\in\mathcal{H}_{\pi}\odot\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X}):(\id\otimes\alpha)(\mu)=U_{\pi,13}^{*}\mu_{12}\}.

In other words, if
\{e_{j}^{\pi}\}_{j=1}^{d_{\pi}}
is an orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
, then

\sum_{j=1}^{d_{\pi}}e_{j}^{\pi}\otimes x_{j}\in\mathcal{G}_{\pi}\iff\forall j\in\{1,\dots,d_{\pi}\}:\alpha(x_{j})=\sum_{k=1}^{d_{\pi}}x_{k}\otimes U_{\pi}(e_{k}^{\pi},e_{j}^{\pi})^{*}.

(3.1)

It is clear that
\mathcal{G}_{\pi}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{\pi}\odot\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X})_{\pi}^{*}=\mathcal{H}_{\pi}\odot\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X})_{\bar{\pi}}
.
We emphasize that this space may very well be zero for certain choices of (irreducible) representations (see Section
4.2
for a concrete example).

The vector space
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}\odot\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X})
carries the
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X})
-valued inner product given on elementary tensors by

\langle\xi\otimes x,\eta\otimes y\rangle:=\langle\xi,\eta\rangle x^{*}y,\quad\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}_{\pi},\quad x,y\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X}).

If
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{G}_{\pi}
, then
\langle\mu,\nu\rangle\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X})^{\alpha}=\mathbb{C}1
, so that
\mathcal{G}_{\pi}
carries a canonical complex-valued inner product. If
\mu\in\mathcal{G}_{\pi}
and
\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{\pi}

Then

\displaystyle\alpha(X_{\pi}(\mu,\xi))=\sum_{j=1}^{d_{\pi}}X_{\pi}(\mu,e_{j}^{\pi})\otimes U_{\pi}(e_{j}^{\pi},\xi).

(3.2)

From this, it follows that

X_{\pi}(\mu,-)\in\operatorname{Mor}(\pi,\mathbb{X})
.
It is then easy to verify that the assignment

\mathcal{G}_{\pi}\ni\mu\mapsto X_{\pi}(\mu,-)\in\operatorname{Mor}(\pi,\mathbb{X})

T\mapsto\sum_{j=1}^{d_{\pi}}e_{j}^{\pi}\otimes T(e_{j}^{\pi})^{*}
. By

\dim(\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X})_{\pi})<\infty
for all
\pi\in\Rep(\mathbb{G})

\mathcal{G}_{\pi}
is a finite-dimensional Hilbert space for every
\pi\in\Rep(\mathbb{G})
. In the sequel, we will write

for
\pi\in\Rep(\mathbb{G})
and we will fix an orthonormal basis

for
\mathcal{G}_{\pi}
. Then

\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X})

. We discuss two examples of compact quantum hypergroups of a general nature.

4.1.
Coideals

Let
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X})\subseteq L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
be a (right) coideal von Neumann algebra, i.e.
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X})
is a von Neumann subalgebra of
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
such that
\Delta{G}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X}))\subseteq L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X})\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
.
In that case, we write
\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G}
, and we think of the object
\mathbb{H}
as being a ‘generalized closed quantum subgroup’ of
\mathbb{G}
. We then have the natural (ergodic) action
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Delta{G}}}{{\curvearrowleft}}\mathbb{G}
. Sometimes, we will also denote this action with
\alpha
.

Let us start by recalling some theory from the algebraic theory of coideals
[
Ch18
,
DCDT24
]
. Consider the two-sided coideal
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G})_{+}:=\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G})\cap\operatorname{Ker}(\epsilon{G})\subseteq\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})
, so that we can define the quotient coalgebra

\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H}):=\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})/\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G})_{+}.

We write
q:\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})\to\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})
for the associated quotient map and
1{H}=q(1)
.
We then have

\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G})=\{a\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G}):q(a_{(1)})\otimes a_{(2)}=1{H}\otimes a\}.

(4.1)

The space
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})
carries the involution
{\dagger}
defined by
q(a)^{\dagger}=q(S{G}(a)^{*})
for
a\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})
. The algebraic dual of
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})
will be denoted by
\mathscr{U}{H}
, which becomes a
*
-algebra for the product and involution given by

(\omega\star\omega^{\prime})(z)=(\omega\otimes\omega^{\prime})\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}(z),\quad\omega^{*}(z)=\overline{\omega(z^{\dagger})},\quad\omega,\omega^{\prime}\in\mathscr{U}_{\mathbb{H}},\quad z\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H}).

The quotient map
q:\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})\to\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})
dualizes to a
*
-algebra embedding

\mathscr{U}{H}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{U}{G}.
We will use this to view functionals on
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})
as functionals on
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})
, without explicit mention. We also recall the natural action

\mathscr{U}_{\mathbb{G}}\curvearrowright\mathcal{H}_{\pi},\quad\omega\xi=(\id\odot\omega)(U_{\pi})\xi,\quad\pi\in\Rep(\mathbb{G}),\quad\omega\in\mathscr{U}{G},\quad\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{\pi}.

Through the canonical embedding
\mathscr{U}{H}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{U}{G}
, we then obtain canonical actions
\mathscr{U}{H}\curvearrowright\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
as well, for every
\pi\in\Rep(\mathbb{G})
.

There is a unique functional
\varphi{H}:\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})\to\mathbb{C}
(not necessarily positive, cf. Proposition
4.3
) such that

\varphi{H}(1{H})=1,\quad(\varphi{H}\odot\id)\Delta{H}(c)=\varphi{H}(c)1=(\id\odot\varphi{H})\Delta{H}(c),\quad c\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H}).

(4.2)

It allows us to define the projection

E:\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})\to\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G}):a\mapsto(\varphi{H}\odot\id)\Delta{G}(a).

(4.3)

It is right
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G})
-linear, preserves
\varphi{G}
and acts on matrix coefficients via

E(U_{\pi}(\xi,\eta))=U_{\pi}(\varphi{H}\xi,\eta),\quad\pi\in\Rep(\mathbb{G}),\quad\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}_{\pi}.

(4.4)

Moreover, since
\epsilon{G}\circ E=\varphi{H}
, we see that
\varphi{H}=\epsilon{G}
on
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G})
.

In the coideal case, we can view the spaces
\mathcal{G}_{\pi}
as subspaces of
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
. Here is the concrete statement:

Given

(2)

\varphi{H}\xi=\xi
.

(3)

For all

\omega\xi=\omega(1{H})\xi

The map

\mathcal{G}_{\pi}\ni\mu\mapsto\xi_{\mu}=(\id\odot\epsilon{G})(\mu)\in\varphi{H}\mathcal{H}_{\pi}

is a well-defined unitary. We have

and
\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{\pi}

The equivalence

follows from (

) and (

follows from (

Proposition 4.2
.

The
*
-isomorphism (
2.2
)
restricts to a
*
-isomorphism

\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})\cong\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}})

Given
\pi\in\Rep(\mathbb{G})
and
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{G}_{\pi}

corresponds to the element

under this

\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})

\pi\in\Irr(\mathbb{G})

Proof.

Clearly the restriction

\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})\to\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}}):a\mapsto a_{(1)}\otimes\overline{R{G}(a_{(2)}^{*})}

is a well-defined injective

\bar{\epsilon}_{\mathbb{G}}:\overline{\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G})}\to\mathbb{C}:\bar{a}\mapsto\epsilon{G}(a^{*})
, and we fix

. Then we consider

. Using that
(\id\odot\bar{\epsilon}{G})\bar{\alpha}(\bar{a})=R{G}(a^{*})
for

, we then find

\alpha(a)=(\id\odot\id\odot\bar{\epsilon}{G})(\alpha\odot\id)(z)=(\id\odot\id\odot\bar{\epsilon}{G})(\id\odot\bar{\alpha})(z)\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G})\odot R{G}(\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G})).

Consequently,

\displaystyle(\id\odot\phi)\Delta{G}(a)

\displaystyle=(\id\odot\phi)(\id\odot\id\odot\bar{\epsilon}{G})(\Delta{G}\odot\id)(z)=(\id\odot\phi)(\id\odot\id\odot\bar{\epsilon}{G})(\id\odot\bar{\alpha})(z)=z,

and the surjectivity is proven. Finally, we compute for

and

\displaystyle(\id\odot\bar{\epsilon}{G})(Z_{\pi}(\mu,\nu))

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{d_{\pi}}X_{\pi}(\mu,e_{j}^{\pi})\bar{\epsilon}{G}(Y_{\pi}(e_{j}^{\pi},\nu))=\sum_{j=1}^{d_{\pi}}X_{\pi}(\mu,e_{j}^{\pi})\langle\delta{G}^{1/4}e_{j}^{\pi},\tilde{\delta}_{\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G}}^{-1/4}\xi_{\nu}\rangle=U_{\pi}(\xi_{\mu},\delta{G}^{1/4}\tilde{\delta}_{\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G}}^{-1/4}\xi_{\nu}),

which finishes the proof.
∎

\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G}^{\mathrm{op}})_{\tilde{\pi}}=\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})_{\pi}

Proposition 4.4

Given

\mu_{\pi}:=\dim_{q}(\pi)^{-1/2}\sum_{j,k=1}^{d_{\pi}}\overline{e_{j}^{\pi}}\otimes e_{k}^{\pi}\otimes U_{\pi}(\delta{G}^{-1/4}e_{j}^{\pi},e_{k}^{\pi})^{*}

We may assume without loss of generality that

\delta{G}

\delta{G}e_{j}^{\pi}=\delta_{j}e_{j}^{\pi}
for

\displaystyle(\id\odot\id\odot\alpha)(\mu)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,k,s,t,p,q=1}^{d_{\pi}}\lambda_{jkst}\overline{e_{j}^{\pi}}\otimes e_{k}^{\pi}\otimes u_{pq}^{*}\otimes u_{sp}^{*}\otimes u_{qt}^{*},

\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}})
by

\|z\|_{u}:=\sup_{\mathcal{H}\in\mathrm{Corr}{G}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X}),L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X}))}\|\theta_{\square}^{\mathcal{H}}(z)\|,\quad z\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}}).

(5.3)

More conceptually, consider the universal double-sided crossed product
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X})\rtimes_{u}\mathbb{G}\ltimes_{u}\mathcal{O}(\bar{\mathbb{X}})

[
AS21
,
DCDR25
]
, in which
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}})
embeds as a corner
[
DCDR25
]
*Proposition 2.8. The norm (
5.3
) is then simply the norm that
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}})
obtains through its embedding in
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X})\rtimes_{u}\mathbb{G}\ltimes_{u}\mathcal{O}(\bar{\mathbb{X}})
. To see this, one can use
[
DCDR25
]
*Proposition 2.14.

It is a consequence of (
5.2
) that
\|z\|_{r}\leq\|z\|_{u}
for
z\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}})
. We write
C^{u}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}})
for the
C^{*}
-completion of
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}})
with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{u}
. There is a unique surjective
*
-homomorphism

\kappa:C^{u}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}})\to C_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}})

that is the identity on
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}})
. It is also clear that
C_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}})^{*}

becomes a Banach algebra for the convolution product coming from the coassociative map
\Delta_{\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}}}:C_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}})\to C_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}})\otimes C_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}})
.
We now arrive at the main result of this section:

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a compact quantum group and let
(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X}),\alpha)
be an ergodic
\mathbb{G}
-
W^{*}
-algebra. The following are equivalent:

(1)

(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X}),\alpha)
is
\mathbb{G}
-injective.

(2)

(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X}),\alpha)
is strongly
\mathbb{G}
-injective.

(3)

|\epsilon_{\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}}}(z)|\leq\|z\|_{r}
for all
z\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}})
.

(4)

\kappa:C^{u}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}})\to C_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}})
is a
*
-isomorphism.

(5)

The Banach algebra
C_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}})^{*}
is unital.

Proof.

The equivalence
(2)\iff(3)
is an immediate consequence of (
5.2
) and
[
DCDR25
]
*Proposition 2.11. The implication
(4)\implies(3)
is trivial. To prove that
(2)\implies(4)
, we note that strong
\mathbb{G}
-injectivity of
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X})
implies that

\|\theta_{\square}^{\mathcal{H}}(z)\|\leq\|\theta_{\square}^{C_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X})}{G}}(z)\|=\|z\|_{r},\quad\mathcal{H}\in\mathrm{Corr}{G}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X}),L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X})),\quad z\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}}),

by combining Proposition
5.1
and
[
DCDR25
]
*Proposition 2.11. Consequently,
\|z\|_{u}\leq\|z\|_{r}
, and
\kappa
is isometric. We now prove
(1)\implies(3)
. The argument proceeds in the same spirit as
[
DCDR25
]
*Theorem 3.6, by reducing to the case where
\mathbb{G}
is second countable. Fix
z=\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}\otimes\overline{y_{j}}\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}})
.
Choose a Hopf
*
-subalgebra
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})\subseteq\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})
of countable dimension with the property that

\alpha(x_{j})\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X})\odot\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H}),\quad\bar{\alpha}(\overline{y_{j}})\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})\odot\mathcal{O}(\bar{\mathbb{X}}),\quad j=1,\dots,n.

As the notation suggests, it is not hard to see that this Hopf
*
-subalgebra is associated to a compact quantum group
\mathbb{H}
. Moreover, the modular data of
\mathbb{H}
is given by restriction of the modular data of
\mathbb{G}
. We may assume that
\Irr(\mathbb{H})\subseteq\Irr(\mathbb{G})
. We write
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H})
for the von Neumann subalgebra of
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
generated by
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})
and
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X}{H})
for the von Neumann subalgebra of
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X})
generated by
\sum_{\pi\in\Irr(\mathbb{H})}\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X})_{\pi}
. Then
\alpha(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X}{H}))\subseteq L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X}{H})\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H})\subseteq L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X}{H})\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
. In particular, we have ergodic actions
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X}{H})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha{H}}}{{\curvearrowleft}}\mathbb{H}
and
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X}{H})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\curvearrowleft}}\mathbb{G}
.

Considering the natural
\mathbb{G}
-equivariant normal conditional expectation
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X})\to L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X}{H})
(which kills the spectral subspaces associated to
\pi\in\Irr(\mathbb{G})\backslash\Irr(\mathbb{H})
), it follows that
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X}{H})
is
\mathbb{G}
-injective. Making use of
[
DR26
]
*Proposition 3.10, we see that
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X}{H})
is
\mathbb{H}
-injective as well, which means that the inclusion
\pi_{\mathbb{X}{H}}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X}{H}))\bar{\otimes}\mathbb{C}1\subseteq B(L^{2}(\mathbb{X}{H}))\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X}{H})
is
\mathbb{H}
-amenable. Since
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})
has countable dimension, it follows from (
3.9
) that
L^{2}(\mathbb{X}{H})
is separable, so that the von Neumann algebra
B(L^{2}(\mathbb{X}{H}))\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X}{H})
is
\sigma
-finite. Consequently, it follows from
[
DCDR25
]
*Theorem 3.10 that the inclusion
\pi_{\mathbb{X}{H}}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X}{H}))\bar{\otimes}\mathbb{C}1\subseteq B(L^{2}(\mathbb{X}{H}))\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X}{H})
is also strongly
\mathbb{H}
-amenable, i.e.
(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X}{H}),\alpha{H})
is strongly
\mathbb{H}
-injective. But since we already proved that
(2)\implies(3)
holds and since
z\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X}{H}\times_{\mathbb{H}}\overline{\mathbb{X}{H}})
, we see that

|\epsilon_{\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}}}(z)|=|\epsilon_{\mathbb{X}{H}\times_{\mathbb{H}}\overline{\mathbb{X}{H}}}(z)|\leq\|z\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X}{H})\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\bar{\mathbb{X}}{H})}=\|z\|_{r},

and the implication is proven. The equivalence
(3)\iff(5)
is trivial.
∎

Definition 5.3
.

We call
\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}}
coamenable if the equivalent conditions in Theorem
5.2
are satisfied.

The following result is surely well-known, but we are unaware of an explicit reference for it in the literature.

Corollary 5.4
.

The quantum dimension function

\mathcal{O}(\operatorname{Fus}(\mathbb{G}))\to\mathbb{C}:\chi(\pi)\mapsto\dim_{q}(\pi)

is bounded (for the norm coming from the inclusion
\mathcal{O}(\operatorname{Fus}(\mathbb{G}))\subseteq L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
) if and only if
\mathbb{G}
is Kac and coamenable.

Proof.

By Theorem
5.2
and the discussion in Subsection
4.2
, the quantum dimension function is bounded on
\mathcal{O}(\operatorname{Fus}(\mathbb{G}))
if and only if
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
is
\mathbb{G}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathbb{G}
-injective, which is equivalent with
\mathbb{G}
being Kac and coamenable by
[
DR25a
]
*Proposition 4.7.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

Coamenability of a (reduced) compact quantum group
\mathbb{G}
is also characterized through the existence of a character on the
C^{*}
-algebra
C(\mathbb{G})
. However, the existence of a character on the
C^{*}
-algebra
C_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}})
is not sufficient to guarantee the coamenability of
\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}}
. To see this, let
\mathbb{H}
be a non-Kac coamenable compact quantum group (e.g.
\mathbb{H}=SU_{q}(2)
),
\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{H}
,
\mathbb{G}=\mathbb{H}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathbb{H}
and consider the natural action
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X})\curvearrowleft\mathbb{G}
studied in Subsection
4.2
. Since
\mathbb{H}
is coamenable, the counit
\epsilon{H}
is bounded on
\mathcal{O}(\operatorname{Fus}(\mathbb{H}))\cong\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}})
, and hence induces a character on
C_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}})
. However, from Corollary
5.4
, the counit
\epsilon_{\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}}}
(corresponding to the quantum dimension function) is not bounded.

The following result was established in the papers
[
AK24
,
DR25a
]
. We can now give a new proof of the hard part:

Corollary 5.6
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a compact quantum group. The following are equivalent for a coideal von Neumann algebra
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G})
:

(1)

(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G}),\Delta{G})
is
\mathbb{G}
-injective.

(2)

\epsilon{G}:\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})\to\mathbb{C}
is bounded (for the reduced norm) and
\mathbb{H}
is a compact quasi-subgroup of
\mathbb{G}
.

(3)

\epsilon{G}:\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G})\to\mathbb{C}
is bounded (for the reduced norm) and
\mathbb{H}
is a compact quasi-subgroup of
\mathbb{G}
.

Proof.

If
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G})
is
\mathbb{G}
-injective,
[
AK24
]
*Proposition 5.8 implies that
\mathbb{H}
is a compact quasi-subgroup of
\mathbb{G}
. In that case, under the isomorphism (
2.2
), we have seen at the end of Subsection
4.1
that
\epsilon_{\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H}}
is simply the restriction of the counit
\epsilon{G}
. Thus, the equivalence
(1)\iff(2)
follows from Theorem
5.2
. The implication
(3)\implies(2)
is trivial. To prove the converse, assume that
(2)
holds and recall from the proof of Proposition
4.3
that the projection (
4.3
) extends uniquely to a normal conditional expectation
E:L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})\to L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G})
. Similarly, the
projection
F:\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})\to R{G}(\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G})):a\mapsto(\id\odot\varphi{H})\Delta{G}(a)
extends to a normal conditional expectation
F:L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})\to R{G}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G}))
. We have
E\circ F=F\circ E
, so
E\circ F
defines a normal conditional expectation
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})\to L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})
. If
x\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G})
, we then have

|\epsilon{G}(x)|=|\epsilon{G}(EF(x))|\leq\|EF(x)\|\leq\|x\|
, and
(3)
is proven.
∎

6.
Outlook

The work in this paper suggests the following lines of research:

•

As discussed in Subsection
3.5
, the
C^{*}
-algebraic theory of compact quantum hypergroups developed in
[
CV99
]
has some deficits. Is it possible to develop a satisfactory theory that resolves these? The example
\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}}
arising from an ergodic compact quantum group action
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X})\curvearrowleft\mathbb{G}
should then fit perfectly in such a theory.

•

As is apparent from Section
5
, the theory of equivariant correspondences as developed in
[
DCDR24
,
DCDR25
]
provides a conceptual bridge between structural properties of the compact quantum hypergroup
\mathbb{X}\times{G}\bar{\mathbb{X}}
and dynamical properties of the
\mathbb{G}
-
W^{*}
-algebra
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X})
. A detailed technical development of this connection, together with its implementation in concrete examples, will appear in forthcoming work.
