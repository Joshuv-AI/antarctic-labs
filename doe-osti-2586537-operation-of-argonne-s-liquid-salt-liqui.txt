 ABSTRACT
 Argonne National Laboratory has constructed a liquid salt-liquid metal separation
 testbed for use in the development and advancement of cathode processing of U/TRU
 co‑deposits generated by pyroprocessing of used nuclear fuel. The U/TRU product
 recovered from the electrorefiner contains adhered and entrained salt that must be
 removed prior to consolidation of the U/TRU alloy for use in advanced reactor fuel
 fabrication. The bottom pour operation utilizes the low melting points of U/TRU
 co‑deposits and higher densities of molten metals compared to molten salts to separate
 and consolidate the U/TRU product. Argonne’s testbed is designed to support the
 development and optimization of bottom-pouring configurations for batch and semi-
 continuous operations, integration of process monitoring and control technologies, and
 determination of operational requirements for implementing in an industrial setting.
 Scoping tests were performed to demonstrate operational aspects of the testbed,
 including operation using single-pour spout and dual-pour spout configurations,
 effectiveness of salt containment and extent of salt vaporization, and the use of sensor
 probes to detect the location of the interface between the metal and salt phases during
 pouring. Recommendations for process optimization testing for further development
 of bottom pour processing to separate U/TRU alloys from adhered salt were made
 based on the results of scoping tests. Completing the recommended activities will
 increase the technical readiness level (TRL) of the liquid salt-liquid metal separation
 operation and consolidation of U/TRU alloys to support industrialization of
 pyroprocessing.

 OPERATION OF ARGONNE’S LIQUID SALT-LIQUID
METAL SEPARATION TESTBED FOR U/TRU PRODUCT
 PROCESSING

1. INTRODUCTION
Pyroprocessing of used nuclear fuel (UNF) uses electrorefining to recover uranium metal and a
co‑deposited uranium/transuranic (U/TRU) alloy product that is suitable for fabrication into fuel for
advanced nuclear reactors. The U/TRU product contains adhered and entrained salt that must be removed
prior to consolidating the alloy into an ingot to use in fuel fabrication. Vaporization methods that are used
to remove the salt from the uranium metal products (e.g., Westphal et al., 2008) are not suitable for
removing salt from U/TRU alloys because americium present in the U/TRU product will volatilize at the
operating temperature of the vaporization method, which is approximately 1200 °C. Retaining minor
actinides such as americium in the U/TRU product is desirable because they can be burned as fuel in fast
reactors. Application of the bottom pour separation method to pyroprocessing products has been
characterized previously at the laboratory scale and feasibility has been demonstrated at the engineering
scale (Cruse 2011, Cruse 2012). However, technical gaps for effective operation remain to be addressed,
such as efficient salt drainage, process monitoring, and continuous or semi-continuous operation, which
require draining metal and salt independently. To that end, a liquid salt-liquid metal separation testbed that
uses a bottom pour configuration was designed and constructed at Argonne National Laboratory to assess
relevant process variables for U/TRU cathode processing and mature the utilized technologies and controls
to support industrial application of pyroprocessing (Schvaneveldt et al., 2024).
Separation by the bottom pour operation is based on the difference in densities of the molten metal and
molten salt. The immiscibility of molten salts and molten metals results in high separation efficiencies.
After the phases have separated with molten salt on top of molten metal, a plug at the bottom of the furnace
is opened, the molten metal is drained into a collection crucible, and then the plug is closed before any salt
is drained. The vertical draining mechanism used in the bottom pour design provides a simple operation to
recover the metal without contaminating salt that requires only a small equipment footprint in the facility.
A series of scoping tests using the newly installed liquid salt-liquid metal testbed were performed to
demonstrate the flexibility of the testbed to assess the use of crucibles with a single or multiple pour spouts.
Continuous processing of typical U/TRU products with 20-30 wt% entrained salt will result in rapid
accumulation of salt in the bottom pour apparatus that must be removed frequently for continuous
processing. Bottom pour configurations with a single pour spout require draining all of the metal before the
salt is recovered through the same drain. Bottom pour configurations with separate pour spouts for metal
and salt allow the salt to be recovered separately while leaving molten metal in the crucible. This would
enable efficient and semicontinuous operation in a pyroprocessing facility. The scoping tests were used to
evaluate the effectiveness of both single and dual-pour crucible operations, demonstrate feasibility of
measuring the metal level during pouring, evaluate salt retention and recovery in the crucibles, and identify
remaining development needs for optimization of U/TRU processing operations to enable industrialization
of pyroprocessing.

2. EXPERIMENTAL OPERATIONS
General operation steps for bottom pour separation are shown in Figure 1a and a schematic of generic
bottom pour equipment with a single drain spout is shown in Figure 1b. The bottom pour apparatus consists
of a crucible with a pour spout and plug in the bottom that is placed inside a furnace. Prior to bottom pour,
the U/TRU electrorefiner product with adhered salt is loaded through the top of the apparatus into the
crucible and melted. The molten metal and salt phases separate into two immiscible layers with the less
dense salt phase accumulating above the metal phase. The plug is then opened upward allowing the heavier
molten metal phase to drain through a spout at the bottom of the furnace. The plug is closed before the salt
phase reaches the drain, such that a small volume of molten metal remains in the crucible. The remaining
metal and the salt phase are then drained through the same spout and collected in a separate catch crucible.
If necessary, vaporized salt that recondensed at the top of the apparatus can be recovered separately during
equipment maintenance.

Figure 1. (a) General bottom pour operation steps and (b) schematic of general bottom pour apparatus
design. During bottom pour operation, metal alloy with adhered salt is loaded into the crucible, melted, and
allowed to separate. Recovery is performed in three steps: (1) metal drains, (2) liquid salt drains separately,
and (3) salt that vaporized and condensed on the crucible lid is recovered.

2.1 Single-Pour and Dual-Pour Crucible and Plug Design
The testbed was used to assess the operation of single- and dual-pouring crucible configurations (Figure 2).
Single-pour crucibles drain metal and salt through the same pour spout, meaning that some metal will be
present in the initial portion of recovered salt. The dual-pour crucible is designed to drain metal and salt
through separate spouts. It consists of a short inner crucible to contain all of the molten metal with some
overlying salt and a tall outer crucible to contain only salt. In this configuration, salt that accumulates in the
inner crucible during processing will overflow into the outer crucible while the metal remains in the inner
crucible. Only metal is collected through the drain of the inner crucible and only salt is collected through
the drain of the outer crucible. In this way, U/TRU product can be continually processed by periodically

removing salt without needing to fully drain metal from the inner crucible. Additional salt drains could be
added if necessary to facilitate salt removal at industrial scales. A small volume of metal is always present
in the inner crucible to prevent salt from being drained from the inner crucible. Salt remaining in the inner
crucible after metal is drained is displaced by molten metal from the next batch of product added to the
apparatus, thereby enabling continuous operation.

Figure 2. Single pour (left) and dual-pour (right) crucibles used in bottom pour scoping tests.

The crucibles and plugs used in the testbed apparatus are constructed from alumina. Components contacted
by both molten metal and molten salt were coated with yttria-stabilized zirconia (YSZ). Two coats of the
YSZ coating were applied by painting the ceramic parts and allowing the parts to air-dry overnight between
coats. After painting, the ceramics were fired in the bottom pour furnace at 850 °C and held overnight to
cure the coating and remove any moisture.
In both crucible configurations, the plugs and drain holes were chamfered to ensure that a good seal was
made between the plug and crucible drain. A plug-lift adapter enabled the separate operation of plugs in the
dual-pour crucible by coupling the lifting mechanism to the desired drain plug (Figure 3). The plug-lift
adapter was designed to easily attach and detach from the plugs so that the operator can switch between
plugs as desired. The plugs were operated independently and vertical movement was controlled by using
pneumatic valves (Figure 4). The valves are designed so that any failure would automatically lower the
plug to seal the crucible drain holes to mitigate spillage. A manual release valve is also incorporated into
the lifting mechanism to enable manual pouring and sealing. Custom LabVIEW™ software (National
Instruments) is used to control the rate at which each plug is opened and the height to which the plug is
raised to drain molten metal or molten salt.

2.2 Bottom Pour Test and Sensor Operations
The bottom pour furnace consists of two semi-cylinder clamshell heaters capable of operating at
temperatures up to 900 °C. The heating zones are monitored with K-type thermocouples connected to the
temperature controller. An overtemperature thermocouple is used to monitor the temperature in the heating
zone and disconnect the furnace power if the internal temperature exceeds the overtemperature set point.
The crucibles rest on a torus-shaped insulating stand that provides sufficient room for draining operations
and positions the crucible and drain hole within the heated zone during operation (Figure 5). Pouring from
within the heated zone reduces the risk of molten metal or molten salt freezing within the drain hole, which

could create an improper seal between the drain hole and plug or cause freezing between a plug and drain
hole. The opening at the bottom of the furnace is sufficiently large to ensure that the streams of poured
metal and salt do not contact the heaters.

Figure 5. Photos of installed bottom pour testbed apparatus showing (a) crucible cover and plug rod in
furnace, and (b) a crucible in the furnace. One side of the clamshell furnace and insulation have been
removed to show the crucible.

The testbed incorporates electrical resistance tomography (ERT) sensors to monitor the levels of salt and
metal in the crucible(s). ERT measures the electrical resistance between two electrodes positioned at
different depths within the crucible that is used to differentiate the presence of metallic and salt phases. The
probes consist of tungsten wires sheathed in ceramic tubes coated with YSZ. The ERT sensors are operated
using custom LabVIEW software to monitor changes in resistance between electrodes during pouring
operations.
Scoping tests were performed using antimony as the metal phase and LiCl-KCl eutectic as the salt phase.
Antimony has a melting temperature similar to those of anticipated U/TRU alloys, making it a suitable
surrogate for development of physical separations based on molten metal properties. These include
assessments of pouring configurations, pour times, the capacity to operate continuously, and other process
operations. The bottom pour tests were assembled by placing the crucibles in the furnace, positioning the
plug in the drain hole, and loading the desired quantities of antimony and salt into the crucible. In the dual-
pour configuration, the inner crucible was placed inside the outer crucible with the drain holes aligned. For
each drain, the plunger was lightly pressed against the crucible by using the lifting/lowering mechanism to
ensure that a good seal was made between plug and crucible. The metal and salt were added into the
crucibles using a funnel. After assembly, the furnace temperature was raised to 300 °C and held overnight
to remove moisture from the salt. After drying, the furnace temperature was increased to the selected
operating temperature (up to 730°C).

3. SCOPING TEST RESULTS
A series of scoping tests using the liquid salt-liquid metal testbed were performed using single and dual-
pour configurations to demonstrate the versatility of the testbed and identify objectives for future
optimization tests. The scoping test conditions are listed in Table 1. Tests were conducted using 0.4-0.7 kg
of antimony and 0.1-1.1 kg LiCl-KCl eutectic salt. The single-pour configuration used large plungers and
drains (2 in. OD) and the dual-pour configuration used narrow plungers and drains (1 in. OD). The tests
provide insights regarding the time needed for pouring, the amount of material poured, and the ability to
tightly reseal the crucible. The dual-pour apparatus was evaluated for leakage of metal from the inner
crucible to the outer crucible (if any) and the ability to separately pour metal from the inner crucible and
salt from the outer crucible.

3.1 Metal Pour from Single and Dual-Pour Crucibles
Metal pouring was performed using a single-pour crucible (Test 1) and the dual-pour crucibles (Tests 2 and
3). The single drain crucible accommodated more metal and salt than the dual-pour inner crucible because
of the relative sizes of the crucibles. However, the difference in pour sizes did not hinder evaluation of the
two configurations. All three tests were performed with both metal and salt loaded into the pouring crucible.
Test 2 was performed with only the inner crucible filled, and Test 3 was performed with salt in both the
inner and outer crucible.

Metal was successfully poured from both crucible configurations. In the single pour crucible, metal drained
within two seconds after the plug was raised. In the dual-pour crucible, metal drained within eight seconds
after the plug was raised. The longer drain time in the dual-pour configuration can be attributed to the
smaller diameter of the drain hole compared to the drain in the single-pour crucible. Early feasibility tests
for bottom pour showed that pour configurations with very small drains required high operating
temperatures (>700 °C) to pour metal, likely due to the viscosity and surface tension of the metal phase
(Cruse 2012). The operating temperature and drain diameter must be co-optimized to ensure repeated
effective and controlled pouring to produce uniform ingots.

Operation of the dual-pour configuration was tested with and without salt present in the outer crucible
(Test 3 and Test 2, respectively) to determine the amount of metal leakage from the inner crucible into outer
crucible (e.g., from where the inner crucible is joined to the outer crucible) and to demonstrate independent
control of the inner plunger in the dual-drain apparatus. Retention of metal in the inner crucible in dual-
pour configurations is imperative to maximize the U/TRU product produced and minimize metal in the salt
waste stream. No antimony was detected in the outer crucible after Test 3 was performed. In Test 2, a small
bead of antimony was detected in the outer crucible after the test. This is most likely a small amount of
antimony that had spilled while the inner crucible was being loaded during set up (Figure 6). Test 2 was
performed without adding any salt in the outer crucible and the antimony bead was not located near the
inner crucible position. Additionally, some antimony was retained in the inner crucible during Test 2 after
pouring was completed (Figure 7a), but no antimony was observed on the outside surface of the inner
crucible (Figure 7b), indicating that the inner crucible plug effectively sealed the drain and prevented the
metal phase leaking from the inner crucible into the outer crucible.

3.2 Probe Placement Data and Detection
Both the single and dual-drain configurations incorporated ERT sensors for monitoring the metal and salt
levels during the scoping tests. Online monitoring of the relative metal and salt levels can be used to directly
control plug opening and closing to ensure that only the desired phase is poured and that the plug is closed
to retain all salt in the crucible. ERT sensors operate by detecting the difference in resistance between
electrodes placed at different levels within the crucibles to differentiate between metal and salt layers during
operation.
The ERT probes used in Tests 2 and 3 detected a transition from the metal phase to atmosphere during
pouring. In both tests, salt volatilization from the inner crucible resulted in only a very thin salt layer
remaining above the molten metal; that salt layer was not detected by the sensors. Test 2 used three probes
inserted at the same depth to demonstrate the sensitivity of the probes (probes at the same depth should
detect the phase transition at the same time). Measurements on all three probes detected the phase transition
simultaneously (Figure 8). Test 3 was conducted with three probes at staggered heights all within the metal
phase to detect changes in the molten metal level as draining proceeded. The probe measurements
successfully monitored movement of the metal interface as draining was performed (Figure 9). These initial
measurements provide confidence that full integration of the ERT sensor into bottom pour equipment will
enable control of U/TRU metal and salt pour rates and ensure salt is retained in the crucible and does not
contaminate the product ingot during metal pouring.

During Test 1, which was performed in the single drain crucible, a small antimony leak was detected by the
ERT probes due to an imperfect seal between the plug and drain hole (Figure 10). Three probes were
inserted in the crucible at approximately the same depth. During detection of the leak, the resistance
measurements quickly fluctuated between <50 mOhm, indicating the probe was in the metal phase, and
1050 mOhm, indicating the probe was in atmosphere (Figure 10). After approximately 35 seconds, the
resistance measurements stabilized at 1050 mOhm. The fluctuation in the data over 35 seconds indicates
that the level of the metal phase remained very near the probe tips during that time and the measurements
stabilized after the metal phase dropped below the probe tips, indicating that a very slow leak of metal
through the drain was occurring. Early detection of such leaking can inform the operator to help mitigate
loss of product.

3.3 Salt Collection and Vaporization
Tests 4 and 5 were used to evaluate salt pouring from the outer crucible of the dual-pour configuration
while retaining metal in the inner crucible. Test 4 was performed at 740 °C and Test 5 was performed at
490 °C. Liquid antimony was successfully retained in the inner crucible from the outer crucible while
manipulating the outer crucible drain and pouring salt in Test 4. In Test 5, a small leak in the outer crucible
was detected during heating to the operating temperature, so the salt pour was performed at the lower
temperature. In both tests, significant quantities of salt had vaporized as the crucible was heated from the
drying temperature to the operating temperature. After the tests, the mass of poured salt was compared to
the original salt mass loaded in the outer crucible. In Test 4 performed at 740 °C, approximately 80% of
the salt vaporized, and in Test 5 performed at 490 °C, approximately 15% of the salt vaporized. The higher
extent of vaporization in Test 4 correlates with the higher operating temperature and longer test duration.
Additionally, visible salt deposits were observed on the assembly above the crucible after both Test 4 and
Test 5 had been completed. While salt vaporization does not affect separation of the metal product from the
salt, it can lead to shorter equipment lifetimes and may result in increased maintenance costs. Crucible
designs that better contain salt vapor by providing a condensation point for vaporized salt are recommended
for industrial operations.

3.4 Crucible and Plunger Durability
Insight regarding the durability of the crucible and plungers was gained during the scoping tests. The ability
of the plunger to reseat and successfully close the drain plug during pouring is required for successful
bottom pour operation. The single-pour crucible plug protrudes past the bottom of the crucible into a cooler
region of the furnace. In previous tests, this caused salt and metal to freeze on the tip during pouring and
sometimes prevented proper re-sealing. In contrast, the dual-pour crucible plungers protruded only slightly
past the bottom of the crucible, keeping the plunger fully in the heated zone and minimizing freezing of
metal and salt on the plunger tip.
All ceramic parts contacted by liquid metal and salt were coated in a YSZ ceramic layer prior to use to
minimize chemical interactions of metal and salt with the alumina crucible. The heat cycling used to
evaluate different pouring configurations placed stress on the YSZ coating. The YSZ coating on the
plungers was inspected after each test and no cracking was observed, indicating that the coating will be
maintained over multiple operation cycles and that semi-continuous operations can be optimized using these
coated crucibles.

3.5 Recommendations for Bottom Pour Optimization
The scoping tests performed using Argonne’s liquid-salt/liquid metal separation testbed provided several
insights into optimization needs for bottom pour processing of U/TRU alloys that contain entrained and
adhered salts. Successful demonstration of the recommended optimization activities would generate
additional operational data needed to successfully operate a semi-continuous U/TRU cathode processor in
an industrial facility. Recommendations for additional modifications and optimization include:
  Optimize dual-pour crucible and plug designs and operation for maximum metal throughput and
 recovery.
  Implement crucible designs with salt capture baffling to control vaporization losses.
  Develop and demonstrate automated pouring using feedback from online ERT sensors.
  Demonstrate continuous or semi-continuous operation.
  Identify recommended equipment maintenance schedules for continuous, semi-continuous, and
 batch operations.

4. SUMMARY
Construction of Argonne’s liquid salt-liquid metal separation testbed apparatus was completed and scoping
tests were performed to demonstrate operation and recommendations for continued development were
made. The testbed was designed to advance cathode processing of U/TRU co-deposits generated by
electrorefining of used nuclear fuel. The testbed meets previously determined U/TRU process development
design requirements, including attaining operating temperatures up to 900 °C, accommodating flexibility
in operation, and evaluating use of sensors for salt and metal level measurements.
The initial bottom pour tests were used to evaluate the feasibility of pouring metal from both single-pour
and dual-pour crucibles. The dual-pour crucible is designed to pour metal and salt independently through
separate drains to enable semi-continuous operation. Tests using the dual-pour crucible successfully drained
metal from the inner crucible seated in a larger crucible within similar timescales to a single drain
configuration. Additionally, the metal was successfully retained in the inner crucible when not pouring and
did not leak into the outer salt crucible, demonstrating that independent metal and salt phase draining is
achievable. Effective implementation of electrical resistance tomography (ERT) sensors to monitor the
metal phase level during pouring was demonstrated during the scoping tests. Recommendations for bottom
pour optimization include methods for effective capture and retention of vaporized salt, implementation of
ERT-controlled pouring, and optimization of crucible design and process parameters for semi-continuous
or continuous equipment operation.
The established liquid salt-liquid metal separation testbed is flexible and modular to enable testing of
alternative sensors, crucible designs, and operational protocols and close technical gaps regarding the
processing and consolidation of U/TRU alloys. Development of hardware and operational protocols that
enable continuous or semi-continuous operation will continue to demonstrate the effectiveness of the
approach and provide key parameter values for scale-up of salt/metal separation operations and support
industrialization of pyroprocessing through metal separation and the consolidation of U/TRU alloys for use
as advanced reactor fuel.
