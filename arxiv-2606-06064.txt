A Two-Graph Refinement of Paulsen’s Lollipop Bounds
Siddhartha Mahajan, Paras Chopra

Abstract

Paulsen introduced a second obstruction for this problem, based on pairs of circles meeting at obtuse angle, in addition to the stem-direction obstruction of Cutler–Karlsson–Sloane. We recast Paulsen’s argument as a weighted problem for two graphs: a K 4 K_{4} -free graph D D of non-close stem pairs and a K 5 K_{5} -free graph E E of non-intriguing circle pairs. We keep the overlap term and analyze near-extremal configurations of D D and E E . This closes all of Paulsen’s remaining gaps up to n = 17 n=17 , and also closes n = 19 n=19 : a ℒ ​ ( 0 ) , a ℒ ​ ( 1 ) , … , a ℒ ​ ( 17 ) = 1 , 2 , 10 , 25 , 45 , 71 , 104 , 142 , 186 , 237 , 294 , 356 , 425 , 500 , 580 , 667 , 761 , 859 , \begin{gathered}a_{\mathcal{L}}(0),a_{\mathcal{L}}(1),\ldots,a_{\mathcal{L}}(17)\\ =1,2,10,25,45,71,104,142,186,237,294,356,425,500,580,667,761,859,\end{gathered} and a ℒ ​ ( 19 ) = 1076 . a_{\mathcal{L}}(19)=1076. 1
Introduction and statement

A
lollipop
is a circle together with a half-line attached at one point
of the circle and extending radially outward. Equivalently, it is determined
by a center
C\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, a radius
r>0
, and a unit vector
u
, and
is the union of

\{P:|P-C|=r\}\quad\text{and}\quad\{C+ru+tu:t\geq 0\}.

Let
a_{\mathcal{L}}(n)
be the largest possible number of connected regions into
which
n
lollipops divide the plane.

We work in generic arrangements: there are no tangencies, no triple crossing
points, and no crossing occurs at an anchor. This entails no loss for an
extremal problem, since a non-generic maximizer can be perturbed without
decreasing the number of regions. If
C
is the total number of crossings
between distinct lollipops, then

\#\text{regions}=C+n+1.

(1)

Thus the region problem is equivalent to maximizing
C
.

The values
a_{\mathcal{L}}(0),\ldots,a_{\mathcal{L}}(4)=1,2,10,25,45
are due to
Cutler, Karlsson, and Sloane
[
1
]
. Paulsen’s recent note proves the
exact values for
n=5,6,7,10
and gives the best previously published upper
bounds for the remaining small cases
[
2
]
. The OEIS entry A389624
has been updated accordingly
[
3
]
.

Our contribution is purely combinatorial once Paulsen’s two geometric inputs
are accepted. We do not change the known construction; instead we sharpen the
upper bound by analyzing how the two extremal obstruction graphs can overlap.

Theorem 1
.

The following values are exact:

\displaystyle a_{\mathcal{L}}(0),a_{\mathcal{L}}(1),\ldots,a_{\mathcal{L}}(17)

\displaystyle=1,2,10,25,45,71,104,142,186,237,294,356,425,500,580,667,761,859,

and

a_{\mathcal{L}}(19)=1076.

Moreover,

964\leq a_{\mathcal{L}}(18)\leq 965,\qquad 1193\leq a_{\mathcal{L}}(20)\leq 1194.

The matching lower bounds in Theorem
1
come from the standard
blow-up of Karlsson’s optimal four-lollipop configuration. We recall the
construction in Appendix
B
. The rest of the paper proves
the upper bounds.

2
Paulsen’s two obstructions

We use Paulsen’s terminology
[
2
]
.

Definition 2
.

A pair of lollipops is
close
if the angle between its two stem
directions is at most
90^{\circ}
. A pair is
intriguing
if its two
circles either do not intersect, or intersect at angle at most
90^{\circ}
,
where the intersection angle is measured after orienting both circles in the
same direction.

The following are the geometric inputs. The first close-pair estimate is the
stem-angle lemma of Cutler–Karlsson–Sloane, and the intriguing-pair estimates
and forced intriguing-pair theorem are due to Paulsen.

Lemma 3

(Cutler–Karlsson–Sloane and Paulsen)
.

For a generic pair of lollipops:

(i)

a close pair has at most
5
crossings;

(ii)

an intriguing pair has at most
5
crossings;

(iii)

a pair that is both close and intriguing has at most
4
crossings.

Furthermore, among any four lollipops there is a close pair, and among any
five lollipops there is an intriguing pair.

For reference, Paulsen’s linear-algebra proof of the last assertion is recalled
in Appendix
A
, with a minor sign-cleanup to handle zero
coefficients.

Given an arrangement of
n
lollipops, define two graphs
D
and
E
on

[n]
:

ij\in D\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad L_{i},L_{j}\text{ are not close},

ij\in E\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad L_{i},L_{j}\text{ are not intriguing}.

Lemma
3
implies

D\text{ is }K_{4}\text{-free},\qquad E\text{ is }K_{5}\text{-free}.

Let
N=\binom{n}{2}
, and let
c_{ij}
be the number of crossings between

L_{i}
and
L_{j}
. The four pair estimates in Lemma

3
are summarized by

c_{ij}\leq 4+\mathbf{1}_{ij\in D}+\mathbf{1}_{ij\in E}+\mathbf{1}_{ij\in D\cap E}.

(2)

Summing over all pairs gives

C\leq 4N+\sigma(D,E),\qquad\sigma(D,E):=|D|+|E|+|D\cap E|.

(3)

Let
t_{r}(n)
denote the number of edges of the balanced complete

r
-partite Turan graph
T_{r}(n)
. Since
D
is
K_{4}
-free and
E

is
K_{5}
-free,

|D|\leq t_{3}(n),\qquad|E|\leq t_{4}(n).

Paulsen additionally uses
|D\cap E|\leq|D|
and obtains

C\leq U_{P}(n):=4\binom{n}{2}+2t_{3}(n)+t_{4}(n).

(4)

Equivalently,

C\leq 7\binom{n}{2}-2\left\lceil\frac{n(n-3)}{6}\right\rceil-\left\lceil\frac{n(n-4)}{8}\right\rceil.

We improve (
4
) by showing that equality, and in
many cases near equality, is incompatible with the simultaneous structures of

D
and
E
.

3
One-defect cases

The easiest improvements use only the equality case of Turan’s theorem.

Proposition 4
.

For
n\in\{8,9,11,13\}
,

C\leq U_{P}(n)-1.

Consequently,

a_{\mathcal{L}}(8)=186,\quad a_{\mathcal{L}}(9)=237,\quad a_{\mathcal{L}}(11)=356,\quad a_{\mathcal{L}}(13)=500.

Proof.

Equality in (
4
) would force

|D|=t_{3}(n),\qquad|E|=t_{4}(n),\qquad D\subseteq E.

Thus
D=T_{3}(n)
and
E=T_{4}(n)
. Since
D\subseteq E
, every part of the
complete quadripartite graph
E
must be contained in one part of the
complete tripartite graph
D
. Equivalently, the part sizes of
T_{4}(n)

must be groupable into the part sizes of
T_{3}(n)
.

For the four values in question this grouping is impossible:

\begin{array}[]{@{}c c c@{}}\hline\cr\hline\cr n&T_{3}(n)\text{ part sizes}&T_{4}(n)\text{ part sizes}\\
\hline\cr 8&3,3,2&2,2,2,2\\
9&3,3,3&3,2,2,2\\
11&4,4,3&3,3,3,2\\
13&5,4,4&4,3,3,3\\
\hline\cr\hline\cr\end{array}

Therefore
\sigma(D,E)\leq 2t_{3}(n)+t_{4}(n)-1
, so

C\leq U_{P}(n)-1
. The construction in Appendix
B

attains the resulting upper bound.
∎

4
Internal edges over an extremal tripartition

The remaining improvements need a small extremal lemma. Assume first that

D=T_{3}(n)
, with parts
A,B,C
of sizes

p\geq q\geq r.

Write

m=|D\setminus E|,\qquad x=|E\setminus D|.

Thus
m
counts missing cross-edges of
E
relative to the tripartition,
while
x
counts internal
E
-edges inside
A
,
B
, and
C
.

Lemma 5
.

Assume
p\geq q\geq r\geq 4
,
D=T_{3}(n)
, and
E
is
K_{5}
-free. With

m
and
x
as above:

(a)

if
m=0
, then

x\leq\left\lfloor p^{2}/4\right\rfloor;

(b)

if
m=1
, then

x\leq\max\left\{\left\lfloor p^{2}/4\right\rfloor,\,p+q-2\right\};

(c)

if
m=2
, then

x\leq\max\left\{\left\lfloor p^{2}/4\right\rfloor,\,p+q-2,\,2p+q-5\right\}.

Proof.

Let
H_{A},H_{B},H_{C}
be the internal graphs induced by
E
on the three parts.
We call a part
active
if its internal graph has at least one edge.

First, each
H_{A},H_{B},H_{C}
is triangle-free. Indeed, if, say,
H_{A}

contained a triangle, then since
m\leq 2
and
|B|,|C|\geq 4
, we could choose
vertices
b\in B
and
c\in C
incident to no missing cross-edge. The
triangle together with
b
and
c
would span a
K_{5}
in
E
, a
contradiction.

Second, suppose
X
and
Y
are two active parts, with third part
Z
.
For every internal edge
e\in H_{X}
and every internal edge
f\in H_{Y}
, some
missing cross-edge must have one endpoint in
e
and one endpoint in
f
.
Otherwise, choosing a vertex of
Z
incident to no missing cross-edge would
make
e\cup f\cup\{z\}
a
K_{5}
in
E
.

If
m=0
, the second observation implies that at most one part is active.
Mantel’s theorem inside the largest active part gives
x\leq\left\lfloor p^{2}/4\right\rfloor
.

Let
m=1
. If only one part is active, the same Mantel bound applies. If
two parts
X,Y
are active, the unique missing cross-edge must join
X
to

Y
, say
xy
. The second observation implies that every edge of
H_{X}

contains
x
, and every edge of
H_{Y}
contains
y
. Hence the two
internal graphs are stars, contributing at most
(|X|-1)+(|Y|-1)\leq p+q-2

edges.

Let
m=2
. One active part gives the Mantel bound. Three active parts are
impossible, since every pair of active parts would require a missing cross-edge
between them. Thus suppose exactly two parts
X,Y
are active, with

|X|=s\geq t=|Y|
. If only one of the two missing cross-edges joins
X
to

Y
, the preceding star argument gives at most
s+t-2\leq p+q-2
internal
edges.

It remains to handle the case where both missing cross-edges join
X
to

Y
. Write them as
a_{1}b_{1}
and
a_{2}b_{2}
, where
a_{i}\in X
and

b_{i}\in Y
, allowing the two missing edges to share an endpoint. The second
observation says that for every
e\in H_{X}
and
f\in H_{Y}
, there is an

i\in\{1,2\}
with
a_{i}\in e
and
b_{i}\in f
.

If the two missing edges share an endpoint in
X
, then every edge of

H_{X}
contains that endpoint, while every edge of
H_{Y}
meets

\{b_{1},b_{2}\}
. Since
H_{Y}
is triangle-free, the latter contributes at
most
2t-4
edges, and
H_{X}
contributes at most
s-1
. The total is at
most
s+2t-5\leq 2s+t-5
. The case of a shared endpoint in
Y
is even
smaller, by symmetry and
s\geq t
.

Finally assume the two missing edges are disjoint. Every edge of
H_{X}
meets

\{a_{1},a_{2}\}
, and every edge of
H_{Y}
meets
\{b_{1},b_{2}\}
. Classify an
edge by which distinguished endpoints it contains. If
H_{X}
contains both
an edge using only
a_{1}
and an edge using only
a_{2}
, then every edge of

H_{Y}
must contain both
b_{1}
and
b_{2}
, so
|E(H_{Y})|\leq 1
and the
triangle-free bound
|E(H_{X})|\leq 2s-4
gives total at most
2s-3\leq 2s+t-5
.
Otherwise all edges of
H_{X}
contain a common distinguished endpoint, so

|E(H_{X})|\leq s-1
; the same classification gives
|E(H_{Y})|\leq 2t-4
, and
the total is at most
s+2t-5\leq 2s+t-5
. This proves the
m=2
bound.
∎

We also need one standard covering fact.

Lemma 6
.

Let
K_{a,b,c,d}
be a complete quadripartite graph with

a\leq b\leq c\leq d
. To destroy all transversal
K_{4}
’s, one must delete
at least
ab
edges. This is sharp.

Proof.

Deleting all edges between the two smallest parts destroys every transversal

K_{4}
, so
ab
deletions suffice.

For the lower bound, assign weight
1/(cd)
to every transversal
K_{4}
.
The total weight is

abcd\cdot\frac{1}{cd}=ab.

An edge between parts of sizes
s
and
t
lies in the product of the other
two part sizes many transversal
K_{4}
’s. This product is at most
cd
, so
each deleted edge covers weight at most
1
. At least
ab
deleted edges
are necessary.
∎

Proposition 7
.

For

Appendix A
Paulsen’s forced intriguing pair

We recall Paulsen’s proof that among five circles, some pair is intriguing.
Suppose, toward a contradiction, that five circles with centers

x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and radii
r_{i}>0
are pairwise non-intriguing. Thus
every pair intersects at an obtuse angle, equivalently

r_{i}^{2}+r_{j}^{2}<|x_{i}-x_{j}|^{2}<(r_{i}+r_{j})^{2}\qquad(i\neq j).

(6)

For each circle set

v_{i}=\frac{1}{r_{i}}\bigl(1,\,r_{i}^{2}-|x_{i}|^{2},\,x_{i}\bigr)\in\mathbb{R}^{4},

and write vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
as triples
(\alpha,\beta,x)
, with

x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Endow
\mathbb{R}^{4}
with the symmetric bilinear form

\langle(\alpha,\beta,x),(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime},x^{\prime})\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\alpha\beta^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}\beta\alpha^{\prime}+x\cdot x^{\prime}.

A direct computation gives

\langle v_{i},v_{i}\rangle=1,\qquad-1<\langle v_{i},v_{j}\rangle<0\quad(i\neq j).

Since five vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
are linearly dependent, choose a nonzero
linear relation and move the negative terms to the other side:

\sum_{i\in P}a_{i}v_{i}=\sum_{j\in N}b_{j}v_{j},\qquad a_{i},b_{j}>0,

where
P
and
N
are nonempty disjoint sets. Replacing the relation by
its negative if necessary, assume
|P|\leq 2
. Let

w=\sum_{i\in P}v_{i}
. Then

\left\langle\sum_{i\in P}a_{i}v_{i},w\right\rangle>0,

because
|P|=1
is immediate and, if
P=\{1,2\}
, the expression is

(a_{1}+a_{2})(1+\langle v_{1},v_{2}\rangle)>0
. On the other hand,

\left\langle\sum_{j\in N}b_{j}v_{j},w\right\rangle<0,

since every cross inner product
\langle v_{j},v_{i}\rangle
, with

j\in N
and
i\in P
, is negative. This contradicts the linear relation.
Therefore five pairwise non-intriguing circles cannot exist.
