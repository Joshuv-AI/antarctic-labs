Strichartz estimates for the Liouville equation on Euclidean tori and applications to Kakeya
Pierre Germain, Mickaël Latocca

Abstract
. We prove Strichartz estimates for the space-time density ρ \rho of solutions to the free Liouville equation on flat tori. In dimension one, we obtain the optimal range of estimates for the density ρ ∈ L t , x p \rho\in L^{p}_{t,x} in terms of f 0 ∈ L v a ​ L x b f_{0}\in L^{a}_{v}L^{b}_{x} . In higher dimensions, we prove that such estimates cannot hold and that a weight has to be added: ρ \rho can be bounded in terms of the norm of | v | γ ​ f 0 |v|^{\gamma}f_{0} . We conjecture a range of optimal estimates, and partially prove them. Finally, these results have natural applications to the X X -ray transform and Kakeya problems on Euclidean cylinders.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
The Liouville equation on the torus and its asymptotic behavior

This article is dedicated to estimates for the free Liouville equation (or kinetic transport equation)

(1)

\partial_{t}f(t,x,v)+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f(t,x,v)=0,\qquad f(0,x,v)=f_{0}(x,v)\geq 0,

set on the torus:
(x,v)\in\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
with

\mathbb{T}^{d}=\mathbb{R}^{d}/(2\pi\mathbb{Z}^{d}).

The solution is given by the explicit formula

f(t,x,v)=f_{0}(x-tv,v),

with corresponding density

\rho(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{0}(x-tv,v)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v.

Space-independent solutions
f(t,x,v)=g(v)
are stationary, and through mixing they are expected to play the role of attractors in appropriate topologies. At the level of the density, this means that

\rho(t,x)\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}f_{0}(x,v)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v.

In this paper, we aim at quantifying this phenomenon. Upon subtracting from
f_{0}
its space average, we consider from now on densities
f_{0}
that have zero average in
x
(and in particular we will jettison the non-negativity hypothesis), and we ask the following question.

Question 1
.

For which indices
p,a,b
does there hold

(2)

\|\rho(t,x)\|_{{L^{p}_{t,x}}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}^{d})}\lesssim\|f_{0}(x,v)\|_{L^{a}_{v}(\mathbb{R}^{d},L^{b}_{x}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}\qquad\mbox{if}\;\;\;\int f_{0}(x,v)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=0\quad\mbox{for all $v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$}

or more generally for which
p,\gamma,a,b

(3)

\|\rho(t,x)\|_{{L^{p}_{t,x}}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}^{d})}\lesssim\||v|^{\gamma}f_{0}(x,v)\|_{L^{a}_{v}(\mathbb{R}^{d},L^{b}_{x}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}\qquad\mbox{if}\;\;\;\int f_{0}(x,v)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=0\quad\mbox{for all $v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$}\;\;?

Why such a choice of norms? On the left-hand side, this is the simplest choice. On the right-hand side, this set of norms is natural since it is invariant by the flow of the Liouville equation (in a way which is reminiscent of the dispersive Strichartz estimates, to which we will come back). Furthermore, the weight
\gamma>0
is needed in dimension
\geq 2
to obtain non-trivial estimates. Finally, a more general set of invariant norms is
\||v|^{\gamma}|D_{x}|^{\delta}\cdot\|_{L^{a}_{v}L^{b}_{x}}
, but we set
\delta=0
for simplicity.

1.2.
Main results

In dimension
1
, it is possible to omit the weight
\gamma
and obtain the following result.

Theorem 1.1

(Euclidean tori: dimension
d=1
)
.

For all
f_{0}=f_{0}(x,v)
such that
\int_{\mathbb{T}}f_{0}(x,v)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=0
, there holds

(4)

\|\rho\|_{L^{p}_{t,x}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})}\lesssim\|f_{0}\|_{L^{a}_{v}L^{b}_{x}},\qquad a=p^{\prime},\qquad b\geq\frac{p}{2},\qquad p>2.

Moreover, these are the only values of
(p,a,b)\in[1,\infty]^{3}
for which such an estimate holds.

In dimension larger or equal to
2
, the weight
\gamma
is needed, and our result is as follows.

Theorem 1.2

(Euclidean tori: dimension
d\geq 2
)
.

We consider the inequality

(5)

\|\rho\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{T}^{d})}\lesssim\||v|^{\gamma}f_{0}\|_{L^{a}_{v}L^{b}_{x}},\quad\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}f_{0}(x,v)\mathrm{d}x=0,

where
(p,a,b,\gamma)\in[1,\infty]^{3}\times\mathbb{R}
.

(i)

Necessary condition: the inequality (
5
) can only hold if

(6)

\gamma=\frac{d}{a^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{p},\qquad p\geq 2,\qquad p^{\prime}<a\leq p,\qquad b\geq\frac{dp}{d+1}.

(ii)

Sufficient condition including the endpoint in
b
: the inequality (
5
) is satisfied if (
6
) holds and furthermore

2<p<\infty,\qquad p^{\prime}<a<\min\left\{2,\frac{dp}{dp-(d+1)}\right\}.

(iii)

Sufficient condition excluding the endpoint in
b
: the inequality (
5
) is satisfied if (
6
) holds and

p^{\prime}<a<p,\qquad b>\frac{dp}{d+1}.

Remark 1.3
.

Including the endpoint in
b
in the above estimates is particularly relevant, since this corresponds to the scale-invariant case.

Both theorems above imply Strichartz estimates for general data: if

f_{0}(x,v)=g_{0}(v)+h_{0}(x,v),\qquad\mbox{with}\;\;\;\int h_{0}(x,v)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=0,

then

\|\rho\|_{L^{p}_{t,x}([0,1]\times\mathbb{T}^{d})}\lesssim\|g_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{v}}+\||v|^{\gamma}h_{0}\|_{L^{a}_{v}L^{b}_{x}},

provided that
p,\gamma,a,b
satisfy the hypotheses of
Theorem

1.1
or
Theorem

1.2
. Notice however that the time interval in the above inequality is now finite.

1.3.
Applications

1.3.1.
The
X
-ray transform on the cylinder

The dual of the map
f_{0}(x,v)\mapsto\rho(t,x)
is the modified
X
-ray transform

[\widetilde{X}f](x,v)=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}f(t,x+tv)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

(it differs from the standard
X
-ray transform by a factor
\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}
).

Given a function in
(x,v)\in\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we denote
\Pi_{0}
and
\Pi_{\neq}
for the orthogonal projections on functions which do not depend on
x
, and functions with zero mean in
x
respectively:

[\Pi_{0}f](x,v)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}f(x,v)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x,\qquad\Pi_{\neq}=\operatorname{Id}-\Pi_{0}.

By duality, the boundedness of

\rho\Pi_{\neq}|v|^{-\gamma}:\,L_{v}^{a}(\mathbb{R}^{d},L^{b}_{x}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))\,\longrightarrow\,L^{p}_{t,x}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}),

which is addressed in
Theorems

1.1
and
1.2
, is equivalent to that of

|v|^{-\gamma}\Pi_{\neq}\widetilde{X}:\,L^{p^{\prime}}_{t,x}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}^{d})\,\longrightarrow\,L_{v}^{a^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},L^{b^{\prime}}_{x}(\mathbb{T}^{d})).

1.3.2.
The Kakeya problem on the cylinder

Consider a family of tubes
\{T_{k}\}
of length
R
and cross section of radius
\delta
, and
\frac{\delta}{R}
-separated directions—this might be on Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
or on the cylinder
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}
.
In the whole space, the parameter
R
is usually normalized to
1
, which is always possible by scaling. But the Kakeya problem on the cylinder is only relevant if
R\to\infty
, both cases being equivalent for bounded
R
.

The Kakeya maximal operator conjecture on Euclidean space states that, for any
\varepsilon>0
,

(7)

\left\|\sum_{k}\mathbf{1}_{T_{k}}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}\lesssim\begin{cases}\left(\frac{R}{\delta}\right)^{\varepsilon}R^{\frac{d+1}{p}}&\mbox{if $p\leq\frac{d+1}{d}$}\\
\left(\frac{R}{\delta}\right)^{\varepsilon}R^{d}\delta^{-d+\frac{d+1}{p}}&\mbox{if $p>\frac{d+1}{d}$}.\end{cases}

We refer to
[
Cor77
]
for the classical proof of the case
d=2
, and to
[
Mat15
]
for a textbook presentation.

We now turn to the case of the cylinder. For simplicity, we focus on the case of tubes with direction
(1,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
with
|v|\lesssim 1
; the general case would only involve an additional weight, which is natural since the combinatorial properties of tubes depend critically on the size of
v
.

Theorem 1.4

(Maximal operator Kakeya on the cylinder)
.

On the cylinder
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}
, consider a family of tubes
\{T_{k}\}_{k\in\mathcal{K}}
with length
R
, cross section of radius
\delta
, and directions
(1,v_{k})
, where the
\{v_{k}\}
are
\frac{\delta}{R}
-separated and of size
\sim 1
. Then for all
p>2
there holds

(8)

\left\|\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\mathbf{1}_{T_{k}}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}^{d})}\lesssim R^{d+\frac{1}{p}}+R^{d}\delta^{\frac{d+1}{p}-d}.

Furthermore, this inequality is optimal: it can be saturated.

The proof of this theorem will be given in
Section

5
; it relies on the boundedness of
\widetilde{X}
which can be deduced by duality from
Theorems

1.1
and
1.2
. Without entering into details for now, it is interesting to give a geometric interpretation of the two terms on the right-hand side of (
8
):

•

The term
R^{d}\delta^{\frac{d+1}{p}-d}
corresponds to the “bush” example, where all tubes intersect at one point; this example is the one which saturates the inequality (
7
) in the whole space for
p\geq\frac{d+1}{d-1}
;

•

The term
R^{d+\frac{1}{p}}
can be understood as an average interaction of tubes which fill up the torus—this effect is absent in the case of the whole space.

1.4.
Related directions

1.4.1.
Strichartz estimates for the Schrödinger equation

This is where it all started! After Strichartz’ original paper
[
Str77
]
regarding estimates in the whole space, Bourgain
[
Bou93
]
initiated the study of estimates on the torus of the type

\|e^{it\Delta}f_{0}\|_{L^{p}_{t,x}([0,1]\times\mathbb{T}^{d})}\lesssim\|f_{0}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})},

(where
p\in[1,\infty],s\geq 0
) which was completed in
[
BD15
]
. One can also ask for estimates of the type

\|e^{it\Delta}f_{0}\|_{L^{p}_{t,x}([0,1]\times\mathbb{T}^{d})}\lesssim\|\widehat{f_{0}}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}

(where
p,q\in[1,\infty]
) but finding the optimal range of
(p,q)
is an open problem, equivalent to the Fourier restriction problem.

The estimates above on
e^{it\Delta}f_{0}
and (
2
), (
3
) have common features: the left-hand side of the equation is a space-time
L^{p}
norm, while the right-hand side norm is invariant through the flow of the linear problem (Liouville of Schrödinger). Furthermore, the Schrödinger and Liouville equations on the torus are intimately tied, since they are exchanged by the Wigner transform—though it does not seem possible to use this correspondence to derive analytical estimates.

1.4.2.
Euclidean space

The analog of
1
can be asked if the space variable ranges over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
; this is a classical subject which is the background of the present article. It will be surveyed in
Section

2
, suffice it for now to say that the conjecture

\|\rho\|_{L^{p}_{t,x}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|f_{0}\|_{L^{a}_{v}(\mathbb{R}^{d},L^{b}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\quad\mbox{if}\quad a^{\prime}=dp,\;\;b=\frac{dp}{d+1},\;\;p>\frac{d+1}{d}

is known if
d=1
or
p\geq\frac{d+2}{d}
.

1.4.3.
General compact manifolds

The Liouville equation can be generalized in a natural way to compact (or geometrically bounded) Riemannian manifolds
M
, by replacing transport along straight lines by transport along geodesics. In this context, Salort
[
Sal07
]
proved the following inequality

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L_{t}^{q}([0,1],L^{p}_{x}(M,L^{r}_{v}(T^{*}_{x}M)))}\lesssim\|\langle v\rangle^{\frac{1}{q}+\varepsilon}f_{0}\|_{L^{a}_{x,v}(TM)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\mbox{if}\qquad\frac{2}{q}=d\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{p}\right),\quad\frac{1}{a}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{p}\right),\quad q>2\geq a.

Furthermore, the value of
q
is optimal, as can be seen from considering stationary solutions of the form
g(|v|)
.

The strength of this estimate is its universality, but its weakness is that it is limited to a finite time interval. As we saw in the case of the torus, global in time estimates require additional assumptions and quantify relaxation to the equilibrium through mixing. Proving such estimates for general manifolds seems to be very difficult, but it is also very interesting!

1.4.4.
Velocity averaging lemmas

Velocity averaging lemmas play a crucial role in the theory of kinetic equations
[
GLPS88
,
GSR02
,
Per02
]
. They are usually formulated in the inhomogeneous case, but as observed in
[
Per04
, Remark 3.2]
, homogeneous versions follow. The basic instance is

\|\chi(t)\rho_{\psi}(t,x)\|_{H^{1/2}_{t,x}}\lesssim\|f_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}},\qquad\rho_{\psi}(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi(v)f_{0}(x-tv,v)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v,

where
f_{0}(x-tv,v)
is the solution of the Liouville equation set in the whole space, and
\chi,\psi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}
. This estimate gives improved regularity for
\rho_{\psi}
compared to
f_{0}
, which can be converted by Sobolev embedding into improved integrability. This is also a feature of our Strichartz estimates, which do give improved integrability—but the cutoff functions appearing above make this estimate local in time and velocity, which is quite different from what we are aiming at.

1.4.5.
Regularity in
v
and Landau damping

After decomposing
f
in Fourier series in
x

f(x,v)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}f_{k}(v)e^{ik\cdot x},

it is easily computed that

\rho(t,x)=(2\pi)^{d/2}\sum_{k}\widehat{f_{k}}(kt)e^{ik\cdot x},

where
\widehat{f_{k}}
stands for the Fourier transform in
v
of
f_{k}
. It is apparent from this formula that regularity in
v
translates into time-decay for
\rho
. See
[
Bed22
]
, Section 2 for a nice discussion and the connection with recent developments around Landau damping
[
BMM16
,
MV11
]
.

Though greater regularity in
v
of the initial data immediately gives better decay in
t
, our aim in the present paper is to obtain decay without assuming any regularity in
v
!

•

•

2.
The case of Euclidean space

This section focuses on the Strichartz problem for the Liouville equation set in the whole Euclidean space. We review the state of the art and give short proofs of the known results, with some new ideas.

2.1.
The conjectures and their equivalent formulations

On
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the density is given in terms of the density through the same formula as on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
, namely

\rho(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{0}(x-tv,v)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v.

We focus here on the question of boundedness of
f_{0}\to\rho
from
L^{a}_{v}L^{b}_{x}
to
L^{p}_{t,x}
. The following conjecture appeared in
[
Chr84
]
, though in the dual form which will be explained below.

Conjecture A
.

There holds

\|\rho\|_{L^{p}_{t,x}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|f_{0}\|_{L^{a}_{v}(\mathbb{R}^{d},L^{b}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}

if

a^{\prime}=dp,\;\;\;\;\;\;b=\frac{dp}{d+1},\;\;\;\;\;\;p>\frac{d+1}{d}.

Notice that, as opposed to the case of the torus, a weight in
v
is not needed.

The dual problem:
The dual of
f_{0}\mapsto\rho
is the modified
X
-ray transform

[\widetilde{X}f](x,v)=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}f(t,x+tv,v)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t,

which maps functions on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
to functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

The
X
-ray transform.
Given a line
\ell
in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
, we denote
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\ell}
the associated surface measure and define the
X
-ray transform on functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
through

[Xf](\ell)=\int_{\ell}f\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\ell}.

Lines in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
are naturally parameterized by their direction
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
and the intersection
y
of the line with the hyperplane orthogonal to
\theta
, which can be identified with
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

If
(x,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we denote
\ell_{x,v}
for the line in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
through
(0,x)
with direction
(1,v)
. Then

[Xf](\ell_{x,v})=\sqrt{1+v^{2}}\,[\widetilde{X}f](x,v).

Observe that
X
and
\widetilde{X}
only differ by a bounded factor if
v
is bounded.

A natural set of mixed norms in the set of lines is
L^{q_{1}}_{\theta}(\mathbb{S}^{d},L^{q_{2}}_{y}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, and the relevant question the boundedness of
X
from
L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
to
L^{q_{1}}_{\theta}(\mathbb{S}^{d},L^{q_{2}}_{y}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
.

Equivalent formulations of the conjecture.

Under the scaling assumption

a^{\prime}=dp,\qquad b=\frac{dp}{d+1},

the following statements are equivalent:

\begin{array}[]{rrcl}\rho:&L^{a}_{v}(\mathbb{R}^{d},L^{b}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))&\longrightarrow&L^{p}_{t,x}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})\\
\widetilde{X}:&L^{p^{\prime}}_{t,x}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})&\longrightarrow&L^{a^{\prime}}_{v}(\mathbb{R}^{d},L^{b^{\prime}}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d})),\\
X:&L^{p^{\prime}}_{t,x}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})&\longrightarrow&L^{a^{\prime}}_{\theta}(\mathbb{S}^{d},L^{b^{\prime}}_{y}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}).\end{array}

Indeed, the two first assertions are equivalent by duality. To see that the second implies the third, we restrict velocities to
|v|\leq 1
and observe that
X
and
\widetilde{X}
are equivalent, and the mixed norms as well. This gives boundedness of
X
for lines whose directions are restricted to a cap of
\mathbb{S}^{d}
, and the full boundedness follows by isotropy. Finally, the same argument gives that the third assertion implies the boundedness of
\widetilde{X}
restricted to velocities
\leq 1
, which can then be extended to all velocities by scaling.

2.3.
Proof of the case
d=1
of
A

This result is equivalent to the optimal
X
-ray transform estimate in dimension 2, which was originally proved in Oberlin–Stein
[
OS82
]
. We propose here a different proof, inspired by the treatment of the Fourier restriction problem in dimension 2 by Hörmander
[
Hör73
]
.

We consider first the operator

(9)

\mathcal{T}[F](t,x)=\iint F(x-tv,x-tw,v,w)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w,

which is linear in
F=F(y,z,v,w)
. On the one hand, we can see that

\|\mathcal{T}[F]\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\lesssim\|F\|_{L^{1}_{v,w}L^{\infty}_{y,z}}.

On the other hand, using that the change of variables
(t,x)\mapsto(x-tv,x-tw)
has Jacobian
|v-w|
, we get the estimate

\|\mathcal{T}[F]\|_{L^{1}_{t,x}}\leq\iiiint|F(x-tv,x-tw,v,w)|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=\|F\|_{L^{1}_{v,w}(|v-w|^{-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w)L^{1}_{y,z}}.

We interpolate between these two estimates and obtain

(10)

\|\mathcal{T}[F]\|_{L^{p}_{t,x}}\lesssim\|F\|_{L^{1}_{v,w}(|v-w|^{-\frac{1}{p}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w)L^{p}_{y,z}}\qquad\text{if }1\leq p\leq\infty.

More specifically, we use
[
BL76
, Theorem 5.4.1]
applied to the measures
\mathrm{d}v\mathrm{d}w
and
|v-w|^{-1}\mathrm{d}v\mathrm{d}w
.
We now write

\rho^{2}(t,x)=\iint f(x-tv,v)f(x-tw,w)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w=\iint F(x-tv,x-tw,v,w)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w

after setting

F(y,z,v,w)=f(y,v)f(z,w).

By the estimate (
10
), we can bound for
p>2

\displaystyle\|\rho\|_{L^{p}_{t,x}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\mathcal{T}[F]\|_{L^{\frac{p}{2}}_{t,x}}\lesssim\|F\|_{L^{1}_{v,w}(|v-w|^{-\frac{2}{p}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w)L^{\frac{p}{2}}_{y,z}}

\displaystyle=\iint|v-w|^{-\frac{2}{p}}\|f(y,v)\|_{L^{\frac{p}{2}}_{y}}\|f(z,w)\|_{L^{\frac{p}{2}}_{z}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}_{v}L^{\frac{p}{2}}_{x}}^{2},

where we have used the Hardy–Littlewood–Sobolev inequality in the last line.

2.4.
The proof of the case
p\geq\frac{d+2}{d}
of
A

By interpolation, it suffices to prove the case
p=\frac{d+2}{d}
, which is the inequality

\|\rho\|_{L^{\frac{d+2}{d}}_{t,x}}\lesssim\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\frac{d+2}{d+1}}_{x,v}}.

We will argue here through dispersive estimates and interpolation, following
[
CP96
,
KT98
]
. The optimal result follows from a combination of these two arguments, which we integrate here for the sake of the reader. The first step is to obtain dispersive estimates.

Lemma 2.1

(Dispersive estimates)
.

If
p\in[1,\infty]
, then

\|\rho(t)\|_{L^{p}_{x}}\lesssim t^{-d(1-\frac{1}{p})}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{x}L^{p}_{v}}

and if
1\leq r\leq p\leq\infty
, then

(11)

\|f(t)\|_{L^{p}_{x}L^{r}_{v}}\lesssim t^{-d\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{r}_{x}L^{p}_{v}}.

Proof.

This follows from complex interpolation between the two following observations. First, by Fubini’s theorem we have
\|\rho(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}_{x}}\leq\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{x,v}}
. We also have the dispersive estimate

|\rho(t,x)|\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f_{0}(x-tv,v)|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v=t^{-d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|f_{0}\left(y,\frac{x-y}{t}\right)\right|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\leq t^{-d}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{x}L^{\infty}_{v}}.

For the more general inequality, one can interpolate with the case
(r,p)=(p,p)
, for which the inequality holds with constant
1
, by conservation of the
L^{p}
norms under Liouville’s equation.
∎

From these dispersive estimates, we will derive Strichartz estimates of the type
L^{a}_{x,v}\to L^{q}_{t}L^{p}_{x}L^{r}_{v}
. Specializing to the case
q=p=\frac{d+2}{d}
,
r=1
and
a=\frac{d+2}{d+1}
gives the desired result.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
(q,p,r,a)\in[1,\infty]^{4}
such that the following conditions hold:

(12)

\frac{2}{q}=d\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{p}\right),\qquad\frac{1}{a}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{p}\right),\qquad p\geq a,\quad\text{and }q>a.

Then there holds

(13)

\|f\|_{L^{q}_{t}(\mathbb{R};L^{p}_{x}L^{r}_{v})}\lesssim\|f_{0}\|_{L^{a}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}.

Remark 2.3
.

The conditions (
12
) are actually also necessary for (
13
) to hold, see
[
KT98
]
. It is proven in
[
GP07
]
that there is no such estimate in the case
(d,q,p,a)=(1,2,\infty,2)
, but there is still an estimate if
L^{\infty}_{x}
is replaced with
{\rm BMO}_{x}
. In the same article it is also shown that in all dimensions
d\geq 1
the endpoint fails despite being conjectured in
[
KT98
]
.

Proof of
Theorem

2.2
.

Assume first that the theorem has already been proven in the more restrictive case
q>2\geq a
. Now, let
(q,p,r,a)
be as in the theorem but such that either
2\leq a<q
or
a<q\leq 2
. Pick
\alpha>0
such that
q\alpha:=q_{\alpha}>2\geq\tilde{a}_{\alpha}=\alpha a
and apply the restricted case to the function
f^{1/\alpha}
solving the Liouville equation, and the exponents
(q_{\alpha},p_{\alpha},r_{\alpha},a_{\alpha})=(\alpha q,\alpha p,\alpha r,\alpha a)
. One can check that the conditions on these coefficients are met so that we have

\|f^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\|_{L^{q_{\alpha}}_{t}L^{p_{\alpha}}_{x}L^{r_{\alpha}}_{v}}\lesssim\|f_{0}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\|_{L^{a_{\alpha}}_{x,v}},

which is nothing but the desired estimates.

We are left with the proof of the theorem in the case
q>2\geq a
, which will appear as very convenient in the following, because of the application of Hardy–Littlewood–Sobolev’s inequality and a square trick.

Observe that the adjoint
S^{*}
of the operator
S
defined for each
t
by
S(t)f_{0}(x,v)=f_{0}(x-tv,v)
is given for all
\phi=\phi(t,x,v)
by
S^{*}\phi(x,v)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(t,x+tv,v)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=\int_{\mathbb{R}}S(-t)\phi(t)(x,v)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t
.

Let’s argue by duality: the boundedness of
S:L^{a}_{x,v}\to L^{q}_{t}L^{p}_{x}L^{r}_{v}
is equivalent to that of its adjoint
S^{*}:L^{q^{\prime}}_{t}L^{p^{\prime}}_{x}L^{r^{\prime}}_{v}\to L^{a^{\prime}}_{x,v}
. since
a^{\prime}\geq 2
, we can write, using a squaring trick:

\displaystyle\|S^{*}\phi\|_{L^{a^{\prime}}_{x,v}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\iint\phi(t,x+tv,v)\phi(s,x+sv,v)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\right\|_{L^{a^{\prime}/2}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\iint\phi(t,x,v)\phi(s,x-(t-s)v,v)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\right\|_{L^{a^{\prime}/2}_{x,v}}.

In the second equality we have used the change of variable
(x,v)\leftarrow(x-tv,v)
.

An application of Young’s integral inequality (since
\frac{a^{\prime}}{2}\geq 1
), followed by Hölder’s inequality with
\frac{2}{a^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}
yields

\displaystyle\|S^{*}\phi\|_{L^{a^{\prime}}_{x,v}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\iint\|\phi(t)\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}_{x}L^{r^{\prime}}_{v}}\|S(t-s)\phi(s)\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}L^{p^{\prime}}_{v}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\iint\|\phi(t)\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}_{x}L^{r^{\prime}}_{v}}|t-s|^{-\delta}\|\phi(s)\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}_{x}L^{r^{\prime}}_{v}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t,

with
\delta=d\left(\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}\right)=d\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{p}\right)=\frac{2}{q}<1
. In the last inequality we have used the dispersion inequality (
11
) applied to the couple
(r^{\prime},p^{\prime})
(instead of
(p,r)
, and note that we have
1\leq p^{\prime}\leq r^{\prime}\leq\infty
).

The conclusion follows from an application of the Hardy–Littlewood–Sobolev inequality.
∎

3.
The Euclidean torus in dimension
1

The aim of this section is to prove
Theorem

1.1
.

3.1.
Necessary condition

We aim at proving that the inequality

(14)

\|\rho(t,x)\|_{L^{p}_{t,x}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})}\lesssim\|f_{0}\|_{L^{a}_{v}L^{b}_{x}},

can only hold for all
f_{0}
with zero average if

a=p^{\prime},\quad b\geq\frac{p}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad p>2.

The scaling argument.
We want to apply a classical scaling argument, but we need to be a bit careful, since the domain
\mathbb{T}
cannot be scaled. We consider the data in
\mathbb{R}^{d}

f_{0}(x,v)=\varphi(\lambda x)\psi(\mu v),

where
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{0}
and
\int\varphi(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=0
. The associated density is

\rho(t,x)=\int\varphi(\lambda(x-tv))\psi(\mu v)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v=\mu^{-1}R(\lambda\mu^{-1}t,\lambda x),

where

R(t,x)=\int\varphi(x-tv)\psi(v)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v.

For a given
t
it follows that

\operatorname{Supp}\rho(t,\cdot)\subset B\left(0,C\left(\frac{t}{\mu}+\frac{1}{\lambda}\right)\right)\subset B\left(0,\frac{1}{4}\right)\qquad\text{if}\;\;\begin{cases}0<t\ll\mu\\
\lambda\gg 1.\end{cases}

Under this condition, which we assume from now on, we can view
f_{0}(x,v)
and
\rho(t,x)
as functions on the torus. Then (
14
) implies that

\left\|\mu^{-1}R(\lambda\mu^{-1}t,\lambda x)\right\|_{L^{p}([0,c\mu]\times\mathbb{T})}\lesssim\left\|\varphi(\lambda x)\psi(\mu v)\right\|_{L^{a}_{v}L^{b}_{x}}

which can also be written

\mu^{-1+\frac{1}{p}}\lambda^{-\frac{2}{p}}\left\|R(t,x)\right\|_{L^{p}([0,c\lambda]\times\mathbb{T})}\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{1}{b}}\mu^{-\frac{1}{a}}\left\|\varphi(x)\psi(v)\right\|_{L^{a}_{v}L^{b}_{x}}.

Considering the limits
\mu\to 0
,
\mu\to\infty
and
\lambda\to\infty
gives the conditions
a=p^{\prime}
and
b\geq\frac{p}{2}
.

A function with a single frequency in
x
.
Choosing

f_{0}(x,v)=\varphi(v)e^{ix},

the density then writes

\rho(t,x)=\widehat{\varphi}(t)e^{ix},

and the inequality (
14
) becomes

\|\widehat{\varphi}\|_{L^{p}_{t}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\|\varphi\|_{L^{a}_{v}(\mathbb{R})},

which is known to hold only if
p=a^{\prime}
and
p\geq 2
, see for instance
[
Tao20
]
.

Why
p
cannot be
2
.
There remains to exclude
p=2
, in which case the inequality would be

\|\rho\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}\lesssim\|f_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{v}L^{1}_{x}}.

From
lemma

3.3
this would imply

\||\partial_{x}|^{-\frac{1}{2}}f_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\lesssim\|f_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{v}L^{1}_{x}}.

Specializing to the case
f_{0}(x,v)=\varphi(x)\psi(v)
, this would mean that

(15)

\||\partial_{x}|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\varphi\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}_{x}},

which is known to fail. Let’s indeed consider
\varphi(x)=\chi_{N}(x)-1=N\chi(Nx)-1
with
\chi\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}
of integral
1
. For
N
sufficiently large, this function is well-defined on
\mathbb{T}
, has zero mean, and
\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim C<\infty
. Its Fourier coefficients are given by
\widehat{\chi_{N}}(k)=\widehat{\chi}\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)
, standing for the Fourier transform over
\mathbb{R}
of
\chi
. Since
\widehat{\chi}(0)=1
, we have
|\widehat{\chi_{N}}(k)|>\frac{1}{2}
if
|k|\leq\sqrt{N}
and
N
sufficiently large. Thus (
15
) would imply

\sum_{1\leq|k|\leq\sqrt{N}}\frac{1}{|k|}\lesssim\sum_{|k|\geq 1}\frac{|\hat{\varphi}(k)|^{2}}{|k|}=\||\partial_{x}|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\varphi\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}\lesssim\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}_{x}}^{2}\leq C

which cannot hold as the left-hand side diverges as
N\to\infty
.

3.2.
The sufficient condition

We aim at proving that the inequality

(16)

\|\rho(t,x)\|_{L^{p}_{t,x}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})}\lesssim\|f_{0}\|_{L^{a}_{v}L^{b}_{x}},

holds for all
f_{0}
with zero average if

a=p^{\prime},\quad b\geq\frac{p}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad p>2.

This result will be proved through a series of lemmas below. First,
Lemmas

3.1
,
3.2
and
3.3
provide estimates in
L^{\infty}_{t,x}
,
L^{4}_{t,x}
and
L^{2}_{t,x}
respectively. Second, these estimates are interpolated in
Lemma

3.4
to obtain all intermediate values of
p
.

Lemma 3.1

(
L^{\infty}
estimate)
.

For any
f_{0}\in L^{1}_{v}L^{\infty}_{x}
there holds

\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})}\lesssim\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{v}L^{\infty}_{x}}.

Proof.

This follows immediately from the formula expressing
\rho(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{0}(x+tv,v)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v
, the triangle inequality and Minkowski’s inequality.
∎

Lemma 3.2

(
L^{4}
estimate)
.

If
\int f_{0}(x,v)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=0
for all
v
, then

\|\rho\|_{L^{4}_{t,x}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})}\lesssim\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\frac{4}{3}}_{v}L^{2}_{x}}.

Proof.

Expanding
f_{0}
in Fourier series in
x

f_{0}(x,v)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}}a_{k}(v)e^{ikx},

we have

\rho(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{0}(x-tv,v)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}}\widehat{a_{k}}(kt)e^{ikx}.

Using this decomposition and Hölder’s inequality gives

\displaystyle\|\rho\|_{L^{4}_{t,x}}^{4}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k_{1}+k_{2}=k_{3}+k_{4}}\int\widehat{a_{k_{1}}}(k_{1}t)\widehat{a_{k_{2}}}(k_{2}t)\overline{\widehat{a_{k_{3}}}(k_{3}t)\widehat{a_{k_{4}}}(k_{4}t)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k_{1}+k_{2}=k_{3}+k_{4}}\|\widehat{a_{k_{1}}}(k_{1}t)\|_{L^{4}_{t}}\|\widehat{a_{k_{2}}}(k_{2}t)\|_{L^{4}_{t}}\|\widehat{a_{k_{3}}}(k_{3}t)\|_{L^{4}_{t}}\|\widehat{a_{k_{4}}}(k_{4}t)\|_{L^{4}_{t}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k_{1}+k_{2}=k_{3}+k_{4}}|k_{1}k_{2}k_{3}k_{4}|^{-\frac{1}{4}}\|\widehat{a_{k_{1}}}(\eta)\|_{L^{4}_{\eta}}\|\widehat{a_{k_{2}}}(\eta)\|_{L^{4}_{\eta}}\|\widehat{a_{k_{3}}}(\eta)\|_{L^{4}_{\eta}}\|\widehat{a_{k_{4}}}(\eta)\|_{L^{4}_{\eta}}.

We now set
b_{k}=|k|^{-\frac{1}{4}}\|\widehat{a_{k}}(\eta)\|_{L^{4}_{\eta}}
so that the above can be written

\|\rho\|_{L^{4}_{t,x}}^{4}=\langle b_{k},b_{-k}*b_{k}*b_{k}\rangle,

where
\langle\,,\,\rangle
stands for the
\ell^{2}
inner product. On the one hand, the Hölder–O’Neil inequality, recalled in (
29
), gives

\|b_{k}\|_{\ell^{\frac{4}{3},2}_{k}}\lesssim\left\|\|\widehat{a_{k}}(\eta)\|_{L^{4}_{\eta}}\right\|_{\ell^{2}_{k}}\quad\text{and}\quad\|\rho\|_{L^{4}_{t,x}}^{4}\lesssim\|b_{k}\|_{\ell^{\frac{4}{3},2}_{k}}\|b_{-k}*b_{k}*b_{k}\|_{\ell^{4,2}_{k}}.

On the other hand, the Young–O’Neil inequality, recalled in (
30
) and the inclusion relation between Lorentz spaces gives

\|b_{-k}*b_{k}*b_{k}\|_{\ell^{4,2}_{k}}\lesssim\|b_{k}\|_{\ell^{\frac{4}{3},2}_{k}}\|b_{k}*b_{k}\|_{\ell^{2,\infty}_{k}}\lesssim\|b_{k}\|_{\ell^{\frac{4}{3},2}_{k}}^{3}

Overall, this gives

\|\rho\|_{L^{4}_{t,x}}^{4}\lesssim\|\widehat{a_{k}}(\eta)\|_{\ell^{2}_{k}L^{4}_{\eta}}^{4}.

Finally, using successively the Hausdorff–Young, Minkowski and Parseval inequalities, we obtain that

\|\rho\|_{L^{4}_{t,x}}\lesssim\|\widehat{a_{k}}\|_{\ell^{2}_{k}L^{4}_{\eta}}\lesssim\|a_{k}(v)\|_{\ell^{2}_{k}L^{\frac{4}{3}}_{v}}\leq\|a_{k}(v)\|_{L^{\frac{4}{3}}_{v}\ell^{2}_{k}}=\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\frac{4}{3}}_{v}L^{2}_{x}}.\qed

Lemma 3.3

(
L^{2}
estimate)
.

Assume that
\int_{\mathbb{T}}f_{0}(x,v)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=0
for all
v
, then

\|\rho\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})}=\||D_{x}|^{-\frac{1}{2}}f_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}.

Proof.

Writing

f_{0}(x,v)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}}a_{k}(v)e^{ikx},

we have

\rho(t,x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}}\widehat{a_{k}}(kt)e^{ikx},

and thus, by Parseval’s and Plancherel’s theorem

\|\rho\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}^{2}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}}|k|^{-1}\|\widehat{a_{k}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\||D_{x}|^{-1/2}f_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.\qed

Theorem

1.1
will be a consequence of the following interpolation lemma, and its consequence on subspaces. More precisely, if
\Pi:f\mapsto f-\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(\cdot,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x
, then if
\Pi:B_{i}\to B_{i}
for all
i\in\{0,1\}
then interpolation commutes with this projection
[
Tri78
, Theorem 1.17.1.1]

(\Pi B_{0},\Pi B_{1})_{\theta,r}=\Pi(B_{0},B_{1})_{\theta,r}.

Lemma 3.4

(Interpolation)
.

Let
T
be a linear operator defined from
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{v}\times\mathbb{T}_{x})
to
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{T}_{x})
be a linear operator with the mapping properties:

(17)

\displaystyle T:L^{1}_{v}L^{\infty}_{x}\longrightarrow L^{\infty}_{t,x},

(18)

\displaystyle T:L^{\frac{4}{3}}_{v}L^{2}_{x}\longrightarrow L^{4}_{t,x},

(19)

\displaystyle T:L^{2}_{v}\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\longrightarrow L^{2}_{t,x}.

Then for all
p\in(2,\infty]
,
T
is a continuous operator
T:L^{p^{\prime}}_{v}L^{\frac{p}{2}}_{x}\longrightarrow L^{p}_{t,x}
.

Proof.

Let
p\in(4,\infty)
and
\theta=1-\frac{4}{p}\in(0,1)
, which is such that
L^{p}_{t,x}=[L^{4}_{t,x},L^{\infty}_{t,x}]_{\theta}
, the complex interpolation space
[
BL76
, Chapter 4]
. Define
\frac{1}{p_{\theta}}=\frac{1-\theta}{4/3}+\theta=\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}
.
From the complex interpolation theorem
[
BL76
, Theorem 4.1.2]
we obtain

T:[L^{\frac{4}{3}}_{v}L^{2}_{x},L^{1}_{v}L^{\infty}_{x}]_{\theta}\to[L^{4}_{t,x},L^{\infty}_{t,x}]_{\theta}=L^{p}_{t,x}.

We now make use of
[
BL76
, Theorem 5.1.2]
, which yields

[L^{\frac{4}{3}}_{v}L^{2}_{x},L^{1}_{v}L^{\infty}_{x}]_{\theta}=L^{p(\theta)}_{v}([L^{2}_{x},L^{\infty}_{x}]_{\theta})=L^{p^{\prime}}_{v}L^{\frac{p}{2}}_{x}.

Similarly, for any
p\in(2,4)
and
\theta=1-\frac{2}{p}
we obtain the continuity:

T:[L^{2}_{v}\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{x},L^{\frac{4}{3}}_{v}L^{2}_{x}]_{\theta}\longrightarrow[L^{2}_{t,x},L^{4}_{t,x}]_{\theta}=L^{p}_{t,x}.

Using again the property of interpolation of vector-valued
L^{q}
spaces mentioned above and the complex interpolation of homogeneous Sobolev spaces there holds

[\dot{H}_{x}^{-\frac{1}{2}},L^{2}_{x}]_{\theta}=\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2}{p}}_{x}.

The conclusion now follows from the fact that on the torus there holds
\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2}{p}}_{x}=H^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2}{p}}_{x}
, and the Sobolev embedding
L^{\frac{p}{2}}_{x}\hookrightarrow H^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2}{p}}_{x}
.
∎

4.
The Euclidean torus in dimension
d\geq 2

Our aim in this section is to prove
Theorem

1.2
.

4.1.
Necessary conditions

We shall prove here the necessary conditions in
Theorem

1.2
for the inequality

(20)

\|\rho\|_{L^{p}_{t,x}}\lesssim\||v|^{\gamma}f_{0}\|_{L^{a}_{v}L^{b}_{x}},

to hold for all
f_{0}
with mean zero in
x
.

The conditions
\gamma=d(1-\frac{1}{a})-\frac{1}{p}
and
p\leq b\frac{d+1}{d}
.
They are obtained through a scaling argument as follows. Data of the form

f_{0}(x,v)=\varphi(\lambda x)\psi(\mu v),\qquad\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\qquad\int\varphi=0

give
\rho(t,x)=\mu^{-d}R(\lambda\mu^{-1}t,\lambda x)
for a function
R
. As long as
0<t\ll\mu
and
\lambda\gg 1
, this can be viewed as a function on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
. The inequality becomes

\mu^{-d+\frac{1}{p}}\lambda^{-\frac{d+1}{p}}\|R\|_{L^{p}([0,c\lambda]\times\mathbb{T}^{d})}\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{d}{b}}\mu^{-\gamma-\frac{d}{a}}\|\varphi(x)\psi(v)\|_{L^{a}_{v}L^{b}_{x}},

which gives
\gamma=d(1-\frac{1}{a})-\frac{1}{p}
and
p\leq b\frac{d+1}{d}
after letting
\mu\to\pm\infty
and
\lambda\to+\infty
.

The condition
a\geq p^{\prime}
.
It is obtained by examining the case of single mode data of the form

(21)

f_{0}(x,v)=\varphi_{1}(v_{1})\varphi_{2}(v^{\prime})e^{ix_{1}},

where
v_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
and
v^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
make up
v=(v_{1},v^{\prime})
.
Then (
3
) becomes

(22)

\widehat{\varphi_{2}}(0)\|\widehat{\varphi_{1}}\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\||v|^{\gamma}\varphi_{1}(v_{1})\varphi_{2}(v^{\prime})\|_{L^{a}_{v}}.

Choosing
\varphi_{1}(v_{1})=\Phi(\lambda v_{1})
and
\varphi_{2}
localized near the sphere
|v^{\prime}|=1
such that
\widehat{\varphi}_{2}(0)\neq 0
, we obtain that
\varphi=\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}
is localized near
|v|=1
as
\lambda\to\infty
. The inequality becomes

\lambda^{-1+\frac{1}{p}}\widehat{\varphi_{2}}(0)\|\widehat{\Phi}\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{1}{a}}\|\Phi\|_{L^{a}}\|\varphi_{2}\|_{L^{a}},

which gives the desired condition as
\lambda\to\infty
.

The condition
a\neq p^{\prime}
.
It relies on a refinement of the previous example: we choose now

\varphi_{1}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{0},\qquad\operatorname{supp}\varphi_{1}\subset\left\{\frac{1}{2}\leq|v_{1}|\leq 1\right\},\qquad\varphi_{2}(v^{\prime})=\langle v^{\prime}\rangle^{-(d-1)}\mathbf{1}_{2\leq|v^{\prime}|<N}.

Observe first that

\widehat{\varphi_{2}}(0)=\int\varphi_{2}\sim\log N.

Second, the condition
a=p^{\prime}
gives
\gamma=\frac{d-1}{p}
and

\displaystyle\||v|^{\gamma}\varphi_{1}(v_{1})\varphi_{2}(v^{\prime})\|_{L^{a}_{v}}

\displaystyle\sim\|\langle v^{\prime}\rangle^{\gamma}\varphi_{2}(v^{\prime})\|_{L^{a}_{v^{\prime}}}\sim\|\langle v^{\prime}\rangle^{\gamma-(d-1)}\mathbf{1}_{|v^{\prime}|\lesssim N}\|_{L^{a}_{v^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\sim\|\langle v^{\prime}\rangle^{-\frac{d-1}{p^{\prime}}}\mathbf{1}_{2\leq|v^{\prime}|\leq N}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}\sim(\log N)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}.

Comparing the two above equivalents and (
22
) gives
\log N\lesssim(\log N)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}
, which excludes
p<\infty
as
N\to\infty
.

The condition
a\leq p
.

Consider a single mode zero-average initial data of the form

f_{0}(x,v)=e^{ix_{1}}\psi_{R}(v),

where
x=(x_{1},x^{\prime})\in\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}^{d-1}
and where
\psi_{R}
is defined for all
v=(v_{1},v^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
by

\psi_{R}(v_{1},v^{\prime})=\frac{\mathbf{1}_{1<v_{1}<R}\mathbf{1}_{|v^{\prime}|<v_{1}}}{v_{1}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}+(d-1)}}.

We begin by computing for
t>0

\rho(t,x)=e^{ix_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-itv_{1}}\psi_{R}(v)\mathrm{d}v=e^{ix_{1}}\int_{1}^{R}e^{-itv_{1}}v_{1}^{-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\mathrm{d}v_{1}=e^{ix_{1}}t^{-\frac{1}{p}}\int_{t}^{tR}e^{-i\tau}\tau^{-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau.

It follows that

\|\rho\|_{L^{p}_{t,x}}^{p}\geq\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t}\left|\int_{t}^{tR}e^{-i\tau}\tau^{-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\right|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t.

The function
H(a,b)=\int_{a}^{b}e^{-i\tau}\tau^{-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau
converges as
(a,b)\to(0,\infty)
to some non-zero complex number. Therefore, there exists
\delta
,
M
and
\varepsilon
such that
|H(a,b)|>\varepsilon
for
a<\delta
and
b>M
. Then, if
R>\frac{M}{\delta}
and
t\in[\frac{M}{R},\delta]
, we have
|H(t,tR)|>\varepsilon
and therefore

(23)

It follows from this expression that

\|f\|_{L^{p,p}}\sim\|f\|_{L^{p}},\qquad\|f\|_{L^{p,q_{1}}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p,q_{2}}}\qquad\mbox{if $q_{1}\geq q_{2}$}.

The Hölder inequality was generalized to the scale of Lorentz spaces by Hunt

. It states that

(29)

\|fg\|_{L^{r,s}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p,s_{1}}}\|g\|_{L^{q,s_{2}}},

whenever

\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q},\qquad\frac{1}{s}=\frac{1}{s_{1}}+\frac{1}{s_{2}},\qquad 0<s_{1},s_{2}\leq\infty,\qquad 0\leq p,q,r\leq\infty.

The Young inequalities were generalized by O’ Neil
[
O’N63
]
, see also
[
Gra11
, p. 73]
, under the form

(30)

\|f*g\|_{L^{r,s}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p,s_{1}}}\|g\|_{L^{q,s_{2}}},

whenever

1+\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q},\qquad\frac{1}{s}=\frac{1}{s_{1}}+\frac{1}{s_{2}},\qquad 1<p,q,r\leq\infty,\qquad 0<s_{1},s_{2}\leq\infty.

Finally, we will also resort to the following sharp version of the Hausdorff–Young inequality

(31)

\|\widehat{f}\|_{L^{p,p^{\prime}}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}},\qquad p\in[2,\infty).

Indeed, the Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}
enjoys the following mapping properties

\mathcal{F}:L^{2}\to L^{2},\qquad\mathcal{F}:L^{1}\to L^{\infty}.

Interpolation via the real method
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\theta,p^{\prime}}
with
\theta=\frac{2}{p}
and for
p\in(2,\infty)
yields the boundedness of
\mathcal{F}:L^{p^{\prime}}=L^{p^{\prime},p^{\prime}}\to L^{p,p^{\prime}}
, which is the claimed inequality.

Appendix B
Interpolation facts

Let
X
be a Banach space, and
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v
the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
, and
w:\mathbb{T}^{d}\to[0,\infty)
, which is positive almost everywhere. We recall that an equivalent norm on
L^{a,r}(w^{a}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v,X)
is given by

\|f\|_{L^{a,r}(w^{a}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v,X)}=\left\|t\nu\left(\{v:\|f(v)\|_{X}>t\}\right)^{\frac{1}{a}}\right\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}{t})},\qquad\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu=w(v)^{a}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v.

This norm is in general not equivalent to the norm

\|f\|_{\mathcal{L}_{w}^{a,r}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v;X)}=\|wf\|_{L^{a,r}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v;X)}.

In the following, we denote by
\mathcal{L}_{w}^{a,r}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v;X)
the space obtained by completion under this norm and after identifying functions by equality almost everywhere.

Observe that when
r=a
there holds

\mathcal{L}^{a,a}_{w}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v;X)=L^{a}(w^{a}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v;X).

Let us recall interpolation notations.
Let
(A_{0},A_{1})
be a compatible couple of Banach spaces, i.e., two
Banach spaces continuously embedded in a common space.

The real method.

For
a\in A_{0}+A_{1}
and
t>0
, the
K
-functional is defined by

K(t,a;A_{0},A_{1}):=\inf\left\{\|a_{0}\|_{A_{0}}+t\|a_{1}\|_{A_{1}},a=a_{0}+a_{1}\in A_{0}+A_{1}\right\},

For
0<\theta<1
and
1\leq q\leq\infty
, the real interpolation space

(A_{0},A_{1})_{\theta,q}
consists of all
a\in A_{0}+A_{1}
such that

\|a\|_{(A_{0},A_{1})_{\theta,q}}:=\left\|t^{-\theta}K(t,a;A_{0},A_{1})\right\|_{L^{q}((0,\infty),\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}{t})}

is finite, with the usual modification when
q=\infty
.

The complex method.

Consider the strip
S=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:0\leq\operatorname{Re}z\leq 1\}
.
The Calderón space
\mathcal{F}(A_{0},A_{1})
consists of all functions

f:\mathbb{C}\to A_{0}+A_{1}
which are analytic on
S
, continuous on
\overline{S}
, and such that the following subsets are bounded:

\{f(z),z\in S\}\subset A_{0}+A_{1},\qquad\{f(j+it):t\in\mathbb{R}\}\subset A_{j},\quad j\in\{0,1\}.

It is endowed with the norm

\|f\|_{\mathcal{F}(A_{0},A_{1})}:=\max_{j\in\{0,1\}}\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\|f(j+it)\|_{A_{j}}.

For
0<\theta<1
, the complex interpolation space
[A_{0},A_{1}]_{\theta}
consists of all elements
a\in A_{0}+A_{1}
for which there exists

f\in\mathcal{F}(A_{0},A_{1})
satisfying
f(\theta)=a
, with norm

\|a\|_{[A_{0},A_{1}]_{\theta}}:=\inf\{\|f\|_{\mathcal{F}(A_{0},A_{1})},f\in\mathcal{F}(A_{0},A_{1}),f(\theta)=a\}.

The interpolation theorems.
Consider a linear operator
T:A_{0}+A_{1}\to B_{0}+B_{1}
, where
(A_{0},A_{1})
and
(B_{0},B_{1})
are compatible couples. Assume also that
T:A_{j}\to B_{j}
is continuous for all
j\in\{0,1\}
, then it follows
[
BL76
, Theorem 3.1.2, Theorem 4.1.2]
that
T:(A_{0},A_{1})_{\theta,r}\to(B_{0},B_{1})_{\theta,r}
is continuous for all
\theta\in(0,1)
and
r\in[1,\infty]
, and also continuous as a map
[A_{0},A_{1}]_{\theta}\to[B_{0},B_{1}]_{\theta}
.

Our goal is to prove the following interpolation facts that are used in this article.
These are not new results, but they are not easily found in the literature.

Proposition B.1
.

The following interpolation embeddings hold:

(i)

Let
a_{0},a_{1}\in[1,\infty)
such that
a_{0}\neq a_{1}
, and
1\leq b_{0},b_{1}\leq\infty
. Let
w_{0},w_{1}:\mathbb{T}\to[0,\infty)
be two measurable functions, taking positive values almost everywhere. Define for
\theta\in(0,1)
:

\frac{1}{a_{\theta}}=\frac{1-\theta}{a_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{a_{1}},\qquad\frac{1}{b_{\theta}}=\frac{1-\theta}{b_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{b_{1}},\qquad w_{\theta}=w_{0}^{\frac{(1-\theta)a_{\theta}}{a_{0}}}w_{1}^{\frac{\theta a_{\theta}}{a_{1}}}.

Then,

[L^{a_{0}}(w_{0}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v;L^{b_{0}}),L^{a_{1}}(w_{1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v;L^{b_{1}})]_{\theta}=L^{a_{\theta}}(w_{\theta}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v;L^{b_{\theta}}).

(ii)

Let
a_{0},a_{1}\in[1,\infty)
such that
a_{0}\neq a_{1}
. Let
\theta\in(0,1)
, and
1\leq r\leq\infty
. Define
a_{\theta}
such that
\frac{1}{a_{\theta}}=\frac{1-\theta}{a_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{a_{1}}
. Let
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
be a Banach space. Let
w:\mathbb{T}\to[0,\infty)
taking positive values almost everywhere. Then

\left(\mathcal{L}_{w}^{a_{0},1}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v;X),\mathcal{L}_{w}^{a_{1},1}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v;X)\right)_{\theta,r}=\mathcal{L}_{w}^{a_{\theta},r}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v;X).

Proof.

(
i
) Our starting point is the following interpolation result
[
BL76
, Theorem 5.1.2]
:

[L^{a_{0}}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v,A_{0}),L^{a_{1}}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v,A_{1})]_{\theta}=L^{a_{\theta}}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v,[A_{0},A_{1}]_{\theta}),

for Banach spaces
A_{0}
and
A_{1}
, for which we take
A_{i}=L^{b_{i}}
in our case. Then we conclude as in
[
BL76
, Theorem 5.5.3]
by observing that the map

S:\mathcal{F}(L^{a_{0}}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v;A_{0}),L^{a_{1}}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v;A_{1}))\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}(L^{a_{0}}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v;A_{0}),L^{a_{1}}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v;A_{1}))

defined by
Sf(z)=w_{0}^{\frac{1-z}{a_{0}}}w_{1}^{\frac{z}{a_{1}}}f(z)
, where
\mathcal{F}(X,Y)
denotes the Calderón class defined in the complex interpolation method is an isometric isomorphism.

(
ii
) Since the multiplication operator by
w
induces an isometric isomorphism
\mathcal{L}_{w}^{a_{i},1}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v;X)\to L^{a_{i},1}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v;X)
, it follows that

K\left(t,f;\mathcal{L}_{w}^{a_{0},1}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v;X),\mathcal{L}_{w}^{a_{1},1}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v;X)\right)=K\left(t,wf;L^{a_{0},1}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v,X),L^{a_{1},1}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v;X)\right),

so that our task boils down to showing the interpolation property

(32)

\left(L^{a_{0},1}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v;X),L^{a_{1},1}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v;X)\right)_{\theta,r}=L^{a_{\theta},r}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v;X).

For any function
f=f(v,x)
, write
F(v)=\|f(v)\|_{X}
. We claim that there holds:

(33)

K(t,f;L^{a_{0},1}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v;X),L^{a_{1},1}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v;X))=K(t,F;L^{a_{0},1},L^{a_{1},1}),

where the spaces
L^{a_{0},1}
are the Lorentz spaces on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
endowed with the Lebesgue measure. Once this claim is proved, then (
32
) follows from (
33
) and
(L^{a_{0},1},L^{a_{1},1})_{\theta,r}=L^{a,r}
, whose proof follows from the reiteration theorem and (
28
).

In order to prove (
33
), start with
f=f_{0}+f_{1}
, and write the associated
F
as
F=G_{1}+G_{2}
with
G_{i}(v)=\frac{F(v)F_{i}(v)}{F_{0}(v)+F_{1}(v)}
where
F_{i}(v)=\|f_{i}(v)\|_{X}
. The triangle inequality implies
F(v)\leq F_{0}(v)+F_{1}(v)
and therefore
0\leq G_{i}(v)\leq F_{i}(v)
. Now, remark that
\{v:G_{i}(v)>t\}\subset\{v:F_{i}(v)>t\}
so that

\|G_{i}\|_{L^{a_{i},1}}\leq\|F_{i}\|_{L^{a_{i},1}}=\|f_{i}\|_{L^{a_{i},1}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v;X)},

which is enough to imply

K(t,F,L^{a_{0},1},L^{a_{1},1})\leq K(t,f,L^{a_{0},1}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v;X),L^{a_{1},1}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v;X)).

It remains to prove the reverse inequality. To this end, consider a decomposition
F=F_{0}+F_{1}
and define
f_{i}(v)=\frac{F_{i}(v)}{F(v)}f(v)
where we recall that
F(v)=\|f(v)\|_{X}
. Then
f=f_{0}+f_{1}
and
\|f_{i}\|_{L^{a_{i},1}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v;X)}=\|F_{i}\|_{L^{a_{i},1}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v)}
which is enough to conclude.
∎
