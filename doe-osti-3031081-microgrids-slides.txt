What We Mean by Energy Service

 What We Mean by Energy Service
Simply put, an energy system provides:
• Reliability: Having power when you need it.
• Availability: Provide enough energy to meet a defined need (thermal control,
  electrical needs, transportation) in terms of power (maximum load) over a length of
  time (energy),
• Quality: Energy is supplied in a way to meet needs of the loads.
 • Voltage: Amplitude of the power wave form.
 • Frequency: Maintaining a balance of power supply and demand.
 • Power Factor maintenance & Reactive Power supply (VAR Support): All impedance devices
 require both active and reactive power.
 • Harmonics Distortion: The quality of the power waveform, which can impact electronic
 devices

Options to Improve Resiliency

 Options to Improve Resiliency
• Grid Improvements – strengthening the grid in areas where damage is possible
  or expected.
• Generation Disbursement – power generation that is more likely to provide
  service when needed.
• Resiliency Hubs – defined locations designed to provide key services to a
  community members during normal times but also during disruptions and
  recovery, typically includes back-up energy services.
• Grid Isolation – isolating from the main grid in times of need (microgrids).
• Critical Energy Service Reductions – reducing need for resilience, move critical
  services to more secure locations, don’t build in vulnerable locations.
• Energy Service Alternatives - transitioning to more resilient methods to meet end
  needs – alternative lighting, locally sourced heating (wood), improved building
  design.

Resiliency Solutions are Messy

 Resiliency Solutions are Messy
Unfortunately, there are no clearcut decision frameworks to assist thinking on expanding local
resiliency, especially due to the cross-cutting nature of resiliency.
Working across stakeholders to develop a local resiliency plans can help address the best mix
of solutions to address critical energy needs as part of a larger resiliency framework.
Understanding what you are trying to protect from (intentional power disruptions (PSPS),
natural disasters of a specific type, etc) is critical to developing a successful resiliency plan?
• What are the services that you are protecting?
• What are the local conditions or concerns?
• What is the current infrastructure and how adaptable is it?
• How are resiliency changes going to be paid for, especially since improved resiliency is
  hard to document with a return on investment (work by Jorden Burns can help clarify this).

 Native Village of Kluti-Kaah
 Planned Community Center
Project Summary
The Native Village of Kluti-Kaah (NVKK) will address critical power
quality issues through the design and implementation of energy
storage and advanced microgrid controls.

Current Energy Landscape and Challenges
NVKK is currently grid connected via Copper Valley Electric
Association, receiving electricity generated primarily from hydropower
in the summer and diesel in the winter. This seasonal switch results in
increased electric costs and poor power quality.

Project Goals and Expected Results
By implementing batteries and microgrid controls, NVKK can isolate
from CVEA when necessary, riding through outages or instability,
 Photo by: Pete Peschang
improving community resiliency. Particularly, this project aims to
protect critical community infrastructure including the Cultural
Learning and Recreational Shelter to provide the community with
essential services during grid failures or extreme weather events.

 • Engage stakeholders.
 • Identify mission drivers.
 • Define resilience for mutual understanding.
 • Develop metrics to access resilience readiness.
 • Set specific resilience goals at the agency and site level.

 •Identify critical missions and infrastructure
 Approach •Define systems and baseline conditions
 •Assess risks based on vulnerabilities and likelihood of threats (past, current, and future)
 •Develop resilience value and impact that is needed to reach identified goals

 Identify Potential Solutions
 •Evaluate energy and water efficiency to optimize facilities and systems that reduces overall demand
 •Evaluate infrastructure improvements to improve resiliency
 •Evaluate distributed energy and water generation opportunities (with islanding controls and storage)
 •Conduct economic analysis that incorporates valuing resiliency

 Develop Roadmap and Prioritize Solutions
 •Prioritize projects and operational improvements based on risk framework
 •Identify funding streams
 •Develop resilience action plan that provides an implementation roadmap for strategic funding and prioritization

 Implement and Measure Progress

 •Conduct project measurement and verification
 •Reevaluate resiliency metrics to assess resilience readiness
 •Review, revise and approve resilience implementation plan based on evaluation of projects
