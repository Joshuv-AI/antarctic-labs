Coarse-Grained Resolution and Pressure–Flux Work Depletion for Navier–Stokes CKN Badness
Runlong Yu

Abstract
We prove a finite-scale coarse-grained decomposition for the three-dimensional incompressible Navier–Stokes equations near the Caffarelli–Kohn–Nirenberg local regularity framework. The first part is a local resolution lemma for the scale-critical quantity: for every spatial filter length ℓ > 0 \ell>0 , Ψ ⁡ ( r ) ≤ 4 ​ Ψ ℓ ​ ( r ) + 4 ​ Ω ℓ ​ ( r ) , \Psi(r)\leq 4\Psi^{\ell}(r)+4\Omega^{\ell}(r), where Ψ ℓ ​ ( r ) \Psi^{\ell}(r) is the corresponding coarse-grained velocity–pressure quantity and Ω ℓ ​ ( r ) \Omega^{\ell}(r) is the explicitly defined subfilter residual. Thus a CKN-bad scale is either visible at the resolved level or is carried by unresolved velocity–pressure oscillation. The second part is an exact fixed-chain depletion theorem for the combined pressure–flux work distribution G ℓ = Π ℓ + ∇ ⋅ ( P ℓ ​ U ℓ ) , Π ℓ = − R ℓ : ∇ U ℓ , G^{\ell}=\Pi^{\ell}+\nabla\cdot(P^{\ell}U^{\ell}),\qquad\Pi^{\ell}=-R^{\ell}:\nabla U^{\ell}, which is the signed work density appearing in the localized resolved-energy balance. For finite-dimensional active test families with common endpoint traces, we obtain a constructive active-work extraction and a weighted telescoping inequality: forward combined work and resolved dissipation are paid by the initial localized kinetic energy, explicit localization leakage, and negative combined work/backscatter.



Keywords.

Navier–Stokes equations; suitable weak solutions; partial regularity; Caffarelli–Kohn–Nirenberg theory; coarse graining; Reynolds stress; pressure work; energy flux; subfilter residual; backscatter.

Introduction

Let
(u,p)
be a suitable weak solution of the three-dimensional incompressible Navier–Stokes equations

\partial_{t}u-\Delta u+\nabla\cdot(u\otimes u)+\nabla p=0,\qquad\nabla\cdot u=0.

(1.1)

The weak-solution framework goes back to Leray and Hopf
[
1
,
2
]
. The local energy inequality and the partial regularity theory of Scheffer and Caffarelli–Kohn–Nirenberg place the local regularity problem in terms of scale-critical quantities; see
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
. A standard CKN quantity in a parabolic cylinder is

r^{-2}\int_{Q_{r}(z_{0})}\left(|u|^{3}+|p-(p)_{B_{r}(x_{0})}(t)|^{3/2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

(1.2)

The subtraction of a spatial pressure mean is essential because the pressure is determined only up to an arbitrary function of time.

A different but compatible viewpoint is obtained by spatial coarse graining. Smooth filters and resolved energy balances are standard tools in the mathematical study of energy transfer and anomalous dissipation; compare
[
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
]
. For a filter length
\ell>0
, set

U^{\ell}=S_{\ell}u,\qquad P^{\ell}=S_{\ell}p,\qquad R^{\ell}=S_{\ell}(u\otimes u)-U^{\ell}\otimes U^{\ell}.

The resolved interscale flux and the combined pressure–flux work distribution are

\Pi^{\ell}=-R^{\ell}:\nabla U^{\ell},\qquad G^{\ell}=\Pi^{\ell}+\nabla\cdot(P^{\ell}U^{\ell}).

(1.3)

The object
G^{\ell}
is the scalar work distribution that appears in the localized resolved-energy identity. It is signed; it is not a pressure norm, not an unsigned flux norm, and not automatically comparable to the positive CKN quantity (
1.2
).

The first result of this paper is a resolution lemma for (
1.2
). With
\Psi(r)
denoting the full CKN quantity,
\Psi^{\ell}(r)
the corresponding resolved quantity, and
\Omega^{\ell}(r)
the residual carried by
(u-U^{\ell},p-P^{\ell})
, we prove

\Psi(r)\leq 4\Psi^{\ell}(r)+4\Omega^{\ell}(r).

Consequently, a full CKN-bad scale is either visible in the resolved velocity–pressure pair or else the subfilter residual is quantitatively large. This statement is algebraic, gauge invariant, and independent of the Navier–Stokes equation. Its role is to prevent a detector based on
(U^{\ell},P^{\ell},R^{\ell})
from being asked to detect information that the filter has removed.

The second result is a finite-chain work depletion theorem for
G^{\ell}
. On a fixed chain of adjacent slabs, finite-dimensional active test families produce a selected nonnegative weight on each slab. The selected combined work satisfies an explicit extraction lower bound in terms of a finite coefficient norm. Summing the exact resolved-energy identity over the chain gives a weighted telescoping estimate: forward combined pressure–flux work and resolved dissipation are controlled by initial localized kinetic energy, localization leakage, and negative combined work, recorded as backscatter.

These two results fit together through the following conditional implication:

\boxed{\Psi(r_{k})\geq\varepsilon_{0}\Longrightarrow\Omega^{\ell}(r_{k})>\eta\varepsilon_{0}\quad\text{or}\quad\Psi^{\ell}(r_{k})\geq c_{0}\varepsilon_{0},}

and, if an independent coarse observability estimate is available,

\boxed{\Psi^{\ell}(r_{k})\geq c_{0}\varepsilon_{0}\Longrightarrow\mathfrak{A}_{k}(G^{\ell})\geq c_{\mathrm{obs}},}

then the depletion theorem applies to the detected resolved work. The difficult implication is the second boxed line. It is not proved here. It is a separate observability or compactness-rigidity problem and may fail through residual concentration, harmonic pressure tails, pressure–flux cancellation, coherent low-frequency resolved profiles, leakage, or backscatter.

The local pressure decompositions used below rely on standard Calderón–Zygmund and harmonic estimates; see

,

]

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Parabolic cylinders and pressure projection

For
z_{0}=(x_{0},t_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}
and
r>0
, set

B_{r}(x_{0})=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:|x-x_{0}|<r\},\qquad Q_{r}(z_{0})=B_{r}(x_{0})\times(t_{0}-r^{2},t_{0}).

When
z_{0}=(0,0)
, we write
B_{r}=B_{r}(0)
and
Q_{r}=Q_{r}(0,0)
.
For an integrable function
f
on
B_{r}(x_{0})
, define

(f)_{B_{r}(x_{0})}(t)=\frac{1}{|B_{r}|}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}f(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x.

We also use the pressure projection

\mathsf{P}_{r,z_{0}}f(x,t)=f(x,t)-(f)_{B_{r}(x_{0})}(t),\qquad(x,t)\in Q_{r}(z_{0}).

(2.1)

At the origin, we write simply
\mathsf{P}_{r}
.

The map
\mathsf{P}_{r,z_{0}}
is linear. In particular, if
p=P+q
, then

\mathsf{P}_{r,z_{0}}p=\mathsf{P}_{r,z_{0}}P+\mathsf{P}_{r,z_{0}}q.

(2.2)

This elementary identity is the reason the pressure part of the resolution lemma is gauge invariant.

2.2
Suitable weak solutions

Definition 2.1

(Suitable weak solution)
.

Let
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}
be a parabolic cylinder. A pair
(u,p)
is a suitable weak solution of (
1.1
) in
Q
if

u\in L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}(Q)\cap L_{t}^{2}H_{x}^{1}(Q),\qquad p\in L^{3/2}(Q),

(u,p)
solves (
1.1
) in the sense of distributions,
\nabla\cdot u=0
, and the local energy inequality holds: for every nonnegative
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(Q)
and almost every
t
,

\displaystyle\int|u(x,t)|^{2}\phi(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+2\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int|\nabla u|^{2}\phi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int|u|^{2}(\partial_{s}\phi+\Delta\phi)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int(|u|^{2}+2p)u\cdot\nabla\phi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s.

(2.3)

The resolution lemma below only needs
u\in L^{3}
and
p\in L^{3/2}
locally. The suitable weak solution framework is included because it is the natural setting in which
\Psi(r)
is used for local regularity.

2.3
Spatial coarse graining

Let
\rho\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{1})
be a nonnegative radial mollifier satisfying
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho\,\mathrm{d}x=1
. For
\ell>0
, set

\rho_{\ell}(x)=\ell^{-3}\rho(x/\ell),\qquad S_{\ell}f=\rho_{\ell}*_{x}f.

The convolution is in the spatial variables only. Whenever
Q_{r}(z_{0})
is under discussion, the fields are assumed to be defined on a spatially enlarged cylinder large enough for the convolution to be evaluated on
Q_{r}(z_{0})
.

For a velocity-pressure pair
(u,p)
, define

U^{\ell}=S_{\ell}u,\qquad P^{\ell}=S_{\ell}p,\qquad R^{\ell}=S_{\ell}(u\otimes u)-U^{\ell}\otimes U^{\ell}.

(2.4)

The resolved interscale work and the combined pressure–flux work distribution are

\Pi^{\ell}=-R^{\ell}:\nabla U^{\ell},\qquad G^{\ell}=\Pi^{\ell}+\nabla\cdot(P^{\ell}U^{\ell}).

(2.5)

The first main theorem concerns the CKN badness resolved by
(U^{\ell},P^{\ell})
; the work distribution
G^{\ell}
enters in the finite-chain depletion theorem.

2.4
Full, coarse, and residual CKN quantities

Fix a cylinder
Q_{r}(z_{0})\Subset Q
and a coarse length
\ell>0
. Define the full CKN badness

\Psi(z_{0},r)=r^{-2}\int_{Q_{r}(z_{0})}\left(|u|^{3}+|\mathsf{P}_{r,z_{0}}p|^{3/2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

(2.6)

Define the coarse badness

\Psi^{\ell}(z_{0},r)=r^{-2}\int_{Q_{r}(z_{0})}\left(|U^{\ell}|^{3}+|\mathsf{P}_{r,z_{0}}P^{\ell}|^{3/2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

(2.7)

Define the subfilter residual

\Omega^{\ell}(z_{0},r)=r^{-2}\int_{Q_{r}(z_{0})}\left(|u-U^{\ell}|^{3}+|\mathsf{P}_{r,z_{0}}(p-P^{\ell})|^{3/2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

(2.8)

At
z_{0}=(0,0)
we omit
z_{0}
from the notation.

All three quantities are invariant under the Navier–Stokes scaling if the ratio
\ell/r
is kept fixed. The full and residual pressure terms are invariant under the transformation
p\mapsto p+a(t)
, and the same is true for the coarse pressure term because
S_{\ell}
acts only in space.

3
Coarse-Grained CKN Resolution

3.1
The resolution lemma

Theorem 3.1

(Resolution lemma)
.

Let
(u,p)
be locally integrable with
u\in L^{3}_{\mathrm{loc}}
and
p\in L^{3/2}_{\mathrm{loc}}
, and let
(U^{\ell},P^{\ell})
be defined by spatial coarse graining as in (
2.4
). For every cylinder
Q_{r}(z_{0})
on which the quantities (
2.6
)–(
2.8
) are finite,

\Psi(z_{0},r)\leq 4\Psi^{\ell}(z_{0},r)+4\Omega^{\ell}(z_{0},r).

(3.1)

More precisely,

\displaystyle\Psi(z_{0},r)

\displaystyle\leq 4r^{-2}\int_{Q_{r}(z_{0})}\left(|U^{\ell}|^{3}+|u-U^{\ell}|^{3}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+2^{1/2}r^{-2}\int_{Q_{r}(z_{0})}\left(|\mathsf{P}_{r,z_{0}}P^{\ell}|^{3/2}+|\mathsf{P}_{r,z_{0}}(p-P^{\ell})|^{3/2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

(3.2)

Corollary 3.2

(Coarse visibility below residual threshold)
.

Let
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and
0<\eta<1/4
. If

\Psi(z_{0},r)\geq\varepsilon_{0}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Omega^{\ell}(z_{0},r)\leq\eta\varepsilon_{0},

then

\Psi^{\ell}(z_{0},r)\geq\left(\frac{1}{4}-\eta\right)\varepsilon_{0}.

(3.3)

Equivalently, if
\Psi^{\ell}(z_{0},r)<(1/4-\eta)\varepsilon_{0}
while
\Psi(z_{0},r)\geq\varepsilon_{0}
, then
\Omega^{\ell}(z_{0},r)>\eta\varepsilon_{0}
.

Remark 3.3

(Minimal hypotheses)
.

The estimate is independent of the equation. It remains valid for any
u\in L^{3}
and
p\in L^{3/2}
on the cylinder. No local pressure formula, no local energy inequality, and no smallness condition on
\ell/r
is used.

3.2
Finite-chain form

Many local regularity and detector arguments use a finite geometric chain of scales. Fix
0<\theta<1
and set

r_{k}=\theta^{k}r_{0},\qquad k=0,1,\dots,N.

For a fixed coarse length
\ell
, define the full, coarse, and residual bad-scale sets

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon_{0}}

\displaystyle=\{0\leq k\leq N-1:\ \Psi(z_{0},r_{k})\geq\varepsilon_{0}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{\ell}_{c_{0}\varepsilon_{0}}

\displaystyle=\{0\leq k\leq N-1:\ \Psi^{\ell}(z_{0},r_{k})\geq c_{0}\varepsilon_{0}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{\ell}_{\eta\varepsilon_{0}}

\displaystyle=\{0\leq k\leq N-1:\ \Omega^{\ell}(z_{0},r_{k})>\eta\varepsilon_{0}\}.

Corollary 3.4

(Finite-chain resolution)
.

Let
0<\eta<1/4
and set
c_{0}=1/4-\eta
. Then

\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon_{0}}\subset\mathcal{B}^{\ell}_{c_{0}\varepsilon_{0}}\cup\mathcal{R}^{\ell}_{\eta\varepsilon_{0}}.

(3.4)

Consequently,

\#\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon_{0}}\leq\#\mathcal{B}^{\ell}_{c_{0}\varepsilon_{0}}+\#\mathcal{R}^{\ell}_{\eta\varepsilon_{0}}.

(3.5)

More generally, for every nonnegative sequence of weights
w_{k}
,

\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}w_{k}\mathbf{1}_{\{\Psi(r_{k})\geq\varepsilon_{0}\}}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}w_{k}\mathbf{1}_{\{\Psi^{\ell}(r_{k})\geq c_{0}\varepsilon_{0}\}}+\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}w_{k}\mathbf{1}_{\{\Omega^{\ell}(r_{k})>\eta\varepsilon_{0}\}}.

(3.6)

This is the finite-chain bookkeeping form of the lemma. It does not count CKN-bad scales by itself; it says that every such count splits into a coarse-visible count and a residual count.

3.3
Conditional detector interface

The following proposition records how the resolution lemma would enter a detector theorem once an independent coarse observability estimate is available.

Let
A_{k}^{\infty}(G^{\ell})
be any scale-
r_{k}
detector seminorm built from the combined work distribution
G^{\ell}
, for instance

A_{k}^{\infty}(G^{\ell})=\sup_{\psi\in\mathfrak{X}_{k},\ \left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{X}_{k}}\leq 1}r_{k}^{-1}\left|\left\langle G^{\ell},\chi_{k}\psi_{k}^{\sharp}\right\rangle\right|,

(3.7)

where
\chi_{k}
is a cutoff on
Q_{r_{k}}
and
\psi_{k}^{\sharp}
is the parabolically rescaled reference test profile.

Proposition 3.5

(Resolution-to-detector template)
.

Fix
k
,
\varepsilon_{0}>0
, and
0<\eta<1/4
. Suppose that on a class
\mathcal{K}_{k}
of coarse-grained Navier–Stokes packages one has the coarse observability implication

\Psi^{\ell}(r_{k})\geq\left(\frac{1}{4}-\eta\right)\varepsilon_{0}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad A_{k}^{\infty}(G^{\ell})\geq c_{\mathrm{obs}}.

(3.8)

Then for every element of
\mathcal{K}_{k}
,

\Psi(r_{k})\geq\varepsilon_{0}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad A_{k}^{\infty}(G^{\ell})\geq c_{\mathrm{obs}}\quad\text{or}\quad\Omega^{\ell}(r_{k})>\eta\varepsilon_{0}.

(3.9)

Proof.

If
\Psi(r_{k})\geq\varepsilon_{0}
and
\Omega^{\ell}(r_{k})\leq\eta\varepsilon_{0}
, Corollary
3.2
gives
\Psi^{\ell}(r_{k})\geq(1/4-\eta)\varepsilon_{0}
. The assumed coarse observability estimate (
3.8
) then gives
A_{k}^{\infty}(G^{\ell})\geq c_{\mathrm{obs}}
.
∎

Remark 3.6

(Where the real difficulty remains)
.

The hypothesis (
3.8
) is not a consequence of the resolution lemma. It is a separate compactness and separation problem. It can fail through pressure–flux cancellation, harmonic pressure tails, coherent low-frequency resolved flow, or other silent profiles. The resolution lemma only removes the subfilter ambiguity.

3.4
Proof of the Resolution Lemma

We prove Theorem
3.1
at the origin. The proof at a general center
z_{0}
is identical.

Write

u=U^{\ell}+(u-U^{\ell}).

The elementary inequality

|a+b|^{3}\leq 4(|a|^{3}+|b|^{3})

(3.10)

gives

\int_{Q_{r}}|u|^{3}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\leq 4\int_{Q_{r}}|U^{\ell}|^{3}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+4\int_{Q_{r}}|u-U^{\ell}|^{3}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

(3.11)

For the pressure, use the projection
\mathsf{P}_{r}
from (
2.1
). Since

p=P^{\ell}+(p-P^{\ell}),

linearity gives

\mathsf{P}_{r}p=\mathsf{P}_{r}P^{\ell}+\mathsf{P}_{r}(p-P^{\ell}).

(3.12)

Using

|a+b|^{3/2}\leq 2^{1/2}\left(|a|^{3/2}+|b|^{3/2}\right),

(3.13)

we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{r}}|\mathsf{P}_{r}p|^{3/2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq 2^{1/2}\int_{Q_{r}}|\mathsf{P}_{r}P^{\ell}|^{3/2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+2^{1/2}\int_{Q_{r}}|\mathsf{P}_{r}(p-P^{\ell})|^{3/2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

(3.14)

Adding (
3.11
) and (
3.4
), and dividing by
r^{2}
, proves the sharper estimate (
3.1
). Since
2^{1/2}\leq 4
, the simpler bound (
3.1
) follows.

Proof of Corollary
3.2
.

By Theorem
3.1
,

\varepsilon_{0}\leq\Psi(r)\leq 4\Psi^{\ell}(r)+4\Omega^{\ell}(r)\leq 4\Psi^{\ell}(r)+4\eta\varepsilon_{0}.

Hence

4\Psi^{\ell}(r)\geq(1-4\eta)\varepsilon_{0},

which is (
3.3
).
∎

Proof of Corollary
3.4
.

For each
k\in\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon_{0}}
, either
\Omega^{\ell}(r_{k})>\eta\varepsilon_{0}
, in which case
k\in\mathcal{R}^{\ell}_{\eta\varepsilon_{0}}
, or
\Omega^{\ell}(r_{k})\leq\eta\varepsilon_{0}
, in which case Corollary
3.2
gives
k\in\mathcal{B}^{\ell}_{c_{0}\varepsilon_{0}}
. This proves (
3.4
). The counting and weighted estimates follow immediately.
∎

3.5
Quantifying the Subfilter Residual

The resolution lemma is useful only to the extent that
\Omega^{\ell}
can be controlled or recognized as an obstruction. This section gives elementary residual bounds in terms of local translation moduli.

3.5.1
Spatial increment moduli

Let
I_{r}(t_{0})=(t_{0}-r^{2},t_{0})
and let

Q_{r}^{\ell}(z_{0})=B_{r+\ell}(x_{0})\times I_{r}(t_{0}).

For
f\in L^{q}(Q_{r}^{\ell}(z_{0}))
, define the local spatial increment modulus

\mathfrak{m}_{q}(f;z_{0},r,\ell)=r^{-2}\sup_{|h|\leq\ell}\int_{I_{r}(t_{0})}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|f(x+h,t)-f(x,t)|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

(3.15)

Here
f
is interpreted on the enlarged spatial cylinder, so the shifted point
x+h
belongs to
B_{r+\ell}(x_{0})
.

Lemma 3.7

(Mollification error by increments)
.

Let
1\leq q<\infty
. If
S_{\ell}
is the spatial mollifier defined above, then

r^{-2}\int_{Q_{r}(z_{0})}|f-S_{\ell}f|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\mathfrak{m}_{q}(f;z_{0},r,\ell).

(3.16)

Moreover,

r^{-2}\int_{Q_{r}(z_{0})}|\mathsf{P}_{r,z_{0}}(f-S_{\ell}f)|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\leq C_{q}\mathfrak{m}_{q}(f;z_{0},r,\ell).

(3.17)

Proof.

For
(x,t)\in Q_{r}(z_{0})
,

f(x,t)-S_{\ell}f(x,t)=\int_{B_{\ell}}\rho_{\ell}(y)\bigl(f(x,t)-f(x-y,t)\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}y.

By Jensen’s inequality and
\int\rho_{\ell}=1
,

|f(x,t)-S_{\ell}f(x,t)|^{q}\leq\int_{B_{\ell}}\rho_{\ell}(y)|f(x,t)-f(x-y,t)|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}y.

Integrating over
Q_{r}(z_{0})
and taking the supremum over
|y|\leq\ell
proves (
3.16
). For the projected estimate, use the elementary bound

\left\lVert g-(g)_{B_{r}}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{q}(B_{r})}\leq 2\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{q}(B_{r})}

for a.e.
t
, then integrate in time.
∎

Proposition 3.8

(Residual bound by local increments)
.

For every
Q_{r}(z_{0})
on which the following quantities are finite,

\Omega^{\ell}(z_{0},r)\leq\mathfrak{m}_{3}(u;z_{0},r,\ell)+C\mathfrak{m}_{3/2}(p;z_{0},r,\ell).

(3.18)

The constant
C
is universal.

Proof.

Apply Lemma
3.7
with
q=3
to
u
and with
q=3/2
to
p
, using the definition (
2.8
).
∎

3.5.2
A scale-invariant Besov corollary

The preceding estimate can be converted into explicit powers of
\ell/r
if spatial increments are controlled.

Corollary 3.9

(Besov-type residual smallness)
.

Suppose that for some
\alpha,\beta\in(0,1]
and constants
M_{u},M_{p}
one has

\displaystyle\sup_{|h|\leq\ell}\int_{Q_{r}^{\ell}(z_{0})}|u(x+h,t)-u(x,t)|^{3}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq M_{u}^{3}\left(\frac{\ell}{r}\right)^{3\alpha}r^{2},

(3.19)

\displaystyle\sup_{|h|\leq\ell}\int_{Q_{r}^{\ell}(z_{0})}|p(x+h,t)-p(x,t)|^{3/2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq M_{p}^{3/2}\left(\frac{\ell}{r}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}\beta}r^{2}.

(3.20)

Then

\Omega^{\ell}(z_{0},r)\leq C\left[M_{u}^{3}\left(\frac{\ell}{r}\right)^{3\alpha}+M_{p}^{3/2}\left(\frac{\ell}{r}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}\beta}\right].

(3.21)

In particular, if
\ell/r
is sufficiently small in terms of
M_{u},M_{p},\alpha,\beta
and
\varepsilon_{0}
, then the residual alternative in Corollary
3.2
is excluded.

Remark 3.10

(No regularity is hidden in the main theorem)
.

Corollary
3.9
is an optional refinement. The resolution estimate itself does not assume these increment bounds. When no such information is available, a large value of
\Omega^{\ell}
is a genuine obstruction rather than a proof defect.

3.6
Pressure Residuals and Harmonic Tails

The pressure part of
\Omega^{\ell}
is often the most delicate term. This section records a standard way to separate local Calderon–Zygmund pressure from harmonic pressure. The goal is not to prove a new pressure theorem, but to identify the pieces that the residual channel contains.

3.6.1
Local pressure split

Let
B_{r}(x_{0})\Subset B_{R}(x_{0})
and let
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{R}(x_{0}))
satisfy
\chi\equiv 1
on a neighborhood of
B_{r}(x_{0})
. For a.e.
t
, define the localized pressure part

p_{\mathrm{loc}}(\cdot,t)=\mathcal{R}_{i}\mathcal{R}_{j}\bigl(\chi u_{i}u_{j}\bigr)(\cdot,t),

(3.22)

where
\mathcal{R}_{i}
are the Riesz transforms on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and summation over
i,j
is understood. Then

p_{h}=p-p_{\mathrm{loc}}

is harmonic in
B_{r}(x_{0})
for a.e.
t
, after the usual pressure normalization.

Thus

p-P^{\ell}=(p_{\mathrm{loc}}-S_{\ell}p_{\mathrm{loc}})+(p_{h}-S_{\ell}p_{h}),

(3.23)

where the identity is understood on cylinders where the convolution is defined. Applying the pressure projection
\mathsf{P}_{r,z_{0}}
and the triangle inequality gives

\displaystyle r^{-2}\int_{Q_{r}(z_{0})}|\mathsf{P}_{r}(p-P^{\ell})|^{3/2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq Cr^{-2}\int_{Q_{r}(z_{0})}|\mathsf{P}_{r}(p_{\mathrm{loc}}-S_{\ell}p_{\mathrm{loc}})|^{3/2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+Cr^{-2}\int_{Q_{r}(z_{0})}|\mathsf{P}_{r}(p_{h}-S_{\ell}p_{h})|^{3/2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

(3.24)

The first term is a localized singular-integral residual; the second is a harmonic tail residual.

3.6.2
Harmonic smoothing bound

The harmonic term can be estimated by interior regularity. The following simple form is sufficient for interpretation.

Lemma 3.11

(Harmonic residual estimate)
.

Let
h(\cdot,t)
be harmonic in
B_{R}(x_{0})
for a.e.
t\in I_{r}(t_{0})
, and assume
0<\ell\leq(R-r)/4
. Then

r^{-2}\int_{Q_{r}(z_{0})}|\mathsf{P}_{r}(h-S_{\ell}h)|^{3/2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\leq C\left(\frac{\ell}{R-r}\right)^{3/2}r^{-2}\int_{I_{r}(t_{0})}\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|h-(h)_{B_{R}}(t)|^{3/2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

(3.25)

The constant is universal up to the fixed mollifier.

Proof.

For
(x,t)\in Q_{r}(z_{0})
,

h(x,t)-S_{\ell}h(x,t)=\int\rho_{\ell}(y)(h(x,t)-h(x-y,t))\,\mathrm{d}y.

Since
h
is harmonic in
B_{R}(x_{0})
and
x,x-y\in B_{(R+r)/2}(x_{0})
, the interior gradient estimate gives

|h(x,t)-h(x-y,t)|\leq C\frac{|y|}{R-r}\left(\frac{1}{|B_{R}|}\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|h(\xi,t)-(h)_{B_{R}}(t)|^{3/2}\,d\xi\right)^{2/3}.

Jensen’s inequality and
|y|\leq\ell
on the support of
\rho_{\ell}
yield

|h-S_{\ell}h|^{3/2}\leq C\left(\frac{\ell}{R-r}\right)^{3/2}\frac{1}{|B_{R}|}\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|h-(h)_{B_{R}}(t)|^{3/2}\,d\xi.

Integrating over
B_{r}\times I_{r}
and applying the projection bound as in Lemma
3.7
proves the estimate after adjusting constants.
∎

Remark 3.12

(Harmonic pressure as a silent channel)
.

The harmonic residual is not a removable gauge artifact. A harmonic pressure component can contribute to local pressure work and may remain significant across several scales. In a detector-to-CKN argument, this term should be recorded explicitly, not hidden inside an unspecified error.

3.6.3
Local part and velocity increments

For the localized pressure
p_{\mathrm{loc}}
in (
3.22
), Calderon–Zygmund boundedness gives

\left\lVert p_{\mathrm{loc}}(\cdot,t)\right\rVert_{L^{3/2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C\left\lVert u(\cdot,t)\right\rVert_{L^{3}(B_{R})}^{2}.

(3.26)

A quantitative increment estimate for
p_{\mathrm{loc}}-S_{\ell}p_{\mathrm{loc}}
can be obtained either directly from increments of
p_{\mathrm{loc}}
or indirectly from commutator estimates for
u\otimes u
. A basic version is

r^{-2}\int_{Q_{r}}|\mathsf{P}_{r}(p_{\mathrm{loc}}-S_{\ell}p_{\mathrm{loc}})|^{3/2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\leq C\mathfrak{m}_{3/2}(p_{\mathrm{loc}};z_{0},r,\ell).

(3.27)

Under additional Besov-type control on
u
, this can be bounded in terms of velocity increments. Such estimates are standard in coarse-graining arguments, but their exact form depends on the chosen function spaces and cutoffs. The important point for the present paper is structural: the pressure residual splits into a local singular-integral residual and a harmonic residual.

3.7
Examples and Necessity of the Residual Alternative

The following examples are not asserted to be Navier–Stokes solutions. They show that the form of the resolution lemma is forced at the level of the scale-critical functionals.

Example 3.13

(Unresolved oscillation)
.

Let
u_{\delta}(x)=a\sin(x_{1}/\delta)e_{1}
on a fixed ball, with
\delta\ll\ell\ll r
, and take
p=0
. The full velocity contribution to
\Psi(r)
is of size
|a|^{3}
, while
S_{\ell}u_{\delta}
is small because the oscillation averages out. Thus
\Psi^{\ell}(r)
can be small although
\Psi(r)
is not. The missing contribution is exactly
r^{-2}\int|u-S_{\ell}u|^{3}
, hence belongs to
\Omega^{\ell}(r)
.

Example 3.14

(Pressure oscillation below the coarse scale)
.

Let
u=0
and
p_{\delta}(x)=b\sin(x_{1}/\delta)
, with
\delta\ll\ell\ll r
. Then the full pressure term
r^{-2}\int|\mathsf{P}_{r}p_{\delta}|^{3/2}
is nontrivial, but the coarse pressure
P^{\ell}=S_{\ell}p_{\delta}
is small. Again the loss is not a failure of the proof; it is the pressure component of
\Omega^{\ell}
.

Example 3.15

(Large smooth resolved fields)
.

A smooth low-frequency field may have large
\Psi^{\ell}(r)
on a moderately large cylinder even though it has no singular behavior. The resolution lemma deliberately does not distinguish singular concentration from large but smooth resolved size. That distinction belongs to a later observability or decay argument, not to the resolution step.

4
Pressure–Flux Work Depletion

4.1
Setup and main theorem

4.1.1
Local solution and common spatial coarse graining

Let

\mathcal{Q}=B_{2r_{0}}(x_{0})\times(T_{0},T_{1})

with
r_{0}>0
, and let
(u,p)
be a suitable weak solution of (
1.1
) in
\mathcal{Q}
. Thus, locally,

u\in L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}\cap L^{2}_{t}H^{1}_{x},\qquad p\in L^{3/2}.

(4.1)

We use the standard weakly continuous representative of
u
. On bounded subcylinders, interpolation gives
u\in L^{3}
.

Choose a nonnegative radial function
\rho\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{1})
with
\int\rho=1
, and write

\rho_{\ell}(x)=\ell^{-3}\rho(x/\ell),\qquad S_{\ell}f=\rho_{\ell}*_{x}f.

Fix one physical length
\ell>0
, small enough that every spatial convolution used below remains inside
B_{2r_{0}}(x_{0})
. Define

U:=S_{\ell}u,\qquad P:=S_{\ell}p,\qquad R:=S_{\ell}(u\otimes u)-U\otimes U,\qquad\Pi:=-R:\nabla U.

(4.2)

The superscript
\ell
will occasionally be restored when the dependence on the filter length matters.

4.1.2
A finite chain of adjacent slabs

Fix
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
0<\theta<1
, and set

r_{k}=\theta^{k}r_{0},\qquad k=0,\ldots,N.

(4.3)

Choose adjacent times

\tau_{k+1}-\tau_{k}=r_{k}^{2},\qquad k=0,\ldots,N-1,

(4.4)

so that every closed slab

\overline{Q_{k}}:=\overline{B_{r_{k}}(x_{0})}\times[\tau_{k},\tau_{k+1}]

is compactly contained in the region on which the coarse package is defined.

Let
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{1})
be radial, nonincreasing in
|x|
, and satisfy

0\leq\chi\leq 1,\qquad\chi\equiv 1\text{ on }B_{\vartheta}

(4.5)

for some
0<\vartheta<1
. Set

\chi_{k}(x)=\chi\left(\frac{x-x_{0}}{r_{k}}\right).

(4.6)

Then

\chi_{k+1}\leq\chi_{k}\qquad\text{pointwise.}

(4.7)

4.1.3
Local work, dissipation, and leakage

Let
I=(t_{-},t_{+})
, let
r>0
, and let

\phi\in C^{\infty}([t_{-},t_{+}];C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{r}(x_{0})))

be nonnegative. Define the localized kinetic energy

K_{\phi}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{2}|U(x,t)|^{2}\phi(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x.

(4.8)

Define the normalized combined work, resolved dissipation, and localization functional by

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{I,r}[\phi]

\displaystyle:=r^{-1}\int_{I}\int\left(\phi\Pi-PU\cdot\nabla\phi\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t,

(4.9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{I,r}[\phi]

\displaystyle:=r^{-1}\int_{I}\int\phi|\nabla U|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t,

(4.10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{I,r}[\phi]

\displaystyle:=r^{-1}\int_{I}\int\left[\frac{1}{2}|U|^{2}(\partial_{t}\phi+\Delta\phi)+\frac{1}{2}|U|^{2}U\cdot\nabla\phi+(RU)\cdot\nabla\phi\right]\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

(4.11)

The distribution

G:=\Pi+\nabla\cdot(PU)

(4.12)

is understood through

\left\langle G,\phi\right\rangle=\int\phi\Pi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t-\int PU\cdot\nabla\phi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

(4.13)

Thus
\mathcal{W}_{I,r}[\phi]=r^{-1}\left\langle G,\phi\right\rangle
.

For the slab
Q_{k}
, we abbreviate

\mathcal{W}_{k}[\phi]=\mathcal{W}_{I_{k},r_{k}}[\phi],\qquad\mathcal{D}_{k}[\phi]=\mathcal{D}_{I_{k},r_{k}}[\phi],\qquad\mathcal{L}_{k}[\phi]=\mathcal{L}_{I_{k},r_{k}}[\phi].

The endpoint quantities use only the base spatial cutoff:

\mathcal{E}_{k}^{-}:=r_{k}^{-1}K_{\chi_{k}}(\tau_{k}),\qquad\mathcal{E}_{k}^{+}:=r_{k}^{-1}K_{\chi_{k}}(\tau_{k+1}).

(4.14)

4.1.4
Finite-dimensional active work coefficients

For each
k
, fix an integer
m_{k}\geq 1
and profiles

\psi_{k,1},\ldots,\psi_{k,m_{k}}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{1}\times(0,1)).

(4.15)

Their physical pullbacks are

\psi_{k,j}^{\sharp}(x,t)=\psi_{k,j}\left(\frac{x-x_{0}}{r_{k}},\frac{t-\tau_{k}}{r_{k}^{2}}\right).

(4.16)

Define the coefficient vector
g_{k}=(g_{k,1},\ldots,g_{k,m_{k}})
by

g_{k,j}:=r_{k}^{-1}\left\langle G,\chi_{k}\psi_{k,j}^{\sharp}\right\rangle=r_{k}^{-1}\int_{Q_{k}}\left[\chi_{k}\psi_{k,j}^{\sharp}\Pi-PU\cdot\nabla(\chi_{k}\psi_{k,j}^{\sharp})\right]\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

(4.17)

The active work coefficient norm is

\mathfrak{A}_{k}(G):=|g_{k}|_{\ell^{2}}.

(4.18)

This is a finite family of continuous distributional pairings. It is deliberately defined without assuming
G\in L^{2}
. If
G
has additional square integrability and the profiles are chosen as a weighted orthonormal family, (
4.18
) can be identified with a finite-dimensional projection norm.

Choose an invertible matrix

B_{k}=(b^{k}_{\alpha j})_{\alpha,j=1}^{m_{k}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{k}\times m_{k}}

(4.19)

and set

\eta_{k,\alpha}=\sum_{j=1}^{m_{k}}b^{k}_{\alpha j}\psi_{k,j}.

(4.20)

Choose
\varepsilon_{k}>0
so that

\varepsilon_{k}\max_{1\leq\alpha\leq m_{k}}\left\lVert\eta_{k,\alpha}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(B_{1}\times(0,1))}\leq\frac{1}{2}.

(4.21)

Define
m_{k}+1
nonnegative weights on
Q_{k}
by

\displaystyle\phi_{k,0}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\chi_{k}(x),

(4.22)

\displaystyle\phi_{k,\alpha}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\chi_{k}(x)\left[1+\varepsilon_{k}\eta_{k,\alpha}\left(\frac{x-x_{0}}{r_{k}},\frac{t-\tau_{k}}{r_{k}^{2}}\right)\right],\quad 1\leq\alpha\leq m_{k}.

(4.23)

Because the profiles are compactly supported in the open reference time interval,

\phi_{k,\alpha}(\cdot,\tau_{k})=\phi_{k,\alpha}(\cdot,\tau_{k+1})=\chi_{k}

(4.24)

for every
\alpha=0,\ldots,m_{k}
.

Set

c_{k}:=\frac{\varepsilon_{k}}{2\sqrt{m_{k}}}\,\sigma_{\min}(B_{k}),

(4.25)

where
\sigma_{\min}(B_{k})>0
is the smallest singular value.

4.1.5
Pressure–flux cancellation ledger

For any admissible
\phi
, define the two signed channels

\mathcal{F}_{I,r}[\phi]:=r^{-1}\int_{I}\int\phi\Pi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t,\qquad\mathcal{P}_{I,r}[\phi]:=-r^{-1}\int_{I}\int PU\cdot\nabla\phi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

(4.26)

Then
\mathcal{W}_{I,r}[\phi]=\mathcal{F}_{I,r}[\phi]+\mathcal{P}_{I,r}[\phi]
. The nonnegative cancellation quantity is

\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{PF}}_{I,r}[\phi]:=\left\lvert\mathcal{F}_{I,r}[\phi]\right\rvert+\left\lvert\mathcal{P}_{I,r}[\phi]\right\rvert-\left\lvert\mathcal{W}_{I,r}[\phi]\right\rvert\geq 0.

(4.27)

Thus individual pressure and flux activity that disappears from the combined observable is recorded by
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{PF}}
, not absorbed into localization error.

4.1.6
Main theorem

Theorem 4.1

(Combined pressure–flux work depletion on a fixed finite chain)
.

Assume the setup above. For every
k=0,\ldots,N-1
, there is an index

\beta_{k}\in\{0,1,\ldots,m_{k}\}

such that, with

\widehat{\phi}_{k}:=\phi_{k,\beta_{k}},\qquad\mathcal{W}_{k}:=\mathcal{W}_{k}[\widehat{\phi}_{k}],\qquad\mathcal{D}_{k}:=\mathcal{D}_{k}[\widehat{\phi}_{k}],\qquad\mathcal{L}_{k}:=\mathcal{L}_{k}[\widehat{\phi}_{k}],

(4.28)

one has

|\mathcal{W}_{k}|\geq c_{k}\mathfrak{A}_{k}(G).

(4.29)

Define

\mathcal{W}_{k}^{+}=\max\{\mathcal{W}_{k},0\},\qquad\mathcal{W}_{k}^{-}=\max\{-\mathcal{W}_{k},0\},

(4.30)

and weights

w_{k}=\frac{r_{k}}{r_{0}}.

(4.31)

Then

\boxed{\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}w_{k}\bigl(\mathcal{W}_{k}^{+}+\mathcal{D}_{k}\bigr)\leq\mathcal{E}_{0}^{-}+\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}w_{k}|\mathcal{L}_{k}|+\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}w_{k}\mathcal{W}_{k}^{-}.}

(4.32)

In particular, if

\mathcal{I}_{+}=\{k:\mathcal{W}_{k}\geq 0\},\qquad\mathcal{I}_{-}=\{k:\mathcal{W}_{k}<0\},

(4.33)

then

\boxed{\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{+}}w_{k}c_{k}\mathfrak{A}_{k}(G)+\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}w_{k}\mathcal{D}_{k}\leq\mathcal{E}_{0}^{-}+\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}w_{k}|\mathcal{L}_{k}|+\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{-}}w_{k}\mathcal{W}_{k}^{-}.}

(4.34)

For every
k\in\mathcal{I}_{-}
, the explicit backscatter quantity satisfies

\mathcal{B}_{k}^{\mathrm{back}}:=\mathcal{W}_{k}^{-}\geq c_{k}\mathfrak{A}_{k}(G).

(4.35)

All quantities in (
4.32
)–(
4.35
) are dimensionless under Navier–Stokes scaling.

Remark 4.2

(What the theorem does and does not say)
.

The theorem is unconditional at fixed
N
, fixed
\ell>0
, and fixed active profiles. It does not assert that
\mathcal{I}_{-}
is small, that
c_{k}
is uniform in a moving-window limit, or that
\sum_{k}w_{k}|\mathcal{L}_{k}|
is summable as
N\to\infty
. Such statements require additional PDE information not contained in the local energy identity.

4.2
The Navier–Stokes-generated coarse package

Lemma 4.3

(Common coarse package)
.

On every interior subcylinder whose spatial
\ell
-neighborhood is contained in
B_{2r_{0}}(x_{0})
, the fields in (
4.2
) satisfy

\partial_{t}U-\Delta U+\nabla\cdot(U\otimes U)+\nabla P=-\nabla\cdot R,\qquad\nabla\cdot U=0

(4.36)

in distributions. Moreover,
R=R^{T}\geq 0
pointwise almost everywhere, and locally

\displaystyle\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{L^{3}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{3}},

(4.37)

\displaystyle\left\lVert P\right\rVert_{L^{3/2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert p\right\rVert_{L^{3/2}},

(4.38)

\displaystyle\left\lVert R\right\rVert_{L^{3/2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{2},

(4.39)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla U\right\rVert_{L^{3}}

\displaystyle\leq C\ell^{-1}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{3}}.

(4.40)

Consequently,

\Pi\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}},\qquad PU\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}},\qquad RU\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}.

(4.41)

Finally,
U
has a representative in
C_{t}L^{2}_{x}
on compact interior spatial sets. In (
4.37
)–(
4.40
), the left-hand norms may be taken on any fixed interior cylinder and the right-hand norms on its spatial
\ell
-neighborhood.

Proof.

Spatial convolution of (
1.1
) gives

\partial_{t}U-\Delta U+\nabla\cdot S_{\ell}(u\otimes u)+\nabla P=0,\qquad\nabla\cdot U=0.

Since

S_{\ell}(u\otimes u)=U\otimes U+R,

we obtain (
4.36
).

Symmetry of
R
is immediate. For
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
,

\displaystyle\xi\cdot R(x,t)\xi

\displaystyle=S_{\ell}\bigl((\xi\cdot u)^{2}\bigr)(x,t)-\bigl(S_{\ell}(\xi\cdot u)(x,t)\bigr)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq 0

by Jensen’s inequality, because
\rho_{\ell}
is nonnegative and has unit mass. Thus
R
is a covariance tensor and is positive semidefinite.

The estimates (
4.37
) and (
4.38
) follow from Young’s inequality. Pointwise,

|R|\leq S_{\ell}(|u|^{2})+|U|^{2},

so another application of Young’s inequality gives (
4.39
). Since

\nabla U=(\nabla\rho_{\ell})*u,\qquad\left\lVert\nabla\rho_{\ell}\right\rVert_{L^{1}}=\ell^{-1}\left\lVert\nabla\rho\right\rVert_{L^{1}},

we obtain (
4.40
). Hölder’s inequality then yields (
4.41
).

A local energy-class weak solution has a weakly continuous
L^{2}
-representative. For a compact set
K
whose
\ell
-neighborhood is interior, the map

T_{\ell}:L^{2}\longrightarrow L^{2}(K),\qquad T_{\ell}f=(\rho_{\ell}*f)|_{K},

is compact, because it maps bounded sets into bounded subsets of every spatial Sobolev space on
K
. A compact linear map sends weakly convergent sequences to strongly convergent sequences. Applying this observation to
u(t_{n})\rightharpoonup u(t)
shows that
U(t_{n})\to U(t)
strongly in
L^{2}(K)
. Hence
U\in C_{t}L^{2}_{x}(K)
.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

The use of one common
\ell
is structural. If a different coarse field is introduced at every scale, endpoint energies generally belong to different resolved systems and there is no automatic common telescoping budget.

4.3
Local combined work identity and pressure gauge

Lemma 4.5

(Local coarse energy identity with endpoint traces)
.

Let
I=(t_{-},t_{+})
and let
\phi\in C^{\infty}([t_{-},t_{+}];C_{c}^{\infty})
be nonnegative. Then

\boxed{\mathcal{W}_{I,r}[\phi]+\mathcal{D}_{I,r}[\phi]=r^{-1}\bigl(K_{\phi}(t_{-})-K_{\phi}(t_{+})\bigr)+\mathcal{L}_{I,r}[\phi].}

(4.42)

Equivalently, the coarse fields satisfy the distributional local balance

\partial_{t}\frac{|U|^{2}}{2}-\Delta\frac{|U|^{2}}{2}+|\nabla U|^{2}+\nabla\cdot\left[\left(\frac{|U|^{2}}{2}+P\right)U+RU\right]=-\Pi.

(4.43)

Proof.

The formal computation follows by taking the scalar product of (
4.36
) with
U
. The only point is justification in time. We give the standard Steklov-average argument.

For
h>0
, write

[f]_{h}(t)=\frac{1}{h}\int_{t}^{t+h}f(s)\,\mathrm{d}s

on a slightly shorter interval. Apply this averaging to (
4.36
) and test the averaged equation with
[U]_{h}\phi
. Every term is integrable by
Lemma

4.3
. Integrating by parts in space and time gives the classical averaged identity. As
h\downarrow 0
,

[U]_{h}\to U\quad\text{in }L^{3}_{\mathrm{loc}},\qquad\nabla[U]_{h}\to\nabla U\quad\text{in }L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}},

while

[U\otimes U]_{h}\to U\otimes U,\quad[R]_{h}\to R,\quad[P]_{h}\to P

in their natural local Lebesgue spaces. The endpoint terms converge because
U\in C_{t}L^{2}_{x}
after spatial coarse graining. Passing to the limit yields (
4.43
).

Multiplying (
4.43
) by
\phi
, integrating over
I\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and integrating by parts gives

\displaystyle K_{\phi}(t_{+})-K_{\phi}(t_{-})+\int_{I}\int\phi|\nabla U|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{I}\int\phi\Pi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{I}\int\frac{1}{2}|U|^{2}(\partial_{t}\phi+\Delta\phi)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{I}\int\left[\left(\frac{1}{2}|U|^{2}+P\right)U+RU\right]\cdot\nabla\phi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

Move the pressure-transport term to the left and divide by
r
. This is exactly (
4.42
).
∎

Remark 4.6

(Why the temporal term is necessary)
.

If
\phi
depends on time, the term

r^{-1}\int\frac{1}{2}|U|^{2}\partial_{t}\phi

is part of the exact PDE identity. Space-time work extraction without this term is incompatible with the localized energy equation. The endpoint condition (
4.24
) ensures that this extra localization cost does not alter the endpoint profiles used in the finite-chain sum.

Lemma 4.7

(Pressure-gauge invariance)
.

Let
a\in L^{3/2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(I)
depend only on time. Replacing
P
by
P+a(t)
leaves
\mathcal{W}_{I,r}[\phi]
unchanged.

Proof.

The change in
\mathcal{W}_{I,r}[\phi]
is

-r^{-1}\int_{I}a(t)\left(\int U(x,t)\cdot\nabla\phi(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\,\mathrm{d}t.

For almost every
t
,

\int U\cdot\nabla\phi\,\mathrm{d}x=-\int\phi\nabla\cdot U\,\mathrm{d}x=0.

Thus the change vanishes. Notice that a spatially harmonic pressure is not, in general, a gauge: only functions of time disappear in this way.
∎

4.4
Explicit localization leakage

For a nonnegative weight on
Q_{r}=B_{r}(x_{0})\times I
, define the scale-adapted seminorm

[\phi]_{\mathfrak{C}(r)}:=\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+r\left\lVert\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+r^{2}\left(\left\lVert\partial_{t}\phi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\left\lVert\nabla^{2}\phi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\right).

(4.44)

Let

\mathscr{A}_{\phi}:=\supp(\partial_{t}\phi)\cup\supp(\nabla\phi)\cup\supp(\nabla^{2}\phi).

(4.45)

Lemma 4.8

(Scale-invariant leakage bound)
.

If
[\phi]_{\mathfrak{C}(r)}\leq M_{\phi}
, then

\boxed{|\mathcal{L}_{I,r}[\phi]|\leq CM_{\phi}\left[r^{-3}\int_{\mathscr{A}_{\phi}}|U|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+r^{-2}\int_{\mathscr{A}_{\phi}}|U|^{3}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+r^{-2}\int_{\mathscr{A}_{\phi}}|R|\,|U|\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\right].}

(4.46)

For the weights
\phi_{k,\alpha}
in (
4.22
)–(
4.23
), the constants
M_{\phi_{k,\alpha}}
are bounded by an explicit number depending only on
\chi
,
\varepsilon_{k}
,
B_{k}
, and finitely many
C^{2}
-norms of the reference profiles.

Proof.

From (
4.11
) and (
4.44
),

|\partial_{t}\phi+\Delta\phi|\leq CM_{\phi}r^{-2},\qquad|\nabla\phi|\leq M_{\phi}r^{-1}.

Substitution into (
4.11
) gives (
4.46
). For
\phi_{k,\alpha}
, the chain rule under the parabolic map (
4.16
) yields

|\partial_{t}\phi_{k,\alpha}|+|\nabla^{2}\phi_{k,\alpha}|\leq C_{k}r_{k}^{-2},\qquad|\nabla\phi_{k,\alpha}|\leq C_{k}r_{k}^{-1},

with the stated explicit dependence.
∎

Remark 4.9

(Purely spatial cutoffs)
.

For
\phi(x,t)=\chi(x)
, the temporal contribution vanishes and
\mathscr{A}_{\phi}
is the spatial transition annulus. For active space-time weights, derivatives of the interior profiles create an additional, explicitly displayed interior localization region.

Remark 4.10

(Scaling)
.

Under

U_{\lambda}(x,t)=\lambda U(\lambda x,\lambda^{2}t),\quad P_{\lambda}(x,t)=\lambda^{2}P(\lambda x,\lambda^{2}t),\quad R_{\lambda}(x,t)=\lambda^{2}R(\lambda x,\lambda^{2}t),

the three terms on the right of (
4.46
) are invariant. Thus the normalization in (
4.9
)–(
4.11
) is dimensionally consistent.

4.5
Constructive active-work extraction

Lemma 4.11

(Nonnegative finite-dimensional work extraction)
.

For every slab
Q_{k}
, there exists
\beta_{k}\in\{0,1,\ldots,m_{k}\}
such that

\left\lvert\mathcal{W}_{k}[\phi_{k,\beta_{k}}]\right\rvert\geq c_{k}\mathfrak{A}_{k}(G),

(4.47)

where
c_{k}

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{k}[\phi_{k,\beta_{k}}]

\displaystyle\geq c_{k}\mathfrak{A}_{k}(G),

(forward)

\displaystyle-\mathcal{W}_{k}[\phi_{k,\beta_{k}}]

\displaystyle\geq c_{k}\mathfrak{A}_{k}(G).

(backscatter)

Proof.

Linearity of

In vector form,

\left(\mathcal{W}_{k}[\phi_{k,\alpha}]-\mathcal{W}_{k}[\phi_{k,0}]\right)_{\alpha=1}^{m_{k}}=\varepsilon_{k}B_{k}g_{k}.

\max_{1\leq\alpha\leq m_{k}}\left\lvert\mathcal{W}_{k}[\phi_{k,\alpha}]-\mathcal{W}_{k}[\phi_{k,0}]\right\rvert\geq\frac{\varepsilon_{k}}{\sqrt{m_{k}}}\left\lVert B_{k}g_{k}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}}\geq\frac{\varepsilon_{k}\sigma_{\min}(B_{k})}{\sqrt{m_{k}}}|g_{k}|_{\ell^{2}}.

Lemma 4.13

(Perturbative stability of the active window)
.

Let

An exact affine parabolic pullback has

Proof.

Weighted telescoping on the finite chain

Lemma 4.15

(Correct finite-chain telescoping)

Proof.

Since
w_{k}r_{k}^{-1}=r_{0}^{-1}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}w_{k}(\mathcal{E}_{k}^{-}-\mathcal{E}_{k}^{+})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r_{0}}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\left[K_{\chi_{k}}(\tau_{k})-K_{\chi_{k}}(\tau_{k+1})\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r_{0}}\Bigg[K_{\chi_{0}}(\tau_{0})-K_{\chi_{N-1}}(\tau_{N})

\displaystyle\hskip 91.04872pt+\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\bigl(K_{\chi_{k}}(\tau_{k})-K_{\chi_{k-1}}(\tau_{k})\bigr)\Bigg].

By (
4.7
), every term in the final sum is nonpositive. The terminal kinetic energy is nonnegative. This proves (
4.52
).

By

\mathcal{W}_{k}+\mathcal{D}_{k}=\mathcal{E}_{k}^{-}-\mathcal{E}_{k}^{+}+\mathcal{L}_{k}.

Multiply by
w_{k}
, sum over every slab, and use (

\sum_{k}w_{k}(\mathcal{W}_{k}+\mathcal{D}_{k})\leq\mathcal{E}_{0}^{-}+\sum_{k}w_{k}\mathcal{L}_{k}.

Since

\mathcal{L}_{k}\leq|\mathcal{L}_{k}|
give (

).

4.7
Local pressure decomposition and harmonic tails

The first theorem is stated for the full pressure work, which is gauge invariant by
Lemma

4.7
. We now record how an active/harmonic split can be introduced without discarding physical harmonic pressure.

Lemma 4.18

(Active/harmonic pressure split and harmonic-polynomial tail)
.

Fix a time interval
I
and concentric balls

B_{r}(x_{0})\subset B_{R}(x_{0}),\qquad 0<r\leq R/4,

whose
\ell
-neighborhoods remain in the solution domain. Let
\zeta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))
satisfy
\zeta\equiv 1
on
B_{R}(x_{0})
, and define for almost every
t

P^{a}(\cdot,t):=\mathcal{R}_{i}\mathcal{R}_{j}\left[\zeta\bigl(U_{i}U_{j}+R_{ij}\bigr)\right](\cdot,t),\qquad P^{h}:=P-P^{a}.

(4.55)

Then
P^{h}(\cdot,t)
is harmonic in
B_{R}(x_{0})
. For every integer
m\geq 0
, there is a harmonic polynomial
H_{m}(\cdot,t)
of degree at most
m
such that

\left\lVert P^{h}(\cdot,t)-H_{m}(\cdot,t)\right\rVert_{L^{3/2}(B_{r})}\leq C_{m}\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{m+3}\left\lVert P^{h}(\cdot,t)-c(t)\right\rVert_{L^{3/2}(B_{R})}

(4.56)

for every scalar function
c(t)
.

If
\phi
is supported in
B_{r}(x_{0})\times I
and
\left\lVert\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq M_{\nabla}r^{-1}
, then the harmonic-tail contribution to pressure work satisfies

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{m}^{h}[\phi]

\displaystyle:=r^{-1}\int_{I}\int_{B_{r}}|P^{h}-H_{m}|\,|U|\,|\nabla\phi|\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

(4.57)

\displaystyle\leq C_{m}M_{\nabla}\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{m+3}r^{-2}\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{L^{3}(I\times B_{r})}\left\lVert P^{h}-c(t)\right\rVert_{L^{3/2}(I\times B_{R})}.

(4.58)

Proof.

Taking the divergence of (
4.36
) gives

-\Delta P=\partial_{i}\partial_{j}(U_{i}U_{j}+R_{ij})

(4.59)

in the interior. By the definition of the Riesz transforms,

-\Delta P^{a}=\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\left[\zeta(U_{i}U_{j}+R_{ij})\right].

Since
\zeta=1
on
B_{R}
, (
4.59
) implies
\Delta P^{h}=0
there.

For a harmonic function
h
in
B_{R}
, the interior
L^{p}
-derivative estimate with
p=3/2
gives

\left\lVert\nabla^{m+1}h\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(B_{R/2})}\leq C_{m}R^{-(m+1)-2}\left\lVert h-c\right\rVert_{L^{3/2}(B_{R})}.

(4.60)

Let
H_{m}
be the Taylor polynomial of
h
at
x_{0}
through degree
m
. Because derivatives of a harmonic function are harmonic and the Taylor polynomial of a harmonic function is harmonic term by term,
H_{m}
is harmonic. Taylor’s theorem and (
4.60
) yield, for

|h(x)-H_{m}(x)|\leq C_{m}r^{m+1}R^{-m-3}\left\lVert h-c\right\rVert_{L^{3/2}(B_{R})}.

Multiplication by
|B_{r}|^{2/3}\simeq r^{2}
gives (
4.56
). Apply this at almost every time and then use Hölder’s inequality in space-time together with

to obtain (
4.58

∎

5
A Conditional Detector-to-CKN Interface

We now record the precise way in which CKN resolution and work depletion fit together. The point is not to assert an unconditional detector theorem, but to state the exact implication that would follow once a separate coarse observability estimate is available.

Let
\mathfrak{A}_{k}(G^{\ell})
be a finite-dimensional active coefficient norm of the form used in
Section

4
. A coarse detector observability statement at scale
r_{k}
would have the form

\Psi^{\ell}(z_{0},r_{k})\geq c_{0}\varepsilon_{0}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\mathfrak{A}_{k}(G^{\ell})\geq a_{0},

(5.1)

for constants
c_{0},a_{0}>0
on a specified compact or admissible class of coarse-grained packages. This implication is not a consequence of the resolution lemma or of the local energy identity. It is a separate compactness-separation problem for the signed distribution
G^{\ell}
.

Theorem 5.1

(Resolution plus work-depletion template)
.

Fix a finite chain and a common coarse length
\ell>0
as in
Section

4
. Let
\varepsilon_{0}>0
, let
0<\eta<1/4
, and set
c_{0}=1/4-\eta
. Suppose that for every scale
r_{k}
under consideration the coarse observability implication (
5.1
) holds with the active norm used in
Theorem

4.1
.

If

\Psi(z_{0},r_{k})\geq\varepsilon_{0}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Omega^{\ell}(z_{0},r_{k})\leq\eta\varepsilon_{0},

then the selected work weight
\widehat{\phi}_{k}
from
Theorem

4.1
satisfies

|\mathcal{W}_{k}[\widehat{\phi}_{k}]|\geq c_{k}a_{0}.

(5.2)

Equivalently, every residual-small full CKN-bad scale lies in one of two branches:

\mathcal{W}_{k}^{+}\geq c_{k}a_{0}\qquad\text{or}\qquad\mathcal{W}_{k}^{-}\geq c_{k}a_{0}.

Moreover the selected work quantities obey the finite-chain inequality

\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}w_{k}(\mathcal{W}_{k}^{+}+\mathcal{D}_{k})\leq\mathcal{E}_{0}^{-}+\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}w_{k}|\mathcal{L}_{k}|+\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}w_{k}\mathcal{W}_{k}^{-}.

(5.3)

Proof.

The resolution lemma,
Corollary

3.2
, gives

\Psi^{\ell}(z_{0},r_{k})\geq\left(\frac{1}{4}-\eta\right)\varepsilon_{0}=c_{0}\varepsilon_{0}

whenever
\Psi(z_{0},r_{k})\geq\varepsilon_{0}
and
\Omega^{\ell}(z_{0},r_{k})\leq\eta\varepsilon_{0}
. The assumed coarse observability implication (
5.1
) then gives
\mathfrak{A}_{k}(G^{\ell})\geq a_{0}
. The active extraction part of
Theorem

4.1
gives

|\mathcal{W}_{k}[\widehat{\phi}_{k}]|\geq c_{k}\mathfrak{A}_{k}(G^{\ell})\geq c_{k}a_{0},

which is (
5.2
). The branch alternative is just the decomposition into positive and negative parts. Finally, (
5.3
) is exactly the weighted telescoping estimate in
Theorem

4.1
.
∎

Remark 5.2

(What remains open)
.

The assumption (
5.1
) is the hard missing bridge. Large
\Psi^{\ell}
may still be invisible to a finite signed detector because of pressure–flux cancellation, poor alignment with the chosen active profiles, harmonic pressure tails, or coherent resolved fields with little localized work. The theorem above is therefore best read as a clean interface statement: once coarse observability is supplied, the rest of the finite-chain ledger is already available from the exact resolved energy identity.

6.1
A spatially constant velocity with harmonic pressure

Example 6.1

(Harmonic pressure performs real local work)
.

Let
a\in C^{1}(I;\mathbb{R}^{3})
, and set

U(x,t)=a(t),\qquad P(x,t)=-a^{\prime}(t)\cdot x,\qquad R=0.

Then

\partial_{t}U+\nabla P=0,\qquad\nabla\cdot U=0,

so this is a smooth local Navier–Stokes solution. The pressure is spatially harmonic and
\Pi=0
, but for a spatial cutoff
\chi

\int_{I}\int\chi G\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{I}\int\chi\,\nabla\cdot(PU)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=-\int_{I}a(t)\cdot a^{\prime}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\int\chi\,\mathrm{d}x.

This is exactly the localized kinetic-energy change. Thus harmonic pressure cannot be removed as a gauge.

6.2
Forward transfer and backscatter are both algebraically possible

Even when

, the sign of
\Pi=-R:\nabla U
is not fixed. For example, with

a divergence-free linear gradient

gives

, while
\nabla U=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,0)
gives
\Pi=-1
. These are algebraic sign tests for the coarse equation; they are not assertions that an arbitrary prescribed pair

is generated by a Navier–Stokes solution. Their role is to show why the theorem must contain a backscatter alternative.

6.3
Pressure–flux cancellation

If the two channels in (

\int|\Pi|+\int|P|^{3/2}\lesssim\text{endpoint kinetic-energy drop}

7
Conclusion

We have proved a finite-scale coarse-grained obstruction calculus for the local Navier–Stokes regularity problem. The first component is the unconditional CKN resolution estimate

\Psi(r)\leq 4\Psi^{\ell}(r)+4\Omega^{\ell}(r),

which separates full local badness into a resolved velocity–pressure contribution and an explicit subfilter residual. The second component is an exact finite-chain depletion theorem for the combined pressure–flux work distribution

G^{\ell}=\Pi^{\ell}+\nabla\cdot(P^{\ell}U^{\ell}).

It shows that finite-dimensional detected resolved work obeys a localized energy payment law with explicit leakage and backscatter.
