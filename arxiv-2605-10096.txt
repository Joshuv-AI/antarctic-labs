Randomly Shifted Steinhaus Longimeters and Buffon Discrepancy
Samuel Korsky

Abstract
Let Ω ⊂ ℝ 2 \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2} be a bounded convex domain. Steinerberger (2026) introduced the Buffon discrepancy problem: given length L L , construct a one-dimensional set S ⊂ Ω S\subset\Omega such that the number of intersections of S S with a line ℓ \ell approximates the Crofton-normalized chord length 2 ​ L π ​ | Ω | ⋅ ℋ 1 ​ ( ℓ ∩ Ω ) . \frac{2L}{\pi|\Omega|}\cdot\mathcal{H}^{1}(\ell\cap\Omega). Steinerberger proved a universal upper bound of order L 1 / 3 L^{1/3} using a Steinhaus longimeter construction, and showed that the disk admits bounded discrepancy. We prove that a randomly shifted Steinhaus construction improves the order of the universal upper bound to L 1 / 5 ​ ( log ⁡ L ) 2 / 5 L^{1/5}(\log L)^{2/5} .



1
Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a compact convex body with nonempty interior. Given a rectifiable one-dimensional set
S\subset\Omega
, one expects, by Crofton-type considerations, that the number of intersections of a typical line
\ell
with
S
should be proportional to the length of the chord
\ell\cap\Omega
. Steinerberger
[
1
]
formalized this as the Buffon discrepancy problem.

We write
|\Omega|
for the area of
\Omega
and
\mathcal{H}^{1}
for one-dimensional Hausdorff measure. For a finite union of line segments
S\subset\Omega
with length
\mathcal{H}^{1}(S)=L
, let
\#(\ell\cap S)
denote the number of transverse intersections of
\ell
with
S
, counted with multiplicity. The exceptional set of lines that contain a segment of
S
, pass through an endpoint, or pass through an intersection point of two segments has measure zero in the line space. Thus we define

\operatorname{disc}_{\Omega}(S)=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}\left|\#(\ell\cap S)-\frac{2L}{\pi|\Omega|}\cdot\mathcal{H}^{1}(\ell\cap\Omega)\right|.

Here
\mathcal{L}
denotes the space of unoriented lines in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, equipped with the usual motion-invariant measure.

Steinerberger proved that for every convex
\Omega
there are sets
S
of length
L
satisfying

\operatorname{disc}_{\Omega}(S)\lesssim_{\Omega}L^{1/3}.

His construction generalizes the Steinhaus longimeter: choose
n
evenly spaced directions, draw parallel lines with spacing
\varepsilon
in each direction, and restrict the resulting grid to
\Omega
. If
n/\varepsilon\sim L
, the discrepancy is (up to a constant) bounded by

\frac{L}{n^{2}}+n.

The first term is an angular quadrature error. The second term is a crude endpoint-rounding error, obtained by summing an
O(1)
error over
n
directions. Optimizing gives
n\sim L^{1/3}
and discrepancy
O_{\Omega}(L^{1/3})
.

The purpose of this note is to show that the endpoint errors do not need to add coherently. We randomly shift each of the
n
parallel-line families so that for any fixed chord, the endpoint errors are independent, centered, and bounded. A simple deterministic net argument then gives a uniform estimate of order

\sqrt{n\log L}+\log L.

This improves the error model to

\frac{L}{n^{2}}+\sqrt{n\log L}+\log L.

Choosing

n\sim L^{2/5}(\log L)^{-1/5}

then gives the following theorem:

Theorem 1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a compact convex body with nonempty interior. Then there exists
C_{\Omega}<\infty
such that for every sufficiently large
L
there is a finite union of line segments
S\subset\Omega
satisfying

\mathcal{H}^{1}(S)=L

and

\operatorname{disc}_{\Omega}(S)\leq C_{\Omega}L^{1/5}(\log L)^{2/5}.

2
The Randomly Shifted Construction

Let

A=|\Omega|,\quad D=\operatorname{diam}(\Omega).

For an integer
n\geq 1
, define

\nu_{k}=\left(\cos\frac{\pi k}{n},\sin\frac{\pi k}{n}\right),\qquad 0\leq k<n.

Let
U_{0},\dots,U_{n-1}
be independent uniform random variables on
[0,1)
. For a spacing
\varepsilon>0
, define the shifted line family

\mathcal{L}_{k}=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:x\cdot\nu_{k}=\varepsilon(q+U_{k})\text{ for some }q\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}.

The randomly shifted Steinhaus set is

S_{n,\varepsilon,U}=\Omega\cap\bigcup_{k=0}^{n-1}\mathcal{L}_{k}.

Since overlaps of different line families are finite sets, length is additive:

\mathcal{H}^{1}(S_{n,\varepsilon,U})=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\mathcal{H}^{1}(\Omega\cap\mathcal{L}_{k}).

Let
\ell
be a line such that
\ell\cap\Omega
is a nondegenerate segment. Write

\ell\cap\Omega=[x,y],\quad h_{\ell}=|x-y|.

Let

t=\frac{y-x}{|y-x|}

be the chord direction. For
0\leq k<n
, define

a_{k}(x,y)=\min(x\cdot\nu_{k},y\cdot\nu_{k}),\quad b_{k}(x,y)=\max(x\cdot\nu_{k},y\cdot\nu_{k}).

The number of transverse intersections of
\ell
with the
k
-th shifted family equals

N_{k}(x,y)=\#\left\{q\in\mathbb{Z}:\varepsilon(q+U_{k})\in[a_{k}(x,y),b_{k}(x,y))\right\},

where the half-open interval convention removes endpoint ambiguity. The projected length of the chord in direction
\nu_{k}
is

b_{k}(x,y)-a_{k}(x,y)=|(y-x)\cdot\nu_{k}|=h_{\ell}|t\cdot\nu_{k}|.

Thus

N_{k}(x,y)=\frac{h_{\ell}|t\cdot\nu_{k}|}{\varepsilon}+\xi_{k}(x,y),

where

\xi_{k}(x,y)=N_{k}(x,y)-\frac{b_{k}(x,y)-a_{k}(x,y)}{\varepsilon}.

We will estimate separately the deterministic main term

\frac{h_{\ell}}{\varepsilon}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}|t\cdot\nu_{k}|

and the random endpoint error

Z(x,y)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\xi_{k}(x,y).

3
The Angular Quadrature Estimate

Here we proceed exactly as in
[
1
]
, with the following lemma:

Lemma 2

(Angular quadrature)
.

Uniformly for every unit vector
t\in S^{1}
,

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}|t\cdot\nu_{k}|=\frac{2n}{\pi}+O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right).

Proof.

Write

t=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta).

Then

|t\cdot\nu_{k}|=\left|\cos\left(\theta-\frac{\pi k}{n}\right)\right|.

The Fourier series for
|\cos u|
is

|\cos u|=\frac{2}{\pi}+\frac{4}{\pi}\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{r}}{1-4r^{2}}\cos(2ru).

The coefficients are
O(r^{-2})
. Summing over
k
gives

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\cos\left(2r\left(\theta-\frac{\pi k}{n}\right)\right)=\begin{cases}n\cos(2r\theta),&n\mid r,\\
0,&n\nmid r.\end{cases}

Therefore

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}|t\cdot\nu_{k}|

\displaystyle=\frac{2n}{\pi}+n\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}O\left(\frac{1}{(jn)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2n}{\pi}+O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right),

uniformly in
\theta
.
∎

Consequently,

\frac{h_{\ell}}{\varepsilon}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}|t\cdot\nu_{k}|=\frac{2n}{\pi\varepsilon}\cdot h_{\ell}+O_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon n}\right).

4
The Endpoint Error

The main probabilistic estimate, and the main contribution of this note, is the following:

Lemma 3

(Uniform endpoint error)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be compact and contained in a ball of radius
D
. Assume
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
and
n\geq 1
. There exist constants

C_{R}=C_{R}(\Omega)\geq e,\quad C_{E}=C_{E}(\Omega)<\infty

such that, if

R=\frac{C_{R}n}{\varepsilon},

then, with probability at least
1-2R^{-10}
,

\sup_{x,y\in\Omega}\left|\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\left(N_{k}(x,y)-\frac{b_{k}(x,y)-a_{k}(x,y)}{\varepsilon}\right)\right|\leq C_{E}\left(\sqrt{n\log R}+\log R\right).

Proof.

We write

Z(x,y)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\left(N_{k}(x,y)-\frac{b_{k}(x,y)-a_{k}(x,y)}{\varepsilon}\right).

Let

T=K_{E}\left(\sqrt{n\log R}+\log R\right),

where
K_{E}=K_{E}(\Omega)
will be chosen large enough below. At the end of the proof we set
C_{E}=2K_{E}
.

If
T\geq n
, then the result is trivial, since each summand is bounded in absolute value by
1
, so
|Z(x,y)|\leq n\leq T
. Hence assume
T<n
. In this case

\delta:=\frac{\varepsilon T}{100n}<\frac{\varepsilon}{100}.

We complete the proof in four steps.

4.1
Fixed Pairs

Fix
x,y\in\Omega
and
k
. Put

a=a_{k}(x,y),\quad b=b_{k}(x,y),\quad d=b-a.

Then

N_{k}(x,y)=\#\{q\in\mathbb{Z}:\varepsilon(q+U_{k})\in[a,b)\}.

For every shift
U_{k}
, the number of shifted lattice points in an interval of length
d
is either

\left\lfloor\frac{d}{\varepsilon}\right\rfloor\quad\text{or}\quad\left\lfloor\frac{d}{\varepsilon}\right\rfloor+1.

Hence

\left|N_{k}(x,y)-\frac{d}{\varepsilon}\right|\leq 1.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{U_{k}}\left[N_{k}(x,y)\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{1}_{\varepsilon(q+u)\in[a,b)}\,du

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{a}^{b}ds=\frac{d}{\varepsilon}.

Therefore the summands in
Z(x,y)
are independent, centered, and bounded by
1
. Hoeffding’s inequality
[
2
]
gives

\mathbb{P}\{|Z(x,y)|>s\}\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{2s^{2}}{n}\right).

4.2
Deterministic Endpoint Net

Choose a deterministic
\delta
-net
\mathcal{P}\subset\Omega
. Since
\Omega
is bounded, there is a constant

C_{\mathrm{net}}=C_{\mathrm{net}}(\Omega)<\infty

such that, for every
0<\delta\leq 1
, one can choose
\mathcal{P}
with

|\mathcal{P}|\leq C_{\mathrm{net}}\delta^{-2}.

Since

\delta=\frac{\varepsilon T}{100n},

we have

|\mathcal{P}|\leq C_{\mathrm{net}}\left(\frac{100n}{\varepsilon T}\right)^{2}=10^{4}C_{\mathrm{net}}\left(\frac{n}{\varepsilon T}\right)^{2}.

Because
T\geq K_{E}\log R\geq 1
, this implies

|\mathcal{P}|\leq 10^{4}C_{\mathrm{net}}\left(\frac{n}{\varepsilon}\right)^{2}.

We now choose the structural constant
C_{R}=C_{R}(\Omega)
in the definition of
R
large enough that

C_{R}^{2}\geq 10^{4}C_{\mathrm{net}}.

Then

|\mathcal{P}|\leq R^{2}.

For each pair
(p,q)\in\mathcal{P}^{2}
, Hoeffding’s inequality gives

\mathbb{P}\{|Z(p,q)|>T\}\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{2T^{2}}{n}\right).

Since
|\mathcal{P}|^{2}\leq R^{4}
, a union bound gives

\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists p,q\in\mathcal{P}:\ |Z(p,q)|>T\right\}\leq 2R^{4}\exp\left(-\frac{2T^{2}}{n}\right).

Since
T^{2}/n\geq K_{E}^{2}\log R
, choosing
K_{E}
sufficiently large gives

2R^{4}\exp\left(-\frac{2T^{2}}{n}\right)\leq R^{-10}.

Thus, with probability at least
1-R^{-10}
,

|Z(p,q)|\leq T\quad\text{for all }p,q\in\mathcal{P}.

4.3
Unstable Net Points

For
p\in\mathcal{P}
and
0\leq k<n
, call
p
unstable in direction
k
if

\operatorname{dist}_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\left(\frac{p\cdot\nu_{k}}{\varepsilon}-U_{k},\ \mathbb{Z}\right)\leq\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon}.

Equivalently,
p
lies within projected distance
\delta
of one of the shifted lines in the
k
-th family. Since
\delta/\varepsilon<1/100
, for fixed
p,k
,

\mathbb{P}(p\text{ is unstable in direction }k)\leq\frac{2\delta}{\varepsilon}=\frac{T}{50n}.

Let

B(p)=\#\{0\leq k<n:\ p\text{ is unstable in direction }k\}.

Then
B(p)
is stochastically dominated by a binomial random variable with mean at most
T/50
. By the Chernoff bound
[
3
]
,

\mathbb{P}\{B(p)>T/5\}\leq\exp(-c_{\mathrm{ch}}T),

where
c_{\mathrm{ch}}>0
is an absolute constant. Since
|\mathcal{P}|\leq R^{2}
and
T\geq K_{E}\log R
, another union bound gives

\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists p\in\mathcal{P}:\ B(p)>T/5\right\}\leq R^{2}\exp(-c_{\mathrm{ch}}T)\leq R^{-10},

provided
K_{E}
is large enough.

Thus, with probability at least
1-R^{-10}
,

B(p)\leq T/5\quad\text{for all }p\in\mathcal{P}.

4.4
Extending the Net to All Endpoints

Assume the two high-probability events from the previous two subsections both occur.

Let
x,y\in\Omega
be arbitrary. Choose
p,q\in\mathcal{P}
such that

|x-p|\leq\delta,\quad|y-q|\leq\delta.

We compare
Z(x,y)
and
Z(p,q)
.

For a fixed direction
k
, the count
N_{k}(x,y)
can differ from
N_{k}(p,q)
only if, while moving
p
to
x
or
q
to
y
, one of the endpoints crosses a shifted lattice line of the
k
-th family. This can occur only if
p
or
q
is unstable in direction
k
. Hence

|N_{k}(x,y)-N_{k}(p,q)|\leq\mathbf{1}_{\{p\text{ unstable in }k\}}+\mathbf{1}_{\{q\text{ unstable in }k\}}.

Summing in
k
,

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}|N_{k}(x,y)-N_{k}(p,q)|\leq B(p)+B(q)\leq\frac{2T}{5}.

The deterministic mean term changes by at most

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{\left||(x-y)\cdot\nu_{k}|-|(p-q)\cdot\nu_{k}|\right|}{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{|(x-p)\cdot\nu_{k}|+|(y-q)\cdot\nu_{k}|}{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2n\delta}{\varepsilon}=\frac{T}{50}.

Therefore

|Z(x,y)-Z(p,q)|\leq\frac{2T}{5}+\frac{T}{50}\leq T.

Since
|Z(p,q)|\leq T
, we obtain

|Z(x,y)|\leq 2T.

Taking the supremum over
x,y\in\Omega
and recalling that
C_{E}=2K_{E}
proves the lemma.
∎

5
Length Concentration

We also need to know that the random construction has length close to its expectation.

Lemma 4

(Length concentration)
.

Let

L_{U}=\mathcal{H}^{1}(S_{n,\varepsilon,U}).

There exists
C_{L}=C_{L}(\Omega)<\infty
such that

\mathbb{E}\left[L_{U}\right]=\frac{n|\Omega|}{\varepsilon},

and, with
R=C_{R}n/\varepsilon
as in Lemma
3
,

\mathbb{P}\left\{\left|L_{U}-\frac{n|\Omega|}{\varepsilon}\right|>C_{L}\sqrt{n\log R}\right\}\leq R^{-10}.

Proof.

Fix
k
. Define the slice-length function

g_{k}(s)=\mathcal{H}^{1}\left(\Omega\cap\{x:x\cdot\nu_{k}=s\}\right).

Then

\mathcal{H}^{1}(\Omega\cap\mathcal{L}_{k})=\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}g_{k}(\varepsilon(q+U_{k})).

Averaging over
U_{k}
gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{U_{k}}\left[\mathcal{H}^{1}(\Omega\cap\mathcal{L}_{k})\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}g_{k}(\varepsilon(q+u))\,du

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}}g_{k}(s)\,ds=\frac{|\Omega|}{\varepsilon}.

Summing in
k
yields

\mathbb{E}\left[L_{U}\right]=\frac{n|\Omega|}{\varepsilon}.

Since
\Omega
is convex,
g_{k}
is concave on its support. Hence it has bounded variation, with

\operatorname{Var}(g_{k})\leq 2\sup_{s}g_{k}(s)\leq 2D.

For any compactly supported function
g
of bounded variation,

\left|\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}g(\varepsilon(q+u))-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(s)\,ds\right|\leq\operatorname{Var}(g).

Indeed, after multiplying by
\varepsilon
, compare each sampled value with the average of
g
on the corresponding interval of length
\varepsilon
:

\displaystyle\left|\varepsilon\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}g(\varepsilon(q+u))-\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(s)\,ds\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\varepsilon(q+u)}^{\varepsilon(q+u+1)}|g(\varepsilon(q+u))-g(s)|\,ds\leq\varepsilon\,\operatorname{Var}(g).

Dividing by
\varepsilon
gives the claim.

Thus each directional length contribution satisfies

\left|\mathcal{H}^{1}(\Omega\cap\mathcal{L}_{k})-\frac{|\Omega|}{\varepsilon}\right|\leq 2D.

The
n
contributions are independent. Hoeffding’s inequality gives

\mathbb{P}\left\{\left|L_{U}-\frac{n|\Omega|}{\varepsilon}\right|>s\right\}\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{2s^{2}}{n(4D)^{2}}\right).

Taking

s=C_{L}\sqrt{n\log R}

with
C_{L}=C_{L}(\Omega)
sufficiently large proves the lemma.
∎

6
The Discrepancy Bound

Proposition 5
.

Let

M=\frac{n|\Omega|}{\varepsilon}

be the expected length of
S_{n,\varepsilon,U}
, and let

R=\frac{C_{R}n}{\varepsilon}

with
C_{R}
as in Lemma
3
. There exists
C_{P}=C_{P}(\Omega)<\infty
such that, with positive probability,

\operatorname{disc}_{\Omega}(S_{n,\varepsilon,U})\leq C_{P}\left(\frac{M}{n^{2}}+\sqrt{n\log R}+\log R\right),

and

\left|\mathcal{H}^{1}(S_{n,\varepsilon,U})-M\right|\leq C_{P}\sqrt{n\log R}.

Proof.

Let
\ell
be a generic line, so that
\ell\cap\Omega=[x,y]
is a segment and all intersections with the constructed grid are transverse. Let

h_{\ell}=\mathcal{H}^{1}(\ell\cap\Omega),\quad t=\frac{y-x}{|y-x|}.

Then

\displaystyle\#\left(\ell\cap{S_{n,\varepsilon,U}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}N_{k}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{h_{\ell}}{\varepsilon}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}|t\cdot\nu_{k}|+Z(x,y).

By Lemma
2
,

\frac{h_{\ell}}{\varepsilon}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}|t\cdot\nu_{k}|=\frac{2n}{\pi\varepsilon}\cdot h_{\ell}+O_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon n}\right).

Since

M=\frac{n|\Omega|}{\varepsilon},

we have

\frac{2n}{\pi\varepsilon}=\frac{2M}{\pi|\Omega|}.

Also,

\frac{1}{\varepsilon n}=\frac{M}{|\Omega|n^{2}}\lesssim_{\Omega}\frac{M}{n^{2}}.

Therefore

\#\left(\ell\cap{S_{n,\varepsilon,U}}\right)=\frac{2M}{\pi|\Omega|}\cdot h_{\ell}+O_{\Omega}\left(\frac{M}{n^{2}}\right)+Z(x,y).

By Lemma
3
, with probability at least
1-2R^{-10}
,

\sup_{x,y\in\Omega}|Z(x,y)|\leq C_{E}\left(\sqrt{n\log R}+\log R\right).

By Lemma
4
, with probability at least
1-R^{-10}
,

\left|\mathcal{H}^{1}(S_{n,\varepsilon,U})-M\right|\leq C_{L}\sqrt{n\log R}.

For
R
sufficiently large, these two events have positive simultaneous probability. On this event, replacing
M
by the actual length

L_{U}=\mathcal{H}^{1}(S_{n,\varepsilon,U})

in the Crofton normalization changes the target term by at most

\frac{2|L_{U}-M|}{\pi|\Omega|}\cdot\mathcal{H}^{1}(\ell\cap\Omega)\leq\frac{2DC_{L}}{\pi|\Omega|}\sqrt{n\log R},

because
\mathcal{H}^{1}(\ell\cap\Omega)\leq D
. Taking the essential supremum over
\ell
and absorbing the displayed constants into
C_{P}
gives

\operatorname{disc}_{\Omega}(S_{n,\varepsilon,U})\leq C_{P}\left(\frac{M}{n^{2}}+\sqrt{n\log R}+\log R\right).

The length estimate also follows after increasing
C_{P}
so that
C_{P}\geq C_{L}
.
∎

7
Optimization and Exact Length

We now prove Theorem
1
. Let

\Phi(L)=L^{1/5}(\log L)^{2/5}.

It suffices to consider large
L
.

Choose an expected length

M=L-K_{0}\Phi(L),

where
K_{0}=K_{0}(\Omega)
is a constant to be fixed. For large
L
, we have
M\sim L
and

\Phi(M)\sim\Phi(L).

Choose

n=\left\lfloor M^{2/5}(\log M)^{-1/5}\right\rfloor

and set

\varepsilon=\frac{n|\Omega|}{M}.

Then

\frac{n}{\varepsilon}=\frac{M}{|\Omega|},

so, with
C_{R}
as in Lemma
3
,

R=\frac{C_{R}n}{\varepsilon}=\frac{C_{R}}{|\Omega|}M\asymp_{\Omega}M.

By Proposition
5
, there is a deterministic choice of phases
U
such that

\operatorname{disc}_{\Omega}(S_{n,\varepsilon,U})\leq C_{P}\left(\frac{M}{n^{2}}+\sqrt{n\log R}+\log R\right),

and

\left|\mathcal{H}^{1}(S_{n,\varepsilon,U})-M\right|\leq C_{P}\sqrt{n\log R}.

Since
R\asymp_{\Omega}M
, the chosen value of
n
gives

\frac{M}{n^{2}}\lesssim_{\Omega}M^{1/5}(\log M)^{2/5},

and

\sqrt{n\log R}\lesssim_{\Omega}M^{1/5}(\log M)^{2/5}.

Also,

\log R=o\left(M^{1/5}(\log M)^{2/5}\right).

Consequently, there is a constant
C_{\mathrm{opt}}=C_{\mathrm{opt}}(\Omega)
such that

\operatorname{disc}_{\Omega}(S_{n,\varepsilon,U})\leq C_{\mathrm{opt}}\Phi(L),

and

\left|\mathcal{H}^{1}(S_{n,\varepsilon,U})-M\right|\leq C_{\mathrm{opt}}\Phi(L).

Choose

K_{0}\geq 2C_{\mathrm{opt}}.

Then

\mathcal{H}^{1}(S_{n,\varepsilon,U})\leq L

and

0\leq L-\mathcal{H}^{1}(S_{n,\varepsilon,U})\leq(K_{0}+C_{\mathrm{opt}})\Phi(L)\lesssim_{\Omega}\Phi(L).

It remains to adjust the length upward to exactly
L
. Let

\Delta=L-\mathcal{H}^{1}(S_{n,\varepsilon,U}).

Choose a closed disk
B\subset\Omega
of radius
\rho_{\Omega}>0
. Inside
B
, add a finite collection
E
of pairwise disjoint line segments with total length exactly
\Delta
, arranged so that no segment of
E
overlaps any segment of
S_{n,\varepsilon,U}
. This can be done with at most

C_{\mathrm{add}}(\Omega)(1+\Delta)

segments. For example, choose a direction not parallel to any of the finitely many directions already used, place disjoint short parallel segments inside
B
, and shorten the final segment so the total length is exactly
\Delta
.

Now, let

S=S_{n,\varepsilon,U}\cup E.

Then

\mathcal{H}^{1}(S)=L.

For every generic line
\ell
, the number of new intersections contributed by
E
is at most the number of added segments, hence at most

C_{\mathrm{add}}(\Omega)(1+\Delta)\lesssim_{\Omega}\Phi(L).

The change in the Crofton target term caused by increasing the length from
\mathcal{H}^{1}(S_{n,\varepsilon,U})
to
L
is at most

\frac{2\Delta}{\pi|\Omega|}\mathcal{H}^{1}(\ell\cap\Omega)\leq\frac{2D}{\pi|\Omega|}\Delta\lesssim_{\Omega}\Phi(L).

Therefore

\operatorname{disc}_{\Omega}(S)\leq C_{\Omega}\Phi(L)=C_{\Omega}L^{1/5}(\log L)^{2/5},

for a final constant
C_{\Omega}
depending only on
\Omega
. This proves Theorem
1
.
