 3. Executive Summary
Launch Alaska, in partnership with Biomass Controls and local stakeholders, is
deploying a community-scale biomass reactor system (Biogenic Refinery) in Kivalina,
Alaska. The project addresses the high cost and logistical challenges with safe
sanitation and waste disposal in remote communities by enabling local, renewable
biomass-based thermal energy and improving operational reliability through clear
performance metrics and operator training.

The Refinery is installed in a customized insulated 20’ shipping container (connex) that
has an access door and several vents for fresh air and exhaust. Inside the container is a
commercial grinder, augers, a control system, and the refinery. The grinder processes
 2

the bagged human excreta from urine diverted dry toilets (UDDTs) mixed with paper
products to reduce moisture to about 35% moisture. At the bottom of the grinder there is
a blower that pulls odor from the grinder into a stainless steel box filled with high carbon
biochar to neutralize odor before exiting the container. Material is then conveyed from
an auger into the Biogenic Refinery. Biogenic material is “from living" while organic
material is "once living”. The system is a refinery because it takes feedstocks and
valorizes them from a waste to a useful product. Biochar produced from a Biogenic
Refinery is often used as a solid amendment as part of the circular economy but is also
used to produce pigment that can replace carbon black and media to filter PFAS.

Over the course of the award (06/01/2023 to 10/31/2025), the team completed a
technology assessment of the Biogenic Refinery then procured and documented spare
parts for the village of Kivalina, defined operational key performance indicator (KPI)
criteria, and developed operator and maintenance manuals to support ongoing
operations. The team developed and tested mobile applications (iOS and Android) that
generate KPI and data/output reports, prepared training materials and recorded training
videos to support operator onboarding and continuity as local staffing changes occur.

Remaining end-of-project activities focus on completing on-site operator training in
Kivalina and nearby at Port Site while conducting third-party validation of system
outputs following training completion. This report summarizes progress against
Statement of Project Objectives (SOPO) tasks, key schedule drivers, challenges
encountered (including travel constraints caused by village water system priorities,
failing of current upstream technologies and local leadership changes), and the planned
path forward to sustain and replicate the approach in other remote communities.

 5. Background
Kivalina is a remote coastal community in northwest Alaska where energy systems face
high delivered fuel costs, harsh operating conditions, and constrained maintenance
resources. Biomass-based thermal energy systems can reduce reliance on imported
fuels when paired with robust monitoring, clear operational guidance, and training that
fits local capacity and turnover.

 6. Project Objectives
The project objective is to support reliable operation of a biomass reactor system in
Kivalina by:
(1) assessing technology readiness and site needs.
(2) defining measurable KPI criteria and documenting operations and maintenance
procedures.
(3) training local operators; and
(4) enabling transparent performance monitoring through KPI and data/output reporting
tools.

SOPO tasks and milestone intent (summary):
Task 1: Technology assessment and spare parts identification/procurement.
Task 2: Define KPI criteria, develop operator and maintenance manuals, and complete
third-party output validation.
Task 3: Conduct on-site training in Kivalina (operator onboarding and maintenance
readiness).
Task 4: Deliver KPI reports and data/output reports through software tools to support
operations and stakeholder reporting.

Task 1 - Technology Assessment and Spare Parts
Task 1.1 (Technology Assessment) was completed via a site visit and assessment by
Biomass Controls. Findings included, for example, out of date controller and display
software, touch screen and communication technology which informed readiness
planning and support requirements.
Task 1.2 (Spare Parts) was completed, with spare parts identified and addressed to
reduce downtime risk. Spare parts were inventoried and then photographed for
identification. This came in hand recently when a part needed to be recovered and an
image was shared to make the process efficient.

 8. Significant Accomplishments and Conclusions
Significant accomplishments to date include:
• Completion of the technology assessment and spare parts planning, improving
readiness for sustained field operations.
• Definition of KPI criteria and completion of digital operator and maintenance manuals,
establishing a clear basis for performance tracking and safe operation.
• Delivery and testing of mobile applications (iOS and Android) that generate KPI
reports and data/output reports to support operations and stakeholder visibility.
• Development of training slide decks and recorded training videos to mitigate risk from
operator turnover.
Key challenges and lessons learned:
• On-site training was delayed due to Kivalina village water system emergencies,
upstream technical failures, and resulting prioritization of community resources, along
with local leadership changes that affected scheduling.
• Remote deployment schedules benefit from early alignment with community seasonal
constraints, contingency travel plans, and redundant training modalities (in-person plus
recorded materials).
• Local operational training and third-party validation were completed.

 9. Path Forward
Near-term path forward:
• During the next visit to Kivalina we will confirm operator proficiency against the
manuals and KPI criteria.

• Conduct third-party validation of system outputs following completion of training and
any required operational shakedown period. We have tested the biochar from the site
first during commissioning at SGS labs and recently from Control Labs, Watsonville,
California.

Operations and replication:
• Continue iterative improvements to KPI dashboards and reporting based on field
feedback.

• Identify long-term maintenance and spare parts replenishment processes with local
stakeholders.
• Evaluate opportunities to replicate the approach (monitoring + training +
documentation) in other remote communities with similar logistical constraints.
