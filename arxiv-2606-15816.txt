Kähler-Ricci Flow: from divisors to cusps
Eleonora Di Nezza, Vincent Guedj, Chinh H. Lu

Abstract
. We study the geometric regularization of positive closed currents by the Kähler-Ricci flow on compact Kähler manifolds. In a previous work of ours, it was shown that the Kähler-Ricci flow immediately smoothes out such a current when it has zero Lelong numbers. We study here the case when T 0 T_{0} has divisorial singularities, showing that the flow gradually replaces the latter by Poincaré type ones, providing an approximation of T 0 T_{0} by complete Kähler metrics with bounded curvature in a Zariski open set.



Introduction

Smoothing properties of the Ricci flow have been known and used for a long time in Kähler geometry
(see e.g.
[
BM87
,
Tian97
,
PSSW08
,
SzTo11
,
ST17
]
). It has been notably shown in
[
GZ17
,
DNL17
]

that one can run the (twisted) Kähler-Ricci flow from a very degenerate initial datum,
namely an arbitrary positive closed current of bidegree
(1,1)
.

By comparison with the riemannian setting, the Kähler analysis is simplified by the fact that
the problem at hand is equivalent to a scalar parabolic equation, a complex Monge-Ampère flow.
Let
(X,\omega)
be a compact Kähler manifold of dimension
n
.
We fix
\varphi_{0}
an
\omega
-plurisubharmonic function and
consider the complex Monge-Ampère flow

(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi_{t})^{n}=e^{\dot{\varphi}_{t}}dV_{X},

starting from
\varphi_{0}
, where
dV_{X}
is a fixed smooth volume form on
X
.
Here
d=\partial+\overline{\partial}
, and
d^{c}=\frac{1}{2i\pi}(\partial-\overline{\partial})
,
so that
dd^{c}=\frac{i}{\pi}\partial\overline{\partial}
.

The function
\varphi_{t}
is the ”maximal weak solution” to the flow.
As shown in
[
GZ17
,
DNL17
]
, it is smooth in a Zariski open set
\Omega
where it solves the equation in the classical sense,
and it has logarithmic singularities at the boundary of this set.
Among all possible solutions to the flow in
\Omega
,
\varphi_{t}
is the unique one having minimal singularities
along
\partial\Omega
.

Equivalently
\omega_{t}=\omega+dd^{c}\varphi_{t}
is a Kähler form in
\Omega

which is a solution of the twisted Kähler-Ricci flow

(KR)

\frac{\partial\omega_{t}}{\partial t}=-{\rm Ric}(\omega_{t})+{\rm Ric}(dV_{X}),

and whose potential
\varphi_{t}
has the smallest singularities along
\partial\Omega
.

Our main goal in this article is to give a precise description of the singularities before the smoothing time.
We will show that divisorial singularities are gradually replaced by Poincaré type ones.
This disproves a folklore conjecture in the field that the Kähler-Ricci flow produces approximants with analytic singularities
(see e.g.
[
DGZ16
, Question 43]
).

Demailly has produced several regularization results of quasi-plurisubharmonic functions, using either
convolutions
[
Dem92
]
or approximations by Bergman kernels
[
DPS01
]
.
While Demailly’s Bergman kernel approach naturally generates approximants with analytic singularities
for
\omega
-plurisubharmonic functions,
the Kähler-Ricci flow smoothing exhibits a more geometric character,
as shown by our main result:

Main Theorem
.

Let
(X,\omega)
be a compact Kähler manifold of dimension
n
.
Let
D=\sum_{j=1}^{r}D_{j}
be an ample log-smooth divisor, i.e. a finite
convex combination of irreducible smooth ample hypersurfaces with simple normal crossings.
Fix
m_{j}\geq 0
,
s_{j}
holomorphic sections of
{\mathcal{O}}(D_{j})
,

h_{j}
hermitian metrics of
{\mathcal{O}}(D_{j})
. Finally set
\varphi_{0}=\sum_{j=1}^{r}m_{j}\log|s_{j}|^{2}

and
T_{0}=\omega+dd^{c}\varphi_{0}\geq 0
.

The solution
(\omega_{t})_{t>0}
of the twisted Kähler-Ricci flow (
KR
) emanating from
T_{0}

satisfies:

•

\omega_{t}=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\max(m_{j}-t,0)[D_{j}]+\beta_{t,D}
,
where
\beta_{t,D}
is a Poincaré metric in
X\setminus\cup_{m_{j}\geq t}D_{j}
;

•

\omega_{t}
is a Kähler form on the whole of
X
as soon as
t>\lambda(\varphi_{0})=\max_{j}m_{j}
.

In particular,
\omega_{t}
has bounded curvature in
X\setminus\cup_{m_{j}\geq t}D_{j}
.

Here
|s_{j}|^{2}
is the norm of
s_{j}
with respect to the metric
h_{j}
.
The current of integration along
D_{j}
satisfies

[D_{j}]=\Theta_{h_{j}}+dd^{c}\log|s_{j}|^{2}
, hence we implicitly assume that

\omega\geq\sum_{j=1}^{r}m_{j}\Theta_{h_{j}}
.
The solution of the flow is explicit when
\omega=\sum_{j=1}^{r}m_{j}\Theta_{h_{j}}

(see Proposition
2.5
).

The proof of the main theorem is simpler when
m_{1}=\cdots=m_{r}
.
When the multiplicities
m_{j}
do not coincide, the analysis requires one to subdivide
the smoothing interval
[0,\lambda(\varphi_{0})]
, and construct a Poincaré metric

\beta_{t,D}
which is smooth near the divisors
\cup_{m_{j}<t}D_{j}
.

In
Section
1

after recalling the construction of the flow,
we establish a general comparison principle (Theorem
1.13
),
as well as the uniform quasi-concavity in time of the potentials (Proposition
1.15
).
The striking uniform lower bound

\dot{\varphi}_{t}\geq n\log t-C
follows then easily.
We then study the zero order asymptotics of
\varphi_{t}
in
Section
2
.
We first analyze the divisorial singularities in Lemma
2.4
, and then establish
uniform estimates along the flow in Proposition
2.7
, and
in Proposition
2.8
when the
m_{j}
’s do not coincide.
We finally prove our main result in
Section
3
, first
refining the control on
\dot{\varphi}_{t}
in Proposition
3.1
, then
obtaining a uniform
{\mathcal{C}}^{2}
-estimate in space (Theorem
3.3
).
Higher order estimates and
the comparison with Poincaré metrics are finally obtained in Theorem
3.6
,
by using Evans-Krylov theory, Schauder estimates, and the quasi-coordinates adapted to Poincaré metrics.

1.
The maximal flow

In the whole article we let
(X,\omega_{X})
be a compact Kähler manifold of dimension
n
.
In this section we let
\omega
be a closed smooth
(1,1)
-form that is semipositive and big,
i.e. with positive volume
\int_{X}\omega^{n}>0
(see Definition
1.3
).

We recall the construction of the maximal solution to the Kähler-Ricci flow (
KR
)
following
[
GZ17
,
DNL17
,
Dan25
]
and we establish new general and important estimates,
notably Theorem
1.13
and Proposition
1.15
, which will be useful
both in this article when
\omega
is Kähler, and in
[
DGL26
]
when

\omega
is merely semi-positive and big.

1.1.
Quasi-plurisubharmonic functions

Analytic singularities

A function is quasi-plurisubharmonic (quasi-psh) if it is locally given as the sum of a smooth and a plurisubharmonic function.

Definition 1.1
.

Quasi-psh functions

\varphi:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
satisfying

\omega+dd^{c}\varphi\geq 0

in the weak sense of currents are called
\omega
-plurisubharmonic (
\omega
-psh for short).
We let
\textup{PSH}(X,\omega)
denote the set of all
\omega
-plurisubharmonic functions which are not identically
-\infty
.

Note that constant functions are
\omega
-psh functions.
A
{\mathcal{C}}^{2}
-smooth function
u
has bounded Hessian, hence
\varepsilon u
is

\omega_{X}
-psh if
0<\varepsilon
is small enough.

Definition 1.2
.

A quasi-psh function
\varphi
has
analytic singularities

if it can be locally written as

\varphi=\frac{1}{2m}\log\sum_{j=1}^{N}|f_{j}|^{2}+u

for some holomorphic functions
f_{1},\dots,f_{N}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and smooth function
u
.
If we can further take
N=1
then we say that
\varphi
has
divisorial singularities
.

Definition 1.3
.

We say that
\omega
is
big
if there exists
\varepsilon>0
and
an
\omega
-psh function
u
such that
\omega+dd^{c}u\geq\varepsilon\omega_{X}
.
The current
\omega+dd^{c}u
is called a
Kähler current
.

The
ample locus

\mathrm{Amp}(\omega)
is the Zariski open subset
of points
x\in X
such that there exists a Kähler current

\omega+dd^{c}u

which is smooth in a neighborhood of
x
.

The notion of analytic singularities depends on the choice of local holomorphic functions,
not only on the ideal sheaf they generate.
We recall the following
[
BGL25
, Lemma 1.7]
:

Lemma 1.4
.

If
\varphi
is a quasi-psh function with analytic singularities, there exists a modification
\pi\colon Y\to X
, isomorphic over

\{\varphi>-\infty\}
, such that
\pi^{\star}\varphi
has divisorial singularities.

Size of singularities

Definition 1.5
.

Given a quasi-psh function
\varphi
on
X
, we let

c(\varphi):=\sup\left\{c>0,\int_{X}e^{-c\varphi}dV_{X}<+\infty\right\}

denote the integrability index of
\varphi
, while
\lambda(\varphi)=c(\varphi)^{-1}
is the Arnold multiplicity of
\varphi
.

We define similarly
c(\varphi,x)
and
\lambda(\varphi,x)=c(\varphi,x)^{-1}
the local version of these invariants,
and let
\nu(\varphi,x)=\sup\{\gamma\geq 0,\;\varphi(y)\leq\gamma\log{\rm d}_{\omega}(x,y)+C\}

be the Lelong number of
\varphi
at
x\in X
.

It follows from Skoda’s integrability theorem
[
GZ
, Theorem 2.50]
that for all
x\in X
,

\frac{\nu(\varphi,x)}{n}\leq\lambda(\varphi,x)\leq\nu(\varphi,x).

We refer the reader to
[
DK01
,
GZ
]
for basic properties of these numerical invariants,
as well as their counterpart in algebraic geometry. We stress here that

\lambda(\varphi)=\sup\{\lambda(\varphi,x),\;x\in X\},

while
c(\varphi)=\inf\{c(\varphi,x),\;x\in X\}
,
and that
(\varphi,x)\mapsto\nu(\varphi,x)

and
(\varphi,x)\mapsto\lambda(\varphi,x)
are upper semi-continuous (see
[
DK01
, Main theorem]
).

Theorem 1.6
.

There exists
\alpha>0
and
C_{\alpha}>0
such that for all
\varphi\in PSH_{0}(X,\omega)
,

\int_{X}e^{-\alpha\varphi}dV_{X}\leq C_{\alpha}.

The exponent
\alpha
only depends on an upper bound on the cohomology class
\{\omega\}
, while
the constant
C_{\alpha}
depends on
\alpha
and
(X,\omega)
in a controlled way
(see
[
DGG23
, Theorem 2.9]
).

Demailly’s approximation

The importance of functions with analytic singularities stems from the following consequence of
Demailly’s regularization theorem
[
DPS01
]
:

Theorem 1.7
.

Any
\varphi\in\textup{PSH}(X,\omega)
can be written as the limit of a decreasing sequence
\varphi_{j}\in\textup{PSH}(X,\omega+2^{-j}\omega_{X})
with analytic singularities,
such that
c(\varphi)=\lim c(\varphi_{j})
. In particular, when
\omega
is Kähler, the sequence
(\varphi_{j})
can be taken in
\textup{PSH}(X,\omega)
.

It is easy to smooth out an
\omega
-psh function with analytic singularities, in particular
any such function can be written as the limit of a decreasing sequence of smooth
\omega
-psh functions.

The set
\textup{PSH}(X,\omega)
is a closed subset of
L^{1}(X)
.
Subsets of
\omega
-psh functions enjoy strong compactness properties:

\textup{PSH}_{A}(X,\omega):=\{u\in\textup{PSH}(X,\omega),\,-A\leq\sup_{X}u\leq 0\}

is a compact subset of
L^{1}(X)
for any
A>0
.
We refer the reader to
[
GZ
]
for further basic properties of
\omega
-psh functions.

Finite energy class

Fix
\varphi\in PSH(X,\omega)
and set
\varphi_{j}:=\max(\varphi,-j)\in PSH(X,\omega)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
.
It has been shown in
[
GZ07
]
that the measures
{\bf 1}_{\{\varphi<-j\}}(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi_{j})^{n}

increase, as
j
increases to
+\infty
, to a positive Radon measure

\mu_{\varphi}
with total mass
\mu_{\varphi}(X)\leq\int_{X}\omega^{n}
.

Definition 1.8
.

The class
\mathcal{E}(X,\omega)
is the set of all
\omega
-psh functions with full Monge-Ampère mass,
i.e. such that
\mu_{\varphi}(X)=\int_{X}\omega^{n}
.

Functions in the finite energy class
\mathcal{E}(X,\omega)
can be unbounded, but they have mild singularities, in particular
their Lelong numbers vanish identically (see
[
DDL18
, Theorem 1.1]
).

1.2.
Basic properties of the flow

Construction

Fix
\varphi_{0}\in\textup{PSH}(X,\omega)
and set
\Omega_{t}=\{x\in X,\nu(\varphi_{0},x)<t\}
.
The latter form an increasing sequence of Zariski open subsets such that

\Omega_{T}=X
as soon as
T>T_{0}=\max_{x\in X}\nu(\varphi_{0},x)
.
When
\omega
is Kähler, by Theorem
1.7
we can approximate
\varphi_{0}
by a decreasing
sequence
(\varphi_{0,j})
of smooth
\omega
-psh functions.
Let
\varphi_{t,j}
denote the unique smooth solutions of

(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi_{t,j})^{n}=e^{\dot{\varphi}_{t,j}}dV_{X},

starting from
\varphi_{0,j}
. It is shown in
[
GZ17
,
DNL17
]
that
for all
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times X
,

•

the sequence
j\mapsto\varphi_{t,j}(x)
decreases to
\varphi_{t}(x)\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
;

•

the limit
\varphi_{t}(x)
is independent of the choice of approximants;

•

the function
x\mapsto\varphi_{t}(x)
belongs to
PSH(X,\omega)
;

•

the function
(t,x)\mapsto\varphi_{t}(x)
is smooth in
\mathbb{R}_{*}^{+}\times\Omega_{s}
, and

(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi_{t})^{n}=e^{\dot{\varphi}_{t}}dV_{X}\;\;\text{ in }\;\;\Omega_{s},\;s<t;

•

the function
\varphi_{t}
may have positive Lelong numbers at some points in
\partial\Omega_{t}
;

•

one has
\varphi_{t}\rightarrow\varphi_{0}
in
L^{1}
as
t\rightarrow 0
,
and the convergence holds in the sense of capacity.

Moreover, the flow becomes completely smooth in finite time (for
t>T_{0}
).

When
\omega
is merely semipositive and big, one can approximate
\varphi_{0}
by a decreasing sequence
(\varphi_{0,j})
of
smooth
(\omega+2^{-j}\omega_{X})
-psh functions (by Theorem
1.7
)
and establish similar results in the ample locus of
\omega
(see
[
Dan25
]
).

Sub/super-solutions

Definition 1.9
.

An upper semicontinuous function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{+}\times X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
is called a subsolution of the flow if

•

\varphi_{t}(\cdot)=\varphi(t,\cdot)
belongs to
PSH(X,\omega)
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
;

•

(t,x)\mapsto\varphi_{t}(x)
is smooth in
\mathbb{R}^{+}_{*}\times\Omega
for
some Zariski dense open set
\Omega\subset X
, and

(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi_{t})^{n}\geq e^{\dot{\varphi}_{t}}dV_{X}\;\;\text{ in }\;\;\Omega.

One defines similarly the notion of supersolution, and the notion of (weak) solution:

Definition 1.10
.

A function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\times X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
is a supersolution of the flow if

(t,x)\mapsto\varphi_{t}(x)
is smooth in
\mathbb{R}^{+}_{*}\times\Omega
for
some Zariski dense open set
\Omega\subset X
, and for
t>0
,

{\bf 1}_{\{\omega+dd^{c}\varphi_{t}\geq 0\}}(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi_{t})^{n}\leq e^{\dot{\varphi}_{t}}dV_{X}\;\;\text{ in }\;\;\Omega.

A function is a weak solution of the flow
if it is both a subsolution and a supersolution.

Note that supersolutions are
not
required to be a family of
\omega
-psh functions.
Let us also stress that these notions of (sub/super)solutions differ significantly
from the ones studied in
[
GLZ20
,
GLZ21
]
. In the latter works the functions under consideration
are globally bounded, so that the Monge-Ampère measures

(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi_{t})^{n}
have no mass at the boundary of
\partial\Omega
,
as follows from the Chern-Levine-Nirenberg inequality
[
GZ
, Theorem 3.14]
.

The crucial problem for us here is to understand the precise asymptotic behavior
of the functions
\varphi_{t}
along
\partial\Omega
.
We provide below examples of different weak solutions to the flow having various types of
asymptotic along
\Omega
(see Example
2.6
).
An important role is therefore played by the following property (see
[
DNL17
, Theorem 5.8]
and
[
Dan25
, Theorem B]
):

Theorem 1.11
.

The solution
constructed in Section
1.2
is the envelope of
all subsolutions to the flow. In particular it has minimal singularities among all
weak solutions to the flow.

In the rest of this article we call this solution
the
solution of the flow.
A simple consequence of this maximality property,
not explicitly stated in
[
GZ17
,
DNL17
]
, is the following:

Lemma 1.12
.

Fix
s>0
.
Let
\varphi_{t}
be the solution of the flow starting from
\varphi_{0}
,
and let
\psi_{t}
denote the solution of the flow starting from
\varphi_{s}
.
Then
\psi_{t}=\varphi_{t+s}
for all
t\geq 0
.

This property follows from the invariance under translations in time:
if
(t,x)\mapsto\varphi(t,x)
is a (sub)solution of the equation then,
for
s>0
, so is the function
(t,x)\mapsto\psi(t,x)=\varphi(t+s,x)
.

1.3.
Comparison principle

Recall that
\omega
is semipositive and big.
Fix
\Omega\subset{\rm Amp}(\omega)
a Zariski open set,
and
\rho
a strictly
\omega
-psh function which is smooth in
\Omega

with
\partial\Omega=\{\rho=-\infty\}
.
The following result is an important variation on the classical parabolic maximum principle:

Theorem 1.13
.

Let
\varphi_{t}
(resp.
\psi_{t}
) be a subsolution (resp. supersolution) of the flow

(\omega+dd^{c}u_{t})^{n}=e^{\dot{u}_{t}+f}dV_{X},

with

\varphi,\psi,f
smooth in
(0,T]\times\Omega
.
Assume the following properties hold:

(1)

\psi
is continuous on
[0,T]\times\Omega
and
\varphi_{0}\leq\psi_{0}
;

(2)

there exists
h\in\mathcal{E}(X,\omega)
such that
\varphi_{t}(x)+h(x)\leq\psi_{t}(x)
,
for all
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\Omega
;

(3)

\rho(x)\leq\psi_{t}(x)
, for all
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\Omega
.

Then
\varphi_{t}(x)\leq\psi_{t}(x)
for all
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\Omega
.

In what follows we often work with sub/supersolutions whose dominant singular part is of the form
(1-t)\log|s|^{2}
, where
s
is a holomorphic section.
In this setting, we apply the above comparison principle to
\psi_{t+\varepsilon}

for which the condition 3) is satisfied and let then
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Let us stress that we allow the density
f
to take values
\pm\infty
at some points of
\partial\Omega
; this will be quite useful in later applications.

Proof.

If the maximum of the function
\varphi_{t}-\psi_{t}-2Ct\varepsilon
is attained in
\Omega
, the classical maximum principle provides the desired result, as the functions are smooth in
\Omega
. In order to force this maximum to be attained in
\Omega
, we need to add
h
and
\rho
to
\varphi_{t}
. To deal with
the lack of regularity of
h
, we use the viscosity theory.

We assume without loss of generality that
\omega+3dd^{c}\rho\geq a\omega_{X}
, for some
a>0
. Fix
C>0
so large that
(\omega/2+dd^{c}\rho)^{n}\geq e^{-C}dV_{X}
in
\Omega
.
Thanks to
[
ALS24
, Proposition 2.5]
we have
P_{\omega}(4h)\in\mathcal{E}(X,\omega)
,
thus up to working with
\frac{1}{4}P_{\omega}(4h)
we can assume that
h\in\mathcal{E}(X,\omega/4)
. Here
P_{\omega}(4h)
denotes the largest
\omega
-psh function that lies below
4h
.
We set
\omega^{\prime}=\omega/4
.

We claim that we can find a concave increasing function
\chi:\mathbb{R}^{-}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{-}
with

\frac{\chi(t)}{t}\to+\infty
as
t\to-\infty
such that
P_{\omega^{\prime}}(\chi(h))\in\mathcal{E}(X,\omega^{\prime})
.
Indeed since
h\in{\mathcal{E}}(X,\omega^{\prime})
there exists a convex increasing function

\chi_{1}:\mathbb{R}^{-}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{-}
with
\chi_{1}(-\infty)=-\infty
,
\chi_{1}(h)\in L^{1}(X,(\omega^{\prime}+dd^{c}h)^{n})

(see
[
GZ07
, Proposition 2.2]
), and moreover

\int_{X}|\chi_{1}(h_{j})|(\omega^{\prime}+dd^{c}h_{j})^{n}\leq C_{1},

for some
C_{1}>0
, where
h_{j}=\max(h,-j)
.
We set
\chi^{\prime}(t)=(-\chi_{1}(t))^{1/2n}
and
\gamma(t)=-(-\chi_{1}(\chi^{-1}(t)))^{1/2}
,
with
\chi(0)=0
. Note that
\gamma
is increasing with
\gamma(-\infty)=-\infty
.
Since the Monge-Ampère measure of
u_{j}=P_{\omega^{\prime}}(\chi(h_{j}))
is concentrated on

D=\{P_{\omega^{\prime}}(\chi(h_{j}))=\chi(h_{j})\}
, we obtain

{\omega^{\prime}}_{u_{j}}^{n}={\bf 1}_{D}(\omega^{\prime}+dd^{c}\chi(h_{j}))^{n}\leq(\chi^{\prime}(h_{j}))^{n}{\omega^{\prime}}_{h_{j}}^{n}\leq(-\chi_{1}(h_{j}))^{1/2}{\omega^{\prime}}_{h_{j}}^{n}.

Thus

\int_{X}|\gamma(u_{j})|(\omega^{\prime}+dd^{c}u_{j})^{n}\leq\int_{X}|\chi_{1}(h_{j})|(\omega^{\prime}+dd^{c}h_{j})^{n}\leq C_{1}

hence
P_{\omega^{\prime}}(\chi(h))\in\mathcal{E}(X,\omega^{\prime})
.

The function
v=P_{\omega/2}(\rho+\chi(h))
is not identically
-\infty
since it dominates

\rho+P_{\omega/4}(\chi(h))
which is
\omega/2
-psh.
Fix
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and consider the function

\varphi_{t,\varepsilon}=(1-\varepsilon)\varphi_{t}+\varepsilon\rho-2C\varepsilon t.

The concavity of
H\mapsto\log\det H
on the set of positive hermitian matrices
ensures that if
\alpha,\beta>0
are positive
(1,1)
-forms such that

\alpha^{n}\geq fdV
and
\beta^{n}\geq gdV
, then

\alpha^{j}\wedge\beta^{n-j}\geq f^{\frac{j}{n}}g^{\frac{n-j}{n}}dV_{X}

for all
0\leq j\leq n
. Using the binomial expansion we infer for all
0<\varepsilon<1
,

((1-\varepsilon)\alpha+\varepsilon\beta)^{n}\geq\left((1-\varepsilon)f^{\frac{1}{n}}+\varepsilon g^{\frac{1}{n}}\right)^{n}dV_{X}.

Using this inequality and the convexity of the exponential, we obtain

\displaystyle((1-\varepsilon/2)\omega+dd^{c}\varphi_{t,\varepsilon})^{n}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left((1-\varepsilon)e^{\dot{\varphi}_{t}/n}+\varepsilon e^{-C/n}\right)^{n}dV_{X}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle e^{(1-\varepsilon)\dot{\varphi}_{t}-C\varepsilon}dV_{X}=e^{\dot{\varphi}_{t,\varepsilon}+C\varepsilon}dV_{X}.

The function
(t,x)\mapsto u(t,x):=\varphi_{t,\varepsilon}+\varepsilon v-\psi_{t}

is upper semicontinuous in
[0,T]\times\Omega
(since
\psi_{t}
is continuous).
The assumptions (2), (3) ensure that
u\leq\varepsilon v-(1-\varepsilon)h\leq\varepsilon\rho+\varepsilon\chi(h)-(1-\varepsilon)h
. The growth condition on
\chi
ensures that
u
reaches its maximum in
[0,T]\times\Omega
.
We claim that the latter is moreover reached
along
(t=0)
, so that

u\leq\sup_{\Omega}(\varphi_{0,\varepsilon}+\varepsilon v-\psi_{0})\leq\varepsilon\sup_{\Omega}(\rho-\psi_{0})\leq 0,

using
\varphi_{0}\leq\psi_{0}
.
Letting
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
gives the conclusion.

Assume by contradiction that
u
reaches its maximum at some
(t_{0},x_{0})\in[0,T]\times\Omega
, with
t_{0}>0
.
At the point
(t_{0},x_{0})
, the classical maximum principle yields

\partial_{t}\varphi_{t,\varepsilon}-\partial_{t}\psi_{t}=\partial_{t}u\geq 0.

Take a local smooth potential
g
of
\omega/2
around
x_{0}
. Since
v
is
\omega/2
-psh, by
[
EGZ11
, Proposition 1.3]
,
g+v
is a viscosity subsolution of the equation
(dd^{c}\cdot)^{n}\geq 0
near
x_{0}
.
Also, the function
(\psi_{t_{0}}+\varepsilon g-\varphi_{t_{0},\varepsilon})
is smooth near
x_{0}
and (by assumption)
\varepsilon(g+v)-(\psi_{t_{0}}+\varepsilon g-\varphi_{t_{0},\varepsilon})
attains its local maximum at
x_{0}
. It thus follows from the definition of viscosity subsolutions that

dd^{c}(\psi_{t_{0}}+\varepsilon g-\varphi_{t_{0},\varepsilon})\geq 0,

at
x_{0}
, which yields

0\leq\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)\omega+dd^{c}\varphi_{t_{0}}\leq(\omega+dd^{c}\psi_{t_{0}}).

Since
\psi
is a supersolution, we infer

e^{{\dot{\psi}}_{t_{0}}+f}dV_{X}\geq(\omega+dd^{c}\psi_{t_{0}})^{n}\geq((1-\varepsilon/2)\omega+dd^{c}\varphi_{t_{0},\varepsilon})^{n}\geq e^{\dot{\varphi}_{t_{0},\varepsilon}+C\varepsilon+f}dV_{X}\geq e^{{\dot{\psi}}_{t_{0}}+C\varepsilon+f}dV_{X},

a contradiction. Thus
t_{0}=0
.
∎

Remark 1.14
.

Prescribing the right asymptotic behavior near
\partial\Omega
is crucial for this result to hold: we provide
in Example
2.6
several weak solutions that do not have the same asymptotic.

1.4.
Quasi-concavity in time

Proposition 1.15
.

Let
\varphi_{t}
be the solution of the flow
(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi_{t})^{n}=e^{\dot{\varphi}_{t}}dV_{X}

starting from
\varphi_{0}\in PSH(X,\omega)
.
The following estimates hold on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times X
:

(1)

\varphi_{0}-Ct+n(t\log t-t)\leq\varphi_{t}\leq\sup_{X}\varphi_{0}+Ct
;

(2)

t\mapsto\varphi_{t}-n(t\log t-t)
is concave;

(3)

n\log t-C\leq\dot{\varphi}_{t}\leq\frac{\varphi_{t}-\varphi_{0}}{t}+n
.

Here
C>0
is independent of
\varphi_{0}
and only depends on
(X,\omega,dV_{X})
.

The bounds on
\dot{\varphi}_{t}
largely generalize
[
ST17
, Lemma 3.2]
which dealt with
bounded

\varphi_{0}
;
the proof is completely different and relies on the concavity property of
\phi_{t}=\varphi_{t}-n(t\log t-t)
.
By quasi-concavity the time derivative
\dot{\varphi}_{t}(x)
is well defined off a countable set of times.

The function
\tilde{\phi_{t}}=\varphi_{t}-n(t\log t-t)+Ct
is increasing in
t
.
This property ensures that
\varphi_{t}
converges to
\varphi_{0}
in the strongest
possible sense as
t
decreases to zero.

Proof.

Fix
C>0
such that
\omega^{n}\leq e^{C}dV_{X}
.
The upper bound
\varphi_{t}\leq\sup_{X}\varphi_{0}+Ct
follows from the maximum principle since
\sup_{X}\varphi_{0}+Ct
is a super-solution.

It follows from Theorem
1.6
that we can find
0<\tilde{\alpha}<\tilde{\alpha}(\{\omega\})

such that
\int_{X}e^{-\tilde{\alpha}\varphi_{0}}\omega^{n}\leq C_{\tilde{\alpha}}
, with

C_{\tilde{\alpha}}
independent of
\varphi_{0}
. Set
\alpha=\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{2}
. Using
[
GZ
, Corollary 11.9 and Theorem 12.1]

we find a unique
u\in PSH(X,\omega)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
such that

||u||_{L^{\infty}(X)}\leq C_{\alpha}^{\prime}
and

\alpha^{n}(\omega+dd^{c}u)^{n}=e^{\alpha(u-\varphi_{0})}dV_{X}.

Since
(1-\alpha t)\varphi_{0}(x)+\alpha tu(x)+n(t\log t-t)
is a subsolution of the flow,
we obtain

(1.1)

\varphi_{0}-C_{\alpha}^{\prime\prime}t+n(t\log t-t)\leq(1-\alpha t)\varphi_{0}+\alpha tu+n(t\log t-t)\leq\varphi_{t},

for all
0\leq t\leq 1/\alpha
, using that
\varphi_{0}\leq 0
.
Observe that
\varphi_{1/\alpha}
is uniformly bounded,

-M_{\alpha}=-C_{\alpha}^{\prime}-\frac{n}{\alpha}(\log\alpha+1)\leq u(x)-\frac{n}{\alpha}(\log\alpha+1)\leq\varphi_{1/\alpha}\leq 0.

The semi-group property and the minimum principle ensure that

-M_{\alpha}\leq\varphi_{t}\leq 0
for all
t\geq 1/\alpha
.
Slightly enlarging
C_{\alpha}^{\prime\prime}
if necessary, we thus obtain
(1)
.

To establish (2) one can proceed by approximation to reduce to the case when
\varphi_{0},\varphi_{t}
are smooth.
Observing that
\ddot{\varphi}_{t}=\Delta_{\omega_{t}}(\dot{\varphi}_{t})
one can differentiate once more and realize that

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta_{\omega_{t}}\right)(t\ddot{\varphi}_{t})\leq\ddot{\varphi_{t}}-nt\left(\frac{dd^{c}\dot{\varphi_{t}}\wedge\omega_{t}^{n-1}}{\omega_{t}^{n}}\right)^{2}

(see computations in
[
GLZ20
, Theorem 2.7]
). The conclusion follows from the maximum principle. Indeed, consider
H(t,x)=t\ddot{\varphi}_{t}(x)
. If the maximum is attained at
(0,x_{0})
we then have
H(t,x)\leq H(0,x_{0})=0
, hence
\ddot{\varphi}_{t}\leq 0
, i.e.
\varphi_{t}
is concave. In particular
\varphi_{t}-n(t\log t-t)
is concave. If the maximum is attained at
t_{0}>0
, at
(t_{0},x_{0})
we have that

0\leq t\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta_{\omega_{t}}\right)(t\ddot{\varphi}_{t})\leq t\ddot{\varphi_{t}}-nt^{2}\left(\frac{(dd^{c}\dot{\varphi_{t}})\wedge\omega_{t}^{n-1}}{\omega_{t}^{n}}\right)^{2}=t\ddot{\varphi_{t}}-\frac{t^{2}}{n}(\Delta_{t}\dot{\varphi_{t}})^{2}=t\ddot{\varphi_{t}}-\frac{1}{n}(t\ddot{\varphi_{t}})^{2}.

In particular
t\ddot{\varphi_{t}}\leq n
at
(t_{0},x_{0})
. Hence
H\leq n
, or equivalently
\ddot{\varphi_{t}}\leq\frac{n}{t}
everywhere. Thus
t\mapsto\varphi_{t}-n(t\log t-t)
is concave.

The function
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\mapsto{\phi}_{t}(x)=\varphi_{t}(x)-n(t\log t-t)\in\mathbb{R}
is concave,
hence
\dot{\phi}_{t}\leq\frac{\phi_{t}-\phi_{0}}{t}
. It follows that
\dot{\varphi}_{t}\leq\frac{\varphi_{t}-\varphi_{0}}{t}+n
.
The function
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\mapsto\tilde{\phi}_{t}(x)=\varphi_{t}(x)-n(t\log t-t)+Ct\in\mathbb{R}
is concave
for
x\in X\setminus(\varphi_{0}=-\infty)
, and bounded below by
\varphi_{0}(x)
.
It follows
that
\frac{d\tilde{\phi}_{t}}{dt}\geq 0

for all times; the lower bound on
\dot{\varphi}_{t}
follows.
∎

2.
Uniform estimates

From now on we assume
\omega
is Kähler and we fix a log smooth divisor
D=\sum_{j}D_{j}
, where the
D_{j}
’s are

irreducible ample divisors with simple normal crossings
.

2.1.
Poincaré metrics

We fix holomorphic
ample
line bundles
L_{1},\ldots,L_{r}
equipped with Hermitian metrics
h_{1},\ldots,h_{r}
and, for any
j
, we denote by
\Theta_{j}
the curvature of
h_{j}
.
For
s_{i}\in H^{0}(X,L_{i})
, we denote by
|s_{i}|=|s_{i}|_{h_{i}}
the norm of
s
with respect to
h_{i}
.
Rescaling
h_{i}
, we can assume that
|s_{i}|\leq\frac{1}{1000}
on
X
.
We assume
D_{j}=(s_{j}=0)
is irreducible, and set

D=\sum_{j=1}^{r}D_{j}\;\;\text{ and }\;\;|s|^{2}=\prod_{j=1}^{r}|s_{j}|^{2}.

Definition 2.1
.

The divisor
D
is log smooth if each divisor
D_{j}
is smooth and the
D_{j}
’s have simple normal crossings,
i.e.
D
is locally isomorphic to
H_{1}+H_{2}+...H_{k}
where

H_{1},\ldots,H_{k}
are the coordinate hyperplanes.

In the whole article we assume that
D
is log smooth. Moreover, since each
L_{j}=\mathcal{O}(D_{j})
is ample, we can choose
h_{j}
such that
\Theta_{j}
is a Kähler form. In particular
\Theta:=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\Theta_{j}
is a Kähler form as well.
Fix
\tilde{\omega}
yet another Kähler form.

Definition 2.2
.

We denote by
\beta_{P}
the following Poincaré metric along the divisor
D
,

\beta_{P}:=\tilde{\omega}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}dd^{c}[-2\log(-\log|s_{i}|^{2})].

We call
\rho_{P}:=-2\sum_{j=1}^{r}\log(-\log|s_{i}|^{2})
a Poincaré potential.
Our choice of normalization
|s_{i}|_{h_{i}}\leq\frac{1}{1000}
ensures that

\beta_{P}
dominates (a multiple of)
\tilde{\omega}
. In local coordinates
for which
D_{i}=(z_{i}=0)
, the form
\beta_{P}
is quasi-isometric to

\beta_{P}^{\prime}=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\frac{idz_{j}\wedge d\overline{z}_{j}}{|z_{j}|^{2}(-\log|z_{j}|^{2})^{2}}+\sum_{j=r+1}^{n}idz_{j}\wedge d\overline{z}_{j}.

We refer the reader to
[
Kob84
]
for basic properties of the Poincaré metric
\beta_{P}
.
We simply mention here that the Riemannian curvature of
\beta_{P}
is bounded in
X\setminus D
.

Definition 2.3
.

We say that
\beta
is a Kähler form of Poincaré type
in
\{{\theta}\}\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
if

•

\beta
is a Kähler form in
X\setminus D
and there exists
c>1
such that
c^{-1}\beta_{P}\leq\beta\leq c\beta_{P}
;

•

\beta=\theta+dd^{c}u
, where
u
is smooth in
X\setminus D
and
|u-\rho_{P}|=O(1)
;

•

\left|\nabla_{\beta_{P}}^{j}u\right|
is bounded in
X\setminus D
for all
j\geq 1
.

A basic example of Kähler form of Poincaré type is provided by the unique solution of

(\tilde{\omega}+dd^{c}\rho)^{n}=\frac{e^{\rho}}{|s|^{2}}dV_{X},

that belongs to
\mathcal{E}(X,\tilde{\omega})
.

2.2.
Evolution of divisorial singularities

We fix multiplicities
m_{j}\geq 0
(not all zero) such that
\sum_{j}m_{j}\Theta_{h_{j}}\leq\omega
, and consider
the initial datum

\varphi_{0}=\sum_{j=1}^{r}m_{j}\log|s_{j}|^{2}\in PSH(X,\omega).

We let
\varphi_{t}
denote the solution of the maximal Kähler-Ricci flow starting from
\varphi_{0}
.
In this section we estimate
\varphi_{t}
up to bounded errors on the smoothing time interval
(0,\lambda_{0}(\varphi_{0})]
.

The following result provides an asymptotic of
\varphi_{t}
along
(s_{j}=0)
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Assume
\varphi_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}\log|s_{i}|^{2}+\psi_{0}\in PSH(X,\omega)
, where

\psi_{0}
a quasi-psh function.
There exists a constant
C>0
such that

\varphi_{t}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{r}\max(m_{i}-t,0)\log|s_{i}|^{2}+Ct.

Lemma
2.4
provides the right logarithmic behavior of
the solution
\varphi_{t}
when
D
is
log smooth, and even when
D
is merely

log canonical
(i.e. when
c(\sum\log|s_{j}|^{2})=1
).
This is no longer the case when
D
is more singular, and one then needs to use a log resolution
of the singularities to establish a refined first order asymptotic
(see
[
DGL26
]
).

Proof.

The proof is identical for one or several components (taking convex combination of each involved quantity),
so we only treat the case
r=1
and
m_{1}=1
for simplicity.

We can assume without loss of generality that
\psi_{0}=0
.
We approximate the initial potential
\varphi_{0}
by
\varphi_{0,\varepsilon}=\log(|s|^{2}+\varepsilon)
and we let
\varphi_{t,\varepsilon}
denote the unique smooth solution to the Monge-Ampère flow with initial datum
\varphi_{0,\varepsilon}
.
Consider

\psi_{t,\varepsilon}=(1-t)\varphi_{0,\varepsilon}+Ct

and observe that

dd^{c}\varphi_{0,\varepsilon}=\frac{-|s|^{2}}{(|s|^{2}+\varepsilon)}\Theta_{1}+\frac{\varepsilon}{(|s|^{2}+\varepsilon)^{2}}\frac{i}{\pi}\partial s\wedge\overline{\partial s}.

Thus

\omega+dd^{c}\psi_{t,\varepsilon}=\left[1-\frac{(1-t)|s|^{2}}{|s|^{2}+\varepsilon}\right]\omega+\frac{(1-t)|s|^{2}}{(|s|^{2}+\varepsilon)}(\omega-\Theta_{h})+\frac{\varepsilon(1-t)}{(|s|^{2}+\varepsilon)^{2}}\frac{i}{\pi}\partial s\wedge\overline{\partial s}\geq 0,

and

\omega+dd^{c}\psi_{t,\varepsilon}\leq B\omega+\frac{\varepsilon(1-t)}{(|s|^{2}+\varepsilon)^{2}}\frac{i}{\pi}\partial s\wedge\overline{\partial s}\leq B\omega+\frac{1}{|s|^{2}+\varepsilon}\frac{i}{\pi}\partial s\wedge\overline{\partial s}

for some
B>0
.
Since
i\partial s\wedge\overline{\partial s}
has rank
1
and

\partial_{t}\psi=-\log(|s|^{2}+\varepsilon)+C
, we infer

(\omega+dd^{c}\psi_{t,\varepsilon})^{n}\leq B^{n}\omega^{n}+\frac{nB^{n-1}}{|s|^{2}+\varepsilon}\frac{i}{\pi}\partial s\wedge\overline{\partial s}\wedge\omega^{n-1}\leq\frac{e^{C}}{|s|^{2}+\varepsilon}\omega^{n}=e^{\partial_{t}\psi}\omega^{n},

if we choose
C>0
large enough.

Since
\psi_{0,\varepsilon}=\varphi_{0,\varepsilon}
, the classical maximum principle ensures that
\varphi_{t,\varepsilon}\leq\psi_{t,\varepsilon}
.
The conclusion follows by letting
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, as
\varphi_{t,\varepsilon}
then decreases to
\varphi_{t}
.
∎

2.3.
Uniform estimates for small times

2.3.1.
Purely divisorial initial datum

When
T_{0}=\sum_{j=1}^{r}m_{j}[D_{j}]
is purely divisorial
(i.e.
\omega=\sum_{j=1}^{r}m_{j}\Theta_{h_{j}}
) and
dV_{X}=\omega^{n}
,
we obtain an explicit decomposition of the metrics
\omega_{t}
.

Proposition 2.5
.

If
\omega=\sum_{j=1}^{r}m_{j}\Theta_{h_{j}}
and
dV_{X}=\omega^{n}
, then for all
(x,t)\in X\times(0,\min_{j}m_{j})
,

\varphi_{t}=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\max(m_{j}-t,0)\log|s_{j}|^{2}+t\rho+n(t\log t-t),

where
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(X,\Theta)
is the unique solution of
(\Theta+dd^{c}\rho)^{n}=e^{\rho}|s|^{-2}\omega^{n}
,
with
|s|^{2}=\Pi_{j=1}^{r}|s_{j}|^{2}_{h_{i}}
.

We thus have a relatively explicit solution to the flow when
\omega=\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}\Theta_{h_{i}}
.

Proof.

Set
\psi_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\max(m_{i}-t,0)\log|s_{i}|^{2}+t\rho+n(t\log t-t)
. Observe that

\psi_{0}=\varphi_{0}
and
\omega+dd^{c}\psi_{t}=t(\Theta+dd^{c}\rho)
in
X\setminus D
,
hence

(\omega+dd^{c}\psi_{t})^{n}=t^{n}(\Theta+dd^{c}\rho)^{n}=t^{n}\frac{e^{\rho}}{|s|^{2}}\omega^{n}=e^{\partial_{t}\psi_{t}}\omega^{n}.

In particular
\psi_{t}\leq\varphi_{t}
as
\psi_{t}
is a weak subsolution.

Now
\psi_{t}
is also a supersolution of the flow with
\psi_{0}=\varphi_{0}
,
and all assumptions from Theorem
1.13
are satisfied, hence we conclude that
\varphi_{t}\leq\psi_{t}
.
∎

If
m_{1}=\cdots=m_{r}=t_{1}
we thus obtain an explicit description of the flow until the smoothing
time
\lambda(\varphi_{0})=m
. If
t_{1}=\min_{i}m_{i}<\lambda(\varphi_{0})=\max_{i}m_{i}
, understanding
the uniform estimate after time
t=m
requires a finer analysis that we develop hereafter.

Example 2.6
.

The same computation allows one to provide several examples of weak solutions to the
flow that have wilder asymptotic behavior near
D
. Consider indeed

\psi_{\alpha}=(1-\alpha t)\log|s|^{2}+\alpha t\rho_{\alpha}+nt\log\alpha+n(t\log t-t),

where
r=m_{1}=1
,

0<\alpha<1
,
\omega=\Theta_{1}
,
and
\rho_{\alpha}\in{\mathcal{E}}(X,\omega)
satisfies

(\omega+dd^{c}\rho_{\alpha})^{n}=e^{\alpha\rho_{\alpha}}|s|^{-2\alpha}\omega^{n}.

The metrics
\omega_{\alpha}=\omega+dd^{c}\psi_{\alpha}
are Kähler in
X\setminus D
with
logarithmic and conic singularities along
D
. They solve the equation in

X\setminus D
, but are more singular than the maximal solution.

2.3.2.
General case

We now provide the order zero asymptotic of the flow on
X\times(0,\min_{i}m_{i})
starting from
\varphi_{0}=\sum_{j}m_{j}\log|s_{j}|^{2}
, without assuming
\omega=\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}\Theta_{h_{i}}
, nor
dV_{X}=\omega^{n}
.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
\rho\in PSH(X,\Theta)
be the unique
solution of
(\Theta+dd^{c}\rho)^{n}=e^{\rho}|s|^{-2}dV_{X}
.
There exists
C_{0}>0
such that
for all
(x,t)\in X\times(0,\min_{i}m_{i})
,

n(t\log t-t)\leq\varphi_{t}(x)-u_{t}(x)-t\rho(x)\leq C_{0}t,

where
u_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\max(m_{i}-t,0)\log|s_{i}|^{2}
.

Proof.

Set
\tilde{\psi}_{t}=\sum_{j}(m_{j}-t)\log|s_{j}|^{2}+t\rho+n(t\log t-t)
. Then, outside
D

\omega+dd^{c}\tilde{\psi}_{t}=\omega+\sum_{j}(m_{j}-t)dd^{c}\log|s_{j}|^{2}+tdd^{c}\rho=\gamma+t(\Theta+dd^{c}\rho)\geq 0

and

(\omega+dd^{c}\tilde{\psi}_{t})^{n}\geq t^{n}\Theta_{\rho}^{n}\geq t^{n}e^{\rho}|s|^{-2}dV_{X}=e^{\partial_{t}\tilde{\psi}_{t}}dV_{X}.

Consequently, the function
\tilde{\psi}_{t}
is a subsolution to the Monge-Ampère flow. By definition, this subsolution lies below
\varphi_{t}
.

For the upper bound, observe that
\omega^{j}\wedge\Theta_{\rho}^{n-j}\leq C\Theta_{\rho}^{n}
for any
j=1,\cdots,n

since
\Theta_{\rho}\geq c\omega
.
Thus for
\psi_{t}:=\sum_{j}(m_{j}-t)\log|s_{j}|^{2}+t\rho+C_{0}t
we obtain (still outside
D
)

(\omega+dd^{c}{\psi}_{t})^{n}=\left(\gamma+t\Theta_{\rho}\right)^{n}\leq\left(A\omega+t_{1}\Theta_{\rho}\right)^{n}\leq C_{1}\Theta_{\rho}^{n}=e^{\partial_{t}\psi_{t}}dV_{X}.

It therefore follows from Theorem
1.13
that
\psi_{t}
is a super-solution with
\varphi_{t}\leq\psi_{t}
.
∎

2.4.
Uniform estimates up to the smoothing time

We assume here that the multiplicities
m_{i}
are not equal and decompose
the interval
(0,\lambda(\varphi_{0})]
accordingly,

0<t_{1}=\min_{i}m_{i}<t_{2}<\cdots<t_{\tilde{r}}=\max_{i}m_{i}=\lambda(\varphi_{0}).

We let

\tilde{D_{1}}=D_{1}+\cdots+D_{i_{1}},\ldots,\tilde{D}_{\tilde{r}}=D_{1+i_{{r}-1}}+\cdots+D_{r}

denote the divisors that have the same multiplicity
\tilde{m}_{j}
,
let
\tilde{s_{j}}
denote the product of the corresponding
s_{i}
’s so that

\tilde{D_{j}}=(\tilde{s_{j}}=0)
.
With these notations one obtains

t_{1}=\tilde{m}_{1}<\tilde{m}_{2}<\cdots<\tilde{m}_{\tilde{r}}=\lambda_{0}(\varphi_{0})
and

\varphi_{0}=\sum_{j=1}^{\tilde{r}}\tilde{m}_{j}\log|\tilde{s_{j}}|^{2}.

Proposition 2.8
.

Fix
1\leq j<{\tilde{r}}-1
.
There exists
C_{j}>0
such that for all
t_{j}<t\leq t_{j+1}
,

2\tilde{m}_{j}\log(t-t_{j})-C_{j}t\leq\varphi_{t}-\sum_{\ell=j+1}^{\tilde{r}}(\tilde{m}_{\ell}-t)\log|\tilde{s_{\ell}}|^{2}-t\hat{\rho}_{j}\leq C_{j}t,

where
\hat{\rho}_{j}
denotes the Poincaré potential along the log smooth divisor

\hat{D}_{j}=\tilde{D}_{j+1}+\cdots+\tilde{D}_{r}
.

Proof.

The proof goes by (finite) induction on
j
. To simplify the notations we only
treat the case
j=1
. It follows from Proposition
2.7
that

(2.1)

\displaystyle\varphi_{t_{1}}=\sum_{\ell=2}^{\tilde{r}}(\tilde{m}_{\ell}-\tilde{m}_{1})\log|\tilde{s_{\ell}}|^{2}+\tilde{m}_{1}{\rho}_{D}+O(1).

By the semigroup property,
\varphi_{t+t_{1}}
is the flow starting from
\varphi_{t_{1}}
. Slightly abusing notations,
we denote this flow by
\varphi_{t}
, with
t\in(0,t_{2}-t_{1})
.
We decompose

{\rho}_{D}=-2\sum_{m_{j}\leq t_{1}}\log(-\log|s_{j}|^{2})+\hat{\rho_{1}},

where

\hat{\rho_{1}}
is a Poincaré potential for the log smooth divisor

\hat{D}_{1}=\tilde{D}_{2}+\cdots+\tilde{D}_{r}

whose components do not disappear at time
t_{1}
.
Consider

\psi_{t}=\sum_{\ell=2}^{\tilde{r}}(\tilde{m}_{\ell}-\tilde{m}_{1}-t)\log|\tilde{s_{\ell}}|^{2}+(t+\tilde{m}_{1})\hat{\rho}_{1}+C_{1}(t+1),\qquad t\in(0,t_{2}-t_{1}).

It follows from (
2.1
) that
\psi_{0}\geq\varphi_{t_{1}}

and
(\omega+dd^{c}\psi_{t})^{n}\leq e^{\partial_{t}\psi}dV_{X}
in
X\setminus\hat{D}_{1}
if
C_{1}
is large enough.
Lemma
2.4
ensures that for
t\in(0,t_{2}-t_{1})
,

\varphi_{t}\leq\sum_{\ell=2}^{\tilde{r}}\max(\tilde{m}_{\ell}-\tilde{m}_{1}-t,0)\log|s_{\ell}|^{2}+Ct.

It follows therefore from Theorem
1.13
that
\varphi_{t}\leq\psi_{t}
.

We now claim that

\varphi_{t}\geq\psi_{t}-C_{2}(t+1)+2\tilde{m_{1}}\log t

for
t\in(0,t_{2}-t_{1})
, if
C_{2}>C_{1}
is chosen large enough. Indeed consider

u_{t}=\psi_{t}+|\tilde{s_{1}}|-C_{2}(1+t)+2\tilde{m_{1}}\log t.

We are going to show that for each fixed
t\in(0,t_{2}-t_{1})
, one has

(2.2)

\displaystyle e^{-{\varphi_{t}}/{t}}(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi_{t})^{n}\leq e^{-{u_{t}}/{t}}(\omega+dd^{c}u_{t})^{n}.

Accepting this, a direct computation shows that
\psi_{t}+\tilde{m}_{1}\rho_{D}
is a subsolution to the flow starting from
\varphi_{t_{1}}
. Thus both
u_{t}
and
\varphi_{t}
belong to
\mathcal{E}(X,\omega,P[\varphi_{t}])
in the terminology of
[
DDL18
]
.
The relative comparison principle
[
DDL18
, Corollary 3.6]
ensures
u_{t}\leq\varphi_{t}
,
providing the desired lower bound on
\varphi_{t}
, since the function
|s_{1}|
is uniformly bounded.

We now establish (
2.2
).
It follows from Proposition
1.15
.(3) and the upper bound from previous step that

e^{-{\varphi_{t}}/{t}}(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi_{t})^{n}=e^{-{\varphi_{t}}/{t}+\dot{\varphi}_{t}}dV_{X}\leq e^{n-{\varphi_{t_{1}}}/{t}}dV_{X}.

On the other hand, observing that

\partial\overline{\partial}|\tilde{s_{1}}|=\frac{1}{4}\frac{\partial\tilde{s_{1}}\wedge\overline{\partial\tilde{s_{1}}}}{|\tilde{s_{1}}|}
, we infer that
there is a uniform constant
c>0
such that

(\omega+dd^{c}u_{t})^{n}\geq\frac{c}{|\tilde{s}_{1}|}\frac{dV_{X}}{|\hat{s}_{1}|^{2}(-\log|\hat{s}_{1}|^{2})^{2}},

where
\hat{s}_{1}=\Pi_{\ell=2}^{\tilde{r}}\tilde{s}_{\ell}
.
Thus

\frac{e^{-{u_{t}}/{t}}(\omega+dd^{c}u_{t})^{n}}{e^{-{\varphi_{t}}/{t}}(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi_{t})^{n}}\geq c\,e^{-n+\frac{\varphi_{t_{1}}-u_{t}}{t}}\frac{1}{|\tilde{s}_{1}||\hat{s}_{1}|^{2}(-\log|\hat{s}_{1}|^{2})^{2}}

By definition

\varphi_{t_{1}}-u_{t}=t\sum_{\ell=2}^{\tilde{r}}\log|\tilde{s_{\ell}}|^{2}-t\hat{\rho_{1}}+\tilde{m_{1}}(-2\log(-\log|\tilde{s_{1}}|^{2})-|\tilde{s_{1}}|+(C_{2}-C_{1})(1+t)-2\tilde{m_{1}}\log t,

while
\prod_{\ell}|\tilde{s}_{\ell}|^{2}e^{-\hat{\rho}_{1}}=|\hat{s_{1}}|^{2}(-\log|\hat{s_{1}}|^{2})^{2}
.
Using the fact that
|\tilde{s}_{1}|\leq C^{\prime}
, we obtain

\displaystyle e^{\frac{\varphi_{t_{1}}-u_{t}}{t}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle|\hat{s_{1}}|^{2}(-\log|\hat{s_{1}}|^{2})^{2}(-\log|\tilde{s_{1}}|^{2})^{-2\tilde{m}_{1}/t}e^{\frac{-|\tilde{s}_{1}|-2\tilde{m_{1}}\log t+(C_{2}-C_{1})(1+t)}{t}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle|\hat{s_{1}}|^{2}(-\log|\hat{s_{1}}|^{2})^{2}(-\log|\tilde{s_{1}}|^{2})^{-2\tilde{m}_{1}/t}e^{C^{\prime\prime}+(C_{3}-2\tilde{m_{1}}\log t)/{t}},

where
C^{\prime\prime}=C_{2}-C_{1}-C^{\prime}
and
C_{3}=C_{2}-C_{1}
.
Combining the previous inequalities yields

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-{u_{t}}/{t}}(\omega+dd^{c}u_{t})^{n}}{e^{-{\varphi_{t}}/{t}}(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi_{t})^{n}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle c\,e^{-n+C^{\prime\prime}+\frac{C_{3}-2\tilde{m}_{1}\log t}{t}}\frac{1}{|\tilde{s}_{1}|(-\log|\tilde{s}_{1}|^{2})^{2\tilde{m}_{1}/t}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c\,e^{-n+C^{\prime\prime}+\frac{C_{3}-2\tilde{m}_{1}\log t}{t}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\log|\tilde{s}_{1}|^{2}-\frac{2\tilde{m}_{1}}{t}\log(-\log|\tilde{s}_{1}|^{2})}.

Since
x=-\log|\tilde{s}_{1}|^{2}\geq-C_{3}
is bounded below, the conclusion follows by noting that
the function
x\mapsto\frac{x}{2}-\frac{2\tilde{m_{1}}}{t}\log x
attains its minimum at
4\tilde{m}_{1}/t
,
hence it is bounded below
by
\frac{2\tilde{m_{1}}}{t}[1-\log 4\tilde{m}_{1}+\log t]
on
[-C_{3},+\infty)
. Thus

\frac{e^{-{u_{t}}/{t}}(\omega+dd^{c}u_{t})^{n}}{e^{-{\varphi_{t}}/{t}}(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi_{t})^{n}}\geq e^{C^{\prime\prime\prime}}e^{\frac{2\tilde{m}_{1}}{t}(1-\log 4\tilde{m}_{1}+C_{3})}

Choosing
C_{2}
large enough so that
(1-\log 4\tilde{m}_{1}+C_{3})\geq 0
concludes the proof.
∎

3.
Higher order estimates

To achieve the higher order analysis we again need to work in each time interval
(t_{\ell},t_{\ell+1})
.
Thanks to Proposition
2.8
we have the following decomposition in
(t_{\ell},t_{\ell+1})
,

\varphi_{t}=\sum_{m_{j}>t_{\ell}}(m_{j}-t)\log|s_{j}|^{2}+t\rho_{\ell}+v_{t}^{\ell},

where
2\tilde{m}_{\ell}\log(t-t_{\ell})-C_{\ell}\leq v_{t}^{\ell}\leq C_{\ell}t
.

3.1.
Time derivative

Proposition 3.1
.

There exists a uniform constant
C_{\ell}>0
such that

n\log(t-t_{\ell})-C_{\ell}\leq\frac{\partial v_{t}^{\ell}}{\partial t},

for all
(x,t)\in X\times(t_{\ell},t_{\ell+1}]
.

Remark 3.2
.

One can also prove that
\frac{\partial v_{t}}{\partial t}\leq C
, when
(x,t)
belongs to
X\times(0,t_{1}]
.
This follows from Proposition
1.15
and Proposition
2.7
. Indeed,

-\sum_{j}\log|s_{j}|^{2}+\rho+\dot{v}_{t}=\dot{\varphi}_{t}\leq\frac{\varphi_{t}-\varphi_{0}}{t}+n\leq\rho-\sum_{j}\log|s_{j}|^{2}+n+A_{2},

where
A_{2}
is the positive constant appearing in Proposition
2.7
.

Proof.

The proof is inspired by the parabolic pluripotential approach developed in
[
GLZ20
]
.

We first treat the case
\ell=0
, i.e. when
t\in(0,\min_{i}m_{i}]
.
Set
\gamma=\omega-\sum_{j}m_{j}\Theta_{j}\geq 0
,
fix
\varepsilon,c>0
and consider

w_{t}=(1-\varepsilon c^{-1})\varphi_{t}+\varepsilon c^{-1}\psi_{t},

where

\psi_{t}=\varphi_{0}+(c+1)t\left(\rho-\sum_{j=1}^{r}\log|s_{j}|^{2}\right).

By the fact that
\omega+dd^{c}\varphi_{0}=\gamma+[D]
and a direct computation we obtain that outside
D

(\omega+dd^{c}\psi_{t})=\gamma+t(c+1)\Theta_{\rho}\geq t(c+1)\Theta_{\rho}

hence

(c+1)^{-n}(\omega+dd^{c}\psi_{t})^{n}\geq e^{n\log t+(c+1)^{-1}\dot{\psi}_{t}}dV_{X}.

Recall that, if
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}
are two positive forms with
\theta_{1}^{n}\geq e^{f_{1}}dV_{X}
and
\theta_{2}^{n}\geq e^{f_{2}}dV_{X}
,
it follows from the the mixed Monge-Ampère inequalities and the convexity of
the exponential that for
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in[0,1]
such that
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}=1
,

\displaystyle(\lambda_{1}\theta_{1}+\lambda_{2}\theta_{2})^{n}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\lambda_{1}^{k}\lambda_{2}^{n-k}e^{\frac{k}{n}f_{1}+\frac{n-k}{n}f_{2}}dV_{X}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\lambda_{1}e^{f_{1}/n}+\lambda_{2}e^{f_{2}/n})^{n}dV_{X}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle e^{\lambda_{1}f_{1}+\lambda_{2}f_{2}}dV_{X}.

Using this inequality and the identity
\frac{1+c^{-1}}{1+c}=c^{-1}
, we obtain

\displaystyle(\omega+dd^{c}w_{t})^{n}

\displaystyle=(1+\varepsilon)^{n}\left(\frac{1-c^{-1}\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}\omega_{\varphi_{t}}+\frac{c^{-1}\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}\omega_{\psi_{t}}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle=(1+\varepsilon)^{n}\left(\frac{1-c^{-1}\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}\omega_{\varphi_{t}}+\frac{(1+c^{-1})\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}\frac{\omega_{\psi_{t}}}{c+1}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle\geq\exp\left(\frac{1-c^{-1}\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}\dot{\varphi}_{t}+\frac{(1+c^{-1})\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}(c+1)^{-1}\dot{\psi}_{t}+\frac{(1+c^{-1})\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}n\log t\right)dV_{X}

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\frac{1-c^{-1}\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}\dot{\varphi}_{t}+\frac{c^{-1}\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}\dot{\psi}_{t}+\frac{(1+c^{-1})\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}n\log t\right)dV_{X}

\displaystyle\geq\exp\left(\frac{\dot{w}_{t}}{1+\varepsilon}+\frac{(1+c^{-1})\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}n\log t\right)dV_{X}.

On the other hand, the function
\varphi_{t}^{\varepsilon}:=\varphi_{(1+\varepsilon)t}
satisfies

\varphi_{0}^{\varepsilon}=w_{0}=\varphi_{0}
and

(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi_{t}^{\varepsilon})^{n}=\exp\left(\frac{\dot{\varphi}_{t}^{\varepsilon}}{1+\varepsilon}\right)dV_{X}.

Thus
w_{t}+(1+c^{-1})n\varepsilon t(\log t-1)
is a subsolution of this rescaled
flow, hence

\varphi_{(1+\varepsilon)t}\geq w_{t}+(1+c^{-1})n\varepsilon t(\log t-1).

Using the definition of
w_{t}
and the upper bound for
v_{t}
provided by Proposition
2.7
,
we infer

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\varphi_{(1+\varepsilon)t}-\varphi_{t}}{\varepsilon t}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{w_{t}-\varphi_{t}}{\varepsilon t}-C_{+}(1+c^{-1})n\log t

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\psi_{t}-\varphi_{t}}{ct}-C+(1+c^{-1})n\log t

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\rho-\sum_{j}\log|s_{j}|^{2}-\frac{v_{t}}{ct}-C+(1+c^{-1})n\log t

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(\rho-\sum_{j}\log|s_{j}|^{2}\right)-C_{0}c^{-1}-C+(1+c^{-1})n\log t.

The lower bound for
\dot{v}_{t}
follows since
\dot{\varphi}_{t}=-\sum_{j}\log|s_{j}|^{2}+\rho+\dot{v}_{t}
.

The estimates for other time intervals follow from similar arguments, with a more singular initial datum.
We assume that
\varphi_{0}=\sum_{j=1}^{r}m_{j}\log|s_{j}|^{2}+h
, where
h\in\mathcal{E}(X,\omega/2)
, while
\sum_{j=1}^{r}\log|s_{j}|^{2}\in PSH(X,\omega/2)
. We first assume
h
is smooth and establish estimates independent of
h
. By the previous step, we have

\dot{\varphi}_{t}\geq-\sum_{j=1}^{r}\log|s_{j}|^{2}+\rho+n\log t-C,

with
C>0
that a priori depends on
h
.
We will show that
C
can be chosen independently of
h
.
We fix
\varepsilon>0
and consider

H(t,x):=\dot{\varphi}_{t}+(1-\varepsilon)\sum_{j=1}^{r}\log|s_{j}|^{2}+2\sum_{j=1}^{r}\log(-\log|s_{j}|^{2})-n(1+\varepsilon)\log t.

The previous step ensures that
H
attains its minimum at some point
(t_{0},x_{0})
such that

t_{0}>0
and
s_{j}(x_{0})\neq 0
. At this point we obtain

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq(\partial_{t}-\Delta_{t})H

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{n(1+\varepsilon)}{t}+\sum_{j=1}^{r}{\rm Tr}_{t}\left((1-\varepsilon)\Theta_{j}-\frac{2\Theta_{j}}{-\log|s_{j}|^{2}}+2|s_{j}|^{-4}(\log|s_{j}|^{2})^{-2}d|s_{j}|^{2}\wedge d^{c}|s_{j}|^{2}\right).

Here,
\Theta_{j}:=-dd^{c}\log|s_{j}|^{2}\geq c_{0}\omega
outside
\{s_{j}=0\}
.
Since
|s_{j}|<1/1000
we infer

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq(\partial_{t}-\Delta_{t})H

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{n(1+\varepsilon)}{t}+\sum_{j=1}^{r}{\rm Tr}_{t}\left(c_{0}\omega+2|s_{j}|^{-4}(\log|s_{j}|^{2})^{-2}d|s_{j}|^{2}\wedge d^{c}|s_{j}|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{n(1+\varepsilon)}{t}+\frac{nc_{1}e^{-\dot{\varphi}_{t}/n}}{\prod_{j=1}^{r}|s_{j}|^{2}(\log|s_{j}|^{2})^{2}},

using the inequality
{\rm Tr}_{\beta}(\alpha)\geq n(\alpha^{n}/\beta^{n})^{1/n}
. It thus follows that
H(t_{0},x_{0})\geq-C_{2}
, with
C_{2}
independent of
\varepsilon
and
h
. The result follows.
∎

3.2.
Poincaré type metrics along the flow

Recall that on each interval
(t_{\ell},t_{\ell+1}]
,

\omega_{t}=\sum_{m_{i}\geq t_{\ell}}^{r}(m_{i}-t)[D_{i}]+t\beta_{t,\ell},

where
\beta_{t,\ell}
is a Kähler form in
X\setminus D
, and
a positive closed current on
X
with no mass along
D
.
We now show that
\beta_{t,\ell}
is a Poincaré type metric along
\hat{D}_{\ell}
.

3.2.1.
{\mathcal{C}}^{2}
-estimate

We first show that
\beta_{t,\ell}
is quasi-isometric to
\omega_{P,\ell}
,
the Poincaré metric associated to the divisor
\hat{D}_{\ell}
.

Theorem 3.3
.

There exists
C_{t}>1
such that on each interval of time
(t_{\ell},t_{\ell+1})
we have

C_{t}^{-1}\omega_{P,\ell}\leq\beta_{t,\ell}\leq C_{t}\,\omega_{P,\ell}.

The key step
is the following upper-bound:

Lemma 3.4
.

There exists a uniform constant
C_{\ell}>0
such that for all
t\in(t_{\ell},t_{\ell+1})
,

\log\operatorname{tr}_{\omega_{P,\ell}}(\omega_{t})\leq C_{\ell}\frac{|\log(t-t_{\ell})|}{(t-t_{\ell})^{2}}.

Proof.

To simplify notations we write
\beta_{t},\omega_{P}
instead of
\beta_{t,\ell},\omega_{P,\ell}
,
and work on the time interval
(0,t_{\ell+1}-t_{\ell})
by replacing
t
by
t-t_{\ell}
.
By Proposition
2.8
we can decompose

\varphi_{t}=\sum_{k=\ell+1}^{\tilde{r}}(\tilde{m}_{k}-t)\log|\tilde{s_{k}}|^{2}+\psi_{t},

where
\psi_{t}=t\hat{\rho}_{\ell}+v_{t}
, and

-tA_{2}+n(\log(t-t_{\ell})-t)\leq v_{t}\leq A_{1}t.

Hence in
X\setminus D
we have

\omega_{t}^{n}\geq t^{n}\beta_{t}^{n}=e^{\partial_{t}\varphi_{t}}dV_{X}=e^{\partial_{t}v_{t}}\frac{e^{\hat{\rho_{\ell}}}}{\prod_{\tilde{m}_{k}\geq t_{\ell}}|s_{k}|^{2}}dV_{X},

with
\beta_{t}=\sum_{k>\ell}\Theta_{k}+dd^{c}{\hat{\rho}_{\ell}}+dd^{c}(v_{t}/t)
.

Let
\varphi_{t,j}
be the solution of the flow starting from

\varphi_{t_{\ell},j}=\sum_{k=\ell}^{\tilde{r}}(\tilde{m}_{k}-\tilde{m}_{1})\log(|\tilde{s}_{k}|^{2}+1/j)-2\tilde{m}_{\ell}\sum_{\tilde{m}_{k}\geq t_{\ell}}\log\left(-\log(|\tilde{s}_{k}|^{2}+1/j)\right)

and define
v_{t,j}
by
\varphi_{t,j}=\sum_{k=\ell}^{\tilde{r}}(\tilde{m}_{k}-t)\log|\tilde{s}_{k}|^{2}+t\hat{\rho_{\ell}}+v_{t,j}
.
Let us stress that
v_{t,j}
is not smooth but it is uniformly bounded from above in
j
thanks to Proposition
2.8
.
We also stress that
v_{t,j}
is not (a priori) uniformly bounded from below.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
and set

\alpha:=t\log u_{j}-Av_{t+\varepsilon,j}+\eta\log|s|^{2},

where
\eta>0
is a small constant,

u_{j}:=\operatorname{tr}_{\omega_{P}}(\omega_{t+\varepsilon,j})
and
A>0
will be specified later.
We are going to establish estimates that are uniform in
j,\eta
, and let eventually

j\rightarrow+\infty
and
\eta\rightarrow 0
.

The function
\log u_{j}
is bounded from above since
\omega_{t+\varepsilon,j}
is smooth and
\omega_{P}
is a Kähler current.
It therefore follows from the uniform
{\mathcal{C}}^{0}
-estimate that

\alpha
reaches its maximum on
[0,T]\times X\setminus D
.
If the maximum of
\alpha
is reached at
t_{0}=0
, then

t\log u_{j}(x)\leq-Av_{\varepsilon,j}(x_{0})+Av_{t+\varepsilon,j}(x)-\eta\log|s|^{2}\leq C_{1}\left(1-\log\varepsilon\right)-\eta\log|s|^{2}.

Taking
\varepsilon=t
and sending
\eta
to zero, and then
j
to
+\infty
we obtain

\log\operatorname{tr}_{\omega_{P}}(\omega_{2t})\leq\frac{C_{2}}{t}(-\log t).

We now assume that
\alpha
reaches its maximum at
(t_{0},x_{0})
with

t_{0}>0
and
x_{0}\in X\setminus D
. We remove the subscript
j
to simplify notations.
Set
\Delta=\Delta_{\omega_{t+\varepsilon}}
and observe that

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)(\alpha)=\log u+\frac{t}{u}\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}-A\frac{\partial v_{t+\varepsilon}}{\partial t}-t\Delta\log u+A\Delta(v_{t+\varepsilon})+\eta\operatorname{tr}_{\omega_{t+\varepsilon}}(\Theta).

Since
\omega_{t+\varepsilon}=(t+\varepsilon)\omega_{P}+dd^{c}v_{t+\varepsilon}
in
X\setminus D
,
the last but one term is

A\Delta(v_{t+\varepsilon})=An-A(t+\varepsilon)\operatorname{tr}_{\omega_{t+\varepsilon}}(\omega_{P}).

We let
-B
denote a lower-bound on the holomorphic bisectional curvature of
\omega_{P}
in
X\setminus D
.
The last but two terms are estimated thanks to Lemma
3.5
,

-t\Delta\log u\leq Bt\,\operatorname{tr}_{\omega_{t+\varepsilon}}(\omega_{P})+t\frac{\operatorname{tr}_{\omega_{P}}({\rm Ric}(\omega_{t+\varepsilon}))}{\operatorname{tr}_{\omega_{P}}(\omega_{t+\varepsilon})}.

Since

\frac{t}{u}\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\frac{t}{u}\Delta_{\omega_{P}}(\log\omega_{t+\varepsilon}^{n}/\omega^{n})=\frac{t}{u}\left\{-\operatorname{tr}_{\omega_{P}}({\rm Ric}\,\omega_{t+\varepsilon})+\operatorname{tr}_{\omega_{P}}({\rm Ric}\,\omega)\right\},

we infer

-t\Delta_{t}\log u+\frac{t}{u}\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}\leq(B+C_{1})t\,\operatorname{tr}_{\omega_{t+\varepsilon}}(\omega_{P}),

using that
\operatorname{tr}_{\omega_{P}}({\rm Ric}\,\omega)\leq C\operatorname{tr}_{\omega_{P}}(\omega)\leq C^{\prime}

and
u^{-1}=[\operatorname{tr}_{\omega_{P}}(\omega_{t+\varepsilon})]^{-1}\leq n^{-1}\operatorname{tr}_{\omega_{t+\varepsilon}}(\omega_{P})
.

Observe now that

u=\operatorname{tr}_{\omega_{p}}(\omega_{t+\varepsilon})\leq n\left(\frac{\omega_{t+\varepsilon}^{n}}{\omega_{P}^{n}}\right)\cdot\left(\operatorname{tr}_{\omega_{t+\varepsilon}}(\omega_{P})\right)^{n-1}\leq Ce^{\partial_{t}v_{t+\varepsilon}}\left(\operatorname{tr}_{\omega_{t+\varepsilon}}(\omega_{P})\right)^{n-1}.

The inequality
(n-1)\log x<x+C_{n}
therefore yields

(3.1)

\log u\leq\frac{\partial v_{t+\varepsilon}}{\partial t}+C_{2}+\operatorname{tr}_{\omega_{t+\varepsilon}}(\omega_{P}).

Altogether we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)(\alpha)

\displaystyle\leq(An+C_{2})-(A-1)\frac{\partial v_{t+\varepsilon}}{\partial t}+\left[(B+C_{1})t+1-A(t+\varepsilon)\right]\operatorname{tr}_{\omega_{t+\varepsilon}}(\omega_{P})+\eta\operatorname{tr}_{\omega_{t+\varepsilon}}(\Theta)

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}-(A-1)\frac{\partial v_{t+\varepsilon}}{\partial t}+\left[(B+C_{1})t+2-A(t+\varepsilon)\right]\operatorname{tr}_{\omega_{t+\varepsilon}}(\omega_{P}),

using
\Theta\leq C_{4}\omega_{P}
and taking
\eta
sufficiently small.
We choose
A=A(\varepsilon)=(B+C_{1})+3/\varepsilon>2
so that
(B+C_{1})t+2-A(t+\varepsilon)\leq-1
.
Observe that
A\leq C/\varepsilon
for some uniform
C>0
.

Let
(x_{0},t_{0})
be a point at which
\alpha
reaches its maximum,
with
t_{0}>0
and
x_{0}\in X\setminus D
as explained above.
The maximum principle and Proposition
3.1
ensure that

\frac{\partial v_{t_{0}+\varepsilon}}{\partial t}+\operatorname{tr}_{\omega_{t_{0}+\varepsilon}}(\omega_{P})(x_{0})\leq C_{3}-(A-2)(n\log(t_{0}+\varepsilon)-C).

It then follows from (
3.1
) that

\log u(x_{0},t_{0})\leq C_{3}-(A-2)(n\log(t_{0}+\varepsilon)-C)+C_{2}\leq C(\varepsilon).

Since
\log|s|^{2}\leq 0
and
v_{t+\varepsilon}
is bounded from below by
\log(t+\varepsilon)
(uniformly in
j
),
we obtain

\alpha(x_{0},t_{0})\leq t_{0}C(\varepsilon)-A(\varepsilon)C_{5}\log(t_{0}+\varepsilon).

Thus, since
v_{t+\varepsilon}
is bounded from above thanks to Proposition
2.8
.

t\log u_{j}\leq t_{0}C(\varepsilon)-A(\varepsilon)C_{5}\log(\varepsilon)+A(\varepsilon)C_{6}-\eta\log|s|^{2}.

Taking
\varepsilon=t
we obtain

t\log u_{j}\leq C_{7}(1-\log t)-\tilde{C}_{5}\frac{\log t}{t}+\frac{\tilde{C}_{6}}{t}-\eta\log|s|^{2},

as
A\sim\frac{1}{t}
.
The desired inequality follows by letting
\eta\to 0
and
j\rightarrow+\infty
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.6
.

Recall that
\omega_{t}=t\beta_{t}
in
X\setminus D
.
It follows from Lemma
3.4
applied with

that
\beta_{t}\leq C_{t}\omega_{P}
.

shows that
\omega_{P}^{n}\leq C_{t}^{\prime}\beta_{t}^{n}

3.2.2.
Higher order estimates

We can now conclude the proof of our main result.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
(\omega_{t})_{t>0}
be the solution to the twisted Kähler-Ricci flow emanating from
T_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}[D_{i}]
.
Let
j(t)
denote the greatest index such that
m_{j(t)}>t
.
If
D=\cup D_{i}
is log-smooth, then
\omega_{t}
is a Kähler form in
X\setminus D
such that

\omega_{t}=\sum_{m_{i}>t}(m_{i}-t)[D_{i}]+\beta_{t},

where
\beta_{t}
is a Poincaré type metric in
X\setminus S_{t}
,
where
S_{t}=D_{1}\cup\cdots D_{j(t)}
. In particular,
\omega_{t}
has bounded curvature in
X\setminus S_{t}
.

Moreover
\omega_{t}
is a global Kähler form when
t>\lambda(\varphi_{0})
.

Proof.

We again work on each time interval
(t_{\ell},t_{\ell+1}]
.
Set
\tilde{m}:=m_{\ell+1}-\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}m_{j}
.
Suppressing the index
\ell
and shifting the interval to
[0,\tilde{m}]
, we use Proposition
2.8
to rewrite the flow on
[0,\tilde{m}]\times X\setminus\cup_{m_{j}\leq t_{\ell+1}}D_{j}
as

\left(\omega+tdd^{c}\rho_{P}-\sum_{j}(m_{j}-t)\Theta_{j}+dd^{c}v_{t}\right)^{n}=e^{\dot{v}_{t}+F}\omega_{P}^{n},

with

We now prove that

Taking the pull back by the quasi-coordinates
