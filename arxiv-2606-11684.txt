1.
Introduction

In 2014, Adachi, Iyama and Reiten
[
2
]
introduced
\tau
-tilting theory as a generalization of tilting theory from the viewpoint of mutation. From then on,
\tau
-tilting theory became popular in the representation theory of finite-dimensional algebras. In the
\tau
-tilting theory, support
\tau
-tilting modules are the most important objects. For support
\tau
-tilting modules over special algebras, we refer to
[
1
,
20
,
10
,
17
]
and the reference therein. For the infinitely generated case, that is, the silting modules, we refer to
[
3
]
. For the connection between
\tau
-tilting modules and homological conjectures, we refer to
[
8
,
27
]
.

For a finite-dimensional algebra
A
, the finitistic dimension of
A
is the supremum of the finite projective dimension of finitely generated modules. It is conjectured that the finitistic dimension of
A
is always finite. This is the famous finitistic dimension conjecture which is still open now. For the study of the finitistic conjecture, we refer to
[
12
,
16
,
19
,
21
,
26
]
and the references therein. In 2022, Gelinas
[
11
]
introduced a new homological notion delooping level to study the finitistic dimension. And he proved the following theorem
[
11
, Proposition 1.3]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

For a finite-dimensional algebra
A
, the finitistic dimension of
A^{op}
is bounded by the depth of
A
and the delooping level of
A
. That is, the depth of
A

\leq
the finitistic dimension of
A^{op}

\leq
the delooping level of
A
.

If the delooping level of
A
is finite for any algebra
A
, then by Theorem
1.1
the finitistic dimension conjecture is proved. Unfortunately, Kershaw and Rickard
[
18
]
constructed a counter-example for this. Moreover, Sen
[
24
]
computed the delooping level for Nakayama algebras. In 2025, Guo and Igusa
[
14
]
introduced the definitions of
k
-delooping level and derived delooping level and give a more precise bound for the finitistic dimension. Later Guo
[
13
]
showed that the derived delooping level is left and right symmetric. Chen and Hu
[
9
]
studied delooping level and derived delooping level in terms of functions. Recently, Chen
[
7
]
showed that the delooping level is not preserved under derived equivalence.

Since
A
is always a support
\tau
-tilting module in
\text{mod}A
, it is natural to ask: Is there a generalized version of Theorem
1.1
in term of support
\tau
-tilting modules? In this paper, we give a positive answer to the question and then apply our result to the finitistic dimension conjecture.

In what follows, let
A
be a finite-dimensional algebra over an algebraically closed field
K
and let
T\in\text{mod}A
be a support
\tau
-tilting module with
B=\text{End}_{A}T
. Denote by
\mathcal{C}=\text{Fac}T=T(S)=\text{Filt(Fac}S)
, where
S=S_{1}\bigoplus\cdots\bigoplus S_{m}
is a semi-brick and
S_{i}
is a brick. We first define the depth of
\mathcal{C}
relative to
T
(See Definition
3.1
for details) and then prove the following result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
A,T,B
and
\mathcal{C}
be as above. Then the depth of
\mathcal{C}
relative to
T

\leq
the finitistic dimension of
B^{op}
.

Keep
A,T,S,S_{i},B
as above. We give the following bijection between bricks in
\mathcal{C}
and simple modules in
\text{Sub}\mathbb{D}T
, which is partially shown in
[
4
]
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
A,T,S,S_{i}
and
B
be as above. Then the functor

\textup{Hom}_{A}(T,-):\textup{Fac}T\to\textup{Sub}\mathbb{D}T

induces a bijection between
\{S_{1},\dots,S_{m}\}
and
\{\text{simple modules in {Sub}}\mathbb{D}T\}
.

By using Theorem
1.3
, we are able to define the
k
-delooping level of
\mathcal{C}
(See Definition
4.4
for details) and give the following theorem.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
A,T,S,S_{i}
and
B
be as above. Let
k
be a positive integer. Then

(1)

The
k
-delooping level of
B

=
the
k
-delooping level of
\mathcal{C}
relative to
T
.

(2)

The finitistic dimension of
B^{op}

\leq
the delooping level of
\mathcal{C}
relative to
T
.

As a corollary of Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.4
, we get that a generalized version of Theorem
1.1
via support
\tau
-tilting modules.

It is natural to ask whether our Theorem
1.4
can be used for the study of the finitistic dimension conjecture. That is, which class of support
\tau
-tilting modules
T
with
B={\rm End}_{A}T
admits the property that the finitistic dimension of
B^{op}
is finite?

Denote by
{\rm Sub}T
the subcategory of
{\rm mod}A
cogenerated by
T
. We give a positive answer to the question above and prove the following result.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
A
be an algebra,
T\in{\rm mod}A
a support
\tau
-tilting module and
B={\rm End}_{A}T
. If
{\rm Fac}T\cap{\rm Sub}T
is of finite representation type, then the finitistic dimension of
B^{op}
is finite.

As an application of Theorem
1.5
, we can get the following corollary.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
A
be an algebra of finite representation type or a minimal representation infinite algebra,
T\in{\rm mod}A
a support
\tau
-tilting module and
B={\rm End}_{A}T
. Then the finitistic dimension of
B^{op}
is finite.

In this section we recall some basic facts on tilting modules,

We first recall the definition of torsion pairs from
[

]

Definition 2.1
.

Let
A
be an algebra. A pair
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
of full subcategories of
\textup{mod}A

(1)

\textup{Hom}_{A}(M,N)=0
for all

,
N\in\mathcal{F}
.

(2)

\textup{Hom}_{A}(M,-)|_{\mathcal{F}}=0
implies
M\in\mathcal{T}
.

(3)

\textup{Hom}_{A}(-,N)|_{\mathcal{T}}=0
implies
N\in\mathcal{F}
.

For a module
M
, denote by
|M|
the number of indecomposable direct summands up to isomorphisms. Denote by
{\rm pd}_{A}M
the projective dimension of
M
. We recall the definition of tilting modules from
[
15
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
A
be an algebra. A module
T\in{\rm mod}A
is called a tilting module if the following two conditions are satisfied:

(1)

{\rm pd}_{A}T\leq 1
.

(2)

\text{Ext}^{1}_{A}(T,T)=0
.

(3)

|T|=|A|
.

We also need the definition of a torsion pair from
[
5
]
.

Proposition 2.3

Let
A
be an algebra and

\mathcal{Y}(T_{A})=\{Y_{B}|\textup{Ext}_{B}^{1}(Y,\mathbb{D}T)=0\}=\{Y_{B}|\textup{Tor}_{1}^{B}(Y,T)=0\}.

Now we recall the tilting theorem from

Theorem 2.4

Let

In this section, we assume that
A
is an algebra and
T\in\text{mod}A
is a support
\tau
-tilting module. Denote by
\text{Fac}T=T(S)=\text{Filt(Fac}S)
, where
S=S_{1}\bigoplus\cdots\bigoplus S_{m}
is a semi-brick and
S_{i}
is a brick. Then we show the main result on the depth relative to

Let
A
be an algebra. Recall from
[
6
]
that the grade of

denoted by
\text{grade}M
is defined as the infimum of the integer

such that
\text{Ext}^{i}_{A}(M,A)\neq 0
. Recall from
[

that the depth of

In the following we give the definition of grade and depth relative to

Definition 3.1
.

Let
A
and
T
be as above. Denote by
\mathcal{C}=\text{Fac}T
.

(1)

The grade of

is defined as

\text{grade}_{T}M=\text{inf}\{i\geq 0|\text{Ext}^{i}_{A}(M,T)\neq 0\}.

(2)

The depth of
\mathcal{C}
is defined as
\text{depth}_{T}\mathcal{C}={\sup\limits_{1\leq i\leq m}}\text{grade}_{T}S_{i}

It is easy to see that the grade of

Lemma 3.3

Let

\text{findim}A=\sup\left\{\text{pd}_{A}M\mid M\in\text{mod}A,\ \text{pd}_{A}M<\infty\right\}.

Dually, we use findim

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
A
be an algebra and
T\in\textup{mod}A
be a support
\tau
-tilting module with

\textup{Fac}T=\textup{Filt(Fac}S)
, where
S=S_{1}\bigoplus\cdots\bigoplus S_{m}
is a semi-brick and
S_{i}
is a brick. Denote by

\mathcal{C}
relative to
T

\leq
the finitistic dimension of

, that is,
{\rm depth}_{T}\mathcal{C}\leq{\rm findim}B^{op}

Proof.

Let
T_{n}\to\cdots\to T_{1}\to T_{0}\to S_{i}\to 0\ \ (*)
be a minimal
T
-presentation of the brick
S_{i}
. We divide the proof into two cases.

(1)

If there exists a brick
S_{i}\in\text{Fac}T
such that
\text{Ext}^{j}_{A}(S_{i},T)=0
for all
j\geq 0
, then findim
B^{op}=+\infty
.

Applying
\text{Hom}_{A}(-,T)
to
(*)
, we get the following long exact sequence

0\to\text{Hom}_{A}(T_{0},T)\to\cdots\to\text{Hom}_{A}(T_{n-1},T)\xrightarrow{\delta_{n}^{*}}\text{Hom}_{A}(T_{n},T)\to\text{coker}(\delta_{n}^{*})\to 0\ \ (**)

Then by Lemma
3.3
one gets that
(**)

\text{coker}(\delta_{n}^{*})
and hence
\text{Ext}_{B}^{n}(\text{coker}(\delta_{n}^{*}),B)\neq 0
. Therefore the projective dimension of coker

and therefore findim

(2)

If
\prod_{j\geq 0}\text{Ext}_{A}^{j}(S_{i},T)\neq 0
holds for each brick

, then there exists minimal

such that
\text{Ext}^{t}_{A}(S_{i},T)\neq 0
. We show the projective dimension of
\text{Ext}^{t}_{A}(S_{i},T)=t
.

If

\text{Ext}^{0}_{A}(S_{i},T)\neq 0
, therefore the projective dimension of
\text{Hom}_{A}(S_{i},T)\geq 0
.
In the following, we show the assertion holds for

. Applying the functor
\text{Hom}_{A}(-,T)
to
(*)
, one gets the following long exact sequence

which is a minimal projective resolution of
\textup{Ext}^{t}_{A}(S_{i},T)
by Lemma

\text{Ext}^{t}_{A}(S_{i},T)=t
. In this case, we have shown that depth
{}_{T}\mathcal{C}\leq\text{findim}B^{op}
.∎

Throughout this section,

To define the delooping level relative to

Theorem 4.1
.

Let

and

and
\{\text{simple modules in {Sub}}\mathbb{D}T\}

Proof.

We divide the proof into two steps.

(1) We show that
\textup{Hom}_{A}(T,S_{i})
is simple. This is actually shown by Asai in
[
4
]
. Here we give a new proof.

On the contrary, suppose that
\textup{Hom}_{A}(T,S_{i})
is not simple. Since

is a brick and a direct summand of

, one gets an exact sequence

with

an indecomposable direct summand of

and

. Applying

to

On the other hand, one has the following exact sequence

0\to R\to\textup{Hom}_{A}(T,T_{i})\to S\to 0

with

simple in
\text{mod}B
.
We have the following commutative diagram

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Comparing

(2) We show for any

5.
Applications to the finitistic dimension conjecture

In this section, we apply Theorem
4.5
to the finitistic dimension conjecture. Throughout this section,
T=\oplus_{i=1}^{n}T_{i}
is a support
\tau
-tilting module with
T_{i}
indecomposable and
\mathcal{C}=\text{Fac}T=T(S)=\text{Filt(Fac}S)
, where
S\simeq S_{1}\bigoplus\cdots\bigoplus S_{m}
and
S_{i}
is a brick. Denote by
B=\text{End}_{A}T
.

For a positive integer
i
, denote by
\Omega^{i}_{T}(\mathcal{C})
the subcategory of the
i
-th syzygy of modules relative to
T
in
\mathcal{C}
and denote by
L_{i}
the set of indecomposable modules in
\Omega^{i}_{T}(\mathcal{C})
and indecomposable direct summands of
T
. We have the following lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
A
be an algebra,
T\in{\rm mod}A
a support
\tau
-tilting module and
\mathcal{C}=\textup{Fac}T
. If
L_{1}
is a finite set, then global
2
-delooping level of
\mathcal{C}
relative to
T
is finite, that is, global-
2
-
\textup{dell}_{T}\mathcal{C}<\infty
.

Proof.

It is obvious that
L_{1}\supseteq L_{2}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq L_{n}\supseteq\cdots
. Since there are finite number of elements in
L_{1}
, one gets there is an
n\geq 1
such that
L_{n}=L_{n+1}
and hence
L_{n+1}=L_{n+2}
by the definition of
L_{i}
. So one gets that
L_{n}=L_{n+2}
. Then for any
M\in\mathcal{C}
, one gets an
N\in\mathcal{C}
such that
\Omega^{n}_{T}(M)
is a direct summand of
\Omega^{n+2}_{T}(N)
, that is, the
2
-delooping level of
M
relative to
T
is no more than
n
. The assertion holds.
∎

To show the main theorem in this section, we also need the following lemma.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
A
be an algebra,
T\in{\rm mod}A
a support
\tau
-tilting module and
\mathcal{C}=\textup{Fac}T
. If
L_{1}
is a finite set, then the delooping level of
\mathcal{C}
relative to
T
is finite, that is,
{\rm dell}_{T}\mathcal{C}<\infty
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that for a module
M
in
\mathcal{C}
the delooping level of
M
relative to
T
is no more than the
2
-delooping level of
M
relative to
T
. By Lemma 5.1, we can assume that
2
-dell
{}_{T}M=n
, that is,
\Omega^{n}_{T}(M)
is a direct summand of
\Omega^{n+2}_{T}(N)
for some
N\in\mathcal{C}
. Then one gets that
\Omega^{n}_{T}(M)
is a direct summand of
\Omega_{T}^{n+1}(\Omega^{1}_{T}N)
. So one gets the delooping level of
S_{i}
is no more than
n
. By Definition
4.4
, the assertion holds.
∎

Now we are in a position to state the main result in this section.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
A
be an algebra,
T\in{\rm mod}A
a support
\tau
-tilting module and
B={\rm End}_{A}T
. If
{\rm Fac}T\cap{\rm Sub}T
is of finite representation type, then
{\rm findim}B^{op}<\infty
.

Proof.

It is not easy to show that
{\rm Fac}T\cap{\rm Sub}T={\rm add}L_{1}
. Since
{\rm Fac}T\cap{\rm Sub}T
is of finite representation type, one gets that
L_{1}
is a finite set. Then the assertion follows from Theorem
4.5

5.1

In the rest of this section we give another hint on the finitistic dimension conjecture via support
\tau
-tilting modules.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
A
be an algebra,
T\in{\rm mod}A
a support
\tau
-tilting module and
B={\rm End}_{A}T
. Let
\overline{A}=A/{\rm ann}T
.
Then
{\rm findim}B^{op}<\infty
if and only if so does
\overline{A}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.7
,
T
is a tilting module in
{\rm mod}\overline{A}
. Since
{\rm Hom}_{A}(T,T)={\rm Hom}_{\overline{A}}(T,T)=B
, one gets that
T
is also a tilting module in
{\rm mod}B^{op}
. Therefore one gets that
\overline{A}
and
B^{op}
are derived equivalent by
[
22
, Theorem 1.1]
. Then by
[
21
, Theorem 1.1]
, the assertion holds.
∎

Recall that an algebra
A
is called minimal representation infinite if it is of infinite representation type and every factor algebra of
A
is of finite representation type. We have the following corollary.

Corollary 5.7
.

Let
A
be a minimal representation infinite algebra,
T\in{\rm mod}A
a support
\tau
-tilting module and
B={\rm End}_{A}T
. Then
{\rm findim}B^{op}<\infty
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.7
,
T
is a tilting module in
{\rm mod}\overline{A}
and is also a tilting module in
{\rm mod}B^{op}
. Since
\overline{A}
is of finite representation type, the finitistic dimension of

5.6
, the assertion holds.
∎
