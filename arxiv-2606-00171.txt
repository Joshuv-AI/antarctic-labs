Dependence of two-particle azimuthal correlations on the forward rapidity gap width in \Pp ​ Pb \Pp\text{Pb} collisions at \sqrtsNN = 8.16 ​ \TeV \sqrtsNN=8.16\TeV


Abstract
One of the most striking features of relativistic heavy ion collisions is the presence of collective flow of thousands of produced particles. This flow can be characterized by the Fourier coefficients ( V n ​ Δ {V_{n\Delta}} ) of the azimuthal angular distributions of charged particles, and its existence can be explained by the formation of a quark gluon plasma, which behaves as a fluid. Surprisingly, the angular distributions of particles from very small systems such as proton-lead ( \Pp ​ Pb \Pp\text{Pb} ), proton-proton ( \Pp ​ \Pp \Pp\Pp ), electron-positron, and photon-proton ( \Pgg ​ \Pp \Pgg\Pp ) collisions also exhibit non-zero Fourier coefficients, raising the question of whether collective flow is present. This paper presents measurements of V n ​ Δ {V_{n\Delta}} from a sample of \Pp ​ Pb \Pp\text{Pb} events at \sqrtsNN = 8.16 ​ \TeV \sqrtsNN=8.16\TeV that are enriched in photon-lead ( \Pgg ​ Pb \Pgg\text{Pb} ) and pomeron-lead ( ​I​P Pb ) interactions by requiring no particles in the proton-going region. Measurements are made as a function of the forward rapidity gap width (the rapidity range in which no particles are found), the transverse momentum of the particles, and the multiplicity of particles in the event. The results are compared to previous measurements of \Pp ​ \Pp \Pp\Pp , \Pp ​ Pb \Pp\text{Pb} , and \Pgg ​ \Pp + ​I​P \Pp \Pgg\Pp+\text{{~\!I\!P}}\Pp events as well as modern event generators.

Introduction

Two-particle azimuthal correlations can provide insights into the so-called collectivity of systems produced in high-energy collisions. Collectivity refers to the correlations among multiple particles in the final state of these interactions. These correlations can help in understanding the system produced in the collision and its hydrodynamic properties. The evidence for collectivity includes both long-range rapidity correlations (

Azimuthal correlations between particle pairs can be quantified via their Fourier decomposition,
\rd N^{\text{pair}}/\rd{\Delta\phi}\propto 1+\sum_{n}2{V_{n\Delta}}\cos(n{\Delta\phi})

{V_{n\Delta}}
are the two-particle Fourier coefficients,
N^{\text{pair}}

{\Delta\phi}

v_{n}

v_{n}=\sqrt{{V_{n\Delta}}}

[

In recent years, collective behavior has been found in small systems such as those produced in

Event selections that suppress multiparton interactions and modify color reconnection processes provide an important discriminator between scenarios based on a collective final-state response and those dominated by initial-state correlations. Selecting events with no particles in a portion of the proton-going
\eta

Therefore, these events allow the study of azimuthal correlations in samples that are complementary to the inclusive measurements. The forward rapidity gap width (
{\Delta\eta^{\mathrm{F}}}
) is defined as the width of the rapidity region (starting at
\eta=3

\Pp\text{Pb}

The
\Pp\text{Pb}

\sqrtsNN=8.16\TeV

[
35

This asymmetry enhances semi-exclusive events, in which the proton remains intact, or dissociates into a low-mass state that escapes undetected, while the lead nucleus breaks up. The sample contains diffractive events, where the
\Pp\text{Pb}

​I​P

[
64

\Pgg\text{Pb}

The sample analyzed here was also studied in Ref.

Two types of events were selected: those in which the final-state particles mostly go in the direction of the outgoing proton, and those in which most of the activity is in the direction of the outgoing lead nucleus. The former sample was used to measure two-particle correlations in
\Pgg\Pp

0.2
Experimental apparatus

The central feature of the CMS apparatus is a superconducting solenoid of 6 m internal diameter, providing a magnetic field of 3.8 T. Within the solenoid volume is the silicon tracker, a lead tungstate crystal electromagnetic calorimeter, and a brass and scintillator hadron calorimeter, each composed of a barrel (EB and HB) and two endcap (EE and HE) sections, and covering the pseudorapidity range
\abs{\eta}<3.0
. The silicon tracker measures charged particles within the range
\abs{\eta}<2.5
; it consists of 1 440 silicon pixel and 15 148 silicon strip detector modules and provides an impact parameter resolution of
\approx 15\mum
. The event selection for this analysis exploits the HF detectors (
\text{HF}+
and
\text{HF}-
) in the
3.0<\abs{\eta}<5.2
region, which use quartz fibers embedded in a steel absorber
[
40
]
.

The analysis in the region
\abs{\eta}<3.0
is based upon objects produced by the CMS particle-flow (PF) algorithm
[
47
]
, which reconstructs and identifies final-state particles with an optimized combination of information from the various elements of the CMS detector.

A more detailed description of the CMS detector, together with a definition of the relevant kinematic variables, can be found in Ref.
[
35
]
. The coordinate system used in this paper is defined as follows:
x
points in the direction of the center of the LHC ring,
y
is upwards and
z
is along the beam line according to the right-hand rule. The
\eta
is defined such as to have positive values when
z>0
.

0.3
Data samples

The analysis is performed using data recorded by CMS during the LHC
\Pp\text{Pb}
run in 2016 corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 174.5
\nbinv

[
49
]
. The beam energies were 6.5
\TeV
for the protons and 2.56
\TeV
per nucleon for the lead nuclei in the laboratory frame, resulting in a center-of-mass energy per nucleon pair (
\sqrtsNN
) of 8.16
\TeV
. The data include samples with the proton going towards the positive and towards the negative
\eta
direction. As a result of the energy difference between the colliding beams, the proton-nucleus center-of-mass frame for a
\Pp\text{Pb}
collision is not at rest with respect to the laboratory frame. Massless particles emitted at
\eta_{\text{cm}}=0
in the
\Pp\text{Pb}
center-of-mass frame are detected at
\eta=+0.465
in the laboratory frame.

The data were selected online with a two level trigger system consisting of the level-1, based on custom hardware processors, and the high-level trigger (HLT), running a version of the full event reconstruction software optimized for fast processing
[
51
,
48
]
.

Monte Carlo samples are used for comparing models to the measurements. Samples are generated with
epos-lhc

[
63
]
, and
\PYTHIA
8.3.10 event generators
[
32
]
. The
epos-lhc
generator is based on the Gribov–Regge model
[
63
]
, which includes nondiffractive (ND), central-diffractive (CD), single-diffractive (SD), and double-diffractive (DD) events, but no photo-induced processes; collective effects are simulated. The
epos-lhc
samples are subsequently processed with
\GEANTfour
to simulate the response of the CMS detector
[
11
]
. Samples were produced with the proton beam in both directions. The
\PYTHIA
8.3.10 generator is based on the
Angantyr
model
[
31
]
. Separate samples of ND, SD, DD events were produced. The generator does not include collective effects nor photo-induced processes. The
\PYTHIA
8.3.10 samples were only used to compare to data corrected to the generator level and, therefore, were not processed to detector level using
\GEANTfour
.

0.4
Event selection

The event selection and the definition of the data samples follow closely those of Ref.
[
54
]
. Events are selected by the level-1 trigger if energy deposits are present in at least one of the HF calorimeters above a threshold of approximately 1
\GeV
. The presence of at least one track with
\pt>0.4\GeV
in the pixel tracker is required at the HLT. The track reconstruction is performed online as part of the HLT trigger with reconstruction algorithms identical to those used offline
[
42
]
. For each event, the reconstructed vertex with the highest number of associated tracks is selected as the primary vertex (PV).

The reconstructed PV is required to be within 15
\cm
of the nominal interaction point along the beam axis
(z)
and within 0.15
\cm
in the transverse plane. High-purity tracks
[
42
]
are used. At least two reconstructed tracks with
\abs{\eta}<2.4
,
\pt>0.4\GeV
are required to be associated with the PV. Beam related background is suppressed by rejecting events for which less than 25% of all reconstructed tracks pass the track selection criteria mentioned above. Additionally, the impact parameter significance of reconstructed tracks with respect to the PV in the longitudinal and transverse directions is required to be less than 3 standard deviations. Finally, the relative uncertainty of the track
\pt
is required to be less than 10%.

A forward rapidity gap is a region with low detector activity and is defined as follows. The region
-3.0<\eta<3.0
is divided into 12 bins that are 0.5 units wide. Energy thresholds are assigned to each bin such that at least 99.7% of the signals due to detector noise or beam gas events
[
54
]
are below the threshold. These are 6
\GeV
for the central region
\abs{\eta}<2.5
, for which all PF candidates are considered, and 13.4
\GeV
for
\abs{\eta}>2.5
for which only neutral hadronic PF candidates are considered due to noise in the electromagnetic calorimeter. A bin is considered empty if the total energy from all PF candidates is below the threshold. In addition, for the central region
\abs{\eta}<2.5
, no high-purity tracks with
\pt>200\MeV
are allowed. The
{\Delta\eta^{\mathrm{F}}}
variable is then defined as the difference between
\eta=3.0
and the higher edge of the first nonempty
\eta
bin. The definition is reversed when the direction of the proton beam is towards negative values of
\eta
.

Events are selected as follows. The hadronic forward
\text{HF}+
calorimeter on the positive
\eta
side is required to have all towers with an energy deposit below 2
\GeV
. The
\text{HF}-
calorimeter on the negative side is required to have at least one tower with an energy deposit above 10
\GeV
. Additionally, the
\eta
bin
[+2.5,+3.0)
is required to be empty, reflecting the lack of activity in the direction of the outgoing proton. Events with a presence of a high-purity track with
\eta>2.5
and
\pt>200\MeV
were removed since their effect on
{\Delta\eta^{\mathrm{F}}}
is not well understood. This selection is referred to in the following as the “nominal selection” and the resulting sample as the “nominal sample”. The definitions are reversed in
\eta
when the direction of the proton beam is towards negative values of
\eta
. The analysis is performed in different
{\Delta\eta^{\mathrm{F}}}
bins from 0.5 to 2.5 in intervals 0.5 units wide and a final bin from 2.5 onwards.

In Ref.
[
54
]
it was found that the
epos-lhc
generator predicts a
{\Delta\eta^{\mathrm{F}}}
spectrum with a shape reasonably consistent with that of the data but with a different yield. The results suggest only a small nondiffractive contribution when the rapidity gap cut
{\Delta\eta^{\mathrm{F}}}>2.5
is applied; an additional set of measurements with such a cut is carried out here. This selection is referred to as “diffraction-enhanced selection” in the following, and the resulting sample is obtained by applying the nominal selection but restricting the events to the last
{\Delta\eta^{\mathrm{F}}}
bin. For events with rapidity gaps on the negative side (not analyzed here), MC studies indicate that they are dominated by
\Pgg\Pp

[

Analysis technique

To ensure high tracking efficiency, only tracks within
\abs{\eta}<2.4
and
0.3<\pt<3\GeV
are used in the analysis. The
N_{\text{trk}}^{\text{offline}}
is taken as the number of tracks from the PV with
\abs{\eta}<2.4
and
\pt>0.4\GeV
. The analysis techniques for two-particle correlations are identical to those used in past CMS measurements
[
45
,
41
,
39
,
55
]
, and are described below. Because of the limited
N_{\text{trk}}^{\text{offline}}
range for the selected events, no low-multiplicity subtraction technique is implemented
[
51
]
.

For each bin in the nominal and diffraction-enhanced selection, tracks tagged as “trigger particles” are those whose
\pt
, referred to as
\pt^{\text{trig}}
, is within a given range (discussed below). The number of trigger particles in the event is denoted by
N_{\text{trig}}
. Particle pairs are formed by associating each trigger particle with the remaining charged tracks within a specified
\pt\equiv\pt^{\text{assoc}}
interval. For the present analysis, identical ranges are used for
\pt^{\text{trig}}
and
\pt^{\text{assoc}}
. Two different
\pt
ranges are studied,
0.3<\pt<3
and
1<\pt<3\GeV
, identical to the ones used in previous studies of the ridge
[
41
]
and of correlated azimuthal anisotropies
[
51
]
in
\Pp\text{Pb}
collisions. For
1<\pt<3\GeV
, only the bin
2\leq N_{\text{trk}}^{\text{offline}}<40
is studied.

The two-dimensional (2D) correlation function is defined as

\frac{1}{N_{\text{trig}}}\frac{\rd^{2}N^{\text{pair}}}{\rd{\Delta\eta}\rd{\Delta\phi}}=B(0,0)\frac{S({\Delta\eta},{\Delta\phi})}{B({\Delta\eta},{\Delta\phi})},

(1)

where
{\Delta\eta}
and
{\Delta\phi}
are the differences between the
\eta
and
\phi
values of the trigger particle and the other track of the pair. The quantity
N^{\text{pair}}
is the number of pairs. The same-event pair distribution,
S({\Delta\eta},{\Delta\phi})
, represents the yield of particle pairs from the event under consideration. The mixed-event pair distribution
B({\Delta\eta},{\Delta\phi})
is constructed by pairing the trigger particles in each event with the associated charged particles from 100 different randomly selected events in the same 0.5
\cm
wide
z
vertex range and from the same track multiplicity class. The same-event and mixed-event pair distributions are first calculated for each event, and then averaged over all the events within the track multiplicity class. The mixed-event distribution is normalized to the sum of background events. The ratio
B(0,0)/B({\Delta\eta},{\Delta\phi})
accounts for pair acceptance effects, with
B(0,0)
representing the mixed-event associated yield for both particles of the pair going in the same direction and thus having maximum pair acceptance.

A correction for tracking inefficiency is applied by weighting each charged particle by the inverse of the track reconstruction efficiency obtained in Ref.
[
36
]
, so that these distributions can be compared with generator-level simulations. This correction is applied to the
N_{\text{trk}}^{\text{offline}}
distribution in the diffraction-enhanced sample. The new corrected quantity is called
N_{\text{ch}}^{\abs{\eta}<2.4,\,\pt>0.3\GeV}
and the average within a
N_{\text{trk}}^{\text{offline}}
bin is used to present the final results for the diffraction-enhanced sample.

The charged particle azimuthal anisotropy harmonics can be extracted via a Fourier decomposition of long-range two-particle
{\Delta\phi}
correlation functions, obtained by averaging the 2D two-particle correlation function over
\abs{{\Delta\eta}}>2
(to remove short-range correlations, such as those from jet fragmentation
[
17
]
). The fitted functional form is:

\frac{1}{N_{\text{trig}}}\frac{\rd N^{\text{pair}}}{\rd{\Delta\phi}}=\frac{N_{\text{assoc}}}{2\pi}[1+\sum_{n}2{V_{n\Delta}}\cos(n{\Delta\phi})],

(2)

where
{V_{n\Delta}}
are the Fourier coefficients and
N_{\text{assoc}}
represents the average number of pairs per trigger particle for a given (
\pt^{\text{trig}}
,
\pt^{\text{assoc}}
) bin. In this analysis, we included terms up to
n=3
, because including higher order
{V_{n\Delta}}
terms has a negligible effect on the results of the Fourier fit.

0.6
Systematic uncertainties

In this section, the sources of the systematic uncertainties in the
{V_{n\Delta}}
Fourier coefficients are discussed. The systematic uncertainties are evaluated as a function of
{\Delta\eta^{\mathrm{F}}}
and
N_{\text{ch}}^{\abs{\eta}<2.4,\,\pt>0.3\GeV}
by varying the way the coefficients are determined.

The general procedure for determining the systematic uncertainties is described in Ref.
[
30
]
. The systematic uncertainty is considered to be significant (and taken into account) when it affects the result by more than its statistical uncertainty, having considered the correlation between the sample modified by the systematic variation and the nominal sample.

The systematic effects considered are the following:

•

The effect of tracking inefficiency and misreconstructed track rate is studied by varying the track quality requirements. The selection thresholds on the significance of the transverse and longitudinal track impact parameters are varied from 2 to 5 standard deviations. In addition, the relative track
\pt
uncertainty is varied from 5–10%.

•

The sensitivity to the PV position along the beam axis
(z_{\text{vtx}})
is studied by comparing events with different
z_{\text{vtx}}
locations from
-15
to
+15\cm
.

•

The possible contamination from overlapping
\Pp\text{Pb}
collisions in a single event, referred to as pileup, is studied by analyzing two subsets of data with high and low pileup, respectively, corresponding to high and low luminosity periods. On average the high-pileup sample has a pileup of 0.25 while the low-pileup has 0.10.

•

The trigger efficiency decreases substantially for
N_{\text{trk}}^{\text{offline}}<10
. To study the effect of this decrease, a parallel sample is studied with events weighted by the inverse of the efficiency, which depends on
N_{\text{trk}}^{\text{offline}}
. The full difference of the results with and without the reweighting is taken as the uncertainty.

•

When the proton beam direction changes, the detectors used for the measurement are different. To study this effect, the results obtained for the two proton beam directions are compared.

For
{V_{2\Delta}}
, the pileup and the change of beam direction are the largest contributors to the systematic uncertainty for the nominal and diffraction-enhanced samples. For
{V_{1\Delta}}
, the pileup and trigger efficiency are the main contributors. This can be observed in Table
0.6
, which shows the unweighted average (across all
{\Delta\eta^{\mathrm{F}}}
bins) of the systematic uncertainty contribution by source for the nominal selection. The individual systematic uncertainties are added in quadrature and amount to about twice the statistical uncertainty. The total uncertainty is obtained by summing the statistical and systematic uncertainties in quadrature. The resulting uncertainties for the diffraction-enhanced sample are of the same order of magnitude as those obtained in
\Pgg\Pp
interactions
[
55
]
for
N_{\text{ch}}^{\abs{\eta}<2.4,\,\pt>0.3\GeV}<5
.

0.8
Summary

Long-range azimuthal
(\phi)
two-particle correlations have been studied for in proton-lead (
\Pp\text{Pb}
) events at
\sqrtsNN=8.16\TeV
as a function of the forward rapidity gap width (
{\Delta\eta^{\mathrm{F}}}
) and the charged-particle multiplicity (
N_{\text{ch}}^{\abs{\eta}<2.4,\,\pt>0.3\GeV}
), where
\pt
is the transverse momentum. Varying
{\Delta\eta^{\mathrm{F}}}
provides a continuous handle on the degree of exclusivity of the selected
\Pp\text{Pb}
events and therefore complements inclusive small-system correlation measurements. Events have been selected by requiring energy deposition in one of the forward calorimeters and no activity in the forward calorimeter on the opposite side. In addition, we require no energy in the region
2.5<\eta<3
, favoring events with activity in the direction of the outgoing Pb beam. For events with
{\Delta\eta^{\mathrm{F}}}>2.5
, the fraction of diffractive events is enhanced, and these events were further studied as a function of charged particle multiplicity. The average track multiplicity is lower in the diffractive sample than for inclusive
\Pp\text{Pb}
events, but larger than for photon-proton (
\Pgg\Pp
) events. The slope of the multiplicity distribution becomes steeper with increasing
{\Delta\eta^{\mathrm{F}}}
. The mean track multiplicity
\langle N_{\text{trk}}^{\text{offline}}\rangle
decreases by 48% when
{\Delta\eta^{\mathrm{F}}}
changes from
0.5<{\Delta\eta^{\mathrm{F}}}<1.0
to
{\Delta\eta^{\mathrm{F}}}>2.5
. The two-dimensional two-particle correlation functions have been averaged over
\abs{{\Delta\eta}}>2
and the resulting
{\Delta\phi}
distributions have been studied in terms of their Fourier coefficients
{V_{1\Delta}}
and
{V_{2\Delta}}
, as a function of
{\Delta\eta^{\mathrm{F}}}
and
N_{\text{ch}}^{\abs{\eta}<2.4,\,\pt>0.3\GeV}
.

The measured values of
{V_{1\Delta}}
are negative and decrease with
{\Delta\eta^{\mathrm{F}}}
, while the central values of
{V_{2\Delta}}
increase monotonically with
{\Delta\eta^{\mathrm{F}}}
, although they are consistent with zero within uncertainties in two of the bins. The results have been compared with the predictions of the
\PYTHIA
8.3.10 model, which does not include collective effects, and with
epos-lhc
model, which includes such effects. The measured values of the elliptic flow coefficient (
v_{2}
) are provided for the bins
[0.5,1)
,
[1,1.5)
,
[2,2.5)
. In the other bins, the
v_{2}
results are consistent with zero. In these bins, 95% confidence level upper limits have been determined; they are stable across the
{\Delta\eta^{\mathrm{F}}}
and
N_{\text{ch}}^{\abs{\eta}<2.4,\,\pt>0.3\GeV}
regions studied, indicating comparable sensitivity in all bins, and reach values up to 0.13 for the most inclusive
N_{\text{trk}}^{\text{offline}}
bin. The values of the upper limits are similar to the values measured in
\Pgg\Pp
events.

These results constrain scenarios in which ridge-like correlations are expected to persist universally across small systems independent of event topology. They also provide a reference for models that predict reduced collective response when the multiparton interactions and color reconnection processes are suppressed.
