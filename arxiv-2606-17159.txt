Superconductivity from emergent dipolar interactions in a fractionalized Fermi liquid Preprint: APS/123-QED
Mina-Lou Schleith, Arthur Bril, Nick Bultinck

Abstract
Starting from the spin-fermion model or Hertz-Millis theory describing electrons coupled to anti-ferromagnetic spin fluctuations we develop a theory to describe the transition from a fractionalized Fermi liquid into a d x 2 − y 2 d_{x^{2}-y^{2}} superconductor. We focus on small electron doping on top of the half-filled state. The doped electrons enter the system as spinon-chargon bound states, which form a small, reconstructed Fermi surface. The bound states are neutral under the emergent U(1) gauge symmetry of the fractionalized Fermi liquid, but interact via a dipolar two-body potential. We show that because of the projective action of translation symmetry on the spinons and chargons, the Fourier components of this repulsive dipolar interaction are peaked at the anti-ferromagnetic wave vector, thereby providing a robust microscopic mechanism for d x 2 − y 2 d_{x^{2}-y^{2}} pairing in a fractionalized metal.

II
Rotating reference frame theory

In contrast to the hole-doped cuprates, the physics of the electron-doped cuprates seems to be completely determined by anti-ferromagnetic (AFM) spin fluctuations. We therefore start from an effective theory with Euclidean action
S=S_{b}+S_{f}

(2)

\displaystyle-t\sum_{\langle{\mathbf{r}},{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}\rangle}(\bar{c}_{\mathbf{r}}c_{{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}}+h.c.)-t^{\prime}\sum_{\langle\langle{\mathbf{r}},{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}\rangle\rangle}(\bar{c}_{\mathbf{r}}c_{{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}}+h.c.)\bigg]

\displaystyle c_{\mathbf{r}}

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle c_{{\mathbf{r}}+{\mathbf{e}}_{x/y}}

\displaystyle{\mathbf{n}}_{\mathbf{r}}

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle-{\mathbf{n}}_{{\mathbf{r}}+{\mathbf{e}}_{x/y}}

(4)

We assume that the pseudogap region of the electron-doped phase diagram corresponds to a fractionalized Fermi liquid, where Luttinger’s theorem is violated due to the presence of non-trivial topological order. To describe this topological metal we first fractionalize the bosonic field

\displaystyle S_{\psi z}

The most straightforward way to express the bosonic part of the action in terms of the spinon fields is via a continuum approximation, as this allows us to exactly rewrite it as

\displaystyle S_{b,\text{cont}}

12

The fractionalized Fermi liquid that we consider in this work is realized by taking the spinons to be gapped, and the U(1) gauge field to be in its deconfined phase.

III
Spinon-chargon bound states

We now consider the half-filled state with gapped spinons and chargons, and imagine injecting a single electron into the system. This will result in the simultaneous creation of a spinon and chargon, which interact strongly via the U(1) gauge field. Our goal is to, within certain approximations, study the bound states formed by this spinon-chargon pair. We will reformulate the problem in the Hamiltonian formalism, where the effect of the U(1) gauge field is to introduce a Coulomb interaction between the chargon and spinon. For this first step we will closely follow the analysis of Ref.
23
.

III.1
Coulomb interaction

The action contains no bare kinetic term for the U(1) gauge field. To introduce dynamics for the gauge field, we assume that the spinon mass is significantly smaller than the chargon mass, and use the spinon polarization to generate a dynamical term

\displaystyle S_{a}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{q}a_{\mu}(q)\Pi(q)\left[\delta_{\mu\nu}-\frac{q_{\mu}q_{\nu}}{q^{2}}\right]a_{\nu}(-q)

(17)

Here we have introduced the notation
q=(\Omega,v{\mathbf{q}})
. To get an expression for
\Pi(q)
we start from the continuum action in Eq. (
17
) and do the standard saddle-point analysis
29
, i.e. impose
|z|^{2}=1
via a Lagrange multiplier, and then fix the Lagrange multiplier to its saddle-point value to generate a non-zero mass
m
for the spinons. Next one can calculate the standard one-loop paramagnetic and diamagnetic contributions to the spinon polarization to find
23

\Pi(q)=\frac{q^{2}+4m^{2}}{8\pi v^{2}|q|}\arctan{\frac{|q|}{2m}}-\frac{m}{4\pi v^{2}},

(18)

To proceed we will work in the Coulomb gauge, and only consider the density-density interaction mediated by
a_{0}
. The current-current interaction mediated by the transverse photon will be ignored. As
\Pi(q)=q^{2}/16\pi v^{2}m
for
|q|\ll m
, the
a_{0}
propagator becomes frequency independent at low energies and momenta. We will study the bound state problem in the Hamiltonian formalism, for which we replace the complete polarization (so not only for small
|q|
) by a frequency-independent expression by setting
\Omega=0
, such that it realizes an instantaneous interaction, which is the Coulomb interaction for this system.

Next we introduce
\pi
, the field canonically conjugate to
z
, and the mode decompositions

\displaystyle z_{{\mathbf{q}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\omega_{{\mathbf{q}}}\chi}}(b_{{\mathbf{q}}}+\mathrm{i}\sigma^{y}\bar{a}_{-{\mathbf{q}}})

(19)

\displaystyle\pi_{\mathbf{q}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\sqrt{\frac{\omega_{\mathbf{q}}\chi}{2}}(b_{\mathbf{q}}-\mathrm{i}\sigma^{y}\bar{a}_{-{\mathbf{q}}})\,,

(20)

where
\omega_{\mathbf{q}}=\sqrt{v{\mathbf{q}}^{2}+m^{2}}
is the spinon dispersion. Ignoring the transverse gauge field component, the spinon action can be written in terms of the spinon mode operators as

\displaystyle S_{b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\beta}\mathrm{d}\tau\sum_{\mathbf{q}}\bigg[\bar{b}_{\mathbf{q}}\partial_{\tau}b_{\mathbf{q}}+\bar{a}_{\mathbf{q}}\partial_{\tau}a_{\mathbf{q}}+\omega_{\mathbf{q}}(\bar{a}_{\mathbf{q}}a_{\mathbf{q}}+\bar{b}_{\mathbf{q}}b_{\mathbf{q}})

(21)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\frac{\mathrm{i}}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{{\mathbf{q}}^{\prime}}a_{0}({\mathbf{q}}^{\prime})(\bar{a}_{{\mathbf{q}}}a_{{\mathbf{q}}+{\mathbf{q}}^{\prime}}-\bar{b}_{\mathbf{q}}b_{{\mathbf{q}}+{\mathbf{q}}^{\prime}})\,,

where
N
is the number of sites in the system. After bringing the action in this form, it is clear that integrating out
a_{0}
produces a Coulomb interaction

H_{C}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{{\mathbf{q}}}V_{\mathbf{q}}\hat{\rho}_{\mathbf{q}}\hat{\rho}_{-{\mathbf{q}}}\,,

(22)

where the interaction potential is given by

V_{\mathbf{q}}=\Pi(0,v{\mathbf{q}})^{-1}=\left[\frac{v^{2}{\mathbf{q}}^{2}+4m^{2}}{8\pi v^{3}|{\mathbf{q}}|}\arctan\frac{v|{\mathbf{q}}|}{2m}-\frac{m}{4\pi v^{2}}\right]^{-1}\,,

(23)

and
\rho_{\mathbf{q}}
is the gauge charge density:

\hat{\rho}_{\mathbf{q}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{{\mathbf{q}}^{\prime}}\left[\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}_{{\mathbf{q}}}\sigma^{z}\hat{\psi}_{{\mathbf{q}}+{\mathbf{q}}^{\prime}}+\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{{\mathbf{q}}}\hat{a}_{{\mathbf{q}}+{\mathbf{q}}^{\prime}}-\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{q}}\hat{b}_{{\mathbf{q}}+{\mathbf{q}}^{\prime}}\right]

(24)

As the interaction potential was obtained using a continuum approximation for the spinons, we need to restore the periodicity under shifts by reciprocal lattice vectors. We do this via the replacement
|\bm{q}|\to 2\sqrt{\sin^{2}(q_{x}/2)+\sin^{2}(q_{y}/2)}
. We also add a momentum-independent shift to the potential to tune the on-site part of the interaction, which is not expected to be faithfully described by the long-wavelength effective theory. Even though we used many approximations to get at the final Coulomb potential, its precise form will actually not matter for the main results presented below, as they are all fixed by the projective symmetry action of the problem. We nevertheless use the spinon polarization in order to get (1) a realistic order of magnitude for the interaction, (2) the correct trends in the parametric dependence on
v
and
m
, and (3) the characteristic
1/{\mathbf{q}}^{2}
divergence at small
{\mathbf{q}}
.

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{0}

{\mathbf{q}}

\displaystyle\hat{f}^{\dagger}_{{\mathbf{q}}^{\prime},n}=

Here we have left the spin index implicit. This bound state is of exactly the same form as in Eq. (

), provided that we define

IV
Dipolar interaction

Having obtained the spinon-chargon bound states, we now determine the interaction between a pair of such bound states. If we write this interaction as

\hat{V}=\frac{1}{2N}\sum_{\bm{q}_{1}\bm{q}_{2}\bm{q}}\sum_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}V_{\bm{q}_{1},\bm{q}_{2};\bm{q}}:\hat{f}_{\bm{q}_{1}+\bm{q},\sigma}^{\dagger}\hat{f}_{\bm{q}_{1},\sigma}\hat{f}_{\bm{q}_{2}-\bm{q},\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\hat{f}_{\bm{q}_{2},\sigma^{\prime}}:\,,

(35)

with
f^{\dagger}_{{\mathbf{q}},\sigma}
the creation operator of a bound state with momentum
{\mathbf{q}}
and spin
\sigma
in the lowest-energy band, then our goal is to determine
V_{{\mathbf{q}}_{1},{\mathbf{q}}_{2};{\mathbf{q}}}
. For this we first invert Eqs. (
32
) and (
33
) to obtain (with spin indices implicit)

\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}_{+,{\mathbf{k}}}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{-{\mathbf{k}}+{\mathbf{q}}}=\sum_{n}\alpha^{n}_{\mathbf{q}}({\mathbf{k}})\hat{f}^{\dagger}_{{\mathbf{q}},n}+\beta^{n}_{{\mathbf{q}}+{\mathbf{Q}}}({\mathbf{k}}+{\mathbf{Q}})\hat{f}^{\dagger}_{{\mathbf{q}}+{\mathbf{Q}},n}

(36)

In this expression we have used that the wave functions are real. We now project in the lowest bound-state band by truncating the sum over
n
, and only keeping the contribution from the band of interest:

\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}_{+,{\mathbf{k}}}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{-{\mathbf{k}}+{\mathbf{q}}}\rightarrow\alpha_{\mathbf{q}}({\mathbf{k}})\hat{f}^{\dagger}_{{\mathbf{q}}}+\beta_{{\mathbf{q}}+{\mathbf{Q}}}({\mathbf{k}}+{\mathbf{Q}})\hat{f}^{\dagger}_{{\mathbf{q}}+{\mathbf{Q}}}

(37)

For the other bilinear we similarly find

\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}_{-,{\mathbf{k}}}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{-{\mathbf{k}}+{\mathbf{q}}}\rightarrow\alpha_{\mathbf{q}}({\mathbf{k}})\hat{f}^{\dagger}_{{\mathbf{q}}}-\beta_{{\mathbf{q}}+{\mathbf{Q}}}({\mathbf{k}}+{\mathbf{Q}})\hat{f}^{\dagger}_{{\mathbf{q}}+{\mathbf{Q}}}

(38)

As sketched in Fig.

A
, two bound states interact via a sum of four Coulomb interactions: two repulsive interactions between spinons or chargons with the same gauge charge, and two attractive interactions between opposite gauge charges. After acting with the Coulomb interactions on the state
f^{\dagger}_{{\mathbf{q}}_{1}}f^{\dagger}_{{\mathbf{q}}_{2}}|0\rangle
, we can project it back in the lowest bound-state band by using Eqs. (
37

V_{\bm{q}_{1},\bm{q}_{2};\bm{q}}=V_{\bm{q}+{\mathbf{Q}}}\lambda(\bm{q}_{1},\bm{q}_{1}+\bm{q})\lambda(\bm{q}_{2},\bm{q}_{2}-\bm{q})

(39)

The detailed derivation is given in the appendix. Note that the interaction potential is shifted by
{\mathbf{Q}}
, which implies that it now diverges at the AFM wavevector. The form factors in the bound state interaction are given by

\displaystyle\lambda(\bm{q}_{1},\bm{q}_{1}+\bm{q})

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\bm{k}}\bigg[(\alpha_{\bm{q}_{1}+\bm{q}}(\bm{k}+\bm{q})-\alpha_{\bm{q}_{1}+\bm{q}}(\bm{k}+\bm{Q}))\beta_{\bm{q}_{1}}(\bm{k})

\displaystyle+(\beta_{\bm{q}_{1}+\bm{q}}(\bm{k}+\bm{q})-\beta_{\bm{q}_{1}+\bm{q}}(\bm{k}+\bm{Q}))\alpha_{\bm{q}_{1}}(\bm{k})\bigg]

From this expression we see that
\lambda({\mathbf{q}}_{1},{\mathbf{q}}_{1}+{\mathbf{Q}})=0
. Expanding around
\bm{q}=\bm{Q}
, at lowest order the interaction takes on a dipolar form

V_{\bm{q}_{1},\bm{q}_{2};\bm{q}}=16\pi m\frac{(\bm{q}-\bm{Q})\cdot\bm{d}(\bm{q}_{1})\,(\bm{q}-\bm{Q})\cdot\bm{d}(\bm{q}_{2})}{(\bm{q}-\bm{Q})^{2}},

(40)

where
{\mathbf{d}}({\mathbf{q}}_{1})
is an effective dipole moment, given in terms of the bound state wavefunctions by

\displaystyle\bm{d}(\bm{q}_{1})=2\sum_{\bm{k}}\bigg[

\displaystyle\nabla_{\bm{k}}\alpha_{\bm{q}_{1}+\bm{Q}}(\bm{k}+\bm{Q})\beta_{\bm{q}_{1}}(\bm{k})

(41)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{k}}\beta_{\bm{q}_{1}+\bm{Q}}(\bm{k}+\bm{Q})\alpha_{\bm{q}_{1}}(\bm{k})\bigg]

The effective dipole moment is plotted in Fig.

The
{\mathbf{Q}}
-shift in the interaction potential and the expression for the form factors follow directly from the sign structure of the bound state wave function in Eq. (
32

V
d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}
-wave superconductivity

As a final step we consider a finite density of spinon-chargon bound states doped in the half-filled state. We take the bound states to be rigid and given by the solution of the two-particle problem – an approximation that should become exact in the dilute limit. As the bound states are fermions, they will form electron pockets centered at the
X
-points in the Brillouin zone.

We now consider the dipolar interaction derived in the previous section in the Cooper channel:

V_{\bm{q}_{1},-\bm{q}_{1};\bm{q}}=V_{\bm{q}+{\mathbf{Q}}}\lambda(\bm{q}_{1},\bm{q}_{1}+\bm{q})\lambda(-\bm{q}_{1},-\bm{q}_{1}-\bm{q})

(42)

From the time-reversal or reflection symmetry we know that
\lambda(-{\mathbf{q}}_{1},-{\mathbf{q}}_{1}-{\mathbf{q}})=\pm\lambda({\mathbf{q}}_{1},{\mathbf{q}}_{1}+{\mathbf{q}})
. It is always possible to choose a gauge for the bound state wave functions such that we get this equality with the plus sign. Other gauge choices merely implement a similarity transformation on the interaction in the Cooper channel, and hence will not affect our conclusions below. We thus find

V_{\bm{q}_{1},-\bm{q}_{1};\bm{q}}=V_{\bm{q}+{\mathbf{Q}}}|\lambda(\bm{q}_{1},\bm{q}_{1}+\bm{q})|^{2}\,,

(43)

which shows that the dipolar interaction is repulsive. As
V_{{\mathbf{q}}+{\mathbf{Q}}}
diverges at
{\mathbf{q}}={\mathbf{Q}}
, and
|\lambda({\mathbf{q}}_{1},{\mathbf{q}}_{1}+{\mathbf{q}})|\leq 1
, the dipolar interaction will be peaked at
{\mathbf{q}}={\mathbf{Q}}
as long as the effective dipole moment
{\mathbf{d}}({\mathbf{q}}_{1})
introduced in the previous section is not too small.

VI
Discussion

To arrive at our results we have assumed that the half-filled state with gapped spinons and chargons is described by a deconfined compact U(1) gauge theory. This cannot really be true, however, and at the largest length scales the U(1) gauge theory must confine. Nevertheless, this does not pose a fundamental problem. First, it is possible that with finite doping, the deconfined phase becomes stable. But even if this is not the case (for example, because the doped electrons enter as gauge neutral bound states), our results should go through as long as the deconfined U(1) gauge theory description applies up to a length scale which is larger than the size of the spinon-chargon bound states and the Cooper pairs. After all, our numerical results were obtained on a
30\times 30
system, so if the FL
∗
description holds up to this scale, our analysis is valid.

Finally, an interesting question that we leave for future work is to obtain an estimate for
T_{c}
. We emphasize that the temperature-dependence of the superconducting gap will not follow the standard BCS behaviour, as the pair-forming interaction itself is temperature dependent (for example, through the dependence of
m
on temperature). It would also be interesting to explore whether similar ideas as the ones used here are applicable in the context of the SU(2) gauge theory for the cuprate pseudogap obtained from the ancilla-layer model
48
;
10
;
11
;
12
.

Derivation of the Shraiman-Siggia interaction

In this section, we derive the form of the spinon-chargon Hamiltonian
H_{\psi z}

\displaystyle S_{\psi z}=\int\mathrm{d}\tau\bigg[

\displaystyle\sum_{{\mathbf{r}},{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}}t_{{\mathbf{r}}{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}}\bar{\psi}_{{\mathbf{r}},-}\psi_{{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime},+}\,z_{{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}}\mathrm{i}\sigma^{y}z_{\mathbf{r}}+\sum_{\mathbf{r}}\bar{\psi}_{{\mathbf{r}},-}\psi_{{\mathbf{r}},+}\,z_{{\mathbf{r}}}\mathrm{i}\sigma^{y}\partial_{\tau}z_{\mathbf{r}}+\text{h.c.}\bigg]

t_{\bm{r}\bm{r}^{\prime}}=\begin{cases}t\quad\bm{r}^{\prime}=\bm{r}\pm{\mathbf{e}}_{x/y}\\

\displaystyle z_{{\mathbf{r}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{{\mathbf{q}}}e^{\mathrm{i}{\mathbf{q}}{\mathbf{r}}}z_{{\mathbf{q}}},

(46)

\displaystyle\psi_{{\mathbf{r}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{{\mathbf{q}}}e^{\mathrm{i}{\mathbf{q}}\cdot{\mathbf{r}}}\psi_{{\mathbf{q}}},

(47)

The conjugate momentum of this theory is

\pi_{\bm{q}}=\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}_{\psi z}}{\delta(\partial_{\tau}z_{\bm{q}})}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\bm{p}\bm{p}^{\prime}}\psi_{-,\bm{p}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\psi_{+,\bm{p}^{\prime}-\bm{p}}z_{\bm{p}-\bm{q}}(\mathrm{i}\sigma^{y}).

(48)

Performing the Legendre transformation
H_{\psi z}=\sum_{\mathbf{q}}\pi_{\mathbf{q}}\partial_{\tau}z_{\mathbf{q}}-\mathcal{L}_{\psi z}
, the Hamiltonian reads

\displaystyle H_{\psi z}=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{\bm{r}}\sum_{\bm{q}_{1}\bm{q}_{2}\bm{q}_{3}\bm{q}_{4}}e^{\mathrm{i}(-\bm{q}_{1}+\bm{q}_{2}+\bm{q}_{3}+\bm{q}_{4})\bm{r}}\epsilon_{\bm{q}_{1}-\bm{q}_{4}}\psi_{{\mathbf{q}}_{1},-}^{\dagger}\psi_{{\mathbf{q}}_{2},+}z_{{\mathbf{q}}_{3}}\mathrm{i}\sigma^{y}z_{{\mathbf{q}}_{4}}+\text{h.c.},

z_{\bm{q}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\omega_{{\mathbf{q}}}\chi}}(b_{{\mathbf{q}}}+\mathrm{i}\sigma^{y}a_{-{\mathbf{q}}}^{\dagger})

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\psi z}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{\bm{r}}\sum_{\bm{q}_{1}\bm{q}_{2}\bm{q}_{3}\bm{q}_{4}}e^{\mathrm{i}(-\bm{q}_{1}+\bm{q}_{2}+\bm{q}_{3}+\bm{q}_{4})\bm{r}}\frac{\epsilon_{\bm{q}_{1}-\bm{q}_{4}}}{2\chi\sqrt{\omega_{\bm{q}_{3}}\omega_{\bm{q}_{4}}}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\Big(\hat{\psi}_{{\mathbf{q}}_{1},-}^{\dagger}\hat{\psi}_{{\mathbf{q}}_{2},+}\hat{a}_{-{\mathbf{q}}_{4}}^{\dagger}\hat{b}_{{\mathbf{q}}_{3}}-\hat{\psi}_{{\mathbf{q}}_{1},-}^{\dagger}\hat{\psi}_{{\mathbf{q}}_{2},+}\hat{a}_{-{\mathbf{q}}_{3}}^{\dagger}\hat{b}_{{\mathbf{q}}_{4}}\Big)+\text{h.c.}

This becomes

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\psi z}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\bm{k}\bm{k}^{\prime}\bm{q}}\frac{\epsilon_{\bm{k}^{\prime}+\bm{k}-\bm{q}}-\epsilon_{\bm{q}}}{2\chi\sqrt{\omega_{-\bm{k}+\bm{q}}\omega_{-\bm{k}^{\prime}+\bm{q}}}}\biggl(\hat{\psi}_{{\mathbf{k}},+}^{\dagger}\hat{b}_{-\bm{k}+\bm{q}}^{\dagger}\hat{\psi}_{\bm{k}^{\prime},-}\hat{a}_{-\bm{k}^{\prime}+\bm{q}}+\hat{\psi}_{{\mathbf{k}},-}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{-\bm{k}+\bm{q}}^{\dagger}\hat{\psi}_{\bm{k}^{\prime},+}\hat{b}_{-\bm{k}^{\prime}+\bm{q}}\biggr).

Appendix B
Details of the numerical simulations

B.1
Coulomb interaction

The Coulomb potential obtained via the spinon polarization is

V_{\mathbf{q}}=\Pi(0,v{\mathbf{q}})^{-1}=\left[\frac{v^{2}{\mathbf{q}}^{2}+4m^{2}}{8\pi v^{3}|{\mathbf{q}}|}\arctan\frac{v|{\mathbf{q}}|}{2m}-\frac{m}{4\pi v^{2}}\right]^{-1}\,.

(51)

As this expression was obtained via a continuum approximation for the spinons, we need to restore the periodicity under shifts by reciprocal lattice vectors. We do this by replacing
|\bm{q}|\to 2\sqrt{\sin^{2}(q_{x}/2)+\sin^{2}(q_{y}/2)}
. We find that in real space this procedure leads to a very large on-site interaction. To reduce this on-site interaction we add an additional strictly on-site interaction term by hand. The precise value of this shift should not matter, as long as it removes the large on-site interaction which results from the replacement
|\bm{q}|\to 2\sqrt{\sin^{2}(q_{x}/2)+\sin^{2}(q_{y}/2)}
. This introduces some arbitrariness, and we simply take the real-space interaction to satisfy

\tilde{V}_{\mathbf{r}}=\begin{cases}V_{\mathbf{r}}&{\mathbf{r}}\neq 0,\\
V_{(a,0)}&{\mathbf{r}}=0,\end{cases}

(52)

The Fourier transform of
\tilde{V}_{\mathbf{r}}
gives the momentum-space potential
\tilde{V}_{\mathbf{q}}
that we use in our numerics.

B.2
Brillouin zone unfolding

To solve the spinon-chargon bound state problem numerically, we work in the folded or first magnetic Brillouin zone (MBZ). This means we obtain two types of bound state wavefunctions that have momentum eigenvalue
{\mathbf{q}}
in the first MBZ or momentum eigenvalue
{\mathbf{q}}+{\mathbf{Q}}
in the second MBZ. To unfold the spectrum, i.e. associate every bound state with the correct momentum in the full Brillouin zone, we replace the Hamiltonian

H\mapsto(H-a\mathbbm{1})\sigma^{z}\otimes\tau^{x}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{N\times N},

(53)

where
\sigma^{z}
acts on
(\psi_{\mathbf{q}}\,\psi_{{\mathbf{q}}+{\mathbf{Q}}})
and
\tau^{x}
flips the gauge charge. The energy of the bound states with momentum
{\mathbf{q}}
in the first MBZ and
{\mathbf{q}}+{\mathbf{Q}}
in the second MBZ are then shifted by
-a
and
+a
respectively and states can easily be associated with the correct momentum.

B.3
Superconductivity

To obtain the superconducting order parameter at temperature
T
, we numerically solve the BCS self-consistency equation

\Delta_{{\mathbf{q}}_{1}}(T)=-\sum_{{\mathbf{q}}}V_{{\mathbf{q}}_{1},-{\mathbf{q}}_{1};{\mathbf{q}}}\frac{\Delta_{{\mathbf{q}}_{1}+{\mathbf{q}}}(T)}{2\sqrt{[E({\mathbf{q}}_{1}+{\mathbf{q}})]^{2}+|\Delta_{{\mathbf{q}}_{1}+{\mathbf{q}}}(T)|^{2}}}\tanh\Biggl[\frac{\sqrt{[E({\mathbf{q}}_{1}+{\mathbf{q}})]^{2}+|\Delta_{{\mathbf{q}}_{1}+{\mathbf{q}}}(T)|^{2}}}{2k_{B}T}\Biggr],

(54)

where
E({\mathbf{q}})
is the bound state dispersion (see Fig.
2

A
) and
V_{{\mathbf{q}}_{1},-{\mathbf{q}}_{1};{\mathbf{q}}}
are the dipolar interaction matrix elements that we derive from the bound state wavefunctions in the next section.
