Introduction

Given an

We call

It is known that the defining inequalities

\displaystyle A

3
Permanents and path families

The planar networks defined in Section
2
have close connections to permanents.
Let
\pi=(\pi_{1},\dotsc,\pi_{n})
be a sequence of source-to-sink paths in a
planar network
F\in\mathcal{F}_{n}
.
We call
\pi
a
path family
if there exists a permutation

w=w_{1}\cdots w_{n}\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
such that
\pi_{i}

is a path from source
i
to sink
w_{i}
.
In this case, we say that
\pi
has
type

w
.
Further, we say that the path family
covers
F

if it contains every edge exactly once.

Let
F
be a planar network of order
n
and let
I,J
be subsets of
[n]
with
|I|=|J|
. Let
\Pi_{I,J}(F)
denote the set of path families from sources indexed by
I
to sinks indexed by
J
.
The following interpretation of
the determinent is
due to Karlin and McGregor
[
7
]

and usually attributed to Lindstrom
[
8
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For
I,J\subseteq[n]
with
|I|=|J|=k
, we have

\det(A_{I,J})=\#\{\pi=(\pi_{1},\dotsc,\pi_{k})\in\Pi_{I,J}(F)\,|\,\pi_{1},\dotsc,\pi_{k}\text{ pairwise nonintersecting }\}.

We have the following combinatorial interpretation of the permanent using path families.

Lemma 3.2
.

For
I,J\subseteq[n]
with
|I|=|J|=k
, we have

\perm(A_{I,J})=|\Pi_{I,J}(F)|
.

There is a simple formula counting path families covering a descending star network.

Lemma 3.3
.

Given descending star network
F=F_{[c_{1},d_{1}]}\bullet\cdots\bullet F_{[c_{r},d_{r}]}
, define
m_{1},\dotsc,m_{r},k_{1},\dotsc,k_{r-1}
by

m_{i}=d_{i}-c_{i}+1,\quad k_{i}=\max\{d_{i+1}-c_{i}+1,0\}.

(3.1)

Then we have

|\Pi_{[n],[n]}(F)|=\frac{m_{1}!\cdots m_{r-1}!}{k_{1}!\cdots k_{r-1}!}\cdot m_{r}!.

(3.2)

Let
h\in[n]
, then define sets
J=[h]
,
\bar{J}=[h+1,n]
,
I=[n]\cap 2\mathbb{Z}
, and
\bar{I}=[n]\smallsetminus 2\mathbb{Z}
.
Lemma
3.3
and several factorial inequalities yield the following relation for path families.

Lemma 3.4
.

Fix
h\in[n]
and let
F=F_{[c_{1},d_{1}]}\bullet\cdots\bullet F_{[c_{r},d_{r}]}
be a descending star network such that
F_{[c_{p},d_{p}]}
is the only star with
[c_{p},d_{p}]\cap[h,h+1]\neq\emptyset
.
We have

|\Pi_{I,I}(F)|\cdot|\Pi_{\bar{I},\bar{I}}(F)|\leq|\Pi_{J,J}(F)|\cdot|\Pi_{\bar{J},\bar{J}}(F)|.

4
Merging stars in a star network

For a general descending star network
F
, there may be multiple stars
F_{[c_{p},d_{p}]}
with
[c_{p},d_{p}]\cap[h,h+1]\neq\emptyset
. We now define a function that can modify the star network
F
. For each number
h\in[n]
, define the
h
-merge function

\phi_{h}:\mathcal{D}_{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{n}
as follows.

1.

Given
F=F_{[c_{1},d_{1}]}\bullet\cdots\bullet F_{[c_{r},d_{r}]}
, define

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle=\max\{k\,|\,[c_{k},d_{k}]\cap[h,h+1]\neq\emptyset\},

\displaystyle q

\displaystyle=\min\{k\,|\,[c_{k},d_{k}]\cap[h,h+1]\neq\emptyset\}.

2.

Define
\phi_{h}(F)=F_{[c_{1},d_{1}]}\bullet\cdots\bullet F_{[c_{q-1},d_{q-1}]}\bullet F_{[c_{p},d_{q}]}\bullet F_{[c_{p+1},d_{p+1}]}\bullet\cdots\bullet F_{[c_{r},d_{r}]}
.

The
h
-merge function gives an equality on the cardinality of path families

Proposition 4.1
.

Fix
h\in[n]
, and let
F
be a descending star network of order
n

with path matrix
A
.
Let
B
be the path matrix of the descending star network

\phi_{h}(F)
. Then we have

(i)

A_{[h],[h]}=B_{[h],[h]}
and

A_{[h+1,n],[h+1,n]}=B_{[h+1,n],[h+1,n]}
.

(ii)

|\Pi_{[h],[h]}(F)|\cdot|\Pi_{[h+1,n],[h+1,n]}(F)|=|\Pi_{[h],[h]}(\phi_{h}(F))|\cdot|\Pi_{[h+1,n],[h+1,n]}(\phi_{h}(F))|
.

A natural generalization of a product of two permanents indexed by even or odd integers is a product of
s
permanents indexed by integers which are equivalent modulo

Define

[c,d]_{r}^{s}=\{x\in[c,d]:\ x\equiv r\ (\text{mod }s)\}.

Next, we have a generalization of Lemma

.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let

be a descending star network. Fix

. For every

(6.1)

We can now extend Theorem
5.1
to a more general result.

.

Let

be the antiadjacency matrix of an

-element unit interval order.

(6.2)

Proof omitted.

7
A Generalization to all TNN matrices

In this section, we will show that (
1.2
) holds for
I=[1,\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor]
.

A polynomial
p(x_{11},x_{12},\cdots,x_{nn})
in
n^{2}
variables is called totally non-negative (TNN) if it satisfies

p(A):=p(a_{11},a_{12},\cdots,a_{nn})\geq 0

(7.1)

for each
n\times n
totally non-negative matrix
A=(a_{ij})
. Therefore, (
1.2
) is true if and only if the polynomial

\perm(X_{[1,\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor],[1,\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor]})\perm(X_{[\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil,n],[\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil,n]})-\perm(X_{[n]\cap 2\mathbb{Z},[n]\cap 2\mathbb{Z}})\perm(X_{[n]\setminus 2\mathbb{Z},[n]\setminus 2\mathbb{Z}})

(7.2)

is TNN.

Drake, Gerrish and Skandera
[
5
]
gave a necessary and sufficient condition for a polynomial which is a difference of “permutation monomials” to be TNN.

Theorem 7.1

(Drake, Gerrish, Skandera)
.

Let
w
and
u
be two permutations in
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
. Then
w
is less than or equal to
u
in the Bruhat order if and only if the polynomial

x_{1,w_{1}}\cdots x_{n,w_{n}}-x_{1,u_{1}}\cdots x_{n,u_{n}}

is totally non-negative.

Let
t=\lfloor\tfrac{n}{2}\rfloor
. The term
\perm(X_{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor,\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor})\perm(X_{\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil,\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil})
contains monomials

x_{1,w_{1}}\cdots x_{t,w_{t}}x_{t+1,w_{t+1}}\cdots x_{n,w_{n}}
such that

w_{1}\cdots w_{t}\ \ \text{permutes}\ \{1,\dotsc,t\},\qquad w_{t+1}\cdots w_{n}\ \ \text{permutes}\ \{t+1,\dotsc,n\}.

For convenience, we set

\begin{split}&\mathfrak{A}_{n}=\mathfrak{S}_{1,\dotsc,t}\times\mathfrak{S}_{t+1,\dotsc,n}\\
=\{&w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}\ |\ w_{1}\cdots w_{t}\ \text{permutes}\ \{1,\dotsc,t\}\ \text{and}\ w_{t+1}\cdots w_{n}\ \text{permutes}\ \{t+1,\dotsc,n\}\}.\end{split}

(7.3)

Also, for future reference, we define

\tilde{\mathfrak{A}}_{n}=\{w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}\ |\ w_{1}\cdots w_{t+1}\ \text{permutes}\ \{1,\dotsc,t+1\}\ \text{and}\ w_{t+2}\cdots w_{n}\ \text{permutes}\ \{t+2,\dotsc,n\}\}.

(7.4)

It is easy to see that
|\mathfrak{A}_{n}|=\lfloor\tfrac{n}{2}\rfloor!\lceil\tfrac{n}{2}\rceil!
.
For example,
\mathfrak{A}_{4}=\{1234,1243,2134,2143\}
.
The product of permanents
\perm(X_{[n]\cap 2\mathbb{Z},[n]\cap 2\mathbb{Z}})\perm(X_{[n]\setminus 2\mathbb{Z},[n]\setminus 2\mathbb{Z}})
contains monomials

x_{1,u_{1}}\cdots x_{n,u_{n}}
such that

u_{1}u_{3}u_{5}\cdots\ \text{permutes}\ \{1,3,5,\cdots\}=[n]\setminus 2\mathbb{Z},\quad u_{2}u_{4}u_{6}\cdots\ \text{permutes}\ \{2,4,6,\cdots\}=[n]\cap 2\mathbb{Z}.

We call the set that consists of
u=u_{1}\cdots u_{n}
satisfying the above conditions
\mathfrak{B}_{n}
, i,e,

\mathfrak{B}_{n}=\{u\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}\ |\ u_{1}u_{2}\cdots u_{n}\ \text{takes odd and even integers alternately and $u_{1}$ is odd}\}

(7.5)

It is easy to see that
|\mathfrak{B}_{n}|=\lfloor\tfrac{n}{2}\rfloor!\lceil\tfrac{n}{2}\rceil!
.
Then, the polynomial (
7.2
) has this number
of positive terms (on the left) and negative terms (on the right). Therefore, if we can find a bijective map
f_{n}:\mathfrak{A}_{n}\rightarrow\mathfrak{B}_{n}
, such that
w\leq f_{n}(w)
for all
w\in\mathfrak{A}_{n}
, by Theorem
7.1
, the polynomial (
7.2
) is TNN.
