I Accomplishments
I.1 Main goals of the project
This project focused on developing and demonstrating novel functionalities for creation, control
and characterization of quantum states under a unified framework, which we refer to as PIQUE
(Parametrically-Induced QUantum Engineering). Building on results and unique insights gener-
ated in first-generation PIQUE (Parametrically-Induced QUantum Engineering) program (DE-
SC0019461), in the next-generation PIQUE program, we employed strong and tunable parametric
interactions for fast, scalable and robust state preparation and control in Josephson junction-based
superconducting circuit platforms. Specifically, the project aimed to accomplish the following ob-
jectives:

 • Co-design approach to multi-qubit entanglement creation and characterization

 • Time-dependent dissipative control

 • Multi-qudit (high-dimensional) entanglement and readout

I.2 Major activities
The project team comprised of researchers from academia, industry and national labs spanning
theoretical and experimental expertise in solid-state superconducting quantum circuits: UMass
Lowell team led theoretical development and provided support for data analysis and modeling to
all experimental efforts 1 ; Raytheon-BBN Technologies (Cambridge, MA) performed characteriza-
tion and measurement of parametric systems for entanglement stabilization; NIST (Boulder. CO)
designed and fabricated multi-qubit parametric devices and performed the qubit readout studies;
AFRL (Rome, NY) developed new designs for tunable couplers in close collaboration with UML
and BBN teams.
 In addition, UML postdoctoral associates and graduate students were hosted by Raytheon-BBN
and NIST Boulder on-site to work directly on experimental realizations of theoretical protocols
developed in this project.

I.3 Significant results
The research under Next-Generation PIQUE project leveraged and advanced the unique insights
and capabilities developed under the BES-funded PIQUE 1.0 project, that concluded in 2021.
Specifically, the exact reservoir engineering schemes and parametric circuit designs developed for
two-qubit systems under PIQUE 1.0, laid the foundation for the studies and extensions conducted
under PIQUE 2.0. Under PIQUE 2.0, we achieved the following milestones:

 • Developed ab-initio theoretical framework for “parametric circuit-QED” using sequential,
 time-dependent Schrieffer Wolff transformations [1]

 • Developed new protocols for reservoir engineering aided by bath correlations [2, 13]

I.4 Key Outcomes and Other Achievements
One of the major outcomes of this research is to clarify and extend the reach of quantum reser-
voir engineering protocols as a standard paradigm for quantum states preparation and control.
Crucially, we found that using several overlapping modular dissipators (instead of a single global
dissipator) can allow an exponential improvement in stabilization performance, with the stabiliza-
tion time growing according to some low-degree polynomial in the system size system size (N ) for
fixed amplitude drives [7]. Remarkably, such modular architectures allow use of bounded-depth
interactions and only O(N) resources [i.e. number of dissipative and unitary drives] for stabilizing
an N -qubit entangled state making such protocols both “modular” and “scalable”. The student
pursuing this project was also awarded the QISE-NET Fellowship (2021 cohort) to carry forward
these investigations; notably, this was one of the only 25 projects selected that year.
 The theoretical framework [1] as well as experimental implementation [3] of the “parametric
circuit-QED” architecture constitute a new paradigm for engineering and controlling archetypal
light-matter interactions. Importantly, the control techniques and coupling modalities we have
demonstrated in few-qubit versions of these architectures can be scaled to larger numbers of qubits
and cavities in a straightforward manner. For instance, we have extended these to a 3-qubit
+ cavity system with no static interactions, but all-to-all parametric interactions mediated via
a single coupler. This general increase in connectivity is not “hard-wired” and so that various
Hamiltonians become software programmable via frequency-selective pumping.
 It is worthwhile to note that the co-design approach taken in this program is most clearly evi-
dent from the common chip designs and coupling modalities developed and employed for all goals
of this program, which in turn are informed by tight collaboration between different participating
institutes. For instance, the two key experimental demonstrations – namely, fast Bell stabilization
led by Raytheon-BBN [3] and joint pulsed parametric-dispersive readout led by NIST Boulder [4]
— were performed on nominally identical chip designs (albeit with slightly different parameters).

IV Impact
Entangled states underpin almost every quantum information processing application, so devel-
opment of multi-partite stabilization protocols with no fidelity-speed tradeoff constitute a major
advancement in the area of quantum state engineering and control. Similarly, research on multi-
qudit entanglement and readout developed under this project provide insights into complex high-
dimensional entangled states, an area where both basic physics and practical challenges still remain
largely unexplored. The research under Next-Generation PIQUE serves to extend notions such
as scalability and resource requirements, restricted for unitary gate-based protocols, to quantum

 5

reservoir engineering on both fronts and hence clarifies its prospects as a generic state preparation
method for larger resource states.
 In addition, the new functionalities developed under this program — such as novel parametric
couplers, and new protocols for multi-qubit readout and characterization — are already enabling
immediate practical applications in circuit-QED-like platforms. These include new quantum control
functionalities such as in-situ tunable interactions (both in sign and magnitude), exact decoupling
or “blind spots” (with high ON/OFF ratio) and frequency-selective multi-qubit readout. The
technical progress accomplished under this project directly advances Priority Area 2 (Enhance
Creation and Control of Coherence in Quantum Systems) identified in the BES roundtable report
on Opportunities for Basic Research for Next-Generation Quantum Systems. Specifically, the multi-
partite, mult-qudit entanglement stabilization and readout addresses the challenge of developing
new theoretical and experimental probes to simultaneously prepare, preserve and measure quantum
correlations.
 On the aspect of workforce development, the strong theory-experiment integration and trian-
gulated collaboration involving academia (UMass Lowell), industry (Raytheon BBN Technologies)
and federal labs (National Institute of Standards and Technology, Boulder and Air Force research
laboratory, Rome) provided opportunities for holistic training of graduate students and postdoc-
toral researchers.
