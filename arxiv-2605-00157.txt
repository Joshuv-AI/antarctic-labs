Asymptotic Replacement for Quantum Channel Products with Applications to Inhomogeneous Matrix Product States
Lubashan Pathirana

Abstract
We develop a product-level trace-Dobrushin theory for finite-dimensional quantum channel products and apply it to deterministic and stationary random inhomogeneous matrix product states in left-canonical CPTP gauge. For a product of channels, the centered trace-Dobrushin coefficient quantifies the residual dependence on the input state, and its decay is the criterion for trace-norm forgetting. In the deterministic setting, this decay is equivalent to asymptotic replacement by a moving replacement channel. For two-sided products, pullback forgetting produces a unique boundary state, which determines the canonical replacement family. For stationary random CPTP cocycles, submultiplicativity of the product coefficient yields a trace-Dobrushin Lyapunov exponent. We prove that the almost sure negativity of this exponent is equivalent to quenched trace-norm memory loss and gives exponential forward and pullback convergence to a unique dynamically stationary random replacement channel. When the ϱ \varrho -mixing profile of the channel environment tends to zero, we obtain annealed super-polynomial estimates, while independence gives annealed exponential estimates. Finally, we transfer these estimates to inhomogeneous matrix product states whose auxiliary transfer maps are CPTP. These channel estimates transfer to deterministic and stationary random inhomogeneous MPS, giving infinite-volume limits of trace-closed finite-volume states, quantitative boundary stability, and correlation bounds governed by the same auxiliary product coefficients.



1
Introduction

Quantum channels are the basic dynamical objects of finite-dimensional quantum information theory.
If
\mathcal{H}\simeq\mathbb{C}^{d}
, a quantum state is a density matrix

\mathcal{S}_{d}:=\{\rho\in M_{d}(\mathbb{C}):\rho\geq 0,\ \operatorname{Tr}\left(\rho\right)=1\}.

A quantum channel is a linear map

\Phi:M_{d}(\mathbb{C})\longrightarrow M_{d}(\mathbb{C})

which is completely positive and trace preserving (CPTP).
Equivalently, by the Choi–Kraus theorem,
\Phi
admits a representation

\Phi(X)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}K_{i}XK_{i}^{*},\qquad\sum_{i=1}^{r}K_{i}^{*}K_{i}=I.

Here
K^{*}
denotes the conjugate transpose of the matrix
K
.
Such maps describe the evolution of finite-dimensional open quantum systems and the state transformations available in quantum information theory.
They are the noncommutative analogues of stochastic matrices: trace preservation is conservation of total mass, while complete positivity is stability under extension by an arbitrary finite-dimensional reference system
[
Sti55
,
Cho75
,
Kra+83
,
NC12
,
Wat18
]
.

The long-time behavior of quantum channels is a central problem in quantum information theory and mathematical physics.
For a single homogeneous channel
\Phi
, one asks whether repeated application of
\Phi
erases the initial state:

\norm{\Phi^n(\rho)-\Phi^n(\sigma)}_{1}\longrightarrow 0,\qquad\rho,\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{d},

where
\norm{\,\cdot\,}
denotes the trace-norm.
Under the usual aperiodicity or primitivity assumptions, this loss of memory is equivalent to convergence toward a unique stationary state,

\Phi^{n}(\rho)\longrightarrow\rho_{*},\qquad\Phi(\rho_{*})=\rho_{*}.

This homogeneous theory is closely related to the spectral theory of completely positive maps, quantum Perron–Frobenius theory, and the distinction between ergodicity, mean ergodicity, mixing, and primitivity
[
EH78
,
FNW92
,
Bur+13
,
CP16
]
.

The present paper studies a more flexible problem.
We consider products of quantum channels, which need not be identical.
Given a time interval
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
and a sequence of channels
(\Phi_{t})_{t\in\mathcal{I}}
, we write

\Phi_{t:s}:=\Phi_{t-1}\circ\Phi_{t-2}\circ\cdots\circ\Phi_{s},\qquad s<t.

Such products arise when the noise mechanism changes in time, when an open system is driven by a time-dependent environment, and when site-dependent matrix product state tensors are represented through finite-dimensional auxiliary transfer channels.
They also arise in stationary random models, where the channel at time
n
is sampled along a probability-preserving base system,

\Phi_{\theta^{n}\omega},\qquad(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\pr,\theta).

The corresponding random products form the cocycle

\Phi_{\omega}^{(n)}:=\Phi_{\theta^{n-1}\omega}\circ\cdots\circ\Phi_{\omega}.

Random and time-inhomogeneous quantum processes of this type have been studied from several complementary viewpoints, including random quantum cocycles, ergodic quantum processes, and stationary limit theorems
[
PS25
,
PS23
,
MS21
,
MS22
]
.

The main feature of the inhomogeneous setting is that there is usually no single limiting state.
Even if the product forgets its initial state, the output may converge toward a moving state rather than toward a fixed density matrix.
Thus, the natural limiting object is not necessarily a fixed channel.
It is a family of replacement channels.
For a state
\rho\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
, the replacement channel with output
\rho
is

R_{\rho}(X):=\operatorname{Tr}\left(X\right)\rho.

It erases the input and replaces it by
\rho
.
We say, informally, that a family of channel products is asymptotically replacing when it converges in induced trace norm to an appropriate family of replacement channels.

The coefficient that detects this replacement behavior is the centered trace-Dobrushin coefficient.
For a positive trace-preserving map
\Phi
, define

\kappa_{\rm tr}(\Phi):=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}X=X^{*}\\
\operatorname{Tr}\left(X\right)=0\\
X\neq 0\end{subarray}}\dfrac{\norm{\Phi(X)}_{1}}{\norm{X}_{1}}.

Equivalently, by
2.2
,

\kappa_{\rm tr}(\Phi)=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho,\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{d}\\
\rho\neq\sigma\end{subarray}}\dfrac{\norm{\Phi(\rho)-\Phi(\sigma)}_{1}}{\norm{\rho-\sigma}_{1}}=\dfrac{1}{2}\sup_{\rho,\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{d}}\norm{\Phi(\rho)-\Phi(\sigma)}_{1}.

Thus
\kappa_{\rm tr}(\Phi)
is the sharp trace-distance contraction coefficient of the channel.
This coefficient is standard at the one-step level.
The point of the present paper is to use the exact coefficient of long products,

\kappa_{t:s}:=\kappa_{\rm tr}(\Phi_{t:s}),

as the intrinsic product-level measure of residual trace-norm memory.
Equivalently,
\kappa_{t:s}
is one half of the trace-norm diameter of the evolved state space
\Phi_{t:s}(\mathcal{S}_{d})
.
It vanishes exactly when the product
\Phi_{t:s}
is a replacement channel.
Its decay is therefore the intrinsic product-level notion of loss of memory.

This viewpoint should be distinguished from computable sufficient criteria.
Markov–Dobrushin minorization and quantum Doeblin coefficients provide upper bounds on
\kappa_{\rm tr}(\Phi)
, often through common lower bounds or semidefinite programs
[
Dob56
,
Dob70
,
ALS21
,
SB24
,
Hir24
]
.
They are useful estimates, but the intrinsic object in this paper is the exact product coefficient
\kappa_{\rm tr}(\Phi_{t:s})
.
It should also be distinguished from Hilbert–Birkhoff projective contraction.
Projective metrics are natural for positive cone maps and non-normalized transfer operators
[
RKW11
]
.
For CPTP products, trace preservation fixes the trace-zero hyperplane, so the centered trace-norm coefficient is the direct measure of state-memory loss.

A final motivation comes from matrix product states.
Matrix product states provide finite-dimensional descriptions of one-dimensional quantum many-body systems
[
FNW92
,
Per+07
,
VMC08
]
.
When the tensors are placed in a left-canonical CPTP gauge, the auxiliary transfer maps are quantum channels.
Asymptotic replacement of the auxiliary products then yields boundary stability, local thermodynamic limits, and correlation estimates controlled by the transfer product across the separating gap.
In this CPTP-gauge setting, the results below extend and complement existing work on homogeneous and random MPS limits and random MPS correlation lengths
[
SB25
,
MS21
,
MS22
,
PW25
]
.

1.1
Main results

Deterministic inhomogeneous products

Let
(\Phi_{n})_{n\in\mathcal{I}}
be a deterministic sequence of CPTP maps on
\mathbb{M}_{d}
.
Here
\mathcal{I}=\mathbb{N}_{0}
in the one-sided setting and
\mathcal{I}=\mathbb{Z}
in the two-sided setting.
For admissible times
s<t
, write

\Phi_{t:s}:=\Phi_{t-1}\circ\Phi_{t-2}\circ\cdots\circ\Phi_{s},\qquad\kappa_{t:s}:=\kappa_{\rm tr}(\Phi_{t:s}).

Definition 1.1

(Strong asymptotic replacement from an initial time)
.

Fix an initial time
s\in\mathcal{I}
.
We say that the products
(\Phi_{t:s})_{t>s}
are strongly asymptotically replacing from
s
if there are states
\eta_{t:s}\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
such that, with

R_{t:s}(X):=\operatorname{Tr}\left(X\right)\eta_{t:s},

one has

\lim_{t\to\infty}\norm{\Phi_{t:s}-R_{t:s}}_{1\to 1}=0.

Here
\norm{\,\cdot\,}_{1\to 1}
denotes the norm induced on superoperators by the trace norm (
\norm{\,\cdot\,}_{1}
) on matrices.

The definition is a channel-level formulation of loss of memory.
The output center may depend on
t
, so the limiting object is a family of replacement channels rather than a single fixed channel.
The next theorem shows that this map-level property is exactly characterized by the product coefficient
\kappa_{t:s}
.
For a reference state
\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
, define

R_{t:s}^{(\tau)}(X):=\operatorname{Tr}\left(X\right)\Phi_{t:s}(\tau).

Theorem 1
.

Fix an initial time
s\in\mathcal{I}
.
Then the following are equivalent.

1.

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\kappa_{t:s}=0
.

2.

The products
(\Phi_{t:s})_{t>s}
are strongly asymptotically replacing from
s
.

3.

For every reference state
\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
,

\norm{\Phi_{t:s}-R_{t:s}^{(\tau)}}_{1\to 1}\to 0
as
t\to\infty
.

4.

For some reference state
\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
,

\norm{\Phi_{t:s}-R_{t:s}^{(\tau)}}_{1\to 1}\to 0
as
t\to\infty
.

Moreover, for every
s<t
and every
\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
,

\kappa_{t:s}\leq\norm{\Phi_{t:s}-R_{t:s}^{(\tau)}}_{1\to 1}\leq 4\kappa_{t:s}.

Thus, the quantitative decay of
\kappa_{t:s}
is equivalent, up to universal constants, to the quantitative convergence of the product to the reference replacement family.

In the two-sided setting, the limiting replacement family can be identified intrinsically by pulling the initial time to the remote past.
This removes the arbitrary reference state from the limiting channel.

Theorem 2
.

Assume that
\mathcal{I}=\mathbb{Z}
.
The following are equivalent.

1.

For every fixed terminal time
t\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to-\infty}\kappa_{t:s}=0
.

2.

There exists a family of states
(\eta_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset\mathcal{S}_{d}
such that, for

R_{t}(X)=\operatorname{Tr}\left(X\right)\eta_{t}
, one has, for every fixed terminal time
t\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\norm{\Phi_{t:s}-R_t}_{1\to 1}\to 0
as
s\to-\infty
.

When these conditions hold, the states
(\eta_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}
are unique.
Writing them as
(\rho_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}
, one has

\rho_{t+1}=\Phi_{t}(\rho_{t}),\qquad t\in\mathbb{Z}.

Moreover, for every reference state
\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
,

\rho_{t}=\lim_{s\to-\infty}\Phi_{t:s}(\tau),\qquad\lim_{s\to-\infty}\norm{R_{t:s}^{(\tau)}-R_t}_{1\to 1}=0.

When the equivalent conditions in
2
hold, we say that the sequence has pullback strong asymptotic replacement.
The theorem shows that the canonical pullback centers form a pullback boundary state
(\rho_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}
.
In particular, the consistency relation

\rho_{t+1}=\Phi_{t}(\rho_{t})

is not an additional hypothesis; it follows from asymptotic replacement from the remote past.
Assume further that the products lose memory in the forward direction, namely that

\kappa_{t:s}\longrightarrow 0\qquad\text{as }t\to\infty

for every fixed
s\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Then the same canonical pullback boundary family is also the forward replacement family.
Indeed, the boundary-state identity gives

\rho_{t}=\Phi_{t:s}(\rho_{s}),\qquad s<t,

and hence

R_{t}=R_{t:s}^{(\rho_{s})}.

Therefore, for every
s<t
and every reference state
\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
,

\norm{\Phi_{t:s}-R_t}_{1\to 1}\leq 4\kappa_{t:s},\qquad\norm{R_{t:s}^{(\tau)}-R_t}_{1\to 1}\leq 2\kappa_{t:s}.

Consequently, for every fixed initial time
s\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\norm{\Phi_{t:s}-R_t}_{1\to 1}\longrightarrow 0,\qquad\norm{R_{t:s}^{(\tau)}-R_t}_{1\to 1}\longrightarrow 0

as
t\to\infty
. Thus, under pullback memory loss and forward memory loss, the reference-dependent forward replacement family is asymptotic to the canonical pullback replacement family.

Random products over a probability-preserving base

We next place the deterministic inhomogeneous theory over a stationary random base.
Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\pr,\theta)
be an invertible probability-preserving system, and let
\omega\mapsto\Phi_{\omega}
be a measurable family of CPTP maps on
\mathbb{M}_{d}
.
For
n\geq 1
, set

\Phi_{\omega}^{(n)}:=\Phi_{\theta^{n-1}\omega}\circ\Phi_{\theta^{n-2}\omega}\circ\cdots\circ\Phi_{\omega},\qquad\Phi_{\omega}^{(0)}:=\mathrm{id}.

More generally, for integers
s<t
, write

\Phi_{\omega;t:s}:=\Phi_{\theta^{t-1}\omega}\circ\Phi_{\theta^{t-2}\omega}\circ\cdots\circ\Phi_{\theta^{s}\omega},\qquad\Phi_{\omega;s:s}:=\mathrm{id}.

Thus
\Phi_{\omega}^{(n)}=\Phi_{\omega;n:0}
.
We write
\kappa_{\omega;t:s}:=\kappa_{\rm tr}(\Phi_{\omega;t:s})
and
\kappa_{n}(\omega):=\kappa_{\omega;n:0}
, so that
\kappa_{\omega;t:s}=\kappa_{t-s}(\theta^{s}\omega)
.

Related random and inhomogeneous quantum-process frameworks appear in
[
PS25
,
PS23
,
MS21
,
MS22
]
.
A recurring theme in these works is the construction of dynamically compatible random states or boundary-state fields.

Definition 1.2

(Dynamically stationary random state)
.

A dynamically stationary random state for the cocycle is a measurable map
\rho:\Omega\to\mathcal{S}_{d}
, written
\omega\mapsto\rho_{\omega}
, such that

\Phi_{\omega}(\rho_{\omega})=\rho_{\theta\omega}\qquad\text{for }\pr\text{-a.e. }\omega.

It is unique if every other such map agrees with it
\pr
-almost surely.

By
Theorem

4.1
, the limit

\lambda_{\rm tr}(\omega)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\kappa_{n}(\omega),\qquad\log 0:=-\infty,

exists for
\pr
-a.e.
\omega
and defines a
\theta
-invariant random variable
\lambda_{\rm tr}:\Omega\to[-\infty,0]
.
We call
\lambda_{\rm tr}
the trace-Dobrushin Lyapunov exponent.
It is constant on ergodic components; in particular, if
\theta
is ergodic, then
\lambda_{\rm tr}
is deterministic almost surely.

For each fixed realization
\omega
, the sequence
\Phi_{\omega},\Phi_{\theta\omega},\Phi_{\theta^{2}\omega},\ldots
is an ordinary deterministic time-inhomogeneous sequence of channels.
Hence, the deterministic replacement results apply fiberwise.
The probabilistic structure is used to quantify the decay of the corresponding product coefficients.
The relevant hypothesis in the general probability-preserving setting is
\lambda_{\rm tr}(\omega)<0
for
\pr
-a.e.
\omega
.
Criteria for verifying this almost-sure negativity are proved in
Section

4.2
.
The trace-Dobrushin criterion below is formulated directly at the level of product contraction.
In particular, eventual strict positivity gives one sufficient mechanism for a negative exponent, but it is not necessary; see
4.10
and
Example

2
.

Theorem 3
.

Assume that
\lambda_{\rm tr}(\omega)<0
with probability one.
Then there exists a unique dynamically stationary random state
\rho:\Omega\to\mathcal{S}_{d}
in the sense of
Definition

1.2
.
Let
R_{\omega}(X):=\operatorname{Tr}\left(X\right)\rho_{\omega}
.
Then there exist a measurable
\theta
-invariant random variable
\beta:\Omega\to(-\infty,0)
, a full-measure
\theta
-invariant set
\Omega_{*}\subseteq\Omega
, and almost surely finite measurable random variables
C_{\beta}^{+}
and
C_{\beta}^{-}
such that, for every
\omega\in\Omega_{*}
and every
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\norm{\Phi_{\omega;n:0}-R_{\theta^n\omega}}_{1\to 1}

\displaystyle\leq 4C_{\beta}^{+}(\omega)e^{\beta(\omega)n},

\displaystyle\norm{\Phi_{\omega;0:-n}-R_\omega}_{1\to 1}

\displaystyle\leq 4C_{\beta}^{-}(\omega)e^{\beta(\omega)n}.

The corresponding state-level estimates hold with the constant
4
replaced by
2
.

We now impose a stochastic decorrelation assumption on the unit-time channel sequence.
Set
X_{j}(\omega):=\Phi_{\theta^{j}\omega}
for
j\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Define

\mathcal{G}_{-\infty}^{k}:=\sigma(X_{j}:j<k),\qquad\mathcal{G}_{k}^{\infty}:=\sigma(X_{j}:j\geq k).

For two sub-
\sigma
-algebras
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
, define the maximal-correlation coefficient by

\varrho_{\rm mc}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}):=\sup\left\{\left|\mathbb{E}[UV]\right|:U\in L^{2}(\mathcal{A}),\,V\in L^{2}(\mathcal{B}),\,\mathbb{E}[U]=\mathbb{E}[V]=0,\,\norm{U}_{2}=\norm{V}_{2}=1\right\}.

For
m\geq 1
, set

\varrho_{m}:=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\varrho_{\rm mc}\left(\mathcal{G}_{-\infty}^{k},\mathcal{G}_{k+m}^{\infty}\right).

We say that the channel environment is
\varrho
-mixing if
\varrho_{m}\to 0
.
This is a decorrelation assumption on the random environment only.
The loss of quantum memory is supplied separately by the negative trace-Dobrushin Lyapunov exponent.
If the two-sided sequence
(X_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
is independent, then
\varrho_{m}=0
for every
m\geq 1
.

Theorem 4
.

Assume that
\lambda_{\rm tr}(\omega)<0
with probability one, and let
\rho:\Omega\to\mathcal{S}_{d}
be the stationary random state from
3
.
Put
R_{\omega}(X):=\operatorname{Tr}\left(X\right)\rho_{\omega}
, and define

\Delta_{n}^{\rm op}(\omega):=\max\left\{\norm{\Phi_{\omega;n:0}-R_{\theta^n\omega}}_{1\to 1},\norm{\Phi_{\omega;0:-n}-R_\omega}_{1\to 1}\right\}.

Then the following hold.

1.

If
\varrho_{m}\to 0
, then for every
p\in\mathbb{N}
there is
C_{p}<\infty
such that
\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{n}^{\rm op}]\leq C_{p}n^{-p}
for all
n\geq 1
.

2.

If
(X_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
are independent, then there are
C<\infty
and
\gamma>0
such that
\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{n}^{\rm op}]\leq Ce^{-\gamma n}
for all
n\geq 1
.

1.2
Inhomogeneous Matrix Product States

The deterministic product theory has a direct application to deterministic inhomogeneous matrix product states in left-canonical gauge.
We use the standard terminology of matrix product states from
[
Per+07
]
.
We write the auxiliary transfer maps in the Schrödinger picture, so the left-canonical condition gives trace-preserving auxiliary channels.
Equivalently, the dual Heisenberg-picture maps are unital.
This is the same convention used in channel-based approaches to inhomogeneous MPS thermodynamic limits
[
SB25
]
.
This channel viewpoint also connects with random MPS, including the homogeneous random setting of
[
LP21
]
and the random or ergodic constructions in
[
MS21
,
MS22
,
PW25
]
.
The advantage of the present formulation is that the exact trace-Dobrushin coefficient
\kappa_{\rm tr}
gives quantitative boundary-stability and trace-closed thermodynamic-limit estimates.
The resulting matrix product states are deterministic inhomogeneous MPS.
They are not assumed to be translation invariant.
The translation-invariant case is recovered, in this gauge, when the Kraus family
\{K_{i}^{[n]}\}_{i}
is independent of
n
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be the finite-dimensional physical single-site Hilbert space.
Let
\mathcal{H}
be the finite-dimensional auxiliary, or bond, Hilbert space.
We write
B(\mathcal{H})
for the algebra of bounded operators on
\mathcal{H}
.
We also write
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H}):=\{\rho\in B(\mathcal{H}):\rho\geq 0,\,\operatorname{Tr}\left(\rho\right)=1\}

for the set of auxiliary density matrices.
Since
\mathcal{H}
is finite-dimensional,
B(\mathcal{H})
is simply the full matrix algebra on
\mathcal{H}
.
Fix an orthonormal basis
\{\ket{1},\ldots,\ket{d_{\mcK}}\}
of
\mathcal{K}
.
For each site
n\geq 1
, let

\left\{K_{i}^{[n]}\right\}_{i=1}^{d_{\mathcal{K}}}\subset B(\mathcal{H})

be deterministic site-dependent MPS tensors.
We assume the left-canonical condition

\sum_{i=1}^{d_{\mathcal{K}}}\left(K_{i}^{[n]}\right)^{*}K_{i}^{[n]}=I_{\mathcal{H}}.

Equivalently, the auxiliary transfer map

\Phi_{n}(\rho):=\sum_{i=1}^{d_{\mathcal{K}}}K_{i}^{[n]}\rho\left(K_{i}^{[n]}\right)^{*}

is completely positive and trace-preserving on
B(\mathcal{H})
.

For
1\leq a\leq b
, write

\mathcal{K}_{[a,b]}:=\bigotimes_{r=a}^{b}\mathcal{K},\qquad\mathcal{A}_{[a,b]}:=B(\mathcal{K}_{[a,b]}).

We identify
\mathcal{A}_{[1,m]}
with a subalgebra of
\mathcal{A}_{[1,n]}
, for
m\leq n
, through the embedding
X\mapsto X\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}}^{\otimes(n-m)}
.
The one-sided quasi-local algebra is

\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{N}}:=\overline{\bigcup_{m\geq 1}\mathcal{A}_{[1,m]}}^{\norm{\cdot}_{\infty}}.

A local observable is an element of some finite-volume algebra
\mathcal{A}_{[1,m]}
.
The thermodynamic-limit statements below concern the expectations of fixed local observables as the volume
n
tends to infinity.

For
0\leq m\leq n
, define the right-tail auxiliary transfer product by

\Theta_{m,n}:=\begin{cases}\Phi_{m+1}\circ\Phi_{m+2}\circ\cdots\circ\Phi_{n},&m<n,\\
\operatorname{id}_{B(\mathcal{H})},&m=n.\end{cases}

Thus
\Theta_{m,n}
is a right-tail, or backward auxiliary, product; in the deterministic-product notation of
Section

3
, it becomes a pullback product after reversing the site index.
For a multi-index
\mathbf{i}=(i_{a},\ldots,i_{b})
, set

\ket{\mathbf i}:=\ket{i_a}\otimes\cdots\otimes\ket{i_b},\qquad K_{\mathbf{i}}^{[a,b]}:=K_{i_{a}}^{[a]}K_{i_{a+1}}^{[a+1]}\cdots K_{i_{b}}^{[b]}.

For
X\in\mathcal{A}_{[a,b]}
, define the inserted auxiliary transfer map by

\widehat{X}_{[a,b]}(Y):=\sum_{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}}\bra{\mathbf i}X\ket{\mathbf j}K_{\mathbf{j}}^{[a,b]}Y\left(K_{\mathbf{i}}^{[a,b]}\right)^{*},\qquad Y\in B(\mathcal{H}).

In particular,

\widehat{I}_{[a,b]}=\Phi_{a}\circ\Phi_{a+1}\circ\cdots\circ\Phi_{b},

and, for
m<n
,

\widehat{I}_{[m+1,n]}=\Theta_{m,n}.

For
n\geq 1
, define the trace-closed finite-volume MPS vector by

\ket{\Psi_n}:=\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}=1}^{d_{\mathcal{K}}}\operatorname{Tr}\left(K_{i_{1}}^{[1]}\cdots K_{i_{n}}^{[n]}\right)\ket{i_1\cdots i_n}.

The trace in the coefficient closes the auxiliary bond.
Whenever
\langle\Psi_{n}|\Psi_{n}\rangle\neq 0
, let
\varphi_{n}
be the normalized
n
-site state on
\mathcal{A}_{[1,n]}
given by

\varphi_{n}(Z):=\frac{\bra{\Psi_n}Z\ket{\Psi_n}}{\langle\Psi_{n}|\Psi_{n}\rangle},\qquad Z\in\mathcal{A}_{[1,n]}.

If
X\in\mathcal{A}_{[1,m]}
and
m\leq n
, we also write
\varphi_{n}(X)
for the expectation of the canonically embedded observable,

\varphi_{n}(X):=\varphi_{n}\left(X\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}}^{\otimes(n-m)}\right).

Thus the thermodynamic limit concerns
\varphi_{n}(X)
for fixed
m\in\mathbb{N}
and fixed
X\in\mathcal{A}_{[1,m]}
as
n\to\infty
.

Theorem 5
.

Assume that, for every fixed
q\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
\kappa_{\rm tr}\left(\Theta_{q,n}\right)\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.
Then there exists a unique right boundary sequence

\rho_{r}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H}),\qquad\rho_{r}=\Phi_{r}(\rho_{r+1}),\qquad r\geq 1,

and a unique state
\varphi_{\infty}
on the one-sided quasi-local algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{N}}
such that, for every
m\geq 1
and every
X\in\mathcal{A}_{[1,m]}
,

\varphi_{\infty}(X)=\operatorname{Tr}\left(\widehat{X}_{[1,m]}(\rho_{m+1})\right).

Let
\ket{\Psi_n}
be the trace-closed finite-volume MPS vector, and let
\varphi_{n}
be the normalized
n
-site state whenever
\langle\Psi_{n}|\Psi_{n}\rangle\neq 0
.
For every fixed local observable
X\in\mathcal{A}_{[1,m]}
, the normalized expectations

\varphi_{n}(X)=\frac{\bra{\Psi_n}\left(X\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}}^{\otimes(n-m)}\right)\ket{\Psi_n}}{\langle\Psi_{n}|\Psi_{n}\rangle},\qquad n\geq m,

are defined for all sufficiently large
n
, and

\varphi_{n}(X)\longrightarrow\varphi_{\infty}(X)\qquad\text{as }n\to\infty.

The same right-tail memory-loss mechanism also controls spatial clustering.
Indeed, once the observable
B
is inserted, the only auxiliary information connecting it to the observable
A
must pass through the identity-transfer product across the gap.

Theorem 6
.

Assume the hypotheses of
5
.
Let
A\in\mathcal{A}_{[p,q]}
and
B\in\mathcal{A}_{[r,s]}

2
The centered trace-Dobrushin coefficient

We begin with the coefficient used throughout the paper.
This is not a new one-step coefficient.
It is the standard trace-distance contraction coefficient, often called the trace-norm Dobrushin contraction coefficient; see, for instance,
[
HR15
,
Hir24
]
.
Our use of it is product-level.
For a product of channels,
2\kappa_{\rm tr}
is exactly the trace-norm diameter of the evolved state space, and hence measures the residual memory of the input state.
The next sections show that the decay of this state-level memory coefficient is equivalent, up to universal constants, to map-level asymptotic replacement.

Let
\mathbb{M}_{d}:=M_{d}(\mathbb{C})
denote the space of
d\times d
complex matrices.
The case
d=1
is trivial, so throughout this section we assume
d\geq 2
.
Let

\mathcal{S}_{d}:=\{\rho\in\mathbb{M}_{d}:\rho\geq 0,\ \operatorname{Tr}\left(\rho\right)=1\}

be the state space.
We write
\|\cdot\|_{1}
for the trace norm.
The centered self-adjoint trace-zero subspace is

\mathsf{H}_{0}:=\{X\in\mathbb{M}_{d}^{\rm sa}:\operatorname{Tr}\left(X\right)=0\}.

Here
\mathbb{M}_{d}^{\rm sa}
denotes the real vector space of self-adjoint matrices in
\mathbb{M}_{d}
.
That is,

\mathbb{M}_{d}^{\rm sa}:=\{X\in\mathbb{M}_{d}:X=X^{*}\}.

This is the natural centered space for trace-preserving dynamics.
Every difference of two density matrices lies in
\mathsf{H}_{0}
.
Every positive trace-preserving linear map sends
\mathsf{H}_{0}
into itself.

Definition 2.1

(Centered trace-Dobrushin coefficient)
.

Let
T:\mathbb{M}_{d}\to\mathbb{M}_{d}
be positive and trace preserving.
The centered trace-Dobrushin coefficient of
T
is

\kappa_{\rm tr}(T):=\sup_{X\in\mathsf{H}_{0}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|T(X)\|_{1}}{\|X\|_{1}}.

Equivalently,
\kappa_{\rm tr}(T)
is the
1\to 1
operator norm of
T
restricted to the real Banach space
(\mathsf{H}_{0},\|\cdot\|_{1})
.

Proposition 2.2
.

For every positive trace-preserving map
T:\mathbb{M}_{d}\to\mathbb{M}_{d}
,

\kappa_{\rm tr}(T)=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho,\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{d}\\
\rho\neq\sigma\end{subarray}}\frac{\|T(\rho)-T(\sigma)\|_{1}}{\|\rho-\sigma\|_{1}}=\frac{1}{2}\sup_{\rho,\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{d}}\|T(\rho)-T(\sigma)\|_{1}.

(2.1)

Equivalently,

\kappa_{\rm tr}(T)=\frac{1}{2}\sup\left\{\|T(E-F)\|_{1}:E,F\text{ orthogonal rank-one projections}\right\}.

(2.2)

Consequently
0\leq\kappa_{\rm tr}(T)\leq 1
.
Moreover,
\kappa_{\rm tr}(T)=0
if and only if
T
is a replacement channel, namely
T(X)=\operatorname{Tr}\left(X\right)\tau
for some
\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
.

Proof.

If
\rho,\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
, then
\rho-\sigma\in\mathsf{H}_{0}
.
Hence

\|T(\rho)-T(\sigma)\|_{1}\leq\kappa_{\rm tr}(T)\|\rho-\sigma\|_{1}.

This proves

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho,\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{d}\\
\rho\neq\sigma\end{subarray}}\frac{\|T(\rho)-T(\sigma)\|_{1}}{\|\rho-\sigma\|_{1}}\leq\kappa_{\rm tr}(T).

Conversely, let
X\in\mathsf{H}_{0}\setminus\{0\}
.
Write the positive-negative decomposition of the self-adjoint matrix
X
as

X=X_{+}-X_{-},\qquad X_{\pm}\geq 0,\qquad X_{+}X_{-}=0.

Since
\operatorname{Tr}\left(X\right)=0
, one has

\operatorname{Tr}\left(X_{+}\right)=\operatorname{Tr}\left(X_{-}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\|X\|_{1}.

Both traces are positive because
X\neq 0
and
\operatorname{Tr}\left(X\right)=0
.
Define

\rho_{+}:=\frac{X_{+}}{\operatorname{Tr}\left(X_{+}\right)},\qquad\rho_{-}:=\frac{X_{-}}{\operatorname{Tr}\left(X_{-}\right)}.

Then
\rho_{+},\rho_{-}\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
, their supports are orthogonal, and

X=\frac{\|X\|_{1}}{2}(\rho_{+}-\rho_{-}).

Since
\|\rho_{+}-\rho_{-}\|_{1}=2
, it follows that

\frac{\|T(X)\|_{1}}{\|X\|_{1}}=\frac{1}{2}\|T(\rho_{+})-T(\rho_{-})\|_{1}=\frac{\|T(\rho_{+})-T(\rho_{-})\|_{1}}{\|\rho_{+}-\rho_{-}\|_{1}}.

Taking the supremum over
X\in\mathsf{H}_{0}\setminus\{0\}
gives the reverse inequality in the first identity of (
2.1
).
The same decomposition also gives

\kappa_{\rm tr}(T)\leq\frac{1}{2}\sup_{\rho,\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{d}}\|T(\rho)-T(\sigma)\|_{1}.

The opposite inequality follows from the first identity in (
2.1
) and from the bound
\|\rho-\sigma\|_{1}\leq 2
for states.
This proves (
2.1
).

We next prove the rank-one formula.
Let
\rho_{+}
and
\rho_{-}
be states with orthogonal supports.
Choose spectral decompositions

\rho_{+}=\sum_{i}p_{i}E_{i},\qquad\rho_{-}=\sum_{j}q_{j}F_{j},

where
E_{i}
and
F_{j}
are rank-one projections and
E_{i}F_{j}=0
for all
i,j
.
Since
\sum_{i}p_{i}=\sum_{j}q_{j}=1
, we have

\rho_{+}-\rho_{-}=\sum_{i,j}p_{i}q_{j}(E_{i}-F_{j}).

By convexity of the trace norm,

\|T(\rho_{+}-\rho_{-})\|_{1}\leq\sup_{i,j}\|T(E_{i}-F_{j})\|_{1}.

Taking the supremum over all orthogonally supported
\rho_{+},\rho_{-}\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
gives

\kappa_{\rm tr}(T)\leq\frac{1}{2}\sup\left\{\|T(E-F)\|_{1}:E,F\text{ orthogonal rank-one projections}\right\}.

The reverse inequality follows because every pair of orthogonal rank-one projections is a pair of states with trace distance
2
.
This proves (
2.2
).

Positivity and trace preservation imply trace-norm contractivity on self-adjoint matrices.
Indeed, if
Y=Y_{+}-Y_{-}
is the positive-negative decomposition of a self-adjoint matrix
Y
, then
T(Y_{+})
and
T(Y_{-})
are positive.
Hence

\|T(Y)\|_{1}\leq\operatorname{Tr}\left(T(Y_{+})\right)+\operatorname{Tr}\left(T(Y_{-})\right)=\operatorname{Tr}\left(Y_{+}\right)+\operatorname{Tr}\left(Y_{-}\right)=\|Y\|_{1}.

Applying this to
Y\in\mathsf{H}_{0}
gives
0\leq\kappa_{\rm tr}(T)\leq 1
.

Finally, suppose
\kappa_{\rm tr}(T)=0
.
Then
T(\rho)=T(\sigma)
for all
\rho,\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
.
Fix
\rho_{0}\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
and set
\tau:=T(\rho_{0})
.
Since
T
is positive and trace preserving,
\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
.
If
A\geq 0
and
A\neq 0
, then
A/\operatorname{Tr}\left(A\right)\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
, and therefore

T(A)=\operatorname{Tr}\left(A\right)\tau.

The same formula holds for
A=0
.
By decomposing a self-adjoint matrix into its positive and negative parts, and then decomposing a general matrix into real and imaginary self-adjoint parts, linearity gives

T(X)=\operatorname{Tr}\left(X\right)\tau\qquad\text{for every }X\in\mathbb{M}_{d}.

The converse implication is immediate.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

The coefficient
\kappa_{\rm tr}(T)
is defined on the real self-adjoint trace-zero space
\mathsf{H}_{0}
, because this is the space generated by differences of states.
It also controls the complex trace-zero space up to a universal factor.
Indeed, if
Z\in\mathbb{M}_{d}
satisfies
\operatorname{Tr}\left(Z\right)=0
, write

Z=A+iB,\qquad A=A^{*},\quad B=B^{*},\quad\operatorname{Tr}\left(A\right)=\operatorname{Tr}\left(B\right)=0.

Then

\norm{A}_{1}\leq\norm{Z}_{1},\qquad\norm{B}_{1}\leq\norm{Z}_{1},

and hence

\norm{T(Z)}_{1}\leq\norm{T(A)}_{1}+\norm{T(B)}_{1}\leq 2\kappa_{\rm tr}(T)\norm{Z}_{1}.

This is why decay of
\kappa_{\rm tr}
gives induced
1\to 1
convergence to replacement channels with only universal constants.

Equation (
2.1
) gives the precise memory-loss interpretation of
\kappa_{\rm tr}
.
The number
2\kappa_{\rm tr}(T)
is exactly the trace-norm diameter of the evolved state space:

\sup_{\rho,\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{d}}\norm{T(\rho)-T(\sigma)}_{1}=2\kappa_{\rm tr}(T).

Thus
\kappa_{\rm tr}(T)=0
exactly when
T
is a replacement channel.
For an inhomogeneous product
\Phi_{t:s}
, the identity

\sup_{\rho,\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{d}}\norm{\Phi_{t:s}(\rho)-\Phi_{t:s}(\sigma)}_{1}=2\kappa_{\rm tr}(\Phi_{t:s})

says that
\kappa_{\rm tr}(\Phi_{t:s})
is the exact residual trace-norm memory after the product.
Consequently,

\kappa_{\rm tr}(\Phi_{t:s})\longrightarrow 0

is exactly uniform trace-norm loss of memory of the initial state.
The deterministic results below show that this state-level memory loss is equivalent, up to universal constants, to convergence of the product toward a family of replacement channels.
The key property that makes the coefficient suitable for products is the following submultiplicativity.

Proposition 2.4
.

If
S,T:\mathbb{M}_{d}\to\mathbb{M}_{d}
are positive and trace preserving, then

\kappa_{\rm tr}(S\circ T)\leq\kappa_{\rm tr}(S)\kappa_{\rm tr}(T).

Proof.

Since
T
is positive, it maps self-adjoint matrices to self-adjoint matrices.
Since
T
is trace preserving, it maps trace-zero matrices to trace-zero matrices.
Hence
T(\mathsf{H}_{0})\subseteq\mathsf{H}_{0}
.

Let
X\in\mathsf{H}_{0}
.
If
T(X)=0
, then the desired estimate is immediate.
If
T(X)\neq 0
, then
T(X)\in\mathsf{H}_{0}\setminus\{0\}
, and therefore

\|S(T(X))\|_{1}\leq\kappa_{\rm tr}(S)\|T(X)\|_{1}\leq\kappa_{\rm tr}(S)\kappa_{\rm tr}(T)\|X\|_{1}.

The same inequality is also true when
T(X)=0
.
Taking the supremum over
X\in\mathsf{H}_{0}\setminus\{0\}
gives the result.
∎

Consequently, if
s<r<t
, then

\kappa_{\rm tr}(\Phi_{t:s})\leq\kappa_{\rm tr}(\Phi_{t:r})\kappa_{\rm tr}(\Phi_{r:s}).

This block submultiplicativity is the basic mechanism behind the deterministic and random product criteria developed below.

2.1
Comparison with one-step contraction criteria

The coefficient
\kappa_{\rm tr}
is a standard trace-distance contraction coefficient.
Our use of it is product-level.
For an inhomogeneous product
\Phi_{t:s}
, the quantity
\kappa_{\rm tr}(\Phi_{t:s})
is the exact residual-memory coefficient of the whole block.
Its decay to zero is precisely uniform trace-norm loss of memory.
The replacement results in
Section

3
show that this state-level memory loss is equivalent, up to universal constants, to map-level asymptotic replacement.
This distinguishes the present approach from one-step sufficient criteria such as Markov–Dobrushin and Doeblin minorization.
Also, we may use certain results in this section (also see
Appendix

B
and
Appendix

A
in the Appendix) as sufficient criteria to obtain decay of
\kappa_{\rm tr}(\Phi_{t:s})
that is needed in subsequent sections.

In the notation often used for contraction coefficients, the trace-distance contraction coefficient is

\eta^{\rm Tr}(\Phi):=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho,\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{d}\\
\rho\neq\sigma\end{subarray}}\frac{\norm{\Phi(\rho)-\Phi(\sigma)}_{1}}{\norm{\rho-\sigma}_{1}}.

By
2.2
, one has
\eta^{\rm Tr}(\Phi)=\kappa_{\rm tr}(\Phi)
.
The point of the present paper is not this one-step identity, but the use of the exact product coefficient
\kappa_{\rm tr}(\Phi_{t:s})
as the intrinsic memory coefficient for an entire block.

Let

\mathcal{P}_{1}:=\left\{P\in\mathbb{M}_{d}:P=P^{*}=P^{2},\ \operatorname{Tr}\left(P\right)=1\right\}

denote the set of rank-one projections.
The state-level Markov–Dobrushin coefficient is based on a common lower bound for the family
\{\Phi(P):P\in\mathcal{P}_{1}\}
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\Phi:\mathbb{M}_{d}\to\mathbb{M}_{d}
be CPTP.
Define

\alpha_{\rm MD}(\Phi):=\sup\left\{\operatorname{Tr}\left(B\right):B\geq 0,\ B\leq\Phi(P)\text{ for every }P\in\mathcal{P}_{1}\right\}.

The supremum is attained in finite dimension.
We also write

\eta_{\rm MD}(\Phi):=1-\alpha_{\rm MD}(\Phi).

Thus
\alpha_{\rm MD}(\Phi)
is the mass of the largest common lower bound, while
\eta_{\rm MD}(\Phi)=1-\alpha_{\rm MD}(\Phi)
is the resulting one-step contraction bound.
The number
\eta_{\rm MD}(\Phi)
is not, in general, the exact trace-distance contraction coefficient.

This is a state-level minorization condition.
It asks whether all output states
\Phi(P)
, with
P
pure, share a common positive part.
In the inhomogeneous-channel framework of
[
Sou25
]
, the same idea is formulated through a trace-maximal common lower bound
B_{\Phi}\in I^{\rm Tr}_{\rm MD}(\Phi)
, and the corresponding estimate can be written as

\norm{\Phi(\rho)-\Phi(\sigma)}_{1}\leq\left(1-\operatorname{Tr}\left(B_{\Phi}\right)\right)\norm{\rho-\sigma}_{1}.

The homogeneous MPS application in
[
SB25
]
uses positivity of such a Markov–Dobrushin lower bound to obtain exponential mixing and a thermodynamic limit.

Proposition 2.6
.

For every CPTP map
\Phi
,

\kappa_{\rm tr}(\Phi)\leq\eta_{\rm MD}(\Phi)=1-\alpha_{\rm MD}(\Phi).

Proof.

Let
B\geq 0
be an admissible common lower bound and put
a:=\operatorname{Tr}\left(B\right)
.
Since
B\leq\Phi(P)
and
\operatorname{Tr}\left(\Phi(P)\right)=1
for every
P\in\mathcal{P}_{1}
, one has
0\leq a\leq 1
.
If
a=1
, then
B
is a state.
Since
B\leq\Phi(P)
and
\operatorname{Tr}\left(\Phi(P)\right)=1
, we have
\Phi(P)=B
for every
P\in\mathcal{P}_{1}
.
Hence
\Phi
is constant on pure states, therefore constant on
\mathcal{S}_{d}
, and by linearity it is a replacement channel.
Thus
\kappa_{\rm tr}(\Phi)=0
.

Assume
a<1
.
If
\rho\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
has spectral decomposition
\rho=\sum_{i}p_{i}P_{i}
, then
B\leq\Phi(P_{i})
for each
i
, and therefore
\Phi(\rho)\geq B
.
Thus

\omega_{\rho}:=\frac{\Phi(\rho)-B}{1-a}

is a state.

Let
X\in\mathsf{H}_{0}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
.
As in the proof of
2.2
, write

X=\frac{\norm{X}_{1}}{2}\left(\rho_{+}-\rho_{-}\right),

where
\rho_{+},\rho_{-}\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
.
Applying the preceding decomposition to
\rho_{+}
and
\rho_{-}
, we obtain

\Phi(\rho_{+})-\Phi(\rho_{-})=\left(1-a\right)\left(\omega_{\rho_{+}}-\omega_{\rho_{-}}\right).

Hence

\norm{\Phi(\rho_+)-\Phi(\rho_-)}_{1}\leq 2\left(1-a\right).

Therefore

\frac{\norm{\Phi(X)}_{1}}{\norm{X}_{1}}=\frac{1}{2}\norm{\Phi(\rho_+)-\Phi(\rho_-)}_{1}\leq 1-a.

Taking the supremum over
X\in\mathsf{H}_{0}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
gives
\kappa_{\rm tr}(\Phi)\leq 1-\operatorname{Tr}\left(B\right)
.
Optimizing over admissible
B
proves the claim.
∎

Thus, every state-level Markov–Dobrushin lower bound gives a valid one-step upper bound on
\kappa_{\rm tr}
.
The converse is false.
A channel may contract trace distance strictly without admitting a nonzero common lower bound for all pure-state outputs.
For example, the qubit amplitude-damping channel with parameter
0<\gamma<1
,

\Gamma_{\gamma}(\rho)=K_{0}\rho K_{0}^{*}+K_{1}\rho K_{1}^{*},\qquad K_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&\sqrt{1-\gamma}\end{pmatrix},\qquad K_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\sqrt{\gamma}\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},

satisfies
\kappa_{\rm tr}(\Gamma_{\gamma})=\sqrt{1-\gamma}<1
, but
\alpha_{\rm MD}(\Gamma_{\gamma})=0
.
Indeed,
\Gamma_{\gamma}(|0\rangle\langle 0|)=|0\rangle\langle 0|
, so any common lower bound
B
must have the form
B=b|0\rangle\langle 0|
with
b\geq 0
.
For a pure input
|\psi\rangle=\alpha|0\rangle+\beta|1\rangle
, the condition
B\leq\Gamma_{\gamma}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)
forces
b\leq\gamma|\beta|^{2}
.
Letting
|\beta|\downarrow 0
gives
b=0
.
Thus
\alpha_{\rm MD}(\Gamma_{\gamma})=0
, even though
\kappa_{\rm tr}(\Gamma_{\gamma})=\sqrt{1-\gamma}<1
.

Other contraction mechanisms fit different geometries.
CP-order Doeblin coefficients impose an algebraic minorization of the whole channel, rather than only a common lower bound on pure-state outputs.
Hilbert–Birkhoff projective coefficients are adapted to cone-preserving maps and are especially useful beyond the trace-preserving setting.
Entropy, Riemannian, and
\chi^{2}
coefficients are adapted to faithful reference-state geometries.
Quantum Wasserstein–Dobrushin coefficients control spatial influence in many-body systems
[
Bak+25

.

3
Deterministic inhomogeneous products

We now pass from a single channel to deterministic products of channels.
Let
(\Phi_{n})_{n\in\mathcal{I}}
be a deterministic sequence of CPTP maps on
\mathbb{M}_{d}
.
The index set is either one-sided,
\mathcal{I}=\mathbb{N}_{0}
, or two-sided,
\mathcal{I}=\mathbb{Z}
, depending on whether only forward products or pullback limits are being discussed.

3.1
Product notation and fixed-start replacement

For
s<t
, we use the chronological convention

\Phi_{t:s}:=\Phi_{t-1}\circ\Phi_{t-2}\circ\cdots\circ\Phi_{s},\qquad\Phi_{s:s}:=\operatorname{id}.

Thus
\Phi_{t:s}
maps a state prepared at time
s
to its state at time
t
.
The cocycle identity is

\Phi_{t:s}=\Phi_{t:u}\circ\Phi_{u:s},\qquad s<u<t.

We write

\kappa_{t:s}:=\kappa_{\rm tr}\bigl(\Phi_{t:s}\bigr).

By
2.4
, the product coefficients satisfy

\kappa_{t:s}\leq\kappa_{t:u}\kappa_{u:s},\qquad s<u<t.

This is the basic deterministic submultiplicativity used throughout the sequel.

For
\zeta\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
, let

R_{\zeta}(X):=\operatorname{Tr}\left(X\right)\zeta.

We call
R_{\zeta}
the replacement channel with output
\zeta
.
If the state
\zeta
depends on the time window, then the corresponding replacement channel is a moving replacement channel.

The first point is that
\kappa_{t:s}
is the exact state-level memory coefficient of the product.
Since
\Phi_{t:s}
is CPTP,
2.2
applied to
T=\Phi_{t:s}
gives

\sup_{\rho,\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{d}}\norm{\Phi_{t:s}(\rho)-\Phi_{t:s}(\sigma)}_{1}=2\kappa_{t:s}.

(3.1)

Equivalently,

\kappa_{t:s}=\dfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{diam}_{1}\bigl(\Phi_{t:s}(\mathcal{S}_{d})\bigr),

where

\operatorname{diam}_{1}(A):=\sup_{x,y\in A}\norm{x-y}_{1}.

Thus, the product coefficient is one half of the trace-norm diameter of the evolved state space.

Definition 3.1

(Asymptotic fixed-start trace-memory loss)
.

Fix an initial time
s\in\mathcal{I}
.
The product sequence has asymptotic trace-memory loss from
s
if

\lim_{t\to\infty}\sup_{\rho,\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{d}}\norm{\Phi_{t:s}(\rho)-\Phi_{t:s}(\sigma)}_{1}=0.

By (
3.1
), for fixed
s
,

Definition

3.1
is equivalent to

\lim_{t\to\infty}\kappa_{t:s}=0.

Thus
\kappa_{t:s}\to 0
is exactly uniform trace-norm loss of memory of the initial state.
The next estimate upgrades this state-level statement to an induced
1\to 1
map-norm statement by comparing the whole product with a replacement channel.

For a reference state
\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
, define

Z_{t:s}^{(\tau)}:=\Phi_{t:s}(\tau),\qquad R_{t:s}^{(\tau)}(X):=\operatorname{Tr}\left(X\right)Z_{t:s}^{(\tau)}.

Thus
R_{t:s}^{(\tau)}
is the replacement channel centered at the evolved reference state.

For a linear map
L:\mathbb{M}_{d}\to\mathbb{M}_{d}
, we write

\norm{L}_{1\to 1}:=\sup_{X\in\mathbb{M}_{d}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\norm{L(X)}_{1}}{\norm{X}_{1}},

where
\norm{\,\cdot\,}_{1}
denotes the trace norm, equivalently the Schatten
1

\mathbb{M}_{d}

Proposition 3.2
.

For every
s<t
, every reference state
\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{d}

\kappa_{t:s}\leq\norm{\Phi_{t:s}-R_\zeta}_{1\to 1}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\norm{\Phi_{t:s}-R_{t:s}^{(\tau)}}_{1\to 1}\leq 4\kappa_{t:s}.

First fix

3.2
Pullback boundary states and moving replacement channels

Two-sided inhomogeneous products have a natural pullback viewpoint: the terminal time is fixed, while the initial time is sent to the remote past.
If products ending at
t
forget remote initial states in trace distance, then the state observed at time
t
becomes independent of the state inserted far in the past.
The resulting time-indexed state is a pullback boundary state, or entrance law, and the corresponding channel-level limit is a moving replacement family

R_{t}(X)=\operatorname{Tr}\left(X\right)\rho_{t}.

Throughout this subsection, assume that the sequence
(\Phi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is indexed by all integers.
For
s<t
, recall that

\Phi_{t:s}=\Phi_{t-1}\circ\Phi_{t-2}\circ\cdots\circ\Phi_{s}.

Definition 3.4

(Pullback boundary state)
.

A pullback boundary state, also called an entrance law, for
(\Phi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a sequence of states

(\rho_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset\mathcal{S}_{d}

such that

\rho_{t+1}=\Phi_{t}(\rho_{t})\qquad\text{for all }t\in\mathbb{Z}.

Equivalently, for every
s<t
,

\rho_{t}=\Phi_{t:s}(\rho_{s}).

The term entrance law is standard in the theory of time-inhomogeneous Markov processes.
The phrase pullback boundary state emphasizes the construction that will arise below: under pullback memory loss, the state
\rho_{t}
is obtained as the limit of states transported from the remote past.

Definition 3.5

(Pullback trace-memory loss)
.

The sequence
(\Phi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
has pullback trace-memory loss if, for every fixed terminal time
t\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\lim_{s\to-\infty}\kappa_{t:s}=0.

By
2.2
, applied to the product
\Phi_{t:s}
,

\sup_{\rho,\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{d}}\norm{\Phi_{t:s}(\rho)-\Phi_{t:s}(\sigma)}_{1}=2\kappa_{t:s}.

Thus, pullback trace-memory loss is exactly the statement that two arbitrary states inserted farther and farther in the past become indistinguishable when observed at the fixed terminal time
t
.

The next result is the state-level deterministic pullback replacement theorem.
It says that pullback decay of the exact trace-Dobrushin coefficient is precisely convergence, uniformly over input states, to a unique moving center
\rho_{t}
.
The induced
1\to 1
replacement statement follows afterward from
3.2
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
(\Phi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a two-sided deterministic sequence of CPTP maps.
The following are equivalent.

1.

Pullback trace-memory loss.

For every fixed terminal time

, we have that
\kappa_{t:s}\to 0
as

.

\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
and every
t\in\mathbb{Z}

\tau
.

s<t

t

to the moving center
\rho_{t}
as
s\to-\infty

The preceding subsections identify

The estimates are stated for a single product

This is the form needed for the deterministic MPS bounds, because a local observable with support up to time
m
only sees the auxiliary tail product after that support.

Recall that
\mathcal{I}
denotes the set of admissible starting times.
Thus
\mathcal{I}=\mathbb{N}_{0}
in the one-sided setting and
\mathcal{I}=\mathbb{Z}
in the two-sided setting.
For
s<t
, set

d_{t:s}:=\sup_{\rho,\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{d}}\norm{\Phi_{t:s}(\rho)-\Phi_{t:s}(\sigma)}_{1}.

By
2.2
,

d_{t:s}=2\kappa_{t:s}.

Thus, every upper bound on
\kappa_{t:s}
immediately gives a trace-diameter bound.
By
3.2
, every such upper bound also gives an induced
1\to 1
replacement bound:

\norm{\Phi_{t:s}-R_{t:s}^{(\tau)}}_{1\to 1}\leq 4\kappa_{t:s},\qquad R_{t:s}^{(\tau)}(X):=\operatorname{Tr}\left(X\right)\Phi_{t:s}(\tau).

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
s<t
.
Suppose that
a_{j}\in[0,1]
and

\kappa_{\rm tr}(\Phi_{j})\leq 1-a_{j},\qquad j=s,\ldots,t-1.

Then

\kappa_{t:s}\leq\prod_{j=s}^{t-1}(1-a_{j})\leq\exp\!\left(-\sum_{j=s}^{t-1}a_{j}\right).

For every
r\in(0,1)
, define

G_{r}(s,t):=\#\{j\in\{s,\ldots,t-1\}:a_{j}\geq r\}.

Then

\kappa_{t:s}\leq(1-r)^{G_{r}(s,t)}.

Consequently, for every reference state
\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
,

d_{t:s}\leq 2\prod_{j=s}^{t-1}(1-a_{j})\leq 2\exp\!\left(-\sum_{j=s}^{t-1}a_{j}\right),

and

\norm{\Phi_{t:s}-R_{t:s}^{(\tau)}}_{1\to 1}\leq 4\prod_{j=s}^{t-1}(1-a_{j})\leq 4\exp\!\left(-\sum_{j=s}^{t-1}a_{j}\right).

In particular, for every
r\in(0,1)
,

d_{t:s}\leq 2(1-r)^{G_{r}(s,t)},\qquad\norm{\Phi_{t:s}-R_{t:s}^{(\tau)}}_{1\to 1}\leq 4(1-r)^{G_{r}(s,t)}.

Proof.

By submultiplicativity,

\kappa_{t:s}\leq\prod_{j=s}^{t-1}\kappa_{\rm tr}(\Phi_{j})\leq\prod_{j=s}^{t-1}(1-a_{j}).

The exponential bound follows from the elementary inequality

1-a\leq e^{-a},\qquad a\in[0,1].

Fix
r\in(0,1)
.
For every
j\in\{s,\ldots,t-1\}
with
a_{j}\geq r
, the corresponding factor satisfies

1-a_{j}\leq 1-r.

All remaining factors are at most
1
.
Therefore

\prod_{j=s}^{t-1}(1-a_{j})\leq(1-r)^{G_{r}(s,t)},

and hence

\kappa_{t:s}\leq(1-r)^{G_{r}(s,t)}.

The estimates for
d_{t:s}
and for the replacement channel follow from

d_{t:s}=2\kappa_{t:s}

and from
3.2
.
∎

The preceding estimate is a one-step criterion.
The genuinely product-level mechanism is obtained by counting contractive blocks.
Suppose that
s<t
and that

\left[u_{1},v_{1}\right),\ldots,\left[u_{m},v_{m}\right)\subseteq\left[s,t\right)

are ordered disjoint subintervals.
This means that

s\leq u_{1}<v_{1}\leq u_{2}<v_{2}\leq\cdots\leq u_{m}<v_{m}\leq t.

If
\kappa_{v_{r}:u_{r}}\leq q_{r}\leq 1
for
r=1,\ldots,m
, then repeated use of submultiplicativity gives

\kappa_{t:s}\leq\prod_{r=1}^{m}q_{r},

(3.2)

with the empty product interpreted as
1
.
Indeed, the product
\Phi_{t:s}
factors into the selected blocks and the intervening gap products.
Every gap product is CPTP and hence has trace-Dobrushin coefficient at most
1
.

Fix
q\in\left(0,1\right)
.
Let
\mathcal{B}_{q}\left(s,t\right)
be the maximum number of pairwise disjoint nonempty subintervals

\left[u_{r},v_{r}\right)\subseteq\left[s,t\right)

such that

\kappa_{v_{r}:u_{r}}\leq q.

By
Equation

3.2
,

\kappa_{t:s}\leq q^{\mathcal{B}_{q}\left(s,t\right)},\qquad d_{t:s}\leq 2q^{\mathcal{B}_{q}\left(s,t\right)}.

For every
\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
, this also gives

\norm{\Phi_{t:s}-R_{t:s}^{\left(\tau\right)}}_{1\to 1}\leq 4q^{\mathcal{B}_{q}\left(s,t\right)}.

Thus, memory loss can be certified by contractive products even when no useful one-step contraction estimate is available.

Definition 3.8
.

Fix integers
\ell\geq 1
,
M\geq\ell
, and
q\in\left(0,1\right)
.
The sequence satisfies the uniform
\left(\ell,M,q\right)
-good-block condition if every admissible interval

\left[r,r+M\right),\qquad r,r+M\in\mathcal{I},

contains a length-
\ell
subblock

\left[u,u+\ell\right)\subseteq\left[r,r+M\right)

such that

\kappa_{u+\ell:u}\leq q.

Proposition 3.9
.

Assume the uniform
\left(\ell,M,q\right)
-good-block condition.
Then for every admissible product
\Phi_{t:s}
,

\kappa_{t:s}\leq q^{\left\lfloor\frac{t-s}{M}\right\rfloor}.

Consequently, for every reference state
\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
,

d_{t:s}\leq 2q^{\left\lfloor\frac{t-s}{M}\right\rfloor},\qquad\norm{\Phi_{t:s}-R_{t:s}^{\left(\tau\right)}}_{1\to 1}\leq 4q^{\left\lfloor\frac{t-s}{M}\right\rfloor}.

In particular,

\kappa_{t:s}\leq q^{-1}\exp\!\left(-\frac{\left|\log q\right|}{M}\left(t-s\right)\right).

Proof.

Set
L:=t-s
and
m:=\left\lfloor L/M\right\rfloor
.
We construct
m
disjoint
q
-contractive blocks inside
\left[s,t\right)
.

Set
r_{0}=s
.
Suppose that
0\leq j\leq m-1
and that
r_{j}
has already been chosen with

r_{j}\leq s+jM.

Then

r_{j}+M\leq s+\left(j+1\right)M\leq s+mM\leq t.

Hence
\left[r_{j},r_{j}+M\right)\subseteq\left[s,t\right)
.
By the uniform good-block condition, there exists a length-
\ell
subblock

\left[u_{j},u_{j}+\ell\right)\subseteq\left[r_{j},r_{j}+M\right)

such that

\kappa_{u_{j}+\ell:u_{j}}\leq q.

Set
r_{j+1}:=u_{j}+\ell
.
Then

r_{j+1}\leq r_{j}+M\leq s+\left(j+1\right)M.

The next block begins after the previous one ends.
Thus, the selected blocks are pairwise disjoint.

This constructs
\left\lfloor\left(t-s\right)/M\right\rfloor
disjoint
q
-contractive blocks in
\left[s,t\right)
.
Hence

\mathcal{B}_{q}\left(s,t\right)\geq\left\lfloor\frac{t-s}{M}\right\rfloor.

The block-count estimate gives

\kappa_{t:s}\leq q^{\left\lfloor\frac{t-s}{M}\right\rfloor}.

The bounds on
d_{t:s}
and
R_{t:s}^{\left(\tau\right)}
follow from
d_{t:s}=2\kappa_{t:s}
and
3.2
.
Finally,

q^{\left\lfloor\frac{t-s}{M}\right\rfloor}\leq q^{-1}\exp\!\left(-\frac{\left|\log q\right|}{M}\left(t-s\right)\right),

because

\left\lfloor\frac{t-s}{M}\right\rfloor\geq\frac{t-s}{M}-1.

∎

A useful special case is uniform contraction at one fixed block length.
If there are
L_{0}\geq 1
and
q\in\left(0,1\right)
such that

\kappa_{u+L_{0}:u}\leq q\qquad\text{for every admissible }u,

then
3.9
applies with
\ell=M=L_{0}
.
Consequently,

\kappa_{t:s}\leq q^{\left\lfloor\frac{t-s}{L_{0}}\right\rfloor},\qquad d_{t:s}\leq 2q^{\left\lfloor\frac{t-s}{L_{0}}\right\rfloor},

and

\norm{\Phi_{t:s}-R_{t:s}^{\left(\tau\right)}}_{1\to 1}\leq 4q^{\left\lfloor\frac{t-s}{L_{0}}\right\rfloor}.

This is the deterministic analogue of a uniform Doeblin condition.
The bounded-gap good-block condition is weaker.
It does not require every block of a fixed length to contract, only that contractive blocks occur with uniformly bounded gaps.

The block mechanism detects contraction that is invisible to one-step criteria.

Example 1
.

Let
D_{Z}
be complete dephasing in the computational basis of a qubit:

D_{Z}\left(\rho\right)=P_{0}\rho P_{0}+P_{1}\rho P_{1},\qquad P_{i}=\left|i\right\rangle\left\langle i\right|.

Let
D_{X}
be complete dephasing in the Hadamard basis:

D_{X}\left(\rho\right)=P_{+}\rho P_{+}+P_{-}\rho P_{-},\qquad P_{\pm}=\left|\pm\right\rangle\left\langle\pm\right|.

Then

\kappa_{\rm tr}\left(D_{Z}\right)=1,\qquad\kappa_{\rm tr}\left(D_{X}\right)=1.

Indeed,
D_{Z}
preserves the trace distance between
P_{0}
and
P_{1}
.
The channel
D_{X}
preserves the trace distance between
P_{+}
and
P_{-}
.
The one-step Markov–Dobrushin lower bound is also zero for both maps, since in each case there are pure inputs whose outputs are orthogonal.
However,

D_{X}\circ D_{Z}=R_{I/2},\qquad R_{I/2}\left(X\right)=\operatorname{Tr}\left(X\right)\frac{I}{2}.

Hence

\kappa_{\rm tr}\left(D_{X}\circ D_{Z}\right)=0.

Thus a two-step block can have perfect trace-memory loss even though every one-step coefficient is trivial.

Remark 3.10

(Certificates for good blocks)
.

Every occurrence of a condition of the form
\kappa_{v:u}\leq q
may be replaced by any rigorous certificate implying that bound.
Such a certificate may come from an exact computation of
\kappa_{\rm tr}
, a Markov–Dobrushin lower bound for the whole block, a CP-order Doeblin bound for the whole block, a projective-diameter estimate for the whole block, or any other valid contraction estimate.
The proofs use only submultiplicativity and the stated upper bounds on selected block coefficients.

The preceding criteria separate two independent mechanisms.
One-step clocks quantify the accumulation of small contractions at individual times.
Good-block clocks quantify contraction that appears only after composing several noncontractive channels.
Both mechanisms are used below in the deterministic MPS convergence estimates.

4
Random CPTP cocycles

We now pass from deterministic inhomogeneous products to stationary random products.
Let
\left(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\pr,\theta\right)
be an invertible probability-preserving system.
We use the standard terminology for measure-preserving systems and ergodicity from
[
Wal00
]
.
Let
\omega\mapsto\Phi_{\omega}
be a measurable map from
\Omega
into the finite-dimensional space of linear maps on
\mathbb{M}_{d}
, and assume that each
\Phi_{\omega}
is CPTP.
For
n\geq 1
, define the forward random product

\Phi_{\omega}^{(n)}:=\Phi_{\theta^{n-1}\omega}\circ\Phi_{\theta^{n-2}\omega}\circ\cdots\circ\Phi_{\omega},\qquad\Phi_{\omega}^{(0)}:=\mathrm{id}.

The associated trace-Dobrushin product coefficient is

\kappa_{n}(\omega):=\kappa_{\rm tr}\left(\Phi_{\omega}^{(n)}\right).

We also set
\kappa_{0}(\omega):=1
.
Since
\mathbb{M}_{d}
is finite-dimensional, the space of linear maps on
\mathbb{M}_{d}
is finite-dimensional.
For each fixed
j
, the map
\omega\mapsto\Phi_{\theta^{j}\omega}
is measurable.
The composition map on linear operators is continuous in finite dimensions.
Hence
\omega\mapsto\Phi_{\omega}^{(n)}
is measurable for every fixed
n
.
Every CPTP map preserves
\mathsf{H}_{0}
.
By definition,
\kappa_{\rm tr}(\Phi)
is the
1\to 1
operator norm of the restriction of
\Phi
to
\mathsf{H}_{0}
, where
\mathsf{H}_{0}
is equipped with the trace norm.
The operator norm depends continuously on the linear map in finite dimensions.
Therefore
\omega\mapsto\kappa_{\rm tr}(\Phi_{\omega}^{(n)})
is measurable.

The base transformation supplies the stationary environment for the sequence of channels.
The contraction of the channel product is a separate property.
It is measured by the decay of
\kappa_{n}(\omega)
.

Definition 4.1

(Base ergodicity)
.

The base transformation
\theta
is ergodic if every
E\in\mathcal{F}
satisfying
\theta^{-1}E=E
has
\pr(E)\in\{0,1\}
.
Equivalently,
\theta
is ergodic if every
E\in\mathcal{F}
satisfying
\pr(E\triangle\theta^{-1}E)=0
has
\pr(E)\in\{0,1\}
.
Here
\triangle
denotes the symmetric difference of sets.

We also refer the reader to
[
Wal00
]
for other equivalent formulations of ergodicity.

Remark 4.2
.

Ergodicity of
\theta
is a property of the randomness.
It does not by itself imply contraction of the products
\Phi_{\omega}^{(n)}
.
The channel-level memory loss studied below is the decay of
\kappa_{n}(\omega)
.
Any stronger temporal decorrelation assumption needed for annealed or high-probability estimates will be stated separately.
For example, let
\theta
be an i.i.d. shift and let
\Phi_{\omega}(X)=U_{\omega}XU_{\omega}^{*}
be a random unitary conjugation.
Then

\Phi_{\omega}^{(n)}(X)=V_{\omega,n}XV_{\omega,n}^{*},\qquad V_{\omega,n}:=U_{\theta^{n-1}\omega}\cdots U_{\omega}.

Thus, every product
\Phi_{\omega}^{(n)}
is again a unitary conjugation.
Since the trace norm is unitarily invariant, unitary conjugations act isometrically on
\mathsf{H}_{0}
.
Hence, when
d\geq 2
, one has
\kappa_{n}(\omega)=1
for all
n
.
Thus, even a strongly mixing or i.i.d. base can generate a channel cocycle with no trace-Dobrushin contraction.

We now introduce the pathwise notions of memory loss used in the random setting.
The first one is the coefficient-level formulation.
It says that, in almost every environment, the product eventually forgets all trace-zero perturbations of the initial state.

Definition 4.3

(Quenched trace-memory loss)
.

The random channel cocycle has quenched trace-memory loss if
\kappa_{n}(\omega)\to 0
for
\pr
-a.e.
\omega
.

By
2.2
, this is equivalent to uniform forgetting of pairs of initial states along almost every realization.
Indeed,

\sup_{\rho,\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{d}}\norm{
\Phi_\omega^{(n)}(\rho)-\Phi_\omega^{(n)}(\sigma)
}_{1}=2\kappa_{n}(\omega).

Consequently, quenched trace-memory loss holds if and only if

\sup_{\rho,\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{d}}\norm{
\Phi_\omega^{(n)}(\rho)-\Phi_\omega^{(n)}(\sigma)
}_{1}\longrightarrow 0

for
\pr
-a.e.
\omega
.
Thus
\kappa_{n}(\omega)
is the random analogue of the deterministic memory coefficient
\kappa_{t:s}
.

The same loss of memory can also be expressed at the channel level.
For a fixed realization
\omega
, the product
\Phi_{\omega}^{(n)}
is an ordinary deterministic inhomogeneous product, so the natural random analogue of strong asymptotic replacement is obtained by requiring the deterministic replacement property for
\pr
-a.e. fiber.

Definition 4.4

(Quenched strong asymptotic replacement)
.

The random channel cocycle is quenched strongly asymptotically replacing if there is a full-measure set
\Omega_{0}\subseteq\Omega
such that, for every
\omega\in\Omega_{0}
, there are states
\eta_{\omega,n}\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
with the following property.
If

R_{\omega,n}(X):=\operatorname{Tr}\left(X\right)\eta_{\omega,n},

then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\norm{\Phi_\omega^{(n)}-R_{\omega,n}}_{1\to 1}=0.

4.1
Trace-Dobrushin Lyapunov exponent

We now pass from the random product coefficients to their exponential growth rate.
Throughout this subsection we use the convention
\log 0=-\infty
.
For
n\geq 1
, set

F_{n}(\omega):=\log\kappa_{n}(\omega).

Since
0\leq\kappa_{n}(\omega)\leq 1
, each
F_{n}
takes values in
\left[-\infty,0\right]
.

Proposition 4.5
.

For all
m,n\geq 0
,

\kappa_{n+m}(\omega)\leq\kappa_{n}\left(\theta^{m}\omega\right)\kappa_{m}(\omega).

Consequently, for
m,n\geq 1
,

F_{n+m}(\omega)\leq F_{m}(\omega)+F_{n}\left(\theta^{m}\omega\right).

Proof.

If
m=0
or
n=0
, the first estimate follows from
\kappa_{0}=1
.
Assume
m,n\geq 1
.
The cocycle identity gives

\Phi_{\omega}^{(n+m)}=\Phi_{\theta^{m}\omega}^{(n)}\circ\Phi_{\omega}^{(m)}.

By
2.4
,

\kappa_{\rm tr}\left(\Phi_{\omega}^{(n+m)}\right)\leq\kappa_{\rm tr}\left(\Phi_{\theta^{m}\omega}^{(n)}\right)\kappa_{\rm tr}\left(\Phi_{\omega}^{(m)}\right).

This is the desired inequality.
Taking logarithms gives the second estimate when both factors on the right are nonzero.
If one factor on the right is zero, then the product estimate forces
\kappa_{n+m}(\omega)=0
.
Thus, the logarithmic inequality remains valid with the convention
\log 0=-\infty
.
∎

We can now apply Kingman’s subadditive ergodic theorem
[
Kin73
]
.

Theorem 4.1

(Trace-Dobrushin Lyapunov exponent)
.

There exists a
\theta
-invariant random variable

\lambda_{\rm tr}:\Omega\to\left[-\infty,0\right]

such that

\lambda_{\rm tr}(\omega)=\lim_{n\to\infty}{\dfrac{1}{n}}\log\kappa_{n}(\omega)

for
\pr
-a.e.
\omega
.
If
\theta
is ergodic, then
\lambda_{\rm tr}
is a.s. constant.

In the ergodic case, this constant satisfies

\lambda_{\rm tr}=\inf_{n\geq 1}{\dfrac{1}{n}}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\log\kappa_{n}\right].

Proof.

We already saw that each
F_{n}
is measurable.
Moreover,
F_{n}^{+}=0\in L^{1}(\pr)
for every
n\geq 1
.
By
4.5
, the sequence
(F_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is subadditive over
\theta
:

F_{n+m}(\omega)\leq F_{m}(\omega)+F_{n}(\theta^{m}\omega).

Kingman’s subadditive ergodic theorem, in its extended-valued form, gives the a.s. existence of

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\dfrac{1}{n}}F_{n}(\omega).

We denote this limit by
\lambda_{\rm tr}(\omega)
.
The same theorem gives that
\lambda_{\rm tr}
is
\theta
-invariant.

If
\theta
is ergodic, then every
\theta
-invariant random variable is a.s. constant.
Thus
\lambda_{\rm tr}
is a.s. constant.
For nonpositive extended random variables, we use the convention

\mathbb{E}[F]:=-\mathbb{E}[-F]\in[-\infty,0].

The ergodic form of Kingman’s theorem gives

\lambda_{\rm tr}=\inf_{n\geq 1}{\dfrac{1}{n}}\,\mathbb{E}[F_{n}]=\inf_{n\geq 1}{\dfrac{1}{n}}\,\mathbb{E}[\log\kappa_{n}].

∎

For integers
s<t
, set

\Phi_{\omega;t:s}:=\Phi_{\theta^{t-1}\omega}\circ\Phi_{\theta^{t-2}\omega}\circ\cdots\circ\Phi_{\theta^{s}\omega},\qquad\kappa_{\omega;t:s}:=\kappa_{\rm tr}\left(\Phi_{\omega;t:s}\right).

Thus
\Phi_{\omega}^{(n)}=\Phi_{\omega;n:0}
and
\kappa_{n}(\omega)=\kappa_{\omega;n:0}
.
The chronological product over the past interval
\left[-n,0\right]
is

\Phi_{\omega;0:-n}=\Phi_{\theta^{-1}\omega}\circ\Phi_{\theta^{-2}\omega}\circ\cdots\circ\Phi_{\theta^{-n}\omega},

and its coefficient is

\kappa_{\omega;0:-n}=\kappa_{n}\left(\theta^{-n}\omega\right).

With these notations, we have the following result.

Proposition 4.6
.

For
\pr
-a.e.
\omega
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\dfrac{1}{n}}\log\kappa_{\omega;0:-n}=\lambda_{\rm tr}(\omega).

Equivalently,

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\dfrac{1}{n}}\log\kappa_{n}\left(\theta^{-n}\omega\right)=\lambda_{\rm tr}(\omega)\qquad\text{for }\pr\text{-a.e. }\omega.

Proof.

Set
F_{n}(\omega):=\log\kappa_{n}(\omega)
, with the convention
\log 0=-\infty
.
Set

G_{n}(\omega):=\log\kappa_{\omega;0:-n}=F_{n}\left(\theta^{-n}\omega\right).

We first check subadditivity over
S:=\theta^{-1}
.
By
4.5
, applied at
\theta^{-(n+m)}\omega
, one has

\kappa_{n+m}\left(\theta^{-(n+m)}\omega\right)\leq\kappa_{n}\left(\theta^{-n}\omega\right)\kappa_{m}\left(\theta^{-(n+m)}\omega\right).

Equivalently,

G_{n+m}(\omega)\leq G_{n}(\omega)+G_{m}\left(\theta^{-n}\omega\right).

Since
S^{n}\omega=\theta^{-n}\omega
, the sequence
\left(G_{n}\right)_{n\geq 1}
is subadditive over
S
.

Each
G_{n}
is measurable and takes values in
\left[-\infty,0\right]
.
Hence
G_{n}^{+}=0\in L^{1}\left(\pr\right)
.
Kingman’s theorem applied to the probability-preserving system
\left(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\pr,\theta^{-1}\right)
gives the a.s. existence of

\lambda_{\rm tr}^{\rm past}(\omega):=\lim_{n\to\infty}{\dfrac{1}{n}}G_{n}(\omega).

By the conditional form of Kingman’s theorem for extended nonpositive subadditive cocycles,

\lambda_{\rm tr}^{\rm past}=\inf_{n\geq 1}{\dfrac{1}{n}}\mathbb{E}\left[G_{n}\mid\mathcal{I}_{\theta^{-1}}\right]\qquad\text{a.s.}

The invariant
\sigma
-algebras of
\theta
and
\theta^{-1}
coincide, so
\mathcal{I}_{\theta^{-1}}=\mathcal{I}_{\theta}
.

It remains to identify the conditional expectations.
Since
G_{n}=F_{n}\circ\theta^{-n}
, it is enough to prove

\mathbb{E}\left[F_{n}\circ\theta^{-n}\mid\mathcal{I}_{\theta}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[F_{n}\mid\mathcal{I}_{\theta}\right].

We use the extended nonpositive convention.
Equivalently, we prove the corresponding identity for the nonnegative random variable
H_{n}:=-F_{n}
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{I}_{\theta}
.
Since
\theta
is invertible and probability preserving,

\int_{A}H_{n}\circ\theta^{-n}\,d\pr=\int_{\theta^{-n}A}H_{n}\,d\pr.

Since
A\in\mathcal{I}_{\theta}
, we have
\theta^{-n}A=A
modulo null sets.
Therefore

\int_{A}H_{n}\circ\theta^{-n}\,d\pr=\int_{A}H_{n}\,d\pr.

Thus

\mathbb{E}\left[H_{n}\circ\theta^{-n}\mid\mathcal{I}_{\theta}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[H_{n}\mid\mathcal{I}_{\theta}\right].

Multiplying by
-1
gives

\mathbb{E}\left[G_{n}\mid\mathcal{I}_{\theta}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[F_{n}\mid\mathcal{I}_{\theta}\right].

Hence

\lambda_{\rm tr}^{\rm past}=\inf_{n\geq 1}{\dfrac{1}{n}}\mathbb{E}\left[G_{n}\mid\mathcal{I}_{\theta}\right]=\inf_{n\geq 1}{\dfrac{1}{n}}\mathbb{E}\left[F_{n}\mid\mathcal{I}_{\theta}\right]=\lambda_{\rm tr}.

This proves the claim.
∎

We conclude this part of the random theory by identifying when the trace-Dobrushin Lyapunov exponent is strictly negative.
This is the key condition behind the exponential form of quenched memory loss.
The first observation is that, in the stationary setting, almost-sure decay of the product coefficients already rules out the borderline case
\lambda_{\rm tr}=0
.

Proposition 4.7
.

The following are equivalent:

1.

\lambda_{\rm tr}(\omega)<0
, for
\pr
-a.e.
\omega
.

2.

\kappa_{\omega;n:0}\longrightarrow 0
, for
\pr
-a.e.
\omega
.

3.

\kappa_{\omega;0:-n}\longrightarrow 0
, for
\pr
-a.e.
\omega
.

Proof.

Assume first that
\lambda_{\rm tr}(\omega)<0
for
\pr
-a.e.
\omega
.
By
Theorem

4.1
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\dfrac{1}{n}}\log\kappa_{\omega;n:0}=\lambda_{\rm tr}(\omega)

for
\pr
-a.e.
\omega
.
Hence
\kappa_{\omega;n:0}\to 0
for
\pr
-a.e.
\omega
.

By
4.6
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\dfrac{1}{n}}\log\kappa_{\omega;0:-n}=\lambda_{\rm tr}(\omega)

for
\pr
-a.e.
\omega
.
Thus
\kappa_{\omega;0:-n}\to 0
for
\pr
-a.e.
\omega
as well.

It remains to prove the converse.
Assume that

\kappa_{\omega;n:0}\longrightarrow 0\qquad\text{for }\pr\text{-a.e. }\omega.

Set
F_{n}(\omega):=\log\kappa_{\omega;n:0}
, with the convention
\log 0=-\infty
.
Let

A:=\left\{\omega:\lambda_{\rm tr}(\omega)=0\right\}.

Since
\lambda_{\rm tr}
is
\theta
-invariant,
A\in\mathcal{I}_{\theta}
.
By Kingman’s conditional variational formula,

\lambda_{\rm tr}=\inf_{n\geq 1}{\dfrac{1}{n}}\mathbb{E}\left[F_{n}\mid\mathcal{I}_{\theta}\right]\qquad\text{a.s.}

Since
F_{n}\leq 0
, we have
\mathbb{E}\left[F_{n}\mid\mathcal{I}_{\theta}\right]\leq 0
.
On
A
, for every
n\geq 1
,

0=\lambda_{\rm tr}\leq{\dfrac{1}{n}}\mathbb{E}\left[F_{n}\mid\mathcal{I}_{\theta}\right]\leq 0.

Therefore

\mathbb{E}\left[F_{n}\mid\mathcal{I}_{\theta}\right]=0\qquad\text{a.s. on }A.

Equivalently, for
H_{n}:=-F_{n}\geq 0
,

\mathbb{E}\left[H_{n}\mid\mathcal{I}_{\theta}\right]=0\qquad\text{a.s. on }A.

Because
A\in\mathcal{I}_{\theta}
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{1}_{A}H_{n}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{1}_{A}\mathbb{E}\left[H_{n}\mid\mathcal{I}_{\theta}\right]\right]=0.

Since
\mathbf{1}_{A}H_{n}\geq 0
, this gives
H_{n}=0
for
\pr
-a.e.
\omega\in A
.
Thus
F_{n}=0
, equivalently

\kappa_{\omega;n:0}=1,

for
\pr
-a.e.
\omega\in A
, for each fixed
n\geq 1
.
Taking a countable intersection over
n
, we get

\kappa_{\omega;n:0}=1\qquad\text{for every }n\geq 1

for
\pr
-a.e.
\omega\in A
.

This contradicts
\kappa_{\omega;n:0}\to 0
on any subset of
A
of positive measure.
Hence
\pr\left(A\right)=0
.
Since
\lambda_{\rm tr}\in\left[-\infty,0\right]
, this proves
\lambda_{\rm tr}<0
a.s.

Finally, assume instead that

\kappa_{\omega;0:-n}\longrightarrow 0\qquad\text{for }\pr\text{-a.e. }\omega.

Set
G_{n}(\omega):=\log\kappa_{\omega;0:-n}
.
As in the proof of
4.6
, the sequence
\left(G_{n}\right)_{n\geq 1}
is subadditive over
\theta^{-1}
.
Applying the argument above to the probability-preserving system
\left(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\pr,\theta^{-1}\right)
shows that the pullback Kingman exponent is negative a.s.
By
4.6
, this exponent agrees with
\lambda_{\rm tr}
.
Therefore
\lambda_{\rm tr}<0
a.s.
∎

4.2
Sufficient criteria for negative exponent

We now record practical criteria for checking
\lambda_{\rm tr}<0
.
The sharp criterion is the existence of a finite block which is strictly contractive with positive probability.
Strict positivity and Doeblin minorization are useful sufficient mechanisms for producing such blocks.

The first criterion is intrinsic.
It says that, in the ergodic case, negativity of the Lyapunov exponent is equivalent to the existence of one fixed block length which contracts on a set of positive probability.

Proposition 4.8
.

Assume that
\theta
is ergodic.
Then
\lambda_{\rm tr}<0
if and only if there exists
L\geq 1
such that

\pr\left\{\omega:\kappa_{\omega;L:0}<1\right\}>0.

Equivalently,
\lambda_{\rm tr}<0
if and only if there exists
L\geq 1
such that

\mathbb{E}\left[\log\kappa_{\omega;L:0}\right]<0,

where the expectation is interpreted in the extended nonpositive sense.

Proof.

Set
F_{L}\left(\omega\right):=\log\kappa_{\omega;L:0}
, with the convention
\log 0=-\infty
.
Since
0\leq\kappa_{\omega;L:0}\leq 1
, one has
F_{L}\leq 0
.
Assume first that
\pr\left\{F_{L}<0\right\}>0
for some
L\geq 1
.
Then
-F_{L}
is a nonnegative extended random variable which is positive on a set of positive probability.
Hence

\mathbb{E}\left[F_{L}\right]=-\mathbb{E}\left[-F_{L}\right]<0.

By the ergodic variational formula in
Theorem

4.1
,

\lambda_{\rm tr}=\inf_{n\geq 1}{\dfrac{1}{n}}\mathbb{E}\left[\log\kappa_{\omega;n:0}\right].

Therefore

\lambda_{\rm tr}\leq{\dfrac{1}{L}}\mathbb{E}\left[F_{L}\right]<0.

Conversely, assume that
\lambda_{\rm tr}<0
.
The same variational formula gives

\inf_{n\geq 1}{\dfrac{1}{n}}\mathbb{E}\left[\log\kappa_{\omega;n:0}\right]<0.

Thus there exists
L\geq 1
such that
\mathbb{E}\left[F_{L}\right]<0
.
Since
F_{L}\leq 0
, this is possible only if
\pr\left\{F_{L}<0\right\}>0
.
Equivalently,

\pr\left\{\omega:\kappa_{\omega;L:0}<1\right\}>0.

∎

The preceding proposition reduces the problem to producing a block with
\kappa_{\omega;L:0}<1
on a set of positive probability.
A convenient way to certify this is through a Doeblin-type minorization.
The following elementary lemma records the contraction consequence of such a minorization.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
S:\mathbb{M}_{d}\to\mathbb{M}_{d}
be CPTP.
Assume that there are
\varepsilon\in\left(0,1\right]
and
\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
such that

S\left(X\right)\geq\varepsilon\,\operatorname{Tr}\left(X\right)\tau\qquad\text{for all }X\geq 0.

Then

\kappa_{\rm tr}\left(S\right)\leq 1-\varepsilon.

Proof.

Let
R_{\tau}\left(X\right):=\operatorname{Tr}\left(X\right)\tau
.
The minorization assumption says that
S-\varepsilon R_{\tau}
is positive.
Assume first that
\varepsilon<1
.
Then

\widetilde{S}:={\dfrac{S-\varepsilon R_{\tau}}{1-\varepsilon}}

is positive and trace preserving.
For every self-adjoint
X\in\mathsf{H}_{0}
, one has
R_{\tau}\left(X\right)=0
, and therefore

S\left(X\right)=\left(1-\varepsilon\right)\widetilde{S}\left(X\right).

Since positive trace-preserving maps are trace-norm contractions on self-adjoint matrices,

\norm{S\left(X\right)}_{1}\leq\left(1-\varepsilon\right)\norm{X}_{1}.

Taking the supremum over nonzero self-adjoint
X\in\mathsf{H}_{0}
gives

\kappa_{\rm tr}\left(S\right)\leq 1-\varepsilon.

If
\varepsilon=1
, then for every
X\geq 0
,

S(X)-R_{\tau}(X)\geq 0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\operatorname{Tr}\left(S(X)-R_{\tau}(X)\right)=0.

Hence
S(X)=R_{\tau}(X)
for every
X\geq 0
.
By linearity,
S=R_{\tau}
on
\mathbb{M}_{d}
, and therefore
\kappa_{\rm tr}(S)=0
.
∎

Strict positivity is a familiar source of such a minorization.
Indeed, in finite dimension, a strictly positive channel sends the compact state space into the interior of the positive cone, and hence admits a uniform lower bound.
The next corollary applies this observation to random blocks.

Corollary 4.10
.

Assume that
\theta
is ergodic.
If there exists
L\geq 1
such that

\pr\left\{\omega:\Phi_{\omega;L:0}\text{ is strictly positive}\right\}>0,

then
\lambda_{\rm tr}<0
.
In particular, if the cocycle is eventually strictly positive, meaning that for
\pr
-a.e.
\omega
there exists
n=n\left(\omega\right)\geq 1
such that
\Phi_{\omega;n:0}
is strictly positive, then
\lambda_{\rm tr}<0
.

Proof.

Let

E_{L}:=\left\{\omega:\Phi_{\omega;L:0}\text{ is strictly positive}\right\}.

By assumption,
\pr\left(E_{L}\right)>0
.
This is a measurable set.
Indeed,
\omega\mapsto\Phi_{\omega;L:0}
is measurable, and the map

S\longmapsto\min_{\rho\in\mathcal{S}_{d}}\lambda_{\min}(S(\rho))

is continuous in finite dimensions.
Moreover,
S
is strictly positive if and only if this minimum is strictly positive.

Fix
\omega\in E_{L}
, and set
S:=\Phi_{\omega;L:0}
.
The map

\rho\longmapsto\lambda_{\min}\left(S\left(\rho\right)\right)

is continuous on the compact state space
\mathcal{S}_{d}
.
Since
S
is strictly positive,
S\left(\rho\right)>0
for every
\rho\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
.
Hence

a_{\omega}:=\min_{\rho\in\mathcal{S}_{d}}\lambda_{\min}\left(S\left(\rho\right)\right)>0.

Thus
S\left(\rho\right)\geq a_{\omega}I
for every
\rho\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
.
If
X\geq 0
and
X\neq 0
, then
X=\operatorname{Tr}\left(X\right)\rho
for some
\rho\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
, so

S\left(X\right)=\operatorname{Tr}\left(X\right)S\left(\rho\right)\geq da_{\omega}\,\operatorname{Tr}\left(X\right){\dfrac{I}{d}}.

The same inequality is trivial for
X=0
.
Set
\varepsilon_{\omega}:=da_{\omega}
.
Since
S
is trace preserving,
0<\varepsilon_{\omega}\leq 1
.

By
Lemma

4.9
,

\kappa_{\omega;L:0}=\kappa_{\rm tr}\left(S\right)\leq 1-\varepsilon_{\omega}<1.

Therefore

E_{L}\subset\left\{\omega:\kappa_{\omega;L:0}<1\right\}.

Hence

\pr\left\{\omega:\kappa_{\omega;L:0}<1\right\}>0.

The conclusion follows from
4.8
.

If the cocycle is eventually strictly positive, then

\pr\left(\bigcup_{L\geq 1}\left\{\omega:\Phi_{\omega;L:0}\text{ is strictly positive}\right\}\right)=1.

Since this is a countable union, there exists
L\geq 1
such that

\pr\left\{\omega:\Phi_{\omega;L:0}\text{ is strictly positive}\right\}>0.

The first part applies.
∎

The strict-positivity assumption is stronger than necessary.
For the trace-Dobrushin exponent, it is enough to have a Doeblin minorization for a fixed random block, even if the block is not strictly positive.

Corollary 4.11
.

Assume that
\theta
is ergodic.
Suppose that there exist
L\geq 1
, a measurable function
\varepsilon:\Omega\to[0,1]
, and a measurable random state
\tau:\Omega\to\mathcal{S}_{d}
such that

\Phi_{\omega;L:0}\left(X\right)\geq\varepsilon\left(\omega\right)\operatorname{Tr}\left(X\right)\tau_{\omega}\qquad\text{for all }X\geq 0

for
\pr
-a.e.
\omega
, and

\mathbb{E}\left[\log\left(1-\varepsilon\right)\right]<0

in the extended nonpositive sense.
Then
\lambda_{\rm tr}<0
.
In particular, it is enough that
\pr\left\{\varepsilon>0\right\}>0
.

Proof.

By
Lemma

4.9
,

\kappa_{\omega;L:0}\leq 1-\varepsilon\left(\omega\right)

for
\pr
-a.e.
\omega
.
Hence

\mathbb{E}\left[\log\kappa_{\omega;L:0}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\log\left(1-\varepsilon\right)\right]<0.

The conclusion follows from
4.8
.
If
\pr\left\{\varepsilon>0\right\}>0
, then
\log\left(1-\varepsilon\right)<0
on a set of positive probability and is never positive.
Thus, its extended expectation is strictly negative.
∎

One common way to verify a Doeblin minorization is to find a common positive lower bound for the images of rank-one projections.
This is the block-level version of the quantum Markov–Dobrushin certificate.

Corollary 4.12
.

Assume that
\theta
is ergodic.
Suppose that there exist
L\geq 1
and a measurable positive operator
B_{\omega}\geq 0
such that

B_{\omega}\leq\Phi_{\omega;L:0}\left(P\right)\qquad\text{for every rank-one projection }P

for
\pr
-a.e.
\omega
, and

\mathbb{E}\left[\log\left(1-\operatorname{Tr}\left(B_{\omega}\right)\right)\right]<0.

Then
\lambda_{\rm tr}<0
.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon\left(\omega\right):=\operatorname{Tr}\left(B_{\omega}\right)
.
If
\varepsilon\left(\omega\right)>0
, set
\tau_{\omega}:=B_{\omega}/\varepsilon\left(\omega\right)
, and choose
\tau_{\omega}
arbitrarily when
\varepsilon\left(\omega\right)=0
.

For every state
\rho
, write a spectral decomposition
\rho=\sum_{i}p_{i}P_{i}
into rank-one projections.
Then

\Phi_{\omega;L:0}\left(\rho\right)=\sum_{i}p_{i}\Phi_{\omega;L:0}\left(P_{i}\right)\geq\sum_{i}p_{i}B_{\omega}=B_{\omega}.

By homogeneity, for every
X\geq 0
,

\Phi_{\omega;L:0}\left(X\right)\geq\operatorname{Tr}\left(X\right)B_{\omega}=\varepsilon\left(\omega\right)\operatorname{Tr}\left(X\right)\tau_{\omega}.

Thus
4.11
applies.
∎

The preceding criteria show that eventual strict positivity is a sufficient mechanism for a negative trace-Dobrushin exponent.
It is not a necessary mechanism.
The next example shows that replacement mixing can occur even when no power of the channel is strictly positive.

Example 2
.

Let the base space be trivial, and let the single channel be the qubit amplitude-damping channel
T_{\gamma}
, where
0<\gamma<1
.
In Bloch coordinates, centered state differences transform as

\left(x,y,z\right)\longmapsto\left(\sqrt{1-\gamma}\,x,\sqrt{1-\gamma}\,y,\left(1-\gamma\right)z\right).

Since the trace norm of a centered qubit difference equals the Euclidean norm of the corresponding Bloch-vector difference,

\kappa_{\rm tr}\left(T_{\gamma}\right)=\sqrt{1-\gamma}<1.

The same computation gives

\kappa_{\rm tr}\left(T_{\gamma}^{n}\right)=\left(\sqrt{1-\gamma}\right)^{n}.

Thus

\lambda_{\rm tr}=\log\sqrt{1-\gamma}<0.

The channel therefore has exponential replacement mixing, and the attracting replacement state is the pure state
\ket{0}\bra{0}
.

However,

T_{\gamma}^{n}\left(\ket{0}\bra{0}\right)=\ket{0}\bra{0}\qquad\text{for every }n\geq 1.

Thus no power of
T_{\gamma}
maps every nonzero positive operator to a strictly positive operator.
Consequently,
T_{\gamma}
is not eventually strictly positive.
This shows that, for CPTP cocycles, eventual strict positivity is only a sufficient subclass of the negative trace-Dobrushin exponent regime.

Removing ergodicity.

The ergodicity assumption in the preceding positive-probability criteria is used only to rule out invariant components on which no good block occurs.
There is a direct non-ergodic replacement that does not require choosing ergodic components.

For a measurable set
E\subset\Omega
, define its two-sided saturation by

\operatorname{Sat}(E):=\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\theta^{-j}E.

Thus, in the general probability-preserving case, the ergodic assumption

\pr(E)>0

should be replaced by

\pr\!\left(\operatorname{Sat}(E)\right)=1.

Equivalently, every invariant measurable set
A\in\mathcal{F}
with
\pr(A)>0
satisfies

\pr(A\cap E)>0.

Under this replacement, the preceding positive-probability sufficient criteria remain valid without assuming that
\theta
is ergodic.

Indeed, let

F_{n}(\omega):=\log\kappa_{\omega;n:0},\qquad F_{n}\leq 0,

and let

\lambda_{\rm tr}(\omega)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}F_{n}(\omega)

be the Kingman exponent. Suppose that
E=\bigcup_{r\geq 1}E_{r}
, where for each
r
there is
a block length
L_{r}
such that

E_{r}\subset\left\{\omega:\kappa_{\omega;L_{r}:0}<1\right\}.

Assume

\pr\!\left(\operatorname{Sat}(E)\right)=1.

We claim that
\lambda_{\rm tr}<0
a.s. Let

Z:=\{\omega:\lambda_{\rm tr}(\omega)=0\}.

Since
\lambda_{\rm tr}\leq 0
and is
\theta
-invariant,
Z
is invariant. If

\pr(Z)>0
, then the saturation assumption gives

\pr(Z\cap E)>0.

Since
E=\bigcup_{r\geq 1}E_{r}
is a countable union, there exists
r
such that

\pr(Z\cap E_{r})>0.

On
E_{r}
one has
F_{L_{r}}<0
, while
F_{L_{r}}\leq 0
everywhere. Hence

\int_{Z}F_{L_{r}}\,d\pr<0.

Applying Kingman’s variational formula to the restricted probability-preserving system on

Z
, normalized by
\pr(Z)
, gives

\int_{Z}\lambda_{\rm tr}\,d\pr\leq\frac{1}{L_{r}}\int_{Z}F_{L_{r}}\,d\pr<0.

But by definition
\lambda_{\rm tr}=0
on
Z
, a contradiction. Therefore

\pr(Z)=0
, and hence
\lambda_{\rm tr}<0
a.s.

For example, in
4.11
, if ergodicity is dropped, it is enough to assume

\pr\!\left(\operatorname{Sat}\{\omega:\varepsilon(\omega)>0\}\right)=1.

Indeed, the Doeblin minorization gives

\kappa_{\omega;L:0}\leq 1-\varepsilon(\omega),

so the event
\{\varepsilon>0\}
is contained in

\left\{\omega:\kappa_{\omega;L:0}<1\right\}.

The preceding saturation principle therefore gives
\lambda_{\rm tr}<0
a.s.

4.3
Stationary random states and replacement estimates

The Lyapunov exponent controls the diameter of long channel products.
To obtain convergence to a rank-one channel, we must identify the moving center of the image sets.
This center is constructed by fixing the terminal time and sending the initial time to the remote past.

For integers
s<t
, recall that

\Phi_{\omega;t:s}:=\Phi_{\theta^{t-1}\omega}\circ\Phi_{\theta^{t-2}\omega}\circ\cdots\circ\Phi_{\theta^{s}\omega},\qquad\kappa_{\omega;t:s}:=\kappa_{\rm tr}\left(\Phi_{\omega;t:s}\right).

Thus the forward product is
\Phi_{\omega}^{(n)}=\Phi_{\omega;n:0}
, and the pullback product ending at
\omega
is

\Phi_{\omega;0:-n}=\Phi_{\theta^{-1}\omega}\circ\Phi_{\theta^{-2}\omega}\circ\cdots\circ\Phi_{\theta^{-n}\omega}.

The product
\Phi_{\omega;0:-n}
is the chronological product over the past interval from time
-n
to time
0
.
It is not the reverse-ordered product on the same interval.

Proposition 4.13
.

Assume that
\kappa_{\omega;0:-n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
for
\pr
-a.e.
\omega
.
Fix
\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
, and set
\rho_{\omega,n}:=\Phi_{\omega;0:-n}\left(\tau\right)
.
Then
\rho_{\omega,n}
converges in trace norm for
\pr
-a.e.
\omega
, and the limit

\rho_{\omega}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Phi_{\omega;0:-n}\left(\tau\right)
is independent of the choice of
\tau
.
After modifying on a null set, the map
\omega\mapsto\rho_{\omega}
is measurable and satisfies

\Phi_{\omega}\left(\rho_{\omega}\right)=\rho_{\theta\omega}\qquad\text{for }\pr\text{-a.e. }\omega.

For the associated random replacement channel
R_{\omega}\left(X\right):=\operatorname{Tr}\left(X\right)\rho_{\omega}
, the following estimates hold for
\pr
-a.e.
\omega
and every
n\geq 1
:

\displaystyle\sup_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{d}}\norm{
\Phi_{\omega;0:-n}\left(\sigma\right)-\rho_\omega}_{1}

\displaystyle\leq 2\kappa_{\omega;0:-n},

\displaystyle\norm{
\rho_{\omega,n}-\rho_\omega}_{1}

\displaystyle\leq 2\kappa_{\omega;0:-n},

\displaystyle\norm{
\Phi_{\omega;0:-n}-R_\omega}_{1\to 1}

\displaystyle\leq 4\kappa_{\omega;0:-n}.

Moreover, if
\eta:\Omega\to\mathcal{S}_{d}
is any measurable random state, then

\norm{
\Phi_{\omega;0:-n}
\left(\eta_{\theta^{-n}\omega}\right)
-
\rho_\omega}_{1}\leq 2\kappa_{\omega;0:-n}

for
\pr
-a.e.
\omega
and every
n\geq 1
.
Finally,
\rho
is the unique stationary random state, up to
\pr
-a.e. equality.

Proof.

Let

\Omega_{0}:=\left\{\omega:\kappa_{\omega;0:-n}\to 0\right\}.

Then
\pr\left(\Omega_{0}\right)=1
.
Fix
\omega\in\Omega_{0}
.
Let
r>n
.
The cocycle identity gives

\rho_{\omega,r}=\Phi_{\omega;0:-n}\left(\Phi_{\omega;-n:-r}\left(\tau\right)\right),\qquad\rho_{\omega,n}=\Phi_{\omega;0:-n}\left(\tau\right).

Both inputs of
\Phi_{\omega;0:-n}
are states.
By
2.2
,

\norm{
\rho_{\omega,r}-\rho_{\omega,n}
}_{1}\leq 2\kappa_{\rm tr}\left(\Phi_{\omega;0:-n}\right)=2\kappa_{\omega;0:-n}.

Since
\kappa_{\omega;0:-n}\to 0
, the sequence
\left(\rho_{\omega,n}\right)_{n\geq 1}
is Cauchy.
The state space
\mathcal{S}_{d}
is complete in trace norm.
Hence
\rho_{\omega,n}
has a trace-norm limit.

The same estimate proves the independence of the reference state.
Indeed, if
\tau,\tau^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
, then

\norm{
\Phi_{\omega;0:-n}\left(\tau\right)
-
\Phi_{\omega;0:-n}\left(\tau'\right)
}_{1}\leq 2\kappa_{\omega;0:-n}\longrightarrow 0.

Measurability follows from pointwise convergence.
For each
n
, the map
\omega\mapsto\rho_{\omega,n}
is measurable because it is obtained from finitely many measurable compositions.
The pointwise limit on
\Omega_{0}
is therefore measurable.
Fix a state
\tau_{0}\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
, and define
\rho_{\omega}:=\tau_{0}
on
\Omega\setminus\Omega_{0}
.

We now prove stationarity.
Let

\Omega_{1}:=\Omega_{0}\cap\theta^{-1}\Omega_{0}.

Then
\pr(\Omega_{1})=1
.
For
\omega\in\Omega_{1}
, the pullback limits exist at both
\omega
and
\theta\omega
.
Using continuity and the cocycle identity,

\Phi_{\omega}(\rho_{\omega})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Phi_{\omega}\left(\Phi_{\omega;0:-n}(\tau)\right)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Phi_{\omega;1:-n}(\tau).

On the other hand,

\rho_{\theta\omega}=\lim_{m\to\infty}\Phi_{\theta\omega;0:-m}(\tau).

Taking
m=n+1
, we have

\Phi_{\theta\omega;0:-(n+1)}=\Phi_{\omega;1:-n}.

Therefore

\rho_{\theta\omega}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Phi_{\omega;1:-n}(\tau).

Thus

\Phi_{\omega}(\rho_{\omega})=\rho_{\theta\omega}

for
\pr
-a.e.
\omega
.

Let

S:=\{\omega:\Phi_{\omega}(\rho_{\omega})=\rho_{\theta\omega}\}.

Then
\pr(S)=1
.
Set

\Omega_{*}:=\Omega_{0}\cap S\cap\bigcap_{j\geq 1}\{\omega:\theta^{-j}\omega\in S\}.

Since
\theta
is invertible and measure preserving,
\pr(\Omega_{*})=1
.
For every
\omega\in\Omega_{*}
, stationarity holds at
\theta^{-j}\omega
for every
j\geq 0
.
Hence, for every
n\geq 1
,

\rho_{\omega}=\Phi_{\omega;0:-n}\left(\rho_{\theta^{-n}\omega}\right).

Applying
2.2
to the two inputs
\sigma
and
\rho_{\theta^{-n}\omega}
gives

\norm{
\Phi_{\omega;0:-n}\left(\sigma\right)-\rho_\omega}_{1}\leq 2\kappa_{\omega;0:-n}

for every
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
.
Taking the supremum over
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
gives the state-level estimate.
Taking
\sigma=\tau
gives

\norm{
\rho_{\omega,n}-\rho_\omega}_{1}\leq 2\kappa_{\omega;0:-n}.

The replacement-channel estimate follows from
3.2
.
Indeed, for the deterministic product
\Phi_{\omega;0:-n}
, use the reference state
\rho_{\theta^{-n}\omega}
.
The associated evolved reference state is

\Phi_{\omega;0:-n}\left(\rho_{\theta^{-n}\omega}\right)=\rho_{\omega}.

Therefore, the corresponding replacement channel is exactly
R_{\omega}
.
Hence

\norm{
\Phi_{\omega;0:-n}-R_\omega}_{1\to 1}\leq 4\kappa_{\rm tr}\left(\Phi_{\omega;0:-n}\right)=4\kappa_{\omega;0:-n}.

The random-boundary estimate follows by taking
\sigma=\eta_{\theta^{-n}\omega}
in the state-level estimate.
This is valid because
\eta_{\theta^{-n}\omega}\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
.

It remains to prove uniqueness.
Let
\eta:\Omega\to\mathcal{S}_{d}
be any stationary random state.
Let

S_{\eta}:=\left\{\omega:\Phi_{\omega}\left(\eta_{\omega}\right)=\eta_{\theta\omega}\right\}.

Then
\pr\left(S_{\eta}\right)=1
.
Replacing
\Omega_{*}
by

\Omega_{*}\cap\bigcap_{j\geq 1}\theta^{j}S_{\eta},

we may assume that the stationarity identity for
\eta
can also be iterated backwards.
Thus, for every
\omega\in\Omega_{*}
and every
n\geq 1
,

\eta_{\omega}=\Phi_{\omega;0:-n}\left(\eta_{\theta^{-n}\omega}\right).

By the random-boundary estimate applied to
\eta
,

\norm{
\eta_\omega-\rho_\omega}_{1}\leq 2\kappa_{\omega;0:-n}.

Letting
n\to\infty
gives
\eta_{\omega}=\rho_{\omega}
for
\pr
-a.e.
\omega
.
Thus, the stationary random state is unique up to
\pr
-a.e. equality.
∎

The pullback construction identifies the stationary random center.
Once this center is known, forward trace-memory loss gives convergence to the replacement channel at the future endpoint.

Proposition 4.14
.

Let
\rho:\Omega\to\mathcal{S}_{d}
be a stationary random state, and define

Proof.

Apply that proposition to the deterministic product

4.4
Quenched rates of convergence

The Dobrushin-Lyapunov exponent gives the exponential rate at which the centered part of the random product contracts.
The pullback construction identifies the moving replacement center.
We now combine these two facts to obtain quenched replacement estimates in the induced trace norm.

Proof.

By
4.7
, the assumption that
\lambda_{\rm tr}(\omega)<0

\pr

\kappa_{\omega;n:0}\to 0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\kappa_{\omega;0:-n}\to 0

for
\pr

\omega

Hence
4.15
gives a stationary random state

\pr

In particular,

\Phi_{\omega}(\rho_{\omega})=\rho_{\theta\omega}\qquad\text{for }\pr\text{-a.e. }\omega,

Definition

It remains to prove the exponential estimates.
Let

Define

\bar{\lambda}_{\rm tr}(\omega):=\begin{cases}\lambda_{\rm tr}(\omega),&\omega\in E,\\

is not negative.

Example 3
.

Take the deterministic one-point base

\Omega=\{\omega_{0}\},\qquad\theta\omega_{0}=\omega_{0},\qquad\pr(\{\omega_{0}\})=1.

Let
d=2
, and define a quantum channel
\Phi:M_{2}\to M_{2}
by

\Phi(X)=K_{0}XK_{0}^{\dagger}+K_{1}XK_{1}^{\dagger},\qquad K_{0}:=|1\rangle\langle 0|,\qquad K_{1}:=|0\rangle\langle 1|.

Equivalently, for

X=\begin{pmatrix}x_{00}&x_{01}\\
x_{10}&x_{11}\end{pmatrix},

one has

\Phi(X)=\begin{pmatrix}x_{11}&0\\
0&x_{00}\end{pmatrix}.

Since

K_{0}^{\dagger}K_{0}+K_{1}^{\dagger}K_{1}=|0\rangle\langle 0|+|1\rangle\langle 1|=I,

the map
\Phi
is completely positive and trace-preserving.
A dynamically stationary random state is, in this deterministic case, simply a
fixed state of
\Phi
. Let

\rho=\begin{pmatrix}a&z\\
\overline{z}&1-a\end{pmatrix}\in\mathcal{S}_{2}.

Then

\Phi(\rho)=\begin{pmatrix}1-a&0\\
0&a\end{pmatrix}.

Hence
\Phi(\rho)=\rho
forces
z=0
and
a=1/2
.
Therefore, the unique dynamically stationary state is

\rho_{*}=\frac{1}{2}I.

However, the trace-Dobrushin Lyapunov exponent is not negative. Let

X:=|0\rangle\langle 0|-|1\rangle\langle 1|=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}.

Then
X=X^{\dagger}
,
\operatorname{Tr}\left(X\right)=0
, and
\norm{X}_{1}=2
. Moreover,

\Phi(X)=-X,

and hence

\Phi^{n}(X)=(-1)^{n}X\qquad\forall n\geq 1.

Therefore

\kappa_{\rm tr}(\Phi^{n})\geq\frac{\norm{\Phi^n(X)}_{1}}{\norm{X}_{1}}=1.

Since
\kappa_{\rm tr}(\Phi^{n})\leq 1
for every CPTP map, we get

\kappa_{\rm tr}(\Phi^{n})=1\qquad\forall n\geq 1.

Consequently,

\lambda_{\rm tr}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\kappa_{\rm tr}(\Phi^{n})=0.

Thus, the uniqueness of the dynamically stationary random state does not imply

\lambda_{\rm tr}<0
. In particular, uniqueness alone does not imply replacement
mixing.

4.5
Annealed bounds under stochastic mixing

We now impose a stochastic decorrelation condition on the random channel environment.
The object to which the condition is applied is the stationary two-sided sequence of one-step channels

X_{j}(\omega):=\Phi_{\theta^{j}\omega},\qquad j\in\mathbb{Z}.

The use of two-sided time is natural here because the base map
\theta
is invertible and the pullback construction of the stationary random state uses negative times.

Since the symbol
\rho
is already used for density matrices and for the stationary random state
\rho_{\omega}
, we denote the maximal-correlation coefficient by
\varrho_{\rm mc}
.
For real-valued square-integrable random variables
U,V
, write

\operatorname{Cov}(U,V):=\mathbb{E}[UV]-\mathbb{E}[U]\mathbb{E}[V].

For sub-
\sigma
-algebras
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
, define

\varrho_{\rm mc}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}):=\sup\left\{|\mathbb{E}[UV]|:\begin{array}[]{l}U\in L^{2}(\mathcal{A}),\ V\in L^{2}(\mathcal{B}),\\
\mathbb{E}U=\mathbb{E}V=0,\quad\|U\|_{L^{2}}=\|V\|_{L^{2}}=1\end{array}\right\},

(4.1)

with the convention that the supremum is
0
if the class is empty.
We also use the standard strong-mixing coefficients

\displaystyle\psi_{\rm mix}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})

\displaystyle:=\sup\left\{\left|\frac{\pr(A\cap B)}{\pr(A)\pr(B)}-1\right|:A\in\mathcal{A},\ B\in\mathcal{B},\ \pr(A)\pr(B)>0\right\},

(4.2)

\displaystyle\varphi_{\rm mix}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})

\displaystyle:=\sup\left\{|\pr(B\mid A)-\pr(B)|:A\in\mathcal{A},\ B\in\mathcal{B},\ \pr(A)>0\right\}.

(4.3)

Thus
0\leq\varrho_{\rm mc}\leq 1
,
0\leq\psi_{\rm mix}\leq\infty
, and
0\leq\varphi_{\rm mix}\leq 1
.
Vanishing of any one of these coefficients is equivalent to independence of
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
.
We use the standard background on these coefficients from
[
Bra05
,
Bra07
]
.

The estimates needed below are the following classical covariance inequalities.
For sub-
\sigma
-algebras
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
,

\displaystyle|\operatorname{Cov}(U,V)|

\displaystyle\leq\varrho_{\rm mc}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})\,\|U\|_{L^{2}}\|V\|_{L^{2}},

\displaystyle U\in L^{2}(\mathcal{A}),\ V\in L^{2}(\mathcal{B}),

(4.4)

\displaystyle|\operatorname{Cov}(U,V)|

\displaystyle\leq\psi_{\rm mix}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})\,\|U\|_{L^{1}}\|V\|_{L^{1}},

\displaystyle U\in L^{1}(\mathcal{A}),\ V\in L^{1}(\mathcal{B}),

(4.5)

\displaystyle|\operatorname{Cov}(U,V)|

\displaystyle\leq 2\varphi_{\rm mix}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})\,\|U\|_{L^{1}}\|V\|_{L^{\infty}},

\displaystyle U\in L^{1}(\mathcal{A}),\ V\in L^{\infty}(\mathcal{B}).

(4.6)

The first inequality is immediate from the definition of maximal correlation, after centering
U
and
V
.
The latter two are standard strong-mixing covariance bounds; see
[
Dou94
, §1.2, Theorem 3]
and
[
Bra07
, Theorem 3.9]
.

We now pass from pairs of
\sigma
-algebras to the channel process.
For integers
a<b
, set

\mathcal{G}_{a}^{b}:=\sigma(X_{j}:a\leq j<b),

and define the past and future
\sigma
-algebras

\mathcal{G}_{-\infty}^{k}:=\sigma(X_{j}:j<k),\qquad\mathcal{G}_{k}^{\infty}:=\sigma(X_{j}:j\geq k).

The half-open convention matches the product convention

\Phi_{\omega;b:a}=\Phi_{\theta^{b-1}\omega}\circ\cdots\circ\Phi_{\theta^{a}\omega},\qquad a<b,

because
\Phi_{\omega;b:a}
is measurable with respect to
\mathcal{G}_{a}^{b}
.

Definition 1
.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}
, define

\displaystyle\varrho_{m}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\varrho_{\rm mc}\left(\mathcal{G}_{-\infty}^{k},\mathcal{G}_{k+m}^{\infty}\right),

(4.7)

\displaystyle\psi_{m}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\psi_{\rm mix}\left(\mathcal{G}_{-\infty}^{k},\mathcal{G}_{k+m}^{\infty}\right),

(4.8)

\displaystyle\varphi_{m}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi_{\rm mix}\left(\mathcal{G}_{-\infty}^{k},\mathcal{G}_{k+m}^{\infty}\right).

(4.9)

We say that the channel environment is
\varrho
-mixing if

\varrho_{m}\to 0
,
\psi
-mixing if
\psi_{m}\to 0
, and

\varphi
-mixing if
\varphi_{m}\to 0
.

The process
(X_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
is strictly stationary, since
X_{j}=X_{0}\circ\theta^{j}
and
\theta
preserves
\pr
.
The coefficients in
1
measure only the randomness
seen by the channel process; they need not detect unrelated invariant factors of the ambient probability space.
The sequences
(\varrho_{m})
,
(\psi_{m})
, and
(\varphi_{m})
are nonincreasing in
m
.
If the random variables
(X_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
are jointly independent, then

\varrho_{m}=\psi_{m}=\varphi_{m}=0,\qquad m\geq 1.

The stronger coefficients imply the maximal-correlation condition used in the proofs.

Proposition 4.16
.

For any sub-
\sigma
-algebras
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
,

\varrho_{\rm mc}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})\leq\psi_{\rm mix}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}),

and

\varrho_{\rm mc}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})\leq 2\varphi_{\rm mix}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})^{1/2}\varphi_{\rm mix}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A})^{1/2}\leq 2\varphi_{\rm mix}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})^{1/2}.

Proof.

If
\psi_{\rm mix}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})=\infty
, the first estimate is trivial.
Otherwise, take centered real random variables
U\in L^{2}(\mathcal{A})
,
V\in L^{2}(\mathcal{B})
with

\|U\|_{L^{2}}=\|V\|_{L^{2}}=1
.
By (
4.5
),

|\mathbb{E}[UV]|=|\operatorname{Cov}(U,V)|\leq\psi_{\rm mix}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})\,\|U\|_{L^{1}}\|V\|_{L^{1}}\leq\psi_{\rm mix}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}).

Taking the supremum over such
U,V
gives
\varrho_{\rm mc}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})\leq\psi_{\rm mix}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
.
The comparison with
\varphi_{\rm mix}
is the classical maximal-correlation bound of Doob; see
[
Doo90
, Lemma 7.1]
and also

[
Bra05
,
Pel83
]
.
∎

Applying
4.16

\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{G}_{-\infty}^{k},\qquad\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{G}_{k+m}^{\infty},

and then taking the supremum over

\varrho_{m}\leq\psi_{m},\qquad\varrho_{m}\leq 2\varphi_{m}^{1/2},\qquad m\geq 1.

Consequently, either
\psi_{m}\to 0
or
\varphi_{m}\to 0

\varrho_{m}\to 0
.
We finally record the separation property that will be used repeatedly. If

r,s,t\in\mathbb{N}
, then

\kappa_{\omega;r:0}=\kappa_{\rm tr}\!\left(\Phi_{\omega;r:0}\right)

is
\mathcal{G}_{-\infty}^{r}
-measurable, while

\kappa_{\theta^{r+s}\omega;t:0}=\kappa_{\rm tr}\!\left(\Phi_{\omega;r+s+t:r+s}\right)

is
\mathcal{G}_{r+s}^{\infty}
-measurable. Therefore
(
4.4
) gives

\displaystyle\left|\operatorname{Cov}\left(\kappa_{\omega;r:0},\kappa_{\theta^{r+s}\omega;t:0}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\varrho_{s}\left\|\kappa_{\omega;r:0}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\kappa_{\theta^{r+s}\omega;t:0}\right\|_{L^{2}}.

This covariance estimate is the probabilistic input behind the annealed contraction recursion.

Lemma 4.17
.

Set

a_{n}:=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\kappa_{\omega;n:0}\right],\qquad n\geq 1.

Then, for all
r,s,t\in\mathbb{N}
,

a_{r+s+t}\leq a_{r}a_{t}+\varrho_{s}(a_{r}a_{t})^{1/2}.

Proof.

We first separate the product into two distant blocks and one middle block.
By the cocycle identity,

\Phi_{\omega;r+s+t:0}=\Phi_{\omega;r+s+t:r+s}\circ\Phi_{\omega;r+s:r}\circ\Phi_{\omega;r:0}.

Using the submultiplicativity of
\kappa_{\rm tr}
from

2.4
, and the bound
\kappa_{\rm tr}(T)\leq 1
for every
CPTP map
T
, we get

\displaystyle\kappa_{\omega;r+s+t:0}

\displaystyle\leq\kappa_{\omega;r+s+t:r+s}\,\kappa_{\omega;r+s:r}\,\kappa_{\omega;r:0}

\displaystyle\leq\kappa_{\omega;r+s+t:r+s}\,\kappa_{\omega;r:0}.

Since

\Phi_{\omega;r+s+t:r+s}=\Phi_{\theta^{r+s}\omega;t:0},

this becomes

\kappa_{\omega;r+s+t:0}\leq\kappa_{\omega;r:0}\,\kappa_{\theta^{r+s}\omega;t:0}.

Taking expectations gives

a_{r+s+t}\leq\mathbb{E}\!\left[\kappa_{\omega;r:0}\,\kappa_{\theta^{r+s}\omega;t:0}\right].

(4.10)

Now set

U(\omega):=\kappa_{\omega;r:0},\qquad V(\omega):=\kappa_{\theta^{r+s}\omega;t:0}.

The variable
U
is measurable with respect to

\mathcal{G}_{0}^{r}
, hence with respect to
\mathcal{G}_{-\infty}^{r}
.
The variable
V
is measurable with respect to

\mathcal{G}_{r+s}^{r+s+t}
, hence with respect to

\mathcal{G}_{r+s}^{\infty}
.
Therefore, by the maximal-correlation covariance bound
(
4.4
) and by the definition of
\varrho_{s}
,

|\operatorname{Cov}(U,V)|\leq\varrho_{s}\,\|U\|_{L^{2}}\,\|V\|_{L^{2}}.

Hence

\mathbb{E}[UV]\leq\mathbb{E}[U]\mathbb{E}[V]+\varrho_{s}\left(\mathbb{E}[U^{2}]\right)^{1/2}\left(\mathbb{E}[V^{2}]\right)^{1/2}.

By stationarity of the channel sequence,

\mathbb{E}[V]=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\kappa_{\theta^{r+s}\omega;t:0}\right]=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\kappa_{\omega;t:0}\right]=a_{t}.

Also
\mathbb{E}[U]=a_{r}
. Since
0\leq\kappa_{\rm tr}(T)\leq 1
for every
CPTP map
T
, we have
U^{2}\leq U
and
V^{2}\leq V
. Therefore

\left(\mathbb{E}[U^{2}]\right)^{1/2}\leq a_{r}^{1/2},\qquad\left(\mathbb{E}[V^{2}]\right)^{1/2}\leq a_{t}^{1/2}.

Combining these estimates with (
4.10
),
we obtain

a_{r+s+t}\leq a_{r}a_{t}+\varrho_{s}(a_{r}a_{t})^{1/2}.

∎

Lemma 4.18
.

Assume that

\lambda_{\rm tr}(\omega)<0\qquad\text{for }\pr\text{-a.e. }\omega,

and that the channel environment is
\varrho
-mixing, i.e.

\varrho_{m}\to 0
. Then, for every
p\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists

A_{p}<\infty
such that

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\kappa_{\omega;n:0}\right]\leq A_{p}n^{-p},\qquad n\geq 1.

Proof.

Put

a_{n}:=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\kappa_{\omega;n:0}\right],\qquad n\geq 1.

We first record two elementary facts about
(a_{n})
.

The sequence
(a_{n})
is nonincreasing.
Indeed, if
1\leq m<n
, then the cocycle identity gives

\Phi_{\omega;n:0}=\Phi_{\omega;n:m}\circ\Phi_{\omega;m:0}.

By submultiplicativity of
\kappa_{\rm tr}
,

\kappa_{\omega;n:0}\leq\kappa_{\omega;n:m}\kappa_{\omega;m:0}\leq\kappa_{\omega;m:0},

because every CPTP map has trace-Dobrushin coefficient at most
1
.
Taking expectations gives
a_{n}\leq a_{m}
.

Next,
a_{n}\to 0
. Since

\lambda_{\rm tr}(\omega)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\kappa_{\omega;n:0}<0

for
\pr
-a.e.
\omega
, we have

\kappa_{\omega;n:0}\longrightarrow 0\qquad\text{for }\pr\text{-a.e. }\omega.

Since
0\leq\kappa_{\omega;n:0}\leq 1
, dominated convergence gives

a_{n}\to 0
.

Fix
p\in\mathbb{N}
, and set

q:=4^{-p}.

Since
\varrho_{m}\to 0
and
a_{n}\to 0
, we may choose

M,N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, with
M\leq N_{0}
, such that

\varrho_{M}+a_{N_{0}}\leq q.

Define

N_{i}:=2^{i}N_{0}+(2^{i}-1)M,\qquad i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Then
N_{i+1}=2N_{i}+M
. Applying

Lemma

4.17
with
r=t=N_{i}
and
s=M
,
we obtain

a_{N_{i+1}}\leq a_{N_{i}}^{2}+\varrho_{M}a_{N_{i}}=a_{N_{i}}\bigl(a_{N_{i}}+\varrho_{M}\bigr).

Since
N_{i}\geq N_{0}

Lemma 4.19
.

Assume that

\lambda_{\rm tr}(\omega)<0\qquad\text{for }\pr\text{-a.e. }\omega,

and that the random variables
(X_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}

Then there exist constants

and

Proof.

Put

a_{n}:=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\kappa_{\omega;n:0}\right],\qquad n\geq 1.

Since
\lambda_{\rm tr}(\omega)<0
for
\pr

\omega

\kappa_{\omega;n:0}\longrightarrow 0\qquad\text{for }\pr\text{-a.e. }\omega.

\displaystyle a_{qN_{0}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\kappa_{\omega;qN_{0}:0}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\!\left[\prod_{j=0}^{q-1}\kappa_{\theta^{jN_{0}}\omega;N_{0}:0}\right]

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=0}^{q-1}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\kappa_{\theta^{jN_{0}}\omega;N_{0}:0}\right]

∎

Lemma 5.1
.

Let

\kappa_{\rm tr}\left(\Theta_{\omega;q,r-1}\right)=\kappa_{\rm tr}\left(\Phi_{\theta^{-q}\omega;0:-L}\right)\leq C_{\beta}^{-}(\theta^{-q}\omega)e^{\beta(\theta^{-q}\omega)L}.

Since
\beta
is
\theta
-invariant on
\Omega_{*}
,

\beta(\theta^{-q}\omega)=\beta(\omega).

Combining the last two estimates gives

\left|\varphi_{\infty,\omega}(AB)-\varphi_{\infty,\omega}(A)\varphi_{\infty,\omega}(B)\right|\leq 4\norm{A}_{\infty}\norm{B}_{\infty}C_{\beta}^{-}(\theta^{-q}\omega)e^{\beta(\omega)L}.

This proves the quenched correlation estimate.

For fixed
A
and
B
, the left-hand side is measurable in
\omega
because
\omega\mapsto\varphi_{\infty,\omega}
is weak-
*
measurable.
∎

Corollary 5.4
.

Assume the hypotheses of
8
.
Let
A\in\mathcal{A}_{[p,q]}
and
B\in\mathcal{A}_{[r,s]}
, where
1\leq p\leq q
and
q+1<r\leq s
.
Set

L:=r-q-1.

Define the connected correlation random variable

\Gamma_{A,B}(\omega):=\left|\varphi_{\infty,\omega}(AB)-\varphi_{\infty,\omega}(A)\varphi_{\infty,\omega}(B)\right|.

If the channel environment is
\varrho
-mixing, then for every
u\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
C_{u}<\infty
such that

\pr\left\{\Gamma_{A,B}\leq C_{u}\norm{A}_{\infty}\norm{B}_{\infty}L^{-u}\right\}\geq 1-L^{-u}.

If the random variables
(X_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
are jointly independent, then there exist constants
C<\infty
and
\gamma>0
such that

\pr\left\{\Gamma_{A,B}\leq C\norm{A}_{\infty}\norm{B}_{\infty}e^{-\gamma L}\right\}\geq 1-e^{-\gamma L}.

Proof.

By
8
, for every
\omega\in\Omega_{*}
,

\Gamma_{A,B}(\omega)\leq 4\norm{A}_{\infty}\norm{B}_{\infty}\kappa_{\rm tr}\left(\Theta_{\omega;q,r-1}\right).

The gap product is

\Theta_{\omega;q,r-1}=\Phi_{\theta^{-q}\omega;0:-L}.

Hence

\kappa_{\rm tr}\left(\Theta_{\omega;q,r-1}\right)=\kappa_{\theta^{-(r-1)}\omega;L:0}.

Since
\theta
preserves
\pr
, the random variable
\kappa_{\theta^{-(r-1)}\omega;L:0}
has the same law as
\kappa_{\omega;L:0}
.
Suppose first that the channel environment is
\varrho
-mixing.
By
Lemma

4.18
, applied with exponent
2u
, there exists
A_{2u}<\infty
such that

\mathbb{E}\left[\kappa_{\rm tr}\left(\Theta_{\omega;q,r-1}\right)\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\kappa_{\omega;L:0}\right]\leq A_{2u}L^{-2u}.

Set

B_{u}:=\max\{A_{2u},1\}.

Markov’s inequality gives

\displaystyle\pr\left\{\kappa_{\rm tr}\left(\Theta_{\omega;q,r-1}\right)>B_{u}L^{-u}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{A_{2u}L^{-2u}}{B_{u}L^{-u}}

\displaystyle\leq L^{-u}.

Therefore, with probability at least
1-L^{-u}
,

\Gamma_{A,B}(\omega)\leq 4B_{u}\norm{A}_{\infty}\norm{B}_{\infty}L^{-u}.

This proves the
\varrho
-mixing claim with
C_{u}:=4B_{u}
.

Now suppose that the random variables
(X_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
are jointly independent.
By
Lemma

4.19
, there exist
A<\infty
and
\eta>0
such that

\mathbb{E}\left[\kappa_{\rm tr}\left(\Theta_{\omega;q,r-1}\right)\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\kappa_{\omega;L:0}\right]\leq Ae^{-\eta L}.

Set

B:=\max\{A,1\},\qquad\gamma:=\frac{\eta}{2}.

Markov’s inequality gives

\displaystyle\pr\left\{\kappa_{\rm tr}\left(\Theta_{\omega;q,r-1}\right)>Be^{-\gamma L}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Ae^{-\eta L}}{Be^{-\gamma L}}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\gamma L}.

Therefore, with probability at least
1-e^{-\gamma L}
,

\Gamma_{A,B}(\omega)\leq 4B\norm{A}_{\infty}\norm{B}_{\infty}e^{-\gamma L}.

This proves the independent case with
C:=4B

Appendix A
Additional comparison with contraction coefficients

The main text uses only the state-level Markov–Dobrushin comparison in
Section

2.1
.
For completeness, we record here how the trace-Dobrushin product coefficient compares with several other contraction mechanisms.
These coefficients answer related but distinct questions.
Some are computable one-step upper bounds.
Some are adapted to projective geometry or faithful-state geometry.
Some are designed for spatial many-body dynamics or for completely bounded memory loss.
There is therefore no single linear hierarchy containing all of them.

CP-order quantum Doeblin coefficients

The state-level Markov–Dobrushin coefficient only asks for a common lower bound at the level of output states.
A stronger certificate asks for a lower bound in the completely positive order.
For
\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
, write
R_{\tau}(X):=\operatorname{Tr}\left(X\right)\tau
for the replacement channel with output
\tau
.

Definition A.1

(CP-order quantum Doeblin coefficient)
.

Let
\Phi:\mathbb{M}_{d}\to\mathbb{M}_{d}
be CPTP.
The CP-order quantum Doeblin coefficient is

\alpha_{\rm Doeb}(\Phi):=\max\left\{c\in[0,1]:\exists\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{d}\text{ such that }\Phi-cR_{\tau}\text{ is completely positive}\right\}.

Equivalently, this coefficient can be characterized through degradability from the erasure channel:

\alpha_{\rm Doeb}(\Phi)=\sup\{\varepsilon:E_{\varepsilon}\succeq_{\rm deg}\Phi\},

where
E_{\varepsilon}
is the erasure channel and
\succeq_{\rm deg}
denotes the degradability order; see
[
Hir24
]
.
We use the normalized Choi convention

J(\Phi):=(\Phi\otimes\operatorname{id})(|\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|),\qquad|\Omega\rangle:=d^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}|i\rangle\otimes|i\rangle.

With this convention,
J(R_{\tau})=\tau\otimes I/d
, and the coefficient has the semidefinite-program form

\alpha_{\rm Doeb}(\Phi)=\max_{\widehat{\tau}\geq 0}\left\{\operatorname{Tr}\left(\widehat{\tau}\right):\widehat{\tau}\otimes I/d\leq J(\Phi)\right\}.

With the opposite input-output Choi convention, the tensor factors are interchanged.

The coefficient gives the trace-distance contraction bound

\kappa_{\rm tr}(\Phi)=\eta^{\rm Tr}(\Phi)\leq 1-\alpha_{\rm Doeb}(\Phi).

Indeed, if
\Phi-cR_{\tau}
is completely positive and
c<1
, then

\Phi=cR_{\tau}+(1-c)\Psi

for a CPTP map
\Psi
.
Therefore, for all states
\rho,\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
,

\Phi(\rho)-\Phi(\sigma)=(1-c)\bigl(\Psi(\rho)-\Psi(\sigma)\bigr),

and trace-norm contractivity gives

\norm{\Phi(\rho)-\Phi(\sigma)}_{1}\leq(1-c)\norm{\rho-\sigma}_{1}.

Optimizing over
c
gives the displayed bound.
The case
c=1
is the replacement-channel case and gives zero trace-distance contraction.

The paper
[
Hir24
]
also introduces transpose and Hermitian-relaxed variants.
The transpose variant is

\alpha_{T}(\Phi):=\alpha_{\rm Doeb}(\mathsf{T}\circ\Phi)

whenever
\mathsf{T}\circ\Phi
is completely positive, where
\mathsf{T}
denotes matrix transposition.
If
\mathsf{T}\circ\Phi
is not completely positive, we omit this term, or equivalently set
\alpha_{T}(\Phi)=-\infty
in the maximum below.
The Hermitian relaxation is

\alpha_{H}(\Phi):=\max\left\{c\in[0,1]:\exists X=X^{*},\ \operatorname{Tr}\left(X\right)=1,\ \Phi-cR_{X}\text{ is completely positive}\right\},

where
R_{X}(Y):=\operatorname{Tr}\left(Y\right)X
.
In the definition of
\alpha_{H}
, the operator
X
is not required to be positive, so
R_{X}
need not be a quantum channel.
It is only a Hermitian relaxation used to bound the trace-distance contraction coefficient.
These variants give an improved estimate

\eta^{\rm Tr}(\Phi)\leq 1-\max\{\alpha_{H}(\Phi),\alpha_{T}(\Phi)\}

when the corresponding quantities are applicable.

The CP-order Doeblin condition implies state-level Markov–Dobrushin minorization.
Indeed, if
\Phi-cR_{\tau}
is completely positive, then for every
P\in\mathcal{P}_{1}
,

c\tau=cR_{\tau}(P)\leq\Phi(P).

Therefore

\alpha_{\rm Doeb}(\Phi)\leq\alpha_{\rm MD}(\Phi),\qquad 1-\alpha_{\rm MD}(\Phi)\leq 1-\alpha_{\rm Doeb}(\Phi).

Thus, CP-order Doeblin minorization is a stronger certificate than state-level Markov–Dobrushin minorization.
Its advantage is that it is algebraic and SDP-computable.
Its disadvantage is that it can be too rigid.

Example 4
.

Let
d\geq 2
, and consider

\Phi(X)=\frac{1}{d+1}\bigl(\operatorname{Tr}\left(X\right)I+X^{T}\bigr).

This map is CPTP.
Indeed, with the normalized Choi convention fixed above,

J(\Phi)=\frac{1}{d(d+1)}(I+F),

where
F
is the flip operator on
\mathbb{C}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d}
.
Thus
J(\Phi)\geq 0
, because
I+F
is twice the projection onto the symmetric subspace.
Trace preservation is immediate from the formula, since

\operatorname{Tr}\left(\Phi(X)\right)=\frac{1}{d+1}\bigl(d\,\operatorname{Tr}\left(X\right)+\operatorname{Tr}\left(X\right)\bigr)=\operatorname{Tr}\left(X\right).

For every
P\in\mathcal{P}_{1}
,

\Phi(P)=\frac{1}{d+1}(I+P^{T})\geq\frac{1}{d+1}I.

Hence

\alpha_{\rm MD}(\Phi)\geq\frac{d}{d+1}>0.

On the other hand,
J(\Phi)
is supported on the symmetric subspace
\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
.
Suppose that
\Phi-cR_{\tau}
were completely positive for some
c>0
and some
\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
.
Then

0\leq cJ(R_{\tau})\leq J(\Phi).

For positive operators
0\leq A\leq B
, one has
\operatorname{supp}(A)\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(B)
.
Therefore
J(R_{\tau})
would have to be supported on
\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
.
But

J(R_{\tau})=\tau\otimes I/d,

and hence

\operatorname{supp}J(R_{\tau})=\operatorname{supp}(\tau)\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d}.

Choose
0\neq u\in\operatorname{supp}(\tau)
.
Since
d\geq 2
, choose
v\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
with
v\perp u
.
Then
u\otimes v\in\operatorname{supp}J(R_{\tau})
, but
u\otimes v
is not symmetric, because
F(u\otimes v)=v\otimes u\neq u\otimes v
.
This contradicts the required support inclusion.
Therefore no such
c>0
exists, and

\alpha_{\rm Doeb}(\Phi)=0.

Thus, CP-order Doeblin minorization is strictly stronger than state-level Markov–Dobrushin minorization.

Hilbert–Birkhoff projective coefficients

Projective methods belong to a different geometric framework.
They are designed for positive cone-preserving maps and are especially natural when the map is not trace-preserving.

Definition A.2

(Hilbert–Birkhoff projective coefficient)
.

Let
A,B\geq 0
be nonzero.
Define

h(A,B):=\log\big(\sup(A/B)\sup(B/A)\big),\qquad\sup(A/B):=\inf\{\lambda>0:A\leq\lambda B\},

with the convention that
\sup(A/B)=\infty
if no such
\lambda
exists.
If
T
is positive and cone preserving, its projective diameter is

\Delta(T):=\sup_{A,B\geq 0,\ A,B\neq 0}h(TA,TB),

and its Hilbert–Birkhoff projective contraction coefficient is

\eta_{\rm HB}(T):=\tanh(\Delta(T)/4).

The coefficient
\eta_{\rm HB}
is submultiplicative and gives Perron–Frobenius type control for positive maps; see
[
Bir57
,
Bus73
,
RKW11
]
.
For trace-preserving positive maps on the positive semidefinite cone, finite projective diameter implies base-norm, hence trace-norm, contraction.
In particular, when
\Delta(T)<\infty
,

\kappa_{\rm tr}(T)\leq\tanh(\Delta(T)/4)<1.

Thus finite projective diameter is a sufficient condition for strict trace contraction.

However, this condition is not necessary.
The finite-diameter projective criterion may fail to detect trace-norm contraction toward a boundary state.

Example 5
.

For
0<\gamma<1
, define the qubit amplitude-damping channel

T_{\gamma}(X)=K_{0}XK_{0}^{*}+K_{1}XK_{1}^{*},

where

K_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&\sqrt{1-\gamma}\end{pmatrix},\qquad K_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\sqrt{\gamma}\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}.

On centered Bloch vectors, the linear part is

(x,y,z)\mapsto\big(\sqrt{1-\gamma}\,x,\sqrt{1-\gamma}\,y,(1-\gamma)z\big).

Therefore

\kappa_{\rm tr}(T_{\gamma})=\sqrt{1-\gamma}<1.

In particular,
T_{\gamma}^{n}(\rho)\to|0\rangle\langle 0|
exponentially for every state
\rho
.

Nevertheless,

T_{\gamma}^{n}(|0\rangle\langle 0|)=|0\rangle\langle 0|\qquad\text{for all }n.

Moreover,

T_{\gamma}^{n}(|1\rangle\langle 1|)=\bigl(1-(1-\gamma)^{n}\bigr)|0\rangle\langle 0|+(1-\gamma)^{n}|1\rangle\langle 1|,

which has full support for every finite
n
.
Thus the two outputs
T_{\gamma}^{n}(|0\rangle\langle 0|)
and
T_{\gamma}^{n}(|1\rangle\langle 1|)
have different supports.
Their Hilbert projective distance is infinite, and hence

\Delta(T_{\gamma}^{n})=\infty\qquad\text{for every }n\geq 1.

Thus strict trace contraction can hold even when all finite-step projective diameters are infinite.

The same example also shows that one-step minorization is not necessary for trace contraction.
Suppose
B\leq T_{\gamma}(P)
for all
P\in\mathcal{P}_{1}
.
Since
T_{\gamma}(|0\rangle\langle 0|)=|0\rangle\langle 0|
, the operator
B
must be of the form
B=b|0\rangle\langle 0|
with
b\geq 0
.
Now take

|\psi_{\varepsilon}\rangle=\sqrt{1-\varepsilon}|0\rangle+\sqrt{\varepsilon}|1\rangle,\qquad P_{\varepsilon}=|\psi_{\varepsilon}\rangle\langle\psi_{\varepsilon}|.

A direct computation gives

T_{\gamma}(P_{\varepsilon})-b|0\rangle\langle 0|\geq 0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad b\leq\gamma\varepsilon.

Since this must hold for all
\varepsilon>0
, one gets
b=0
.
Hence

\alpha_{\rm MD}(T_{\gamma})=0.

Since CP-order Doeblin minorization implies state-level Markov–Dobrushin minorization, it follows also that

\alpha_{\rm Doeb}(T_{\gamma})=0.

Thus

\alpha_{\rm MD}(T_{\gamma})=0,\qquad\alpha_{\rm Doeb}(T_{\gamma})=0,\qquad\kappa_{\rm tr}(T_{\gamma})<1.

This example shows that strict trace contraction is strictly more general than strict positivity, finite projective diameter, CP-order Doeblin minorization, and one-step Markov–Dobrushin minorization.

Projective contraction coefficients

The random positive-cocycle literature also uses a normalized projective coefficient which is closely related to, but not identical with, the Hilbert–Birkhoff contraction coefficient.

Definition A.3

(Projective contraction coefficient)
.

For states
A,B\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
, set

m(A,B):=\sup\{\lambda\geq 0:\lambda B\leq A\},

and define

d_{\rm proj}(A,B):=\frac{1-m(A,B)m(B,A)}{1+m(A,B)m(B,A)}.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{M}_{d}\to\mathbb{M}_{d}
be a positive map such that
\operatorname{Tr}\left(\varphi(A)\right)>0
for every
A\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
.
Define its normalized projective action by

\varphi\cdot A:=\frac{\varphi(A)}{\operatorname{Tr}\left(\varphi(A)\right)}.

The projective contraction coefficient is

c(\varphi):=\sup_{A,B\in\mathcal{S}_{d}}d_{\rm proj}(\varphi\cdot A,\varphi\cdot B).

This coefficient is useful for positive maps and random positive cocycles, where the maps need not be trace preserving; see
[
MS21
,
MS22
]
.
It measures the diameter of the normalized image of the state space in a bounded projective metric.

Let
h
denote Hilbert’s projective metric on the positive semidefinite cone.
For comparable nonzero positive operators
A,B
, one has

h(A,B)=-\log\bigl(m(A,B)m(B,A)\bigr),

and therefore

d_{\rm proj}(A,B)=\tanh\!\left(\frac{h(A,B)}{2}\right).

Thus
d_{\rm proj}
is a bounded transform of Hilbert’s projective metric.
However, the coefficient
c(\varphi)
is not the same as the Hilbert–Birkhoff contraction coefficient

\eta_{\rm HB}(\varphi)=\tanh\!\left(\frac{\Delta(\varphi)}{4}\right),

where
\Delta(\varphi)
is the Hilbert projective diameter.
The former records the bounded projective diameter of the normalized image, while the latter is the Birkhoff–Hopf Lipschitz contraction ratio for Hilbert’s projective metric.

In the CPTP case, the normalization is trivial, but
c
remains a projective coefficient rather than a trace-norm memory coefficient.
It is well adapted to strict positivity and projective convergence.
By contrast,
\kappa_{\rm tr}
is adapted to trace-norm replacement mixing, including convergence toward nonfaithful boundary states.

For instance, in the amplitude-damping example of
Example

5
, one has

\kappa_{\rm tr}(T_{\gamma}^{n})=(1-\gamma)^{n/2}\longrightarrow 0.

Nevertheless,

T_{\gamma}^{n}(|0\rangle\langle 0|)=|0\rangle\langle 0|

and

T_{\gamma}^{n}(|1\rangle\langle 1|)=\bigl(1-(1-\gamma)^{n}\bigr)|0\rangle\langle 0|+(1-\gamma)^{n}|1\rangle\langle 1|.

The first output is rank one, whereas the second has full support for every finite
n
.
Hence

m(T_{\gamma}^{n}(|0\rangle\langle 0|),T_{\gamma}^{n}(|1\rangle\langle 1|))=0,

and therefore

c(T_{\gamma}^{n})=1\qquad\text{for every }n\geq 1.

Thus, the projective contraction coefficient does not detect the trace-norm replacement convergence of amplitude damping toward a boundary state.
This is exactly the kind of boundary-attractor behavior for which the centered trace-Dobrushin coefficient is the more appropriate CPTP product coefficient.

Entropy, Riemannian, and
\chi^{2}
coefficients

Entropy and
L^{2}
-type contraction coefficients are adapted to divergence and faithful-state geometries.
They are extremely useful for entropy production, functional inequalities, and local spectral analysis.
They are not the same object as the centered trace-Dobrushin coefficient
\kappa_{\rm tr}
.

Definition A.4

(Relative entropy contraction coefficient)
.

For a CPTP map
\Phi
, define

\eta_{\rm D}(\Phi):=\sup_{\rho,\sigma}\frac{D(\Phi(\rho)\|\Phi(\sigma))}{D(\rho\|\sigma)},

where

D(\rho\|\sigma):=\operatorname{Tr}\left(\rho(\log\rho-\log\sigma)\right).

The supremum is taken over pairs of states with
0<D(\rho\|\sigma)<\infty
.

Definition A.5

(Riemannian contraction coefficient)
.

Fix a monotone Riemannian metric
g^{\mathfrak{m}}
on the faithful state space.
Assume that
\Phi(\rho)>0
whenever
\rho>0
.
The associated Riemannian contraction coefficient is

\eta_{\rm Riem,\mathfrak{m}}(\Phi):=\sup_{\rho>0}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}X=X^{*}\\
\operatorname{Tr}\left(X\right)=0\\
X\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{g^{\mathfrak{m}}_{\Phi(\rho)}(\Phi(X),\Phi(X))}{g^{\mathfrak{m}}_{\rho}(X,X)}.

Definition A.6

(
\chi^{2}
contraction coefficient)
.

Let
\omega>0
be a faithful state with
\Phi(\omega)=\omega
, and let
\chi^{2}_{\omega}
be a chosen quantum
\chi^{2}
-divergence associated with a monotone metric.
The corresponding
\chi^{2}
contraction coefficient is

\eta_{\chi^{2},\omega}(\Phi):=\sup_{\rho\neq\omega}\frac{\chi^{2}_{\omega}(\Phi(\rho))}{\chi^{2}_{\omega}(\rho)}.

When the Riemannian coefficient is defined, the comparison results for monotone metrics imply inequalities of the form

\eta^{\rm Tr}(\Phi)\leq\sqrt{\eta_{\rm Riem,\mathfrak{m}}(\Phi)}

in the appropriate setting; see
[
HR15
,
Hir24
]
.
Thus, strict Riemannian contraction implies strict trace-distance contraction.

For a unital qubit channel with Bloch matrix
A_{\Phi}
, the usual comparison takes the simple form

\eta_{\rm Riem,\mathfrak{m}}(\Phi)=\eta_{\rm D}(\Phi)=\|A_{\Phi}\|_{\infty}^{2},\qquad\eta^{\rm Tr}(\Phi)=\|A_{\Phi}\|_{\infty}.

Thus, in this case, the trace-distance coefficient is the square root of the corresponding Riemannian and relative-entropy contraction coefficient.

These comparison inequalities are useful, but they do not replace
\kappa_{\rm tr}
.
The entropy, Riemannian, and
\chi^{2}
coefficients are tied to divergence, infinitesimal, or faithful-reference-state geometries.
By contrast,
\kappa_{\rm tr}
is defined directly on the self-adjoint trace-zero space and remains well behaved for boundary-attracting channels.
For example, amplitude damping has a nonfaithful attracting state, so the centered trace-Dobrushin coefficient still captures trace-norm replacement mixing, while faithful-state Riemannian coefficients are not the natural tool for that boundary limit.

Quantum Wasserstein–Dobrushin conditions

The many-body quantum Dobrushin framework uses a spatial influence matrix rather than a scalar auxiliary-channel coefficient.
It is designed to control how local deviations propagate through a spatial quantum system.

A typical setup starts from a many-body update
\Phi
decomposed into local pieces
\Phi_{i}
, where
\Phi_{i}
is the part of the dynamics associated with updating site
i
.
The relevant metric is a quantum Wasserstein-1 norm
\norm{\cdot}_{W_{1}}
on traceless Hermitian perturbations.
In the formulation of
[
Bak+25
]
, the influence of a deviation at site
j
on the update at site
i
is measured by a quantity of the form

D^{(\Phi)}_{ij}:=\sup\left\{\norm{\Phi_i(X)}_{W_{1}}:X=X^{*},\ \operatorname{Tr}\left(X\right)=0,\ \operatorname{tr}_{j}X=0,\ \norm{X}_{W_{1}}=1\right\},

up to the normalization convention used in the decomposition of
\Phi
.
Equivalently, one may take the supremum over pairs of
j
-neighboring quantum states.
A quantum Wasserstein–Dobrushin condition is then a contraction condition on this influence matrix, for instance, a bound on its column sums or on its
1\to 1
norm strictly below one.

This is a spatial path-coupling criterion.
It controls the propagation of local deviations and yields rapid mixing for suitable many-body dynamics.
In the high-temperature Gibbs-state setting, the same framework can also be used to prove structural consequences such as decay of conditional mutual information.
This is a different problem from the scalar trace-memory-loss problem studied in the present paper.
Here the deviation is the whole auxiliary trace-zero component of a finite-dimensional channel product.
In the Wasserstein–Dobrushin setting, the deviation is spatially localized and the coefficient records how that localization propagates across sites.
Thus the two theories use Dobrushin-type language for different geometries:
\kappa_{\rm tr}
is a scalar trace-norm memory coefficient for auxiliary channel products, while
D^{(\Phi)}
is a spatial influence matrix for many-body quantum dynamics.

Diamond-norm variants

One can define completely bounded versions of centered memory loss, but the most naive unconstrained version is not useful for ordinary state-memory loss.

Definition A.7

(Unconstrained diamond centered coefficient)
.

For a CPTP map
\Phi:\mathbb{M}_{d}\to\mathbb{M}_{d}
, define

\kappa_{\diamond}(\Phi):=\sup_{r\geq 1}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}X=X^{*}\in\mathbb{M}_{d}\otimes M_{r}\\
\operatorname{Tr}\left(X\right)=0,\ X\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\norm{(\Phi\otimes\operatorname{id}_r)(X)}_{1}}{\norm{X}_{1}}.

Here
\operatorname{Tr}\left(X\right)=0
denotes the global trace on
\mathbb{M}_{d}\otimes M_{r}
.

For every channel
\Phi
, one has

\kappa_{\diamond}(\Phi)=1.

Indeed, since
\Phi\otimes\operatorname{id}_{r}
is positive and trace preserving, trace-norm contractivity on self-adjoint matrices gives
\kappa_{\diamond}(\Phi)\leq 1
.
Conversely, choose
r\geq 2
, let
Y=Y^{*}\in M_{r}
be nonzero with
\operatorname{Tr}\left(Y\right)=0
, and let
\rho_{*}\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
be any state.
Then
X:=\rho_{*}\otimes Y
is self-adjoint and globally trace zero, and

(\Phi\otimes\operatorname{id}_{r})(X)=\Phi(\rho_{*})\otimes Y.

Since
\Phi(\rho_{*})
is a state,
\norm{\Phi(\rho_*)}_{1}=1
, and hence

\norm{(\Phi\otimes\operatorname{id}_r)(X)}_{1}=\norm{\Phi(\rho_*)}_{1}\norm{Y}_{1}=\norm{Y}_{1}=\norm{X}_{1}.

Thus, the supremum is at least one, and so
\kappa_{\diamond}(\Phi)=1
.

The obstruction is that the global trace-zero condition permits deviations that are entirely stored in the reference system.
These deviations are invisible to the system channel and therefore survive unchanged.
Consequently, the unconstrained diamond-centered coefficient cannot detect ordinary state-memory loss.

Meaningful completely bounded variants must remove this reference-only obstruction.
For example, one may restrict to perturbations with fixed reference marginal, equivalently to self-adjoint
X\in\mathbb{M}_{d}\otimes M_{r}
satisfying

\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb{M}_{d}}(X)=0,

or one may compare the amplified channel directly with an amplified replacement channel,

\Phi\otimes\operatorname{id}_{r}\quad\text{versus}\quad R_{\rho_{*}}\otimes\operatorname{id}_{r}.

Such variants measure entanglement-assisted memory loss.
They are relevant if one wants stability against arbitrary reference systems, but they are not needed for the MPS boundary-stability problems considered in this paper.

Summary of implications

The directly comparable one-step certificates satisfy the quantitative chain

\alpha_{\rm Doeb}(\Phi)\leq\alpha_{\rm MD}(\Phi),\qquad\kappa_{\rm tr}(\Phi)\leq\eta_{\rm MD}(\Phi)=1-\alpha_{\rm MD}(\Phi)\leq 1-\alpha_{\rm Doeb}(\Phi).

Equivalently,

\alpha_{\rm Doeb}(\Phi)>0\Longrightarrow\alpha_{\rm MD}(\Phi)>0\Longrightarrow\kappa_{\rm tr}(\Phi)<1.

Both implications are proper.

Example

4
shows that state-level Markov–Dobrushin minorization can hold when CP-order Doeblin minorization fails.

Example

5
shows that strict trace contraction can hold when both one-step minorization criteria fail.

Projective and faithful-state routes give additional sufficient conditions.
For a positive cone-preserving map with finite Hilbert projective diameter,

\Delta(\Phi)<\infty\Longrightarrow\kappa_{\rm tr}(\Phi)\leq\eta_{\rm HB}(\Phi)=\tanh\!\left(\Delta(\Phi)/4\right)<1.

In the faithful Riemannian setting where the comparison theorem applies,

\eta^{\rm Tr}(\Phi)\leq\sqrt{\eta_{\rm Riem,\mathfrak{m}}(\Phi)}.

Hence, by
\eta^{\rm Tr}(\Phi)=\kappa_{\rm tr}(\Phi)
,

\eta_{\rm Riem,\mathfrak{m}}(\Phi)<1\Longrightarrow\kappa_{\rm tr}(\Phi)<1.

These routes are not part of the same linear inclusion chain.
Projective coefficients are adapted to cone-preserving positive maps, especially non-trace-preserving ones.
Entropy, Riemannian, and
\chi^{2}
coefficients are adapted to faithful-state geometries.
Quantum Wasserstein–Dobrushin coefficients are adapted to spatial many-body dynamics.
Diamond variants are useful only after specifying how reference systems are constrained.

For products, the intrinsic object is the block coefficient

\kappa_{t:s}:=\kappa_{\rm tr}(\Phi_{t:s}).

By
2.2
,

\kappa_{t:s}<1\Longleftrightarrow\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho,\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{d}\\
\rho\neq\sigma\end{subarray}}\frac{\norm{\Phi_{t:s}(\rho)-\Phi_{t:s}(\sigma)}_{1}}{\norm{\rho-\sigma}_{1}}<1.

Thus
\kappa_{t:s}<1
is exactly strict trace contraction on state differences for the whole block.
This block-level statement is the sharp trace-memory-loss criterion used in the rest of the paper.

The one-step part of the hierarchy is

\left\{\Phi:\alpha_{\rm Doeb}(\Phi)>0\right\}\subsetneq\left\{\Phi:\alpha_{\rm MD}(\Phi)>0\right\}\subsetneq\left\{\Phi:\kappa_{\rm tr}(\Phi)<1\right\}.

At the product level, this is further enlarged:

\left\{\text{one-step strict trace contraction}\right\}\subsetneq\left\{\text{finite-block strict trace contraction}\right\}.

The first line compares one-step certificates for a single channel.
The second line is a product-level statement.
It allows loss of memory to arise only after composing several channels.

Example

1
gives a two-step block with

\kappa_{\rm tr}(D_{Z})=\kappa_{\rm tr}(D_{X})=1,\qquad\kappa_{\rm tr}(D_{X}\circ D_{Z})=0.

Appendix B
Bistochastic Hilbert–Schmidt contraction

We record a Hilbert–Schmidt sufficient criterion for a negative trace-Dobrushin exponent in the bistochastic case.
The criterion is based on contraction of the channel on the complex trace-zero Hilbert–Schmidt subspace.
Since every bistochastic channel fixes the maximally mixed state
I/d
, such contraction forces random products to approach the completely depolarizing replacement channel

\Omega\left(X\right):=\operatorname{Tr}\left(X\right)\frac{I}{d}.

Thus, the conclusion is depolarizing replacement toward
I/d
, not merely dephasing.

This condition is the fixed-dimensional analogue of the second-singular-value condition that appears in the quantum-expander literature.
In that literature, one often studies asymptotic families of channels on growing matrix algebras, together with Kraus-rank sparsity, Hilbert–Schmidt contraction, and fixed-state entropy conditions.
Here, the matrix algebra is fixed, and no Kraus-rank sparsity assumption is imposed.
We only use the trace-zero Hilbert–Schmidt contraction as a sufficient condition for trace-norm replacement mixing of random bistochastic products.
Averages of random unitaries and unitary-design constructions provide important sources of unital Hilbert–Schmidt contraction; see
[
Has07
,
Lan26
]
.
For the broader quantum-expander framework and recent random-channel constructions, see
[
LY25
]
.
Related transfer-operator contraction estimates also appear in random MPS and PEPS correlation-length estimates; see
[
LP21
]
.

Throughout this subsection, write

\norm{X}_{2}:=\operatorname{Tr}\left(X^{*}X\right)^{1/2}

for the Hilbert–Schmidt norm.
We also introduce the complex trace-zero space

\mathsf{H}_{0}^{\mathbb{C}}:=\left\{X\in\mathbb{M}_{d}:\operatorname{Tr}\left(X\right)=0\right\}.

The symbol
\mathsf{H}_{0}
continues to denote the real self-adjoint trace-zero space.

Definition B.1

(Bistochastic Hilbert–Schmidt contraction coefficient)
.

Let
T:\mathbb{M}_{d}\to\mathbb{M}_{d}
be bistochastic, meaning CPTP and unital.
Define

s_{0}\left(T\right):=\sup_{X\in\mathsf{H}_{0}^{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\left\{0\right\}}\frac{\norm{T\left(X\right)}_{2}}{\norm{X}_{2}}.

We call
s_{0}\left(T\right)
the trace-zero Hilbert–Schmidt contraction coefficient of
T
.
Equivalently,
s_{0}\left(T\right)
is the largest singular value of the restriction
T|_{\mathsf{H}_{0}^{\mathbb{C}}}
with respect to the Hilbert–Schmidt inner product.
We say that
T
is Hilbert–Schmidt contractive on the trace-zero subspace if

s_{0}\left(T\right)<1.

In this case,
1-s_{0}\left(T\right)
is the associated trace-zero Hilbert–Schmidt singular-value gap.

For every bistochastic channel
T
, one has
0\leq s_{0}\left(T\right)\leq 1
.
Indeed, complete positivity and unitality give the Schwarz inequality

T\left(X\right)^{*}T\left(X\right)\leq T\left(X^{*}X\right),

and trace preservation gives

\norm{T\left(X\right)}_{2}^{2}=\operatorname{Tr}\left(T\left(X\right)^{*}T\left(X\right)\right)\leq\operatorname{Tr}\left(T\left(X^{*}X\right)\right)=\operatorname{Tr}\left(X^{*}X\right)=\norm{X}_{2}^{2}.

Thus
T
is Hilbert–Schmidt nonexpansive, and in particular
s_{0}\left(T\right)\leq 1
.
Moreover,
T\mapsto s_{0}\left(T\right)
is continuous on the set of bistochastic maps.
This follows because
T\mapsto T|_{\mathsf{H}_{0}^{\mathbb{C}}}
is a continuous restriction map and the Hilbert–Schmidt operator norm is continuous in finite dimension.
Hence, if
\omega\mapsto\Phi_{\omega}
is a measurable assignment of bistochastic channels, then

\omega\mapsto s_{0}\left(\Phi_{\omega}\right)

is measurable.
We use the convention
\log 0=-\infty
.

Proposition B.2
.

Assume that
\Phi_{\omega}
is bistochastic almost surely.
Then the constant random state

\rho_{\omega}=I/d

is dynamically stationary in the sense of
Definition

1.2
.
Moreover, for every
n\geq 1
,

\kappa_{\omega;n:0}\leq\sqrt{d}\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}s_{0}\left(\Phi_{\theta^{j}\omega}\right).

Consequently,

\lambda_{\rm tr}\left(\omega\right)\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\log s_{0}\left(\Phi_{\theta^{j}\omega}\right)

whenever the right-hand side is defined in
\left[-\infty,0\right]
.

Proof.

Since each
\Phi_{\omega}
is unital and trace preserving,

\Phi_{\omega}\left(I/d\right)=I/d.

Hence, the constant random state
\rho_{\omega}=I/d
is stationary.
Let
X\in\mathsf{H}_{0}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
.
Then
X\in\mathsf{H}_{0}^{\mathbb{C}}
.
By the comparison between trace norm and Hilbert–Schmidt norm,

\norm{\Phi_{\omega;n:0}\left(X\right)}_{1}\leq\sqrt{d}\,\norm{\Phi_{\omega;n:0}\left(X\right)}_{2}.

The complex trace-zero space
\mathsf{H}_{0}^{\mathbb{C}}
is invariant under every trace-preserving map.
Therefore,

\norm{\Phi_{\omega;n:0}\left(X\right)}_{2}\leq\left(\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}s_{0}\left(\Phi_{\theta^{j}\omega}\right)\right)\norm{X}_{2}.

Since
\norm{X}_{2}\leq\norm{X}_{1}
, we obtain

\norm{\Phi_{\omega;n:0}\left(X\right)}_{1}\leq\sqrt{d}\left(\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}s_{0}\left(\Phi_{\theta^{j}\omega}\right)\right)\norm{X}_{1}.

Taking the supremum over
X\in\mathsf{H}_{0}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
gives the displayed bound on
\kappa_{\omega;n:0}
.
With the convention
\log 0=-\infty
, this bound implies

\frac{1}{n}\log\kappa_{\omega;n:0}\leq\frac{1}{n}\log\sqrt{d}+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\log s_{0}\left(\Phi_{\theta^{j}\omega}\right).

Letting
n\to\infty
gives the exponent estimate, because
n^{-1}\log\sqrt{d}\to 0
.
∎

The preceding proposition turns Hilbert–Schmidt contraction along the random product into an upper bound on the trace-Dobrushin exponent.

Theorem B.1
.

Assume that
\theta
is ergodic and that
\Phi_{\omega}
is bistochastic almost surely.
If
\mathbb{E}\left[\log s_{0}\left(\Phi_{\omega}\right)\right]<0

in the extended nonpositive sense, then

\lambda_{\rm tr}\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\log s_{0}\left(\Phi_{\omega}\right)\right]<0.

Consequently, the cocycle has quenched exponential replacement mixing with the constant stationary random state
\rho_{\omega}=I/d
.
Equivalently, the forward products converge exponentially to the completely depolarizing replacement channel

R_{\rm unif}\left(X\right):=\operatorname{Tr}\left(X\right)\frac{I}{d}.

In particular, for every deterministic
\beta<0
satisfying
\lambda_{\rm tr}<\beta
, there is an a.s. finite measurable random variable
C_{\beta}\left(\omega\right)
such that, for every
n\geq 1
,

\sup_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{d}}\norm{
\Phi_{\omega;n:0}\left(\sigma\right)-I/d
}_{1}\leq C_{\beta}\left(\omega\right)e^{\beta n},

and

\norm{
\Phi_{\omega;n:0}-R_{\rm unif}
}_{1\to 1}\leq C_{\beta}\left(\omega\right)e^{\beta n}.

Proof.

Set

g\left(\omega\right):=\log s_{0}\left(\Phi_{\omega}\right)\in\left[-\infty,0\right].

For
M\geq 1
, define

g_{M}\left(\omega\right):=\max\left\{g\left(\omega\right),-M\right\}.

Then
g_{M}\in L^{1}\left(\pr\right)
,
g\leq g_{M}\leq 0
, and
g_{M}\downarrow g
pointwise as
M\to\infty
.
By
B.2
,

\log\kappa_{\omega;n:0}\leq\log\sqrt{d}+\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}g\left(\theta^{j}\omega\right)\leq\log\sqrt{d}+\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}g_{M}\left(\theta^{j}\omega\right).

Taking expectations gives

\mathbb{E}\left[\log\kappa_{\omega;n:0}\right]\leq\log\sqrt{d}+n\mathbb{E}\left[g_{M}\right].

Since
\theta
is ergodic, Kingman’s variational formula in
Theorem

4.1
gives

\lambda_{\rm tr}=\inf_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\log\kappa_{\omega;n:0}\right].

Therefore

\lambda_{\rm tr}\leq\inf_{n\geq 1}\left(\frac{1}{n}\log\sqrt{d}+\mathbb{E}\left[g_{M}\right]\right)=\mathbb{E}\left[g_{M}\right].

Letting
M\to\infty
and using monotone convergence for
-g_{M}\uparrow-g
, we obtain

\lambda_{\rm tr}\leq\mathbb{E}\left[g\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\log s_{0}\left(\Phi_{\omega}\right)\right].

The assumption
\mathbb{E}\left[g\right]<0
gives
\lambda_{\rm tr}<0
.

The constant random state
\rho_{\omega}=I/d
is stationary because every
\Phi_{\omega}
is unital and trace preserving.
The uniqueness part of
3
identifies this constant random state with the stationary random state produced by the negative-exponent theorem.
The state-level exponential estimate follows from
3
.
The
1\to 1
replacement-channel estimate follows from the operator-norm part of
3
, after increasing
C_{\beta}\left(\omega\right)
if necessary.
∎

The preceding argument only uses Hilbert–Schmidt contraction of the relevant product.
Therefore, the same criterion applies when such contraction appears first after forming fixed-length blocks.

Corollary B.3
.

Assume that
\theta
is ergodic and that
\Phi_{\omega}
is bistochastic almost surely.
For
L\geq 1
, define
s_{0,L}\left(\omega\right):=s_{0}\left(\Phi_{\omega;L:0}\right).

If

\mathbb{E}\left[\log s_{0,L}\right]<0

for some
L\geq 1
, in the extended nonpositive sense, then
\lambda_{\rm tr}<0
.
More precisely,

\lambda_{\rm tr}\leq\frac{1}{L}\mathbb{E}\left[\log s_{0,L}\right]<0.

Consequently, the cocycle has quenched exponential replacement mixing with the constant stationary random state

\rho_{\omega}=I/d.

Equivalently, the forward products converge exponentially to the completely depolarizing replacement channel

R_{\rm unif}\left(X\right):=\operatorname{Tr}\left(X\right)\frac{I}{d}.

Proof.

Set

h\left(\omega\right):=\log s_{0,L}\left(\omega\right)\in\left[-\infty,0\right].

For
M\geq 1
, define

h_{M}\left(\omega\right):=\max\left\{h\left(\omega\right),-M\right\}.

Then
h_{M}\in L^{1}\left(\pr\right)
,
h\leq h_{M}\leq 0
, and
h_{M}\downarrow h
pointwise as
M\to\infty
.

Fix
q\geq 1
.
The product
\Phi_{\omega;qL:0}
decomposes into the
q
bistochastic blocks

\Phi_{\omega;qL:0}=\Phi_{\theta^{\left(q-1\right)L}\omega;L:0}\circ\cdots\circ\Phi_{\theta^{L}\omega;L:0}\circ\Phi_{\omega;L:0}.

Repeating the Hilbert–Schmidt estimate from
B.2
at the block level gives

\kappa_{\omega;qL:0}\leq\sqrt{d}\prod_{r=0}^{q-1}s_{0,L}\left(\theta^{rL}\omega\right).

Hence, with the convention
\log 0=-\infty
,

\log\kappa_{\omega;qL:0}\leq\log\sqrt{d}+\sum_{r=0}^{q-1}h\left(\theta^{rL}\omega\right)\leq\log\sqrt{d}+\sum_{r=0}^{q-1}h_{M}\left(\theta^{rL}\omega\right).

Taking expectations and using stationarity of
\theta
gives

\mathbb{E}\left[\log\kappa_{\omega;qL:0}\right]\leq\log\sqrt{d}+q\mathbb{E}\left[h_{M}\right].

No ergodicity of
\theta^{L}
is needed for this estimate.
By Kingman’s variational formula in
Theorem

4.1
,

\lambda_{\rm tr}\leq\frac{1}{qL}\mathbb{E}\left[\log\kappa_{\omega;qL:0}\right].

Therefore

\lambda_{\rm tr}\leq\frac{1}{qL}\log\sqrt{d}+\frac{1}{L}\mathbb{E}\left[h_{M}\right].

Letting
q\to\infty
gives

\lambda_{\rm tr}\leq\frac{1}{L}\mathbb{E}\left[h_{M}\right].

Letting
M\to\infty
and using monotone convergence for
-h_{M}\uparrow-h
, we obtain

\lambda_{\rm tr}\leq\frac{1}{L}\mathbb{E}\left[h\right]=\frac{1}{L}\mathbb{E}\left[\log s_{0,L}\right].

The assumed negativity of this expectation gives
\lambda_{\rm tr}<0
.
The replacement-mixing conclusions follow from
3
.
The stationary random state is
\rho_{\omega}=I/d
because every block and every one-step channel is bistochastic.
∎
