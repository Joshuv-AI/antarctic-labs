Non-special Divisors, LCPs of Codes, and LCD Codes on Kummer Extensions
Huachao Zhang, Chang-An Zhao

Abstract
Recently, constructions of linear complementary pairs (LCPs) of codes and linear complementary dual (LCD) codes on function fields have attracted considerable attention due to the wide range of applications of these codes. Such constructions rely on non-special divisors of degrees g g and g − 1 g-1 . In this work, we investigate Kummer extensions defined by y m = f ⁡ ( x ) y^{m}=f(x) with f ​ ( x ) ∈ 𝔽 q ​ ( x ) f(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(x) and establish an arithmetic characterization of non-special divisors whose support can contain non-totally ramified places. Based on this characterization, we explicitly construct non-special divisors of degree g − 1 g-1 on the GK curve. Moreover, utilizing pure gaps, we explicitly provide several families of effective non-special divisors of degree g g on Kummer extensions with the same multiplicities. We then develop a general framework for constructing LCPs of algebraic geometry (AG) codes on Kummer extensions. By virtue of canonical divisors, we show that the security parameters of LCPs of AG codes can be determined within this framework, which also enables the construction of LCD AG codes. Finally, we illustrate our results with representative examples, including LCPs of codes on the GK curve and LCD codes on quotients of the Hermitian curve. Index terms: Algebraic geometry codes, LCPs of codes, LCD codes, non-special divisors, Kummer extensions.

In the past year, numerous studies have emerged on non-special divisors of small degree and the construction of LCPs of AG codes on the Kummer extension

II-A

Function Fields

Let
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a function field of genus
g
with full constant field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. We denote by
\mathbb{P}_{F}
the set of places of
F
, by
\Omega_{F}
the module of Weil differentials of
F
, by
v_{P}
the discrete valuation of
F
with respect to the place
P\in\mathbb{P}_{F}
, and by
\operatorname{Div}(F)
the free abelian group generated by the places of
F
.
A place of degree one is called a rational place of
F
. The set of all rational places of
F
is denoted by
\mathbb{P}_{F}^{1}
. An element
D\in\operatorname{Div}(F)
is called a divisor of
F
and its degree is given by
\deg D:=\sum_{P\in\operatorname{supp}D}v_{P}(D)\cdot\deg P
, where
\operatorname{supp}D
is the support of
D
.
A divisor
D
is said to be effective if
v_{P}(D)\geq 0
for all
P\in\mathbb{P}_{F}
.
For a non-zero element
z\in F
, we denote by
(z)
,
(z)_{\infty}
, and
(z)_{0}
the principal divisor, the pole divisor and the zero divisor of
z
, respectively.

For a non-zero element
\omega\in\Omega_{F}
, we denote by
(\omega)
the canonical divisor corresponding to
\omega
.
Two divisors
D_{1},D_{2}\in\operatorname{Div}(F)
are said to be equivalent, denoted by
D_{1}\sim D_{2}
, if there is a function
z\in F
such that
D_{1}-D_{2}=(z)
.
All canonical divisors of
F
are equivalent since
\Omega_{F}
is a one-dimensional vector space over
F
. For two equivalent divisors
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
, we have
\ell(D_{1})=\ell(D_{2})
and
\deg D_{1}=\deg D_{2}
.

Given a divisor
D\in\operatorname{Div}(F)
, the Riemann-Roch space associated to
D
is defined by

\mathcal{L}(D):=\{z\in F\mid(z)\geq-D\}\cup\{0\}.

It is a vector space of finite dimension over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. We denote by
\ell(D)
the dimension of
\mathcal{L}(D)
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Let
W
be a canonical divisor of
F
. Then for each divisor
D\in\operatorname{Div}(F)
, the Riemann-Roch Theorem says that

\ell(D)=\deg D+1-g+\ell(W-D).

Since
\ell(W-D)\geq 0
, we have
\ell(D)\geq\deg D+1-g
. The divisor
D
is called non-special if

\ell(D)=\deg D+1-g.

Otherwise, the divisor
D
is called special. A non-special divisor of degree
g-1
can be derived from an effective non-special divisor of degree
g
.

Lemma 2
.

[
1
, Lemma 3]

Assume that

is an effective non-special divisor of degree
g
. If there exists a rational place
P\in\mathbb{P}_{F}^{1}
such that

, then

is a non-special divisor of degree
g-1
.

Next, we introduce the theory of Weierstrass semigroups. Let
P_{1},\dots,P_{s}
be
s
distinct rational places of
F
. The Weierstrass semigroup at

The complementary set

is called the set of gaps at
P_{1},\dots,P_{s}
.
There exists a special class of gaps known as pure gaps. One can use dimensions of Riemann-Roch spaces to characterize pure gaps.
An

is a pure gap at

if and only if

\ell\left(\sum_{i=1}^{s}a_{i}P_{i}\right)=\ell\left(\sum_{i=1}^{s}a_{i}P_{i}-P_{j}\right)\text{ for all }1\leq j\leq s.

The set of all pure gaps at
P_{1},\dots,P_{s}
is denoted by

. In particular, when

, we have
G(P_{1})=G_{0}(P_{1})
.
In fact, the set

contains exactly
g

.
The following result concerns pure gaps at several places.

Proposition 3
.

[
41

,
1\leq t\leq s
, and
\{i_{1},\dots,i_{t}\}\subseteq\{1,\dots,s\}
.
Let

Then
(a_{i_{1}},\dots,a_{i_{t}})\in G_{0}(P_{i_{1}},\dots,P_{i_{t}})

II-B

LCPs of AG Codes and LCD AG codes

A linear code
\mathcal{C}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is a linear subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
. We call
n
the length of
\mathcal{C}
and
k=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathcal{C}
the dimension of
\mathcal{C}
.
The minimum distance of
\mathcal{C}
is defined as

d(\mathcal{C}):=\min\{\operatorname{wt}(\mathbf{c})\mid 0\neq\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}\},

where
\operatorname{wt}(\mathbf{c}):=\#\{i\mid c_{i}\neq 0\}
for
\mathbf{c}=(c_{1},\dots,c_{n})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
.
We call
[n,k,d(\mathcal{C})]
the parameters of a linear code
\mathcal{C}
. Let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
be the standard inner product on
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
. Then the code

\mathcal{C}^{\bot}:=\{\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}\mid\langle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{c}\rangle=0\text{ for all }\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}\}

is called the dual code of
\mathcal{C}
.

Definition 4
.

Two linear codes
\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
are said to be equivalent, denoted by
\mathcal{C}_{1}\sim\mathcal{C}_{2}
, if there exists a vector
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast})^{n}
such that

\mathcal{C}_{2}=\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathcal{C}_{1}:=\{(a_{1}c_{1},\dots,a_{n}c_{n})\mid(c_{1},\dots,c_{n})\in\mathcal{C}_{1}\}.

Note that two equivalent codes have the same length, dimension and minimum distance.

Definition 5
.

A pair of linear codes
(\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2})
of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is called a linear complementary pair (LCP) if

\mathcal{C}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{C}_{2}=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}.

Given an LCP of codes
(\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2})
, the minimum distance
d(\mathcal{C}_{1})
measures resistance against FIA, and
d(\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\bot})
measures resistance against SCA.
The joint security against the two attacks is provided by
\min\{d(\mathcal{C}_{1}),d(\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\bot})\}
, which is called the security parameter of
(\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2})
.

Definition 6
.

A linear code
\mathcal{C}
of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is called a linear complementary dual (LCD) code if

\mathcal{C}\oplus\mathcal{C}^{\bot}=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}.

Observe that if
\mathcal{C}
is an LCD code, then
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C}^{\bot})
is an LCP of codes with the security parameter
d(\mathcal{C})
.

F/\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a function field of genus
g
with full constant field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Let
P_{1},\dots,P_{n}
be
n

.
Suppose that

The following theorem determines the parameters of the AG code

.

Theorem 7
.

[
39

Suppose that

The dual code of the AG code

\omega

For two divisors

III
Characterization of Non-special Divisors

In this section, we consider how to determine non-special divisors on a function field.
Firstly, on a Kummer extension
F=\mathbb{F}_{q}(x,y)/\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
defined by (
2
), we give an arithmetic criterion to determine all non-special divisors of the form
\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}D_{i}
for any integer
n\geq r
.
Then, on a function field of genus
g
, we point out that another non-special divisor of degree
g-1
can be derived from a known non-special divisor of degree
g-1
and a known canonical divisor. Moreover, we show that pure gaps can be used to identify effective non-special divisors of degree
g
.

III-A

Characterization of Non-special Divisors on Kummer Extensions

In this subsection, for the Kummer extension
F=\mathbb{F}_{q}(x,y)/\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
defined by (
2
), we present our main result as follows, which characterizes all non-special divisors generated by
D_{1},\dots,D_{n}
.

Theorem 17
.

For any integer
n\geq r
, let
(a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}
. Then
D=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}D_{i}
is a non-special divisor if and only if

\ell(D)=\sum_{t=0}^{m-1}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}\left\lfloor\frac{r_{i}a_{i}+t\lambda_{i}}{m}\right\rfloor d_{i}+1\right).

(6)

In order to prove the theorem above, we provide a lemma, which presents an equation related to the genus
g
of
F
.

Lemma 18
.

For any integer
n\geq r
,

\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sum_{t=0}^{m-1}\left\lfloor\frac{t\lambda_{i}}{m}\right\rfloor d_{i}+m-1=-g.

Proof.

We have that

\displaystyle~\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sum_{t=0}^{m-1}\left\lfloor\frac{t\lambda_{i}}{m}\right\rfloor d_{i}+m-1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\sum_{i=0}^{n}\frac{m\lambda_{i}-m-\lambda_{i}+\gcd(m,\lambda_{i})}{2}\cdot d_{i}+m-1~~~(\text{from Lemma \ref{lemma:floor_sum} (i)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\frac{-m\sum_{i=0}^{n}d_{i}+\sum_{i=0}^{n}\gcd(m,\lambda_{i})d_{i}}{2}+m-1~~~\left(\text{from $\sum_{i=0}^{n}\lambda_{i}d_{i}=0$}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~-\frac{2-2m+\sum_{i=0}^{n}\left(m-\gcd(m,\lambda_{i})\right)\cdot d_{i}}{2}=-g

∎

Now we proceed to prove Theorem
17
.

Proof of Theorem
17
.

Note that
\deg D=\sum_{i=0}^{n}r_{i}a_{i}d_{i}
. We will show that

\sum_{t=0}^{m-1}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}\left\lfloor\frac{r_{i}a_{i}+t\lambda_{i}}{m}\right\rfloor d_{i}+1\right)=\deg D-g+1.

(7)

For each
0\leq t\leq m-1
and
0\leq i\leq n
, let
0\leq t_{i}\leq m-1
such that
t\lambda_{i}=m\left\lfloor\frac{t\lambda_{i}}{m}\right\rfloor+t_{i}
.
Then we obtain that

t\lambda_{i}/r_{i}=\frac{m}{r_{i}}\left\lfloor\frac{t\lambda_{i}/r_{i}}{m/r_{i}}\right\rfloor+t/r_{i}.

Thus
t/r_{i}
is an integer with
0\leq t/r_{i}\leq m/r_{i}-1
.
Since
\gcd(m/r_{i},\lambda_{i}/r_{i})=1
, we have that
t_{i}/r_{i}
runs
r_{i}
times over
\{0,1,\dots,m/r_{i}-1\}
as
t
ranges from
0
to
m-1
.
Thus, we get that

\displaystyle~\sum_{t=0}^{m-1}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}\left\lfloor\frac{r_{i}a_{i}+t\lambda_{i}}{m}\right\rfloor d_{i}+1\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\sum_{t=0}^{m-1}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}\left(\left\lfloor\frac{r_{i}a_{i}+t_{i}}{m}\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor\frac{t\lambda_{i}}{m}\right\rfloor\right)d_{i}+1\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sum_{t=0}^{m-1}\left\lfloor\frac{a_{i}+t_{i}/r_{i}}{m/r_{i}}\right\rfloor d_{i}+\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sum_{t=0}^{m-1}\left\lfloor\frac{t\lambda_{i}}{m}\right\rfloor d_{i}+m

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\sum_{i=0}^{n}r_{i}d_{i}\sum_{t=0}^{m/r_{i}-1}\left\lfloor\frac{a_{i}+t}{m/r_{i}}\right\rfloor+\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sum_{t=0}^{m-1}\left\lfloor\frac{t\lambda_{i}}{m}\right\rfloor d_{i}+m

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\sum_{i=0}^{n}r_{i}a_{i}d_{i}+\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sum_{t=0}^{m-1}\left\lfloor\frac{t\lambda_{i}}{m}\right\rfloor d_{i}+m-1+1~~~(\text{from Lemma \ref{lemma:floor_sum} (ii)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\deg D-g+1~~~(\text{from Lemma \ref{lemma:floor_sum_g}}).

The conclusion follows directly from the definition of non-special divisors, which completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 19

III-B

Determining Non-special Divisors by Canonical Divisors and Pure Gaps

In this subsection, let
F
be a function field of genus
g
. First, we show that a non-special divisor of degree
g-1
and a canonical divisor can determine another non-special divisor of degree
g-1
.

Theorem 22
.

Suppose that
A\in\operatorname{Div}(F)
is a non-special divisor of degree
g-1
. Then for each canonical divisor
W\in\operatorname{Div}(F)
, the divisor
W-A
is also a non-special divisor of degree
g-1
.

Proof.

By the Riemann-Roch Theorem, there is a canonical divisor
W^{\prime}
such that

\ell(A)=\deg A+1-g+\ell(W^{\prime}-A).

Since
\ell(A)=\deg A+1-g
, we get that
\ell(W^{\prime}-A)=0
. On the other hand, we have
\deg(W^{\prime}-A)=g-1
. Then
W^{\prime}-A
is a non-special divisor of degree
g-1
.
Thus
W-A\sim W^{\prime}-A
is also a non-special divisor of degree
g-1
for any canonical divisor
W
.
∎

In
[
34
, Proposition 5]
, Moreno, López, and Matthews related Weierstrass semigroups to effective non-special divisors of degree
g
.
In the next theorem, we show how pure gaps determine effective non-special divisors of degree
g
.

Theorem 23
.

Suppose that
Q_{1},\dots,Q_{s}
are
s
distinct rational places of
F
with genus
g\geq 1
.
Let
(c_{1},\dots,c_{s})\in\mathbb{N}^{s}
. Suppose that
\sum_{i=1}^{s}c_{i}=g
and

\{(c_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{s})\mid 1\leq a_{i}\leq c_{i}\ \text{ for }2\leq i\leq s\}\subseteq G_{0}(Q_{1},\dots,Q_{s}),

and
\{a_{1}\mid 1\leq a_{1}\leq c_{1}\}\subseteq G(Q_{1})
.
Then
\sum_{i=1}^{s}c_{i}Q_{i}
is an effective non-special divisor of degree
g
.

Proof.

By the definition of pure gaps, we have

\displaystyle\ell\left(\sum_{i=1}^{s}c_{i}Q_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\ell\left(\sum_{i=1}^{s}c_{i}Q_{i}-Q_{s}\right)

\displaystyle=\ell\left(\sum_{i=1}^{s}c_{i}Q_{i}-2Q_{s}\right)=\dots=\ell\left(\sum_{i=1}^{s}c_{i}Q_{i}-c_{s}Q_{s}\right)=\ell\left(\sum_{i=1}^{s-1}c_{i}Q_{i}\right).

(12)

By Proposition
3
, we have

\{(c_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{s-1})\mid 1\leq a_{i}\leq c_{i}\text{ for }2\leq i\leq s-1\}\subseteq G_{0}(Q_{1},\dots,Q_{s-1}).

Then by the same argument as (
12
), we obtain that
\ell(\sum_{i=1}^{s-1}c_{i}Q_{i})=\ell(\sum_{i=1}^{s-2}c_{i}Q_{i})
.
Therefore, by induction, we conclude that

\ell\left(\sum_{i=1}^{s}c_{i}Q_{i}\right)=\ell\left(\sum_{i=1}^{s-1}c_{i}Q_{i}\right)=\ell\left(\sum_{i=1}^{s-2}c_{i}Q_{i}\right)=\dots=\ell\left(c_{1}Q_{1}\right).

Since
\{a_{1}\mid 1\leq a_{1}\leq c_{1}\}\subseteq G(Q_{1})
, we get that

\ell\left(c_{1}Q_{1}\right)=\ell\left((c_{1}-1)Q_{1}\right)=\ell\left((c_{1}-2)Q_{1}\right)=\dots=\ell\left(0\right)=1.

On the other hand, we have
\deg\left(\sum_{i=1}^{s}c_{i}Q_{i}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{s}c_{i}=g
. Thus
\sum_{i=1}^{s}c_{i}Q_{i}
is an effective non-special divisor of degree
g
.
∎

\displaystyle~~~~+\sum_{i=0}^{q^{2}-q-1}\left\lfloor\frac{i-k(q^{2}-q+1)-j}{q^{2}-q+1}\right\rfloor+1

\displaystyle=k(q^{2}-q+1)+j+\sum_{i=0}^{q}\left\lfloor\frac{(i-k)(q^{2}-q+1)-1-j}{q^{3}+1}\right\rfloor

\displaystyle~~~~-k(q^{2}-q)+\sum_{i=0}^{q^{2}-q-1}\left\lfloor\frac{i-j}{q^{2}-q+1}\right\rfloor+1.

∎

IV-B

Kummer Extensions with the Same Multiplicities

In this subsection, we consider the Kummer extension
F=\mathbb{F}_{q}(x,y)/\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
with the same multiplicities, which is given by (
5
):

y^{m}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}(x-\alpha_{i})^{\lambda},

where
\gcd(m,r\lambda)=1
and
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}
are pairwise distinct elements of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Recall that
f(x)
has exactly one pole
Q_{0}\in\mathbb{P}_{F}
and
r
zeros
Q_{1},\dots,Q_{r}\in\mathbb{P}_{F}
with identical multiplicities.
The genus of
F
is given by

g=\frac{(m-1)(r-1)}{2}.

sing Theorem
23
together with the pure-gap results from Propositions
15
and
16
, we construct three families of effective non-special divisors of degree
g
, whose support is contained in
\{Q_{0},Q_{1},\dots,Q_{r}\}
.

Suppose that
\lambda=1
and
r<m
. In
[
34
, Theorem 8]
, Moreno, López and Matthews provided explicit effective non-special divisors of degree
g
. The form of these divisors is

\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}\left\lfloor\frac{im}{r}\right\rfloor Q_{i}.

In the next proposition, we generalize such divisor forms to arbitrary
m
,
r
and
\lambda
with
\gcd(m,r\lambda)=1
.
Related similar results appear in
[
29
, Example 3.6]
.

Proposition 25
.

The divisor

D=\sum_{i=1}^{r-\left\lfloor\frac{r}{m}\right\rfloor-1}\left\lfloor\frac{m(r-i)}{r}\right\rfloor Q_{i}

(14)

is an effective non-special divisor of degree
g
.

Proof.

By Proposition
14
, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{r-\left\lfloor\frac{r}{m}\right\rfloor-1}\left\lfloor\frac{m(r-i)}{r}\right\rfloor=\sum_{i=1}^{r-\left\lfloor\frac{r}{m}\right\rfloor-1}\left(m-\left\lceil\frac{im}{r}\right\rceil\right)=\sum_{i=0}^{r-\left\lfloor\frac{r}{m}\right\rfloor-2}\left(m-\left\lceil\frac{(i+1)m}{r}\right\rceil\right)=g,

and

\left\{j\mid 1\leq j\leq\left\lfloor\frac{m(r-1)}{r}\right\rfloor\right\}=\left\{j\mid 1\leq j\leq m-\left\lceil\frac{m}{r}\right\rceil\right\}\subseteq G(Q_{1}).

Note that
\left\lfloor\frac{m\left(r-r+\left\lfloor\frac{r}{m}\right\rfloor+1\right)}{r}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\frac{m\left\lceil\frac{r}{m}\right\rceil}{r}\right\rfloor\geq 1
.
Combining Theorem
23
and Proposition
15
, we conclude that
D
is an effective non-special divisor of degree
g
.
∎

The support of divisors presented in the above proposition does not contain
Q_{0}
.
We now construct effective non-special divisors of degree
g
, whose support includes
Q_{0}
, splitting the discussion into two cases:
m>r
and
m<r
.

Proposition 26
.

Suppose that
m>r
. Then the divisor

D=(r-1)Q_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}\left(\left\lfloor\frac{m(r-i)}{r}\right\rfloor-1\right)Q_{i}

(15)

is an effective non-special divisor of degree
g
.

Proof.

From Proposition
13
together with
m>r
, we have
\{1,2,\dots,r-1\}\subseteq G(Q_{0})
.
Let integers
1\leq j\leq m-1-\left\lfloor\frac{m}{r}\right\rfloor
and
1\leq k\leq r-1
satisfy
mk-rj=r-1
.
For each
1\leq i\leq r-1
, let

t_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}m-\left\lceil\frac{(k+i)m}{r}\right\rceil+j,&\text{ if }k+i\neq r,\\
m-\left\lceil\frac{(k+i)m}{r}\right\rceil+j-1,&\text{ if }k+i=r.\\
\end{array}\right.

Since
mk=r(j+1)-1
, we have

\left\lceil\frac{(k+i)m}{r}\right\rceil=\left\lceil\frac{r(j+1)+im-1}{r}\right\rceil=j+1+\left\lceil\frac{im-1}{r}\right\rceil.

Substitution yields

t_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}m-1-\left\lceil\frac{im-1}{r}\right\rceil,&\text{if}~k+i\neq r,\\
m-1-\left\lceil\frac{im-1}{r}\right\rceil-1,&\text{if}~k+i=r.\\
\end{array}\right.

Note that

\left\lceil\frac{im-1}{r}\right\rceil=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\left\lceil\frac{im}{r}\right\rceil-1&im\equiv 1\pmod{r},\\
\left\lceil\frac{im}{r}\right\rceil&im\not\equiv 1\pmod{r}.\end{array}\right.

Since
mk\equiv-1\pmod{r}
, then
im\equiv 1\pmod{r}
if and only if
m(i+k)\equiv 0\pmod{r}
, which is equivalent to
i+k=r
.
As a consequence, for all
1\leq i\leq r-1
,

t_{i}=m-1-\left\lceil\frac{im}{r}\right\rceil=\left\lfloor\frac{m(r-i)}{r}\right\rfloor-1.

Since
\left\lfloor\frac{m\left(r-r+1\right)}{r}\right\rfloor-1=\left\lfloor\frac{m}{r}\right\rfloor-1\geq 0
, we have
t_{i}\geq 0
for all
1\leq i\leq r-1
.
Let
1\leq u\leq r-1-\left\lfloor\frac{r}{m}\right\rfloor
denote the largest integer with
t_{u}\geq 1
.
By Propositions
3
and
16
, we get that

\displaystyle\left\{(mk-rj,a_{1},\dots,a_{u})\mid 1\leq a_{i}\leq t_{i}\text{ for }1\leq i\leq u\right\}\subseteq G_{0}(Q_{0},Q_{1},\dots,Q_{u}).

Using Lemma
1
(i), we compute the degree

\displaystyle\deg\left((r-1)Q_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{u}\left(\left\lfloor\frac{m(r-i)}{r}\right\rfloor-1\right)Q_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle r-1+\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}\left(m-2-\left\lfloor\frac{im}{r}\right\rfloor\right)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle r-1+(m-2)(r-1)-\frac{mr-m-r+1}{2}

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\frac{(m-1)(r-1)}{2}=g.

An application of Theorem
23
shows that

D=(r-1)Q_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{u}\left(\left\lfloor\frac{m(r-i)}{r}\right\rfloor-1\right)Q_{i}

is an effective non-special divisor of degree
g
.
∎

Proposition 27
.

Suppose that
m<r
. Then the divisor

D=(m-1)Q_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{r-2}\left\lfloor\frac{m(r-i-1)}{r}\right\rfloor Q_{i}

(16)

is an effective non-special divisor of degree
g
.

Proof.

By Proposition
13
and
m<r
, we have
\{1,2,\dots,m-1\}\subseteq G(Q_{0})
.
Let integers
1\leq j\leq m-1-\left\lfloor\frac{m}{r}\right\rfloor
and
1\leq k\leq r-1
satisfy
mk-rj=m-1
.
For each
1\leq i\leq r-2
, define

t_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}m-\left\lceil\frac{(k+i)m}{r}\right\rceil+j,&\text{if}~k+i\neq r,\\
m-\left\lceil\frac{(k+i)m}{r}\right\rceil+j-1,&\text{if}~k+i=r,\\
\end{array}\right.

The identity
mk=rj+m-1
yields

\left\lceil\frac{(k+i)m}{r}\right\rceil=\left\lceil\frac{(k+i)m}{r}\right\rceil=\left\lceil\frac{rj+(i+1)m-1}{r}\right\rceil=j+\left\lceil\frac{(i+1)m-1}{r}\right\rceil.

Substituting this expression,

t_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}m-\left\lceil\frac{(i+1)m-1}{r}\right\rceil,&\text{if}~k+i\neq r,\\
m-\left\lceil\frac{(i+1)m-1}{r}\right\rceil-1,&\text{if}~k+i=r.\\
\end{array}\right.

Note that

\left\lceil\frac{(i+1)m-1}{r}\right\rceil=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\left\lceil\frac{(i+1)m}{r}\right\rceil-1&(i+1)m\equiv 1\pmod{r},\\
\left\lceil\frac{(i+1)m}{r}\right\rceil&(i+1)m\not\equiv 1\pmod{r}.\end{array}\right.

Since
m(k-1)\equiv-1\pmod{r}
, then
(i+1)m\equiv 1\pmod{r}
if and only if
m(i+k)\equiv 0\pmod{r}
, which is equivalent to
i+k=r
.
Thus for each
1\leq i\leq r-1
, we have

t_{i}=m-\left\lceil\frac{(i+1)m}{r}\right\rceil=\left\lfloor\frac{m(r-i-1)}{r}\right\rfloor.

Since
\left\lfloor\frac{(r-r+2-1)m}{r}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\frac{m}{r}\right\rfloor=0
, we have
t_{i}\geq 0
for all
1\leq i\leq r-2
.
Let
1\leq u\leq r-2
denote the largest integer satisfying
t_{u}\geq 1
. By Proposition
16
, we get

\displaystyle\left\{(mk-rj,a_{1},\dots,a_{u})\mid 1\leq a_{i}\leq t_{i}\text{ for }1\leq i\leq u\right\}\subseteq G_{0}(Q_{0},Q_{1},\dots,Q_{u}).

Using Lemma
1
(i), we compute the degree

\displaystyle\deg\left((m-1)Q_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{u}\left\lfloor\frac{m(r-i-1)}{r}\right\rfloor Q_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle m-1+\sum_{i=1}^{r-2}\left\lfloor\frac{m(r-i-1)}{r}\right\rfloor=m-1+\sum_{i=1}^{r-2}\left(m-1-\left\lfloor\frac{(i+1)m}{r}\right\rfloor\right)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle(m-1)(r-1)-\sum_{i=1}^{r-2}\left\lfloor\frac{(i+1)m}{r}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\frac{m}{r}\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor\frac{m}{r}\right\rfloor

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle(m-1)(r-1)-\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}\left\lfloor\frac{im}{r}\right\rfloor=(m-1)(r-1)-\frac{mr-m-r+1}{2}

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\frac{(m-1)(r-1)}{2}=g.

It follows from Theorem
23
that

D=(m-1)Q_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{u}\left\lfloor\frac{m(r-i-1)}{r}\right\rfloor Q_{i}

is an effective non-special divisor of degree
g
.
∎

V
Constructing LCPs of Codes and LCD codes on Kummer Extensions

In this section, we develop a general framework for constructing LCPs of AG codes on a Kummer extension.
For an LCP of AG codes
(C_{\mathcal{L}}(D,G),C_{\mathcal{L}}(D,H))
constructed via a canonical divisor, its security is determined by the minimum distance of
C_{\mathcal{L}}(D,G)
. Moreover, we derive a sufficient condition for
C_{\mathcal{L}}(D,G)
to be an LCD code.
Then we apply this result to the function field of the GK curve and the Kummer extension with the same multiplicities. In particular, we construct LCPs of codes on the function field of the GK curve and LCD codes on a Kummer extension given by a linearized polynomial.

Consider the Kummer extension
F=\mathbb{F}_{q}(x,y)/\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
defined by

y^{m}=\alpha\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{r}p_{i}(x)^{\lambda_{i}},

where
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
p_{1}(x),\dots,p_{r}(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
are pairwise distinct monic irreducible polynomials,
\lambda_{i}\geq 1
for
1\leq i\leq r
.
Let
\lambda_{0}:=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{i}
. Let
P_{i}\in\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)}
be the zero of
p_{i}(x)
and let
P_{0}\in\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)}
be the pole of
x
.
For
0\leq i\leq r
, recall that
r_{i}:=\gcd(m,\lambda_{i})
and

D_{i}:=\frac{r_{i}}{m}\operatorname{Con}_{F/\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)}(P_{i})=\sum_{Q\in\mathbb{P}_{F},Q|P}Q.

Let
n
be an integer with
r_{0}\mid n
. Suppose that there exists a function
h\in F
with
(h)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}R_{i}-\frac{n}{r_{0}}D_{0}
, where
R_{1},R_{2},\dots,R_{n}
are
n
distinct rational places of
F
with
\operatorname{supp}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}R_{i})\cap\operatorname{supp}(\sum_{i=0}^{r}D_{i})=\varnothing
.
Our construction for LCPs of AG codes is given below.

Theorem 29
.

Suppose that
\sum_{i=0}^{r}a_{i}D_{i}
and
\sum_{i=0}^{r}b_{i}D_{i}
are two non-special divisors of degree
g-1
and
I\subsetneqq\{1,2,\dots,r\}
. Let
D=(h)_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}R_{i}
.
Suppose that
s
is an integer satisfying that

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq i\leq r}\frac{(a_{i}-b_{i})r_{i}}{\lambda_{i}}\leq s\leq\frac{(b_{0}-a_{0})r_{0}+n}{\lambda_{0}}~~\text{ and }

(17)

\displaystyle\frac{g-1+\sum_{i\in I\cup\{0\}}(b_{i}-a_{i})r_{i}}{\lambda_{0}-\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}}<s<\frac{n-g+1+\sum_{i\in I\cup\{0\}}(b_{i}-a_{i})r_{i}}{\lambda_{0}-\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}}.

(18)

Define the divisors

\displaystyle G=\sum_{i=1,i\notin I}^{r}a_{i}D_{i}+\sum_{i\in I}(s\lambda_{i}/r_{i}+b_{i})D_{i}+(n/r_{0}-s\lambda_{0}/r_{0}+b_{0})D_{0}~~\text{and}

\displaystyle H=\sum_{i=1,i\notin I}^{r}(s\lambda_{i}/r_{i}+b_{i})D_{i}+\sum_{i\in I}a_{i}D_{i}+a_{0}D_{0}.

Set
k=s(\lambda_{0}-\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i})-\sum_{i\in I\cup\{0\}}(b_{i}-a_{i})r_{i}
.
Then
(C_{\mathcal{L}}(D,G),C_{\mathcal{L}}(D,H))
is an LCP of AG codes with parameters

\displaystyle[n,n-k,\geq k-g+1]\text{ and }[n,k,\geq n-k-g+1],

respectively.

Proof.

It is obvious that
\operatorname{supp}D\cap\operatorname{supp}G=\operatorname{supp}D\cap\operatorname{supp}H=\varnothing
. Direct degree computation gives

\displaystyle\deg G=g-1+\sum_{i\in I\cup\{0\}}(b_{i}-a_{i})r_{i}-s\left(\lambda_{0}-\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}\right)+n\text{ and }

\displaystyle\deg H=g-1-\sum_{i\in I\cup\{0\}}(b_{i}-a_{i})r_{i}+s\left(\lambda_{0}-\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}\right).

From (
18
), we obtain that
2g-2<\deg G,\deg H<n
and
\deg(G+H)=2g-2+n
. On the other hand, by (
17
), we have that

\displaystyle\gcd(G,H)=\sum_{i=0}^{r}a_{i}D_{i}\text{ and }

\displaystyle{\rm lmd}(G,H)=\sum_{i=0}^{r}(s\lambda_{i}/r_{i}+b_{i})D_{i}+(b_{0}+n/r_{0}-s\lambda_{0})D_{0}.

Thus
\gcd(G,H)
is a non-special divisor of degree
g-1
.
By Theorem
22
, the divisor

\displaystyle{\rm lmd}(G,H)-D

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{r}(s\lambda_{i}/r_{i}+b_{i})D_{i}+(n/r_{0}-s\lambda_{0}+b_{0})D_{0}-D

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{r}b_{i}D_{i}+s\sum_{i=0}^{r}\frac{\lambda_{i}}{r_{i}}D_{i}-s\frac{\lambda_{0}}{r_{0}}D_{0}+\frac{n}{r_{0}}D_{0}-D

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{r}b_{i}D_{i}+(y^{s})-(h)\sim\sum_{i=0}^{r}b_{i}D_{i}

is also a non-special divisor of degree
g-1
. Thus
(C_{\mathcal{L}}(D,G),C_{\mathcal{L}}(D,H))
is an LCP of AG codes by Theorem
8
.
The parameters of
C_{\mathcal{L}}(D,G)
and
C_{\mathcal{L}}(D,H)
are obtained from Theorem
7
.
∎

Using a canonical divisor generated by
D_{0},D_{1},\dots,D_{r}
, we establish the following corollary, which generalizes the main results in
[
12
, Theorems 9 and 10]
.

Corollary 30
.

Suppose that
\sum_{i=0}^{r}a_{i}D_{i}
is a non-special divisor of degree
g-1
and
I\subsetneqq\{1,2,\dots,r\}
.
Let
\omega
be a Weil differential such that
(\omega)=\sum_{i=0}^{r}b_{i}D_{i}
and
D=(h)_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}R_{i}
.
Suppose that
s
is an integer satisfying that

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq i\leq r}\frac{(2a_{i}-b_{i})r_{i}}{\lambda_{i}}\leq s\leq\frac{(b_{0}-2a_{0})r_{0}+n}{\lambda_{0}}~~\text{ and }

(19)

\displaystyle\frac{g-1+\sum_{i\in I\cup\{0\}}(b_{i}-2a_{i})r_{i}}{\lambda_{0}-\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}}<s<\frac{n-g+1+\sum_{i\in I\cup\{0\}}(b_{i}-2a_{i})r_{i}}{\lambda_{0}-\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}}.

(20)

Define the divisors

\displaystyle G=\sum_{i=1,i\notin I}^{r}a_{i}D_{i}+\sum_{i\in I}(s\lambda_{i}/r_{i}+b_{i}-a_{i})D_{i}+(n/r_{0}-s\lambda_{0}/r_{0}+b_{0}-a_{0})D_{0}~~\text{and}

\displaystyle H=\sum_{i=1,i\notin I}^{r}(s\lambda_{i}/r_{i}+b_{i}-a_{i})D_{i}+\sum_{i\in I}a_{i}D_{i}+a_{0}D_{0}.

Set
k=s(\lambda_{0}-\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i})-\sum_{i\in I\cup\{0\}}(b_{i}-2a_{i})r_{i}
.
Then
(C_{\mathcal{L}}(D,G),C_{\mathcal{L}}(D,H))
is an LCP of AG codes with parameters

\displaystyle[n,n-k,\geq k-g+1]\text{ and }[n,k,\geq n-k-g+1],

respectively. The security parameter of
(C_{\mathcal{L}}(D,G),C_{\mathcal{L}}(D,H))
is
d(C_{\mathcal{L}}(D,G))
.
Moreover, the AG code
C_{\mathcal{L}}(D,G)
is an LCD code if
\operatorname{res}_{R_{i}}\left(\frac{y^{s}}{h}\omega\right)=\operatorname{res}_{R_{j}}\left(\frac{y^{s}}{h}\omega\right)
for all
1\leq i<j\leq n
.

Proof.

By Theorem
22
, we have that
\sum_{i=0}^{r}(b_{i}-a_{i})D_{i}
is a non-special divisor of degree
g-1
.
An application of Theorem
29
together with Theorem
9
yields that
(C_{\mathcal{L}}(D,G),C_{\mathcal{L}}(D,H))
is an LCP of AG codes.
On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle G+H

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{r}b_{i}D_{i}+s\sum_{i=0}^{r}\frac{\lambda_{i}}{r_{i}}D_{i}-s\frac{\lambda_{0}}{r_{0}}D_{0}+\frac{n}{r_{0}}D_{0}

\displaystyle=(\omega)+(y^{s})-(h)+D.

Then we get
G=D-H+\left(\frac{y^{s}}{h}\omega\right)
and
v_{R_{i}}\left(\frac{y^{s}}{h}\omega\right)=-1
for all
1\leq i\leq n
. Thus

C_{\mathcal{L}}(D,H)^{\bot}=\mathbf{a}\cdot C_{\mathcal{L}}(D,G)\sim C_{\mathcal{L}}(D,G),

where
\mathbf{a}=\left(\operatorname{res}_{R_{1}}\left(\frac{y^{s}}{h}\omega\right),\dots,\operatorname{res}_{R_{n}}\left(\frac{y^{s}}{h}\omega\right)\right)
.
This implies that
d(C_{\mathcal{L}}(D,G))
is the security parameter of
(C_{\mathcal{L}}(D,G),C_{\mathcal{L}}(D,H))
.
Moreover, it follows from Theorem
9
that
C_{\mathcal{L}}(D,G)
is an LCD code if
\operatorname{res}_{R_{i}}\left(\frac{y^{s}}{h}\omega\right)=\operatorname{res}_{R_{j}}\left(\frac{y^{s}}{h}\omega\right)
for all
1\leq i<j\leq n
.
∎

LCPs of Codes on the GK Curve

In this subsection, consider the function field of the GK curve
F=\mathbb{F}_{q^{6}}(x,y)/\mathbb{F}_{q^{6}}(x)
defined by (
13
):

y^{q^{3}+1}=(x^{q}+x)\left(\frac{x^{q^{2}}-x}{x^{q}+x}\right)^{q+1}=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{q}(x-\alpha_{i})\right)\left(\prod_{i=1}^{q^{2}-q}(x-\beta_{i})^{q+1}\right),

where
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q},\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{q^{2}-q}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
.
The genus of
F
is
g=\frac{1}{2}(q^{3}+1)(q^{2}-2)+1
and has
N=q^{8}-q^{6}+q^{5}+1
rational places.
By (
4
), a canonical divisor takes the form

(dx)=\sum_{i=1}^{q}q^{3}Q_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{q^{2}-q-1}(q^{2}-q)D_{i}-(q^{3}+2)Q_{0},

(21)

where
Q_{0}=\frac{1}{q^{3}+1}(x)_{\infty}
,
Q_{i}=\frac{1}{q^{3}+1}(x-\alpha_{i})_{0}
for
1\leq i\leq q
, and
D_{i}=\frac{1}{q^{2}-q+1}(x-\beta_{i})_{0}
for
1\leq i\leq q^{2}-q
.
For each
1\leq j\leq q^{2}-q
, we rewrite

y^{q+1}=\frac{x^{q}+x}{(x^{q}+x)^{{q+1}}}\left(\prod_{i=1,i\neq j}^{q^{2}-q}(x-\beta_{i})\right)^{q+1}(x-\beta_{j})^{q+1}.

Since
\beta_{j}^{q}+\beta_{j}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, there exists
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
such that
\gamma^{q+1}=\beta_{j}^{q}+\beta_{j}
. Then

\frac{\beta_{j}^{q}+\beta_{j}}{(\beta_{j}^{q}+\beta_{j})^{{q+1}}}\left(\prod_{i=1,i\neq j}^{q^{2}-q}(\beta_{j}-\beta_{i})\right)^{q+1}

is an
(q+1)
-th power of an element in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{6}}
. Thus
D_{j}
exactly consists of
q+1
distinct rational places
Q_{j,1},\dots,Q_{j,q+1}
.
We obtain that the number of rational places except
\{Q_{i}\mid 0\leq i\leq q\}\cup\{Q_{j,k}\mid 1\leq j\leq q^{2}-q,\,1\leq k\leq q+1\}
is

q^{8}-q^{6}+q^{5}+1-(q+1)-(q^{2}-q)(q+1)=q^{8}-q^{6}+q^{5}-q^{3}=(q^{3}+1)(q^{5}-q^{3}).

Therefore, there exist
q^{5}-q^{3}
elements
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\dots,\gamma_{q^{5}-q^{3}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{6}}
such that the zero of

VI
Conclusion

In this paper, we studied LCPs of AG codes and LCD AG codes on Kummer extensions. We provided an arithmetic characterization of non-special divisors with arbitrary degree and support possibly containing non-totally ramified places.
Using this characterization and pure gaps, we explicitly constructed non-special divisors of degree
g-1
on the GK curve and three families of effective non-special divisors of degree
g
on Kummer extensions with the same multiplicities.
We then developed a general framework for constructing LCPs of AG codes and showed how to obtain LCD AG codes under specific conditions. Finally, we illustrated our results with explicit examples.

While Theorem
17
gives a characterization of non-special divisors, the existence problem for these divisors is left as an open question.
In Section
V
, we fix
I=\varnothing
and
\omega=dx
when constructing LCPs of AG codes and LCD AG codes from Corollary
30
.
In fact, more new families of LCPs of AG codes and LCD AG codes can be generated by varying the choice of
I
and the Weil differential
\omega
.
In the future, we will focus on the existence of non-special divisors characterized in Theorem
17
and exploit alternative parameter selections to construct further families of LCPs of codes and LCD codes.
