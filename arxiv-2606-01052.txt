Planckian Gravitons from an Imaginary-Time Clock
Michael R.R. Good, Eric V. Linder

Abstract
We present a simple derivation of the exact Planck spectrum of the quadrupole radiation from point masses moving apart nonrelativistically, essentially an analog for gravitational radiation. The standard Einstein quadrupole radiation formula gives emitted power proportional to the square of the third derivative of x ​ ( t ) 2 x(t)^{2} . In our moving-mass picture, imaginary-time periodicity appears as a product-log trajectory of a quadrupole source. In the frequency domain, the power becomes proportional to the Planck distribution, ω 3 / ( e 2 ​ π ​ c ​ ω / κ − 1 ) \omega^{3}/(e^{2\pi c\omega/\kappa}-1) . The resulting Planckian graviton energy spectrum has finite total energy and finite graviton number. The emitted spectrum is purely kinematic in origin: no equilibrium, horizon, or stochastic source is assumed.

II
Rectilinear quadrupole power

The leading nonrelativistic gravitational-wave power is the Einstein quadrupole formula
22
,

P(t)=\frac{G}{5c^{5}}\sum_{i,j}\left(\dddot{I}_{ij}\right)^{2},

(2)

where

I_{ij}=m\left(x_{i}x_{j}-\frac{1}{3}r^{2}\delta_{ij}\right),

(3)

is the trace-free mass quadrupole moment. For rectilinear motion along the
x
-axis,

\mathbf{r}(t)=(x(t),0,0),

(4)

so that

I_{xx}=\frac{2}{3}mx^{2},\qquad I_{yy}=I_{zz}=-\frac{1}{3}mx^{2},

(5)

with all off-diagonal components zero. Hence

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}\left(\dddot{I}_{ij}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{2m}{3}\right)^{2}\left[\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}}{\mathrm{d}t^{3}}x^{2}\right]^{2}+2\left(\frac{m}{3}\right)^{2}\left[\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}}{\mathrm{d}t^{3}}x^{2}\right]^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{2m^{2}}{3}\left[\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}}{\mathrm{d}t^{3}}x^{2}\right]^{2}.

(6)

Substitution into Eq. (
2
) gives

P(t)=\frac{2Gm^{2}}{15c^{5}}\left[\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}}{\mathrm{d}t^{3}}x(t)^{2}\right]^{2}.

(7)

Equivalently,

\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}}{\mathrm{d}t^{3}}\,x^{2}=6\dot{x}\ddot{x}+2x\dddot{x}.

(8)

Thus, unlike for
electromagnetic dipole radiation, the
rectilinear gravitational power is not proportional to
\ddot{x}^{2}
.
It is governed instead by the third derivative of the quadrupole coordinate
x^{2}
.

Let us define the source

S(t)\equiv\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}}{\mathrm{d}t^{3}}x(t)^{2},

(9)

and use the symmetric Fourier convention

\displaystyle\widehat{S}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}S(t)e^{-i\omega t}\,\mathrm{d}t,

\displaystyle S(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\widehat{S}(\omega)e^{i\omega t}\,\mathrm{d}\omega.

(10)

Since
S(t)
is real, the associated positive-frequency energy spectrum is

\frac{\mathrm{d}E}{\mathrm{d}\omega}=\frac{4Gm^{2}}{15c^{5}}\left|\widehat{S}(\omega)\right|^{2}.

(11)

With the symmetric Fourier convention in Eq. (
10
), Parseval’s theorem gives

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}E}{\mathrm{d}\omega}\,\mathrm{d}\omega=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}P(t)\,\mathrm{d}t.

(12)

III
A Planckian quadrupole trajectory

To motivate the quadrupole source history (or
x(t)
trajectory), we first demand the analytic structure associated with a Planck denominator (i.e. the Bose-Einstein phase space distribution), and then ask what quadrupole history realizes it. Let

u=\frac{\kappa t}{c},

(13)

and let
y\sim x^{2}>0
denote the dimensionless quadrupole coordinate, proportional
to
x^{2}
. As in the Hawking
34
;
33
black hole temperature case, the Planck denominator for Euclidean thermal periodicity is tied to complex time, with
\kappa
setting the corresponding inverse-temperature scale
32

branch point, each circuit contributing one such thermal period
26
. Imaginary-time periodicity with period
2\pi c/\kappa
suggests that the inverse map
u=u(y)
contains a logarithmic branch,
because writing

y=re^{i\theta},

gives

\ln y=\ln r+i\theta.

Taking
y
once around the origin in the complex plane corresponds to

\theta\to\theta+2\pi,

and therefore

\ln y\to\ln r+i(\theta+2\pi)=\ln y+2\pi i.

Thus, the logarithm acquires an imaginary-time shift after one circuit around
the branch point at
y=0
, i.e.,
\ln y\rightarrow\ln y+2\pi i
.

The most direct trial is therefore a pure logarithm,

u=\ln y,\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad y=\mathrm{e}^{u}.

(14)

This has the desired imaginary-time shift, but it is not an acceptable
quadrupole history because
y^{\prime\prime\prime}(u)=e^{u}
, so the source grows at late times and its Fourier transform is not defined as an ordinary convergent integral.
The logarithm gives the branch structure, but by itself does not give late-time decay (i.e., at large
y
).

The simplest repair is to keep the same logarithmic branch while adding
one linear term to the inverse map:

y+\ln y=u.

(15)

The logarithmic continuation still shifts
u
by
2\pi i
, while the source now decays in both the past:

y^{\prime\prime\prime}(u)\sim\mathrm{e}^{u}\quad(u\to-\infty),

(16)

and the future

y^{\prime\prime\prime}(u)\sim-\frac{2}{u^{3}}\quad(u\to+\infty).

(17)

Thus, the Fourier integral is well-defined upon adding a linear term. Equivalently, the Fourier phase
takes the gamma-transform form,
with
\Omega\equiv\omega c/\kappa
,

\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\Omega u}=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\Omega y}y^{-\mathrm{i}\Omega},

(18)

so the logarithmic monodromy is retained while the source has the decay
needed for a finite spectrum.

15

(19)

product log. Restoring dimensions,

Spectrum, energy, and graviton count

For the trajectory in Eq. (
20
)
the source Fourier transform to compute the radiation spectrum is

\widehat{S}(\omega)=\frac{\epsilon^{2}c^{2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-i\Omega u}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}}{\mathrm{d}u^{3}}W(e^{u})\,\mathrm{d}u.

\Omega>0

\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-i\Omega u}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}}{\mathrm{d}u^{3}}W(e^{u})\,\mathrm{d}u\right|^{2}=\frac{\Omega^{3}}{e^{2\pi\Omega}-1}.

\frac{\mathrm{d}E}{\mathrm{d}\omega}=\frac{4Gm^{2}c^{2}\epsilon^{4}}{15\kappa^{3}}\frac{\omega^{3}}{e^{2\pi c\omega/\kappa}-1}.

(30)

This is the Planck energy spectrum.
Equivalently, writing
2\pi c\omega/\kappa=\hbar\omega/(k_{B}T)

T=\frac{\hbar\kappa}{2\pi ck_{B}}.

(31)

The total emitted energy is finite:

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}E}{\mathrm{d}\omega}\,\mathrm{d}\omega

\displaystyle=\frac{4Gm^{2}c^{2}\epsilon^{4}}{15\kappa^{3}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\omega^{3}}{e^{2\pi c\omega/\kappa}-1}\,\mathrm{d}\omega.

(32)

Using

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\omega^{3}}{e^{2\pi c\omega/\kappa}-1}\,\mathrm{d}\omega=\frac{\kappa^{4}}{240c^{4}},

(33)

we obtain

E=\frac{Gm^{2}\epsilon^{4}\kappa}{900c^{2}}.

(34)

E\propto\kappa

E\propto T

conflict with the Stefan–Boltzmann law. Eq. (
30

equilibrium radiance per unit area. The explicit
\kappa^{-3}

fixes the frequency dependence, while the overall scaling is set by the

V
Deformations of the Log Clock

The product-log trajectory in Eq. (
20
)
is the minimal way to combine two requirements. The
logarithm supplies the imaginary-time branch structure,
while the added linear term supplies the late-time decay
needed for an ordinary Fourier spectrum. It is useful to
separate these two roles by replacing the linear repair
with a positive-power repair,

u=\ln y+y^{p},\qquad p>0.

(39)

The case
p=1
is the product-log clock used above.
For integer
p
, the repair term
y^{p}
is single-valued
around
y=0
, so the only monodromy is still

\ln y\to\ln y+2\pi i
. Noninteger values of
p
are also useful as continuous spectral deformations, although in that case, the repair term itself introduces additional
branch structure.

Eq. (
39
) can be solved in
terms of the Lambert function. Writing
z=y^{p}
, one
has

pu=\ln z+pz,

(40)

and hence

y_{p}(u)=\left[\frac{1}{p}\,W\!\left(p\,\mathrm{e}^{pu}\right)\right]^{1/p}.

(41)

Thus, the corresponding rectilinear trajectory is

x_{p}(t)=\epsilon\frac{c^{2}}{\kappa}\sqrt{y_{p}(t)},\qquad q_{p}(t)=x_{p}(t)^{2}=\epsilon^{2}\frac{c^{4}}{\kappa^{2}}\,y_{p}(t).

(42)

The original trajectory is recovered for

p=1
, since
y_{1}(u)=W(\mathrm{e}^{u})
. See Figure
1
for illustration.

42
), associated with the Lambert-
W

p=\tfrac{1}{2}

p=1

p=2

, giving

.

Differentiating Eq. (
39
) gives

\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}u}=\frac{y}{1+py^{p}}.

(43)

Therefore, the velocity is

v_{p}(t)=\dot{x}_{p}(t)=\frac{\epsilon c}{2}\,\frac{\sqrt{y}}{1+py^{p}}.

(44)

This makes the role of the repair term transparent. The
factor
\sqrt{y}
is inherited from the logarithmic clock,
while the denominator regulates the late-time motion. At
large
y
,

\frac{v_{p}}{c}\sim\frac{\epsilon}{2p}\,y^{1/2-p}.

(45)

Thus, the source returns asymptotically to rest for

p>1/2
, approaches a constant velocity for
p=1/2
,
and eventually leaves the rest-to-rest nonrelativistic
class for
p<1/2
.
(One could also turn this derivation around by starting with a velocity regularization of the form Eq. (
44
) and deriving Eq. (
39
) from it.)

The dimensionless quadrupole source is obtained by three
derivatives with respect to
u
:

\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}y}{\mathrm{d}u^{3}}=\frac{y\left[1-p(p^{2}+3p-2)y^{p}+p^{2}(p-1)(2p-1)y^{2p}\right]}{(1+py^{p})^{5}}.

(46)

The physical source is therefore

S_{p}(t)\equiv\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}q_{p}}{\mathrm{d}t^{3}}=\epsilon^{2}c\kappa\,\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}y}{\mathrm{d}u^{3}}.

(47)

As
u\to-\infty
, one has
y\sim\mathrm{e}^{u}
, and hence

\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}y}{\mathrm{d}u^{3}}\sim\mathrm{e}^{u}.

(48)

The early-time behavior is therefore unchanged: all
members of the family retain the logarithmic clock near
the branch point. At late times,
y^{p}\sim u
, so

y\sim u^{1/p}
. For generic
p\neq 1,1/2
,

\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}y}{\mathrm{d}u^{3}}\sim\frac{(p-1)(2p-1)}{p^{3}}\,y^{1-3p}\sim\frac{(p-1)(2p-1)}{p^{3}}\,u^{1/p-3}\ ,

(49)

and thus
y^{\prime\prime\prime}
vanishes at late times as
u\to+\infty
for
p>1/3
.
The coefficient of this leading term vanishes at

p=1
and
p=1/2
. In particular, for the product-log
case
p=1
,

\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}y}{\mathrm{d}u^{3}}=\frac{y(1-2y)}{(1+y)^{5}}\sim-\frac{2}{u^{3}},\qquad u\to+\infty,

(50)

which is the late-time falloff used in the exact 3D
Planckian construction.

The corresponding Fourier transform can also be written
in closed form. Define

{\cal I}_{p}(\Omega)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\Omega u}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}y}{\mathrm{d}u^{3}}\,\mathrm{d}u,\qquad\Omega=\frac{c\omega}{\kappa}.

(51)

For
p>1/2
, the source has sufficient late-time decay
for the transform to be understood as an ordinary
oscillatory integral, with the usual small damping
prescription. Integrating twice by parts gives

\displaystyle{\cal I}_{p}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{(\mathrm{i}\Omega)^{2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\Omega u}\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}u}\,\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=\frac{(\mathrm{i}\Omega)^{2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{-\mathrm{i}\Omega}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\Omega y^{p}}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\frac{(\mathrm{i}\Omega)^{2}}{p\sqrt{2\pi}}\,(\mathrm{i}\Omega)^{-(1-\mathrm{i}\Omega)/p}\Gamma\!\left(\frac{1-\mathrm{i}\Omega}{p}\right).

(52)

Therefore

|{\cal I}_{p}(\Omega)|^{2}=\frac{\Omega^{4-2/p}}{2\pi p^{2}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\pi\Omega/p}\left|\Gamma\!\left(\frac{1-\mathrm{i}\Omega}{p}\right)\right|^{2}.

(53)

The energy spectrum of the deformed clock is then

\frac{\mathrm{d}E_{p}}{\mathrm{d}\omega}=\frac{4Gm^{2}\epsilon^{4}}{15c}\,|{\cal I}_{p}(c\omega/\kappa)|^{2}.

(54)

Eq. (
53
) makes clear why the
product-log clock
p=1
is distinguished within the
deformed family. For
p=1
,

|{\cal I}_{1}(\Omega)|^{2}=\frac{\Omega^{2}}{2\pi}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\pi\Omega}|\Gamma(1-\mathrm{i}\Omega)|^{2}.

(55)

Using

|\Gamma(1-\mathrm{i}\Omega)|^{2}=\frac{\pi\Omega}{\sinh\pi\Omega},

(56)

one obtains

|{\cal I}_{1}(\Omega)|^{2}=\frac{\Omega^{3}}{\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\Omega}-1}.

(57)

Substitution into Eq. (
54
)
reproduces Eq. (
30
). Thus the
linear repair
y
is a convenient
regularization and the member of this family for which the logarithmic clock and the Fourier transform combine
to give the exact 3D Planck energy spectrum.

For
p\neq 1
, the logarithmic branch still organizes the
analytic structure, but the spectral weight is deformed by
the gamma function in Eq. (
53
). In the
infrared,

|{\cal I}_{p}(\Omega)|^{2}\sim\frac{\Gamma(1/p)^{2}}{2\pi p^{2}}\,\Omega^{4-2/p},\qquad\Omega\to 0.

(58)

Hence

\frac{\mathrm{d}E_{p}}{\mathrm{d}\omega}\propto\omega^{4-2/p},\qquad\frac{\mathrm{d}N_{p}}{\mathrm{d}\omega}\propto\omega^{3-2/p},\qquad\omega\to 0.

(59)

The total radiated energy is infrared finite for

p>2/5
, while the total graviton number is infrared
finite for
p>1/2
. The latter condition is the same
condition that makes the velocity in
Eq. (
44
) return to zero at late
times. Thus the physically clean rest-to-rest,
finite-graviton deformations satisfy
p>1/2
, with the
product-log clock
p=1
sitting safely inside this class.

This also clarifies why the late-time tail alone does not
determine the spectrum in the low-frequency limit. In the product-log
case the source behaves as
\mathrm{d}^{3}y/\mathrm{d}u^{3}\sim-2/u^{3}

at late times, but the exact transform gives

|{\cal I}_{1}(\Omega)|^{2}=\frac{\Omega^{3}}{\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\Omega}-1}\sim\frac{\Omega^{2}}{2\pi},\qquad\Omega\to 0.

(60)

The infrared behavior is controlled by the full source
history and its global moment structure. The deformed family, therefore,
teaches a useful lesson:
the logarithm supplies the imaginary-time clock, the
positive-power term supplies convergence, and the
linear repair
p=1
is the special choice that yields the
exact Planckian graviton spectrum.

VI
Discussion

The preceding section shows that the exact Planck form is
not a generic consequence of adding any late-time repair to
the logarithmic clock; it is selected by the product-log
member
p=1
. The result is best interpreted
as a kinematic out-of-equilibrium construction. No horizon is present. The Planck factor arises because the dimensionless quadrupole coordinate satisfies the inverse relation
y+\ln y=u
, whose logarithmic branch structure for imaginary-time periodicity produces the Bose-Einstein factor in frequency space (as for the black hole case).

We emphasize the contrast with electromagnetic radiation induced by the acceleration of a charge.
For rectilinear motion, this distinction is illustrated:

\textrm{photons:}\quad\ddot{x}(t),\qquad\textrm{gravitons:}\quad\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}}{\mathrm{d}t^{3}}x(t)^{2}.

(61)

Nonrelativistic gravitational radiation is governed by the third derivative of the mass quadrupole.
Thus, Eq. (
20
) should not be read as making the acceleration

\ddot{x}(t)
Planckian. A (timelike) worldline
x(t)
of course enters both cases, but the radiating source functional is different: electromagnetic
dipole radiation involves
\ddot{x}(t)
, whereas the present gravitational
calculation involves
\mathrm{d}^{3}x(t)^{2}/\mathrm{d}t^{3}
. It is this quadrupole source,
not the bare acceleration, whose Fourier transform gives the Planck factor.

VI.1
Normalized quadrupole source primitive

The same comparison suggests a useful dimensionless primitive for the
radiating source, analogous to the von Neumann entanglement entropy for particle production in contexts like mirrors, black holes, and beta decay
30
.

Since the gravitational power is controlled by the
third derivative of the quadrupole coordinate
q=x^{2}
, rather than by the acceleration
\ddot{x}
, define

{\cal S}_{g}(t)\equiv\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{45}}\,\frac{1}{c^{2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}x(t)^{2}.

(62)

This quantity is dimensionless.
Recall that when the power is sourced by the acceleration, the entanglement entropy
{\cal S}_{\rm acc}\sim\dot{x}
, i.e., one less time derivative than the source, so our definition
{\cal S}_{g}
is directly analogous.

For the product-log clock

y+\ln y=u
, with
q=\epsilon^{2}c^{4}y/\kappa^{2}
, it becomes

{\cal S}_{g}(u)=\epsilon^{2}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{45}}\,\frac{y}{(1+y)^{3}}.

(63)

Thus
{\cal S}_{g}
vanishes as
u\to\pm\infty
and has the finite
maximum

{\cal S}_{g,\max}=\frac{4}{27}\,\epsilon^{2}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{45}},\qquad y=\frac{1}{2}.

(64)

It is therefore a localized quadrupole-source amplitude associated with
the prescribed history.

The normalization in Eq. (
62
) is useful because its time derivative is exactly proportional to the radiating source:

\dot{\cal S}_{g}=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{45}}\,\frac{1}{c^{2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}}{\mathrm{d}t^{3}}x(t)^{2}.

(65)

Consequently, the instantaneous power may be written as

P(t)=\frac{6Gm^{2}}{\pi c}\,\dot{\cal S}_{g}^{\,2},

(66)

in close analogy to the acceleration case, i.e., the Larmor formula
P(t)\sim\dot{\cal S}_{\rm acc}^{\,2}
.
Equivalently,

E=\frac{6Gm^{2}}{\pi c}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\dot{\cal S}_{g}^{\,2}\,\mathrm{d}t=\frac{Gm^{2}\epsilon^{4}\kappa}{900c^{2}},

(67)

which is the same total energy obtained from the Planckian frequency
spectrum. This gives a compact consistency check: the entropic variable whose derivative gives the quadrupole radiation source also reproduces the time-domain norm of the emitted radiation.

While the entropy object Eq. (
62
) is not the equilibrium thermodynamic entropy, it plays an operational role as the normalized primitive of the quadrupole source. In the full tensor description, the
corresponding object is therefore additive in the mass density because
I_{ij}[\rho_{1}+\rho_{2}]=I_{ij}[\rho_{1}]+I_{ij}[\rho_{2}]
; thus
\mathcal{S}_{ij}\propto\ddot{I}_{ij}
. In this sense
{\cal S}_{g}
packages
the relevant source information: its derivative carries the radiated power, and the logarithmic clock
y+\ln y=u
supplies the analytic structure responsible for the spectral Bose-Einstein factor.

VI.2
Frequency spectrum versus angular pattern

The Planckian character of Eq. (
30
) is a statement about the
frequency spectrum, without an angular distribution. The radiation field is not isotropic.
Rectilinearity selects a preferred spatial axis, and the transverse-traceless projection
of the corresponding trace-free quadrupole gives the usual spin-two angular pattern.
If
\theta
denotes the angle between the observation direction and the rectilinear axis, then

\frac{dE}{d\omega\,d\Omega_{\hat{\mathbf{n}}}}=\frac{15}{32\pi}\sin^{4}\theta\,\frac{dE}{d\omega}.

(68)

Here
d\Omega_{\hat{\mathbf{n}}}=\sin\theta\,d\theta\,d\phi
is the solid-angle element on the observation sphere, with

0\leq\theta\leq\pi
and
0\leq\phi<2\pi
. The factor
15/(32\pi)

normalizes the angular distribution, since

\int d\Omega_{\hat{\mathbf{n}}}\,\sin^{4}\theta=32\pi/15
.

A strictly isotropic breathing mode does not produce the present radiation because a
spherically symmetric expansion or contraction changes only the monopole part of the
source; its trace-free quadrupole moment vanishes, and hence there is no gravitational
radiation at this order. Thus, while the logarithmic imaginary-time structure provides the directionally independent Bose-Einstein factor, the spin-two quadrupole projection provides the angular dependence. The radiation distribution is therefore Planckian in frequency but anisotropic in angle,
and the thermal signature is out of equilibrium in the absence of a blackbody field.

VII
Conclusions

We have shown that the standard quadrupole formula reduces, for rectilinear nonrelativistic motion, to a
source term
proportional to
[\dddot{x^{2}}]^{2}
where the product-log trajectory

x(t)=\epsilon\frac{c^{2}}{\kappa}\sqrt{W\!\left(e^{\kappa t/c}\right)},

(69)

gives an exact Planck spectrum for the emitted gravitational radiation,

\frac{\mathrm{d}E}{\mathrm{d}\omega}=\frac{4Gm^{2}c^{2}\epsilon^{4}}{15\kappa^{3}}\frac{\omega^{3}}{e^{2\pi c\omega/\kappa}-1}.

(70)

The total energy and total graviton number are

E=\frac{Gm^{2}\epsilon^{4}\kappa}{900c^{2}},\qquad N=\frac{Gm^{2}\epsilon^{4}\zeta(3)}{15\pi^{3}\hbar c}.

(71)

The construction demonstrates exact Planckian graviton radiation from nonrelativistic quadrupole kinematics. Its physical significance demonstrates both a thermal clock in imaginary time and a spectral origin rather than a thermodynamic one: the radiation is Planck-distributed, without an equilibrium bath.

There is no spacetime horizon in this construction. The variables

u=\kappa t/c
and
y\propto x^{2}
range over

u\in(-\infty,\infty)
and
y\in(0,\infty)
, with the displacement
unbounded only asymptotically at late times. Thus, the Planck factor is not attributed to a causal boundary, a null-shell geometry, or a statistical KMS periodicity, but to the logarithmic monodromy of the prescribed quadrupole source history.
