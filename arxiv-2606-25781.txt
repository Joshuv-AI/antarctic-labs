Interfaces and non-uniqueness in a cross-diffusion system with independent drifts
Charles Elbar, Guy Parker

Abstract
We study a one-dimensional cross-diffusion system of two populations. Their densities are diffused with a common pressure that depends on the total density, but are transported by two independent external potentials. Starting from segregated initial data with a single interface, we show that the way the two densities meet is not given by the equation alone: it depends on the notion of solution one chooses. When the drifts push the two phases towards each other at the interface, the vanishing-viscosity solution creates an overlap, whereas a segregated weak solution also exists. The two solutions are distinct, so the Cauchy problem is not well posed in the class of weak solutions. We first prove the result for an explicit stationary segregated solution, and then extend the non-uniqueness to general segregated data.

Introduction

We consider the evolution of two densities
\rho(t,x)
and
\mu(t,x)
on the whole space
\mathbb{R}
over a time interval
[0,T]
for some
T>0
.
They evolve according to the cross-diffusion system:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho-\partial_{x}(\rho\,\partial_{x}f^{\prime}(\rho+\mu))-\partial_{x}(\rho\,\partial_{x}V)

\displaystyle=0,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mu-\partial_{x}(\mu\,\partial_{x}f^{\prime}(\rho+\mu))-\partial_{x}(\mu\,\partial_{x}W)

\displaystyle=0.

(1.2)

Here, the system is equipped with non-negative initial densities
\rho(0,\cdot)=\rho_{0}
and
\mu(0,\cdot)=\mu_{0}
.
The functions
V,W\in C^{3}(\mathbb{R})
are external potentials, and the non-linearity
f^{\prime}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is the pressure law.

We are motivated by the following phenomenon: suppose the two populations are initially segregated, that is
\rho_{0}
is supported
on
\{x\leq 0\}
and
\mu_{0}
on
\{x\geq 0\}
, and they meet at the single interface
x=0
. A natural question is whether the segregation persists. If the two drifts coincide, that is
V\equiv W
, then the common velocity simply transports the interface, and the two densities stay segregated. The interesting regime is when the drifts are different and, in particular, when they push the two phases towards one another at the interface.

The velocity of
\rho
is
-\partial_{x}f^{\prime}(\rho+\mu)-\partial_{x}V
and that of
\mu
is
-\partial_{x}f^{\prime}(\rho+\mu)-\partial_{x}W
.
At the interface, the pressure terms are the same, so the motion of the two phases depends only on the difference of the drifts.
The two phases approach each other exactly when

\partial_{x}W(0)-\partial_{x}V(0)>0.

(1.3)

At this point, we show that the notion of solution matters. One may select solutions by vanishing viscosity, adding
\varepsilon\,\partial_{xx}
to each equation and letting
\varepsilon\to 0
.
On one hand, this is the natural approach that usually selects a physical solution and is the one for which existence is usually done. On the other hand, one may obtain a weak solution that keeps the two phases segregated. We show that, under
(
1.3
), these two approaches yield different solutions, hence showing non-uniqueness.

1.1
Main ideas

Our results are of two kinds.

•

An explicit example.
For suitable convex
f
and potentials
V,W
there exists an explicit stationary state
(\bar{\rho},\bar{\mu})
in which the gradient of the pressure is equal to each drift on its own side of the interface. We then prove that no vanishing-viscosity solution can be segregated when (
1.3
) holds. In particular,
(\bar{\rho},\bar{\mu})
is not the vanishing-viscosity solution, and the two are distinct weak solutions with the same data.

•

General data.
We then show that the phenomenon is not an artefact of the explicit profile. For the power-type pressures

f_{\alpha}^{\prime}(s)=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}s^{\alpha-1}\quad(\alpha\neq 1),\quad f_{1}^{\prime}(s)=\log s,

with
\alpha>\frac{1}{3}
, and for any segregated initial data, the segregated
solution and the vanishing-viscosity solution are again different.

The method behind both results is the relative-entropy functional

\Phi[\rho,\mu]=\int_{\mathbb{R}}h(\rho,\mu)\mathop{}\mathrm{d}x,\quad h(u,v)=(u+v)\log(u+v)-u\log u-v\log v.

(1.4)

Here
h
is non-negative and vanishes exactly where
uv=0
, so
\Phi
measures the overlap of the two phases:
\Phi=0
iff the state is segregated. The main identity, which can be made at the viscous level and sending
\varepsilon\to 0
is the lower bound

\Phi[\rho(t),\mu(t)]\geq\int_{0}^{t}\langle q(s),\partial_{x}V-\partial_{x}W\rangle\mathop{}\mathrm{d}s,

where
q
is a distribution that coincides with a (negative) Dirac mass for densities with a shared interface. Thus the right-hand side is strictly positive under (
1.3
). A segregated solution has
\Phi\equiv 0
and therefore cannot satisfy this bound.

Non-Uniqueness for Systems:

It is well known that systems of uniformly parabolic equations can exhibit wild behaviour, leading to the global ill-posedness of solutions, even in the presence of smooth or analytic coefficients (cf.
[
60
,
59
]
).
Furthermore, when the data is chosen to be singular enough, non-uniqueness may even develop for a linear elliptic equation of a single species (see
[
57
,
55
]
, see also
[
50
]
for parabolic systems).
Consequently, a stronger notion of solution, such as an entropy solution
[
18
]
, or renormalised solution
[
7
]
, is often necessary to recover a full well-posedness theory.
However, despite such classical counterexamples, the literature exposing non-uniqueness for cross-diffusion systems is sparse and, general, pathological counterexamples may appear divorced from the practical existence theory.

The contribution of this work is to establish the non-uniqueness of solutions for System (
1.1
)–(
1.2
), precisely by showing that sharp-interspecies interfaces can spontaneously breakdown, leading to a zone of mixing.
To the best of the authors’ knowledge, this result is the first of its kind amongst such models.
Moreover, this phenomenon has not been observed in the context of cross-diffusion systems but, rather, bears a striking resemblance to the non-uniqueness behaviour observed for the Muskat problem
[
51
]
.

The Muskat problem considers the evolving interface of two fluids of variable density which initially sit in segregation, one atop the other.
Consequent to the gravitational forces acting on the system, it has been shown that the fluid produces an unstable interface when the denser fluid is initialised on top of the profile.
Moreover, as was established throughout
[
53
,
19
,
52
,
32
]
, the interface may spontaneously yield to a zone of mixing which grows over time. This is exactly the behaviour that we establish for System (
1.1
)–(
1.2
).

An Entropy Approach to Non-uniqueness:

As a novel approach to non-uniqueness, we study the time dissipation of the
relative entropy
(
1.4
).
That is to say, the entropy corresponding to the difference between the entropy of the sum and the sum of the individual entropies.
This specific choice of entropy is distinguished amongst those which measure the mixing of the two densities because its dissipation does not depend on the pressure gradient.

As a contrasting example, one could hope to establish a similar result by studying the dissipation of the energy

\mathcal{G}[\rho,\mu]=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho\mu\mathop{}\mathrm{d}x

which also vanishes if and only if the densities are segregated.
A formal calculation of the dissipation of
\mathcal{G}
along the flow of System (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) shows that this choice of relative entropy produces a term

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}f^{\prime}(S)\left(\rho\partial_{x}\mu+\mu\partial_{x}\rho\right)\mathop{}\mathrm{d}x.

(1.9)

However, due to the inherent formation of inter-species interfaces,
\partial_{x}\rho,\partial_{x}\mu
can not behave better than measures (cf.
[
48
]
).
On the other hand, the pressure gradient
\partial_{x}f^{\prime}(S)
may lack continuity precisely at the interface.
Consequently, one can not guarantee that the Integral (
1.9
) can be identified or even given meaning in the limiting system.
This means that relative entropies such as
\mathcal{G}
are unsuitable for our non-uniqueness method.

Having identified a suitable candidate entropy and calculated its dissipation through the viscous approximation, a delicate aspect of our argument is show that the dissipation of the relative entropy has a positive sign over a time interval of positive measure.
In particular, one must show that this sign is preserved in the vanishing viscosity limit.
By establishing the positivity of the relative entropy, we establish a positive mixing of the two phases after the initial time.
In contrast, the regularity of segregated solutions is too low to justify this dissipation argument and so, since segregated solutions are not obtained as the limit of a smooth approximating system, we do not obtain a contradiction.

When studying general initial data, a crucial aspect of our analysis is to prove that the interface can neither vanish, nor jump away from the neighbourhood where the two drifts enforce mixing.
It is essential to retain both properties in the vanishing viscosity limit.
The latter result is shown by exploiting the regularity established for segregated solutions in
[
56
]
, however, proving that the interface persists is a more delicate matter, particularly in the regime of slow-diffusion.

Indeed, if
f(s)\propto s^{\alpha},\alpha>1
, diffusive effects degenerate around areas of low density.
Consequently, the aggregate density may vanish to a vacuum in areas where the drifts encourage the separation of the two phases.
To ensure that this behaviour can not occur when the sign of the drifts is favourable, we prove that the system decouples if a vacuum state occurs around the interface thus yielding the governing equations

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho

\displaystyle=\partial_{xx}(\rho^{\alpha})+\partial_{x}(\rho\partial_{x}V)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mu

\displaystyle=\partial_{xx}(\mu^{\alpha})+\partial_{x}(\mu\partial_{x}W).

Given the decoupled system, we construct sub-solutions inspired by the travelling wave profiles introduced in
[
61
]
(see also
[
62
, Chapter X,
\mathsection
3]
).
Moreover, by using the comparison principle proven in
[
42
]
, the sub-solution construction shows that
\rho
and
\mu
must mix as long as the difference of the drifts possesses the correct sign.
Thus, we contradict the existence of a vacuum state.

An explicit segregated solution

Here we prove Theorem

Proof of Theorem

We use the following notations in our proof:

\bar{\rho}(x)=g\left(A-V(x)\right)\mathbf{1}_{(-\infty,0]}(x),\quad\bar{\mu}(x)=g\left(A-W(x)\right)\mathbf{1}_{[0,\infty)}(x),

and define
(\bar{\rho}(t,x),\bar{\mu}(t,x))=(\bar{\rho}(x),\bar{\mu}(x))
for every
t\in[0,T]
.
By construction and assumptions
(A3)
,
\bar{\rho}
and
\bar{\mu}
are non-negative.
Moreover, we have

\supp\bar{\rho}\subset(-\infty,0],\quad\supp\bar{\mu}\subset[0,+\infty).

We define
\bar{S}=\bar{\rho}+\bar{\mu}
. Passing to the limits as
x\to 0^{-}
and
x\to 0^{+}
, and using
V(0)=W(0)=0
, we obtain that
\bar{S}
can be extended continuously at 0, with
\bar{S}(0)=g(A)=\sigma_{*}

and:

f^{\prime}(\bar{S}(x))+V(x)=f^{\prime}(g(A-V(x)))+V(x)=A-V(x)+V(x)=A\quad\text{for }x<0.

Similarly,

f^{\prime}(\bar{S}(x))+W(x)=A\quad\text{for }x>0.

We can take the derivative
\partial_{x}
on each side of the line, and then multiplying by
\bar{\rho}
and

\bar{\rho}\partial_{x}f^{\prime}(\bar{S})+\bar{\rho}\partial_{x}V=0,\quad\bar{\mu}\partial_{x}f^{\prime}(\bar{S})+\bar{\mu}\partial_{x}W=0,\quad\text{for a.e. }x\in\mathbb{R}.

It is then clear that

Proposition 3.1

(Limit entropy)

Lemma 3.3

Let

Proof of Theorem

.

The first part of Theorem

3.2
. Now assume by contradiction that

Non-uniqueness for general initial data

In this section, we prove Theorem

Indeed, any vanishing viscosity solution must satisfy Inequality (

Thus, given suitable potentials and initial datum, the sign of the relative entropy contradicts the segregation property.
In extending the approach to general initial data the novel considerations which concern the interface are two-fold.

1.

Localisation:
Firstly, by Assumptions
(B1)
–
(B3)

In this scenario, the relative entropy (

4.2
Interface persistence

Under our standing Assumption
(A2)
, the two drifts point towards one another so as to encourage the mixing of the two phases, whilst, due to

Under these hypotheses, it is proven by contradiction that the interface can not vanish by means of the aggregate density obtaining a state of vacuum.

Indeed, in supposing that a vacuum state occurs at the interface of the two densities, we show that each of the two species satisfy a non-linear drift-diffusion equation and their evolution becomes decoupled.
Such parabolic equations satisfy comparison principles and Harnack inequalities, thus, it is shown that the independently evolving densities will overlap, contradicting the existence of a vacuum state.

\rho,\mu\in C^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2},2+\alpha}_{loc}((0,T]\times\mathbb{R})

The function

,

may be chosen, for each

The proof holds analogously for

Proposition 4.7

(Fast-Diffusion)
.

Let
(\rho,\mu)
denote a solution of System (
4.4
) with
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{3},1)
.
Further, suppose that
\rho,\mu\in L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R})
.
Then
\rho,\mu>0
on
(0,T]\times\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

It appears that a comparison principle for singular drift-diffusion equations is missing within the current literature.
Consequently, the result is proven via the Harnack Inequality for fast diffusion:
[
25
, Corollary 16.1]
.
The proof is presented for the density
\rho
whilst a completely analogous argument holds for
\mu
.

To proceed, it is verified that
\rho
satisfies the structural condition specified in
[
25
, Inequality 5.2, Page 33]
.
This condition states that there must exist
C_{0},C_{1},C>0
for which
\rho
satisfies the following two inequalities.

\displaystyle(\partial_{x}\rho^{\alpha}+\rho\partial_{x}V)\cdot\partial_{x}\rho\geq C_{0}\alpha|\rho|^{\alpha-1}|\partial_{x}\rho|^{2}-C^{2}|\rho|^{\alpha+1}

(4.8)

\displaystyle|\partial_{x}\rho^{\alpha}+\rho\partial_{x}V|\leq C_{1}\alpha|\rho|^{\alpha-1}|\partial_{x}\rho|+C|\rho|^{\alpha}.

(4.9)

To verify Inequality (
4.8
), we write

\displaystyle(\partial_{x}\rho^{\alpha}+\rho\partial_{x}V)\cdot\partial_{x}\rho=\alpha\rho^{\alpha-1}|\partial_{x}\rho|^{2}+(\rho^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}\partial_{x}\rho)\cdot(\partial_{x}V\rho^{\frac{3-\alpha}{2}}).

Via Young’s Inequality, it follows that

\displaystyle(\partial_{x}\rho^{\alpha}+\rho\partial_{x}V)\cdot\partial_{x}\rho\geq\frac{\alpha}{2}|\rho|^{\alpha-1}|\partial_{x}\rho|^{2}-\frac{1}{2\alpha}|\partial_{x}V|^{2}\rho^{3-\alpha}.

Letting
c=\|\rho^{1-\alpha}\partial_{x}V\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R})}
, the above inequality then yields

\displaystyle(\partial_{x}\rho^{\alpha}+\rho\partial_{x}V)\cdot\partial_{x}\rho\geq\frac{\alpha}{2}|\rho|^{\alpha-1}|\partial_{x}\rho|^{2}-\frac{c^{2}}{2\alpha}|\rho|^{\alpha+1}

which verifies Inequality (
4.8
).
In particular,
c
is finite since
\partial_{x}V\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
and
\rho\in L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R})
.
To verify Inequality (
4.9
), it is sufficient to recognise that

\displaystyle|\partial_{x}\rho^{\alpha}+\rho\partial_{x}V|\leq\alpha|\rho|^{\alpha-1}|\partial_{x}\rho|+c|\rho|^{\alpha}.

Having checked the sufficient structural conditions, it follows from
[
25
, Corollary 16.1]
that there exists
\Lambda>0
, which depends only on
c
, and
\gamma=\gamma(\alpha)>0
, such that the inequality

\gamma^{-1}\sup_{x\in[x_{0}-\sigma,x_{0}+\sigma]}\rho(t_{0},x)\leq\rho(t_{0},x_{0})\leq\gamma\inf_{x\in[x_{0}-\sigma,x_{0}+\sigma]}\rho(t_{0},x)

(4.10)

is satisfied for any
\sigma\in(0,\Lambda)
and any
(t_{0},x_{0})\in(0,T]\times\mathbb{R}
for which
t_{0}
satisfies

t_{0}\geq 64\sigma^{2}\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R})}^{1-\alpha}.

(4.11)

Notice that, in contrast to the classical Harnack Inequality in the uniformly parabolic regime, this inequality is elliptic in the sense that (
4.10
) only considers data at time
t_{0}
.

Now, fix
t_{0}\in(0,T],x_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
and let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
be such that
\rho(t_{0},x_{0})>0
.
Further, fix
\sigma>0
such that Inequality (
4.11
) is satisfied and choose
k\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
x_{1}\in[x_{0}-k\sigma,x_{0}+k\sigma]
.
To conclude that
\rho(t_{0},x_{1})>0
, Inequality (
4.10
) is iterated in the following fashion.

\displaystyle\rho(t_{0},x_{0})

\displaystyle\leq\gamma\inf_{x\in[x_{0}-\sigma,x_{0}+\sigma]}\rho(t_{0},x)\leq\gamma^{2}\inf_{x\in[x_{0}-2\sigma,x_{0}+2\sigma]}\rho(t_{0},x)

\displaystyle\dots

\displaystyle\leq\gamma^{k}\inf_{x\in[x_{0}-k\sigma,x_{0}+k\sigma]}\rho(t_{0},x)\leq\rho(t_{0},x_{1}).

Since the point
(t_{0},x_{1})\in(0,T]\times\mathbb{R}
was chosen arbitrarily, we conclude the claim.
∎

Remark 4.8
.

Since the equation for the sum can also be written as a parabolic equation equipped with an
L^{\infty}_{t,x}
drift, we could have applied similar arguments to prove that the sum can not produce a vacuum state without the decoupling argument.
On the other hand, the following argument for the slow diffusion necessitates the decoupling of the system.

As a juxtaposition to the linear and fast-diffusive regimes, the slow-diffusive pressure law does not exhibit an infinite speed of propagation as the density approaches vacuum.
Moreover, it is not guaranteed that the interface will persist in the presence of drift terms which encourage the separation of the two densities.
Consequently, we utilise the specific structure of the drift terms around the origin to construct pressure sub-solutions.
Due to the comparison principle, these sub-solutions ensure a mixing zone around the origin.

Then, the equality

q=\partial_{x}\rho_{t}-\frac{\rho}{S}\partial_{x}S_{t}=-\delta_{u_{t}(1)}S_{t}

is satisfied in the sense of distributions.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.1
, the equality

\rho_{t}(x)=S_{t}(x)\mathbf{1}_{(-\infty,u_{t}(1)]}(x)

is satisfied.
Thus, for a smooth test function
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R})
the following equality holds in the sense of distributions.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi\left(\partial_{x}\rho_{t}-\frac{\rho}{S}\partial_{x}S\right)\mathop{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathrm{d}t=-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}\varphi\rho_{t}+\varphi\frac{\rho}{S}\partial_{x}S\mathop{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\partial_{x}\varphi S_{t}-\varphi\partial_{x}S\right)\mathbf{1}_{\{x\leq u_{t}(1)\}}\mathop{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathrm{d}t=-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{-\infty}^{u_{t}(1)}\partial_{x}\varphi S_{t}-\varphi\partial_{x}S_{t}\mathop{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{T}\varphi(t,u_{t}(1))S_{t}(u_{t}(1))\mathop{}\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi\left(-\delta_{u_{t}(1)}S_{t}\right)\mathop{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathrm{d}t.

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.9
.

Let
(\rho,\mu)
denote the segregated solution to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) as constructed in
[
56
, Theorem 3.5]
.
Since the initial data satisfies Assumption
(B1)
, we may further suppose
(\rho,\mu)
that can be obtained from a vanishing viscosity approximation by the existence theory of
[
58
, Theorem 1.3]
.
Then, by Proposition
3.2
it follows that

\Phi[\rho(t),\mu(t)]\geq\int_{0}^{t}\langle q(s),\partial_{x}V-\partial_{x}W\rangle\mathop{}\mathrm{d}s.

for almost every
t\in[0,T]
.

Now, let
U
be a neighbourhood of the origin such that
-\partial_{x}(V-W)>0
on
U
.
By Proposition
4.2
, there exists
\tau_{0}>0
such that
u_{t}(1)\in U
for almost every
t\in[0,\tau_{0}]
.
Consequently, the inequality

\partial_{x}W(u_{t}(1))-\partial_{x}V(u_{t}(1))>0\text{ for }t\in[0,\tau_{0}]

is satisfied.
Applying Proposition
4.12
, it further follows that, for every
\tau\in(0,\tau_{0})
there exists
A\subset(0,\tau)
such that
S>0
for almost every
t\in A
.
In particular, Proposition
4.12
is valid in the regime
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{3},1)
since the result:
[
58
, Theorem 1.3]
shows that vanishing viscosity solutions with
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
initial datum are bounded in
L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R})
.

By applying Proposition
4.13
together with Inequality (
1.14
), we derive the positivity of the relative entropy for almost every
\tau\in(0,\tau_{0})
.

\displaystyle\Phi[\rho(\tau),\mu(\tau)]

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{\tau}\langle q(s),\partial_{x}V-\partial_{x}W\rangle\mathop{}\mathrm{d}s=-\int_{0}^{\tau}\partial_{x}(V(u_{s}(1))-W(u_{s}(1)))S(u_{s}(1))\mathop{}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\geq-\int_{A}\partial_{x}(V(u_{s}(1))-W(u_{s}(1)))S(u_{s}(1))\mathop{}\mathrm{d}s>0.

.
However, the relative entropy of a segregated solution must be zero for all time.
Consequently, the segregated solution can not coincide with the vanishing viscosity solution and there exist at least two distinct solutions to System (
