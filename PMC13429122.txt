Neuroprotective Delivery of Dexmedetomidine Liposomes Mitigates Cerebral Energy Crisis by Modulating Calcium-Driven Aerobic Glycolysis in Cerebral Ischemia-Reperfusion Injury
Authors: Shan Wang, Yiling Lian, Tingting Wu, Yuan Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionCerebral ischemia-reperfusion injury (CIRI) involves complex pathogenesis with limited therapeutic options. This study investigated whether dexmedetomidine liposomes (Dex-lip) exert neuroprotective effects by modulating astrocytic aerobic glycolysis via calcium signaling.MethodsIn vivo CIRI model mice received Dex-lip, with or without the aerobic glycolysis inhibitor 2-deoxy-D-glucose (2-DG). Neurological function, cortical calcium levels, aerobic glycolysis-related protein expression, lactate production, and hippocampal neuronal damage were assessed. In vitro, astrocytes were treated with the calcium channel blocker nifedipine to evaluate intracellular calcium, lactate levels, and glycolysis-related gene expression.ResultsDex-lip treatment reduced cortical calcium overload, upregulated aerobic glycolysis key proteins, increased lactate production, and attenuated hippocampal neuronal injury. These neuroprotective effects were abolished by 2-DG. In vitro, nifedipine lowered astrocytic calcium concentration, which paradoxically increased lactate production, elevated ECAR, and upregulated HK1, HK2, and PFK1 mRNA expression, confirming that calcium reduction promotes aerobic glycolysis.ConclusionDex-lip ameliorates CIRI by reducing astrocytic calcium overload, enhancing aerobic glycolysis and lactate supply to neurons, thereby preventing apoptosis. This study provides a mechanistic basis and therapeutic strategy for CIRI.Keywords: cerebral ischemia-reperfusion injury, dexmedetomidine liposomes, aerobic glycolysis, calcium ions, astrocytes

Introduction

Cerebral ischemia-reperfusion injury (CIRI) stands as one of the most devastating perioperative complications, with an incidence rate of 4.8–9.7% in high-risk surgical populations, significantly contributing to poor clinical outcomes and elevated mortality.1 According to World Health Organization (WHO) reports, approximately 5.7 million individuals worldwide suffer disability or death annually due to CIRI-related pathologies, imposing a substantial socioeconomic burden on patients, families, and healthcare systems.2 These realities underscore the translational imperative to develop mechanism-driven therapeutic strategies targeting CIRI pathogenesis, particularly agents capable of modulating calcium dysregulation and metabolic reprogramming in neuroglial cells.

Dexmedetomidine, a highly selective α2-adrenergic receptor agonist, exerts neuroprotective effects against CIRI through multiple mechanisms, including downregulation of TLR4 expression,3 activation of PINK1/Parkin-mediated mitophagy,4 modulation of autophagy via the PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway.5 Despite these promising effects, dexmedetomidine’s clinical application is limited by poor BBB permeability and systemic side effects, necessitating targeted drug delivery strategies. In the research on the treatment of CIRI, targeted nanomedicine has emerged as a novel therapeutic approach, offering advantages such as enhanced drug targeting and reduced side effects. Studies have shown that nanomaterial-based targeted drug delivery systems can effectively improve neurological deficits following CIRI.6,7 However, further investigation is still needed to elucidate the key mechanisms by which targeted nanomedicine regulates CIRI.

CIRI is a complex pathophysiological process whose underlying mechanisms remain incompletely understood. Studies suggest that multiple pathological factors, including oxidative stress, neuronal apoptosis, metabolic dysfunction, and calcium overload, contribute to CIRI.8–10 Among these, neuronal apoptosis is a critical determinant of neurological deficits and represents a key pathological event in CIRI.11,12 Notably, calcium overload (Ca2⁺ overload) has been widely recognized as a pivotal trigger of neuronal apoptosis, initiating a cascade of downstream effects such as caspase activation, impaired ATP synthesis, and activation of calcium-dependent proteases and endonucleases.13 Given its central role in neuronal death, strategies targeting Ca2⁺ overload mitigation have emerged as a major research focus in CIRI treatment.

Importantly, a growing body of evidence has established a critical but underexplored link between calcium signaling and glycolysis regulation in CIRI. Calcium directly modulates key glycolytic enzymes: Ca2+-dependent kinases (CaMKII) can phosphorylate and activate phosphofructokinase-2 (PFK-2), thereby promoting glycolytic flux. Conversely, sustained calcium overload inhibits pyruvate dehydrogenase (PDH) via Ca2+-sensitive phosphatases, shifting metabolism away from oxidative phosphorylation. However, to date, no study has systematically investigated how calcium-dependent glycolytic regulation affects astrocyte-to-neuron lactate shuttling in the context of CIRI. This knowledge gap forms a central rationale for the present study.

A growing body of evidence suggests that astrocytes play a pivotal role in Ca2+-mediated CIRI. During cerebral ischemia-reperfusion, Ca2+ overload not only impairs neuronal function but also significantly influences astrocytic activity. Astrocytes possess unique calcium signaling regulatory mechanisms, and their intracellular calcium homeostasis is crucial for maintaining normal cellular functions and neuron-astrocyte interactions. Under ischemia-reperfusion conditions, Ca2+ overload can disrupt astrocytic calcium homeostasis, thereby impairing their physiological functions, including synaptic regulation and ionic balance maintenance. These astrocytic dysfunctions may subsequently exacerbate neuronal damage, creating a vicious cycle of injury.14–16 Consequently, in-depth investigation of the relationship between Ca2+ overload and astrocyte dysfunction is of fundamental importance for understanding the pathogenesis of CIRI.

Notably, dexmedetomidine directly affects astrocytes. Recent studies confirmed that α2A receptor activation elevates astrocytic calcium, increases bestrophin-1 channel permeability, and promotes GABA release to regulate neuronal excitability.17 Astrocytes belong to neuroglial cells and are the most numerous cell type in the mammalian nervous system.18–20 They play important roles in regulating synaptic activity, remodeling neuronal structure, maintaining ionic homeostasis, and nutritional metabolism.21 Studies have found that in pathological processes such as CIRI and other brain injuries, the functional deficits of astrocytes can directly affect the survival and plasticity of neurons.22 It has been reported that by sorting out protective astrocytes and co-culturing them with neurons, neurons can be protected against CIRI and neuronal apoptosis can be reduced.23

The energy metabolic mechanism of the brain nervous system has currently attracted extensive attention, especially the transfer of lactate, an energy source, between neurons and astrocytes. Astrocytes use lactate produced through the aerobic glycolysis pathway to supply energy to neurons.24 Astrocytes selectively and highly express lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), which converts pyruvate into lactate.25 It is precisely based on the differential expression of these enzymes in astrocytes and their strongly reduced state that enables astrocytes to convert glycogen or glucose into lactate even under normal oxygen partial pressure, that is, aerobic glycolysis.

Regarding dexmedetomidine and glycolysis, existing studies have focused on its inhibitory effects in other cell types. In LPS-stimulated macrophages, dexmedetomidine suppresses HIF1α-dependent glycolysis, decreasing ECAR, glucose consumption, and lactate production.26 Similarly, in myocardial ischemia-reperfusion injury, dexmedetomidine inhibits glycolysis and reduces lactate levels to alleviate cardiac damage.27 However, whether dexmedetomidine exerts a similar effect on astrocytic aerobic glycolysis remains unclear. Unlike macrophages or cardiomyocytes, astrocytes constitutively produce lactate via aerobic glycolysis to fuel neurons—a specialized metabolic adaptation essential for neuronal survival. Therefore, the effect of dexmedetomidine on astrocytic aerobic glycolysis may differ fundamentally due to distinct metabolic demands and signaling contexts.

In the brain, neurons mainly rely on lactate produced by astrocytes through aerobic glycolysis to meet their energy needs. Some studies have shown that when the excitability of neurons increases and they are in an active state, neurons will preferentially use lactate for energy supply.28 The glutamate released by neurons can enter astrocytes, driving glycogenolysis and thus promoting astrocytes to produce lactate through aerobic glycolysis to meet the energy metabolic needs of neurons.29,30 The production of lactate in aerobic glycolysis is an important signal for neurotransmission and memory processing. As an energy substrate, lactate shuttles from astrocytes to neurons and has a neuroprotective effect.31 In the case of an energy crisis, astrocytes can up-regulate aerobic glycolysis to increase lactate production. Lactate can be transported to neurons as an energy substrate to relieve the energy deficiency of neurons.32 Once the aerobic glycolysis function in astrocytes is impaired, the production and transportation of lactate between astrocytes and neurons cannot proceed normally, ultimately inducing neuronal apoptosis due to energy depletion and further triggering brain functional disorders.

Therefore, in this project, dexmedetomidine liposomes (Dex-lip) are designed to target the astrocytes in the cerebral cortex of rats with CIRI models. This approach aims to reduce the intracellular calcium ion content, promote the metabolic activity of aerobic glycolysis and lactate production in astrocytes, provide energy support for neurons, and inhibit neuronal apoptosis (Scheme 1). This research provides a theoretical basis for further clarifying the pathogenesis of CIRI and offers new ideas for the specific targeted treatment of CIRI.

The protective effects of Dex-lip against CIRI are mediated through: (1) alleviating calcium overload in astrocytes; (2) promoting aerobic glycolysis and lactate production; (3) providing metabolic support to neurons and inhibiting apoptosis. All experiments were conducted in accordance with the guidelines approved by the Ethics Committee of Hainan Medical University (HMUIRB202007).

Methods

Preparation of Dex-Lip

A homogeneous lipid solution was prepared by dissolving phospholipids, cholesterol, Mannose-PEG2000-DSPE, and dexmedetomidine in anhydrous ethanol at a molar ratio of 5:1:1:0.8. The aqueous phase consisted of PBS buffer containing 9 mg/mL NaCl, which was placed in a vessel under magnetic stirring at 60°C. The lipid-ethanol solution was then injected into the aqueous phase using a syringe at a constant rate of 1–2 mL/min. Upon rapid diffusion of ethanol into the aqueous phase, the lipids and dexmedetomidine co-assembled to form drug-loaded liposomes in a one-step process. Ethanol was subsequently removed from the system by rotary evaporation under reduced pressure. The resulting crude liposome suspension was extruded multiple times through a polycarbonate membrane with a defined pore size (100 nm) to obtain liposomes of uniform size. Unencapsulated dexmedetomidine was separated and removed by gel exclusion chromatography. The purified liposome suspension, designated as Mannose-PEG2000-DSPE-modified Dex-lip, was collected for further use.

Construction of CIRI Model

After the mice were anesthetized, the fur on the neck was shaved and disinfected. A midline incision of about 1 cm in length was made on the neck to expose the external carotid artery, internal carotid artery, and common carotid artery. The external carotid artery was isolated and ligated. A silicone suture was carefully inserted through the incision of the external carotid artery to block the blood flow of the middle cerebral artery. After 90 minutes of ischemia, the suture was removed to restore the cerebral blood flow perfusion.

All mice were divided into the following groups: ①Healthy mice (Control); ② Model mice (CIRI); ③ Dexmedetomidine liposome group (Dex-lip): CIRI model mice were given Dex-lip at a dose of 20 mg/kg; ④Dexmedetomidine liposome + aerobic glycolysis inhibitor group (2-DG+Dex-lip): CIRI model mice were first given 2-DG (20 mg/kg), and 12 hours later, they were intervened with Dex-lip. All drugs were injected via the tail vein once a day for 7 days.

All surgical procedures were performed under isoflurane anesthesia (induction at 4%, maintenance at 1.5–2% in 100% oxygen). During the surgery, body temperature was maintained at 37.0±0.5°C using a heating pad. At the end of the experiment, mice were euthanized by cervical dislocation under deep isoflurane anesthesia, which is consistent with the recommendations of the American Veterinary Medical Association (AVMA) Guidelines for the Euthanasia of Animals (2020 edition). All efforts were made to minimize the number of animals used and their suffering.

TTC Staining

After anesthetizing the mice in each group, their heads were cut off to remove the brains. The removed brain tissues were placed in a −20°C freezer for 10 minutes. Then, the frozen mouse brains were placed on a clean Petri dish. Starting from the front side of the brain tissue, slices were cut successively backward with a thickness of 2 mm. These slices were immediately put into the prepared 2% TTC staining solution and incubated in a constant - temperature incubator at 37°C in the dark for 20 minutes. The stained slices were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde at 4°C for 12 hours and photographed with a digital camera. The Sigma-Scan software was used to quantitatively analyze the size of the infarct area in the white part of the mouse brain tissue.

Neurological Function Scoring

All neurobehavioral observations were conducted at a fixed time and in a fixed environment. According to the modified Garcia score, the degree of neurological function impairment in mice was evaluated from six aspects: spontaneous movement, postural symmetry, forelimb extension function, climbing movement, bilateral tactile sensation of the body, and bilateral whisker touch response. The scoring range was 0–5 points. The higher the score, the more severe the neurological function impairment, and a score of 0 indicated normal neurological function.

TUNEL Fluorescence Staining

Brain tissues from mice in each group were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde solution, followed by dehydration, clearing, paraffin infiltration, and embedding. Coronal sections of 5 μm thickness were prepared. After dewaxing and rehydration, the sections were subjected to TUNEL fluorescence staining according to the manufacturer’s protocol. The stained sections were then observed and imaged under a fluorescence microscope.

Morris Water Maze Test

The Morris water maze test consists of two parts: the place navigation test and the platform-crossing test. Before the experiment, the water maze was evenly divided into four quadrants with different markings, and the platform was placed in the second quadrant. Clear water was poured into the maze so that the water surface was 2–3 cm above the platform. (1) Place navigation test: For 5 consecutive days, mice in each group were placed into the water facing the pool wall in sequence according to the quadrant order. The time taken for the mice to find the platform within 60s, which is the escape latency, was recorded. If a mouse failed to find the platform within 60s, it was guided to the platform and allowed to stay there for 20s before being removed, and the latency was recorded as 60s to help the mice get familiar with the environment and the task. (2) Spatial exploration test: On the 6th day, the platform was removed. Mice were placed into the water from a quadrant far away from the original platform position. The number of times the mice crossed the original platform position within 60s was recorded to evaluate their spatial memory and cognitive abilities.

Detection of Lactate Content and Proteins Related to Aerobic Glycolysis

The cerebral cortices of mice in each group were collected and placed on ice. The lactate content in each tissue was detected according to the instructions of the lactate assay kit. Additionally, proteins were extracted from the cortices, and the concentration of the protein samples was quantified using the BCA kit. Protein expression was detected by Western blot. After preparing the gel, the samples were loaded. The proteins were completely separated by electrophoresis at 80 V, and then transferred to a 0.22 µm PVDF membrane at a current of 250 mA. After blocking, the PVDF membrane was incubated with primary antibodies against HK1, PFKP, PKM2, LDHA, Calmodulin, calbindin, and tubulin overnight at 4°C. The next day, the membrane was washed three times with TBST buffer (10 min each time). Then it was incubated with the secondary antibody for 2 h at room temperature. After that, the membrane was washed three times with TBST buffer (10 min each time). Finally, the membrane was developed using ECL chemiluminescent working reagent. The protein bands were observed and captured using an imaging system, and the relative protein expression levels were detected using Image J software.

Detection of Neuronal Damage by Nissl and NeuN Staining

The paraffin-embedded brain sections of mice in each group were dewaxed in xylene solutions with different concentration gradients. For rehydration, the sections were processed in a decreasing order of ethanol concentration. First, the sections were immersed in 100% ethanol for 5 minutes, then in 95%, 90%, 80%, and 70% ethanol for 3 minutes each in sequence. Finally, the sections were rinsed with distilled water 2–3 times, 2 minutes each time, to wash away the residual ethanol. Subsequently, the sections were immersed in Nissl staining solution at 56°C for 1 hour. After differentiation with the differentiating solution, the sections were dehydrated with absolute ethanol, cleared with xylene, and mounted with neutral gum. Finally, the morphological structure of neurons was observed under a microscope.

The paraffin-embedded brain sections of mice in each group were successively placed in xylene and gradient alcohols, and finally rinsed with PBS. The slides were immersed in antigen retrieval solution and subjected to microwave or high-pressure antigen retrieval. After cooling, the slides were rinsed with PBS. Blocking serum was dropped onto the slides and incubated at room temperature for 20–30 minutes. Then the serum was shaken off without washing. The primary antibody against NeuN was dropped onto the slides and incubated overnight at 4°C or for 1.5 hours at 37°C, followed by PBS rinsing. The secondary antibody was dropped onto the slides and incubated at room temperature for 40 minutes, and then the slides were rinsed with PBS. DAB chromogenic solution was dropped onto the slides, and the slides were mounted with neutral gum. The results were observed under a microscope and photographed for record.

Transcriptome Sequencing

After collecting the cerebral cortex tissues from mice in each group, the tissues were quickly cooled on ice and then placed into centrifuge tubes that had been treated to be RNase-free. The samples were immediately snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen and then transferred to a −80°C freezer for storage. RNA was extracted from the mouse cerebral cortex tissues using Trizol reagent according to the instructions. The concentration of the extracted RNA was detected using a NanoDrop spectrophotometer. Samples with good RNA purity were selected according to relevant standards to provide high - quality RNA samples for subsequent sequencing. The sequencing of the samples was entrusted to Shanghai Bosenhao Company.

RT-PCR

The primers were designed and synthesized by Guangzhou Ribobio Co., Ltd. The primer sequences are shown in Table 1. Total RNA was extracted from the cerebral cortex of each group using the conventional Trizol method. Reverse transcription was carried out according to the instructions of the RNA reverse transcription kit under the following conditions: 15 min at 37°C, 5 s at 85°C, and then held at 4°C. After reverse transcription, the operation was performed according to the instructions of the SYBR Green Real - time PCR kit under the following conditions: pre-denaturation at 95°C for 30s, denaturation at 95°C for 5 s, and annealing at 60°C for 34s. Steps 2 and 3 were repeated for 40 cycles. The mRNA expression level was calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method.

Isolation, Purification and Culture of Primary Astrocytes

Newborn mice within 24 hours after birth were taken. Their heads were cut off under aseptic conditions, and the brains were removed and placed in a petri dish containing dissection solution. The meninges were peeled off, and the cerebral cortex was taken and minced. Then it was digested with 1 mL of 2.5% trypsin for 5 minutes. The digestion was stopped with complete culture medium. After passing through a sieve, the tissue fluid was collected and centrifuged (1600 rpm, 4°C, 5 minutes). The supernatant was discarded, and the cells were resuspended in fresh culture medium. The cells were seeded into plastic culture flasks (5 mL per flask) that had been soaked in 0.001% poly-lysine and cultured in an incubator at 37°C with 5% CO2. The culture medium was completely replaced after 24 hours. Thereafter, half of the culture medium was replaced every 2–3 days. On the 8th day of culture, the culture medium was completely replaced. After incubating for 2–3 hours, the flasks were placed on a constant-temperature shaker at 37°C and shaken at 240 rpm for 18 hours. The culture medium was discarded, and the cells were digested and the cell suspension was collected. The suspension was centrifuged at 800 rpm and 4°C for 5 minutes. The supernatant was discarded, and the cells were resuspended for subsequent experiments.

Detection of Intracellular Calcium Ion Concentration

The astrocytes cultured for one week were divided into three groups: the normal group, the calcium inhibition group, and the Dex-lip group. Each group of cells was incubated with the working solution of Fluo-4 AM (0.5–5 μM) at 37°C for 30–60 minutes for fluorescent probe loading. The cells were washed three times with PBS and then incubated for another 20–30 minutes to ensure that Fluo-4 AM was completely converted into Fluo-4 inside the cells. The fluorescence of Fluo-4 was detected using a 3D NANOLIVE (STEVE 3D Cell Explorer) to determine the changes in intracellular calcium ion concentration.

Lactate Content

For astrocytes and astrocytes treated with the calcium ion inhibitor nifedipine, after 24 hours of culture, the supernatant was collected to detect the lactate content.

Determination of Aerobic Glycolysis Activity

The extracellular acidification rate (ECAR) is a parameter for aerobic glycolysis flux and activity. According to the instruction manual, the ECAR level in the cells after drug administration was measured by the Seahorse XF96e bio - analyzer using the XF aerobic glycolysis stress test kit (Santa Clara, CA, USA). The Seahorse cellular energy metabolism analyzer was used to detect and analyze the aerobic glycolysis capacity and aerobic glycolysis reserve of the cells. The glucose content and lactate content in the culture medium were detected using test kits respectively, and the glucose utilization and lactate production of the cells were calculated.

Data Analysis

Statistical analysis was carried out using GraphPad Prism software (version 9.4.0, San Diego, CA, USA). The data were presented in the form of the mean±SD. To analyze the differences between the two groups, t-tests and non-parametric tests were utilized. Statistical significance was defined as a P value of less than 0.05.

Results

Characterization of Dex-Lip

Mannose-PEG2000-DSPE, a modifier with targeting function, was used to modify liposomes. Meanwhile, dexmedetomidine was encapsulated into liposomes via the one-step ethanol injection method, as shown in the schematic diagram (Figure 1A). Transmission electron microscopy showed that Dex-lip presented a regular spherical morphology (Figure 1B). The average particle size of Dex-lip was 137.90±16.31 nm. This particle size was within an ideal range, which could ensure good fluidity of the liposomes in vivo. The average polydispersity index (PDI) of Dex-lip was 0.137±0.068, indicating that the particle size distribution of the liposomes was relatively concentrated and had good uniformity. The average potential of Dex-lip was 7.62±4.60 mV (Figure 1C and D).

Therapeutic Effect of Dex-Lip on CIRI

Mice with successfully established CIRI models were treated with Dex-lip, as shown in the schematic diagram in Figure 3A. Triphenyltetrazolium chloride (TTC) staining revealed that normal brain tissue appeared uniformly red after staining, whereas a large white damaged area was observed in the brains of CIRI mice, indicating severe brain tissue damage in the CIRI model. Compared with the CIRI group, TTC staining of the Dex-lip treatment group showed a significantly reduced damaged area, suggesting that Dex-lip had a certain protective effect against CIRI, as presented in Figure 3B. The damaged area accounted for 56.53±5.95% of the total brain slice area in the CIRI group, 25.67±3.25% in the Dex-lip group, and 44.00±5.65% in the 2-DG+Dex-lip group, as shown in Figure 3C. Neurological scores were 3.71±0.49 in the CIRI group, 1.14±0.69 in the Dex-lip group, and 3.14±0.69 in the 2-DG+Dex-lip group, as shown in Figure 3D.

In fluorescence-stained sections, apoptotic neurons were scarce in the normal group, whereas the model group displayed prominent neuronal apoptosis with intense fluorescence signals. Treatment with Dex-lip significantly attenuated neuronal apoptosis in the CIRI group. By contrast, co-administration of 2-DG with Dex-lip failed to produce an obvious reduction in neuronal apoptosis, as presented in Figure 3E.

Neurological Function Evaluation

The Place navigation test is mainly used to evaluate the spatial learning ability of mice. As shown in Figure 4A, when searching for the platform, the search behavior of CIRI mice was aimless and random. In contrast, the search behavior of control mice showed certain tendency and purpose. Some mice even swam directly to the platform based on their memory. The same phenomenon was observed in the Dex-lip group. In the place navigation test, during the consecutive days of the experiment, as the number of training sessions increased, the escape latency of normal mice gradually shortened, indicating that their spatial learning ability was continuously improving. However, due to brain damage, CIRI mice had prolonged escape latency, and the shortening trend was not obvious or was rather slow, suggesting that their spatial learning ability was impaired. After Dex-lip intervention, the learning ability of CIRI mice was improved, as shown in Figure 4C. The results of the Spatial exploration test showed that control mice swam at or near the center (the original platform position) and continuously crossed the platform point. Compared with the control group, the swimming paths of CIRI model mice showed poor purpose, and the number of times the mice crossed the platform was significantly reduced, with a significant difference. Compared with CIRI mice, the number of times the mice in the Dex-lip group crossed the platform increased significantly, while there was no difference in the 2-DG+Dex-lip group, as shown in Figure 4B and D.

In the cerebral cortex of normal mice, hexokinase 1 (HK1), lactate dehydrogenase A (LDHA), pyruvate kinase M2 (PKM2), and phosphofructokinase (PFKP) were highly expressed, whereas their expression levels were low in the cerebral cortex of CIRI mice. Following treatment with Dex-lip, the expression of these aerobic glycolysis-related enzymes increased, as shown in Figure 5B.

Calmodulin (CaM) is a calcium-binding protein widely present in cells. It can bind to Ca2⁺ to form a Ca2⁺-CaM complex, which in turn activates various downstream signaling molecules. The expression of Calmodulin protein is relatively high in the normal group, but relatively low in the CIRI group, resulting in an increase in the intracellular Ca2⁺concentration and an imbalance in Ca2⁺content in CIRI. However, after treatment with Dex-lip, the expression of Calmodulin-protein decreased. When intervened with 2-DG, the effect of Dex-lip was weakened, as shown in Figure 5C. Calbindin (Cab) is a neuron-specific calcium-binding protein that can buffer the intracellular Ca2⁺ concentration and protect neurons from damage caused by Ca2⁺ overload. In this study, no difference was found in the expression of Cab protein in the cerebral cortex of each group. Additionally, no difference was found in the expression of CaM protein in the cortex of each group.

Neuronal Damage

Neuronal injury and death are the core pathological changes of CIRI. Nissl staining can show the Nissl bodies in neurons and reflect the functional state of neurons. NeuN staining is a staining method for neuron-specific nuclear proteins and can be used to label mature neurons. Both were of great value in evaluating neuronal damage in CIRI. In the Nissl-stained images of normal brain tissue sections, neurons had a complete morphology, being polygonal or circular. Nissl bodies were evenly distributed in the neuronal cytoplasm, appearing as dark-blue or purple-blue granular structures with clear staining. However, significant changes occurred in the Nissl-staining results of the brain ischemia-reperfusion injury area. Neuronal swelling could be observed, and the Nissl bodies start to dissolve, manifested as a decrease in the number of Nissl body granules, a lighter color, and uneven staining. After intervention with Dex-lip, the morphology of Nissl bodies was restored, the number increased, and the staining became uniform. Pretreatment with 2-DG could effectively reduce the protective effect of Dex-lip on neuronal injury, as shown in Figure 6A.

Expression of Genes Related to Aerobic Glycolysis and Calcium Ions

HK1 and HK2 are the first key enzymes in the aerobic glycolysis pathway. They catalyze the phosphorylation of glucose to form glucose-6-phosphate, enabling glucose to enter the subsequent aerobic glycolysis reactions. This is one of the rate-limiting steps of aerobic glycolysis. PFK1 is a key rate-limiting enzyme in the process of aerobic glycolysis, which catalyzes the phosphorylation of fructose-6-phosphate to form fructose-1,6-bisphosphate. HK1, HK2, and PFK1 are crucial for maintaining cellular energy metabolism. The results showed that the mRNA expression levels of HK1, HK2, and PFK1 in the CIRI group were significantly lower than those in the normal group. After intervention with Dex-lip, the mRNA expression levels increased significantly. Calcium Ions are Involved in the Regulation of Aerobic Glycolysis

In normal astrocytes, the fluorescence intensity of calcium ions increases, indicating an increase in calcium ion concentration. After using the calcium channel inhibitor Nifedipine, the fluorescence intensity of intracellular calcium ions decreases, suggesting a reduction in calcium ion concentration. However, after astrocytes were treated with Dex-lip for 24 hours, the fluorescence intensity of intracellular calcium ions increased significantly, as shown in Figure 9A. The schematic diagram of astrocytes after Nifedipine treatment is shown in Figure 9B. We found that compared with the lactate content in the normal group (3.28±0.15 mmol/mL), the lactate content increased significantly to 5.99±0.15 mmol/mL after Nifedipine treatment, indicating that inhibiting calcium ion concentration can increase aerobic glycolysis, as shown in Figure 9C. The extracellular acidification rate (ECAR) is a parameter for the flux and activity of aerobic glycolysis. Compared with normal astrocytes, the ECAR of astrocytes treated with Nifedipine was at a higher level. During the entire detection process, the basal ECAR value of normal cells reached 32.11±2.12 mpH/min. After Nifedipine treatment, the ECAR increased rapidly, with a peak value of up to 50.51±6.51 mpH/min, indicating that astrocytes in the Nifedipine group had an active aerobic glycolysis flux and high aerobic glycolysis activity, as shown in Figure 9D. Compared with the normal group, there was also a significant difference in the oxygen consumption rate (OCR) of astrocytes in the Nifedipine group. It is speculated that this effect may be related to the enhanced aerobic glycolysis function after calcium ion inhibition, as shown in Figure 9E.

Discussion

This study focuses on the ameliorative effect of Dex-lip on CIRI. From the perspective of pharmaceutics, the characteristics of Dex-lip in this study show certain similarities and unique features compared with the liposomal drug preparations reported in relevant literature. Mannose-PEG2000-DSPE was used as a targeting functional modifier to modify the liposomes, aiming to enhance the targeting ability of the liposomes and enable them to reach the lesion site more precisely.33–35 The Dex-lip prepared in this study presents a regular spherical morphology. The average particle size, PDI, and average potential are all within an ideal range. These characteristics ensure good fluidity, uniformity, and stability of Dex-lip in vivo. Compared with the single dexmedetomidine drug, the release time of Dex-lip is prolonged. This sustained-release property helps maintain the effective concentration of the drug in the body and improves the therapeutic effect.

The ability of Dex-lip to cross the BBB is primarily attributed to its mannose-modified surface, which engages receptor-mediated transcytosis through mannose receptors on brain endothelial cells. This mechanism is consistent with previous reports on mannosylated nanocarriers for CNS delivery.33 Additionally, the mildly positive zeta potential (7.62±4.60 mV) of Dex-lip may contribute to electrostatic interactions with the negatively charged luminal surface of BBB endothelial cells, further enhancing its brain permeability via adsorptive-mediated transcytosis.

In vivo experiments found from the results of TTC staining, neurological scoring, and infarct area that Dex-lip has a significant protective effect on CIRI, reducing the necrosis and apoptosis of neurons. The Morris water maze test (including the place navigation test and the spatial probe test) further confirmed the improvement effect of Dex-lip on the neurological function of CIRI mice from the perspective of behavioral science. However, the effect of the 2-DG+Dex-lip group was not as good as that of the pure Dex-lip group. 2-DG is a glucose analog that can inhibit the glycolysis process, indicating that the inhibition of aerobic glycolysis may interfere with the protective effect of Dex-lip on CIRI to a certain extent. Similarly, when detecting the lactate content and the expression of aerobic glycolysis-related enzymes in the cerebral cortex of CIRI mice, it was found that they were significantly lower than those in normal mice. This can be explained by the fact that astrocytes in the cerebral cortex are the main sites of aerobic glycolysis, and astrocytes use the lactate produced by the glycolytic pathway to supply energy for neurons.36 The CIRI group showed a decrease in lactate content and aerobic glycolysis-related enzymes, indicating abnormal aerobic glycolysis in astrocytes in the CIRI cortex, while Dex-lip can improve the abnormal aerobic glycolysis function of astrocytes after CIRI and increase lactate production.

Our transcriptomic analysis (RNA-seq, Figure 7) further elucidated the molecular basis of these observations. Specifically, CIRI induced significant downregulation of HK1, HK2, and PFK1 (Figure 7C), which are rate-limiting enzymes of the glycolytic pathway. KEGG pathway enrichment analysis revealed that differentially expressed genes were significantly enriched in calcium signaling and carbohydrate metabolism pathways (Figures 7B and D). Dex-lip treatment reversed the downregulation of HK1, HK2, and PFK1 at both the mRNA (Figure 8) and protein (Figure 5B) levels, indicating that Dex-lip acts at the transcriptional level to restore astrocytic glycolytic capacity in the ischemic cortex.

Astrocytes are a type of neuroglial cells, which play crucial roles in regulating synaptic activities, reshaping neuronal structures, maintaining ionic homeostasis, and supporting nutritional metabolism.21 Specifically, astrocytes are involved in the metabolic coupling between neurons and astrocytes. They supply neurons with lactate, an essential energy-yielding substance, to ensure the normal functioning of neurons.37 Previous studies have shown that when neurons are in an active state with increased excitability, they preferentially utilize lactate for energy. The glutamate released by active neurons can enter astrocytes, triggering glycogen breakdown. This process promotes astrocytes to produce lactate through glycolysis, thereby meeting the energy metabolic demands of neurons.28–30 It has been found that during pathological processes such as cerebral ischemia - reperfusion injury and other forms of brain damage, the functional impairment of astrocytes can directly affect the survival and plasticity of neurons.22,38 Once the glycolytic function in astrocytes is disrupted, the normal production and transport of lactate between astrocytes and neurons are hindered. Eventually, this leads to energy depletion in neurons, inducing their apoptosis and further causing brain functional disorders. Reports indicate that by isolating protective astrocytes and co-culturing them with neurons, neurons can be protected against CIRI, and neuronal apoptosis can be reduced.23 In this experiment, Nissl and NeuN staining in the CIRI group clearly showed severe neuronal apoptosis. However, after the administration of Dex-lip, the aerobic glycolytic function was restored, the lactate content increased, and neuronal apoptosis was decreased. Unlike other interventions that target astrocytic glycolysis-such as AMPK activators or HIF-1α stabilizers—our approach via calcium regulation offers the advantage of simultaneously addressing calcium overload, a well-established trigger of neuronal apoptosis, thereby providing a dual therapeutic benefit.

We isolated primary astrocytes and treated them with the calcium ion inhibitor Nifedipine. Subsequently, a significant increase in the intracellular lactate content and aerobic glycolysis function of astrocytes was observed. This indicates that calcium ions are involved in the regulation of aerobic glycolysis. Calcium ions (Ca2+) serve as highly versatile intracellular signals capable of modulating various neural functions.39,40 Excessive Ca2+ can lead to neuronal apoptosis, thereby inducing neurological diseases. In this study, we found that the Ca2+ content increased and severe neuronal apoptosis occurred in the CIRI group. Our transcriptomic and qPCR results revealed that CIRI led to a significant increase in CalM1 (calmodulin) mRNA expression (Figure 8), consistent with intracellular calcium overload. Dex-lip treatment reversed this increase, suggesting that Dex-lip restores calcium homeostasis by downregulating calmodulin expression. Calmodulin (CaM) is a calcium-binding protein closely associated with intracellular calcium homeostasis. Our research revealed that CIRI led to a decrease in the expression of CaM protein, resulting in an increase and imbalance of intracellular Ca2+ concentration. However, after treatment with Dex-lip, the decrease in CaM protein expression was alleviated. This suggests that Dex-lip may maintain intracellular calcium homeostasis by regulating the expression of calcium - binding proteins, thus reducing the damage to neurons caused by Ca2+ overload. Previous studies have shown that calcium homeostasis imbalance is one of the important mechanisms underlying neuronal damage caused by CIRI. Many neuroprotective agents improve calcium homeostasis by regulating calcium channels or calcium-binding proteins.41 Based on the above research, we confirmed that after CIRI, Ca2+ overload in cortical astrocytes affects the glycolysis of astrocytes, leading to a decrease in lactate content and neuronal apoptosis. In contrast, Dex-lip can regulate the calcium ion pathway, promote glycolytic metabolic activity and lactate production in astrocytes, provide energy support for neurons, inhibit neuronal apoptosis, and protect neurons from the damage caused by calcium overload.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, discrepancies between in vivo (calcium reduction) and in vitro (calcium increase) Dex-lip effects on astrocytic calcium require further investigation. Second, direct lactate transfer from astrocytes to neurons was not measured, and the downstream calcium signaling pathways mediating aerobic glycolysis remain to be elucidated.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that mannose-modified dexmedetomidine liposomes (Dex-lip) exert neuroprotective effects in a murine cerebral ischemia-reperfusion injury (CIRI) model, likely by reducing astrocytic calcium overload, upregulating aerobic glycolysis-related enzymes (HK1, HK2, PFKP, LDHA, PKM2), and increasing lactate production, thereby attenuating hippocampal neuronal damage—effects largely reversed by the glycolysis inhibitor 2-DG. However, several limitations should be acknowledged, including the discrepancy between in vivo (calcium reduction) and in vitro (calcium elevation) Dex-lip effects, the lack of direct evidence for astrocyte-to-neuron lactate transfer, and the need for loss-of-function studies to establish causation. The brain-targeting and sustained-release properties of the liposomal formulation are critical for overcoming dexmedetomidine’s poor blood-brain barrier permeability, supporting its translational promise. Collectively, these findings provide a mechanistic basis for calcium-regulated astrocytic glycolysis in CIRI and offer preliminary evidence for Dex-lip as a potential targeted therapeutic, though future studies are warranted to resolve calcium signaling discrepancies, confirm lactate shuttling, identify downstream transcriptional regulators, and evaluate long-term safety and efficacy in larger animal models.
