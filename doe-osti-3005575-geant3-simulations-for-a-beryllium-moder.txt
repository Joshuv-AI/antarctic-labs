 Abstract
 Measurements of neutron-induced reactions, particularly on short-lived
 nuclei, are crucial for nuclear astrophysics and applications. The Neutron
 Target Demonstrator (NTD) aims to address this by using a strong mod-
 erated spallation neutron source coupled with an ion storage ring. This
 work investigates the potential of improving the neutron moderator, a
 key component of the NTD. Using GEANT3 simulations, we compare the
 performance of the existing 80 cm carbon moderator with a new moder-
 ator design, which utilizes beryllium pieces available at LANL to form a
 core surrounded by carbon. Simulations were performed 800 MeV pro-
 tons incident on a tungsten target. The results demonstrate that the
 beryllium-core moderator increases the density of moderated neutrons
 within the central beam pipe by roughly a factor 4 compared to the pure
 carbon moderator. This enhancement makes the beryllium-core design a
 very promising option for future experiments focused on neutron-induced
 measurements in inverse kinematics.

1 Introduction
The Neutron Target Demonstrator (NTD) provides a new opportunity to mea-
sure neutron-induced reactions in inverse kinematics [1]. Measurements of
neutron-induced cross sections are important for understanding the nucleosyn-
thesis of elements beyond iron, as well as for several applications. Current
methods are often limited by the half-life and the corresponding activity of
the target isotopes [2, 3, 4]. This limitations can be overcome by combining
a spallation-driven, moderated neutron source with an ion storage ring, which
allows experiments to be performed in inverse kinematics [5].
 First proof-of-principle experiments have been successfully performed within
the NTD project [6]. These experiments, and their corresponding GEANT-3
simulations [7], have utilized a cubical carbon moderator with an 80 cm side
length.
 A potential path to improving the neutron density is the use of different mod-
erator materials [8]. Beryllium is a prime candidate, as it has been shown to be a
superior moderator to carbon for spherical systems smaller than 2.5 m in diam-
eter and 800 MeV proton energy [8]. This report presents the results of detailed
GEANT3 simulations for a new moderator configuration based on beryllium
(Be) pieces available at the Los Alamos Neutron Science Center (LANSCE),
comparing its performance to the existing carbon moderator.

2 Moderator Geometries and Simulation Setups
The simulations in this work investigate several moderator configurations based
on materials available at LANL. All setups share common features: neutrons are
produced from a tungsten target located at the center of the moderator, and
the analysis focuses on the neutron properties within a central, air-filled ion
beam pipe, which represents the interaction region for future inverse kinematics
experiments.

2.1 Moderator Configurations
The configuration of the moderator used in the experiments so far was a pure
carbon (C) Moderator. It is a cube of approximately 80 cm side length, with a
total volume of 0.481 m3 , which was constructed of of 2 types of carbon pieces
- (76.3 x 10.8 x 10.8) cm3 and (16.8 x 10.8 x 10.8) cm3 (see [1, 6] for details).
 The alternative design is a beryllium-core/carbon (Be+C) moderator. This
design was driven by the inventory of available beryllium pieces at LANSCE,
listed in Table 1, whose total volume (0.151 m3 ) is significantly less than that
of the carbon moderator. To create a large-volume moderator, these Be pieces
were arranged to form a central core (approx. 55 x 55 x 55 cm3 ), which was then
surrounded by 26 of the existing graphite blocks. The assembled moderator has
nearly the same outer dimensions as the pure carbon moderator consisting of 54
carbon pieces. A beryllium density of 1.84 g/cm3 was used in the simulations.

2.2 Simulation Parameters
GEANT3 simulations were performed for the moderator configurations under
different beam conditions, summarized in Table 3. Each simulation was initiated
with 108 primary protons. The neutrons are produced in the center of the setup,
where the tungsten target is positioned and from where they are emitted in all
directions. The dimension of the tungsten target are a cylindrical target with a
radius of 3.5 cm and a length of 25 cm. Further details can be found in Reference
[1]. The moderated neutrons penetrate through the ion pipe, which will be taken
as the reference point of the distribution of the neutrons within the moderator.
The ion pipe will in the future experiments contain the recycling ions, that
interact with the neutron target. In the previous activation experiments the
monitor material has been positioned along the position of the beam pipe. This
stresses the importance of understanding the neutron distribution at exactly this
position. The following comparisons will all show the outcome of the simulation

differences between the different moderator materials become visible. Figure

with a clear highest intensity peak at lower energies. The intensity at high
energies, at which the neutrons get produced seems to be comparable to the
one in the carbon moderator, but with a more defined spread in the position.
Along the Energy axes going down to lower energies the neutrons do not spread
out as much, as within the moderator Carbon moderator, and the majority
stays within a position range of -25 cm to 25 cm. This hints again at the fast
moderation process the neutrons seem to undergo within the Be+C moderator.
The moderated neutrons show a much higher intensity in the Be+C moderator.
Still some of these neutrons escape the moderator material, but the majority is
within its borders. This stresses again the efficiency of the Be+C moderator.
The results achieved with a moderator made out of carbon could be reached
with a Be+C moderator of a smaller size, making the experiment even easier to
conduct at various places.

3.2 Simulated Reaction Yields
To quantify the impact on observables of activation experiments, we simulated
the reaction yields for gold (197 Au), a standard activation monitor. The yield
is defined as reactions per incident proton per target atom. For the full-cube
geometry (Figure 8), the Be+C moderator produces a much higher yield for
the 197 Au(n,γ) reaction, consistent with the enhanced thermal flux. For the
high-energy 197 Au(n,2n) reaction, the yields are almost identical.

 4 Conclusion and Outlook
 The GEANT3 simulations presented in this report strongly suggest that a mod-
 erator with a beryllium core surrounded by carbon or even the beryllium core
 alone is a superior choice for the Neutron Target Demonstrator. In a full-cube
 geometry, the Be-core configuration increases the density of moderated neutrons
 in the interaction region by a factor of approximately 4, which would directly
 translate to higher reaction rates and enable measurements on isotopes with
 smaller cross sections or lower beam intensities. The remarkable gain in ther-
 mal neutron flux makes it a compelling upgrade for the NTD program. A more
 comprehensive study with spherical moderators of different sizes and materials
 including beryllium and carbon can be found in [8].
 Given that previous activation experiments with a carbon moderator have
 shown excellent agreement with these GEANT3 of simulations [6], the next log-
 ical step is to perform an experimental validation. An activation measurement
 using the available beryllium pieces at LANSCE would provide a direct test of
 these findings and pave the way for implementing this enhanced moderator in
 future experiments with stored, radioactive ion beams in inverse kinematics.
