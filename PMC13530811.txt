Poster 49. Osteochondral Allograft Transplantation of the Knee in “Ideal” Candidates: Clinical Outcomes and Graft Survivorship
Authors: William D. Bugbee
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Objective:Osteochondral allograft (OCA) transplantation of the knee is an effective treatment for chondral and osteochondral lesions. Recent literature suggests that the most ideal candidates for OCA transplantation may be young, non-obese patients with a small lesion due to osteochondritis dissecans or chondral trauma. The purpose of this study was to assess outcomes following osteochondral allograft transplantation in a cohort of 'ideal' candidates.Methods:We identified 109 patients (116 knees) who underwent primary OCA transplantation for osteochondritis dissecans or a traumatic chondral injury, age 30 years or younger, non-obese, and had lesion(s) totaling 8 cm2 or less. All patients had a minimum follow-up of two years. Reoperations were assessed and allograft failure was defined as any reoperation that involved removal of the allograft. Patient-reported clinical outcomes of pain, function, and satisfaction were captured.Results:Mean age was 20.1 years and 63.8% were male. Mean total graft area was 5.4 cm2. Following the OCA transplantation, 23 knees (19.8%) underwent a reoperation. One knee (0.9%) was classified as an allograft failure (revision OCA transplantation at 5.2 years). Survivorship was 100% at 5 years and 98.4% at 10 years (Figure 1). At a median follow-up of 5.5 years, pain and function had improved, and 90.1% of patients were satisfied with the results of the OCA transplantation.Conclusions:In this cohort of 'ideal' cartilage repair patients undergoing osteochondral allograft transplantation, graft survivorship and clinical outcomes were excellent, with high satisfaction, pain relief, and functional improvement. Outcomes were equal or superior to other cartilage repair techniques.
