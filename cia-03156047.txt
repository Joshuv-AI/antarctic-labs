Britain Reconsidering Stand for Caribbean Bases Conference

(Trinidad's Premier Eric Williams' increasingly violent
campaign to force the United States to give up its naval base
at Chaguaramas is leading British Foreign and Colonial Office
Officials to reconsider Britain's position favoring a tripartite
conference of the United States, Britain, and the West Indies
Federation to review the US-UK 1941 Caribbean bases agree-
ment.)

(London, which previously sought to placate Williams be-
cause he might eventually become the federal prime minister,
now apparently sees little hope of moderating his position that
"either I break Chaguaramas or Chaguaramas breaks me,"

In an effort to checkmate him, the Colonial Office is contem-
plating calling West Indian leaders including Williams to London
to "read him the riot act,"' according to the American Embassy
in London,

(Williams' obsession with the issue and his most recent
tactic of linking it with alleged US-UK exploitation of West
Indian racial problems have gained him additional local support.
The influential Torchlight, a Grenada newspaper, for instance,
‘has reversed its policy of defending the US position on Chaguara-
mas. Trinidad's opposition party, which is pro-US, is also
showing increasing signs of disintegration,]

(in view of such support and faced with a British effort
to undermine him, Williams may carry out his threat to call
an election on the base question, The American consul general
in Port of Spain fears that an election in the immediate future
would strengthen Williams' position not only in Trinidad but
in the federation as a whole. }

Guatemalan Political Tension Remains High

Guatemalan President Ydigoras appears to have abandoned
his attempt to bridge the deep political rift between the left and
the right. The President has precipitated a bitter public quar-
rel with Mario Mendez Montenegro, leader of the leftist non-
Communist Revolutionary party (PR), in an apparent effort to
undermine Mendez within his own party and possibly to provoke

him into violent action ah would justify strong government
i j rty.

The PR's rapidly growing political strength--as demon-
strated in the Guatemala City election victory on 5 July and an
earlier provincial by-election--indicates that it is likely to be
the strongest single party in the congressional elections late
this year. This has alarmed Guatemalan rightists who have long
been pressing Ydigoras to take strong measures against the
party--even to outlawing it--'before it is too late." There is
strong evidence that the bombings in the capital last month were
initiated by some of Ydigoras' own followers who, by blaming
the bombings on the PR, hoped to justify annulment of the PR
municipal election victory. Subsequent bombings were probably
perpetrated by a rival group in an attempt to force a showdown
between Ydigoras and the PR. An extreme rightist group is re-
ported to be considering plans to oust
Ydigoras if the President does not take sufficiently strong meas-
ures against the PR.

Mario Mendez Montenegro has proven a resourceful, aggres-
sive leader of the leftist forces and was primarily responsible
for purging his party of Communists and. pro-Communists. To the
extent that Ydigoras' tactics weaken Mendez‘ position as PR
leader, they will strengthen those of Mendez’ rivals among the

leftists who are disposed to work with the Communists.
