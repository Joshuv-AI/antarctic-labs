All-optical NV-NV cross-relaxation features for a dense NV ensemble

A diamond sample enriched with !" 𝑁 and having a 3.8 ppm nitrogen-vacancy (NV)
concentration ([NV]) is exposed to a controllable magnetic field. We experimentally determine
the all-optical photoluminescence (AO-PL) contrast of the NV ensemble as a function of on-axis
magnetic field 𝐵∥ (defined as parallel to NVs along [111] symmetry axis) and off-axis magnetic
field 𝐵$ (defined as perpendicular to NVs along both [111] and '111( symmetry axes). The AO-
PL contrast for each on/off-axis magnetic field value (𝐵∥ , 𝐵$ ) is determined by the normalized
difference in intensities between (i) a measured AO-PL signal 𝐼%&' ; and (ii) an AO-PL reference
𝐼()* . 𝐼()* given by the maximum measured AO-PL from a given scan of (𝐵∥ , 𝐵$ ) values in which
the NV orientation classes are spectrally separated. The AO-PL contrast is then calculated as 𝐶 =
1 − 𝐼%&' /𝐼()* .

We vary both 𝐵∥ and 𝐵$ over a range of about 1.5 to –1.5 mT, with step size ≈ 0.02 mT. The
resulting two-dimensional plot of experimental AO-PL contrast is in reasonable agreement with a
numerical simulation based on a two-NV coupled Hamiltonian with a fixed magnetic dipolar
interaction strength between the NVs. See Fig. 1. Differences between the experimental and
simulated magnitudes of AO-PL contrast line features likely arise from the simplicity of the
model used. When the applied magnetic field is near zero (≲ 0.1 mT), spin transitions for NVs
along all four axes are spectrally overlapped, resulting in maximum NV-NV cross-relaxation and
AO-PL contrast. The relatively large magnetic field linewidth measured in AO-PL contrast
features at near-zero-field may be attributed to the influence of local electric and/or magnetic
field and strain gradients. The experimental AO-PL contrast maximizes at 𝐵∥ ≈ −0.02 mT, likely
due to a background magnetic field (e.g., from the Earth and other lab instrumentation).
Additionally, significant AO-PL contrast is observed in both experiment and simulation at
specific polar angles θ from 𝐵∥ , consistent with the effect of cross-relaxation among different NV
orientations resulting from near-degenerate spin transitions. For example, at 𝜃 = 0° (𝐵∥ ≠ 0 and
𝐵$ = 0), the magnetic field is aligned with 𝑛7, and the three non-aligned NV orientations (along
𝑛7- , 𝑛7. and 𝑛7/ ) experience equal magnetic field projections. In the experiment, this configuration
produces a broad, stripe-like feature with large AO-PL contrast (vertical around 𝐵$ = 0) due to
the enhanced cross-relaxation between the three overlapping NV spin resonances.

We next measure AO-PL contrast as a function of 𝐵∥ and 𝐵$ from a diamond sample with ≈ 0.3
ppm NV concentration and a diamond sample with ≈ 3.8 ppm NV concentration; and compare
both sets of results to the numerical simulation. See Fig. 2. Clusters of linear AO-PL contrast
features arising from NV-NV cross-relaxation are experimentally observed near zero-field, 𝜃 =
0° , and 𝜃 = 39.3° for both the low and high NV concentration samples, which have relatively
weak and strong NV-NV dipolar interaction strength, respectively. In addition, there is a non-
negligible AO-PL contrast background, increasing with bias field magnitude, due to the mixing

of NV electronic spin states from magnetic fields that are not aligned with the NV quantization
axis.

Hyperfine interaction and NV-NV cross-relaxation

The experimental and simulation results also display multiple parallel structures within each 𝜃-
dependent AO-PL contrast feature. To investigate the role of the NV hyperfine interaction in this
AO-PL sub-structure, we conduct comparative measurements on diamond samples with the same
NV concentration, enriched with either !" 𝑁 or !0 𝑁. Figure 3 shows AO-PL experimental results
for positive-only values of 𝐵∥ and 𝐵$ , taken with a smaller magnetic field step size (≈ 0.01 mT)
than the results in Figure 1(b). (Similar results are found for the other (𝐵∥ , 𝐵$ ) quadrants.) In
addition, we measure AO-PL contrast at fixed 𝐵∥ = 1.24 mT by scanning 𝐵$ with an even finer
step size (≈ 0.002 mT) to resolve the individual cross-relaxation line shapes more clearly (solid
lines in Figure 3(b)). As 𝐵$ approaches 0 mT, there are overlapping, unresolved AO-PL contrast
peaks due to the increasing number of spin transition degeneracies between different NV
orientations. At 𝐵$ ≈ 1.05 mT, equivalent to 𝜃 ≈ 40° in Figure 3(a), two sets of NV orientations
(along 𝑛7, and 𝑛7- ; 𝑛7. and 𝑛7/ ) are near-degenerate. In this parameter regime, the NV hyperfine
interaction provides additional depolarization channels and splits the NV-NV cross-relaxation
resonances, with associated AO-PL contrast features. In particular, the number of AO-PL contrast
peaks varies between the two samples. The !" 𝑁 spectrum in Figure 3(b, top) shows five peaks
with a separation Δ𝐵!"1 ≈ 0.09 mT between neighboring peaks. The ratio of individual peak
amplitudes follows 1:2:3:2:1, as expected for the !" 𝑁 nuclear spin quantum number 𝐼 = 1.
Similarly, the !0 𝑁 spectrum in Figure 3(b, bottom) displays three peaks with Δ𝐵!01 ≈ 0.13 mT,
and a ratio of individual peak amplitude given by 1:2:1, consistent with 𝐼 = ½ for !0 𝑁.

To further characterize the effects of NV-NV cross-relaxation and the NV hyperfine interaction
on PL features, we perform microwave-based continuous-wave optically detected magnetic
resonance (CW-ODMR) measurements on !0 𝑁 sample as a function of 𝐵$ and for fixed 𝐵∥ =
1.24 mT, with results summarized in Figure 4(c). These microwave-based measurements extract

the spin transition frequencies for all NV orientations and determine the values of 𝐵$ where
these spin transitions become nearly degenerate. In addition, CW-ODMR measurements clearly
resolve small (few MHz) splittings from NV hyperfine interactions. These CW-ODMR
spectroscopic measurements are consistent with the 𝐵$ values and amplitude ratios for the
observed AO-PL LIA features, see dashed vertical lines in Figure 4; and also with the ratio of
AO-PL contrast peak amplitudes for !0 𝑁 sample shown in Figure 3(b, lower right). Comparing
to the AO-PL LIA measurements for 𝐵$ ≈ 0.4 mT and 0.65 mT, the increased number of NVs
with near-degenerate spin transitions at 𝐵$ ≈ 0.92 mT, 1.05 mT, and 1.18 mT contributes to the
larger AO-PL LIA output amplitudes at the larger 𝐵$ values [Figure 4(a)].

Pulsed AO operation for improved noise rejection
The precision of CW AO-PL operation is limited by laser intensity noise, as optical intensity
fluctuations affect the contrast of NV AO-PL features, leading to inaccurate readings of magnetic
fields from AO-PL intensity changes. One can employ lock-in detection by amplitude
modulating magnetic fields (as we presented in the previous section) to improve SNR. However,
this approach of magnetic field modulation adds design complexity for nuclear fusion operations
inside a tokamak. We develop an alternative technique through pulsed optical illumination of the
NV-diamond sample. Figure 6 shows the pulsed AO-PL measurement sequence, which consists
of an optical and readout pulse after a fixed optical dark time. Measurement normalization can be
done from different AO-PL readouts within one optical pulse to suppress common-mode noise.

Figure 6. Measurement sequence for pulsed AO-PL operation consists of an initial optical NV spin polarization pulse,
followed by an optical readout pulse that also serves as optical NV spin re-polarization during repetition. A detection
pulse is applied shortly before the optical readout pulse to capture the full NV time-resolved PL trace.

AO-PL contrast is calculated as 𝐶 = 1 − 𝐼%&' /𝐼()* , where 𝐼%&' and 𝐼()* are the AO-PL feature
intensities acquired at different timings within one optical pulse. Figure 7 shows the time-
dependency of AO-PL contrast for near-zero-field NV-NV cross-relaxation AO-PL features, as
we vary the 𝐼%&' measurement timing and fix 𝐼()* as the steady-state PL intensity at the end of the
optical pulse. Figure 7(a) shows that large AO-PL contrast can be obtained by measuring 𝐼%&'
shortly after the optical pulse is turned on; and Figure 7(b) shows the resulting near-zero-field
AO-PL feature when 𝐼%&' is acquired at 1 𝜇𝑠 after the optical pulse.

We further study the dependency of AO-PL contrast on optical dark time 𝜏, across two diamond
samples with 4 ppm and 2 ppm NV concentration and both enriched with !" 𝑁. Figure 8 shows
that for both samples, the measured AO-PL contrast initially increases with dark time 𝜏, reaches
a maximum value, and then decreases at longer dark time 𝜏. The diamond sample with 4 ppm
[NV] reaches a larger AO-PL contrast at a shorter dark time 𝜏~2 ms.

A bi-exponential model 𝑎 ∙ 𝑒 34/6 + 𝑐 ∙ 𝑒 34/7 is in good agreement with our measured results,
revealing two different time scales for each diamond sample, with 𝑏 ≈ 4.81 (6.93) ms and 𝑑 ≈
1.77 (2.9) ms for the 4 ppm [NV] (2 ppm [NV], respectively) sample. We associate the longer
time scale 𝑏 to the NV spin-lattice relaxation time, and the shorter time scale 𝑑 to the NV-NV
cross-relaxation time. These results suggest that diamonds with higher [NV] and shorter spin-
lattice relaxation time are more desirable for AO operation, due to their larger AO-PL contrast,
larger PL emission intensities, and shorter dark time 𝜏 for higher sequence duty cycle.

AO-PL measurements at large magnetic fields

As a step towards AO-PL magnetometry inside a tokamak, we characterize AO-PL contrast as a
function of applied magnetic field up to 300 mT using a custom electromagnet. Figure 10 shows
the optomechanical system to position a NV-diamond sample in the center of the electromagnet
with two separate rotational degrees of freedom. The optical excitation is delivered through a
single-mode fiber carrying the first-order beam diffracted by an acoustic optical modulator.
Due to the complexity of optical system, optical intensity fluctuations are too strong to measure
AO-PL contrast reliably with CW operation. Therefore, we apply the pulsed AO-PL
measurement sequence described in Figure 6 to suppress common-mode noise. Figure 11 shows
the measured AO-PL contrast as a function of applied magnetic fields up to 300 mT, with 4
different rotational placements. We observe prominent reductions of AO-PL contrast around 50
mT due to NV-P1 cross-relaxation; and around 102 mT due to ground-state level anti-crossing.
We further find that the overall AO-PL contrast changes are dependent on NV orientation
alignment relative to the applied magnetic field.

Measured AO-PL contrast changes at large bias fields are caused by the depolarization of NV
electronic spins due to spin-mixing from off-axis magnetic fields 𝐵$ , which is distinct from NV-

NV cross-relaxation described in the previous sections. Therefore, the change of off-axis
magnetic fields 𝐵$ can induce larger AO-PL contrast than the change of on-axis magnetic fields
𝐵∥ that are aligned with NV symmetry axes. We investigate AO-PL sensitivity to small off-axis
magnetic fields produced by a separate coil around the NV-diamond sensor at various bias
magnetic fields 𝐵, as shown in Figure 12. There are noticeable AO-PL contrast changes as we
increase the current to the coil, with bias magnetic field up to 𝐵 = 120 mT.

In ongoing work, we are calibrating and applying stronger off-axis magnetic fields 𝐵$ to induce
larger AO-PL contrast changes; optimize the alignment under the targeted 𝐵 = 300 mT large
bias magnetic field for the best AO sensitivity; and use the measured sensitivity to project AO-PL
magnetometer sensitivity under ~10 T bias magnetic fields, such as used in a tokamak reactor.
