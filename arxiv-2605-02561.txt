Quantitative homogenization of elliptic equations with infinitely many scales
Zhongwei Shen, Yao Xu, Jinping Zhuge

Abstract
. In this paper, we develop a general homogenization theory for elliptic equations with coefficients that oscillate periodically at infinitely many scales ε = ( ε 1 , ε 2 , ⋯ ) ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) ∞ \varepsilon=(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},\cdots)\in(0,1)^{\infty} , with ε 1 > ε 2 > ⋯ \varepsilon_{1}>\varepsilon_{2}>\cdots and ε n → 0 \varepsilon_{n}\to 0 as n → ∞ n\to\infty . Such problems arise naturally in the study of fractal materials and diffusion in fluids. Under suitable scale-separation assumptions, we prove a qualitative homogenization theorem and obtain optimal L 2 L^{2} convergence rates. We also establish interior and boundary Lipschitz estimates that are uniform in ε \varepsilon . Keywords: Multiscale homogenization; convergence rates; uniform Lipschitz estimates.

Before stating our main assumptions and results,

A_{\infty}(y_{0},y_{1},\cdots)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}B_{\ell}(y_{0},y_{1},\cdots,y_{\ell}),

A^{\varepsilon}(x)=A_{\infty}\Big(x,\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{1}},\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{2}},\cdots\Big).

At scale

Quantitative convergence rates.

As for qualitative homogenization, to establish the quantitative convergence rates, we also start by considering the approximate equation (

This is by no means a direct consequence of the classical reiterated homogenization method; new ideas are required, as we now explain below.

Let us first mention that, the reiterated homogenization process can be broken down into a sequence of
H
-convergence of intermediate matrices, homogenizing the smallest scale in each step, i.e.,

(1.19)

\displaystyle A_{n}(x,x/\varepsilon_{1},\cdots,x/\varepsilon_{n})

\displaystyle\xrightarrow[\varepsilon_{n}\to 0]{H}A_{n}^{n-1}(x,x/\varepsilon_{1},\cdots,x/\varepsilon_{n-1})

\displaystyle\xrightarrow[\varepsilon_{n-1}\to 0]{H}A_{n}^{n-2}(x,x/\varepsilon_{1},\cdots,x/\varepsilon_{n-2})

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle\xrightarrow[\varepsilon_{1}\to 0]{H}A^{0}_{n}(x)=\widehat{A}_{n}(x).

Our method will take advantage of the special structure of the multiscale coefficients
A_{n}^{\varepsilon}(x)
, written as

A_{n}^{\varepsilon}(x)=A_{n-1}\Big(x,\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{1}},\cdots,\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{n-1}}\Big)+B_{n}\Big(x,\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{1}},\cdots,\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{n-1}},\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{n}}\Big).

The key observation is that the first term on the right-hand side, called the principal part, oscillates relatively slowly with a large amplitude; while the second term, called the tail part, oscillates relatively faster with a smaller amplitude. This structure appears not only in
A_{n}^{\varepsilon}(x)
, but also in every intermediate matrix

, taking the form of

A_{n}^{k,\varepsilon}(x)=A_{k-1}\Big(x,\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{1}},\cdots,\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{k-1}}\Big)+B^{n}_{k}\Big(x,\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{1}},\cdots,\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{k-1}},\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{n}}\Big),

where
\{B^{n}_{k}\}_{1\leq k\leq n-1}
are given by the backward recursive equations (
2.4
) and (
2.5
). Using the
H
-stability and a careful estimate of the recursive equations, we are able to show that

) and (
3.8
). In this case, we prove an improved error estimate that takes into account of the smallness of
E_{1}
:

\|u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\varepsilon(\|E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla_{x}E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}})+\text{similar terms};

see Theorem
3.6
for the precise statement.
This improvement - exploiting the smallness of
E_{1}

in the one-step homogenization - allows us to carry out the reiterated homogenization argument as in (
1.19
) with accumulated errors that can be controlled uniformly in
n
under the additional assumption
[\delta]_{\alpha}<\infty
for some
\alpha>0
; see Theorem
3.8
. It turns out that the optimal
L^{2}
convergence rate can be derived if
\alpha=1
, as in Theorem
1.2
.

Uniform Lipschitz estimates.

The proof of the uniform Lipschitz estimates proceeds largely within the framework of
[
She17
]
, a structured refinement of
[
AS16
]
based on the
L^{2}
convergence rates.
Since the essential difficulty is not reduced by considering the approximate problem (
1.18
) and establishing the Lipschitz estimates uniform in
n
, we will work directly on the original problem with infinitely many scales.

In multiscale homogenization, the uniform Lipschitz estimates are established through an iterative argument, in contrast to the proof of convergence rate, proceeding from the largest scale down to the smaller scales. To illustrate our main ideas, we take the simplified setting
\mathrm{div}(A^{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon})=0
in
B_{1}
. Recall that in
[
NSX20
]
, under appropriate scale-separation conditions, it was shown that for any
r\in(\varepsilon_{k+1},\varepsilon_{k})

(1.20)

\bigg(\fint_{B_{r}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\leq C\bigg(\fint_{B_{\varepsilon_{k}}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2},

where
C
is a large constant. Iterating this estimate, we obtain

\bigg(\fint_{B_{\varepsilon_{n}}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\leq C^{n}\bigg(\fint_{B_{1}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}.

This large-scale estimate is satisfactory for a fixed number of scales. However, for infinitely many scales considered in the present paper, as
n\to\infty
and
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
, the constant
C^{n}\to\infty
and the previous method fails. The failure indicates that an essential modification is needed for infinitely many scales.

Recall the well-known quantity

(1.21)

H(r)=H(r;u_{\varepsilon}):=\frac{1}{r}\inf_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\bigg(\fint_{B_{r}}|u_{\varepsilon}-P|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2},

where
\mathcal{P}
denotes the linear space of affine functions. This quantity, measuring how flat a function is, is frequently used to establish the large-scale Lipschitz estimates in elliptic homogenization. Let
h(r)=|\nabla P_{r}|
with
P_{r}\in\mathcal{P}
being the minimizer in (
1.21
). Roughly speaking,
h(r)
is comparable to the
L^{2}
average of
|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|
in
B_{r}
and generally larger than
H(r)
. In our situation, these two quantities combined can be used to establish (
1.20
) between two successive scales. The new idea here is establishing an iterative formula for
H(r)
and
h(r)
, across all scales, with improved errors again due to the special structure of the coefficients. More precisely, under certain scale-separation conditions, we show that for any
r\in[\varepsilon_{m+1},\varepsilon_{m}]
,

(1.22)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle H(r)\leq C_{0}\Big(\frac{r}{\varepsilon_{m}}\Big)^{\lambda}H(\varepsilon_{m})+C_{1}M_{m}\big\{H(\varepsilon_{m})+h(\varepsilon_{m})\big\},\\
\displaystyle|h(r)-h(\varepsilon_{m})|\leq C_{0}H(\varepsilon_{m})+C_{1}M_{m}\big\{H(\varepsilon_{m})+h(\varepsilon_{m})\big\},\end{cases}

where
\lambda\in(0,1),C_{0},C_{1}
are constants independent of
\varepsilon
and
m
. The constants
\{M_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfy

\displaystyle M_{m}\leq\max\bigg\{\sum_{j=0}^{m}\frac{\varepsilon_{m}}{\varepsilon_{j}}\sum_{k=j}^{\infty}\delta_{k},\sum_{j=0}^{m}\frac{\varepsilon_{m}}{\varepsilon_{j}}\cdot\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{\alpha}\delta_{k+m}\bigg\}.

For large
m
, the extra factor
M_{m}
becomes small, ensuring
\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}M_{m}<\infty
, under certain scale-separation condition on
\varepsilon
and decay condition on
\delta
. This crucial improvement actually follows from the extra factor
[\delta]_{\alpha}
in the
L^{2}
convergence rate of Theorem
3.8
as well as the rescaling properties that gradually shift the oscillation to the small tail part of the coefficients. Finally, with
\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}M_{m}<\infty
, we can iterate (
1.22
) as
m\to\infty
and obtain the uniform boundedness of
H(r)+h(r)
for all
r\in(0,1)
.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 addresses the qualitative homogenization of the Dirichlet problem (
1.1
) and
gives the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
Section 3 is devoted to the convergence rates and contains the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
The interior Lipschitz estimates (Theorem
1.5
) are established in Section 4, and
the boundary Lipschitz estimates (Theorem
1.6
) are proved in Section 5.

2.
Qualitative homogenization

2.1.
Reiterated homogenization

As discussed in the introduction, we shall start with the approximate problem (
1.17
) for arbitrary but fixed
n\geq 1
.
In this subsection, we review the method of reiterated homogenization to analyze (
1.17
) and provide some formal calculations adapted to our settings that will be referenced throughout the subsequent sections.

By the method of reiterated homogenization, the homogenized matrix
\widehat{A}_{n}(x)
under
\varepsilon\mathrel{\mathchoice{\rightarrow\mspace{-10mu}\rightarrow}{\rightarrow\mspace{-10mu}\rightarrow}{\rightarrow\mspace{-10mu}\rightarrow}{\rightarrow\mspace{-10mu}\rightarrow}}0
is obtained by repeatedly homogenizing one scale at a time in order from the smallest scale to the largest.
Precisely, we first fix
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},\cdots,\varepsilon_{n-1}
in (
1.17
) and let
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
. Then the fastest variable of equation (
1.17
) will be homogenized and the solution
u_{n,\varepsilon}\to u^{n-1}_{n,\varepsilon}
weakly in
H^{1}(\Omega)
, where
u^{n-1}_{n,\varepsilon}
is the weak solution of

(2.1)

\begin{cases}-\mathrm{div}(A_{n}^{n-1,\varepsilon}(x)\nabla u^{n-1}_{n,\varepsilon})=f&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u^{n-1}_{n,\varepsilon}=g&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

A_{n}^{n-1,\varepsilon}(x)=A^{n-1}_{n}(x,x/\varepsilon_{1},\cdots,x/\varepsilon_{n-1})
, and
A^{n-1}_{n}(y_{0},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-1})
is the
(n-1)
-scale intermediate matrix, given by

(2.2)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &A_{n}^{n-1}(y_{0},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-1})\\
&=\fint_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Big\{A_{n}(y_{0},\cdots,y_{n})+A_{n}(y_{0},\cdots,y_{n})\nabla_{y_{n}}\chi_{A_{n}}(y_{0},\cdots,y_{n})\Big\}dy_{n}.\end{aligned}

Here
\chi_{A_{n}}=(\chi_{A_{n}}^{j})_{1\leq j\leq d}
are the correctors satisfying the equations w.r.t. the variable
y_{n}
,

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\mathrm{div}_{y_{n}}(A_{n}\nabla_{y_{n}}\chi_{A_{n}}^{j})=\mathrm{div}_{y_{n}}(A_{n}e^{j})=\mathrm{div}_{y_{n}}(B_{n}e^{j})\quad\text{in }\mathbb{T}^{d},\\
\chi_{A_{n}}^{j}\text{ is $1$-periodic in }y_{n}\text{ and }\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\chi_{A_{n}}dy_{n}=0.\end{cases}

By (
2.2
) and (
1.2
), and a simple calculation,

(2.3)

\displaystyle A_{n}^{n-1}(y_{0},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-1})

\displaystyle=B_{0}(y_{0})+B_{1}(y_{0},y_{1})+\cdots+B_{n-2}(y_{0},\cdots,y_{n-2})

\displaystyle+B_{n-1}(y_{0},\cdots,y_{n-1})+\fint_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}B_{n}dy_{n}+\fint_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}B_{n}\nabla_{y_{n}}\chi_{A_{n}}dy_{n}.

Recall that the matrix
A_{n}^{n-1}
satisfies the ellipticity, boundedness and periodicity conditions as in (
1.4
)–(
1.6
).

Now the equation (
2.1
) has
n-1
scales, and we apply the same procedure to homogenize the smallest scale
\varepsilon_{n-1}
. We obtain the
(n-2)
-scale intermediate matrix in a form of
A_{n}^{n-2,\varepsilon}(x)=A_{n}^{n-2}(x,x/\varepsilon_{1},\cdots,x/\varepsilon_{n-2})
and repeat this procedure. In general, for
0\leq k\leq n-1
, the
k
-scale intermediate matrix is derived via the backward recursive equations:

(2.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}B_{k}^{n}(y_{0},\cdots,y_{k})&=B_{k}(y_{0},\cdots,y_{k})+\fint_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}B_{k+1}^{n}(y_{0},\cdots,y_{k+1})dy_{k+1}\\
&+\fint_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}B_{k+1}^{n}(y_{0},\cdots,y_{k+1})\nabla_{y_{k+1}}\chi_{A_{n}^{k+1}}(y_{0},\cdots,y_{k+1})dy_{k+1},\end{split}

and

(2.5)

\displaystyle A_{n}^{k}(y_{0},\cdots,y_{k})=\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1}B_{\ell}(y_{0},\cdots,y_{\ell})+B_{k}^{n}(y_{0},\cdots,y_{k}),

where we have used the convention
B^{n}_{n}=B_{n}
,
A_{n}^{n}=A_{n}
(to include the case
k=n-1
), and
\chi_{A_{n}^{k+1}}=(\chi_{A_{n}^{k+1}}^{j})_{1\leq j\leq d}
are the corresponding correctors corresponding to
A_{n}^{k+1}
w.r.t. the variable
y_{k+1}
:

(2.6)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\mathrm{div}_{y_{k+1}}(A_{n}^{k+1}\nabla_{y_{k+1}}\chi_{A_{n}^{k+1}}^{j})=\mathrm{div}_{y_{k+1}}(A_{n}^{k+1}e^{j})=\mathrm{div}_{y_{k+1}}(B^{n}_{k+1}e^{j})\quad\text{in }\mathbb{T}^{d},\\
\chi_{A_{n}^{k+1}}^{j}\text{ is $1$-periodic in }y_{k+1}\text{ and }\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\chi_{A_{n}^{k+1}}^{j}dy_{k+1}=0.\end{cases}

The final matrix
A^{0}_{n}(y_{0})
is the homogenized matrix obtained by the method of reiterated homogenization, and we write
\widehat{A}_{n}(y_{0})=A_{n}^{0}(y_{0})
.

It was proved in
[
AB96
,
NSX20
]
that if
\varepsilon\mathrel{\mathchoice{\rightarrow\mspace{-10mu}\rightarrow}{\rightarrow\mspace{-10mu}\rightarrow}{\rightarrow\mspace{-10mu}\rightarrow}{\rightarrow\mspace{-10mu}\rightarrow}}0
, then

u_{n,\varepsilon}
converges weakly to
u_{n,0}
in
H^{1}(\Omega)
and
A_{n}^{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{n,\varepsilon}
converges weakly to
\widehat{A}_{n}\nabla u_{n,0}
in
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where
u_{n,0}
is the solution of (
1.18
).
In this qualitative reiterated homogenization theorem, we only require that
\Omega
is a bounded Lipschitz domain,
f\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
and
g\in H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
.

2.2.
Compactness of
H
-convergence

An effective way to universally control the size of the intermediate and homogenized matrices is using the notion of
H
-convergence (or
G
-convergence in the case of symmetric coefficient matrices).
Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded domain. For
0<\alpha\leq\beta<\infty
, let
\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta;\Omega)
denote the set of
A\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
that satisfy

(2.7)

A(x)\xi\cdot\xi\geq\alpha|\xi|^{2},\quad A^{-1}(x)\xi\cdot\xi\geq\beta^{-1}|\xi|^{2},\quad\text{a.e. }x\in\Omega.

Definition 2.1

(
[
Tar09
]
)
.

We say a sequence
\{A_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}\subset\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta;\Omega)

H
-converges to
\bar{A}\in\mathcal{M}(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime};\Omega)
(denoted by
A_{k}\xrightarrow[k\to\infty]{H}\bar{A}
) for some
0<\alpha^{\prime}\leq\beta^{\prime}<\infty
, if for all
f\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
, the sequence of solutions
\{u_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}\subset H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
of
-\mathrm{div}(A_{k}\nabla u_{k})=f
converges to
\bar{u}
weakly in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
; and the sequence
A_{k}\nabla u_{k}
converges to
\bar{A}\nabla\bar{u}
weakly in
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where
\bar{u}
is the weak solution of
-\mathrm{div}(\bar{A}\nabla\bar{u})=f
in
\Omega
.

Recall that the
H
-limit of an
H
-converging sequence is unique. Also observe that the condition (
2.7
) in the class
\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta;\Omega)
is equivalent to the condition (
1.4
) and (
1.7
). Indeed, if
A\in\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta;\Omega)
, by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,
\beta^{-1}|\xi|^{2}\leq\xi\cdot A^{-1}\xi\leq|\xi||A^{-1}\xi|
for a.e.
x\in\Omega
. Thus,
|A^{-1}\xi|\geq\beta^{-1}|\xi|
for any
\xi
. Replacing
\xi
by
A\xi
in this inequality, we get
|A\xi|\leq\beta|\xi|
, which gives the upper bound of
A
. Conversely, if
A
satisfies (
1.4
) and (
1.7
), then we can show
\xi\cdot A^{-1}\xi\geq\mu^{3}|\xi|^{2}
and thus
A\in\mathcal{M}(\mu,\mu^{-3};\Omega)
.

The importance of the class
\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta;\Omega)
is reflected by its compactness under
H
-convergence.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
Tar09
, Theorem 6.5]
)
.

For any sequence
\{A_{k}\}\subset\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta;\Omega)
there exists a subsequence
\{A_{k_{\ell}}\}
and an element
\bar{A}\in\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta;\Omega)
such that
A_{k_{\ell}}

H
-converges to
\bar{A}
.

This compactness can be used to establish the uniform upper and lower bounds of the intermediate and homogenized coefficient matrices at each reiterated step.
Consider the coefficient matrix
A_{n}(y_{0},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-1},x/\varepsilon_{n})
as a function of
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
(y_{0},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-1})\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{d(n-1)}
are fixed parameters. We know from (
1.4
), (
1.7
), and the previous argument that

A_{n}(y_{0},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-1},x/\varepsilon_{n})\in\mathcal{M}(\mu,\mu^{-3})\quad\text{for each }y_{0},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-1}.

Hereafter we write
\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta)=\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta;\Omega)
if the coefficient matrices are defined in the entire
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and drop in every
\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta;\Omega)
for any bounded
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. As
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
, by the classical homogenization theorem with only one oscillating scale, it holds

A_{n}(y_{0},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-1},x/\varepsilon_{n})\xrightarrow[\varepsilon_{n}\to 0]{H}A_{n}^{n-1}(y_{0},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-1}),

where
A_{n}^{n-1}
is given by (
2.2
).
Therefore, by Lemma
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle A_{n}^{n-1}(y_{0},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-1})\in\mathcal{M}(\mu,\mu^{-3})\quad\text{for each }y_{0},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-1}.

Then, by setting
y_{n-1}=x/\varepsilon_{n-1}
, we get

A_{n}^{n-1}(y_{0},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-2},x/\varepsilon_{n-1})\in\mathcal{M}(\mu,\mu^{-3})\quad\text{for each }y_{0},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-2}.

This allows us to apply the same argument repeatedly to conclude that for
0\leq k\leq n

(2.8)

\displaystyle A_{n}^{k}(y_{0},\cdots,y_{k})\in\mathcal{M}(\mu,\mu^{-3})\quad\text{for each }y_{0},\cdots,y_{k}.

In other words, each
A_{n}^{k}
, including
\widehat{A}_{n}=A_{n}^{0}
, satisfies the ellipticity and boundedness conditions with the constant depending only on
\mu
.
In particular, by (
2.5
),

(2.9)

\displaystyle\|B_{k}^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}=\|A^{k}_{n}-A_{k-1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|A^{k}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|A_{k-1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C,

where
C
depends only on
\mu
(thus uniform in
n
and
k
).

2.3.
Stability of
H
-convergence

In this subsection, we use the stability result of
H
-convergence, demonstrated in
[
BM82
,
EMP97
,
Tar09
, etc.]
, to establish the convergence of
\widehat{A}_{n}
as

, as well as their Lipschitz regularity uniform in

.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
Tar09
, Lemma 10.9]
)
.

Let
\{A_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
and
\{A_{k}^{\prime}\}_{k\geq 1}
be two sequences of matrices in
\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta;\Omega)
such that for each
k\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\|A_{k}-A_{k}^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\tau\quad\text{for some }\tau>0.

Suppose that
A_{k}

H
-converges to
\bar{A}
and
A_{k}^{\prime}

H
-converges to
\bar{A}^{\prime}
as
k\rightarrow\infty
. Then

\displaystyle\|\bar{A}-\bar{A}^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C\tau,

where
C=\beta/\alpha
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Assume (
1.4
)–(
1.6
).
Let
\widehat{A}_{n}
denote the homogenized matrix in (
1.18
) derived through reiterated homogenization. Then there exists a constant
C>0
, depending only on
\mu
, such that for any
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
,
n<m

(2.10)

\|\widehat{A}_{n}-\widehat{A}_{m}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C\sum_{k=n+1}^{m}\|B_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}}.

If in addition assume

(2.11)

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|\nabla_{y_{0}}B_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}}<\infty,

then for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and any
x_{1},x_{2}\in\Omega
,

(2.12)

\displaystyle|\widehat{A}_{n}(x_{1})-\widehat{A}_{n}(x_{2})|\leq C\sum_{k=0}^{n}\|\nabla_{y_{0}}B_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}}|x_{1}-x_{2}|.

Proof.

By the reiterated homogenization described as in Section
2.1
, we have
A_{n}^{\varepsilon}\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\mathrel{\mathchoice{\rightarrow\mspace{-10mu}\rightarrow}{\rightarrow\mspace{-10mu}\rightarrow}{\rightarrow\mspace{-10mu}\rightarrow}{\rightarrow\mspace{-10mu}\rightarrow}}0]{H}\widehat{A}_{n}
and

. Note that by (

and

. Due to (

),

\displaystyle\|A_{n}^{\varepsilon}-A_{m}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\sum_{k=n+1}^{m}\|B_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}}.

By Lemma
2.3
, this yields (
2.10
).

For the estimate (

), we set

in

to be

and

respectively, and denote

\displaystyle A_{n,i}^{\varepsilon}(x)=A_{n}(x_{i},x/\varepsilon_{1},\cdots,x/\varepsilon_{n}),\quad i=1,2.

In this situation, by the process of reiterated homogenization,

\displaystyle A_{n,i}^{\varepsilon}(x)\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\mathrel{\mathchoice{\rightarrow\mspace{-10mu}\rightarrow}{\rightarrow\mspace{-10mu}\rightarrow}{\rightarrow\mspace{-10mu}\rightarrow}{\rightarrow\mspace{-10mu}\rightarrow}}0]{H}\widehat{A}_{n}(x_{i}),

\displaystyle|A_{n,1}^{\varepsilon}(x)-A_{n,2}^{\varepsilon}(x)|\leq\|\nabla_{y_{0}}A_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}|x_{1}-x_{2}|\leq\sum_{k=0}^{n}\|\nabla_{y_{0}}B_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}}|x_{1}-x_{2}|.

Therefore, Lemma
2.3
implies (
2.12

∎

Theorem 2.5
.

Assume (
1.4
)–(
1.6
). Then there exists a homogenized matrix
\widehat{A}\in\mathcal{M}(\mu,\mu^{-3};\Omega)
such that
\widehat{A}_{n}\to\widehat{A}
uniformly in

as

, and

(2.13)

\|\widehat{A}_{n}-\widehat{A}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\|B_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}},

where

is the constant given in Lemma
2.4
.
Moreover, if in addition (
2.11
) holds,
then
\widehat{A}
is Lipschitz in
\Omega
and

(2.14)

\displaystyle|\widehat{A}(x_{1})-\widehat{A}(x_{2})|\leq C\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|\nabla_{y_{0}}B_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}}|x_{1}-x_{2}|.

Proof.

According to Lemma
2.4
,
\{\widehat{A}_{n}\}_{n}
is a Cauchy sequence in

under our assumption, whose limit, denoted by

as

in (

in (

).
∎

For further applications in quantitative homogenization via reiterated homogenization, the size estimates of the intermediate matrices
A^{k}_{n}
and

are crucial.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
B_{k}^{n}
and
A_{n}^{k}
be matrices given in Section
2.1
. We have for each
0\leq k\leq n
,

(2.15)

\displaystyle\|B_{k}^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\sum_{\ell=k}^{n}\|B_{\ell}\|_{L^{\infty}},

and for each
0\leq j\leq k
,

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{y_{j}}A_{n}^{k}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\sum_{\ell=j}^{n}\|\nabla_{y_{j}}B_{\ell}\|_{L^{\infty}},

(2.16)

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{y_{j}}B_{k}^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\sum_{\ell=j}^{n}\|\nabla_{y_{j}}B_{\ell}\|_{L^{\infty}},

where
C
depends only on
\mu
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Lemma
2.4
. Recall that for fixed parameters
y_{0},y_{1},\cdots,y_{k}
,

(2.17)

\displaystyle A_{n}(y_{0},\cdots,y_{k},x/\varepsilon_{k+1},\cdots,x/\varepsilon_{n})\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\mathrel{\mathchoice{\rightarrow\mspace{-10mu}\rightarrow}{\rightarrow\mspace{-10mu}\rightarrow}{\rightarrow\mspace{-10mu}\rightarrow}{\rightarrow\mspace{-10mu}\rightarrow}}0]{H}A_{n}^{k}(y_{0},\cdots,y_{k}),

and
A_{k-1}(y_{0},\cdots,y_{k-1})
(as a constant matrix in

)
H
-converges to itself. Also note that

\displaystyle|A_{n}(y_{0},\cdots,y_{k},x/\varepsilon_{k+1},\cdots,x/\varepsilon_{n})-A_{k-1}(y_{0},\cdots,y_{k-1})|\leq\sum_{\ell=k}^{n}\|B_{\ell}\|_{L^{\infty}}.

As a result, Lemma
2.3
yields

\displaystyle|A_{n}^{k}(y_{0},\cdots,y_{k})-A_{k-1}(y_{0},\cdots,y_{k-1})|\leq C\sum_{\ell=k}^{n}\|B_{\ell}\|_{L^{\infty}},

which, together with (
2.5
) (which can be written as

), gives

\displaystyle|B_{k}^{n}(y_{0},\cdots,y_{k})|\leq C\sum_{\ell=k}^{n}\|B_{\ell}\|_{L^{\infty}},

where

depends only on
\mu
.

On the other hand, since for
0\leq j\leq k
and any

3.
Convergence rates

3.1.
Recursive systems

To obtain the convergence rates for the equation (
1.1
), it suffices to consider the approximate problem (
1.17
) with
n
scales and establish convergence rates independent of
n
. Thus, we will fix
n>1
.
In view of the process of reiterated homogenization described in Section
2.1

or (
1.19
), we need to consider all intermediate problems with coefficient matrices
A_{n}^{k,\varepsilon}(x)
.
Recall that
A^{k}_{n}
is obtained through the backward recursive system (
2.5
) and (
2.4
), which can be written in a concise form

\begin{cases}A_{n}^{k}=A_{k-1}+B^{n}_{k},\\
B^{n}_{k}=B^{n}_{k+1}+\langle B_{k+1}^{n}(I+\nabla_{y_{k+1}}\chi_{A^{k+1}_{n}})\rangle_{y_{k+1}},\end{cases}

for
1\leq k\leq n-1
with
B_{n}^{n}=B_{n}
and
A^{n}_{n}=A_{n}
, where
\langle\cdot\rangle_{y_{k+1}}
denotes the mean value over
y_{k+1}\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
. To prove a convergence rate uniform in the number of scales, we need accurate size estimates of
B^{n}_{k}
that could be accumulated through the recursive system. The
L^{\infty}
bound of
B^{n}_{k}
has been given by (
2.15
), which is sufficient for us. We also need the
L^{\infty}
bound of
\nabla_{y_{j}}B^{n}_{k}
decaying as
n,k\to\infty
, which is not implied by (
2.16
).

Denote for
0\leq k\leq n

\delta_{k}^{n}=\max_{0\leq j\leq k}\|\nabla_{y_{j}}B_{k}^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}.

We have the following backward recursive estimates for
\delta^{n}_{k}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

It holds that
\delta_{n}^{n}\leq\delta_{n}
, and for
0\leq k\leq n-1
,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\delta_{k}^{n}\leq\delta_{k}+\delta_{k+1}^{n}+C_{0}\sum_{\ell=k+1}^{n}\delta_{\ell}\delta_{k+1}^{n}+C_{0}[\delta]_{0}\bigg(\sum_{\ell=k+1}^{n}\delta_{\ell}\bigg)^{2},

where
C_{0}
depends only on
d,\mu
.

Proof.

The estimate for
\delta_{n}^{n}
is trivial, since
B_{n}^{n}=B_{n}
. For
0\leq j\leq k\leq n-1
, by the equation (
2.6
) of
\chi_{A_{n}^{k+1}}
, one can see that for each
(y_{0},\cdots,y_{k})
,

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{y_{k+1}}\chi_{A_{n}^{k+1}}\|_{L^{2}_{y_{k+1}}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leq C\|B_{k+1}^{n}\|_{L^{2}_{y_{k+1}}(\mathbb{T}^{d})},

and

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{y_{k+1}}\nabla_{y_{j}}\chi_{A_{n}^{k+1}}\|_{L^{2}_{y_{k+1}}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla_{y_{j}}B_{k+1}^{n}\|_{L^{2}_{y_{k+1}}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}

\displaystyle\quad+C\|\nabla_{y_{j}}A_{n}^{k+1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{y_{k+1}}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\|\nabla_{y_{k+1}}\chi_{A_{n}^{k+1}}\|_{L^{2}_{y_{k+1}}(\mathbb{T}^{d})},

where
C
depends only on
d,\mu
. In view of (
2.4
), this implies that

\displaystyle|\nabla_{y_{j}}B_{k}^{n}|\leq|\nabla_{y_{j}}B_{k}|+\fint_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\nabla_{y_{j}}B_{k+1}^{n}|dy_{k+1}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\fint_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Big(|\nabla_{y_{j}}B_{k+1}^{n}||\nabla_{y_{k+1}}\chi_{A_{n}^{k+1}}|+|B_{k+1}^{n}||\nabla_{y_{j}}\nabla_{y_{k+1}}\chi_{A_{n}^{k+1}}|\Big)dy_{k+1}

\displaystyle\leq|\nabla_{y_{j}}B_{k}|+\|\nabla_{y_{j}}B_{k+1}^{n}\|_{L^{2}_{y_{k+1}}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}+C\|\nabla_{y_{j}}B_{k+1}^{n}\|_{L^{2}_{y_{k+1}}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\|B_{k+1}^{n}\|_{L^{2}_{y_{k+1}}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}

\displaystyle\qquad+C\|B_{k+1}^{n}\|_{L^{2}_{y_{k+1}}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}\|\nabla_{y_{j}}A_{n}^{k+1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{y_{k+1}}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}.

Since by Lemma
2.6
,

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{y_{j}}A_{n}^{k+1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\sum_{\ell=j}^{n}\|\nabla_{y_{j}}B_{\ell}\|_{L^{\infty}},

we deduce that

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{y_{j}}B_{k}^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla_{y_{j}}B_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla_{y_{j}}B_{k+1}^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}+C\|B_{k+1}^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla_{y_{j}}B_{k+1}^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\quad+C\|B_{k+1}^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\sum_{\ell=j}^{n}\|\nabla_{y_{j}}B_{\ell}\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Taking the supremum over
0\leq j\leq k
, we obtain

\displaystyle\delta_{k}^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\delta_{k}+\delta_{k+1}^{n}+C\sum_{\ell=k+1}^{n}\delta_{\ell}\delta_{k+1}^{n}+C\bigg(\sum_{\ell=k+1}^{n}\delta_{\ell}\bigg)^{2}\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}\delta_{\ell}

\displaystyle\leq\delta_{k}+\delta_{k+1}^{n}+C\sum_{\ell=k+1}^{n}\delta_{\ell}\delta_{k+1}^{n}+C[\delta]_{0}\bigg(\sum_{\ell=k+1}^{n}\delta_{\ell}\bigg)^{2},

where we have used Lemma
2.6
as well as the assumption (
1.10
). The constant
C
depends only on
d
and
\mu
. The proof is complete.
∎

The next lemma gives a good control for the recursive system (
3.1
).

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\delta_{k}^{n}
satisfy the recursive conditions in Lemma
3.1
. Suppose
[\delta]_{1}<\infty
. Then for any
n\geq 1
and
0\leq k\leq n
,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\delta_{k}^{n}\leq\exp\big\{C_{0}[\delta]_{1}\big\}\cdot\big\{1+C_{0}[\delta]_{0}[\delta]_{1}\big\}\cdot R_{k}^{n},

where

(3.3)

\displaystyle R_{k}^{n}=\sum_{j=k}^{n}\delta_{j}.

Proof.

For fixed
n
, we write (
3.1
) as

\displaystyle\delta_{k}^{n}\leq a_{k}\delta_{k+1}^{n}+b_{k},

where

\displaystyle a_{k}=1+C_{0}R_{k+1}^{n},\quad b_{k}=\delta_{k}+C_{0}[\delta]_{0}(R_{k+1}^{n})^{2}.

By using a backward induction on
k
, we can show that

\displaystyle\delta_{k}^{n}\leq\prod_{\ell=k}^{n-1}a_{\ell}\delta_{n}^{n}+\sum_{j=k}^{n-1}\prod_{\ell=k}^{j-1}a_{\ell}b_{j},

where we have employed the convention that
\prod_{\ell=k}^{j-1}a_{\ell}=1
if
j-1<k
. To bound the first term, we note that for each
k
,

\displaystyle\prod_{\ell=k}^{n-1}a_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\prod_{\ell=k}^{n-1}(1+C_{0}R_{\ell+1}^{n})=\exp\bigg\{\sum_{\ell=k}^{n-1}\log(1+C_{0}R_{\ell+1}^{n})\bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq\exp\bigg\{\sum_{\ell=k}^{n-1}C_{0}R_{\ell+1}^{n}\bigg\}\leq\exp\bigg\{C_{0}\sum_{j=k+1}^{n}\sum_{\ell=k}^{j-1}\delta_{j}\bigg\}\leq\exp\big\{C_{0}[\delta]_{1}\big\},

which gives

\displaystyle\prod_{\ell=k}^{n-1}a_{\ell}\delta_{n}^{n}\leq\exp\big\{C_{0}[\delta]_{1}\big\}\delta_{n}^{n}.

For the second term, since
\prod_{\ell=k}^{j-1}a_{\ell}\leq\prod_{\ell=k}^{n-1}a_{\ell}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=k}^{n-1}\prod_{\ell=k}^{j-1}a_{\ell}b_{j}

\displaystyle\leq\exp\big\{C_{0}[\delta]_{1}\big\}\cdot\sum_{j=k}^{n-1}\left\{\delta_{j}+C_{0}[\delta]_{0}(R_{j+1}^{n})^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\exp\big\{C_{0}[\delta]_{1}\big\}\cdot\bigg(R_{k}^{n-1}+C_{0}[\delta]_{0}R_{k}^{n}\sum_{j=k}^{n-1}R_{j+1}^{n}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\big\{C_{0}[\delta]_{1}\big\}\cdot\big\{R_{k}^{n-1}+C_{0}[\delta]_{0}[\delta]_{1}R_{k}^{n}\big\}.

Combining these two estimates, we obtain the desired inequality (
3.2
).
∎

For future use, we introduce the notation

(3.4)

\displaystyle R_{k}:=\sum_{\ell=k}^{\infty}\delta_{\ell}\quad\text{for }k\geq 0.

Notice that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{\alpha}R_{k}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{\alpha}\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}\delta_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{j}k^{\alpha}\delta_{j},

and that there exist

depending only on
\alpha\in[0,\infty)
such that

\displaystyle c_{\alpha}j^{\alpha+1}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{j}k^{\alpha}\leq C_{\alpha}j^{\alpha+1}.

It follows that

(3.5)

\displaystyle c_{\alpha}[\delta]_{\alpha+1}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{\alpha}R_{k}\leq C_{\alpha}[\delta]_{\alpha+1}.

such that

. Note that the smoothing at

We recall two lemmas taken from

Lemma 3.4
.

Suppose that

and

where

.

Lemma 3.5
.

Suppose that

where

.

Let
\eta_{\varepsilon}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
be a cutoff function satisfying

,
\eta_{\varepsilon}=1
on
\Omega\setminus\Omega_{4\varepsilon}
,
\eta_{\varepsilon}=0
on
\Omega_{3\varepsilon}
, where

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{1,1}
domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
u_{\varepsilon}
and
u_{0}
be the weak solutions of (
3.6
) and (

), respectively. Then

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}&\leq C\varepsilon\big\{\|E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla_{x}E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\varepsilon\|\nabla_{x}E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\\
&\qquad+(\|E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\varepsilon\|E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla_{x}E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}})\|\nabla_{x}A\|_{L^{\infty}}\\
&\qquad+\varepsilon\|E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|\nabla_{x}A\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\big\}\times(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{H^{3/2}(\partial\Omega)}),\end{split}

where
C
depends only on
d,\mu
and
\Omega
.

Proof.

The proof is a refinement of that in
[
NSX20
, Lemmas 3.2 and 4.1]
. Let

(3.18)

w_{\varepsilon}=u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}-\varepsilon S_{\varepsilon}(\eta_{\varepsilon}\chi^{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{0}).

Then

\displaystyle-\mathrm{div}(A^{\varepsilon}\nabla w_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{div}[(A^{\varepsilon}-\widehat{A})\nabla u_{0}]+\mathrm{div}[A^{\varepsilon}S_{\varepsilon}\big(\eta_{\varepsilon}(\nabla_{y}\chi)^{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{0}\big)]

\displaystyle\qquad+\varepsilon\mathrm{div}\big[A^{\varepsilon}S_{\varepsilon}\big([\nabla_{x}(\eta_{\varepsilon}\chi\nabla u_{0})]^{\varepsilon}\big)\big]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{div}\big[(A^{\varepsilon}-\widehat{A})\nabla u_{0}-S_{\varepsilon}\big((A^{\varepsilon}-\widehat{A})\eta_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{0}\big)\big]

\displaystyle\qquad+\mathrm{div}[A^{\varepsilon}S_{\varepsilon}\big(\eta_{\varepsilon}(\nabla_{y}\chi)^{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{0}\big)-S_{\varepsilon}\big(A^{\varepsilon}(\nabla_{y}\chi)^{\varepsilon}\eta_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{0}\big)]

\displaystyle\qquad+\mathrm{div}\big[S_{\varepsilon}\big(F(x,x/\varepsilon)\eta_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{0}\big)\big]

\displaystyle\qquad+\varepsilon\mathrm{div}\big[A^{\varepsilon}S_{\varepsilon}\big([\nabla_{x}(\eta_{\varepsilon}\chi\nabla u_{0})]^{\varepsilon}\big)\big],

where
F=F(x,y)
is given by (
3.11
).
It follows that for any test function
\psi\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}A^{\varepsilon}\nabla w_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\psi dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}|(A^{\varepsilon}-\widehat{A})\nabla u_{0}-S_{\varepsilon}\big((A^{\varepsilon}-\widehat{A})\eta_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{0}\big)||\nabla\psi|dx

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\Omega}\left|A^{\varepsilon}S_{\varepsilon}\left(\eta_{\varepsilon}(\nabla_{y}\chi)^{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{0}\right)-S_{\varepsilon}\left(\eta_{\varepsilon}A^{\varepsilon}(\nabla_{y}\chi)^{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{0}\right)||\nabla\psi|dx\right.

\displaystyle\quad+\bigg|\int_{\Omega}S_{\varepsilon}\big(F(x,x/\varepsilon)\eta_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{0}\big)\cdot\nabla\psi dx\bigg|+C\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}\big|S_{\varepsilon}\big([\nabla_{x}(\eta_{\varepsilon}\chi\nabla u_{0})]^{\varepsilon}\big)\big||\nabla\psi|dx

\displaystyle=J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3}+J_{4}.

We will estimate
J_{1}
-
J_{4}
separately, skipping some detailed calculations, which can be found in
[
NSX20
, Lemma 3.2]
.

By Lemma
3.5
, we obtain

\displaystyle J_{1}\leq C\|A-\widehat{A}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{4\varepsilon})}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{5\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle+C\varepsilon\big\{\|\nabla_{x}(A-\widehat{A})\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|A-\widehat{A}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{2}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{3\varepsilon})}\big\}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

and

\displaystyle J_{2}\leq C\varepsilon\|\nabla_{x}A\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla_{y}\chi\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

For
J_{3}
, using the skew-symmetry of the flux corrector
\phi
, we have

\displaystyle J_{3}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\bigg|\int_{\Omega}S_{\varepsilon}\big([\nabla_{x}(\phi\nabla u_{0}\eta_{\varepsilon})]^{\varepsilon}\big)\cdot\nabla\psi dx\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq C\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{4\varepsilon})}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{5\varepsilon})}+C\varepsilon\|\nabla_{x}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle+C\varepsilon\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}\|\nabla^{2}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{3\varepsilon})}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

For
J_{4}
, we have

\displaystyle J_{4}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\chi\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{4\varepsilon})}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{5\varepsilon})}+C\varepsilon\|\nabla_{x}\chi\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\qquad+C\varepsilon\|\chi\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}\|\nabla^{2}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{3\varepsilon})}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Summing up
J_{1}
-
J_{4}
, we obtain

(3.19)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left|\int_{\Omega}A^{\varepsilon}\nabla w_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\psi dx\right|\\
&\leq C\varepsilon\Big\{\big(\|A-\widehat{A}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}+\|\chi\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}\big)\|\nabla^{2}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{3\varepsilon})}\\
&\qquad\qquad+\big(\|\nabla_{x}(A-\widehat{A})\|_{\infty}+\|\nabla_{x}A\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla_{y}\chi\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}\\
&\qquad\qquad+\|\nabla_{x}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}+\|\nabla_{x}\chi\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}\big)\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Big\}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\\
&\quad+C\big(\|A-\widehat{A}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}+\|\chi\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}\big)\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{4\varepsilon})}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{5\varepsilon})}\\
&\leq C\varepsilon\Big\{\|E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{2}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{3\varepsilon})}\\
&\qquad\qquad+\big(\|\nabla_{x}A\|_{L^{\infty}}\|E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla_{x}E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Big\}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\\
&\qquad+C\|E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{4\varepsilon})}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{5\varepsilon})},\end{split}

where we have used Lemma
3.3
in the second inequality, and
C
depends only on
d
and
\mu
. Setting
\psi=w_{\varepsilon}
in the above inequality, we deduce that

(3.20)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}&\leq C\Big\{\|E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\big(\varepsilon\|\nabla^{2}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{3\varepsilon})}+\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{4\varepsilon})}\big)\\
&\qquad+\varepsilon\big(\|\nabla_{x}A\|_{L^{\infty}}\|E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla_{x}E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Big\}.\end{split}

Note that by the energy estimate and
H^{2}
estimate in
C^{1,1}
domains,

(3.21)

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)}),

(3.22)

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{2}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C(\|\nabla\widehat{A}\|_{L^{\infty}}+1)(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{H^{3/2}(\partial\Omega)}),

and by the boundary layer estimate,

(3.23)

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{4\varepsilon})}\leq C\varepsilon^{1/2}\big(\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{1/2}\|\nabla^{2}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{1/2}\big).

Moreover, it follows from the first line of (
3.10
) and the equation (
3.9
) that

(3.24)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\nabla\widehat{A}\|_{L^{\infty}}&\leq C\big\{\|\nabla_{x}A\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla_{x}A\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\chi\|_{L^{\infty}H^{1}}+\|A\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla_{x}\chi\|_{L^{\infty}H^{1}}\big\}\\
&\leq C\|\nabla_{x}A\|_{L^{\infty}},\end{split}

where we have used the fact
\|A\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\mu^{-1}
. By inserting (
3.21
)-(
3.24
) into (
3.20
), we obtain

(3.25)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}&\leq C\varepsilon^{1/2}\Big\{\|E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla_{x}A\|_{L^{\infty}}^{1/2}+\varepsilon^{1/2}\|\nabla_{x}E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\\
&\qquad+\varepsilon^{1/2}\|E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla_{x}A\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big\}\cdot\Big(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{H^{3/2}(\partial\Omega)}\Big).\end{split}

Next, we apply a duality argument as in the proof of
[
NSX20
, Lemma 4.1]
. Let
v_{\varepsilon}
be the solution of the dual problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\mathrm{div}(A^{*}(x,{x}/{\varepsilon})\nabla v_{\varepsilon})=G&\text{in }\Omega,\\
v_{\varepsilon}=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

and
v_{0}
the corresponding homogenized solution. Define

\widetilde{w}_{\varepsilon}=v_{\varepsilon}-v_{0}-\varepsilon S_{\varepsilon}\big(\widetilde{\eta}_{\varepsilon}(\chi^{*})^{\varepsilon}\nabla v_{0}\big)\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),

where
\widetilde{\eta}_{\varepsilon}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
is a cutoff function such that
0\leq\widetilde{\eta}_{\varepsilon}\leq 1
,
\widetilde{\eta}_{\varepsilon}=1
on
\Omega\setminus\Omega_{10\varepsilon}
,
\widetilde{\eta}_{\varepsilon}=0
on
\Omega_{8\varepsilon}
. Note that
\widetilde{w}_{\varepsilon}
satisfies the same estimates as
w_{\varepsilon}
. Then

(3.26)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \bigg|\int_{\Omega}w_{\varepsilon}\cdot Gdx\bigg|&=\bigg|\int_{\Omega}A^{\varepsilon}\nabla w_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla v_{\varepsilon}dx\bigg|\\
&\leq\bigg|\int_{\Omega}A^{\varepsilon}\nabla w_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\widetilde{w}_{\varepsilon}dx\bigg|+\bigg|\int_{\Omega}A^{\varepsilon}\nabla w_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla v_{0}dx\bigg|\\
&\qquad+\bigg|\int_{\Omega}A^{\varepsilon}\nabla w_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\big[\varepsilon S_{\varepsilon}\big(\eta_{\varepsilon}(\chi^{*})^{\varepsilon}\nabla v_{0}\big)\big]dx\bigg|\\
&=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.\end{aligned}

By a calculation as in
[
NSX20
, Lemma 4.1]
, using (
3.25
), we see that

(3.27)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} I_{1}\leq&C\varepsilon\big\{\|E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\|E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|\nabla_{x}A\|_{L^{\infty}}+\varepsilon\|\nabla_{x}E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\\
&\quad+\varepsilon\|E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|\nabla_{x}A\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\big\}\cdot\left(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{H^{3/2}(\partial\Omega)}\right)\|G\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.\end{aligned}

Using (
3.19
), we have

(3.28)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} I_{2}&\leq C\varepsilon\big\{\|E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla_{x}E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla_{x}A\|_{L^{\infty}}\big\}\\
&\qquad\cdot(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{H^{3/2}(\partial\Omega)})\|G\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\end{aligned}

and

(3.29)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} I_{3}&\leq C\varepsilon\big\{\|E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla_{x}E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla_{x}A\|_{L^{\infty}}\big\}\\
&\qquad\cdot\big\{\|E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\varepsilon\|\nabla_{x}E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\varepsilon\|E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla_{x}A\|_{L^{\infty}}\big\}\\
&\qquad\cdot(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{H^{3/2}(\partial\Omega)})\|G\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.\end{aligned}

Inserting the estimates for

-
I_{3}
into (

), we obtain

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{\Omega}w_{\varepsilon}\cdot Gdx\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\Big\{\|E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla_{x}E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\varepsilon\|\nabla_{x}E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}

\displaystyle+(\|E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\varepsilon\|E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla_{x}E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}})\|\nabla_{x}A\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle+\varepsilon\|E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|\nabla_{x}A\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\Big\}\cdot(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{H^{3/2}(\partial\Omega)})\|G\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

By duality and (

), we conclude that

\displaystyle\|u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\Big\{\|E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla_{x}E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\varepsilon\|\nabla_{x}E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+(\|E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\varepsilon\|E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla_{x}E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}})\|\nabla_{x}A\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\varepsilon\|E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|\nabla_{x}A\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\Big\}\cdot(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{H^{3/2}(\partial\Omega)}),

where we have also used the estimate

(3.30)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\varepsilon S_{\varepsilon}(\eta_{\varepsilon}\chi^{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{0})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}&\leq C\varepsilon\|E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\\
&\leq C\varepsilon\|E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)}).\end{split}

The proof is complete.

3.3.
Reiterated homogenization: quantitative version

In this subsection, we prove the optimal convergence rates in Theorem
1.2
by a quantitative version of reiterated homogenization.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

In view of the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we know that
u_{n,\varepsilon}\to u_{\varepsilon}
in
H^{1}(\Omega)
and
u_{n,0}\to u_{0}
in
H^{1}(\Omega)
as
n\to\infty
, where
u_{n,\varepsilon}
is the weak solution of the approximate problem (
1.17
) and
u_{n,0}
is the solution of the corresponding homogenized equation (
1.18
). If we can establish a convergence rate for
u_{n,\varepsilon}
, which is uniform in
n
, then we obtain the same convergence rate of
u_{\varepsilon}
by taking
n\to\infty
.

Fix
n\geq 1
and consider
1\leq k\leq n
. Let
u_{n,\varepsilon}^{k}
be the weak solution to

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\mathrm{div}(A_{n}^{k}(x,\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{1}},\cdots,\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{k}})\nabla u_{n,\varepsilon}^{k})=f&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u_{n,\varepsilon}^{k}=g&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

where
A_{n}^{k}
is given by (
2.5
) with the convention
A_{n}^{n}=A_{n}
. We will apply Theorem
3.6
and the method of reiterated homogenization.

Denote
A^{\prime}(x,y)=E_{0}(x)+E_{1}(x,y)
, where

\displaystyle E_{0}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1}B_{\ell}\Big(x,\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{1}},\cdots,\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{\ell}}\Big),

\displaystyle E_{1}(x,y)

\displaystyle=B_{k}^{n}\Big(x,\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{1}},\cdots,\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{k-1}},y\Big).

Then
A_{n}^{k}\big(x,{x}/{\varepsilon_{1}},\cdots,{x}/{\varepsilon_{k}}\big)=A^{\prime}\big(x,{x}/{\varepsilon_{k}}\big)
. Thanks to (
2.8
) and (
2.9
),
A^{\prime}
satisfies the conditions in the first two lines of (
3.7
), with a universal ellipticity constant
\mu^{\prime}
depending only on
d
and
\mu
. Moreover, by a direct calculation, we deduce from Lemmas
2.6
and
3.2
that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}&\leq\|B_{k}^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\sum_{\ell=k}^{n}\delta_{\ell}\leq CR_{k},\\
\|\nabla_{x}E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}&\leq\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\varepsilon_{j}^{-1}\|\nabla_{y_{j}}B_{k}^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\varepsilon_{j}^{-1}\delta_{k}^{n}\\
&\leq\exp\big\{C_{0}[\delta]_{1}\big\}\big\{1+C_{0}[\delta]_{0}[\delta]_{1}\big\}\cdot R_{k}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\varepsilon_{j}^{-1}\\
&=\Lambda_{0}R_{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\varepsilon_{j}^{-1},\\
\|\nabla_{x}A^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}}&\leq\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\varepsilon_{j}^{-1}\|\nabla_{y_{j}}A_{n}^{k}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\varepsilon_{j}^{-1}\sum_{\ell=j}^{n}\delta_{\ell}\leq C[\delta]_{0}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\varepsilon_{j}^{-1},\end{split}

where
R_{k}^{n}
and
R_{k}
are defined by (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) respectively,
C
depends only on
d
and
\mu
, and

\displaystyle\Lambda_{0}:=\exp\big\{C_{0}[\delta]_{1}\big\}\big\{1+C_{0}[\delta]_{0}[\delta]_{1}\big\}.

Observe that, during the one-step homogenization,
u_{n,\varepsilon}^{k}
converges to
u_{n,\varepsilon}^{k-1}
as
\varepsilon_{k}\rightarrow 0
. Applying Theorem
3.6
to this case, we have

(3.31)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|u_{n,\varepsilon}^{k}-u_{n,\varepsilon}^{k-1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\\
&\leq C\varepsilon_{k}\Big\{\|E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla_{x}E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\varepsilon_{k}\|\nabla_{x}E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\\
&\qquad+(\|E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\varepsilon_{k}\|E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla_{x}E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}})\|\nabla_{x}A^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}}\\
&\qquad+\varepsilon_{k}\|E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|\nabla_{x}A^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\Big\}\cdot\Big(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{H^{3/2}(\partial\Omega)}\Big)\\
&\leq C\varepsilon_{k}\bigg\{R_{k}+\Lambda_{0}R_{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\varepsilon_{j}^{-1}+\varepsilon_{k}\bigg(\Lambda_{0}R_{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\varepsilon_{j}^{-1}\bigg)^{2}\\
&\qquad+\bigg(R_{k}+\varepsilon_{k}\Lambda_{0}R_{k}^{2}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\varepsilon_{j}^{-1}\bigg)[\delta]_{0}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\varepsilon_{j}^{-1}+\varepsilon_{k}\bigg([\delta]_{0}R_{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\varepsilon_{j}^{-1}\bigg)^{2}\bigg\}\\
&\qquad\qquad\cdot\Big(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{H^{3/2}(\partial\Omega)}\Big)\\
&\leq C\bigg\{\Lambda R_{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{\varepsilon_{k}}{\varepsilon_{j}}+\bigg(\Lambda R_{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{\varepsilon_{k}}{\varepsilon_{j}}\bigg)^{2}\bigg\}\cdot\Big(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{H^{3/2}(\partial\Omega)}\Big),\end{split}

where

(3.32)

\displaystyle\Lambda=\Lambda_{0}+[\delta]_{0},

and
C
depends only on
d,\mu
and
\Omega
. The constant
\Lambda\geq 1
will be fixed throughout. Note that
u_{n,\varepsilon}^{n}=u_{n,\varepsilon}
and
u_{n,\varepsilon}^{0}=u_{n,0}
. On the other hand, by the Poincaré inequality and the energy estimate, we trivially have

(3.33)

\displaystyle\|u_{n,\varepsilon}^{k}-u_{n,\varepsilon}^{k-1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{H^{3/2}(\partial\Omega)}),

where
C
depends only on
d,\mu
and
\Omega
. Combining (
3.31
) and (
3.33
), and by considering
\Lambda R_{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{\varepsilon_{k}}{\varepsilon_{j}}\geq 1
and
\Lambda R_{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{\varepsilon_{k}}{\varepsilon_{j}}\leq 1
separately, the estimate (
3.31
) can be improved to

(3.34)

\displaystyle\|u_{n,\varepsilon}^{k}-u_{n,\varepsilon}^{k-1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\Lambda R_{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{\varepsilon_{k}}{\varepsilon_{j}}\cdot\Big(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{H^{3/2}(\partial\Omega)}\Big).

Summing (
3.34
) in
k
from
1
to
n
, we obtain that

(3.35)

\displaystyle\|u_{n,\varepsilon}-u_{n,0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|u_{n,\varepsilon}^{k}-u_{n,\varepsilon}^{k-1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Lambda R_{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{\varepsilon_{k}}{\varepsilon_{j}}\cdot\Big(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{H^{3/2}(\partial\Omega)}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C\Lambda[\delta]_{1}\sup_{1\leq k\leq n}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{\varepsilon_{k}}{\varepsilon_{j}}\cdot\Big(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{H^{3/2}(\partial\Omega)}\Big),

where we have used (
3.5
) with
\alpha=0
in the last step. It follows from (
2.18
) and (
2.20
) that
u_{n,\varepsilon}\rightarrow u_{\varepsilon}
and
u_{n,0}\rightarrow u_{0}
in
H^{1}(\Omega)
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Hence, taking the limit in (
3.35
) as
n\to\infty
, one gets

(3.36)

\displaystyle\|u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\Lambda[\delta]_{1}\sup_{k\geq 1}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{\varepsilon_{k}}{\varepsilon_{j}}\cdot\Big(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{H^{3/2}(\partial\Omega)}\Big).

Finally, we show that (
3.36
) implies the desired estimate. Let
\mathcal{E}:=\sup_{k\geq 1}\frac{\varepsilon_{k}}{\varepsilon_{k-1}}
. If
\mathcal{E}<1/2
, then
\varepsilon_{k}\leq\mathcal{E}^{k-j}\varepsilon_{j}
for any
k>j\geq 0
.
It follows that

\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{\varepsilon_{k}}{\varepsilon_{j}}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\mathcal{E}^{k-j}\leq\frac{\mathcal{E}}{1-\mathcal{E}}\leq 2\mathcal{E}.

which, together with (
3.36
), implies (
1.12
). Now if
\mathcal{E}\geq 1/2
, then (
1.12
) is a consequence of
H
-stability. In fact, by the observation
\widehat{A}_{0}=B_{0}
and Theorem
2.5
, we have

\displaystyle\|A^{\varepsilon}-\widehat{A}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|A^{\varepsilon}-B_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|B_{0}-\widehat{A}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\delta_{k}\leq C[\delta]_{1},

from which the energy estimate yields

\|u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\Lambda[\delta]_{1}\{\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega)}\}.

This concludes (
1.12
) for the case
\mathcal{E}\geq 1/2
and therefore ends the proof.
∎

3.4.
Suboptimal convergence rate in Lipschitz domains

To prove the uniform Lipschitz estimate, we establish a suboptimal algebraic convergence rate in a Lipschitz domain with less regular boundary data. Again, we begin with a one-step convergence rate.

Theorem 3.7
.

Suppose
\Omega
is a bounded Lipschitz domain. Let
u_{\varepsilon}
be the solution of equation (
3.6
) and
u_{0}
the solution of the homogenized equation (
3.8
). Then there exists
\sigma>0
, depending only on
\mu
and
\Omega
, such that

\displaystyle\|u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\big\{\varepsilon^{\sigma}\|E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\varepsilon\|\nabla_{x}E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\varepsilon\|E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla_{x}A\|_{L^{\infty}}\big\}

\displaystyle\qquad\times(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega)}),

where
C
depends only on
d,\mu
and
\Omega
.

Proof.

First of all, using the Poincaré inequality as well as (
3.30
), we derive from (
3.20
) that

(3.37)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\Big\{\|E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\big(\varepsilon\|\nabla^{2}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{3\varepsilon})}+\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{4\varepsilon})}\big)\\
&\qquad+\varepsilon\big(\|\nabla_{x}A\|_{L^{\infty}}\|E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla_{x}E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Big\}.\end{split}

The estimates for
\|\nabla^{2}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{3\varepsilon})}
and
\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{4\varepsilon})}
are more or less standard. We provide the details with emphasis on the dependence of
A
as required in the desired estimates.

According to Meyers’ estimate (see
[
Mey63
,
Gia83
]
), there exists
s>2
, depending only on
\mu
and
\Omega
, such that

(3.38)

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}\leq C(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega)}),

where
C
depends only on
\mu
and
\Omega
. By Hölder’s inequality, we have

(3.39)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{4\varepsilon})}&\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{s}}\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}\\
&\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{s}}\big\{\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega)}\big\}.\end{aligned}

By the interior
H^{2}
estimate for elliptic equations with Lipschitz coefficients, it holds that for any
x\in\Omega
,

\displaystyle\fint_{B(x,\omega(x)/8)}|\nabla^{2}u_{0}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big([\omega(x)]^{-2}+\|\nabla\widehat{A}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\Big)\fint_{B(x,\omega(x)/4)}|\nabla u_{0}|^{2}+C\fint_{B(x,\omega(x)/4)}|f|^{2},

where
\omega(x)=\mathrm{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)
. Integrating both sides of the above inequality in
x
over
\Omega\setminus\Omega_{3\varepsilon}
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{3\varepsilon}}|\nabla^{2}u_{0}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\varepsilon}}[\omega(y)]^{-2}|\nabla u_{0}|^{2}dy+C\|\nabla_{x}A\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{0}|^{2}+C\int_{\Omega}|f|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{0}|^{s}\Big)^{2/s}\Big(\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\varepsilon}}[\omega(y)]^{-\frac{2s}{s-2}}dy\Big)^{\frac{s-2}{s}}

\displaystyle\qquad+C\|\nabla_{x}A\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{0}|^{2}+C\int_{\Omega}|f|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{-1-\frac{2}{s}}\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\|\nabla_{x}A\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{0}|^{2}+C\int_{\Omega}|f|^{2},

where we have used (
3.24
) in the first step. Therefore,

(3.40)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \varepsilon\|\nabla^{2}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{3\varepsilon})}&\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{s}}\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}+C\varepsilon\|\nabla_{x}A\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\\
&\qquad+C\varepsilon\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\\
&\leq C(\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{s}}+\varepsilon\|\nabla_{x}A\|_{L^{\infty}})\big\{\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega)}\big\},\end{aligned}

where we have used (
3.38
).

Finally, substituting (
3.39
) and (
3.40
) into (
3.37
), we obtain the desired result with
\sigma=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{s}
.
∎

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain,
u_{\varepsilon}
and
u_{0}
be the solutions to (
1.1
) and (
1.9
), respectively.
Suppose that
\delta=\{\delta_{j}\}_{j\geq 0}
satisfies
[\delta]_{1}<\infty
. Then there exists
\sigma>0
, depending only on
\mu
and
\Omega
, such that for any
0<\alpha\leq 1
,

(3.41)

\displaystyle\|u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\Lambda[\delta]_{\alpha}\bigg(\sup_{k\geq 1}\frac{\varepsilon_{k}}{\varepsilon_{k-1}}\bigg)^{\alpha\sigma}\Big(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega)}\Big),

where
\Lambda
is defined by (
3.32
) and
C
depends only on
d,\mu
and
\Omega
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{E}=\sup_{k\geq 1}\frac{\varepsilon_{k}}{\varepsilon_{k-1}}
. As before, without loss of generality, assume
\mathcal{E}<1/2
. Then

\sup_{k\geq 1}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{\varepsilon_{k}}{\varepsilon_{j}}\leq 2\mathcal{E}.

Fix
\alpha\in(0,1]
. Let
m=\lfloor\mathcal{E}^{-\sigma}\rfloor
, where
\sigma\in(0,1)
is given as in Theorem
3.7
. First, note that

\|A^{\varepsilon}-A_{m}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\sum_{k=m+1}^{\infty}\delta_{k}\leq[\delta]_{\alpha}(m+1)^{-\alpha}\leq[\delta]_{\alpha}\mathcal{E}^{\alpha\sigma}.

By the stability of
H
-convergence in Lemma
2.3
, we have

\|\widehat{A}-\widehat{A}_{m}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C[\delta]_{\alpha}\mathcal{E}^{\alpha\sigma}.

Therefore, by the energy estimates, we obtain

\|u_{\varepsilon}-u_{m,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|u_{0}-u_{m,0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C[\delta]_{\alpha}\mathcal{E}^{\alpha\sigma}(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega)}).

Hence, to prove the desired estimate, it suffices to show

(3.42)

\|u_{m,\varepsilon}-u_{m,0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C[\delta]_{\alpha}\mathcal{E}^{\alpha\sigma}(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega)}).

To this end, let
1\leq k\leq m
. As in the proof of Theorem
1.2
, using Theorem
3.7
instead of Theorem
3.6
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\|u_{m,\varepsilon}^{k}-u_{m,\varepsilon}^{k-1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\varepsilon_{k}^{\sigma}\|E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\varepsilon_{k}\|\nabla_{x}E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\varepsilon_{k}\|E_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla_{x}A^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\times(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega)})

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg\{\varepsilon_{k}^{\sigma}R_{k}+\Lambda_{0}R_{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{\varepsilon_{k}}{\varepsilon_{j}}+R_{k}[\delta]_{0}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{\varepsilon_{k}}{\varepsilon_{j}}\bigg\}

\displaystyle\qquad\times(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega)})

\displaystyle\leq C\big\{R_{k}\mathcal{E}^{\sigma}+\Lambda R_{k}\mathcal{E}\big\}(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega)}).

Summing in
k
from
1
to
m
gives

\displaystyle\|u_{m,\varepsilon}-u_{m,0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\Lambda\mathcal{E}^{\sigma}\sum_{k=1}^{m}R_{k}\Big(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega)}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C\Lambda\mathcal{E}^{\sigma}[\delta]_{\alpha}m^{1-\alpha}\big(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega)}\big),

where we have used the fact

\sum_{k=1}^{m}R_{k}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\delta_{j}\sum_{k=1}^{\min\{m,j\}}1\leq[\delta]_{\alpha}m^{1-\alpha}.

In view of our choice of
m
, we obtain (
3.42
). Note that the constant
C
is independent of
\alpha
.
∎

Remark 3.9
.

By mimicking the proof of Theorem
3.8
, we can also show a similar result in
C^{1,1}
domains parallel to Theorem
1.2
. In fact, under the assumptions of Theorem
1.2
, for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
, we have

\quad\|u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\Lambda[\delta]_{\alpha}\bigg(\sup_{k\geq 1}\frac{\varepsilon_{k}}{\varepsilon_{k-1}}\bigg)^{\alpha}\cdot(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{H^{3/2}(\partial\Omega)}),

where
C
depends only on
d,\mu
and
\Omega
.

4.
Interior Lipschitz estimates

In this section we establish the interior Lipschitz estimates for
u_{\varepsilon}
. In the quantitative Campanato-type iteration, we approximate the solution by affine functions at any given scale with controlled errors. This involves the rescaling argument. Since we have infinitely many oscillating scales, we must ensure that certain properties of the coefficients are preserved when we zoom in from large scales to small scales.

4.1.
Rescaling

Let
u_{\varepsilon}
be a weak solution to

(4.1)

\displaystyle-\mathrm{div}(A^{\varepsilon}(x)\nabla u_{\varepsilon})=f\quad\text{in }B_{1},

where
B_{1}=B(0,1)
. We consider rescaling properties of the equation and show the rescaled equations have similar characters with universal upper bounds.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and we zoom in to
\varepsilon_{m}
-scale. Let
v_{m}(x)=u_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon_{m}x)
, which satisfies

\displaystyle-\mathrm{div}\Big(A\Big(\frac{\varepsilon_{m}x}{\varepsilon_{0}},\frac{\varepsilon_{m}x}{\varepsilon_{1}},\cdots,\frac{\varepsilon_{m}x}{\varepsilon_{m}},\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{m+1}/\varepsilon_{m}},\cdots\Big)\nabla v_{m}\Big)=\varepsilon_{m}^{2}f(\varepsilon_{m}x)\quad\text{in }B_{1/\varepsilon_{m}}.

Note that if
j\leq m
, then
\varepsilon_{m}/\varepsilon_{j}\leq 1
. Thus, in the rescaled coefficient matrix, the first
m+1
variables are all slow variables.
Relabel
\widetilde{y}_{j}=y_{j+m}
for
j\geq 1
and define

\displaystyle\widetilde{A}(x,\widetilde{y}_{1},\widetilde{y}_{2},\cdots)=A\Big(\frac{\varepsilon_{m}x}{\varepsilon_{0}},\frac{\varepsilon_{m}x}{\varepsilon_{1}},\cdots,\frac{\varepsilon_{m}x}{\varepsilon_{m}},\widetilde{y}_{1},\widetilde{y}_{2},\cdots\Big).

Then
\widetilde{A}
possesses the same structure as
A
, which can be written as

\displaystyle\widetilde{A}(x,\widetilde{y}_{1},\cdots)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\widetilde{B}_{\ell}(x,\widetilde{y}_{1},\cdots,\widetilde{y}_{\ell}),

where

\displaystyle\widetilde{B}_{0}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{m}B_{j}\Big(\frac{\varepsilon_{m}x}{\varepsilon_{0}},\frac{\varepsilon_{m}x}{\varepsilon_{1}},\cdots,\frac{\varepsilon_{m}x}{\varepsilon_{j}}\Big),

\displaystyle\widetilde{B}_{\ell}(x,\widetilde{y}_{1},\cdots,\widetilde{y}_{\ell})

\displaystyle=B_{\ell+m}\Big(\frac{\varepsilon_{m}x}{\varepsilon_{0}},\frac{\varepsilon_{m}x}{\varepsilon_{1}},\cdots,\frac{\varepsilon_{m}x}{\varepsilon_{m}},\widetilde{y}_{1},\cdots,\widetilde{y}_{\ell}\Big),

for
\ell\geq 1
. Denote also
\widetilde{\delta}_{\ell}:=\max\{\|\widetilde{B}_{\ell}\|_{L^{\infty}},\max_{0\leq j\leq\ell}\|\nabla_{\widetilde{y}_{j}}\widetilde{B}_{\ell}\|_{L^{\infty}}\}
. By
\widetilde{y}_{j}=y_{j+m}
and a direct computation, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{\delta}_{0}

\displaystyle\leq\max\bigg\{\sum_{\ell=0}^{m}\delta_{\ell},\sum_{\ell=0}^{m}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\frac{\varepsilon_{m}}{\varepsilon_{j}}\delta_{\ell}\bigg\}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{m}\frac{\varepsilon_{m}}{\varepsilon_{j}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{m}\delta_{\ell},

\displaystyle\widetilde{\delta}_{\ell}

\displaystyle\leq\max\bigg\{\delta_{\ell+m},\sum_{j=0}^{m}\frac{\varepsilon_{m}}{\varepsilon_{j}}\delta_{\ell+m}\bigg\}=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\frac{\varepsilon_{m}}{\varepsilon_{j}}\delta_{\ell+m}\quad\text{for }\ell\geq 1.

Therefore,

(4.2)

\displaystyle[\widetilde{\delta}]_{0}:=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\widetilde{\delta}_{k}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{m}\frac{\varepsilon_{m}}{\varepsilon_{j}}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\delta_{k}+\sum_{j=0}^{m}\frac{\varepsilon_{m}}{\varepsilon_{j}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\delta_{k+m}=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\frac{\varepsilon_{m}}{\varepsilon_{j}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\delta_{k},

and for
\alpha>0

(4.3)

\displaystyle[\widetilde{\delta}]_{\alpha}:=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{\alpha}\widetilde{\delta}_{k}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{m}\frac{\varepsilon_{m}}{\varepsilon_{j}}\cdot\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{\alpha}\delta_{k+m}.

Note that
\widetilde{A},\widetilde{B}_{\ell}
and
\widetilde{\delta}_{\ell}
depend on
m
.

In the sequel, we impose a convenient scale-invariant condition for later calculation: there exists some fixed
K\in(1,2]
such that

(4.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\varepsilon_{m}}{\varepsilon_{j}}\leq K\quad\text{for each }m\in\mathbb{N}_{+},

The constant
K
will be chosen close to
1
later.
It can be seen from (
4.2
)–(
4.3
) that for each
\alpha\geq 0
,
[\widetilde{\delta}]_{\alpha}
has a universal upper bound independent of
m
, i.e.,

(4.5)

\displaystyle[\widetilde{\delta}]_{\alpha}\leq(K+1)[\delta]_{\alpha}.

Moreover, the approximate matrix
\widetilde{A}_{n}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}\widetilde{B}_{\ell}
of
n
microscopic scales obviously satisfies condition (
1.4
)–(
1.6
).

From this perspective, if an estimate holds for
u_{\varepsilon}
with constants depending on the characters of
A
, then the same estimate is also valid for
v_{m}
with new constants depending on the corresponding characters of
\widetilde{A}
. Crucially, we are able to show that the constants in the estimates from Section
4.3
depend monotonically on
[\delta]_{\alpha}
. Since
[\widetilde{\delta}]_{\alpha}
admits a uniform upper bound via (
4.5
), this allows us to establish a series of estimates for
v_{m}
that are uniform in
m
.

As a preparation, we show that the condition (
4.4
) implies the exponential decay of
\varepsilon_{k}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
1=\varepsilon_{0}>\varepsilon_{1}>\varepsilon_{2}>\cdots
be a sequence of positive numbers. Suppose (
4.4
) is fulfilled for
m\geq 1
. Then, for
1\leq k\leq m
,

(4.6)

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon_{m}}{\varepsilon_{k}}\leq K(1-K^{-1})^{m-k}.

Proof.

By considering instead the sequence
1=\varepsilon_{k-1}/\varepsilon_{k-1}>\varepsilon_{k}/\varepsilon_{k-1}>\cdots
, which still satisfies

\displaystyle\sum_{j=k}^{m}\frac{\varepsilon_{m}/\varepsilon_{k-1}}{\varepsilon_{j}/\varepsilon_{k-1}}=\sum_{j=k}^{m}\frac{\varepsilon_{m}}{\varepsilon_{j}}\leq K\quad\text{for each }m\geq k,

it is sufficient to focus on the case
k=1
.

According to (
4.4
), it holds
\varepsilon_{m}^{-1}\geq K^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{j}^{-1}
. Thus, for each
m\geq 2

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{j}^{-1}-\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\varepsilon_{j}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{m}^{-1}\geq K^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{j}^{-1},

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{j}^{-1}

\displaystyle\geq(1-K^{-1})^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\varepsilon_{j}^{-1}.

By iteration, we deduce that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{j}^{-1}\geq(1-K^{-1})^{1-m}\varepsilon_{1}^{-1},

which implies

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{m}\leq K\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{j}^{-1}\bigg)^{-1}\leq K(1-K^{-1})^{m-1}\varepsilon_{1}.

The case
m=1
is trivial.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

Conversely, the decay of
\varepsilon_{k}
in (
4.6
) also implies the uniform boundedness of
\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{\varepsilon_{m}}{\varepsilon_{k}}
. In fact, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{\varepsilon_{m}}{\varepsilon_{k}}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{m}K(1-K^{-1})^{m-k}\leq K^{2}.

Furthermore, for
m\geq k

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=m}^{\infty}\frac{\varepsilon_{\ell}}{\varepsilon_{k}}\leq K\sum_{\ell=m}^{\infty}(1-K^{-1})^{\ell-k}\leq K^{2}(1-K^{-1})^{m-k}.

4.2.
Approximation

As a preliminary step, we establish an approximate result for
u_{\varepsilon}
by
u_{0}
based on the suboptimal convergence rate in Theorem
3.8
, following an approach used in
[
NXZar
, Lemma 6.1]
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
u_{\varepsilon}
be a weak solution of (
4.1
) with
f\in L^{2}(B_{1})
. Assume
[\delta]_{1}<\infty
. Then for each
r\in[\varepsilon_{1},1/2]
, there exists a weak solution
u_{0}
to the equation

-\mathrm{div}(\widehat{A}\nabla u_{0})=f\quad\text{ in }B_{r},

such that for any
0<\alpha\leq 1
,

\displaystyle\bigg(\fint_{B_{r}}|u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\Lambda[\delta]_{\alpha}\bigg(\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{r}+\sup_{k\geq 2}\frac{\varepsilon_{k}}{\varepsilon_{k-1}}\bigg)^{\alpha\sigma}

\displaystyle\quad\times\bigg\{\bigg(\fint_{B_{2r}}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+r^{2}\bigg(\fint_{B_{2r}}|f|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg\},

where
\Lambda
is given by (
3.32
), and
\sigma
and
C
depend only on
d
and
\mu
.

Proof.

Since the desired estimate is scale-invariant for
r\in[\varepsilon_{1},1/2]
, without loss of generality, we can assume
r=1/2
. Indeed, by setting
\widetilde{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon/r,\widetilde{A}(y_{0},y_{1},\cdots)=A(ry_{0},y_{1},\cdots)
and
v_{\widetilde{\varepsilon}}(x)=u_{\varepsilon}(rx)
for
r\in[\varepsilon_{1},1/2]
, we have

-\mathrm{div}(\widetilde{A}^{\widetilde{\varepsilon}}(x)\nabla v_{\widetilde{\varepsilon}})=\widetilde{f}\quad\text{in }B_{1},

where
\widetilde{f}(x)=r^{2}f(rx)
. It is not hard to see that

\widetilde{A}(y_{0},y_{1},\cdots)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}B_{\ell}(ry_{0},y_{1},\cdots,y_{\ell})

and each term satisfies the condition (
1.10
) with the same bound
\delta_{\ell}
. This reduces the problem to the case
r=1/2
.

For
(1/2)<t<(3/4)
, let
u_{0}^{t}
be the weak solution to

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\mathrm{div}(\widehat{A}(x)\nabla u_{0}^{t})=f&\text{in }B_{t}\\
u_{0}^{t}=u_{\varepsilon}&\text{on }\partial B_{t}.\end{cases}

By Theorem
3.8
,

(4.7)

\displaystyle\|u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}^{t}\|_{L^{2}(B_{1/2})}\leq C\Lambda[\delta]_{\alpha}\Big(\sup_{k\geq 1}\frac{\varepsilon_{k}}{\varepsilon_{k-1}}\Big)^{\alpha\sigma}\Big\{\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{1}(\partial B_{t})}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(B_{t})}\Big\}.

Let

\displaystyle u_{0}=4\int_{1/2}^{3/4}u_{0}^{t}dt.

Note that
u_{0}
satisfies the homogenized equation

-\mathrm{div}(\widehat{A}(x)\nabla u_{0})=f\quad\text{in }B_{1/2}.

It follows from (
4.7
) and an integration in
t
that

\displaystyle\|u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(B_{1/2})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4\int_{1/2}^{3/4}\|u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}^{t}\|_{L^{2}(B_{1/2})}^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq C^{2}\Lambda^{2}[\delta]_{\alpha}^{2}\Big(\sup_{k\geq 1}\frac{\varepsilon_{k}}{\varepsilon_{k-1}}\Big)^{2\alpha\sigma}\int_{1/2}^{3/4}\Big(\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{1}(\partial B_{t})}^{2}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(B_{t})}^{2}\Big)dt

\displaystyle\leq C^{2}\Lambda^{2}[\delta]_{\alpha}^{2}\Big(\sup_{k\geq 1}\frac{\varepsilon_{k}}{\varepsilon_{k-1}}\Big)^{2\alpha\sigma}\Big(\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{1}(B_{3/4})}^{2}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C^{2}\Lambda^{2}[\delta]_{\alpha}^{2}\Big(\sup_{k\geq 1}\frac{\varepsilon_{k}}{\varepsilon_{k-1}}\Big)^{2\alpha\sigma}\Big(\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}^{2}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}^{2}\Big),

where we have used the co-area formula in the third inequality and the Caccioppoli inequality in the last inequality.
∎

4.3.
Large-scale estimates

In this subsection, we establish the large-scale estimates for
u_{\varepsilon}
at scales
between
\varepsilon_{1}
and
1
. This is a one-step result that will be iterated to all scales.

Let
u_{\varepsilon}\in H^{1}(B_{1})
be a solution to
-\mathrm{div}(A^{\varepsilon}(x)\nabla u_{\varepsilon})=f
in
B_{1}
, where
f\in L^{p}(B_{1})
for some
p>d
. For
0<r\leq 1
, define

\displaystyle H(r;u_{\varepsilon})=\frac{1}{r}\inf_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\bigg(\fint_{B_{r}}|u_{\varepsilon}-P|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+r\bigg(\fint_{B_{r}}|f|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p},

where
\mathcal{P}
denotes the linear space of affine functions.
Let
P_{r}
(minimizer) be the affine function achieving the infimum in
H(r;u_{\varepsilon})
, i.e.,

\displaystyle H(r;u_{\varepsilon})=\frac{1}{r}\bigg(\fint_{B_{r}}|u_{\varepsilon}-P_{r}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+r\bigg(\fint_{B_{r}}|f|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p},

and set

\displaystyle h(r;u_{\varepsilon})=|\nabla P_{r}|.

For the sake of brevity, in the sequel we may omit the identifier
u_{\varepsilon}
in
H(r;u_{\varepsilon})
and
h(r;u_{\varepsilon})
if it causes no confusion.

Theorem 4.4
.

Suppose that
[\delta]_{1}<\infty
and
0<\alpha\leq 1
. There exists a constant
c_{0}\in(0,1)
, depending on
d,\mu,p,[\delta]_{0}
and
[\delta]_{1}
, such that if for any
k\geq 2

(4.8)

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{\varepsilon_{k}}{\varepsilon_{k-1}}\bigg)^{\alpha\sigma}\leq c_{0}|\log\varepsilon_{1}|^{-1},

then for
r\in[\varepsilon_{1},1]
,

\displaystyle H(r;u_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}r^{\lambda}H(1;u_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\quad+CM_{0}\bigg[\Big(\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{r}\Big)^{\alpha\sigma}+r^{\lambda}+\sup_{k\geq 2}\bigg(\frac{\varepsilon_{k}}{\varepsilon_{k-1}}\bigg)^{\alpha\sigma}\bigg]\Big\{H(1;u_{\varepsilon})+h(1;u_{\varepsilon})\Big\},

where

(4.9)

M_{0}:=\max\bigg\{[\delta]_{\alpha},{\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|\nabla_{y_{0}}B_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}}}\bigg\},

\sigma
is given in Theorem
4.3
,
\lambda\in(0,1)
,
C\geq C_{0}\geq 1
. Furthermore, the constants
\lambda
and
C_{0}
depend only on
d,\mu,p
and
[\delta]_{0}
, and
C
depends additionally on
\alpha,[\delta]_{1}
.

Remark 4.5
.

It is important to keep track of the dependence of the constants on
[\delta]_{0}
and
[\delta]_{1}

Corollary 4.6
.

Under the same assumptions as in Theorem

\displaystyle H(r;u_{\varepsilon})\leq C_{0}r^{\lambda}H(1;u_{\varepsilon})+CM_{0}\{H(1;u_{\varepsilon})+h(1;u_{\varepsilon})\},

where

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
H(r)
and

be two nonnegative continuous functions on the interval
(0,1]
and let
\kappa\in(0,1/4)
. Assume that

(4.18)

\displaystyle\max_{r\leq t\leq 2r}H(t)\leq C_{0}H(2r),~~~~~\max_{r\leq t,s\leq 2r}|h(t)-h(s)|\leq C_{0}H(2r),

for any

, and for some fixed

,

(4.19)

\displaystyle H(\theta r)\leq\frac{1}{2}H(r)+C_{0}\big(\omega_{1}(\kappa/r)+\omega_{2}(r)+\gamma\big)\{H(2r)+h(2r)\},

for any

, where

\omega_{1},\omega_{2}
are nonnegative increasing functions on

\omega_{i}(0)=0

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\omega_{i}(s)}{s}ds<\infty.

Then there exists a small constant

decreasingly, such that if

, we have

\displaystyle\max_{\kappa\leq r\leq 1}\{H(r)+h(r)\}\leq C\{H(1)+h(1)\},

where
C
depends only on
C_{0},\theta
, and
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}
.

Remark 4.8
.

The constant
C
is increasing in
C_{0}
and decreasing in
\theta
.

Proof.

The lemma can be proved by a combination of
[
NXZar
, Lemma 6.2]
and
[
GZ22
, Lemma 6.7]
.

By the second inequality in (
4.18
),

\displaystyle h(r)\leq h(2r)+C_{0}H(2r)

for
\kappa\leq r\leq 1/2
, from which we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{1/2}\frac{h(r))}{r}dr\leq\int_{2s}^{1}\frac{h(r)}{r}dr+C_{0}\int_{2s}^{1}\frac{H(r)}{r}dr

for

. This implies that

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{2s}\frac{h(r)}{r}dr

\displaystyle\leq\int_{1/2}^{1}\frac{h(r)}{r}dr+C_{0}\int_{2s}^{1}\frac{H(r)}{r}dr

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}(\log 2)[h(1)+H(1)]+C_{0}\int_{2s}^{1}\frac{H(r)}{r}dr.

By this estimate and the second inequality in (
4.18
), it follows that for
\kappa\leq s\leq 1/4

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} h(s)&\leq(\log 2)^{-1}\int_{s}^{2s}\frac{|h(r)-h(s)|+h(r)}{r}dr\\

Moreover, by the first inequality in (

\displaystyle H(2s)

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}(\log 2)^{-1}\int_{2s}^{1}\frac{H(r)}{r}dr,\qquad\text{if }\kappa\leq s\leq 1/8,

\displaystyle H(2s)

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}^{2}H(1),\qquad\text{if }1/8\leq s\leq 1/4,

which, together with (

\displaystyle H(s)+h(s)\leq C_{1}\bigg\{h(1)+H(1)+\int_{s}^{1}\frac{H(r)}{r}dr\bigg\}

for

, where

depends only on
C_{0}
increasingly. Combined with (
4.19
), this implies that

\displaystyle H(\theta r)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}H(r)+C_{2}(\omega_{1}(\kappa/r)+\omega_{2}(r)+\gamma)\{h(1)+H(1)\}

\displaystyle\qquad+C_{2}(\omega_{1}(\kappa/r)+\omega_{2}(r)+\gamma)\int_{r}^{1}\frac{H(t)}{t}dt,

where
C_{2}
depends only on
C_{0}
increasingly. Dividing this inequality by
r

\displaystyle\int_{a\theta\kappa}^{b\theta}\frac{H(r)}{r}dr

\displaystyle\qquad+C_{2}\int_{a\kappa}^{b}\frac{\omega_{1}(\kappa/r)+\omega_{2}(r)+\gamma}{r}dr\int_{r}^{1}\frac{H(t)}{t}dt.

and

Lemma 4.10

Assume

Proof.

Clearly, by (
4.24

(4.25)

\sum_{\ell=m}^{\infty}M_{\ell}\leq\sum_{\ell=m}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\frac{\varepsilon_{\ell}}{\varepsilon_{j}}\sum_{k=j}^{\infty}\delta_{k}+\sum_{\ell=m}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\frac{\varepsilon_{\ell}}{\varepsilon_{j}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{\alpha}\delta_{k+\ell}.

We estimate the two terms on the right-hand side separately.

By condition (
4.4
), we have

\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}}\leq\frac{3}{\varepsilon_{k}},

where we have used the fact
K\leq 2
.
It follows that

(4.26)

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=m}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\frac{\varepsilon_{\ell}}{\varepsilon_{j}}\sum_{k=j}^{\infty}\delta_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=m}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{\varepsilon_{\ell}}{\varepsilon_{j}}\delta_{k}+\sum_{\ell=m}^{\infty}\sum_{k=\ell+1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\frac{\varepsilon_{\ell}}{\varepsilon_{j}}\delta_{k}

\displaystyle\leq 3\sum_{\ell=m}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}\frac{\varepsilon_{\ell}}{\varepsilon_{k}}\delta_{k}+3\sum_{k=m+1}^{\infty}(k-1)\delta_{k}.

By Lemma
4.1
and Remark
4.2
, we calculate that for
m\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=m}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}\frac{\varepsilon_{\ell}}{\varepsilon_{k}}\delta_{k}=\sum_{\ell=m}^{\infty}\varepsilon_{\ell}\delta_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\sum_{\ell=m}^{\infty}\frac{\varepsilon_{\ell}}{\varepsilon_{k}}\delta_{k}+\sum_{k=m}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell=k}^{\infty}\frac{\varepsilon_{\ell}}{\varepsilon_{k}}\delta_{k}

\displaystyle\leq 9\bigg\{[\delta]_{0}(1-K^{-1})^{m-1}+\max\{m^{-1},(1-K^{-1})^{m-1}\}\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}k\delta_{k}

\displaystyle+m^{-1}\sum_{k=m}^{\infty}k\delta_{k}\bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq 9([\delta]_{0}+[\delta]_{1})\max\{m^{-1},(1-K^{-1})^{m-1}\}.

On the other hand, by the assumption

\sum_{k=m+1}^{\infty}(k-1)\delta_{k}\leq\frac{1}{m^{\rho}}[\delta]_{1+\rho}.

Bringing the last two displayed inequalities back into (
4.26
), we obtain

\sum_{\ell=m}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\frac{\varepsilon_{\ell}}{\varepsilon_{j}}\sum_{k=j}^{\infty}\delta_{k}\leq 27([\delta]_{0}+[\delta]_{1})\max\{m^{-1},(1-K^{-1})^{m-1}\}+\frac{3}{m^{\rho}}[\delta]_{1+\rho}.

Next, we turn to the second term on the right-hand side of (
4.25
). By using
[\delta]_{1+\rho}<\infty
and
\alpha=\rho/2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=m}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\frac{\varepsilon_{\ell}}{\varepsilon_{j}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{\alpha}\delta_{k+\ell}\leq 3\sum_{\ell=m}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{\alpha}\delta_{k+\ell}

\displaystyle\leq 3\sum_{\ell=m}^{\infty}\sum_{k=\ell+1}^{\infty}k^{\alpha}\delta_{k}=3\sum_{k=m+1}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell=m}^{k-1}k^{\alpha}\delta_{k}

\displaystyle\leq 3\sum_{k=m+1}^{\infty}k^{1+\alpha}\delta_{k}\leq\frac{3}{m^{\rho-\alpha}}\sum_{k=m+1}^{\infty}k^{1+\rho}\delta_{k}\leq\frac{3}{m^{\rho/2}}[\delta]_{1+\rho}.

As a result, we get

\sum_{\ell=m}^{\infty}M_{\ell}\leq 27([\delta]_{0}+[\delta]_{1})\max\{m^{-1},(1-K^{-1})^{m-1}\}+\frac{6}{m^{\rho/2}}[\delta]_{1+\rho}.

Obviously, the right-hand side of the above inequality converges to zero as
m\to\infty
and there exists
m_{0}
, depending on
\rho,T,[\delta]_{0}
and
[\delta]_{1+\rho}
such that
\sum_{\ell=m_{0}}^{\infty}M_{\ell}\leq T^{-1}
. This ends the proof.
∎

Now we are ready to prove our main theorems in this section.

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
c_{0}
be given by Lemma
4.9
. Suppose
[\delta]_{1+\rho}<\infty
for some
\rho>0
and the conditions (
4.4
) and (
4.23
) hold with
\alpha=\rho/2
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
. There exists
K_{0}\in(1,2]
, depending only on
d,\mu,p
and
[\delta]_{0}
, such that if
1<K\leq K_{0}
, then for any
r\in(0,1]
,

\displaystyle H(r;u_{\varepsilon})+h(r;u_{\varepsilon})\leq C\{H(1;u_{\varepsilon})+h(1;u_{\varepsilon})\},

where
C
depends on
d,\mu,p,[\delta]_{0},\rho
and
[\delta]_{1+\rho}
.

Proof.

Let
C_{1}
be given by Lemma
4.9
. By Lemma
4.10
, there exists
m_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(4.27)

\displaystyle 64C_{1}^{2}\sum_{\ell=m_{0}}^{\infty}M_{\ell}\leq 1,

and therefore,

(4.28)

\displaystyle 64C_{1}^{2}M_{\ell}\leq 1\quad\text{for all }\ell\geq m_{0}.

We claim that for
\ell\geq m_{0}\geq 0

(4.29)

\displaystyle\begin{split}H(\varepsilon_{\ell+1})&\leq C_{0}^{\ell-m_{0}+1}\Big(\frac{\varepsilon_{\ell+1}}{\varepsilon_{m_{0}}}\Big)^{\lambda}H(\varepsilon_{m_{0}})\\
&\qquad+\sum_{j=m_{0}}^{\ell}C_{0}^{\ell-j}\Big(\frac{\varepsilon_{\ell+1}}{\varepsilon_{j+1}}\Big)^{\lambda}C_{1}M_{j}\{H(\varepsilon_{j})+h(\varepsilon_{j})\}.\end{split}

This can be proved by induction on
\ell
. The case
\ell=m_{0}
is given by Lemma
4.9
. Suppose that it is true for
\ell+1
. Then, by Lemma
4.9
and using the inductive hypothesis, we have

\displaystyle H(\varepsilon_{\ell+2})

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\Big(\frac{\varepsilon_{\ell+2}}{\varepsilon_{\ell+1}}\Big)^{\lambda}H(\varepsilon_{\ell+1})+C_{1}M_{\ell+1}\{H(\varepsilon_{\ell+1})+h(\varepsilon_{\ell+1})\}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\Big(\frac{\varepsilon_{\ell+2}}{\varepsilon_{\ell+1}}\Big)^{\lambda}\bigg\{C_{0}^{\ell-m_{0}+1}\Big(\frac{\varepsilon_{\ell+1}}{\varepsilon_{m_{0}}}\Big)^{\lambda}H(\varepsilon_{m_{0}})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sum_{j=m_{0}}^{\ell}C_{0}^{\ell-j}\Big(\frac{\varepsilon_{\ell+1}}{\varepsilon_{j+1}}\Big)^{\lambda}C_{1}M_{j}\big\{H(\varepsilon_{j})+h(\varepsilon_{j})\big\}\bigg\}

\displaystyle\qquad+C_{1}M_{\ell+1}\{H(\varepsilon_{\ell+1})+h(\varepsilon_{\ell+1})\}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}^{\ell-m_{0}+2}\Big(\frac{\varepsilon_{\ell+2}}{\varepsilon_{m_{0}}}\Big)^{\lambda}H(\varepsilon_{m_{0}})

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{j=m_{0}}^{\ell+1}C_{0}^{\ell+1-j}\Big(\frac{\varepsilon_{\ell+2}}{\varepsilon_{j+1}}\Big)^{\lambda}C_{1}M_{j}\{H(\varepsilon_{j})+h(\varepsilon_{j})\},

which is exactly the claim.

Taking the summation of (
4.29
) from
m_{0}
to
k>m_{0}
, we obtain by Lemma
4.1
that

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=m_{0}}^{k}H(\varepsilon_{\ell+1})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell=m_{0}}^{k}C_{0}^{\ell-m_{0}+1}\Big(\frac{\varepsilon_{\ell+1}}{\varepsilon_{m_{0}}}\Big)^{\lambda}H(\varepsilon_{m_{0}})

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{\ell=m_{0}}^{k}\sum_{j=m_{0}}^{\ell}C_{0}^{\ell-j}\Big(\frac{\varepsilon_{\ell+1}}{\varepsilon_{j+1}}\Big)^{\lambda}C_{1}M_{j}\{H(\varepsilon_{j})+h(\varepsilon_{j})\}

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{0}(1-K^{-1})^{\lambda}H(\varepsilon_{m_{0}})\sum_{\ell={m_{0}}}^{k}[C_{0}(1-K^{-1})^{\lambda}]^{\ell-m_{0}}

\displaystyle\quad+2\sum_{j=m_{0}}^{k}C_{1}M_{j}\{H(\varepsilon_{j})+h(\varepsilon_{j})\}\sum_{\ell=j}^{k}[C_{0}(1-K^{-1})^{\lambda}]^{\ell-j}

\displaystyle\leq 2H(\varepsilon_{m_{0}})+\sum_{\ell=m_{0}}^{k}4C_{1}M_{\ell}\{H(\varepsilon_{\ell})+h(\varepsilon_{\ell})\},

where in the last step we have required
1<K\leq K_{0}
and
K_{0}\in(1,2]
is close to
1
such that

(4.30)

\displaystyle C_{0}(1-K_{0}^{-1})^{\lambda}\leq 1/2.

Therefore, by (
4.28
),

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=m_{0}}^{k+1}H(\varepsilon_{\ell})

\displaystyle=H(\varepsilon_{m_{0}})+\sum_{\ell=m_{0}}^{k}H(\varepsilon_{\ell+1})

\displaystyle\leq 3H(\varepsilon_{m_{0}})+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\ell={m_{0}}}^{k}H(\varepsilon_{\ell})+\sum_{\ell=m_{0}}^{k}4C_{1}M_{\ell}h(\varepsilon_{\ell}),

which leads to

(4.31)

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=m_{0}}^{k+1}H(\varepsilon_{\ell})\leq 6H(\varepsilon_{m_{0}})+\sum_{\ell=m_{0}}^{k}8C_{1}M_{\ell}h(\varepsilon_{\ell}).

On the other hand, using the estimate of
h
in Lemma
4.9
, we have for
k\geq m_{0}
,

\displaystyle|h(\varepsilon_{k+1})-h(\varepsilon_{m_{0}})|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell=m_{0}}^{k}|h(\varepsilon_{\ell+1})-h(\varepsilon_{\ell})|

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\sum_{\ell=m_{0}}^{k}H(\varepsilon_{\ell})+\sum_{\ell=m_{0}}^{k}C_{1}M_{\ell}\{H(\varepsilon_{\ell})+h(\varepsilon_{\ell})\}

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{1}\sum_{\ell=m_{0}}^{k}H(\varepsilon_{\ell})+\sum_{\ell=m_{0}}^{k}C_{1}M_{\ell}h(\varepsilon_{\ell}),

where we have used the fact
M_{\ell}\leq 1
for
\ell\geq m_{0}
derived from (
4.28
). Substituting (
4.31
) into the inequality above and using (
4.27
), this gives

\displaystyle h(\varepsilon_{k+1})

\displaystyle\leq h(\varepsilon_{m_{0}})+12C_{1}H(\varepsilon_{m_{0}})+\sum_{\ell=m_{0}}^{k}16C_{1}^{2}M_{\ell}h(\varepsilon_{\ell})+\sum_{\ell=m_{0}}^{k}C_{1}M_{\ell}h(\varepsilon_{\ell})

\displaystyle\leq h(\varepsilon_{m_{0}})+12C_{1}H(\varepsilon_{m_{0}})+\frac{1}{2}\sup_{m_{0}\leq\ell\leq k}h(\varepsilon_{\ell}).

Taking the supremum over
k
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{m_{0}\leq\ell\leq k}h(\varepsilon_{\ell})\leq h(\varepsilon_{m_{0}})+12C_{1}H(\varepsilon_{m_{0}})+\frac{1}{2}\sup_{m_{0}\leq\ell\leq k}h(\varepsilon_{\ell}),

which implies,

\displaystyle\sup_{\ell\geq m_{0}}h(\varepsilon_{\ell})

\displaystyle\leq 2\{h(\varepsilon_{m_{0}})+12C_{1}H(\varepsilon_{m_{0}})\}.

Combining this estimate with (
4.31
) and (
4.27
), and taking
k\to\infty
, we also have

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=m_{0}}^{\infty}H(\varepsilon_{\ell})\leq 6H(\varepsilon_{m_{0}})+\sum_{\ell=m_{0}}^{\infty}8C_{1}M_{\ell}h(\varepsilon_{\ell})\leq h(\varepsilon_{m_{0}})+8C_{1}H(\varepsilon_{m_{0}}).

Consequently, we conclude that

(4.32)

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=m_{0}}^{\infty}H(\varepsilon_{\ell})+\sup_{\ell\geq m_{0}}h(\varepsilon_{\ell})\leq C\{h(\varepsilon_{m_{0}})+H(\varepsilon_{m_{0}})\},

where
C
depends only on
C_{1}
.

Furthermore, using the estimates in Lemma
4.9
recursively, it holds for
1\leq\ell\leq m_{0}

(4.33)

\displaystyle H(\varepsilon_{\ell})+h(\varepsilon_{\ell})\leq C\{H(\varepsilon_{\ell-1})+h(\varepsilon_{\ell-1})\}\leq\cdots\leq C^{\ell}\{H(1)+h(1)\},

where
C
depends only on
d,\mu,p,\rho,[\delta]_{0}
and
[\delta]_{1}
. According to Lemma
4.1
, we know
\varepsilon_{k}\rightarrow 0
as
k\rightarrow\infty
. This means that for any
r\in(0,1]
there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\varepsilon_{k+1}<r\leq\varepsilon_{k}
. Using Lemma
4.9
again, we have

(4.34)

\displaystyle H(r)+h(r)\leq C\{H(\varepsilon_{k})+h(\varepsilon_{k})\}.

Combining (
4.32
), (
4.33
) and (
4.34
), we finally obtain

\displaystyle H(r)+h(r)\leq C\{H(1)+h(1)\}\quad\text{for all }r\in(0,1),

where
C
also depends on
m_{0}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.12
.

The estimate (
4.32
) actually implies a stronger result:

\lim_{r\to 0}H(r)\to 0,

which reflects the flatness of the solution
u_{\varepsilon}
, beyond the boundedness of
\nabla u_{\varepsilon}
.

Theorem 4.13
.

Let
c_{0},K_{0}
be given in Theorem
4.11
. Suppose
[\delta]_{1+\rho}<\infty
for some
\rho>0
and the conditions (
4.4
) and (
4.23
) hold with
\alpha=\rho/2
for each
m\geq 0
. Let
u_{\varepsilon}
be a solution to
-\mathrm{div}(A^{\varepsilon}(x)\nabla u_{\varepsilon})=f
in
B_{R}
,
0<R\leq 1
and
f\in L^{p}(B_{R})
with
p>d
. Then if
K\leq K_{0}
,

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R/2})}\leq C\bigg\{\bigg(\fint_{B_{R}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+R\bigg(\fint_{B_{R}}|f|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}\bigg\},

where
C
depends only on
d,\mu,p,[\delta]_{0},\rho
and
[\delta]_{1+\rho}
.

Proof.

By translation and dilation, we only need to prove the bound of
|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(0)|
with
R=1
. In fact, since
\varepsilon_{k}\rightarrow 0
as
k\rightarrow\infty
, for any
R\in(0,1)
, there exists
m
such that
\varepsilon_{m+1}<R\leq\varepsilon_{m}
. By setting
v(x)=u_{\varepsilon}(Rx)
, we obtain

\displaystyle-\mathrm{div}\Big(A\Big(\frac{Rx}{\varepsilon_{0}},\cdots,\frac{Rx}{\varepsilon_{m}},\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{m+1}/R},\cdots\Big)\nabla v\Big)=R^{2}f(Rx)\quad\text{in }B_{1}.

By a similar argument as Section
4.1
, it is not hard to verify that all the conditions keep fulfilled for this rescaled equation.

Now assume
R=1
. For any
0<r\leq 1
, by the Caccioppoli inequality, we have

(4.35)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \bigg(\fint_{B_{r/2}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}&\leq C\inf_{q\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{r}\bigg(\fint_{B_{r}}|u_{\varepsilon}-q|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+Cr\bigg(\fint_{B_{r}}|f|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}\\
&\leq C\{H(r;u_{\varepsilon})+h(r;u_{\varepsilon})\},\end{aligned}

where
C
depends only on
d
and
\mu
. On the other hand, observing that

\displaystyle h(r;u_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=|\nabla P_{r}|=\frac{C}{r}\inf_{q\in\mathbb{R}}\bigg(\fint_{B_{r}}|P_{r}-q|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{r}\bigg(\fint_{B_{r}}|u_{\varepsilon}-P_{r}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+\frac{C}{r}\inf_{q\in\mathbb{R}}\bigg(\fint_{B_{r}}|u_{\varepsilon}-q|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{r}\inf_{q\in\mathbb{R}}\bigg(\fint_{B_{r}}|u_{\varepsilon}-q|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2},

we obtain from the Poincaré inequality that

(4.36)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} H(r;u_{\varepsilon})+h(r;u_{\varepsilon})&\leq\frac{C}{r}\inf_{q\in\mathbb{R}}\bigg(\fint_{B_{r}}|u_{\varepsilon}-q|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+Cr\bigg(\fint_{B_{r}}|f|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}\\
&\leq C\bigg(\fint_{B_{r}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+Cr\bigg(\fint_{B_{r}}|f|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}.\end{aligned}

Combining (
4.35
) and (
4.36
), as well as Theorem
4.11
, we know

\displaystyle\bigg(\fint_{B_{r/2}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\{H(r;u_{\varepsilon})+h(r;u_{\varepsilon})\}

\displaystyle\leq C\{H(1;u_{\varepsilon})+h(1;u_{\varepsilon})\}

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg\{\bigg(\fint_{B_{1}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+\bigg(\fint_{B_{1}}|f|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}\bigg\}.

Since this estimate holds for any
r\in(0,1)
, by
letting
r\rightarrow 0
, we obtain

\displaystyle|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(0)|\leq C\bigg\{\bigg(\fint_{B_{1}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+\bigg(\fint_{B_{1}}|f|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}\bigg\}.

This is the desired estimate.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

By Theorem
4.13
, it suffices to verify that (
4.4
) holds with
K\leq K_{0}
and (
4.23
) holds with
\alpha=\rho/2
. Let
\nu>0
be small enough such that
(1-\nu^{1/N})^{-1}\leq K_{0}
and
|\log\nu|\nu^{\frac{\alpha\sigma}{N}}\leq c_{0}
. By condition (
1.14
), we have

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{\varepsilon_{k+1}}{\varepsilon_{k}}\Big)^{N}\leq\varepsilon_{1}\leq\nu\quad\text{for }k\geq 1,

from which we deduce that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\varepsilon_{k}}{\varepsilon_{j}}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\prod_{\ell=j}^{k-1}\frac{\varepsilon_{\ell+1}}{\varepsilon_{\ell}}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{k}\nu^{\frac{k-j}{N}}\leq(1-\nu^{1/N})^{-1}\leq K_{0}.

On the other hand, for any

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{\varepsilon_{k}}{\varepsilon_{k-1}}\bigg)^{\alpha\sigma}\leq\Big(\frac{\varepsilon_{m+1}}{\varepsilon_{m}}\Big)^{\frac{\alpha\sigma}{N}}\leq c_{0}\Big|\log\frac{\varepsilon_{m+1}}{\varepsilon_{m}}\Big|^{-1},

where we have used (
1.14

\displaystyle\Big|\log\frac{\varepsilon_{m+1}}{\varepsilon_{m}}\Big|\Big(\frac{\varepsilon_{m+1}}{\varepsilon_{m}}\Big)^{\frac{\alpha\sigma}{N}}\leq|\log\nu|\nu^{\frac{\alpha\sigma}{N}}\leq c_{0}

in the last one. In summary, all the conditions in Theorem
4.13

1.15
).
∎

Remark 4.14
.

As pointed out in Remark
1.7
, the requirement for
\varepsilon_{1}<\nu
is due to a technical reason in our method. Here we construct an example that will cause a problem. Let
\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},\cdots)
be given by

\varepsilon_{j}=\begin{cases}e^{-j},\quad&\text{if }1\leq j\leq m,\\
e^{-m}\tau^{j-m},\quad&\text{if }j>m.\end{cases}

Assume
0<\tau\ll e^{-1}
. It is easy to see that such
\varepsilon
satisfies the scale-separation condition (
1.14
). However, the first
m
scales are not separated well and homogenization does not take place above
\varepsilon_{m}
-scale. Our method is not able to show a Lipschitz estimate uniformly in both
m
and
\tau
. This situation needs a more careful analysis, which will not be discussed in the present paper.

The smallness assumption of
\varepsilon_{1}
in the uniform Lipschitz estimate can be removed for the most interesting cases.

Lemma 4.15
.

Suppose that
[\delta]_{1+\rho}<\infty
for some
\rho>0
and there exists a constant
\vartheta\in(0,1)
such that

(4.37)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{j}\geq\vartheta^{j}\quad\text{for each }j.

Let
u_{\varepsilon}
be the same as in Theorem
1.5
. Then for any
0<R\leq 1
,

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R/2})}\leq C\bigg\{\bigg(\fint_{B_{R}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+R\bigg(\fint_{B_{R}}|f|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}\bigg\},

where
C
depends only on
d,\mu,p,\vartheta,\rho,[\delta]_{0}
and
[\delta]_{1+\rho}
.

Proof.

By dilation, it suffices to consider
R=1
. Denote the continuity modulus of
A^{\varepsilon}
by

\omega(r):=\sup_{|x_{1}-x_{2}|<r}|A^{\varepsilon}(x_{1})-A^{\varepsilon}(x_{2})|.

For
0<r\leq\frac{1}{2}
, set
n=n(r)=\lfloor\varrho|\log r|\rfloor
where
\varrho=\frac{1}{2|\log\vartheta|}
. We calculate that

\displaystyle\omega(r)

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla A_{n}^{\varepsilon}(x)\|_{L^{\infty}}\cdot r+2\sum_{\ell=n+1}^{\infty}\|B_{\ell}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\varepsilon_{j}^{-1}\delta_{\ell}r+2\sum_{\ell=n+1}^{\infty}\delta_{\ell}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}(\ell+1)\delta_{\ell}\varepsilon_{n}^{-1}r+2\sum_{\ell=n+1}^{\infty}\delta_{\ell}\leq([\delta]_{0}+[\delta]_{1})\varepsilon_{n}^{-1}r+2[\delta]_{1+\rho}(n+1)^{-1-\rho}

\displaystyle\leq C\vartheta^{-\lfloor\varrho|\log r|\rfloor}r+C(\lfloor\varrho|\log r|\rfloor+1)^{-1-\rho}\leq Cr^{1/2}+C|\log r|^{-1-\rho},

where we have used the assumption (
4.37
) and
C
depends only on
[\delta]_{0},[\delta]_{1+\rho}
and
\vartheta
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1/2}\frac{\omega(r)}{r}dr<\infty,

i.e.,
A^{\varepsilon}(x)
is Dini continuous. By the Lipschitz estimate of elliptic equations with Dini continuous coefficients (see
[
Lie86
]
; also see
[
Li17
]
for elliptic systems), we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1/2})}\leq C\bigg\{\bigg(\fint_{B_{1}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+\bigg(\fint_{B_{1}}|f|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}\bigg\},

where
C
depends only on
d,\mu,p
and
\omega
.
∎

Theorem 4.16
.

Let
\{A_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
,
\delta
and
u_{\varepsilon}
satisfy the same assumptions as in Theorem
1.5
. Suppose that there exists
N\geq 1
such that

(4.38)

\bigg(\frac{\varepsilon_{k+1}}{\varepsilon_{k}}\bigg)^{N}\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{i+1}}{\varepsilon_{i}}\leq\bigg(\frac{\varepsilon_{k+1}}{\varepsilon_{k}}\bigg)^{1/N},\quad\text{for any }k>i\geq 0.

Then (
1.15
) holds with a constant
C
depends on the same parameters.

Proof.

We only need to consider two cases. If
\varepsilon_{1}=\varepsilon_{1}/\varepsilon_{0}<\nu
with
\nu
given by Theorem
1.5
, then Theorem
4.16
follows directly from Theorem
1.5
. Now if
\varepsilon_{1}\geq\nu
, then by the second inequality of (
4.38
), we have

\frac{\varepsilon_{k+1}}{\varepsilon_{k}}\geq\varepsilon_{1}^{N}\geq\nu^{N},

for all
k>0
.
Let
\vartheta=\nu^{N}
. By iterating the above inequality, we have

\varepsilon_{k}\geq\vartheta^{k}.

Hence, Lemma
4.15
gives the desired estimate.
∎

Remark 4.17
.

Theorem
4.16
covers the interesting case
\varepsilon_{j}=\varepsilon_{1}^{j}
for any
\varepsilon_{1}\in(0,1)
. More generally, it covers the case
\varepsilon_{j}=\varepsilon_{1}^{\beta_{j}}
, where
C^{-1}\leq\beta_{j}-\beta_{j-1}\leq C
for some constant
C>0
, and
\varepsilon_{1}\in(0,1)
. If
\varepsilon_{j}=\varepsilon_{1}^{\beta_{j}}
and
\beta_{j}-\beta_{j-1}\simeq j^{m}
for some

, then we may still establish some analog of Theorem

, under a faster decay assumption of
\delta=\{\delta_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
, such as

.

5.
Boundary Lipschitz estimates

This section is devoted to proving the boundary Lipschitz estimates. The proofs are more or less parallel to those in the previous section, and we present only the sketch for the Dirichlet boundary value problem.

Let
\gamma>0
and
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a
C^{1,\gamma}
function with

(5.1)

\displaystyle\psi(0)=0\quad\text{and}\quad\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}+[\nabla\psi]_{C^{0,\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{d-1})}\leq L.

Set

\displaystyle D_{r}=D(r,\psi)=\{(x^{\prime},x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|x^{\prime}|<r\text{~and~}\psi(x^{\prime})<x_{d}<10(L+d)r\},

\displaystyle\Delta_{r}=\Delta(r,\psi)=\{(x^{\prime},\psi(x^{\prime}))\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|x^{\prime}|<r\}.

Note that
D_{r}
is a Lipschitz domain for any
r>0
with a Lipschitz constant depending only on
L
and
d
.
We introduce a scaling-invariant norm for functions on
\Delta_{r}
,

\displaystyle\|g\|_{C^{1,\gamma}(\Delta_{r})}=\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Delta_{r})}+r\|\nabla_{\mathrm{tan}}g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Delta_{r})}+r^{1+\gamma}[\nabla_{\mathrm{tan}}g]_{C^{0,\gamma}(\Delta_{r})},

where
\nabla_{\mathrm{tan}}g
denotes the tangential gradient of
g
and

\displaystyle[g]_{C^{0,\gamma}(\Delta_{r})}=\sup_{x,y\in\Delta_{r},x\neq y}\frac{|g(x)-g(y)|}{|x-y|^{\gamma}}.

Let
u_{\varepsilon}
be a weak solution to

(5.2)

\displaystyle-\mathrm{div}(A^{\varepsilon}(x)\nabla u_{\varepsilon})=f\quad\text{in }D_{r},\quad u_{\varepsilon}=g\ \ \text{on }\Delta_{r}.

For
r\in(0,1)
, we can rescale the equation to a domain of size
1
. In fact, by setting
v(x)=u_{\varepsilon}(rx)
, we have

\displaystyle-\mathrm{div}(\widetilde{A}^{\varepsilon}(x)\nabla v)=\widetilde{f}\ \ \text{in }D(1,\psi_{r}),\quad v=\widetilde{g}\ \ \text{on }\Delta(1,\psi_{r}),

where
\widetilde{A}(y_{0},y_{1},\cdots)=A(ry_{0},y_{1},\cdots)
,
\widetilde{f}(x)=r^{2}f(rx)
,
\widetilde{g}(x)=g(rx)
and
\psi_{r}(x^{\prime})=r^{-1}\psi(rx^{\prime})
. It is easy to see that the rescaling argument works well for the boundary estimates as in the interior case. Especially, the function
\psi_{r}
satisfies condition (
5.1
) with the same
L
and
\|g\|_{C^{1,\gamma}(\Delta(r,\psi))}=\|\widetilde{g}\|_{C^{1,\gamma}(\Delta(1,\psi_{r}))}
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
f\in L^{2}(D_{1})
and
g\in C^{1}(\Delta_{1})
, with
\|g\|_{C^{1}(\Delta_{r})}=\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Delta_{r})}+r\|\nabla_{\mathrm{tan}}g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Delta_{r})}
. Assume that
[\delta]_{1}<\infty
. Let
u_{\varepsilon}
be a weak solution of (
5.2
). Then for each
r\in[\varepsilon_{1},1/2]
, there exists a weak solution
u_{0}
