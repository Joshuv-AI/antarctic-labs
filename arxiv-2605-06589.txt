Master equations with an individual noise on finite state graphs
Wilfrid Gangbo, Sebastian Munoz, Jeremy Wu, Zhaoyu Zhang

Abstract
We develop a classical well-posedness and regularity theory on a finite connected weighted graph for an extended mean field game system, its associated master equation, and a Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equation on the probability simplex, all in the presence of an individual noise operator. The geometric structure is inherited from the logarithmic-mean activation functional of discrete optimal transport, under which the entropic Fokker–Planck equation appears as a gradient flow on the graph and the individual noise operator is a bilinear form in the probability vector and the Wasserstein gradient. A central technical step is a quantitative preservation-of-positivity estimate for the discrete continuity equation, which rules out finite-time boundary degeneracy and yields a classical solution theory for the master equation on the open simplex without imposing any boundary condition. As an application, we recover a Nash equilibrium interpretation of the discrete system in terms of Markov chains on the graph. Our setup is inspired by the computational algorithms for optimal mass transport of [ 11 , 12 ] and provides a rigorous well-posedness theory for several of the equations derived in [ 26 ] .

Introduction

This paper develops a classical well-posedness and regularity theory on a finite connected weighted graph
\mathbb{G}=(\mathbb{V},\mathbb{E},\omega)
for three equations that arise in the study of mean field games (MFG) with individual noise: the extended MFG system (
1.5
), its associated master equation (
1.6
), and the Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equation on the probability simplex
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{G})
satisfied by the value function of an action-minimization problem. All three share a common source of analytic difficulty: the natural geometric structure on
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{G})
, inherited from discrete optimal transport, is degenerate on the boundary of the simplex. Our contribution is a classical solution theory in the relative interior
\mathcal{P}_{0}(\mathbb{G})
, resting on a quantitative lower bound on densities that rules out finite-time boundary degeneracy and on a regularity theory that upgrades the value function of the action-minimization problem to class
C^{2}
in both variables. As a byproduct, the master equation is uniquely solvable on the open simplex without imposing any boundary condition on
\partial\mathcal{P}_{0}(\mathbb{G})
. We also use this theory to construct Nash equilibria for a continuous-time Markov-chain MFG on
\mathbb{G}
. For the rest of this present section, we prefer to postpone the notations to Section

Notations and preliminaries

Let

denote an undirected graph with vertices
\mathbb{V}=\{1,\cdots,n\}

, endowed with a “weight”

For simplicity, assume that the graph is connected and simple, with no self-loops or multiple edges. We identify
\mathbb{V}
with
\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}\},
the canonical orthonormal basis in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and for

(i,j)\in\mathbb{E}
. Let

\omega_{\min}:=\min_{(i,j)\in\mathbb{E}}\omega_{ij},\quad\omega_{\max}=\max_{(i,j)\in\mathbb{E}}\omega_{ij}.

(1.8)

In standard notation, an
n

\mathbb{R}^{n}
which we denote by
\mu=(\mu^{1},\cdots,\mu^{n}).
We denote by
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{G})
the probability simplex on
\mathbb{G}
and for
\epsilon\geq 0
, we set

\mathcal{P}_{\epsilon}(\mathbb{G}):=\big\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{G}):\mu^{i}>\epsilon\;\;\forall i\in\mathbb{V}\big\}.

Let
\mathbb{S}(n)
denote the set of skew-symmetric
n\times n
matrices. The divergence of
(m^{ij})_{ij}\in\mathbb{S}(n)
on
\mathbb{G}
is the row vector
\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\cdot m\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
defined by
(\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\cdot m)^{i}=\sum_{j\not=i}^{n}\sqrt{\omega_{ij}}m^{ji}.
If
\tilde{m}\in\mathbb{S}(n)
, we set

(m,\tilde{m}):=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathbb{E}}m^{ij}\tilde{m}^{ij},\quad\|m\|^{2}=(m,m).

Here the coefficient
1/2
accounts for the fact that whenever
(i,j)\in\mathbb{E}
, then
(j,i)\in\mathbb{E}
. If
u\in\mathbb{R}^{n},
then
\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is the skew-symmetric matrix defined by

(\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u)^{ij}=\sqrt{\omega_{ij}}(u^{i}-u^{j}),\qquad\forall i,j\in\mathbb{V}.

We have the integration by parts formula

\big(\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\cdot m,u\big)=-\big(m,\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u\big),\quad\forall m\in\mathbb{S}(n),\,\forall u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(1.9)

A function
K:\mathbb{S}(n)\to\mathbb{R}
is differentiable at

if there exists

such that

K(m)-K(m^{0})-(m-m^{0},P)=o\big(\|m-m^{0}\|_{\ell_{2}}\big).

We call

at
m^{0}
and write

Note that since

is not defined on the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{n\times n},
its partial derivatives

do not exist per se. However, we adopt the notation

D_{m^{ij}}K(m):=\big(D_{m}K(m)\big)^{ij},\qquad\forall(i,j)\in\mathbb{E},\;i<j.

We then have that

K(m)-K(m^{0})-\sum_{i<j}D_{m^{ij}}K\big(m^{0}\big)\big(m^{ij}-m_{0}^{ij}\big)=o\big(\|m-m^{0}\|_{\ell_{2}}\big).

We denote by
{\bf 1}
the element of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
whose components are all equal to
1
. The tangent space of
\mathcal{P}_{0}(\mathbb{G})
at
\mu
and the orthogonal projection
\Pi_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{0}}

\mathbb{R}^{n}_{0}:=\Big\{V\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\;\sum_{i=1}^{n}V^{i}=0\Big\},\qquad\Pi_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{0}}:V\to V-{{\bf 1}\over n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}V^{i}.

Given a function
\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{P}_{0}(\mathbb{G})\to\mathbb{R}

the Fréchet derivative of

on

, and thus

admits an extension, which we continue to denote by

, and the extension has a Fréchet derivative

on

(1.10)

We shall denote the components of

\theta(r,s)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{r-s\over\log r-\log s}&\text{if }r\neq s,rs>0\\
r&\text{if }r=s>0\\

) are as in Theorem
1.1
; in particular,
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\rho_{i}\equiv 1
since
A
is skew-symmetric. We prove Theorem
1.1
via two lemmas of independent interest.

Lemma 2.1

(Spatial propagation of smallness)
.

For
\epsilon>0
, let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{\epsilon}(\mathbb{G})
,
t_{0}\in(0,T]
, and assume that
\rho\in C^{1}([0,t_{0}];(0,1)^{n})
is a classical solution to (
1.3
). There exists a constant
K>1
, depending only on
n
,
\omega_{\min}
,
\omega_{\max}
, and
h
, such that the following holds: if
\delta\in(0,\frac{\epsilon}{K})
,
i_{0}\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, and
t_{0}
is the first time such that
\rho_{i_{0}}(t_{0})=\delta,

then

\min_{s\in[0,t_{0}]}\rho_{i}(s)\leq K\delta\;\text{ for all }\;i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

(2.1)

Proof.

Let
C_{0}>1
be a constant to be chosen later. Since
\delta<\epsilon/K<\epsilon
, then
t_{0}>0
and
\dot{\rho}_{i_{0}}(t_{0})\leq 0
. Below, we let
C
denote a generic constant that may increase with each line and depends on
n
,
\omega_{\min}
,
\omega_{\max}
, and
\|h\|_{\infty}
, but does not depend on
C_{0}
or
\delta
. Using (
1.2
),

0\geq\dot{\rho}_{i_{0}}(t_{0})=\sum_{j}\sqrt{\omega_{i_{0}j}}A_{ji_{0}}+\sum_{j}(\rho_{j}-\rho_{i_{0}})\omega_{i_{0}j}\geq-CC_{0}\delta+\sum_{\rho_{j}>C_{0}\delta}\sqrt{\omega_{i_{0}j}}A_{ji_{0}}+\sum_{\rho_{j}>C_{0}\delta}(\rho_{j}-\rho_{i_{0}})\omega_{i_{0}j}

and so,

0\geq-CC_{0}\delta-\sum_{\rho_{j}>C_{0}\delta}\sqrt{\omega_{i_{0}j}}(\rho_{i_{0}}+\rho_{j})h(\rho_{j}/\rho_{i_{0}})+\sum_{\rho_{j}>C_{0}\delta}(\rho_{j}-\rho_{i_{0}})\omega_{i_{0}j}.

We conclude that

0\geq-CC_{0}\delta-\sum_{\rho_{j}>C_{0}\delta}\sqrt{\omega_{i_{0}j}}\rho_{j}h(\rho_{j}/\delta)+\sum_{\rho_{j}>C_{0}\delta}\rho_{j}\omega_{i_{0}j}=-CC_{0}\delta+\sum_{\rho_{j}>C_{0}\delta}\sqrt{\omega_{i_{0}j}}\rho_{j}(\sqrt{\omega_{i_{0}j}}-h(\rho_{j}/\delta)).

Hence, recalling (
1.1
), we may fix
C_{0}=C_{0}(h,\omega_{\min})
large enough so that
\sqrt{\omega_{\min}}-h(u)>\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\omega_{\min}}
for
u>C_{0}
.
We conclude that

\sum_{\rho_{j}>C_{0}\delta}\rho_{j}\omega_{i_{0}j}\leq CC_{0}\delta.

(2.2)

Let
j
be a neighbor of
i_{0}
. Either
\rho_{j}(t_{0})\leq C_{0}\delta
, and thus
\min_{[0,t_{0}]}\rho_{j}\leq C_{0}\delta
, or else
\rho_{j}(t_{0})>C_{0}\delta
, and thus

\min_{[0,t_{0}]}\rho_{j}\leq\rho_{j}(t_{0})\leq\sum_{\rho_{j}>C_{0}\delta}\rho_{j}(t_{0})\leq{CC_{0}\delta\over\omega_{\min}}

In conclusion, for a constant
C
that now depends on
\omega_{\min}
and for every neighbor
j
of
i_{0}
,

\min_{s\in[0,t_{0}]}\rho_{j}(s)\leq\rho_{j}(t_{0})\leq CC_{0}\delta.

We now denote by
C_{*}>1
the current value of
C
and fix the choice
K=(C_{*}C_{0})^{n-1}
. Let
i_{1}
be a neighbor of
i_{0}
. Repeating the same argument with
(i_{0},\delta,t_{0})
replaced by
(i_{1},C_{*}C_{0}\delta,\bar{t}_{0})
, where
\bar{t}_{0}\in\operatorname{argmin}_{s\in[0,t_{0}]}\rho_{i_{1}}(s)
, we infer that

\min_{s\in[0,t_{0}]}\rho_{j}(s)\leq\rho_{j}(\overline{t}_{0})\leq(C_{*}C_{0})^{2}\delta

for every neighbor
j
of
i_{1}
. Proceeding in this way, since
\mathbb{G}
is connected, we conclude after at most
n-1
iterations along a shortest path from
i_{0}
to any vertex
i
that

\min_{s\in[0,t_{0}]}\rho_{i}(s)\leq(C_{*}C_{0})^{n-1}\delta\leq K\delta,\quad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

This concludes the proof. ∎

Lemma 2.2

(Waiting time estimate)
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{\epsilon}(\mathbb{G})
,
0<t_{0}<t_{1}\leq T
, and assume that
\rho\in C^{1}([0,t_{1}];(0,1)^{n})
is a classical solution to (
1.3
). There exist constants
K>1
and
c_{0}>0
, depending on
n
,
\omega_{\min}
,
\omega_{\max}
and
h
, such that the following holds: if
\delta\in(0,\frac{\epsilon}{K})
and
t_{0}
,
t_{1}
are, respectively, the first times such that

\min_{i}\rho_{i}(t_{0})=\delta,\;\;\min_{i}\rho_{i}(t_{1})=\frac{1}{2K}\delta,

(2.3)

then
t_{1}-t_{0}\geq c_{0}.

Proof.

Let
K
be the constant of Lemma
2.1
. By definition of
t_{0}
,
\rho_{i}(s)>\delta
for all
s\in[0,t_{0})
and all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Thus, by Lemma
2.1
applied to
i_{0}\in\operatorname{argmin}_{i}\rho_{i}(t_{1})
with
\delta
replaced by
\frac{\delta}{2K}
,

\min_{s\in[t_{0},t_{1}]}\rho_{i}(s)=\min_{s\in[0,t_{1}]}\rho_{i}(s)\leq\frac{\delta}{2}\;\;\text{ for every }\;i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

(2.4)

Since the continuity equation preserves mass,
\sum_{i}\rho_{i}(t_{0})=1
, so
n\delta\leq 1
and there exists a vertex
i
such that
\rho_{i}(t_{0})\geq\frac{1}{n}
. On the other hand, since
h
is bounded, the continuity equation implies that

\bigl|\dot{\rho}_{i}\bigr|\leq\sum_{j}|A_{ij}|\,\sqrt{\omega_{ij}}+\sum_{j}|\rho_{j}-\rho_{i}|\,\omega_{ij}\leq 2\|h\|_{\infty}\sum_{j}\sqrt{\omega_{ij}}+2\sum_{j}\omega_{ij}\leq C.

(2.5)

Therefore, letting
t_{2}\in\operatorname{argmin}_{s\in[t_{0},t_{1}]}\rho_{i}(s)
and using the fact that
\delta\leq\frac{1}{n}
, we get

\frac{1}{2n}\leq\frac{1}{n}-\frac{\delta}{2}\leq\rho_{i}(t_{0})-\rho_{i}(t_{2})\leq C|t_{0}-t_{2}|\leq C(t_{1}-t_{0}).

(2.6)

This proves the claim for
c_{0}:=1/(2nC)
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By standard ODE theory, since
\min_{1\leq i\leq n}\mu^{i}\geq\epsilon>0
and
A\in C^{1}((0,T)\times(0,1)^{n};\mathbb{S}(n))
, local existence for (
1.3
) holds and the unique solution can be continued (with strictly positive components) until a maximal time
T_{\max}\in(0,T]
. Moreover, we must have

T_{\max}=T\;\;\text{ or }\;\liminf_{t\uparrow T_{\max}}\min_{i}\rho_{i}(t)=0,

(2.7)

since otherwise the existence interval could be extended.

Let
\delta_{0}=\frac{\epsilon}{2K}
, where
K>1
is the constant of Lemma
2.2
. If
\min_{1\leq i\leq n,\;s\in[0,T_{\max})}\rho_{i}(s)\geq\frac{\delta_{0}}{2K}
, then
T_{\max}=T
and there is nothing to prove. Otherwise, by Lemma
2.2
, if
t_{1}
is the first time such that
\min_{i}\rho(t_{1})=\frac{1}{2K}\delta_{0}
, then

t_{1}\geq c_{0}>0.

(2.8)

If
\min_{i,s}\rho_{i}(s)\geq\frac{\delta_{0}}{(2K)^{2}}
, then again we are done. Otherwise, applying Lemma
2.2
again, if
t_{2}
is the first time after
t_{1}
such that
\min_{i}\rho_{i}(t_{2})=\frac{1}{(2K)^{2}}\delta_{0}
, then since
t_{2}-t_{1}\geq c_{0}
we have

t_{2}\geq 2c_{0}.

(2.9)

Continuing in this way, and letting
t_{0}:=0
, we obtain a sequence
t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{j}
such that

t_{j}-t_{0}\geq jc_{0},\quad\min_{1\leq i\leq n,s\in[0,t_{j}]}\rho_{i}(s)=\min_{1\leq i\leq n}\rho_{i}(t_{j})=\frac{\delta_{0}}{(2K)^{j}}.

(2.10)

After finitely many steps, we must have
(j+1)c_{0}>T
, and therefore, by a final application of Lemma
2.2
, we conclude that
T_{\max}=T
, and

\min_{1\leq i\leq n,s\in[0,T]}\rho_{i}(s)>\frac{\delta_{0}}{(2K)^{(j+1)}}=\frac{\epsilon}{(2K)^{(j+2)}}.

(2.11)

By the optimality of
j
, one has
jc_{0}\leq T
, and so

\min_{1\leq i\leq n,s\in[0,T]}\rho_{i}(s)\geq c\epsilon e^{-rT},\quad r=\frac{\log(2K)}{c_{0}},\quad c=\frac{1}{(2K)^{2}}.

(2.12)

The precise estimate (
1.4
) then follows by applying the above argument to the restriction of
\rho
to the interval
[0,t]
.
∎

3
The extended MFG system and the master equation

Throughout this section, the hypotheses on
H
,
B
,
g
, and the auxiliary function
a
are those of Theorem
1.2
. For later referencing, we record them here: for all
(\mu,p)\in(0,1)^{n}\times\mathbb{S}(n)
and
(i,j)\in\mathbb{E}
,

\displaystyle(B(\mu,p),p)

\displaystyle\geq(H(\mu,p),\mu)-C_{1},

(3.1)

\displaystyle H_{i}(\mu,p)

\displaystyle\geq-C_{1},

(3.2)

\displaystyle|B_{ij}(\mu,p)|

\displaystyle\leq(\mu^{i}+\mu^{j})\,a(\mu^{j}/\mu^{i},p),

(3.3)

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq i\leq n,\,\rho\in[0,1]^{n}}|g_{i}(\rho)|

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}.

(3.4)

The uniqueness half of Theorem
1.2
additionally requires the Lasry–Lions monotonicity conditions: for all
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\mathbb{G})
and
p_{1},p_{2}\in\mathbb{S}(n)
with
(\mu_{1},p_{1})\neq(\mu_{2},p_{2})
,

\displaystyle(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2},H(\mu_{1},p_{1})-H(\mu_{2},p_{2}))<(p_{1}-p_{2},B(\mu_{1},p_{1})-B(\mu_{2},p_{2})),

(3.5)

\displaystyle(g(\mu_{1})-g(\mu_{2}),\mu_{1}-\mu_{2})\geq 0.

(3.6)

Well-posedness for extended MFG on a continuous state space under (
3.5
) has been studied, for instance, in
[
35
,
37
]
. When treating the master equation, we will use the following slightly stronger (differential) version of (
3.5
): for all
(\rho,p)\in(0,1)^{n}\times\mathbb{S}(n)
and
(\eta,q)\in(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{0}\times\mathbb{S}(n))\setminus\{(0,0)\}
,

(D_{\mu}H(\rho,p)[\eta]+D_{p}H(\rho,p)[q],\eta)<(D_{\mu}B(\rho,p)[\eta]+D_{p}B(\rho,p)[q],q).

(3.7)

This section proves Theorem
1.2
in two steps. First, a priori bounds for a homotopic family of MFG systems, combined with Theorem
1.1
, yield existence and uniqueness of classical solutions to (
1.5
) via a Schaefer fixed-point argument. Second, continuous differentiability of the solution map in the initial data allows us to define the value function and verify that it is the unique classical solution of the master equation (
1.6
).

3.1
Existence and uniqueness of classical solutions to the MFG system

We begin by obtaining a priori estimates, uniform in
\lambda\in[0,1]
and
(t,\mu)\in[0,T)\times\mathcal{P}_{0}(\mathbb{G})
, for classical solutions
(\phi,\rho):[t,T]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times(0,1)^{n}
of the homotopic family

\begin{cases}\dot{\phi}(s)=H(\rho(s),\lambda\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\phi(s))-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}\phi(s)&s\in(t,T)\\
\dot{\rho}(s)=\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\cdot(B(\rho(s),\lambda\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\phi(s)))+\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}\rho(s)&s\in(t,T)\\
\phi(T)=g(\rho(T)),\quad\rho(t)=\mu.\end{cases}

(3.8)

Lemma 3.1

(Lasry–Lions estimate)
.

Let
(\phi,\rho)
be a classical solution to (
3.8
). Then

\lambda(\phi(T),\rho(T))\leq\lambda(\phi(t),\mu)+2C_{1}(T-t).

(3.9)

Proof.

By (
3.1
), (
3.2
), and the duality of (
3.8
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}(\lambda\phi(s),\rho(s))=

\displaystyle-(B(\rho(s),\lambda\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\phi(s)),\lambda\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\phi(s))+\lambda(H(\rho(s),\lambda\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\phi(s)),\rho)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}-(1-\lambda)(H(\rho,\lambda\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\phi),\rho)\leq 2C_{1}.

We conclude the proof by integrating from
t
to
T
.
∎

Lemma 3.2

(Upper bound on
\phi
)
.

If
(\phi,\rho)
is a classical solution to (
3.8
), then

\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\phi_{i}(s)\leq C_{1}(T-s)+\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\phi_{i}(T),\quad s\in[t,T].

(3.10)

Proof.

For
s\in[t,T]
, let
M(s)=\max_{i}\phi_{i}(s)
. Since
\phi
is a classical solution, we see that
M
is Lipschitz continuous and, if
i_{s}\in\operatorname{argmax}_{1\leq j\leq n}(\phi_{j}(s))
, then
M^{\prime}(s)=\dot{\phi}_{i_{s}}(s)
for almost every
s
. Moreover,
\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}\phi_{i_{s}}(s)\leq 0
, and thus from (
3.2
),

M^{\prime}(s)=\dot{\phi}_{i_{s}}(s)\geq H_{i_{s}}\geq-C_{1}.

(3.11)

The claim follows by integrating over
[s,T]
.
∎

Proposition 3.3

(Uniform bounds on
\lambda\phi
)
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{\epsilon}(\mathbb{G})
and
t\in[0,T)
. For any classical solution
(\phi,\rho)
to (
3.8
),

\epsilon\big\|\max_{1\leq i\leq n}|\lambda\phi_{i}|\big\|_{L^{\infty}(t,T)}\leq(4(T-t)+3)C_{1}.

(3.12)

Proof.

By Lemma
3.2
, we have

\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\lambda\phi_{i}(s)\leq\lambda(T-t+1)C_{1},\quad s\in[t,T],

(3.13)

so to prove the result it suffices to bound from below the function
s\mapsto\lambda\Phi(s)=(\lambda\phi(s),\textbf{1})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda\phi_{i}(s).

Testing the first equation of (
3.8
) against
1
, using (
3.2
) and the fact that
(\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}\phi,\textbf{1})=0
, we have

\dot{\Phi}(s)\geq\sum_{i}H_{i}\geq-C_{1}n.

Therefore, noting that
1=\sum_{i}\mu^{i}\geq n\epsilon
, we obtain that

\lambda\Phi(s)\geq\lambda\Phi(t)-\lambda C_{1}n(s-t)\geq\lambda\Phi(t)-\frac{C_{1}}{\epsilon}(s-t),\quad s\in[t,T],

(3.14)

so it suffices to bound
\lambda\Phi(t)
from below. By Lemma
3.1
, we have

-C_{1}\leq(\lambda\phi(t),\mu)+2C_{1}(T-t)=(\lambda\phi(t)^{+},\mu)+2C_{1}(T-t)-(\lambda\phi(t)^{-},\mu).

(3.15)

Moreover, by Lemma
3.2
and (
3.4
),

(\lambda\phi(t)^{+},\mu)\leq\max_{i}\lambda\phi_{i}(t)\leq\max_{i}\phi_{i}(t)\leq C_{1}(T-t)+\max_{i}\phi_{i}(T)\leq C_{1}(T-t+1),

and therefore

-C_{1}\leq(\lambda\phi(t),\mu)+2C_{1}(T-t)\leq C_{1}(2(T-t)+1)-(\lambda\phi(t)^{-},\mu).

(3.16)

Thus,

\lambda\Phi(t)^{-}\leq(\lambda\phi^{-}(t),\textbf{1})\leq\frac{1}{\epsilon}(\lambda\phi^{-}(t),\mu)\leq\frac{3C_{1}(T-t+1)}{\epsilon}.

(3.17)

Recalling (
3.13
), we conclude that

\lambda\phi_{i}(s)=\lambda\Phi(s)-\sum_{k\not=i}\lambda\phi_{k}(s)\geq\lambda\Phi(s)-(n-1)\max_{k}\lambda\phi_{k}(s),

for all
i
. We use (
3.14
) and (
3.17
) to get that

\lambda\Phi(s)\geq-\frac{3C_{1}(T-t+1)}{\epsilon}-\frac{C_{1}}{\epsilon}(s-t)\geq-\frac{C_{1}(4(T-t)+3)}{\epsilon},\quad s\in[t,T],

and hence, using again that
n\epsilon\leq 1
,

\lambda\phi_{i}(s)\geq-\frac{C_{1}(4(T-t)+3)}{\epsilon}-(n-1)C_{1}(T-t+1)\geq-\frac{C_{1}(5(T-t)+4)}{\epsilon},

for all
i
and
s\in[t,T].
Together with (
3.13
), this proves the claim.
∎

We may now combine the estimates on
\phi
with the interiority estimates of the previous section to obtain a lower bound on
\rho_{i}
that does not deteriorate for arbitrarily small time horizons.

Lemma 3.4

(A priori lower bound on
\rho
)
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{\epsilon}(\mathbb{G})
,
\lambda\in[0,1]
,
t\in[0,T)
, and let
(\phi,\rho)
be a classical solution to (
3.8
). There exists a constant
c>0
, depending only on
n
,
\omega_{\min}
,
\omega_{\max}
,
a
,
C_{1}
,
\epsilon
, and monotonically on
T
, such that

\min_{1\leq i\leq n}\rho_{i}(s)\geq c\epsilon,\quad s\in[t,T].

(3.18)

Proof.

Let
L:=\left\|\max_{1\leq i\leq n}|\lambda\phi_{i}|\right\|_{L^{\infty}(t,T)}
. By Proposition
3.3
,
L\leq\frac{(5(T-t)+4)C_{1}}{\epsilon}\leq\frac{(5T+4)C_{1}}{\epsilon}
. Moreover, for
(i,j)\in\mathbb{E}
,

|(\lambda\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\phi)_{ij}|=\sqrt{\omega_{ij}}\,|\lambda\phi_{j}-\lambda\phi_{i}|\leq 2\sqrt{\omega_{\max}}\,L=:M.

The constant
M=M(T)
depends only on
n
,
\omega_{\max}
,
C_{1}
,
\epsilon
, and monotonically on
T
, and bounds

\|\lambda\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(t,T)}\leq M.

Define

h(u):=\sup_{|p|\leq M}a(u,p),\quad u>0.

Then
h
is bounded and satisfies (
1.1
). In addition, by (
3.3
),

|B_{ij}(\rho(s),\lambda\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\phi(s))|\leq(\rho_{i}(s)+\rho_{j}(s))\,h(\rho_{j}(s)/\rho_{i}(s)),\quad(i,j)\in\mathbb{E},

so the second equation in (
3.8
) is of the form (
1.3
). Applying Theorem
1.1
to the time-translated equation
\tilde{\rho}(\sigma):=\rho(\sigma+t)
on
[0,T-t]
yields
\min_{i}\rho_{i}(s)\geq c^{\prime}\epsilon e^{-r^{\prime}(s-t)}\geq c^{\prime}\epsilon e^{-r^{\prime}T}
for
s\in[t,T]
, with
c^{\prime},r^{\prime}>0
depending only on the above parameters; setting
c:=c^{\prime}e^{-r^{\prime}T}
gives (
3.18
). Finally, since the above choice of
M
is monotone in
T
, the constant
c
may be chosen monotonically in
T
as well (in particular, it does not deteriorate as
T\downarrow 0
).
∎

With these estimates in hand, we may now establish the main result of this subsection.

Proposition 3.5

(Existence and uniqueness for the extended MFG system)
.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
1.2
, for every
(t,\mu)\in[0,T)\times\mathcal{P}_{0}(\mathbb{G})
, the system (
1.5
) admits a classical solution
(\phi,\rho)\in C^{2}([t,T];{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times(0,1)^{n})
, which is unique if additionally (
3.5
)–(
3.6
) hold.

Proof.

The system (
1.5
) corresponds to the case
\lambda=1
of the homotopic family (
3.8
).
Fix
(t,\mu)\in[0,T)\times\mathcal{P}_{0}(\mathbb{G})
. Let
\mathbb{X}=C^{0}([t,T];{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
be equipped with the uniform norm. Noting that
\mathbb{X}
is a Banach space, we define an operator
\mathcal{T}:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{X}
as follows. Let
\varphi\in\mathbb{X}
. By Theorem
1.1
, applied to the time-translated equation
\tilde{\rho}(\sigma):=\rho(\sigma+t)
on
[0,T-t]
with
A_{ij}(\sigma,\mu):=B_{ij}(\mu,\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\varphi(\sigma+t))
and
h(u)=\sup_{|p|\leq\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\varphi\|_{\infty}}a(u,p)
, there exists a unique classical solution
\rho\in C^{1}([t,T];(0,1)^{n})
to

\dot{\rho}(s)=\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\cdot(B(\rho(s),\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\varphi(s)))+\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}\rho(s),\quad s\in[t,T],\quad\rho(t)=\mu.

(3.19)

Since
\rho_{i}>0
for all
i
, the backward ODE

\dot{\phi}(s)=H(\rho(s),\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\varphi(s))-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}\phi(s),\;\;s\in[t,T],\quad\phi(T)=g(\rho(T)),

(3.20)

then has a unique classical solution
\phi
, and we may define
\mathcal{T}(\varphi):=\phi
. Thanks to the lower bound (
1.4
), smooth dependence on parameters for ODE solutions, and the Arzelà-Ascoli theorem, the operator
\mathcal{T}
is continuous and compact. More precisely, given a bounded family
\{\varphi_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha}\subset\mathbb{X}
with
\sup_{\alpha}\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\varphi_{\alpha}\|_{L^{\infty}(t,T)}\leq M
, we may apply Theorem
1.1
with the fixed choice
h(u):=\sup_{\|p\|\leq M}a(u,p)
. Moreover, by Proposition
3.3
, the set

\{\varphi\in\mathbb{X}:\varphi=\lambda\mathcal{T}(\varphi)\;\;\text{for some }\;0\leq\lambda\leq 1\}

(3.21)

is bounded. Therefore, by Schaefer’s fixed point theorem,
\mathcal{T}
has a fixed point
\varphi
, so that
\phi:=\mathcal{T}(\varphi)=\varphi
. Defining
\rho
by (
3.19
), we see that
(\phi,\rho)
is a classical solution to (
1.5
), and
C^{2}
regularity follows from
H\in C^{1}
and
B\in C^{1}
. Finally, uniqueness under (
3.5
) and (
3.6
) follows by adapting the classical Lasry–Lions argument
[
33
]
to our discrete setting. If
(\rho^{i},\phi^{i})
,
i=1,2
, are two solutions to (
1.5
), differentiating
(\rho^{1}-\rho^{2},\phi^{1}-\phi^{2})
and using (
1.5
) gives

\displaystyle\Big(\rho^{1}(T)-\rho^{2}(T),\phi^{1}(T)-\phi^{2}(T)\Big)-\Big(\rho^{1}(t)-\rho^{2}(t),\phi^{1}(t)-\phi^{2}(t)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u(t,\mu)=

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0^{+}}{u(t+h,\mu)-u\big(t+h,\rho(t+h,t,\mu)\big)+u\big(t+h,\rho(t+h,t,\mu)\big)-u(t,\mu)\over h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\nabla_{\mathcal{W}}u(t,\mu),B(\mu,\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u(t,\mu))\big)-\Delta_{\text{ind}}u(t,\mu)+H(\mu,\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u(t,\mu))-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}u(t,\mu),

where we used
\Delta_{\text{ind}}u(t,\mu)=-(\nabla_{\mathcal{W}}u(t,\mu),\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\mu)
.

Next, we prove uniqueness. Let
v\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\mathcal{P}_{0}(\mathbb{G});{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
be another classical solution to (
1.6
). Fix
(t,\mu)\in[0,T)\times\mathcal{P}_{0}(\mathbb{G})
and let
(\phi(\cdot),\rho(\cdot)):=(\phi(\cdot,t,\mu),\rho(\cdot,t,\mu))
be the unique classical solution of (
1.5
) on
[t,T]
with initial measure
\mu
at time
t
.
Moreover, we define the function
z:[t,T]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
by

z(s):=v(s,\rho(s)).

Using the chain rule, we compute for
s\in(t,T)
,

\displaystyle\dot{z}(s)=

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v(s,\rho(s))+\delta_{\mu}v(s,\rho(s))[\dot{\rho}(s)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v(s,\rho(s))-\big(\nabla_{\mathcal{W}}v(s,\rho(s)),B(\rho(s),\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\phi(s))+\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\rho(s)\big).

Now, using the fact that
v
solves the master equation, we obtain

\displaystyle\dot{z}(s)=

\displaystyle\big(\nabla_{\mathcal{W}}v,B(\rho,\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v)\big)-\Delta_{\text{ind}}v+H(\rho,\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v)-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}v-\big(\nabla_{\mathcal{W}}v,B(\rho,\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\phi)+\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\rho\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\nabla_{\mathcal{W}}v,B(\rho,\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v)\big)+\big(\nabla_{\mathcal{W}}v,\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\rho\big)+H(\rho,\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v)-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}v-\big(\nabla_{\mathcal{W}}v,B(\rho,\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\phi)+\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\rho\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\nabla_{\mathcal{W}}v,B(\rho,\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v)-B(\rho,\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\phi)\big)+H(\rho,\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v)-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}v.

Letting
\eta(s)=z(s)-\phi(s)
for
s\in[t,T]
and
\Gamma(s)=\nabla_{\mathcal{W}}v(s,\rho(s))
, we have
\eta(T)=0
and

\displaystyle\dot{\eta}(s)

\displaystyle=\big(\Gamma,B(\rho,\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\phi+\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\eta)-B(\rho,\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\phi)\big)+H(\rho,\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\phi+\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\eta)-H(\rho,\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\phi)-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}\eta=:f(s,\eta(s)).

Since
\eta\equiv 0
is a solution to this ODE (with terminal condition
\eta(T)=0
), it must be the only solution due to the uniqueness coming from the regularity of
f
. Hence
v(s,\rho(s))=\phi(s)
for all
s\in[t,T]
. In particular,
v(t,\mu)=\phi(t)=u(t,\mu)
.
∎

4.1
Assumptions

Throughout this section,
\mathcal{L}:[0,+\infty)^{n}\times\mathbb{S}(n)\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}

\mathcal{F}:[0,1]^{n}\to\mathbb{R}

\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\in C^{\infty}\big((0,+\infty)^{n}\times\mathbb{S}(n)\big),\quad\mathcal{L}(\mu,0)=0,

(4.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\;\text{is (jointly) convex and (jointly) lower semicontinuous},

(4.2)

\displaystyle D_{mm}^{2}\mathcal{L}(\mu,m)>0\quad\forall(\mu,m)\in(0,1)^{n}\times\mathbb{S}(n),

(4.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\mu,m)\geq C_{\mathcal{L}}\big(\|m\|_{\ell_{2}}^{p_{0}}-1\big)\quad\forall(\mu,m)\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{G})\times\mathbb{S}(n),

(4.4)

\displaystyle(m,D_{m}\mathcal{L}(\mu,m))+(\mu,D_{\mu}\mathcal{L}(\mu,m))\;\text{and}\;\min_{i}-D_{\mu^{i}}\mathcal{L}(\mu,m)\;\text{are bounded from below}.

(4.5)

On the Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}(\mu,\cdot)
, defined as the Legendre transform of
\mathcal{L}(\mu,\cdot)
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}\in C^{\infty}((0,1)^{n}\times\mathbb{S}(n)),

(4.6)

\displaystyle|D_{p^{ij}}\mathcal{H}(\mu,p)|\leq(\mu^{i}+\mu^{j})a\Big({\mu^{j}\over\mu^{i}},p\Big)\quad\forall(\mu,p)\in(0,1)^{n}\times\mathbb{S}(n),

(4.7)

\displaystyle\lim_{u\to 0}a(u,p)=\lim_{u\to\infty}a(u,p)=0\;\text{ locally uniformly in }p\in\mathbb{S}(n).

(4.8)

On the source
\mathcal{F}
and terminal cost
\mathcal{U}_{T}

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\in C^{2}([0,1]^{n})\cap C^{\infty}((0,1)^{n}),\quad D^{2}\mathcal{F}<0\text{ on }(0,1)^{n},

(4.9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{T}\in C^{2}([0,1]^{n})\cap C^{\infty}((0,1)^{n}),\quad\mathcal{U}_{T}|_{\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{G})}\text{ is convex}.

(4.10)

For instance,
\mathcal{L}_{\theta}

By Legendre duality, for every
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\mathbb{G})
and
w\in\mathbb{S}(n)

p:=D_{m}\mathcal{L}(\mu,w)

D_{\mu}\mathcal{L}(\mu,w)=-D_{\mu}\mathcal{H}(\mu,p).

(4.11)

Remark 4.1
.

For every
\varrho\in(0,1)^{n}
,

, and

(4.12)

Indeed, (
4.3

We shall apply the theory developed in Section
3
by setting
F:=D_{\mu}\mathcal{F}
,
U_{T}:=D_{\mu}\mathcal{U}_{T}
, and

H(\mu,p):=D_{\mu}\mathcal{H}(\mu,-p)+F(\mu),\;\;B(\mu,p)=-D_{p}\mathcal{H}(\mu,-p),\;\;g(\mu)=U_{T}(\mu).

(4.13)

By (

) and (

),

and

. Furthermore,

. By Remark
4.1
, condition (
4.12
) holds; since
(\eta,DF[\eta])=(\eta,D^{2}\mathcal{F}[\eta])
for

3.7
) and hence (
3.5
). Assumption (
4.5
) implies that (
3.1
) and (
3.2

3.3

).

Remark 4.2

(Hölder regularity from coercivity)
.

Assume (
4.4
). Let
(\rho,m)\in\mathcal{C}_{t}^{T}(\mu,\cdot)
and set
w:=m+\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\rho
. Then

\int_{t}^{T}\|w(s)\|_{\ell_{2}}^{p_{0}}\,ds\leq\frac{1}{C_{\mathcal{L}}}\int_{t}^{T}\mathcal{L}(\rho(s),w(s))\,ds+(T-t).

Since
\rho(s)\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{G})
for all
s
,
\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\rho(s)\|_{\ell_{2}}
is bounded uniformly in
s
by a constant depending only on
(\mathbb{G},\omega)
; hence

\int_{t}^{T}\|m(s)\|_{\ell_{2}}^{p_{0}}\,ds\leq C\Big(1+\int_{t}^{T}\mathcal{L}(\rho(s),m(s)+\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\rho(s))\,ds\Big),

for a constant
C
depending only on
p_{0},C_{\mathcal{L}},T
and the graph data.
Moreover, the continuity equation implies
\dot{\rho}=-\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\cdot m
in the distributional sense and thus

\|\dot{\rho}(s)\|_{\ell_{2}}\leq C_{\rm div}\|m(s)\|_{\ell_{2}}
for a.e.
s
, where
C_{\rm div}
depends only on
(\mathbb{G},\omega)
.
Consequently
\dot{\rho}\in L^{p_{0}}(t,T;\ell_{2})
and for all
s_{1},s_{2}\in[t,T]
,

\|\rho(s_{2})-\rho(s_{1})\|_{\ell_{2}}\leq|s_{2}-s_{1}|^{1/p_{0}^{\prime}}\|\dot{\rho}\|_{L^{p_{0}}(t,T;\ell_{2})}.

In particular,
\rho
is
1/p_{0}^{\prime}
–Hölder continuous in time.

4.2
Existence and uniqueness of a minimizer for
\mathcal{U}(t,\mu)

We set

\iota_{0}:=-\max\mathcal{U}_{T}^{-}-T\max|\mathcal{F}|-TC_{\mathcal{L}},\qquad\iota^{T}:=T\max_{\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{G})}|\mathcal{F}|+\max_{\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{G})}\mathcal{U}_{T}.

(4.14)

Proposition 4.3

(Existence of a unique minimizer in
\mathcal{C}_{t}^{T}(\mu,\cdot)
)
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{G})
and
t\in[0,T).
Then, the following hold:

(i)

\iota_{0}\leq\mathcal{U}(t,\mu)\leq\iota^{T}
.

(ii)

There exists a unique
(\rho,m)
that minimizes
(\bar{\rho},\bar{m})\to\mathcal{A}_{t}^{T}(\rho,\bar{m})+\mathcal{U}_{T}(\bar{\rho}(T))
over
\mathcal{C}_{t}^{T}(\mu,\cdot)
.

(iii)

\mathcal{U}(t,\cdot)
is strictly convex on
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{G}).

(iv)

There is a constant
R>0
depending only on
\max|\mathcal{F}|
,
\max|\mathcal{U}_{T}|
,
p_{0}
,
C_{\mathcal{L}}
and
T
(but independent of
t
or
\mu
) such that if
(\rho,m)
is as in (ii), then
\|\dot{\rho}\|_{L^{p_{0}}(t,T)}\leq R.

Proof.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{G})
and
t\in[0,T).
Using (
4.4
), we obtain the first identity in (i). We set

A^{ij}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\quad\omega_{ij}&\text{if}\quad\qquad j\in N(i)\\
\quad 0&\quad\text{if}\;j\not\in N(i),i\not=j\\
-\sum_{k\in N(i)}\omega_{ik}&\text{if}\;\qquad\qquad i=j.\end{array}\right.

Setting

\rho(s)=\mu e^{(s-t)A},\qquad m:=-\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\rho\qquad\forall s\in[t,T],

we find that
\rho(t)=\mu
and

\dot{\rho}-\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\cdot\big(\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\rho\big)=0,\quad\text{and}\quad m+\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\rho=0.

By the second identity in (
4.1
),
\mathcal{L}(\rho,m+\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\rho)=0
and thus

\mathcal{U}(t,\mu)\leq\mathcal{A}_{t}^{T}(\rho,m)+\mathcal{U}_{T}(\rho_{T})=-\int_{t}^{T}\mathcal{F}(\rho)ds+\mathcal{U}_{T}(\rho_{T})\leq\iota^{T},

(4.15)

which proves the second identity in (i).

The convexity of
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{U}_{T}
, together with the strict convexity of
-\mathcal{F}
, ensures that
(\bar{\rho},\bar{m})\to\mathcal{A}_{t}^{T}(\bar{\rho},\bar{m})+\mathcal{U}_{T}(\bar{\rho}(T))
is strictly convex over
\mathcal{C}_{t}^{T}(\mu,\cdot)
, and thus there is at most one minimizer. Combined with the existence of a minimizer (established below), this implies (iii).

Let
(\rho_{k},m_{k})_{k}\subset\mathcal{C}_{t}^{T}(\mu,\cdot)
be a minimizing sequence and assume without loss of generality that
\mathcal{A}_{t}^{T}(\rho_{k},m_{k})+\mathcal{U}_{T}(\rho_{k}(T))\leq\mathcal{U}(t,\mu)+1.
From the boundedness of
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{U}_{T}^{-}
, we deduce an upper bound on
\int_{t}^{T}\mathcal{L}(\rho_{k},m_{k}+\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\rho_{k})\,ds
. By the coercivity assumption (
4.4
) and Remark
4.2
,
(m_{k})_{k}
is bounded in
L^{p_{0}}\big((t,T);\mathbb{S}(n)\big)
and
(\dot{\rho}_{k})_{k}
is bounded in
L^{p_{0}}\big((t,T);\ell_{2}\big)
. Consequently, the
1/p_{0}^{\prime}
-Hölder constant of each
\rho_{k}
is bounded by a constant independent of
k
. Passing to a subsequence if necessary, we apply the Arzelà–Ascoli theorem to obtain that
(\rho_{k})_{k}
is uniformly convergent to some
\rho.
Furthermore, since
p_{0}>1
, we may assume that
(m_{k})_{k}
converges weakly to some
m
in
L^{p_{0}}\big((t,T);\mathbb{S}(n)\big)
. We have that
(\rho,m)\in\mathcal{C}_{t}^{T}(\mu,\cdot)
. We use the joint convexity of
\mathcal{L}
and the fact that
\mathcal{L}
is bounded from below to conclude that

\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\int_{t}^{T}\mathcal{L}(\rho_{k},m_{k}+\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\rho_{k})ds\geq\int_{t}^{T}\Big(\mathcal{L}(\rho,m+\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\rho)\Big)ds.

Since
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{U}_{T}
are continuous, we conclude that
(\rho,m)
is a minimizer, which proves (ii). As written above, this also proves (iii). By Remark
4.2
, we find an upper bound for
\|\dot{\rho}\|_{L^{p_{0}}(t,T)}
which depends only on
\max|\mathcal{U}|
,
\max|\mathcal{F}|
,
\max|\mathcal{U}_{T}|
,
p_{0}
,
C_{\mathcal{L}}
and
T
. This, together with (i), implies (iv).
∎

Proposition 4.4

(Characterization of minimizing paths in
\mathcal{C}_{t}^{T}(\mu,\cdot)
)
.

Given
\epsilon>0
and
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{\epsilon}(\mathbb{G})
, there exists a unique
(\rho,m)
minimizing
(\bar{\rho},\bar{m})\to\mathcal{A}_{t}^{T}(\bar{\rho},\bar{m})+\mathcal{U}_{T}(\bar{\rho}(T))
over
\mathcal{C}_{t}^{T}(\mu,\cdot)
. Furthermore:

(i)

(\rho,m)
is uniquely characterized as follows:
\rho\in C^{\infty}([t,T];(0,1)^{n})
, and there exists
\phi\in C^{\infty}([t,T];{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
such that
m:=D_{p}\mathcal{H}(\rho,-\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\phi)-\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\rho
and the pair
(\phi,\rho)
solves the forward–backward system (
1.7
).

(ii)

There exists
c_{\epsilon}>0
independent of
\mu
and
t
, but depending on
\epsilon
and
T
, such that the range of
\rho
is contained in
\mathcal{P}_{c_{\epsilon}}(\mathbb{G}).

(iii)

There exists a constant
C
independent of
\mu
,
\epsilon
, and
t
, but depending on the data, such that
\|\phi\|_{\ell_{\infty}}\leq C/\epsilon
on
[t,T].

Proof.

By Proposition
3.5
, the system (
1.7
) admits a unique solution
(\phi,\rho)
. Set
m:=D_{p}\mathcal{H}(\rho,-\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\phi)-\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\rho
. By standard convex analysis (see (
4.11
)),

\mathcal{L}(\rho,m+\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\rho)+\mathcal{H}(\rho,-\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\phi)=(m+\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\rho,-\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\phi),

and

D_{m}\mathcal{L}(\rho,m+\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\rho)=-\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\phi\quad\text{and}\quad D_{\mu}\mathcal{L}(\rho,m+\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\rho)=-D_{\mu}\mathcal{H}(\rho,-\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\phi)

(4.16)

Let
(\bar{\rho},\bar{m})\in\mathcal{C}_{t}^{T}(\mu,\cdot).
Using the fact that
\mathcal{L}
and
-\mathcal{F}
are convex, we infer

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{t}^{T}(\bar{\rho},\bar{m})\geq\mathcal{A}_{t}^{T}(\rho,m)+

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{T}\big(D_{m}\mathcal{L}(\rho,m+\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\rho),\bar{m}+\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\bar{\rho}-m-\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\rho\big)ds

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{T}\Big(\big(D_{\mu}\mathcal{L}(\rho,m+\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\rho),\bar{\rho}-\rho\big)-\big(\delta_{\mu}\mathcal{F}(\rho),\bar{\rho}-\rho\big)\Big)ds.

Since
F-\delta_{\mu}\mathcal{F}
lies in the span of
{\bf 1}
and
\bar{\rho}-\rho\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{0}
, we may replace
\delta_{\mu}\mathcal{F}
by
F
. Combining this with the second identity in (
1.7
) and (
4.16
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{t}^{T}(\bar{\rho},\bar{m})\geq\mathcal{A}_{t}^{T}(\rho,m)-

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{T}\big(\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\phi,\bar{m}-m+\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\bar{\rho}-\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\rho\big)ds

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{T}\Big(\big(\dot{\phi}+\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}\phi-F(\rho),\bar{\rho}-\rho\big)+\big(F(\rho),\bar{\rho}-\rho\big)\Big)ds

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}_{t}^{T}(\rho,m)-

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{T}\Big(\big(\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\phi,\bar{m}-m\big)+\big(\dot{\phi},\bar{\rho}-\rho\big)\Big)ds

Using the fact that
(\bar{\rho},\bar{m})
and
(\rho,m)
belong to
\mathcal{C}_{t}^{T}(\mu,\cdot)
, we obtain

\mathcal{A}_{t}^{T}(\bar{\rho},\bar{m})\geq\mathcal{A}_{t}^{T}(\rho,m)-\big(\phi(T),\bar{\rho}(T)-\rho(T)\big)=\mathcal{A}_{t}^{T}(\rho,m)-\big(U_{T}(\rho(T)),\bar{\rho}(T)-\rho(T)\big).

Since
\mathcal{U}_{T}
is convex and

\big(\delta_{\mu}\mathcal{U}_{T}(\rho(T)),\bar{\rho}(T)-\rho(T)\big)=\big(U_{T}(\rho(T)),\bar{\rho}(T)-\rho(T)\big),

we conclude that

\mathcal{A}_{t}^{T}(\bar{\rho},\bar{m})+\mathcal{U}_{T}(\bar{\rho}(T))\geq\mathcal{A}_{t}^{T}(\rho,m)+\mathcal{U}_{T}(\rho(T)).

This proves that
(\rho,m)
minimizes
(\bar{\rho},\bar{m})\mapsto\mathcal{A}_{t}^{T}(\bar{\rho},\bar{m})+\mathcal{U}_{T}(\bar{\rho}(T))
over
\mathcal{C}_{t}^{T}(\mu,\cdot)
. By Proposition
4.3
(ii),
(\rho,m)
is uniquely determined, proving (i). For (ii) and (iii), Lemma
3.4
and Proposition
3.3
applied to (
1.7
) on
[t,T]
yield constants that depend monotonically on
T
, hence uniform in
t\in[0,T)
. ∎

4.3
Properties of the subdifferential of
\mathcal{U}(t,\cdot)

We denote by
\partial_{\cdot}\mathcal{U}(t,\cdot)(\mu)
the subdifferential of
\mathcal{U}(t,\cdot)
with respect to the
\ell_{2}
–metric. Since
\mathcal{U}(t,\cdot)
is convex, this is the set of

such that

\mathcal{U}(t,\nu)-\mathcal{U}(t,\mu)\geq(\nu-\mu,q),\qquad\forall\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{G}).

When
\partial_{\cdot}\mathcal{U}(t,\cdot)(\mu)
contains a unique element, we denote it by
\delta_{\mu}\mathcal{U}(t,\mu).

Lemma 4.5
.

There exists a constant
\gamma
(which depends only on the data, including
p_{0}
and
C_{\mathcal{L}}
) such that the following holds: if
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{G})
,

,

, and

, then the following hold:

where
C_{\rm div}
depends only on
(\mathbb{G},\omega)
.
Therefore, the
1/p_{0}^{\prime}
-Hölder constant of

is bounded by a constant
R_{\rm data}
independent of
t
and
\mu
that depends only on the data.

From the hypothesis
\mathcal{A}_{t}^{T}(\rho,m)+\mathcal{U}_{T}(\rho(T))\leq\mathcal{U}(t,\mu)+1
and the boundedness of
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{U}_{T}^{-}
, we deduce an upper bound on
\int_{t}^{T}\mathcal{L}(\rho,m+\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\rho)\,ds
in terms of
1+|\mathcal{U}(t,\mu)|
. By the coercivity assumption (
4.4
) and the argument in Remark
4.2
, we obtain the first inequality. For the second, we use that by (
1.15
),
\|\dot{\rho}\|_{\ell_{2}}\leq C_{\rm div}\|m\|_{\ell_{2}}
where
C_{\rm div}
depends only on
(\mathbb{G},\omega)
. Since Proposition
4.3
provides an upper bound on
\mathcal{U}
, the
1/p_{0}^{\prime}
-Hölder constant
R_{\rm data}
is bounded independently of
t
and
\mu
. ∎

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
\epsilon>0
and
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{\epsilon}(\mathbb{G})
. Then

(i)

\partial_{\cdot}\mathcal{U}(t,\cdot)(\mu)\not=\emptyset
and
\mathcal{U}(t,\cdot)
is Fréchet differentiable almost everywhere on
\mathcal{P}_{0}(\mathbb{G})
.

(ii)

If
q\in\partial_{\cdot}\mathcal{U}(t,\cdot)(\mu)
and
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{\epsilon}(\mathbb{G})
, then
\epsilon\|q\|_{\ell_{2}}\leq 2\big(\iota^{T}-\iota_{0}\big)
for all
t\in[0,T].

Proof.

(i) By Proposition
4.3
,
\mathcal{U}(t,\cdot)
is convex and thus, since it assumes only finite values, locally Lipschitz continuous. Since
\mathcal{P}_{0}(\mathbb{G})
is an open subset of the affine hyperplane
\{\nu\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\sum_{i}\nu_{i}=1\}
, part (i) follows from Rademacher’s theorem. (ii) Suppose that
q\in\partial_{\cdot}\mathcal{U}(t,\cdot)(\mu)
and assume without loss of generality that

Then

Proposition 4.13

(Time differentiability of

Proof.

Fix

,
\epsilon>0
, and
\mu_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{\epsilon}(\mathbb{G})
. Given

5.1
A variational formula for
D_{\mu^{i}}\mathcal{H}

Definition 5.1
.

We say that
(\bar{\mathcal{L}},\mathcal{H})
satisfies the unique momentum property if the following holds: whenever
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\mathbb{G})
,
\bar{v},p_{1},p_{2}\in\mathbb{S}(n)
and

\bar{\mathcal{L}}(\mu_{i},\bar{v})+\mathcal{H}(\mu_{i},p_{i})=(\bar{v},p_{i})_{\mu_{i}},\quad\forall i\in\{1,2\}

then
p_{1}=p_{2}.

The proof of the following useful lemma is left to the reader.

Lemma 5.2
.

Assume that
\bar{v},p\in\mathbb{S}(n)
and
\mu\in(0,1)^{n}
are such that
\bar{\mathcal{L}}(\mu,\bar{v})+\mathcal{H}(\mu,p)=(\bar{v},p)_{\mu}
. Then,

D_{v^{ij}}\bar{\mathcal{L}}(\mu,\bar{v})=\theta(\mu^{i},\mu^{j})p^{ij},\quad D_{p^{ij}}\mathcal{H}(\mu,p)=\theta(\mu^{i},\mu^{j})\bar{v}^{ij}

and

D_{\mu^{i}}\bar{\mathcal{L}}(\mu,\bar{v})+D_{\mu^{i}}\mathcal{H}(\mu,p)=\sum_{j\in N(i)}\partial_{1}\theta(\mu^{i},\mu^{j})\bar{v}^{ij}p^{ij}

For
a=(a^{1},\cdots,a^{i-1},a^{i+1},\cdots,a^{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
we define
v[a]\in\mathbb{S}(n)
by setting
v^{kl}[a]=0
if
(k,l)\not\in\mathbb{E}
and for
(k,l)\in\mathbb{E}
we set

v^{kl}[a]:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\hfill v^{kl}&\;\text{if}\quad i\not\in\{k,l\}\\
\hfill v^{il}+a^{l}&\;\text{if}\hfill k=i\\
\hfill v^{ki}-a^{k}&\;\text{if}\hfill l=i.\end{array}\right.

Set

\ell(a,v,i)\equiv\ell(\mu,p,a,v,i):=D_{\mu^{i}}\bar{\mathcal{L}}(\mu,v[a]+\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\log\mu)-\sum_{l\in N(i)}\big(v[a]+\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\log\mu\big)^{il}p^{il}\partial_{1}\theta(\mu^{i},\mu^{l})

and

K_{i}(\mu,p,v):=D_{\mu^{i}}\bar{\mathcal{L}}(\mu,v+\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\log\mu)-\sum_{l\in N(i)}\big(v+\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\log\mu\big)^{il}p^{il}\partial_{1}\theta(\mu^{i},\mu^{l})

so that

K_{i}(\mu,p,v[a])=\ell(\mu,p,a,v,i).

Given
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\mathbb{G}),

v,p\in\mathbb{S}(n)
we often use the notation

\bar{v}:=v+\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\log\mu

Observe that if
k<l
and
(k,l)\in\mathbb{E}
then

D_{v^{kl}}K_{i}(\mu,p,v)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcr}\hfill D_{v^{il}}D_{\mu^{i}}\bar{\mathcal{L}}(\mu,\bar{v})-p^{il}\partial_{1}\theta(\mu^{i},\mu^{l})&\;\text{if}&k=i\\
\hfill D_{v^{ki}}D_{\mu^{i}}\bar{\mathcal{L}}(\mu,\bar{v})-p^{ki}\partial_{1}\theta(\mu^{k},\mu^{i})&\;\text{if}&l=i\\
\hfill D_{v^{kl}}D_{\mu^{i}}\bar{\mathcal{L}}(\mu,\bar{v})&\;\text{if}&k,l\not=i.\end{array}\right.

(5.1)

Lemma 5.3
.

Suppose that
(\bar{\mathcal{L}},\mathcal{H})
satisfies the unique momentum property,
i\in\mathbb{V}
,
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\mathbb{G}),

D_{\mu^{i}}\bar{\mathcal{L}}(\mu,\cdot)
is convex and

are such that
\bar{\mathcal{L}}(\mu,v+\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\log\mu)+\mathcal{H}(\mu,p)=(v+\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\log\mu,p)_{\mu}.
Then

-\ell(0,v,i)=\max_{a}\{-\ell(a,v,i)\}=D_{\mu^{i}}\mathcal{H}(\mu,p).

If we further assume that
D_{v^{kl}}D_{\mu^{i}}\bar{\mathcal{L}}(\mu,\bar{v})\equiv 0
whenever

then

D_{\mu^{i}}\mathcal{H}(\mu,p)=\sup_{w\in\mathbb{S}(n)}\sum_{l\in N(i)}\big(w+\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\log\mu\big)^{il}p^{il}\partial_{1}\theta(\mu^{i},\mu^{l})-D_{\mu^{i}}\bar{\mathcal{L}}(\mu,w+\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\log\mu)

and the maximum is attained when

.

Proof.

We set
\bar{v}:=v+\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\log\mu
and note that since
\ell(\cdot,v,i)
is a convex function, its critical points are minimizers. To conclude the first part of the proof, it suffices to show that
D_{v^{ij}}K_{i}(\mu,p,v)=0
for
j\in N(i).
But if
j\in N(i)
, then in light of (

)

D_{v^{ij}}K_{i}(\mu,p,v)=D_{v^{ij}}D_{\mu^{i}}\bar{\mathcal{L}}(\mu,\bar{v})-p^{ij}\partial_{1}\theta(\mu^{i},\mu^{j})=D_{\mu^{i}}D_{v^{ij}}\bar{\mathcal{L}}(\mu,\bar{v})-p^{ij}\partial_{1}\theta(\mu^{i},\mu^{j}).

(5.2)

Let

be such that

\bar{\mathcal{L}}(\mu+te_{i},\bar{v})+\mathcal{H}(\mu+te_{i},p_{t})=(p_{t},\bar{v})_{\mu+te_{i}}.

Since
(\bar{\mathcal{L}},\mathcal{H})
satisfies the unique momentum property,

. We have that

is a critical point for

, and so by Lemma

Thus,

and thus by (

),
D_{v^{ij}}K_{i}(\mu,p,v)=0,
which proves the first claim.

Now, using the fact that

is a convex function and since we know that

for
j\in N(i)
, it remains to show that

when
k\not=i
and
l\not=i.
But by assumption
D_{v^{kl}}D_{\mu^{i}}\bar{\mathcal{L}}(\mu,\bar{v})\equiv 0
, which together with (

. ∎

(5.4)

is a Nash equilibrium for the system
(L,g,\mu).
In the case (
5.4
) fails,
v^{0,\mu}
is merely a restricted Nash equilibrium for the system
(L,g,\mu).

Remark 5.14
.

The admissibility hypothesis in Theorem
5.13
is expected to hold on sufficiently fine nearest-neighbor discretizations of
\mathbb{T}^{d}
. With mesh size
h
one has
\omega_{ij}=h^{-2}
on each edge, so that

Q^{ij}[v^{0,\mu}|\rho^{0,\mu}]=h^{-2}-h^{-1}\partial_{1}\theta\Big((\rho_{s}^{0,\mu})^{i},(\rho_{s}^{0,\mu})^{j}\Big)(\bar{v}_{s}^{0,\mu})^{ij}.

In the model family (
5.3
),

(\bar{v}_{s}^{0,\mu})^{ij}=h_{ij}^{\prime}\Big(-\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u\big(s,\rho_{s}^{0,\mu}\big)^{ij}\Big),

so an
n
-uniform bound on
\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u
yields an
n
-uniform bound on
\bar{v}^{0,\mu}
. Since the
h^{-2}
term then dominates the drift correction,
Q^{ij}[v^{0,\mu}|\rho^{0,\mu}]\geq 0
on every edge once
h
is small enough, in agreement with the limiting MFG system whenever
\rho^{0,\mu}
converges to a smooth positive solution. It remains an interesting open problem to establish uniform estimates for a rigorous proof of this limiting argument.

Proof.

Let
v\in C^{1}([0,T];\mathbb{S}(n))
be an admissible control for
\rho^{0,\mu}.
Let
(X_{t})_{t}
be a Markov chain generated by the pair
\big(\mu,Q[v|\rho^{0,\mu}]\big)
and set
M_{t}:=X_{t}-X_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}X_{\tau}Q[v|\rho^{0,\mu}]_{\tau}d\tau
. As in Definition
5.6
, let
\big(\mathcal{F}_{t}\big)_{0\leq t\leq T}
be a filtration on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathbb{P}^{\mu},\mathcal{F})
such that each
\mathcal{F}_{t}
contains the
\mathbb{P}^{\mu}
–null sets, and
(X_{t})_{0\leq t\leq T}
is
\big(\mathcal{F}_{t}\big)_{0\leq t\leq T}
–progressively measurable. Since
(M_{t})
is a
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t}
–martingale and
\sigma\{X_{t}=e_{i}\}\subset\mathcal{F}_{t}
, we have that

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{t}^{T}\Big(u\big(s,\rho^{0,\mu}_{s}\big),dM_{s}\Big)\bigg|\sigma\{X_{t}=e_{i}\}\bigg]=\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{t}^{T}\Big(u\big(s,\rho^{0,\mu}_{s}\big),dM_{s}\Big)\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t}\Big]\bigg|\sigma\{X_{t}=e_{i}\}\Bigg]=0.

(5.12)

By Proposition
5.5
,
X_{0\#}\mathbb{P}^{\mu}=\mu
, so

\mathbb{E}_{0,e_{i}}\Big[u(0,X_{0},\rho^{0,\mu})\Big]=u(0,e_{i},\rho^{0,\mu}),

which is independent of
v
. In light of Lemma
5.12
, (
5.12
) with
t=0
implies that if (
5.4
) holds then

J(0,i;v^{0,\mu},v^{0,\mu})=u(0,e_{i},\rho^{0,\mu})\leq J(0,i;v^{0,\mu},v).

Hence,
v^{0,\mu}
is a Nash equilibrium for the system
(L,g,\mu).
Similarly, in case (
5.4
) fails, by Lemma
5.12
,
v^{0,\mu}
is a restricted Nash equilibrium for the system
(L,g,\mu).
