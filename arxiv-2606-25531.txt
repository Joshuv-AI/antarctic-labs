On Subhomogeneous Operator Systems
Markus Dannemüller, Tim Netzer

Abstract
We study subhomogeneity for finite-dimensional operator systems, and collect and extend characterizations in terms of the C ∗ C^{*} -envelope, d d -maximality, complete positivity, dual d d -minimality, and non-commutative boundary conditions. We then show that the dual of a subhomogeneous operator system, while not necessarily subhomogeneous itself, is always a quotient of a subhomogeneous system. We complement these characterizations with examples and counterexamples, including minimal and maximal systems over certain polyhedral cones.



1  Introduction

Operator systems provide a framework for studying the self-adjoint parts of unital
C^{*}
-algebras and the completely positive maps between them. In finite dimensions, this viewpoint connects several themes: matrix orders, matrix convexity, and the geometry of spectrahedra. A central problem is to understand when the higher matrix levels of an operator system are already controlled by finitely many low levels. This question is natural both from the perspective of operator algebra, where it is related to finite-dimensional and subhomogeneous representations, and from the perspective of convexity, where it asks how much of a matrix convex set is determined by bounded-size matrix points.

For
C^{*}
-algebras, subhomogeneity is a repesentation theoretic finiteness condition: A
C^{*}
-algebra is
d
-subhomogeneous if all irreducible representations have dimension at most
d
. Equivalently, such an algebra embeds into matrices of size
d
over a commutative
C^{*}
-algebra, and it is characterized by automatic complete positivity of
d
-positive maps. In this paper we study the corresponding notion for finite-dimensional operator systems. The relevant replacement for an embedding of a
C^{*}
-algebra is a realization of the operator system inside
{\rm Mat}_{d}(A)
with
A
commutative; equivalently, one may ask whether the
C^{*}
-envelope is
d
-subhomogeneous.

This places subhomogeneity in direct contact with the hierarchy of minimal and maximal operator system structures. The constructions of minimal and maximal matrix orders over a fixed cone, and their refinements obtained by fixing the first
d
levels, have been studied in connection with spectrahedral containment and quantum information; see, for example,
[
14
,
6
]
. Indeed,
d
-subhomogeneity of an operator system is the same as
d
-maximality. Thus a representation-theoretic property of the
C^{*}
-envelope is equivalent to a purely matrix-order property of the system itself.

Our first main result is a collection and extension of equivalent characterizations of
d
-subhomogeneity. For a finite-dimensional operator system
S
, the following conditions are all equivalent:
S
admits a
d
-commutative realization, its
C^{*}
-envelope is
d
-subhomogeneous,
S
is
d
-maximal, every
d
-positive map into
S
is completely positive, the dual system
S^{\vee}
is
d
-minimal, and a certain boundary-compression condition holds at all higher matrix levels. These equivalences unify algebraic, order-theoretic, duality-theoretic, and geometric viewpoints.

We then study the behavior of duals. Duality exchanges maximality and minimality, so one should not expect the dual of a subhomogeneous operator system to remain subhomogeneous. Nevertheless, we prove a positive substitute: the dual of a
d
-subhomogeneous operator system is always a quotient of a
d
-subhomogeneous system. This complements related projection results for finite-dimensional realizations of matrix convex sets, such as those in
[
8
]
.

The final part of the paper develops examples. For cones over cross-polytopes, we show a sharp contrast: The interval and square cases have subhomogeneous minimal operator systems, whereas dimension at least three gives minimal systems that are not subhomogeneous.

These examples show that even for polyhedral cones, subhomogeneity depends delicately on the dimension and geometry of the cone and on the matrix convex structure it induces.

Throughout the paper, all operator systems are finite-dimensional. This ensures, among other things, that duals always exist. We expect, however, that many of the results can also be adapted to the infinite-dimensional setting.

2  Preliminaries on Operator Systems

We begin by recalling the basic notions and notation for operator systems and
C^{*}
-algebras, that will be used throughout. See, for instance,
[
12
]
for more details and proofs.

Throughout, all general vector spaces are finite-dimensional real vector spaces. In the operator algebra setting, one often works with complex vector spaces. However, these always carry an involution, all maps are
*
-linear, and positivity is determined by the self-adjoint part of the space. Thus everything can be reduced to the real case, and no generality is lost by this restriction.

A subset
C\subseteq V
of a vector space
V
is a
convex cone
if
C+C\subseteq C
and
\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}\cdot C\subseteq C
. It is
proper
if it is closed, pointed, and has non-empty interior, i.e. if
C\cap-C=\{0\}
and
\operatorname{int}(C)\neq\emptyset
.
Denote by
{\rm Her}_{s}(\mathbb{C})\subseteq{\rm Mat}_{s}(\mathbb{C})
the real vector space of hermitian matrices of size
s
.

An
operator system

on

is a collection of proper convex cones

S_{s}\subseteq V\otimes{\rm Her}_{s}(\mathbb{C})\eqqcolon{\rm Her}_{s}(V)

for

, together with a distinguished interior point

, called the

. The collection
(S_{s})_{s\in\mathbb{N}}
, also called

, is required to be closed under conjugation by scalar matrices: for all

and

, we have
m^{*}S_{s}m\subseteq S_{t}
.

Every
C^{*}
-algebra

gives rise to an abstract operator system structure on the self-adjoint space

, by taking the cone of positive elements inside the self-adjoint part of
A\otimes{\rm Mat}_{s}(\mathbb{C})={\rm Mat}_{s}(A)
as the cone at level
s
. By slight abuse of notation, we denote this operator system structure again by

.

Given operator systems

and

and

\phi(S_{1})\subseteq T_{1}

. If

To simplify notation, we write

\phi[a]\coloneqq(\phi\otimes{\rm id}_{{\rm Her}_{s}(\mathbb{C})})(a)

For a given operator system

Under the canonical isomorphism

\phi[a]\in{\rm Mat}_{s^{2}}(\mathbb{C})_{+}

\phi\in S^{\vee}_{s}
.

The Choi-Effros Theorem says that every abstract operator system is of the form

C^{*}
-algebra

It is therefore natural to ask for the smallest such

Both the class of finite-dimensional

A

The class of

3  Maximal Operator Systems

Let
C\subseteq V
be a proper cone. There are, in general, many operator system structures on
V
whose first level is
C
. Among them there is a smallest and a largest one with respect to level-wise inclusion

1

. The
minimal operator system over
C
, denoted
C^{\rm min}
, is the smallest such structure, while the
maximal operator system over
C
, denoted

, is the largest. Explicit descriptions of these two constructions can be found, for instance, in
[
6
]
.

The results in
[
14

generalized these constructions by fixing not only the first level, but the first
d

up to level
d
. We first record a useful characterization of the largest such structure.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
S
be an operator system on
V
, and let
d\in\mathbb{N}
be fixed. For
s\in\mathbb{N}
and
a\in V\otimes{\rm Her}_{s}(\mathbb{C})
, the following are equivalent:

(i)

m^{*}am\in S_{d}
for all
m\in{\rm Mat}_{s,d}(\mathbb{C})
.

(ii)

\phi[a]\in{\rm Mat}_{ds}(\mathbb{C})_{+}
for all
\phi\in{\rm CP}(S,{\rm Mat}_{d}(\mathbb{C}))
.

(iii)

\phi[a]\in{\rm Mat}_{ds}(\mathbb{C})_{+}
for all
\phi\in{\rm UCP}(S,{\rm Mat}_{d}(\mathbb{C}))
.

If
s\leqslant d
, these conditions are further equivalent to
a\in S_{s}
.

Proof.

The implication
(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
is standard. Take
v=\sum_{i=1}^{d}e_{i}\otimes v_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{s}
, set
m=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{d})\in{\rm Mat}_{s,d}(\mathbb{C})
, and let
e=\sum_{i=1}^{d}e_{i}\otimes e_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d}
. Then

v^{*}\phi[a]v=e^{*}\phi[m^{*}am]e.

Since
\phi
is completely positive and
m^{*}am\in S_{d}
by assumption, the right-hand side is nonnegative.

Conversely, assume
(ii)
. By biduality, it suffices to show that
\phi[m^{*}am]\in{\rm Mat}_{d^{2}}(\mathbb{C})_{+}
for every
\phi\in{\rm CP}(S,{\rm Mat}_{d}(\mathbb{C}))
. This follows from

\phi[m^{*}am]=(I_{d}\otimes m)^{*}\phi[a](I_{d}\otimes m),

together with
(ii)
.

The implication
(ii)\Rightarrow(iii)
is immediate. For
(iii)\Rightarrow(ii)
, use the standard normalization argument: after splitting off zero blocks and conjugating by an invertible matrix, any completely positive map into
{\rm Mat}_{d}(\mathbb{C})
becomes unital. If the target size decreases in this process, we enlarge it again by taking a direct sum with normalized positive functionals.

Finally, suppose
s\leqslant d
. Then the preceding conditions imply
a\in S_{s}
. Indeed, one may either apply
(ii)
and biduality directly, or use
(i)
to embed
a
into level
d
and then compress back to level

.

The

Indeed,

4  Subhomogeneous Operator Systems

We now extend the notion of subhomogeneity from
C^{*}
-algebras to operator systems, by means of several equivalent properties. The equivalence of
(i)
,
(iii)
,
(iv)
, as well as part of the connection with

in the following theorem already appears in

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
S
be an operator system on a finite-dimensional vector space
V
. The following are equivalent:

(i)

S
has a
d
-commutative realization.

(ii)

C^{*}_{\rm env}(S)
is
d
-subhomogeneous.

(iii)

S
is
d
-maximal.

(iv)

For every operator system
T

d
-positive map
\phi\colon T\to S
is completely positive.

(v)

The dual system

is

-minimal.

(vi)

For every

, there exists an isometry

such that
v^{*}xv\in\partial S_{d}
.

Proof.

By
Section

2
, items
(i)
and
(ii)
are equivalent. By
Section

3
, items
(iii)
and
(iv)
are equivalent.

We now show
(i)\Rightarrow(iv)
. Let
\psi\colon S\to{\rm Mat}_{d}(A)
be a
d
-commutative realization of

, and let
\phi\colon T\to S
be

-positive. Then

is

-positive and thus completely positive, as explained in

2

We next prove (

with its natural compact Hausdorff topology; see

\psi\colon V\to{\rm Mat}_{d}({\rm C}(X))\cong{\rm C}(X,{\rm Mat}_{d}(\mathbb{C})),\qquad\psi(v)(\phi)=\phi(v).

For

Since

Next,

follows by duality. Indeed, assume

\phi\colon S^{\vee}\to T

It remains to show

Assume first that

Conversely, assume

This implies

5  Duals of Subhomogeneous Operator Systems

Theorem

4.1
shows, among other things, that the dual of a
d
-subhomogeneous operator system is
d
-minimal, and thus will in general not be subhomogeneous. However, we can prove that duals of subhomogeneous systems are
projections/quotients
of subhomogeneous systems. This complements the same result for systems with a finite-dimensional realization, obtained in
[
8
]
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
S
be a
d
-subhomogeneous operator system on a finite-dimensional vector space
V
. Then
S^{\vee}
is the quotient (i.e. the closed linear image) of a
d
-subhomogeneous operator system.

Proof.

Since
S
is
d
-subhomogeneous, we have a
d
-commutative realization

\psi\colon S\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Mat}_{d}(A).

We will show that
\mathrm{Mat}_{d}(A)
itself projects onto
S^{\vee}
.

In the proof of
Theorem

4.1
we have seen that we can choose
A={\rm C}(X)
where
X={\rm UCP}(S,{\rm Mat}_{d}(\mathbb{C}))
is a compact convex subset of a Euclidean space.
For
x\in X
and
\operatorname{ev}_{x}
the evaluation map at
x
, we consider the map

\pi_{x}\coloneqq(\operatorname{ev}_{x}\circ\psi)^{\prime}\colon\mathrm{Mat}_{d}(\mathbb{C})\to V^{\prime}

where we have identified
{\rm Mat}_{d}(\mathbb{C})
with its dual space, via the usual trace inner product.

Let
\lambda
be any finite Borel measure with full support on
X
(take for example the Lebesgue measure). We use it to define the following map:

\pi\colon\mathrm{Mat}_{d}({\rm C}(X))\to V^{\prime};\quad a\mapsto\int_{X}\pi_{x}(a(x)){\rm d}\lambda(x).

We first check that this is well-defined. For
a\in{\rm Mat}_{d}({\rm C}(X))
and
x\in X
we have

\pi_{x}(a(x))=[v\mapsto{\rm tr}(a(x)^{*}\psi(v)(x))],

which depends continuously on
x
. So the integrand is continuous on
X
and hence the integral exists. Thus,
\pi
is a well-defined
*
-linear map.

We now check that
\pi
is completely positive as a map to
S^{\vee}
. For

a\in\left({\rm Mat}_{d}({\rm C}(X))\otimes{\rm Mat}_{s}(\mathbb{C})\right)_{+}={\rm Mat}_{ds}({\rm C}(X))_{+}

and
b\in S_{s}
we have

\pi[a](b)=\int_{X}{\rm tr}(\underbrace{a(x)}_{\rm psd}\cdot\underbrace{\psi[b](x)}_{\rm psd}){\rm d}\lambda(x)\geqslant 0,

since
\psi
is completely positive. This implies
\pi[a]\in S^{\vee}_{s},
which was to be shown.

We finally need to show that
\pi
is surjective onto
S^{\vee}
, up to closure. If
c\in V^{\prime}\otimes{\rm Her}_{s}(\mathbb{C})
does not belong to the closure of
\pi[{\rm Mat}_{ds}({\rm C}(X))_{+}],
there exists some
b\in V\otimes{\rm Her}_{s}(\mathbb{C})
with

c(b)<0\ \mbox{ and }\ \pi[a](b)\geqslant 0\mbox{ for all }a\in{\rm Mat}_{ds}({\rm C}(X)_{+}.

The second condition implies that
\psi[b]
is positive, which is easily shown using that
\lambda
has full support on
X
, and classical self-duality of the psd cone. Since
\psi
is a realization of
S,
this further implies
b\in S_{s}
. This finally yields
c\notin S_{s}^{\vee},
which finishes the proof.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

In the case that
S
has a
d
-dimensional realization, the above proof shows that
S^{\vee}
is a projection of
{\rm Mat}_{d}(\mathbb{C}),
thus generalizing the result from
[
8
]
.

6  Examples

Let
Q\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be the cone over the
n
-cube, i.e. the polyhedral cone
defined by the
2n
inequalities

x_{0}\pm x_{i}\geqslant 0,\quad i=1,\ldots n.

By
[
6
]
, the maximal operator system over
Q
is obtained by imposing the same inequalities at every level: For
a=(a_{0},\ldots,a_{n})\in{\rm Her}_{s}(\mathbb{C})^{n+1}
we have

a\in Q^{\rm max}_{s}\ \Leftrightarrow\ a_{0}\pm a_{i}\in{\rm Mat}_{s}(\mathbb{C})_{+}

for
i=1,\ldots,n
. We now ask whether this system is
d
-minimal for some
d
. By
Theorem

4.1
this is equivalent to its dual system being
d
-subhomogeneous. The dual system is the minimal system over the dual cone, see
[
1
]
for example. The dual cone of
Q
is the cone over the cross-polytope, which we denote by
C
. It has
2^{n}
facets and
2n
extreme rays. For
n\leqslant 2
the cones
Q
and
C
are isomorphic, but for
n\geqslant 3
they are different.

Now it turns out that
d
-minimality and
d
-subhomogeneity depend sharply on the dimension. Note that very similar arguments can also be found in
[
5
,
11
]
in the context of matrix extreme points of free spectrahedra.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
Q\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be the cone over the cube and
C\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
the cone over the cross-polytope.

(i)

For
n=1
,
Q^{\mathrm{max}}
is
1
-minimal and
C^{\rm min}
is
1
-subhomogeneous.

(ii)

For
n=2
,
Q^{\mathrm{max}}
is
2
-minimal and
C^{\rm min}
is
2
-subhomogeneous.

(iii)

For
n\geqslant 3
,
Q^{\mathrm{max}}
is not
d
-minimal and
C^{\rm min}
is not
d
-subhomogeneous for any
d\in\mathbb{N}.

Proof.

For
n=1
,
Q
is a simplex cone, and thus minimal and maximal systems coincide (see for example
[
6
]
). For
n=2
we need to show that
Q_{2}^{\max}
generates all higher levels of
Q^{\max}
, as explained in
Section

3
. So let
s\geqslant 3
and take
a\in Q^{\rm max}_{s}
. Since
a_{0}\pm a_{i}\geqslant 0
holds for
i=1,2,
we must have
a_{0}\geqslant 0.
Further, any congruence transformation that splits off a zero block in
a_{0}
simultaneously splits off the same zero block in
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
. After potentially decreasing
s
we can thus assume
a_{0}=I_{s}
. The inequalities now state that the eigenvalues of
a_{1},a_{2}
are in
[-1,1].
Since classical convex combinations are allowed in the construction from
Section

3
, we can even assume that these eigenvalues are all
\pm 1
, and thus

a_{i}=2p_{i}-I_{s}

for some orthogonal projections
p_{1},p_{2}.
By Halmos’ two projections theorem,
p_{1},p_{2}
(and thus also
a_{1},a_{2}
) can be decomposed simultaneously into blocks of size at most
2
, by a unitary conjugation. Since block-sums are allowed in the construction from
Section

3
, we are done.

For
n\geqslant 3
, fix
s\in\mathbb{N}
and choose orthogonal projections
p_{1},\dots,p_{n}
which generate
{\rm Mat}_{s}(\mathbb{C})
as an algebra; this is possible by
[
3
]
. Set
a_{i}=2p_{i}-I_{s}
and consider

a=(I_{s},a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in Q_{s}^{\max}.

We will show that
a
does not arise from lower levels of
Q^{\max}
as in
Section

3
. But we first show that
a
lies on a classical extreme ray of
Q_{s}^{\max}.

By the Krein-Milman theorem we can write
a=\sum_{j}b^{(j)}
for
b^{(j)}
on extreme rays of
Q_{s}^{\max}
. We thus have

\sum_{j}(b_{0}^{(j)}\pm b_{i}^{(j)})=I_{s}\pm a_{i}

for each
i=1,\ldots n
, and by the inequalities defining
Q^{\max}
each term on the left-hand side is positive semidefinite. Hence, we get

\ker(I_{s}\pm a_{i})\subseteq\ker(b_{0}^{(j)}\pm b_{i}^{(j)})

for all
i=1,\ldots,n
.

This implies that each
b_{0}^{(j)}
is proportional to the identity: Taking
v\in\ker(p_{i})=\ker(I_{s}+a_{i})
and
w\in\ker(p_{i})^{\perp}=\ker(I_{s}-p_{i})=\ker(I_{s}-a_{i})
, we get

w^{*}(b_{0}^{(j)}+b_{i}^{(j)})v=0=v^{*}(b_{0}^{(j)}-b_{i}^{(j)})w.

Taking the hermitian conjugate of the second term and adding both yields
w^{*}b_{0}^{(j)}v=0
. This implies that
\ker(p_{i})
is an invariant subspace of
b_{0}^{(j)}
, and thus
p_{i}
and
b_{0}^{(j)}
commute. But since the
p_{i}
generate
{\rm Mat}_{s}(\mathbb{C})
,
b_{0}^{(j)}
is indeed proportional to
I_{s},
say
b_{0}^{(j)}=\alpha_{j}I_{s}.

Since
b^{(j)}
is classically extreme in
Q_{s}^{\max}
, the matrices
\alpha_{j}^{-1}b^{(j)}_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
have eigenvalues
{\pm 1}
, so we write them as

\alpha_{j}^{-1}b^{(j)}_{i}=2q_{i}^{(j)}-I_{s}

for projections
q_{i}^{(j)}
. Looking at the sum above, we get
1=\sum_{j}\alpha_{j}
and
p_{i}=\sum_{j}\alpha_{j}q_{i}^{(j)}
for each
i=1,\ldots,n
. Now it is straightforward to show that this can only be true if all
q_{i}^{(j)}
equal
p_{i}
. We have thus proven that
a
is indeed extreme in
Q_{s}^{\max}
.

Now finally assume that
a
arises from a lower level
d<s
as in
Section

3
:

a=\sum_{j}v_{j}^{*}x_{j}v_{j}

with
x_{j}\in Q_{d}^{\max}.

By extremality of
a
, each term
v_{j}^{*}x_{j}v_{j}
is already proportional to
a
. But the first entry of
a
is
I_{s}
, while the first entry of
v_{j}^{*}x_{j}v_{j}
can have at most rank
d
. This is a contradiction. So
Q^{\max}
is indeed not
d
-minimal.
∎

We finish with another example, where the base cone is not polyhedral.

Example 6.2
.

Let
P={\rm Mat}_{2}(\mathbb{C})_{+}
. Then the minimal operator system
P^{\min}
is not
3
-subhomogeneous.
Indeed, at level
s
, the cone
P_{s}^{\min}
is the cone of separable positive semidefinite elements in
{\rm Mat}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\otimes{\rm Mat}_{s}(\mathbb{C})
. If
P^{\min}
were
3
-subhomogeneous, then it would be
3
-maximal by
Theorem

4.1
. Hence, by
Section

3
, every entangled state in
{\rm Mat}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\otimes{\rm Mat}_{s}(\mathbb{C})
would have an entangled compression of the second tensor factor to dimension
3
.

However, there exist PPT entangled states in
{\rm Mat}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\otimes{\rm Mat}_{4}(\mathbb{C})
; see
[
10
]
. Let
\sigma
be such a state. For every isometry
v\in{\rm Mat}_{4,3}(\mathbb{C})
, the compression

(I_{2}\otimes v)^{*}\sigma(I_{2}\otimes v)

is again PPT, because partial transposition on the first tensor factor commutes with compression on the second tensor factor. By the Peres–Horodecki criterion, PPT is equivalent to separability in dimensions
2\times 3
; see
[
9
]
. Thus every such
3
-dimensional compression of
\sigma
is separable. This contradicts the compression criterion above, and therefore
P^{\min}
is not
3
-subhomogeneous.

Remark 6.3
.

Clearly,
d
-subhomogeneity passes from extensions to subsystems and
d
-minimality passes to quotients/linear images. The other directions fail in general. For example,
[
4
]
provides a
8
-dimensional concrete operator system
S\subseteq{\rm Mat}_{3}(\mathbb{C})
which is not
d
-minimal for any
d.
Note that
{\rm Mat}_{3}(\mathbb{C})
is
3
-minimal. The dual
S^{\vee}
is thus not
d
-subhomogeneous for any
d
, although it is a quotient of
{\rm Mat}_{3}(\mathbb{C}),
which is
3
-subhomogeneous.

Some Open Questions

We close with several open questions suggested by the results above.

Let
C\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a polyhedral cone. What can be said, in general, about subhomogeneity of
C^{\min}
? For simplex cones the question is trivial, while the first nontrivial case in dimension
n=2
is
2
-subhomogeneous. Is this true for every polyhedral cone in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
? Does the answer depend on the number and arrangement of its extreme rays?

In dimensions
n\geqslant 3
, our examples provide counterexamples to subhomogeneity. These examples have
2n
extreme rays, whereas simplex cones have only
n+1
extreme rays. It is therefore natural to ask what happens for polyhedral cones whose number of extreme rays lies between these two cases.

One may also ask analogous questions for minimal systems over non-polyhedral cones. A particularly interesting case, because of its connection with quantum information, is the cone of positive semidefinite matrices
P={\rm Mat}_{n}(\mathbb{C})_{+}
, see also
Section

6
. For the minimal system over
P
one has

P_{s}^{\min}={\rm Mat}_{n}(\mathbb{C})_{+}\otimes_{\min}{\rm Mat}_{s}(\mathbb{C})_{+},

the cone of mixed separable states. Thus
d
-homogeneity would mean that entanglement can always be detected after compressing the second tensor factor to dimension
d
. The preceding example shows that for
n=2
one cannot take
d=3
, but it does not rule out the possibility of some larger
d
.

Equivalently, one can ask the following question:

Given
n\geqslant 1
, does there exist
d\geqslant 1
such that, for every
s\geqslant 1
and every mixed entangled state
\sigma\in{\rm Mat}_{n}(\mathbb{C})\otimes{\rm Mat}_{s}
, some compression of the second tensor factor to
{\rm Mat}_{d}(\mathbb{C})
remains entangled?
