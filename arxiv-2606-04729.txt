A note on momentum subtraction schemes for quark bilinears and semileptonic operators Journal: Physics Letters B
P. A. Boyle, M. Bruno, M. Gorbahn, S. Jäger, C. Lehner, F. Moretti, J. Parrino

Abstract
In this work we examine a family of regularization invariant (RI) symmetric momentum (SMOM) schemes for semi-leptonic operators. By working with chirally symmetric and massless QCD, we relate the semi-leptonic operators with their corresponding flavor-changing vector currents, whose renormalization in pure QCD is protected by the Ward identity. For the latter, we extend the original RI/SMOM scheme [ 1 ] to a family projectors suitable to be promoted to the semi-leptonic case and demonstrate their equivalence to Ref. [ 2 ] , relevant in particular for the perturbative calculation of the corresponding Wilson coefficients.

2
Theoretical framework

We start by listing the working assumptions of our study. Firstly, we work with massless QCD, as is the standard choice for renormalization schemes like
{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}
or
\mathrm{RI}
.
Secondly, the schemes we consider are defined by conditions imposed on the renormalized, four-dimensional Green’s functions, whereby we require that the Ward identities implied by isosymmetric QCD apply. We therefore do not need to specify a regularization scheme – the primary virtue of the RI schemes. In practice, we may imagine a lattice discretization of QCD retaining good chiral symmetry at finite cutoff and with a lattice spacing sufficiently fine to ignore discretization errors. These particular assumptions simplify the practicalities of carrying out the renormalization on the lattice, much like dimensional regularization simplifies the perturbative renormalization of gauge theories, but nothing in the following depends on them.

We begin our treatment by introducing the expectation value of the quark propagator for flavor
r

S_{r}(p)=\int d^{4}x\,e^{ipx}\,\langle\psi_{r}(x)\overline{\psi}_{r}(0)\rangle\,.

(2)

All fields and operators are bare unless explicitly specified and expectation values are taken in isosymmetric QCD. In our derivation, we restrict ourselves to momenta sufficiently large to ignore the effects of spontaneous chiral-symmetry breaking
[
35
,
36

37
,
12

. In this limit and in the absence of a mass term, the inverse propagator is

S_{r}^{-1}(p)=-i\not{p}\,\Sigma_{r}(p^{2})\,.

\Gamma_{\mu,rs}^{X}(p,p^{\prime})=\int d^{4}x\,d^{4}y\,e^{ipx-ip^{\prime}y}\,\langle\psi_{r}(x)[\overline{\psi}_{r}\Gamma^{X}\psi_{s}](0)\overline{\psi}_{s}(y)\rangle\,,

\Gamma^{V}=\gamma_{\mu}\,,\quad\Gamma^{A}=\gamma_{\mu}\gamma_{5}\,,\quad\Gamma^{L}=\gamma_{\mu}P_{L}\,.

(6)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\mu}^{A}=\Lambda_{\mu}^{V}\gamma_{5}\,,\quad\Lambda_{\mu}^{L}=\Lambda_{\mu}^{V}P_{L}\,.

We impose

3
Wave function renormalization

The fist step in our program is the renormalization of the fields, generically given by

\psi^{R}=Z^{1/2}\,\psi\,.

(9)

Since the subtraction point is uniquely defined, we drop from the notation the dependence on the scale
p^{2}=-\mu^{2}
.
In the RI/MOM scheme the wave-function renormalization factor, which we denote with
Z^{\gamma}
, is fixed by the condition
[
9
]

\frac{1}{16N_{c}}\mathrm{tr}\left[i\frac{\partial S_{R}^{-1}(p)}{\partial p_{\mu}}\gamma_{\mu}\right]=1\,,

(10)

which implies

Z^{\gamma}=\Sigma(p^{2})+\frac{p^{2}}{2}\Sigma^{\prime}(p^{2})\,.

(11)

Instead, we label with
Z^{\not{p}}
the quark field renormalization factor in the RI’/MOM and RI/SMOM schemes, fixed by the different condition

\frac{1}{4N_{c}p^{2}}\mathrm{tr}\left[i\not{p}\,S_{R}^{-1}(p^{2})\right]=1\,,

(12)

which, using Eq. (
3
), leads to the relation

Z^{\not{p}}=\Sigma(p^{2})\,.

(13)

The two different wave-function renormalization factors can be easily related to each other according to

Z^{\gamma}-Z^{\not{p}}=\frac{p^{2}}{2}\Sigma^{\prime}(p^{2})\,.

(14)

4
Four-fermion operator

For a generic wave-function renormalization factor
Z
, renormalized amputated amplitudes read

\Lambda_{\mu}^{X,R}(p,p^{\prime})=Z_{X}Z^{-1}\Lambda_{\mu}^{X}(p,p^{\prime})\,.

(15)

By introducing a generic projector
[P_{\mu}]^{\alpha\beta,ab}
obeying

\mathrm{tr}[P^{\mu}\gamma_{\mu}]=1\,,

(16)

we impose the following renormalization condition on the vector flavor-changing bilinear operator

Z_{V}\frac{\mathrm{tr}[P^{\mu}\Lambda_{\mu}^{V}(p,p^{\prime})]}{Z}=1\,.

(17)

The next step in our discussion is the amputated four-fermion amplitude
\Lambda_{\mathcal{O}}
. Since our goal is to focus on QCD corrections, the four-fermion amplitude factorizes in the isosymmetric limit according to

\begin{split}[\Lambda_{\mathcal{O}}(p,p^{\prime})]^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta,ab}&\,=[\Lambda_{\mu}^{L}(p,p^{\prime})]^{\alpha\beta,ab}[\gamma^{\mu}P_{L}]^{\gamma\delta}\\
&\,\equiv[\Lambda_{\mu}^{L}(p,p^{\prime})]\otimes[\gamma^{\mu}P_{L}]\,.\end{split}

(18)

Renormalization conditions of the form

\mathcal{P}[\Lambda_{\mathcal{O}}(p,p^{\prime})]=1\,,

(19)

may be imposed both in pure QCD and subsequently in the full theory, using a projector with the general form

P^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta,ab}=[P^{\mu}P_{R}]^{\alpha\beta,ab}[\gamma_{\mu}P_{R}]^{\gamma\delta}=[P^{\mu}P_{R}]\otimes[\gamma_{\mu}P_{R}]\,,

(20)

with the understanding that its action is specified by

\mathcal{P}[\Lambda_{\mathcal{O}}(p,p^{\prime})]\equiv P^{\beta\alpha\delta\gamma,ba}[\Lambda_{\mathcal{O}}(p,p^{\prime})]_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta,ab}\,.

(21)

Notice how the equation above defines a double-trace scheme differing from Ref.
[
2
]
based on a single trace.
In the absence of isospin-breaking effects, the renormalization of the left current and of the semi-leptonic operator is completely fixed by its vector component

\mathcal{P}[\Lambda_{\mathcal{O}}(p,p^{\prime})]=\mathrm{tr}[P^{\mu}\Lambda_{\mu}^{V}(p,p^{\prime})]\,.

(22)

In fact, thanks to chiral symmetry and by demanding the additional property
\{P_{\mu},\gamma_{5}\}=0
, we have (for
p,p^{\prime},q\gg\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}
)

\mathrm{tr}[P^{\mu}\gamma_{5}\Lambda^{A}_{\mu}(p,p^{\prime})]=-\mathrm{tr}[P^{\mu}\Lambda_{\mu}^{V}(p,p^{\prime})]\,,

(23)

which implies that

\mathrm{tr}[P^{\mu}P_{R}\Lambda_{\mu}^{L}(p,p^{\prime})]=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}[P^{\mu}\Lambda_{\mu}^{V}(p,p^{\prime})]\,,

(24)

namely the renormalization of the axial and left currents, in QCD, is completely fixed by
Z_{V}
which becomes the central investigation of our study.

The bare amputated amplitude of the vector operator for general kinematics obeys the Ward Identity

q^{\mu}\Lambda_{\mu}^{V}(p,p^{\prime})=iS^{-1}(p)-iS^{-1}(p^{\prime})\,.

(25)

Once expressed in terms of renormalized fields and operators, the Ward identity,

q^{\mu}\Lambda_{\mu}^{V,R}(p,p^{\prime})=iZ_{V}\left(S_{R}^{-1}(p)-S_{R}^{-1}(p^{\prime})\right)\,,

(26)

keeps its functional form if
Z_{V}=1
. As a consequence the renormalization condition in Eq. (
17
) turns into

\mathrm{tr}[P^{\mu}\Lambda_{\mu}^{V}(p,p^{\prime})]=Z(p^{2})\,.

(27)

When this is the case, once the projector and operator are promoted to the semi-leptonic case pure QCD corrections are absent order by order, making them preferable in general, but also for the perturbative calculation of the corresponding Wilson coefficients
[
2
]
. Therefore in the rest of the manuscript, we focus on schemes in which we can prove that
Z_{V}=1
, or said differently, where we have equations of the form (
27
).

5
Vector current

We restrict the discussion to the popular MOM and SMOM kinematic configurations, defined by
q_{\mu}=0
and
q^{2}=-\mu^{2}
respectively.
Since in both cases
p^{2}
,
p^{\prime 2}
and
p\cdot p^{\prime}
are all linearly dependent, a generic tensor decomposition of the amplitude is

\Lambda_{\mu}^{V}(p,p^{\prime})=\gamma^{\nu}T_{\mu\nu}^{i}(p,p^{\prime})F_{i}(p^{2})\,,

(28)

with
F_{i}(p^{2})
scalar functions.

5.1
Exceptional kinematics

In the original RI/MOM proposal the focus was on exceptional kinematics
[
9
]
. Despite being less appealing in high-precision modern calculations, e.g. due to the pion-pole contamination at low momenta
[
35
,
36
,
9
,
37
,
12
]
, we discuss it to illustrate (non-perturbatively) why the choice of projector is important. This will set the stage for the more-involved non-exceptional kinematics, the target of this work.

Here, the Ward identity in Eq. (
25
) reduces to

\Lambda_{\mu}^{V}(p,p)=i\frac{\partial S^{-1}(p)}{\partial p^{\mu}}\,.

(29)

By examining the derivative of the propagator on the r.h.s., we immediately notice that the relevant Lorentz structures are
\gamma_{\mu}
and
p_{\mu}\not{p}
, fixing the tensor in Eq. (
28
) to be
[
11
]

T_{\mu\nu}^{i}(p,p)=\left\{g_{\mu\nu}\,,\frac{p_{\mu}p_{\nu}}{\mu^{2}}\right\}^{i}\,,

(30)

with
g_{\mu\nu}
denoting the Minkowski metric.
By saturating Eq. (
29
) with
\gamma^{\mu}
, we find

\frac{1}{16N_{c}}\mathrm{tr}\left[\gamma^{\mu}\Lambda_{\mu}^{V}(p,p)\right]=Z^{\gamma}\,,

(31)

which automatically implies that
Z_{V}=1
when the amputated Green’s function is renormalized with
Z^{\gamma}
. Instead, when we project the amplitude with the other structure, via eqs. (
13
) and (
14
) we find

\frac{1}{4N_{c}p^{2}}\mathrm{tr}[\Lambda_{\mu}^{V}p^{\mu}\not{p}]=4Z^{\gamma}-3Z^{\not{p}}\,.

(32)

By defining the appropriate linear combination that cancels the term proportional to
Z^{\gamma}
, we recover the projector
[
11
]

P_{\mu}^{\mathrm{RI^{\prime}}}=\frac{1}{12N_{c}}\gamma^{\nu}\left(g_{\mu\nu}-\frac{p_{\mu}p_{\nu}}{p^{2}}\right)\,,

(33)

for which
Z_{V}=1
when the amputation is performed with propagators renormalized with
Z^{\not{p}}
, since
P_{\mu}^{\mathrm{RI^{\prime}}}
obeys the relation

\mathrm{tr}[P^{\mu,\mathrm{RI^{\prime}}}\Lambda_{\mu}^{V}(p,p)]=Z^{\not{p}}\,.

(34)

From the physical point of view, we observe that the projector above attempts to restore the on-shell condition
F_{1}=\Sigma
by projecting away the structure proportional to
p_{\mu}\not{p}
.

This interplay between the projector and the field renormalization is a well-known fact and the condition above practically defines the RI’/MOM scheme
[
10
,
11
]
. The latter was introduced to overcome the practical difficulties in implementing
Z^{\gamma}
in Lattice QCD calculations, compared to the more easily accessible definition
Z^{\not{p}}
.
In Refs.
[
11
,
38
,
2
]
,
P_{\mu}^{\mathrm{RI^{\prime}}}
is derived by fixing the coefficients
c_{i}
in

P_{\mu}^{\mathrm{RI^{\prime}}}=\frac{1}{12N_{c}}\gamma^{\nu}T_{\mu\nu}^{i}(p,p)\,c_{i}\,,

(35)

to be solutions of the linear system

M^{ij}c_{j}=\{1,0\}^{i}\,,

(36)

with the matrix

M^{ij}=\frac{1}{3N_{c}}T_{\alpha\mu}^{i}(p,p)T_{\beta\nu}^{j}(p,p)g^{\alpha\beta}g^{\mu\nu}\,.

(37)

Our derivation, based on a simple linear combination of different variants of the projected Ward identity, confirms the results above.

5.2
Non-exceptional kinematics

Having established a framework parallel to the tensor decomposition of Refs.
[
11
,
33
,
2
]
, we now proceed to non-exceptional kinematics, a modern approach in momentum schemes due to the reduced sensitivity to non-perturbative effects at low energies

The Ward identity for SMOM kinematics simplifies to

q^{\mu}\Lambda_{\mu}^{V}(p,p^{\prime})=iS^{-1}(q)=\not{q}Z^{\not{p}}\,,

and when saturated with
\not{q}

\frac{1}{4N_{c}q^{2}}\mathrm{tr}[\not{q}q^{\mu}\Lambda_{\mu}^{V}(p,p^{\prime})]=Z^{\not{p}}\,,

(39)

it tells us that the projector
P_{\mu}^{\not{q}}=\frac{1}{4N_{c}}\frac{q_{\mu}\not{q}}{q^{2}}

equal to unity

We conclude that the
\not{q}

To extend this scheme, we consider the following additional projections

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4N_{c}p^{2}}\mathrm{tr}[\not{p}q^{\mu}\Lambda_{\mu}^{V}(p,p^{\prime})]=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}Z^{\not{p}}\,,

(40)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4N_{c}p^{2}}\mathrm{tr}[\not{p}^{\prime}q^{\mu}\Lambda_{\mu}^{V}(p,p^{\prime})]=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}Z^{\not{p}}\,.

Following the idea of Ref.

P_{\mu}^{\mathrm{RI/SMOM}_{y}}=\frac{1}{2N_{c}p^{2}}(y\not{p}+(y-1)\not{p}^{\prime})q_{\mu}\,,

satisfying the renormalized Ward identity for any value of

and incorporating

When inserted in Eq. (

\not{p}[F_{1}-\frac{1}{2}F_{2}+\frac{1}{2}F_{3}]-\not{p}^{\prime}[F_{1}+\frac{1}{2}F_{4}-\frac{1}{2}F_{5}-F_{6}]=\not{q}Z^{\not{p}}\,.

M^{ij}c_{j}=\left\{1,-\frac{x}{2},\frac{x}{2},\frac{1-x}{2},-\frac{1-x}{2},-(1-x)\right\}^{i}\,,

5.3
Fierz identities for the semi-leptonic operator

Before concluding we show the equivalence between the double-trace projectors for the semi-leptonic operator derived above with the single-trace convention used in Ref.
[
2
]
. To do so we rely on the Fierz identity

[P_{R}]^{\alpha\beta}[P_{L}]^{\gamma\delta}=\frac{1}{2}[\gamma^{\nu}P_{L}]^{\alpha\delta}[\gamma_{\nu}P_{R}]^{\gamma\beta}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{\nu}P_{L}\tilde{\otimes}\gamma_{\nu}P_{R}\,,

(51)

where the
\tilde{\otimes}
denotes open tensor indices that result in single fermion traces when applied as a projector.

Factoring off the color index contraction and using the expression for
P_{\mu}^{\overline{\mathrm{RI}}/\mathrm{SMOM}_{x}}
from Eq. (
50
), we rewrite our general form for the projector in Eq. (
20
) for SMOM kinematics as

\begin{split}P^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}&=[P^{\mu}P_{R}]^{\alpha\beta}[P_{L}\gamma_{\mu}]^{\gamma\delta}=\frac{1}{2}[P^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}P_{L}\gamma_{\mu}]^{\alpha\delta}[\gamma_{\nu}P_{R}]^{\gamma\beta}\\
&=-\frac{1}{12N_{c}\mu^{2}}\left[(x+1)\not{p}+(x-2)\not{p}^{\prime}\right]\gamma^{\nu}\not{q}P_{R}\tilde{\otimes}\gamma_{\nu}P_{R}\,.\end{split}

(52)

After simple algebraic manipulations we find

\begin{split}P^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}=&\frac{1}{12N_{c}\mu^{2}}\left[\mu^{2}(x-2)\gamma^{\mu}P_{R}\tilde{\otimes}\gamma_{\mu}P_{R}\right.\\
&\left.-2(x+1)\not{p}P_{R}\tilde{\otimes}\not{p}P_{R}+2(x+1)\not{p}^{\prime}P_{R}\tilde{\otimes}\not{p}P_{R}\right.\\
&\left.+2(x-2)\not{p}^{\prime}P_{R}\tilde{\otimes}\not{p}^{\prime}P_{R}-2(x-2)\not{p}P_{R}\tilde{\otimes}\not{p}^{\prime}P_{R}\right.\\
&\left.+(2x-1)\gamma^{\mu}\not{p}^{\prime}\not{p}P_{R}\tilde{\otimes}\gamma_{\mu}P_{R}\right],\end{split}

(53)

which matches the expression of the projector given in Eq. (2.34) of Ref.
[
2
]
.
Similarly, plugging the expression for
P^{\mu}
from Eq. (
33
) in Eq. (
20
), and making again use of the identity in Eq. (
51
), yields the projector introduced in Eq. (2.20) of Ref.
[
2
]
.
This concludes our proof on the equivalence of the
two prescriptions.

6
Conclusions

With our work, by building on Ref.
[
2
]
, we have introduced a family of projectors for the renormalization of the vector quark bilinear operator in QCD for RI/SMOM schemes which satisfy the Ward identity, in the sense that
Z_{V}=1
to all orders in perturbation theory.
This property becomes particularly convenient when the quark bilinear is paired with a lepton-neutrino pair to build a four-fermion EFT operator, relevant e.g. in hadronic
\tau
decays. In this framework a matching with the full Standard Model is required and this is typically performed up to a fixed order in perturbation theory. More specifically, by considering isospin-breaking effects to first order the most relevant corrections are of
O(\alpha\alpha_{s})
. As observed in Ref.
[
2
]
, choosing schemes in which
Z_{V}=1
leads to a smaller scale dependence of the Wilson coefficients and consequently to a better assessment of systematic errors due to missing higher orders, a very important aspect in high-precision calculations.

With our study, we have promoted the renormalization of the quark bilinear to the semi-leptonic operator, thereby obtaining a family of projectors that naturally satisfy the Ward identity in QCD. Our family elegantly incorporates
P_{\mu}^{\not{q}}

[
1
]
, which remains a very natural choice also for the renormalization of
\mathcal{O}_{rs}
.

Thanks to Fierz identities we have proven the equivalence with Ref.
[
2
]
, concluding that the Wilson coefficients calculated in that reference can be used also with the proposed double-trace form of the projectors derived in this work.
