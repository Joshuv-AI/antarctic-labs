1.0 Executive Summary
The objective of the project was to improve the resilience of the non-regional-grid-connected electrical
generation system at Grand Canyon West (GCW) to withstand short-term disruptions and rising energy
costs by installing an 885-kW solar photovoltaic (PV) array and 750-kilowatts (kW)/2,145-kilowatt hour
(kWh) battery energy storage system (BESS). The system is designed to provide 50% of the annual
energy needed for the essential facilities at GCW, supplementing the existing diesel generators, saving
approximately $463,339 per year and an estimated $11,583,475 over the 25-year life of the system.
The project aligns with the Tribe’s conservation ethic and self-determination in the area of energy
development.

2.0 Background
Grand Canyon West (GCW), the primary economic engine for the Hualapai Tribe, is not interconnected
to the regional electric utility grid and is instead served by a Hualapai-owned and operated micro-grid
which has been energized by three CAT 750-kW diesel generators since 2015. Diesel generation
produces energy at a less than economic cost of 36 cents per kWh, so the Tribe was seeking a lower
cost power source. Additional reasons to construct the project included extending the life of the diesel
generators, reducing noise from the existing generators, and furthering the Tribe’s conservation ethic.

The Hualapai Reservation comprises of approximately one million acres in northwest Arizona and was
established by Executive Order in 1883. Tribal membership is some 2,400 with just over half living on
the reservation. Traditionally, Hualapai inhabited an area up to seven million acres in northwestern
Arizona, from the Grand Canyon southward to the Bill Williams and Santa Maria Rivers and from the
Black Mountains eastward to the San Francisco Peaks located near what is today Flagstaff, Arizona.

A snapshot from the 2020 American Community Survey shows the population’s median age of 30.1
years, living in 513 households (3.15 persons per home). Twenty seven percent of adults do not have a
high school diploma. The highest levels of employment occur in the arts & entertainment, education,
transportation, and government with a labor participation of 43% for those 16 years of age and older.
The unemployment rate is 20.4%. Median annual income is $37,679 which results in a 35.3% poverty
rate. Median home values are $60,300 and 73% of homes have internet. The Tribe is governed by a
member-elected, nine-person Tribal Council and a Tribal Council appointed judiciary.

5.0 Conclusions and Recommendations
The solar PV array and BESS worked nearly flawlessly up until the evening of July 18th when the Zig-Zag
transformer caught fire. This resulted in the solar PV array and BESS systems shutting down leaving the
micro-grid off-line for over 24 hours. The Zig-Zag transformer provides a path to ground for the neutral
current caused by the load imbalance and drifting voltages on the existing micro-grid. As a result of
the Zig-Zag’s failure, the solar array and battery storage system have been offline since the evening of
July 18, 2024.

For the nine-month period of operations, the estimated reduction in generator diesel fuel use was
approximately 210,000 gallons, a cost savings of over $600,000 (see graph). This exceeded the 2020
projected savings of $463,339 for twelve months, largely due to the increase in the price of diesel fuel
over the past three years. Looking at the system’s performance starting in November of 2023 and
ending in June of 2024 – the eight full months of operation – the solar PV array and BESS supplied 55%
of the energy for the micro-grid. The solar PV array generated 1,097.2 MWh of actual energy as
compared to the estimated 1,223 MWhs during the eight months of full operation. This was a decrease
of ~10% in expected energy production which can be mostly attributed to afternoon curtailment and
mentioned above.

When operating, the system’s efficiency was approximately 90% with about half of the power loss
attributable to the direct current (DC) solar to alternating current (AC) conversion, which matched
Solon’s designed de-rating from 885 KV-DC to 840 KV-AC. There were additional losses of 4-5% from
the Mega Pack to the Zig-Zag transformer, and through the 20.8 KV step-up transformer as the power
exits the fence line onto the micro-grid.

As a result of the failure, Solon (in association with Tesla, Caterpillar & the HTUA), ran simulations to
determine the cause of the failure and provide a solution given that the equipment and workmanship
was their warranty responsibility. Initial solutions ranged from: 1) installing a new Zig-Zag transformer
(52-week lead time) or a rental unit in the meantime, 2) connecting the 14 inverters directly to the
micro-grid and by-pass the Mega Pack, and 3) replacing the main Delta-Wye transformer with a Wye-
Wye transformer. As of the date of this report, Solon’s proposed solution is to install a new and larger
Zig-Zag transformer to handle the load imbalances on the existing micro-grid. 1

Presentations were made at DOE’s annual program review in November of 2021, 2022, 2023 and 2024.
Also, the project was presented at the DOE’s Clean Energy Summit in Pechanga, California on February
28, 2024.

6.0 Lessons Learned

 • Setup system monitoring to alert users of potential problems such as the neutral current
 overload that appears to have caused the failure of the Zig-Zag transformer on July 18.

 • Resolve any system design issues, such as properly sizing the Zig-Zag transformer and increasing
 the size of the battery storage to store extra energy from the solar array, prior to developing
 Phase 2.

 • Work on optimizing the system to minimize the micro-grid’s diesel fuel consumption.

 • Increase the load on the micro-grid to reduce solar PV curtailment through grid expansion
 and/or by deploying EV charging stations around the campus. Conversely, increasing the size of
 the battery will reduce curtailment if increasing the load is not forthcoming.

 • The battery resolved a significant technical problem by absorbing excessive VARs produced by
 the micro-grid.

 • Retain qualified personnel: HTUA Generation Manager is a 4-year degreed electric utility and
 communications engineer.

 • Be sure to adequately budget for the replacement of the main battery which should be
 replaced in about 9 years.

1
 As of November 4, 2025, the new and properly sized Zig-Zag transformer ordered by Solon has yet to be shipped from
