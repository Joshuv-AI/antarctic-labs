Parameter optimization of IPT systems based on genetic algorithms

Jin Guo, Langtao Xing

ABSTRACT
Output power and transmission efficiency are key performance indicators for Wireless Power Transfer (WPT). Inductive Power Transfer (IPT) systems feature numerous resonant parameters that are interrelated, making it challenging to determine specific component values during system design. This paper investigates an S-P type IPT system and establishes an equivalent circuit model for the mutual inductance of the contactless transformers. The influence characteristics of coupling coefficient k, resonant angular frequency ω, and equivalent load resistance Re on circuit output power and transmission efficiency are analyzed. By setting the efficiency-power product as the objective function and k, ω, and Re as decision variables, this paper innovatively transforms the parameter design problem of the IPT system into a nonlinear parameter optimization problem. This approach not only enables the fitness function to better escape local optima. By adjusting the duty cycle of the Buck-Boost circuit, the equivalent load resistance is optimized to its optimal value. Optimize the parameters k, ω, and Re to maximize the system’s efficiency-power product output. Through parameter design, the IPT system attains a globally optimal design for both output power and transmission efficiency, resolving the challenge of determining parameters for resonant compensation structures.

1. Introduction Wireless Power Transfer (WPT) technology is an integrated approach that leverages power electronics and modern control theory to achieve wireless electrical energy transmission via electric or magnetic fields. This technology has become a research hotspot both domestically and internationally. Inductive Power Transfer (IPT) systems are widely applied in the field of wireless charging for electric vehicles, demonstrating broad application prospects [ 1 – 3 ]. Output power and transmission efficiency are critical performance parameters for evaluating IPT systems. Parameters such as the non-contact transformer specifications, resonant frequency, coupling coefficient, equivalent output load resistance, and resonant network parameters all influence output power and transmission efficiency [ 4 ]. Fluctuations in the positional offset between the primary and secondary coils of the non-contact transformer are common, primarily manifesting as vertical displacement, horizontal displacement, and spatial angular displacement, as illustrated in Fig 1 . The structure of the non-contact transformer determines parameters such as the coupling coefficient, self-inductance, and mutual inductance. Therefore, the optimized design of the non-contact transformer is another critical factor affecting the transmission efficiency and output power of wireless charging. These factors can all cause variations in the coupling coefficient. The equivalent load resistance changes during the charging process for different load objects. The accuracy of the resonant capacitor components also affects the output characteristics. Significant variations in IPT system circuit parameters can cause substantial fluctuations in output voltage, current, power, and transmission efficiency characteristics, complicating control and potentially leading to system instability in severe cases [ 5 , 6 ]. Consequently, optimizing the circuit parameters of IPT systems remains a current research challenge. 10.1371/journal.pone.0360165.g001 Fig 1 Coil space offset. To address the challenge of designing circuit parameters for IPT systems, researchers have conducted in-depth investigations and studies from various perspectives. In [ 7 ], the non-contact transformer coil was optimized to maintain a constant coupling coefficient during axial or lateral displacement. In [ 8 ], the output characteristics of the non-contact transformer’s lateral offset capability were enhanced by employing an optimized compensation capacitor method. In [ 9 – 12 ], an equivalent magnetic circuit model for contactless transformers was constructed to optimize their structural design and enhance wireless power transfer performance. In [ 13 ], the impact of variations in sensitivity parameters—such as load, mutual inductance, and capacitance deviation—on wireless charging systems was thoroughly analyzed. The study also demonstrated that impedance matching circuits can maximize power transfer efficiency. In [ 14 , 15 ], under coil spatial displacement conditions, the effects of load and quality factor on the output characteristics of a wireless charging system were analyzed, and the system parameter design was optimized. The aforementioned methods analyze the impact of circuit parameters on wireless charging systems from various perspectives and employ diverse techniques to optimize the structure of contactless transformers and circuit parameter design. While these approaches have improved the performance of wireless charging systems to a certain extent, several issues remain worthy of further refinement. Additionally, some scholars have combined multi-objective functions with nonlinear optimization algorithms to perform parameter optimization design within IPT systems. Genetic algorithms (GA) can handle complex combinatorial problems with faster convergence rates and higher efficiency. The application of genetic algorithms for optimizing WPT systems has emerged in recent years [ 16 – 18 ]. In [ 19 ], genetic algorithms were employed to optimize the coil, but the impact of optimization process details on the results was overlooked. In [ 20 ], a genetic algorithm was applied in two steps to design a resonant WPT system operating in the megahertz range. First, the algorithm extracted equivalent circuit parameters, then determined suitable values for the variable capacitor to eliminate frequency splitting. In [ 21 ], an optimization method based on a universal algorithm is proposed. During the design iteration, the coil track layout is interpreted using binary strings, and practical considerations and specific implementation methods for the optimization process are introduced. In [ 22 ], the receiving coil was optimized through a genetic algorithm, resulting in improved coupling performance. The proposed system achieved optimal transmission efficiency. In [ 23 ], a multi-objective particle swarm optimization algorithm is introduced for designing ring couplers to enhance wireless power transfer performance. The aforementioned scholars primarily conducted parameter analysis and optimization design targeting coupled structures, maximum output power, or efficiency enhancement, without comprehensively considering output power and transmission efficiency metrics for global system optimization. To address this gap, this paper employs the efficiency-power product as the objective function and k , ω , and R e as decision variables to achieve global optimal parameter design for IPT systems. In this work, based on the S-P type IPT system under study, an equivalent circuit model for non-contact transformer mutual inductance is established. First, the influence characteristics of coupling coefficient k , resonant angular frequency ω , and equivalent load resistance R e on circuit output power and transmission efficiency are analyzed. Subsequently, by treating the efficiency product as the objective function and k , ω , and R e as decision variables, the parameter design problem of the IPT system is innovatively transformed into a nonlinear parameter optimization problem. The equivalent load resistance is optimized by adjusting the duty cycle of the Buck-Boost circuit, achieving the maximum efficiency-power product output through parameter tuning of k , ω , and R e . Finally, an experimental wireless charging platform was constructed to validate the feasibility of the theoretical analysis. This work demonstrates the significant theoretical value of genetic algorithm-based parameter optimization design, while also providing a foundation for designing adaptive automatic tracking systems and enhancing the performance of wireless charging systems. The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: Section II analyzes the output characteristics of power and transmission efficiency for S-P type IPT systems and presents the analytical process for the maximum efficiency-power product objective. Section III details the specific process of genetic algorithm-based parameter optimization for the IPT system. Section IV constructs a wireless power transmission device to validate the theoretical analysis. The concluding section summarizes the research findings. 2. Basic principles of the IPT system Based on the inductive coupling method for power transmission, non-contact transformers in wireless charging circuits suffer from drawbacks such as high leakage inductance and low coupling coefficient. Therefore, resonant networks must be incorporated to enhance transmission efficiency and output power. Common resonant compensation structures include S-S, S-P, P-S, and P-P configurations, where the first S or P denotes series or parallel compensation on the primary side, and the second S or P indicates series or parallel compensation on the secondary side. The S-P compensation network offers the advantage of a significantly larger output resonant current. This paper primarily analyzes the S-P type resonant compensation circuit for wireless charging. 2.1. Parameter optimization of IPT systems based on genetic algorithms This paper combines adaptive genetic algorithms with nonlinear programming to optimize the system, enabling the algorithm to rapidly locate the global optimum solution. Genetic algorithms encode potential solutions to a problem as chromosomes, which undergo multiple generations of evolution (selection, crossover, mutation). The process terminates when defined constraints or iteration limits are met. Simultaneously, it employs a global parallel search to identify the optimal individual within the population, thereby selecting the best candidate as the solution. The nonlinear programming model for the system first defines the objective function, constraints, and parameters to be optimized. The IPT system adopts the maximum efficiency-power product as its optimization objective function. Considering inverter cost and high-frequency electromagnetic interference factors, the upper and lower limits of the inverter operating angular frequency are determined as ω min and ω max . Considering the distance between primary and secondary coils, offset position, and output power requirements in ICPT system applications, the upper and lower limits of the non-contact transformer coupling coefficient are set as k min and k max . The output power must exceed the system’s rated design power P e . To minimize system volume, reduce costs, and enhance transmission efficiency, the resonant inductance value should not be excessively large. After defining the constraint ranges for k and ω , the upper limits L P_h and L S_h for L P and L S can be determined based on the actual application requirements for the non-contact transformer’s area and distance, combined with the resonance conditions of the S-P resonant network. Based on the above analysis, the IPT system adopts the maximum efficiency-power product as its objective function, with the coupling coefficient k , resonant angular frequency ω , and equivalent load resistance R e serving as optimization variables. In the genetic algorithm depicted in Fig 8 , the coupling coefficient k , resonant angular frequency ω , and equivalent load resistance R e are encoded. In GA, the floating-point encoding method is suitable for representing numbers with a large range, as well as for genetic search in a large space, and also facilitates handling complex decision variable constraints. Therefore, this paper adopts the floating-point encoding method for the optimization variables, and the chromosome of the optimization parameters is described as: X = [ x 1 , x 2 , x 3 ] = [ k , ω , R e ] (12) The population size is set to 500, and the number of generations is set to 55. The algorithm uses the condition that the fitness change does not exceed a threshold of 0.02 as the termination criterion, and outputs the current maximum fitness value. The efficiency-power product λ serves as the fitness function, where individuals with higher function values exhibit greater fitness and superior quality. Individuals with higher fitness values within the population have a greater probability of being selected. Selected individuals form a new population and continue to reproduce to generate the next generation. The selection operation uses the proportional selection operator, which is a selection method that determines the probability of an individual’s offspring being inherited based on a probability proportional to the fitness of each individual. The probability P i that individual i is selected is given by: P i = λ i ∑ i = 1 n λ i i = 1 , 2 , 3 … (13) Where λ i is the fitness value of individual i. The crossover process is the main step in generating new individuals throughout the genetic algorithm. Parts of the chromosomes between two individuals are exchanged with a probability of P s. The value of P s cannot be too large, otherwise it will destroy the excellent individuals in the population, but it cannot be too small either, otherwise it will slow down the evolutionary speed of the population. In this paper, the mutation rate during the execution of genetic algorithms is used to maintain population diversity and prevent premature convergence. Therefore, this paper makes a trade-off between premature convergence and slow convergence of the algorithm through the trial-and-error method. When the algorithm is executed, the crossover rate P s is selected as 0.7 and the mutation rate as 0.03. Every 55 generations of genetic algorithm evolution, the obtained local optimal values are used as new individual chromosomes for further evolution. Eq. (11) serves as the fmincon function and its constraints in this optimization algorithm. The termination generation is set to 55, and computation stops when the weighted average change in the fitness function falls below the deviation of the fitness function value. Conclusion This paper investigates the S-P type IPT system and establishes an equivalent circuit model for the non-contact transformer mutual inductance. The expressions for the output power and transmission efficiency of the S-P resonant compensation network are derived. The influence characteristics of the coupling coefficient k , resonant angular frequency ω , and equivalent load resistance R e on the circuit output power and transmission efficiency are analyzed. The resonant network parameters of the IPT system are optimized using the efficiency-power product and a genetic algorithm. This approach resolves the challenge of determining parameters for resonant compensation structures. By setting the efficiency-power product as the objective function and k , ω , and R e as decision variables, the paper innovatively transforms the IPT system design problem into a nonlinear parameter optimization problem. This not only enables the fitness function to escape local optima more effectively but also significantly accelerates the algorithm’s convergence speed. The equivalent load resistance is optimized by adjusting the duty cycle of the Buck-Boost circuit. Parameters k , ω , and R e are tuned to achieve the maximum efficiency power product output. Through parameter design, the IPT system attains a globally optimal design for both output power and transmission efficiency. Supporting information S1 Data Minimum dataset. (XLSX)
