Executive Summary of CRADA Work:

This TCF will focus on developing use-cases for scalable and optimal utilization of hydrogen systems
(combination of electrolyzers, hydrogen storage, and fuel cells) when integrated with the power grid. We
will utilize and mature the electrolyzer dispatch control developed in SWR-20-06.

Summary of Research Results:

Task 1: Hydrogen systems: Architecture and modelling (NREL):

This task will focus on developing and refining the fundamental simulation models for assets
(electrolyzers, fuel-cells, compressed hydrogen storage) which form the hydrogen systems.
These asset models will be integrated into different architectures to represent application-based
configurations of integrated hydrogen systems. NREL will be developing the asset models and
Eaton will help refine them during development.

Goal: A stable hydrogen system model with controllable electrolyzer, fuel-cell and compressed
hydrogen storage in multiple configurations representing use-cases of interest.

Task 2: Market valuation of energy markets, Demand profiles (NREL, Eaton)

This task will focus on evaluating the market opportunities to estimate the performance
requirements for HYPER-V, demand profiles (to serve as use-cases), and revenue estimation by
providing these services. As described in the commercialization plan (Pg. 2), grid ancillary
service markets will be the primary focus for this agreement. The configuration needs and
relevance of HYPER-V to other H2@SCALE markets using hydrogen as fuel (e.g.,
transportation, long-term storage) will be explored for future commercialization opportunities
using HYPER-V in higher TRL. Eaton will provide data/feedback to determine the demand
profiles and help design the market scenarios. NREL will develop candidate markets and demand
profiles including the inputs from Eaton.

Goal: Demand profiles based on three or more service markets, and approach for revenue
estimation.

Task 3: HYPER-V based Hydrogen Systems: Optimal configuration, control, and sizing (NREL):

Service requests like different kinds of demand response, resource adequacy and backup power
can be condensed into demand profiles to be served. Ideally, HYPER-V will translate these
demand profiles into energy and power response needed by the aggregated site. This task will
consider the following:

 a) Optimal control of hydrogen systems for stable integration.

 b) Application specific configurations to identify if certain configurations of assets will be
 better suited for a given market. Long term, this will help develop competitive products
 best suited for the respective markets.

 c) Sizing of hydrogen systems and storage to balance the system configurations for cost and
 effective application for a given application.

NREL will be responsible for maturing HYPER-V to implement the use-cases determining
optimal configuration, control, and sizing for multiple use-cases. Eaton will review intermediate
results and help evaluate the results for commercialization viability.

Goal: Stable HYPER-V based control of hydrogen systems for grid support; Identifying the impact
of different configurations and sizing strategies on the system design.

Task 4: Lab Implementation: HYPER-V based hydrogen systems (NREL):

This task focuses on integrating hydrogen systems in a simulated environment with HYPER-V
based controller for real-time control. Under this task, HYPER-V will be refined to optimize the
response of hydrogen system assets for a service request and operate the system in a stable
manner. Introduction of additional assets on site (e.g., PV, storage, system load) should affect the
response from HYPER-V as anticipated. Therefore, this task will develop the use-cases and
demonstrate the performance of HYPER-V using the simulation model at the end of the project.
NREL will be responsible for implementing the demonstration in a real-time controller. Eaton
will review the developments and evaluate the outcomes for commercialization in markets of
interest. This is also a deliverable for CRADA (D4).

Goal: A functional software-based integration of HYPER-V to a commercial site model simulated
in real-time to demonstrate the performance of HYPER-V.
