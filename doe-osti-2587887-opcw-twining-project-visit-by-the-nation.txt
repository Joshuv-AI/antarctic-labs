PURPOSE FOR TRIP
Through the Organisation for Prohibition of Chemical Weapons (OPCW) Twinning and Assistance
Program, Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory, Forensic Science Center (LLNL) has been
paired with Morocco’s National Laboratory of Scientific and Technical Police (LNPST), with the
goal of mentoring LNPST to become an OPCW Designated Laboratory. The purpose of this visit
was for LLNL staff to mentor staff from LNPST as they participated in hands-on, laboratory-based
exercises to analyze proficiency test samples prepared by LLNL. This visit was designed to help
LNPST scientists strengthen their skills in OPCW proficiency testing.

OVERVIEW OF MENTORING STRATEGY
LLNL developed a five-day agenda to mentor staff from LNPST. LLNL designed a three-sample,
environmental proficiency test (PT), to be used as a mentoring tool for the LNPST staff (see
Appendix A). The PT was designed to be of greater difficulty than the OPCW CCACT samples
previously analyzed by LNPST and included a spiked chemical not in OPCW databases and would
require LNPST staff to solve a chemical structure elucidation problem. The visit agenda included
introductions of participants, a tour of LLNL laboratories, hands-on, laboratory-based sample
preparation exercises, exercises in data interpretation to enable LNPST to correlate data from
multiple analytical techniques (e.g. GC-MS (EI), GC-MS (CI), GC-NCD, GC-FPD, and LC-MS), and a
lecture on the use of high-resolution LC-MS. The goals of the visit were to ensure that LNPST staff
could successfully analyze the proficiency test samples by the end of the visit and to familiarize
them with equipment that they expect to procure for their laboratory.

The visit was facilitated by LLNL scientists who served interpreters, translating from French to
English. While not always necessary because most of the LNPST staff had a good working
knowledge of English (primary language of LLNL), the interpreters made communications more
efficient.

IMPLEMENTATION
Activities during the visit were conducted as described below.

Day 1. Introductions to laboratory staff and the PT scenario.
Introductions between staff members were made and a tour of key LLNL sample preparation and
instrumentation labs was given. LLNL emphasized the importance of maintaining an inventory
of key sample preparation supplies (e.g. disposable vials, solvents, pipets, vortex mixers, etc.),
the importance of having access to a suite of analytical instruments (e.g. GC-element specific
detectors, GC-MS, LC-MS, and NMR), and the importance of maintaining an inventory of
chemicals for use as references and in micro synthesis.
An introduction was given to the scenario of the PT, on which the LNPST team would spend the
remainder of the week working. LNPST was introduced to the strategies and tools
(e.g. whiteboards and sample preparation flow charts) that LLNL uses to facilitate team meetings

and communications. LNPST strategized the types of sample preparation and analysis they would
first perform to provide the maximum amount of information about the samples in a minimal
amount of time.
The test scenario and analysis request were as follows:

 A State Party has presented, in accordance with the Convention, an inspection
 request for a challenge inspection. The Director General, in accordance with Part
 X of the Verification Annex, sent a challenge inspection team to a chemical
 research and development (R&D) laboratory that has been accused of performing
 chemical weapon research.

 The facility described is an R&D laboratory for synthesis and process development
 for intermediates and new chemical compounds. Large numbers of liquid and solid
 waste containers were found on the premises. The inspection team collected
 samples from a container labelled "Aqueous Waste“, another from a container
 labelled “Organic Waste“ and a “Soil” sample near the waste containers.

 Please analyze the three samples for the presence of any Scheduled Chemicals and
 chemicals listed in the list of “Additional Non-Scheduled Reportable Chemicals”
 (QDOC/LAB/WI/PT04 Annex 1).

 Non-Scheduled precursors/reaction products, which are one reaction step away
 from Scheduled Chemicals, and whose phosphorus, sulfur, nitrogen and/or arsenic
 containing moiety is still present, must also be reported with the exception of those
 chemicals that would be reportable only because of being one reaction step away
 from chemicals in Schedule 3.

 Note: Do not assume any correlation between the samples.

Day 2. Focus on data analysis and daily briefings.
Using computers equipped with relevant software (Agilent ChemStation, Themo Qual Browser,
and OPCW OCAD and VGWD databases), LNPST staff analyzed GC-FPD, GC-N, GC-MS (EI) and
GC-MS (CI) datafiles from the PT samples that were previously collected by LLNL staff. Datafiles
given to LNPST staff on Day 1 contained information that would be available from the first
preparations of PT samples -- direct injections of the organic liquid sample and direct injections
of an organic extract from the soil sample. LNPST staff learned the value of fusing data from GC
element-specific detectors and GC-MS (EI). This exercise was designed to enhance LNPST skills
because LNPST currently has a GC-N detector and anticipates the procurement of a GC-FPD
(which is critical to identifying Scheduled Chemicals containing sulfur and phosphorus). After
their first look at the data, LNPST identified three of eight spiking chemicals —
N,N-dipropylbutaimidamide, N,N-dipropylethanimidamide, and dipinacolyl methylphosphonate.

Day 3. Focus on data analysis and daily briefings.
LNPST staff continued to examine collected datafiles; the main activity of the day was to examine
datafiles associated with silylation (TMS and TBDMS) derivatizations and to continue to look for
reportable chemicals in the PT samples. Another focus of the day was to impart the importance
of resolving the identities of any nitrogen-, phosphorus-, or sulfur-containing chemicals detected
with element specific detectors (i.e. identification of pesticide interferences vs. reportable
chemicals). By the end of the day, LNPST had identified another spiking chemical (dipentyl
methylphosphonate), bringing their total to four of eight chemicals identified. In addition, LNPST
clearly experienced and recognized the value of fusing data provided by GC-element specific
detectors with GC-MS (EI) and GC-MS (CI); this PT exercise represented the first time that staff
were able to obtain experience with this activity.

Day 4. Focus on sample preparation and continued data analysis.
Most of the day was spent in the sample preparation laboratory where LNPST staff received
hands-on training with various preparation procedures for organic, aqueous, and soil samples.
LNPST staff were introduced to the use of a Reacti-Vap™ Evaporator, which allows multiple
samples to be evaporated and heated in parallel (this is a capability that LNPST currently does
not have, but is expecting to obtain).

After laboratory exercises, LNPST staff continued to examine collected datafiles and identified
methylphosphonic acid, ethylphosphonic acid, and propylphosphonic acid in the samples
(bringing their total to seven out of eight chemicals identified).

Day 5. Focus on continued data analysis, introduction to liquid chromatography-high resolution
 mass spectrometry, and next steps for LNPST.

With guidance from their mentors, LNPST identified the last chemical,
N,N-diethylethanimidamide, in the organic sample. The identification of this chemical was the
most difficult part of the PT, as this chemical was not in any mass spectrometric database and
served to illustrate the reason why a laboratory needs synthesis capabilities for chemical
identification. LNPST staff received a briefing on the use of LC-HRMS; while the LNPST laboratory
does not currently have this capability, they are hoping to procure this instrumentation in the
future.

SUMMARY AND FOLLOW-ON WORK
LNPST staff successfully completed a “dry” LLNL-designed PT. During the visit, they were
introduced to and practiced fusing data from multiple instruments to identify reportable
chemicals and test chemicals. LNPST staff were introduced to the procedures by which LLNL
executes PT. They were also introduced to new instruments (e.g. LC-MS) and equipment that
they are expected to procure for their laboratory.
Suggested follow-up work includes continued communications with the laboratory by
teleconferencing and a potential follow-up visit by LLNL to the LNPST laboratory after they
procure their new instrumentation (e.g. GC-FPD and LC-MS).
