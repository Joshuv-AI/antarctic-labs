The Science

The AMOC is a large-scale ocean circulation that strongly affects environmental features such
as sea level, sea ice and tropical cyclones. Earth system models often struggle to simulate
AMOC accurately, especially at coarse resolutions where mesoscale ocean eddies are unre-
solved, which degrades their predictions of other environmental phenomena. The AMOC is
sustained in part by changes in surface density, or water mass transformations, in the subpolar
North Atlantic Ocean. At higher densities where these transformations converge, surface water
mass formation (SWMF) injects dense water into the AMOC. Researchers recently investigated
the connection between SWMF and the properties of AMOC source waters in global ocean-sea
ice simulations at non-eddying and eddying resolution, using the DOE Energy Exascale Earth
System Model (E3SM).

 1

The Impact

Researchers found that, prior to an AMOC decline in the non-eddying simulation, SWMF occurs
at progressively lighter densities in the Irminger and Labrador seas, while the seawater exported
from these regions becomes increasingly warm and salty. The decreasing density of SWMF
is caused by the surface ocean becoming too fresh due to the lack of eddies. Since SWMF
tends to be relatively cool and fresh, the warmer and saltier export indicates that the SWMF
contribution to the AMOC source waters is weakened when eddies are absent. These results
demonstrate the need for greater fidelity of mesoscale ocean dynamics in these deep water
formation regions when predicting AMOC. They also demonstrate the diagnostic power of water
mass analysis in multiple coordinate systems for understanding AMOC in Earth system models.

Summary

To complete this study, researchers developed a suite of water mass diagnostics to be compat-
ible with E3SM simulation data. These diagnostics were applied to two ocean-sea ice simula-
tions run using E3SM Version 2.0 on non-eddy-resolving and eddy-resolving ocean meshes.
The atmospheric component was forced using reanalysis data spanning 1948–2009. The sim-
ulations were analyzed over a 5-year period coinciding with surface freshening and AMOC
decline in the non-eddying simulation. SWMF was calculated from the surface heat, salt, and
freshwater fluxes, and transport across boundaries was calculated from the mesh edge ve-
locities. Both SWMF and cross-boundary transport were binned by density, temperature, and
salinity to produce diagnostics in density coordinates and in temperature–salinity coordinates.
