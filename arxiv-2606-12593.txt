generalized norm retrieval and generalized phase retrieval in Hilbert spaces
Gholamreza Rahimlou ∗, Vahid Sadri

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce generalized phase retrieval (briefly, g-phase retrieval) and generalized norm retrieval (briefly, g-norm retrieval). Then, we present some properties of these concepts in Hilbert spaces, with special emphasis on ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} . Finally, the stability of g-norm retrieval for ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} is proved.

1.
Introduction

The concept of frames in a separable Hilbert space was originally introduced by Duffin and Schaeffer in the context of non-harmonic Fourier series
[
5
]
. Frames are redundant sets of vectors that provide a stable but non-unique representation for each vector in the space
[
1
,
4
,
6
,
8
,
7
,
10
,
9
]
. Generalized frames (g-frames) and g-Riesz bases were introduced by Sun
[
10
]
and further developed in subsequent works.

Phase retrieval and norm retrieval are among the most actively studied topics in applied harmonic analysis. Phase retrieval for Hilbert space frames was introduced in
[
2
]
and has since become a significant area of research
[
3
,
11
]
. In general, phase retrieval aims to recover a signal (up to a unimodular constant, e.g., a sign in the real case or a complex phase in the complex case) from the magnitudes of its measurements obtained through a redundant linear system. Norm retrieval, on the other hand, focuses on recovering only the norm of the signal from such measurements.

Although much work has been devoted to complex infinite-dimensional phase retrieval, only a few papers have addressed infinite-dimensional real phase retrieval or norm retrieval. In this paper, we introduce two new generalizations:
generalized phase retrieval
(g-phase retrieval) and
generalized norm retrieval
(g-norm retrieval). These notions extend the classical framework by replacing vectors with bounded linear operators between Hilbert spaces, thus providing a unified approach that encompasses both standard frames and g-frames.

We present several properties of g-phase retrieval and g-norm retrieval in general Hilbert spaces, with a particular focus on the finite-dimensional Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Among the main contributions, we establish the stability of g-norm retrieval for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. More precisely, we show that if a family of operators performs g-norm retrieval up to a small error, then it is possible to construct a nearby family that performs exact g-norm retrieval. This stability result is of practical importance for applications such as signal processing and phase retrieval with noisy measurements.

Throughout this paper,
H
and
K
denote separable Hilbert spaces, and
\mathcal{B}(H,K)
is the set of all bounded linear operators from
H
to
K
. When
K=H
, we write
\mathcal{B}(H)
instead of
\mathcal{B}(H,H)
. Let
\mathrm{GL}(H)
be the set of all
U\in\mathcal{B}(H)
that have a bounded inverse. For
U\in\mathcal{B}(H,K)
,
\mathcal{R}(U)
stands for the range of
U
. Given a closed subspace
W
of
H
,
P_{W}
denotes the orthogonal projection onto
W
. Finally,
\{H_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is a sequence of Hilbert spaces indexed by a countable set
\mathbb{I}\subset\mathbb{Z}
.

Definition 1.1

(
frame
)
.

Let
\{f_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{I}}
be a sequence of members of
H
. We say that
\{f_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{I}}
is a frame for
H
if there exist
0<A\leq B<\infty
such that for each
f\in H
,

(1)

\displaystyle A\|f\|^{2}\leq\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}|\langle f,f_{i}\rangle|^{2}\leq B\|f\|^{2}.

The constants
A
and
B
are called frame bounds. If the right hand of (
1
) holds, we say that
\{f_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{I}}
is a Bessel sequence with bound
B
.
We say that
\{f_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{I}}
is a
A
-tight frame (or just tight frame) for
H
, if we have

\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}|\langle f,f_{i}\rangle|^{2}=A\|f\|^{2},

for each
f\in H
. If
A=1
, the set
\{f_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{I}}
is called a Parseval frame.

Definition 1.2

(
g-frame
)
.

A family
\Lambda:=\{\Lambda_{i}\in\mathcal{B}(H,H_{i})\}_{i\in\mathbb{I}}
is called a g-frame for
H
with respect to
\{H_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{I}}
, if there exist
0<A\leq B<\infty
such that

(2)

A\|x\|^{2}\leq\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\|\Lambda_{i}x\|^{2}\leq B\|x\|^{2},\ \ x\in H.

Throughout this paper, let
\Lambda=\{\Lambda_{i}\in\mathcal{B}(H,H_{i}):i\in I\}
.
The constants
A
and
B
are called the lower and upper g-frame bounds for
\Lambda
, respectively.
We say that
\Lambda
is a g-Bessel sequence with bound
B
if only the right-hand side inequality of (
2
) holds.

\Lambda
is called a tight g-frame if
A=B
.Also,
\Lambda
is called a Parseval g-frame if
A=B=1
.

If
\Lambda
is a g-Bessel sequence, then the synthesis and analysis operators are defined by (for more details, we refer to
[
10
,
6
,
9
]
)

\displaystyle T_{\Lambda}:

\displaystyle(\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\oplus H_{i})_{\ell^{2}}\rightarrow H,\qquad T_{\Lambda}^{*}:H\rightarrow(\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\oplus H_{i})_{\ell^{2}},

\displaystyle T_{\Lambda}(\{x_{i}

\displaystyle\}_{i\in\mathbb{I}})=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\Lambda_{i}^{\ast}(x_{i}),\qquad T_{\Lambda}^{\ast}(x)=\{\Lambda_{i}x\}_{i\in\mathbb{I}},

where, the notation
(\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\oplus H_{i})_{\ell^{2}}
will indicate the space

\displaystyle\big\{\{x_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\ |\ x_{i}\in H_{i}\ ,\ \sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\|x_{i}\|^{2}<\infty\big\}.

It is a Hilbert space with pointwise operations, and inner product defined by

\langle\{x_{i}\},\{y_{i}\}\rangle:=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\langle x_{i},y_{i}\rangle,\ \ \{x_{i}\},\{y_{i}\}\in(\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\oplus H_{i})_{\ell^{2}}.

Now, the g-frame operator is given by

S_{\Lambda}x=T_{\Lambda}T^{*}_{\Lambda}x=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\Lambda^{\ast}_{i}\Lambda_{i}x,\ \ x\in H.

So, the g-frame operator is positive, self-adjoint and invertible. So, we can get

(3)

\displaystyle AI\leq S_{\Lambda}\leq BI,

and

(4)

\displaystyle x=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\Lambda_{i}^{\ast}\Lambda_{i}S_{\Lambda}^{-1}x,\qquad(x\in H).

It is obvious that when
\Lambda
is a g-Bessel sequence with the bound
B
, then for each
i\in\mathbb{I}
,

\|\Lambda_{i}\|=\sup_{\|x\|=1}\|\Lambda_{i}x\|\leq\sqrt{B},

therefore, we have

\|\Lambda_{i}^{*}(h_{i})\|\leq\sqrt{B}\|h_{i}\|,\qquad(\forall h_{i}\in H_{i}).

The following is a result for a stability of g-frames.

Lemma 1.1

(
[
6
]
)
.

Let
\Lambda
is a g-frame for
H
with bounds
A,B
and
\Theta:=\{\Theta_{i}\in\mathcal{B}(H,H_{i})\}_{i\in\mathbb{I}}
be a family of operators. If there exist a
0<R<A
such that

(5)

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\|\Lambda_{i}x-\Theta_{i}x\|^{2}\leq R\|x\|^{2},\qquad(\forall x\in H),

then,
\Theta
is a g-frame for
H
with bounds
(\sqrt{A}-\sqrt{R})^{2}
and
(\sqrt{B}+\sqrt{R})^{2}
.

Notice that, in Lemma
1.1
, when
\Lambda
is a just g-Bessel sequence for
H
with a bound
B
and there exists a
R>0
such that the relation (
5
) holds, then
\Theta
is a g-Bessel sequence too with a bound
(\sqrt{B}+\sqrt{R})^{2}
.

2.
generalized norm and generalized phase retrieval

This section is devoted to the definitions and basic properties of generalized norm retrieval and generalized phase retrieval. After establishing the necessary notation, we examine how these concepts relate to classical norm retrieval and phase retrieval.

Definition 2.1
.

We call that
\Lambda
is a generalized phase retrieval (or briefly g-phase retrieval) if for each
x,y\in H
satisfying
\|\Lambda_{i}x\|=\|\Lambda_{i}y\|
for all
i\in\mathbb{I}
, then there exist some scalar
\lambda
such that
|\lambda|=1
and
x=\lambda y
.

Definition 2.2
.

We say that
\Lambda
is a generalized norm retrieval (or briefly g-norm retrieval) if for each
x,y\in H
satisfying
\|\Lambda_{i}x\|=\|\Lambda_{i}y\|
for all
i\in\mathbb{I}
, then
\|x\|=\|y\|
.

It is clear that if
\Lambda
is a g-phase retrieval, then it is a g-norm retrieval. Also, if
\Lambda
is a tight g-frame, then it dose g-norm retrieval. Indeed, if
A
is a tight g-frame bound for
\Lambda
and
\|\Lambda_{i}x\|=\|\Lambda_{i}y\|
for each
x,y\in H
and
i\in\mathbb{I}
, we have

\displaystyle A\|x\|^{2}=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\|\Lambda_{i}x\|^{2}=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\|\Lambda_{i}y\|^{2}=A\|y\|^{2},

and we get
\|x\|=\|y\|
.

Example 2.1
.

Let
H=\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
be a standard basis. For each
x\in H
, we define

\displaystyle\Lambda_{1}:

\displaystyle H\rightarrow\mathbb{C},\qquad\Lambda_{1}x=\langle x,e_{1}\rangle,

\displaystyle\Lambda_{2}:

\displaystyle H\rightarrow\mathbb{C},\qquad\Lambda_{2}x=\langle x,e_{2}\rangle,

\displaystyle\Lambda_{3}:

\displaystyle H\rightarrow\mathbb{C},\qquad\Lambda_{3}x=\langle x,e_{1}+e_{2}\rangle.

So, it is obvious that
\{\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2},\Lambda_{3}\}
is a g-frame for
H
with bounds
1
and
3
. Now, assume that
\|\Lambda_{i}x\|=\|\Lambda_{i}y\|
where
x=(x_{1},x_{2})
,
y=(y_{1},y_{2})
and
i=1,2,3
. Therefore, we get

\displaystyle\begin{cases}|x_{1}|=|y_{1}|,\\
|x_{2}|=|y_{2}|,\\
|x_{1}+x_{2}|=|y_{1}+y_{2}|.\end{cases}

Then, the set
\{\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2},\Lambda_{3}\}
is a g-norm retrieval and g-phase retrieval with
\lambda=\pm 1
.

Example 2.2
.

Let
H=\mathbb{R}^{3}
. For each
x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in H
, we define

\displaystyle\Lambda_{1}:

\displaystyle H\rightarrow H,\qquad\Lambda_{1}x=(x_{1},0,0),

\displaystyle\Lambda_{2}:

\displaystyle H\rightarrow H,\qquad\Lambda_{2}x=(0,x_{2},0),

\displaystyle\Lambda_{3}:

\displaystyle H\rightarrow H,\qquad\Lambda_{3}x=(0,0,x_{3}),

\displaystyle\Lambda_{4}:

\displaystyle H\rightarrow H,\qquad\Lambda_{4}x=(0,0,x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}).

So, it is obvious that
\{\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2},\Lambda_{3},\Lambda_{4}\}
is a g-frame for
H
with bounds
1
and
4
. Suppose that
\|\Lambda_{i}x\|=\|\Lambda_{i}y\|
such that
x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
,
y=(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})
and
i=1,2,3,4
.
Then, the set
\{\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2},\Lambda_{3},\Lambda_{4}\}
is a g-norm retrieval and g-phase retrieval with
\lambda=\pm 1
.

Example 2.3
.

Let
H=\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}
be a standard basis. We define

\displaystyle\Lambda_{1}:

\displaystyle H\rightarrow H,\qquad\Lambda_{1}e_{1}=e_{2},\qquad\Lambda_{1}e_{2}=e_{3},\qquad\Lambda_{1}e_{3}=e_{1},

\displaystyle\Lambda_{2}:

\displaystyle H\rightarrow H,\qquad\Lambda_{2}e_{1}=e_{1},\qquad\Lambda_{2}e_{2}=e_{3},\qquad\Lambda_{2}e_{3}=0.

So, it is obvious that
\{\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}\}
is a g-frame for
H
with bounds
1
and
2
. But, if
\|\Lambda_{2}x\|=\|\Lambda_{2}y\|
where
x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
,
y=(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})
, therefore
\|x\|\neq\|y\|
and so the set dose not g-norm retrieval and g-phase retrieval.

Example 2.4
.

Let
H=\mathbb{R}^{2}
and for any
x=(x_{1},x_{2})
define

\displaystyle\Lambda_{1}:

\displaystyle H\rightarrow H,\qquad\Lambda_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})=(x_{1},x_{2}),

\displaystyle\Lambda_{2}:

\displaystyle H\rightarrow H,\qquad\Lambda_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})=(x_{2},-x_{1}).

So, it is obvious that
\{\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}\}
is a g-tight frame for
H
with the bound
2
and so the set dose g-norm retrieval, but the set dose not g-phase retrieval.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\{\Lambda_{i}U\in\mathcal{B}(H,H_{i})\}_{i\in\mathbb{I}}
be a g-norm retrieval for all
U\in\mathcal{GL}(H)
. Then
\{\Lambda_{i}\in\mathcal{B}(H,H_{i})\}_{i\in\mathbb{I}}
is also a g-norm retrieval.

Proof.

Suppose that
x,y\in H
and
\|\Lambda_{i}x\|=\|\Lambda_{i}y\|
for any
i\in\mathbb{I}
. For any
U\in\mathcal{GL}(H)
there exist
x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in H
such that
Ux^{\prime}=x
and
Uy^{\prime}=y
. Thus
\|\Lambda_{i}Ux^{\prime}\|=\|\Lambda_{i}Uy^{\prime}\|
. Therefore, via the assumption, we get
\|Ux^{\prime}\|=\|Uy^{\prime}\|
and so
\|x\|=\|y\|
.
∎

Lemma 2.1
.

If
\|Ux\|=\|Uy\|
for any
x,y\in H
and each
U\in\mathcal{GL}(H)
, then there exists a scalar
\lambda
such that
y=\lambda x
and
|\lambda|=1
.

Proof.

Let
k\in\mathbb{I}
and
x,y\in H
where
x\neq 0
and
\|Ux\|=\|Uy\|
for each
U\in\mathcal{GL}(H)
. Choose an orthonormal basis
\{e_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{I}}
for
H
with
e_{k}=\dfrac{x}{\|x\|}
and suppose that
y=\sum_{i\in I}y_{i}e_{i}
. Define
U:H\rightarrow H
with
Ue_{k}=e_{k}
and
Ue_{i}=\frac{1}{2}e_{i}
for any
i\in\mathbb{I}
and
i\neq k
. So,
U\in\mathcal{GL}(H)
and we get

Uy=y_{k}e_{k}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\neq k}y_{i}e_{i}.

Now, we have

\displaystyle\|x\|^{2}=\|Ux\|^{2}=\|Uy\|^{2}=y_{k}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i\neq k}y_{i}^{2}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\|x\|^{2}=\|y\|^{2}=y_{k}^{2}+\sum_{i\neq k}y_{i}^{2}.

Therefore,
\sum_{i\neq k}y_{i}^{2}=0
and so
y_{i}=0
for any
i\neq k
. Then, we get

y=y_{k}e_{k}=\frac{y_{k}}{\|x\|}x,

such that
\dfrac{|y_{k}|}{\|x\|}=1
.
∎

Theorem 2.2
.

The following conditions are equivalent.

(I)

\{\Lambda_{i}\in\mathcal{B}(H,H_{i})\}_{i\in\mathbb{I}}
is a g-phase retrieval.

(II)

\{\Lambda_{i}U\in\mathcal{B}(H,H_{i})\}_{i\in\mathbb{I}}
is a g-phase retrieval for all
U\in\mathcal{GL}(H)
.

(III)

\{\Lambda_{i}U\in\mathcal{B}(H,H_{i})\}_{i\in\mathbb{I}}
is a g-norm retrieval for all
U\in\mathcal{GL}(H)
.

Proof.

(I)\Rightarrow(II)
. Let
i\in\mathbb{I}
and
x,y\in H
. If
\|\Lambda_{i}Ux\|=\|\Lambda_{i}Uy\|
where
U\in\mathcal{B}(H)
is a invertible, so there exists a scalar
\lambda
such that
Ux=\lambda Uy
and
|\lambda|=1
. Then
x=\lambda y
.

(II)\Rightarrow(III)
is clear.

(III)\Rightarrow(I)
. Assume that
x,y\in H
and
\|\Lambda_{i}x\|=\|\Lambda_{i}y\|
for any
i\in\mathbb{I}
. Then
\|\Lambda_{i}UU^{-1}x\|=\|\Lambda_{i}UU^{-1}y\|
for each
U\in\mathcal{B}(H)
. So, via the assumption, we get
\|U^{-1}x\|=\|U^{-1}y\|
for each
U\in\mathcal{B}(H)
and by Lemma
2.1
the proof is completed.
∎

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
H=\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

\Lambda
is a g-norm retrieval on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For any partition
\{I_{i}\}_{i=1,2}
of
\mathbb{I}
we have
\spa\{\Lambda^{\ast}_{i}(H_{i})\}_{i\in I_{1}}^{\perp}\perp\spa\{\Lambda^{\ast}_{i}(H_{i})\}_{i\in I_{2}}^{\perp}
.

Proof.

Assume that
\{I_{i}\}_{i=1,2}
is a partition of
\mathbb{I}
, also
x\in\spa\{\Lambda^{\ast}_{i}(H_{i})\}_{i\in I_{1}}^{\perp}
and
y\in\spa\{\Lambda^{\ast}_{i}(H_{i})\}_{i\in I_{2}}^{\perp}
. For each
i\in I
, it is easy to check that

\|\Lambda_{i}(x+y)\|^{2}=\|\Lambda_{i}x\|^{2}+\|\Lambda_{i}y\|^{2}=\|\Lambda_{i}(x-y)\|^{2}.

So,
\|\Lambda_{i}(x+y)\|=\|\Lambda_{i}(x-y)\|
, and by assumption,
\|x+y\|=\|x-y\|
. This implies that
\langle x,y\rangle=0
.
∎

In general, if
\Lambda
is a g-tight frame on
H
and a g-norm retrieval, then Theorem
2.3
is holds. Indeed, assume that
A
is a bound of
\Lambda
, also

and

\displaystyle\langle x,y\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\frac{1}{A^{2}}\langle\Lambda^{\ast}_{i}\Lambda_{i}x,y\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{A^{2}}\Big\{\sum_{i\in I_{1}}\langle\Lambda^{\ast}_{i}\Lambda_{i}x,y\rangle+\sum_{i\in I_{2}}\langle\Lambda^{\ast}_{i}\Lambda_{i}x,y\rangle\Big\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{A^{2}}\Big\{\sum_{i\in I_{1}}\langle x,\Lambda^{\ast}_{i}\Lambda_{i}y\rangle+\sum_{i\in I_{2}}\langle\Lambda^{\ast}_{i}\Lambda_{i}x,y\rangle\Big\}

\displaystyle=0.

Also, when
\Lambda
is a g-frame and
\Lambda_{i}S_{\Lambda}^{-1}=S_{\Lambda}^{-1}\Lambda_{i}
for each

is holds for
\Lambda
on

. Indeed, if
x\in\spa\{\Lambda^{\ast}_{i}(H_{i})\}_{i\in I_{1}}^{\perp}
and

again, then by (

\displaystyle\langle x,y\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\langle\Lambda^{\ast}_{i}\Lambda_{i}S^{-1}x,y\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I_{1}}\langle x,\Lambda^{\ast}_{i}\Lambda_{i}S^{-1}y\rangle+\sum_{i\in I_{2}}\langle\Lambda^{\ast}_{i}\Lambda_{i}S^{-1}x,y\rangle

\displaystyle=0.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
H=\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

\Lambda
is a g-norm retrieval on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For each non orthogonal vectors
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
there exist
i\in\mathbb{I}
and

such that
\langle\Lambda^{\ast}_{i}(h_{i}),x\rangle\langle\Lambda^{\ast}_{i}(h_{i}),y\rangle\neq 0
.

Proof.

Suppose that there exist non orthogonal vectors
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\langle\Lambda^{\ast}_{i}(h_{i}),x\rangle\langle\Lambda^{\ast}_{i}(h_{i}),y\rangle=0

for each
h_{i}\in H_{i}
and

. Define,

and

, therefore by Theorem
2.3
, we get

and this is a contraction.
∎

The inverse of Theorem

can be hold with addition two condition on the operators

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\Lambda=\{\Lambda_{i}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C})\}_{i\in I}
and
I_{1},I_{2}
be arbitrary partition of
\mathbb{I}
.
If
\spa\{\Lambda_{i}^{*}(\mathbb{C})\}^{\perp}_{i\in I_{1}}\perp\spa\{\Lambda_{i}^{*}(\mathbb{C})\}^{\perp}_{i\in I_{2}}
and all of the operators
\Lambda_{i}^{*}
are surjective, then
\Lambda
is a g-norm retrieval on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
i\in I
so that
\|\Lambda_{i}x\|=\|\Lambda_{i}y\|
. Via Riesz representation theorem, we have a unique
z_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
where
\Lambda_{i}x=\langle x,z_{i}\rangle
and
\Lambda_{i}y=\langle y,z_{i}\rangle
. Therefore,
|\langle x,z_{i}\rangle|=|\langle y,z_{i}\rangle|
or
\langle x,z_{i}\rangle=\pm\langle y,z_{i}\rangle
. Now, we define
I_{1}=\{i\in\mathbb{I},\quad\langle x,z_{i}\rangle=-\langle y,z_{i}\rangle\}
and
I_{2}=\mathbb{I}/I_{1}
. So,
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
are a partition for
\mathbb{I}
and also if

then
\langle x+y,z_{i}\rangle=0
and for
i\in I_{2}
we have
\langle x-y,z_{i}\rangle=0
. By hypothesis, there exist
w_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
such that

. Thus
x+y\in\spa\{\Lambda_{i}^{*}w_{i}\}^{\perp}_{i\in I_{1}}
and

. This implies that
\langle x+y,x-y\rangle=0
and we conclude that
\|x\|=\|y\|
.
∎

, we have

(6)

\displaystyle R_{h,\varepsilon}(x,y):=\big|\langle x,y\rangle-\varepsilon\|x\|\|y\|\big|^{2}+\frac{1}{\|h\|^{4}}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}|\langle\Lambda^{\ast}_{i}(h),x\rangle|^{2}|\langle\Lambda^{\ast}_{i}(h),y\rangle|^{2}\geq C_{0}\|x\|^{2}\|y\|^{2}.

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
is clear by Theorem
2.4
.

(2)\Rightarrow(3)
. Let
\varepsilon\neq 0
and
0\neq h\in\mathbb{C}
. If
x=0
or
y=0
, then (
6
) holds true. Define for each
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

F_{h,\varepsilon}(x,y)=\big|\langle x,y\rangle-\varepsilon\big|^{2}+\frac{1}{\|h\|^{4}}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}|\langle\Lambda^{\ast}_{i}(h),x\rangle|^{2}|\langle\Lambda^{\ast}_{i}(h),y\rangle|^{2}.

So,
F_{h,\varepsilon}
is a continuous function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let

C_{0}:=\min_{(x,y)\in\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}}F_{h,\varepsilon}(x,y),

where
\mathcal{S}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\quad\|x\|=1\}
. By (2) we get
C_{0}>0
and for all non-zero vectors
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

R_{h,\varepsilon}(x,y)=\|x\|^{2}\|y\|^{2}F_{h,\varepsilon}\Big(\frac{x}{\|x\|},\frac{y}{\|y\|}\Big)\geq C_{0}\|x\|^{2}\|y\|^{2}.

(3)\Rightarrow(1)
. Suppose that
\Lambda
is not a g-norm retrievable for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then, by Theorem
2.5
, there exist a partition
I_{1},I_{2}
for
\mathbb{I}
and non-zero vectors
x\in\spa\{\Lambda^{\ast}_{i}(H_{i})\}_{i\in I_{1}}^{\perp}
and
y\in\spa\{\Lambda^{\ast}_{i}(H_{i})\}_{i\in I_{2}}^{\perp}
such that
\langle x,y\rangle\neq 0
. Now, for
\varepsilon:=\frac{\langle x,y\rangle}{\|x\|\|y\|}
and
h=(1,0)
we get

R_{h,\varepsilon}(x,y)=0
. Thus
C_{0}=0
and this is a contradiction.
∎

In the next result, the stability of g-norm retrievable for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
will be discussed.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
\Lambda=\{\Lambda_{i}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C})\}_{i\in\mathbb{I}}
be a g-norm retrievable and g-Bessel sequence for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
where
|\mathbb{I}|<\infty
, also all of the operators
\Lambda_{i}^{*}
are surjective. Then there exists a
\lambda>0
such that any set
\Theta:=\{\Theta_{i}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C})\}_{i\in\mathbb{I}}
satisfying

\max_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\|\Lambda_{i}-\Theta_{i}\|<\lambda

is also a g-norm retrievable and g-Bessel sequence for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon\neq 0
and
0\neq h\in\mathbb{C}
. Via Theorem
2.6
, there exists
C_{0}>0
so that,

\displaystyle R_{h,\varepsilon}(x,y):=\big|\langle x,y\rangle-\varepsilon\|x\|\|y\|\big|^{2}+\frac{1}{\|h\|^{4}}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}|\langle\Lambda^{\ast}_{i}(h),x\rangle|^{2}|\langle\Lambda^{\ast}_{i}(h),y\rangle|^{2}\geq C_{0}\|x\|^{2}\|y\|^{2},

for each
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Assume that
B
is the bound of g-Bessel for
\Lambda
and first, we put

0<\lambda<|\mathbb{I}|
. So, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we can write

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\|\Lambda_{i}x-\Theta_{i}x\|^{2}<|\mathbb{I}|^{2}\|x\|^{2}.

Thus, by Lemma
1.1
, the set
\Theta
is a g-Bessel sequence for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the bound
B^{\prime}:=|\mathbb{I}|+\sqrt{B}
. Now, put

(7)

\displaystyle 0<\lambda<\min\Big\{|\mathbb{I}|,\frac{C_{0}}{(B+B^{\prime})(\sqrt{B}+\sqrt{B^{\prime}})}\Big\},

and

\displaystyle R^{\prime}_{h,\varepsilon}(x,y):=\big|\langle x,y\rangle-\varepsilon\|x\|\|y\|\big|^{2}+\frac{1}{\|h\|^{4}}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}|\langle\Theta^{\ast}_{i}(h),x\rangle|^{2}|\langle\Theta^{\ast}_{i}(h),y\rangle|^{2}.

Notice that,
\|\Lambda^{*}_{i}h\|\leq\sqrt{B}\|h\|
and
\|\Theta^{*}_{i}h\|\leq\sqrt{B}\|h\|
. Now, we have

\displaystyle R_{h,\varepsilon}(x,y)-R^{\prime}_{h,\varepsilon}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\|h\|^{4}}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\Big(|\langle\Lambda^{\ast}_{i}(h),x\rangle|^{2}|\langle\Lambda^{\ast}_{i}(h),y\rangle|^{2}-|\langle\Theta^{\ast}_{i}(h),x\rangle|^{2}|\langle\Theta^{\ast}_{i}(h),y\rangle|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\|h\|^{4}}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\Big||\langle\Lambda^{\ast}_{i}(h),x\rangle||\langle\Lambda^{\ast}_{i}(h),y\rangle|-|\langle\Theta^{\ast}_{i}(h),x\rangle||\langle\Theta^{\ast}_{i}(h),y\rangle|\Big|

\displaystyle\qquad\times\Big||\langle\Lambda^{\ast}_{i}(h),x\rangle||\langle\Lambda^{\ast}_{i}(h),y\rangle|+|\langle\Theta^{\ast}_{i}(h),x\rangle||\langle\Theta^{\ast}_{i}(h),y\rangle|\Big|.

Since,

\displaystyle\Big||\langle\Lambda^{\ast}_{i}(h),x\rangle||\langle\Lambda^{\ast}_{i}(h),y\rangle|

\displaystyle-|\langle\Theta^{\ast}_{i}(h),x\rangle||\langle\Theta^{\ast}_{i}(h),y\rangle|\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\Big|\langle\Lambda^{\ast}_{i}(h),x\rangle\langle\Lambda^{\ast}_{i}(h),y\rangle-\langle\Theta^{\ast}_{i}(h),x\rangle\langle\Theta^{\ast}_{i}(h),y\rangle\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\langle\Lambda^{\ast}_{i}(h),x\rangle\langle\Lambda^{\ast}_{i}(h)-\Theta_{i}^{*}(h),y\rangle-\langle\Lambda^{\ast}_{i}(h)-\Theta_{i}^{*}(h),x\rangle\langle\Theta^{\ast}_{i}(h),y\rangle\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\|x\|\|y\|\|\Lambda^{\ast}_{i}(h)-\Theta_{i}^{*}(h)\|\Big(\|\Lambda^{\ast}_{i}(h)\|+\|\Theta_{i}^{*}(h)\|\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda(\sqrt{B}+\sqrt{B^{\prime}})\|x\|\|y\|\|h\|^{4},

therefore, we get

\displaystyle R_{h,\varepsilon}

\displaystyle(x,y)-R^{\prime}_{h,\varepsilon}(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda(\sqrt{B}+\sqrt{B^{\prime}})\|x\|\|y\|\frac{1}{\|h\|^{2}}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\Big||\langle\Lambda^{\ast}_{i}(h),x\rangle||\langle\Lambda^{\ast}_{i}(h),y\rangle|+|\langle\Theta^{\ast}_{i}(h),x\rangle||\langle\Theta^{\ast}_{i}(h),y\rangle|\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\lambda(\sqrt{B}+\sqrt{B^{\prime}})\|x\|\|y\|\frac{1}{\|h\|^{2}}\Bigg\{\Big(\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}|\langle\Lambda^{\ast}_{i}(h),x\rangle|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}|\langle\Lambda^{\ast}_{i}(h),y\rangle|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\Big(\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}|\langle\Theta^{\ast}_{i}(h),x\rangle|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}|\langle\Theta^{\ast}_{i}(h),y\rangle|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Bigg\}

\displaystyle=\lambda(\sqrt{B}+\sqrt{B^{\prime}})\|x\|\|y\|\frac{1}{\|h\|^{2}}\Bigg\{\Big(\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}|\langle h,\Lambda_{i}x\rangle|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}|\langle h,\Lambda_{i}y\rangle|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\Big(\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}|\langle h,\Theta_{i}x\rangle|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}|\langle h,\Theta_{i}y\rangle|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda(\sqrt{B}+\sqrt{B^{\prime}})\|x\|\|y\|\Bigg\{\Big(\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\|\Lambda_{i}x\|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\|\Lambda_{i}y\|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}+\Big(\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\|\Theta_{i}x\|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\|\Theta_{i}y\|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda(\sqrt{B}+\sqrt{B^{\prime}})(B+B^{\prime})\|x\|^{2}\|y\|^{2}.

Thus, we conclude that

\displaystyle R^{\prime}_{h,\varepsilon}(x,y)

\displaystyle\geq R_{h,\varepsilon}(x,y)-\lambda(\sqrt{B}+\sqrt{B^{\prime}})(B+B^{\prime})\|x\|^{2}\|y\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\Big(C_{0}-\lambda(\sqrt{B}+\sqrt{B^{\prime}})(B+B^{\prime})\Big)\|x\|^{2}\|y\|^{2},

and by
(3)\Rightarrow(1)
Theorem
2.6
, the proof is completed.
∎
