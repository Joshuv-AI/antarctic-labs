1 High-Level Project Progress, including Big Wins
LLNL led two major Technical Areas (TAs) within the PEDOMETER program: TA3 focused on
biocontainment and TA4 focused on developing electrochemical TNT degradation sensors and
testbeds. Key technical accomplishments are highlighted below:

TA3: Engineer functional plant dependent kill switch in the P. putida GMO host

Key outcomes include:
1. Following screening of exudate/environmental biosensor and toxin-inactivator parts for
 functionality in P. putida KT2440, we identified functional parts for kill switch development.
 These include: 1) the 4-hydroxybenzoate (4-HBA)-responsive transcription factor PobR as the
 sensor, which gave the highest signal and dynamic range (~190-fluorescence fold-change,
 10-100 µM detection range); 2) EcoRI restriction endonuclease as a toxin alone; and 3) Colicin
 E2 with a corresponding mutant Immunity protein as a toxin-inactivator system.

 1

2. We created & tested two circuit designs for cell killing upon plant removal (via 4-HBA signal
 removal) based on EcoRI-SsrA system and the Colicin E2-Immunity system. Functionality of
 the SsrA system in P. putida was poor. Therefore, we focused on screening variants for the
 Colicin E2-Immunity circuit. We observed significant mutational escape in functional circuits
 in P. putida, preventing identification of a stable kill switch design.
3. As a result of our results in P. putida, we opted to switch the host chassis to P. fluorescens
 SBW25, which we previously found had limited modes of mutational escape compared to P.
 putida.1 We established a functional 4-HBA exudate biosensor based on PobR in P.
 fluorescens. Future work can focus on developing a kill switch based on this sensor in P.
 fluorescens.

A key takeaway from our work is the importance of chassis host strain choice for kill switch design
and actuator choice. Genetic instability of the kill switch circuit is a major barrier toward
establishing a kill switch, which is host dependent. Addition of a host down-selection step to
regulator and actuator screening stages would be beneficial to hasten kill switch development.

TA4: Develop Testbeds

Key outcomes include:
1. We established electrochemical parameters and electrode modifications for sensitive
 detection of TNT (10s of ppb). We showed that molecularly imprinted matrices made of
 poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene) (PEDOT) enhanced sensitivity to TNT, and that utilizing
 differential pulse voltammetry enabled concentration-dependent detection.
2. There are ideal carbon inks that act as effective chemical sensors (on par and improving upon
 commercial solutions) that enable bespoke electrode array designs that can be integrated into
 testbeds. We tested a variety of carbon electrode materials using both commercial and custom
 microfabricated platforms. Leveraging the findings from the first key outcome, we also
 established ideal PEDOT thicknesses and parameters to enhance detection of TNT on the
 most ideal carbon inks, and found the best-performing ink to be screen printed onto an
 electrode array. This electrode material can also detect 3-indole-acetic acid and serotonin,
 two high-priority chemicals present in plant exudate with little understanding of how they
 modulate both spatially and temporally. There is immense promise in being able to integrate
 this technology into plant testbeds.
3. We fabricated the first electrode array to be integrated into the EcoFAB testbed, enabling new
 modalities of analysis with the EcoFAB. This platform is comprised of 32 electrodes spread
 over the root chamber. There is the possibility of continuing this collaboration with LBNL to
 utilize this platform for other applications.

Ultimately, we found that electrochemical sensors are capable of quantifying trace levels of TNT
in solution, given the optimal parameterization of electrode material, transduction layers deposited
on the electrode surface, and electrochemical input parameters. Importantly, we designed and
fabricated a multi-electrode array that can be used with the existing EcoFAB plant testbeds,
adding new modalities.

the deposition process (both oxidation and reduction currents) also led to plating of the counter
electrode, which was not ideal. To address this, we tested galvanostatic electropolymerization,
which is a constant current applied to the electrode surface. Thickness is altered either by the
magnitude of the current or the length of time the current is applied. We sought to standardize
thicknesses by controlling the total amount of charge applied to the surface, in Coulombs. Fig.
8A shows the different current and times, with the resulting charge applied to the electrode
surface. The parameter in darker purple shows MIPEDOT deposited at a higher current in a
shorter amount of time compared to the other parameters, where the current was held at 35 μA

compared to a bare GC electrode, it was observed that the first peak at ~-0.4 V was enhanced
with the MIPEDOT coating (Fig. 8B). In comparing all MIPEDOT thicknesses (Fig. 8C), we saw

was not observed. In addition, the MIPEDOT parameter plated at a higher current but shorter time
yielded poorer signal compared to deposition techniques in which a lower current was held for
longer periods of time. Thus, we moved forward with galvanostatic electrodeposition for
subsequent MIPEDOT plating and characterization, with the aim of modifying MIPEDOT
thicknesses by modulating plating time instead of current magnitude.
