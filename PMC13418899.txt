First-principles study of novel Cs3SCl anti-perovskite and performance assessment of solar cell structures with different hole transport layers and back contact metals
Authors: Md. Earshad Ali, Anis Attour, Md. Nobiul Islam, Karim Kriaa, Md. Azizur Rahman, S. AlFaify, Noureddine Elboughdiri
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Anti-perovskite materials have recently gained special importance for environmentally friendly, lead-free, and low-cost renewable energy technologies. In this study, the structural, electronic, dynamic, thermodynamic, mechanical, optical, and photovoltaic properties of Cs3SCl anti-perovskite are analyzed in detail by density functional theory (DFT) and an SCAPS-1D simulator. The results show that Cs3SCl is thermodynamically, dynamically, and mechanically stable, with a ductile nature due to its B/G ratio of 2.03. The electronic band structure analysis identified the compound as a direct bandgap semiconductor, with bandgaps of 1.185 eV and 2.051 eV obtained by GGA-PBE and HSE06 methods, respectively. This suitable bandgap is highly favorable for visible light absorption. Optical analysis shows that Cs3SCl exhibits high absorption coefficients of about (2.6–0.2) × 105 cm−1 in the ultraviolet, visible, and near-infrared regions. In addition, its favorable refraction, low reflectivity, and excellent dielectric properties further strengthen its potential for solar energy harvesting, charge-carrier generation, and optoelectronic applications. A fully lead-free Al/FTO/SnS2/Cs3SCl/HTL/Se solar cell was designed and optimized to evaluate the photovoltaic potential of the material. After systematic optimization of the hole transport layer (HTL), back-contact metal, device temperature, absorber layer thickness, defect density, and shallow acceptor density, the Cu2Te-based device exhibited the highest performance. At a 0.750 µm absorber layer thickness, 1 × 1015 cm−3 defect density, and 1 × 1017 cm−3 shallow acceptor density, the device achieves an open-circuit voltage of 0.752 V, a short-circuit current density of 39.23 mA cm−2, a fill factor of 84.43%, and a power conversion efficiency of 24.92%. Overall, these results indicate that Cs3SCl is a highly promising material for future generations of high-efficiency, environmentally friendly solar cells, visible-light-dependent photocatalytic technologies, and advanced optoelectronic devices.

Introduction

The current global energy crisis, the rapidly growing demand for semiconductor materials in modern optoelectronic technologies, and the increasing pressure of environmental risks have made the search for sustainable and renewable energy sources more urgent than ever before. The development of new materials with advanced properties for future generations of electronic and energy-intensive devices is currently considered one of the main goals of modern scientific research.1,2 In this context, the main objective of this research is to increase the efficiency of renewable energy production. In recent years, perovskite materials have revolutionized photocatalysis and photovoltaic technologies.3–5 The addition of these components has significantly improved the overall performance of the respective devices.6 Notably, the power conversion efficiency of perovskite solar cells increased from 3.8% to approximately 26.7% between 2009 and 2024, clearly demonstrating the extraordinary potential of this material class.6–9 In 2025, Md. E. Ali reported the first design and performance evaluation of an Al/FTO/SnS2/Na3SBr/CuO/Au anti-perovskite solar cell (APSC) employing Na3SBr as the absorber material. Using the SCAPS-1D simulation platform, the proposed device demonstrated an impressive power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 27.172%.1 Additionally, in 2019, a conversion efficiency of about 17% was achieved in hydrogen production from solar energy using a MAP(I0.85Br0.15)3/Si-based structure, further strengthening the multifaceted potential of perovskite-based energy technologies.10

Recent studies on functional and energy-based materials have shown that defect engineering, compositional tuning, interface optimization, and crystal-structure regulation play important roles in improving the overall performance of devices.11–13 Defect passivation techniques have been shown to increase the operational stability of semiconductor devices by reducing carrier trapping and recombination.11 Similarly, theoretical studies have shown that the electronic structure, optical response, and charge-transport properties of materials can be effectively controlled through doping and compositional modification.12,13 On the other hand, recent studies based on energy conversion and energy storage technologies have further highlighted the importance of efficient ion transport, interfacial stability, and charge-transfer processes.14–16 In addition, crystal-structure repair, surface-energy homogenization, and crystal-facet engineering have emerged as effective strategies to enhance the long-term stability and performance of materials.15,17 At the same time, numerical reliability optimization has been established as an important method for evaluating the reliability and predicting the performance of advanced electronic devices.18 Also, selenium doping significantly modifies the electronic structure of ZrO2 by reducing the bandgap and enhancing optical properties such as dielectric response and absorption, demonstrating its potential for improved optoelectronic applications.19 These advances collectively indicate that integrated analysis of their structural, electronic, optical, and transport properties is crucial for the design of next-generation energy-based materials.

Although perovskite-based solar cells and photocatalytic materials have shown significant progress, several fundamental problems remain in the industrialization of these technologies. In particular, the lack of long-term stability, rapid performance degradation upon exposure to light and air, and the toxic nature of some of the materials used have limited the commercial application of this technology. Due to these limitations, the search for alternative and more stable materials has now become an important area of research. In recent studies, materials with anti-perovskite structures have attracted much attention from researchers.1,20–23 Anti-perovskite is essentially a cubic crystal structure, where the conventional ABX3 perovskite structure is inverted to form X3BA. Here X acts as a one-dimensional positive ion (A+), B as a two-dimensional negative ion (B2−), and A as a one-dimensional negative ion (X−).7,24–27 Such materials are naturally occurring on Earth, are generally non-toxic, and exhibit bandgaps suitable for solar cells.28–31 Various studies have proven that anti-perovskite materials hold tremendous potential in many areas of modern technology. These include optoelectronic devices, solar energy conversion systems, switching memory devices, energy storage device, superconductors, hydrogen storage systems, and high-efficiency photocatalysis.32–36 Overall, existing research results indicate that anti-perovskite materials have enormous potential for use in future generation energy and optoelectronic technologies due to their environmental friendliness, structural stability, and high performance.1,2,20,21,28

However, extensive studies have already been conducted on the water-splitting efficiency of Na3SI-based Al/Na3SI/Se composites, which provide important insights into light-induced photocatalytic performance.2 Similarly, hydride-type anti-perovskite compounds such as K3SH, K3SeH, and K3TeH are capable of storing 3.24, 2.49, and 2.008 wt% hydrogen, respectively, and can release hydrogen in the temperature range of 672 K to 964 K, which identifies them as potential hydrogen storage materials.35 On the other hand, a thin-film all-solid-state battery (ASSB) device with a LiCoO2/Li3OCl/graphite structure fabricated using layer-by-layer technology exhibited a discharge capacity of 120 mA h g−1 in the voltage range of 2.2–4.2 V, indicating another important potential of anti-perovskite materials in energy storage technology.37,38 The photocatalytic efficiency of Co3ZnN anti-perovskite under visible light was found to be about six times higher than that of conventional materials, indicating its high light-sensitive response.39 Theoretical analysis also showed that the hydrogen evolution reaction (HER) performance of Ni3InN compounds can be significantly improved by controlling strain and applying appropriate doping.40 In addition, recent studies have shown that Ca6BiSbN2 and Sr6BiSbN2, among the X6BiSbN2 (X = Mg, Ca, Sr) type double anti-perovskite materials, are suitable as absorber layers in solar cells and also have potential for optoelectronic and thermoelectric applications.41 Also, a 2025 study showed that Ni3AC (A = Mg, Zn, Cd) anti-perovskite compounds behave as Ni3MgC-type superconductors, and their superconducting transition temperature was determined to be around 8.644 K.42 In 2024, p-type doping of Sr3BiN and Mg3BiN compounds achieved the highest ZT values of 1.49 and 1.18 at 900 K, respectively, making them highly promising for future thermoelectric technologies.43

This study proposes an environmentally friendly and lead-free Cs3SCl anti-perovskite compound as a potential absorber material for next-generation solar energy and optoelectronic technologies. Being composed of relatively safe elements such as cesium, sulfur, and chlorine, it can be considered a viable alternative to conventional toxic perovskite materials. Although there has been extensive research on various materials in the anti-perovskite class, to the best of our knowledge, integrated photovoltaic analysis based on density functional theory (DFT) and SCAPS-1D on Cs3SCl has not been previously reported. Therefore, it is scientifically crucial to evaluate its fundamental properties and applicability in solar cells. Generally, such anti-perovskite compounds have a cubic crystal structure and crystallize under the Pm3̄m space group, which is presented in Fig. 1(a).6,7 In this study, the structural, thermodynamic, dynamic, mechanical, electronic, and optical properties of Cs3SCl have been systematically analyzed using DFT methods. The results obtained indicate the high stability, suitable direct bandgap, and strong light-absorption ability of the compound, which are essential for efficient solar energy harvesting. At the same time, a completely lead-free Al/FTO/SnS2/Cs3SCl/HTL/Se solar cell structure has been designed and optimized using SCAPS-1D to verify its real photovoltaic potential. The optimal structure has been determined by analyzing various hole transport layers (HTLs), back-contact metal, device temperature, absorber layer thickness, defect density, and shallow acceptor density. The optimized Al/FTO/SnS2/Cs3SCl/Cu2Te/Se device achieved a power conversion efficiency of 24.92%, which clearly demonstrates the high photovoltaic potential of this material. Overall, the research results establish Cs3SCl as a highly promising material for environmentally friendly, sustainable, and high-efficiency solar energy and optoelectronic technologies.

Simulation methodology

First-principles DFT-based computational methods of Cs3SCl anti-perovskite

In this research, the structural stability, dynamic and thermodynamic behavior, mechanical elasticity, electrical response, and optical properties of the inorganic cubic 3D bulk anti-perovskite compound Cs3SCl have been analyzed in detail using first-principles Density Functional Theory (DFT) methods. All computational simulations were conducted using the widely used and reliable Quantum ESPRESSO software package, which is known for its high accuracy in structural, thermal, phonon-based stability, elastic properties, and optoelectronic analysis.1,44–46 This study uses scalar-relativistic Norm-Conserving (NC) pseudopotentials (Cs.pbe-sp-hgh.UPF, S.pbe-hgh.UPF, and Cl.pbe-hgh.UPF), which provide high numerical accuracy and results while maintaining computational efficiency.2,47 The Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof (PBE) functional, belonging to the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) class, has been applied to consider exchange-correlation effects,6,48 which provides more realistic results than the Local Density Approximation (LDA) for crystal structures.7,49 Extensive convergence tests have been performed on all important parameters to ensure the accuracy of the calculations. The analysis of the effect on total energy in the plane-wave-based calculations, as shown in Fig. 2(a), indicates that the energy converges correctly at ecutwfc = 50 Ry and ecutrho = 300 Ry. However, for more accurate results, ecutwfc = 60 Ry and ecutrho = 360 Ry were adopted in all final calculations.20,50 Similarly, to verify Brillouin zone sampling, Γ-centered Monkhorst–Pack k-point meshes from 1 × 1 × 1 to 9 × 9 × 9 were tested. Although the energy remains stable on a 4 × 4 × 4 mesh, the result is illustrated in Fig. 2(b). Furthermore, a 6 × 6 × 6 Γ-centered k-point grid is used in all calculations for greater reliability. A strong convergence threshold of 1 × 10−6 Ry has been set to maintain high accuracy in electronic self-consistent field (SCF) calculations.21,51,52 All these convergence tests confirm that the computational setup used makes the research results reliable and numerically stable. However, for more accurate prediction of the bandgap, the HSE06 hybrid functional has been applied. In this case, a 6 × 6 × 6 Γ-centered k-point grid and an equal density 6 × 6 × 6 q-point mesh have been used for Brillouin zone sampling, which ensures the accuracy of the hybrid functional calculations.28,53 In addition, a 10 × 10 × 10 Γ-centered k-point mesh has been adopted to more accurately analyze the optical properties.28,54–56 Additionally, the electrostatic potential of the proposed anti-perovskite compound has been analyzed. Since no surface or vacuum is naturally present in a three-dimensional bulk system, for this purpose, the cubic bulk structure of Cs3SCl is first transformed into a (1 1 2) supercell and then constructed into a slab shape along the (0 0 1) direction. A vacuum layer of about 25 Å thickness is added along the Z-axis of the slab. It is noteworthy that this slab model was used only for electrostatic potential calculations and was not applied to structural optimization or other numerical analyses.1,57–60 All pseudopotential files used in this study were collected from official sources of Quantum ESPRESSO, specifically from PSlibrary and the Hartwigsen–Goedecker–Hutter (HGH) pseudopotential dataset.

Computational methods for solar cell device design and performance analysis using Cs3SCl anti-perovskite

This study uses the widely used SCAPS-1D (version 3.3.09) solar cell simulation software, developed by Professor Marc Burgelman, to analyze the design and performance of Cs3SCl-based anti-perovskite solar cells.1,61–64 SCAPS-1D was selected as suitable for this work due to its high consistency with previously published research and strong device modeling capabilities.7,65–67 The SCAPS-1D software is capable of building solar cell structures consisting of up to seven different layers, where each layer can be defined as n-type, p-type, or intrinsic. The physical behavior of the device is primarily determined by three fundamental equations: Poisson's equation, the carrier transport equation, and the continuity equation. These equations collectively determine the charge carrier transport, generation and recombination processes, and the distribution of the internal electric field. In this study, a single-absorber anti-perovskite solar cell (APSC) structure has been designed, where Cs3SCl is used as the main absorber layer.21,65–68 The main objective of this proposed device design is to develop lead-free solar cells and analyze the device performance using various potential hole transport layers (HTL). The final proposed solar cell structure is Al/FTO/SnS2/Cs3SCl/HTL/Se. In this structure, Cs3SCl acts as the active light-absorbing layer. Aluminum (Al, 4.1 eV)1,2,69 is used as the front contact due to its relatively low work function, which facilitates efficient electron collection from the FTO/ETL side and minimizes contact resistance. On the other hand, selenium (Se, 5.9 eV)20,28,70 is employed as the back contact material after evaluating several candidate metals, where Se provided the most favorable energy alignment with the HTL, leading to improved hole extraction and overall device performance. During device simulation, CuO, Sb2S3, Zn3P2, and Cu2Te were used separately as HTLs, while SnS2 was kept fixed as the electron transport layer (ETL). All other layer parameters were kept unchanged except for the change in HTL so that the actual impact of HTL can be accurately analyzed. All simulations were conducted at a temperature of 300 K, a frequency of 1 × 106 Hz, and under standard AM 1.5 G solar radiation.21,65–68 The absorber layer thickness, defect density, shallow acceptor concentration, effective device temperature, and back-contact metal have been optimized in stages to achieve maximum efficiency. Two important interface layers, SnS2/Cs3SCl and Cs3SCl/HTL, are included in the model to ensure realistic device behavior. These interfaces reduce charge recombination, strengthen the internal electric field, and improve the overall chemical and electrical stability of the device.21,65–68

Result and discussion

Structural characteristics of Cs3SCl anti-perovskite

The optimized crystal structure of the Cs3SCl compound is shown in Fig. 1(a), which adopts a cubic anti-perovskite configuration with the high-symmetry Pm3̄m space group. The unit cell consists of five atoms, where Cs atoms occupy the face-centered positions [(0.5, 0, 0), (0, 0.5, 0), and (0, 0, 0.5)], the S atom is located at the cube corner (0, 0, 0), and the Cl atom resides at the body-centered position (0.5, 0.5, 0.5). This atomic arrangement confirms the typical anti-perovskite framework and ensures structural symmetry and stability. The corresponding Brillouin zone and high-symmetry k-path (Γ–X–M–Γ–R–X) used for electronic structure calculations are illustrated in Fig. 1(b), which is appropriate for cubic systems. The structural optimization was performed by minimizing the total energy as a function of lattice constant, as shown in Fig. 2(c). The equilibrium lattice constant is obtained at the minimum energy point, indicating the most stable configuration. The optimized lattice parameter (ao = 6.617 Å), unit cell volume (Vo = 1955.75 (bohr)3), and total energy (Eo = −171.80103 Ry) are listed in Table 1. The obtained lattice constant is consistent with related anti-perovskite compounds such as Cs3SBr, confirming the reliability of the present calculations. Additionally, convergence tests with respect to k-point sampling and cutoff energy were carefully performed to ensure numerical accuracy.

Phonon features of Cs3SCl anti-perovskite

Fig. 2(d) presents the phonon dispersion spectrum of the Cs3SCl anti-perovskite compound, which provides insight into the lattice vibrational properties and dynamical stability of the material. Phonon dispersion analysis is a well-established method for evaluating lattice stability, as it reflects the collective atomic vibrations within the crystal.7,21,71,72 In this study, the phonon properties of Cs3SCl were calculated based on the fully optimized lattice parameters and atomic positions along the high-symmetry path Γ–X–M–Γ–R–X. The obtained phonon spectrum exhibits well-defined acoustic and optical branches throughout the Brillouin zone.21,73–75 Notably, no imaginary (negative) phonon frequencies are observed, confirming the dynamical stability of the Cs3SCl structure. In addition, the acoustic branches smoothly approach zero frequency near the Γ-point, indicating proper long-wavelength vibrational behavior. These results confirm that the optimized structure is dynamically stable and suitable for further electronic and optical investigations.

Mechanical features of Cs3SCl anti-perovskite

This information is important not only for understanding the fundamental scientific behavior of the material but also for guiding the design of photocatalysis, photovoltaic, and optoelectronic devices. In the present study, we determined the elastic constant of Cs3SCl anti-perovskite to obtain a clear picture of its mechanical properties. Key mechanical properties such as Bulk modulus, Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio, and overall deformation resistance have been determined. The obtained values (22.392 GPa, 25.83 GPa, and 1.91 GPa, respectively) satisfy these conditions, confirming the mechanical stability of the Cs3SCl structure. The calculated bulk modulus (B = 8.61 GPa) indicates that the material is relatively compressible, while the shear modulus (G = 4.241 GPa) and Young's modulus (E = 10.789 GPa) suggest low resistance to shear and elastic deformation. The relatively low Debye temperature suggests weak interatomic bonding and soft lattice behavior. Overall, these results confirm that Cs3SCl is mechanically stable, relatively soft, elastically anisotropic, and exhibits ductile characteristics, making it suitable for further investigation in optoelectronic applications.

Formation energy of Cs3SCl anti-perovskite

Formation energy is a very important indicator in assessing the thermal stability of a solid, because it indicates how energetically favorable the compound is compared to its constituent elements. In general, if the formation energy of a system is negative, it exhibits a tendency to form spontaneously and is considered thermally stable. In this study, the formation energy of Cs3SCl anti-perovskite compounds was determined using the total energy obtained from first-principles-based Density Functional Theory (DFT) calculations. The calculation is carried out using eqn (15) and (16), which are consistent with the established and widely used methods in previous studies.20,77–80 The results obtained are presented in Table 3. According to the calculated results, the total formation energy of the Cs3SCl compound was found to be −14.04 eV, while the formation energy per atom was determined to be −2.81 eV per atom. In both cases, the negative value clearly indicates that the formation of this compound is energetically favorable and its synthesis is thermally feasible in a real environment. Additionally, this significantly negative formation energy value indicates the existence of strong chemical bonds between the Cs, S, and Cl atoms, which increases the overall stability of the crystal structure. Such a strong bond helps prevent structural degradation and enables the compound to maintain its stability even under high temperatures or external environmental influences. Overall, these results from the formation energy analysis demonstrate that the Cs3SCl anti-perovskite compound is thermally stable and suitable for long-term usability. Electronic features of Cs3SCl anti-perovskite

The electronic characteristics of a component are primarily controlled by the distribution and motion of its electrons and energy levels. These factors counted not only the electrical conductivity but also the material's response to external electric fields. In electronic characteristics analysis, three primary factors are usually considered: band structure, electric potential, and partial density of states (PDOS).1,2 In this current study, electrostatic potential characteristics analysis was of particular significance, as it enables the calculation of electron affinity, a critical parameter for evaluating the suitability of a material in photovoltaic or photocatalytic applications.6,7 Extensive analysis of the band structure, PDOS, and potential profile was performed to evaluate whether the proposed Cs3SCl anti-perovskite could work effectively in real-world electronic devices. As well, these analyses provide reliable information about carrier transport, energy level configuration, and the potential of the material for technological applications.20,21,28

Bandgap features of Cs3SCl anti-perovskite

Determining the electronic bandgap is important because it indicates whether a material is a semiconductor or a metal. The bandgap directly affects the optical absorption capacity of a material, charge carrier transport, and the performance of devices such as photodetectors or solar cells. Through the bandgap value of a material, we can predict how efficiently it is able to create and transport charges when light falls on it. In the present study, the band structure of Cs3SCl anti-perovskite is shown in Fig. 3(a), where the Fermi level is set to zero for the convenience of analysis.1,2,6,7 In Fig. 3(a), the electronic band structure of Cs3SCl anti-perovskite is presented in two different exchange–correlation methods, where the clear semiconducting nature of the material can be observed in both GGA-PBE and HSE06. Although the bandgap of 1.185 eV is found to be relatively small in the GGA-PBE calculations, the HSE06 method shows a significantly larger bandgap of 2.051 eV, which is usually due to the reduction of self-interaction defects resulting from the use of hybrid functionals. In both methods, the valence band maximum and conduction band minimum are located at the same high-symmetry Γ–Γ point, which directly confirms the presence of a bandgap. This feature facilitates rapid electron–hole pair formation after light absorption and enables efficient charge extraction without additional phonon assistance. Also, the relatively high dispersion of the conduction band facilitates electron transport, which can play an important role in reducing carrier recombination. Comparative analysis of GGA-PBE and HSE06 results suggests that the electronic structure of Cs3SCl provides a strong foundation suitable for solar energy harvesting. Specifically, the bandgap value found by HSE06 establishes it as a potential material for photovoltaic absorbers, photodetectors, and other optoelectronic devices.20,21,28

Optical properties of Cs3SCl anti-perovskite

The analysis of optical properties is essential to evaluate the suitability of a material for optoelectronic and photovoltaic applications.1,21,28 Optical properties describe how a material interacts with incident light through absorption, refraction, reflection, transmission, and energy dissipation processes, which are directly governed by its electronic structure.1,21,28 In solar cell and photocatalytic applications, these properties determine the efficiency of light absorption and the generation of charge carriers. Therefore, a detailed understanding of optical behavior is necessary to assess the material's light-harvesting capability. In this work, the optical properties of the Cs3SCl anti-perovskite compound have been systematically investigated. Key parameters such as the real and imaginary parts of the dielectric function, absorption coefficient, reflectivity, refractive index, extinction coefficient, penetration depth, optical depth, and energy loss function have been analyzed over a wide range of photon energy and wavelength.

This material can play an effective role in increasing light penetration and reducing reflection loss, especially in high-efficiency solar cells. Among the investigated HTLs, Cu2Te exhibits the most favorable overall energy-band alignment, facilitating efficient hole extraction while suppressing interfacial charge recombination. The relatively smooth valence-band transition between Cs3SCl and Cu2Te reduces carrier accumulation at the interface and promotes effective charge transport toward the back contact. Consequently, the Cu2Te-based configuration is expected to provide superior photovoltaic performance compared with the other HTLs investigated in this work.

Photovoltaic performance analysis of Cs3SCl anti-perovskite–based solar cell

Fig. 11(a) presents the J–V characteristics of Cs3SCl absorber-based solar cells using different hole transport layers (HTL). It can be seen that almost similar J–V profiles are maintained for all structures, indicating that the Cs3SCl absorber layer exhibits stable charge generation and transport capabilities. However, the change in HTL results in noticeable differences in short-circuit current density (JSC), fill factor (FF), and overall power conversion efficiency (PCE). According to the data in Table 9, the device employing Cu2Te as the HTL achieves the highest JSC (39.354 mA cm−2) and the highest PCE (22.72%). This superior performance can be attributed to several factors. First, the favorable valence-band alignment at the Cs3SCl/Cu2Te interface facilitates efficient hole extraction from the absorber layer. Second, the relatively small interfacial energy barrier suppresses carrier accumulation and minimizes recombination losses at the absorber/HTL junction. Third, the improved charge-transfer characteristics enhance carrier collection efficiency, resulting in a higher fill factor. Furthermore, the efficient transport of photogenerated holes toward the back contact contributes to maintaining a high current density. As a result of these combined effects, the Cu2Te-based device exhibits the best overall photovoltaic performance among all investigated HTLs. On the other hand, relatively low FF and PCE are observed in Sb2S3-based solar cells. This decrease is mainly related to the less favorable band offset and higher charge loss at the Cs3SCl/Sb2S3 junction. As a result, a portion of the generated carriers cannot be effectively collected. The structures using CuO and Sb3S3 exhibit intermediate performance, wherein in both cases FF and PCE maintain relatively high values. Especially in the Sb2S3-based structure, the overall efficiency is slightly reduced due to the slightly lower JSC, despite the FF being almost equivalent to Cu2Te. The external quantum efficiency (EQE) spectra shown in Fig. 11(b) show that all HTLs maintain high quantum efficiency over a wide range of the visible spectrum. This indicates that the Cs3SCl absorber layer is highly efficient in light absorption and charge generation. However, the slight change in EQE in the long wavelength region reflects the difference in HTL-dependent charge extraction efficiency. Overall, these results clearly indicate that HTL selection plays an important role in determining the performance of Cs3SCl-based solar cells. In particular, since the highest photovoltaic performance is achieved using Cu2Te HTL, it can be considered a very promising hole transport layer for the proposed Cs3SCl anti-perovskite solar cell structure.

According to the Shockley–Read–Hall (RSRH) recombination theory, increasing defect density accelerates the non-radiative recombination process, which directly reduces the charge carrier lifetime significantly.6,7 In the present study, the effects of changing defect densities at different layers of a solar cell on the overall device structure have been analyzed using eqn (34) and (35).1,6,7 Gaining a clear understanding of these defect-related effects is essential for high-performance solar cell design and achieving improved power conversion efficiency.34

Although the present study is entirely based on first-principles DFT calculations, the obtained results strongly indicate that Cs3SCl is a physically viable and potentially synthesizable anti-perovskite compound. The optimized crystal structure confirms a stable cubic phase with Pm3̄m symmetry, while the absence of imaginary frequencies in the phonon dispersion spectrum verifies its dynamical stability. In addition, the calculated elastic constants satisfy the Born stability criteria, confirming the mechanical robustness of the material. Beyond structural stability, Cs3SCl exhibits promising optoelectronic properties, including a moderate bandgap, appreciable optical absorption in the UV–visible region, and balanced charge carrier mobility, which are essential for photovoltaic and optoelectronic applications. These combined characteristics suggest that the material possesses the fundamental requirements for functional device performance. Importantly, previously reported studies on anti-perovskite systems provide strong evidence supporting the experimental feasibility of this class of materials. For example, alkali-metal-based anti-perovskites such as Na3OX (X = Cl, Br, I, BH4) have been successfully synthesized and characterized, demonstrating their practical viability.95 Similarly, Ca3MN-type anti-perovskites (M = As, P, Sb, Ge, Bi, Pb, Sn) have been experimentally realized with confirmed structural stability.96 Furthermore, the successful preparation of nitride-based anti-perovskites, such as Mg3AsN, highlights the flexibility and realizability of these systems.97 These experimental advancements strongly suggest that related compounds, including Cs3SCl, are likely to be synthesizable under appropriate conditions. However, it is worth noting that halide-containing materials may face stability challenges under ambient environmental conditions, which may require suitable encapsulation strategies for practical applications. Overall, this work provides a comprehensive theoretical foundation for Cs3SCl, confirming its stability and highlighting its strong potential for future experimental realization and applications in photovoltaic and optoelectronic technologies.

Conclusions

In this work, a comprehensive first-principles investigation of the lead-free anti-perovskite Cs3SCl compound was performed to assess its suitability for photovoltaic and optoelectronic applications. The DFT-based calculations confirmed that Cs3SCl is structurally, mechanically, and dynamically stable, highlighting its feasibility for practical device implementation. Electronic structure analysis revealed a direct bandgap semiconductor with bandgap values of 1.185 eV (GGA-PBE) and 2.051 eV (HSE06) at the Γ-point, indicating favorable characteristics for efficient solar energy harvesting and visible-light absorption. The optical study demonstrated strong absorption in visible-light wavelength region, with absorption coefficients reaching ∼2.5 × 104 cm−1, together with suitable reflection and dielectric responses. These characteristics suggest that Cs3SCl can serve as an efficient light-harvesting material for advanced photovoltaic and optoelectronic technologies. To evaluate its device-level applicability, a lead-free and environmentally friendly solar cell based on Cs3SCl was designed and optimized using SCAPS-1D simulations. Various hole transport layers, back-contact metals, absorber thicknesses, defect densities, and doping concentrations were systematically examined. Among all investigated configurations, the Al/FTO/SnS2/Cs3SCl/Cu2Te/Se device exhibited the best photovoltaic performance, delivering a VO of 0.752 V, JSC of 39.23 mA cm−2, FF of 84.43%, and a maximum PCE of 24.92%. Optimal performance was achieved with an absorber thickness of 0.750 µm, a defect density of 1 × 1015 cm−3, and a shallow acceptor density of 1 × 1017 cm−3. The device also showed an extended photoresponse from 300 to 1200 nm and an EQE exceeding 98.56% within the 350–900 nm range. It should be noted that the achieved PCE of 24.92% represents an optimized simulation result under ideal conditions. While practical implementation may yield lower efficiency due to material and interface limitations, the results demonstrate the strong potential of Cs3SCl-based anti-perovskite solar cells for high-performance photovoltaic applications. Overall, the excellent stability, favorable electronic structure, strong optical absorption, and outstanding simulated photovoltaic performance establish Cs3SCl as a highly promising anti-perovskite absorber material. Considering the abundance, low cost, and non-toxic nature of its constituent elements, Cs3SCl represents a sustainable candidate for future high-efficiency solar cells and environmentally friendly optoelectronic technologies.
