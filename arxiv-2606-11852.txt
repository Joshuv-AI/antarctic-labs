The relaxation complexity of the standard simplex is logarithmic
Simon Keil, Stefan Weltge

Abstract
For a set X X of integer points, the relaxation complexity rc ⁡ ( X ) \operatorname{rc}(X) is the smallest number of facets of any polyhedron P P such that P ∩ ℤ d = X P\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}=X . In this paper, we focus on the case where X X is the discrete standard simplex Δ d = { 𝟎 , 𝐞 1 , … , 𝐞 d } \Delta_{d}=\{\mathbf{0},\mathbf{e}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{e}_{d}\} . We show that rc ⁡ ( Δ d ) = O ⁡ ( log ⁡ d ) \operatorname{rc}(\Delta_{d})=O(\log d) by an explicit, elementary construction. This improves upon the previously best-known upper bound rc ⁡ ( Δ d ) = O ⁡ ( d / log ⁡ d ) \operatorname{rc}(\Delta_{d})=O(d/\sqrt{\log d}) due to Aprile, Averkov, Di Summa, and Hojny (2024) and matches an asymptotic lower bound by Averkov and Schymura (2022).



1
Introduction

Given a set
X\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
of integer points, we call a polyhedron
P\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
a
relaxation
of
X
if
P\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}=X
. For a set
X
of integer points, the
relaxation complexity

\operatorname{rc}(X)
is the smallest number of facets of any relaxation of
X
. The relaxation complexity can be seen as the minimal number of inequalities needed in an integer programming formulation without auxiliary variables whose feasible (integer) points are precisely the points of
X
. This concept was introduced by Kaibel and Weltge
[
9
,
15
]
, who studied relaxation complexities of integer points corresponding to well-known combinatorial optimization problems such as the traveling salesman problem.
For several of these problems, they established exponential lower bounds, showing that auxiliary variables are often necessary to obtain compact integer programming formulations.

More general notions of relaxation complexity have also been studied.
Given an additional set
Y\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, a natural generalization is to consider the smallest number
\operatorname{rc}(X,Y)
of facets of a polyhedron
P
such that
X\subseteq P
and
(Y\setminus X)\cap P=\emptyset
.
Clearly, we have
\operatorname{rc}(X)=\operatorname{rc}(X,\mathbb{Z}^{d})\geq\operatorname{rc}(X,Y)
.
For subsets
X\subseteq\{0,1\}^{d}
, Jeroslow
[
8
]
already studied
\operatorname{rc}(X,\{0,1\}^{d})
under the name
index
.
Averkov, Hojny, and Schymura
[
3
,
4
]
presented approaches to compute
\operatorname{rc}(X,Y)
for finite sets
X
and
Y
, and discussed in which situations
\operatorname{rc}(X)
can be approximated by
\operatorname{rc}(X,Y)
for particular finite sets
Y
.
Moreover, they pointed out that the notion of
\operatorname{rc}(X,Y)
generalizes concepts that are examined in other fields, such as machine learning (see, e.g.,
[
2
,
6
,
7
,
12
,
13
]
) and social choice (see, e.g.,
[
10
,
11
,
14
]
).

While the relaxation complexity provides a basic measure of how compactly a set
X
can be described by linear inequalities, its precise value is known only for very few choices of
X
, among them discrete hypercubes
[
9
]
, cross-polytopes and axis-aligned boxes
[
3
]
.
However, its value is unknown for the arguably simplest (full-dimensional) set of integer points, the discrete standard simplex
\Delta_{d}=\{\mathbf{0},\mathbf{e}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{e}_{d}\}
, where
\mathbf{0}
denotes the all-zero vector and
\mathbf{e}_{i}
denotes the vector that has a
1
in the
i
-th coordinate and
0
elsewhere.
Clearly, since
\Delta_{d}=\{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:x_{1},\dots,x_{d}\geq 0,\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}\leq 1\}
, we have
\operatorname{rc}(\Delta_{d})\leq d+1
.
In
[
9
]
, it was asked whether this bound is tight.

The work of Averkov and Schymura
[
5
]
implies that
\operatorname{rc}(\Delta_{d})=d+1
indeed holds for
d\leq 4
.
However, they were only able to prove a lower bound of
\operatorname{rc}(\Delta_{d})=\Omega(\log d)
for general
d
.
In fact, they showed that
\operatorname{rc}(X)=\Omega(\log d)
holds for any finite
d
-dimensional set of integer points
X
.

For
d\geq 5
, suppose that
P
is a relaxation of
\Delta_{d}
with fewer than
d+1
facets.
This implies that
P
is unbounded and hence must be defined by linear inequalities with irrational coefficients (otherwise
P
would contain a rational ray and hence infinitely many integer points).
Somewhat surprisingly, Aprile, Averkov, Di Summa, and Hojny
[
1
]
constructed such relaxations for
d\geq 5
, showing
\operatorname{rc}(\Delta_{d})\leq d
and hence providing a natural example of a set of integer points for which the relaxation complexity is strictly smaller than its rational counterpart.
Moreover, they established the asymptotic bound
\operatorname{rc}(\Delta_{d})=O(d/\sqrt{\log(d)})
, still leaving a gap to the lower bound by Averkov and Schymura.

In this paper, we close this gap by providing an explicit, elementary construction of a logarithmic-size relaxation of
\Delta_{d}
.
Hence, we obtain the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

We have
\operatorname{rc}(\Delta_{d})=\Theta(\log d)
.

Combining this with the lower bound by Averkov and Schymura, this shows that the minimum of the relaxation complexity over all finite
d
-dimensional sets of integer points is
\Theta(\log d)
.

2
Construction

It is easy to see that
\operatorname{rc}(\Delta_{d})
is monotonically non-decreasing (see, e.g.,
[
1
]
).
Hence, to show that
\operatorname{rc}(\Delta_{d})=O(\log d)
, it suffices to consider the case where
d=2^{k}-1
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
.
We claim that
\operatorname{rc}(\Delta_{d})\leq 6k
in this case.
To this end, define
S=\{0,1\}^{k}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
.
Since
|S|=2^{k}-1=d
, we may identify
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\mathbb{R}^{S}
and
\Delta_{d}=\{\mathbf{0}\}\cup\{\mathbf{e}_{v}:v\in S\}
.
For a vector
v\in\{0,1\}^{k}
and
i\in[k]
, define

b_{i}(v):=\sum_{j=1}^{i}2^{j-1}v_{j}

to be the integer represented by the first
i
bits of
v
.
We also set
b_{0}(v):=0
.
Let
\alpha>1
be a real number of algebraic degree at least
2^{k}
, i.e., a number such that
1,\alpha,\alpha^{2},\dots,\alpha^{2^{k}-1}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
.
For a vector
z\in\mathbb{R}^{S}
, we will consider the affine linear expressions

\displaystyle x_{i}(z)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{v\in S}v_{i}z_{v}

for
i=1,\dots,k
and

\displaystyle y_{i}(z)

\displaystyle:=1+\sum_{v\in S}(\alpha^{b_{i}(v)}-1)z_{v}

for
i=0,\dots,k
.
The claimed relaxation is given by the polyhedron
P\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{S}
consisting of all points
z\in\mathbb{R}^{S}
satisfying the following
6k
linear inequalities for
i=1,\dots,k
:

\displaystyle 0\leq\,

\displaystyle x_{i}(z)

\displaystyle\leq 1

(1)

\displaystyle y_{i-1}(z)\leq\,

\displaystyle y_{i}(z)

\displaystyle\leq\alpha^{2^{i-1}}y_{i-1}(z)

(2)

\displaystyle\alpha^{2^{i-1}}y_{i-1}(z)-M(1-x_{i}(z))\leq\,

\displaystyle y_{i}(z)

\displaystyle\leq y_{i-1}(z)+Mx_{i}(z)

(3)

Here,
M
is any constant with
M\geq\alpha^{2^{k}+2^{k-1}-1}
.
The key idea underlying our construction of
P
is the following observation, in which we use the notation
x(z):=(x_{1}(z),\dots,x_{k}(z))
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Every
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{S}
satisfies
z\in P
if and only if
x(z)\in\{0,1\}^{k}
and
y_{i}(z)=\alpha^{b_{i}(x(z))}
for
i=0,\dots,k
.

Proof.

Suppose that
z\in P\cap\mathbb{Z}^{S}
. Note that by the definition of
x_{i}(z)
, we have
x(z)\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}
.
Therefore, the constraints (
1
) imply
x(z)\in\{0,1\}^{k}
. Moreover, the constraints (
2
) and (
3
) imply that
y_{i}(z)=\alpha^{2^{i-1}x_{i}(z)}y_{i-1}(z)
for
i=1,\dots,k
.
Combining this with
y_{0}(z)=1=\alpha^{b_{0}(x(z))}
yields
y_{i}(z)=\alpha^{b_{i}(x(z))}
.

Conversely, suppose that
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{S}
satisfies
x(z)\in\{0,1\}^{k}
and
y_{i}(z)=\alpha^{b_{i}(x(z))}
for
i=0,\dots,k
. Then, the constraints (
1
) are clearly satisfied. To see that the constraints (
2
) and (
3
) are also satisfied, note that the assumption implies that

y_{i}(z)=\alpha^{2^{i-1}x_{i}(z)}y_{i-1}(z)\quad\text{ for }i=1,\ldots,k.

(4)

Therefore, the constraints (
2
) are also satisfied. To handle the constraints (
3
), we distinguish two cases. If
x_{i}(z)=0
, then we need to check
\alpha^{2^{i-1}}y_{i-1}(z)-M\leq y_{i}(z)\leq y_{i-1}(z)
.
This holds since we have

\alpha^{2^{i-1}}y_{i-1}(z)-M=\alpha^{2^{i-1}}\alpha^{b_{i-1}(x(z))}-M\leq\alpha^{2^{k-1}}\alpha^{2^{k}-1}-M\leq 0\leq y_{i}(z)

and

y_{i}(z)=y_{i-1}(z)

by (
4
).
If
x_{i}(z)=1
, then we need to check
\alpha^{2^{i-1}}y_{i-1}(z)\leq y_{i}(z)\leq y_{i-1}(z)+M
.
This holds since we have

\alpha^{2^{i-1}}y_{i-1}(z)=y_{i}(z)

by (
4
) and

y_{i}(z)=\alpha^{b_{i}(x(z))}\leq\alpha^{2^{k}-1}\leq M\leq y_{i-1}(z)+M.\qed

From the above result, we can derive the following two lemmas, which show that
P
is indeed a relaxation of
\Delta_{d}
, thereby proving our main claim and hence Theorem
1.1
.

Lemma 2.2
.

We have
\Delta_{d}\subseteq P\cap\mathbb{Z}^{S}
.

Proof.

Note that
x(\mathbf{0})=\mathbf{0}
and
x(\mathbf{e}_{v})=v
for all
v\in S
and that
y_{i}(\mathbf{0})=1=\alpha^{b_{i}(\mathbf{0})}=\alpha^{b_{i}(x(\mathbf{0}))}
and
y_{i}(\mathbf{e}_{v})=\alpha^{b_{i}(v)}=\alpha^{b_{i}(x(\mathbf{e}_{v}))}
for all
v\in S
and
i=0,\ldots,k
.
The claim follows from Proposition
2.1
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

We have
P\cap\mathbb{Z}^{S}\subseteq\Delta_{d}
.

Proof.

Let
z\in P\cap\mathbb{Z}^{S}
. By Proposition
2.1
, we have

y_{k}(z)=\alpha^{b_{k}(x(z))}.

(5)

On the other hand, by the definition of
y_{k}(z)
, we have

y_{k}(z)=1+\sum_{v\in S}(\alpha^{b_{k}(v)}-1)z_{v}=\sum_{v\in S}\alpha^{b_{k}(v)}z_{v}+\left(1-\sum_{v\in S}z_{v}\right).

(6)

By assumption, the set
B=\{\alpha^{m}:0\leq m\leq 2^{k}-1\}
is linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
. Moreover,
y_{k}(z)
is in the linear span of
B
over
\mathbb{Q}
. Thus, we can compare the coefficients of each element of
B
in either of the above representations of
y_{k}(z)
.

To this end, note that
b_{k}
is a bijection between
S
and
\{1,\dots,2^{k}-1\}
and
b_{k}(\mathbf{0})=0
. If
x(z)=\mathbf{0}
, the only non-zero coefficient in (
5
) is
1
for
\alpha^{b_{k}(\mathbf{0})}=1
, and therefore the representation in (
6
) implies
z_{v}=0
for all
v\in S
, i.e.,
z=\mathbf{0}\in\Delta_{d}
.
If
x(z)\neq\mathbf{0}
, then the only non-zero coefficient in (
5
) is
1
for
\alpha^{b_{k}(x(z))}
, and therefore the representation in (
6
) implies
z_{v}=0
for all
v\in S\setminus\{x(z)\}
and
z_{x(z)}=1
, i.e.,
z=\mathbf{e}_{x(z)}\in\Delta_{d}
.
∎

3
Remarks

Our results settle the asymptotic behavior of
\operatorname{rc}(\Delta_{d})
and the asymptotic minimum of the relaxation complexity over any finite
d
-dimensional set of integer points, answering two questions posed by Aprile et al.
[
1
]
. Moreover, our results answer their question of whether the minimum of the relaxation complexity is attained by the discrete standard simplex, at least in the asymptotic case and up to constant factors.

They also proposed to study the relaxation complexity with respect to arbitrary subfields of
\mathbb{R}
and remarked that their construction works for every field extension over
\mathbb{Q}
of degree at least
d+1
. Since, by the primitive element theorem, every field extension of degree at least
d+1
over
\mathbb{Q}
contains an element of algebraic degree at least
d+1
, the same holds for our construction.
