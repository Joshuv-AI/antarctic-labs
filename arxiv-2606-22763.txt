Well-Posedness of the Schrödinger - Intermediate Long Wave system
Diego F. Correa-Castañeda

Abstract




Abstract.

The system coupled by the Schrödinger equation and the Intermediate Long Wave (ILW) equation is a particular model describing the interaction of long and short waves.
We prove local and global well-posedness of the initial value problem associated with this system in Sobolev spaces of low regularity. Our approach relies on the use of energy estimates, Bourgain spaces, and use of Tao’s gauge transformation.

1
Introduction

We consider the following coupled system:

\begin{cases}iu_{t}+u_{xx}=\alpha uv+\gamma\,|u|^{2}u\\
v_{t}-\nu\,\mathscr{G}_{\delta}v_{xx}+\rho\,vv_{x}=\beta\left(|u|^{2}\right)_{x}\end{cases}\,\,\,\,\,\,(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

(1.1)

where
u(t,x)
is complex-valued,
v(t,x)
is real-valued, and
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\nu
and
\rho
are real constants. The parameter
\delta>0
characterizes the depth of the fluid, and
\mathscr{G}_{\delta}
is the Fourier multiplier defined by the symbol
-i\left(\coth{(\delta\xi)}-\frac{1}{\delta\xi}\right)
.

This system is a realization of the general framework describing interactions between long waves (L) and short waves (S) under weakly coupled nonlinearities:

\begin{cases}iS_{t}+ic_{S}S_{x}+S_{xx}=\alpha SL+\gamma|S|^{2}S\\
L_{t}+c_{L}L_{x}+\nu P(D_{x})L+\lambda(L^{2})_{x}=\beta(|S|^{2})_{x}.\end{cases}

(1.2)

Such systems arise in various physical contexts, ranging from capillary-gravity waves to plasma physics (see
[
3
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
23
]
). Specifically, (
1.1
) represents a coupling between a nonlinear Schrödinger-type equation and the Intermediate Long Wave (ILW) equation:

v_{t}-\,\mathscr{G}_{\delta}v_{xx}+\,vv_{x}=0.

(1.3)

Formally, when
\delta\rightarrow\infty
, the operator
\mathscr{G}_{\delta}
tends to the Hilbert transform
\mathscr{H}
, which is defined as the Fourier multiplier by
-i\sgn(\xi)
, obtaining the Benjamin-Ono equation (BO) (see
[
1
]
,
[
5
]
):

v_{t}-\,\mathscr{H}v_{xx}+\,vv_{x}=0.

(1.4)

The limit case
\delta\rightarrow 0
can be considered as follows: define the scale transformation

v_{\delta}(t,x):=\frac{3}{\delta}v\left(\frac{3}{\delta}t,x\right),

which transforms (
1.3
) into the following scaled ILW equation:

\partial_{t}v_{\delta}-\frac{3}{\delta}\mathscr{G}_{\delta}\partial_{x}^{2}v_{\delta}+v_{\delta}\partial_{x}v_{\delta}=0.

(1.5)

Then, under suitable assumptions, when
\delta\rightarrow 0
, equation (
1.5
) is known to converge to the Korteweg-de Vries equation (KdV) (see
[
1
]
,
[
6
]
):

v_{t}+v_{xxx}+vv_{x}=0.

(1.6)

We also remark that while equations (
1.3
), (
1.4
), and (
1.6
) are completely integrable systems, this does not appear to be the case for the system (
1.1
). Nonetheless, the system (
1.1
) possesses several conserved quantities. For smooth solutions, the following functionals remain invariant on time:

\mathcal{E}_{1}(t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}v(t,x)\,dx=\mathcal{E}_{1}(0),

(1.7)

\mathcal{E}_{2}(t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|u(t,x)\right|^{2}\,dx=\mathcal{E}_{2}(0),

(1.8)

\mathcal{E}_{3}(t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}v^{2}(t,x)\,dx-\frac{2\beta}{\alpha}\text{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(t,x)\overline{u_{x}(t,x)}\,dx\,=\mathcal{E}_{3}(0),

(1.9)

and

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}_{4}(t):=&\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u(t,x)|^{2}\,v(t,x)\,dx\,+\,\frac{\nu}{2\beta}\int_{\mathbb{R}}v(t,x)\,\mathcal{G}_{\delta}v_{x}(t,x)\,dx\,-\frac{\rho}{6\beta}\int_{\mathbb{R}}v^{3}(t,x)\,dx\,\\
&+\,\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u_{x}(t,x)|^{2}\,dx\,+\,\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u(t,x)|^{4}\,dx=\mathcal{E}_{4}(0).\end{split}

(1.10)

Beyond their physical significance, these conservation laws ensure that local solutions in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})
can be extended globally.

Our principal purpose in this work is to study the Cauchy problem associated to (
1.1
) in low regularity Sobolev spaces. In this regards, we know plenty of well-posedness results for the equations involved in this system individually but not for the system itself.

We aim to establish the local well-posedness of the Cauchy problem associated with (
1.1
) for initial data in
H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
for all
s\geq 0
, and provide the global theory for the case
s=1/2
. Our main result in this manuscript is the following:

Theorem 1
.

Let
s\geq 0
be given. In system (
1.1
), take
\alpha=1
,
\beta=v
,
\rho=\nu^{2}
, and assume that
|\nu|\not=1
. For all
(u_{0},v_{0})\in H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
and all
T>0
, there exist
b>\frac{1}{2}
,
0<\theta<1
and a solution

(u,v)\in X^{s+\frac{1}{2},b}_{\text{S}}(T)\times\left(C_{T}H^{S}_{x}\cap L^{4}_{T}W^{s,4}_{x}\cap X^{s-\theta,\theta}_{\text{BO}}(T)\right)

(1.11)

of the system (
1.1
), such that

u(0)=u_{0},\,\,\,v(0)=v_{0},\,\,\text{and}

(1.12)

w=\partial_{x}P_{hi}(e^{-\frac{\nu}{2}iF[u]})\in Y^{s,\frac{1}{2}}(T),

(1.13)

where
F[u]
is some primitive of
u
defined in (
3.1
).
This solution is unique in the class
v\in L^{\infty}(]0,T[;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))\cap L^{4}(]0,T[\times\mathbb{R})
and
w\in X^{0,\frac{1}{2}}_{\text{BO}}(T)
. Moreover, the flow-map data solution
u_{0}\mapsto u
is continuous from
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
into
C([0,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{R}))
.

Remark 1
.

Recently, Dai and Forlano in
[
7
]
considered the so called extended Schrödinger-Benjamin Ono system:

\begin{cases}iu_{t}+u_{xx}=\alpha uv+\gamma\,|u|^{2}u\\
v_{t}-\nu\,\mathscr{H}v_{xx}+\rho\,vv_{x}=\beta\left(|u|^{2}\right)_{x}\end{cases}\,\,\,\,\,\,(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

(1.14)

They have obtained similar results as the one depicted above. Our analysis here is independent of the results in
[
7
]
.

In
[
14
]
Linares, Mendez, and Pilod studied the Cauchy problem associated to the system (
1.14
) and established a local well-posedness result for smooth data. On the other hand, Chapouto, Li, Oh, and Pilod in
[
5
]
proved local and global well-posedness results for the ILW equation (
1.3
) in
H^{s}(M)
,
M=\mathbb{R}
or
M=\mathbb{T}
, for
s\geq 0
, using the approach introduced by Molinet and Pilod in
[
19
]
to obtain similar results for the BO equation. These two facts inspired us to treat the Cauchy problem for system (
1.1
). For a more complete account related to the ILW equation see
[
17
]
.

Before describing the method employed in this work, it is instructive to review several related models of interest that arise as particular cases of (
1.2
):

\begin{cases}iu_{t}+u_{xx}=\alpha uv+\gamma|u|^{2}u\\
v_{t}-\nu\mathscr{H}v_{xx}=\beta(|u|^{2})_{x}\end{cases}\,\,\,\,\,\,(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

(1.15)

and,

\begin{cases}iu_{t}+u_{xx}=\alpha uv+\gamma|u|^{2}u\\
v_{t}-\nu\mathscr{G}_{\delta}v_{xx}=\beta(|u|^{2})_{x}\end{cases}\,\,\,\,\,\,(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

(1.16)

The system (
1.15
) is known in the literature as the Schrödinger-Benjamin Ono system. Related to this system, we just mention that, using Bourgain spaces, Bekiranov, Ogawa and Ponce proved local well-posedness of the associated Cauchy problem with initial data in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{s-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})
for all
s\geq 0
, when
|\nu|\not=1
(see
[
2
]
). The case
|\nu|=1
was covered by Pecher with a result of well-posedness in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{s-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})
for all
s>0
(see
[
22
]
). We also comment that, in
[
9
]
, Domingues obtained sharp local well-posedness results in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{k}(\mathbb{R})
in the non-resonant case
|\nu|\not=1
under some restrictions on the parameters
(s,k)
which can be decoupled from
\left(s,s-\frac{1}{2}\right)
. We refer to
[
14
]
for a detailed review of the literature concerning the system (
1.15
).

Note that these studies can be replicated for the system (
1.16
). Indeed, following
[
5
]
, for each equation of (
1.16
) one can apply the Galilean transformation

\tilde{u}(t,x):=u\left(t,x+\nu\delta^{-1}t\right),\,\,\text{and,}\,\,\tilde{v}(t,x):=v\left(t,x+\nu\delta^{-1}t\right),\,\,\,\,\,(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

(1.17)

which yields the following system (omitting the tildes):

\begin{cases}i\left(u_{t}-\nu\delta^{-1}u_{x}\right)+u_{xx}=\alpha uv+\gamma|u|^{2}u\\
v_{t}-\nu\mathscr{H}v_{xx}=\nu\mathscr{Q}_{\delta}v_{x}+\beta(|u|^{2})_{x}\end{cases}\,\,\,\,\,\,(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

(1.18)

where
\mathscr{Q}_{\delta}:=\left(\tau_{\delta}-\mathscr{H}\right)\partial_{x}
and
\tau_{\delta}
is the Fourier multiplier by
-i\coth{\left(\delta\xi\right)}
.

By considering the Duhamel formulation for (
1.18
), establishing crucial estimates in Bourgain spaces for each integral equation, and utilizing a contraction mapping argument, we obtain the local well-posedness of the associated Cauchy problem. Specifically, local well-posedness is established for initial data in
H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
for all
s\geq-\frac{1}{2}
in the non-resonant case (
|\nu|\not=1
). In the resonant case (
|\nu|=1
), local well-posedness holds in
H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
for all
s>-\frac{1}{2}
. We remark that the natural Bourgain spaces associated with the first and second equations in (
1.18
) are
X^{s+\frac{1}{2},b}_{\text{S}}
and
X^{s,b}_{\text{BO}}
, respectively, with
b>\frac{1}{2}
(see Subsection
2.2
below for the definition of the functional spaces).

We now return to the system (
1.1
). Applying the Galilean transformation for this system, we get

\begin{cases}i\left(u_{t}-\nu\delta^{-1}u_{x}\right)+u_{xx}=\alpha uv+\gamma\,|u|^{2}u\\
v_{t}-\nu\,\mathscr{H}v_{xx}+\rho\,vv_{x}=\nu\mathscr{Q}_{\delta}v_{x}+\beta\left(|u|^{2}\right)_{x}\end{cases}\,\,\,\,\,\,(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

(1.19)

In contrast to system (
1.18
), from the work of Molinet, Saut and Tzvetkov
[
18
]
on the equation (
1.4
), it can be inferred that it is not plausible to directly apply a fixed-point argument to the system (
1.19
). To overcome this obstacle, we use a compactness argument.

First, we work with smooth initial data. In fact, following
[
14
]
, we use energy estimates to obtain smooth solutions for the system (
1.1
). Then, to consider initial data in low regularity Sobolev spaces we follow the approach used by Molinet and Pilod
[
19
]
since the main difficulty is introduced by the second equation in (
1.19
). We define a suitable gauge transformation, as the considered by Tao in
[
24
]
to treat the second equation in (
1.19
). Specifically, let
(u,v)
be a smooth solution of the system (
1.19
) with initial data
(u_{0},v_{0})\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{\infty}(\mathbb{R)}
. We also work with the parameters
\alpha=1
and
\beta=\nu
. Consider a function
F:=F(t,x)
such that
F_{x}=v
and

F_{t}-\nu\mathscr{H}F_{xx}+\frac{\rho}{2}F_{x}^{2}=\nu\mathscr{Q}_{\delta}F_{x}+\nu|u|^{2}.

(1.20)

See Section
3
for an explicit construction of
F
. Define

w:=-\frac{\nu}{2}iP_{\text{+hi}}\left(e^{-\frac{\nu}{2}iF}v\right).

(1.21)

Setting
\rho=\nu^{2}
it can be shown that
w
satisfies the following equation:

w_{t}-\nu\mathscr{H}w_{xx}=\mathscr{N}_{\delta}(u,v,w).

(1.22)

where

\mathscr{N}_{\delta}(u,v,w):=\nu^{2}\partial_{x}P_{\text{+hi}}\left(\partial_{x}^{-1}w\,P_{-}\partial_{x}v\right)+\nu^{2}\partial_{x}P_{\text{+hi}}\left(P_{\text{lo}}e^{-\frac{\nu}{2}iF}\,P_{-}\partial_{x}v\right)\\
-\frac{\nu^{2}}{2}i\partial_{x}P_{\text{+hi}}\left(e^{-\frac{\nu}{2}iF}\,\mathscr{Q}_{\delta}v\right)-\frac{\nu^{2}}{2}i\partial_{x}P_{\text{+hi}}\left(e^{-\frac{\nu}{2}iF}\,|u|^{2}\right).

(1.23)

The system (
1.19
), coupled with equation (
1.22
), forms a
3\times 3
system. While for the coupled system (
1.18
) it was sufficient to work within
X^{s+\frac{1}{2},b}_{\text{S}}
and
X^{s,b}_{\text{BO}}
, the inclusion of the gauge-transformed variable
w
in (
1.19
) needs a more delicate choice of functional spaces. Adopting the framework proposed in
[
19
]
and
[
5
]
, for the second equation of (
1.19
) we employ the intersection space
L^{\infty}_{T}H^{s}_{x}\cap L^{4}_{T}W^{s,4}_{x}\cap X^{s-\theta,\theta}_{\text{BO}}(T)
, with
0\leq\theta\leq 1
, and for equation (
1.22
), we work with
Y^{s,\frac{1}{2}}(T)
. We also remark that the
Y^{s,\frac{1}{2}}
- norm of the first three terms of
\mathscr{N}_{\delta}(u,v,w)
were bounded in
[
19
]
and
[
5
]
.

For the first equation of system (
1.19
), following the method employed for (
1.18
), we work with
X^{s+\frac{1}{2},b}_{\text{S}}(T)
, the time-localized version of
X^{s+\frac{1}{2},b}_{\text{S}}
. In fact, for the Duhamel formulation of this equation, we apply estimates in Bourgain spaces, obtaining

\|u\|_{X^{s+\frac{1}{2},b}_{\text{S}}(T)}\lesssim\|u(0)\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}+\|uv\|_{X^{s+\frac{1}{2},b-1}_{\text{S}}}+\||u|^{2}u\|_{X^{s+\frac{1}{2},b-1}_{\text{S}}(T)}.

(1.24)

While it is tempting to apply a bilinear estimate of the type

\left\|uv\right\|_{X^{s+\frac{1}{2},b}_{\text{S}}(T)}\lesssim\|u\|_{X^{s+\frac{1}{2},b}_{\text{S}}(T)}\|v\|_{X^{s,b}_{\text{BO}}(T)}

(1.25)

to the second term on the right-hand side of (
1.24
), a difficulty arises:
X^{s,b}_{\text{BO}}
is not the space where
v
lies. Recalling that
v
is instead placed in a subset of
X^{s-\theta,\theta}_{\text{BO}}
, the primary technical challenge of this work is therefore to obtain an estimate of type

\left\|uv\right\|_{X^{s+\frac{1}{2},a}_{\text{S}}}\lesssim\|u\|_{X^{s+\frac{1}{2},b}_{\text{S}}}\left\|v\right\|_{X^{s-\theta,\theta}_{\text{BO}}},

for any
0\leq\theta\leq 1
depending on
s\geq 0
,
b>\frac{1}{2}
and some
a\leq 0
. Specifically, in Section
2
, we prove the required inequality (
4.22
) for the non-resonant case
|\nu|\neq 1
. We also remark that this method does not work in the resonant case
|\nu|=1
.

Combining these estimates with a bootstrap argument and the conservation laws (
1.8
), (
1.9
), and (
1.10
), we obtain the desired result. A final important remark regards the smallness assumptions to implement the Molinet - Pilod argument. In Remark
2
we explain how to overcome this difficulty.

This paper is organized as follows: in Section 2, we introduce the notations, define the function spaces, and recall some classical linear estimates. In Section 3 we introduce the gauge transformation and we work its a priori estimates. Section 5 is devoted to prove the key nonlinear estimates, which are used in Section 6 to prove the main part of Theorem
1
. In the appendix, we make the energy estimates, which are used to prove local well-posedness for smooth initial data.

Notation, function spaces and preliminary estimates

2.1
Notation

For any positive numbers
a
and
b
, the notation
a\lesssim b
means that there exists a positive constant
c
such that
a\leq cb
. We also write
a\sim b
when
a\lesssim b
and
b\lesssim a
. Moreover, if
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
\alpha_{+}
and
\alpha_{-}
will denote a number slightly greater and lesser than
\alpha
, respectively.

For
u=u(t,x)\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
,
\mathscr{F}u=\hat{u}
will denote its space-time Fourier transform whereas
\mathscr{F}_{x}u=(u)^{\wedge_{x}}
and
\mathscr{F}_{t}u=(u)^{\wedge_{t}}
will denote its Fourier transform in space and time, respectively. For
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the Bessel and Riesz potentials of order
s
,
J_{x}^{s}
and
D_{x}^{s}
, by

\widehat{J_{x}^{s}u}^{x}(t,\xi)=\left(1+\xi^{2}\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}\widehat{u}^{x}(t,\xi)\quad\text{and}\quad\widehat{D_{x}^{s}u}^{x}(t,\xi)=|\xi|^{s}\widehat{u}^{x}(t,\xi).

(2.1)

Throughout the paper, we fix a cutoff function
\eta
such that
\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
,
0\leq\eta\leq 1
,
\text{supp}(\eta)\subset[-2,2]
, and
\eta|_{[-1,1]}=1
. We define

\phi(\xi):=\eta(\xi)-\eta(2\xi)\quad\text{and}\quad\phi_{2^{l}}(\xi):=\phi(2^{-l}\xi).

(2.2)

Summations over capitalized variables such as
N
are presumed to be dyadic with
N\geq 1
; i.e., these variables range over numbers of the form
2^{n},n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Then we have
\sum_{N}\phi_{N}(\xi)=1-\eta(2\xi)
for all
\xi\neq 0
and
\text{supp}(\phi_{N})\subset\{\frac{1}{2}N\leq|\xi|\leq 2N\}
.

Let us define the Littlewood-Paley multipliers by

\widehat{P_{N}u}^{x}(t,\xi)=\phi_{N}(\xi)\widehat{u}^{x}(t,\xi)\quad\text{and}\quad P_{\geq N}:=\sum_{K\geq N}P_{K}.

(2.3)

We also define the operators
P_{\text{hi}}
,
P_{\text{HI}}
,
P_{\text{lo}}
, and
P_{\text{LO}}
by

P_{\text{hi}}=\sum_{N\geq 2}P_{N},\quad P_{\text{HI}}=\sum_{N\geq 8}P_{N},\quad P_{\text{lo}}=I-P_{\text{hi}},\quad\text{and}\quad P_{\text{LO}}=I-P_{\text{HI}}\text{.}

(2.4)

Let
P_{+}
and
P_{-}
denote the projections on the positive and the negative Fourier frequencies, respectively. Then

\widehat{P_{\pm}u}^{x}(t,\xi):=\chi_{\mathbb{R}_{\pm}}(\xi)\widehat{u}^{x}(t,\xi),

(2.5)

and we also define
P_{\pm\text{hi}}=P_{\pm}P_{\text{hi}}
,
P_{\pm\text{HI}}=P_{\pm}P_{\text{HI}}
,
P_{\pm\text{lo}}=P_{\pm}P_{\text{lo}}
and
P_{\pm\text{LO}}=P_{\pm}P_{\text{LO}}.
Observe that
P_{\text{hi}}
,
P_{\text{HI}}
,
P_{\text{lo}}
, and
P_{\text{LO}}
are bounded operators on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
for
1\leq p\leq\infty
while
P_{\pm}
is only bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
for
1<p<\infty
. We also note that

\mathscr{H}=-iP_{+}+iP_{-}\text{.}

(2.6)

Finally, we denote by
U(\cdot)
and
V(\cdot)
the free groups associated with the linearized Schrödinger and Benjamin-Ono equations, respectively, which is to say,

\widehat{\text{U}(t)u}^{x}(t,\xi):=e^{-it\left(\xi^{2}-\nu\delta^{-1}\xi\right)}\widehat{u}^{x}(t,\xi),

(2.7)

and,

\widehat{\text{V}(t)u}^{x}(t,\xi):=e^{-it\left(\nu\xi|\xi|\right)}\widehat{u}^{x}(t,\xi).

(2.8)

2.2
Function spaces

For
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
is the usual Lebesgue space with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{L^{P}}
and for
s\in\mathbb{R},
the real-valued Sobolev spaces
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
and
W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R})
denote the spaces of all real-valued functions with the usual norms

\|f\|_{H^{s}}=\left\|J^{s}f\right\|_{L^{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad\|f\|_{W^{s,p}}=\|J_{x}^{s}f\|_{L^{p}}\text{.}

(2.9)

Let us see how the symbol
\mathscr{Q}_{\delta}
acts in Sobolev spaces.

Lemma 1
.

Let
s\geq 0
. Then, we have

\left\|\mathscr{Q}_{\delta}f\right\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\delta^{-1}\left(1+\delta^{-s}\right)\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

(2.10)

In particular, we have

\left\|\mathscr{Q}_{\delta}f\right\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}}

uniformly in
\delta\geq 1
.

Proof.

See the proof of Lemma 2.3 in
[
5
]
.

∎

For
1<p<\infty
, we define the space
\tilde{L}^{p}
as

||f||_{\tilde{L}^{p}}=||P_{lo}f||_{L^{p}}+\left(\sum_{N}||P_{N}f||_{L^{p}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\text{.}

(2.11)

Observe that when
p\geq 2
, the Littlewood-Paley theorem on the square function and Minkowski’s inequality imply that the injection
\tilde{L}^{p}\hookrightarrow L^{p}
is continuous. Moreover, if
u=u(t,x)
is a real-valued function defined for
x\in\mathbb{R}
and in the time interval
[0,T]
with
T>0,

B
is one of the spaces defined above, and
1\leq p\leq\infty,
we will define the mixed space-time spaces
L_{T}^{p}B_{x}
and
L_{t}^{p}B_{x}
by the norms

||u||_{L_{T}^{p}B_{x}}=\left(\int_{0}^{T}||u(\cdot,t)||_{B}^{p}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\quad\text{and}\quad||u||_{L_{t}^{p}B_{x}}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}||u(\cdot,t)||_{B}^{p}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\text{,}

(2.12)

respectively.

For
s,b\in\mathbb{R}
we introduce the Bourgain spaces
X^{s,b}
and
Z^{s,b}
related to the Benjamin-Ono equation as the completion of the Schwartz space
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
under the norms

\|u\|_{X^{s,b}_{\text{S}}}:=\left\|\left\langle\tau+\xi^{2}-\nu\delta^{-1}\xi\right\rangle^{b}\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\widehat{u}(\tau,\xi)\right\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi}},

\|u\|_{X^{s,b}_{\text{BO}}}:=\left\|\left\langle\tau-\nu\xi|\xi|\right\rangle^{b}\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\widehat{u}(\tau,\xi)\right\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi}},

\|u\|_{Z^{s,b}}:=\left\|\left\langle\tau-\nu\xi|\xi|\right\rangle^{b}\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\widehat{u}(\tau,\xi)\right\|_{L^{2}_{\xi}\,L^{1}_{\tau}},

\|u\|_{\tilde{Z}^{s,b}}:=\left(\sum_{N\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}\left\|P_{N}u\right\|_{Z^{s,b}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

and,

\|u\|_{Y^{s,b}}:=\|u\|_{X^{s,b}_{\text{BO}}}+\|u\|_{\tilde{Z}^{s,b-\frac{1}{2}}},

where
\langle x\rangle:=1+|x|
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

We will also use the localized (in time) version of these spaces. Let
T>0
be a positive time and
||\cdot||_{B}

\sigma:=\tau+\xi^{2}-\nu\delta^{-1}\xi
,
\sigma_{1}:=\tau_{1}-\nu\xi_{1}|\xi_{1}|,
and
\sigma_{2}=\tau_{2}+\xi^{2}_{2}-\nu\delta^{-1}\xi_{2}.
Using a duality argument, we have

\|uv\|_{X^{1/2,a}_{\text{S}}}=\sup_{\|h\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}=1}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\Omega(\tau_{1},\xi_{1},\tau,\xi)f(\tau_{2},\xi_{2})g(\tau_{1},\xi_{1})h(\tau,\xi)\,dV\right|,

(4.25)

where
dV:=d\tau_{1}\,d\xi_{1}\,d\tau\,d\xi
. We decompose
\mathbb{R}^{4}
as the union of the following sets:

\displaystyle A:=\left\{(\tau_{1},\xi_{1},\tau,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{4}:|\xi_{1}|\leq 1\right\},

\displaystyle B:=\left\{(\tau_{1},\xi_{1},\tau,\xi)\in A^{c}:|\sigma|\geq|\sigma_{1}|,|\sigma_{2}|\right\},

\displaystyle C:=\left\{(\tau_{1},\xi_{1},\tau,\xi)\in A^{c}:|\sigma_{1}|\geq|\sigma|,|\sigma_{2}|\right\},\,\text{and,}

\displaystyle D:=\left\{(\tau_{1},\xi_{1},\tau,\xi))\in A^{c}:|\sigma_{2}|\geq|\sigma|,|\sigma_{1}|\right\}.

From equality (
4.25
) we have

\|uv\|_{X^{1/2,a}_{\text{S}}}\leq I_{A}+I_{B}+I_{C}+I_{D},

(4.26)

where

I_{U}:=\sup_{\|h\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}=1}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\left(\chi_{U}\Omega\right)(\tau_{1},\xi_{1},\tau,\xi)f(\tau_{2},\xi_{2})g(\tau_{1},\xi_{1})h(\tau,\xi)\,dV\right|,

for all
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}
. We aim to prove that each term on the right-hand side of (
4.26
) is bounded by
C\|u\|_{X^{\frac{1}{2},a}}\|v\|_{X^{-\theta,\theta}}
for some constant
C>0
. We need the so-called resonance function:

R:=-\sigma+\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}.

(4.27)

Observe that

R=\left(1-\nu\sgn(\xi_{1})\right)\xi^{2}_{1}-\left(2\xi-\nu\delta^{-1}\right)\xi_{1}\\
=-\left(1+\nu\sgn(\xi_{1})\right)\xi^{2}_{1}-\left(2\xi_{2}-\nu\delta^{-1}\right)\xi_{1}.

(4.28)

We divide each set
U\in\left\{B,C,D\right\}
into the following regions:

\displaystyle U_{1}:=\left\{(\tau_{1},\xi_{1},\tau,\xi)\in U:|R|\leq\frac{|1-|\nu||}{2}\xi_{1}^{2}\right\},\,\text{and}

\displaystyle U_{2}:=\left\{(\tau_{1},\xi_{1},\tau,\xi)\in U:|R|\geq\frac{|1-|\nu||}{2}\xi_{1}^{2}\right\}.

Using
\langle\xi\rangle\lesssim\langle\xi_{1}\rangle\langle\xi_{2}\rangle
, we get

\Omega(\tau_{1},\xi_{1},\tau,\xi)\lesssim\frac{\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{\theta+\frac{1}{2}}}{\langle\sigma\rangle^{|a|}\langle\sigma_{1}\rangle^{\theta}\langle\sigma_{2}\rangle^{b}}.

(4.29)

In
U_{1}
a better estimate for
\Omega
can be obtained: let us see that
\langle\xi\rangle\lesssim\langle\xi_{2}\rangle
in
U_{1}
. In fact,

\displaystyle 2|\xi|

\displaystyle=\left|\left(1-\nu\sgn(\xi_{1})\right)\xi_{1}-\left(2\xi-\nu\delta^{-1}\right)-\left(1-\nu\sgn(\xi_{1})\right)\xi_{1}-\nu\delta^{-1}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\left(1-\nu\sgn(\xi_{1})\right)\xi_{1}-\left(2\xi-\nu\delta^{-1}\right)\right|+\left(1+|\nu|\right)|\xi_{1}|+|\nu|\delta^{-1}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\left|1-|\nu|\right|}{2}+1+|\nu|\right)|\xi_{1}|+|\nu|\delta^{-1},

which implies that

\langle\xi\rangle\lesssim_{|\nu|,\delta^{-1}}\langle\xi_{1}\rangle.

(4.30)

On the other hand,

\displaystyle 2|\xi_{2}|

\displaystyle=\left|-\left(1+\nu\sgn(\xi_{1})\right)\xi_{1}-\left(2\xi_{2}-\nu\delta^{-1}\right)+\left(1+\nu\sgn(\xi_{1})\right)\xi_{1}-\nu\delta^{-1}\right|

\displaystyle\geq\left|\left(1+\nu\sgn(\xi_{1})\right)\xi_{1}-\nu\delta^{-1}\right|-\left|-\left(1+\nu\sgn(\xi_{1})\right)\xi_{1}-\left(2\xi_{2}-\nu\delta^{-1}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\geq\left|1-|\nu|\right||\xi_{1}|-|\nu|\delta^{-1}-\frac{\left|1-|\nu|\right|}{2}|\xi_{1}|

\displaystyle=\frac{\left|1-|\nu|\right|}{2}|\xi_{1}|-|\nu|\delta^{-1},

which implies that

\langle\xi_{1}\rangle\lesssim_{|\nu|,\delta^{-1}}\langle\xi_{2}\rangle.

(4.31)

Putting together the inequalities (
4.30
) and (
4.31
), we get
\langle\xi\rangle\lesssim\langle\xi_{2}\rangle
in
U_{1}
, as we wanted. As a consequence,

\left(\chi_{U_{1}}\Omega\right)(\tau_{1},\xi_{1},\tau,\xi)\lesssim\frac{\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{\theta}}{\langle\sigma\rangle^{|a|}\langle\sigma_{1}\rangle^{\theta}\langle\sigma_{2}\rangle^{b}}.

(4.32)

Let us make another observation about
U_{1}
: in this region,

\displaystyle\left|1-|\nu|\right||\xi_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\left(1-\nu\sgn(\xi_{1})\right)\xi_{1}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|2\left(1-\nu\sgn(\xi_{1})\right)\xi_{1}-\left(1-\nu\sgn(\xi_{1})\right)\xi_{1}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|2\left(1-\nu\sgn(\xi_{1})\right)\xi_{1}-\left(2\xi-\nu\delta^{-1}\right)\right|+\left|\left(2\xi-\nu\delta^{-1}\right)-\left(1-\nu\sgn(\xi_{1})\right)\xi_{1}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|2\left(1-\nu\sgn(\xi_{1})\right)\xi_{1}-\left(2\xi-\nu\delta^{-1}\right)\right|+\frac{\left|1-|\nu|\right|}{2}|\xi_{1}|,

where in the last inequality we used the definition of
U_{1}
. Hence,

\left|1-|\nu|\right||\xi_{1}|\leq\left|2\left(1-\nu\sgn(\xi_{1})\right)\xi_{1}-\left(2\xi-\nu\delta^{-1}\right)\right|+\frac{\left|1-|\nu|\right|}{2}|\xi_{1}|,

which implies that

\frac{\left|1-|\nu|\right|}{2}|\xi_{1}|\leq\left|2\left(1-\nu\sgn(\xi_{1})\right)\xi_{1}-\left(2\xi-\nu\delta^{-1}\right)\right|\,\,\,\,\text{in}\,\,\,\,U_{1}.

(4.33)

Now we return to the problem of bounding each term on the right hand side of (
4.26
). Let us start with
I_{A}
and
I_{B}
. In fact, using Cauchy - Schwartz inequality and the Fubini Theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\left(\chi_{A\cup B}\Omega\right)(\tau,\tau_{1},\xi,\xi_{1})f(\tau_{2},\xi_{2})g(\tau_{1},\xi_{1})h(\tau,\xi)\,dV\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(\chi_{A\cup B}\Omega\right)(\tau,\tau_{1},\xi,\xi_{1})f(\tau_{2},\xi_{2})g(\tau_{1},\xi_{1})\,d\tau_{1}\,d\xi_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi}}\|h\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\chi_{A\cup B}\Omega\right|^{2}\,d\tau_{1}\,d\xi_{1}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|f(\tau_{2},\xi_{2})\right|^{2}\left|g(\tau_{1},\xi_{1})\right|^{2}\,d\tau_{1}\,d\xi_{1}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi}}\|h\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\chi_{A\cup B}\Omega\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{\tau,\xi}\,L^{2}_{\tau_{1},\xi_{1}}}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\|h\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)},

which implies that
I_{A\cup B}\leq\left\|\chi_{A\cup B}\Omega\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{\tau,\xi}\,L^{2}_{\tau_{1},\xi_{1}}}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}
. For that reason, let us prove that

\left\|\chi_{A\cup B}\Omega\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{\tau,\xi}\,L^{2}_{\tau_{1},\xi_{1}}}\lesssim 1.

(4.34)

In fact, by (
4.29
)

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\chi_{A}\Omega\right|^{2}\,d\tau_{1}\,d\xi_{1}\lesssim\frac{1}{\langle\sigma\rangle^{2|a|}}\int_{-1}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{2\theta+1}}{\langle\sigma_{1}\rangle^{2\theta}\langle\sigma_{2}\rangle^{2b}}\,d\tau_{1}\,d\xi_{1}\lesssim\frac{1}{\langle\sigma\rangle^{|a|}}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{2\theta+1}}{\left\langle R+\sigma\right\rangle^{2\theta}}\,d\xi_{1},

where the last inequality was obtained due to Lemma
9
and identity (
4.28
). Since the integration domain in the last integral has finite measure,

\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(\chi_{A}\Omega\right)(\tau_{1},\xi_{1},\tau,\xi)^{2}\,d\tau_{1}\,d\xi_{1}\lesssim 1.

Taking square roots and the supremum over
\tau
and
\xi
, we get

\left\|\chi_{A}\Omega\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{\tau,\xi}\,L^{2}_{\tau_{1},\xi_{1}}}\lesssim 1.

(4.35)

Now, we turn to the set
B
. First, consider
B_{1}
. Using inequality (
4.32
), Lemma
9
and the fact that
|R|=|-\sigma+\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}|\leq 3|\sigma|
in
B
, we get

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\left(\chi_{B_{1}}\Omega\right)(\tau_{1},\xi_{1},\tau,\xi)\right|^{2}\,d\tau_{1}d\xi_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\langle\sigma\rangle^{2|a|}}\iint\frac{\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{2\theta}}{\langle\sigma_{1}\rangle^{2\theta}\langle\sigma_{2}\rangle^{2b}}\,d\tau_{1}d\xi_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int\frac{\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{2\theta}}{\langle R\rangle^{2|a|}\langle R+\sigma\rangle^{2\theta}}\,d\xi_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{|R|\leq|R+\sigma|}\frac{\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{2\theta}}{\langle R\rangle^{2\left(|a|+\theta\right)}}\,d\xi_{1}+\int_{|R|\geq|R+\sigma|}\frac{\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{2\theta}}{\langle R+\sigma\rangle^{2\left(|a|+\theta\right)}}\,d\xi_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\mathop{\text{\large$\int$}}\nolimits\frac{\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{2\theta}}{\langle R+p(\sigma)\rangle^{2\left(|a|+\theta\right)}}\,d\xi_{1},

where
p(\sigma)\in\{0,\sigma\}
. Making the substitution
\mu=R+p(\sigma)
in the last integral, and using inequality (
4.33
), we get

\displaystyle\mathop{\text{\large$\int$}}\nolimits\frac{\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{2\theta}}{\langle R+p(\sigma)\rangle^{2\left(|a|+\theta\right)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathop{\text{\large$\int$}}\nolimits\frac{\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{2\theta}}{\langle\mu\rangle^{2\left(|a|+\theta\right)}}\frac{d\mu}{\left|2\left(1-\nu\sgn(\xi_{1})\right)\xi_{1}-\left(2\xi-\nu\delta^{-1}\right)\right|}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\mathop{\text{\large$\int$}}\nolimits\frac{1}{\langle\mu\rangle^{2\left(|a|+\theta\right)}}\frac{d\mu}{\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{1-2\theta}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{d\mu}{\langle\mu\rangle^{2\left(|a|+\theta\right)}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle 1,

where for the last inequality we observe that
2\left(|a|+\theta\right)=\left(2|a|+\theta\right)+\theta>1+\theta>1
.

Then

\left\|\chi_{B_{1}}\Omega\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{\tau,\xi}\,L^{2}_{\tau_{1},\xi_{1}}}\lesssim 1.

(4.36)

Now, consider the region
B_{2}
. Using inequality (
4.29
), Lemma
9
and the fact that in
B_{2}
,
\xi_{1}^{2}\lesssim|R|=|-\sigma+\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}|\leq 3|\sigma|
,

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\chi_{B_{2}}\Omega\right|^{2}\,d\tau_{1}d\xi_{1}\lesssim\frac{1}{\langle\sigma\rangle^{2|a|}}\int\frac{\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{2\theta+1}}{\langle R+\sigma\rangle^{2\theta}}d\xi_{1}\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{d\xi_{1}}{\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{4|a|-1-2\theta}\langle R+\sigma\rangle^{2\theta}}\lesssim 1,

where the last inequality is due to Lemma
12
, taking
\ell_{1}=2|a|-\frac{1}{2}-\theta
and
\ell_{2}=2\theta
. Then

\left\|\chi_{B_{2}}\Omega\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{\tau,\xi}\,L^{2}_{\tau_{1},\xi_{1}}}\lesssim 1.

(4.37)

Combining inequalities (
4.35
), (
4.36
) and (
4.37
), we obtain the estimate (
4.34
), and moreover

I_{A}+I_{B}\lesssim\|u\|_{X^{1/2,a}_{\text{S}}}\|v\|_{X^{-\theta,\,\theta}_{\text{BO}}}.

Now, we leave to the region
C
. Note that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\left(\chi_{C}\Omega\right)(\tau,\tau_{1},\xi,\xi_{1})f(\tau_{2},\xi_{2})g(\tau_{1},\xi_{1})h(\tau,\xi)\,dV\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(\chi_{C}\Omega\right)(\tau,\tau_{1},\xi,\xi_{1})f(\tau_{2},\xi_{2})h(\tau,\xi)\,d\tau\,d\xi\right\|_{L^{2}_{\tau_{1},\xi_{1}}}\|g\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\chi_{C}\Omega\right|^{2}\,d\tau\,d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|f(\tau_{2},\xi_{2})\right|^{2}\left|h(\tau,\xi)\right|^{2}\,d\tau\,d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{\tau_{1},\xi_{1}}}\|g\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\chi_{C}\Omega\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{\tau_{1},\xi_{1}}\,L^{2}_{\tau,\xi}}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\|h\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)},

obtaining
I_{C}\leq\left\|\chi_{D}\Omega\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{\tau_{1},\xi_{1}}\,L^{2}_{\tau,\xi}}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}
. Therefore, let us prove that

\left\|\chi_{C}\Omega\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{\tau_{1},\xi_{1}}\,L^{2}_{\tau,\xi}}\lesssim 1.

(4.38)

First, consider
C_{1}
. Using inequality (
4.29
) and Lemma
9
,

\displaystyle\iint\left|\chi_{C_{1}}\Omega\right|^{2}d\tau\,d\xi\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{2\theta}}{\langle\sigma_{1}\rangle^{2\theta}}\int_{|\sigma_{2}-\sigma|\leq 2|\sigma_{1}|}\frac{d\xi}{\langle R-\sigma_{1}\rangle^{2|a|}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{2\theta}}{\langle\sigma_{1}\rangle^{2\theta}}\int_{|\mu|\leq 2|\sigma_{1}|}\frac{1}{\langle\mu\rangle^{2|a|}}\frac{d\mu}{2|\xi_{1}|}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{1-2\theta}\langle\sigma_{1}\rangle^{2\theta}}\left[\frac{(1+\mu)^{-2|a|+1}}{-2|a|+1}\right]_{0}^{2|\sigma_{1}|}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{1-2\theta}\langle\sigma_{1}\rangle^{2|a|-1+2\theta}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle 1.

For
C_{2}
we make similar considerations, but taking into account that in this region
\xi_{1}^{2}\lesssim|R|=|-\sigma+\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}|\leq 3|\sigma_{1}|
. Indeed

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\chi_{C_{2}}\Omega\right|^{2}\,d\tau_{1}d\xi_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{2\theta+1}}{\langle\sigma_{1}\rangle^{2\theta}}\int\frac{d\xi}{\langle R-\sigma_{1}\rangle^{2|a|}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{2\theta}}{\langle\sigma_{1}\rangle^{2|a|-1+2\theta}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{4|a|-2+2\theta}}\lesssim 1,

It shows the estimate (
4.38
).

At last, let us show that
I_{D}=O\left(\|u\|_{X^{s+\frac{1}{2},a}}\|v\|_{X^{s-\theta,\theta}}\right)
. Indeed, making explicitly the change of variables
\xi_{2}=\xi-\xi_{1}
and
\tau_{2}=\tau-\tau_{1}
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\left(\chi_{D}\Omega\right)(\tau,\tau_{1},\xi,\xi_{1})f(\tau_{2},\xi_{2})g(\tau_{1},\xi_{1})h(\tau,\xi)\,dV

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\left(\chi_{\tilde{D}}\tilde{\Omega}\right)(\tau_{2},\tau_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{1})f(\tau_{2},\xi_{2})g(\tau_{1},\xi_{1})h(\tau,\xi)\,d\tilde{V},

where
d\tilde{V}:=d\tau_{1}\,d\xi_{1}\,d\tau_{2}\,d\xi_{2}
,
\tilde{\Omega}(\tau_{1},\xi_{1},\tau_{2},\xi_{2}):=\Omega(\tau_{1},\xi_{1},\tau_{1}+\tau_{2},\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})
, and
\tilde{D}:=\left\{(\tau_{1},\xi_{1},\tau_{2},\xi_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{4}:(\tau_{1},\xi_{1},\tau_{1}+\tau_{2},\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})\in D\right\}
. Define
\tilde{D_{1}}
and
\tilde{D_{2}}
in a similar way. Using Cauchy-Schartz inequality and Fubini’s theorem , we get

I_{D}\lesssim\left\|\chi_{\tilde{D}}\tilde{\Omega}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{\tau_{2},\xi_{2}}\,L^{2}_{\tau_{1},\xi_{1}}}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

Therefore we have to prove that

\left\|\chi_{\tilde{D}}\tilde{\Omega}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{\tau_{2},\xi_{2}}\,L^{2}_{\tau_{1},\xi_{1}}}\lesssim 1.

(4.39)

Consider
D_{1}
. Using inequality (
4.32
), Lemma
9
and the fact that
|R|=|-\sigma+\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}|\leq 3|\sigma_{2}|
in
D
, we get

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\chi_{\tilde{D_{1}}}\tilde{\Omega}\right|^{2}\,d\tau_{1}\,d\xi_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\langle\sigma_{2}\rangle^{2b}}\iint\frac{\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{2\theta}}{\langle\sigma\rangle^{2|a|}\langle\sigma_{1}\rangle^{2\theta}}\,d\tau_{1}d\xi_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int\frac{\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{2\theta}}{\langle R\rangle^{2b}\langle R+\sigma_{2}\rangle^{2\left(|a|+\theta\right)-1}}\,d\xi_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{|R|\leq|R+\sigma_{2}|}\frac{\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{2\theta}}{\langle R\rangle^{2\left(|a|+\theta\right)+\left(2b-1\right)}}\,d\xi_{1}+\int_{|R|\geq|R+\sigma_{2}|}\frac{\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{2\theta}}{\langle R+\sigma_{2}\rangle^{2\left(|a|+\theta\right)+\left(2b-1\right)}}\,d\xi_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\mathop{\text{\large$\int$}}\nolimits\frac{\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{2\theta}}{\langle R+p(\sigma_{2})\rangle^{2\left(|a|+\theta\right)+\left(2b-1\right)}}\,d\xi_{1},

where
p(\sigma_{2})\in\{0,\sigma_{2}\}
. Making the substitution
\mu=R+\sigma_{2}
in the last integral, and using inequality (
4.33
),

\displaystyle\mathop{\text{\large$\int$}}\nolimits\frac{\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{2\theta}}{\langle R+p(\sigma_{2})\rangle^{2\left(|a|+\theta\right)+\left(2b-1\right)}}\,d\xi_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathop{\text{\large$\int$}}\nolimits\frac{\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{2\theta}}{\langle\mu\rangle^{2\left(|a|+\theta\right)+\left(2b-1\right)}}\frac{d\mu}{\left|2\left(1+\nu\sgn(\xi_{1})\right)\xi_{1}+\left(2\xi_{2}-\nu\delta^{-1}\right)\right|}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\mathop{\text{\large$\int$}}\nolimits\frac{1}{\langle\mu\rangle^{2\left(|a|+\theta\right)+\left(2b-1\right)}}\frac{d\mu}{\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{1-2\theta}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathop{\text{\large$\int$}}\nolimits\frac{d\mu}{\langle\mu\rangle^{2\left(|a|+\theta\right)+\left(2b-1\right)}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle 1.

Finally, let us turn to the region
D_{2}
. Using inequality (
4.29
), Lemma
9
and the fact that in
D_{2}
,
\xi_{1}^{2}\lesssim|R|=|-\sigma+\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}|\leq 3|\sigma_{2}|
, we get

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\chi_{\tilde{D_{2}}}\Omega\right|^{2}\,d\tau_{1}d\xi_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\langle\sigma_{2}\rangle^{2b}}\mathop{\text{\large$\int$}}\nolimits\frac{\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{2\theta+1}}{\langle R+\sigma_{2}\rangle^{2\left(|a|+\theta\right)-1}}d\xi_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{R}$}}$}}\nolimits\frac{d\xi_{1}}{\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{4b-1-2\theta}\langle R+\sigma\rangle^{2\left(|a|+\theta\right)-1}}\lesssim 1,

where the last inequality is due to Lemma
12
, taking
\ell_{1}=2b-\frac{1}{2}-\theta
and
\ell_{2}=2\left(|a|+\theta\right)-1
and noting that

\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}=(2b-1)+(\theta+2|a|)-\frac{1}{2}>0+1-\frac{1}{2}=\frac{1}{2}.

It shows the estimate (
4.39
).
∎

5
Well-posedness

5.1
A priori estimates for smooth solutions

Let us first derive a priori estimates for smooth solutions to the system (
1.19
). Let
(u,v)
a smooth solution of (
1.19
) with initial data
(u(0),v(0)):=(u_{0},v_{0})
and
w:=w(u,v)
the gauge transformation defined in (
3.3
). Given
T\in\left(0,T^{*}\right)
, where
T^{*}
is the maximal time of existence given in Theorem
10
, we define

N_{T}^{s}\equiv N_{T}^{s}(u,v):=\max\Big(\left\|u\right\|_{X^{s+\frac{1}{2},b}_{\text{S}}(T)},\left\|v\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{s}_{x}},\left\|v\right\|_{L^{4}_{T}W^{s,4}_{x}},\\
\left\|w(0)\right\|_{H^{s}},\left\|\chi_{[0,T]}(t)\mathscr{N}_{\delta}(u,v,w)\right\|_{X^{s+\frac{1}{2},b}_{\text{S}}(T)}\Big),

(5.1)

where
\mathscr{N}_{\delta}(u,v,w)
is defined in (
1.23
). We first note that the function
T\mapsto N_{T}^{s}
is continuous and non-decreasing. On the other hand, considering the Duhamel formulation of the first equation of (
1.19
), applying the classic linear estimates in Bourgain spaces, and then, applying estimates (
4.22
) and (
4.20
), we get

\left\|u\right\|_{X^{s+\frac{1}{2},b}_{\text{S}}(T)}\lesssim\left\|u(0)\right\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}+T^{\kappa}\left\|u\right\|_{X^{\frac{1}{2},b}_{\text{S}}(T)}\left\|v\right\|_{X^{s-\theta,\theta}_{\text{BO}}(T)}\\
+T^{\kappa}\left\|u\right\|_{X^{s+\frac{1}{2},b}_{\text{S}}(T)}\left\|v\right\|_{X^{-\theta,\theta}_{\text{BO}}(T)}+T^{\kappa}\left\|u\right\|_{X^{\frac{1}{2},b}_{\text{S}}(T)}^{2}\left\|u\right\|_{X^{s+\frac{1}{2},b}_{\text{S}}(T)}.

(5.2)

Taking into account that

\|w(0)\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\left(1+\|v_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\right)\|v_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{x}},

(5.3)

and inequalities (
5.2
), (
3.5
) and (
4.6
), we obtain

\lim_{T\searrow 0}N_{T}^{s}\lesssim\left(1+\|u_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\|v_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\right)\left(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}+\|v_{0}\|_{H^{s}}\right).

(5.4)

Moreover, from inequality (

). As a consequence, we have the following estimate:

\frac{d}{dt}E_{c}^{s}(t)\lesssim\left\|v_{x}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\left\|v\right\|_{H^{s}_{x}}^{2}+\left\|u\right\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}_{x}}^{2}\left\|v\right\|_{H^{s}_{x}}+\left\|u\right\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}_{x}}^{4}+J+K,

(6.1)

where

J:=\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}}D^{s}_{x}v\,\partial_{x}D^{s}_{x}\left(|u(t)|^{2}\right)\,dx\,\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\,K:=2\alpha\,Im\int_{\mathbb{R}}uD^{s+1}_{x}\overline{u}\,D^{s}_{x}v\,dx.

The terms
J
and
K
in inequality (
6.1
) do not seem to be easy to estimate. Instead, as in the proof of Proposition 3.1 of
[
14
]
, we introduce an additional term in the usual energy from which
I
and
II
can be controlled. Indeed, applying
D^{s-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}
to the second equation of (
1.1
), multiplying by
D^{s-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\left(|u|^{2}\right)
, and integrating over
\mathbb{R}
, we get

\int_{\mathbb{R}}D^{s-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\left(|u|^{2}\right)\partial_{t}D^{s-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}v\,dx=\nu\int_{\mathbb{R}}D^{s-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\left(|u|^{2}\right)\mathscr{G}_{\delta}\partial_{x}^{2}D^{s-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}v\,dx\\
-\frac{\rho}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}D^{s-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\left(|u|^{2}\right)\partial_{x}D^{s-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\left(v^{2}\right)\,dx,

which implies,

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}}D^{s-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\left(|u|^{2}\right)D^{s-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}v\,dx=\nu\int_{\mathbb{R}}D^{s-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\left(|u|^{2}\right)\mathscr{G}_{\delta}\partial_{x}^{2}D^{s-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}v\,dx\\
-\frac{\rho}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}D^{s-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\left(|u|^{2}\right)\partial_{x}D^{s-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\left(v^{2}\right)\,dx\\
+\int_{\mathbb{R}}D^{s-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\partial_{t}\left(|u|^{2}\right)D^{s-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}v\,dx:=I+II+III.

(6.2)

With these considerations in mind, we define the modified energy
E^{s}_{m}(t)
by

E_{m}^{s}(t):=E^{s}_{c}(t)+\int_{\mathbb{R}}D^{s-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}v(t)\,D^{s-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\left(|u(t)|^{2}\right)\,dx.

Then, adding the inequalities (
6.1
) and (
6.2
), we get

\frac{d}{dt}E^{s}_{m}(t)\lesssim\left\|v_{x}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\left\|v\right\|_{H^{s}_{x}}^{2}+\left\|u\right\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}_{x}}^{2}\left\|v\right\|_{H^{s}_{x}}+\left\|u\right\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}_{x}}^{4}+J+K+I+II+III.

(6.3)

Since
H^{r}(\mathbb{R})
is a Banach algebra for all
r>\frac{1}{2}
,

\displaystyle II=-\frac{\rho}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}D^{s}_{x}\left(|u|^{2}\right)\partial_{x}D^{s-1}_{x}v\,dx\leq\frac{|\rho|}{2}\left\|D^{s}_{x}\left(|u|^{2}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\left\|\partial_{x}D^{s-1}_{x}v\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{H^{s}_{x}}^{2}\left\|v\right\|_{H^{s}_{x}}.

On the other hand, using the first equation of (
1.1
),

\displaystyle III

\displaystyle=2Re\int_{\mathbb{R}}D^{s-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}v\,D^{s-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\left(\overline{u}u_{t}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle=-2Im\int_{\mathbb{R}}D^{s-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}v\,D^{s-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\left(\overline{u}u_{xx}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle=-2Im\int_{\mathbb{R}}D^{s-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}v\,D^{s-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\left(\overline{u}u_{xx}+\overline{u}_{x}u_{x}-|u_{x}|^{2}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle=-2Im\int_{\mathbb{R}}D^{s-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}v\,D^{s-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\partial_{x}\left(\overline{u}u_{x}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle=2Im\int_{\mathbb{R}}D^{s-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\partial_{x}v\,D^{s-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\left(\overline{u}u_{x}\right)\,dx.

Now, using
\partial_{x}=-D_{x}\mathscr{H}
,

\displaystyle III

\displaystyle=-2Im\int_{\mathbb{R}}D^{s+\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\mathscr{H}v\,D^{s-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\left(\overline{u}u_{x}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle=2Im\int_{\mathbb{R}}D^{s}_{x}v\,\mathscr{H}D^{s}_{x}\left(\overline{u}u_{x}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle=2Im\int_{\mathbb{R}}D^{s}_{x}v\,\left[\mathscr{H}D^{s}_{x};\overline{u}\right]u_{x}\,dx+2Im\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(D^{s}_{x}v\right)\overline{u}\mathscr{H}\partial_{x}D^{s}_{x}u\,dx

\displaystyle=2Im\int_{\mathbb{R}}D^{s}_{x}v\,\left[\mathscr{H}D^{s}_{x};\overline{u}\right]u_{x}\,dx+2Im\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(D^{s}_{x}v\right)\,\overline{u}\,D^{s+1}_{x}u\,dx

\displaystyle:=III_{1}+III_{2}.

Making
\alpha=1
, we get
K+III_{2}=0
. Using Cauchy-Schwarz, Lemma
6
and Sobolev embedding,

\displaystyle III_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|D^{s}_{x}v\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\left\|\left[\mathscr{H}D^{s}_{x};\overline{u}\right]u_{x}\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|D^{s}_{x}v\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\left\|D^{s-1}_{x}\partial_{x}\overline{u}\right\|_{L^{\infty-}_{x}}\left\|\partial_{x}u\right\|_{L^{2+}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|D^{s}_{x}v\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\left\|D^{\frac{1}{2}+}D^{s-1}_{x}\partial_{x}\overline{u}\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\left\|\partial_{x}u\right\|_{L^{2+}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|v\right\|_{H^{s}_{x}}\left\|u\right\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}_{x}}^{2}.

Finally, making
\beta=\nu
, we obtain

\displaystyle J+I

\displaystyle=\nu\int_{\mathbb{R}}D^{s}_{x}v\,\partial_{x}D^{s}_{x}\left(|u(t)|^{2}\right)\,dx+\nu\int_{\mathbb{R}}D^{s-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\left(|u|^{2}\right)\mathscr{G}_{\delta}\partial_{x}^{2}D^{s-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}v\,dx

\displaystyle=\nu\int_{\mathbb{R}}D^{s+\frac{1}{2}}_{x}v\,\partial_{x}D^{s-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\left(|u(t)|^{2}\right)\,dx-\nu\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}D^{s-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\left(|u|^{2}\right)\mathscr{G}_{\delta}\partial_{x}D^{s-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}v\,dx

\displaystyle=\nu\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}D^{s-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\left(|u(t)|^{2}\right)\left(D^{s+\frac{1}{2}}_{x}v-\mathscr{G}_{\delta}D^{s-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\partial_{x}v\right)\,\,dx

\displaystyle\leq|\nu|\left\|\partial_{x}D^{s-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\left(|u(t)|^{2}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\left\|\left(|\xi|-\xi\left(\coth{(\delta\xi)}-\frac{1}{\delta\xi}\right)\right)|\xi|^{s-\frac{1}{2}}\widehat{v}\right\|_{L^{2}_{\xi}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}\|F_{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\|v\|_{H^{s-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}},

where

F_{\delta}(\xi):=|\xi|-\xi\coth(\delta\xi)+\frac{1}{\xi},\,\,\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}.

Now, we show that
F_{\delta}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
. In fact, for all
\xi>0
,

F_{\delta}(\xi)=\xi-\xi\coth(\delta\xi)+\frac{1}{\delta},

and then

F_{\delta}^{{}^{\prime}}(\xi)=\frac{\left(4\delta\xi-2\right)e^{2\delta\xi}+2}{\left(e^{2\delta\xi}-1\right)^{2}}\geq 0,

which implies that
F_{\delta}\nearrow
in
(0,\infty)
. Furthermore, since
\left|F_{\delta}(\xi)\right|\leq|\xi|
, we get the limit
\lim_{\xi\rightarrow 0}\,F_{\delta}(\xi)=0
. Then,
F_{\delta}\geq 0
in
(0,\infty)
. In fact,
F_{\delta}\geq 0
in
\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
because
F_{\delta}
is even. Since
\lim_{\xi\rightarrow\infty}\,F_{\delta}(\xi)=\frac{1}{\delta}
, we conclude that
F_{\delta}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
. Moreover,
\|F_{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}=\frac{1}{\delta}
. Returning to the inequality (
6.3
), we obtain

\frac{d}{dt}E^{s}_{m}(t)\lesssim\left\|v_{x}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\left\|v\right\|_{H^{s}_{x}}^{2}+\left\|u\right\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}_{x}}^{2}\left\|v\right\|_{H^{s}_{x}}+\left\|u\right\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}_{x}}^{4}.

(6.4)

Therefore, we have the following result:

Proposition 7
.

Let
s>\frac{1}{2}
and
T>0
. In system (
1.1
), suppose that
\alpha=1
and
\beta=\gamma
. There exist constants
c_{s},\kappa_{1,s}>0
such that for any
(u,v)\in C\left([0,T]\,;H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{R})\right)
solution of (
1.1
) satisfying

\left\|(u(t),v(t))\right\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\times H^{s}_{x}}\leq\frac{1}{c_{s}}

(6.5)

the following estimates hold true:

\frac{1}{2}\left(\left\|u(t)\right\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}_{x}}+\left\|v(t)\right\|_{H^{s}_{x}}\right)\leq E_{m}^{s}(t)\leq\frac{3}{2}\left(\left\|u(t)\right\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}_{x}}+\left\|v(t)\right\|_{H^{s}_{x}}\right),

(6.6)

and,

\partial_{t}E_{m}^{s}(t)\lesssim\left(1+\left\|\partial_{x}v(t)\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\right)E_{m}^{s}(t).

(6.7)

Moreover,

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|(u(t),v(t))\right\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\times H^{s}_{x}}\leq 2e^{\kappa_{1,s}\left(T+\left\|\partial_{x}v\right\|_{L^{1}_{T}\,L^{\infty}_{x}}\right)}\left\|\left(u(0),v(0)\right)\right\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\times H^{s}_{x}}.

(6.8)

6.2
Energy Estimates for the Difference of Two Solutions of (
1.1
)

Let
(u_{1},v_{1})
and
(u_{2},v_{2})
two solutions of the system (
1.1
). We define

\displaystyle w:=u_{1}-u_{2}\,\,\,\,\,u:=u_{1}+u_{2}

\displaystyle z:=v_{1}-v_{2}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,v:=v_{1}+v_{2}.

Using
u_{1}=\frac{1}{2}(u-w)
,
u_{2}=\frac{1}{2}(u-w)
,
v_{1}=\frac{1}{2}(v+z)
and
v_{1}=\frac{1}{2}(v-z)
, we find that
(w,z)
is a solution of the following system:

\begin{cases}iw_{t}+w_{xx}=\frac{1}{2}(vw+uz)+\gamma\left(\frac{1}{4}|w|^{2}w+\frac{1}{2}|u|^{2}w+\frac{1}{4}u^{2}\overline{w}\right)\\
z_{t}-\nu\,\mathscr{G}_{\delta}z_{xx}+\frac{\rho}{2}(vz)_{x}\,=\beta\,\partial_{x}\text{Re}(\overline{u}w)\end{cases}\,\,\,\,\,\,(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

(6.9)

The aim of this subsection is to derive energy estimates on
(w,z)
. As in the last subsection, let us define the following modified energies
(w,z)
:

\tilde{E}_{m}^{0}(t):=\|w(t)\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+\left\|D^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}w(t)\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|z(t)\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+\text{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}}J^{-1}_{x}z\left(\overline{u}w\right)(t)\,dx,

and

\tilde{E}_{m}^{s}(t):=\|w(t)\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+\left\|D^{s+\frac{1}{2}}_{x}w(t)\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+\|z(t)\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|D^{s}_{x}z\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}\\
+\text{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}}D^{s-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}z(t)D^{s-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\left(\overline{u}w\right)(t)\,dx,

for all
t\geq 0
. In subsections
6.2.1
and
6.2.2
we derive the desired estimates for
\tilde{E}_{m}^{0}(t)
and
\tilde{E}_{m}^{s}(t)
, respectively.

6.2.1
Energy Estimates at the level
s=0

We begin by multiplying the second equation of (
6.9
) by
z
and integrating to obtain

\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\|z\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}=-\frac{\rho}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}}z^{2}v_{x}\,dx+\nu\text{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}}z\left(\overline{u}w\right)_{x}\,dx\lesssim\|v_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\|z\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+J,

(6.10)

where
J=\nu\text{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}}z\left(\overline{u}w\right)_{x}\,dx
. This term cannot be estimated directly; instead, it will be compensated for by a correction coming from a modified energy term. Now, by multiplying the first equation by
2\overline{w}
, integrating and taking the imaginary part, we get

\partial_{t}\|w\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}}=\text{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}}uz\overline{w}\,dx+\frac{\gamma}{2}\text{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}\overline{w}^{2}\,dx\leq\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\|z\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\|w\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\frac{|\gamma|}{2}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}^{2}\|w\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}.

(6.11)

Now, applying
D^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}
to the first equation of (
6.9
), multiplying by
D^{s}_{x}\overline{w}
and taking the imaginary part, we obtain

\partial_{t}\left\|D^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}w\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}=\text{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}}D^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\overline{w}D^{\frac{1}{2}}(vw)\,dx+\text{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}}D^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\overline{w}D^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}(uz)\,dx\\
+\frac{\gamma}{2}\text{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}}D^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\overline{w}D^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\left(|w|^{2}w\right)\,dx+\gamma\text{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}}D^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\overline{w}D^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\left(|u|^{2}w\right)\,dx\\
+\frac{\gamma}{2}\text{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}}D^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\overline{w}D^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\left(u^{2}\overline{w}\right)\,dx:=I_{1}+\cdots+I_{5}.

(6.12)

Using Cauchy-Schwartz and Lemma
6
, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\text{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}}D^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\overline{w}\left[D^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x};v\right]w\,dx+\text{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}}v\left|D^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}w\right|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\text{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}}D^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\overline{w}\left[D^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x};v\right]w\,dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|D^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}w\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\left\|D^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\partial_{x}v\right\|_{L^{2+}_{x}}\|w\|_{L^{\infty-}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v\|_{H^{s}_{x}}\|w\|^{2}_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}}.

We decompose
I_{2}
as follows:

\displaystyle I_{2}=\text{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}}D^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\overline{w}\left[D^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x};u\right]z\,dx+\text{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(D^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\overline{w}\right)u\left(D^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}z\right)\,dx:=I_{2,1}+I_{2,2}.

Using Lemma
6
, we get

\displaystyle I_{2,1}\lesssim\left\|D^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}w\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\left\|D^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\partial_{x}u\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\|z\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}}\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}_{x}}\|z\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\|w\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}}.

The term
I_{2,2}
cannot be handled directly and will be canceled with another term coming from a modified term of the energy. Now, using the fractional Leibniz rule we get

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|D^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}w\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\left(\left\|D^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\left(|w|^{2}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}+\left\||w|^{2}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\left\|D^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}w\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|D^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}w\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\left(\left\|D^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}w\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}^{2}+\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}^{2}\left\|D^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}w\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\|u_{1}\|_{H^{s}_{x}}+\|u_{2}\|_{H^{s}_{x}}\right)^{2}\|w\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}}^{2},

\displaystyle I_{4}+I_{5}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|D^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}w\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\left(\left\|D^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\left(|u|^{2}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}+\left\||u|^{2}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\left\|D^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}w\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|D^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}w\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\left(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\left\|D^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}u\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}^{2}\left\|D^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}w\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\|u_{1}\|_{H^{s}_{x}}+\|u_{2}\|_{H^{s}_{x}}\right)^{2}\left(\|w\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}}\|u\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}}+\|w\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}}^{2}\right).

To compensate the contributions
J
and
I_{2,2}
, we now derive the modified energy term. In fact, note that

J=\nu\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{u}w\mathscr{H}D_{x}z\,dx.

On the other hand, from the second equation of (
6.9
),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}z+\nu\mathscr{G}_{\delta}D^{2}_{x}z+\frac{\rho}{2}\left(vz\right)_{x}=\nu\partial_{x}\text{Re}\left(\overline{u}w\right)

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\partial_{t}J^{-1}_{x}z+\nu\mathscr{G}_{\delta}D^{2}_{x}J^{-1}_{x}z+\frac{\rho}{2}\partial_{x}J^{-1}_{x}\left(vz\right)=\nu\text{Re}\partial_{x}J^{-1}_{x}\left(\overline{u}w\right)

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\text{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{u}w\partial_{t}J^{-1}_{x}z\,dx+\nu\text{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{u}w\mathscr{G}_{\delta}D^{2}_{x}J^{-1}_{x}z\,dx+\frac{\rho}{2}\text{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{u}w\partial_{x}J^{-1}_{x}(vz)\,dx=0.

Therefore,

\frac{d}{dt}\text{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{u}wJ^{-1}_{x}z\,dx=-\nu\text{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{u}w\mathscr{G}_{\delta}D^{2}_{x}J^{-1}_{x}z\,dx-\frac{\rho}{2}\text{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{u}w\partial_{x}J^{-1}_{x}(vz)\,dx\\
+\text{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{t}\left(\overline{u}w\right)J^{-1}_{x}z\,dx:=II_{1}+II_{2}+II_{3}.

(6.13)

Note that

\displaystyle J+II_{1}=\nu\text{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{u}w\left(\mathscr{H}D_{x}-\mathscr{G}_{\delta}D^{2}_{x}J^{-1}_{x}\right)z\,dx

\displaystyle\leq|\nu|\left\|\overline{u}w\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\left\|D_{x}\left(\mathscr{H}-D_{x}J^{-1}_{x}\mathscr{G}_{\delta}\right)z\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq|\nu|\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\|w\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\left\|\tilde{F}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\|z\|_{L^{2}_{x}},

where

\displaystyle\tilde{F}(\xi):=|\xi|\left|\frac{|\xi|}{\sqrt{1+\xi^{2}}}\left(\coth{(\delta\xi)-\frac{1}{\delta\xi}}\right)-\sgn(\xi)\right|,\,\,\,\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}.

Let us show that
\left\|\tilde{F}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}<\infty
. Indeed, when
\xi>0
,

\displaystyle F(\xi)=\sqrt{\frac{\xi^{2}}{1+\xi^{2}}}\left|\left(\xi\coth{\left(\delta\xi\right)-\sqrt{1+\xi^{2}}}\right)-\frac{1}{\delta}\right|.

Note that

\displaystyle\lim_{\xi\rightarrow\infty}\left(\xi\coth{(\delta\xi)-\sqrt{1+\xi^{2}}}\right)=

\displaystyle\lim_{\xi\rightarrow\infty}\xi\left(\coth{(\delta\xi)-1}\right)+\lim_{\xi\rightarrow\infty}\left(\xi-\sqrt{1+\xi^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\xi\rightarrow\infty}\frac{2\xi}{e^{2\delta\xi}-1}-\lim_{\xi\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\xi+\sqrt{1+\xi^{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

which implies that
\left\|\tilde{F}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}}<\infty
. When
\xi<0,

\displaystyle\tilde{F}(\xi)=\sqrt{\frac{\xi^{2}}{1+\xi^{2}}}\left|\sqrt{1+\xi^{2}}-\xi\coth{(\delta\xi)+\frac{1}{\delta}}\right|,

and observing that

\displaystyle\lim_{\xi\rightarrow-\infty}\left(\sqrt{1+\xi^{2}}-\xi\coth{(\delta\xi)}\right)=

\displaystyle\lim_{\eta\rightarrow\infty}\left(\sqrt{1+\eta^{2}}-\eta\coth{(\delta\eta)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\eta\rightarrow\infty}\left(\sqrt{1+\eta^{2}}-\eta\right)+\lim_{\eta\rightarrow\infty}\eta\left(1-\coth{(\delta\eta)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

we get
\left\|\tilde{F}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{R}^{-}}}<\infty
. Hence,

J+II_{1}\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\|z\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\|w\|_{L^{2}_{x}}.

Using Cauchy-Schwarz,

II_{2}\lesssim\left\|\overline{u}w\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\left\|\partial_{x}J^{-1}_{x}(vz)\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\|w\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\|v\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\|z\|_{L^{2}_{x}}.

On the other hand,

II_{3}=\text{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{u}_{t}wJ^{-1}_{x}z\,dx+\text{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{u}w_{t}J^{-1}_{x}z\,dx:=II_{3,1}+II_{3,2}.

(6.14)

Using the first equation of (
1.1
) in
(u_{1},v_{1})
and
(u_{2},v_{2})
, we get

II_{3,1}=\text{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{u}_{xx}wJ^{-1}_{x}z\,dx-\text{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\overline{u_{1}}v_{1}+\overline{u_{2}}v_{2}\right)wJ^{-1}_{x}z\,dx\\
-\gamma\text{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(|u_{1}|^{2}\overline{u_{1}}+|u_{2}|^{2}\overline{u_{2}}\right)wJ^{-1}_{x}\,dx:=II_{3,1,1}+II_{3,1,2}+II_{3,1,3}.

Using
\partial_{x}\left(\overline{u}_{x}w\right)=\overline{u}_{xx}w+\overline{u}_{x}w_{x}
, we obtain

\displaystyle II_{3,1,1}

\displaystyle=\text{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}\left(\overline{u}_{x}w\right)J^{-1}_{x}z\,dx-\text{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{u}_{x}w_{x}J^{-1}_{x}z\,dx

\displaystyle=-\text{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{u}_{x}wJ^{-1}_{x}\partial_{x}z\,dx-\text{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{u}_{x}w_{x}J^{-1}_{x}z\,dx:=II_{3,1,1,1}+II_{3,1,1,2}.

Using Cauchy-Schwartz and Lemma
6
,

\displaystyle II_{3,1,1,1}\leq\left\|\overline{u}_{x}w\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\left\|J^{-1}_{x}\partial_{x}z\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

The contribution

We decompose

\displaystyle II_{3,1,1,2,1}\lesssim\left\|J^{-1}_{x}\partial_{x}z\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\left\|D^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\partial_{x}\overline{u}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\left\|D^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}w\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}_{x}}\|z\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\|w\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}}.

\displaystyle II_{3,1,1,2,2}=\text{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathscr{H}D^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\left(\overline{u}D^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}w\right)\mathscr{H}D_{x}J^{-1}_{x}\,dx

\displaystyle=-\text{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(D^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\overline{w}\right)uD^{\frac{3}{2}}_{x}J^{-1}_{x}z\,dx.

Hence,

\displaystyle I_{2,2}+II_{3,1,1,2,2}

\displaystyle=\text{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(D^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\overline{w}\right)u\left(D^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}-D^{\frac{3}{2}}_{x}J^{-1}_{x}\right)z\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\left\|D^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}w\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\left\|\left(D^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}-D^{\frac{3}{2}}_{x}J^{-1}_{x}\right)z\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{s}_{x}}\|w\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}}\|z\|_{L^{2}_{x}},

(6.16)

\displaystyle IV_{3,2,1,1,2}
