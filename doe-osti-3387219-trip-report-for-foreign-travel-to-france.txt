Subject: Trip Report for Foreign Travel to France to attend IMORN-33, July 2026

This trip report summarizes the activities of a NCSP sponsored trip to the 33rd International
Meeting on Reactor Noise (IMORN-33) held at the Laboratory of Subatomic Physics &
Cosmology (LPSC) in Grenoble, France the week of July 6th, 2026.

IMORN-33 (July 6th – 8th, 2026)

IMORN-33 brought together a small international group (~30 attendees) focused on
reactor-noise measurements, interpretation, and applications across zero-power systems and
power-reactor environments. The meeting combined experimental updates, detector
development, and analytic methods, with contributions ranging from long-standing experts to
newer researchers and students. Discussions emphasized practical use cases, continued
refinement of noise techniques, and the importance of carrying this work forward to future
reactors and upcoming IMORN meetings.

Attendees on behalf of NCSP from LANL: Cole Kostelac and Flynn Darby

Meeting Location

Laboratory of Subatomic Physics & Cosmology (LPSC), Grenoble, France

Monday, July 6th, 2026:

The first day of IMORN-33 opened with self-introductions reflecting a wide range of
experience—from senior experts with decades in the field to current PhD students from across
Europe. Elsa Merle (LPSC) outlined ongoing molten-salt and CFD-coupled noise work, followed
by remarks from LPSC laboratory director Laurent Derome on broader research areas, including
neutron electric dipole moment studies, neutrino programs, cosmology projects, and
applications in medical physics.

Imre Pászit provided a historical overview of IMORN/SMORN, tracing the origins of
reactor-noise meetings from the 1960s to a period of disruption in the early 2000s and the more
recent return to an annual IMORN format. Hash Hashemian (Analysis and Measurements
Services, AMS) gave a perspective on practical noise applications in operating plants,
describing several cases where noise diagnostics identified flow or vibration issues in time to
prevent larger operational problems. Oszvald Glöcker (Nuclear Energy Consultant, Hungary)
discussed the need to integrate reactor-noise analysis into routine plant management and

Technical presentations covered a range of applications:

 • Hash Hashemian reviewed U.S. deployment needs and policy drivers for new reactors.
 • Frederik Reitsma (International Atomic Energy Agency IAEA) discussed the outlook for
 advanced reactor deployment and fuel-supply dynamics.
 • Wenqing Yao (China Nuclear Power Engineering Co., CNPE) outlined pressure-noise
 analysis work involving pipe-break scenarios.
 • Bhaskar (Canadian Nuclear Laboratories, CNL) presented recent signal-noise analysis
 capabilities for CANDU systems.
 • Glöcker described criteria for signal validation and fault detection.
 • Hashemian closed the session with practical online calibration monitoring of pressure
 transmitters.

LANL/NCSP Presentation: Flynn Darby presented on the MUSIC organic-scintillator
benchmark (P7), focusing on prompt-neutron decay constants as a benchmark parameter for
subcritical configurations. The talk led to several technical questions concerning OS modeling,
alpha values in concrete response, and how these comparisons shift near criticality.

During breaks, Vincent Lamirand (Swiss Federal Institute of Technology, EPFL) discussed
potential sharing of CROCUS-oscillated samples for upcoming Flat-Top oscillator
measurements. Later, Professor Daniel Siefman (University of California, Berkeley) outlined the
proposal for a new working group on noise for critical-assay applications, with Cole Kostelac
already a member and Flynn Darby intending to join.

Tuesday, July 7th, 2026:

Day 2 included a mix of detector development updates, real measurement results, and
supporting theoretical or simulation studies. Imre Pászit discussed advances in Cf-252
source-driven noise measurements, including reactivity extraction from spectral ratios and the
evolution of detector concepts; Flynn Darby and Cole Kostelac later noted privately that NCERC
last used a similar detector around 2007 before the source decayed. Additional
measurement-focused presentations covered CROCUS work, molten-salt–related experiments,
gamma-noise observations, and practical instrumentation developments. Theory and modeling
contributions were interspersed throughout the session to provide context for interpretation and
system behavior.

LANL/NCSP Presentation: Cole Kostelac presented detailed work on the delayed-gamma
term in Cohn-alpha, walking through the derivation of the mean generation rate and its
effect on the interpretation of gamma-noise measurements. The talk prompted several

Measurement-Focused Points

 • Cf-252 source-driven noise: reactivity extraction from spectral ratios; demonstration of
 older and newer detector concepts; historic Mihalczo-era material shown.
 • CROCUS experimental updates: modulation-noise visualizations and pseudo-random
 oscillation measurements compared with Transient Fission Matrix (TFM) and Monte
 Carlo (MC).
 • Molten Salt Reactor (MSR)-related observations: references to historic Molten Salt
 Reactor Experiment (MSRE) “bubble noise”
 • POLLEN oscillator (an instrumented pile oscillator for CROCUS), fission chambers,
 SAFFRON (a novel 3D detection system composed of 160 miniature detectors, System
 for Analysis of the Flux and Reactivity of sR-driven cOres in Nuclear)
 • Gamma-noise work: BGO-based measurements discussed by Satoshi Gunji (Japan
 Atomic Energy Agency, JAEA).
 • Stellaria molten-salt reactor: discussion of potential noise-measurement paths for future
 zero-power model ALVIN.

(Theoretical/simulation elements—moment-level kinetics shifts, TFM modeling,
machine-learning concepts, two-group MSR noise, and spatial Cohn-alpha behavior—were
presented to support interpretation of the above experimental work.)

Wednesday, July 8th, 2026:

Day 3 focused on a small set of presentations highlighting new measurements in CROCUS,
reactor-noise behavior in power-reactor contexts, and forward-looking discussions about the
IMORN series. Real measurement results were the center of the morning, with supporting
modeling work referenced where needed. The session closed with remarks on meeting
participation, the role of operators, and continued interest in sustaining the community into
future IMORN meetings.

Measurement-Focused Points

 • CROCUS neutron-correlation measurements (High-resolutionOnline Neutronics
 Experiments for dYnamics, HONEY): Vincent Lamirand showed position-sensitive He-3
 tube data at very low power (≈10 mW); observable clustering noted, though limited by
 low statistics.
