Introduction
• This project is sponsored by the Nuclear Criticality Safety Program (NCSP) in
  collaboration with Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory (LLNL) with a novel
  interest surrounding the transportation of fissile material: there is a concern
  regarding the inadvertent introduction of the material in a low-temperature
  environment.
• The low-temperature environment will be bounded down to temperatures not
  below -40 ºC, since this is the limit allotted for packages containing general
  radioactive material exposed to air [1].
• Eventually, the goal of the project is to conduct experiments spanning multiple
  fission energies at these low temperatures, concluding in an International
  Criticality Safety Benchmark Evaluation Project (ICSBEP) benchmark.

Cases
• This experimental regime will be conducted on Comet, “a general purpose,
  vertical-lift critical assembly.” [2]
• There were two cases of interest when contributing to this project based on [3]:
  − Both: The overall system (containing every plates) will have an outer diameter (OD) of
 14.98 in., with the HEU plates being 0.1179 in. thick. Additionally, one 2 in. thick
 annular reflector is placed around the system.
  − Case 1: A fast system that does not utilize polyethylene moderator within the vacuum
 chamber. It utilizes 26 HEU “Jemima” plates of varying inner diameters (IDs) ranging
 between 2.5-10 in. and 0.25 in. aluminum spacers.
  − Case 5: A thermal system that utilizes 1 in. thick polyethylene plates with 10 HEU
 “Jemima” plates. This case can be retrofitted to use either 5/8 in. or 7/8 in. aluminum
 spacers to control the leakage of neutrons from the system.

Data Considerations
• One of the main considerations of the projects involved the temperature effects
  on the neutron multiplication of the system. The data most affected by the
  temperature was:
  − Continuous-energy cross-sections: These were Doppler broadened in 5 ºC
 decrements from room temperature to -40 ºC using NJOY2016 by Peter Brain.
  − Thermal Scattering Law (TSL; S(α, β)): These provided significant reactivity change
 which will be explored later. The TSLs used were produced by Kemal Remic of Oak
 Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL).
 • TSL describes the transfer of momentum and energy of neutrons at low energies incident upon
 chemically bound nuclei. This becomes less important at high energies as neutrons become
 “insensitive to chemical structures.” [5]

Temperature
• These perturbation gave an idea of the reactivity insertion due to temperature
  change which was integral to the project scope.
• This was the parameter of most interest to the project. This was varied between
  -40 ºC and RT (assumed to be 20 ºC) with 5 ºC decrements.
• When performing the perturbations this affected the TMP, and cross-section
  data cards used within the MCNPⓇ input deck.
• Thermal contraction due to temperature reduction was not explored.

Conclusion & Future Work
• Δkeff demonstrated a monotonic growth w.r.t. both assembly insertion and
  reflector addition in a fully inserted configuration.
• Caveat: This did not hold true for the lower reflector heights where keff
  fluctuated; however, the system was still significantly subcritical.
• There were limitations with the model, namely density and dimensions were
  not changing with temperature due to thermal contraction/expansion.
  Additionally, there were no TSL data at lower temperatures for Al. Future work
  will be dedicated to confronting these challenges.
