Weyl algebras on Braverman-Kazhdan spaces
Chun-Hsien Hsu

Abstract
. Let G G be a split, simply connected, almost simple algebraic group over a field of characteristic zero, and let P P be a maximal parabolic subgroup of G G . We study the ring of differential operators on P der \ G P^{\mathrm{der}}\backslash G , showing that it shares several key structural properties with classical Weyl algebras. We also develop a corresponding theory of D D -modules.

1.
Introduction

Throughout the paper
K
is a field of characteristic zero. For an affine
K
-variety
X
, let
\mathcal{D}(X)
be the ring of differential operators on
X
in the sense of Grothendieck. Explicitly, let
\mathcal{D}_{0}:=K[X]
and for
i\geq 1
let

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{i}(X):=\{D\in\mathrm{End}_{K}(K[X]):[D,f]:=Df-fD\in\mathcal{D}_{i-1}(X)\textrm{ for all }f\in K[X]\}.

Here we have identified
f\in K[X]
as an element in
\mathrm{End}_{K}(K[X])
by function multiplication. Then
\mathcal{D}(X):=\bigcup_{i\geq 0}\mathcal{D}_{i}(X)
is a subalgebra of
\mathrm{End}_{K}(K[X]).
Note that
\{\mathcal{D}_{i}(X)\}_{i\geq 0}
is the order filtration of
\mathcal{D}(X)
. When
X
is smooth,
\mathcal{D}(X)
enjoys many desirable properties. For instance:

(1)

The ring
\mathcal{D}(X)
is a simple Noetherian domain.

(2)

The associated graded
K
-algebra
\mathrm{gr}\,\mathcal{D}(X)
with respect to the order filtration is naturally isomorphic to
K[T^{\ast}X^{\mathrm{sm}}],
where
T^{\ast}X^{\mathrm{sm}}=T^{\ast}X
denotes the cotangent bundle of
X
.

(3)

Every finitely generated left
\mathcal{D}(X)
-module satisfies Bernstein inequality. In particular, this allows one to define the notion of holonomic
D
-module.

These statements may no longer hold if
X
is singular. In the present paper, we show that the above properties remain valid for a new family of singular varieties.

Let
G
be a split, simply connected, almost simple algebraic group over
K,
and let
P
be a parabolic subgroup of
G
. The scheme
X_{P}^{\circ}:=P^{\mathrm{der}}\backslash G
is strongly quasi-affine, i.e.,
K[X_{P}^{\circ}]
is a finitely generated
K
-algebra, and the natural map
X_{P}^{\circ}\to X_{P}:=\mathrm{Spec}\,K[X_{P}^{\circ}]
is an open immersion. We refer to
X_{P}
as a
Braverman-Kazhdan space
. The variety
X_{P}
is generally singular; it is smooth precisely when it is a vector space. Nevertheless,
X_{P}
is normal, and the complement
X_{P}-X_{P}^{\circ}
has codimension at least
2
. Consequently, every differential operator on
X_{P}^{\circ}(K)
extends uniquely to
X_{P}(K)
.

When
K=\mathbb{C}
and
B
is a Borel subgroup,
\mathcal{D}(X_{B})
was first studied in
[
1
]
, where it was shown that
\mathcal{D}(X_{B})
is Noetherian and that the category of
\mathcal{D}(X_{B})
-modules can be described as a glued category. Properties (1)-(3) were subsequently established in
[
16
,
10
]
. A key observation in
[
2
,
1
]
is that if
P\supset B
is the minimal parabolic subgroup associated to a simple root of
G
, then the natural projection
X_{B}\to X_{P}
is a fibration with fiber
\mathbb{A}^{2}-\{0\}
. This allows analytic difficulties to be bypassed when constructing “Fourier automorphisms” on
\mathcal{D}(X_{B})
by reducing to the classical Weyl algebra
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{A}^{2}-\{0\})=\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{A}^{2})
. However, this fiber bundle method is special to Borel subgroups, since in this case the fibers are essentially affine spaces, whose ring of differential operators is well understood.

In the present paper, we prove that
\mathcal{D}(X_{P})
also enjoys properties (1)-(3) when
P
is maximal and
G
is not of type
E
or
F

W_{X_{P}}
, which relies on

, and is not used elsewhere in the paper.
. In
[

, by developing the necessary harmonic analysis on
X_{P}
in the archimedean case, the author introduced a
\mathbb{C}

W_{X_{P}}
in the spirit of
[

, and proved that
W_{X_{P}}
can be defined over
\mathbb{Q}

\mathcal{D}(X_{P}).
The present paper serves as a continuation of this work to study
W_{X_{P}}
and completes the basic theory of

.

We now outline our approach. We review the definition of
W_{X_{P}}
in §

W_{X_{P}}
is a simple domain by identifying a distinguished collection of elements in

that generate the identity. In the classical Weyl algebra, this is a consequence of the product rule, i.e.,
[x,\partial]=1
. The existence of such elements plays a fundamental role throughout the paper.

We next prove that every finitely generated left
W_{X_{P}}
-module enjoys Bernstein inequality, namely, its Gelfand–Kirillov dimension lies between
\dim X_{P}
and
2\dim X_{P}
. In particular, this implies

. As an application, we prove the existence of Bernstein-Sato polynomials on

and establish meromorphic continuation of Igusa’s local zeta functions of

(in the archimedean setting).

We close this introduction by relating our work to the broader Braverman-Kazhdan-Ngô program, and more generally to the Poisson summation conjecture. Roughly speaking, it is expected that on an affine normal spherical variety
X
over
\mathbb{R}
, there exists a Schwartz space
\mathcal{S}(X(\mathbb{R}))
with a suitable Fourier theory that allows one to generalize Tate’s thesis, in which local
L
-functions are realized as the greatest common divisor of zeta integrals. Furthermore, one expects that
\mathcal{S}(X(\mathbb{R}))
is local, so in particular it should be stable under multiplication by the regular functions
\mathbb{C}[X]
. Equivalently, this suggests that in the archimedean case

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(X(\mathbb{R}))=\{f\in L^{2}(X(\mathbb{R})):Df\in L^{2}(X(\mathbb{R}))\textrm{ for all }D\in\mathcal{D}(X)\}

as proposed in
[

. The identity above is confirmed when

and

is maximal
[

as a consequence of meromorphic continuation of Igusa’s local zeta functions. Interestingly, the study of
\mathcal{D}(X_{P})
also leads to a better understanding of the composition series of degenerate principal series
\mathrm{Ind}_{P}^{G}\chi
(see
[

). These perspectives suggest a new connection between harmonic analysis on
X
and the theory of
D
-modules on
X
, which may merit further investigation. We refer the reader to
[
8
]
for a more detailed proposal.

Preliminaries

Let

. For a differential operator

is the smallest integer

Often we view

as an element in

. To avoid confusion between the two natural ways

and

for the product of

and

2.1.
Weyl algebras on Braverman-Kazhdan spaces

Lemma 2.1
.

The spherical variety

Proof.

As

[

,

∎

3.
Basic properties of Weyl algebras

In this section, we establish fundamental properties of the algebra
W_{X_{P}}
. Recall that

\displaystyle\partial_{i}=(2\pi\sqrt{-1})^{d_{P}}\mathcal{F}_{P^{\mathrm{op}}|P}\circ x_{i}^{\mathrm{op}}\circ\mathcal{F}_{P|P^{\mathrm{op}}},

so we will often identify

as coordinate functions in

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. We have
\mathrm{ord}(f(\partial))=rd_{P}
for all
0\neq f\in V_{-r\omega_{P}}
.

Proof.

For
j\geq 0,
let
W_{j}
be the subspace in
W_{X_{P}}
consisting of operators of order at most
j
. By (
2.2
) the inclusion
\mathbb{Q}[X_{P^{\mathrm{op}}}]\longrightarrow W_{X_{P}}
induces a

-equivariant map

\displaystyle V_{-r\omega_{P}}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow W_{rd_{P}}/W_{rd_{P}-1}.

Since
\mathrm{ord}(\partial_{1}^{r})=rd_{P}
and

is irreducible, this map is injective, and the lemma follows.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

The set

\displaystyle\big\{b_{1}(\mathbf{x})b_{2}^{\mathrm{op}}(\mathbf{\partial})\in W_{X_{P}}:b_{1},b_{2}\in\mathcal{B}\big\}

.

Proof.

Suppose on the contrary that there is nontrivial linear relation over

Choose

to be minimal. Since

is a faithful

-module, the identity is the same as

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in J}c_{j}b_{1j}(\mathbf{x})b_{2j}^{\mathrm{op}}(\mathbf{\partial})(f)=0

for all

Choose

such that

Proposition 3.3

(1)

Let

Lemma 3.5

Let

\displaystyle\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}_{r}}b(\mathbf{x})b^{\mathrm{op}}(\mathbf{\partial})

\displaystyle=Z_{r}(E),

\displaystyle\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}_{r}}b^{\mathrm{op}}(\mathbf{\partial})b(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle Z_{r}(t):=\prod_{n=0}^{r-1}J(t-n).

We may assume

acts on

by the scalar multiplication by

Now let

Consider its action on

applying the computation above to

can be identified as an element in

Lemma 3.6
.

For any

4.
D
-modules

Let
R
be a finitely generated
K
-algebra. A family of
K
-vector spaces
\mathcal{F}=(F_{m})_{m\geq 0}
is a
filtration
of
R
if
it is an exhaustive ascending chain of
K
-vector subspaces of
R
and satisfies

\displaystyle F_{m}\cdot F_{n}\subseteq F_{m+n}\textrm{ for all }m,n\geq 0.

In this case we say
R
is a
filtered algebra
, and we will write
(R,\mathcal{F})
if we want to specify the filtration
\calf
. We adopt the convention that
F_{m}=0
if
m<0
. Let

\displaystyle\mathrm{gr}\,R=\mathrm{gr}^{\calf}(R):=\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}F_{m}/F_{m-1}

be the associated graded algebra. Given a set of generators
Z
of
R
, we say a filtration
\calf
of
R
is
standard
with respect to
Z
if
F_{0}=K
and

\displaystyle F_{m}:=F_{1}^{m}:=\mathrm{span}_{K}\{D_{1}\cdots D_{r}:D_{i}\in Z,r\leq m\}.

Let
M
be a left
R
-module. By a filtration
\Gamma=(\Gamma_{i})_{i\geq 0}
on
M
with respect to
(R,\mathcal{F})
, we mean an exhaustive ascending chain of
K
-vector subspaces of
M
such that

\displaystyle F_{m}\cdot\Gamma_{i}\subseteq\Gamma_{i+m}\textrm{ for all }i,m\geq 0.

A filtration
\Gamma
is
good
if
\dim\Gamma_{i}:=\dim_{K}\Gamma_{i}<\infty
for all
i
, and
\Gamma
is
standard
if
\Gamma_{i}=F_{i}\cdot\Gamma_{0}.
Let

\displaystyle\mathrm{gr}^{\Gamma}(M):=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}\Gamma_{i}/\Gamma_{i-1}

be the associated graded module of
\mathrm{gr}^{\calf}(R)
. Here
\Gamma_{-1}=0
.

We recall the definition of Gelfand-Kirillov dimension in this setting and record its standard properties (see for instance
[
18
, Chapter 8]
).

Definition 4.1
.

Let
Z\subseteq R
be a finite set of generators and
\mathcal{F}
be a standard filtration of
R
with respect to
Z
. Let
M
be a left
R
-module with a good standard filtration
\Gamma
(with respect to
\mathcal{F}
) with
\dim_{K}\Gamma_{0}<\infty
. We define the

(or simply GK-dimension) of

, to be

\displaystyle\mathrm{GK}(M):=\limsup_{k\to\infty}\log_{k}\dim\Gamma_{k}.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated left
R
-module with a good standard filtration
\Gamma
. We have the following.

(1)

\mathrm{GK}(M)
does not depend on
Z
and
\Gamma_{0}
.

(2)

\mathrm{GK}(M)=\mathrm{GK}(\mathrm{gr}^{\Gamma}(M))
.

(3)

Given an exact sequence of finitely generated
R
-modules

0\to M_{1}\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow M_{2}\longrightarrow 0,

we have
\mathrm{GK}(M)\geq\sup\{\mathrm{GK}(M_{1}),\mathrm{GK}(M_{2})\}
and the equality holds if the exact sequence splits.

(4)

\mathrm{GK}(M)\leq\mathrm{GK}(R)
.

∎

We define the

on any quasi-projective
K
-variety

. It is a quasi-coherent sheaf of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules. Let
\mathcal{T}
be the tangent sheaf of

Suppose

is affine. One has

\mathcal{D}_{1}(K[X])=K[X]+\mathcal{T}(X).

Let
\Delta(X)\subseteq\mathrm{End}_{K}(K[X])
be the
K

\mathcal{D}_{1}(X),
so
\Delta(X)\subseteq\mathcal{D}(X)
. In general the containment is strict, but when

is smooth,
\Delta(X)=\mathcal{D}(X)
. For more discussions, we refer the reader to
[

, Chapter 15]
.

Theorem 4.8
.

We have
\mathcal{D}(X_{P})=W_{X_{P}}.

Proof.

Since
X_{P}-X_{P}^{\circ}
has codimension at least
2
and
X_{P}
is normal, we have
\mathcal{D}(X_{P})=\mathcal{D}(X_{P}^{\circ})
. The containment
W_{X_{P}}\subseteq\mathcal{D}(X_{P}^{\circ})
follows from (
2.2
). Thus we only need to show
\mathcal{D}(X_{P})\subseteq W_{X_{P}}.

Let
D\in\mathcal{D}(X_{P}).
We may assume
[E,D]=\ell D
for some integer
\ell
. We claim there is
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
such that
b(\mathbf{x})D,Db(\mathbf{x})\in W_{X_{P}}
for all
b\in\mathcal{B}_{r}
. Note that
\mathcal{D}(X_{P})
is equipped with a natural rational
G
-action from (
2.1
). Assume first
\mathbb{C}D
is fixed by
B
. Let
U:=V(x_{1})^{c}:=\{x_{1}\neq 0\}\subset X_{P}.
It is an affine open subvariety of
X_{P}^{\circ}
. Since
\mathcal{T}(U)=\mathcal{T}(X_{P}^{\circ})_{x_{1}}
is generated by
\mathfrak{g}
as a
K[U]
-module, Proposition
4.7
implies

\displaystyle\Delta(U)\subseteq(W_{X_{P}})_{x_{1}}\subseteq\mathcal{D}(U).

As
U
is smooth,
\Delta(U)=\mathcal{D}(U)
and the inclusions above are equalities. In particular,
x_{1}^{r}D
and
Dx_{1}^{r}
are in
W_{X_{P}}
for some
r
large. Since
\mathbb{C}D
is fixed by
B,
by the
B
-action we have
b(\mathbf{x})D,Db(\mathbf{x})\in W_{X_{P}}
for all
b\in\mathcal{B}_{r}.
For general
D
, consider the finite dimensional
G
-submodule
V\subseteq\mathcal{D}(X_{P})
generated by
D
. As
V
is semisimple, the general case follows from the theory of highest weight.

By the previous discussion and Lemma
3.5
,
Z_{r}(-E-2s_{k}-2)D,DZ_{r}(E)\in W_{X_{P}}.

Note that
DZ_{r}(E)=Z_{r}(E-\ell)D.
If
\ell\geq 0,
then
Z_{r}(t-\ell)
and
Z_{r}(-t-2s_{k}-2)
are coprime. Thus
D\in W_{X_{P}}
. Assume
\ell<0
. For any
b\in\mathcal{B}_{-\ell}
,
[E,b(\mathbf{x})D]=0
and thus
b(\mathbf{x})D\in W_{X_{P}}.
Therefore, Lemma
3.5
implies

Z_{-\ell}(-E-2s_{k}-2)D\in W_{X_{P}}.

For the same reason, for any
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
b\in\mathcal{B}_{r}

\displaystyle Z_{r-\ell}(-E-2s_{k}-2)b^{\mathrm{op}}(\partial)D\in W_{X_{P}}.

Since for any
h\in K[t]
and
b\in\mathcal{B}_{r}
,
b^{\mathrm{op}}(\partial)h(E)=h(E+r)b^{\mathrm{op}}(\partial),

we have

\displaystyle W_{X_{P}}

\displaystyle\ni b^{\mathrm{op}}(\partial)Z_{-\ell}(-E-2s_{k}-2)D=Z_{-\ell}(-E-2s_{k}-2+r)b^{\mathrm{op}}(\partial)D.

For
r
sufficiently large,
Z_{-\ell}(t+r)
and
Z_{r-\ell}(t)
are coprime, so
b^{\mathrm{op}}(\partial)D\in W_{X_{P}}
. Consequently, there is

large such that
b(\mathbf{x})D
and
b^{\mathrm{op}}(\partial)D
for all

. Corollary

implies

.
∎

Lemma 4.9
.

We have natural isomorphisms of

-modules

\displaystyle W_{X_{P}}/W_{X_{P}}\mathfrak{g}

\displaystyle\cong K[X_{P}]\oplus K[X_{P^{\mathrm{op}}}]

and

\displaystyle W_{X_{P}}/W_{X_{P}}(\mathfrak{g}+\mathfrak{m}_{P}^{\mathrm{ab}})\cong K[X_{P}].

Proof.

Consider the homomorphism of
W_{X_{P}}
-modules

\displaystyle\varphi:W_{X_{P}}/W_{X_{P}}\mathfrak{g}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow K[X_{P}]\oplus K[X_{P^{\mathrm{op}}}]

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle\mapsto(D(1),\mathcal{F}_{P|P^{\mathrm{op}}}(D)(1)).

Let
r\geq 1
and
b\in\mathcal{B}_{r}
. Then
\varphi(b(\mathbf{x}))=(b,0)
and
\varphi(b^{\mathrm{op}}(\mathbf{\partial}))=(0,b^{\mathrm{op}}).
Also
\varphi(1)=(1,1)
and
\varphi(E)=(0,-2(s_{k}+1))
by Corollary
3.9
. Thus
\varphi
is surjective.

Let

such that

. Note that for a smooth affine variety

Thus by localization and the argument in the first part of the proof of Theorem

, there exists
r\geq 0
such that

for all

. Therefore, if

there is some

for all

. Corollary

\displaystyle W_{X_{P}}/W_{X_{P}}(\mathfrak{g}+\mathfrak{m}_{P}^{\mathrm{ab}})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow K[X_{P}]

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle\mapsto D(1).

∎

Proposition 4.10
.

We have an isomorphism of
W_{X_{P}}
-modules

\displaystyle H^{i}(X_{P}^{\circ},\mathcal{O}_{X_{P}^{\circ}})\cong\begin{cases}K[X_{P}]&\textrm{if }i=0,\\
K[X_{P^{\mathrm{op}}}]&\textrm{if }i=\dim X_{P}-1,\\
0&\textrm{if }i\neq 0,\dim X_{P}-1.\end{cases}

Therefore,

\displaystyle H^{\ast}(X_{P}^{\circ},\mathcal{O}_{X_{P}^{\circ}})\cong W_{X_{P}}/W_{X_{P}}\mathfrak{g}.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{O}_{X_{P}^{\circ}}
is a left
\mathcal{D}_{X_{P}^{\circ}}
-module,
H^{i}(X_{P}^{\circ},\mathcal{O}_{X_{P}^{\circ}})
is a
W_{X_{P}}
-module for all
i
. We compute
H^{i}(X_{P}^{\circ},\mathcal{O}_{X_{P}^{\circ}})
as
K[X_{P}]
-modules with compatible
\mathfrak{g}
-action.

Clearly
H^{0}(X_{P}^{\circ},\mathcal{O}_{X_{P}^{\circ}})=K[X_{P}]
. Let
\mathfrak{p}\subset K[X_{P}]
be the maximal ideal generated by
\mathcal{B}_{1}.
Let
i>0
. By
[
23
, 4.6.2]
, we have an isomorphism of
K[X_{P}]
-modules

\displaystyle H^{i}(X_{P}^{\circ},\mathcal{O}_{X_{P}^{\circ}})\cong H^{i+1}_{\mathfrak{p}}(K[X_{P}]),

where the latter is the local cohomology. By
[
12
, Proposition 2.14(1)]
,

H^{i+1}_{\mathfrak{p}}(K[X_{P}])\subseteq(H^{i+1}_{\mathfrak{p}}(K[X_{P}]))_{\mathfrak{p}}\cong H^{i+1}_{\mathfrak{p}K[X_{P}]_{\mathfrak{p}}}(K[X_{P}]_{\mathfrak{p}}).

Since

is Cohen-Macaulay,
H^{i+1}_{\mathfrak{p}K[X_{P}]_{\mathfrak{p}}}(K[X_{P}]_{\mathfrak{p}})=0
unless
i=\dim X_{P}-1
by
[
23
, Corollary 4.6.9]
. Thus
H^{i}(X_{P}^{\circ},\mathcal{O}_{X_{P}^{\circ}})=0
unless
i=\dim X_{P}-1
.

Now we compute
U:=H^{\dim X_{P}}_{\mathfrak{p}}(K[X_{P}]).
Consider the space
K[x_{1}^{-1},\ldots,x_{\dim V_{P}}^{-1}]
with the
K[V_{P}]
-module structure given by

\displaystyle\left(\prod_{i}x_{i}^{a_{i}}\right)\left(\prod_{i}x_{i}^{-b_{i}}\right):=\begin{cases}\prod_{i}x_{i}^{-(b_{i}-a_{i})}&\textrm{if }b_{i}\geq a_{i}\textrm{ for all }i,\\
0&\textrm{otherwise.}\end{cases}

It has a natural
\mathfrak{g}
-action given by derivation. Let
I
be the kernel of the map
K[V_{P}]\to K[X_{P}]
. The variety
X_{P}
is Gorenstein by Lemma
2.1
, so by the Grothendieck local duality
[
21
,
Tag 0AAK
]
and the computation in
[
17
]

\displaystyle U\cong\{f\in K[x_{1}^{-1},\ldots,x_{\dim V_{P}}^{-1}]:I\cdot f=0\}

as
K[X_{P}]
-modules with compatible
\mathfrak{g}
-actions. As a
\mathfrak{g}
-module,

is isomorphic to
K[X_{P^{\mathrm{op}}}].

Let
e_{0}
be a nonzero element in
H^{\dim X_{P}-1}(X_{P}^{\circ},\mathcal{O}_{X_{P}^{\circ}})
that generates the trivial
\mathfrak{g}
-representation. It follows that the kernel of the homomorphism

\displaystyle W_{X_{P}}

\displaystyle\to H^{\dim X_{P}-1}(X_{P}^{\circ},\mathcal{O}_{X_{P}^{\circ}})

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle\mapsto D(e_{0})

contains
W_{X_{P}}\mathfrak{g}
and

Therefore, by Lemma
4.9

H^{\dim X_{P}-1}(X_{P}^{\circ},\mathcal{O}_{X_{P}^{\circ}})
contains a
W_{X_{P}}
-submodule isomorphic to

. Hence
H^{\dim X_{P}-1}(X_{P}^{\circ},\mathcal{O}_{X_{P}^{\circ}})\cong K[X_{P^{\mathrm{op}}}]
.
∎

4.3.
Associated graded algebra of
W_{X_{P}}

Now we study the associated graded algebra of
W_{X_{P}}
with respect to the order filtration via quantization. Our discussion is largely motivated by
[
10
]
.

For
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z},
we let

W_{X_{P},\lambda}:=\{D\in W_{X_{P}}:[E,D]=\lambda D\}.

Let
(F_{m}^{\mathrm{ord}})_{m\geq 0}
be the order filtration on
W_{X_{P}},
and put

F_{m}^{\mathrm{ord}}W_{X_{P},\lambda}:=F_{m}^{\mathrm{ord}}W_{X_{P}}\cap W_{X_{P},\lambda}.

For
m\in\mathbb{Z},
let

\displaystyle\widehat{F}_{m}W_{X_{P}}:=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{F}_{m}W_{X_{P},\lambda}:=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}}\{D\in W_{X_{P},\lambda}:\operatorname{ord}\bigl(\mathcal{F}_{P|P^{\mathrm{op}}}(D)\bigr)\leq m+\lambda d_{P}\big\}.

Lemma 4.11
.

We have
\widehat{F}_{m}W_{X_{P}}=0
for
m<0
and
\widehat{F}_{0}W_{X_{P}}=K[X_{P}].

Proof.

Let
m\leq 0
. Suppose there is
0\neq D\in\widehat{F}_{m}W_{X_{P}}
. We may assume
D\in W_{X_{P},\lambda}
and let
n=\operatorname{ord}(D)
. If
n=0
, then
D\in V_{-\lambda\omega_{P}}^{\lor},
so by Lemma
3.1

\mathrm{ord}(\mathcal{F}_{P|P^{\mathrm{op}}}(D))=\lambda d_{P}\geq m+\lambda d_{P}.
When
m<0,
the inequality is strict, which contradicts the definition of
\widehat{F}_{m}W_{X_{P}}.
If
n>0
, there is
b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}\in\mathcal{B}_{1}
such that

p=[[\cdots[D,b_{1}],b_{2}],\dots,b_{n}]\in K[X_{P}]

is a nonzero homogeneous function of degree
\lambda+n
. Then by Lemma
3.1

\displaystyle(\lambda+n)d_{P}=\mathrm{ord}(\mathcal{F}_{P|P^{\mathrm{op}}}(p))\leq m+\lambda d_{P}+nd_{P}-n,

which is impossible as
m\leq 0
.
∎

Lemma 4.12
.

For
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z},

\mathcal{F}_{P\mid P^{\mathrm{op}}}\left(F_{m}^{\mathrm{ord}}W_{X_{P},\lambda}\right)\subseteq F_{m+\lambda d_{P}}^{\mathrm{ord}}W_{X_{P^{\mathrm{op}}},-\lambda}.

Proof.

We show that for any
m\in\mathbb{Z}

(4.1)

\displaystyle\widehat{F}_{m}W_{X_{P}}\subseteq F_{m}^{\mathrm{ord}}W_{X_{P}}.

When
m\leq 0,
this follows from Lemma
4.11
. Suppose
m>0.
Let
D\in\widehat{F}_{m}W_{X_{P},\lambda}
. By the definition of order and induction, it suffices to show
[D,f]\in\widehat{F}_{m-1}W_{X_{P}}
for all
f\in K[X_{P}].
We may assume
0\neq f\in V^{\lor}_{-j\omega_{P}}.
Then

\displaystyle\operatorname{ord}(\mathcal{F}_{P|P^{\mathrm{op}}}([D,f]))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{ord}([\mathcal{F}_{P|P^{\mathrm{op}}}(D),\mathcal{F}_{P|P^{\mathrm{op}}}(f)])

\displaystyle\leq(m+\lambda d_{P})+jd_{P}-1=(m-1)+(\lambda+j)d_{P}.

Since
[D,f]\in W_{X_{P},\lambda+j},

[D,f]\in\widehat{F}_{m-1}W_{X_{P}}
. This proves (
4.1
).

Let
D\in F_{m}^{\mathrm{ord}}W_{X_{P},\lambda}
so that
A:=\mathcal{F}_{P|P^{\mathrm{op}}}(D)\in W_{X_{P^{\mathrm{op}}},-\lambda}
. As

\displaystyle\mathrm{ord}(\mathcal{F}_{P^{\mathrm{op}}|P}(A))=\mathrm{ord}_{P}(D)\leq m=(m+\lambda d_{P})+(-\lambda)d_{P},

we have
A\in\widehat{F}_{m+\lambda d_{P}}W_{X_{P^{\mathrm{op}}}}.
Applying (
4.1
) for
P^{\mathrm{op}},
we conclude that
A\in F_{m+\lambda d_{P}}^{\mathrm{ord}}W_{X_{P^{\mathrm{op}}},-\lambda}.
This proves the lemma.
∎

For a filtered
K
-algebra
(R,\mathcal{F}),
its Rees algebra is a subalgebra of
R[\hbar]
given by

\displaystyle R_{\hbar}:=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}R_{\hbar}^{i}:=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\hbar^{i}F_{i}.

It is a graded
K[\hbar]
-algebra. One has a natural isomorphism of graded

-algebras

\displaystyle R_{\hbar}/\hbar R_{\hbar}\cong\mathrm{gr}^{\mathcal{F}}R.

Apply this construction to
W_{X_{P}}=\mathcal{D}(X_{P})
with respect to the order filtration. Define for

of order at most

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{P|P^{\mathrm{op}},\hbar}(\hbar^{m}D):=\hbar^{m+d_{P}\lambda}\mathcal{F}_{P|P^{\mathrm{op}}}(D).

By Lemma
4.12
, this defines an homorphism of graded
K[\hbar]
-algebra

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{P|P^{\mathrm{op}},\hbar}:W_{X_{P},\hbar}\longrightarrow W_{X_{P^{\mathrm{op}}},\hbar},

and
\mathcal{F}_{P^{\mathrm{op}}|P,\hbar}\circ\mathcal{F}_{P|P^{\mathrm{op}},\hbar}=\mathrm{Id}.
This induces an isomorphism

(4.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{P|P^{\mathrm{op}}}:\mathrm{gr}\,W_{X_{P}}\longrightarrow\mathrm{gr}\,W_{X_{P^{\mathrm{op}}}}

Apply the construction of the Rees algebra to the sheaf
\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{D}_{X_{P}^{\circ}}
with respect to the order filtration to obtain a sheaf of graded
K[\hbar]
-algebras
\mathcal{D}_{\hbar}.
Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{X_{P}^{\circ}}:=\mathcal{D}_{\hbar}/\hbar\mathcal{D}_{\hbar}.

Note that
\mathcal{A}
is canonically isomorphic to
p_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{T^{\ast}X_{P}^{\circ}}
, where
p:T^{\ast}X_{P}^{\circ}\to X_{P}^{\circ}
is the natural projection of the cotangent bundle. Let
\mathcal{D}_{\hbar}(X_{P}^{\circ}):=\Gamma(X_{P}^{\circ},\mathcal{D}_{\hbar})=\mathcal{D}(X_{P}^{\circ})_{\hbar}
. Then
\mathcal{D}_{\hbar}(X_{P}^{\circ})=W_{X_{P},\hbar}
by Theorem
4.8
, and we have a natural inclusion of algebras

\displaystyle\varphi:\mathrm{gr}\,W_{X_{P}}=\mathrm{gr}\,\mathcal{D}_{\hbar}(X_{P}^{\circ})\cong\mathcal{D}_{\hbar}(X_{P}^{\circ})/\hbar\mathcal{D}_{\hbar}(X_{P}^{\circ})\hookrightarrow\Gamma(X_{P}^{\circ},\mathcal{A})=K[T^{\ast}X_{P}^{\circ}].

Theorem 4.13
.

Endow
W_{X_{P}}
with the order filtration. Then we have a natural isomorphism of graded
K
-algebras

\displaystyle\mathrm{gr}\,W_{X_{P}}=\mathrm{gr}\,\mathcal{D}(X_{P})\xrightarrow{\sim}K[T^{\ast}X_{P}^{\circ}].

In particular, the map
\mathcal{F}_{P|P^{\mathrm{op}}}:W_{X_{P}}\longrightarrow W_{X_{P^{\mathrm{op}}}}
induces an isomorphism of
K
-algebras

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{P|P^{\mathrm{op}}}:K[T^{\ast}X_{P}^{\circ}]\longrightarrow K[T^{\ast}X_{P^{\mathrm{op}}}^{\circ}]

such that
\mathcal{F}_{P^{\mathrm{op}}|P}\circ\mathcal{F}_{P|P^{\mathrm{op}}}=\mathrm{Id}.
∎

Proof.

We may assume
K=\mathbb{C}
. For ease of notation, we let
X:=X_{P}^{\circ}
and write
\otimes=\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}.
Note that we have a natural isomorphism
T^{\ast}X\cong(\mathfrak{p}^{\mathrm{der}}\backslash\mathfrak{g})^{\vee}\times^{P^{\mathrm{der}}}G,
so

is a
P^{\mathrm{ab}}=M^{\mathrm{ab}}
-torsor of

Let
\mathcal{P}:=P\backslash G
and
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{P}}(\lambda)
be the line bundle on
\mathcal{P}
associated to
\lambda\omega_{P}
for
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}.
For any variety
Y
over
\mathcal{P},
let
\mathcal{O}_{Y}(\lambda)
be the pullback to

\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{P}}(\lambda).
By the natural

we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}[T^{\ast}X]\cong\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma(\widetilde{X},\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(\lambda))=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{Ind}_{P}^{G}(\mathbb{C}[(\mathfrak{p}^{\mathrm{der}}\backslash\mathfrak{g})^{\lor}]\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\lambda}),

where

is the

-dimensional representation
\lambda\omega_{P}
of

is the induction functor (c.f.
[

Note that
\mathbb{C}[E,\hbar]
is a subalgebra of

Decompose

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\hbar}(X)=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{D}_{\hbar}(X)_{\lambda}

as eigenspaces of the operator

Then

\displaystyle\mathrm{gr}\,\mathcal{D}(X)\cong\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{D}_{\hbar}(X)_{\lambda}/\hbar\mathcal{D}_{\hbar}(X)_{\lambda}.

Since
\varphi
is

-equivariant, by the

-action,
\varphi
is surjective if and only if for all

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\hbar}(X)_{\lambda}/\hbar\mathcal{D}_{\hbar}(X)_{\lambda}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Ind}_{P}^{G}(\mathbb{C}[(\mathfrak{p}^{\mathrm{der}}\backslash\mathfrak{g})^{\lor}]\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\lambda})

For

and a left
\mathbb{C}[E,\hbar]
-module

be the left

\displaystyle E\cdot^{(\lambda)}m:=(E+(\lambda+s_{k}+1)\hbar)\cdot m,\quad\forall m\in M.

Therefore, to prove the theorem, we only need to show

\displaystyle{}^{(\lambda)}\mathcal{D}_{\hbar}(X)_{\lambda}/\hbar{}^{(\lambda)}\mathcal{D}_{\hbar}(X)_{\lambda}\cong\mathrm{Ind}_{P}^{G}(\mathbb{C}[(\mathfrak{p}^{\mathrm{der}}\backslash\mathfrak{g})^{\lor}]\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\lambda}).

The module
\mathbb{C}[G]
admits a regular

be the universal enveloping algebra of

and

be its Rees algebra with respect to the PBW filtration. Differentiating the left regular action induces an algebra homomorphism

which induces a left

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}[G]\otimes U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})\cong D_{\hbar}(G).

Any element

induces a morphism
\mathbb{C}[X_{P}][\hbar]\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}[G][\hbar]
. The morphism is trivial iff

Therefore,

(4.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\hbar}(X)\cong\left(\mathbb{C}[G]\otimes\left(U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})/U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})\cdot\mathfrak{p}^{\mathrm{der}}\right)\right)^{P^{\mathrm{der}}}.

Under this isomorphism, the
G

D_{\hbar}(X)
translates to the right regular
G
-action on
\mathbb{C}[G].
The
P

D_{\hbar}(X),
where

with a

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}
. The algebra isomorphism
\mathbb{C}[E,\hbar]\cong\mathfrak{p}^{\mathrm{der}}\cdot U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{p})\backslash U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{p})

Consider

The action of

on

induces an isomorphism of left

-modules

\displaystyle M(\lambda)\cong U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})/U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})\cdot\mathfrak{p}^{\mathrm{der}}.

Under this isomorphism,

Then the

\displaystyle(E-(s_{k}+1)\hbar)\cdot m-m\cdot E,\quad\forall m\in M(\lambda).

It follows that (

) induces an isomorphism of graded
\mathbb{C}[E,\hbar]
-modules

\displaystyle{}^{(\lambda)}\mathcal{D}_{\hbar}(X)_{\lambda}\cong\mathrm{Ind}_{P}^{G}(M(\lambda)).

In conclusion to prove the assertion we only need to show the natural short exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow\hbar M(\lambda)\longrightarrow M(\lambda)\longrightarrow M(\lambda)/\hbar M(\lambda)\cong\mathbb{C}[(\mathfrak{p}^{\mathrm{der}}\backslash\mathfrak{g})^{\lor}]\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\lambda}\longrightarrow 0

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow\hbar\mathrm{Ind}_{P}^{G}(M(\lambda))\longrightarrow\mathrm{Ind}_{P}^{G}(M(\lambda))\longrightarrow\mathrm{Ind}_{P}^{G}(\mathbb{C}[(\mathfrak{p}^{\mathrm{der}}\backslash\mathfrak{g})^{\lor}]\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\lambda})\longrightarrow 0.

Assume
\lambda\geq 0
. It suffices to show

is induced from the action of the universal enveloping algebra
U((\mathfrak{so}_{2n+2})_{\mathbb{C}})
on
\mathcal{S}(X_{n}(\mathbb{R}))
. In other words, we have a natural surjection

\displaystyle U((\mathfrak{so}_{2n+2})_{\mathbb{C}})\longrightarrow W_{X_{n}}(\mathbb{C}).

Equip
U((\mathfrak{so}_{2n+2})_{\mathbb{C}})
with the PBW filtration. The induced filtration
\mathcal{F}
on
W_{X_{n}}(\mathbb{C})
is standard with

\displaystyle F_{1}=B_{1}+\mathfrak{so}_{2n}(\mathbb{C})+\mathbb{C}E.

By
[
15
]

\mathrm{gr}^{\mathcal{F}}(W_{X_{n}}(\mathbb{C}))\cong\mathbb{C}[T^{\ast}X_{n}^{\mathrm{sm}}]
as explained in
[
6
, Remark 4.3]
.

Let us compute the Bernstein-Sato polynomial of
\varphi(\mathbf{x})=x_{1}
on
X_{n}
. Observe that since
d_{P}=2,
we have
[\partial_{i},x_{j}]\in\mathbb{C}+\mathfrak{so}_{2n}(\mathbb{C})+\mathbb{C}E
for all
i,j
by (
2.2
). As
[x_{i},Y]\in\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{x_{j}\}
for
Y\in\mathfrak{so}_{2n}(\mathbb{C})+\mathbb{C}E,
dually
[\partial_{i},Y]\in\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{\partial_{j}\}
for all
i
. Recall
\Delta(X_{n})\subset W_{X_{n}}
is the subalgebra of
W_{X_{n}}
generated by
\mathbb{C}[X_{n}]
and
\mathfrak{so}_{2n}(\mathbb{C})+\mathbb{C}E.
Then each element in
W_{X_{n}}
can be written as
\sum_{i}D_{i}b_{i}(\mathbf{\partial})
for some
b_{i}\in\mathbb{C}[X_{n}]
and
D_{i}\in\Delta(X_{n})
.

We have
\partial_{1}\cdot x_{1}^{s+1}=J(s+1)x_{1}^{s}.
On the other hand, suppose

\displaystyle B(s)\cdot\varphi^{s}=\sum_{i,j}s^{i}D_{ij}b_{ij}(\partial)\varphi\cdot\varphi^{s}

for some
B\in\mathbb{C}[s]
and
D_{ij}\in\Delta(X_{n}),b_{ij}\in\mathbb{C}[X_{n}].
Note that given
b\in\mathbb{C}[X_{n}]
there is
b^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C}[x_{1}]
such that for any
n\geq 0,

b(\partial)(x_{1}^{n})=b^{\prime}(\partial_{1})(x_{1}^{n})
. Thus we may assume
b_{ij}(\partial)=\partial_{1}^{j}.
Since elements in
\Delta(X_{n})
are contained in the nonnegative weight spaces of
[E,\cdot],

D_{i0}=0
for all
i
. It follows that
J(s+1)|B(s),
and thus
b_{\varphi}(s)=J(s+1)
.

4.6.
Applications to zeta integrals

For locally integrable functions
f_{1},f_{2}
on
X_{P}^{\circ}(\mathbb{R})
define pairings

\displaystyle\langle f_{1},f_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle:=\int_{X_{P}^{\circ}(\mathbb{R})}f_{1}(x)f_{2}(x)dx,

\displaystyle(f_{1},f_{2})

\displaystyle:=\langle f_{1},\bar{f}_{2}\rangle,

whenever the integral is absolutely convergent. Notice that for
f_{1},f_{2}\in L^{2}(X_{P}(\mathbb{R}))

\displaystyle(f_{1},f_{2})=(\mathcal{F}_{P|P^{\mathrm{op}}}(f_{1}),\mathcal{F}_{P|P^{\mathrm{op}}}(f_{2})).

Let
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(X_{P}^{\circ}(\mathbb{R}))
and
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(X_{P}^{\circ}(\mathbb{R})).
Let
D\in W_{X_{P}}(\mathbb{C})
. We claim

\displaystyle\langle D(\varphi),f\rangle=\langle\varphi,D^{\ast}(f)\rangle

for some
D^{\ast}\in W_{X_{P}}(\mathbb{C})
. This is clear if
D\in\mathbb{C}[X_{P}],
where
D^{\ast}=D
. To prove the claim, it suffices to show the existence of
\partial_{i}^{*}
for each
i
. Choose
f^{\prime}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(X_{P}^{\circ}(\mathbb{R}))
such that
f^{\prime}|_{\mathrm{Supp}(f)}=1
. We have

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{i}(\varphi),f\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\partial_{i}(\varphi f^{\prime}),f\rangle

\displaystyle=(\partial_{i}(\varphi f^{\prime}),\bar{f})

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{F}_{P|P^{\mathrm{op}}}(\partial_{i}(\varphi f^{\prime})),\mathcal{F}_{P|P^{\mathrm{op}}}(\bar{f}))

\displaystyle=(2\pi\sqrt{-1})^{d_{P}}(x_{i}^{\mathrm{op}}\mathcal{F}_{P|P^{\mathrm{op}}}(\varphi f^{\prime}),\mathcal{F}_{P|P^{\mathrm{op}}}(\bar{f}))

\displaystyle=(2\pi\sqrt{-1})^{d_{P}}(\mathcal{F}_{P|P^{\mathrm{op}}}(\varphi f^{\prime}),x_{i}^{\mathrm{op}}\mathcal{F}_{P|P^{\mathrm{op}}}(\bar{f}))

\displaystyle=(-1)^{d_{P}}(\mathcal{F}_{P|P^{\mathrm{op}}}(\varphi f^{\prime}),\mathcal{F}_{P|P^{\mathrm{op}}}(\partial_{i}(\overline{f})))

\displaystyle=(-1)^{d_{P}}(\mathcal{F}_{P|P^{\mathrm{op}}}(\varphi f^{\prime}),\mathcal{F}_{P|P^{\mathrm{op}}}(\overline{\partial_{i}(f)}))

\displaystyle=(-1)^{d_{P}}(\varphi f^{\prime},\overline{\partial_{i}(f)})

\displaystyle=\langle\varphi,(-1)^{d_{P}}\partial_{i}(f)\rangle.

Hence
\partial_{i}^{\ast}=(-1)^{d_{P}}\partial_{i}.
This is the integration by parts on
X_{P}(\mathbb{R})
.

Theorem 4.26
.

Let
\varphi\in\mathbb{R}[X_{P}]-\mathbb{R}
and
U:=\{\varphi>0\}.
Let
f\in\mathcal{S}(X_{P}(\mathbb{R})).
The integral

\displaystyle Z_{+}(f,s):=\int_{U}f(x)\varphi(x)^{s}dx

originally defined on
\mathrm{Re}(s)\geq 0
extends to a meromorphic function on
\mathbb{C}
. Furthermore, if we write
b_{\varphi}(s)=\prod_{\lambda}(s+\lambda),
and let

\displaystyle\gamma_{\varphi}(s):={\prod_{\lambda}\Gamma(s+\lambda)},

where
\Gamma(s)
is the gamma function. Then
\gamma_{\varphi}(s)^{-1}Z_{+}(f,s)
is entire.

Proof.

Assume first
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(X_{P}^{\circ}(\mathbb{R})).
Choose an ascending sequence of compact subsets
U_{n}
of
U
such that
U=\cup_{n}U_{n}
. Then
\mathbf{1}_{U_{n}}\to\mathbf{1}_{U}
in
L^{1}(X_{P}(\mathbb{R}))
. Let
b_{\varphi}
be the Bernstein-Sato polynomial of
\varphi
so that for all
x\in U

\displaystyle b_{\varphi}(s)\cdot\varphi(x)^{s}=(D(s)\cdot\varphi^{s+1})(x)

for some
D(s)\in W_{X_{P}}[s].
Note that
\varphi(x)^{s}
is well-define for all
x\in U,
so this identity becomes an identity of holomorphic functions on
\mathrm{Re}(s)>0.

Then for
\mathrm{Re}(s)>0

\displaystyle b_{\varphi}(s)Z_{+}(f,s)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle D(s)\varphi^{s+1},f\mathbf{1}_{U_{n}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle\varphi^{s+1},D(s)^{\ast}(f\mathbf{1}_{U_{n}})\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\varphi^{s+1},D(s)^{\ast}(f)\mathbf{1}_{U}\rangle

\displaystyle=Z_{+}(D(s)^{\ast}f,s+1).

For general
f\in\mathcal{S}(X_{P}(\mathbb{R}))
, choose by smooth truncations
f_{i}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(X_{P}^{\circ}(\mathbb{R}))
converging to
f
in
L^{1}(X_{P}(\mathbb{R})).
Then by Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem for
\mathrm{Re}(s)>0

\displaystyle b_{\varphi}(s)Z_{+}(f,s)=\lim_{i\to\infty}b(s)Z_{+}(f_{i},s)=\lim_{i\to\infty}Z_{+}(D(s)^{\ast}f_{i},s+1)=Z_{+}(D(s)^{\ast}f,s+1).

Observe that RHS is absolutely convergent for
\mathrm{Re}(s)>-1.
Therefore, by repeating the process we can analytically extend the domain of definition of
Z_{+}(f,s)
to all
\mathbb{C},
with possible poles controlled by
b_{\varphi}(s+k)
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
as claimed.
∎

Let
f\in\mathcal{S}(X_{P}(\mathbb{R}))
and
\varphi\in\mathbb{R}[X_{P}]-\mathbb{R}.
For a quasi-character
\chi:\mathbb{R}^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times},
define the zeta integral

\displaystyle Z(f,\chi):=\int_{X_{P}^{\circ}(\mathbb{R})}f(x)\chi(\varphi(x))dx.

Note that we can write
\chi=|\cdot|^{s}\mathrm{sgn}^{j}
where
s\in\mathbb{C}
and
j\in\{0,1\}.
For
\mathrm{Re}(s)>0
, write

\displaystyle Z(f,\chi)=\int_{\varphi>0}f(x)\varphi(x)^{s}+(-1)^{j}\int_{\varphi<0}f(x)(-\varphi(x))^{s}dx.

Since
b_{\varphi}=b_{-\varphi},
by Theorem
4.26

Z(f,\chi)
can be extended to a meromorphic function in
\chi
such that
\gamma_{\varphi}(s)^{-1}Z(f,|\cdot|^{s}\mathrm{sgn}^{j})
is entire. The same argument applies to
X_{P}(\mathbb{C})
using the fact that
W_{X_{P}}(\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C})=W_{X_{P}}(\mathbb{C})\oplus\overline{W_{X_{P}}(\mathbb{C})}
under the complex conjugation (c.f.
[
13
, pp. 71-73]
).
