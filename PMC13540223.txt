Estimated cardiac deaths associated with treating chronic lymphocytic leukemia with ibrutinib versus zanubrutinib in the United States
Authors: Jennifer R. Brown, Talha Munir, Andrew H. Lipsky, Swetha Challagulla, David Campbell, Keri Yang, Qianhong Fu, Wassim Aldairy, Rhys Williams, Constantine S. Tam
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundIn the absence of real-world comparative studies of cardiac death risk, this study estimates the potential reduction in cardiac mortality associated with treating chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL) patients with zanubrutinib instead of ibrutinib in first-line (1L) or second-line or later (2L+) in the United States (US).MethodsNumber needed to treat (NNT) for incremental cardiac death with ibrutinib versus zanubrutinib was applied to real-world ibrutinib utilization from Symphony over 5 years (2020–2024). Primary analysis utilized head-to-head cardiac death risks from ALPINE (2L+). An exploratory 1L analysis utilized indirect comparisons from SEQUOIA and RESONATE-2. Sensitivity analyses assessed cardiac death risk with 10+ years of ibrutinib utilization (2014–2024).ResultsIn 2L+ CLL, NNT for incremental cardiac death was 54 patients. Based on real-world ibrutinib use, 81 cardiac deaths could potentially have been avoided with zanubrutinib over 5 years. The exploratory 1L CLL NNT per cardiac death was 46, corresponding to 140 potentially avoidable deaths if patients were treated with zanubrutinib. Over a 10+ year period, the number of potentially avoidable deaths increased to 318 (2L+) and 489 (1L).ConclusionsThe NNT model suggests substantial cardiac death reductions if zanubrutinib were utilized instead of ibrutinib for CLL treatment in US practice.

Introduction

Chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL) is an indolent hematologic malignancy characterized by abnormal B-cell lymphocytes in the peripheral blood, bone marrow, and lymph nodes [1]. In CLL, Bruton tyrosine kinase (BTK) has been identified as an essential component of signaling pathways regulating cell proliferation and survival [2]. Ibrutinib was the first covalent BTK inhibitor (cBTKi) developed to specifically target this pathway and subsequently approved for the treatment of CLL [1]. Ibrutinib demonstrated improvements in progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) compared to ofatumumab in second-line or later (2L+) CLL/small lymphocytic lymphoma (SLL) patients [3]. In the RESONATE-2 study, ibrutinib also demonstrated superior PFS and OS compared to chlorambucil in a first-line (1L) setting [4,5].

However, the risk of cardiac adverse events, including hypertension, atrial fibrillation, and ventricular arrythmias, associated with ibrutinib is well documented from both clinical trials and studies in real-world settings [6–12]. The development of new or worsened hypertension after ibrutinib initiation for CLL was associated with a more than twofold increased risk of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACEs) [13]. In a large, international pharmacovigilance database, treatment with ibrutinib was associated with a range of worsened cardiac outcomes, including severe and occasionally fatal events [12]. Prior to the initiation of ibrutinib, a cardiac risk assessment including thorough review of comorbidities, concomitant medications, and cardiovascular functional assessment is recommended in order to anticipate risk of cardiac comorbidities in an effort to prevent interruption of cancer treatment [14–16]. The association of ibrutinib with severe and occasionally fatal cardiac complications underscores the need for safer BTKi options for patients. The off-target effects combined with patients’ predisposing risk factors and concomitant cardiovascular morbidities all contribute to the development of cardiovascular adverse events [17].

Zanubrutinib is a covalent, irreversible, next-generation cBTKi specifically designed to maximize BTK occupancy and minimize off-target inhibition, resulting in a more favorable safety profile. In the randomized phase III ALPINE trial (NCT03734016) in 2L+ CLL, zanubrutinib established superiority over ibrutinib, with a sustained PFS benefit (hazard ratio [HR], 0.68; 95% confidence interval [CI], 0.54–0.84) and higher overall response rate (85.6% versus 75.4%) [17]. In the ALPINE trial, cardiac events were lower with zanubrutinib compared to ibrutinib (25.9% versus 35.5%), including occurrence of atrial fibrillation (7.1% versus 17.0%). While this study provides head-to-head comparative data showing that zanubrutinib was associated with lower cardiac risk compared to ibrutinib in a 2L+ setting, there is an absence of real-world comparative studies evaluating the risk of treatment-related cardiac death.

The efficacy of zanubrutinib for the 1L treatment of CLL has been shown in the phase III SEQUOIA (NCT03336333) with improved PFS compared to bendamustine–rituximab that was sustained over 5 years (HR 0.29; 95% CI 0.21–0.41) [18,19]. Zanubrutinib was well tolerated with low rates of any adverse event or cardiotoxicity while on therapy, albeit with a small increased risk of atrial fibrillation compared to bendamustine–rituximab (7.1% versus 3.5% at median follow-up of 61.2 months) [19]. Data from these comparative trials suggest zanubrutinib may be associated with fewer cardiovascular adverse events and a lower risk for cardiac death compared to ibrutinib [20,21]. Real-world studies continue to support the favorable cardiovascular adverse events profile of zanubrutinib observed in clinical trials, finding low rates of treatment-limiting adverse events [22,23].

While lower rates of cardiovascular morbidity and mortality have been observed with zanubrutinib, the impact of BTKi treatment choice on cardiac death at the United States (US) population level is unclear.

The purpose of this study is to better understand the risk of cardiac death associated with ibrutinib and zanubrutinib for CLL in the US in the absence of direct comparative real-world data. The number needed to treat (NNT) analysis is a statistical method to translate the efficacy of alternative treatments into a single estimate of the number of patients needed to be treated with one treatment versus another to achieve one additional outcome of interest within a specified time period [24,25]. The NNT analysis is an established method to contextualize the value of treatments and has been used to support evidence-based practice in hematologic malignancies [26–29]. The aim of this study is to conduct an NNT modeling analysis to estimate the number of potential cardiac deaths that could be avoided by using zanubrutinib instead of ibrutinib for the treatment of 2L+ CLL in US clinical practice over 5 years, based on the published cardiac death risk data in the head-to-head ALPINE study and real-world data on ibrutinib use. Additionally, an exploratory, hypothesis-generating analysis aims to investigate the number of cardiac deaths that could potentially be avoided with zanubrutinib versus ibrutinib in the 1L setting, using data from the SEQUOIA and RESONATE-2 clinical trials, respectively. Sensitivity analyses were conducted to assess the cardiac death risk of ibrutinib versus zanubrutinib over a 10+ year period in the US.

Methods

Analysis overview

An NNT modeling analysis was conducted to assess how many patients with CLL would need to be treated with zanubrutinib instead of ibrutinib to prevent one incremental cardiac death in the 2L+ setting. The NNT framework calculates the number of patients who need to be treated with one treatment versus another to achieve an incremental outcome by dividing 1 by the absolute difference of event risk probability (1/[event risk treatment1 – event risk treatment2] [24,25]. The NNT for incremental cardiac death risk in this study was calculated as the reciprocal of the absolute cardiac death risk increase for ibrutinib when compared with zanubrutinib (1/[cardiac death risk with ibrutinib – cardiac death risk with zanubrutinib]). The primary analysis examined the potential total number of avoidable deaths over a 5-year period. The NNT to avoid incremental cardiac death in 2L+ treatment of CLL was calculated from the risk of cardiac death with ibrutinib and zanubrutinib and then applied to the number of patients receiving ibrutinib in the US using real-world data over 5 years (Figure 1). In an exploratory analysis, the NNT for incremental cardiac death risk associated with ibrutinib versus zanubrutinib treatment was assessed for 1L CLL. The exploratory analysis assessed the incremental impact of treatment on cardiac death over a 5-year period by applying NNT estimates to real-world treatment utilization of ibrutinib. In addition, exploratory analyses estimated the number of additional cardiac deaths that were associated with ibrutinib compared to zanubrutinib over a 10+ year period for the primary 2L+ and exploratory 1L CLL treatment settings by applying the NNT for incremental cardiac death to the number of patients who received ibrutinib in the US over this period.

Cardiac death risk inputs

In the primary analysis, cardiac death risks for zanubrutinib and ibrutinib in 2L+ treatment of CLL were extracted from the results of the ALPINE study (0.0% for zanubrutinib versus 1.852% for ibrutinib) [20]. In the ALPINE study, no cardiac deaths were reported among patients treated with zanubrutinib. There were 6 cardiac deaths among patients treated with ibrutinib (acute cardiac failure [n = 1], cardiac arrest [n = 2], congestive cardiomyopathy [n = 1], and myocardial infarction [n = 2]) during the 5-year period. Baseline characteristics of patients treated with zanubrutinib and ibrutinib were similar across the randomized arms of ALPINE. For zanubrutinib and ibrutinib arms, respectively, the median age was 67 versus 68, the proportion who are male was 65.1% versus 71.4%, and the proportion with ECOG PS score ≥1 was 60.6% versus 62.5%. Additional patient characteristics, including existing cardiovascular disease, concomitant medications, and comorbidity burden, that could influence the risk of cardiac death were not published. However, these factors were assumed to be balanced between arms by randomization. The proportion of patients with any-grade treatment-emergent hypertension in ALPINE was similar between zanubrutinib- and ibrutinib-treated arms (27.2% and 25.3%, respectively) [20].

In the 1L treatment setting exploratory analysis, cardiac death risks for zanubrutinib were extracted from SEQUOIA study results and cardiac death risks for ibrutinib were extracted from the RESONATE-2 study for indirect comparison (Table 1) [5,19]. RESONATE-2 was selected as the comparator because it provides the longest follow-up for ibrutinib monotherapy in the 1L setting, offering the most robust dataset for long-term cardiac event estimation. Study populations were similar between SEQUOIA and RESONATE-2. The SEQUOIA study included 64% males, with a median age of 70 years, and 48% had an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status score of 1 [18]. In comparison, the RESONATE-2 study population included 65% males, with a median age of 73 years, and 48% had an ECOG performance status score 1 [4]. Real-world treatment utilization inputs

The number of CLL patients receiving ibrutinib in the real-world setting from 1 January 2020 to 31 December 2024 (5-year period) was estimated from the US Symphony Integrated Dataverse database (Symphony, Blue Bell, PA, USA) [30]. Treatment patterns of CLL were also examined over the period of 2014 to 2024 (10+ year period) in a sensitivity analysis. The US Symphony Integrated Dataverse database provides a comprehensive longitudinal source of medical, hospital, and prescription claims in the US. While the database captures diagnosis-coded cardiac-related encounters, cardiac death data were not available. Approximately 30%–50% of patients with CLL are represented in the database. Patients diagnosed with CLL between 1 January 2014 and 31 December 2024, were identified and assessed for utilization of ibrutinib in 2L+ and 1L treatment of CLL using prescription and medical claims to define treatment regimens and line progression. For the sensitivity analysis assessing the impact of duration of therapy, patient counts by time period were calculated from treatment initiation to the end of treatment, which was defined by discontinuation, switching to another BTKi, or progression to the next line of therapy.

Sensitivity analysis

The NNT and avoidable cardiac deaths may be impacted by the time on treatment. To explore the robustness of study results to the impact of time on treatment, NNT over a 5-year period was conducted in the primary analysis (2L+ CLL) and exploratory analysis (1L CLL), using real-world patient counts stratified by time on treatment in cohorts of 0–12 months, >12–36 months, >36–60 months, and >60 months.

Among these CLL patients, a total of 10,884 were treated with ibrutinib, including 4,419 in the 2L+ setting and 6,465 patients in the 1L treatment setting. As an exploratory analysis, ibrutinib treatment patterns were assessed over a 10+ year period from 1 January 2014 to 31 December 2024. During this period, 308,261 patients with a CLL diagnosis were identified and 122,446 received any treatment. Among the CLL patients receiving treatment, a total of 39,738 were treated with ibrutinib, including 17,213 in the 2L+ setting and 22,525 in the 1L treatment setting.

Cardiac death NNT for 2L+ CLL

The NNT with ibrutinib versus zanubrutinib in 2L+ CLL resulting in an incremental cardiac death was calculated using cardiac death data from the ALPINE study (Table 3). The NNT with ibrutinib resulting in an incremental cardiac death was then applied to the real-world utilization of ibrutinib over time-on-treatment periods to calculate the total number of cardiac deaths that may be avoided. The NNT model estimated that up to 81 cardiac deaths may be avoided over a 5-year period in US clinical practice, if 2L+ CLL patients were treated with zanubrutinib instead of ibrutinib. Additionally, the model estimates for every 54 patients treated with zanubrutinib instead of ibrutinib for 2L+ CLL, one additional cardiac death can be prevented over 5 years (Figure 2). In the exploratory analysis utilizing 10+ year treatment patterns, the NNT model estimated in the US that up to 318 cardiac deaths may be avoided if 2L+ CLL patients were treated with zanubrutinib instead of ibrutinib. Sensitivity analysis results

In the primary analysis, among 4,419 patients identified in the Symphony Integrated Dataverse database who received ibrutinib in the 2L+ setting over a 5‑year period, the distribution by time on treatment was 2,111 patients for 0–12 months, 1,434 for >12–36 months, 814 for >36–60 months, and 60 for >60 months. The sensitivity analysis yielded an NNT of 54, corresponding to 61 cardiac deaths avoided with zanubrutinib in the 2L+ setting over a 5‑year period.

In the exploratory analysis, among 6,465 patients identified in the Symphony Integrated Dataverse database who received ibrutinib in the 1L setting over a 5‑year period, the distribution by time on treatment was 2,812 patients for 0–12 months, 2,097 for >12–36 months, 1,461 for >36–60 months, and 95 for >60 months. The sensitivity analysis yielded an NNT of 46, corresponding to 64 cardiac deaths avoided with zanubrutinib in the 1L setting over a 5‑year period.

Discussion

Since the approval of the first-generation therapy, ibrutinib, and continuing with next-generation zanubrutinib, BTKi therapy has been a cornerstone treatment for CLL. However, growing evidence from clinical trials and real-world studies suggests ibrutinib carries a unique spectrum of cardiotoxicity and increased risk for cardiac death compared to zanubrutinib [12,31–33]. In the absence of real-world head-to-head comparative data on the risk of cardiac death between zanubrutinib and ibrutinib, this study was the first to explore the impact treatment selection could make at the US population level. We developed an NNT model employing similar statistical methods consistent with those used in prior studies of hematologic malignancies, with the aim of contextualizing the number of patients who would need to be treated with a particular therapy to achieve an incremental outcome [26–29]. Our model suggests a clinically meaningful reduction in mortality. In this NNT model analysis, over a 5-year period from 2020 to 2024, switching from ibrutinib to zanubrutinib may potentially avoid an estimated 81 and 140 cardiac deaths in 2L+ and 1L settings, respectively. In the sensitivity analysis utilizing ibrutinib treatment patterns from 2014 to 2024, the estimated number of potentially preventable cardiac deaths increased to approximately 318 and 489 cardiac deaths in 2L+ and 1L settings, respectively. Consistent with previous research, this study supports the importance of considering cardiac mortality risk in evaluating the net benefits of each BTKi in treatment selection decisions [34].

The selectivity of zanubrutinib is thought to reduce toxicity and convey lower occurrence of adverse events [21]. In a pooled analysis of 1,550 patients treated with zanubrutinib for a median duration of 34.4 months, only 13 (0.8%) required a dose reduction, and 16 (1.0%) patients discontinued therapy for treatment-emergent cardiovascular events [9]. Lower rates of cardiac events with zanubrutinib can allow patients whose CLL is well maintained on therapy to remain on therapy without treatment interruption, holds, dose reductions, or discontinuation [17,21,35]. The risk of treatment-related cardiac adverse events is an important consideration when selecting therapy options for individual patients, and the more favorable safety profile of zanubrutinib supports its position as a preferred treatment option over ibrutinib.

Given the observed cardiotoxicity of ibrutinib and BTKi treatments, several guidelines provide recommendations to optimize patient outcomes [14–16]. This is of particular interest in CLL, which typically presents in older patients who are more likely to have comorbidities from their advanced age [17,36,37]. Proactive risk mitigation strategies often start with patient screening and treatment selection practices. Prior to treatment selection and initiation of CLL treatment, patients should receive a baseline cardiovascular risk assessment that includes blood pressure, pulse, electrocardiogram, and patient history for comorbidities and concomitant medications, with bleeding risk or drug interactions [14,38]. It is critical that physicians and patients have choice in BTKi to account for these individual factors and preferences to optimize treatment outcomes and safety. Especially for patients with risk factors for cardiovascular events, but indeed for all patients given that sudden death is not a predictable outcome, treatments with lower cardiac toxicity need to be accessible without restrictions.

The risk for a CLL patient experiencing a cardiovascular adverse event increases with time from treatment initiation and varies across cBTKi therapies [32,39]. Previous studies have found atrial fibrillation may develop more quickly with ibrutinib, with an elevated risk during the first 6 months of treatment that persists over time [9,40]. To account for the impact of treatment duration on cardiovascular risk, this model analysis applied a longer time horizon of 60+ months to capture short-term and longer-term cardiac death risks.

The sensitivity analysis showed that the NNT for zanubrutinib to prevent incremental cardiac death in the 2L+ CLL setting over 5 years was robust to alternative time-on-treatment assumptions. However, the variability in time to adverse events and potential for long-term risks with treatment highlight the need for continued monitoring in the real world. Routine monitoring for cardiovascular toxicities during CLL treatment should be conducted at a frequency tailored to patient-specific risk [14,41]. As recommendations for patient screening, monitoring, and mitigation of adverse events are adopted in clinical practice, greater use of next-generation BTKi therapies, reductions in treatment interruptions, and improvements in patient outcomes may be observed, but further research is needed to establish the impact of these strategies including use of next-generation BTKi therapies on treatment-related cardiac deaths.

Limitations

The NNT analyses utilized treatment-related cardiac death risks from clinical trials and real-world treatment utilization data from the US Symphony Integrated Dataverse database. These findings should be interpreted with consideration of differences in clinical trial populations and variations in reporting of cardiac deaths, which may potentially limit comparability of outcomes across trials. Additionally, observed rates of cardiac deaths in clinical trials may not be representative of all patients or populations, and limited sample size and duration of follow-up may underestimate the cardiac death risks. As awareness of cardiac death risk evolves, real-world prescribing patterns may change as patients’ cardiac risk is considered in treatment selection. This may impact the number of cardiac deaths potentially avoidable with zanubrutinib.

The exploratory 1L analysis utilized cardiac death inputs from trials that enrolled patients over different periods (RESONATE-2 enrollment 2013–2014; SEQUOIA enrollment 2017–2019). Cardiovascular monitoring was less established in the earlier period in which patients were enrolled in the RESONATE-2 trial of ibrutinib, which may inflate cardiac mortality attributed to ibrutinib independent of drug effect. Interpretation of the exploratory analysis should consider population and study differences between the RESONATE-2 and SEQUOIA trials. Cardiac deaths reported from trials were utilized in the exploratory analysis without matching or statistical adjustment.

Finally, this model assumes patients remain on therapy as per trial protocols. In real-world practice, early discontinuation of ibrutinib due to non-cardiac toxicities (e.g., arthralgia) might reduce the effective exposure period and reduce the absolute number of observed cardiac deaths. Conversely, the number of observed cardiac deaths in the real world may be higher if patients switching to zanubrutinib carry residual cardiovascular risk from prior ibrutinib exposure. Further real-world studies are needed to validate the comparative cardiac death risk of treating CLL patients with ibrutinib compared to zanubrutinib over longer periods, as well as to evaluate the broader impacts of treatment beyond the risk of cardiac death to other cardiovascular events and health impacts. If new data become available, a future meta-analysis incorporating additional comparative evidence could strengthen cardiac death risk estimates for NNT analysis.

Conclusion

This study estimates that treating patients in the US with zanubrutinib instead of ibrutinib for CLL could potentially prevent approximately 81 cardiac deaths in the 2L+ setting and 140 cardiac deaths in the 1L settings over a 5-year period. Over a 10+ year period, the number of potentially avoidable cardiac deaths with zanubrutinib increases to 318 and 489 in the 2L+ and 1L setting respectively. These results highlight the potential benefits of the safer cardiac profile of zanubrutinib compared to ibrutinib and the importance of treatment options for patients. The study findings should be interpreted based on model assumptions and data inputs including potential differences in the patient populations treated in the clinical trials versus in the real world.
