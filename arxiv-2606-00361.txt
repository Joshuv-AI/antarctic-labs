Casimir effect near spontaneously Lorentz-breaking magnetic vacua in Plebański nonlinear electrodynamics
C. A. Escobar, R. Linares, A. Martín-Ruiz

Abstract
We study the Casimir response of electromagnetic fluctuations near magnetic vacua that spontaneously break Lorentz symmetry in gauge-invariant nonlinear electrodynamics. The theory is formulated in the Plebański first-order representation, with a single-invariant Hamiltonian potential V ^ ​ ( P ) \widehat{V}(P) taken as the fundamental nonlinear object. This formulation is particularly useful because the nontrivial vacua are obtained as stationary points of the effective Hamiltonian, rather than as extrema of V ^ ​ ( P ) \widehat{V}(P) itself. In the magnetic branch, the symmetry-breaking condition is governed by S m ​ ( P ) ≡ V ^ P ​ ( P ) + 2 ​ P ​ V ^ P ​ P ​ ( P ) S_{\rm m}(P)\equiv\widehat{V}_{P}(P)+2P\widehat{V}_{PP}(P) , whose vanishing also signals the degeneracy of the Hamiltonian constraint structure and the loss of rank of the longitudinal magnetic response. We first linearize around a regular purely magnetic background P ¯ \bar{P} , with S m ​ ( P ¯ ) ≠ 0 S_{\rm m}(\bar{P})\neq 0 , and obtain an ordinary Maxwell-like branch together with an extraordinary anisotropic branch controlled by α ⁡ ( P ¯ ) = V ^ P ​ ( P ¯ ) / S m ​ ( P ¯ ) \alpha(\bar{P})=\widehat{V}_{P}(\bar{P})/S_{\rm m}(\bar{P}) . We then compute the regularized parallel-plate Casimir energy for magnetic backgrounds perpendicular and parallel to the plates. As the regular background approaches the exact Lorentz-breaking vacuum P ⋆ P_{\star} , where S m ​ ( P ⋆ ) = 0 S_{\rm m}(P_{\star})=0 , the extraordinary branch becomes singular and, in the parallel configuration, the Casimir energy diverges within the regular-sector description. A direct analysis on the degenerate surface shows, however, that the extraordinary branch does not survive as an independent physical propagating mode for generic momenta. The divergence is therefore not a prediction of an infinite vacuum energy at the exact Lorentz-breaking state, but a diagnostic of the noncommutativity between quantizing the regular theory and imposing S m ​ ( P ⋆ ) = 0 S_{\rm m}(P_{\star})=0 before quantization.



I
Introduction

The Casimir effect is one of the most direct manifestations of the
quantum structure of the electromagnetic vacuum

Casimir 1948
;
Milton 2001
. In its standard form, the confinement of
vacuum fluctuations between conducting boundaries modifies the
zero-point spectrum and produces a measurable force. Because the effect
is determined by the spectrum of admissible field modes, it provides a
sensitive probe of modifications to the underlying electromagnetic
dynamics.

This spectral sensitivity makes the Casimir effect a useful arena for
testing departures from ordinary Lorentz-invariant propagation. If the
field equations contain a preferred direction, either explicitly or
effectively, the allowed mode spectrum can depend not only on the plate
separation, but also on the orientation of the background structure
relative to the boundaries.

Casimir observables have therefore been studied in several
Lorentz-violating field-theory settings. For parallel plates, local and
nonperturbative Green-function approaches show that Lorentz symmetry
breaking can rescale the effective plate separation, modify the vacuum
stress, and introduce orientation-dependent corrections to the Casimir
force

Escobar et al. 2020a
;
Escobar et al. 2020b
;
Cruz et al. 2017
;
Cruz et al. 2018
;
Cruz et al. 2020
;
Bezerra de Mello and Cruz 2023
. Related analyses in cylindrical
and spherical geometries further show that the interplay between a
preferred direction and the boundary geometry can become essential

Escobar-Ruiz et al. 2021
;
Martín-Ruiz et al. 2020
.

Nonlinear electrodynamics (NLED), from the Born–Infeld theory onward,
provides a natural framework in which the electromagnetic vacuum can
behave as an effective medium

Born and Infeld 1934
;
Boillat 1970
;
Novello et al. 2000
;
Obukhov and Rubilar 2002
. In
the presence of background electromagnetic fields, small fluctuations
may propagate anisotropically, as in the classic analysis of photon
propagation in the Euler–Heisenberg vacuum

Bialynicka-Birula and Bialynicki-Birula 1970
. This leads to modified dispersion
relations, birefringence, effective optical metrics, and altered mode
densities. Related developments in nonlinear electrodynamics without
birefringence, including Born–Infeld and ModMax-type structures, also
show that nonlinear constitutive properties can strongly constrain the
propagation of electromagnetic fluctuations

Russo and Townsend 2023
.

Within this NLED context, two important reference
points are the Euler–Heisenberg effective theory, which gives the
standard nonlinear description of low-energy QED vacuum polarization

Heisenberg and Euler 1936
;
Schwinger 1951
, and ModMax electrodynamics,
a conformal and duality-invariant nonlinear extension of Maxwell theory

Bandos et al. 2020
. The Casimir effect in these nonlinear theories was
recently analyzed in Ref.
Sorge 2024
. In that work, the magnetic
background is treated as a prescribed classical configuration around
which the theory is linearized, so that the fluctuation problem is
equivalent to propagation in an anisotropic effective optical medium.
The situation considered here is different: the preferred magnetic
direction is tied to a stationary vacuum configuration of the nonlinear
Hamiltonian itself, and therefore belongs to the vacuum structure rather
than to an externally imposed background.

More precisely, the preferred direction arises from a nontrivial
magnetic vacuum of a gauge-invariant nonlinear theory formulated in the
Plebański first-order Hamiltonian representation

Plebański 1970
;
Escobar and Potting 2020
. Such vacua spontaneously
break Lorentz symmetry by selecting a particular direction.
This is conceptually related to effective field-theory descriptions of
Lorentz and CPT violation, such as the Standard-Model Extension

Colladay and Kostelecký 1997
;
Colladay and Kostelecký 1998
, where fixed
background tensors encode preferred spacetime directions. Here,
however, the preferred direction is not inserted as an explicit
Lorentz-violating coefficient in the action and is not an external
medium through which fluctuations propagate. It is part of the
dynamical vacuum structure of the nonlinear electromagnetic theory.

We work directly with a single-invariant Plebański Hamiltonian
potential
\widehat{V}(P)
, instead of starting from a conventional
Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{F})
. This is not merely a change of
variables. In the Plebański representation, the reduced effective
Hamiltonian is obtained naturally in terms of the Hamiltonian variables

\mathbf{D}
and
\mathbf{H}
, and the conditions for magnetic symmetry breaking,
local stability, and constraint degeneracy can be formulated within a
single Hamiltonian framework. In the magnetic branch, the relevant
combination is
Escobar and Potting 2020

S_{\rm m}(P)=\widehat{V}_{P}(P)+2P\widehat{V}_{PP}(P).

(1)

Stable Lorentz-breaking magnetic vacua occur at
P=P_{\star}>0
with

S_{\rm m}(P_{\star})=0.

(2)

This magnetic-branch factor plays several roles at once. It determines
the nontrivial magnetic stationarity condition, governs the degeneracy
of the reduced effective Hamiltonian constraint structure, and, as we shall show,
controls the extraordinary branch of electromagnetic fluctuations. Its
appearance in the strong-field causality criteria for Plebański-type
nonlinear electrodynamics further supports the interpretation of

S_{\rm m}=0
as a critical propagation surface

Schellstede et al. 2016
;
Russo and Townsend 2024
. We will not impose the full
set of causal inequalities in what follows; the point needed here is
that the same magnetic-branch factor marks the breakdown of the regular
propagation description.

The origin of this critical behavior is constitutive. A recent analysis
showed that, at a nontrivial magnetic stationary point, the linearized
magnetic map
\delta\mathbf{H}\mapsto\delta\mathbf{B}
loses rank in the
longitudinal direction

Escobar and Linares 2026
. This observation is important for
the Casimir problem. The extraordinary fluctuation branch in the
regular sector probes the inverse of this longitudinal magnetic
response. Therefore, the singular behavior encountered below is not an
infinite direct response of the exact vacuum. It is the singular limit
of a regular inverse-response description continued toward a point
where the underlying constitutive map changes rank.

The central question of this work is therefore not the usual one of
computing the Casimir energy in a fixed anisotropic medium. Instead, we
ask how the regular-sector Casimir spectrum behaves as a purely magnetic
background approaches a dynamically selected Lorentz-breaking vacuum
lying on a degenerate Hamiltonian surface. This requires distinguishing
two inequivalent procedures: quantizing the regular two-branch theory
and then taking the limit
S_{\rm m}\to 0
, or imposing the exact degeneracy
condition from the outset and quantizing the corresponding critical
fluctuation system.

We proceed in two stages. First, we work in the regular sector,

S_{\rm m}(\bar{P})\neq 0,

(3)

where the fluctuation spectrum contains two propagating branches. We
derive the corresponding dispersion relations and compute the
regularized parallel-plate Casimir energy for magnetic backgrounds
perpendicular and parallel to the plates. Second, we analyze the
approach to the exact Lorentz-breaking vacuum,
P_{\star}
, and show that
the regular-sector description breaks down in a controlled way. A
direct analysis on the degenerate surface reveals that the
extraordinary branch does not survive as an independent physical
propagating mode for generic momenta.

The resulting picture is qualitatively different from conventional
Casimir problems in anisotropic media. In the present setting, the
apparent Casimir divergence is not a prediction of infinite vacuum
energy. It is a diagnostic of the noncommutativity between quantizing
the regular theory and imposing the degenerate condition

S_{\rm m}(P_{\star})=0
before quantization.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
II

introduces the Plebański Hamiltonian framework and the
Lorentz-violating magnetic vacua. Section
III

derives the electromagnetic fluctuation spectrum in the regular sector.
Section
IV
formulates the parallel-plate Casimir
problem and evaluates the regular-sector vacuum energy.
Section
V
analyzes the approach to the
degenerate Lorentz-violating vacuum, illustrates the mechanism in
explicit models, and studies the exact fluctuation spectrum on the
degenerate surface. Section
VI
summarizes the main
results and their physical implications. The derivation of the
standing-wave cavity spectrum from the perfect-conductor boundary
conditions is given in Appendix
A
.

II
Plebański framework and Lorentz-violating magnetic vacua

II.1
Plebański nonlinear electrodynamics

We formulate the problem directly within the Plebański first-order
representation of nonlinear electrodynamics, where the antisymmetric
tensor
P^{\mu\nu}
and the gauge potential
A_{\mu}
are treated as
independent variables. We restrict attention to a single-invariant
sector, in which the nonlinear dynamics is encoded in a structural
Hamiltonian potential
\widehat{V}(P)
.

The source-free first-order Lagrangian density is

\widehat{\mathcal{L}}(P^{\mu\nu},A_{\mu})=-P^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\widehat{V}(P),

(4)

with

P=\frac{1}{4}P_{\mu\nu}P^{\mu\nu}.

(5)

Introducing the Hamiltonian variables

D_{i}=P^{0i},\qquad H_{i}=-\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{ijk}P^{jk},

(6)

one has

P=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbf{H}^{2}-\mathbf{D}^{2}\right).

(7)

Variation with respect to
P^{\mu\nu}
gives the inverse constitutive
relation

F^{\mu\nu}=-\widehat{V}_{P}P^{\mu\nu},

(8)

with
F^{\mu\nu}=\partial^{\mu}A^{\nu}-\partial^{\nu}A^{\mu}
. In vector form Eq. (
8
) reads

\mathbf{E}=-\widehat{V}_{P}\,\mathbf{D},\qquad\mathbf{B}=-\widehat{V}_{P}\,\mathbf{H}.

(9)

Variation with respect to
A_{\mu}
, together with the Bianchi identity
for
F_{\mu\nu}
, yields the Maxwell-like equations

\nabla\cdot\mathbf{D}=0,\qquad\nabla\times\mathbf{H}=\partial_{t}\mathbf{D},

(10)

and

\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B}=0,\qquad\nabla\times\mathbf{E}=-\partial_{t}\mathbf{B}.

(11)

Thus the theory remains gauge invariant, while the nonlinear
constitutive relations allow for nontrivial electromagnetic vacuum
structure.

After implementation of the second-class constraints, the dynamics is
governed by the reduced effective Hamiltonian density
Escobar and Potting 2020

\mathcal{H}_{\rm eff}(\mathbf{D},\mathbf{H})=-\mathbf{H}^{2}\,\widehat{V}_{P}(P)+\widehat{V}(P),

(12)

with
P
given by Eq. (
7
). For later convenience we define

h=\mathbf{H}^{2},\qquad d=\mathbf{D}^{2},\qquad P=\frac{1}{2}(h-d),

(13)

so that

\mathcal{H}_{\rm eff}(h,d)=-h\,\widehat{V}_{P}(P)+\widehat{V}(P).

(14)

The relevant point is that the vacuum problem is formulated at the
level of the reduced effective Hamiltonian. The physical vacuum configurations
are obtained by extremizing
\mathcal{H}_{\rm eff}
with respect to the
canonical variables
\mathbf{D}
and
\mathbf{H}
, not by extremizing the
structural potential
\widehat{V}(P)
alone. As a result, magnetic
stationarity, Hessian stability, and possible degeneracies of the
constraint structure are all described within a single Hamiltonian
framework. This is the main reason why the Plebański representation
is the natural framework for the present problem.

II.2
Magnetic Lorentz-violating vacua

We now identify the class of magnetic vacua relevant for the Casimir
problem. Applying the stationarity conditions to

\mathcal{H}_{\rm eff}
, one finds

\frac{\partial\mathcal{H}_{\rm eff}}{\partial D_{i}}=D_{i}\left(h\widehat{V}_{PP}-\widehat{V}_{P}\right),

(15)

and

\frac{\partial\mathcal{H}_{\rm eff}}{\partial H_{i}}=-H_{i}\left(\widehat{V}_{P}+h\widehat{V}_{PP}\right).

(16)

Besides the trivial vacuum, the theory may admit a nontrivial purely
magnetic branch characterized by

\mathbf{D}_{\star}=0,\qquad\mathbf{H}_{\star}\neq 0.

(17)

Defining

h_{\star}=\mathbf{H}_{\star}^{2},\qquad P_{\star}=\frac{h_{\star}}{2}>0,

(18)

the nontrivial magnetic stationarity condition becomes

\widehat{V}_{P}(P_{\star})+h_{\star}\widehat{V}_{PP}(P_{\star})=\widehat{V}_{P}(P_{\star})+2P_{\star}\widehat{V}_{PP}(P_{\star})=0.

(19)

It is useful to introduce the magnetic-branch function

S_{\rm m}(P)\equiv\widehat{V}_{P}(P)+2P\widehat{V}_{PP}(P).

(20)

The exact Lorentz-breaking magnetic vacuum is therefore defined by

S_{\rm m}(P_{\star})=0,\qquad P_{\star}>0.

(21)

Since
\mathbf{H}_{\star}
is a nonzero spatial vector, this vacuum selects
a preferred direction and spontaneously breaks Lorentz symmetry while
preserving gauge invariance.

The same function
S_{\rm m}(P)
will play a central role throughout the
paper. It determines the magnetic stationarity condition, controls the
degeneracy of the reduced Hamiltonian constraint structure, and appears
in the extraordinary fluctuation branch derived below.

Not every stationary magnetic solution is physically acceptable. Local
stability requires positivity of the nonzero Hessian eigenvalues of the
reduced Hamiltonian. In terms of the variables
(h,d)
, these conditions
are controlled, up to positive numerical factors, by

\mathcal{H}_{d}>0,\qquad\mathcal{H}_{hh}>0.

(22)

The first condition is governed by

\mathcal{H}_{d}=\frac{1}{2}\left(h\widehat{V}_{PP}-\widehat{V}_{P}\right).

(23)

Evaluated at the magnetic vacuum, where

h_{\star}\widehat{V}_{PP}(P_{\star})=-\widehat{V}_{P}(P_{\star})
, this gives

\mathcal{H}_{d}\big|_{\star}=-\widehat{V}_{P}(P_{\star}).

(24)

Hence local stability implies

\widehat{V}_{P}(P_{\star})<0.

(25)

This sign condition will be important when identifying the physically
admissible side from which the regular propagation sector approaches
the degenerate surface.

The second nontrivial stability condition is

\mathcal{H}_{hh}=-\frac{1}{4}\left(3\widehat{V}_{PP}+h\widehat{V}_{PPP}\right)>0.

(26)

At the magnetic vacuum this becomes

\mathcal{H}_{hh}\big|_{\star}=-\frac{1}{4}\left[3\widehat{V}_{PP}(P_{\star})+2P_{\star}\widehat{V}_{PPP}(P_{\star})\right]>0.

(27)

Together with boundedness of the reduced effective Hamiltonian, these conditions
will be imposed in the explicit stable examples discussed in
Sec.
V
.

Finally, we must distinguish the exact degenerate magnetic vacuum from
the regular magnetic backgrounds used in the fluctuation calculation.
The exact vacuum is the self-sustained stationary configuration

P_{\star}
satisfying
S_{\rm m}(P_{\star})=0
. By contrast, the regular-sector
spectrum in Secs.
III
and

IV
is computed around a constant purely magnetic
background

\bar{\mathbf{D}}=0,\qquad\bar{\mathbf{H}}=\bar{H}\,\hat{\mathbf{n}},\qquad\bar{P}=\frac{1}{2}\bar{H}^{2},

(28)

with

S_{\rm m}(\bar{P})\neq 0.

(29)

Such regular backgrounds should be understood as an ambient
regular-sector continuation used to define the limiting fluctuation
spectrum. The physical Lorentz-breaking vacuum is reached only in the
limit

\bar{P}\to P_{\star}.

(30)

Therefore, as we will show, quantizing the regular two-branch theory at

S_{\rm m}(\bar{P})\neq 0
and then taking
\bar{P}\to P_{\star}
is not
equivalent to imposing
S_{\rm m}(P_{\star})=0
before quantization.

III
Electromagnetic fluctuations in the regular sector

III.1
Linearization around a regular magnetic background

We now study small electromagnetic fluctuations around a constant
purely magnetic background in the regular sector. This background is not assumed to lie on the degenerate vacuum surface;
rather, the regular-sector analysis is performed with

S_{\rm m}(\bar{P})\neq 0
. We write

\bar{\mathbf{D}}=0,\qquad\bar{\mathbf{H}}=\bar{H}\,\hat{\mathbf{n}},\qquad\hat{\mathbf{n}}^{\,2}=1,

(31)

with

\bar{P}=\frac{1}{2}\bar{H}^{2}.

(32)

We introduce perturbations according to

\mathbf{D}=\delta\mathbf{D},\qquad\mathbf{H}=\bar{\mathbf{H}}+\delta\mathbf{H}.

(33)

The background-dependent coefficients that enter the linearized
constitutive relations are

p=\widehat{V}_{P}(\bar{P}),\qquad u=\widehat{V}_{PP}(\bar{P}),\qquad s=p+\bar{H}^{2}u=S_{\rm m}(\bar{P}).

(34)

Thus the regular sector corresponds to
s\neq 0
.

To first order, the variation of the invariant
P
is

\delta P=\bar{\mathbf{H}}\cdot\delta\mathbf{H}.

(35)

From the electric constitutive relation

\mathbf{E}=-\widehat{V}_{P}\,\mathbf{D},

(36)

one obtains

\delta\mathbf{E}=-p\,\delta\mathbf{D}.

(37)

For the magnetic induction,

\mathbf{B}=-\widehat{V}_{P}\,\mathbf{H},

(38)

the linear variation gives

\delta\mathbf{B}=-p\,\delta\mathbf{H}-u(\bar{\mathbf{H}}\cdot\delta\mathbf{H})\bar{\mathbf{H}}.

(39)

Decomposing the magnetic fluctuation into transverse and longitudinal
components with respect to
\hat{\mathbf{n}}
,

\delta\mathbf{H}=\delta\mathbf{H}_{\perp}+\delta H_{\parallel}\,\hat{\mathbf{n}},\qquad\delta H_{\parallel}=\hat{\mathbf{n}}\cdot\delta\mathbf{H},

(40)

Eq. (
39
) becomes

\delta\mathbf{B}=-p\,\delta\mathbf{H}_{\perp}-s\,\delta H_{\parallel}\,\hat{\mathbf{n}}.

(41)

Equation (
41
) is the key local structure
behind the singular limit. In the regular sector, the magnetic response
map
\delta\mathbf{H}\mapsto\delta\mathbf{B}
is invertible in both the
transverse and longitudinal channels. However, the longitudinal
eigenvalue is precisely
-s=-S_{\rm m}(\bar{P})
. Hence, as the regular
background approaches the exact magnetic vacuum,

\delta\mathbf{B}_{\parallel}=-S_{\rm m}(\bar{P})\,\delta H_{\parallel}\,\hat{\mathbf{n}}\longrightarrow 0.

(42)

The direct longitudinal response therefore does not diverge; it loses
rank. Singular factors arise only when the regular map is inverted
before taking the limit:

\delta H_{\parallel}\,\hat{\mathbf{n}}=-\frac{1}{S_{\rm m}(\bar{P})}\,\delta\mathbf{B}_{\parallel}.

(43)

Thus the factors of
1/S_{\rm m}
that appear below should be understood as
inverse-response singularities associated with a rank-changing
constitutive map. This is the same mechanism identified in the
constitutive analysis of Hamiltonian degeneracy in
Ref.
Escobar and Linares 2026
.

The linearized field equations are

\nabla\cdot\delta\mathbf{D}=0,\qquad\nabla\times\delta\mathbf{H}=\partial_{t}\delta\mathbf{D},

(44)

and

\nabla\cdot\delta\mathbf{B}=0,\qquad\nabla\times\delta\mathbf{E}=-\partial_{t}\delta\mathbf{B}.

(45)

They define an anisotropic effective propagation problem controlled by
the regular-sector coefficients
p
and
s
. The extraordinary
fluctuation branch will become singular precisely when the longitudinal
response coefficient
s
approaches zero.

III.2
Plane-wave spectrum

We now determine the bulk fluctuation spectrum in the regular sector.
Consider plane-wave perturbations

\delta X(\mathbf{x},t)=\delta X_{0}\,e^{i(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x}-\omega t)}.

(46)

The linearized Maxwell equations imply

\mathbf{k}\cdot\delta\mathbf{D}=0,\qquad\mathbf{k}\times\delta\mathbf{H}=-\omega\,\delta\mathbf{D},

(47)

and

\mathbf{k}\cdot\delta\mathbf{B}=0,\qquad\mathbf{k}\times\delta\mathbf{E}=\omega\,\delta\mathbf{B}.

(48)

Because the background preserves axial symmetry around

\hat{\mathbf{n}}
, we choose coordinates such that

\hat{\mathbf{n}}=\hat{\mathbf{z}},\qquad\mathbf{k}=(\kappa,0,q),

(49)

where

q\equiv k_{\parallel},\qquad\kappa\equiv k_{\perp}.

(50)

Writing

\delta\mathbf{H}=(H_{x},H_{y},H_{z}),

(51)

one obtains the algebraic system

p(\omega^{2}-q^{2})H_{x}+pq\kappa H_{z}=0,

(52)

p(\omega^{2}-q^{2}-\kappa^{2})H_{y}=0,

(53)

and

p\kappa qH_{x}+(s\omega^{2}-p\kappa^{2})H_{z}=0.

(54)

The decoupled equation for
H_{y}
gives the ordinary branch

\omega_{1}^{2}=q^{2}+\kappa^{2}.

(55)

This mode has the standard Maxwell dispersion relation. Its frequency
is independent of the nonlinear anisotropic coefficient
s
, although
its normalization is still determined by the underlying Hamiltonian
theory.

The coupled
(H_{x},H_{z})
sector is described by

\begin{pmatrix}p(\omega^{2}-q^{2})&pq\kappa\\
p\kappa q&s\omega^{2}-p\kappa^{2}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}H_{x}\\
H_{z}\end{pmatrix}=0.

(56)

Its determinant contains a static constrained solution and a nonstatic
propagating branch. The latter is

\omega_{2}^{2}=q^{2}+\frac{p}{s}\,\kappa^{2}.

(57)

Introducing the regular-sector anisotropy parameter

\alpha(\bar{P})=\frac{p}{s}=\frac{\widehat{V}_{P}(\bar{P})}{\widehat{V}_{P}(\bar{P})+2\bar{P}\widehat{V}_{PP}(\bar{P})}=\frac{\widehat{V}_{P}(\bar{P})}{S_{\rm m}(\bar{P})},

(58)

the two propagating branches can be written as

\omega_{1}^{2}=k_{\parallel}^{2}+k_{\perp}^{2},\qquad\omega_{2}^{2}=k_{\parallel}^{2}+\alpha\,k_{\perp}^{2}.

(59)

The regular optical regime requires real frequencies for arbitrary
transverse momenta, and therefore

\alpha>0.

(60)

For the stable magnetic vacua considered in this work,

\widehat{V}_{P}(P_{\star})<0
. Hence, sufficiently close to the degenerate
surface,
p=\widehat{V}_{P}(\bar{P})
is also negative. The condition

\alpha=p/s>0
then selects the physically admissible side of the
regular sector,

s=S_{\rm m}(\bar{P})<0.

(61)

Along this approach,

\alpha(\bar{P})\to+\infty\qquad\text{as}\qquad\bar{P}\to P_{\star}.

(62)

Thus the ordinary branch remains Maxwell-like, while the extraordinary
branch directly probes the inverse longitudinal magnetic response. Its
singular behavior as
S_{\rm m}(\bar{P})\to 0
is the first spectral signal
that the regular two-branch description cannot be continued naively to
the exact degenerate vacuum.

III.3
Polarization structure

The two branches also have distinct polarization structure. The ordinary
branch is obtained from the decoupled sector,

H_{x}=H_{z}=0,\qquad H_{y}\neq 0.

(63)

It therefore corresponds to a Maxwell-like polarization transverse to
both the propagation plane and the magnetic background direction.

The extraordinary branch lies in the
(H_{x},H_{z})
sector. For

\kappa\neq 0
, Eqs. (
52
)–(
54
) give

H_{x}=-\frac{sq}{p\kappa}\,H_{z}.

(64)

This is the second propagating polarization of the regular theory. Its
existence relies on the invertibility of the longitudinal magnetic
response, and its dispersion depends explicitly on the ratio

p/s=\alpha
.

The collinear sector
\kappa=0
is exceptional because the distinction
between transverse and longitudinal propagation relative to

\hat{\mathbf{n}}
degenerates. In the regular bulk theory this
lower-dimensional sector can be treated separately, but it does not
affect the Casimir mode density considered below. The same exceptional
sector will reappear when the exact degenerate surface is analyzed in
Sec.
V
.

IV
Casimir effect in the regular propagation regime

IV.1
Parallel-plate geometry

We now formulate the parallel-plate Casimir problem for the
regular-sector fluctuation spectrum derived in
Sec.
III
. The conducting plates are located
at

z=0,\qquad z=a.

(65)

Throughout this section, the calculation is performed around a regular
purely magnetic background
\bar{P}
satisfying

S_{\rm m}(\bar{P})\neq 0.

(66)

Thus, the mode sums below should not be interpreted as the Casimir
energy evaluated directly at the exact degenerate vacuum
P_{\star}
.
The exact vacuum problem is addressed separately in
Sec.
V
, where the condition

S_{\rm m}(P_{\star})=0
is imposed before quantization.

For a perfect conductor, the boundary conditions are imposed on the
physical electromagnetic fields,

\hat{\mathbf{z}}\times\delta\mathbf{E}=0,\qquad\hat{\mathbf{z}}\cdot\delta\mathbf{B}=0,\qquad z=0,a.

(67)

Since the fundamental variables in the Plebański formulation are

\delta\mathbf{D}
and
\delta\mathbf{H}
, the translation of
Eq. (
67
) into cavity mode conditions is not
automatic. In Appendix
A
we verify that, for the
two orientations considered below, the normal momentum is quantized as

k_{z}=\frac{n\pi}{a},\qquad n=1,2,\ldots,

(68)

while the momentum components parallel to the plates remain continuous.
The
n=0
sector does not contribute to the separation-dependent part
of the regularized vacuum energy relevant for the force and is omitted
from the finite Casimir contribution.

The extraordinary dispersion relation,

\omega_{2}^{2}=k_{\parallel}^{2}+\alpha k_{\perp}^{2},

(69)

is defined relative to the preferred direction selected by the regular
magnetic background, not relative to the cavity normal. Therefore the
Casimir spectrum depends on the orientation of
\bar{\mathbf{H}}
with
respect to the conducting plates.

In this section we write

\alpha\equiv\alpha(\bar{P})=\frac{\widehat{V}_{P}(\bar{P})}{S_{\rm m}(\bar{P})},\qquad\alpha>0,

(70)

where the positivity condition ensures real regular-sector frequencies.
The background is kept fixed when the plate separation is varied, so

\alpha
is treated as independent of
a
in the computation of the
force.

The formal zero-point energy per unit area is

\frac{E_{0}}{A}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r=1}^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int\frac{\differential^{2}k}{(2\pi)^{2}}\,\omega_{r,n},

(71)

where
r=1,2
labels the ordinary and extraordinary propagating
branches in the regular sector. This expression is ultraviolet
divergent. We use zeta-function regularization and retain only the
finite geometry-dependent part after subtracting contributions
independent of the plate separation.

For reference, a single Maxwell-like polarization contributes

\frac{E_{\rm one}}{A}=-\frac{\pi^{2}}{1440a^{3}},

(72)

so that the standard Maxwell result is

\frac{E_{\rm Maxwell}}{A}=-\frac{\pi^{2}}{720a^{3}}.

(73)

IV.2
Regular magnetic background perpendicular to the plates

We first consider a regular magnetic background oriented normal to the
cavity,

\bar{\mathbf{H}}=\bar{H}\,\hat{\mathbf{z}}.

(74)

In this configuration, the preferred direction coincides with the
normal to the plates, and therefore

k_{\parallel}=k_{z}=\frac{n\pi}{a},\qquad k_{\perp}^{2}=k_{x}^{2}+k_{y}^{2}.

(75)

The ordinary and extraordinary frequencies are

\omega_{1,n}^{\perp}=\sqrt{k_{x}^{2}+k_{y}^{2}+\left(\frac{n\pi}{a}\right)^{2}},

(76)

and

\omega_{2,n}^{\perp}=\sqrt{\left(\frac{n\pi}{a}\right)^{2}+\alpha(k_{x}^{2}+k_{y}^{2})}.

(77)

Thus, in the perpendicular geometry, the anisotropy rescales only the
continuous momentum components parallel to the plates.

The ordinary branch contributes the one-polarization Maxwell result,

\frac{E_{1}^{\perp}}{A}=-\frac{\pi^{2}}{1440a^{3}}.

(78)

For the extraordinary branch, define

q_{x}=\sqrt{\alpha}\,k_{x},\qquad q_{y}=\sqrt{\alpha}\,k_{y}.

(79)

The frequency becomes isotropic in the variables
(q_{x},q_{y})
, while the
measure transforms as

\differential k_{x}\,\differential k_{y}=\frac{1}{\alpha}\,\differential q_{x}\,\differential q_{y}.

(80)

Hence the finite contribution of the extraordinary branch is

\frac{E_{2}^{\perp}}{A}=-\frac{\pi^{2}}{1440a^{3}}\frac{1}{\alpha}.

(81)

The total regular-sector Casimir energy is therefore

\frac{E_{\rm Cas}^{\perp}}{A}=-\frac{\pi^{2}}{1440a^{3}}\left(1+\frac{1}{\alpha}\right).

(82)

The corresponding force per unit area is

\frac{F_{\rm Cas}^{\perp}}{A}=-\frac{\partial}{\partial a}\left(\frac{E_{\rm Cas}^{\perp}}{A}\right)=-\frac{\pi^{2}}{480a^{4}}\left(1+\frac{1}{\alpha}\right).

(83)

In this orientation, the anisotropy modifies only the continuum
momentum measure. Consequently, the perpendicular regular-sector energy
remains finite in the critical limit
\alpha\to+\infty
.

IV.3
Regular magnetic background parallel to the plates

We now consider the complementary configuration in which the regular
magnetic background lies parallel to the conducting plates. Without loss
of generality, we choose

\bar{\mathbf{H}}=\bar{H}\,\hat{\mathbf{x}}.

(84)

Then

k_{\parallel}=k_{x},\qquad k_{\perp}^{2}=k_{y}^{2}+\left(\frac{n\pi}{a}\right)^{2}.

(85)

The corresponding frequencies are

\omega_{1,n}^{\parallel}=\sqrt{k_{x}^{2}+k_{y}^{2}+\left(\frac{n\pi}{a}\right)^{2}},

(86)

and

\omega_{2,n}^{\parallel}=\sqrt{k_{x}^{2}+\alpha\left[k_{y}^{2}+\left(\frac{n\pi}{a}\right)^{2}\right]}.

(87)

Unlike the perpendicular case, the anisotropy now acts directly on the
discretized cavity momentum.

The ordinary branch again gives

\frac{E_{1}^{\parallel}}{A}=-\frac{\pi^{2}}{1440a^{3}}.

(88)

For the extraordinary branch, introduce

q_{y}=\sqrt{\alpha}\,k_{y},\qquad a_{\rm eff}=\frac{a}{\sqrt{\alpha}}.

(89)

The frequency then takes the standard isotropic form with effective
plate separation
a_{\rm eff}
, while the momentum measure contributes
a Jacobian factor
1/\sqrt{\alpha}
. Therefore

\frac{E_{2}^{\parallel}}{A}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\left[-\frac{\pi^{2}}{1440a_{\rm eff}^{3}}\right]=-\frac{\pi^{2}}{1440a^{3}}\,\alpha.

(90)

The full regular-sector Casimir energy is

\frac{E_{\rm Cas}^{\parallel}}{A}=-\frac{\pi^{2}}{1440a^{3}}\left(1+\alpha\right),

(91)

with corresponding force

\frac{F_{\rm Cas}^{\parallel}}{A}=-\frac{\partial}{\partial a}\left(\frac{E_{\rm Cas}^{\parallel}}{A}\right)=-\frac{\pi^{2}}{480a^{4}}\left(1+\alpha\right).

(92)

The difference between Eqs. (
82
) and
(
91
) is geometric. When the magnetic background
is perpendicular to the plates, the anisotropy rescales only the
continuous momentum measure. When it is parallel to the plates, it also
rescales the discrete cavity spacing. This is why the parallel
configuration develops the divergent regular-sector contribution in the
critical limit
\alpha(\bar{P})\to+\infty
, analyzed in the next
section.

V
Critical approach to the degenerate Lorentz-violating vacuum

V.1
Breakdown of the regular-sector limit

The Casimir energies obtained in Sec.
IV
were
derived entirely within the regular propagation sector, where

S_{\rm m}(\bar{P})\neq 0,

(93)

and the fluctuation spectrum contains two propagating branches. We now
examine what happens when the regular magnetic background approaches
the exact Lorentz-breaking magnetic vacuum,

\bar{P}\to P_{\star},\qquad S_{\rm m}(P_{\star})=0.

(94)

Using the regular-sector Casimir energies obtained in
Eqs. (
82
) and (
91
), this
limit gives

\frac{E_{\rm Cas}^{\perp}}{A}=-\frac{\pi^{2}}{1440a^{3}}\left(1+\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)\longrightarrow-\frac{\pi^{2}}{1440a^{3}},

(95)

whereas

\frac{E_{\rm Cas}^{\parallel}}{A}=-\frac{\pi^{2}}{1440a^{3}}(1+\alpha)\longrightarrow-\infty.

(96)

The divergence in the parallel configuration is a signal that
the regular two-branch optical description cannot be evaluated directly
at the exact degenerate vacuum.

This conclusion is also evident from the extraordinary dispersion
relation,

\omega_{2}^{2}=k_{\parallel}^{2}+\alpha k_{\perp}^{2}.

(97)

As
\alpha\to+\infty
, any regular-sector extraordinary fluctuation
with nonzero transverse momentum is pushed to arbitrarily large
frequency. The apparent divergent Casimir contribution is therefore not
a prediction of an infinite physical force at the exact vacuum. It is a
signal that the mode being summed over ceases to be a finite-energy
propagating degree of freedom in the critical theory.

Equivalently, the divergence is the Casimir manifestation of the
inverse-response singularity discussed in
Sec.
III.1
. The direct longitudinal
magnetic response does not diverge at
P_{\star}
; it vanishes. What
diverges is the regular inverse map used to construct the extraordinary
propagating branch before the degenerate limit is taken. Thus, taking

\bar{P}\to P_{\star}
after quantizing the regular sector is a singular
limiting procedure.

This raises the key question addressed below: what is the
fluctuation spectrum if the degenerate vacuum condition

S_{\rm m}(P_{\star})=0
is imposed before quantization?

V.2
Rational asymmetric model

We now illustrate the mechanism in explicit nonlinear Hamiltonian
potentials that admit stable Lorentz-breaking magnetic vacua. The
following examples were shown in Ref.
Plácido-Flores et al. 2026

to possess parameter regions in which boundedness of the reduced
Hamiltonian, magnetic stationarity, and Hessian stability coexist.

The first example is the rational asymmetric model

\widehat{V}(P)=-P+\frac{\lambda P^{3}}{1+\eta P^{2}},

(98)

where
\lambda
and
\eta
are real parameters. Its first two
derivatives are

\widehat{V}_{P}=-1+\lambda P^{2}\frac{3+\eta P^{2}}{(1+\eta P^{2})^{2}},

(99)

and

\widehat{V}_{PP}=-\frac{2\lambda P(\eta P^{2}-3)}{(1+\eta P^{2})^{3}}.

(100)

Therefore, the magnetic-branch function

S_{\rm m}(P)=\widehat{V}_{P}(P)+2P\widehat{V}_{PP}(P)

(101)

is

S_{\rm m}(P)=-\frac{1+(3\eta-15\lambda)P^{2}+3\eta^{2}P^{4}+\eta^{2}(\eta-\lambda)P^{6}}{(1+\eta P^{2})^{3}}.

(102)

The magnetic Lorentz-breaking vacuum is determined by

S_{\rm m}(P_{\star}^{\rm rat})=0,\qquad P_{\star}^{\rm rat}=\frac{h_{\star}^{\rm rat}}{2}>0.

(103)

Equivalently, in terms of
h_{\star}^{\rm rat}
, the stationarity
condition becomes

\displaystyle 0={}

\displaystyle(\eta^{3}-\eta^{2}\lambda)(h_{\star}^{\rm rat})^{6}+12\eta^{2}(h_{\star}^{\rm rat})^{4}

(104)

\displaystyle+(48\eta-240\lambda)(h_{\star}^{\rm rat})^{2}+64.

Thus, the admissible magnetic vacuum corresponds to the positive real
root of Eq. (
104
) lying in the stable parameter
domain.

The reduced Hamiltonian is bounded from below for

\eta>0,\qquad\eta\geq\frac{9}{8}\lambda,

(105)

while coexistence of boundedness, magnetic spontaneous Lorentz
breaking, and Hessian stability requires

\frac{9}{8}\lambda\leq\eta<\left(\frac{25}{16}+\frac{5\sqrt{5}}{16}\right)\lambda.

(106)

In this region the stationary point is not an unstable toy solution; it
is a locally stable magnetic vacuum of the reduced Hamiltonian system.

The regular-sector anisotropy parameter is

\alpha_{\rm rat}(P)=\frac{\widehat{V}_{P}(P)}{S_{\rm m}(P)}.

(107)

Using Eqs. (
99
) and (
102
), one obtains

\alpha_{\rm rat}(P)=\frac{\left[1+(2\eta-3\lambda)P^{2}+\eta(\eta-\lambda)P^{4}\right](1+\eta P^{2})}{1+(3\eta-15\lambda)P^{2}+3\eta^{2}P^{4}+\eta^{2}(\eta-\lambda)P^{6}}.

(108)

The denominator is precisely the numerator of the degeneracy function
in Eq. (
102
). Hence, as the regular background approaches
the stable magnetic vacuum from the admissible real-frequency side,

P\to P_{\star}^{\rm rat},\qquad S_{\rm m}(P)\to 0,\qquad\alpha_{\rm rat}(P)\to+\infty.

(109)

The extraordinary branch therefore becomes singular at the same surface
where the Hamiltonian constraint structure degenerates. This provides a
concrete stable realization of the critical Casimir behavior described
above.

V.3
Logarithmic model

As a second example, consider the logarithmic Hamiltonian potential

\widehat{V}(P)=-P-\frac{\lambda}{\eta}\ln(1+\eta P),

(110)

with physical domain

1+\eta P>0.

(111)

The derivatives are

\widehat{V}_{P}=-1-\frac{\lambda}{1+\eta P}=-\frac{1+\eta P+\lambda}{1+\eta P},

(112)

and

\widehat{V}_{PP}=\frac{\lambda\eta}{(1+\eta P)^{2}}.

(113)

Therefore,

S_{\rm m}(P)=\widehat{V}_{P}+2P\widehat{V}_{PP}=-\frac{(\eta P)^{2}-(\lambda-2)\eta P+(\lambda+1)}{(1+\eta P)^{2}}.

(114)

The magnetic vacuum is determined by

S_{\rm m}(P_{\star}^{\log})=0,

(115)

or

(\eta P_{\star}^{\log})^{2}-(\lambda-2)\eta P_{\star}^{\log}+(\lambda+1)=0.

(116)

The two formal roots are

\eta P_{\pm}=\frac{\lambda-2\pm\sqrt{\lambda(\lambda-8)}}{2}.

(117)

The branch compatible with local stability is the larger positive root,

\displaystyle P_{\star}^{\log}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda-2+\sqrt{\lambda(\lambda-8)}}{2\eta},

(118)

\displaystyle h_{\star}^{\log}

\displaystyle=2P_{\star}^{\log}=\frac{\lambda-2+\sqrt{\lambda(\lambda-8)}}{\eta}.

The reduced Hamiltonian is bounded from below for

\lambda\geq 0,\qquad\eta>0.

(119)

The stronger conditions for boundedness, nontrivial magnetic
stationarity, and Hessian stability select the region

\lambda>8,\qquad\eta>0.

(120)

In this domain, Eq. (
118
) gives a real positive magnetic
vacuum and the logarithmic argument remains in the physical domain.

The corresponding anisotropy parameter is

\alpha_{\log}(P)=\frac{\widehat{V}_{P}(P)}{S_{\rm m}(P)}=\frac{(1+\eta P+\lambda)(1+\eta P)}{(\eta P)^{2}-(\lambda-2)\eta P+(\lambda+1)}.

(121)

The denominator vanishes at the magnetic degeneracy surface. Therefore,
on the admissible real-frequency side of the regular sector,

P\to P_{\star}^{\log},\qquad S_{\rm m}(P)\to 0,\qquad\alpha_{\log}(P)\to+\infty.

(122)

The logarithmic model thus gives a second stable realization of the same
critical mechanism: the extraordinary regular-sector mode becomes
singular precisely at the magnetic Lorentz-breaking vacuum.

The rational and logarithmic examples show that the singular behavior
of the regular-sector Casimir spectrum is not an artifact of an
unstable model. It occurs in theories for which the reduced Hamiltonian
is bounded from below and the magnetic branch satisfies the required
local stability conditions. The divergence of
\alpha
is therefore a
structural consequence of approaching the Hamiltonian-degenerate surface

S_{\rm m}=0
, rather than a pathology of a particular choice of
\widehat{V}(P)
.

V.4
Exact fluctuation spectrum on the degenerate surface

We now impose the magnetic degeneracy condition from the outset and
analyze the fluctuation spectrum directly at the exact
Lorentz-breaking vacuum. At the critical surface,

S_{\rm m}(P_{\star})=\widehat{V}_{P}(P_{\star})+2P_{\star}\widehat{V}_{PP}(P_{\star})=0.

(123)

The electric response remains

\delta\mathbf{E}=-p_{\star}\,\delta\mathbf{D},\qquad p_{\star}=\widehat{V}_{P}(P_{\star})<0,

(124)

while the magnetic response reduces to

\delta\mathbf{B}=-p_{\star}\,\delta\mathbf{H}_{\perp}.

(125)

The longitudinal magnetic response is absent. This is the crucial
difference between the regular and exact degenerate theories: the
longitudinal channel that is invertible for
S_{\rm m}(\bar{P})\neq 0
becomes
a zero-response direction at
S_{\rm m}(P_{\star})=0
.

Choose the magnetic vacuum direction as

\hat{\mathbf{n}}=\hat{\mathbf{z}},\qquad\mathbf{k}=(\kappa,0,q),

(126)

where
q=k_{\parallel}
and
\kappa=k_{\perp}
. Writing

\delta\mathbf{H}=(H_{x},H_{y},H_{z}),

(127)

the ordinary sector remains

p_{\star}(\omega^{2}-q^{2}-\kappa^{2})H_{y}=0.

(128)

Since
p_{\star}\neq 0
, this gives the Maxwell-like dispersion relation

\omega^{2}=q^{2}+\kappa^{2}.

(129)

The important point is that the degenerate theory does not contain the
same two propagating oscillators as the regular theory. In the regular
sector, the decoupled field
H_{y}
gives the ordinary branch, while the
coupled
(H_{x},H_{z})
sector gives the extraordinary branch. On the
degenerate surface, however, the longitudinal magnetic response has
lost rank. Therefore the coupled sector must be reanalyzed after
imposing
S_{\rm m}(P_{\star})=0
, rather than obtained by assigning a limiting
frequency to the regular extraordinary mode.

The would-be extraordinary sector is described by the degenerate
coupled block

\begin{pmatrix}p_{\star}(\omega^{2}-q^{2})&p_{\star}q\kappa\\
p_{\star}\kappa q&-p_{\star}\kappa^{2}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}H_{x}\\
H_{z}\end{pmatrix}=0.

(130)

Its determinant is

\det=-p_{\star}^{\,2}\kappa^{2}\omega^{2}.

(131)

Thus, for generic momenta with
\kappa\neq 0
, a nontrivial solution of
this block requires
\omega=0
. The coupled
(H_{x},H_{z})
sector therefore
does not supply a nonstatic propagating branch on the degenerate
surface. It is either static or constrained, and hence it does not
define an independent harmonic oscillator contributing a zero-point
energy
\frac{1}{2}\omega
to the Casimir sum.

By contrast, the
H_{y}
sector remains nondegenerate and gives the
ordinary Maxwell-like dispersion relation in
Eq. (
129
). Consequently, after imposing

S_{\rm m}(P_{\star})=0
from the outset, the generic propagating spectrum
contains only the ordinary branch. The extraordinary branch that exists
for
S_{\rm m}(\bar{P})\neq 0
is removed by the rank change of the magnetic
constitutive map.

There is a special collinear sector with
\kappa=0
, where the above
rank argument becomes degenerate. Such modes form a lower-dimensional
subset of momentum space and do not reconstruct the full extraordinary
branch. In particular, they do not affect the Casimir mode density in
the parallel-plate configurations considered here.

The comparison with the regular spectrum is therefore sharp. For every
fixed regular background with
S_{\rm m}(\bar{P})\neq 0
, however close it is
to
P_{\star}
, the theory still contains two propagating branches,

\omega_{1}^{2}=k_{\parallel}^{2}+k_{\perp}^{2},\qquad\omega_{2}^{2}=k_{\parallel}^{2}+\alpha k_{\perp}^{2},

(132)

with

\alpha(\bar{P})=\frac{\widehat{V}_{P}(\bar{P})}{S_{\rm m}(\bar{P})}.

(133)

Hence the regular-sector zero-point energy is obtained by summing over
both frequencies. As
\bar{P}\to P_{\star}
from the admissible side,

\alpha(\bar{P})\to+\infty
, and the regular-sector parallel Casimir
energy can become parametrically large.

This large response is a property of the regular two-branch theory near
the degenerate surface. It is not the Casimir energy of the exact
degenerate vacuum. If
S_{\rm m}(P_{\star})=0
is imposed first, the coupled
sector that generated
\omega_{2}
no longer provides an independent
propagating oscillator for generic momenta. Thus the exact degenerate
spectrum and the limiting regular spectrum are physically inequivalent.

This makes explicit the noncommutativity of the problem. Denoting
by
\mathcal{Q}_{\rm reg}
the quantization of the regular two-branch
theory and by
\mathcal{Q}_{\rm deg}
the quantization performed after
imposing the degenerate condition from the outset, the result can be
summarized as

\lim_{\bar{P}\to P_{\star}}\mathcal{Q}_{\rm reg}\!\left[S_{\rm m}(\bar{P})\neq 0\right]\neq\mathcal{Q}_{\rm deg}\!\left[S_{\rm m}(P_{\star})=0\right].

(134)

The divergent regular-sector Casimir energy is therefore not the
physical vacuum energy of the exact Lorentz-breaking state. It is a
diagnostic of the change in the number of propagating degrees of
freedom at a Hamiltonian-degenerate surface.

VI
Conclusions

In this work we have studied the Casimir response of electromagnetic
fluctuations near magnetic vacua that spontaneously break Lorentz
symmetry in gauge-invariant nonlinear electrodynamics. The analysis was
carried out in the Plebański first-order Hamiltonian formulation, where
the nonlinear theory is defined by a single-invariant structural
potential
\widehat{V}(P)
and the vacuum problem is naturally formulated in
terms of the reduced effective Hamiltonian.

The central object controlling the analysis is the magnetic-branch
function

S_{\rm m}(P)=\widehat{V}_{P}(P)+2P\widehat{V}_{PP}(P).

(135)

Its vanishing at
P=P_{\star}
defines the exact Lorentz-breaking
magnetic vacuum and the associated Hamiltonian-degenerate surface.
The same quantity also controls the longitudinal magnetic response and
the extraordinary fluctuation branch in the regular sector. This
identification is the main reason why the Casimir problem considered
here is not simply the standard Casimir effect in an externally imposed
anisotropic medium.

We first quantized the electromagnetic fluctuations around a regular
purely magnetic background
\bar{P}
, with
S_{\rm m}(\bar{P})\neq 0
. In this
sector the spectrum contains two propagating branches: an ordinary
Maxwell-like polarization,

\omega_{1}^{2}=k_{\parallel}^{2}+k_{\perp}^{2},

(136)

and an extraordinary anisotropic polarization,

\omega_{2}^{2}=k_{\parallel}^{2}+\alpha k_{\perp}^{2},\qquad\alpha(\bar{P})=\frac{\widehat{V}_{P}(\bar{P})}{S_{\rm m}(\bar{P})}.

(137)

For stable magnetic vacua,
\widehat{V}_{P}(P_{\star})<0
, and the physically
admissible real-frequency approach to the degenerate surface corresponds
to
\alpha(\bar{P})\to+\infty
.

The regular-sector Casimir energy depends strongly on the orientation
of the magnetic background relative to the plates. When the regular
magnetic background is perpendicular to the plates, the anisotropy
rescales only the continuous momentum measure, giving

\frac{E_{\rm Cas}^{\perp}}{A}=-\frac{\pi^{2}}{1440a^{3}}\left(1+\frac{1}{\alpha}\right).

(138)

When the regular magnetic background is parallel to the plates, the
anisotropy also rescales the discrete cavity momentum, yielding

\frac{E_{\rm Cas}^{\parallel}}{A}=-\frac{\pi^{2}}{1440a^{3}}(1+\alpha).

(139)

Thus the parallel configuration develops a divergent regular-sector
contribution as
\alpha\to+\infty
, whereas the perpendicular
configuration remains finite.

This divergence should not be interpreted as an infinite physical
Casimir force of the exact Lorentz-breaking vacuum. Its origin is the
singular inverse-response limit of the regular theory. At the degenerate
surface the direct longitudinal magnetic response does not diverge; it
vanishes. The divergence appears only because the regular-sector
extraordinary branch is constructed by inverting a longitudinal
constitutive channel whose rank changes at
S_{\rm m}(P_{\star})=0
.

We verified this interpretation by analyzing the fluctuation problem
directly on the degenerate surface. For generic momenta, the
extraordinary branch of the regular theory does not survive as an
independent physical propagating mode. The exact degenerate vacuum
supports only the ordinary Maxwell-like propagating branch, up to
possible lower-dimensional collinear sectors that do not affect the
Casimir mode density considered here.

An important consequence of this analysis is the noncommutativity
summarized in Eq. (
134
): quantizing the regular
two-branch theory and then taking
\bar{P}\to P_{\star}
is not equivalent
to imposing the degenerate condition before quantization.

The apparent Casimir divergence is a diagnostic of this
noncommutativity. It signals a change in the number of physical
propagating degrees of freedom at a Hamiltonian-degenerate surface,
rather than a conventional vacuum-energy correction evaluated at the
exact Lorentz-breaking state.

From this perspective, the Casimir effect becomes a probe of the
constraint and constitutive structure of nonlinear gauge theories with
spontaneous Lorentz symmetry breaking. The result suggests that quantum
vacuum observables can be sensitive not only to modified dispersion
relations, but also to singular surfaces where the regular optical
description and the exact Hamiltonian theory cease to be equivalent.

Appendix A
Cavity eigenmodes in the regular anisotropic sector

In this appendix we justify the standing-wave quantization used in the
parallel-plate calculation. The point is slightly more delicate than in
ordinary Maxwell theory because the perfect-conductor boundary
conditions are imposed on the physical fields
\delta\mathbf{E}
and

\delta\mathbf{B}
, whereas the Plebański formulation uses

\delta\mathbf{D}
and
\delta\mathbf{H}
as Hamiltonian variables.

We work throughout this appendix in the regular propagation sector,

p\equiv\widehat{V}_{P}(\bar{P})\neq 0,\qquad s\equiv S_{\rm m}(\bar{P})\neq 0.

(140)

The linearized constitutive relations are

\delta\mathbf{E}=-p\,\delta\mathbf{D},

(141)

and

\delta\mathbf{B}=-p\,\delta\mathbf{H}_{\perp}-s\,\delta H_{\parallel}\,\hat{\mathbf{n}},

(142)

where the perpendicular and longitudinal components are defined with
respect to the regular magnetic background direction

\hat{\mathbf{n}}
.

The plates are located at
z=0
and
z=a
, with normal

\hat{\mathbf{z}}
. The perfect-conductor boundary conditions are

\hat{\mathbf{z}}\times\delta\mathbf{E}=0,\qquad\hat{\mathbf{z}}\cdot\delta\mathbf{B}=0,\qquad z=0,a.

(143)

Using Eq. (
141
), the tangential electric
condition is equivalent, for
p\neq 0
, to

\delta D_{x}=0,\qquad\delta D_{y}=0,\qquad z=0,a.

(144)

Let the magnetic fluctuation have harmonic time dependence and be
translationally invariant along the plates,

\delta\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{x},t)=\mathbf{h}(z)\,e^{i(k_{x}x+k_{y}y-\omega t)}.

(145)

The linearized Ampère equation,

\nabla\times\delta\mathbf{H}=\partial_{t}\delta\mathbf{D},

(146)

then gives, up to an irrelevant common factor,

\delta D_{x}\propto ik_{y}h_{z}-\frac{dh_{y}}{dz},\qquad\delta D_{y}\propto\frac{dh_{x}}{dz}-ik_{x}h_{z}.

(147)

Therefore Eq. (
144
) requires

ik_{y}h_{z}-\frac{dh_{y}}{dz}=0,\qquad\frac{dh_{x}}{dz}-ik_{x}h_{z}=0,\qquad z=0,a.

(148)

We now specialize to the two orientations used in the main text.

A.1
Regular magnetic background perpendicular to the plates

For a regular magnetic background normal to the plates,

\hat{\mathbf{n}}=\hat{\mathbf{z}},

(149)

the normal magnetic boundary condition follows from

\delta B_{z}=-s\,h_{z}.

(150)

Since
s\neq 0
in the regular sector,
\delta B_{z}=0
implies

h_{z}=0,\qquad z=0,a.

(151)

Substituting this into Eq. (
148
) gives

\frac{dh_{x}}{dz}=0,\qquad\frac{dh_{y}}{dz}=0,\qquad h_{z}=0,\qquad z=0,a.

(152)

The compatible standing-wave profiles may therefore be chosen as

h_{x}(z),h_{y}(z)\propto\cos(k_zz),\qquad h_{z}(z)\propto\sin(k_zz),

(153)

with

k_{z}=\frac{n\pi}{a},\qquad n=0,1,2,\ldots.

(154)

The
n=0
sector does not contribute to the separation-dependent part
of the regularized vacuum energy relevant for the force. It is
therefore omitted from the finite Casimir contribution, and one may
take
n=1,2,\ldots
in the mode sums.

For this orientation,

k_{\parallel}=k_{z},\qquad k_{\perp}^{2}=k_{x}^{2}+k_{y}^{2}.

(155)

Substitution into the two regular bulk branches gives

\omega_{1,n}^{\perp}=\sqrt{k_{x}^{2}+k_{y}^{2}+\left(\frac{n\pi}{a}\right)^{2}},

(156)

and

\omega_{2,n}^{\perp}=\sqrt{\left(\frac{n\pi}{a}\right)^{2}+\alpha(k_{x}^{2}+k_{y}^{2})}.

(157)

These are the frequencies used in the perpendicular configuration.

A.2
Regular magnetic background parallel to the plates

For a regular magnetic background parallel to the plates, chosen along
the
x
axis,

\hat{\mathbf{n}}=\hat{\mathbf{x}},

(158)

the normal component of the magnetic induction is

\delta B_{z}=-p\,h_{z}.

(159)

Since
p\neq 0
, the condition
\delta B_{z}=0
again implies

h_{z}=0,\qquad z=0,a.

(160)

The tangential electric boundary conditions then reduce once more to

\frac{dh_{x}}{dz}=0,\qquad\frac{dh_{y}}{dz}=0,\qquad h_{z}=0,\qquad z=0,a.

(161)

Thus the allowed normal momenta are again

k_{z}=\frac{n\pi}{a},\qquad n=0,1,2,\ldots.

(162)

As in the perpendicular configuration, the
n=0
contribution does not
affect the separation-dependent part of the regularized vacuum energy
and is excluded from the finite Casimir force.

For this orientation,

k_{\parallel}=k_{x},\qquad k_{\perp}^{2}=k_{y}^{2}+k_{z}^{2}.

(163)

Therefore the two regular-sector frequencies become

\omega_{1,n}^{\parallel}=\sqrt{k_{x}^{2}+k_{y}^{2}+\left(\frac{n\pi}{a}\right)^{2}},

(164)

and

\omega_{2,n}^{\parallel}=\sqrt{k_{x}^{2}+\alpha\left[k_{y}^{2}+\left(\frac{n\pi}{a}\right)^{2}\right]}.

(165)

These are the frequencies used in the parallel configuration.

The derivation above shows that, for the two special orientations
considered in the main text, the perfect-conductor boundary conditions
are compatible with the standard standing-wave quantization of the
normal momentum. The anisotropy modifies the dispersion relation, and
therefore the orientation dependence of the zero-point energy, but it
does not modify the allowed values of
k_{z}
in the regular sector.

This conclusion relies essentially on
s=S_{\rm m}(\bar{P})\neq 0
. It should not
be extrapolated directly to the exact degenerate surface

S_{\rm m}(P_{\star})=0
. In particular, when the magnetic background is
perpendicular to the plates, the normal magnetic boundary condition

\delta B_{z}=-s\,h_{z}=0
no longer constrains
h_{z}
once
s=0
. This is
another manifestation of the fact that the exact degenerate vacuum must
be quantized separately, rather than obtained by a naive continuation
of the regular cavity spectrum.
