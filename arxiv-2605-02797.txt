Quantitative Weak Unique Continuation on Annular Domains for Backward Degenerate Parabolic Equations with Degenerate Interior Points This work was carried out with the support of the National Natural Science Foundation of China under grant nos. 12131008 and U23B2033, and National Key R&D Program of China under grant no. 2024YFA1013101.
Dong-Hui Yang, Bao-Zhu Guo, Guojie Zheng, Jie Zhong

Abstract
In this paper, we establish a quantitative weak unique continuation theorem on an annular domain for a backward degenerate parabolic equation with a degenerate interior point. Our methodology hinges on approximating the solution of the degenerate parabolic equation through solutions of non-degenerate parabolic counterparts. Subsequently, we establish Carleman estimates for the non-degenerate parabolic equation across two separate domains. By virtue of these estimates, we deduce a quantitative weak unique continuation property for the degenerate parabolic equation, thereby substantiating the weak unique continuation result for the original degenerate parabolic equation.

In this paper, we investigate a form of weak unique continuation for degenerate parabolic equations with degenerate interior points using Carleman estimates and approximation methods. \begin{cases}\partial_{t}\varphi+\Div(|x|^{\alpha}\nabla\varphi)=0,&\text{ in }Q,\\

Let

Lemma 2.1

Let

where

, the

,

, and the diameter of

Lemma 2.2

For every
N\geq 2
and
\alpha\in(0,2)

(N-2+\alpha)\left\||x|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}u\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq 2\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;w)}

Proof.

The first part of the proof is derived from

[

For the second part, utilizing Lemma

\frac{N-2+\alpha}{2m}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;w)}\leq\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;w)}\text{ with }m:=\sup_{x\in\Omega}|x|+1

and

\frac{N-2+\alpha}{2m^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;w)},

\square

Lemma 2.4

Let
\varphi_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
S=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial x_{i}}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\Omega;w))
,
g\in L^{2}(Q;w^{-1})
. If
\varphi\in L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w))
is a weak solution of the problem

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\varphi-\mathrm{Div}(w\nabla\varphi)=g-S,&\text{ in }Q,\\
\varphi=0,&\text{ on }\partial Q,\\
\varphi(0)=\varphi_{0},&\text{ in }\Omega,\end{cases}

then

\begin{split}&\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{\Omega}|\varphi(x,t)|^{2}\,dx+\iint_{Q}|\nabla\varphi(x,t)|^{2}w\,dx\,dt\\
&\leq C\left(\|\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|g\|_{L^{2}(Q;w^{-1})}^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;w^{-1})}^{2}\right),\end{split}

where the constant
C>0
depends only on
\alpha
,

, and
\Omega
.

Proof.

The proof is straightforward. Actually, we can readily verify that

\begin{split}&\iint_{\Omega\times(0,\tau)}w\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx\,dt+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\varphi(x,\tau)|^{2}\,dx\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\varphi_{0}|^{2}\,dx+\iint_{\Omega\times(0,\tau)}g\varphi\,dx\,dt+\iint_{\Omega\times(0,\tau)}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x_{i}}f_{i}\,dx\,dt\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\varphi_{0}|^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\iint_{\Omega\times(0,\tau)}g^{2}w^{-1}\,dx\,dt+\varepsilon\iint_{\Omega\times(0,\tau)}\varphi^{2}w\,dx\,dt\\
&+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\iint_{\Omega\times(0,\tau)}\sum_{i=1}^{N}f_{i}^{2}w^{-1}\,dx\,dt+\varepsilon\iint_{\Omega\times(0,\tau)}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}w\,dx\,dt\end{split}

holds for all
\tau\in(0,T)
and
\varepsilon>0
.
By choosing
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{4}\min\left\{\left(\frac{N-2+\alpha}{2m}\right)^{2},1\right\}
and using (

), we derive (
2.7
).

\square

Approximations

In this section, Theorem

Lemma 3.1

There exists a
C^{2,1}
function
\psi_{\varepsilon}:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
with
\varepsilon\in(0,R)
such that

\psi_{\varepsilon}(x)=|x|\text{ for }|x|\geq\varepsilon,|x|\leq\psi_{\varepsilon}(x)\leq 2\varepsilon\text{ on }[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon],

and

\psi_{\varepsilon}\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\text{ on }{\mathbb{R}},|\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|\leq C\text{ on }{\mathbb{R}},|\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}|\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon},\text{ and }|\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime\prime}|\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{2}}\text{ on }{\mathbb{R}},

(3.1)

where the constants
C>0
are absolute.

Proof.

Consider the polynomial function

\psi_{\varepsilon}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{4}a_{i}x^{4-i},

where
a_{0},\ldots,a_{4}
are constants to be determined. To this end, we impose the conditions

\begin{split}\psi_{\varepsilon}(-\varepsilon)=\varepsilon,\psi_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon)=\varepsilon,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(-\varepsilon)=-1,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(\varepsilon)=1,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}(\pm\varepsilon)=0,\end{split}

which yield the solution

\psi_{\varepsilon}(x)=\frac{3\varepsilon}{8}+\frac{3}{4\varepsilon}x^{2}-\frac{1}{8\varepsilon^{3}}x^{4},\forall x\in[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon],\text{ and}\psi_{\varepsilon}(x)=|x|,\forall|x|\geq\varepsilon.

(3.2)

This is the desired function.

\square

Remark 3.1

Let
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
, and consider the function
\psi_{\varepsilon}(x)=\psi_{\varepsilon}(|x|)
defined in Lemma
3.1
. We have

\begin{split}\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}&=\frac{3}{2\varepsilon}x-\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{3}}|x|^{2}x,\\
\frac{\partial^{2}\psi_{\varepsilon}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}&=\delta_{ij}\left[\frac{3}{2\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{3}}|x|^{2}\right]-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{3}}x_{i}x_{j},\\
\Delta\psi_{\varepsilon}&=N\left[\frac{3}{2\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{3}}|x|^{2}\right]-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{3}}|x|^{2},\\
D^{2}\psi_{\varepsilon}\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}&=\left(\frac{3}{2\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{3}}|x|^{2}\right)^{2}x-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{3}}\left(\frac{3}{2\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{3}}|x|^{2}\right)|x|^{2}x,\end{split}

(3.3)

on
B_{\varepsilon}
, and

\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}=\frac{x}{|x|},\;\frac{\partial^{2}\psi_{\varepsilon}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}=\delta_{ij}|x|^{-1}-x_{i}x_{j}|x|^{-3},\;\Delta\psi_{\varepsilon}=(N-1)|x|^{-1},

(3.4)

on
\Omega\setminus B_{\varepsilon}
.

Denote

\begin{split}w_{\varepsilon}(x)=(\psi_{\varepsilon}(x))^{\alpha}=(\psi_{\varepsilon}(|x|))^{\alpha},x\in\Omega.\end{split}

Lemma 3.2

Let
z\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w_{\varepsilon})
. Then,

(N+\alpha-2)\|w_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\alpha}}z\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq 2\|z\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w_{\varepsilon})}.

(3.5)

Furthermore,

\|z\|{L^{2}(\Omega;w_{\varepsilon})}\leq\frac{2m}{N+\alpha-2}\|\nabla z\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;w_{\varepsilon})},

(3.6)

and

\|z\|{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{2m^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}}{N+\alpha-2}\|\nabla z\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;w_{\varepsilon})}.

(3.7)

Proof.

As
w_{\varepsilon}\in C^{2,1}(\overline{\Omega})
, we derive

\begin{split}2\int_{\Omega}w_{\varepsilon}^{1-\frac{2}{\alpha}}z(x\cdot\nabla z)\,dx&=\int_{\Omega}w_{\varepsilon}^{1-\frac{2}{\alpha}}x\cdot\nabla z^{2}\,dx\\
&=\int_{\Omega}\Div\left(w_{\varepsilon}^{1-\frac{2}{\alpha}}z^{2}x\right)\,dx-\int_{\Omega}z^{2}\Div\left(w_{\varepsilon}^{1-\frac{2}{\alpha}}x\right)\,dx\\
&=-(N+\alpha-2)\int_{\Omega}z^{2}w_{\varepsilon}^{1-\frac{2}{\alpha}}\,dx-(2-\alpha)\int_{B_{\varepsilon}}z^{2}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha-3}[\psi_{\varepsilon}-x\cdot\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}]\,dx.\end{split}

Consequently,

\begin{split}(N+\alpha-2)\int_{\Omega}z^{2}w_{\varepsilon}^{1-\frac{2}{\alpha}}\,dx&\leq-2\int_{\Omega}w_{\varepsilon}^{1-\frac{2}{\alpha}}z(x\cdot\nabla z)\,dx\\
&=2\int_{\Omega}\left(w_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\alpha}}z\right)\left(w_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\alpha}}x\cdot\nabla z\right)\,dx\\
&\leq 2\left(\int_{\Omega}w_{\varepsilon}^{1-\frac{2}{\alpha}}z^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}w_{\varepsilon}^{1-\frac{2}{\alpha}}|x|^{2}|\nabla z|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\leq 2\left(\int_{\Omega}w_{\varepsilon}^{1-\frac{2}{\alpha}}z^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla z|^{2}w_{\varepsilon}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\end{split}

where
\alpha\in(0,2)
and
\psi_{\varepsilon}-x\cdot\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}=\frac{3}{8\varepsilon^{3}}(\varepsilon^{2}-|x|^{2})^{2}\geq 0
on
B_{\varepsilon}
. This leads to

(N+\alpha-2)\|w_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\alpha}}z\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq 2\|\nabla z\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;w_{\varepsilon})}.

Hence, (
3.5
) holds.
Finally, by (
3.5
) and
\varepsilon^{\alpha}\leq w_{\varepsilon}\leq m^{\alpha}
in
\Omega
, we obtain (
3.6
) and (
3.7
).

\square

is a given function satisfying
f_{k}w_{k}^{-1}\in L^{2}(Q;w_{k})
(i.e.,
f_{k}\in L^{2}(Q;w_{k}^{-1})
).
Denote

W_{k}=\left\{\varphi\in L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w_{k}))\colon\partial_{t}\varphi\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\Omega;w_{k}))\right\}.

Clearly,
W_{k}\subset C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))
by Lemma
2.2
. Furthermore,
W_{k}\hookrightarrow L^{2}(Q)
is compact.

Definition 3.1

We call
\varphi_{k}\in W_{k}
a solution of (
3.8
) if

-\iint_{Q}\varphi_{k}\partial_{t}\psi\,dx\,dt+\iint_{Q}\left(\nabla\varphi_{k}\cdot\nabla\psi\right)w_{k}\,dx\,dt=\iint_{Q}f_{k}\psi\,dx\,dt+\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{k}(x)\psi(x,0)\,dx

for any
\psi\in W_{k}
with
\psi(T)=0
.

Lemma 3.3

Let
\varphi_{k}\in W_{k}
(
k\in\mathbb{N}
) denote the solution of (
3.8
), where
\varphi_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
f_{k}w_{k}^{-1}\in L^{2}(\Omega;w_{k})
. Then, the following estimate holds:

\max_{t\in[0,T]}\|\varphi_{k}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\varphi_{k}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w_{k}))}\leq C\left(\|\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|f_{k}w_{k}^{-1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;w_{k})}\right),

where the positive constant
C
depends solely on
\alpha
,
N
, and
\Omega
.
Moreover, if
f_{k}=0
, then for all
0\leq t\leq\tau\leq T
, the following inequality is satisfied:

\|\varphi_{k}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|\varphi_{k}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

Let
\varphi_{k}\in W_{k}
denote the test function. By multiplying
\varphi_{k}
to both sides of (
3.8
) and integrating over the interval
(0,t)
, we derive

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{k}^{2}(t)\mathrm{d}x+\iint_{\Omega\times(0,t)}|\nabla\varphi_{k}|^{2}w_{k}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\iint_{\Omega\times(0,t)}f_{k}\varphi_{k}\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{0}^{2}\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq\delta\iint_{\Omega\times(0,t)}\varphi_{k}^{2}w_{k}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{2\delta}\iint_{\Omega\times(0,1)}f_{k}^{2}w_{k}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{0}^{2}\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

By applying (
3.6
) from Lemma
3.2
and setting
\delta=\frac{N+\alpha-2}{4m}
, we obtain

\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{k}^{2}(t)\mathrm{d}x+\iint_{\Omega\times(0,t)}|\nabla\varphi_{k}|^{2}w_{k}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\leq\frac{2m}{N+\alpha-2}\iint_{\Omega\times(0,t)}f_{k}^{2}w_{k}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{0}^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

Defining
C=\max\left\{\frac{2m}{N+\alpha-2},1\right\}
, we conclude the proof of Lemma
3.3
.

\square

Theorem 3.1

Let
\widehat{\varphi}_{0}\in W
denote the solution of equation (
1.1
) with initial data
\varphi_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
fw^{-1}\in L^{2}(\Omega;w)
. For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\widehat{\varphi}_{k}
be the solution of equation (
3.8
) with initial data
\varphi_{k}=\varphi_{0}
and source term
f_{k}=f
. Then, the following weak convergence results hold:

\begin{split}\widehat{\varphi}_{k}&\rightharpoonup\widehat{\varphi}_{0}\text{ weakly in }L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)),\\
\widehat{\varphi}_{k}&\rightharpoonup\widehat{\varphi}_{0}\text{ weakly in }L^{2}(Q).\end{split}

(3.9)

Furthermore, we have:

\widehat{\varphi}_{k}(T)\rightharpoonup\widehat{\varphi}_{0}(T)\text{ weakly in }L^{2}(\Omega).

(3.10)

Proof.
The proof will be divided into several steps.

Step 1.
Observe that

\begin{split}\iint_{Q}(fw_{k}^{-1})^{2}w_{k}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t&=\iint_{Q}(fw^{-1})^{2}(ww_{k}^{-1})w\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\leq\iint_{Q}(fw^{-1})^{2}w\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t,\end{split}

(3.11)

and

\iint_{\Omega\times(0,t)}|\nabla\widehat{\varphi}_{k}|^{2}w\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\iint_{\Omega\times(0,t)}|\nabla\widehat{\varphi}_{k}|^{2}w_{k}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t,

since
w\leq w_{k}
in
\Omega
. By Lemma
3.3
, we have

\begin{split}\max_{t\in[0,T]}\|\widehat{\varphi}_{k}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w))}\leq C\left(\|\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|fw^{-1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;w)}\right),\end{split}

where the constant
C>0
depends only on
\alpha
,
N
, and
\Omega
. Consequently, there exists a subsequence of
\{\widehat{\varphi}k\}{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, still denoted by itself, and
\widetilde{\varphi}_{0}\in L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w))
, such that

\begin{split}\widehat{\varphi}_{k}&\rightharpoonup\widetilde{\varphi}_{0}\text{ weakly in }L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)),\end{split}

(3.12)

and by Lemma
2.2
, we obtain

\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\rightharpoonup\widetilde{\varphi}_{0}\text{ weakly in }L^{2}(Q).

(3.13)

Furthermore, we have

\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\rightharpoonup\widetilde{\varphi}_{0}\text{ weakly in }L^{2}(Q;w).

Step 2
. Next, we aim to prove that
\widetilde{\varphi}_{0}
constitutes a weak solution of (
1.1
).
Assume
\psi\in W
with
\psi(T)=0
. By leveraging a density argument, we can assume
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\overline{Q})
and that
\psi(\cdot,t)
is compactly supported in
\Omega
for any
t
. Utilizing the definitions of weak solution (refer to Definitions
3.1
and
3.1
) and equations (
3.12
) and (
3.13
), it suffices to demonstrate

\iint_{Q}(\nabla\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\cdot\nabla\psi)w_{k}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\to\iint_{Q}(\nabla\widetilde{\varphi}_{0}\cdot\nabla\psi)w\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\text{ as }k\to\infty.

(3.14)

Firstly, for any
\gamma>0
, by (
3.12
), there exists
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
k\geq k_{0}
, we obtain

\left|\iint_{Q}(\nabla\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\cdot\nabla\psi)w\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t-\iint_{Q}(\nabla\widetilde{\varphi}_{0}\cdot\nabla\psi)w\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\right|<\frac{1}{2}\gamma.

(3.15)

Secondly, observe that
w=w_{k}
on
\Omega\setminus B_{\frac{1}{k}}
, implying

\iint_{(\Omega\setminus B_{\frac{1}{k}})\times(0,T)}(\nabla\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\cdot\nabla\psi)w_{k}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t=\iint_{(\Omega\setminus B_{\frac{1}{k}})\times(0,T)}(\nabla\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\cdot\nabla\psi)w\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.

(3.16)

Thirdly, by utilizing the same reasoning as employed in (
3.11
) from Step 1, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\iint_{B_{\frac{1}{k}}\times(0,T)}(\nabla\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\cdot\nabla\psi)w_{k}\,dx\,dt\right|

(3.17)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\iint_{B_{\frac{1}{k}}\times(0,T)}|\nabla\widehat{\varphi}_{k}|^{2}w_{k}\,dx\,dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\iint_{B_{\frac{1}{k}}\times(0,T)}|\nabla\psi|^{2}w_{k}\,dx\,dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

(3.18)

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{T}\left(\sup_{Q}|\nabla\psi|\right)\left(\|\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|fw^{-1}\|_{L^{2}(Q;w)}\right)\left(w_{k}(B_{\frac{1}{k}})\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

(3.19)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\psi}\sqrt{T}\left(\|\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|fw^{-1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;w)}\right)\frac{1}{k^{\frac{N+\alpha}{2}}}

(3.20)

given that

w_{k}(B_{\frac{1}{k}})=\int_{B_{\frac{1}{k}}}w_{k}\,dx=\int_{B_{\frac{1}{k}}}\frac{1}{k^{\alpha}}\,dx=C\frac{1}{k^{N+\alpha}},

where the constant
C_{\psi}
depends exclusively on
\alpha,N,\Omega,
and
\psi
.
Fourthly,

\begin{split}&\left|\iint_{B_{\frac{1}{k}}\times(0,T)}(\nabla\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\cdot\nabla\psi)w\,dx\,dt\right|\\
&\leq\left(\iint_{B_{\frac{1}{k}}\times(0,T)}|\nabla\widehat{\varphi}_{k}|^{2}w\,dx\,dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\iint_{B_{\frac{1}{k}}\times(0,T)}|\nabla\psi|^{2}w\,dx\,dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\leq C\sqrt{T}\left(\sup_{Q}|\nabla\psi|\right)\left(\|\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(Q;w)}\right)\left(w(B_{\frac{1}{k}})\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\leq C_{\psi}\sqrt{T}\left(\|\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(Q;w)}\right)\frac{1}{k^{\frac{N+\alpha}{2}}}\end{split}

(3.21)

in accordance with

w(B_{\frac{1}{k}})=\int_{B_{\frac{1}{k}}}w\,dx=\int_{B_{\frac{1}{k}}}|x|^{\alpha}\,dx=C\frac{1}{k^{N+\alpha}},

where the constant
C_{\psi}
is contingent only on
\alpha,N,\Omega,
and
\psi
.
Ultimately, since
w=w_{k}
on
\Omega\setminus B_{\frac{1}{k}}
, by (
3.15
), (
3.16
), (
3.17
), and (
3.21
), we derive

\displaystyle\left|\iint_{Q}(\nabla\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\cdot\nabla\psi)w_{k}\,dx\,dt-\iint_{Q}(\nabla\widetilde{\varphi}_{0}\cdot\nabla\psi)w\,dx\,dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\iint_{Q}(\nabla\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\cdot\nabla\psi)w_{k}\,dx\,dt-\iint_{Q}(\nabla\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\cdot\nabla\psi)w\,dx\,dt\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\iint_{Q}(\nabla\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\cdot\nabla\psi)w\,dx\,dt-\iint_{Q}(\nabla\widetilde{\varphi}_{0}\cdot\nabla\psi)w\,dx\,dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\iint_{(\Omega\setminus B_{\frac{1}{k}})\times(0,T)}(\nabla\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\cdot\nabla\psi)w_{k}\,dx\,dt-\iint_{(\Omega\setminus B_{\frac{1}{k}})\times(0,T)}(\nabla\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\cdot\nabla\psi)w\,dx\,dt\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\iint_{B_{\frac{1}{k}}\times(0,T)}(\nabla\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\cdot\nabla\psi)w_{k}\,dx\,dt\right|+\left|\iint_{B_{\frac{1}{k}}\times(0,T)}(\nabla\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\cdot\nabla\psi)w\,dx\,dt\right|+\frac{1}{2}\gamma

\displaystyle\leq C_{\psi}\sqrt{T}\left(\|\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(Q;w)}\right)\frac{1}{k^{\frac{N+\alpha}{2}}}+\frac{1}{2}\gamma,

thus validating (
3.14
). From this and
Step 1
, we have established that

-\iint_{Q}\widetilde{\varphi}_{0}\partial_{t}\psi\,dx\,dt+\iint_{Q}(\nabla\widetilde{\varphi}_{0}\cdot\nabla\psi)w\,dx\,dt=\iint_{Q}f\psi\,dx\,dt+\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{0}(x)\psi(x,0)\,dx.

This confirms that
\widetilde{\varphi}_{0}
is a solution of (
1.1
).

Step 4
. Given that
\widetilde{\varphi}_{0}
and
\widehat{\varphi}_{0}
are solutions of (
1.1
) with initial data
\varphi_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
fw^{-1}\in L^{2}(\Omega;w)
, by the uniqueness of the solution of (
1.1
), we conclude that
\widetilde{\varphi}_{0}=\widehat{\varphi}_{0}
.

Step 5
. Finally, let
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\overline{Q})
and
\psi(\cdot,t)
be compactly supported in
\Omega
for any
t\in[0,T]
. Multiplying
\psi
on both sides of (
3.8
) with
\widehat{\varphi}_{k}(0)=\varphi_{0}
and
f_{k}=f
, we obtain

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\widehat{\varphi}_{k}(T)\psi(T)\,dx-\iint_{Q}\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\partial_{t}\psi\,dx\,dt+\iint_{Q}w_{k}\nabla\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\cdot\nabla\psi\,dx\,dt=\iint_{Q}f\psi\,dx\,dt+\int_{\Omega}\widehat{\varphi}_{k}(0)\psi(0)\,dx.\end{split}

Multiplying
\psi
on both sides of (
1.1
), we obtain

\int_{\Omega}\widehat{\varphi}_{0}(T)\psi(T)\,dx-\iint_{Q}\widehat{\varphi}_{0}\partial_{t}\psi\,dx\,dt+\iint_{Q}w\nabla\widehat{\varphi}_{0}\cdot\nabla\psi\,dx\,dt=\iint_{Q}f\psi\,dx\,dt+\int_{\Omega}\widehat{\varphi}_{0}(0)\psi(0)\,dx.

By (
3.14
) and
\widehat{\varphi}_{0}=\widetilde{\varphi}_{0}
,
\widehat{\varphi}_{k}(0)=\widehat{\varphi}_{0}(0)=\varphi_{0}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\rightharpoonup\widehat{\varphi}_{0}
weakly in
L^{2}(Q)
, we obtain

\int_{\Omega}\widehat{\varphi}_{k}(T)\psi(T)\,dx\to\int_{\Omega}\widehat{\varphi}_{0}(T)\psi(T)\,dx.

This indicates that
\widehat{\varphi}_{k}(T)\to\widehat{\varphi}_{0}(T)
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
since
\psi(T)
is arbitrary.

\square

Corollary 3.1

Under the hypotheses stated in Theorem
3.1
, and with the additional assumptions that the initial data
\varphi_{0}
belongs to
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\cap H^{2}(\Omega)
and
f_{k}=f
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the following strong convergence results hold:

\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\to\widehat{\varphi}_{0}\quad\text{in }L^{2}(Q),

and

\widehat{\varphi}_{k}(T)\to\widehat{\varphi}_{0}(T)\quad\text{in }L^{2}(\Omega).

Furthermore, if
f=0
,
\varphi_{0}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\cap H^{3}(\Omega)
, and
\Div(w_{k}\nabla\varphi_{0})\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, then the following additional strong convergence results hold:

\frac{\partial\widehat{\varphi}_{k}}{\partial\nu}\to\frac{\partial\widehat{\varphi}_{0}}{\partial\nu}\quad\text{in }L^{2}(\partial Q),

and

\nabla\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\to\nabla\widehat{\varphi}_{0}\quad\text{in }[L^{2}(K\times(0,T))]^{N}

for every measurable subset
K\subset\Omega
satisfying
\dist(0,K)>0
.

Proof.
The proof will be split into several steps.

Given
\varphi_{0}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\cap H^{2}(\Omega)
, the solution
\widehat{\varphi}_{k}
of (
3.8
) satisfies (noting that
H_{w_{k},0}^{1}=H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
)

\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{3}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega))\cap H^{1}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega))\quad\text{and}\quad\partial_{t}\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\in L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)).

(3.22)

Multiplying
\partial_{t}\widehat{\varphi}_{k}
on both sides of (
3.8
) and integrating over
\Omega\times(0,t)
, we get

\begin{split}\iint_{\Omega\times(0,t)}(\partial_{t}\widehat{\varphi}_{k})^{2}\,dx\,dt+\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\Omega\times(0,t)}\partial_{t}\left(|\nabla\widehat{\varphi}_{k}|^{2}w_{k}\right)\,dx\,dt=\iint_{\Omega\times(0,t)}f\partial_{t}\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\,dx\,dt.\end{split}

This leads to

\begin{split}&\iint_{\Omega\times(0,t)}|\partial_{t}\widehat{\varphi}_{k}|^{2}\,dx\,dt+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\widehat{\varphi}_{k}(t)|^{2}w_{k}\,dx\\
&=\iint_{\Omega\times(0,t)}f\partial_{t}\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\,dx\,dt+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\widehat{\varphi}_{k}(0)|^{2}w_{k}\,dx\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\Omega\times(0,t)}|\partial_{t}\widehat{\varphi}_{k}|^{2}\,dx\,dt+\frac{m^{\alpha}}{2}\left(\iint_{\Omega\times(0,t)}(fw^{-1})^{2}w\,dx\,dt+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi_{0}|^{2}\,dx\right).\end{split}

for all
t\in(0,T]
.
Consequently:

\iint_{\Omega\times(0,t)}|\partial_{t}\widehat{\varphi}_{k}|^{2}\,dx\,dt+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\widehat{\varphi}_{k}(t)|^{2}w_{k}\,dx\leq m^{\alpha}\left(\iint_{\Omega\times(0,t)}(fw^{-1})^{2}w\,dx\,dt+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi_{0}|^{2}\,dx\right).

(3.23)

Thus, by (
2.3
), we have

\begin{split}\|\partial_{t}\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H_{w}^{-1}(\Omega))}&=\sup_{\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H_{w,0}^{1}(\Omega))}\leq 1}\langle\partial_{t}\widehat{\varphi}_{k},\psi\rangle_{L^{2}(0,T;H_{w}^{-1}(\Omega)),L^{2}(0,T;H_{w,0}^{1}(\Omega))}\\
&=\sup_{\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H_{w,0}^{1}(\Omega))}\leq 1}\iint_{\Omega\times(0,t)}(\partial_{t}\widehat{\varphi}_{k})\psi\,dx\,dt\\
&\leq C\sup_{\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H_{w,0}^{1}(\Omega))}\leq 1}\|\partial_{t}\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times(0,t))}\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times(0,t))}\leq C\|\partial_{t}\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times(0,t))},\end{split}

(3.24)

where the constant
C>0
depends only on
\alpha,N
, and
\Omega
, and we utilized (
2.3
) in the last inequality. From this and Theorem
3.1
, we obtain that there exists a subsequence of
\{\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\}
, still denoted by itself, such that:

\partial_{t}\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\rightharpoonup\partial_{t}\widehat{\varphi}_{0}\text{ weakly in }L^{2}(0,T;H_{w}^{-1}(\Omega)).

From this, (
3.24
), and the compact embedding
W\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\Omega\times(0,t))
, we have:

\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\to\widehat{\varphi}_{0}\text{ strongly in }L^{2}(\Omega\times(0,t)).

Step 2
. Next, utilizing (
3.23
) and the inequality
w\leq w_{k}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we derive:

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\widehat{\varphi}_{k}(T)|^{2}w\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\widehat{\varphi}_{k}(T)|^{2}w_{k}\,dx\leq m^{\alpha}\left(\iint_{Q}(fw^{-1})^{2}w\,dx\,dt+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi_{0}|^{2}\,dx\right).

This demonstrates the existence of a subsequence of
\{\widehat{\varphi}_{k}(T)\}
, still denoted by itself, and
\xi\in H_{w,0}^{1}(\Omega)
, such that
\widehat{\varphi}_{k}(T)\rightharpoonup\xi
weakly in
H_{w,0}^{1}(\Omega)
, and
\widehat{\varphi}_{k}(T)\to\xi
strongly in
L^{2}(\Omega)
by Lemma
2.3
. This, coupled with (
3.10
), yields
\xi=\widehat{\varphi}_{0}(T)
.
This concludes the proof of Corollary
3.1
.

Step 3
. Finally, selecting
\zeta\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),0\leq\zeta\leq 1
satisfying:

\zeta=1\text{ on }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{2R},\zeta=0\text{ on }B_{R},|\nabla\zeta|\leq\frac{C}{R}\text{ on }\mathbb{R}^{N}

with
\text{ dist}(K,0)\geq 2R>0
and
B_{4R}\subset\Omega
, we apply the trace theorem to obtain:

\begin{split}\iint_{\partial Q}\left(\frac{\partial\widehat{\varphi}_{k}}{\partial\nu}\right)^{2}\,dS\,dt&\leq C\sum_{k=0}^{2}\iint_{Q}\left|D^{k}(\zeta\widehat{\varphi}_{k})\right|^{2}\,dx\,dt,\end{split}

(3.25)

where
C>0
is a constant dependent solely on
\Omega
. Observing that
h_{k}=\zeta\widehat{\varphi}_{k}
solves the equation:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}h_{k}-\Div(w_{k}\nabla h_{k})=\zeta f-2w_{k}\nabla\zeta\cdot\nabla\widehat{\varphi}_{k}-\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\Div(w_{k}\nabla\zeta),&\text{ in }(\Omega\setminus B_{R})\times(0,T),\\
h_{k}=0,&\text{ on }\partial(\Omega\setminus B_{R})\times(0,T),\\
h_{k}(0)=\zeta\varphi_{0},&\text{ in }\Omega\setminus B_{R},\end{cases}

which is uniformly parabolic. Given
f=0
,
\zeta\varphi_{0}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega\setminus B_{R})
,
\Div(w_{k}(\nabla(\zeta\varphi_{0}))\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega\setminus B_{R})
, and
\partial_{t}[2w_{k}\nabla\zeta\cdot\nabla\widehat{\varphi}_{k}+\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\Div(w_{k}\nabla\zeta)]\in L^{2}(Q)
by (
3.22
), we invoke the standard regularity theory for uniformly parabolic equations (see Theorem 5 in Chapter 7.1.3 in
[
11
]
, pages 360-361) to establish the existence of a constant
C>0
dependent on
\alpha,T,R
, and
\Omega
, such that:

\begin{split}\|\partial_{t}h_{k}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega\setminus B_{R}))}+\|h_{k}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{3}(\Omega\setminus B_{R}))}\leq C\|\varphi_{0}\|_{H^{3}(\Omega)}.\end{split}

(3.26)

This implies that
\{\frac{\partial\widehat{\varphi}_{k}}{\partial\nu}\}
is bounded in
L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\partial\Omega))
. By the classical Sobolev compact embedding theorem, there exists a subsequence of
\{\frac{\partial\widehat{\varphi}_{k}}{\partial\nu}\}
, still denoted by itself, such that:

\frac{\partial\widehat{\varphi}_{k}}{\partial\nu}\to\frac{\partial\widehat{\varphi}_{0}}{\partial\nu}\text{ strongly in }L^{2}(\partial Q).

Analogously to (
3.24
), noting that
H_{w,0}^{1}(\Omega\setminus B_{R})=H_{0}^{1}(\Omega\setminus B_{R})
from
R^{\alpha}\leq w\leq m^{\alpha}
on
\Omega\setminus B_{R}
, we utilize (
3.23
) to obtain:

\begin{split}\|\partial_{t}\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega\setminus B_{R};w))}&=\sup_{\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega\setminus B_{R};w))}\leq 1}\iint_{Q}(\partial_{t}\widehat{\varphi}_{k})\psi w\,dx\,dt\\
&\leq C\|\partial_{t}\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega\setminus B_{R};w))}\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega\setminus B_{R};w))}\leq C\|\varphi_{0}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega\setminus B_{R})}.\end{split}

By (
3.23
), (
3.26
),
L^{2}(\Omega\setminus B_{R};w)=L^{2}(\Omega\setminus B_{R})
,
\partial_{t}h_{k}=\zeta\partial_{t}\widehat{\varphi}_{k}
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and the compact embedding:

\{h\in L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega\setminus B_{R})\cap H^{2}(\Omega\setminus B_{R}))\colon\partial_{t}h\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega\setminus B_{R}))\}\hookrightarrow L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega\setminus B_{R})),

we deduce:

\nabla h_{k}\to\nabla(\zeta\widehat{\varphi}_{0})\text{ strong in }[L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega\setminus B_{R}))]^{N}.

Furthermore, given
\zeta=0
on
\Omega\setminus B_{2R}
, we conclude:

\nabla\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\to\nabla\widehat{\varphi}_{0}\text{ strong in }[L^{2}(K\times(0,T))]^{N}.

This completes the proof of the corollary.

\square

4
Carleman estimates

In this section, we start by conducting a series of calculations aimed at deriving Theorem
4.3
. It is worth noting that the computation of the Carleman estimate hinges on the selection of weight functions (as exemplified by the functions
\eta_{\varepsilon}\ (\varepsilon>0)
presented below). Consequently, the Carleman estimate stated in Theorem

4.1

4.2

4.3

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+\Div(w_{\varepsilon}\nabla u)=g,&\text{ in }Q,\\

(4.1)

where

,
g\in L^{2}(Q;w_{\varepsilon}^{-1})
.
Assume

We assume

Taking

\begin{split}\nabla w_{\varepsilon}&=\alpha\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha-1}\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon},\quad\nabla\eta_{\varepsilon}=\gamma(2-\alpha)\psi_{\varepsilon}^{1-\alpha}\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon},\quad\nabla\xi=\gamma(2-\alpha)\Theta\psi_{\varepsilon}^{1-\alpha}\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}.\end{split}

\begin{split}\Div(w_{\varepsilon}\nabla\xi)&=\gamma(2-\alpha)\Theta(|\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\psi_{\varepsilon}\Delta\psi_{\varepsilon}),\quad\xi_{t}=\Theta^{\prime}\eta_{\varepsilon},\quad\xi_{tt}=\Theta^{\prime\prime}\eta_{\varepsilon}.\end{split}

(4.2)

Now, we compute
(P_{1}v,P_{2}v)_{L^{2}(Q)}

(i): Compute
(P_{11}v,P_{21}v)_{L^{2}(Q)}

\partial Q

For the approximate Proposition

This process is further divided into multiple steps.

Step 1: Analysis of the part on

by Lemma
