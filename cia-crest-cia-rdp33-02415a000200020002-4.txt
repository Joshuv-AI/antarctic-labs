1. Photo coverage of manv targets is requ J:^ d °tire

each U-2 mission. To insure adequate coverage the entire
area of the mission must have 25 per cent or sc loud

cover. With CAT I or CAT II conditions at least 75 per
cent of the targets will be photographed clearly on a y
one flight.

2. A specific target reporting CAT 1 or II d ?es

j. mpan that the entire area is CAT I or II. In fact,
over Cuba, particularly during the winter, cloud coverage
varies sreatly from one location to another. This change
of ctoud cover is due to prevailing wind time or day
and topographical influences such as land, water an
mountain contrasts,

3. A normal weather observation by a. ground observer
reporting a "good" or "fair" day does not imply that

the day is favorable for high altitude photography. Clouds
at all 7 levels within the camera's cone of _ observation
must be considered to determine what portion of the groun
3KS be photographed , Certain clouds which are of no
significance to the ground observer are extremely
critical when considering high altitude photography.

area to CAT III conditions decreases _ the probability or
successful photography in two significant ways:

a Up to 50 per cent of the earth| s surface
scanned by the camera lens will be invisible
because of intervening clouds. Another way of
stating this is that any given target has up to
a 50 per cent chance of being obscured by cloud
and therefore invisible to the camera.

b At any time the camera is not on a line
between the target and the sun, cloud shadows
can, and do, darken visible terrain sufficiently
to detract significantly from optimum photographic
quality? Thil adverse shadow effect is a function
of cloud amount (horizontal cover), cloud thickness
(vertical development) and the sun an gle. Cumulu
