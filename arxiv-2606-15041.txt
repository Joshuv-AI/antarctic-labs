Dense finite Sidon sets on arithmetic progressions
Yuchen Ding

Abstract
. Let S ⊂ { 1 , 2 , … , n } S\subset\{1,2,\ldots,n\} be a Sidon set with | S | = n 1 / 2 + O ⁡ ( n 1 / 2 − δ ) |S|=n^{1/2}+O(n^{1/2-\delta}) for some fixed δ > 0 \delta>0 . 1.
Introduction

A set
S\subset\mathbb{Z}
is called a Sidon set if all sums
a+b
, with

(a,b)\in S^{2}
considered as unordered pairs, are distinct. Let
S_{n}

be the largest cardinality of a Sidon subset of
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
.
The classical upper bound of Erdős and Turán
[
6
]
, together
with Singer’s construction
[
11
]
and prime gap estimates of
Baker, Harman and Pintz
[
2
]
, gives the near square root
size estimate

(1.1)

S_{n}=n^{1/2}+O\left(n^{21/80}\right).

Uniform distribution questions for dense Sidon sets have a long history.
Lindström
[
9
]
proved well distribution in residue classes,
and Kolountzakis
[
8
]
obtained quantitative refinements
for the result of Lindström. Cilleruelo’s work on gaps
in dense Sidon sets
[
3
]
is another important input in this
circle of ideas. In particular, it is used by Ortega and Prendiville in
the improved Fourier uniformity estimate recalled below.

In
[
4
]
, the author proved that if
S\subset[1,n]
is a
Sidon set with
|S|=S_{n}
, then for every
\varepsilon>0
,

\sum_{a\in S}a=\frac{1}{2}n^{3/2}+O\left(n^{111/80+\varepsilon}\right).

The same paper also considered sums in residue classes. For fixed

m\geq 1
and
0\leq r<m
, it was shown conditionally that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in S\\
a\equiv r\,(\mathrm{mod}\,m)\end{subarray}}a\sim\frac{1}{2m}n^{3/2}.

The condition imposed there was a lower bound for

|S\cap[1,k]|
throughout a long terminal range of
k
. As observed by
Balasubramanian and Dutta
[
1
]
, this condition is
rarely compatible with the expected distribution of a dense Sidon set.
The final remark of
[
4
]
explicitly asks whether the asymptotic remains true in residue class situation after removing this additional
condition. One purpose of the present note is to answer this question
unconditionally, in fact for all fixed power sums.

The author’s subsequent paper
[
5
]
developed these weighted
summation questions further. It proved, for

S=\{a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdots<a_{t}\}\subset[1,n],

that for every positive integer
\ell
,

\sum_{i=1}^{t}a_{i}^{\ell}=\frac{t}{\ell+1}n^{\ell}+O\left(n^{\ell+3/8}\right)

whenever

t\geq n^{1/2}-n^{1/4}.

Moreover, for
|S|=S_{n}
the author proved

\sum_{a\in S}a=\frac{1}{2}n^{3/2}+O\left(n^{221/160}\right),

which slightly improves the error term in
[
4
]
. The same
paper also proved the almost all estimate

\sum_{a\in S}a=\frac{1}{2}n^{3/2}+O\left(n^{11/8}\log n\right)

for all
n\leq N
, with at most

O\left(\frac{N}{(\log N)^{7/19}}\right)

exceptions, and established asymptotic formulae for more general
weighted sums

\sum_{i=1}^{t}i^{s}a_{i}^{\ell}.

One of the results below gives a
residue class analogue of these weighted sums.

Balasubramanian and Dutta
[
1
]
later introduced
a direct formula for the
m
-th element of a dense finite Sidon set.
As consequences of their formula, they recovered some results of

[
4
,
5
]
by a different argument and improved the
almost all error term from

[
5
]
, using a prime gap estimate of
Heath-Brown
[
7
]
. Precisely, they proved for every
\varepsilon>0
,

\sum_{a\in S}a=\frac{1}{2}n^{3/2}+O\left(n^{11/8}\right)

for all
n\leq N
, with at most

O\left(N^{4/5+\varepsilon}\right)

exceptions. Their observation about the conditional residue class theorem
in
[
4
]
is one motivation for the present note. We use the
same prime gap device below to sharpen the residue class error term for
almost all values of
n
.

We revisit the residue class problem using the
Fourier uniformity of extremal Sidon sets, due to Ortega and
Prendiville
[
10
]
. This gives a short unconditional
proof of the residue class weighted sum asymptotic formula. In fact, the same
argument gives a more general statement on a dense Sidon set has
the expected density on every finite arithmetic progression.

For a Sidon set

S\subset[1,n]
, put

(1.2)

\Phi(S,n)=n^{1/2}\left(\left|1-\frac{|S|}{n^{1/2}}\right|+n^{-1/4}\right)^{1/2}.

The new results are the following asymptotic formulae.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\ell\geq 0
be fixed, and let
S\subset[1,n]
be a Sidon set.
Then, for every finite arithmetic progression
P\subset[1,n]
,

(1.3)

\sum_{a\in S\cap P}a^{\ell}=\frac{|S|}{n}\sum_{a\in P}a^{\ell}+O\left(n^{\ell}\Phi(S,n)\log(2n)\right).

The implied constant depends only on
\ell
.

Corollary 2
.

Let
m\geq 1
,
0\leq r<m
, and
\ell\geq 0
be fixed integers.
Let
S\subset[1,n]
be a Sidon set. Then, uniformly for all intervals
of integers
I\subset[1,n]
,

(1.4)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in S\cap I\\
a\equiv r\,(\mathrm{mod}\,m)\end{subarray}}a^{\ell}=\frac{|S|}{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in I\\
a\equiv r\,(\mathrm{mod}\,m)\end{subarray}}a^{\ell}+O\left(n^{\ell}\Phi(S,n)\log(2n)\right).

The implied constant depends only on
\ell
and
m
.

For the maximal cases below, we recall the standard estimate

S_{n}=n^{1/2}+O\left(n^{21/80}\right),

already stated in (
1.1
). This is the only
additional input needed to pass from the general residue class formula
to maximal Sidon sets. The next corollary contains the unconditional
maximal Sidon set form of the residue class result asked for in the
final remark of
[
4
]
.

Corollary 3
.

Let
m\geq 1
,
0\leq r<m
, and
\ell\geq 0
be fixed integers.
Let
S\subset[1,n]
be a Sidon set. Then

(1.5)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in S\\
a\equiv r\,(\mathrm{mod}\,m)\end{subarray}}a^{\ell}=\frac{|S|}{m(\ell+1)}n^{\ell}+O\left(n^{\ell}\Phi(S,n)\log(2n)\right).

The implied constant depends only on
\ell
and
m
.
In particular, if
|S|=S_{n}
, then

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in S\\
a\equiv r\,(\mathrm{mod}\,m)\end{subarray}}a=\frac{1}{2m}n^{3/2}+O\left(n^{221/160}\log(2n)\right),

where the implied constant depends only on
m
.

In particular, if
|S|\sim n^{1/2}
and

\Phi(S,n)\log(2n)=o(n^{1/2})
, then the expected asymptotic formula
in each fixed residue class follows. Note that
\Phi(S,n)\log(2n)=o(n^{1/2})
indeed happens if
|S|=n^{1/2}+O(n^{1/2-\delta})
for some
\delta>0
.
Under the hypothesis

|S|\geq n^{1/2}-n^{1/4}
, the same corollary gives the error term

O\big(n^{\ell+3/8}\log(2n)\big)
, with the implied constant depending only on

\ell
and
m
.

The next theorem is a weighted generalization of
Theorem
1
,
giving parallel estimates after weighting

a_{i}\in S
by
i^{s}a_{i}^{\ell}
.

Theorem 4
.

Let
m\geq 1
,
0\leq r<m
, and
s,\ell\geq 0
be fixed
integers. Let

S=\{a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdots<a_{t}\}\subset[1,n]

be a Sidon set. Then, uniformly for all intervals of integers

I\subset[1,n]
,

(1.6)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq t\\
a_{i}\in I\\
a_{i}\equiv r\,(\mathrm{mod}\,m)\end{subarray}}i^{s}a_{i}^{\ell}=\frac{t^{s+1}}{n^{s+1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in I\\
a\equiv r\,(\mathrm{mod}\,m)\end{subarray}}a^{s+\ell}+O\left(t^{s}n^{\ell}\Phi(S,n)\log(2n)\right).

The implied constant depends only on
s
,
\ell
, and
m
.
In particular,

(1.7)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq t\\
a_{i}\equiv r\,(\mathrm{mod}\,m)\end{subarray}}i^{s}a_{i}^{\ell}=\frac{t^{s+1}}{m(s+\ell+1)}n^{\ell}+O\left(t^{s}n^{\ell}\Phi(S,n)\log(2n)\right).

Corollary 5
.

Let
m\geq 1
,
0\leq r<m
, and
s,\ell\geq 0
be fixed integers.
Let

S=\{a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdots<a_{t}\}\subset[1,n]

be a Sidon set with
t=S_{n}
. Then

(1.8)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq t\\
a_{i}\equiv r\,(\mathrm{mod}\,m)\end{subarray}}i^{s}a_{i}^{\ell}=\frac{1}{m(s+\ell+1)}n^{\ell+(s+1)/2}+O\left(n^{\ell+s/2+61/160}\log(2n)\right).

The implied constant depends only on
s
,
\ell
, and
m
.

The following almost all theorem follows by combining

Theorem
4
with the prime gap input used by
Balasubramanian and Dutta
[
1
]
.

Theorem 6
.

Let
N\geq 2
and
\varepsilon>0
. For all but

O(N^{4/5+\varepsilon})
integers
n\leq N
, the following assertion
holds: for any fixed integers
m\geq 1
,
0\leq r<m
, and

s,\ell\geq 0
, if

S=\{a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdots<a_{t}\}\subset[1,n]

is a Sidon set with
t=S_{n}
,
then

(1.9)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq t\\
a_{i}\equiv r\,(\mathrm{mod}\,m)\end{subarray}}i^{s}a_{i}^{\ell}=\frac{1}{m(s+\ell+1)}n^{\ell+(s+1)/2}+O\left(n^{\ell+s/2+3/8}\log n\right).

The implied constant in the formula depends only on
s
,
\ell
, and

m
. The exceptional set constant may also depend on
\varepsilon
.
In particular, for
s=0
and
\ell=1
,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in S\\
a\equiv r\,(\mathrm{mod}\,m)\end{subarray}}a=\frac{1}{2m}n^{3/2}+O\left(n^{11/8}\log n\right),

where the implied constant depends only on
m
.

I hope these results would be useful for further investigations of the Sidon sets in future.

2.
Proofs

2.1.
Fourier input

We use the following consequences of Ortega and Prendiville’s
Fourier uniformity theorem for extremal Sidon
sets
[
10
, Theorem 6.3]
.
We write
\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
, identifying it with
[0,1)
in integrals. For a
set
A\subset\mathbb{Z}
, let
1_{A}
denote its indicator function. For a
function
G:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{C}
, put

\|G\|_{\infty}=\sup_{\alpha\in\mathbb{T}}|G(\alpha)|
. For a finitely
supported function
f:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{C}
, write

\widehat{f}(\alpha)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}}f(x)\mathrm{e}^{2\pi i\alpha x}\qquad(\alpha\in\mathbb{T}).

Lemma 7
.

Let
S\subset[1,n]
be a Sidon set. Then

\left\|\widehat{1_{S}}-\frac{|S|}{n}\widehat{1_{[1,n]}}\right\|_{\infty}\ll\Phi(S,n).

Proof.

Ortega and Prendiville proved

[
10
, Theorem 6.3]
that

(2.1)

\left\|\widehat{1_{S}}-\frac{|S|}{n}\widehat{1_{[1,n]}}\right\|_{\infty}\ll n^{1/2}\left(\left|1-\frac{|S|}{n^{1/2}}\right|+n^{-1/4}\right)^{1/2}.

This is precisely the claimed bound.
∎

Lemma 8
.

Let
P\subset[1,n]
be a finite arithmetic progression. Then

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\big|\widehat{1_{P}}(\alpha)\big|\,{\rm d}\alpha\ll\log(2n).

Proof.

If
P
is empty or consists of one point, the estimate is immediate.
Otherwise, the result follows from the standard progression estimate of
Ortega and Prendiville
[
10
, Lemma 3.2]
, since

|P|\leq n
.
∎

Lemma 9
.

Let
S\subset[1,n]
be a Sidon set. Then, uniformly for every finite
arithmetic progression
P\subset[1,n]
,

|S\cap P|=\frac{|S|}{n}|P|+O\left(\Phi(S,n)\log(2n)\right).

The implied constant is absolute.

Proof.

Put

h=1_{S}-\frac{|S|}{n}1_{[1,n]}.

Then

|S\cap P|-\frac{|S|}{n}|P|=\sum_{x\in P}h(x)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}}1_{P}(x)h(x).

For finitely supported functions on
\mathbb{Z}
, Fourier orthogonality gives

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}}1_{P}(x)h(x)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\widehat{1_{P}}(\alpha)\widehat{h}(-\alpha)\,{\rm d}\alpha.

Since

\widehat{h}=\widehat{1_{S}}-\frac{|S|}{n}\widehat{1_{[1,n]}}
, we
obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}}1_{P}(x)h(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\widehat{1_{P}}(\alpha)\left(\widehat{1_{S}}-\frac{|S|}{n}\widehat{1_{[1,n]}}\right)(-\alpha)\,{\rm d}\alpha

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\widehat{1_{S}}-\frac{|S|}{n}\widehat{1_{[1,n]}}\right\|_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\big|\widehat{1_{P}}(\alpha)\big|{\rm d}\alpha.

Combining
Lemma
8
with
Lemma
7
gives

|S\cap P|-\frac{|S|}{n}|P|\ll\Phi(S,n)\log(2n).

The bound is uniform in
P
, as required.
∎

2.2.
Proofs of the power sum estimates

Proof of
Theorem
1
.

Let

E=\Phi(S,n)\log(2n).

Let
P\subset[1,n]
be a finite arithmetic progression. For

1\leq k\leq n
, put

P(k)=P\cap[1,k],\qquad A_{P}(k)=|S\cap P(k)|,\qquad R_{P}(k)=|P(k)|.

Since
P(k)
is again a finite arithmetic progression,

Lemma
9
gives, uniformly in
k
,

A_{P}(k)=\frac{|S|}{n}R_{P}(k)+O(E).

For
\ell\geq 1
, the Abel summation gives

\displaystyle\sum_{a\in S\cap P}a^{\ell}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\big(A_{P}(k)-A_{P}(k-1)\big)k^{\ell}

\displaystyle=n^{\ell}A_{P}(n)-\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}A_{P}(k)\left((k+1)^{\ell}-k^{\ell}\right).

Substituting the estimate for
A_{P}(k)
into the summation, we get

(2.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{a\in S\cap P}a^{\ell}=\frac{|S|}{n}\left(n^{\ell}R_{P}(n)-\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}R_{P}(k)\left((k+1)^{\ell}-k^{\ell}\right)\right)+O(n^{\ell}E).

Using again Abel’s summation we get

(2.3)

\displaystyle n^{\ell}R_{P}(n)-\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}R_{P}(k)\left((k+1)^{\ell}-k^{\ell}\right)=\sum_{a\in P}a^{\ell}.

Combining (
2.2
) with (
2.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{a\in S\cap P}a^{\ell}=\frac{|S|}{n}\sum_{a\in P}a^{\ell}+O(n^{\ell}E).

This proves (
1.3
) for
\ell\geq 1
, with the
implied constant depending only on
\ell
. The case
\ell=0
is
exactly
Lemma
9
.
∎

Proof of
Corollary
2
.

For an interval
I\subset[1,n]
, the set

\{a\in I:a\equiv r\,(\mathrm{mod}\,m)\}

is a finite arithmetic progression. Applying
Theorem
1
to this
progression gives the claim.
∎

Proof of
Corollary
3
.

Apply
Theorem
1
to

P=\{a\in[1,n]:a\equiv r\,(\mathrm{mod}\,m)\}.

The elementary estimate for sums of powers in a fixed residue class is

(2.4)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a\leq n\\
a\equiv r\,(\mathrm{mod}\,m)\end{subarray}}a^{\ell}=\frac{1}{m(\ell+1)}n^{\ell+1}+O(n^{\ell}).

Multiplying by
|S|/n
, the extra error is

O(|S|n^{\ell-1})
, which is absorbed by

O(n^{\ell}\Phi(S,n)\log(2n))
, since
|S|\leq n
and

\Phi(S,n)\geq n^{1/2}\left(n^{-1/4}\right)^{1/2}=n^{3/8}.

Hence,
Theorem
1
together with
(
2.4
) implies
(
1.5
) for
\ell\geq 1
. The case

\ell=0
follows similarly from

|P|=n/m+O(1)
.
If
|S|=S_{n}
, then (
1.1
) gives

|S|=n^{1/2}+O(n^{21/80})
. Then

\Phi(S,n)\ll n^{1/2}\left(n^{-19/80}+n^{-1/4}\right)^{1/2}\ll n^{61/160}.

Taking
\ell=1
in
(
1.5
) and replacing
|S|
in the main term
therefore gives the desired result.
∎

2.3.
Proof of the weighted theorem and maximal corollary

Proof of
Theorem
4
.

Put

E=\Phi(S,n)\log(2n)

and

P=\{a\in I:a\equiv r\,(\mathrm{mod}\,m)\}.

The set
P
is a finite arithmetic progression. For
a_{i}\in S
, the
index
i
is the initial counting function of
S
at
a_{i}
. Hence

Lemma
9
, applied to the interval

[1,a_{i}]
, gives

(2.5)

i=\frac{t}{n}a_{i}+O(E).

If
s=0
, the formula is exactly
Corollary
2
.
Assume now that
s\geq 1
. Put
y_{i}=ta_{i}/n
. Since

0\leq i,y_{i}\leq t
, we have

i^{s}-y_{i}^{s}=(i-y_{i})\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}i^{s-1-j}y_{i}^{j}.

Together with (
2.5
), this gives

(2.6)

i^{s}=\left(\frac{t}{n}a_{i}\right)^{s}+O_{s}(t^{s-1}E).

Therefore

(2.7)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq t\\
a_{i}\in I\\
a_{i}\equiv r\,(\mathrm{mod}\,m)\end{subarray}}i^{s}a_{i}^{\ell}=\frac{t^{s}}{n^{s}}\sum_{a\in S\cap P}a^{s+\ell}+O_{s}(t^{s}n^{\ell}E).

Applying
Corollary
2
with
\ell
replaced by

s+\ell
, we get

(2.8)

\sum_{a\in S\cap P}a^{s+\ell}=\frac{t}{n}\sum_{a\in P}a^{s+\ell}+O_{s,\ell,m}(n^{s+\ell}E).

Combining (
2.7
) and
(
2.8
) proves (
1.6
).

Taking
I=[1,n]
and using

(2.9)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a\leq n\\
a\equiv r\,(\mathrm{mod}\,m)\end{subarray}}a^{s+\ell}=\frac{1}{m(s+\ell+1)}n^{s+\ell+1}+O_{s,\ell,m}(n^{s+\ell})

gives (
1.7
). The extra term obtained after
multiplication by
t^{s+1}/n^{s+1}
is

O(t^{s+1}n^{\ell-1})
, which is absorbed by the stated error term.
∎

Proof of
Corollary
5
.

By (
1.1
), if
t=S_{n}
, then

\Phi(S,n)\ll n^{1/2}\left(n^{-19/80}+n^{-1/4}\right)^{1/2}\ll n^{61/160}.

Substituting this bound into
Theorem
4
, with

I=[1,n]
, gives

(2.10)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq t\\
a_{i}\equiv r\,(\mathrm{mod}\,m)\end{subarray}}i^{s}a_{i}^{\ell}=\frac{t^{s+1}}{m(s+\ell+1)}n^{\ell}+O\left(n^{\ell+s/2+61/160}\log n\right).

Finally,

(2.11)

t^{s+1}=n^{(s+1)/2}+O_{s}\left(n^{s/2+21/80}\right),

again by (
1.1
). This replacement error is
absorbed by the error term in
(
2.10
). Combining
(
2.10
) and
(
2.11
) gives (
1.8
).
∎

2.4.
Proof of the almost all theorem

Lemma 10
.

Let
N\geq 2
and
\varepsilon>0
. For all but

O(N^{4/5+\varepsilon})
integers
n\leq N
, one has

S_{n}=n^{1/2}+O\left(n^{1/4}\right).

The exceptional set constant depends on
\varepsilon
, and the implied
constant in the displayed estimate is absolute.

Proof.

The upper bound
S_{n}\leq n^{1/2}+O(n^{1/4})
is the classical theorem
of Erdős and Turán. We prove the corresponding lower bound for almost all

n
, following the prime gap argument used by Balasubramanian and
Dutta
[
1
]
.

Let
p_{j}
be the
j
-th prime and put
g_{j}=p_{j+1}-p_{j}
.
Heath-Brown’s theorem
[
7
, Theorem 1]
gives, for every

\varepsilon>0
,

(2.12)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{j}\leq x\\
g_{j}\geq p_{j}^{1/2}\end{subarray}}g_{j}\ll x^{3/5+\varepsilon},

with the implied constant depending on
\varepsilon
. If

p_{j}^{2}-1\leq n<p_{j+1}^{2}-1

and
g_{j}<p_{j}^{1/2}
, Singer’s construction gives
S_{n}\geq p_{j}
, and
also

n^{1/2}-p_{j}<p_{j+1}-p_{j}=g_{j}<p_{j}^{1/2}\ll n^{1/4}.

Thus
S_{n}\geq n^{1/2}-O(n^{1/4})
for all such
n
.

It remains to count the
n\leq N
lying in intervals belonging to a bad
gap
g_{j}\geq p_{j}^{1/2}
. Their number is at most

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{j}\leq\sqrt{N+1}\\
g_{j}\geq p_{j}^{1/2}\end{subarray}}\left(p_{j+1}^{2}-p_{j}^{2}\right)\ll\sqrt{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{j}\leq\sqrt{N+1}\\
g_{j}\geq p_{j}^{1/2}\end{subarray}}g_{j}\ll N^{4/5+\varepsilon},

where Bertrand’s postulate is used in the middle estimate and
(
2.12
) is used in the final estimate. The
final implied constant depends on
\varepsilon
. This proves the lemma.
∎

Proof of
Theorem
6
.

For all
n\leq N
outside the exceptional set in

Lemma
10
, a maximal Sidon set satisfies

\left|1-\frac{|S|}{n^{1/2}}\right|\ll n^{-1/4}.

Hence
\Phi(S,n)\ll n^{3/8}
. Applying

Theorem
4
, with
I=[1,n]
, gives

(2.13)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq t\\
