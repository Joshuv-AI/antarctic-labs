VORT: Adaptive Power-Law Memory for NLP Transformers
Nabil Mlaiki

Abstract
Standard Transformers impose near-exponential decay on the influence of distant tokens, conflicting with the power-law structure of long-range dependencies in natural language. We introduce the Variable-Order Retention Transformer ( vort ), a memory architecture in which each ingested token is assigned a learnable fractional order α i ∈ [ δ , 1 ] \alpha_{i}\in[\delta,1] that governs a Grünwald–Letnikov power-law retention kernel. Because the fractional weighted sum is non-Markovian, we approximate it through a sum-of-exponentials (SOE) decomposition computed by Gauss–Laguerre quadrature on a Laplace-type integral representation of the kernel weights. Each exponential component admits a one-step Markovian recurrence at O ⁡ ( S ​ d v ) O(Sd_{v}) per step, where S = O ⁡ ( log ⁡ ( T / ε ) ) S=O(\log(T/\varepsilon)) terms suffice for ε \varepsilon -uniform accuracy on horizon [ 1 , T ] [1,T] . Retrieval is keyed and associative via a linear-attention accumulator with an exact O ⁡ ( K ​ S ​ d ϕ ​ d v ) O(KSd_{\phi}d_{v}) -per-step recurrence. Four results are established: (i) an SOE approximation theorem with geometric convergence rate from the analyticity of the integrand after a log-change of variables; (ii) a quantisation bound valid on [ δ , 1 ] [\delta,1] with correct analysis near α = 0 \alpha=0 ; (iii) a direct L 2 L^{2} energy argument (Proposition) showing that for α > 1 / 2 \alpha>1/2 any mixture with fixed minimum decay rate Λ > 0 \Lambda>0 incurs L 2 ​ ( [ 1 , T ] ) L^{2}([1,T]) error at least N α ​ ( T ) − C ⁡ ( Λ ) → ∞ N_{\alpha}(T)-C(\Lambda)\to\infty , with the Λ \Lambda -dependence made explicit; and (iv) linear convergence of a gradient plasticity rule under the Polyak–Łojasiewicz condition. Two synthetic experiments confirm the architectural advantage: a Zipf-distributed retrieval benchmark and an entity label-copy task with uniform lag distribution, the latter ruling out prior-matching as an explanation for the power-law kernel’s advantage.

Background

Throughout,
d\in\mathbb{N}
is embedding dimension,
n
is sequence length,
\Gamma
the Euler
gamma function,
B
the beta function, and
\psi=\Gamma^{\prime}/\Gamma
the digamma function.

Grünwald–Letnikov fractional sum.

For
\alpha\in(0,1)
the GL weighted sum of a sequence
\{f_{t}\}
is

\bigl(I^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{GL}}f\bigr)(t):=\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}w_{j}^{(\alpha)}\,f_{t-j},\qquad w_{j}^{(\alpha)}:=\frac{\Gamma(j+\alpha)}{\Gamma(\alpha)\,\Gamma(j+1)},

(2)

with
w_{j}^{(\alpha)}>0
for all
j\geq 0
and
\alpha>0
.
By Stirling’s formula,

w_{j}^{(\alpha)}\sim\frac{j^{\alpha-1}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\quad\text{as }j\to\infty,

(3)

so the kernel is heavy-tailed:
\sum_{j\geq 0}w_{j}^{(\alpha)}=\infty
for all
\alpha>0

(see Figure
4
a for log-log plots confirming the algebraic slope
\alpha-1
).
The
z
-transform identity

W_{\alpha}(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}w_{j}^{(\alpha)}\,z^{j}=(1-z)^{-\alpha},\quad|z|<1,

(4)

follows from the generalised binomial series
[
30
,
33
]
.

Non-Markovian structure.

The GL sum (
2
) has no exact two-term recurrence
S_{t}=c\,S_{t-1}+b\,f_{t}

with fixed scalars
c,b
: any such recurrence has
z
-transform
b/(1-cz)
, a rational
function, while
(1-z)^{-\alpha}
is irrational for
\alpha\notin\mathbb{Z}
.
More generally, no rational function of any finite degree equals
(1-z)^{-\alpha}

identically, since the latter has a branch point at
z=1
and infinitely many non-zero
Taylor coefficients.

Long-memory processes.

A stationary process
\{X_{t}\}
has long memory with parameter
d\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2})
if

S(\lambda)\sim c_{0}|\lambda|^{-2d}
near
\lambda=0

[
4
]
.
The ARFIMA
(0,d,0)
model
[
14
,
19
]
generates such
processes via
(1-B)^{d}X_{t}=\varepsilon_{t}
.
Its optimal one-step predictor has coefficients
\pi_{j}\sim j^{d-1}/\Gamma(d)

[
5
]
, establishing that statistically optimal prediction
of long-memory sequences requires power-law weights.

3
The VORT Mechanism

Sum-of-Exponentials Approximation

The GL weight admits the Laplace-type integral representation

w_{j}^{(\alpha)}=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda j}\,\rho_{\alpha}(\lambda)\,d\lambda,\qquad\rho_{\alpha}(\lambda):=\frac{e^{-\alpha\lambda}(1-e^{-\lambda})^{-\alpha}e^{-\lambda}}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(1-\alpha)},

(5)

obtained from the beta-function representation
w_{j}^{(\alpha)}=B(j,1-\alpha)/B(\alpha,1-\alpha)

via the substitution
\lambda=-\log\mu
.
The density
\rho_{\alpha}
satisfies
\rho_{\alpha}\geq 0
,

\rho_{\alpha}(\lambda)\sim\lambda^{-\alpha}/(\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(1-\alpha))
as
\lambda\to 0^{+}
,
and
\rho_{\alpha}(\lambda)\leq Ce^{-\lambda}
for large
\lambda
.

An
S
-point SOE approximation is

\hat{w}_{j}^{(\alpha)}=\sum_{s=1}^{S}c_{s}^{(\alpha)}\,\bigl(\lambda_{s}^{(\alpha)}\bigr)^{j},\qquad c_{s}^{(\alpha)}>0,\quad\lambda_{s}^{(\alpha)}\in(0,1),

(6)

where
\lambda_{s}=e^{-\xi_{s}}
,
c_{s}=\omega_{s}\rho_{\alpha}(\xi_{s})
for quadrature nodes

\xi_{s}>0
and weights
\omega_{s}>0
.
Each term
c_{s}(\lambda_{s})^{j}
is a decaying geometric sequence, so the auxiliary state

\mathbf{M}_{t}^{(s)}=\lambda_{s}\,\mathbf{M}_{t-1}^{(s)}+c_{s}\,\mathbf{v}_{t},\qquad s=1,\ldots,S,\quad\mathbf{M}_{0}^{(s)}=\mathbf{0},

(7)

is an exact one-step Markovian update.
The approximated fractional state
\hat{\mathbf{M}}_{t}^{(\alpha)}=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\mathbf{M}_{t}^{(s)}

approximates
\mathbf{M}_{t}^{(\alpha)}=\sum_{i=1}^{t}w_{t-i}^{(\alpha)}\mathbf{v}_{i}
with error
bounded in Theorem
3.1
.

Theorem 3.1

(SOE approximation; geometric convergence)
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,
T\geq 2
.
Set
\lambda_{\min}=\varepsilon/(2T)
and
\lambda_{\max}=2\log(2T/\varepsilon)
.
The truncation error outside
[\lambda_{\min},\lambda_{\max}]
satisfies

\int_{0}^{\lambda_{\min}}\rho_{\alpha}(\lambda)\,d\lambda\leq\varepsilon/4
and

\int_{\lambda_{\max}}^{\infty}\rho_{\alpha}(\lambda)\,d\lambda\leq\varepsilon/4

for all
j\in[0,T]
.
Under the substitution
u=\log(\lambda/\lambda_{\min})
, the integrand becomes
real-analytic on
[0,L]
where
L=\log(\lambda_{\max}/\lambda_{\min})=O(\log(T/\varepsilon))
,
and extends analytically into the strip
\{u+iv:|v|<\pi/2\}
(the nearest singularity
of
\rho_{\alpha}
in the
\lambda
-plane is at
\lambda=2\pi i
, which under

\lambda=\lambda_{\min}e^{u+iv}
corresponds to
v=\pi/2
in the
u
-plane, as shown in the proof).
Choosing

S=O\!\left(\log\frac{T}{\varepsilon}\right)

(8)

Gauss–Legendre nodes gives quadrature error
\leq\varepsilon/2
, so

\max_{0\leq j\leq T}|\hat{w}_{j}^{(\alpha)}-w_{j}^{(\alpha)}|\leq\varepsilon
.
Figure
4
b illustrates this geometric convergence empirically for

\alpha=0.5
,
T=1000
.

Proof.

Truncation.

For the lower tail:
\rho_{\alpha}(\lambda)\leq C\lambda^{-\alpha}
near
\lambda=0
gives

\int_{0}^{\lambda_{\min}}\rho_{\alpha}\,d\lambda\leq C\lambda_{\min}^{1-\alpha}/(1-\alpha)
,
which is
\leq\varepsilon/4
for
\lambda_{\min}=(\varepsilon(1-\alpha)/(4C))^{1/(1-\alpha)}
;
the choice
\lambda_{\min}=\varepsilon/(2T)
achieves this for
T\geq T_{0}(\alpha)
.
For the upper tail:
\rho_{\alpha}(\lambda)\leq Ce^{-\lambda}
gives

\int_{\lambda_{\max}}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda j}\rho_{\alpha}\,d\lambda\leq C\int_{\lambda_{\max}}^{\infty}e^{-2\lambda}\,d\lambda=(C/2)e^{-2\lambda_{\max}}=(C/2)(\varepsilon/(2T))^{2}\leq\varepsilon/4
for small
\varepsilon
.

Analyticity and quadrature.

Let
I(j)=\int_{\lambda_{\min}}^{\lambda_{\max}}e^{-\lambda j}\rho_{\alpha}(\lambda)\,d\lambda
.
Under
u=\log(\lambda/\lambda_{\min})
, the interval maps to
[0,L]
and the integrand becomes

g(u)=e^{-\lambda_{\min}e^{u}j}\rho_{\alpha}(\lambda_{\min}e^{u})\lambda_{\min}e^{u}
.
Since
\lambda_{\min}e^{u}\in[\lambda_{\min},\lambda_{\max}]
is bounded away from
the singularity of
\rho_{\alpha}
at
\lambda=0
, the function
g
is real-analytic
in
u\in[0,L]
.
To extend analytically into a complex strip, write
\lambda=\lambda_{\min}e^{u+iv}

for
v
real.
The singularities of
\rho_{\alpha}(\lambda)\propto(1-e^{-\lambda})^{-\alpha}

occur where
1-e^{-\lambda}=0
, i.e. at
\lambda_{*}=2\pi ik
for

k\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
.
The nearest singularity to the real segment
\lambda\in[\lambda_{\min},\lambda_{\max}]

is
\lambda_{*}=2\pi i
(at
|{\lambda_{*}}|=2\pi
).
Under the substitution, the condition
\lambda_{\min}e^{u+iv}=2\pi i
becomes

e^{u+iv}=2\pi i/\lambda_{\min}
, i.e.
v=\pi/2+2k\pi
for the principal value.
The nearest singularity in the
u
-plane occurs at
v=\pi/2-\arg(\lambda_{\min}/2\pi)
;
for real
\lambda_{\min}>0
this gives
v=\pi/2
.
Hence
g(u+iv)
is analytic for
|v|<\pi/2
, and bounded there by some
M_{g}<\infty

(since
\lambda_{\min}e^{u}
stays in the compact set
[\lambda_{\min},\lambda_{\max}]
for

u\in[0,L]
and
|v|<\pi/2
, where
\rho_{\alpha}
has no singularities).
The
S
-point Gauss–Legendre error for functions analytic in a strip of half-width

\pi/2
on
[0,L]
and bounded by
M_{g}
satisfies

[
27
,
17
]
:

|I(j)-\hat{I}_{S}(j)|\leq\frac{4M_{g}L\,e^{-\pi^{2}S/L}}{e^{\pi^{2}S/L}-1},

(9)

which is
\leq\varepsilon/2
for

S\geq\frac{L}{\pi^{2}}\log\!\left(\frac{8M_{g}L}{\varepsilon}\right)=O\!\left(\log\frac{T}{\varepsilon}\right).

(10)

Setting
\xi_{s}=\lambda_{\min}e^{u_{s}}
and
c_{s}=\omega_{s}\rho_{\alpha}(\xi_{s})

with Gauss–Legendre weights
\omega_{s}>0
gives the SOE with

\lambda_{s}=e^{-\xi_{s}}\in(0,1)
. ∎∎

Per-Token Routing and Bank States

Each token
\mathbf{x}_{i}
is assigned a fractional order

\alpha_{i}=\delta+(1-\delta)\,\sigma\!\left(W_{\alpha}\bigl[\mathbf{x}_{i};\;H(\mathbf{a}_{i});\;\mathbf{e}_{i}\bigr]+b_{\alpha}\right)\in[\delta,1],

(11)

where
\sigma
is the logistic sigmoid,
\delta>0
is a minimum order (avoiding
the singularity of
\Gamma
at
0
),
H(\mathbf{a}_{i})=-\sum_{j}a_{ij}\log a_{ij}
is the
Shannon entropy of the attention distribution at position
i
, and
\mathbf{e}_{i}\in\{0,1\}

is an entity indicator from a shallow NER probe.
High-entropy tokens (contextually ambiguous) receive low
\alpha_{i}
and are compressed
aggressively; entity tokens receive high
\alpha_{i}
for near-lossless retention
(illustrated in Figure
2
b).

For efficiency,
K
parallel banks operate at fixed orders
\alpha_{k}=\delta+(1-\delta)k/K
,

k=1,\ldots,K
.
Token
i
is routed to bank
k_{i}^{*}=\argmin_{k}|\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{k}|
.
Bank
k
accumulates values of tokens assigned to it:

\mathbf{M}_{t}^{(k)}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\leq t,\;k_{i}^{*}=k\end{subarray}}\hat{w}_{t-i}^{(\alpha_{k})}\,\mathbf{v}_{i}=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\mathbf{M}_{t}^{(k,s)},\qquad\mathbf{M}_{t}^{(k,s)}=\lambda_{s}^{(k)}\,\mathbf{M}_{t-1}^{(k,s)}+c_{s}^{(k)}\,\mathbf{1}[k_{t}^{*}=k]\,\mathbf{v}_{t},

(12)

updated in
O(Sd_{v})
per step.

Keyed Linear-Attention Retrieval

Let
\mathbf{v}_{i}=V\mathbf{x}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{v}}
,
\mathbf{k}_{i}=K_{P}\mathbf{x}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{k}}
(fixed at ingestion),

\mathbf{q}_{t}=Q\mathbf{x}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{k}}
.
The decayed value of token
i
at time
t
is

\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{i}(t)=\hat{w}_{t-i}^{(\alpha_{i})}\mathbf{v}_{i}=\sum_{s=1}^{S}c_{s}^{(\alpha_{i})}(\lambda_{s}^{(\alpha_{i})})^{t-i}\mathbf{v}_{i}
.
The retrieval output is

\mathbf{o}_{t}=\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{i<t}\exp\!\bigl(\mathbf{q}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{k}_{i}/\sqrt{d_{k}}\bigr)\cdot\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{i}(t)}{\displaystyle\sum_{i<t}\exp\!\bigl(\mathbf{q}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{k}_{i}/\sqrt{d_{k}}\bigr)+\varepsilon_{0}},

(13)

with smoothing constant
\varepsilon_{0}>0
.
Using the random-feature approximation
\exp(\mathbf{q}^{\top}\mathbf{k}/\sqrt{d_{k}})\approx\phi(\mathbf{q})^{\top}\phi(\mathbf{k})

[
8
]
, define

\displaystyle\mathbf{G}_{t}^{(k,s)}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{s}^{(k)}\,\mathbf{G}_{t-1}^{(k,s)}+c_{s}^{(k)}\,\mathbf{1}[k_{t}^{*}=k]\,\phi(\mathbf{k}_{t})\,\mathbf{v}_{t}^{\top}\;\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\phi}\times d_{v}},

(14)

\displaystyle\mathbf{b}_{t}^{(k,s)}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{s}^{(k)}\,\mathbf{b}_{t-1}^{(k,s)}+c_{s}^{(k)}\,\mathbf{1}[k_{t}^{*}=k]\,\phi(\mathbf{k}_{t})\;\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\phi}},

(15)

with all initial states zero, costing
O(d_{\phi}d_{v})
and
O(d_{\phi})
per step respectively.
The output (
13
) becomes

\mathbf{o}_{t}=\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{k,s}\phi(\mathbf{q}_{t})^{\top}\mathbf{G}_{t}^{(k,s)}}{\displaystyle\sum_{k,s}\phi(\mathbf{q}_{t})^{\top}\mathbf{b}_{t}^{(k,s)}+\varepsilon_{0}},

(16)

computed in
O(KSd_{\phi}d_{v})
per step.
The full
vort
layer combines this with local attention:

\mathrm{\textsc{vort}}(\mathbf{x}_{t})=\mathbf{o}_{t}+\mathrm{Attn}_{\mathrm{local}}(\mathbf{x}_{t}).

(17)

Complexity.

Per position: bank updates (
12
) cost
O(KSd_{v})
; retrieval
accumulator updates (
14
)–(
15
) cost
O(KSd_{\phi}d_{v})
;
routing (
11
) costs
O(d^{2})
.
Total auxiliary memory:
O(KSd_{\phi}d_{v}L)
for
L
layers.
With
K=10
,
S=15
,

,

approximately

Quantisation Bound

Theorem 4.1

(Kernel quantisation error on
[\delta,1]
)
.

Let
0<\delta\leq\alpha<\beta\leq 1
,
\Delta=\beta-\alpha
, and

k_{\gamma}(t)=t^{\gamma-1}/\Gamma(\gamma)
for
t\geq 1
.
Then for all
t\geq 1
and all
\alpha,\beta\in[\delta,1]
:

\left|k_{\beta}(t)-k_{\alpha}(t)\right|\leq\frac{\Delta\,t^{\beta-1}}{\Gamma(\beta)}\Bigl(\left|\log t\right|+\sup_{\gamma\in[\delta,1]}\left|\psi(\gamma)\right|\Bigr),

(18)

where
\sup_{\gamma\in[\delta,1]}|\psi(\gamma)|<\infty
.
For the uniform grid with spacing
1/K
:

\sup_{\alpha\in[\delta,1]}|k_{\alpha}(t)-k_{\alpha_{k^{*}}}(t)|=O((\log t)/K)
.

Near
\alpha=0
: since
\Gamma(\alpha)\sim 1/\alpha
as
\alpha\to 0^{+}
, we have

k_{\alpha}(t)=t^{\alpha-1}/\Gamma(\alpha)\sim\alpha t^{-1}\to 0
for any fixed
t\geq 1
.
The kernel shrinks to zero as
\alpha\to 0^{+}
, not to infinity.

Proof.

Write
k_{\gamma}(t)=\exp((\gamma-1)\log t-\log\Gamma(\gamma))
.
Differentiating in
\gamma
:

\partial k_{\gamma}/\partial\gamma=k_{\gamma}(t)(\log t-\psi(\gamma))
.
The mean-value theorem yields
\xi\in(\alpha,\beta)
with

k_{\beta}(t)-k_{\alpha}(t)=\Delta\cdot k_{\xi}(t)(\log t-\psi(\xi))
.
Since
t\geq 1
and
\xi\leq\beta
:
t^{\xi-1}\leq t^{\beta-1}
.
On
[\delta,1]
,
\Gamma
is continuous, so
1/\Gamma(\xi)\leq 1/\Gamma_{\min}(\delta)<\infty
.
For
\xi\leq\beta
and
\Gamma
decreasing on
(0,x^{*})\supset[\delta,1]
(where
x^{*}\approx 1.46
),
we have
\Gamma(\xi)\geq\Gamma(\beta)
, so
k_{\xi}(t)\leq t^{\beta-1}/\Gamma(\beta)
.
Taking absolute values and using
|\psi(\xi)|\leq\sup_{[\delta,1]}|\psi|<\infty
gives
(
18
). ∎∎

Lower Bound: Power Laws vs. Fixed Exponential Mixtures

The following theorem is proved by a self-contained direct argument using only
the
L^{2}
inner product, the Laplace transform, and basic complex analysis.
It avoids Stieltjes-function theory, Kolmogorov widths, and equioscillation theorems.

Proposition 4.2

(Scaling lower bound on exponential-mixture error)
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
g_{\alpha}(t)=t^{\alpha-1}
for
t\geq 1
, and

f(t)=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\pi_{m}e^{-\lambda_{m}t}
with
\pi_{m}\geq 0
,
\sum_{m}\pi_{m}=C_{f}
,

\lambda_{m}>0
.
Set
\Lambda:=\min_{m}\lambda_{m}>0
and
R:=\sum_{m,m^{\prime}}\pi_{m}\pi_{m^{\prime}}/(\lambda_{m}+\lambda_{m^{\prime}})
.
Define

N_{\alpha}(T):=\int_{1}^{T}t^{2\alpha-2}\,dt=\begin{cases}\dfrac{T^{2\alpha-1}-1}{2\alpha-1}&\alpha\neq\tfrac{1}{2},\\[6.0pt]
\log T&\alpha=\tfrac{1}{2}.\end{cases}

(19)

Then for all
T\geq 1
:

\int_{1}^{T}\bigl(f(t)-g_{\alpha}(t)\bigr)^{2}\,dt\;\geq\;N_{\alpha}(T)-2C_{f}\,\Gamma(\alpha)\,\Lambda^{-\alpha}-C_{f}^{2}\,R.

(20)

For
\alpha>1/2
the right-hand side grows as
T^{2\alpha-1}/(2\alpha-1)
minus the

fixed
constants
2C_{f}\Gamma(\alpha)\Lambda^{-\alpha}
and
C_{f}^{2}R
, so it
diverges as
T\to\infty
for any mixture with
\Lambda>0
.
For
\alpha=1/2
it grows as
\log T
.

Important caveat:
the correction term
2C_{f}\Gamma(\alpha)\Lambda^{-\alpha}

is
O(\Lambda^{-\alpha})
and diverges as
\Lambda\to 0^{+}
; the bound is therefore
useful only for mixtures whose smallest rate
\Lambda
is bounded away from zero.
Exponential components with
\lambda_{m}\to 0
approach constant functions and can
partially mimic
g_{\alpha}
near
t=1
; preventing this requires either
\Lambda>0

fixed or a separate treatment of the near-constant component.

Proof.

Expand the squared error directly:

\int_{1}^{T}(f-g_{\alpha})^{2}\,dt=\int_{1}^{T}g_{\alpha}^{2}\,dt-2\int_{1}^{T}f\cdot g_{\alpha}\,dt+\int_{1}^{T}f^{2}\,dt.

(21)

The first term is
N_{\alpha}(T)
by definition.
For the cross term, each summand satisfies

\int_{1}^{T}e^{-\lambda_{m}t}t^{\alpha-1}\,dt\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda_{m}t}t^{\alpha-1}\,dt=\Gamma(\alpha)\lambda_{m}^{-\alpha}\leq\Gamma(\alpha)\Lambda^{-\alpha},

(22)

so
\int_{1}^{T}f\cdot g_{\alpha}\,dt\leq C_{f}\Gamma(\alpha)\Lambda^{-\alpha}
.
For the quadratic term,

\int_{1}^{T}f^{2}\,dt\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}f^{2}\,dt=\sum_{m,m^{\prime}}\pi_{m}\pi_{m^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(\lambda_{m}+\lambda_{m^{\prime}})t}\,dt=\sum_{m,m^{\prime}}\frac{\pi_{m}\pi_{m^{\prime}}}{\lambda_{m}+\lambda_{m^{\prime}}}=:C_{f}^{2}R(T),

(23)

where
R(T)\leq R=\sum_{m,m^{\prime}}\pi_{m}\pi_{m^{\prime}}/(\lambda_{m}+\lambda_{m^{\prime}})
is a fixed constant
for any fixed mixture.
Since
\int_{1}^{T}f^{2}\,dt\geq 0
, substituting (
22
)–(
23
) into
(
21
) gives (
20
).

For
\alpha>1/2
:
N_{\alpha}(T)\to\infty
while the subtracted terms

2C_{f}\Gamma(\alpha)\Lambda^{-\alpha}
and
C_{f}^{2}R
are constants in
T
.
Hence the right-hand side of (
20
) eventually becomes positive and
diverges, establishing that
\int_{1}^{T}(f-g_{\alpha})^{2}\,dt\to\infty
.

For
\alpha=1/2
:
N_{1/2}(T)=\log T\to\infty
at rate
O(\log T)
; the same
argument applies.

For
\alpha<1/2
:
N_{\alpha}(T)\to 1/(1-2\alpha)<\infty
, so (
20
)
gives a positive constant lower bound
1/(1-2\alpha)-2C_{f}\Gamma(\alpha)\Lambda^{-\alpha}-C_{f}^{2}R
, which is positive when
\Lambda
is large enough (i.e. when all exponentials
decay fast enough that their inner product with
g_{\alpha}
is small).
The bound is not claimed to be tight for all
\Lambda
. ∎∎

Remark 4.3
.

The bound (
20
) is a direct energy argument, not a dimension-counting argument.
Thus parameter counting alone does not give approximation lower bounds for nonlinear families, and the above proof avoids this by working entirely in
L^{2}([1,T])
.
The case
\alpha>1/2
is the empirically relevant one for
vort
: tokens routed to high-
\alpha
banks (
\alpha_{i}>0.5
) are precisely those for which a fixed-capacity exponential model has increasing error as context length increases.
For
\alpha\leq 1/2
, the conclusion is weaker, but still guarantees a positive floor on the approximation error.
A fully rigorous lower bound uniform in the mixture parameters
\{\pi_{m},\lambda_{m}\}
(rather than pointwise in
\Lambda=\min_{m}\lambda_{m}
) would need tools from rational approximation theory
[
13
]
and is left to future work.

Bank-State Growth

Proposition 4.4

(Bank-state growth)
.

Let
\left\|\mathbf{v}_{t}\right\|\leq V
for all
t
.
For fixed
\alpha\in(0,1)
:

\mathbf{M}_{t}^{(\alpha)}=\sum_{i=1}^{t}w_{t-i}^{(\alpha)}\mathbf{v}_{i}
satisfies

\left\|\mathbf{M}_{t}^{(\alpha)}\right\|\leq V\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}w_{j}^{(\alpha)}\sim Vt^{\alpha}/\Gamma(\alpha+1)
,
so
\left\|\mathbf{M}_{t}^{(\alpha)}\right\|=O(t^{\alpha})
, sublinear for all
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

Proof.

The triangle inequality gives
\left\|\mathbf{M}_{t}^{(\alpha)}\right\|\leq V\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}w_{j}^{(\alpha)}
.
By the hockey-stick identity for generalised binomials,

\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}w_{j}^{(\alpha)}=\binom{t+\alpha-1}{t-1}
.
Stirling gives
\binom{t+\alpha-1}{t-1}=\Gamma(t+\alpha)/(\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(t+1))\sim t^{\alpha-1}/\Gamma(\alpha)\cdot t=t^{\alpha}/\Gamma(\alpha)
.
Using
\alpha\Gamma(\alpha)=\Gamma(\alpha+1)
gives the asymptotic
t^{\alpha}/\Gamma(\alpha+1)
. ∎∎

5
Gradient-Driven Memory Plasticity

After initial routing,
\alpha_{i}
is refined by retrieval feedback.
The retrieval score of token
i
for query at position
t
is

r_{it}=\exp(\mathbf{q}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{k}_{i}/\sqrt{d_{k}})\cdot\hat{w}_{t-i}^{(\alpha_{i})}
,
normalised weight
p_{it}=r_{it}/\sum_{i^{\prime}<t}r_{i^{\prime}t}
,
and the plasticity loss is

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ret}}=-\sum_{t}\log p_{i^{*}(t),t},

(24)

where
i^{*}(t)
is the ground-truth source token.
The gradient
\partial\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ret}}/\partial\alpha_{i}
is computed through

\hat{w}_{t-i}^{(\alpha_{i})}=\sum_{s}c_{s}(\alpha_{i})\lambda_{s}(\alpha_{i})^{t-i}
via the
SOE parametrisation, where
\partial c_{s}/\partial\alpha
and
\partial\lambda_{s}/\partial\alpha

are computable from the quadrature construction.

Theorem 5.1

(Plasticity convergence)
.

Let
F(\alpha)=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ret}}(\alpha)
for
\alpha\in[\delta,1]
.
Suppose
F
is
L
-smooth and satisfies the Polyak–Łojasiewicz condition

|F^{\prime}(\alpha)|^{2}\geq 2\mu(F(\alpha)-F^{*})
for some
\mu>0
and
F^{*}=\inf F
.
Under gradient descent with step
\eta\in(0,1/L]
:

F(\alpha^{(l)})-F^{*}\leq(1-\eta\mu)^{l}(F(\alpha^{(0)})-F^{*}).

(25)

Proof.

By
L
-smoothness at the iterate
\alpha^{(l+1)}=\alpha^{(l)}-\eta F^{\prime}(\alpha^{(l)})
:

F(\alpha^{(l+1)})\leq F(\alpha^{(l)})-\eta\!\left(1-\frac{\eta L}{2}\right)|F^{\prime}(\alpha^{(l)})|^{2}.

(26)

Since
\eta\leq 1/L
, the factor
\eta(1-\eta L/2)\geq\eta/2>0
.
The PL condition gives
|F^{\prime}(\alpha^{(l)})|^{2}\geq 2\mu(F(\alpha^{(l)})-F^{*})
, so

F(\alpha^{(l+1)})-F^{*}\leq(1-2\mu\eta(1-\eta L/2))(F(\alpha^{(l)})-F^{*})\leq(1-\mu\eta)(F(\alpha^{(l)})-F^{*})
.
Iterating gives (
25
). ∎∎

Remark 5.2
.

The PL condition holds locally near any strict local minimum
\alpha^{*}
of a smooth
F
:
if
F^{\prime}(\alpha^{*})=0
and
F^{\prime\prime}(\alpha^{*})>0
, then
|F^{\prime}(\alpha)|^{2}\geq F^{\prime\prime}(\alpha^{*})(F(\alpha)-F(\alpha^{*}))

in a neighbourhood of
\alpha^{*}
(the PL condition with
2\mu=F^{\prime\prime}(\alpha^{*})
).
Global convexity is not required.

8
Discussion

Summability and finite horizons.

The theoretical GL kernel is non-summable (
\sum_{j\geq 0}w_{j}^{(\alpha)}=\infty
)
and this is used to motivate the power-law design.
In the actual
vort
implementation, the SOE approximation operates on a finite horizon

[1,T]
with
S
terms chosen for that horizon, so the effective kernel is a finite
exponential mixture and is summable.
The distinction matters philosophically: the theoretical non-summability justifies why
no
fixed
-capacity exponential model can track the kernel as
T
grows
(Proposition
4.2
), but in practice the SOE is re-calibrated for each
deployment context, adapting its capacity to the operating horizon.

Relation to state-space models.

S4
[
16
]
parameterises a diagonal LTI system with exponential impulse
responses—exactly the SOE structure used in each of
vort
’s banks.
The architectural difference is that
vort
’s fractional order is per-token and
content-adaptive, whereas S4’s poles are global and fixed.
Mamba
[
15
]
introduces input-dependent selection via the discretisation
step
\Delta_{t}
, which modulates the time constant within an exponential envelope;
the resulting kernel remains summable, whereas
vort
’s GL kernel with
\alpha>0

is non-summable (Equation (
3
)).

Relation to linear attention.

The retrieval formula (
13
) is a variant of linear attention

[
22
]
in which each token’s value contribution is modulated
by the fractional retention factor
\hat{w}_{t-i}^{(\alpha_{i})}
.
This modification and the standard kernel-feature approximation are orthogonal.

Relation to positional encodings.

ALiBi
[
34
]
and RoPE
[
41
]
modulate attention scores
by position-dependent biases but do not alter the memory accumulation rule.

vort
operates at the accumulation level and is complementary: a positional bias
can be added inside (
13
) without changing the fractional mechanism.

Limitations.

The PL convergence in Theorem
5.1
is local.
The NER probe adds a pipeline dependency.
For tasks with predominantly short-range dependencies, the fractional component adds
overhead without benefit.
The lower bound of Proposition
4.2
is tight only for
\alpha>1/2
; for

\alpha<1/2
it gives a positive but bounded lower bound.
The pilot experiment uses short sequences (
n=10{,}000
) and a classification setting;
behaviour at
n\geq 10^{5}
and on language modelling remains to be validated.

9
Conclusion

The Variable-Order Retention Transformer assigns each token a learnable
Grünwald–Letnikov fractional retention kernel of order
\alpha_{i}\in[\delta,1]
,
realised through a sum-of-exponentials decomposition that converts the non-Markovian
fractional sum into
S=O(\log(T/\varepsilon))
exact Markovian auxiliary recurrences.
Retrieval is keyed and associative via explicit linear-attention accumulators
(
14
)–(
15
) at
O(KSd_{\phi}d_{v})
per step.
The four results provide theoretical support for the mechanism:
Theorem
3.1
gives geometric convergence of the SOE from the analyticity of the
log-transformed integrand;
Theorem
4.1
gives a quantisation bound on
[\delta,1]
with correct

\alpha\to 0^{+}
behaviour (
k_{\alpha}(t)\to 0
, not
\infty
);
Proposition
4.2
gives a direct energy argument showing that for

\alpha>1/2
any
M
-term exponential mixture with fixed minimum decay rate incurs

L^{2}([1,T])
error growing with
T
; the proof is self-contained and avoids parameter-
counting arguments that are invalid for nonlinear families, while noting that a fully
mixture-parameter-uniform bound would require rational approximation theory

[
13
]
;
and Theorem
5.1
establishes local linear convergence of the plasticity
update under the PL condition (Figure
3
b shows the
layer-wise convergence of
\alpha_{i}^{(l)}
for entity and non-entity tokens).
Two synthetic experiments—a Zipf-distributed retrieval task and an entity label-copy
task with uniform lags—confirm that power-law kernels retain retrieval accuracy at long
lags where exponential models degrade substantially (Figure
2
a), with the second experiment ruling out
prior-matching as an explanation.
The power-law retention mechanism is well-matched to the algebraic decay of long-range
dependencies in natural language, and the SOE architecture makes it computationally
competitive with standard linear-attention recurrences.
