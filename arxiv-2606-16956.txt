Multisymmetric polynomials on set-theoretic quiver representations
Radford Green, Cornell Holmes, Mee Seong Im

Abstract
. Eventually constant set-valued representations of a quiver are set-theoretic analogues of nilpotent representations. In recent work by Green–Holmes–Im, the authors enumerated eventually constant set-valued representations for equioriented cyclic quivers using the directed matrix-tree theorem. In this paper, we extend this enumeration to finite quivers without sinks for which every vertex is the target of sufficiently long paths. We encode the representations as directed acyclic graphs and introduce a recursive source-removal method for certain classes of directed acyclic graphs. This yields a strictly upper triangular matrix enumerator in the incidence algebra of the subset lattice. To compute the cardinality of the eventually constant representations, we compress this enumerator to a matrix indexed by cardinality vectors, the set-theoretic analogues of dimension vectors. We conclude by specializing the formulas to the Jordan quiver and recovering the multisymmetric generating polynomial for the cyclic quiver without using the matrix-tree theorem.

1.
Introduction

Cayley’s formula states that the number of unrooted trees with vertex set
V
of cardinality
n
is
n^{n-2}
. Rooted trees are trees together with a choice of a vertex (root), and there are
n^{n-1}
of them.
Tom Leinster showed eventually constant functions correspond to rooted trees in
[
10
]
, providing a conceptual proof that avoids calculation and algebraic manipulation of
q
-binomial coefficients and algebraic partitions. An endomorphism
f:V\to V
of a finite set is
eventually constant
if its iterated compositions
f^{m}=f\circ\cdots\circ f
eventually stabilize to a constant map. Since there are
n^{n}
self-maps of
V
, this shows that the probability that a function is eventually constant is
1/n
.
In
[
8
,
3
,
4
]
, the authors generalize the notion of eventual constancy. Instead of a single endomorphism
f
on one set
V
, they consider cyclically oriented
k
-tuples
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})
of maps across
k
disjoint nonempty finite sets
V_{1},\ldots,V_{k}
, where

V_{1}\xrightarrow{f_{1}}V_{2}\xrightarrow{f_{2}}\ldots\xrightarrow{f_{k-1}}V_{k}\xrightarrow{f_{k}}V_{1}.

In this setting, subscripts are taken modulo
k
, and a
transversal

C\subseteq\coprod_{i=1}^{k}V_{i}
is a subset that contains a single element
c_{i}
in each set
V_{i}
. A
k
-tuple
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})
of maps is
eventually constant
if there exists a transversal
C
such that all sufficiently long cyclic compositions
f_{i+n}\circ f_{i+n-1}\circ\cdots\circ f_{i+1}\circ f_{i}
map into
C
.

To derive a weighted enumeration of the eventually constant
k
-tuples, we identify each
k
-tuple with an oriented graph. Each oriented graph has a unique cycle along a transversal
C\subseteq\coprod_{i=1}^{k}V_{i}
, and removing its edges produces a forest rooted at
C
. The directed matrix-tree theorem
[
2
]
gives a determinantal formula for the weighted count of the resulting set of forests rooted at each transversal, and summing the cycle-weighted counts of the forests over all transversals yields the enumeration.

In this paper, we generalize eventual constancy from cyclically oriented maps to collections of sets and functions between them in any orientation, and then construct matrix enumerators. These collections of sets and oriented functions are the set-theoretic analogues of quiver representations,
[
5
,
6
]
. Furthermore, eventually constant set-theoretic representations are analogous to nilpotent linear representations of quivers
[
12
]
.

Fix a
quiver

Q:=(Q_{0},Q_{1})
, a directed graph, where
Q_{0}
is a finite set of vertices and
Q_{1}
is a finite set of arrows between them. We assume that
Q
has no sinks and that all vertices are the target of arbitrarily long paths.
Classically, a linear
representation

W
of
Q
is a collection
\{W_{a}\}_{a\in Q_{0}}
of finite dimensional vector spaces indexed by
a\in Q_{0}
, and a set
\{W_{\alpha}:W_{a}\to W_{b}\mid\alpha:a\to b\}
of linear maps indexed by
\alpha\in Q_{1}
.
Passing to the set-theoretic analogue, a
set-valued representation
replaces each finite dimensional vector space
W_{a}
with a finite set, and replaces each linear map
W_{\alpha}:W_{a}\to W_{b}
with a function. Equivalently, a linear representation is a functor from the path category on
Q
to the category of finite dimensional vector spaces, and a set-valued representation is a functor from this path category to the category of finite sets.

In place of the sets
V_{1},\ldots,V_{k}
from the cyclic case, we fix a tuple
V:=(V_{a})_{a\in Q_{0}}
of nonempty finite disjoint sets. Let
\overline{V}:=\coprod_{a\in Q_{0}}V_{a}
. Instead of the equioriented maps
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})
, we study the collection of set-valued representations of
Q
with the underlying
Q_{0}
-indexed set
V
. A set-valued representation
W
belongs to
\mathsf{SetRep}(Q,V)
if
W_{a}=V_{a}
for each
a\in Q_{0}
. A classical representation is
nilpotent
if all sufficiently long compositions are trivial. Since sets lack the zero object, we exchange nilpotency for constancy, and define a
transversal
of
V
as a subset
C\subseteq\overline{V}
that contains a single element
c_{a}
in each set
V_{a}
. A representation
W\in\mathsf{SetRep}(Q,V)
is
eventually constant
if there exists a transversal
C
such that for all sufficiently long paths
\alpha_{\ell}\cdots\alpha_{1}
in
Q
, the composition
W_{\alpha_{\ell}}\circ\cdots\circ W_{\alpha_{1}}
maps into
C
. Let
\mathsf{EC}
denote the subset of eventually constant representations in
\mathsf{SetRep}(Q,V)
.

To algebraically enumerate the eventually constant representations of
Q
on
V
, we construct a directed graph
G(Q)
on
\overline{V}
and identify each representation of
Q
on
V
with a directed spanning subgraph. After assigning each directed subgraph a monomial weight in a polynomial ring of commuting variables, we define the generating function
P(\mathbf{x})
as the sum of weights over all graphs corresponding to an eventually constant representation. As in the
k
-cyclic case, these directed graphs become acyclic after removing edges directed along a transversal. However, unlike the cyclic case, these graphs may have higher out-degree. As a result, representations induce sets of directed acyclic graphs (DAGs) rather than sets of forests, and tree-enumeration techniques no longer apply.

We weigh collections of DAGs via recursive source removal
[
7
,
14
]
. A
DAG assignment

\mathcal{D}
on the directed graph
G(Q)
assigns each subset
U\subseteq\overline{V}
a set
\mathcal{D}(U)
of directed acyclic subgraphs of
G(Q)
on the vertex set
U
. The assignment
\mathcal{D}
is
source-factorizable
if it satisfies certain axioms dictating how source edges can be attached to and removed from graphs in the assignment to form other graphs in the assignment. If
\mathcal{D}
is source-factorizable, then the weights of the source edges that can be attached to graphs are encoded by an
attaching weight matrix

M
. The matrix
M
is indexed over the power set
\mathcal{P}(\overline{V})
ordered by inclusion, and
M
is strictly upper triangular. For all subsets
S\subseteq U\subseteq\overline{V}
, the weight of the subset of DAGs in
\mathcal{D}(U)
with sink set
S\subseteq U
is given by the entry
(I-M)^{-1}_{S,U}
. See Theorem
3.2
, and Corollary
3.4
for a formulation with chains. Also, see
[
15
,
17
]
for more on incidence algebras. The eventually constant representations induce a source-factorizable family of DAGs, so we derive a matrix enumerator for
P(\mathbf{x})
in Section
4.3
.

We replace the dimension vector of a linear representation with the
cardinality vector
. For each subset
U\subseteq\overline{V}
, let
\mathbf{U}:=(|U\cap V_{a}|)_{a\in Q_{0}}\in\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}
. Then, the generating function for
\mathsf{EC}
gives an expression for
|\mathsf{EC}|
in terms of subsets
S\subseteq U
of
\overline{V}
, and the contribution from each pair
S
,
U
only depends on their cardinality vectors. This uniformity allows us to condense the strictly upper triangular matrix enumerator, passing from the matrix
M
indexed on subsets
U,\,S\subseteq\overline{V}
to the
attaching cardinality matrix

N
indexed on vectors
\mathbf{U},\,\mathbf{S}
. See Section
2.6
for an example.

To state the matrix

and the enumeration result, we establish structure on the set of cardinality vectors
\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}
. Equip
\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}
with the pointwise partial order, so that for each vector

, we have
\mathbf{c}\leq\mathbf{d}
if

c_{a}\leq d_{a}
for all vertices
a\in Q_{0}
. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathbf{n}:=(n)_{a\in Q_{0}}\in\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}
.

Let
\mathbf{0}=(0)_{a\in Q_{0}}
and

\mathbf{\overline{V}}=(|V_{a}|)_{a\in Q_{0}}
.
Define the interval between
\mathbf{0}
and

\mathbf{\overline{V}}
as

[\mathbf{0},\mathbf{\overline{V}}]=\prod_{a\in Q_{0}}[0,|V_{a}|]\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}
. Define exponentiation, the factorial, and

-norm on
\mathbf{c},\,\mathbf{d}\in[\mathbf{0},\mathbf{\overline{V}}]
, by

\mathbf{c}^{\mathbf{d}}:=\prod_{a\in Q_{0}}c_{a}^{d_{a}}\in\mathbb{N},\qquad\mathbf{c}!:=\prod_{a\in Q_{0}}c_{a}!\in\mathbb{N},\qquad|\mathbf{c}|:=\sum_{a\in Q_{0}}c_{a}\in\mathbb{N},

respectively.
Let
\mathsf{A}(Q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{0}\times Q_{0}}
be the

of

with entries in
[\mathbf{0},\mathbf{\overline{V}}]^{2}
, which is given by

(1)

2.1.
Introduction to graphs

Let
G=(G_{V},G_{E})
be a finite directed multigraph, where

G_{V}
is the set of vertices and
G_{E}
is the set of edges. Each edge
e\in G_{E}
is directed from a single vertex
s(e)\in G_{V}
called its source, to a single vertex
t(e)\in G_{V}
called its target. We permit multiple parallel edges and
loops
(edges from a vertex to itself).

An (oriented)
subgraph
of
G
is a graph
H=(H_{V},H_{E})
satisfying
H_{V}\subseteq G_{V}
and
H_{E}\subseteq G_{E}
, such that the sources and targets are inherited from
G
. We say a subgraph
H
is a
spanning subgraph
if the vertex sets are the same,
H_{V}=G_{V}
. Given a set
U
, we say a graph is
on

U
if it has vertex set
U
.

Let
H,G
be graphs with disjoint edge sets (but not necessarily disjoint vertex sets). Their
edge-disjoint union

H\star G
is the graph on the combined vertex set
H_{V}\cup G_{V}
with edge set
H_{E}\sqcup G_{E}
. Each edge of
H\star G
retains its respective source and target from
H
or
G
, respectively.

We define two subgraphs of
G
induced by a subset
T\subseteq G_{V}
. First, the
restriction

G|_{T}
is the subgraph on the vertex set
T
consisting of all edges in
G
whose source and target lie in
T
. Second,
G^{\mathsf{out}}_{T}
is the spanning subgraph of
G
consisting of all edges with source in
T
.

A
walk
of length
\ell\geq 1
in
G
is a sequence of edges
e_{\ell}\cdots e_{1}
where the target of each edge
e_{i-1}
is the source of the edge
e_{i}
for
i=2,\ldots,\ell
. The
source
of a walk is the source
s(e_{1})
of its first edge, and the
target
of a walk is the target
t(e_{\ell})
of its final edge. A walk is
closed
if its source and target are the same, and
G
is a
directed acyclic graph
(DAG) if it does not admit any closed walk. Since
G
is finite,
G
is acyclic if and only if its walks are bounded in length.

The
successors
of each vertex
u\in G_{V}
are the vertices
v\in G_{V}
that are the target of a walk with source
u
. In particular, if there is an edge from
u
to
v
, then
v
is a
direct successor
of
u
.
For each subset
T\subseteq G_{V}
,
T
is
closed
under successors in
G
if
T
contains all successors of each of its vertices

, or equivalently, if
T
contains all direct successors of each of its vertices.
Thus,
T
is closed under successors if and only if all edges with source in
T
belong to
G|_{T}
, if and only if
G=G^{\mathsf{out}}_{G_{V}\setminus T}\star G|_{T}
. See Figure

When algebraically enumerating graphs, we fix an ambient graph
G
and work in the polynomial rings of its commuting edge and vertex variables,

R_{G_{E}}:=\mathbb{Z}[x_{e}:e\in G_{E}],\quad R_{G_{V}}:=\mathbb{Z}[x_{v}:v\in G_{V}].

For each subgraph
H
of
G
, we define the monomial weight

{w}(H):=\prod_{e\in H_{E}}x_{e}\in R_{G_{E}}.

Then, we extend the function additively. For each subset
\mathcal{S}
of subgraphs of
G
, define

{w}(\mathcal{S}):=\sum_{H\in\mathcal{S}}{w}(H)\in R_{G_{E}}.

Note that
\mathcal{S}
is finite since
G
is, hence the sum is finite.

If
H
and
H^{\prime}
are subgraphs of
G
with disjoint edge sets, then
H\star H^{\prime}
is also a subgraph of
G
. By disjointness, the weight factors as

{w}(H\star H^{\prime})=\prod_{e\in H_{E}\sqcup H^{\prime}_{E}}x_{e}={w}(H){w}(H^{\prime})\in R_{G_{E}}.

We pass from edge to vertex variables by the homomorphism
R_{G_{E}}\to R_{G_{V}}
induced by the target specialization
x_{e}\mapsto x_{t(e)}
, where
t(e)
is the target vertex of
e
. Under this specialization, the monomial weight of the graph
H
becomes

w(H)=\prod_{e\in H_{E}}x_{t(e)}\in R_{G_{V}}.

Equivalently, the exponent of each variable
x_{v}
is the in-degree of the vertex
v\in G_{V}
in
H
.

attaching weight
,

(4)

{A}(T,U):={w}(\mathcal{A}(T,U))\in R_{G_{E}}.

With these weights, we construct the
attaching weight matrix

{M}
. Define the square matrix indexed by the subsets of
G_{V}
with entries in
R_{G_{E}}
given by

(5)

{M}_{T,U}:=\begin{cases}(-1)^{|U\setminus T|-1}{A}(U\setminus T,{U})&\text{if }T\subsetneq U,\\
\hskip 51.21495pt0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Since
{M}
is strictly upper triangular, it is nilpotent. So,
(I-{M})^{-1}
expands as a finite geometric sum in
{M}
, with
I
denoting the identity matrix.

Finally, we define the sink-restricted subsets of our constructions above.
For all subsets
T\subseteq U\subseteq G_{V}
and
S\subseteq G_{V}
, let
\mathcal{D}_{S}(U)
and
\mathcal{B}_{S}(T,U)
denote subsets of DAGs in
\mathcal{D}(U)
and
\mathcal{B}(T,U)
that have sink set
S
,

(6)

\mathcal{D}_{S}(U):=\{H\in\mathcal{D}(U):\mathsf{Snk}(H)=S\},\quad\mathcal{B}_{S}(T,U):=\{H\in\mathcal{B}(T,U):\mathsf{Snk}(H)=S\}.

Note
\mathcal{D}_{S}(U)
is empty if
S\not\subseteq U
, and
\mathcal{B}_{S}(T,U)
is empty if
S\not\subseteq U\setminus T
. The attaching weight matrix gives an enumeration for
\mathcal{D}_{S}(U)
in Theorem
3.2
.

Example 2.3
.

Consider the source-factorizable DAG assignment
\mathcal{D}^{(1)}
of in-forests on
G
. For all subsets
T\subseteq U\subseteq[10]
, the attaching graphs
\mathcal{A}(T,U)
are precisely the graphs that assign each vertex in
T
exactly one edge with target in
U\setminus T
. These graphs identify with the functions from

to
U\setminus T
, so it can be shown that the attaching weight factors into a product over the source vertices:

(7)

Under the target variable specialization
x_{u,v}\mapsto x_{v}
, the inner sum simplifies to

(8)

where
e_{1}
is the elementary symmetric polynomial of degree

,
e_{1}(U\setminus T):=\sum_{v\in U\setminus T}x_{v}
.
Under the target variable specialization, the attaching weight matrix

has entries

(9)

M_{T,U}=\begin{cases}(-1)^{|U\setminus T|-1}e_{1}(T)^{|U\setminus T|}&\text{if }T\subsetneq U,\\
\hskip 51.21495pt0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Also, the sink vertices of an in-forest are precisely its roots, so for all subsets

,
\mathcal{D}^{(1)}_{S}(U)
is the subset of forests on

with root set

.

Let

-tuple

in

such that
\Gamma_{i-1}<\Gamma_{i}
for each

. We say

to its last element

.

For each pair of elements

.

2.4.
Set-valued quiver representations

In this section, fix a finite
quiver

Q=(Q_{0},Q_{1})
, which is a finite directed multigraph, where
Q_{0}
is the set of vertices and
Q_{1}
is the set of arrows. We also fix a
Q_{0}
-indexed tuple
V:=(V_{a})_{a\in Q_{0}}
of nonempty, finite, disjoint sets and abbreviate
\overline{V}:=\coprod_{a\in Q_{0}}V_{a}
. For each arrow
\alpha\in Q_{1}
, let
s(\alpha)
and
t(\alpha)
denote its
source
and
target
vertices, respectively, written
\alpha:s(\alpha)\to t(\alpha)
. For each subset
U\subseteq\overline{V}
and each vertex
a\in Q_{0}
, let
U_{a}:=U\cap V_{a}
.

A
path
of length
\ell\geq 1
in
Q
is a sequence of arrows
p:=\alpha_{\ell}\cdots\alpha_{1}
where
t(\alpha_{i-1})=s(\alpha_{i})
for each
i=2,\ldots,\ell
.
As with directed graphs, the source of a path is the source of its first arrow and the target of a path is the target of its last arrow. A path of length
\geq 1
is an
oriented cycle
if it has the same source and target. A
successor
of a vertex
a\in Q_{0}
is any vertex
b\in Q_{0}
for which there is a path
p
from
a
to
b
, and a subset
X\subseteq Q_{0}
is
closed under successors
if
X
contains the successors of each of its vertices.
A vertex is a
sink
if it has no successors.

Throughout this paper, we assume
Q
has no sinks and that every vertex is the target of a path of length
\geq|Q_{0}|
. Since
Q_{0}
is finite, this is equivalent to assuming every vertex is the target of arbitrarily long paths, or rather every vertex is a successor of a vertex belonging to a directed cycle.

The
adjacency matrix

\mathsf{A}(Q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{0}\times Q_{0}}
is defined such that
\mathsf{A}(Q)_{b,a}
is the number of arrows from
a
to
b
. The
in-degree
of a vertex
b\in Q_{0}
is the number of arrows with target
b
, denoted
d_{in}^{Q_{0}}(b)\in\mathbb{N}
.

In the literature, see e.g.
[
5
,
6
]
, a set-valued representation
W
of a quiver
Q
consists of a collection
\{W_{a}\}_{a\in Q_{0}}
of finite sets and a collection
\{W_{\alpha}:W_{s(\alpha)}\to W_{t(\alpha)}\}_{\alpha\in Q_{1}}
of maps. But in this paper, a
set-valued representation

W
of
Q
is a
Q_{0}
-indexed tuple
(W_{a})_{a\in Q_{0}}
of finite sets and a
Q_{1}
-indexed tuple
(W_{\alpha}:W_{s(\alpha)}\to W_{t(\alpha)})_{\alpha\in Q_{1}}
of maps between these sets.

Alternatively, let
\mathcal{Q}
be the free category generated by
Q
, called the
path category
. Its objects are the vertices
a\in Q_{0}
, its morphisms are paths in
Q
, and its composition is path concatenation. On each vertex
a\in Q_{0}
, we also specify a
0
-length path on
a
to serve as the identity. Then, set-valued representations correspond precisely to functors from
\mathcal{Q}
to the category of finite sets. For any subset
U\subseteq\overline{V}
, let
\mathsf{SetRep}(Q,(U_{a})_{a\in Q_{0}})
denote the set of
set-valued representations on
U
, which are the set-valued representations
W
of
Q
such that
W_{a}=U_{a}
for each
a\in Q_{0}
. Hereafter, we may omit the prefix “set-valued”.

Example 2.5
.

Let
Q
be the
3
-cycle quiver with vertex set
Q_{0}=\{1,2,3\}
and arrows

1\xrightarrow{\alpha_{1}}2\xrightarrow{\alpha_{2}}3\xrightarrow{\alpha_{3}}1.

Then,
Q
has no sinks and every vertex is the successor of an element in a directed cycle.

Define the disjoint sets
V_{1}=\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\}
,
V_{2}=\{y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}\}
, and
V_{3}=\{z_{1},z_{2},z_{3}\}
. A representation
W
on
V:=(V_{1},V_{2},V_{3})
is determined by its
3
maps on arrows,

V_{1}\xrightarrow{W_{\alpha_{1}}}V_{2}\xrightarrow{W_{\alpha_{2}}}V_{3}\xrightarrow{W_{\alpha_{3}}}V_{1}.

Morphisms in the path category
\mathcal{Q}
are paths in
Q
. Paths
p:=\alpha_{2}\alpha_{1}\alpha_{3}
and
p^{\prime}:=\alpha_{2}\alpha_{1}
concatenate to form the path
pp^{\prime}=\alpha_{2}\alpha_{1}\alpha_{3}\alpha_{2}\alpha_{1}
. The path
pp^{\prime}
evaluates to the composite map
W_{pp^{\prime}}=W_{\alpha_{2}}\circ W_{\alpha_{1}}\circ W_{\alpha_{3}}\circ W_{\alpha_{2}}\circ W_{\alpha_{1}}:V_{1}\to V_{3}
.

Let
U\subseteq\overline{V}
. Then a representation
W\in\mathsf{SetRep}(Q,(U_{a})_{a\in Q_{0}})

stabilizes
in a subset
S\subseteq U
if
S
is closed under the maps
(W_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in Q_{1}}
, and for all sufficiently long paths
p
in
Q
, the function
W_{p}
maps into
S
. More precisely,
W
stabilizes in
S
if for all arrows
\alpha\in Q_{1}
,
W_{\alpha}
maps
S_{s(\alpha)}
into
S_{t(\alpha)}
, and if for all paths
p:=\alpha_{\ell}\cdots\alpha_{1}
in
Q
longer than a fixed length
\ell_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
,
W_{p}
maps
U_{s(\alpha_{1})}
into
S_{t(\alpha_{\ell})}
. Let
\mathsf{EC}_{S}(U)
denote the set of representations on
U
that stabilize in
S\subseteq U
. Note that
\ell_{0}
can be taken as
|U|-|S|
. To see this, consider any representation
W
, any path
p:=\alpha_{\ell}\cdots\alpha_{1}
, and any vertex
u\in U_{s(\alpha_{1})}
.
If the sequence of vertices
(u,W_{\alpha_{1}}(u),\ldots,W_{\alpha_{\ell}\cdots\alpha_{1}}(u))
repeats before entering
S
, then there exists a nontrivial path
p^{\prime}
and vertex
v\in U\setminus S
for which
W_{p^{\prime}}(v)=v
, so
W\not\in\mathsf{EC}_{S}(U)
. Thus, if
W\in\mathsf{EC}_{S}(U)
and
\ell>|U|-|S|
, then
W_{p}(u)\in S
.

A
transversal
of
V
is a vertex subset
C\subseteq\overline{V}
such that for each vertex
a\in Q_{0}
,
C_{a}\subseteq V_{a}
is a singleton. We denote the unique element of each set
C_{a}
by
c_{a}
, and denote the set of transversals as
\mathcal{T}(V)
. We say that a representation on
V
is
eventually constant
if it stabilizes in a transversal, and we let
\mathsf{EC}
denote the set of such representations on
\overline{V}
.
Given a vertex subset
U\subseteq\overline{V}
, we say that a representation
W\in\mathsf{SetRep}(Q,(U_{a})_{a\in Q_{0}})
is
eventually constant on
U
if the representation stabilizes in a transversal
C\in\mathcal{T}(V)
contained in
U
.

Observe that the closure condition is redundant for transversals
C\in\mathcal{T}(V)
. If
W\in\mathsf{SetRep}(Q,V)
and for all sufficiently long paths
p
in
Q
the function
W_{p}
maps into
C
, then
C
is closed under the functions
(W_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in Q_{1}}
; for each arrow
\alpha:a\to b
, there are sufficiently long paths
p
in
Q
with target
a\in Q_{0}
, so
\alpha p
is a sufficiently long path. Thus,
W_{p}
maps into
\{c_{a}\}
, and
W_{\alpha}\circ W_{p}
maps into
\{c_{b}\}
, so
W_{\alpha}(c_{a})=c_{b}
.

A quiver
Q^{\prime}
is a
subquiver
of
Q
if all vertices and arrows in
Q^{\prime}
belong to
Q
, and the source and target of each arrow in
Q^{\prime}
is the same as in
Q
. If
Q^{\prime}
is a subquiver of
Q
, then each representation
W\in\mathsf{SetRep}(Q,(U_{a})_{a\in Q_{0}})
restricts to a representation
W|_{Q^{\prime}}\in\mathsf{SetRep}(Q^{\prime},(U_{a})_{a\in Q^{\prime}_{0}})
.

If
W\in\mathsf{SetRep}(Q,V)
and
S\subseteq\overline{V}
, a
subrepresentation
of
W
is a representation
W^{\prime}\in\mathsf{SetRep}(Q,(S_{a})_{a\in Q_{0}})
such that each arrow
\alpha\in Q_{1}
,
W_{\alpha}^{\prime}:S_{s(\alpha)}\to S_{t(\alpha)}
is the restriction of the map
W_{\alpha}:V_{s(\alpha)}\to V_{t(\alpha)}
. Note that if
S
is closed under the maps
(W_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in Q_{1}}
, then
W
induces a subrepresentation
W^{\prime}\in\mathsf{SetRep}(Q,(S_{a})_{a\in Q_{0}})
.

2.6.
Cardinality vectors and attaching cardinality matrices

As in Section
2.4
, fix a finite quiver
Q=(Q_{0},Q_{1})
and a
Q_{0}
-indexed tuple
V:=(V_{a})_{a\in Q_{0}}
of finite, nonempty, disjoint sets.

Equip
\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}
with the pointwise order, so that

\mathbf{c}\leq\mathbf{d}
if and only if
c_{a}\leq d_{a}
for each
a\in Q_{0}
. Define the factorial of
\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}
as the product of its component factorials,

\mathbf{c}!:=\prod_{a\in Q_{0}}c_{a}!\in\mathbb{N}.

We extend to multinomials. If
\mathbf{c}\leq\mathbf{d}
, or more generally if
\mathbf{c}_{1}+\ldots+\mathbf{c}_{\ell}=\mathbf{d}
,
let

\binom{\mathbf{d}}{\mathbf{c}}:=\frac{\mathbf{d}!}{\mathbf{c}!(\mathbf{d}-\mathbf{c})!}=\prod_{a\in Q_{0}}\binom{d_{a}}{c_{a}}\in\mathbb{N},\qquad\binom{\mathbf{d}}{\mathbf{c}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{c}_{\ell}}=\frac{\mathbf{d}!}{\mathbf{c}_{1}!\cdots\mathbf{c}_{\ell}!}\in\mathbb{N}.

Define the
L_{1}
-norm on
\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}
by
|\mathbf{c}|:=\sum_{a\in Q_{0}}c_{a}\in\mathbb{N}
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathbf{n}:=(n)_{a\in Q_{0}}\in\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}
. As established in Section
1
, the
cardinality vector
of a subset
U\subseteq\overline{V}
is
\mathbf{U}:=(|U_{a}|)_{a\in Q_{0}}\in\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}
. Recall that
\mathbf{\overline{V}}=(|V_{a}|)_{a\in Q_{0}}
.
Let
[\mathbf{0},\mathbf{\overline{V}}]\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}
be the partially ordered interval between
\mathbf{0}
and
\mathbf{\overline{V}}
.

Next, we consider vectors in
R_{\overline{V}}^{Q_{0}}
. For each subset
S\subseteq V_{a}
, denote the elementary symmetric polynomial of
S
of degree
1
as
e_{1}(S):=\sum_{u\in S}x_{u}\in R_{\overline{V}}
.
Extending to subsets
U\subseteq\overline{V}
, define the polynomial-valued vector
\mathbf{e}_{1}(U)
by

\mathbf{e}_{1}(U):=(e_{1}(U_{a}))_{a\in Q_{0}}\in R_{\overline{V}}^{Q_{0}}.

For any polynomial vector
\mathbf{q}\in R_{\overline{V}}^{Q_{0}}
and any

, define exponentiation by

as

For example, for any subset

, we have

Define the strictly upper triangular

(11)

\hskip 45.5244pt0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

3.
Weighted enumeration of directed acyclic graphs

Recall the constructions in Sections
2.1
and
2.2
. Let
\mathcal{D}
denote a fixed source-factorizable DAG assignment on a finite graph
G
. We derive a weighted enumeration formula for DAGs in
\mathcal{D}
. The formula follows from a recursive expression for DAG weights in terms of attaching weights.
To establish this recursion, we make two reductions. In Theorem
3.2
, we decompose DAG weights into sums over the weights
w(\mathcal{B}_{S}(T,U))
, and in Lemma
3.1
, we factor the weights
{w}(\mathcal{B}_{S}(T,U))
by attaching weights.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a source-factorizable DAG assignment on a graph
G
. For any subsets
S,\,T,\,U\subseteq G_{V}
, the weight of
\mathcal{B}_{S}(T,U)
factors as

(13)

\displaystyle{w}(\mathcal{B}_{S}(T,U))={A}({T},U)\cdot{w}(\mathcal{D}_{S}({U\setminus T})).

Proof.

We construct a weight preserving bijection

(14)

\mathcal{A}(T,U)\times\mathcal{D}_{S}({U\setminus T})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{B}_{S}(T,U),

given by the map
(H^{\prime},H)\mapsto H^{\prime}\star H
, with inverse
B\mapsto(B^{\mathsf{out}}_{T},B|_{U\setminus T})
.

Fix any pair
(H^{\prime},H)\in\mathcal{A}(T,U)\times\mathcal{D}_{S}({U\setminus T})
. Then
H^{\prime}\star H\in\mathcal{B}(T,U)
by definition of
\mathcal{A}(T,U)
. The sinks in
H^{\prime}\star H
are the sinks in
H
, since
H^{\prime}
contributes outgoing edges for all vertices in
T
and no outgoing edges for all vertices in
U\setminus T
. Thus
\mathsf{Snk}(H^{\prime}\star H)=\mathsf{Snk}(H)=S
, so
H^{\prime}\star H\in\mathcal{B}_{S}(T,U)
. Also

(H^{\prime},H)=((H^{\prime}\star H)^{\mathsf{out}}_{T},(H^{\prime}\star H)|_{U\setminus T}),

since
H
contributes no outgoing edges in
T
, and all edges of
H^{\prime}
start in
T
.

Fix any
H\in\mathcal{B}_{S}(T,U)
. Since
T\subseteq\mathsf{Nss}(H)
, all edges of
H
terminate in
U\setminus T
. So
{U\setminus T}
is closed under successors in
H
,
H=H^{\mathsf{out}}_{T}\star H|_{U\setminus T}
, and
H
,
H|_{U\setminus T}
have the same outgoing edges on
U\setminus T
. Therefore,
H
and
H|_{U\setminus T}
share sinks on
U\setminus T
, so
\mathsf{Snk}(H|_{U\setminus T})=\mathsf{Snk}(H)=S
. By source-factorizability,
H|_{U\setminus T}\in\mathcal{D}(U\setminus T)
, so
H|_{U\setminus T}\in\mathcal{D}_{S}(U\setminus T)
. Since edges of
H^{\mathsf{out}}_{T}
terminate in
U\setminus T
and
H^{\mathsf{out}}_{T}\star H|_{U\setminus T}=H\in\mathcal{B}(T,U)
, we have
H^{\mathsf{out}}_{T}\in\mathcal{A}(T,U)
.

The graphs in each pair
(H^{\prime},H)\in\mathcal{A}(T,U)\times\mathcal{D}_{S}({U\setminus T})
have disjoint edges, so the isomorphism in (
14
) gives

{w}(\mathcal{B}_{S}(T,U))=\sum_{H^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}(T,U)}\sum_{H\in\mathcal{D}_{S}({U\setminus T})}{w}(H^{\prime}){w}(H)={A}({T},U)\cdot{w}(\mathcal{D}_{S}({U\setminus T})).

Observe that the proof still holds even if any of the sets
\mathcal{B}_{S}(T,U)
,
\mathcal{A}(T,U)
, or
\mathcal{D}_{S}(U\setminus T)
is empty, which occurs
in particular when
S\not\subseteq U\setminus T
.
∎

Recall the attaching weight matrix
{M}
given by (
5
), and let
I
denote the identity matrix. Since
{M}
is strictly upper triangular,
{M}
is nilpotent and
(I-{M})^{-1}
is invertible and expands as a finite geometric sum in
{M}
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be source-factorizable on a graph
G
. For any vertex subsets
S,\,U\subseteq G_{V}
, we have

(15)

{w}(\mathcal{D}_{S}(U))=(I-{M})^{-1}_{S,U}.

Proof.

Fix
S
and construct two
\mathcal{P}(G_{V})
-indexed row-vectors in
R_{G_{E}}
, a vector
\alpha
to weigh each set
\mathcal{D}_{S}(U)
, and a vector
\beta
to encode the boundary condition. For each
U\subseteq G_{V}
, define

\alpha_{U}:={w}(\mathcal{D}_{S}(U)),\quad\beta_{U}:=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }U=S,\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

For all subsets
U\subseteq G_{V}
, we prove the following assertion,

(16)

\alpha_{U}=\beta_{U}+\sum_{\varnothing\neq T\subseteq U}(-1)^{|T|-1}{w}(\mathcal{B}_{S}(T,U)).

In the first case, assume
S
is not a proper subset of
U
. Then
\mathcal{B}_{S}(T,U)
is empty for each nonempty subset
T\subseteq U
since
S\not\subseteq U\setminus T
, so (
16
) reduces to the assertion
\alpha_{U}=\beta_{U}
. If
S=U
, then
\mathcal{D}_{S}(U)
contains only the edgeless graph, which has weight
1
. So,
\alpha_{U}=1=\beta_{U}
. If
S
is not contained in
U
, then
\mathcal{D}_{S}(U)
is empty, thus
\alpha_{U}=0=\beta_{U}
.

In the remaining case, assume
S\subsetneq U
. Then all DAGs
H\in\mathcal{D}_{S}(U)
have non-sink sources; since
H
is finite, acyclic, and has edges, it admits nontrivial walk
\omega
of maximal length with starting vertex
v_{1}
. Since
\omega
cannot be extended further,
v_{1}
must be a source, and since
\omega
is nontrivial,
v_{1}
is not a sink, so
\mathsf{Nss}(H)
is nonempty. Consequently, the following binomial expansion is
0
,

\sum_{T\subseteq\mathsf{Nss}(H)}(-1)^{|T|}=\sum_{i=0}^{|\mathsf{Nss}(H)|}\binom{|\mathsf{Nss}(H)|}{i}(-1)^{i}=(1-1)^{|\mathsf{Nss}(H)|}=0,

which gives the identity

(17)

\sum_{\varnothing\neq T\subseteq\mathsf{Nss}(H)}(-1)^{|T|-1}=1.

Thus, we can multiply each summand
{w}(H)
of
{w}(\mathcal{D}_{S}(U))
by (
17
). We can then swap the order of summation to isolate the weights
{w}(\mathcal{B}_{S}(T,U))
and recover (
16
),

\displaystyle\alpha_{U}

\displaystyle=\beta_{U}+\sum_{H\in\mathcal{D}_{S}(U)}\left({w}(H)\sum_{\varnothing\neq T\subseteq\mathsf{Nss}(H)}(-1)^{|T|-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\beta_{U}+\sum_{\varnothing\neq T\subseteq U}\sum_{H:T\subseteq\mathsf{Nss}(H)}(-1)^{|T|-1}{w}(H)

\displaystyle=\beta_{U}+\sum_{\varnothing\neq T\subseteq U}(-1)^{|T|-1}{w}(\mathcal{B}_{S}(T,U)).

Now for each
U\subseteq G_{V}
, apply Lemma
3.1
to (
16
) to factor each weight
{w}(\mathcal{B}_{S}(T,U))

(18)

\alpha_{U}=\beta_{U}+\sum_{\varnothing\neq T\subseteq U}\alpha_{U\setminus T}\left((-1)^{|T|-1}{A}(T,U)\right).

The summands in (
18
) are indexed over the nonempty subsets
T\subseteq U
. We rearrange these terms into a sum over the proper subsets of
U
by complementation,

\{T\subseteq U:T\neq\varnothing\}\to\{T\subsetneq U\},\quad T\mapsto U\setminus T.

Reindexing takes (
18
) to a sum involving the weighted matrix
{M}
,

\displaystyle\alpha_{U}

\displaystyle=\beta_{U}+\sum_{\varnothing\neq T\subseteq U}\alpha_{U\setminus T}\left((-1)^{|T|-1}{A}(T,U)\right)

\displaystyle=\beta_{U}+\sum_{T\subsetneq U}\alpha_{T}\left((-1)^{|U\setminus T|-1}{A}({U\setminus T},U)\right)

\displaystyle=\beta_{U}+\sum_{T\subsetneq U}\alpha_{T}{M}_{T,U}

\displaystyle=(\beta+\alpha{M})_{U}.

Since this holds for all
U\subseteq G_{V}
, we have
\alpha=\beta+\alpha{M}
. This implies
\alpha=\beta(I-{M})^{-1}
, and thus the result,

{w}(\mathcal{D}_{S}(U))=(\beta(I-{M})^{-1})_{U}=(I-{M})^{-1}_{S,U}.\qed

Since
M
is strictly upper triangular and
(I-M)^{-1}=I+(I-M)^{-1}M
, we have the following recursive formula for all subsets
S\subseteq U\subseteq G_{V}
,

(19)

w(\mathcal{D}_{S}(U))=\delta_{S,U}+\sum_{S\subseteq T\subsetneq U}w(\mathcal{D}_{S}(T))M_{T,U}.

Here,
\delta_{S,U}
is
1
if
U=S
, and
\delta_{S,U}
is
0
otherwise.

Remark 3.3
.

Let
P
be a finite partially ordered set. Let
X
be a strictly upper triangular
P\times P
matrix with entries in a ring. We recall the notation for chains in a poset from Section
2.3
.

As in
[
17
, Theorem 4.7.1]
, we make an elementary observation. For all integers
d\in\mathbb{N}
and elements
b,\,c\in P
, each entry
(X^{d})_{b,c}
yields a sum over the
(d+1)
-tuples
\Gamma:=(b,\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{d-1},c)
in
P^{d+1}
that start with
b
and end with
c
,

(20)

(X^{d})_{b,c}=\sum_{\Gamma}X_{b,\Gamma_{1}}\cdots X_{\Gamma_{d-1},c}.

By strict upper triangularity, summands over non-increasing tuples in (
20
) vanish. This leaves a sum over only the tuples
\Gamma
that correspond to strictly increasing chains from
b
to
c
of length
d
.
Consequently, the geometric expansion of the matrix
(I-X)^{-1}
yields the finite sum

(21)

(I-X)^{-1}_{b,c}=\sum_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{C}(b,c)}X_{b,\Gamma_{1}}\cdots X_{\Gamma_{L(\Gamma)-1},c}.

We also can compress the matrix slightly. If
X[b,c]
is the submatrix of
X
indexed over the elements
y,\,z
with
b\leq y,\,z\leq c
, then (
21
) reduces to the evaluation

(22)

(I-X[b,c])^{-1}_{b,c}=\sum_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{C}(b,c)}X_{b,\Gamma_{1}}\cdots X_{\Gamma_{L(\Gamma)-1},c}=(I-X)^{-1}_{b,c}.

The power set
\mathcal{P}(G_{V})
is finite and partially ordered, and the attaching weight matrix
M
is strictly upper triangular, so we can apply (
21
) to expand
(I-M)^{-1}
by strictly increasing chains in the partially ordered power set
\mathcal{P}(G_{V})
. By Theorem
3.2
, we have the following.

Corollary 3.4
.

If
\mathcal{D}
is source-factorizable on
G
, then for all subsets
S\subseteq U\subseteq G_{V}
, we have the following sum over chains in
\mathcal{P}(G_{V})
,

\begin{split}{w}(\mathcal{D}_{S}(U))&=\sum_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{C}(S,U)}\prod_{\ell=1}^{L(\Gamma)}\left((-1)^{|\Gamma_{\ell}\setminus\Gamma_{\ell-1}|-1}{A}({\Gamma_{\ell}\setminus\Gamma_{\ell-1}},{\Gamma_{\ell}})\right)\\
&=\sum_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{C}(S,U)}(-1)^{|U\setminus S|-L(\Gamma)}\prod_{\ell=1}^{L(\Gamma)}{A}({\Gamma_{\ell}\setminus\Gamma_{\ell-1}},{\Gamma_{\ell}}).\end{split}

4.
Eventually constant set-valued representations

Fix a finite quiver
Q
, whose vertex set
Q_{0}
indexes a tuple
(V_{a})_{a\in Q_{0}}
of finite, nonempty, disjoint sets, and let
\overline{V}
be the disjoint union of
V_{a}
, where
a\in Q_{0}
. Assume
Q
has no sinks and each vertex
a\in Q_{0}
is the target of a path of length
\geq|Q_{0}|
.

4.1.
Representable graphs

In this section, we construct a DAG assignment and relate it to the set of stabilizing representations. Then, we prove the assignment is source-factorizable and calculate its attaching weights. We use the constructions on quivers given in Sections
2.2
and
2.4
, and the graph operations given in Section
2.1
.

For each vertex subset
U\subseteq\overline{V}
, let
\mathsf{DAG}(U)
be the set of directed acyclic subgraphs of
G(Q)
on
U
. Recall
G(Q)
is the graph on
\overline{V}
with
Q_{1}
-labeled edges, and for each
U\subseteq\overline{V}
,
\mathbf{U}:=(|U_{a}|)_{a\in Q_{0}}\in\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}
. Observe
\mathbf{U}\geq\mathbf{1}
if and only if each set
U_{a}
is nonempty, or equivalently if
U
contains a transversal of
V
.

Definition 4.1
.

We define a DAG assignment
\mathcal{D}
on the graph
G(Q)
as follows. For each subset
U\subseteq\overline{V}
, let
\mathcal{D}(U)\subseteq\mathsf{DAG}(U)
be the set of representable directed acyclic subgraphs on
U
.
Recall that a graph
H
on
U
is representable if there exists a subset
T\subseteq U
and a tuple of functions
(W_{\alpha}:T_{s(\alpha)}\to U_{t(\alpha)})
such that
H=G(W)
. The graphs induced by such tuples form the set
\mathcal{R}_{U}(T\to U)
, and when we restrict to such tuples with each
\mathsf{im}\>W_{\alpha}\subseteq S_{t(\alpha)}
, we have the set
\mathcal{R}_{U}(T\to S)
. Stated without reference to tuples,
H\in\mathcal{R}_{U}(T\to U)
if for each arrow
\alpha\in Q_{1}
and each vertex
u\in T_{s(\alpha)}
,
H
has a unique edge
(u,v)^{(\alpha)}
to some vertex
v\in U_{t(\alpha)}
, and no other edges.

When
\mathbf{U}\geq\mathbf{1}
, then
H
is representable if and only if there exists a representation
W\in\mathsf{SetRep}(Q,(U_{a}))
such that
H=G(W)^{\mathsf{out}}_{T}
. In this case,
G(W)^{\mathsf{out}}_{T}=G((W_{\alpha}|_{T_{s(\alpha)}})_{\alpha\in Q_{1}})
.

Now, we isolate two graph-theoretic observations. First, let
H
be a subgraph of
G(Q)
and suppose
T\subseteq H_{V}
is closed under successors. Then
H^{\mathsf{out}}_{H_{V}\setminus T}
is acyclic if and only if all sufficiently long walks in
H
have target in
T
. If
H^{\mathsf{out}}_{H_{V}\setminus T}
is acyclic, then all sufficiently long walks in
H
cannot be entirely contained in
H^{\mathsf{out}}_{H_{V}\setminus T}
. Such walks must eventually reach a vertex in
T
, and thus their target also lies in
T
by closure. Conversely, if
H^{\mathsf{out}}_{H_{V}\setminus T}
is not acyclic, then it contains a directed cycle. Since all edges in
H^{\mathsf{out}}_{H_{V}\setminus T}
have their source in
H_{V}\setminus T
, this cycle cannot contain any vertex in
T
. Thus,
H
admits arbitrarily long walks whose targets never enter
T
.

Next, consider any subsets
T\subseteq U\subseteq\overline{V}
and
G(W)\in\mathcal{R}_{U}(T\to U)
. Since
Q
has no sinks,
\mathsf{Snk}(G(W))=U\setminus T
; for each vertex
u\in T_{a}
, there exists an arrow
\alpha:a\to b
in
Q
, so
G(W)
has the outgoing edge
(u,W_{\alpha}(u))^{(\alpha)}
. Therefore,
\mathcal{D}_{U\setminus T}(U)=\mathcal{D}(U)\cap\mathcal{R}_{U}(T\to U)
.

By the following Lemma, representations that stabilize in a fixed subset
S\subseteq U
decompose via a weight preserving bijection into a DAG in
\mathcal{D}_{S}(U)
and a representation on
S
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Fix subsets
S\subseteq U
in
\overline{V}
with
\mathbf{1}\leq\mathbf{S}
. Then there is a weight preserving bijection between subgraphs of
G(Q)
,

\{G(W):W\in\mathsf{EC}_{S}(U)\}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{D}_{S}(U)\times\mathcal{R}(S),

given by
G(W)\mapsto(G(W)^{\mathsf{out}}_{U\setminus S},G(W)|_{S})
, with inverse
(H,H^{\prime})\mapsto H\star H^{\prime}
. Thus,

(23)

\sum_{W\in\mathsf{EC}_{S}(U)}w(G(W))={w}(\mathcal{D}_{S}(U)){w}(\mathcal{R}(S)),

which we abbreviate as
w(\mathsf{EC}_{S}(U))
.

Proof.

Fix any representation
W\in\mathsf{EC}_{S}(U)
. Since
W
stabilizes in
S
, the set
S
is closed under successors in
G(W)
,
G(W)=G(W)^{\mathsf{out}}_{U\setminus S}\star G(W)|_{S}
, and all sufficiently long walks in
G(W)
have target in
S
. Thus
G(W)^{\mathsf{out}}_{U\setminus S}
is acyclic. Since
Q
has no sinks, we know
\mathsf{Snk}(G(W)^{\mathsf{out}}_{U\setminus S})=S
, so
G(W)^{\mathsf{out}}_{U\setminus S}\in\mathcal{D}_{S}(U)
. The maps
(W_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in Q_{1}}
are closed on
S
, so
W
induces a subrepresentation in
\mathsf{SetRep}(Q,(S_{a})_{a\in Q_{0}})
,
W^{\prime}:=(W_{\alpha}^{\prime}:S_{s(\alpha)}\to S_{t(\alpha)})_{\alpha\in Q_{1}}
.
We then see
G(W)|_{S}=G(W^{\prime})
, so
G(W)|_{S}\in\mathcal{R}(S)
.

Conversely, fix any pair
(G(W),G(W^{\prime}))\in\mathcal{D}_{S}(U)\times\mathcal{R}(S)
, with
W^{\prime}\in\mathsf{SetRep}(Q,(S_{a})_{a\in Q_{0}})
and
W:=(W_{\alpha}:(U\setminus S)_{a}\to U_{a})_{\alpha\in Q_{1}}
. For each arrow
\alpha\in Q_{1}
, the maps
W_{\alpha}
and
W^{\prime}_{\alpha}
glue to form a map
X_{\alpha}:U_{s(\alpha)}\to U_{t(\alpha)}
, by

X_{\alpha}(u):=\begin{cases}W_{\alpha}(u)&\text{if }u\in U_{s(\alpha)}\setminus S_{s(\alpha)},\\
W^{\prime}_{\alpha}(u)&\text{if }u\in S_{s(\alpha)}.\end{cases}

Then,
X:=(X_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in Q_{1}}
is in
\mathsf{SetRep}(Q,(U_{a})_{a\in Q_{0}})
and

G(W)\star G(W^{\prime})=G(X)
.
For each arrow
\alpha\in Q_{1}
,
X_{\alpha}
extends
W^{\prime}_{\alpha}
on
S_{s(\alpha)}\subseteq U_{s(\alpha)}
, so
X_{\alpha}
maps
S_{s(\alpha)}
into
S_{t(\alpha)}
. Also,
S
is closed on
G(X)
,
G(X)^{\mathsf{out}}_{U\setminus S}=G(W)
, and
G(W)
is acyclic, so all sufficiently long walks in
G(X)
have target in
S
. Thus
X
stabilizes in
S
, so
X\in\mathsf{EC}_{S}(U)
.
∎

To make use of Lemma
4.2
, we need to weigh the sets
\mathcal{R}(S)
and
\mathcal{D}_{S}(U)
. We next prove that
\mathcal{D}
is source-factorizable so that we can decompose
w(\mathcal{D}_{S}(U))
by its attaching weights.

Fix any subsets
T\subseteq U\subseteq\overline{V}
. For the nontriviality axiom, the edgeless graph is in the subset
\mathcal{R}_{U}(\varnothing\to U)
of
\mathcal{D}(U)
.
For the source-removal axiom, we fix
G(W)\in\mathcal{B}(T,U)
in
\mathcal{R}_{U}(S\to U)
and need to show
G(W)|_{U\setminus T}\in\mathcal{D}(U\setminus T)
. First,
G(W)|_{U\setminus T}
is acyclic since it is a subgraph of
G(W)
. Second,
T\subseteq\mathsf{Nss}(G(W))
, so the vertices in
T
are sources in
G(W)
, so each map
W_{\alpha}:S_{s(\alpha)}\to U_{t(\alpha)}
restricts to a map
W^{\prime}_{\alpha}:(S\setminus T)_{s(\alpha)}\to(U\setminus T)_{t(\alpha)}
. This yields a tuple of functions
W^{\prime}:=(W^{\prime}_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in Q_{1}}
, and
G(W)|_{U\setminus T}=G(W^{\prime})
, so
G(W)|_{U\setminus T}
is representable. Thus
G(W)|_{U\setminus T}\in\mathcal{D}(U\setminus T)
.

The following lemma verifies the remaining uniformity axiom. This proves that
\mathcal{D}
is source-factorizable and gives the form of the attaching sets.

Lemma 4.3
.

For all subsets
T\subseteq U\subseteq\overline{V}
and each DAG
H\in\mathcal{D}(U\setminus T)
, the attaching set
\mathcal{A}_{H}(T,U)
is
\mathcal{R}_{U}(T\to U\setminus T)
.

Proof.

Suppose
S\subseteq U\setminus T
is the subset such that
H\in\mathcal{R}_{U\setminus T}(S\to U\setminus T)
, and fix any
H^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}_{U}(T\to U\setminus T)
. Since
T
and
S
are disjoint, the tuples of maps representing
H
and
H^{\prime}
glue to form a tuple representing
H^{\prime}\star H
as in Lemma
4.2
. Also, all edges in both
H^{\prime}
and
H
have targets in
U\setminus T
, and each vertex in
T
has an outgoing edge in
H^{\prime}
, so
T\subseteq\mathsf{Nss}(H^{\prime}\star H)
. Finally,
H^{\prime}\star H
is acyclic since walks are bounded in length; each walk enters
U\setminus T
in at most one step and then follows a walk of bounded length in the acyclic graph
H
. So,
H^{\prime}\star H\in\mathcal{B}(T,U)
, and thus
H^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}_{H}(T,U)
.

Conversely, let
H^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}_{H}(T,U)
. Then
H^{\prime}\star H\in\mathcal{B}(T,U)
, so
H^{\prime}\star H\in\mathcal{R}_{U}(S\to U)
for some subset
S\subseteq U
. Since no vertices in
T
are sinks in
H^{\prime}\star H
,
T
is disjoint from
\mathsf{Snk}(H^{\prime}\star H)=U\setminus S
, so
T\subseteq S
. Thus for each arrow
\alpha\in Q_{1}
and each vertex
u\in T_{s(\alpha)}
,
H^{\prime}\star H
has a single outgoing edge
(u,v)^{(\alpha)}
to a vertex
v\in U_{t(\alpha)}\setminus T=(U\setminus T)_{t(\alpha)}
. This edge belongs to
H^{\prime}
since
u\not\in U\setminus T
, so
H^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}_{U}(T\to U\setminus T)
.
∎

Fix any subsets
S,\,T\subseteq U\subseteq\overline{V}
. It remains to calculate
\mathcal{R}(S)
and the set of attaching graphs,
\mathcal{A}(T,U)
. Since
\mathcal{R}_{S}(S\to S)=\mathcal{R}(S)
and
\mathcal{R}_{U}(T\to U\setminus T)=\mathcal{A}(T,U)
, we can determine both weights by calculating
w(\mathcal{R}_{U}(T\to S))
.

We decompose the graphs in
\mathcal{R}_{U}(T\to S)
by the arrows
\alpha\in Q_{1}
.
For each arrow
\alpha\in Q_{1}
, associate each function
f:T_{a}\to S_{b}
with its
\alpha
-indexed graph
f^{(\alpha)}
on
U
with the edge set

\{(u,f(u))^{(\alpha)}:u\in T_{a}\}
.
Define the set of graphs
\mathsf{hom}(T_{a},S_{b})^{(\alpha)}:=\{f^{(\alpha)}:f\in\mathsf{hom}(T_{a},S_{b})\}
and note that its weight factors as

(24)

{w}(\mathsf{hom}(T_{a},S_{b})^{(\alpha)})=\sum_{f:T_{a}\to S_{b}}\prod_{u\in T_{a}}x_{u,f(u)}^{(\alpha)}=\prod_{u\in T_{a}}\sum_{v\in S_{b}}x_{u,v}^{(\alpha)}.

There is a weight preserving bijection,

\mathcal{R}_{U}(T\to S)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}\prod_{\alpha\in Q_{1}}\mathsf{hom}(T_{s(\alpha)},S_{t(\alpha)})^{(\alpha)},

by
G(W)\mapsto(W_{\alpha}^{(\alpha)})_{\alpha\in Q_{1}}
. Its inverse is given by sending
(f^{(\alpha)})_{\alpha\in Q_{1}}
to the edge-disjoint union over all arrows
\alpha\in Q_{1}
.
Since the edges of each map
f^{(\alpha)}
are pairwise disjoint, the weight factors as

(25)

\displaystyle{w}(\mathcal{R}_{U}(T\to S))=\prod_{\alpha\in Q_{1}}{w}(\mathsf{hom}(T_{s(\alpha)},S_{t(\alpha)})^{(\alpha)})=\prod_{\alpha:a\to b}\prod_{u\in T_{a}}\sum_{v\in S_{b}}x_{u,v}^{(\alpha)}.

Finally, we can weigh
\mathcal{R}(S)
and
\mathcal{A}(T,U)
. Since
\mathcal{R}(S)=\mathcal{R}_{S}(S\to S)
, the weight of the representations on
S\subseteq\overline{V}
is given by

(26)

{w}(\mathcal{R}(S))=\prod_{\alpha:a\to b}\prod_{u\in S_{a}}\sum_{v\in S_{b}}x_{u,v}^{(\alpha)}.

Since
\mathcal{A}(T,U)=\mathcal{R}_{U}(T\to U\setminus T)
, the attaching weight of
T\subseteq U\subseteq\overline{V}
is given by

(27)

{A}(T,U)=w(\mathcal{A}(T,U))=\prod_{\alpha:a\to b}\prod_{u\in T_{a}}\sum_{v\in(U\setminus T)_{b}}x_{u,v}^{(\alpha)}.

4.2.
Generating function in edge variables

In this section, we derive the generating function
P(\mathbf{x})
in directed edge variables. We use the notation established in Sections
2.2
,
2.4
, and
2.6
, and the results from the above Section
4.1
.

By (
27
), the entries of the attaching weight matrix
M
are given by

(28)

{M}_{T,U}=\begin{cases}(-1)^{|U\setminus T|-1}\prod_{\alpha:a\to b}\prod_{u\in(U\setminus T)_{a}}\sum_{v\in T_{b}}x_{u,v}^{(\alpha)}&\text{if }T\subsetneq U,\\
\hskip 51.21495pt0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Consider any subsets
S\subseteq U\subseteq\overline{V}
. Theorem
3.2
gives the weighted enumerator for
\mathcal{D}_{S}(U)
in terms of the attaching weight matrix
M
,
and (
26
) gives the weight of
\mathcal{R}(S)
. So, by Lemma
4.2
, the weight of
\mathsf{EC}_{S}(U)
is

{w}(\mathsf{EC}_{S}(U))=\left(\prod_{\alpha:a\to b}\prod_{u\in S_{a}}\sum_{v\in S_{b}}x_{u,v}^{(\alpha)}\right)(I-{M})^{-1}_{S,U}.

Expanded by chains in the power set lattice
\mathcal{P}(\overline{V})
, Corollary
3.4
gives

\displaystyle{w}(\mathsf{EC}_{S}(U))

\displaystyle=w(\mathcal{R}(S))\sum_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{C}(S,U)}(-1)^{|U\setminus S|-L(\Gamma)}\prod_{\ell=1}^{L(\Gamma)}{A}({\Gamma_{\ell}\setminus\Gamma_{\ell-1}},{\Gamma_{\ell}})

\displaystyle=\prod_{\alpha:a\to b}\prod_{u\in S_{a}}\sum_{v\in S_{b}}x_{u,v}^{(\alpha)}\sum_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{C}(S,U)}(-1)^{|U\setminus S|-L(\Gamma)}\prod_{\ell=1}^{L(\Gamma)}\prod_{\alpha:a\to b}\prod_{u\in({\Gamma_{\ell}\setminus\Gamma_{\ell-1}})_{a}}\sum_{v\in(\Gamma_{\ell-1})_{b}}x_{u,v}^{(\alpha)}.

To weigh
\mathsf{EC}
in terms of the sets
\mathsf{EC}_{S}(U)
, we note that
\mathsf{EC}
is partitioned by the sets

\{\mathsf{EC}_{C}(\overline{V}):C\in\mathcal{T}(V)\}.

Consider any representation
W\in\mathsf{EC}
. For all sufficiently long paths
p
in
Q
with target
b
,
\mathsf{im}\>W_{p}
is contained within a singleton of
\{c_{b}\}\subseteq V_{b}
. Also, this singleton is unique since sufficiently long paths with target
b
exist by assumption on
Q
. The collection
(c_{b})_{b\in Q_{0}}
uniquely determines the transversal
C
for which
W\in\mathsf{EC}_{C}(\overline{V})
, verifying pairwise disjointness.

Consequently, the generating function expands as the sum over these transversals,

(29)

{P}(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{C\in\mathcal{T}(V)}w(\mathsf{EC}_{C}(\overline{V}))=\sum_{C\in\mathcal{T}(V)}\left(\prod_{\alpha:a\to b}x_{c_{a},c_{b}}^{(\alpha)}\right)(I-{M})^{-1}_{C,\overline{V}}.

We now establish that the generating function
P(\mathbf{x})
is invariant under permutations of the vertices within each set
V_{a}
. Let
\mathfrak{S}_{a}
denote the symmetric group on the set
V_{a}
for each vertex
a\in Q_{0}
, and let
\mathfrak{S}:=\prod_{a\in Q_{0}}\mathfrak{S}_{a}
denote the product group. The natural action of
\mathfrak{S}
on the vertex set
\overline{V}
is given by
{\sigma}\cdot u:=\sigma_{a}(u)
for
u\in V_{a}
. This induces an action on the polynomial ring of edge variables
R_{G(Q)_{E}}
. For any
{\sigma}=(\sigma_{a})_{a\in Q_{0}}\in\mathfrak{S}
and any arrow
\alpha:a\to b
in
Q_{1}
, this induced action is given by

(30)

{\sigma}\cdot x_{u,v}^{(\alpha)}:=x_{\sigma_{a}(u),\sigma_{b}(v)}^{(\alpha)}.

In (
29
), the generating function expands as a sum over the weights of the sets of representations stabilizing in each transversal. Fortunately, we do not have to calculate each of these weights independently, since for each transversal
C\in\mathcal{T}(V)
,
{\sigma}\cdot w(\mathsf{EC}_{C}(\overline{V}))=w(\mathsf{EC}_{{\sigma}(C)}(\overline{V}))
. To see this, note that for all subsets
S,T\subseteq U\subseteq\overline{V}

(31)

{\sigma}\cdot w(\mathcal{R}_{U}(T\to S))=\prod_{\alpha\in Q_{1}}\prod_{u\in T_{s(\alpha)}}\sum_{v\in S_{t(\alpha)}}x_{\sigma_{s(\alpha)}(u),\sigma_{t(\alpha)}(v)}^{(\alpha)}=w(\mathcal{R}_{{\sigma}(U)}({\sigma}(T)\to{\sigma}(S))).

Thus, the action commutes with the attaching weights, yielding
{\sigma}\cdot A(U\setminus T,U)=A({\sigma}(U)\setminus{\sigma}(T),{\sigma}(U))
, and
{\sigma}\cdot w(\mathcal{R}(C))=w(\mathcal{R}({\sigma}(C)))
. By the chain formulation, we have

\displaystyle{\sigma}\cdot w(\mathsf{EC}_{C}(\overline{V}))

\displaystyle=w(\mathcal{R}({\sigma}(C)))\sum_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{C}(C,\overline{V})}(-1)^{|\overline{V}\setminus C|-L(\Gamma)}\prod_{\ell=1}^{L(\Gamma)}A({\sigma}(\Gamma_{\ell})\setminus{\sigma}(\Gamma_{\ell-1}),{\sigma}(\Gamma_{\ell}))

\displaystyle=w(\mathsf{EC}_{{\sigma}(C)}(\overline{V})).

The group
\mathfrak{S}
acts transitively on the set of transversals
\mathcal{T}(V)
. Also, for each transversal
C
, a permutation
{\sigma}\in\mathfrak{S}
fixes
C
if and only if it fixes the single element
c_{a}
in each set
V_{a}
. Thus, the size of the stabilizer of
C
is
\prod_{a\in Q_{0}}(|V_{a}|-1)!=(\mathbf{\overline{V}}-\mathbf{1})!
, where
\mathbf{1}=(1)_{a\in Q_{0}}
. By the orbit-stabilizer theorem, the generating function is an orbit sum over any fixed transversal
C_{0}\in\mathcal{T}(V)
,

(32)

P(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{(\mathbf{\overline{V}}-\mathbf{1})!}\sum_{{\sigma}\in\mathfrak{S}}{\sigma}\cdot w(\mathsf{EC}_{C_{0}}(\overline{V})).

Equivalently, we can write the generating function as a sum over the left cosets of the stabilizer,

(33)

P(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{\overline{{\sigma}}\in\mathfrak{S}/\text{Stab}(C_{0})}{\sigma}\cdot w(\mathsf{EC}_{C_{0}}(\overline{V})).

The generating function is invariant under this action, since for any
{\mu}\in\mathfrak{S}
,

(34)

{\mu}\cdot P(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{(\mathbf{\overline{V}}-\mathbf{1})!}\sum_{{\sigma}\in\mathfrak{S}}{\mu}{\sigma}\cdot w(\mathsf{EC}_{C_{0}}(\overline{V}))=P(\mathbf{x}).

Remark 4.4
.

First, consider a transversal

4.4.
Cardinality of the set of eventually constant representations

In this section, we derive a matrix enumeration formula for the cardinality of the set of eventually constant representations. We pass from the attaching weight matrix
M
to the compressed attaching cardinality matrix
N
. We use the notation and results in Sections
2.6
and
4.3
.

Consider the homomorphism
R_{\overline{V}}\to\mathbb{Z}
induced by specializing all variables to
1
,
x_{v}\mapsto 1
. Since each subgraph of
G(Q)
is assigned a monomial, the polynomial weights of each subset of graphs
\mathcal{A}(T,U)
,
\mathcal{R}(S)
,
\mathcal{D}_{S}(U)
,
\mathsf{EC}_{S}(U)
, and
\mathsf{EC}
evaluate to their cardinalities.

For all subsets

, the attaching set has size

(44)

|\mathcal{A}(U\setminus T,U)|=\prod_{b\in Q_{0}}\mathbf{T}_{b}^{(\mathsf{A}(Q)(\mathbf{U}-\mathbf{T}))_{b}}=\mathbf{T}^{\mathsf{A}(Q)(\mathbf{U}-\mathbf{T})}.

Therefore, Corollary
3.4
yields

(45)

|\mathcal{D}_{S}(U)|=\sum_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{C}(S,U)}(-1)^{|U\setminus S|-L(\Gamma)}\prod_{\ell=1}^{L(\Gamma)}\bm{\Gamma}_{\ell-1}^{\mathsf{A}(Q)(\bm{\Gamma}_{\ell}-\bm{\Gamma}_{\ell-1})}.

The contribution of each chain
\Gamma=(S,\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{L(\Gamma)-1},U)
in
\mathcal{P}(\overline{V})
only depends on the chain of cardinality vectors
(\mathbf{S},\bm{\Gamma}_{1},\ldots,\bm{\Gamma}_{L(\Gamma)-1},\mathbf{U})
in
[\mathbf{0},\mathbf{\overline{V}}]\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}
. Grouping chains by their fiber under this map yields a sum over chains in
[\mathbf{0},\mathbf{\overline{V}}]
. In turn, this sum gives an enumeration formula with the attaching cardinality matrix

, a strictly upper triangular matrix indexed on
[\mathbf{0},\mathbf{\overline{V}}]
.

Theorem 4.7
.

Fix any subsets
S\subseteq U\subseteq\overline{V}
. The cardinality of
\mathcal{D}_{S}(U)
is given by

(46)

|\mathcal{D}_{S}(U)|=(\mathbf{U}-\mathbf{S})!(I-N)^{-1}_{\mathbf{S},\mathbf{U}}.

As a sum over chains
\bm{\Gamma}=(\mathbf{S},\bm{\Gamma}_{1},\ldots,\bm{\Gamma}_{L(\bm{\Gamma})-1},\mathbf{U})
in
\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}
, we also have

(47)

|\mathcal{D}_{S}(U)|=\sum_{\bm{\Gamma}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}}(\mathbf{S},\mathbf{U})}(-1)^{|U\setminus S|-L(\bm{\Gamma})}\binom{\mathbf{U}-\mathbf{S}}{\bm{\Gamma}_{1}-\mathbf{S},\ldots,\mathbf{U}-\bm{\Gamma}_{L(\bm{\Gamma})-1}}\prod_{\ell=1}^{L(\bm{\Gamma})}\bm{\Gamma}_{\ell-1}^{\mathsf{A}(Q)(\bm{\Gamma}_{\ell}-\bm{\Gamma}_{\ell-1})}.

Proof.

Consider the map
\mathcal{P}(\overline{V})\to[\mathbf{0},\mathbf{\overline{V}}]
given by
T\mapsto\mathbf{T}
. Since this map is strictly increasing, it induces a map on chains,
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{P}(\overline{V})}(S,U)\to\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}}(\mathbf{S},\mathbf{U})
, given by
(S,\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{L(\Gamma)-1},U)\mapsto(\mathbf{S},\bm{\Gamma}_{1},\ldots,\bm{\Gamma}_{L(\Gamma)-1},\mathbf{U}).

Now, we fix any chain
\bm{\Gamma}=(\mathbf{S},\bm{\Gamma}_{1},\ldots,\bm{\Gamma}_{d-1},\mathbf{U})
in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}}(\mathbf{S},\mathbf{U})
and calculate the size of its fiber. Consider any subchain
(S,\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{\ell-1})
of subsets that corresponds to subchain
(\bm{\Gamma}_{0},\ldots,\bm{\Gamma}_{\ell-1})
. For each vertex
a\in Q_{0}
, there are
\binom{\mathbf{U}_{a}-(\bm{\Gamma}_{\ell-1})_{a}}{(\bm{\Gamma}_{\ell})_{a}-(\bm{\Gamma}_{\ell-1})_{a}}
ways to extend to an appropriate subset
(\Gamma_{\ell})_{a}\subseteq U_{a}
. Taking the product over each vertex in
Q_{0}
, there are
\binom{\mathbf{U}-\bm{\Gamma}_{\ell-1}}{\bm{\Gamma}_{\ell}-\bm{\Gamma}_{\ell-1}}
ways to choose a set
\Gamma_{\ell}\setminus\Gamma_{\ell-1}
so that
(S,\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{\ell})
extends to
(\bm{\Gamma}_{0},\ldots,\bm{\Gamma}_{\ell})
. Consequently, the size of the fiber is

\binom{\mathbf{U}-\mathbf{S}}{\bm{\Gamma}_{1}-\mathbf{S}}\binom{\mathbf{U}-\bm{\Gamma}_{1}}{\bm{\Gamma}_{2}-\bm{\Gamma}_{1}}\cdots\binom{\mathbf{U}-\bm{\Gamma}_{d-2}}{\bm{\Gamma}_{d-1}-\bm{\Gamma}_{d-2}}\binom{\mathbf{U}-\bm{\Gamma}_{d-1}}{\mathbf{U}-\bm{\Gamma}_{d-1}}=\binom{\mathbf{U}-\mathbf{S}}{\bm{\Gamma}_{1}-\mathbf{S},\ldots,\mathbf{U}-\bm{\Gamma}_{d-1}}.

Grouping chains of subsets by their fiber under this map, we have

\begin{split}&\sum_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{P}(\overline{V})}(S,U)}\prod_{\ell=1}^{L(\Gamma)}\bm{\Gamma}_{\ell-1}^{\mathsf{A}(Q)(\bm{\Gamma}_{\ell}-\bm{\Gamma}_{\ell-1})}\\
&\qquad=\sum_{\bm{\Gamma}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}}(\mathbf{S},\mathbf{U})}\binom{\mathbf{U}-\mathbf{S}}{\bm{\Gamma}_{1}-\mathbf{S},\ldots,\mathbf{U}-\bm{\Gamma}_{L(\bm{\Gamma})-1}}\prod_{\ell=1}^{L(\bm{\Gamma})}\bm{\Gamma}_{\ell-1}^{\mathsf{A}(Q)(\bm{\Gamma}_{\ell}-\bm{\Gamma}_{\ell-1})}.\end{split}

Then, (
47
) follows from (
45
). Finally, we can factor each coefficient in (
47
) by

(48)

(-1)^{|U\setminus S|-L(\bm{\Gamma})}\binom{\mathbf{U}-\mathbf{S}}{\bm{\Gamma}_{1}-\mathbf{S},\ldots,\mathbf{U}-\bm{\Gamma}_{L(\bm{\Gamma})-1}}=(\mathbf{U}-\mathbf{S})!\prod_{\ell=1}^{L(\bm{\Gamma})}\frac{(-1)^{|\bm{\Gamma}_{\ell}-\bm{\Gamma}_{\ell-1}|-1}}{(\bm{\Gamma}_{\ell}-\bm{\Gamma}_{\ell-1})!}.

Decomposing each coefficient in (
47
) by (
48
) gives

|\mathcal{D}_{S}(U)|=(\mathbf{U}-\mathbf{S})!\sum_{\bm{\Gamma}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}}(\mathbf{S},\mathbf{U})}\prod_{\ell=1}^{L(\bm{\Gamma})}N_{\bm{\Gamma}_{\ell-1},\bm{\Gamma}_{\ell}},

which is equivalent to (
46
) by applying the matrix-chain equivalence given in (
21
).
∎

Consider any vertex subsets
S\subseteq U\subseteq\overline{V}
. The cardinality of the set of representations on
S
is
|\mathcal{R}(S)|=\prod_{b\in Q_{0}}\mathbf{S}_{b}^{(\mathsf{A}(Q)\mathbf{S})_{b}}=\mathbf{S}^{\mathsf{A}(Q)\mathbf{S}}
.
Thus, Theorem
4.7
and Lemma
4.2
give

(49)

|\mathsf{EC}_{S}(U)|=|\mathcal{R}(S)||\mathcal{D}_{S}(U)|=\mathbf{S}^{\mathsf{A}(Q)\mathbf{S}}(\mathbf{U}-\mathbf{S})!(I-N)^{-1}_{\mathbf{S},\mathbf{U}}.

In particular, observe that
|\mathsf{EC}_{S}(U)|
depends only on the cardinality vectors
\mathbf{S}
and
\mathbf{U}
.

Recall that
\mathbf{1}=(1,\ldots,1)
is a
Q_{0}
-indexed vector.
Fix any transversal
C\subseteq\overline{V}
. Then
\mathbf{C}=\mathbf{1}
,
|\mathcal{R}(C)|=1
, and
|\mathsf{EC}_{C}(\overline{V})|=(\mathbf{\overline{V}}-\mathbf{1})!(I-N)^{-1}_{\mathbf{1},\mathbf{\overline{V}}}
.
Also, observe that there are
\prod_{a\in Q_{0}}|V_{a}|=\mathbf{\overline{V}}^{\mathbf{1}}
many transversals on
\overline{V}
. Since
\mathbf{\overline{V}}^{\mathbf{1}}(\mathbf{\overline{V}}-\mathbf{1})!=\mathbf{\overline{V}}!
, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 4.8
.

The cardinality of the set of eventually constant set-valued representations is

(50)

|\mathsf{EC}|=\mathbf{\overline{V}}!(I-N)^{-1}_{\mathbf{1},\mathbf{\overline{V}}}.

Stated in terms of chains in
\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}
, the cardinality is given by

(51)

|\mathsf{EC}|=\sum_{\bm{\Gamma}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}}(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{\overline{V}})}(-1)^{|\overline{V}|-|Q_{0}|-L(\bm{\Gamma})}\binom{\mathbf{\overline{V}}}{\mathbf{1},\bm{\Gamma}_{1}-\mathbf{1},\ldots,\mathbf{\overline{V}}-\bm{\Gamma}_{L(\bm{\Gamma})-1}}\prod_{\ell=1}^{L(\bm{\Gamma})}\bm{\Gamma}_{\ell-1}^{\mathsf{A}(Q)(\bm{\Gamma}_{\ell}-\bm{\Gamma}_{\ell-1})}.

Remark 4.9
.

As observed above, the cardinality
|\mathsf{EC}_{S}(U)|
depends only on the cardinality vectors
\mathbf{S}
and
\mathbf{U}
. So, we can define the function

by

such that

and
\mathbf{U}=\mathbf{d}

The recurrence for

in (

\mathsf{A}(Q)\mathbf{c}=(c_{k},c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{k-2},c_{k-1}).

We first modify our conventions. Instead of working in the polynomial ring

Write the right-hand side as

P_{U}(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{T}\geq\mathbf{1}\\
T\subsetneq U\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|U\setminus T|-1}\left(\mathbf{e}_{1}(T)^{\mathbf{U}^{-}}-2^{k}\mathbf{e}_{1}(T)^{\mathbf{U}^{-}-\mathbf{2}}\mathbf{e}_{2}(T)^{\mathbf{1}}\right).

This gives

as a sum over proper subsets

with
\mathbf{T}\geq\mathbf{1}
. To simplify, we pass to a sum over all subsets of

)

\mathbf{e}_{1}(T)^{\mathbf{U}^{-}-\mathbf{2}}

\mathbf{T}

\mathbf{e}_{1}(T)^{\mathbf{U}^{-}}-2^{k}\mathbf{e}_{1}(T)^{\mathbf{U}^{-}-\mathbf{2}}\mathbf{e}_{2}(T)^{\mathbf{1}}
is

, since
\mathbf{e}_{1}(T)^{\mathbf{U}^{-}}
and
\mathbf{e}_{2}(T)^{\mathbf{1}}
are

. Next, we can subtract

from (

P_{U}(\mathbf{x})-F(U)=\sum_{T\subseteq U}(-1)^{|U\setminus T|-1}\left(\mathbf{e}_{1}(T)^{\mathbf{U}^{-}}-2^{k}\mathbf{e}_{1}(T)^{\mathbf{U}^{-}-\mathbf{2}}\mathbf{e}_{2}(T)^{\mathbf{1}}\right).

For each

and

, define the polynomials

A_{i}(S):=(-1)^{n_{i}-|S|}e_{1}(S)^{n_{i-1}},\quad\text{and}\quad B_{i}(S):=(-1)^{n_{i}-|S|}e_{1}(S)^{n_{i-1}-2}2e_{2}(S).

For each

, the terms in (
67
) expand as

(-1)^{|U\setminus T|-1}\mathbf{e}_{1}(T)^{\mathbf{U}^{-}}=-\prod_{i=1}^{k}A_{i}(T_{i}),\quad(-1)^{|U\setminus T|-1}\big(-2^{k}\mathbf{e}_{1}(T)^{\mathbf{U}^{-}-\mathbf{2}}\mathbf{e}_{2}(T)^{\mathbf{1}}\big)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}B_{i}(T_{i}).

Since
\sum_{T_{1}\subseteq U_{1}}\cdots\sum_{T_{k}\subseteq U_{k}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}A_{i}(T_{i})=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{T_{i}\subseteq U_{i}}A_{i}(T_{i})
(and an analogous statement holds for
B_{i}(T_{i})
), we can rewrite (
67
) as

P_{U}(\mathbf{x})-F(U)=-\prod_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{T_{i}\subseteq U_{i}}A_{i}(T_{i})+\prod_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{T_{i}\subseteq U_{i}}B_{i}(T_{i}).

So, the result reduces to proving that
\prod_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{T_{i}\subseteq U_{i}}A_{i}(T_{i})=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{T_{i}\subseteq U_{i}}B_{i}(T_{i})
.

We fix
i=1,\ldots,k
and consider the sum over the
A_{i}(T_{i})
terms first. For each
T_{i}\subseteq U_{i}
, we can expand
e_{1}(T_{i})^{n_{i-1}}
as a sum over functions from
[n_{i-1}]
to
T_{i}
,
\sum_{\varphi:[n_{i-1}]\to T_{i}}\prod_{j=1}^{n_{i-1}}x_{\varphi(j)}
. After, we can swap the order of summation to get

(68)

\displaystyle\sum_{T_{i}\subseteq U_{i}}A_{i}(T_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{T_{i}\subseteq U_{i}}\Big((-1)^{n_{i}-|T_{i}|}\sum_{\varphi:[n_{i-1}]\to T_{i}}\prod_{j=1}^{n_{i-1}}x_{\varphi(j)}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\varphi:[n_{i-1}]\to U_{i}}\sum_{\mathsf{im}\>\varphi\subseteq T_{i}\subseteq U_{i}}(-1)^{n_{i}-|T_{i}|}\prod_{j=1}^{n_{i-1}}x_{\varphi(j)}.

Here
\mathsf{im}\>\varphi
is the image of the map. For each function
\varphi
, we have

\sum_{\mathsf{im}\>\varphi\subseteq T_{i}\subseteq U_{i}}(-1)^{n_{i}-|T_{i}|}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }\mathsf{im}\>\varphi=U_{i},\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Thus, if any
n_{j-1}<n_{j}
, then
\prod_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{T_{j}\subseteq U_{j}}A_{j}(T_{j})=0
. So, this product can be nonzero only if
n_{1}\geq n_{2}\geq\ldots\geq n_{k}\geq n_{1}
, or equivalently, if all cardinalities
n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}
are equal. If so, then (
68
) is a sum over the bijections
\varphi:[n_{i}]\to U_{i}
, which gives
\sum_{T_{i}\subseteq U_{i}}A_{i}(T_{i})=n_{i}!\prod_{v\in U_{i}}x_{v}
. Thus,
\prod_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{T_{j}\subseteq U_{j}}A_{j}(T_{j})=(n_{1}!)^{k}\prod_{v\in U}x_{v}
.

Now, we fix
i=1,\ldots,k
again and do the same for the sum over the
B_{i}(T_{i})
terms,

(69)

\displaystyle\sum_{T_{i}\subseteq U_{i}}B_{i}(T_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{T_{i}\subseteq U_{i}}\Big((-1)^{n_{i}-|T_{i}|}\sum_{\varphi:[n_{i-1}-2]\to T_{i}}\prod_{j=1}^{n_{i-1}-2}x_{\varphi(j)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u,w\in T_{i}\\
u\neq w\end{subarray}}x_{u}x_{w}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\varphi:[n_{i-1}-2]\to U_{i}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u,w\in U_{i}\\
u\neq w\end{subarray}}\sum_{\mathsf{im}\>\varphi\cup\{u,w\}\subseteq T_{i}\subseteq U_{i}}(-1)^{n_{i}-|T_{i}|}x_{u}x_{w}\prod_{j=1}^{n_{i-1}-2}x_{\varphi(j)}.

As before, the alternating sum vanishes unless
\mathsf{im}\>\varphi\cup\{u,w\}=U_{i}
. If
n_{j-1}<n_{j}
for some
j
, then
\prod_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{T_{j}\subseteq U_{j}}B_{j}(T_{j})=0
. If instead all cardinalities
n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}
are equal, then (
69
) is a sum over all functions
\varphi:[n_{i}-2]\to U_{i}
and pairs
u,\,w\in U_{i}
such that
\mathsf{im}\>\varphi\cup\{u,w\}=U_{i}
. These maps
\varphi
are precisely the injections from
[n_{i}-2]
, with
\{u,w\}=U_{i}\setminus\mathsf{im}\>\varphi
. In these cases,
x_{u}x_{w}\prod_{j=1}^{n_{i}-2}x_{\varphi(j)}=\prod_{v\in U_{i}}x_{v}
. There are
(n_{i}-2)!\binom{n_{i}}{2}
ways to choose an injection from
[n_{i}-2]
into
U_{i}
, and there are two ways to order the remaining elements
u,\,w
, so
\sum_{T_{i}\subseteq U_{i}}B_{i}(T_{i})=n_{i}!\prod_{v\in U_{i}}x_{v}
. Thus,
\prod_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{T_{j}\subseteq U_{j}}B_{j}(T_{j})=(n_{1}!)^{k}\prod_{v\in U}x_{v}
.

Therefore,
P_{U}(\mathbf{x})=F(U)
, and the result follows by induction.
∎

6.
Partition algorithm for the Jordan quiver

We shift from matrix enumerators to outline an algorithm inspired by
[
13
,
9
,
16
,
18
,
1
]
.
This gives an alternative method to recursively compute the generating function over Jordan quivers.
Instead of removing sources from acyclic graphs, we record the successive partitions of a finite set
V
induced by identifying vertices mapped to the same output under compositions of increasing length.

Fix a set
V
of cardinality
n\geq 2
and a positive integer
j\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
Q
be the Jordan quiver with
j
loops, which are labeled by
1,\ldots,j
.

6.1.
Successor states

We work with partitions and equivalence relations on a finite set
V
. Elements of a partition
\mathsf{P}
are called
blocks
, and the block containing
u\in V
is denoted
[u]_{\mathsf{P}}
. Note that
u\in V
is in a unique block in a fixed partition.
More generally, if a subset
U\subseteq V
is contained within a block of
\mathsf{P}
, let
[U]_{\mathsf{P}}
denote the block containing
U
. Partitions are ordered so that
\mathsf{P}\leq\mathsf{P}^{\prime}
if
\mathsf{P}
is
finer
than
\mathsf{P}^{\prime}
, meaning each block of
\mathsf{P}
is contained in a unique block of
\mathsf{P}^{\prime}
. We also say that
\mathsf{P}^{\prime}
is
coarser
than
\mathsf{P}
. The finest partition is the
discrete partition

\mathsf{P}_{0}:=\{\{u\}:u\in V\}
, and the coarsest partition is
\{V\}
.

Fix an element
c\in V
. A
state at
c
is a tuple
\mathsf{S}=(\mathsf{P},\mathsf{P}^{\prime},\Phi)
comprised of partitions
\mathsf{P}\leq\mathsf{P}^{\prime}
and an injective map
\Phi:\mathsf{P}^{\prime}\to\mathsf{P}^{j}
, such that
\Phi([c]_{\mathsf{P}^{\prime}})=([c]_{\mathsf{P}},\ldots,[c]_{\mathsf{P}})
. For a block
U\in\mathsf{P}^{\prime}
, we denote the entries of the
j
-tuple
\Phi(U)
as
(\Phi(U)_{1},\ldots,\Phi(U)_{j})
.
Let
\mathcal{S}(c)
denote the set of states at
c
, and consider two of its subsets. Let
\mathcal{S}_{I}(c)\subseteq\mathcal{S}(c)
denote the subset of
initial states

(\mathsf{P}_{0},\mathsf{P},\Phi)
that start with the discrete partition
\mathsf{P}_{0}
, and let
\mathcal{S}_{T}(c)\subseteq\mathcal{S}(c)
denote the subset of
terminal states

(\mathsf{P},\{V\},\Phi)
that end with the coarsest partition
\{V\}
.

Given partitions
\mathsf{P}\leq\mathsf{P}^{\prime}
, there is a canonical surjective quotient map
\mathsf{P}\twoheadrightarrow\mathsf{P}^{\prime}
sending each block
U\in\mathsf{P}
to
[U]_{\mathsf{P}^{\prime}}
, the block in
\mathsf{P}^{\prime}
containing
U
. This map is surjective since each block of
\mathsf{P}^{\prime}
is a union of blocks of
\mathsf{P}
, and this is a quotient map since
\mathsf{P}^{\prime}
is obtained from
\mathsf{P}
by identifying the blocks of
\mathsf{P}
that lie in the same block of
\mathsf{P}^{\prime}
.
This induces a map on
j
-tuples
\mathsf{P}^{j}\to(\mathsf{P}^{\prime})^{j}
. So if we have a state
\mathsf{S}=(\mathsf{P},\mathsf{P}^{\prime},\Phi)\in\mathcal{S}(c)
, the map
\Phi:\mathsf{P}^{\prime}\to\mathsf{P}^{j}
passes to a map
\overline{\Phi}:\mathsf{P}^{\prime}\to(\mathsf{P}^{\prime})^{j}
by the composition

\overline{\Phi}(U):=([\Phi(U)_{1}]_{\mathsf{P}^{\prime}},\ldots,[\Phi(U)_{j}]_{\mathsf{P}^{\prime}}),

where
\Phi(U)_{i}\in\mathsf{P}
is the
i
-th entry of
\Phi(U)
.
Next, we define a partition
\mathsf{P}^{\prime\prime}
and map
\Phi^{\prime}:\mathsf{P}^{\prime\prime}\to(\mathsf{P}^{\prime})^{j}
so that we can form the
successor
of
\mathsf{S}
,
\mathsf{S}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}(c)
, where
\mathsf{S}^{\prime}:=(\mathsf{P}^{\prime},\mathsf{P}^{\prime\prime},\Phi^{\prime})
.
Construct
\mathsf{P}^{\prime\prime}
by identifying elements
u
and
v\in V
if and only if

\overline{\Phi}([u]_{\mathsf{P}^{\prime}})=\overline{\Phi}([v]_{\mathsf{P}^{\prime}})
. Equivalently,
\mathsf{P}^{\prime\prime}
is induced from the fibers of the map

u\mapsto\overline{\Phi}([u]_{\mathsf{P}^{\prime}})
.
Then, define
\Phi^{\prime}:\mathsf{P}^{\prime\prime}\to(\mathsf{P}^{\prime})^{j}
so that for each
u\in V
,

\Phi^{\prime}([u]_{\mathsf{P}^{\prime\prime}}):=\overline{\Phi}([u]_{\mathsf{P}^{\prime}})=([\Phi([u]_{\mathsf{P}^{\prime}})_{1}]_{\mathsf{P}^{\prime}},\ldots,[\Phi([u]_{\mathsf{P}^{\prime}})_{j}]_{\mathsf{P}^{\prime}}).

The function is well-defined and injective since
[U]_{\mathsf{P}^{\prime\prime}}=[T]_{\mathsf{P}^{\prime\prime}}
if and only if
\overline{\Phi}(U)=\overline{\Phi}(T)
. Also
\Phi^{\prime}
preserves
c
, since

\Phi^{\prime}([c]_{\mathsf{P}^{\prime\prime}})=([[c]_{\mathsf{P}}]_{\mathsf{P}^{\prime}},\ldots,[[c]_{\mathsf{P}}]_{\mathsf{P}^{\prime}})=([c]_{\mathsf{P}^{\prime}},\ldots,[c]_{\mathsf{P}^{\prime}}).

6.2.
Recursive algorithm

Fix an element
c\in V
and let
\mathcal{M}(c)
be the subset of representations
W\in\mathsf{SetRep}(Q,V)
whose maps
W_{1},\ldots,W_{j}
fix
c
.

Each representation
W\in\mathcal{M}(c)
induces an initial state,
\mathsf{S}_{1}(W):=(\mathsf{P}_{0},\mathsf{P}_{1},\Phi_{1})
. Form
\mathsf{P}_{1}
by identifying vertices
u,v\in V
whenever
W_{i}(u)=W_{i}(v)
for each map
W_{1},\ldots,W_{j}
, and define the map
\Phi_{1}:\mathsf{P}_{1}\to\mathsf{P}_{0}^{j}
by
\Phi_{1}([u]_{1}):=(\{W_{1}(u)\},\ldots,\{W_{j}(u)\})
. Then
\Phi_{1}
is injective by construction of
\mathsf{P}_{1}
, and since the maps of
W
fix
c
, we have
\Phi_{1}([c]_{1})=(\{c\},\ldots,\{c\})
.

The initial state
\mathsf{S}_{1}(W)
induces a sequence of states at
c
given by the successor relation,
(\mathsf{S}_{m}(W))_{m=1}^{n-1}
. For each
m<n-1
, define
\mathsf{S}_{m+1}(W):=\mathsf{S}_{m}(W)^{\prime}
. We write
\mathsf{S}_{m}(W):=(\mathsf{P}_{m-1},\mathsf{P}_{m},\Phi_{m})
and
[u]_{m}:=[u]_{\mathsf{P}_{m}}
.
By induction, we see that each partition
\mathsf{P}_{m}
identifies vertices
u,v\in V
if and only if
W_{p}(u)=W_{p}(v)
for all paths
p=i_{m}\cdots i_{1}
of length
m
. Also for each
u\in V
,

\Phi_{m}([u]_{m})=([W_{1}(u)]_{m-1},\ldots,[W_{j}(u)]_{m-1}).

Note that if
\mathsf{P}_{m-1}=\mathsf{P}_{m}
, then the sequence of partitions stabilizes since
\mathsf{P}_{m+1}=\mathsf{P}_{m}
; if
[u]_{m+1}=[v]_{m+1}
, then
[W_{i}(u)]_{m}=[W_{i}(v)]_{m}
for each
i=1,\ldots,j
, so
[W_{i}(u)]_{m-1}=[W_{i}(v)]_{m-1}
for each
i=1,\ldots,j
, so
[u]_{m}=[v]_{m}
.

The representation
W\in\mathcal{M}(c)
is eventually constant if and only if the final state
\mathsf{S}_{n-1}(W)
is terminal.
If
W\in\mathsf{EC}
, then all vertices
u
are identified in
\mathsf{P}_{n-1}
since for all
(n-1)
-length paths
p=i_{n-1}\cdots i_{1}
,
W_{p}(u)=c
. So,
\mathsf{P}_{n-1}=\{V\}
. If
W\not\in\mathsf{EC}
, then there is a path
p=i_{n-1}\cdots i_{1}
for which
W_{p}
is not the constant map at
c
. Thus for some
u\in V
, we have
W_{p}(u)\neq c
and
W_{p}(c)=c
, so
[u]_{n-1}\neq[c]_{n-1}
. Consequently,
\mathsf{P}_{n-1}\neq\{V\}
, so
\mathsf{S}_{n-1}(W)
is not terminal.

Next, note that the map
W\mapsto\mathsf{S}_{1}(W)
from
\mathcal{M}(c)\to\mathcal{S}_{I}(c)
is a bijection. For the inverse map, consider an initial state
(\mathsf{P}_{0},\mathsf{P},\Phi)\in\mathcal{S}_{I}(c)
. Since
\mathsf{P}_{0}
is the discrete partition, we can define endomorphisms
W_{1},\ldots,W_{j}\in\mathsf{End}(V)
by
(\{W_{1}(u)\},\ldots,\{W_{j}(u)\}):=\Phi([u]_{\mathsf{P}})
.
Each map
W_{i}
fixes
c
, since
(\{W_{1}(c)\},\ldots,\{W_{j}(c)\})=\Phi([c]_{\mathsf{P}})=(\{c\},\ldots,\{c\}).

Now, we turn to target specialized weights and recursively construct maps

\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{n-1}
that assign weights to states at
c
.
For each initial state
\mathsf{S}\in\mathcal{S}_{I}(c)
, let

\mu_{1}(\mathsf{S}):=w(W)=\prod_{i=1}^{j}\prod_{u\in V}x_{W_{i}(u)}\in R_{V},

for
W
the unique representation in
\mathcal{M}(c)
corresponding to
\mathsf{S}
. Set
\mu_{1}
to
0
elsewhere.
Given the assignment
\mu_{m}
, define
\mu_{m+1}:\mathcal{S}(c)\to R_{V}
. For each state
\mathsf{S}
, let

\mu_{m+1}(\mathsf{S}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathsf{T}:\mathsf{T}^{\prime}=\mathsf{S}\end{subarray}}\mu_{m}(\mathsf{T}).

By induction, the weight assignments admit another characterization.
For each
m\leq n-1
and each state
\mathsf{S}\in\mathcal{S}(c)
,
\mu_{m}(\mathsf{S})
reduces to the weight

\mu_{m}(\mathsf{S})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}W:\mathsf{S}_{m}(W)=\mathsf{S}\end{subarray}}w(W),

the weighted sum over all representations
W
whose
m
-th successor state is
\mathsf{S}
.

Recall that
W\in\mathcal{M}(c)
is eventually constant if and only if
\mathsf{S}_{n-1}(W)
is a terminal state in
\mathcal{S}_{T}(c)
. Therefore, the weight of the eventually constant representations that fix
c
is the following sum over the terminal states

\sum_{\mathsf{S}\in\mathcal{S}_{T}(c)}\mu_{n-1}(\mathsf{S})=\sum_{W\in\mathsf{EC}\cap\mathcal{M}(c)}w(W).

Summing over all elements
c\in V
then yields the generating function,

P(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{c\in V}\sum_{\mathsf{S}\in\mathcal{S}_{T}(c)}\mu_{n-1}(\mathsf{S}).
