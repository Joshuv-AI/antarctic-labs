 This report presents the main outcomes of our research focused on understanding the
behavior and effects of dust particles in fusion plasmas, particularly in the edge regions of
tokamaks and stellarators. We conducted computer modeling studies of burst injections of
carbon and tungsten dust particles in DIII-D like divertor plasmas. The studies investigated
effects of transient influx of the low- and high-Z material plasma contaminants in form of dust
on the edge plasma dynamics in a modern mid-size tokamak. We also performed theoretical and
computational studies of the forces acting on non-spherical dust grains in magnetized and non-
magnetized plasmas. In addition, we cooperated with General Atomics slag management group
on development of DIII-D dust injection experiments to measure trajectories of dust grains in the
divertor plasma and compare them with DUSTT code predictions for validation of the dust
modeling capabilities. We collaborated with JET experimentalists on evaluation of radiative
losses induced by injection of mixed neon-deuterium ice pellets for disruption and run-away
electron generation mitigation during thermal and current quench phases. We also cooperated
with experimentalists at LHD fusion device (Japan) on modeling support of experiments on
injection of boron granules in fusion plasma discharges to assess their dynamics and
effectiveness for in situ dynamic wall conditioning.

 The existence of such force on non-spherical grain in homogeneous, stationary, non-

 Effects of transient carbon and tungsten dust injection on the edge plasmas of a medium size
tokamak were studied using time-dependent simulations with DUSTT/UEDGE coupled codes
[3]. The simulations were performed for low- and high-power plasma regimes in DIII-D like
geometry. The simulations demonstrated that in medium size tokamaks the ability of dust to
reach the core plasma from outer divertor strongly depends on pattern and strength of plasma
flows in the SOL and the dust inertia. In particular, strong plasma flows in the low-power
discharges can cause significant acceleration of carbon dust toroidally and hinder its ability to
penetrate the core due to action of the centrifugal force. While in the high-power plasma regime
and for tungsten dust the possibility for dust to reach the core plasma is mainly controlled by
dust ablation rate in the surrounding plasma. The simulations also show that while magnitude of
impact of dust injection on edge plasma parameters is mainly controlled by amount of dust and is

 3

similar for both carbon and tungsten, the duration of the effects is longer in cases when the dust
can enter the core region. We also found that dust injection causes prompt contamination of the
core plasma with impurities in early stages of dust ablation even when it is localized in the
divertor region, see Fig. 2. The simulations suggest that fast parallel transport and upstream
cross-field impurity diffusion are responsible for such an effect rather than direct downstream
spreading of dust ablated impurities from divertor to the core region. The maximum core
impurity fraction produced by dust injection was evaluated, which shows that already ~10 mg of
injected dust can cause the fraction to exceed maximum tolerable levels, and the core impurity
content become much larger if the dust is able to cross the separatrix into the core.
 We found that radiative plasma cooling by dust-ablated impurities has synergistic effect on
the core impurity contamination, when impurities radiating in the core region further facilitate
dust penetration into the core by reducing dust ablation rate in the cooler edge plasma. The effect
of dust injection on upstream plasma conditions was found to be complex, where electron
temperature at outer mid-plane separatrix can transiently rise in the high-power regime due to
reduction of the anomalous cross-field thermal diffusivity induced by dust injection via chain of
effects in the SOL plasmas.

 The obtained results demonstrate that dust impact on the edge plasmas is a complex
synergistic phenomenon involving the effects of inhomogeneous plasma on dust acceleration and
ablation and conversely the non-local effects of ablated impurities on plasma conditions.
 We cooperated with DIII-D scientists on developing experimental approach to evaluation of
dust injection impact on edge plasma for testing physical models implemented in DUSTT-
UEDGE package [7-9]. The experiments provided new data, which was not available before, by
employing recent hardware upgrades on DIII-D including the Divertor Materials Evaluation
System (DiMES) pellet injector. Experimental validation of DUSTT-UEDGE code is essential
for developing predictive modeling capabilities for evaluation of dust impact on plasmas of
future fusion reactors. For these purposes we performed UEDGE-DUSTT simulations showing
expected dust trajectories in realistic DIII-D plasma conditions constrained with measurements
of 2D profiles of electron density and temperature in DIII-D divertor. The simulations predict
that injections with a few m/s initial speed toward the separatrix is sufficient for a large carbon
grain to reach the core plasma. We also identified an unexpected radial pellet acceleration
 ! !
presumably due to plasma currents and corresponding j × B forces acting on electrically
conducting pellets of large ~1mm sizes.
 We also collaborated with the JET group on studies of the efficacy of shattered
neon/deuterium pellets in reducing the electro-magnetic and the thermal loads during disruptions
and the avoidance/suppression of the formation of runaway electrons [4]. We modelled dynamics
of plasma electron energy losses induced by radiating impurities using a 0D model solving
coupled rate equations for ionization dynamics of all charge states of introduced impurities and
calculations of impurity radiation and total electron energy losses. The simulation results show
that radiation and total electron energy losses on deuterium particles are negligible in comparison
to those on neon, as deuterium is quickly fully ionized in the thermal quench phase. We found
that charge exchange between neon and deuterium has small impact on neon ionization dynamics
and as a consequence on neon radiation losses. Only in a case where large amounts of deuterium
are introduced initially, ionization of deuterium can significantly increase electron density, which
then can increase neon radiation losses. This work complements our studies of the effects of
injected impurities in the form of grains on fusion plasmas.
 In collaboration with National Institute for Fusion Sciences (Japan) and PPPL groups we
also analyzed how boron powder injected into the Large Helical Device (LHD) using an
Impurity Powder Dropper (IPD) behaves under varying drop rates [5,6], revealing that increased
powder rates lead to expanded ablation areas toward the outboard side of the torus due to
enhanced plasma flow in the upper divertor leg; simulations using DUSTT and EMC3-EIRENE
codes, incorporating realistic plasma profiles and powder size distributions, successfully
replicate experimental observations and highlight the importance of controlling powder
trajectories for effective and uniform boron deposition in fusion plasma environments. The
simulations reveal that while plasma flows in the upper divertor leg significantly deflects dust

 5

trajectories, the contribution of boron ions—whether from sputtering on divertor plates or from
the dust particles themselves—to the deflection is negligible due to their low density compared
to the plasma. At higher dust drop rates, radiation cooling from the ablated boron lowers electron
temperatures, reducing plasma flow and shifting ablation positions inward toward the Last
Closed Flux Surface (LCFS). These findings help optimize impurity powder injections for
improved plasma performance and wall conditioning in fusion devices.
