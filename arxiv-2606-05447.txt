Data Detection for Massive MIMO Systems with 1-Bit Quantized Dithered Linear Precoding
Amin Radbord, Antti Tölli, Italo Atzeni

Abstract
The power consumption of the analog-to-digital converters (ADCs) and digital-to-analog converters (DACs) in fully digital massive multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) systems motivates the adoption of low-resolution architectures. In particular, 1-bit DACs reduce the power consumption and hardware complexity at the transmitter, but introduce severe transmit-side quantization distortion. In this paper, we investigate data detection for a point-to-point massive MIMO system with 1-bit DACs at the transmitter, where the linearly precoded signal is dithered prior to quantization, and either full-resolution or 1-bit ADCs at the receiver. Assuming that the dither vector applied at the transmitter is known at the receiver, we first develop soft-estimation-based data detection methods with symbol-independent dither removal for both full-resolution and 1-bit ADCs. We then introduce a new symbol-dependent linearization of the transmitted signal at the output of the 1-bit DACs and use it to derive maximum-likelihood (ML)-based data detection methods that directly recover the data symbol vector from the received signal. For full-resolution ADCs, this leads to an ML-based method with and without dither removal. For 1-bit ADCs, we develop an approximate ML-based method that exploits the derived statistics of the received signal without dither removal. We also propose low-complexity variants of the ML-based methods to mitigate the exponential complexity growth with the number of streams. Numerical results in terms of symbol error rate highlight the critical role of the dither power and demonstrate that the proposed ML-based methods (along with their low-complexity variants) achieve significant gains over a baseline based on binary ML detection via a homotopy algorithm.

I
Introduction

Deploying massive
MIMO
(
MIMO
) systems is essential to exploit the wide bandwidths available at high frequencies for 6G and future wireless networks
[
17
]
. To realize massive
MIMO
arrays, fully digital architectures are particularly attractive since they provide highly flexible beamforming and large-scale spatial multiplexing without the need for the complex beam-management schemes of their hybrid analog-digital counterparts
[
1
]
. However, the power consumption of the
ADC
and
DAC
grows linearly with the bandwidth and exponentially with the number of resolution bits
[
2
]
. This challenge has motivated extensive research on low-resolution
ADC
/
DAC
architectures. Among them, simple 1-bit
ADC
/
DAC
are especially appealing due to their minimal power consumption and complexity
[
32
,
13
,
3
]
, which makes large-scale deployment in massive arrays feasible. In particular, 1-bit
DAC
relax the requirements for costly radio-frequency components at the transmitter and improve the energy efficiency by allowing the power amplifiers to operate without back-off.

I-A

State of the Art

There exists a vast literature on precoding design with 1-bit
DAC
. Symbol-level precoding, which directly designs the 1-bit output exploiting constructive interference or constructive regions
[
22
,
11
]
or based on the
MMSE
(
MMSE
) criterion
[
12
]
, can achieve strong performance at the expense of high computational complexity and symbol-dependent optimization. In contrast, quantized linear precoding applies linear precoding followed by independent 1-bit quantization, thereby offering a favorable trade-off between performance and complexity
[
10
]
. For instance,
[
27
]
focused on linear precoding design for doubly 1-bit quantized systems, i.e., systems employing 1-bit
DAC
and
ADC
; however, its formulation relies on additional analog gains after quantization and involves several analytical approximations. Low-resolution transceiver architectures have also been investigated from energy-efficiency and information-theoretic perspectives. For instance,
[
5
]
proposed optimized precoding to maximize the energy efficiency in a multi-user downlink massive
MIMO
systems with low-resolution
ADC
/
DAC
, while
[
31
]
analyzed the achievable rate of massive
MIMO
relay systems with variable-resolution
ADC
/
DAC
with correlated fading. However, these works do not address direct data detection under transmit-side 1-bit quantization. To mitigate the quantization distortion, Gaussian dithering before the 1-bit
DAC
was proposed in
[
23
,
24
]
. These works showed that a suitable dither power can reduce the strong correlation among quantization distortion components across the transmit antennas and partially restore amplitude information, particularly for higher-order
QAM
(
QAM
) constellations. More broadly, the benefits of dithering are widely recognized in low-resolution quantized systems
[
19
]
, including massive
MIMO
systems with 1-bit
ADC

[
4
]
.

While 1-bit
DAC
reduce power consumption and hardware complexity at the transmitter, they also introduce severe transmit-side quantization distortion, which motivates developing receive-side data detection methods that compensate for this distortion. In this paper, we focus on a point-to-point massive
MIMO
system with 1-bit
DAC
and either full-resolution or 1-bit
ADC
at the receiver. This setting is particularly relevant because the receiver can mitigate the transmit-side distortion by processing the signal received across its many antennas. Despite the advances in precoding design, direct receive-side data detection that accounts for 1-bit
DAC
quantization at the transmitter remains unexplored for point-to-point massive
MIMO
. In general, data detection methods for 1-bit quantized systems have been developed primarily for multi-user uplink massive
MIMO
systems with 1-bit
ADC
at the receiver. In this context,
[
9
]
introduced a two-stage near
ML
(
ML
) data detection method, where the first stage identifies a subset of candidates for the subsequent stage. The work in
[
14
]
considered an approximate
ML
data detection problem and solved it via deep-unfolded gradient descent, while
[
21
]
proposed a gradient-descent-based approach applied to a smooth surrogate of the likelihood function of the 1-bit quantized signals. Moreover,
[
20
]
reduced the
ML
search space through a two-step framework exploiting the Hessian of the log-likelihood objective. The work in
[
16
]
proposed soft-estimation-based data detection techniques based on the statistical properties of the soft-estimated symbols. However, all these methods are tailored to multi-user uplink systems with 1-bit
ADC
and therefore do not directly apply to the point-to-point massive
MIMO
setting considered here. In our case, 1-bit quantization takes place at the transmitter before channel propagation and additive noise, which leads to a fundamentally different data detection problem.

Since no existing data detection method directly matches the considered system model, a natural baseline consists in first detecting the binary transmit signal at the output of the 1-bit
DAC
and then recovering the original data symbols in a subsequent
ML
-based data detection stage. This motivates considering binary data detection methods as a starting point. Among them,
[
26
]
introduced binary
ML
detection based on a homotopy algorithm for multi-user uplink massive
MIMO
systems with either full-resolution or 1-bit
ADC
under quadrature phase-shift keying signaling, whose real-valued representation yields a binary transmit vector. The homotopy algorithm traces a solution path from a convex relaxation to the original nonconvex binary
ML
problem, thereby reducing the risk of convergence to poor local minima. This method was shown to outperform approaches based on sphere decoding
[
6
]
, semidefinite relaxation
[
28
]
, and
AMP
(
AMP
)
[
18
]
in systems with full-resolution
ADC
, as well as generalized
AMP

[
29
,
30
]
and the two-stage near
ML
data detection method from
[
9
]
with 1-bit
ADC
. However, the formulation in
[
26
]
ultimately addresses binary
ML
detection at the receiver and does not account for the effect of 1-bit
DAC
at the transmitter on the recovery of phase and amplitude information. Therefore, applying it to our setting would require an additional
ML
-based data detection stage after detecting the output of the
DAC
, which introduces error propagation between the two stages.

I-B

Contribution

In this paper, we consider a point-to-point massive
MIMO
system with 1-bit
DAC
and either full-resolution or 1-bit
ADC
at the receiver. Unlike
[
23
,
24
]
, which optimized and evaluated dithering for multi-user downlink transmission with single-antenna users, we focus on a distinct multi-stream point-to-point massive
MIMO
setting in which the transmitter employs linear precoding and dithering followed by 1-bit quantization. In this setting, dithering is not only a transmit-side design parameter but also directly shapes the statistics of the received signal and the resulting data detection problem. This motivates the development of data detection methods that explicitly account for dithering at the transmitter. In this context, we propose new soft-estimation-based and
ML
-based data detection methods for systems with full-resolution and 1-bit
ADC
. For soft-estimation-based data detection, we derive new combining matrices with dither removal based on the
LMMSE
(
LMMSE
) criterion. For
ML
-based data detection, we develop new methods both with and without dither removal. The proposed methods capture different trade-offs between performance and computational complexity under distinct
ADC
resolutions and operating regimes.

The contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:

•

Assuming that the dither vector applied at the transmitter is known at the receiver, so that dither removal can be performed, we propose soft-estimation-based data detection methods with symbol-independent dither removal, namely

BLMMSE-DR
(
BLMMSE-DR
)
and

D-BLMMSE-DR
(
D-BLMMSE-DR
)
for full-resolution and 1-bit
ADC
, respectively.

•

We introduce a new symbol-dependent linearization of the transmitted signal at the output of the 1-bit
DAC
. Based on this, we develop an
ML
-based data detection method for full-resolution
ADC
that directly detects the data symbol vector from the received signal. We begin by presenting this method without dither removal, deriving the full likelihood function of the received signal along with its statistics. We then incorporate symbol-dependent dither removal, which yields the

ML-DR
(
ML-DR
)
method. For data detection with 1-bit
ADC
, we develop an approximate

-based method, referred to as

D-ML
(
D-ML
)
, which exploits the previously derived statistics of the received signal without dither removal. For both proposed data detection methods, we also develop low-complexity variants to mitigate the exponential complexity growth with the number of streams.

•

Numerical results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed methods in terms of
SER
(
SER
), also in comparison with a baseline referred to as
two-stage hoML
(
two-stage hoML
), whose first stage employs the homotopy algorithm in
[
26
]
. First, the results highlight the critical role of the dither power: an optimal operating point appears only at moderate-to-high

(

), while all the methods benefit from dither removal for moderate-to-large dither powers. Second, thanks to dither removal, the soft-estimation-based methods can even outperform the

baseline despite their significantly lower complexity. Third, the proposed

-based methods provide the largest gains:

and

, together with their low-complexity variants, consistently outperform both the soft-estimation-based methods and
two-stage hoML
for full-resolution and 1-bit
ADC
, respectively.

Part of this work was presented in our conference paper
[
15
]
, which derived the likelihood function of the received signal along with its statistics and proposed the
ML
-based data detection method without dither removal for the case of full-resolution
ADC
.

III
Linearization of the Transmitted and Received Signals

The 1-bit
DAC
and
ADC
introduce nonlinear distortion at the transmitter and receiver, respectively, which requires the linearization of their input-output relationships to obtain a tractable framework. In this regard, we first review the Bussgang decomposition to linearize the 1-bit quantized transmitted and received signals, along with the corresponding receiver design used for the soft-estimation-based data detection baseline considered for comparison. Then, we propose a new symbol-dependent linearization of the transmitted signal, which serves as the basis for the proposed
ML
-based data detection methods presented in Sections
IV
and
V
.

III-A

Symbol-Independent Linearization of the Transmitted and Received Signals

In this section, we utilize the well-known Bussgang decomposition
[
7
]
, which allows to represent the output of a nonlinear system as a scaled version of the input plus an uncorrelated distortion term.

We linearize the transmitted signal
\mathbf{x}_{\textrm{q}}
in (
2
) with respect to
\mathbf{x}_{\textrm{d}}
(and, thus, with respect to
\mathbf{u}
and
\mathbf{d}
) as

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{\textrm{q}}=\mathbf{F}_{\textnormal{\tiny{TX}}}\mathbf{x}_{\textrm{d}}+\mathbf{q}_{\textnormal{\tiny{TX}}},

(7)

where
\mathbf{q}_{\textnormal{\tiny{TX}}}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}^{N}
is a zero-mean, non-Gaussian distortion vector that is uncorrelated with
\mathbf{x}_{\textrm{d}}
and
\mathbf{F}_{\textnormal{\tiny{TX}}}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}^{N\times N}
is the symbol-independent Bussgang gain matrix at the transmitter. To obtain
\mathbf{F}_{\textnormal{\tiny{TX}}}
in closed form, we assume
\mathbf{x}_{\textrm{d}}\sim\mathcal{C}\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}_{N},\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{x}_{\textrm{d}}})

Doubly 1-Bit Quantized

Here, we turn to

-based data detection. We begin by noting that the symbol-dependent dither removal adopted in Section

for full-resolution

24

\mathbf{y}

\mathbf{r}

remains input-dependent for any dither distribution
[
19

, and the term associated with the dither vector
\mathbf{d}
does not appear as an independent additive contribution that can be removed by direct subtraction. Accordingly, we first use the statistics of the received signal
\mathbf{y}
(without dither removal) given in Section
IV-B
to develop an approximated

-based method based on the 1-bit quantized received signal
\mathbf{r}
in (
4
). Then, we introduce a low-complexity variant with reduced search space.

D-ML
.
Defining
\tilde{\mathbf{r}}\triangleq\big[\mathrm{Re}[\mathbf{r}]^{\mathrm{T}},\mathrm{Im}[\mathbf{r}]^{\mathrm{T}}\big]^{\mathrm{T}}\in\mathbb{R}^{2M}
and recalling the definition of
\tilde{\mathbf{y}}
from Section
IV-B
, we have

\tilde{\mathbf{r}}
depends only on the sign of the corresponding element of
\tilde{\mathbf{y}}
. Moreover, let
\mathcal{P}\triangleq\big\{m\in\{1,\dots,2M\}:\tilde{y}_{m}\geq 0\big\}
and
\mathcal{\bar{P}}\triangleq\big\{m\in\{1,\dots,2M\}:\tilde{y}_{m}<0\big\}
denote, respectively, the sets of the indices corresponding to the positive and negative elements of
\tilde{\mathbf{y}}

\tilde{y}_{m}
is the
m

\tilde{\mathbf{y}}

\tilde{\mathbf{r}}

\tilde{\mathbf{y}}

\tilde{\mathbf{r}}
for a given
\mathbf{x}
can be defined as

\displaystyle\zeta_{\textrm{D-ML}}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle\triangleq\mathbb{P}[\tilde{y}_{m}\geq 0,\forall m\in\mathcal{P},\ \tilde{y}_{m}<0,\forall m\in\mathcal{\bar{P}}|\mathbf{x}].

(38)

Now, we focus on the statistics of
\tilde{\mathbf{y}}
to derive the probability in (
38

IV-B

\tilde{\mathbf{y}}

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{y}}=\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\tilde{\mathbf{y}}|\mathbf{x}}+\tilde{\mathbf{n}}^{\prime},

(39)

with
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\tilde{\mathbf{y}}|\mathbf{x}}
given in (

\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\tilde{\mathbf{n}}}(\mathbf{x})
derived in (

\tilde{\mathbf{n}}
approximately follows

(see Section

\tilde{\mathbf{n}}^{\prime}
approximately follows
\mathcal{N}\big(\mathbf{0}_{2M},\mathbf{\Sigma}_{\tilde{\mathbf{n}}}(\mathbf{x})\big)
, with
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{\tilde{\mathbf{n}}}(\mathbf{x})
derived in (
67
) in Appendix
B
.

To make (

\mathbf{\Sigma}_{\tilde{\mathbf{n}}}(\mathbf{x})
and treating the elements of
\tilde{\mathbf{n}}^{\prime}
as independent random variables, which in turn implies independent elements of
\tilde{\mathbf{y}}
.

\displaystyle=\prod_{m=1}^{2M}\Phi(\bar{\mu}_{\tilde{\mathbf{y}}|\mathbf{x},m}),

VI
Complexity Analysis and Numerical Results

In this section, we first analyze the computational complexity of the proposed methods and then present numerical results to evaluate their performance.

VI-A

Complexity Analysis

The computational complexity of the proposed methods is provided in Table
I
. We distinguish between complexity per channel, accounting for the computations required for a given channel realization, and complexity per data symbol vector.

Data detection with full-resolution
ADC
.
The complexity of
BLMMSE-DR
in Section
IV-A
is
\mathcal{O}(M^{3}+MN^{2})
per channel due to matrix inversion and multiplications for obtaining the soft estimates, and
\mathcal{O}(KL)
per data symbol vector due to the minimum-distance mapping in (
6
).
ML
in Section
IV-B
requires computing the statistics of the received signal for all the
L^{K}
candidate data symbol vectors for each channel realization, which yields a per channel complexity of
\mathcal{O}(MN^{2}L^{K})
. It then performs an exhaustive search over the same
L^{K}
candidates, with complexity per data symbol vector of
\mathcal{O}(M^{3}L^{K})
. The full-search
ML-DR
method in Section
IV-B
evaluates the likelihood function in (
33
) for all the
L^{K}
candidates. For each candidate, obtaining the symbol-dependent statistics has complexity of
\mathcal{O}(MN^{2})
, while evaluating (
33
) with the full covariance matrix
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{{\tilde{\mathbf{y}}}^{\textrm{(DR)}}|{\mathbf{x}}}
additionally requires matrix inversion and log-determinant computation with complexity of
\mathcal{O}(M^{3})
. Therefore, the total complexity of full-search
ML-DR
is
\mathcal{O}\big((M^{3}+MN^{2})L^{K}\big)
. As discussed in Section
IV-B
, neglecting the off-diagonal elements of the covariance matrix reduces the total complexity to
\mathcal{O}(MN^{2}L^{K})
, whereas restricting the search space to
\nu^{K}
candidates identified by
BLMMSE-DR
, with
\nu\leq L
, further reduces the complexity to
\mathcal{O}(MN^{2}\nu^{K})
.

Data detection with 1-bit
ADC
.
The complexity of
D-BLMMSE-DR
in Section
V-A
is the same as that of
BLMMSE-DR
. The full-search
D-ML
method in Section
V-B
has total complexity of
\mathcal{O}(MN^{2}L^{K})
, as it neglects the off-diagonal elements of
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{\tilde{\mathbf{n}}}(\mathbf{x})
. Similar to
ML-DR
, the complexity reduces to
\mathcal{O}(MN^{2}\nu^{K})
when the search space is restricted to
\nu^{K}
candidates identified by
D-BLMMSE-DR
.

Owing to their moderate complexity, the proposed soft-estimation-based method can support large
K
. On the other hand, the
ML
-based methods are mainly suitable for small-to-moderate
K
and large
M
.

VII
Conclusions

In this paper, we studied data detection for a point-to-point massive
MIMO
system with 1-bit quantized dithered linear precoding at the transmitter and either full-resolution or 1-bit
ADC
at the receiver. Assuming that the dither vector applied at the transmitter is known at the receiver, we developed soft-estimation-based data detection methods with symbol-independent dither removal, namely
BLMMSE-DR
and
D-BLMMSE-DR
for full-resolution and 1-bit
ADC
, respectively. Then, we introduced a new symbol-dependent linearization of the 1-bit quantized transmitted signal, based on which we derived
ML
-based data detection methods that directly recover the data symbol vector from the received signal. Specifically, we proposed
ML-DR
for full-resolution
ADC
, which incorporates symbol-dependent dither removal, and
D-ML
for 1-bit
ADC
. For both methods, we developed low-complexity variants to mitigate the exponential complexity growth with the number of data streams. Numerical results in terms of
SER
highlighted the critical role of the dither power and showed that the proposed
ML
-based methods, together with their low-complexity variants, achieve significant gains over both the soft-estimation-based methods and the
two-stage hoML
baseline. For full-resolution
ADC
,
ML-DR
consistently outperformed
two-stage hoML
and achieved up to two orders of magnitude lower
SER
than the soft-estimation-based methods, while dither removal provided a
2\times

SER
reduction compared with the case without dither removal. For 1-bit
ADC
,
D-ML
achieved a
10\times

SER
reduction over the other methods. Future work may consider extension to imperfect
CSI
, the optimization of the dither power, and precoding design tailored to the proposed data detection methods.

Appendix C
Formulation of Two-Stage hoML

In the first stage of
two-stage hoML
, we estimate
\mathbf{x}_{\textrm{q}}
in (
2
) (i.e., the output of the 1-bit
DAC
) by means of the homotopy algorithm in
[
26
]
, which approximates a binary
ML
detection problem by solving a sequence of penalty-based non-convex relaxations. In the second stage, we apply full-search
ML
-based data detection to this estimate to detect the data symbol vector
\mathbf{u}
. Define
\tilde{\mathbf{u}}\triangleq\big[\mathrm{Re}[\mathbf{u}]^{\mathrm{T}},\mathrm{Im}[\mathbf{u}]^{\mathrm{T}}\big]^{\mathrm{T}}\in\mathbb{R}^{2K}
and

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{\textrm{q}}

\displaystyle\triangleq\big[\mathrm{Re}[\mathbf{x}_{\textrm{q}}]^{\mathrm{T}},\mathrm{Im}[\mathbf{x}_{\textrm{q}}]^{\mathrm{T}}\big]^{\mathrm{T}}=[\tilde{x}_{\textrm{q},1},\ldots,\tilde{x}_{\textrm{q},2N}]\in\mathbb{R}^{2N},

(68)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{W}}

\displaystyle\triangleq\begin{bmatrix}\mathrm{Re}[\mathbf{W}]&-\mathrm{Im}[\mathbf{W}]\\
\mathrm{Im}[\mathbf{W}]&\mathrm{Re}[\mathbf{W}]\end{bmatrix}^{\mathrm{T}}=[\tilde{\mathbf{w}}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{\mathbf{w}}_{2N}]\in\mathbb{R}^{2K\times 2N}.

(69)

In the first stage, the penalty-based
ML
detection problem of the binary signal
\mathbf{x}_{\textrm{q}}
based on either
\mathbf{y}
in (
3
) or
\mathbf{r}
in (
4
) is formulated as
[
26
]

\displaystyle\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}-\sqrt{\frac{\eta_{\textnormal{\tiny{RX}}}}{2}}\leq\tilde{x}_{\textrm{q},n}\leq\sqrt{\frac{\eta_{\textnormal{\tiny{RX}}}}{2}},\\
\forall n\in\{1,\dots,2N\}\end{subarray}}{\mathrm{minimize}}

\displaystyle\ \zeta_{\textrm{bML}}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{\textrm{q}})-\lambda\|\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{\textrm{q}}\|_{2}^{2},

(70)

with
\lambda\geq 0
and where
\zeta_{\textrm{bML}}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{\textrm{q}})
is the log-likelihood function of
\tilde{\mathbf{y}}
or
\tilde{\mathbf{r}}
, as given in
[
26
]
. For a given
\lambda>0
, the penalty term
-\lambda\|\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{\textrm{q}}\|_{2}^{2}
pushes the entries of
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{\textrm{q}}
toward the boundaries
\pm\sqrt{\frac{\eta_{\textnormal{\tiny{RX}}}}{2}}
. Then, the homotopy algorithm in
[
26
]
gradually increases

and finally returns the binary signal
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{\textrm{q}}^{\star}\triangleq[\tilde{x}_{\textrm{q},1}^{\star},\ldots,\tilde{x}_{\textrm{q},2N}^{\star}]\in\mathbb{R}^{2N}
as an estimate of
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{\textrm{q}}
. In the second stage, following similar steps as in (

)–(
44
), the
ML
data detection problem applied to
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{\textrm{q}}^{\star}
is formulated as

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69054pt\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{\textrm{two-stage hoML}}=\operatornamewithlimits{argmax}_{\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{S}^{K}}\sum_{n=1}^{2N}\mathrm{log}\bigg(\Phi\bigg(\sqrt{\frac{2}{\sigma^{2}}}\tilde{x}_{\textrm{q},n}^{\star}\tilde{\mathbf{w}}_{n}^{\mathrm{T}}\tilde{\mathbf{u}}\bigg)\bigg).

(71)

The

, where

is the number of homotopy iterations in the first stage.
