1.0 Background
 The Atmospheric Radiation Measurement (ARM) User Facility, established in 1989 with funding from
 the U.S. Department of Energy (DOE), provides researchers with strategically located atmospheric
 observatories and data to advance basic science questions for understanding the Earth’s atmosphere.
 ARM data are being used to improve the representation of clouds, aerosols, and their interactions with
 Earth’s radiant energy in earth system models. ARM operates a network of fixed, mobile, and aerial
 observatories that are openly available to the scientific community.

 At these observatories, ARM operates more than 370 instrument systems that provide ground-based
 observations of aerosol, surface, cloud, and precipitation properties and more. To ensure the continued
 excellence of these observations, ARM relies on a team of expert instrument scientists and engineers,
 known as mentors.

 Mentors play a critical role in the stewardship and evolution of ARM instrumentation. They are expected
 to have deep expertise in the theory, operation, and performance of their instrument areas along with a
 broad understanding of relevant atmospheric processes. In addition to ensuring high-quality data
 production, mentors contribute to advancing scientific capabilities by developing novel data retrievals and
 analysis approaches that enhance the usability and impact of ARM data.

 ARM seeks to engage the most qualified individual(s) or collaborative teams to serve as mentors.
 Selection is based on a combination of technical expertise, scientific insight, demonstrated experience,
 and the ability to support ARM’s mission in a timely and collaborative manner. ARM is committed to an
 open and transparent selection process.

 Roles and responsibilities for lead mentors are outlined for mentors based at universities and national
 laboratories.

2.0 Lead Mentor Selection Policy
 ARM’s policy for selecting mentors is to identify, evaluate, and select the most qualified candidates with
 demonstrated expertise in relevant measurements, instrumentation, and/or software-hardware integration.
 Candidates should have recognized experience with the specific instrument or similar instrument systems
 and be able to perform the mentor duties in a timely manner. ARM designates a lead mentor for each
 system who serves at the subject-matter expert for that instrument and resultant measurements and is
 accountable for the overall performance, data quality, and advancement of the instrument.

3.0 Lead Mentor Selection

 3.1 Process
  • The ARM Instrument Operations Manager holds overall responsibility for the selection of instrument
 mentors across the ARM facility, with the exception of instruments associated with the ARM Aerial
 Facility (AAF. The ARM AAF Manager oversees the mentorship selection for the AAF-related
 systems).

 1

 • When a mentorship opportunity becomes available, an announcement is posted to the ARM website
 and distributed through newsletters and social media as possible. In addition, qualified candidates
 from the broader scientific and technical community may be directly encouraged to apply.
 • Interested individuals or teams are required to submit:
 – A current resume or CV for each member supporting the mentorship
 • A statement of qualifications as they relate to the criteria outline in 3.2
 – A budget proposal for the proposed level of effort
 – Any additional materials requested in the announcement
 • All applications are compiled and distributed to the Mentor Selection Committee, which is
 responsible for evaluating the candidates and providing scores based on the criteria in section 3.2. The
 ARM Instrument Operations Manager coordinates the review process and ensures consistency and
 transparency.

3.2 Criteria
Candidates will be selected based on a weighted criterion. Generally, the weights are an even split across
criteria but may change based on the needs of the mentorship position and will be predetermined before
applications are accepted. The candidates will be scored from 1 to 5 in the following areas. The scores
will be averaged from all members of the selection committee and a final score given to each candidate.
 • Technical experience operating and calibrating instruments
 Hands-on experience with the instrument to be mentored, or a closely related instrument, including
 calibration, troubleshooting, and evaluating instrument performance.
 • Technical approach (optional, depending on mentorship call)
 For mentorships involving new or evolving measurements, candidates may be asked to propose a
 technical approach. This includes recommended instrumentation, deployment strategies, and methods
 to meet ARM’s measurement objectives, Full requirements will be noted in the mentorship call.
 • Scientific background
 Demonstrated experience using atmospheric data for scientific research. Mentors are expected to
 engage with the research community to support ARM’s science goals when applicable.
 • ARM familiarity and experience
 Prior experience with ARM processes, tools, and data systems is not required but can facilitate a
 smoother startup or transition into the mentorship role.
 • Institutional resources and support
 The candidate’s institution should offer complementary expertise and logistical support (e.g.,
 technical staff, laboratory space, computing resources). Institutional capacity to support a team-based
 mentorship model is also considered.
 • Budget constraints (not part of weighted score)
 As with any program, the fee provided to a mentor is commensurate with the level of expertise
 required by the ARM User Facility for a particular measurement capability and the availability of
 funds. While there is some room for negotiating effort, cost is a significant factor.

 2

 Also, of consideration is the accessibility to instrument locations. It is anticipated that multiple site
 visits may be required per year. Reasonable proximity to a site and/or the ease of travel to a site will
 impact the budget.

 The budget will factor into the final discussions after the scores in the areas noted above have been
 assessed.

For many ARM instruments, well-established, consensus practices exist to guide operation and
calibration. However, some instruments are sufficiently new, complex, or outside the normal range of
ARM measurements that consensus practices are not yet established or known within the ARM
community. For these special cases, the mentor selection criteria must be identified by the Mentor
Selection Committee to determine if additional requirements are needed before a new lead mentor
position is announced.

To reduce ambiguity and improve consistency in the evaluation process, a standardized set of
guidelines(Table 1) will be followed for assessing candidates for lead mentor positions.

The decision to compete a mentorship will be based on a majority vote by the committee with the
Instrument Operations Manager holding the deciding vote in the result of a tie.

3.3 Mentor Replacement
At times, a lead mentor may need to change due to retirement, resignation, or performance concerns. In
such cases, the Instrument Operations Manager must be notified with as much advanced notice as
possible by either the departing lead mentor or their home institution. A succession plan should identify
either an existing associate mentor who could transition to the lead mentor role or a new candidate. If a
viable succession plan is not provided, the Mentor Selection Committee can vote to compete the
mentorship. The viability of a succession plan will be determined by the Mentor Selection Committee.

When a transition is necessary, the lead mentor’s institution may propose a qualified replacement
candidate. The Mentor Selection Committee will review the candidate’s materials as required in 3.1 to
determine whether the individual meets the selection criteria. If the proposed candidate is approved, they
may be appointed directly without a full, open call.

If the outgoing lead mentor is unable to continue their responsibilities during the transition period, the
Selection Committee may take one of the following actions:
 • Approve the institution’s replacement candidate as lead mentor, with or without caveats. If concerns
 are raised, a follow-up review may be required after a defined probationary period.
 • Initiate a full, open search for a new lead mentor. The institution’s proposed candidate may still be
 considered and will serve as the acting lead mentor until the search is complete.

It is common for external parties to express interest in competing for ARM mentorship roles. Historically,
ARM has granted the right of first refusal to the lead mentor’s home institution to propose a replacement
lead mentor. However, if a viable succession plan is not in place prior to the transition time, the
mentorship may be opened to a competitive selection process. During such competitions, all qualified
candidates – including those outside the current institution – are invited to apply. When evaluating
candidates for mentorships associated with larger teams or established institutional groups (radar or
aerosols), the institutional resources and support provided will be given additional consideration in the
selection criteria.

While ARM strives for a competitive mentor selection process, the Instrument Operations Manager
reserves the right to appoint lead mentors and restructure mentorship teams to best support the facility’s
scientific and operational goals. This includes adjustments in response to significant or unforeseen
budgetary changes, shifts in programmatic priorities, or evolving instrument needs. Such actions will be
made in consultation with the Mentor Selection Committee and ensure that ARM maintains flexibility to
optimize resources allocation, maintain data quality, and uphold its commitment to the user community.
ARM will strive to communicate changes transparently and engage affected personnel during any
transition process.

3.4 Mentor Reviews

3.4.1 Routine Reviews

Mentorships will undergo a routine review every three to four years as part of ARM’s continuous
improvement efforts. The goal of this process is to ensure that mentorship roles are effectively supporting
ARM’s mission and delivering high-quality data to the scientific community.

Areas of Evaluation

The routine review will assess multiple aspects of each mentorship, including but not limited to:
 • Data quality: Evaluation of instrument performance and the mentor's role in ensuring high-quality,
 reliable data.
 • Productivity metrics: Review of ingest and downstream value-added product (VAP) performance,
 including output volume and quality.
 • Responsiveness to issues: Analysis of the mentor’s response time and effectiveness in addressing
 instrument problems, including DQPR (Data Quality Problem Report) statistics.
 • Self-assessment: A summary from the mentor outlining current challenges, barriers, and opportunities
 for improvement.
 • Additional factors: Consideration of any other areas relevant to the performance or impact of the
 mentorship.

These reviews are intended to be constructive, with the goal of identifying areas for improvement,
realigning efforts with ARM’s strategic goals, or, if necessary, determining whether a mentorship should
be competed.

3.4.2 In-Depth Review

If a routine review identifies significant concerns or unresolved issues or if significant concerns arise in
another way, an in-depth review of the mentorship may be initiated. This review may be initiated in the
following situations:
 • Lack of transparency in how mentorship resources are being allocated or managed.
 • Concerns regarding the cost-to-benefit ratio of the mentorship’s activities (e.g., cost per instrument
 relative to other mentorships accounting for instrument complexity)
 • Consistent lack of engagement with ARM infrastructure or the broader user community.
 • A pattern of not completing agreed-upon tasks, or persistent underperformance in instrument
 management.
 • A majority determination by the Mentor Selection Committee that additional review is warranted.

The Instrument Operations Manager (IOM) will formally notify the mentor team of the review and
outline the specific areas requiring further evaluation.

 5

Review Process
 • The IOM and the mentor team may meet regularly during the review period to address outstanding
 questions or provide clarification.
 • All efforts will be made to minimize disruption to normal mentorship activities during this time.
 • Additional reviewers may be engaged, as needed, to provide technical or programmatic input. These
 reviews will be conducted with appropriate sensitivity and confidentiality.

Outcomes and Actions

Upon completion, the IOM will present the findings of the in-depth review to the Mentor Selection
Committee for deliberation. Based on the review outcomes, the committee may take one of the following
actions:
1. Implement an improvement plan: If the concerns are actionable, the mentor team will be provided
 with a clear improvement plan, including defined goals and timelines for resolution.
2. Initiate a competitive process: If the concerns are not deemed efficiently resolvable through an
 improvement plan or the mentor team fails to meet expectations of the improvement plan, immediate
 changes are required – the mentorship may be competed through an open selection process.

In cases where improvements are attempted but remain unsatisfactory, the Mentor Selection Committee
retains the authority to initiate competition at a later stage based on majority vote.

3.5 Associate Mentors
While the Mentor Selection Process is used to appoint all lead mentors, lead mentors may designate
associate mentors to assist in fulfilling their responsibilities. These individuals play a key role in
supporting instrument operations, particularly in systems with complex requirements or broad technical
scope. In some cases, especially for complex instruments, associate mentors may have a particular skill
set that complements the lead mentor’s (software or hardware expertise). In other cases, due to
similarities in instrumentation, lead mentors may have mentorship of more than one unique instrument.

In these cases, the lead mentor may share his/her funds for ARM instrument support. If ARM funds are
shared, the lead mentor is required to notify the ARM Instrument Operations Manager and provide a brief
description of the assistant’s duties and a copy of the assistant’s resume. These personnel are designated
as associate mentors and must be registered in ARM’s mentor metadata database. Although associate
mentors can perform all routine mentor duties, only the ARM lead mentor has final responsibility for all
data quality and troubleshooting issues. Typical duties performed (but limited to) by ARM associate
mentors are:
 • Instrument troubleshooting and repairs (hardware, software, data communications, etc.)
 • Instrument calibrations
 • Submitting and tracking information for all ARM instrument-related databases with the exception of
 Data Quality Reports (DQRs)—only mentors can submit DQRs, preferably the lead mentor.
 However, in the case of a mentor team, one member of the team must be identified as being

 responsible for the DQRs if different than the lead mentor. The ARM Instrument Operations Manager
 and the ARM Data Quality Manager must be notified.
  • Writing and submitting documentation in support of ARM required reports
  • Providing instrument training.

 3.6 Co-Memberships
 In the special case where an ARM lead mentor has a commercial interest in his/her instrument, a
 co-mentor may be necessary and will be determined by the ARM Instrument Operations Manager. The
 co-mentor cannot be affiliated with the lead mentor’s institution and is the only mentor that can request
 and justify major repairs and upgrades for the instrument. The purpose of co-mentorship is to avoid any
 perception of impropriety in using ARM funds. While most instances of assigning co-mentorship to date
 have been due to specific contracts with small vendors, such decisions are determined on a case-by-case
 basis. Not all cases require co-mentors. Usually, major upgrades (depending on cost) are vetted through
 Engineering Requests that require ARM approval. In other cases, the ARM Instrument Operations
 Manager will coordinate with the ARM Engineering Manager and approve the request. In these situations,
 there is an intervening review and approval process that can be used to assure proper allocations of funds
 for instrument repairs and upgrades.

4.0 ARM Lead Mentor Selection Committee
 The ARM Mentor Selection Committee is composed of:
  • ARM Instrument Operations Manager (Chair)
  • ARM Technical Director
  • ARM Associate Director for Operations
  • ARM Data Quality Office Representative
  • ARM Site Manager(s)
  • Subject-matter expert (optional) – Can be from the User Executive Committee (UEC), Atmospheric
 System Research (ASR) community, or other if they are willing to contribute.

 Additional temporary members may be appointed as needed by the committee chair upon approval by the
 DOE Program Manager to serve as subject-matter experts (with full voting rights) in consideration of a
 specific candidate pool of lead mentor applicants for a specific instrument or instrument system. The
 temporary members would only serve as needed and not become permanent members of the committee.
 The ARM IOM is responsible for assembling resumes of qualified candidates and distributing the
 documents to committee members for review prior to a meeting. Meetings will be arranged by the ARM
 IOM and will be conducted by conference calls or in conjunction with other meetings where members are
 present. After review and discussion, the lead mentor is selected based on the weighted scores. The
 chair casts the tie-breaking vote if needed. If the lead mentor candidate is from the institution of the chair,
 the chair will be disqualified as the tie-breaker and the Director will cast the tie-breaking vote.
