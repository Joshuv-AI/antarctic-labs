Scanning electron microscopy and energy dispersive X-ray analysis evaluation of silver nanoparticles-modified bioceramic and resin-based root canal sealers
Authors: Hetal Jivraj Maheshwari, Kailash M. Attur, Nikunj A. Patel, Shylaja K. Attur, Rutvi C. Upadhyay, Shraddha S. Patel
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:Effective endodontic treatment relies on the achievement of an impermeable seal within the canal environment, where dentinal tubule penetration and interfacial adaptation are crucial for entombing residual bacteria. Incorporation of silver nanoparticles (AgNPs) into sealers has been proposed to enhance their physicochemical and antibacterial properties.Aim:This study aimed to analyze the depth of intratubular penetration, interfacial adaptation, and the elemental composition of traditional and AgNP-containing sealers by scanning electron microscopy and energy dispersive X-ray analysis (SEM-EDAX).Materials and Methods:A total of 52 recently extracted single-rooted teeth were grouped into four divisions at random (n = 13): Group 1 – AH Plus; Group 2 – AH Plus + AgNPs; Group 3 – BIO-C; Group 4 – BIO-C + AgNPs. Following biomechanical preparation with ProTaper Gold up to F3, Obturation was carried out using a single-cone approach. Sectioning was performed at the coronal, middle, and apical thirds of the root. SEM assessed sealer penetration and interfacial adaptation, while EDAX evaluated elemental distribution. Statistical analysis was conducted using one-way analysis of variance followed by Tukey’s post hoc test (P < 0.05).Results:All groups exhibited sealer penetration, with significantly greater values in coronal and middle thirds than apical. AgNP-modified groups demonstrated enhanced penetration and improved interfacial adaptation compared to conventional sealers. EDAX confirmed uniform silver distribution in nanoparticle groups.Conclusion:AgNP incorporation improved dentinal tubule penetration and interfacial characteristics of both AH Plus and BIO-C sealers. SEM-EDAX proved effective for microstructural and elemental evaluation.

INTRODUCTION

Root canal therapy seeks to eradicate residual microbes from the infected canal space and to inhibit their re-entry through effective sealing.[1] Despite advances in instrumentation and irrigation, the complete eradication of microorganisms from the radicular space remains difficult because of its anatomical complexity.[23] Therefore, the quality of obturation and the properties of the sealer, the sealing quality achieved during obturation, and physiomechanical properties of the sealer play a decisive role in long-term treatment success.[4]

Endodontic sealers are expected to fill canal irregularities, penetrate dentinal tubules, and establish a stable interface with dentin.[5] Greater penetration depth is considered beneficial as it may enhance mechanical interlocking and contribute to the entrapment of remaining microorganisms.[67] In addition, optimal interfacial adaptation reduces micro gaps that could otherwise permit leakage and bacterial recolonization.[8]

Advanced imaging techniques have facilitated the evaluation of these properties at a microscopic level. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) allows detailed visualization of sealer adaptation and penetration patterns,[9] whereas energy dispersive X-ray analysis (EDAX) provides insight into the elemental composition at the sealer–dentin interface.[10] The combined use of these techniques enables a comprehensive assessment of both structural and compositional characteristics.

Recently, there has been growing interest in modifying endodontic materials with nanoparticles to enhance their functional properties.[11] Among these, silver nanoparticles (AgNPs) have attracted attention due to their small size and well-documented antimicrobial potential.[121314] Their incorporation into sealers may not only improve antibacterial efficacy but also influence physical characteristics such as flow, penetration ability, and interaction with dentinal structures.[1215]

Limited evidence exists on the effect of nanoparticle-modified sealers on dentinal tubule penetration and elemental distribution using SEM-EDAX. Therefore, this study assessed comparatively dentinal tubule penetration and interface adaptation of conventional and AgNPs – incorporated endodontic sealers.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study design and sample size

This experimental study was certified by the Institutional Ethics Committee (NPDCH/SSIP2.0/PoC/2024/104). Fifty-six single-rooted teeth which were extracted due to orthodontic and periodontal reason were used in this investigation. Based on the type of endodontic sealer employed, in total 52 samples were selected and randomly split into four groups (n = 13 each):

Sample selection and preparation

Fifty-two recently extracted mandibular arch bicuspids with single canal configuration and fully developed apices were selected. Teeth with fractures, caries, resorptive defects, prior endodontic intervention, calcifications, or developmental anomalies were omitted. All samples were cleaned and immersed in 0.1% thymol solution (Isochem Laboratories) until further use. All study specimens were subsequently sterilized in an autoclave at 121°C for 15 min to ensure asepsis. Preoperative radiovisiographs were acquired from buccolingual and mesiodistal direction for all the samples to ensure a single canal configuration. The crowns of all specimens were sectioned off to establish a uniform radicular length of about 12–14 mm. A size #10 K-file (Mani Inc., Tochigi, Japan) was used to determine the working length, followed by canal instrumentation using Protaper Universal (Dentsply, Maillefer, Ballaigues, Switzerland) rotary files up to size F3, following standard protocols. Irrigation was done using 3% sodium hypochlorite (Prime Sodium Hypochlorite, Maharashtra, India) during instrumentation. Final irrigation protocol included:

Obturation procedure

The single-cone method was used to obturate every canal using equivalent F3 gutta-percha cones (Protaper Universal Gutta-Percha Points; Dentsply Sirona, Maillefer, Ballaigues, Switzerland). The respective sealers were applied on the canal walls using a lentulo spiral to ensure uniform distribution. Gutta-percha cones were then placed to working length, and excess material was removed. The samples were kept at 37°C and 100% humidity for 7 days.

Post-hoc Tukey honestly significant difference [Table 2]: In the critical apical region, the BIO-C + AgNP and BIO-C groups both performed significantly better than the AH Plus groups (P < 0.001). While the addition of AgNPs, numerically reduced gap widths in both AH Plus and BIO-C, these improvements were not statistically significant when compared to their respective controls in the apical third (P > 0.05). SEM imaging of the BIO-C + AgNP group showed a distinct interface with the dentin, characterized by a mineral-rich zone containing high Calcium and Phosphorus. In contrast, the resin-based AH Plus groups displayed a more particulate matrix dominated by Zinc-rich fillers (27.74%) and radiopacifying oxides.

Intragroup analysis revealed that all groups demonstrated significantly larger gap widths progressing from coronal to apical thirds [P < 0.05, Table 3]. The magnitude of increase was most pronounced in the AH Plus group (coronal-to-apical difference: 6.20 μm), whereas BIO-C + AgNP showed the most consistent adaptation across root levels (difference: 3.55 μm). This suggests that bioceramic sealers maintain better marginal integrity even in anatomically challenging apical regions.

DISCUSSION

The present study evaluated the interfacial adaptation and elemental composition of AgNP-modified bioceramic and resin-based root canal sealers using SEM-EDAX analysis. The results demonstrated that bioceramic sealers exhibited significantly superior marginal adaptation compared to resin-based sealers across all root canal thirds.

This observation is consistent with known anatomical variations, as the apical region typically exhibits reduced tubule density and smaller diameters, in addition to limitations in irrigant penetration and smear layer removal.[316]

The superior interfacial adaptation observed in the BIO-C + AgNPs group (8.40 ± 1.75 μm apically) aligns with findings by D’Amico et al., who reported that bioceramic sealers demonstrate significantly greater tubular penetration compared to resin-based counterparts in their systematic review and meta-analysis.[17] This enhanced adaptation can be attributed to the hydrophilic setting reaction and calcium silicate chemistry, which promote hydroxyapatite formation at the sealer-dentin interface.[18] Our EDAX analysis confirmed this bioactive potential, revealing elevated calcium (34.73%) and phosphorus (11.85%) levels in the BIO-C + AgNPs group, consistent with the mineral profile necessary for interfacial hydroxyapatite deposition.

The consistently larger gap widths observed in the apical third across all groups corroborate findings by Rekha et al., who attributed this pattern to increased anatomical complexity, reduced sealer flow, and thepresence of apical ramifications that impede complete adaptation.[19] Our finding that bioceramic sealers maintained better consistency across root thirds (interaction effect P = 0.006) supports observations by Zedan et al., who demonstrated that calcium silicate-based sealers achieve superior marginal adaptation even in anatomically challenging regions due to their expansion during setting.[20]

The incorporation of AgNPs resulted in numerically reduced gap widths in both sealer types, though this improvement did not reach statistical significance in the apical third (P > 0.05). This finding is consistent with Nouroloyouni et al., who reported that bioceramic sealers demonstrate minimal gap formation regardless of modifications, suggesting that the base material chemistry is the primary determinant of interfacial adaptation.[21] The antimicrobial benefits of AgNP incorporation, as reviewed by Vilela et al., may therefore provide additional clinical advantages without compromising the sealing ability of the parent material.[15]

Intragroup analysis revealed that all groups demonstrated significantly larger gap widths progressing from coronal to apical thirds (P < 0.05). The magnitude of this increase was most pronounced in the AH Plus group (coronal-to-apical difference: 6.20 μm), while BIO-C + AgNPs showed the most consistent adaptation across root levels (difference: 3.55 μm). This finding supports the systematic review by Mina and Almohaimede, who concluded that bioceramic sealers demonstrate superior sealing ability compared to conventional sealers, particularly in the critical apical region.[22]

The EDAX analysis revealed distinct elemental profiles correlating with adaptation performance. The resin-based AH Plus sealer was characterized by high carbon content (33.14%) and heavy metal radiopacifiers, including tungsten (16.43%) and zirconium (20.01%), consistent with its epoxy resin-amine composition.[23] In contrast, the bioceramic groups displayed mineral-rich profiles with calcium content ranging from 19.95% to 34.73%, supporting the bioactive setting mechanism described by Vilela et al. The detection of silver at 5.09% in AH Plus + AgNPs and 1.30% in BIO-C + AgNPs confirmed successful nanoparticle incorporation in the experimental groups.[15]

The significant interaction effect observed in our two-way ANOVA (P = 0.006) indicates that the impact of anatomical location on gap width depends on the sealer type used. This finding has important clinical implications, as it suggests that bioceramic sealers may be particularly advantageous in cases with complex apical anatomy where resin-based sealers demonstrate compromised adaptation.[24]

This laboratory-based study is limited by small sample size, single AgNP concentration, 2D SEM analysis, and lack of long-term or in vivo evaluation; future studies should incorporate micro-computed tomography and clinical models.

CONCLUSION

Within the constraints of this study, it has been observed that both sealer type and root canal level significantly influenced dentinal tubule penetration and interfacial adaptation. Bioceramic sealers demonstrated superior performance compared to resin-based sealers. Incorporation of AgNPs enhanced penetration in both materials; however, the intrinsic properties of the base sealer remained the dominant factor. Penetration decreased and interfacial gaps increased toward the apical third. EDAX confirmed successful incorporation of silver in nanoparticle-modified groups.
