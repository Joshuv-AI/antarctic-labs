PRPO: Perception-Reinforced Policy Optimization via Token-Level Dynamic Advantage Reshaping
Qiming Li, Tianlun Li, Xiaolong Cheng, Hangyu Li, Ruiyan Gong, Kangning Niu, Kaitao Jiang, Mu Xu

Abstract
Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) has become an effective paradigm for improving the reasoning capability of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs). However, existing RLVR methods primarily rely on trajectory-level outcome rewards, which assign identical learning signals across all generated tokens. This coarse-grained credit assignment is fundamentally mismatched to multimodal reasoning, where only a sparse subset of tokens is causally grounded in visual evidence. Consequently, these pivotal perceptual tokens receive weak supervision and are often overwhelmed by language priors or reasoning-template tokens. To address this limitation, we propose Perception-Reinforced Policy Optimization (PRPO) , a token-level reinforcement learning framework that explicitly identifies and reinforces pivotal perceptual tokens within long-horizon multimodal reasoning trajectories. PRPO introduces Robust Visual Dependency (RVD) , a principled metric that identifies tokens whose predictions are both visually grounded and perturbation-stable, filtering out brittle or noisy visual tokens. Based on RVD, we further propose Perceptual Advantage Reshaping (PAR) , a token-level credit assignment technique that amplifies perceptually informative tokens while preserving stable gradients for non-perceptual tokens. Extensive experiments on seven multimodal reasoning benchmarks demonstrate that PRPO consistently outperforms strong LVLM baselines across both 3B and 7B model scales, achieving average gains of 23.3% and 21.1%, respectively. PRPO achieves state-of-the-art performance with improved training efficiency and stronger cross-task generalization. Our findings highlight the importance of fine-grained credit assignment for scalable multimodal reinforcement learning.

Despite these advances, existing multimodal RLVR methods largely inherit designs from language-only settings, which assign a single trajectory-level reward to the final response and broadcast the same optimization signal across all generated tokens. While this design is useful for purely textual reasoning, it becomes fundamentally mismatched in multimodal reasoning. To better characterize this mismatch, we conduct an error analysis on MathVerse
(
41
)
using Qwen2.5-VL-7B. As shown in Figure
1
, the majority of failures do not stem from insufficient reasoning capability, but from errors in visual perception occurring within long reasoning trajectories. Specifically, LVLMs often ground on incorrect visual regions, misread local evidence, or extract erroneous numerical cues at intermediate perception steps. Such behaviors either lead to isolated
Step Perception Errors
or accumulate into
Cascading Perception Errors
. In contrast, failures attributable purely to reasoning errors are relatively rare. Furthermore, we find that key perceptual tokens in multimodal reasoning (e.g., coordinates, numerical values, or geometric cues) are both sparse and highly influential, yet they receive the same supervision as non-perceptual linguistic tokens under standard RL objectives.

We identify this mismatch as the
Perceptual Credit Assignment Problem
, which contains two key issues: (i) sparse perceptual signals cannot be explicitly identified or reinforced from trajectory-level rewards, and (ii) uniform advantage assignment causes gradient dilution, where critical perceptual signals are overwhelmed by abundant reasoning tokens. As a result, existing RL methods tend to optimize surface-level reasoning fluency rather than improving visually grounded decision-making.

To address this problem, we propose
Perception-Reinforced Policy Optimization (PRPO)
, a dynamic token-level reinforcement learning framework that explicitly targets perceptual credit assignment in multimodal reasoning.
PRPO
contains two key components:
(i) Robust Visual Dependency (RVD)
, identifies tokens that are genuinely grounded in visual evidence and robust to perturbations, and
(ii) Perceptual Advantage Reshaping (PAR)
, which reshapes trajectory-level advantage into fine-grained token-level learning signals based on RVD, amplifying informative perceptual tokens while preserving stable optimization gradients on reasoning-template tokens.

Extensive experiments on seven multimodal reasoning benchmarks demonstrate that
PRPO
consistently improves performance across both 3B and 7B models, with average gains of 23.3% and 21.1%, respectively. Moreover,
PRPO
exhibits stronger performance under the same training budget and cross-task generalization, indicating that fine-grained perceptual reinforcement is more effective than uniform trajectory-level optimization in multimodal RLVR context.

Our contributions are three-fold:

•

We identify
Perceptual Credit Assignment
as a key bottleneck in multimodal RLVR: most failures arise from sparse but critical perception errors rather than insufficient reasoning capability, while existing trajectory-level rewards fail to provide effective supervision to pivotal perceptual tokens.

•

We propose
PRPO
, a token-level RL framework with
RVD
and
PAR
technique to identify and reinforce pivotal perceptual tokens while preserving stable optimization gradients.

•

We achieve state-of-the-art (SOTA) results across seven multimodal reasoning benchmarks, demonstrating the importance of fine-grained credit assignment for multimodal reinforcement learning.

Related Work

Dominant Strategies in Multimodal RL.

Current efforts to enhance multimodal RL largely focus on augmenting elements outside the core optimization algorithm. These include data-driven methods emphasizing the construction of visually-grounded datasets
(
2
;
10
;
12
)
and CoT distillation
(
4
;
9
;
17
)
, as well as reward-centric methodologies designing improved feedback mechanisms
(
16
;
5
;
30
)
. Recognizing the vision-language gap, recent works like Perception-Aware Policy Optimization (PAPO)
(
29
)
explicitly account for modality differences to engineer perception-sensitive signals. However, even with such modality-aware designs or modality-agnostic algorithmic advances like DAPO
(
38
)
, these strategies still broadcast a uniform learning signal across the entire sequence. This indiscriminate credit assignment creates a severe bottleneck where critical, sparse visual signals are easily submerged by high-volume linguistic priors.

Pivotal Tokens Identification in LVLMs.

To mitigate gradient dilution, it is crucial to isolate specific perceptual tokens from critical reasoning. In text-only domains, this involves identifying high-entropy “forking points”
(
28
)
or low-confidence errors
(
24
)
. For LVLMs, approaches like VPPO
(
8
)
attempt to address this by utilizing KL Divergence-based visual dependency to reweight trajectory-level advantages, aiming to focus gradient updates on visually-grounded steps. However, relying on a single visual dependency metric risks amplifying noise-sensitive tokens, and trajectory-level reward reshaping is fundamentally insufficient to precisely reinforce visually important tokens. To tackle this, our PRPO framework introduces a precise dynamic token-level optimization paradigm. By jointly evaluating visual dependency and consistency, we systematically filter out stochastic noise to isolate genuinely pivotal perceptual tokens. We then dynamically reshape their learning advantages, explicitly amplifying critical perceptual signals without triggering gradient collapse or compromising foundational reasoning capabilities.

Preliminaries: Group-based Policy Optimization

In multimodal reinforcement learning from verifier feedback (RLVF), we consider a dataset
\mathcal{D}
where each sample consists of a visual input
I
, a textual query
q
, and a ground-truth answer
a
. Let
\pi_{\theta}
denote the policy parameters of a LVLM.

Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)

GRPO
(
21
)
serves as a value-model-free baseline that estimates advantages through intra-group relative performance. For a prompt
(I,q)
, the model generates a group of
G
responses
\{o_{i}\}_{i=1}^{G}
, each receiving a binary outcome reward
R_{i}\in\{0,1\}
based on its equivalence to
a
. The advantage
\hat{A}_{i}
is computed by normalizing rewards within the group:

\hat{A}_{i}=\frac{R_{i}-\text{mean}(\{R_{k}\}_{k=1}^{G})}{\text{std}(\{R_{k}\}_{k=1}^{G})+\eta},\quad\text{where }R_{i}=\begin{cases}1.0,&\text{if }\text{eq}(a,o_{i})\\
0.0,&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(1)

where
\eta
is a small constant for numerical stability. The objective is to maximize the clipped surrogate loss with an integrated KL penalty:

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\text{GRPO}}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{G}\sum_{i=1}^{G}\left(\frac{1}{|o_{i}|}\sum_{t=1}^{|o_{i}|}\min\left(r^{i}_{t}(\theta)\hat{A}_{i},\text{clip}(r^{i}_{t}(\theta),1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon)\hat{A}_{i}\right)\right)\right]

\displaystyle\text{with}\ \ r^{i}_{t}(\theta)=\frac{\pi_{\theta}(o^{i}_{t}|I,q,o_{<t})}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(o^{i}_{t}|I,q,o_{<t})}.

(2)

Decoupled Clip and Dynamic Sampling (DAPO)

DAPO
(
38
)
refines the optimization landscape by introducing an asymmetric clipping mechanism and dynamic sampling strategies. It utilizes a token-level objective with decoupled thresholds
(\epsilon_{\text{low}},\epsilon_{\text{high}})
and global length normalization:

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\text{DAPO}}(\theta)=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\frac{1}{\sum_{i=1}^{G}|o_{i}|}\sum_{i=1}^{G}\sum_{t=1}^{|o_{i}|}\min\Big(r_{t}^{i}(\theta)\hat{A}_{i},\,\text{clip}\big(r_{t}^{i}(\theta),1-\epsilon_{\text{low}},1+\epsilon_{\text{high}}\big)\hat{A}_{i}\Big)\Bigg]

(3)

4.1
Robust Visual Dependency (RVD)

In this section, we detail the methodology for identifying
pivotal perceptual tokens
within long multimodal reasoning trajectories. Following established paradigms
(
8
)
, we adopt Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence as a principled heuristic metric to quantify the sensitivity of individual tokens to visual evidence, serving as the foundation for our robust dependency analysis.

Definition 4.1 (Visual Dependency).

Let
\mathcal{P}_{\text{str}}
be a strong perturbation operator that maps
I
to a state
\tilde{I}_{\text{str}}
where the visual information is severely corrupted (e.g., via a stochastic black mask). We define the Visual Dependency
S_{t}
to formally quantify the policy’s dependency on visual information for a specific output token
o_{t}
:

S_{t}\coloneqq D_{\text{KL}}\big(\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid I,q,o_{<t})\,\|\,\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid\mathcal{P}_{\text{str}}(I),q,o_{<t})\big)

(4)

S_{t}
measures the absolute magnitude of the visual signal required for prediction. A high
S_{t}
indicates that the visual input
I
serves as a necessary perception anchor for the generation of token
o_{t}
, distinguishing visual grounded tokens from pure language priors.

Definition 4.2 (Visual Inconsistency).

Let
\mathcal{P}_{\text{wk}}
be a weak perturbation operator that introduces an infinitesimal stochastic jitter
\bm{\delta}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}\cdot{I})
to the pixel space. Crucially,
\mathcal{P}_{\text{wk}}
ensures that the visual semantics remain strictly invariant.
We define the Visual Inconsistency
S^{\prime}_{t}
to measure the model’s sensitivity to numerical noise for a specific output token
o_{t}
:

S^{\prime}_{t}\coloneqq D_{\text{KL}}\big(\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid I,q,o_{<t})\,\|\,\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid\mathcal{P}_{\text{wk}}(I),q,o_{<t})\big)

(5)

S^{\prime}_{t}
probes the local curvature of the policy distribution on the perceptual manifold. It functions as a stability probe to identify numerical instability rather than semantic shift. A robust
pivotal perceptual tokens
must exhibit high
S_{t}
(strong
Visual Dependency
) but low
S^{\prime}_{t}
(weak
Visual Inconsistency
).

To provide a principled foundation for PRPO, we conduct a fine-grained diagnostic analysis on the MathVerse benchmark using Qwen2.5-VL-7B with a rollout factor of 8. By computing
S_{t}
and
S^{\prime}_{t}
for each tokens, we uncover two critical findings regarding the distribution of visual signals:

Finding 4.1: Visual Signal Sparsity.

Our analysis reveals that perceptual tokens are extremely sparse within long-horizon reasoning trajectories. As illustrated in Figure
4
, both
S_{t}
and
S^{\prime}_{t}
exhibit a dramatic
long-tail characteristic
. The vast majority of generated tokens reside in the low-value region (
S_{t},S^{\prime}_{t}<0.1
), suggesting that the policy is predominantly driven by language priors rather than visual evidence. Traditional RL methods (e.g., GRPO and DAPO) that broadcast advantages uniformly across the trajectory suffer from
gradient dilution
in multi-modal context, where rare but critical visual signals are submerged by linguistic background noise.

Finding 4.2: The Coupled Nature of Visual Dependency & Inconsistency.

Visual Dependency (
S_{t}
) and Inconsistency (
S^{\prime}_{t}
) are inherently coupled rather than trivially separable. By projecting tokens across both dimensions (Figure
4
), we can roughly partition them into three distinct regimes:

•

Linguistic Tokens (low
S_{t}
).

The vast majority of tokens fall into this region (the blue cluster). Unaffected by either perturbation, they are governed entirely by linguistic priors.

•

Brittle Anchors (high
S_{t}
, high
S^{\prime}_{t}
).

A substantial subset of tokens exhibits strong visual dependency but equally high sensitivity to numerically unstable pixel-level features (the red cluster).
Reinforcing them solely based on
S_{t}
would amplify noise rather than informative perception.

•

Pivotal Perceptual Tokens (high
S_{t}
, low
S^{\prime}_{t}
).

The true
pivotal perceptual tokens
form a narrow and highly structured subset (the orange cluster).
These tokens exhibit strong semantic grounding while remaining stable under local perturbations, indicating that their predictions are driven by informative visual evidence.
We refer to these tokens as
pivotal perceptual tokens
, as they represent the causal interface where visual evidence is incorporated into the reasoning trajectory.

This decomposition exposes a fundamental limitation of pivotal token selection strategies, such as Jensen-Shannon Divergence, single KL Divergence
(
8
)
and hidden state similarity
(
11
)
, which fail to distinguish
semantically grounded perception
from
numerically brittle responses
.
A dual-metric synergistic approach (
S_{t}\uparrow,S^{\prime}_{t}\downarrow
) is necessary to filter out stable
pivotal perceptual tokens
that drive visually-grounded reasoning. Further performance analysis of these metrics is provided in the Appendix
B.2
.

Definition 4.3 (Robust Visual Dependency).

Let
S_{t}
and
S^{\prime}_{t}
be the Visual Dependency and Visual Inconsistency at step of token
t
, respectively. The hyperparameter
\eta
ensures numerical stability and serves as a baseline sensitivity threshold. We define the Robust Visual Dependency (
\text{RVD}_{t}
) to precisely quantify the real contribution of visual perception to the generation of token
o_{t}
:

\text{RVD}_{t}=S_{t}\cdot\text{ReLU}\left(1-\frac{S^{\prime}_{t}}{S_{t}+\eta}\right)

(6)

The RVD index acts as a non-linear information filter. By utilizing the ReLU-gated ratio, it effectively suppresses tokens in the
stochastically brittle
region (where
S^{\prime}_{t}\geq S_{t}
) while preserving the original magnitude of
Golden Anchors
(where
S^{\prime}_{t}\ll S_{t}
). This ensures that the subsequent reinforcement signal is concentrated on tokens that are both semantically grounded and numerically robust.

4.2
Token-Level Dynamic Perceptual Advantage Reshaping (PAR)

While RVD provides a fine-grained measure of perceptual relevance, directly using it for credit assignment leads to suboptimal optimization behavior due to its highly skewed distribution:

(1) Long-tailed distribution.

RVD exhibits a heavy-tailed structure, where the majority of tokens concentrate near zero, while only a small subset of perceptually grounded tokens take large values. Directly using such signals results in inefficient credit allocation.

(2) Gradient collapse.

A naive transformation (e.g., linear or monotonic scaling) causes most token-level weights to shrink toward zero, effectively collapsing gradients onto a sparse subset of visual tokens. This leads to a biased optimization process that over-focuses on perceptual tokens while neglecting linguistic reasoning, ultimately degrading general reasoning performance.

To address these issues, we propose
Perceptual Advantage Reshaping (PAR)
, a differentiable nonlinear mapping that transforms raw perceptual signals into bounded token-level weights. The resulting
S-shaped synaptic response curve
suppresses low-signal tokens, amplifies informative perceptual tokens beyond a threshold, and enforces smooth saturation, thereby preventing gradient explosion while preserving stable optimization.

Synaptic S-shaped Reshaping.

Formally, let
x=\mathrm{RVD}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
denote the raw perceptual stimulus. To achieve a strictly bounded and mathematically smooth transformation, we formulate the reshaped advantage
\mathcal{T}(x)
via a generalized logistic function:

\mathcal{T}(x)=m+\frac{M-m}{1+\exp\left(-k\cdot(x-\mu)\right)}

(7)

where the synaptic activation is governed by four highly interpretable parameters: the activation threshold
\mu>0
, the upper saturation bound
M
and the lower suppression baseline
m
(
M>m\geq 0
), and a scaling factor
k>0
that dictates the sharpness of the activation.

Token-level Dynamic Perceptual Advantage Reshaping (PAR).

Standard trajectory-level reinforcement learning suffers from coarse credit assignment, uniformly broadcasting a scalar reward to all tokens regardless of their perceptual contributions. To resolve this, we leverage our synaptic response function
\mathcal{T}(\cdot)
to establish
PAR
:

\hat{A}_{t}^{i,\text{PAR}}=\hat{A}^{i}\cdot\mathcal{T}(\mathrm{RVD}_{t}^{i})

(8)

This mechanism organically redistributes the learning signals. Tokens exhibiting strong perceptual relevance (post-threshold) receive amplified advantages, directing the optimization focus toward semantically pivotal regions. Conversely, perceptual noise is softly discounted. Critically, the strictly positive lower bound
m
in
\mathcal{T}(\cdot)
guarantees a non-zero gradient even for suppressed tokens, preserving dense learning signals and preventing optimization dead zones across the sequence.

Training Objectives.

We formulate our final policy objective by integrating
PAR
into a clipped surrogate framework for robust optimization:

\mathcal{J}_{\text{PRPO}}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\frac{1}{\sum_{i=1}^{G}|o^{i}|}\sum_{i=1}^{G}\sum_{t=1}^{|o^{i}|}\min\Big(r_{t}^{i}(\theta)\hat{A}_{t}^{i,\text{PAR}},\;\text{clip}\big(r_{t}^{i}(\theta),1-\epsilon_{\text{low}},1+\epsilon_{\text{high}}\big)\hat{A}_{t}^{i,\text{PAR}}\Big)\Bigg]

(9)

By bounding dynamically reshaped advantages within an asymmetric trust region, PRPO achieves precise multimodal alignment without overfitting to visual noise or compromising language priors.

Conclusion

We propose PRPO, a token-level reinforcement learning framework that addresses the Perceptual Credit Assignment Problem in multimodal reasoning. By introducing Robust Visual Dependency (RVD) and Perceptual Advantage Reshaping (PAR), we move beyond coarse trajectory-level rewards to precisely reinforce pivotal perceptual tokens while filtering out numerical noise and linguistic priors. These results demonstrate that fine-grained token-level credit assignment is essential for aligning multimodal reasoning systems at scale.

Appendix B
Theoretical Analysis of RVD

B.1
Theoretical Derivation of KL Divergence

In this section, we provide a rigorous theoretical grounding for utilizing the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence as the fundamental metric for quantifying both Visual Dependency (
S_{t}
) and Visual Inconsistency (
S^{\prime}_{t}
). We demonstrate its representational capacity from two perspectives: Information Theory (macroscopic semantic dependency) and Information Geometry (microscopic numerical stability).

1. Information-Theoretic Perspective: Bounding Conditional Mutual Information.

Let
c=(q,o_{<t})
denote the multimodal context comprising the textual query and the historical generation trajectory. The original policy distribution is
P=\pi_{\theta}(\cdot|I,c)
, and the perturbed distribution is
P^{\prime}=\pi_{\theta}(\cdot|\tilde{I},c)
, where
\tilde{I}=\mathcal{P}(I)
.

The KL divergence
D_{KL}(P\parallel P^{\prime})
quantifies the expected excess surprise (in nats) experienced by the policy when the visual evidence is altered. From an information-theoretic standpoint, if a token
o_{t}
is entirely governed by the language prior (i.e., it is visually independent), the generative Markov chain forms
I\rightarrow c\rightarrow o_{t}
. In this regime, the conditional mutual information
I(o_{t};I|c)\approx 0
, leading to
P\approx P^{\prime}
and consequently
D_{KL}\to 0
.

Conversely, for a pivotal perceptual token, the generation is causally dependent on
I
. Applying a strong information-corrupting perturbation
\mathcal{P}_{str}
forces the policy to fall back on its language prior,
\pi_{\theta}(\cdot|\mathcal{P}_{str}(I),c)\approx\pi_{\theta}(\cdot|c)
. Therefore, the Visual Dependency
S_{t}
can be viewed as an approximation of the Pointwise Mutual Information (PMI) between the token and the visual input:

S_{t}=D_{KL}(\pi_{\theta}(\cdot|I,c)\parallel\pi_{\theta}(\cdot|\mathcal{P}_{str}(I),c))\approx\mathbb{E}_{o_{t}\sim P}\left[\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}(o_{t}|I,c)}{\pi_{\theta}(o_{t}|c)}\right]

This derivation proves that
S_{t}
theoretically lower-bounds the macroscopic semantic reliance of the token on the uncorrupted image.

2. Information Geometry Perspective: Probing Local Curvature via Fisher Information.

While
S_{t}
captures semantic dependency, it cannot distinguish whether a high KL value arises from robust visual grounding or brittle numerical over-sensitivity. To explain the representational power of Visual Inconsistency (
S^{\prime}_{t}
), we analyze the KL divergence under the weak, information-preserving perturbation
\mathcal{P}_{wk}(I)=I+\delta
, where
\delta\to 0
represents infinitesimal stochastic pixel jitter.

Using the Taylor series expansion of the KL divergence around
I
, the first-order term vanishes, and the second-order term is governed by the Fisher Information Matrix (FIM), denoted as
\mathcal{F}_{I}(\theta)
:

S^{\prime}_{t}=D_{KL}(\pi_{\theta}(\cdot|I,c)\parallel\pi_{\theta}(\cdot|I+\delta,c))\approx\frac{1}{2}\delta^{\top}\mathcal{F}_{I}(\theta)\delta+\mathcal{O}(\|\delta\|^{3})

where
\mathcal{F}_{I}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{o_{t}\sim P}[\nabla_{I}\log\pi_{\theta}(o_{t}|I,c)\nabla_{I}\log\pi_{\theta}(o_{t}|I,c)^{\top}]
.

This geometric derivation reveals the profound meaning of
S^{\prime}_{t}
: it directly measures the local curvature of the policy manifold in the visual representation space.

•

A high
S^{\prime}_{t}
implies that the local Hessian (Fisher Information) is extremely sharp. The model relies on brittle, high-frequency spurious correlations, causing the token distribution to collapse under imperceptible noise.

•

A low
S^{\prime}_{t}
implies a flat, locally Lipschitz-continuous optimization landscape, indicating that the token generation is grounded in robust, low-frequency semantic features.

Synthesis: The Theoretical Necessity of Robust Visual Dependency (RVD).

By uniting these two derivations, the rationale behind our RVD formula becomes theoretically complete. The macro-level KL divergence (
S_{t}
) acts as a
semantic magnitude filter
, capturing the total visual information volume. Concurrently, the micro-level KL divergence (
S^{\prime}_{t}
) acts as a
geometric sharpness penalty
, evaluating the stability of the Fisher Information. Our non-linear mapping
RVD_{t}=S_{t}\cdot\text{ReLU}(1-S^{\prime}_{t}/S_{t})
systematically filters out predictions that reside on sharp, high-variance geometric manifolds, preserving only the tokens that possess both high mutual information and stable local curvature.

B.2
Analysis of Heuristic Metrics for Visual Dependency

Identifying critical, visually-grounded tokens within long-horizon multimodal reasoning trajectories is the key to implementing the PRPO framework. By summarizing related prior works, we identify the following categories of heuristic mathematical metrics and compare them with our proposed approach:

Jensen-Shannon Divergence (JSD).

This method is a symmetrized and smoothed version of KL divergence. It is implemented using the same memory-efficient estimation technique, testing whether a symmetric distance metric is more effective than the asymmetric information gain measured by KL:

\mathcal{S}_{\text{JSD}}(s_{t},I)=D_{\text{JS}}\left(\pi_{\theta}(\cdot|s_{t},I)\parallel\pi_{\theta}(\cdot|s_{t},I^{\prime})\right)

(10)

Top-1 Probability Drop.

This simple heuristic measures only the change in probability for the token
o_{t}
that was actually sampled, testing how much the image boosts the confidence of the final choice:

\mathcal{S}_{\text{Top-1}}(s_{t},I)=\pi_{\theta}(o_{t}|s_{t},I)-\pi_{\theta}(o_{t}|s_{t},I^{\prime})

(11)

KL Divergence (VPPO).

This is the strategy predominantly used in recent baselines (e.g., VPPO). It measures the total change across the entire vocabulary distribution. VPPO’s implementation uses a memory-efficient estimation of the true KL value:

\mathcal{S}_{\text{KL}}(s_{t},I)=D_{\text{KL}}\left(\pi_{\theta}(\cdot|s_{t},I)\parallel\pi_{\theta}(\cdot|s_{t},I^{\prime})\right)

(12)

Robust Visual Dependency (RVD, Ours).

Unlike the single-metric approaches above that risk amplifying noise-sensitive tokens, RVD employs a dual-metric synergistic approach to systematically filter out stochastic noise and isolate genuinely pivotal perceptual tokens. It explicitly evaluates Visual Dependency (
S_{t}
) against a strong information-corrupting perturbation (
\mathcal{P}_{str}
) and Visual Inconsistency (
S^{\prime}_{t}
) against a weak, information-preserving perturbation (
\mathcal{P}_{wk}
). Formally, it is defined as:

\text{RVD}_{t}=S_{t}\cdot\text{ReLU}\left(1-\frac{S^{\prime}_{t}}{S_{t}+\eta}\right)

(13)

where
S_{t}=D_{\text{KL}}\left(\pi_{\theta}(\cdot|s_{t},I)\parallel\pi_{\theta}(\cdot|s_{t},\mathcal{P}_{str}(I))\right)
and
S^{\prime}_{t}=D_{\text{KL}}\left(\pi_{\theta}(\cdot|s_{t},I)\parallel\pi_{\theta}(\cdot|s_{t},\mathcal{P}_{wk}(I))\right)
, with
\eta
acting as a baseline sensitivity threshold for numerical stability.

To validate the effectiveness of our proposed metric, we evaluate PRPO equipped with different heuristic indicators across six diverse reasoning benchmarks. As presented in Table

Drawing inspiration from prior works, we systematically evaluate these strategies from two critical dimensions: Information-Corrupting and Information-Preserving perturbations.

B.3.1
Information-Corrupting Perturbations

Information-corrupting perturbations are designed to severely degrade the visual evidence, forcing the model to rely solely on its language prior. This allows us to quantify the macroscopic Visual Dependency (
S_{t}
). We find that there are six common degradation strategies:

•

Random Patch Masking:
Aligned with the underlying Vision Transformer (ViT) architecture of our base model, the input image is partitioned into
14\times 14
patches. Each patch is independently masked (set to zero) with a specific dropout probability (e.g.,
p=0.5
).

•

Additive Gaussian Noise:
Intense Gaussian noise (
\sigma=189
) is added to the pixel space. This extreme variance ensures that approximately 50% of the pixels are saturated to their minimum or maximum values, effectively erasing localized visual semantics.

•

Heavy Gaussian Blur:
A severe Gaussian blur filter (
r=6.0
) is applied to the image, comprehensively destroying high-frequency fine-grained details while preserving basic color layouts.

•

Complete Masking:
The original image is entirely replaced by a solid, neutral gray canvas (RGB:
[128,128,128]
), acting as a continuous blinding mechanism.

•

Modality Truncation:
The visual input is entirely omitted from the prompt, reducing the generation to a purely text-only setting.

•

Semantic-Aware Masking:

[
29
]
Instead of uniform random dropout, this approach utilizes a pre-trained vision encoder (e.g., DINOv2) to extract patch-level saliency scores from aggregated middle-layer self-attention maps. Patches with the highest semantic importance are preferentially masked, strategically corrupting the core visual evidence.

Consistent with findings from previous studies
[
8
,
29
]
, our preliminary experiments indicate that
Random Patch Masking
yields the most reliable gradient signals for identifying visually dependent tokens. Building upon this, we further investigate the sensitivity of the masking ratio (patch dropout probability) and its direct impact on overall model performance in Table

Information-Preserving Perturbations

While strong perturbations capture semantic dependency, they are insufficient for identifying numerically brittle anchors. Therefore, we complement RVD with Information-Preserving Perturbations (
\mathcal{P}_{wk}
) to measure Visual Inconsistency (
S^{\prime}_{t}
). These perturbations introduce imperceptible stochastic jitter to the pixel space, ensuring that the core visual semantics remain strictly invariant. The primary strategy evaluated is:

•

Infinitesimal Pixel Jitter:
We inject low-variance Gaussian noise
\delta\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{wk}^{2})
directly into the normalized image tensor, where
\sigma_{wk}
is meticulously calibrated to be perceptually indistinguishable to the human eye. This acts as a microscopic probe to evaluate the local curvature and stability of the policy distribution.

By jointly applying these two opposing perturbation paradigms, RVD effectively isolates tokens that are both semantically grounded and mathematically robust.

Appendix E
Limitations

PRPO introduces additional computational overhead during training due to the estimation of visual dependency under perturbed inputs, which requires multiple forward passes for each trajectory. Although this overhead can be partially mitigated through batched computation and shared rollout reuse, it increases the overall training cost compared to standard trajectory-level RL methods.

•

If this information is not available online, the authors are encouraged to reach out to the asset’s creators.
