1. Accomplishments

What are the major goals of the project?
The project aimed to drive strong-field excitation of bound electronic coherences in gas-phase
atoms and molecules using a novel source of near-single-cycle optical field transients, based on
industrial-grade Yb:solid-state laser technology. Key objectives included generating near-single-
cycle field transients and isolated attosecond pulses, reconstructing time-dependent wave packets
in atoms and molecules, probing their decoherence using attosecond interferometry, and
controlling the dynamic evolution of systems on attosecond to few-femtosecond timescales. A
secondary goal of the project was to develop field-resolved measurement platforms as a novel
approach for studying electron dynamics in atomic and molecular systems.

What was accomplished under these goals?

Significant progress was made in generating and characterizing few-cycle pulses from industrial-
grade laser sources, and in developing attosecond sources based on these platforms using high-
order harmonic generation. Major technical achievements include:

  Multidimensional Solitary States (MDSS): Demonstrated efficient generation and
 extreme compression of multidimensional solitary states in molecular gas-filled hollow-
 core fibers driven by ~100 fs to >1 ps pulse duration Yb lasers. Developed a detailed
 understanding of the impacts of rotational nonlinearity on nonlinear propagation in
 molecular gas-filled hollow-core fibers.
  Laser Waveform Measurement: Developed novel approaches to field-resolved
 measurements, including a single-shot near-infrared laser waveform measurement
 technique and a real-time electro-optic waveform measurement technique.
  Nonlinear Pulse Compression: Realized carrier-envelope phase (CEP)-stable near-
 single-cycle pulses through both MDSS and two-stage hybrid compression of a Yb:KGW
 amplifier.

  Attosecond Science Platforms: Used polarization gating to demonstrate CEP-dependent
 high-order harmonic generation (HHG) using post-compressed Yb:solid-state laser.

What opportunities for training and professional development has the project provided?
The award supported the PhD dissertations of 4 Graduate Students [(PhD 2020), (PhD 2023),
(PhD 2025), and (DOE SCGSR fellowship 2024, PhD 2025)]. It also provided training for an
undergraduate student who is now pursuing a PhD, and postdoctoral scholar.

How have the results been disseminated to communities of interest?

Results were disseminated through peer-reviewed publications in journals such as Nature
Photonics, APL Photonics, and Journal of Optics, as well as through invited and contributed

talks at international conferences. The results have also been disseminated to DOE labs SLAC
National Accelerator Laboratory and Argonne National Laboratory through collaborations with
the groups of Matthias Kling, Joe Robinson, and Linda Young.

4. Impact

What is the impact on the development of the principal discipline(s) of the project?
The project led to new understanding of optical nonlinearity in molecular systems.

What is the impact on other disciplines?

The development of MDSS and single-shot waveform measurement techniques provides a robust
platform for attosecond science using high-repetition-rate, industrial-grade lasers. These

technologies lower the barrier to entry for complex ultrafast electron dynamics studies in
laboratories without specialized custom laser infrastructure.

infrastructure?

What is the impact on technology transfer?

device.
