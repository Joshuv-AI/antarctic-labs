1.
Introduction

Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
be a partition, namely, a non-increasing sequence of positive integers. Corso and Nagel
[
CN
]
studied the Ferrers ideal associated to the partition
\lambda
and exhibited various connections between the combinatorics of Young diagrams and the algebraic invariants of the monomial ideals and monomial algebras associated to
I_{\lambda}
. Hoang and Vu
[
HV1
,
HV2
]
further explored the relationships between Young tableaux and skew tableaux and the algebraic properties and algebraic invariants of the tableau and skew tableau ideals, which generalize Ferrers ideals.

In this work, we establish another connection between the combinatorics of Young diagrams and that of the cover ideals of the Ferrers graphs associated with
\lambda
. We note that the combinatorics of Young diagrams is itself a fascinating subject of study, arising naturally in the representation theory of symmetric groups and general linear groups
[
Ful
]
. We now introduce some notation.

Assume that
\lambda_{1}=m
. Denote by
S=\mathrm{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}]
the standard graded polynomial ring over a field
\mathrm{k}
. The Ferrers graph associated with a partition
\lambda
, denoted by
G_{\lambda}
, is the bipartite graph with bipartition

V(G_{\lambda})=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}\cup\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}\}

and edge set

E(G_{\lambda})=\bigl\{\{x_{i},y_{j}\}\mid 1\leq j\leq\lambda_{i}\bigr\}.

The edge ideal and cover ideal associated with the Ferrers graph
G_{\lambda}
are denoted by
I_{\lambda}
and
J_{\lambda}
, respectively. From the work of Fröberg
[
Fr
]
and Corso and Nagel
[
CN
]
, the projective dimension and regularity of
I_{\lambda}
are known. Since
J_{\lambda}
is the Alexander dual of
I_{\lambda}
, Terai’s theorem
[
Te
]
allows us to determine the regularity and projective dimension of
J_{\lambda}
as well.

While the projective dimension and regularity of powers of
I_{\lambda}
are well understood
[
HHZ
,
HV1
]
, the corresponding results for powers of
J_{\lambda}
have remained unknown. In this work, we determine the projective dimension of all powers of
J_{\lambda}
. Let
d(\lambda)
denote the number of distinct parts of
\lambda
. Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\lambda
be a partition. Then, for all
t\geq 1
,

\pd(S/J_{\lambda}^{t})=1+\min\{t,d(\lambda)\}.

It is worth noting that explicit formulas for the depth of powers of ideals are relatively rare, even in the case of edge ideals of graphs
[
BC
,
MTV
,
HHV
]
. For cover ideals of graphs, when the symbolic depth function is not constant, the only recent explicit results are those for paths
[
DHNT
]
and cycles
[
DHV
]
. Moreover, although the depth of symbolic powers of cover ideals of graphs is known to be weakly decreasing
[
HKTT
]
, it may exhibit several plateau regions before stabilizing. The behavior established in this work for Ferrers graphs is particularly simple: the depth decreases by one at each step until stabilization. This phenomenon resembles that of powers of edge ideals of Cohen–Macaulay trees
[
HHV
]
.

Example 1.2
.

Let
\lambda=(8,8,8,3,2,2)
. Then
d(\lambda)=3
. Hence, for all
t\geq 1
,

\pd(S/J_{\lambda}^{t})=1+\min\{t,3\}.

Recently, Dung, Hang, and Vu
[
DHV
]
, building on Hochster’s formula
[
Hoc
]
, translated the problem of computing the depth of symbolic powers of cover ideals into the problem of determining the regularity of certain admissible subgraphs. We refer the reader to the next section for further details.

In the next section, we recall the necessary notation and results concerning admissible subgraphs. In Section
3
, we apply these tools to establish our main theorem.

2.
Depth of symbolic powers of cover ideals via admissible subgraphs

In this section, we first review the definitions and basic properties of depth for monomial ideals. We then recall the notion of admissible subgraphs and explain how it can be used to compute the depth of symbolic powers of cover ideals of hypergraphs. Finally, we recall several results concerning Ferrers ideals.

Throughout the first two subsections, let

S=\mathrm{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]

be a standard graded polynomial ring over a field
\mathrm{k}
with homogeneous maximal ideal

\mathfrak{m}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}).

We begin by recalling the notion of depth. For a finitely generated graded
S
-module
L
, the depth of
L
is defined by

\depth(L)=\min\{i\mid H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{i}(L)\neq 0\},

where
H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(L)
denotes the
i
-th local cohomology module of
L
with respect to
\mathfrak{m}
.

2.1.
Graphs and their edge ideals and cover ideals

We recall some basic notions from graph theory; for further details, see
[
Di
]
. Let
G
be a simple graph with vertex set
V(G)=\{1,\ldots,n\}
and edge set
E(G)
. Throughout, we assume that
G
has no isolated vertices.

A graph
H
is a
subgraph
of
G
if
V(H)\subseteq V(G)
and
E(H)\subseteq E(G)
. It is an
induced subgraph
of
G
if, for every subset
e\subseteq V(H)
, the set
e
is an edge of
H
if and only if it is an edge of
G
.

A subset
W\subseteq V(G)
is called a
vertex cover
of
G
if
W\cap e\neq\emptyset
for every edge
e\in E(G)
. It is called a
minimal vertex cover
if no proper subset of
W
is a vertex cover of
G
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
G
be a graph with vertex set
V(G)=\{1,\ldots,n\}
and edge set
E(G)
. The
edge ideal
and
cover ideal
of
G
, denoted by
I(G)
and
J(G)
, respectively, are defined by

I(G)=(x_{i}x_{j}\mid\{i,j\}\in E(G))\quad\text{and}\quad J(G)=\bigcap_{\{i,j\}\in E(G)}(x_{i},x_{j}).

In particular,
J(G)
is the Alexander dual of
I(G)
. It is well known that

J(G)=(x_{W}\mid W\text{ is a minimal vertex cover of }G),

where
x_{W}=\prod_{i\in W}x_{i}
.

The
t
-th symbolic power of
J(G)
is given by

J(G)^{(t)}=\bigcap_{\{i,j\}\in E(G)}(x_{i},x_{j})^{t}.

2.2.
Admissible subgraphs

Definition 2.2
.

Let
G
be a graph with vertex set
V(G)=[n]
. A subgraph
H
of
G
is called a
t
-admissible subgraph
of
G
if there exists an exponent vector
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
such that

E(H)=\bigl\{\{i,j\}\in E(G)\;\big|\;a_{i}+a_{j}<t\bigr\}.

The set of all
t
-admissible subgraphs of
G
is denoted by
\operatorname{Adm}_{t}(G)
.

The following result is
[
DHV
, Lemma 2.11]
. We state it in terms of projective dimension, since this formulation is more convenient for our application. The depth and projective dimension are related by the Auslander–Buchsbaum formula.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
G
be a simple graph. Then

\pd(S/J(G)^{(t)})=\max\{\reg(I(H))\mid H\in\operatorname{Adm}_{t}(G)\}.

2.3.
Ordered matchings

We recall the notion of ordered matching introduced by Constantinescu and Varbaro
[
CV
]
, which plays a crucial role in the study of the depth of symbolic powers of edge ideals. Let
G
be a simple graph. A subset
M\subseteq E(G)
is called a
matching
of
G
if no two edges in
M
share a common vertex. A subset of vertices
U\subseteq V(G)
is called
independent
if

\{u,v\}\notin E(G)
for all
u,v\in U
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let

M=\{\{a_{1},b_{1}\},\ldots,\{a_{r},b_{r}\}\}

be a matching of
G
. The matching
M
is called an
ordered matching
of
G
if

(1)

\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}\}
is an independent set;

(2)

\{a_{i},b_{j}\}\in E(G)
implies that
i\leq j
.

The
ordered matching number
of
G
, denoted by
\nu_{o}(G)
, is the maximum cardinality of an ordered matching of
G
.

2.4.
Ferrers graphs

Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
be a partition with

m=\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}\geq 1.

By convention, we set
\lambda_{0}=m+1
and
\lambda_{n+1}=0
. We denote by
\mu
the conjugate partition of
\lambda
. The Ferrers graph
G_{\lambda}
is the bipartite graph on

X=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}\quad\text{and}\quad Y=\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}\}

with

N(x_{i})=\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{\lambda_{i}}\}\quad\text{and}\quad N(y_{j})=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{\mu_{j}}\}

for all
i=1,\ldots,n
and
j=1,\ldots,m
. The Ferrers ideal associated with the partition
\lambda
is the edge ideal of the Ferrers graph in the polynomial ring

S=\mathrm{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}].
From
[
CN
, Corollary 2.2 and Corollary 2.6]
, we obtain the following result.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
be a partition. Then

(1)

\pd(S/I_{\lambda})=\max\{\lambda_{j}+j-1\mid 1\leq j\leq n\},

(2)

\reg(S/I_{\lambda})=1.

We now compute the ordered matchings of Ferrers graphs.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\lambda
be a partition. Then

\nu_{o}(G_{\lambda})=d(\lambda).

Proof.

Let

a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdots<a_{r}
be the row indices such that

\lambda_{a_{1}}>\lambda_{a_{2}}>\cdots>\lambda_{a_{r}}.

Since they correspond to distinct parts of
\lambda
, we have
r\leq d(\lambda)
. For simplicity of notation, set
b_{i}=\lambda_{a_{i}}
for
i=1,\ldots,r
. Then

M=\{\{x_{a_{1}},y_{b_{1}}\},\ldots,\{x_{a_{r}},y_{b_{r}}\}\}

is an ordered matching of
G_{\lambda}
. Hence,

\nu_{o}(G_{\lambda})\geq d(\lambda).

Conversely, suppose that

M=\{\{x_{a_{1}},y_{b_{1}}\},\ldots,\{x_{a_{r}},y_{b_{r}}\}\}

is an ordered matching of
G_{\lambda}
. By definition, we have

\{x_{a_{i}},y_{b_{j}}\}\notin E(G_{\lambda})

whenever
i>j
. It follows that

\lambda_{a_{i}}<b_{j}\leq\lambda_{a_{j}}.

Hence,

\lambda_{a_{1}}>\lambda_{a_{2}}>\cdots>\lambda_{a_{r}},

which implies that
r\leq d(\lambda)
. The proof is complete.
∎

3.
Proof of the main result

In this section, we analyze the admissible subgraphs of Ferrers graphs and prove our main results. Since
G_{\lambda}
is bipartite, a result of Herzog, Hibi, and Trung
[
HHT
]
implies that

J_{\lambda}^{t}=J_{\lambda}^{(t)}

for all
t\geq 1
. Hence, the results of the previous section apply.

Since
G_{\lambda}
is bipartite, it is convenient to write a certificate of admissibility in terms of a tuple

(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})\in\NN^{n}\times\NN^{m}.
A subgraph
H
of
G_{\lambda}
is
t
-admissible with respect to
(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})
if

\{x_{i},y_{j}\}\in E(H)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\{x_{i},y_{j}\}\in E(G_{\lambda})\text{ and }a_{i}+b_{j}<t.

We first consider the problem of projective dimension and begin by proving that the maximum bound is attained.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
be a partition with
d(\lambda)
distinct parts. For every
1\leq t\leq d(\lambda)
, there exists an admissible subgraph
H
of
G_{\lambda}
such that

\reg(I(H))=1+t.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
t
. The case
t=1
is clear, since
G_{\lambda}
is the only admissible subgraph and

\reg(I(G_{\lambda}))=2.

Now assume that
t\geq 2
. In particular,
d(\lambda)\geq 2
. Let
p
be the smallest index such that

\lambda_{p}>\lambda_{p+1},
and let

\lambda^{\prime}=(\lambda_{p+1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}).

Then

d(\lambda^{\prime})=d(\lambda)-1.
By the induction hypothesis, there exists an admissible subgraph
H^{\prime}
of
G_{\lambda^{\prime}}
such that

\reg I(H^{\prime})=t.

Assume that the certificate for
H^{\prime}
is given by
\mathbf{a}^{\prime}
and
\mathbf{b}^{\prime}
. Define

a_{i}=t-1\quad\text{for all }i=1,\ldots,p,\text{ and }a_{j}=a^{\prime}_{j}\quad\text{for }j>p,

and

b_{j}=1+b^{\prime}_{j}\quad\text{for }j=1,\ldots,\lambda_{p}-1,\text{ and }b_{j}=0\quad\text{for }j\geq\lambda_{p}.

Then the admissible subgraph
H
corresponding to the certificate
(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})
is the disjoint union of
H^{\prime}
and a complete bipartite graph. Hence,

\reg(I(H))=\reg(I(H^{\prime}))+1=t+1.

The proof is complete.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By
[
HKTT
]
, we know that
\pd(S/J_{\lambda}^{t})
is weakly increasing and that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\pd(S/J_{\lambda}^{t})=1+\nu_{o}(G_{\lambda}).

By Lemma
2.3
, Lemma
3.1
, and Lemma
2.6
, it remains to prove that if
H
is a
t
-admissible subgraph of
G_{\lambda}
, then

\reg(I(H))\leq t+1.

For each
i=0,\ldots,t-1
, let

Y_{i}=\{j\mid b_{j}=i\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad X_{i}=\{\ell\mid a_{\ell}\leq t-1-i\}.

Denote by
H_{i}
the induced subgraph of
G_{\lambda}
on the vertex set
X_{i}\cup Y_{i}
. By definition,
H
is the union of the graphs
H_{0},\ldots,H_{t-1}
. Equivalently,

I(H)=I(H_{0})+\cdots+I(H_{t-1}).

Since each
H_{i}
is a (possibly empty) Ferrers graph, the conclusion follows from a theorem of Kalai and Meshulam
[
KM
]
.
∎

G_{\lambda}
