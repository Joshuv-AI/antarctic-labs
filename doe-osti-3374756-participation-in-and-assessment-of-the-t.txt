 EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Third DOE/NRC Criticality Safety for Commercial-Scale HALEU Fuel Cycle and Transportation
(DNCSH) Public Workshop (Workshop #3) was conducted to identify nuclear data and criticality safety
validation benchmark gaps relevant to transportable microreactors. This report summarizes results from a
pre-workshop survey, workshop participation and content, and post-workshop feedback to inform
DNCSH priority activities and potential focus areas for the third Experimental Access Work (EAW)
proposal call.

The pre-workshop survey received 52 responses, with respondents highlighting strong interest in several
transportable microreactor concepts and emphasizing needs spanning nuclear data adequacy, benchmark
coverage, transport and accident scenarios, regulatory considerations, and methods. The workshop
registration page received 342 views, resulting in 173 registrations and 135 participants (124
virtual and 11 in person) from 39 organizations. Government and national laboratory organizations
represented 64.4% of attendees. Following the workshop, the post-workshop survey received 13
responses indicating that the workshop was largely effective in identifying microreactor-relevant
validation challenges.

Across both surveys and workshop discussion, the dominant technical priority was improved validation
benchmark coverage tailored to microreactor materials, spectra, and transport configurations. Participants
emphasized the need for benchmarks and supporting nuclear data for advanced moderators, tristructural-
isotropic (TRISO)/graphite systems, and key structural/reflector/absorber materials. Respondents also
expressed interest in validation gaps related to credible normal conditions of transport and hypothetical
accident conditions across the microreactor lifecycle as well as in documenting best practices for using
sensitivity/uncertainty methods. Finally, the workshop reinforced the value of continued expansion of
the DNCSH application model repository with transport-relevant bounding models.

 1. INTRODUCTION

Advanced reactor development and deployment is introducing fuel forms, materials, and configurations
that differ from those used in traditional light-water reactors (LWRs), including tristructural-isotropic
(TRISO)-based fuels, moderators, high-temperature materials, and compact core designs. These attributes
can fall outside the range of applicability of existing criticality safety validation bases and may require
additional work in code validation and benchmark applicability.

The DOE/NRC Criticality Safety for Commercial-Scale HALEU Fuel Cycle and Transportation
(DNCSH) project was established through the Inflation Reduction Act of 2022 (H.R. 5376) to support the
experimental and analytical basis supporting nuclear criticality safety analyses for high-assay low-
enriched uranium (HALEU) fuel cycle operations and transportation activities as well as to help address
critical experiment validation gaps that can impede licensing and regulatory approvals for HALEU-
related operations [1]. An initial public workshop was held in February 2024 to address HALEU
transportation validation gaps [2]. The resulting call for proposals was released in April 2024 and resulted
in funding for the execution and/or evaluation of 16 experiment and analysis projects [3]. A second
public workshop was held in August 2025 to address facility and operational validation gaps, precluding a
second call for proposals [4]. The resulting call for proposals was released in October 2025 and resulted
in funding for 10 projects [5].

Building on these efforts, the Third DNCSH Public Workshop (Workshop #3) emphasized nuclear data
and benchmark gaps relevant to transportable microreactors, including configurations and scenarios
encountered during manufacturing, packaging, transport, deployment, and post-operation handling [6]. To
publicize the workshop and support a targeted discussion, a pre-workshop survey was distributed to
community members and other stakeholders. Survey responses were categorized into topical bins: nuclear
data adequacy and gaps, validation benchmarks and gaps, transport and accident scenarios, regulatory
framework, methods & modeling, and collaboration opportunities. The survey also collected input on
microreactor concepts of interest and on potential additions to the DNCSH application model repository
[7].

Workshop #3 was conducted with both virtual and in-person participation options. Presentations
summarized DNCSH goals and progress, US Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC) considerations
relevant to microreactor transportation, ongoing DNCSH nuclear data efforts, and a benchmark gap
assessment for representative microreactor applications and transport configurations. A post-workshop
survey was used to solicit feedback on workshop effectiveness and to capture priority topics that should
guide subsequent DNCSH activities, including potential focus areas for the third DNCSH proposal call
for Experimental Access Work (EAW).

This report summarizes the pre-workshop survey results, workshop participation and content, and post-
workshop feedback, and it identifies potential outcomes and actions resulting from Workshop #3. The
appendices provide detailed survey responses and self-reported attendee affiliations.

accounted for 64.4% of participants, and nongovernment participants (industry, academic, and public)

National Laboratory. Other organizations with notable attendance included Brookhaven National

(typically with one to three attendees), including other national laboratories, federal and international
government entities, universities, companies, and public/independent representatives. Figure 3-2

 4. POST-WORKSHOP SURVEY

Following the workshop, a survey was sent to attendees requesting feedback on the workshop and topics
that should be prioritized to address validation and benchmark needs for transportable microreactors. The
survey questions can be found in Appendix D.

A total of 13 responses were received. Figure 4-1 shows the distribution of attendee feedback on the
effectiveness of the workshop. The responses indicate that the workshop largely met its objective of
identifying validation gaps and challenges specific to transportable microreactors.

 Figure 4-1. Attendee feedback on workshop effectiveness.

The open-ended responses provided useful input regarding the gaps and needs. Similar to the pre-
workshop survey, the responses were binned in the following categories:

• Nuclear data adequacy and gaps
• Validation benchmarks and gaps
• Transport and accident scenarios
• Regulatory framework
• Methods & modeling
• Collaboration opportunities

Figure 4-2 shows the categorized nuclear data and criticality safety analysis topics selected by the
participants. The dominant priority was improved validation benchmarks and benchmark coverage
tailored to microreactors, including experiments involving advanced/solid moderators (e.g., ZrH, YH, and
CaH), unique moderator configurations, heat pipe materials, and structural/shielding materials.
Respondents also identified targeted nuclear data and uncertainty needs, including emphasis on stainless
steel constituents (Cr, Fe) and Gd.

Another priority was the need to define and standardize transport and accident scenarios that support
verification and validation (V&V) and upper subcritical limit (USL) studies.

The detailed participant responses and their topic categories can be found in Appendix E.

 5. OUTCOMES AND ACTIONS

Workshop #3 and its associated surveys reinforced that the dominant needs for transportable
microreactors are improved validation benchmark coverage and strengthened nuclear data support for
HALEU systems and microreactor-relevant materials. Some of the key structural/reflector/absorber
materials identified were stainless steel constituents Fe/Cr/Ni, Be, B, and Gd as well as advanced/solid
moderators (e.g., ZrH, YH, CaH). During the workshop, and across both pre- and post-workshop input, a
need was identified related to transport under normal conditions of transport and hypothetical accident
conditions as well as lifecycle-representative configurations (e.g., partially assembled states,
package/array effects, flooding/water ingress, and post-operation conditions). Finally, the workshop
highlighted expanding the DNCSH application model repository with transport-relevant models.

Some of the recommended actions are as follows:

• Define and document a consistent set of representative transport and accident scenarios for use in
 V&V and USL development.

• Prioritize new benchmark experiments and/or evaluations targeting microreactor materials, spectra,
 and configurations—especially advanced moderators, reflector/structural materials, and absorbers.

• Expand the DNCSH application model repository to include bounding transport packages and
 lifecycle states to enable consistent sensitivity/uncertainty (S/U) and similarity assessments.

• Document best practices for using S/U methods and investigate validation methods that account for
 benchmark redundancy/correlation and their implications for safety margins.

These actions, together with continued engagement (including a focused multiday validation methods
workshop), provide a practical path to inform future DNCSH activities and potential topics under the third
EAW proposal call.
