Abstract
. We classify polar homogeneous foliations on rank one symmetric spaces of noncompact type up to orbit equivalence.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
53C35, 53C12, 57S20, 57S25

1.
Introduction

An isometric action of a Lie group
G
on a Riemannian manifold
M
is polar if it admits a section,
that is, a connected submanifold
\Sigma
that meets every orbit of the action orthogonally.
We also say that the
G
-action is hyperpolar if it admits a flat section.
The archetypal example of a polar action is the standard representation of the orthogonal group

\mathsf{O}(2)
on the Euclidean plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Here, the sections are precisely the straight lines that go through the origin.
Similarly, the action of
\mathsf{O}(n)
on the vector space
\mathrm{Sym}_{n}(\mathbb{R})

of all
n\times n
symmetric matrices is also polar and
the subspace of all diagonal matrices is a section.
In view of this example, the elements of a section are commonly understood as canonical forms
of the elements of the ambient space.
Polar actions (and in particular cohomogeneity one actions) have been extensively used
in invariant theory
[
31
]
as well as in the construction of new examples
of manifolds with exceptional holonomy
[
12
]
or inhomogeneous Einstein
metrics
[
10
]
, to name a few examples.
For a general treatment on the main properties of polar actions, we refer the reader
to
[
23
,
31
]
.

In this article, we are interested in classifying polar actions on Riemannian manifolds
up to orbit equivalence.
The natural choice of ambient spaces in which to tackle this problem are symmetric spaces.
This is due to two reasons:
on the one hand, symmetric spaces have large isometry groups with a well understood structure;
on the other hand, a section of a polar action is a totally geodesic submanifold, and those can
be characterized algebraically in symmetric spaces by means of so-called Lie triple systems.

The first systematic classification of polar actions was carried out by Dadok
[
15
]
,
who showed that every polar representation is orbit equivalent to the isotropy representation
of a symmetric space.
This result also yields the classification of polar actions on round spheres.
Later, Wu
[
36
]
derived the classification of polar actions on real hyperbolic spaces.

There have been significant advances in the classification of polar actions on (irreducible)
symmetric spaces of compact type.
Indeed, Podestà and Thorbergsson
[
32
]
described all polar actions
on symmetric spaces of compact type and rank one
(that is, spheres and projective spaces over the normed real division algebras).
From Dadok’s result, one can relate these actions to Cartan’s classification of symmetric
spaces.
This work was later revised by Gorodski and Kollross
[
21
]
,
who found a missing example in the case of the Cayley projective plane.
Moreover, Biliotti
[
9
]
conjectured that if
M
is an irreducible symmetric
space of compact type and rank greater than one, then every polar action on
M
is hyperpolar.
This conjecture was shown to be true by Kollross and Lytchak
[
29
]
.
Additionally, Kollross proved that every hyperpolar action on
M
is
either of cohomogeneity one or orbit equivalent to a Hermann action.
The proof of this fact, as well as the classification of both families of actions,
can be found in
[
26
]
.

In contrast with the compact case, results concerning polar actions on symmetric spaces
of noncompact type are quite scarce.
The only such spaces in which polar actions have been completely determined
are the real hyperbolic space (as mentioned above) and the complex
hyperbolic space
[
16
]
.
The special case of cohomogeneity one actions has enjoyed the most attention.
Their classification is the result of a collective effort that started
in
[
2
]
and finally ended in
[
33
]
.

We say that an isometric action induces a homogeneous foliation if all of its
orbits have the same type
(that is, if all its isotropy subgroups are conjugate).
Berndt and Tamaru classified all codimension one homogeneous foliations
on irreducible symmetric spaces of noncompact type—see
[
7
]

and the subsequent generalization
[
34
]
to the reducible case.
Moreover, Berndt, Tamaru and the first author developed in
[
5
]

a method to construct all hyperpolar homogeneous foliations on irreducible symmetric
spaces of noncompact type.
More recently, we have obtained the classification of
codimension two polar homogeneous foliations on irreducible symmetric spaces of
noncompact type, see
[
18
]
.
These foliations turn out to be lower dimensional analogues of the codimension one
examples constructed by Berndt and Tamaru.
The main difficulty in dealing with polar homogeneous foliations of larger codimension
is that their sections are not known to satisfy any restriction apart from being
totally geodesic.
In fact, one can see that if
M
is an irreducible symmetric space of noncompact type,
then every boundary component of
M
is the section of a polar homogeneous foliation
on
M

[
5
]
.
However, using the theory of maximal solvable subalgebras of real semismple Lie algebras,
one can immediately give constraints on the subgroups of the isometry group of
M

that act polarly inducing a foliation
[
18
, Proposition 4.2]
.

The purpose of this article is to classify all polar homogeneous foliations on
symmetric spaces of noncompact type and rank one.
As we mentioned earlier, the only symmetric spaces of noncompact type on which
polar actions have been completely classified are the real and complex
hyperbolic spaces.
We also refer the reader to
[
4
]
for a direct proof of the
classification of polar homogeneous foliations on complex hyperbolic spaces.
Because of this, the ambient spaces under our consideration are the
quaternionic hyperbolic spaces
\mathbb{H}\mathsf{H}^{n}
(where
n\geq 2
)
and the Cayley hyperbolic plane
\mathbb{O}\mathsf{H}^{2}
.
In these spaces, the works of Kollross
[
27
,
28
]

provide partial classifications of polar actions that preserve a proper totally
geodesic submanifold, and it turns out that all of the actions found in his results possess
singular orbits.
In addition, Berndt and Tamaru
[
6
]
derived the classification of
cohomogeneity one actions on the Cayley hyperbolic plane.
In the same paper, the authors reduce the classification of cohomogeneity one
actions on quaternionic hyperbolic spaces to that of the so-called protohomogeneous
vector subspaces of quaternionic Euclidean vector spaces.
The latter problem was solved by the first author, Domínguez-Vázquez and
Rodríguez-Vázquez in
[
17
]
.
At any rate, there are no results concerning polar actions (of cohomogeneity greater than one)
without singular orbits on
\mathbb{H}\mathsf{H}^{n}
or
\mathbb{O}\mathsf{H}^{2}

(or more generally, actions that do not preserve a proper totally geodesic submanifold).

We now state the main theorems that will be proved in this article.
Let
M
denote a rank one Riemannian symmetric space of noncompact type
that can be represented by a symmetric pair
(G,K)
.
More explicitly,
M=\mathbb{F}\mathsf{H}^{n}
is a hyperbolic space over one of the real normed
division algebras
\mathbb{F}\in\{\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C},\mathbb{H},\mathbb{O}\}
, with
n=2
if
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{O}
.
Throughout this paper, we work with the symmetric metric on
\mathbb{F}\mathsf{H}^{n}
that pinches its
sectional curvature between
-1
and
-1/4
.
Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{n}
be an Iwasawa decomposition of the Lie algebra of
G
.
We can write
\mathfrak{n}=\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2\alpha}
as a direct sum of vector spaces,
where
\mathfrak{g}_{2\alpha}
is the center of
\mathfrak{n}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\cong\mathbb{F}^{n-1}
as an
\mathbb{F}
-module.
Given a subspace
\mathfrak{b}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}
and an abelian subspace
\mathfrak{v}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
, we construct the subalgebra
\mathfrak{s}_{\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{v}}=(\mathfrak{a}\ominus\mathfrak{b})\oplus(\mathfrak{n}\ominus\mathfrak{v})
.
We will show that the connected subgroup
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{v}}
of
G
whose Lie algebra is

\mathfrak{s}_{\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{v}}
acts polarly on
\mathbb{F}\mathsf{H}^{n}
inducing a foliation.
Moreover, any section of the action of
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{v}}
is a real hyperbolic space whose curvature is equal to the maximum sectional curvature attained by a
2
-plane in
\mathbb{F}\mathsf{H}^{n}
.
We will also show that the actions of
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{b}_{1},\mathfrak{v}_{1}}
and
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{b}_{2},\mathfrak{v}_{2}}
on
\mathbb{F}\mathsf{H}^{n}

are orbit equivalent if and only if
\dim\mathfrak{b}_{1}=\dim\mathfrak{b}_{2}
and
\dim\mathfrak{v}_{1}=\dim\mathfrak{v}_{2}
.
Thus, it makes sense to define
\mathsf{S}_{a,b}=\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{v}}
,
where
a=\dim\mathfrak{b}\in\{0,1\}
and
b=\dim\mathfrak{v}\in\{0,\dots,n-1\}
.
This definition is correct up to isometric congruence.
The main result of this paper is:

Theorem A
.

Every nontrivial polar homogeneous foliation on a rank one symmetric space of noncompact type is isometrically congruent to the orbit foliation induced by a group
\mathsf{S}_{a,b}
with
a\in\{0,1\}
and
b\in\{0,\dots,n-1\}
.

Note that if
a=0
and
b=0
, then
\mathsf{S}_{0,0}
acts transitively on
\mathbb{F}\mathsf{H}^{n}
.
If
a+b=1
then the action of
\mathsf{S}_{a,b}
is of cohomogeneity one
[
7
]
.
If
a=1
and
b=0
, then
\mathsf{S}_{1,0}
is nilpotent and its action induces the horosphere foliation.
If
a=0
and
b=1
, then
\mathsf{S}_{0,1}
is solvable and its action induces the so-called solvable foliation.
If
a=1
we get
\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{s}_{1,b}=\mathfrak{n}\ominus\mathfrak{v}
above;
in this case all the orbits of the action of
\mathsf{S}_{1,b}
are congruent and are contained in horospheres of
\mathbb{F}\mathsf{H}^{n}
.

Taking into account that the trivial action is also polar we obtain

Theorem B
.

There are exactly
2n+1
congruency classes of homogeneous polar foliations on
\mathbb{F}\mathsf{H}^{n}
up to isometric congruence.

Remarkably, the number of congruency classes of homogeneous foliations on hyperbolic
spaces does not depend on base field
\mathbb{F}
, but only on
\dim_{\mathbb{F}}M
.
In particular, in the Cayley hyperbolic plane
\mathbb{O}\mathsf{H}^{2}
we obtain two homogeneous polar foliations of codimension one,
one of codimension two, the transitive action, and the trivial action, up to isometric congruence.

In this paper we tackle quaternionic hyperbolic spaces and the Cayley hyperbolic plane.
Parts of the proof are common to both cases, but some arguments are specific of the normed division algebra under consideration.

Theorem
A
follows directly from the more precise statement given below,
which is already known to be true for real and complex hyperbolic spaces:

Theorem C
.

Let
\mathbb{F}\mathsf{H}^{n}=G/K
,
n\geq 2
, be a rank one symmetric space of noncompact type.
Then the following assertions are true:

(1)

Given a vector subspace
\mathfrak{b}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}
and an abelian subspace
\mathfrak{v}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
, the subgroup
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{v}}\subseteq{G}
acts polarly on
\mathbb{F}\mathsf{H}^{n}
inducing a foliation.

(2)

Any nontrivial polar homogeneous foliation on
\mathbb{F}\mathsf{H}^{n}
is isometrically congruent to the orbit foliation of a subgroup
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{v}}
as in item (
1
).

(3)

Given subspaces
\mathfrak{b}_{1}
,
\mathfrak{b}_{2}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}

and abelian subspaces
\mathfrak{v}_{1}
,
\mathfrak{v}_{2}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
,
the actions of
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{b}_{1},\mathfrak{v}_{1}}
and
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{b}_{1},\mathfrak{v}_{2}}
are orbit equivalent
if and only if
\dim\mathfrak{b}_{1}=\dim\mathfrak{b}_{2}
and
\dim\mathfrak{v}_{1}=\dim\mathfrak{v}_{2}
.

A consequence of Theorem
C
is that every nontrivial polar homogeneous
foliation on a hyperbolic space has a section of constant curvature.
This will be shown in Proposition
3.1
.
Combining this with Lemma
2.1
, one can show that every nontrivial
polar action on a hyperbolic space has a section of constant curvature.
This result is also known to be true if the ambient space is an irreducible
symmetric space of compact type,
see
[
32
, Proposition 1B.1]
for the rank one case
and
[
29
, Theorem 1.1]
for the case of higher rank.
By contrast, in
[
5
, Proposition 4.2]
the authors give examples
of polar actions on irreducible symmetric spaces of noncompact type
that possess sections of nonconstant curvature.
These observations lead us to the following characterization of irreducible
symmetric spaces of noncompact type and higher rank:

Corollary D
.

Let
M
be an irreducible symmetric space.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

M
is a symmetric space of noncompact type and rank
r\geq 2
.

(ii)

M
admits a polar action whose sections have nonconstant curvature.

We now describe the structure of this paper.
In Section
2
we present the basic facts and notation that will be needed in the rest of the paper.
In particular, we introduce generalized Heisenberg algebras in §
2.1
.
These are used in Subsection
2.2
to deduce some formulae that are essential in our calculations.
We also recall the classification of totally geodesic submanifolds of rank one symmetric spaces (§
2.3
) and the basic facts about polar actions in Subsection
2.4
.
The proofs of items (
1
) and (
3
)
of Theorem
C
are tackled in Section
3
.
In Section
4
, we provide some general results concerning polar
homogeneous foliations on symmetric spaces of noncompact type and rank one.
Finally, the proof of Theorem
C
(
2
) is carried out in
Section
5
for quaternionic hyperbolic spaces
and in Section
6
for the Cayley hyperbolic plane.

2.
Preliminaries

The purpose of this section is to introduce the general notions and results concerning symmetric spaces of noncompact type and rank one, as well as polar actions.
The solvable model
M=AN
allows us to view a rank one symmetric space of noncompact type as an extension of a one-dimensional Lie group by a two-step nilpotent (or abelian) Lie group.
The subgroup
N
is an example of a generalized Heisenberg group, while
M
belongs to the class of Damek–Ricci spaces.
By exploiting the algebraic structure of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{n}
as a generalized Heisenberg algebra, we are able to perform general calculations with relative ease.

2.1.
Generalized Heisenberg algebras

A detailed account of the classification and geometry of both generalized Heisenberg groups and Damek–Ricci spaces can be found in
[
8
]
.
We also refer the reader to
[
13
]
for a treatment focused on rank one symmetric spaces.

Let
\mathfrak{v}
and
\mathfrak{z}
be real vector spaces and
\beta\colon\scalebox{0.9}{$\bigwedge$}^{2}\,\mathfrak{v}\to\mathfrak{z}

a skew-symmetric bilinear map,
We construct the vector space
\mathfrak{n}=\mathfrak{v}\oplus\mathfrak{z}
and endow it with an inner product

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathfrak{n}}
such that
\mathfrak{v}
and
\mathfrak{z}
are orthogonal,
and a Lie bracket given by
[\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{z}]=0
and
[U,V]=\beta(U\wedge V)
for all
U
,
V\in\mathfrak{v}
.

Each vector
Z\in\mathfrak{z}
gives rise to a skew-symmetric endomorphism
J_{Z}\in\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{v})
by letting

\langle J_{Z}U,V\rangle_{\mathfrak{n}}=\langle[U,V],Z\rangle_{\mathfrak{n}}
for each
U
,
V\in\mathfrak{z}
.
We say that
\mathfrak{n}
is a
generalized Heisenberg algebra
if

J_{Z}^{2}=-\lvert Z\rvert_{\mathfrak{n}}^{2}\operatorname{id}_{\mathfrak{v}}
for every
Z\in\mathfrak{v}
.
In other words, we require
J\colon\mathfrak{z}\to\operatorname{End}(\mathfrak{v})
to extend to an algebra homomorphism
J\colon\operatorname{Cl}(\mathfrak{z})\to\operatorname{End}(\mathfrak{v})
defined on the Clifford algebra of
\mathfrak{z}
(with the aforementioned inner product).
In particular, generalized Heisenberg algebras are closely related to Clifford modules.
The classification of Clifford modules implies the classification of generalized Heisenberg algebras
[
8
, Section 3.1.2]
.

A consequence of this definition is that the maps
J_{Z}
with
\lvert Z\rvert_{\mathfrak{n}}=1
become complex structures on the real vector space
\mathfrak{v}
.
Because a complex structure is also a linear isomorphism, the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{n}
is two-step nilpotent (except in the case
\mathfrak{z}=0
, where
\mathfrak{n}
is abelian) and its center is
\mathfrak{z}
.
A subspace
\mathfrak{w}\subseteq\mathfrak{v}
is said to be
totally real
if it is totally real with respect to all
J_{Z}
, where
Z\in\mathfrak{z}
and
\lvert Z\rvert_{\mathfrak{n}}=1
.
Equivalently,
\mathfrak{w}
is totally real if and only if it is an abelian subspace of
\mathfrak{v}
.

We will use the following formulae involving
U
,
V\in\mathfrak{v}
and
X
,
Y\in\mathfrak{z}
:

\displaystyle J_{X}J_{Y}+J_{Y}J_{X}={}

\displaystyle-2\langle X,Y\rangle_{\mathfrak{n}}\operatorname{id}_{\mathfrak{v}},

\displaystyle\langle J_{X}U,J_{X}V\rangle_{\mathfrak{n}}={}

\displaystyle\lvert X\rvert^{2}\langle U,V\rangle_{\mathfrak{n}},

\displaystyle\langle J_{X}U,J_{Y}U\rangle_{\mathfrak{n}}={}

\displaystyle\lvert U\rvert_{\mathfrak{n}}^{2}\langle X,Y\rangle_{\mathfrak{n}},

\displaystyle[V,J_{X}V]={}

\displaystyle\lvert V\rvert_{\mathfrak{n}}^{2}X.

Let
V\in\mathfrak{v}
be a nonzero vector and define
\mathfrak{J}V=\{J_{Z}V\colon Z\in\mathfrak{z}\}
.
It is clear from the skew-symmetry of the operators
J_{Z}
that
\mathbb{R}V
and
\mathfrak{J}V
are orthogonal subspaces of
\mathfrak{v}
.
Furthermore, it is easy to check that an element
W\in\mathfrak{v}
belongs to
\mathfrak{v}\ominus(\mathbb{R}V\oplus\mathfrak{J}V)
if and only if
\langle V,W\rangle_{\mathfrak{n}}=0
and
[V,W]=0
.

In this article, we make use of the following fact:
Suppose
(V,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
is a Euclidean vector space.
Then the exterior square
\scalebox{0.9}{$\bigwedge$}^{2}\,V
becomes a Euclidean vector space
with the inner product that is characterized by

\langle u\wedge v,w\wedge x\rangle=\langle u,w\rangle\langle v,x\rangle-\langle u,x\rangle\langle v,w\rangle

for all
u,\,v,\,w,\,x\in V
.

2.2.
Noncompact rank one symmetric spaces

Here we gather some general facts on symmetric spaces of noncompact type, focusing mainly on those of rank one.
See
[
24
]
for a thorough treatment of symmetric spaces.
We also refer to
[
19
]
for more information on symmetric spaces of noncompact type, as well as to
[
25
, Chapter 6]
for the structure theory of real semisimple Lie groups and algebras.

Let
M
be an
n
-dimensional symmetric space of noncompact type.
For the purposes of this article, we assume that
M
is connected and irreducible as a Riemannian manifold.
We can present
M
as a Riemannian homogeneous space
G/K
, where
G
is the identity component
I^{0}(M)
of the full isometry group
I(M)
and
K=G_{o}
is the isotropy subgroup corresponding to a basepoint
o\in M
.
The Lie group
G
is simple and noncompact, whereas
K
is a maximal compact subgroup of
G
.
Moreover, the sectional curvature of
M
is nonpositive and the Riemannian exponential map
\exp_{o}\colon T_{o}M\to M
is a global diffeomorphism.

Let us consider the isometry algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, which is simple.
The Killing form of
\mathfrak{g}
is denoted by
\mathcal{B}
.
One can show that there exists an involutive automorphism
\theta\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
with the property that the symmetric bilinear form
\mathcal{B}_{\theta}(X,Y)=-\mathcal{B}(X,\theta Y)
is positive definite.
We say in this case that
\theta
is the Cartan involution of
\mathfrak{g}
, and it is unique up to inner automorphisms.
The eigenspace decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}
associated with
\theta
(where
\mathfrak{k}=\ker(1-\theta)
and
\mathfrak{p}=\ker(1+\theta)
) is known as the Cartan decomposition of
\mathfrak{g}
.
Because
\mathfrak{k}
coincides with the isotropy algebra at
o
, we may identify
\mathfrak{p}
with the tangent space
T_{o}M
.
The restriction of
\mathcal{B}_{\theta}
to
\mathfrak{p}
is an
\operatorname{Ad}(K)
-invariant inner product on
\mathfrak{p}\cong T_{o}M
, so that it may be extended to a
G
-invariant metric on
M
.
Since
M
is irreducible, this
G
-invariant metric is homothetic to the original metric on
M
.
It should be noted that if a Lie group action on
M
is polar, then it is also polar with respect to any multiple of the metric on
M
, so it will be convenient for us to work with an adequate rescaling of said metric.
We also remark that if
X\in\mathfrak{g}
is any vector, then the adjoint of
\operatorname{ad}(X)
is
\operatorname{ad}(X)^{*}=-\operatorname{ad}(\theta X)
.

We now choose a maximal abelian subspace
\mathfrak{a}
of
\mathfrak{p}
.
Any two choices of
\mathfrak{a}
are conjugate by
K
, so the dimension of
\mathfrak{a}
is a well-defined invariant of
M
, known as the rank of
M
.
Let
\mathfrak{a}^{*}
denote the dual vector space of
\mathfrak{a}
, which inherits an inner product
from the one on
\mathfrak{a}
.
Then, any linear form
\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}
determines a vector subspace

\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}=\big\{X\in\mathfrak{g}\colon[H,X]=\lambda(H)X\text{ for all }H\in\mathfrak{a}\big\}.

Note that, since the family
\operatorname{ad}(\mathfrak{a})
consists of derivations,
one sees that
[\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda},\mathfrak{g}_{\mu}]\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda+\mu}

whenever
\mu\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}
.
If both
\lambda
and
\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}
are nonzero, we say that
\lambda
is a (restricted) root of
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}
is its corresponding root space.
The set of roots is denoted by
\Sigma
.
Because the family
\operatorname{ad}(\mathfrak{a})\subseteq\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
is a commuting family of symmetric endomorphisms with respect to
\mathcal{B}_{\theta}
, it follows that
\mathfrak{g}
can be decomposed as the orthogonal direct sum

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{0}\oplus\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Sigma}\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}.

This vector space decomposition is known as the root space decomposition of
\mathfrak{g}
.
Moreover, the set
\Sigma\subseteq\mathfrak{a}^{*}
is a (perhaps nonreduced) abstract root system in the sense of
[
25
, Section 2.5]
.
We also set
\mathfrak{k}_{\lambda}=(1+\theta)\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}
and
\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}=(1-\theta)\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}
for each
\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}
.
It is readily shown that
\mathfrak{g}_{0}=\mathfrak{k}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{a}
.
In particular, the adjoint action of
\mathfrak{k}_{0}
normalizes every root space.
The connected subgroup of
G
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{k}_{0}
is denoted by
K_{0}
, and it is a compact subgroup of
K
.

Now, we fix a notion of positivity on
\Sigma
.
We let
\Sigma^{+}
be the corresponding set of positive roots and
\Lambda\subseteq\Sigma^{+}
be the subset of simple roots.
One sees that
\Lambda
is a basis of
\mathfrak{a}^{*}
, and thus its cardinality is equal to the rank of
M
.
Furthermore, the vector subspace
\mathfrak{n}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Sigma^{+}}\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}
is a nilpotent subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
.
The Iwasawa decomposition theorem asserts that
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{n}
(vector space direct sum).
In addition, by taking
A
,
N
and
AN
to be the connected subgroups of
G
whose Lie algebras
are
\mathfrak{a}
,
\mathfrak{n}
and
\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{n}
respectively,
one sees that the multiplication maps
K\times A\times N\to G

and
A\times N\to AN
are diffeomorphisms.
In particular, the evaluation map
g\in AN\to g\cdot o\in M
is also a diffeomorphism,
and by pulling back the Riemannian metric on
M
to
AN

we conclude that
M
is isometric to a solvable Lie group endowed with a left-invariant Riemannian metric.

From now on, we suppose that
M
is of rank one, so that
M=\mathbb{F}\mathsf{H}^{n}
is a hyperbolic space over a finite-dimensional
real division algebra
\mathbb{F}\in\{\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C},\mathbb{H},\mathbb{O}\}
.
We may choose
{G}
and
{K}
as in Table
1
, so that
{G}
is (up to a finite cover) the identity component of the isometry group of
M
and
{K}={G}_{o}
is the isotropy subgroup at a point
o\in M
.
Because
M
is a symmetric space of rank one, any maximal abelian subspace
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
is one-dimensional.

2.3.
Totally geodesic submanifolds of symmetric spaces of rank one

In this subsection we recall the classification of complete totally geodesic submanifolds (of dimension greater than one) of the hyperbolic spaces
\mathbb{F}\mathsf{H}^{n}
under investigation.
Wolf
[
35
]
classified totally geodesic submanifolds of the compact rank one symmetric spaces, and we may apply the duality of symmetric spaces to derive our classification in hyperbolic spaces.
See also
[
28
, Section 5]
for a detailed treatment of the totally
geodesic submanifolds of
\mathbb{O}\mathsf{H}^{2}
.
From this point on, we denote by
\mathbb{R}\mathsf{H}^{k}(c)
the real hyperbolic space of constant curvature
-c^{-2}
.
In particular, we have
\mathbb{R}\mathsf{H}^{k}(2)=\mathbb{R}\mathsf{H}^{k}
.

Before going through the classification, we recall that every (complete) totally geodesic submanifold
\Sigma
of a symmetric space of noncompact type
M
is closed and embedded.
In addition,
\Sigma
is determined by its tangent space at any point
p\in\Sigma
because
\Sigma=\exp_{p}(T_{p}\Sigma)
.
Furthermore, a subspace
\mathfrak{v}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}\cong T_{o}M
is the tangent space of a totally geodesic submanifold
\Sigma
of
M
containing
o=eK
if and only if
[[\mathfrak{v},\mathfrak{v}],\mathfrak{v}]\subseteq\mathfrak{v}
.
The subspaces of
\mathfrak{p}
that satisfy the latter condition are known as Lie triple systems.

The case of
M=\mathbb{R}\mathsf{H}^{n}
is by far the simplest.
For each
2\leq k\leq n
there exists a unique complete
k
-dimensional totally geodesic submanifold of
M
, and it is congruent to
\mathbb{R}\mathsf{H}^{k}
.
This submanifold can be obtained as the orbit through
o=e{K}
of the subgroup
\mathsf{SO}^{0}(1,k)\subseteq\mathsf{SO}^{0}(1,n)
embedded in the standard manner.

Let us consider the complex case.
For each
2\leq k\leq n
, the orbits through the origin of the standard subgroups
\mathsf{SO}^{0}(1,k)
and
\mathsf{SU}(1,k)

are totally geodesic
\mathbb{R}\mathsf{H}^{k}(2)
and
\mathbb{C}\mathsf{H}^{k}
, respectively.
One sees that
\mathbb{R}\mathsf{H}^{k}(2)
is a totally real submanifold of
\mathbb{C}\mathsf{H}^{n}
, whereas
\mathbb{C}\mathsf{H}^{k}
is a complex submanifold.
We also have a totally geodesic
\mathbb{C}\mathsf{H}^{1}\cong\mathbb{R}\mathsf{H}^{2}(1)
appearing as an orbit of
\mathsf{SU}(1,1)
.
Unlike the submanifolds
\mathbb{R}\mathsf{H}^{k}(2)
, this
\mathbb{R}\mathsf{H}^{2}(1)
is a complex submanifold of
\mathbb{C}\mathsf{H}^{n}
.
These examples give all totally geodesic submanifolds of
\mathbb{C}\mathsf{H}^{n}
up to congruence.

Now we list all totally geodesic submanifolds of
\mathbb{H}\mathsf{H}^{n}
up to congruence.
On the one hand, the orbits through
o=e{K}
of the subgroups
\mathsf{SO}^{0}(1,k)
,
\mathsf{SU}(1,k)
, and
\mathsf{Sp}(1,k)

(
2\leq k\leq n
), all embedded in the standard manner,
give rise to totally geodesic
\mathbb{R}\mathsf{H}^{k}(2)
,
\mathbb{C}\mathsf{H}^{k}
, and
\mathbb{H}\mathsf{H}^{k}
, respectively.
In particular, the submanifold
\mathbb{R}\mathsf{H}^{k}(2)
is totally real,

\mathbb{C}\mathsf{H}^{k}
is totally complex, and
\mathbb{H}\mathsf{H}^{k}
is quaternionic.
On the other hand, the orbit
\mathsf{Sp}(1,1)\cdot o
is a totally geodesic
\mathbb{H}\mathsf{H}^{1}\cong\mathbb{R}\mathsf{H}^{4}(1)
of constant curvature equal to
-1
.
Because
\mathbb{R}\mathsf{H}^{4}(1)
has constant curvature, any
k
-dimensional real subspace of
T_{o}\mathbb{R}\mathsf{H}^{4}(1)
exponentiates to a totally geodesic
\mathbb{R}\mathsf{H}^{k}(1)
inside
\mathbb{R}\mathsf{H}^{4}(1)
.

Finally, we comment on the Cayley case.
The submanifolds
\mathbb{R}\mathsf{H}^{2}(2)\subseteq\mathbb{C}\mathsf{H}^{2}\subseteq\mathbb{H}\mathsf{H}^{2}
appear as the orbits of the subgroups
\mathsf{SO}^{0}(1,2)\subseteq\mathsf{SU}(1,2)\subseteq\mathsf{Sp}(1,2)
at
o
.
The Lie algebra embedding
\mathfrak{sp}(1,2)\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{f}_{4}^{-20}
is given explicitly in
[
28
, Proposition 5.3]
.
In addition, we have a totally geodesic
\mathbb{O}\mathsf{H}^{1}\cong\mathbb{R}\mathsf{H}^{8}(1)
arising as the orbit of the action of an
\mathsf{SO}^{0}(1,8)\subseteq\mathsf{F}_{4}^{-20}
.
Similarly to the quaternionic case, the fact that
\mathbb{O}\mathsf{H}^{1}
has constant curvature means that every subspace of
T_{o}\mathbb{O}\mathsf{H}^{1}
exponentiates to a totally geodesic submanifold isometric to
\mathbb{R}\mathsf{H}^{k}
.
Altogether these examples yield all totally geodesic submanifolds of
\mathbb{O}\mathsf{H}^{2}

up to congruence.

2.4.
Polar actions

We now recall the basic notions concerning polar actions on Riemannian manifolds.
Suppose
M
is a complete Riemannian manifold and
H
is a Lie group acting isometrically on
M
.
For the purposes of this article, we may assume that
H
acts effectively on
M
, which means that the only element of
H
that acts trivially on
M
is the identity element.
Hence, we can view
H
as a subgroup of the isometry group
I(M)
, and if
H
is connected, then it corresponds to a subgroup of the identity component
I^{0}(M)
.

The action of
H\subseteq I(M)
on
M
is called proper if the shear map
H\times M\to M\times M
defined by
(h,p)\mapsto(h\cdot p,p)
is a proper map.
This condition is equivalent to
H
being a closed subgroup of
I(M)
.
If the action of
H
is proper, then one sees that the isotropy subgroups of the action are compact, the
H
-orbits are closed and embedded in
M
, and the orbit space
M/H
is Hausdorff when endowed with the quotient topology.

The orbits of a proper isometric action can be classified according to their type.
We say that two orbits
H\cdot p
and
H\cdot q
have the same type if the isotropy subgroups
H_{p}
and
H_{q}
are conjugate.
The orbit type of the orbit
H\cdot p
is denoted by
[H\cdot p]
.
In addition, given two orbits
H\cdot x,\,H\cdot y
, we write
[H\cdot x]\leq[H\cdot y]
if
H_{y}
is conjugate to a subgroup of
H_{x}
.
The relation
\leq
on the set of orbit types of the
H
-action can be shown to be a partial ordering and it possesses a maximum element.
The
H
-orbits whose orbit type is maximum with respect to
\leq
are called principal orbits,
and their union constitutes a dense open subset of
M
.
A nonprincipal orbit
H\cdot p
is called singular if its dimension is strictly smaller than that of the principal orbits,
and exceptional if it has the same dimension as the principal orbits.

In this work, we are interested in distinguishing isometric actions in terms of their orbits.
To this effect, we say that two isometric actions
H_{1}\curvearrowright M
and
H_{2}\curvearrowright M
are orbit equivalent if there exists an isometry of
M
that carries the orbits of the
H_{1}
-action to the orbits of the
H_{2}
-action.

Given a proper isometric action of
H\subseteq I(M)
on
M
, a complete and injectively immersed submanifold
\Sigma\subseteq M
is called a section of the action if

(i)

\Sigma
meets all orbits of
H
, and

(ii)

For every point
p\in\Sigma
, the tangent spaces
T_{p}(H\cdot p)
and
T_{p}\Sigma
are orthogonal.

The action of
H
is called polar if it admits a section.
It can be shown that if
\Sigma
is a section of the
H
-action,
then
\Sigma
is totally geodesic in
M
and any other section of the action
is congruent to
\Sigma
under an element of
H
.
If one (and thus every) section is flat with the induced metric, then we say that the action of
H
is hyperpolar.

Podestà and Thorbergsson observed in
[
32
, Lemma 1A.4]
that if
H
is a compact Lie group acting polarly on a simply connected symmetric space
M
with singular orbits, then any section of the action
H\curvearrowright M
contains a totally geodesic hypersurface.
In particular, if
M
is a symmetric space of compact type and rank one, then it is shown from this and the fact that any polar action on
M
has at least one singular orbit
[
32
, Lemma 1A.2]
that any section of the action
H\curvearrowright M
has constant sectional curvature.
The argument by Podestà and Thorbergsson also applies in the noncompact case.
This was noted by Kollross when
M
is the Cayley hyperbolic plane
[
28
, Lemma 6.1]
, but the proof carries over to the general case.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
M
be a Riemannian manifold and
H
a Lie group acting nontrivially and polarly on
M
with section
\Sigma
.
If the action of
H
has a singular orbit, then
\Sigma
contains a totally geodesic hypersurface.
In particular, if
M
is a symmetric space of noncompact type and rank one, then
\Sigma
is isometric to
\mathbb{R}\mathsf{H}^{k}(c)
, where
c\in\{1,2\}
.

Proof.

Let
p\in\Sigma
be a point such that
H\cdot p
is singular.
Such a point exists because
\Sigma
meets all orbits.
The slice representation
H_{p}\curvearrowright\nu_{p}(H\cdot p)
defined
by the equation
h\cdot\xi=h_{*p}\xi

is a nontrivial polar representation with section
T_{p}\Sigma
, see
[
3
]
.

We consider the normalizer and centralizer of
T_{p}\Sigma
in
H_{p}
, which are defined respectively as follows:

N_{H_{p}}(T_{p}\Sigma)=\{g\in H_{p}\colon g(T_{p}\Sigma)=T_{p}\Sigma\},\quad Z_{H_{p}}(T_{p}\Sigma)=\{g\in H_{p}\colon g|_{T_{p}\Sigma}=\operatorname{id}_{T_{p}\Sigma}\}.

Then,
N_{H_{p}}(T_{p}\Sigma)
is a closed subgroup of
H_{p}
,
Z_{H_{p}}(T_{p}\Sigma)
is an open normal subgroup of
N_{H_{p}}(T_{p}\Sigma)
, and the quotient
\Pi(T_{p}\Sigma)=N_{H_{p}}(T_{p}\Sigma)/Z_{H_{p}}(T_{p}\Sigma)
is a finite group, known as the polar group of the slice representation
H_{p}\curvearrowright\nu_{p}(H\cdot p)

(see for instance
[
23
]
).
Because the orbit
H\cdot p
is singular, the polar group
\Pi(T_{p}\Sigma)
is nontrivial and generated by reflections along hyperplanes of
T_{p}\Sigma
.

Let
g\in N_{H_{p}}(T_{p}\Sigma)
be an element such that
g_{*p}\in\mathsf{O}(T_{p}\Sigma)
is the reflection along a hyperplane
V\subseteq T_{p}\Sigma
.
Then
g\cdot\Sigma=\Sigma
and the restriction
g\colon\Sigma\to\Sigma
is an involutive isometry.
Denote by
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\Sigma
the connected component of
\operatorname{Fix}(g)
containing
p
.
We have that
\mathcal{H}
is a totally geodesic hypersurface of
\Sigma
whose tangent space at
p
is
V
.

Now, suppose that
M
is a symmetric space of noncompact type and rank one.
From the classification of totally geodesic submanifolds of hyperbolic spaces (see Section
2.3
), we see that the only totally geodesic submanifolds of
M
admitting a totally geodesic hypersurfaces are those of constant curvature, so the second claim follows. ∎

It is worth mentioning that if
H
is a Lie group acting nontrivially, nontransitively and polarly on
\mathbb{C}\mathsf{H}^{n}
, then the section of the
H
-action is totally real and isometric to
\mathbb{R}\mathsf{H}^{k}(2)
, see
[
16
, Proposition 2.4]
.
As far as we are aware, in all known examples of polar actions with singular orbits on
\mathbb{H}\mathsf{H}^{n}
and
\mathbb{O}\mathsf{H}^{2}
the section is a totally geodesic
\mathbb{R}\mathsf{H}^{k}(2)

[
27
,
28
]
.
Therefore, it is natural to conjecture whether the value
c=1
can be omitted from the statement of Lemma
2.1
.
Moreover, a consequence of Theorem
C
is that every polar action without singular orbits on a hyperbolic space
M\neq\mathbb{R}\mathsf{H}^{n}
has a section isometric to
\mathbb{R}\mathsf{H}^{k}(2)
.
This will be proved in Proposition
3.1
.

We say that the action of
H
on
M
induces a foliation if it has no singular orbits.
In the case that
M
is a symmetric space of noncompact type
(or more generally a Hadamard manifold)
it follows from
[
5
, Proposition 2.1]
that all the orbits of
H
are principal,
so all isotropy subgroups are conjugate in
H
.

From
[
5
, Theorem 4.1]
we have the following criterion of polarity (see also
[
20
]
for the first criterion of polarity):

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
M=G/K
be a Riemannian symmetric space of noncompact type with Cartan decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}
.
Let
H\subseteq G
be a closed subgroup acting on
M
in such a way that its orbits form a foliation on
M
.
We define

\mathfrak{h}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp}=\big\{\xi\in\mathfrak{p}:\langle\xi,X\rangle=0,\text{ for all $X\in\mathfrak{h}$}\big\}.

Then:

(i)

The group
H
acts polarly on
M
if and only if
\mathfrak{h}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp}
is a Lie triple system in
\mathfrak{p}
and
[\mathfrak{h}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp},\mathfrak{h}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp}]
is orthogonal to
\mathfrak{h}
.

(ii)

The group
H
acts hyperpolarly on
M
if and only if
\mathfrak{h}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp}
is an abelian subspace of
\mathfrak{p}
.

Moreover, if
H_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp}
denotes the subgroup of
G
whose Lie algebra is
[\mathfrak{h}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp},\mathfrak{h}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp}]\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp}
, then
H_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp}\cdot o
is a section of the action of

on

.

3.
The examples

In this section we study the geometry of the orbits of the subgroups
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{v}}

appearing in Theorem
C
.
In particular, we prove Theorem
C
(
3
).

Let
M=\mathbb{F}\mathsf{H}^{n}=G/K
be a symmetric space of rank one and noncompact type,
where
G
and
K
are chosen according to Table
1
.
We choose a Cartan decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathfrak{g}
, the Lie algebra of
G
,
a
1
-dimensional subspace
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
, and

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{-2\alpha}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2\alpha}

the restricted root space decomposition with respect to
\mathfrak{a}
.
We write
\mathfrak{g}_{0}=\mathfrak{k}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{a}
as usual.
We have a unique simple root
\alpha
and the positive roots are
\alpha,\,2\alpha
(except then
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
, where
2\alpha
is not a root).

Let
\mathfrak{b}
a subspace of
\mathfrak{a}
, and
\mathfrak{v}
a totally real subspace of
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
.
We define the vector subspace
\mathfrak{s}_{\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{v}}=(\mathfrak{a}\ominus\mathfrak{b})\oplus(\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\ominus\mathfrak{v})\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2\alpha}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{n}
,
which is a Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{n}
,
and we let
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{v}}
the connected subgroup of
G
whose Lie algebra is
\mathfrak{s}_{\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{v}}
.
Because the Lie exponential map
\operatorname{Exp}\colon\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{n}\to AN
is a diffeomorphism, we have that

\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{v}}=\operatorname{Exp}(\mathfrak{s}_{\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{v}})
is a closed subgroup of
AN
(and thus of
G
).

Our first task is to prove Theorem
C
(
1
).

Proposition 3.1
.

The group
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{v}}
acts polarly on
M
inducing a foliation.
In addition,
if and
k=\dim\mathfrak{b}+\dim\mathfrak{v}\geq 2
, then
every section of the action of
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{v}}
is isometric to
\mathbb{R}\mathsf{H}^{k}(2)
.

Proof.

Since
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{v}}
is a subgroup of
AN
,
and the action of
AN
on
M
is simply transitive,
it is clear that
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{v}}
induces a foliation on
M
.
We just have to use the criterion of polarity given in Proposition
2.2
to check that
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{v}}
acts polarly on
M
.

We have
(\mathfrak{s}_{\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{v}})_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp}=\mathfrak{b}\oplus(1-\theta)\mathfrak{v}
.
The fact that
\mathfrak{v}
is totally real in
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
implies that

(\mathfrak{s}_{\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{v}})_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp}
is a Lie triple system in
\mathfrak{p}
and
[\mathfrak{v},\mathfrak{v}]=0
.
This leads to

[(\mathfrak{s}_{\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{v}})_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp},(\mathfrak{s}_{\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{v}})_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp}]\subseteq(1+\theta)(\mathfrak{v}\oplus[\theta\mathfrak{v},\mathfrak{v}])\subseteq(1+\theta)\mathfrak{v}\oplus\mathfrak{k}_{0}
, which is orthogonal to
\mathfrak{s}_{\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{v}}
, as we wanted to check.

We now prove the second assertion.
In fact, because
(1-\theta)\mathfrak{v}
is contained in
\mathfrak{a}\oplus(1-\theta)\mathfrak{v}
, it suffices to show that
\mathfrak{a}\oplus(1-\theta)\mathfrak{v}
is the Lie triple system corresponding to a real hyperbolic space
\mathbb{R}\mathsf{H}^{m}(2)
, where
m=1+\dim\mathfrak{v}\geq 2
.
Let
\Sigma
be the unique totally geodesic submanifold of
M
with
o\in\Sigma
and
T_{o}\Sigma=\mathfrak{a}\oplus(1-\theta)\mathfrak{v}
.
Then
\Sigma
is automatically a simply connected symmetric space of rank one,
so we can determine it by choosing any unit vector in
T\Sigma
and computing its Jacobi operator.
For instance, if we take
B\in T_{o}\Sigma
it is straightforward to check that

R_{B}\xi=-\operatorname{ad}(B)^{2}\xi=\begin{cases}0,&\xi\in\mathfrak{a},\\
-\frac{1}{4}\xi,&\xi\in(1-\theta)\mathfrak{v}.\end{cases}

By looking at (
1
), it is clear that
R_{B}
is the Jacobi operator of a real hyperbolic space whose curvature is equal to
-\frac{1}{4}
, so necessarily
\Sigma=\mathbb{R}\mathsf{H}^{m}(2)
, as desired.
∎

Next we deal with the congruence problem of the actions under investigation.
We first need

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{v}
be an abelian subspace of
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
and
\xi\in\mathfrak{v}
.
Then, there exists
k\in K_{0}
such that
\operatorname{Ad}(k)\mathfrak{v}=\mathfrak{v}
and
\operatorname{Ad}(k)(\xi)=-\xi
.

Proof.

Recall that
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\cong\mathbb{F}^{n-1}
as an
\mathbb{F}
-module,
and that
K_{0}
acts on
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
as its standard representation if
\mathbb{F}\in\{\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C},\mathbb{H}\}
,
or as the unique spin representation
\mathsf{Spin}(7)\to\mathsf{SO}(8)
if
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{O}
(see Table
1
).

If
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{O}
, then
\mathfrak{v}
is
1
-dimensional and

\mathsf{Spin}(7)
acts transitively on the unit sphere of
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}

[
11
]
,
so we may assume
\mathbb{F}\in\{\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C},\mathbb{H}\}
.
By linearity we may also suppose that
\lvert\xi\rvert=1
.
Let
\{\xi,e_{2},\dots,e_{k}\}
be an orthonormal basis of
\mathfrak{v}
.
Since
\mathfrak{v}
is abelian, it is also totally real in
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
.
Thus, the elements of this basis are not only orthonormal, but
\mathbb{F}
-orthonormal.
We complete this basis to an
\mathbb{F}
-orthonormal basis

\{\xi,e_{2},\dots,e_{k},e_{k+1},\dots,e_{n-1}\}
of
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
.
In particular,
\{-\xi,e_{2},\dots,e_{n-1}\}
is another
\mathbb{F}
-orthonormal basis of
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
.
Since
K_{0}
acts transitively on the set of all ordered
\mathbb{F}
-orthonormal bases of
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
,
we deduce that there exists
k\in K_{0}
such that
\operatorname{Ad}(k)\xi=-\xi
and
\operatorname{Ad}(k)e_{i}=e_{i}
for all
i
.
Because of this,
\operatorname{Ad}(k)\mathfrak{v}=\mathfrak{v}
as well, so the result is proved. ∎

The proof of Theorem
C
(
3
) follows readily from the next result.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{b}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{b}_{2}
be subspaces of
\mathfrak{a}
,
and
\mathfrak{v}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{v}_{2}
totally real subspaces of
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
.
Then, the actions of
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{b}_{1},\mathfrak{v}_{1}}
and
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{b}_{2},\mathfrak{v}_{2}}
on
\mathbb{F}\mathsf{H}^{n}

are orbit equivalent
if and only if
\dim\mathfrak{b}_{1}=\dim\mathfrak{b}_{2}
and
\dim\mathfrak{v}_{1}=\dim\mathfrak{v}_{2}
.

Proof.

Since the group
K_{0}
acts transitively on abelian subspaces of the same dimension of
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}

(see for example
[
18
, Lemma 3.2]
),

\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{b}_{1},\mathfrak{v}_{1}}
and
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{b}_{2},\mathfrak{v}_{2}}
are conjugate by an element of
K_{0}
if

\dim\mathfrak{b}_{1}=\dim\mathfrak{b}_{2}
and
\dim\mathfrak{v}_{1}=\dim\mathfrak{v}_{2}
.
In particular, their actions are orbit equivalent.

For the converse, let
\mathcal{S}_{\xi}
denote the shape operator of a subgroup
S
of
AN

with respect to a normal vector
\xi
.
Using the Koszul formula for left-invariant vectors of
AN
, it is easy to see that
the shape operator satisfies

\langle\mathcal{S}_{\xi}X,X\rangle_{AN}=-\langle X,[X,\xi]\rangle_{AN}=-\frac{1}{2}\langle X,[X,\xi]\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\langle[\theta X,X],\xi\rangle

for all
X\in\mathfrak{s}
and
\xi\in(\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{n})\ominus\mathfrak{s}
.

If
\mathfrak{b}_{i}=0
we consider the orbit through the origin
\mathsf{S}_{0,\mathfrak{v}_{i}}\cdot o
.
As a submanifold of
AN
, its normal space at
o
can be identified with
\mathfrak{v}_{i}
.
Let
\xi\in\mathfrak{v}_{i}
.
By Lemma
3.2
there exists
k\in K_{0}
such that
\operatorname{Ad}(k)
leaves
\mathfrak{v}_{i}
invariant
and maps
\xi
to
-\xi
.
In particular,
\operatorname{Ad}(k)
leaves
\mathfrak{s}_{0,\mathfrak{v}_{i}}
invariant,
so
k
maps
\mathsf{S}_{0,\mathfrak{v}_{i}}\cdot o
to itself
sending
\xi
to
-\xi
.
Since
k
is an extrinsic isometry,

-\mathcal{S}_{\xi}=\mathcal{S}_{k_{*}\xi}=k_{*}\mathcal{S}_{\xi}k_{*}^{-1}
,
which implies
\operatorname{tr}\mathcal{S}_{\xi}=0
.
As
\xi\in\mathfrak{v}_{i}
is arbitrary,
we conclude that
\mathsf{S}_{0,\mathfrak{v}_{i}}\cdot o
is minimal.

If
\mathfrak{b}_{i}=\mathfrak{a}
then
\mathfrak{s}_{\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{v}_{i}}\subseteq\mathfrak{n}
is a nilpotent ideal of
\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{n}
.
Thus,
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{v}_{i}}
is a nilpotent normal subgroup of
AN
.
This implies that for each
g\in AN
,

\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{v}_{i}}\cdot g(o)=gg^{-1}\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{v}_{i}}g\cdot o=g(\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{v}_{i}}\cdot o)
,
so any two orbits of
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{v}_{i}}
are isometrically congruent to each other.
Hence, one orbit is minimal if and only if all of them are.
We calculate the mean curvature vector of
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{v}_{i}}\cdot o
at
o
.
Note that the normal space of
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{v}_{i}}\cdot o
at
o
can be identified with

\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{v}_{i}
.
If
\xi\in\mathfrak{v}_{i}
, the same argument as above implies
\operatorname{tr}\mathcal{S}_{\xi}=0
,
so it suffices to calculate
\mathcal{S}_{B}
.
If
U\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\ominus\mathfrak{v}_{i}
we get

\langle\mathcal{S}_{B}U,U\rangle_{AN}=\frac{1}{2}\langle[\theta U,U],B\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\langle\lvert U\rvert^{2}H_{\alpha},B\rangle=\frac{1}{4}\lvert U\rvert^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\lvert U\rvert_{AN}^{2}.

Similarly,
\langle\mathcal{S}_{B}X,X\rangle_{AN}=\lvert X\rvert_{AN}^{2}
for
X\in\mathfrak{g}_{2\alpha}
.
Altogether this implies that the mean curvature vector of
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{v}_{i}}\cdot o
at
o
is

\frac{1}{2}(\dim\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}-\dim\mathfrak{v}+2\dim\mathfrak{g}_{2\alpha})B,

which is nonzero.

Therefore, if the actions of
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{b}_{1},\mathfrak{v}_{1}}
and
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{b}_{2},\mathfrak{v}_{2}}
are orbit equivalent
we must have
\mathfrak{b}_{1}=\mathfrak{b}_{2}
.
For dimension reasons we also need
\dim\mathfrak{v}_{1}=\dim\mathfrak{v}_{2}
, as claimed.
∎

4.
General results

From now on, our aim is to show that any homogeneous polar foliation on a rank one symmetric space of noncompact type is induced by the action of a group whose action is orbit equivalent to one of the examples given in Theorem
C
(
1
).
We follow the notation from Section
3
.

We assume that
H
is a closed subgroup of
G
that acts polarly on
M
inducing a foliation.
The action of
H
is assumed to be nontrivial and nontransitive.

A Borel subalgebra of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is a maximal solvable subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
.
Borel subalgebras of semisimple Lie algebras have been described in
[
30
]
.

If
\mathfrak{g}
is the Lie algebra of a symmetric space of rank one and noncompact type,
then the description of its Borel subalgebras is relatively easy.
In this case there exists a Cartan decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}
and we have two possibilities:
either it is maximally compact, that is, a maximal abelian subspace of
\mathfrak{k}
;
or maximally noncompact, that is,
\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{n}
,
where
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{n}
is an Iwasawa decomposition and
\mathfrak{t}
is a maximal abelian subspace of

\mathfrak{k}_{0}=\mathfrak{k}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{0}
.

Borel subalgebras are relevant in this context because of the following result
[
18
, Proposition 4.2]
:

Proposition 4.1
.

The leaves of a homogeneous polar foliation on a symmetric space of noncompact type
M=G/K
coincide, up to isometric congruence, with the orbits of a connected closed solvable subgroup
S
of
G
whose Lie algebra
\mathfrak{s}
is contained in a maximally noncompact Borel subalgebra of the form
\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{n}
, where
\mathfrak{t}\subseteq\mathfrak{k}_{0}
is abelian.

According to Proposition
4.1
above, we may assume from now on that, up to orbit equivalence, the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
of
H
is contained in a maximally noncompact subalgebra of the form
\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{n}
, with
\mathfrak{t}\subseteq\mathfrak{k}_{0}
an abelian subspace.

As a matter of notation, if
\mathfrak{v}
and
\mathfrak{w}
are vector subspaces of
\mathfrak{g}
,
by
\mathfrak{v}_{\mathfrak{w}}
we mean the orthogonal projection of
\mathfrak{v}
onto
\mathfrak{w}
.

We recall the result
[
18
, Lemma 4.3]
, whose short proof we include here for completeness.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{q}
be a Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{n}

and
\lambda\in\{\alpha,2\alpha\}
.
If
\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}_{\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{n}}
,
then
\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}
.

Proof.

Recall that
\mathfrak{q}_{\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{n}}
denotes the orthogonal projection of
\mathfrak{q}
onto
\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{n}
.
Take
X\in\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}_{\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{n}}
.
Then there exist vectors
S
,
T\in\mathfrak{t}
such that
S+B
,
T+X\in\mathfrak{q}
.
In particular,
\operatorname{ad}(S)X+\lambda(B)X=[S+B,T+X]\in\mathfrak{q}
.
This means that the linear map
\operatorname{ad}(S)+\lambda(B)\operatorname{id}_{\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}}
preserves
\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}

and carries
\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}
to
\mathfrak{q}
.
Since
S\in\mathfrak{t}
, the linear transformation
\operatorname{ad}(S)
is skew-adjoint, so
\operatorname{ad}(S)+\lambda(B)\operatorname{id}_{\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}}
is a linear isomorphism and it follows that
\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}
.
∎

Following the notation from Proposition
2.2
,
we let
\Sigma\subseteq M
be the section through
o
of the action, and let
\mathfrak{h}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp}
be its tangent space at
o
.
Before continuing, we prove a result that will be used several times.

Lemma 4.3
.

Consider the following conditions for a subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{n}
:

(i)

There exists
\xi_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
such that
B+(1-\theta)\xi_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{h}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp}
.

(ii)

Either
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{h}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp}
or
\mathfrak{p}_{\alpha}\cap\mathfrak{h}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp}\neq 0
.

If
\mathfrak{h}
satisfies
(
i
)
, then there exists
g\in N

such that
\operatorname{Ad}(g)\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{n}
satisfies
(
ii
)
.
Conversely, if
\mathfrak{h}
satisfies
(
ii
)
, then
\mathfrak{h}
is conjugate
under
N
to a subalgebra of
\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{n}
satisfying
(
i
)
.

Note that, in particular, conditions (
i
)
and (
ii
) are equivalent up to orbit equivalence.

Proof.

Assume that there exists
\xi_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
such that
B+(1-\theta)\xi_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{h}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp}
.
If
\xi_{\alpha}=0
, this clearly implies that
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{h}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp}
and it suffices to take
g
to be the identity element.
Assume
\xi_{\alpha}\neq 0
.
Then we define
g=\operatorname{Exp}(-2\xi_{\alpha}/\lvert\xi_{\alpha}\rvert^{2})\in N
and consider the conjugate subgroup

\hat{H}=gHg^{-1}\subseteq{G}
.
The Lie algebra

\hat{\mathfrak{h}}=\operatorname{Ad}(g)\mathfrak{h}
of
\hat{H}
is orthogonal to

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ad}(g^{-1})^{*}(B+\xi_{\alpha})={}

\displaystyle e^{\frac{2}{\lvert\xi_{\alpha}\rvert^{2}}\operatorname{ad}(\xi_{\alpha})^{*}}(B+\xi_{\alpha})=e^{-\frac{2}{\lvert\xi_{\alpha}\rvert^{2}}\operatorname{ad}(\theta\xi_{\alpha})}(B+\xi_{\alpha})

\displaystyle\equiv{}

\displaystyle B+\xi_{\alpha}-\frac{2\lvert\xi_{\alpha}\rvert^{2}}{\lvert\xi_{\alpha}\rvert^{2}}H_{\alpha}=\xi_{\alpha}\pmod{\theta\mathfrak{n}}.

Since
\theta\mathfrak{n}
is already orthogonal to
\operatorname{Ad}(g)\mathfrak{h}
, we obtain that
\xi_{\alpha}
is perpendicular to
\operatorname{Ad}(g)\mathfrak{h}
.
Hence
(1-\theta)\xi_{\alpha}\in\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp}
and
\mathfrak{p}_{\alpha}\cap\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp}\neq 0
.

Conversely, if
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{h}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp}
, then
B\in\mathfrak{h}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp}
and the result follows trivially.
Assume
\mathfrak{p}_{\alpha}\cap\mathfrak{h}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp}\neq 0
.
Then, there is a nonzero vector
\xi_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
such that
(1-\theta)\xi_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{h}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp}
.
We define
g=\operatorname{Exp}(2\xi_{\alpha}/\lvert\xi_{\alpha}\rvert^{2})\in N
and consider the conjugate subgroup
\hat{H}=g{H}g^{-1}\subseteq{G}
.
A calculation similar to the one above shows that
B+(1-\theta)\xi_{\alpha}\in\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp}
and the result follows.
∎

Now we show that indeed one of these two equivalent conditions holds.
This could have been obtained as a consequence of
[
18
, Lemma 4.4]
,
but we can simplify the proof in our case.

Lemma 4.4
.

The action of
H
is orbit equivalent to the action of a group
\hat{H}
whose Lie algebra is contained in
\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{n}
and satisfying the following condition: there is
\xi_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
such that
B+(1-\theta)\xi_{\alpha}\in\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp}
.

Proof.

We define the vector subspace
\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}=\mathfrak{h}+\mathfrak{g}_{2\alpha}
, which is also a Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Assume first that
\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}_{\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{n}}=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{n}
.
According to Lemma
4.2
, we get
\mathfrak{n}\subseteq\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}
.
We show that
\mathfrak{n}\subseteq\mathfrak{h}
.
Let
X\in\mathfrak{g}_{2\alpha}
.
We can write
X=[U,V]
, with
U
,
V\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
.
Since
\mathfrak{n}\subseteq\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}=\mathfrak{h}+\mathfrak{g}_{2\alpha}
,
there exist
Y
,
Z\in\mathfrak{g}_{2\alpha}
such that
U+Y
,
V+Z\in\mathfrak{h}
.
But then,
X=[U,V]=[U+Y,V+Z]\in\mathfrak{h}
and it follows that
\mathfrak{g}_{2\alpha}\subseteq\mathfrak{h}
.
This readily implies
\mathfrak{n}\subseteq\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}=\mathfrak{h}+\mathfrak{g}_{2\alpha}=\mathfrak{h}
,
which yields
\mathfrak{h}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}
.
Since the action of
H
is not transitive, this gives
\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{h}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp}

and the result follows in this case.

Assume now that
\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}_{\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{n}}\neq\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{n}
.
Thus, there exists a nonzero vector in
\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{n}
that is orthogonal to both
\mathfrak{h}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2\alpha}
.
We may write this vector as
\xi_{0}+\xi_{\alpha}
, where
\xi_{0}\in\mathfrak{a}
and
\xi_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
, and because
\xi_{0}+\xi_{\alpha}
and
\theta\mathfrak{n}
are both orthogonal to
\mathfrak{h}
, we obtain that
\xi_{0}+(1-\theta)\xi_{\alpha}
is orthogonal to
\mathfrak{h}
.
If
\xi_{0}\neq 0
we are done after normalization.
If
\xi_{0}=0
, we simply apply Lemma
4.3
to finish.
∎

We end this section by recalling
[
18
, Proposition 4.8]
adapted to our particular case:

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
H
be a connected closed subgroup of
G
inducing a homogeneous polar foliation on a rank one symmetric space of noncompact type
M
.
Assume that its Lie algebra is contained in a maximally noncompact Borel subalgebra
\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{n}
,
and
\mathfrak{h}_{\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{n}}=\mathfrak{z}\oplus(\mathfrak{n}\ominus\mathfrak{v}_{\alpha})
, where
\mathfrak{v}_{\alpha}
is an abelian subspace of
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
, and
\mathfrak{z}
is a subspace of
\mathfrak{a}
.
Let
\tilde{H}
be the connected Lie subgroup of

Although the results presented in this section are valid for all hyperbolic spaces,

5.
Polar foliations on quaternionic hyperbolic spaces

Let
H
be a Lie group acting polarly on
\mathbb{H}\mathsf{H}^{n}
(where

) inducing a foliation.
It follows from Proposition

that we may assume (up to orbit equivalence) that

is solvable and its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
is contained in a maximally noncompact subalgebra of the form
\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{n}
, where
\mathfrak{t}\subseteq\mathfrak{k}_{0}
is abelian.

Proposition 5.1
.

Suppose
H
is a closed connected subgroup of
\mathsf{Sp}(1,n)
acting polarly on
M=\mathbb{H}\mathsf{H}^{n}
in such a way that its orbits form a homogeneous foliation.
Let
\Sigma
be a section of the action.
Then, either
\Sigma
is totally real or the action of
H
is trivial.

Proof.

First we show that
\Sigma
is a space of constant curvature.
From the classification of totally geodesic submanifolds in
\mathbb{H}\mathsf{H}^{n}

(see Subsection
2.3
),
it suffices to show that
\Sigma
is not isometric to a complex or quaternionic hyperbolic space.

Firstly, let us suppose that
\Sigma=\mathbb{H}\mathsf{H}^{k}
for some
k\in\{2,\dots,n\}
.
This means that
\Sigma
is a quaternionic submanifold of
M
.
In addition, if
p\in\Sigma
is any point, the subspace
T_{p}(H\cdot p)=\nu_{p}\Sigma
is also quaternionic,
so we see that the orbits of the action
H\curvearrowright M
are quaternionic.
Because a quaternionic submanifold of a quaternionic Kähler manifold is totally geodesic
[
22
, Theorem 5]
, we deduce that
H\cdot p
is a totally geodesic submanifold of
M
for all
p\in M
.
If the action of
H
is not transitive,
then any two distinct orbits
H\cdot p
and
H\cdot q
are totally geodesic (hence minimal), and
[
1

guarantees that both
H\cdot p
and
H\cdot q
consist of one point.
Since
p
and
q
are arbitrary, we conclude that the action of
H
is trivial.

Now, assume that
\Sigma=\mathbb{C}\mathsf{H}^{k}
for some
2\leq k\leq n
.
Additionally, if the action of
H
is not transitive, by lemmas
4.3
and
4.4
,
we can assume, up to orbit equivalence,
that
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{h}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp}
or
\mathfrak{p}_{\alpha}\cap\mathfrak{h}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp}\neq 0

Suppose
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{h}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp}
.
Then the restriction of the Jacobi operator
R_{B}
to
\mathfrak{h}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp}
has the same spectral decomposition as that of the Jacobi operator of a unit vector in
T\mathbb{C}\mathsf{H}^{k}
.
In other words, there exists a nonzero vector
X\in\mathfrak{g}_{2\alpha}
and a subspace
\mathfrak{v}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
such that
\mathfrak{h}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp}=\mathfrak{a}\oplus(1-\theta)\mathfrak{v}\oplus\mathbb{R}(1-\theta)X

As the action of
H
is polar,
we have for every

that

is orthogonal to
\mathfrak{h}
.
This implies that
\mathfrak{v}
is invariant under
J_{X}
,
and so is the orthogonal complement
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\ominus\mathfrak{v}
.
As the dimension of

is at most

\mathfrak{v}\neq\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
.
Choose a nonzero vector

\mathfrak{v}
.
We have

,

\mathfrak{h}
.

Now assume
\mathfrak{h}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp}\cap\mathfrak{p}_{\alpha}\neq 0
.
Thus, we consider a vector

and

R_{(1-\theta)\xi_{\alpha}}

\mathfrak{h}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp}=\mathbb{R}(1-\theta)\xi_{\alpha}\oplus\mathbb{R}(1-\theta)J_{Z}\xi_{\alpha}\oplus\mathfrak{w}

where

and

\displaystyle{}[J_{X}\xi_{\alpha},J_{Y}\xi_{\alpha}]

is a space of constant curvature.
Assume that

Lemma 5.2

\mathfrak{h}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{2\alpha}\neq 0

tH_{\alpha}+(1-\theta)X\in\mathfrak{w}
, where
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
X\in\mathfrak{g}_{2\alpha}
.
Recall that
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\ominus(\mathbb{R}\xi_{\alpha}\oplus\mathfrak{v})\subseteq\mathfrak{h}_{\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{n}}
is invariant under
J_{X}
, so we deduce that
V
,
J_{X}V\in\mathfrak{h}_{\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{n}}
.
As a consequence, we may select vectors
T
,
T^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{t}
such that
T+V
,
T^{\prime}+J_{X}V\in\mathfrak{h}
.
Therefore, we have
[T,J_{X}V]+[V,T^{\prime}]+\tfrac{1}{2}\lvert V\rvert^{2}X=[T+V,T^{\prime}+J_{X}V]\in\mathfrak{h}
, and we obtain

0=\Bigl<[T,J_{X}V]+[V,T^{\prime}]+\frac{1}{2}\lvert V\rvert^{2}X,tH_{\alpha}+(1-\theta)X\Bigr>=\frac{1}{2}\lvert V\rvert^{2}\lvert X\rvert^{2},

thus forcing
X=0
.
This proves the desired assertion.∎

Because
\mathfrak{w}
is contained in the one-dimensional space
\mathfrak{a}
, we see that either
\mathfrak{w}=\mathfrak{a}
or
\mathfrak{w}=0
.
The case
\mathfrak{w}=\mathfrak{a}
was already dealt with in Subsection
6.1
,
so we may assume directly that
\mathfrak{w}=0
, giving
\mathfrak{h}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp}=(1-\theta)(\mathbb{R}\xi_{\alpha}\oplus\mathfrak{v})\subseteq\mathfrak{p}_{\alpha}
.
Lemma
6.2
then implies that

\dim\mathfrak{h}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp}=1
, so
\mathfrak{v}=0
and

\mathfrak{h}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp}=\mathbb{R}(1-\theta)\xi_{\alpha}
.
We therefore conclude with:

Proposition 6.4
.

If
\mathfrak{h}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp}\cap\mathfrak{p}_{\alpha}\neq 0
,
then
\mathfrak{h}_{\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{n}}=(\mathfrak{a}\ominus\mathfrak{b})\oplus(\mathfrak{n}\ominus\ell)
,
where
\mathfrak{b}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}
is a vector subspace and
\ell\subseteq\mathfrak{p}_{\alpha}
is a line.

6.3.
End of proof
