Executive Summary

This ﬁnal technical report summarizes the key accomplishments on the unfolding using
diGusion model project, a DOE award received by PI Pierre-Hugues Beauchemin at Tufts
University. This project main goal was to investigate the potential of diGusion models for
unfolding experimental High Energy Physics data from detector eGects while controlling
systematics uncertainties. The project accomplished its goals by completing the following
objectives:

 1) Performing an object-by-object, event-by-event unfolding of various kinematic
 distributions reconstructed from detector data in HEP in a way that keeps
 correlations between unfolded observables while demonstrating competitive
 performance compared to standard algorithms used in the ﬁeld;

 2) Address the generalization problem by developing an unfolding algorithm capable to
 correctly infer the underlying distributions of observables and processes never seen
 before, while controlling the dominant theoretical uncertainties aGecting the
 process, therefore increasing the eGectiveness, the precision, and the applicability
 of the developed algorithm;

 3) Understand the theoretical foundations between the developed algorithm so to
 extend it to applications beyond experimental HEP, for broader beneﬁts to the
 society.

This report provides an overview of the accomplishments related to each of these key
objectives.

Overview of Research Accomplishments

Major goals of the project

The main objectives of the proposed research were to overcome two core challenges in the
analysis of data from HEP experiments:
 A) Improving the precision and accuracy of unfolding techniques using discrete-time
 diffusion and score-matching models;
 B) Providing a means for efficient and robust systematic uncertainty estimation.
With this project, we aim at providing both theoretical foundations, and experimental
accuracy on the solutions to be developed. Our project aimed at providing applications to
both the Energy Frontier and the Intensity Frontier unfolding problems.

Solution Proposed: Unfolding using DiGusion Models

Current strategies used in Experimental HEP for unfolding data from detector eGects begin
by ﬁrst binning measured observables into a histogram and then to use simulation data to
provide a prior on the probability that the number of measured events in a given bin of that
distribution comes from a given bin of the truth underlying distribution. Such approach
suGers from many shortcomings including: large variance, unless some level of bias is
introduced, lost of any physics correlation between individually unfolded distribution,
impeding on a holistic interpretation of the results, and inﬂation of the experimental and
theoretical systematic uncertainties aGecting the simulation data used in the process.

Our approach consists in using DiGusion-based models that, like other generative methods,
are designed to map an initial, known noise distribution (e.g. normal) to a target data
distribution (e.g. images, text). With diGusion models, this map is conceptualized as
reversing, or "de-noising", a process where larger and larger (Gaussian) noise is added to the
data. It has been shown that learning the denoising process can be realized by training a
neural network to estimate the target distribution’s score function, the gradient of the log
density with respect to the data. The idea behind our approach to unfolding is therefore to
train diGusion models to estimate the probability of a true underlying quantity y given the
observed quantity x, i.e. L(y|x) = p(y|x), using simulated data. This method would have the
advantage of modeling directly the likelihood, something that is only approximated by other
implicit or explicit function estimate. It should also have the advantage of avoiding the need
to train and calibrate auxiliary classiﬁers, as well as avoiding mode collapse and sensitivity
to hyper-parameters during training. We therefore believed that these diGusion-based

methods can deal with the higher dimensional latent space for reliable and robust unfolding
analysis of HEP data, and that’s why we based our research objectives on such approach.

To meet this global objective, our project therefore proposes to:

 1- Deﬁne performance reference points to aim at with a new 1D DiGusion Model
 unfolding using traditional unfolding methods in a set of benchmark tests.

 2- Demonstrate that the same quantity can be unfolded, event-by-event, without
 introducing bias;

 3- Demonstrate that our diGusion model can generate the conditional distribution,
      p(y|x), we need to perform unfolding;

 4- Testing the unfolding algorithm on concrete examples taken from Frontier data
 analyses.

Solution Proposed: Capturing Systematic Uncertainties

There are at least two diGerent types of uncertainties to investigate with respect to diGusion-
based models: those related to the input models used in the training phase and those
derived from sampling from the trained model. For input uncertainties, because the diGusion
models used to unfold are equivalent at estimating and minimizing a likelihood function over
a high-dimensional space, extending the conditional density p(y|x) to include additional
parameters, z, related to detector or physics model uncertainties, and have the network
learn the density’s dependence on these parameters, p(y, z|x), would amount at controlling
some of these systematics. We proposed to tackle this by conditioning the diGusion model
on a set of distribution moments from a set of distributions covering the theory modeling
uncertainties. Tackling the experimental uncertainties requires details of some detectors,
beyond the scope of this project.

The second source of uncertainty in estimating the unfolding is inherent due to sampling as
well as due to potentially sup-optimal parameterizations of the stochastic dynamics and
length scale of diGusion time. While this is an active area in the ﬁeld, connections can be
made to optimal transport and control theory that allow us to quantify and optimize the
tradeoG. The goal would then be to evaluate if this could be used to severely reduce the
resource usage requires to estimate sampling uncertainties.

What was accomplished under these goals?

The Conditional Denoising DiGusion Probabilistic Model (cDDPM) for Unfolding

As a ﬁrst step, we developed and validated a set of reference samples to be used to
benchmark the development of our unfolding algorithm. We used W+jets events generated
by the Pythia generator with realistic toy models for simulating detector eGects. We then
obtained a set of performance benchmark comparisons by using various instances of
traditional unfolding methods to unfold our test samples, and quantify their performance,
but also to understand their ﬂaws. Together with the systematic uncertainties estimated in
published W+jets measurements by the ATLAS Collaboration, the performance of the
traditional unfolding methods provided us with a set of comparatives allowing us to evaluate
the performance of our algorithm.

We then implemented a standard Denoising DiGusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM) in our
framework and used this to show that the 1D binned unfolding of a set of observables
typically measured in W+jets analyses could be done with similar performance than the
traditional unfolding methods. We then started to investigate the possibility of adapting
DDPM to perform object-based event-by-event unfolding of our benchmark data, where
every single object (jets or electrons) of every event would be unfolded, an extension over
our planed objectives. Our preliminary results were encouraging, suggesting that we could
possibly outperformed traditional methods and other ML-based unfolding approaches.

To tackle the bias problem, i.e. to get an unfolding algorithm that is not biased toward the
distributions used in the training phase, we trained the algorithm only using ﬂat distributions
of detector modelled data, i.e. fully agnostic of the underlying theoretical prior. The idea was
that if the training does not depend at all on the physics of the prior distributions used for
training, then there cannot be any bias toward such prior in the unfolding algorithm that we
propose. The results of such unfolding is presented in Fig. 1 below, where we can see that a
model of physically meaningful distribution at detector level (green), generated by a targeted
true distribution (blue), could be properly unfolded (orange agrees well with blue, i.e. their
ratio is essentially 1.0) from a DDPM unfolding algorithm trained on a ﬂat detector-level
distribution (purple) generated by a true distribution slightly tweaked from ﬂat so that the
detector data get ﬂat (pink).

 Fig. 1

The results are quite astonishing: over the test samples, no dependence on the prior was
observed, i.e. that the modeling of how the kinematics variables map from true to
reconstructed levels is suGicient to unfold, without information about how these
observables distribute (i.e. their probability density function).

Building on this result, we investigated the potential for using our DDPM algorithm to solve
an even more complicated problem: the generalization problem, i.e. the development of an
unfolding algorithm that could be used to unfold any distribution regardless of the physics it
contains. The idea would therefore be to develop a universal unfolding algorithm that would
not only be unbiased with respect to prior distributions, but would apply to any data
distribution regardless of what was used for training, while keeping the correlations between
the unfolded distributions and not relying on any bias. This is much more ambitious than our
original goal. It however rapidly became clear that a trade-oG between our extreme approach
to the solution to the bias problem (not using any physically relevant prior information in the
training phase), and our aim at developing a universal unfolding algorithm, had to be adopted
since these two objectives clash. To generalize on training data so to be able to anticipate
unfolding of unseen distributions requires inductive bias, i.e. using prior information about
what the outcome of the unfolding could be so to accomplish the task. This is at the core of
generative AI models.

Our approach at tackling this dichotomy between bias and generality therefore consisted in
using a broad range of prior information about distributions to be unfolded and let the

unfolding algorithm to interpolate and extrapolate between the training sets so to unfold a
very large set of possible distributions, including unseen ones, without favoring any of the
distributions used in the training sets. That would, at the same time, work toward solving the
uncertainty aspect of the project. To do this, we implemented the three following ideas:

 1. Conditioning the noise sampling of the diGusion model on the detector distribution;

 2. Using moments of detector distributions in the training set;

 3. Training with a large enough set of processes and theoretical predictions to delineate
 a space of outcomes the unfolding algorithm could easily extrapolate to, starting from
 unseen data distributions.

The ﬁrst element of our solution led to the development of our conditional DDPM (cDDPM),
a variant of the models already available on the market where rather than restricting the
weight of the condition on the training phase, we force an optimal account of this condition.
This allows us to sample from the conditional probability P(x|y) without explicitly evaluating
the prior distribution over the data space. Incorporating the distributional moments in the
training maps between the input 𝑥⃗ to the detector and its output 𝑦⃗ allows enhancing the
model inductive bias with minimal assumptions toward the actual prior distribution,
enabling the interpolating and extrapolating capabilities of our algorithm to unfold unseen
distributions, while limiting the bias. Finally, using a large set of processes, both already
observed (e.g. W+jets) and completely hypothetical (e.g. a speciﬁc dark matter model), and
adding a set of diGerent theoretical predictions for the known process, allow providing a
larger number of rigid references from which the above interpolation and extrapolation could
be achieved, including navigating the systematic space, by treating systematic variations of
a given distributions as extra training data to extrapolate/interpolate from. An example of
result obtained with our generalized cDDPM unfolder is presented in Fig. 2 below. We can
see that our generalized unfolder (applied on a top-pair test sample, when no top-pair
sample is used in the training) is performing equally well as a dedicated unfolder where both
the training and the test sets include top-pair samples, although not necessarily the same.

 Fig. 2

With our approach, we also demonstrated that we could unfold a plethora of new physics
model, without bias (or at least well within our uncertainty budget) while using only SM
processes in the training phase. That can signiﬁcantly impact the precision and the
portability of limits obtained in searches for new physics at collider experiments.

Not only the algorithm that we developed addresses the problem of generalization while
providing a solution to the problem of bias, but we also demonstrated that our other original
objective of unfolding on objects so to preserve correlations between unfolded distributions
has also been attained: calculating the invariant distribution of jets from the unfolded data
and comparing it from the true jet mass distribution and the unfolded jet mass distributions
yielded consistent results, as demonstrated in Fig.3 below.

Our eGort at developing the unfolding algorithm aimed at the inception of this project took
an unexpected turn where we managed to produce a much more powerful unfolding
algorithm than what was originally anticipated, something that can rapidly become a
reference in the ﬁeld. This is particularly true given that, so far, from the data that we used in
the process, our algorithm is extremely fast, and resource-light, a pragmatic value that
contrast with most other ML-based unfolding algorithms on the market.

Toward Theoretical Understanding: The Ensemble Inverse Problem

As an attempt to generalize and provide a better theoretical understanding of the cDDPM
algorithm used to unfold data distributions in High Energy Physics (HEP), we generalized the
unfolding problem to a new class of multivariate statistical problems that we referred to as
the Ensemble Inverse Problem (EIP). The aim of introducing EIP was to be able to reach the
target generalization of the cDDPM by inverting for an ensemble that is distributed according
to the pushforward of a prior under a forward process. Such generalization opens up the
space of applications: the EIP also arises, for example, in full waveform inversion (FWI), and
inverse imaging with unknown priors.

To tackle the EIP, we considered noniterative inference-time methods that construct
posterior samplers based on a new class of conditional generative models, which we call
ensemble inverse generative models. For the posterior modeling, these models additionally
use the ensemble information contained in the observation set on top of single

measurements. Unlike existing methods, using information from the observation ensemble
allows the proposed method to turn a standard sample-wise posterior sampler into a
distribution-dependent posterior sampler. This allows the model to exploit information
about the unknown prior that is implicitly contained in the observation set, providing an
inductive bias for generalization to unseen priors.

After developing and implementing the algorithm, we then demonstrated that this training
procedure implicitly encodes the likelihood model. That showed that this use of ensemble
information helps doing posterior inferences and enables generalization to unseen priors.

We ﬁnally turned back to applying this method to the unfolding application in HEP. We
noticed that the proposed methods display superior performances than standard or other
ML-based methods across a set of four unseen physics processes, illustrating the
eGectiveness of the proposed methods in utilizing latent ensemble information for unfolding
without knowledge of the priors. Our proposed methods achieve comparable performance
to the cDDPM, indicating that the algorithm could automatically extract the core ensemble
information from data and eliminate redundant information.

It is worth also mentioning that the framework also has limitations: its performance depends
on the informativeness of the observation set and on the quality, capacity, and stability of
the permutation-invariant encoder, whose optimal design is not addressed in this work. Yet,
it gives conﬁdence on the value of the cDDPM algorithm to unfold kinematic distributions in
HEP experiments.

Broader Impacts of this Project

The above reported accomplishments for this project, together with the process and
experience gained by realizing them lead to various broader impact beneﬁting HEP, other
disciplines, development of human resources, and the society more broadly.

Impact on the development of experimental High Energy Physics

This project will impact the ﬁeld of HEP and AI development in multiple ways. The resulting
unfolding strategy that we developed will be directly applied to a set of QCD measurements
in W+jets events using ATLAS data. Using diGusion models to unfold the data will result in
improved precision, generality, and ﬂexibility, and will also improve interpretability and
robustness of the results of such measurements, by allowing to control the systematic
uncertainty better, as well as keeping all correlations into account in the interpretation
phase of the measurement. Our work will also aim at faster calculation, necessary for more
ambitious statistical analyses on larger dataset. It will ﬁnally allow to provide, because of its
generality, unfolding of new physics searches, allowing for more precise and thus tighter
limits, as well as oGering readily available constraining tests for theory not yet developed.
Similar impact will also be achieved on the data analysis and cross section measurements
of neutrino experiments. Our work will help the broader HEP community reaching similar
objectives, hopefully extending the applications of our ML object-based event-by-event
unfolding to a large range of HEP data measurements. This project will also impact the AI
development in general by showing how denoising models can be used to solve constrained
inverse problem, in which the noise process is not fully random, but should be constrained
to replicate detector eGects. It will also help showing how nuisance parameters can be
accounted for in the training phase, allowing to embed account of uncertainties directly in
the denoising process.

Impact on other disciplines

Unfolding problems are not unique to HEP, but take a very similar form in astrophysics, Earth
science, and many other disciplines. The outcome of our project paves the way for these
other disciplines to get inspired by our work to improve the correction of their data for
instrumental artifacts, in a way that account for uncertainties in the modeling phase. The
precision that is gained will be proﬁtable to any adaptation of our unfolding strategy.
Discussions to extend our work to astrophysics has already been initiated.

Our work on EIP aims precisely at extending the approach with developed beyond HEP and
even beyond physical sciences. We already benchmarked our approach to two applications
beyond experimental HEP:
 a) We studied the 2D full waveform inversion (FWI), in which the goal is to reconstruct
 subsurface velocity maps of various families from seismic waveform recordings;
 b) We performed image inversion task, where we present a mixtures of MNIST digit
 images that are corrupted by stochastic pixel-wise noise, with the goal of recovering
 the corresponding clean images for a set of blurred observations with a uniform but
 unknown interpolation parameter.

That could be used to help with handwriting recognition, or speech recognition in noisy
background environment, for example.

Conclusion

The project aimed at using diGusion model approach to Machine Learning techniques to
tackle unfolding of detector-level distributions in experimental High Energy Physics, to
prepare the ground for signal reconstruction in neutrino physics, and open the possibility of
applications beyond the scope of experimental HEP. These goals have been met and
surpassed. The cDDPD algorithm that we developed allowed for an object-by-object event-
by-event unfolding in a way that addresses the generalization problem, i.e. that allows
unfolding processes never encountered before. This solution to the generalization problem
simultaneously addressed the other goal of controlling systematic uncertainties of
theoretical origin through the developed ML-based unfolding techniques. We did however
not manage to ﬁnd a way to address the experimental systematic uncertainties, although the
Ensemble Inverse Problem (EIP) approach we investigated oGers so basis for future work in
this. That generalization of the underlying algorithmic structure though the EIP provided
stronger theoretical foundations for explaining the success of the cDDPM algorithm, while
oGering clear way for broader applications beyond the ﬁeld of HEP.
