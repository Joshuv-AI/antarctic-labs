Universal critical behavior in ideal Bose-Einstein condensation
Arturo Camacho-Guardian, Leon Kleebank, Frank Vewinger, Martin Weitz, Julian Schmitt, Rosario Paredes, Victor Romero-Rochín

Abstract
Ideal Bose-Einstein condensation (BEC) remains a paradigmatic example of a continuous phase transition and a cornerstone for understanding quantum degenerate bosonic matter. We demonstrate that critical behavior of the ideal Bose gas near the BEC phase transition falls into three distinct classes, determined exclusively by the low-energy scaling of the density of states. Depending on its scaling exponent, which is controlled by dimensionality and confinement, the transition displays either the usual algebraic divergences of thermodynamic susceptibilities, divergent behavior with marginal logarithmic corrections, or a more subtle form of criticality, where only the correlation length diverges. Our work provides a unified framework for criticality in noninteracting bosonic systems. This classification applies broadly to atomic, photonic, polaritonic, and magnonic condensates, where dimensionality, confinement, and spectral engineering can strongly reshape the density of states.



Introduction.—
Continuous phase transitions are characterized by the emergence of long-range correlations and non-analytic behavior of thermodynamic observables
Fisher 1974
. Near a critical point, universal scaling laws depend only on a few key properties, such as dimensionality, symmetries and conservation laws, rather than on microscopic details
Widom 1965
;
Kadanoff et al. 1967
. Critical exponents quantify the singular behavior of fundamental quantities such as the correlation length and susceptibilities as the transition is approached. In interacting quantum systems, as ultracold atoms
Donner et al. 2007
;
Giorgini et al. 1996
or condensed matter
MacKinnon 1994
;
Aizenman and Fernández 1998
;
Belim et al. 2016
;
Chen and Schnyder 2019
, determination of these exponents have required to account for fluctuations beyond mean-field theory
Wilson and Kogut 1974
;
Ma 1976
;
Amit and Martin-Mayor 2005
.

In systems of noninteracting bosons, macroscopic occupation of the ground state is the signature of Bose-Einstein condensation (BEC)
Landau and Lifshitz 1980
;
Bagnato et al. 1987
;
Dalfovo et al. 1999
. Qualifying such a transition as critical requires to show that density fluctuations growing without bound are accompanied by a diverging isothermal compressibility, as the critical point is approached. Critical behavior in these systems emerges from both quantum statistics and the low-energy single-particle density of states (DOS). Since its prediction by Einstein
Einstein 2005
, BEC has been realized across a wide range of systems, including ultracold atoms, exciton-polaritons, magnons, or photons
Anderson et al. 1995
;
Davis et al. 1995
;
Deng et al. 2010
;
Demokritov et al. 2006
;
Klaers et al. 2010
;
Schofield et al. 2024
;
Pieczarka et al. 2024
. Yet, most experimental realizations occur under conditions far from the case of a homogeneous, non-interacting three-dimensional (3D) gas. While early ultracold-atom experiments necessarily involved harmonic confinement and weak interactions
Anderson et al. 1995
;
Davis et al. 1995
, later assisted by Feshbach tuning
Chin et al. 2010
and tailored trapping potentials
Bloch et al. 2008
, polaritonic
Deng et al. 2010
;
Byrnes et al. 2014
, and magnonic
Demokritov et al. 2006
condensates are driven-dissipative far-from-equilibrium systems that emerge in quasi-two-dimensional (2D) geometries.

Perhaps one of the closest realizations to Einstein’s original BEC prediction is the one emerging in low-dimensional photon gases in dye-filled optical microcavities
Klaers et al. 2010
;
Marelic and Nyman 2015
;
Greveling et al. 2018
, since these essentially noninteracting Bose gases still thermalize by absorption and re-emission processes with a thermal bath of dye molecules and therefore resemble to good approximation the case of an ideal gas at thermal equilibrium. Direct measurements of thermodynamic observables such as the specific heat, the equation of state, or the compressibility
Damm et al. 2016
;
Busley et al. 2022
, as well as of the spatial phase correlations
Kleebank et al. 2025
of the optical quantum gas have confirmed its critical character. Together with the ability to tune the dimensionality and trap potentials for the photons

;
Karkihalli Umesh et al. 2024
, these developments have opened the possibility for probing critical phenomena in Bose gas systems from a fundamental quantum statistics and thermodynamics point-of-view, calling for a unified systematic classification of ideal BEC based on its thermodynamic singularities. Inspired by interacting Bose gases and quantum fluids, which belong to the distinct XY universality class characterized by a complex order parameter
Lipa et al. 2003
;
Burovski et al. 2006
;
Kosterlitz 1974
, also ideal Bose gases confined in trap potentials
V(\mathbf{r})

\sigma<1

\sigma=1

Scaling laws of ideal BEC.—
The thermodynamic properties of the ideal Bose gas at temperature
T
are governed by the grand potential
Landau and Lifshitz 1980
,

\Omega=k_{\text{B}}T\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho(\epsilon)\,\ln\!\left(1-e^{-\epsilon/k_{\text{B}}T+\tilde{\mu}}\right)d\epsilon,

(1)

with
\tilde{\mu}=\mu/k_{\text{B}}T
, where
\mu\leq 0
is the chemical potential. The single-particle DOS
\rho(\epsilon)
is fixed in the thermodynamic limit and encodes information about the confining potential
V(\mathbf{r})
,
e.g.
harmonic, box, power-law, or soft-wall Pöschl–Teller potentials
Pöschl and Teller 1933
;
Nieto 1978
;
Kleebank et al. 2025
. It scales linearly with an extensive generalized volume
{\cal V}

Romero-Rochin 2005
;
Sandoval-Figueroa and Romero-Rochin 2008
, may depend on intensive potential parameters, and is strongly influenced by the dimensionality
d
; for a detailed discussion, see the SI
SI
.

The onset of BEC at temperature
T
is controlled by the particle density
n=N/{\cal V}
,

\displaystyle n

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{{\cal V}}\left(\frac{\partial\Omega}{\partial\mu}\right)_{T,\mathcal{V}}=\frac{1}{{\cal V}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\rho(\epsilon)}{e^{\epsilon/k_{\text{B}}T-\tilde{\mu}}-1}d\epsilon\>.

(2)

At
\tilde{\mu}=0
, the gas exhibits BEC if
n
remains finite, and this occurs only if the low-energy DOS vanishes sufficiently rapidly with energy
\epsilon
. Writing

\rho(\epsilon)\sim\epsilon^{\sigma}
, the integral diverges for
\sigma\leq 0
and BEC is impossible at finite
T
and

; for example, a homogeneous Bose gas in a 2D box trap has
\sigma=0
and does not condense, see Fig.
1
. The scaling exponent
\sigma>0
alone dictates whether ideal-gas condensation can occur.

At
\epsilon\to 0
, the DOS can quite generally be written as

\rho(\epsilon)={\cal V}C\epsilon^{\sigma}+\textrm{higher powers in $\epsilon$}\>,

(3)

where
C
depends on fundamental constants, the mass of the particles, and intensive trap parameters, if any. The exponent
\sigma
is fixed entirely by the low–energy part of the confining potential. This can be seen by semiclassically evaluating the DOS,

\rho(\epsilon)=\frac{1}{(2\pi\hbar)^{d}}\int d^{d}r\,d^{d}p\;\delta\!\left(\epsilon-\frac{p^{2}}{2m}-V(\mathbf{r})\right)\>.

(4)

To characterize the corresponding low-energy behavior, we measure energy from the global minimum of the external potential
V(\mathbf{r})
, which we set to zero for simplicity. Since only the local form of the potential near its minimum contributes to the DOS as
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
, we approximate

V(\mathbf{r})\simeq V_{0}r^{s}\>,

(5)

where
s
characterizes the power–law confinement. We assume isotropy; the anisotropic generalization is given in the SM
SI
. Inserting Eq. (
5
) into Eq. (
4
), one finds that the spatial region accessible to energy
\epsilon
scales as
\epsilon^{d/s}
, giving the universal infrared form
\rho(\epsilon\to 0^{+})\propto\mathcal{V}\,\epsilon^{\sigma}
with the scaling exponent

\sigma=\left(\frac{d}{2}-1\right)+\frac{d}{s},

(6)

which shows explicitly that
\sigma
is determined by confinement and dimensionality. Note that
\sigma
is independent of the details of
V(\mathbf{r})
away from the minimum. Here, the generalized volume associated to the potential
V(\mathbf{r})
in Eq. (
5
) scales as
\mathcal{V}\sim V_{0}^{-d/s}
, see SI
SI
. Thus, the DOS near threshold is governed solely by the local curvature or flatness of the potential. Equation (
6
) reproduces familiar limits: For a box (
s\to\infty
), one finds
\rho(\epsilon)\propto\epsilon^{d/2-1}
allowing BEC in
d>2
, while for harmonic confinement (
s=2
), one obtains
\rho(\epsilon)\propto\epsilon^{d-1}
, allowing BEC in
d\geq 2

Bagnato et al. 1987
;
Pethick and Smith 2008
;
Morales-Amador et al. 2024a
.

As the BEC limit is realized by
\tilde{\mu}\to 0^{-}
, we can write, see Eq. (
2
),

\displaystyle n=C_{0}T^{\sigma+1}g_{\sigma+1}(\tilde{\mu})+\dots,

(7)

where
C_{0}=Ck^{\sigma+1}\Gamma(\sigma+1)
with Gamma function
\Gamma(x)
. The Bose function

g_{\sigma+1}(\tilde{\mu})=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\sigma+1)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{\sigma}}{e^{x-\tilde{\mu}}-1}dx\>

(8)

is non-analytic at
\tilde{\mu}=0
and for
\sigma>0
and
|\tilde{\mu}|<1
can be expressed by asymptotic expansions

;
Wood 1992
:

\displaystyle g_{\sigma+1}(\tilde{\mu})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Gamma(-\sigma)(-\tilde{\mu})^{\sigma}+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta(\sigma+1-k)}{k!}\tilde{\mu}^{k}\>\>\>\>\>\sigma\neq 1,2,3,\dots

(9a)

\displaystyle g_{\sigma+1}(\tilde{\mu})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{\mu}^{\sigma}}{\sigma!}\left[H_{\sigma}-\ln(-\tilde\mu)\right]+\sum_{k=0,k\neq\sigma}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta(\sigma+1-k)}{k!}\tilde{\mu}^{k}\>\>\>\>\>\sigma=1,2,3,\dots\>

(9b)

Here,
H_{\sigma}
denotes the harmonic number and
\zeta
the Riemann zeta function. For
\sigma>0
, one has the critical phase-space density
g_{\sigma+1}(0)=\zeta(\sigma+1)
.

Substituting Eqns. (
9a
) and (
9b
) into the expression Eq. (
7
) for the density
n
and expanding near criticality (
\tilde{\mu}\to 0^{-}
), we obtain three quantiatively different regimes of criticality,

\zeta(\sigma+1)t\approx-\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}\Gamma(-\sigma)(-\tilde{\mu})^{\sigma}+\cdots&(\sigma<1)\\
&&\\
-\tilde{\mu}\ln(-\tilde\mu)+\cdots&(\sigma=1)\\
&&\\
\zeta(\sigma)\tilde{\mu}+\cdots&(\sigma>1)\end{array}\right.\!,

(10)

Here
t
denotes the reduced temperature or density, respectively,

t=(\sigma+1)\frac{T-T_{\text{c}}}{T_{\text{c}}}=\frac{n_{\text{c}}-n}{n_{\text{c}}}.

(11)

relative to the critical point. Fixing either
n
or
T
yields finite critical values
T_{\text{c}}
or
n_{\text{c}}
, respectively. Specifically, in the critical regime
t\ll 1
. Note that
\zeta(\sigma+1)=(n/C_{0}T^{\sigma+1})_{c}
is the critical phase-space density. Equation (
10
) allows to solve for the reduced chemical potential in terms of
t
,

-\tilde{\mu}\approx\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}{\cal A}_{\tilde{\mu}}\,t^{1/\sigma}&(\sigma<1)\\
&&\\
-{\cal B}_{\tilde{\mu}}\,t(\ln t)^{-1}&(\sigma=1)\\
&&\\
{\cal C}_{\tilde{\mu}}\,t&(\sigma>1)\end{array}\right.\!.

(12)

As all thermodynamic quantities depend on the chemical potential
\tilde{\mu}
, critical exponents can be extracted.

We first consider the isothermal compressibility
\kappa_{T}=n^{-2}(k_{\text{B}}T)^{-1}({\partial n}/{\partial\tilde{\mu}})_{T}
, which follows from Eq. (
7
). One finds

\kappa_{T}\approx\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}{\cal A}_{\kappa}\,t^{-(1/\sigma-1)}&(\sigma<1)\\
&&\\
-{\cal B}_{\kappa}\ln t&(\sigma=1)\\
&&\\
{\cal C}_{\kappa}+\cdots&(\sigma>1)\end{array}\right.\!\>.

(13)

Thus, the obtained algebraic scaling form
\kappa_{T}\sim t^{-\gamma_{T}}
implies a critical exponent
\gamma_{T}=1/\sigma-1
for
\sigma<1
. At
\sigma=1
, the compressibility shows a logarithmic divergence. By contrast, for
\sigma>1
, the compressibility does not diverge at all, so that no
\gamma_{T}
exponent can be assigned.

Similarly, we analyze the critical scaling of the pressure
p
, deduced from
\Omega=-p{\cal V}
and Eq. (
1
),

p\approx k_{B}C_{0}T^{\sigma+2}\left[\zeta(\sigma+2)+\zeta(\sigma+1)\tilde{\mu}+\cdots\right].

(14)

At
T=T_{\text{c}}
, using Eq. (
12
),

p-p_{\text{c}}\approx\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}{\cal A}_{p}(n-n_{\text{c}})^{1/\sigma}&(\sigma<1)\\
&&\\
{\cal B}_{p}(n-n_{\text{c}})\frac{1}{\ln(n-\nc)}&(\sigma=1)\\
&&\\
{\cal C}_{p}(n-n_{\text{c}})&(\sigma>1)\end{array}\right.\!.

(15)

Thus,
p-p_{\text{c}}\sim(n-n_{\text{c}})^{\delta_{T}}
gives a critical exponent
\delta_{T}=1/\sigma
for
\sigma\leq 1
, with logarithmic corrections at
\sigma=1
, and the mean-field-like value
\delta_{T}=1
for
\sigma>1
. Finally, note that the heat capacity
C_{\mathcal{V}}
for ideal Bose gases at constant volume
\mathcal{V}
does not diverge at
T_{\text{c}}
for any confinement. This is because, near BEC, it scales with
g_{2+\sigma}(\tilde{\mu})\sim\zeta(2+\sigma)
, which is finite for any value of
\sigma
. One finds
C_{\cal V}\sim\mathcal{V}T^{\sigma+1}\sim N

\mu=0
. Therefore, a critical exponent associated to the heat capacity may not be meaningful.

Correlations and fluctuations at criticality.—
A key observable to describe the critical behavior of a quantum gas near a phase transition is the density–density correlation function
G(\textbf{x},\textbf{x}^{\prime})=\langle n(\textbf{x})n(\textbf{x}^{\prime})\rangle-\langle n(\textbf{x})\rangle\langle n(\textbf{x}^{\prime})\rangle
and its associated correlation length
\xi
. Inhomogeneous systems,
e.g.
, in harmonic trap potentials, break translational invariance, so that
G(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\neq G(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\prime})
. The correlations then depend separately on the relative and the center–of–mass coordinates,
\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\prime},
and
\mathbf{R}=(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{x}^{\prime})/2
. Within the local density approximation (LDA), the subsystem at
\mathbf{R}
is treated as a homogeneous gas with a locally varying chemical potential

\mu_{\mathrm{LDA}}(\mathbf{R})=\mu-V(\mathbf{R})\>.

(16)

For a homogeneous ideal Bose gas in
d
dimensions in the quantum-degenerate regime (
\mu\to 0^{-}
), the density–density correlation function is
SI
,

G_{\mathrm{hom}}(r)\approx\mathcal{A}_{d}\left|\>\left(\frac{1}{\xi r}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}-1}K_{\frac{d}{2}-1}\!\left(\frac{r}{\xi}\right)\right|^{2},

(17)

with a temperature-dependent correlation amplitude
\mathcal{A}_{d}
, correlation length
\xi=\sqrt{\hbar^{2}/2m|\mu|}
,
K_{\ell}(z)
the modified Bessel function of second kind and order
\ell
. The correlator in Eq. (
17
) depends on the relative coordinate
r
only, indicating that the system is translationally-invariant. Correspondingly, for an inhomogeneous system the LDA yields

G_{\mathrm{LDA}}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{R})\approx\mathcal{A}_{d}\left|\left(\frac{1}{\xi(\mathbf{R})|\mathbf{r}|}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}-1}K_{\frac{d}{2}-1}\!\left(\frac{|\mathbf{r}|}{\xi(\mathbf{R})}\right)\right|^{2}\>,

(18)

with the local correlation length

\xi^{-1}(\mathbf{R})=\sqrt{\xi^{-2}+\frac{2mV(\mathbf{R})}{\hbar^{2}}}.

(19)

Integrating out the center-of-mass coordinate
\mathbf{R}
, using Eq. (
5
), gives us the isotropic correlation function between two distinct locations in the trap,

\mathcal{G}(r)=\int d^{d}R\,G_{\mathrm{LDA}}(r,\textbf{R})\>.

(20)

For large but finite values of
\xi
, Eq. (
20
) can be evaluated using the large–argument form,
r/\xi\gg 1
, of the modified Bessel function
SI
. One obtains the density-density correlation function

\mathcal{G}(r)\sim\mathcal{V}\;\frac{\xi^{-(d-3+d/s)}}{r^{\,d-1+d/s}}\,e^{-2r/\xi}\>.

(21)

which indicates that the spatial correlations exhibit a self-similar power-law decay with an exponent determined by the confining potential as the correlation length
\xi\to\infty
very close to BEC. The correlation length itself diverges with critical exponent is
\xi\sim t^{-\nu_{T}}
. Using Eq. (
12
),

\xi\approx\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}{\cal A}_{\xi}\,t^{-1/2\sigma}&(\sigma<1)\\
&&\\
{\cal B}_{\xi}\,[t^{-1}\ln t]^{1/2}&(\sigma=1)\\
&&\\
{\cal C}_{\xi}\,t^{-1/2}&(\sigma>1)\end{array}\right.\!.

(22)

Here, clearly the exponent
\nu_{T}=1/2\sigma
for
\sigma\leq 1
is universal for all systems showing the same scaling behavior of the DOS; for example, from Eq. (
6
), the 1D Bose gas in a V-shaped linear trap (
d=1,s=1
) exhibits the same universality as the 3D box-trapped gas (
d=3,s\rightarrow\infty
), both having
\sigma=1/2
. At
\sigma=1
, the divergence is modified by logarithmic corrections and loses its
\sigma

Kleebank et al. 2025
;
Gnusov et al. 2026

\sigma>1

\nu_{T}=1/2
is obtained. The result from Eq. (
22

Scaling relations.—
To obtain a prediction for the algebraic decay of the spatial correlation function
{\cal G}(r)
at criticality,
\mu=0
, and identify a critical exponent
\eta_{T}

, one can use, first, the limit

of the Bessel function
K_{d/2-1}(r/\xi)
and, then take the strict limit
\xi\to\infty
. In this case, the local correlation length scales as
\xi^{-1}(R)\!\sim\!{\cal V}^{-s/2d}R^{s/2}
and the correlation function becomes

\mathcal{G}(r)\approx\mathcal{V}\frac{\mathcal{D}_{d}}{r^{d-2+\eta_{T}}},

(23)

where the critical exponent
\eta_{T}
is identified as,

\eta_{T}=d-2+\frac{2d}{s}=2\sigma.

(24)

The isothermal compressibility and the correlation function are tied by the fluctuation–response relation,

\displaystyle\kappa_{T}=\frac{1}{n^{2}k_{B}T}\int d^{d}r\,\mathcal{G}(r),

(25)

which when used in conjunction with the asymptotic behavior of

. This yields the Fisher scaling relation

Conclusions.—
Within a purely thermodynamic framework, our work establishes a unifying classification for critical scaling behavior of ideal Bose gases near the continuous (
i.e.
, second-order) BEC phase transition. The classification criterion is based solely on the low-energy scaling of the DOS,
\rho(\epsilon)\sim\epsilon^{\sigma}
. Our analysis demonstrates that the exponent
\sigma
not only determines whether BEC is thermodynamically allowed but also, if the case, uniquely predicts the critical exponents. Three classes emerge: (i)
\sigma<1
, a fluctuation-governed regime where the compressibility diverges and all standard critical exponents are well defined; (ii)
\sigma=1
, a marginal regime where logarithmic corrections appear; and (iii)
\sigma>1
, a regime with critical exponents resembling that of the mean-field prediction where only the correlation length diverges with the exponent
\nu_{T}=1/2
,
\delta_{T}=1
, while other familiar exponents such as
\gamma_{T}
and
\eta_{T}
cease to be meaningful.

In a broader perspective, our findings reveal that the non-analytic behavior of thermodynamic quantities at the BEC point originates from geometry and spectral shaping alone. Besides quantum fluids of light, where recent experiments have obtained results consistent with the presented theory
Kleebank et al. 2025
;
Gnusov et al. 2026
, the investigation here exposed is particularly relevant for condensed matter platforms including the emerging designs of band structures, flat bands in layered systems, and synthetic dimensions, where the DOS near the ground state can be tuned deliberately
Danieli et al. 2024
;
Argüello-Luengo et al. 2024
. Extensions of this approach to both weakly interacting as well as nonequilibrium systems may further illuminate how universality and critical behaviour evolves from the ideal-gas paradigm to the full complexity of quantum critical phenomena in bosonic matter
Sieberer et al. 2025
, encompassing physical realizations including ultracold atoms in uniform traps
Navon et al. 2021
, exciton-polaritons in engineered semiconductor microcavities
Bloch et al. 2022
, or photons in dye-filled cavities
Klaers et al. 2010
. Our classification therefore provides a common language to compare BEC transitions across platforms where the single-particle spectrum can be highly nontrivial.

S1. Thermodynamic Limit and Density of States at low lying energies for arbitrary confining potentials

In this section we derive the low lying energy expression of the density of states (DOS) for a generic potential, using the known semiclassical expression and, in the following section, we provide examples with standard confinement geometries. With this procedure, we can (a) identify the generalized volume

, corresponding to the particular external confining potential, and that defines the thermodynamic limit and (b) extract the low-energy power-law exponent
\sigma
. We show that the low-energy DOS takes the form,

\rho(\epsilon)\xrightarrow[\epsilon\to 0^{+}]{}{\cal V}\,C\,\epsilon^{\sigma},

(27)

with
C
a constant. As shown in the main text, this DOS expression completely determines both the existence of Bose–Einstein
condensation (BEC) and the corresponding universality classes of the transition.

We consider the ideal Hamiltonian for
N
particles

H=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\frac{\mathbf{p}_{i}^{2}}{2m}+V(\mathbf{r}_{i})\right),

(28)

where the external potential
V(\mathbf{r})
defines the geometry and the thermodynamic
limit via an extensive generalized volume

. In this section, we assume that the potential, near its vanishing minimum, can be either a separable power law,

V(\mathbf{r})\approx\sum_{i=1}^{d}V_{i}\,x_{i}^{s_{i}},

(29)

with
V_{i}>0
and
s_{i}>0
, or an isotropic potential,

V(\mathbf{r})\approx V_{0}\,r^{s}\>,

(30)

with
V_{0}>0
and
r^{2}=x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+x_{d}^{2}
. Again, below we give specific examples of these generic forms.

S1.1 Identifying the generalized volume

For simplicity, let the ideal gas be confined by an external isotropic given by Eq. (
30
). In the high-temperature limit the gas can be considered classical and the partition function for
N
particles at a given temperature
T
can be integrated to give,
Sandoval-Figueroa and Romero-Rochin 2008

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{h^{dN}N!}\int d^{dN}r\int d^{dN}p\>e^{-H/k_{B}T}

(31)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{T}^{dN}N!}\left(\mathcal{N}_{d}(k_{B}T)^{d/s}V_{0}^{-d/s}\right)^{N}\>,

where
\mathcal{N}_{d}=\int d^{d}xe^{-x^{s}}
is a number and
\lambda_{T}=h/\sqrt{2\pi mk_{B}T}
the de Broglie wavelength. The free energy
F=-k_{B}T\ln Z
, for large
N
, is

F=-Nk_{B}T\left(\ln\left[\frac{\mathcal{N}_{d}(k_{B}T)^{d/s}}{\lambda_{T}^{d}}\frac{V_{0}^{-d/s}}{N}\right]+1\right)\>.

(32)

One finds that
F/N
is finite in the thermodynamic limit
F\to\infty
,
N\to\infty
, if and only if
V_{0}^{-d/s}/N
also remains finite in such a limit. This requires that
V_{0}^{-d/s}\to\infty
. This allows us to define

\mathcal{V}=V_{0}^{-d/s}

(33)

as an extensive
volume
, generalizing
V
for a box. Although the units need not those of a volume, this variable plays the role of a volume. In the same way, one defines a generalized
pressure

Sandoval-Figueroa and Romero-Rochin 2008
;
Romero-Rochin 2005
as
\mathcal{P}=-(\partial F/\partial{\mathcal{V}})_{N,T}
, such that
{\mathcal{P}V}
is an energy. In particular, for the classical ideal gas,
{\mathcal{P}V}=Nk_{B}T
, as expected.

S1.2 Semiclassical derivation of the low-energy DOS

We begin with the semiclassical expression for the density of states

\rho(E)=\frac{1}{(2\pi\hbar)^{d}}\int d^{d}r\int d^{d}p\;\delta\!\left(E-\frac{p^{2}}{2m}-V(\mathbf{r})\right).

(34)

The integral over momentum space can be done, obtaining,

\rho(\epsilon)=\frac{\Omega_{d}\,m}{(2\pi\hbar)^{d}}\int_{V(\mathbf{r})\leq E}d^{d}r\,\left[2m(E-V(\mathbf{r}))\right]^{\frac{d}{2}-1}.

(35)

where
\Omega_{d}
is the solid angle in
d
dimensions.

Our interest is for small energies
\epsilon
above the minimum of the potential that we assume has the separable power law form
given in Eq. (
29
). The density of states becomes,

\rho(\epsilon)\approx\frac{\Omega_{d}\,m}{(2\pi\hbar)^{d}}\int_{\cal D}d^{d}r\;\left[2m\left(\epsilon-\sum_{i=1}^{d}V_{i}x_{i}^{s_{i}}\right)\right]^{\frac{d}{2}-1},

(36)

where
{\cal D}=\{\mathbf{x}:\epsilon-\sum_{i=1}^{d}V_{i}x_{i}^{s_{i}}\geq 0\}
.

To make the low-energy scaling explicit, we rescale the spatial
coordinates as

y_{i}=\left(\frac{V_{i}}{\epsilon}\right)^{1/s_{i}}x_{i},\qquad d^{d}r=\epsilon^{\sum_{i}1/s_{i}}\prod_{i}V_{i}^{-1/s_{i}}\,d^{d}y

(37)

yielding

\displaystyle\rho(\epsilon)

\displaystyle\approx\Biggl[\frac{\Omega_{d}\,m}{(2\pi\hbar)^{d}}(2m)^{\frac{d}{2}-1}\prod_{j=1}^{d}V_{j}^{-1/s_{j}}\int_{\mathcal{D}}d^{d}y\;\left(1-\sum_{i=1}^{d}y_{i}^{s_{i}}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}-1}\Biggr]\epsilon^{\frac{d}{2}-1+\sum_{i}1/s_{i}}

(38)

where
\mathcal{D}=\{\mathbf{y}:\sum_{i}y_{i}^{s_{i}}\leq 1\}
. The term in brackets is a positive constant, independent of
\epsilon
, thus displaying the low energy scaling of the density of states,

\rho(\epsilon)\sim\mathcal{V}\epsilon^{\,\frac{d}{2}-1+\sum_{i}1/s_{i}},

(39)

where
\mathcal{V}\sim\prod_{j=1}^{d}V_{j}^{-1/s_{j}}.

For isotropic potentials
V(\mathbf{r})\approx V_{0}\>r^{s}
, one gets the simpler, yet, clearer scaling,

\rho(\epsilon)\sim\mathcal{V}\epsilon^{\,\frac{d}{2}-1+\frac{d}{s}},

(40)

with a generalized volume
\mathcal{V}\sim V_{0}^{-d/s}
as expected.

Therefore, the exponent
\sigma
of the DOS,
\rho(\epsilon)\sim\mathcal{V}\>\epsilon^{\sigma}
, is either

\displaystyle\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{2}-1+\sum_{i}1/s_{i}

(41)

\displaystyle\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{2}-1+\frac{d}{s}\>.

(42)

S3 Two-particle Green’s function, LDA, and trapped correlations

In this section, we study the decay of the density-density correlations within the local density approximation (LDA) for trapped systems. We show that the DOS governs the behaviour of long-range decay of the correlations.

S1.1
S3.1 Single-particle Green’s function

Our starting point is the single-particle Green’s function at equal time. We first assume an ideal Bose gas in
d
dimensions confined in a homogeneous trap,

G_{1,\textrm{hom}}(\mathbf{r})=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int d^{d}k\frac{e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}}}{e^{(\epsilon_{k}-\mu)/k_{B}T}-1}\>,

(72)

where
r=|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\prime}|
is the relative separation of the correlated points and
\epsilon_{k}=\hbar^{2}k^{2}/2m
. It is convenient to introduce the correlation length

\xi^{-1}=\sqrt{\frac{2m|\mu|}{\hbar^{2}}},

(73)

which is the dominant length in the vicinity of the onset of BEC, namely, for
\mu\to 0^{-}
. In this limit, the homogeneous
single–particle Green’s function can be approximated,

G_{1,\textrm{hom}}(\mathbf{r})\approx\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}k_{B}T\int d^{d}k\frac{e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}}}{\epsilon_{k}-\mu}\>,

(74)

which can be exactly integrated, giving

G_{1,\mathrm{hom}}(r)\sim\left(\frac{1}{\xi r}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}-1}K_{\frac{d}{2}-1}(r/\xi),

(75)

where

K_{\nu}
is the modified Bessel function of the second kind. Up to an overall temperature dependent prefactor, not relevant for critical properties,
Eq. (
75
) describes the spatial decay of single–particle
correlations in a homogeneous ideal Bose gas near BEC.

Here, we should note that for
r/\xi\ll 1

we find that

G_{1,\mathrm{hom}}(r)\sim\frac{1}{r^{d-2+\eta}}

(76)

with
\eta
the so-called anomalous dimension of the field which is stricly zero
\eta=0
for non-interacting homogenoues BEC, of course, as expected.

In what follows, we will use Eq. (
75
) as the local building block
to construct the two–particle Green’s function in a trapped gas within the
LDA.

S1.2
S3.2 Density–density correlations

As discussed in the main text, the
two–particle
(density–density) Green’s function is the relevant object for thermodynamic response functions. We will employ a local-density approximation, for this purpose it is useful to change to the center–of–mass
and relative coordinates,

\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\prime},\qquad\mathbf{R}=\frac{\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{x}^{\prime}}{2},

(77)

so that
G(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})=G(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{R})
.
For an ideal gas, Wick’s theorem implies that the local two–particle
correlator can be written as

G_{2}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{R})=\rho(\mathbf{R})\delta^{d}(\mathbf{r})+\bigl|G_{1}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{R})\bigr|^{2}\>,

(78)

where
\rho(\mathbf{R})
is the local density. This correlation function is exactly related to the isothermal compressibility by,

\kappa_{T}=\frac{\mathcal{V}}{N^{2}k_{B}T}\int d^{d}r\int d^{d}R\,G_{2}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{R})\>.

(79)

Near BEC,
\kappa_{T}
takes large values and the relevant contribution of the correlation are for large values of
r
, the distance between the two correlated spatial points. Thus, the relevant quantity to study is the center-of-mass integrated correlation function, that is,

\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{r})=\int d^{d}R\,G_{2}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{R})=\int d^{d}R\,\bigl|G_{1}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{R})\bigr|^{2}\>.

(80)

S1.3
S3.3 Local density approximation for the trapped gas

In a trapped system translational invariance is broken and

G_{1}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\neq G_{1}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\prime})
. Within the
local density approximation, one assumes that around each point
\mathbf{R}

the system behaves as a homogeneous gas characterized by a
local

chemical potential,

\mu_{\mathrm{LDA}}(\mathbf{R})=\mu-V(\mathbf{R}),

(81)

where
V(\mathbf{R})
is the trapping potential. The corresponding local
inverse coherence length is

\xi^{-1}(\mathbf{R})=\sqrt{\frac{2m\,|\mu_{\mathrm{LDA}}(\mathbf{R})|}{\hbar^{2}}}=\sqrt{\xi^{-2}+\frac{2mV(\mathbf{R})}{\hbar^{2}}}\>.

(82)

Within LDA, the inhomogeneous single–particle Green’s function is then
approximated by the homogeneous one evaluated at the local scale
\xi(\mathbf{R})
,

G_{1}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{R})\simeq G_{1,\mathrm{hom}}\!\left(\mathbf{r};\xi(\mathbf{R})\right),

(83)

with
G_{1,\mathrm{hom}}
given by Eq. (
75
). Consequently, the
two–particle Green’s function in the trapped system is

G_{2}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{R})=\bigl|G_{1,\mathrm{hom}}(\mathbf{r};\xi(\mathbf{R}))\bigr|^{2}.

(84)

Our task is to analyze the asymptotic behavior of the averaged correlator

\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{r})
in Eq. (
80
) for a power–law trap

V(\mathbf{R})=V_{0}R^{s}
. Now, we study two different regimes, the large-distance regime
r/\xi\gg 1
, for large but finite
\xi
, and at criticality
r/\xi\ll 1
.

S1.4
S3.4 Asymptotics for
r/\xi\gg 1

We first focus on the large–distance regime
r/\xi\gg 1
. In this limit, the modified Bessel function admits the asymptotic expansion

K_{\nu}(x)\sim\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2x}}\,e^{-x}\qquad(x\gg 1),

(85)

so that, using Eq. (
75
), the leading form of the
two–particle

Green’s function is

G_{2}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{R})\sim\left(\frac{1}{\xi(\mathbf{R})r}\right)^{d-2}\frac{\xi(\mathbf{R})}{r}\,e^{-2r/\xi(\mathbf{R})}.

(86)

For the spherically symmetric power–law trap
V(R)=V_{0}R^{s}
,
we can expand the local correlation length around its central value
\xi
, allowed by the thermodynamic limit
V_{0}\to 0
,

\xi^{-1}(\mathbf{R})\approx\xi^{-1}+\frac{mV_{0}}{\hbar^{2}}\xi R^{s}\>.

(87)

Substituting into Eq. (
86
) and integrating over the center of mass coordinate
\mathbf{R}
, one obtains, see Eq. (
80
),

\mathcal{G}(r)\sim\mathcal{V}\;\frac{\xi^{-(d-3+d/s)}}{r^{\,d-1+d/s}}\,e^{-2r/\xi}\>,

(88)

which is Eq. (22) of the main text. This expression gives the very relevant result that
\xi/2
is the correlation length near BEC for any confining potential, a divergent length
\xi\sim t^{-\nu_{T}}
as
\mu\to 0^{-}
. In the main text we have found the exponent
\nu_{T}
in terms of the exponent
\sigma
of the DOS. Note that
\mathcal{G}(r)
is proportional to the generalized volume
\mathcal{V}=V_{0}^{-d/s}
; this is an important observation because its presence renders the compressibility as an intensive quantity, see Eq. (
79
).

S1.5
S3.5 Asymptotics for
r/\xi\ll 1

We now consider the opposite limit

r/\xi\ll 1.

(89)

This is the situation where the system sits at criticality
\mu=0
, then the local coherence length is

\xi^{-1}(\mathbf{R})=\left(\frac{2mV_{0}R^{s}}{\hbar^{2}}\right)^{1/2}\>.

(90)

Substitution into the LDA correlation function
G_{2}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{R})
, gives

G_{2}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{R})\sim\left(\frac{V_{0}R^{s/2}}{r}\right)^{d-2}K_{\frac{d}{2}-1}^{\,2}\!\bigl(\sqrt{\frac{2mV_{0}}{\hbar^{2}}}R^{s/2}r\bigr).

(91)

After integrating out the center-of-mass coordinate one gets,

\mathcal{G}(r)\sim\frac{\mathcal{V}}{r^{\,2d-4+\frac{2d}{s}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dz\;z^{\frac{2d}{s}-3+d}K_{\frac{d}{2}-1}^{\,2}(z),

(92)

where the remaining integral over
z
is finite and dimensionless. From this expression one can read off the

exponent by appealing to Fisher form

\mathcal{G}(r)\sim\frac{1}{r^{\,d-2+\eta_{T}}},

(93)

thus identifying

\eta_{T}=d-2+\frac{2d}{s}.

(94)

Equivalently,

\eta_{T}=2\sigma.

(95)
