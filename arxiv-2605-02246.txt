Jongchon Kim

Abstract
. We prove weighted L 2 L^{2} and refined L p L^{p} decoupling estimates for functions whose Fourier transforms are supported in a small neighborhood of the unit sphere or the truncated paraboloid with an additional lower-dimensional frequency localization property. As a special case, we recover the fractal L 2 L^{2} restriction estimate of Du and Zhang, with a sharper dependence on the density of the weight. We also derive weighted refined decoupling estimates related to the Falconer distance set problem, improving earlier results under the stronger assumption that the underlying weight is α \alpha -dimensional at every scale.

1.
Introduction

Let
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a compact strictly convex
C^{2}
hypersurface with Gaussian curvature comparable to
1
, such as the unit sphere or the truncated paraboloid.
We prove weighted decoupling estimates for functions whose Fourier transforms are supported in the
R^{-1}
-neighborhood
N_{R^{-1}}(S)
of
S
, under an additional frequency localization assumption. This type of estimate was introduced by Du, Ou, Ren, and Zhang
[
DORZ23b
]
in connection with the Falconer distance set problem. We obtain an improved bound under the stronger assumption that the underlying weight is
\alpha
-dimensional at every scale. Weighted variants of decoupling estimates have also appeared recently in other contexts; see, for example,
[
GW25
,
Kim25
,
CLPY26
]
.

We first recall the wave packet setup. Let
\{\theta\}
denote a canonical covering of
N_{R^{-1}}(S)
by parallelepipeds of dimensions
R^{-1/2}\times\cdots\times R^{-1/2}\times R^{-1}
. For each
\theta
, let
\mathbb{T}_{\theta}
denote a collection of parallelepipeds dual to
\theta
forming a partition of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, each of dimensions
R^{1/2}\times\cdots\times R^{1/2}\times R
. Let
v(T)
denote the long direction of
T\in\mathbb{T}_{\theta}
orthogonal to
\theta
. A wave packet
f_{T}
,
T\in\mathbb{T}_{\theta}
, has Fourier support in a small dilate of
\theta
and decays rapidly away from
T
. Any function with Fourier support in
N_{R^{-1}}(S)
admits a decomposition into wave packets associated with
\mathbb{T}(R)=\cup_{\theta}\mathbb{T}_{\theta}
; see e.g.
[
BCLP24
, Appendix A.2]
.

Let
\mathbb{W}\subset\mathbb{T}(R)
. Following
[
DORZ23b
]
, we say that a sum of wave packets
\sum_{T\in\mathbb{W}}f_{T}
has
(r,m)
-concentrated frequencies
, for some
r\geq R^{-1/2}
and
1\leq m\leq d
, if there exists an
m
-dimensional subspace
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

\angle(v(T),V)\leq r,\;\;\text{for all }T\in\mathbb{W}.

This condition is vacuous when
m=d
or
r\geq\pi/2
.

For
\alpha\in[0,d]
and a set
Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, define

\displaystyle\gamma_{\alpha,R}(Y)=\sup_{1\leq\rho\leq R}\sup_{B_{\rho}}\rho^{-\alpha}|Y\cap B_{\rho}|,

where the second supremum is taken over all cubes
B_{\rho}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
of side
length
\rho
, referred to as a
\rho
-cube. We also define

\overline{\gamma_{\alpha,R}}(Y)=\sup_{R^{1/2}\leq\rho\leq R}\sup_{B_{\rho}}\rho^{-\alpha}|Y\cap B_{\rho}|,

which ignores scales below
R^{1/2}
. Additionally, we define

\mathcal{A}_{d,m,\alpha,\beta}(R,Y)=\gamma_{\alpha,R^{1/2}}(Y)\overline{\gamma_{\beta,R}}(Y)R^{\beta}\sup_{B_{R^{1/2}}}|Y\cap B_{R^{1/2}}|^{m-1}R^{-\frac{m(d+1)}{2}}.

This quantity records the density parameters of
Y
that appear in the main estimates.
We denote by
A\lesssim B
an inequality
A\leq CB
with an absolute constant
C
, and by
A\lessapprox B
the estimate
A\leq C_{\epsilon}R^{\epsilon}B
for every
\epsilon>0
. We can now state our main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
2\leq m\leq d
. Let
f=\sum_{T\in\mathbb{W}}f_{T}
be a sum of wave packets with
(R^{-1/2},m)
-concentrated frequencies, and let
Y\subset B_{R}
be a union of unit cubes.
Suppose
0\leq\alpha,\beta\leq d
satisfy
\beta\geq\alpha+m-d
. Then

\|f\|_{L^{2}(Y)}\lessapprox\mathcal{A}_{d,m,\alpha,\beta}(R,Y)^{\frac{1}{m(m+1)}}\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

Remark 1.2
.

The same estimate holds more generally for any union of unit cubes in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Indeed, the general case follows by partitioning
\mathbb{R}^{d}
into
R
-cubes and summing the local estimates in
Theorem

1.1
.

Remark 1.3
.

The bound is monotonic in
\beta
since

\beta\leq\beta^{\prime}\;\;\implies\;\;\overline{\gamma_{\beta,R}}(Y)R^{\beta}\leq\overline{\gamma_{\beta^{\prime},R}}(Y)R^{\beta^{\prime}}.

Accordingly,
Theorem

1.1
gives the strongest bound when
\beta=\max(0,\alpha+m-d)
.

Remark 1.4
.

The factor
\gamma_{\alpha,R^{1/2}}(Y)
allows the induction to close for
\beta<m
in the proof of
Theorem

1.1
. It is
not clear, however, whether this factor is genuinely necessary.
On the other hand, when
\beta\geq m
, this factor can be removed by taking
\alpha=d
, since
\gamma_{d,R^{1/2}}(Y)=1
whenever
Y
contains at least one unit cube.

Theorem

1.1
yields corresponding weighted
L^{2}
estimates for the Fourier extension operator associated with the surface
S
.
To keep the presentation simple, we focus on the case
m=d
, where
S=\{(\xi,|\xi|^{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1},\;\;\xi\in B_{1}^{n}(0)\}
is the truncated paraboloid. Let

e^{it\Delta}f(x)=(2\pi)^{-n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}e^{it|\xi|^{2}}\widehat{f}(\xi)d\xi

denote the solution to the Schrödinger equation
iu_{t}=\Delta u
with initial data
f
. For
f
with Fourier transform supported in
B_{1}^{n}(0)
, we let

(1.1)

Uf(x,t)=\varphi(R^{-1}t)e^{it\Delta}f(x),\;\;(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1},

where
\widehat{\varphi}
is a smooth bump function such that
1_{[-1/2,1/2]}\leq\widehat{\varphi}\leq 1_{[-1,1]}
. Note that
\widehat{Uf}
is supported on the
R^{-1}
-neighborhood of the truncated paraboloid in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Moreover,
\|e^{it\Delta}f\|_{L^{2}(Y)}\sim\|Uf\|_{L^{2}(Y)}
for
Y\subset B_{R}^{n+1}(0)
and, by Plancherel’s theorem,
\|Uf\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\sim R\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
. Thus, applying
Theorem

1.1
to
Uf
in the case
m=d=n+1
yields the following.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
n\geq 1
,
0\leq\alpha\leq n+1
and
Y\subset B^{n+1}_{R}(0)
be a union of unit cubes. For all
f
whose Fourier transform is supported in
B_{1}^{n}(0)
, we have

\|e^{it\Delta}f\|_{L^{2}(Y)}\lessapprox\Big(\gamma_{\alpha,R^{1/2}}(Y)\overline{\gamma_{\alpha,R}}(Y)R^{\alpha}\sup_{B_{R^{1/2}}}|Y\cap B_{R^{1/2}}|^{n}\Big)^{\frac{1}{(n+1)(n+2)}}\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

Theorem

1.5
sharpens the fractal
L^{2}
restriction estimate of Du and Zhang
[
DZ19
, Theorem 1.6]
; the product
\gamma_{\alpha,R^{1/2}}(Y)\overline{\gamma_{\alpha,R}}(Y)
replaces the factor
\gamma_{\alpha,R}(Y)^{2}
in that paper. The improvement is significant when one of the two factors is much smaller than
\gamma_{\alpha,R}(Y)
. We give such an example for which
Theorem

1.5
is sharp.

Proposition 1.6
.

For each
\frac{n}{2}\leq\alpha<n+1
, there exist
f
with
\widehat{f}
supported in
B_{1}^{n}(0)
and a union of unit cubes
Y\subset B^{n+1}_{R}(0)
such that
\gamma_{\alpha,R^{1/2}}(Y)\sim 1
and
\overline{\gamma_{\alpha,R}}(Y)=\gamma_{\alpha,R}(Y)\sim R^{\frac{n+1-\alpha}{2}}
for which

\|e^{it\Delta}f\|_{L^{2}(Y)}\gtrapprox\Big(\gamma_{\alpha,R^{1/2}}(Y)\overline{\gamma_{\alpha,R}}(Y)R^{\alpha}\sup_{B_{R^{1/2}}}|Y\cap B_{R^{1/2}}|^{n}\Big)^{\frac{1}{(n+1)(n+2)}}\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

We recall that the weighted
L^{2}
restriction estimate in
[
DZ19
]
yields sharp
L^{2}
bounds for the Schrödinger maximal function and has further applications to, e.g., the spherical average Fourier decay rates of fractal measures and the Falconer distance set problem (cf.
[
Car80
,
Fal85
,
Mat87
]
). We also mention related work
[
Sha21
]
on weighted Fourier restriction estimates for weights of low fractal dimension, as well as
[
DLWZ24
]
, which studies weighted
L^{2}\to L^{p}
restriction estimates under lower-dimensional wave packet concentration properties.

.

By interpolating
Theorem

1.1
and refined decoupling
[
GIOW20
,

(see

3.1
below for a statement), we obtain the following.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
2\leq m\leq d
. Let
f=\sum_{T\in\mathbb{W}}f_{T}
be a sum of wave packets with
(R^{-1/2},m)
-concentrated frequencies. Suppose
0\leq\alpha,\beta\leq d
satisfy
\beta\geq\alpha+m-d
. Then for
2\leq p\leq p_{m}:=\frac{2(m+1)}{m-1}
,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p}(Y)}\lessapprox\mathcal{A}_{d,m,\alpha,\beta}(R,Y)^{\frac{1}{m}\big(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{m}}\big)}M^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\bigg(\sum_{T\in\mathbb{W}}\|f_{T}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

When
\beta\leq m
and
d-\frac{m+1}{2}\leq\alpha\leq d
,
Theorem

1.7
yields sharp estimates for variants of the example in Proposition
1.6
; see
Section

2
.

We compare
Theorem

1.7
with the earlier result
[
DORZ23b
, Theorem 1.2(b)]
,

\|f\|_{L^{p}(Y)}\lessapprox R^{-\frac{1}{2}(d-\alpha)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{m}})}M^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\bigg(\sum_{T\in\mathbb{W}}\|f_{T}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}},

which was proved under the assumption that
Y
is
\alpha
-dimensional at the scale
R^{1/2}
:

\sup_{B_{\rho}}\rho^{-\alpha}|Y\cap B_{\rho}|\lesssim 1,\;\;\text{for}\;\;\rho=R^{1/2}.

This estimate is sharp in general for
d-\frac{m+1}{2}\leq\alpha\leq d
, and remains sharp for
\alpha\geq m
even under the stronger assumption that
Y
is
\alpha
-dimensional
up to scale

R
, that is,
\gamma_{\alpha,R}(Y)\lesssim 1
. Under this stronger assumption,
Theorem

1.7
yields

\|f\|_{L^{p}(Y)}\lessapprox R^{-\frac{1}{2}(d+1-\frac{m+1}{m}\alpha)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{m}})}M^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\bigg(\sum_{T\in\mathbb{W}}\|f_{T}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}},

which improves on the preceding estimate when
\alpha<m
.

Falconer distance set problem

We discuss implications of our weighted refined decoupling estimates for the Falconer distance set problem. Let
d\geq 2
and
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a compact set. Define the distance set of
E
by

\Delta(E)=\{|x-y|:x,y\in E\}.

Falconer
[
Fal85
]
proved that the distance set has positive Lebesgue measure whenever
\dim_{H}(E)>\frac{d+1}{2}
. It is conjectured that
|\Delta(E)|>0
under the weaker condition
\dim_{H}(E)>\frac{d}{2}
. Despite numerous partial results and significant advances, this conjecture remains open; see
[
GIOW20
,
DORZ23a
]
and references therein. Currently, the best-known thresholds are
\dim_{H}(E)>\frac{5}{4}
for
d=2

[
GIOW20
]
and

\dim_{H}(E)>\frac{d}{2}+\frac{d}{4d+2}

for
d\geq 3

[
DORZ23a
]
. In fact, these works prove a stronger result that there is a point
x\in E
for which the pinned distance set

\Delta_{x}(E)=\{|x-y|:y\in E\}

has positive Lebesgue measure.

A key ingredient in these works is a decomposition of a fractal measure into good and bad parts. In
[
DORZ23b
]
, the authors introduced a variant of this decomposition that simplifies the treatment of the bad part, at the cost of making the good part more difficult to handle, compared with
[
DORZ23a
]
. To deal with the good part, they established weighted refined decoupling estimates for functions with
(r,m)
-concentrated frequencies, recovering the results of
[
DORZ23a
]
for
d\geq 4
. On the other hand, in the case
d=3
, this method yielded a weaker threshold of
\dim_{H}(E)>1.757\cdots
, compared to
\dim_{H}(E)>\frac{12}{7}=1.714\cdots
obtained in
[
DORZ23a
]
.

It remains open whether the threshold

\dim_{H}(E)>\frac{12}{7}
can be recovered in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
within the framework of
[
DORZ23b
]
by further improving the decoupling estimates for functions with
(r,m)
-concentrated frequencies. Our weighted refined decoupling estimates provide partial progress in this direction.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a compact set. Then there is a point
x\in E
such that
|\Delta_{x}(E)|>0
whenever
\dim_{H}(E)>\frac{\sqrt{241}-5}{6}=1.754\cdots
.

Theorem

1.8
gives a modest improvement on the threshold from
[
DORZ23b
]
. However, it falls short of recovering the threshold obtained in
[
DORZ23a
]
.

We close the introduction by briefly discussing the proof of the main result,

Theorem

1.1
.
The argument is based on a weighted version of the standard broad–narrow analysis (see, e.g.,
[
BG11
,
Gut18
,
DZ19
]
), following
[
Kim25
]
. In the broad case, we combine refined decoupling
[
GIOW20
,
DORZ23b
]
with the multilinear Kakeya estimates
[

]
to obtain a multilinear estimate. This approach goes back to
[

,

]
in the setting of multilinear refined Strichartz estimates. In the narrow case, we use a weighted

estimate derived from refined decoupling at an intermediate scale, and then apply induction on scales, adapting ideas from
[
DZ19
,
DORZ23b
]
.

Throughout the proof, we work primarily with

estimates, which slightly simplifies the proof in both the broad and the narrow cases. In particular, our formulation of the multilinear weighted

estimate, inspired by
[
Dem20
]
, allows us to avoid the technical argument in
[
DZ19
]
that effectively replaces a product of integrals by an integral of a product.

Sharp examples

In this section, we prove sharpness results for
Theorem

1.7
and Proposition
1.6
. We use an example from
[
DORZ23b
]
, which is adapted from

]

[

]

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
d-\frac{m+1}{2}\leq\alpha\leq d
and
0\leq\beta\leq m
. Let
\{Q\}
be a collection of
R^{1/2}
-cubes which partition the slab
B^{d-m}_{R^{1/2}}(0)\times B^{m}_{R}(0)
. There exist a function
f=\sum_{T\in\mathbb{W}}f_{T}
with
(R^{-1/2},m)
-concentrated frequencies and a union of unit cubes
Y\subset B^{d-m}_{R^{1/2}}(0)\times B^{m}_{R}(0)
satisfying the following properties.

(i)

f(x,t)=U\psi(x,t)
for a function
\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d-1})
, where
U
is defined in (
1.1
).

(ii)

Y
is
\alpha
-dimensional up to scale
R^{1/2}
, i.e.,
\gamma_{\alpha,R^{1/2}}(Y)\sim 1
.

(iii)

\max_{B_{R^{1/2}}}|Y\cap B_{R^{1/2}}|\sim R^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}
, and
\overline{\gamma_{\beta,R}}(Y)R^{\beta}\sim|Y|\sim R^{\frac{\alpha+m}{2}}
.

(iv)

Each
R^{1/2}
-cube
Q\subset B^{d-m}_{R^{1/2}}(0)\times B^{m}_{R}(0)
is contained in at most
M
dilated tubes
\{2T\}_{T\in\mathbb{W}}
for
M\approx R^{\frac{d-\alpha}{p_{m}}}
.

(v)

For each
2\leq p\leq p_{m}
,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p}(Y)}\gtrapprox\mathcal{A}_{d,m,\alpha,\beta}(R,Y)^{\frac{1}{m}\big(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{m}}\big)}M^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}(\sum_{T\in\mathbb{W}}\|f_{T}\|_{L^{p}}^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Proof.

We recall the example from
[
DORZ23b
]
. Let

\kappa=\frac{d-\alpha}{2(m+1)}.

We note that
0\leq\kappa\leq\frac{1}{4}
for the range of
\alpha
under consideration.
For a small
0<c<1
, let
J=(R^{-\kappa}\mathbb{Z}^{m-1})\cap B^{m-1}_{c}(0)
. For each
j\in J
, let
\widehat{\psi_{j}}
be a smooth bump function which approximates the indicator function of the rectangle

O_{j}=B^{d-m}_{cR^{-1/2}}(0)\times B^{m-1}_{cR^{-1}}(j),

and take
\psi=\sum_{j\in J}\psi_{j}
.
We write
x=(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{d-m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m-1}
and
\xi=(\xi^{\prime},\xi^{\prime\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{d-m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m-1}
.
Define
f=U\psi
, so that
f=\sum_{j\in J}f_{j}
, where

f_{j}(x,t)=U\psi_{j}(x,t)=\varphi(R^{-1}t)c_{d}\int e^{i(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime},t)\cdot(\xi^{\prime},\xi^{\prime\prime},|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}+|\xi^{\prime\prime}|^{2})}\widehat{\psi_{j}}(\xi)d\xi.

Then
|f_{j}|\sim|O_{j}|
on the slab
B^{d-m}_{R^{1/2}}(0)\times B^{m}_{R}(0)
and
|f_{j}|
decays rapidly away from this slab. Thus, we have

\|f_{j}\|_{L^{p}}\sim|O_{j}|R^{\frac{d+m}{2p}}.

Let
f_{j}=\sum_{T\in\mathbb{T}_{j}}f_{T}
denote a wave packet decomposition of
f_{j}
. Let
\mathbb{W}=\cup_{j\in J}\mathbb{T}_{j}
. Then each
T\in\mathbb{W}
is parallel to the
m
-dimensional subspace
V=\{0\}^{d-m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Therefore,

f=\sum_{j\in J}f_{j}=\sum_{T\in\mathbb{W}}f_{T}

has
(R^{-1/2},m)
-concentrated frequencies. Moreover, every
Q
is contained in
\leq M
of the dilated tubes
\{2T\}_{T\in\mathbb{W}}
, where

M\approx\#J\sim R^{\kappa(m-1)}=R^{\frac{d-\alpha}{2(m+1)}(m-1)}=R^{\frac{d-\alpha}{p_{m}}}.

Let

Y=B^{d-m}_{R^{1/2}}(0)\times\Big(B^{m}_{R}(0)\cap\big((2\pi R^{\kappa}\mathbb{Z}^{m-1}\times 2\pi R^{2\kappa}\mathbb{Z})+B^{m}_{1}(0)\big)\Big).

Choosing
c>0
sufficiently small, we have, for
(x,t)\in Y
and

\xi\in\bigcup_{j}O_{j}
,

(x,t)\cdot(\xi,|\xi|^{2})=(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime},t)\cdot(\xi^{\prime},\xi^{\prime\prime},|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}+|\xi^{\prime\prime}|^{2})\in 2\pi\mathbb{Z}+[-0.01,0.01].

Thus,

|f(x,t)|\sim\#J|f_{j}(x,t)|\sim\#J|O_{j}|,\;\;(x,t)\in Y.

Note that

|Y|\sim R^{\frac{d-m}{2}}R^{(1-\kappa)(m-1)+1-2\kappa}=R^{\frac{d+m}{2}-\kappa(m+1)}=R^{\frac{m+\alpha}{2}}.

We have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p}(Y)}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\#J|O_{j}||Y|^{\frac{1}{p}}\sim\#J|O_{j}|R^{\frac{m+\alpha}{2p}}

\displaystyle M^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}(\sum_{T\in\mathbb{W}}\|f_{T}\|_{L^{p}}^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim M^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}(\sum_{j\in J}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{p}}^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}\approx(\#J)^{\frac{1}{2}}|O_{j}|R^{\frac{d+m}{2p}}.

Hence, we have the lower bound

(2.1)

\frac{\|f\|_{L^{p}(Y)}}{M^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}(\sum_{T\in\mathbb{W}}\|f_{T}\|_{L^{p}}^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}}\gtrapprox(\#J)^{\frac{1}{2}}R^{-\frac{d-\alpha}{2p}}\sim R^{-\frac{d-\alpha}{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{m}})}.

On the other hand, we have

|Y\cap B_{\rho}|\lesssim\begin{cases}\rho^{d-m}&\rho\in[1,R^{\kappa}],\\
\rho^{d-m}(\rho R^{-\kappa})^{m-1}\leq\rho^{d-\frac{m+1}{2}}&\rho\in[R^{\kappa},R^{2\kappa}],\\
\rho^{d-m}(\rho R^{-\kappa})^{m-1}(\rho R^{-2\kappa})=R^{-\frac{d-\alpha}{2}}\rho^{d}&\rho\in[R^{2\kappa},R^{1/2}].\end{cases}

It follows from these bounds that
Y
is
\alpha
-dimensional up to scale
R^{1/2}
. Moreover,

\gamma_{\alpha,R^{1/2}}(Y)\sim 1\;\text{ and }\;\max_{B_{R^{1/2}}}|Y\cap B_{R^{1/2}}|\sim R^{\alpha/2}.

For
R^{1/2}\leq\rho\leq R
,

\displaystyle|Y\cap B_{\rho}|\lesssim R^{\frac{d-m}{2}}(\rho R^{-\kappa})^{m-1}(\rho R^{-2\kappa})=R^{\frac{\alpha-m}{2}}\rho^{m}

and there exists

where the above inequality is tight. Thus, when

,

\overline{\gamma_{\beta,R}}(Y)R^{\beta}\sim R^{\frac{m+\alpha}{2}}\sim|Y|.

Combining these estimates, we get

\Big(\gamma_{\alpha,R^{1/2}}(Y)\overline{\gamma_{\beta,R}}(Y)R^{\beta}\sup_{B_{R^{1/2}}}|Y\cap B_{R^{1/2}}|^{m-1}R^{-\frac{m(d+1)}{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{m}\big(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{m}}\big)}\sim R^{-\frac{d-\alpha}{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{m}})},

which, combined with (
2.1
), verifies the claim. ∎

A special case of Proposition
2.1
yields Proposition
1.6
.

Proof of Proposition
1.6
.

Let
d=m=n+1
,
\alpha=\beta
and
p=2
. Consider the example from Proposition
2.1
. We have

\|U\psi\|_{L^{2}(Y)}\gtrapprox\Big(\gamma_{\alpha,R^{1/2}}(Y)\overline{\gamma_{\alpha,R}}(Y)R^{\alpha}\sup_{B_{R^{1/2}}}|Y\cap B_{R^{1/2}}|^{n}\Big)^{\frac{1}{(n+1)(n+2)}}R^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|U\psi\|_{L^{2}}.

Since

∎

In this section, we verify the weighted refined decoupling estimate,

Theorem 3.1

(Refined decoupling)

For the case

,

3.1
was obtained in

]
. When

.

Proof of
Theorem

1.7
.

Let
2\leq p\leq p_{m}
.
We may assume that
\Big(\sum_{T\in\mathbb{W}}\|f_{T}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}=1
. For dyadic
\lambda\in[R^{-100d},1]
, let
\mathbb{W}_{\lambda}:=\{T\in\mathbb{W}:\|f_{T}\|_{p}\sim\lambda\}
and

\lambda

Since

is a wave packet, we have

\|f_{T}\|_{L^{2}}\sim|T|^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\|f_{T}\|_{L^{p}}

.
By Hölder,

\|f_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}(Y)}\leq\|f_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(Y)}^{(m+1)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{m}})}\|f_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p_{m}}(\cup Q)}^{(m+1)(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}.

Applying

1.1
to
\|f_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(Y)}
and

3.1
to
\|f_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p_{m}}(\cup Q)}

∎

Following

,

,

, we derive a weighted estimate from

3.1
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
1\leq m\leq d
. Let
\mathbb{W}\subset\mathbb{T}(R)
and

be a sum of wave packets with
(ER^{-1/2},m)
-concentrated frequencies for some

Proof.

When
m=1

\mathbb{W}
as a union of
\mathbb{W}_{\theta}=\mathbb{W}\cap\mathbb{T}_{\theta}

where we used the fact that

is negligible outside of

and

\lessapprox 1

Combining these bounds yields the claim for

.

Now let
2\leq m\leq d
. Arguing as in the proof of
Theorem

1.7
, we may assume that
\|f_{T}\|_{2}
is comparable to a fixed constant for every

. By dyadic pigeonholing, there exist
M
and a collection
\{Q\}
of
R^{1/2}
-cubes in

, such that each
Q
is contained in
\sim M
of the dilated tubes

By Hölder and

,
we have

By (

\|f\|_{L^{2}(Y_{1})}\lessapprox E^{\frac{d-m}{m+1}}\Big(\frac{|Y_{1}|M}{|T|\#\mathbb{W}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{m+1}}\Big(\sum_{T\in\mathbb{W}}\|f_{T}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Finally, it suffices to observe that

\displaystyle|Y_{1}|M\sim\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}\sum_{T\in\mathbb{W}:Q\subset 2T}|Y\cap Q|\leq\sum_{T\in\mathbb{W}}|Y\cap 2T|\leq\#\mathbb{W}\max_{T\in\mathbb{W}}|Y\cap 2T|.

∎

.

Next, we establish a weighted

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
2\leq m\leq d
. Let
f=\sum_{T\in\mathbb{W}}f_{T}
be a sum of wave packets with
(r,m)
-concentrated frequencies.
Let
H
be a non-negative measurable function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
which is essentially

and satisfies

. Assume that
\gamma_{\alpha,r^{-2}}(H)\lesssim 1
. Then for any

\|f\|_{L^{2}(H)}\lessapprox\big(|T|^{-1}\max_{T\in\mathbb{W}}H(2T)\big)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}r^{(d+1-\frac{m+1}{m}\alpha)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{m}})}(r^{2}R)^{\frac{d+1}{2}(\frac{1}{p_{d}}-\frac{1}{p})}\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

Proof.

We may assume that
\|f_{T}\|_{L^{2}}
is comparable to a fixed constant for every

, arguing as in the proof of
Theorem

1.7
.

Since

is essentially constant at scale 1, it is comparable to a function which is constant on each lattice unit cube. Thus, without loss of generality, we may assume that
H
is constant on lattice unit cubes. For
\lambda>0
, let

denote the union of lattice unit cubes where

is comparable to
\lambda
. By dyadic pigeonholing, there exists a dyadic

such that

The term involving

is negligible and will be omitted in what follows.

It suffices to prove the estimate under the additional assumption that

Y_{\lambda}
is contained in a fixed
R
-cube
B_{R}
and each dilated tube
2T
intersects
B_{R}
for each
T\in\mathbb{W}
. Indeed, after partitioning

\mathbb{R}^{d}
into lattice
R
-cubes, one may apply the local estimate on each

. Summing up these local estimates yields the claimed bound.

We partition

by
R^{1/2}
-cubes. Then by dyadic pigeonholing, there exists

and a collection of
R^{1/2}
-cubes

such that each

is contained in

\|f\|_{L^{2}(Y_{\lambda})}\lessapprox\|f\|_{L^{2}(Y_{\lambda,M})},\;\text{ where }\;Y_{\lambda,M}=Y_{\lambda}\cap(\cup Q).

Note that

|Y_{\lambda,M}|\lesssim\lambda^{-1}H(Y_{\lambda,M}).

By Hölder,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{2}(H)}\lessapprox\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}|Y_{\lambda,M}|^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(Y_{\lambda,M})}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{1}{p}}H(Y_{\lambda,M})^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(Y_{\lambda,M})}.

By

3.4

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p}(Y_{\lambda,M})}\lessapprox

\displaystyle\gamma_{\alpha,r^{-2}}(Y_{\lambda,M})^{\frac{m+1}{m}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{m}})}

\displaystyle r^{(d+1-\frac{m+1}{m}\alpha)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{m}})}(r^{2}R)^{\frac{d+1}{2}(\frac{1}{p_{d}}-\frac{1}{p})}M^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\bigg(\sum_{T\in\mathbb{W}}\|f_{T}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Note that

\gamma_{\alpha,r^{-2}}(Y_{\lambda,M})\lesssim\lambda^{-1}\gamma_{\alpha,r^{-2}}(H)\lesssim\lambda^{-1}.

Combining these estimates and using (

), we get

(3.4)

\begin{split}\|f\|_{L^{2}(H)}\lessapprox\lambda^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{m+1}{m}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{m}})}\Big(\frac{H(Y_{\lambda,M})M}{|\mathbb{W}||T|}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}r^{(d+1-\frac{m+1}{m}\alpha)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{m}})}(r^{2}R)^{\frac{d+1}{2}(\frac{1}{p_{d}}-\frac{1}{p})}\|f\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

The exponent of

\frac{1}{p}-\frac{m+1}{m}\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{m}}\right)=\frac{1}{m}\left(\frac{m-1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right),

which is non-negative for the relevant range of

. Note that

H(Y_{\lambda,M})M\sim\sum_{Q}\sum_{T\in\mathbb{W}:Q\subset 2T}H(Y_{\lambda,M}\cap Q)\leq\sum_{T\in\mathbb{W}}H(Y_{\lambda,M}\cap 2T)\leq|\mathbb{W}|\max_{T\in\mathbb{W}}H(2T).

Combining these observations with (
3.4
) finishes the proof.
∎

Theorem

.

Q
is contained in
\sim M_{j}
dilated tubes
\{2T\}_{T\in\mathbb{W}_{j}}
for each
1\leq j\leq m
. Then

\#\mathcal{Q}\lessapprox\nu^{-O(1)}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\left(\frac{\#\mathbb{W}_{j}}{M_{j}}\right)^{1/(m-1)}.

We derive a multilinear estimate by combining the refined decoupling estimate with Proposition
5.2
, adapting the approach from
[
DGLZ18
]
and
[
Dem20
]
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
2\leq m\leq d
and let
U_{1},\ldots,U_{m}\subset\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
be
\nu
-transverse caps. For each
j=1,\cdots,m
, let
\mathbb{W}_{j}\subset\mathbb{T}(R)
be a collection of tubes whose directions lie in
U_{j}
. Let
f_{j}=\sum_{T\in\mathbb{W}_{j}}f_{T}
be a sum of wave packets with
(ER^{-1/2},m)
concentrated frequencies for some
E\geq 1
. Let
\mathcal{Q}
be a collection of disjoint
R^{1/2}
-cubes in
B_{R}
and
Y\subset\cup_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}Q
be a union of unit cubes in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{2}(Y\cap Q)}^{2/m}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lessapprox\nu^{-O(1)}E^{\frac{d-m}{m+1}}\Big(|Y|\max_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}|Y\cap Q|^{m-1}R^{-\frac{m(d+1)}{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{m(m+1)}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{m}}.

Proof.

Arguing as in the proof of
Theorem

1.7
, we may assume that, for each
1\leq j\leq m
,
\|f_{T}\|_{2}
is comparable to a fixed constant (which may depend on
j
) for every
T\in\mathbb{W}_{j}
.

By dyadic pigeonholing, we may find dyadic
M_{1},M_{2},\cdots,M_{m}\geq 1
and a subcollection
\mathcal{Q}_{1}\subset\mathcal{Q}
such that

\Big(\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{2}(Y\cap Q)}^{2/m}\Big)^{1/2}\lessapprox\Big(\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{1}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{2}(Y\cap Q)}^{2/m}\Big)^{1/2}

and each
Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{1}
is contained in
2T
for
\sim M_{j}
tubes
T\in\mathbb{W}_{j}
for every
j=1,2,\cdots,m
.

Let
Y_{1}=\cup_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{1}}Y\cap Q
. By Hölder and the proof of (
3.3
), we have

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{1}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{2}(Y\cap Q)}^{2/m}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{2}(Y_{1})}^{1/m}

\displaystyle\lessapprox E^{\frac{d-m}{m+1}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\Big(\frac{|Y_{1}|M_{j}}{|T|\#\mathbb{W}_{j}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{m(m+1)}}\Big(\sum_{T\in\mathbb{W}_{j}}\|f_{T}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2m}}.

Note that
\sum_{T\in\mathbb{W}_{j}}\|f_{T}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\sim\|f_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
.
By Proposition
5.2
, we have

\prod_{j=1}^{m}\frac{M_{j}}{\#\mathbb{W}_{j}}\lessapprox\nu^{-O(1)}(\#\mathcal{Q}_{1})^{-(m-1)}.

Combining these bounds, we get

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{2}(Y\cap Q)}^{2/m}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lessapprox\nu^{-O(1)}E^{\frac{d-m}{m+1}}\Big(|Y_{1}|^{m}(\#\mathcal{Q}_{1})^{-(m-1)}R^{-\frac{m(d+1)}{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{m(m+1)}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{m}}.

The claimed bound follows since

\displaystyle|Y_{1}|^{m}(\#\mathcal{Q}_{1})^{-(m-1)}=|Y_{1}|\Big(\frac{\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{1}}|Y\cap Q|}{\#\mathcal{Q}_{1}}\Big)^{m-1}\leq|Y|\max_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{1}}|Y\cap Q|^{m-1}.

∎

6.
Proof of
Theorem

1.1

To facilitate the induction argument, we prove a slightly more general version of
Theorem

1.1
with an additional parameter
E\geq 1
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
2\leq m\leq d
. Let
f=\sum_{T\in\mathbb{W}}f_{T}
be a sum of wave packets with
(ER^{-1/2},m)
-concentrated frequencies for some
E\geq 1
, and let
Y\subset B_{R}
be a union of unit cubes.
Suppose
0\leq\alpha,\beta\leq d
satisfy
\beta\geq\alpha+m-d
. Then for each
0<\epsilon<1
, there exists an absolute constant
C_{\epsilon}
which may depend on
\epsilon
and

for which

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{2}(Y)}\leq C_{\epsilon}R^{\epsilon}E^{\frac{d-m}{m+1}}\mathcal{A}_{d,m,\alpha,\beta}(R,Y)^{\frac{1}{m(m+1)}}\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

Theorem

1.1
is then a special case of
Theorem

6.1
for
E=1
.

The estimate is trivial if
Y
is empty. Thus, we assume that
Y
contains at least one unit cube. Accordingly, we have

(6.1)

\gamma_{\alpha,R^{1/2}}(Y)\geq 1,\;\;1\leq|Y|\leq\overline{\gamma_{\beta,R}}(Y)R^{\beta},\;\;\sup_{B_{R^{1/2}}}|Y\cap B_{R^{1/2}}|\geq 1.

Moreover, we may assume that
2T\cap Y
is non-empty for each
T\in\mathbb{W}
since the contribution of
f_{T}
outside
2T
is negligible.

For a given
0<\epsilon<1
, we choose
0<\epsilon_{1}\ll\epsilon
sufficiently small relative to
\epsilon
.
For
K=R^{\epsilon_{1}}
, consider a canonical covering of
N_{K^{-2}}(S)
by parallelepipeds
\tau
of dimensions
K^{-1}\times\cdots\times K^{-1}\times K^{-2}
.
For each
T\in\mathbb{W}
, there exist
\sim 1
rectangles
\tau
containing
2\theta(T)
, where
\theta(T)
denotes the
\theta
for which
T\in\mathbb{T}_{\theta}
. We fix one such
\tau=\tau(T)
for each
T\in\mathbb{W}
and let
\mathbb{W}_{\tau}=\{T\in\mathbb{W}:\tau(T)=\tau\}
, so that

f=\sum_{\tau}f_{\tau},\;\;\text{where}\;\;f_{\tau}=\sum_{T\in\mathbb{W}_{\tau}}f_{T}.

Let
N
denote the number of caps
\tau
; thus
N\sim K^{d-1}
. For each lattice
K^{2}
-cube
B
intersecting
Y
, define

S(B)=\{\tau:\|f_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}(B\cap Y)}\geq\frac{1}{2N}\|f\|_{L^{2}(B\cap Y)}\}.

The definition of
S(B)
ensures that for each
K^{2}
-cube
B
,

\|f\|_{L^{2}(B\cap Y)}\sim\|\sum_{\tau\in S(B)}f_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}(B\cap Y)}.

We say that a
K^{2}
-cube
B
intersecting
Y
is narrow if there exists a
(m-1)
-dimensional subspace
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that for every
\tau\in S(B)
,

\angle(G(\tau),V)\leq K^{-1},

where
G:S\to\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
is a choice of unit normal map and
\angle(G(\tau),V)
denotes the smallest angle between any non-zero vectors
u\in G(\tau)
and
v\in V
. Otherwise, we say that
B
is broad. If
B
is broad, then there exist
\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\cdots,\tau_{m}\in S(B)
such that

(6.2)

|v_{1}\wedge v_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{m}|\gtrsim K^{-(m-1)},

for any
v_{i}\in G(\tau_{i})
.

Let
\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{broad}}
denote the collection of broad
K^{2}
-cubes and
\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{narrow}}
denote the collection of narrow
K^{2}
-cubes. Then we have

Y=Y_{\operatorname{broad}}\bigsqcup Y_{\operatorname{narrow}},

where
Y_{\operatorname{broad}}
is the union of
B\cap Y
over
B\in\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{broad}}
and
Y_{\operatorname{narrow}}
is defined similarly. We say that we are in the broad case if
\|f\|_{L^{2}(Y_{\operatorname{broad}})}\geq\|f\|_{L^{2}(Y_{\operatorname{narrow}})}
. Otherwise, we say that we are in the narrow case. We handle each case in the following subsections.

6.1.
Broad case

In this subsection,
we write
A\lessapprox B
for estimates of the form
A\leq K^{O(1)}B
; such a loss is harmless as long as
K^{O(1)}\leq R^{\epsilon/2}
.

In the broad case, we have
\|f\|_{L^{2}(Y)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}(Y_{\operatorname{broad}})}
.
For each
B\in\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{broad}}
, there exist
\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\cdots,\tau_{m}\in S(B)
satisfying (
6.2
) for any
v_{i}\in G(\tau_{i})
. We fix such an
m
-tuple and denote
\bar{\tau}(B)=(\tau_{1},\cdots,\tau_{m})
. Let

\mathfrak{T}=\{\bar{\tau}(B):B\in\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{broad}}\}.

Since
\#\mathfrak{T}\lesssim K^{O(1)}
, by dyadic pigeonholing, there exist
\bar{\tau}=(\tau_{1},\cdots,\tau_{m})\in\mathfrak{T}
and a sub-collection
\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{broad}}^{1}\subset\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{broad}}
such that
\bar{\tau}(B)=\bar{\tau}
for all
B\in\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{broad}}^{1}
and

\|f\|_{L^{2}(Y_{\operatorname{broad}})}\lessapprox\|f\|_{L^{2}(Y_{\operatorname{broad}}^{1})},\text{ where }\;Y_{\operatorname{broad}}^{1}=\bigcup_{B\in\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{broad}}^{1}}B\cap Y.

Let
B\in\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{broad}}^{1}
. Since
\tau_{j}\in S(B)
for each
j
and
N\sim K^{d-1}\lessapprox 1
, we have

\|f\|_{L^{2}(Y\cap B)}^{2}\lessapprox\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|f_{\tau_{j}}\|_{L^{2}(Y\cap B)}^{2/m}.

Let
Q\subset B_{R}
be a lattice
R^{1/2}
-cube. Summing the estimate over
B\in\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{broad}}^{1}
contained in
Q
using Hölder, we obtain

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{2}(Y_{\operatorname{broad}}^{1}\cap Q)}^{2}\lessapprox\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|f_{\tau_{j}}\|_{L^{2}(Y_{\operatorname{broad}}^{1}\cap Q)}^{2/m}.

Summing this over
Q\subset B_{R}
by using the multilinear estimate,
Theorem

5.3
, we get

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{2}(Y)}\lessapprox E^{\frac{d-m}{m+1}}\Big(|Y|\max_{B_{R^{1/2}}}|Y\cap B_{R^{1/2}}|^{m-1}R^{-\frac{m(d+1)}{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{m(m+1)}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|f_{\tau_{j}}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{m}}.

Using (
6.1
) and
\|f_{\tau_{j}}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}}
, the desired bound follows in the broad case.

6.2.
Narrow case

In the narrow case, we proceed by induction on the scale
R
. Unlike in the broad case, this requires tracking powers of
K
throughout the argument. In the final step, we use the induction hypothesis at the scale
R/K^{2}
, which results in the gain of a factor of
K^{-2\epsilon}
. Therefore, the accumulated factor
K^{O(\epsilon_{1})}
is harmless provided that
\epsilon_{1}
is chosen sufficiently small so that
O(\epsilon_{1})\leq\epsilon
, which will be assumed from now on. Accordingly, throughout this section, we write
A\lessapprox B
to mean
A\leq c_{\epsilon_{1}}K^{O(\epsilon_{1})}B
for some absolute constant
c_{\epsilon_{1}}>0
.

For the induction-on-scale argument, we may assume that
R\gg_{\epsilon}1
, so that
c_{\epsilon_{1}}\leq K^{\epsilon}
, and the desired estimate holds for all scales up to
R/2
; for the base case
R\ll_{\epsilon}1
, the trivial bound
\|f\|_{L^{2}(Y)}\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}}
and (
6.1
) yield the claimed estimate.

6.2.1.
Decoupling over narrow
K^{2}
-cubes

We recall that

\|f\|_{L^{2}(B\cap Y)}\sim\|\sum_{\tau\in S(B)}f_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}(B\cap Y)}

for each
B\in\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{narrow}}
. For each
\tau\in S(B)
, consider a wave packet decomposition

f_{\tau}=\sum_{T_{1}\in\mathbb{T}_{\tau}}f_{T_{1}},

where
\mathbb{T}_{\tau}\subset\mathbb{T}(K^{2})
and each
T_{1}\in\mathbb{T}_{\tau}
has dimensions
K\times\cdots\times K\times K^{2}
with the long side orthogonal to
\tau
. We recall that
2T_{1}
denotes the concentric
K^{2\epsilon_{1}}
-dilate of
T_{1}
. Since we are concerned with
f_{\tau}
on
Y
, the contribution of
T_{1}\in\mathbb{T}_{\tau}
such that
2T_{1}
does not contain any unit cube from
Y
is negligible. Hence, after trimming wave packets if necessary, we may assume that
2T_{1}
contains at least one unit cube from
Y
, so
K^{-(d+1)}\leq|2T_{1}\cap Y|/|T_{1}|\lessapprox 1
for any
T_{1}\in\mathbb{T}_{\tau}
.

By dyadic pigeonholing, there exists a dyadic
\eta
with
K^{-(d+1)}\leq\eta\lessapprox 1
and a sub-collection
\mathbb{T}_{\tau,\eta}\subset\mathbb{T}_{\tau}
such that
|2T_{1}\cap Y|\sim\eta|T_{1}|
for every
T_{1}\in\mathbb{T}_{\tau,\eta}
and

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{2}(Y)}^{2}

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{narrow}}}\|\sum_{\tau\in S(B)}f_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}(B\cap Y)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\log K\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{narrow}}}\|\sum_{\tau\in S(B)}\sum_{T_{1}\in\mathbb{T}_{\tau,\eta}}f_{T_{1}}\|_{L^{2}(B\cap Y)}^{2}.

Let
\mathbb{T}(K^{2};B)
denote the collection of
T_{1}\in\cup_{\tau\in S(B)}\mathbb{T}_{\tau,\eta}
such that
2T_{1}
intersects
B
.
By the localization of
f_{T_{1}}
, the contribution of
T_{1}\notin\mathbb{T}(K^{2};B)
is negligible on
B
. Note that for each
B\in\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{narrow}}
,
\sum_{T_{1}\in\mathbb{T}(K^{2};B)}f_{T_{1}}
has
(O(K^{-1}),m-1)
-concentrated frequencies by the definition of narrow
K^{2}
-cubes and the fact that the angle between any two vectors in
G(\tau)
is
O(K^{-1})
. By
Theorem

3.2
, with
m
and
R
replaced by
m-1
and
K^{2}
, respectively, we have

\|\sum_{\tau\in S(B)}\sum_{T_{1}\in\mathbb{T}_{\tau,\eta}}f_{T_{1}}\|_{L^{2}(B\cap Y)}\lessapprox\eta^{\frac{1}{m}}\Big(\sum_{T_{1}\in\mathbb{T}(K^{2};B)}\|f_{T_{1}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

which yields

(6.3)

\|f\|_{L^{2}(Y)}^{2}\lessapprox\eta^{\frac{2}{m}}\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{narrow}}}\sum_{T_{1}\in\mathbb{T}(K^{2};B)}\|f_{T_{1}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

6.2.2.
Organizing small tubes
T_{1}

For each
\tau
, we cover

by finitely overlapping parallelepipeds
\square

with the long side perpendicular to
\tau

\square
is the

dilation of some

. We denote the collection of
\square
by
\mathbb{B}_{\tau}
and let
\mathbb{B}=\cup_{\tau}\mathbb{B}_{\tau}
. In addition, given
\square\in\mathbb{B}
, we denote by
\tau(\square)
the
\tau
for which
\square\in\mathbb{B}_{\tau}
.

For each
T_{1}\in\cup_{B\in\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{narrow}}}\mathbb{T}(K^{2};B)
, we fix
\square\in\mathbb{B}
such that
T_{1}\in\mathbb{T}_{\tau(\square)}
and
2T_{1}\subset\square
, and denote it by
\square(T_{1})
.
For each
\square\in\mathbb{B}
, let
\mathbb{T}_{\square}
denote the collection of all
T_{1}\in\cup_{B\in\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{narrow}}}\mathbb{T}(K^{2};B)
such that
\square(T_{1})=\square
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{narrow}}}\sum_{T_{1}\in\mathbb{T}(K^{2};B)}\|f_{T_{1}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lessapprox\sum_{\square}\sum_{T_{1}\in\mathbb{T}_{\square}}\|f_{T_{1}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

since
T_{1}\in\mathbb{T}(K^{2};B)
for
\lessapprox 1
narrow

Given the wave packet decomposition

\|f_{T_{1}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim_{\epsilon_{1}}\|f_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}(2T_{1})}^{2}+K^{-\frac{200d}{\epsilon_{1}}}\|f_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

We let

\displaystyle\sum_{\square}\sum_{T_{1}\in\mathbb{T}_{\square}}\|f_{T_{1}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

where we have used the fact that the dilated tubes

have
\lessapprox 1

Let
\mathbb{W}_{\square}
denote the collection of

intersects
\square

boxes

\|f\|_{L^{2}(Y)}^{2}\lessapprox\eta^{\frac{2}{m}}\sum_{\square}\|f_{\square}\|_{L^{2}(Y_{\square})}^{2}.

We also note the following which will be used later.

\sum_{\square}\|f_{\square}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\sim\sum_{\square}\sum_{T\in\mathbb{W}_{\square}}\|f_{T}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{T\in\mathbb{W}}\|f_{T}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\sim\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

6.2.3.
Parabolic scaling

We fix
\square\in\mathbb{B}_{\tau}
. We handle
\|f_{\square}\|_{L^{2}(Y_{\square})}^{2}
by parabolic scaling followed by induction on scale. We briefly recall the standard parabolic scaling argument (see e.g.
[
Gut16
]
) for completeness.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that the surface
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the graph of a smooth function
h
over
[-1,1]^{d-1}
such that
h(0)=\partial h(0)=0
and
\frac{1}{2}I\leq\partial^{2}h\leq 2I
on
[-1,1]^{d-1}
as quadratic forms.

We begin by giving a formal description of the covering of
S
by caps
\tau
of dimensions
K^{-1}\times\cdots K^{-1}\times K^{-2}
. Partition
[-1,1]^{d-1}
by cubes
\tau^{\prime}
of the form
c_{\tau^{\prime}}+[-K^{-1},K^{-1}]^{d-1}
. Then
\tau
can be identified with the image of
[-2,2]^{d}
under the affine transformation
A_{\tau}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
defined by

A_{\tau}(\zeta,t)=(c_{\tau^{\prime}}+K^{-1}\zeta,\,h(c_{\tau^{\prime}})+K^{-1}\partial h(c_{\tau^{\prime}})\cdot\zeta+K^{-2}t),\;\;(\zeta,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{R}.

Note that

A_{\tau}^{-1}(\omega,s)=\Big(K(\omega-c_{\tau^{\prime}}),K^{2}\big(s-h(c_{\tau^{\prime}})-\partial h(c_{\tau^{\prime}})\cdot(\omega-c_{\tau^{\prime}})\big)\Big).

Let
L_{\tau}
denote the transpose of the linear part of
A_{\tau}
:

L_{\tau}(x^{\prime},x_{d}):=(K^{-1}x^{\prime}+K^{-1}x_{d}\partial h(c_{\tau}^{\prime}),K^{-2}x_{d}),\;(x^{\prime},x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{R}.

Let
R_{1}
denote the new scale

R_{1}=R/K^{2}.

We have defined
\square
and
T_{1}\in\mathbb{T}_{\square}
so that they are translates of
L_{\tau}^{-1}(B_{R_{1}}(0))
and
L_{\tau}^{-1}(B_{1}(0))
, respectively.

For
\zeta\in\tau^{\prime}
, define

\tilde{h}(\zeta)=h(\zeta)-h(c_{\tau^{\prime}})-\partial h(c_{\tau^{\prime}})\cdot(\zeta-c_{\tau^{\prime}}).

Then

A_{\tau}^{-1}(\zeta,h(\zeta))=(K(\zeta-c_{\tau^{\prime}}),K^{2}\tilde{h}(\zeta)).

Hence, the affine map
A_{\tau}^{-1}
transforms
\{(\zeta,h(\zeta)):\zeta\in\tau^{\prime}\}\subset S
to the surface
S_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
defined as the graph of the function
h_{1}
, where

h_{1}(\omega)=K^{2}\tilde{h}(c_{\tau^{\prime}}+K^{-1}\omega),\;\;\omega\in[-1,1]^{d-1}.

The new surface
S_{1}
has properties similar to
S
. Indeed, note that
h_{1}(0)=\partial h_{1}(0)=0
,
\partial_{i}\partial_{j}h_{1}(\omega)=\partial_{i}\partial_{j}h(c_{\tau^{\prime}}+K^{-1}\omega)
, thus
\frac{1}{2}I\leq\partial^{2}h_{1}\leq 2I
on
[-1,1]^{d-1}
as quadratic forms.

Each
\theta\subset\tau
is transformed to
\theta_{\tau}:=A_{\tau}^{-1}\theta=A_{\tau}^{-1}A_{\theta}([-2,2]^{d})
. Let
c_{\theta_{\tau}^{\prime}}=K(c_{\theta^{\prime}}-c_{\tau^{\prime}})
.
The composition
A_{\tau}^{-1}A_{\theta}
is the affine transform

(A_{\tau}^{-1}A_{\theta})(\zeta,t):=(c_{\theta_{\tau}^{\prime}}+R_{1}^{-1/2}\zeta,\,h_{1}(c_{\theta_{\tau}^{\prime}})+R_{1}^{-1/2}\partial h_{1}(c_{\theta_{\tau}^{\prime}})\cdot\zeta+R_{1}^{-1}t),

which is associated with the canonical cap
\theta_{\tau}
of dimensions
R_{1}^{-1/2}\times\cdots\times R_{1}^{-1/2}\times R_{1}^{-1}
covering
N_{R_{1}^{-1}}(S_{1})
.

Let
\widehat{g_{T}}=\widehat{f_{T}}\circ A_{\tau}
. Then
g_{T}
is a wave packet associated with a tube in
\mathbb{T}(R_{1})
.
Indeed, under the parabolic scaling, a wave packet
f_{T}
localized to
T\in\mathbb{W}_{\square}
in physical space and
\theta\subset\tau
in frequency space is transformed into the wave packet
g_{T}
localized to
L_{\tau}(T)
and
\theta_{\tau}
, respectively. Moreover,

g_{\square}:=\sum_{T\in\mathbb{W}_{\square}}g_{T}

has
(O(E)R_{1}^{-1/2},m)
-concentrated frequencies. Indeed, if
V
is an
m
-dimensional subspace for which
\angle(v(T),V)\leq ER^{-1/2}
for
T\in\mathbb{W}
, then
\angle(v(L_{\tau}(T)),L_{\tau}(V))\lesssim KER^{-1/2}
for
T\in\mathbb{W}_{\square}
, which is a consequence of the following.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
u,\,v\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
be unit vectors and
L=L_{\tau}
. Then

\angle(Lu,Lv)\lesssim K\angle(u,v).

Proof.

We note that

\|Lu\|\geq\|L^{-1}\|^{-1},\;\;\|Lv\|\geq\|L^{-1}\|^{-1}.

Using the bound
\angle(x,y)\sim\|\frac{x}{\|x\|}-\frac{y}{\|y\|}\|\lesssim\frac{\|x-y\|}{\min(\|x\|,\|y\|)}
, we have

\angle(Lu,Lv)\lesssim\frac{\|Lu-Lv\|}{\min(\|Lu\|,\|Lv\|)}\lesssim\|L^{-1}\|\|L\|\|u-v\|\sim\|L^{-1}\|\|L\|\angle(u,v).

The claim follows from the bounds
\|L\|\lesssim K^{-1}
and
\|L^{-1}\|\lesssim K^{2}
.
∎

6.2.4.
Induction on scale

Fix
\square\in\mathbb{B}_{\tau}
.
Recall that
g_{\square}
is a sum of wave packets associated with tubes in
\mathbb{T}(R_{1})
and it has
(O(E)R_{1}^{-1/2},m)
-concentrated frequencies for
R_{1}=R/K^{2}
. Moreover,
L_{\tau}(Y_{\square})
is a union of unit cubes contained in the cube
L_{\tau}(\square)
of side length
R_{1}
. By the induction hypothesis, we have

(6.6)

\frac{\|f_{\square}\|_{L^{2}(Y_{\square})}}{\|f_{\square}\|_{L^{2}}}=\frac{\|g_{\square}\|_{L^{2}(L_{\tau}(Y_{\square}))}}{\|g_{\square}\|_{L^{2}}}\leq C_{\epsilon}R_{1}^{\epsilon}O(E)^{\frac{d-m}{m+1}}\mathcal{A}_{d,m,\alpha,\beta}(R_{1},L_{\tau}(Y_{\square}))^{\frac{1}{m(m+1)}}.

To bound the density parameter
\mathcal{A}_{d,m,\alpha,\beta}(R_{1},L_{\tau}(Y_{\square}))
, we use the following result.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
1\leq\rho\leq R_{1}
. Then

\sup_{B_{\rho}}|L_{\tau}(Y_{\square})\cap B_{\rho}|\lessapprox\eta^{-1}K^{-d}\sup_{B_{K\rho}}|Y\cap B_{K\rho}|.

Proof.

Note that
L_{\tau}^{-1}(B_{\rho})
is a parallelepiped of dimensions
K\rho\times\cdots\times K\rho\times K^{2}\rho
. Let
N
denote the number of tubes
T_{1}\in\mathbb{T}_{\square}
such that
2T_{1}
intersects
L_{\tau}^{-1}(B_{\rho})
. Then we have

(6.7)

|L_{\tau}(Y_{\square})\cap B_{\rho}|=|\det L_{\tau}||Y_{\square}\cap L_{\tau}^{-1}(B_{\rho})|\leq|\det L_{\tau}||2T_{1}|N\lessapprox N

since
|\det L_{\tau}|=K^{-(d+1)}
and
|2T_{1}|\lessapprox K^{d+1}
.

If
2T_{1}
intersects
L_{\tau}^{-1}(B_{\rho})
, then it is contained in
2L_{\tau}^{-1}(B_{\rho})
which denotes the
K^{2\epsilon_{1}}
-dilate of
L_{\tau}^{-1}(B_{\rho})
. Since the dilated tubes
\{2T_{1}\}
have
\lessapprox 1
overlap,
2L_{\tau}^{-1}(B_{\rho})
contains at least
\gtrapprox N
disjoint tubes
2T_{1}
with
T_{1}\in\mathbb{T}_{\square}
. Since
|Y\cap 2T_{1}|\sim\eta|T_{1}|\sim\eta K^{d+1}
for
T_{1}\in\mathbb{T}_{\square}
, we have

(6.8)

\eta K^{d+1}N\lessapprox|Y\cap 2L_{\tau}^{-1}(B_{\rho})|.

On the other hand, by covering the parallelepiped
2L_{\tau}^{-1}(B_{\rho})
by
\lessapprox K
cubes of side-length
K\rho
,

|Y\cap 2L_{\tau}^{-1}(B_{\rho})|\lessapprox K\sup_{B_{K\rho}}|Y\cap B_{K\rho}|.

Combined with (
6.7
) and (
6.8
), this estimate yields

\eta K^{d+1}|L_{\tau}(Y_{\square})\cap B_{\rho}|\lessapprox K\sup_{B_{K\rho}}|Y\cap B_{K\rho}|,

from which the claim follows.
∎

In particular, by the lemma, we have

\sup_{B_{R_{1}^{1/2}}}|L_{\tau}(Y_{\square})\cap B_{R_{1}^{1/2}}|\lessapprox\eta^{-1}K^{-d}\sup_{B_{R^{1/2}}}|Y\cap B_{R^{1/2}}|.

Moreover, since
\rho\leq R_{1}^{1/2}\iff K\rho\leq R^{1/2}
, we have

\displaystyle\gamma_{\alpha,R_{1}^{1/2}}\big(L_{\tau}(Y_{\square})\big)

\displaystyle\lessapprox\eta^{-1}K^{\alpha-d}\gamma_{\alpha,R^{1/2}}(Y)

\displaystyle\overline{\gamma_{\beta,R_{1}}}\big(L_{\tau}(Y_{\square})\big)

\displaystyle\lessapprox\eta^{-1}K^{\beta-d}\overline{\gamma_{\beta,R}}(Y).

Combining these bounds, we obtain

\mathcal{A}_{d,m,\alpha,\beta}(R_{1},L_{\tau}(Y_{\square}))\lessapprox\eta^{-(m+1)}K^{-\beta+\alpha+m-d}\mathcal{A}_{d,m,\alpha,\beta}(R,Y).

By the assumption
\beta\geq\alpha+m-d
, we have
K^{-\beta+\alpha+m-d}\leq 1
. Thus, by (
6.6
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{\|f_{\square}\|_{L^{2}(Y_{\square})}}{\|f_{\square}\|_{L^{2}}}

\displaystyle\lessapprox K^{-2\epsilon}C_{\epsilon}R^{\epsilon}E^{\frac{d-m}{m+1}}\eta^{-\frac{1}{m}}\mathcal{A}_{d,m,\alpha,\beta}(R,Y)^{\frac{1}{m(m+1)}}.

Recall that
\lessapprox K^{-2\epsilon}
denotes
\leq c_{\epsilon_{1}}K^{O(\epsilon_{1})}K^{-2\epsilon}
. Since we assume that
\epsilon_{1}
is sufficiently small relative to
\epsilon
and
R\gtrsim_{\epsilon}1
is sufficiently large, we may assume that

c_{\epsilon_{1}}K^{O(\epsilon_{1})}K^{-2\epsilon}\leq c_{\epsilon_{1}}K^{-\epsilon}\leq 10^{-d}.

Therefore, summing the square of the estimate on
\|f_{\square}\|_{L^{2}(Y_{\square})}
over
\square
using (
6.4
) and (
6.5
) yields

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{2}(Y)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\epsilon}R^{\epsilon}E^{\frac{d-m}{m+1}}\mathcal{A}_{d,m,\alpha,\beta}(R,Y)^{\frac{1}{m(m+1)}}\|f\|_{L^{2}},

which closes the induction.
