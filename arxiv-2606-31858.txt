The Logic of Data Access and Data Exchanges
Alexandru Baltag, Sonja Smets

Abstract
We investigate a new logic that extends Dynamic Epistemic Logic (DEL), by combining standard epistemic modalities K a ​ φ K_{a}\varphi and K A ​ φ K_{A}\varphi for (individual and distributed) propositional knowledge with operators K a φ ​ x K_{a}^{\varphi}x , K A φ ​ x K_{A}^{\varphi}x denoting (conditional) non-propositional knowledge of a number (in which an agent a a or a group A A have knowledge of the value of some variable x x , if given additional information φ \varphi ). We also generalize these operators, by considering formulas | x | A φ ≤ N |x|_{A}^{\varphi}\leq N (for any natural number N N ), saying that: conditional on φ \varphi , A A can narrow down the possible values of variable x x to at most N N possibilities . In order to name and compare such hypothetical values, we extend the logic further with definite descriptions based on minimization operators : μ N ​ x A φ \mu_{N}x_{A}^{\varphi} denotes the least of the N N possible values of x x (according to some fixed order ≤ \leq ) that are considered possible by group A A (given condition φ \varphi ). On this static base, we consider DEL-style extensions with dynamic modalities for general ‘data-exchange events’ (covering private and public propositional announcements, but also secret hacking of a private database, or public sharing of one’s data via open-source repositories, etc). In such scenarios, whole ‘chunks’ of information may be exchanged or modified: once access to a given source is gained, all the ‘data’ stored at that specific location becomes available. We give complete axiomatizations for the resulting logics, and prove their decidability and co-expressivity.

Introduction

Information may come in both propositional form (Boolean variables) and non-propositional form (e.g. numbers, names, addresses, pictures, videos, etc). Such
data

values of local variables
(e.g. a cryptographic key or a password), that are stored and processed in certain

agent

information that is stored at it).

Our proofs make an innovative use of known methods. For the static logic, we first use filtration to obtain a quasi-model: this is not a ‘real’ model, but just a syntactic construction, in which
variables have no values
(and the group relations are non-standard, as they are

5
5

5

In effect, the ‘values’ of variables

Defining the total order on values over the tree requires a use of the Order Extension Principle, and hence of the Axiom of Choice.
Finally, the completeness for the dynamic logic uses DEL-style reduction axioms; but again, the details are quite tantalizing. Even the soundness of some reduction laws (e.g., the ‘Cutoff-Min. Change’ axiom) is not at all obvious!

We should mention a self-imposed limitation: due to the page-limit, we chose

Distributed Location
For any

\mathcal{L}(p)=\mathcal{L}(v)\,\,=\,\,\{\epsilon\},\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\mathcal{L}(K_{A}\varphi)=\mathcal{L}(\mu_{N}x_{A}^{\varphi})\,\,=\,\,A,

\,\,\,\,\,\mathcal{L}(Px_{1}\ldots x_{n})=\mathcal{L}(F(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}))\,\,=\,\,\bigcup\{\mathcal{L}(x_{i}):1\leq i\leq n\},

Unravelling: the tree of histories
Let us fix a quasi-model
S
, a formula
\varphi_{0}
and a type
\Delta_{0}\in S
with
\varphi_{0}\in\Delta_{0}
. We will construct a model for
\varphi_{0}
, based on an unravelling of
S
around
\Delta_{0}
.
A

is a finite sequence
h=(\Delta_{0},A^{1},\Delta_{1},\ldots,A^{n},\Delta_{n})
of any length

, where
\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{n}\in S
are types and

A^{1},\ldots,A^{n}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{\Sigma}
are groups, such that we have

\Delta_{i-1}\sim_{A^{i}}\Delta_{i}
for all

H
be the set of all histories.
We denote by
last(h):=\Delta_{n}
the last state in history

, and by
\to_{A}
the natural
forward one-step relation
on histories in
H
, given by putting:
h\to_{A}h^{\prime}
iff
h^{\prime}=(h,A,\Delta^{\prime})
(with
last(h)\sim_{A}\Delta^{\prime}=last(h^{\prime})
). We denote by
\leftarrow_{A}
the
backward one-step relation
, defined as the converse of the forward relation:
h\leftarrow_{A}h^{\prime}
iff
h^{\prime}\to_{A}h
.
The one-step relations structure

into a
tree rooted at
\Delta_{0}
(with the immediate successor relation given by
h\to h^{\prime}
iff
h\to_{A}h^{\prime}
for some group

). In particular, we have
the tree property

every two nodes

of the tree are connected by a unique non-redundant path

(-in which neighboring nodes are immediate successors, in one order or another, and no nodes are repeated).

Epistemic relations on histories

To make this tree into a model for our restricted vocabulary
\mathcal{V}_{\Sigma}
, we define our
single-agent indistinguishability relations

\sim_{a}\subseteq H\times H
on histories, by putting

\sim_{a}\,\,:=\,\,\left(\bigcup_{A\ni a}\to_{A}\cup\bigcup_{A\ni a}\leftarrow_{A}\right)^{*},\vskip-5.69054pt

where
\leftarrow_{A}
is the converse of
\to_{A}
, the unions range over groups
A\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{\Sigma}
s.t.

, and
R^{*}
is the reflexive-transitive closure of
R
.
Since our goal is to build a standard model, the
group indistinguishability relations

are taken to be simply the
intersections

\sim_{A}\,\,:=\,\,\bigcap_{a\in A}\sim_{a}
of all the individual relations.

It is useful to give more concrete characterizations of the relations
\sim_{A}
(and
\sim_{a}
) on histories:

Lemma 5.10
.

The following are

, for

and histories

1.

;

2.

A\subseteq A^{i}
, for all groups

that appear as transition labels on the non-redundant path from

to
h^{\prime}

Proof.

Use the definitions of
\sim_{a}
and
\sim_{A}=\bigcap_{a\in A}\sim_{a}
on

Lemma 5.11
.

If

Proof.

The proof is by
induction on the length
N

from

to

For the

Inductive case

and
h^{\prime}
has length

on histories, we have either

(-since the non-redundant path from

∎

Lemma 5.22

(“Undefined-Minimum Lemma”)

Lemma 5.24
.

(“Truth Lemma”)

\underline{h}(x)_{\mathbf{M}}=[h,x]

Predicative Atoms

\varphi=P\overline{{x}}

\overline{{x}}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})

(ii)
Propositional atoms

\underline{h}(p)=\top_{\mathbf{D}}

(iii)
Basic variables

(iv)
Boolean Case

\varphi\to\psi

1

2

(v)
K_{A}

6
Completeness and Decidability Proofs for
LDAE

In this section we prove Theorem
4.6
. We first establish our co-expressivity result:
LDAE
and
LDA
are provably co-expressive
. For this, we need a few preliminary notions and results.

Reducible expressions

A formula
\theta
in
LDAE
is said to be
reducible
if it is provably equivalent to a ’static’ formula; i.e., if there exists some formula
\theta^{\prime}
in the static fragment
LDA
s.t.

\vdash\,\theta\leftrightarrow\theta^{\prime}

is provable in
\mathbf{LDAE}
. A term
x\in Var_{LDAE}
is
reducible
if it is provably equal to a static term; i.e., if there exists some
x^{\prime}\in Var_{LDA}
s.t.
\vdash\,x=x^{\prime}
is provable in
\mathbf{LDAE}
. Finally, an event
{\mathbf{e}}=({\mathbf{E}},e)
is
reducible
if all preconditions
pre_{f}
and all post-conditions of the form
\underline{e}(p)
or
\underline{e}(v)
are reducible (for all
f\in E
,
p\in Prop
and
v\in V
).

Lemma 6.1
.

(Replacement of Equivalents)

Suppose that
\vdash\varphi\leftrightarrow\varphi^{\prime}
,
\vdash\theta\leftrightarrow\theta^{\prime}
,
\vdash x=x^{\prime}
,
\vdash y=y^{\prime}
and
\vdash x_{i}=x^{\prime}_{i}
(for all
i=1,n
) are provable in
\mathbf{LDAE}
. Then the following are also provable in
\mathbf{LDAE}
:

1.

\vdash\varphi\!\!\to\!\!x|y=\varphi\!\!\to\!\!x^{\prime}|y^{\prime}

2.

\vdash F(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=F(x^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,x^{\prime}_{n})

3.

\vdash\mu_{N}x_{A}^{\varphi}=\mu_{N}(x^{\prime})_{A}^{\varphi^{\prime}}

4.

\vdash Px_{1}\ldots x_{n}\leftrightarrow Px^{\prime}_{1}\ldots x^{\prime}_{n}

5.

\vdash(\varphi\to\theta)\leftrightarrow(\varphi^{\prime}\to\theta^{\prime})

6.

\vdash K_{A}\varphi\leftrightarrow K_{A}\varphi^{\prime}

7.

\vdash[{\mathbf{e}}]\varphi\leftrightarrow[{\mathbf{e}}]\varphi^{\prime}

8.

\vdash{\mathbf{e}}(x)={\mathbf{e}}(x^{\prime})

We first prove a preliminary “one-step reduction” result:

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
{\mathbf{e}}
be any reducible event.
Then, for every ’static’ formula
\theta
in
LDA
and every ’static’ term
x\in Var_{LDA}
, the formula
[{\mathbf{e}}]\theta
and the term
{\mathbf{e}}(x)
are also reducible.

Proof.

Since
{\mathbf{e}}=({\mathbf{E}},e)
is reducible, its precondition
pre_{e}
is also reducible. Let us fix some static formula
\rho
s.t.
\vdash pre_{e}\leftrightarrow\rho
is provable in
\mathbf{LDAE}
.
We’ll prove both claims simultaneously, by induction on sub-expression complexity (-and we’ll make liberal use of Lemma
6.1
, without explicitly mentioning it):

For
\theta:=p
: since
e
is reducible, there exists some static formula
\theta^{\prime}
s.t.
\vdash\underline{e}(p)\leftrightarrow\theta^{\prime}
.
Using the Change of Facts axiom, we can see that
[{\mathbf{e}}]p
is provably equivalent to
\rho\to\theta^{\prime}
.

For
\theta:=Px_{1}\ldots x_{n}
: by the induction hypothesis, for all
i=1,n
we have
\vdash{\mathbf{e}}(x_{i})=x^{\prime}_{i}
for some static terms
x^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,x^{\prime}_{n}\in Var_{LDA}
. Using also the Indiscernability axiom and the Property Change reduction axiom,
[{\mathbf{e}}]\theta
is provably equivalent to
pre_{e}\to Px^{\prime}_{1}\ldots x^{\prime}_{n}
, and hence also to
\rho\to Px^{\prime}_{1}\ldots x^{\prime}_{n}
.

For
\theta\,:=\,\varphi\to\psi
is similar: by induction, there exist static formulas
\varphi_{e}
and
\psi_{e}
s.t.
\vdash[{\mathbf{e}}]\varphi\leftrightarrow\varphi^{\prime}
and
\vdash[{\mathbf{e}}]\psi\leftrightarrow\psi^{\prime}
are provable. Using the
[{\mathbf{e}}]
-Distributivity axiom, we obtain
\vdash[{\mathbf{e}}]\theta\leftrightarrow(\varphi^{\prime}\to\psi^{\prime})
.

For
\theta:=K_{A}\varphi
: by induction, for each
f\in E
there exists some static formula
\varphi_{f}
s.t.
\vdash[\mathbf{f}]\varphi\leftrightarrow\varphi_{f}
. Putting this together with the Knowledge Update axiom, we get
\vdash[{\mathbf{e}}]\theta\leftrightarrow\bigwedge\{\rho\to K_{\underline{e}(A)}\varphi_{f}:f\sim_{A}e\})
.

For
x:=v
(basic variable): since
e
is reducible, there exists some static term
x^{\prime}
s.t.
\vdash\underline{e}(v)=x^{\prime}
. Using this and the Value Change axiom, we obtain that
\vdash{\mathbf{e}}(x)\,=\,(\rho\!\!\to\!\!x^{\prime}|\top)
, so
{\mathbf{e}}(x)
is reducible.

For
x:=F(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
: by the induction hypothesis, there exist static terms
x^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,x^{\prime}_{n}
s.t.
\vdash{\mathbf{e}}(x_{i})=x^{\prime}_{i}
for all
i\leq n
. Using the Functional Change reduction axiom, we obtain
\vdash{\mathbf{e}}(x)\,=\,(\rho\!\!\to\!\!F(x^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,x^{\prime}_{n}))
.

For
x:=\varphi\!\!\to\!\!y|z
: by the induction hypothesis, there exist static terms
y^{\prime},z^{\prime}
and static formula
\varphi^{\prime}
, s.t.

\vdash{\mathbf{e}}(y)=y^{\prime}
,
\vdash{\mathbf{e}}(z)=z^{\prime}
and
\vdash[{\mathbf{e}}]\varphi\leftrightarrow\varphi^{\prime}
are provable. Using these, as well as the Case Change reduction axiom, we obtain
\vdash{\mathbf{e}}(x)\,=\,(\varphi^{\prime}\!\!\to\!\!y^{\prime}|z^{\prime})
, and so
{\mathbf{e}}(x)
is reducible.

For
x:=\mu_{N}y_{A}^{\varphi}
: first note that by definition, the reducibility of
{\mathbf{e}}=({\mathbf{E}},e)
implies the reducibility of
all

\mathbf{f}=({\mathbf{E}},f)
with
f\in E
.
By the induction hypothesis and Proposition
4.5
, for every
f\in E
there exist a static formula
\varphi_{f}
and a static term
y_{f}
, s.t.
\vdash\langle{\mathbf{f}}\rangle\varphi\leftrightarrow\varphi_{f}
and
\vdash{\mathbf{f}}(y)=y_{f}
.
Thus, if we put

\eta\,\,\,:=\,\,\,\{n_{N}(y_{f})_{\underline{f}(A)}^{\varphi_{f}}:f\sim_{A}e,n\leq N\}^{\Diamond}\leq N,

then
\vdash\eta\leftrightarrow Val_{e}^{N}(x_{A}\varphi)^{\Diamond}\leq N
is provable in
\mathbf{LDAE}
. Using the Min. Val. Change axiom,
{\mathbf{e}}(\mu_{N}y_{A}^{\varphi})
is provably equal to

\rho\!\!\to\!\!\left(\eta\!\!\to\!\!min\,\{\mu_{N}(y_{f})_{\underline{f}(A)}^{\varphi_{f}}:f\sim_{A}e\}|\bot\right)
.
∎

Using this, we can establish our full reduction result:

Lemma 6.3
.

(Co-expressivity of
LDAE
and
LDA
)

All expressions (formulas, terms and events)
\alpha
of
LDAE
are reducible. As a consequence,
LDAE
and
LDA
have the same expressive power.

Proof.

Induction on the subexpression-complexity of the expression
\alpha
.
The base cases
\alpha:=p\in Prop
and
\alpha:=v\in V
are trivial.
The cases
\alpha:=\varphi\!\!\to\!\!x|y
,
\alpha:=F(\overline{{x}})
,
\alpha:=\mu_{N}x_{A}^{\varphi}
,
\alpha:=P\overline{{x}}
,
\alpha:=\varphi\to\theta
and
\alpha:=K_{A}\varphi
are straightforward: use the induction hypothesis and parts 1-6 of Lemma
6.1
.

For
\alpha:=[{\mathbf{e}}]\varphi
: by induction,
{\mathbf{e}}
and
\varphi
are reducible, so there exists static
\varphi^{\prime}
s.t.
\vdash\varphi\leftrightarrow\varphi^{\prime}
. By Lemma
6.1
.7,
\vdash\alpha\leftrightarrow[{\mathbf{e}}]\varphi^{\prime}
, and by Lemma
6.2

[{\mathbf{e}}]\varphi^{\prime}
is reducible (since
{\mathbf{e}}
is reducible and
\varphi^{\prime}
is static), so
\vdash[{\mathbf{e}}]\varphi^{\prime}\leftrightarrow\varphi^{\prime\prime}
for some static
\varphi^{\prime\prime}
. Using transitivity of equivalence, we get
\vdash\alpha\leftrightarrow\varphi^{\prime\prime}
, so
\alpha
is reducible.

For
\alpha:={\mathbf{e}}(x)
: by induction,
{\mathbf{e}}
and
x
are reducible, so there exists a static term
x^{\prime}
s.t.
\vdash x=x^{\prime}
. By Lemma
6.1
.8, we have
\vdash\alpha={\mathbf{e}}(x^{\prime})
, and by Lemma
6.2

{\mathbf{e}}(x^{\prime})
is reducible (since
{\mathbf{e}}
is reducible and
x^{\prime}
is static), so
\vdash{\mathbf{e}}(x^{\prime})=x^{\prime\prime}
for some static term
x^{\prime\prime}
. Using transitivity of equality, we obtain
\vdash\alpha=x^{\prime\prime}
.

Finally, the case
\alpha:={\mathbf{e}}=({\mathbf{E}},e)
is straightforward: for all
f\in E
,
p\in Prop
and
v\in V
, the preconditions
pre_{f}
and postconditions
\underline{f}(p)
and
\underline{f}(v)
are
<{\mathbf{e}}
in the subexpression complexity order, so they are all reducible (by the induction hypothesis), and hence
{\mathbf{e}}
is also reducible (by definition).
∎

Proof of
Theorem
4.6
: Completeness follows immediately from Lemma
6.3
, the soundness of
\mathbf{LDAE}
, and the completeness of the system
\mathbf{LDA}
(Theorem
4.2
); decidability follows from the decidability of the static logic
LDA
(Theorem
4.2
) and the co-expressivity of
LDA
and
LDAE
(Lemma
6.3
).

7
Conclusions and Future Work

In this paper we axiomatize a decidable but richly expressive logic, that deals with a large class of data-exchange events, while capturing group knowledge of both propositional and non-propositional data, as well as a group’s ability to narrow down the values of a variable to finitely many possibilities.

There are a number of things still left to do.
As already mentioned, one
can add common knowledge operators
C_{A}\varphi
, but pre-encoding their dynamics requires a generalization to polyadic conditionals
C_{A}^{e}\varphi
as in
[
12
]
.
We leave this for a journal version, where we also plan to explore the relationships of our formalism with the Logic of Functional Dependence
[
14
]
and Graded (Multi)Modal Logic.

Our axioms are somewhat complicated due to the fact that we did
not
succeed to prove FMP (Finite Model Property) for our logics.
This would have allowed us to replace cutoff-minimization
\mu_{N}x_{A}^{\varphi}
by
plain minimization
min\,x_{A}^{\varphi}

(over all
A
-possible
x
-values given
\varphi
), which would greatly simplify our axioms.
On the other hand, we
don’t
have a counterexample to FMP, so this issue is still an open question.
