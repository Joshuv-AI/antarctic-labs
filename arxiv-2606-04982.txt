Convergence of parallel overlapping domain decomposition methods with impedance boundary conditions for time-harmonic Maxwell equations in heterogeneous media
Luyu Cen, Shihua Gong, Euan A. Spence, Yue Yu

Abstract
This paper analyzes the convergence of parallel overlapping domain-decomposition methods with impedance boundary conditions for the time-harmonic Maxwell equations in heterogeneous media. We prove that the parallel iterative method is well-posed in an appropriate function space, and characterize the error propagation operator through impedance-to-impedance maps that describe interactions between neighboring subdomains. For strip domain decompositions, we derive explicit convergence estimates in terms of the norms of the impedance-to-impedance maps. At the discrete level, we develop the finite-element counterpart of these results based on Nédélec-element discretisations. Under the assumption that the discrete impedance-to-impedance maps approximate their continuous counterparts as the mesh is refined, we show that the discrete method inherits the convergence behavior of the continuous method. We illustrate this theory with numerical experiments for strip domain decompositions, and also present numerical experiments for checkerboard domain decompositions that go beyond our theory.

Introduction

Context: the numerical solution of the time-harmonic Maxwell equations

The time-harmonic Maxwell equations model electromagnetic wave propagation in a wide range of applications, including radar, wireless communications, and medical imaging. At high frequencies, however, their numerical solution becomes particularly challenging. Indeed, to resolve the oscillations in the solution, one needs to use sufficiently fine meshes, leading to very large discrete systems. Furthermore, these systems are typically non-Hermitian and highly indefinite, making the design of scalable and robust iterative solvers a difficult task. The situation is even more complicated in heterogeneous media, where spatially varying material parameters further affect both the underlying propagation of the wave and its numerical approximation.

Existing theory of two-level domain-decomposition methods for Maxwell.

By the discussion in the last paragraph, the linear systems arising from the FEM grow at least like
k^{d}
as
k\to\infty

One class of DD methods for which there exists rigorous theory are two-level methods; i.e., subdomain solves (on overlapping subdomains) are combined with a global coarse solve.
Convergence theory for two-level methods

The subject of this paper: theory of one-level domain-decomposition methods for Maxwell.

This paper is concerned with the theory of one-level overlapping domain-decomposition methods for the time-harmonic Maxwell equations, for which, to our knowledge, there is no existing convergence theory (at either at the continuous or the discrete level).

It is important to emphasise that the character of one-level methods is very different from that of the two-level methods described above. Indeed, in two-level methods, the coarse space must resolve the propagative behaviour of the waves (otherwise the behaviour is worse than that of the corresponding one-level method; see, e.g.,
[
6

We emphasise that, when using a two-level method, the coarse problem is usually still very large, and so one often solves it

To further illustrate the difference between one- and two-level methods, we note that the theory of two-level methods described above is all based on showing that the field of values of the preconditioned system does not contain the origin, with GMRES convergence results then following from the Elman’s estimate

The contribution of this paper.

This paper studies a parallel overlapping Schwarz method with impedance boundary conditions on subdomain interfaces (see Definition

At the discrete level, this method can be viewed as a particular type of

The paper

This paper studies of one-level Maxwell DD methods with impedance transmission conditions from the perspective introduced (in the Helmholtz context) in

The main contributions of this paper are as follows.

•

We develop a PDE-level framework for parallel overlapping Schwarz methods with impedance transmission conditions for time-harmonic Maxwell equations in
variable-coefficient
media. We prove well-posedness of the iteration for general Lipschitz subdomains (using the harmonic-analysis results of
[
39
,
26
,
13
]
), derive the subdomain error equations, and show that the resulting error propagation operator can be expressed in terms of impedance-to-impedance maps between artificial interfaces.

•

For strip decompositions, we give sufficient conditions on the operator norms of these impedance-to-impedance maps for the parallel Schwarz method to converge, thereby reducing the analysis of the convergence to bounds on interface maps rather than on the full error propagation operator directly. Analogous formulations are available for more general decompositions, at the cost of more complicated interface geometries (see
[
20
, §3.2 and §6.2]
).

•

At the discrete level, we show that the Nédélec finite element discretization of the parallel Schwarz method can be interpreted as a restricted additive Schwarz (RAS-imp) method, and we formulate the discrete error propagation operator in terms of discrete impedance-to-impedance maps. Under the assumption that the discrete maps approximate their continuous counterparts as
h\to 0
, we prove that the discrete method inherits the convergence behavior of the continuous one.

•

We provide numerical experiments for two-dimensional Maxwell problems that validate the power contractivity of the discrete error propagation operator, illustrate the boundedness of the impedance-to-impedance maps, and demonstrate the effectiveness of RAS-imp as both a stationary iteration and a Krylov preconditioner for strip and checkerboard decompositions in homogeneous and heterogeneous media.

Definition 2.1

(Maxwell interior impedance problem)
.

Given a bounded, simply connected, Lipschitz open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
,

L^{\infty}

\mu,\epsilon
, and
\lambda
satisfying

0<\mu_{min}\leq\mu(\mathbf{x})\leq\mu_{max}<\infty,\hskip 10.00002pt0<\epsilon_{min}\leq\epsilon(\mathbf{x})\leq\epsilon_{max}<\infty\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for a.e. }\mathbf{x}\in\Omega

(2.2)

0<\lambda_{\min}\leq\lambda(\mathbf{x})\leq\lambda_{\max}<\infty\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ for a.e. }\mathbf{x}\in\partial\Omega,

(2.3)

and data

\left\{\begin{aligned} \curl(\mu^{-1}\curl\mathbf{u})-k^{2}\epsilon\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{f}~~&\text{ in }\Omega,\\

(2.4)

The variational form of problem (

) is: find

such that

where the sesquilinear form

(2.7)

\mathbf{n}\times\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{n}\times\nabla v+\mathbf{n}\times\mathbf{w}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ and }\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{n}\cdot\nabla v+\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{w}.

Since
\mathbf{w}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(D)
, both
\mathbf{n}\times\mathbf{w}
and
\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{w}
are in
{\bf L}^{2}(\partial D)
by the standard trace theorem (see, e.g.,
[
33
, Theorem 3.37]
)
and the fact that
\mathbf{n}\in{\bf L}^{\infty}(\partial D)
(by, e.g.,
[
33
, Page 96]
). Part (a) of Theorem
2.7
therefore implies Part (a) of Theorem
2.6
.
Finally
v\in H^{3/2}(D)
if and only if
v\in H^{1}(D)
with
\nabla v\in H^{1/2}(D)
if and only if
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbf{H}^{1/2}(D)
, so that (since
\mathbf{u}-\nabla\phi=\mathbf{w}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(D)
) Part (b) of Theorem
2.7
implies Part (b) of Theorem
2.6
.
∎

2.3
Boundedness of impedance maps

The follow result is a consequence of Lemma
2.3
and Corollary
2.5
, and is needed for the well-posedness results in the next subsection.

Lemma 2.8

(Boundedness of impedance maps)
.

Let
D
be a bounded, simply connected, open set, with
\partial D
Lipschitz and connected, and let
D^{\prime}
be a Lipschitz subdomain of
D
.
Let
\mu,\epsilon\in W^{1,\infty}(D)
satisfy (
2.2
), and let
\lambda\in L^{\infty}(D)
satisfy (
2.3
). Given
k>0
, there exists
C,C^{\prime}>0
such that the following is true.

(i) For all

\mathbf{u}\in\bm{U}(D)
,

\|\mu^{-1}\curl\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{n}-{\mathrm{i}}k\lambda\mathbf{u}_{T}\|_{0,\partial D^{\prime}}\leq C\|\mathbf{u}\|_{\bm{U}(D^{\prime})}\leq C^{\prime}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{\bm{U}(D)}.

(2.15)

(ii) For all
\mathbf{u}\in\bm{U}_{0}(D)
,

\|\mu^{-1}\curl\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{n}-{\mathrm{i}}k\lambda\mathbf{u}_{T}\|_{0,\partial D^{\prime}}\leq C\|\mu^{-1}\curl\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{n}-{\mathrm{i}}k\lambda\mathbf{u}_{T}\|_{0,\partial D}.

Proof.

For the first bullet point, the first inequality follows from the definition (
2.8
) of the norm on
\bm{U}(D)
and the second inequality follows from Corollary
2.5
.
For the second bullet point, by (
2.15
) we only need to show that, for
\mathbf{u}\in\bm{U}_{0}(D)
(defined by (
2.9
)),

\|\mathbf{u}\|_{\bm{U}(D)}\leq C\|\mu^{-1}\curl\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{n}-{\mathrm{i}}k\lambda\mathbf{u}_{T}\|_{0,\partial D},

which follows from (
2.11
).
∎

2.4
The parallel Schwarz method and its wellposedness

We decompose
\Omega
into
N
overlapping open Lipschitz subdomains
\Omega_{\ell}
, that is
\overline{\Omega}=\cup_{\ell=1}^{N}\overline{\Omega_{\ell}}
.
Let
\{\chi_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{N}
be a partition of unity (PoU) of
\Omega
subordinate to the open cover
\{\Omega_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{N}
; i.e.,

\begin{gathered}\operatorname{supp}\chi_{\ell}\subset\overline{\Omega_{\ell}},\quad 0\leq\chi_{\ell}(\mathbf{x})\leq 1\mbox{ for }\mathbf{x}\in\overline{\Omega_{\ell}},\quad\sum_{\ell}\chi_{\ell}(\mathbf{x})=1\mbox{ for }\mathbf{x}\in\overline{\Omega},\end{gathered}

(2.16)

and assume, additionally, that each
\chi_{\ell}\in C^{0,1}(\overline{\Omega_{\ell}})=W^{1,\infty}(\Omega_{\ell})
.

Definition 2.9

(Parallel Schwarz method with impedance boundary condition)
.

Let
\mu,\epsilon,\lambda,\mathbf{f},
and
\mathbf{g}
be as in the Maxwell interior impedance problem of Definition
2.1
.
Given
\{\Omega_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{N}
and
\lambda\in L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)
, let
\widetilde{\lambda}\in L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega_{\ell})
for all
\ell=1,\ldots,N
, be such that
\widetilde{\lambda}=\lambda
on
\partial\Omega
.

Given

\mathbf{u}^{n}
, define
\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}
by

\left\{\begin{aligned} &(\curl(\mu^{-1}\curl)-k^{2}\epsilon)\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}=\mathbf{f}\quad\text{ in }\Omega_{\ell},\\
&(\mu^{-1}\curl\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}-{\mathrm{i}}k\lambda\mathbf{n}_{\ell}\times\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1})\times\mathbf{n}_{\ell}=\mathbf{g}_{T}\quad\text{ on }\partial\Omega_{\ell}\cap\partial\Omega,\\
&(\mu^{-1}\curl\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}-{\mathrm{i}}k\widetilde{\lambda}\mathbf{n}_{\ell}\times\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1})\times\mathbf{n}_{\ell}=(\mu^{-1}\curl\mathbf{u}^{n}-{\mathrm{i}}k\widetilde{\lambda}\mathbf{n}_{\ell}\times\mathbf{u}^{n})\times\mathbf{n}_{\ell}\quad\text{ on }\partial\Omega_{\ell}\backslash\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(2.17)

and then define
\mathbf{u}^{n+1}
by

\mathbf{u}^{n+1}:=\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\chi_{\ell}\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}.

(2.18)

Observe that the parallel Schwarz method can be viewed
either
as taking
\mathbf{u}^{n}
to
\mathbf{u}^{n+1}

(as in Definition
2.9
)

or
as taking

\bm{\mathfrak{u}}^{n}=(\mathbf{u}_{1}^{n},\mathbf{u}_{2}^{n},\cdots,\mathbf{u}_{N}^{n})
to

\bm{\mathfrak{u}}^{n+1}=(\mathbf{u}_{1}^{n+1},\mathbf{u}_{2}^{n+1},\cdots,\mathbf{u}_{N}^{n+1})

(with the boundary value problems defining
\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}
involving
\mathbf{u}^{n}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\chi_{\ell}\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n}
).

We now give two well-posedness results for the parallel Schwarz method, corresponding to each of these two interpretations. The result involving
\bm{\mathfrak{u}}^{n}
requires the following notation: let

\mathbb{U}:=\prod_{\ell=1}^{N}\bm{U}(\Omega_{\ell})\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbb{U}_{0}:=\prod_{\ell=1}^{N}\bm{U}_{0}(\Omega_{\ell})

(2.19)

equipped with the norm
\|\cdot\|^{2}_{\mathbb{U}}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\|\cdot\|^{2}_{\bm{U}(\Omega_{\ell})}
.

Theorem 2.10

(Well-posedness of the parallel Schwarz method in
\mathbb{U}
)
.

Let
\mu,\epsilon,\lambda,\mathbf{f},
and
\mathbf{g}
be as in the Maxwell interior impedance problem of Definition
2.1
. Suppose
\Omega_{\ell},~\ell=1,2,\cdots,N
are bounded Lipschitz open sets, and let
\widetilde{\lambda}
be as in Definition
2.9
.
Suppose further that each
\Omega_{\ell}
is simply connected with connected boundary.
Given
\bm{\mathfrak{u}}^{n}=(\mathbf{u}_{1}^{n},\mathbf{u}_{2}^{n},\cdots,\mathbf{u}_{N}^{n})\in\mathbb{U}(\Omega)
, let
\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}(\ell=1,2,\cdots,N)
be defined by (
2.17
). Then
\bm{\mathfrak{u}}^{n+1}\in\mathbb{U}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

By the definition of
\mathbb{U}
(
2.19
),
we need to show that
\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}\in\bm{U}(\Omega_{\ell})
for all
\ell=1,\ldots,N
. Since
\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}
satisfies the PDE (
2.17
), by Lemma
2.3
, it is sufficient to show that

(\mu^{-1}\curl\mathbf{u}^{n}-{\mathrm{i}}k\widetilde{\lambda}\mathbf{n}_{\ell}\times\mathbf{u}^{n})\times\mathbf{n}_{\ell}\in{\bf L}^{2}_{T}(\partial\Omega_{\ell}\setminus\partial\Omega)
.
Since
\mathbf{u}^{n}:=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\chi_{j}\mathbf{u}_{j}^{n}
,

\displaystyle(\mu^{-1}\curl\mathbf{u}^{n}-{\mathrm{i}}k\widetilde{\lambda}\mathbf{n}_{\ell}\times\mathbf{u}^{n})\times\mathbf{n}_{\ell}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt=\bigg(\mu^{-1}\curl\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\chi_{j}\mathbf{u}_{j}^{n}\bigg)-{\mathrm{i}}k\widetilde{\lambda}\mathbf{n}_{\ell}\times\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\chi_{j}\mathbf{u}_{j}^{n}\big)\big)\times\mathbf{n}_{\ell}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\bigg(\mu^{-1}(\nabla\chi_{j}\times\mathbf{u}_{j}^{n}+\chi_{j}\curl\mathbf{u}_{j}^{n})-{\mathrm{i}}k\widetilde{\lambda}\mathbf{n}_{\ell}\times(\chi_{j}\mathbf{u}_{j}^{n})\bigg)\times\mathbf{n}_{\ell}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\chi_{j}(\mu^{-1}\curl\mathbf{u}_{j}^{n}-{\mathrm{i}}k\widetilde{\lambda}\mathbf{n}_{\ell}\times\mathbf{u}_{j}^{n})\times\mathbf{n}_{\ell}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mu^{-1}(\nabla\chi_{j}\times\mathbf{u}_{j}^{n})\times\mathbf{n}_{\ell}.

(2.20)

By Lemma
2.8
(i),

\mathbf{u}_{j}^{n}\in\bm{U}(\Omega_{j})
has an
L^{2}
impedance trace on any Lipschitz interface in
\Omega_{j}
; therefore
(\mu^{-1}\curl\mathbf{u}_{j}^{n}-{\mathrm{i}}k\widetilde{\lambda}\mathbf{n}_{\ell}\times\mathbf{u}_{j}^{n})\times\mathbf{n}_{\ell}\in{\bf L}^{2}_{T}(\partial\Omega_{\ell}\setminus\partial\Omega).

To deal with the second term on the right-hand side of (
2.20
), first observe that, by assumption,
\mathbf{u}_{j}^{n}\in{\bf U}(\Omega_{j})={\bf W}(\Omega_{j})
by Theorem
2.4
, so that
\mathbf{u}_{j}^{n}\in\mathbf{H}^{1/2}(\Omega_{j})
by the definition of
{\bf W}(\Omega_{j})
(
2.12
). Thus, for any
\ell
such that
\partial\Omega_{\ell}\cap\Omega_{j}\neq\emptyset
, Theorem
2.6
implies that
\mathbf{u}_{j}^{n}\in{\bf L}^{2}_{T}(\partial\Omega_{\ell}\setminus\partial\Omega)
(since

\mathbf{u}_{j}^{n}=(\mathbf{n}_{\ell}\times\mathbf{u}_{j}^{n})\times\mathbf{n}_{\ell}+(\mathbf{n}_{\ell}\cdot\mathbf{u}_{j}^{n})\mathbf{n}_{\ell}
on
\partial\Omega_{\ell}\setminus\partial\Omega
).
Since
\chi_{j}\in C^{1,1}(\overline{\Omega_{j}})=W^{1,\infty}(\Omega_{j})
,
\nabla\chi_{j}\in L^{\infty}
and thus

(\nabla\chi_{j}\times\mathbf{u}_{j}^{n})\times\mathbf{n}_{\ell}\in{\bf L}^{2}_{T}(\partial\Omega_{\ell}\setminus\partial\Omega)
.

We have therefore shown that

(\mu^{-1}\curl\mathbf{u}^{n}-{\mathrm{i}}k\widetilde{\lambda}\mathbf{n}_{\ell}\times\mathbf{u}^{n})\times\mathbf{n}_{\ell}\in{\bf L}^{2}(\partial\Omega_{\ell}\setminus\partial\Omega)
and the proof is complete.
∎

The second well-posedness result requires a regularity assumption.

Assumption 2.11

(Regularity of
\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}
)
.

\{\Omega_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{N},\mu,\epsilon,\lambda,

{\bf g}_{T}
, and
\{\chi_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{N}
are such that the solution
\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}
of (
2.17
) is such that
(\nabla\chi_{\ell}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}\in{\bf L}^{2}(\Omega_{\ell})
.

We first prove a well-posedness result under Assumption
2.11
, and then discuss why Assumption
2.11
is required and when we expect it to hold (see Remark
2.13
,
Conjecture
2.14
, and the associated discussion below).

Theorem 2.12

(Well-posedness of the parallel Schwarz method in
{\bf U}(\Omega)
under Assumption
2.11
)
.

Let
\mu,\epsilon,\mathbf{f},
and
\mathbf{g}
be as in the Maxwell interior impedance problem of Definition
2.1
.
Suppose, additionally, that

•

each
\chi_{\ell}\in C^{1,1}(\overline{\Omega_{\ell}})
for all
\ell=1,\ldots,N
,

•

\mu,\epsilon\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
,

•

\mathbf{f}\in\mathbf{H}(\difdiv,\Omega)
,

•

\{\Omega_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{N},\mu,\epsilon,\lambda,

{\bf g}_{T}
, and
\{\chi_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{N}
are such that Assumption
2.11
holds,

•

each
\Omega_{\ell}
is simply connected with connected boundary, and

•

\widetilde{\lambda}
is as in Definition
2.9
.

Then, given
\mathbf{u}^{n}\in{\bf U}(\Omega)
,
\mathbf{u}^{n+1}
defined by Definition
2.9
is in
{\bf U}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.8
(i) and the fact that
\mathbf{u}^{n}\in{\bf U}(\Omega)
(by assumption), the impedance data in the problem (
2.17
) defining
\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}
is in
{\bf L}^{2}(\partial\Omega_{j})
.
We now apply Lemma
2.3
to obtain that
\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}\in{\bf U}(\Omega_{j})
; the assumptions of Lemma
2.3
are satisfied with
\Omega
replaced by
\Omega_{j}
by the fact about the impedance data noted above, along with the assumptions that
\difdiv{\bf f}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,

\mu,\epsilon\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
, and
\widetilde{\lambda}
is as in Definition
2.9
.

We now need to show that
\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}:=\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\chi_{\ell}\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}
is in
{\bf U}(\Omega)
.
By linearity, it is sufficient to show that
\chi_{\ell}\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}\in{\bf U}(\Omega)
for all
\ell=1,\ldots,N
.

Since
\mu\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega_{j})
,
{\bf U}(\Omega_{j})={\bf W}(\Omega_{j})
by Theorem
2.4
, and thus
\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}
and
\curl\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}
are both in
\mathbf{H}^{1/2}(\Omega_{j})
.
Recall that the product of a
C^{0,1}
function and an
H^{1/2}
function is in
H^{1/2}
by,
e.g.,
[
23
, Theorem 1.4.1.1]
.
Thus, since
\chi
and
\nabla\chi
are both
C^{0,1}
,
\chi_{\ell}\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}
and
\curl(\chi_{\ell}\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1})=\nabla\chi_{\ell}\times\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}+\chi_{\ell}\curl\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}
are both in
\mathbf{H}^{1/2}(\Omega_{\ell})
and hence (by extension by zero) in
\mathbf{H}^{1/2}(\Omega)
. By
Theorem
2.4
and the definition of
{\bf W}(\Omega)
(
2.12
), it therefore remains to show that

\curl\bigl(\mu^{-1}\curl(\chi_{\ell}\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1})\bigr)\in{\bf L}^{2}(\Omega)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ and }\hskip 10.00002pt\difdiv(\chi_{\ell}\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1})\in L^{2}(\Omega).

Since
\chi_{\ell}
is supported in
\Omega_{\ell}
,
it is sufficient to show that

\curl\bigl(\mu^{-1}\curl(\chi_{\ell}\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1})\bigr)\in{\bf L}^{2}(\Omega_{\ell})\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ and }\hskip 10.00002pt\difdiv(\chi_{\ell}\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1})\in L^{2}(\Omega_{\ell}).

For the condition on the divergence,
taking the divergence of the PDE in (
2.17
) and using
the assumption that
\difdiv\mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, we see that

\difdiv(\epsilon\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1})\in L^{2}(\Omega)
; since
\epsilon\geq\epsilon_{\min}>0
by (
2.2
), this implies that
\difdiv\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}\in L^{2}(\Omega)

and then
\difdiv(\chi_{\ell}\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1})\in L^{2}(\Omega)
by the product rule for differentiation and the fact that
\chi\in C^{0,1}(\overline{\Omega})=W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
.
Now

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\curl\bigl(\mu^{-1}\curl(\chi_{\ell}\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1})\bigr)

\displaystyle=\curl\bigl(\chi_{\ell}\mu^{-1}\curl\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}\bigr)+\curl\bigl(\mu^{-1}\nabla\chi_{\ell}\times\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}\bigr)

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt=\nabla\chi_{\ell}\times\bigl(\mu^{-1}\curl\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}\bigr)+\chi_{\ell}\curl\bigl(\mu^{-1}\curl\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}\bigr)+\nabla\mu^{-1}\times\bigl(\nabla\chi_{\ell}\times\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}\bigr)

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt+\mu^{-1}\curl\bigl(\nabla\chi_{\ell}\times\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}\bigr).

The PDE (
2.17
) implies that
\curl\bigl(\mu^{-1}\curl\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}\bigr)\in{\bf L}^{2}(\Omega_{\ell})
. Since
\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1},\curl\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}\in{\bf L}^{2}(\Omega)
, to show that

\curl(\mu^{-1}\curl(\chi_{\ell}\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}))\in{\bf L}^{2}(\Omega)
we only need to show that

\curl\bigl(\nabla\chi_{\ell}\times\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}\big)=(\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}\cdot\nabla)\nabla\chi_{\ell}-(\nabla\chi_{\ell}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}+(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1})\nabla\chi_{\ell}-(\Delta\chi_{\ell})\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}

is in
{\bf L}^{2}(\Omega)
.
However,
since
\nabla\chi_{\ell}\in C^{0,1}(\overline{\Omega})=W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
,
and by Assumption
2.11
,
all the terms in the last displayed equation are in
{\bf L}^{2}(\Omega)
and the proof is complete.
∎

Remark 2.13

(The origin of Assumption
2.11
)
.

In the proof of Theorem
2.12
, we saw that

\curl\bigl(\mu^{-1}\curl(\chi_{\ell}\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1})\bigr)-\chi_{\ell}\curl\big(\mu^{-1}\curl\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}\big)

(2.21)

involved
(\nabla\chi_{\ell}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}
– i.e., first derivatives of
\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}
– and such derivatives are not controlled by
\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}
belonging to
{\bf U}(\Omega_{\ell})
.

We highlight that this issue is not present in the Helmholtz setting, since the Helmholtz analogue of
{\bf U}(\Omega)
,
U(\Omega):=\{u\in H^{1}(\Omega):\Delta u\in L^{2}(\Omega),\partial u/\partial n\in L^{2}(\partial\Omega)\}
, contains
H^{1}(\Omega)
, and so controls the first derivatives in

\Delta(\chi_{\ell}u_{\ell}^{n+1})-\chi_{\ell}\Delta u_{\ell}^{n+1}
(see
[
20
, Lemma 2.11]
, with the well-posedness result in
\bm{U}(\Omega)
then
[
20
, Theorem 2.12]
).

Discussion on when we expect Assumption
2.11
to hold.

Without specific assumptions on the geometry of the subdomains and the partition of unity
\{\chi_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{N}
, the term

(\nabla\chi_{\ell}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}

involves arbitrary first derivatives of
\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}
.
However,
\mathbf{H}^{1}
regularity of Maxwell solutions requires that the impedance data be
\mathbf{H}^{1/2}

(since the impedance boundary condition involves the tangential trace, which is in
\mathbf{H}^{1/2}
if the function is in
\mathbf{H}^{1}
) and this will not be the case for
\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}
because of the lack of compatibility
between
{\bf g}_{T}
and the impedance trace of
\mathbf{u}^{n}

in (
2.17
) when
\partial\Omega_{\ell}\setminus\partial\Omega
meets
\partial\Omega_{\ell}\cap\partial\Omega
.

Conjecture 2.14
.

Suppose that
(i)

\Omega:=\big\{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\,|\,x_{1}\in[0,L],\,(x_{2},x_{3})\in\Omega_{p}\big\},

where
\Omega_{p}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a Lipschitz polygon, (ii) each
\chi_{\ell}
is a function of only the
x_{1}
variable, and (iii) each
\chi_{\ell}
is zero in a neighbourhood of
\partial\Omega_{\ell}\setminus\partial\Omega
.
Then Assumption
2.11
holds.

The assumption in
Conjecture
2.14
that each
\chi_{\ell}
is a function of only the
x_{1}
variable essentially restricts attention to the strip decompositions studied from §
3.2
onward, where
\Omega_{\ell}
is only overlapped by
\Omega_{\ell-1}
and
\Omega_{\ell+1}
(strictly speaking, Conjecture
2.14
allows more overlaps, but in practice these would be redundant).

Proving Conjecture
2.14
is beyond the scope of this paper, but we now outline why we expect it to hold.

In the set up of Conjecture
2.14
,
each

{\rm supp}(\nabla\chi_{\ell})
is an annular-type region whose boundary intersects
\partial\Omega_{\ell}
only on
\partial\Omega_{\ell}\cap\partial\Omega
; i.e., in this region (where we care about the regularity of
\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}
) the impedance data for
\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}
only comes from
{\bf g}_{T}
(and so there is not the matching issue described above).

The Maxwell solution
\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}\in{\bf L}^{2}(\Omega_{\ell})
by wellposedness of the interior impedance problem (Lemma
2.2
).
\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}
has weak singularities near the edges of
\Omega_{\ell}
created by the corners of the 2-d polygon
\Omega_{p}
; i.e.,
\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}\sim r^{\alpha}
for
\alpha
depending on
\Omega_{p}
, where
r
is the distance to the corner of
\Omega_{p}
measured in polar coordinates in the
(x_{2},x_{3})
plane.
However, one expects the derivatives of
\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}
in the
x_{1}
direction to have the same regularity as
\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}
itself (since differentiating in the
x_{1}
direction will not affect the
r^{\alpha}
asymptotics); i.e., we expect
(\nabla\chi_{\ell}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}_{\ell}^{n+1}\in{\bf L}^{2}(\Omega_{\ell})
.

For perfect electric conductor boundary conditions, singularities of Maxwell solutions were famously characterised in terms of singularities of Laplace solutions in
[
12
,
10
]
, and global Maxwell regularity results were obtained from corresponding Laplace ones in, e.g.,
[
11
, §4.5d]
,
[
37
, Theorem 5.5.5]
. We expect that these type of arguments can also be used to prove Conjecture
2.14
.

2.5
A norm on the space
\bm{U}_{0}(D)

The following result assumes that the impedance parameter
\lambda
is constant; recall that this is natural when the impedance boundary condition is considered as a first-order approximation to the radiation condition. Without loss of generality, we set
\lambda
to one.

Theorem 2.15

(Norm on
\bm{U}_{0}(D)
)
.

Suppose that
\mu
and
\epsilon
satisfy the assumptions of Lemma
2.2
, and, additionally,
\lambda=1
. Then

\|\mathbf{u}\|^{2}_{1,k,\partial D}:=\|\mu^{-1}\curl\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{n}\|^{2}_{0,\partial D}+k^{2}\|\mathbf{u}_{T}\|^{2}_{0,\partial D}

is a norm on
\bm{U}_{0}(D)

equivalent to
\|\cdot\|_{\bm{U}(D)}
(
2.8
).
Furthermore,

\|\mathbf{u}\|_{1,k,\partial D}^{2}=\|\pm\mu^{-1}\curl\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{n}-ik\mathbf{u}_{T}\|_{0,\partial D}^{2}.

(2.22)

Proof.

For
\mathbf{u}\in\bm{U}_{0}(D)
and
\lambda=1
,

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle\int_{D}(\curl(\mu^{-1}\curl\mathbf{u})-k^{2}\epsilon\mathbf{u})\cdot\overline{\mathbf{u}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{D}(\mu^{-1}\curl\mathbf{u}\cdot\curl\overline{\mathbf{u}}-k^{2}\epsilon|\mathbf{u}|^{2})+\int_{\partial D}\mathbf{n}\times\mu^{-1}\curl\mathbf{u}\cdot\overline{\mathbf{u}}_{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{D}(\mu^{-1}|\curl\mathbf{u}|^{2}-k^{2}\epsilon|\mathbf{u}|^{2})+\int_{\partial D}\mathbf{n}\times\mu^{-1}\curl\mathbf{u}\cdot\overline{\mathbf{u}}_{T},

so that

\Im\left(\int_{\partial D}\mathbf{n}\times\mu^{-1}\curl\mathbf{u}\cdot\overline{\mathbf{u}}_{T}\right)=0.

Thus,

\displaystyle\|\pm\mu^{-1}\curl\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{n}-ik\mathbf{u}_{T}\|_{0,\partial D}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial D}(|\mu^{-1}\curl\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{n}|^{2}+k^{2}|\mathbf{u}_{T}|^{2})

\displaystyle\mp 2k\Im\left(\int_{\partial D}\mu^{-1}\curl\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{n}\cdot\overline{\mathbf{u}}_{T}\right)

\displaystyle=\|\mu^{-1}\curl\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{n}\|^{2}_{0,\partial D}+k^{2}\|\mathbf{u}_{T}\|^{2}_{0,\partial D}.

For the norm equivalence, by the triangle inequality and the definition of

,

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}\|_{1,k,\partial D}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\mu^{-1}\curl\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{n}-ik\mathbf{u}_{T}\|_{0,\partial D}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mu^{-1}\curl\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{n}\|^{2}_{0,\partial D}+k^{2}\|\mathbf{u}_{T}\|^{2}_{0,\partial D}\leq C\|\mathbf{u}\|_{\bm{U}(D)}^{2}.

Furthermore, for

∎

5.3
The discrete error propagation operator

In this subsection, we derive an operator representation of the error
propagation for the discrete parallel Schwarz iteration. The construction is
based on the local error equation and the associated discrete Maxwell-harmonic
spaces.

Define the local discrete errors and global discrete error by

\mathbf{e}_{h,\ell}^{n}:=\mathbf{u}_{h}|_{\Omega_{\ell}}-\mathbf{u}_{h,\ell}^{n}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbf{e}_{h}^{n}:=\mathbf{u}_{h}-\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n}.

(5.18)

The following proposition gives the local error equation, where the new local
error is driven by the previous global error through
b_{h,\ell}

Proposition 5.7

(Variational characterisation of the discrete error)
.

Given
\mathbf{e}_{h}^{n}
,
the local discrete error
\mathbf{e}_{h,\ell}^{n+1}

satisfies the variational problem

a_{\Omega_{\ell}}(\mathbf{e}_{h,\ell}^{n+1},\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell})=b_{h,\ell}(\mathbf{e}_{h}^{n},\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell})\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ for all }\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell}\in\bm{U}_{h,\ell},

and then

\mathbf{e}_{h}^{n+1}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{h,\ell}^{\top}\mathbf{e}_{h,\ell}^{n+1}.

Proof.

By the definition of
\mathbf{e}_{h,\ell}^{n+1}
(
5.18
),

a_{\Omega_{\ell}}(\mathbf{e}_{h,\ell}^{n+1},\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell})=a_{\Omega_{\ell}}(\mathbf{u}_{h}|_{\Omega_{\ell}}-\mathbf{u}_{h,\ell}^{n+1},\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell})\\
=a_{\Omega_{\ell}}((\mathbf{u}_{h}-\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n})|_{\Omega_{\ell}},\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell})-a_{\Omega_{\ell}}(\mathbf{u}_{h,\ell}^{n+1}-\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n}|_{\Omega_{\ell}},\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell}).

For the second term on the right-hand side, by (
5.14
), the definition of
b_{h,\ell}
(
5.11
) and the fact that
\mathbf{u}_{h}
is the solution to the discrete variational problem,

a_{\Omega_{\ell}}(\mathbf{u}_{h,\ell}^{n+1}-\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n}|_{\Omega_{\ell}},\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell})=a(\mathbf{u}_{h}-\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n},\mathcal{R}^{\top}_{h,\ell}\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell})\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ for all }\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell}\in\bm{U}_{h,\ell}.

Thus,

\displaystyle a_{\Omega_{\ell}}(\mathbf{e}_{h,\ell}^{n+1},\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell})=a_{\Omega_{\ell}}((\mathbf{u}_{h}-\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n})|_{\Omega_{\ell}},\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell})-a(\mathbf{u}_{h}-\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n},\mathcal{R}^{\top}_{h,\ell}\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell})=b_{h,\ell}(\mathbf{e}_{h}^{n},\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell}),

by (
5.11
).
Finally, by (

\mathbf{e}_{h}^{n+1}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{h,\ell}^{\top}(\mathbf{u}_{h}|_{\Omega_{\ell}}-\mathbf{u}_{h,\ell}^{n+1})=\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{h,\ell}^{\top}\mathbf{e}_{h,\ell}^{n+1}.

∎

\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell}\in\bm{U}_{h,\ell}\cap\mathbf{H}_{0,\Gamma_{\ell}}(\curl,\Omega_{\ell})

(\bm{\phi}_{\Gamma,m})_{T}\neq 0
on
\Gamma

\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{\Gamma,\ell}\in\bm{U}_{h,\ell}

of
\mathbf{u}_{\Gamma}

.

For
\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell}\in\bm{U}_{0,h,\ell}
, the discrete impedance data satisfies

\langle{\rm imp}^{h}_{\Gamma_{\ell}}(\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell}),(\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell})_{T}\rangle_{\Gamma_{\ell}}=a_{\Omega_{\ell}}(\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell},\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell})\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for all }\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell}\in\bm{U}_{h,\ell}.

Proof.

For any
\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell}\in\bm{U}_{h,\ell}
, let
\mathbf{w}_{\Gamma_{\ell}}\in{\bf U}_{h,T}(\Gamma_{\ell})
be defined as
(\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell})_{T}
on
\Gamma_{\ell}
.

\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell}-\hat{\mathbf{w}}_{\Gamma_{\ell},\ell}\in\mathbf{H}_{0,\Gamma_{\ell}}({\rm curl},\Omega_{\ell})
. Since
\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell}\in\bm{U}_{0,h,\ell}
,
a_{\Omega_{\ell}}(\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell},\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell})=a_{\Omega_{\ell}}(\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell},\hat{\mathbf{w}}_{\Gamma_{\ell},\ell})+a_{\Omega_{\ell}}(\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell},\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell}-\hat{\mathbf{w}}_{\Gamma_{\ell},\ell})=a_{\Omega_{\ell}}(\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell},\hat{\mathbf{w}}_{\Gamma_{\ell},\ell}).
By Definition
5.12
,
a_{\Omega_{\ell}}(\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell},\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell})=a_{\Omega_{\ell}}(\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell},\hat{\mathbf{w}}_{\Gamma_{\ell},\ell})=\langle{\rm imp}^{h}_{\Gamma_{\ell}}(\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell}),(\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell})_{T}\rangle_{\Gamma_{\ell}}
.
∎

The last proposition establishes that for discrete Maxwell-harmonic functions, the action of the local bilinear form is fully characterized by the impedance data on the interface.
Based on this observation, we are now ready to formally define the discrete Maxwell-harmonic norm as follows.

Definition 5.14

(Discrete Maxwell-harmonic norm)
.

We define the norms on
\bm{U}_{0,h,\ell}
and
\mathbb{U}_{0,h}
by

\|\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell}\|_{\bm{U}_{0,h,\ell}}:=\|\iimp_{\Gamma_{\ell}}^{h}\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell}\|_{0,\Gamma_{\ell}}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\|\mathfrak{v}_{h}\|_{\mathbb{U}_{0,h}}:=\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\|\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell}\|_{\bm{U}_{0,h,\ell}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

where
\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell}\in\bm{U}_{0,h,\ell}
and
\mathfrak{v}_{h}:=(\mathbf{v}_{h,1},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{h,N})^{\top}\in\mathbb{U}_{0,h}
.

We next record how this data is
supported on the two sides of the interface.

Lemma 5.15
.

For
\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell}\in\bm{U}_{0,h,\ell}^{+}
,

{\rm imp}^{h}_{\Gamma_{\ell}}(\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell})=\bm{0}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{on }\Gamma_{\ell}^{-}.

For
\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell}\in\bm{U}_{0,h,\ell}^{-}
,

{\rm imp}^{h}_{\Gamma_{\ell}}(\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell})=\bm{0}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{on }\Gamma_{\ell}^{+}.

Proof.

We prove the first equation; the proof of the second is similar.
Since
\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell}\in\bm{U}_{0,h,\ell}^{+}
,
a_{\Omega_{\ell}}(\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell},\hat{\mathbf{w}}_{\Gamma_{\ell}^{-},\ell})=0
for any
\mathbf{w}_{\Gamma_{\ell}^{-}}\in\bm{U}_{h,T}(\Gamma_{\ell}^{-})
. By definition of the discrete impedance data (Definition
5.12
),
\langle{\rm imp}^{h}_{\Gamma_{\ell}}(\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell}),\mathbf{w}_{\Gamma_{\ell}^{-}}\rangle_{\Gamma_{\ell}^{-}}=0
. We thus have
{\rm imp}^{h}_{\Gamma_{\ell}}(\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell})=\bm{0}
on
\Gamma_{\ell}^{-}
.
∎

Let
s\in\{+,-\}
. For
\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell}\in\bm{U}_{0,h,\ell}^{s}
, we define

{\rm imp}^{h}_{\Gamma_{\ell}^{s}}(\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell}):={\rm imp}^{h}_{\Gamma_{\ell}}(\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell})|_{\Gamma_{\ell}^{s}}.

(5.23)

This one-sided support property allows us to decompose a discrete
Maxwell-harmonic function into two components, corresponding to the impedance
data on
\Gamma_{\ell}^{-}
and
\Gamma_{\ell}^{+}
, respectively.

Lemma 5.16

(Decomposition of
\bm{U}_{0,h,\ell}
)
.

For each
\ell=1,\cdots,N
,

\bm{U}_{0,h,\ell}=\bm{U}_{0,h,\ell}^{+}\oplus\bm{U}_{0,h,\ell}^{-}.

Moreover, for
\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell}=\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell}^{+}+\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell}^{-},~\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell}^{\pm}\in\bm{U}_{0,h,\ell}^{\pm},

\|\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell}\|_{\bm{U}_{0,h,\ell}}^{2}=\|\mathbf{v}^{+}_{h,\ell}\|_{\bm{U}^{+}_{0,h,\ell}}^{2}+\|\mathbf{v}^{-}_{h,\ell}\|_{\bm{U}^{-}_{0,h,\ell}}^{2},

(5.24)

where

\|\cdot\|_{\bm{U}_{0,h,\ell}^{\pm}}:=\|{\rm imp}^{h}_{\Gamma_{\ell}}(\cdot)\|_{0,\Gamma_{\ell}^{\pm}}.

Proof.

For
\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell}\in\bm{U}_{0,h,\ell}
, let

\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell}^{\pm}\in\bm{U}_{0,h,\ell}^{\pm}
be the discrete
Maxwell-harmonic functions determined by the one-sided impedance data

{\rm imp}^{h}_{\Gamma_{\ell}^{\pm}}(\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell})
: for all
\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell}\in\bm{U}_{h,\ell}
,

\displaystyle a_{\Omega_{\ell}}(\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell}^{+},\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell})

\displaystyle=\langle{\rm imp}^{h}_{\Gamma_{\ell}^{+}}(\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell}),(\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell})_{T}\rangle_{\Gamma_{\ell}^{+}},

\displaystyle a_{\Omega_{\ell}}(\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell}^{-},\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell})

\displaystyle=\langle{\rm imp}^{h}_{\Gamma_{\ell}^{-}}(\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell}),(\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell})_{T}\rangle_{\Gamma_{\ell}^{-}}.

By Lemma
5.15
and Proposition
5.13
,

\displaystyle a_{\Omega_{\ell}}(\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell}^{+}+\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell}^{-},\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell})

\displaystyle=\langle{\rm imp}^{h}_{\Gamma_{\ell}^{+}}(\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell}),(\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell})_{T}\rangle_{\Gamma_{\ell}^{+}}+\langle{\rm imp}^{h}_{\Gamma_{\ell}^{-}}(\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell}),(\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell})_{T}\rangle_{\Gamma_{\ell}^{-}}

\displaystyle=\langle{\rm imp}^{h}_{\Gamma_{\ell}}(\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell}),(\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell})_{T}\rangle_{\Gamma_{\ell}}

\displaystyle=a_{\Omega_{\ell}}(\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell},\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell}).

Thus,
\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell}=\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell}^{+}+\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell}^{-}
.
If
\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell}\in\bm{U}_{0,h,\ell}^{+}\cap\bm{U}_{0,h,\ell}^{-}
, then
Lemma
5.15
gives

{\rm imp}^{h}_{\Gamma_{\ell}^{-}}(\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell})=\bm{0},\hskip 10.00002pt{\rm imp}^{h}_{\Gamma_{\ell}^{+}}(\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell})=\bm{0}.

Hence
{\rm imp}^{h}_{\Gamma_{\ell}}(\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell})=\bm{0}
. By
Proposition
5.13
,

a_{\Omega_{\ell}}(\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell},\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell})=0,\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ for all }\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell}\in\bm{U}_{h,\ell}.

By local well-posedness,
\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell}=\bm{0}
.
Therefore,
\bm{U}_{0,h,\ell}=\bm{U}_{0,h,\ell}^{+}\oplus\bm{U}_{0,h,\ell}^{-}
. (
5.24
) follows directly from the definition
5.14
.
∎

Having identified discrete Maxwell-harmonic functions with their impedance
data on the interfaces, we now define how such data is transferred from one
interface to its neighboring interfaces through a local Maxwell solve.

Definition 5.17

(Discrete impedance-to-impedance map)
.

Let
s\in\{+,-\}
.
Given
\mathbf{g}_{T}\in\bm{U}_{h,T}(\Gamma_{\ell}^{s})
, let

\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell}\in\bm{U}_{h,\ell}
be the unique solution of

a_{\Omega_{\ell}}(\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell},\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell})=\langle\mathbf{g}_{T},(\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell})_{T}\rangle_{\Gamma_{\ell}^{s}}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for all }\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell}\in\bm{U}_{h,\ell}.

(5.25)

Let
\Omega_{j,\ell}:=\Omega_{j}\cap\Omega_{\ell}
,
then
\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma_{\ell}^{s}\to\Gamma_{\ell-1}^{+}}^{h}:\bm{U}_{h,T}(\Gamma_{\ell}^{s})\to\bm{U}_{h,T}(\Gamma_{\ell-1}^{+})
and
\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma_{\ell}^{s}\to\Gamma_{\ell+1}^{-}}^{h}:\bm{U}_{h,T}(\Gamma_{\ell}^{s})\to\bm{U}_{h,T}(\Gamma_{\ell+1}^{-})
are defined as the solutions of

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma_{\ell}^{s}\to\Gamma_{\ell-1}^{+}}^{h}{\bf g}_{T},\mathbf{v}_{h,\Gamma_{\ell-1}^{+}}\rangle_{\Gamma_{\ell-1}^{+}}=a_{\Omega_{\ell,\ell-1}}(\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell},\hat{\mathbf{v}}^{+}_{h,\ell-1})-a_{\Omega_{\ell}}(\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell},\mathcal{R}^{\top}_{h,\ell-1}\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{h,\ell-1}^{+}),

(5.26)

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma_{\ell}^{s}\to\Gamma_{\ell+1}^{-}}^{h}{\bf g}_{T},\mathbf{v}_{h,\Gamma_{\ell+1}^{-}}\rangle_{\Gamma_{\ell+1}^{-}}=a_{\Omega_{\ell,\ell+1}}(\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell},\hat{\mathbf{v}}^{-}_{h,\ell+1})-a_{\Omega_{\ell}}(\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell},\mathcal{R}^{\top}_{h,\ell+1}\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{h,\ell+1}^{-}),

for all
\mathbf{v}_{h,\Gamma_{\ell-1}^{+}}\in\bm{U}_{h,T}(\Gamma_{\ell-1}^{+}),\mathbf{v}_{h,\Gamma_{\ell+1}^{-}}\in\bm{U}_{h,T}(\Gamma_{\ell+1}^{-})
, respectively.

Theorem 5.18
.

Let
s\in\{+,-\}
,
{\bf g}_{T}\in\bm{U}_{h,T}(\Gamma_{\ell}^{s})
and suppose
\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell}
satisfies (
5.25
). Then

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma_{\ell}^{s}\to\Gamma_{\ell-1}^{+}}^{h}{\bf g}_{T},(\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell-1})_{T}\rangle_{\Gamma_{\ell-1}^{+}}=b_{h,\ell-1}(\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}^{\top}_{h,\ell}\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell},\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell-1}),~~\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ for all }\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell-1}\in\bm{U}_{h,{\ell-1}}.

(5.27)

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma_{\ell}^{s}\to\Gamma_{\ell+1}^{-}}^{h}{\bf g}_{T},(\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell+1})_{T}\rangle_{\Gamma_{\ell+1}^{-}}=b_{h,\ell+1}(\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}^{\top}_{h,\ell}\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell},\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell+1}),~~\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ for all }\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell+1}\in\bm{U}_{h,{\ell+1}}.

Proof.

We only provide a sketch of the proof here, as it closely follows the proof in
[
21
, Lemma 4.8]
.
Write
\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell-1}=\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{h,\ell-1}^{+}+\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}_{h,\ell-1}^{+}
, where
(\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{h,\ell-1}^{+})_{T}
coincides with
(\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell-1})_{T}
on
\Gamma_{\ell-1}^{+}
and is zero on other edges and
(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}_{h,\ell-1}^{+})_{T}
vanishes on
\Gamma_{\ell-1}^{+}
. Thus, by Proposition
5.3
,

\langle\mathcal{I}^{h}_{\Gamma_{\ell}^{s}\to\Gamma_{\ell-1}^{+}}{\bf g}_{T},(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}_{h,\ell-1}^{+})_{T}\rangle_{\Gamma_{\ell-1}^{+}}=0=b_{h,\ell-1}(\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{h,\ell}^{\top}\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell},\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}_{h,\ell-1}^{+}).

From Definition
5.17
, Proposition
5.3
and the definition of
b_{h,\ell-1}
in (
5.11
), we can get the following result:

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{I}^{h}_{\Gamma_{\ell}^{s}\to\Gamma_{\ell-1}^{+}}{\bf g}_{T},(\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{h,\ell-1}^{+})_{T}\rangle_{\Gamma_{\ell-1}^{+}}

\displaystyle=a_{\Omega_{\ell,\ell-1}}(\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell},\hat{\mathbf{v}}^{+}_{h,\ell-1})-a_{\Omega_{\ell}}(\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell},\mathcal{R}^{\top}_{h,\ell-1}\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{h,\ell-1}^{+})

\displaystyle=b_{h,\ell-1}(\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{h,\ell}^{\top}\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell},\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{h,\ell-1}^{+}).

∎

The next result connects the volume-based error transfer operator

\mathcal{T}_{h,j,\ell}
with the interface-based impedance-to-impedance map.
It shows that applying
\mathcal{T}_{h,j,\ell}
to a local Maxwell-harmonic
error and then taking its impedance data is equivalent to applying the
discrete impedance-to-impedance map to the original impedance data.

Theorem 5.19
.

Let
\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell}\in\bm{U}_{0,h,\ell}^{s}
with
s\in\{+,-\}
. For
(j,t)\in\{(\ell-1,+),(\ell+1,-)\}
,

{\rm imp}^{h}_{\Gamma_{j}^{t}}(\mathcal{T}_{h,j,\ell}\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell})=\mathcal{I}^{h}_{\Gamma_{\ell}^{s}\rightarrow\Gamma_{j}^{t}}({\rm imp}^{h}_{\Gamma_{\ell}^{s}}(\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell})).

(5.28)

Proof.

We give the proof for the case when
(j,t)=(\ell-1,+)
; the proofs of the other cases are similar. By Proposition
5.11
,
\mathcal{T}_{h,\ell-1,\ell}\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell}\in\bm{U}_{0,h,\ell-1}^{+}
. Then
\langle{\rm imp}^{h}_{\Gamma_{\ell-1}^{+}}(\mathcal{T}_{h,\ell-1,\ell}\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell}),(\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell-1})_{T}\rangle_{\Gamma_{\ell-1}^{+}}=a_{\Omega_{\ell-1}}(\mathcal{T}_{h,\ell-1,\ell}\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell},\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell-1})
for all
\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell-1}\in\bm{U}_{h,\ell-1}
from Proposition
5.13
and Lemma
5.15
. By the definition of
\mathcal{T}_{h,\ell-1,\ell}
(Definition
5.9
),
a_{\Omega_{\ell-1}}(\mathcal{T}_{h,\ell-1,\ell}\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell},\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell-1})=b_{h,\ell-1}(\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{h,\ell}^{\top}\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell},\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell-1})
. Therefore,

\langle{\rm imp}^{h}_{\Gamma_{\ell-1}^{+}}(\mathcal{T}_{h,\ell-1,\ell}\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell}),(\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell-1})_{T}\rangle_{\Gamma_{\ell-1}^{+}}=b_{h,\ell-1}(\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{h,\ell}^{\top}\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell},\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell-1}).

Furthermore, since
\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell}\in\bm{U}_{0,h,\ell}^{s}
,
\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell}
solves
a_{\Omega_{\ell}}(\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell},\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell})=\langle{\rm imp}^{h}_{\Gamma_{\ell}^{s}}(\mathbf{w}_{h,\ell}),(\mathbf{v}_{h,\ell})_{T}\rangle_{\Gamma_{\ell}^{s}}
. By Theorem
5.18
, (
5.28
) holds.
∎

6
Convergence analysis

In this section, we study the convergence of the discrete Schwarz error
propagation operator to its continuous-level analogue.
The main
idea is to represent both the continuous and discrete error propagation
operators by corresponding impedance-to-impedance maps on the
interfaces. This reduces the comparison of the powers

\bm{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{n}
and
\bm{\mathcal{T}}^{n}
to the
comparison of the interface operators
\bm{\mathcal{I}}^{h}
and

\bm{\mathcal{I}}
.

6.1
Definition of the impedance-to-impedance map acting on the global interface data

We first collect the impedance traces on all artificial interfaces into global
interface vectors. This allows us to express one Schwarz error-propagation step
as a single impedance-to-impedance operator acting on the global interface data.
For
s\in\{+,-\}
, let

\bm{\Gamma}^{s}:=\prod_{\ell=1}^{N}\Gamma_{\ell}^{s},\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbb{L}_{T}(\bm{\Gamma}^{s}):=\prod_{\ell=1}^{N}{\bf L}^{2}_{T}(\Gamma_{\ell}^{s})

(6.1)

equipped with the norm

\|\mathbf{g}^{s}\|_{0,\bm{\Gamma}^{s}}^{2}:=\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\|\mathbf{g}^{s}_{\ell}\|^{2}_{0,\Gamma_{\ell}^{s}},\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for }\mathbf{g}^{s}=(\mathbf{g}^{s}_{1},\dotso,\mathbf{g}^{s}_{N})\in\mathbb{L}_{T}(\bm{\Gamma}^{s}).

We define

\mathbb{L}_{T}(\bm{\Gamma}):=\mathbb{L}_{T}(\bm{\Gamma}^{-})\times\mathbb{L}_{T}(\bm{\Gamma}^{+})
equipped with the norm

\|\mathbf{g}\|_{0,\bm{\Gamma}}^{2}:=\|\mathbf{g}^{-}\|^{2}_{0,\bm{\Gamma}^{-}}+\|\mathbf{g}^{+}\|^{2}_{0,\bm{\Gamma}^{+}},\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for }\mathbf{g}=(\mathbf{g}^{-},\mathbf{g}^{+})\in\mathbb{L}_{T}(\bm{\Gamma}).

We define
\text{imp}_{\bm{\Gamma}^{s}}:\mathbb{U}_{0}\rightarrow\mathbb{L}_{T}(\bm{\Gamma}^{s})
by

{\rm imp}_{\bm{\Gamma}^{s}}(\mathfrak{v}):=\big({\rm imp}_{\Gamma_{1}^{s}}(\mathbf{v}_{1}),\ldots,{\rm imp}_{\Gamma_{\ell}^{s}}(\mathbf{v}_{\ell}),\ldots,{\rm imp}_{\Gamma_{N}^{s}}(\mathbf{v}_{N})\big).

for
\mathfrak{v}=(\mathbf{v}_{1},\dotso,\mathbf{v}_{\ell},\dotso,\mathbf{v}_{N})\in\mathbb{U}_{0}
.
Combining
s=+
and
s=-
, we define
\text{imp}_{\bm{\Gamma}}:\mathbb{U}_{0}\rightarrow\mathbb{L}_{T}(\bm{\Gamma})
by

\text{imp}_{\bm{\Gamma}}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\text{imp}_{\bm{\Gamma}^{-}}\\
\text{imp}_{\bm{\Gamma}^{+}}\end{array}\right).

(6.2)

These definitions, along with the definition of
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{U}_{0}}
in (
2.23
), imply the following.

Lemma 6.1
.

For any
\mathfrak{v}=(\mathbf{v}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{v}_{N})\in\mathbb{U}_{0}
with zero impedance data on
\partial\Omega
,

\|\mathfrak{v}\|_{\mathbb{U}_{0}}=\|\operatorname{imp}\mathfrak{v}\|_{0,\bm{\Gamma}},

(6.3)

where
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{U}_{0}}
is defined by (
2.23
).

For
t\in\{+,-\}
, we define the impedance-to-impedance map
\mathcal{I}_{s\rightarrow t}:\mathbb{L}_{T}(\bm{\Gamma}^{s})\rightarrow\mathbb{L}_{T}(\bm{\Gamma}^{t})
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{s\rightarrow-}

\displaystyle:=\begin{pmatrix}0&\\
\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma_{1}^{s}\rightarrow\Gamma_{2}^{-}}&0&\\
&\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma_{2}^{s}\rightarrow\Gamma_{3}^{-}}&0&\\
&&\ddots&\ddots&\\
&&&\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma_{N-2}^{s}\rightarrow\Gamma_{N-1}^{-}}&0&\\
&&&&\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma_{N-1}^{s}\rightarrow\Gamma_{N}^{-}}&0\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{s\rightarrow+}

\displaystyle:=\begin{pmatrix}0&\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma_{2}^{s}\rightarrow\Gamma_{1}^{+}}\\
&0&\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma_{3}^{s}\rightarrow\Gamma_{2}^{+}}\\
&&0&\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma_{4}^{s}\rightarrow\Gamma_{3}^{+}}\\
&&&\ddots&\ddots\\
&&&&0&\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma_{N}^{s}\rightarrow\Gamma_{N-1}^{+}}\\
&&&&&0\end{pmatrix}.

We define the impedance-to-impedance map
\bm{\mathcal{I}}:\mathbb{L}_{T}(\bm{\Gamma})\rightarrow\mathbb{L}_{T}(\bm{\Gamma})
by

\bm{\mathcal{I}}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathcal{I}_{-\rightarrow-}&\mathcal{I}_{+\rightarrow-}\\
\mathcal{I}_{-\rightarrow+}&\mathcal{I}_{+\rightarrow+}\end{array}\right).

The following result shows that the global error propagation operator

\bm{\mathcal{T}}
is completely represented, at the level of
interface impedance traces, by the global impedance-to-impedance map

\bm{\mathcal{I}}
.

Theorem 6.2
.

Suppose that
\{\Omega_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{N}
is a strip decomposition with
N\geq 2
.
Then

{\rm imp}_{\bm{\Gamma}}(\bm{\mathcal{T}}\mathfrak{v})=\bm{\mathcal{I}}{\rm imp}_{\bm{\Gamma}}(\mathfrak{v})\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ for all }\mathfrak{v}\in\mathbb{U}_{0}.

(6.4)

Proof.

For
\mathfrak{v}=(\mathbf{v}_{1},\dotso,\mathbf{v}_{N})\in\mathbb{U}_{0}
, by the definition of
{\rm imp}
(
6.2
) and the fact that
\bm{\mathcal{T}}=\bm{\mathcal{L}}+\bm{\mathcal{U}}
,

\displaystyle{\rm imp}_{\bm{\Gamma}}(\bm{\mathcal{T}}\mathfrak{v})

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}{\rm imp}_{\bm{\Gamma}^{-}}(\bm{\mathcal{T}}\mathfrak{v})\\
{\rm imp}_{\bm{\Gamma}^{+}}(\bm{\mathcal{T}}\mathfrak{v})\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}{\rm imp}_{\bm{\Gamma}^{-}}(\bm{\mathcal{L}}\mathfrak{v})+{\rm imp}_{\bm{\Gamma}^{-}}(\bm{\mathcal{U}}\mathfrak{v})\\
{\rm imp}_{\bm{\Gamma}^{+}}(\bm{\mathcal{L}}\mathfrak{v})+{\rm imp}_{\bm{\Gamma}^{+}}(\bm{\mathcal{U}}\mathfrak{v})\end{pmatrix}.

(6.5)

Now
{\rm imp}_{\bm{\Gamma}^{-}}(\bm{\mathcal{U}}\mathfrak{v})=\bm{0}
and
{\rm imp}_{\bm{\Gamma}^{+}}(\bm{\mathcal{L}}\mathfrak{v})=\bm{0}
by the definition of PoU, and error propagation operator defined in (
3.3
).
More precisely,
by the definitions of
\bm{\mathcal{L}}
and

\bm{\mathcal{U}}
,
the
j
-th row of (
6.5
) is

{\rm imp}_{\Gamma_{j}^{-}}(\mathcal{T}_{j,j-1}\mathbf{v}_{j-1})+{\rm imp}_{\Gamma_{j}^{-}}(\mathcal{T}_{j,j+1}\mathbf{v}_{j+1})

where
{\rm imp}_{\Gamma_{j}^{-}}(\mathcal{T}_{j,j+1}\mathbf{v}_{j+1})={\rm imp}_{\Gamma_{j}^{-}}(\chi_{j+1}\mathbf{v}_{j+1})
by (
3.3
).
By the support property of the partition of unity,

\chi_{j+1}
vanishes in a neighbourhood of
\Gamma_{j}^{-}
. Hence

\chi_{j+1}\mathbf{v}_{j+1}
, and therefore its impedance trace, vanish on

\Gamma_{j}^{-}
.
Consequently,

{\rm imp}_{\Gamma_{j}^{-}}(\bm{\mathcal{U}}\mathfrak{v})=\bm{0}
. The identity
{\rm imp}_{\Gamma_{j}^{+}}(\bm{\mathcal{L}}\mathfrak{v})=\bm{0}
follows analogously.
It remains to express

{\rm imp}_{\bm{\Gamma}^{-}}(\bm{\mathcal{L}}\mathfrak{v})
and

{\rm imp}_{\bm{\Gamma}^{+}}(\bm{\mathcal{U}}\mathfrak{v})
in terms
of the impedance-to-impedance maps.
Similar to the proof in Lemma
4.1
,
by (
3.3
), (
3.6
) and Definition
3.2
,

\displaystyle{\rm imp}_{\Gamma_{j}^{-}}(\mathcal{T}_{j,j-1}\mathbf{v}_{j-1})={\rm imp}_{\Gamma_{j}^{-}}(\chi_{j-1}\mathbf{v}_{j-1})

\displaystyle={\rm imp}_{\Gamma_{j}^{-}}(\mathbf{v}_{j-1})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma_{j-1}^{-}\to\Gamma_{j}^{-}}{\rm imp}_{\Gamma_{j-1}^{-}}(\mathbf{v}_{j-1})+\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma_{j-1}^{+}\to\Gamma_{j}^{-}}{\rm imp}_{\Gamma_{j-1}^{+}}(\mathbf{v}_{j-1}).

Applying the same argument to all interfaces gives:

\displaystyle{\rm imp}_{\bm{\Gamma}}(\bm{\mathcal{T}}\mathfrak{v})=\begin{pmatrix}{\rm imp}_{\bm{\Gamma}^{-}}(\bm{\mathcal{L}}\mathfrak{v})\\
{\rm imp}_{\bm{\Gamma}^{+}}(\bm{\mathcal{U}}\mathfrak{v})\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{I}_{-\rightarrow-}{\rm imp}_{\bm{\Gamma}^{-}}(\mathfrak{v})+\mathcal{I}_{+\rightarrow-}{\rm imp}_{\bm{\Gamma}^{+}}(\mathfrak{v})\\
\mathcal{I}_{-\rightarrow+}{\rm imp}_{\bm{\Gamma}^{-}}(\mathfrak{v})+\mathcal{I}_{+\rightarrow+}{\rm imp}_{\bm{\Gamma}^{+}}(\mathfrak{v})\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{I}_{-\rightarrow-}&\mathcal{I}_{+\rightarrow-}\\
\mathcal{I}_{-\rightarrow+}&\mathcal{I}_{+\rightarrow+}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}{\rm imp}_{\bm{\Gamma}^{-}}(\mathfrak{v})\\
{\rm imp}_{\bm{\Gamma}^{+}}(\mathfrak{v})\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\bm{\mathcal{I}}{\rm imp}_{\bm{\Gamma}}(\mathfrak{v}).

∎

The result (
6.4
) is useful because, by (
6.3
), the norm of the local error is
equivalent to the norm of its impedance data on the interior interfaces.

We now above the discrete analogue of Theorem
6.2
.
For
s\in\{+,-\}
, let

\mathbb{U}_{h,T}(\bm{\Gamma}^{s}):=\prod_{\ell=1}^{N}\bm{U}_{h,T}(\Gamma_{\ell}^{s}),\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbb{U}_{h,T}(\bm{\Gamma}):=\mathbb{U}_{h,T}(\bm{\Gamma}^{-})\times\mathbb{U}_{h,T}(\bm{\Gamma}^{+}).

Both spaces are equipped with the product
L^{2}
-norms

\|\mathbf{g}_{h}^{s}\|_{0,\bm{\Gamma}^{s}}^{2}:=\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\|\mathbf{g}_{h,\ell}^{s}\|_{0,\Gamma_{\ell}^{s}}^{2},\hskip 10.00002pt\|\mathbf{g}_{h}\|_{0,\bm{\Gamma}}^{2}:=\|\mathbf{g}_{h}^{-}\|_{0,\bm{\Gamma}^{-}}^{2}+\|\mathbf{g}_{h}^{+}\|_{0,\bm{\Gamma}^{+}}^{2}.

The discrete global
impedance-to-impedance map

\bm{\mathcal{I}}^{h}:\mathbb{U}_{h,T}(\bm{\Gamma})\to\mathbb{U}_{h,T}(\bm{\Gamma})
is defined by

\bm{\mathcal{I}}^{h}:=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{I}^{h}_{-\to-}&\mathcal{I}^{h}_{+\to-}\\
\mathcal{I}^{h}_{-\to+}&\mathcal{I}^{h}_{+\to+}\end{pmatrix}.

Repeating the proof of Theorem
6.2
, but now using Lemma
5.15
and Theorem
5.19
,
we obtain the following result.

Theorem 6.3
.

Suppose that
\{\Omega_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{N}
is a strip decomposition with
N\geq 2
. Then

{\rm imp}^{h}_{\bm{\Gamma}}(\bm{\mathcal{T}}_{h}\mathfrak{v}_{h})=\bm{\mathcal{I}}^{h}{\rm imp}^{h}_{\bm{\Gamma}}(\mathfrak{v}_{h})\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ for all }\mathfrak{v}_{h}\in\mathbb{U}_{0,h}.

6.2
Convergence of the discrete error propagation

First, we show that norms of powers of the
error propagation matrix
equal norms of powers of the
global impedance-to-impedance map. This
identity allows us to reduce the analysis of the error propagation to the analysis of the global impedance-to-impedance map.

Theorem 6.4
.

Suppose that
\{\Omega_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{N}
is a strip decomposition with
N\geq 2
. Then, for
n\geq 1
,

\|\bm{\mathcal{T}}^{n}\|_{\mathbb{U}_{0}}=\|\bm{\mathcal{I}}^{n}\|_{0,\bm{\Gamma}},\hskip 10.00002pt\|\bm{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{n}\|_{\mathbb{U}_{0,h}}=\|(\bm{\mathcal{I}}^{h})^{n}\|_{0,\bm{\Gamma}}.

Proof.

We prove the first identity;
the proof of the second is similar, using Definitions
5.14
and
5.17
, along with Theorem
6.3
.
Let

\widetilde{\mathbb{U}}_{0}:=\{\mathfrak{v}\in\mathbb{U}_{0}\,:\,\iimp\mathfrak{v}=\bm{0}\text{ on }\partial\Omega\}.

Since Lemma
6.1
only applies to elements of
\widetilde{\mathbb{U}}_{0}
, we first show that the operator norm of
\bm{\mathcal{T}}^{n}
over
\mathbb{U}_{0}
can be restricted to
\widetilde{\mathbb{U}}_{0}
, i.e.,

\|\bm{\mathcal{T}}^{n}\|_{\mathbb{U}_{0}}=\sup_{\mathfrak{v}\in\mathbb{U}_{0}}\frac{\|\bm{\mathcal{T}}^{n}\mathfrak{v}\|_{\mathbb{U}_{0}}}{\|\mathfrak{v}\|_{\mathbb{U}_{0}}}=\sup_{\mathfrak{v}\in\widetilde{\mathbb{U}}_{0}}\frac{\|\bm{\mathcal{T}}^{n}\mathfrak{v}\|_{\mathbb{U}_{0}}}{\|\mathfrak{v}\|_{\mathbb{U}_{0}}}.

(6.6)

The inequality
\geq
in (
6.6
) is immediate from
\widetilde{\mathbb{U}}_{0}\subset\mathbb{U}_{0}
. For the reverse inequality, given any
\mathfrak{v}\in\mathbb{U}_{0}
, by Lemma
2.3
there exists a unique
\mathfrak{v}_{0}\in\widetilde{\mathbb{U}}_{0}
such that
\iimp\mathfrak{v}_{0}=\iimp\mathfrak{v}
on
\bm{\Gamma}
, and by (
2.23
),

\|\mathfrak{v}_{0}\|_{\mathbb{U}_{0}}\leq\|\mathfrak{v}\|_{\mathbb{U}_{0}}.

By (
3.3
),
\bm{\mathcal{T}}
depends only on the interface impedance data
\iimp_{\bm{\Gamma}}
and
\iimp_{\bm{\Gamma}}\mathfrak{v}=\iimp_{\bm{\Gamma}}\mathfrak{v}_{0}
; we therefore have
\bm{\mathcal{T}}\mathfrak{v}=\bm{\mathcal{T}}\mathfrak{v}_{0}
, and inductively
\bm{\mathcal{T}}^{n}\mathfrak{v}=\bm{\mathcal{T}}^{n}\mathfrak{v}_{0}
. Therefore

\frac{\|\bm{\mathcal{T}}^{n}\mathfrak{v}\|_{\mathbb{U}_{0}}}{\|\mathfrak{v}\|_{\mathbb{U}_{0}}}=\frac{\|\bm{\mathcal{T}}^{n}\mathfrak{v}_{0}\|_{\mathbb{U}_{0}}}{\|\mathfrak{v}\|_{\mathbb{U}_{0}}}\leq\frac{\|\bm{\mathcal{T}}^{n}\mathfrak{v}_{0}\|_{\mathbb{U}_{0}}}{\|\mathfrak{v}_{0}\|_{\mathbb{U}_{0}}},

which gives the inequality
\leq
in (
6.6
).
Applying the norm equivalence from Lemma
6.1
to both the numerator and denominator,
and then using Theorem
6.2
,
we have

\|\bm{\mathcal{T}}^{n}\|_{\mathbb{U}_{0}}=\sup_{\mathfrak{v}\in\widetilde{\mathbb{U}}_{0}}\frac{\|\iimp(\bm{\mathcal{T}}^{n}\mathfrak{v})\|_{0,\bm{\Gamma}}}{\|\iimp\mathfrak{v}\|_{0,\bm{\Gamma}}}=\sup_{\mathfrak{v}\in\widetilde{\mathbb{U}}_{0}}\frac{\|\bm{\mathcal{I}}^{n}(\iimp\mathfrak{v})\|_{0,\bm{\Gamma}}}{\|\iimp\mathfrak{v}\|_{0,\bm{\Gamma}}}.

Finally, since the impedance map
\iimp_{\bm{\Gamma}}:\widetilde{\mathbb{U}}_{0}\to\mathbb{L}_{T}(\bm{\Gamma})

(where
\mathbb{L}_{T}(\bm{\Gamma})
is defined by (
6.1
))
is surjective by Lemma
2.3
,

\|\bm{\mathcal{T}}^{n}\|_{\mathbb{U}_{0}}=\sup_{\mathbf{g}\in\mathbb{L}_{T}(\bm{\Gamma})}\frac{\|\bm{\mathcal{I}}^{n}\mathbf{g}\|_{0,\bm{\Gamma}}}{\|\mathbf{g}\|_{0,\bm{\Gamma}}}=\|\bm{\mathcal{I}}^{n}\|_{0,\bm{\Gamma}}.

∎

Assumption 6.5

(Convergence of the discrete impedance-to-impedance map)
.

As
h\to 0
, the discrete interface map converges to the continuous one in
operator norm: i.e.,

\|\bm{\mathcal{I}}^{h}-\bm{\mathcal{I}}\|_{0,\bm{\Gamma}}\to 0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ as }h\to 0.

Remark 6.6

(Discussion of Assumption
6.5
)
.

In the Helmholtz case,
[
21
, Theorem 5.5]
proves Assumption
6.5
using a local analysis
(away from the impedance boundary)
of the FEM error for the Helmholtz interior impedance problem in a rectangular cuboid (corresponding to one subdomain in a strip decomposition). Crucial ingredients in this analysis are (i)
H^{2}
regularity of the solution of the Helmholtz equation in a convex polyhedron (used in
[
21
, Lemma 5.8]
), and (ii) an adaption of the “elliptic-projection” duality argument to work in a weighted norm
[
21
, Theorem 5.7]
.

The obstacles to establishing an analogous result in the Maxwell setting here are (i) the reduced regularity – the Maxwell solution is only
\mathbf{H}^{1}
in a convex polyhedron, with this only currently proved for homogeneous impedance data
[
40
]
(see the discussion around Assumption
2.11
) and (ii) the fact that the Maxwell analogues of the “elliptic-projection” duality arguments are both more involved, and the subject of recent research
[
31
,
8
,
32
]
.

Theorem 6.7
.

Suppose that

\{\Omega_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{N}
is a strip decomposition with
N\geq 2
and Assumption
6.5
holds.
Then,
for any fixed integer
n\geq 1
, as
h\to 0
,

\|\bm{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{n}\|_{\mathbb{U}_{0,h}}\to\|\bm{\mathcal{T}}^{n}\|_{\mathbb{U}_{0}}

Proof.

By Theorem
6.4
,

\displaystyle\left|\|\bm{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{n}\|_{\mathbb{U}_{0,h}}-\|\bm{\mathcal{T}}^{n}\|_{\mathbb{U}_{0}}\right|=\left|\|(\bm{\mathcal{I}}^{h})^{n}\|_{0,\bm{\Gamma}}-\|\bm{\mathcal{I}}^{n}\|_{0,\bm{\Gamma}}\right|\leq\left\|(\bm{\mathcal{I}}^{h})^{n}-\bm{\mathcal{I}}^{n}\right\|_{0,\bm{\Gamma}}.

Arguing by induction (exactly as in the proof of
[
21
, Theorem 4.17]
), one
can show that

\left\|(\bm{\mathcal{I}}^{h})^{n}-\bm{\mathcal{I}}^{n}\right\|_{0,\bm{\Gamma}}\leq nC^{n-1}\left\|\bm{\mathcal{I}}^{h}-\bm{\mathcal{I}}\right\|_{0,\bm{\Gamma}},

where the constant
C
depends on the constant in
(
3.8
)
(i.e., the boundedness of impedance-to-impedance maps).
The result then follows from Assumption
6.5
.
∎

Remark 6.8

(Comparison with the Helmholtz results of
[
20
,
21
,
27
]
)
.

The Maxwell results in this paper are not quite as extensive as the Helmholtz results in
[
20
,
21
,
27
]
in the following three ways:

(i) The wellposedness result of Theorem
2.12
requires the regularity assumption Assumption
2.11
, whereas the Helmholtz analogue of Theorem
2.12
(
[
20
, Theorem 2.12]
) is proved for general Lipschitz subdomains.

(ii) We have assumed that the discrete impedance-to-impedance maps converge to their continuous counterparts (Assumption
6.5
) while the Helmholtz analgoue of this result is proved in
[
21
, Theorem 5.5]
.

(iii) We have not attempted to bound the impedance-to-impedance maps in the high-frequency limit at the continuous level as done for the Helmholtz case in
[
27
]
.

Regarding (i) and (ii): the obstacles to establishing these results are because of the well-known fact that the Maxwell solution is less regular that the analogous Laplace/Helmholtz solution (see the discussion
below Conjecture
2.14
and
in Remark
6.6
).

Regarding (iii): the bounds in
[
27
]
use so-called
semiclassical defect measures
(see, e.g.,
[
43
, Chapter 5]
,
[
36
]
), and, to our knowledge, the theory of these measures has not yet been established for the time-harmonic Maxwell equations.

7
Numerical tests

The numerical experiments in this section are organized around three stages: first
we observe the convergence of the RAS-imp preconditioner, then we identify its convergence mechanism (via the impedance-to-impedance maps), and finally we report iteration counts of RAS-imp across a broader range of
parameter regimes and geometries.

More precisely: we begin in
§
7.2
by plotting the residual history of the RAS-imp method applied
to a representative problem with a nonzero source term on strip decompositions,
which illustrates the basic convergence behavior of the iteration. We then turn
from residuals to the error propagation operator itself: in §
7.3
, we
show that suitable powers of this operator are contractive on strip
decompositions, and in §
7.4
we compute the norms of the underlying
impedance-to-impedance maps, which—together with Theorem
4.2
—account
for the power contractivity observed in §
7.3
. Moving beyond the
strip decompositions covered by our theoretical analysis, §
7.5

considers checkerboard decompositions and finds the same power-contractivity
behavior. The section concludes with a broader study in §
7.6
, where
we report iteration counts for RAS-imp and the corresponding
right-preconditioned GMRES method under varying decompositions, overlaps,
wavenumbers, and PDE coefficients.

7.1
Set up

We consider the following two-dimensional time-harmonic Maxwell’s equations on a domain
\Omega
, coupled with impedance boundary conditions:

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{\curl}(\mu^{-1}\rot\mathbf{u})-k^{2}\epsilon\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle=\bm{f}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{in }\Omega,

(7.1)

\displaystyle\bm{\tau}(\mu^{-1}\rot\mathbf{u}-ik\lambda\mathbf{u}\cdot\bm{\tau})

\displaystyle={\bf g}_{T}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{on }\partial\Omega,

where
\overrightarrow{\curl}=\left(\dfrac{\partial}{\partial y},-\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x}\right)
,
\rot\mathbf{u}=\dfrac{\partial\mathbf{u}_{y}}{\partial x}-\dfrac{\partial\mathbf{u}_{x}}{\partial y}
, and
\bm{\tau}=(-\mathbf{n}_{y},\mathbf{n}_{x})
denotes the unit tangent vector.
The corresponding sesquilinear form is

a_{\Omega}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}):=\int_{\Omega}\left(\mu^{-1}\rot\mathbf{u}\rot\bar{\mathbf{v}}-k^{2}\epsilon\mathbf{u}\cdot\bar{\mathbf{v}}\right)\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}-ik\int_{\partial\Omega}\lambda(\mathbf{u}\cdot\bm{\tau})(\bar{\mathbf{v}}\cdot\bm{\tau})\mathrm{d}s,

(7.2)

and the discrete variational problem reads:

a_{\Omega}(\mathbf{u}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})=\int_{\Omega}\bm{f}\cdot\bar{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}+\int_{\partial\Omega}{\bf g}_{T}\cdot\bar{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\,\mathrm{d}s.

(7.3)

Both homogeneous media (
\epsilon=1
,
\mu=1
,
\lambda=1
) and heterogeneous media with spatially varying permittivity are considered.

All experiments are carried out on two-dimensional rectangular domains, discretized with the lowest-order Nédélec finite elements on a mesh of size
h\propto k^{-3/2}
chosen to suppress the pollution effect
[
32
]
.
The domain
\Omega=[0,L_{x}]\times[0,L_{y}]
is partitioned into
N
non-overlapping rectangular subdomains, each then enlarged by appending all neighboring elements within a distance
\delta
. The overlap
\delta
is either prescribed as a fixed value or set to
\delta=\eta H
, where
H=L_{x}/N
denotes the subdomain size and
\eta
the overlap ratio.

the RAS-imp preconditioner, terminated when
\|{\bf r}\|_{2}/\|\bm{b}\|_{2}\leq 10^{-6}

7.3
Power contractivity for strip decompositions

In this subsection, we investigate the power contractivity of RAS-imp
on a strip domain decomposition.
For the error equation (
5.19
) arising in the parallel Schwarz method, convergence is typically established by verifying that the spectral radius
\rho(\bm{\mathcal{T}}_{h})
of the iteration matrix is strictly less than
1
. Direct computation of
\rho(\bm{\mathcal{T}}_{h})
, however, is prohibitively expensive. We therefore reduce this computation to a spectral problem for a much smaller matrix
\mathbf{E}
.
Let

\widetilde{\mathbf{R}}_{h}^{\mathrm{T}}:=[\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}^{\mathrm{T}}_{h,1},\dotso,\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}^{\mathrm{T}}_{h,N}],\hskip 20.00003pt\mathcal{\mathbf{R}}_{h}:=[\mathcal{R}_{h,1},\dotso,\mathcal{R}_{h,N}]^{\mathrm{T}}

(7.4)

and

\mathbf{Q}_{h}:=[A_{h,1}^{-1}\mathcal{R}_{h,1}A_{h},\dotso,A_{h,N}^{-1}\mathcal{R}_{h,N}A_{h}]^{\mathrm{T}}.

The definition of the RAS-imp Richardson iteration (
5.16
)
implies that the global error
\mathbf{e}_{h}^{n}:=\mathbf{u}_{h}-\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n}
satisfies

\mathbf{e}^{n}_{h}=\mathbf{E}\mathbf{e}^{n-1}_{h},~~~\mathbf{E}:=I-B_{h}^{-1}A_{h}.

where

B_{h}^{-1}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{h,\ell}^{\mathrm{T}}A_{h,\ell}^{-1}\mathcal{R}_{h,\ell}.

By the definition of
B_{h}^{-1}
and the fact that
\sum_{\ell}\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{h,\ell}^{T}\mathcal{R}_{h,\ell}=I

(
5.17
),

\mathbf{E}=\sum_{\ell}\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{h,\ell}^{T}(\mathcal{R}_{h,\ell}-A_{h,\ell}^{-1}\mathcal{R}_{h,\ell}A_{h})=\widetilde{\mathbf{R}}_{h}^{\mathrm{T}}(\mathbf{R}_{h}-\mathcal{\mathbf{Q}}_{h}).

(7.5)

In addition, by Definition
5.9
of
\bm{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
and the definition of
b_{h,\ell}
in (
5.11
),

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{h,\ell,j}\mathbf{e}^{n}_{h,j}=

\displaystyle\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{h,\ell}^{T}\mathbf{e}^{n}_{h,j}\right)\Big|_{\Omega_{\ell}}-A_{h,\ell}^{-1}\mathcal{R}_{h,\ell}A_{h}\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{h,j}^{T}\mathbf{e}^{n}_{h,j}

Thus,

\mathbf{e}^{n+1}_{h,\ell}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathcal{T}_{h,\ell,j}\mathbf{e}^{n}_{h,j}=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{h,\ell}^{T}\mathbf{e}^{n}_{h,j}\right)\Big|_{\Omega_{\ell}}-A_{h,\ell}^{-1}\mathcal{R}_{h,\ell}A_{h}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{h,j}^{T}\mathbf{e}^{n}_{h,j}.

Then by the definitions of

\mathbf{R}_{h}
and
\widetilde{\mathbf{R}}_{h}

(
7.4
),
the error propagation matrix can be written as

\bm{\mathcal{T}}_{h}=(\mathbf{R}_{h}-\mathcal{\mathbf{Q}}_{h})\widetilde{\mathbf{R}}_{h}^{\mathrm{T}}.

(7.6)

By (
7.5
) and (
7.6
),
\mathbf{E}
and
\bm{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
are products of the same two factors in opposite orders, and therefore share the same non-zero eigenvalues; in particular,
\rho(\bm{\mathcal{T}}_{h})=\rho(\mathbf{E})
. Since
\mathbf{E}
is the smaller of the two, we work with
\mathbf{E}
in what follows.
To quantify the contraction in a
k
-explicit way, we equip
\mathbb{C}^{M}
, where
M
is the dimension of the finite element space,
with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{D_{k}}
induced from the norm

\|\mathbf{u}\|_{\iimp,k,\Omega}^{2}:=\|\curl\mathbf{u}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}+k^{2}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}+k\|\mathbf{u}_{T}\|_{0,\partial\Omega}^{2},

(7.7)

on
X_{\rm imp}(\Omega)
. Note that the well-posedness results in §
2
were not explicit in
k
, so the norm (
2.1
) was left unweighted; here we are interested in the
k
-dependence of the FEM error, and (
7.7
) weights the terms with
k
in the natural way. We then compute

\|\mathbf{E}^{s}\|:=\max_{0\neq\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{C}^{M}}\frac{\|\mathbf{E}^{s}\mathbf{v}\|_{D_{k}}}{\|\mathbf{v}\|_{D_{k}}},\hskip 10.00002pts\geq 1.

(7.8)

Table
1
reports
\|\mathbf{E}^{s}\|
for varying
k
and
N
in the homogeneous case. In every configuration,
\|\mathbf{E}^{s}\|
decreases gradually with
s
and then drops to a value well below
1
at
s\sim N
.
This matches the stepwise decay observed in Figures
3
and
4
: the abrupt drops in the residual curves coincide with the iterations

at which
\|\mathbf{E}^{s}\|
contracts.
In particular,
\rho(\mathbf{E})\leq\|\mathbf{E}^{N}\|^{1/N}<1
, so the iteration converges asymptotically.

\mathcal{I}_{-\to+}

\mathcal{I}_{-\to-}

\rho(k,\delta,L)=\sup_{{\bf g}_{T}\in{\bf L}^{2}_{T}(\Gamma^{-})}\frac{\|\mathcal{I}_{-\to+}{\bf g}_{T}\|_{{\bf L}^{2}_{T}(\Gamma_{\delta})}}{\|{\bf g}_{T}\|_{{\bf L}^{2}_{T}(\Gamma^{-})}},\hskip 10.00002pt\gamma(k,\delta,L)=\sup_{{\bf g}_{T}\in{\bf L}^{2}_{T}(\Gamma^{-})}\frac{\|\mathcal{I}_{-\to-}{\bf g}_{T}\|_{{\bf L}^{2}_{T}(\Gamma_{\delta})}}{\|{\bf g}_{T}\|_{{\bf L}^{2}_{T}(\Gamma^{-})}}.

) and (

Power contractivity for checkerboard decompositions

Figure 6
:
Checkerboard domain decomposition into overlapping subdomains.

In this subsection, we consider checkerboard decompositions of rectangular domains, illustrated in Figure
6
: an
m\times m
partition with
N=m^{2}
subdomains. Compared with the strip layout, each interior subdomain has more neighbors
[
20
, §6.3]
.
The theoretical results of

[
18
]
for one-level Schwarz methods with PML transmission conditions applied to the Helmholtz equation indicate that one expects the contractive regime – where

\|\mathbf{E}^{s}\|<1
– to occur roughly when

s\sim 2\sqrt{N}

(with
2\sqrt{N}
equal to the number of subdomains in the
x_{1}
directions plus the number of subdomains in the
x_{2}
direction.
Tables
5
and
6
therefore report
\|\mathbf{E}^{s}\|
for
s
near
2\sqrt{N}
, in the homogeneous and variable-coefficient (with
\epsilon
given by (
7.9
)) cases respectively. In the homogeneous case,
\|\mathbf{E}^{s}\|<1
is reached by
s=2\sqrt{N}+2
in essentially every configuration, the only outlier within the displayed range being
8\times 8
at
k=30
. In the variable-coefficient case the threshold is shifted slightly later, but a few more powers again bring
\|\mathbf{E}^{s}\|<1
; i.e., power contractivity persists with a higher threshold.

7.6
Iteration counts of the RAS-imp preconditioned Richardson method

In this subsection, we consider the source-free Maxwell equation on the unit square
\Omega=[0,1]^{2}
, with impedance data generated from the plane wave

\mathbf{u}^{\rm pw}(\mathbf{x})=\left(-k_{2}\exp(i\bm{k}\cdot\mathbf{x}),\,k_{1}\exp(i\bm{k}\cdot\mathbf{x})\right)^{T},

where
\bm{k}=(k_{1},k_{2})^{T}
satisfies
|\bm{k}|=k
. We report iteration counts of the RAS-imp preconditioned Richardson method over a range of parameters, using a direct solver for each local subproblem and terminating once the relative residual falls below
10^{-6}
(with at most
500
iterations). Both strip and checkerboard decompositions are considered, in homogeneous and heterogeneous media (
7.9
). For comparison, the iteration counts of the right-preconditioned restarted GMRES method (restart
50
, at most
20
restarts) under the same stopping criterion are shown in parentheses.

Table
7
reports the strip decomposition with homogeneous parameters. Convergence is achieved in all configurations, the iteration counts are robust in
k
for fixed
N
, and grow with
N
for fixed
k
as expected from the longer information-propagation distance.
GMRES consistently requires fewer iterations, confirming the effectiveness of RAS-imp as a Krylov preconditioner.

Table
8
reports the corresponding results for the variable-permittivity profile (
7.9
). Convergence is generally slower than in the homogeneous case.
The deterioration is also visible in the GMRES counts; a plausible cause is that the larger
\epsilon
in the central region lowers the local wave speed and may promote internal reflections, hindering information transfer across subdomain interfaces.
