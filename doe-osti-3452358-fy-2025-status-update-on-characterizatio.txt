 1. INTRODUCTION

The intent of this letter is to provide an update on the characterization of Idaho State University’s (ISU)
Aerojet General Nucleonics (AGN)-201M reactor. This project falls under IR-25OR01020607 of the
Critical Benchmark Execution program through the DOE/NRC Collaboration for Criticality Safety
Support for Commercial-Scale HALEU for Fuel Cycles and Transportation (DNCSH) project.

The AGN-201M reactor is a low-power reactor used for academic and industrial research. The fuel for
this reactor is UO2 powder enriched to near 20 wt % 235U and matrixed in polyethylene discs. The fuel is
reflected by a large amount of graphite, which is surrounded by lead and water to provide gamma and
neutron shielding, respectively. The hydrogenous uranium mixture in the fuel provides a nearly ideal
moderator-to-fuel ratio for thermal neutron utilization; minimal 235U mass is required to reach a critical
configuration. To support ongoing efforts to use high-assay low-enriched uranium (HALEU) in advance
reactor designs, a benchmarking project with this reactor is underway.

 2. M4 STATUS UPDATE FOR FY 2025

For FY 2025, the primary objective is complete disassembly of the reactor and physical measurements of
the reactor components. The reactor is in use during the school year, and availability for disassembly is
limited to the summer timeframe. The reactor had not been disassembled in more than 30 years, and some
components, such as the fuel, are irreplaceable. Therefore, significant preparation was needed. Due to the
limited availability and significant preparation for disassembly, the project timeline includes a
contingency to allow another summer to complete disassembly and characterization.

After the project was approved and funding was received, planning began for the disassembly of the
reactor. Disassembly officially began on May 27, 2025, with the opening of the reactor tank to perform
gas sampling to measure fission products. Upon confirmation that dose from gaseous fission products
would be sufficiently low, the ISU team (faculty, staff, and student researchers) began disassembling the
reactor and its components. Disassembly and physical characterization were the primary effort through
the summer, and discussions are beginning on developing a procedure for reassembly and an approach to
critical measurements. Primary characterization has been dimensional and mass measurements of the fuel
and components, and associated measurement uncertainties have been documented. Oak Ridge National
Laboratory supported ISU by performing scanning of the fuel and components with a coordinate
measurement machine (CMM) to provide precise dimensional measurements of items with complex
geometries and to capture imperfections of the components. Radiographs were performed on the fuel to
view the distribution of the fuel within the polyethylene plates. The disassembly and measurements were
performed under approved criticality safety procedures and radiological controls. Assembly of the reactor
will begin before the end of the calendar year after a repair on the water shielding tank is complete. When
characterization is complete, ISU will produce a report of the reactor characteristics. This report will be
publicly available.

 3. CONCLUSION

With growing efforts to incorporate HALEU fuel into advanced nuclear reactor designs, benchmark-level
criticality experiments are needed to fill gaps in data available to validate the new design models. The
AGN-201M reactors are ideal due to their enrichment and near optimal moderation to maximize
reactivity. The current efforts for FY 2025 are ongoing, within schedule, and within budget.
