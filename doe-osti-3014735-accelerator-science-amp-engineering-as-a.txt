Accelerator Science & Engineering (AS&E) Workforce Roundtable and AS&E
 Traineeship Principal Investigator Meeting

A one-and-half-day meeting on the Accelerator Science & Engineering (AS&E) Workforce Roundtable
and a one-day meeting of Traineeship Principal Investigators took place at Facility for Rare Isotope Beams
at Michigan State University on January 21-23, 2025. The meeting identified critical issues in the AS&E
workforce and potential solutions, with a focus on DOE’s major accelerator facilities. The Traineeship PIs
meeting focused on sharing experience, efficiency, and improvements of the Traineeship Programs
conducted in six US universities. Several managers from the DOE High Energy Physics Office participated
in the meetings. The discussions resulted in a decision to conduct similar meetings every year.

A roundtable meeting was called with participants comprising 8 SC National Laboratories, 1 NNSA
National Laboratory, and the Office of Science Nuclear Physics User Facility FRIB, at Michigan State
University, who were also the hosts for the in-person meeting. The participants were charged to perform an
approximate census of the current accelerator workforce and to assemble an approximate workforce
projection with a 10-year horizon. The meeting also shared best practices for workforce development and
established actions for the laboratories to continue coordination.
The census counted over 4100 members of the accelerator workforce with unique skillsets for accelerator
research, design, construction and operation at the ten institutions. The workforce projections illustrate the
challenges that laboratories face in workforce planning to support facility operations, projects and to ensure
the continuation of research to be at the forefront of accelerator science.
Best practices for workforce planning were shared including re-training, using such programs as USPAS
to acquire new skills, supported by research opportunities and small projects to enable workers to develop
in new areas. Best practices in recruitment and retention were also highlighted with strong encouragement
of programs such as PPPL’s Apprenticeship scheme targeting technicians and HEP’s university-based
Accelerator Traineeship Programs.
The importance of the rest of the accelerator workforce ecosystem cannot be understated, in particular the
university programs that develop the new generation of scientists and engineers. The roundtable noted the
success of hands-on programs using facilities at the universities in the areas of sources and advanced
acceleration, and the recent MSU Cryogenics Initiative. In recognition of the wider ecosystem, plans were
discussed to expand the roundtable participants to universities and critical members of the accelerator
industry in the next year and continue annually.
The PIs of the AS&E Traineeship programs from Michigan State, Old Dominion, Stony Brook, Cornell,
Northern Illinois Universities and Illinois Institute of Technology shared their experience in workforce
development. Also, the director of USPAS reported on AS&E students and workforce training experience.
