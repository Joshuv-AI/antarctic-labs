On the Calderón problem with piecewise polynomial anisotropic conductivities and many-flat-face interfaces
Cătălin I. Cârstea

Abstract
We prove uniqueness for a finite-dimensional anisotropic Calderón problem in dimension n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 . The conductivity is a symmetric uniformly elliptic matrix field which is cellwise polynomial with respect to a known finite partition, and it may be discontinuous across interfaces. Under a geometric assumption allowing the cells to be reached successively through sufficiently many flat interface patches, the local Dirichlet-to-Neumann map on an initial boundary patch determines all polynomial pieces. The proof combines a one-cell recovery result from flat-face boundary data with a layer-stripping argument, using Runge approximation and unique continuation to propagate the data across already recovered cells. As a consequence of a finite-dimensional analytic stability theorem, the recovery is Hölder stable on compact admissible parameter sets.

Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
, be a bounded domain and let

\sigma(x)
be a symmetric positive definite matrix field. We study the
inverse problem of determining
\sigma
in the conductivity equation

\operatorname{div}(\sigma\nabla u)=0\quad\text{in }\Omega,

(1)

from a local Dirichlet-to-Neumann (DN) map.

In the anisotropic
\sigma
case the natural uniqueness statement is conjectured to be modulo boundary-fixing diffeomorphisms. If
F:\overline{\Omega}\to\overline{\Omega}
is a diffeomorphism with

F|_{\partial\Omega}=\operatorname{Id}
, then

(F_{*}\sigma)(y)=\frac{DF(x)\sigma(x)DF(x)^{T}}{|\det DF(x)|},\qquad x=F^{-1}(y),

(2)

has the same full boundary measurements as
\sigma
; see, for example,

[
21
,
22
,
25
]
. One could hope to avoid this gauge by adding a priori structural assumptions to the problem, as will be done in this paper.

Simply assuming
\sigma(x)
is polynomial in
x
does not, by itself, eliminate the gauge. For example, let

\Omega=B(0,1)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
A
be a nonzero skew-symmetric
matrix, for instance the generator of rotations in the
(x_{1},x_{2})
-plane.
Set
R_{\theta}=\exp(\theta A)
and

F_{\varepsilon}(x)=R_{\varepsilon(1-|x|^{2})}x.

(3)

Then
F_{\varepsilon}
is a diffeomorphism of the ball onto itself,

F_{\varepsilon}|_{\partial B(0,1)}=\operatorname{Id}
. Since
Ax\cdot x=0
, the Jacobian
determinant of
F_{\varepsilon}
is equal to one. Therefore the push-forward of the
constant conductivity
I
is

(F_{\varepsilon*}I)(y)=B_{\varepsilon}(y)B_{\varepsilon}(y)^{T},\qquad B_{\varepsilon}(y)=I-2\varepsilon(Ay)\otimes y.

(4)

Thus
I
and
F_{\varepsilon*}I
are distinct uniformly elliptic polynomial
conductivities on the ball, but they have the same boundary measurements.

We impose two types of a priori structure: a known finite partition and
polynomial dependence on each cell. The domain is subdivided into known cells

D_{\alpha}
, and on each cell the conductivity is assumed to be the restriction of a known-degree matrix-valued polynomial:

\sigma|_{D_{\alpha}}=P_{\alpha}|_{D_{\alpha}},\qquad P_{\alpha}\in\operatorname{Poly}_{\leq N_{\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\operatorname{Sym}_{n}).

(5)

The polynomial pieces may be discontinuous across interfaces. The partition
and the degree bounds are assumed known. The inverse problem is to determine
all polynomial pieces
P_{\alpha}
from a local Dirichlet-to-Neumann map on
one measured part of
\partial\Omega
.

[

,

]

Related piecewise constant finite-dimensional results for Schrödinger

[

For anisotropic conductivities, boundary measurements naturally determine the
coefficient only up to the boundary-fixing diffeomorphism gauge. The boundary
determination input used below is only the zeroth-order part of the standard
principal-symbol calculation for the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map: the local map
determines the induced boundary metric; see
[
22
,
25
]
.

The argument in this paper has two steps. The first is boundary determination. On a flat face with conormal
\nu
, the
boundary-normal metric invariant determines the conormal Schur form of

\sigma
relative to
\nu
, a scaled Schur-complement invariant on the face.
Three independent conormal directions determine a positive definite matrix at a
point from the corresponding Schur forms. If the entries of
\sigma
are
polynomials of degree at most
N
, then the Schur forms on at least
N+3

suitably generic flat faces determine the entire polynomial matrix. We believe
that
\max(3,N+2)
faces are sufficient, but we
prove the slightly weaker
N+3
-face version because it keeps this part of the
argument much easier to follow.

The global result is obtained by layer stripping. Once some cells have been
recovered, they form a known intervening region between the original measured
boundary and the unrecovered part of the domain. The local
Dirichlet-to-Neumann map on the measured boundary determines the corresponding
local map on the newly accessible internal interface by the standard
known-region inner-extension argument. The main text records the statement and
idea of this step; the trace-space and approximation details are placed in the
appendix.

The geometric hypothesis has three components at each recovery step: sufficiently
many accessible flat faces, a generic interpolation condition on the supporting
hyperplanes together with suitable triple-intersection sets, and
face-connectedness of the recovered region to the measured patch through a chain
of cells sharing relatively open flat faces. This condition fits polyhedral or
mesh-type models, where the partition is known and the flat faces are part of
the geometry.

We now introduce the notation and geometric hypotheses used in the main theorem.
After these assumptions have been fixed, the theorem is stated in the introduction;
the proof is given in Section
4
.

1.1
Assumptions and notation

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain and let

\Gamma\subset\partial U
be a relatively open set. We use the following
trace-space convention throughout the paper. Let
\Gamma^{\circ}
denote the
relative interior of
\Gamma
after removing any lower-dimensional edges of
the flat subdivision. We write

H^{1/2}_{0}(\Gamma;\partial U)=\overline{C_{c}^{\infty}(\Gamma^{\circ})}^{\,H^{1/2}(\partial U)}

(6)

and write
H^{-1/2}(\Gamma;\partial U)
for the corresponding dual
space, with respect to the
H^{-1/2}
-
H^{1/2}
pairing. When the ambient
boundary is clear we omit
\partial U
from the notation. Equivalently, the
Dirichlet traces used below are first compactly supported away from the edges of

\Gamma
, and arbitrary elements of the displayed spaces are obtained by
completion. This convention is used in particular for flat interfaces having
edges. The lower-dimensional edge set is handled by completion of the
facewise trace spaces.

If
\sigma\in L^{\infty}(U;\operatorname{Sym}_{n})
is uniformly elliptic, the local
Dirichlet-to-Neumann map on
\Gamma
is the bounded operator

\Lambda^{U}_{\sigma,\Gamma}:H^{1/2}_{0}(\Gamma;\partial U)\to H^{-1/2}(\Gamma;\partial U)

(7)

defined weakly by

\langle\Lambda^{U}_{\sigma,\Gamma}f,h\rangle=\int_{U}\sigma\nabla u_{f}\cdot\nabla v_{h}\,\,dx,

(8)

where
u_{f}\in H^{1}(U)
solves
\operatorname{div}(\sigma\nabla u_{f})=0
in
U
with
trace
f
on
\partial U
, and
v_{h}\in H^{1}(U)
is any function with trace

h
. The right-hand side is independent of the choice of
v_{h}
.

We use the following coefficient class. A finite flat Lipschitz subdivision
means a finite Lipschitz subdivision whose nonempty relatively open interfaces
are contained in affine hyperplanes, up to lower-dimensional edges and corners;
the accessible exterior patches used in the recovery order are required to be
flat in the same sense. The coefficient class itself is recorded separately.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{D}=\{D_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in A}
be a finite family of pairwise
disjoint connected Lipschitz domains such that

\overline{\Omega}=\bigcup_{\alpha\in A}\overline{D_{\alpha}}.

(9)

Let
N_{\alpha}\geq 0
. When all degrees are bounded by the same integer

N
, we write
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{D},N)
for

\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{D},\{N_{\alpha}\})
with
N_{\alpha}=N
for every cell.
We say that
\sigma
belongs to

\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{D},\{N_{\alpha}\})
if there are symmetric matrix
polynomials

P_{\alpha}\in\operatorname{Poly}_{\leq N_{\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\operatorname{Sym}_{n})

(10)

such that
\sigma=P_{\alpha}
a.e. in
D_{\alpha}
, and
\sigma
is uniformly
elliptic in
\Omega
.

The domains
D_{\alpha}
and the degree bounds
N_{\alpha}
are known. The
unknowns are the coefficients of the polynomial matrices
P_{\alpha}
.

The class includes conductivities with jumps across interfaces, as in cellwise
parametrized models. At an accessible interface, the local boundary
determination argument is applied from the side of the cell being recovered,
where the coefficient is the restriction of a single polynomial.

We also use the following geometric genericity condition for finite families of
supporting hyperplanes.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
N\geq 0
. A finite family
\{H_{i}\}_{i=1}^{M}
of distinct affine
hyperplanes in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called
N
-generic if, for each
i
, there is a
set

J_{i}\subset\{1,\ldots,M\}\setminus\{i\},\qquad|J_{i}|=N+2,

(11)

such that:

(a)

whenever
j,k\in J_{i}
,
j\neq k
, the normals of

H_{i},H_{j},H_{k}
are linearly independent;

(b)

inside
H_{i}
, the induced hyperplanes

H_{i}\cap H_{j}
,
j\in J_{i}
, form a simple affine arrangement: pairwise
intersections have codimension two in
H_{i}
, and no pairwise intersection
is contained in a third induced hyperplane.

For
N=0
the condition reads:
|J_{i}|=2
, the three normals indexed by

i
and
J_{i}
are linearly independent, and condition (b) reduces to the
codimension-two requirement.

1.2
Admissible flat-face recovery orders

The recovery order records two geometric requirements: enough generic accessible
faces for Theorem
2.6
, and face-connectedness of the
recovered region to the measured boundary patch so that Lemma

A.3
and Proposition
3.1
apply.

Let
A
be the finite index set of cells and let

\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{L}
be an ordering of
A
. Define the remaining
domain at step
r
by

\Omega_{r}=\operatorname{int}\left(\bigcup_{s=r}^{L}\overline{D_{\alpha_{s}}}\right),\qquad r=1,\ldots,L,

(12)

so that
\Omega_{1}=\Omega
, while
\Omega_{r+1}
is obtained from

\Omega_{r}
by removing the recovered cell
D_{\alpha_{r}}
. For
r>1
put

G_{r}=\Omega\setminus\overline{\Omega_{r}}.

(13)

Thus
G_{r}
is the region recovered before step
r
.

The model case is a polyhedral mesh. At the first step one uses flat exterior
faces contained in the measured set. After some cells have been recovered,
they form a known region. A remaining cell is recoverable when sufficiently
many of its open planar faces, relative to the boundary of the remaining domain,
are accessible in generic position. These faces may be original exterior faces
or interfaces with the previously recovered region; the condition is imposed on
relatively open face patches rather than on lower-dimensional contacts.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\partial\Omega
be the initial measured set. The ordering

\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{L}
is called an admissible flat-face recovery order
relative to
\Sigma
and the degree bounds
N_{\alpha}
if the following
conditions hold for each
r
:

(i)

\Omega_{r}
is a Lipschitz domain. If
r>1
, then the already
recovered region
G_{r}
is a nonempty face-connected Lipschitz domain whose
boundary contains a nonempty relatively open flat patch

\Sigma_{r}\subset\Sigma
. This patch contains a relatively compact subpatch
with an auxiliary exterior half-ball: there is a ball
B_{r}
such that, after
an affine change of coordinates in
B_{r}
,

\Omega\cap B_{r}=\{x_{n}>0\}\cap B_{r},\qquad B_{r}\cap\partial\Omega=\{x_{n}=0\}\cap B_{r}\Subset\Sigma_{r}.

(14)

The face-connectedness is taken with respect to the cells already recovered:
every cell in
G_{r}
can be joined to the cell adjacent to
\Sigma_{r}
by a
chain of already recovered cells meeting along nonempty relatively open flat
faces.

(ii)

The cell
D_{\alpha_{r}}
has flat accessible patches

\Gamma_{r,i}\subset\partial D_{\alpha_{r}}\cap\partial\Omega_{r},\qquad i=1,\ldots,M_{r},

(15)

contained in the relative interiors of flat faces. For
r=1
, these
patches are contained in the measured set
\Sigma
. For
r>1
, each patch
is either contained in
\Sigma
or lies in

\partial G_{r}\cap\partial\Omega_{r}
, that is, on an interface with the already
recovered region.

(iii)

M_{r}\geq N_{\alpha_{r}}+3
, and the supporting hyperplanes

H_{r,1},\ldots,H_{r,M_{r}}
of the accessible patches are

N_{\alpha_{r}}
-generic. We fix sets
J_{r,i}
,
|J_{r,i}|=N_{\alpha_{r}}+2
,
witnessing Definition
1.2
.

(iv)

For every
i
and every two distinct indices
j,k\in J_{r,i}
, there
is a nonempty relatively open set

W_{r,ijk}\subset H_{r,i}\cap H_{r,j}\cap H_{r,k}.

(16)

These common triple regions are the regions on which the Schur forms from
three faces will be compared in the local recovery step.

(v)

For
r>1
, put

\Xi_{r}=\partial G_{r}\cap\partial\Omega_{r},

(17)

the full interface between the recovered region and the remaining domain.
Every accessible patch which is not already contained in
\Sigma
lies in

\Xi_{r}
. The interface
\Xi_{r}
is a finite union of relatively open flat
interface patches, up to lower-dimensional edges and corners.

The formulation uses the remaining domains because the inner-extension step is
applied to connected recovered regions, not only to the adjacency graph of the
partition. Once the recovered region is face-connected through flat interfaces
and the coefficient in that region is piecewise polynomial, Lemma

A.3
supplies the Runge approximation needed in
Proposition
3.1
.

1.3
Main result

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
n\geq 3
, let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain, and let

\mathcal{D}=\{D_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in A}
be a known finite flat Lipschitz
subdivision of
\Omega
. Let
N_{\alpha}\geq 0
. Suppose that

\sigma^{(1)},\sigma^{(2)}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{D},\{N_{\alpha}\})
, and assume
that, at every recovery step and for
j=1,2
, the polynomial extension of

\sigma^{(j)}|_{D_{\alpha_{r}}}
is positive definite on the triple-intersection
sets
W_{r,ijk}
appearing in Definition
1.3
. We also
assume that the polynomial extensions in the recovered cells adjacent to the
auxiliary exterior half-balls remain elliptic in those half-balls whenever the
half-balls are used.

Let
\Sigma\subset\partial\Omega
be a nonempty relatively open measured set.
Assume that the subdivision admits an admissible flat-face recovery order
relative to
\Sigma
, in the sense of Definition
1.3
.
If

\Lambda^{\Omega}_{\sigma^{(1)},\Sigma}=\Lambda^{\Omega}_{\sigma^{(2)},\Sigma},

(18)

then

\sigma^{(1)}=\sigma^{(2)}\quad\text{in }\Omega.

(19)

Equivalently, the polynomial matrices defining the two conductivities agree on
every cell of the subdivision.

The same uniqueness theorem gives a compact Hölder stability estimate after
one regards the polynomial coefficients as finite-dimensional parameters. Let

\mathcal{V}=\prod_{\alpha\in A}\operatorname{Poly}_{\leq N_{\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\operatorname{Sym}_{n}).

(20)

For
P=(P_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in A}\in\mathcal{V}
, write
\sigma_{P}
for the
cellwise coefficient
\sigma_{P}=P_{\alpha}
in
D_{\alpha}
. Let

\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{V}
be an open set on which the subdivision,
recovery order, and measured patch are fixed, and on which the ellipticity and
auxiliary positivity hypotheses of Theorem
1.4
hold. Such open sets
are obtained, for example, by imposing the relevant ellipticity conditions with
a positive margin on the closures of the cells and on the finitely many auxiliary
sets used in the recovery order.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{V}
be as above and let

K\Subset\mathcal{A}
be compact. Then there are constants

C>0
and
\theta\in(0,1]
such that, for all
P,Q\in K
,

\max_{\alpha\in A}\|P_{\alpha}-Q_{\alpha}\|_{C(\overline{D_{\alpha}})}\leq C\bigl\|\Lambda^{\Omega}_{\sigma_{P},\Sigma}-\Lambda^{\Omega}_{\sigma_{Q},\Sigma}\bigr\|_{\mathcal{L}(H^{1/2}_{0}(\Sigma),H^{-1/2}(\Sigma))}^{\theta}.

(21)

The constants depend on
K
, the fixed subdivision and recovery order, and the
chosen norms, but not on
P,Q\in K
.

Proof.

The space
\mathcal{V}
is finite-dimensional. The map

F(P)=\Lambda^{\Omega}_{\sigma_{P},\Sigma}

(22)

from
\mathcal{A}
to

\mathcal{L}(H^{1/2}_{0}(\Sigma),H^{-1/2}(\Sigma))
is real analytic in the
operator norm. Indeed, after fixing a bounded right inverse for the trace map,
the zero-boundary part of the solution is obtained by inverting the uniformly
elliptic Dirichlet operator

L_{P}:H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\to H^{-1}(\Omega),\qquad\langle L_{P}u,v\rangle=\int_{\Omega}\sigma_{P}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\,\,dx.

(23)

The map
P\mapsto L_{P}
is affine, and inversion is real analytic on the open
set of bounded isomorphisms. Composing with the trace extension and the weak
formula for the DN map gives the claimed analyticity.

By Theorem
1.4
,

F(P)=F(Q)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad P=Q,\qquad P,Q\in\mathcal{A}.

(24)

Applying
[
13
, Theorem 3.1]
with recovered quantity

R(P)=P
gives a Hölder estimate for
P-Q
on
K
. Since all norms on
the finite-dimensional space
\mathcal{V}
are equivalent, this gives
(
21
).
∎

The proof separates the algebraic and analytic parts of the argument. Section

2
proves the one-cell recovery theorem from flat-face Schur invariants. Section
3
proves the inner-extension result,
which determines the local DN map on an internal interface from the local DN map
on the measured boundary through a known region. Section

4
combines these two ingredients to prove Theorem

1.4
.

Relative to the piecewise constant anisotropic result of

[
2
]
, the present theorem treats polynomial
anisotropic pieces. The geometric role of our many-face hypothesis is analogous
to the curved-boundary hypothesis in that work: there the variation of tangent
planes gives, through boundary determination, enough independent directions to
identify the anisotropic tensor, while here the same information is supplied by
several flat faces with independent conormals. This removes the Euclidean gauge
by combining the corresponding Schur forms. The inner-extension argument then
supplies the local DN information needed at internal faces before the local
polynomial recovery theorem is applied.

2
Flat-cell recovery

This section proves the local result used at each recovery step. The question
is local to a single cell: if boundary invariants of a polynomial conductivity
are known on sufficiently many flat faces, do they determine the polynomial
matrix? The theorem below gives an affirmative answer under a genericity
condition, using only the zeroth-order boundary invariant, the conormal Schur
form. The argument rests on two elementary observations. First, along each
supporting hyperplane the Schur form of a polynomial conductivity is itself a
matrix of polynomials, so its knowledge on a small patch propagates to the
whole hyperplane by the identity theorem for polynomials. Second, at a point
lying on three hyperplanes with linearly independent conormals, the three Schur
forms determine the matrix. The polynomial pieces are then recovered by a
divisibility argument on the resulting hyperplane arrangement.

2.1
The Schur form as a polynomial face invariant

Let
V\simeq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
H\subset V
be an affine hyperplane.
At a point of
H
, choose a nonzero conormal
\ell\in V^{*}
to

H
. We view a conductivity matrix here as a positive definite symmetric
bilinear form on covectors. For such a form
A
, define
its conormal Schur form by

S_{\ell}(A)([\xi],[\eta])=A(\ell,\ell)A(\xi,\eta)-A(\ell,\xi)A(\ell,\eta),\qquad[\xi],[\eta]\in V^{*}/\mathbb{R}\ell\simeq T^{*}H.

(25)

This is independent of the representatives
\xi,\eta
. Replacing

\ell
by
c\ell
multiplies
S_{\ell}(A)
by
c^{2}
; in particular

S_{-\ell}=S_{\ell}
, so the Schur form does not depend on a choice of side
of
H
, and equalities of Schur forms are independent of the normalization of
the conormal. When a Euclidean unit conormal to
H
is fixed we write simply

S_{H}(A)
.

If Euclidean coordinates are chosen so that
H=\{x_{n}=0\}
and

\ell=dx_{n}
, and if

A=\begin{pmatrix}A_{T}&b\\
b^{T}&c\end{pmatrix},

(26)

then

S_{H}(A)=cA_{T}-bb^{T},

(27)

as a quadratic form on
T^{*}H
. Equivalently, if

DF|_{H}=\begin{pmatrix}I_{n-1}&-b/c\\
0&1/c\end{pmatrix},

(28)

then the pushed-forward conductivity
F_{*}A
has, at the face, zero
normal-tangential block, normal-normal entry equal to one, and tangential block

S_{H}(A)
. This identifies the Schur form as the gauge-invariant content of
the conductivity along the face.

The boundary-determination input used below is naturally stated for Riemannian
metrics. The connection with the conductivity coefficient
is through the associated Riemannian metric. For
n\geq 3
, set

g_{\sigma}=(\det\sigma)^{1/(n-2)}\sigma^{-1},

(29)

viewed as a covariant metric. The conductivity equation for
\sigma
is the
Laplace–Beltrami equation for
g_{\sigma}
, in the standard anisotropic
conductivity correspondence; the inverse relation is

\sigma=(\det g_{\sigma})^{1/2}\,g_{\sigma}^{-1}.

(30)

Let

h_{H}(\sigma)=g_{\sigma}|_{TH}
denote the tangential metric induced on the
face. In coordinates as above,

h_{H}(\sigma)=(\det S_{H}(\sigma))^{1/(n-2)}S_{H}(\sigma)^{-1},\qquad S_{H}(\sigma)=(\det h_{H}(\sigma))h_{H}(\sigma)^{-1}.

(31)

Indeed, with
B=A_{T}-bc^{-1}b^{T}
, the block inverse formula gives

h_{H}(\sigma)=(\det\sigma)^{1/(n-2)}B^{-1}
. Since

\det\sigma=c\det B
and
S_{H}(\sigma)=cB
, this gives the first
identity in (
31
); taking determinants gives the
second. Note that the two formulas in (
31
) are
mutually inverse bijections of the positive definite cone of

(n-1)\times(n-1)
symmetric matrices: if

h=(\det S)^{1/(n-2)}S^{-1}
, then

\det h=(\det S)^{1/(n-2)}
and therefore
S=(\det h)h^{-1}
.

For polynomial coefficients the Schur form is a polynomial face invariant.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
P\in\operatorname{Poly}_{\leq N}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\operatorname{Sym}_{n})
, let
H\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be
an affine hyperplane with a fixed unit conormal, and fix affine coordinates on

H
. Then every entry of
x\mapsto S_{H}(P(x))
,
x\in H
, is a
polynomial of degree at most
2N
on
H
. In particular, if

P_{1},P_{2}\in\operatorname{Poly}_{\leq N}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\operatorname{Sym}_{n})
and

S_{H}(P_{1})=S_{H}(P_{2})
on a nonempty relatively open subset of
H
, then

S_{H}(P_{1})=S_{H}(P_{2})
on all of
H
.

Proof.

By (
25
), every entry of
S_{H}(P(x))
is a quadratic
expression, with constant coefficients, in the entries of
P(x)
, and the
entries of
P|_{H}
are polynomials of degree at most
N
in affine
coordinates on
H
. The last statement is the identity theorem for
polynomials: a polynomial on
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
vanishing on a nonempty open set
vanishes identically.
∎

No positivity is required in Lemma
2.1
; the entries of

S_{H}(P)
are polynomials on all of
H
, whether or not
P
remains
elliptic there.

The Schur form is supplied on measured patches by the following standard
zeroth-order boundary determination fact. It is the principal-symbol part of
the anisotropic boundary determination argument in
[
22
,
25
]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
W
be a domain whose boundary is smooth in a neighborhood of a relatively
open patch
\Gamma
. Let
g_{1},g_{2}
be smooth Riemannian metrics in a
one-sided neighborhood of
\Gamma
. If the local Riemannian
Dirichlet-to-Neumann maps agree on
\Gamma
, then

g_{1}|_{T\Gamma}=g_{2}|_{T\Gamma}\qquad\text{on }\Gamma.

(32)

Indeed, in boundary coordinates the principal symbol of the Riemannian
Dirichlet-to-Neumann map is
|\xi|_{g|_{T\Gamma}}
. Equality of the local maps
therefore gives equality of this principal symbol, and hence of the induced
boundary metrics.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
W
be a Lipschitz domain whose boundary is flat in a neighborhood of a
relatively open patch
\Gamma\subset H
, and suppose that

2.2
Three faces determine the matrix pointwise

We next record the finite-dimensional algebraic step which removes the

Lemma 2.4
.

Let

be affine hyperplanes through a point

be nonzero conormals to

at

(35)

as quadratic forms on

.

Proof.

Rescaling each

to Euclidean unit length multiplies both sides of
(

the conormals to be unit. Set

and

g_{B}=(\det B)^{1/(n-2)}B^{-1}

and let
T_{r}=\ker\ell_{r}
be the tangent space of

at
x_{0}

each

as in
(

expresses the matrix of

, and likewise for

.
Since the two maps in (
31

bijections of the positive definite cone, the hypothesis
(
35

2.3
Vanishing on a hyperplane arrangement

If
L\subset E
is
an affine hyperplane, a defining affine function for
L
means a nonconstant
affine function
\ell:E\to\mathbb{R}
such that
L=\{\ell=0\}
; it is unique up to
multiplication by a nonzero constant. The coordinate ring of an affine space
is simply the ring of polynomial functions on that space. Thus the coordinate
ring of
L_{r}
is
\operatorname{Poly}(L_{r})
, equivalently

\operatorname{Poly}(E)/(\ell_{r})
. Saying that

\ell_{a}|_{L_{r}}
divides
q|_{L_{r}}
means that

q|_{L_{r}}=(\ell_{a}|_{L_{r}})q_{a,r}
for some polynomial function

q_{a,r}
on
L_{r}
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
E
be an affine space of dimension at least two, and let

L_{1},\ldots,L_{s}\subset E
,
s\geq 2
, be distinct affine hyperplanes with
simple pairwise intersections:
L_{a}\cap L_{b}
has codimension two in
E

for
a\neq b
, and no such intersection is contained in a third
L_{c}
.
If
q\in\operatorname{Poly}_{\leq s-2}(E)
vanishes on every pairwise
intersection
L_{a}\cap L_{b}
, then
q=0
.

Proof.

Let
\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{s}
be defining affine functions for

L_{1},\ldots,L_{s}
. We first show that
q
vanishes on each one of the
hyperplanes
L_{r}
. Fix
r
. For every
a\neq r
, the intersection

L_{a}\cap L_{r}
is a hyperplane inside the affine space
L_{r}
, and

q|_{L_{r}}
vanishes on it. Since

L_{a}\cap L_{r}=\{\ell_{a}|_{L_{r}}=0\}
, this implies that

\ell_{a}|_{L_{r}}
divides
q|_{L_{r}}
in
\operatorname{Poly}(L_{r})
.
Indeed, after choosing affine coordinates on
L_{r}
in which

\ell_{a}|_{L_{r}}
is one of the coordinate functions, the assertion is just the
fact that a polynomial which vanishes when that coordinate is zero has that
coordinate as a factor.

The hyperplanes
L_{a}\cap L_{r}\subset L_{r}
, with
a\neq r
, are distinct. If

L_{a}\cap L_{r}=L_{b}\cap L_{r}
for some distinct
a,b\neq r
, then this
codimension-two intersection in
E
would be contained in the third
hyperplane
L_{b}
, contradicting the simplicity assumption. Hence the affine
linear functions
\ell_{a}|_{L_{r}}
,
a\neq r
, define distinct hyperplanes in

L_{r}
. In the polynomial ring
\operatorname{Poly}(L_{r})
, these linear
factors are pairwise coprime. Since each of them divides
q|_{L_{r}}
, their
product divides
q|_{L_{r}}
:

\prod_{a\neq r}\ell_{a}|_{L_{r}}\;\;\text{divides}\;\;q|_{L_{r}}.

(37)

The product has degree
s-1
, whereas
\deg(q|_{L_{r}})\leq s-2
. Therefore

q|_{L_{r}}=0
.

Since
r
was arbitrary,
q
vanishes on every
L_{r}
. Applying the same
one-factor argument in
E
, each
\ell_{r}
divides
q
in

\operatorname{Poly}(E)
. The defining functions
\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{s}

are pairwise coprime because the hyperplanes
L_{1},\ldots,L_{s}
are distinct.
Consequently their product
\ell_{1}\cdots\ell_{s}
, of degree
s
, divides

q
. But
\deg q\leq s-2
, so this is possible only if
q=0
.
∎

2.4
The one-cell recovery theorem

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
n\geq 3
. Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a cell and let

\Gamma_{i}\subset\partial D\cap H_{i},\qquad i=1,\ldots,M,

(38)

be nonempty relatively open flat patches contained in the relative interiors of
flat faces of
D
. Let

P_{j}\in\operatorname{Poly}_{\leq N}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\operatorname{Sym}_{n}),\qquad j=1,2,

(39)

be uniformly positive definite in the cell
D
. Assume:

(i)

M\geq N+3
and the hyperplanes
H_{1},\ldots,H_{M}
are

N
-generic; fix sets
J_{i}
,
|J_{i}|=N+2
, witnessing Definition

1.2
;

(ii)

for every
i
and every two distinct indices
j,k\in J_{i}
, there is
a nonempty relatively open subset

W_{ijk}\subset H_{i}\cap H_{j}\cap H_{k}

(40)

on which
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
are both positive definite.

If the Schur forms agree on all accessible patches,

S_{H_{i}}(P_{1})=S_{H_{i}}(P_{2})\qquad\text{on }\Gamma_{i},\qquad i=1,\ldots,M,

(41)

then
P_{1}=P_{2}
as matrix polynomials. Consequently the two conductivity
pieces agree in
D
.

By the genericity condition (a), each triple intersection

H_{i}\cap H_{j}\cap H_{k}
appearing in (ii) is a nonempty affine subspace of
dimension
n-3
; when
n=3
it is a single point, and
W_{ijk}
is that
point.

Proof.

Step 1: propagation along the supporting hyperplanes.

By Lemma
2.1
, the entries of
S_{H_{i}}(P_{1})
and

S_{H_{i}}(P_{2})
are polynomials on
H_{i}
, so the equality
(
41
) on the nonempty relatively open
patch
\Gamma_{i}
extends to

S_{H_{i}}(P_{1})=S_{H_{i}}(P_{2})\qquad\text{on all of }H_{i},\qquad i=1,\ldots,M.

(42)

Step 2: pointwise recovery on triple intersections.

Fix
i
and distinct
j,k\in J_{i}
, and put

F_{ijk}=H_{i}\cap H_{j}\cap H_{k}
. Let
x\in W_{ijk}
. The three hyperplanes

H_{i},H_{j},H_{k}
pass through
x
with linearly independent conormals, the
matrices
P_{1}(x)
and
P_{2}(x)
are positive definite, and by
(
42
) the three Schur form equalities hold
at
x
. Lemma
2.4
gives

P_{1}(x)=P_{2}(x)
. Thus
Q=P_{1}-P_{2}
vanishes on
W_{ijk}
, a nonempty
relatively open subset of the affine subspace
F_{ijk}
. The entries of

Q
are polynomials, so by the identity theorem on
F_{ijk}
,

Q=0\qquad\text{on }F_{ijk}\qquad\text{for every }i\text{ and all distinct }j,k\in J_{i}.

(43)

Step 3: vanishing on each supporting hyperplane.

Fix
i
and set

E=H_{i},\qquad L_{j}=H_{i}\cap H_{j}\subset H_{i},\qquad j\in J_{i}.

(44)

By the genericity conditions,
\{L_{j}:j\in J_{i}\}
is a family of
s=N+2

distinct hyperplanes of
E
with simple pairwise intersections, and

L_{j}\cap L_{k}=F_{ijk}
. Every scalar entry

q=(P_{1}-P_{2})_{\beta\gamma}|_{H_{i}}
is a polynomial of degree at most

N=s-2
on
E
, and it vanishes on every
L_{j}\cap L_{k}
by
(
43
). Lemma
2.5
gives

q=0
. Thus

(P_{1}-P_{2})|_{H_{i}}=0\qquad\text{for every }i=1,\ldots,M.

(45)

Step 4: divisibility.

Let
\ell_{i}
be a defining affine function for
H_{i}
. For each scalar
entry
Q_{\beta\gamma}=(P_{1}-P_{2})_{\beta\gamma}
, the identity

Q_{\beta\gamma}|_{H_{i}}=0
implies that
\ell_{i}
divides

Q_{\beta\gamma}
. The hyperplanes are distinct, hence the factors

\ell_{i}
are pairwise coprime, and

\ell_{1}\ell_{2}\cdots\ell_{M}\quad\text{divides}\quad Q_{\beta\gamma}.

(46)

But
M\geq N+3>N\geq\deg Q_{\beta\gamma}
. Hence
Q_{\beta\gamma}=0
. Since
this holds for all entries,
P_{1}=P_{2}
as matrix polynomials.
∎

Theorem
2.6
uses the Schur forms on the accessible

2.3
. More
precisely, if

is a domain whose boundary is flat near
\Gamma_{i}
, if
the interior side of

near
\Gamma_{i}

3
Inner extension of the local DN map

We use the following known-region continuation step. Suppose that the
conductivity has already been recovered in a connected region lying between the
measured boundary and an internal interface. Then the local DN map on the
measured boundary determines the local DN map on that internal interface. This
is the scalar analogue of the inner-extension step used in Ikehata’s inclusion
argument
[
16
]
and in the layer-stripping proof for piecewise
homogeneous elasticity
[
11
]
.

Let
\Omega_{2}\subset\Omega_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be bounded Lipschitz domains and put

G=\Omega_{1}\setminus\overline{\Omega_{2}},\qquad\Sigma_{1}=\partial\Omega_{1}\cap\partial G,\qquad\Sigma_{2}=\partial\Omega_{2}\cap\partial G.

(48)

The set
G
is the known region and
\Sigma_{2}
is the full interface through
which the remaining domain is seen. The local boundary determination step may
later use only a smaller patch
\Gamma_{2}\subset\Sigma_{2}
, but the
continuation statement is most naturally stated on the whole interface. The
full-interface trace spaces are the facewise spaces described in Appendix

A
. Notice that
\Omega_{2}
is not assumed to be
compactly contained in
\Omega_{1}
; when neighborhoods of
\Omega_{2}
are used
below, the closure is taken relative to
\Omega_{1}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\Omega_{2}\subset\Omega_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be bounded Lipschitz domains such
that
G=\Omega_{1}\setminus\overline{\Omega_{2}}
is a nonempty face-connected
Lipschitz known region of the kind described in Lemma

A.2
. Let
\Gamma_{1}\subset\Sigma_{1}
be a nonempty
relatively open flat patch containing a relatively compact subpatch with an
auxiliary exterior half-ball: after an affine change of coordinates in a ball

B
,

\Omega_{1}\cap B=\{x_{n}>0\}\cap B,\qquad B\cap\partial\Omega_{1}=\{x_{n}=0\}\cap B\Subset\Gamma_{1},

(49)

and the coefficient in the adjacent cell of
G
extends as a uniformly
elliptic polynomial coefficient to
B
. Let

\Gamma_{2}\subset\Sigma_{2}
be any relatively open subpatch.

Let
\sigma^{(1)},\sigma^{(2)}
be uniformly elliptic conductivities on

\Omega_{1}
, and assume that

\sigma^{(1)}=\sigma^{(2)}=\sigma^{0}\quad\text{in }G,

(50)

where
\sigma^{0}
is known in
G
, is piecewise polynomial there, and is
extended to a known uniformly elliptic coefficient on
\Omega_{1}
. If

\Lambda^{\Omega_{1}}_{\sigma^{(1)},\Gamma_{1}}=\Lambda^{\Omega_{1}}_{\sigma^{(2)},\Gamma_{1}},

(51)

then the local DN maps on the full accessible interface agree:

\Lambda^{\Omega_{2}}_{\sigma^{(1)},\Sigma_{2}}=\Lambda^{\Omega_{2}}_{\sigma^{(2)},\Sigma_{2}}.

(52)

Consequently, by restriction to any subpatch
\Gamma_{2}\subset\Sigma_{2}
,

\Lambda^{\Omega_{2}}_{\sigma^{(1)},\Gamma_{2}}=\Lambda^{\Omega_{2}}_{\sigma^{(2)},\Gamma_{2}}.

(53)

Idea of the proof.

One introduces the single-layer operator
S^{\Sigma_{2}}_{\tau}
, which maps an
interface source on
\Sigma_{2}
to the trace on
\Sigma_{2}
of the corresponding
Green solution in
\Omega_{1}
. Equality of the two inner single-layer
operators follows once one knows equality of the Green pairings

H(\mathcal{G}_{\sigma^{(1)}}F)=H(\mathcal{G}_{\sigma^{(2)}}F)

(54)

for sources
F,H
compactly supported in the known region
G
. Interface
sources are obtained as limits of such interior sources, by translating them a
small distance into
G
along flat interface patches.

For sources
F,H
whose supports are compactly contained in
G
, the reference solutions

\mathcal{G}_{\sigma^{0}}F
and
\mathcal{G}_{\sigma^{0}}H
solve the homogeneous
equation near the remaining domain, in the relative sense inside
\Omega_{1}
.
Lemma
A.3
approximates their restrictions to

\Omega_{2}
by reference solutions whose boundary values are supported in

\Gamma_{1}
. The standard DN-difference identity then expresses the above
Green pairings in terms of the outer local DN map and the known coefficient

\sigma^{0}
. Hence the pairings, and therefore the full-interface
single-layer operators, agree for
\sigma^{(1)}
and
\sigma^{(2)}
.

Finally, the transmission identity on
\Sigma_{2}
gives

\bigl(\Lambda^{\Omega_{2}}_{\sigma^{(j)},\Sigma_{2}}-\Lambda^{G,+}_{\sigma^{0},\Sigma_{2}}\bigr)S^{\Sigma_{2}}_{\sigma^{(j)}}=I.

(55)

The operator in parentheses is injective by the usual gluing argument and
uniqueness of the zero Dirichlet problem in
\Omega_{1}
. Therefore it is the
inverse of the single-layer operator. Since the known-region DN map is fixed
and the single-layer operators agree, the inner DN maps agree. The details,
including the trace spaces on interfaces with edges, are given in Appendix

A
.

4
Proof of the main theorem

We prove Theorem
1.4
by combining the one-cell recovery theorem with

.

Appendix A
Details of the inner-extension argument

This appendix gives the proof of Proposition

. The
argument is included to fix the trace-space conventions on the full internal
interface and to make clear that only relative neighborhoods of the remaining

A.1
Interface trace spaces and layer potentials

Let
\Omega_{2}\subset\Omega_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be bounded Lipschitz domains and put

G=\Omega_{1}\setminus\overline{\Omega_{2}}.

(63)

Assume that
G
is a nonempty connected Lipschitz domain. Its boundary has
an outer part and an inner part, denoted by

\Sigma_{1}=\partial\Omega_{1}\cap\partial G,\qquad\Sigma_{2}=\partial\Omega_{2}\cap\partial G.

(64)

We use a relatively open measured patch
\Gamma_{1}\subset\Sigma_{1}
. The set

\Sigma_{2}
is the full accessible inner interface. Although the later
boundary determination step may use only a smaller patch

\Gamma_{2}\subset\Sigma_{2}
, the single-layer argument is naturally formulated on
the full accessible interface
\Sigma_{2}
, since a source supported on one patch
can have nonzero trace on the rest of the interface.

Because
\Sigma_{2}
may be a finite union of flat faces meeting along edges,
we fix once and for all the trace spaces used on this full interface. Let

\Sigma_{2}^{\circ}
be the union of the relative interiors of those faces. Define

\mathcal{H}^{1/2}(\Sigma_{2})
to be the completion of

C_{c}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{2}^{\circ})
in the common trace norm

\begin{split}\|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1/2}(\Sigma_{2})}=\inf\{&\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{2})}+\|v\|_{H^{1}(G)}:\\
u|_{\Sigma_{2}}=v|_{\Sigma_{2}}=\varphi,\\
&u=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega_{2}\setminus\overline{\Sigma_{2}},\quad v=0\text{ on }\partial G\setminus\overline{\Sigma_{2}}\}.\end{split}

(65)

Here traces are understood facewise and then completed. We put

\mathcal{H}^{-1/2}(\Sigma_{2})=\bigl(\mathcal{H}^{1/2}(\Sigma_{2})\bigr)^{*}.

(66)

The duality extends the
L^{2}
-pairing on

C_{c}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{2}^{\circ})
. The embedded space

C_{c}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{2}^{\circ})
is dense in this dual by the standard facewise
Sobolev scale and the finite decomposition into flat faces. On a compact
subpatch of one face these spaces agree with the usual Sobolev trace spaces.
All traces and conormal distributions below are understood facewise and then
completed in the spaces
\mathcal{H}^{\pm 1/2}(\Sigma_{2})
.

We fix a known uniformly elliptic piecewise polynomial coefficient

\sigma^{0}
on
\Omega_{1}
which agrees with the recovered coefficient in

G
. Inside
\Omega_{2}
, any such extension may be chosen; it serves as the
reference coefficient for the Green-operator comparison. For

\tau\in L^{\infty}(\Omega_{1};\operatorname{Sym}_{n})
uniformly elliptic, write

\mathcal{G}_{\tau}F=U\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{1})

(67)

for the weak solution of

\int_{\Omega_{1}}\tau\nabla U\cdot\nabla\phi\,dx=F(\phi),\qquad\phi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{1}).

(68)

If
f\in\mathcal{H}^{-1/2}(\Sigma_{2})
, let

T_{f}\in H^{-1}(\Omega_{1})
be the interface source

T_{f}(\phi)=\langle f,\phi|_{\Sigma_{2}}\rangle,\qquad\phi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{1}),

(69)

and define the single-layer operator on the full accessible interface by

S^{\Sigma_{2}}_{\tau}f=(\mathcal{G}_{\tau}T_{f})|_{\Sigma_{2}}.

(70)

Here and below, traces and pairings on
\Sigma_{2}
are understood in the

\mathcal{H}^{1/2}
-
\mathcal{H}^{-1/2}
sense fixed in
(
65
). The mapping properties used later are recorded in
the following lemma.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\gamma
be a uniformly elliptic coefficient on
\Omega_{2}
, let

\beta
be a uniformly elliptic coefficient on
G
, and let
\tau
be a
uniformly elliptic coefficient on
\Omega_{1}
. The local DN maps

\begin{gathered}\Lambda^{\Omega_{2}}_{\gamma,\Sigma_{2}}:\mathcal{H}^{1/2}(\Sigma_{2})\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}^{-1/2}(\Sigma_{2}),\\
\Lambda^{G,+}_{\beta,\Sigma_{2}}:\mathcal{H}^{1/2}(\Sigma_{2})\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}^{-1/2}(\Sigma_{2})\end{gathered}

(71)

are bounded, where
\Lambda^{G,+}_{\beta,\Sigma_{2}}
uses zero Dirichlet boundary values on

\partial G\setminus\overline{\Sigma_{2}}
and the conormal on
\Sigma_{2}
with
normal pointing out of
\Omega_{2}
. Consequently

K_{\gamma,\beta}:=\Lambda^{\Omega_{2}}_{\gamma,\Sigma_{2}}-\Lambda^{G,+}_{\beta,\Sigma_{2}}

(72)

is bounded from
\mathcal{H}^{1/2}(\Sigma_{2})
to

\mathcal{H}^{-1/2}(\Sigma_{2})
. The interface source map

f\mapsto T_{f}
is bounded

\mathcal{H}^{-1/2}(\Sigma_{2})\to H^{-1}(\Omega_{1})
, and

S^{\Sigma_{2}}_{\tau}:\mathcal{H}^{-1/2}(\Sigma_{2})\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}^{1/2}(\Sigma_{2})

(73)

is bounded. If
\tau=\gamma
in
\Omega_{2}
and
\tau=\beta
in
G
, then

K_{\gamma,\beta}S^{\Sigma_{2}}_{\tau}f=f,\qquad f\in\mathcal{H}^{-1/2}(\Sigma_{2}).

(74)

Proof.

For
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{2}^{\circ})
, the assertion is the standard
trace theory on each flat face component. The DN estimates follow from the
usual energy minimization and trace theorem on
\Omega_{2}
and on
G
. On a
single smooth flat face the operator
S^{\Sigma_{2}}_{\tau}
is the classical
single-layer trace operator; in the present variational formulation its
boundedness follows directly from the estimate

\|\mathcal{G}_{\tau}T_{f}\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{1})}\leq C\|T_{f}\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega_{1})}
. The
definition of the norm in (
65
) gives the required
bound for the trace of
\mathcal{G}_{\tau}T_{f}
on
\Sigma_{2}
, and the dual
definition of
\mathcal{H}^{-1/2}(\Sigma_{2})
gives

\|T_{f}\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega_{1})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{-1/2}(\Sigma_{2})}
. Summing
the facewise estimates over the finite set of faces and passing to the
completion gives the stated global bounds.

We record the transmission identity. For smooth
f
supported
away from the edges, integrate by parts separately on
\Omega_{2}
and on

G
. On each flat face the conormal jump of

U=\mathcal{G}_{\tau}T_{f}
is exactly
f
, with the sign convention in

\Lambda^{G,+}_{\beta,\Sigma_{2}}
. The support condition on the test traces eliminates edge terms. For general
f

traces and the source in the above spaces by elements of

C_{c}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{2}^{\circ})
; the identity is continuous under these
approximations. This proves (
74
).

A.2
Unique continuation and Runge approximation

We use the following unique continuation input. A finite union of cells in the
known subdivision is called face-connected if any two cells in the union can be
joined by a chain of cells in the union such that consecutive cells share a
nonempty relatively open flat face. The statement below is the scalar
conductivity analogue of the Holmgren-based UCP step used in

[
11
]
.

Lemma A.2
.

Let
G
be a face-connected finite union of Lipschitz subdomains whose
pairwise interfaces contain relatively open flat faces. Assume that on each
subdomain
E\subset G
, the coefficient
\sigma^{0}
is the restriction of a
symmetric matrix polynomial which remains uniformly elliptic in a neighborhood
of
\overline{E}
. The union may include one auxiliary analytic collar or
ball attached to a flat exterior face, with
\sigma^{0}
given there by the same
polynomial extension as in the adjacent cell of the known region. If

u\in H^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(G)
satisfies

\operatorname{div}(\sigma^{0}\nabla u)=0\quad\text{in }G

(75)

and
u
vanishes in a nonempty open subset of
G
, then
u\equiv 0
in

G
.

Proof.

Inside one subdomain the coefficients are real analytic and uniformly elliptic,
so solutions are real analytic in the interior of that subdomain. Hence
vanishing on a nonempty open subset of one subdomain implies vanishing
throughout its connected component.

We next propagate vanishing across one flat interface. Let neighboring
subdomains
E_{-}
and
E_{+}
share a relatively open flat face
F
, and
suppose that
u
vanishes in
E_{-}
up to a nonempty subpatch

F_{0}\Subset F
. Since
u\in H^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
is a weak solution across

F
, the two traces of
u
agree in
H^{1/2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(F)
, and the
conormal fluxes agree in
H^{-1/2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(F)
: for every test function
supported near
F_{0}
, integration by parts on the two sides gives

[u]_{F_{0}}=0,\qquad[\sigma^{0}\nabla u\cdot\nu]_{F_{0}}=0.

(76)

The trace and conormal derivative from the
E_{-}
-side are zero, because
u
is identically
zero there. Hence the trace of
u|_{E_{+}}
and its conormal derivative on

F_{0}
are zero. The tangential derivatives of the zero trace vanish, and
ellipticity gives the normal derivative from the conormal derivative. Thus

u|_{E_{+}}
has zero Dirichlet and zero normal derivative on the
noncharacteristic analytic hypersurface
F_{0}
.

The solution on the
E_{+}
-side is analytic up to the face by extension
across the flat interface. Since the coefficients are real analytic and
uniformly elliptic on each side, and the interface
F_{0}
is flat, hence
analytic, extend the polynomial coefficient from
E_{+}
across
F_{0}
to a full
neighborhood of the face. Extend
u|_{E_{+}}
by zero to the other side. The zero Dirichlet trace gives
an
H^{1}
extension, and the zero conormal trace removes the boundary term in the
weak formulation. The extension is therefore a weak solution of an analytic
uniformly elliptic equation in a full neighborhood of
F_{0}
. Interior
analytic regularity gives real analyticity of the extension, hence

u|_{E_{+}}
is real analytic up to
F_{0}
from the
E_{+}
-side.

Holmgren’s uniqueness theorem
[
18
]
then applies directly. It gives

u=0
in a one-sided neighborhood of
F_{0}
in
E_{+}
. Analytic continuation
inside
E_{+}
then gives vanishing throughout that subdomain. Iterating along
a face chain proves the claim.
∎

Lemma A.3
.

Assume that
G=\Omega_{1}\setminus\overline{\Omega_{2}}
is a face-connected
finite union of cells as in Lemma
A.2
, and that

\Gamma_{1}\subset\Sigma_{1}
contains a relatively compact nonempty flat
subpatch in the following explicit sense: there is a ball
B
such that,
after an affine change of coordinates in
B
,

\Omega_{1}\cap B=\{x_{n}>0\}\cap B,\qquad B\cap\partial\Omega_{1}=\{x_{n}=0\}\cap B\Subset\Gamma_{1},

(77)

and the coefficient in the cell of the known region adjacent to
B\cap\partial\Omega_{1}

extends as a uniformly elliptic polynomial coefficient to the whole ball

B
. Let
\sigma^{0}
be a known
uniformly elliptic piecewise polynomial coefficient on
\Omega_{1}
, agreeing
with the already recovered coefficient in
G
, and satisfying the hypotheses
of Lemma
A.2
in
G
. If

V\subset\Omega_{1}
is relatively open and

\overline{\Omega_{2}}^{\,\Omega_{1}}:=\overline{\Omega_{2}}\cap\Omega_{1}\subset V,

(78)

and if
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{1})
satisfies

\operatorname{div}(\sigma^{0}\nabla u)=0\quad\text{in }V,

(79)

where the equation in
V
is understood in the relative sense inside

\Omega_{1}
, then there are functions
u_{k}\in H^{1}(\Omega_{1})
, solving

\operatorname{div}(\sigma^{0}\nabla u_{k})=0\quad\text{in }\Omega_{1},

(80)

whose boundary traces are supported in
\Gamma_{1}
, such that

u_{k}\to u\quad\text{in }H^{1}(\Omega_{2}).

(81)

Proof.

Use the ball
B
from the statement, shrinking it if necessary while
preserving the displayed flatness and extension properties. Put

\Omega_{0}=\Omega_{1}\cup B.

(82)

In the above coordinates,
\Omega_{0}
is a Lipschitz domain obtained from

\Omega_{1}
by attaching the exterior half-ball

B^{-}:=B\cap\{x_{n}<0\}
across the flat patch

B\cap\partial\Omega_{1}
. Extend
\sigma^{0}
to
\Omega_{0}
by the same
elliptic polynomial on
B
. Thus the exterior half-ball is an auxiliary
analytic cell attached to the face-connected known region, as allowed in Lemma

A.2
. It supplies the initial open set outside

\Omega_{1}
from which unique continuation propagates the vanishing of the
annihilator into the known region.

Let
\mathcal{G}_{\Omega_{0}}
denote the Green operator for the extended
coefficient
\sigma^{0}
on
\Omega_{0}
with zero Dirichlet boundary
condition. Consider the subspaces of
H^{1}(\Omega_{2})

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=\{v|_{\Omega_{2}}:\ v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{1}),\ \operatorname{div}(\sigma^{0}\nabla v)=0\text{ in }V\},

(83)

\displaystyle Y

\displaystyle=\{(\mathcal{G}_{\Omega_{0}}F)|_{\Omega_{2}}:\ F\in H^{-1}(\Omega_{0}),\ \operatorname{supp}F\Subset B^{-}\}.

(84)

Each element of
Y
is the restriction to
\Omega_{2}
of a solution of the
homogeneous equation in
\Omega_{1}
. Since the solution has zero trace on

\partial\Omega_{0}
, its trace on
\partial\Omega_{1}
is supported in

B\cap\partial\Omega_{1}\Subset\Gamma_{1}
. It is therefore enough to prove
that
Y
is dense in
X
.

By Hahn–Banach, suppose that
\ell\in(H^{1}(\Omega_{2}))^{*}
annihilates
Y
.
Define
\widetilde{\ell}\in H^{-1}(\Omega_{0})
by

\widetilde{\ell}(\phi)=\ell(\phi|_{\Omega_{2}}),\qquad\phi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{0}).

(85)

Let
w=\mathcal{G}_{\Omega_{0}}\widetilde{\ell}
. For every

F\in H^{-1}(\Omega_{0})
with
\operatorname{supp}F\Subset B^{-}
, self-adjointness gives

0=\ell((\mathcal{G}_{\Omega_{0}}F)|_{\Omega_{2}})=\widetilde{\ell}(\mathcal{G}_{\Omega_{0}}F)=F(w).

(86)

Thus
w=0
in the nonempty open set
B^{-}
. Since
\widetilde{\ell}
is supported in
\overline{\Omega_{2}}^{\,\Omega_{1}}
,
the function
w
solves the homogeneous equation in the known region and in
the auxiliary half-ball. Lemma

A.2
, applied to the face-connected union of the
auxiliary half-ball and the known region, gives

w=0\quad\text{in }G

(87)

and also in the auxiliary half-ball.

Let
v|_{\Omega_{2}}\in X
, and extend
v
by zero from
\Omega_{1}
to

\Omega_{0}
. Since
w
vanishes in
G
and
w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{0})
, its restriction to

\Omega_{2}
has zero trace both on
\Sigma_{2}
and on any part of

\partial\Omega_{2}
lying on
\partial\Omega_{1}
. Extending this restriction by
zero to
\Omega_{1}
gives an admissible test function for the equation
satisfied by
v
in the relative neighborhood
V
of

\overline{\Omega_{2}}^{\,\Omega_{1}}
. Hence

\ell(v|_{\Omega_{2}})=\widetilde{\ell}(v)=\int_{\Omega_{0}}\sigma^{0}\nabla w\cdot\nabla v\,dx=\int_{\Omega_{2}}\sigma^{0}\nabla w\cdot\nabla v\,dx=0.

(88)

Thus
\ell
vanishes on
X
, and
Y
is dense in
X
.
∎

Lemma A.4
.

For every
f\in\mathcal{H}^{-1/2}(\Sigma_{2})
, the interface source
T_{f}

defined in (
69
) can be approximated in

H^{-1}(\Omega_{1})
by sources whose supports are compactly contained in

G
. More precisely, the approximating sources may be chosen on offset
surfaces inside the known region and at positive distance from
\Omega_{2}
.

Proof.

The map
f\mapsto T_{f}
is bounded from

\mathcal{H}^{-1/2}(\Sigma_{2})
to
H^{-1}(\Omega_{1})
, by the trace theorem
and the definition of
\mathcal{H}^{1/2}(\Sigma_{2})
. Since

C_{c}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{2}^{\circ})
is dense in

\mathcal{H}^{-1/2}(\Sigma_{2})
, it is enough to prove the claim for

f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{2}^{\circ})
. A partition of unity reduces this case to
the situation in which
\operatorname{supp}f
is compactly contained in the relative
interior of a single flat face.

In such a face patch choose coordinates in which

\Sigma_{2}=\{x_{n}=0\}
, the side belonging to
G
is
\{x_{n}>0\}
, and

\operatorname{supp}f\Subset\{x_{n}=0\}
is away from the edges. For all sufficiently small

\varepsilon>0
, the translated support

\{(x^{\prime},\varepsilon):x^{\prime}\in\operatorname{supp}f\}
is compactly contained in
G
. Define

F_{\varepsilon}(\phi)=\int_{\Sigma_{2}}f(x^{\prime})\phi(x^{\prime},\varepsilon)\,dx^{\prime},\qquad\phi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{1}).

(89)

Then
F_{\varepsilon}\in H^{-1}(\Omega_{1})
and

\operatorname{supp}F_{\varepsilon}\Subset G
. Moreover,

\displaystyle|F_{\varepsilon}(\phi)-T_{f}(\phi)|

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{2})}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{2}}|\phi(x^{\prime},\varepsilon)-\phi(x^{\prime},0)|^{2}\,dx^{\prime}\right)^{1/2}

(90)

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{1/2}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{2})}\|\phi\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{1})}.

(91)

The last inequality follows from the fundamental theorem of calculus in the
normal variable and Cauchy’s inequality, applied in the flat coordinate
cylinder containing the support. Hence

F_{\varepsilon}\to T_{f}
in
H^{-1}(\Omega_{1})
on this patch. Summing the
finitely many partition-of-unity pieces proves the result for smooth
f

supported away from edges, and density gives the stated result for all

f\in\mathcal{H}^{-1/2}(\Sigma_{2})
.
∎

A.3
Proof of the inner-extension proposition

The proof has three steps. First we reduce equality of the inner single-layer
operators to equality of Green pairings whose sources lie in the known region.
Then Runge approximation through the known region and equality of the outer local DN
maps determine those pairings. Finally, the full-interface transmission
identity shows that the difference between the interior DN map and the DN map
of the known region is the inverse of the single-layer operator.

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

Write
\mathcal{G}_{j}=\mathcal{G}_{\sigma^{(j)}}
and

\mathcal{G}_{0}=\mathcal{G}_{\sigma^{0}}
. We divide the proof into three steps.

Step 1: Reduction to Green pairings with sources in the known region.

We first reduce equality of full-interface single-layer operators to equality
of Green pairings with sources in the known region. We prove the single-layer equality

S^{\Sigma_{2}}_{\sigma^{(1)}}=S^{\Sigma_{2}}_{\sigma^{(2)}}.

(92)

By Lemma
A.4
and boundedness of the Green
operators, it suffices to prove that

H(\mathcal{G}_{1}F)=H(\mathcal{G}_{2}F)

(93)

whenever
F,H\in H^{-1}(\Omega_{1})
have supports compactly contained in the
known region
G
, in particular at positive distance from
\Omega_{2}
.
Applying Lemma
A.4
to
T_{f}
and
T_{h}
, and then using
the boundedness of the Green operators, gives

\langle h,S^{\Sigma_{2}}_{\sigma^{(1)}}f\rangle=\langle h,S^{\Sigma_{2}}_{\sigma^{(2)}}f\rangle

(94)

for all
f,h\in\mathcal{H}^{-1/2}(\Sigma_{2})
. Thus
(
93
) implies (
92
).

Step 2: Determination of the Green pairings from the outer DN map.

We prove (
93
). Let

U_{0}=\mathcal{G}_{0}F
and
V_{0}=\mathcal{G}_{0}H
. Since the supports of

F
and
H
have positive distance from
\Omega_{2}
, both
U_{0}
and

V_{0}
solve the reference homogeneous equation in a relative neighborhood of

\overline{\Omega_{2}}^{\,\Omega_{1}}
. By Lemma

A.3
, there are reference solutions
U_{k},V_{k}
in

\Omega_{1}
, with boundary traces supported in
\Gamma_{1}
, such that

U_{k}\to U_{0},\qquad V_{k}\to V_{0}\quad\text{in }H^{1}(\Omega_{2}).

(95)

For
j=1,2
, let
Z_{j,k}
solve

\operatorname{div}(\sigma^{(j)}\nabla Z_{j,k})=0
in
\Omega_{1}
with

Z_{j,k}|_{\partial\Omega_{1}}=U_{k}|_{\partial\Omega_{1}}
, and set

W_{j,k}=Z_{j,k}-U_{k}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{1})
. Since

\sigma^{(j)}-\sigma^{0}
is supported in
\Omega_{2}
, the function

W_{j,k}
is the unique solution of

\int_{\Omega_{1}}\sigma^{(j)}\nabla W_{j,k}\cdot\nabla\phi\,dx=-\int_{\Omega_{2}}(\sigma^{(j)}-\sigma^{0})\nabla U_{k}\cdot\nabla\phi\,dx,\qquad\phi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{1}).

(96)

The right-hand side in (
96
) converges in

H^{-1}(\Omega_{1})
. Indeed,
U_{k}\to U_{0}
in
H^{1}(\Omega_{2})
and

\sigma^{(j)}-\sigma^{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega_{2})
, so

\left|\int_{\Omega_{2}}(\sigma^{(j)}-\sigma^{0})\nabla(U_{k}-U_{0})\cdot\nabla\phi\,dx\right|\leq C\|U_{k}-U_{0}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{2})}\|\phi\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{1})}.

(97)

The limit problem is the one satisfied by

W_{j}=\mathcal{G}_{j}F-\mathcal{G}_{0}F
: since
\mathcal{G}_{j}F
and

\mathcal{G}_{0}F
have the same source
F
, and since the two coefficients
agree in
G
,

\int_{\Omega_{1}}\sigma^{(j)}\nabla W_{j}\cdot\nabla\phi\,dx=-\int_{\Omega_{2}}(\sigma^{(j)}-\sigma^{0})\nabla U_{0}\cdot\nabla\phi\,dx.

(98)

The Lax–Milgram energy estimate therefore gives

\|W_{j,k}-W_{j}\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{1})}\leq C\|U_{k}-U_{0}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{2})}\to 0.

(99)

Using the definition of the local DN maps, the fact that
U_{k},V_{k}
solve the
reference equation, and
\sigma^{(j)}=\sigma^{0}
in
G
, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle(\Lambda^{\Omega_{1}}_{\sigma^{(j)},\Gamma_{1}}-\Lambda^{\Omega_{1}}_{\sigma^{0},\Gamma_{1}})U_{k}|_{\partial\Omega_{1}},V_{k}|_{\partial\Omega_{1}}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{\Omega_{2}}(\sigma^{(j)}-\sigma^{0})\nabla(U_{k}+W_{j,k})\cdot\nabla V_{k}\,dx.

(100)

The limit on the right is justified by the three strong convergences

U_{k}\to U_{0}
and
V_{k}\to V_{0}
in
H^{1}(\Omega_{2})
, together with
(
99
). Hence

\int_{\Omega_{2}}(\sigma^{(j)}-\sigma^{0})\nabla(U_{k}+W_{j,k})\cdot\nabla V_{k}\,dx\longrightarrow\int_{\Omega_{2}}(\sigma^{(j)}-\sigma^{0})\nabla(U_{0}+W_{j})\cdot\nabla V_{0}\,dx.

(101)

Since
V_{0}=\mathcal{G}_{0}H
, the definition of the Green operator gives

H(W_{j})=\int_{\Omega_{1}}\sigma^{0}\nabla V_{0}\cdot\nabla W_{j}\,dx
. The limit
equation for
W_{j}
, tested with
V_{0}
, gives

\int_{\Omega_{1}}\sigma^{(j)}\nabla W_{j}\cdot\nabla V_{0}\,dx=-\int_{\Omega_{2}}(\sigma^{(j)}-\sigma^{0})\nabla U_{0}\cdot\nabla V_{0}\,dx.

(102)

Since
\sigma^{(j)}=\sigma^{0}
in
G
, combining the last two identities yields

H(W_{j})=-\int_{\Omega_{2}}(\sigma^{(j)}-\sigma^{0})\nabla(U_{0}+W_{j})\cdot\nabla V_{0}\,dx.

(103)

Combining this identity with (
101

H(W_{j})=-\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{\Omega_{2}}(\sigma^{(j)}-\sigma^{0})\nabla(U_{k}+W_{j,k})\cdot\nabla V_{k}\,dx.

(104)

Thus
H(\mathcal{G}_{j}F)=H(\mathcal{G}_{0}F)+H(W_{j})
is determined by the outer
localized DN map
\Lambda^{\Omega_{1}}_{\sigma^{(j)},\Gamma_{1}}
and the known
reference coefficient. Since the two outer localized DN maps agree by
(
51
), the values in
(
93
) are equal for

. By Step 1, this gives
(
92
).

It remains to pass from the full-interface single-layer operator to the

\Sigma_{2}
. Let

\Lambda^{G,+}_{\sigma^{0},\Sigma_{2}}
denote the DN map for the known region
G
,
with Dirichlet boundary values supported on
\Sigma_{2}
and zero Dirichlet boundary values on the

\partial G

\Omega_{2}
;
equivalently it is the negative of the usual outward conormal derivative for

\Sigma_{2}

K_{j}=\Lambda^{\Omega_{2}}_{\sigma^{(j)},\Sigma_{2}}-\Lambda^{G,+}_{\sigma^{0},\Sigma_{2}}.

(105)

By Lemma
A.1
,
K_{j}
is a bounded map

\mathcal{H}^{1/2}(\Sigma_{2})\to\mathcal{H}^{-1/2}(\Sigma_{2})

S^{\Sigma_{2}}_{\sigma^{(j)}}
is a bounded map

K_{j}S^{\Sigma_{2}}_{\sigma^{(j)}}f=f,\qquad f\in\mathcal{H}^{-1/2}(\Sigma_{2}).

(106)

For smooth
f
supported away from the edges, this follows by integrating by
parts separately on
\Omega_{2}
and on
G
. If
U=\mathcal{G}_{j}T_{f}
, then

U|_{\Omega_{2}}
and

solve the homogeneous equations on the two sides

\Sigma_{2}
, have the same trace

\Sigma_{2}

A.1
then passes the identity from smooth

to every

106
).

The operators in (
106
) act between

S^{\Sigma_{2}}_{\sigma^{(j)}}:\mathcal{H}^{-1/2}(\Sigma_{2})\to\mathcal{H}^{1/2}(\Sigma_{2}),\qquad K_{j}:\mathcal{H}^{1/2}(\Sigma_{2})\to\mathcal{H}^{-1/2}(\Sigma_{2}).

(107)

The operator

, glue the solution in

with boundary value
\varphi
on
\Sigma_{2}
to the solution in

with the same boundary value on
\Sigma_{2}
and zero value on

on

.

Equation (

) says
K_{j}S^{\Sigma_{2}}_{\sigma^{(j)}}=I

Since the single-layer operators agree for

and

agree. The DN map

of the known region is known and

The same proof gives the corresponding determination statement. If

is known in
