A. Executive Summary
 The U.S. Department of Energy (DOE) is launching the Tribal Energy Capacity
Expansion Prize (the prize). This prize aims to foster the vital role of Tribes in energy decision-
making and permitting, expanding their capacity to participate in these processes by providing
them with direct cash prizes and technical assistance. The prize has three phases that focus on
(Phase 1) identifying capacity needs, (Phase 2) forming capacity development plans, and (Phase
3) implementing those plans to participate in energy decision-making and related activities.

B. Prize Overview
 Up to $6 million in cash prizes and expert engagement in the form of technical assistance,
will be provided to winning teams across the three-phase prize. Participants selected as winners
in Phase 1 may continue to participate in the prize and receive awards in Phases 2 and 3,
provided that they fulfill all eligibility criteria and satisfy the submission requirements for each
phase.
 Phase 1 consists of two distinct rounds, Phase 1A (concluded) and Phase 1B. In Phase 1,
participants will identify their capacity-building needs to improve their Tribe’s engagement in
energy decision-making and permitting processes. Up to 14 winning participants across both
rounds will receive $40,000 and 40 hours of technical assistance. Winners from both rounds
Phase 1A and 1B are eligible to submit to Phase 2.
 In Phase 2, winning teams will prepare detailed capacity development and energy action
plans. At the end of Phase 2, up to 14 winning teams will receive $200,000 each in cash and 150
hours of technical assistance. Teams representing multiple Tribal entities will be eligible to
compete for a bonus prize of $150,000 each in cash, with up to 4 bonus prize winners in this
phase. Winning teams from Phase 2 will be eligible to compete in Phase 3.
 In Phase 3, winning teams will work to implement their capacity building and energy
action plans. At the end of Phase 3, up to 14 winning teams will receive $125,000 each in cash.
Teams representing multiple Tribal entities will be eligible to compete for a bonus prize of
$50,000 each in cash, with up to 4 bonus prize winners in this phase.

C. Eligibility
 The prize is intended to support capacity development for Tribes that are considering or
may participate in energy decision-making and permitting. All participating teams must include
a federally recognized Tribal entity (see the definition below) and may also include support
organizations. All project plans must be focused on expanding the capacity of Tribe(s) or Tribal
communities in a way that is relevant to energy development processes.
 In line with Executive Order 14153, “Unleashing Alaska’s Extraordinary Resource
Potential,” signed January 20, 2025, this prize encourages participation from Alaska Native
Villages and regional and Village corporations; however, participation is open to all Tribal
participants and multi-Tribe collaborative teams as outlined in the eligibility requirements in the
following sections.
Definitions and Team Roles related to eligibility:
 The following are key terms that clarify how teams are structured and how submissions
may be scoped. These roles and terms are subject to the eligibility criteria outlined in these
official rules.
 Participant Team (or “Team”): The team that participates in the prize by entering a
submission. Membership in a team may shift from one phase to another as outlined in the
eligibility criteria below. A team must include at least one federally recognized Tribal entity (as
defined directly below) and may also include non-Tribal members.
 Tribal Entity: An Indian Tribe, Tribal organization, or intertribal organization, as
defined in this section.

Native Claims Settlement Act (85 Stat. 688) [43 U.S.C. 1601 et seq.], which is recognized as
eligible for the special programs and services provided by the United States to Indians because of
their status as Indians.

of the Indian Self-Determination and Education Assistance Act (25 U.S.C. § 5304). Specifically,
per 25 U.S.C. §5304, “Tribal organization” means the recognized governing body of any Indian

II. Background
 A. Prize Background
 The Tribal Energy Capacity Expansion Prize is part of the American-Made program ,
which is your fast track to energy innovation. Powered by the U.S. Department of Energy,
American-Made program transforms innovative ideas into real energy solutions through prizes,
collegiate competitions, technical assistance, and opportunities to work with a network of
experts.
 Unleashing American energy requires the participation of Tribal Nations across the
country, and many Tribal governments and support organizations have called for additional
resources to engage in energy infrastructure permitting and decision-making processes. In
response, DOE seeks to promote the vital work that Tribal Nations and Tribal-supporting
organizations do to support Tribal participation in energy decision-making processes that affect
their communities, cultural resources, economic opportunities, and the homelands and spaces
that they steward.
 The Tribal Energy Capacity Expansion Prize (the prize), part of the American-Made
program, is funded by the DOE Integrated Energy Systems Office and administered by the
National Laboratory of the Rockies (NLR). This prize supports the involvement of Tribal
Nations in decision-making processes for the development of all types of energy infrastructure.
Participation in the prize is not an indication of support for any specific energy technology or
project but rather an opportunity to build up resources and capabilities to engage in decision-
making processes.
 Developing energy infrastructure is essential for providing abundant, reliable, and secure
domestic energy. Energy planning and permitting are intensive processes that must balance the
competing needs of availability, technology, economy, environment, and society. It is important
that Tribal perspectives are represented and heard during planning efforts; however, increasing
development can strain Tribal resources, creating an urgent need for Tribes to build internal
capacity for effective participation in decision-making processes.
 Key areas of capacity building that this prize could support include, but are not limited to:
 • Expanding capacity and internal expertise to participate in energy infrastructure
 permitting and related energy processes, including those associated with large energy
 loads (e.g. data centers).
 • Increasing understanding and developing resources to ensure that potential economic
 benefits of energy and associated infrastructure development will flow to Tribal
 communities, including through Tribal benefit agreements.
 • Developing Tribal energy education and workforce training programs, such as setting up
 or attaining registered apprenticeship standards and expanding apprenticeship readiness
 training opportunities for Tribal members.

                                           Page 13 of 51

 • Adopting new tools and technology, including the use of artificial intelligence (AI) and
 machine learning (ML), to improve permitting processes, reduce costs and capacity
 needs, or shorten project timelines.
 • Advancing understanding of workforce, cultural, and economic considerations relevant
 to Tribal participation in permitting and development processes.
 • Pursuing the capacity development needed to engage in energy permitting and decision-
 making considerations that are relevant to other federal funding opportunities, such as
 those administered by the DOE Office of Indian Energy.
 These examples include only a sample of Tribal needs and priorities. The prize is
designed to build Tribal capacity in ways that reflect the priorities of participants and support a
wide range of efforts and expertise that Tribes may want to develop to enhance effective
participation in energy decision-making. Increased internal capacity within Tribes can be
pursued in a myriad of ways, including but not limited to direct hiring, employing trusted
subcontractors, attending conferences, supporting research, developing workforce training, and
engaging in development processes. Teams can include Tribal entities from all geographies and
focus on any energy technology or combination of technologies, including associated activities
and infrastructure such as transmission infrastructure, large energy end-user projects (e.g., data
centers), and energy technology supply chain development.
 While the prize may facilitate participation in permitting, regulatory processes, and
government-to-government consultation, the prize is independent from these processes. The
prize is also not connected to any specific energy projects or to any community or Tribal benefit
agreements; however, teams may choose to pursue involvement in specific decision-making
processes, engagement around specific energy projects, or the identification of potential
advantages of energy development that relate to proposed or existing community and Tribal
benefits agreements.
 Technical assistance will be made available to provide winning teams access to experts
and the best available information to support their work. The technical assistance is designed to
be responsive to the specific needs of each team as they develop and implement their capacity
development plans. Technical assistance will be provided by technical and subject-matter
experts from the National Laboratory of the Rockies (NLR). Technical assistance providers will
not provide support in writing or editing any materials that will form the basis for future Tribal
Energy Capacity Expansion Prize submissions. There will also be opportunities for peer-to-peer
learning among the participating Tribal entities.
 B. Prize Phases
 The Tribal Energy Capacity Expansion Prize contains three phases: Phase 1––Capacity
Needs, Phase 2––Capacity Development, and Phase 3––Implementation, respectively (Figure 1).
Any future phases of this prize are at the sole discretion of DOE.
1. Phase 1 – Capacity Needs
 In Phase 1, each team submits an application focused on a federally recognized Tribe or
Tribal community. The application must describe the existing capacity needs of that Tribe or
Tribal community to engage in energy decision-making. A team submitting to Phase 1B must

                                           Page 14 of 51

include the Tribal entity that is the subject of the application. A team may also include other
eligible support organizations.
 Teams submit a short summary of areas of highest-priority need or concern to the Tribe
related to energy decision-making or permitting, current capabilities to address these needs or
concerns, and anticipated internal capacity building efforts.
 Based on the summary of needs and capabilities, up to 14 selected winners from Phase 1
(total across both rounds) will each be awarded $40,000 cash and 40 hours of technical
assistance. This technical assistance can be used to build or investigate capacity in areas of
interest and develop plans related to the second phase of this effort. Unused hours can be rolled
over to Phase 2 if they are not depleted before the Phase 2 submission deadline. The total prize
pool for Phase 1 is a combined $560,000 across all rounds. Winning teams in Phase 1 will be
encouraged to submit to Phase 2, which will focus on developing a capacity development plan.
2. Phase 2 – Capacity Development
 The winning teams from Phase 1 will be eligible to compete in Phase 2. Teams draft
detailed capacity development and energy action plans that align with the needs identified in
Phase 1. To develop their plans, teams refine their priority focus areas, identify possible
impact(s) related to energy development, and engage with Tribal and other community members.
Teams use the information they gather to further define the types of capacity building that will
enable the Tribal entity(ies) to effectively engage in energy development opportunities,
ultimately creating a capacity development plan to expand efforts in these areas.
 In the energy action component of their plans, teams will define the types of energy
decision-making and development activities they are planning to engage in with their expanded
capacity. These activities may include, but are not limited to, conducting environmental,
workforce, or cultural assessments; developing partnerships; improving permitting process
engagement; or pursuing potential benefits and opportunities from energy development. In Phase
2 teams are encouraged to expand to include multiple Tribes if intertribal collaboration would be
relevant to their capacity-building and energy goals. Up to 4 teams may receive a bonus prize for
demonstrating collaboration among multiple Tribes (explained in more detail in Section III).
 A maximum of 14 Phase 2 winners will be awarded cash prizes of $200,000. DOE may

3. Phase 3 – Implementation
 In Phase 3, teams will begin implementing their capacity development and energy action
plans, building resources to engage in existing, proposed, or future energy development projects,
or associated energy infrastructure projects (e.g., large energy end users or transmission).
Activities during this phase include, but are not limited to:
 • Expanding capacity and internal expertise to participate in energy infrastructure
 permitting and related processes, such as siting or transmission interconnection.

                                           Page 15 of 51

 • Increasing understanding and developing resources to ensure that potential economic
 benefits of energy and associated infrastructure development will flow to Tribal
 communities, including through Tribal benefit agreements.
 • Developing Tribal energy education and workforce training programs, including setting
 up or attaining registered apprenticeship standards and expanding apprenticeship
 readiness training opportunities for Tribal members.
 • Adopting new tools and technologies, including the use of AI and ML, to improve
 permitting processes, reduce costs and capacity needs, or shorten project timelines.
 • Advancing understanding of workforce, cultural, and economic considerations relevant to
 Tribal participation in permitting and development processes.
 • Pursuing the capacity development needed to engage in energy permitting and decision-
 making considerations that are relevant to other federal funding opportunities, such as
 those administered by the DOE Office of Indian Energy.
 Phase 3 teams evaluate their capacity development and energy action plans against their
long-term energy development goals and update their plans to reflect an ongoing strategy for
involvement in energy decision-making and development. Over time and throughout the three
phases, capacity-building needs, plans, and implementation are expected to evolve and be refined
as they are implemented.

Tribe Collaborative Bonus Prize. The maximum total prize pool for Phase 3 is a combined
$1,950,000.

V. Phase 3
 I. Phase 3 Goal
 In Phase 3, teams will begin to implement their capacity development and energy action
plans. Teams will take steps to build the capacities they have identified to participate in
decision-making, permitting, and/or development processes for energy projects or associated
infrastructure. Activities during this phase may include, but are not limited to, engagement in
permitting processes, participation in environmental monitoring and research efforts, or efforts to
ensure that potential economic or workforce benefits flow to Tribal communities. Participants
will also update their plans to reflect a long-term strategy to maintain, expand, or scale back the
capacities they have developed, as appropriate for their context.
 It is expected that capacity-building needs, plans, engagement, and implementation will
evolve and be refined over time. Phase 3 proposals may deviate from Phase 2 proposals, but
they must be led by a team that was selected as a winner of Phase 2.

 A. Phase 3 Prizes
 Phase 3 will award a maximum of 14 winners cash prizes of $125,000 each, and up to 4
of the winning teams may receive an additional $50,000 cash prize each for the Multi-Tribe
Collaborative Bonus Prize.
 B. How to Enter
 Go to HeroX and follow the instructions for registering and submitting all required
materials before the deadline. Participants also can form teams or find partners through the prize
HeroX platform’s Forum page.
 C. Process Overview
 Phase 3 consists of the following steps:
 Progress and Submission – Participants implement their capacity development and
 energy action plan focusing on a single or multiple Tribes (based on a single-Tribe
 pathway or a multi-Tribe collaborative pathway selected in Phase 2). It is generally
 accepted that the capacity development needs identified in Phase 2 may change during
 implementation. Participants will also finalize and initiate their plan, collaborating with
 other partners as appropriate. Teams will also complete their submission packages and
 submit them online before the phase closes.
 Assessment – The prize administrator screens submissions for eligibility and completion
 and assigns subject-matter-expert reviewers to independently score the content of each
 submission. The reviewer criteria assess the following team activities:
 Capacity Development: Demonstrate the steps that have been taken to
 implement the capacity development efforts specified in the capacity development
 and energy action plan. Describe and justify how any modifications to the plan

 enable the Tribal entity(ies) to address considerations related to local energy
 and/or related infrastructure development.
 Energy Action: Describe how the expanded capacity is being used to engage in
 energy decision-making and development activities. Describe approaches for
 continuing involvement in priority activities and processes that are responsive to
 the evolving needs of the Tribal entity(ies).

 Team Capabilities: The team has evolved to effectively implement the plans
 developed in previous phases and has demonstrated competency overall.

 Multi-Tribe Collaboration: If relevant, cultivate relationships with other Tribal
 entities engaging in energy development processes to support Tribal capacity
 building. It may also be helpful to engage other public or private organizations.
 Winner Notification – After the winners are notified, the prize administrator requests the

 D. Multi-Tribe Collaborative Bonus Prize
 Starting in Phase 2, teams may add members to form a multi-Tribe collaborative team
that includes more than one Tribal entity (i.e., Indian Tribe, Tribal organization, intertribal
organization, or Tribal community) in its membership and representing at least one Tribal entity
from a winning Phase 1 team. Up to 4 cash prizes of $150,000 each will be awarded for the
Multi-Tribe Collaborative Bonus Prize in Phase 2. To be considered for the bonus prize, a team
must submit an additional bonus prize narrative.
 Within the multi-Tribe collaborative team, there must be a designated lead participant,
which will be responsible for managing correspondence, reporting, and distributing the award
among members of the multi-Tribe collaborative team. The lead participant may be one of the
Tribal entities in the multi-Tribe collaborative team or a supporting organization. If the lead
participant is a supporting organization, the submission package must include a written
explanation of the relationship between the supporting organization and participating Tribal
entities, including how the lead participant organization has been engaged to serve the expressed
interests of the Tribe(s) or Tribal communities. New Tribal entities joining the multi-Tribe
collaborative team do not need to have participated in Phase 1.
 There is no upper limit on the number of Tribes or partner organizations that may be part
of the team. Teams may add or subtract additional eligible Tribes or partner members between
Phase 2 and Phase 3, and a multi-Tribe collaborative team may change the lead participant
between Phase 2 and Phase 3. The team must continue to include at least one Tribal entity from
a winning Phase 1 team.

 L. Program Policy Factors
 While the scores of the expert reviewers will be carefully considered, it is the role of the
prize judge to maximize the impact of prize funds. Some factors outside the control of
participants and beyond the independent expert reviewer scope of review may need to be
considered to accomplish this goal. The following is a list of such factors. In addition to the
reviewers’ scores, the below program policy factors may be considered in determining winners:
  • Geographic and energy development area variety, with an emphasis on encouraging
 participation from Alaska Native villages or regional or village corporations
  • The degree to which the project concepts, efforts undertaken, and learning can be applied
 to other projects, Tribes, and communities
  • Variety of energy technology type and project stage of development
  • The project’s geographic diversity and potential economic impact
  • Whether the use of additional DOE funds and provided resources are nonduplicative and
 compatible with the stated goals of this program and the DOE mission generally
  • The degree to which the submission exhibits technological or programmatic diversity
 when compared to the existing DOE project portfolio and other participants
  • The degree to which the submission is likely to lead to increased employment and
 manufacturing in the United States or provide other economic benefits to U.S. taxpayers
  • The degree to which the submission will accelerate transformational technological,
 financial, or workforce advances in areas that industry by itself is not likely to undertake
 because of technical or financial uncertainty
  • The degree to which the submission supports complementary DOE-funded efforts or
 projects, which, when taken together, will best achieve the goals and objectives of DOE
  • The degree to which the submission expands DOE’s funding to new participants and
 recipients who have not been supported by DOE in the past
  • The degree to which the submission enables new and expanding market segments

 M. National Environmental Policy Act Compliance
 DOE’s administration of this prize may be subject to the National Environmental Policy
Act (NEPA) (42 U.S.C. § 4321, et seq.). NEPA requires Federal agencies to consider the
environmental effects of certain proposed actions as part of an agency’s decision-making
processes. NEPA applies when a proposed action constitutes a “major Federal action”; actions
with no or minimal Federal involvement, e.g., where a Federal agency cannot control the
outcome of the action, are not considered “major Federal actions” (42 U.S.C. §
4336e(10)(B)(i)). For additional background on NEPA, please see DOE’s NEPA website
at http://nepa.energy.gov/.
