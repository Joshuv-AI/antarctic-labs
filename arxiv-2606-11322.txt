Abstract
Event Horizon Telescope (EHT) images of black holes appear as rings with a brightness asymmetry. Here, we expand on our previous study of the asymmetry magnitude a 1 a_{1} to study the position angle of the peak brightness asymmetry PA 1 \mathrm{PA}_{1} in general relativistic magnetohydrodynamic (GRMHD) models. For larger spin magnitudes ( a ∗ > 0 a_{*}>0 and a ∗ ≲ − 0.5 a_{*}\lesssim-0.5 ), the mean PA 1 \mathrm{PA}_{1} falls within 1 ​ σ 1\sigma of the approaching limb of the black hole, regardless of viewing inclination, disk magnetization, or source. By comparing the ( a 1 , PA 1 ) (a_{1},\mathrm{PA}_{1}) distribution in M87* observations with models, we demonstrate that we can mildly disfavor low-magnitude spins and strongly disfavor all spin vectors that point toward Earth. The alignment of PA 1 \mathrm{PA}_{1} relative to the large-scale jet axis may suggest that M87*’s disk does not have a large tilt. By combining PA 1 \mathrm{PA}_{1} with the pattern speed measured in optimistic 2026 M87* video conditions, the EHT can constrain whether M87* is prograde or retrograde with ∼ 84 % \sim 84\% accuracy. In Sgr A*, we show that a detection of ( a 1 , PA 1 ) (a_{1},\mathrm{PA}_{1}) could constrain the magnitude and direction of the galactic center spin vector. Finally, if future EHT expansions increase the sample of horizon-scale sources, a simple set of observables (ring diameter, asymmetry magnitude, and asymmetry angle) could enable robust constraints on black hole mass, spin, and inclination.

2.1
Model Library

Here, we compare EHT data to a library of synthetic data. Following the PATOKA pipeline
(
87
)
, we use the
“Illinois v5”
library, which contains synthetic images of the
“v5”
version of the Illinois GRMHD models.
The library spans 5 parameters (magnetization mode MAD/SANE, black hole spin
a_{*}
, viewing inclination
i

R_{\text{high}}

First, we simulate the evolution of the accretion flow using KHARMA (
69

36

Each accretion disk falls into one of two magnetization modes: (1) standard and normal evolution
(
65

73

“SANE”

43

The dimensionless black hole spin is
a_{*}\equiv Jc/GM^{2}\in[-1,1]

a_{*}>0

Retrograde systems have
a_{*}<0

In a retrograde system with zero tilt, the black hole spin axis vector is antiparallel to the disk angular momentum vector.
We span 13 values in black hole spin:
a_{*}\in\{0,\pm 0.25,\pm 0.5,\pm 0.75,\pm 0.85,\pm 0.94,\pm 0.97\}
(N. J. Bowden et al., in preparation).

Next, we generate synthetic images using the radiative transfer code

(
59
)
.
The viewing inclination

is defined as the angle between the observer line of sight and the angular momentum vector of the disk.
In our convention,
i=0
is face-on above a counter-clockwise rotating accretion disk,
i=90^{\circ}
is edge-on, and
i=180^{\circ}
is face-on below a clockwise rotating disk.
By default, the spin and disk angular momentum axes project onto the vertical axis of the image, unless the image
\mathrm{PA}
is rotated to match observations.

For M87*, we consider
i\in\{17,163^{\circ}\}
, assuming the angular momentum vector of the disk is coaxial with the large-scale jet
(
83

For Sgr A*, we currently span
i\in\{30,50,90^{\circ}\}
. Intermediate inclinations such as
i\sim 30^{\circ}
are weakly favored by observations
(
30
)
.
We do not yet sample
i>90^{\circ}
for Sgr A*, but
the distribution of
a_{1}
is entirely independent of whether the disk is viewed above or below, while the
\mathrm{PA}_{1}
distribution is reflected by symmetry.

The ion and electron temperatures may differ
(
76

. The ion temperature
T_{i}
is determined by KHARMA, while the electron temperature

controls emission.
We assign the electron temperature during radiative transfer using the

60

:

\frac{T_{i}}{T_{e}}=R_{\text{high}}\frac{\tilde{\beta}^{2}}{1+\tilde{\beta}^{2}}+R_{\text{low}}\frac{1}{1+\tilde{\beta}^{2}}

(1)

for

P_{\textrm{gas}}

P_{\textrm{mag}}

\beta_{\textrm{crit}}=1

Thus,

smoothly varies from
R_{\text{low}}
when plasma
\beta\ll 1
to
R_{\text{high}}
when
\beta\gg 1
. The parameter

is the dimensionless maximum ratio of

. Physically, it corresponds to how efficiently we heat electrons in regions with low magnetic field strength (where there is typically less emission).
For Sgr A*, we consider
R_{\text{high}}\in\{40,160\}
and
R_{\text{low}}=1
.
For M87*, we span
R_{\text{high}}\in\{10,40,80,160\}
and
R_{\text{low}}\in\{1,10\}

R_{\text{high}}>1
and
R_{\text{low}}=10
are motivated by observations
(
32
;
33
)
.

These models are imaged at a typical cadence of
5\,GMc^{-3}

\sim 100
sec for Sgr A* and
\sim 44

2.2
Asymmetry Measurements

To measure ring asymmetry, we use the VIDA and Comrade packages
(
80
;
81
)
.
VIDA fits a geometric model to an image using divergence minimization.
Comrade uses Bayesian inference to fit a geometric model to the raw visibility data.

Our geometric model is an
m
-ring truncated at
m=4
with a Gaussian background component. The total intensity can be modeled as
I_{tot}=I_{ring}+I_{bg}
, for the ring intensity
I_{ring}
and the background intensity
I_{bg}
. The Gaussian is:

I_{bg}(x,y)\propto(1-f)\exp\left(-\frac{(x^{\prime})^{2}}{2\sigma_{g}^{2}}-\frac{(y^{\prime})^{2}}{2\sigma_{g}^{2}(1+\tau_{g})^{2}}\right)

(2)

where
(1-f)
scales the intensity of the Gaussian,
\sigma_{g}
is the width,
\tau_{g}
is the Gaussian ellipticity,
\xi_{g}
is the rotation angle of the Gaussian,
(x_{g},y_{g})
is the center,
x^{\prime}=(x-x_{g})\cos\xi_{g}+(y-y_{g})\sin\xi_{g}
, and
y^{\prime}=-(x-x_{g})\cos\xi_{g}+(y-y_{g})\sin\xi_{g}
.
The
m=4

m
-ring model is:

I_{ring}(r,\theta)\propto\left[1+\sum_{m=1}^{4}a_{m}\cos\left(m\mathrm{PA}-\mathrm{PA}_{m}\right)\right]\exp\left(-\frac{(r-r_{0})^{2}}{2\sigma_{r}^{2}}\right).

(3)

for ring radius
r_{0}
, ring thickness
\sigma_{r}
, brightness asymmetry magnitude
a_{m}
, position angle
\mathrm{PA}_{m}
, and Fourier component
m
. Notice this corresponds to a Fourier series truncated at
m=4
and wrapped around a ring with Gaussian thickness.
This geometric model provides a good fit to both M87* and Sgr A* data
(
25
;
22
;
28
)
. The fits are insensitive to minor changes in the geometric model or in the parameter bounds
(
7
, see Appendix B in)
.

The
m=1
component captures the large-scale brightness asymmetry. Higher
m
components typically have lower amplitudes, modifying the asymmetry shape or fitting small fluctuations. For this work, we consider
(a_{1},\mathrm{PA}_{1})
as the overall brightness asymmetry magnitude and position angle respectively.

We have applied the Comrade package to EHT 2017–2018 M87* observations, finding
(a_{1},\mathrm{PA}_{1})
to be
(0.41\pm 0.04,169.7^{\circ}\pm 5.1^{\circ})
and
(0.69\pm 0.10,210.5^{\circ}\pm 3.5^{\circ})
respectively. These fits provide a good description of the EHT data
(
7
, Section 3.1)
.

To simulate asymmetry data for M87*, we generate synthetic observations of the v5 M87* library. We rotate the ground truth images so each model’s jet axis aligns with the observed jet axis

(
83

17

-\vec{J}
jet has position angle
\mathrm{PA}_{\mathrm{jet}}=-72^{\circ}
with standard deviation
<1^{\circ}

(
7

(
11

. Finally, we optimize the parameters of our
m
-ring model using Comrade. To ensure the asymmetry samples are independent, we downsample to a cadence of
125\,t_{g}
, which is comparable to or longer than the correlation timescale. After downsampling, we have 240 asymmetry measurements for each combination of
(a_{*},R_{\text{high}},R_{\text{low}})
, and 1920 for each
a_{*}
.
Validation of the recovered
a_{1}
values can be found in
7

(u,v)
coverage on the recovered
\mathrm{PA}_{1}
are described in Section
5.3
.

For observed EHT Sgr A* data, extraction of
(a_{1},\mathrm{PA}_{1})
is difficult, given limitations in snapshot
(u,v)
coverage and the faster source variability of Sgr A*. Feasible methods include:
(1) static imaging, where a static image is generated then time-averaged image-domain features are measured via VIDA;
(2) dynamical imaging, where a video is generated then time-variable image-domain features are measured via VIDA;
(3) snapshot geometric modeling, where Comrade is applied to short time windows to measure time-variable features; and
(4) full-track geometric modeling, where a geometric modeling pipeline fits source structure and source variability to measure time-averaged features
(
27

In this work, we adopt the values reported using the third method in

For simulated asymmetry data of Sgr A*, we use asymmetry measurements performed in the image domain via VIDA. As with synthetic M87* data, we downsample to a cadence of

to ensure asymmetry measurements in the Sgr A* models are independent.

4.1
Spin Constraints with Static M87* Data

\plotone

It is possible to constrain black hole spin using the brightness asymmetry. Here, we extend the methodology of
7
to form constraints with both the asymmetry magnitude and position angle
(a_{1},\mathrm{PA}_{1})
.

We apply the two-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) test.
It can test whether two samples (the asymmetry in observations and each model) come from the same underlying distribution, without assuming a parameterized form for the distribution.
It outputs the probability
p
that the difference in the samples’ cumulative distributions is as large or larger than measured, under the null hypothesis that the two samples are drawn from the same distribution.

The KS test is only suited for one-dimensional, non-wrapped data. Thus we convert each asymmetry distribution
(a_{1},\mathrm{PA}_{1})
to asymmetry phase space:
(a_{1x},a_{1y})=(a_{1}\cos\mathrm{PA}_{1},\,a_{1}\sin\mathrm{PA}_{1})
. We apply the KS test to
a_{1x}
and
a_{1y}
separately, then combine the resulting
p
values using the standard Fisher’s method (which calculates a
\chi^{2}
via
\chi^{2}_{2k}=-2\sum_{i}^{k}\log p_{i}
for
p
-values
i
through
k
with
2k=4
degrees of freedom).

We illustrate the 2017–2018 M87* asymmetry constraints using a “pizza plot” (Figure
4
). We plot
p
for each M87* model across model parameters, marginalizing across
R_{\text{low}}
. Roughly half of each pizza are strongly disfavored with

lies in the North, which is strongly disfavored. While previous studies also found the M87* spin vector points away from Earth
(
32

, here we evaluate the claim statistically. Measurements of
\mathrm{PA}_{1}
across two independent epochs favor M87* spin vectors pointed away from Earth with high probability.

Rather than calculating
p
, one might instead estimate the Bayesian evidence for each model. We leave the interesting problem of developing an optimal method of model–observation comparison to future work.

Beyond spin constraints,
\mathrm{PA}_{1}
may also enable constraints of disk tilt (where the disk angular momentum and spin vectors are not parallel or antiparallel).
Models predict the jet axis likely aligns with the disk angular momentum vector
(
55
)
. Thus
if
\mathrm{PA}_{1}\approx\mathrm{PA}_{\mathrm{spin}}+90^{\circ}
for non-zero tilt as well, then its location perpendicular to the jet axis would suggest that the disk and spin vectors are approximately coaxial, i.e. that M87*’s tilt is small. See Section
5.3
for additional tilt discussion.

Overall, current asymmetry measurements in M87* mildly favor a spinning black hole, and strongly favor spin vectors that point away from the Earth. Future EHT observations should improve these constraints.

5.1
Future Sources Beyond M87* and Sgr A*

Future extensions to the EHT may enable horizon-scale asymmetry analysis for sources beyond M87* and Sgr A*. There are
\sim 17
sources with a ring angular diameter
\gtrsim 5\mu
as
(
68
;
70
)
. Ground-based multiwavelength extensions to the EHT
(

79

could attempt to extract asymmetry in several new sources. The space-based mission Black Hole Explorer (BHEX) could provide horizon-scale analysis for 14 sources
(
47
)
.

For these new sources, three total intensity ring observables would be simple to measure and would enable straightforward parameter constraints. (1) A measurement of the ring angular diameter would constrain mass-to-distance ratio
M/D

(
32
, e.g.)
. (2) A measurement of the brightness asymmetry magnitude would constrain the spin and inclination
(
7
)
. (3) A measurement of the brightness asymmetry position angle would constrain the spin vector position angle (this work).

Joint measurements of multiple observables would enable further parameter inference.
If there is a prior for
\mathrm{PA}_{\mathrm{disk}}
(e.g. from a large-scale jet),
then a joint measurement of
\Omega_{p}
and
\mathrm{PA}_{1}
would constrain whether the black hole is prograde or retrograde (as with M87* in Section
3.2
).
A measure of
\mathrm{PA}_{\mathrm{disk}}
and
\mathrm{PA}_{1}

might also be used to infer disk tilt or low
|a_{*}|
.
For large tilts,
\mathrm{PA}_{1}
may deviate from the expected value of
\mathrm{PA}_{1}\approx\mathrm{PA}_{\mathrm{jet}}\pm 90^{\circ}
(see Section
5.3
).

\mathrm{PA}_{1}
in low
|a_{*}|
models may also deviate from expectations (see Figure
1
).
These scenarios could be distinguished using
a_{1}
, since low
|a_{*}|
models exhibit the lowest
a_{1}

(
7
)
.
For nearly edge-on models, contributions from the
m=2
mode
a_{2}
begin to exceed
a_{1}
(see Figure
1

m=2

m

5.2
Spin Alignment and Black Hole Growth History

A joint constraint on
a_{*}
and
\mathrm{PA}_{\mathrm{spin}}
could give insight into the black hole’s growth history.
Previous studies on growth history used cosmological simulations
(
74
;
5
, e.g.)
, numerical accretion models, and surrogate models
(
84
, e.g.)
. Three mechanisms are frequently proposed for supermassive black hole growth: (1) coherent accretion, where accretion roughly aligns with the galactic angular momentum vector; (2) incoherent accretion, where the accretion is uncorrelated with the galactic angular momentum vector; and (3) hierarchical mergers, where growth is driven by successive black hole–black hole mergers. Coherent accretion drives high
a_{*}
that align with the large-scale gas flow. Incoherent accretion drives low
|a_{*}|
that randomly misaligns with large-scale gas flow. Hierarchical mergers drive high
|a_{*}|
(albeit with a large dispersion) that randomly misalign with the large-scale gas flow.
Beyond growth histories, spin alignment also influences the orbits of nearby stars via Lense-Thirring precession
(
52
)
and resonant friction
(
53
)
.

In M87*,
a_{1}
disfavors
|a_{*}|\lesssim 0.25
and mildly favors
|a_{*}|\gtrsim 0.5
.

\mathrm{PA}_{1}
suggests the spin vector is relatively aligned with the large scale jet, and so is tilted from angular momentum of the surrounding pc-scale gas environment
(
45
, recent studies suggest the tilt between the pc-scale gas and the jet is
\beta\gtrsim 11^{\circ}
, with a more typical value of
\beta\approx 27^{\circ}
, per)
.
Non-zero spin disfavors incoherent accretion. Sufficiently large tilt from the pc-scale gas may suggest mergers played a role or that the large-scale gas has changed orientation. Precise constraints on the growth history will depend on the spin and the model.

5.3
Uncertainty in Position Angles and Spin Constraints

The analysis presented here is model-dependent.
Going sequentially, there are three sources of uncertainty in the GRMHD simulations that might affect
\mathrm{PA}_{1}
.

(1) Boundary and initial conditions. Our models are typically initialized with an aligned Fishbone-Moncrief torus, with magnetic field conditions that produce the MAD or SANE state, and without inflow at the outer boundaries
(
34
;
87
)
.

The v5 library also contains several tilted disks. We consider
\sim 30
independent samples from a set of Sgr A* models with
a_{*}=\pm 0.5
, tilt
\beta=10^{\circ}
,
R_{\text{high}}\in\{40,160\}
, viewed at
i_{\mathrm{spin}}=30^{\circ}
with respect to the spin axis and at azimuth
\alpha\in\{0^{\circ},90^{\circ}\}
(where
\alpha=0^{\circ}
corresponds to a disk tilted out of the screen towards the observer, and
\alpha=90^{\circ}
corresponds to a disk tilted within the plane of the screen). All tilted models exhibited a mean
\mathrm{PA}_{1}
within
1\sigma
of the black hole’s approaching limb and the untilted mean
\mathrm{PA}_{1}
. This is consistent with previous studies of mild tilts. Deviations in
\mathrm{PA}_{1}
from expectations may increase with sufficiently large tilt (
\beta\gtrsim 30^{\circ}
) for certain
a_{*}
,
i
, and
\alpha

(
14
;
23
)
.

SANE models are discussed in Appendix
A
, showing broadly consistent trends. The effect of alternate boundary conditions (such as wind-fed GRMHD models) on
\mathrm{PA}_{1}
is not yet understood.

(2) Collisionless dynamics. Our simulations assume the plasma is an ideal fluid, and thus do not include kinetic or non-ideal fluid effects. Recent 2D global kinetic simulations, which include kinetic effects (and a more complete electron energization treatment), produce remarkably similar results to MAD GRMHD models
(
58
)
. Non-ideal GRMHD modeling with viscosity and heat conduction produce nearly indistinguishable mean asymmetry behavior
(
18
, see Figures 3 and 9 of)
.

(3) Thermodynamics and electron energization. In our models, the ion internal energy is evolved assuming a set adiabatic index
\gamma_{i}
, with
\gamma_{i}=4/3
and
\gamma_{i}=5/3
in the v3 and v5 libraries respectively. The larger adiabatic index is favored for current EHT sources
(
37
)
. Still, Appendix
A
shows that results are consistent for lower
\gamma_{i}
.

Our models assume a thermal electron distribution function, following the standard
R_{\text{high}}
prescription
(
60
)
. One sample of GRMHD models with a nonthermal prescription exhibit surprisingly similar mean values of
(a_{1},\mathrm{PA}_{1})

(see
23
, Figure 3)
. A more accurate
“two-temperature”
treatment would evolve a separate electron internal energy equation, with electron heating, cooling, and a nonthermal tail
(
46
;
75
)
. One two-temperature GRMHD library shows starkly similar
\mathrm{PA}_{1}
behavior, with
\mathrm{PA}_{1}
falling on the black hole’s approaching limb for all
|a_{*}|\gtrsim 0.3
, wider standard deviations for
|a_{*}|\lesssim 0.3
, and a bias towards larger
\mathrm{PA}_{1}
than the approaching limb for
|a_{*}|\lesssim 0.3
.

(
13
, compare Figure
2
here with Figure 11 of)
.

We assume isotropic synchrotron emission.
Increased anisotropy perpendicular to the magnetic field in the mirror limit or whistler limit produces similar
\mathrm{PA}_{1}
across multiple sources, inclinations, and spins. Increased anisotropy parallel to the magnetic field with the firehose limit can change the mean
\mathrm{PA}_{1}
if
a_{*}
is low
(
35
)
.
However, these limits are disfavored by the observed circular polarization fraction
(
39
;
35
;
26
)
and by 2D global kinetic simulations
(
58
, i.e. by particle-in-cell simulations;)
.

There are two additional uncertainties in the radiative transfer treatment:

(4) Mass density scaling. While the equations of GRMHD are scale invariant, radiative transfer requires scaled quantities in order to calculate outputs in physical units. The accretion mass density
\mathcal{M}
is thus scaled to match the observed source’s flux density. There may be uncertainties in the source’s target flux (from annual variability or unresolved large-scale emission), and future sources may have different values of
M
,
D
, and
\mathcal{M}
. Large changes in these parameters affect optical depth and thus source morphology
(
87
)
. We leave an analysis of
(a_{1},\mathrm{PA}_{1})
across changes in
M
,
D
, and
\mathcal{M}
to future work.

(5) The
“fast-light”
approximation. We perform radiative transfer for a snapshot image along a single GRMHD timeslice, i.e. ignoring changes in the source during the light-crossing time.
7

a_{1}

\mathrm{PA}_{1}
is also unchanged. The fast-light treatment generates a mean and standard deviation of
\mathrm{PA}_{1}=191^{\circ}\pm 22^{\circ}
, while slow-light produces
\mathrm{PA}_{1}=194^{\circ}\pm 22^{\circ}

\mathrm{PA}_{1}

6
Conclusions

Here, we have analyzed the position angle of the brightness asymmetry seen in EHT models and sources. The direction of the brightness asymmetry depends on spin:
\mathrm{PA}_{1}=\mathrm{PA}_{1}(a_{*})
. Thus, a measurement of
\mathrm{PA}_{1}
corresponds to a constraint of the black hole spin vector on the sky.

In M87*, we show a joint measurement of the brightness asymmetry magnitude and direction
(a_{1},\mathrm{PA}_{1})
allows us to disfavor low spins. This is discussed in detail in prior work
(
7
)
.
Further, we can disfavor high spins pointed toward Earth, providing a statistically significant model-dependent constraint that M87* has
|a_{*}|\gtrsim 0.2
and has a spin vector pointed along the receding jet. The alignment of
\mathrm{PA}_{1}
perpendicular to the jet axis may allow us to infer a limited tilt in the M87* accretion disk.
The 2026 M87* video should provide a joint constraint on the pattern speed
\Omega_{p}
and
\mathrm{PA}_{1}
, constraining whether M87* is prograde or retrograde with
\sim 84\%
confidence.

In Sgr A*, we demonstrate that EHT data is beginning to constrain
(a_{1},\mathrm{PA}_{1})
with high uncertainty. Future analysis should enable robust horizon-scale constraints on the direction and magnitude of the Sgr A* spin vector. This could offer insight into its growth history, point the way to the near-horizon jet, and contextualize studies of galactic center stellar orbits.

Future expansions to the EHT such as BHEX may enable us to perform horizon-scale analysis on over a dozen sources. A set of simple total intensity ring observables should enable straightforward parameter inference in these new sources. The ring diameter is known to scale with
M/D
. The brightness asymmetry magnitude scales with spin magnitude and inclination
(
7
)
. And the brightness asymmetry position angle depends on the spin vector position angle. This will complement independent constraints through other observables, such as the linear polarization fraction and structure.

Spin constraints using brightness asymmetry could offer insight into jet-launching, disk tilt, and potentially black hole growth history.
The leading model for jet-launching is Blandford-Znajek, which predicts that jet power is a function of spin.
Prior M87* analysis of
a_{1}
enables spin constraints that are consistent with Blandford-Znajek
(
7
)
. Analysis of
\mathrm{PA}_{1}
here are consistent with our prior results.
Improved constraints using future independent observations will enable an indirect test for Blandford-Znajek, by checking continued consistency between spin and jet power.

\mathrm{PA}_{1}
provides independent evidence that the M87* spin vector is relatively aligned with the jet axis, i.e. that the disk has little tilt, consistent with prior results
(
17
)
. If Sgr A* also exhibits little to no tilt,
\mathrm{PA}_{1}
may point the way to the near-horizon jet.
In other sources,
\mathrm{PA}_{1}
may constrain whether the black hole spin vector is aligned with or tilted from the disk and larger galactic center structure.
More generally, joint measurements of
(a_{1},\mathrm{PA}_{1})
may offer insight into black hole growth, as different growth histories predict different probability distributions for black hole spin magnitudes and spin alignment with the surrounding galactic structure.

Appendix A
SANE Models and the v3 Illinois GRMHD Library

The majority of the above analysis focused on MAD models from the latest v5 model library. In Figure
9
, we show the
\mathrm{PA}_{1}
distributions for weakly magnetized SANE models and the earlier v3 version of the Illinois GRMHD model library. All models show broadly consistent trends:
\mathrm{PA}_{1}
falls within
1\sigma
of the black hole’s approaching limb for all models with
a_{*}>0
or
a_{*}\lesssim-0.5

In SANE models, the rotation curve is roughly Keplerian, unlike MAD models which exhibit sub-Keplerian rotation
(
15
;
19
)
. Thus, Doppler beaming plays a larger role in driving asymmetry in SANEs.
This generates larger
a_{1}
for prograde spins and shifts the minimum
a_{1}
to occur at more retrograde spins
(
7
)
. Nevertheless, the mean
\mathrm{PA}_{1}
exhibits surprisingly similar behavior between MADs and SANEs, suggesting our results are relatively insensitive to the accretion magnetization.

Compared to the newer v5 library, the v3 library was run for a shorter duration (
t=15\,000
–
30\,000\,t_{g}
) with less resolution in the grid, sampling different
R_{\text{high}}
s (
1
instead of
80
) and fewer spins (
a_{*}\in\{0,\pm 0.5,\pm 0.94\}
). It also used an ion adiabatic index of
\gamma_{i}=4/3
instead of
\gamma_{i}=5/3
, which is now preferred
(
37
)
. The effect of each of these changes has not yet been fully investigated.
\mathrm{PA}_{1}
generally shows similar trends between the two libraries, suggesting our overall results may be robust against changes to
\gamma_{i}
. However, there are two differences with v3. First,
\mathrm{PA}_{1}
seems to take longer to converge to the black hole’s approaching limb in v3 as we go from zero-spin to strongly retrograde. This is expected; the lower adiabatic index drives higher midplane temperatures, which enables more pressure support and faster rotation
(
16
, see Appendix A of )
. Thus Doppler beaming plays a stronger role on the side of the receding limb than the approaching limb in v3 than in v5.
Second, v3 seems to exhibit a wider standard deviation in
\mathrm{PA}_{1}
.
Differences aside, the results are broadly consistent.

Figure 9:
As in Figure
6
, we plot
\mathrm{PA}_{1}
circular means with
1\sigma
ranges, now for MAD (left subplots), SANE (right), v3 (bottom), and v5 (top) models.
Models are shown in orange, 2017-2018 M87* is shown in purple, and the black hole’s approaching limb is shown as a dashed gray line. X-axis ticks show the spins sampled in each model library. Images are oriented so that disk angular momentum vectors project along

\mathrm{PA}=0
, and retrograde spin vectors project along
\mathrm{PA}=180^{\circ}
. Consistent trends in
\mathrm{PA}_{1}
across magnetization and library versions are visible.
