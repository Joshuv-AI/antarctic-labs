ABSTRACT
Sandia National Laboratories has tested and evaluated an updated SMAD digitizer developed by
the French Alternative Energies and Atomic Energy Commission (CEA). The SMAD digitizers
are intended to record sensor output for seismic and infrasound monitoring applications. The
purpose of this digitizer evaluation is to measure the performance characteristics in such areas as
power consumption, input impedance, sensitivity, full scale, self-noise, dynamic range, system
noise, response, passband, and timing. Since their last evaluation by Sandia, the SMAD digitizers
have been revised to update their firmware and improve their handling of timing. The SMAD
digitizers are being evaluated for potential use in the International Monitoring System (IMS) of
the Comprehensive Nuclear-Test-Ban Treaty (CTBT). This evaluation follows two earlier
evaluations of the SMAD digitizer, in 2012 and 2016, for similar applications.

1 INTRODUCTION
Sandia National Laboratories has tested and evaluated an updated SMAD digitizer, developed by
the French Alternative Energies and Atomic Energy Commission (CEA). The purpose of this
evaluation of the SMAD digitizer is to measure its performance characteristics in such areas as
power consumption, input impedance, sensitivity, full scale, self-noise, dynamic range, system
noise, response, passband, and timing and compare their performance to the manufacturer’s
specifications and IMS requirements. This evaluation follows earlier evaluations of the SMAD
for similar applications (Hart & Merchant, 2012 and Merchant, 2016).

 Figure 1 CEA SMAD Digitizers

The two SMAD digitizers tested, #282 and #283, were configured to perform testing in 2024
over a variety of sample rates (20, 40, 50, and 100 sps) and gain levels (1x, 2x, 4x, and 8x), with
the specific configuration noted in each test. In addition, subsequent retesting of three SMAD
digitizers (#282, #283, and #313) was performed in 2025 for a subset of the original tests to
confirm operation at cold temperatures, following modifications to the SMAD timing board.

The digitizers were configured to simultaneously record data locally on an internal memory card
and stream data over Ethernet to a computer using a CD1.1 data receiver. The digitizers were
configured to synchronize to their internal GNSS timing module and maintain an active GNSS
lock continuously.

2 TEST PLAN
This test plan section describes the overall scope and process for how the testing of the digitizers
will be performed. For a description of the individual test configuration details, see the relevant
section of each test.

2.1 Test Facility
Testing of the digitizers was performed at Sandia National Laboratories’ Facility for Acceptance,
Calibration and Testing (FACT) located near Albuquerque, New Mexico, USA. The FACT site
is at approximately 1830 meters in elevation.

Sandia National Laboratories (SNL), Ground-based Monitoring R&E Department has the
capability of evaluating the performance of preamplifiers, digitizing waveform recorders, and
analog-to-digital converters/high-resolution digitizers for geophysical applications.

Tests are based on the Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers (IEEE) Standard 1057 for
Digitizing Waveform Recorders and Standard 1241 for Analog to Digital Converters. The
analyses based on these standards were performed in the frequency domain or time domain as
required. When possible, instrumentation calibrations are traceable to the International System
of Units (SI) via the National Institute for Standards Technology (NIST).

Most of the digitizer testing, except for tests performed in the temperature chamber, were
performed within the FACT site underground bunker due to the bunker’s stable temperature.

12 Volts, unless otherwise noted in the test configuration.

3.9 Dynamic Range
Dynamic Range is defined to be the ratio between the power of the largest and smallest signals
that may be measured on the digitizer channel.

3.9.1 Measurand
The Dynamic Range is measured in dB as the ratio between the power in the largest and smallest
signals. The largest signal is defined to be a sinusoid with amplitude equal to the full-scale input
of the digitizer channel. The smallest signal is defined to have power equal to the self-noise of
the digitizer channel. This definition of dynamic range is consistent with the definition of signal-
to-noise and distortion ratio (SINAD) for digitizers (IEEE Std 1241-2010 Section 9.2).

3.9.2 Configuration
There is no test configuration for the dynamic range test.

3.9.3 Analysis

corrupted with noise from electronics that refresh at a 1 Hz multiple.

 81

Amplitude levels were chosen to maximize the signal to noise ratio on the digitizer channel and
the reference meter to better resolve harmonic peaks in the sinusoids PSD. An additional
consideration is the Agilent 3458A has a variable peak amplitude and resolution level depending
on whether the sinusoidal signal amplitude is within its 100 V, 10 V, 1 V, or 0.1 V ranges.

The meter and the digitizer channel record the described AC voltage signal simultaneously. The

channel. The meter is configured to record at 100 Hz, which is sufficiently above the frequency
of the signal of interest to reduce the Agilent 3458A Meter’s response roll-off at 1 Hz to less than
0.013 %.

4 SUMMARY
Power Consumption
The SMAD digitizers were found to consume between 7 and 9 W of power in general operation.
Power consumption was not observed to change with gain levels. Disabling Ethernet or unused
recording channels did little to impact power consumption. The SMAD was confirmed to
operate over 11 V to 20 V of power, with slightly increased consumption at higher voltages.

Input Impedance
The SMAD digitizer channels were found to have an input impedance that was within 0.0644 %
of the nominal 32 kilohms. In addition, channels did not have any measurable capacitance, far
less than 1 nF.

DC Accuracy
The SMAD digitizer channels were found to have bit-weights that were consistent with the
nominal values to within 0.81% across all evaluated sample rates and gain levels.

AC Accuracy
The SMAD digitizer channels were found to have bit-weights that were consistent with the
nominal values to within 0.55% across all evaluated sample rates and gain levels.

AC Full Scale
The SMAD digitizer channels could fully resolve peak-to-peak amplitudes at or about their full
scale without any apparent signs of distortion across the 20 Hz, 40 Hz, 50 Hz, and 100 Hz
sample rates and gains of 1x, 2x, 4x, and 8x.

AC Over Scale
The SMAD digitizer channels were all determined to have a full-scale amplitude that exceeded
the nominally specified full scale by at least 1.55% across the 20 Hz, 40 Hz, 50 Hz, and 100 Hz
sample rates and gains of 1x, 2x, 4x, and 8x.

Input Shorted Offset
The SMAD digitizer channels were found to have a DC offset that in all cases was less than 50
uV. In general, offsets were unique to each channel and primarily a function of temperature.

Self-Noise
The SMAD digitizers have self-noise levels that are typically between 0.5 and 2 counts rms of
noise, depending upon the sampling rate and gain levels, with between 21 and 22.5 noise free
bits in the power spectra. Self-noise levels scaled well with gain levels up to 4x, with 5 to 6 dB
of noise reduction at each gain level. At a gain of 8x, there was a 4 dB decrease in noise.

Dynamic Range
Dynamic range values varied with the application passband, sample rate, gain setting, and
termination resistor. However, dynamic ranges were generally between 129 dB and 139 dB.
Dynamic range is generally higher at lower sample rates and gain levels and lower at higher
sample rates and gain levels.

 121

System Noise
System noise plots are provided to demonstrate the impact of the digitizer self-noise for a variety
of seismometer and infrasound sensor applications.

Temperature Self-Noise
The SMAD digitizers demonstrated proper functionality across at -20°C and 60°C range of
temperatures, with only a small change in DC offset as a function of temperature.

Relative Transfer Function
The SMAD digitizer exhibited less than 8 microseconds of timing skew between channels.

Analog Bandwidth
The SMAD digitizer channels exhibited a bandwidth of approximately 85% of the Nyquist rates
at sample rates of 20, 50, and 100 Hz. At sample rates of 40 Hz, bandwidth was approximately
70% of Nyquist. Bandwidth was not observed to vary with gain level.

Total Harmonic Distortion
The SMAD digitizer channels exhibited harmonic distortion ranging between -115 and -110 dB
at a gain of 1x, -116 dB and -110 dB at a gain of 2x, -118 dB and -110 dB at a gain of 4x, and -
120 dB and -110 dB at a gain of 8x.

Modified Noise Power Ratio
The SMAD digitizer channels exhibited a modified noise power ratio at a sample rate of 20 Hz
and gains of 1x and 2x at just better than 22 bits. At a sample rate of 40 sps at gain of 4x and 8x,
the modified noise power ratio was at approximately 21.5 bits.

Common Mode Rejection
The SMAD digitizer channels exhibited common mode rejection ratios of between 66 and 75 dB.
As expected, the common mode levels were unchanged for each unique physical digitizer
channel and did not vary with sample rate or gain.

Crosstalk
The SMAD digitizer channels exhibited crosstalk that was measured to be better than between
-153.5 and -145.2 dB. The measurement was limited due to there being no observable crosstalk
on the channel self-noise.

Time Tag Accuracy
The SMAD digitizers were measured to have time tag accuracy values with a mean level of
between -33 and -98 microseconds using GNSS.

 122

Time Tag Drift
The SMAD digitizers using GNSS for time synchronization drifted by between 6 and 8 us/hr
when maintained at a temperature near 25 C, for a total drift of between 100 to 140 us over 16
hours. Drift rates were more variable when temperatures were changing over -20 to 60°C, with
total drift of between 500 and 1200 microseconds over a similar 16 hour period. All digitizers
recovered back to their original time tag accuracy within 5 to 15 minutes of the timing source
becoming available, with larger timing drifts resulting in a timing system reset that caused a brief
data gap.

Calibrator
The SMAD digitizers demonstrated the ability to accurately generate sinusoidal calibration
signals at amplitudes of between 1 V and 10.0 V and frequencies between 0.05 Hz and 10 Hz.

Sensor Compatibility Verification
The SMAD digitizer was able to demonstrate the proper operation and calibration of a
Nanometrics Trillium Horizon 120 seismometer, MB2005 infrasound sensor, and MB3a
infrasound sensors.

CD1.1 Status Flag Verification
The SMAD digitizer demonstrated the transmission of CD1.1 status flags for tamper switches,
calibration underway, GPS receiver unlocked, clock differential too large, and GPS off.

CD1.1 Integration
The SMAD digitizers were successfully operated with data collection using a CD1.1 receiver in
a local installation of NDC-In-A-Box.
