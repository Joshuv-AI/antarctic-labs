1 Introduction
The idea of a neutron target enables the measurement of neutron-induced reac-
tions in inverse kinematics [1]. This idea is part of the LANSCE strategy to stay
a worldwide leader for neutron-induced research [2, 3]. The proof-of-principle
experiment outlined in [4, 5] is the core of the LDRD project 20240004DR.
The current approach is to use an ion source capable of accelerating single-charged
ions up to about 60 keV. A beam of 78 Kr+ ions will be created to interact with a
neutron target and implanted. During the passage of the target, neutrons can be
captured and the freshly produced 79 Kr+ ions will be implanted along with the
main beam. The energy in the center of mass is largely determined by the energy
of the ions. Hence, changing the terminal voltage of the ion source enables an
energy-dependent determination of the implanted 79 Kr/78 Kr ratio. Figure 1 shows
the evaluated 78 Kr(n,γ) cross section [6]. This cross section has a well-suited reso-
nance at Ecm = 451 eV in the center of, mass, which corresponds to a beam energy
of Ebeam = 35.63 keV. A successful scan of this resonance proofs the principle of
investigating neutron-induced reactions in inverse kinematics.

The neutron target consists of an ion beam pipe close to a spallation neutron target
[7] driven by the LANSCE accelerator [8]. The spallation target including the ion
beam pipe are surrounded by material optimized for moderating neutrons. Moder-
ators made of beryllium or at least with a beryllium core have been shown to yield
higher neutron target densities than graphite moderators [9, 10]. In this report, we
will therefore discuss 2 realistic moderator geometries made from pieces already
available at LANSCE. The focus will be on a particular background component,
which depends on the choice of the geometry and composition of moderator. We

 2

will estimate the signal-to-background ratio for the implantation experiment. The
signal is the implanted 79 Kr/78 Kr ratio as a result of the beam passing the neu-
tron target. The background is the 79 Kr/78 Kr ratio resulting from captures of stray
neutrons by already implanted implanted 78 Kr atoms.
During the experiment, the spallation target will receive 1 µA average LANSCE
proton beam - either directly from the LINAC or compressed in the PSR (Proton
Storage Ring). We need pulsed beam with a pulse length of at most 1 ms and a
repetition rate of at most 1 pulse every 5 s (0.2 Hz or less). The krypton beam
will be pulsed too and synchronized with the proton beam. The duration of the
krypton beam will be about 10 ms to cover the time when neutrons are actually
present and will be off otherwise, see Figure 2. This ensures a sufficiently high
ratio of freshly produced 79 Kr+ ions to 78 Kr+ ions from the beam. One scenario
could be that LINAC pulse with the same structure as for the PSR/Lujan center
would be delivered to the Blue Room every 5 s. Those pulses have a width of
about 625 µs and a total charge of 5 µC. If there a technical problems, we can also
accept those pulses after compression in the PSR.

7.3 cm x 7.3 cm. This means, 4 carbon pieces (76.3 cm x 10.8 cm x 10.8 cm) in
clover arrangement can be almost perfectly replaced by 18 beryllium pieces.

4 Conclusion
 • The suggested Be-C moderator increases the expected 79 Kr/78 Kr ratio al-
 most by a factor of 4 while the background situation remains largely un-
 changed. The signal-to-background ratio is therefore improved by a factor
 of 4 compared to the pure C moderator used during experiments so far.

 • The by far largest contribution to the background from neutron captures by
 already implanted 78 Kr comes from neutrons leaking out of the moderator
 walls. This component can be reduced by more than 1 order of magnitude
 with 1 mm Cd surrounding the moderator and the ion pipe.

 • The background ratio scales with the duration of the implantation. Shorter

 11

 runs reduce this background component linearly while the signal ratio re-
 mains unchanged. In other words, a reduction of the implantation time from
 4 h to 2 h will reduce the number of 79 Kr implanted with the beam by only a
 factor of 2, while the 79 Kr atoms resulting from neutron captures by already
 implanted 78 Kr atoms by a factor of 4.
