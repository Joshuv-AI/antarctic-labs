Abstract

53

Phenylketonuria (PKU), caused by variants in the phenylalanine hydroxylase (PAH) gene, is

54

the most common autosomal recessive Mendelian phenotype in amino acid metabolism. We

55

estimated that globally 0.45 million have PKU, with global prevalence 1:23,930 live births

56

(range 1:4,500 [Italy] – 1:125,000 [Japan]). Comparing genotypes and metabolic phenotypes

57

from 16,092 patients revealed differences in diseases severity in 51 countries from 17 world

58

regions, with the global phenotype distribution of 62% classic PKU, 22% mild PKU and 16%

59

mild hyperphenylalaninemia. A gradient in genotype and phenotype distribution existed

60

across Europe, from classic PKU in the east, to mild PKU in the southwest and mild

61

hyperphenylalaninemia in the south. The p.Y414C-associated genotype can be traced from

62

northern to western Europe, from Sweden via Norway, Denmark to central Europe. The

63

frequency of classic PKU increases from Europe (56%) via Middle-East (71%) to Australia

64

(80%). Of 758 PAH variants, p.R408W (22.2%), c.1066-11G>A (6.4%) and p.R261Q (5.5%)

65

were most common and responsible for two prevalent genotypes: p.[R408W];[R408W]

66

(11.4%) and c.[1066-11G>A];[1066-11G>A] (2.6%). Most genotypes (73%) were compound

67

heterozygous, 27% were homozygous and 55% of 3,659 different genotypes occurred in a

68

single patient only. PAH variants were scored using an allelic phenotype value and correlated

69

with pre-treatment blood phenylalanine levels (n=6,796) and tetrahydrobiopterin test

70

results (n=4,381), enabling a genotype-based phenotype (88%) and tetrahydrobiopterin

71

responsiveness (83%) prediction. This study shows that large genotype databases enable

72

accurate phenotype prediction, allowing appropriate targeting of therapies to optimize

73

clinical outcome.

Introduction

75

Phenylketonuria (PKU; MIM 261600) is the most frequent inborn errors of the amino acid

76

metabolism. It is caused by more than 1,180 biallelic variations in the phenylalanine

77

hydroxylase (PAH) gene located on chromosome 12q22-24.1.1 These autosomal recessive

78

inherited variations lead to a deficiency in the PAH enzyme, which normally hydroxylates

79

phenylalanine into tyrosine, with the help of a cofactor (tetrahydrobiopterin; BH 4),

80

molecular oxygen, and non-heme iron.2

81

The clinical picture is highly heterogenous as it depends on the degree of residual PAH

82

activity and blood phenylalanine (Phe) levels. Usually, lower residual enzyme activity results

83

in higher the blood phenylalanine levels and a more severe PKU phenotype.3

84

The severity of PKU is classified according to the pre-treatment blood phenylalanine

85

concentration and daily dietary phenylalanine tolerance, ranging from the most severe

86

classical PKU (cPKU) with pre-treatment blood phenylalanine levels of >1,200 µmol/L, to mild

87

PKU (mPKU) with pre-treatment blood phenylalanine levels of 600–1,200 µmol/L and mild

88

hyperphenylalaninemia (MHP) with pre-treatment phenylalanine blood levels of 120–600

89

μmol/L.1,4

90

Untreated PKU in newborns can result in global developmental delay or severe irreversible

91

intellectual disabilities, as well as growth failure, hypopigmentation, motor deficits, and

92

ataxia, and seizures.1 The population of patients with PKU is also heterogeneous in terms of

93

treatment history and diet compliance.4,5 Early diagnosis and treatment with a low-Phe diet

94

has enabled an almost normal life for the majority of PKU patients.6 Pharmacological

95

treatment with BH4 (sapropterin) and enzyme substitution therapy with Phe ammonia lyase

96

(PAL) provide alternative treatment options for some patients.7

97

PKU is one of the most frequent inherited disorders in Caucasians, with an incidence of

98

roughly 1:10,000 live births in the USA,8 although the prevalence of PKU varies significantly

99

among ethnicities and geographic regions worldwide. In Europe, the incidence of PKU ranges

100

from 1: 850 in the Karachay-Cherkess Republic (Russia)9 to only 1:112,000 live births in

101

Finland.10 PKU occurs less often Japan, with an incidences of 1:120,000.11

102

A large number of PAH variations (>1,300) give rise to a wide scale of residual PAH enzyme

103

activities that correspond to different PKU phenotypes.12,13 Associations between genotypes
4

104

and in vitro residual PAH activity have been documented for many PAH variations.3

105

Therefore, the molecular genetics of PKU and genotype-based phenotype prediction may be

106

clinically useful, particularly where treatment recommendations are unclear (e.g. due to

107

borderline blood phenylalanine levels) or for genetic counseling of patients’ families.

108

The major goal of this study was the analysis of the largest phenotypes and genotypes

109

database to elucidate the current distribution of PKU worldwide and to create an overview

110

of the severity of PAH variants, genotypes and the resulting phenotypes worldwide, in

111

various geographic regions and respective countries. Furthermore, this work improves the

112

accuracy the genotypic phenotype prediction by the use of allelic phenotype value (APV)

113

value and the genotypic BH4 responsiveness prediction.

Subjects and methods

116

LITERATURE SEARCH

117

An electronic search using the databases MEDLINE (via Pub Med), the Cochrane library and

118

Web of Science was carried out to compare articles covering the epidemiology and genetics

119

of PKU in different world regions between January 1980 and October 2019. Key words

120

included Phenylketonuria[MeSH Terms] OR "phenylalanine hydroxylase/deficiency"[MeSH

121

Terms] OR hyperphenylalaninaemia[Title/Abstract] OR

122

hyperphenylalaninemia[Title/Abstract] OR PAH deficiency[Title/Abstract] OR phenylalanine

123

hydroxylase deficiency[Title/Abstract] OR phenylketonuri*[Title/Abstract] OR

124

pku[Title/Abstract]) AND ( "1980/01/01"[PDat] : "2019/12/31"[PDat] ) AND

125

Humans[Mesh] Filters: Publication date from 1980/01/01 to 2019/12/31; Humans.

126

This literature search yielded 5,459 records without duplicates, of which 1,118 papers with

127

an appropriate title and abstract were assessed. The final number of relevant records was

128

256. (Supplementary Figure 1)

DATABASES

133

The PAH locus-specific database PAHvdb, ClinVar, HGMD and LOVD databases were

134

searched for variants. PAHvdb is linked with the genotypes-phenotypes BIOPKU database

135

and was used for analyses in this work. It follows the HGVS nomenclature recommendations.

136

The BIOPKU database encompasses information about more than 16,900 PKU patients from

137

51 countries, providing information on patients’ genotype, corresponding metabolic

138

phenotypes, BH4 responsiveness (where reported) and highest blood Phe levels before

139

starting the treatment (where reported). Individual information was collected from the

140

published literature or anonymized records submitted online. Phenotype information was

141

unknown for 690 patients. Table 1 shows the information included in the database.

142

DEFINITION OF PHENOTYPES

143

We used the term “PKU” to refer to the entire spectrum of phenotypes due to PAH

144

deficiency. Since not all countries use the same nomenclature for the severity of PKU and

145

HPA, we used in this study the following 3 phenotype groups: classical PKU (cPKU; pre-

146

treatment blood Phe >1200 µmol/L); mild PKU (mPKU; pre-treatment blood Phe 600–1200

147

µmol/L) and mild HPA (MHP; pre-treatment blood Phe 120–600). Patients with a moderate

148

PKU (pre-treatment blood Phe 900–1,200 µmol/L) were included in the mPKU group and

149

MHP included MHP-no treatment (blood Phe 120–360 µmol/L) and MHP-gray zone (360–600

150

µmol/L) categories.4 Any classifications, not fitting into one of the above groups (due to

151

different country-specific classifications), were reassigned on the basis of reported pre-

152

treatment blood Phe levels and the genotype.

153

DEFINITION OF THE ALLELIC (APV) AND GENOTYPIC PHENOTYPE VALUE (GPV)

154

APV is a value defining the association of a variant with the corresponding metabolic

155

phenotype, thus defining its severity. APV was calculated for variants occurring in a

156

functionally hemizygous constellation (i.e. in a combination with an inactive 0-allele) in at

157

least 5 patients.14 APVs range between 0 and 10, with following classification definitions

158

cPKU (APV = 0-2.7), mPKU (APV = 2.8-6.6) and MHP (APVs 6.7-10).14

159

The genotypic phenotype value (GPV) was calculated from APVs of both alleles and was

160

assigned to a higher APV (APVmax). This calculation was based on the fact that the milder
6

161

variant (with a higher APV) is always dominant over the severe one.14,15 Possible effects of

162

interallelic complementation and epigenetic factors, which may influence the

163

phenotype16,17, were not considered in this study.

164

DEFINITION OF BH4 RESPONSIVENESS

165

BH4 responsiveness was defined as a ≥30% reduction of blood Phe levels within 24-48 hours

166

after the administration of BH4 (20mg/kg body weight)18,19. A linear discriminant analysis was

167

applied to predict BH4 responsiveness based on the APV for untested patients.

168

The statistical analysis was performed using R, an open source software and flexible

170

programming language used for the statistical data analysis as well as graphic creations

171

(https://www.r-project.org/ ). A total of 16,196 records with a complete genotype

172

information (variant 1 and 2 known) were analyzed.

Results

175

PREVALENCE OF PKU

176

Based on the literature search and reports from national screening centers, the prevalence

177

of PKU was estimated for 64 countries. For parts of Africa, Asia, Southern America and

178

Caribbean there was no information. The estimated total number of PKU patients (all

179

phenotypes) from those 64 countries in 2018 was 360,466. For the remaining 257 countries

180

we were unable to find credible PKU prevalence sources; we used the average regional

181

prevalence of 64 countries, multiplied by their populations, resulting in an additional 94,114

182

PKU patients (total about 0.45 million PKU patients). The global PKU prevalence was

183

estimated to be 1: 23,930 newborns (Figure 1, Supplementary Table 1).

184

The PKU prevalence was highest in European and certain Middle Eastern populations. Italy

185

(1:4,000) and Ireland (1:4,545) had even higher prevalence than Iran and Jordan (both 1:

186

5,000) or Turkey (1:6,667). Saudi Arabia (1:14,245), Iraq (1:14,286) or United Arab Emirates

187

(1:14,493) had lower PKU prevalence, however.

7

188

PKU prevalence was also high in central European countries PKU affect patients at high rates

189

as well e.g. Germany (1:5,360), Czechia (1:5,521) and Slovakia (1:5,753). Slovenia (1:7,143),

190

Austria (1:5,764) or Poland (1:8,309) had similar rates to eastern Europe, e.g. Estonia

191

(1:7,143), Russia (1: 7,714) Belarus (1:7,692), and Croatia (1:8,333).

192

PKU occurred slightly less frequently in western Europe, e.g. in France (1:9,091), United

193

Kingdom (1:10,000), Belgium (1:11,000) or the Netherlands (11,546), and in southern

194

Europe, e.g. Spain (1:10,115) and Portugal (1:12,500).

195

Northern Europe presented with the lowest PKU rates in Europe, e.g. for Norway (1:11,457),

196

Sweden (1: 12,681), Denmark (1: 13,434), or only 1:112,000 in Finland.

197

PKU occurred more frequently In Canada (1:15,000) than in the United States of America

198

(1:25,000) or in Latin American countries, including Chile (1:19,231), Brazil (1:25,000),

199

Mexico (1:27,778), Argentina (1:33,333) or Peru (1:46,970).

200

The lowest PKU prevalence was reported in Asian countries, such as Thailand (1:227,273),

201

Japan (1:125,000), Philippines (1:116,006) or Singapore (1:83,333). One exception was China

202

where the PKU prevalence was 1:15,924, which was comparable to Europe.

DESCRIPTIVE ANALYSIS OF THE PHENYLALANINE HYDROXYLASE GENE LOCUS-SPECIFIC DATABASE

204

Substitutions were by far the most frequent variant type In the PAHvdb (80.5%), followed by

205

deletions (12.9%) and duplications (2.1%). Of all variants, 691 were missense variants

206

(58.3%), followed by 165 frameshift variants (13.9%) and 155 splice site (13.1%) variants.

207

Nonsense variants (6.9%), synonymous variants (4.9%) and in-frame variants (1.9%) were

208

less frequent. Extension, complex and unknown variants, as well as polymorphisms,

209

accounted for the remaininder. Exon 6 contained the most variants (14.1%), followed by

210

Exon 7 (12.2%) and Exon 3 (9.9%). Most variants (59.2%) were located in the central catalytic

211

domain, 17.5% in the N-terminal regulatory domain and 5.4% in the C-terminal

212

oligomerization domain of the PAH monomer. The remaining variants (17.9%) were either in

213

the intronic or UTR regions. Only 7.7% of all variants were located in one of the 4 cofactor

214

binding regions (Supplementary Figure 2).

215

The APV was known for 589/1,186 variants. Most variants (441) were defined as severe 0-

216

alleles (APV=0), 32 variants as cPKU phenotype, 52 as mPKU and 64 as mild MHP alleles.

DESCRIPTIVE ANALYSIS OF THE BIOPKU DATABASE

218

As of October 2019, the BIOPKU database contained anonymized data on more than 16,900

219

PKU patients. A total of 16,196 PKU patients with 3,659 different genotypes were analyzed.

220

Information on the PKU phenotype was available for 16,902 patients. Maximum

221

pretreatment Phe levels were recorded for only 6,796 patients and the BH4 test information

222

GLOBAL PKU PHENOTYPE AND GENOTYPE DISTRIBUTION

224

Information on the PKU phenotype was available for 16,092 out of 16,196 patients (99%). Of

225

these, most had cPKU (9,923; 61.7%), 3,521 (21.9%) had mPKU, and 2,648 had MHP (16.4%).

226

Information about the phenotype was unavailable for 104 patients

227

The comparison of pre-treatment Phe level with the phenotype (n=6,796) is illustrated in

228

Figure 2A and with the GPVs (n=6,371) in Figure 2B. The interquartile range (n, median, 25 th–

229

75th percentile) was smallest and lowest for the MHP patient group: 1,283, 320 µmol/L, 242–

230

432 µmol/L, and was larger for mPKU and cPKU (1,487, 793 µmol/L, 660–793 µmol/L, and

231

3,599, 1,550 µmol/L, 1,270–1936 µmol/L, respectively).

232

A total of 758 different alleles were identified in this study. The three most prevalent

233

variants were p.R408W, with an allele frequency (AF) =22.2%, c.1066-11G>A (IVS10-11G>A;

238

(5.8%) homozygotes. The three most prevalent genotypes were: p.[R408W];[R408W] with a

Discussion

372

The aim of this retrospective study was to elucidate the PKU prevalence and distribution of

373

causative PAH variants worldwide and in different countries. We provided a rough

374

estimation of global PKU prevalence by calculating the number of affected patients for

375

countries based on the provided PKU prevalence and the total population in 2018 22 and an

376

average prevalence for countries not employing newborn screening for PKU. Overall, it

377

seems that there are about 0.45 million PKU patients worldwide, of whom at least two thirds

378

have PKU that requires treatment (most patients had a severe, cPKU phenotype). An

379

improvement in early diagnosis via NBS in states without this should be an urgent priority.

380

Although PKU has been found to be most common in European populations,8 its similar

381

prevalence in certain Middle Eastern countries (particularly Turkey and Iran) was a

382

remarkable finding.23,24 A possible contributing factors may be a tendency to consanguinity

383

in Arabic cultures, especially first cousin marriages, which favors the autosomal recessive

384

inheritance of PKU.25 Prevalence and marriage status was missing for many countries,

385

making it more difficult to assess this proposed causal relationship for all states.26

386

We identified 758 different PKU alleles in this study, emphasizing the strong genetic

387

heterogeneity of PKU. Information of about 10,000 genotypes and phenotypes from PKU

388

patients in Europe enabled the a more detailed study of their distribution across different

389

regions and countries than has been possible previously.27,28 The severe p.R408W variant
14

390

was the most common overall, especially among Eastern European populations, in

391

accordance with previous studies.29,30 Furthermore, p.R408W dominates most Central

392

European populations (Poland, Slovakia, Czechia, Austria, or Germany), also supporting

393

previous studies.31-35

394

Previous research had suggested that some PKU variants appeared to have been carried by

395

migration, e.g. p.R408W, IVS10-11G>A, IVS12+1G>A or p.F299C and R408Q.27 Our study

396

confirms and extends the knowledge on how certain variants appear to have spread within

397

Europe and to different regions and countries. In particular, the distribution of PKU variants

398

across Europe was consistent with successive waves of historical migration, and Figure 4

399

summarizes the likely geographical routes of transmission of these variants within Europe.

400

For example, Germany has occupied a crossroads location during several migration waves

401

throughout history, and displays a broad spectrum of PAH variants, with similarities to some

402

other populations, such as Northern European countries (Denmark, Sweden and Norway). 35

403

This implies a genetic connection of these regions, possibly during the Germanic settlement

404

of Scandinavia. Norway’s most frequent variant, p.G46S, did not occurring in the German

405

group. Furthermore, the occurrence of IVS10-11G>A in Germany was surprisingly high in our

406

analysis, which might be explained by immigration from Turkey. This variant, often described

407

as the “Mediterranean mutation”, may be of Italian origin.36 and has been found mainly in

408

Southern countries or the Middle-East.37,38 Our study showed that IVS10-11G>A has the

409

highest AF in Middle Eastern countries (Turkey, Iran and Israel), and in Portugal and Spain,

410

The p.R408W-associated genotype, the most common variant in people with Slavic roots,

411

followed the east-central-southwest European axis, starting from Estonia, with the highest

412

number of severe PKU patients, via Russia, Poland, Czechia, Slovakia, Germany, France, and

413

Italy, down to Spain. The high prevalence of cPKU in Russia, and the dominance of the severe

414

variant, p.R408W, were striking, given that Russia has a diverse ethnic composition, with

415

>160 ethnic groups.39

416

Another well-documented axis exists for the p.Y414C-associated genotype, from northern to

417

western Europe, i.e. from Sweden via Norway, Denmark and Germany to the Netherlands.

418

Some variants, e.g., IVS12+1G>A, seem to have migrated within Scandinavian countries

419

(Denmark >Sweden >Norway) at high frequency. The same was true for the p.E390G-

420

associated genotype concentrating between Slovenia, Croatia, Serbia and Austria. A typical
15

421

east-west gradient can be seen for IVS10-11G>A, originating from Western Asia and the

422

Middle-East, i.e., from Armenia (48%) via Turkey, Iran, Israel to Spain. Similar consequences

423

of migration were evident for the very mild p.A300S-associated genotypes, migrating from

424

Turkey via Israel, Italy to Spain. No trends for p.V245A and p.A403V distribution across

425

Europe could be seen, however.

426

The influence of migration patterns during history can also be seen in the heterogenous

427

spectrum of PAH variations in Latin America. These countries were strongly marked by

428

immigration of Europeans, especially from southern Europe, during colonial times.40 An

429

example is p.V388M, which was especially prevalent in Brazil and Chile, once colonies of

430

Portugal and Spain. The Argentina cohort was more prevalent for variations frequently

431

occurring in eastern, central and northern Europe; e.g., as p.R408W, p.R261Q and IVS10-

432

11G>A. In contrast to Brazil or Argentina, Mexico was characterized by a high percentage of

433

indigenous population, which could be a reason for the exceptional high AF of the

434

IVS1+5G>T splice variant there.41

435

The USA has one of the most multi-racial and ethnic populations in the world. The strongest

436

ancestral influence is European, mainly from Germany, Ireland, England, Italy and France 42,

437

which explains a comparable distribution of PKU variants in the USA and Europe. 43 African

438

Americans have a much lower PKU incidence than Caucasian Americans,44 and observing

439

changes in the growth of causative variants in USA, major ethnic groups there (Caucasian,

440

African-American, Hispanic, Asian) grow at different rates would be an interesting area for

441

future research.

442

While the overall PKU prevalence in China was 1:15,924, its distribution across the country

443

varies significantly with higher rates in the north in comparison to the south.45 In accordance

444

with previous reports, p.R234Q accounts for 23% of all variants in China. 46 Three additional

445

variants p.R243Q, Ex6-96A>G and p.R241C (AF>5%) were common in Korea, Taiwan as well,

446

indicating commonalities of migration movements and evolution among those nationalities.

447

Prevalent PKU variants for Europe, Middle-East, Latin America and the USA were uncommon

448

in the Asian populations.

449

Preliminary data indicated a value of large patient databases with the genotype and

450

phenotype information and introduction of the APV and GPV for the genotypic phenotype

451

prediction.14 This study extends this previous knowledge with additional information, and
16

452

has confirmed the power of genotyping in phenotype definition and BH4 responsiveness in

453

PKU, thus offering a powerful tool in the personalized medicine of this inherited metabolic

454

disease.

455

This study also documents that functional mild variants (in a compound heterozygous

456

constellation), with a substantial residual PAH activity, were always dominant over inactive

457

severe variants (0-alleles) and gave raise to a milder phenotype and potential BH4

458

responsiveness. Compared with severe classic PKU variants, they were much rarer in

459

number, but determined the milder metabolic phenotype. Interestingly, homozygous mild

460

variants have higher APV and thus milder phenotype than when in a compound

461

heterozygous state with a 0-alle14. Two inactive severe variants are never BH4-responsive, as

462

a rule, although anecdotal reports sometimes suggest this.47

463

In conclusion, this study provides an overview of the current distribution of the most

464

important PAH variants and patients genotypes in various world regions. This information,

465

together with the APV value, helps to predict the PKU phenotype and the possible treatment

466

options. Despite immense progress in diagnosis and treatment of PKU in the last decade

467

there are still too many areas of the world without adequate access to this care. Especially

468

data from Africa, certain Asian and Southern American as well as Caribbean countries was

469

missing.
