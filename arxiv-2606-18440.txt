Algebraic Networks and Architectural Degenerations First Draft
Giacomo Graziani

Abstract
. We study the geometry of polynomial neural networks with monomial activation functions and no bias. We introduce a general framework of algebraic networks, together with their realization maps and associated affine neurovarieties. In this setting we define morphisms, subnetworks, symmetry groups and quotient parameter spaces and we discuss geometric notions of identifiability and reducibility. Our main goal is to relate the singularities of neurovarieties to degenerations of the underlying architecture. For fully connected networks, we define the architectural degeneracy locus as the locus of functions admitting a representation by parameters with a rank-deficient layer or an inactive hidden neuron. We prove that, for fully connected networks with non-increasing widths and scalar output, full parameters give smooth points of the corresponding neurovariety under explicit layerwise regularity assumptions. In particular, for these architectures, the singular locus is contained in the architectural degeneracy locus.



1.
Introduction

Neural networks are usually presented through their parameter spaces. However,
from a geometric point of view, the fundamental object is the space of functions
realized by a fixed architecture. For polynomial neural networks this space has
an algebraic nature: after choosing the input and output spaces, the realization
map is a polynomial map from the parameter space to a finite-dimensional vector
space of polynomial functions and the Zariski closure of its image is an
algebraic variety. Following the terminology used in the recent literature, we
refer to these varieties as neurovarieties.

This point of view has been developed in several directions. The dimension of
neurovarieties was proposed in
[
7
]
as a measure of expressivity for
deep polynomial neural networks and has since been studied for fully connected
architectures, convolutional architectures and attention models

[
8
,
2
,
12
,
5
,
11
]
. More generally, the program of
neuroalgebraic geometry emphasizes that algebro-geometric invariants of
function spaces, such as dimension, degree, singularities and critical point
counts, reflect statistical and optimization-theoretic properties of the
corresponding models
[
9
]
. In
[
4
]
the author studied the
projective geometry of algebraic neural layers and the stable behavior of their
generic Euclidean distance degree. The present paper moves from single layers
to deep architectures and focuses on the relation between singularities and
degenerations of the architecture.

The starting point is the following guiding principle: singularities of a
neurovariety should arise from structural degenerations of the network. This
principle appears in different forms in the recent literature. For linear
networks and for certain convolutional polynomial networks, singular functions
are closely related to smaller subnetworks. In the case of polynomial
convolutional networks, Shahverdi, Marchetti and Kohn prove that, apart from
the vertex of the affine cone, singularities come from functions which can be
represented by smaller convolutional architectures
[
11
]
. In

[
11
]
, Shahverdi, Marchetti and Kohn study singularities of MLPs and
CNNs with generic polynomial activations: for MLPs they show that sparse
subnetworks often give singular points, while for CNNs they obtain a complete
description in terms of subnetworks. The survey
[
9
]
formulates this
phenomenon as part of a broader dictionary between the geometry of
neuromanifolds and learning-theoretic behavior, where subnetworks and implicit
bias correspond to singularities.

The goal of this paper is to give a geometric framework in which this principle
can be made precise, at least in a class of fully connected polynomial networks.
We restrict to networks with monomial activations and no bias. This allows us
to work with homogeneous polynomial maps and with affine neurovarieties in a
fixed vector space of forms. The first part of the paper is foundational: we
introduce algebraic networks, morphisms, subnetworks, symmetry groups and
quotient parameter spaces. The notion of subnetwork is inspired by the abstract
formalism of submodels developed by Shahverdi, Marchetti, Bökman and Kohn
in
[
6
]
, where deep models are treated as sequential compositions of
parametric maps and submodels are used to separate the identifiable part of the
architecture from degenerate representations. Our definitions are more
specialized, since they are designed for algebraic networks with monomial
activations, but they retain the same basic idea: a subnetwork is an
architecture embedded in a larger one which realizes the same end-to-end
function after inclusion of its parameter space.

A second ingredient is the quotient by the natural symmetries of the
parameterization. For fully connected monomial networks, these symmetries are
generated by permutations of hidden neurons and by rescalings compatible with
the activation degrees. They are the algebraic counterpart of the usual
parameter symmetries appearing in identifiability results for polynomial neural
networks
[
7
,
8
,
12
]
. We remove these symmetries by considering
the affine quotient of the parameter space. This gives a geometric formulation
of identifiability: a network is generically finitely identifiable when the
induced map from the quotient parameter space to the neurovariety has
zero-dimensional general fiber.

The main object introduced in this paper is the architectural degeneracy locus.
For a fully connected network
X
we define
\mathcal{W}^{\circ}
as the locus of full
parameters, namely parameters for which all matrices have maximal rank and no
hidden neuron has zero outgoing column. The architectural degeneracy locus

\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arch}}\left(X\right)=\overline{\Phi_{X}\left(\mathcal{W}\setminus\mathcal{W}^{\circ}\right)}

is the locus of functions admitting at least one degenerate representation. In
the fully connected case, under mild assumptions on the widths, we show that
this locus coincides with the closure of the union of the neurovarieties of
proper rank-constrained subnetworks. Thus
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arch}}\left(X\right)
provides a geometric
realization of the idea that the architecture has effectively lost rank or that
some hidden neuron is inactive.

1.1.
Structure of the paper

In Section 1 we introduce algebraic networks. An algebraic network is a triple

X=(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{W},\boldsymbol{\underline{r}})
, where
\mathcal{N}
is a sequence of finite-dimensional vector
spaces,
\mathcal{W}
is an irreducible algebraic parameter space inside the product
of the spaces of linear layers and
\boldsymbol{\underline{r}}
is the vector of activation degrees.
The realization map

\Phi_{X}:\mathcal{W}\longrightarrow X_{\mathrm{out}}\otimes\mathrm{Sym}^{r}(X_{\mathrm{in}}^{\ast})

associates with a parameter the polynomial function computed by the network,
and the affine neurovariety
\mathcal{F}\left(X\right)
is the Zariski closure of its image. We also define morphisms of algebraic networks. The definition is rigid: it
requires compatibility with both the linear layers and the activation maps. This
rigidity ensures that a morphism of networks induces a well-defined closed
embedding between the corresponding neurovarieties.

In Section 2 we study subnetworks and architectural degenerations. We define
subnetworks categorically, as certain morphisms whose maps on hidden layers are
inclusions of subspaces spanned by subsets of neurons. We then introduce the
full parameter locus
\mathcal{W}^{\circ}
and the architectural degeneracy locus

\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arch}}\left(X\right)
. For fully connected networks satisfying a mild condition on the widths, we prove that
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arch}}\left(X\right)
is the closure of the union of the
neurovarieties of proper rank-constrained subnetworks. We also introduce a
notion of reducedness: a network is reduced if its neurovariety cannot be
realized by a proper rank-constrained version of the same architecture.

In Section 3 we discuss symmetries and quotient parameter spaces. To an
algebraic network we associate a group
\mathcal{G}\left(X\right)
of rescalings and
permutations preserving the parameter space. This group is linearly reductive,
and therefore the affine quotient

\mathfrak{W}\left(X\right)=\mathrm{Spec}\bigl(\mathbb{C}\left[\mathcal{W}\right]^{\mathcal{G}\left(X\right)}\bigr)

exists. The realization map factors through a morphism
\phi_{X}:\mathfrak{W}\left(X\right)\to\mathcal{F}\left(X\right)
.
We use this quotient to define identifiability and finite identifiability in
geometric terms. We also prove that, for fully connected networks, generic
finite identifiability implies reducedness.

In Section 4 we prove the regularity result outside the architectural
degeneracy locus. We first introduce good parameters, namely parameters
satisfying

\ker d\Phi_{X,w}=\mathfrak{g}\left(X\right)\cdot w.

This condition says that the only infinitesimal deformations of the parameter
which do not change the realized function are those coming from infinitesimal
symmetries. For fully connected networks with non-increasing widths, the full
quotient
\mathfrak{W}^{\circ}\left(X\right)
is smooth and formal local isomorphisms between the
quotient and the neurovariety imply goodness of the corresponding parameters.

The technical core of the paper is a layerwise formal reconstruction argument.
For each layer we introduce an auxiliary parameter space
Q_{a,B,r}(E)

parametrizing decompositions of sums of powers with vector coefficients. A
point of this space is called regular if the associated secant parameterization
induces an isomorphism on completed local rings. This condition is a local
version of identifiability for the layer. We then show that, if the relevant
layerwise triples are regular, the completed local rings of the successive
quotients
\mathcal{P}_{t}
are isomorphic. Iterating over the layers gives an
isomorphism

\widehat{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{F}\left(X\right),\Phi_{X}\left(w\right)}}\simeq\widehat{\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{W}^{\circ}\left(X\right),\left[w\right]}}

for every full parameter
w
. Since the right-hand side is regular, the
function
\Phi_{X}(w)
is a smooth point of the neurovariety.

1.2.
Main results

The main result of the paper is the following

Theorem

(see Theorem
35
)
.

Let
X
be a fully connected network with non-increasing widths and
scalar output and suppose that for every layer
t
either
r_{t}\geq 3

or
r_{t}=2
and
\dim\mathcal{N}_{t}=\dim\mathcal{N}_{t+1}
. Let
w\in\mathcal{W}
. If

\Phi_{X}\left(w\right)
is singular, then
w
contains a matrix
with non-maximal rank or a matrix with a vanishing column. Moreover

X
is reduced in the sense of Definition
8
and
generically finitely identifiable.

The proof of Theorem
35
is obtained by combining
the layerwise regularity criterion of Theorem

27
with a classification
of regular triples in Proposition
34
. The first
ingredient shows that, under a suitable local regularity condition at each
layer, every full parameter realizes a smooth point of the neurovariety.
The second ingredient verifies this local condition in the concrete cases
appearing in the statement of the theorem.

The remaining conclusions follow from the quotient-theoretic part of the
paper. We introduce good parameters in Definition
18

and prove in Proposition
21
that the existence of
a good parameter is equivalent, for fully connected networks, to generic
finite identifiability. Finally, Lemma
16
shows
that a fully connected generically finitely identifiable network is reduced.
Thus the same local analysis which excludes singularities from the full
locus also implies the identifiability and reducedness statements in
Theorem
35
.

These results should be viewed as a first formal instance of the principle
that singularities of neurovarieties arise from degenerations of the
architecture. The framework is intentionally general enough to separate the
formal mechanism from the particular fully connected case treated here. The
hope is that the same language of algebraic networks, subnetworks, quotient
parameter spaces and architectural degeneracy loci can be adapted to other
algebraic architectures, such as convolutional, equivariant or tensor-network
models.

2.
Algebraic networks and realizations

All vector spaces and schemes are over
\mathbb{C}
. We restrict, for our
definition, to networks that have monomial activation and without
bias.

Definition 1
.

An algebraic network is a triple
X=\left(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{W},\boldsymbol{\underline{r}}\right)
where

•

\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{N}_{0},\dots,\mathcal{N}_{L}
is a sequence of finite dimensional vector
spaces together with the choice of a basis
\left(e_{i}^{j}\right)_{\begin{array}[]{c}i=1,\dots,L-1\\
j=1,\dots,\dim\mathcal{N}_{i}\end{array}}
for each space
\mathcal{N}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{N}_{L-1}
. The spaces
\mathcal{N}_{0}
and

\mathcal{N}_{L}
are called the input space and the output space respectively,
denoted with
X_{\mathrm{in}}
and
X_{\mathrm{out}}
, the other spaces are called the hidden
layers of the network and the number of hidden layers is called the
length of the network.

•

\mathcal{W}
is a Zariski closed and irreducible subset of

(2.1)

\mathcal{W}\subseteq\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}\left(\mathcal{N}_{0},\mathcal{N}_{1}\right)\times\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}\left(\mathcal{N}_{1},\mathcal{N}_{2}\right)\times\dots\times\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}\left(\mathcal{N}_{L-1},\mathcal{N}_{L}\right)

containing 0, called the parameter space of the network. We say that

X
is fully connected if equality holds in (
2.1
)
and more generally that
X
is decomposable if
\mathcal{W}
writes as a
product of
\mathcal{W}_{i}\subseteq\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}\left(\mathcal{N}_{i-1},\mathcal{N}_{i}\right)
.

•

\boldsymbol{\underline{r}}=\left(r_{1},\dots,r_{L-1}\right)
is a vector of integers
r_{i}\geq 1

called the activation degrees. We say that the network is linear if

r_{i}=1
for every
i
, that is is quadratic if
r_{i}=2
for
every
i
and so on.

The realization map is

\begin{aligned} \Phi_{X}:\mathcal{W}&\to X_{\mathrm{out}}\otimes\mathrm{Sym}^{r}\left(X_{\mathrm{in}}^{\ast}\right)\\
w=\left(w_{1},\dots,w_{L}\right)&\mapsto\Phi_{X}\left(w\right)\end{aligned}\quad\quad\begin{aligned} \Phi_{X}\left(w\right):X_{\mathrm{in}}&\to X_{\mathrm{out}}\\
x&\mapsto\left(w_{L}\circ\sigma_{r_{L-1}}\circ\dots\circ w_{2}\circ\sigma_{r_{1}}\circ w_{1}\right)\left(x\right)\end{aligned}

where
r=r_{1}\cdot\dots\cdot r_{L-1}
is called the total degree
and
\sigma_{r_{i}}:\mathcal{N}_{i}\to\mathcal{N}_{i}
is the functions that raises
each component (with respect to the fixed basis) to the power
r_{i}
.
Finally we denote with
\mathcal{F}\left(X\right)\subseteq X_{\mathrm{out}}\otimes\mathrm{Sym}^{r}\left(X_{\mathrm{in}}^{\ast}\right)

the Zariski closure of the image of
\Phi_{X}
, called the affine
neurovariety associated with
X
. since
\mathcal{W}
is assumed to be irreducible,
the image
\Phi_{X}\left(\mathcal{W}\right)
is irreducible and hence also
the variety
\mathcal{F}\left(X\right)
is irreducible.

In view of
[
3
]
and
[
6
]
we give the following definition
of morphism between PNN

Definition 2
.

Given two algebraic networks
X=\left(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{W},\boldsymbol{\underline{r}}\right)

and
Y=\left(\mathcal{N}^{\prime},\mathcal{W}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{\underline{r}}^{\prime}\right)
of the same length
L
,
total degree
r
and
X_{\mathrm{in}}=Y_{\mathrm{in}}
,
X_{\mathrm{out}}=Y_{\mathrm{out}}
, we define a morphism

f:X\to Y
as a pair

f=\left(\left(f_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{L-1},\beta\right)

where
\beta:\mathcal{W}\to\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
is an algebraic morphism and
f_{i}:\mathcal{N}_{i}\to\mathcal{N}_{i}^{\prime}

are linear maps with
f_{0}=\mathrm{id}_{X_{\mathrm{in}}}
and
f_{L}=\mathrm{id}_{X_{\mathrm{out}}}

and such that

f_{i}\circ w_{i}=\beta\left(w\right){}_{i}\circ f_{i-1},\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad f_{i}\circ\sigma_{r_{i}}=\sigma_{r_{i}^{\prime}}\circ f_{i}

for every
i=1,\dots,L-1
. In the case when
\mathcal{W}=\prod\mathcal{W}{}_{i}
and

\mathcal{W}^{\prime}=\prod\mathcal{W}^{\prime}_{i}
are decomposable we say that the morphism respects
the decomposition if
\beta=\prod\beta_{i}
with
\beta_{i}:\mathcal{W}{}_{i}\to\mathcal{W}^{\prime}_{i}

are morphisms of affine varieties.

Given a morphism
f:X\to Y
as in Definition
2

we have a natural map
\mathcal{F}\left(f\right):\mathcal{F}\left(X\right)\to\mathcal{F}\left(Y\right)

with
\mathcal{F}\left(f\right):\Phi_{X}\left(w\right)\mapsto\Phi_{Y}\left(\beta\left(w\right)\right)

which is continuous for the Zariski topology, due to the polynomiality
of
\beta
. We see that the definition of
\mathcal{F}\left(f\right)
is
well-posed in the next Lemma. See
[
1
]
for other examples.

Lemma 3
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a morphism of algebraic
networks as in Definition
2
. Then
\Phi_{Y}\left(\beta\left(w\right)\right)=\Phi_{X}\left(w\right)
.
In particular
\Phi_{X}\left(\mathcal{W}\right)\subseteq\Phi_{Y}\left(\mathcal{W}^{\prime}\right)

hence
\mathcal{F}\left(X\right)\subseteq\mathcal{F}\left(Y\right).
Thus
f
induces a
well-defined closed embedding

\mathcal{F}\left(f\right):\mathcal{F}\left(X\right)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{F}\left(Y\right).

Proof.

Let
w=\left(w_{1},\dots,w_{L}\right)\in\mathcal{W}
. By Definition
2
,
for every
i=1,\dots,L
we have
f_{i}\circ w_{i}=\beta\left(w\right)_{i}\circ f_{i-1}

and for every
i=1,\dots,L-1
we have
f_{i}\circ\sigma_{r_{i}}=\sigma_{r_{i}^{\prime}}\circ f_{i}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\Phi_{Y}\left(\beta\left(w\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\beta\left(w\right)_{L}\circ\sigma_{r_{L-1}^{\prime}}\circ\beta\left(w\right)_{L-1}\circ\cdots\circ\beta\left(w\right)_{2}\circ\sigma_{r_{1}^{\prime}}\circ\beta\left(w\right)_{1}

\displaystyle=f_{L}\circ w_{L}\circ\sigma_{r_{L-1}}\circ w_{L-1}\circ\cdots\circ w_{2}\circ\sigma_{r_{1}}\circ w_{1}\circ f_{0}=\Phi_{X}\left(w\right).

The rest is clear.
∎

Remark 4
.

The notion of morphism in Definition
2
is rather
rigid. Indeed, the compatibility condition
f_{i}\circ\sigma_{r_{i}}=\sigma_{r_{i}^{\prime}}\circ f_{i}

strongly restricts the possible linear maps
f_{i}
and therefore
one should not expect many morphisms between two fixed algebraic networks.
The motivation for this rigidity is categorical. In view of
[
3
]
,
a network can be regarded as a directed diagram whose vertices are
the latent spaces
\mathcal{N}_{0},\mathcal{N}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{N}_{L}
and whose elementary
arrows are the linear layers and the activation maps. A morphism of
networks is then required to be a morphism of these underlying diagrams:
the maps
f_{i}
act on the vertices, while
\beta
transforms the
parameter-dependent arrows. The commutativity conditions in Definition

2
say precisely that all elementary faces of
this diagram commute. Consequently, the end-to-end realization is
preserved, as shown in Lemma
3
.

3.
Subnetworks

Fix two finite-dimensional vector spaces
V_{\mathrm{in}},V_{\mathrm{out}}
, an integer
L\geq 1

and total degree
r
, we consider the category
\mathrm{Net}=\mathrm{Net}\left(V_{\mathrm{in}},V_{\mathrm{out}},L,\boldsymbol{\underline{r}}\right)

whose objects are algebraic networks
X
with
X_{\mathrm{in}}=V_{\mathrm{in}}
,
X_{\mathrm{out}}=V_{\mathrm{out}}
,
length
L
and activations whose total degree is
r
. Morphisms
are as in Definition
2
and one readily checks
that we form a category. We rephrase the definition of subnetwork
given in
[
6
]
.

Definition 5
.

Given an object
X=\left(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{W},\boldsymbol{\underline{r}}\right)
of
\mathrm{Net}
, a subnetwork

Y=\left(\mathcal{N}^{\prime},\mathcal{W}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{\underline{r}}^{\prime}\right)
of
X
is a morphism
s:Y\to X

such that

•

Y_{\mathrm{in}}=X_{\mathrm{in}}
and
Y_{\mathrm{out}}=X_{\mathrm{out}}
and
s_{i}:\mathcal{N}_{i}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{N}_{i}
is the inclusion
of the subspace spanned by a subset of neurons

•

\beta:\mathcal{W}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{W}
is a closed embedding.

If
s:Y\to X
is a subnetwork we define the support of the subnetwork
as the image of the closed embedding

\mathcal{F}\left(s\right):\mathcal{F}\left(Y\right)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{F}\left(X\right).

We say that a subnetwork
Y\subseteq X
is strict if at least one of the
maps
s_{i}
is not an isomorphism. if either at least one of the
maps
s_{i}
or
\beta
is not an isomorphism we say that it is
proper, denoted
Y\subsetneq X
.

3.1.
Degenerate loci

Definition 6
.

Let
X=\left(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{W},\boldsymbol{\underline{r}}\right)
be an algebraic network. We define

\mathcal{W}^{\circ}\subseteq\mathcal{W}
as the subset of parameters
w=\left(w_{1},\ldots,w_{L}\right)

such that every matrix
w_{i}
has maximal rank and, for every hidden
layer
t=1,\ldots,L-1
and every neuron
e_{t}^{j}\in\mathcal{N}_{t}
, one
has
w_{t+1}\left(e_{t}^{j}\right)\neq 0
. We set

\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arch}}\left(X\right)=\overline{\Phi_{X}\left(\left(\mathcal{W}^{\circ}\right)^{\mathrm{c}}\right)}.

Remark 7
.

The definition of
\mathcal{W}^{\circ}
means that, with the exception of the matrix

w_{1}:X_{\mathrm{in}}\to\mathcal{N}_{1}
, we require all the component
w_{2},\dots,w_{L}

of
w
to have no column which is identically 0. Note that in general

\mathcal{W}^{\circ}
is an open subset of
\mathcal{W}
, even though it can be empty. A
notable case when it is non empty is the case when
X
is fully connected.

\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arch}}\left(X\right)
is then the closure of the locus of functions
admitting at least one degenerate representation, namely a representation
by a parameter for which either some matrix has non-maximal rank or
some hidden neuron has zero outgoing column. While having non-maximal
rank is related to the function belonging to a subnetwork, for instance
functions for which a neuron is not used, the condition about the
columns captures the idea that a function is realized by a neuron
that has inputs but doesn’t produce any output.

Definition 8
.

Let
X=\left(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{W},\boldsymbol{\underline{r}}\right)
be an algebraic
network of length
L
and let
\underline{\boldsymbol{d}}=\left(d_{1},\dots,d_{L}\right)

be a sequence of non-negative integers. We define
X^{\underline{\boldsymbol{d}}}
as the
subnetwork of
X
obtained with
\mathcal{N}\left(X^{\underline{\boldsymbol{d}}}\right)=\mathcal{N}\left(X\right)
,
but with space of parameters

\mathcal{W}^{\underline{\boldsymbol{d}}}=\left\{W\in\mathcal{W}\,|\,\mathrm{rk}\left(W_{i}\right)\leq d_{i}\quad\mbox{for every $i$}\right\}

whenever they are irreducible. We say that
\underline{\boldsymbol{d}}
is proper if
\mathcal{W}^{\underline{\boldsymbol{d}}}\neq\mathcal{W}

and that the network
X
is reduced if there exists no proper sequence

\underline{\boldsymbol{d}}
such that
\mathcal{F}\left(X^{\underline{\boldsymbol{d}}}\right)=\mathcal{F}\left(X\right)
.

Proposition 9
.

Let
X=\left(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{W},\boldsymbol{\underline{r}}\right)

be a fully connected network and suppose that for every
t=1,\ldots,L-1

either
\dim\mathcal{N}_{t}\leq\dim\mathcal{N}_{t-1}
or
\dim\mathcal{N}_{t}\leq\dim\mathcal{N}_{t+1}
.
Then we have

\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arch}}\left(X\right)=\overline{\bigcup_{\mathcal{W}^{\underline{\boldsymbol{d}}}\neq\mathcal{W}}\mathcal{F}\left(X^{\underline{\boldsymbol{d}}}\right)}.

Moreover under the previous assumptions on
\dim\mathcal{N}_{t}
we see that

X
is reduced if only if
\mathcal{F}\left(X\right)\neq\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arch}}\left(X\right)
.

Proof.

Clearly the inclusion

\overline{\bigcup_{\mathcal{W}^{\underline{\boldsymbol{d}}}\neq\mathcal{W}}\mathcal{F}\left(X^{\underline{\boldsymbol{d}}}\right)}\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arch}}\left(X\right)

holds for every network. Let
w\in\mathcal{W}\setminus\mathcal{W}^{\circ}
, then either some
matrix
w_{i}
has non-maximal rank, or some hidden neuron has zero
outgoing column. If some matrix
w_{i}
has non-maximal rank, then

w\in\mathcal{W}^{\underline{\boldsymbol{d}}}
for some
\mathcal{W}^{\underline{\boldsymbol{d}}}\neq\mathcal{W}
. If some hidden neuron has
zero outgoing column there exist a layer
t
and a basis vector
e_{t}^{j}\in\mathcal{N}_{t}

such that
w_{t+1}\left(e_{t}^{j}\right)=0
. In the case when
\dim\mathcal{N}_{t}\leq\dim\mathcal{N}_{t-1}

we let
\widetilde{w}
be the parameter obtained from

w
by replacing the
j
-th row of
w_{t}
by zero and leaving
all the other matrices unchanged. Since
X
is fully connected, we
still have
\widetilde{w}\in\mathcal{W}
and clearly
\Phi_{X}\left(\widetilde{w}\right)=\Phi_{X}\left(w\right)
.
In the case when
\dim\mathcal{N}_{t}\leq\dim\mathcal{N}_{t+1}
having a zero column
already means that the matrix does not have maximal rank. In either
case
\Phi_{X}\left(w\right)\in\mathcal{F}\left(X^{\underline{\boldsymbol{d}}}\right)
for some proper

\underline{\boldsymbol{d}}
and we conclude that

\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arch}}\left(X\right)\subseteq\overline{\bigcup_{\mathcal{W}^{\underline{\boldsymbol{d}}}\neq\mathcal{W}}\mathcal{F}\left(X^{\underline{\boldsymbol{d}}}\right)}

since the right-hand side is closed. Since
X
is fully connected,
each
\mathcal{W}^{\underline{\boldsymbol{d}}}
is a product of determinantal varieties, hence irreducible.
Therefore each
\mathcal{F}\left(X^{\underline{\boldsymbol{d}}}\right)
is irreducible. Moreover there
are only finitely many possible
\underline{\boldsymbol{d}}
’s. If
\mathcal{F}\left(X\right)=\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arch}}\left(X\right)

then the irreducible variety
\mathcal{F}\left(X\right)
is a finite union
of closed subsets

\mathcal{F}\left(X\right)=\bigcup_{\mathcal{W}^{\underline{\boldsymbol{d}}}\neq\mathcal{W}}\mathcal{F}\left(X^{\underline{\boldsymbol{d}}}\right).

Hence one of them must be equal to
\mathcal{F}\left(X\right)
, namely
\mathcal{F}\left(X^{\underline{\boldsymbol{d}}}\right)=\mathcal{F}\left(X\right)

for some proper
\underline{\boldsymbol{d}}
.
∎

4.
Symmetries

Fix an algebraic network
X=\left(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{W},\boldsymbol{\underline{r}}\right)
and from now on,
unless otherwise stated, we define its fully connected envelope as

\widehat{X}=\left(\mathcal{N},\widehat{\mathcal{W}},\boldsymbol{\underline{r}}\right)
where

\widehat{\mathcal{W}}=\prod\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}\left(\mathcal{N}_{i-1},\mathcal{N}_{i}\right)

If
\alpha_{i}=\dim\left(\mathcal{N}_{i}\right)
, in view of
[
12
, Lemma 4]

we define the group of symmetries of
\widehat{X}
as

\mathcal{G}\left(\widehat{X}\right)=\prod_{i=1}^{L-1}\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\alpha_{i}}\rtimes S_{\alpha_{i}}

and the action of an element
g=\left(D_{i},P_{i}\right)_{i=1,\dots,L}\in\mathcal{G}\left(\widehat{X}\right)

on
w=\left(w_{i}\right)_{i=1,\dots,L}\in\widehat{\mathcal{W}}
is

g\cdot w=\left(P_{i\hskip 0.0pt}D_{i}\hskip 0.0ptw_{i}\hskip 0.0ptD_{i-1}^{-r_{i-1}}\hskip 0.0pt\hskip 0.0ptP_{i-1}^{-1}\hskip 0.0pt\right)_{i=1,\dots,L}

with the conventions that
P_{0}=D_{0}=P_{L}=D_{L}=\mathrm{id}
.
More generally we define

\mathcal{G}\left(X\right)=\left\{g\in\mathcal{G}\left(\widehat{X}\right)\,|\,g\cdot\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{W}\right\}

and let
\mathfrak{W}\left(X\right)
denote the affine scheme
\mathrm{Spec}\left(\Bbbk\left[\mathcal{W}\right]^{\mathcal{G}\left(X\right)}\right)
.

Lemma 10
.

Let
X=\left(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{W},\boldsymbol{\underline{r}}\right)
be an algebraic
network, then
\mathcal{W}^{\circ}
is invariant under the action of
\mathcal{G}\left(X\right)
.

Proof.

Let
g\cdot w_{i}=P_{i}D_{i}w_{i}D_{i-1}^{-r_{i-1}}P_{i-1}^{-1}

be the action of
g=\left(D,P\right)\in\mathcal{G}\left(X\right)
,
then
P_{i-1}^{-1}
exchanges the order of the columns and
D_{i-1}^{-r_{i-1}}

is the product by a diagonal matrix hence they do not generate zero
columns, on the left the product
P_{i}D_{i}
acts on the lines by
permuting them and multiplying them by elements of the torus. This
shows that
\mathcal{G}\left(X\right)
preserves
\mathcal{W}^{\circ}
.
∎

Theorem 11
.

The group
\mathcal{G}\left(X\right)

is linearly reductive, in particular
\mathfrak{W}\left(X\right)
has a natural
structure of affine algebraic variety over
\mathbb{C}
such that the map

\pi_{X}:\mathcal{W}\to\mathfrak{W}\left(X\right)
is algebraic, dominant and for every
morphism
\xi:\mathcal{W}\to Y
of algebraic varieties over
\mathbb{C}
which is
constant on the orbits of the action of
\mathcal{G}\left(X\right)

there exists a unique morphism
\xi^{\prime}:\mathfrak{W}\left(X\right)\to Y
such
that

\xi=\xi^{\prime}\circ\pi_{X}.

Moreover, if
U\subseteq\mathcal{W}
is an open subset that is invariant under the
action of
\mathcal{G}\left(X\right)
and over which the action is
free, then the image of
U
inside
\mathfrak{W}\left(X\right)
is smooth.

Proof.

While these facts are standard, we sketch the proof referring to to

[
10
]
for the details: in
[
10
, Chapter 1, Section 1, Definition 1.4]

says that an algebraic group is linearly reductive if for every finite
dimensional representation
A
with invariant subspace
A^{\prime}\subseteq A

there exists an invariant complement
A=A^{\prime}\oplus A"
. For the torus

\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\alpha}
this is clear since it is diagonalizable,
hence every representations decomposes into weight spaces

V=\bigoplus_{\chi\in X^{\ast}\left(\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\alpha}\right)}V_{\chi}

hence every
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\alpha}
-subrepresentation is a sum of
weight-space pieces and one can choose a
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\alpha}
-invariant
complement. Therefore
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\alpha}
is linearly reductive.
In view of Maschke’s theorem, the finite group
S_{\alpha}
is linearly
reductive in characteristic zero. Finally, the semidirect product

\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\alpha}\rtimes S_{\alpha}
is linearly reductive because
it is an extension of a linearly reductive group by a linearly reductive
group. Concerning
\mathcal{G}\left(X\right)
we see that it is a
closed subgroup of
\mathcal{G}\left(\widehat{X}\right)
since it
is a stabilizer of a subvariety under an algebraic action and since

\mathcal{G}\left(X\right)\subseteq\prod_{i=1}^{L-1}\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\alpha_{i}}\rtimes S_{\alpha_{i}}=T\rtimes\Gamma

then
\mathcal{G}\left(X\right)
has identity component contained
in the torus
T
. Hence
\mathcal{G}\left(X\right)^{{{}^{\circ}}}
is
a subtorus of
T
, therefore diagonalizable, hence linearly reductive.
The quotient
\mathcal{G}\left(X\right)/\mathcal{G}\left(X\right)^{{{}^{\circ}}}

is finite and embeds into a subgroup of
\Gamma
. Again since we
work in characteristic 0 then this finite quotient is linearly reductive
by Maschke’s theorem. Since linear reductivity is preserved under
extensions,
\mathcal{G}\left(X\right)
is linearly reductive. Finally
the quotient exists in following the discussion in
[
10
, Chapter 1, Section 2]
,
when it is shown that

\mathrm{Spec}\left(\Bbbk\left[\mathcal{W}\right]^{\mathcal{G}\left(X\right)}\right)

is an affine algebraic scheme that has the required universal property.
∎

Definition 12
.

Let
X
be an algebraic network with
parametrization
\phi_{X}:\mathfrak{W}\left(X\right)\to\mathcal{F}\left(X\right)
,
then we say that it is

•

identifiable if
\phi_{X}
is an isomorphism onto its image;

•

finitely identifiable if
\phi_{X}
is finite.

We say that the above property holds generically (i.e. generically
identifiable and generically finitely identifiable) if the defining
property holds for the generic fibre.

Remark 13
.

Note that Definition
12
in essence states
that an algebraic network
X
is generically finitely identifiable
if and only if for the generic
x\in\mathcal{F}\left(X\right)
the fibre
\phi_{X}^{-1}\left(x\right)

is 0-dimensional, in particular
\dim\mathfrak{W}\left(X\right)=\dim\mathcal{F}\left(X\right)
.

Lemma 14
.

Let
X
be an algebraic network and

Y\subseteq X
a subnetwork such that there exists a closed embedding
j:\mathfrak{W}\left(Y\right)\to\mathfrak{W}\left(X\right)

that makes the diagram

\textstyle{\mathfrak{W}\left(Y\right)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{j}
\scriptstyle{\phi_{Y}}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{W}\left(X\right)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{\mathcal{F}\left(Y\right)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

In view of our assumptions the fibres of

are contained
in the fibres of

and one of the fibres of

is 0-dimensional. It follows that the general fibre of
\phi_{Y}

is 0-dimensional and

is generically finitely identifiable. Assume
now that
\phi_{X}
is a finite morphism, then the composition

.
∎

Proposition 15
.

Let
Y\subseteq X
be a subnetwork such that

\mathcal{G}\left(Y\right)=\mathcal{G}\left(X\right)
. Then there
exists a closed embedding
j:\mathfrak{W}\left(Y\right)\to\mathfrak{W}\left(X\right)

that makes the diagram

\textstyle{\mathfrak{W}\left(Y\right)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{j}
\scriptstyle{\phi_{Y}}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{W}\left(X\right)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\phi_{X}}
\textstyle{\mathcal{F}\left(Y\right)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathcal{F}\left(X\right)}

commutative. In particular

(1)

If
X
is finitely identifiable, then also
Y
is finitely identifiable;

(2)

if
X
is generically finitely identifiable and
j\left(\mathfrak{W}\left(Y\right)\right)

In view of Theorem

the group
\mathcal{G}\left(X\right)

-invariants
is exact, in particular the map

∎

Lemma 16
.

Let
X
be a fully connected generically
finitely identifiable network, then
X
is reduced. Moreover if
X

is also finitely identifiable, then every
X^{\underline{\boldsymbol{d}}}
is finitely identifiable.

Proof.

Let
X
be fully connected, then
\mathcal{G}\left(X^{\underline{\boldsymbol{d}}}\right)=\mathcal{G}\left(X\right)

since
\mathcal{G}\left(X\right)
is made of invertible matrices
hence they preserve the rank. Suppose that there exists a proper
\underline{\boldsymbol{d}}

such that
\mathcal{F}\left(X^{\underline{\boldsymbol{d}}}\right)=\mathcal{F}\left(X\right)
. Clearly
d_{i}\neq 0

for every
i
since a fully connected network with positive-dimensional
layers realizes non-zero functions. For a general parameter in
\mathcal{W}
,
all matrices have all entries that are non-zero and the stabilizer
under
\mathcal{G}\left(X\right)
is finite hence 0-dimensional.
Hence
\dim\mathfrak{W}\left(X\right)=\dim\mathcal{W}-\dim\mathcal{G}\left(X\right)
.
Similarly, since
d_{i}\geq 1
for every
i
, a general point of each
determinantal variety

\left\{A\in\mathrm{Hom}\left(\mathcal{N}_{i-1},\mathcal{N}_{i}\right)\mid\mathrm{rk}\left(A\right)\leq d_{i}\right\}

has rank
d_{i}
and all entries non-zero. Therefore a general point
of
\mathcal{W}^{\underline{\boldsymbol{d}}}
also has finite stabilizer under
\mathcal{G}\left(X\right)
,
and hence
\dim\mathfrak{W}\left(X^{\underline{\boldsymbol{d}}}\right)=\dim\mathcal{W}^{\underline{\boldsymbol{d}}}-\dim\mathcal{G}\left(X\right)
.
Since
\underline{\boldsymbol{d}}
is proper and
\mathcal{W}^{\underline{\boldsymbol{d}}}
is a proper product of determinantal
varieties we have
\dim\mathcal{W}^{\underline{\boldsymbol{d}}}<\dim\mathcal{W}
, hence and
\dim\mathfrak{W}\left(X^{\underline{\boldsymbol{d}}}\right)<\dim\mathfrak{W}\left(X\right)
.
Since the network
X
is generically finitely identifiable we have
that

\dim\mathcal{F}\left(X^{\underline{\boldsymbol{d}}}\right)\leq\dim\mathfrak{W}\left(X^{\underline{\boldsymbol{d}}}\right)<\dim\mathfrak{W}\left(X\right)=\dim\mathcal{F}\left(X\right)

but this contradicts the assumption that
\dim\mathcal{F}\left(X^{\underline{\boldsymbol{d}}}\right)=\dim\mathcal{F}\left(X\right)

and
X
is reduced. To prove the last statement suppose that
X

is a fully connected finitely identifiable network, then from
\mathcal{G}\left(X^{\underline{\boldsymbol{d}}}\right)=\mathcal{G}\left(X\right)

it follows that the subnetworks
X^{\underline{\boldsymbol{d}}}
satisfy the assumptions
of Proposition
15
.
∎

5.
Regularity outside the architectural degeneracy locus

5.1.
Good parameters

For an algebraic network
X=\left(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{W},\boldsymbol{\underline{r}}\right)
denote with
\mathfrak{W}^{\circ}=\mathfrak{W}^{\circ}\left(X\right)

the quotient of
\mathcal{W}^{\circ}
under the action of
\mathcal{G}\left(X\right)
:
it is well defined in view of Lemma
10
and
Theorem
11
, moreover we have inclusions
as open subsets
\mathfrak{W}^{\circ}\subseteq\mathfrak{W}
. Let us restrict now to fully connected
networks
X
whose widths are non-increasing, that is

\dim X_{\mathrm{in}}\geq\dim\mathcal{N}_{1}\geq\dots\geq\dim\mathcal{N}_{L}\geq\dim X_{\mathrm{out}}.

The importance of this conditions is that

Proposition 17
.

Let
X
be a fully
connected network with non-increasing widths, then every parameter
in
\mathcal{W}^{\circ}
has trivial stabilizer under the action of
\mathcal{G}\left(X\right)
,
in particular
\mathfrak{W}^{\circ}\left(X\right)
is a smooth affine variety.

Proof.

Let
w=\left(w_{1},\dots,w_{L}\right)\in\mathcal{W}^{\circ}
and
g=\left(g_{1},\dots,g_{L}\right)

be such that
g\cdot w=w
. First note that the
w_{i}
’s are all
surjective, hence they have a right inverse and from
g_{1}\cdot w_{1}=w_{1}

we conclude that
g_{1}=1
. Inductively from
g_{i}w_{i}g_{i-1}^{-r_{i-1}}=w_{i}

we conclude that
g=1
. We conclude in view of Theorem
11
.
∎

Definition 18
.

Let
X
be a fully connected algebraic
network and let
\mathfrak{g}\left(X\right)=\mathrm{Lie}\mathcal{G}\left(X\right)
,
we say that a parameter
w\in\mathcal{W}
is good if

\ker d\Phi_{X,w}=\mathfrak{g}\left(X\right)\cdot w.

Remark 19
.

The idea behind Definition
18
is that, considering
the morphism from the quotient stack
\bar{\Phi}_{X}:\left[\mathcal{W}/\mathcal{G}\left(X\right)\right]\to\mathcal{F}\left(X\right)

and the corresponding morphism of tangent complexes at the point
\left[w\right]

\left[\mathfrak{g}\left(X\right)\cdot w\to T_{\mathcal{W},w}\right]\to\left[0\to T_{\Phi_{X}\left(w\right)}\mathcal{F}\left(X\right)\right]

then the condition of Definition
18
is equivalent
to the injectivity of the
H^{0}
map

d\bar{\Phi}_{X,\left[w\right]}:\frac{T_{\mathcal{W},w}}{\mathfrak{g}\left(X\right)\cdot w}\to T_{\Phi_{X}\left(w\right)}\mathcal{F}\left(X\right)

is injective.

Lemma 20
.

The set of good points is open in
\mathcal{W}

and hence also in
\mathfrak{W}\left(X\right)
.

Proof.

Let
N=\dim\mathcal{W}
and for
w\in\mathcal{W}
denote with
\rho_{w}:\mathfrak{g}\left(X\right)\to T_{X,w}

the action of the Lie algebra on the tangent space, then a parameter
is good if and only if
\mathrm{rk}\left(\rho_{w}\right)+\mathrm{rk}\left(d\Phi_{X,w}\right)=N

and this is equivalent to
\mathrm{rk}\left(\rho_{w}\right)+\mathrm{rk}\left(d\Phi_{X,w}\right)\geq N

hence the set of good parameters can be written as the union

\bigcup_{a+b\geq N}\left\{w\in\mathcal{W}\,|\,\mathrm{rk}\left(\rho_{w}\right)\geq a,\,\,\mathrm{rk}\left(d\Phi_{X,w}\right)\geq b\right\}

which is Zariski open.
∎

Proposition 21
.

Let
X
be fully connected, then
X

is generically finitely identifiable if and only if there exists a
good parameter
w\in\mathcal{W}
. In particular if there exists a good parameter,
then
X
is reduced.

Proof.

In view of Lemma
20
for the general parameter

w
we have
\mathrm{rk}\left(d\Phi_{X,w}\right)=\dim\mathcal{W}-\dim\left[\mathfrak{g}\left(X\right)\cdot w\right]
,
in particular
\dim\mathcal{F}\left(X\right)=\dim\mathcal{W}-\dim\left[\mathcal{G}\left(X\right)\cdot w\right]

and we conclude that

\dim\mathfrak{W}\left(X\right)=\dim\mathcal{F}\left(X\right)

but since the map
\mathfrak{W}\left(X\right)\to\mathcal{F}\left(X\right)
is dominant
we conclude that
X
is generically finitely identifiable. Suppose
now that that
\phi_{X}:\mathfrak{W}\left(X\right)\to\mathcal{F}\left(X\right)
is
generically finite. Since we are in characteristic 0 then it is generically
unramified hence the general parameter is good. The last statement
follows from Lemma
16
.
∎

Proposition 22
.

Let
w\in\mathcal{W}^{\circ}
and suppose that the map

\phi_{X}:\mathfrak{W}\left(X\right)\to\mathcal{F}\left(X\right)
induces an isomorphism
between the completed local rings

\widehat{\phi_{X,w}}:\widehat{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{F}\left(X\right),\Phi_{X}\left(w\right)}}\to\widehat{\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{W}\left(X\right),\left[w\right]}}

then
w
is good. Moreover, given
U\subseteq\mathcal{F}\left(X\right)
an open
subset contained in
\Phi_{X}\left(\mathcal{W}^{\circ}\right)\backslash\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arch}}\left(X\right)

such that
\widehat{\phi_{X,w}}
is an isomorphism for every parameter
over
U
, then

\phi_{X|\phi_{X}^{-1}\left(U\right)}:\phi_{X}^{-1}\left(U\right)\to U

is étale.

Proof.

Let
\pi:\mathcal{W}\to\mathfrak{W}
be the quotient,
p=\Phi_{X}\left(w\right)
and

q=\pi\left(w\right)\in\mathfrak{W}\left(X\right)
, then the local map
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{F}\left(X\right),p}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{W}\left(X\right),q}

induces an isomorphism on the completion, hence, dualizing the isomorphism

\frac{\mathfrak{m}_{p}}{\mathfrak{m}_{p}^{2}}\to\frac{\mathfrak{m}_{q}}{\mathfrak{m}_{q}^{2}}

we deduce that
d\phi_{X,q}:T_{\mathfrak{W}\left(X\right),q}\to T_{\mathcal{F}\left(X\right)_{p}}

is an isomorphism, in particular it is injective. It follows from
Proposition
17
that
the action of
\mathcal{G}\left(X\right)
is free at
w
and
\ker\left(d\pi_{w}\right)=\mathfrak{g}\left(X\right)\cdot w
.
In view of the injectivity of

, from

we deduce that
\ker\left(d\Phi_{X,w}\right)=\mathfrak{g}\left(X\right)\cdot w
.
The last statement follows from the fact that in characteristic 0
for schemes of finite type, formal étaleness implies local étalness.
∎

is the open subset
of
a
-uples
\left(\left[\ell_{1}\right],\dots,\left[\ell_{a}\right]\right)

that span
\mathbb{P}\left(E\right)
. Moreover

is the variety of decompositions of the sums
\lambda_{1}\ell_{1}^{r}+\dots+\lambda_{a}\ell_{a}^{r}

with
\lambda_{i}\neq 0
up to permutation and up to the rescaling.
Thus, when
\dim B=1
, regularity of a triple at a point is the local
version of Waring identifiability: near the chosen point, the affine
parametrization induces an isomorphism on completed local rings.

Lemma 29

Let

Let

Proof.

We argue by induction on

, viewed as linear forms
on

are linearly independent linear forms. After a linear change of coordinates
on

coordinate

algebraically independent. For

We see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}\left[T_{1},\dots,T_{m_{t-1}}\right]

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{C}\left[T_{1},\dots,T_{m_{t-1}}\right]

\displaystyle T_{i}

\displaystyle\mapsto T_{i}^{r_{t-1}}

is injective and the algebraic independence of the
q_{i}^{\left(t-1\right)}

identifies

and since the widths are non-increasing,

and since

are part
of a linear coordinate system on the polynomial algebra

and hence are algebraically independent.
∎

Remark 31
.

We recall that, given a variety
X
over
\mathbb{C}
and a closed point

, the functor of formal deformations is the functor

\mathrm{Def}_{X,x}:\mathrm{Art}_{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathrm{Sets}

defined over the local artinian
\mathbb{C}
-algebras with residue field

as

\mathrm{Def}_{X,x}\left(A\right)=\left\{\tilde{x}:\mathrm{Spec}A\to X\,|\,\tilde{x}\left(\mathfrak{m}_{A}\right)=x\right\}.

This functor can be represented using local ring homomorphisms

Lemma 32

Let

and suppose

Lemma 33

Let

Denote with

and
v=\left(w_{t+2},\dots,w_{L}\right)\in V

\rho_{t}\left(w\right)=\left(q^{\left(t\right)},w_{t+1},v\right)\in Z_{t}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\rho_{t+1}\left(w\right)=\left(q^{\left(t+1\right)},v\right)\in Z_{t+1}.

First note that, since

and the widths are non-increasing,
the stabilizers of
\rho_{t}\left(w\right)
and
\rho_{t+1}\left(w\right)

\mathcal{G}_{t}\left(X\right)

\mathcal{G}_{t+1}\left(X\right)

are trivial. Indeed, if an element of
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{m_{t}}\rtimes S_{m_{t}}

the identity. Then, using recursively that the matrices

Z_{t}
and
Z_{t+1}

by invariant open neighborhoods of

and
\rho_{t+1}\left(w\right)

the quotients

we want to see that there exists a functorial isomorphism

\mathrm{Def}_{\mathcal{P}_{t},\mathcal{P}_{t}\left(w\right)}\simeq\mathrm{Def}_{\mathcal{P}_{t+1},\mathcal{P}_{t+1}\left(w\right)}

\mathbb{C}
-algebra

\mathbb{C}

Fix a representative

of
\mathcal{P}_{t}\left(w\right)

By definition we have

\mathrm{Def}_{\mathcal{P}_{t}\left(w\right)}\left(A\right)=\left\{\tilde{\gamma}\in\mathcal{P}_{t}\left(A\right)\,|\,\tilde{\gamma}\mod{\mathfrak{m}_{A}=\mathcal{P}_{t}\left(w\right)}\right\}

\mathbb{C}
