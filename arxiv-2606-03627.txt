On the prime field spherical restriction conjecture in four dimensions: breaking the Stein–Tomas exponent and applications
Thang Pham, Boqing Xue

Abstract
We introduce a method based on horizontal slicing and a plane-then-line stopping-time decomposition for the prime field spherical restriction problem in four dimensions. The method is designed to overcome the Kloosterman obstruction in the spherical Bochner–Riesz kernel by decomposing each critical horizontal slice into rich-plane, rich-line-and-poor-plane, and poor-line-and-poor-plane components, which are then treated by distinct affine-geometric mechanisms. As a quantitative consequence of this structural method, we prove that R S j ∗ ​ ( 2 → r ) ≲ 1 R_{S_{j}}^{*}(2\to r)\lesssim 1 for every nonzero sphere S j ⊂ 𝔽 4 S_{j}\subset\mathbb{F}^{4} and every r > 23 / 7 r>23/7 . As an application, we obtain the first improvement over the twenty-year-old ( d + 1 ) / 2 (d+1)/2 threshold in the four-dimensional prime field Erdős–Falconer distance problem.

Let

Among the varieties studied in the literature, the spherical case stands out
as both the most geometrically natural and the most analytically challenging. For

By

In a 2008 paper, Iosevich and Koh

R_{S_{j}}^{*}(2\to r)\lesssim 1\iff r\geq\frac{2n+4}{n}\quad\mbox{for even dimensions }n\geq 2.

(3)

In the same paper, Iosevich and Koh established the conjecture in two dimensions
via a direct geometric argument, but this approach does not extend to higher even
dimensions. For
n\geq 4
, the conjecture remains one of the central open problems
in finite field harmonic analysis. The difficulty of the conjecture can be understood
from the following analytic obstruction.

For the paraboloid and the cone, the relevant Fourier kernels are governed by explicit quadratic Gauss sums. This algebraic structure exposes precise geometric estimates, which can then be used effectively to establish restriction and extension bounds. In contrast, for nonzero spheres, the corresponding Bochner–Riesz kernel is governed instead by Kloosterman-type sums. Although Weil bounds give square-root cancellation, there is no Gauss-sum-type closed form which exposes the same planar geometry. Thus, the difficulty is mainly structural. We refer to this as the Kloosterman obstruction.

This obstruction makes it far from clear what the correct general mechanism
for attacking Conjecture (
3

that one can obtain evidence for the conjecture only after imposing additional
structure on the test functions: Kang and Koh proved the conjectured estimates
for certain restricted classes of test functions, including

-coordinate
functions and homogeneous functions of degree zero
[

Kang and Koh introduced an

[

Yang in their study of the mapping properties of the

-operator

[

currently available methods, and suggest that new geometric-analytic ideas are
needed to treat arbitrary test functions in higher even dimensions.

The main contribution of this paper is a new structural framework for breaking the Stein–Tomas barrier for nonzero spheres. The obstruction is not merely that the known exponent is difficult to improve; rather, the classical Stein–Tomas argument treats a set
E\subset\mathbb{F}^{4}
globally and allows several worst-case estimates to be saturated simultaneously. Our method prevents this simultaneous saturation.

The first step is a horizontal
3+1
slicing of physical space. If
E
is supported on few horizontal slices, the horizontal half-moment already gives a gain over the Cauchy–Schwarz bound. The difficult case is the many-slices case, where the active slices have critical three-dimensional size, with central scale
|E_{z}|\approx p^{3/2}
. In that case, the problem becomes local: for each slice
A\subset\mathbb{F}^{3}
, one must improve the diagonal
L^{4}
estimate for

(\mathbf{1}_{A}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{z\}})*\widetilde{K}.

The decisive step is a plane-then-line stopping-time decomposition

A=A_{1}\sqcup A_{2}\sqcup A_{3}.

Here,
A_{1}
is the rich-line-and-poor-plane piece,
A_{2}
is the rich-plane piece, and
A_{3}
is the poor-line-and-poor-plane residual. These are precisely the three affine ways in which a critical slice can concentrate and obstruct an
affine-energy saving
. The proof treats them by three different mechanisms: mean-zero and reflection analysis for line packets, direct planar packet estimates, and affine-energy incidence estimates for the remaining part. After optimizing the rich-poor thresholds, this local structural analysis gives a power saving over the diagonal Stein–Tomas bound.

Thus, the exponent
23/7
should be viewed as a quantitative certificate of the method rather than as the sole novelty of the paper. The main novelty is the replacement of a global worst-case estimate by a slice-by-slice affine stopping-time analysis. This provides a concrete route toward stronger even-dimensional spherical restriction estimates, although substantial new input would be needed beyond the present four dimensional prime field setting.

1.2
Overview of the proof: breaking the Stein–Tomas barrier

We now explain how the method breaks the Stein–Tomas barrier. The classical Stein–Tomas argument gives, for indicator functions,

\|\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{E}}\|_{L^{2}(S_{j},d\sigma)}\lesssim|E|^{1/2}+p^{-1/8}|E|^{3/4}.

At the critical scale
|E|\approx p^{5/2}
, the second term is
|E|^{7/10}
. This estimate is obtained by combining several inequalities, each of which is sharp in some model configuration. The key point of the proof is that a near-critical set cannot saturate all of these inequalities simultaneously, and the role of the method is to make this incompatibility quantitative.

The first dichotomy is horizontal. If
E
is supported on few horizontal slices, the half-moment
\sum_{z}|E_{z}|^{1/2}
already improves on Cauchy–Schwarz. If
E
is supported on many slices, this horizontal gain can disappear; then the active slices lie in the critical three-dimensional range, with central scale
|E_{z}|\approx p^{3/2}
. The problem is therefore reduced to a local diagonal
L^{4}
-saving for a single slice. This is where the plane-then-line stopping-time decomposition enters.

Horizontal slicing and the first source of gain.

The slicing reduction is partly motivated by Lewko’s bilinear approach to the finite field paraboloid problem
[
25
]
, but the spherical setting forces a different architecture. In the paraboloid case, the explicit Gauss-sum structure converts separated interactions into planar bilinear geometry. For nonzero spheres, the Kloosterman-type Bochner–Riesz kernel does not expose an analogous geometry. A direct
2+2
bilinear decomposition of
\mathbb{F}^{4}
therefore does not reveal the saving needed here. The effective replacement is the
3+1
horizontal splitting, which separates the global restriction estimate into a horizontal distribution problem and a local affine-structure problem inside
\mathbb{F}^{3}
slices.

We therefore write points of
\mathbb{F}^{4}
as
(u,z)
, with
u\in\mathbb{F}^{3}
,
and decompose

E=\bigsqcup_{z\in\mathbb{F}}(E_{z}\times\{z\}),\qquad E_{z}:=\{u\in\mathbb{F}^{3}:(u,z)\in E\}.

Let

F_{z}:=(\mathbf{1}_{E_{z}}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{z\}})*\widetilde{K},

where
\widetilde{K}
is the unnormalized Bochner–Riesz kernel
associated with
S_{j}
. The restriction norm is reduced to diagonal

L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})
norms of the individual
F_{z}
’s and off-diagonal

L^{2}
-interactions between
F_{z}
and
F_{z^{\prime}}
. In particular, one
obtains

\|\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{E}}\|_{L^{2}(S_{j},d\sigma)}\lesssim|E|^{1/2}+|E|^{3/8}\left(\sum_{z\in\mathbb{F}}\|F_{z}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}\right)^{1/2},

and, by using only the diagonal estimate and the off-diagonal interaction
estimate,

\|\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{E}}\|_{L^{2}(S_{j},d\sigma)}\lesssim|E|^{1/2}+p^{-1/4}|E|^{5/8}\left(\sum_{z\in\mathbb{F}}|E_{z}|^{1/2}\right)^{1/4}.

The classical Stein–Tomas bound is recovered from the trivial
Cauchy–Schwarz estimate

\sum_{z\in\mathbb{F}}|E_{z}|^{1/2}\leq p^{1/2}|E|^{1/2}.

Thus, the first possible gain is purely horizontal. If
E
is supported
on
m
active horizontal levels, then

\sum_{z}|E_{z}|^{1/2}\leq m^{1/2}|E|^{1/2}.

Whenever
m\leq p^{1-\gamma}
, this improves the Cauchy–Schwarz bound
by a power of
p
, and the horizontal-slice estimate converts that
saving directly into a saving over the Stein–Tomas exponent. This is
the few-slices case.

The many-slices case and the local diagonal problem.

The difficult case is the complementary situation in which the number of active
slices is close to
p
. Then the half-moment

\sum_{z}|E_{z}|^{1/2}
may be essentially as large as Cauchy–Schwarz permits,
so the preceding horizontal gain disappears. In the near-critical regime,
the dyadic-regular decomposition then leads to slices in the three-dimensional
critical range, with central scale

|E_{z}|\approx p^{3/2}.

Thus, the problem becomes local: for a single slice
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{3}
and
a height
z\in\mathbb{F}
, one must improve the diagonal fourth-moment quantity
\|(\mathbf{1}_{A}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{z\}})*\widetilde{K}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}
. The unconditional diagonal estimate gives

\left\|(\mathbf{1}_{A}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{z\}})*\widetilde{K}\right\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}\lesssim p^{-1/2}|A|^{3/4}.

At
|A|\approx p^{3/2}
, this is the local form of the
Stein–Tomas diagonal bound. The decisive step is to prove a power
improvement for this fourth moment, uniformly for slices in the relevant
critical window.

More concretely, after optimizing the structural estimates below, the proof
establishes

\big\|(\mathbf{1}_{A}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{z\}})*\widetilde{K}\big\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}\lesssim p^{-11/10}|A|^{11/10}+p^{-9/20}|A|^{7/10},\qquad p^{41/28}\leq|A|\leq p^{5/3}.

At the central scale
|A|\approx p^{3/2}
, the unconditional diagonal estimate gives
O(p^{5/8})
, whereas the optimized structural estimate gives
O(p^{3/5})
. This is the
new local power saving which replaces the missing horizontal saving in the
many-slices case.

An affine structure enters the proof.

A direct fourth-moment expansion gives, for
B\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{3}
,

\big\|(\mathbf{1}_{B}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{0\}})*\widetilde{K}\big\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{4}\lesssim p^{-4}\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{aff}}(B),

where
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{aff}}(B)
counts coincidences of weighted affine
sums

\rho x+\sigma y=\rho^{\prime}z+\sigma^{\prime}w,\qquad\rho+\sigma=\rho^{\prime}+\sigma^{\prime},\qquad\rho,\sigma,\rho^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}^{\times},\qquad x,y,z,w\in B.

Moreover,

\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{aff}}(B)\lesssim pM_{4}(B),\qquad M_{4}(B):=\sum_{\pi}|B\cap\pi|^{4}.

This affine-energy mechanism is effective for sets with no large affine
concentration. It is not effective for an arbitrary slice
A
, because large
affine energy may be caused by genuine structure: rich affine planes and rich
affine lines. These pieces are not errors to be discarded; they are the
possible extremal obstructions and must be estimated directly.

For this reason the proof uses a deliberately ordered plane-then-line stopping-time decomposition

A=A_{1}\sqcup A_{2}\sqcup A_{3},

as mentioned. We first remove rich affine planes, producing the rich-plane piece. Only after those planes have been removed do we remove rich affine lines from the remaining plane-poor set, producing the rich-line-and-poor-plane piece. The remaining piece is poor with respect to both affine lines and affine planes. This order is essential: the rich-line branch must retain a poor-plane property, while the remaining branch must be poor in both affine dimensions so that affine-energy and incidence estimates become effective. Thus, the decomposition is not merely a size decomposition; it separates the possible affine obstructions in a way compatible with the later analytic estimates. Each of the three branches then gives a power saving after the rich-poor thresholds
K_{\Pi}
and
K_{L}
are optimized.

Throughout this paper, let
K_{\Pi}
and
K_{L}
be two threshold parameters satisfying

1\leq K_{\Pi}\leq p^{2},\qquad 1\leq K_{L}\leq p,\qquad K_{L}\leq K_{\Pi}.

(4)

The poor-line-and-poor-plane branch

The piece
A_{3}
satisfies

\max_{\ell}|A_{3}\cap\ell|<K_{L},\qquad\max_{\pi}|A_{3}\cap\pi|<K_{\Pi}.

This is the part for which the affine-energy method is genuinely useful.
The fourth-moment expansion and the bound

\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{aff}}(A_{3})\lesssim pM_{4}(A_{3})
reduce the problem to
estimating the fourth plane moment
M_{4}(A_{3})
. Since all rich lines and rich
planes have been removed, incidence geometry gives a saving for this moment:
Vinh’s point–plane estimate handles the low and middle richness ranges,
Rudnev’s point–plane incidence theorem handles the high richness range, and a
packing argument controls the very rich planes. The resulting estimate is

\displaystyle\big\|(\mathbf{1}_{A_{3}}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{z\}})*\widetilde{K}\big\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{4}\lesssim{}

\displaystyle\frac{|A|^{4}}{p^{4}}+\frac{|A|^{2}}{p}+\frac{|A|K_{L}^{2}}{p}+\frac{|A|^{3}K_{L}}{p^{3}}+\frac{|A|K_{\Pi}^{3}}{p^{3}}.

Thus, the remaining part is controlled because the absence of rich affine
substructures forces its affine energy to be smaller than the worst-case
Stein–Tomas energy.

The rich-plane branch.

The rich-plane piece is written as a disjoint union of planar packets

A_{2}=\bigsqcup_{i\in I_{2}}A_{2,i},\qquad A_{2,i}\subseteq\pi_{i},\qquad|A_{2,i}|\geq K_{\Pi},\qquad|I_{2}|\leq|A|/K_{\Pi}.

This is the part where the affine-energy method is not the right point of
view: a set concentrated on a plane can have large affine energy simply because
many affine relations already live inside that plane. We therefore estimate
each planar packet directly at the operator level.

Let
B\subseteq\pi_{i}
be one such packet. If the normal vector to

\pi_{i}
is anisotropic, then the quadratic form remains nondegenerate on

\pi_{i}
. In this case the packet has genuine two-dimensional quadratic
oscillation, and the desired estimate follows from Gauss-sum analysis together
with Weil bounds for the associated Kloosterman sums:

\big\|(\mathbf{1}_{B}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{z\}})*\widetilde{K}\big\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{4}\lesssim p^{-1}|B|^{2}.

Planes with isotropic normal require a separate argument, because the quadratic
phase degenerates along a null direction. We choose coordinates adapted to
this null direction. In these coordinates, the packet contribution splits
into a rigid geometric part, controlled directly from how the packet is
distributed along the null lines, and an oscillatory part, controlled by
Kloosterman-type cancellation. This recovers the scale-uniform packet bound

\big\|(\mathbf{1}_{B}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{z\}})*\widetilde{K}\big\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{4}\lesssim p^{-1}|B|^{2}+|B|.

Thus, every rich planar packet, including the degenerate isotropic ones,
satisfies a better estimate than the general diagonal bound.

Finally, since each packet has size at least
K_{\Pi}
and the number of
packets is at most
|A|/K_{\Pi}
, Minkowski’s inequality gives

\big\|(\mathbf{1}_{A_{2}}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{z\}})*\widetilde{K}\big\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{4}\lesssim\frac{|A|^{4}}{pK_{\Pi}^{2}}+\frac{|A|^{4}}{K_{\Pi}^{3}}.

The rich-line-and-poor-plane branch.

The rich-line-and-poor-plane piece is the most singular obstruction. Write

A_{1}=\bigsqcup_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}B_{\ell},\qquad B_{\ell}\subseteq\ell,\qquad|B_{\ell}|\geq K_{L},\qquad|\mathcal{L}|\leq|A|/K_{L},

and use the poor-plane condition

\max_{\pi}|A_{1}\cap\pi|<K_{\Pi}.

On each selected line,

\mathbf{1}_{B_{\ell}}=\beta_{\ell}\mathbf{1}_{\ell}+f_{\ell},\qquad\beta_{\ell}:=\frac{|B_{\ell}|}{p},\qquad\sum_{x\in\ell}f_{\ell}(x)=0.

Accordingly,

(\mathbf{1}_{A_{1}}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{0\}})*\widetilde{K}=G_{\rm avg}+G_{\rm bal}.

The full-line average
G_{\rm avg}
is controlled using explicit Gauss-sum
behavior of complete affine lines together with the poor-plane condition. The
balanced part is subtler. The selected lines are split into anisotropic and
isotropic directions according to
Q_{3}(v_{\ell})\neq 0
or
Q_{3}(v_{\ell})=0
.
The
L^{4}
-mass of the balanced contribution is converted into a spherical
variance problem. A rank decomposition of that variance separates the
zero-rank, anisotropic rank-one, and isotropic rank-one contributions. The
mean-zero condition on each
f_{\ell}
eliminates the most dangerous
one-dimensional averages. The remaining correlations are organized by
anisotropic affine reflections and, in the isotropic case, by the planes

\mathcal{H}_{v,\lambda}=\{x:x\cdot v=\lambda\}
. The required estimates then follow from reflection analysis, the poor-plane and rich-line conditions, line-multiplicity bounds, and Weil bounds for Kloosterman and Salié-type sums.

The final rich-line estimate is

\displaystyle\big\|(\mathbf{1}_{A_{1}}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{z\}})*\widetilde{K}\big\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{4}\lesssim{}

\displaystyle\frac{|A|^{4}}{pK_{L}^{4}}+\frac{|A|^{4}}{p^{2}K_{L}^{2}}+\frac{|A|^{2}K_{\Pi}^{2}}{p^{2}K_{L}^{2}}+\frac{|A|^{2}K_{\Pi}}{pK_{L}}+\frac{|A|K_{\Pi}^{3}}{p^{3}}.

Putting the three branches together.

Combining all the estimates from the three branches by the triangle
inequality in
L^{4}
gives a direct slice estimate with two free thresholds

K_{\Pi}
and
K_{L}
. Choosing

K_{L}\approx p^{6/5}|A|^{-1/5},\qquad K_{\Pi}\approx p^{2/5}|A|^{3/5},

balances the three branches and yields

\big\|(\mathbf{1}_{A}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{z\}})*\widetilde{K}\big\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}\lesssim p^{-11/10}|A|^{11/10}+p^{-9/20}|A|^{7/10}

throughout the critical slice range
p^{41/28}\leq|A|\leq p^{5/3}
.

This local diagonal saving is inserted back into the horizontal-slice
reduction. The remaining regular dyadic pieces are handled either by the
global auxiliary bounds, which cover the very small and very large cases, or
by the half-moment estimate, which covers the horizontally sparse cases. For
regular horizontal slices one obtains

\|\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{E}}\|_{L^{2}(S_{j},d\sigma)}\lesssim|E|^{16/23}.

After decomposing an arbitrary
E
into dyadic regular horizontal classes,
this gives

\|\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{E}}\|_{L^{2}(S_{j},d\sigma)}\lesssim(\log p)|E|^{16/23}.

Since

\frac{16}{23}=\frac{7}{10}-\frac{1}{230},

this is a genuine improvement over the Stein–Tomas exponent
7/10
.
The usual epsilon-removal argument then gives the restriction estimate

\|\widehat{f}\|_{L^{2}(S_{j},d\sigma)}\lesssim_{r}\|f\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}\qquad\text{for every }0<r<\frac{23}{16}.

Difficulties over arbitrary finite fields

The prime field assumption is only needed in the many-slices regime, where the
proof seeks a power saving for the diagonal slice quantity with

|A|\approx p^{3/2}
. In the poor-line-and-poor-plane branch, this saving comes
from point–plane incidence geometry, specifically Rudnev’s theorem, whose
positive-characteristic range is controlled by the characteristic. Over

\mathbb{F}_{p}
, the characteristic and the field size are the same, so the critical
slice scale lies within the usable incidence range.

For arbitrary finite fields
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
q=p^{r}
with
r>1
, the incidence threshold
is still governed by
p
, not by
q
. Thus, sets of size
q^{3/2}
may
fall outside the range where the same argument applies. In addition, proper
subfields can create large affine energy without producing rich

\mathbb{F}_{q}
-lines or rich
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-planes at the stopping-time thresholds. The
prime field hypothesis removes these subfield obstructions and lets the
absence of rich affine structure yield a genuine affine-energy saving.

2
Preliminaries and lemmas

2.1
Quadratic and affine geometry

We write

Q_{3}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2},\qquad Q_{4}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})=Q_{3}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})+x_{4}^{2},

with the associated bilinear form being the usual dot product.

A nonzero vector
v
is called isotropic for
Q
if
Q(v)=0
, and
anisotropic if
Q(v)\neq 0
. A quadratic form is called isotropic if it
has a nonzero isotropic vector, and anisotropic otherwise. We use the same
terminology for affine lines and affine planes according to their directions or
normal vectors.

We begin with a basic fact about isotropic directions: two orthogonal isotropic directions in
\mathbb{F}^{3}
must coincide. This fact will be used later in the rich-line analysis.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
u,w\in\mathbb{F}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
satisfy
Q_{3}(u)=Q_{3}(w)=0
. If
u\cdot w=0
, then
w\in\mathbb{F}u
. Consequently, an isotropic line contained in the plane
\{x:u\cdot x=\lambda\}
, with
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}
, must be parallel to
u
.

Proof.

The nondegenerate ternary quadratic space
(\mathbb{F}^{3},Q_{3})
has Witt index one. Equivalently, the radical of
u^{\perp}
is
\mathbb{F}u
. Thus, an isotropic vector
w\in u^{\perp}
must be proportional to
u
; otherwise
\operatorname{span}\{u,w\}
would be a two-dimensional totally isotropic subspace. The consequence follows because a line contained in
\{x:u\cdot x=\lambda\}
has direction in
u^{\perp}
.
∎

For each anisotropic direction
[n]\in\mathbb{P}^{2}(\mathbb{F})
, we always fix a representative
n
. It satisfies
Q_{3}(n)\neq 0
. Define the set
\mathfrak{R}
of anisotropic affine reflections on
\mathbb{F}^{3}
by

\mathfrak{R}=\big\{R_{n,k}:\,[n]\in\mathbb{P}^{2}(\mathbb{F}),k\in\mathbb{F}\big\},

where

R_{n,k}(x)=x+\left(k-\frac{2n\cdot x}{Q_{3}(n)}\right)n,\qquad x\in\mathbb{F}^{3}.

Indeed,
R_{n,k}
is the affine reflection across the plane
n\cdot x=kQ_{3}(n)/2
. We denote its linear part by

R_{n}(x)=x-\frac{2n\cdot x}{Q_{3}(n)}n,\qquad x\in\mathbb{F}^{3}.

For a non-zero
v
, one checks that
Q_{3}(R_{n}(v))=Q_{3}(v)
. So
R_{n,k}
sends an isotropic (or anisotropic) line to an isotropic (or anisotropic) line, respectively.

After fixing the reflection notation, we record elementary counting estimates for how anisotropic
affine reflections act on affine lines. These estimates will be used in the rich-line-and-poor-plane branch.

Lemma 2.2
.

The following counting facts hold, with implied constants being absolute.

(i) If
x\neq y
, then at most one anisotropic affine reflection sends
x
to
y
.

(ii) Let
\ell=b+\mathbb{F}v
and
\ell^{\prime}=c+\mathbb{F}w
be anisotropic affine lines, i.e.,
Q_{3}(v),Q_{3}(w)\neq 0
.

•

If
[v]\neq[w]
, then there are
O(1)
anisotropic affine reflections sending
\ell
to
\ell^{\prime}
.

•

If
[v]=[w]
but
\ell\neq\ell^{\prime}
, there are again
O(1)
such reflections.

•

If
\ell=\ell^{\prime}
, the reflections preserving
\ell
and moving points of
\ell
nontrivially are the reflections whose normal is parallel to
v
, and there are
O(p)
of them.

(iii) Let
\ell=b+\mathbb{F}v
and
\ell^{\prime}=c+\mathbb{F}w
be isotropic affine lines, i.e.,
Q_{3}(v),Q_{3}(w)=0
.

•

If
[v]=[w]
, then any anisotropic reflection sending one to the other preserves
[v]
, has normal in
v^{\perp}
, and the two lines must lie in a same plane
\{x\in\mathbb{F}^{3}:\,x\cdot v=\lambda\}
for some
\lambda
. For a fixed ordered pair of such parallel isotropic lines, there are at most
p
such reflections.

•

If
[v]\neq[w]
, then
v\cdot w\neq 0
, and any anisotropic reflection sending one to the other has normal direction of the form
[v-\mu w]
with some
\mu\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}
.

Proof.

For (i), the equation
R_{n,k}x=y
implies
0\neq y-x\in\mathbb{F}n
. Thus, the normal direction
n
is forced by
x
and
y
; once it is fixed, the scalar
k
is determined by the same equation.

For (ii) and (iii), assume
R_{n,k}\ell=\ell^{\prime}
. Then there is some scalar
\mu\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}
so that

\mu w=R_{n}(v)=v-\frac{2n\cdot v}{Q_{3}(n)}n.

(5)

For (ii), evaluating
Q_{3}
on both sides of (
5
) shows that
\mu^{2}Q_{3}(w)=Q_{3}(v)
, giving at most two choices for
\mu
. For each such
\mu
, one has
v-\mu w=\frac{2n\cdot v}{Q_{3}(n)}n\in\mathbb{F}n
.

In the case
[v]\neq[w]
, the vector
v-\mu w
is non-zero, which determines a unique normal direction
n
of the reflection. With the normal direction fixed, the condition
R_{n,k}\ell=\ell^{\prime}
determines at most one value of
k
, because varying
k
translates the image line in the normal direction, which is not parallel to
\ell^{\prime}
in the distinct-direction case.

In the case
[v]=[w]
, we assume without loss of generality that
v=w
. Now the relation
(1-\mu)v=\frac{2n\cdot v}{Q_{3}(n)}n
shows that, either
n\parallel v
, or
n\cdot v=0
.

When
n\parallel v
, calculation reveals that
R_{n,k}(b+sv)=b+(k-s-\frac{2v\cdot b}{Q_{3}(v)})v
for
s\in\mathbb{F}
. So
\ell=R_{n,k}\ell=\ell^{\prime}
. In such case, we get
p
such reflections as
k
varies in
\mathbb{F}
.

When
n\cdot v=0
, one verifies that
R_{n,k}(b+sv)=R_{n,k}(b)+sv
. The condition
R\ell=\ell^{\prime}
shows that the vector
R_{n,k}(b)-c=b-c+\big(k-\frac{2n\cdot b}{Q_{3}(n)}\big)n
is parallel to
v
. Since
Q_{3}(v)\neq 0
, one has
v^{\perp}\cap\mathbb{F}v=\{0\}
. Now the natural quotient map
\pi:\,\mathbb{F}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}^{3}/(\mathbb{F}v)
, restricted to
v^{\perp}
, is injective. If
\ell\neq\ell^{\prime}
, then
b\neq c
. Let
m
be the unique vector in
v^{\perp}
such that
\pi(m)=\pi(b-c)
. Then
0\neq m=\big(k-\frac{2n\cdot b}{Q_{3}(n)}\big)n
. So the normal direction
n
, if exists, is determined, and then
k
is also determined. If
\ell=\ell^{\prime}
, then we may assume without loss of generality that
b=c
. In view of
n\not\parallel v
, one has
k=\frac{2n\cdot b}{Q_{3}(n)}
. In such case,
F_{n,k}
is the identity map when restricted to
\ell
.

Consider (iii). If
[v]=[w]
, then combining (
5
) and
n\not\parallel v
yields
n\cdot v=0
. , i.e.
n\in v^{\perp}
.
In such situation, the reflection
R_{n,k}
preserves
x\cdot v
. Thus, two parallel isotropic lines can be interchanged only if they lie in the same plane
\mathcal{H}_{v,\lambda}:=\{x\in\mathbb{F}^{3}:\,x\cdot v=\lambda\}
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}
. Once the normal direction is chosen, there is at most one compatible affine parameter, giving at most
O(p)
reflections because the projective line
v^{\perp}
has
O(p)
anisotropic directions.

Finally, if
[v]\neq[w]
, then
R_{n}(v)\neq v
and so
n\cdot v\neq 0
by (
5
). Also, (
5
) shows that
n\in\mathbb{F}(v-\mu w)
. On the other hand, Lemma
2.1
shows that
v\cdot w\neq 0
. Then
Q_{3}(v-\mu w)=-2\mu v\cdot w\neq 0
for any
\mu\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}
. so these are precisely all possible anisotropic normal directions as
\mu
varies.
∎

We next define the affine energy used for the poor-plane-and-poor-line piece and compare it with the fourth plane moment.

For
B\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{3}
, define

\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{aff}}(B):=\#\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}x,y,z,w\in B,\\
\rho,\sigma,\rho^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}\end{array}:\begin{array}[]{c}\rho+\sigma=\rho^{\prime}+\sigma^{\prime},\\
\rho x+\sigma y=\rho^{\prime}z+\sigma^{\prime}w\end{array}\right\}.

The second equation is an equality in
\mathbb{F}^{3}
. The equality

\rho+\sigma=\rho^{\prime}+\sigma^{\prime}
says that the two weighted sums have the
same total affine weight. Also define the fourth plane moment

M_{4}(B):=\sum_{\pi}|B\cap\pi|^{4}.

The following comparison converts affine-energy estimates into plane-moment estimates.

Lemma 2.3
.

For every
B\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{3}
, it satisfies that

\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{aff}}(B)\lesssim pM_{4}(B).

Proof.

For an ordered quadruple
Q=(x,y,z,w)\in B^{4}
, let
N(Q)
be the number of
quadruples
(\lambda,\beta,\lambda^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})\in(\mathbb{F}^{\times})^{4}
satisfying

\lambda+\beta=\lambda^{\prime}+\beta^{\prime},\qquad\lambda x+\beta y=\lambda^{\prime}z+\beta^{\prime}w.

Note that

\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{aff}}(B)=\sum_{Q\in B^{4}}N(Q).

Let
d(Q)
be the affine dimension of the set
\{x,y,z,w\}
. Thus,

d(Q)\in\{0,1,2,3\}
. We claim that

N(Q)=0\quad\text{if }d(Q)=3,\qquad N(Q)\lesssim p^{3-d(Q)}\quad\text{if }d(Q)=0,1,2.

(6)

To see this, rewrite the two defining equations as one homogeneous linear equation in
\mathbb{F}^{4}
:

\lambda(1,x)+\beta(1,y)-\lambda^{\prime}(1,z)-\beta^{\prime}(1,w)=0.

This is a linear system in the four scalar unknowns
(\lambda,\beta,\lambda^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})
. The rank of the lifted vectors
(1,x),(1,y),(1,z),(1,w)
is
d(Q)+1
. Therefore the solution space over all of
\mathbb{F}^{4}
has dimension at most
3-d(Q)
. For
d(Q)=0,1,2
, this gives at most
p^{3-d(Q)}
solutions, and restricting to non-zero coefficients only decreases the count. For
d(Q)=3
, the only solution over
\mathbb{F}
is the zero solution. This is excluded because the coefficients must lie in
\mathbb{F}^{\times}
. Hence
N(Q)=0
in that case, proving (
6
). As a result,

\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{aff}}(B)\lesssim pC_{2}(B)+p^{2}C_{1}(B)+p^{3}C_{0}(B),

where
C_{d}(B)
denotes the number of ordered quadruples in
B^{4}
of affine dimension exactly
d
.

On the other hand,

M_{4}(B)=\sum_{\pi}\sum_{x,y,z,w\in B\cap\pi}1,

so each ordered quadruple
(x,y,z,w)
is counted once for each affine plane containing the set
\{x,y,z,w\}
. No three-dimensional quadruple can lie in an affine plane; a two-dimensional quadruple lies in exactly one affine plane; a one-dimensional quadruple lies on a unique affine line, which is contained in exactly
p+1
affine planes; a zero-dimensional quadruple is a single point, while the number of affine planes through a point equals
p^{2}+p+1
. Therefore

M_{4}(B)=C_{2}(B)+(p+1)C_{1}(B)+(p^{2}+p+1)C_{0}(B).

Now one sees that
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{aff}}(B)\lesssim pM_{4}(B)
.
∎

2.2
Incidence theorems

The incidence estimates in this subsection will be applied to estimate the poor-line-and-poor-plane piece.

For a point set
P\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{3}
and a family
\mathcal{H}
of
affine planes, the point-plane incidence number is defined by

I(P,\mathcal{H}):=\#\{(x,H)\in P\times\mathcal{H}:x\in H\}.

First, Vinh’s point–plane estimate
[
37
, Theorem 4]
over prime fields will be used to control the rich planes.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
P\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{3}
be a point set and let
\mathcal{H}
be a family of
affine planes. Then

\left|I(P,\mathcal{H})-\frac{|P||\mathcal{H}|}{p}\right|\lesssim p\sqrt{|P||\mathcal{H}|}.

In particular, if every plane in
\mathcal{H}
contains at least
K
points
of
P
with
K\geq 2|P|/p
, then

|\mathcal{H}|\lesssim\frac{p^{2}|P|}{K^{2}}.

The next incidence estimate, from de Zeeuw
[
39
, Theorem 1.1]
, which is also a consequence of Rudnev
[
35
, Theorem 3]
, is effective when the point set is
not larger than the plane family and collinear point multiplicity is controlled.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
P\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{3}
be a point set and let
\mathcal{H}
be a family of
affine planes. Suppose
|P|\leq|\mathcal{H}|
and
|P|\lesssim p^{2}
. Assume that at most
k
points of
P
are contained in a common affine line. Then

I(P,\mathcal{H})\lesssim|P|^{1/2}|\mathcal{H}|+k|\mathcal{H}|.

We will also need Rudnev’s affine point–plane bound
[
35
, Theorem 3]
in the dual regime where the point set is larger than the plane family.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
P
be a set of points and
\mathcal{H}
be a set of planes in
\mathbb{F}^{3}
. Suppose that
|P|\geq|\mathcal{H}|
and
|\mathcal{H}|\lesssim p^{2}
. Assume that at most
k
planes in
\mathcal{H}
contain a common affine line. Then

I(P,\mathcal{H})\lesssim|P||\mathcal{H}|^{1/2}+k|P|.

Combining the preceding incidence estimates gives a dyadic bound for planes whose richness exceeds both square-root size and line multiplicity.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
P\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{3}
, and assume
|P|\lesssim p^{2}
. For

K\geq 1
, let

\mathcal{H}_{K}:=\{\pi:\ K\leq|P\cap\pi|<2K\}.

There is an absolute constant
C_{0}\geq 1
such that, whenever

K>C_{0}\bigl(|P|^{1/2}+L(P)\bigr),\qquad L(P):=\max_{\ell\ \mathrm{affine\ line}}|P\cap\ell|

one has

|\mathcal{H}_{K}|\lesssim\frac{|P|^{2}}{K^{2}}.

Proof.

The assertion is trivial if
|\mathcal{H}_{K}|=0
, so assume
|\mathcal{H}_{K}|>0
. We first show that

|\mathcal{H}_{K}|<|P|
. Assume on the contrary that
|\mathcal{H}_{K}|\geq|P|
, then Lemma
2.5
gives

K|\mathcal{H}_{K}|\leq I(P,\mathcal{H}_{K})\lesssim\bigl(|P|^{1/2}+L(P)\bigr)|\mathcal{H}_{K}|,

since every plane in

\mathcal{H}_{K}
is
K
-rich. For
C_{0}
sufficiently large this contradicts

K>C_{0}(|P|^{1/2}+L(P))
.

Now we have

|\mathcal{H}_{K}|<|B|\lesssim p^{2}
. Therefore, by Lemma
2.6
,

K|\mathcal{H}_{K}|\leq I(\mathcal{H}_{K},P)\lesssim|P||\mathcal{H}_{K}|^{1/2}+k|P|,

(7)

where
k
is the maximum number of collinear planes in
\mathcal{H}
. Suppose
k
planes of

\mathcal{H}_{K}
contain a common affine line
\ell
. Let

r:=|P\cap\ell|\leq L(P)
. The parts of these planes away from
\ell
are
pairwise disjoint, so

|P|\geq r+k(K-r).

Since
K>C_{0}(|P|^{1/2}+L(P))
, taking
C_{0}
large gives
K\geq 2L(P)\geq 2r
,
and hence
k\lesssim|P|/K
. Substituting this into (
7
), calculation yields
|\mathcal{H}_{K}|\lesssim|P|^{2}/K^{2}
.
∎

A simple counting bound is useful when the richness is strong relative to the set size and line multiplicity

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
P\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{3}
, and let
\mathcal{H}_{K}
be
as in Lemma
2.7
. There exists an absolute constant
C_{1}\geq 1
such that, if
K^{2}\geq C_{1}|P|L(P)
, then

|\mathcal{H}_{K}|\lesssim\frac{|P|}{K}.

Proof.

For
x\in P
, denote
r(x):=\#\{\pi\in\mathcal{H}_{K}:\ x\in\pi\}
. Combining Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

(K|\mathcal{H}_{K}|)^{2}\leq I(P,\mathcal{H}_{K})^{2}=\Big(\sum_{x\in P}r(x)\Big)^{2}\leq|P|\sum_{x\in P}r(x)^{2}=|P|\sum_{\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}_{K}}|P\cap\pi\cap\pi^{\prime}|.

The diagonal terms of above sum contribute at most
2K|\mathcal{H}_{K}|
. If
\pi\neq\pi^{\prime}
, then

\pi\cap\pi^{\prime}
is either empty or an affine line, and therefore

|P\cap\pi\cap\pi^{\prime}|\leq L(P)
. Hence

K^{2}|\mathcal{H}_{K}|^{2}\lesssim|P|\bigl(K|\mathcal{H}_{K}|+L(P)|\mathcal{H}_{K}|^{2}\bigr).

Now if
K^{2}\geq C_{1}|P|L(P)
for some sufficiently large
C_{1}
, then
K^{2}|\mathcal{H}_{K}|^{2}\lesssim|P|\cdot K|\mathcal{H}_{K}|
and so
|\mathcal{H}_{K}|\lesssim|P|/K
.
∎

2.3
Gauss sums

Recall that
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{p}
with
p
an odd prime. We assume without loss of generality that
\chi
is the non-trivial additive character given by
\chi(y)=e^{2\pi iy/p}

(y\in\mathbb{F})
. Let
\eta
be the Legendre symbol. The classical Gaussian sum is defined as

G_{\eta}=\sum\limits_{x\in\mathbb{F}}\eta(y)\chi(y)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}}\chi(x^{2}),

which satisfies
G_{\eta}^{2}=\eta(-1)p
and
|G_{\eta}|=p^{1/2}
.

For more general Gauss sums, we have the following two elementary identities for one parameter and two parameters, respectively.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
A,B,C\in\mathbb{F}
with
A\neq 0
. Then

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}}\chi(Ax^{2}+Bx+C)=\eta(A)G_{\eta}\,\chi\left(C-\frac{B^{2}}{4A}\right).

Lemma 2.10
.

Let

Q:\mathbb{F}^{2}\to\mathbb{F}
be a nondegenerate homogeneous quadratic form. Write

Q(z)=z^{T}Mz,\qquad z\in\mathbb{F}^{2},

where
M
is an invertible symmetric
2\times 2
matrix over
\mathbb{F}
. Define
the dual quadratic form by

\widetilde{Q}(\xi):=\xi^{T}M^{-1}\xi,\qquad\xi\in\mathbb{F}^{2}.

Then there exists a constant
\gamma_{Q}\in\mathbb{C}
, depending only on
Q
and

\chi
, with
|\gamma_{Q}|=1
, such that for every

\rho\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}
and every
\xi\in\mathbb{F}^{2}
,

\sum_{z\in\mathbb{F}^{2}}\chi\left(-\frac{\rho}{4}Q(z)-z\cdot\xi\right)=\gamma_{Q}\,p\,\chi\left(\frac{\widetilde{Q}(\xi)}{\rho}\right).

We mention that, the general identity as above usually involves a factor
\eta^{d}(-\rho/4)
on the right-hand side when
Q
has
d
variables. For
d=2
, this factor is
1
, and so the quantities outside the additive character
\chi
is independent of the variable
\rho
in the leading coefficient.

The following two lemmas are the classical Weil bound for Kloosterman sums and Salié-type sums. See Weil
[
38
]
and Iwaniec–Kowalski
[
13
, Thm. 11.11]
..

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
A,B\in\mathbb{F}
. If
(A,B)\neq(0,0)
, then

\left|\sum_{\rho\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}}\chi(A\rho+B/\rho)\right|\lesssim p^{1/2}.

If
A=B=0
, then the same sum equals
p-1
.

The same square-root cancellation is needed for the Salié-type variant.

Lemma 2.12
.

For all
A,B\in\mathbb{F}
,

\left|\sum_{\rho\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}}\eta(\rho)\chi(A\rho+B/\rho)\right|\lesssim p^{1/2}.

We also need one exact spectral identity involving Kloosterman kernel in two variables.

Lemma 2.13
.

Assume
j,\lambda\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}
, and set
c=j\lambda/4
. Let

\mathcal{K}(\rho,\omega):=\sum_{v\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}}\chi\!\left(\frac{vj}{2}\rho+\frac{\lambda}{2v}\omega\right),\qquad(\rho,\omega)\in\mathbb{F}^{2}.

For every
g:\mathbb{F}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
,

\sum_{y,y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}^{2}}g(y)\overline{g(y^{\prime})}\mathcal{K}(y-y^{\prime})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{F}\\
\xi\eta=c\end{subarray}}|\widehat{g}(\xi,\eta)|^{2}.

(8)

Proof.

For
y=(\rho,\omega)
, write the dual variable as
(\xi,\eta)
. Directly,

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{K}}(\xi,\eta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\rho,\omega}\sum_{v\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}}\chi\!\left(\frac{j\rho v}{2}+\frac{\omega\lambda}{2v}-\xi\rho-\eta\omega\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}}\Big(\sum_{\rho}\chi\big((jv/2-\xi)\rho\big)\Big)\Big(\sum_{\omega}\chi\big((\lambda/(2v)-\eta)\omega\big)\Big).

The two inner sums are both equal to
p
exactly when

\xi=jv/2
and
\eta=\lambda/(2v)
, and are zero
otherwise. Such a situation occurs for some
v\neq 0
if and only if
\xi\eta=c
. Thus

\widehat{\mathcal{K}}(\xi,\eta)=p^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\{\xi\eta=j\lambda/4\}}=\widehat{\mathcal{K}}(-\xi,-\eta).

(9)

Combining Fourier inversion, we then have

\displaystyle\sum_{y,y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}^{2}}g(y)\overline{g(y^{\prime})}\mathcal{K}(y-y^{\prime})

\displaystyle=p^{-2}\sum_{y,y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}^{2}}g(y)\overline{g(y^{\prime})}\sum\limits_{\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{F}}\widehat{\mathcal{K}}(\xi,\eta)\chi\big((y-y^{\prime})\cdot(\xi,\eta)\big)

\displaystyle=p^{-2}\sum_{\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{F}}\widehat{\mathcal{K}}(\xi,\eta)|\widehat{g}(-\xi,-\eta)|^{2}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{F}\\
\xi\eta=c\end{subarray}}|\widehat{g}(\xi,\eta)|^{2}.

The proof is completed.
∎

2.4
Fourier transforms

We now fix the Fourier normalization and record the kernel estimates for a single horizontal slice.

Throughout,
p
is an odd prime,
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{p}
, and
\chi:\mathbb{F}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
is a fixed nontrivial additive character. For
f:\mathbb{F}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
, we use the unnormalized Fourier transform

\widehat{f}(\xi)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}^{n}}f(x)\chi(-x\cdot\xi),\qquad\xi\in\mathbb{F}^{n},

with inversion formula

f(x)=p^{-n}\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{F}^{n}}\widehat{f}(\xi)\chi(x\cdot\xi).

Fix
j\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}
and let

S_{j}:=\{x\in\mathbb{F}^{4}:\ Q_{4}(x)=j\}.

We write
d\sigma
for the normalized surface measure on
S_{j}
:

d\sigma(\xi):=\frac{1}{|S_{j}|}\mathbf{1}_{S_{j}}(\xi).

Also write

Q_{4}(x):=x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2}+x_{4}^{2}.

With this normalization, the normalized sphere kernel splits into a delta mass and an explicit oscillatory part.

Lemma 2.14
.

One has

|S_{j}|=p^{3}-p.

Moreover,

(d\sigma)^{\vee}(x)=\frac{p^{2}}{p^{2}-1}\delta_{0}(x)+\widetilde{K}(x),

where

\widetilde{K}(x)=\frac{1}{p^{2}-1}\sum_{r\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}}\chi\!\left(-jr-\frac{Q_{4}(x)}{4r}\right).

(10)

Proof.

See Theorem 6 of
[
9
]
for the first formula. Combining Lemma 4 and Remark 2 of
[
12
]
, the second formula follows.
∎

The next lemma identifies the Fourier multiplier of the oscillatory part.

Lemma 2.15
.

For every
\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{4}
,

\widehat{\widetilde{K}}(\omega)=\frac{p^{3}}{p^{2}-1}\mathbf{1}_{S_{j}}(\omega)-\frac{p^{2}}{p^{2}-1}.

Proof.

The conclusion follows directly by taking Fourier transforms in the identity from Lemma
2.14
, noticing that
\widehat{(d\sigma)^{\vee}}(\omega)=p^{4}d\sigma(\omega)
.
∎

The pointwise decay of this oscillatory kernel supplies the basic
L^{\infty}
input.

Lemma 2.16
.

One has

\big\|\widetilde{K}\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}\lesssim p^{-3/2}.

Proof.

In (
10
), we make use of orthogonality of characters when
Q_{4}(x)=0
, and of upper bound for Kloosterman sum by the Weil bound when
Q_{4}(x)\neq 0
. The conclusion then follows.
∎

For
h:\,\mathbb{F}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}
and
z\in\mathbb{F}
, define

(h\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{z\}})(u,t)=h(u)\mathbf{1}_{\{z\}}(t),\qquad(u,t)\in\mathbb{F}^{3}\times\mathbb{F}.

We next record the Fourier transform of a horizontal slice.

Lemma 2.17
.

Let
h:\,\mathbb{F}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
and
z\in\mathbb{F}
. Then

\widehat{\bigl(h\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{z\}}\bigr)}(\xi,s)=\chi(-zs)\widehat{h}(\xi),\qquad(\xi,s)\in\mathbb{F}^{3}\times\mathbb{F}.

Proof.

We have

\widehat{h\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{z\}}}(\xi,s)=\sum\limits_{u\in\mathbb{F}^{3}}\sum\limits_{t\in\mathbb{F}}h(u)\mathbf{1}_{\{z\}}(t)\chi(-u\cdot\xi-ts)=\chi(-zs)\widehat{h}(\xi).

∎

Combining the multiplier formula with Plancherel’s theorem gives the basic
L^{2}
estimate for one slice.

Lemma 2.18
.

For
h:\,\mathbb{F}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}
and
z\in\mathbb{F}
, it satisfies that

\|(h\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{z\}})*\widetilde{K}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{2}\lesssim p\|h\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}^{2}.

Proof.

By Plancherel’s theorem and Lemma
2.17
, one obtains

\displaystyle\|(h\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{z\}})*\widetilde{K}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{2}

\displaystyle=p^{-4}\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{F}^{3}}\sum_{s\in\mathbb{F}}|\widehat{h\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{z\}}}(\xi,s)|^{2}|\widehat{\widetilde{K}}(\xi,s)|^{2}

\displaystyle=p^{-4}\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{F}^{3}}|\widehat{h}(\xi)|^{2}\sum_{s\in\mathbb{F}}|\widehat{\widetilde{K}}(\xi,s)|^{2}.

For fixed
\xi\in\mathbb{F}^{3}
, the equation
Q_{4}(\xi,s)=j
has at most two solutions in
s
. So Lemma
2.15
implies

\sum_{s\in\mathbb{F}}|\widehat{\widetilde{K}}(\xi,s)|^{2}\leq 2\,\Big|\frac{p^{3}}{p^{2}-1}\Big|^{2}+p\,\Big|\frac{p^{2}}{p^{2}-1}\Big|^{2}\lesssim p^{2}.

Substituting this into the Plancherel formula on
\mathbb{F}^{3}
gives

\|(h\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{z\}})*\widetilde{K}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{2}\lesssim p^{-2}\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{F}^{3}}|\widehat{h}(\xi)|^{2}=p\|h\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}^{2}.

The lemma then follows.
∎

For the
L^{4}
estimate, we use a one-dimensional quadratic large-sieve bound.

Lemma 2.19
.

Let
d\geq 1
. Let
Q
be a non-zero quadratic form. Then, for every function

h:\mathbb{F}\to\mathbb{C}
,

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}^{d}}\left|\sum_{\tau\in\mathbb{F}}h(\tau)\chi\left(-\tau Q(x)\right)\right|^{2}\leq 2p^{d}\sum_{\tau\in\mathbb{F}}|h(\tau)|^{2}.

Proof.

For
s\in\mathbb{F}
, the level set
E_{s}:=\{x\in\mathbb{F}^{d}:Q(x)=s\}
satisfies
|E_{s}|\leq 2p^{d-1}
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}^{d}}\Big|\sum_{\tau\in\mathbb{F}}h(\tau)\chi\left(-\tau Q(x)\right)\Big|^{2}=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}^{d}}|\widehat{h}(Q(x))|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\in\mathbb{F}}|E_{s}|\,|g(s)|^{2}\leq 2p^{d-1}\sum_{s\in\mathbb{F}}|g(s)|^{2}=2p^{d}\sum_{\tau\in\mathbb{F}}|h(\tau)|^{2}.

∎

The following reduction expresses the single-slice

\mathcal{D}_{\tau}
.

Lemma 2.20
.

Let
h:\,\mathbb{F}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
and

. Then

\|(h\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{z\}})*\widetilde{K}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{4}\lesssim p^{-7}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}^{3}}\sum_{\tau\in\mathbb{F}}|\mathcal{D}_{\tau}(u)|^{2},

\mathcal{D}_{\tau}(u):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho,\sigma\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}\\

Proof.

Using the explicit kernel formula in Lemma
2.14
and the change of variables

, one can verify that

(h\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{z\}})*\widetilde{K})(u,t)=\frac{1}{p^{2}-1}\sum_{\rho\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}}\mathcal{B}_{\rho}(u)\chi\Big(-\frac{\rho(t-z)^{2}}{4}\Big).

For fixed
u

|(h\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{z\}})*\widetilde{K})(u,t)|^{2}
into sums in
\rho,\sigma\in\mathbb{F}^{\ast}
, and then grouping the terms by

\tau=\rho-\sigma
, we obtain

|(h\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{z\}})*\widetilde{K})(u,t)|^{2}\lesssim p^{-4}\Bigg|\sum_{\tau\in\mathbb{F}}\mathcal{D}_{\tau}(u)\chi\Big(-\frac{\tau(t-z)^{2}}{4}\Big)\Bigg|,

Applying Lemma
2.19
with
h(\tau)=\mathcal{D}_{\tau}(u)
and
Q(t)=(t-z)^{2}/4
for each given

\|(h\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{z\}})*\widetilde{K}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{4}\lesssim p^{-8}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}^{3}}\sum_{t\in\mathbb{F}}\Bigg|\sum_{\tau\in\mathbb{F}}\mathcal{D}_{\tau}(u)\chi\left(-\frac{\tau(t-z)^{2}}{4}\right)\Bigg|^{2}\lesssim p^{-7}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}^{3}}\sum_{\tau\in\mathbb{F}}|D_{\tau}(u)|^{2}.

∎

2.5
A plane-then-line decomposition

In this subsection, we show a key lemma that decomposes a slice into three pieces, each composed of packets of certain types.

Lemma 2.21
.

Let
K_{\Pi}
,
K_{L}
be parameters satisfying
1\leq K_{\Pi}\leq p^{2}
,
1\leq K_{L}\leq p
and
K_{L}\leq K_{\Pi}
. For any
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{3}
, there is a disjoint
decomposition

A=A_{2}\sqcup A_{1}\sqcup A_{3}

with the following properties.

First, the rich-plane piece has a packet decomposition

A_{2}=\bigsqcup_{i\in I_{2}}A_{2,i},\qquad A_{2,i}\subseteq\pi_{i},\qquad|A_{2,i}|\geq K_{\Pi},

where the
A_{2,i}
are pairwise disjoint and the
\pi_{i}
are affine planes.

Second, the rich-line-and-poor-plane piece has a packet decomposition

A_{1}=\bigsqcup_{i\in I_{1}}A_{1,i},\qquad A_{1,i}\subseteq\ell_{i},\qquad|A_{1,i}|\geq K_{L},

where the
A_{1,i}
are pairwise disjoint and the
\ell_{i}
are affine lines.

Consequently, we have
|I_{2}|\leq|A|/K_{\Pi}
,

|I_{1}|\leq|A|/K_{L},\qquad\max_{\pi\,\mathrm{affine\ plane}}|A_{1}\cap\pi|<K_{\Pi},

and the remaining poor-line-and-poor-plane piece satisfies

\max_{\pi\,\mathrm{affine\ plane}}|A_{3}\cap\pi|<K_{\Pi},\qquad\max_{\ell\,\mathrm{affine\ line}}|A_{3}\cap\ell|<K_{L}.

Proof.

Start with
R^{(0)}=A
. If there is an affine plane
\pi
such that
|R^{(0)}\cap\pi|\geq K_{\Pi}
, choose one such plane, call it
\pi_{1}
, set

A_{2,1}:=R^{(0)}\cap\pi_{1},\qquad R^{(1)}:=R^{(0)}\setminus A_{2,1},

and continue. At the
i
-th plane step, if the current remainder

R^{(i-1)}
has an affine plane
\pi_{i}
with
|R^{(i-1)}\cap\pi_{i}|\geq K_{\Pi}
, set

A_{2,i}:=R^{(i-1)}\cap\pi_{i},\qquad R^{(i)}:=R^{(i-1)}\setminus A_{2,i}.

The packets
A_{2,i}
are disjoint because each packet is removed before the next one is selected. The algorithm must stop after finitely many steps, since at each step at least one point is removed. In fact, since every selected packet has size at least
K_{\Pi}
, the number of selected plane packets is at most
|A|/K_{\Pi}
. When the plane stage stops, the current remainder, denoted
R_{\mathrm{pl}}
, satisfies

\max_{\pi\,\mathrm{affine\ plane}}|R_{\mathrm{pl}}\cap\pi|<K_{\Pi}.

Take

A_{2}:=\bigsqcup_{i}A_{2,i}.

Now run the same greedy procedure with affine lines inside
R_{\mathrm{pl}}
.
Start with
U^{(0)}=R_{\mathrm{pl}}
. If there is an affine line
\ell

such that
|U^{(0)}\cap\ell|\geq K_{L}
, choose one such line, call it
\ell_{1}
, set

A_{1,1}:=U^{(0)}\cap\ell_{1},\qquad U^{(1)}:=U^{(0)}\setminus A_{1,1},

and continue. At the
i
-th line step, choose a line
\ell_{i}
with

|U^{(i-1)}\cap\ell_{i}|\geq K_{L}
, set

A_{1,i}:=U^{(i-1)}\cap\ell_{i},\qquad U^{(i)}:=U^{(i-1)}\setminus A_{1,i},

and stop only when no such line remains. Again the packets
A_{1,i}
are
disjoint, and their number is at most
|A|/K_{L}
. Define

A_{1}:=\bigsqcup_{i}A_{1,i},\qquad A_{3}:=A\setminus(A_{1}\cup A_{2}).

By construction,
A_{3}
has no affine line section of size at least
K_{L}
.
Moreover, because the line stage only removes points from
R_{\mathrm{pl}}
,
all affine plane sections can only decrease. Hence
A_{1}
and
A_{3}
retain the no-rich-plane property inherited from
R_{\mathrm{pl}}
. This proves the lemma.
∎

The indice for the slice
A_{i}

(i=1,2,3)
is chosen so that a single packet in
A_{i}
is contained in an
i
-dimensional space.

3
Reduction to horizontal slices

For
E\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{4}
, denote its cross sections by

E_{z}:=\{u\in\mathbb{F}^{3}:\ (u,z)\in E\},\qquad z\in\mathbb{F}.

Then
E=\bigsqcup_{z\in\mathbb{F}}(E_{z}\times\{z\})
, a disjoint union of horizontal-slices, satisfying
|E|=\sum_{z\in\mathbb{F}}|E_{z}|
.

Recall that

(f\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{z\}})(u,t)=\mathbf{1}_{E_{z}}(u)\mathbf{1}_{\{z\}}(t),\qquad(u,t)\in\mathbb{F}^{3}\times\mathbb{F}.

for
f:\,\mathbb{F}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
and
z\in\mathbb{F}
. One has
\mathbf{1}_{E}=\sum\nolimits_{z\in\mathbb{F}}\mathbf{1}_{E_{z}}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{z\}}
.
With the convolution taken on
\mathbb{F}^{4}
, we denote

F_{z}:=(\mathbf{1}_{E_{z}}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{z\}})*\widetilde{K}.

Then
\mathbf{1}_{E}*\widetilde{K}=\sum\nolimits_{z\in\mathbb{F}}F_{z}
.

The next proposition is the horizontal-slice reduction: it separates the global restriction norm into the size of
E
and the contribution of the individual horizontal slices.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{4}
. Then the following two estimates hold. First,

\|\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{E}}\|_{L^{2}(S_{j},d\sigma)}\lesssim|E|^{1/2}+|E|^{3/8}\Big(\sum_{z\in\mathbb{F}}\|F_{z}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}\Big)^{1/2}.

Second,

\|\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{E}}\|_{L^{2}(S_{j},d\sigma)}\lesssim|E|^{1/2}+p^{-1/4}|E|^{5/8}\Big(\sum_{z\in\mathbb{F}}|E_{z}|^{1/2}\Big)^{1/4}.

Remark 3.2
.

Since
\sum_{z\in\mathbb{F}}|E_{z}|^{1/2}\leq p^{1/2}|E|^{1/2}
by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, the second formula of Proposition
3.1
always implies

\|\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{E}}\|_{L^{2}(S_{j},d\sigma)}\lesssim|E|^{1/2}+p^{-1/8}|E|^{3/4}.

At
|E|\approx p^{5/2}
this is exactly the Stein–Tomas exponent
|E|^{7/10}
. Thus, the new estimate is only sharper when the horizontal slice profile has a nontrivial saving in its half-moment.

We first expand the convolution slice by slice, keeping the diagonal terms and the off-diagonal interactions separate.

Lemma 3.3
.

With the notation above,

\|\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{E}}\|_{L^{2}(S_{j},d\sigma)}\lesssim|E|^{1/2}+|E|^{3/8}\Bigg(\sum_{z\in\mathbb{F}}\|F_{z}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{2}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z,z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}\\
z\neq z^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\|F_{z}F_{z^{\prime}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}\Bigg)^{1/4}.

Proof.

Using Lemma
2.14
, one obtains that

\mathbf{1}_{E}*(d\sigma)^{\vee}=\frac{p^{2}}{p^{2}-1}\mathbf{1}_{E}+\mathbf{1}_{E}*\widetilde{K}.

Then

\displaystyle\|\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{E}}\|_{L^{2}(S_{j},d\sigma)}^{2}=\frac{1}{|S_{j}|}\sum_{\xi\in S_{j}}|\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{E}}(\xi)|^{2}=\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{F}^{4}}\mathbf{1}_{E}(x)\mathbf{1}_{E}(y)(d\sigma)^{\vee}(y-x)

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{F}^{4}}\mathbf{1}_{E}(y)(\mathbf{1}_{E}*(d\sigma)^{\vee})(y)\lesssim|E|+\sum_{y\in\mathbb{F}^{4}}|\mathbf{1}_{E}(y)(\mathbf{1}_{E}*\widetilde{K})(y)|.

(11)

By Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle\sum_{y\in\mathbb{F}^{4}}|1_{E}(y)(\mathbf{1}_{E}*\widetilde{K})(y)|\leq\|\mathbf{1}_{E}\|_{L^{4/3}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}\|\mathbf{1}_{E}*\widetilde{K}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}=|E|^{3/4}\Big\|\sum_{z\in\mathbb{F}}F_{z}\Big\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=|E|^{3/4}\Big\|\sum_{z,z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}}F_{z}\overline{F_{z^{\prime}}}\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{1/2}\leq|E|^{3/4}\Big(\sum_{z,z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}}\|F_{z}F_{z^{\prime}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=|E|^{3/4}\Big(\sum\limits_{z\in\mathbb{F}}\|F_{z}\|^{2}_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}_{4})}+\sum_{z,z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}\atop z\neq z^{\prime}}\|F_{z}F_{z^{\prime}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}\Big)^{1/2}.

Combining the above two formulae, the first expression then follows.
∎

The diagonal contribution is controlled directly by the
L^{2}
and
L^{\infty}
bounds for the four-dimensional
kernel.

Lemma 3.4
.

We have

\sum_{z\in\mathbb{F}}\|F_{z}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{2}\lesssim p^{-1}|E|\,\sum_{z\in\mathbb{F}}|E_{z}|^{1/2}.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.18
, one obtains

\|F_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{2}\lesssim p\|\mathbf{1}_{E_{z}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}^{2}=p|E_{z}|.

On the other hand, Young’s inequality and Lemma
2.16
give

\|F_{z}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}\leq\|\mathbf{1}_{E_{z}}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{z\}}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}\|\widetilde{K}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}\lesssim|E_{z}|\,p^{-3/2}.

It follows that

\|F_{z}\|_{L^{4}}^{4}\leq\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}^{4}}|F_{z}(x)|^{2}\|F_{z}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\lesssim p|E_{z}|\cdot|E_{z}|^{2}p^{-3}=p^{-2}|E_{z}|^{3}.

(12)

Taking square roots, summing in
z
yields

\sum_{z\in\mathbb{F}}\|F_{z}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{z\in\mathbb{F}}p^{-1}|E_{z}|\cdot|E_{z}|^{1/2}\leq p^{-1}\sum_{z\in\mathbb{F}}|E_{z}|\cdot\sum_{w\in\mathbb{F}}|E_{w}|^{1/2}=p^{-1}|E|\,\sum_{w\in\mathbb{F}}|E_{w}|^{1/2}.

The proof is completed.
∎

For the off-diagonal terms, it is convenient to separate the vertical variable and regard the kernel
as a family of three-dimensional convolution kernels. For
\tau\in\mathbb{F}
, define the three-dimensional kernel

\widetilde{K}_{\tau}(u):=\widetilde{K}(u,\tau),\qquad u\in\mathbb{F}^{3}.

Lemma 3.5
.

For every
\tau\in\mathbb{F}
and every
f:\mathbb{F}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}
,

\|f*\widetilde{K}_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{3})},\qquad\|f*\widetilde{K}_{\tau}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}\lesssim p^{-3/2}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}.

In particular, for every set
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{3}
, one has

\|\mathbf{1}_{A}*\widetilde{K}_{\tau}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}\lesssim p^{-3/4}|A|^{3/4}.

Proof.

By Young’s inequality, the fact
\|\widetilde{K}_{\tau}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}\leq\|\widetilde{K}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}
, and Lemma
2.16
, one deduces

\|(f*\widetilde{K}_{\tau})(u)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}=\|f\ast\widetilde{K}_{\tau}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}\|\widetilde{K}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}\lesssim p^{-3/2}\|f\|_{L^{1}(F^{3})}.

For the
L^{2}
estimate, one sees by Fourier inversion on
\mathbb{F}^{4}
that

\widetilde{K}_{\tau}(u)=\widetilde{K}(u,\tau)=p^{-4}\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{F}^{3}}\sum_{s\in\mathbb{F}}\widehat{\widetilde{K}}(\xi,s)\chi(u\cdot\xi+\tau s),\qquad(u,\tau)\in\mathbb{F}^{4}.

Hence

\displaystyle\widehat{\widetilde{K}}_{\tau}(\eta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}^{3}}\widetilde{K}_{\tau}(u)\chi(-u\cdot\eta)=p^{-4}\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{F}^{3}}\sum_{s\in F}\widehat{\widetilde{K}}(\xi,s)\chi(\tau s)\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}^{3}}\chi(u\cdot(\xi-\eta))

\displaystyle=p^{-1}\sum_{s\in\mathbb{F}}\widehat{\widetilde{K}}(\eta,s)\chi(\tau s),\qquad\eta\in\mathbb{F}^{3}.

Now we apply Lemma
2.15
.
For given
\eta
, there at most
2
solutions in
s
to the equality
Q_{4}(\eta,s)=j
. Therefore, if
\tau\neq 0
then

|\widehat{\widetilde{K}}_{\tau}(\eta)|=\frac{p^{2}}{p^{2}-1}\Big|\sum_{Q_{4}(\eta,s)=j}\chi(\tau s)\Big|\leq\frac{2p^{2}}{p^{2}-1}\lesssim 1.

If
\tau=0
, then

|\widehat{K_{0}}(\eta)|\leq\frac{p^{2}}{p^{2}-1}\Big|\sum\limits_{Q_{4}(\eta,s)=j}1+1\Big|\lesssim 1.

By Plancherel on
\mathbb{F}^{3}
,

\|f*\widetilde{K}_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}^{2}=p^{-3}\sum_{\eta\in\mathbb{F}^{3}}|\widehat{f}(\eta)|^{2}|\widehat{\widetilde{K}}_{\tau}(\eta)|^{2}\lesssim p^{-3}\sum_{\eta\in\mathbb{F}^{3}}|\widehat{f}(\eta)|^{2}=\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}^{2}.

The
L^{4}
-estimate for indicators is given by

\|\mathbf{1}_{A}*\widetilde{K}_{\tau}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}^{4}\leq\|\mathbf{1}_{A}*\widetilde{K}_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}^{2}\|\mathbf{1}_{A}*\widetilde{K}_{\tau}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}^{2}\lesssim|A|\cdot p^{-3}|A|^{2}=p^{-3}|A|^{3}.

∎

Applying the fixed-
\tau
kernel estimates from Lemma
3.5
to two distinct slices gives an asymmetric
product estimate, which can then be symmetrized by exchanging the two slices.

Lemma 3.6
.

For
z,z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}
with
z\neq z^{\prime}
,

\|F_{z}F_{z^{\prime}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}\lesssim p^{-1}\min\left\{|E_{z}|\,|E_{z^{\prime}}|^{1/2},\,|E_{z}|^{1/2}|E_{z^{\prime}}|\right\}.

Proof.

Write
\delta:=z-z^{\prime}\neq 0
. For
t=z+\tau
one has

F_{z}(u,t)=(\mathbf{1}_{E_{z}}*\widetilde{K}_{\tau})(u),\qquad F_{z^{\prime}}(u,t)=(\mathbf{1}_{E_{z^{\prime}}}*\widetilde{K}_{\tau+\delta})(u).

Therefore

\displaystyle\|F_{z}F_{z^{\prime}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{2}=\sum_{t\in\mathbb{F}}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}^{3}}|F_{z}(u,t)F_{z^{\prime}}(u,t)|^{2}=\sum_{\tau\in\mathbb{F}}\|(\mathbf{1}_{E_{z}}*\widetilde{K}_{\tau})(\mathbf{1}_{E_{z^{\prime}}}*\widetilde{K}_{\tau+\delta})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}^{2}.

Using Hölder’s inequality and Lemma
3.5
,

\|(\mathbf{1}_{E_{z}}*\widetilde{K}_{\tau})(\mathbf{1}_{E_{z^{\prime}}}*\widetilde{K}_{\tau+\delta})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}\leq\|\mathbf{1}_{E_{z}}*\widetilde{K}_{\tau}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}\|\mathbf{1}_{E_{z^{\prime}}}*\widetilde{K}_{\tau+\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}\lesssim p^{-3/2}|E_{z}|\,|E_{z^{\prime}}|^{1/2}.

It follows that

\|F_{z}F_{z^{\prime}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}\lesssim\big(p\cdot p^{-3}|E_{z}|^{2}\,|E_{z^{\prime}}|\big)^{1/2}=p^{-1}|E_{z}|\,|E_{z^{\prime}}|^{1/2}.

Interchanging the roles of
z
and
z^{\prime}
gives the symmetric alternative, and the minimum of the two bounds proves the proposition.
∎

We now combine the diagonal estimate with the asymmetric off-diagonal estimate to prove the two asserted bounds.

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\sum_{z,z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}\atop z\neq z^{\prime}}\|F_{z}F_{z^{\prime}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}\leq\sum_{z,z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}\atop z\neq z^{\prime}}\|F_{z}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}\|F_{z^{\prime}}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}\leq\Big(\sum_{z\in\mathbb{F}}\|F_{z}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}\Big)^{2}.

Also,

\sum\limits_{z\in\mathbb{F}}\|F_{z}\|^{2}_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}_{4})}\leq\Big(\sum_{z\in\mathbb{F}}\|F_{z}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}\Big)^{2}.

The first formula follows by combining Lemma
3.3
.

By Lemmas
3.4
and
3.6
, we have

\sum_{z\in\mathbb{F}}\|F_{z}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{2}\lesssim p^{-1}|E|\sum_{z}|E_{z}|^{1/2},

and

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z,z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}\\
z\neq z^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\|F_{z}F_{z^{\prime}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}\lesssim p^{-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z,z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}\\
z\neq z^{\prime}\end{subarray}}|E_{z}|^{1/2}|E_{z^{\prime}}|\leq p^{-1}\sum_{z\in\mathbb{F}}|E_{z}|^{1/2}\cdot\sum_{z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}}|E_{z^{\prime}}|=p^{-1}|E|\sum_{z\in\mathbb{F}}|E_{z}|^{1/2}.

The second formula then follows by combining Lemma
3.3
.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

For every
E\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{4}
,

\|\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{E}}\|_{L^{2}(S_{j},d\sigma)}\lesssim|E|^{1/2}+p^{-3/4}|E|,

and

\|\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{E}}\|_{L^{2}(S_{j},d\sigma)}\lesssim p^{1/2}|E|^{1/2}.

Proof.

The first estimate follows (

), together with the
L^{\infty}
-bound in Lemma
2.15
.

For the second estimate,
Plancherel on
\mathbb{F}^{4}
gives

\|\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{E}}\|_{L^{2}(S_{j},d\sigma)}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{|S_{j}|}\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{F}^{4}}|\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{E}}(\xi)|^{2}=\frac{p^{4}|E|}{|S_{j}|},

and the fact
|S_{j}|\approx p^{3}
.
∎

4
Estimates from the poor-line-and-poor-plane pieces

In this section, we deal with the packet
A_{3}
obtained from the plane-then-line decomposition of a set
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{3}
. For simplicity, we use the notation
B
for
A_{3}
. It satisfies that

\max\limits_{\ell\,\,\text{affine line}}|B\cap\ell|\leq K_{L},\qquad\max\limits_{\pi\,\,\text{affine plane}}|B\cap\pi|\leq K_{\Pi},

(13)

where
K_{\Pi}
and
K_{L}
are parameters satisfying (
4
). The main purpose of this section is to establish the following
L^{4}
-estimate.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
B\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{3}
and
z\in\mathbb{F}
. Suppose that
|B|\lesssim p^{2}
and (
13
) holds. Then

\displaystyle\big\|(\mathbf{1}_{B}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{z\}})*\widetilde{K}\big\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{4}\lesssim\frac{|B|^{4}}{p^{4}}+\frac{|B|^{2}}{p}+\frac{|B|K_{L}^{2}}{p}+\frac{|B|^{3}K_{L}}{p^{3}}+\frac{|B|K_{\Pi}^{3}}{p^{3}}.

Recall that

\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{aff}}(B)=\#\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}x,y,z,w\in B,\\
\rho,\sigma,\rho^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}\end{array}:\begin{array}[]{c}\rho+\sigma=\rho^{\prime}+\sigma^{\prime},\\
\rho x+\sigma y=\rho^{\prime}z+\sigma^{\prime}w\end{array}\right\}.

We first reduce the analytic
L^{4}
quantity to this affine energy.

Lemma 4.2
.

For
B\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{3}
and
z\in\mathbb{F}
, it satisfies that

\big\|(\mathbf{1}_{B}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{z\}})*\widetilde{K}\big\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{4}\lesssim p^{-4}\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{aff}}(B).

Proof.

Applying Lemma
2.20
with
f=\mathbf{1}_{B}
, yields

\|(\mathbf{1}_{B}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{z\}})*\widetilde{K}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{4}\lesssim p^{-7}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}^{3}}\sum_{\tau\in\mathbb{F}}|\mathcal{D}_{\tau}(u)|^{2},

where

D_{\tau}(u)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho,\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}\\
\rho-\sigma^{\prime}=\tau\end{subarray}}\mathcal{B}_{\rho}(u)\overline{\mathcal{B}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(u)},\qquad\mathcal{\mathcal{}}B_{\rho}(u)=\sum_{x\in B}\chi\left(-\frac{j}{\rho}-\frac{\rho}{4}Q_{3}(u-x)\right).

Expanding
|\mathcal{D}_{\tau}(u)|^{2}
, and writing the variable by
\rho,\sigma^{\prime},\rho^{\prime},\sigma\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}
and
x,w,z,y\in B
, with
\rho-\sigma^{\prime}=\rho^{\prime}-\sigma=\tau
. And the phase contains the terms

-\frac{\rho}{4}Q_{3}(u-x)+\frac{\sigma^{\prime}}{4}Q_{3}(u-w)+\frac{\rho^{\prime}}{4}Q_{3}(u-z)-\frac{\sigma}{4}Q_{3}(u-y)

plus terms independent of
u
.
Because the total weights agree, the quadratic terms in
u
cancel. The
remaining dependence on
u
is the character

\chi\left(\frac{1}{2}u\cdot(\rho x-\sigma^{\prime}w-\rho^{\prime}z+\sigma y)\right).

The sum over
u\in\mathbb{F}^{3}
is zero unless
\rho x+\sigma y=\rho^{\prime}z+\sigma^{\prime}w
, and is
p^{3}
when this equality holds. Therefore

\sum_{u,\tau}|\mathcal{D}_{\tau}(u)|^{2}\leq p^{3}\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{aff}}(B).

Consequently
\|(\mathbf{1}_{B}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{z\}})*\widetilde{K}\|_{4}^{4}\lesssim p^{-4}\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{aff}}(B)
. The proof is completed.
∎

Recall that

M_{4}(B):=\sum_{\pi\ \mathrm{affine\ plane}}|B\cap\pi|^{4}.

The remaining task is to estimate this plane moment using the poor-line and poor-plane hypotheses.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
B\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{3}
. Assume
p\leq|B|\lesssim p^{2}
and (
13
) holds. Then

M_{4}(B)\lesssim\frac{|B|^{4}}{p}+p^{2}|B|^{2}+p^{2}|B|K_{L}^{2}+|B|^{3}K_{L}+|B|K_{\Pi}^{3}.

Proof.

For dyadic
K
with
1\leq K\leq K_{\Pi}
, define
\mathcal{H}_{K}:=\{\pi:\ K\leq|B\cap\pi|<2K\}
. Then

M_{4}(B)\lesssim\sum_{K}K^{4}|\mathcal{H}_{K}|,

where the sum is over dyadic
1\leq K\leq K_{\Pi}
. We split this sum into low,
middle, and high richness ranges.

In the low range

K<2|B|/p
, we use the trivial bound
|\mathcal{H}_{K}|\lesssim p^{3}
, which comes from the
number of affine planes in
\mathbb{F}^{3}
. Therefore, the dyadic sum satisfies

\sum_{K<2|B|/p}K^{4}|\mathcal{H}_{K}|\lesssim p^{3}\left(\frac{|B|}{p}\right)^{4}=\frac{|B|^{4}}{p}.

Set

K_{0}:=C_{0}\bigl(|B|^{1/2}+L(B)\bigr),\qquad L(B):=\max_{\ell\ \mathrm{affine\ line}}|B\cap\ell|,

with
C_{0}
as in Lemma
2.7
. In
the middle range
2|B|/p\leq K\leq K_{0}
, Lemma
2.4
gives

|\mathcal{H}_{K}|\lesssim\frac{p^{2}|B|}{K^{2}}.

Since
|B|\lesssim p^{2}
, one has
|B|/p\lesssim|B|^{1/2}\leq K_{0}
, up to harmless
absolute constants. Then

\sum_{|B|/p\lesssim K\leq K_{0}}K^{4}|\mathcal{H}_{K}|\lesssim p^{2}|B|\sum_{K\leq K_{0}}K^{2}\lesssim p^{2}|B|K_{0}^{2}\lesssim p^{2}|B|^{2}+p^{2}|B|L(B)^{2}.

It remains to handle the high range
K>K_{0}
. Split it into two subranges. Let
C_{1}
be the constant obtained from Lemma
2.8
. If

K^{2}<C_{1}|B|L(B)
, with the estimate
|\mathcal{H}_{K}|\lesssim|B|^{2}/K^{2}
from Lemma
2.7
, one obtains

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K>K_{0}\\
K^{2}<C_{1}|B|L(B)\end{subarray}}K^{4}|\mathcal{H}_{K}|\lesssim|B|^{2}\sum_{K^{2}\lesssim|B|L(B)}K^{2}\lesssim|B|^{3}L(B).

If
K^{2}\geq C_{1}|B|L(B)
, Lemma
2.8
gives

|\mathcal{H}_{K}|\lesssim|B|/K
, and therefore

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K>K_{0}\\
K^{2}\geq C_{1}|B|L(B)\end{subarray}}K^{4}|\mathcal{H}_{K}|\lesssim|B|\sum_{K\leq K_{\Pi}}K^{3}\lesssim|B|K_{\Pi}^{3}.

Putting all bounds together, and noticing
L(B)\leq K_{L}
, the proof is completed.
∎

We now combine the small-set estimate with the energy-moment argument to prove the proposition.

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

When
|B|<p
, the same approach as in (
12
) shows that

\big\|(\mathbf{1}_{B}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{z\}})*\widetilde{K}\big\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{4}\lesssim p^{-2}|B|^{3}\leq p^{-1}|B|^{2}.

When
p\leq|B|\lesssim p^{2}
, the conclusion follows directly by combining Lemmas
4.2
,
2.3
and
4.3
.
∎

5
Estimates from the rich-plane pieces

In this section, we deal with the rich-plane-piece
A_{2}
produced by the stopping-time decomposition of a set
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{3}
. Moreover concretely,

A_{2}=\bigsqcup_{i\in I_{2}}A_{2,i},\qquad A_{2,i}\subseteq\pi_{i},\qquad|A_{2,i}|\geq K_{\Pi},

where
\pi_{i}

(i\in I_{2})
are affine planes, and the set
I_{2}
of indices satisfies
|I_{2}|\leq|A|/K_{\Pi}
.

The next proposition is the rich-plane estimate that will later be combined with the other two structural pieces.

Proposition 5.1
.

For any
z\in\mathbb{F}
, it satisfies that

\|(\mathbf{1}_{A_{2}}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{z\}})\ast\widetilde{K}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{4}\lesssim\frac{|A|^{4}}{pK_{\Pi}^{2}}+\frac{|A|^{4}}{K_{\Pi}^{3}}.

For each single packet
A_{2,i}

(i\in I_{2})
, denoted by
B
for simplicity, we prove the following estimates in two separate cases: the underlying plane has an anisotropic or isotropic normal vector.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\pi\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{3}
be an affine plane whose normal vector is anisotropic for the quadratic form
Q_{3}
.
Then, for
B\subseteq\pi
and
z\in\mathbb{F}
,

\big\|(1_{B}\otimes 1_{\{z\}})*\widetilde{K}\big\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{4}\lesssim p^{-1}|B|^{2}.

When the normal vector is isotropic, one additional physical-space term remains and contributes the extra
|B|
term.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
\pi\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{3}
be an affine plane whose normal vector is isotropic for the quadratic form
Q_{3}
.
Then, for
B\subseteq\pi
and
z\in\mathbb{F}
,

\big\|(1_{B}\otimes 1_{\{z\}})*\widetilde{K}\big\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{4}\lesssim p^{-1}|B|^{2}+|B|.

Assuming the two packet estimates, the bound for
A_{2}
follows by summing the packet norms and using
|I_{2}|\leq|A|/K_{\Pi}
.

Proof of Proposition
5.1
.

By Propositions
5.2
and
5.3
,

\|(1_{A_{2,i}}\otimes 1_{\{z\}})*\widetilde{K}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}\lesssim p^{-1/4}|A_{2,i}|^{1/2}+|A_{2,i}|^{1/4}.

Minkowski’s inequality leads to

\|(\mathbf{1}_{A_{2}}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{z\}})\ast\widetilde{K}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}\leq\sum_{i\in I_{2}}\|(1_{A_{2,i}}\otimes 1_{\{z\}})*\widetilde{K}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}\lesssim\sum\limits_{i\in I_{2}}\big(p^{-1/4}|A_{2,i}|^{1/2}+|A_{2,i}|^{1/4}\big).

With
|I_{2}|\leq|A|/K_{\Pi}
, one deduces by Hölder’s inequality that

\sum_{i\in I_{2}}|A_{2,i}|^{1/2}\leq|I_{2}|^{1/2}\Big(\sum_{i\in I_{2}}|A_{2,i}|\Big)^{1/2}\leq\frac{|A|}{K_{\Pi}^{1/2}},

\sum_{i\in I_{2}}|A_{2,i}|^{1/4}\leq|I_{2}|^{3/4}\Big(\sum_{i\in I_{2}}|A_{2,i}|\Big)^{1/4}\leq\frac{|A|}{K_{\Pi}^{3/4}}.

It follows that

\|(\mathbf{1}_{A_{2}}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{z\}})\ast\widetilde{K}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{4}\lesssim\frac{|A|^{4}}{pK_{\Pi}^{2}}+\frac{|A|^{4}}{K_{\Pi}^{3}}.

∎

5.1
Estimates from the anisotropic-normal rich-plane packets

We first handle anisotropic normals. In this case the relevant horizontal and complementary binary quadratic forms are nondegenerate.

Proof of Proposition
5.2
.

Translations in the vertical variable only translate the convolution, and therefore preserve its
L^{4}
-norm. So we may assume without loss of generality that
z=0
. Then, translations in the horizontal variable preserve both its
L^{4}
-norm and the vertical variable. So we may assume without loss of generality that
\pi
is a linear plane, and denote it by
U
.

Choose a fixed linear isomorphism
\iota:\,\mathbb{F}^{2}\to U
, and denote

B^{\flat}:=\iota^{-1}(B)\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{2},

the coordinate copy of
B
, satisfying
|B^{\flat}|=|B|
. Define the binary quadratic form

Q_{U}(y):=Q_{3}(\iota(y)),\qquad y\in\mathbb{F}^{2}.

Let
n
be an anisotropic normal vector to
U
. Since

Q_{3}(n)\neq 0
, the restriction of
Q_{3}
to
U=n^{\perp}
is nondegenerate. So
Q_{U}
is a nondegenerate quadratic form on

\mathbb{F}^{2}
. Note that
Q_{4}(u,t):=Q_{3}(u)+t^{2}
for
(u,t)\in\mathbb{F}^{3}\times\mathbb{F}
. Let
V:=(U^{\perp}\times\{0\})\oplus(\{0\}\times\mathbb{F})\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{4}
.
Then
\mathbb{F}^{4}=(U\times\{0\})\oplus V
is an orthogonal decomposition for
Q_{4}
. Choose a fixed linear isomorphism
\kappa:\mathbb{F}^{2}\to V
, and define

Q_{V}(w):=Q_{4}(\kappa(w)),\qquad w\in\mathbb{F}^{2}.

Since
U^{\perp}=\mathbb{F}n
and
Q_{3}(n)\neq 0
, the form
Q_{V}
is also a
nondegenerate binary quadratic form. Therefore every point of
\mathbb{F}^{4}
may be written uniquely as
(\iota(y),0)+\kappa(w)
for
y,w\in\mathbb{F}^{2}
, and in these coordinates

Q_{4}\bigl((\iota(y),0)+\kappa(w)\bigr)=Q_{U}(y)+Q_{V}(w).

For
y,w\in\mathbb{F}^{2}
, write

F(y,w):=\bigl((1_{B}\otimes 1_{\{0\}})*\widetilde{K}\bigr)\bigl((\iota(y),0)+\kappa(w)\bigr).

This is merely the function
(1_{B}\otimes 1_{\{0\}})*\widetilde{K}
expressed
in the coordinates
(y,w)
.

For
\rho\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}
, define

B_{\rho}(y):=\sum_{x\in B^{\flat}}\chi\left(-\frac{\rho}{4}Q_{U}(y-x)\right),\qquad y\in\mathbb{F}^{2}.

Using the explicit formula (
10
) for
\widetilde{K}
, and making the change of
variables
\rho=1/r
, we get

F(y,w)=\sum_{x\in B^{\flat}}\widetilde{K}\bigl((\iota(y-x),0)+\kappa(w)\bigr)=\frac{1}{p^{2}-1}\sum_{\rho\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}}\chi\left(-\frac{j}{\rho}\right)B_{\rho}(y)\chi\left(-\frac{\rho}{4}Q_{V}(w)\right).

Squaring and grouping the terms according to

\tau=\rho+\sigma
, we obtain

F(y,w)^{2}=\frac{1}{(p^{2}-1)^{2}}\sum_{\tau\in\mathbb{F}}D_{\tau}(y)\chi\left(-\frac{\tau}{4}Q_{V}(w)\right),

where

D_{\tau}(y):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho,\sigma\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}\\
\rho+\sigma=\tau\end{subarray}}\chi\left(-\frac{j}{\rho}-\frac{j}{\sigma}\right)B_{\rho}(y)B_{\sigma}(y),\qquad y\in\mathbb{F}^{2}.

Applying Lemma
2.19
to each
y\in\mathbb{F}^{2}
, yields

\displaystyle\sum_{w\in\mathbb{F}^{2}}\left|\sum_{\tau\in\mathbb{F}}D_{\tau}(y)\chi\left(-\frac{\tau}{4}Q_{V}(w)\right)\right|^{2}\lesssim p^{2}\sum_{\tau\in\mathbb{F}}|D_{\tau}(y)|^{2}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\|F\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{4}=\sum_{y,w\in\mathbb{F}^{2}}|F(y,w)^{2}|^{2}\lesssim p^{-6}\mathfrak{D}(B),\qquad\mathfrak{D}(B):=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{F}^{2}}\sum_{\tau\in\mathbb{F}}|D_{\tau}(y)|^{2}.

To get
\|F\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{4}\lesssim p^{-1}|B|^{2}
, it remains to prove

\mathfrak{D}(B)\lesssim p^{5}|B|^{2}.

(14)

Next, we express
\mathfrak{D}(B)
on the Fourier side. For simplicity, we write

b(\xi):=\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{B^{\flat}}}(\xi),\qquad\xi\in\mathbb{F}^{2}.

Lemma
2.10
gives

\sum\limits_{z\in\mathbb{F}^{2}}\chi\Big(-\frac{\rho}{4}Q_{U}(z)-z\cdot\xi\Big)=\gamma_{U}\,p\,\chi\Big(\frac{\widetilde{Q}_{U}(\xi)}{\rho}\Big),\qquad\rho\in\mathbb{F}^{\times},

for some
\gamma_{U}\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\gamma_{U}|=1
, where
\widetilde{Q}_{U}
is the dual quadratic form to
Q_{U}
. In particular, since the dimension is
2
, the square of Legendre symbol equals to
1
, and
\gamma_{U}
is independent of
\rho
. Hence

\widehat{B_{\rho}}(\xi)=\gamma_{U}\,p\,b(\xi)\chi\left(\frac{\widetilde{Q}_{U}(\xi)}{\rho}\right).

Taking the Fourier transform of
D_{\tau}
, using
\widehat{B_{\rho}B_{\tau}}=p^{-2}\widehat{B_{\rho}}\ast\widehat{B_{\tau}}
on
\mathbb{F}^{2}
, the two Gauss factors
p\cdot p
cancel the factor
p^{-2}
. We obtain that

\widehat{D_{\tau}}(\zeta)=\gamma_{U}^{2}\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{F}^{2}}b(\xi)b(\zeta-\xi)K_{\tau}(\xi,\zeta),

where

K_{\tau}(\xi,\zeta):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}\\
\tau-\rho\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}\end{subarray}}\chi\left(\frac{A_{\zeta}(\xi)}{\rho}+\frac{B_{\zeta}(\xi)}{\tau-\rho}\right),

with

A_{\zeta}(\xi):=\widetilde{Q}_{U}(\xi)-j,\qquad B_{\zeta}(\xi):=\widetilde{Q}_{U}(\zeta-\xi)-j.

By Plancherel’s theorem on
\mathbb{F}^{2}
for each given
\tau\in\mathbb{F}
,

\mathfrak{D}(B)=p^{-2}\sum_{\zeta\in\mathbb{F}^{2}}\sum_{\tau\in\mathbb{F}}\left|\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{F}^{2}}b(\xi)b(\zeta-\xi)K_{\tau}(\xi,\zeta)\right|^{2}.

For
\zeta=0
, we use the trivial estimate

|K_{\tau}(\xi,0)|\leq p
. Thus,

\sum_{\tau\in\mathbb{F}}\left|\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{F}^{2}}b(\xi)b(-\xi)K_{\tau}(\xi,0)\right|^{2}\lesssim p^{3}\Bigg(\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{F}^{2}}|b(\xi)b(-\xi)|\Bigg)^{2}.

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and Plancherel’s theorem,

\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{F}^{2}}|b(\xi)b(-\xi)|\leq\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{F}^{2}}|b(\xi)|^{2}=p^{2}\|\mathbf{1}_{B^{\flat}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{2})}^{2}=p^{2}|B|.

Hence the contribution of
\zeta=0
to
\mathfrak{D}(B)
is
\lesssim p^{5}|B|^{2}
. It remains to prove that

\Sigma:=\sum_{0\neq\zeta\in\mathbb{F}^{2}}\sum_{\tau\in\mathbb{F}}\Big|\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{F}^{2}}b(\xi)b(\zeta-\xi)K_{\tau}(\xi,\zeta)\Big|^{2}\lesssim p^{7}|B|^{2}.

The non-zero
\zeta
-terms are split according to whether the map below has finite fibers or an exceptional isotropic line. For each
\zeta\neq 0
, define

\Phi_{\zeta}:\mathbb{F}^{2}\to\mathbb{F}^{2},\qquad\Phi_{\zeta}(\xi):=(A_{\zeta}(\xi),B_{\zeta}(\xi)).

Let

E_{\zeta}:=\begin{cases}\mathbb{F}\zeta,&\text{if }\widetilde{Q}_{U}(\zeta)=0,\\
\varnothing,&\text{if }\widetilde{Q}_{U}(\zeta)\neq 0,\end{cases}\qquad\Omega_{\zeta}:=\mathbb{F}^{2}\setminus E_{\zeta}.

Then
\Sigma\lesssim\Sigma_{1}+\Sigma_{2}
, where

\Sigma_{1}:=\sum_{0\neq\zeta\in\mathbb{F}^{2}}\sum_{\tau\in\mathbb{F}}\Big|\sum_{\xi\in\Omega_{\zeta}}b(\xi)b(\zeta-\xi)K_{\tau}(\xi,\zeta)\Big|^{2},\quad\Sigma_{2}:=\sum_{0\neq\zeta\in\mathbb{F}^{2}}\sum_{\tau\in\mathbb{F}}\Big|\sum_{\xi\in E_{\zeta}}b(\xi)b(\zeta-\xi)K_{\tau}(\xi,\zeta)\Big|^{2}.

For
\zeta\neq 0
be fixed, we now characterize the condition for
\xi\notin\Omega_{\zeta}
. Let

B_{\widetilde{Q}}
denote the symmetric bilinear form associated
with
\widetilde{Q}_{U}
, so that

\widetilde{Q}_{U}(a-b)=\widetilde{Q}_{U}(a)+\widetilde{Q}_{U}(b)-2B_{\widetilde{Q}}(a,b).

For a given value
(\alpha,\beta)
, the condition
\Phi_{\zeta}(\xi)=(\alpha,\beta)
gives

\displaystyle\beta-\alpha=B_{\zeta}(\xi)-A_{\zeta}(\xi)=\widetilde{Q}_{U}(\zeta-\xi)-\widetilde{Q}_{U}(\xi)=\widetilde{Q}_{U}(\zeta)-2B_{\widetilde{Q}}(\zeta,\xi).

Since
\widetilde{Q}_{U}
is nondegenerate and hence the linear functional
\xi\mapsto B_{\widetilde{Q}}(\zeta,\xi)

is not zero. Thus, the fiber lies on an affine line
L
. Intersecting
L
with
A_{\zeta}(\xi)=\alpha
, i.e. with
\widetilde{Q}_{U}(\xi)=\alpha+j
, gives at most two points unless the restriction of
\widetilde{Q}_{U}
to
L
is constant.

Write
L=\xi_{0}+\mathbb{F}v
. The polynomial
t\mapsto\widetilde{Q}_{U}(\xi_{0}+tv)
is constant only if

\widetilde{Q}_{U}(v)=0,\qquad B_{\widetilde{Q}}(\xi_{0},v)=0.

In a nondegenerate binary quadratic space, the orthogonal complement of a
non-zero isotropic vector
v
is exactly
\mathbb{F}v
. Thus,
\xi_{0}\in\mathbb{F}v
, so
the line is the isotropic line
\mathbb{F}v
through the origin. For this line also to satisfy

\frac{\widetilde{Q}_{U}(\zeta)-(\beta-\alpha)}{2}=B_{\widetilde{Q}}(\zeta,\xi_{0}+tv)=B_{\widetilde{Q}}(\zeta,\xi_{0})+tB_{\widetilde{Q}}(\zeta,v),\qquad t\in\mathbb{F},

we must have
B_{\widetilde{Q}}(\zeta,v)=0
, and hence
\zeta\in\mathbb{F}v
. Therefore the only positive-dimensional fibers
occur when

\zeta\neq 0,\qquad\widetilde{Q}_{U}(\zeta)=0,\qquad\xi\in L_{\zeta}:=\mathbb{F}\zeta.

On this exceptional line,

\widetilde{Q}_{U}(\xi)=0,\qquad\widetilde{Q}_{U}(\zeta-\xi)=0,

so
A_{\zeta}(\xi)=B_{\zeta}(\xi)=-j
.

Now we estimate
\Sigma_{1}
. On the finite-fiber part, orthogonality in the reciprocal parameters gives a large-sieve estimate. In particular, we claim that, for every function
h:\mathbb{F}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
supported on a subset
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{2}
,

\sum_{\tau\in\mathbb{F}}\Big|\sum_{\xi\in\Omega}h(\xi)K_{\tau}(\xi,\zeta)\Big|^{2}\lesssim p^{3}\sum_{\xi\in\Omega}|h(\xi)|^{2}.

(15)

To prove this, Cauchy-Schwarz inequality leads to

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{\xi\in\Omega}h(\xi)K_{\tau}(\xi,\zeta)\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle=\Bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}\\
\tau-\rho\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\xi\in\Omega}h(\xi)\chi\left(\frac{A_{\zeta}(\xi)}{\rho}+\frac{B_{\zeta}(\xi)}{\tau-\rho}\right)\Bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq p\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}\\
\tau-\rho\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}\end{subarray}}\left|\sum_{\xi\in\Omega}h(\xi)\chi\left(\frac{A_{\zeta}(\xi)}{\rho}+\frac{B_{\zeta}(\xi)}{\tau-\rho}\right)\right|^{2}.

Summing in
\tau
, and making the change of variables
u=\rho^{-1}
,
v=(\tau-\rho)^{-1}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\tau}\Big|\sum_{\xi\in\Omega}h(\xi)K_{\tau}(\xi,\zeta)\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq p\sum_{u,v\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}}\Big|\sum_{\xi\in\Omega}h(\xi)\chi(uA_{\zeta}(\xi)+vB_{\zeta}(\xi))\Big|^{2}.

Now we extend the sum to all
u,v\in\mathbb{F}
, expand the square and use orthogonality in
u,v
. Then the last expression is

=p\cdot p^{2}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi,\xi^{\prime}\in\Omega\\
A_{\zeta}(\xi)=A_{\zeta}(\xi^{\prime})\\
B_{\zeta}(\xi)=B_{\zeta}(\xi^{\prime})\end{subarray}}h(\xi)\overline{h(\xi^{\prime})}=p^{3}\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}}\Bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi\in\Omega\\
\Phi_{\zeta}(\xi)=(\alpha,\beta)\end{subarray}}h(\xi)\Bigg|^{2}\lesssim p^{3}\sum_{\xi\in\Omega}|h(\xi)|^{2},

since the fibers of
\Phi_{\zeta}
inside
\Omega
have size
2
. This proves (
15
).

Using (
15
) with

h(\xi)=b(\xi)b(\zeta-\xi)
on
\Omega_{\zeta}
for each
\zeta\neq 0
, summing over
\zeta
, and then applying Plancherel’s theorem on
\mathbb{F}^{2}
, yields

\displaystyle\Sigma_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\neq\zeta\in\mathbb{F}^{2}}\sum_{\tau\in\mathbb{F}}\Big|\sum_{\xi\in\Omega_{\zeta}}b(\xi)b(\zeta-\xi)K_{\tau}(\xi,\zeta)\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim p^{3}\sum_{\zeta,\xi\in\mathbb{F}^{2}}|b(\xi)b(\zeta-\xi)|^{2}=p^{3}\Big(\sum_{\xi}|b(\xi)|^{2}\Big)^{2}=p^{7}|B|^{2}.

Now let us estimate
\Sigma_{2}
, which involves the exceptional isotropic lines, where the kernel is constant along the fiber. Note that, if
\widetilde{Q}_{U}
is anisotropic, then there are no such exceptional directions and
\Sigma_{2}=0
. Otherwise, there are exactly two isotropic lines, denoted by
\mathfrak{L}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{L}_{2}
, for the nondegenerate binary quadratic form
\widetilde{Q}_{U}
. Hence

\Sigma_{2}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2}\sum_{0\neq\zeta\in\mathfrak{L}_{i}}\sum_{\tau\in\mathbb{F}}\Big|\sum_{\xi\in\mathfrak{L}_{i}}b(\xi)b(\zeta-\xi)K_{\tau}(\xi,\zeta)\Big|^{2}.

On
\mathfrak{L}:=\mathfrak{L}_{i}

(i=1,2)
, the
kernel
K_{\tau}(\xi,\zeta)
is independent of
\zeta
and
\xi
and equals

K_{0}(\tau):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}\\
\tau-\rho\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}\end{subarray}}\chi\left(-\frac{j}{\rho}-\frac{j}{\tau-\rho}\right).

Therefore,

\sum_{0\neq\zeta\in\mathfrak{L}}\sum_{\tau\in\mathbb{F}}\Big|\sum_{\xi\in\mathfrak{L}}b(\xi)b(\zeta-\xi)K_{\tau}(\xi,\zeta)\Big|^{2}=\sum_{\tau\in\mathbb{F}}|K_{0}(\tau)|^{2}\sum_{0\neq\zeta\in{\mathfrak{L}}}\Big|\sum_{\xi\in{\mathfrak{L}}}b(\xi)b(\zeta-\xi)\Big|^{2},

Writing

g(\rho):=1_{\mathbb{F}^{\times}}(\rho)\chi\left(-\frac{j}{\rho}\right),

one sees that
K_{0}=g*g
as a convolution on the additive group of
\mathbb{F}
. By Plancherel’s theorem on
\mathbb{F}
,

\sum_{\tau\in\mathbb{F}}|K_{0}(\tau)|^{2}=p^{-1}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}}|\widehat{g}(\lambda)|^{4}.

By Lemma
2.11
,

|\widehat{g}(\lambda)|=\Bigg|\sum_{\rho\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}}\chi\left(-\frac{j}{\rho}-\lambda\rho\right)\Bigg|\lesssim p^{1/2}.

So
\sum_{\tau\in\mathbb{F}}|K_{0}(\tau)|^{2}\lesssim p^{2}
.

Let
b_{\mathfrak{L}}
be the restriction of
b
to the line
{\mathfrak{L}}
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{\zeta\in{\mathfrak{L}}}\Bigg|\sum_{\xi\in{\mathfrak{L}}}b(\xi)b(\zeta-\xi)\Bigg|^{2}=\|b_{\mathfrak{L}}*b_{\mathfrak{L}}\|_{L^{2}({\mathfrak{L}})}^{2}\leq\|b_{\mathfrak{L}}\|_{L^{1}({\mathfrak{L}})}^{2}\|b_{\mathfrak{L}}\|_{L^{2}({\mathfrak{L}})}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq p\|b_{\mathfrak{L}}\|_{L^{2}({\mathfrak{L}})}^{4}\leq p\Big(\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{F}^{2}}|b(\xi)|^{2}\Big)^{2}=p^{5}|B|^{2},

where we have used Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, the fact that
{\mathfrak{L}}
has
p
points, and Plancherel’s theorem. It follows that
\Sigma_{2}\lesssim p^{7}|B|^{2}
and then (
14
) holds. The proof is completed.
∎

5.2
Estimates from the isotropic-normal rich-plane packets

We next treat isotropic normals. Here a Witt coordinate system exposes the degenerate direction of the plane.

Proof of Proposition
5.3
.

Similarly as before, we assume without loss of generality that
z=0
, and
\pi
is a linear plane.

Denote by
e
a non-zero normal vector of
\pi
with
Q_{3}(e)=0
.
Choose a Witt basis
e,e^{\prime},e^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{F}^{3}
and a parameter
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}
such that

e\cdot e=e^{\prime}\cdot e^{\prime}=0,\qquad e\cdot e^{\prime}=1,\qquad e\cdot e^{\prime\prime}=e^{\prime}\cdot e^{\prime\prime}=0,\qquad e^{\prime\prime}\cdot e^{\prime\prime}=\lambda.

Every horizontal element
x\in\mathbb{F}^{3}
has a unique expression

x=ue+ve^{\prime}+we^{\prime\prime},\qquad Q_{3}(x)=2uv+\lambda w^{2}.

The plane
\pi
with normal
e
is

\pi=e^{\perp}=\{ue+we^{\prime\prime}:\,u,w\in\mathbb{F}\}.

We use coordinates
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
, dual to
(u,v,w)
, by writing a horizontal frequency as

\xi=\alpha e+\beta e^{\prime}+\lambda^{-1}\gamma e^{\prime\prime}.

Then

x\cdot\xi=v\alpha+u\beta+w\gamma,\qquad Q_{3}(\xi)=2\alpha\beta+\lambda^{-1}\gamma^{2}.

For
A\subseteq e^{\perp}
, define

A_{w}:=\{u\in\mathbb{F}:ue+we^{\prime\prime}\in A\},

and set the density and balanced function by

\rho_{A}(w):=\frac{|A_{w}|}{p},\qquad f_{w}(u):=\mathbf{1}_{A_{w}}(u)-\rho_{A}(w).

Then
\sum_{u}f_{w}(u)=0
, and

V_{A}:=\sum_{w,u\in\mathbb{F}}|f_{w}(u)|^{2}=\sum_{w\in\mathbb{F}}\left(|A_{w}|-\frac{|A_{w}|^{2}}{p}\right)\leq|A|.

(16)

Since
A\subseteq e^{\perp}
, one has
1_{A}(ue+ve^{\prime}+we^{\prime\prime})=0
unless
v=0
. In view of Lemma
2.17
,

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{A}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{0\}}}(\xi,s)

\displaystyle=\widehat{1_{A}}(\xi)=\widehat{1_{A}}(\alpha e+\beta e^{\prime}+\lambda^{-1}\gamma e^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{u,v,w\in\mathbb{F}}1_{A}(ue+ve^{\prime}+we^{\prime\prime})\chi(-(u\beta+v\alpha+w\gamma))

\displaystyle=\sum_{u,w\in\mathbb{F}}1_{A}(ue+we^{\prime\prime})\chi(-u\beta-w\gamma):=\mathcal{F}_{A}(\beta,\gamma)

(17)

for any
(\xi,s)\in\mathbb{F}^{3}\times\mathbb{F}
. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{A}(u,v,w,t):=\big((\mathbf{1}_{A}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{0\}})\ast\widetilde{K}\big)(x,t)=p^{-4}\sum\limits_{\xi\in\mathbb{F}^{3}}\sum\limits_{s\in\mathbb{F}}\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{A}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{0\}}}(\xi,s)\widehat{\widetilde{K}}(\xi,s)\chi(x\cdot\xi+ts)

\displaystyle=p^{-4}\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma,s\in\mathbb{F}}\mathcal{F}_{A}(\beta,\gamma)\widehat{\widetilde{K}}(\alpha e+\beta e^{\prime}+\lambda^{-1}\gamma e^{\prime\prime},s)\chi(v\alpha+u\beta+w\gamma+ts).

Recall Lemma
2.15
that

\widehat{\widetilde{K}}(\alpha e+\beta e^{\prime}+\lambda^{-1}\gamma e^{\prime\prime},s)=\frac{p^{3}}{p^{2}-1}\mathbf{1}_{\{2\alpha\beta+\lambda^{-1}\gamma^{2}+s^{2}=j\}}-\frac{p^{2}}{p^{2}-1}.

Denote
n_{A}(w):=|A_{w}|
, then

\mathcal{F}_{A}(0,\gamma)=\sum_{w\in\mathbb{F}}\Big(\sum\limits_{u\in\mathbb{F}}1_{A}(ue+we^{\prime\prime})\Big)\chi(-w\gamma)=\widehat{n_{A}}(\gamma).

In the expression for
\mathcal{T}_{A}
, The spherical part of
\widehat{\widetilde{K}}
with
\beta=0
contributes

p^{-4}\sum_{\alpha,\gamma,s\in\mathbb{F}}\widehat{n_{A}}(\gamma)\cdot\frac{p^{3}}{p^{2}-1}\mathbf{1}_{\{\lambda^{-1}\gamma^{2}+s^{2}=j\}}\cdot\chi(v\alpha+w\gamma+ts)=\frac{\mathbf{1}_{\{v=0\}}}{p^{2}-1}\Sigma_{n_{A}}(w,t):=\mathcal{P}_{A}(u,v,w,t),

where

\Sigma_{n_{A}}(w,t):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma,s\in\mathbb{F}\\
\lambda^{-1}\gamma^{2}+s^{2}=j\end{subarray}}\widehat{n_{A}}(\gamma)\chi(w\gamma+ts).

The spherical part of
\widehat{\widetilde{K}}
with

\beta\neq 0
has the unique solution
\alpha=\frac{j-\lambda^{-1}\gamma^{2}-s^{2}}{2\beta}
, and hence contributes

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p(p^{2}-1)}\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}}\sum_{\gamma,s\in\mathbb{F}}\mathcal{F}_{A}(\beta,\gamma)\chi\!\left(u\beta+w\gamma+ts+\frac{v(j-\lambda^{-1}\gamma^{2}-s^{2})}{2\beta}\right):=\mathcal{R}_{A}(u,v,w,t).

Finally, the constant part of
\widehat{\widetilde{K}}
gives

\displaystyle-\frac{p^{2}}{p^{2}-1}\mathbf{1}_{\{v=0\}}\mathbf{1}_{\{t=0\}}\left(p^{-2}\sum_{\beta,\gamma}\mathcal{F}_{A}(\beta,\gamma)\chi(u\beta+w\gamma)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=-\frac{p^{2}}{p^{2}-1}\mathbf{1}_{\{v=0\}}\mathbf{1}_{\{t=0\}}\mathbf{1}_{A}(ue+we^{\prime\prime}):=-\mathcal{L}_{A}(u,v,w,t),

in view of Fourier inversion on
\mathbb{F}^{2}
according to (
17
). This gives the decomposition

\mathcal{T}_{A}=\mathcal{P}_{A}+\mathcal{R}_{A}-\mathcal{L}_{A}.

The decomposition reduces the packet estimate to three separate bounds. We claim that

\|\mathcal{P}_{A}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{4}\lesssim p^{-1}|A|^{2},

(18)

\|\mathcal{L}_{A}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{4}\lesssim|A|,

(19)

\|\mathcal{R}_{A}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{4}\lesssim|A|+p^{-1}|A|^{2}.

(20)

Then Proposition
5.3
follows immediately by adding the above three upper bounds together.

For (
19
), the term
\mathcal{L}_{A}
is supported on points in
A
with
v=0
and
t=0
, and is bounded by
O(1)
, leading to
\|\mathcal{L}_{A}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{4}\lesssim|A|
.

Now it remains to prove (
18
) and (
20
). They will be proved in the two subsequent lemmas. Then the proof of Proposition
5.3
will be completed.
∎

The first lemma controls the
\beta=0
spherical contribution by reducing it to additive energy on a plane conic.

Lemma 5.4
.

With notations as above, we have

\|\mathcal{P}_{A}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{4}\lesssim p^{-1}|A|^{2}.

Proof.

The term
\mathcal{P}_{A}
is supported on
v=0
and is independent of
u
, so

\|\mathcal{P}_{A}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{4}=\frac{p}{(p^{2}-1)^{4}}\|\Sigma_{n_{A}}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{2})}^{4}.

Let
C_{j,\lambda}:=\{(\gamma,s):\lambda^{-1}\gamma^{2}+s^{2}=j\}
, and denote
a(\gamma,s):=\widehat{n_{A}}(\gamma)
. The fourth power of
the extension is

\displaystyle\|\Sigma_{n_{A}}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{2})}^{4}=\sum\limits_{\eta\in\mathbb{F}^{2}}\Bigg|\sum\limits_{x\in C_{j,\lambda}}a(x)\chi(\eta\cdot x)\Bigg|^{4}

\displaystyle\qquad=p^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in C_{j,\lambda}\\
x+y=x^{\prime}+y^{\prime}\end{subarray}}a(x)a(y)\overline{a(x^{\prime})a(y^{\prime})}=p^{2}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{F}^{2}}\Bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in C_{j,\lambda}\\
x+y=m\end{subarray}}a(x)a(y)\Bigg|^{2}.

For
m\neq 0
, the equations
x\in C_{j,\lambda}
and
m-x\in C_{j,\lambda}

cut the conic by a line, hence give at most two points. Consequently,

\Bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in C_{j,\lambda}\\
x+y=m\end{subarray}}a(x)a(y)\Bigg|^{2}\lesssim\max\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in C_{j,\lambda}\\
x+y=m\end{subarray}}|a(x)a(y)|^{2}\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in C_{j,\lambda}\\
x+y=m\end{subarray}}|a(x)|^{2}|a(y)|^{2}.

and then

\sum_{0\neq m\in\mathbb{F}^{2}}\Bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in C_{j,\lambda}\\
x+y=m\end{subarray}}a(x)a(y)\Bigg|^{2}\lesssim\sum_{m\in\mathbb{F}^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in C_{j,\lambda}\\
x+y=m\end{subarray}}|a(x)|^{2}|a(y)|^{2}=\Bigg(\sum_{x\in C_{j,\lambda}}|a(x)|^{2}\Bigg)^{2}.

For
m=0
, Cauchy’s inequality gives

\Bigg|\sum_{x\in C_{j,\lambda}}a(x)a(-x)\Bigg|^{2}\leq\Bigg(\sum_{x\in C_{j,\lambda}}|a(x)|^{2}\Bigg)^{2}.

For each
\gamma
there are at most two values of
s
with

\lambda^{-1}\gamma^{2}+s^{2}=j
. Together with Plancherel’s theorem, one gets

\sum_{x\in C_{j,\lambda}}|a(x)|^{2}=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma,s\in\mathbb{F}\\
\lambda^{-1}\gamma^{2}+s^{2}=j\end{subarray}}|\widehat{n_{A}}(\gamma)|^{2}\lesssim\sum_{\gamma\in\mathbb{F}}|\widehat{n_{A}}(\gamma)|^{2}=p\sum_{w\in\mathbb{F}}|A_{w}|^{2}.

In view of
0\leq|A_{w}|\leq p
, the above discussions lead to

\|\mathcal{P}_{A}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{4}\lesssim p^{-3}\left(\sum_{w\in\mathbb{F}}|A_{w}|^{2}\right)^{2}\leq p^{-3}\left(p\sum_{w\in\mathbb{F}}|A_{w}|\right)^{2}=p^{-1}|A|^{2}.

In particular, the formula (
18
) holds.
∎

The second lemma controls the remaining balanced contribution in terms of the fiber variance
V_{A}\leq|A|
.

Lemma 5.5
.

With notations as previously, we have

\|\mathcal{R}_{A}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{4}\lesssim|A|+p^{-1}|A|^{2}.

Proof.

Recall (
16
), it is sufficient to show that

\|\mathcal{R}_{A}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{4}\lesssim V_{A}+p^{-1}V_{A}^{2}.

Note that, when
\beta\neq 0

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{A}(\beta,\gamma)

\displaystyle=\sum_{w\in\mathbb{F}}\Big(\sum\limits_{u\in\mathbb{F}}1_{A}(ue+we^{\prime\prime})\chi(-u\beta)\Big)\chi(-w\gamma)=\sum\limits_{w\in\mathbb{F}}\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{A_{w}}}(\beta)\chi(-w\gamma)

\displaystyle=\sum_{w\in\mathbb{F}}\widehat{f}_{w}(\beta)\chi(-w\gamma)=\sum_{w_{0}\in\mathbb{F}}\sum\limits_{u_{0}\in\mathbb{F}}f_{w_{0}}(u_{0})\chi(-\beta u_{0})\chi(-w_{0}\gamma).

One obtains that

\mathcal{R}_{A}(u,v,w,t)=\frac{1}{p(p^{2}-1)}\sum_{w_{0},u_{0}\in\mathbb{F}}f_{w_{0}}(u_{0})\mathfrak{K}_{v}(u-u_{0},w-w_{0},t),

(21)

where

\mathfrak{K}_{v}(a,b,t):=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}}\sum_{\gamma,s\in\mathbb{F}}\chi\!\left(a\beta+b\gamma+ts+\frac{v(j-\lambda^{-1}\gamma^{2}-s^{2})}{2\beta}\right),\qquad a,b,t,v\in\mathbb{F}.

When
v=0

\mathfrak{K}_{0}(a,b,t)=p^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\{b=0\}}\mathbf{1}_{\{t=0\}}(q\mathbf{1}_{\{a=0\}}-1).

Since
\sum_{u_{0}\in\mathbb{F}}f_{w}(u_{0})=0
, we deduce that

\mathcal{R}_{A}(u,0,w,t)=\frac{p^{2}}{p^{2}-1}f_{w}(u)\mathbf{1}_{\{t=0\}}.

(22)

Hence,

\sum\limits_{u,w,t\in\mathbb{F}}|\mathcal{R}_{A}(u,0,w,t)|^{4}\lesssim\sum_{w,u\in\mathbb{F}}|f_{w}(u)|^{4}=V_{A}.

Now, to get the aimed upper bound of
\|\mathcal{R}_{A}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{4}
, it is sufficient to prove that

\Sigma:=\sum\limits_{u,w,t\in\mathbb{F}}\sum\limits_{v\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}}|\mathcal{R}_{A}(u,v,w,t)|^{4}\lesssim p^{-1}V_{A}^{2}.

(23)

For
v\neq 0
, we use the standard Gauss sum identity in Lemma
2.9

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}}\chi(Ax^{2}+Bx)=\eta(A)G_{\eta}\,\chi\!\left(-\frac{B^{2}}{4A}\right),\qquad A\neq 0,

where
\eta

.
It follows that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{K}_{v}(a,b,t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}}\chi(a\beta+\frac{vj}{2\beta})\sum_{\gamma\in\mathbb{F}}\chi\!\left(b\gamma-\frac{v\gamma^{2}}{2\beta\lambda}\right)\sum_{s\in\mathbb{F}}\chi\!\left(ts-\frac{vs^{2}}{2\beta}\right)

\displaystyle=G_{\eta}^{2}\sum\limits_{\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}}\chi(a\beta+\frac{vj}{2\beta})\cdot\eta\big(-\frac{v}{2\beta\lambda}\big)\chi\Big(\frac{b^{2}\beta\lambda}{2v}\Big)\cdot\eta\big(-\frac{v}{2\beta}\big)\chi\Big(\frac{t^{2}\beta}{2v}\Big)

\displaystyle=\eta\big(-\frac{1}{\lambda}\big)p\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}}\chi\!\left(\beta\left(a+\frac{b^{2}\lambda+t^{2}}{2v}\right)+\frac{vj}{2\beta}\right).

(24)

Insert the above expression into the definition of
\mathcal{R}_{A}
. For its fourth moment expansion, we use the variables
z_{i}=(u_{i},w_{i})

(i=1,2,3,4)
for
(u_{0},w_{0})

\beta_{i}

p

Next, we analyze the oscillations of the additive character.
For

\Delta_{0}=\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}-\beta_{3}-\beta_{4},\qquad\Delta_{-1}=\beta_{1}^{-1}+\beta_{2}^{-1}-\beta_{3}^{-1}-\beta_{4}^{-1},

The remaining Kloosterman form is organized by pair variables so that the spectral identity from the preliminaries applies. We group the variables into ordered pairs

\Sigma=p^{-5}\sum_{\sigma,\ell\in\mathbb{F}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{F}\\

, and the sum is split into two parts:
\Sigma_{1}
sums over the terms with
\sigma\neq 0
and
\Sigma_{0}
sums over those with
\sigma=0

First consider
\sigma\neq 0
. We claim that, for fixed

, the number of non-vanishing summands in (

. Indeed, the equations

\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}=\sigma,\quad\rho\beta_{1}^{2}-\rho\sigma\beta_{1}+\sigma=0,

so there are at most

when

and

By Plancherel’s theorem on
\mathbb{F}^{2}
and the preceding fiber bound,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{F}\\
\xi\eta=c\end{subarray}}\left|\widehat{\mathfrak{m}_{A}^{\sigma,\ell}}(\xi,\eta)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{F}}\left|\widehat{\mathfrak{m}_{A}^{\sigma,\ell}}(\xi,\eta)\right|^{2}=p^{2}\sum_{\rho,\omega\in\mathbb{F}}\left|\mathfrak{m}_{A}^{\sigma,\ell}(\rho,\omega)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4p^{2}\sum\limits_{\rho,\omega\in\mathbb{F}}\max\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi=(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},w_{1},w_{2})\\
\delta_{0}(\pi)=\sigma,\;\delta_{1}(\pi)=\ell\\
\delta_{-1}(\pi)=\rho,\;\delta_{2}(\pi)=\omega\end{subarray}}|\widehat{f}_{w_{1}}(\beta_{1})|^{2}|\widehat{f}_{w_{2}}(\beta_{2})|^{2}.

\displaystyle\lesssim p^{2}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in\mathbb{F}^{\times},\;w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathbb{F}\\
\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}=\sigma,\;\beta_{1}w_{1}+\beta_{2}w_{2}=\ell\end{subarray}}|\widehat{f}_{w_{1}}(\beta_{1})|^{2}|\widehat{f}_{w_{2}}(\beta_{2})|^{2}.

Summing in
\sigma\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}
and
\ell\in\mathbb{F}

\displaystyle\Sigma_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim p^{2}\sum_{\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}}\sum_{w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathbb{F}}|\widehat{f}_{w_{1}}(\beta_{1})|^{2}|\widehat{f}_{w_{2}}(\beta_{2})|^{2}

\displaystyle=p^{2}\Big(\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}}\sum_{w\in\mathbb{F}}|\widehat{f}_{w}(\beta)|^{2}\Big)^{2}\leq p^{2}\Big(p\sum\limits_{w\in\mathbb{F}}\sum\limits_{u\in\mathbb{F}}|f_{w}(u)|^{2}\Big)^{2}\leq p^{4}V_{A}^{2}.

It remains to treat
\sigma=0
. First consider
\ell=0
. Then the equation
\beta_{2}=-\beta_{1}
and
\beta_{1}w_{1}+\beta_{2}w_{2}=0
forces
w_{1}=w_{2}
, and then
\delta_{-1}(\pi)=\delta_{2}(\pi)=0
. Thus,
\mathfrak{m}_{A}^{0,0}
is supported at
(\rho,\omega)=(0,0)
, and

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}_{A}^{0,0}(0,0)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}}\sum_{w\in\mathbb{F}}\widehat{f}_{w}(\beta)\widehat{f}_{w}(-\beta)=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}}\sum_{w\in\mathbb{F}}|\widehat{f}_{w}(\beta)|^{2}\leq p\sum\limits_{w\in\mathbb{F}}\sum\limits_{u\in\mathbb{F}}|f_{w}(u)|^{2}=pV_{A},

in view of the fact that
f_{w}
is real-valued. Consequently, one sees similarly as previously that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{F}\\
\xi\eta=c\end{subarray}}\left|\widehat{\mathfrak{m}_{A}^{0,0}}(\xi,\eta)\right|^{2}\leq\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}}p^{2}V_{A}^{2}\leq p^{3}V_{A}^{2}\leq p^{4}V_{A}^{2}.

Now assume
\sigma=0
and
\ell\neq 0
. Write
\beta_{1}=\beta
and
\beta_{2}=-\beta
. In this situation, one has

\beta(w_{1}-w_{2})=\ell,\quad\rho=0,\quad\omega=\beta w_{1}^{2}-\beta w_{2}^{2}=\ell(w_{1}+w_{2}).

So
w_{1}\neq w_{2}
and
\beta=\ell/(w_{1}-w_{2})
. For
\xi\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathfrak{m}_{A}^{0,\ell}}\left(\xi,\frac{c}{\xi}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathbb{F}\\
w_{1}\neq w_{2}\end{subarray}}\widehat{f}_{w_{1}}\left(\frac{\ell}{w_{1}-w_{2}}\right)\widehat{f}_{w_{2}}\left(-\frac{\ell}{w_{1}-w_{2}}\right)\chi\left(-\frac{c\ell}{\xi}(w_{1}+w_{2})\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{s\in\mathbb{F}}D_{\ell}(s)\chi\big(-\frac{c\ell}{\xi}s\big),

where

D_{\ell}(s):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w_{1}+w_{2}=s\\
w_{1}\neq w_{2}\end{subarray}}\widehat{f}_{w_{1}}\left(\frac{\ell}{w_{1}-w_{2}}\right)\widehat{f}_{w_{2}}\left(-\frac{\ell}{w_{1}-w_{2}}\right),\qquad s\in\mathbb{F}.

It follows by Plancherel’s theorem in
s
and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality inside each fiber
w_{1}+w_{2}=s
that

\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}}\left|\widehat{\mathfrak{m}_{A}^{0,\ell}}\left(\xi,\frac{c}{\xi}\right)\right|^{2}\leq p\sum_{s\in\mathbb{F}}|D_{\ell}(s)|^{2}\leq p^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathbb{F}\\
w_{1}\neq w_{2}\end{subarray}}\Bigg|\widehat{f}_{w_{1}}\left(\frac{\ell}{w_{1}-w_{2}}\right)\Bigg|^{2}\,\Bigg|\widehat{f}_{w_{2}}\left(-\frac{\ell}{w_{1}-w_{2}}\right)\Bigg|^{2}.

Therefore, with the change of variables
r=\frac{\ell}{w_{1}-w_{2}}
for fixed
(w_{1},w_{2})
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{F}\\
\xi\eta=c\end{subarray}}\left|\widehat{\mathfrak{m}_{A}^{0,\ell}}\left(\xi,\frac{c}{\xi}\right)\right|^{2}\leq p^{2}\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathbb{F}\\
w_{1}\neq w_{2}\end{subarray}}\Bigg|\widehat{f}_{w_{1}}\left(\frac{\ell}{w_{1}-w_{2}}\right)\Bigg|^{2}\,\Bigg|\widehat{f}_{w_{2}}\left(-\frac{\ell}{w_{1}-w_{2}}\right)\Bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq p^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathbb{F}\\
w_{1}\neq w_{2}\end{subarray}}\sum_{r\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}}|\widehat{f}_{w_{1}}(r)|^{2}|\widehat{f}_{w_{2}}(-r)|^{2}\leq p^{2}\sum_{r\in\mathbb{F}}\Big(\sum_{w\in\mathbb{F}}|\widehat{f}_{w}(r)|^{2}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq p^{2}\Big(\sum_{r,w\in\mathbb{F}}|\widehat{f}_{w}(r)|^{2}\Big)^{2}=p^{2}(pV_{A})^{2}=p^{4}V_{A}^{2}.

Now we have proved that
\Sigma_{2}\lesssim p^{4}V_{A}^{2}
.

Finally, we have
\Sigma=p^{-5}(\Sigma_{1}+\Sigma_{0})\lesssim p^{-1}V_{A}^{2}
, i.e., (
23
) holds. The lemma then follows. And the proof of Proposition
5.3
is completed.
∎

6
Estimates from the rich-line-and-poor-plane pieces.

In this section, we aim at the following estimate.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
K_{\Pi}
,
K_{L}
be parameters satisfying
1\leq K_{\Pi}\leq p^{2}
,
1\leq K_{L}\leq p
and
K_{L}\leq K_{\Pi}
. Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{3}
and
z\in\mathbb{F}
. Suppose
A_{1}
is the rich-line-and-poor-plane-piece produced by the stopping-time decomposition of
A
from Lemma
2.21
. Then

\displaystyle\big\|(\mathbf{1}_{A_{1}}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{z\}})*\widetilde{K}\big\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{4}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{|A|^{4}}{pK_{L}^{4}}+\frac{|A|^{4}}{p^{2}K_{L}^{2}}+\frac{|A|^{2}K_{\Pi}^{2}}{p^{2}K_{L}^{2}}+\frac{|A|^{2}K_{\Pi}}{pK_{L}}+\frac{|A|K_{\Pi}^{3}}{p^{3}}.

For simplicity, we will use the notation

A_{1}=\bigsqcup_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}B_{\ell}

instead of
A_{1}=\bigsqcup_{i\in I_{1}}A_{1,i}
as in Lemma
2.21
. One has
|\mathcal{L}|\leq|A|/K_{L}
. Each packet
B_{\ell}
is contained in a distinct affine line

\ell=b_{\ell}+\mathbb{F}v_{\ell}
with rich-line property
|B_{\ell}|\geq K_{L}
. The total cardinality satisfies
\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}|B_{\ell}|=|A_{1}|\leq|A|
. The poor-plane property says that

\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{3}\\
\text{ affine plane}\end{subarray}}|A_{1}\cap\pi|<K_{\Pi}.

For each selected line, define the density and the balanced function of the line packet by

\beta_{\ell}:=\frac{|B_{\ell}|}{p},\qquad f_{\ell}:=\mathbf{1}_{B_{\ell}}-\beta_{\ell}\mathbf{1}_{\ell}.

We split

\mathbf{1}_{A_{1}}=\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}\beta_{\ell}\mathbf{1}_{\ell}+\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}f_{\ell}.

Correspondingly,

(\mathbf{1}_{A_{1}}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{0\}})*\widetilde{K}=G_{\rm avg}+G_{\rm bal},

where

G_{\rm avg}:=\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}\beta_{\ell}(\mathbf{1}_{\ell}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{0\}})*\widetilde{K},\qquad G_{\rm bal}:=\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}(f_{\ell}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{0\}})*\widetilde{K}.

Next, we further split the line directions according to the ternary quadratic form

Q_{3}
:

\mathcal{L}_{\rm iso}:=\{\ell\in\mathcal{L}:Q_{3}(v_{\ell})=0\},\qquad\mathcal{L}_{\rm an}:=\{\ell\in\mathcal{L}:Q_{3}(v_{\ell})\neq 0\}.

Put

f_{\rm iso}:=\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\rm iso}}f_{\ell},\qquad f_{\rm an}:=\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\rm an}}f_{\ell},

and

G_{\rm iso}:=(f_{\rm iso}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{0\}})*\widetilde{K},\qquad G_{\rm an}:=(f_{\rm an}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{0\}})*\widetilde{K}.

Thus,
G_{\rm bal}=G_{\rm iso}+G_{\rm an}
.

The decomposition above reduces Proposition 6.1 to three estimates: the full-line average, the anisotropic balanced part, and the isotropic balanced part.
The first estimate controls the averaged full-line contribution.

Proposition 6.2
.

The following holds:

\|G_{\rm avg}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{4}\lesssim\frac{|A|^{3}}{p^{3}}+\frac{|A|^{4}}{p^{4}}+\frac{|A|K_{\Pi}^{3}}{p^{3}}.

The next estimate treats the balanced contribution from packets carried by anisotropic directions.

Proposition 6.3
.

The following holds:

\|G_{\rm an}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{4}\lesssim\frac{|A|^{4}}{pK_{L}^{4}}+\frac{|A|^{4}}{p^{2}K_{L}^{2}}+\frac{|A|^{2}K_{\Pi}^{2}}{p^{2}K_{L}^{2}}+\frac{|A|^{2}}{p}.

The final component is the balanced contribution from isotropic directions, where parallel isotropic packets require a sharper reflection count.

Proposition 6.4
.

The following holds:

\|G_{\rm iso}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{4}\lesssim\frac{|A|^{4}}{pK_{L}^{4}}+\frac{|A|^{4}}{p^{2}K_{L}^{2}}+\frac{|A|^{2}K_{\Pi}}{pK_{L}}+\frac{|A|K_{\Pi}}{p}.

Once these three estimates are available, the remaining terms are absorbed using the standing threshold range.

Proof of Proposition
6.1
.

Similar as previously, we may assume that
z=0
. If
A_{1}=\emptyset
, then the
L^{4}
-norm equals zero. Otherwise
|A|\geq|A_{1}|\geq K_{L}
. Also recall the range of
K_{\Pi}
and
K_{L}
in (
4
). The average-line terms
|A|^{3}/p^{3}
and
|A|^{4}/p^{4}
, the balanced term
|A|^{2}/p
, and the isotropic term
|A|K_{\Pi}/p
are absorbed by the five displayed terms. The conclusion follows by combining the above three propositions.
∎

6.1
Basic properties and lemmas

For the balanced function
f_{\ell}
, it is not hard to check that

\sum_{x\in\ell}f_{\ell}(x)=0,\quad\|f_{\ell}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}\leq 2|B_{\ell}|,\quad\|f_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}^{2}=|B_{\ell}|-\frac{|B_{\ell}|^{2}}{p}\leq|B_{\ell}|,\quad|f_{\ell}|\leq 1.

For any selected subfamily
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{L}
, we define

f_{\mathcal{M}}:=\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{M}}f_{\ell}.

We first record the uniform size and plane-concentration bounds for arbitrary subfamilies of the selected line packets.

Lemma 6.5
.

For
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{L}
, one has
|\mathcal{M}|\leq|A|/K_{L}
,

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}^{3}}f_{\mathcal{M}}(x)=0,\qquad\|f_{\mathcal{M}}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}\lesssim|A|,\qquad\|f_{\mathcal{M}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}^{2}\lesssim|A|+\frac{|A|^{2}}{K_{L}^{2}}.

Moreover, for every affine plane
\pi\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{3}
,

\sum_{x\in\pi}|f_{\mathcal{M}}(x)|^{2}\lesssim K_{\Pi}+\frac{|A|^{2}}{K_{L}^{2}}.

(28)

Furthermore,

\sum\limits_{\ell\in\mathcal{M}}|B_{\ell}|^{1/2}\leq\frac{|A|}{K_{L}^{1/2}}

(29)

Proof.

The cardinality of
\mathcal{M}
does not exceed that of
\mathcal{L}
, which is at most
|A|/K_{L}
. The mean-zero identity follows from the mean-zero identity on each selected line. The
L^{1}
-bound follows from

\|f_{\mathcal{M}}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}\leq\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{M}}\|f_{\ell}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}\leq\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{M}}2|B_{\ell}|\leq 2|A|.

Moreover,
|\mathcal{M}|\leq|\mathcal{L}|\leq|A|/K_{L}
. For the
L^{2}
-bound, expand

\|f_{\mathcal{M}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}^{2}=\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{M}}\left\|f_{\ell}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}^{2}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}\\
\ell\neq\ell^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}^{3}}f_{\ell}(x)f_{\ell^{\prime}}(x).

The diagonal contribution is at most
\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{M}}|B_{\ell}|\leq|A|
. If two selected lines are distinct, then they are disjoint or meet in one point, and the corresponding cross inner product is
O(1)
. Hence the cross contribution is
O(|\mathcal{M}|^{2})=O(|\mathcal{L}|^{2})=O\big((|A|/K_{L})^{2}\big)
. So

\|f_{\mathcal{M}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}^{2}\lesssim|A|+\frac{|A|^{2}}{K_{L}^{2}}.

Next, write
f_{\mathcal{M}}=g_{\pi}+h_{\pi}
, where

g_{\pi}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\in\mathcal{M}\\
\ell\subseteq\pi\end{subarray}}f_{\ell},\qquad h:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\in\mathcal{M}\\
\ell\not\subseteq\pi\end{subarray}}f_{\ell}|_{\pi}

For contained lines, the poor-plane condition gives

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\in\mathcal{M}\\
\ell\subseteq\pi\end{subarray}}\|f_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}^{2}\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\in\mathcal{M}\\
\ell\subseteq\pi\end{subarray}}|B_{\ell}|\leq|A_{1}\cap\pi|<K_{\Pi}.

Similarly as before, the off-diagonal contribution is at most the square of the number of contained lines, which is
O\left((|A|/K_{L})^{2}\right)
.
On the other hand, every line not contained in
\pi
meets
\pi
in at most one point. Since
|f_{\ell}|\leq\mathbf{1}_{\ell}
,

\|h_{\pi}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}^{2}\leq\sum\limits_{x\in\pi}\Big|\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\in\mathcal{M}\\
\ell\not\subseteq\pi\end{subarray}}\mathbf{1}_{\ell}(x)\Big|^{2}=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}\\
\ell,\ell^{\prime}\not\subseteq\pi\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{x\in\pi}\mathbf{1}_{\ell}(x)\mathbf{1}_{\ell^{\prime}}(x)\leq|\mathcal{M}|^{2}\leq\frac{|A|^{2}}{K_{L}^{2}}.

Now (
28
) follows.

Finally, the rich-line property
|B_{\ell}|\geq K_{L}
implies
|B_{\ell}|^{1/2}\leq|B_{\ell}|/K_{L}^{1/2}
. Then

\sum\limits_{\ell\in\mathcal{M}}|B_{\ell}|^{1/2}\leq\sum\limits_{\ell\in\mathcal{M}}\frac{|B_{\ell}|}{K_{L}^{1/2}}\leq\frac{|A|}{K_{L}^{1/2}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

For anisotropic directions, isotropic point pairs cannot lie on the same selected line, so a simple quadratic-fiber count suffices.

Lemma 6.6
.

We have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\mathbb{F}^{3}\\
x\neq y\\
Q_{3}(x-y)=0\end{subarray}}|f_{\rm an}(x)f_{\rm an}(y)|\lesssim\frac{|A|^{2}}{K_{L}},

Proof.

Expanding the left-hand side, obtains

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}_{\rm an}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\ell,\,y\in\ell^{\prime}\\
x\neq y\\
Q_{3}(x-y)=0\end{subarray}}|f_{\ell}(x)f_{\ell^{\prime}}(y)|.

If two points lie on the same selected anisotropic line
\ell=b+\mathbb{F}v
, then
Q_{3}(x-y)=0
forces
x=y
, since
Q_{3}(v)\neq 0
. So this situation cannot happen. If
\ell\neq\ell^{\prime}=c+\mathbb{F}w
, with
Q_{3}(w)\neq 0
, then for fixed
x\in\ell
the equation
Q_{3}(x-c-tw)=0
is a genuine quadratic in
t
, hence has at most two solutions in
y
. Therefore, we get further upper bounds

\lesssim\sum\limits_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}_{\rm an}}\sum\limits_{x\in\ell}|f_{\ell}(x)|\leq|\mathcal{L}_{\rm an}|\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\rm an}}\|f_{\ell}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}\lesssim|\mathcal{L}_{\rm an}|\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\rm an}}|B_{\ell}|\leq\frac{|A|^{2}}{K_{L}}.

∎

For isotropic directions, same-line pairs create an additional diagonal contribution, while distinct-line pairs remain controlled by line richness.

Lemma 6.7
.

We have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\mathbb{F}^{3}\\
x\neq y\\
Q_{3}(x-y)=0\end{subarray}}|f_{\rm iso}(x)f_{\rm iso}(y)|\lesssim p|A|+\frac{|A|^{2}}{K_{L}},

Proof.

Expanding the left-hand side, obtains

\sum\limits_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}_{\rm iso}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\ell,\,y\in\ell^{\prime}\\
x\neq y\\
Q_{3}(x-y)=0\end{subarray}}|f_{\ell}(x)||f_{\ell^{\prime}}(y)|.

If
\ell=\ell^{\prime}
, then

\sum_{x,y\in\ell}|f_{\ell}(x)||f_{\ell}(y)|=\|f_{\ell}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}^{2}\lesssim|B_{\ell}|^{2}\leq p|B_{\ell}|.

So the diagonal contribution is
O\big(p\sum\nolimits_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\rm iso}}|B_{\ell}|\big)=O(p|A|)
.

Now let
\ell\neq\ell^{\prime}
. For fixed
x\in\ell
, write
\ell^{\prime}=c+\mathbb{F}w
. The equation

0=Q_{3}(x-c-tw)=Q_{3}(x-c)-2t(x-c)\cdot w

is linear in
t
, because
Q_{3}(w)=0
. If
(x-c)\cdot w\neq 0
, there is at most one solution. If
(x-c)\cdot w=0
, then the equation holds for all
t
only if
Q_{3}(x-c)=0
. In that exceptional case,
x-c
is isotropic and orthogonal to
w
, so Lemma
2.1
gives
x-c\in\mathbb{F}w
, i.e.
x\in\ell^{\prime}
. Since
\ell\neq\ell^{\prime}
, this can occur for at most one point
x\in\ell\cap\ell^{\prime}
. Recall that
|f_{\ell}|,|f_{\ell^{\prime}}|\leq 1
, a fixed ordered pair
(\ell,\ell^{\prime})
contributes
O\big(\|f_{\ell}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}+\|f_{\ell^{\prime}}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{F}^{3})})=O(|B_{\ell}|+|B_{\ell^{\prime}}|)
. Therefore the off-diagonal contribution is

\lesssim\sum_{\ell\neq\ell^{\prime}}(|B_{\ell}|+|B_{\ell^{\prime}}|)\lesssim|\mathcal{L}_{\rm iso}|\sum_{\ell}|B_{\ell}|\leq\frac{|A|^{2}}{K_{L}}.

Combining the diagonal and off-diagonal contributions proves the lemma.
∎

6.2
Estimates from the average of lines

The average term is estimated directly from the explicit kernel on a complete affine line, with separate behavior for anisotropic and isotropic directions.

Proof of Proposition
6.2
.

Split
G_{\rm avg}=G_{\rm avg}^{\rm an}+G_{\rm avg}^{\rm iso}
according to whether
Q_{3}(v_{\ell})\neq 0
or
Q_{3}(v_{\ell})=0
.

Write
\ell=b_{\ell}+\mathbb{F}v_{\ell}
, with
b_{\ell},v_{\ell}\in\mathbb{F}^{3}
. Recalling

\widetilde{K}(u,t)=\frac{1}{p^{2}-1}\sum_{\rho\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}}\chi\left(-\frac{j}{\rho}-\frac{\rho}{4}(Q_{3}(u)+t^{2})\right),

we have

\displaystyle((\mathbf{1}_{\ell}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{0\}})*\widetilde{K})(u,t)=\sum_{s\in\mathbb{F}}\widetilde{K}(u-b_{\ell}-sv_{\ell},t)

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{p^{2}-1}\sum_{\rho\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}}\chi\left(-\frac{j}{\rho}-\frac{\rho}{4}(Q_{3}(u-b_{\ell})+t^{2})\right)\sum_{s\in\mathbb{F}}\chi\left(\frac{\rho}{2}s\,(u-b_{\ell})\cdot v_{\ell}-\frac{\rho}{4}s^{2}Q_{3}(v_{\ell})\right).

(30)

First, consider the case
Q_{3}(v_{\ell})\neq 0
. By Lemma
2.11
, the above inner sum over
s
is a Gauss sum, and calculation leads to

\displaystyle((\mathbf{1}_{\ell}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{0\}})*\widetilde{K})(u,t)=\frac{\eta(-Q_{3}(v_{\ell}))G_{\eta}}{p^{2}-1}\sum\limits_{\rho\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}}\eta(\rho)\chi\Bigg(-\frac{j}{\rho}+\frac{\rho}{4}\Big(\frac{((u-b_{\ell})\cdot v_{\ell})^{2}}{Q_{3}(v_{\ell})}-Q_{3}(u-b_{\ell})-t^{2}\Big).\Bigg).

Here
G_{\eta}
is the Gauss sum of size
p^{1/2}
and
\eta
is the Legendre symbol. The remaining
\rho
-sum is a Salié-type sum with at least one non-zero coefficient, which is also of magnitude
p^{1/2}
by Lemma
2.12
. So

\|(\mathbf{1}_{\ell}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{0\}})*\widetilde{K}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}\lesssim p^{-1}.

Therefore,

\|G_{\rm avg}^{\rm an}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}\lesssim\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\rm an}}\beta_{\ell}\cdot p^{-1}\leq p^{-2}\sum\limits_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}|B_{\ell}|\leq|A|p^{-2}.

h_{\rm an}:=\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\rm an}}\beta_{\ell}\mathbf{1}_{\ell}
. By Lemma
2.18
,

\|G_{\rm avg}^{\rm an}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{2}=\|(h_{\rm an}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{0\}})*\widetilde{K}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{2}\lesssim p\|h_{\rm an}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}^{2}.

The diagonal part of
\left\|h_{\mathrm{an}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}^{2}
is

\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\rm an}}\beta_{\ell}^{2}|\ell|=p\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\rm an}}\left(\frac{|B_{\ell}|}{p}\right)^{2}\leq\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\rm an}}|B_{\ell}|\leq|A|.

The off-diagonal terms occur only at intersections of two distinct lines, and each pair contributes
O(\beta_{\ell}\beta_{\ell^{\prime}})
. Thus the off-diagonal contribution is at most

\Big(\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\rm an}}\beta_{\ell}\Big)^{2}\leq\Big(\frac{|A|}{p}\Big)^{2}.

Therefore
\|G^{\mathrm{an}}_{\mathrm{avg}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{2}\lesssim p|A|+p^{-1}|A|^{2}
, and then

\|G_{\rm avg}^{\rm an}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{4}\leq\|G_{\rm avg}^{\rm an}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{2}\|G_{\rm avg}^{\rm an}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{2}\lesssim p^{-3}|A|^{3}+p^{-5}|A|^{4}.

(31)

Second, consider the case
Q_{3}(v_{\ell})=0
. Now the inner sum over
s
in (
30
) becomes an exponential sum,
which vanishes unless
(u-b_{\ell})\cdot v_{\ell}=0
, and equals
p
otherwise. Hence
(\mathbf{1}_{\ell}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{0\}})*\widetilde{K}
is supported on the plane

{\mathcal{H}_{\ell}}:=\{u\in\mathbb{F}^{3}:\,(u-b_{\ell})\cdot v_{\ell}=0\},

and the remaining
\rho
-sum is a Kloosterman sum. Combining Lemma
2.11
, we arrive at

|((\mathbf{1}_{\ell}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{0\}})*\widetilde{K}))(u,t)|\lesssim p^{-1/2}\mathbf{1}_{{\mathcal{H}_{\ell}}}(u).

(32)

Group isotropic lines by the planes
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
, and set

w_{H}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\rm iso}\\
{\mathcal{H}_{\ell}}=\mathcal{H}\end{subarray}}\beta_{\ell},\qquad W:=\sum_{\mathcal{H}}w_{\mathcal{H}},\qquad M:=\sup_{\mathcal{H}}w_{\mathcal{H}}

for any affine plane
\mathcal{H}

, one can verify that

. Hence, for a fixed
\mathcal{H}

\ell
with

. By (

|G_{\rm avg}^{\rm iso}(u,t)|\lesssim p^{-1/2}\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\rm iso}}\beta_{\ell}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{\ell}}(u)=p^{-1/2}\sum_{\mathcal{H}}w_{\mathcal{H}}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{H}}(u).

and sum over
u\in\mathbb{F}^{3}
. The contribution of four equal planes is at most
|\mathcal{H}|\sum_{\mathcal{H}}w_{\mathcal{H}}^{4}\leq p^{2}M^{3}W\leq p^{-2}K_{\Pi}^{3}|A|
. If not all four planes are equal, then at least two distinct planes occur, and two distinct affine planes intersect in at most a line, hence in at most
p
points. Therefore the remaining contribution is at most
p\left(\sum_{\mathcal{H}}w_{\mathcal{H}}\right)^{4}=pW^{4}\leq p^{-3}|A|^{4}
.
Therefore

\|G_{\rm avg}^{\rm iso}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{4}\lesssim p^{-2}\sum\limits_{t\in\mathbb{F}}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}^{3}}\Big(\sum_{\mathcal{H}}w_{\mathcal{H}}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{H}}(u)\Big)^{4}\lesssim p^{-3}K_{\Pi}^{3}|A|+p^{-4}|A|^{4}.

(33)

Combining (
31
) and (
33
), we conclude that

\|G_{\rm avg}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{4}\lesssim p^{-3}|A|^{3}+p^{-3}K_{\Pi}^{3}|A|+p^{-4}|A|^{4}.

The proof is completed.
∎

6.3
Reduction of the balanced-line-packet-estimate to reflection-analysis

In this subsection, we reduce the estimates involving
G_{\rm bal}
to terms involving anisotropic affine reflections through a series of lemmas.

The first reduction rewrites the
L^{4}
norm as a variance over level sets of the quadratic phase
\psi_{\eta}
.

Lemma 6.8
.

For every function
h:\mathbb{F}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}
,

\|(h\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{z\}})*\widetilde{K}\|_{4}^{4}\lesssim p^{-4}\sum_{\eta\in\mathbb{F}^{3}}X_{\eta}(h)^{2},

where

X_{\eta}(h):=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}}\Bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathbb{F}^{3}\\
\psi_{\eta}(x)=\lambda\end{subarray}}h(x)\Bigg|^{2},\qquad\psi_{\eta}(x):=\eta\cdot x-\frac{1}{4}Q_{3}(x).

Proof.

By Lemma
2.20
,

\|(h\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{z\}})*\widetilde{K}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{4}\lesssim p^{-7}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}^{3}}\sum_{\tau\in\mathbb{F}}|\mathcal{D}_{\tau}(u)|^{2},

where

\mathcal{D}_{\tau}(u):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho,\sigma\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}\\
\rho-\sigma=\tau\end{subarray}}\mathcal{B}_{\rho}(u)\overline{\mathcal{B}_{\sigma}(u)},\qquad\mathcal{B}_{\rho}(u):=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}^{3}}h(x)\chi\left(-\frac{j}{\rho}-\frac{\rho}{4}Q_{3}(u-x)\right).

Set
u=2\eta
. Since
Q_{3}(2\eta-x)=4Q_{3}(\eta)-4\eta\cdot x+Q_{3}(x)
, we have

\mathcal{B}_{\rho}(2\eta)=\chi\left(-\frac{j}{\rho}-\rho Q_{3}(\eta)\right)W_{\rho}(\eta),

where

W_{\rho}(\eta):=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}^{3}}h(x)\chi(\rho\psi_{\eta}(x))=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}}\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathbb{F}^{3}\\
\psi_{\eta}(x)=\lambda\end{subarray}}h(x)\Big)\chi(\rho\lambda).

And then

D_{\tau}(2\eta)=\chi(-\tau Q_{3}(\eta))\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho,\sigma\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}\\
\rho-\sigma=\tau\end{subarray}}\chi\Big(-\frac{j}{\rho}+\frac{j}{\sigma}\Big)W_{\rho}(\eta)\overline{W_{\sigma}}(\eta).

Now

\sum_{\tau\in\mathbb{F}}|\mathcal{D}_{\tau}(2\eta)|^{2}\leq\|h_{\eta}*\widetilde{h}_{\eta}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F})}^{2},

where

h_{\eta}(\rho):=\mathbf{1}_{\rho\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}}\chi(-j/\rho)W_{\rho}(\eta),\qquad\widetilde{h}_{\eta}(\sigma):=\overline{h_{\eta}(-\sigma)}.

Young’s inequality and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality give

\|h_{\eta}*\widetilde{h}_{\eta}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F})}^{2}\leq\|h_{\eta}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{F})}^{2}\|h_{\eta}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F})}^{2}\leq p\|h_{\eta}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F})}^{4}.

Plancherel’s theorem on
\mathbb{F}
gives

\|h_{\eta}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F})}^{2}\leq\sum_{\rho\in\mathbb{F}}|W_{\rho}(\eta)|^{2}=p\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}}\Bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathbb{F}^{3}\\
\psi_{\eta}(x)=\lambda\end{subarray}}h(x)\Bigg|^{2}=pX_{\eta}(h).

Substituting this into the preceding estimate yields

\|(h\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{z\}})*\widetilde{K}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{4}\lesssim p^{-7}\sum\limits_{\eta\in\mathbb{F}^{3}}p(pX_{\eta}(h))^{2}\lesssim p^{-4}\sum_{\eta\in\mathbb{F}^{3}}X_{\eta}(h)^{2}.

∎

The next step expands this variance and isolates the only non-canceling contribution, namely the rank-one locus.

Lemma 6.9
.

Let
h:\,\mathbb{F}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a mean-zero function. It satisfies that

\sum_{\eta\in\mathbb{F}^{3}}X_{\eta}(h)^{2}\lesssim\mathcal{R}(h)+p^{3}\|h\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}^{4},

where

\mathcal{R}(h):=p\sum_{[e]\in\mathbb{P}^{2}(\mathbb{F})}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}}|H_{e,\alpha}(h)|^{2},\quad H_{e,\alpha}(h):=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{F}^{3}}h(y+ae)h(y)\chi\bigl(\alpha(2e\cdot y+aQ_{3}(e))\bigr).

Proof.

Expanding
X_{\eta}(h)
, one obtains

X_{\eta}(h)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\mathbb{F}^{3}\\
\psi_{\eta}(x)=\psi_{\eta}(y)\end{subarray}}h(x)h(y).

The condition
\psi_{\eta}(x)=\psi_{\eta}(y)
is equivalent to

\eta\cdot(x-y)=\frac{1}{4}(Q_{3}(x)-Q_{3}(y)).

Thus

\sum_{\eta\in\mathbb{F}^{3}}X_{\eta}(h)^{2}=\sum_{x,y,z,w\in\mathbb{F}^{3}}h(x)h(y)h(z)h(w)\big(N(x,y,z,w)-p\big),

where
N(x,y,z,w)
is the number of
\eta\in\mathbb{F}^{3}
solving

\eta\cdot(x-y)=\frac{1}{4}(Q_{3}(x)-Q_{3}(y)),\quad\eta\cdot(z-w)=\frac{1}{4}(Q_{3}(z)-Q_{3}(w)),

(34)

and the
(-p)
-term comes from the mean value property of
h
:

p\sum_{x,y,z,w\in\mathbb{F}^{3}}h(x)h(y)h(z)h(w)=p\Big|\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}^{3}}h(x)\Big|^{4}=0.

If
x-y
and
z-w
are linearly independent, the two displayed equations for
\eta
are independent affine-linear equations in
\mathbb{F}^{3}
, and hence have exactly
p
solutions. Therefore
N(x,y,z,w)-p=0
on the rank-two locus. If both vectors vanish, the number of solutions is
p^{3}
. If exactly one vector vanishes, or if the two non-zero vectors are linearly dependent, the number of solutions is at most
p^{2}
.

Now the one-zero rank-one terms can be bounded by

\lesssim p^{2}\sum\limits_{x\in\mathbb{F}^{3}}|h(x)|^{2}\cdot\Bigg|\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}z,w\in\mathbb{F}^{3}\\
z\neq w\end{subarray}}h(z)h(w)\Bigg|=p^{2}\|h\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}^{4},

because the mean value property shows

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}z,w\in\mathbb{F}^{3}\\
z\neq w\end{subarray}}h(z)h(w)=\Big|\sum\limits_{z\in\mathbb{F}^{3}}h(z)\Big|^{2}-\sum\limits_{z\in\mathbb{F}^{3}}|h(z)|^{2}=-\|h\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}^{2}.

The rank-zero terms can be bounded by
O(p^{3}\|h\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}^{4})
.

It remains to consider the non-zero rank-one part. In that part both

x-y
and
z-w
are non-zero and span the same projective direction. We
write this direction as
[e]\in\mathbb{P}^{2}(\mathbb{F})
, always fix a representative

e
, and write

x=y+ae,\qquad z=w+be,\qquad a,b\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}.

In this situation, the two equations in (
34
) are consistent if and only if

T:=2e\cdot(y-w)+(a-b)Q_{3}(e)=0.

The remaining terms are exactly

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(h)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{[e]\in\mathbb{P}^{2}(\mathbb{F})}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}y,w\in\mathbb{F}^{3}\\
a,b\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}\end{subarray}}h(y+ae)h(y)h(w+be)h(w)\big(p^{2}\mathbf{1}_{T=0}-p\big)

\displaystyle=p\sum_{[e]\in\mathbb{P}^{2}(\mathbb{F})}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}}\Bigg|\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{F}^{3}}h(y+ae)h(y)\chi\bigl(\alpha(2e\cdot y+aQ_{3}(e))\bigr)\Bigg|^{2}.

The proof is completed.
∎

We now split the rank-one term according to whether the projective direction is anisotropic or isotropic:

\mathcal{R}(h)=\mathcal{R}^{\prime}(h)+\mathcal{R}^{\prime\prime}(h),

where

\mathcal{R}^{\prime}(h):=p\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[e]\in\mathbb{P}^{2}(\mathbb{F})\\
Q_{3}(e)\neq 0\end{subarray}}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}}|H_{e,\alpha}(h)|^{2},\qquad\mathcal{R}^{\prime\prime}(h):=p\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[e]\in\mathbb{P}^{2}(\mathbb{F})\\
Q_{3}(e)=0\end{subarray}}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}}|H_{e,\alpha}(h)|^{2}.

Recall that
f_{\mathcal{M}}=\sum\nolimits_{\ell\in\mathcal{M}}f_{\ell}
for any selected line family
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{L}
. Also recall that
\mathfrak{R}
is the set of anisotropic affine reflections
R_{n,k}
, with
n
being a fixed representative
[n]\in\mathbb{P}^{2}(\mathbb{F})
,
k\in\mathbb{F}
, and

R_{n,k}(x)=x+\left(k-\frac{2n\cdot x}{Q_{3}(n)}\right)n,\qquad x\in\mathbb{F}^{3}.

For
h:\,\mathbb{F}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, we further define

N_{R}(h):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathbb{F}^{3}\\
x\neq Rx\end{subarray}}h(x)h(Rx).

For anisotropic rank-one directions, the rank-one term is encoded by affine reflections.

Lemma 6.10
.

Let
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{L}
. Then

\mathcal{R}^{\prime}(f_{\mathcal{M}})\lesssim p^{2}\sum_{R\in\mathfrak{R}}|N_{R}(f_{\mathcal{M}})|^{2}.

Proof.

For
e
with
Q_{3}(e)\neq 0
, we decompose
\mathbb{F}^{3}=e^{\perp}\oplus\mathbb{F}e
.
Thus every
y\in\mathbb{F}^{3}
is written uniquely as
y=c+se
with
c\in e^{\perp}
and
s\in\mathbb{F}
. For each such
c
, define

f_{c,e}(s):=f_{\mathcal{M}}(c+se),\qquad s\in\mathbb{F}.

Then
e\cdot y=e\cdot(c+se)=sQ_{3}(e)
, and

H_{e,\alpha}(f_{\mathcal{M}})=\sum_{c\in e^{\perp}}\sum_{s\in\mathbb{F}}\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}}f_{c,e}(s+a)f_{c,e}(s)\chi(\alpha Q_{3}(e)(2s+a)).

Now set
t=s+a
. Since
a\neq 0
, this is equivalent to
t\neq s
, and

2s+a=s+t
. Hence

\displaystyle H_{e,\alpha}(f_{\mathcal{M}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{c\in e^{\perp}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s,t\in\mathbb{F}\\
s\neq t\end{subarray}}f_{c,e}(s)f_{c,e}(t)\chi(\alpha Q_{3}(e)(s+t))

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{F}}\Bigg(\sum\limits_{c\in e^{\perp}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}s,t\in\mathbb{F}\\
s+t=k\\
s\neq t\end{subarray}}f_{c,e}(s)f_{c,e}(t)\Bigg)\chi(\alpha Q_{3}(e)k).

Now Plancherel’s theorem gives

\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}}|H_{e,\alpha}(f_{\mathcal{M}})|^{2}\leq p\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{F}}\Bigg|\sum\limits_{c\in e^{\perp}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}s,t\in\mathbb{F}\\
s+t=k\\
s\neq t\end{subarray}}f_{c,e}(s)f_{c,e}(t)\Bigg|^{2}.

Notice that the condition
s+t=k
is exactly

t=k-s,\qquad c+te=R_{e,k}(c+se).

The condition
s\neq t
is exactly
c+se\neq R_{e,k}(c+se)
. With
x=c+se
, one has

\sum\limits_{c\in e^{\perp}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}s,t\in\mathbb{F}\\
s+t=k\\
s\neq t\end{subarray}}f_{c,e}(s)f_{c,e}(t)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathbb{F}^{3}\\
x\neq R_{e,k}x\end{subarray}}f_{\mathcal{M}}(x)f_{\mathcal{M}}(R_{e,k}\,x).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{\prime}(f_{\mathcal{M}})

\displaystyle=p\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[e]\in\mathbb{P}^{2}(\mathbb{F})\\
Q_{3}(e)\neq 0\end{subarray}}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}}|H_{e,\alpha}(f_{\mathcal{M}})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq p^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[e]\in\mathbb{P}^{2}(\mathbb{F})\\
Q_{3}(e)\neq 0\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{F}}\Bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathbb{F}^{3}\\
x\neq R_{e,k}x\end{subarray}}f_{\mathcal{M}}(x)f_{\mathcal{M}}(R_{e,k}x)\Bigg|^{2}=p^{2}\sum_{R\in\mathfrak{R}}|N_{R}(f_{\mathcal{M}})|^{2}.

∎

Inserting the expansion of
f_{\mathcal{M}}
into the definition of
N_{R}(f_{\mathcal{M}})
, yields

N_{R}(f_{\mathcal{M}})=\sum\limits_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\ell,\,Rx\in\ell^{\prime}\\
x\neq Rx\end{subarray}}f_{\ell}(x)f_{\ell^{\prime}}(Rx).

In view of Lemma
2.2
, we further splits the ordered pairs
(\ell,\ell^{\prime})
according to the following cases: (i)
R\ell\neq\ell^{\prime}
; (ii)
R\ell=\ell^{\prime}
.

Then

|N_{R}(f_{\mathcal{M}})|\leq A_{R}^{\mathcal{M}}+B_{R}^{\mathcal{M}},\qquad B_{R}^{\mathcal{M}}=S_{R}^{\mathcal{M}}+D_{R}^{\mathcal{M}}.

Here

A_{R}^{\mathcal{M}}=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}\\
R\ell\neq\ell^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\ell,\,Rx\in\ell^{\prime}\\
x\neq Rx\end{subarray}}|f_{\ell}(x)||f_{\ell^{\prime}}(Rx)|,\qquad B_{R}^{\mathcal{M}}=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\in\mathcal{M}\\
R\ell\in\mathcal{M}\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\ell\\
x\neq Rx\end{subarray}}|f_{\ell}(x)||f_{R\ell}(Rx)|,

The line pairs, with
R\ell=\ell^{\prime}
, are harmless because each reflected line meets the target
line in at most one point.

Lemma 6.11
.

For
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{L}
,

\sum_{R\in\mathfrak{R}}(A_{R}^{\mathcal{M}})^{2}\lesssim\frac{|A|^{4}}{K_{L}^{2}}.

Proof.

Since
\ell\cap R^{-1}\ell^{\prime}
has at most one point, and
|f_{\ell}|\leq 1
, so

A_{R}^{\mathcal{M}}\lesssim|\mathcal{M}|^{2}\leq|\mathcal{L}|^{2}\leq\frac{|A|^{2}}{K_{L}^{2}}.

Also,

\Big\|\sum\limits_{\ell\in\mathcal{M}}|f_{\ell}(x)|\Big\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}\leq\sum\limits_{\ell\in\mathcal{M}}\|f_{\ell}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}\lesssim\sum\limits_{\ell\in\mathcal{M}}|B_{\ell}|\leq|A|.

For each ordered pair
x\neq y
, at most one anisotropic affine reflection sends
x
to
y
.
One sees that

\sum_{R\in\mathfrak{R}}A_{R}^{\mathcal{M}}\leq\sum\limits_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\mathbb{F}^{3}\\
x\neq y\end{subarray}}|f_{\ell}(x)f_{\ell^{\prime}}(y)|\leq\Big\|\sum\limits_{\ell\in\mathcal{M}}|f_{\ell}(x)|\Big\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}^{2}\leq|A|^{2}.

Now we conclude that

\sum_{R\in\mathfrak{R}}(A_{R}^{\mathcal{M}})^{2}\leq\sup\limits_{R}A_{R}^{\mathcal{M}}\cdot\sum\limits_{R}A_{R}^{\mathcal{M}}\lesssim\frac{|A|^{4}}{K_{L}^{2}}.

∎

For isotropic rank-one directions, the same expansion is better expressed through the foliation by isotropic lines inside the planes
\mathcal{H}_{e,\lambda}
.

Lemma 6.12
.

For
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{L}
,

\mathcal{R}^{\prime\prime}(f_{\mathcal{M}})\leq p^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[e]\in\mathbb{P}^{2}(\mathbb{F})\\
Q_{3}(e)=0\end{subarray}}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}}|C_{e}^{\mathcal{M}}(\lambda)|^{2},

where

\mathcal{H}_{e,\lambda}:=\{y\in F_{p}^{3}:\,2e\cdot y=\lambda\},\qquad C_{e}^{\mathcal{M}}(\lambda):=\sum_{y\in\mathcal{H}_{e,\lambda}}f_{\mathcal{M}}(y)\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}}f_{\mathcal{M}}(y+ae).

Proof.

When
Q_{3}(e)=0
, one has

H_{e,\alpha}(f_{\mathcal{M}})=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{F}^{3}}f_{\mathcal{M}}(y+ae)f_{\mathcal{M}}(y)\chi\bigl(\alpha(2e\cdot y)\bigr)=\sum\limits_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}}C_{e}^{\mathcal{M}}(\lambda)\chi(\alpha\lambda).

Then Plancherel’s theorem gives

\mathcal{R}^{\prime\prime}(f_{\mathcal{M}})=p\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[e]\in\mathbb{P}^{2}(\mathbb{F})\\
Q_{3}(e)=0\end{subarray}}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}}|H_{e,\alpha}(f_{\mathcal{M}})|^{2}\leq p^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[e]\in\mathbb{P}^{2}(\mathbb{F})\\
Q_{3}(e)=0\end{subarray}}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}}|C_{e}^{\mathcal{M}}(\lambda)|^{2}.

∎

The
L^{1}
mass of these coefficients is controlled by the total number of isotropic point pairs generated by the packet.

Lemma 6.13
.

For
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{L}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[e]\in\mathbb{P}^{2}(\mathbb{F})\\
Q_{3}(e)=0\end{subarray}}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}}|C_{e}^{\mathcal{M}}(\lambda)|\leq\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\mathbb{F}^{3}\\
x\neq y\\
Q_{3}(x-y)=0\end{subarray}}|f_{\mathcal{M}}(x)f_{\mathcal{M}}(y)|.

Proof.

Taking absolute values in the definition of
C_{e}^{\mathcal{M}}(\lambda)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[e]\in\mathbb{P}^{2}(\mathbb{F})\\
Q_{3}(e)=0\end{subarray}}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}}|C_{e}^{\mathcal{M}}(\lambda)|\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[e]\in\mathbb{P}^{2}(\mathbb{F})\\
Q_{3}(e)=0\end{subarray}}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{H}_{e,\lambda}}\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}}|f_{\mathcal{M}}(y)f_{\mathcal{M}}(y+ae)|=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\mathbb{F}^{3}\\
x\neq y\\
Q_{3}(x-y)=0\end{subarray}}|f_{\mathcal{M}}(x)f_{\mathcal{M}}(y)|.

The last inequality holds because
x=y+ae
,
a\neq 0
, and
Q_{3}(e)=0

imply
x\neq y
and
Q_{3}(x-y)=0
. Conversely, every ordered pair

(x,y)
with
x\neq y
and
Q_{3}(x-y)=0
determines the isotropic
projective direction
[e]=[x-y]
, and then
\lambda=2e\cdot y
, after our
fixed choice of representative
e
.
∎

6.4
Estimates from the anisotropic-direction balanced-line-packets

We next prove the anisotropic balanced estimate by bounding its anisotropic and isotropic contributions separately.
The anisotropic contribution for
f_{\rm an}
follows from the reflection reduction and a counting argument for reflected anisotropic lines.

Lemma 6.14
.

We have

\mathcal{R}^{\prime}(f_{\rm an})\lesssim p^{2}\Big(\frac{|A|^{4}}{K_{L}^{2}}+\frac{|A|^{3}}{K_{L}}+p|A|^{2}\Big).

Proof.

By Lemma
6.10
, we have

\mathcal{R}^{\prime}(f_{\rm an})\lesssim p^{2}\sum_{R\in\mathfrak{R}}|N_{R}(f_{\rm an})|^{2}\lesssim p^{2}\Big(\sum_{R\in\mathfrak{R}}(A_{R}^{\mathcal{L}_{\rm an}})^{2}+\sum_{R\in\mathfrak{R}}(B_{R}^{\mathcal{L}_{\rm an}})^{2}\Big),

where the summands with
R\ell\neq\ell^{\prime}
satisfy

\sum_{R\in\mathfrak{R}}A_{R}(f_{\rm an})^{2}\lesssim\frac{|A|^{4}}{K_{L}^{2}}

by Lemma
6.11
, and the summands with
R\ell=\ell^{\prime}
are given by

B_{R}^{\mathcal{L}_{\rm an}}=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}\\
R\ell=\ell^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\ell,\,Rx\in\ell^{\prime}\\
x\neq Rx\end{subarray}}|f_{\ell}(x)||f_{\ell^{\prime}}(Rx)|=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\rm an}\\
R\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\rm an}\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\ell\\
x\neq Rx\end{subarray}}|f_{\ell}(x)f_{R\ell}(Rx)|.

Cauchy-Schwarz inequality leads to

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\ell\\
x\neq Rx\end{subarray}}|f_{\ell}(x)f_{R\ell}(Rx)|\leq\|f_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}\|f_{R\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}\lesssim|B_{\ell}|^{1/2}|B_{R\ell}|^{1/2}.

Another Cauchy-Schwarz inequality shows

B_{R}^{\mathcal{L}_{\rm an}}\lesssim\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\rm an}\\
R\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\rm an}\end{subarray}}|B_{\ell}|^{1/2}|B_{R\ell}|^{1/2}\leq\sum\limits_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\rm an}}|B_{\ell}|\leq|A|.

On the other hand, we have

\sum_{R\in\mathfrak{R}}B_{R}^{\mathcal{L}_{\rm an}}\lesssim\sum\limits_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}}|B_{\ell}|^{1/2}|B_{\ell^{\prime}}|^{1/2}\sum\limits_{R}1.

Where the inner sum is over
R\in\mathfrak{R}
satisfying
R\ell=\ell^{\prime}
and the following property: if
\ell=\ell^{\prime}
, then
R
moves
\ell
non-trivially. To count the number of reflections in the inner sum, Lemma
2.2
shows the following: two anisotropic lines with distinct projective directions have only
O(1)
reflections between them; two distinct parallel anisotropic lines again have only
O(1)
; and for reflections satisfying
R\ell=\ell
and moving points of
\ell
non-trivially, only the normals parallel to the line direction move points of
\ell
nontrivially, giving
O(p)
preserving reflections. Combining (
29
), one arrives at

\sum_{R\in\mathfrak{R}}B_{R}^{\mathcal{L}_{\rm an}}\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}_{\rm an}\\
\ell\neq\ell^{\prime}\end{subarray}}(|B_{\ell}||B_{\ell^{\prime}}|)^{1/2}+p\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\rm an}}|B_{\ell}|\lesssim\frac{|A|^{2}}{K_{L}}+p|A|.

It follows that

\sum_{R\in\mathfrak{R}}(B_{R}^{\mathcal{L}_{\rm an}})^{2}\lesssim|A|\left(\frac{|A|^{2}}{K_{L}}+p|A|\right)=\frac{|A|^{3}}{K_{L}}+p|A|^{2}.

Thus

\mathcal{R}^{\prime}(f_{\rm an})\lesssim p^{2}\Big(\frac{|A|^{4}}{K_{L}^{2}}+\frac{|A|^{3}}{K_{L}}+p|A|^{2}\Big).

∎

The isotropic contribution for
f_{\rm an}
uses the poor-plane bound on each isotropic foliation plane together with the anisotropic pair bound.

Lemma 6.15
.

We have

\mathcal{R}^{\prime\prime}(f_{\rm an})\lesssim p^{2}\Big(\frac{|A|^{4}}{K_{L}^{3}}+\frac{|A|^{2}K_{\Pi}^{2}}{K_{L}^{2}}\Big).

Proof.

Recall the definitions in Lemma
6.12
. For
Q_{3}(e)=0
, the plane
\mathcal{H}_{e,\lambda}
is foliated by isotropic lines parallel to
e
. So we can write

C_{e}^{\mathcal{L}_{\rm an}}(\lambda)=\sum\limits_{L\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{e,\lambda}}\Big|\sum_{x\in L}f_{\rm an}(x)\Big|^{2}-\sum\limits_{y\in\mathcal{H}_{e,\lambda}}|f_{\rm an}(y)|^{2}.

where the first sum is over all isotropic contained lines
L
parallel to
e
.

For the second term, one obtains by (
28
) that

\sum\limits_{y\in\mathcal{H}_{e,\lambda}}|f_{\rm an}(y)|^{2}\lesssim K_{\Pi}+\frac{|A|^{2}}{K_{L}^{2}}.

For the first term, we split the sum over
\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\rm an}
in the expansion of
f_{\rm an}
into two cases:
\ell
is contained in
\mathcal{H}_{e,\lambda}
or not. Using the poor-plane property and rich-line property, one arrives at

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\rm an}\\
\ell\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{e,\lambda}\end{subarray}}K_{L}\leq\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\rm an}\\
\ell\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{e,\lambda}\end{subarray}}|B_{\ell}|\leq K_{\Pi},

so the number of anisotropic contained lines in
\mathcal{H}_{e,\lambda}
is at most
K_{\Pi}/K_{L}
. Switching the order of sums over
x
and
\ell
, and applying Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, yields

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{L\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{e,\lambda}}\Bigg|\sum_{x\in L}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\rm an}\\
\ell\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{e,\lambda}\end{subarray}}f_{\ell}(x)\Bigg|^{2}\leq\sum\limits_{L\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{e,\lambda}}\Bigg(\frac{K_{\Pi}}{K_{L}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\rm an}\\
\ell\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{e,\lambda}\end{subarray}}\Big|\sum_{x\in L}f_{\ell}(x)\Big|^{2}\Bigg).

Interchange the order of the two sums over
L
and
\ell
. For a given anisotropic line
\ell
contained in
\mathcal{H}_{e,\lambda}
, it is not parallel to the isotropic leaves, and so meets each leaf
L
in at most one point. The term
\sum\nolimits_{x\in L}f_{\ell}(x)
is either
0
or equals
f_{\ell}(x)
for exactly one
x\in L\cap\ell
. Since every point of
\ell
lies on exactly one leaf,

\sum\limits_{L\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{e,\lambda}}\Big|\sum_{x\in L}f_{\ell}(x)\Big|^{2}=\sum\limits_{x\in\ell}|f_{\ell}(x)|^{2}=\|f_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}^{2}\lesssim|B_{\ell}|.

Then

\sum\limits_{L\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{e,\lambda}}\Bigg|\sum_{x\in L}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\rm an}\\
\ell\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{e,\lambda}\end{subarray}}f_{\ell}(x)\Bigg|^{2}\leq\frac{K_{\Pi}}{K_{L}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\rm an}\\
\ell\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{e,\lambda}\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{L\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{e,\lambda}}\Big|\sum_{x\in L\cap\ell}f_{\ell}(x)\Big|^{2}\lesssim\frac{K_{\Pi}}{K_{L}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\rm an}\\
\ell\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{e,\lambda}\end{subarray}}|B_{\ell}|\leq\frac{K_{\Pi}^{2}}{K_{L}}.

All selected lines not contained in
\mathcal{H}_{e,\lambda}
meet the plane in at most one point, giving

\sum\limits_{L\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{e,\lambda}}\Bigg|\sum_{x\in L}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\rm an}\\
\ell\not\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{e,\lambda}\end{subarray}}f_{\ell}(x)\Bigg|^{2}\leq|\mathcal{L}_{\rm an}|^{2}\lesssim\frac{|A|^{2}}{K_{L}^{2}}.

Putting above bounds together, we conclude that

|C_{e}^{\mathcal{L}_{\rm an}}(\lambda)|\lesssim K_{\Pi}+\frac{|A|^{2}}{K_{L}^{2}}+\frac{K_{\Pi}^{2}}{K_{L}}\lesssim\frac{|A|^{2}}{K_{L}^{2}}+\frac{K_{\Pi}^{2}}{K_{L}},

since
K_{L}\leq K_{\Pi}
.

Next, one gets by Lemmas
6.13
and
6.6
that

\displaystyle J_{\rm an}:

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[e]\in\mathbb{P}^{2}(\mathbb{F})\\
Q_{3}(e)=0\end{subarray}}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}}|C_{e}^{\mathcal{L}_{\rm an}}(\lambda)|\leq\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\mathbb{F}^{3}\\
x\neq y\\
Q_{3}(x-y)=0\end{subarray}}|f_{\rm an}(x)f_{\rm an}(y)|\lesssim\frac{|A|^{2}}{K_{L}}.

Consequently, combining Lemma
6.12
,

\mathcal{R}^{\prime\prime}(f_{\rm an})\leq p^{2}\sup\limits_{[e],\lambda}|C_{e}^{\mathcal{L}_{\rm an}}(\lambda)|\cdot J_{\rm an}\lesssim p^{2}\Big(\frac{|A|^{2}}{K_{L}^{2}}+\frac{K_{\Pi}^{2}}{K_{L}}\Big)\cdot\frac{|A|^{2}}{K_{L}}.

The proof is completed.
∎

The proposition now follows by combining the variance reduction with the two rank estimates.

Proof of Proposition
6.3
.

By Lemma
6.5
, one has
\|f_{\rm an}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}^{2}\lesssim|A|+\frac{|A|^{2}}{K_{L}^{2}}
.

Combining Lemmas
6.8
,
6.9
,
6.14
,
6.15
, and the fact
K_{L}\leq K_{\Pi}
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\|G_{\rm an}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{4}

\displaystyle\lesssim p^{-4}\big(\mathcal{R}^{\prime}(f_{\rm an})+\mathcal{R}^{\prime\prime}(f_{\rm an})\big)+p^{-1}\|f_{\rm an}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}^{4}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{|A|^{4}}{p^{2}K_{L}^{2}}+\frac{|A|^{2}}{p}+\frac{|A|^{2}K_{\Pi}^{2}}{p^{2}K_{L}^{2}}+\frac{|A|^{4}}{pK_{L}^{4}}.

Here the term
|A|^{3}/(p^{2}K_{L})
has been absorbed into the other terms, since

\frac{|A|^{3}}{p^{2}K_{L}}\leq\frac{|A|^{3}}{pK_{L}^{2}}\lesssim\frac{|A|^{4}}{pK_{L}^{4}}+\frac{|A|^{2}}{p}.

∎

6.5
Estimates from the Isotropic-direction balanced-line-packets

The isotropic balanced branch is more delicate because affine reflections may either preserve an isotropic direction or exchange two distinct isotropic directions. We handle it by separating reflection pairs that preserve an isotropic direction from those that exchange two distinct isotropic directions.

Lemma 6.16
.

It satisfies that

\mathcal{R}^{\prime}(f_{\rm iso})\lesssim p^{2}\Bigg(\frac{|A|^{4}}{K_{L}^{2}}+\frac{p|A|^{2}K_{\Pi}}{K_{L}}\Bigg).

Proof.

By Lemma
6.10
,

\mathcal{R}^{\prime}(f_{\rm iso})\lesssim p^{2}\sum_{R\in\mathfrak{R}}|N_{R}(f_{\rm iso})|^{2},

where
\mathfrak{R}
is the set of anisotropic affine reflections, and

N_{R}(f_{\rm iso})=\sum\limits_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}_{\rm iso}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\ell,\,Rx\in\ell^{\prime}\\
x\neq Rx\end{subarray}}f_{\ell}(x)f_{\ell^{\prime}}(Rx).

In view of Lemma
2.2
, the proof splits the ordered pairs
(\ell,\ell^{\prime})
according to three cases: (i)
R\ell\neq\ell^{\prime}
; (ii)
R\ell=\ell^{\prime}
with
\ell,\ell^{\prime}
having the same isotropic direction; (iii)
R\ell=\ell^{\prime}
with
\ell,\ell^{\prime}
having distinct isotropic directions. And we denote

N_{R}^{\mathcal{L}_{\rm iso}}\leq A_{R}^{\mathcal{L}_{\rm iso}}+B_{R}^{\mathcal{L}_{\rm iso}},\qquad B_{R}^{\mathcal{L}_{\rm iso}}=S_{R}^{\mathcal{L}_{\rm iso}}+D_{R}^{\mathcal{L}_{\rm iso}},

where
A_{R}^{\mathcal{L}_{\rm iso}}
,
S_{R}^{\mathcal{L}_{\rm iso}}
and
D_{R}^{\mathcal{L}_{\rm iso}}
denote the absolute-valued contribution of the terms in cases (i), (ii) and (iii), respectively.

For
A_{R}^{\mathcal{L}_{\rm iso}}
, Lemma
6.11
shows that

\sum_{R\in\mathfrak{R}}(A_{R}^{\mathcal{L}_{\rm iso}})^{2}\lesssim\frac{|A|^{4}}{K_{L}^{2}}.

Next, we consider
S_{R}^{\mathcal{L}_{\rm iso}}
. Cauchy-Schwarz inequality shows that

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\ell,\,Rx\in\ell^{\prime}\\
x\neq Rx\end{subarray}}|f_{\ell}(x)f_{\ell^{\prime}}(Rx)|\leq\|f_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}\|f_{\ell^{\prime}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}\lesssim|B_{\ell}|^{1/2}|B_{\ell^{\prime}}|^{1/2}.

Then

\sum\limits_{R\in\mathfrak{R}}S_{R}^{\mathcal{L}_{\rm iso}}\lesssim\sum\limits_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}\sum\limits_{R}|B_{\ell}|^{1/2}|B_{\ell^{\prime}}|^{1/2},

where the sums are over line pairs
\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}_{\rm iso}
with the same direction, and over the reflections
R\in\mathfrak{R}
with
R\ell=\ell^{\prime}
.

Let the common isotropic direction of
\ell
and
\ell^{\prime}
be
[v]
. A reflection
R
preserving
[v]
has normal in
v^{\perp}
. By Lemma
2.2
, two parallel isotropic lines can be interchanged by a anisotropic reflection only inside a common plane
\mathcal{H}_{v,\lambda}=\{x\in\mathbb{F}^{3}:\,x\cdot v=\lambda\}
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}
. And each ordered pair has at most
p
possible reflections. Now we further obtain

\sum\limits_{R\in\mathfrak{R}}S_{R}^{\mathcal{L}_{\rm iso}}\lesssim p\sum\limits_{[v],\lambda}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}_{\rm iso}\\
\ell,\ell^{\prime}\parallel v,\ \ell,\ell^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{v,\lambda}\end{subarray}}|B_{\ell}|^{1/2}|B_{\ell^{\prime}}|^{1/2}=p\sum\limits_{[v],\lambda}\Bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\rm iso}\\
\ell\parallel v,\ \ell\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{v,\lambda}\end{subarray}}|B_{\ell}|^{1/2}\Bigg)^{2},

where the outer sum is over all isotropic direction
[v]\in\mathbb{P}^{2}(\mathbb{F})
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}
.

Now put

M_{v,\lambda}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\rm iso}\\
\ell\parallel v,\ \ell\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{v,\lambda}\end{subarray}}|B_{\ell}|

for each
[v]
and
\lambda
. One has
M_{v,\lambda}<K_{\Pi}
by the no-rich-plan condition. We also have
\sum_{[v],\lambda}M_{v,\lambda}\leq|A|
because the selected line packets are disjoint and their total size is at most
|A|
. So
\sum\nolimits_{[v],\lambda}M_{v,\lambda}^{2}\leq|A|K_{\Pi}
. Combining the rich-line property
|B_{\ell}|\geq K_{L}
, one obtains

\sum\limits_{R\in\mathfrak{R}}S_{R}^{\mathcal{L}_{\rm iso}}\lesssim p\sum_{[v],\lambda}\frac{M_{v,\lambda}^{2}}{K_{L}}\leq\frac{p|A|K_{\Pi}}{K_{L}}.

Moreover, for each fixed
R
, one also has

S_{R}^{\mathcal{L}_{\rm iso}}\leq\sum\limits_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\rm iso}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\ell\\
x\neq Rx\end{subarray}}|f_{\ell}(x)f_{R\ell}(Rx)|\leq\sum\limits_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\rm iso}}\|f_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}\|f_{R\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}\leq\sum\limits_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\rm iso}}|B_{\ell}|\leq|A|

by applying Cauchy-Schwarz inequalities in
x
and then in
\ell
. Now one arrives at

\sum_{R\in\mathfrak{R}}(S_{R}^{\mathcal{L}_{\rm iso}})^{2}\lesssim\frac{p|A|^{2}K_{\Pi}}{K_{L}}.

We now treat
D_{R}^{\mathcal{L}_{\rm iso}}
, where
R\ell=\ell^{\prime}
and the two
selected isotropic lines
\ell,\ell^{\prime}
have distinct isotropic directions
[v],[w]
, respectively. By
Lemma
2.1
, we have
v\cdot w\neq 0
. By Lemma
2.2
, if an anisotropic affine
reflection sends the direction
[v]
to the direction
[w]
, then its normal
direction is of the form
[v-\mu w]
for some
\mu\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}
. Moreover,
Q_{3}(v-\mu w)=-2\mu\,v\cdot w\neq 0
, so these are indeed anisotropic normal directions.

Given an ordered pair of distinct isotropic directions
([v],[w])
. Fix representatives
v,w
with
v\cdot w\neq 0
, and choose a non-zero vector
\nu_{v,w}\in\langle v,w\rangle^{\perp}
, so that
v,w,\nu_{v,w}
form a basis of
\mathbb{F}^{3}
. Hence every point

x\in\mathbb{F}^{3}
has a unique coordinate representation
x=\rho v+\sigma w+\tau\nu_{v,w}
with
\rho,\sigma,\tau\in\mathbb{F}
. In these coordinates, let us write

\ell=\ell^{v}_{\sigma,\tau}:=\{\rho v+\sigma w+\tau\nu_{v,w}:\rho\in\mathbb{F}\},\qquad\sigma,\tau\in\mathbb{F},

\ell^{\prime}=\ell^{w}_{\rho^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}}:=\{\rho^{\prime}v+\sigma^{\prime}w+\tau^{\prime}\nu_{v,w}:\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}\},\qquad\rho^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}.

Then

\displaystyle D_{R}^{\mathcal{L}_{\rm iso}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{[v]\neq[w]}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma,\tau,\rho^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}\\
R\ell_{\sigma,\tau}^{v}=\ell_{\rho^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}}^{w}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\ell^{v}_{\sigma,\tau},\,Rx\in\ell^{w}_{\rho,\tau}\\
x\neq Rx\end{subarray}}|f_{\ell^{v}_{\sigma,\tau}}(x)f_{\ell^{w}_{\rho^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}}}(Rx)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum\limits_{[v]\neq[w]}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma,\tau,\rho^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}\\
R\ell_{\sigma,\tau}^{v}=\ell_{\rho^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}}^{w}\end{subarray}}|B_{\ell^{v}_{\sigma,\tau}}|^{1/2}|B_{\ell^{w}_{\rho^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}}}|^{1/2},

where the outermost sum is over distinct isotropic directions
[v],[w]
.

To simplify the above formula, we investigate the condition
R\ell_{\sigma,\tau}^{v}=\ell_{\rho^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}}^{w}
for fixed
[v],[w]
. We must have
R=R_{n_{\mu},k}
, with
n_{\mu}:=v-\mu w
, for some
\mu\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}
and
k\in\mathbb{F}
. At this stage,
[v],[w]
gives a one-parameter family of the possible normal directions. Calculation gives

\displaystyle R_{n_{\mu},k}(\rho v+\sigma w+\tau\nu_{v,w})

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\sigma}{\mu}+k\right)v+\mu(\rho-k)w+\tau\nu_{v,w}.

Therefore
R_{n_{\mu},k}\ell^{v}_{\sigma,\tau}=\ell^{w}_{\rho^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}}
exactly when

\rho^{\prime}=\frac{\sigma}{\mu}+k,\qquad\tau^{\prime}=\tau.

The second equality shows that any admissible intersecting line pair can be parametrized by
(\rho^{\prime},\sigma,\tau)
, namely by
\bigl(\ell^{v}_{\sigma,\tau},\ell^{w}_{\rho^{\prime},\tau}\bigr)
. And the first equality show that, if this line pair is given, then the reflection is of the form
R=R_{n_{\mu},k}
with
k=\rho^{\prime}-\frac{\sigma}{\mu}
.

For simplicity, in the following we would write
\rho^{\prime}=\rho
, and the notation for parameters and line pairs become
(\rho,\sigma,\tau)
, namely by
\bigl(\ell^{v}_{\sigma,\tau},\ell^{w}_{\rho,\tau}\bigr)
, respectively. Now we arrive at

\displaystyle D_{R}^{\mathcal{L}_{\rm iso}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum\limits_{[v]\neq[w]}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho,\sigma,\tau\in\mathbb{F}\\
R\ell_{\sigma,\tau}^{v}=\ell_{\rho,\tau}^{w}\end{subarray}}|B_{\ell^{v}_{\sigma,\tau}}|^{1/2}|B_{\ell^{w}_{\rho,\tau}}|^{1/2},

where
[v],[w]
are isotropic directions, and non-vanishing terms have imposed condition
n_{\mu}=v-\mu w
and
k=\rho-\sigma/\mu
with
R=R_{n_{\mu},k}
.

To estimate
\sum\nolimits_{R\in\mathfrak{R}}(D_{R}^{\mathcal{L}_{\rm iso}})^{2}
, we expand the square of the above expression, sum over
R
, and interchange the order of summation. Then we get sums over two distinct direction pairs
([v],[w]),([v^{\prime}],[w^{\prime}])
, then two intersecting line pairs
(\rho,\sigma,\tau)
,
(\rho^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})
, and then the reflection
R
.

First, consider the case
([v^{\prime}],[w^{\prime}])=([v],[w])
. If the same reflection
R=R_{n_{\mu},k}
matches both line pairs
(\rho,\sigma,\tau)
and
(\rho^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})
. Then
\rho-\frac{\sigma}{\mu}=k=\rho^{\prime}-\frac{\sigma^{\prime}}{\mu}
. Equivalently,

\sigma-\sigma^{\prime}=\mu(\rho-\rho^{\prime}).

If
(\rho,\sigma)\neq(\rho^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})
, then the above condition determines
\mu
uniquely if a solution exists. Hence in this case there are
O(1)
possible normal directions, and for each such normal direction the parameter
k
is determined by
k=\rho-\sigma/\mu
. If
(\rho,\sigma)=(\rho^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})
, then the above condition imposes no condition on

\mu
, and there are
O(p)
exceptional normal directions.

Second, consider the case
([v^{\prime}],[w^{\prime}])=([w],[v])
. Similar arguments as above work. We treat this case by multiplying
2
on the final upper bound, which causes no harm.

Third, consider the case that the two direction pairs are different as sets. Then their possible normal directions of reflections lie on different projective lines. Two different such projective lines meet in at most one point. And, when the normal direction is determined, the affine reflection parameter
k
is determined by the line-pair equations. So, the common reflection multiplicity is
O(1)
.

The preceding discussion gives the following bound:

\displaystyle\sum_{R\in\mathfrak{R}}(D_{R}^{\mathcal{L}_{\rm iso}})^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim p\sum\limits_{[v]\neq[w]}\sum\limits_{\rho,\sigma,\tau,\tau^{\prime}}|B_{\ell^{v}_{\sigma,\tau}}|^{1/2}|B_{\ell^{w}_{\rho,\tau}}|^{1/2}|B_{\ell^{v}_{\sigma,\tau^{\prime}}}|^{1/2}|B_{\ell^{w}_{\rho,\tau^{\prime}}}|^{1/2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}[v]\neq[w]\\
[v^{\prime}]\neq[w^{\prime}]\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho,\sigma,\tau\\
\rho^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}\end{subarray}}|B_{\ell^{v}_{\sigma,\tau}}|^{1/2}|B_{\ell^{w}_{\rho,\tau}}|^{1/2}|B_{\ell^{v^{\prime}}_{\sigma^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}}}|^{1/2}|B_{\ell^{w^{\prime}}_{\rho^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}}}|^{1/2}.

The second term can be bounded by

\displaystyle\leq\Bigg(\sum\limits_{[v],[w]}\sum\limits_{\rho,\sigma,\tau}|B_{\ell^{v}_{\sigma,\tau}}|^{1/2}|B_{\ell^{w}_{\rho,\tau}}|^{1/2}\Bigg)^{2}\leq\Big(\sum\limits_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}_{\rm iso}}|B_{\ell}|^{1/2}|B_{\ell^{\prime}}|^{1/2}\Big)^{2}=\Big(\sum\limits_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\rm iso}}|B_{\ell}|^{1/2}\Big)^{4}\leq\frac{|A|^{4}}{K_{L}^{2}}.

For the first term, fix a direction pair

([v],[w])
. For each
\rho,\sigma
, Cauchy’s inequality in the

\tau
-sum gives

\displaystyle\sum_{\rho,\sigma}\Big(\sum_{\tau}\left(|B_{\ell^{v}_{\sigma,\tau}}||B_{\ell^{w}_{\rho,\tau}}|\right)^{1/2}\Big)^{2}\leq\sum_{\rho,\sigma}\Big(\sum_{\tau}|B_{\ell^{v}_{\sigma,\tau}}|\Big)\Big(\sum_{\tau}|B_{\ell^{w}_{\rho,\tau}}|\Big)

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum_{\sigma,\tau}|B_{\ell^{v}_{\sigma,\tau}}|\Big)\Big(\sum_{\rho,\tau}|B_{\ell^{w}_{\rho,\tau}}|\Big)=M_{v}M_{w},

where

M_{v}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\rm iso}\\
\ell\parallel v\end{subarray}}|B_{\ell}|.

Summing over pairs of distinct isotropic directions gives

\sum_{[v],[w]}M_{v}M_{w}\leq\Big(\sum_{[v]}M_{v}\Big)^{2}\leq|A|^{2},

because the selected line packets are disjoint and their total size is at most

|A|
.

It follows that

\sum_{R\in\mathfrak{R}}(D_{R}^{\mathcal{L}_{\rm iso}})^{2}\lesssim\frac{|A|^{4}}{K_{L}^{2}}+p|A|^{2}.

Putting everything together, the proposition is proved by seeing
p|A|^{2}\leq p|A|^{2}K_{\Pi}/K_{L}
.
∎

Now the isotropic term for
f_{\rm iso}
is controlled by the uniform plane bound and the isotropic pair envelope.

Lemma 6.17
.

We have

\mathcal{R}^{\prime\prime}(f_{\rm iso})\lesssim p^{2}\left(K_{\Pi}+\frac{|A|^{2}}{K_{L}^{2}}\right)\left(p|A|+\frac{|A|^{2}}{K_{L}}\right).

Proof.

Similarly as in the proof of Lemma
6.15
, we have

C_{e}^{\mathcal{L}_{\rm iso}}(\lambda)=\sum\limits_{L\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{e,\lambda}}\Big|\sum_{x\in L}\sum\limits_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\rm iso}}f_{\ell}(x)\Big|^{2}+O\Big(K_{\Pi}+\frac{|A|^{2}}{K_{L}^{2}}\Big),

where the first sum is over all isotropic contained lines
L
parallel to
e
. Again, we split it into two cases:
\ell
is contained in
\mathcal{H}_{e,\lambda}
or not.

If a selected isotropic line
\ell
is contained in
\mathcal{H}_{e,\lambda}
, then Lemma
2.1
implies that
\ell
is parallel to
e
. Hence
\ell
is one of the leaves of the foliation. Its own contribution to the corresponding leaf sum
is zero because of the mean value property of
f_{\ell}
.

Every selected isotropic line not contained in
\mathcal{H}_{e,\lambda}
meets
the plane in at most one point, and hence contributes to at most one leaf. Similarly as in the proof of Lemma
6.15
, their contribution is
O(|A|^{2}/K_{L}^{2})
. Therefore

|C_{e}^{\mathcal{L}_{\rm iso}}(\lambda)|\lesssim K_{\Pi}+\frac{|A|^{2}}{K_{L}^{2}}.

Combining Lemmas
6.13
and
6.7
, one gets

J_{\rm iso}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[e]\in\mathbb{P}^{2}(\mathbb{F})\\
Q_{3}(e)=0\end{subarray}}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}}|C_{e}^{\mathcal{L}_{\rm iso}}(\lambda)|\lesssim p|A|+\frac{|A|^{2}}{K_{L}}.

Now Lemma
6.12
leads to

\mathcal{R}^{\prime\prime}(f_{\rm iso})\leq p^{2}\sup\limits_{[e],\lambda}|C_{e}^{\mathcal{L}_{\rm iso}}(\lambda)|\cdot J_{\rm iso}\lesssim p^{2}\left(K_{\Pi}+\frac{|A|^{2}}{K_{L}^{2}}\right)\left(p|A|+\frac{|A|^{2}}{K_{L}}\right).

∎

Combining the two rank bounds with the variance estimate gives the stated isotropic balanced
estimate.

Proof of Proposition
6.4
.

By Lemma
6.5
, one has
\|f_{\rm iso}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}^{2}\lesssim|A|+\frac{|A|^{2}}{K_{L}^{2}}
.

Combining Lemmas
6.8
,
6.9
,
6.16
and
6.17
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\|G_{\rm iso}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{4}

\displaystyle\lesssim p^{-4}\big(\mathcal{R}^{\prime}(f_{\rm iso})+\mathcal{R}^{\prime\prime}(f_{\rm iso})\big)+p^{-1}\|f_{\rm iso}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}^{3})}^{4}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{|A|^{4}}{p^{2}K_{L}^{2}}+\frac{|A|^{2}K_{\Pi}}{pK_{L}}+\frac{|A|K_{\Pi}}{p}+\frac{|A|^{4}}{pK_{L}^{4}}.

∎

7
Proof of the theorems

We now assemble the estimates obtained in the preceding sections and derive the
two main consequences of the paper. The first step is to combine the estimates
for the three pieces into a single optimized slice estimate. We then use this slice estimate in two different ways: first to prove the improved spherical restriction estimate, and then, with a different choice of parameters, to obtain the Erdős–Falconer distance consequence.

7.1
Optimized slice estimates

We begin by packaging the three local estimates proved above into a single
slice bound with free threshold parameters. Recall that the plane–then–line
decomposition writes a slice
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{3}
as

A=A_{2}\sqcup A_{1}\sqcup A_{3},

where the three pieces are controlled respectively by corresponding estimates. The following result records the combined
bound before the thresholds
K_{L}
and
K_{\Pi}
are optimized.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
K_{\Pi}
,
K_{L}
be parameters satisfying
1\leq K_{\Pi}\leq p^{2}
,
1\leq K_{L}\leq p
and
K_{L}\leq K_{\Pi}
. Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{3}
and
z\in\mathbb{F}
. Assume that
|A|\lesssim p^{2}
. Then

\displaystyle\|(\mathbf{1}_{A}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{z\}})*\widetilde{K}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}^{4}\,\,\lesssim\,\,\frac{|A|^{4}}{pK_{L}^{4}}+\frac{|A|^{4}}{p^{2}K_{L}^{2}}+\frac{|A|^{2}K_{\Pi}^{2}}{p^{2}K_{L}^{2}}+\frac{|A|^{2}K_{\Pi}}{pK_{L}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\frac{|A|K_{\Pi}^{3}}{p^{3}}+\frac{|A|^{4}}{pK_{\Pi}^{2}}+\frac{|A|^{4}}{K_{\Pi}^{3}}+\frac{|A|K_{L}^{2}}{p}+\frac{|A|^{3}K_{L}}{p^{3}}.

Proof.

Apply the plane-then-line decomposition to
A
, and obtain
A=A_{2}\sqcup A_{1}\sqcup A_{3}
as in Lemma
2.21
.
In particular,
|A_{3}|\leq|A|\lesssim p^{2}
, which meets the cardinality requirement of Proposition
4.1
. Now the conclusion combines the threshold estimates from Propositions
4.1
,
5.1
and
6.1
, with some terms absorbed into the others.
∎

Next, we record the direct consequence of the above estimate with thresholds
K_{L}
and
K_{\Pi}
chosen for the restriction problem.

Corollary 7.2
.

Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{3}
and
z\in\mathbb{F}
. Assume

p^{41/28}\leq|A|\leq p^{5/3}.

Then

\|(\mathbf{1}_{A}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{z\}})*\widetilde{K}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}\lesssim p^{-11/10}|A|^{11/10}+p^{-9/20}|A|^{7/10}.

(35)

Proof.

The conclusion follows from Theorem
7.1
by choosing

K_{L}\approx p^{6/5}|A|^{-1/5},\qquad K_{\Pi}\approx p^{2/5}|A|^{3/5}.

∎

The distance-set application requires a different balance of the threshold parameters. We therefore record a second consequence of Theorem

Corollary 7.3

Then

\|(\mathbf{1}_{A}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{z\}})*\widetilde{K}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}\lesssim p^{-111/124}|A|.

7.2
Proof of Theorem
1.1

We now pass from a single horizontal slice to an arbitrary set with regular
horizontal slices. The preceding slice estimate will only be used in the
remaining critical range; outside that range, the global estimates and the
half-moment estimate already give the desired gain over Stein–Tomas.

Lemma 7.4
.

Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{4}
be a set with regular horizontal slices, i.e.,

E=\bigsqcup_{z\in Z}(E_{z}\times\{z\}),\qquad|Z|\approx p^{-a}|E|,\qquad|E_{z}|\approx p^{a}\quad(z\in Z)

for some
0\leq a\leq 3
. Then, for every
j\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}
,

\|\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{E}}\|_{L^{2}(S_{j},d\sigma)}\lesssim|E|^{16/23}.

Proof.

Write
|Z|=p^{b}
for some
0\leq b\leq 1
. Then
|E|\approx p^{a+b}
.

If
a+b\leq\frac{69}{28}
, then
p^{-3/4}|E|\leq|E|^{16/23}
and Lemma
3.7
gives
\|\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{E}}\|_{L^{2}(S_{j},d\sigma)}\lesssim|E|^{16/23}
.

If
a+b\geq\frac{23}{9}
, then
p^{1/2}|E|^{1/2}\leq|E|^{16/23}
and Lemma
3.7
gives again
\|\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{E}}\|_{L^{2}(S_{j},d\sigma)}\lesssim|E|^{16/23}
.

If
10a+33b\leq 46
, the second estimate in Proposition
3.1
gives

\|\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{E}}\|_{L^{2}(S_{j},d\sigma)}\lesssim|E|^{1/2}+p^{-1/4}p^{5(a+b)/8}(p^{b+a/2})^{1/4}\lesssim|E|^{16/23}.

The remaining region is
69/28<a+b<23/9
and
10a+33b>46
. Noting that
0\leq b\leq 1
, one has
a>41/28
. Moreover,

46<10a+33b<10a+33\left(\frac{23}{9}-a\right)=\frac{253}{3}-23a,

which gives
a<5/3
. In this region, we can apply Corollary
7.2
to get

\|(\mathbf{1}_{E_{z}}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{z\}})*\widetilde{K}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}\lesssim p^{-11/10}p^{11a/10}+p^{-9/20}p^{7a/10}=p^{(11a-11)/10}+p^{(14a-9)/20}

for each slice
E_{z}
. Using the first estimate in Proposition
3.1
, we get

\displaystyle\|\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{E}}\|_{L^{2}(S_{j},d\sigma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim|E|^{1/2}+p^{3(a+b)/8}\left(p^{b}\left(p^{(11a-11)/10}+p^{(14a-9)/20}\right)\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=|E|^{1/2}+p^{-11/20+37a/40+7b/8}+p^{-9/40+29a/40+7b/8}\lesssim|E|^{16/23}.

The last step follows from the following two inequalities about the exponents:

211a+165b<211a+165\left(\frac{23}{9}-a\right)=46a+\frac{1265}{3}<46\cdot\frac{5}{3}+\frac{1265}{3}=\frac{1495}{3}<506,

9a+55b=9(a+b)+46b<9\cdot\frac{23}{9}+46\cdot 1=69.

The proof is completed.
∎

It remains to remove the dyadic regularity assumption on the horizontal slices. This costs only a logarithmic factor, since the possible slice sizes are
partitioned into
O(\log p)
dyadic classes.

Proposition 7.5
.

For every
E\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{4}
,

\|\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{E}}\|_{L^{2}(S_{j},d\sigma)}\lesssim(\log p)|E|^{16/23}.

Proof.

Decompose
E
into horizontal slices as previously. Then decompose the non-empty slices dyadically according to
|E_{z}|
. There are
O(\log p)
dyadic classes. Applying Lemma
7.4
, the preceding dyadic-regular estimate to each class and summing by the triangle
inequality gives

\|\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{E}}\|_{L^{2}(S_{j},d\sigma)}\lesssim(\log p)\,|E|^{16/23}.

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We use Proposition
7.5
. Thus, for every

E\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}^{4}
,

\|\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{E}}\|_{L^{2}(S_{j},d\sigma)}\lesssim(\log p)|E|^{16/23}\lesssim_{\nu}p^{\nu}|E|^{16/23}.

for every
\nu>0
. Set
r_{*}:=\frac{23}{16}
. Then
16/23=1/r_{*}
, and the above formula becomes

\|\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{E}}\|_{L^{2}(S_{j},d\sigma)}\lesssim_{\nu}p^{\nu}|E|^{1/r_{*}}.

Passing this estimate to arbitrary functions can be done by a standard decomposition. More precisely, for every
1<r_{0}<r_{*}
and every
\nu>0
,

\|\widehat{h}\|_{L^{2}(S_{j},d\sigma)}\lesssim_{r_{0},\nu}p^{\nu}\|h\|_{L^{r_{0}}(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{4})}

(36)

for all
h:\mathbb{F}_{p}^{4}\to\mathbb{C}
.

By duality, (
36
) is equivalent to

R_{S_{j}}^{*}(2\to r_{0}^{\prime})\lesssim_{r_{0},\nu}p^{\nu},

where
R_{S_{j}}^{*}(2\to r_{0}^{\prime})
denotes the extension constant

\|(g\,d\sigma)^{\vee}\|_{L^{r_{0}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{4})}\leq R_{S_{j}}^{*}(2\to r_{0}^{\prime})\|g\|_{L^{2}(S_{j},d\sigma)}.

The nonzero sphere
S_{j}
has positive Fourier dimension by the usual
Gauss-sum decay estimate. Therefore, by the

\epsilon
-removal lemma, see
[
23
, Lemma 16]
, we have
for every
s>r_{0}^{\prime}
,

R_{S_{j}}^{*}(2\to s)\lesssim_{r_{0},s}1.

(37)

Now let
1<r<\frac{23}{16}
. Choose
r_{0}
such that
r<r_{0}<\frac{23}{16}
, then
r^{\prime}>r_{0}^{\prime}.
Applying (
37
) with
s=r^{\prime}
, we obtain

R_{S_{j}}^{*}(2\to r^{\prime})\lesssim_{r}1.

Dualizing back gives

\|\widehat{f}\|_{L^{2}(S_{j},d\sigma)}\lesssim_{r}\|f\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{4})}

for every
1<r<23/16
.

It remains to treat
0<r\leq 1
. For every
f:\mathbb{F}_{p}^{4}\to\mathbb{C}
,
the trivial estimate gives

\|\widehat{f}\|_{L^{2}(S_{j},d\sigma)}\leq\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{4})}.

If
0<r\leq 1
, then
\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{4})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{4})}
. Hence

\|\widehat{f}\|_{L^{2}(S_{j},d\sigma)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{4})}

also holds for
0<r\leq 1
.

Consequently, for every
0<r<\frac{23}{16}
, we have

\|\widehat{f}\|_{L^{2}(S_{j},d\sigma)}\lesssim_{r}\|f\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{4})}.

The implicit constant is independent of
p
,
j
, and
f
. By duality, this proves
Theorem
1.1
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2

For
t\in\mathbb{F}
, define

\nu_{E}(t):=\#\{(x,y)\in E\times E:Q_{4}(x-y)=t\}.

Then

\sum_{t\in\mathbb{F}}\nu_{E}(t)=|E|^{2}.

The following lemma is a summary of
[
17
, Proposition 2.3, Proposition 2.4, and Lemma 3.1]

Lemma 7.6
.

Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{4}
. If
|E|\gtrsim p^{2}
, then

|\Delta(E)|\gtrsim\frac{|E|^{4}}{|E|^{4}/p+p^{3}|E|\displaystyle\max_{j\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}}\|\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{E}}\|_{L^{2}(S_{j},d\sigma)}^{2}}.

In particular, if

\|\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{E}}\|_{L^{2}(S_{j},d\sigma)}\lesssim p^{-2}|E|^{3/2},

for every
j\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}
, then
|\Delta(E)|\gtrsim p
.

Lemma 7.7
.

Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{4}
be a set with regular horizontal slices, i.e.,

E=\bigsqcup_{z\in Z}(E_{z}\times\{z\}),\qquad|Z|\approx p^{-a}|E|,\qquad|E_{z}|\approx p^{a}\quad(z\in Z)

for some
0\leq a\leq 3
. Assume that
|E|\gtrsim p^{77/31}
. Then, for every
j\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}
,

\|\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{E}}\|_{L^{2}(S_{j},d\sigma)}\lesssim p^{-2}|E|^{3/2}.

Proof.

Write
|Z|=p^{b}
for some
0\leq b\leq 1
. Then
|E|\approx p^{a+b}
.

When
|E|\geq p^{5/2}
, Lemma (
3.7
) shows that

\|\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{E}}\|_{L^{2}(S_{j},d\sigma)}\lesssim p^{1/2}|E|^{1/2}\leq p^{-2}|E|^{3/2}.

In the following, we assume that
p^{77/31}\lesssim|E|\leq p^{5/2}
, i.e.,
77/31\leq a+b\leq 5/2
.

If
6a+5b\geq 14
, the second estimate in Proposition
3.1
gives

\|\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{E}}\|_{L^{2}(S_{j},d\sigma)}\lesssim|E|^{1/2}+p^{-1/4}p^{5(a+b)/8}(p^{b+a/2})^{1/4}\lesssim p^{-2}|E|^{3/2}.

It remains to consider the case
6a+5b<14
. In such situation,
a\geq 77/31-1=46/31
. And

a<14-5(a+b)<14-5\cdot\frac{77}{31}=\frac{49}{31}.

In this region, Corollary
7.3
tells

\|(\mathbf{1}_{E_{z}}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\{z\}})*\widetilde{K}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}^{4})}\lesssim p^{-111/124+a}

for each slice
E_{z}
. Using the first estimate in Proposition
3.1
, we get

\displaystyle\|\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{E}}\|_{L^{2}(S_{j},d\sigma)}\lesssim|E|^{1/2}+p^{3(a+b)/8}\left(p^{b}\cdot p^{-111/124+a}\right)^{1/2}\leq p^{-2}|E|^{3/2},

in view of
a+b\geq 77/31
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

By dyadic pigeonholing in the horizontal slice sizes, we may pass to a subset

E^{\prime}\subseteq E
such that
\Delta(E^{\prime})\subseteq\Delta(E)
,

|E^{\prime}|\gtrsim|E|/\log p\gtrsim p^{77/31},

and all nonempty horizontal slices of
E^{\prime}
have comparable size. By Lemma
7.7
, we deduce that

\|\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{E^{\prime}}}\|_{L^{2}(S_{j},d\sigma)}\lesssim p^{-2}|E^{\prime}|^{3/2}

for every nonzero
j\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}
. From Lemma
7.6
, we conclude that

|\Delta(E)|\geq|\Delta(E^{\prime})|\gtrsim p.

The proof is completed.
∎
