L ∞ L^{\infty} -estimate of Kähler-Einstein potentials on stable varieties
Rui Tang

Abstract
We study the asymptotic behavior of Kähler–Einstein potentials on stable varieties near the singularities. Using iterated logarithmic functions associated with a defining function of the non-klt locus, we obtain refined lower bounds for the Kähler–Einstein potential, improving previous estimates of Di Nezza–Guedj–Guenancia and Datar–Fu–Song. Under additional assumptions on the log resolution, we also establish upper bounds. The proofs are based on the construction of explicit subsolutions and supersolutions for degenerate complex Monge–Ampère equations together with refined integrability estimates in pluripotential theory.



Introduction

The Kähler-Einstein metric is a central object in complex differential geometry. Given a complex manifold
X
, a Kähler-Einstein metric on
X
is a Kähler metric whose Ricci curvature is proportional to the metric itself. The existence and uniqueness of Kähler–Einstein metrics on canonically polarized (i.e.
K_{X}
ample) compact complex manifolds were established in the celebrated works of Aubin
[
1
]
and Yau
[
25
]
.

Over the past two decades, this theory has been extended to canonically polarized varieties
X
with mild singularities arising in the Minimal Model Program (MMP). Eyssidieux–Guedj–Zeriahi
[
12
]
constructed Kähler–Einstein metrics on varieties with Kawamata log terminal (klt) singularities, while Berman–Guenancia
[
3
]
treated the log canonical (lc) case. More generally, if
X
has semi-log canonical singularities, Berman–Guenancia
[
3
]
showed that
X
admits a Kähler-Einstein metric if and only if
X
is a stable variety in the sense of Kollár-Shepherd-Barron and Alexeev (KSBA).
These singular Kähler-Einstein metrics are defined to be a Kähler form
\omega_{KE}=\omega+dd^{c}\varphi_{KE}
on the regular part
X_{reg}
of
X
, where
\omega\in c_{1}(K_{X})
, such that

\mathrm{Ric}(\omega_{KE})=-\omega_{KE}\text{ and }\int_{X_{reg}}\omega_{KE}^{n}=c_{1}(K_{X})^{n}.

Here
\varphi_{KE}
is called a Kähler-Einstein potential. Due to the complexity of singularities, the behavior of
\omega_{KE}
near
X_{sing}
is quite mysterious. A fundamental open problem is the following

Question

What is the asymptotic behavior of
\varphi_{KE}
near
X_{sing}
?

Known results

If
X
has klt singularities, the Kähler–Einstein potential is known to be locally bounded near
X_{sing}
. In contrast, if
X
has lc singularities, the potential necessarily diverges to
-\infty
near the non-klt locus
\mathrm{Nklt}(X)
(see Definition
1.5
).
Song
[
24
]
proved that the Kähler–Einstein potential is locally bounded outside the non-klt locus. More recently, Di Nezza–Guedj–Guenancia
[
10
,
11
]
obtained a more precise estimate:

Fact A

For any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a constant
C_{\epsilon}
such that

(0.1)

\varphi_{KE}\geq-(n+\nu_{X}+\epsilon)\log(-\log|s|)+C_{\epsilon},

where
n=\dim X
,
\nu_{X}\in\{1,\dots,n\}
is an integer defined as in Definition
Set-up
below, and
(s=0)
is any reduced divisor containing the non-klt locus of
X
.

On the other hand, Datar–Fu–Song
[
7
, Theorem 1.1.(2)]
proved the existence of local Kähler-Einstein metric near isolated log canonical singularities and obtained a lower bound with a worse coefficient:

Fact B

Let
(X,x)
be a germ of an isolated log canonical (non-log terminal) singularity. let
\pi:Y\to X
be a log resolution, and let
E
be the reduced exceptional divisor. Fix a defining section
s_{E}
of
E
and a Hermitian metric
|\cdot|_{h}
on
\mathcal{O}_{Y}(E)
. Then there exists a local Kähler-Einstein metric
\omega=dd^{c}\varphi_{KE}
near
x
, and for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a constant
C_{\epsilon}
such that

(0.2)

\pi^{*}\varphi_{KE}\geq-(2n+\epsilon)\log(-\log|s_{E}|_{h}^{2})+C_{\epsilon}.

Nevertheless, a quantitative description of growth of the potential near the non-klt locus is still missing in general, except for some particular examples.

\exemname

\the\smf@thm
.

Let
X
be Baily-Borel-Satake compactification of a ball quotient of dimension
n
, then it has isolated singularities and admits a log resolution
\pi:Y\to X
whose exceptional divisor over each singularity is an abelian variety with discrepancy
-1
. In this case,
\nu_{X}=1
and

\pi^{*}\varphi_{KE}=-(n+1)\log(-\log|s_{E}|^{2})+\mathcal{O}(1),

where
s_{E}
is a defining section of the exceptional divisor.

\exemname

\the\smf@thm
.

(see
[
20
, p. 54-57]
)
Let
X
be a Hilbert modular surface, then X has isolated singularities. Let
\pi:Y\to X
be the minimal resolution, then the exception divisor over each singularity is a cycle of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
’s, and each
\mathbb{P}^{1}
has discrepancy
-1
. In this case,
n=\nu_{X}=2
, and

\pi^{*}\varphi_{KE}=-4\log(-\log|s_{E}|^{2})+\mathcal{O}(1),

where
s_{E}
is a defining section of the exceptional divisor.

The goal of this paper is to improve the lower bound (
0.1
), (
0.2
) by using iterated logarithmic functions associated with the non-klt locus, and to obtain an explicit upper bound which also diverges to
-\infty
near
\mathrm{Nklt}(X)
for the Kähler–Einstein potential in some special cases.

We first introduce a function
\rho
on a general stable variety which cut out the non-klt locus:

Set-up

(
Cut out non-klt locus
) Let
X
be a stable variety of dimension
n
. Let
L
be a (very) ample line bundle on
X
, and let
|\cdot|_{h}
be a Hermitian metric on
L
. Let
\mathcal{I}
be the ideal sheaf of the non-klt locus of
X
(see Definition
1.5
). After replacing
L
by a large multiple of it, we can find some sections
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{k}\in\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,L)
such that they globally generate
\mathcal{I}\otimes L
. Then the vanishing set of the function
\rho:=\sum_{i=1}^{k}|\sigma_{i}|_{h}^{2}
is precisely the non-klt locus of
X
. By rescaling the Hermitian metric, we always assume
\rho<e^{-1}
, then
-\log\rho>1
.

The two examples above show that the asymptotic behavior of
\varphi_{KE}
near
\mathrm{Nklt}(X)
is related to the combinatorial structure of exceptional divisors on a log resolution. Motivated by this, we introduce the following

\definame

\the\smf@thm
.

Let
X
be a compact complex manifold of dimension
n
, and let
D=\sum_{i\in I}D_{i}
be a reduced divisor with simple normal crossing support. We define the following function
m_{(X,D)}:D\to\{1,2,\dots,n\}
by

m_{(X,D)}(x)=\#\{i\in I:x\in\mathrm{supp}(D_{i})\}.

And we define
\nu(X,D)
to be the maximal number of
D_{i}
’s that intersect simultaneously:

\displaystyle\nu(X,D)

\displaystyle=\max_{x\in D}m_{(X,D)}(x)

Then we always have
1\leq\nu(X,D)\leq n
.

Now let
X
be a stable variety, let
\pi:(Y,E=\sum_{i}a_{i}E_{i})\to X
be a log resolution of
X
, where
E_{i}
is either an exceptional divisor or set above the codimension 1 singularities. Then we have
a_{i}\geq-1
. Let
E_{lc}=\sum_{a_{i}=-1}E_{i}
. We denote
\nu_{X}(\pi)=\nu(Y,E_{lc})
and introduce the following invariants:

\nu_{X}=\inf_{\pi}\nu_{X}(\pi),

where
\pi
runs over all log resolutions of
X
. Since
\nu_{X}(\pi)\in\{1,\dots,n\}
for every log resolution
\pi
, the infimum is always attained by some log resolution.

\remaname

\the\smf@thm
.

The quantity
\nu(X,D)-1

coincides with the dimension of the dual complex associated to
(X,D)
in the sense of de Fernex, Kollár and Xu, see
[
8
]
.

Our results can be divided into two parts: the first part is an unconditional lower bounded, which improves the lower bound obtained in (
0.1
); the second part concerns an upper bounded (which also grows in
\log\log
form) under some geometrical assumption.

0.1
Unconditional lower bound

Theorem A

(=
Theorem
3
)
.

Let
X
be a stable variety of dimension
n
, and
\omega_{X}\in c_{1}(K_{X})
be a smooth Kähler form.
Let
\omega=\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi_{KE}
be the Kähler-Einstein metric on
X
.
Then for any
k\geq 1
and
\epsilon>0
, there exists a constant
C_{k,\epsilon}
such that

\varphi_{KE}\geq-(n+\nu_{X})\sum_{j=1}^{k}\log^{(j)}(-\log\rho)-\epsilon\log^{(k)}(-\log\rho)+C_{k,\epsilon}.

Here
\rho
is the function defined in
Set-up
, and
\log^{(j)}(\cdot):=\underbrace{\log\circ\dots\circ\log}_{\text{j times}}\ (\cdot)
.

\remaname

\the\smf@thm
.

When
k=1
, our result recovers the lower bound (
0.1
):

\varphi_{KE}\geq-(n+\nu_{X}+\epsilon)\log(-\log\rho)+C_{\epsilon}.

The above two examples show that this lower bound is almost sharp (at least for isolated singularities), with an error term
-\epsilon\log(-\log\rho)
. Our result for
k\geq 2
shows that this error term can be replaced by less singular terms consisting of iterated logarithmic functions.

As we mentioned before, we know qualitatively that
\varphi_{KE}\to-\infty
near
\mathrm{Nklt}(X)
, but to understand the asymptotic behavior of
\varphi_{KE}
, we would like to have an upper bound which also diverges to
-\infty
near
\mathrm{Nklt}(X)
. In the spirit of the above two examples, some multiple of
-\log(-\log\rho)
should be a candidate. The general case however seems to be difficult, we only prove an upper bound under some additional geometric assumption on the resolution.

0.2
Conditional upper bound

Our first upper bound is the following

Theorem B

(=
Theorem
3
)
.

Let
X
be as in Theorem
A
. Let
d=\dim X_{sing}
.
Assume
X
admits a log resolution
\pi:Y\to X
satisfying

(1)

\pi
is a log crepant resolution (i.e. every exceptional divisor of
\pi
has discrepancy -1);

(2)

\pi
is moreover a log resolution of
(X,\mathcal{I})
, where
\mathcal{I}
is the ideal sheaf of
\mathrm{Nklt}(X)
(
=X_{sing}
under condition (1)).

Then there is a constant
C>0
such that

\varphi_{KE}\leq-(n-d+1)\log(-\log\rho)+C.

We shall note that this upper bound is far from being sharp, which can already be seen in the case of the Hilbert modular surface. However, the coefficient
(n-d+1)
can not be improved for a general stable variety. Indeed, if
X
has codimension 1 singularities, then
d=n-1
and
n-d+1=2
. Let
\nu:X^{\nu}\to X
be the normalization, then there exists a reduced divisor
C
on
X^{\nu}
, called the conductor divisor, such that
\nu^{*}K_{X}=K_{X^{\nu}}+C
and
K_{X^{\nu}}+C
is ample. The pull back of the Kähler-Einstein metric on
X
coincide with the Kähler-Einstein metric on the pair
(X^{\nu},C)
. In
[
17
]
, it is proved that
\omega_{KE}
has cusp singularities near
C\cap X^{\nu}_{reg}
, i.e.
\varphi_{KE}=-2\log(-\log|s_{C}|)+\mathcal{O}(1)
, where
s_{C}
is a section of
\mathcal{O}_{X^{\nu}}(C)
that cuts out
C^{\nu}
. Thus the coefficient of
-\log(-\log\rho)
can not be larger than
2
.

When
\pi
has another special form, we will be able to deduce a similar upper bound and a possibly better lower bound:

Theorem C

(=
Theorem
4
)
.

Let
X
be as in Theorem
A
. Let
d=\dim X_{sing}
. Assume
X
admits a log resolution
\pi:Y\to X
that satisfies the following two conditions:

(1)

\pi
is a log crepant resolution;

(2’)

\pi
is a composition of finitely many blow-ups along smooth centers.

We write
K_{Y}=\pi^{*}K_{X}-E
. Let
s
be a section of
\mathcal{O}_{Y}(E)
that cuts out
E
and let
h
be a Hermitian metric on
\mathcal{O}_{Y}(E)
. Then we have

-(n-d+1)\log(-\log|s|^{2}_{h})+\mathcal{O}(1)\geq\pi^{*}\varphi_{KE}\geq-(n+\nu_{X}(\pi))\log(-\log|s|^{2}_{h})+\mathcal{O}(1).

\remaname

\the\smf@thm
.

By definition we have
\nu_{X}(\pi)\geq\nu_{X}
, it is not clear that if the equality holds or not under these assumptions. If the equality holds, then we do get a sharp lower bound that improve the lower bound in Theorem
A
.

\remaname

\the\smf@thm
.

As we will see, the main difficulty for deducing an upper bound is that, there may exist an exceptional divisor lying over
\mathrm{Nklt}(X)
but with discrepancy
>-1
. It is hard to distinguish this kind of exceptional divisors with the exceptional divisors whose discrepancy
=-1
from a metric viewpoint.

The condition
(1)
in the above two theorems rules out this case. In dimension
2
, this condition is always satisfied by taking the minimal resolution. For higher dimensional case, some properties of varieties satisfying this condition is researched in
[
6
]
.

Strategy of proof

We briefly explain the main ideas of the proof. Let

\pi:Y\to X

be a log resolution. Then constructing the singular Kähler-Einstein metric on
X
boils down to solving the following degenerate Monge-Ampère equation on
Y
:

(0.3)

(\pi^{*}\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\psi)^{n}=e^{\psi}\prod_{i}|s_{i}|_{h_{i}}^{2a_{i}}\,dV_{Y},

where
K_{Y}=\pi^{*}K_{X}+\sum_{i}a_{i}E_{i},

s_{i}
is a section cutting out
E_{i}
, and
|\cdot|_{h_{i}}
is a Hermitian metric on
\mathcal{O}_{Y}(E_{i})
. Once we get a solution
\psi
on
Y
, the
\pi^{*}\omega_{X}
-pshness of
\psi
will ensure that it descends to an
\omega_{X}
-psh function
\varphi_{KE}
.

The proof of the lower bound estimate in Theorem
A
is based on constructing explicit
sub-solutions. The idea comes from
[
10
]
. More
precisely, under the notation of
Set-up
, we consider functions of the form

\psi_{k,\epsilon}=-(n+\nu_{X})\sum_{j=1}^{k}\log^{(j)}(-\log\rho)-\epsilon\log^{(k)}(-\log\rho).

The proof is divided into three main steps.

—

First, one proves that
\pi^{*}\psi_{k,\epsilon}

are
\pi^{*}\omega_{X}
-plurisubharmonic after rescaling the
Hermitian metric
|\cdot|_{h}
on
L
if necessary. Since it is pulled back from
X
, on which
\omega_{X}
is a Kähler form, this is not difficult (see Lemma
3
).

—

Secondly, one checks that
\pi^{*}\psi_{k,\epsilon}
has finite Monge–Ampère energy. This allows us to work inside the finite-energy class, where the
comparison principle
is available.

—

Thirdly, a refined integrability estimate shows that the density obtained from the candidate
sub-solution satisfies Kołodziej’s condition (see Section
2
). Solving an auxiliary Monge–Ampère equation then produces a bounded correction term
u
, such that

\pi^{*}\psi_{k,\varepsilon}+u

is a genuine sub-solution of the Kähler–Einstein equation (
0.3
).
The comparison principle then gives

\pi^{*}\varphi_{\mathrm{KE}}\geq\psi_{k,\varepsilon}+u,

which proves Theorem
A
.

For Theorem
B
, under the
additional assumption that all exceptional divisors have
discrepancy
-1
, we construct an explicit super-solution of the form

-(n-d+1)\log(-\log\rho)+C,

where
d=\dim X_{\mathrm{sing}}
. The key point is to estimate the
Monge–Ampère measure of
-\log(-\log\rho)
on the log resolution. Since
\rho
cuts out the non-klt locus, this function is singular along
E_{i}
’s that lies above
\mathrm{Nklt}(X)
. The assumption
(2)
in Theorem
B
assures that one can write locally

\log\pi^{*}\rho=\sum_{i=1}^{p}b_{i}\log|z_{i}|^{2}+O(1),

where
\cup_{i=1}^{p}(z_{i}=0)
lies above
\mathrm{Nklt}(X)=X_{sing}
. The form
dd^{c}\log(-\log\pi^{*}\rho)
has two types of terms: one involving the curvature term

dd^{c}\log\pi^{*}\rho
, and one involving

\frac{d\log\pi^{*}\rho\wedge d^{c}\log\pi^{*}\rho}{(-\log\pi^{*}\rho)^{2}}.

The latter has rank one, which simplifies the expansion of the Monge-Ampère measure, and it has poles of the form
\frac{1}{|z_{i}|^{2}}
near generic points of
(z_{i}=0),\ 1\leq i\leq p
. Our assumption
(1)
in Theorem
B
assures that this pole can be controlled by the poles in the right hand side of the Kähler-Einstein equation (
0.3
). The remaining estimates reduce to showing that certain mixed terms
involving
\pi^{*}\omega_{X}
vanish to sufficient order along the exceptional
divisors. This is where the dimension
d=\dim X_{\mathrm{sing}}
enters.

This proves that the above logarithmic function is a super-solution, and
the comparison principle yields the desired upper bound.

Finally, when the
resolution is obtained by a sequence of blow-ups along smooth centers, we will be above to construct suitable
\pi^{*}\omega_{X}
-psh functions directly on
Y
. By taking the combinatorial structure of exceptional divisors into consideration, we will be able to deduce the two-sided estimate of Theorem
C
.

1
Preliminaries

In this section, we collect some essential materials for understanding the singular Kähler-Einstein metrics.
X
will denote a compact Kähler manifold of dimension
n
unless otherwise specified. Recall that for a smooth closed real
(1,1)
-form
\theta
, a
\theta
-psh function is a quasi-psh function
\varphi
(i.e. locally the sum of a smooth function and a psh function) such that
\theta+dd^{c}\varphi\geq 0
in the sense of current. The set of
\theta
-psh functions is denoted by
\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta)
.

1.1
Non-pluripolar product.

In
[
4
]
, the authors define a non-pluripolar product which sends a
p
-tuple of any closed positive
(1,1)
-currents
(T_{1},\dots,T_{p})
to a closed positive
(p,p)
-current
\langle T_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge T_{n}\rangle
on any complex manifold (not necessarily compact Kähler). They proved that this product is always well defined on a compact Kähler manifold (cf.
[
4
, Prop.1.6]
). In particular, the non-pluripolar product
T\to\langle T^{n}\rangle
gives a well-defined measure which puts no mass on a pluripolar set.

Given a smooth
(1,1)
form
\theta
and a
\theta
-psh function
\varphi
, we define the non-pluripolar Monge-Ampère measure of
\varphi
as
\mathrm{MA}(\varphi):=\langle(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}\rangle
. We shall mention that, if
\varphi
is locally bounded outside a (complete) pluripolar subset
A\subset X
, then
\langle(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}\rangle
is well-defined if and only if the Bedford–Taylor product
(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}
, which is defined on the open subset
X\setminus A
, has locally finite mass near each point of A. In this case,
\mathrm{MA}(\varphi)=\langle(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}\rangle
is just the trivial extension of
(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}
to
X
(see
[
4
, p. 204]
).

1.2
Big cohomology class and singularity type of currents.

A
Kähler current
is a positive closed
(1,1)
-current that dominates a smooth Kähler form. We say that a cohomology class
\alpha\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
is
big
if it can be represented by a Kähler current; it is
pseudo-effective
(psef for short) if it can be represented by a positive current.

Fix a smooth form
\theta\in\alpha
, if
T_{1},T_{2}
are two closed positive currents in
\alpha
, we can write
T_{i}=\theta+dd^{c}\varphi_{i}
. We say that
T_{1}
is less singular than
T_{2}
if their global potentials satisfy
\varphi_{1}\geq\varphi_{2}+O(1)
. This definition is clearly independent of the choice of
\theta
and the potentials, hence is well defined. A closed positive current
T_{min}
in
\alpha
is said to have
minimal singularities
if it is less singular than any other positive current in
\alpha
. A
\theta
-psh function
\varphi
is said to have minimal singularities if
\theta+dd^{c}\varphi
is so. Note that this current is not unique in general. One way to construct such a current is to define the upper envelop:

V_{\theta}:=\sup\{\varphi\in\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta):\varphi\leq 0\ \text{on}\ X\}.

This is a well defined
\theta
-psh function once
\alpha
is psef. It is clear that
\theta+dd^{c}V_{\theta}
has minimal singularities.

A positive current
T=\theta+dd^{c}\varphi
is said to have
analytic singularities
if locally on
X
we have

\varphi=\frac{c}{2}\log\sum_{i=1}^{N}|f_{i}|^{2}+u,

where
u
is a smooth function and
f_{i}
’s are holomorphic functions.

1.3
Currents of full Monge-Ampère mass.

Given a smooth form
\theta
in a big cohomology class
\alpha
, the volume
\mathrm{vol}(\alpha)
of
\alpha
, introduced in
[
5
]
, satisfies following inequality
[
4
, Prop.1.20]
:

\int_{X}\mathrm{MA}(\varphi)\leq\mathrm{vol}(\alpha),

where
\varphi
is any
\theta
-psh function. The functions such that the equality holds are said to have
full Monge-Ampère mass
. These functions are also defined as the
finite energy class
, denoted by
\mathcal{E}(X,\theta)
. This class appears naturally in pluripotential theory, it can also be characterized by weighted Monge-Ampère energy functionals or be viewed as the maximal domain on which
\mathrm{MA}(\varphi)
can be defined as a measure which does not charge any pluripolar sets, for more details refer to
[
2
, §2]
,
[
4
, §2]
or
[
15
]
. What is important to us is that the following comparison principle holds
[
4
, Coro.2.3]
:

\propname

\the\smf@thm

(Comparison Principle)
.

For
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta)
, we have

\int_{\{\varphi<\psi\}}\mathrm{MA}(\psi)\leq\int_{\{\varphi<\psi\}}\mathrm{MA}(\varphi).

Now, for simplicity we assume
\theta
is semi-positive and big. In this case,
V_{\theta}\equiv 0
, and a
\theta
-psh function has minimal singularities if and only if it is bounded. To check whether a
\theta
-psh function has finite energy is generally not an easy thing. However, for an important subset
\mathcal{E}^{1}(X,\theta)\subset\mathcal{E}(X,\theta)
we do have a useful criterion. We now recall the definition of this subset. First, if
\varphi\in\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
, we set

E(\varphi):=\frac{1}{(n+1)\mathrm{vol}(\alpha)}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\int_{X}\varphi\ \langle(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{j}\wedge\theta^{n-j}\rangle,

and for any
\theta
-psh function
\varphi
, we define

E(\varphi):=\inf\left\{E(\psi):\psi\in\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta)\cap L^{\infty}(X),\ \psi\geq\varphi\right\}\in[-\infty,+\infty).

Then we set

\mathcal{E}^{1}(X,\theta)=\left\{\varphi\in\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta):E(\varphi)>-\infty\right\}.

[
4
, Proposition 2.11]
shows that: for a
\theta
-psh function
\varphi\in\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta)
,

\varphi\in\mathcal{E}^{1}(X,\theta)\Longleftrightarrow\varphi\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta)\text{ and }\int_{X}\varphi\mathrm{MA}(\varphi)>-\infty,

Then it is clear that
\mathcal{E}^{1}(X,\theta)\subset\mathcal{E}(X,\theta)
, as we mentioned before.

Now given a Borel subset
K\subset X
, the
capacity
of
K
is defined to be

\mathrm{Cap}_{\theta}(K)=\sup\left\{\int_{K}\mathrm{MA}(\varphi):\varphi\in\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta),\ 0\leq\varphi\leq 1\right\}.

To check that if
\varphi
is in
\mathcal{E}^{1}
or not, it suffices to compute the capacity decay of sublevel sets
[
2
, Lemma 2.9]
:

\lemmname

\the\smf@thm
.

Let
\varphi\in\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta)
. If

\int_{t=0}^{+\infty}t^{n}\mathrm{Cap}_{\theta}(\varphi<-t)<+\infty,

then
\varphi\in\mathcal{E}^{1}(X,\theta)
.

1.4
Condition K

Due to the breakthrough work of Kołodziej
[
23
]
, we introduce the following notion (see also
[
13
, Section 1.3]
):

\definame

\the\smf@thm

(Condition (K))
.

We say that a function
w:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
satisfies
Condition (K)
if it is convex increasing, and there is an increasing function
h:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
satisfying

\int^{\infty}\frac{1}{h(t)}dt<\infty,

such that
w(t)=t\cdot(\log(1+t))^{n}\cdot h^{n}\circ(\log(1+\log(1+t)))
.

—

if
h(t)=(e^{e^{t}-1}-1)^{\epsilon/n}\cdot(e^{t}-1)^{-1}
, then
w(t)=t^{1+\epsilon};

—

if
h(t)=(e^{t}-1)^{\epsilon/n}
, then
w(t)=t\cdot(\log(1+t))^{n+\epsilon}
;

—

if
h(t)=t^{1+\epsilon/n}
, then
w(t)=t\cdot(\log(1+t))^{n}\cdot(\log(1+\log(1+t)))^{n+\epsilon}
.

The following theorem is proved in
[
23
, Theorem 2.5.2]
(together with theorems for semi-positive and big form developed later in
[
14
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
16
]
).

\theoname

\the\smf@thm
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold of dimension
n
. Let
\theta
be a semi-positive and big
(1,1)
-form, and let
dV_{X}
be a a smooth volume form on

. Suppose that
w
is a function satisfying

.
Then for any non-negative
f\in L^{1}(X)
such that

(1.1)

\int_{X}w\circ f\ dV_{X}<\infty,

As a corollary, we have

\coroname

\the\smf@thm

has a unique bounded solution

1.5
Log canonical pairs and stable varieties.

We will use some terminology coming from the minimal model program (cf.
[
21
]
).
By definition, a
pair

(X,D)
consists of a complex projective normal variety
X
and an effective Weil
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
D
.
Assume
K_{X}+D
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier and take a log resolution
\pi:X^{\prime}\to X
, then there are rational numbers
a_{i}
such that

K_{X^{\prime}}=\pi^{*}(K_{X}+D)+\sum a_{i}E_{i},

here
E_{i}

. The rational number
a_{i}
is called the
discrepancy
of
E_{i}
.

We say that the pair
(X,D)
is a
log canonical
(
lc
for short) pair if
K_{X}+D
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier and

; it is a

(

for short) pair if
K_{X}+D
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier and
a_{i}>-1,\ \forall i
. These definitions are independent of the choice of
\pi
.

Stable varieties appear naturally when one considers compactifications of the moduli space of canonically polarized smooth varieties. They have semi-log canonical singularities:

\definame

\the\smf@thm
.

Let
X
be a projective projective variety, we say that
X
has
semi-log canonical
(
slc
for short) singularity if

1.

X
is Gorenstein in codimension
1
and satisfies Serre’s
S_{2}
condition;

2.

K_{X}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier;

3.

Let
\nu:X^{\nu}\to X
be the normalization of
X
and
C_{X^{\nu}}
be the conductor divisor on
X^{\nu}
. Then the pair
(X^{\nu},C_{X^{\nu}})
is a log canonical pair.

If
X
is slc, then
C_{X^{\nu}}
is necessarily reduced, and we have
\nu^{*}K_{X}=K_{X^{\nu}}+C_{X^{\nu}}
. Intuitively, the codimension
1
singularities of

are ordinary nodes. For more details about this notion we refer to
[
22
]
.

\definame

\the\smf@thm
.

We say that
X
is a
stable variety
if it has slc singularity and the canonical divisor
K_{X}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-ample.

\definame

\the\smf@thm
.

Let
X
be a stable variety, let
\nu:X^{\nu}\to X
be its normalization. Take a log resolution
\mu:Y\to X^{\nu}
of the lc pair
(X^{\nu},C_{X^{\nu}})
and let

. Then we have

where

. The

of

Let
\omega_{eucl}
be the euclidean metric on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Let
1\leq p\leq\nu\leq n
be integers. Let
[c_{i}^{\ell}]_{1\leq i\leq p,\ 1\leq\ell\leq\nu}

p\times\nu

\alpha_{\ell}=\sum_{1\leq i\leq p}c_{i}^{\ell}\frac{dz_{i}}{z_{i}}

\gamma_{\ell}
be a smooth
(1,0)

\mathbb{C}^{n}

Proof.

Let

\beta_{\ell}:=\alpha_{\ell}+\gamma_{\ell},\qquad\Theta:=\sqrt{-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\nu}\beta_{\ell}\wedge\overline{\beta_{\ell}}.

We want to prove that

\omega_{\mathrm{eucl}}^{n-p}\wedge\Theta^{p}\geq C_{V}\frac{\omega_{\mathrm{eucl}}^{n}}{\prod_{i=1}^{p}|z_{i}|^{2}}

in a neighbourhood of
0

Since the
p\times\nu

[c_{i}^{\ell}]

p

L_{0}=\{\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{p}:1\leq\ell_{1}<\cdots<\ell_{p}\leq\nu\}

\det(c_{i}^{\ell_{j}})_{1\leq i,j\leq p}\neq 0.

We write locally

\gamma_{\ell}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}g_{j}^{\ell}\,dz_{j},

g_{j}^{\ell}
are smooth functions. Then

\beta_{\ell}=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\left(\frac{c_{i}^{\ell}}{z_{i}}+g_{i}^{\ell}\right)dz_{i}+\sum_{j=p+1}^{n}g_{j}^{\ell}\,dz_{j}.

Consider the
p\times p
matrix

A=\left[\frac{c_{i}^{\ell_{j}}}{z_{i}}+g_{i}^{\ell}\right]_{1\leq i,j\leq p},

its determinant is

\det(A)(z):=\det\left(\frac{c_{i}^{\ell_{j}}}{z_{i}}+g_{i}^{\ell_{j}}(z)\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq p}=\frac{1}{z_{1}\cdots z_{p}}\det\left(c_{i}^{\ell_{j}}+z_{i}g_{i}^{\ell_{j}}(z)\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq p}.

Since

\det(c_{i}^{\ell_{j}})\neq 0,

the continuity of determinant implies that, after shrinking

\displaystyle\omega_{eucl}^{n-p}\wedge\Theta^{p}

\displaystyle=\omega_{eucl}^{n-p}\wedge\left(\sqrt{-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\nu}\beta_{\ell}\wedge\overline{\beta_{\ell}}\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\geq\omega_{eucl}^{n-p}\wedge\left(\iota_{L_{0}}|\det(A)|^{2}\bigwedge_{i=1}^{p}(\sqrt{-1}dz_{i}\wedge d\bar{z}_{i})\right)

\omega_{eucl}^{n-p}\wedge\Theta^{p}\geq C\,\frac{\omega_{eucl}^{n}}{\prod_{i=1}^{p}|z_{i}|^{2}},
