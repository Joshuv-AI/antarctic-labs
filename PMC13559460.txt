Machine learning-based prediction and external validation of treatment-related myelosuppression in patients with non-small cell lung cancer receiving PD-1 inhibitors plus platinum-doublet chemotherapy
Authors: Jingyao Hui, Rui Hu, Zhenni Yang, Jiale Ding, Yaru Guan, Xiaoya Li, Long Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe application of programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) inhibitors combined with platinum-based double-drug chemotherapy in patients with non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) is becoming increasingly widespread. However, the impact of this treatment regimen on the bone marrow hematopoietic system is well-defined. Therefore, we have to consider the risk of bone marrow suppression in NSCLC patients after receiving this treatment regimen. Our objective was to identify risk factors for the risk of myelosuppression, one of the serious complications of PD-1 inhibitor plus platinum-based dual-agent chemotherapy, in patients with NSCLC and to develop an effective machine learning (ML) model to predict this risk.MethodsWe retrospectively enrolled patients with NSCLC who received PD-1 inhibitor plus platinum-doublet chemotherapy at the Department of Respiratory Medicine, The First Hospital of Lanzhou University between July 2018 and March 2026. A subset of these patients was randomly divided into a training set (70%) and a test set (30%). In the training set, feature selection was performed using recursive feature elimination (RFE), least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO), and random forest (RF). Multiple ML models were constructed and evaluated, with the area under the curve (AUC) as the primary performance metric. Model interpretability was assessed using Shapley Additive Explanations (SHAP). External validation was performed using a temporally distinct subsequent cohort.ResultsFeature selection using RFE, LASSO, and RF identified age, body mass index (BMI), tumor size, platelet count, red cell distribution width (RDW), total protein, white blood cell count (WBC), and red blood cell count (RBC) as significant risk factors for myelosuppression in patients with NSCLC receiving PD-1 inhibitor plus platinum-doublet chemotherapy. Among the developed ML models, light gradient boosting machine (LightGBM) demonstrated the best performance, achieving AUCs of 0.898 in the training set, 0.841 in the test set, and 0.793 in external validation.ConclusionsThe LightGBM model effectively predicts the risk of myelosuppression in patients with NSCLC receiving PD-1 inhibitor plus platinum-doublet chemotherapy and may provide useful support for clinical decision-making.Keywords: Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), chemotherapy, machine learning (ML), prediction, myelosuppression

Highlight box

Key findings

• We developed a machine learning (ML) model to predict the risk of myelosuppression in patients with non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) receiving programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) inhibitors combined with platinum-based doublet chemotherapy, and conducted rigorous external validation.

What is known and what is new?

• Myelosuppression is a common adverse effect of chemotherapy, but previous studies have primarily focused on traditional chemotherapy regimens. There remains a gap in predictive models for myelosuppression risk specifically associated with the combination of PD-1 inhibitors and platinum-based doublet chemotherapy.

• This study is the first to identify eight independent risk factors (age, body mass index, tumor size, platelet count, red blood cell distribution width, total protein, white blood cell count, and red blood cell count) in this specific patient population, and has developed a ML prediction model based on these factors.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• The identified independent risk factors should be incorporated into clinical practice to guide early monitoring and timely intervention for these indicators, thereby reducing the risk of myelosuppression in such patients. The light gradient boosting machine model, which performed best in the study, demonstrated strong predictive performance and can provide valuable support for personalized clinical decision-making.

Introduction

Lung cancer (LC) is the most commonly diagnosed cancer and the leading cause of cancer-related death worldwide (1). In 2022, approximately 2.3 million new cases and 1.9 million deaths were reported globally (1). Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), the predominant histological subtype of LC, accounts for more than 85% of all cases. Notably, around 40% of patients are ineligible for surgery resection and therefore require systemic chemotherapy (2,3). Multimodal therapy is currently the recommended treatment strategy for NSCLC (4-6). In particular, the combination of programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) inhibitors with platinum-based doublet chemotherapy has become a standard regimen for both adjuvant and neoadjuvant treatment (7-10). This approach not only improves prognosis (10). but may also increase resectability in selected patients by influencing surgical evaluation (11). However, not all patients benefit from this regimen (12,13). Nearly half of patients with NSCLC experience adverse events following combined immunotherapy and chemotherapy, and approximately 20% require hospital readmission due to treatment-related toxicity (14).

Myelosuppression is one of the most common adverse events associated with antitumor therapy. Its pathophysiological basis involves the cytotoxic effects of anticancer agents on the bone marrow hematopoietic system (15). These effects can lead to reductions in platelet count, red blood cell count (RBC), and white blood cell count (WBC), thereby increasing the risks of bleeding, infection, and other complications. Myelosuppression may also disrupt treatment continuity and, in severe cases, become life-threatening (16,17).

Cancer-related clinical characteristics are highly complex and often strongly intercorrelated. Traditional statistical methods, such as logistic regression, are therefore increasingly insufficient for risk factor identification and risk prediction in oncology (18,19). By contrast, machine learning (ML) algorithms often perform better when applied to high-dimensional and complex datasets (20,21). However, the “black-box” nature of ML models limits interpretability. Shapley Additive Explanations (SHAP), a game theory-based method, has emerged as one of the most widely used approaches for interpreting ML models and addressing this limitation. Although several previous studies have investigated adverse events following multimodal therapy in patients with cancer (22-24), few have specifically examined the risk of myelosuppression in patients with NSCLC receiving PD-1 inhibitors combined with platinum-based doublet chemotherapy. Therefore, we collected data from patients treated at the Department of Respiratory Medicine, The First Hospital of Lanzhou University between July 2018 and March 2026. After feature selection, we constructed multiple ML models, including support vector machine (SVM), gradient boosting machine (GBM), neural network (NNET), eXtreme gradient boosting (XGBoost), light gradient boosting machine (LightGBM), and categorical boosting (CatBoost), to predict the risk of myelosuppression in this population. GBM and XGBoost capture complex nonlinear interactions through gradient boosting. LightGBM and CatBoost optimize training efficiency and missing value handling. NNET can fit highly unstructured patterns. Multi-model comparison can avoid the contingency of a single algorithm, and enhance the reliability and generalization ability of the prediction results. We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD reporting checklist (available at https://jtd.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jtd-2026-1555/rc).

Methods

Data source

A total of 400 patients with NSCLC who received PD-1 inhibitor plus platinum-doublet chemotherapy at the Department of Respiratory Medicine, The First Hospital of Lanzhou University, were included according to predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria. PD-1 inhibitors include pembrolizumab, sintilizumab, and camrelizumab. Platinum agents include cisplatin or carboplatin. The combination regimen of two-drug chemotherapy included gemcitabine, etoposide, pemetrexed, and paclitaxel. The study period spanned July 2018 to March 2026. Patients treated before January 2025 were assigned to the model development cohort (training and testing set, n=335), while those treated between January 2025 and March 2026 were assigned to a temporally distinct external validation cohort (n=65). This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. All patient data were de-identified, and the requirement of informed consent for this retrospective study was waived. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of The First Hospital of Lanzhou University (No. LDYYLL2026-615).

Data collection

All characteristics, except for the variables used to define the outcome, were defined as the first available measurement after hospital admission but before the receipt of a PD-1 inhibitor plus platinum-based dual-agent chemotherapy to ensure that the independent variables were captured strictly before the onset of the myelosuppression outcome measure. Data collection was performed strictly according to the prespecified inclusion and exclusion criteria. The inclusion criteria were: (I) pathologically confirmed NSCLC; (II) age 18–80 years; and (III) receipt of at least one cycle of PD-1 inhibitor plus platinum-doublet chemotherapy. The exclusion criteria were: (I) missing key data; (II) severe organ failure; (III) pregnancy or lactation; and (IV) a history of severe allergy to combined immunotherapy and chemotherapy.

Collected features included: (I) general characteristics, such as sex, age, and body mass index (BMI); (II) medical history, such as diabetes, hypertension, and smoking history; (III) treatment-related variables, such as treatment regimen, bone metastasis, and tumor size; and (IV) hematological and biochemical indices, such as RBC, WBC, red cell distribution width (RDW), and total protein (TP).

Outcome measure

According to guideline recommendations (25), myelosuppression was defined as a positive outcome if the minimum values of the relevant indicators within 21 days after PD-1 inhibitor plus platinum-doublet chemotherapy met the criteria for grade ≥2 myelosuppression. Specifically, the thresholds were as follows: neutrophil count <1.5×109/L, WBC <3.0×109/L, platelet count <75×109/L, and hemoglobin <100 g/L.

Model development and evaluation

Patients treated before January 2025 were randomly divided into a training set (70%) and a test set (30%). In the training set, features selected by at least two of the following three algorithms, including RFE, LASSO, and RF, were retained as the final predictors. Multiple ML models were constructed, and feature importance was ranked within each model. Model performance was evaluated using the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve, recall, sensitivity, and other metrics, with AUC as the primary index. Calibration curves and decision curve analysis (DCA) were also used to identify the optimal model. SHAP was applied to quantify the marginal contributions of individual features to the overall prediction and model output.

Statistical analysis

The training set was first preprocessed. Samples with more than 20% missing values were excluded to reduce bias; otherwise, missing values were imputed using predictive mean matching (PMM). The specific parameters were set as follows: the imputation method was PMM; the number of imputation was 5 times (m=5). The number of iterations was 5. All continuous variables with outliers were Winsorization, with the upper and lower limits truncated by 1% to minimize the effect of extreme values. Data standardization: Before model training, all continuous variables were Z-score standardized, and the standardized parameters were calculated based on the training set only and applied synchronously to the test set and external validation set to avoid data leakage. Categorical variables were directly included in the model after factorization without standardization. Duplicate records were removed. The preprocessing strategy derived from the training set was then applied to the test set and external validation set. The sample imbalance issue is addressed by using the Synthetic Minority Over-sampling Technique (SMOTE) algorithm to achieve sample balance. Feature selection was performed using RFE, RF, and LASSO, and 5-fold cross-validation during model development. Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation, and categorical variables as frequency and percentage. Categorical variables were compared using the chi-square test. Continuous variables were compared using the t-test when the assumptions of normality and homogeneity of variance were satisfied; otherwise, the Mann-Whitney U test was used. A two-sided P<0.05 was considered statistically significant. All figures and tables were generated using R version 4.5.2 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

Feature selection

Using a combined strategy based on LASSO, RFE, and RF in the training set, we identified age, BMI, tumor size, platelet count, RDW, TP, WBC, and RBC as risk factors for myelosuppression in patients with NSCLC receiving PD-1 inhibitor plus platinum-doublet chemotherapy (Figure 2A-2E). This means that patients with advanced age, poor nutritional status, larger tumor diameter, and abnormal hematological indicators such as platelet, WBC, RBC, RDW and TP may be more prone to bone marrow suppression.

SHAP-based model interpretation

To address the limited interpretability of ML models, SHAP was applied to provide an in-depth interpretation of the optimal model. In the LightGBM model, SHAP feature importance ranking indicated that age, BMI, tumor size, platelet count, RDW, TP, WBC, and RBC contributed to the model in descending order (Figure 7A). Additional SHAP visualizations included the summary plot (Figure 7B), force plots (Figure 7C,7D), the SHAP feature dependence plot visualizes the relationship between each variable and the model’s predictive contribution. For instance, age, BMI, and TP all exhibit a clear threshold effect, with their positive predictive contribution significantly increasing after exceeding a specific threshold (Figure 7E). The cumulative contribution curve indicates that the top four most important features (age, BMI, tumor size, and platelets) contribute the majority of the model’s predictive information, and the marginal contribution of subsequent features gradually decreases (Figure 7F). The sample-feature heatmap intuitively displays the feature contribution patterns of different patient groups (Figure 7G). The average absolute bar chart quantifies and validates the stability of each feature’s contribution, where age, BMI, tumor size, and platelets not only have the highest contribution but also show the most consistent effect across different samples (Figure 7H).

Discussion

In this study, we included 335 patients with NSCLC from the Department of Respiratory Medicine, The First Hospital of Lanzhou University, all of whom received PD-1 inhibitor plus platinum-doublet chemotherapy. Among them, 35 patients (10.45%) developed myelosuppression, which is lower than the previously reported incidence of 36.47% (26). One possible explanation is that grade 1 myelosuppression typically does not require clinical intervention; therefore, our outcome definition included only grade ≥2 myelosuppression. In addition, we performed external validation using a temporally distinct cohort of 65 patients. Using RF, LASSO, and RFE for feature selection, we identified age, BMI, tumor size, platelet count, RDW, TP, WBC, and RBC as key risk factors for myelosuppression in patients with NSCLC receiving PD-1 inhibitor plus platinum-doublet chemotherapy. Among all constructed ML models, the LightGBM showed the best performance, with AUCs of 0.898, 0.841, and 0.793 in the training, test, and external validation sets, respectively. In addition, DCA showed that the net clinical benefit of the model was better than that of “all intervention” or “none intervention” strategies in most coverage ranges of threshold probability, indicating that the model could effectively guide preventive intervention decision-making without increasing additional clinical burden, and had practical clinical application value. LightGBM employs a histogram-based algorithm and a leaf-wise growth strategy, enabling efficient capture of complex interactions among high-risk features. It also incorporates gradient-based one-side sampling to focus on difficult-to-classify samples, thereby improving the identification of minority-class myelosuppression events. In addition, its built-in regularization and efficient handling of complex features may contribute to its superior performance in the present study. In order to further promote the practical application of our research results, we built an interactive web risk prediction tool based on the LightGBM model developed in this study, which has been deployed on the shinyapps.io cloud platform. Clinicians can directly by the following link to access and use the tool: <https://hjy121.shinyapps.io/myemply/>. Li et al. (27). predicted the risk of myelosuppression in patients with LC receiving platinum-doublet chemotherapy in 2025; however, their study neither considered the use of PD-1 inhibitors nor included external validation. Although Dong et al. (26). applied ML methods to predict chemotherapy related adverse events, only a single RF model was used. In 2026, Chen et al. (28). developed a model to predict chemotherapy-related adverse events in patients with LC; however, their approach relied only on a nomogram rather than ML methods. To address these limitations, we employed RF, LASSO, and RFE for feature selection and developed multiple ML models, including SVM, GBM, NNET, XGBoost, LightGBM, and CatBoost, to predict the risk of myelosuppression in patients with NSCLC receiving PD-1 inhibitor plus platinum-doublet chemotherapy. Older patients were more likely to develop myelosuppression. One possible explanation is that impaired renal and hepatic function in older adults reduces the clearance of chemotherapeutic agents, thereby increasing systemic toxicity (29). In addition, older patients are often in a state of chronic inflammation, which may further suppress bone marrow hematopoietic function (1). These findings are consistent with previous reports (27). Patients with low body weight are more likely to be malnourished, which may reduce bone marrow reserve capacity and decrease the number of hematopoietic stem cells. Moreover, insufficient protein and nutrient intake may impair bone marrow repair, thereby increasing the risk of myelosuppression (30,31). This observation is also supported by previous studies (32). Larger tumors are generally associated with higher chemotherapy doses and frequencies and may also release increased levels of inflammatory mediators that suppress bone marrow hematopoiesis. Lower platelet counts and WBC levels in patients with NSCLC often indicate diminished bone marrow reserve, while concomitant bleeding and infection may further reduce tolerance to chemotherapy (33). Low RBC levels combined with elevated RDW may indicate decompensated bone marrow reserve with ongoing ineffective hematopoiesis, thereby increasing the risk of myelosuppression. In addition, low TP levels may increase the free fraction of chemotherapeutic agents, thereby enhancing toxicity; inadequate protein availability is also associated with impaired bone marrow repair (34).

Limitations

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, as a single-center study, it may be subject to selection bias. Second, the relatively small sample size may have increased the risk of type II error. Third, due to limitations in data availability, we were unable to assess dynamic changes in clinical features over time; therefore, trajectory-based analyses were not performed.

Conclusions

We developed and validated multiple ML models to predict the risk of myelosuppression in patients with NSCLC receiving PD-1 inhibitor plus platinum-doublet chemotherapy. Among these models, LightGBM demonstrated the best predictive performance and may serve as a valuable tool for risk stratification in this population.
