Public Executive Summary
Electric vehicles offer much higher well-to-wheel efficiency than gasoline vehicles and can
significantly reduce U.S. dependence on foreign oil, lower greenhouse gas emissions, improve
local air quality, and support continued technological and economic growth. Despite these
advantages, widespread adoption remains constrained by limitations in current battery
technology, including high cost, limited driving range, and long charging times. One promising
approach to address these challenges is to reduce onboard energy storage and instead deliver
power wirelessly to vehicles while they are at a traffic stop or in motion.

Wireless charging for EVs is commonly implemented using inductive approach, which relies on
magnetic coupling between coils and therefore requires ferrites for magnetic flux guidance and
shielding that add cost, weight, and volume. Inductive systems also exhibit substantial reductions
in power transfer capability and efficiency when the receiver coil is misaligned from the
transmitter pad. Capacitive wireless charging, in contrast, uses electric-field coupling and
eliminates the need for ferrites, enabling operation at much higher frequencies and allowing for
more compact, cost-effective, and misalignment-tolerant charging pads suitable for dynamic
operation. This project investigated and demonstrated a breakthrough 50-kW capacitive wireless
charging system for both stationary and dynamic EV charging, operating at multi-MHz
frequencies and achieving a power transfer density of 150 kW/m2. The system delivered a 10-
fold reduction in fringing fields, a 10-fold increase in energy transferred to a moving vehicle, and
a 4-fold reduction in grid power pulsations compared to state-of-the-art wireless charging
solutions.

The project goals were achieved through three primary technical innovations. The first
innovation focused on a novel high-power, high-frequency capacitive charging architecture
incorporating directly paralleled GaN-based multi-MHz inverters, lossless power combiners, and
split-inductor matching networks. This approach enabled the development and demonstration
of a record-breaking 50-kW capacitive wireless charging system operating at 6.78 MHz and
delivering 44.3 kW with a power transfer density of 133 kW/m2, outperforming best competing
capacitive systems by at least an order of magnitude in both power capability and power transfer
density. The second innovation involved the development of metasurface-based capacitive
couplers designed to shape the electric-field distribution and confine energy transfer to the
intended region. Using this approach, metasurface-based couplers were designed and
demonstrated for the 50-kW system, reducing fringing fields by 10-fold relative to conventional
couplers and enabling the system to meet electric-field safety limits at relevant distances from
the charging pads. The third innovation focused on the development of an intelligent energy
management system (iEMS) that applies data-driven decision methods and optimization to
schedule wireless charging events across large numbers of vehicles within a distribution network.
This system demonstrated a 4-fold reduction in peak-to-average power ratio when compared to
baseline inductive charging scenarios, indicating that dynamic capacitive charging can be
integrated into electric distribution systems without imposing excessive stress on the grid.

Together, these results show that high-power capacitive wireless charging is technically feasible
and offers a viable pathway toward more flexible and efficient electrification of transportation.

 3

By enabling high power transfer across large air gaps, maintaining performance under
misalignment, ensuring compliance with electric-field safety limits, and reducing grid stress, the
technology developed in this effort addresses key barriers to EV adoption and lays the
groundwork for future deployment of roadway-embedded charging infrastructure.

High-Power Multi-MHz Capacitive Wireless Charging System
Capacitive systems must operate at multi-MHz frequencies due to small coupling capacitances.
However, power processing capability of these systems is limited by the current ratings of
commercially available GaN transistors. Direct device paralleling can improve current handling
capability but often suffers from uneven current sharing between paralleled devices due to
device mismatch and PCB parasitics. Existing device-paralleling PCB layouts in the literature lack
the symmetry needed for stable MHz operation and are limited to a few hundred kHz. Similarly,
direct converter-level paralleling, an alternative power scaling approach, generates large
circulating currents from even minor mismatches, degrading efficiency. Passive and active
current-sharing methods developed for kHz inductive systems are ineffective at multi-MHz
frequencies. To scale power in multi-MHz capacitive wireless charging systems, a combined
strategy was developed that employs device-level paralleling through highly symmetric PCB
layouts and converter-level paralleling through lossless power combining networks. This dual
approach addresses the limitations of individual devices and converters, enabling efficient and
scalable high-power operation.

Figure 2 illustrates the proposed PCB design for directly paralleling two GaN transistors in a full-
bridge inverter at multi-MHz frequencies. Each transistor pair is placed symmetrically, with the
paralleled devices positioned on opposite PCB layers and their switch nodes interconnected using
dense via stitching to ensure symmetry, and balanced current sharing. This layout was
experimentally validated in a 6.78-MHz capacitive wireless charging system, where it
demonstrated a twofold increase in power-handling capability. Although device-level paralleling
improves performance, it becomes increasingly challenging to scale to large numbers of devices.

 6

To address this, a high-power system architecture was developed in which multiple inverters and
rectifiers are paralleled using a transformer-based power-combining network, as shown in Fig. 3.
Among several combining approaches examined, the transformer-based design offered the best
combination of scalability, efficiency, and mismatch tolerance. By combining both device-level
and converter-level paralleling, a 50-kW, 6.78-MHz capacitive wireless charging system was
developed using twelve two-transistor-paralleled full-bridge inverters (of Fig. 2) and twelve full-
bridge rectifiers. A photograph of the developed prototype is shown in Fig. 4. The system
achieved record-breaking performance of 44.3 kW output power at 230-V input and output, with
85.2% peak efficiency and 133 kW/m² power transfer density. As summarized in Fig. 5, this
system demonstrates an order-of-magnitude improvement in power capability and power
transfer density compared to the best competing capacitive systems and outperforms
commercial inductive chargers in power transfer density at least by a factor of three.

Field-Focused Coupler Development and Field Measurement
The fringing electric fields of wireless charging systems must remain within safe levels, as defined
by the International Commission on Non-Ionizing Radiation Protection (ICNIRP), in regions
occupied by people and animals, such as outside the perimeter of the vehicle chassis. Previous
approaches to reducing fringing fields in capacitive wireless charging systems primarily used
dielectric materials for field guidance. However, dielectric materials introduce frequency-
dependent losses and have limited effectiveness in large-air-gap applications. The proposed
solution to this challenge is illustrated in Fig. 6(a). Convex curved plates naturally suppress lateral
fringing fields due to their non-planar charge distribution, but their large physical volume makes
them impractical for electric-vehicle integration. To emulate the field-shaping properties of
curved plates while maintaining a compact geometry, metasurfaces were proposed to create
curved-plate-like field distributions using planar “meta-plates.” The required surface-impedance
distribution for the metasurface can be implemented using patterned metallic structures that
generate the appropriate distributed impedance. In practical capacitive wireless charging
systems, this distributed impedance can be approximated using a sequence of lumped capacitive
elements. As shown in Fig. 6(b), the metasurface-based coupler (referred to as “metacoupler”) is
constructed using multiple metallic sheets separated by controlled air gaps, each forming a
lumped capacitance that contributes to the overall field-focusing behavior.

Figure 7 shows top views of the conventional and metacoupler-based charging pads developed
for the 50-kW system. Since the electric-field probe is limited to measurements below 1000 V/m,
field measurements were performed at 500 W and then scaled to 50 kW using the proportionality
between field strength and the square root of transmitted power. At a lateral distance of 70 cm
from charging pad center (typical passenger car edge distance), the metacoupler-based charging
pad produced an electric field of 7.19 V/m, compared to 77.03 V/m for the conventional coupler,
a 10.7x reduction, as shown in Fig. 8. The measured field profiles closely match full-wave
simulations and confirm the field-focusing behavior provided by the metacoupler design. When
scaled to 50-kW operation, the designed metacoupler-based charging pad maintains fringing-
field levels at the light-duty and heavy-duty vehicle edges within 110 V/m, the ICNIRP public-
exposure limit at 6.78 MHz, demonstrating that the developed 50-kW system satisfies the
required electric-field safety standards.

Intelligent Energy Management Systems for Dynamic Wireless Charging
Dynamic wireless charging requires an energy-management framework fundamentally different
from those used in residential or stationary public charging. Unlike traditional EV charging, where
demand is largely deferrable and localized, dynamic charging involves mobile, spatially
distributed loads that interact with traffic flow, roadway geometry, and segment-level power
availability. Effective management therefore requires real-time coordination of vehicle
trajectories, stochastic energy needs, and evolving grid conditions. To support this, detailed
traffic, EV-usage, and grid datasets were collected and used to develop probabilistic models of
vehicle arrivals, travel speeds, state-of-charge evolution, charging demand, and distribution-
system loading. These data-driven models capture the uncertainties inherent in roadway-
embedded charging and provide the foundation for optimizing energy delivery at system scale.

 Figure 9: Wireless charging on multiple roads in power distribution networks.

Using these models, an intelligent energy management system (iEMS) was developed to
coordinate dynamic wireless charging across both roadway and distribution-network levels. A
representative dynamic wireless charging system within a distribution circuit is illustrated in Fig.
9. The resulting framework employs a hierarchical structure, in which a system-level operator
determines global charging targets while local controllers allocate power to individual vehicles as
they traverse each charging segment. This architecture incorporates transformer thermal limits,
feeder-loading constraints, spatial variations in charger availability, and the limited time vehicles
spend over each charging pad. The hierarchical control framework is implemented using online
optimization method which is based on model predictive control (MPC). This method can
effectively handle uncertainties in the system. The workflow of the proposed MPC-based online
iEMS optimization is illustrated in Fig. 10. To achieve scalability, reinforcement-learning-based
threshold policies were trained to approximate optimal decisions with low computational
overhead. As shown in Fig. 11, system-level simulations demonstrate more than an 80%
reduction in peak-to-average power ratio (PAR) for networks of 100 EVs, with strong
performance maintained when scaled to 1,000–5,000 EVs distributed across highway networks.

 Tasks Milestones and Deliverables
 The techno-economic and market analyses were completed and
 incorporated into an updated T2M Plan. The analysis included life-
 cycle cost comparisons for dynamic versus stationary charging,
 breakeven EV penetration estimates, and sensitivity studies on
 battery-size reduction and infrastructure cost. Results indicate that
 dynamic wireless charging becomes cost-competitive at EV
 penetration levels as low as 21% (8 - 33% under sensitivity
 scenarios) and can also reduce total ownership cost for electric
 yard tractors by 21% at electrified ports. These findings, combined
 with market segmentation, early beachhead opportunities,
 stakeholder outreach, and adoption-barrier inputs, were
 synthesized into an updated commercialization strategy and
 submitted to ARPA-E.

Project Activities
The project focused on developing a field-focused, load-leveled, capacitive dynamic wireless
charging system for electric vehicles, integrating three major technical thrusts: (i) high-power
multi-MHz capacitive wireless charging system using advanced power combining techniques, (ii)
metasurface-based capacitive couplers designed to reduce fringing electric fields while
maintaining large-air-gap, high-density power transfer, and (iii) a data-driven intelligent energy-
management system (iEMS) capable of coordinating charging for thousands of vehicles across a
distribution network. The project demonstrated a record-breaking 50-kW capacitive wireless
charging system operating at 6.78 MHz and delivering 44.3 kW with a power transfer density of

 17

133 kW/m2. Full-scale metacouplers were designed and experimentally validated, achieving 10x
fringing field reduction compared to conventional couplers and enabling the developed 50-kW
system to meet ICNIRP safety standards with light-duty and heavy-duty vehicles. The iEMS was
developed using real and synthetic traffic, charging-demand, and grid datasets and demonstrated
>80% peak-to-average power ratio (PAR) reduction for 100 EVs and a 4:1 system-level PAR
reduction when scaled to 1,000–5,000 EVs. Together, these results validate the feasibility of safe,
high-power capacitive dynamic charging and show its potential for reducing battery size
requirements, mitigating grid stress, and enabling more pervasive EV electrification. The project
benefited from a 3-month no-cost extension, in which the team was able to also identify the next
steps needed for the successful commercialization of this potentially transformative technology.

Follow-On Funding
A Plus-Up proposal has been submitted to ARPA-E to de-risk the commercialization challenges
associated with the technology developed under the currently funded project. The Plus-Up
proposal seeks to develop a fully integrated and packaged version of the dynamic capacitive
wireless charging system, deploy this system in a real-world environment in collaboration with
the local transit authority (Tompkins Consolidated Area Transit) and a major seaport (Port of Long
Beach), collect performance data, and finalize the go-to-market strategy.
