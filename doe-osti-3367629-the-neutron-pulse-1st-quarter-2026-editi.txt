  Operations Summary
 Things have been busy at National Criticality Experiments Research Center (NCERC) since the start
 of the year. This quarter NCERC hosted three weeks of criticality safety courses allowing students
 hands-on experience with fissionable material. They also performed an experiment funded by the
 DOE-NRC Collaboration (DNCSH) in support of data for TerraPower. Additionally, this quarter
 included staging, measurement, and repacking operations to prepare for future experiments.
 NCSP Hands-On Training
 The Nuclear Criticality Safety Program’s (NCSP) class
 for Criticality Safety Engineers (CSEs) course was held
 at NCERC this quarter. This class allows the engineers
 to get hands-on training with neutron detection
 equipment and techniques. During this course the
 instructors go over the regulations and safety rules for
 conducting of subcritical and critical experiments.

 Criticality Safety for PF-4
 In addition to the NCSP flagship criticality safety class,
 two courses targeting fissile material handlers at the
 LANL Plutonium Facility (PF-4) were held. These
 courses support pit production activities by reinforcing
 criticality safety and conduct of operations principals
 essential to safe, efficient operations.

ZiaCore Fuel Activities
The ZiaCore experiment demonstrates a novel
microreactor concept using commercially available power
reactor fuel, zirconium hydride moderator, and custom
heat pipes. It utilizes the Deimos testbed to drive the
electrically heated test section critical.
New, commercial uranium oxide (UO2) fuel was procured
from Westinghouse Electric Company for use in the
demonstration. NCERC personnel carefully weighed then
loaded the pellets into custom zircalloy tubes to prepare
for the experiment.

 Mixed Actinide ZPPR Plates
 In addition to the commonly used Plutonium Alloy No-
 Nickel (PANN) ZPPR plates, the NCERC inventory
 contains Plutonium Uranium Molybdenum (PUMN) and
 Plutonium Uranium High Pu-240 (PUMH) plates. These
 plates are sometimes referred to as “MOX” although
 they are not in an oxide form.
 The Plutonium Uranium Mixed Actinide (PUMA)
 experiment has been designed to use both types of
 plates for the first time at NCERC. In a prior year, a
 subset of the plates were opened to perform mass and
 dimensional measurements.
 To prepare for the PUMA experiment, the remaining
 120 SAVY containers were opened this February to
 perform Material Control and Accountability (MC&A)
 activities, label individual plates, and test fit into the

SHEBA Fuel Receipt
The Solution High Energy Burst Assembly (SHEBA) was a critical assembly operated at Technical Area
(TA)-18 at LANL. The low-enriched uranium fuel was originally blended down from a high-enriched
uranium solution. After the closure of TA-18, the uranium was precipitated out of solution at the CMR
Facility. Recently, the SHEBA fuel was shipped to NCERC for staging in preparation for future uranium
solution experiments.
