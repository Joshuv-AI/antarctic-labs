SCOPE AND OBJECTIVE
 Westinghouse Electric Company is a nuclear reactor vendor headquartered in the U.S. that is
developing advanced reactor technology for the U.S. and global markets. Westinghouse has been relying
on the neutronics Argonne Reactor Codes (ARC) executed through the NEAMS Workbench and its
PyARC module that are developed under the DOE-NE Nuclear Energy Advanced Modeling and
Simulation (NEAMS) and Advanced Reactor Technology (ART) – Fast Reactor programs. Through this
user experience, Westinghouse identified several enhancements that would benefit the ARC codes’
usability by the US industry and therefore its commercialization potential.
 The enhancements were proposed to deliver both improvements in workflow and analysis capabilities
to better support effective fast reactor core design and analysis to the nuclear industry. The PyARC
workflow was extended in this project by integrating non-neutronic ARC codes DASSH and NUBOW-
3D. The Ducted Assembly Steady-State Heat equation (DASSH) code is developed at ANL to perform
steady-state thermal hydraulic sub-channel analysis in liquid metal fast reactor assemblies to determine
optimized coolant flow and temperature distributions, which in this project was updated and validated for
lead fast reactor (LFR) applications. The interface between REBUS and NUBOW-3D were improved in
this project to assess the impact of the core restraint design and thermal induced expansion effects on the
reactivity of the core, and to model the deformations of the fuel assemblies induced by temperature and
irradiation.
 Finally, the ARC models that were extensively verified and validated through various SFR-based
modeling benchmarks are extended in this project through code-to-code comparison on relevant LFR-
specific neutronics benchmarks against Monte-Carlo neutronic solutions. Overall, this work enables
verification of the capability of the ARC codes for a wide range of Generation-IV reactor designs.
 The outcome of this project is the release of a comprehensive modeling toolkit of validated, robust
and efficient codes, as well as their user interface, that enables industry to perform a wide range of fast
reactor analyses for design and licensing of their concepts.

PROJECT ACCOMPLISHMENTS

 Benefit to DOE
In addition to benefitting LFR development, the work carried out in this project results in several benefits
to DOE by providing/enhancing tools supporting accelerated deployment of U.S. industry fast reactor
concepts (including Natrium, ARC-100, Oklo Aurora), or by supporting directly DOE-funded projects
(including DOE ART Fast Reactor and JAEA CRADA) for SFR design and modeling.

Key improvements to the PyARC, DASSH, PERSENT and NUBOW-3D codes were completed in this
project, enabling a wider range of applications and improved usability:
 - PyARC: streamlined capability to estimate DPA (displacement per atom), integration of the
 DASSH/NUBOW workflow, and verification against wide range of Monte Carlo stochastic
 simulations.
 - DASSH: added correlations to enable modeling of LFRs, and verified DASSH against high-
 fidelity CFD simulations.
 - NUBOW-3D: improved input definition and improved output
 - PERSENT: updates to the PERSENT manual were completed to detail the bowing worth
 capability. Completed code-to-code verification work based on an LFR model.

Training on these capabilities was provided to Westinghouse in March 2024. Those capabilities are
already being leveraged in most DOE and industry programs cited above.

 Market Viability

The outcome of this project is the release of a comprehensive modeling toolkit of validated, robust and
efficient codes, as well as a user interface, that enables industry to perform a wide range of fast reactor
analyses. By both broadening the range of reactor designs that can be simulated with the ARC package
and improving its usability, the work removes critical barriers for industry adoption of the ARC package
and its commercialization while accelerating the path toward licensing and commercialization of
advanced fast reactors.

 Market Interest
The capabilities developed in PyARC, PERSENT, DASSH and NUBOW in this work are already being
leveraged in several DOE-funded projects (including DOE ART Fast Reactor). They are also being
applied by ANL in support of the JAEA CRADA for JSFR development. Finally, various U.S. reactor
vendors have used or expressed interest to use some of these new capabilities in the near future.

Commercialization Achievements and Success Stories The modeling capabilities
developed were applied in Westinghouse-sponsored project (TO#3 under Westinghouse-
ANL CRADA) to optimize the design of a Lead Fast Reactor. This project optimization
helped identify high performance core concepts that meet design and safety constraints
while maximizing economics performance, thus informing industrial plans from both the
technical and business standpoint. This has been published in:
- Nicolas Stauff, Fausto Franceschini, Kyle Ramey, Scott Richards, Jun Liao, Martin
Jonson, Paolo Ferroni “Core Design and Performance of the Westinghouse Lead Fast

Reactor with UO2 and MOX Configurations”, Nuclear Engineering and Design, Volume
442, October 2025, 114266.
- Nicolas E. Stauff, Kyle Ramey, Scott Richards, Fausto Franceschini, Jun Liao, Martin
Jonson, Paolo Ferroni, “Core Design Optimization of the Westinghouse Lead Fast
Reactor,” ICAPP, Las Vegas, 2024. https://www.osti.gov/biblio/2371502

Path Forward
Additional effort is still needed to continue NUBOW-3D development (including post-
processing and PyARC integration with connection to PERSENT). Those efforts are
planned to be supported under DOE ART Fast Reactor program.

Several publications will be presented at upcoming ANS and PHYSOR conferences in
addition to those already listed to communicate the new capabilities developed as part of
this project.

Lessons Learned and Mitigating Actions
Challenges that were observed in this project and mitigating actions taken:
- ANL staff developing the original version of DASSH left the project. We took the
 action to transition to a new Fortran-based version of DASSH that enables improved
 maintainability and better computing performance, overall facilitating use and
 distribution to the broader community
- The level of effort required to improve NUBOW-3D was under-estimated. Support
 from other programs (DOE-NE ART/FR) was obtained to help support this task.
