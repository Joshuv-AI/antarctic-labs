Chiral-Induced Spin Selectivity Regulates Triplet formation in Heliobacterial Photosynthesis Preprint: AIP/123-QED
Parul Raghuvanshi, Vishvendra Singh Poonia

Abstract
Triplet formation and its regulation have always been of central interest in understanding the photophysical behavior of living systems. In organic systems, excessive triplet formation poses significant challenges, as it can promote photochemical damage and reduce the efficiency of charge separation processes, making its regulation critically important.Here, we present a theoretical investigation of the intrinsic quantum spin dynamics governing triplet formation in the heliobacterial reaction center, a system that operates without any internal magnetic field. Using an open quantum systems approach based on the Lindblad formalism, we simulate the spin-correlated radical pair dynamics occurring during charge separation in the heliobacterial reaction center. The study systematically examines how triplet formation is regulated by variations in two key parameters, hyperfine coupling strengths and recombination rates, and how this regulation is further influenced by the inclusion of chirality-induced spin selectivity (CISS) in conjunction with the radical pair mechanism (RPM). Our results demonstrate that the CISS effect significantly suppresses triplet formation across the parameter space relevant to the heliobacterial molecular environment, revealing an intrinsic quantum protective mechanism operating through spin control in heliobacterial photosynthesis.

In this work, we observe that triplet formation is strongly regulated in the parameter space of hyperfine coupling and recombination time when the CISS effect is incorporated with the radical pair mechanism in heliobacteria. We find that the triplet yield is highly sensitive to the angle
\chi
, which controls the degree of spin-selective electron transfer in chiral medium, reflecting the significant role of the CISS effect in governing the spin dynamics.Across all considered scenarios, the triplet yield is slightly higher for intermediate angles (
\chi=\pi/6,\pi/4,
and
\pi/3
) compared to both the
\chi=0
and
\chi=\pi/2
cases. In contrast, the configuration
\chi=\pi/2
leads to a robust and pronounced suppression of the triplet yield across the majority of the hyperfine coupling–recombination time parameter space. We further observe that in all plots , the weak hyperfine coupling region (
-6
MHz to
6
MHz) emerges as a critical window. Within this narrow interval, the triplet yield undergoes rapid and significant variation, indicating that the spin dynamics are highly sensitive . This arises due to the near-degeneracy between the singlet and triplet states, which makes the system highly responsive to small perturbations in the molecular environment, leading to enhanced singlet-triplet mixing. In this regime, the mixing is so efficient that the system does not remain in a single spin state; instead, it rapidly oscillates between states. However, strong triplet suppression does not occur in this regime. Rather, suppression emerges in the moderate-to-strong coupling regime (Regions 3 and 5), where a finite energy splitting allows coherent spin evolution over longer timescales.
These two regions are characterized by consistently strong triplet suppression as the angle
\chi
increases, suggesting as they may play a central role in the protective spin dynamics of the system. To identify which nuclei are physically relevant to these regimes, we surveyed the literature
16
for hyperfine coupling values of nuclei that are present in the molecular environment of heliobacteria that fall within these regimes. From this analysis, we find that the nuclei lying in Region 3 are C10, C12, C1, and C3, along with isotopes of
15
N, specifically N21 and N24, while the nuclei found out in Region 5 are C6 and C16. Since these nuclei fall within these regimes that exhibit the most consistent and sustained triplet suppression under CISS effect, we propose that these nuclei play a significant role in protecting the system by suppressing triplet state formation within their respective coupling regimes. This suggests that the specific hyperfine environment created by these nuclei may be evolutionarily optimized to enhance the quantum protective mechanism within the radical pair system of heliobacteria.
In the present study, we have restricted our analysis to systems with up to two nuclear spins, which is sufficient to capture the essential physics of the radical pair mechanism (RPM) in conjunction with the CISS effect in heliobacteria. In both cases, the overall qualitative behavior of the triplet yield remains similar, confirming the robustness of the observed trends. Nevertheless, this work can be extended to systems with a larger number of nuclear spins to investigate the spin dynamics more comprehensively. Future studies may also incorporate additional spin interaction parameters, such as exchange and dipolar coupling, to explore their influence on spin dynamics and triplet yield suppression, and further investigate how chirality-induced spin selectivity contributes to the protective mechanism in heliobacteria when considering a larger and more realistic molecular environment.
