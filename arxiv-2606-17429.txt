Extension of Ulrich bundles
Supravat Sarkar

Abstract
. We study extension of Ulrich bundles from a smooth nondegenerate subvariety X X of ℙ n \mathbb{P}^{n} . If X X is a complete intersection of dimension ≥ 2 \geq 2 , we show that the extension is not possible except in the trivial case. For an arbitrary X X , we characterize when the extension is possible, assuming some condition on the extended vector bundle. As an application, we generalize previous results of López and Zamora. We also give several classes of examples of Ulrich bundles on curves that extend to the ambient projective space.



Keywords
: Ulrich bundle, extension

MSC Number:14J60, 14H60

1.
Introduction

We work throughout over the field
k=\mathbb{C}
of complex numbers. Ulrich bundles are important objects of study from the viewpoints of both commutative algebra and algebraic geometry. Let us recall the definition. Let
X
be a smooth subvariety of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, and
E
a nonzero vector bundle on
X
. In
[
10
]
, the following were proved to be equivalent:

(1)

There exists a linear resolution

0\to L_{c}\to L_{c-1}\to\cdots\to L_{0}\to E\to 0,

with
c=\operatorname{codim}(X,\mathbb{P}^{n})

and
L_{i}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(-i)^{\oplus b_{i}}.

(2)

The cohomology
H^{\bullet}(X,E(-p))

vanishes for
1\leq p\leq\dim(X).

(3)

If
\pi:X\to\mathbb{P}^{\dim(X)}

is a finite linear projection, then the vector bundle
\pi_{*}E

is trivial.

We call
E
an
Ulrich bundle
if these equivalent conditions are satisfied. By condition
(2)
, Ulrichness of a vector bundle on
X
depends only on the very ample line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)
, not on the space of sections giving the projective embedding. So for any smooth projective variety
X
and very ample line bundle
L
on
X
, we call a vector bundle
E
on
X
to be
L
-Ulrich if
H^{i}(X,E\otimes L^{-p})=0
for all
i
and
1\leq p\leq\dim X.
We usually omit
L
when
L=\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)
for a given projective embedding of
X
. For details about Ulrich bundles, see
[
3
]
,
[
7
]
or
[
9
]
. Ulrich bundles are closely related to several important areas: minimal resolution conjecture (
[
2
]
), representations of Clifford algebras(
[
6
]
), Boij–Söderberg theory (
[
11
]
).

The goal of this article is to study when an Ulrich bundle on
X
extends to a vector bundle on the linear span of
X
in the ambient space
\mathbb{P}^{n}
. To avoid unnecessarily complicated notations and trivialities, we will assume
X
is nondegenerate, that is, not contained in a hyperplane. A major conjecture in the area of Ulrich bundles, proposed in
[
10
]
, says that every smooth subvariety of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
carries an Ulrich bundle. This was proved for complete intersections in
[
16
]
. Our first result says that a Ulrich bundle on a complete intersection of dimension
\geq 2
never extends as a vector bundle to the ambient space, except in the trivial cases.

Theorem A
.

Let
X
be a smooth nondegenerate subvariety of
\mathbb{P}^{n}

of dimension
\geq 2
which is a complete intersection.
Suppose
\mathcal{E}
is a vector bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
such that

\mathcal{E}|_{X}
is Ulrich. Then
X=\mathbb{P}^{n}

and
\mathcal{E}
is trivial.

Next, we study extension of Ulrich bundles from an arbitrary smooth nondegenerate subvariety of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, but we impose some condition on the extended vector bundle. For
n\geq 1
and a nonzero vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, let
c_{1}(\mathcal{E})
be its first Chern class, regarded as an integer by the identification
\mathbb{Z}\cong\text{Pic }\mathbb{P}^{n}
taking
1
to the ample generator. Define
\mu(\mathcal{E}):=\frac{c_{1}(\mathcal{E})}{\operatorname{rank}\mathcal{E}}
, and
\tau(\mathcal{E})=\mu(\mathcal{E}(k))
, where
k
is the smallest integer such that
\mathcal{E}(k)
is globally generated. One clearly have
\tau(\mathcal{E}(t))=\tau(\mathcal{E})

for all
t\in\mathbb{Z}
. Also,
\tau(\mathcal{E})\geq 0
, and
\tau(\mathcal{E})=0
if and only if
\mathcal{E}
is trivial upto line bundle twist. As examples, it is not hard to show that
\tau(T_{\mathbb{P}^{n}})=\frac{1}{n}
and
\tau(\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}})=\frac{n-1}{n}
, where
T_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
and
\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
are tangent and cotangent bundles of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
.

Our result is the following.

Theorem B
.

Let
n,l,r
be positive integers,
X
a nondegenerate smooth
subvariety of degree
l
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, and

\mathcal{E}
a rank
r
vector bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
such that

\mathcal{E}|_{X}
is
\mathcal{O}_{X}(a)
-Ulrich. Suppose
\tau(\mathcal{E})<1.

Then
\mathcal{E}(1-a)
is globally generated and one of the following
holds:

(1)

a=n=2
,
X=\mathbb{P}^{2}
,
2\mid r
, and
\mathcal{E}\cong T_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}^{\oplus\frac{r}{2}},

(2)

X\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
,
l\mid r
,

(3)

a=1
,
X=\mathbb{P}^{n}
, and
\mathcal{E}
is trivial.

(4)

a=1
,
l=4
,
n=4
or
5
, and
X\cong\mathbb{P}^{2}.

(5)

a=1
,
X\cong\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(E)

for some ample vector bundle
E
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, and
\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(E)}(1).

Also, except in (1), we have
\tau(\mathcal{E})=\frac{l-1}{l}.

As an application to Theorem
B
, we determine when twists of the wedge powers and some symmetric and tensor powers of
T_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
restricts to Ulrich bundles on subvarieties.

Theorem C
.

Let
a,k,n,p
be integers with
p,a,n>0
and
p<n
.
Suppose
X
is a nondegenerate smooth subvariety of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
.

(1)

Suppose
\left(\bigwedge^{p}T_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\right)(k)\big|_{X}

is
\mathcal{O}_{X}(a)
-Ulrich. Then
p=n-1
and one of the following holds:

(a)

X
is a rational normal curve,
a=1
and
k=-(n-1)
.

(b)

a=n=2
,
X=\mathbb{P}^{2}
and
k=0
.

(2)

Suppose
\left(S^{p}T_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\right)(k)\big|_{X}

is
\mathcal{O}_{X}(a)
-Ulrich. Then the same conclusion as in (1) holds.

(3)

Suppose
T_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{\otimes p}(k)\big|_{X}

is
\mathcal{O}_{X}(a)
-Ulrich. Then the same conclusion as in (1) holds.

Here by a
rational normal curve
we mean the image of the
n
-uple embedding
\mathbb{P}^{1}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n}
, or its translate by an automorphism of
\mathbb{P}^{n}.

The converse of Theorem
C
in each case is true, and can be proved using Lemma
3.2
. Since
\Omega^{p}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
is a twist of
\bigwedge^{n-p}T_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
, Theorem
C
(1) also determines when
\Omega^{p}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(k)|_{X}
is
\mathcal{O}_{X}(a)
-Ulrich. In particular, the cases
p=1
and
n-1
of Theorem
C

(1)
is equivalent to the characterization of
(X,a,k)
such that
T_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(k)|_{X}
or
\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(k)|_{X}
is
\mathcal{O}_{X}(a)
-Ulrich, which is essentially the content of
[
19
, Theorem 1.2]
, as explained in
[
19
, §2]
. So, our result generalizes
[
19
]
.

Finally, we give a whole class of examples of Ulrich bundles on curves that extend to vector bundles on the ambient space
\mathbb{P}^{n}.

Theorem D
.

(1)

Let
X
be a smooth projective curve that is a complete intersection in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
. Then there is a nontrivial Ulrich bundle on
X
that extends to a vector bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
.

(2)

Let
a,l,n
be positive integers. Suppose either

l=n=2
or
l\geq n\geq 3
. Let
X
be a smooth rational
curve of degree
l
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, and
\mathcal{F}
an

\mathcal{O}_{X}(a)
-Ulrich bundle on
X
of rank
r
with

l\mid r
. Then
\mathcal{F}
extends to a vector bundle
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
on

\mathbb{P}^{n}
with
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}(1-a)
globally generated.

A couple of comments regarding the global generation conclusion of Theorem
D
(2): given
\mathcal{F}
on
X
, we want to extend it to
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
that has as much positivity property as possible. Since
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{F}(-a))=0
by Ulrichness, we cannot have
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}(-a)
to be globally generated. In this sense, Theorem
D
(2) gives the optimal result. Without the global generation, Theorem
D
(2) would also follow from
[
20
]
. Our new content here is the global generation, which is a very strong property for vector bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, see
[
1
]
.

2.
Extending Ulrich bundles from complete intersections

In this section, we will prove Theorem
A
. First we prove some Lemmas. Recall that a vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
on a subvariety
X
on
\mathbb{P}^{N}
is called
arithmetically Cohen-Macaulay
(ACM for short) if
H^{i}(X,\mathcal{E}(k))=0
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
0<i<\dim X
. Ulrich bundles are ACM by
[
3
, §2, (2.1)]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
be a Cohen–Macaulay subvariety of
dimension
n\geq 3
,
H
a hypersurface in
\mathbb{P}^{N}

not containing
X
. Suppose
D:=X\cap H

is integral, and
\mathcal{E}
is a vector bundle on
X
such that

\mathcal{E}|_{D}
is ACM. Then
\mathcal{E}
is ACM.

Proof.

Replacing
\mathcal{E}
by
\mathcal{E}(k)
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, it suffices to show
H^{i}(X,\mathcal{E})=0

for
0<i<n.
The short exact sequence

(1)

0\to\mathcal{E}(m)\to\mathcal{E}(m+1)\to\mathcal{E}(m+1)|_{D}\to 0

for
m\in\mathbb{Z}
and the assumption that
\mathcal{E}|_{D}
is ACM
shows that there are injections

H^{i}(X,\mathcal{E}(m))\hookrightarrow H^{i}(X,\mathcal{E}(m+1))\text{ for }2\leq i<n\text{ and }m\in\mathbb{Z}.

Thus, we have an injection
H^{i}(X,\mathcal{E})\hookrightarrow H^{i}(X,\mathcal{E}(t))

for all
t>0
and
2\leq i<n.
By Serre vanishing,
H^{i}(X,\mathcal{E}(t))=0

for
t\gg 0,

hence
H^{i}(X,\mathcal{E})=0

for
2\leq i<n.

Again by (
1
), and using
\dim D\geq 2
, we have surjections

H^{1}(X,\mathcal{E}(m))\twoheadrightarrow H^{1}(X,\mathcal{E}(m+1))

for all
m\in\mathbb{Z}
. Thus we have a surjection
H^{1}(X,\mathcal{E}(-t))\twoheadrightarrow H^{1}(X,\mathcal{E})

for all
t>0
. As
X
is Cohen-Macaulay, we have
H^{1}(X,\mathcal{E}(-t))=0

for all
t\gg 0
by

[
15
, Theorem III.7.6]
.
Thus,
H^{1}(X,\mathcal{E})=0.

∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose
X
is a nondegenerate smooth subvariety of
\mathbb{P}^{N}
and

a>0
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}
are such that
\mathcal{O}_{X}(k)

is
\mathcal{O}_{X}(a)
-Ulrich. Then
X=\mathbb{P}^{N}
and
a=k+1.

Further, if
N\geq 2
, then
k=0
.

Proof.

Let
n=\dim X.
Since
h^{0}\bigl(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(k-a)\bigr)=0,

we have
k\leq a-1.
Note that

(2)

h^{0}\bigl(X,K_{X}(na-k)\bigr)=h^{n}\bigl(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(k-na)\bigr)=0

by Serre duality.

Fujita’s conjecture is true for basepoint-free ample line bundles
(see
[
18
, §5.6]
),
So,
K_{X}(n+1)

is globally generated.

This forces
na-k\leq n,

hence

n(a-1)\leq k\leq a-1.

So equality holds, hence
a=k+1,
and
(a-1)(n-1)=0.

If
n\geq 2
, then
a=1
, so
k=0
.
By
[
3
, §2, (2.1)]
,
\deg X=h^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=1.
So
X=\mathbb{P}^{N}
, being nondegenerate.

If
n=1
, then by (
2
),
h^{0}\bigl(X,K_{X}(1)\bigr)=0,

so
h^{0}(X,K_{X})=0.
This implies
X\cong\mathbb{P}^{1},

and
\deg K_{X}(1)<0.
As
\deg K_{X}(1)=-2+\deg X,

we get
\deg X=1,

so
N=1
and
X=\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
∎

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
A
.

Proof of Theorem
A
:

Proof.

As
X
is a complete intersection, there are Cohen–Macaulay
subvarieties of
\mathbb{P}^{n}

X=X_{0}\subsetneq X_{1}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq X_{c}=\mathbb{P}^{n},

where each
X_{i}
is the intersection of
X_{i+1}
with a hypersurface
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
.

Since
\mathcal{E}|_{X}
is ACM, being Ulrich, repeated application of Lemma
2.1
shows that
\mathcal{E}
is ACM.
By Horrocks’ criterion (see
[
21
, Section 2.3]
),
\mathcal{E}
is split, that is,
\mathcal{E}\cong\bigoplus_{i}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(a_{i}).

So each
\mathcal{O}_{X}(a_{i})
is Ulrich. By Lemma
2.2
, we have
X=\mathbb{P}^{n}
and
a_{i}=0
for all
i
. So
\mathcal{E}
is trivial.
∎

3.
Extending Ulrich bundles from arbitrary subvarieties

In this section, we will prove Theorem
B
and
C
. First we prove the following Lemmas.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
n
be a positive integer,
\mathcal{E}
a vector bundle on

\mathbb{P}^{n}
of rank
r>0.
We have the following:

(1)

For
1\leq p\leq n-1
, we have
\mu\!\left(\bigwedge^{p}\mathcal{E}\right)=p\,\mu(\mathcal{E}),

and
\tau\!\left(\bigwedge^{p}\mathcal{E}\right)\leq p\,\tau(\mathcal{E})
,

(2)

For
p>0
,
we have
\mu\!\left(S^{p}\mathcal{E}\right)=p\,\mu(\mathcal{E}),

and
\tau\!\left(S^{p}\mathcal{E}\right)\leq p\,\tau(\mathcal{E})
,

(3)

For
p>0
,
we have
\mu\!\left(\mathcal{E}^{\otimes p}\right)=p\,\mu(\mathcal{E}),

and
\tau\!\left(\mathcal{E}^{\otimes p}\right)\leq p\,\tau(\mathcal{E})
,

(4)

If
H
is a linear subvariety of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
of dimension
\geq 1
, then
\tau(\mathcal{E}|_{H})\leq\tau(\mathcal{E})
.

Proof.

For (4), note that if
\mathcal{E}(k)
is globally generated then so is
\mathcal{E}(k)|_{H}
.

To prove first part of (1), we can restrict
\mathcal{E}
to a line. So we can assume
n=1
, hence
\mathcal{E}
is split. The rest is a
standard computation and can also be deduced from

[
13
, Lemma 2.1]
.
For the second part, note that if
k
is the smallest integer such that

\mathcal{E}(k)
is globally generated, then
\bigwedge^{p}(\mathcal{E}(k))=(\bigwedge^{p}\mathcal{E})(kp)

is also globally generated. So

\tau\!\left(\bigwedge^{p}\mathcal{E}\right)\leq\mu\!\left(\bigwedge^{p}(\mathcal{E}(k))\right)=p\,\mu(\mathcal{E}(k))=p\,\tau(\mathcal{E}).

Proofs of (2) and (3) are exactly similar.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
n
be a positive integer,
i:\mathbb{P}^{1}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n}

the
n
-uple embedding. We have

(i)

i^{*}\!\left(\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(2)\right)\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(n-1)^{\oplus n}.

(ii)

i^{*}\!\left(T_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(-1)\right)\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)^{\oplus n}.

Proof.

(i)
: It suffices to show that
i^{*}\!\left(\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(2)\right)

is
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(n)
-Ulrich. This can be seen by restricting the Euler exact sequence of

\mathbb{P}^{n}
to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. It also follows from the “if” part of

[
19
, Theorem 1.2(ii)]
.

(ii): Follows from (i), and the observation that

T_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(-1)\cong\bigwedge^{n-1}\!\left(\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(2)\right)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(-n+2).

∎

Proof of Theorem
B
:

Proof.

Suppose the Theorem is not true. We want to get a contradiction. Let
X
be such that the Theorem fails and
d=\dim X
be smallest, let
L=\mathcal{O}_{X}(1),c=c_{1}(\mathcal{E}).
So, the top self-intersection of
L
is
l.
As
X
is nondegenerate and
n>0
, we have
d>0.

We consider two cases separately.

Case 1:
d=1
.

Let
c=c_{1}(\mathcal{E})
, and
k
the smallest integer so that
\mathcal{E}(k)
is globally generated. So,
\tau(\mathcal{E})=\frac{c_{1}(\mathcal{E}(k))}{r}=\frac{c}{r}+k
.
Also,
\mathcal{E}(k^{\prime})
is globally generated for all
k^{\prime}\geq k.

Since
\mathcal{E}|_{X}
is
\mathcal{O}_{X}(a)
-Ulrich, so

H^{\bullet}\bigl(X,\mathcal{E}(-a)|_{X}\bigr)=0.

So
\mathcal{E}(-a)
is not globally generated, hence
-a\leq k-1,

that is,

(3)

a+k\geq 1.

We have

\deg(\mathcal{E}(-a)|_{X})=l(c-ra),

and

\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{E}(-a)|_{X})=r.

By Riemann-Roch,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\chi\bigl(X,\mathcal{E}(-a)|_{X}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\deg(\mathcal{E}(-a)|_{X})+(1-g)\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{E}(-a)|_{X})

\displaystyle=l(c-ra)+r(1-g).

So,
cl=r(g-1+al),

that is,

(4)

\frac{c}{r}=\frac{g-1}{l}+a.

hence,

(5)

\tau(\mathcal{E})=\frac{g-1}{l}+a+k.

Now using (
3
),

1>\tau(\mathcal{E})=\frac{g-1}{l}+a+k\geq\frac{g-1}{l}+1,

so
\frac{g-1}{l}<0,
hence
g=0
. So,
X\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

Now by (
5
),

1>\tau(\mathcal{E})=a+k-\frac{1}{l},

so using (
3
) again,

1\leq a+k<1+\frac{1}{l}.

Hence
a+k=1
, and
\tau(\mathcal{E})=\frac{l-1}{l}.
Also,
\mathcal{E}(1-a)=\mathcal{E}(k)
is globally generated. Further, by (
4
),
\frac{r}{l}=ar-c\in\mathbb{Z}
, so
r\mid l
. So (2) of Theorem
B
holds, a contradiction.

Case 2:
d\geq 2
.

Subcase 1:
a>1
.

Let
F
be a general degree
a
hypersurface in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
and let
Y=F\cap X.

Then
Y
is nondegenerate in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
: otherwise there is a hyperplane

H
with
F\cap X\subseteq H\cap X,
taking degrees we get
al\leq l,

a contradiction.

By
[
3
, §2, (2.4)]
,
\mathcal{E}|_{Y}
is
\mathcal{O}_{Y}(a)
-Ulrich.
By minimality of
d
, Theorem
B
is valid for
(Y,\mathcal{E})
.
As
\dim Y<\dim X\leq n

and
a>1
, we see that (2) of Theorem
B
is the only possibility, with
(X,l)
replaced by

(Y,\deg Y=al)
. So,
d=2
and
Y\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}.

Noting that
\mathcal{O}_{X}(Y)\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}(a)

and applying adjunction,

-2=aL\cdot(aL+K_{X})=a^{2}L^{2}+aL\cdot K_{X}=a\bigl(L\cdot K_{X}+al\bigr).

Hence
a\mid 2.
As
a>1
, we have
a=2.
So
L\cdot K_{X}=-2l-1.

Applying adjunction for a smooth hyperplane section of
X
of genus
g\geq 0
, we get

-2\leq 2g-2=L\cdot(L+K_{X})=l+L\cdot K_{X}=-l-1.

So,
l\leq 1,
that is,
l=1.
As
X
is nondegenerate, we have
X=\mathbb{P}^{n},
and

n=d=2.

As
\mathcal{E}
is
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(2)
-Ulrich, by

[
5
, §5]
, we have
2\mid r
and
\mathcal{E}\cong T_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}^{\oplus r/2}.

Thus (1) of Theorem
B
holds, a contradiction.

Subcase 2:
a=1
.

Let
H^{\prime}
be a general linear subvariety of codimension
d-1
in

\mathbb{P}^{n}
,
Y:=X\cap H^{\prime},
and
H
be the linear span of
Y
.
So,
Y
is a smooth connected curve,
Y\hookrightarrow H
is
nondegenerate, and
\mathcal{E}|_{Y}
is
\mathcal{O}_{Y}(1)
-Ulrich by
[
3
, §2, (2.4)]
.
Also by Lemma
3.1
, we have
\tau(\mathcal{E}|_{H})\leq\tau(\mathcal{E})<1.

Now minimality of
d
shows Theorem
B
is true for

(Y,\mathcal{E}|_{H})
. Hence
Y\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
. So, the sectional genus
g(X,L)
of
X
is
0
. By
[
17
, Theorem 1.2]
, we have
\Delta(X,L)=0.
Using nondegeneracy of
X
and
[
12
, Corollary 4.3 and Theorem 4.9]
, we see that one of (3)-(5) in Theorem
B
holds, or
X
is a smooth quadric hypersurface. But this last case is ruled out by Theorem
A
.

Also, as Theorem
B
is true for

(Y,\mathcal{E}|_{H})
, we have
\mathcal{E}|_{H}
globally generated, and
\tau(\mathcal{E}|_{H})=\frac{l-1}{l}.
As
\mathcal{E}|_{Y}
is Ulrich, so
\mathcal{E}(-1)|_{H}
is not globally generated. So,
\tau(\mathcal{E}|_{H})=\mu(\mathcal{E}|_{H})
. If
\mathcal{E}
is not globally generated, then

1>\tau(\mathcal{E})\geq\mu(\mathcal{E}(1))=\mu(\mathcal{E}|_{H})+1=2-\frac{1}{l}\geq 1,

a contradiction. So,
\mathcal{E}
is globally generated,
\tau(\mathcal{E})=\mu(\mathcal{E})=\mu(\mathcal{E}|_{H})=\frac{l-1}{l}.
So, again Theorem
B
holds, a contradiction.
∎

Now we prove Theorem
C
, as an application of Theorem
B
.

Proof of Theorem
C
:

Proof.

First of all, note that by Lemma
3.1
, we have

\tau\!\left(\left(\bigwedge^{p}T_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\right)(k)\right)\leq\frac{p}{n}<1,

so we can apply Theorem
B
. We consider two cases separately.

Case 1:

\dim X=1
.

As
X
is nondegenerate, we have
l\geq n
. By Theorem
B
,

X\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
, and

1-\frac{1}{l}=\tau\!\left(\left(\bigwedge^{p}T_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\right)(k)\right)\leq\frac{p}{n}\leq 1-\frac{1}{n}\leq 1-\frac{1}{l}.

This forces
p=n-1
and
l=n
, hence
X
is a rational normal curve. The rest is an easy computation using Lemma
3.2
, noting that
\left(\bigwedge^{n-1}(T_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(-1))\right)\Big|_{X}

is a direct sum of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(n-1)
.

Case 2:

\dim X\geq 2
.

If
a>1
, then we see that only possibility is that (1) in Theorem
B

holds with
r=2
. So (b) holds.

Now suppose
a=1
. Let
H
be a general hyperplane in

\mathbb{P}^{n}
and
Y=X\cap H
. Since

T_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}|_{H}\simeq T_{H}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{H}(1).

Note that
\left(\bigwedge^{p}T_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\right)(k)\Big|_{H}

contains both
\left(\bigwedge^{p}T_{H}\right)(k)

and
\left(\bigwedge^{p-1}T_{H}\right)(k+1)

as direct summands.

By
[
3
, §2, (2.4)]
,
\left(\bigwedge^{p}T_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\right)(k)\Big|_{Y}

is
\mathcal{O}_{Y}(1)
-Ulrich, so both
\left(\bigwedge^{p}T_{H}\right)(k)\Big|_{Y}
and

\left(\bigwedge^{p-1}T_{H}\right)(k+1)\Big|_{Y}

are
\mathcal{O}_{Y}(1)
-Ulrich.

By
[
4
, Lemma 2.4(iii)]
,
we have

\mu\!\left(\left(\bigwedge^{p}T_{H}\right)(k)\right)=\mu\!\left(\left(\bigwedge^{p-1}T_{H}\right)(k+1)\right).

Now using Lemma
3.1
, this forces
p=p-1,

a contradiction.

∎

Remark 3.3
.

Since
\tau\bigl(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(k)\bigr)=0,
Lemma
2.2
can also be considered as a consequence of Theorem
B
.

4.
Extending vector bundles from rational curves

In this section, we prove Theorem
D
. We need the following Lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
l\geq 2
be an integer, and

k[X,Y]_{l^{2}-l}\otimes k[X,Y]_{l-2}\xrightarrow{\ \beta\ }k[X,Y]_{l^{2}-2}

be the multiplication map. If
V\subseteq k[X,Y]_{l-2}

is a general subspace of dimension
l+1
, then
\ker\beta\cap\bigl(V\otimes k[X,Y]_{l-2}\bigr)=0.

Proof.

It suffices to exhibit some subspace
V\subseteq k[X,Y]_{l-2}

of dimension
l+1
such that
\ker\beta\cap\bigl(V\otimes k[X,Y]_{l-2}\bigr)=0.

We claim that

V:=\operatorname{span}\left\{X^{i(l-1)}Y^{(l-i)(l-1)}\;\middle|\;0\leq i\leq l\right\}

works.

Note that

\operatorname{codim}\ker\beta=\dim k[X,Y]_{l^{2}-2}=l^{2}-1=(l-1)(l+1)=\dim\bigl(V\otimes k[X,Y]_{l-2}\bigr).

So it suffices to show that the restriction of
\beta
,

V\otimes k[X,Y]_{l-2}\xrightarrow{\ \beta_{1}\ }k[X,Y]_{l^{2}-2}

is surjective.

Let
0\leq t\leq l^{2}-2.

We show
X^{t}Y^{l^{2}-2-t}\in\operatorname{im}\beta_{1}.

Write
t=(l-1)q+r,
with
0\leq q\leq l,0\leq r\leq l-2.

We have

X^{t}Y^{l^{2}-2-t}=X^{q(l-1)}Y^{(l-q)(l-1)}\cdot X^{r}Y^{l-2-r}\in\operatorname{im}\beta_{1}.

∎

The following Theorem is key to the proof of Theorem
D
(2).

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
i:\mathbb{P}^{1}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n}

be an embedding whose image is not contained in a hyperplane and
i^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(1)=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(l).

Then there is a globally generated vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
with
i^{*}\mathcal{E}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(l-1)^{\oplus l}
.

Proof.

Note that since the image of
i
is not contained in a hyperplane, we have
l\geq n
.

We first introduce the following notation. For integers

n,d\geq 1
, let
P_{n}(d)
be the dual of the evaluation map

H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d))\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d).

So,
P_{n}(d)
is a globally generated vector bundle on

\mathbb{P}^{n}
fitting into an exact sequence

(6)

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(-d)\longrightarrow H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d))^{*}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\longrightarrow P_{n}(d)\longrightarrow 0.

For example, by the Euler exact sequence
P_{n}(1)=T_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(-1).

By
[
8
, Lemma 2.1(a)]
,
P_{1}(d)
is a balanced vector bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
,
hence

(7)

P_{1}(d)\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)^{\oplus d}.

Using (
6
), for
n\geq 2
, we have a natural identification

H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n},P_{n}(d))=H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d))^{*}.

Now we return to the proof. Note that by
[
14
, Theorem, (i)]
, the map

H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(l-1))\xlongrightarrow{\eta}H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(l^{2}-l)),

induced by
i
, is surjective. Let
W
be the kernel of
\eta
. We have the following commutative diagram of vector bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with exact rows and columns:

(8)

.

Proof of Theorem
D
:

Proof.

(1):
Let
g
be the genus of
X
. Choose a general line bundle on
X
of degree
g-1
, so
\omega_{X}\otimes L^{-1}
is also a general line bundle of degree
g-1
, where
\omega_{X}
is the canonical line bundle of
X
. Hence

h^{0}(X,L)=h^{0}(X,\omega_{X}\otimes L^{-1})=0.

So, by Serre duality,

H^{\bullet}(X,L)=0=H^{\bullet}(X,\omega_{X}\otimes L^{-1})=0.

Now let
\mathcal{F}=L\oplus(\omega_{X}\otimes L^{-1}).
So,
H^{\bullet}(X,\mathcal{F})=0
, hence
\mathcal{F}(1)
is Ulrich. Further,
\textrm{det }\mathcal{F}\cong\omega_{X}
extends to a line bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, as
X
is a complete intersection. Now by
[
20
]
, we see that the Ulrich bundle
\mathcal{F}(1)^{\oplus r}
extends to a vector bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
for any integer
r\geq n/2.

(2):

Let
i:\mathbb{P}^{1}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n}

be an embedding with image
X
.

By Theorem
4.2
, there is a globally generated vector bundle

\mathcal{E}
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
with

i^{*}\mathcal{E}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(l-1)^{\oplus l}.

Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}:=\mathcal{E}(a-1)^{\oplus\frac{r}{l}}.

We have
i^{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(a)\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(al),

and the only
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(al)
-Ulrich bundle on

\mathbb{P}^{1}
of rank
r
is
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(al-1)^{\oplus r}\cong i^{*}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
. So,
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}|_{X}

is the unique
\mathcal{O}_{X}(a)
-Ulrich bundle of rank
r
,
hence
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}|_{X}\cong\mathcal{F}.

Finally, as
\mathcal{E}
is globally generated, so
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}(1-a)

is globally generated.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

In the notations of the proof of Theorem
D
(2), note that

\tau(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}})\leq\mu(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}(1-a))=\mu(\mathcal{E}^{\oplus{\frac{r}{l}}})=\mu(\mathcal{E})=\frac{l-1}{l}<1.

Hence, case (2) of Theorem
B
indeed occurs. Clearly, cases (1) and (3) also occurs. We do not know whether cases (4) or (5) can occur.
