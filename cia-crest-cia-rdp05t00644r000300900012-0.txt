GUEST LIST - MAY 1979

W. MICHAEL BLUMENTHAL, The Secretary of the Treasury

Secretary Blumenthal, Berlin bom and a refugee from Hitler’s Germany, spent
his teen-age years in Shanghai, the city to which his parents had fled in 1938.

In 1947, he landed in California at the age of 21 and worked in low-paying jobs to
support himself while studying international economics at the University of Calif-
ornia. He was awarded, a scholarship to Princeton, earned two master's degrees and
a Ph.D. in economics, taught for a while, but then switched to the more exciting
world of business, joining a subsidiary of Crown Cork and Seal, where he quickly
climbed to Vice President and Director.

In 1961 he joined the Kennedy Administriion as one of three Deputy Assistant
Secretaries of State. In September 1963 he and his family moved to Geneva while
he was in charge of negotiating the Kennedy Round of tariff agreements, and after
four years in the trade job he became President of the Bendix Corporation in 1967.
He rose to become chairman and turned a company whose profits had slumped, into a
company that had improved earnings for six consecutive years and was known in busi-
ness periodicals as one of the best-managed companies in the country.

In his spare time he plays tennis, enjoys jogging, is a lover of classical
music and a voracious reader.

LLEWELLYN KING, Publisher, The Energy Daily and Steel Week

Mr. King was bom in Rhodesia in 1939. He started his journalistic career as
a foreign correspondent in Africa, doing work for a variety of international news
organizations, including the United Press and Time Magazine. In 1959, he moved
to London and worked in Fleet Street for four years before immigrating to New York,
where he worked for the New York Herald Tribune. Later, he was in public relations
and publishing in New York City, where he launched a magazine for women. In 1966,
he joined the Washington Daily News and Post. He also was active in the Washington-
Baltimore Newspaper Guild and, in 1969, was elected its president.

He left the Post to join Nucleonics Week as Washington editor, a position he
relinquished early in 1973 to found Weekly Energy Report, now published as The Energy
Daily. Mr. King also publishes Steel Week, which began publication in 1978.

During his career, Mr. King has contributed to more than fifty publications.

MARSHALL LOEB, Economics Editor and Columnist, TIME

At the beginning of 1979, Marshall Loeb became the first Economics Editor of
TIME Magazine, capping a journalistic career that started over 30 years ago in his
native Chicago. Initially a sports reporter for two Chicago weekly newspapers, he
went on to become a daily newspaper reporter, feature writer and copy- desk editor,
foreign correspondent and magazine writer.

Following graduation from the University of Missouri School of Journalism in
1950, Mr. Loeb attended Goettingen University in West Germany and later served as a
United Press staff correspondent in Frankfurt. Returning to the United States, he
joined the St. Louis Globe -Democrat .

In June 1956, Mr. Loeb joined TIME as a contributing editor with the Business
Section. He became an associate editor in 1960 and senior editor in 1965. In 1968
he was appointed editor of the Business Section. Throughout his tenure at TIME, Mr.
Loeb has been an innovator, initiating the Executive View column, The Economy and
Energy sections of the magazine, and TIME'S Board of Economists, which he chairs. In
the early '70s he divided his time between the Economy and Energy sections, super-
vising coverage of Watergate and the fall of Vietnam. He went on to be editor of the
Nation, Economy £j Business, and Energy sections between 1975 and 1978. At TIME, he
has won every major award in economic and financial journalism.

His wife Peggy is a teacher and they are parents of a son and a daughter.

RUSSELL B. LONG, Chairman, Senate Finance Committee

The son of parents who both served in the U.S. Senate, Senator Long has repre-
sented the State of Louisiana in that distinguished body for almost 30 years. He
was first elected in November 1948 and has been re-elected to every succeeding six-
year term since.

He was born in Shreveport, educated at his State University, where he went on
to earn his law degree in 1942. While in law school he was elected to The Order of
the Coif.

His career in the Senate has been outstanding and has included a term as the
Majority Whip. Among his committee assignments have been those on Commerce, Foreign
Relations, and the Merchant Marine Subcommittee. He is currently Chairman of the
powerful Finance Committee and alternate Chairman of the Joint Committee on Internal
Revenue Taxation.

Senator Long is married to the former Carolyn Bason.

Bom in Oklahoma and raised in Texas, he graduated in 1945 from the Coast Guard
Academy with a B.S. in marine engineering. He served until 1949 in the Far East and
on the U.S. west coast. In 1952 he received a law degree from the University of
California School of Law at Berkeley and joined the firm of Cravath, Swaine and Moore
in New York City.

In 1956 Mr. Miller joined Textron Inc., became a Vice President in 1957 and
President in 1960. In 1968 he became CEO and was elected Chairman and CEO in 1974,
which post he held until taking his position at the Federal Reserve Board.

A former member of The Business Council, including two years as Vice Chairman
in 1973 and 1974, Mr. Miller has been active throughout his business career in public
service. He was a member of the Business Roundtable and Chairman of the Conference
Board and of the National Alliance of Businessmen.

Married to the former Ariadna Rogojarsky, they reside in Washington, D.C.

PAUL H. NITZE, Director of Policy Studies, Committee on the Present Danger

Mr. Nitze brings to his current position a strong background in government. He
has served as the representative of the Secretary of Defense to the United States
Delegation to the Strategic Arms Limitation Talks with the Soviet Union, as Deputy
Secretary of Defense, Secretary of the Navy, and as Assistant Secretary of Defense for
International Security Affairs.

Bom in Amherst, Massachusetts in 1907, Mr. Nitze was graduated cum laude from
Harvard University in 1928, subsequently joining the investment banking firm of Dillon
Read 5 Company in New York. In 1941, he left his post as Vice President of that firm
to become Financial Director of the Office of the Coordinator of Inter-American Affairs .
Following brief periods as Chief of the Metals and Minerals Branch of the Board of
Economic Welfare, Director of Foreign Procurement and Development for the Foreign
Economic Administration, and Vice Chairman of the United States Strategic Bombing
Survey, Mr. Nitze served the Department of State, beginning as Deputy Director of the
Office of International Trade Policy. Later, he became Director of that Agency's Policy
Planning Staff. Between 1953 and 1961, Mr. Nitze left government service to assume the
presidency of the Foreign Service Educational Foundation in Washington, D.C. He holds
numerous directorships throughout the country.

Married to the former Phyllis Pratt, Mr. and Mrs. Nitze have two sons and two
daughters, and reside at Bel Alton, Maryland and in Washington, D.C.

MICHAEL NOVAK, Resident Scholar, American Enterprise Institute

Currently the Ledden-Watson 1 Distinguished Professor of Religion at Syracuse Uni-
versity and a Resident Scholar at The American Enterprise Institute, Michael Novak's
career spans the fields of philosophy, politics, journalism, teaching, and writing.
Bom in 1933 in Johnstown, Pennsylvania, he has undergraduate degrees from Stonehill
College and the Gregorian University in Rome. In addition, he studied theology at
Catholic University and taught at Harvard and Stanford.

DEAN RUSK, Sibley Professor of International Law, University of Georgia School of Law

Mr. Rusk is with us from his native state of Georgia. A graduate of Davidson
College in North Carolina in 1931, he received Bachelor's and Master's degrees as a
Rhodes Scholar at Oxford University, and studied law at the University of California
at Berkeley, class of 1940.

From 1934 to 1940, he was Associate Professor and Dean of the Faculty at Mills
College in California. After six years service with the U.S. Army, from 1940 to 1946,
he joined the Department of State, where he held positions as Assistant Secretary for
United Nations Affairs and for Far Eastern Affairs before leaving the Department in A
1952 to become President of the Rockefeller Foundation. H

In 1961, he returned to Washington as President Kennedy's Secretary of State and
remained in this Cabinet post until 1969, returning then to Georgia where, in 1970,
he became the Sibley Professor of International Law at the University of Georgia Law
School, an academic post he currently holds.

He and his wife Virginia are the parents of two sons and a daughter.

GENERAL GEORGE M. SEIGNIOUS II, Director, U. S. Arms Control $ Disarmament Agency

General Seignious was sworn in as Director of the U. S. Arms Control and Dis-
armament Agency on December 4, 1978. In 1977 he had been selected as the at-large
member of the U.S. SALT Delegation.

A native of South Carolina, he served for 32 years as a Regular Army officer,
retiring in 1974 to accept the presidency of the Citadel, from which he had graduated
in 1942.

His military command assignments included serving as assistant division commander
of the 2nd Infantry Division in Korea, commanding general of the 3rd Infantry Division
in Germany, and the U.S. Commander in Berlin. While in Berlin he participated in the
Quadripartite negotiations on Berlin. At various times in the Pentagon he was an
assistant to the Secretary of the Army, assistant executive secretary of the Joint
Chiefs of Staff and deputy director of the plans and policy directorate. He became A

JOSEPH J. SISCO, President, American University

Born in Chicago in 1919, Dr. Sisco graduated from Knox College, magna cum laude,
taught high school briefly in Galesburg, Illinois, and thereafter served as an Infantry
officer with the U. S. Army- At the end of World War II, he resumed his studies, re-
ceiving M.A. and Ph.D. degrees from the University of Chicago, specializing in the
Soviet affairs area.

He began his career with the Department of State in 1951 and held a wide variety
of senior positions specializing in political and international affairs. He was the
U.S. Representative to the Fifth Special Session of the General Assembly on the Middle
East, U. S. Negotiator on the Arab-Israeli settlement, Assistant Secretary for Near
Eastern and South Asian Affairs, and eventually the Under Secretary for Political Affairs,
the number three post in the Department and top career post in the Service. For his
work. Dr. Sisco received the Department's Superior Service Award and, in 1966, was
named one of the ten outstanding career officers in Government service. Dr. Sisco has
attended Business Council meetings before.

Retiring from government service in 1976, he became President of American Univer-
sity in Washington, the position he currently holds.

Married to the former Jean Head, they are parents of two daughters.

ELMER B. STAATS, Comptroller General of the United States

Mr. Staats, who took over as Comptroller General of the United States in March
of 1966, came to his position from the Bureau of the Budget,

He first joined the Bureau of the Budget in 1939 and served in various capacities
until 1953, when he left for about a year to work for Marshall Field and Company in
Oiicago. He returned to Washington as Executive Officer of the Operations Coordinat-
ing Board and held this post until returning to the Budget Office in 1958.

In 1959, he was appointed Deputy Director of the Budget, a post he had also held
m President Truman's Administration from 1950 to 1953, and he was in this post when
appointed by President Johnson to the 15-year term as Comptroller General .to head the
General Accounting Office.

Mr. Staats is married to the former Margaret Rich, They have one son and two
daughters, and they reside in Washington, D.C.

Admiral Turner was sworn in as Director of Central Intelligence on March 9, 1977
following a distinguished career with the U.S. Navy.

A native of Highland Park, Illinois, he entered Amherst College in 1941, received
an appointment to the U.S. Naval Academy in 1943 and graduated in the Class of 1947.
One year out of the Academy, he entered Oxford University as a Rhodes Scholar for work
on his Master's degree.

In May 1970, after a variety of naval assignments at sea and on shore, he was
promoted to Rear Admiral and soon assumed command of a Carrier Task Group of the Sixth
Fleet, after which he directed the Systems Analysis Division in the Office of the Chief
of Naval Operations. In August 1974, following two years as President of the Naval War
College at Newport, he became commander of the United States Second Fleet and NATO
Striking Fleet Atlantic. In August 1975, one month prior to his promotion to Admiral,
he became Commander in Chief, Allied Forces Southern Eirope, with headquarters in Naples,
Italy , and it was from this command that he returned to Washington to assume his present
duties .

Admiral Turner and his wife, Patricia, have a married daughter and a married son.

BRIDGE

urday mornings at 9:30.

GOLF

Prizes will be awarded for ladies' golf. There is no entry fee and the Caloway
System will be used to determine winners. This is a proven and workable system
that makes all scores very close, tending to favor neither the low nor the high
handicap player. All cards for complete rounds of play on any of the courses on

the Valley Room immediately upon completion of play. If the cards are turned in
all at one time just prior to dinner there is not enough time or manpower to

players (4 foursomes) at convenient hours on both Friday and Saturday mornings.
These times will be posted on the bulletin board in the Tower Lounge. If you want
to make use of them, call the Upper Cascades starter, mention The Business Council,
tell him when you want to play and he will make every effort to accommodate you.

TENNIS

entry fee. Prize winners will be announced at the Friday dinner. All those who
wish to participate are asked to sign up with the tennis professional, Mr. Pat
Haynes, before 10 o'clock on Friday morning . There will not be a tournament on
Saturday morning but tennis courts will be held in the name of The Business Council
for any ladies who wish to play.
