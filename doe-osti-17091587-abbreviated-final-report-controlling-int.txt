 Controlling Interfaces for Actinide Processing Modernization
 Principal Investigator: Tae Wook Heo
 Project Tracking Number: 25-ERD-005

Project Overview
Science-based innovation in actinide interface engineering is essential for modernizing
production and requires a novel approach to systematically investigate interactions between
actinides and transition metals. This LDRD ER project established an integrated multiscale
modeling and experimental framework for investigating complex actinide interfaces. The
framework includes machine-learning interatomic potentials for uranium (U)-niobium (Nb) and
U oxide-Nb systems, parameterized mesoscale models for evaluating the chemical and
mechanical stability of mixed U- and Nb-oxide phases, and a phase-field model for simulating
phase evolution at U-Nb interfaces. We also established experimental protocols for preparing
diffusion-couple samples for controlled interface studies and developed McEPMA for efficient,
high-resolution chemical mapping of actinide interfaces. These capabilities were applied to
depleted uranium (DU)-Nb interfaces to investigate the effects of environment-induced oxygen
on interface formation and properties. The results revealed complex formation mechanisms
involving phase separation during cooling and substantial variability in the chemical and
microstructural characteristics of oxygen-containing interfaces. A data-driven machine learning
approach was further used to evaluate microstructure-property relationships in mixed U- and
Nb-oxide phases representative of observed interfacial structures. Outcomes from this ER will be
incorporated into the new LDRD SI project (IMPACT: Integrated Multiscale Predictions for
Actinide Interface Characterization and Testing, 27-SI-002), which will advance science-based,
data-driven process optimization and maximize the value of lab-scale experiments for process
qualification and design certification.

Mission Impact
This LDRD ER project supported one new postdoc (Dr. Nathan Bieberdorf), one summer intern
(Mr. Bryce Everson), and two existing postdocs (Dr. Kate Elder and Dr. Adrian Gonzales). Its
outcomes established research capabilities that can help improve production yield and advance
production modernization in support of LLNL’s Nuclear Deterrence/Advanced Materials and
Manufacturing missions. The project strengthened LLNL’s actinide interface science capabilities
by addressing the need for robust, integrated modeling and experimental approaches. Reliable
control of actinide interfaces is critical to achieving NNSA production goals and advancing the
stockpile-stewardship mission. This project leveraged novel resources developed through
multiple internal and external projects to address complex actinide interface and processing
challenges. Outcomes from FY25-26 activities of this ER are expected to support LLNL capability
sustainment within the DOE/NNSA stockpile-stewardship mission by contributing to a new LDRD
SI project, IMPACT: Integrated Multiscale Predictions for Actinide Interface Characterization and
Testing (tracking code: 27-SI-002). More broadly, this work has the potential for positioning LLNL
