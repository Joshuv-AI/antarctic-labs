Completeness and Incompleteness for Expanding Gödel-Löb Logics
Somayeh Chopoghloo, David Fernández-Duque, Joost J. Joosten, Sofía Santiago-Fernández

Abstract
Expanding products of modal logics are bimodal logics obtained from the combination of a ‘horizontal component’ logic and a ‘vertical component’ logic, lying between the fusion and the Cartesian product of the two logics. Gabelaia et al. showed that expanding products are often decidable when the first component is Noetherian, although their methods are semantical and do not yield complete axiomatisations. They do, however, propose a candidate, dubbed the expanding commutator of the two logics and known to be complete in many ‘non-Noetherian’ cases. In this paper, we consider various expanding products of modal logics whose vertical component is 𝖦𝖫 \sf GL . We show that the standard axiomatisation is complete when the horizontal component is either 𝖪𝟦 {\sf K4} or 𝖦𝖫 {\sf GL} , but incomplete when it is 𝖦𝗋𝗓 {\sf Grz} or any logic between 𝖪𝟦 ​ .3 {\sf K4.3} and 𝖦𝗋𝗓 ​ .3 {\sf Grz.3} , thus yielding a partial solution to a question posed by Gabelaia et al. more than two decades ago.

2.1
Expanding domain semantics

Let us begin by recalling the original expanding domain semantics used by Gabelaia et al.

Definition 2.1

(

Let
\mathcal{C}_{h}

\mathcal{C}_{v}

\mathfrak{F}=(W,\prec_{h})\in\mathcal{C}_{h}

(i)

for all
u,w\in W^{x}
.

We denote the class of all such frames by
(\mathcal{C}_{h}\times\mathcal{C}_{v})^{\sf e}

A valuation on

•

•

and

•

such that

A formula

2.2
Embedding domain semantics

The class of expanding domain models should be regarded as the ‘official’ semantics for our logics, and our main results pertain to this class. However, a central theme of our strategy will be to broaden this semantics to obtain classes of frames that are easier to construct, but which can ultimately be transformed into expanding domain frames. We will begin with a rather innocuous, but technically convenient, generalisation of expanding domain semantics. As we will only apply this generalised semantics to transitive logics, we will assume transitivity when working with this class of frames.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{h}
and
\mathcal{C}_{v}
be two classes of transitive unimodal frames. An embedding domain frame (or
{\sf e}
-frame) is a triple
(\mathfrak{F},f,e)
, where
\mathfrak{F}=(W,\prec_{h})\in\mathcal{C}_{h}
is the
horizontal frame
,
f:W\to\mathcal{C}_{v}
is a function assigning to each
x\in W
a vertical frame
f(x)=(W^{x},\prec_{v}^{x})\in\mathcal{C}_{v}
, and
e
is a function assigning to each pair
x,y\in W
with
x\prec_{h}y
an embedding from
f(x)
to
f(y)
, i.e. an injective function
e_{xy}\colon W^{x}\to W^{y}
satisfying

(i)

u\prec_{v}^{x}w
iff
e_{xy}(u)\prec_{v}^{y}e_{xy}(w)
for all
u,w\in W^{x}
, and

(ii)

if
x\prec_{h}y\prec_{h}z
then
e_{yz}\circ e_{xy}=e_{xz}
.

We denote the class of all such
{\sf e}
-frames by
(\mathcal{C}_{h}\rtimes\mathcal{C}_{v})^{\sf e}
.

Note that the second condition specifically applies to transitive frames and is one of the reasons that we will not define embedding models for non-transitive classes, although it should be possible to modify the definition for a non-transitive setting.
In practice,
(W,\prec_{h})
will usually be a tree, in which case it suffices to define
e_{xy}
when
y
is an immediate successor of
x
and then use
(ii)
to recursively define
e_{xy}
in other cases.

Semantics for embedding domain models (or
{\sf e}
-models) are defined analogously to those for expanding domain models, except that we set

•

\mathfrak{M},(x,u)\Vdash\Diamond_{h}\varphi
iff there is
y\in W
such that
x\prec_{h}y
and
\mathfrak{M},(y,e_{xy}(u))\Vdash\varphi
.

We adopt the same conventions for
\sf e
-frames as we did for expanding domain frames, e.g. an
\sf e
-frame inherits the properties (such as Noetherianness) of its relations, and we identify a logic with its class of frames when using the notation
({\sf L}_{h}\rtimes{\sf L}_{v})^{\sf e}
.

Working with
\sf e
-frames rather than expanding domain frames will be more convenient for technical reasons, but in theory the distinction is inessential due to the following.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{h}
and
\mathcal{C}_{v}
be two classes of transitive frames.

1.

If a formula is satisfiable in an expanding domain frame
(\mathcal{C}_{h}\times\mathcal{C}_{v})^{\sf e}
, then it is satisfiable in a
(\mathcal{C}_{h}\rtimes\mathcal{C}_{v})^{\sf e}
-frame.

2.

If a formula is satisfiable in a
(\mathcal{C}_{h}\rtimes\mathcal{C}_{v})^{\sf e}
-frame whose horizontal component is a tree, then it is satisfiable in an expanding domain frame in
(\mathcal{C}_{h}\times\mathcal{C}_{v})^{\sf e}
.

Proof.

1
.

Clearly, every expanding domain model can be regarded as an embedding domain model by just letting the embeddings be the inclusion maps.

2
.

Consider a
(\mathcal{C}_{h}\rtimes\mathcal{C}_{v})^{\sf e}
-model
\mathfrak{M}=((W,\prec_{h}),f,e,V)
where
(W,\prec_{h})
is a tree. We can moreover assume that for
x,y\in W
,
x\neq y
implies that
W^{x}\cap W^{y}=\varnothing
by a standard disjoint union construction.

Let
W^{\infty}=\bigcup_{x\in W}W^{x}
and define
{\sim}\subseteq W^{\infty}\times W^{\infty}
to be the least equivalence relation such that
w\sim e_{xy}(w)
whenever
x\prec_{h}y
and
w\in W^{x}
. Since
(W,\prec_{h})
is a tree and the embeddings satisfy
e_{yz}\circ e_{xy}=e_{xz}
, every
\sim
-equivalence class is precisely the collection of copies of a point obtained by repeatedly applying embeddings along a branch of the tree.

For each
x\in W
, let
\widehat{W}^{x}=\{[w]_{\sim}\mid w\in W^{x}\}
, and define a relation
\widehat{\prec_{v}}^{x}
on
\widehat{W}^{x}
by

[u]_{\sim}\widehat{\prec_{v}}^{x}[w]_{\sim}\quad\text{iff}\quad u\prec_{v}^{x}w.

Now let
\widehat{f}(x)=(\widehat{W}^{x},\widehat{\prec_{v}}^{x})
. It is not hard to check that
((W,\prec_{h}),\widehat{f})
is an expanding domain frame. Moreover, if
\widehat{V}^{x}(p)=\{[w]_{\sim}\mid w\in V^{x}(p)\}
, then by induction on a formula
\varphi\in\mathcal{L}_{hv}
, we have

\mathfrak{M},(x,w)\Vdash\varphi\quad\text{iff}\quad\widehat{\mathfrak{M}},(x,[w]_{\sim})\Vdash\varphi,

for every
x\in W
and
w\in W^{x}
.

∎

Thus,
(\mathcal{C}_{h}\times\mathcal{C}_{v})^{\sf e}
and
(\mathcal{C}_{h}\rtimes\mathcal{C}_{v})^{\sf e}
are essentially equivalent whenever the horizontal frame is a tree. The latter will be the case in all of the main constructions of the text, so, for practical purposes, they will be interchangeable for us. This explains our intentional choice to use similar notation for the two classes.
Working with
(\mathcal{C}_{h}\rtimes\mathcal{C}_{v})^{\sf e}
is convenient, since constructions can be a bit more flexible, so in the sequel we will work with this class as our ‘default’ semantics, appealing to Lemma
2.4
to apply our results to ‘proper’ expanding domain models.

2.3
Forward-confluent semantics

Our proof of completeness cannot be a ‘proof by canonical model’, since the canonical model for
\sf GL
is not Noetherian.
However, it will be convenient to construct the canonical model as an ‘intermediate’ model. The issue is that this model will not be based on an expanding or even embedding domain frame. Instead, it belongs to a wider class of bi-relational Kripke models, called
forward-confluent models
, which validate the expanding commutator axioms. As we will see, this semantics can be regarded as extending expanding domain semantics; however, the reader should be warned that this extension is less innocuous than that given by embedding domain models, as we will illustrate in
Section

4
.

Definition 2.5
.

A forward-confluent frame is a triple
\mathcal{F}=(W,\prec_{h},\prec_{v})
, where
\prec_{h}
and
\prec_{v}
are binary relations on
W
satisfying the properties of Left Commutativity (LC) and Church-Rosser (CR):

(LC)

if
x\prec_{v}x^{\prime}\prec_{h}y^{\prime}
, then there exists
y\in W
such that
x\prec_{h}y\prec_{v}y^{\prime}
;

(CR)

if
x^{\prime}\succ_{v}x\prec_{h}y
, then there exists
y^{\prime}\in W
such that
x^{\prime}\prec_{h}y^{\prime}\succ_{v}y
.

Given classes of frames
\mathcal{C}_{h}
and
\mathcal{C}_{v}
, the class of all forward-confluent frames where
(W,\prec_{h})\in\mathcal{C}_{h}
and
(W,\prec_{v})\in\mathcal{C}_{v}
is denoted by
\mathcal{C}_{h}\rtimes\mathcal{C}_{v}
.

A forward-confluent model
\mathcal{M}=(W,\prec_{h},\prec_{v},\left\llbracket\cdot\right\rrbracket)
is a forward-confluent frame equipped
with a valuation
\left\llbracket\cdot\right\rrbracket:\mathrm{Prop}\to\mathcal{P}(W)
.

Models assign to each formula
\varphi\in\mathcal{L}_{hv}
a truth set
\left\llbracket\varphi\right\rrbracket\subseteq W
by extending
\left\llbracket\cdot\right\rrbracket
inductively as follows:

•

\left\llbracket\neg\varphi\right\rrbracket:=W\setminus\left\llbracket\varphi\right\rrbracket
,

•

\left\llbracket\varphi\wedge\psi\right\rrbracket:=\left\llbracket\varphi\right\rrbracket\cap\left\llbracket\psi\right\rrbracket
,

•

\left\llbracket\Diamond_{\alpha}\varphi\right\rrbracket:=\{w\in W\mid\exists u\in W\ (w\prec_{\alpha}u\ \text{and}\ u\in\left\llbracket\varphi\right\rrbracket)\}
for
\alpha\in\{h,v\}
.

A formula
\varphi\in\mathcal{L}_{hv}
is
satisfied
in
\mathcal{M}
if there exists some
w\in W
such that
w\in\left\llbracket\varphi\right\rrbracket
, in which case we write
\mathcal{M},w\Vdash\varphi
. When the underlying model is clear from context, we simply write
w\Vdash\varphi
. Validity in forward-confluent models and frames is defined in the usual way. In particular, for a forward-confluent frame
\mathcal{F}
, the notation
\mathcal{F}\vDash\varphi
indicates validity of
\varphi
on
\mathcal{F}
.

Remark 2.6
.

To aid in legibility, we use a gothic font as in e.g.
\mathfrak{M}
to denote expanding or embedding domain models and a calligraphic font as in e.g.
\mathcal{M}
to denote forward-confluent models.

We adopt the conventions of expanding domain models regarding notation and terminology; in particular,
(W,\prec_{h},\prec_{v})
is
transitive
if both
\prec_{h}
and
\prec_{v}
are, and, while the relations will typically be transitive, non-transitive relations will be allowed in principle. Moreover, when writing
{\sf L}_{h}\rtimes{\sf L}_{v}
, we identify each logic with its corresponding class of frames. The following is an easy observation; note that transitivity is not assumed.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
\mathcal{F}=(W,\prec_{h},\prec_{v})
be an arbitrary bi-relational frame.
Using
\models
to denote first-order validity, we have:

1.

\mathcal{F}\models\mbox{(LC)}
if and only if
\mathcal{F}\vDash\Diamond_{v}\Diamond_{h}p\to\Diamond_{h}\Diamond_{v}p
, and

2.

\mathcal{F}\models\mbox{(CR)}
if and only if
\mathcal{F}\vDash\Diamond_{h}\Box_{v}p\to\Box_{v}\Diamond_{h}p
.

Proof.

We comment only on the left-to-right direction of the second item. So, we assume
\mathcal{F}\models\mbox{(CR)}
and for arbitrary valuation, and
x\in W
we assume
x\Vdash\Diamond_{h}\Box_{v}p
. Consequently, we can find
y
with
x\prec_{h}y\Vdash\Box_{v}p
. We need to see that
x\Vdash\Box_{v}\Diamond_{h}p
.
Let
x^{\prime}
be such that
x\prec_{v}x^{\prime}
.
We know by (CR) that there is some
y^{\prime}
with
x^{\prime}\prec_{h}y^{\prime}\succ_{v}y
, whence
y^{\prime}\Vdash p
since
y\Vdash\Box_{v}p
.
Thus,
x^{\prime}\Vdash\Diamond_{h}p
and, since
x^{\prime}
was arbitrary,
x\Vdash\Box_{v}\Diamond_{h}p
.
∎

Remark 2.8
.

We adapt the terminology of ‘left commutativity’ and ‘Church-Rosser’ from Gabelaia et al.
[
14
]
, who use them to refer to the respective formulas of Proposition
2.7
. Note moreover that
\Diamond_{h}\Box_{v}p\to\Box_{v}\Diamond_{h}p
and
\Diamond_{v}\Box_{h}p\to\Box_{h}\Diamond_{v}p
are equivalent, which reflects the symmetry observed in (CR).

Gabelaia et al.
[
14
]
had already shown that any expanding domain model can be transformed into a bi-relational Kripke model; as it turns out, their construction moreover yields forward-confluent models.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{h}
and
\mathcal{C}_{v}
be classes of Kripke frames closed under disjoint unions.
If a formula is satisfied in a
(\mathcal{C}_{h}\times\mathcal{C}_{v})^{\sf e}
-model, then it is satisfied in a
\mathcal{C}_{h}\rtimes\mathcal{C}_{v}
-model.

Proof.

Given an expanding domain model
\mathfrak{M}=(\mathfrak{F},f,V)
where
\mathfrak{F}=(W,\prec_{h})
,
f(x)=(W^{x},\prec_{v}^{x})
for each
x\in W
and
V=(V^{x})_{x\in W}
, we define a model
\overline{\mathfrak{M}}:=(\overline{W},\prec_{h},\prec_{v},\left\llbracket\cdot\right\rrbracket)
, where

•

\overline{W}:=\{(x,u)\mid x\in W,u\in W^{x}\}
;

•

(x,u)\prec_{h}(y,w)
iff
x\prec_{h}y
and
u=w
;

•

(x,u)\prec_{v}(y,w)
iff
x=y
and
u\prec_{v}^{x}w
;

•

\left\llbracket p\right\rrbracket:=\{(x,u)\mid u\in V^{x}(p)\}
for any
p\in\mathrm{Prop}
.

We can show that
\mathfrak{M},(x,u)\Vdash\varphi
if and only if
\overline{\mathfrak{M}},(x,u)\Vdash\varphi
by an easy induction on
\varphi\in\mathcal{L}_{hv}
, whose details are left to the reader. It only remains to verify that the confluence conditions are met.

(LC)

Suppose
(x,u)\prec_{v}(x^{\prime},u^{\prime})\prec_{h}(y^{\prime},w^{\prime})
. Then
x=x^{\prime}
,
u\prec_{v}^{x}u^{\prime}
,
u^{\prime}=w^{\prime}
, and
x^{\prime}\prec_{h}y^{\prime}
. Consider
(y^{\prime},u)
. Since
x=x^{\prime}
and
x^{\prime}\prec_{h}y^{\prime}
, we have
x\prec_{h}y^{\prime}
, and therefore
(x,u)\prec_{h}(y^{\prime},u)
. Moreover, since
u\prec_{v}^{x}u^{\prime}
and
x\prec_{h}y^{\prime}
, the subframe condition implies
u\prec_{v}^{y^{\prime}}u^{\prime}
. As
u^{\prime}=w^{\prime}
, we conclude
(y^{\prime},u)\prec_{v}(y^{\prime},w^{\prime})
.

(CR)

Suppose
(x^{\prime},u^{\prime})\succ_{v}(x,u)\prec_{h}(y,w)
. Then
x=x^{\prime}
,
u\prec_{v}^{x}u^{\prime}
,
u=w
, and
x\prec_{h}y
. Consider
(y,u^{\prime})
. Since
x\prec_{h}y
and
x=x^{\prime}
, we obtain
(x^{\prime},u^{\prime})\prec_{h}(y,u^{\prime})
. Furthermore, from
u\prec_{v}^{x}u^{\prime}
and the subframe condition we get
u\prec_{v}^{y}u^{\prime}
, which yields
(y,w)\prec_{v}(y,u^{\prime})
since
u=w
.

It is readily verified that
(\overline{W},\prec_{h})
and
(\overline{W},\prec_{v})
are isomorphic to disjoint unions of frames from
\mathcal{C}_{h}
and
\mathcal{C}_{v}
, respectively, so indeed the new model belongs to
\mathcal{C}_{h}\rtimes\mathcal{C}_{v}
.
∎

3
Expanding commutators

In this section, we review the expanding commutator of two modal logics, i.e. the Hilbert-style calculi
[\mathsf{L}_{h},\mathsf{L}_{v}]^{\sf e}
given individual unimodal logics
{\sf L}_{h}
and
{\sf L}_{v}
.
Before this, let us briefly recall the unimodal logics that will be used in the text.

We formulate unimodal logics in the language with a single modality
\Diamond
, which we denote
\mathcal{L}_{\Diamond}
. For our purposes, a
logic
is a set of formulas containing all propositional tautologies and closed under modus ponens, substitution and necessitation.
The basic modal logic
\sf K
is then the least logic over
\mathcal{L}_{\Diamond}
containing
\Box(p\to q)\to(\Box p\to\Box q)
and
\Diamond p\leftrightarrow\neg\Box\neg p
,
3
3

3

This axiom is required when
\Diamond
is taken as a primitive.
and any logic extending
\sf K
is a
normal modal logic.

The following well-known modal logics will appear in the text.

•

\sf K4
is obtained from
\sf K
by adding the transitivity axiom
\Diamond\Diamond p\to\Diamond p.

•

\sf S4
is obtained from
\sf K4
by adding the reflexivity axiom
p\to\Diamond p.

•

\sf Grz
is obtained from
\sf S4
by adding the Grzegorczyk axiom

p\to\Diamond(p\wedge\Box(\Diamond p\to p)).

•

\sf GL
is obtained by extending
\sf K4
with the Löb axiom

\Diamond p\to\Diamond(p\wedge\neg\Diamond p).

•

Given a normal modal logic
\sf L
, the logic
\sf L.3
is obtained from
\sf L
by adding the weak connectedness axiom, also known as the
\sf 3
axiom,

(\Diamond p\wedge\Diamond q)\to\Diamond(p\wedge\diamonddot q)\vee\Diamond(q\wedge\diamonddot p).

Expanding commutators of modal logics combine two individual modal logics
\mathsf{L}_{h}
and
\mathsf{L}_{v}
, with
\Diamond_{h}
replacing the modality of
\mathsf{L}_{h}
and
\Diamond_{v}
that of
\mathsf{L}_{v}
. Accordingly, say that a
horizontal instance
of a unimodal formula
\varphi\in\mathcal{L}_{\Diamond}
is the formula
\varphi_{h}
obtained by replacing every instance of
\Diamond
by
\Diamond_{h}
and, similarly, a
vertical instance
of
\varphi
is the formula
\varphi_{v}
obtained by replacing every instance of
\Diamond
by
\Diamond_{v}
.
Our definition of
logic
can be applied uniformly to
\mathcal{L}_{hv}
, where substitution can be applied to arbitrary formulas of
\mathcal{L}_{hv}
and necessitation can be applied to either modality.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathsf{L}_{h}
and
\mathsf{L}_{v}
be normal unimodal logics.
The
expanding commutator

[{\sf L}_{h},{\sf L}_{v}]^{\sf e}
is the least logic over
\mathcal{L}_{hv}
containing all horizontal instances of formulas of
\mathsf{L}_{h}
, all vertical instances of formulas of
\mathsf{L}_{v}
, and the expanding product axiom schemes

\Diamond_{v}\Diamond_{h}p\to\Diamond_{h}\Diamond_{v}p
(Left commutativity)

\Diamond_{v}\Box_{h}p\to\Box_{h}\Diamond_{v}p
(Church–Rosser).

If
\sf L
is any logic (over either the unimodal or bimodal language), we say that a formula
\varphi
is
derivable in
\sf L
from a set
\Gamma
of formulas
, and write
\Gamma\vdash_{\sf L}\varphi
, if either
\vdash_{\sf L}\varphi
or there exist formulas
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n}\in\Gamma
such that

\vdash_{\sf L}(\gamma_{1}\land\cdots\land\gamma_{n})\to\varphi.

Here,
\vdash_{\sf L}\varphi
means that
\varphi
is a
theorem of
{\sf L}
. We may write
\vdash
instead of
\vdash_{\sf L}
when
\sf L
is clear from context.

Remark 3.2
.

Let
\mathsf{L}_{h},\mathsf{L}_{v}
be unimodal logics, let
\varphi,\psi,\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{n}\in\mathcal{L}_{hv}
. The usual reasoning is available in the expanding commutator
[{\sf L}_{h},{\sf L}_{v}]^{\sf e}
for both modalities given
\alpha\in\{h,v\}
.

1.

\vdash\Box_{\alpha}\varphi_{1}\wedge...\wedge\Box_{\alpha}\varphi_{n}\leftrightarrow\Box_{\alpha}(\varphi_{1}\wedge...\wedge\varphi_{n})
.

2.

\vdash\Diamond_{\alpha}(\varphi_{1}\wedge...\wedge\varphi_{n})\to\Diamond_{\alpha}\varphi_{1}\wedge...\wedge\Diamond_{\alpha}\varphi_{n}
.

3.

If
\;\vdash\varphi\to\psi
, then
\;\vdash\Diamond_{\alpha}\varphi\to\Diamond_{\alpha}\psi
.

As we have seen, the class of forward-confluent frames can be seen as generalising expanding domain frames; however, in many (though not all!) cases, the logics coincide, and in particular, the expanding commutator is sound for classes of forward-confluent frames.
Note that the following does not require transitivity and can be applied to any expanding commutator.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
{\sf L}_{h},{\sf L}_{v}
be unimodal logics. The expanding commutator
[\mathsf{L}_{h},\mathsf{L}_{v}]^{\sf e}
is sound for the class
{\sf L}_{h}\rtimes{\sf L}_{v}
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that the expanding product axioms are valid over
{\sf L}_{h}\rtimes{\sf L}_{v}
. The only interesting cases are left commutativity and the Church–Rosser, whose soundness has already been shown in Proposition
2.7
.
∎

The following is already stated for expanding domain frames by Gabelaia et al.
[
14
]
, but it is instructive to note that it can be derived using forward-confluent frames as an intermediary.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
{\sf L}_{h}\in\{\sf K4,GL\}
. The expanding commutator
[{\sf L}_{h},\sf GL]^{\sf e}
is sound for
({\sf L}_{h}\times{\sf GL})^{\sf e}
and
({\sf L}_{h}\rtimes{\sf GL})^{\sf e}
.

Proof.

This follows immediately from Proposition
3.3
together with Lemmas
2.9
and
2.4
.
∎

5
The canonical forward-confluent model

Now that we have exhibited some incomplete expanding commutators, it is time to focus on two prominent cases where these are complete: namely,
[{\sf K4},{\sf GL}]^{\sf e}
and
[{\sf GL},{\sf GL}]^{\sf e}
. A go-to technique for proving the completeness of various modal logics is to construct their canonical model, but it is well known that the canonical model for
\sf GL
is not Noetherian, nor is the canonical model for an expanding commutator based on an expanding domain (or even embedding domain) frame in any reasonable sense. Nevertheless, constructing it will prove very useful as an intermediate step; notably, the canonical model of an expanding commutator is based on a forward-confluent frame, our starting point for extracting
\sf e
-frames (and, hence, expanding domain frames).

As with any other (poly)modal logic, a set
\Phi
of formulas is
consistent
in
[{\mathsf{L}_{h}},{\mathsf{L}_{v}}]^{\sf e}
if
\Phi\nvdash_{[{\mathsf{L}_{h}},{\mathsf{L}_{v}}]^{\sf e}}\perp
. The set
\Phi
is
maximally consistent
in
[{\mathsf{L}_{h}},{\mathsf{L}_{v}}]^{\sf e}
if it is consistent and maximal with respect to set inclusion among all consistent sets.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\mathsf{L}_{h}
,
\mathsf{L}_{v}
be unimodal logics. The
canonical model for
[\mathsf{L}_{h},\mathsf{L}_{v}]^{\sf e}
is the tuple

\mathcal{M}^{\rm c}:=(W^{\rm c},\prec_{h}^{\rm c},\prec_{v}^{\rm c},\left\llbracket\cdot\right\rrbracket^{\rm c}),

where

•

W^{\rm c}
is the set of all maximally consistent sets in
[\mathsf{L}_{h},\mathsf{L}_{v}]^{\sf e}
;

•

\prec_{\alpha}^{\rm c}
for
\alpha\in\{h,v\}
is the binary relation on
W^{\rm c}
defined by
\Phi\prec_{\alpha}^{\rm c}\Psi
iff for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{L}_{hv}
,
\varphi\in\Psi
implies
\Diamond_{\alpha}\varphi\in\Phi
;

•

\left\llbracket p\right\rrbracket^{\rm c}:=\{\Phi\in W^{\rm c}\mid p\in\Phi\}
for
p\in\mathrm{Prop}
.

The standard properties of canonical models can be adapted to the present bimodal setting. Since the arguments follow the usual lines, we omit the proofs.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\Phi\in W^{\rm c}
and
\alpha\in\{h,v\}
. Then,
\Phi\prec_{\alpha}^{\rm c}\Psi
iff for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{L}_{hv}
,
\Box_{\alpha}\varphi\in\Phi
implies
\varphi\in\Psi
.

Proof.

For a proof, see, e.g.,
[
4

.

As usual, one of the most crucial ‘basic’ properties of the canonical model is the witness lemma, both in its standard version and its modified version for
\sf GL

Lemma 5.3

(Witness property)
.

Let
\Phi\in W^{\rm c}
,
\alpha\in\{h,v\}
and
\varphi\in\mathcal{L}_{hv}
. If
\Diamond_{\alpha}\varphi\in\Phi
, then there exists some
\Psi\in W^{\rm c}
such that
\Phi\prec_{\alpha}^{\rm c}\Psi
and
\varphi\in\Psi
. If moreover
\mathsf{L}_{\alpha}
extends
\mathsf{GL}
, then we may choose
\Psi
such that
\varphi\wedge\neg\Diamond_{\alpha}\varphi\in\Psi
.

Proof.

The standard witness lemma is very well known (see, e.g.,
[
4
, Lemma 4.20]
). The modified version for
\sf GL
is also well known, but we review the proof: suppose that
\mathsf{L}_{\alpha}
extends
\sf GL
and
\Diamond_{\alpha}\varphi\in\Phi
. Since
\Phi
is maximal consistent, it is closed under derivability; but, by the Löb axiom (in the form we have stated it), from
\Diamond_{\alpha}\varphi
, we can infer
\Diamond_{\alpha}(\varphi\wedge\neg\Diamond_{\alpha}\varphi)
, hence the latter belongs to
\Phi
. Then, apply the standard witness lemma to find
\Psi\succ_{h}\Phi
with
\varphi\wedge\neg\Diamond_{\alpha}\varphi\in\Psi
.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
\mathsf{L}_{h}
,

be unimodal logics and let

1.

\mathcal{M}^{\rm c}
is forward-confluent.

2.

For

\mathsf{L}_{\alpha}

3.

The truth lemma holds for

, i.e. for any
\Phi\in W^{\rm c}
and
\varphi\in\mathcal{L}_{hv}

\mathcal{M}^{\rm c},\Phi\Vdash\varphi
.

Proof.

1
.

6
Moments

Since the canonical model is not an expanding domain model (or even an embedding domain model), we need to ‘extract’ a model of this desired form from it. The decidability proof of Gabelaia et al.
[
14
]
uses the well-established techniques of building
quasimodels
, possibly partial models where points are labelled by formulas intended to be true at that point. Following the terminology of more recent work (e.g.
[
8
]
), quasimodels are made up of
moments
viewed as instances of vertical frames, suggestive of an intuition where the horizontal relation represents time. As our goal is to show completeness for expanding commutators with
{\sf GL}
as the vertical component, our moments are specifically tailored for this logic. Each point in a moment is labelled by a type, as defined below.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
\Sigma\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{hv}
be closed under subformulas and single negations; we write this as
\Sigma\Subset\mathcal{L}_{hv}
. A
\Sigma
-type is any
\Phi\subseteq\Sigma
satisfying the following properties:

1.

\neg\varphi\in\Phi
iff
\varphi\notin\Phi
, for every
\neg\varphi\in\Sigma
, and

2.

\varphi\wedge\psi\in\Phi
iff
\varphi,\psi\in\Phi
, for every
\varphi\wedge\psi\in\Sigma
.

The set of all finite
\Sigma
-types is denoted by
\mathrm{T}_{\Sigma}
.

We define for
\alpha\in\{h,v\}
,

•

\Phi\prec_{\alpha}^{\Sigma}\Psi
iff whenever either
\varphi
or
\Diamond_{\alpha}\varphi\in\Psi
then
\Diamond_{\alpha}\varphi\in\Phi
, for every
\Diamond_{\alpha}\varphi\in\Sigma
.

We adopt the convention that, for
\alpha\in\{h,v\}
, a map is a
homomorphism
with respect to
\prec_{\alpha}
whenever it preserves the corresponding
\prec_{\alpha}
-relation of the structures under consideration.

Definition 6.2
.

Let
\Sigma\Subset\mathcal{L}_{hv}
(as in Definition
6.1
). A
\Sigma
-quasimoment is a structure
\mathfrak{m}=(|\mathfrak{m}|,\prec_{v}^{\mathfrak{m}},\ell^{\mathfrak{m}})
, where
(|\mathfrak{m}|,\prec_{v}^{\mathfrak{m}})
is a finite, tree-like strict partial order with root
r^{\mathfrak{m}}
and
\ell^{\mathfrak{m}}\colon|\mathfrak{m}|\to T_{\Sigma}
is a homomorphism with respect to
\prec_{v}
.

A
\Sigma
-moment is a
\Sigma
-quasimoment
\mathfrak{m}=(|\mathfrak{m}|,\prec_{v}^{\mathfrak{m}},\ell^{\mathfrak{m}})
such that for every
w\in|\mathfrak{m}|
and every
\Diamond_{v}\varphi\in\Sigma
, if
\Diamond_{v}\varphi\in\ell^{\mathfrak{m}}(w)
, then there exists
v\succ_{v}^{\mathfrak{m}}w
such that
\varphi\in\ell^{\mathfrak{m}}(v)
.

We denote the set of
\Sigma
-moments by
\mathbb{M}_{\Sigma}
.

Moments are similar to vertical frames but with additional syntactic data. Accordingly, given two points that are horizontally related and a moment for the first one, we identify the conditions that a potential moment for the second point must satisfy.

Definition 6.3
.

Let
\Sigma\Subset\mathcal{L}_{hv}
, and
\mathfrak{m}
and
\mathfrak{n}
be
\Sigma
-quasimoments.

1.

Say that
\mathfrak{n}
is a successor of
\mathfrak{m}
, denoted
\mathfrak{m}\prec_{h}^{\Sigma}\mathfrak{n}
, if there exists an embedding
e\colon|\mathfrak{m}|\to|\mathfrak{n}|
such that for every
w\in|\mathfrak{m}|
, we have
\ell^{\mathfrak{m}}(w)\prec_{h}^{\rm\Sigma}\ell^{\mathfrak{n}}(e(w))
.

2.

Moreover, we write
\mathfrak{m}\sqsubseteq^{\Sigma}\mathfrak{n}
if
\mathfrak{m}
embeds into
\mathfrak{n}
preserving both the root and the labeling, in which case we say that
\mathfrak{m}
is a submoment of
\mathfrak{n}
.

The key property of moments used in the decidability proof of Gabelaia et al.
[
14
]
is that they are well-quasi-ordered, itself a consequence of Kruskal’s theorem. This property will also be crucial for our completeness proof of
[\mathsf{GL},\mathsf{GL}]^{\sf e}
.

Theorem 6.4

(Kruskal
[
18
]
)
.

For any finite set
\Lambda
, the class of finite
\Lambda
-labelled trees is well-quasi-ordered under embeddability, i.e., for every infinite sequence
(\mathfrak{T}_{n})_{n<\omega}
, there exist
i<j<\omega
such that
\mathfrak{T}_{i}
embeds into
\mathfrak{T}_{j}
via an embedding which preserves labels.

As moments can be seen as
T_{\Sigma}
-labelled trees, we can immediately apply Kruskal’s theorem to them to obtain the following.

Corollary 6.5
.

Let
\Sigma\Subset\mathcal{L}_{hv}
.
The set
\mathbb{M}_{\Sigma}
is well-quasi-ordered by
\sqsubseteq^{\Sigma}
.

7
Simulations

The extraction of quasimodels from the canonical model is performed via the use of simulations, which relate moments and maximal consistent sets. In this section,
\mathcal{M}^{\rm c}
denotes the canonical model of a logic
[\mathsf{L}_{h},\mathsf{GL}]^{\sf e}
, where, in principle,
\mathsf{L}_{h}
is a normal extension of
\sf K4
. In practice, it may be assumed that
\mathsf{L}_{h}\in\{\mathsf{K4},\mathsf{GL}\}
.

Definition 7.1
.

Let
\Sigma\Subset\mathcal{L}_{hv}
and let
\mathfrak{m}
be a
\Sigma
-quasimoment. A map
\sigma\colon|\mathfrak{m}|\to W^{\mathrm{c}}
is called a simulation if it is a homomorphism with respect to
\prec_{v}
and
\ell^{\mathfrak{m}}(w)=\sigma(w)\cap\Sigma
for all
w\in|\mathfrak{m}|
.

For
\Phi\in W^{\mathrm{c}}
, we write
\mathfrak{m}\rightharpoonup\Phi
and say that
\mathfrak{m}
simulates
\Phi
if there exists a simulation
\sigma\colon|\mathfrak{m}|\to W^{\mathrm{c}}
such that
\sigma(r^{\mathfrak{m}})=\Phi
.

Recall that, unlike quasimoments, moments are required to witness every formula of the form
\Diamond_{v}\varphi
labelling a node by a suitable
\prec_{v}
-successor labelled with
\varphi
. For technical reasons, it is convenient to record instances where a diamond formula still requires such a witness, called ‘defects’. We also treat occurrences of
\Diamond_{h}
-formulas as horizontal defects, which are realized by extending the quasimoment with a successor.

Definition 7.2
.

Let
\Sigma\Subset\mathcal{L}_{hv}
. A potential defect
\delta
of a
\Sigma
-quasimoment
\mathfrak{m}
is a pair
\delta=(w,\Diamond_{\alpha}\varphi)
such that
\Diamond_{\alpha}\varphi\in\ell^{\mathfrak{m}}(w)
, where
\alpha\in\{h,v\}
.

•

A potential defect
\delta
is called a horizontal defect if
\alpha=h
. We say that a
\Sigma
-quasimoment
\mathfrak{n}
realizes the horizontal defect
(w,\Diamond_{h}\varphi)
if
\mathfrak{m}\prec_{h}^{\Sigma}\mathfrak{n}
via an embedding
e:|\mathfrak{m}|\to|\mathfrak{n}|
with
\varphi\in\ell^{\mathfrak{n}}(e(w))
.

•

A potential defect
\delta
is called a vertical defect if
\alpha=v
and there is no
u\succ_{v}^{\mathfrak{m}}w
such that
\varphi\in\ell^{\mathfrak{m}}(u)
.

It will often be convenient to construct moments by first building quasimoments and then iteratively removing the vertical defects.
We may use this strategy to show that every maximal consistent set in the canonical model is simulated by some moment.

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
\Sigma\Subset\mathcal{L}_{hv}
and let
\Phi\in W^{\rm c}
. Then there exists a
\Sigma
-moment
\mathfrak{m}
such that
\mathfrak{m}\rightharpoonup\Phi
.

Proof.

To prove this, we construct a sequence
(\mathfrak{m}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\Sigma
-quasimoments and show that it eventually stabilizes at a
\Sigma
-moment
\mathfrak{m}
such that
\mathfrak{m}\rightharpoonup\Phi
.

Consider the sequence
(\mathfrak{m}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
defined recursively as:

•

\mathfrak{m}_{0}:=(\{w\},\prec_{v}^{0},\ell^{0})
, where
\prec_{v}^{0}:=\emptyset
and
\ell^{0}(w):=\Phi\cap\Sigma
.

•

Given
\mathfrak{m}_{i}=(|\mathfrak{m}_{i}|,\prec_{v}^{i},\ell^{i})
, the
\Sigma
-quasimoment
\mathfrak{m}_{i+1}
is obtained by extending
\mathfrak{m}_{i}
as follows: for every vertical defect
(u,\Diamond_{v}\varphi)
of
\mathfrak{m}_{i}
with
\ell^{i}(u)=\Psi\cap\Sigma
, introduce a fresh node
u^{\prime}
such that
u\prec_{v}^{i+1}u^{\prime}
, and define
\ell^{i+1}(u^{\prime}):=\Psi^{\prime}\cap\Sigma

where
\Psi^{\prime}\in W^{\mathrm{c}}
is chosen so that
\Psi\prec_{v}^{\mathrm{c}}\Psi^{\prime}
and
\varphi\wedge\neg\Diamond_{v}\varphi\in\Psi^{\prime}
, as given by the witness property (Lemma
5.3
).

In this case, we say that
u
is a
defect node of
\mathfrak{m}_{i}
, and
u^{\prime}

realizes the defect

(u,\Diamond_{v}\varphi)
in
\mathfrak{m}_{i+1}
, or simply
u^{\prime}

realizes a defect at

u
.

We first see that the sequence is well-defined.

Claim 1
.

\mathfrak{m}_{i}
is a
\Sigma
-quasimoment for any
i\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof of Claim
1
.

We proceed by induction on
i\in\mathbb{N}
. The base case is immediate. For the inductive step, it suffices to verify that the homomorphism condition is preserved at the fresh nodes added in
\mathfrak{m}_{i+1}
, since it holds elsewhere by the inductive hypothesis.

Let
u^{\prime}\in|\mathfrak{m}_{i+1}|
be a fresh node realizing a defect at
(u,\Diamond_{v}\varphi)
of
\mathfrak{m}_{i}
, with
\ell^{i+1}(u)=\Psi\cap\Sigma
and
\ell^{i+1}(u^{\prime})=\Psi^{\prime}\cap\Sigma
, where
\Psi\prec_{v}^{\rm c}\Psi^{\prime}
and
\varphi\wedge\neg\Diamond_{v}\varphi\in\Psi^{\prime}
. Let
\Diamond_{v}\psi\in\Sigma
.

1.

If
\psi\in\Psi^{\prime}\cap\Sigma
, then
\Diamond_{v}\psi\in\Psi
by
\Psi\prec_{v}^{\rm c}\Psi^{\prime}
. Hence,
\Diamond_{v}\psi\in\Psi\cap\Sigma
.

2.

If
\Diamond_{v}\psi\in\Psi^{\prime}\cap\Sigma
, then
\Diamond_{v}\Diamond_{v}\psi\in\Psi
by
\Psi\prec_{v}^{\rm c}\Psi^{\prime}
. Since
\Psi
is maximal consistent, transitivity yields
\Diamond_{v}\psi\in\Psi
. Thus,
\Diamond_{v}\psi\in\Psi\cap\Sigma
. ∎

We next show that the sequence of
\Sigma
-quasimoments stabilizes, i.e. there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathfrak{m}_{k}=\mathfrak{m}_{j}
for all
j\geq k
. The
\Sigma
-quasimoment
\mathfrak{m}_{k}
is called the
limit
of
(\mathfrak{m}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Claim 2
.

The sequence
(\mathfrak{m}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
stabilizes.

Proof of Claim
2
.

Let
\mathrm{def}_{i}(u)
denote the set of formulas
\Diamond_{v}\varphi
such that
(u,\Diamond_{v}\varphi)
is a vertical defect of
\mathfrak{m}_{i}
. It suffices to show that

\mathrm{def}_{i+1}(u^{\prime})\subsetneq\mathrm{def}_{i}(u)

(3)

for every
u^{\prime}\in|\mathfrak{m}_{i+1}|
realizing a defect node
u\in|\mathfrak{m}_{i}|
. Indeed,
\mathfrak{m}_{i}
only extends properly to
\mathfrak{m}_{i+1}
in order to realize existing defects. Since
\Sigma
is finite, there are only finitely many defects in
\mathfrak{m}_{0}
. By (
3
), whenever a fresh node is added to realize a defect node, its set of defects is strictly contained in that of the node it realizes. It follows that along any branch of the construction, the sets of defects form a strictly decreasing chain with respect to inclusion, so the construction must stabilize.

To show the inclusion in (
3
), consider
\Diamond_{v}\psi\in\mathrm{def}_{i+1}(u^{\prime})
such that
u^{\prime}
is a fresh node realizing the defect
(u,\Diamond_{v}\varphi)
in
\mathfrak{m}_{i}
. Let
\ell^{i+1}(u)=\Psi\cap\Sigma
and
\ell^{i+1}(u^{\prime})=\Psi^{\prime}\cap\Sigma
, where
\Psi\prec_{v}^{\rm c}\Psi^{\prime}
and
\varphi\wedge\neg\Diamond_{v}\varphi\in\Psi^{\prime}
. Since
\Diamond_{v}\psi\in\Psi^{\prime}
and
\Psi\prec_{v}^{\rm c}\Psi^{\prime}

\Diamond_{v}\Diamond_{v}\psi\in\Psi

\Psi

\Diamond_{v}\psi\in\Psi

\Diamond_{v}\psi\in\Psi\cap\Sigma

that defect nodes have no successors. Thus,
(u,\Diamond_{v}\psi)
is, in particular, a vertical defect of
\mathfrak{m}_{i}
, showing that
\mathrm{def}_{i+1}(u^{\prime})\subseteq\mathrm{def}_{i}(u)
. Finally, the inclusion is strict: indeed,
\Diamond_{v}\varphi\in\mathrm{def}_{i}(u)
but by construction
\varphi\wedge\neg\Diamond_{v}\varphi\in\Psi^{\prime}
. Hence,
\Diamond_{v}\varphi\notin\Psi^{\prime}

\Diamond_{v}\varphi\notin\mathrm{def}_{i+1}(u^{\prime})

∎

Let
\mathfrak{m}
denote the limit of
(\mathfrak{m}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
. We conclude that
\mathfrak{m}
is the desired
\Sigma
-moment simulating
\Phi\in W^{\rm c}
.

Claim 3
.

\mathfrak{m}
is a
\Sigma
-moment satisfying
\mathfrak{m}\rightharpoonup\Phi

Proof of Claim
3

To see that
\mathfrak{m}
is a
\Sigma
-moment, it suffices to show that for every

with
\Diamond_{v}\varphi\in\ell^{i}(u)

u^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{m}_{j}
such that

and
\varphi\in\ell^{j}(u^{\prime})
. The result follows by an easy induction on

, whose details are left to the reader.

Thus, we conclude
\mathfrak{m}\rightharpoonup\Phi
via the simulation
\sigma:|\mathfrak{m}|\to W^{\mathrm{c}}
defined by setting
\sigma(u):=\Psi
for every

with
\ell^{\mathfrak{m}}(u)=\Psi\cap\Sigma

∎

∎

.

Let
\Sigma\Subset\mathcal{L}_{hv}

\mathfrak{m}

\Sigma

\mathfrak{m}

recursively on the number of nodes of

•

has no vertical defects, we simply set

Let
\Sigma\Subset\mathcal{L}_{hv}

\Phi\in W^{\rm c}

\mathfrak{m}\rightharpoonup\Phi

Let
\delta=(w,\Diamond_{h}\varphi)
be a horizontal defect of
\mathfrak{m}
and let
\sigma:|\mathfrak{m}|\to W^{\rm c}
be the simulation such that
\mathfrak{m}\rightharpoonup\Phi

\{(\mathfrak{m}_{i},e_{i},\sigma_{i})\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}

8
Quasimodel extraction

Next, we show how simulations can be used to extract quasimodels from the canonical model, leading to a proof of completeness for

{\sf e}

Definition 8.1
.

Next we turn our attention to

Let

\prec_{v}^{\mathfrak{m}}

\Sigma\Subset\mathcal{L}_{hv}

2.

Proof.

By the truth lemma (Theorem

\sigma(r^{\prime})\succ_{v}^{\rm c}\Phi
we conclude
\Diamond_{v}\,{\rm Sim}(\mathfrak{m}\!\upharpoonright_{r^{\prime}})\in\Phi
.

(\Leftarrow)

The base case is satisfied by defining the simulation
\sigma:|\mathfrak{m}|\to W^{\rm c}
as
\sigma(r^{\mathfrak{m}}):=\Phi
given that
\mathfrak{m}
is simply a single node
r^{\mathfrak{m}}
.
It now suffices to show
\ell^{\mathfrak{m}}(r^{\mathfrak{m}})=\Phi\cap\Sigma
assuming that
\bigwedge\ell^{\mathfrak{m}}(r^{\mathfrak{m}})\in\Phi
. The inclusion
\subseteq
holds trivially by definition of
\ell^{\mathfrak{m}}
and the maximality of
\Phi
. For the reverse inclusion, let
\varphi\in\Phi\cap\Sigma
and suppose towards a contradiction that
\varphi\notin\ell^{\mathfrak{m}}(r^{\mathfrak{m}})
. Since
\Sigma
is closed under single negations, we have that
\neg\varphi\in\Sigma
. Therefore, since
\ell^{\mathfrak{m}}(r^{\mathfrak{m}})
is a finite
\Sigma
-type, we obtain
\neg\varphi\in\ell^{\mathfrak{m}}(r^{\mathfrak{m}})
. From
\bigwedge\ell^{\mathfrak{m}}(r^{\mathfrak{m}})\in\Phi
it then follows that
\neg\varphi\in\Phi
, that is,
\varphi\notin\Phi
, which contradicts the assumption.

For the inductive step, assume that
\mathrm{Sim}(\mathfrak{m})\in\Phi
. Then, for any
r^{\prime}\succ_{v}^{\mathfrak{m}}r^{\mathfrak{m}}
we have that
\Diamond_{v}\mathrm{Sim}(\mathfrak{m}\!\upharpoonright_{r^{\prime}})\in\Phi
. By the witnessing Lemma
5.3
, there is some
\Phi^{\prime}\succ_{v}^{\rm c}\Phi
such that
\mathrm{Sim}(\mathfrak{m}\!\upharpoonright_{r^{\prime}})\in\Phi^{\prime}
. Therefore, by the inductive hypothesis there is some simulation
\sigma_{r^{\prime}}:|\mathfrak{m}\!\upharpoonright_{r^{\prime}}|\to W^{\rm c}
for which
\mathfrak{m}\!\upharpoonright_{r^{\prime}}\rightharpoonup\Phi^{\prime}
. If we define
\sigma:|\mathfrak{m}|\to W^{\rm c}
by setting
\sigma(r^{\mathfrak{m}}):=\Phi
and
\sigma(w):=\sigma_{r^{\prime}}(w)
for
w
reachable from some
r^{\prime}\succ_{v}^{\mathfrak{m}}r^{\mathfrak{m}}
, it is easy to conclude that
\sigma:|\mathfrak{m}|\to W^{\rm c}
is a simulation satisfying
\mathfrak{m}\rightharpoonup\Phi
; details are left to the reader. ∎

With these formulas at hand, we are ready to prove a version of Lemma
8.4
for
[\mathsf{GL},\mathsf{GL}]^{\sf e}
, the last ingredient in our proof.

Lemma 9.2
.

Let
\Sigma\Subset\mathcal{L}_{hv}
and let
\mathcal{M}^{\rm c}
be the canonical model of the expanding commutator
\sf[GL,GL]^{e}
.
If
\Phi\in W^{\rm c}
, then there exists a finite
\Sigma
-quasimodel
\mathfrak{Q}
and a
\mathfrak{Q}
-morphism
\pi^{\mathfrak{Q}}
for
\sf[GL,GL]^{e}
such that
\pi^{\mathfrak{Q}}(r^{\mathfrak{Q}})=\Phi
.

Proof.

The proof is almost identical to that of Lemma
8.4
:
we define a sequence
\{(\mathfrak{Q_{i}},\pi^{i})\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
where each
\mathfrak{Q}_{i}
is a weak
\Sigma
-quasimodel and
\pi^{i}
is a
\mathfrak{Q}_{i}
-morphism for
\sf[GL,GL]^{e}
. As before,
\mathfrak{Q}_{0}
is a weak
\Sigma
-quasimodel consisting of a single point
r^{0}
such that
\pi^{0}(r^{0}):=\Phi
and
\mu^{0}(r^{0})
is chosen to satisfy
\mu^{0}(r^{0})\rightharpoonup\Phi
according to Lemma
7.3
.

The key difference is in the inductive step.
Assume that
\mathfrak{Q}_{i}
and
\pi^{i}
have been defined.
For every horizontal defect
\delta
of some
\mu^{i}(t)
with
t\in T^{i}
that is not realized by any moment of
\mathfrak{Q}_{i}
, add a fresh successor
s\succ_{h}^{i+1}t
.
Choose
\Phi^{\prime}\succ_{h}^{\rm c}\pi^{i}(t)
and a
\Sigma
-moment
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
as provided by Lemma
7.5
.
In the proof of Lemma
8.4
, we would have set
\pi^{i+1}(s)=\Phi
; however, this could lead to an ill-founded quasimodel.

This is where the
\sf GL
axiom and our simulation formulas come into play.
By Theorem
9.1
, we have
{\rm Sim}(\mathfrak{m}^{\prime})\in\Phi^{\prime}
. Hence, by the definition of
\prec_{h}^{\rm c}
, it follows that
\Diamond_{h}\,{\rm Sim}(\mathfrak{m}^{\prime})\in\pi^{i}(t)
.
Using the canonical witness property (Lemma
5.3
), there exists
\Psi^{\prime}\,\succ_{h}^{\rm c}\,\pi^{i}(t)
such that

{\rm Sim}(\mathfrak{m}^{\prime})\wedge\neg\Diamond_{h}\,{\rm Sim}(\mathfrak{m}^{\prime})\in\Psi^{\prime}.

Set
\pi^{i+1}(s):=\Psi^{\prime}
and
\mu^{i+1}(s):=\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
. Then again by Theorem
9.1
, we have
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}\rightharpoonup\Psi^{\prime}
.

We claim that the sequence stabilizes at some
i\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying the statement, that is,
\mathfrak{Q}_{i}
is a finite
\Sigma
-quasimodel where the horizontal defects of its moments are all realized. Towards a contradiction, suppose not. Then, the sequence
\{(\mathfrak{Q}_{i},\pi^{i})\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is increasing, with
\mathfrak{Q}_{i}\subseteq\mathfrak{Q}_{i+1}
, and uniformly finitely bounded. Let
\mathfrak{Q}_{\infty}:=\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\mathfrak{Q}_{i}
and
\pi^{\infty}:=\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\pi^{i}
. Then,
\mathfrak{Q}^{\infty}
defines an infinite
\Sigma
-quasimodel.
By König’s lemma, there exists an infinite branch
t_{0}\prec_{h}^{\infty}t_{1}\prec_{h}^{\infty}t_{2}\prec_{h}^{\infty}\cdots
in
\mathfrak{Q}_{\infty}
. By Kruskal’s Theorem
6.4
, there exist
i<j
such that
\mu^{\infty}(t_{i})\sqsubseteq^{\Sigma}\mu^{\infty}(t_{j})
. By Theorem
9.1
, it follows that
{\rm Sim}(\mu^{\infty}(t_{i}))\in\pi^{\infty}(t_{j})
, contradicting
\neg\Diamond_{h}\,{\rm Sim}(\mu^{\infty}({t_{i}}))\in\pi^{\infty}(t_{i})
which is given by construction.
∎

Theorem 9.3
.

The logic
[\sf{GL},\sf{GL}]^{\sf e}
is sound and complete with respect to the following classes:

1.

the class of (finite) expanding domain frames
(\sf{GL}\times\sf{GL})^{\sf e}
;

2.

the class of (finite) embedding domain frames
(\sf{GL}\rtimes\sf{GL})^{\sf e}
;

3.

the class of (finite) forward-confluent frames
\sf{GL}\rtimes\sf{GL}
.

Proof.

Analogous to the proof of Theorem
5.5
, except that we now use Lemma
9.2
.
∎

Since the logic

We have shown that

\sf Grz

\sf GL.3

\sf K4.3

[

of determining which expanding commutators are complete when one of the components is Noetherian.

We could likewise vary the vertical component and consider other logics of the form
[{\sf GL},{\sf L}_{v}]^{\sf e}

{\sf L}_{v}\in\{\mathsf{K4},\mathsf{K4.3}\}
will lead to incompleteness, but setting
{\sf L}_{v}\in\{{\sf Grz},{\sf GL.3},{\sf Grz.3}\}
to completeness. The reason for this is that the formulas
{\rm Sim}(\mathfrak{m})
are available over the class of
\sf Grz
models
[

but usually not for non-Noetherian logics, so our proof strategy could be adapted only in the former case.

Question 10.2

In those cases in which the commutator is incomplete, is the expanding domain logic finitely axiomatisable?
Can a natural axiomatisation be provided?

Note that ‘natural’ here is not meant to have a technical meaning, e.g. Peano arithmetic is not finitely axiomatisable but one may argue that the axioms are natural.

Another very intriguing line of inquiry is to consider intuitionistic companions of expanding products. We can define a Gödel-Tarski embedding
t

•

t(\Box\varphi)=\boxdot_{h}\Box_{v}t(\varphi)

If
\sf L
