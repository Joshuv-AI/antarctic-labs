Barbed suture-assisted tension reduction and de-epithelialization for
clip closure of a fecalith-associated rectal fistula

Zhijun He, Yanhua Nong, Xiaotong Wang, Yunxiao Liang, Wenhua Zhang

Endoscopic approaches, including endoscopic clips, stenting, and suturing, provide
minimally invasive options for gastrointestinal fistulas, although their efficacy
may be limited in complex cases.

A 71-year-old woman, 12 years post–rectal cancer resection, presented with a chronic
rectal fistula. Colonoscopy showed a 0.7-cm fistulous opening at the colorectal
anastomosis (

). Abdominal computed
tomography revealed a 2.8-cm high-density fecalith impacted within the fistula tract
(

).

Endoscopic extraction followed by fistula closure was planned. After gradual dilation
of the fistula tract using the gastroscope shaft, the endoscope was advanced into
the cavity. The fecalith was fragmented and extracted with snares and forceps (

).

Partial approximation of the fistula edges was first achieved using a transanal
barbed suture technique to reduce local tension. Subsequently, circumferential
electrocoagulation was applied to de-epithelialize the fistula edges. Finally, a 19
mm endoscopic clip was deployed to completely close the fistula opening (

,

). Follow-up endoscopy and contrast
radiography performed 3 to 4 weeks later confirmed complete fistula healing without
residual leakage (

).

Endoscopic clip-assisted closure has demonstrated efficacy in gastrointestinal
fistulas; however, success may decrease in larger or chronic fistulas because of
fibrosis, epithelialization, and excessive tissue tension.

Electrocoagulated edges enhance tissue
engagement and positioning for the clip, and may promote fistula healing through
de-epithelialization.

In the
present case, barbed sutures provided sustained tension reduction without knot tying
and minimized suture slippage, thereby facilitating the approximation of the fistula
edges before endoscopic clip deployment.

This combined minimally invasive strategy—barbed suture–assisted tension reduction,
edge electrocoagulation, and endoscopic clip closure—offers a feasible rescue option
for larger or chronic colorectal fistula refractory to the endoscopic clip alone;
further studies are needed to assess long-term efficacy and reproducibility.
