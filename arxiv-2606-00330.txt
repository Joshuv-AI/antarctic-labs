Singular central limit theorems for the spherical ensemble and beyond
Djalil Chafaï, David García-Zelada, Yuan Yuan Xu

Abstract
. We study the fluctuations of logarithmic Green singularities in the spherical ensemble, viewed as a random discretization of the two-sphere. Smooth observables exhibit the usual Sobolev or Gaussian free field fluctuations, whereas logarithmic singularities live on a larger logarithmic scale and asymptotically decouple in high-dimension, producing an explicit white-noise limit. The result gives precise asymptotics for logarithmic potentials and characteristic polynomials, with constants expressed through chordal geometry on the sphere.

60B20 - Random matrices; 60F05 - Central limit and other weak theorems; 60G55 - Point processes; 82B21 - Continuum particle systems in equilibrium statistical mechanics; 31C12 - Potential theory on Riemannian manifolds and other spaces.

1.
Introduction

The spherical ensemble is a unitary invariant random matrix model obtained
by taking the ratio of two independent complex Ginibre matrices. Its
spectrum is a heavy-tailed determinantal Coulomb gas on the complex plane.
Its equilibrium measure is the complex Cauchy distribution. This planar gas
is the image by the stereographic projection of a chordal gas on the
two-sphere. Our first main result is a Central Limit Theorem (CLT) for linear
statistics for test functions with an arbitrary finite number of
logarithmic singularities. The normalization is at the order of the square root of the
logarithm of the dimension. The contributions of the singularities are asymptotically
independent, and the asymptotic variance depends only on the
weights of the singularities. We deduce a multipoint CLT for the logarithm
of the modulus of the characteristic polynomial, which is the logarithmic
potential of the empirical spectral distribution. We also deduce a
multipoint CLT for the linear statistics tested against the Green function of the
two-sphere. As a second main result, we compute covariance asymptotics showing that the
associated unnormalised fields are log-correlated. The approach relies on an interplay
between probabilistic, analytic, and geometric arguments. Furthermore,
we also establish, under suitable assumptions, a matrix-universality extension
when the Ginibre matrices are replaced by Girko
matrices. Finally, we discuss possible geometric universality extensions in which the
sphere is replaced by a more general compact Riemann surface, as well as
natural open problems related to Fisher–Hartwig asymptotics and Gaussian
multiplicative chaos.

In short, this work is about the high-dimensional spectral analysis of random matrices from the spherical ensemble, a famous model of mathematical physics. We obtain CLTs for test functions with logarithmic singularities. This covers in particular the asymptotic analysis of the logarithm of the modulus of the characteristic polynomial.

Definition 1.1

(Logarithmic singularity)

Let

We say that a smooth

The basic example that will give us a lot of information about the other ones is the function

We are now ready to state our first main result. The message is that the asymptotic variance depends on the test function only via the singularities weights. This phenomenon reminds the residue method in complex analysis.

Theorem 1.2

(CLT for linear statistics with multiple-singularities test function)
.

If
f:\overline{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{k}\}\to\mathbb{R}
is smooth, and has logarithmic singularities at the distinct points
p_{1},\dots,p_{k}\in\overline{\mathbb{C}}

\frac{L_{n}(f)-\mathbb{E}(L_{n}(f))}{\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}\log n}}=\frac{L_{n}(f)-n\int f\mathrm{d}\mu}{\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}\log n}}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\mathrm{d}}\mathcal{N}(0,c_{1}^{2}+\cdots+c_{k}^{2}).

A proof of Theorem
1.2

1.8

1.7.
CLT for counting function

It is natural to ask about the behavior for other singular test functions such that indicators of subsets. In this special case, the linear statistics is a counting function. Let us specialize to radial subsets, namely the indicator

4.1
, or the Costin–Lebowitz type result for determinantal point process from

More generally, it is known that for two-dimensional determinantal Coulomb gases and the indicators of regular subsets of the bulk, the variance behaves like

A comprehensive classification of the nature of the fluctuation depending on the kind of singularity of the test function and the type of decay of the two-points correlation is still to be done.

2.9.
Law of large numbers and GFF CLT

Following
48

(2.36)

\frac{L_{n}(f)-n\int f\mathrm{d}\mu}{n}=\frac{1}{n}\int f\mathrm{d}L_{n}-\int f\mathrm{d}\mu\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0

is

\mathbb{E}L_{n}=n\mu\quad\text{for all $n\geq 1$}.

with respect to

.
Regarding the fluctuation, and following

. The last formulation comes from the fact that

and

Now still following

\mathrm{Cov}(L_{n}(f),L_{n}(g))\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\tfrac{1}{4\pi}\langle f,g\rangle,

|\mathbb{E}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\alpha X_{n}})\mathbb{E}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\beta Y_{n}})|

is bounded from below.
The latter occurs at least for
\alpha
and
\beta
small since
its limit is
|\gamma(\alpha)\gamma(\beta)|
.
This implies that

\mathbb{E}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(\alpha X+\beta Y)})=\gamma(\alpha)\gamma(\beta)
for every
\alpha,\beta

small enough and, in particular,

\mathbb{E}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(\alpha(X-Y))})=|\gamma(\alpha)|^{2}
for
\alpha
small.
Since

X-Y\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1/2)
,
we also know that

\mathbb{E}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\alpha(X-Y)})=\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha^{2}/4}

which implies that

|\gamma(\alpha)|^{2}=e^{-\alpha^{2}/4}
at
least for
\alpha
small enough.
Consider
\widetilde{X}
an independent copy
of
X
so that

\mathbb{E}(e^{i(\alpha(X-\widetilde{X}))})=|\gamma(\alpha)|^{2}

for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
.
The moments of
X-\widetilde{X}

can be obtained by differentiating

|\gamma(\alpha)|^{2}
at the origin so
that they coincide with the ones of

X-Y
implying that both
X-\widetilde{X}
and
X-Y
have the same law and

|\gamma(\alpha)|^{2}=\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha^{2}/4}\quad\mbox{ for every }\alpha\in\mathbb{R}.

But this entails that
\gamma(\alpha)
is non-zero for every
\alpha
so that

X
and
Y
are independent
by Lemma
3.3
. Because of this independence and
since

X-Y
is Gaussian, we may use Cramér’s decomposition theorem
to conclude that
X
and

Y
are Gaussian and by using that
X
and
Y
have the same law
we get

(3.9)

X\sim\mathcal{N}\bigg(0,\frac{1}{4}\bigg)

which is what we wanted : the CLT (
1.13
) for
k=1
and
p_{1}=0
.

Suppose that

has a single singularity located at

having weight

. By linearity we can assume that

. By the

invariance of (

) and

, we can assume that

, so the CLT for

3.3.
General case

Consider
f
with logarithmic singularities at

with weights

. Take

(3.11)

and

, and that

We may apply iteratively Lemma

to

\psi_{n}=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(\alpha_{1}f_{1}+\dots+\alpha_{j-1}f_{j-1})/\sqrt{\log(n)}}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\varphi_{n}=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\alpha_{j}f_{j}/\sqrt{\log(n)}}

to get that the random variables

.
Indeed,
if we already know that

for

3.4.
Lemmas for localization and decoupling

Lemma 3.1

(Uniform variance bound for Lipschitz functions)
.

For all
g:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}
and
n\geq 1
,

(3.14)

\mathrm{Var}(L_{n}(g))\leq\frac{1}{2}{\|g\|}_{\mathrm{Lip.}}^{2}\quad\text{where}\quad{\|g\|}_{\mathrm{Lip.}}=\sup_{w\neq u}\frac{|g(w)-g(u)|}{d(w,u)}.

In particular, if
{\|g\|}_{\mathrm{Lip.}}<\infty
then
L_{n}(g)-\mathbb{E}(L_{n}(g))=O_{L^{2},n\to\infty}(1)
.

Proof.

By the formula (
2.35
) for
\mathrm{Var}(L_{n}(g))=\mathrm{Cov}(L_{n}(g),L_{n}(g))
and
the Lipschitz bound, it remains to control

(3.15)

\int\Bigl(\int d(w,u)^{2}|K_{n}(w,u)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu(w)\Bigr)\mathrm{d}\mu(u).

By
\mathrm{SO}(3)
invariance of
\mu
and
K_{n}
, the integral above equals the
integral with
u
fixed.
Now, by combining (
2.9
) and (
2.11
), it follows that the image
of
\mu
by the map
w\mapsto d(w,u)^{2}
is uniform on
[0,1]
.
Combining with the expression (
2.22
) of the kernel
K_{n}
in terms of the chordal distance
d
, we get

(3.16)

\int d(w,u)^{2}|K_{n}(w,u)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu(w)=n^{2}\int d(w,u)^{2}(1-d(w,u)^{2})^{n-1}\mathrm{d}\mu(w)=n^{2}\int_{0}^{1}x(1-x)^{n-1}\mathrm{d}x=\frac{n}{n+1}\leq 1.

∎

Lemma 3.2

(
L^{2}
Decoupling)
.

If
f,g\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mu)
have disjoint supports and at least one of them is compact in
\mathbb{C}
or, equivalently,
their supports in
\overline{\mathbb{C}}

are disjoint, then

(3.17)

\mathrm{Cov}(L_{n}(f),L_{n}(g))\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0.

Proof.

Denote by
\mathcal{A}
the support of
f
and by

\mathcal{B}
the support of
g
. Since
\mathcal{A}\cap\mathcal{B}=\varnothing
, we get,
from (
2.35
),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}(L_{n}(f),L_{n}(g))

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{B}}f(x)g(y)|K_{n}(x,y)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\mathrm{d}\mu(y).

The assumptions on
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
give
\rho=d(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})=\inf_{(u,v)\in\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{B}}d(u,v)\in(0,1)
, and thus, by (
2.22
),

(3.18)

|\mathrm{Cov}(L_{n}(f),L_{n}(g))|\leq n^{2}(1-\rho^{2})^{n-1}\|f\|_{1}\|g\|_{1}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0.

∎

Lemma 3.3

(Decoupling)
.

Let
\psi_{n},\varphi_{n}:\overline{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathbb{C}
be
bounded measurable functions such that
|\psi_{n}|,|\varphi_{n}|\leq 1
. Suppose there are
two disjoint closed sets

\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\subset\overline{\mathbb{C}}
such that

(3.19)

\psi_{n}(x)=1\mbox{ for }x\notin\mathcal{A}\quad\text{and}\quad\varphi_{n}(x)=1\mbox{ for }x\notin\mathcal{B}.

Define

\Psi_{n}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\psi_{n}(Z_{n,i})
and

\Phi_{n}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\varphi_{n}(Z_{n,i})
where
Z_{n}=(Z_{n,1},\ldots,Z_{n,n})
is distributed according to (
1.1
).
Then, if along a subsequence

|\mathbb{E}[\Psi_{n}]\mathbb{E}[\Phi_{n}]|\geq\varepsilon

for some
\varepsilon>0
, along the same
subsequence

(3.20)

\mathbb{E}[\Psi_{n}\Phi_{n}]\sim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[\Psi_{n}]\mathbb{E}[\Phi_{n}].

Proof.

Recall that
\mathcal{P}_{n}
is the set
of polynomials of degree less or equal than
n-1
weighted
with
(1+|z|^{2})^{-(n-1)/2}
, as in (
2.18
).
Recall also that

j_{n}:\mathcal{P}_{n}\to L^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mu)

denotes the (isometric) inclusion
and that
j_{n}^{*}:L^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mu)\to\mathcal{P}_{n}

is the orthogonal projection from
L^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mu)
onto the space of polynomials
\mathcal{P}_{n}
. The
determinant identity (
2.27
) gives

\mathbb{E}[\Psi_{n}]=\det(j_{n}^{*}\psi_{n}j_{n}),\quad\mathbb{E}[\Phi_{n}]=\det(j_{n}^{*}\varphi_{n}j_{n}),\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\mathbb{E}[\Psi_{n}\Phi_{n}]=\det(j_{n}^{*}\psi_{n}\varphi_{n}j_{n}),

where a function
g:\overline{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathbb{R}
inside the determinant represent
the multiplication operator by
g
.
We omit the indices
n
to simplify notation.
Our goal is to study the expression

\frac{\mathbb{E}[\Psi\Phi]}{\mathbb{E}[\Psi]\mathbb{E}[\Phi]}=\frac{\det(j^{*}\psi\varphi j)}{\det(j^{*}\psi j)\det(j^{*}\varphi j)}=\det\big((j^{*}\psi\varphi j)(j^{*}\psi j)^{-1}(j^{*}\varphi j)^{-1}\big).

Since

|\det(1+A_{n})-1|\leq\|A_{n}\|_{1}\mathrm{e}^{\|A_{n}\|_{1}}
for any
matrix
A_{n}
of size
n
, it is enough to control the operator

1-(j^{*}\psi\varphi j)(j^{*}\psi j)^{-1}(j^{*}\varphi j)^{-1}=\Big[(j^{*}\varphi j)(j^{*}\psi j)-(j^{*}\psi\varphi j)\Big](j^{*}\psi j)^{-1}(j^{*}\varphi j)^{-1}.

We will bound the operator norm of

(j^{*}\psi j)^{-1}
and of
(j^{*}\varphi j)^{-1}

and show that

\|(j^{*}\varphi j)(j^{*}\psi j)-(j^{*}\psi\varphi j)\|_{1}\to 0
which will allow us to conclude.
Since
\psi=1
outside
\mathcal{A}
and
\varphi=1
outside
\mathcal{B}

we get that

\psi\varphi=\varphi+\psi-1

which allows us to write

j^{*}\psi\varphi j=j^{*}\varphi j+j^{*}\psi j-j^{*}j.

Using that
j^{*}jj^{*}=j^{*}
and that
jj^{*}j=j
, we obtain

(j^{*}\varphi j)(j^{*}\psi j)-(j^{*}\psi\varphi j)=\big(j^{*}(\varphi-1)j\big)\big(j^{*}(\psi-1)j\big).

Now, it is enough to show that

•

\lim_{n\to\infty}\big\|\big(j^{*}(\varphi-1)j\big)\big(j^{*}(\psi-1)j\big)\big\|_{1}=0

•

\|(j^{*}\varphi j)^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op.}}
is bounded in
n
and

•

\|(j^{*}\psi j)^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op.}}
is bounded in
n
.

We denote by
\|A\|_{p}
the Schatten
p
-norm of
A
, the
\ell^{p}
norm of its singular values. In particular
\|A\|_{\infty}=\|A\|_{\mathrm{op.}}
.
Now

(3.21)

\displaystyle\big\|\big(j^{*}(\varphi-1)j\big)\big(j^{*}(\psi-1)j\big)\big\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\|j^{*}\|_{4}\big\|(\varphi-1)jj^{*}(\psi-1)\big\|_{2}\|j\|_{4}

(3.22)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{n}\Bigl(\int_{\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}}|\varphi(x)-1|^{2}|K(x,y)|^{2}|\psi(y)-1|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\mathrm{d}\mu(y)\Bigr)^{1/2}.

Since
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
are disjoint compact
closed subsets of the compact space
\overline{\mathbb{C}}
,
Equation
(
2.22
) tells us that there is some constant
C>0

such that
|K(x,y)|\leq C^{-1}n\mathrm{e}^{-Cn}
for every
x\in\mathcal{A}
and
y\in\mathcal{B}
.
We obtain

\displaystyle\sqrt{n}\Bigl(\int_{\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}}|\varphi(x)-1|^{2}|K(x,y)|^{2}|\psi(y)-1|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\mathrm{d}\mu(y)\Bigr)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{n}\Bigl(\int_{\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{A}}|\varphi(x)-1|^{2}|K(x,y)|^{2}|\psi(y)-1|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\mathrm{d}\mu(y)\Bigr)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq 4C^{-1}n^{\frac{3}{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-Cn}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0.

Now we bound
\|(j^{*}\varphi j)^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op.}}
which is
the inverse of the least singular value of
j^{*}\varphi j
.
We have
\|j^{*}\varphi j\|_{\mathrm{op.}}\leq\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{op.}}\leq 1
by hypothesis
so that every singular value of
j^{*}\varphi j
is bounded by
1
.
If we add the fact that the absolute value of the determinant
is the product of singular values,
we get that the least singular value is bounded from below by the absolute value
of the determinant.
It is enough to show that
|\det(j^{*}\varphi j)|
is bounded from below
but this is part of the hypotheses because

|\det(j^{*}\varphi j)|=|\mathbb{E}[\Phi]|\geq|\mathbb{E}[\Phi]\mathbb{E}[\Psi]|\geq\varepsilon
. A similar reasoning works
to bound
\|(j^{*}\psi j)^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op.}}
. ∎

1.3
and Theorem

4.1.
Mean, variance, and one-point CLT via cumulants

Following Lemma
4.1
, for all fixed
p\in\overline{\mathbb{C}}
, since
\kappa_{m}(L_{n}(g_{p}))
converges to a finite value as
n\to\infty
for all
m\geq 3
, while
\kappa_{2}(L_{n}(g_{p}))\to+\infty
, the Fréchet–Shohat theorem gives

(4.1)

\frac{L_{n}(g_{p})-\mathbb{E}(L_{n}(g_{p}))}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}(L_{n}(g_{p}))}}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\mathrm{d}}\mathcal{N}(0,1).

Since moreover
\mathbb{E}(L_{n}(g_{p}))=-\frac{n}{2}
and
\mathrm{Var}(L_{n}(g_{p}))\sim\frac{1}{4}\log(n)
, we get an alternative cumulants based proof of (
1.16
).

Lemma 4.1

(Cumulants of
L_{n}(g_{p})
)
.

For all
p\in\overline{\mathbb{C}}
and all
n\geq 1
and
m\geq 1
,

(4.2)

\kappa_{m}(L_{n}(g_{p}))=\frac{(-1)^{m}(m-1)!}{2^{m}}H_{n}^{(m-1)}

where
H^{(s)}_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k^{s}}
is the generalized harmonic number. In particular, with
H_{n}=H_{n}^{(1)}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k}
,

(4.3)

\mathbb{E}(L_{n}(g_{p}))=\kappa_{1}(L_{n}(g_{p}))=-\frac{n}{2},\quad\mathrm{Var}(L_{n}(g_{p}))=\kappa_{2}(L_{n}(g_{p}))=\frac{1}{4}H_{n}\sim\frac{\log(n)}{4}

while for all
m\geq 3
, denoting
\zeta(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{s}}
the Riemann zeta function,

(4.4)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\kappa_{m}(L_{n}(g_{p}))=\frac{(-1)^{m}(m-1)!}{2^{m}}\zeta(m-1).

Proof.

By the
\mathrm{O}(3)
invariance of (
1.4
), the law of

L_{n}(g_{p})
does not depend on
p
. We take
p=\infty
in order to benefit from its radiality (we could alternatively take
g_{0}
). We have

g_{\infty}(w)=-\frac{1}{2}\log(1+|w|^{2})
. For all
1\leq k\leq n
, with

\xi_{n,k}
as in (
2.29
), the Laplace transform of

S_{n,k}=-\frac{1}{2}\log(1+\xi_{n,k}^{2})
is

(4.5)

\mathbb{E}(\mathrm{e}^{\theta S_{n,k}})=\mathbb{E}((1+|\xi_{n,k}|^{2})^{-\frac{\theta}{2}})=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{n!}{(k-1)!(n-k)!}\frac{x^{k-1}(1+x)^{\frac{-\theta}{2}}}{(1+x)^{n+1}}\mathrm{d}x=\frac{n!}{(n-k)!}\frac{\Gamma(n-k+1+\frac{\theta}{2})}{\Gamma(n+1+\frac{\theta}{2})}

for all real
\theta>-2(n-k+1)
. It follows then by (
2.29
) that
the log-Laplace transform of
S_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}S_{n,k}=L_{n}(g_{\infty})
is

(4.6)

\log\mathbb{E}(\mathrm{e}^{\theta S_{n}})=n\bigl(\log\Gamma(n+1)-\log\Gamma(n+1+\tfrac{\theta}{2})\bigr)+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\bigl(\log\Gamma(k+1+\tfrac{\theta}{2})-\log\Gamma(k+1)\bigr)

for every
\theta>-2
. Differentiating
(
4.6
) at
\theta=0
gives the cumulants of
S_{n}
:

(4.7)

\kappa_{m}(S_{n})=\frac{1}{2^{m}}\big(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\psi_{m-1}(k)-n\psi_{m-1}(n+1)\big)=\frac{1}{2^{m}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\big(\psi_{m-1}(k)-\psi_{m-1}(n+1)\big)

where
\psi_{m}=\psi^{(m)}
is the polygamma
functions given by

\frac{\mathrm{d}^{m}}{\mathrm{d}\theta^{m}}\log\Gamma(x+\tfrac{\theta}{2})\big|_{\theta=0}=\frac{1}{2^{m}}\psi_{m-1}(x)
.
We have by using a recursion argument after
differentiating
\log\Gamma(s+1)=\log s+\log\Gamma(s)
that for

k\in\{1,\dots,n\}

\psi_{m-1}(n+1)-\psi_{m-1}(k)=(m-1)!(-1)^{m-1}(H_{n}^{(m)}-H_{k-1}^{(m)}).

The sum

\sum_{k=1}^{n}(H_{n}^{(m)}-H_{k-1}^{(m)})=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{j^{m}}1_{j\geq k})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{j^{m}}1_{j\geq k})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{j^{m-1}}=H_{n}^{(m-1)}

allows us to obtain

(4.8)

\kappa_{m}(S_{n})=\frac{(-1)^{m}(m-1)!}{2^{m}}H_{n}^{(m-1)}.

∎

4.2.
Proof of Corollary
1.3

From (
4.3
) we know that
\mathbb{E}(L_{n}(g_{p}))=-\frac{n}{2}
, for all

.
Proving (

(4.9)

\left(\frac{L_{n}(g_{p_{1}})+\frac{n}{2}}{\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}\log(n)}},\ldots,\frac{L_{n}(g_{p_{k}})+\frac{n}{2}}{\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}\log(n)}}\right)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\mathrm{d}}\mathcal{N}(0,I_{k}).

4.3.
Proof of Theorem

The formula for the variance in (
1.19
) comes from (
4.3
). The rest of this section is devoted to the proof of the formula for the off-diagonal covariance in (

). It is split in three lemmas.

Lemma 4.2

(Covariance for antipodal points)
.

For all
p\in\overline{\mathbb{C}}
, denoting
p^{*}=T(-T^{-1}(p))
its
antipodal, for all
n\geq 1
,

(4.11)

\mathrm{Cov}\big(L_{n}(g_{p}),L_{n}(g_{p^{*}})\big)=-\frac{n}{4}\psi_{1}(n+1)=-\frac{1}{4}+O_{n\to\infty}\Big(\frac{1}{n}\Big)

where
\psi_{1}
is the polygamma function of order
1
, second order derivative of
\log\Gamma
.

Proof.

By the invariance
under rotations of (
1.4
), the result is the
same for any
p
. We take
p=0
, which gives
p^{*}=\infty
. Now, for

t,s\in\mathbb{R}
, we set
g=t\,g_{0}+sg_{\infty}
. Since
g
is radial, the
Kostlan formula (
2.28
) gives

(4.12)

\mathbb{E}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(tL_{n}(g_{0})+sL_{n}(g_{\infty}))})=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{B(k+1+\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{2},n-k+\mathrm{i}\frac{s}{2})}{B(k+1,n-k)}.

where
B
is the Euler Beta function. Taking logarithms and using
B(a,b)=\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)/\Gamma(a+b)
gives

(4.13)

\log\mathbb{E}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(tL_{n}(g_{0})+sL_{n}(g_{\infty}))})=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\Big(\log\Gamma(k+1+\mathrm{i}\tfrac{t}{2})+\log\Gamma(n-k+\mathrm{i}\tfrac{s}{2})-\log\Gamma(n+1+\mathrm{i}\tfrac{t+s}{2})\Big)+C,

where
C
does not depend on
(t,s)
. Only the last term contributes to the
mixed derivative at
(t,s)=(0,0)
, hence

(4.14)

-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t\partial s}\log\mathbb{E}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(tL_{n}(g_{0})+sL_{n}(g_{\infty}))})\Big|_{t=s=0}=-\frac{n}{4}\psi_{1}(n+1),

which is the covariance. Finally, the asymptotics comes from
\psi_{1}(x)=1/x+O_{x\to\infty}(1/x^{2})
.
∎

Lemma 4.3

(Covariance for distinct non-antipodal points)

If

,
x\cdot y\in(-1,1)
where
p=T(x)
,

\mathrm{Cov}(L_{n}(g_{p}),L_{n}(g_{q}))=-\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{4}\log\Big(\frac{1-x\cdot y}{2}\Big)+o_{n\to\infty}(1)=-\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{2}\log d(p,q)+o_{n\to\infty}(1).

According to (
4.11
), the formula (
4.15
)
remains valid when the points are antipodal,

, but (

)
is sharper.

Proof.

For notational convenience,

(6.46)

\displaystyle\int|\Delta_{z}\widetilde{h_{p}}(z)|\operatorname{d}\!{}^{2}z=

\displaystyle\int\left|\Delta_{z}\Big[\frac{1}{2}\log\big(|z|^{2}+n^{-2L}\big)\chi(|z|)\Big]\right|\operatorname{d}\!{}^{2}z

(6.47)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{2n^{-2L}}{(|z|^{2}+n^{-2L})^{2}}\chi(|z|)\operatorname{d}\!{}^{2}z+O(\log n)=O(\log n).

The same also applies to

(6.48)

\displaystyle\widetilde{L_{n}(h_{p})}:=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int\Delta_{z}\widetilde{h_{p}}(z)\int_{\eta_{0}}^{T}\operatorname{Im}\mathrm{Tr}G^{z}(\mathrm{i}\eta)\operatorname{d}\!{}\eta\operatorname{d}\!{}^{2}z,

with

(6.49)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big|L_{n}(h_{p})-\widetilde{L_{n}(h_{p})}\Big|^{k}=O\big(n^{-\epsilon/3}\big).

Hence for any fixed

,

6.41
) and (
6.48

\mathcal{X}_{n}

Proof of Lemma
6.6
.

The case for
k=1
was proved in
(
26
, Lemma 4.2)
, following the proof of
(
29
, Lemma 4)
. With a minor modification, the proof can be extended to general
k\geq 1
. For completeness, we present the details below. The proof crucially relies on the local laws in Lemma
6.3
and the tail bound for the smallest singular value in Lemma
6.4
.

Note that the spectrum of
H^{z}
defined in (
6.32
) is symmetrically distributed. By a simple spectral decomposition of
H^{z}
, we have

(6.57)

\displaystyle I:=\int_{0}^{\eta_{0}}\operatorname{Im}\mathrm{Tr}G^{z}(\mathrm{i}\eta)\mathrm{d}\eta=2\int_{0}^{\eta_{0}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\eta}{(\lambda_{j}^{z})^{2}+\eta^{2}}\operatorname{d}\!{}\eta,\qquad\eta_{0}=n^{-1-\epsilon},

where
\big(\lambda^{z}_{j}\big)_{j=1}^{n}
are the non-negative eigenvalues of
H^{z}
which are ordered non-decreasingly.
Let
L>0
be a large number to be fixed. Set
\eta_{L}=n^{-L}
and
\eta_{2}=n^{-1-\epsilon/2}
. Then we split the above sum of eigenvalues into three parts:

(6.58)

\displaystyle I=

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{\lambda^{z}_{j}\geq\eta_{2}}+\sum_{\eta_{L}\leq\lambda^{z}_{j}\leq\eta_{2}}+\sum_{\lambda^{z}_{j}\leq\eta_{L}}\Big)\int_{0}^{\eta_{0}}\frac{2\eta}{(\lambda_{j}^{z})^{2}+\eta^{2}}\operatorname{d}\!{}\eta=:I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.

For the first part of (
6.58
), we have

(6.59)

\displaystyle I_{1}\leq

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{\eta_{0}}\sum_{\lambda_{j}\geq\eta_{2}}\frac{\eta}{\lambda_{j}^{2}+\eta_{2}^{2}}\operatorname{d}\!{}\eta\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\eta_{0}^{2}}{(\lambda^{z}_{j})^{2}+\eta_{2}^{2}}=\frac{\eta_{0}^{2}}{2\eta_{2}}\operatorname{Im}\mathrm{Tr}G^{z}(\mathrm{i}\eta_{2})\leq\frac{\eta_{0}^{2}}{\eta_{2}}\frac{\eta_{3}}{\eta_{2}}\operatorname{Im}\mathrm{Tr}G^{z}(\mathrm{i}\eta_{3}),

where in the last step we used that the function
\eta\operatorname{Im}\mathrm{Tr}G^{z}(\mathrm{i}\eta)
is non-decreasing in
\eta>0
, and chose

\eta_{3}=n^{-1+\xi}
for a small
0<\xi<\epsilon/2
. Applying the local law in (
6.22
) for
G^{z}(\mathrm{i}\eta_{3})
, the third property of Lemma
6.2
, and that
m^{z}
from (
6.23
) is uniformly bounded, we obtain that

(6.60)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[|I_{1}|^{k}\big]=O\big(n^{-k\epsilon/2}\big).

We next estimate the second and third part of (
6.58
). Using that
\partial_{\eta}\log(\lambda^{2}+\eta^{2})=\frac{2\eta}{\lambda^{2}+\eta^{2}}
, we have

I_{2}+I_{3}=\Big(\sum_{\eta_{L}\leq\lambda^{z}_{j}\leq\eta_{2}}+\sum_{\lambda^{z}_{j}\leq\eta_{L}}\Big)\int_{0}^{\eta_{0}}\frac{2\eta}{(\lambda_{j}^{z})^{2}+\eta^{2}}\operatorname{d}\!{}\eta=\Big(\sum_{\eta_{L}\leq\lambda^{z}_{j}\leq\eta_{2}}+\sum_{\lambda^{z}_{j}\leq\eta_{L}}\Big)\log\Big(1+\frac{\eta_{0}^{2}}{(\lambda^{z}_{j})^{2}}\Big).

Note that for
\eta_{2}=n^{-1-\epsilon/2}
and
\eta_{3}=n^{-1+\xi}
for any small
\xi>0

\frac{\eta_{3}}{\eta_{2}}\operatorname{Im}\mathrm{Tr}G^{z}(\mathrm{i}\eta_{3})\geq\operatorname{Im}\mathrm{Tr}G^{z}(\mathrm{i}\eta_{2})=2\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\eta_{2}}{(\lambda^{z}_{j})^{2}+\eta_{2}^{2}}\geq\frac{2}{\eta_{2}}\#\big\{1\leq j\leq n:\lambda_{j}^{z}\leq\eta_{2}\big\}.

Hence using the local law in (
6.22
), by the definition of stochastic domination in (
6.20
), we have

\#\big\{1\leq j\leq n:\lambda_{j}^{z}\leq\eta_{2}\big\}\prec 1.

Note that
\big(\lambda^{z}_{i}\big)_{i=1}^{n}
coincide with singular values of
X^{z}=\widetilde{A}-z\widetilde{B}
defined in (
6.32
) arranged non-decreasingly. Hence

(6.61)

\displaystyle I_{2}=\sum_{\eta_{L}\leq\lambda^{z}_{j}\leq\eta_{2}}\log\Big(1+\frac{\eta_{0}^{2}}{(\lambda^{z}_{j})^{2}}\Big)\prec(C_{L}\log n)\mathbf{1}_{\lambda^{z}_{1}\leq\eta_{2}}.

Using Lemma
6.2
and the tail bound for the smallest singular value in (
6.30
), we have

(6.62)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[|I_{2}|^{k}\big]\prec(C_{L}\log n)^{k}{\mathbb{P}}\big(\lambda^{z}_{1}\leq\eta_{2}\big)=O\big(n^{-\epsilon/2}\big).

The third part
I_{3}
in (
6.58
) can be bounded similarly. That is,

(6.63)

\displaystyle I_{3}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda^{z}_{j}\leq\eta_{L}}\log\Big(1+\frac{\eta_{0}^{2}}{(\lambda^{z}_{j})^{2}}\Big)\leq-2\sum_{\lambda^{z}_{j}\leq\eta_{L}}\log(\lambda^{z}_{j})\leq-2n\log\big(\lambda^{z}_{1}\big)\mathbf{1}_{\lambda^{z}_{1}\leq\eta_{L}},

where we used that
\log((\lambda^{z}_{j})^{2}+(\eta_{0})^{2})\leq 0

for
\lambda^{z}_{j}\leq\eta_{L}=n^{-L}
. Hence by integration by parts, we have

(6.64)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[|I_{3}|^{k}\big]\leq

\displaystyle(2n)^{k}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(-\log\big(\lambda^{z}_{1}\big)\Big)^{k}\mathbf{1}_{\lambda^{z}_{1}\leq\eta_{L}}\Big]

(6.65)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(2n)^{k}\bigg((L\log n)^{k}\mathbb{P}\big(\lambda^{z}_{1}\leq n^{-L}\big)+k\int_{L\log n}^{\infty}s^{k-1}\mathbb{P}\big(\lambda^{z}_{1}\leq\mathrm{e}^{-s}\big)\operatorname{d}\!{}s\bigg)=O\big(n^{-100}\big),

by choosing a sufficiently large
L>0
depending on
k
using (
6.30
).

Summing up (
6.60
), (
6.62
) and (
6.65
), we finish the proof of Lemma
6.6
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
6.7
.

We will present the proof for any fixed
p\in{\mathbb{C}}
. For the infinity point
p=\infty
, the proof is similar using that the law of
{\tilde{A}}{\tilde{B}}^{-1}
is invariant under inversions. From now on, we will use the letter
w\in{\mathbb{C}}
to replace
p
.

For notational simplicity, we use
(\sigma^{w}_{i}=\sigma_{i}-w)_{i=1}^{n}
to denote the eigenvalues of the matrix
{\tilde{A}}{\tilde{B}}^{-1}-w
, which are arranged by modulus in a non-decreasing order,
i.e.,
|\sigma^{w}_{1}|\leq|\sigma^{w}_{2}|\leq\cdots\leq|\sigma^{w}_{n}|
. For any matrix
X\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}
, we use
(\lambda_{i}(X))_{i=1}^{n}
to denote the singular values of
X
, which are arranged in a non-decreasing order. Then we have

(6.66)

\displaystyle\big|L_{n}(h_{w})-L_{n}(\widetilde{h_{w}})\big|\leq

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{|\sigma^{w}_{i}|\leq n^{-L/2}}\log|\sigma^{w}_{i}|\chi(|\sigma^{w}_{i}|)\right|+\left|\sum_{|\sigma^{w}_{i}|\leq n^{-L/2}}\frac{1}{2}\log\big(|\sigma^{w}_{i}|^{2}+n^{-2L}\big)\chi(|\sigma^{w}_{i}|)\right|

(6.67)

\displaystyle\qquad+\left|\sum_{|\sigma^{w}_{i}|\geq n^{-L/2}}\log\Big(1+\frac{n^{-2L}}{|\sigma^{w}_{i}|^{2}}\Big)\chi(|\sigma^{w}_{i}|)\right|=:E_{1}+E_{2}+E_{3}.

It is easy to check
E_{2}\leq E_{1}
and
|E_{3}|\leq n^{-L+1}
using that
\log(1+|x|)\leq|x|
. We next estimate
E_{1}
. Note that the matrices
{\tilde{A}}
,
{\tilde{B}}
and
{\tilde{A}}{\tilde{B}}^{-1}-w
are almost surely invertible. By the Weyl–Horn–Johnson inequality, we have
\prod_{i=1}^{k}|\sigma^{w}_{i}|\geq\prod_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}(AB^{-1}-w)
. Hence the first term
E_{1}
is bounded by

(6.68)

\displaystyle E_{1}=\Big|\sum_{|\sigma^{w}_{i}|\leq n^{-L/2}}\log|\sigma^{w}_{i}|\chi(|\sigma^{w}_{i}|)\Big|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big|\log(\lambda_{i}({\tilde{A}}{\tilde{B}}^{-1}-w))\big|\mathbf{1}_{|\sigma^{w}_{i}|\leq n^{-L/2}}.

By the min-max theorem for singular values, we have

(6.69)

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}({\tilde{A}}{\tilde{B}}^{-1}-w)\geq\lambda_{i}({\tilde{A}}-w{\tilde{B}})\lambda_{n}({\tilde{B}}^{-1})=\|{\tilde{B}}\|^{-1}\lambda_{i}({\tilde{A}}-w{\tilde{B}})\geq\|{\tilde{B}}\|^{-1}\lambda_{1}({\tilde{A}}-w{\tilde{B}}),

with
\|{\tilde{B}}\|=\lambda_{n}({\tilde{B}})
. Note that
\|{\tilde{B}}\|_{\max}\leq\|{\tilde{B}}\|\leq n\|{\tilde{B}}\|_{\max}
and
{\tilde{B}}=B/\sqrt{n}
. From the moment condition
(Cond3)
on the matrix
B
and by the Markov inequality, for any fixed large
D>0
, we have

(6.70)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\Big(\|{\tilde{B}}\|\geq n\Big)\leq{\mathbb{P}}\Big(\|{\tilde{B}}\|_{\max}\geq n\Big)\leq n^{2}{\mathbb{P}}\Big(|B_{11}|\geq n^{1/2}\Big)\leq n^{2}\frac{\mathbb{E}|B_{11}|^{D}}{n^{D/2}}\leq C_{D}n^{-D/4},

for sufficiently large
n
. On the event
\Omega:=\{\|{\tilde{B}}\|\leq n\}
, using
\lambda_{i}({\tilde{A}}{\tilde{B}}^{-1}-w)\geq n^{-1}\lambda_{1}({\tilde{A}}-w{\tilde{B}})
and
\lambda_{1}({\tilde{A}}-w{\tilde{B}})\leq n\lambda_{1}({\tilde{A}}{\tilde{B}}^{-1}-w)\leq n|\sigma^{w}_{1}|\leq n^{-L/2+1}
, we have

(6.71)

\displaystyle E_{1}\leq n\Big(\log n+\big|\log(\lambda_{1}({\tilde{A}}-w{\tilde{B}}))\big|\Big)\mathbf{1}_{\lambda_{1}({\tilde{A}}-w{\tilde{B}})\leq n^{-L/2+1}}.

We next show that the contribution from the event
\Omega^{c}
is negligible. Note that from (
6.36
), (
6.43
) and the local law in (
6.22
), we obtain that, for any fixed
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\mathbb{E}\big[|L_{n}(h_{w})|^{k}\big]=n^{k}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\big(\operatorname{Im}m^{w}(\mathrm{i}\eta)-\operatorname{Im}m^{w=0}(\mathrm{i}\eta)\big)\mathrm{d}\eta\Big)^{k}+O_{\prec}\big((\log n)^{k}\big)=O\big((n\log n)^{k}\big),

where we also used that
m^{z}
defined in (
6.23
) satisfies
|m^{w}(\mathrm{i}\eta)|=O(\eta^{-1})
. Hence by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and using (
6.70
), we have

\mathbb{E}\Big[\big|L_{n}(h_{w})|^{k}\mathbf{1}_{\Omega^{c}}\Big]\leq C_{k}(n\log n)^{k}{\mathbb{P}}\big(\|{\tilde{B}}\|\geq n\big)\leq C_{D}(\log n)^{k}n^{k-D/4}.

By choosing
D
sufficiently large depending on
K_{0}
, the contribution to
\mathbb{E}|L_{n}(h_{w})|^{k}~(1\leq k\leq K_{0})
from the event
\Omega^{c}
is negligible. The same also applies to
\mathbb{E}|L_{n}(\widetilde{h_{w}})|^{k}
. Hence using (
6.71
), and that
E_{2}\leq E_{1}
,
|E_{3}|\leq n^{-L+1}
, we conclude

(6.72)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big|L_{n}(h_{w})-L_{n}(\widetilde{h_{w}})\big|^{k}\leq(2n)^{k}\mathbb{E}\left[\Big(\log n+\big|\log(\lambda_{1}({\tilde{A}}-w{\tilde{B}}))\big|\Big)^{k}\mathbf{1}_{\lambda_{1}({\tilde{A}}-w{\tilde{B}})\leq n^{-L/2+1}}\right]+O(n^{-L+1}),

for any
k\leq K_{0}
. Thus using Lemma
6.4
we have

(6.73)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big|L_{n}(h_{w})-L_{n}(\widetilde{h_{w}})\big|^{k}\leq

\displaystyle C_{k}n^{k}\mathbb{E}\left[\Big((\log n)^{k}+\big|\log(\lambda_{1}({\tilde{A}}-w{\tilde{B}}))\big|^{k}\Big)\mathbf{1}_{\lambda_{1}({\tilde{A}}-w{\tilde{B}})\leq n^{-L/2+1}}\right]+O(n^{-k(L-1)})

(6.74)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C^{\prime}_{k}n^{k}\bigg((L\log n)^{k}\mathbb{P}\big(\lambda_{1}({\tilde{A}}-w{\tilde{B}})\leq n^{-L/2+1}\big)+\int_{L/4\log n}^{\infty}ks^{k-1}\mathbb{P}\big(\lambda_{1}({\tilde{A}}-w{\tilde{B}})\leq\mathrm{e}^{-s}\big)\operatorname{d}\!{}s\bigg)=O(n^{-100k}),

for any
k\leq K_{0}
, by choosing a sufficiently large
L>0
which depends on
K_{0}
using (
6.30
). This proves Lemma
6.7
.
∎

We next prove Lemma

26

Lemma 6.10

(Lemma 3.1 from
47
)
.

Let
h
be a complex-valued random variable with finite moments. Define the
(p,q)
-cumulant of
h
to be

(6.75)

\displaystyle\kappa^{(p,q)}(h):=(-\mathrm{i})^{p+q}\Big(\frac{\partial^{p+q}}{\partial s^{p}\partial t^{q}}\log\mathbb{E}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}sh+\mathrm{i}t\overline{h}}\Big)\Big|_{s,t=0},

provided that the right side above exists.
Let
f:{\mathbb{C}}\times{\mathbb{C}}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
be a smooth function and denote its derivatives by

f^{(p,q)}(z_{1},z_{2}):=\frac{\partial^{p+q}}{\partial z^{p}_{1}\partial z^{q}_{2}}f(z_{1},z_{2}).

Then for any fixed
l\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we have

(6.76)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\bar{h}f(h,\bar{h})\big]=\sum_{p+q+1=1}^{l}\frac{1}{p!q!}\kappa^{(p,q+1)}(h)\mathbb{E}\big[f^{(p,q)}(h,\bar{h})\big]+R_{l+1}\,,

provided that all the expectations in (
6.76
) exist. The error term satisfies

(6.77)

\displaystyle|R_{l+1}|\leq C_{l}\mathbb{E}|h|^{l+1}\max_{p+q+1=l+1}\Big\{\sup_{|z|\leq M}|f^{(p,q)}(z,\bar{z})|\Big\}+C_{l}\mathbb{E}\Big[|h|^{l+1}1_{|h|>M}\Big]\max_{p+q+1=l+1}\|f^{(p,q)}(z,\bar{z})\|_{\infty},

and
M>0
is an arbitrary fixed cutoff.

Proof of Lemma
6.9
.

Recall the definitions of

,
\widetilde{L_{n}(f_{w})}
,
\widetilde{L_{n}(f_{n})}
, and
\widetilde{L_{n}(h_{p})}

and

(6.78)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{X}_{n}}=F_{n}\left(\mathbb{Q}\big[\widetilde{L_{n}(f_{w_{1}})}\big],\cdots,\mathbb{Q}\big[\widetilde{L_{n}(f_{w_{k_{1}}})}\big],\mathbb{Q}\big[\widetilde{L_{n}(f_{n}^{(1)})}\big],\cdots,\mathbb{Q}\big[\widetilde{L_{n}(f_{n}^{(k_{3})})}\big]\right),\qquad k=k_{1}+k_{3}.

We introduce two independent matrix flows below interpolating between general Girko matrices and Ginibre matrices. Define the following Ornstein – Uhlenbeck matrix flows

(6.79)

\operatorname{d}\!{}{\tilde{A}}_{t}=-\frac{1}{2}{\tilde{A}}_{t}\operatorname{d}\!{}t+\frac{\operatorname{d}\!{}\mathcal{B}^{(1)}_{t}}{\sqrt{n}},\qquad\operatorname{d}\!{}{\tilde{B}}_{t}=-\frac{1}{2}{\tilde{B}}_{t}\operatorname{d}\!{}t+\frac{\operatorname{d}\!{}\mathcal{B}^{(2)}_{t}}{\sqrt{n}},

, where

(Cond1)
–
(Cond3)

\mathcal{B}^{(1)}_{t}
and
\mathcal{B}^{(2)}_{t}
are two independent

(6.80)

\displaystyle H^{w}_{t}=\begin{pmatrix}0&X^{w}_{t}\\
(X^{w}_{t})^{*}&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad H_{t}=\begin{pmatrix}0&X_{t}\\
(X_{t})^{*}&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad X^{w}_{t}={\tilde{A}}_{t}-w{\tilde{B}}_{t},\qquad X_{t}={\tilde{B}}_{t},

G_{t}
, respectively.
With a slight abuse of notation, we often use

to denote the resolvent of either

or
H_{t}
.
We also introduce the following notations:

(6.81)

\displaystyle H_{t}^{(1)}=\begin{pmatrix}0&{\tilde{A}}_{t}\\
({\tilde{A}}_{t})^{*}&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad H_{t}^{(2)}=\begin{pmatrix}0&{\tilde{B}}_{t}\\
({\tilde{B}}_{t})^{*}&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad\operatorname{d}\!{}\mathcal{M}^{(l)}_{t}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\operatorname{d}\!{}\mathcal{B}^{(l)}_{t}\\
\big(\operatorname{d}\!{}\mathcal{B}^{(l)}_{t}\big)^{*}&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad l=1,2.

For notational simplicity, we may often drop the dependence

and write
H^{(l)}_{t}=(h^{(l)}_{ij})_{i,j\in\llbracket 2n\rrbracket}
for

.
Note that the non-zero entries
(h^{(l)}_{aB})
for

and

are independent random variables that depend on time.

Notice that
\mathrm{Tr}G_{t}^{(w)}(\mathrm{i}\eta)
is purely imaginary, because the spectrum of

is symmetrically distributed, from the block structure of

in (

\mathrm{Tr}G_{t}^{(w)}(\mathrm{i}\eta)=\mathrm{i}\operatorname{Im}\mathrm{Tr}G_{t}^{(w)}(\mathrm{i}\eta)
. Then we define the following short-hands for time-dependent versions of
\widetilde{L_{n}(f_{w_{i}})}
and
\widetilde{L_{n}(f^{(i)}_{n})}
:

(6.82)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{i,t}:=\begin{cases}&\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}\int_{\eta_{0}}^{T}\mathrm{Tr}\big[G_{t}^{w_{i}}(\mathrm{i}\eta)-G_{t}(\mathrm{i}\eta)\big]\mathrm{d}\eta,\quad\qquad\qquad 1\leq i\leq k_{1},\\
&\frac{\mathrm{i}}{4\pi}\int\Delta_{z}f^{(i-k_{1})}_{n}(z)\int_{\eta_{0}}^{T}\mathrm{Tr}G^{z}_{t}(\mathrm{i}\eta)\operatorname{d}\!{}\eta\operatorname{d}\!{}^{2}z,\qquad k_{1}+1\leq i\leq k,\end{cases}

where
T=n^{100}
and
\eta_{0}=n^{-1-\epsilon}
with

, and also the time-dependent version of

:

(6.83)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{X}_{n,t}}:=F_{n}\left(\mathcal{L}_{1,t}-\mathbb{E}^{\mathrm{Gin}}\big[\mathcal{L}_{1}\big],\cdots,\mathcal{L}_{k,t}-\mathbb{E}^{\mathrm{Gin}}\big[\mathcal{L}_{k}\big]\right),

where
\mathbb{E}^{\mathrm{Gin}}[\cdot]
denotes the corresponding expectation with respect to Ginibre matrices. In contrast to (
6.78
), we use
\mathcal{L}_{i,t}-\mathbb{E}^{\mathrm{Gin}}\big[\mathcal{L}_{i}\big]
instead of
\mathbb{Q}\big[\mathcal{L}_{i,t}\big]=\mathcal{L}_{i,t}-\mathbb{E}\big[\mathcal{L}_{i,t}\big]
, which simplifies the computations since
\mathbb{E}^{\mathrm{Gin}}[\cdot]
is time-independent. This modification is really minor for comparison, since we also note that for
t=0
,

(6.84)

\displaystyle\Big|\mathbb{E}\big[\widetilde{\mathcal{X}_{n}}\big]-\mathbb{E}\big[\widetilde{\mathcal{X}_{n,0}}\big]\Big|=O((\log n)^{C}n^{-c_{0}/3}),

by a simple Taylor expansion of
F_{n}
using (
6.5
), assuming we have proved the second statement (
6.56
),
i.e.,
for
t=0
,

(6.85)

\displaystyle\Big|\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}_{i,0}]-\mathbb{E}^{\mathrm{Gin}}[\mathcal{L}_{i}]\Big|=O(n^{-c_{0}/3}),\qquad 1\leq i\leq k.

We start with proving the second statement (
6.56
). We first use the matrix flows (
6.79
) and Itô’s formula to derive the dynamics of
G^{(w)}_{t}
, and hence
\mathcal{L}_{i,t}~(1\leq i\leq k)
, as functions of
\big\{h^{(1)}_{aB}\big\}
and
\big\{h^{(2)}_{aB}\big\}
in (
6.81
) for
a\in\llbracket n\rrbracket
and
B\in\llbracket n+1,2n\rrbracket
. More precisely,

(6.86)

\displaystyle\operatorname{d}\!{}\mathcal{L}_{i,t}=

\displaystyle\Theta_{i,t}\operatorname{d}\!{}t+\operatorname{d}\!{}M_{i,t},\qquad 1\leq i\leq k,

with the drift function
\Theta
given by

(6.87)

\displaystyle\Theta_{i,t}=\sum_{a=1}^{n}\sum_{B=n+1}^{2n}\sum_{l=1}^{2}\left(-\frac{1}{2}h^{(l)}_{aB}\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{i,t}}{\partial h^{(l)}_{aB}}-\frac{1}{2}\overline{h^{(l)}_{aB}}\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{i,t}}{\partial\overline{h^{(l)}_{aB}}}+\frac{1}{n}\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{L}_{i,t}}{\partial h^{(l)}_{aB}\partial\overline{h^{(l)}_{aB}}}\right)=:

\displaystyle~\Theta^{(1)}_{i,t}+\Theta^{(2)}_{i,t}+\Theta^{(3)}_{i,t},

and the diffusion term
\operatorname{d}\!{}M_{t}
given by

(6.88)

\displaystyle\operatorname{d}\!{}M_{i,t}=

\displaystyle\sum_{a=1}^{n}\sum_{B=n+1}^{2n}\sum_{l=1}^{2}\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{i,t}}{\partial h^{(l)}_{aB}}\frac{\operatorname{d}\!{}\mathcal{M}^{(l)}_{aB}}{\sqrt{n}}+\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{i,t}}{\partial\overline{h^{(l)}_{aB}}}\frac{\big(\operatorname{d}\!{}\mathcal{M}^{(l)}_{aB}\big)^{*}}{\sqrt{n}}\right).

Taking the expectation of (
6.86
), the diffusion terms in (
6.88
) vanish from the martingale property of Brownian motions, and we have

(6.89)

\displaystyle\frac{\operatorname{d}\!{}\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}_{i,t}]}{\operatorname{d}\!{}t}=\mathbb{E}\big[\Theta^{(1)}_{i,t}\big]+\mathbb{E}\big[\Theta^{(2)}_{i,t}\big]+\mathbb{E}\big[\Theta^{(3)}_{i,t}\big].

We next apply the cumulant expansion formula in Lemma
6.10
to the first two drift terms,
i.e.,
\Theta^{(1)}_{i,t}
and
\Theta^{(2)}_{i,t}
in (
6.87
), with respect to
\big(h^{(l)}_{aB}\big)
and
\big(\overline{h^{(l)}_{aB}}\big)
. We remark that the cumulant expansion formula has been widely used in random matrix theory recently,
e.g.,
47
;
66
;
30
.
From
(Cond2)
-
(Cond3)
, we find that the second order cumulants of
\big(h^{(l)}_{aB}\big)
are invariant over time,
i.e.,

(6.90)

\displaystyle\kappa^{(1,1)}\big(h^{(l)}_{aB}\big)=\frac{1}{n},\qquad\kappa^{(0,2)}\big(h^{(l)}_{aB}\big)=\kappa^{(2,0)}\big(h^{(l)}_{aB}\big)=0,

and the higher-order cumulants are bounded by

(6.91)

\displaystyle|\kappa^{(p,q)}\big(h^{(l)}_{aB}\big)|\lesssim n^{-2-c_{0}},\qquad p+q=3,4,\qquad|\kappa^{(p,q)}\big(h^{(l)}_{aB}\big)|\lesssim n^{-\frac{p+q}{2}},\qquad p+q\geq 5,

uniformly for any
t\geq 0
. We then write out the second order terms in the expansions of
\Theta^{(1)}_{i,t}
and
\Theta^{(2)}_{i,t}
,
i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\Theta^{(1)}_{i,t}+\Theta^{(2)}_{i,t}\big]=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{a=1}^{n}\sum_{B=n+1}^{2n}\sum_{l=1}^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[h^{(l)}_{aB}\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{i,t}}{\partial h^{(l)}_{aB}}+\overline{h^{(l)}_{aB}}\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{i,t}}{\partial\overline{h^{(l)}_{aB}}}\right]

(6.92)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{a=1}^{n}\sum_{B=n+1}^{2n}\sum_{l=1}^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{L}_{i,t}}{\partial h^{(l)}_{aB}\partial\overline{h^{(l)}_{aB}}}\right]+\mbox{higher order terms},

using (
6.90
). Then we observe that the above second order cumulant expansion terms will cancel precisely with the third drift term in (
6.87
).
This cancellation is a consequence of the fact that the second moments of the matrix entries in (
6.79
) do not change with time. Therefore, the remaining terms in (
6.105
) are the higher order cumulant expansion terms in (
6.3
).

To estimate the higher order terms in (
6.3
), we introduce the following differentiation rules obtained from (
6.80
) and the definition of resolvent:

(6.93)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial(G^{w}_{t})_{ij}}{\partial h^{(1)}_{aB}}=-(G^{w}_{t})_{ia}(G^{w}_{t})_{Bj},\qquad\frac{\partial(G^{w}_{t})_{ij}}{\partial\overline{h^{(1)}_{aB}}}=-(G^{w}_{t})_{iB}(G^{w}_{t})_{aj},

(6.94)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial(G^{w}_{t})_{ij}}{\partial h^{(2)}_{aB}}=w(G^{w}_{t})_{ia}(G^{w}_{t})_{Bj},\qquad\frac{\partial(G^{w}_{t})_{ij}}{\partial\overline{h^{(2)}_{aB}}}=\overline{w}(G^{w}_{t})_{iB}(G^{w}_{t})_{aj},

(6.95)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial(G_{t})_{ij}}{\partial h^{(1)}_{aB}}=\frac{\partial(G_{t})_{ij}}{\partial\overline{h^{(1)}_{aB}}}=0,\qquad\qquad\frac{\partial(G_{t})_{ij}}{\partial h^{(2)}_{aB}}=-(G_{t})_{ia}(G_{t})_{Bj},\qquad\qquad\frac{\partial(G_{t})_{ij}}{\partial\overline{h^{(2)}_{aB}}}=-(G_{t})_{iB}(G_{t})_{aj},

for any
i,j\in\llbracket 2n\rrbracket
,
a\in\llbracket n\rrbracket
, and

B\in\llbracket n+1,2n\rrbracket
, together with the following lemma. The proof relies on the above differentiation rules and the local laws in Lemma
6.3
, and is postponed until the end of this section.

Lemma 6.11
.

For any
p,q\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
p+q\geq 1
, we have

(6.96)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T_{0}]}\left|\frac{\partial^{p+q}\mathcal{L}_{i,t}}{\partial(h^{(l)}_{aB})^{p}(\overline{h^{(l)}_{aB}})^{q}}\right|\prec n^{2(p+q)\epsilon},\qquad 1\leq i\leq k,\qquad l=1,2,\qquad T_{0}=100\log n.

Applying Lemma
6.10
, we stop the cumulant expansions in (
6.3
) at the fourth order,
i.e.,

\displaystyle\frac{\operatorname{d}\!{}\mathbb{E}\big[\mathcal{L}_{i,t}\big]}{\operatorname{d}\!{}t}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{a=1}^{n}\sum_{B=n+1}^{2n}\sum_{l=1}^{2}\left(\sum_{p+q+1=3}^{4}\frac{\kappa^{(p+1,q)}(h^{(l)}_{aB})}{p!q!}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\partial^{p+q+1}\mathcal{L}_{i,t}}{\partial(h^{(l)}_{aB})^{p+1}\partial(\overline{h^{(l)}_{aB}})^{q}}\right]\right)

(6.97)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{a=1}^{n}\sum_{B=n+1}^{2n}\sum_{l=1}^{2}\left(\sum_{p+q+1=3}^{4}\frac{\kappa^{(p+1,q)}(\overline{h^{(l)}_{aB}})}{p!q!}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\partial^{p+q+1}\mathcal{L}_{i,t}}{\partial(\overline{h^{(l)}_{aB}})^{p+1}\partial(h^{(l)}_{aB})^{q}}\right]\right)+R_{5},

with the truncating error
R_{5}
given as in (
6.77
) and bounded by

(6.98)

\displaystyle|R_{5}|=O_{\prec}(n^{-1/2+c\epsilon}),

for some constant
c>0
, using the fifth cumulant bound (
6.91
), the differentiation rules (
6.93
)-(
6.95
), and the local law estimates in (
6.22
). Such truncation argument is standard and frequently used in previous works, so we postpone the details till the end of this section. Moreover, using the cumulant bounds in (
6.91
) and Lemma
6.11
, the third and fourth order terms with

above are bounded by

. Therefore, we obtain

(6.99)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\operatorname{d}\!{}\mathbb{E}\big[\mathcal{L}_{i,t}\big]}{\operatorname{d}\!{}t}\right|=O(n^{-c_{0}/2}),

using that

is chosen sufficiently small. We remark that the above error bounds holds uniformly for any

.

Integrating (
6.99
) over
t\in[0,T_{0}]
with

, we obtain

(6.100)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T_{0}]}\Big|\mathbb{E}\big[\mathcal{L}_{i,t}\big]-\mathbb{E}\big[\mathcal{L}_{i,T_{0}}\big]\Big|=O(n^{-c_{0}/3}).

Note that
H^{(w)}_{t}
is defined as in (
6.80
) with the time dependent matrix

under the evolution (
6.79
). For any fixed

,

in distribution equals to

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}^{(w)}_{t}:=\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{t}{2}}X^{(w)}+\sqrt{1-\mathrm{e}^{-t}}{X^{\mathrm{Gin}}},

with
\mathcal{X}^{(w)}_{0}=X^{(w)}
defined in (
6.32
) and
\mathcal{X}^{(w)}_{\infty}={X^{\mathrm{Gin}}}
being the Ginibre counterpart which is independent of

. We use

to denote the corresponding resolvents defined as in (

) and (
6.34
) and

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{G}^{(w)}_{T_{0}}(\mathrm{i}\eta)-\mathcal{G}^{(w)}_{\infty}(\mathrm{i}\eta)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\|\mathcal{G}^{(w)}_{T_{0}}(\mathrm{i}\eta)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|\mathcal{G}^{(w)}_{\infty}(\mathrm{i}\eta)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|\mathcal{X}^{(w)}_{T_{0}}-\mathcal{X}^{(w)}_{\infty}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\prec n^{-50},\qquad\eta\geq\eta_{0},

(6.103)

\displaystyle\Big|\mathbb{E}\big[\mathcal{L}_{i,T_{0}}\big]-\mathbb{E}^{\mathrm{Gin}}\big[\mathcal{L}_{i}\big]\Big|=O(n^{-10}).

Combining (

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T_{0}]}\Big|\mathbb{E}\big[\mathcal{L}_{i,t}\big]-\mathbb{E}^{\mathrm{Gin}}\big[\mathcal{L}_{i}\big]\Big|=O(n^{-c_{0}/3}).

Recall the definitions of
\Theta^{(1)}_{j,t}

from (

\mathrm{Var}(L_{n}(f_{1}+\dots+f_{m}))
. By construction,
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}
have disjoint compact support, and
f_{j}

has a single logarithmic singularity
at
p_{j}
with weight
c_{j}
.

7.3.2.
Asymptotic de-correlation of singularities.

Since
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
have disjoint and compact supports, we have

\rho=\mathrm{dist}(\mathrm{supp}(f_{1}),\mathrm{supp}(f_{2}))>0
.
Moreover, the off-diagonal exponential decay of the kernel (
2.22
) for the spherical ensemble gets substituted by

|K_{n}(p,q)|\leq Cn\mathrm{e}^{-c\sqrt{n}d(p,q)}
, for some constants
C,c>0
and all
p,q\in S
,
see for instance
(
14
, right after Th. 2.4)
. By proceeding as in Lemma

3.2
, this gives

(7.5)

|\mathrm{Cov}(L_{n}(f_{1}),L_{n}(f_{2}))|\leq C^{2}n^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-2c\rho\sqrt{n}}\Bigl(\int|f_{1}|\mathrm{d}\sigma\Bigr)\Bigl(\int|f_{2}|\mathrm{d}\sigma\Bigr).

The integrals above are finite because
\log\left|\cdot\right|
is integrable near
0

so that the covariance vanishes as
n\to\infty
. Then,

(7.6)

\mathrm{Var}(L_{n}(f_{1}+\cdots+f_{m}))\underset{n\to\infty}{\sim}\mathrm{Var}(L_{n}(f_{1}))+\cdots+\mathrm{Var}(L_{n}(f_{m}))

and the asymptotic variance problem is therefore further reduced to the single singularity case
m=1
.

7.3.3.
Reduction to variance of truncation.

Let
f:S\setminus\{p\}\to\mathbb{R}
be
one of the
f_{j}
so
that its support is contained in the coordinate
neighborhood

\varphi_{j}(\mathbb{D})
and we may
rescale it so that
c_{j}=1
. The variance is

(7.7)

\mathrm{Var}(L_{n}(f))=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D}}(\chi(z)\log|z|-\chi(w)\log|w|)^{2}|K_{n}(z,w)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\sigma(z)\mathrm{d}\sigma(w).

To proceed, we use the truncation decomposition

(7.8)

\log|z|=\ell_{n}(z)+r_{n}(z)\quad\text{where}\quad\ell_{n}(z)=\max\big(\log|z|,-\tfrac{1}{2}\log(n)\big)\quad\text{and}\quad r_{n}(z)=\log|z|-\ell_{n}(z).

We have
\ell_{n}(z)=\log|z|
if
|z|\geq 1/\sqrt{n}
while

\ell_{n}(z)=-\frac{1}{2}\log n
if
|z|\leq 1/\sqrt{n}
,
which tells us that
r_{n}(z)=0
if
|z|\geq 1/\sqrt{n}
.
For the variance associated to
r_{n}
we have

(7.9)

\int_{\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D}}(\chi(z)r_{n}(z)-\chi(w)r_{n}(w))^{2}|K_{n}(z,w)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\sigma(z)\mathrm{d}\sigma(w)\leq 2\int_{\mathbb{D}}(\chi(z)r_{n}(z))^{2}K_{n}(z,z)\mathrm{d}\sigma(z),

where in the last term we have used that
K_{n}
is a projection and we have discarded
an integral of a negative term. Now, we use that
K_{n}(z,z)\leq Cn
for some
C>0

and that
\sigma
has a bounded density on any fixed compact set of
\mathbb{D}
to obtain

(7.10)

\int_{\mathbb{D}}(\chi(z)r_{n}(z))^{2}K_{n}(z,z)\mathrm{d}\sigma(z)\leq\widetilde{C}n\int_{|z|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}}|r_{n}(z)|^{2}\mathrm{d}^{2}z=2\pi\widetilde{C}n\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}}(\log(\sqrt{n}r))^{2}r\mathrm{d}r=2\pi\widetilde{C}\int_{0}^{1}s(\log s)^{2}\mathrm{d}s<\infty.

Therefore
\sup_{n\geq 1}\mathrm{Var}(L_{n}(\chi r_{n}))<\infty
. It follows that if

\mathrm{Var}(L_{n}(\chi\ell_{n}))\to+\infty
as
n\to\infty
then

(7.11)

\mathrm{Var}(L_{n}(f))\underset{n\to\infty}{\sim}\mathrm{Var}(L_{n}(\chi\ell_{n})).

This reduces the problem to the computation of the asymptotic variance for a sequence of bounded
functions.

7.3.4.
Asymptotic variance of the sequence of truncated functions.

Recalling that

(7.12)

\mathrm{Var}(L_{n}(f))=\frac{1}{2}\int\Big(\int(f(z)-f(w))^{2}|K_{n}(z,w)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\sigma(z)\Big)\mathrm{d}\sigma(w),

we may follow the ideas of

(
12
, Theorem 5.8)

to exploit the asymptotics

(7.13)

\frac{1}{n^{2}}\Big|K_{n}\Big(z+\frac{v_{1}}{\sqrt{n}},z+\frac{v_{2}}{\sqrt{n}}\Big)\Big|^{2}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}C_{z}^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-\pi C_{z}\sigma_{z}|v_{1}-v_{2}|^{2}}\quad\text{with}\quad C_{z}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}K_{n}(z,z)

and where
\sigma_{z}
is the density at

z
of
\sigma
with respect to Lebesgue measure
(
15
, Theorem 1.1)
.
We obtain

(7.14)

\mathrm{Var}(L_{n}(f))\underset{n\to\infty}{\sim}\frac{1}{4}\int\Big(\frac{1}{2\pi\sigma_{w}}\|\nabla f_{w}\|^{2}\Big)\mathrm{d}\sigma(w)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int\|\nabla f_{w}\|^{2}\mathrm{d}^{2}w

holds even for a function
f

that depends nicely enough on
n
.
So, if this asymptotics holds for
f=\chi\ell_{n}
despite its dependency on
n
,
then
\mathrm{Var}(L_{n}(\chi\ell_{n}))
would behave like

(7.15)

\frac{1}{4\pi}\int\|\nabla(\chi\ell_{n})(w)\|^{2}\mathrm{d}^{2}w=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{|w|\geq\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla(\chi\log|\cdot|)(w)\|^{2}\mathrm{d}^{2}w+\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}}^{1/2}\frac{1}{r^{2}}(2\pi r\mathrm{d}r)=O(1)+\frac{1}{4}\log(n).
