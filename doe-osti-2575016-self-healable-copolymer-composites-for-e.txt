3. Executive Summary:

 In this project, we designed, synthesized and fabricated using X-winder technologies pre-
 commercialized novel, self-healingable commodity copolymer fiber-reinforced
 composites to extend the H2 hose service life beyond the current target of 1000 fills.
 These studies demonstrated that these composites are able to withstand over 25,000
 damage-repair cycles, which was the main objective of the proposed project, in the
 temperature range of -40 to +80 °C under variable pressures. When micro-cracks are
 formed after about 1,000 cycles/fueling per hose in the inner composite layer, these
 micro-cracks self-heal, thus extending the lifetime of a prototype inner layer of the hose.
 These composites were tested by exposure to H2 fuel and demonstrated the ability to
 recover from mechanical damage. Thermomechanical testing combined with analysis of
 the stress and strain fields across the cross-section of the inner layer composite hoses
 also identified the ring-like stresses on the edge of inner layers arising from changes of
 the fiber winding angle directions from +45° to -45°. The developed novel concept that
 self-healing commodity copolymers can be used in the development of prototype
 composites for the extended service of H2 dispensing hoses will have major implications
 for other energy-related technologies, where the extended service life in harsh
 environments is expected. In this project, we optimized, validated, and demonstrated
 novel copolymer fiber-reinforced composites for H2 dispensing hose applications, which
 can be utilized in future manufacturing using commodity materials. The estimated cost of
 materials (exluding labor) is in the range of < $1.0/ft.

 5. Background:

Phase 1 (Budget Period 1) of this project focused on designing of various self-healing polymeric
system that retained properties after multiple damage-repair cycles. The initial target values of
1000 damage-repair cycles under laboratory conditions were achieved.

Phase 2 (Budget Period 2) focuses on development of composites composed of the copolymers
developed during Phase 1 and commodity polypropylene (Innegra TM) fiber mats. These
materials were tested to achieve desirable mechanical properties for hose applications and self-
healing properties. The specific objective was to prove that self-healing composites can be
achieved using commodity materials. Performance target of 10,000 damage-repair cycles under
laboratory testing conditions was achieved during this budget period.

Phase 3 (Budget Period 3) focuses on achieving an optimal performance and extended damage-
repair cycles. The objective of this budget period is to demonstrate the ability of the self-healable
copolymer inner layer composite to achieve a minimum of 25,000 damage-repair cycles and to
test the durability of fiber- copolymer matrix interfacial properties under pressure (35-100-35
MPa) and temperature cycles (-40°C to 80°C).

 6. Project Objectives

The main objective of this project is to develop, demonstrate and pre-commercialize a novel,
self-healable commodity copolymer fiber-reinforced composites to extend the hose service life
beyond the current target of 1000 fills a d 25,000 damage-self-repair cycles. The proposed
technology is based on the novel concept in self-healing commodity copolymers that will be used
in the development and building of prototype composites for the extended service of H2
dispensing hoses. This project will focus on the design, fabrication, material optimization,
validation and demonstration of the novel composites for dispensing hose applications. The
project will be performed in three phases. The Phase 1 objective is to copolymerize acrylic based
copolymers with self-healing properties, specifically for lower temperature self-healing and crack
recovery. This phase will also focus on molecular dynamic (MD) simulations to accelerate and
guide polymer development efforts. The Phase 2 objective is to integrate the copolymers with
commodity polypropylene (InnegraTM) fibers and mats to achieve optimal properties to extend
the hose service life beyond the current target of 1000 fills. Considering that the current cost of
acrylic polymers is in the $1.5-2.5/kg range, making these materials self-healable does not add
additional expenditures, but the add-on value will be the ability of self-healing. An ultimate
objective is to achieve and maintain properties under pressure (35-100-35 MPa) and
temperature cycles (-40°C to 85°C). The Phase 3 objective is to conduct fatigue testing and
analysis in order to optimize composite properties to closely match hose operating conditions
targeted for over 1000 fills.

 Self-healable copolymers, including poly(methyl methacrylate/n-butyl acrylate) (p(MMA/nBA)) and
poly(2,2,2 trifluoroethyl methacrylate/n-butyl acrylate) (p(TFEMA/nBA)) with various compositions were
synthesized by statistical, atom transfer radial polymerization (ATRP), and colloidal polymerization and
characterized using Fourier transform Infrared (FTIR), Raman, nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR), and
molecular dynamic simulations (MD) to elucidate the governing mechanism of self-healing. Optical
images of self-healable p(MMA/nBA) and p(TFEMA/nBA) copolymers are shown in Figure 1. MD
simulations revealed that inter-digitated copolymer chain conformations are reflected in the highest
cohesive energy density (CEDvdW) values for statistical/alternating copolymers [45/55, 50/50
p(MMA/nBA) and p(TFEMA/nBA)] which facilitate highest self-healing efficiency. Outside this
compositional range, self-healing is not achieved. These data agreed with experimental findings.

45/55 p(TFEMA/nBA) copolymer film within 60 min (B) under ambient conditions.

 Inner layer composite hoses with winding angles of 45, 60 and 75 ° exhibit significantly greater
modulus compared to neat copolymer films due to high strength resulting for the incorporation of
InnegraTM fibers and the composite geometry. Friction of bound fibers between different layers strengthen
the material, especially when the winding angle of 45 ° was employed. Interestingly, the composite with
winding angle of 90 ° displays a modulus lower than the copolymer film. This is because all fiber loops
are parallel to each other, and there is low friction between different layers. Upon stretching, the interface
failure between the polymer matrix and fibers results in delamination of the composite. It should be noted
that slippage of the specimen inside clamps is inevitable during tensile measurements, even though the
maximum clamping force has been used. This is attributed to the decreased thickness of specimens with
increased elongation.

 An accelerated shelf-life test for inner layer composite hoses was conducted in extremely harsh
environments to accelerate the rate of deterioration of the product and shorten the test period. The 3-
layer composite hoses with winding angle of 45 ° were kept in liquid N2 (- 196 °C) and heating oven (85
°C) alternatively, each for 2 h, with a total time of 8 h per day. This test lasted 10 days, followed by tensile
tests. Mechanical properties including elastic modulus, strain at break and ultimate strength of inner layer
composite hoses after the accelerated shelf-life test recover 100% of their original values (Figure 5, Table
3). Furthermore, the p(MMA/nBA) copolymer in intact inner layer composite hoses (Figure 6A) and after
the accelerated shelf-life tests (Figure 6B) characterized by 1H NMR spectroscopy show no change of
chemical composition. In a nutshell, both chemical integrity of the copolymer binder and mechanical
properties of inner layer composite hoses are maintained after shelf-life tests.

 Temperature cross-sensitivity test was carried out to determine effects of temperature on self-healing
efficiency for the inner layer composite hoses. Inner layer composite hoses were dip coated with a layer
of p(MMA/nBA) copolymer. A mechanical scratch was generated on the surface of the inner layer
composite hose using a razor blade which is attached to a micro-scratcher instrument, at the normal force
of 250 mN. The inner layer composite hose was kept in liquid N2 (- 196°C), ambient environment (20°C),
and heating oven (85°C), each for 2 h, sequentially (Figure 7). After freezing in liquid N2 for 2 h, the
scratch still existed on the hose surface because copolymer chain mobility decreased drastically and
prevented self-healing. Furthermore, cracks were generated on the hose surface because the copolymer
becomes brittle, and its volume decreases due to the dramatic temperature drop (Figure 7B). After
warming up at 21 °C for 2 h, the mechanical scratch and cracks partially self-healed as observed by the
decrease in cut/cracks depth. After heating at 85 °C for 2 h, the scratch and all cracks completely self-
healed (Figure7D). This is because copolymer chains have high mobility at elevated temperatures which
promotes self-healing. Extremely harsh experimental conditions (- 196 °C) were selected to show that
the self-healing capability of inner layer composite hoses is not jeopardized by extremely low
temperatures. The inner layer composite hoses can completely self-heal as temperature increases. Such
material behaviors could be beneficial in practical applications as the copolymer is capable of self-healing
at room temperature, and high temperature further promotes self-healing.

Figure 7. Optical images of inner layer composite hoses after mechanical damage (A); after exposure at
-196 °C for 2h (B); followed by exposure at 21 °C for 2h (C); after self-healing at 85 °C for 2hours (D).

 Thermomechanical testing and analysis of the stress and strain fields across the cross-section
of the composite hose were performed using COMSOL Multiphysics® software. There are two main
components of simulations that were explored: (1) development of micromechanical model that enables
modeling of a single layer of the composite hose and (2) development of combined microanalysis for
macroanalysis predictions that result in material properties under different loading conditions applied to
the composite hose to evaluate responses.Using the material properties generated from the
microanalysis, the following loading conditions were applied to the composite hose to evaluate
response to the applied loads:

 1. An internal pressure of 35 and 100 MPa to analyze the stress distribution of the composite hose.
 2. A load of bending using a 30N force along with the load of internal pressure of 35 and 100 MPa
 to analyze the stress distribution of the composite hose with one fixed and both fixed ends.

For this purpose, an internal pressure load of 35 and 100 MPa was applied to the inner part of each hose
as shown in Figure 8 a and b. Fixed constraints were used at both ends of the model. Once the load and
boundary conditions were selected, a meshing sequence was employed to provide accurate results
across the model. For the composite hose model in these studies, the Mapped node was used to create
a structured quadrilateral meshing for the boundaries of the 3D model.

 25,000 damage-repair cycle tests were performed on 47/53 p(MMA/nBA) copolymer films with Mn of
50 kDa. Figure 1 illustrates a schematic diagram of the damage-repair cycle test. Each time, one
thousand cuts were conducted using a razor blade on the middle region (highlighted in yellow,
dimensions: 10 × 3 mm). The cut width and depth are 3-15 and 5-80 µm, respectively. The damage was
applied 3 times a day at intervals of 4 h, and specimens were allowed to heal for 16 h. Optical images of
damaged and self-healed copolymer films were collected. One representative set of magnified views of
p(MMA/nBA)-50 kDa, with a selected number of cuts, is also shown in Figure 17. Self-healing of
mechanical damage can be observed.
 After completing 25,000 damage-repair cycles, specimens were allowed to self-heal for 48 h, followed
by tensile measurements. The gauge length was 10 mm for all specimens, and the speed of the
crosshead was set as 60 mm/min. Specimens were tested in triplicate. The mechanical properties of
intact and self-healed specimens are summarized in Table 7, indicating that the strain at break (εbreak)
and elastic modulus (E) for all copolymer specimens after 25,000 damage-repair cycles can recover ≥
85% of their original values. Stress-strain curves are shown in Figure 18.

8. Significance and Conclusions

 - Self-healable copolymers, including p(MMA/nBA) and p(TFEMA/nBA) with various
 compositions, have been synthesized by statistical, ATRP, and colloidal polymerization and
 characterized.
 - MD simulations revealed that inter-digitated copolymer chain conformations are reflected in
 the highest CEDvdW values for statistical/alternating copolymers [45/55, 50/50 p(MMA/nBA)
 and p(TFEMA/nBA)], which facilitate the highest self-healing efficiency. Outside this
 compositional range, self-healing is not achieved. These data agree with experimental
 findings.
 - In collaboration with SRNL, initial permeability experiments were conducted on p(MMA/nBA)
 and p(TFEMA/nBA) and showed similar H2 gas permeability to well-known elastomers. Self-
 healing performance was observed by membrane burst-repair cycle tests, an up-and-coming
 analytical tool for practical assessment of self-healable copolymers utilized in H2 dispense
 hose applications. The preliminary data show that this laboratory test may be the closest lab
 test to mimic the performance of H2 dispense hoses in real-life refueling station environments.
 - Composite hoses with different winding angles, layer numbers and diameters have been
 fabricated using p(MMA/nBA) copolymer and polypropylene (InnegraTM) fibers. Winding
 parameters and procedures have been optimized for improved quality of inner-layer
 composite hoses.
 - Fabrication of inner-layer composite hoses presents advanced repeatability and
 repetitiveness; the chemical integrity of the copolymer binder and mechanical properties were
 maintained after the accelerated shelf-life test conducted in an extremely harsh environment.
 Temperature cross-sensitivity measurement manifests that the self-healing process can be
 paused, achieved, and accelerated at extremely low, ambient, and high temperatures.
 - Macroanalysis predictions were developed using COMSOL Multiphysics® software, which
 results in material properties under different loading conditions applied to the composite
 hose to evaluate responses. A comparison of stress distribution under internal pressure
 loads of 35 MPa and 100 MPa was performed, showing that stresses were higher in the
 innermost layers.
 - Heat transfer mechanisms were determined by applying a constant load of internal pressure
 of 35 MPa and a temperature of -40 °C, and a variable external load of -40 °C, 0 °C, 25 °C,
 and 80 °C. They showed that heat transfers from outer layers to inner layers of the H2 carrying
 hose as the ambient temperature is increased from 0 to 80 °C.
 - Stress distribution was also performed to simulate the actual bending cycle during H2 fuel
 refueling by applying an internal pressure load and bending load on the inner layer
 composite hose model. A model of an inner-layer composite hose with an outer aluminum
 layer to simulate a commercial hose was developed to evaluate the stress distribution upon
 application of a pressure load of 35 MPa and 100 MPa. This analysis illustrated that the
 outer layers surrounding the inner layer composite hose material in a commercial hose will
 lower the stresses on the inner layers.
 - The goal of 25,000 damage-repair cycles on self-healable copolymer films and inner layer
 composite hose was achieved. Recovery of mechanical properties and chemical/physical
 integrity was illustrated experimentally.
 - Manuscripts are being prepared for publication.
