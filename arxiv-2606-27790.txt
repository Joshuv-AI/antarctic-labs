The derived moduli of perverse sheaves
Peter J. Haine, Mauro PORTA, Jean-Baptiste Teyssier

Abstract
. We construct higher derived Artin stacks parametrizing constructible sheaves on complex algebraic varieties and compact real analytic varieties. Furthermore, we show that every perversity function gives rise to an open substack of perverse sheaves, which is a 1 1 -Artin stack locally of finite presentation that generalizes usual character stacks. As a sample application of the derived structure, we construct new examples of cohomological Hall algebras associated to punctured Riemann surfaces.

1.
Introduction

This paper is concerned with the construction, in a high level of generality, of a derived Artin stack parametrizing perverse sheaves on a fixed stratified space satisfying some finiteness conditions.
The main results of this paper appeared first in a lesser generality in the first versions of
[
PT23
,
HPT26
]
.
We subsequently decided to remove this material from the later versions of said papers and collect it here, in order to make them accessible to a wider community, without a heavy background in stratified homotopy theory.

Historical context

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group.
The
character variety of
\Gamma
is a scheme
\mathbb{X}(\Gamma)
parametrizing finite dimensional semi-simple representations of
\Gamma
.
Character varieties have a rich history; perhaps the first systematic treatment is
[
LM85
]
, although predated by work of Weil
[
Wei60
,
Wei62
]
, Artin
[
Art69
]
and Procesi
[
Pro74
]
(see
[
Sik12
]
for a more recent treatment).
They are ubiquitous in various areas of mathematics and physics, such as low-dimensional topology
[
Bé20
,
GJS23
]
, gauge theory
[
AB83
,
Hit87
]
, mirror symmetry
[
HT03
]
, nonabelian Hodge theory
[
Sim93
,
Sim94a
,
Sim94b
]
and the geometric Langlands program
[
BZN18
]
.
As a result, their topology has been deeply investigated, especially in the case of surface groups (i.e., fundamental groups of complex algebraic curves).
They provide important examples of symplectic and Poisson varieties
[
Gol84
,
GM88
]
, and their quantization has equally been studied in detail
[
BZBJ18
,
GJS25
]
.
The papers
[
HLRV11
,
HLRV13
]
marked a landmark achievement in understanding their homology.

Character varieties can be seen as
good moduli spaces
of more fundamental
character stacks
.
These are Artin stacks parametrizing representations of
\Gamma
together with their automorphisms; their singularities are milder than those of the character varieties.
Moreover, when
\Gamma=\pi_{1}(X,x)
for a (sufficiently nice) topological space
X
, by the monodromy correspondence, character varieties can equally be described as the moduli of local systems on
X
.
In this case the
higher homotopy groups
provide additional structure on the character variety: that of a
derived scheme
.
This additional structure is a fundamental tool, heavily exploited in the geometric Langlands program; it also leads to a better understanding of the singularities of the character variety, and certain phenomena (e.g., their symplectic nature) becomes more transparent working simultaneously at the
stacky and derived
level
[
PTVV13
]
.
This is easy to see:

Example 1
.

Since the
2
-sphere
S^{2}
is simply connected, the connected component of the character
stack
of
\pi_{1}(S^{2})=\ast
corresponding to representations of dimension
n
coincides with
\mathrm{BGL}_{n}
(the character variety is in this case reduced to a single point).
On the other hand, the tangent complex of derived stack
\mathbf{LC}_{n}(S^{2})
at the trivial local system of rank
n
is given by
\mathrm{H}^{\ast}_{\mathrm{sing}}(S^{2};\mathbb{Z})^{\oplus n}[1]
, see
[
TV08
, Proposition 2.2.6.6]
.

At the same time, local systems admit natural generalizations: constructible sheaves and perverse sheaves.
Combining ideas of stratified homotopy theory (that have been developed by the authors in
[
HPT26
]
and recalled in §
2
in a way that they can be used as a black box) with the theory of moduli of flat objects of a category
[
TV07
,
TV16
,
DPS22
]
, in this paper, we construct both constructible and perverse generalizations of character stacks.
We are only concerned with generalizing character
stacks
; good moduli spaces for the perverse character stack has been obtained recently in
[
Lam25
]
.
In the even more recent work
[
CL26
]
, it is shown that the perverse character stack carries (under additional niceness hypotheses) a canonical shifted Poisson structure.
Both of these works should be understood as
continuations and completions
of the current paper.

Moduli of perverse sheaves

In
[
GMV96
]
, the authors attached to each complex projective variety
X
equipped with a Whitney stratification
P
a finite quiver
Q(X,P)

with relations
, whose representations coincide with perverse sheaves on
(X,P)
, with respect to the middle perversity.
Out of this, it is easy to construct an Artin stack
\mathbf{Rep}_{Q(X,P)}
of representations of
Q(X,P)
, which can be interpreted as a moduli of perverse sheaves.
Before going any further, let us explain the two main limitations of this approach:

Poor functoriality

The quiver
Q(X,P)
depends on several choices (and most notably a projective embedding of
X
chosen as a function of the stratification
P
).
This makes it hard to control how
\mathbf{Rep}_{Q(X,P)}
depends on the stratified
(X,P)
itself.
For instance, it is not completely clear from this perspective how to let
P
vary.
Instead, our method allows us to construct a moduli stack of sheaves that are perverse with respect to
some
stratification, see
4
below.
In a similar spirit, in
[
NS96
,
Nit99
]
the authors deliberately chose an alternative presentation of perverse sheaves because the quiver of
[
GMV96
]
did not interact well with the Riemann–Hilbert correspondence.

Absence of a derived structure

Recall from
[
Por25
, §I.5.5]
(or
[
DPS22
, §II.2.6]
) that a standard way to produce a derived enhancement of the moduli of objects of an
abelian
category
\mathcal{A}
is to exhibit
\mathcal{A}
as the heart of a sufficiently nice
t
-structure
\tau
on a stable
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}

of finite type
.
In this case, the finitness allows us to form Toën and Vaquié’s moduli of object construction
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}
.
The
t
-structure allows us to cut out an open substack
\mathbf{Coh}_{\mathsf{ps}}(\mathcal{C},\tau)
of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}
, which is the desired enhancement of the moduli of objects of
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit}
.
Notice that the derived enhancement
depends
on the choice of
(\mathcal{C},\tau)
.

In the case at hand, the natural choice would be the derived category of representations of
Q(X,P)
, but since this quiver has relations, its derived category is not generally of finite type in the sense of
[
TV07
]
:

Example 2
.

Let
X=\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
be the complex projective plane, stratified in a copy of
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}
.
It is shown in
[
MV86
, Example 6.3]
that the category of perverse sheaves in this case is equivalent to the category of representations of the quiver

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bullet }
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bullet\ ,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces b}

subject to the relations
ab=ba=0
.
In other words, this category of perverse sheaves is the category of representations of the preprojective algebra of the quiver
A_{2}
, which has infinite homological dimension – see e.g.,
[
BBK02
, Remark 4.11]
.

For this special example, it is easy to find a good replacement, namely using the
2
-Calabi-Yau completion (a.k.a. Ginzburg dg-algebra) of
A_{2}

[
Kel11
,
BCS24
]
) instead of its preprojective algebra.
For more complicated stratified spaces, we would not know how to proceed in this direction.
Even worse,
1
shows that even when
Q(X,P)
is homologically of finite type, the resulting derived structure does not match the naturally expected one.

Instead, our method provides a canonical derived structure, as a concrete incarnation of the geometry of the stratification: the tangent complex of our derived stack is highly sensitive to the cohomology of the
strata and the links
of
(X,P)
.
In this approach, the homological finitess property of
Q(X,P)
is replaced by the finiteness theorems of the exit-path
\infty
-categories proved in
[
HPT26
]
.
The construction of the perverse cohomological Hall algebra (see
4.3.1
) is a first piece of evidence that the derived structure we construct here is very robust.
Another piece of evidence has been recently obtained by Christ–Lampetti
[
CL26
]
, where they show that the derived stack of perverse sheaves constructed here has a canonical shifted Poisson structure.

Our approach has also some other
minor-but-pleasant
features: dropping the projectivity and Whitney assumptions on
(X,P)
, and the restriction to middle perversity.

Moduli of constructible complexes

Central to our approach is the construction of a bigger, highly non-separated higher Artin stack in the sense of Simpson
[
Sim96
]
.
This larger derived stack, denoted
\mathbf{Cons}_{P}(X)
, parametrizes
constructible complexes
and is obtained as a
moduli of objects
in the sense of Toën–Vaquié
[
TV07
]
.
A subtle point that deserves to be mentioned here is that
\mathbf{Cons}_{P}(X)
is realized as the moduli of objects of the
large
stable
\infty
-category
\mathrm{Cons}_{P}(X;\mathrm{Mod}_{k})
of
unbounded constructible complexes with infinite dimensional stalks
.
The key insight is that the finiteness property of the exit-path category
\Pi_{\infty}(X,P)
(proven in a large variety of examples in
[
HPT26
]
) allows to show that
\mathrm{Cons}_{P}(X;\mathrm{Mod}_{k})
, which a priori is very large, is in fact of finite type in the sense of
[
TV07
]
.
This allows us to bootstrap off the main theorem of
loc. cit.

One of the fundamental points is that the stalk functors are corepresentable in
\mathrm{Cons}_{P}(X;\mathrm{Mod}_{k})
by objects that are compact, but with infinite dimensional stalks (see
2.3.8
and
[
PT23
, Warning 6.5.2]
).
This implies that the moduli of objects of
\mathrm{Cons}_{P}(X;\mathrm{Mod}_{k})
(whose construction is recalled in
4.1.5
) actually parametrizes
P
-constructible sheaves with
perfect
stalks.

As several papers already demonstrated
[
AZ01
,
TV16
,
BLM+21
,
DPS22
]
, a sufficiently well-behaved
t
-structure
\tau
allows one to cut out a better behaved open
1
-Artin substack inside of
\mathbf{Cons}_{P}(X)
, parametrizing families of
\tau
-flat objects.
Indeed, the bulk of this paper is devoted to check that the perverse
t
-structure (of any perversity) is well-behaved in this sense.

We can now state our main results.
The first main theorem below is a consequence of

and the finiteness results of

]
, recalled in the main text as Theorems

2.4.4
.
The statements involve

(in the sense of

); the key example of such is a locally finite stratification a of real analytic manifold by subanalytic subsets.
We also use the term
algebraic stratified space
to mean a stratification of the
\mathbb{R}
-points of an
\mathbb{R}
-variety by Zariski locally closed subsets (see

).

(Fixed stratification, see

.

Let

The
\infty

is compactly generated with full subcategory of compact objects

[

Let

2.
Recollections on stratified homotopy theory

In this section, we collect some general material on (hyper)constructible (hyper)sheaves and exodromic stratifications.
These results are not original: most of them were obtained in
[
Lur17
, Appendix A]
or in
[
PT23
,
HPT23
,
HPT26
]
.
The reader already acquainted with these ideas is invited to skip this section.

2.1.
Stratified topological spaces

Let
P
be a poset.
We equip the underlying set of
P
with the topology whose open subsets are the upward-closed subsets
Q\subset P
.
A
stratified space
is a topological space
X
equipped with a continuous map to a poset.
For a subset
S\subset P
, we set
X_{S}\coloneqq S\times_{P}X
and we let
X_{S}\to S
be the induced stratification; we denote by
i_{S}\colon X_{S}\hookrightarrow X
the inclusion.
For
a\in P
, the subset
X_{a}
is the
stratum of
(X,P)
over
a
.
The collection of stratified spaces organize into a category in an obvious manner.

Remark 2.1.1
.

We abuse notation by denoting a stratification of
X
by
P
as
(X,P)
instead of
X\to P
and refer to
(X,P)
as a stratified space.

Example 2.1.2
.

Let
X
be a topological space equipped with a partition
S=\{X_{p}\}_{p\in P}
into disjoint locally closed subspaces.
We define a partial order on
P
by
p\leqslant q
if and only if
X_{p}\cap\overline{X_{q}}\neq\emptyset
.
This gives rise to a continuous map
X\to P
, which is a stratification in the above sense.

Example 2.1.3
.

Let
f\colon Y\to Q
be a stratified space.
Write
C(Y)\coloneqq\ast\sqcup\left(Y\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}\right)
.
The set
C(Y)
is endowed with the topology whose open subsets are the subsets
U\subset C(Y)
such that
U\cap\left(Y\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}\right)
is open and if
\ast\in U
, then

C_{\varepsilon}(Y)\coloneqq\{\ast\}\sqcup\left(Y\times(0,\varepsilon)\right)\subset U

for some
\varepsilon>0
.
Let
Q^{\lhd}
be the poset obtained from
Q
by adding a smallest element
-\infty
.
We define a continuous map
g\colon C(Y)\to Q^{\lhd}
by sending
\ast
to
-\infty
and
(y,t)\in Y\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}
to
f(y)
.
We refer to
(C(Y),Q^{\lhd})
as the
cone
of
(Y,Q)
.

The following should be thought as a (purely topological) regularity condition on a stratified space (akin to the notion of a Whitney stratification).

Definition 2.1.4
.

Let
(X,P)
be a stratified space.
We say that
(X,P)
is
conically stratified
if for every point
x\in X
lying over
a\in P
, there exists a topological space
Z
, a stratified space
(Y,P_{>a})
and a map of stratified spaces
(Z\times C(Y),P_{\geqslant a})\to(X,P)
inducing an homeomorphism between
Z\times C(Y)
and an open neighbourhood of
x
in
X
.

Definition 2.1.5
.

Let
X
be a topological space.
We say that a stratification
X\to P
is
locally finite
if for every point
x\in X
, there is an open neighborhood
U
of
x
such that
U
intersects only finitely many strata of
(X,P)
.

Example 2.1.6

(Simplicial complexes)
.

Let
(V,S)
be a simplicial complex, and regard
S
as a poset ordered by inclusion.
Write
\Delta^{(V,S)}
for the geometric realization of
(V,S)
.
There is a natural stratification
\Delta^{(V,S)}\to S
with contractible strata, see
[
Lur17
, Definition A.6.7]
.
Moreover,
[
Lur17
, Proposition A.6.8]
shows that if
(V,S)
is
locally finite
, this is a conical stratification.

Example 2.1.7
.

If
X
is a differentiable manifold equipped with a
Whitney stratification

P
(see e.g.,
[
NV23
, Definition 2.5]
), then it is conically stratified.
In fact, it is even
conically smooth
in the sense of
[
AFT17
]
, see
[
NV23
]
.

We conclude by singling out two classes of stratified spaces that are particularly important in this paper.

Definition 2.1.8
.

A
subanalytic stratified space
is the data of a triple
(M,X,P)
where
M
is a smooth
\mathbb{R}
-analytic space,
X\subset M
is a locally closed subanalytic subset, and
X\to P
is a locally finite stratification by subanalytic subsets of
M
.

Definition 2.1.9
.

An
algebraic stratified space
is the data of a stratified space
(X,P)
where
X
is (the real points of) an algebraic variety over
\mathbb{R}
and
X\to P
is a finite stratification by Zariski locally closed subsets.

2.2.
Hypersheaves, hyperconstancy, and hyperconstructibility

We content ourselves to introduce our notation, and we refer to
[
HPT23
, §1.1]
,
[
HPT26
, §1.2]
, or
[
Por25
, §III.2.1]
for a more thorough review of these notions.

Notation 2.2.1
.

Let
X
be a topological space.
We denote by
\mathrm{PSh}(X)
the
\infty
-category of
\mathrm{Spc}
-valued presheaves on
X
and by
\mathrm{Sh}(X)
(resp.,
\mathrm{Sh}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X)
) the full subcategory spanned by sheaves (resp., hypersheaves).
For
\mathcal{E}\in\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}
, we set

\mathrm{Sh}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X;\mathcal{E})\coloneqq\mathrm{Sh}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X)\otimes\mathcal{E}\simeq\FunR(\mathrm{Sh}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X)^{\mathrm{op}},\mathcal{E})\ ,

and similarly for (pre)sheaves.

Remark 2.2.2
.

Sheaves and hypersheaves on topological spaces coincide in many situations that are the central focus of interest of the current paper. For example, this is the case if
X
admits a CW structure
[
Hoy16
]
.
The reader uneasy with hypersheaves and only interested in reasonable geometric examples can therefore simply ignore the attribute
hyper
in the rest of the paper.

Remark 2.2.3
.

The functoriality of the tensor product in
\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}
induces for every
\mathcal{E}\in\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}
a
hypersheafification functor

(-)^{\hyp}\colon\mathrm{PSh}(X;\mathcal{E})\to\mathrm{Sh}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X;\mathcal{E})
, which is still a localization by
[
Por25
, Lemma I.4.11]
.

Notation 2.2.4
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a map of topological spaces and let
\mathcal{E}\in\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}
.
We denote by

f^{-1}\colon\mathrm{PSh}(Y;\mathcal{E})\leftrightarrows\mathrm{PSh}(X;\mathcal{E})\colon f_{\ast}

the induced adjunction.
The functor
f_{\ast}
preserves (hyper)sheaves, and it is part of an adjunction

f^{\ast,\hyp}\colon\mathrm{Sh}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(Y;\mathcal{E})\leftrightarrows\mathrm{Sh}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X;\mathcal{E})\colon f_{\ast}\ .

Inspection reveals that
f^{\ast,\hyp}
can be written as
(-)^{\hyp}\circ f^{-1}
.

Notation 2.2.5
.

For a topological space
X
, we denote by
\Gamma_{X}\colon X\to\ast
the unique map.

Definition 2.2.6
.

Let
X
be a topological space and let
\mathcal{E}\in\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}
.
We say that a hypersheaf
F\in\mathrm{Sh}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X;\mathcal{E})
is
hyperconstant
if
F
lies in the image of

\Gamma_{X}^{\ast,\hyp}\colon\mathcal{E}\to\mathrm{Sh}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X;\mathcal{E})\hbox to0.0pt{\ .\hss}

We say that
F
is
locally hyperconstant
if
F
is hyperconstant on an open cover of
X
.
We denote by
\LC^{\hyp}(X;\mathcal{E})
the full subcategory of
\mathrm{Sh}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X;\mathcal{E})
spanned by locally hyperconstant hypersheaves.

Definition 2.2.7

(
[
Lej21
]
)
.

Let
(X,P)
be a stratified space and let
\mathcal{E}\in\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}
.
We say that
F\in\mathrm{Sh}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X;\mathcal{E})
is
hyperconstructible
if for every
p\in P
, the hypersheaf
i_{p}^{\ast,\hyp}(F)
is locally hyperconstant on
X_{p}
.
We denote by
\Cons_{P}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X;\mathcal{E})
the full subcategory of
\mathrm{Sh}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X;\mathcal{E})
spanned by hyperconstructible hypersheaves on
(X,P)
.

Remark 2.2.8
.

When
\mathcal{E}=\mathrm{Spc}
, we write
\Cons_{P}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X)
instead of
\Cons_{P}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X;\mathrm{Spc})
.

Remark 2.2.9
.

Given a map of stratified spaces
f\colon(X,P)\to(Y,Q)
and
\mathcal{E}\in\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}
, the hypersheaf pullback
f^{\ast,\hyp}:\mathrm{Sh}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(Y;\mathcal{E})\to\mathrm{Sh}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X;\mathcal{E})
restricts to a functor

(2.2.10)

f^{\ast,\hyp}\colon\Cons_{Q}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(Y;\mathcal{E})\to\Cons_{P}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X;\mathcal{E})\ .

Warning 2.2.11
.

Let
k
be a field and let
\mathrm{Mod}_{k}^{\heartsuit}
be the abelian category of

-vector spaces.
Then inside

we allow constructible sheaves with infinite dimensional stalks.
While this goes against the typical convention in algebraic geometry, it is crucial for us to allow such objects, for instance in the proof of
4.1.9
. See also
[
PT23
, Warning 6.5.2]
.

We conclude with a remark on the functoriality in the coefficient
\infty
-category
\mathcal{E}
.

Recollection 2.2.12
.

Let
X
be a topological space.
Let
L\colon\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{D}
be a left adjoint functor between presentable
\infty
-categories with right adjoint
R\colon\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{E}
.
We denote by

the induced left adjoint.
Concretely,
L^{\hyp}
is obtained via the composition of

L\circ-\colon\mathrm{PSh}(X;\mathcal{E})\to\mathrm{PSh}(X;\mathcal{D})

with the hypersheafification functor
(-)^{\hyp}
.
Via the identification

\mathrm{Sh}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X;\mathcal{E})\simeq\FunR(\mathrm{Sh}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X)^{\mathrm{op}},\mathcal{E})

supplied by
[
Lur17
, Proposition 4.8.1.17]
, the right adjoint
R^{\hyp}
of

is given by composing with

.

Remark 2.2.13
.

The formation of
L^{\hyp}
commutes with hypersheaf pullback.

Example 2.2.14
.

Let
A\in\mathrm{dCRing}
and let
M\in\mathrm{Mod}_{A}
.
In the setting of
2.2.12
, we set
L\coloneqq M\otimes_{A}(-)
and

M\otimes_{A}^{\hyp}(-)\coloneqq(M\otimes_{A}(-))^{\hyp}\colon\mathrm{Sh}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X;\mathrm{Mod}_{A})\to\mathrm{Sh}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X;\mathrm{Mod}_{A})\ .

Similarly, given a morphism
A\to B
in
\mathrm{dCRing}
, the adjunction

B\otimes_{A}(-)\colon\mathrm{Mod}_{A}\leftrightarrows\mathrm{Mod}_{B}\colon\Res_{B/A}(-)\ ,

given by extension and restriction of scalars induces an adjunction

The projection formula for modules immediately implies that for every

and every

, the canonical morphism

2.3.
Atomic generation and exodromic stratified spaces

Following ideas of Clausen–Jansen
[
CJ24
]
, in
[
HPT26
]
we introduced the special class of
exodromic
stratified spaces that we recall below, together with their main properties and examples.

Definition 2.3.1

(Atomic generation)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\in\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}
.

(1)

An object
c\in\mathcal{C}
is
atomic
(or
absolutely compact
) if

\Map_{\mathcal{C}}(c,-)\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathrm{Spc}

commutes with all colimits.
We write
\mathcal{C}^{\at}
for the full subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
spanned by atomic objects.

(2)

We say that
\mathcal{C}
is
atomically generated
if the unique colimit-preserving extension
\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{C}^{\at})\to\mathcal{C}
of the inclusion
\mathcal{C}^{\at}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}
is an equivalence.

Definition 2.3.2

(Exodromic spaces, cf.
[
HPT26
, Definition 3.5]
)
.

A stratified space
s\colon X\to P
is
exodromic
if the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

The
\infty
-category
\Cons_{P}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X)
is atomically generated.

(2)

The subcategory
\Cons_{P}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X)\subset\mathrm{Sh}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X)
is closed under both limits and colimits.

(3)

The functor
s^{\ast,\hyp}\colon\Fun(P,\mathrm{Spc})\simeq\mathrm{Sh}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(P)\to\Cons_{P}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X)
preserves limits.

In this case, we define the
\infty
-category of exit-paths
of
(X,P)
as

\Piinfty(X,P)\coloneqq(\Cons_{P}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X)^{\at})^{\mathrm{op}}\ .

Condition (2) should be thought as a
categorical
regularity condition on the stratification, that weakens the conicality condition (see
[
HPT26
, §0.2]
or
[
Por25
, §III.2.5]
for additional comments on this topic).
The main source of examples is:

Example 2.3.3

(Conical stratifications)
.

Let
(X,P)
be a conically stratified space with locally weakly contractible strata.
Then
(X,P)
is exodromic and moreover
\Pi_{\infty}(X,P)
is computed by the simplicial model
\Sing_{P}(X)
of
[
Lur17
, Definition A.6.2]
, see
[
PT23
, Theorem 5.17 & Remark 5.18]
.
In particular, if
(V,S)
is a locally finite simplicial complex, then with the notation of
2.1.6

(\Delta^{(V,S)},S)
is exodromic.
Moreover, using
[
Lur17
, Theorem A.6.10]
there is a natural equivalence

(2.3.4)

\Piinfty(\Delta^{(V,S)},S)\simeq S\ .

Before stating further examples, let us recall a few important general features of the exodromy equivalence.

Theorem 2.3.5

(Exodromy with coefficients,
[
HPT26
, Corollary 4.1.15]
)
.

Let
(X,P)
be an exodromic stratified space.
For every
\mathcal{E}\in\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}
, there is a canonical comparison functor

\Fun(\Piinfty(X,P),\mathcal{E})\to\Cons_{P}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X;\mathcal{E})\ .

If
\mathcal{E}
compactly assembled or
P
noetherian and
\mathcal{E}
stable, then this functor is an equivalence.

Theorem 2.3.6

(Functoriality,
[
HPT26
, Theorem 5.1.7-(3)]

.

Let

be a compactly assembled presetnable

-category.
Then the functor

of (

which furthermore restricts to a functor
\Pi_{\infty}(f)\colon\Pi_{\infty}(X,P)\to\Piinfty(Y,Q)
.
Moreover for every
\mathcal{E}
as in
Theorem

, the diagram

(2.3.7)

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Cons_{Q}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(Y;\mathcal{E})}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Fun(\Piinfty(Y,Q),\mathcal{E})}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Cons_{P}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X;\mathcal{E})}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

commutes.

Recollection 2.3.8

(Objects of
\Piinfty(X,P)
)
.

Let
(X,P)
be an exodromic stratified space with locally weakly contractible strata.
There is a natural identification

where
\Piinfty(X_{p})
denotes the underlying homotopy type of the stratum

Thus, every point

On the other hand, different points of

Lemma 2.3.9
.

Let

be an exodromic stratified space with locally weakly contractible strata and let

Let

Exodromic stratifications allow to go beyond conical examples, mainly thanks to:

(Stability under coarsening,

, Theorem 3.3.5]
)
.

Let

be an exodromic stratified space, and let

(2)

.

Example 2.3.11

(Subanalytic stratifications)
.

Let
(M,X,P)
be a subanalytic stratified space, in the sense of

.
By

]

Example 2.3.12

(Algebraic stratifications)

Let

be an algebraic stratified space, in the sense of

.
Then

2.4.
Finiteness conditions

Fundamental to the construction of moduli spaces are the following finiteness conditions on exodromic stratified spaces:

Definition 2.4.1
.

For an exodromic stratified space
(X,P)
, we say that:

(1)

(X,P)
is
categorically compact
if
\Piinfty(X,P)
is a compact object in
\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}
;

(2)

(X,P)
is
locally categorically compact
if
X
admits a fundamental system of open subsets
U
such that
(U,P)
is exodromic and categorically compact.

Example 2.4.2

(Simplicial complexes)
.

Let
(V,S)
be a locally finite simplicial complex.
Equation (
2.3.4
) shows that the exodromic stratified space
(\Delta^{(V,S)},S)
is locally categorically compact and that it is categorically compact if and only if the set
S
is finite.

The following are the two main finiteness results proven in
[
HPT26
]
. They generalize the classical finiteness theorems of homotopy groups of Lefschetz–Whitehead, Łojasiewicz, and Hironaka.
They are the conceptual basis for the rest of this paper.

Theorem 2.4.3

(Subanalytic finiteness)
.

Let
(M,X,P)
be a subanalytic stratified space.
Then:

(1)

The stratified space
(X,P)
admits a refinement by a locally finite triangulation.

(2)

The stratified space
(X,P)
is exodromic with locally weakly contractible strata.

(3)

The stratified space
(X,P)
is locally categorically compact.

(4)

If
X
is compact, then
(X,P)
admits a refinement by a
finite
triangulation.
Hence
(X,P)
is categorically compact.

Theorem 2.4.4

(Algebraic finiteness)
.

Let
(X,P)
be an algebraic stratified space.
Then:

(1)

If
X
is affine,
(X,P)
admits a categorically compact locally categorically compact conical refinement
(X,R)
with locally weakly contractible strata and with
R
finite.

(2)

The stratified space
(X,P)
is exodromic with locally weakly contractible strata.

(3)

The stratified space
(X,P)
is categorically compact and locally categorically compact.

Remark 2.4.5
.

In the conical setting, stronger finiteness results have been obtained by Volpe in
[
Vol24
, Theorem 1.5 & Corollary 1.6]
.

The proofs of
Theorems

2.4.3
and
2.4.4
rely on the following more general stability properties:

Proposition 2.4.6

(
[
HPT26
, Theorem 3.6.2]
)
.

Let
(X,P)
be an exodromic stratified space and
S\subset P
a locally closed subposet.
If
(X,P)
is categorically compact, then so is
(X_{S},S)
.

Proposition 2.4.7

(
[
HPT26
, Theorem 3.6.4]
)
.

Let
(X,R)
be an exodromic stratified space and let
R\to P
be a map of posets.
If
(X,R)
is categorically compact, then so is
(X,P)
.

2.5.

We conclude this section by recalling

]
.
Let us highlight that a number of important results in the constructible setting require local categorical compactness.

Recollection 2.5.1
.

Let
X
be a topological space, let

be a closed immersion and let

be the open complement.
By
[

, Corollary 2.18 & Proposition 2.26]
for every presentable stable
\infty
-category

exhibit
\mathrm{Sh}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X;\mathcal{E})
as a recollement of
\mathrm{Sh}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(Y;\mathcal{E})
and
\mathrm{Sh}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(U;\mathcal{E})
in the sense of
[

In particular,
i_{\ast}\colon\mathrm{Sh}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(Y;\mathcal{E})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Sh}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X;\mathcal{E})

whereas

admits a left adjoint

.
For a locally closed immersion

between topological spaces presented as the composition of a closed immersion

followed by an open immersion

, we set

i^{!,\hyp}\coloneqq\iota^{!,\hyp}\circ j^{\ast,\hyp}\colon\mathrm{Sh}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X;\mathcal{E})\to\mathrm{Sh}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(Y;\mathcal{E})\ .

This does not depend on the choice of the presentation of
i
.
Observe also that
i^{!,\hyp}
admits a left adjoint given by

i_{!}^{\hyp}\coloneqq j_{!}^{\hyp}\circ\iota_{\ast}\colon\mathrm{Sh}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(Y;\mathcal{E})\to\mathrm{Sh}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X;\mathcal{E})\ .

.

3.
Perverse sheaves and flat families

In this section we collect, the basic facts about the perverse
t
-structure and about how it varies in families.
The main result is

3.3.5
, which allows to prove that the perverse
t
-structure
universally satisfies openness of flatness
in the sense of
[

, Definition II.2.54]
.

Convention 3.0.1
.

Throughout the whole section we fix a derived commutative ring
A
and consider the associated presentable stable
\infty
-category
\mathrm{Mod}_{A}
, equipped with its standard
t
-structure
\tau=((\mathrm{Mod}_{A})_{\geqslant 0},(\mathrm{Mod}_{A})_{\leqslant 0})
, see
[

, Proposition 7.1.1.13]
.
In particular
\tau

3.1.
Hypersheaves and
t
-structures

We start by recalling the basics of the induced
t
-structure on
\mathrm{Mod}_{A}
-valued hypersheaves.

Recollection 3.1.1
.

Let
X
be a topological space.
Recall from
[
Lur18
, Proposition 2.1.1.1]
that
\mathrm{Sh}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X;\mathrm{Mod}_{A})
is equipped with a
t
-structure

\tau^{\hyp}_{X}=\big(\mathrm{Sh}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X;\mathrm{Mod}_{A})_{\geqslant 0},\mathrm{Sh}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X;\mathrm{Mod}_{A})_{\leqslant 0}\big)

whose coconnective part is

\mathrm{Sh}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X;\mathrm{Mod}_{A})_{\leq 0}\coloneqq\mathrm{Sh}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X;(\mathrm{Mod}_{A})_{\leqslant 0})\ .

It follows from
loc. cit.
that
\tau_{X}^{\hyp}
is accessible, left complete, and compatible with filtered colimits.
We refer to
\tau_{X}^{\hyp}
as the

on
\mathrm{Sh}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X;\mathrm{Mod}_{A})
.

Recollection 3.1.2
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a map of topological spaces.
Then
[
Lur18
, Remark 1.3.2.8]
shows that the functor

is

and
\tau^{\hyp}_{Y}

Consequently, its right adjoint

f_{\ast}\colon\mathrm{Sh}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X;\mathrm{Mod}_{A})\to\mathrm{Sh}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(Y;\mathrm{Mod}_{A})

is left (but typically not right)

-exact.

We make repeated use of the following elementary observation:

Lemma 3.1.3
.

Let
f\colon\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
be a functor in
\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}
.
Assume that
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
are stable and equipped with

and
\tau^{\prime}=(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\geqslant 0},\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leqslant 0})

If

Remark 4.1.3

Given a map of stratified spaces

of

.

Lemma 4.1.4
.

Let
(X,P)
be a stratified space and let

be a derived commutative ring.
Then, the presheaf

\mathbf{Cons}_{P}(-)\colon\Open(X)^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{PSh}(\mathrm{dAff}_{k})

Our first task is to compare the derived prestack

with the

of the (large)

-category

For the convenience of the reader, we briefly recall the Toën and Vaquié’s construction (see

, §II.2.4]
for a more extended survey):

Recollection 4.1.5

(Toën and Vaquié’s moduli of objects)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\in\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{k}
.
Its

is the derived stack

\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}(\Spec(A))\coloneqq\Fun^{\mathrm{st}}_{k}((\mathcal{C}^{\omega})^{\mathrm{op}},\mathrm{Perf}(A))^{\simeq}\ ,

where
\Fun^{\mathrm{st}}_{k}
denotes the
\infty
-category of exact,
k
-linear functors.
When
\mathcal{C}
is a compact object of
\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{k}
,

[
TV07
, Theorem 3.6]
states that
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}
is a locally geometric derived stack locally of finite presentation.
Moreover, Corollary 3.17 in

states that if

Lemma 4.1.6
.

Let
(X,P)
be an exodromic stratified space with locally weakly contractible strata.
Let

. Then:

(1)

The exodromy equivalence of

restricts to an equivalence

\Fun(\Piinfty(X,P),\mathrm{Perf}_{A})\simeq\Cons^{\mathrm{hyp}}_{P,\omega}(X;\mathrm{Mod}_{A})\ .

The second statement follows from the first and the fact that the assignment

satisfies flat hyperdescent

, Corollary D.6.3.3 & Proposition 2.8.4.2-(10)]

The first statement follows from the fact that, in the exodromy equivalence

evaluation at an object

corresponds to taking the stalk at a point

representing

, see
Theorem

2.3.6
and
2.3.8
.
∎

Lemma 4.1.7
.

Let
(X,P)
be an exodromic stratified space.
Set
\mathcal{C}\coloneqq\Cons_{P}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X;\mathrm{Mod}_{k})
.
Then for every

, there is a canonical equivalence

(4.1.8)

\Fun_{k}^{\mathrm{st}}\big((\mathcal{C}^{\omega})^{\mathrm{op}},\mathrm{Mod}_{A}\big)\simeq\Cons^{\mathrm{hyp}}_{P}(X;\mathrm{Mod}_{A})\ .

Proof.

Note that
\mathcal{C}
is a compactly generated presentable stable
k
-linear
\infty
-category in virtue of
2.3.9
.
We have:

\displaystyle\Cons_{P}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X;\mathrm{Mod}_{A})

\displaystyle\simeq\Cons_{P}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X)\otimes\mathrm{Mod}_{A}

\displaystyle\simeq(\Cons_{P}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(X)\otimes\mathrm{Mod}_{k})\otimes_{\mathrm{Mod}_{k}}\mathrm{Mod}_{A}

\displaystyle\simeq\mathcal{C}\otimes_{\mathrm{Mod}_{k}}\mathrm{Mod}_{A}

\displaystyle\simeq\Fun_{k}^{\mathrm{R}}(\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathrm{Mod}_{A})

\displaystyle\simeq\Fun_{k}^{\mathrm{st}}((\mathcal{C}^{\omega})^{\mathrm{op}},\mathrm{Mod}_{A})\ ,

and the conclusion follows.
∎

Lemma 4.1.9
.

In the setting of
4.1.7
, assume furthermore that
(X,P)
has locally weakly contractible strata.
Then the equivalence (
4.1.8
) restricts to an equivalence

\Fun_{k}^{\mathrm{st}}\big((\mathcal{C}^{\omega})^{\mathrm{op}},\mathrm{Perf}(A)\big)\simeq\Cons^{\mathrm{hyp}}_{P,\omega}(X;\mathrm{Mod}_{A})\ .

In particular, there is a canonical equivalence of derived stacks

\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}\simeq\mathbf{Cons}_{P}(X)\ .

Proof.

Under the equivalence (
4.1.8
), the hypersheaf
F\in\Cons^{\mathrm{hyp}}_{P}(X;\mathrm{Mod}_{A})
corresponds to the assignment

G\longmapsto\Hom_{\Cons^{\mathrm{hyp}}_{P}(X;\mathrm{Mod}_{A})}(A\otimes_{k}^{\hyp}G,F)\ .

Note also that for a functor in
\Fun_{k}^{\mathrm{st}}\big((\mathcal{C}^{\omega})^{\mathrm{op}},\mathrm{Mod}_{A}\big)
, the condition to land in
\mathrm{Perf}(A)
can be checked on a subset of objects generating
\mathcal{C}^{\omega}
under finite colimits and retracts.
Hence, the condition to land in
\mathrm{Perf}(A)
can be checked on a set of compact generators of
\mathcal{C}
.
Since
(X,P)
has locally weakly contractible strata,
2.3.9
implies that
\mathcal{C}
is compactly generated by the
x_{\sharp}^{\cons}(k)
for
x\in X

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\Hom_{\Cons^{\mathrm{hyp}}_{P}(X;\mathrm{Mod}_{A})}(A\otimes_{k}^{\hyp}x_{\sharp}^{\cons}(k),F)

\displaystyle\simeq\Hom_{\Cons^{\mathrm{hyp}}_{P}(X;\mathrm{Mod}_{A})}(x_{\sharp}^{\cons}(A),F)

\displaystyle\simeq\Hom_{\mathrm{Mod}_{A}}(A,x^{\ast,\hyp}(F))

\displaystyle\simeq x^{\ast,\hyp}(F)\ .

The conclusion thus follows.
∎

In order to invoke Toën and Vaquié’s representability result, one needs a compactness assumptions on

This are provided by

2.4.3
and
2.4.4
via:

Lemma 4.1.10
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be the collection of finite simplicial sets with

\mathrm{PSh}_{\mathcal{K}}\colon\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}\to\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}^{\mathrm{rex},\mathrm{idem}}

(See
[

Then, for every

(1)

We have
\mathrm{PSh}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathcal{C})=\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{C})^{\omega}
.

(2)

If
\mathcal{C}
is compact, then
\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{C})
is a compact object in
\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}
.

(3)

If
\mathcal{C}
is compact, the tensor product

\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{C})_{k}\coloneqq\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{C})\otimes\mathrm{Mod}_{k}

is compact in
\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{k}

Proof.

By construction,
\mathrm{PSh}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathcal{C})
is the smallest full subcategory of

containing the image of the Yoneda embedding and closed under

-indexed colimits. In particular,

and the equality holds thanks to

For (2), assume that

is compact in
\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}
.
By
[

, the right adjoint
\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}^{\mathrm{rex},\mathrm{idem}}\to\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}
commutes with filtered colimits.
Thus,
\mathrm{PSh}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathcal{C})
is a compact object of
\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}^{\mathrm{rex},\mathrm{idem}}

Since
\mathrm{Ind}

Corollary 4.1.11

Let
(X,P)

The exodromy equivalence yields

(2)
