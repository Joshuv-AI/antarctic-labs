EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
 The overall objective of this project was to complete a feasibility study for a Regional Direct Air Capture
(DAC) Hub that encompasses Illinois, Indiana, and Kentucky. The geographic construct of this hub was
based on the Illinois Basin – a geological area where at least seven (7) geological storage studies were
conducted. The Hub was designed to assure a capacity to capture, store, and utilize at least 1,000,000
metric tons (tonnes) of CO2 from the atmosphere annually 1 metric tonnes per annum (MTA), starting
from an initial capacity of 200,000 tonnes of CO2 per annum (KTA). The Hub also included:(1) clean energy
providers to supply renewable energy to the DAC units; (2) geological storage facilities to inject and
sequestrate the captured CO2; (3) CO2 utilization facilities that would uptake the captured CO2 and
convert it into products; and (4) small business organizations that would assist in workforce development
eﬀorts for the region.

 This closeout report consolidates the tasks undertaken and accomplished as deliverables for the
project. The project team had successfully completed all required tasks of phase 0a, conﬁrming that the
project is feasible based on the preliminary conceptual designs and operational requirements of the DAC
systems. Of particular interest, the hub ownership structure was identiﬁed and was implemented in follow
on projects that would transition the hub infrastructure to a build/operate phase.

1. DAC Hub Description
1.1. Description of the DAC Hub Owner and Team
 The project team has evaluated various consortia and legal structures for ownership of the
DAC hub in the Illinois Basin. A detailed analysis of consortia structure, rationalization for the
selection, draft agreement, and the proposed ownership structure can be found in Appendix A.

1.2. All Potential Site Locations – Transport, Geological Storage, and Utilization
 Appendix B outlines the sites which were identiﬁed for geological storage and/or utilization (non EOR)
of captured CO2. These sites include multiple states. It was anticipated that more sites could be identiﬁed
in future. The strategy for a phased development of the sites is also discussed in Appendix B. This strategy
accommodates the expected infusion of private sector capital beyond 2030. It also mitigates business and
ﬁnancial related risks.
 Storage sites and transport routes are also discussed. Many of the storage sites are within 15 miles of
the planned capture facilities. The status of Class VI well permitting is also discussed. There is suﬃcient
space for the location of DAC units. A portfolio approach was used for utilization.

1.3. Initial Design of BOP of DAC Hub Plant (OSBL)
 Most of the required balance of plants (BOP) are already present in the vicinity of the DAC
deployment sites, thus some infrastructure already exists at each of the sites. These details are
discussed in Appendix B. The BOP needs are also discussed in Appendix C as part of the
preliminary business plan and buildout strategy.

1.4. Preliminary Business plan and Timeline for Buildout
 Appendix C outlines the preliminary business plan for the hub based on the ownership
structure outlined in Appendix A. A pathway from public/private partnership funding to
exclusively private sector funding is outlined. Inclusion of DAC technologies beyond the anchoring
technologies is also outlined.

2. DAC Technology Selection
2.1. Selection of Anchoring DAC Technologies
 CarbonCapture, Inc. (CCI) and Heirloom were the anchor DAC technologies. A detailed
technology description of CCI and Heirloom can be found in Appendix D.

2.2. Conceptual Design for Initial DAC Hub Capacity
 The team considered several sites to conduct the initial 200 KTA pre-FEED design using CCI
and Heirloom technology. A more detailed plan can be found in Appendix E for the conceptual
design details.

 5

2.3. CO2 Conversion Technology Selection
 Hub partner CarbonCure would provide commercial CO2 conversion technology to obtain low
carbon concrete. Hub partner Ozinga was a CO2 oﬀ taker with several plants already incorporating
CarbonCure’s technology. Carboncure would bring in more existing/potential clients as a CO2 oﬀ-
taker. A detailed technology description of CarbonCure can be found in Appendix F.

2.4. Preliminary Technology Maturation Plan (TMP)
 Preliminary Technology Maturation Plan (TMP) for the anchor DAC technologies: CCI, Heirloom, and
CO2 conversion technology provider: CarbonCure were developed. Detailed versions of the preliminary
descriptions of the TMPs can be found in Appendix G.

2.5. DAC Hub Data Tables
 Detailed data tables for the anchor DAC technologies: CCI, Heirloom, and CO2 conversion technology
provider: CarbonCure were developed. These data tables can be found in Appendix H.

2.6. Preliminary Life Cycle Analysis (LCA)
 A preliminary high-level LCA was performed based on the anchor tenant CCI and Heirloom’s
technology. The detailed energy and material (carbon) balance is shown in Appendix I.

2.7. Safety, Security, and Regulatory Requirements
 A detailed description of the Safety, Security, and Regulatory requirements for the overall hub
can be found in Appendix J.
