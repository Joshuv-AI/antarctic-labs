Higher-spin self-dual gravity from holomorphic planes in twistor space
Nicolas Boulanger, Yannick Herfray, Lionel Mason, Noémie Parrini

Abstract
We prove a ‘nonlinear graviton theorem’ for higher-spin self-dual gravity. We consider small deformations of the complex structure of the non-projective twistor space that are bounded in a specified region near the origin and investigate the space M H ​ S M_{HS} of holomorphically embedded complex planes ℂ 2 \mathbb{C}^{2} that intersect the origin. We show that this space is an infinite dimensional complex manifold with a canonical projection onto a four-dimensional holomorphic self-dual spacetime ℳ \mathcal{M} , and discuss the geometry induced on this new higher-spin space. Solutions of higher-spin self-dual gravity are then obtained by choosing an embedding of spacetime ℳ \mathcal{M} into higher-spin space M H ​ S M_{HS} , with higher-spin symmetries arising from the different choices of embedding. Integrability of the theory is manifested in the form of a Lax pair for the system that we present. We conjecture that chiral higher-spin gravity can similarly be realized by considering deformations that are unconstrained at the origin.

7
Discussion

In this article we studied a generalisation of Penrose’s nonlinear graviton theorem
[
19
]
where, instead of considering complex deformation
\textrm{P}\mathscr{T}
of the projective twistor space
\mathbb{PT}
preserving the Poisson structure along the holomorphic fibration
\mathbb{PT}\to\mathbb{C}\textrm{P}^{1}
, we considered a complex deformation
\mathscr{T}
of the twistor space
\mathbb{T}
: The crucial difference lies in the relaxed requirement
3
on the Euler vector field, which generates the quotient
\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{PT}
. As a result, the complex deformations that we considered are not deformations of
\textrm{P}\mathcal{T}
but they are rigid enough to be interpreted as being generated by higher-spin fields, see Proposition
1
. In analogy with Penrose’s classical result, the higher-spin space
M_{HS}
is then defined as the space of holomorphic embeddings
\mathbb{C}^{2}_{0}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{T}
.

Under these general assumptions, negative helicity higher-spin fields will source the deformation and, thus, one is forced to allow for singularities in the embeddings
\mathbb{C}^{2}_{0}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{T}
. This is consistent with the fact that, as discussed in section
6
, such singularities are necessary to obtain the Plebanski formulation of (the Poisson contraction of) chiral higher-spin gravity. Therefore, singularities in the embedding of the spacetime point seem to be a key feature of the presence of negative helicity fields, a point which was not apparent from
[
17
]
.

On the other hand, the appearance of negative helicity fields, and related singularities of the planes, can be prevented by the extra assumption that the complex deformations are bounded at the origin
4
, which is the main setup of this work. Under this extra assumption, we proved (see Theorem
2
) that the moduli space
M_{HS}
of holomorphic embeddings
\mathbb{C}^{2}_{0}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{T}
is a infinite dimensional complex manifold and canonically projects
M_{HS}\to\mathcal{M}
on a usual self-dual four-dimensional spacetime. This leads to a generalization of the nonlinear graviton theorem, summarized in Theorems
3
and
4
, identifying complex deformations of twistor space with solutions of higher-spin self-dual gravity. An novel feature of the construction is that higher-spin symmetries are here geometrically realized as choices of embeddings
\mathcal{M}\hookrightarrow M_{HS}
of spacetime
\mathcal{M}
into the higher-spin space.

There are two main outcomes of the present article, whose investigation is left for future work. On the one hand, our twistor construction naturally produces a notion of higher-spin geometry, in the form of an infinite dimensional complex manifold equipped with an infinite number of higher-spin frames, the higher-spin symmetry appearing as a result of the restriction of this infinite dimensional geometry to a four-dimensional slice. On the other hand, it naturally suggests an extension of our results to include negative helicity interactions. This is because the boundness assumption
4
here seems to merely play the role of excluding such interactions. As we already emphasized, however, such negative helicity interactions have to come, in the present setting, hand-in-hand with singularities in the holomorphic embeddings
\mathbb{C}^{2}_{0}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{T}
. The geometrical understanding, as well as the physical interpretation, of such singularities is likely to come with interesting subtleties that we leave for future work. Finally, Penrose’s original result
[
26
]
was intrinsically an equivalence between conformal self-dual metric and deformation of (projective) twistor space, with Einstein metrics coming as a further restriction. In contrast, for conformal higher-spin theory

[
27
,
28
]
,
while related twistor actions have been written some time ago already
[
29
,
30
]
,
the question as to whether or not there exists a related nonlinear graviton theorem, and how it would relate to the present results, is still open.
