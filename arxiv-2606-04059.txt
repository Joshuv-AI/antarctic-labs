Integrable motion of curves associated with the Fokas-Lenells equation and related spin system Journal: Physics Letters A
R. Ramakrishnan, Sagardeep Talukdar, M. Lakshmanan, Avadh Saxena

Abstract
In this article, we study the gauge equivalence between the integrable Fokas-Lenells equation (FLE) and an associated spin equation through a gauge transformation and the zero curvature condition. We also construct the Lax pair for the generalized spin equation to confirm its integrability. Further, by mapping a generalized spin system on a moving space curve in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} , we show its geometrical equivalence with the FLE. In particular, the associated evolution equations for the curvature and torsion of the space curve are shown to be equivalent to the FLE through a complicated complex transformation unlike the case of the well known Heisenberg spin equation and the nonlinear Schrödinger equation.



1
Introduction

The study of nonlinear integrable systems is a very fascinating area of research due to the mathematical elegance and physical applications. In general, these nonlinear integrable systems possess interesting mathematical structures such as symmetries, conserved quantities, Lax pairs and bi-Hamiltonian structures. In particular the systems admitting soliton solutions, in areas such as nonlinear optics, Bose-Einstein condensation, etc., have attracted a large number of researchers in these fields over the past few decades.
From the vast sea of integrable nonlinear evolution equations, one of the most well known equations is the nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLSE)

\displaystyle iu_{t}+\gamma u_{xx}+\rho|u|^{2}u=0,

(1)

which is used as the standard model for pulse propagation in fiber optics. Here,
\gamma
, and
\rho
are real constants and
u
is the complex-valued function of
x
and
t
. Eq. (
1
) is completely integrable and can be linearized by the inverse scattering transform technique
[
1
]
. Solutions arising from system (
1
) are particularly interesting because of their potential to be used as carriers in long distance communication through optical fibers. However, the NLSE in (
1
) can only describe the propagation of pulses up to the first order nonlinear effect, the so called Kerr effect.

To address this issue, more than a decade ago, Fokas and Lenells proposed a more general version of Eq. (
1
) when certain higher order nonlinear effects are considered, namely self-steepening, Raman-scattering and third order dispersion. The resultant equation is the following one named as the Fokas-Lenells equation,

\displaystyle iu_{t}-\nu u_{tx}+\gamma u_{xx}+\rho|u|^{2}(u+i\nu u_{x})=0,

(2)

which was deduced in Ref.
[
2
]
by means of the bi-Hamiltonian framework. We note here that Eq. (
2
) and Eq. (
1
) are related to each other in the same way that the Camassa-Holm equation is related to the Korteweg de-Vries equation from the bi-Hamiltonian perspective. One can see that Eq. (
2
) reduces to Eq. (
1
) when
\nu=0
. Further, under an appropriate transformation
u\rightarrow e^{ix}u
and
\nu=\gamma=\sigma=1
, Eq. (
2
) gets converted to

\displaystyle u_{tx}+u-2iu_{x}-u_{xx}-i|u|^{2}u_{x}=0,

(3)

which is integrable in the present form and admits a Lax pair
[
3
]
. The model (
2
) was derived by A. S. Fokas in Ref.
[
2
]
, developed by Lenells in
[
4
]
and its physical foundation was discussed in
[
5
]
. An expression for the one-soliton solution of (
2
) was obtained by the Riemann-Hilbert approach in
[
3
]
. Additionally, through the Hirota bilinear method the authors in
[
6
]
obtained the soliton solutions, and the rogue wave solution through the Darboux transformation was obtained in Ref.
[
7
]
.

It is worth noting that the FLE in Eq. (
2
) is also equivalent to a very different form under a gauge transformation followed by a change of variables introduced in
[
5
]
. Upon the transformation
u\rightarrow\sqrt{\frac{a}{|\rho|}}be^{i(bx+2abt)}u
,
\xi=2(x+at)
,
\tau=\frac{-ab^{2}t}{2}
,
a=\gamma/\nu>0
, and
b=1/\nu
, Eq. (
2
) transforms into another integrable form given as

\displaystyle u_{\tau\xi}=u-2i\sigma|u|^{2}u_{\xi},\quad\quad\sigma=\pm 1.

(4)

From the viewpoint of the spectral problem, Eq. (
2
) belongs to the derivative NLSE hierarchy that is related to the Kaup-Newell (KN) spectral problem. The form of FLE in (
4
) is also well studied in the context of soliton solutions. The
N
-soliton solution through the Hirota bilinear method is investigated in Refs.
[
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
]
and through the inverse scattering transform method is studied in
[
14
]
. Also, the breather and rogue wave solutions of the Eq. (
4
) are studied in
[
15
]
using the KP hierarchy reduction method. Its perturbation theory through the inverse scattering technique was discussed in
[
16
]
. To avoid any confusion and to analyse the above equations systematically, hereafter in this manuscript we rename Eq. (
4
) as type-I FLE and Eq. (
3
) as type-II FLE.

The above type of integrable nonlinear evolution equations in (1+1) and (2+1) dimensions have interesting geometrical connections with integrable moving space curves and surfaces
[
17
,
18
]
. Such a geometrical connection between the Heisenberg spin system and the NLSE has been effectively demonstrated in
[
19
,
20
]
. Here the geometric relation between the evolution of the curvature and torsion of the moving curve associated with the spin chain mapped onto the NLSE through a complex transformation, is established. The same geometrical connection has been systematically demonstrated in an entirely different way, namely the gauge equivalence method
[
21
,
22
]
. In this procedure, the Lax pairs of the integrable nonlinear evolution equations and the corresponding integrable spin system are connected through a gauge transformation and the zero curvature equation.

Over the time, these two formalisms have been widely used to understand the integrability property of the nonlinear evolution equations. It is worth noting here that the connection between the Landau-Lifshitz equation and the higher-order nonlinear systems has been studied using gauge equivalence procedure in
[
23
]
. Also, such connections between the next order corrected Heisenberg spin equation and the fourth order Lakshmanan-Porsezian-Daniel equation (LPDE)
[
24
]
as well as the generalized spin equation and the derivative NLSE (DNLSE)
[
25
]
, have been established in a similar fashion. However, to the best of our knowledge, the geometrical connection for the nonlinear evolution equations having mixed space and time derivatives has not been properly addressed in the scientific literature. To address this issue, in this paper we study the geometrical connection among the type-I and type-II FLEs and the associated spin problems.

This paper is organized as follows. In Sec. 2, we study the gauge equivalence between type-I and type-II FLEs and derive the associated spin systems. As a consequence, from the Lax pairs of the type-I and type-II FLEs, we construct the Lax pairs of the corresponding spin equations, which in turn provide the signature of the integrability of the spin problems. In Sec. 3, we investigate the above mentioned geometrical connection via the so called space curve mapping formalism and suggest the suitable modification in the usual complex transformation is used to exactly map the time evolution of the curvature and torsion equations of space curves to the respective integrable nonlinear evolution equations. Finally, we briefly conclude our main results in Sec. 4.

2
Gauge equivalence of the Fokas-Lenells system

In this section, we introduce the concept of gauge equivalence for both types of FLEs given by Eqs. (
3
) and (
4
) and show an interesting connection between the considered types of FLE and the generalized Landau-Lifshitz type spin equations. In doing so, we recall that to characterize the integrable evolution of a nonlinear system, one has to typically introduce a pair of associated linear operators, commonly referred to as a Lax pair. The key idea is that the mutual compatibility of these operators governs the dynamics of the nonlinear equation and guarantees its integrability. In particular, the underlying beauty is that upon inserting the Lax pair associated with the nonlinear equation into the compatibility condition also known as the zero curvature equation, it reproduces the original nonlinear system. For instance, we may consider a Lax pair for the system, written in the following compact form:

\Psi_{x}=U_{i}\,\Psi,\qquad\Psi_{t}=V_{i}\,\Psi,

(5)

where
U_{i}
and
V_{i}
are matrix-valued functions depending on the field
u(x,t)
, its derivatives, the space–time variables, and a spectral parameter
\lambda
.
The compatibility condition
\Psi_{xt}=\Psi_{tx}
leads to

U_{it}-V_{ix}+[U_{i},V_{i}]=0,

(6)

which encodes the nonlinear evolution equation satisfied by
u(x,t)
. Eq. (
6
) is the zero curvature equation as discussed earlier.

Let
G
denote a Lie group associated with the algebra to which
U_{i}
and
V_{i}
belong.
A gauge transformation by an element
g(x,t;\lambda_{0})\in G
, defined for a fixed spectral parameter
\lambda_{0}
, acts on the Jost function as

\Phi(x,t;\lambda,\lambda_{0})=g^{-1}(x,t;\lambda_{0})\Psi(x,t;\lambda),

(7)

where the matrix
g(x,t;\lambda_{0})
is,

\displaystyle g(x,t,\lambda_{0})=\Psi(x,t,\lambda)|_{\lambda=\lambda_{0}}.

(8)

Now, two integrable systems are said to be gauge equivalent if the Lax pair
(U_{i},V_{i})
is converted to a new pair
(U_{i}^{\prime},V_{i}^{\prime})
through a
\lambda
independent gauge transformation such as

U_{i}^{\prime}=g^{-1}U_{i}g-g^{-1}g_{x},\qquad V_{i}^{\prime}=g^{-1}V_{i}g-g^{-1}g_{t},

(9)

and the transformed system satisfies

\Phi_{x}=U_{i}^{\prime}\Phi,\qquad\Phi_{t}=V_{i}^{\prime}\Phi.

(10)

Requiring the consistency of these two relations gives rise to the gauge-transformed compatibility condition

U_{i}^{\prime}{{}_{t}}-V_{i}^{\prime}{{}_{x}}+[U_{i}^{\prime},V_{i}^{\prime}]=g^{-1}\Big(U_{it}-V_{ix}+[U_{i},V_{i}]-(U_{i0t}-V_{i0x}+[U_{i0},V_{i0}])\Big)g=0,

(11)

where
U_{i0}=U_{i}|_{\lambda=\lambda_{0}}
and
V_{i0}=V_{i}|_{\lambda=\lambda_{0}}
.
Hence, the new pair
(U_{i}^{\prime},V_{i}^{\prime})
generates another nonlinear evolution equation that is gauge equivalent to the original one. Subsequently, to express the transformed nonlinear evolution equation in terms of
the spin field
``S"
, we introduce,

S=g^{-1}\sigma_{3}g;\quad S^{2}=I,

(12)

with
\sigma_{3}
denoting the diagonal Pauli matrix. Then through straightforward differentiation one obtains,

S_{x}=g^{-1}[\sigma_{3},U_{0}]g,\qquad S_{t}=g^{-1}[\sigma_{3},V_{0}]g.

(13)

These identities form the basis for constructing explicit Landau–Lifshitz–type spin equations that are dynamically equivalent to the FLE equations.

2.1
Type-I Fokas-Lenells equation

The type-I FLE also known as the Fokas-Lenells derivative nonlinear Schrödinger equation (DNLSE)
[
8
]

\displaystyle u_{xt}-u+2i|u|^{2}u_{x}=0.

The Lax pair associated with system (

\displaystyle\frac{-i\lambda^{2}}{2}\sigma_{3}+\lambda u_{x},

\displaystyle V_{1}=

\displaystyle\frac{i}{2\lambda^{2}}\sigma_{3}-\frac{i}{\lambda}\sigma_{3}u+i\sigma_{3}u^{2}.

The gauge transformation given by Eq. (

\displaystyle\frac{i}{2}(\lambda_{0}^{2}-\lambda^{2})g^{-1}\sigma_{3}g+(\lambda-\lambda_{0})g^{-1}u_{x}g,

\displaystyle V_{1}^{\prime}=

\displaystyle\frac{i}{2}(\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}-\frac{1}{\lambda_{0}^{2}})g^{-1}\sigma_{3}g-i(\frac{1}{\lambda}-\frac{1}{\lambda_{0}})g^{-1}\sigma_{3}ug.

\displaystyle SS_{x}=

\displaystyle 2\lambda_{0}g^{-1}u_{x}g,

(20)

and,

\displaystyle S_{t}=

\displaystyle-\frac{2i}{\lambda_{0}}g^{-1}ug,

(21)

\displaystyle SS_{t}=

\displaystyle-\frac{2i}{\lambda_{0}}g^{-1}\sigma_{3}ug.

(22)

Inserting Eqs. (

\displaystyle U_{1}^{\prime}=

\displaystyle\frac{i}{2}(\lambda_{0}^{2}-\lambda^{2})S+\frac{1}{2\lambda_{0}}(\lambda-\lambda_{0})SS_{x},

\displaystyle V_{1}^{\prime}=

\displaystyle\frac{i}{2}(\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}-\frac{1}{\lambda_{0}^{2}})S+\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2}(\frac{1}{\lambda}-\frac{1}{\lambda_{0}})SS_{t}.

Finally, substituting the above Eqs. (

\displaystyle S_{t}-\frac{1}{2i\lambda_{0}^{2}}[S,S_{xt}]-\frac{1}{\lambda_{0}^{4}}S_{x}=0,S^{2}=I.

(25)

\displaystyle S_{t}-\frac{1}{2i}[S,S_{xt}]-S_{x}=0,S^{2}=I.

(26)

Here we note that an attempt has already been made to derive the spin equation corresponding to Eq. (

2.2
Type-II Fokas-Lenells equation

The Type-II FLE being the integrable generalization of the NLSE, preserves the Lax integrability in the following form
[
3
]
,

\displaystyle u_{xt}+\alpha\beta^{2}u-2i\alpha\beta u_{x}-\alpha u_{xx}-i\alpha\beta^{2}|u|^{2}u_{x}=0.

(27)

The Lax pair
U_{2},V_{2}
in accordance with Eq. (

) is,

\displaystyle U_{2}=

\displaystyle\lambda u_{x}-i\lambda^{2}\sigma_{3},

(28)

\displaystyle V_{2}=

\displaystyle\alpha\lambda u_{x}+\frac{i\alpha\beta^{2}}{2\lambda}\sigma_{3}u-\frac{i\alpha\beta^{2}}{2}\sigma_{3}u^{2}-i\eta^{2}\sigma_{3},

(29)

where

\displaystyle\eta=\sqrt{\alpha}(\lambda-\frac{\beta}{2\lambda}).

Using the gauge transformation given by Eq. (

\displaystyle 2\lambda_{0}g^{-1}\sigma_{3}u_{x}g,

\displaystyle 2\lambda_{0}g^{-1}u_{x}g,

(34)

\displaystyle S_{t}=

\displaystyle 2\alpha\lambda_{0}g^{-1}\sigma_{3}u_{x}g+\frac{i\alpha\beta^{2}}{\lambda_{0}}g^{-1}ug,

\displaystyle SS_{t}=

\displaystyle 2\alpha\lambda_{0}g^{-1}u_{x}g+\frac{i\alpha\beta^{2}}{\lambda_{0}}g^{-1}\sigma_{3}ug.

Substituting Eqs. (

\displaystyle U_{2}^{\prime}=

\displaystyle\frac{(\lambda-\lambda_{0})}{2\lambda_{0}}SS_{x}+i(\lambda_{0}^{2}-\lambda^{2})S,

\displaystyle V_{2}^{\prime}=

\displaystyle(\frac{\alpha\lambda}{2\lambda_{0}}-\frac{\alpha\lambda_{0}}{2\lambda})SS_{x}+i\alpha[(\lambda_{0}^{2}-\lambda^{2})+\frac{\beta^{2}}{4}(\frac{1}{\lambda_{0}^{2}}-\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}})]S+(\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2\lambda}-\frac{1}{2})SS_{t}.

Space Curve Mapping Formalism

In this section, we investigate the geometric relationship between the FLE and the generalized spin system using the differential geometric method, namely the space curve mapping formalism. This mapping can be applied between the time evolution of the curvature and torsion of the moving space curve and the spin system using the complex transformation which is also widely known as the Hasimoto transformation for the curve or the Lakshmanan transformation for the spin systems and the method is known as Lakshmanan equivalence
[
27
,
28
,
29
,
30
]
.

3.1
Type-I Fokas-Lenells equation

We consider a moving space curve in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
represented by the orthogonal trihedral
\vec{e_{i}},i=1,2,3,
following the Serret-Frenet equations and the time evolution of the trihedral on the space curve

\begin{pmatrix}\vec{e_{1}}\\
\vec{e_{1}}\\
\vec{e_{1}}\end{pmatrix}_{x}=L\begin{pmatrix}\vec{e_{1}}\\
\vec{e_{1}}\\
\vec{e_{1}}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}\vec{e_{1}}\\
\vec{e_{1}}\\
\vec{e_{1}}\end{pmatrix}_{t}=M\begin{pmatrix}\vec{e_{1}}\\
\vec{e_{1}}\\
\vec{e_{1}}\end{pmatrix},

(41)

where

L=\begin{pmatrix}0&\kappa&0\\
-\kappa&0&\tau\\
0&-\tau&0\end{pmatrix},M=\begin{pmatrix}0&\omega_{1}&\omega_{2}\\
-\omega_{1}&0&\omega_{3}\\
-\omega_{2}&-\omega_{3}&0\end{pmatrix}.

Here
\vec{e_{1}},\vec{e_{2}},
and
\vec{e_{3}}
are the unit tangent, normal and binormal vectors. Also,
\kappa=(\vec{e_{1x}}.\vec{e_{1x}})^{2}
is the curvature and
\tau=\kappa^{-2}\vec{e_{1}}.(\vec{e_{1x}}\times\vec{e_{1xx}})
is the torsion associated with the curve.
Now the considered vector form of the spin equation in (
26
) is

\vec{S_{t}}=(\vec{S}\times\vec{S_{xt}})+\vec{S_{x}}.

(42)

Then we associate the spin field
\vec{S}(x,t)
corresponding to the type-I FLE (
42
) with the unit tangent vector
\vec{e_{1}}
, that is
\vec{e_{1}}(x,t)=\vec{S}(x,t)
, and allow it to evolve as (
42
) so that we have the evolution equation

\vec{e_{1t}}=(\vec{e_{1}}\times\vec{e_{1xt}})+\vec{e_{1x}}.

(43)

Now using the Serret-Frenet equation (
41
), the evolution equation can be rewritten in terms of the trihedral as

\vec{e_{1t}}=-(\omega_{1}\tau+\omega_{2x}-\kappa)\vec{e_{2}}+(\omega_{1x}-\omega_{2}\tau)\vec{e_{3}},

(44)

where
\omega_{1}
,
\omega_{2}
and
\omega_{3}
are given below.
Unlike the case of other equations, a complication arises in (
43
) as the evolution equation of
\vec{e_{1t}}
involves the mixed space and time derivatives. But since the (
\vec{e_{1}},\vec{e_{2}},\vec{e_{3}}
) form an orthogonal trihedral there is a close relationship. By taking the compatibility condition
L_{t}-M_{x}+[L,M]=0
for Eqs. (
41
), one obtains the following system of coupled nonlinear evolution equations for the curvature and torsion:

\displaystyle\kappa_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega_{1x}-\omega_{2}\tau,

\displaystyle\tau_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega_{2}\kappa+\omega_{3x},

\displaystyle\omega_{2x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\omega_{1}\tau+\omega_{3}\kappa.

In the above equations,

\displaystyle\omega_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(\kappa-\kappa_{xt})}{(1+\tau)},

\displaystyle\omega_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa_{t},

\displaystyle\omega_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\tau(\kappa-\kappa_{xt})}{\kappa(1+\tau)}+\frac{\kappa_{xt}}{\kappa}.

The resultant time evolution equations of the curvature and torsion take the following forms

\displaystyle\kappa_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{(1+\tau)}\bigg[\frac{(\kappa_{xt}-\kappa)}{(1+\tau)}\bigg]_{x},

(45)

\displaystyle\tau_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg[\frac{(\kappa_{xt}-\kappa)}{\kappa(1+\tau)}\bigg]_{x}-\frac{\kappa}{(1+\tau)}\bigg[\frac{(\kappa_{xt}-\kappa)}{(1+\tau)}\bigg]_{x}.

(46)

We note here that for the well known Heisenberg spin chain equation

\vec{S_{t}}=(\vec{S}\times\vec{S_{xx}}),

(47)

the associated evolution equations for curvature and torsion, as given in
[
20
]
, are

\displaystyle\kappa_{t}=

\displaystyle(\kappa\tau)_{x}+\kappa_{x}\tau,

(48)

\displaystyle\tau_{t}=

\displaystyle\kappa\kappa_{x}+\bigg(\frac{\kappa_{xx}}{\kappa}-\tau^{2}\bigg)_{x}.

(49)

The above coupled nonlinear evolution equations (
48
)-(
49
) can be combined through the complex transformation

q(x,t)=\kappa(x,t)e^{i\int\tau(x,t)dx},

(50)

to obtain the well known nonlinear Schrödinger equation,

iq_{t}+q_{xx}+\frac{1}{2}|q|^{2}q=0.

(51)

It is important to note here that the same transformation can not be used for the nonlinear evolution equations which have mixed space and time derivatives like FLE.
Instead the following evolution equations

\displaystyle\kappa_{t}=-\frac{1}{(1+\tau)}\bigg[\bigg(\frac{\kappa(\kappa_{xt}-\kappa)}{(1+\tau)}\bigg)_{x}-4\kappa^{2}\kappa_{x}\bigg],

(52)

\displaystyle\tau_{t}=\bigg(\frac{\kappa_{xt}-\kappa}{\kappa(1+\tau)}\bigg)_{x}-4\kappa\kappa_{x},

(53)

are obtained from the type-I FLE (
14
) using the complex transformation
u(x,t)=\kappa(x,t)e^{i\int(\tau(x,t)+1)dx}
. It is evident that there are differences between (
45
)-(
46
) and (
52
)-(
53
). The associated differences can be eliminated with further careful modification in the complex transformation as
u(x,t)=\kappa(x,t)e^{i\int(\tau(x,t)+b(x,t)+1)dx}e^{i\int c(x,t)dt}
or even more general transformation of the form
u(x,t)=f(\kappa(x,t),\tau(x,t))e^{i\int\tau(x,t)dx}e^{ia(x,t)}
and with suitable selection of functions
b(x,t)
and
c(x,t)
or
f(\kappa,\tau)
and
a(x,t)
.

3.2
Type-II Fokas-Lenells equation

To perform the space curve mapping for the spin equation (

\alpha=\beta=1

\vec{S_{t}}=-\delta(\vec{S}\times\vec{S_{xx}})+\delta(\vec{S}\times\vec{S_{xt}})-(\delta^{2}-1)\vec{S_{x}},

\vec{e_{1t}}=[-\delta(\omega_{2x}+\omega_{1}\tau)+\delta\kappa\tau+(1-\delta^{2})\kappa]\vec{e_{2}}+[\delta(\omega_{1x}-\omega_{2}\tau)-\delta\kappa_{x}]\vec{e_{3}}.

(56)

As followed in the previous section, the compatibility condition
L_{t}-M_{x}+[L,M]=0
leads to the following system of coupled nonlinear evolution equations for

and

\displaystyle\kappa_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega_{1x}-\omega_{2}\tau,

\displaystyle\tau_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega_{2}\kappa+\omega_{3x},

\displaystyle\omega_{2x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\omega_{1}\tau+\omega_{3}\kappa,

where

\displaystyle\omega_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1+\delta\tau)}[(1-\delta^{2})\kappa-\delta^{2}(\kappa_{xt}-\kappa_{xx})+\delta\kappa\tau],

\displaystyle\omega_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta(\kappa_{t}-\kappa_{x}),

\displaystyle\omega_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\delta(\kappa_{xt}-\kappa_{xx})}{\kappa}+\frac{\tau}{\kappa(1+\delta\tau)}[(1-\delta^{2})\kappa-\delta^{2}(\kappa_{xt}-\kappa_{xx})+\delta\kappa\tau].

After substituting the value of
\delta=1/2
, we have the evolution equations for the curvature and torsion as

\displaystyle\kappa_{t}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2+\tau)}\bigg[\bigg(\frac{(\kappa(3+2\tau)-\kappa_{xt}+\kappa_{xx})}{(2+\tau)^{2}}\bigg)_{x}+\kappa_{x}\tau\bigg],

4
Conclusions

In this article, we have succeeded to deduce the generalized spin equation equivalent to the FLE through the well known gauge equivalence method. The integrability of the identified spin system is also confirmed by the presentation of the associated Lax pair. Also, we have mapped the FLE to the corresponding spin system on a suitable moving space curve. Unlike the case of the other evolution equations, the present case requires the simultaneous consideration of the evolution of all three unit vectors constituting the orthogonal trihedral associated with the space curve. We have also given appropriate modifications in the usual complex transformation, used in the Heisenberg spin hierarchy, for the nonlinear evolution equations which have mixed space and time derivatives. We hope the method indicated in the paper will help to identify new integrable spin systems.
