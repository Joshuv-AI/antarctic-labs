The stability of Margulis space-times with parabolic holonomy elements Thanks: This work was supported by the National Research Foundation of Korea(NRF) grant funded by the Korea government (MEST) (No. NRF-2022R1A2C3003162).
Suhyoung Choi

Abstract
. Let 𝖤 \mathsf{E} be a flat Lorentzian space of signature ( 2 , 1 ) (2,1) . A Margulis space-time is a noncompact complete flat Lorentzian 3 3 -manifold 𝖤 / Γ \mathsf{E}/\Gamma , where the holonomy group Γ \Gamma is a free group of rank 𝗀 ≥ 2 \mathsf{g}\geq 2 acting freely and properly discontinuously by isometries. We consider the case where Γ \Gamma contains a parabolic element. We show that sufficiently small deformations of Γ \Gamma still act properly discontinuously on 𝖤 \mathsf{E} provided their linear parts are Fuchsian; moreover, the number of conjugacy classes of parabolic elements may increase or decrease under deformation. Our proof combines our previous compactification of 𝖤 / Γ \mathsf{E}/\Gamma relative to parabolic holonomy elements with a partial generalization of the work of Carrière. However, this result depends only on the parts on parabolic actions of our earlier work. We believe that the shortness of the proof of this openness result is of independent interest.

2.2.
Geometric structures on manifolds

A pair

A

The geometry

The geometry

,

We have inclusions

({\mathbf{Isom}}(\mathsf{E}),\mathsf{E})\subset({\mathbf{Aff}}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}),{\mathbb{R}}^{3})\subset({\mathsf{SL}}_{\pm}(4,{\mathbb{R}}),{\mathbb{S}}^{3}).

Hence, a flat Lorentzian manifold, and so a Margulis space-time,
can be considered an affine
3
-manifold
and also a real projective
3
-manifold.

Let
N
be a

-dimensional
manifold with a universal cover

.
Given an affine (resp. projective) structure on

Given two affine manifolds

is a map that locally sends a chart to a chart.
A

is an affine manifold

under a properly discontinous and free action

A

in an affine (resp. projective) manifold is an arc

under which it is locally path-connected geodesic-metric
space induced from a Riemannian metric. For

we define

as an image of the holonomy

.

Let

denote the quotient homomorphism.
A representation

is an
affine deformation of a Fuchsian representation
if

\Pi\circ\mathcal{L}\circ h
is a Fuchsian representation.

We note that the space of discrete faithful characters

\mathfrak{F}_{n}\subset{\rm Hom}(F_{n},{\mathsf{PSO}}(2,1))/{\mathsf{PSO}}(2,1)

is homeomorphic to a union of open cells with some boundary points.
We call
\mathfrak{F}_{n}
the
Fricke subspace
.
This is a semialgebraic set where a dense open set is a disjoint union of
open cells, and
boundary strata consist of representations with parabolic elements.
Here only the boundary-parallel homotopy classes can map to parabolic elements by
the theory of Fricke. (See
[
15
]
where this is essentially proved.)
Of course, different open components
consist of characters of representations of the fundamental groups of
surfaces with distinct topological structures.

We recall the standard cone neighborhood theory following from
the triangulations of the semialgebraic sets as in Theorem 9.2.1 of
[
1
]
:
We define a
cone over a semialgebraic set

to be a subspace
homeomorphic to a product
X\times[0,1]
with
X\times\{0\}

collapsed to a point. The image of
X\times\{0\}
is called the

cone point
.
A point
x
of a semi-algebraic set has a neighborhood with a semi-algebraic homeomorphism to a cone
C
over a semi-algebraic set
S

with the cone point
x
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\rho:F_{n}\rightarrow{\mathbf{Isom}}(\mathsf{E})
be an affine deformation of a Fuchsian representation whose image contains parabolic elements.
Then
\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{e}}
and
\mathcal{F}^{{\mathrm{e},\mathrm{l}}}
are semialgebraic sets.
There is a cone neighborhood
N
of
\rho
in
\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{e}}_{n}
where
the subset
N^{\prime}
of
N
consisting of representations without parabolic image elements is
an open dense cone, which is a complement of a semialgebraic subset of codimension

\geq 1
in
N
passing through
\rho
.

Proof.

The subset
\hat{D}_{n}
of
\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{e}}_{n}
of representations
with some parabolic image elements is a union of finitely many semi-algebraic subsets of codimension
\geq 1
passing through
\rho

since only peripheral elements can have a parabolic holonomy.
The subset in question is the inverse image of
\hat{D}_{n}
.

Define
\mathcal{L^{\prime}}:=\Pi\circ\mathcal{L}\circ h
.
This can be considered a fiber bundle

with fibers that can be identified with

.
The proposition follows from the standard
cone-neighborhood theory of semi-algebraic sets.
∎

For the general case,
