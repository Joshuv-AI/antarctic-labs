Remark 1

A sum

A tropical prevariety is a finite intersection of tropical hypersurfaces. If
\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}\}\subset\mathbb{T}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, we denote by
V(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})=\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}V(f_{i})
the associated tropical prevariety.

Tropical prevarieties are in general not tropical varieties in the traditional sense (see
[
15
, §3.2]
), but they are (rational) polyhedral sets in
\mathbb{T}^{n}

We denote by
\mathbb{T}^{*}=(\mathbb{R},\odot,0)
the tropical torus. The tropical hypersurface
V(f)
consists of two parts:
\{a\in\mathbb{T}^{n}\>:\>ev_{f}(a)=0_{\mathbb{T}}\}\subset\mathbb{T}^{n}\setminus(\mathbb{T}^{*})^{n}
, and
\{a\in\mathbb{T}^{n}\>:\>ev_{f}(a)\!\text{``}\!=\!\!\!\text{''}0_{\mathbb{T}}\}
, which is sometimes referred to as the bend locus of
f
.

The bend locus of
f
refers to the set of points where this minimum is attained simultaneously by at least two distinct monomials. If a single monomial attains the minimum, it strictly dominates. In contrast, if two monomials achieve the minimum, they are considered balanced. Thus the bend locus of
f
is empty unless
f
has at least two monomials, something which we will assume. Note that
V(f)\cap(\mathbb{T}^{*})^{n}
is contained in the bend locus of
f
.

Definition 2
.

If
ev_{f}(a)\!\!\text{``}\!=\!\!\!\text{''}0_{\mathbb{T}}
, we denote by
S(a)
the set of monomials where
ev_{f}(a)
reaches its minimum, this is
S(a):=\{I\in S\>:\>ev_{f}(a)=a_{I}+\langle I,a\rangle\}
.

Convention 1
.

In general we will try to use different notation for a tropical polynomial
f=\bigoplus_{I\in S}a_{I}\odot x^{I}
and for its induced evaluation map
ev_{f}(x)=min_{I\in S}\{a_{I}+\langle I,x\rangle\}
, where
\mathbb{T}^{n}
is endowed with coordinates
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
. This distinction has the purpose of making a smooth transition between the concepts and notation from commutative algebra and the actual computations using tropical operations.

Definition 3
.

A monomial
J\in S
of a tropical polynomial
f
is irrelevant if
ev_{f}(a):=min_{I\in S\setminus\{J\}}\{a_{I}+\langle I,a\rangle\},
otherwise we say that it is relevant.

The binary relation on
\mathbb{T}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
defined by
f\sim g
if and only if
ev_{f}=ev_{g}
is a semiring congruence; we denote by
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
the quotient semiring, which is the semiring of global tropical functions on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
.

If
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}
is an additive monoid, and

is a commutative semiring with 1, the Mal’cev-Neumann semiring

consists of the set of formal sums

with

is a well-ordered subset of

. This defines a map

.

We define

, which is a sub-semiring of

, and

Example 1
.

We denote by
\{0,\infty\}=\mathbb{B}\subset\mathbb{T}

2.1.
Tropical differential algebra

We give basic definitions from tropical differential algebra adapted for this particular case. In this introduction we follow the notation from Convention
1
with the purpose of making a smooth transition between the objects from differential equations, and the actual computations using tropical mathematics. The source for this material is
[
8
]
.

A differential monomial in one differential variable
y
is a monomial in the variables
\{y_{i}=y^{(i)}\>:\>i\in\mathbb{N}\}
, this is, a finite expression of the form

(11)

E=\prod_{i\in\mathbb{N}}y_{i}^{m_{i}},\>m_{i}\in\mathbb{N},\>m_{i}=0\text{ for almost all }i.

in order to lighten up the notation, it is better to use sub-indexes
y_{i}=y^{(i)}
to denote the order of derivation. We can identify (
11
) with the vector
(m_{0},m_{1},\ldots)=M\in\mathbb{N}^{\oplus\mathbb{N}}
and write
E=E_{M}
. The differential order of
E_{M}
is
ord(E_{M})=\max\{i\>:\>m_{i}\neq 0\}
, so
M\in\mathbb{N}^{ord(E_{M})}
.

An ordinary tropical differential equation in one variable is a finite sum
P=P(y)=\sum_{M_{i}\in S}t^{a_{M_{i}}}E_{M_{i}}=\bigoplus_{M_{i}\in S}a_{M_{i}}\odot E_{M_{i}}
as in (
2
) consisting of differential monomials (
11
). Recall that the support of
P
is
Supp(P)=\{M_{1},\ldots,M_{s}\}\subset\mathbb{N}^{\oplus\mathbb{N}}
, and the differential order of
P
is
ord(P)=\max\{ord(E_{M})\>:\>M\in Supp(P)\}
.

Definition 4
.

We denote by
\mathbb{B}[\![t]\!]=\mathbb{B}[\![t^{\mathbb{N}}]\!]
the semiring of formal power series with coefficients in
\mathbb{B}
. We denote by
\tfrac{d}{dt}:\mathbb{B}[\![t]\!]\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{B}[\![t]\!]
the formal derivation of series.

The isomorphism
Supp:\mathbb{B}[\![t]\!]\xrightarrow{}\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N})
from Example
1
can be promoted to an isomorphism of pairs
(R,d)
of semirings
R
equipped with a derivation

. By Example
1
, we can express the elements of
\mathbb{B}[\![t]\!]
either

(1)

as formal sums
\varphi=\sum_{i\in A}1_{\mathbb{B}}t^{i}
, where the derivation of series is performed with the usual conventions, or

(2)

as sets
A\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N})
.

As for the case of tropical polynomials, a differential polynomial
P
as in (

) induces a map

, called the differential evaluation, as follows: if

E(\varphi)=dev_{E}(\varphi):=\prod_{i\in\mathbb{N}}(\tfrac{d^{i}\varphi}{dt^{i}})^{m_{i}},

and then we extend by linearity:
P(\varphi):=dev_{P}(\varphi)=\sum_{i}t^{a_{M_{i}}}E_{M_{i}}(\varphi).

If
\overline{\mathbb{N}}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}\subset\mathbb{T}
, we denote by

is the usual

-adic valuation homomorphism. We have a commutative diagram:

\textstyle{\mathbb{B}[\![t]\!]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

We say that

if the following expression vanishes tropically (see Remark

(12)

\displaystyle ord_{t}(P(\varphi)\!)

\displaystyle=ord_{t}(\sum_{i}t^{a_{M_{i}}}E_{M_{i}}(\varphi)\!)=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}a_{i}\odot ord_{t}(E_{M_{i}}(\varphi)\!)

\displaystyle=\min_{j}\{a_{j}+ord_{t}(E_{M_{j}}(\varphi)\!)\}.

Continuing with our conventions, we will try to use different notation for a differential polynomial
P=\sum_{M_{i}\in S}t^{a_{M_{i}}}E_{M_{i}}=\bigoplus_{M_{i}\in S}a_{M_{i}}\odot E_{M_{i}}
and for its induced evaluation map
ord_{t}(P(\varphi)\!)=\min_{M_{i}\in S}\{a_{M_{i}}+ord_{t}(E_{M_{i}}(\varphi)\!)\}
.

If

(1)

, or

(2)

, which appears only when

is not a monomial.

For
M\in\mathbb{N}^{[k]}
, sometimes we write

and

. If

), so

. We have the following result.

Proposition 1
.

If
P=\sum_{M_{i}\in S}t^{a_{M_{i}}}E_{M_{i}}
has no constant term, then the set of tropical solutions
\varphi
of
P
satisfying
ord_{t}(P(\varphi)\!)=0_{\mathbb{T}}
is the set of polynomials in
t
with coefficients in
\mathbb{B}
having degree at most
\min ord(P)-1
.

Proof.

Again,
ord_{t}(P(\varphi)\!)=0_{\mathbb{T}}
happens if and only if
P(\varphi)=0
(the null series) if and only if
E_{M_{i}}(\varphi)=0
for all
i=1,\ldots,s
. And
E_{M}(\varphi)=0
iff
deg(\varphi)\leq\min ord(E)-1
.
∎

Convention 2
.

From now on our differential polynomials

will satisfy:

(1)

have no constant term (this is relevant for our applications),

(2)

they are not a monomial (

.

(3)

they are differential equations, i.e.
ord(P)=k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.

Since we already computed in Prop.

the tropical solutions satisfying

, from now on we will be interested only on the tropical solutions satisfying
ord_{t}(P(\varphi)\!)\text{``}=\text{'' }0_{\mathbb{T}}
(in particular,

). We will also drop the adjective tropical in solution, and refer to them only as solutions.

Definition 5
.

For
P=P(y)=\sum_{M_{i}\in S}t^{a_{M_{i}}}E_{M_{i}}
as in (

), we denote by

endowed with the order induced by inclusion of subsets.

Example 2

(The linear case)
.

For us, an
otde

is linear of order

if

, this is, if

(in particular, it is homogeneous and generic). In this case,

is a

-semimodule, and the set

consists of additive-irreducible elements which gives some sort of order-theoretic basis.

.

It is possible to have

, see
[

, Theorem 3]
for a description of the case in which

is linear.

An important subset of

is its set

of minimal elements, which was introduced in
[

]
for

linear (homogeneous, in

\Sigma=\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}\}
of

In the next section we will see that for

3.
General properties of elementary
k
-solutions

Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. In the case or a linear differential polynomial
P
(see Example
2
) the particular structure of the support set makes possible to bound the number of monomials of every minimal solution, this is one of the reasons why elementary
k
-series were not considered in that case.

In this Section, we introduce the set of elementary
k
-series
\pi_{k}(\mathbb{B}[\![t]\!])\subset\mathbb{B}[\![t]\!]
, which are important in the tropical theory and have many properties, for instance, if
\mathbb{Z}_{S}
denotes the parameter space of
otde
s having support
S
of differential order
k
, and
Sol_{k}(P)
is the set of elementary
k
-series which are solutions of
P\in\mathbb{Z}_{S}
, then the elements of
Sol_{k}(P)
are polynomials, and we have
\mu(Sol(P)\!)\subset Sol_{k}(P)
. Besides there is a
bundle
presentation

Sol_{k}(P)\xrightarrow{}I_{S}\xrightarrow{\pi_{1}}\mathbb{Z}_{S},

where
\pi_{1}:I_{S}\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{Z}_{S}
is the first projection from the incidence variety

(13)

I_{S}=\{(P,\varphi)\in\mathbb{Z}_{S}\times\pi_{k}(\mathbb{B}[\![t]\!])\>:\>\varphi\in Sol_{k}(P)\},

and
I_{S}
can be described by some integral points (
\overline{\mathbb{Z}}
-rational points) of certain tropical hypersurfaces inside
(\mathbb{T}^{*})^{\#S}\times\mathbb{T}
.

These tropical algebro-geometric properties makes easier to focus on
Sol_{k}(P)
rather than
\mu(Sol(P)\!)
for the case of a general support
S
; in particular, if
Sol_{k}(P)
is finite, then
\mu(Sol(P)\!)
is also finite, so this study can be considered as an extension of
[
12
]
.

3.1.
Elementary
k
-series and solutions

Elementary
k
-series arise from a more detailed study of the family of maps

\displaystyle ord_{t}(E_{M}(-)\!):\mathbb{B}[\![t]\!]

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{}\overline{\mathbb{N}}

\displaystyle\varphi

\displaystyle\mapsto ord_{t}(E_{M}(\varphi)\!)

for all
M\in\mathbb{N}^{[k]}\setminus\{0\}
. If
P
is an
otde
of differential order
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, then we will show that for any
\varphi\in\mathbb{B}[\![t]\!]
, the tropical sum
ord_{t}(P(\varphi)\!)\neq\infty
from (
12
) depends only on an elementary
k
-series
\pi_{k}(\varphi)\subseteq\varphi
, and we will use this observation to define the set
Sol_{k}(P)
of elementary
k
- solutions of
P
.

We denote by
[k-1]:=\{0,\ldots,k-1\}
and by
\mathcal{P}([k-1])
its power set. Since
\mathbb{N}=[k-1]\sqcup[k-1]^{c}
, any
\varphi\in\mathbb{B}[\![t]\!]
can be uniquely decomposed as
\varphi=\varphi_{[k-1]}+\varphi_{[k-1]^{c}}
, where
\varphi_{[k-1]}=\varphi\cap[k-1]
.

Definition 6
.

For
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
fixed, we define the map
\pi_{k}:\mathbb{B}[\![t]\!]\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{B}[\![t]\!]
as

(14)

\pi_{k}(\varphi)=\begin{cases}\varphi,&\text{if }\varphi\in\mathcal{P}([k-1]),\\
t^{ord_{t}\varphi}&\text{if }ord_{t}(\varphi)\notin[k-1],\\
\varphi_{[k-1]}+t^{ord_{t}\varphi_{[k-1]^{c}}},&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

The set of elementary
k
-series is the image
Im(\pi_{k})\subset\mathbb{B}[\![t]\!]
of the map
\pi_{k}
. If
0\neq\varphi\in Im(\pi_{k})
, then it is of the form
\varphi=R\cup B
with

(1)

ord_{t}(\varphi)\notin[k-1]
, then
R=\emptyset
and
B=\{q\}
with
k\leq q<\infty
,

(2)

ord_{t}(\varphi)\in[k-1]
, then
\emptyset\neq R\subset[k-1]
and the subcases

(a)

B=\emptyset
,

(b)

B=\{q\}
with
k\leq q<\infty
,

We now describe some important properties of the map
\pi_{k}
.

Lemma 1
.

For
\varphi\in\mathbb{B}[\![t]\!]
and
M\in\mathbb{N}^{[k]}=\bigoplus_{j=0}^{k}\mathbb{N}\cdot e_{j}
, we have:

(1)

\pi_{k}(\varphi)\subseteq\varphi
,

(2)

\pi_{k}(\pi_{k}(\varphi)\!)=\pi_{k}(\varphi)
,

(3)

ord_{t}(\varphi)=ord_{t}(\pi_{k}(\varphi)\!)

(4)

ord_{t}(E_{M}(\varphi)\!)=ord_{t}(E_{M}(\pi_{k}(\varphi)\!)
.

Proof.

The first three properties are immediate from Definition
6
. For the fourth one, it is enough to show that
ord_{t}(\frac{d^{j}\varphi}{dt^{j}})=ord_{t}(\frac{d^{j}\pi_{k}(\varphi)}{dt^{j}})
for all
j\in\{0,\ldots,k\}
. Suppose
\varphi\neq 0
, then we have the following cases:

(1)

q=ord_{t}(\varphi)\notin[k-1]
. Here
ord_{t}(\frac{d^{j}\varphi}{dt^{j}})=q-j
for all
j\in\{0,\ldots,k\}
. On the other hand, we have
\varphi_{[k-1]}=0
and
B=\{q\}
with
q\geq k
, so
ord_{t}(\frac{d^{j}\pi_{k}(\varphi)}{dt^{j}})=q-j
for all
j\in\{0,\ldots,k\}
.

(2)

ord_{t}(\varphi)\in[k-1]
. We have two sub-cases:

(a)

B(\varphi)=0
. Here
\varphi=\pi_{k}(\varphi)
, and we are done.

(b)

B(\varphi)=\{q\}
with
q\geq k
. If
\emptyset\neq\varphi_{[k-1]}=\{0\leq r_{1}<\cdots<r_{s}\leq k-1\}
, we have

ord_{t}(\frac{d^{j}\varphi}{dt^{j}})=\begin{cases}r_{i}-j,&\text{ if }r_{i-1}<j\leq r_{i},\\
q-j,&\text{ if }r_{s}+1\leq j\\
\end{cases}

and this is clearly just
ord_{t}(\frac{d^{j}\pi_{k}(\varphi)}{dt^{j}})
, which ends the proof.∎

From now on, if
\emptyset\neq R\in\mathcal{P}([k-1])
, we will denote it as
R=\{0\leq r_{1}<\cdots<r_{s}\leq k-1\}
.

Lemma
1
represents the basis for what comes next. In particular, this is enough to prove that the set
\mu(Sol(P)\!)
is some sort of order-theoretic basis for
Sol(P)
in the following sense.

Proposition 2
.

Let
P=\sum_{M_{i}\in S}t^{a_{M_{i}}}E_{M_{i}}
be as in (
2
) of order

. Then we have

Sol(P)=\bigcup_{\varphi\in\mu(\operatorname{Sol}(P)\!)}\operatorname{Sol}(P)\cap\langle\varphi\rangle^{\uparrow}.

Proof.

It is enough to show that any
\varphi\in Sol(P)
contains a minimal solution.

By point (4) of Lemma
1
, we know that both
\varphi
and
\pi_{k}(\varphi)
yield the same value under the map
ord_{t}(E_{M}(-)\!)
for all
M\in\mathbb{N}^{[k]}
, in particular if
\varphi\in Sol(P)
, then
\pi_{k}(\varphi)\in Sol(P)\cap Im(\pi_{k})
since
P
has order
k
, and since
\pi_{k}(\varphi)\subset\varphi
also by (1) of the same Lemma, it is enough to show that elements in
Sol(P)\cap Im(\pi_{k})
contain a minimal solution, but this is immediate since these elements have finite support.
∎

Remark 3
.

Lemma
1
says that
\pi_{k}
is basically an interior operator on the poset
(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N}),\subseteq)
, but not quite, since it does not preserve the order.

In the language of subposets of
(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N}),\subset)
, we have shown that
\mu(Sol(P)\!)\subset Sol(P)\subset\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N})
is coinitial.

Definition 7
.

If
P
has order
k
, the set of elementary
k
-solutions of

is
Sol_{k}(P)=Sol(P)\cap Im(\pi_{k})
.

By Prop.
2
, the map
\pi_{k}:\mathbb{B}[\![t]\!]\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{B}[\![t]\!]
restricts to a map
\pi_{k}:Sol(P)\xrightarrow{}Sol_{k}(P)
. Going from
Sol(P)
to
Sol_{k}(P)
is very useful, not only because elements of
Sol_{k}(P)
all have

, but also due to the inclusion

(15)

\mu(Sol(P)\!)\subseteq Sol_{k}(P).

Indeed, if
\varphi\in\mu(Sol(P)\!)
is not in
Sol_{k}(P)
, then
\pi_{k}(\varphi)\in Sol(P)
satisfies
\pi_{k}(\varphi)\subsetneq\varphi
, which contradicts the minimality of
\varphi
. Note that the converse is not true, see Example
6
.

In order to go further, we need to compute explicitly the values
ord_{t}(E_{M}(\varphi)\!)\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}
for all
M\in\mathbb{N}^{[k]}
and
\varphi\in Im(\pi_{k})
.

Definition 8
.

Given
\sum_{j=0}^{k}m_{j}e_{j}=M\in\mathbb{N}^{[k]}
and
R\in\mathcal{P}([k-1])
, we define
|M|_{R}(z)
as
|M|_{\emptyset}(z)=\sum_{j}m_{j}(z-j)
, and as

(16)

|M|_{R}(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{r_{1}}m_{j}(r_{1}-j)+\cdots+\sum_{j=r_{s-1}+1}^{r_{s}}m_{j}(r_{s}-j)+\sum_{j=r_{s}+1}^{k}m_{j}(z-j),

if
\emptyset\neq R=\{0\leq r_{1}<\cdots<r_{s}\leq k-1\}
. Here

is a variable.

Note that every
|M|_{R}(z)
can be expressed as a tropical monomial

(17)

|M|_{R}(z)=c(M,R)+d(M,R)z=c(M,R)\odot z^{d(M,R)},\quad c(M,R)\in\mathbb{Z}\text{ and }d(M,R)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},

indeed,
c(M,R)=\sum_{j=0}^{r_{1}}m_{j}(r_{1}-j)+\cdots+\sum_{j=r_{s-1}+1}^{r_{s}}m_{j}(r_{s}-j)-\sum_{j=r_{s}+1}^{k}jm_{j}
and

if
\emptyset\neq R=\{0\leq r_{1}<\cdots<r_{s}\leq k-1\}
, while
c(M,\emptyset)=-\sum_{j}jm_{j}
and

Lemma 3
