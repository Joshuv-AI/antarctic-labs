Executive Summary
This document is a Record of Decision regarding changes or modifications to the LAMP Conceptual
Design. Specifically, the following change has been reviewed and is approved:
  Change of the LAMP MEBT beam energy from 3.0 MeV to 2.1 MeV.
This change impacts:
  The design of certain components within the LAMP MEBT, such as the rebuncher and beam
 chopper structures;
  The design of the LAMP radiofrequency quadrupole; and
  The design of the first LAMP drift tube linac tank.

This change is assessed as being beneficial to the project for the following reasons: cost reduction; risk
reduction; and lowered potential for radiation generation and activation in portions of the beamline which
may be expected to require routine access for maintenance.

1 Introduction
The LAMP Conceptual Design (LCD), as described in the LAMP Conceptual Design Report [1]
incorporates a radiofrequency quadrupole (RFQ) with an output beam energy of 3.0-MeV; the medium-
energy beam transport line (MEBT) transports beam from the end of the RFQ into the first LAMP drift-
tube linac (DTL) tank. The MEBT contains components, such as rebunchers and beam choppers, designed
for the specific output energy of the RFQ, and the DTL tank is designed to accept this beam energy.
An RFQ output energy of 3.0 MeV was initially selected for expected performance benefits in transporting
the accelerated and bunched ion beams from the RFQ exit to the entrance of the DTL. Specifically, higher
beam energies generally lead to easier propagation of the beams, as the impact of space-charge forces is
inversely proportional to beam energy.
General consequences of higher beam energies also include, for otherwise similar systems, higher-strength
magnetic and electric fields for beam focusing and guiding. In the case of the LAMP MEBT line, higher
energies results in more challenging beam chopper requirements. Also, with a MEBT energy of 3.0 MeV,
generation of neutrons through both intentional beam loss (e.g. via routine operations such as beam
chopping and pulse selection) and undesired beam loss (e.g. halo interception in the higher-energy sections
of the RFQ and in the MEBT) will also occur.
A study has been conducted to assess the impact of several potential changes to the LAMP Conceptual
Design, including lowering the LCD MEBT energy from 3.0 MeV to 2.1 MeV. This Record of Decision
documents the elements driving the decision to adopt the proposed lowered beam energy for the MEBT.

2 Potential Impacts
This section describes the changes under consideration, and brief assessments of the potential impacts of
those proposed changes.

2.1 Summary of proposed change

The design energy of the LAMP MEBT would be lowered from 3.0 to 2.1 MeV.

2.2 Impact on Key Performance Parameters

The proposed change is not anticipated to impact the ability of the project to meet its Objective Key
Performance Parameters.

2.3 Impact on Accelerator Performance

The proposed changes are not expected to have a significant impact on overall performance of the LAMP
accelerator as a whole.

2.4 Impact on Technology Maturation

The proposed change will not introduce any new Critical Technology Elements (CTEs) to the Project.

By reducing the voltage requirement on the MEBT chopper, the proposed changes should make addressing
one of the LAMP project’s CTEs easier; it will not mitigate the core requirements on chopper turn on / turn
off speed, and thus not eliminate the CTE.

2.5 Impact on Project Risks

The proposed change has the potential to impact the following entries in the LAMP risk registry:

  #61 – Shielding: the lowered MEBT energy should reduce the requirement for radiation shielding
 in the region of the RFQ and MEBT beamlines.

  #114 – RFQ-Diacrode interface: if the new RFQ design requires significantly less RF power than
 the conceptual design RFQ, the coupler design could be simplified or eased, helping to mitigate
 this risk.

  #119 – RF Power Requirement: if the new RFQ design requires significantly less RF power than
 the conceptual design RFQ, the net RF power requirements on the project could be reduced.
 However, this will be balanced to an extent by additional power required in the DTL, and new RF
 sources could potentially be required.

2.6 Impact on Commissioning, Operations and Maintenance

The proposed changes are not expected to have a significant impact on commissioning or operation of the
LAMP accelerator.

Maintenance is expected to be easier with the MEBT at 2.1 MeV versus the conceptual design’s 3.0 MeV,
as lack of neutron radiation generation and activation in the RFQ and MEBT should reduce or eliminate
the need for local shielding, providing for easier access, and reducing or eliminating the need for shielding
configuration verification before restarting operations. Beamline components in the RFQ and MEBT,
including consumables such as ConFlat flanges, will not be activated, helping to simplify waste-stream
management.

2.7 Impact on Major Accelerator Subsystems

2.7.1 Ion Sources

No impact is expected on the ion sources from this change.

2.7.2 Low-Energy Beam Transport

No impact is expected on the low-energy beam transport (LEBT) portion of the LAMP accelerator design
from this change.

2.7.3 Radiofrequency Quadrupole

The output energy of the LAMP radiofrequency quadrupole would be reduced from 3.0 to 2.1 MeV. This
will result in a physically shorter RFQ, with an anticipated length reduction from 5.5 m to 3.75 m, a
reduction in required RF power from 0.8 MW (peak) to 0.33 MW (peak), and corresponding reduction in
cooling requirements. Beam losses within the RFQ will not result in neutron radiation generation.

2.7.4 Medium-Energy Beam Transport

Generally, to achieve the same focusing and steering effects, beam optics elements (e.g. quadrupoles,
steering magnets, solenoids) can be made shorter, can be operated at lower current (e.g. lower fields), or a

combination thereof. Magnetic elements in the LAMP MEBT are not expected to represent significant
design challenges arising from the reduction in beam energy.

Several types of components used in the LAMP MEBT design are sensitive to beam energy (or velocity).
Changing the beam energy from 3.0 MeV to 2.1 MeV will reduce the beam velocity from 0.080 c to 0.067 c;
this will impact the design of the MEBT rebuncher and beam chopper structures. As RF structures, the
rebunchers must in general have their gap spacing matched to the energy of the beam; a design for a 2.1-
MeV rebuncher already exists and been built for Fermilab. The chopper is a travelling-wave structure, with
group velocity matched to the beam velocity. As with the rebuncher structures, a chopper structure designed
for 2.1-MeV beam energy is already in service. For a similar structure length, with a lower beam energy
the beam chopper is expected to require a lower operating voltage to achieve the same intended effect on
the beam.

No required changes are anticipated to other beamline components such as diagnostics, vacuum pumping
systems, etc.

Beam losses within the MEBT, either intentional (e.g. from diverting beam into the chopper dump) or
unintentional (e.g. beam halo lost on an aperture) will not result in neutron radiation generation and potential
activation.

2.7.5 Drift Tube Linac

The design of the LAMP Drift Tube Linac first tank would be changed to accept an incoming beam energy
of 2.1 MeV, versus the LCD value of 3.0 MeV. This will result in modest increases to the length of the
first DTL tank and RF power consumption. For instance, based on the v8e/f Tank 1 model, which has an
input energy of 2.1 MeV and an output energy of 8.6 MeV, the DTL tank length would increase by
approximately 0.95 m (from 4.04 m in the LCD, to 4.99 m); and peak RF power dissipated on the tank
walls would increase from 0.193 MW to 0.221 MW. RF power delivered to the beam would increase by
900 W / mA.

Generally, these changes are not expected to pose significant additional challenges for fabrication, cooling,
or RF sources for DTL Tank 1. We also note that the design of the LAMP DTL will continue to be refined
through the preliminary-to-final design process.

Beam losses within DTL Tank 1 will not result in generation of neutron radiation or activation within the
first approximate 0.5 meters of the drift tube linac tank. However, the tank’s output energy will be above
the neutron generation threshold energy, beam losses in downstream portions of the tank will still result in
radiation generation and the potential for activation.

No impact is expected on the remainder of the drift-tube linac.

2.7.6 Controls and Diagnostics

No significant impact on accelerator controls and diagnostics is expected; component count is unchanged
and no new beam characterization techniques are required.

2.7.7 DC and Pulsed Power

No significant impact is expected on DC power systems; required currents for quadrupole and steering
corrector magnets may change, but not enough to warrant a reconsideration of the DC power supply
selection.

Pulsed power requirements may be eased somewhat from this change, e.g. operating voltage of the MEBT
choppers may be reduced. Timing requirements, e.g. rise- and fall-times, are not expected to change.

2.7.8 RF Systems

Low-Level RF

No changes are anticipated; the number of RF systems is not changing and the performance requirements
are likewise not expected to change.

High Power RF

The lower wall losses and lower exit energy of the RFQ result in a significant reduction in the required RF
power to drive the RFQ. In principle, the RFQ could be driven using two tetrodes vs. a Diacrode, this
approach should be considered. Overall RF infrastructure is expected to be simpler; cost impact is expected
to be minimal vs. the conceptual design configuration.

2.7.9 Vacuum Systems

The overall beamline volume is reduced due to the shorter length of the RFQ. No significant changes to
the vacuum system requirements are anticipated.

2.7.10 Cooling Systems

Cooling requirements for the RFQ will be reduced due to the lower RF power needed; cooling requirements
for DTL Tank 1 will be increased. Overall, cooling requirements are anticipated to be somewhat lowered
by the change.

2.7.11 Protective Systems

Impacts on Protective Systems are expected to be neutral to positive. The change will eliminate generation
of neutron radiation in the RFQ and MEBT, which may ease or eliminate personnel access control
requirements in the areas immediately adjacent to these sections of the LAMP accelerator. The change is
not expected to have a net impact on machine protection.

2.7.12 Infrastructure

The overall LAMP design is not mature enough to definitively assess whether this change will have a
positive, negative or neutral impact on aspects such as the stackable shield wall at the boundary between
sectors A and J. However, the elimination of neutron radiation generation upstream of the first DTL tank is
anticipated to have a net positive benefit on the overall infrastructure requirements.

The overall shorter RFQ will be neutral or beneficial for installation concerns.

3 Assessment of the Proposed Changes
The proposed changes have been evaluated by a panel consisting of Greg Dale, John Lewellen, Michael
Holloway, Kip Bishofberger, Anna Alexander, Tyler Thornton, and Henry Gaus, with further discussion
and advice from the contributors listed in the Acknowledgements section.

The evaluation was conducted by assessing the potential impact of the proposed changes across a variety
of factors, using the LAMP Conceptual Design as a baseline for comparison. Comparisons are made
between the conceptual design and the proposed change for each factor, and quantified using a 5-level scale
ranging from -2 (notably worse than the LAMP Conceptual Design) to +2 (notably improved from the
LAMP Conceptual Design). Each factor is assigned a relative weight, or importance; the product of the
scores and weights for each factor are then summed to provide an aggregate score assessing the proposed
change. A positive aggregate score indicates the proposed change is favored over the conceptual design; a
negative score indicates the conceptual design is preferred. A list of the factors, along with description and
weights used in this evaluation, is given below in Table 1.

Scores are averaged over all evaluators to provide a final score for the proposed change.

4 Comments, Concerns, Suggestions
This section presents additional comments, concerns and suggestions from the evaluation panel members
regarding the criteria in the evaluation table.

Mission and Beam Performance

  Ranking as neutral vs. the conceptual design, assuming this is independent from the 65 keV and no
 LFB discussion.

  Not anticipating a difference vs. conceptual design.

  Simulations show that transmission through MEBT and DTL improve, MEBT chopper specs are
 relaxed, and diagnostic acquisition can be placed near beamline for flavor-specific measurements.

Technical Maturity

  All the components are still the same.

  No additional CTEs. Designing for the MEBT chopper CTE becomes easier at lower beam energy
 since the beam will be easier to deflect.

Integration and Interface Risk

  Operating the RFQ at an energy below the threshold for neutron production will significantly
 simplify the accelerator electronics on and around the MEBT.

  Facility infrastructure (hand stacked shielding wall) will not need to be moved into a new
 configuration upon LAMP installation.

Safety and Authorization Basis Impact

  May reduce the need to remove and restack some existing shielding.

  Operating the RFQ at an energy below the threshold for neutron production will significantly
 simplify the shielding around the RFQ and MEBT and the subsequent interfaces with the personnel
 safety systems.

  Both designs are likely to require removing and restacking shielding. 2.1 MeV possibly avoids
 changing the radiation envelope.

Capital Cost

  Less power into the RFQ brings less expensive options for RF power into view, but this is not likely
 to be a significant advantage or disadvantage.

  Assessing as neutral, assuming same or similar cost when it is all said and done. Indeed, less power
 in the RFQ brings "cheaper" options into view, but not significant advantage or disadvantage.

  Reducing the RFQ output energy seems to reduce the length of the RFQ by ~ 33% while only
 requiring a smaller increase in the first DTL tank length. The net RF power reduction in the new
 design is also significant, likely resulting in significant reduction in additional RF infrastructure to
 support.

Lifecycle Cost: Operations and Maintenance

  Less neutrons produced in the MEBT increases lifetime of MEBT components.

  Maintenance of activated RFQ and MEBT components in the earlier design would be much more
 difficult and much more expensive.

Schedule Risk

  Reduced requirements for shielding and removed activation concerns with the new design will
 reduce the installation and commissioning duration.

  Delivery time for the RFQ is not likely to be too different based on this change.

Upgrade and Future Flexibility

  Ease of modifying LEBT components if not activated could result in an increased ability to support
 future performance upgrades or new operational modes.

  Don't have enough info to assess, for just changing the MEBT to 2.1 while keeping the LEBT at
 100.

Operability and Commissioning Complexity

  Not having to move the hand stack shielding will allow for the RSS devices to live in the MEBT
 allowing startup in the LEBT through MEBT to start before DTL is ready to receive beam.

  Removing the activation concern will allow easier access to the downstream RFQ and MEBT
 components which will simplify and improve commissioning.

Reliability and Maintainability (RAMI)

  Fewer neutrons produced in the MEBT increases lifetime of MEBT components.

  Removing the activation concern from the RFQ and MEBT regions will improve the reliability of
 nearby components, reduce failures, reduce maintenance and disposal difficulties, significantly
 improve access and reduce maintenance complexity, and improve operational robustness.

5 Conclusions
The proposed change is assessed as being generally beneficial to the Project, and is approved.
