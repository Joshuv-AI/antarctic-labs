On “discrete” solutions of the Euler system of gas dynamics
Anna Abbatiello, Eduard Feireisl

Abstract
The method of Convex Integration has revealed a number of rather disturbing facts concerning well-posedness of the Euler system of gas dynamics. In particular, there is a dense set of “wild” initial data, for which the problem admits infinitely many physically admissible (entropy) weak solutions. We identify the class of initial data enjoying the following properties: • they give rise to a family of weak solutions with increasing entropy profiles; • the solutions are “discrete”, meaning they attain only a finite number of constant states; • the solutions reach a prescribed terminal entropy profile when time goes to infinity.

Introduction

The Euler system of gas dynamics is
one of the simplest models in continuum mechanics governing
the time evolution of a compressible inviscid liquid like a gas.
It can be formally seen as the zero dissipation limit of problems describing
real fluids featuring viscosity and heat diffusion. Despite its apparent simplicity, the structure of solutions of the Euler system is still quite poorly understood, in particular in the multidimensional setting. Smooth solutions develop singularities in a finite time, while the problem is ill–posed in the larger class of weak solutions even if some
physically relevant admissibility criteria are imposed.

1.1
Euler system

The time evolution of the phase variables, the mass density
\varrho=\varrho(t,x)
, the momentum
{\bf m}={\bf m}(t,x)
, and the energy
E=E(t,x)

is described by means of a system of field equations:

Mass conservation:

\partial_{t}\varrho+{\rm div}_{x}{\bf m}=0;

(1.1)

Momentum balance:

\partial_{t}{\bf m}+{\rm div}_{x}\left(\frac{{\bf m}\otimes{\bf m}}{\varrho}\right)+\nabla_{x}p=0;

(1.2)

Energy balance:

\partial_{t}E+{\rm div}_{x}\left[\left(E+p\right)\frac{{\bf m}}{\varrho}\right]=0,

(1.3)

where
p
is the pressure. In addition, we introduce the absolute temperature

\vartheta
, the internal energy
e
, and the entropy
s
through Gibbs’ relation

\vartheta Ds=De+pD\left(\frac{1}{\varrho}\right).

(1.4)

It is convenient to pass to new state variables
(\varrho,s,{\bf m})
, and write

\displaystyle p=p(\varrho,s),\ E=E(\varrho,s,{\bf m})=\frac{1}{2}\frac{|{\bf m}|^{2}}{\varrho}+\varrho e(\varrho,s).

(1.5)

Finally, the Second Law of Thermodynamics is enforced through
entropy inequality:

\partial_{t}(\varrho s)+{\rm div}_{x}(s{\bf m})\geq 0.

(1.6)

We suppose the fluid is confined to a bounded domain
\Omega\subset R^{d}
,
d=2,3
, and we consider the time evolution of the system for
t\in[0,T)
,
T\leq\infty
. Furthermore, we assume the boundary
\partial\Omega
is impermeable, meaning

{\bf m}\cdot{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega}=0,

(1.7)

where
{\bf n}
denotes the outer normal vector. The original state of the system is determined by the initial conditions

\varrho(0,\cdot)=\varrho_{0},\ {\bf m}(0,\cdot)={\bf m}_{0},\ s(0,\cdot)=s_{0}\ \Rightarrow\ E(0,\cdot)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{|{\bf m}_{0}|^{2}}{\varrho_{0}}+\varrho_{0}e(\varrho_{0},s_{0}).

(1.8)

Here and hereafter, we tacitly assume
\varrho_{0}>0
to avoid problems with vacuum zones.

The present state-of-the-art of the mathematical theory of the Euler system includes the following facts:

•

The Euler system is well–posed, locally in time, in the class of sufficiently regular initial data, see e.g. Benzoni-Gavage and Serre
[
3

or Schochet
[

.

•

Regular solutions may develop singularities in the form of shock waves appearing in a finite time, see e.g. the monograph of Smoller

[

]
.

•

Regular solutions may develop implosions (blow-up type singularities)
in a finite time, see Chen et al.
[

]
,
[

]
.

•

The theory of convex integration
developed in the context of fluid mechanics in the seminal work of De Lellis and Székelyhidi
[

and
[

]
,
revealed several rather surprising facts concerning well–posedness of the Euler system even in the class of

1.2
Discrete solutions of the Euler system

Luo, Xie, and Xin
[
17
]
showed that the simplified isentropic (barotropic)
Euler system admits weak solutions that may attain only a finite number of constant states. We call these solutions
discrete
. Our aim is to extend this result to the full Euler system of gas dynamics (
1.1
)–(
1.3
), where, in addition, the method will generate a family of solutions with increasing entropy production rate. More specifically, our goal is to show that
there is a set
I_{\varrho,s}
of initial densities and entropies
(\varrho_{0},s_{0})
dense in the

L^{p}-
topology enjoying the following properties:

•

For each
(\varrho_{0},s_{0})\in I_{\varrho,s}
, there exists
{\bf m}_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;R^{d})
such that the Euler system (
1.1
)–(
1.8
)
admits an infinite family of weak solutions
(\varrho^{\lambda},{\bf m}^{\lambda},s^{\lambda})_{\lambda\geq 0}
in
(0,T)\times\Omega
emanating from the initial data
(\varrho_{0},{\bf m}_{0},s_{0})
.

•

For each
\lambda\geq 0
, there exists a finite integer
K
such that

(\varrho^{\lambda},{\bf m}^{\lambda},s^{\lambda})(t,x)\in\left\{(\varrho_{k},{\bf m}_{k},s_{k})\Big|k=1,\dots,K\right\}\ \mbox{for a.a.}\ (t,x)\in(0,T)\times\Omega.

(1.9)

for some constant vectors
(\varrho_{k},{\bf m}_{k},s_{k})\in(0,\infty)\times R^{d}\times R
.

•

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varrho^{\lambda_{1}}s^{\lambda_{1}}(t,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\varrho^{\lambda_{2}}s^{\lambda_{2}}(t,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \mbox{for a.a.}\ t\in(0,T),

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varrho^{\lambda_{1}}s^{\lambda_{1}}\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\neq\int_{\Omega}\varrho^{\lambda_{2}}s_{\lambda^{2}}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \mbox{in}\ (0,T)

(1.10)

whenever
\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}
.

Property (
1.9
) means that the solutions are discrete - they attain only a finite number of states. Property (
1.10
) says that
these solutions fail to be maximal neither in DiPerna’s nor in Dafermos’ sense
as specified in Feireisl, Lukáčová, and Yu
[
15
]
.

Alternatively, instead of prescribing the initial distribution of the density
and the entropy, we may fix the initial density profile
\varrho_{0}
, together with the initial kinetic energy

E_{0,{\rm kin}}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{|{\bf m}_{0}|^{2}}{\varrho_{0}}.

Our final objective is to show that the same set of initial data give rise
to a family of solutions attaining a given terminal entropy profile
\widetilde{s}
, meaning

\varrho s(t,\cdot)\nearrow\widetilde{s}\ \mbox{as}\ t\to T.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the concept of admissible weak solution to the Euler system and state our main results.
Next, we reformulate the problem and reduce the task to the investigation
of the incompressible Euler system, see Section
3
. In Section
4
,
we adapt the result of Luo, Xie, Xin
[
17
]
to the transformed problem.
We complete the proof of the main results by constructing solutions with the increasing entropy production rate, and solutions with a given terminal entropy in Section
5
. The paper is concluded by Section
6
, where
the impact of the main results on the long time behaviour of the system is discussed, together with their admissibility in a larger class of
dissipative (measure–valued) solutions, see e.g. Feireisl et al.
[
14
]
.

2
Weak formulation and the main results

Throughout the whole paper, we consider solutions with positive density
\varrho
. In particular, all terms including
\varrho^{-1}
are well defined. For the sake of simplicity, we suppose the gas is
polytropic
, meaning

p=(\gamma-1)\varrho e,\ \mbox{with the adiabatic constant}\ 1<\gamma\leq\frac{5}{3}.

(2.1)

Definition 2.1

(
Admissible weak solution).

Let the initial data

\varrho_{0}\ \mbox{measurable},\ 0<\underline{\varrho}\leq\varrho_{0}(x)\leq\overline{\varrho}\ \mbox{for a.a.}\ x\in\Omega,\ {\bf m}_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;R^{d}),\ s_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;R^{d})

(2.2)

be given. We say that a trio
(\varrho,{\bf m},s)\in L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega;R^{d+2})
is
admissible weak solution
to the Euler system (
1.1
)–(
1.8
) if the following holds:

•

Absence of vacuum zones:

{\rm ess}\inf_{(0,T)\times\Omega}\varrho>0.

(2.3)

•

Mass conservation:

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\varrho\partial_{t}\varphi+{\bf m}\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi\Big]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t=-\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{0}\varphi(0,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

(2.4)

for any
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}([0,T)\times\overline{\Omega})
.

•

Momentum balance:

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left[{\bf m}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+\left(\frac{{\bf m}\otimes{\bf m}}{\varrho}\right):\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+p(\varrho,s){\rm div}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t=-\int_{\Omega}{\bf m}_{0}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}(0,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

(2.5)

for any
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in C^{1}_{c}([0,T)\times\overline{\Omega};R^{d})
,
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\cdot{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega}=0
.

•

Energy balance:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left[E(\varrho,s,{\bf m})\partial_{t}\varphi+\Big(E(\varrho,s,{\bf m})+p(\varrho,s)\Big)\frac{{\bf m}}{\varrho}\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}E(\varrho_{0},s_{0},{\bf m}_{0})\varphi(0,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

(2.6)

for any
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}([0,T)\times\overline{\Omega})
.

•

Entropy inequality:

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\varrho s\partial_{t}\varphi+s{\bf m}\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi\Big]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\leq-\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{0}s_{0}\varphi(0,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

(2.7)

for any
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}([0,T)\times\overline{\Omega})
,
\varphi\geq 0
.

Remark 2.2
.

Since no smothness of
\partial\Omega
is assumed, the boundary condition

{\bf v}\cdot{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega}=0

is understood in the sense

\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot{\bf v}\ \,{\rm d}{x}=-\int_{\Omega}\phi{\rm div}_{x}{\bf v}\ \,{\rm d}{x}

for any
\varphi\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
. Similarly, the spaces
C^{k}(\overline{\Omega})
are defined as restrictions of functions in
C^{k}(R^{d})
to

\overline{\Omega}
.

We are ready to formulate our first result.

Theorem 2.3

(
Discrete solutions).

Let
\Omega\subset R^{d}
,
d=2,3
be a bounded domain.

There exists a set
I_{\varrho,s}
of the initial data

(\varrho_{0},s_{0})\in I_{\varrho,s},

dense in
L^{q}(\Omega;(0,\infty))\times L^{q}(\Omega)
,
1\leq q<\infty
,
enjoying the following properties:

•

For any
(\varrho_{0},s_{0})\in I_{\varrho,s}
, there exists
{\bf m}_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;R^{d})
such that the Euler system admits infinitely many
admissible weak solutions
(\varrho^{\lambda},{\bf m}^{\lambda},s^{\lambda})_{\lambda\geq 0}
emanating from the initial data
(\varrho_{0},{\bf m}_{0},s_{0})
.

•

For each
\lambda\geq 0
, there exists a finite integer
K=K(\lambda)
such that

(\varrho^{\lambda},{\bf m}^{\lambda},s^{\lambda})(t,x)\in\left\{(\varrho_{k},{\bf m}_{k},s_{k})\Big|k=1,\dots,K\right\}\ \mbox{for a.a.}\ (t,x)\in(0,T)\times\Omega.

(2.8)

for some constant vectors
(\varrho_{k},{\bf m}_{k},s_{k})\in(0,\infty)\times R^{d}\times R
.

•

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varrho^{\lambda_{1}}s^{\lambda_{1}}(t,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\varrho^{\lambda_{2}}s^{\lambda_{2}}(t,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \mbox{for a.a.}\ t\in(0,T),

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varrho^{\lambda_{1}}s^{\lambda_{1}}\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\neq\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\lambda_{2}}s_{\lambda_{2}}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \mbox{in}\ (0,T).

(2.9)

whenever
\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}
.

Remark 2.4
.

It will be clear from the proof of Theorem
2.3
that the solutions satisfy also the renormalized version of the entropy inequality, namely

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\varrho Z(s)\partial_{t}\varphi+Z(s){\bf m}\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi\Big]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\leq-\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{0}Z(s_{0})\varphi(0,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

for any
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}([0,T)\times\overline{\Omega})
,
\varphi\geq 0
,
and any
Z\in C(R)
,
Z^{\prime}(s)\geq 0
.

Remark 2.5
.

As we shall see below, given
(\varrho_{0},s_{0})\in I_{\varrho,s}
, there exist infinitely many initial momenta
{\bf m}_{0}
satisfying Theorem
2.3
with an arbitrarily large norm. In particular, we have

|{\bf m}_{0}|=\sqrt{2\varrho_{0}\Lambda-\varrho_{0}dp(\varrho_{0},s_{0})},

for any positive
\Lambda
, for which

2\varrho_{0}\Lambda-\varrho_{0}dp(\varrho_{0},s_{0})>0.

Theorem
2.3
claims the existence of infinitely many weak solutions
for a dense set of initial densities and entropies. Our next result shows that there are infinitely many solutions
of the Euler system with a prescribed initial density
\varrho_{0}
and the kinetic energy
E_{0,\rm kin}
.
In particular, the initial kinetic energy need not be large as in Feireisl et al.
[
13
]
.

(

Let

for some constant vectors
(\varrho_{k},{\bf m}_{k},s_{k})\in(0,\infty)\times R^{d}\times R
.

•

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varrho^{\lambda_{1}}s^{\lambda_{1}}(t,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\varrho^{\lambda_{2}}s^{\lambda_{2}}(t,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \mbox{for a.a.}\ t\in(0,T),

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varrho^{\lambda_{1}}s^{\lambda_{1}}\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\neq\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\lambda_{2}}s_{\lambda_{2}}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \mbox{in}\ (0,T).

whenever
\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}
.

Under certain restrictions, the conclusion of Theorem
2.6
remains

\varrho_{0}

.

Corollary 2.7

(
Discrete solutions with prescribed initial energy profile).

Suppose
\Omega\subset R^{d}
is a bounded domain, where either
d=2
, or
d=3
and
\gamma<\frac{5}{3}
. Let
\Lambda>0
be a positive constant.

There exists a set
I_{\varrho,E}
dense in

,
where

J=\left(\Lambda;\frac{2}{d}\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\Lambda\right)

(\varrho_{0},E_{0})\in I_{\varrho,E}

,
{\bf m}_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;R^{d})

.

Note that thanks to the hypotheses in Corollary

is non–empty. Moreover, it is easy to check that

there exists
\Lambda>0
such that

Theorems
2.3
,
2.6
as well as Corollary

will be proved in

.

Our final result asserts the existence of solutions with a given terminal profile
\widetilde{s}
. For simplicity, we restrict ourselves to the

\displaystyle\underline{s}

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{s}(x)\leq\overline{s}\ \mbox{for all}\ x\in\overline{\Omega},

\displaystyle\left|\left\{x\in\overline{\Omega}\ \Big|\ \widetilde{s}\ \mbox{is not continuous at}\ x\right\}\right|_{d}=0,

(

3
Reformulation

Our first task is to reformulate the original problem in the Convex Integration framework. The procedure has already been used in
[
13
]
.

3.1
The case of prescribed initial density and entropy

Since the solutions claimed in Theorem
2.3
attain only a discrete set
of states, we focus on the
initial data
\varrho_{0}
,
s_{0}
enjoying the same property.
Given a bounded domain
\Omega\subset R^{d}
, we consider its decomposition
into a finite union of
N
subdomains,

\Omega_{n}\subset\Omega\ \mbox{for all}\ n=1,\dots,N,\ \Omega_{i}\cap\Omega_{j}=\emptyset\ \mbox{for}\ i\neq j,\ |\Omega\setminus\cup_{n=1}^{N}\Omega_{n}|_{d}=0,

(3.1)

together with piecewise constant initial data,

\displaystyle\varrho_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega),\ \varrho_{0}(x)

\displaystyle=\varrho_{0,n}>0\ \mbox{a positive constant in}\ \Omega_{n},

\displaystyle s_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega),\ s_{0}(x)

\displaystyle=s_{0,n}\in R\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega_{n},\ n=1,\dots,N.

(3.2)

The decomposition is arbitrary and no regularity of
\partial\Omega_{n}
is required.
Obviously, the set of initial data
(\varrho_{0},s_{0})
belonging to the
class (
3.1
), (
3.2
) is dense
in

L^{q}(\Omega;(0,\infty))\times L^{q}(\Omega)
for any finite
1\leq q<\infty
, modulo a suitable refinement of the sets
\Omega_{n}
.

Following the strategy of
[
13
]
we set

\displaystyle\varrho(t,x)

\displaystyle=\varrho_{0,n}\ \mbox{for all}\ t\in[0,T]\times\Omega_{n},\

\displaystyle s(t,x)

\displaystyle=s_{0,n}\ \mbox{for all}\ t\in[0,T]\times\Omega_{n},\ n=1,\dots,N.

(3.3)

To determine the momentum
{\bf m}
, we solve the problem:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{n}}{\bf m}_{n}\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\mbox{for all}\ \varphi

\displaystyle\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\overline{\Omega}_{n}),

(3.4)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{n}}\left[{\bf m}_{n}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+\left(\frac{{\bf m}_{n}\otimes{\bf m}_{n}}{\varrho_{0,n}}-\frac{1}{d}\frac{|{\bf m}_{n}|^{2}}{\varrho_{0,n}}\mathbb{I}\right):\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right]\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\mbox{for all}\ \boldsymbol{\varphi}

\displaystyle\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\overline{\Omega}_{n};R^{d}),

(3.5)

supplemented with the kinetic energy constraint

\frac{1}{2}\frac{|{\bf m}_{n}|^{2}}{\varrho_{0,n}}=\Lambda-\frac{d}{2}p(\varrho_{0,n},s_{0,n})\ \mbox{a.a. in}\ (0,T)\times\Omega_{n},

(3.6)

where
\Lambda
is a suitable constant independent of
n=1,\dots,N
chosen in such a way that

\Lambda-\frac{d}{2}p(\varrho_{0,n},s_{0,n})>0\ \mbox{for all}\ n=1,\dots,N.

(3.7)

Note carefully that the test functions in (
3.4
), (
3.5
) do not vanish on the boundary of
\Omega_{n}
. In particular, the momentum defined as

{\bf m}(t,x)={\bf m}_{n}(t,x)\ \mbox{for}\ t\in(0,T),\ x\in\Omega_{n},\ n=1,\dots,N,

(3.8)

satisfies the integral identities (
3.4
), (

4
Discrete solutions via Convex Integration

As already mentioned above, the method of Convex Integration provides
infinitely many
solutions
to problem (
3.4
)–(
3.6
), see Chiodaroli
[
7
]
. Here, we use a refined version of this result due to Luo, Xie and Xin
[
17
]
.
First we claim there exist
\frac{d(d+3)}{d}
vectors
{\bf m}^{\ell}_{n}\in R^{d}
,

\ell=1,\dots,\frac{d(d+3)}{d}
such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{|{\bf m}^{\ell}_{n}|^{2}}{\varrho_{0,n}}

\displaystyle=\Lambda-\frac{d}{2}p(\varrho_{0,n},s_{0,n}),

(4.1)

\displaystyle(0,0)

\displaystyle\in{\rm int}\left({\rm convex\ hull}\left[\left({\bf m}^{\ell}_{n};\frac{{\bf m}^{\ell}_{n}\otimes{\bf m}^{\ell}_{n}}{\varrho_{0,n}}-\frac{1}{d}\frac{|{\bf m}^{\ell}_{n}|}{\varrho_{0,n}}\right),\ \ell=1,\dots,\frac{d(d+3)}{d}\right]\right),

\displaystyle\mbox{in}\ R^{d}\times R^{d\times d}_{0,{\rm sym}},

(4.2)

see
[

, Lemma 3]
. The specific form of the vectors
{\bf m}^{\ell}_{n}
may depend on
the constants
\varrho_{0,n}
,
s_{0,n}
as well as on
\Lambda
.

Now, we report the following crucial result shown by Luo, Xie, and Xin
[
17
, Proposition 1]
.

Proposition 4.1

(
Convex integration).

Given
n=1,\dots,N
, problem (
3.4
), (
3.5
) admits infinitely many solutions
{\bf m}_{n}
satisfying

{\bf m}_{n}(t,x)\in\left\{{\bf m}^{\ell}_{n}\Big|\ \ell=1,\dots,\frac{d(d+3)}{2}\right\}\ \mbox{for a.a.}\ (t,x)\in(0,T)\times\Omega_{n},

(4.3)

with the constant vectors
{\bf m}^{\ell}_{n}
specified in (
4.1
), (
4.2
).

Remark 4.2
.

More precisely,
[
17
, Proposition 1]
is applied for

\underline{{\bf m}}=0
,
\underline{{\bf U}}=0
,
\varrho=\varrho_{0,n}
,
q=2\varrho_{0,n}\Lambda-d\varrho_{0,n}{p(\varrho_{0,n},s_{0,n})}
,
{\bf B}\equiv 0
in the notation
of
[
17
]
.

It is easy to see that (
4.1
), (
4.3
) imply (
3.6
). In particular, equation (
3.5
) can be written in the form

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{n}}\left[{\bf m}_{n}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+\left(\frac{{\bf m}_{n}\otimes{\bf m}_{n}}{\varrho_{0,n}}\right):\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+p(\varrho_{0,n},s_{0,n}){\rm div}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}-\frac{2}{d}\Lambda{\rm div}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right]\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t=0

(4.4)

for all
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\overline{\Omega}_{n};R^{d})
. Thus summing up
over
n
and using test functions with vanishing normal trace, we conclude

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left[{\bf m}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+\left(\frac{{\bf m}\otimes{\bf m}}{\varrho}\right):\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+p(\varrho,s){\rm div}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right]\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t=0

(4.5)

for all
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\overline{\Omega};R^{d})
,
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\cdot{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega}=0
.

In view of (
3.4
), it is a routine matter to check that

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\varrho\partial_{t}\varphi+{\bf m}\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi\Big]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t=-\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{0}\varphi(0,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

(4.6)

for any
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}([0,T)\times\overline{\Omega})
, and

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\varrho s\partial_{t}\varphi+s{\bf m}\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi\Big]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t=-\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{0}s_{0}\varphi(0,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

(4.7)

for any
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}([0,T)\times\overline{\Omega})
. Thus the entropy balance is satisfied as equality.

Finally, seeing that the total energy

E=\frac{1}{2}\frac{|{\bf m}|^{2}}{\varrho}+\varrho e(\varrho,s)

is piecewise constant, specifically,

E=\Lambda+\varrho_{0,n}e(\varrho_{0,n},s_{0,n})-\frac{d}{2}p(\varrho_{0,n},s_{0,n})\ \mbox{a.a. in}\ (0,T)\times\Omega_{n},\ n=1,\dots,N,

we may repeat the above arguments to deduce the energy balance

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left[E(\varrho,{\bf m},s)\partial_{t}\varphi+\Big(E(\varrho,{\bf m},s)+p(\varrho,s)\Big)\frac{{\bf m}}{\varrho}\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t=0

(4.8)

for any
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}((0,T)\times\overline{\Omega})
. Finally, seeing that
the total energy is constant, we may extend (
4.8
) to

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left[E(\varrho,{\bf m},s)\partial_{t}\varphi+\Big(E(\varrho,{\bf m},s)+p(\varrho,s)\Big)\frac{{\bf m}}{\varrho}\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t=0

(4.9)

for any
\varphi\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\overline{\Omega})
,
\varphi(0,\cdot)=\varphi(T,\cdot)
.

At this stage, one is tempted to conclude that
(\varrho,s,{\bf m})
is an admissible
weak solution of the Euler system. However, this is not true since, by virtue of (
4.5
),

{\bf m}\in C_{\rm weak}([0,T];L^{q}(\Omega;R^{d}))\ \mbox{for any}\ 1\leq q<\infty,\ {\bf m}(0,\cdot)={\bf m}(T,\cdot)=0,

while the initial energy

E(0,\cdot)=\Lambda+\varrho_{0}e(\varrho_{0},s_{0})-\frac{d}{2}p(\varrho_{0},s_{0})>\varrho_{0}e(\varrho_{0},s_{0}).

In other words, the (total) energy experiences a positive jump at the
initial time

Fortunately, by virtue of (

E(\varrho,s,{\bf m})(t,\cdot)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{|{\bf m}(t,\cdot)|^{2}}{\varrho}+\varrho e(\varrho,s)(t,\cdot)

for a.a.
t\in(0,T)
.
Thus performing a simple time shift, we have proved the following result.

5.1
Proof of Theorems
2.3
,
2.6
- solutions with a prescribed entropy production rate and initial kinetic energy

The idea is quite simple; we divide the time interval
(0,T)
into a finite number of segments and augment the entropy on each of them. Note that such a process is compatible with the entropy
inequality
(
2.7
).

Let

0=\tau_{0}<\tau_{1}<\dots<\tau_{M}=T,\ T\ \mbox{finite},\ \tau_{M}<\infty\ \mbox{if}\ T=\infty.

be a division of the time interval
[0,T)
, where

\tau_{1}
belongs to the subset of full measure in
(0,T)
, for which Proposition

4.3
guarantees the existence of a weak solution in the class
(
4.10
)–(
4.13
).
Let
the initial data
\varrho_{0}
,
s_{0}
, and
{\bf m}_{0}
as well as the constant
\Lambda

be the same as in Proposition
4.3
. Applying Proposition
4.3
, we obtain a weak solution of the Euler system in the space time domain
(0,\tau_{1})\times\Omega
satisfying (
4.10
)–(
4.13
).

Now, we repeat the whole construction elaborated in Section
4
on the
time interval
(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})
, with the initial data

\displaystyle\varrho(\tau_{1},\cdot)

\displaystyle=\varrho_{0},\ \varrho(\tau_{1},\cdot)=\varrho_{0,n}\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega_{n},\ n=1,\dots,N,

\displaystyle{\bf m}(\tau_{1},\cdot)

\displaystyle=0,

(5.1)

and

s(\tau_{1},\cdot)=\widehat{s},\ \ \mbox{for some}\ \widehat{s}=\widehat{s}_{0,n}\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega_{n},\ \widehat{s}_{0,n}>s_{0,n}\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega_{n}.

(5.2)

As pointed out above, the entropy profile
\widehat{s}
is compatible with the
entropy inequality.

Finally, we fix a constant
\widehat{\Lambda}
satisfying

\widehat{\Lambda}-\frac{d}{2}p(\varrho_{0,n},\widehat{s}_{0,n})>0\ \mbox{for all}\ n=1,\dots,N.

(5.3)

The arguments specified in Section
4
yield the existence of a weak solution on the
time interval
(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})
enjoying the properties (
4.10
)–(
4.13
), and with the initial momentum

{\bf m}(\tau_{1},\cdot)=0.

In particular, by virtue of (
4.11
), the two solutions can be concatenated
at the time
\tau_{1}

yielding a new solution in
(0,\tau_{2})\times\Omega

at level of the conservation mass, momentum, and entropy production as

{\bf m}
is weakly continuous at
t=\tau_{1}
. The new solution satisfies
the entropy inequality (
2.7
), with the entropy production rate

\sigma=\delta_{t=\tau_{1}}\varrho(\widehat{s}-s)>0,

specifically

\partial_{t}(\varrho s)+{\rm div}_{x}(s{\bf m})=\sigma\geq 0

in the sense of distributions in
(0,\tau_{2})\times\Omega
.

Finally, we have to make sure that the energy conservation (
2.6
) holds in the whole interval
(0,\tau_{2})
, meaning, the total energy must
equal the same constant on each set
\Omega_{n}
,
n=1,\dots,N
. In accordance with (
4.13
), this amounts to showing

\Lambda+\varrho_{0,n}e(\varrho_{0,n},s_{0,n})-\frac{d}{2}p(\varrho_{0,n},s_{0,n})=\widehat{\Lambda}+\varrho_{0,n}e(\varrho_{0,n},\widehat{s}_{0,n})-\frac{d}{2}p(\varrho_{0,n},\widehat{s}_{0,n})\ \mbox{for all}\ n=1,\dots,N.

(5.4)

Moreover, by virtue of (
2.1
), this is equivalent to

\Lambda-\widehat{\Lambda}=\left(1-\frac{d}{2}(\gamma-1)\right)\varrho_{0,n}\Big(e(\varrho_{0,n},\widehat{s}_{0,n})-e(\varrho_{0,n},{s}_{0,n})\Big)\ \mbox{for all}\ n=1,\dots,N,

where
\left(1-\frac{d}{2}(\gamma-1)\right)\geq 0
as long as
1<\gamma\leq\frac{2}{d}+1
.
In view of Gibbs’ relation (
1.4
), the internal energy
e
is a strictly
increasing function of the entropy
s
, and
there are two alternatives:

1.

d=3
,
\gamma=\frac{5}{3}
. We may consider
\widehat{\Lambda}=\Lambda
, and the entropy in the form

\widehat{s}_{0,n}={s}_{0,n}+\lambda,\ \lambda\geq 0

as long as the parameter
\lambda
satisfies

\Lambda>\frac{3}{2}p(\varrho_{0,n},s_{0,n}+\lambda).

2.

\widehat{\Lambda}=\Lambda-\lambda
,
\lambda>0
, where
\lambda
is a small positive parameter. The entropy
\widehat{s}
is then chosen as

\widehat{s}_{0,n}=\widehat{s}_{0,n}(\lambda),

solving the equation

\lambda=\left(1-\frac{d}{2}(\gamma-1)\right)\varrho_{0,n}\Big(e(\varrho_{0,n},\widehat{s}_{0,n}(\lambda))-e(\varrho_{0,n},{s}_{0,n})\Big)\ \mbox{for all}\ n=1,\dots,N,

\widehat{\Lambda}>\frac{3}{2}p(\varrho_{0,n},\widehat{s}_{0,n}).

In both cases, the procedure yields a family of solutions
(\varrho^{\lambda},{\bf m}^{\lambda},s^{\lambda})_{\lambda\geq 0}
claimed in Theorems
2.3
,
2.6
. The process can be continued up to
\tau_{M}=T
.
We have proved Theorems
2.3
,
2.6
.

To show Corollary
2.7

E=\frac{|{\bf m}|^{2}}{\varrho}+\varrho e(\varrho,s)=\Lambda-\frac{d}{2}p(\varrho,s)+\varrho e(\varrho,s)=\Lambda+\left(\frac{1}{\gamma-1}-\frac{d}{2}\right)p(\varrho,s).

Since

the discrete values of the total energy
E_{0,n}
in
\Omega_{n}
can be prescribed in terms

as long as
E_{0,n}>\Lambda
,
and the kinetic energy remains positive, meaning

Clearly, the above construction can be repeated for a different choice of the

(\tau_{i})_{i=1}^{I}
to obtain a large variety of solutions. For instance,
one could asymptotically approach a given total entropy profile
\Phi

t\in[0,T]\mapsto\int_{\Omega}\varrho s(t,x)\ \,{\rm d}{x}=\Phi(t)

In particular, the time

can be chosen arbitrarily close to

.
This observation yields the following corollary.

Corollary 5.1

,

identified in Theorems

[

]
.

Finally, we note that, by virtue of Proposition

, the problem on each interval

,

,
admits infinitely many solutions.

5.2
Proof of Theorem
2.9
- solutions with a prescribed terminal entropy profile

Similarly to the preceding part, we consider an (infinite) sequence of times

0=\tau_{0}<\tau_{1}<\dots\tau_{m}<T,\ t_{m}\to T\ \mbox{as}\ m\to\infty,

and concatenate solutions at the points
\tau_{m}
, m=1,2,…

Similarly to (
5.1
), we fix the initial data

\varrho(\tau_{m},\cdot)=\varrho_{0},\ \varrho(\tau_{m},\cdot)=\varrho_{0,n}\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega_{n},\ n=1,\dots,N,\ m=0,1,\dots,

and

{\bf m}(\tau_{n},\cdot)=0,\ m=1,2,\dots.

In addition, keeping in mind
d=3
,
\gamma=\frac{5}{3}
,
in particular,

p=\frac{2}{3}\varrho e,

we choose
\Lambda>0
so that

\Lambda-\frac{3}{2}p(\varrho_{0,n},s_{0,n})>0,\ n=1,\dots,N

Recalling formula (
5.4
) and the hypothesis
d=3
,
\gamma=\frac{5}{3}
, the constant
\Lambda
, and consequently the total energy will be the same at any time interval
\tau_{m},\tau_{m+1}
as long as the initial entropies are chosen so the
s(\tau_{m},\cdot)\leq\overline{s}
.

Finally, we define the entropy “initial data” at
\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\dots
by recursion.
In accordance with the hypotheses of Theorem
2.9
, we start with

s_{0}(x)=s_{0,n}\ \mbox{for}\ x\in\Omega_{n}\equiv\Omega_{0,n},\ n=1,\dots,N\equiv N(0),

s_{0}(x)<\widetilde{s}(x),\ \frac{3}{2}p(\varrho_{0}(x),\widetilde{s}(x))<\Lambda.

Next, for
m=1,2,\dots
, we define recursively a refinement of

\Omega
-decomposition:

•

\displaystyle\Omega_{m,n}

\displaystyle\subset\Omega,\ m=0,1,\dots,\ n=1,\dots,N(m),\ \Omega_{m,i}\cap\Omega_{m,j}=\emptyset\ \mbox{for}\ i\neq j,

\displaystyle|\Omega\setminus\cup_{n=1}^{N(m)}\Omega_{m,n}|_{d}

\displaystyle=0\ \mbox{for}\ m=1,2,\dots;

•

For any
\Omega_{m,n}
, there is
\Omega_{m-1,j}
such that

\Omega_{m,n}\subset\Omega_{m-1,j}

(5.7)

for any

•

\sup_{n=1,\dots,N(m)}{\rm diam}[\Omega_{m,n}]\to 0\ \mbox{as}\ m\to\infty.

(5.8)

Finally, we fix the initial data for the entropy,

s(\tau_{m},\cdot)=\inf_{y\in\Omega_{m,n}}\widetilde{s}(y)\ \mbox{for}\ x\in\Omega_{m,n},\ n=1,\dots,N(m).

Now, thanks to (

s(\tau_{m-1},\cdot)\leq s(\tau_{m},\cdot)\ \mbox{for all}\ m=1,2,\dots.

In particular, our choice of piecewise constant initial entropies is compatible

). We also note that

, whenever

, in particular the density remains continuous.

Finally, we have

\int_{\Omega}s(t,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}=\sum_{n=1}^{N(m)}(\inf_{\Omega_{n,m}}\widetilde{s})|\Omega_{m,n}|\ \mbox{for}\ t\in[\tau_{m},\tau_{m+1}),

\sum_{n=1}^{N(m)}(\inf_{\Omega_{n,m}}\widetilde{s})|\Omega_{m,n}|\to\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{s}\ \,{\rm d}{x}

as the profile
\widetilde{s}
is Riemann integrable. Seeing that the sequence

(s(\tau_{m},\cdot))_{m=1}^{\infty}
is non–decreasing, we conclude

\lim_{t\to T-}\|s(t,\cdot)-\widetilde{s}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}=0.

We have proved Theorem

.

6
Discrete weak solutions, admissibility, and the long time behaviour

We finish the paper by discussing the implications of the results in the
broader context of the theory of general dissipative measure–valued solutions
of the complete Euler system, see e.g.
[
14
, Part II, Chapter 5]
,
in particular their admissibility and the long time behaviour.
As confirmed by the results of the present paper, the Euler system in
the physically relevant multidimensional case is ill posed even in the class of
entropy admissible weak solutions.

6.1
General equations of state

The polytropic constraint (
2.1
) may be restrictive for certain models arising in astrophysics, where the pressure includes the effect of thermal radiation:

p(\varrho,\vartheta)=\varrho\vartheta+a\vartheta^{4},

see e.g. Battaner
[
2
]
. Revisiting the proof of Theorem
2.3
,
2.6
, specifically relation (
5.5
), we easily observe that it can be extended to the case

p(\varrho,s)=\sum_{i=1}^{I}p_{i}(\varrho,s),\ e(\varrho,s)=\sum_{i=1}^{I}e_{i}(\varrho,s),\ p_{i}(\varrho,s)=(\gamma_{i}-1)\varrho e(\varrho,s),

1<\gamma_{i}\leq\frac{2}{d}+1,\ i=1,\dots,I.

More general equations of state including a non–monotone dependence of
p

on the density
\varrho
can be handled case by case as long as Gibbs’ equation (
1.4
) is satisfied.

6.2
Measure valued solutions

Any weak solution
(\varrho,{\bf m},s)
can be associated with a mono–atomic measure valued solution

(t,x)\in(0,T)\times\Omega\to\delta_{(\varrho,{\bf m},s)(t,x)}\in\mathfrak{P}(R^{d+2}),

where the symbol
\mathfrak{P}
stands for the family of Borel probability measures. Moreover, it is easy to check that any
convex combination

of measure–valued solutions is a measure–valued solution (cf.
[
14
, Part II]
). Consequently, the quantity

(t,x)\in(0,T)\times\Omega\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{I}\mu_{i}\delta_{(\varrho^{\lambda_{i}},{\bf m}^{\lambda_{i}},s^{\lambda_{i}})(t,x)},\ \mu_{i}\geq 0,\ \sum_{i=1}^{I}\mu_{i}=1

where
(\varrho^{\lambda_{i}},{\bf m}^{\lambda_{i}},s^{\lambda_{i}})
are the solutions
claimed in Theorems
2.3
,
2.6
, provides an example of a (truly) measure–valued solution attaining only a finite number of constant values.

6.3
Admissibility

Several attempts have been made to restore well–posedness of the Euler system by imposing suitable
admissibility criteria
that go beyond the mere satisfaction of the entropy inequality (
1.6
).

As a matter of fact, (
1.6
) can be interpreted as

\partial_{t}(\varrho s)+{\rm div}_{x}(s{\bf m})=\sigma\geq 0,

(6.1)

where
\sigma
can be interpreted as a non–negative measure representing the

entropy production rate
.
There are two selection criteria based on the specific form of
\sigma
:

In his pioneering paper
[
12
]
, DiPerna introduced the following partial
ordering in the class of solutions of the Euler system,

\displaystyle(\varrho^{1},{\bf m}^{1},s^{1})

\displaystyle\prec_{DiP}(\varrho^{2},{\bf m}^{2},s^{2})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow_{\rm def}\qquad

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varrho^{1}s^{1}(t+,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\varrho^{2}s^{2}(t+,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \mbox{for all}\ t\in(0,T).

DiPerna conjectured that the
measure–valued
solutions of the Euler system that are maximal with respect to
\prec_{DiP}
must be weak solutions.
In view of Theorem
2.3
, in particular (
2.9
), we conclude that
the solutions claimed in Theorem
2.3

are not
maximal with respect
to
\prec_{DiP}
.

Dafermos
[
9
]
proposed a refined selection criterion based on partial ordering

\displaystyle(\varrho_{1},{\bf m}_{1},s_{1})

\displaystyle\prec_{Daf}(\varrho_{2},{\bf m}_{2},s_{2})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow_{\rm{def}}

\displaystyle\mbox{there exists}\ \tau\geq 0\ \mbox{such that}\ (\varrho_{1},{\bf m}_{1},s_{1})(t,\cdot)

\displaystyle=(\varrho_{2},{\bf m}_{2},s_{2})(t,\cdot)\ \mbox{for all}\ t\leq\tau,

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}^{+}}{\,{\rm d}t}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{2}s_{2}(\tau,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle>\frac{{\rm d}^{+}}{\,{\rm d}t}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{1}s_{1}(\tau,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}.

As we have shown in
[
15
]
, the measure valued solutions that are maximal with respect to
\prec_{Daf}
must be weak solutions. Unfortunately, the weak solutions claimed in Theorems
2.3
,
2.6

are not

maximal with respect to
\prec_{Daf}
. This can be seen in the proof
based on the choice of the time interval decomposition

\tau_{0}=0<\tau_{1}<\dots\tau_{M}=T
. Indeed we can always construct a solution based on a new decomposition
\tau^{\prime}_{0}=0<\tau^{\prime}_{1}<\dots
, where

\tau^{\prime}_{1}<\tau_{1}
.

Like most of their counterparts obtained via Convex Integration, the
“discrete” solutions discussed in the present paper are eliminated by selection criteria based on the maximality of the entropy production rate. Very roughly indeed, one may say that the same method can be used to produce a new solution that is “greater” than the original one. This fact can be interpreted in a positive way that these solutions might be
unphysical
.
Note that the selection criteria based on maximality of the entropy production
can identify a unique semigroup solution of the Euler system, see

[
4
]
,
[
15
]
.

6.4
Long–time behaviour

The weak solutions obtained in the present paper feature several
“pathological” properties in the asymptotic limit
t\to\infty
. To begin with, the initial density profile
\varrho_{0}
is conserved. Solutions obtained in
Theorem
2.3
conserve also the entropy profile
s_{0}
or its slight modification after the time
\tau_{1}
. Solutions claimed in Theorem
2.6

conserve even the initial distribution of the kinetic energy

\frac{1}{2}\frac{|{\bf m}|^{2}}{\varrho}

or modification after
\tau_{1}
.
These properties evidently suggest that the solutions are not physically realistic. Still, they are entropy admissible solutions of the Euler system of gas dynamics.

6.4.1
Time–periodic solutions

It is worth noting that the weak solutions obtained in Section
4
via
Proposition
4.1
are, in fact,
time periodic
entropy admissible weak solutions of the Euler system, cf. the energy balance (
4.9
).
Accordingly, we may deduce the following form of the result stated in Theorem

2.3
.

Theorem 6.1

(
Time periodic solutions).

Let
\Omega\subset R^{d}
,
d=2,3
be a bounded domain.

There exists a set
I_{\varrho,s}
of the initial data

(\varrho_{0},s_{0})\in I_{\varrho,s},

dense in
L^{q}(\Omega;(0,\infty))\times L^{q}(\Omega)
,
1\leq q<\infty
,
enjoying the following properties:

•

For any
(\varrho_{0},s_{0})\in I_{\varrho,s}
, there exists
{\bf m}_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;R^{d})
such that the Euler system admits infinitely many
admissible weak solutions
(\varrho^{T},{\bf m}^{T},s^{T})_{T>0}
emanating from the initial data
(\varrho_{0},{\bf m}_{0},s_{0})
.

•

For each
T>0
, there exists a finite integer
K=K(T)
such that

(\varrho^{T},{\bf m}^{T},s^{T})(t,x)\in\left\{(\varrho_{k},{\bf m}_{k},s_{k})\Big|k=1,\dots,K\right\}\ \mbox{for a.a.}\ (t,x)\in(0,T)\times\Omega.

for some constant vectors
(\varrho_{k},{\bf m}_{k},s_{k})\in(0,\infty)\times R^{d}\times R
.

•

The solutions are time-periodic with the period
T>0
starting from a positive time, say,
\tau_{1}>0
:

(\varrho^{T},{\bf m}^{T},s^{T})(t+T,\cdot)=(\varrho^{T},{\bf m}^{T},s^{T})(t,\cdot)\ \mbox{for any}\ t>\tau_{1}.

(6.2)

Remark 6.2
.

A similar result can be shown under the hypotheses of Theorem
2.6
. We leave the details to the interested reader.

It worth noting that the periodicity stated in (
6.2
) indeed holds for

any

t>\tau_{1}
as
\varrho^{T}
,
s^{T}
are strongly continuous
in
[\tau_{1},\infty)
, while
{\bf m}^{T}
is weakly continuous in
[\tau_{1},\infty)

ranging in
L^{q}(\Omega)
,
1\leq q<\infty
.

6.4.2
Solutions converging to equilibria

Revisiting the proof of Theorem
2.6
, we can show the existence of global in time weak solutions converging to equilibrium
{\bf m}=0
. For the sake of simplicity, we follow the hypotheses of Theorem
2.9
focusing on the case

d=3
,
\gamma=\frac{5}{3}
. Evoking the situation
handled in the proof of Theorem (
2.3
) we choose a sequence of time

\tau_{0}<\tau_{1}<\tau_{2}<\dots<\tau_{m}\to\infty
.
At each point
\tau_{m}
we choose the initial data for the entropy to be

s(\tau_{m},x)=s_{m,n}\ \mbox{for}\ x\in\Omega_{n},\ s_{m,n}\geq s_{m-1,n},

\Lambda-\frac{3}{2}p(\varrho_{n},s_{m,n})\searrow 0\ \mbox{\ }\mbox{as}\ m\to\infty\ \mbox{uniformly for}\ n=1,\dots,N.

Note that the quantity

\Lambda-\frac{3}{2}p(\varrho_{n},s_{m,n})=\frac{1}{2}\frac{|{\bf m}(x)|^{2}}{\varrho_{n}(x)},\ x\in\Omega_{n}.

represent the kinetic energy of the solution in the time interval

(t_{m},t_{m+1})
. As the choice of

\Lambda-\frac{3}{2}p(\varrho_{n},s_{m,n})

and the sequence
\tau_{m}\to\infty
was arbitrary, we have shown the following result.

Theorem 6.3

(
Convergence to equilibria).

Let
\Omega\subset R^{3}
, be a bounded domain, and let
\gamma=\frac{5}{3}
.

There exists a set
I_{\varrho,s}
of the initial data

(\varrho_{0},s_{0})\in I_{\varrho,s},

dense in
L^{q}(\Omega;(0,\infty))\times L^{q}(\Omega)
,
1\leq q<\infty
,
enjoying the following properties:

•

For any
(\varrho_{0},s_{0})\in I_{\varrho,s}
, there exists
{\bf m}_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;R^{d})
such that the Euler system admits infinitely many
admissible weak solutions
(\varrho^{\lambda},{\bf m}^{\lambda},s^{\lambda})_{\lambda>0}
in
(0,\infty)\times\Omega
emanating from the initial data
(\varrho_{0},{\bf m}_{0},s_{0})
.

•

For each
T>0
, there exists a finite integer
K=K(T,\lambda)
such that

(\varrho^{\lambda},{\bf m}^{\lambda},s^{\lambda})(t,x)\in\left\{(\varrho_{k},{\bf m}_{k},s_{k})\Big|k=1,\dots,K\right\}\ \mbox{for a.a.}\ (t,x)\in(0,T)\times\Omega.

for some constant vectors
(\varrho_{k},{\bf m}_{k},s_{k})\in(0,\infty)\times R^{d}\times R
.

•

For any profile

\chi\in C^{1}[0,\infty),\ \chi(t)>0,\ \chi^{\prime}(t)\leq 0,\ \chi(t)\to 0\ \mbox{as}\ t\to\infty,

there exist
\underline{\lambda}=\underline{\lambda}(\chi)
,
\overline{\lambda}=\overline{\lambda}(\chi)
, and a time
\tau=\tau(\chi)>0
such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{|{\bf m}^{\underline{\lambda}}|^{2}}{\varrho^{\underline{\lambda}}}(t,\cdot)

\displaystyle\leq\chi(t)\ \mbox{for a.a.}\ t>\tau,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{|{\bf m}^{\overline{\lambda}}|^{2}}{\varrho^{\overline{\lambda}}}(t,\cdot)

\displaystyle\geq\chi(t)\ \mbox{for a.a.}\ t>\tau,\ \frac{1}{2}\frac{|{\bf m}^{\overline{\lambda}}|^{2}}{\varrho^{\overline{\lambda}}}(t,\cdot)\to 0\ \mbox{as}\ t\to\infty.

(6.3)

Property (
6.3
) means that the solution approaches asymptotically the equilibrium state

(\varrho_{s},{\bf m}_{s},s_{s}),\ \varrho_{s}=\varrho_{0},\ {\bf m}_{s}=0,\ p(\varrho_{0},s_{s})=\frac{2}{3}\Lambda.

The rate of convergence can be arbitrarily fast/slow depending on the choice of
the profile
\chi
.

Remark 6.4
.

As matter of fact, there are solutions stabilizing to equilibrium in an arbitrarily short positive time
\tau
. Indeed it is enough to extend
the solutions claimed in Proposition
4.3
as

\varrho(t,\cdot)=\varrho_{0},\ {\bf m}(t,\cdot)=0,\ s(t,\cdot)=\widehat{s}(t,\cdot)\ \mbox{for}\ t\geq\tau,

where
\widehat{s}(t,\cdot)\geq s(\tau-,\cdot)
is chosen so that the total energy is conserved, meaning

\varrho_{0,n}e(\varrho_{0,n},\widehat{s})=\varrho_{0,n}e(\varrho_{0,n},s_{0,n})+\Lambda-\frac{d}{2}p(\varrho_{0,n},s_{0,n})=\Lambda>\varrho_{0,n}e(\varrho_{0,n},s_{0,n})\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega_{n},

cf. (
4.13
).
