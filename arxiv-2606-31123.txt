Symplectic leaves of meromorphic Hitchin systems
Jia Choon Lee and Sukjoo Lee

Abstract
. The moduli space of meromorphic Higgs bundles admits a Poisson structure due to the independent work of Bottacin and Markman. In this paper, we revisit the symplectic leaves of this Poisson structure for the tame case. We study the partial compactification of the restricted Hitchin map on the symplectic leaves to an algebraically completely integrable system. In particular, we show that such a partial compactification is realized by the moduli spaces of ξ → \vec{\xi} -parabolic Higgs bundles. These same moduli spaces also provide a symplectic resolution of the normalization of the closure of the corresponding symplectic leaves. Finally, we discuss connectedness results for the corresponding Betti moduli spaces under the tame non-abelian Hodge correspondence.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Overview

Higgs bundles were introduced by Hitchin in his study of the self-duality equations on Riemann surfaces
[
16
]
. One of the fundamental features of the moduli space of stable Higgs bundles is that it carries a natural holomorphic symplectic structure, and the Hitchin map defines an algebraically completely integrable system, now known as the Hitchin system
[
15
,
9
]
.

A natural generalization is obtained by allowing the Higgs field to have poles at marked points. Let
C
be a smooth projective curve and let
D\subset C
be a reduced effective divisor. For simplicity, only in this overview section, we discuss the case where
D
consists of a single point
p
. A meromorphic Higgs bundle is a pair
(E,\Phi)
, where
E
is a rank
r
vector bundle on
C
and
\Phi:E\to E\otimes K_{C}(D)
is an
\mathcal{O}_{C}
-linear map. We write
\mathrm{Higgs}^{s}(C,D)
for the moduli space of stable meromorphic Higgs bundles, which is also known as the meromorphic Hitchin system. Unlike the case without poles,
\mathrm{Higgs}^{s}(C,D)
does not carry a holomorphic symplectic structure on the whole space. Instead, it carries a holomorphic Poisson structure due to the independent work of Bottacin
[
7
]
and Markman
[
24
]
.

For a Poisson variety, a basic geometric problem is to understand its symplectic leaves. In the setting of meromorphic Higgs bundles, the residue at the pole provides the relevant local model. The residue
\mathrm{res}_{p}(\Phi)
is an element of
\mathfrak{gl}_{r}
. Identifying
\mathfrak{gl}_{r}\cong\mathfrak{gl}_{r}^{*}
under the trace pairing, the adjoint orbits in
\mathfrak{gl}_{r}
are the symplectic leaves of the standard Lie–Poisson structure and their induced symplectic forms are the Kirillov–Kostant–Souriau forms. This suggests that, on the global moduli space
\mathrm{Higgs}^{s}(C,D)
, the loci obtained by requiring the residue to lie in a fixed adjoint orbit
\mathcal{O}
should play the role of symplectic leaves. We denote these loci by
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)
.

Question 1.1
.

For an arbitrary adjoint orbit
\mathcal{O}
, is
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)
a symplectic leaf of
\mathrm{Higgs}^{s}(C,D)
?

Recall that a symplectic leaf of a Poisson variety is a maximal connected locally closed smooth subvariety tangent to the characteristic distribution of the Poisson structure. Up to the question of connectedness, this picture is already present in the work of Bottacin
[
7
, Theorem 4.7.5]
and Markman
[
24
, Corollary 8.10]
, especially for symplectic leaves of maximal rank. For the purposes of the present paper, we will need to keep track of the explicit infinitesimal description of
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)
in terms of characteristic distribution and to address the connectedness of
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)
to ensure that it is a single symplectic leaf.

Once the symplectic leaf is identified, a further question is to understand the Hitchin map restricted to it. It is shown in
[
7
, Section 4.7]
and
[
24
, Section 8.3]
that the Hitchin map on
\mathrm{Higgs}^{s}(C,D)
is a Poisson integrable system in a generalized sense. However, this does not immediately imply that its restriction to an arbitrary symplectic leaf is an algebraically completely integrable system in the sense that it is a proper flat Lagrangian fibration with generic fibers abelian varieties. There are two basic issues. First, when the symplectic leaf is not maximal rank, the corresponding spectral curves are typically singular, so the usual description of the generic Hitchin fibers as Jacobians of smooth spectral curves no longer applies directly. Second, if the symplectic leaf is not closed in
\mathrm{Higgs}^{s}(C,D)
, the restricted Hitchin map may fail to be proper.

Question 1.2
.

For an arbitrary adjoint orbit
\mathcal{O}
, can the restricted Hitchin map on
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)
be partially compactified to an algebraically completely integrable system?

This question is closely related to the ”Lax project” formulated by Boalch in
[
5
, Section 1]
. Boalch proposes to classify integrable systems admitting a Lax representation, meaning that it is isomorphic to a symplectic leaf of a meromorphic Hitchin system, with the Hamiltonians given by restricting the Hitchin map.
1
1

1

This framework also includes irregular meromorphic Higgs fields, which we do not consider in this paper.
However, Boalch restricts the program to a class of ”good” Lax representations, which correspond to closed symplectic leaves of maximal rank in our case, since a Poisson integrable system does not necessarily restrict to an algebraically completely integrable system on every symplectic leaf. Thus the present question can be viewed as complementary to the ”good” Lax representations by considering symplectic leaves of arbitrary rank.

There are natural candidates for such a partial compactification. First, one may take the closure of
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)
in
\mathrm{Higgs}^{s}(C,D)
. Second, one may replace the residue orbit
\mathcal{O}
by its closure
\overline{\mathcal{O}}
and consider the corresponding moduli space
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},s}(C,D)
. While these two constructions are not a priori the same, one of our results is that they coincide. However, this partial compactification is generally singular, reflecting the singularities of
\overline{\mathcal{O}}.

The key point is that the singularities of
\overline{\mathcal{O}}
admit a natural resolution by adding compatible flag data. This suggests replacing a meromorphic Higgs bundle with residue in
\overline{\mathcal{O}}
by a parabolic Higgs bundle with conditions on the flags determined by
\mathcal{O}
. This leads us to consider the moduli space of
\vec{\xi}
-parabolic Higgs bundles, whose smoothness was established previously in
[
20
,
21
]
, in order to resolve
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},s}(C,D)
. Moreover, these moduli spaces form algebraically completely integrable systems, providing an answer to the partial compactification question above.

As a byproduct, we obtain some topological consequences for certain Betti moduli spaces via Simpson’s tame non-abelian Hodge correspondence
[
30
]
. In particular, we obtain connectedness results for moduli spaces of filtered local systems and for irreducible loci of character varieties with prescribed local monodromy.

1.2.
Main results

Let
C
be a smooth projective complex curve of genus
g
, and let
D=p_{1}+\cdots+p_{n}
be a reduced effective divisor. We fix a rank
r
and a degree
d
. To each
p_{i}
, we assign a conjugacy class
\mathcal{O}_{i}
of
\mathfrak{gl}_{r}
and its closure
\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{i}
. We set
\mathcal{O}:=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{O}_{i}
and
\overline{\mathcal{O}}:=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\overline{\mathcal{O}_{i}}
. Throughout this article, we always assume that for each
A_{i}\in\mathcal{O}_{i}
, the sum of traces is zero;

(1)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{tr}(A_{i})=0\quad\text{for }A_{i}\in\mathcal{O}_{i}.

Note that if there exists a meromorphic Higgs bundle
(E,\Phi)
with

\mathrm{res}_{p_{i}}(\Phi)\in\mathcal{O}_{i}
for all
i
, then the condition
(
1
) holds automatically by the residue theorem applied to
\mathrm{tr}(\Phi)
. As before, we write

\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)\subset\mathrm{Higgs}^{s}(C,D)
and

\mathrm{Higgs}^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},s}(C,D)\subset\mathrm{Higgs}^{s}(C,D)
for the corresponding stable
loci defined by the orbits
\mathcal{O}_{i}
and their closures
\overline{\mathcal{O}_{i}}
, respectively.
We use the analogous notation

\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},ss}(C,D)\subset\mathrm{Higgs}^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},ss}(C,D)\subset\mathrm{Higgs}^{ss}(C,D)
for the semistable moduli spaces.

Theorem 1.3

(Theorem
3.12
, Theorem
3.13
)
.

For a product of conjugacy classes
\mathcal{O}
satisfying the condition (
1
), if
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)
is non-empty, then it is irreducible and is a symplectic leaf of the Poisson variety
\mathrm{Higgs}^{s}(C,D)
whose closure in
\mathrm{Higgs}^{s}(C,D)
is
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},s}(C,D)
. In particular,
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},s}(C,D)
is irreducible.

The proof of Theorem
1.3
has an infinitesimal part and a global part. First, we give a self-contained deformation theoretic proof to show that the tangent space of
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)
agrees with the characteristic distribution of the Bottacin–Markman Poisson structure. For the global part, in order to identify the closure of
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)
and prove its connectedness, we use a comparison with the corresponding moduli space of
\vec{\xi}
-parabolic Higgs bundles.

For each
p_{i}\in D
, we fix a length
\ell_{i}
partition
{\underline{m}}_{i}=(m_{i,1},\dots,m_{i,\ell_{i}})
of
r
and a tuple
{\underline{\xi}}_{i}=(\xi_{i,1},\dots,\xi_{i,\ell_{i}})\in\mathbb{C}^{\ell_{i}}
. We write
\vec{m}=({\underline{m}}_{1},\dots,{\underline{m}}_{n})
and
\vec{\xi}=({\underline{\xi}}_{1},\dots,{\underline{\xi}}_{n})
. Then a parabolic Higgs bundle is a triple
(E,E_{D}^{\bullet},\Phi)
where
(E,\Phi)
is a meromorphic Higgs bundle and
E_{D}^{\bullet}
is a collection of flags
E_{p_{i}}^{\bullet}
of length
\ell_{i}
which are compatible with the restriction
\Phi|_{p_{i}}
. In particular, we call this triple
(E,E_{D}^{\bullet},\Phi)
a
\vec{\xi}
-parabolic Higgs bundle if the residue
\mathrm{res}_{p_{i}}(\Phi)
acts by scalar multiplication by
\xi_{i,a}
on each associated graded piece
E^{a-1}_{p_{i}}/E^{a}_{p_{i}}
for
a=1,\cdots,\ell_{i}
; see Definition
4.1
.

For a system of weights
\vec{\alpha}
, we write
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\text{par}}(C,D,\vec{m},\vec{\alpha})
(resp.
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\vec{\xi}\text{-par}}(C,D,\vec{m},\vec{\alpha})
)
for the moduli space of
\vec{\alpha}
-semistable parabolic (resp.
\vec{\xi}
-parabolic) Higgs bundles of type
\vec{m}
. By construction,
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\vec{\xi}\text{-par}}(C,D,\vec{m},\vec{\alpha})
is closed in
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\text{par}}(C,D,\vec{m},\vec{\alpha})
. We say
\vec{\alpha}
is
generic
if
\vec{\alpha}
-semistability implies
\vec{\alpha}
-stability.

Assumption 1.4
.

Throughout this article, we assume the existence of generic weights
\vec{\alpha}
and fix one such choice.

Remark 1.5
.

This assumption holds when the numerical data
(r,d,\vec{m})
is primitive; i.e.
\mathrm{gcd}(r,d,\{m_{i,a}\})=1
.

The moduli space
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\vec{\xi}\text{-par}}(C,D,\vec{m},\vec{\alpha})
has been studied in our previous works
[
20
,
21
]
and there are two results we would like to recall:

(1)

The moduli space
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\vec{\xi}\text{-par}}(C,D,\vec{m},\vec{\alpha})
is smooth
[
20
, Theorem 2.6]
.

(2)

We describe the spectral correspondence for
\vec{\xi}
-parabolic Higgs bundles
[
21
, Theorem 1.2]
. In particular, the restricted Hitchin map
h:\mathrm{Higgs}^{\vec{\xi}\text{-par}}(C,D,\vec{m},\vec{\alpha})\to A
factors through an affine subspace
B(\vec{m})_{\vec{\xi}}\subset A

[
21
, Corollary 1.4]
, and we write

h(\vec{m})_{\vec{\xi}}:\mathrm{Higgs}^{\vec{\xi}\text{-par}}(C,D,\vec{m},\vec{\alpha})\to B(\vec{m})_{\vec{\xi}}

for the induced Hitchin map. The affine subspace
B(\vec{m})_{\vec{\xi}}
is a linear system of curves contained in a given holomorphic symplectic surface obtained by blowing up the ruled surface
\mathbb{P}(K_{C}(D)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{C})
. Over the locus of smooth curves in
B(\vec{m})_{\vec{\xi}}
, the fibers are the corresponding Jacobian varieties.

By the work of Logares–Martens
[
23
]
, the ambient moduli space of parabolic Higgs bundles
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathrm{par}}(C,D,\vec{m},\vec{\alpha})
carries a natural Poisson
structure. We prove the following analogue of
Theorem
1.3
(a similar statement can also be found in
[
23
, Section 3.2.4]
and
[
3
, Proposition 7.1]
for real reductive groups).

Theorem 1.6

(Theorem
4.11
)
.

The moduli space
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\vec{\xi}\text{-par}}(C,D,\vec{m},\vec{\alpha})
is irreducible and is a closed symplectic leaf of the Poisson variety
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\text{par}}(C,D,\vec{m},\vec{\alpha})
.

Together with the spectral correspondence recalled above, the restricted Hitchin map yields the structure of an algebraically completely integrable system on
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\vec{\xi}\text{-par}}(C,D,\vec{m},\vec{\alpha})
.

Theorem 1.7

(Theorem
5.4
)
.

Let the notation be as above. The Hitchin map
h(\vec{m})_{\vec{\xi}}
forms an algebraically completely integrable system. In other words,
h(\vec{m})_{\vec{\xi}}
is a proper flat Lagrangian fibration over the affine base
B(\vec{m})_{\vec{\xi}}
whose generic fibers are abelian varieties.

The case
\vec{\xi}=0
corresponds to strongly parabolic Higgs bundles, which has been studied by Scheinost–Schottenloher
[
28
]
and Su–Wang–Wen
[
32
]
. It is also generalized to the
G
-parahoric setting by Baraglia–Kamgarpour–Varma
[
1
]
.

To relate
\vec{\xi}
-parabolic Higgs bundles with meromorphic Higgs bundles with fixed residue classes, we need the linear algebra fact: For each
i
, there is a one-to-one correspondence between the set of combinatorial data
({\underline{m}}_{i},{\underline{\xi}}_{i})
and the set of conjugacy classes
\mathcal{O}_{i}\in\mathfrak{gl}_{r}
, up to permutation, in the sense that every element of the orbit
\mathcal{O}_{i}
has a unique
compatible flag of type
({\underline{m}}_{i},{\underline{\xi}}_{i})
; see Appendix
A
for
details. Whenever
\mathcal{O}_{i}
is the conjugacy class determined by
({\underline{m}}_{i},{\underline{\xi}}_{i})
, we write

\mathcal{O}_{i}=\mathcal{O}({\underline{m}}_{i},{\underline{\xi}}_{i})
. We write
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}(\vec{m},\vec{\xi})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{O}_{i}
where

\mathcal{O}_{i}=\mathcal{O}({\underline{m}}_{i},{\underline{\xi}}_{i})
for every
i
. In this case, we say that

(\vec{m},\vec{\xi})
corresponds to
\mathcal{O}
. For such corresponding data,
(
1
) is equivalent to the following condition:

(2)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{a=1}^{\ell_{i}}m_{i,a}\xi_{i,a}=0.

On the level of the moduli spaces, we observe that there is a well-defined morphism by forgetting the flags

F:\mathrm{Higgs}^{\vec{\xi}\text{-par}}(C,D,\vec{m},\vec{\alpha})\longrightarrow\mathrm{Higgs}^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},ss}(C,D)

for small enough weights
\vec{\alpha}
(see Lemma
5.6
and Proposition
5.7
).

Proposition 1.8

(Proposition
5.8
)
.

The forgetful map
F
satisfies the following properties.

(1)

The image of
F
contains
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},s}(C,D)
and
F
is projective over this locus.

(2)

F
is an isomorphism over
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)\subset\mathrm{Higgs}^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},s}(C,D)
. Moreover, it is a symplectomorphism.

(3)

F
is compatible with Hitchin maps.

Hence, the introduction of
\vec{\xi}
-parabolic Higgs bundles serves two purposes. First, via the forgetful map
F
, the irreducibility of
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\vec{\xi}\text{-par}}(C,D,\vec{m},\vec{\alpha})
implies the irreducibility of
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)
and
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},s}(C,D)
. Second, combining Theorem
1.7
and Proposition
1.8
justifies that
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\vec{\xi}\text{-par}}(C,D,\vec{m},\vec{\alpha})
and its Hitchin map
h(\vec{m})_{\vec{\xi}}
provide a partial compactification of the restricted Hitchin map on
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)
to an algebraically completely integrable system. Moreover, under extra assumptions, the forgetful map
F
resolves the singularities of the normalization of the closure
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},ss}(C,D)
.

Theorem 1.9

(Theorem
5.11
)
.

Assume that
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)\neq\emptyset
and
F
is proper and surjective.
Then the normalization of
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},ss}(C,D)
has symplectic singularities and the forgetful map
F
yields a symplectic resolution. Moreover, this resolution is compatible with the Hitchin maps.

Note that when semistability coincides with stability, e.g. when
(r,d)=1
, then the properness and surjectivity required in Theorem
5.11
follow from Proposition
1.8
. We also note that symplectic resolutions of Higgs moduli spaces without poles have been studied by Kiem–Yoo
[
19
]
and Tirelli
[
33
]
. In our setting, we focus primarily on singularities coming from orbit closures of the residues.

1.3.
Applications to Betti moduli spaces

We finally explain a consequence of our connectedness theorem to the Betti side. Let

C^{\circ}=C\setminus D
. A filtered local system on
C^{\circ}
is a local system

\mathbb{L}
together with, for each
p_{i}\in D
, a decreasing left-continuous
filtration
\{\mathbb{L}_{\widetilde{p}_{i}}^{\beta}\}_{\beta\in\mathbb{R}}

on a nearby stalk, preserved by the local monodromy around
p_{i}
. Fix Betti-side
local data, called a residue diagram and denoted by

P^{\vec{\beta},\vec{\lambda}}_{\vec{m}}
, consisting of filtration jumps
\vec{\beta}
,
local monodromy eigenvalues
\vec{\lambda}
, and Jordan types on the associated
graded pieces; see Section
6
for details. We denote the corresponding
stable moduli space of filtered local systems by
\mathcal{M}^{s}_{\mathrm{Betti}}(P^{\vec{\beta},\vec{\lambda}}_{\vec{m}})
.

By Simpson’s tame non-abelian Hodge correspondence, recalled in
Theorem
6.5
, this Betti moduli space is homeomorphic to the stable
moduli space of parabolic Higgs bundles with the corresponding Dolbeault-side
residue diagram
P^{\vec{\alpha},\vec{\xi}}_{\vec{m}}
:

\mathcal{M}^{s}_{\mathrm{Dol}}(P^{\vec{\alpha},\vec{\xi}}_{\vec{m}})\simeq_{\mathrm{top}}\mathcal{M}^{s}_{\mathrm{Betti}}(P^{\vec{\beta},\vec{\lambda}}_{\vec{m}}).

In the case where the Dolbeault residue acts by a scalar on each parabolic
graded piece, the Dolbeault-side moduli space is precisely the moduli space of

\vec{\xi}
-parabolic Higgs bundles studied above. Therefore, the connectedness of
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\vec{\xi}\text{-par}}(C,D,\vec{m},\vec{\alpha})
(Theorem
1.7
)
induces the following connectedness result on the Betti side.

Theorem 1.10

(Theorem
6.6
)
.

Let
P^{\vec{\beta},\vec{\lambda}}_{\vec{m}}
be a Betti-side residue diagram whose
corresponding Dolbeault-side residue diagram, via Simpson’s table
1
, is of scalar graded
residue data of type
(\vec{m},\vec{\xi})
. Suppose that a generic system of parabolic weights exists as in Assumption
1.4
. If
\mathcal{M}^{s}_{\mathrm{Betti}}(P^{\vec{\beta},\vec{\lambda}}_{\vec{m}})

is nonempty, then it is connected.

A particularly important special case occurs when the Betti filtration is
trivial, i.e. when all filtration jumps are
\beta=0
. Then a filtered local
system is just an ordinary local system on
C^{\circ}
with prescribed local
monodromy, which is known as a character variety.

Let
\mathcal{C}=(\mathcal{C}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{C}_{n})
be a collection of conjugacy
classes in
GL_{r}(\mathbb{C})
satisfying the determinant condition

\prod_{i=1}^{n}\det(\mathcal{C}_{i})=1.

Assume moreover that, for each
i
, all eigenvalues of
\mathcal{C}_{i}
have the
same argument. Under Simpson’s table
1
, this is exactly the condition that the
compatible Dolbeault-side parabolic weights are constant at each puncture, say

\underline{\alpha}_{i}=(\alpha_{i,1})
. We write
\chi(C^{\circ},\mathcal{C})
for the
character variety of representations of
\pi_{1}(C^{\circ})
whose local monodromy
around
p_{i}
lies in
\mathcal{C}_{i}
, and
\chi(C^{\circ},\mathcal{C})^{\mathrm{irr}}

for its irreducible locus. Combining Theorem
1.3
with Theorem
6.5
, we obtain the following connectedness statement.

Theorem 1.11

(Theorem
6.7
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}=(\mathcal{C}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{C}_{n})
be
q
-indivisible such that
\prod_{i=1}^{n}\det(\mathcal{C}_{i})=1.
Assume that, for each
i
, all the eigenvalues of
\mathcal{C}_{i}
have a common argument. Then the irreducible locus

\chi(C^{\circ},\mathcal{C})^{\operatorname{irr}}

is connected whenever it is nonempty.

Connectedness of
\chi(C^{\circ},\mathcal{C})
for generic semisimple

\mathcal{C}
, and for curves of arbitrary genus, was established by
Hausel–Letellier–Rodriguez-Villegas
[
14
]
.
Theorem
1.11
extends this picture in some
directions by allowing the prescribed monodromy classes
\mathcal{C}_{i}
to be
nongeneric, under the common-argument assumption on the eigenvalues. In genus
zero, related connectedness results for certain nongeneric local monodromy data
were obtained by Shu
[
29
]
via multiplicative quiver varieties.

Finally, it is natural to ask whether the symplectic resolution constructed on the
Dolbeault side for the normalization of
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},ss}(C,D)
has a
Betti-side counterpart under the tame non-abelian Hodge correspondence. A Springer-type resolution of the character varieties with fixed
GL_{r}(\mathbb{C})
-conjugacy classes at the punctures, obtained by incorporating flags, already appears in the work of Letellier
[
22
]
. This question is related to Simpson’s isosingularity philosophy
[
31
,
33
]
and
to the Betti-side symplectic resolutions studied by Schedler–Tirelli
[
27
]
.

2.
Poisson geometry

In this article, we work over the field of complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
. We recall some standard facts on holomorphic Poisson structures. Let
X
be a smooth complex variety.

Definition 2.1
.

A holomorphic Poisson structure on
X
is a bivector

\pi\in H^{0}(X,\wedge^{2}T_{X})

such that
[\pi,\pi]=0
, where
[-,-]
denotes the Nijenhuis–Schouten bracket. Equivalently,
\pi
defines a bracket
\{-,-\}
on
\mathcal{O}_{X}
by
\{f,g\}:=\pi(df,dg)
, and this bracket is skew-symmetric, satisfies the Leibniz rule, and satisfies the Jacobi identity. A smooth variety equipped with a holomorphic Poisson structure is called a holomorphic Poisson variety.

The bivector
\pi
induces an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-linear map

\pi^{\sharp}:T_{X}^{*}\longrightarrow T_{X},\qquad\alpha\longmapsto\pi(\alpha,-).

The image of
\pi^{\sharp}
is called the characteristic distribution of the Poisson structure.

A holomorphic symplectic variety is the nondegenerate case of a Poisson variety. Indeed, suppose that
\pi^{\sharp}
is an isomorphism. Let
\omega^{\flat}:T_{X}\to T_{X}^{*}
be its inverse, and let
\omega\in H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{2})
be the corresponding nondegenerate skew-symmetric two-form. The closedness of
\omega
is equivalent to the Poisson condition for
\pi
:

d\omega=0\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad[\pi,\pi]=0.

Indeed, let
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
be local one-forms, and set
X=\pi^{\sharp}(\alpha)
,
Y=\pi^{\sharp}(\beta)
, and
Z=\pi^{\sharp}(\gamma)
. With the standard convention for the Nijenhuis–Schouten bracket, one has

\frac{1}{2}[\pi,\pi](\alpha,\beta,\gamma)=d\omega(X,Y,Z),

up to sign convention. Hence,
\omega\in H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{2})
is a nondegenerate closed holomorphic two-form and this is called a holomorphic symplectic form.

Definition 2.2
.

A symplectic leaf of a holomorphic Poisson variety
(X,\pi)
is a maximal connected locally closed smooth subvariety tangent to the characteristic distribution. Equivalently, it is a connected subvariety
L\subset X
such that
T_{x}L=\operatorname{Im}(\pi_{x}^{\sharp})

for every
x\in L
, and which is maximal with this property.

On each symplectic leaf
L
, the restriction of
\pi
is nondegenerate as a bivector on
L
. Its inverse is a holomorphic symplectic form on
L
. Thus a Poisson variety is foliated by symplectic leaves, whose dimensions may vary according to the rank of
\pi
.

We will use the following criterion for restricting a Poisson bivector to a smooth subvariety.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
(X,\pi)
be a holomorphic Poisson variety and let
Y\subset X
be a smooth subvariety. We say that
\pi
is tangent to
Y
if, for every
y\in Y
, the image of

\pi_{y}^{\sharp}:T_{y}^{*}X\to T_{y}X

is contained in
T_{y}Y
. Equivalently, the restriction
\pi|_{Y}
, originally a section of
\wedge^{2}T_{X}|_{Y}
, actually lies in
H^{0}(Y,\wedge^{2}T_{Y})
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
(X,\pi)
be a holomorphic Poisson variety, and let
Y\subset X
be a smooth subvariety. Suppose that along
Y
the map
\pi^{\sharp}
factors as

T_{X}^{*}|_{Y}\longrightarrow T_{Y}^{*}\xrightarrow{\ \sigma\ }T_{Y}\longrightarrow T_{X}|_{Y},

where the first map is the natural restriction, the last map is induced by the inclusion
Y\subset X
, and
\sigma:T_{Y}^{*}\to T_{Y}
is an isomorphism. Then the two-form
\omega_{Y}
induced by the inverse
\sigma^{-1}
is closed. Hence
(Y,\omega_{Y})
is a holomorphic symplectic variety.

Proof.

The factorization implies that
\pi
is tangent to
Y
. Therefore
\pi
restricts to a bivector
\pi_{Y}\in H^{0}(Y,\wedge^{2}T_{Y})
, and the induced map
\pi_{Y}^{\sharp}:T_{Y}^{*}\to T_{Y}
is precisely
\sigma
. Hence
\pi_{Y}
is nondegenerate and we have the induced holomorphic two form.

Since
\pi
is Poisson,
[\pi,\pi]=0
. The Nijenhuis–Schouten bracket is compatible with restriction of tangent bivectors to a smooth subvariety, so
[\pi_{Y},\pi_{Y}]=0
. By the equivalence above for nondegenerate bivectors, this is equivalent to
d\omega_{Y}=0
.
∎

Example 2.5
.

Let
G
be a complex reductive group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. The dual vector space
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
carries the Lie–Poisson structure, whose symplectic leaves are the coadjoint orbits
[
8
, Section 1.3]
.

For
G=GL_{r}(\mathbb{C})
, we identify its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{r}
with
\mathfrak{gl}_{r}^{*}
by the nondegenerate trace pairing

\mathfrak{gl}_{r}\times\mathfrak{gl}_{r}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C},\qquad(A,B)\longmapsto\operatorname{tr}(AB).

Under this identification, the coadjoint action of
GL_{r}
on
\mathfrak{gl}_{r}^{*}
becomes the usual conjugation action on
\mathfrak{gl}_{r}
. Hence the coadjoint orbits are precisely the conjugacy classes in
\mathfrak{gl}_{r}
.

If
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathfrak{gl}_{r}
is the conjugacy class of
A
, then its tangent space at
A
is
T_{A}\mathcal{O}=[A,\mathfrak{gl}_{r}]
. Indeed, differentiating the conjugation action
g\cdot A=gAg^{-1}
at the identity gives
X\mapsto[X,A]
, which has the same image as
X\mapsto[A,X]
up to sign. We write
\omega^{\mathcal{O}}
for the induced symplectic form on
\mathcal{O}
. If tangent vectors are written as
[X,A]
and
[Y,A]
, then
\omega_{A}^{\mathcal{O}}([X,A],[Y,A])=\operatorname{tr}(A[X,Y])
.
For example, the trace-orthogonal complement of
T_{A}\mathcal{O}=[A,\mathfrak{gl}_{r}]
is the centralizer
Z_{\mathfrak{gl}_{r}}(A)
, since
\operatorname{tr}(B[A,X])=\operatorname{tr}([B,A]X)
.
Thus
B
annihilates
T_{A}\mathcal{O}
under the trace pairing if and only if
[B,A]=0
.

3.
Meromorphic Higgs bundles

We now introduce one of the main objects of this paper: the moduli space of meromorphic Higgs bundles with fixed residue conjugacy classes. For completeness, we begin in Section
3.1
by giving algebraic definitions of the relevant moduli spaces. We then discuss the Poisson geometry of these moduli spaces by studying infinitesimal deformations in Section
3.2
.

3.1.
Nitsure’s construction and residue conditions

We recall the GIT construction of the moduli space of
K_{C}(D)
-twisted Higgs bundles following Nitsure
[
26
]
.

For
N\gg 0
, every semistable
K_{C}(D)
-twisted Higgs bundle
(E,\Phi)
of rank
r
and degree
d
satisfies
H^{1}(C,E(N))=0
, and
E(N)
is globally generated. Hence

h^{0}(C,E(N))=\chi(E(N))=:P(N)

is independent of
(E,\Phi)
. Fix a vector space
V
of dimension
P(N)
.

Let
Q
be the relevant open subscheme of the Quot scheme parametrizing quotients

q:V\otimes\mathcal{O}_{C}(-N)\twoheadrightarrow E

such that
E
has rank
r
and degree
d
, and such that the induced map

V\xrightarrow{\sim}H^{0}(C,E(N))
is an isomorphism. Let

V\otimes\mathcal{O}_{C\times Q}(-N)\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{E}

be the universal quotient. Adding the Higgs field gives a parameter scheme
R
over
Q
whose points consist of a quotient

q:V\otimes\mathcal{O}_{C}(-N)\twoheadrightarrow E

together with a Higgs field

\Phi:E\to E\otimes K_{C}(D)
.
The group
G:=\mathrm{GL}(V)
acts on
R
by changing the framing. Since scalar matrices act trivially on the corresponding Higgs bundle, one may equivalently work with
\mathrm{PGL}(V)
.

Nitsure constructs a
G
-linearization such that the GIT-semistable points are precisely the slope-semistable Higgs bundles. Let
R^{ss}\subset R
and
R^{s}\subset R^{ss}
denote the semistable and stable loci. The coarse moduli space of semistable meromorphic Higgs bundles is

\mathrm{Higgs}^{ss}(C,D):=R^{ss}//G.

Its closed points correspond to S-equivalence classes of semistable Higgs bundles. The stable locus is the geometric quotient

\mathrm{Higgs}^{s}(C,D):=R^{s}/G\subset\mathrm{Higgs}^{ss}(C,D),

and its points correspond to isomorphism classes of stable meromorphic Higgs bundles.

Remark 3.1
.

As
\mathrm{Higgs}^{ss}(C,D)
and
\mathrm{Higgs}^{s}(C,D)
may not be connected, we follow the convention of
[
7
,
24
]
and restrict our focus to the connected component of the semistable locus containing
(E,\Phi)
where the underlying
E
is stable. By an abuse of notation, we will retain the notation
\mathrm{Higgs}^{ss}(C,D)
and
\mathrm{Higgs}^{s}(C,D)
for this component for the remainder of this paper.

We now impose residue conditions. Let
(\mathcal{E},\Phi_{\mathrm{univ}})
be a universal family of Higgs bundles on
C\times R
. A universal Higgs field
\Phi_{\mathrm{univ}}
on
R
has residues along the marked points. For each
p_{i}\in D
, restriction to
\{p_{i}\}\times R
gives a relative endomorphism

\mathrm{res}_{p_{i}}(\Phi_{\mathrm{univ}})\in\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{E}_{p_{i}}).

where
\mathcal{E}_{p_{i}}
is the restriction of
\mathcal{E}
to
\{p_{i}\}\times R
.
Equivalently, the residues define a
G
-equivariant section of the relative endomorphism bundle

\mathrm{res}_{D}:R\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{E}_{p_{i}}),

where
G
acts by conjugation on the fibers. On the other hand, let
P_{i}:=\operatorname{Fr}(\mathcal{E}_{p_{i}})\to R

be the frame bundle of
\mathcal{E}_{p_{i}}
. Thus
P_{i}
is a principal

\mathrm{GL}_{r}
-bundle, and we have a natural identification
\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{E}_{p_{i}})\cong P_{i}\times^{\mathrm{GL}_{r}}\mathfrak{gl}_{r}
,
where
\mathrm{GL}_{r}
acts on
\mathfrak{gl}_{r}
by conjugation.

Now let
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}_{1}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{O}_{n}
be a product of adjoint orbits, and let

\overline{\mathcal{O}}=\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{1}\times\cdots\times\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{n}

be the corresponding product of orbit closures satisfying the condition (
1
). Then we set

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{i}(\mathcal{E}_{p_{i}}):=P_{i}\times^{\mathrm{GL}_{r}}{\mathcal{O}}_{i}\subset\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{E}_{p_{i}}),\qquad\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{i}(\mathcal{E}_{p_{i}}):=P_{i}\times^{\mathrm{GL}_{r}}\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{i}\subset\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{E}_{p_{i}}).

Define

\displaystyle R^{\mathcal{O},ss}:=R^{ss}\cap\mathrm{res}_{D}^{-1}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{O}_{i}(\mathcal{E}_{p_{i}})\right),

\displaystyle R^{\mathcal{O},s}:=R^{s}\cap\mathrm{res}_{D}^{-1}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{O}_{i}(\mathcal{E}_{p_{i}})\right),

\displaystyle R^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},ss}:=R^{ss}\cap\mathrm{res}_{D}^{-1}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{i}(\mathcal{E}_{p_{i}})\right),

\displaystyle R^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},s}:=R^{s}\cap\mathrm{res}_{D}^{-1}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{i}(\mathcal{E}_{p_{i}})\right).

where
\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{O}_{i}(\mathcal{E}_{p_{i}})
is regarded as a relative
locally closed subset of
\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{E}_{p_{i}})
over
R
. Since
\mathcal{O}
and
\overline{\mathcal{O}}
are invariant under conjugation, these are
G
-invariant subschemes. We define

\displaystyle\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},ss}(C,D):=R^{\mathcal{O},ss}//G,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D):=R^{\mathcal{O},s}/G,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Higgs}^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},ss}(C,D):=R^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},ss}//G,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Higgs}^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},s}(C,D):=R^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},s}/G.

We record the basic topological properties of these moduli spaces.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let the notation be as above.

(1)

\mathrm{Higgs}^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},ss}(C,D)\subset\mathrm{Higgs}^{ss}(C,D)
and
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},s}(C,D)\subset\mathrm{Higgs}^{s}(C,D)
are closed.

(2)

\mathrm{Higgs}^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},s}(C,D)\subset\mathrm{Higgs}^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},ss}(C,D)
and
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)\subset\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},ss}(C,D)
are open.

(3)

\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)\subset\mathrm{Higgs}^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},s}(C,D)
is open.

Proof.

Since
\overline{\mathcal{O}}
is closed in
\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathfrak{gl}_{r}
, the space
R^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},ss}
is a closed
G
-invariant subscheme of

R^{ss}
. Since
\pi:R^{ss}\to\mathrm{Higgs}^{ss}(C,D)
is a good quotient, the image
of a closed
G
-invariant subset is closed. Thus

\mathrm{Higgs}^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},ss}(C,D)
is closed in
\mathrm{Higgs}^{ss}(C,D)
.

Intersecting with the open stable locus gives

\mathrm{Higgs}^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},s}(C,D)=\mathrm{Higgs}^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},ss}(C,D)\cap\mathrm{Higgs}^{s}(C,D)
.
Therefore
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},s}(C,D)
is closed in
\mathrm{Higgs}^{s}(C,D)
and open in

\mathrm{Higgs}^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},ss}(C,D)
. This proves (1) and the first statement of (2).

The same intersection argument gives

\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)=\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},ss}(C,D)\cap\mathrm{Higgs}^{s}(C,D)
,
so
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)
is open in
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},ss}(C,D)
. This proves the
second statement of (2).

Finally, each orbit
\mathcal{O}_{i}
is open in its closure
\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{i}
. Hence

\mathcal{O}=\prod_{i}\mathcal{O}_{i}
is open in
\overline{\mathcal{O}}=\prod_{i}\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{i}
. Therefore

R^{\mathcal{O},s}
is open in
R^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},s}
. Since the stable quotient is geometric,
this descends to an open inclusion

\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)\subset\mathrm{Higgs}^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},s}(C,D)
.
This proves (3).
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Although
R^{\mathcal{O},ss}
is open in
R^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},ss}
, it is generally not saturated
with respect to the good quotient map

R^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},ss}\to R^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},ss}//G
. Indeed, passing to an
S
-equivalent
polystable representative may move the residue from
\mathcal{O}
to the boundary

\overline{\mathcal{O}}\setminus\mathcal{O}
. Thus we do not claim that
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},ss}(C,D)
is
open in
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},ss}(C,D)
.

There is a well-defined Hitchin map

h:\mathrm{Higgs}^{ss}(C,D)\to A:=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{r}H^{0}(C,K_{C}(D)^{\otimes j})

defined by the coefficients of the characteristic polynomial of the Higgs field
\Phi
. We will revisit how we describe the base of the restriction over
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},ss}
in Section
5.1
.

We next explain why the orbit-closure loci are singular in general. We work on the open locus considered by Markman
[
24
]
, where the underlying vector bundle is stable and a level structure is chosen along
D
.

Let
\mathcal{U}_{D}
denote the moduli space of stable vector bundles equipped with a
D
-level structure. A point of
\mathcal{U}_{D}
is a pair
(E,\eta)
, where
E
is a stable vector bundle of rank
r
and degree
d
, and
\eta:E|_{D}\xrightarrow{\sim}V_{D}
is a framing along
D
. By Markman’s description,
T^{*}\mathcal{U}_{D}
parametrizes triples
(E,\Phi,\eta)
, where
(E,\eta)\in\mathcal{U}_{D}
and

\Phi\in H^{0}(C,\mathcal{E}nd(E)\otimes K_{C}(D))
.

Using the level structure, the residues of
\Phi
become matrices. Thus we obtain the residue map
\mu:T^{*}\mathcal{U}_{D}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}
,
where

\mathfrak{R}:=\left\{(A_{i})_{i}\in\bigoplus_{i}\mathfrak{gl}_{r}\ \middle|\ \sum_{i}\operatorname{tr}(A_{i})=0\right\}.

Here the subscript
(-)_{i}
denotes the
i
-th component of the direct sum. Explicitly,

\mu(E,\Phi,\eta)=\bigl(\eta_{i}\circ\mathrm{res}_{p_{i}}(\Phi)\circ\eta_{i}^{-1}\bigr)_{i}.

Lemma 3.4
.

The residue map
\mu:T^{*}\mathcal{U}_{D}\to\mathfrak{R}
is smooth.

Proof.

It is enough to show that
d\mu
is surjective at every point. Let

y=(E,\Phi,\eta)
. Since
E
is stable,
H^{0}(C,\mathcal{E}nd(E))=\mathbb{C}\cdot\mathrm{id}_{E}
. Consider the residue map for Higgs-field variations

\mathrm{res}_{D}:H^{0}(C,\mathcal{E}nd(E)\otimes K_{C}(D))\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{i}\mathcal{E}nd(E_{p_{i}}).

Its image is contained in the residue space

\mathfrak{R}_{E}:=\left\{(B_{i})_{i}\in\bigoplus_{i}\mathcal{E}nd(E_{p_{i}})\ \middle|\ \sum_{i}\mathrm{tr}(B_{i})=0\right\}.

We claim that
\mathrm{res}_{D}
is surjective onto
\mathfrak{R}_{E}
. Indeed, the residue sequence

0\to\mathcal{E}nd(E)\otimes K_{C}\to\mathcal{E}nd(E)\otimes K_{C}(D)\to\bigoplus_{i}\mathcal{E}nd(E_{p_{i}})\to 0

gives a connecting homomorphism
\delta:\bigoplus_{i}\mathcal{E}nd(E_{p_{i}})\longrightarrow H^{1}(C,\mathcal{E}nd(E)\otimes K_{C})
,
and
\operatorname{coker}(\mathrm{res}_{D})
identifies with
\operatorname{Im}(\delta)
.
By Serre duality and the trace pairing, the dual of
\delta
is the evaluation
map

H^{0}(C,\mathcal{E}nd(E))\longrightarrow\left(\bigoplus_{i}\mathcal{E}nd(E_{p_{i}})\right)^{*},\qquad s\longmapsto\bigl((B_{i})_{i}\mapsto\sum_{i}\mathrm{tr}(s(p_{i})B_{i})\bigr).

where
(-)^{*}
denotes the linear dual. By the assumption that
E
is stable,
H^{0}(C,\mathcal{E}nd(E))=\mathbb{C}\cdot\mathrm{id}_{E}
, hence the annihilator of

\operatorname{Im}(\mathrm{res}_{D})=\textrm{ker}(\delta)
is spanned by the functional
(B_{i})_{i}\longmapsto\sum_{i}\mathrm{tr}(B_{i})
. Thus

\operatorname{Im}(\mathrm{res}_{D})=\left\{(B_{i})_{i}\in\bigoplus_{i}\mathcal{E}nd(E_{p_{i}})\ \middle|\ \sum_{i}\mathrm{tr}(B_{i})=0\right\}=\mathfrak{R}_{E}.

Now vary only the Higgs field, keeping
E
and the framing
\eta
fixed:

\Phi_{\varepsilon}=\Phi+\varepsilon\psi
, where

\psi\in H^{0}(C,\mathcal{E}nd(E)\otimes K_{C}(D))
. Then
d\mu_{y}(\psi)=\bigl(\eta_{i}\circ\mathrm{res}_{p_{i}}(\psi)\circ\eta_{i}^{-1}\bigr)_{i}
. Conjugation by the framings
\eta_{i}
identifies
\mathfrak{R}_{E}
with

\mathfrak{R}
. Since
\mathrm{res}_{D}
is surjective onto
\mathfrak{R}_{E}
, the image of

d\mu_{y}
contains all of
\mathfrak{R}
. Hence
d\mu_{y}
is surjective.
∎

As before, take
\mathcal{O}
and its closure
\overline{\mathcal{O}}
satisfying the residue condition (
1
). Note that the singular locus of
\overline{\mathcal{O}}
, denoted by
\mathrm{Sing}(\overline{\mathcal{O}})
, is given by
\overline{\mathcal{O}}\setminus\mathcal{O}
(see
[
18
,
25
,
11
]
and references therein). Define

(T^{*}\mathcal{U}_{D})^{\overline{\mathcal{O}}}:=\mu^{-1}(\overline{\mathcal{O}})
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
y=(E,\Phi,\eta)\in(T^{*}\mathcal{U}_{D})^{\overline{\mathcal{O}}}
, and write
A=\mu(y)
. If
A\in\operatorname{Sing}(\overline{\mathcal{O}})
, then
(T^{*}\mathcal{U}_{D})^{\overline{\mathcal{O}}}
is singular at
y
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.4
,
\mu
is smooth at
y
. Since

(T^{*}\mathcal{U}_{D})^{\overline{\mathcal{O}}}=\mu^{-1}(\overline{\mathcal{O}})
, the formal local structure theorem for smooth morphisms gives a noncanonical isomorphism

\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{(T^{*}\mathcal{U}_{D})^{\overline{\mathcal{O}}},y}\simeq\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\overline{\mathcal{O}},A}[[t_{1},\ldots,t_{N}]]

for some
N
. If
A\in\operatorname{Sing}(\overline{\mathcal{O}})
, then
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\overline{\mathcal{O}},A}
is not regular. A formal power series ring over a nonregular complete local ring is again nonregular. Hence
(T^{*}\mathcal{U}_{D})^{\overline{\mathcal{O}}}
is singular at
y
.
∎

We now pass from
T^{*}\mathcal{U}_{D}
to the Higgs moduli. Let
G_{D}
be the level group acting on the framing
\eta
. The group
G_{D}
acts on
T^{*}\mathcal{U}_{D}
, and
\mu
is equivariant for the adjoint action. Since
\overline{\mathcal{O}}
is invariant under conjugation, the locus
(T^{*}\mathcal{U}_{D})^{\overline{\mathcal{O}}}
is
G_{D}
-invariant.

On the Higgs-stable locus, the
G_{D}
-action is free (see
[
24
, Lemma 6.7]
): an element fixing
(E,\Phi,\eta)
gives an automorphism of the stable Higgs pair
(E,\Phi)
preserving the level structure, hence is scalar, and the level structure kills the scalar. Therefore the quotient map is smooth, and the quotient identifies with the corresponding Markman open subset of
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},s}(C,D)
.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
(E,\Phi)\in\mathrm{Higgs}^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},s}(C,D)
be a stable Higgs bundle whose underlying vector bundle
E
is stable. Suppose that the residue
(\mathrm{res}_{p_{i}}\Phi)_{i}
belongs to
\operatorname{Sing}(\overline{\mathcal{O}})
. Then
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},s}(C,D)
is singular at
(E,\Phi)
.

Proof.

Choose a level structure
\eta
and set
y=(E,\Phi,\eta)
. Then
\mu(y)
is conjugate to the residue tuple and hence lies in
\operatorname{Sing}(\overline{\mathcal{O}})
. By Proposition
3.5
,
(T^{*}\mathcal{U}_{D})^{\overline{\mathcal{O}}}
is singular at
y
. Since the quotient map from the level-structured locus to the corresponding open subset of
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},s}(C,D)
is smooth, smoothness downstairs would imply smoothness upstairs, a contradiction.
∎

Example 3.7
.

Let
r=2
and
D=\{p\}
. Let
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
be the nilpotent cone, i.e. the closure of the nonzero nilpotent orbit. Writing

A=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&-a\end{pmatrix},

we have
\mathcal{N}=\{a^{2}+bc=0\}\subset\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
. Hence

\operatorname{Sing}(\mathcal{N})=\{0\}
. Therefore, if
E
is stable and
(E,\Phi)
is a stable meromorphic Higgs bundle with
\mathrm{res}_{p}(\Phi)=0
, then
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{N},s}(C,p)
is singular at
(E,\Phi)
. Formally locally, the singularity contains the factor
\mathbb{C}[[a,b,c]]/(a^{2}+bc)
, up to a formal smooth factor.

3.2.
The deformation theory

We study the infinitesimal deformation theory for
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)
and compare it with the characteristic distribution of the Bottacin–Markman Poisson structure on
\mathrm{Higgs}^{s}(C,D)

[
24
,
7
]
.

Let
\mathcal{E}=(E,\Phi)
be a stable meromorphic Higgs bundle. Consider the complex

C_{\text{mero},\mathcal{E}}^{\bullet}:\qquad\mathcal{E}nd(E)\xrightarrow{[\Phi,\cdot]}\mathcal{E}nd(E)\otimes K_{C}(D),

placed in degrees
0,1
. Then it is well known that
T_{\mathcal{E}}\mathrm{Higgs}^{s}(C,D)\cong\mathbb{H}^{1}(C_{\mathrm{mero},\mathcal{E}}^{\bullet})
.

Let
\mathcal{E}nd(E)^{\vee}
be a
\mathcal{O}_{C}
-linear dual of
\mathcal{E}nd(E)
. The trace pairing induces an isomorphism
\mathcal{E}nd(E)^{\vee}\cong\mathcal{E}nd(E)
. Then the
\mathcal{O}_{C}
-linear dual is identified with

(C_{\text{mero},\mathcal{E}}^{\bullet})^{\vee}:\qquad\mathcal{E}nd(E)\otimes K_{C}^{-1}(-D)\xrightarrow{-[\Phi,\cdot]}\mathcal{E}nd(E)

placed in degrees
-1,0
. We define the Serre-dual complex
D(C^{\bullet}_{\text{mero},\mathcal{E}}):=(C_{\text{mero},\mathcal{E}}^{\bullet})^{\vee}\otimes K_{C}[-1]
. Then Serre duality gives
T_{\mathcal{E}}^{*}\mathrm{Higgs}^{s}(C,D)\cong\mathbb{H}^{1}(D(C_{\text{mero},\mathcal{E}}^{\bullet}))
. In particular, there is a canonical inclusion map of complexes
D(C_{\text{mero},\mathcal{E}}^{\bullet})\to C_{\text{mero},\mathcal{E}}^{\bullet}
given by

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{E})(-D)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{E})\otimes K_{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{E})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{E})\otimes K_{C}(D).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces-[\Phi,\cdot]}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces id}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces-id\otimes\iota}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces[\Phi,\cdot]}

where
\iota:K_{C}\hookrightarrow K_{C}(D)
is a canonical inclusion. Then the induced morphism on the hypercohomology
\mathbb{H}^{1}(-)
gives so-called Bottacin–Markman Poisson map

\pi_{\mathcal{E}}^{\sharp}:T_{\mathcal{E}}^{*}\mathrm{Higgs}^{s}(C,D)\to T_{\mathcal{E}}\mathrm{Higgs}^{s}(C,D).

Now let
\mathcal{E}=(E,\Phi)\in\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)
and set

A_{i}:=\mathrm{res}_{p_{i}}(\Phi)\in\mathcal{O}_{i}
. Define

\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{O},\mathcal{E}}:=\left\{s\in\mathcal{E}nd(E)\otimes K_{C}(D)\ \middle|\ \mathrm{res}_{p_{i}}(s)\in T_{A_{i}}\mathcal{O}_{i}\text{ for all }i\right\}.

Since
T_{A_{i}}\mathcal{O}_{i}=[A_{i},\mathcal{E}nd(E)_{p_{i}}]
, we have a well-defined complex

C_{\mathcal{O},\mathcal{E}}^{\bullet}:\qquad\mathcal{E}nd(E)\xrightarrow{[\Phi,\cdot]}\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{O},\mathcal{E}},

placed in degrees
0,1
.

Proposition 3.8
.

There is a canonical identification
T_{\mathcal{E}}\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)\cong\mathbb{H}^{1}(C_{\mathcal{O},\mathcal{E}}^{\bullet})
.

Proof.

Choose an affine open cover
\mathfrak{U}=(U_{\alpha})
such that each marked point lies in a unique open set. A first-order deformation as a meromorphic Higgs bundle is represented by a Čech cocycle

(u_{\alpha\beta},v_{\alpha})\in\check{C}^{1}(\mathfrak{U},\mathcal{E}nd(E))\oplus\check{C}^{0}(\mathfrak{U},\mathcal{E}nd(E)\otimes K_{C}(D))

satisfying
\delta u=0
and
\delta v=[\Phi,u]
, where
\delta
is a Čech differential.

The deformation preserves the residue orbit condition if and only if, for every
i
, the first-order residue variation
\mathrm{res}_{p_{i}}(v_{\alpha})
lies in
T_{A_{i}}\mathcal{O}_{i}
on the open set containing
p_{i}
. This is exactly the condition that
v_{\alpha}
is a section of
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{O},\mathcal{E}}
. Therefore first-order deformations inside
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)
are governed by
C_{\mathcal{O},\mathcal{E}}^{\bullet}
, and the tangent space is its first hypercohomology.
∎

Next, we describe the Serre-dual complex
D(C^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{O},\mathcal{E}})
. Note that by definition,
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{O},\mathcal{E}}
is locally free and satisfies

\mathcal{E}nd(E)\otimes K_{C}\subset\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{O},\mathcal{E}}\subset\mathcal{E}nd(E)\otimes K_{C}(D).

By taking the
\mathcal{O}_{C}
-linear dual, we have

\mathcal{K}^{\vee}_{\mathcal{O},\mathcal{E}}\otimes K_{C}\to\mathcal{E}nd(E)^{\vee}

which is an inclusion, because
\mathcal{K}^{\vee}_{\mathcal{O},\mathcal{E}}\otimes K_{C}
is torsion free, so any kernel must vanish. Since
\mathcal{E}nd(E)^{\vee}\cong\mathcal{E}nd(E)
under the trace pairing, we can identify
\mathcal{K}^{\vee}_{\mathcal{O},\mathcal{E}}\otimes K_{C}
as a subsheaf of
\mathcal{E}nd(E)
. More precisely, the trace pairing sends a section
s
in
\mathcal{E}nd(E)
to a local map
\mathcal{E}nd(E)\otimes K_{C}(D)\to K_{C}(D)
which restricts to
f:\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{O},\mathcal{E}}\to K_{C}(D)
. Then
f
is a section of
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{O},\mathcal{E}}^{\vee}\otimes K_{C}=\mathcal{H}om(\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{O},\mathcal{E}},K_{C})
if and only if the residues of
f(t)=\mathrm{tr}(s\cdot t)
are zero for all
t\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{O},\mathcal{E}}
. Recall from Example
2.5
that the orthogonal complement of
T_{A_{i}}\mathcal{O}_{i}
under the trace pairing is the centralizer of
A_{i}
, denoted by
Z_{\mathcal{E}nd(E)_{p_{i}}}(A_{i})
. Hence, we have

\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{O},\mathcal{E}}^{\vee}\otimes K_{C}=\left\{s\in\mathcal{E}nd(E)\ \middle|\ s(p_{i})\in Z_{\mathcal{E}nd(E)_{p_{i}}}(A_{i})\text{ for all }i\right\}

which will be denoted by
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{O},\mathcal{E}}
. Then the Serre-dual complex
D(C^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{O},\mathcal{E}})
is given by

D(C^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{O},\mathcal{E}}):\qquad\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{O},\mathcal{E}}\xrightarrow{-[\Phi,\cdot]}\mathcal{E}nd(E)\otimes K_{C}

placed in degrees
0,1
.

Proposition 3.9
.

The moduli space
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)
is smooth of dimension

\mathrm{dim}\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)=2r^{2}(g-1)+2+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{dim}\mathcal{O}_{i}.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{E}
is stable, we have
\mathbb{H}^{0}(C_{\mathcal{O},\mathcal{E}}^{\bullet})=\mathbb{C}\cdot\mathrm{id}_{E}
. By Serre duality, the obstruction space is given by
\mathbb{H}^{2}(C_{\mathcal{O},\mathcal{E}}^{\bullet})\cong\mathbb{H}^{0}(D_{\mathcal{O},\mathcal{E}}^{\bullet})^{\vee}
. A section
contributing to
\mathbb{H}^{0}(D_{\mathcal{O},\mathcal{E}}^{\bullet})
is a global endomorphism of

E
commuting with
\Phi
, with the required centralizer condition at the marked
points. By stability, such an endomorphism is scalar. Conversely, scalar
endomorphisms clearly define elements of
\mathbb{H}^{0}(D_{\mathcal{O},\mathcal{E}}^{\bullet})
.
Therefore
\mathbb{H}^{2}(C_{\mathcal{O},\mathcal{E}}^{\bullet})=\mathbb{C}\cdot\mathrm{id}_{E}
. Thus the trace-free obstruction space vanishes, and
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)
is
smooth. By applying the standard argument of taking the determinant of the Higgs bundles and the traceless part of the complex (see e.g.
[
20
, Corollary 2.11]
), it follows that the obstruction space for the traceless part of the complex vanishes, and hence,
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)
is
smooth.

It remains to compute the dimension. The quotient of

\mathcal{E}nd(E)\otimes K_{C}(D)
by the degree-one term of

C_{\mathcal{O},\mathcal{E}}^{\bullet}
is supported at
D
, and its fiber at
p_{i}
is
\mathcal{E}nd(E_{p_{i}})/T_{\mathrm{res}_{p_{i}}(\Phi)}\mathcal{O}_{i}
. Hence its length is
\sum_{i=1}^{n}(r^{2}-\mathrm{dim}\mathcal{O}_{i})
.
Therefore

\displaystyle\chi(C_{\mathcal{O},\mathcal{E}}^{\bullet})

\displaystyle=\chi(\mathcal{E}nd(E))-\chi(\mathcal{E}nd(E)\otimes K_{C}(D))+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(r^{2}-\mathrm{dim}\mathcal{O}_{i})

\displaystyle=r^{2}(1-g)-r^{2}(g-1+n)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(r^{2}-\mathrm{dim}\mathcal{O}_{i})

\displaystyle=2r^{2}(1-g)-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{dim}\mathcal{O}_{i}

where the second equality follows from Riemann–Roch theorem. Now the result follows from Proposition
3.8
and the identity
\mathrm{dim}\mathbb{H}^{1}(C^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{O},\mathcal{E}})=2-\chi(C^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{O},\mathcal{E}})
.
∎

Next, we describe a symplectic form on
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)
. By Serre duality and Proposition
3.8
, we have
T_{\mathcal{E}}^{*}\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)\cong\mathbb{H}^{1}(D(C_{\mathcal{O},\mathcal{E}}^{\bullet}))
. In particular, there is a canonical inclusion
D(C^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{O},\mathcal{E}})\hookrightarrow C^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{O},\mathcal{E}}
given by

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces-[\Phi,\cdot]}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces inc}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces-id\otimes\iota}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces[\Phi,\cdot]}

where both vertical maps are canonical inclusions. Indeed, any section

maps to

because the residue of

at

is zero. Note that this inclusion is an isomorphism away from

while at

Lemma 3.10
.

Let the notation be as above. Then the following holds.

(1)

(2)

For

where

For

.

For
\mathcal{E}\in\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)
, the image of the Bottacin–Markman Poisson map

is precisely
T_{\mathcal{E}}\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)
. Moreover, the induced nondegenerate bivector on
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)

defines a holomorphic symplectic form
\omega^{\mathcal{O}}
.

Theorem 3.12
.

If
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)\neq\emptyset
, then
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)
is connected. Moreover,
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},s}(C,D)
is irreducible and it becomes the closure of
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)
in
\mathrm{Higgs}^{s}(C,D)
, denoted by
\overline{\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)}
. In other words,

\overline{\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)}=\mathrm{Higgs}^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},s}(C,D).

We postpone the proof of Theorem
3.12
to the next subsection, since it uses the forgetful morphism from the corresponding
\vec{\xi}
-parabolic moduli space.

Theorem 3.13
.

The moduli space
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)
is a symplectic leaf of the Bottacin–Markman Poisson structure on
\mathrm{Higgs}^{s}(C,D)
.

Proof.

Let
L
be the symplectic leaf through a point of
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)
. By Corollary
3.11
, the tangent space of
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)
agrees with the characteristic distribution at every point. Hence
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)
is tangent to the characteristic distribution. Since it is connected by Theorem
3.12
, maximality of the symplectic leaf gives
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)\subset L
. Moreover, this inclusion is open, because both sides have the same tangent space along
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)
. To show that
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)=L
, it is enough to prove that
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)
is closed in
L
since
L
is connected by definition.

By Theorem
3.12
, the closure of
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)
is
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},s}(C,D)
. If

L\cap\left(\mathrm{Higgs}^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},s}(C,D)\setminus\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)\right)=\emptyset,

then
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)
is closed in
L
. Suppose otherwise, and let
\mathcal{E}
be an element of this intersection. Then there is a stratum
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\subset\overline{\mathcal{O}}\setminus\mathcal{O}
such that
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O}^{\prime},s}(C,D)
contains
\mathcal{E}
. Since
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
is a boundary stratum, we have
\mathrm{dim}\mathcal{O}^{\prime}<\mathrm{dim}\mathcal{O}
. Therefore

\mathrm{dim}\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O}^{\prime},s}(C,D)<\mathrm{dim}\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)=\mathrm{dim}L.

By Corollary
3.11
, the rank of the Poisson map at
\mathcal{E}
is
\mathrm{dim}\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O}^{\prime},s}(C,D)
, which is strictly less than
\mathrm{dim}L
. This contradicts the fact that the rank of the Poisson map is constant along the symplectic leaf
L
and equals
\mathrm{dim}L
. Hence
L
does not meet the boundary, so
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)
is closed in
L
.
∎

4.
\vec{\xi}
-parabolic Higgs bundles

In this section, we discuss the geometry of the moduli space of
\vec{\xi}
-parabolic Higgs bundles.

4.1.
\vec{\xi}
-parabolic Higgs bundles and their moduli

Definition 4.1
.

Fix local flag types
{\underline{m}}_{i}=(m_{i,1},\dots,m_{i,\ell_{i}})
and local eigenvalue data
{\underline{\xi}}_{i}=(\xi_{i,1},\dots,\xi_{i,\ell_{i}})
at each marked point
p_{i}\in D
satisfying the residue condition (
1
). A parabolic Higgs bundle on
(C,D)
consists of the following data:

•

a vector bundle
E
on
C
of rank
r
and degree
d
;

•

for each
i
, a flag

0=E_{p_{i}}^{\ell_{i}}\subset E_{p_{i}}^{\ell_{i}-1}\subset\cdots\subset E_{p_{i}}^{0}=E_{p_{i}},\qquad\mathrm{dim}(E_{p_{i}}^{a-1}/E_{p_{i}}^{a})=m_{i,a};

•

a meromorphic Higgs field
\Phi\in H^{0}(C,\mathcal{E}nd(E)\otimes K_{C}(D))
such that each residue preserves the corresponding flag.

In particular, we say that a parabolic Higgs bundle is a
\vec{\xi}
-parabolic Higgs
bundle if its residues satisfy the following condition

\textrm{gr}_{a}(\mathrm{res}_{p_{i}}\Phi)=\xi_{i,a}\mathrm{id}\qquad\text{for all }i,a.

Remark 4.2
.

The notion of
\vec{\xi}
-parabolic Higgs bundles is essentially a special case of parabolic Higgs bundles with fixed residue diagrams in the sense of Simpson
[
30
]
(see Example
6.3
). It has also appeared in several closely related forms in the literature; see
[
3
,
29
,
34
]
.

Example 4.3
.

When
\vec{\xi}=0
, the residue acts trivially on each graded piece, so one recovers the usual notion of a strongly parabolic Higgs bundle.

Choose parabolic weights
0\leq\alpha_{i,1}<\alpha_{i,2}<\cdots<\alpha_{i,\ell_{i}}<1
for
1\leq i\leq n
. If
F\subset E
is a saturated
\Phi
-invariant proper subbundle, the induced flag on
F_{p_{i}}
is defined by
F_{p_{i}}^{a}:=F_{p_{i}}\cap E_{p_{i}}^{a}.
Its parabolic degree is

\operatorname{pardeg}_{\vec{\alpha}}(F):=\textrm{deg}(F)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{a=1}^{\ell_{i}}\alpha_{i,a}\mathrm{dim}(F_{p_{i}}^{a-1}/F_{p_{i}}^{a}),

and the parabolic slope is

\operatorname{par\mu}_{\vec{\alpha}}(F):=\frac{\operatorname{pardeg}_{\vec{\alpha}}(F)}{\textrm{rank}(F)}.

A parabolic Higgs bundle is called
\vec{\alpha}
-semistable (resp.
\vec{\alpha}
-stable) if for every proper nonzero
\Phi
-invariant saturated subbundle
F\subset E
one has

\operatorname{par\mu}_{\vec{\alpha}}(F)\leq\,\operatorname{par\mu}_{\vec{\alpha}}(E)\qquad(\text{resp. }\operatorname{par\mu}_{\vec{\alpha}}(F)<\,\operatorname{par\mu}_{\vec{\alpha}}(E)).

Throughout, we choose
\vec{\alpha}
to be generic, so that semistability is
equivalent to stability. We denote by

\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathrm{par}}(C,D,\vec{m},\vec{\alpha})

(resp.
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\vec{\xi}\text{-par}}(C,D,\vec{m},\vec{\alpha})
) the moduli space
of
\vec{\alpha}
-semistable parabolic Higgs bundles
(resp.
\vec{\xi}
-parabolic Higgs bundles). Note that

is closed in

.

\bm{{\mathcal{H}}}(\vec{m})
given by
\lambda\cdot(E,E_{D}^{\bullet},\Phi)\mapsto(E,E_{D}^{\bullet},\lambda\Phi)
. We can also define a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on
\mathcal{N}(\vec{m})
by scaling. Then the morphism
P
is
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-equivariant. To show that
\bm{\mathcal{H}}(\vec{m})
is semi-projective, it is enough to show that
{\bm{\mathcal{H}}(\vec{m})}^{\mathbb{C}^{*}}
is proper and the limit
\lim_{\lambda\to 0}\lambda\cdot(E,\Phi,E^{\bullet}_{D})
exists for any
(E,E_{D}^{\bullet},\Phi)
.

First, for properness of
{\bm{\mathcal{H}}(\vec{m})}^{\mathbb{C}^{*}}
, note that

\bm{\mathcal{H}}(\vec{m})^{\mathbb{C}^{*}}=\mathcal{H}(\vec{m})_{\vec{0}}^{\mathbb{C}^{*}}\subset h(\vec{m})^{-1}_{\vec{0}}(0)\subset\mathcal{H}(\vec{m})_{\vec{0}}

where
h(\vec{m})_{\vec{0}}:\mathcal{H}(\vec{m})_{\vec{0}}\to B(\vec{m})_{\vec{0}}
is the restriction map over
\vec{0}\in\mathcal{N}(\vec{m})
and
0\in B(\vec{m})_{\vec{0}}
is the origin of the vector space
B(\vec{m})_{\vec{0}}
. By the properness of
h(\vec{m})_{\vec{0}}
, the closed subset
\mathcal{H}(\vec{m})_{\vec{0}}^{\mathbb{C}^{*}}
is proper and so is
\bm{\mathcal{H}}(\vec{m})^{\mathbb{C}^{*}}
.

For the existence of limit, let
(E,\Phi,E^{\bullet}_{D})\in\mathcal{H}(\vec{m})_{\vec{\xi}}
. Consider the image
L^{\circ}
of

\mathbb{C}^{*}\times\{(E,E^{\bullet}_{D},\Phi)\}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{*}\times\bm{\mathcal{H}}(\vec{m})\to\bm{\mathcal{H}}(\vec{m})

Clearly,
h(\vec{m})(L^{\circ})\subset\bm{B}(\vec{m})
has a limit at
0\in B(\vec{m})_{\vec{0}}\subset\bm{B}(\vec{m}).
Since
h(\vec{m})
is proper, it follows that
L^{\circ}
also has a limit i.e.
\lim_{\lambda\to 0}\lambda\cdot(E,\Phi,E^{\bullet}_{D})\in\mathcal{H}(\vec{m})_{0}
exists (cf.
[
36
, Theorem 5.2]
)
∎

Using Lemma
4.5
, we prove the following connectedness result.

Proposition 4.6
.

Under the same assumption, the moduli space
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\vec{\xi}\text{-par}}(C,D,\vec{m},\vec{\alpha})
is connected.

Proof.

Since the morphism
P:\bm{\mathcal{H}}(\vec{m})\to\mathcal{N}(\vec{m})
is
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-equivariant with positive weights on the base, we can apply the results in
[
13
, Theorem 7.2.1]
for any complex line in
\mathcal{N}(\vec{m})
passing through the origin. This implies that every fiber has the same cohomology. In particular
H^{0}(\mathcal{H}(\vec{m})_{0})\cong H^{0}(\mathcal{H}(\vec{m})_{\vec{\xi}})
. By the same argument used in
[
36
, Theorem 5.2]
, we get
\mathcal{H}(\vec{m})_{0}
is connected. Therefore, the conclusion follows.
∎

For later use, we record the flatness of the Hitchin map.

Lemma 4.7
.

With the notation as above, the morphism

{\bm{h}}(\vec{m}):\bm{\mathcal{H}}(\vec{m})\to\bm{B}(\vec{m})

is flat. In particular, for every
\vec{\xi}\in\mathcal{N}(\vec{m})
, the induced Hitchin map

h(\vec{m})_{\vec{\xi}}:\mathrm{Higgs}^{\vec{\xi}\text{-par}}(C,D,\vec{m},\vec{\alpha})\to B(\vec{m})_{\vec{\xi}}

is flat.

Proof.

The second statement follows from the first by base change along the inclusion

B(\vec{m})_{\vec{\xi}}\subset\bm{B}(\vec{m})
. Thus we prove the flatness of

\bm{h}(\vec{m})
.

We use miracle flatness. Since
\bm{\mathcal{H}}(\vec{m})
is smooth and
\bm{B}(\vec{m})
is
an affine space, it is enough to show that every fiber of
\bm{h}(\vec{m})
has the
expected dimension. By the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-equivariance of
\bm{h}(\vec{m})
, the fibers over
points in the same
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-orbit are isomorphic. Moreover, the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-orbit closure of
any point
(b,\vec{\xi})\in\bm{B}(\vec{m})=B(\vec{m})_{\vec{\xi}}\times\mathcal{N}(\vec{m})
contains
(0,\vec{0})
.
Therefore, by upper semicontinuity of fiber dimension,

\mathrm{dim}\bm{h}(\vec{m})^{-1}(b,\vec{\xi})\leq\mathrm{dim}\bm{h}(\vec{m})^{-1}(0,\vec{0}).

By
[
32
, Theorem 6.8]
, the central fiber has the expected
dimension:

\mathrm{dim}\bm{h}(\vec{m})^{-1}(0,\vec{0})=\mathrm{dim}\mathcal{H}(\vec{m})_{0}-\mathrm{dim}B(\vec{m})_{0}.

On the other hand, by the general lower bound for fiber dimensions,

\mathrm{dim}\bm{h}(\vec{m})^{-1}(b,\vec{\xi})=\mathrm{dim}h(\vec{m})_{\vec{\xi}}^{-1}(b)\geq\mathrm{dim}\mathcal{H}(\vec{m})_{\vec{\xi}}-\mathrm{dim}B(\vec{m})_{\vec{\xi}}.

By Lemma
4.9
and (
4
), the dimension formula of
B(\vec{m})_{\vec{\xi}}
, the right-hand side is
independent of
\vec{\xi}
and equals
\mathrm{dim}\mathcal{H}(\vec{m})_{0}-\mathrm{dim}B(\vec{m})_{0}
. Hence every fiber of
\bm{h}(\vec{m})
has the expected dimension, and the result follows from the miracle flatness.
∎

The deformation theory

We study the deformation-theoretic construction of the holomorphic symplectic form on

; see

Let
\mathcal{E}=(E,E_{D}^{\bullet},\Phi)
be a stable
\vec{\xi}
-parabolic Higgs bundle. Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}\!\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{E})

\displaystyle:=\{f\in\mathcal{E}nd(E)\mid f_{p_{i}}(E_{p_{i}}^{a})\subset E_{p_{i}}^{a}\ \text{for all }a,i\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{SP}\!\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{E})

\displaystyle:=\{f\in\mathcal{E}nd(E)\mid f_{p_{i}}(E_{p_{i}}^{a-1})\subset E_{p_{i}}^{a}\ \text{for all }a,i\}.

Consider the two-term complex

C_{\vec{\xi}\text{-par},\mathcal{E}}^{\bullet}:\qquad\mathcal{P}\!\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{E})\xrightarrow{\ [\Phi,\cdot]\ }\mathcal{SP}\!\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{E})\otimes K_{C}(D),

Lemma 4.8
.

There is a canonical identification

T_{\mathcal{E}}\mathrm{Higgs}^{\vec{\xi}\text{-par}}(C,D,\vec{m},\vec{\alpha})\cong\mathbb{H}^{1}(C_{\vec{\xi}\text{-par},\mathcal{E}}^{\bullet})
.

Proof.

An infinitesimal deformation consists of a deformation of the bundle and the quasi-parabolic flags, together with a deformation
\Phi+\varepsilon\dot{\Phi}
of the Higgs field preserving the deformed flags and the fixed graded residue eigenvalues. Infinitesimal automorphisms preserving the flags are sections of
\mathcal{P}\!\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{E})
, and their infinitesimal action on Higgs fields is
f\mapsto[\Phi,f]
. Since the graded residue eigenvalues are fixed, the induced infinitesimal deformation on each graded piece vanishes; equivalently,
\dot{\Phi}
lies in
\mathcal{SP}\!\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{E})\otimes K_{C}(D)
. The standard Čech deformation calculation (see e.g.
[
7
, Section 5]
) therefore gives the stated identification.
∎

The trace pairing induces a natural sheaf morphism

\mathcal{P}\!\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{E})\otimes\mathcal{SP}\!\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{E})\to\mathcal{O}_{C}(-D),\qquad(f,g)\longmapsto\mathrm{tr}(fg).

which is nondegenerate. Thus we have canonical identifications

\mathcal{P}\!\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{E})^{\vee}\cong\mathcal{SP}\!\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{E})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{C}(D)
and

\mathcal{SP}\!\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{E})^{\vee}\cong\mathcal{P}\!\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{E})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{C}(D)
, where
(-)^{\vee}
denotes the
\mathcal{O}_{C}
-linear dual. Then the Serre-dual complex of
C^{\bullet}_{\vec{\xi}\text{-par}}
,
D(C^{\bullet}_{\vec{\xi}\text{-par},\mathcal{E}})=(C^{\bullet}_{\vec{\xi}\text{-par},\mathcal{E}})^{\vee}\otimes K_{C}[-1]
, is given by

D(C^{\bullet}_{\vec{\xi}\text{-par},\mathcal{E}}):\qquad\mathcal{P}\!\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{E})\xrightarrow{\ -[\Phi,\cdot]\ }\mathcal{SP}\!\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{E})\otimes K_{C}(D),

placed in degrees
0,1
.

Lemma 4.9
.

The dimension of
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\vec{\xi}\text{-par}}(C,D,\vec{m},\vec{\alpha})
is

\mathrm{dim}\mathrm{Higgs}^{\vec{\xi}\text{-par}}(C,D,\vec{m},\vec{\alpha})=2r^{2}(g-1)+2+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(r^{2}-\sum_{a=1}^{\ell_{i}}m_{i,a}^{2}\right).

Equivalently,

\mathrm{dim}\mathrm{Higgs}^{\vec{\xi}\text{-par}}(C,D,\vec{m},\vec{\alpha})=2r^{2}(g-1)+2+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{dim}\mathcal{O}_{i},

where
\mathcal{O}_{i}=\mathcal{O}({\underline{m}}_{i},{\underline{\xi}}_{i})
.

Proof.

The argument goes parallel to the proof of Proposition
3.9
. By Lemma
4.8
, the tangent space is
\mathbb{H}^{1}(C_{\text{par},\mathcal{E}}^{\bullet})
. Since
\mathcal{E}
is
\vec{\alpha}
-stable,
\mathbb{H}^{0}(C_{\text{par},\mathcal{E}}^{\bullet})\cong\mathbb{C}
, and Serre duality gives
\mathbb{H}^{2}(C_{\text{par},\mathcal{E}}^{\bullet})\cong\mathbb{C}
. Hence

\mathrm{dim}\mathbb{H}^{1}(C_{\text{par},\mathcal{E}}^{\bullet})=2-\chi(C_{\text{par},\mathcal{E}}^{\bullet})
. At each marked point

,

\mathrm{dim}\mathcal{P}\!\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{E})_{p_{i}}=\sum_{a\leq b}m_{i,a}m_{i,b},\qquad\mathrm{dim}\mathcal{SP}\!\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{E})_{p_{i}}=\sum_{a<b}m_{i,a}m_{i,b}.

Using the exact sequences defining
\mathcal{P}\!\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{E})
and
\mathcal{SP}\!\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{E})
as subsheaves of
\mathcal{E}nd(E)
, together with Riemann–Roch for
\mathcal{E}nd(E)
and
\mathcal{E}nd(E)\otimes K_{C}(D)

\mathrm{dim}\mathbb{H}^{1}(C_{\text{par},\mathcal{E}}^{\bullet})=2r^{2}(g-1)+2+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(r^{2}-\sum_{a=1}^{\ell_{i}}m_{i,a}^{2}\right).

The final expression follows from the standard formula

\mathrm{dim}\mathcal{O}_{i}=r^{2}-\sum_{a}m_{i,a}^{2}
for the conjugacy class associated to

.
∎

Main results

5.1.
Integrable systems

In this section, we study integrable system structures for both
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)
and
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\text{par}}(C,D,\vec{m},\vec{\alpha})
. There are many different conventions for the definition of an integrable system. We first set this clearly.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
X
be a symplectic variety and
B
be a smooth variety such that
\mathrm{dim}X=2\mathrm{dim}B
. A morphism
h:X\to B
is called an algebraically completely integrable system (simply called integrable system) if
h
is a proper flat morphism whose generic smooth fibers are Lagrangian abelian varieties (see
[
9
, Definition 2.9]
).

Remark 5.2
.

In general, one may not need to require the properness of
h
. On the other hand, for the base
B
, it is often required to be affine (See
[
5
, Definition 1]
).

More generally, one can consider the case where
X
is Poisson, not necessarily symplectic, and
\mathrm{dim}X<2\mathrm{dim}B
. In this case, a generic fiber is required to be coisotropic, instead of Lagrangian. We call such
h:X\to B
a Poisson integrable system.

Since
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\vec{\xi}\text{-par}}(C,D,\vec{m},\vec{\alpha})
is a symplectic leaf of
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\text{par}}(C,D,\vec{m},\vec{\alpha})
(Theorem
4.11
) and
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)
is a symplectic leaf of
\mathrm{Higgs}^{s}(C,D)
(Theorem
3.13
), it is natural to ask whether the restriction of the Hitchin map
h
gives an integrable system. We first consider the
\vec{\xi}
-parabolic case.

Example 5.3
.

Both the parabolic Hitchin system
h:\mathrm{Higgs}^{\text{par}}(C,D,\vec{m},\vec{\alpha})\to A
and the meromorphic Hitchin system
h:\mathrm{Higgs}^{s}(C,D)\to A
are known to be Poisson integrable systems (see
[
23
]
for parabolic one and
[
24
]
for meromorphic one). However, it is worth noting that these results only imply that the generic symplectic leaves (corresponding to maximal orbits
\mathcal{O}
) are algebraically integrable integrable system.

Theorem 5.4
.

The Hitchin map

h(\vec{m})_{\vec{\xi}}:\mathrm{Higgs}^{\vec{\xi}\text{-par}}(C,D,\vec{m},\vec{\alpha})\to B(\vec{m})_{\vec{\xi}}

is an algebraically completely integrable system.

Proof.

Recall that we have a symplectic form
\omega^{\vec{\xi}\text{-par}}
on the smooth moduli space
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\vec{\xi}\text{-par}}(C,D,\vec{m},\vec{\alpha})
.

It remains to show that a generic fiber of the Hitchin map

h(\vec{m})_{\vec{\xi}}:\mathrm{Higgs}^{\vec{\xi}\text{-par}}(C,D,\vec{m},\vec{\alpha})\to B(\vec{m})_{\vec{\xi}}

is a Lagrangian abelian variety. Let
b\in B^{\mathrm{sm}}(\vec{m})_{\vec{\xi}}
be a point such that the corresponding spectral curve
\Sigma_{b}\subset S_{\vec{\xi}}
is smooth. By the spectral correspondence recalled above, the map
Q_{\vec{\xi}}:\mathrm{Higgs}^{\vec{\xi}\text{-par}}(C,D,\vec{m},\vec{\alpha})\hookrightarrow M_{S_{\vec{\xi}}}
identifies the Hitchin fiber
h(\vec{m})_{\vec{\xi}}^{-1}(b)
with the Jacobian
\textrm{Pic}^{d^{\prime}}(\Sigma_{b})
for the appropriate degree
d^{\prime}
. Therefore, a generic fiber is an abelian variety.

To check Lagrangian, first note that Lemma
4.9
and the description of the Hitchin base (
4
) imply that

\mathrm{dim}\mathrm{Higgs}^{\vec{\xi}\text{-par}}(C,D,\vec{m},\vec{\alpha})=2\mathrm{dim}B(\vec{m})_{\vec{\xi}}
. Therefore, it suffices to show that a generic fiber
h(\vec{m})_{\vec{\xi}}^{-1}(b)
is coisotropic. This follows from a minor modification of
[
23
, Proposition 3.12, 3.13]
. Indeed, we replace the spectral curve
X_{s}
used in
loc.cit.
by
\Sigma_{b}
. Then the same argument in the proof implies that
h(\vec{m})_{\vec{\xi}}^{-1}(b)
is coisotropic.

Finally, since the Hitchin map is proper (Proposition
4.4
) and flat (Lemma
4.7
), it is an algebraically completely integrable system.

∎

Next, we move on to
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)
. We first restrict the base of the Hitchin map as done in
\vec{\xi}
-parabolic Higgs bundles. For each orbit
\mathcal{O}_{i}
, let
({\underline{m}}_{i},{\underline{\xi}}_{i})
be the corresponding combinatorial data. By Theorem
A.3
, we have

X_{{\underline{m}}_{i},{\underline{\xi}}_{i}}(E_{p_{i}})=\overline{\mathcal{O}_{i}}
.
Therefore, if
\mathrm{res}_{p_{i}}(\Phi)\in\overline{\mathcal{O}_{i}}
, then there exists a flag
E_{p_{i}}^{\bullet}
of type
({\underline{m}}_{i},{\underline{\xi}}_{i})
compatible with
\mathrm{res}_{p_{i}}(\Phi)
. Choosing such a flag for each marked point
p_{i}
, we obtain a
\vec{\xi}
-parabolic Higgs bundle
(E,E_{D}^{\bullet},\Phi)
where
\vec{\xi}=({\underline{\xi}}_{1},\cdots,{\underline{\xi}}_{n})
. We regard as a parabolic lift of the meromorphic Higgs bundle
(E,\Phi)
.

As explained in Section
4.1
, the image of the Hitchin map of any such
\vec{\xi}
-parabolic Higgs bundle lies in the affine subspace
B(\vec{m})_{\vec{\xi}}\subset A
. Since the characteristic polynomial is independent of the choice of compatible flags, it follows that the original meromorphic Higgs bundle
(E,\Phi)
also satisfies
h(E,\Phi)\in B(\vec{m})_{\vec{\xi}}
. To emphasize that we are considering the meromorphic Hitchin map, we write
B^{\overline{\mathcal{O}}}
for
B(\vec{m})_{\vec{\xi}}
, that is where the locus of meromorphic Higgs bundles with residue condition
\mathrm{res}_{D}(\Phi)\in\overline{\mathcal{O}}
maps to under the Hitchin map.

Remark 5.5
.

A parabolic lift of
(E,\Phi)
is not unique in general. It is unique only over the open orbit locus, i.e. when
\mathrm{res}_{D}(\Phi)\in\mathcal{O}
. If
\mathrm{res}_{p_{i}}(\Phi)\in\overline{\mathcal{O}_{i}}\setminus\mathcal{O}_{i}
for some
i
, then there can be several compatible flags of type
({\underline{m}}_{i},{\underline{\xi}}_{i})
on

. This freedom is parametrized by the fiber of

over
\mathrm{res}_{p_{i}}(\Phi)
. Different choices of compatible flags may give different pure one-dimensional sheaves under the spectral construction, but their supports are the same; see

Suppose that

lies in a product of smaller strata

corresponding to the stratum

. With this choice, the same argument shows that the Hitchin image lies in

(see

). Hence, after this embedding, we may still regard the Hitchin image as lying in

•

For

For

5.2.
Parabolic lifts on the moduli spaces

In the previous section, we introduced a parabolic lift of a meromorphic Higgs bundle
(E,\Phi)
whose residue lies in
\overline{\mathcal{O}}
. We promote this on the level of moduli spaces by studying the stability conditions. The key input is that, for sufficiently small parabolic weights
\vec{\alpha}
, stability of the underlying meromorphic Higgs bundle forces stability of every compatible parabolic lift.

Lemma 5.6
.

There exists
0<\varepsilon_{0}<1
, depending only on
(r,\vec{m})
, such that for any system of parabolic weights
\vec{\alpha}=(\alpha_{i,a})
with
0<\alpha_{i,a}<\varepsilon_{0}
, and for any stable meromorphic Higgs bundle
(E,\Phi)
, every compatible
\vec{\xi}
-parabolic lift of
(E,\Phi)
is
\vec{\alpha}
-stable.

Proof.

Let
F\subset E
be a proper nonzero
\Phi
-invariant subbundle. Since
(E,\Phi)
is stable,

\delta(F):=\textrm{deg}(E)\textrm{rank}(F)-\textrm{deg}(F)\textrm{rank}(E)>0.

Since
\delta(F)
is an integer,
\delta(F)\geq 1
. For a compatible parabolic lift, set

n_{i,a}(F):=\mathrm{dim}(F_{p_{i}}^{a-1}/F_{p_{i}}^{a}),\qquad 0\leq n_{i,a}(F)\leq m_{i,a},

and

w_{\alpha}(F):=\sum_{i,a}\alpha_{i,a}n_{i,a}(F),\qquad w_{\alpha}(E):=\sum_{i,a}\alpha_{i,a}m_{i,a}.

The parabolic stability inequality is equivalent to

\delta(F)>\textrm{rank}(E)w_{\alpha}(F)-\textrm{rank}(F)w_{\alpha}(E).

Since the integers
n_{i,a}(F)
are bounded in terms of
\vec{m}
, there is a constant
M=M(r,\vec{m})
such that

\left|\textrm{rank}(E)w_{\alpha}(F)-\textrm{rank}(F)w_{\alpha}(E)\right|\leq M\cdot\max_{i,a}\alpha_{i,a}

for every proper nonzero
\Phi
-invariant subbundle
F
. Choose
\varepsilon_{0}<1/M
. Then the right-hand side has absolute value
<1
, while
\delta(F)\geq 1
. Hence the parabolic stability inequality holds for every such
F
.
∎

Unlike stable objects, it is not clear in general that every semistable meromorphic Higgs bundle admits a compatible semistable or stable
\vec{\xi}
-parabolic lift. Next, we introduce the map of forgetting parabolic structures. This can be seen more generally as a map between moduli spaces of parabolic Higgs bundles of different flag types (see e.g.
[
23
, Section 4]
and
[
29
, Section 3.5]
).

Proposition 5.7
.

Choose a system of parabolic weights
\vec{\alpha}
sufficiently small as in Lemma
5.6
. Then, for
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}(\vec{m},\vec{\xi})
, forgetting the flags defines a morphism

F:\mathrm{Higgs}^{\vec{\xi}\text{-par}}(C,D,\vec{m},\vec{\alpha})\longrightarrow\mathrm{Higgs}^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},ss}(C,D).

Proof.

Let
(E,E_{D}^{\bullet},\Phi)
be a stable
\vec{\xi}
-parabolic Higgs bundle. For every proper nonzero
\Phi
-invariant subbundle
F\subset E
, parabolic stability gives

\frac{\textrm{deg}F+w_{\alpha}(F)}{\textrm{rank}F}<\frac{\textrm{deg}E+w_{\alpha}(E)}{\textrm{rank}E}.

Equivalently,
\textrm{deg}(E)\textrm{rank}(F)-\textrm{deg}(F)\textrm{rank}(E)>\textrm{rank}(E)w_{\alpha}(F)-\textrm{rank}(F)w_{\alpha}(E)
.
By the smallness of the weights, the right-hand side has absolute value
<1
. Since the left-hand side is an integer, it is nonnegative. Hence the underlying meromorphic Higgs bundle
(E,\Phi)
is semistable. The residue condition
\mathrm{res}_{D}(\Phi)\in\overline{\mathcal{O}}
follows from Theorem
A.3
.
∎

Proposition 5.8
.

Under the assumptions of Proposition
5.7
, the forgetful map
F
satisfies the following properties.

(1)

\mathrm{Higgs}^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},s}(C,D)\subseteq\operatorname{Im}(F)
.

(2)

Over
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},s}(C,D)
, the morphism
F
is projective. Moreover, over
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)
it is an isomorphism and is compatible with the symplectic forms
\omega^{\vec{\xi}\text{-par}}
and
\omega^{\mathcal{O}}
.

(3)

F
is compatible with the Hitchin maps.

Proof.

The first statement follows from Theorem
A.3
and Lemma
5.6
. For a stable meromorphic Higgs bundle
(E,\Phi)\in\mathrm{Higgs}^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},s}(C,D)
, there exists at least one parabolic lift and Lemma
5.6
guarantees that such a lift is
\vec{\alpha}
-stable.

Next we prove projectivity over the stable orbit-closure locus. Fix a stable meromorphic Higgs bundle
(E,\Phi)
with residues
A_{i}=\mathrm{res}_{p_{i}}(\Phi)
in
\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{i}
for all
i
. The fiber of
F
over
(E,\Phi)
is given by

\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left\{F_{i}^{\bullet}\in\mathrm{Flag}_{{\underline{m}}_{i}}(E_{p_{i}})\ \middle|\ (A_{i}-\xi_{i,a}\mathrm{id})(F_{i}^{a-1})\subset F_{i}^{a}\text{ for all }a\right\}

because every
\vec{\xi}
-parabolic lift is
\vec{\alpha}
-stable. This is a closed subvariety of a product of partial flag varieties, hence projective. Globally, these fibers assemble into the relative incidence scheme inside the product of the universal flag bundles over
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},s}(C,D)
. Since the incidence conditions are closed,

is projective over
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},s}(C,D)
.

In particular, if
(E,\Phi)\in\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)
, there is a unique lift so that

is bijective over
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)
. Proposition
5.10
below shows that
dF
is an isomorphism over
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)
. Since both sides are smooth,
F
is étale over this locus. Hence,
F
is an isomorphism over this locus. The compatibility of symplectic structures is also proved in Proposition
5.10
.

Finally,
F
is compatible with the Hitchin maps because forgetting the parabolic flags does not change the Higgs field, and hence does not change its characteristic polynomial. Therefore the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Higgs}^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},ss}(C,D)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B(\vec{m})_{\vec{\xi}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B^{\overline{\mathcal{O}}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h(\vec{m})_{\vec{\xi}}}

∎

To compare deformation complexes
C_{\vec{\xi}\text{-par}}^{\bullet}
and
C_{\mathcal{O}}^{\bullet}
, we need the following linear algebra fact.

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
V
be a finite-dimensional complex vector space, and let

A\in End(V)
be an element of the conjugacy class
\mathcal{O}({\underline{m}},{\underline{\xi}})
. Let
0=F^{\ell}\subset F^{\ell-1}\subset\cdots\subset F^{1}\subset F^{0}=V

be the compatible flag of type
({\underline{m}},{\underline{\xi}})
. Set

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle:=\{\phi\in End(V)\mid\phi(F^{a})\subset F^{a}\text{ for all }b\},

\displaystyle SP

\displaystyle:=\{\phi\in End(V)\mid\phi(F^{a-1})\subset F^{b}\text{ for all }a\}.

Then
[A,P]=SP
. Moreover, if
\phi\in End(V)
satisfies
[A,\phi]\in SP
, then
\phi\in P
.

Proof.

First, it is clear that
[A,P]\subset SP
. We now compare dimensions. Since the compatible flag of
A
is canonical, every
endomorphism commuting with
A
preserves this flag. Thus the centralizer
Z(A)
of
A
lies in
P
. Hence

Z_{P}(A):=Z(A)\cap P=Z(A)
, and therefore

\mathrm{dim}[A,P]=\mathrm{dim}P-\mathrm{dim}Z_{P}(A)=\mathrm{dim}P-\mathrm{dim}Z(A).

For the conjugacy class
\mathcal{O}({\underline{m}},{\underline{\xi}})
, the centralizer dimension is
\mathrm{dim}Z(A)=\sum_{a=1}^{\ell}m_{a}^{2}
.
On the other hand,
P/SP
is naturally identified with

\bigoplus_{a=1}^{\ell}End(F^{a-1}/F^{a})
, so
\mathrm{dim}(P/SP)=\sum_{a=1}^{\ell}m_{a}^{2}.

Thus
\mathrm{dim}[A,P]=\mathrm{dim}SP
. Since
[A,P]\subset SP
, we obtain
[A,P]=SP
.

Now suppose that
\phi\in End(V)
satisfies
[A,\phi]\in SP
. By the equality just
proved, there exists
\psi\in P
such that
[A,p]=[A,\phi]
. Then
\phi-\psi\in Z(A)
. Since
Z(A)\subset P
, we have
\phi-\psi\in P
, and hence
\phi\in P
.
∎

Take

x=(E,E_{D}^{\bullet},\Phi)\in\mathrm{Higgs}^{\vec{\xi}\text{-par}}(C,D,\vec{m},\vec{\alpha})

such that

lies in
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)
. For each

, set

There is a natural morphism of complexes

given by the inclusions in degrees

and

. In degree

, this is the obvious inclusion

\mathcal{P}\!\mathcal{E}nd(E)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{E}nd(E)
. In degree

, if

, then at each marked point

one has

. By Lemma

for all

.

The differential

It is enough to show that the hypercohomology of the cokernel complex

in degrees

and

It remains to check that the isomorphism preserves the symplectic forms. Recall that the symplectic forms

described in (

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D(C_{\vec{\xi}\text{-par},x}^{\bullet})}

Theorem 5.11
.

Assume that
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D)\neq\emptyset
and
F
is proper and surjective.
Then the normalization of
\mathrm{Higgs}^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},ss}(C,D)
has symplectic singularities and the forgetful map
F
yields a symplectic resolution. Moreover, this resolution is compatible with the Hitchin maps.

Proof.

Let
U=\mathrm{Higgs}^{\mathcal{O},s}(C,D),X=\mathrm{Higgs}^{\overline{\mathcal{O}},ss}(C,D)
and
Y=\mathrm{Higgs}^{\vec{\xi}\textrm{-par}}(C,D,\vec{m},\vec{\alpha})
. Since
F
is surjective by assumption and
Y
is irreducible by Proposition
4.6
,
X
is irreducible. Since
Y
is smooth,
F
factors through the normalization
X^{\nu}
of
X
:

Y\xlongrightarrow{\widetilde{F}}X^{\nu}\to X

Moreover,
\widetilde{F}
is proper because
F
is proper, and
\widetilde{F}
is birational because
F
is an isomorphism over the dense open
U\subset X
by Proposition
5.8
.

Since
U
lies in the regular locus
X_{\text{reg}}
of
X
by Proposition
3.9
, we can also regard
U
as a dense open subset of
V:=(X^{\nu})_{\text{reg}}
. By Beauville’s definition of symplectic singularities
[
2
, Definition 1.1 and Remark 1.2]
, it suffices to check that the symplectic form
\omega^{\mathcal{O}}
on
U
extends to
V
. Set
W=\widetilde{F}^{-1}(V)
and
f:\widetilde{F}|_{W}:W\to V
. Then

is a proper birational morphism between smooth varieties. Since

is holomorphic on

on

is holomorphic if and only if

is holomorphic implies that

extends to a holomorphic 2-form

on

. Moreover, since

implies that

is nondegenerate everywhere on

. Therefore, we see that
\omega_{V}
is a holomorphic symplectic form on

. Hence,

∎

6.
Applications to Betti moduli spaces

The goal of this section is to explain how the results of this article can be transported to the Betti side via Simpson’s tame non-abelian Hodge correspondence. As before, let
C
be a smooth projective curve of genus
g\geq 0
, and let
D=\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}\}\subset C
be a reduced divisor. We write
C^{\circ}=C\setminus D
and fix the rank
r
.

Definition 6.1
.

A filtered local system on
C^{\circ}
is a local system
\mathbb{L}
together with, for each
p_{i}\in D
, a decreasing left-continuous filtration
\{\mathbb{L}_{\widetilde{p}_{i}}^{\beta}\}_{\beta\in\mathbb{R}}
on a nearby stalk
\mathbb{L}_{\widetilde{p}_{i}}
, preserved by the local monodromy
T_{i}
around
p_{i}
. Here
\widetilde{p}_{i}
denotes a chosen nearby point in
C^{\circ}
.

There is also an analogous definition of a filtered Higgs bundle, which is equivalent to the definition of a parabolic Higgs bundle
(E,E_{D}^{\bullet},\Phi,\vec{\alpha})
on
C
used in this paper. See
[
30
,
35
,
21
]
for more details.

At each
p_{i}
, both filtered local systems and parabolic Higgs bundles contain the data of a filtered vector space together with an endomorphism preserving the filtration.

Definition 6.2
.

Let
V
be a finite-dimensional complex vector space equipped with a decreasing left-continuous filtration
\{V^{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in\mathbb{R}}
, and let
T:V\to V
be an endomorphism preserving the filtration, i.e.
T(V^{\gamma})\subseteq V^{\gamma}
for all
\gamma
. The associated graded vector space is

\operatorname{gr}(V)=\bigoplus_{\gamma}\operatorname{gr}_{\gamma}(V),

where
\operatorname{gr}_{\gamma}(V)=V^{\gamma}/V^{\gamma+\epsilon}
for sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
. The endomorphism
T
induces an endomorphism
\operatorname{gr}(T)
on
\operatorname{gr}(V)
.

To the data

Appendix A
Conjugacy classes and compatible flags

In this section, we collect some facts needed to relate conjugacy classes to compatible parabolic flags. The analogous results for nilpotent orbits can be found in
[
6
, Section 3]
. We include the direct proofs in our setting for completeness.

Let
V
be a complex vector space of dimension

r
. Fix a partition
{\underline{m}}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{\ell})
of
r
and a tuple

{\underline{\xi}}=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{\ell})\in\mathbb{C}^{\ell}
. A partial flag of type
{\underline{m}}
is a chain

0=F^{\ell}\subset F^{\ell-1}\subset\cdots\subset F^{1}\subset F^{0}=V
such that

\mathrm{dim}(F^{a-1}/F^{a})=m_{a}
for
1\leq a\leq\ell
. We denote the corresponding
partial flag variety by
\mathrm{Flag}_{{\underline{m}}}(V)
.

Definition A.1
.

An endomorphism
A\in End(V)
is
compatible with
({\underline{m}},{\underline{\xi}})
if there
exists a flag
F^{\bullet}\in\mathrm{Flag}_{{\underline{m}}}(V)
such that

A(F^{a})\subset F^{a},\qquad(A-\xi_{a}\mathrm{id})(F^{a-1})\subset F^{a}\quad\text{for all }1\leq a\leq\ell.

Equivalently, the induced endomorphism on each graded piece is scalar:

\textrm{gr}_{a}(A)=\xi_{a}\mathrm{id}_{F^{a-1}/F^{a}}.

Such a flag is called a

compatible flag
for
A
.

Define the incidence variety

\widetilde{X}_{{\underline{m}},{\underline{\xi}}}(V):=\left\{(A,F^{\bullet})\in End(V)\times\mathrm{Flag}_{{\underline{m}}}(V)\ \middle|\ (A-\xi_{a}\mathrm{id})(F^{a-1})\subset F^{a}\text{ for all }a\right\},

and let
\mu_{{\underline{m}},{\underline{\xi}}}:\widetilde{X}_{{\underline{m}},{\underline{\xi}}}(V)\to End(V)
be the projection

(A,F^{\bullet})\mapsto A
. We write

X_{{\underline{m}},{\underline{\xi}}}(V):=\mu_{{\underline{m}},{\underline{\xi}}}(\widetilde{X}_{{\underline{m}},{\underline{\xi}}}(V))
.

Proposition A.2
.

The variety
\widetilde{X}_{{\underline{m}},{\underline{\xi}}}(V)
is smooth and irreducible, and the morphism

\mu_{{\underline{m}},{\underline{\xi}}}
is projective. Moreover,
X_{{\underline{m}},{\underline{\xi}}}(V)
is a closed irreducible

\mathrm{GL}(V)
-stable subvariety of
End(V)
.

Proof.

Let
\mathrm{pr}:\widetilde{X}_{{\underline{m}},{\underline{\xi}}}(V)\to\mathrm{Flag}_{{\underline{m}}}(V)
be the projection.
For a fixed flag
F^{\bullet}
, the fiber of
\mathrm{pr}
is the affine linear subspace
of
End(V)
defined by

(A-\xi_{a}\mathrm{id})(F^{a-1})\subset F^{a}
for all
a
. Its translation
vector space is

\{B\in End(V)\mid B(F^{a-1})\subset F^{a}\text{ for all }a\},

whose dimension depends only on the type
{\underline{m}}
. Hence

\widetilde{X}_{{\underline{m}},{\underline{\xi}}}(V)
is an affine bundle over the smooth irreducible
variety
\mathrm{Flag}_{{\underline{m}}}(V)
. In particular,
\widetilde{X}_{{\underline{m}},{\underline{\xi}}}(V)
is smooth
and irreducible.

The morphism
\mu_{{\underline{m}},{\underline{\xi}}}
is projective because it is the restriction of
the projection
End(V)\times\mathrm{Flag}_{{\underline{m}}}(V)\to End(V)
, and

\mathrm{Flag}_{{\underline{m}}}(V)
is projective. Hence its image

X_{{\underline{m}},{\underline{\xi}}}(V)
is closed. Since
\widetilde{X}_{{\underline{m}},{\underline{\xi}}}(V)
is irreducible,
its image is irreducible. The
\mathrm{GL}(V)
-stability is immediate from the
definition.
∎

We now explain how conjugacy classes are encoded by pairs
({\underline{m}},{\underline{\xi}})
. Let

\mathcal{O}\subset End(V)
be a conjugacy class and choose
A\in\mathcal{O}
. For
each eigenvalue
\lambda
, write
V_{\lambda}
for the generalized eigenspace and

A|_{V_{\lambda}}=\lambda I+N_{\lambda}
, where
N_{\lambda}
is nilpotent. If the

N_{\lambda}
is the partition
\underline{\mu}^{(\lambda)}
, write

(\underline{\mu}^{(\lambda)})^{t}=(m_{\lambda,1},\ldots,m_{\lambda,\ell_{\lambda}})
for its

repeating each eigenvalue

times, gives a pair

({\underline{m}},{\underline{\xi}})
.

Theorem A.3
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be a conjugacy class and let
({\underline{m}},{\underline{\xi}})
be the associated pair as above.

(1)

X_{{\underline{m}},{\underline{\xi}}}(V)=\overline{\mathcal{O}}
. In particular, an endomorphism
A\in End(V)
is compatible with
({\underline{m}},{\underline{\xi}})
(admitting a compatible flag)
if and only if
A\in\overline{\mathcal{O}}
.

(2)

For any
A\in\mathcal{O}
, the compatible flag is unique.

Proof.

Let
A\in\mathcal{O}
. For each eigenvalue
\lambda
, let
V_{\lambda}
be the generalized
\lambda
-eigenspace and set
N_{\lambda}=(A-\lambda\mathrm{id})|_{V_{\lambda}}

F^{\bullet}_{\lambda}:V_{\lambda}\supset N_{\lambda}V_{\lambda}\supset N_{\lambda}^{2}V_{\lambda}\supset\cdots
, which clearly satisfies the compatibility condition
(A-\lambda\mathrm{id})(F^{a-1})\subset F^{a}
for all
a.
Moreover, if the Jordan type of
N_{\lambda}
is
\underline{\mu}^{(\lambda)}
, then the successive quotients of the image filtration have dimensions given by the conjugate partition
(\underline{\mu}^{(\lambda)})^{t}
(see e.g.
[
21

). After choosing an ordering of the eigenvalue data, these filtrations give a compatible flag of type
({\underline{m}},{\underline{\xi}})
. Hence
\mathcal{O}\subset X_{{\underline{m}},{\underline{\xi}}}(V)
.

Conversely, let
B\in X_{{\underline{m}},{\underline{\xi}}}(V)
and choose a compatible flag
F^{\bullet}

F^{\bullet}
must respect the generalized eigenspace decomposition
V=\bigoplus V_{\lambda}
of
B

\lambda

N_{\lambda}=(B-\lambda\mathrm{id})|_{V_{\lambda}}

be the induced filtration of

implies that

\mathrm{dim}(N_{\lambda}^{a}V_{\lambda})\leq\mathrm{dim}(F^{a}_{\lambda}),\quad\textrm{for all }a.

Let
\underline{\nu}^{(\lambda)}
be the Jordan type of

. Argue as in the preceding paragraph, we have
\mathrm{dim}(N_{\lambda}^{a}V_{\lambda})=\mathrm{dim}(V_{\lambda})-\sum_{i=1}^{a}(\underline{\nu}^{(\lambda)})_{i}^{t}
and
\mathrm{dim}(F^{a}_{\lambda})=\mathrm{dim}(V_{\lambda})-\sum_{i=1}^{a}(\underline{\mu}^{(\lambda)})_{i}^{t}

\sum_{i=1}^{a}(\underline{\nu}^{(\lambda)})^{t}_{i}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{a}(\underline{\mu}^{(\lambda)})^{t}_{i},\quad\textrm{for all }a.

This means that

lies in the closure of the nilpotent orbit of type
\underline{\mu}^{(\lambda)}

Finally, we establish uniqueness. Suppose
