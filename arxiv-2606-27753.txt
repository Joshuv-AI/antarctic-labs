Diagram groups and groups of piecewise linear homeomorphisms of the line with global fixed points
Daniel S. Farley

1.
Introduction

In
[
2
]
, Bieri and Strebel studied groups of piecewise linear homeomorphisms of the line, denoted by
G(I;A,P)
. These groups are defined by three parameters: (i) a closed interval
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
; (ii) a multiplication subgroup

P
of the positive real numbers, and (iii) a
\mathbb{Z}[P]
-submodule
A
of
\mathbb{R}
. The group
G(I;A,P)
is then defined to be the collection of all piecewise linear homeomorphisms
h
of
I
such that:

(1)

h(I\cap A)=I\cap A
;

(2)

the derivative
h^{\prime}(x)
, where defined, is always in
P
, and

(3)

the points
x
where
h^{\prime}(x)
is undefined lie in
A
.

For instance, these definitions imply that
the generalized Thompson group
F_{n}
(
n\geq 2
) is
G([0,1];\mathbb{Z}[1/n],\langle n\rangle)
.

One somewhat unusual feature of
[
2
]
is that the authors consider the role of the interval
I
in great depth. Following their example, this author began to consider the groups
G([a,b];\mathbb{Z}[1/n],\langle n\rangle)
for arbitrary
a
and
b
, with the idea of studying their topological finiteness properties
F_{n}
. (Here we recall that
[
4
]
and
[
5
]
proved, respectively, that the Thompson groups
F_{2}
and
F_{n}
have type
F_{\infty}
.)
After beginning this project, the author learned of
[
13
]
and
[
1
]
. In
[
13
]
, Golan and Sapir showed that the stabilizer in
F_{2}
of a sequence

0<r_{1}<r_{2}<r_{3}<\ldots<r_{m}<1
of rational numbers is always finitely generated and that
the isomorphism types of these stabilizers depend only on
m
and the
set
\{i\mid r_{i}\text{ is a dyadic rational }\}
. In
[
1
]
, it was shown
that the stabilizer in
F_{2}
of a single rational number is always finitely presented, and the authors explicitly computed a finite presentation.

In view of the results from
[
2
]
,
[
13
]
and
[
1
]
, it is natural to consider the following class of groups.

Definition 1.1
.

(The groups
G_{n,\ell}
)
Let
n\geq 2
and let
\ell=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{k})\in[0,1]^{k}

be an increasing sequence. We let
G_{n,\ell}
denote the subgroup
of
G([r_{1},r_{k}];\mathbb{Z}[1/n],\langle n\rangle)
that fixes
\ell
.

We will prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

The group
G_{n,\ell}
is isomorphic to a diagram group
\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{P}_{n,\ell},\omega_{n,\ell})
, where the semigroup presentation
\mathcal{P}_{n,\ell}
and base word
\omega_{n,\ell}
can be computed algorithmically from the
n
-ary expansions of the members of
\ell
.

If
\ell\in\mathbb{Q}^{k}
, then
\mathcal{P}_{n,\ell}
is a finite semigroup presentation
and
G_{n,\ell}
is of type
F_{\infty}
. If any member of
\ell
is irrational, then
\mathcal{P}_{n,\ell}
is infinite and
G_{n,\ell}
is
not finitely generated.

The
F_{\infty}
statement extends some of the above-mentioned results from
[
13
]
and
[
1
]
to arbitrary
n
, since
F_{\infty}
groups are finitely presentable. Note also that the restriction of entries of
\ell
to
[0,1]
is done without loss of generality, since, if
\ell\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
, then there is an affine transformation of
\mathbb{R}
conjugating
G_{n,\ell}
to
G_{n,\ell^{\prime}}

for some
\ell^{\prime}\in[0,1]^{k}
.

The theory of diagram groups was developed initially by Kilibarda in her thesis
[
17
]
, and later by Guba and Sapir
[
15
]
. A major impetus for the development of this theory was Guba’s observation
[
15
]
that Thompson’s group
F_{2}
is a diagram group. Our main theorem is a generalization of Guba’s observation, and raises the possibility of applying diagram group methods to the study of the groups
G_{n,\ell}
. We do not attempt to summarise such methods here, but refer the interested reader to
[
15
]
and the recent survey
[
11
]
, which features an extensive bibliography. We can mention the following corollary.

Corollary 1.3
.

For arbitrary
n
and
\ell
:

(1)

The integral homology groups
H_{k}(G_{n,\ell})
are free abelian, and

(2)

The group
G_{n,\ell}
acts properly by isometries on a locally finite
CAT(0) cubical complex.

Part (1) is due to results of
[
11
,
14
]
and (2) is due to results of
[
8
]
.

The proof of Theorem
1.2
uses methods that were developed by Hughes and the author in
[
7
]
, and later refined by the author in

[
9
]
. For a given
G_{n,\ell}
, the results of
[
7
]
and
[
9
]
produce a simplicial complex
\Delta_{n,\ell}
on which

G_{n,\ell}
acts freely and cellularly.
The main observation in the proof of Theorem
1.2
is
that the quotient of the
2
-skeleton
\Delta_{n,\ell}^{(2)}
by the action of
G_{n,\ell}
is isomorphic to a certain singular square complex
S(\mathcal{P}_{n,\ell},\omega_{n,\ell})
defined in
[
15
]
, called the
Squier complex
. The group

\pi_{1}(S(\mathcal{P}_{n,\ell},\omega_{n,\ell}))
is isomorphic to

D(\mathcal{P}_{n,\ell},\omega_{n,\ell})
by results of
[
15
]
. Thus, we have the following sequence of isomorphisms:

G_{n,\ell}\,\cong\,\pi_{1}(G_{n,\ell}\backslash\Delta_{n,\ell})\,\cong\,\pi_{1}(S(\mathcal{P}_{n,\ell},\omega_{n,\ell}))\,\cong\,D(\mathcal{P}_{n,\ell},\omega_{n,\ell}),

from which the isomorphism result of Theorem
1.2
follows. The finiteness results of Theorem
1.2
will be deduced from a general framework that was described by the author in
[
9
]
.

Our proof will be as self-contained as possible, but we will nevertheless need to use results from
[
9
]
and
[
10
]
rather extensively. Very little background in diagram groups is assumed or needed, since we will effectively define a diagram group as the fundamental group of the relevant Squier complex.

We now briefly outline the paper.
In Section
2
, we introduce semigroup presentations
\mathcal{P}
and their associated diagram groups

D(\mathcal{P},\omega)
, where
w
is a base word. In Section
3
, we walk the reader through a procedure which, given

and
\ell
, produces a semigroup presentation
\mathcal{P}_{n,\ell}
and word

such that
G_{n,\ell}

is isomorphic to

. The justification of the procedure from Section
3
begins in Section
4
and ends in Section
7
. Section
4
introduces the concept of “local generators” for a generalized Thompson group, and considers in detail the base case in which
\ell=(a,b)
. Section
5
describes local generators for the groups

using the device of a labelled tree
T_{n,\ell}
.
Section
6
describes the construction of a CAT(0) cubical complex

on which the group

acts, and proves that the action is always free. Section

concludes the proof of the isomorphism statement in Theorem

by showing that the

is isomorphic to a connected component of the Squier complex for

. Finally, Section

property for the case in which

is a list of rational numbers, and shows that

]
and a framework from
[

.

2.
Diagram groups

Definition 2.1
.

(Semigroup presentations; equivalence modulo a presentation)
Let
\Sigma
be an alphabet. A
positive word
in the alphabet
\Sigma
is a string of symbols from
\Sigma
without any occurrences of inverses (e.g., if
\Sigma=\{a,b\}
, then
aba
is positive, but
ab^{-1}a
is not). The
free monoid

\Sigma^{\ast}
on
\Sigma
is the collection of all positive words in the symbols
\Sigma
, including the empty word, which we denote by
\varepsilon
. The
free semigroup

\Sigma^{+}
on

\Sigma
consists of all non-empty positive words in the symbols
\Sigma
. In both
\Sigma^{\ast}
and
\Sigma^{+}
, the operation is concatenation of words.

We write
\omega^{\prime}\equiv\omega^{\prime\prime}
if
\omega^{\prime}
and
\omega^{\prime\prime}
are identical, letter for
letter, as words.

A
semigroup presentation

\mathcal{P}
is a pair
\langle\Sigma\mid\mathcal{R}\rangle
, where
\Sigma
is an alphabet and
\mathcal{R}
is a collection of ordered pairs
(r_{1},r_{2})\in\Sigma^{+}\times\Sigma^{+}
. Two words
\omega^{\prime},\omega^{\prime\prime}\in\Sigma^{+}
are

modulo
\mathcal{P}
if there is a sequence

\omega^{\prime}\equiv\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{m}\equiv\omega^{\prime\prime}

such that, for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,m-1\}
, there is an ordered pair
(r_{1},r_{2})\in\mathcal{R}
such that
\omega_{i+1}
is the result of replacing some occurrence of either
r_{1}
or
r_{2}
in
\omega_{i}
with (respectively)
r_{2}
or
r_{1}
. We write
\omega^{\prime}=_{\mathcal{P}}\omega^{\prime\prime}
, or simply
\omega^{\prime}=\omega^{\prime\prime}
if the presentation
\mathcal{P}
is clear from the context. Equivance module
\mathcal{P}
is an equivalence relation on
\Sigma^{+}
. We denote the equivalence class of a word
\omega
by
[\omega]
.

We will write
r_{1}\rightarrow r_{2}
for members of
\mathcal{R}
, in place of
(r_{1},r_{2})

Definition 2.2
.

[
15
]
(The Squier complex of a semigroup presentation)
Let
\mathcal{P}=\langle\Sigma\mid\mathcal{R}\rangle
be a semigroup presentation.
The
Squier complex

S(\mathcal{P})
of
\mathcal{P}
is a singular square complex defined as follows.
The set of vertices of
S(\mathcal{P})
is
\Sigma^{+}
. A directed edge is denoted by a triple

(a,r_{1}\rightarrow r_{2},b)
, where
(r_{1}\rightarrow r_{2})\in\mathcal{R}
and

a,b\in\Sigma^{\ast}
. The directed edge in question has
ar_{1}b
as its initial vertex and
ar_{2}b
as its terminal vertex. Squares take the general form

(a,r_{1}\rightarrow r_{2},b,r^{\prime}_{1}\rightarrow r^{\prime}_{2},c)
, where

(r_{1}\rightarrow r_{2})
,
(r^{\prime}_{1}\rightarrow r^{\prime}_{2})\in\mathcal{R}
and
a,b,c\in\Sigma^{\ast}
. The attaching map of a typical square is indicated in Figure

.

If
\omega\in\Sigma^{+}
, then we let
S(\mathcal{P},\omega)
denote the connected component of
S(\mathcal{P})
containing the vertex
\omega
.

Definition 2.3
.

[
15
]
(The diagram group
D(\mathcal{P},\omega)
)
Let
\mathcal{P}=\langle\Sigma\mid\mathcal{R}\rangle
be a semigroup presentation and let
\omega\in\Sigma^{+}
.
The diagram group
D(\mathcal{P},\omega)
is the fundamental group of

S(\mathcal{P},\omega)
with basepoint at
\omega
.

Remark 2.4
.

Assume that a semigroup presentation
\mathcal{P}
is given.
Guba and Sapir
[
15
]
describe how to make a certain class of oriented labelled planar graphs, called
semigroup diagrams over
\mathcal{P}
, into a group with respect to a natural stacking operation.
Strictly speaking, one needs to fix a base word
\omega\in\Sigma^{+}
, and then the semigroup diagrams over
\mathcal{P}
whose top and bottom labels are
\omega
become a group, which is denoted by
D(\mathcal{P},\omega)
. It is a theorem of
[
15
]
that
D(\mathcal{P},\omega)
is isomorphic to
\pi_{1}(S(\mathcal{P},\omega))
.

In the main argument of this paper, we can avoid any mention of semigroup diagrams and simply use the isomorphism

D(\mathcal{P},\omega)\cong\pi_{1}(S(\mathcal{P},\omega))
as a definition
of
D(\mathcal{P},\omega)
(as above).

4.
Local generation of the groups
G_{n,(a,b)}

Many generalized Thompson groups
G
have piecewise definitions: for a given
h\in G
, there is a finite subdivision of
Dom(h)
into pieces
P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}
such that the restriction of
h
to
P_{i}
is a function having certain additional properties.
A central idea in
[
7
]
and
[
9
]
is to collect these smaller functions
into an object called an inverse semigroup
S
, and then to view
G
as being “locally generated” (or locally determined) by the inverse semigroup
S
. Both
[
7
]
and
[
9
]

also use the semigroup
S
as the starting point for a construction of a space on which
G
acts. This is the general pattern to be followed in the remainder of the paper.

In this section and the next, we will carefully describe inverse semigroups
S_{n,\ell}
that “locally generate”
G_{n,\ell}
. The analysis in the current section is confined to the groups
G_{n,(a,b)}
(i.e.,

). Some of the material here overlaps with standard accounts such as
[
6
]
, which covers the case
(n,a,b)=(2,0,1)
.

4.1.
Inverse semigroups and associated groups

Definition 4.1
.

(Inverse semigroups; the action of an inverse semigroup on a set)
Let
X
be a set. A
partial bijection
of
X
is a bijective function
h:A\rightarrow B
between subsets
A
,
B
of
X
. The function with empty domain and range will be denoted by
0
.

An
inverse semigroup

S
is a collection of partial bijections that is closed under compositions and inverses, where compositions are defined “on overlaps”: if
s_{1},s_{2}\in S
, then the domain of
s_{1}\circ s_{2}
is
s_{2}^{-1}(Dom(s_{1}))
.
If
S
is an inverse semigroup of partial bijections of
X
, then we say that
S

acts
on
X
.

Definition 4.2
.

[
7
,
9
]
(The group
\Gamma(S)
)

Let
S
be an inverse semigroup acting on a set
X
. A bijection
h:X\rightarrow X

is
locally determined
by
S
if, for some
m
, there are partitions

\mathcal{P}_{1}=\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}\}
and
\mathcal{P}_{2}=\{B_{1},\ldots,B_{m}\}
of
X
and partial bijections
s_{i}:A_{i}\rightarrow B_{i}
(for
i=1,\ldots,m
) such that
h_{\mid A_{i}}=s_{i}
and
s_{i}\in S
for all
i
. We sometimes denote such an
h
by

h(s_{1},\ldots,s_{m})
.

We let
\Gamma(S)
be the collection of all homeomorphisms of
X
that are locally determined by

S
.

Remark 4.3
.

The set
\Gamma(S)
becomes a group under composition of functions if
\Gamma(S)\neq\emptyset
. The latter is true precisely when
X
can be partitioned by the domains of some collection
\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}\}\subseteq S
.

4.2.
Standard
n
-adic intervals and standard
n
-adic transformations

Definition 4.4
.

(
N
-adic numbers, standard
n
-adic intervals; standard
n
-adic transformations;
S_{n}
)
If
a
and
m
are non-negative integers, then
a/n^{m}
is an
n
-adic
number. The interval

I=\left[\frac{a}{n^{m}},\frac{a+1}{n^{m}}\right)

is a
standard
n
-adic interval
if
I\subseteq[0,1]
.

If
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
are standard
n
-adic intervals, then we let
\sigma_{I_{1}}^{I_{2}}
be the unique orientation-preserving linear homeomorphism from
I_{1}
to
I_{2}
. We say that
\sigma_{I_{1}}^{I_{2}}
is a
standard
n
-adic transformation
, and we let
S_{n}
be the set of all
n
-adic transformations.

Definition 4.5
.

(The address of a standard
n
-adic interval)
Let
\omega\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}^{\ast}
. The set of all real
numbers
x
such that
x
admits an
n
-ary expansion

.\omega a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}\ldots

is the closure of a standard
n
-adic interval. If we omit the number

.\omega\overline{n-1}
,
then the remaining
x
form a standard
n
-adic interval, which we denote
I_{\omega}
.

Conversely, if a standard
n
-adic interval
I
is given, then there is a unique
\omega
such that
I=I_{\omega}
. We sometimes refer to
\omega
as the
address
of
I
in this circumstance.

The assignment
\omega\mapsto I_{\omega}
determines a bijection
between
\{0,\ldots,n-1\}^{\ast}
and the collection of all standard
n
-adic intervals.

Convention 4.6
.

In view of the bijection between addresses and standard
n
-adic intervals, we will write

\sigma^{\omega_{2}}_{\omega_{1}}
in place of
\sigma^{I_{\omega_{2}}}_{I_{\omega_{1}}}
.

Lemma 4.7
.

(Basic properties of the transformations

\sigma_{\omega_{1}}^{\omega_{2}}
)
Let
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}
be words in the alphabet
\{0,\ldots,n-1\}
.

(1)

The standard
n
-adic transformation
\sigma_{\omega_{1}}^{\omega_{2}}
is defined by a rule of the form

x\mapsto ax+b
,
where
a
is a power of
n
and
b
is an
n
-adic number in
[0,1)
.

(2)

The transformation
\sigma_{\omega_{1}}^{\omega_{2}}
acts by prefix replacement. That is, if
x
admits an
n
-ary expansion of the form
.\omega_{1}a_{1}a_{2}\ldots

in which not all of the
a_{i}
are
n-1
, then

\sigma_{\omega_{1}}^{\omega_{2}}(x)=.\omega_{2}a_{1}a_{2}\ldots.

The unique continuous extension of
\sigma_{\omega_{1}}^{\omega_{2}}
to the closure of
I_{\omega_{1}}
acts by prefix replacement on
.\omega_{1}\overline{n-1}
as well.

(3)

The transformation
\sigma_{\omega_{1}}^{\omega_{2}}
fixes a unique
x
in

I_{\omega_{1}}
if and only if

\omega_{i}\equiv\omega_{j}\tau
(
\{i,j\}=\{1,2\}
) for some
\tau\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}^{+}-\{n-1\}^{+}
. If the latter holds, then the fixed point
is the rational number
.\omega_{j}\overline{\tau}
.

(4)

The transformation
\sigma_{\omega_{1}}^{\omega_{2}}
has multiple fixed points if and only if
\omega_{1}\equiv\omega_{2}
, and thus
\sigma_{\omega_{1}}^{\omega_{2}}=id_{\omega_{1}}
. The same is true of the continuous extension of
\sigma_{\omega_{1}}^{\omega_{2}}
.

Proof.

We prove (3), leaving (1), (2) and (4) to the reader.

Assume first that
\sigma_{\omega_{1}}^{\omega_{2}}
has a unique fixed point
x
in
I_{\omega_{1}}
. We assume that

.b_{1}b_{2}\ldots
is the unique
n
-ary expansion of
x
that does not end in an infinite sequence of
n-1
s. Since
x\in I_{\omega_{1}}\cap I_{\omega_{2}}
, both
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
are prefixes of

b_{1}b_{2}b_{3}\ldots
. If
\omega_{1}\equiv\omega_{2}
, then
the transformation
\sigma_{\omega_{1}}^{\omega_{2}}
is the identity on

I_{\omega_{1}}
, and thus there are infinitely many fixed points, contrary to our hypothesis. It must therefore be that
\omega_{1}\equiv\omega_{2}\tau
or
\omega_{2}\equiv\omega_{1}\tau
, for some
\tau\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}^{+}
.
Suppose, for a contradiction, that
\tau\in\{n-1\}^{+}
and, without loss of generality, that
\omega_{2}\equiv\omega_{1}\tau
. We apply the
m
th power of
\sigma_{\omega_{1}}^{\omega_{2}}
to
x
, assuming that
x=.\omega_{1}b_{\ell}b_{\ell+1}\ldots
is the
n
-ary expansion of
x
that does not end in an infinite sequence of
n-1
s:

\displaystyle.\omega_{1}b_{\ell}b_{\ell+1}\ldots

\displaystyle=\left(\sigma_{\omega_{1}}^{\omega_{2}}\right)^{m}(.\omega_{1}b_{\ell}b_{\ell+1}\ldots)

\displaystyle=.\omega_{1}\tau^{m}b_{\ell}b_{\ell+1}\ldots.

Comparing these
n
-ary expansions, and letting
m
be arbitrarily large, we find that
b_{j}=n-1
for arbitrarily large
j
. This is a contradiction. Thus,

\tau\not\in\{n-1\}^{+}
, which proves the forward direction.

Conversely, assume without loss of generality that
\omega_{1}\equiv\omega_{2}\tau
, where
\tau\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}^{+}-\{n-1\}^{+}
. Consider the
number
x
with the
n
-ary expansion
.\omega_{2}\overline{\tau}
. We apply
\sigma_{\omega_{1}}^{\omega_{2}}
to
x
, using (2):

\displaystyle\sigma_{\omega_{1}}^{\omega_{2}}(x)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{\omega_{1}}^{\omega_{2}}(.\omega_{2}\tau\tau\tau\ldots)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{\omega_{1}}^{\omega_{2}}(.\omega_{1}\tau\tau\ldots)

\displaystyle=.\omega_{2}\tau\tau\tau\ldots.

It follows that
.\omega_{2}\overline{\tau}
is a fixed point of

\sigma_{\omega_{1}}^{\omega_{2}}
. If
z=.b_{1}b_{2}b_{3}
is an arbitrary fixed point, where infinitely many of the
b_{i}
are not equal to
n-1
, then
\omega_{1}
is a prefix of
z
. We can therefore
write
\omega_{2}\equiv b_{1}b_{2}\ldots b_{\alpha}
,
\omega_{1}\equiv b_{1}b_{2}\ldots b_{\beta}
, and
\tau\equiv b_{\alpha+1}\ldots b_{\beta}
, for some
\alpha<\beta
. Applying
\sigma_{\omega_{1}}^{\omega_{2}}
, we find that

.b_{1}b_{2}b_{3}\ldots\,\,=\,\,.b_{1}\ldots b_{\alpha}b_{\beta+1}b_{\beta+2}\ldots.

Comparing these
n
-ary expansions, we conclude that

b_{\alpha+m}=b_{\beta+m}
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
. We easily conclude that
z=.\omega_{2}\overline{\tau}
, so the fixed point of

\sigma_{\omega_{1}}^{\omega_{2}}
is unique.
∎

4.3.
Local generation of the groups
G_{n,(a,b)}

Lemma 4.8
.

(Subdivision into standard
n
-adic transformations)
Let
f:[a,b)\rightarrow[c,d)
be an increasing linear homeomorphism such that

(1)

a
,
b
,
c
, and
d
are
n
-adic numbers in
[0,1]
, and

(2)

f^{\prime}(x)
is a fixed integral power of
n
, for all
x\in[a,b)
.

There are partitions
\{I_{1},\ldots,I_{m}\}
and
\{I^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,I^{\prime}_{m}\}
of
[a,b)
and
[c,d)
(respectively) into standard
n
-adic intervals such that, for each
j
,
f_{\mid I_{j}}:I_{j}\rightarrow I^{\prime}_{j}
is a standard
n
-adic transformation.

Proof.

We can first write
a
and
b
over a common power of
n
:

a=\frac{\alpha}{n^{k}};\quad\quad b=\frac{\beta}{n^{k}}.

Subdivide
[a,b)
into the intervals

\left[\frac{\alpha}{n^{k}},\frac{\alpha+1}{n^{k}}\right),\quad\left[\frac{\alpha+1}{n^{k}},\frac{\alpha+2}{n^{k}}\right),\quad\ldots\quad\left[\frac{\beta-1}{n^{k}},\frac{\beta}{n^{k}}\right).

Each of these intervals is a standard
n
-adic interval.
The conditions on
f
imply that
f(x)=n^{\alpha}x+d
, where
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}
and
d\in\mathbb{Z}[1/n]
. It follows easily that the restriction of

f
to each subinterval also satisfies the hypotheses of Lemma
4.8
. It therefore suffices to prove the lemma in the case that
[a,b)
is a standard
n
-adic interval.

Since
b-a
is a power of
n
and
f^{\prime}(x)
is also a power of
n
,
d-c
is a power of
n
;
d-c=n^{m}
, say. Let

c=\frac{\gamma}{n^{\ell}}\quad\text{and}\quad d=\frac{\delta}{n^{\ell}},

where
\gamma,\delta\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
.
A direct calculation shows that
\delta-\gamma=n^{\ell+m}
. It follows that
\ell+m
is a non-negative integer. We subdivide
[c,d)
into intervals as follows:

\left[\frac{\gamma}{n^{\ell}},\frac{\gamma+1}{n^{\ell}}\right),\quad\left[\frac{\gamma+1}{n^{\ell}},\frac{\gamma+2}{n^{\ell}}\right),\quad\ldots\quad\left[\frac{\delta-1}{n^{\ell}},\frac{\delta}{n^{\ell}}\right).

We note that there are precisely
n^{\ell+m}
of these intervals. If we now subdivide the domain
[a,b)
into
n^{\ell+m}
equal parts, then each of these parts remains a standard
n
-adic interval, and the restrictions of
f
map each of these parts to one of the above intervals by a standard
n
-adic transformation.
∎

Lemma 4.9
.

(Local generation of
G_{n,(a,b)}
by standard
n
-adic transformations)

Let
[a,b]\subseteq[0,1]
and
n>1
. If
h\in G_{n,(a,b)}
, then there are
standard
n
-adic transformations
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{\ell}
such that
the restriction of
\gamma(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{\ell})
to
[a,b]
is equal to
h
.

Proof.

This is entirely straightforward if
h
has no singularities, so we assume that
h
has a non-empty collection of singularities
a<s_{1}<s_{2}<\ldots<s_{\delta}<b
. Consider the (unique) piecewise linear extension
\hat{h}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
of
h
such that
\hat{h}
and
h
share the same set of singularities. By continuity of
\hat{h}
, we can find
s_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}[1/n]\cap[0,a]

and
s_{\delta+1}\in\mathbb{Z}[1/n]\cap[b,1]
such that

\hat{h}(s_{0}),\hat{h}(s_{\delta+1})\in[0,1]
.

We apply Lemma
4.8
to each interval
[s_{i},s_{i+1})
(
i=0,\ldots,\delta
), and thus find
a sequence of standard
n
-adic transformations
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{\ell}
such that
\gamma:=\gamma(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{\ell})=\hat{h}_{\mid[s_{0},s_{\delta+1})}
. Clearly,
\gamma
has the desired property.
∎

Local generation of the groups

Throughout this section, we write

-ary automaton for

5.1.
The labelled tree

Definition 5.1

-accepting state
of

.

Definition 5.2
.

(left and right failure)
Let
\omega=a_{1}\ldots a_{m}
(

).
If
\omega
is not accepted by

, then there is a (possibly empty) maximal proper prefix

such that
\omega^{\prime}
is accepted by
A_{n}(x)
.

(1)

Assume
\omega
is not accepted by

and
\omega^{\prime}
is as above.
We say that
\omega

left fails
if

is less than the label of the outgoing edge from the

-accepting state of

(2)

Assume

We say that

Lemma 5.3
.

(Interpretation of acceptance and failure)
Let
\omega
be a node in

and let

be the standard
n
-adic interval whose address is

.

(1)

\omega
left fails if and only if, for every

,

;

(2)

\omega
right fails if and only if, for every

,

;

(3)

\omega
is accepted by

(

)
if and only if

;

(4)

if no

-ary expansion of

is terminating, then

is accepted by

if and only if

;

(5)

if

admits a terminating expansion, then

is accepted by

if and only if

is in the interior of

or
r_{k}
is the right endpoint of
I_{\omega}
. (Here

is the right endpoint of
[a,b)
).

Proof.

All parts of the lemma follow from elementary facts about the

-ary expansion of a real number. We leave the details to the reader.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

(Stabilizers of the points
r_{i}
in
S_{n}
)
Let
\sigma_{\omega_{1}}^{\omega_{2}}\in S_{n}
.

(1)

If
i<k
or if
i=k
and
r_{k}
has no terminating
n
-ary expansion, then

\sigma_{\omega_{1}}^{\omega_{2}}(r_{i})=r_{i}
if and only if
\omega_{1}
and

\omega_{2}
are accepted by
A_{n}(r_{i})
, and have the same accepting states.

(2)

If
r_{k}
has a terminating
n
-ary expansion and neither
\omega_{1}
nor
\omega_{2}
right fail, then the unique continuous
extension of
\sigma_{\omega_{1}}^{\omega_{2}}
to the closure of
\omega_{1}
fixes

if and only if

\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
are accepted by
A_{n}(r_{k})
and have the same accepting states.

Proof.

We first prove (1). Under the given hypotheses, the unique
n
-ary expansion
of
r_{i}
that does not end in an infinite sequence of
n-1
s is also the expansion that labels the automaton
A_{n}(r_{i})
. We consider two cases.

Assume first that
r_{i}
is irrational. In this case, the automaton

A_{n}(r_{i})
is a labelled directed ray. Assume that
\sigma_{\omega_{1}}^{\omega_{2}}(r_{i})=r_{i}
. By Lemma
4.7
(3), if
r_{i}
were the unique fixed point of

\sigma_{\omega_{1}}^{\omega_{2}}
, then
r_{i}
would be rational. Thus,
\sigma_{\omega_{1}}^{\omega_{2}}
has multiple fixed points.
Lemma
4.7
(4) now implies that
\omega_{1}\equiv\omega_{2}
. The words
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
are both accepted by
A_{n}(r_{i})
(by Lemma
5.3
(3-4)) and clearly they must have the same accepting state.

Conversely, if both
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
are accepted by
A_{n}(r_{i})
and they have the same
accepting state, then
\omega_{1}\equiv\omega_{2}
(since
A_{n}(r_{i})
is a ray) and one easily concludes that
\sigma_{\omega_{1}}^{\omega_{2}}
fixes
r_{i}
. This handles the case in which
r_{i}
is irrational.

Assume that
r_{i}
is rational and
\sigma_{\omega_{1}}^{\omega_{2}}
fixes
r_{i}
. It follows that
r_{i}\in I_{\omega_{1}}\cap I_{\omega_{2}}
, so

accepts both
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
by Lemma

(3-4). If
\omega_{1}\equiv\omega_{2}
, then
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
clearly have the same accepting states. It therefore suffices to consider the case in which

\omega_{1}\not\equiv\omega_{2}
. The transformation
\sigma_{\omega_{1}}^{\omega_{2}}
has a unique fixed point, by our current hypothesis and Lemma
4.7
(4). We conclude from
Lemma
4.7
(3) that either
\omega_{1}\equiv\omega_{2}\tau
or
\omega_{2}\equiv\omega_{1}\tau
, where
\tau\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}^{+}-\{n-1\}^{+}
. We assume the former, the latter case being similar. Thus,
\omega_{1}\equiv\omega_{2}\tau
, and

r_{i}=.\omega_{2}\overline{\tau},

since
.\omega_{2}\overline{\tau}
and
r_{i}
are both fixed points
of
\sigma_{\omega_{1}}^{\omega_{2}}
.
Let
\alpha
be the word in
\{0,\ldots,n-1\}^{\ast}
that labels the path
in

from
v
to

, and let

where the digits after the decimal point agree place by place. It now follows from Remark

, for some

m

Conversely, if
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
are both accepted by
A_{n}(r_{i})
and have the same accepting state

, then
\omega_{1}\equiv\omega_{2}\tau
or

. (The case in which

, where
\tau\in\{0,1,2,\ldots,n-1\}^{+}
. Since

lies on the loop of

. If we let
\beta

to itelf in

, then we must have that

. Lemma

(3) now implies that

. We let
\alpha

from

to

of

Now (2). We note that

(2),

cannot be the left endpoint of either

Assume that

5.4.

Theorem 5.15
.

(Local generation of

)

\Gamma(S_{n,\ell})=G_{n,\ell}.

Proof.

Let

. It follows that

is a homeomorphism of the interval

and

, where

(2\leq i\leq k-1)
. Since

is in the domain
of

,

must be in the domain of
\sigma_{\gamma}
, for some
\gamma\in\{1,\ldots,\beta\}
. Suppose that
\sigma_{\gamma}=\sigma_{\omega_{1}}^{\omega_{2}}
. We note that

must accept
\omega_{1}
by Lemma
5.3
(3). Since
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
are both reduced and have the same label by the definition of

5.4
(1) implies that

\sigma_{\omega_{1}}^{\omega_{2}}(r_{i})=r_{i}
. Since

was arbitrary,

.

Let

. Since

, we can find
\sigma_{\alpha_{1}}^{\beta_{1}},\ldots,\sigma_{\alpha_{d}}^{\beta_{d}}\in S_{n}
such that the restriction of

to

, by Lemma
4.9
. After sufficient subdivision, we can further assume that the nodes
\alpha_{\delta}
and
\beta_{\delta}
are each accepted by at most one

.
By Lemma
5.3
, we can further assume that each node

\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d}

\beta_{i}
can neither left nor right fail, since
h

preserves the interval
[r_{1},r_{k}]
. In particular, each
\beta_{i}
necessarily has some non-null label
in
T_{n,\ell}
.

Let

If
\sigma_{\alpha_{i}}^{\beta_{i}}
fixes some

and

Theorem 6.13

(A CAT(0) construction for the groups

)

.

Proof.

We first claim that, if

, then

for some

of the form

\{[id_{D_{1}},D_{1}],\ldots,[id_{D_{\alpha}},D_{\alpha}]\}.

Consider

. We can assume that

, so

is the union of

(Lemma

). We can therefore perform a sequence of expansions at

. Thus, the vertices

and

represent partitions of

(or

) by members of

. These partitions have a common refinement; the corresponding vertex

Proposition 6.17

(The action of

on
\Delta(n,\ell)
) The function

defined by

on
\Delta(n,\ell)

Proof.

The proof that
\cdot
is a group action of
G_{n,\ell}
on
\Delta(n,\ell)
is straightforward (or see
[
7
]
or
[
9
]
). It is also clear that the action is cube-permuting.

Next we show that the action of
G_{n,\ell}
on vertices is free. Let
v=\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{\alpha}\}
be such that the supports of the

are arranged from left to right. Assume

is such that

. It follows that

for

is order-preserving. Let

where

on vertices is free.

Assume

is height-preserving (i.e., cardinality preserving ), it will necessarily fix the unique vertex

by the above reasoning. Thus,

acts freely on

.

Assume that
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
have the same type.
Let
v_{1}=\{[f_{1},D_{1}],\ldots,[f_{\alpha},D_{\alpha}]\}

that restricts to

. We evaluate

\displaystyle g\cdot v_{1}

\displaystyle=\{[gf_{1},D_{1}],\ldots,[gf_{\alpha},D_{\alpha}]\}

\displaystyle=\{[g_{1}s_{1},D_{1}],\ldots,[g_{\alpha}s_{\alpha},D_{\alpha}]\}

\displaystyle=\{[g_{1},s_{1}(D_{1})],\ldots,[g_{\alpha},s_{\alpha}(D_{\alpha})]\}

It follows that

If

8.
Finiteness properties

The paper
[
9
]
created a framework for establishing finiteness properties of the groups
\Gamma(S)
that act on
the complexes
\Delta^{f}_{\mathcal{B}}
. This framework uses the well-known finiteness criterion due to Brown
[
5
]

in an essential way. We summarise as much of this material as we will need in Subsection
8.1
, and then establish finiteness properties of the groups
G_{n,\ell}
in Subsection
8.2
.

8.1.
A template for proving
F_{\infty}

Convention 8.1
.

(The inverse semigroups
S
and
\widehat{S}
)
Assume that an expansion set
\mathcal{B}
over a linearly ordered set
X
and an inverse semigroup
S

acting on
X
are given.

We assume that
\mathcal{B}
is defined in the “usual” way:
i.e., members of
\mathcal{B}
are equivalence classes

[f,D]
, where
f\in\widehat{S}
and
D\in\mathcal{D}^{+}(S)
. Two such classes
[f_{1},D_{1}]
and
[f_{2},D_{2}]
are the same if there is
s:D_{1}\rightarrow D_{2}
in
S
such that
f_{2}s=f_{1}
on
D_{1}
. See Definition
6.2
.

We construct the inverse semigroup
\widehat{S}
to have the following properties:

(1)

S\subseteq\widehat{S}
;

(2)

each
\hat{s}\in\widehat{S}
is a finite disjoint union of members of
S
;

(3)

each
\hat{s}\in\widehat{S}
is continuous and order-preserving;

(4)

\widehat{S}
is closed under (continuous) disjoint unions: i.e., if
\hat{s}_{1},\hat{s}_{2}\in\widehat{S}
have disjoint domains and images, then the disjoint union

\hat{s}_{1}\coprod\hat{s}_{2}
is in
\widehat{S}
provided that the function in question is continuous and has an interval as its domain;

(5)

\widehat{S}
has a non-empty subset of full-support elements; i.e., bijections from
X
to
X
. Such elements obviously form a group, which we have denoted by

\Gamma(S)
(Definition
4.2
) ;

(6)

for each
b\in\mathcal{B}
,
supp(b)
is the domain of some
\hat{s}\in\widehat{S}
.

The inverse semigroups
\widehat{S}_{n,\ell}
(Definition
6.2
) all satisfy the above conditions on
\widehat{S}
.

We assume that the above conventions are in place throughout the rest of this subsection.

Definition 8.2
.

(A collection of definitions)

(1)

(
lk_{\uparrow}(b,v)
) if
b\in\mathcal{B}
and
v
is a vertex such that
b\precneq v
, then

lk_{\uparrow}(b,v)
is the simplicial complex whose vertex set consists of vertices
v^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{E}(b)
such that
b\precneq v^{\prime}\preceq v
, and whose simplices are
\preceq
-ascending
chains in
\mathcal{E}(b)
on those vertices;

(2)

(
\widehat{S}_{b}
) if
b=[f,D]\in\mathcal{B}
, then

\widehat{S}_{b}
is the set of all
\hat{s}\in\widehat{S}

such that: (i) the domain and image of
\hat{s}
are both

f(D)
, and (ii)
[\hat{s}f,D]=[f,D]
;

(3)

(consecutively ordered) A vertex
v=\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{m}\}
is
consecutively ordered
if
supp(v)
is an interval, and the supports
supp(b_{1})
,
supp(b_{2})
,
supp(b_{3})
,
\ldots
are arranged from left to right.
(We note that
supp(v)
need not be all of
[r_{1},r_{k}]
.)

(4)

(rich in contractions) We say that
\mathcal{B}
is
rich in contractions
if there is a constant
C_{1}
such that, for any consecutively ordered vertex
v=\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{m}\}

(m\geq C_{1})
, there is always a consecutively ordered subset
v^{\prime}=\{b_{\alpha},\ldots,b_{\beta}\}
such that
v^{\prime}\in\mathcal{E}(b^{\prime})-\{b^{\prime}\}
, for some
b^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}
;

(5)

(bounded contractions) We say that
\mathcal{B}
has
the
bounded contractions property
if there is a constant
C_{0}
such that, for all
b\in\mathcal{B}
and for all
v\in\mathcal{E}(b)
,
|v|\leq C_{0}
.

Theorem 8.3
.

[
9
]
(A template for proving that

)
Let
\mathcal{B}
be an expansion set over

; let

8.2.
Finiteness properties of
G_{n,\ell}

Theorem 8.4
.

(Finiteness properties of
G_{n,\ell}
)
If
\ell
is a sequence of rational numbers, then
G_{n,\ell}
has type
F_{\infty}
. If at least one entry in
\ell
is irrational, then
G_{n,\ell}
is not
finitely generated.

Proof.

Let
\ell=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{k})
. If
r_{i}
is irrational for some
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
, then every
h\in G_{n,\ell}
fixes an open neighborhood of
r_{i}
by Lemma
4.7
(3-4). Thus, if
G_{n,\ell}
were finitely generated, then there would exist an open neighborhood
of
r_{i}
on which every
h\in G_{n,\ell}
is the identity. It is, however, straightforward to argue that there are homeomorphisms
g\in G_{n,\ell}
that move points arbitrarily close to
r_{i}
. This is a contradiction, and proves that
G_{n,\ell}
is infinitely generated.

Now assume that each
r_{i}
is rational. We consider points (1)-(5) from
Theorem
8.3
. Point (1) is covered in the proof of Theorem

6.13
. If
\{b\}\precneq v
, then there is a sequence

\{b\}=v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{m}=v

such that, for all
i
,
v_{i+1}\in C(v_{i},v^{\prime}_{i})
. We note that this condition completely determines
v_{1}
; indeed, we must always have
v_{1}=E_{1}(b)
, for any sequence as above. It now follows from Definition
8.2
(1) that
lk(b,v)
is a point, and therefore contractible, proving (2). The stabilizer group
\widehat{S}_{b}
is, by definition, the collection of all transformations
\widehat{s}
in
\widehat{S}_{n,\ell}

such that: (i) the domain and range of
\widehat{s}
is
supp(b)
, and
(ii)
\widehat{s}\cdot b=b
. Let
[f,D]\in\mathcal{B}
, and let
\widehat{s}\in\widehat{S}_{b}
. It follows that
[f,D]=[\widehat{s}f,D]
, so there is some
h:D\rightarrow D

(h\in S_{n,\ell})
such that
\widehat{s}fh_{\mid D}=f
. We note that
h
is necessarily the identity (Lemma
4.7
(4)), so we have the equality

\widehat{s}f=f
. If
y\in f(D)
, then there is
x\in D
such that

f(x)=y
. Evaluating
\widehat{s}
at
y
, we find
\widehat{s}(y)=y
, so

\widehat{s}
is the identity on all of
f(D)
. Thus,
\widehat{S}_{b}
is the trivial group. Each
\mathcal{E}(b)
consists of two points, so (3) is satisfied. Point (4) is satisfied since the orbits
of
\mathcal{B}_{n,\ell}
under the action of
G_{n,\ell}
are in one-to-one correspondence with the letters of
\Sigma_{n,\ell}
, which are finite in number under the current hypotheses.

Finally, we consider (5). For a given
b\in\mathcal{B}_{n,\ell}
,

\mathcal{E}(b)=\{E_{0}(b),E_{1}(b)\},

where
E_{0}(b)
contains one member, and
E_{1}(b)
contains no more than
n
members. Thus, the bounded contractions property is satisfied with
C_{0}=n
.
We claim that
\mathcal{B}
is rich in contractions with constant
C_{1}=kn-n+2
. Indeed, suppose that the vertex
v=\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{\beta}\}
is consecutively ordered and
\beta\geq kn-n+2
. It suffices to argue that

\mathcal{L}(v)=\mathcal{L}(b_{1})\ldots\mathcal{L}(b_{\beta})

contains a subword of the form
x^{n}
, for then we are guaranteed the required contraction by case (ii) in the proof of Lemma
7.2
. We consider the maximal subwords of
\mathcal{L}(v)
having the form
x^{\alpha}
, for some
\alpha>0
. There are at most
k-1
such words, since any two maximal subwords of the
form
x^{\alpha^{\prime}}
and
x^{\alpha^{\prime\prime}}
must be separated by a label from
one of the automata
A_{n}(r_{m})
, with
m\in\{2,\ldots,k-1\}
, and the label
\mathcal{L}(v)
cannot contain more than one label from a given automaton
A_{n}(r_{m})
. Thus, if there were no subword of the form
x^{n}
, we would have at most

(k-1)(n-1)
occurrences of “
x
” in the word
\mathcal{L}(v)
. However, the remaining letters of
\mathcal{L}(v)
can be no more than
k
in number,
since each letter is chosen from the labels of a given automaton

A_{n}(r_{m})
, and at most one label can be selected from each automaton.
It follows that
\mathcal{L}(v)
contains no more than
(k-1)(n-1)+k=kn-n+1
symbols. However,
\mathcal{L}(v)=\beta>kn-n+1
by hypothesis. This is a contradiction. This proves (5).

Since (1)-(5) are satisfied, it now follows from Theorem
8.3
that
G_{n,\ell}
has type
F_{\infty}
when all of the
r_{i}
are rational.

∎
