Comparison of different exact generalized Langevin equations with a non-linear potential of mean force and an observable-dependent mass and friction
Benjamin J. A. Héry, Lucas Tepper, Roland R. Netz

Abstract
The Mori-Zwanzig projection formalism constitutes a powerful and robust framework for deriving equations of motion in terms of generalized Langevin equations (GLEs) for an arbitrary observable using evolution and projection operators. Based on this framework, we analyze the properties of four distinct GLEs for a scalar observable including a Markovian force derived from a generally non-linear potential, a non-Markovian friction force, and an orthogonal force, commonly interpreted as a random force. While all four GLEs are exact, they differ in the memory friction kernel, which may either be dependent or independent of the observable, and by the potential, which may either include or exclude the effective kinetic energy of the observable. Inclusion of the kinetic energy in the potential is advantageous for observables whose velocity satisfies Wick’s theorem, since this reproduces the correct distribution of the observable and its velocity even without contributions from the friction force and the orthogonal force.

II
Results

Generic derivation of a GLE

We consider a classical system of
N
particles or atoms in three-dimensional space with the generalized
position vector
\vec{r}\equiv\left(r_{1},\cdots,r_{3N}\right)^{T}
, the generalized momentum
vector
\vec{p}\equiv\left(p_{1},\cdots,p_{3N}\right)^{T}
, and the microscopic state of the
system
\vec{w}\equiv\left(r_{1},\cdots,r_{3N},p_{1},\cdots,p_{3N}\right)^{T}
, which
evolves in phase space denoted as
W=\mathbb{R}^{6N}
. We assume that the microscopic dynamics of
the system are derived from the generic time-independent many-body Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H(\vec{w})\equiv\sum_{n=1}^{3N}\frac{p_{n}^{2}}{2m_{n}}+V(\vec{r}),

(1)

where the first term represents the particle kinetic energy with
m_{n}
the mass of the
n
-th coordinate
and
V(\vec{r})
accounts for a generic interaction potential. The expectation value of an arbitrary
scalar time-independent Schrödinger-type observable
O_{S}(w)
is given by

\displaystyle o(t)\equiv\int d^{6N}\vec{w}\>\rho(t,\vec{w})O_{S}(\vec{w}).

(2)

For Hamiltonian dynamics, the time-dependent probability density distribution
\rho(t,\vec{w})
is
determined by the Liouville equation

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\rho(t,\vec{w})}{\partial t}=-\mathcal{L}\rho(t,\vec{w})

(3)

with the solution
\rho(t,\vec{w})=e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\rho(0,\vec{w})
and where

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=\sum_{n=1}^{3N}\left[\frac{p_{n}}{m_{n}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r_{n}}-\frac{\partial V(\vec{r})}{\partial r_{n}}\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{n}}\right]

(4)

defines the anti-self-adjoint Liouville operator. In sec.
S1
of
the supplementary material, we show that the expectation value of
O_{S}(w)
can alternatively be written as

\displaystyle o(t)=\int d^{6N}\vec{w}\>\rho(0,\vec{w})O(t,\vec{w})\equiv\langle O(t,\vec{w})\rangle,

(5)

where we defined the time-dependent Heisenberg-type observable

\displaystyle O(t,\vec{w})\equiv e^{t\mathcal{L}}O_{S}(\vec{w}).

(6)

In the following, we consider the observable

\displaystyle A(t,\vec{w})\equiv e^{t\mathcal{L}}A_{S}(\vec{r}),

(7)

where we choose
A_{S}(\vec{r})
to depend only on positional degrees of freedom, and we review the
main steps to derive a GLE for
A(t,\vec{w})
. First, we compute the first and the second partial
time derivatives of
A(t,\vec{w})
,

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\dot{A}(t,\vec{w})=e^{t\mathcal{L}}\dot{A}_{S}(\vec{w})\\
\ddot{A}(t,\vec{w})=e^{t\mathcal{L}}\ddot{A}_{S}(\vec{w}),\end{array}\right.

where we use the notation
\dot{A}_{S}(\vec{w})\equiv\mathcal{L}A_{S}(\vec{r})
and

\ddot{A}_{S}(\vec{w})\equiv\mathcal{L}^{2}A_{S}(\vec{r})
. We define
\mathcal{I}
as the
identity operator and introduce pairs of projection operators,
\mathcal{P}_{1}
and

\mathcal{Q}_{1}
and
\mathcal{P}_{2}
and
\mathcal{Q}_{2}
, which satisfy
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{P}_{1}+\mathcal{Q}_{1}
and
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{P}_{2}+\mathcal{Q}_{2}
, so that we
can decompose
\ddot{A}(t,\vec{w})
as

\displaystyle\ddot{A}(t,\vec{w})=e^{t\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{P}_{1}+\mathcal{Q}_{1})\ddot{A}_{S}(\vec{w}).

(10)

Next, we recall the Dyson decomposition identity
45
;
92

\displaystyle e^{t\mathcal{L}}=\int_{0}^{t}ds\>e^{s\mathcal{L}}\mathcal{P}_{2}\mathcal{L}e^{\left(t-s\right)\mathcal{Q}_{2}\mathcal{L}}+e^{t\mathcal{Q}_{2}\mathcal{L}},

(11)

and obtain the generic GLE

\displaystyle\ddot{A}(t,\vec{w})

\displaystyle=F_{\text{eff}}(t,\vec{w})

(12)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}ds\>F_{\text{f}}(s,t-s,\vec{w})+F_{\mathcal{Q}}(t,\vec{w}),

where we defined the effective force

\displaystyle F_{\text{eff}}(t,\vec{w})\equiv e^{t\mathcal{L}}\mathcal{P}_{1}\ddot{A}_{S}(\vec{w}),

(13)

the orthogonal force

\displaystyle F_{\mathcal{Q}}(t,\vec{w})\equiv e^{t\mathcal{Q}_{2}\mathcal{L}}\mathcal{Q}_{1}\ddot{A}_{S}(\vec{w}),

(14)

and the non-Markovian friction force

\displaystyle F_{\text{f}}(s,t,\vec{w})\equiv e^{s\mathcal{L}}\mathcal{P}_{2}\mathcal{L}F_{\mathcal{Q}}(t,\vec{w}).

(15)

As
\mathcal{P}_{1}
and
\mathcal{P}_{2}
are in general different projection operators, the procedure
leading to eq.
12
is thus a dual-projection scheme
83
;
4
;
47
.

Different projection operators

We choose the initial distribution
\rho(0,\vec{w})
in eq.
5
to be
the Boltzmann distribution determined by
H(\vec{w})
, i.e.

\displaystyle\rho(0,\vec{w})=\rho_{eq}(\vec{w})\equiv\frac{e^{-\beta H(\vec{w})}}{Z(\beta)},

(16)

such that
\mathcal{L}\rho_{eq}(\vec{w})=0
and where
Z(\beta)\equiv\int d^{6M}\vec{w}\>e^{-\beta H(\vec{w})}
is the partition function and
\beta\equiv(k_{B}T)^{-1}
the
inverse thermal energy, and define the conditional ensemble expectation of the generic observable

O(t,\vec{w})
with respect to
A_{S}(\vec{r})
by

\displaystyle\langle O(t,\vec{w})\rangle_{A}\equiv\frac{\langle O(t,\vec{w})\delta(A_{S}(\vec{r})-A)\rangle}{\langle\delta(A_{S}(\vec{r})-A)\rangle},

(17)

In sec.
S2
and
sec.
S3
, we detail how to construct a projection operator, derive its
generic properties using the Mori-Zwanzig formalism, and introduce all projections that we require for the
different GLEs. In sec.
S4
, we define the Mori projection
operator
64

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{M}O(t,\vec{w})\equiv\langle O(t,\hat{\vec{w}})\rangle+\frac{\langle O(t,\hat{\vec{w}})\dot{A}_{S}(\hat{\vec{w}})\rangle}{\langle\dot{A}_{S}^{2}(\hat{\vec{w}})\rangle}\dot{A}_{S}(\vec{w})

(18)

\displaystyle+\frac{\langle O(t,\hat{\vec{w}})(A_{S}(\hat{\vec{r}})-\langle A_{S}(\tilde{\vec{r}})\rangle)\rangle}{\langle(A_{S}(\hat{\vec{r}})-\langle A_{S}(\tilde{\vec{r}})\rangle)^{2}\rangle}(A_{S}(\vec{r})-\langle A_{S}(\hat{\vec{r}})\rangle).

where integration variables are denoted with a hat, i.e.
\hat{\vec{w}}
.

In sec.
S5
, we define the positional Zwanzig projection
operator
37
;
28

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{Z}O(t,\vec{w})\equiv\langle O(t,\hat{\vec{w}})\rangle_{A_{S}(\vec{r})},

(19)

from which we construct the linear-velocity projection operator
83

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{LV}O(t,\vec{w})

\displaystyle\equiv\mathcal{P}_{Z}O(t,\vec{w})

(20)

\displaystyle+\frac{\langle O(t,\hat{\vec{w}})\dot{A}_{S}(\hat{\vec{w}})\rangle_{A_{S}(\vec{r})}}{\langle\dot{A}_{S}^{2}(\hat{\vec{w}})\rangle_{A_{S}(\vec{r})}}\dot{A}_{S}(\vec{w}),

which consists of
\mathcal{P}_{Z}
plus an additional term that contains
\dot{A}_{S}(w)
. Finally, in sec.
S6
we define the kinetic-energy projection
operator
4

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{KE}O(t,\vec{w})

\displaystyle\equiv\mathcal{P}_{LV}O(t,\vec{w})

(21)

\displaystyle+\frac{\langle O(t,\hat{\vec{w}})(\dot{A}_{S}^{2}(\hat{\vec{w}})-\langle\dot{A}_{S}^{2}(\tilde{\vec{w}})\rangle_{A_{S}(\vec{r})})\rangle_{A_{S}(\vec{r})}}{\langle(\dot{A}_{S}^{2}(\hat{\vec{w}})-\langle\dot{A}_{S}^{2}(\tilde{\vec{w}})\rangle_{A_{S}(\vec{r})})^{2}\rangle_{A_{S}(\vec{r})}}

\displaystyle\times\left(\dot{A}_{S}^{2}(\vec{w})-\langle\dot{A}_{S}^{2}(\hat{\vec{w}})\rangle_{A_{S}(\vec{r})}\right),

which includes
\mathcal{P}_{LV}
plus an additional term that contains
\dot{A}_{S}^{2}(\vec{w})

and which can be interpreted as the effective kinetic energy of the observable.

Four different GLEs

By choosing different combinations of
\mathcal{P}_{M}
,
\mathcal{P}_{LV}
,
\mathcal{P}_{KE}
for

\mathcal{P}_{1}
and
\mathcal{P}_{2}
in the dual projection scheme, we derive four different
GLEs. The discussion in the main text focuses on observables
A(t,w)
whose velocities satisfy the conditional
Wick’s theorem given by

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\langle\dot{A}_{S}^{2n}(\hat{\vec{w}})\rangle_{A}=\frac{(2n)!}{2^{n}n!}(\langle\dot{A}_{S}^{2}(\hat{\vec{w}})\rangle_{A})^{n}\\
\langle\dot{A}_{S}^{2n+1}(\hat{\vec{w}})\rangle_{A}=0\end{array}\right.

for integer
n
, in the supplementary material we also discuss the case where eq.
II
does not hold.
First, by choosing
\mathcal{P}_{1}=\mathcal{P}_{LV}
and
\mathcal{P}_{2}=\mathcal{P}_{M}
, we obtain the observable-independent-friction GLE (I-GLE)
83

\displaystyle\ddot{A}(t,\vec{w})=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{M_{\text{eff}}(A(t,\vec{w}))}\frac{dU_{\text{eff}}(A(t,\vec{w}))}{dA}

(24)

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}ds\>\Gamma_{\text{I}}(t-s)\dot{A}(s,\vec{w})+F_{\text{I}}(t,\vec{w}),

where in sec.
S7
it is shown that the observable-dependent effective mass is defined by

\displaystyle M_{\text{eff}}(A)\equiv\frac{1}{\beta\langle\dot{A}_{S}^{2}(\hat{w})\rangle_{A}},

(25)

while the effective potential is given by

\displaystyle U_{\text{eff}}(A)\equiv U_{\text{pmf}}(A)+\frac{1}{\beta}\log M_{\text{eff}}(A),

(26)

and where the potential of mean force is given by

\displaystyle U_{\text{pmf}}(A)\equiv-\frac{1}{\beta}\log\langle\delta(A_{S}(\hat{\vec{r}})-A)\rangle.

(27)

Second, by choosing
\mathcal{P}_{1}=\mathcal{P}_{2}=\mathcal{P}_{LV}
, we obtain the
observable-dependent-friction GLE (D-GLE)
82

\displaystyle\ddot{A}(t,\vec{w})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{M_{\text{eff}}(A(t,\vec{w}))}\frac{dU_{\text{eff}}(A(t,\vec{w}))}{dA}

(28)

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}ds\>\Gamma_{\text{D}}(t-s,A(s,\vec{w}))\dot{A}(s,\vec{w})+F_{\text{D}}(t,\vec{w}).

Third, by choosing
\mathcal{P}_{1}=\mathcal{P}_{KE}
and
\mathcal{P}_{2}=\mathcal{P}_{M}
, we
obtain the observable-independent-friction kinetic-energy GLE (IKE-GLE)

\displaystyle\ddot{A}(t,\vec{w})=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{M_{\text{eff}}(A(t,\vec{w}))}\frac{\partial U_{\text{eff}}^{KE}(A(t,\vec{w}),\dot{A}(t,\vec{w}))}{\partial A}

(29)

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}ds\>\Gamma_{\text{IKE}}(t-s)\dot{A}(s,\vec{w})+F_{\text{IKE}}(t,\vec{w}),

where the effective potential is given by

\displaystyle U_{\text{eff}}^{KE}(A,\dot{A})

\displaystyle=U_{\text{pmf}}(A)

(30)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\beta}\log M_{\text{eff}}(A)+\frac{1}{2}M_{\text{eff}}(A)\dot{A}^{2},

see sec.
S8
for details.

Finally, by choosing
\mathcal{P}_{1}=\mathcal{P}_{KE}
and
\mathcal{P}_{2}=\mathcal{P}_{LV}
,
we obtain the observable-dependent-friction kinetic-energy GLE (DKE-GLE)
4

\displaystyle\ddot{A}(t,\vec{w})=-\frac{1}{M_{\text{eff}}(A(t,\vec{w}))}\frac{\partial U_{\text{eff}}^{KE}(A(t,\vec{w}),\dot{A}(t,\vec{w}))}{\partial A}

(31)

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}ds\>\Gamma_{\text{DKE}}(t-s,A(s,\vec{w}))\dot{A}(s,\vec{w})+F_{\text{DKE}}(t,\vec{w}).

We show in sec.
S9
that for the I-GLE and the IKE-GLE the observable-independent memory kernels are given by

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\alpha}(t)\equiv\frac{\langle\ddot{A}_{S}\vec{w})F_{\alpha}(t,\vec{w})\rangle}{\langle\dot{A}_{S}^{2}\vec{w})\rangle},

(32)

where
\alpha=I,IKE
and
F_{\alpha}(t,\vec{w})
satisfies

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\langle F_{\alpha}(t,\vec{w})\rangle=0\\
\langle F_{\alpha}(t,\vec{w})A_{S}(\hat{\vec{r}})\rangle=0\\
\langle F_{\alpha}(t,\vec{w})\dot{A}_{S}\vec{w})\rangle=0.\end{array}\right.

Furthermore,
F_{\alpha}(t,\vec{w})
and
\Gamma_{\alpha}(t)
satisfy a second-moment relation (SMR) if

\langle F_{\alpha}(t,\hat{\vec{w}})\rangle_{A}=0
, i.e.

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\alpha}(t)=\frac{\langle F_{\alpha}(0,\vec{w})F_{\alpha}(t,\vec{w})\rangle}{\langle\dot{A}_{S}^{2}(\vec{w})\rangle},

(36)

for
\alpha=I,IKE
.
We show in sec.
S10
that the D-GLE and the DKE-GLE display
observable-dependent memory kernels given by

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\beta}(t,A)\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{\langle\ddot{A}_{S}(\hat{\vec{w}})F_{\beta}(t,\hat{\vec{w}})\rangle_{A}}{\langle\dot{A}_{S}^{2}(\hat{\vec{w}})\rangle_{A}}

(37)

\displaystyle-\frac{d\log\langle\delta(A_{S}(\hat{\vec{r}})-A)\rangle}{dA}\frac{\langle F_{\beta}(t,\hat{\vec{w}})\dot{A}_{S}^{2}(\hat{\vec{w}})\rangle_{A}}{\langle\dot{A}_{S}^{2}(\hat{\vec{w}})\rangle_{A}}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\langle\dot{A}_{S}^{2}(\hat{\vec{w}})\rangle_{A}}\frac{\partial\langle F_{\beta}(t,\hat{\vec{w}})\dot{A}_{S}^{2}(\hat{\vec{w}})\rangle_{A}}{\partial A},

where
\beta=D,DKE
, and
F_{\beta}(t,\vec{w})
satisfies

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\langle F_{\beta}(t,\hat{\vec{w}})\rangle_{A}=0\\
\langle F_{\beta}(t,\hat{\vec{w}})A_{S}(\hat{\vec{r}})\rangle_{A}=0\\
\langle F_{\beta}(t,\hat{\vec{w}})\dot{A}_{S}(\hat{\vec{w}})\rangle_{A}=0,\end{array}\right.

which implies that eq.
II
holds for
F_{\beta}(t,\hat{\vec{w}})
.
Furthermore, we show that
F_{\beta}(t,\vec{w})
and

\Gamma_{\beta}(t)
satisfy an observable-dependent SMR, i.e.

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\beta}(t,A)=\frac{\langle F_{\beta}(0,\hat{\vec{w}})F_{\beta}(t,\hat{\vec{w}})\rangle_{A}}{\langle\dot{A}_{S}^{2}(\hat{\vec{w}})\rangle_{A}},

(41)

if
\langle F_{\beta}(t,\hat{\vec{w}})\dot{A}_{S}^{2}(\hat{\vec{w}})\rangle_{A}=0
or if
\langle F_{\beta}(t,\hat{\vec{w}})\dot{A}_{S}^{2}(\hat{\vec{w}})\rangle_{A}
solves the partial differential equation (PDE)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\langle F_{\beta}(t,\hat{\vec{w}})\dot{A}_{S}^{2}(\hat{\vec{w}})\rangle_{A}}{\partial A}=

\displaystyle\beta\langle F_{\beta}(t,\hat{\vec{w}})\dot{A}_{S}^{2}(\hat{\vec{w}})\rangle_{A}

(42)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dA}\left[U_{\text{pmf}}(A)-\frac{1}{2\beta}\log M_{\text{eff}}(A)\right].

We stress that the SMRs in eq.

and

not hold in general, but only when the restrictive condition
\langle F_{\alpha}(t,\hat{\vec{w}})\rangle_{A}=0

III
Discussion and conclusion

In this paper, we present four exact GLEs and their respective derivations for a scalar observable of interest

A
that satisfies Wick’s theorem (cf. eq.
II
) using different
projection operators: the I-GLE, the D-GLE, the IKE-GLE, and the DKE-GLE. We highlight
that when eq.
II
holds, all four GLEs display the same effective
mass
M_{\text{eff}}(A)
but are generally characterized by different effective potentials and
different memory kernels.

On one hand, we find that the effective potential of the IKE-GLE and the DKE-GLE
exactly reproduces the joint distribution of
A
and
\dot{A}
without any contributions of the friction or orthogonal forces,
which, we argue, makes them preferable to GLEs that lack this property. Moreover,
sec.
S8
derives expressions for
M_{\text{eff}}(A)
and

U_{\text{eff}}^{KE}(A,\dot{A})
(cf. eqs
S8.99

and
S8.100
) when eq.
II

does not hold and demonstrates that both quantities depend non-linearly on
U_{\text{pmf}}
.
Therefore, satisfying Wick’s theorem is a core prerequisite for the IKE-GLE and the
DKE-GLE to correctly reproduce the joint statistics of
A
and
\dot{A}
.

On the other hand, we show that no SMR between the orthogonal force and the memory kernel holds
in general for the four GLEs. As a matter of fact, in order for such a relation to hold the
orthogonal force must satisfy additional conditions that go beyond eqs.
II

or
II
. This is a subtle but important point, since
eqs.
II
and
II

are direct results from the Mori-Zwanzig formalism. They specify which properties are expected to
hold for each orthogonal force based on the choice of
\mathcal{P}_{2}
in Eq.
14
.
Thus, the SMR is not expected to hold in the general case. This provides a reinterpretation of other
works that explain the breaking of the SMR as a consequence of a non-harmonic

U_{\text{pmf}}(A)

28
;
41
.

Finally, Fig.
1
compares MD-data for the fast-folding protein Villin
using the fraction of native contacts reaction coordinate
A(t)
to simulations of the CM-GLE,
the I-GLE, and the IKE-GLE. We see that the I-GLE and the IKE-GLE
accurately sample the joint distribution
P(A,\dot{A})
although the observable does not satisfy
eq.
II
. This means that we can approximate the joint
probability distribution of
A(t)
and its velocity by
the corresponding Boltzmann distribution, while using
the I-GLE or the IKE-GLE to accurately reproduce kinetic quantities of the MD simulations, including
the potential of mean force, the configuration-dependent mass, and the memory kernel,
which demonstrates the usefulness of the GLEs discussed in this paper.

S2
Projection operator formalism for discrete sets

We review the general theory of projection operator formalism presented by H. Vroylandt in
ref.
82
. First, we consider a set

\displaystyle\varepsilon\equiv\bigcup_{k\in I}\{E_{k}(\vec{w})\}

(S2.5)

of observables where
I\subset\mathbb{N}
is a set of indices. Thus, the projection operator

\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}
that is associated with
\varepsilon
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}O(t,\vec{w})\equiv\sum_{k,k^{\prime}\in I}\langle O(t,\hat{\vec{w}})E_{k}(\hat{\vec{w}})\rangle(\hat{G}_{\varepsilon}^{-1})_{k,k^{\prime}}E_{k^{\prime}}(\vec{w})

(S2.6)

where
\hat{G}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}
is the inverse of the Gram matrix
\hat{G}_{\varepsilon}
defined
component-wise by

\displaystyle(\hat{G}_{\varepsilon})_{k,k^{\prime}}\equiv\langle E_{k}(\hat{\vec{w}})E_{k^{\prime}}(\hat{\vec{w}})\rangle

(S2.7)

which satisfies

\displaystyle\sum_{k"\in I}(\hat{G}_{\varepsilon}^{-1})_{k,k"}(\hat{G}_{\varepsilon})_{k",k^{\prime}}=\delta_{k,k^{\prime}}.

(S2.8)

Second, we show that
\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}
defines a linear operator, i.e.

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}(\lambda_{1}O_{1}(t,\vec{w})+\lambda_{2}O_{2}(t^{\prime},\vec{w}))=\lambda_{1}\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}O_{1}(t,\vec{w})+\lambda_{2}\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}O_{2}(t^{\prime},\vec{w})

(S2.9)

for any linear combination of Heisenberg-type observables
\lambda_{1}O_{1}(t,\vec{w})+\lambda_{2}O_{2}(t^{\prime},\vec{w})
. We compute

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}(\lambda_{1}O_{1}(t,\vec{w})+\lambda_{2}O_{2}(t^{\prime},\vec{w}))=\sum_{k,k^{\prime}\in I}\langle(\lambda_{1}O_{1}(t,\hat{\vec{w}})+\lambda_{2}O_{2}(t^{\prime},\hat{\vec{w}}))E_{k}(\hat{\vec{w}})\rangle(\hat{G}_{\varepsilon}^{-1})_{k,k^{\prime}}E_{k^{\prime}}(\vec{w}),

(S2.10)

and use that
\langle\cdot\rangle
defined in eq.
5
and the
summations over the indices
k
and
k^{\prime}
are linear operators to recover
eq.
S2.9
. Third, we show that each
E_{k}(\vec{w})
is an invariant of

\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}
, i.e.

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}E_{l}(\vec{w})=E_{l}(\vec{w}).

(S2.11)

We compute

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}E_{l}(\vec{w})=\sum_{k,k^{\prime}\in I}\langle E_{l}(\hat{\vec{w}})E_{k}(\hat{\vec{w}})\rangle(\hat{G}_{\varepsilon}^{-1})_{k,k^{\prime}}E_{k^{\prime}}(\vec{w}),

(S2.12)

and use eq.
S2.7
and eq.
S2.8
to recover
eq.
S2.11
. Fourth, we show that
\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}
defines an
idempotent operator, i.e.

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}^{2}O(t,\vec{w})=\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}O(t,\vec{w}).

(S2.13)

We compute

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}^{2}O(t,\vec{w})=\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}\left(\sum_{k,k^{\prime}\in I}\langle O(t,\hat{\vec{w}})E_{k}(\hat{\vec{w}})\rangle(\hat{G}_{\varepsilon}^{-1})_{k,k^{\prime}}E_{k^{\prime}}(\vec{w})\right)

(S2.14)

and use that
\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}
defines a linear operator and that each
E_{k}(\vec{w})
is
an invariant of
\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}
to recover eq.
S2.13
. Fifth, we show
that
\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}
defines a self-adjoint operator, i.e.

\displaystyle\langle O_{1}(t,\hat{\vec{w}})\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}O_{2}(t^{\prime},\hat{\vec{w}})\rangle=\langle O_{2}(t^{\prime},\hat{\vec{w}})\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}O_{1}(t,\hat{\vec{w}})\rangle

(S2.15)

for any Heisenberg-type observables
O_{1}(t,\vec{w})
and
O_{2}(t^{\prime},\vec{w})
. We compute

\displaystyle\langle O_{1}(t,\hat{\vec{w}})\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}O_{2}(t^{\prime},\hat{\vec{w}})\rangle=\sum_{k,k^{\prime}\in I}\langle O_{1}(t,\hat{\vec{w}})E_{k}(\hat{\vec{w}})\rangle(\hat{G}_{\varepsilon}^{-1})_{k,k^{\prime}}\langle O_{2}(t^{\prime},\hat{\vec{w}})E_{k^{\prime}}(\hat{\vec{w}})\rangle,

(S2.16)

reorder the right-hand side of eq.
S2.16
, and use that

\hat{G}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}
is a symmetric positive real matrix to recover
eq.
S2.15
. Sixth, we define the complementary projection operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}O(t,\vec{w})\equiv(\mathcal{I}-\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon})O(t,\vec{w})

(S2.17)

where
\mathcal{I}O(t,\vec{w})\equiv O(t,\vec{w})
is the identity operator. By construction,

\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}
also defines a linear, idempotent, and self-adjoint operator. Therefore,
for any generic observables
O_{1}(t,\vec{w})
and
O_{2}(t^{\prime},\vec{w})
, the correlation

\displaystyle\langle[\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}O_{1}(t,\hat{\vec{w}})]\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}O_{2}(t^{\prime},\hat{\vec{w}})\rangle=0

(S2.18)

vanishes. In particular, it follows for each
E_{k}(\vec{w})
that

\displaystyle\langle E_{k}(\hat{\vec{w}})\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}O(t,\hat{\vec{w}})\rangle=0.

(S2.19)

Seventh, we decompose
\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}
into a sum of orthogonal projection operators, i.e.

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}O(t,\vec{w})\equiv\underset{j\geq 1}{\sum}\mathcal{P}_{\tilde{\varepsilon}_{j}}O(t,\vec{w})\\
\mathcal{P}_{\tilde{\varepsilon}_{j}}O(t,\vec{w})\equiv\langle O(t,\hat{\vec{w}})\tilde{E}_{l}(\hat{\vec{w}})\rangle(\hat{\tilde{G}}_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}^{-1})_{l,l}\tilde{E}_{l}(\vec{w})\end{array}\right.

where

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\mathcal{P}_{\tilde{\varepsilon}_{j}}\mathcal{P}_{\tilde{\varepsilon}_{j^{\prime}}}O(t,\vec{w})=\delta_{j,j^{\prime}}\mathcal{P}_{\tilde{\varepsilon}_{j}}O(t,\vec{w})\\

\hat{G}_{\varepsilon}
defines a symmetric real positive matrix so that there

\hat{T}_{\varepsilon\tilde{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle(\hat{\tilde{G}}_{\tilde{\varepsilon}})_{l,l^{\prime}}=\sum_{k,k^{\prime}\in I}(\hat{T}_{\varepsilon\tilde{\varepsilon}}^{-1})_{l,k}(\hat{G}_{\varepsilon})_{k,k^{\prime}}(\hat{T}_{\varepsilon\tilde{\varepsilon}})_{k^{\prime},l^{\prime}}=\delta_{l,l^{\prime}}.

(S2.24)

We use eq.
S2.24
to compute the inverse of

and plug the result into

. We recover

where

\displaystyle E_{k}(\vec{w})=\sum_{l\in I}(\hat{T}_{\varepsilon\tilde{\varepsilon}})_{k,l}\tilde{E}_{l}(\vec{w}).

(S2.25)

Therefore, decomposing a sum of orthogonal projection operators is equivalent to transforming

\tilde{\varepsilon}

S3
Projection operator formalism for continuous sets

We extend the result of sec.
S2
to continuous projection sets of the form

\displaystyle\varepsilon\equiv\bigcup_{z\in\mathbb{R}}\{E(z,\vec{w})\}.

(S3.26)

Hence, the action of
\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}
on

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}O(t,\vec{w})\equiv\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dz\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dz^{\prime}\>\langle O(t,\hat{\vec{w}})E(z,\hat{\vec{w}})\rangle\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}(z,z^{\prime})E(z^{\prime},\vec{w}),

where
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}
is the inverse of the Gram kernel
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}
defined element-wise by

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}(z,z^{\prime})\equiv\langle E(z,\hat{\vec{w}})E(z^{\prime},\hat{\vec{w}})\rangle

and satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dz"\>\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}(z,z")\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}(z",z^{\prime})=\delta(z-z^{\prime}).

(S3.29)

Similar to sec.
S2

\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}
first defines a linear operator, i.e.

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}(\lambda_{1}O_{1}(t,\vec{w})+\lambda_{2}O_{2}(t^{\prime},\vec{w}))=\lambda_{1}\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}O_{1}(t,\vec{w})+\lambda_{2}\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}O_{2}(t^{\prime},\vec{w})

E(z,\vec{w})

\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}E(z",\vec{w})=E(z",\vec{w}).

(S3.31)

Third,
\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}
defines an idempotent operator, i.e.

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}^{2}O(t,\vec{w})=\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}O(t,\vec{w}).

(S3.32)

Fourth,
\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}
defines a self-adjoint operator, i.e.

\displaystyle\langle O_{1}(t,\hat{\vec{w}})\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}O_{2}(t^{\prime},\hat{\vec{w}})\rangle=\langle O_{2}(t^{\prime},\hat{\vec{w}})\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}O_{1}(t,\hat{\vec{w}})\rangle

for general Heisenberg-type observables
O_{1}(t,\vec{w})
and
O_{2}(t^{\prime},\vec{w})
. Fifth, the
complementary projection operator defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}O(t,\vec{w})\equiv(\mathcal{I}-\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon})O(t,\vec{w})

(S3.34)

also defines a linear, idempotent, and self-adjoint operator. Therefore, we recover that for all
Heisenberg-type observables
O_{1}(t,\vec{w})
and
O_{2}(t^{\prime},\vec{w})

\displaystyle\langle[\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}O_{1}(t,\hat{\vec{w}})]\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}O_{2}(t^{\prime},\hat{\vec{w}})\rangle=0

\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}
defines an orthogonal projection and we note in particular that

\displaystyle\langle E(z,\hat{\vec{w}})\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}O(t,\hat{\vec{w}})\rangle=0.

(S3.36)

Sixth, we now consider the countable union of continuous sets

\displaystyle\varepsilon=\bigcup_{i\geq 1}\varepsilon_{i},

(S3.37)

where each subset is defined by

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{i}=\bigcup_{z_{i}\in\mathbb{R}}\{E_{i}(z_{i},\vec{w})\}.

(S3.38)

Using these properties we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}O(t,\vec{w})\equiv\sum_{i,i^{\prime}\geq 1}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dz_{i}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dz_{i^{\prime}}^{\prime}\>\langle O(t,\hat{\vec{w}})E_{i}(z_{i},\hat{\vec{w}})\rangle(\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}(z_{i},z_{i^{\prime}}^{\prime}))_{i,i^{\prime}}E_{i^{\prime}}(z_{i^{\prime}}^{\prime},\vec{w}),

(S3.39)

where
\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}
is a symmetric real positive Gram kernel matrix defined component-wise by

\displaystyle(\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}(z_{i},z_{i^{\prime}}^{\prime}))_{i,i^{\prime}}=\langle E_{i}(z_{i},\hat{\vec{w}})E_{i^{\prime}}(z_{i^{\prime}}^{\prime},\hat{\vec{w}})\rangle.

(S3.40)

As in sec.
S2

\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{\varepsilon\tilde{\varepsilon}}(z^{\prime},z^{\prime})
such that

\displaystyle(\hat{\tilde{\mathcal{G}}}_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}(z_{j},z_{j^{\prime}}^{\prime}))_{j,j^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,i^{\prime}\geq 1}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dz_{i}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dz_{i^{\prime}}^{\prime}\>(\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{\varepsilon\tilde{\varepsilon}}^{-1}(z_{j},z_{i}))_{j,i}(\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}(z_{i},z_{i^{\prime}}^{\prime}))_{i,i^{\prime}}(\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{\varepsilon\tilde{\varepsilon}}(z_{i^{\prime}}^{\prime},z_{j^{\prime}}^{\prime}))_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}

(S3.41)

\displaystyle=(\hat{\tilde{\mathcal{G}}}_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}(z_{j},z_{j^{\prime}}^{\prime}))_{j,j^{\prime}}\delta_{j,j^{\prime}}

so that
\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}
can be decomposed into a sum of orthogonal projection operators, i.e.

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}O(t,\vec{w})\equiv\underset{j\geq 1}{\sum}\mathcal{P}_{\tilde{\varepsilon}_{j}}O(t,\vec{w})\\
\mathcal{P}_{\tilde{\varepsilon}_{j}}O(t,\vec{w})\equiv\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dz\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dz^{\prime}\>\langle O(t,\hat{\vec{w}})\tilde{E}_{j}(z,\hat{\vec{w}})\rangle(\hat{\tilde{\mathcal{G}}}_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}^{-1}(z,z^{\prime}))_{j,j}\tilde{E}_{j^{\prime}}(z^{\prime},\vec{w})\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\mathcal{P}_{\tilde{\varepsilon}_{j}}\mathcal{P}_{\tilde{\varepsilon}_{j^{\prime}}}O(t,\vec{w})=\delta_{j,j^{\prime}}\mathcal{P}_{\tilde{\varepsilon}_{j}}O(t,\vec{w})\\

and where

is related to

via

(S3.46)

In practice, this corresponds to transforming

Construction of
