A Parameter-Free First-Order Algorithm for Non-Convex Optimization with 𝒪 ~ ( ϵ − 5 / 3 ) \tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-5/3}) Global Rate
Sichao Xiong, Sadok Jerad, Coralia Cartis

Abstract
We introduce PF-AGD , the first parameter-free, deterministic, accelerated first-order method to achieve 𝒪 ( ϵ − 5 / 3 log ( 1 / ϵ ) ) \mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-5/3}\log(1/\epsilon)) oracle complexity bound when minimizing sufficiently smooth, non-convex functions; this is the best-known bound for first-order methods on smooth non-convex objectives. Unlike existing methods possessing this rate that require a priori knowledge of smoothness constants, we use an adaptive backtracking scheme and a gradient-based restart mechanism to estimate local curvature. This yields a practical algorithm that matches best-known theoretical rates. Empirically, PF-AGD outperforms the practical variant of AGD-Until-Guilty ( 7 ), as well as other parameter-free variants, and is a viable alternative to nonlinear conjugate gradient methods.

Introduction

Optimization algorithms are the workhorses of modern machine learning. A central question in optimization theory is the characterization of an algorithm’s
evaluation complexity
: for an objective function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, the number of iterations and oracle calls required to achieve
\epsilon
-stationarity, i.e., a point
x
such that
\|\nabla f(x)\|\leq\epsilon
(see
32
and
13
for an overview).

Problem Formulation.

We study unconstrained optimization problems of the form
\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)
, where
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is a potentially non-convex function bounded below. We assume that
f
is
L_{1}
-smooth (i.e.,
\nabla f
is
L_{1}
-Lipschitz continuous) and has
L_{3}
-Lipschitz third-order derivatives, but
L_{1}
and
L_{3}
are unknown; in this sense, our method is parameter-free. We consider
deterministic first-order
algorithms that access
f
and
\nabla f
through an oracle and measure complexity by the number of
\nabla f
oracle calls to compute an
\epsilon
-stationary point. While our theoretical framework is established under standard Lipschitz smoothness assumptions, these global assumptions can be significantly relaxed; the method only requires smoothness along the actual path taken by the iterates. This enables application to problems where global smoothness fails but local smoothness holds along the iterates’ trajectory.

Related Work.

We focus on non-convex optimization; for an extensive treatment of the convex counterpart, we refer the reader to
[
18
]
. For non-convex
L_{1}
-smooth objectives, steepest descent requires
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-2})
iterations to reach an
\epsilon
-stationary point
[
31
]
. A seminal development in non-convex optimization was the
AGD-Until-Guilty
framework
[
7
]
, which pioneered the application of accelerated gradient techniques to non-convex settings. Although
f
may not be strongly convex,
7
optimize a quadratically regularized objective assuming that it is strongly convex; if the anticipated progress bounds do not hold, the algorithm issues a certificate of non-convexity and exploits negative curvature to guarantee sufficient decrease. Under
L_{2}
-Lipschitz Hessians and
L_{3}
-Lipschitz third derivatives,
AGD-Until-Guilty
variants achieve the first rates surpassing
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-2})
, namely, of
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-7/4})
and
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-5/3})
, respectively.

A key limitation of

is that it requires problem parameters (
L_{1}
and
L_{3}
) to calibrate step sizes and momentum. In practice, these quantities are rarely known and difficult to estimate; moreover, the theoretically specified algorithm is impractical to run as the global constants are usually overly pessimistic. Indeed,
7

1

Contributions.

A central obstacle to achieving a parameter-free algorithm is that Nesterov acceleration relies on a tight coupling between the fixed step sizes and momentum. We circumvent this limitation with
PF-AGD
, a deterministic first-order algorithm, and prove that it preserves the optimal rate without prior knowledge of problem constants. Concretely, our contributions are:

•

We show that optimal accelerated rates for non-convex optimization do not require prior knowledge of global smoothness constants, giving the first
deterministic parameter-free
algorithm that attains an
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-5/3})
gradient complexity. Notably, the bound is independent of the problem dimension and is suitable for problems where
d
may be large.

•

Unlike existing methods that require problem constants
L_{1}
and
L_{3}
, we develop a novel backtracking and restart scheme that estimates local curvature using only gradient information.

•

Our method is a practical alternative to nonlinear CG and is implemented faithfully to theory. Benchmarks on standard test functions and ML tasks also show that
PF-AGD
consistently outperforms
AGD-Until-Guilty
(
7
) and
28
.

2
PF-AGD Algorithm

Conceptually,
PF-AGD
preserves acceleration by behaving like accelerated gradient descent with adaptive
L_{1}
estimation when the function is locally strongly convex, and switches to negative curvature exploitation when this assumption fails. The algorithm comprises two loops: an
inner loop
(§
2.1
), indexed by
t
, which performs accelerated gradient steps while estimating
L_{1}
and detecting non-convexity; an
outer loop
(§
2.2
), indexed by
k
, which exploits negative curvature and refines the estimate
M_{k}
of the third-order Lipschitz constant
L_{3}
. A violation in the inner loop triggers early termination, returning a witness pair
(u,v)
used to update
M_{k}
and produce a new iterate. The proofs justifying our design choices are deferred to Appendix
A
.

Adaptive Backtracking for
L_{1}
.

Following
14
, at each inner iteration
k
we maintain an estimate
L_{1}^{(k)}>0
of the gradient Lipschitz constant and take a gradient step of size
\alpha_{k}=1/L_{1}^{(k)}
in direction
d_{k}=-\nabla f(x_{k})
. Standard Armijo backtracking accepts a step if

f(x_{k}+\alpha_{k}d_{k})\;\leq\;f(x_{k})+c\cdot\alpha_{k}\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),d_{k}\rangle,\qquad c\in(0,1),

(1)

and otherwise shrinks
\alpha_{k}\leftarrow\rho\,\alpha_{k}
with fixed
\rho\in(0,1)
, but this fails to preserve the
\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{Q}\log(1/\varepsilon))
convergence rate of accelerated methods. To recover this rate, we replace
\rho
with an adaptive factor
\hat{\rho}
based on how badly the condition is violated. The violation map is

v(\alpha_{k})\;:=\;\tfrac{f(x_{k}+\alpha_{k}d_{k})-f(x_{k})}{c\cdot\alpha_{k}\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),d_{k}\rangle},\quad\hat{\rho}\bigl(v(\alpha_{k})\bigr)\;:=\;\max\bigl\{\varepsilon_{\min},\;\rho{\,\tfrac{1-c}{1-c\cdot v(\alpha_{k})}}\bigr\},

(2)

so condition (
1
) can be written as
v(\alpha_{k})\geq 1
, and
\varepsilon_{\min}\ll 1
prevents numerical instability. Intuitively, the shrinkage factor reduces step sizes more aggressively for larger violations. This bounds the Lipschitz estimate (
L_{1}^{(k)}\leq\max\{L_{1}^{(0)},\tfrac{L_{1}}{2(1-c)\rho}\}
), preventing step sizes from collapsing to zero.

2.1
The Inner Loop

We maintain three iterates:
y_{t}
(gradient step),
x_{t}
(extrapolated point), and
w^{\min}_{t}
, the best auxiliary descent point selected from
\{\zeta_{0},\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{t}\}
, where
\zeta_{t}
is obtained by a gradient step from
y_{t}
. We write
x_{0}^{t}:=(x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})
for the sequence of iterates through index
t
; similarly for
y_{0}^{t}
. The inner loop,
Modified-AGD
, initially assumes that
f
is pathwise
\sigma
-strongly convex, i.e., that

f(u)\geq f(v)+\langle\nabla f(v),\,u-v\rangle+\tfrac{\sigma}{2}\|u-v\|^{2}

(3)

holds for all
(u,v)\in\{(y_{s},x_{s}),(w,x_{s}),\,(w,y_{s}),\,(y_{s},w)\}
and
s=0,\ldots,t-1
, where
w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the vector returned by
Certify-Progress
. Unlike global strong convexity, (
3
) only requires convexity-like behavior along the trajectory of iterates. This relaxation makes it possible for
Find-Witness
to detect violations directly from observed iterates. Under this assumption, the algorithm runs accelerated steps via
AGD-Step
while
Certify-Progress
monitors for violations of (
3
). If a violation is detected, a witness pair
(u,v)
is returned; if
f(y_{t})>f(y_{0})
, it returns
Restart
; otherwise, it returns
Null
. In effect, the witness pair provides a concrete certificate that the function violates local convexity, which the outer loop can then exploit.

AGD-Step
.

Algorithm
4
estimates
L_{1}
via Adaptive Backtracking (Algorithm
1
), updates the momentum parameter
\omega
, and takes a Nesterov step to produce
(x_{t},y_{t})
. It then performs an additional line search on
\zeta_{t}:=y_{t}-\frac{1}{L^{(t)}_{1}}\nabla f(y_{t})
to ensure the descent condition

f(w^{\min}_{t})\;\leq\;f(\zeta_{t})\;\leq\;f(y_{t})-\tfrac{1}{2L_{1}^{(t)}}\|\nabla f(y_{t})\|^{2}

(4)

holds; if violated,
L^{(t)}_{1}
is multiplied by
\gamma>1
until the condition is satisfied (guaranteed whenever
L_{1}^{(t)}\geq L_{1}
). The condition is sufficient to give us the following progress bounds.

Technical contributions.

The Lyapunov analysis of
14
establishes the recursion only for
w=x^{*}
, where
\nabla f(w)=0
eliminates the gradient slack and
f(y_{k})-f(x^{*})\geq 0
holds automatically. Both properties fail in our setting, since
Certify-Progress
must check progress against observable iterates (
y_{0}
and
w^{\min}_{t}
) rather than against an unknown minimizer. We therefore extend the analysis along two tracks. For an
arbitrary
reference point
w
(Appendix
A.2.1
), the recursion acquires an additive
\|\nabla f(w)\|^{2}
slack as in (
6
); a concrete counterexample (Appendix
A.2.2
) shows this slack cannot be removed in general. For a
descent
reference point
w
satisfying (
4
), the slack vanishes at the cost of a
(1+2\Delta_{k})
factor (Appendix
A.2.3
) whose product across iterations contributes the
(3Q_{t})^{m}
term in (
8
). This necessitates the introduction of
w^{\min}_{t}
, which satisfies (
4
) by construction and enables the tighter bound. A second problem is that
f(y_{t})>f(y_{0})
can occur during accelerated steps and break the monotonicity requirement for Corollary
1
(
11
). We introduce a restart in this case. Naively overwriting
y_{t}
destroys the Lyapunov chain because the auxiliary point
z_{t}(y_{t},x_{t})
jumps. We resolve this with a momentum correction (Algorithm
6
, line 4) chosen precisely so that
z_{t}(y_{t},x_{t})=z
remains invariant before and after the restart, preserving the telescoping structure and the accelerated rate. Chaining these recursions inductively while accounting for possible restarts yields the following proposition; see Appendix
A.4.1
for the full proof.

Proposition 1

(Progress bounds)
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be
L_{1}
-smooth. Fix
w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and assume
f
is pathwise strongly convex. Given Algorithm
2
,
x_{0}=y_{0}
and
L_{1}^{(0)}>\sigma
, with
c\in[1/2,1)
and restarts handled by Algorithm
6
, with
\psi(w)=f(y_{0})-f(w)+\frac{\sigma}{2}\|w-y_{0}\|^{2}
:

f(y_{t})-f(w)\leq e^{-t/\sqrt{Q_{t}}}Q^{3/2}_{t}\psi(w)+\tfrac{2Q_{t}^{2}}{\sigma}\|\nabla f(w)\|^{2},

(7)

where
Q_{t}
is the condition number at iteration
t
. If additionally
w
satisfies (
4
),

f(y_{t})-f(w)\leq e^{-t/\sqrt{Q_{t}}}\;(3Q_{t})^{m}\tfrac{Q_{t}^{3/2}}{Q_{0}^{3/2}}\;\psi(w),

(8)

where

counts the total number of condition-number increases.

The presence of restarts affects both bounds slightly: bound (7) acquires a factor of two in the slack term and bound (8) involves
m
. We track this in lines 5 and 11 of
Modified-AGD
. Next, we look at the key subroutine which detects violations based on the progress bound.

Certify-Progress
.

Algorithm
5
checks whether the iterates maintain sufficient progress under the pathwise strong convexity assumption, using the bounds of Proposition
1
. Line 1 checks bound (
7
) with
w=y_{0}
. Line 4 checks bound (
8
) with
w=w^{\min}_{t}
, which satisfies the descent condition (
4
) by construction. It returns one of three outcomes:
Null
(no violation detected); a witness
w_{t}\in\{y_{0},w^{\min}_{t}\}
whose progress bound has been violated, triggering
Find-Witness
; or
Restart
, triggered when
f(y_{t})>f(y_{0})
, which is required for (
11
) to apply.

Restart-Handler
.

A restart is triggered by
f(y_{t})>f(y_{0})
. Since
f(y_{s})\leq f(y_{0})
for all
s<t
(otherwise a restart would have been triggered earlier), the algorithm backtracks to
y_{t-1}
and takes a steepest descent step with line search. Provided
L_{1}^{(t)}\geq L_{1}
, this guarantees
f(y_{t})<f(y_{t-1})\leq f(y_{0})
, restoring the descent condition. To prevent the restart from breaking the Lyapunov recursion, the Lyapunov point
z
must be kept invariant: the update
x_{t}\leftarrow(z+\scriptstyle\sqrt{Q_{t}^{\text{agd}}}y_{t})/(1+\scriptstyle\sqrt{Q_{t}^{\text{agd}}})
in line 4 of Algorithm
6
achieves exactly this, ensuring
z_{t}(y_{t},x_{t})=z
regardless of the new iterates.

Find-Witness
.

Given the witness
w_{t}
returned by
Certify-Progress
,
Find-Witness
iterates over previous indices
j<t
searching for the exact pair
(u,v)
violating (
3
). At each step it checks the four candidate pairs

3
Global Rate of Convergence for PF-AGD

The total complexity is obtained by combining the number of outer iterations required to reduce the objective value (§
3.1
), and the number of inner iterations needed to either certify sufficient progress or detect non-convexity (§
3.2

G\leq 2T+2b+2\bigl\lceil\log_{\gamma}(\bar{L}_{1}/L_{1}^{(0)})\bigr\rceil

With the boundedness of

(13)

Conclusion and Future Work.

Our results suggest that theoretically optimal methods can also be practically competitive, with
PF-AGD
matching the empirical performance of nonlinear CG methods. Taken together, our results indicate that the dependence of accelerated non-convex methods on global smoothness constants is not fundamental. Moreover, comparison with
28
suggests that leveraging
L_{3}
yields an empirically more efficient algorithm than parameter-free methods assuming an
L_{2}
-Lipschitz Hessian. We note that negative curvature remains rarely exploited in practice, suggesting the loss function is “effectively convex” in large portions of the optimization trajectory, consistent with empirical observations of
7
. This phenomenon merits further investigation.

We identify three avenues for extending this work. Future research can focus on removing the logarithmic factor, a byproduct of the inner loop, via alternative acceleration schemes.
28
offer a parameter-free approach without the logarithmic overhead; however, it necessitates iterate averaging and a non-standard momentum parameter of
\omega_{k}=k/(k+1)
, where
k
counts steps within the current epoch. Crucially, their convergence analysis relies on a Jensen-type inequality for gradients, which we hypothesize cannot be directly generalized to third-order smoothness. While nonlinear CG is empirically robust for non-convex problems, theoretical guarantees are lacking. We defer the development of analysis for nonlinear CG on non-convex landscapes to future research. Finally, the current algorithm is deterministic and relies on exact gradient computations. This requirement limits scalability in modern deep learning contexts where mini-batching is standard (e.g., Adam
[
24
]
and SGD
[
37
]
). Future work will seek to extend this framework to the stochastic setting by integrating the stochastic line search techniques proposed by
33
,
5
, and
41
.

In this section, we introduce notation and briefly overview the definitions and results we use throughout. We begin by characterizing the regularity of the functions under consideration.

Definition 1

(
L_{p}
-Lipschitz
p
-th derivative)
.

Let

is
p

\|\nabla^{p}f(x)-\nabla^{p}f(y)\|\leq L_{p}\|x-y\|\qquad\text{for all }x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
\|\cdot\|
denotes the operator norm induced by the Euclidean norm. In particular, for

, we say that

L_{1}
-smooth
if its gradient is
L_{1}
-Lipschitz continuous.

While Lipschitz continuity of the derivatives provides an upper bound on how quickly the function can change, we also require a lower bound on the curvature to ensure the existence of a unique minimizer. This is captured by the notion of strong convexity.

Definition 2

(
\sigma
-strong convexity)
.

A function

is said to be

f(y)\geq f(x)+\langle\nabla f(x),y-x\rangle+\tfrac{\sigma}{2}\|y-x\|^{2}.

By combining the upper bound provided by

-smoothness and the lower bound provided by
\sigma

. This relationship is formalized by the condition number.

Definition 3

Let

be
L_{1}
-smooth and
\sigma
-strongly convex. The condition number of

Since

by definition, it follows that

k
and setting
c_{1},c_{2}=1
, we have

L_{1}^{(k)}\leq\max\Bigl\{L_{1}^{(0)},\;\frac{L_{1}}{2(1-c)\rho}\Bigr\}:=\bar{L}_{1}

Proof.

Applying Proposition
2
with
c_{1},c_{2}=1
gives

\alpha_{k}\geq\min\Bigl\{\alpha_{0},\,\rho\frac{2(1-c)}{L_{1}}\Bigr\}

and we get the desired bound by taking the reciprocal as per the definition of
L_{1}^{(k)}
.
∎

It remains to combine adaptive line search with NAG, leading to Algorithm
10
. For adaptive algorithms, we denote the
k
-th iteration estimate of the condition number as
Q_{k}:=L_{1}^{(k)}/\sigma
with the convention that
Q_{-1}:=Q_{0}
.

Algorithm 10

Nesterov’s Accelerated Gradient Descent (Adaptive)

1:

Input:

x_{k},y_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\nabla f(x_{k})
,
estimate
L_{1}^{(k)}
of the Lipschitz constant with
L_{1}^{(k)}>\sigma>0

2:

Output:
next points
x_{k+1},y_{k+1}

3:

y_{k+1}\leftarrow x_{k}-\frac{1}{L_{1}^{(k)}}\,\nabla f(x_{k})

4:

\omega_{k}\leftarrow\tfrac{\sqrt{L_{1}^{(k)}}-\sqrt{\sigma}}{\sqrt{L_{1}^{(k)}}+\sqrt{\sigma}}

5:

x_{k+1}\leftarrow(1+\omega_{k})\,y_{k+1}-\omega_{k}\,y_{k}

To establish that adaptive line search preserves the accelerated rate of NAG,
14
employ a Lyapunov argument based on the function
V^{(w)}_{t}
defined by

V^{(w)}_{t}(y_{k},x_{k})=f(y_{k})-f(w)+\frac{\sigma}{2}\,\|z_{t}(y_{k},x_{k})-w\|^{2},

(15)

where
w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and the auxiliary point
z_{t}(y_{k},x_{k})
is defined as

z_{t}(y_{k},x_{k})=x_{k}+\sqrt{Q_{t-1}}\,(x_{k}-y_{k}).

The first result gives a recursion for the iterates while keeping
Q_{k}
fixed in the auxiliary point.

Lemma 2

(Cavalcanti et al.
[
14
, Lemma 1]
)
.

Let
f
be
L_{1}
-smooth and
\sigma
-strongly convex. If the Lipschitz constant estimates
L^{(k)}_{1}
of accelerated gradient descent (Algorithm
10
) are generated by adaptive backtracking (Algorithm
1
) with
c\in\mathopen{[}1/2,1\mathopen{)}
,
w=x^{*}
and
L^{(0)}_{1}>\sigma
, then for
k\geq 0

\displaystyle(1+\delta_{k+1})V^{(x^{*})}_{k+1}(y_{k+1},x_{k+1})-V^{(x^{*})}_{k+1}(y_{k},x_{k})\leq 0,

where
\delta_{k+1}=1/(\sqrt{Q_{k}}-1)
.

Note that the proof does not require
w=x^{*}
to be a minimizer. Next we want to keep the iterates fixed and obtain a recursion for varying
t
indices of
z_{t}(y_{k},x_{k})
, i.e.,
Q_{t}
.

Lemma 3

(Cavalcanti et al.
[
14
, Lemma 2]
)
.

Let
f
be
L_{1}
-smooth and
\sigma
-strongly convex. Given initial points
x_{0}=y_{0}
, if the estimates
L^{(k)}_{1}
of the Lipschitz constant in accelerated gradient descent (Algorithm
10
) are generated monotonically by adaptive backtracking (Algorithm
1
) with
c\in\mathopen{[}1/2,1\mathopen{)}
and
L^{(0)}_{1}>\sigma
, then for
k\geq 0

\displaystyle V^{(x^{*})}_{k+1}(y_{k},x_{k})\leq\frac{Q_{k}^{2}}{Q_{k-1}^{2}}V^{(x^{*})}_{k}(y_{k},x_{k}).

This proof does rely on the optimality of
w=x^{*}
; specifically, the non-negativity of the objective gap
f(y_{k})-f(x^{*})\geq 0
in (30) and (36), and implicitly,
\nabla f(x^{*})=0
in (37).

A.2
Generalized Lyapunov Analysis for Adaptive AGD

The analysis proceeds along two parallel tracks, distinguished by what is known about
w
. In the first track (Sections
A.2.1
and
A.2.4
), we show that the Lyapunov recursion acquires an additional slack term proportional to
\|\nabla f(w)\|^{2}
. A concrete counterexample in Section
A.2.1
shows this slack is necessary. In the second track (Sections
A.2.3
and
A.2.5
), the additional assumption that
w
satisfies the descent condition allows the slack term to be eliminated entirely, recovering a cleaner recursion. The first track feeds into the non-convexity certificate in line 1 of
Certify-Progress
with
w=y_{0}
; the second feeds into the stall detection in line 4 with
w=w^{\min}_{t}
.

A.2.1
Lyapunov Recursion for Arbitrary Reference Points

As previously mentioned, we no longer have the fact that
f(y_{k})-f(w)
is non-negative. Nevertheless, the following lemma provides us a good lower bound for our analysis.

Lemma 4
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be differentiable. Fix
w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. If for
k=0,1,\ldots,t-1
we have

f(y_{k})\geq f(w)+\nabla f(w)^{\top}(y_{k}-w)+\frac{\sigma}{2}\lVert y_{k}-w\rVert^{2}.

(16)

Then for all
w,y_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

f(y_{k})-f(w)\geq-\frac{1}{\sigma}\|\nabla f(w)\|^{2}.

Proof.

By the pathwise strong convexity assumption,

f(y_{k})-f(w)\geq\langle\nabla f(w),y_{k}-w\rangle+\frac{\sigma}{2}\|y_{k}-w\|^{2}.

Using Young’s inequality, for any
\gamma>0
,

\langle a,b\rangle\geq-\frac{1}{2\gamma}\|a\|^{2}-\frac{\gamma}{2}\|b\|^{2},

and choosing
\gamma=\frac{\sigma}{2}
gives

\langle\nabla f(w),y_{k}-w\rangle\geq-\frac{1}{\sigma}\|\nabla f(w)\|^{2}-\frac{\sigma}{4}\|y_{k}-w\|^{2}.

Substituting into the previous inequality yields

f(y_{k})-f(w)\geq-\frac{1}{\sigma}\|\nabla f(w)\|^{2}+\frac{\sigma}{4}\|y_{k}-w\|^{2}\geq-\frac{1}{\sigma}\|\nabla f(w)\|^{2}.

∎

With this lemma we can derive the following recursion for the auxiliary point with an additional slack term proportional to
\|\nabla f(w)\|^{2}
.

Lemma 5
.

Let
f
be
L_{1}
-smooth. Fix
w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and assume that the pathwise strong convexity condition in (
16
) holds. Given initial points
x_{0}=y_{0}
, if the estimates
L^{(k)}_{1}
of the Lipschitz constant in accelerated gradient descent (Algorithm
10
) are generated monotonically by adaptive backtracking (Algorithm
1
) with
c\in[1/2,1)
and
L^{(0)}_{1}>\sigma
, then for
k\geq 0
,

V^{(w)}_{k+1}(y_{k},x_{k})\leq\frac{Q_{k}^{3/2}}{Q_{k-1}^{3/2}}V^{(w)}_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})+\frac{Q_{k}^{3/2}}{\sigma Q_{k-1}^{3/2}}\|\nabla f(w)\|^{2}.

(17)

In particular, when
w=x^{*}
is a minimizer of
f
,
\nabla f(w)=0
and (
17
) reduces to

V^{(x^{*})}_{k+1}(y_{k},x_{k})\;\leq\;\frac{Q_{k}^{3/2}}{Q_{k-1}^{3/2}}V^{(x^{*})}_{k}(y_{k},x_{k}).

Proof.

We prove the bound for each
k\geq 0
directly. The case
k=0
follows from
Q_{-1}:=Q_{0}
,

\displaystyle z_{1}(y_{0},x_{0})=x_{0}+\sqrt{Q_{0}}(x_{0}-y_{0})=x_{0}=x_{0}+\sqrt{Q_{-1}}(x_{0}-y_{0})=z_{0}(y_{0},x_{0}).

Moreover, we have that

\displaystyle V^{(w)}_{1}(y_{0},x_{0})

\displaystyle=f(y_{0})-f(w)+\frac{\sigma}{2}\|z_{1}(y_{0},x_{0})-w\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{Q_{0}^{3/2}}{Q_{-1}^{3/2}}\Bigl(f(y_{0})-f(w)+\frac{\sigma}{2}\|z_{0}(y_{0},x_{0})-w\|^{2}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{Q_{0}^{3/2}}{Q_{-1}^{3/2}}V^{(w)}_{0}(y_{0},x_{0}),

which establishes the base case.
To prove the inductive step, we divide the analysis into two cases, each representing a possible sign of
\langle x_{k}-y_{k},x_{k}-w\rangle
.
For each case, we bound

\displaystyle D_{k}:=

\displaystyle\,\|x_{k}-w+\sqrt{Q_{k}}(x_{k}-y_{k})\|^{2}-\|z_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})-w\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ 2(\sqrt{Q_{k}}-\sqrt{Q_{k-1}})\langle x_{k}-w,x_{k}-y_{k}\rangle+(Q_{k}-Q_{k-1})\|x_{k}-y_{k}\|^{2}.

(18)

In turn, bounds on (
18
) translate into bounds on
V^{(w)}_{k+1}(y_{k},x_{k})-V^{(w)}_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})
, since

\displaystyle V^{(w)}_{k+1}(y_{k},x_{k})-V^{(w)}_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})=

\displaystyle\ \frac{\sigma}{2}D_{k}.

(19)

Then, to prove the inductive step, we express bounds on (
19
) in terms of
V^{(w)}_{k+1}
and
V^{(w)}_{k}
.

Case 1:
\langle x_{k}-y_{k},x_{k}-w\rangle\geq 0
.

Since
L^{(k)}_{1}\geq L^{(k-1)}_{1}
, then
\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}/\sqrt{Q_{k}}\leq 1
, so that

\displaystyle\sqrt{Q_{k}}-\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}\leq\frac{Q_{k}}{\sqrt{Q_{k}}}-\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}\frac{\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}{\sqrt{Q_{k}}}=\frac{Q_{k}-Q_{k-1}}{\sqrt{Q_{k}}}.

Hence, applying the inequality above to (
18
) and then adding a non-negative
\|x_{k}-w\|^{2}
term to it, we get

\displaystyle D_{k}\leq

\displaystyle\ 2\frac{Q_{k}-Q_{k-1}}{\sqrt{Q_{k}}}\langle x_{k}-w,x_{k}-y_{k}\rangle+(Q_{k}-Q_{k-1})\|x_{k}-y_{k}\|^{2}+\frac{Q_{k}-Q_{k-1}}{Q_{k}}\|x_{k}-w\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{Q_{k}-Q_{k-1}}{Q_{k}}\|x_{k}-w+\sqrt{Q_{k}}(x_{k}-y_{k})\|^{2}.

(20)

Plugging (
20
) back into (
19
) yields

\displaystyle V^{(w)}_{k+1}(y_{k},x_{k})-V^{(w)}_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Q_{k}-Q_{k-1}}{Q_{k}}\frac{\sigma}{2}\|x_{k}-w+\sqrt{Q_{k}}(x_{k}-y_{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{Q_{k}-Q_{k-1}}{Q_{k}}\Bigl(V^{(w)}_{k+1}(y_{k},x_{k})-(f(y_{k})-f(w))\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Q_{k}-Q_{k-1}}{Q_{k}}V^{(w)}_{k+1}(y_{k},x_{k})+\frac{Q_{k}-Q_{k-1}}{\sigma Q_{k}}\|\nabla f(w)\|^{2},

(21)

where the last inequality follows from Lemma
4
, namely
f(y_{k})-f(w)\geq-\frac{1}{\sigma}\|\nabla f(w)\|^{2}
.
Thus, rearranging terms in (
21
) and then multiplying both sides by
Q_{k}/Q_{k-1}
, we obtain

\displaystyle V^{(w)}_{k+1}(y_{k},x_{k})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Q_{k}}{Q_{k-1}}V^{(w)}_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})+\frac{Q_{k}-Q_{k-1}}{\sigma Q_{k-1}}\|\nabla f(w)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Q^{3/2}_{k}}{Q^{3/2}_{k-1}}V^{(w)}_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})+\frac{Q^{3/2}_{k}}{\sigma Q^{3/2}_{k-1}}\|\nabla f(w)\|^{2}.

Case 2:
\langle x_{k}-y_{k},x_{k}-w\rangle<0
.

As in the previous case, we start by bounding the gap (
18
). The assumption gives

\|y_{k}-w\|^{2}=\|x_{k}-w\|^{2}-2\langle x_{k}-w,x_{k}-y_{k}\rangle+\|x_{k}-y_{k}\|^{2}\;\geq\;\|x_{k}-w\|^{2}.

(22)

Splitting
Q_{k}-Q_{k-1}=\sqrt{Q_{k}}(\sqrt{Q_{k}}-\sqrt{Q_{k-1}})+\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}(\sqrt{Q_{k}}-\sqrt{Q_{k-1}})
and adding/subtracting
(\sqrt{Q_{k}}-\sqrt{Q_{k-1}})/\sqrt{Q_{k}}\cdot\|x_{k}-w\|^{2}
, (
18
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle D_{k}=

\displaystyle\,2\frac{\sqrt{Q_{k}}-\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}{\sqrt{Q_{k}}}\langle x_{k}-w,\sqrt{Q_{k}}(x_{k}-y_{k})\rangle+\sqrt{Q_{k}}(\sqrt{Q_{k}}-\sqrt{Q_{k-1}})\|x_{k}-y_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}(\sqrt{Q_{k}}-\sqrt{Q_{k-1}})\|x_{k}-y_{k}\|^{2}+(1-1)\frac{\sqrt{Q_{k}}-\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}{\sqrt{Q_{k}}}\|x_{k}-w\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\frac{\sqrt{Q_{k}}-\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}{\sqrt{Q_{k}}}\,\big\|x_{k}-w+\sqrt{Q_{k}}(x_{k}-y_{k})\big\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}(\sqrt{Q_{k}}-\sqrt{Q_{k-1}})\,\|x_{k}-y_{k}\|^{2}-\frac{\sqrt{Q_{k}}-\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}{\sqrt{Q_{k}}}\,\|x_{k}-w\|^{2}.

(23)

Next, we use the following elementary inequality, which is a consequence of
\|a/\alpha+b\alpha\|^{2}\geq 0
:

\displaystyle\|a-b\|^{2}=\|a\|^{2}-2\langle a,b\rangle+\|b\|^{2}\leq(1+1/\alpha^{2})\|a\|^{2}+(1+\alpha^{2})\|b\|^{2}.

Since
z_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})-x_{k}=\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}(x_{k}-y_{k})
, we have
Q_{k-1}\|x_{k}-y_{k}\|^{2}=\|z_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})-x_{k}\|^{2}=\|(z_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})-w)-(x_{k}-w)\|^{2}
. Applying the inequality with
a=z_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})-w
,
b=x_{k}-w
,
\alpha^{2}=\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}/\sqrt{Q_{k}}
,

\displaystyle Q_{k-1}\|x_{k}-y_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{Q_{k}}}{\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}\Big)\|z_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})-w\|^{2}+\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}{\sqrt{Q_{k}}}\Big)\|x_{k}-w\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{Q_{k}}+\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}{\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}\,\|z_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})-w\|^{2}+\frac{\sqrt{Q_{k}}+\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}{\sqrt{Q_{k}}}\,\|x_{k}-w\|^{2}.

(24)

Multiplying (
24
) by
(\sqrt{Q_{k}}-\sqrt{Q_{k-1}})/\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}
and using

(\sqrt{Q_{k}}-\sqrt{Q_{k-1}})(\sqrt{Q_{k}}+\sqrt{Q_{k-1}})=Q_{k}-Q_{k-1}
yields

\displaystyle\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}(\sqrt{Q_{k}}-\sqrt{Q_{k-1}})\,\|x_{k}-y_{k}\|^{2}

(25)

\displaystyle\leq\;\frac{Q_{k}-Q_{k-1}}{Q_{k-1}}\,\|z_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})-w\|^{2}+\frac{\sqrt{Q_{k}}-\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}{\sqrt{Q_{k}}}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{Q_{k}}+\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}{\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}\,\|x_{k}-w\|^{2}.

Substituting (
25
) into (
23
) and applying (
22
),

\displaystyle D_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sqrt{Q_{k}}-\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}{\sqrt{Q_{k}}}\,\big\|x_{k}-w+\sqrt{Q_{k}}(x_{k}-y_{k})\big\|^{2}

(26)

\displaystyle+\frac{Q_{k}-Q_{k-1}}{Q_{k-1}}\,\|z_{k}-w\|^{2}+\frac{\sqrt{Q_{k}}-\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}{\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}\,\|y_{k}-w\|^{2}.

Combining (
26
) with (
19
) gives

V_{k+1}^{(w)}(y_{k},x_{k})-V_{k}^{(w)}(y_{k},x_{k})\leq\frac{\sigma}{2}\Big(A\|x_{k}-w+\sqrt{Q_{k}}(x_{k}-y_{k})\|^{2}+B\|z_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})-w\|^{2}+C\|y_{k}-w\|^{2}\Big),

(27)

where
A:=(\sqrt{Q_{k}}-\sqrt{Q_{k-1}})/\sqrt{Q_{k}}
,

B:=(Q_{k}-Q_{k-1})/Q_{k-1}
,

C:=(\sqrt{Q_{k}}-\sqrt{Q_{k-1}})/\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}
.

It remains to express the three norms in terms of the Lyapunov values. By Lemma
4
,

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma}{2}\,\|x_{k}-w+\sqrt{Q_{k}}(x_{k}-y_{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq V_{k+1}^{(w)}(y_{k},x_{k})+\frac{1}{\sigma}\|\nabla f(w)\|^{2},

(28)

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma}{2}\,\|z_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})-w\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq V_{k}^{(w)}(y_{k},x_{k})+\frac{1}{\sigma}\|\nabla f(w)\|^{2}.

(29)

Applying pathwise strong convexity with (
16
), we obtain

\displaystyle V^{(w)}_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})

\displaystyle=f(y_{k})-f(w)+\frac{\sigma}{2}\|z_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})-w\|^{2}

(30)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\sigma}{2}\|y_{k}-w\|^{2}+\frac{\sigma}{2}\|z_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})-w\|^{2}+\langle\nabla f(w),y_{k}-w\rangle.

(31)

We can further lower bound the inner product using Young’s inequality. Fix
\gamma=\sigma/2
and use

\langle a,b\rangle\;\geq\;-\frac{1}{2\gamma}\|a\|^{2}-\frac{\gamma}{2}\|b\|^{2}=-\frac{1}{\sigma}\|a\|^{2}-\frac{\sigma}{4}\|b\|^{2}.

With
a=\nabla f(w)
,
b=y_{k}-w
this gives

\langle\nabla f(w),y_{k}-w\rangle\;\geq\;-\frac{1}{\sigma}\|\nabla f(w)\|^{2}-\frac{\sigma}{4}\|y_{k}-w\|^{2}.

Plugging in (
31
) we get

V^{(w)}_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})\geq\frac{\sigma}{4}\|y_{k}-w\|^{2}+\frac{\sigma}{2}\|z_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})-w\|^{2}-\frac{1}{\sigma}\|\nabla f(w)\|^{2}

so rearranging we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma}{2}\|y_{k}-w\|^{2}\leq 2V^{(w)}_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})+\frac{2}{\sigma}\|\nabla f(w)\|^{2}-\sigma\|z_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})-w\|^{2}

(32)

We begin by substituting (
32
) into (
27
), yielding

\displaystyle V^{(w)}_{k+1}(y_{k},x_{k})-V^{(w)}_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})\leq\;

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{Q_{k}}-\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}{\sqrt{Q_{k}}}\frac{\sigma}{2}\|x_{k}-w+\sqrt{Q_{k}}(x_{k}-y_{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\Bigl(\frac{Q_{k}-Q_{k-1}}{Q_{k-1}}-2\frac{\sqrt{Q_{k}}-\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}{\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}\Bigr)\frac{\sigma}{2}\|z_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})-w\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+2\frac{\sqrt{Q_{k}}-\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}{\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}V^{(w)}_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})+\frac{2}{\sigma}\frac{\sqrt{Q_{k}}-\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}{\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}\|\nabla f(w)\|^{2}.

(33)

Note that the new coefficient in front of
\frac{\sigma}{2}\|z_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})-w\|^{2}
is non-negative:

\displaystyle\frac{Q_{k}-Q_{k-1}}{Q_{k-1}}-2\frac{\sqrt{Q_{k}}-\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}{\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}=\Bigl(\frac{\sqrt{Q_{k}}}{\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}-1\Bigr)^{2}\geq 0.

Hence we may apply (
28
) and (
29
) to upper bound the remaining normed terms:

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma}{2}\|x_{k}-w+\sqrt{Q_{k}}(x_{k}-y_{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq V^{(w)}_{k+1}(y_{k},x_{k})+\frac{1}{\sigma}\|\nabla f(w)\|^{2},

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma}{2}\|z_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})-w\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq V^{(w)}_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})+\frac{1}{\sigma}\|\nabla f(w)\|^{2}.

Plugging these into (
33
) gives

\displaystyle V^{(w)}_{k+1}(y_{k},x_{k})-V^{(w)}_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})\leq\;

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{Q_{k}}-\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}{\sqrt{Q_{k}}}\Bigl(V^{(w)}_{k+1}(y_{k},x_{k})+\frac{1}{\sigma}\|\nabla f(w)\|^{2}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\!+\Bigl(\frac{Q_{k}-Q_{k-1}}{Q_{k-1}}-2\frac{\sqrt{Q_{k}}-\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}{\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}\Bigr)\Bigl(V^{(w)}_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})+\frac{1}{\sigma}\|\nabla f(w)\|^{2}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\!+2\frac{\sqrt{Q_{k}}-\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}{\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}V^{(w)}_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})+\frac{2}{\sigma}\frac{\sqrt{Q_{k}}-\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}{\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}\|\nabla f(w)\|^{2}.

(34)

Now the cancellation is explicit on the
V^{(w)}_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})
terms:

\displaystyle\Bigl(\frac{Q_{k}-Q_{k-1}}{Q_{k-1}}-2\frac{\sqrt{Q_{k}}-\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}{\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}+2\frac{\sqrt{Q_{k}}-\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}{\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}\Bigr)V^{(w)}_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{Q_{k}-Q_{k-1}}{Q_{k-1}}V^{(w)}_{k}(y_{k},x_{k}).

Similarly, the
\frac{1}{\sigma}\|\nabla f(w)\|^{2}
coefficients combine as

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{Q_{k}}-\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}{\sqrt{Q_{k}}}+\Bigl(\frac{Q_{k}-Q_{k-1}}{Q_{k-1}}-2\frac{\sqrt{Q_{k}}-\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}{\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}\Bigr)+2\frac{\sqrt{Q_{k}}-\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}{\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{\sqrt{Q_{k}}-\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}{\sqrt{Q_{k}}}+\frac{Q_{k}-Q_{k-1}}{Q_{k-1}}.

Therefore (
34
) simplifies to

\displaystyle V^{(w)}_{k+1}(y_{k},x_{k})-V^{(w)}_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})\leq\;

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{Q_{k}}-\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}{\sqrt{Q_{k}}}\,V^{(w)}_{k+1}(y_{k},x_{k})+\frac{Q_{k}-Q_{k-1}}{Q_{k-1}}\,V^{(w)}_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\sigma}\Bigl(\frac{\sqrt{Q_{k}}-\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}{\sqrt{Q_{k}}}+\frac{Q_{k}-Q_{k-1}}{Q_{k-1}}\Bigr)\|\nabla f(w)\|^{2}.

Moving all
V^{(w)}_{k+1}(y_{k},x_{k})
terms to the left-hand side and all
V^{(w)}_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})
to the right-hand side, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}{\sqrt{Q_{k}}}V^{(w)}_{k+1}(y_{k},x_{k})\leq

\displaystyle\ \frac{Q_{k}}{Q_{k-1}}V^{(w)}_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})+C_{k}\|\nabla f(w)\|^{2},

(35)

where

C_{k}=\frac{\sqrt{Q_{k}}-\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}{\sigma\sqrt{Q_{k}}}+\frac{Q_{k}-Q_{k-1}}{\sigma Q_{k-1}}=\frac{1}{\sigma}\Bigl(\frac{Q_{k}}{Q_{k-1}}-\frac{\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}{\sqrt{Q_{k}}}\Bigr)\leq\frac{Q_{k}}{\sigma Q_{k-1}}

Multiplying both sides of (
35
) by
\sqrt{Q_{k}}/\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}
, and then using the fact that
\sqrt{Q_{k}}\geq 0
yields

\displaystyle V^{(w)}_{k+1}(y_{k},x_{k})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sqrt{Q}_{k}}{\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}\frac{Q_{k}}{Q_{k-1}}V^{(w)}_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})+\frac{\sqrt{Q}_{k}}{\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}C_{k}\|\nabla f(w)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Q_{k}^{3/2}}{Q_{k-1}^{3/2}}V^{(w)}_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})+\frac{Q_{k}^{3/2}}{\sigma Q_{k-1}^{3/2}}\|\nabla f(w)\|^{2}

Therefore, both when
\langle x_{k}-w,x_{k}-y_{k}\rangle\geq 0
and when
\langle x_{k}-w,x_{k}-y_{k}\rangle<0
, the inequality

\displaystyle V^{(w)}_{k+1}(y_{k},x_{k})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Q_{k}^{3/2}}{Q_{k-1}^{3/2}}V^{(w)}_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})+\frac{Q_{k}^{3/2}}{\sigma Q_{k-1}^{3/2}}\|\nabla f(w)\|^{2}

holds generically for all
y_{k},x_{k}
, proving the lemma.
∎

A.2.2
Necessity of the Slack Term

The
\|\nabla f(w)\|^{2}
term cannot be removed in general. The following example (Figure

f(x)=\frac{1}{2}x^{2}+\log(\cosh(x)),\qquad\sigma=1,\qquad L_{1}=2,

,
L_{1}^{(0)}=1
,
\alpha_{0}=1
, backtracking parameters
c=0.5
,
\rho=0.8
, and comparison point
w=-1
.

At
k=0
, we have
\nabla f(2)=2+\tanh(2)\approx 2.964
, giving initial Lyapunov value

V_{1}^{(-1)}(y_{0},x_{0})=(3.325-0.934)+\frac{1}{2}(3)^{2}=6.891.

The backtracking check yields
v(1)\approx 0.559<1

A.2.3
Lyapunov Recursion for Descent Reference Points

Under the additional descent condition that
w
lies below
y_{k}
by at least one gradient step, we strengthen Lemma
4
and obtain a tighter recursion without the
\|\nabla f(w)\|^{2}
slack.

Lemma 6
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be differentiable. Let
y_{k},w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be points such that the following descent condition holds for some
L^{(k)}_{1}>0
:

f(w)\leq f(y_{k})-\frac{1}{2L^{(k)}_{1}}\|\nabla f(y_{k})\|^{2}.

(36)

Assume that for all
k=0,\dots,t-1
we have

f(w)\geq f(y_{k})+\nabla f(y_{k})^{\top}(w-y_{k})+\frac{\sigma}{2}\lVert w-y_{k}\rVert^{2}.

(37)

Then the squared distance between
y_{k}
and
w
is bounded by the function gap as follows:

\frac{\sigma}{2}\|y_{k}-w\|^{2}\leq 4Q_{k}(f(y_{k})-f(w)).

Proof.

From the descent assumption (
36
), we can rearrange terms to obtain:

\|\nabla f(y_{k})\|^{2}\leq 2L^{(k)}_{1}(f(y_{k})-f(w)).

By pathwise strong convexity (
37
), we have:

\displaystyle f(y_{k})-f(w)

\displaystyle\leq\langle\nabla f(y_{k}),y_{k}-w\rangle-\frac{\sigma}{2}\|y_{k}-w\|^{2}.

Applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality (
\langle a,b\rangle\leq\|a\|\|b\|
) to the inner product term:

f(y_{k})-f(w)\leq\|\nabla f(y_{k})\|\|y_{k}-w\|-\frac{\sigma}{2}\|y_{k}-w\|^{2}

(38)

Since
f(y_{k})-f(w)\geq 0
by assumption, the right-hand side of (
38
) must be non-negative. Thus:

\displaystyle\|\nabla f(y_{k})\|\|y_{k}-w\|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\sigma}{2}\|y_{k}-w\|^{2}

\displaystyle\|y_{k}-w\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\sigma}\|\nabla f(y_{k})\|.

Squaring both sides yields:

\|y_{k}-w\|^{2}\leq\frac{4}{\sigma^{2}}\|\nabla f(y_{k})\|^{2}.

Substituting the bound for
\|\nabla f(y_{k})\|^{2}
yields

\|y_{k}-w\|^{2}\leq\frac{4}{\sigma^{2}}\left[2L^{(k)}_{1}(f(y_{k})-f(w))\right]=\frac{8L^{(k)}_{1}}{\sigma^{2}}(f(y_{k})-f(w)).

Multiplying both sides by
\frac{\sigma}{2}
and using the definition
Q_{k}=L^{(k)}_{1}/\sigma
:

\frac{\sigma}{2}\|y_{k}-w\|^{2}\leq 4\frac{L^{(k)}_{1}}{\sigma}(f(y_{k})-f(w))=4Q_{k}(f(y_{k})-f(w)).

∎

Applying the lemma above, we derive a recursion analogous to Lemma
3
that eliminates the
\|\nabla f(w)\|^{2}
term at the cost of an additional
(1+2\Delta_{k})
factor.

Lemma 7
.

In addition to Lemma
5
, let w be such that
f(w)\leq f(y_{k})-\frac{1}{2L^{(k)}_{1}}\|\nabla f(y_{k})\|^{2}
. Assume that the pathwise strong convexity condition in (
37
) holds. Then

V^{(w)}_{k+1}(y_{k},x_{k})\leq(1+2\Delta_{k})\frac{Q^{3/2}_{k}}{Q^{3/2}_{k-1}}V^{(w)}_{k}(y_{k},x_{k}),

where
\Delta_{k}:=Q_{k}-Q_{k-1}\geq 0
.

Proof.

We prove the bound for each
k\geq 0
directly. The case
k=0
follows from
Q_{-1}:=Q_{0}
,

\displaystyle z_{1}(y_{0},x_{0})=x_{0}+\sqrt{Q_{0}}(x_{0}-y_{0})=x_{0}=x_{0}+\sqrt{Q_{-1}}(x_{0}-y_{0})=z_{0}(y_{0},x_{0}).

Moreover, we have that

\displaystyle V^{(w)}_{1}(y_{0},x_{0})

\displaystyle=f(y_{0})-f(w)+\frac{\sigma}{2}\|z_{1}(y_{0},x_{0})-w\|^{2}

\displaystyle=(1+2\Delta_{0})\frac{Q_{0}^{3/2}}{Q_{-1}^{3/2}}\Bigl(f(y_{0})-f(w)+\frac{\sigma}{2}\|z_{0}(y_{0},x_{0})-w\|^{2}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=(1+2\Delta_{0})\frac{Q_{0}^{3/2}}{Q_{-1}^{3/2}}V^{(w)}_{0}(y_{0},x_{0}),

where
\Delta_{0}=0
, establishing the base case. To prove the inductive step, we again divide the analysis in two cases. For each case, we bound

\displaystyle D_{k}:=

\displaystyle\,\|x_{k}-w+\sqrt{Q_{k}}(x_{k}-y_{k})\|^{2}-\|z_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})-w\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ 2(\sqrt{Q_{k}}-\sqrt{Q_{k-1}})\langle x_{k}-w,x_{k}-y_{k}\rangle+(Q_{k}-Q_{k-1})\|x_{k}-y_{k}\|^{2}.

(39)

In turn, bounds on (
39
) translate into bounds on
V^{(w)}_{k+1}(y_{k},x_{k})-V^{(w)}_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})
, since

\displaystyle V^{(w)}_{k+1}(y_{k},x_{k})-V^{(w)}_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})=

\displaystyle\ \frac{\sigma}{2}D_{k}.

(40)

Then, to prove the inductive step, we express bounds on (
40
) in terms of
V^{(w)}_{k+1}
and
V^{(w)}_{k}
.

Case 1:
\langle x_{k}-y_{k},x_{k}-w\rangle\geq 0
.

This time plugging (
20
) back into (
19
) from Lemma
5
yields

\displaystyle V^{(w)}_{k+1}(y_{k},x_{k})-V^{(w)}_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Q_{k}-Q_{k-1}}{Q_{k}}\frac{\sigma}{2}\|x_{k}-w+\sqrt{Q_{k}}(x_{k}-y_{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Q_{k}-Q_{k-1}}{Q_{k}}V^{(w)}_{k+1}(y_{k},x_{k}),

(41)

where the last inequality follows from the definition of
V^{(w)}_{k}
, as
f(y_{k})-f(w)\geq 0
implies

\displaystyle V^{(w)}_{k+1}(y_{k},x_{k})\geq\frac{\sigma}{2}\|x_{k}-w+\sqrt{Q_{k}}(x_{k}-y_{k})\|^{2}.

(42)

Thus, rearranging terms in (
41
) and multiplying both sides by
Q_{k}/Q_{k-1}
, we obtain

\displaystyle V^{(w)}_{k+1}(y_{k},x_{k})\leq\frac{Q_{k}}{Q_{k-1}}V^{(w)}_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})\leq(1+2\Delta_{k})\frac{Q_{k}^{3/2}}{Q_{k-1}^{3/2}}V^{(w)}_{k}(y_{k},x_{k}),

where the second inequality holds because
Q_{k}/Q_{k-1}\geq 1
and
\Delta_{k}\geq 0
.

Case 2:
\langle x_{k}-y_{k},x_{k}-w\rangle<0
.

From (
27
) in Lemma
5
we have

V_{k+1}^{(w)}(y_{k},x_{k})-V_{k}^{(w)}(y_{k},x_{k})\leq\frac{\sigma}{2}\Big(A\|x_{k}-w+\sqrt{Q_{k}}(x_{k}-y_{k})\|^{2}+B\|z_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})-w\|^{2}+C\|y_{k}-w\|^{2}\Big),

(43)

where
A:=(\sqrt{Q_{k}}-\sqrt{Q_{k-1}})/\sqrt{Q_{k}}
,

B:=(Q_{k}-Q_{k-1})/Q_{k-1}
,

C:=(\sqrt{Q_{k}}-\sqrt{Q_{k-1}})/\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}
.

Now, as in (
42
), the fact that
f(y_{k})-f(w)\geq 0
implies

\displaystyle V^{(w)}_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})=f(y_{k})-f(w)+\frac{\sigma}{2}\|z_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})-w\|^{2}\geq\frac{\sigma}{2}\|z_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})-w\|^{2}.

(44)

Applying Lemma
6
, we obtain

\displaystyle 4Q_{k}V^{(w)}_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})\geq 4Q_{k}(f(y_{k})-f(w))\geq\frac{\sigma}{2}\|y_{k}-w\|^{2}.

(45)

Plugging in (
42
), (
44
) and (
45
) back into (
43
), and then moving all
V^{(w)}_{k+1}(y_{k},x_{k})
terms to the left-hand side and all
V^{(w)}_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})
to the right-hand side, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}{\sqrt{Q_{k}}}V^{(w)}_{k+1}(y_{k},x_{k})\leq

\displaystyle\ \Bigl(\frac{Q_{k}}{Q_{k-1}}+4Q_{k}\frac{\sqrt{Q_{k}}-\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}{\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}\Bigr)V^{(w)}_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})

(46)

Multiplying both sides of (
46
) by
\sqrt{Q_{k}}/\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}
, and using the fact that
\sqrt{Q_{k}}\geq\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}
yields

\displaystyle V^{(w)}_{k+1}(y_{k},x_{k})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sqrt{Q}_{k}}{\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}\Bigl(\frac{Q_{k}}{Q_{k-1}}+4Q_{k}\frac{\sqrt{Q_{k}}-\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}{\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}\Bigr)V^{(w)}_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})

\displaystyle=\Bigl(1+\frac{4\Delta_{k}}{\phi_{k}+1}\Bigr)\frac{Q^{3/2}_{k}}{Q^{3/2}_{k-1}}V^{(w)}_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})

\displaystyle\leq(1+2\Delta_{k})\frac{Q^{3/2}_{k}}{Q^{3/2}_{k-1}}V^{(w)}_{k}(y_{k},x_{k}),

where
\Delta_{k}:=Q_{k}-Q_{k-1}\geq 0
and
\phi_{k}=\sqrt{Q_{k}/Q_{k-1}}\geq 1
.

The equality holds because

\frac{\sqrt{Q_{k}}}{\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}\Bigl(\frac{Q_{k}}{Q_{k-1}}+4Q_{k}\frac{\sqrt{Q_{k}}-\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}{\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}\Bigr)=\phi_{k}\Bigl(\phi_{k}^{2}+4Q_{k}(\phi_{k}-1)\Bigr).

Using
Q_{k}=\phi_{k}^{2}Q_{k-1}
, this becomes
\phi_{k}^{3}\Bigl(1+4Q_{k-1}(\phi_{k}-1)\Bigr)
. Moreover,

\Delta_{k}=Q_{k}-Q_{k-1}=Q_{k-1}(\phi_{k}^{2}-1)=Q_{k-1}(\phi_{k}-1)(\phi_{k}+1),

so
Q_{k-1}(\phi_{k}-1)=\frac{\Delta_{k}}{\phi_{k}+1}
. Substituting yields

\frac{\sqrt{Q_{k}}}{\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}\Bigl(\frac{Q_{k}}{Q_{k-1}}+4Q_{k}\frac{\sqrt{Q_{k}}-\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}{\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}}\Bigr)=\Bigl(1+\frac{4\Delta_{k}}{\phi_{k}+1}\Bigr)\frac{Q_{k}^{3/2}}{Q_{k-1}^{3/2}}.

Multiplying both sides by
V_{k}^{(w)}(y_{k},x_{k})\geq 0
gives the claimed equality. The final inequality holds because
\phi_{k}+1\geq 2
. Therefore, in both cases, the inequality

\displaystyle V^{(w)}_{k+1}(y_{k},x_{k})\leq(1+2\Delta_{k})\frac{Q^{3/2}_{k}}{Q^{3/2}_{k-1}}V^{(w)}_{k}(y_{k},x_{k}),

holds generically for all
y_{k},x_{k}
, proving the lemma.
∎

A.2.4
Convergence Bound for Arbitrary Reference Points

With the recursions above on the auxiliary point as well as Lemma
2
, we are ready to derive the new progress bounds.

Proposition 3
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be
L_{1}
-smooth. Fix
w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and assume that the pathwise strong convexity condition in (
3
) holds. Given initial points
x_{0}=y_{0}
, if the estimates
L_{1}^{(t)}
of the Lipschitz constant in accelerated gradient descent (Algorithm
10
) are generated monotonically by adaptive backtracking (Algorithm
1
) with
c\in[1/2,1)
and
L_{1}^{(0)}>\sigma
, then for
t\geq 0

f(y_{t})-f(w)\;\leq\;e^{\frac{-t}{\sqrt{Q_{t-1}}}}Q_{t-1}^{3/2}\,\psi(w)\;+\;\frac{\|\nabla f(w)\|^{2}}{\sigma}\;Q_{t-1}^{3/2}\Bigl(\sqrt{Q_{t-1}}-1\Bigr),

where
\psi(w)=f(y_{0})-f(w)+\frac{\sigma}{2}\|w-y_{0}\|^{2}
.

Proof.

Combining Lemmas
2
and
5
, we have for every
0\leq k\leq t-1

\displaystyle V^{(w)}_{k+1}(y_{k+1},x_{k+1})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1+\delta_{k}}V^{(w)}_{k+1}(y_{k},x_{k})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1+\delta_{k}}\frac{Q_{k}^{3/2}}{Q_{k-1}^{3/2}}V^{(w)}_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})+\frac{1}{1+\delta_{k}}\frac{Q_{k}^{3/2}}{\sigma Q_{k-1}^{3/2}}\|\nabla f(w)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}})\frac{Q_{k}^{3/2}}{Q_{k-1}^{3/2}}V^{(w)}_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})+(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{Q_{k-1}}})\frac{Q_{k}^{3/2}}{\sigma Q_{k-1}^{3/2}}\|\nabla f(w)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{Q_{t-1}}})\frac{Q_{k}^{3/2}}{Q_{k-1}^{3/2}}V^{(w)}_{k}(y_{k},x_{k})+(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{Q_{t-1}}})\frac{Q_{k}^{3/2}}{\sigma Q_{k-1}^{3/2}}\|\nabla f(w)\|^{2},

where the last inequality uses
Q_{k}\leq Q_{t-1}
and the monotonicity of
x\mapsto 1-1/\sqrt{x}
.

By induction on
t
, we obtain that for all
t\geq 0
,

\displaystyle V^{(w)}_{t}(y_{t},x_{t})\leq

\displaystyle\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{Q_{t-1}}}\Bigr)^{t}\frac{Q_{t-1}^{3/2}}{Q_{0}^{3/2}}\,V^{(w)}_{0}(y_{0},x_{0})+\frac{\|\nabla f(w)\|^{2}}{\sigma}\;\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{Q_{t-1}}}\Bigr)^{t-i}\frac{Q_{t-1}^{3/2}}{Q_{i-1}^{3/2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{Q_{t-1}}}\Bigr)^{t}\frac{Q_{t-1}^{3/2}}{Q_{0}^{3/2}}\,V^{(w)}_{0}(y_{0},x_{0})+\frac{\|\nabla f(w)\|^{2}}{\sigma}\;\frac{Q_{t-1}^{3/2}}{Q_{0}^{3/2}}\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{Q_{t-1}}}\Bigr)^{t-i}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{Q_{t-1}}}\Bigr)^{t}Q_{t-1}^{3/2}\,\psi(w)+\frac{\|\nabla f(w)\|^{2}}{\sigma}\;Q_{t-1}^{3/2}\Bigl(\sqrt{Q_{t-1}}-1\Bigr)\Bigl(1-\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{Q_{t-1}}}\Bigr)^{t}\Bigr).

By definition of
V^{(w)}_{t}
and non-negativity of the quadratic term,

f(y_{t})-f(w)\;\leq\;V^{(w)}_{t}(y_{t},x_{t})\qquad\forall t.

Combining this with the above yields

\displaystyle f(y_{t})-f(w)\leq

\displaystyle\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{Q_{t-1}}}\Bigr)^{t}Q_{t-1}^{3/2}\,\psi(w)+\frac{\|\nabla f(w)\|^{2}}{\sigma}\;Q_{t-1}^{3/2}\Bigl(\sqrt{Q_{t-1}}-1\Bigr)\Bigl(1-\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{Q_{t-1}}}\Bigr)^{t}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{\frac{-t}{\sqrt{Q_{t-1}}}}Q_{t-1}^{3/2}\,\psi(w)+\frac{\|\nabla f(w)\|^{2}}{\sigma}\;Q_{t-1}^{3/2}\Bigl(\sqrt{Q_{t-1}}-1\Bigr)\quad\text{since $Q_{t-1}\geq 1$}.

∎

A.3
Estimating the Third-Order Lipschitz Constant

In this section, we address the remaining key limitation of the
AGD-Until-Guilty
framework: its dependence on prior knowledge of the third-order Lipschitz constant
L_{3}
. The algorithm
Guarded-Non-Convex-AGD
by Carmon et al. requires prior knowledge of
L_{3}
in two places: to set the regularization parameter
\alpha=2L_{3}^{1/3}\epsilon^{2/3}
and the negative-curvature step size
\eta=\sqrt{2\alpha/L_{3}}
. We justify the changes in
PF-AGD
which allow us to replace the use of
L_{3}
with a running estimate
M_{k}
.

The section is organized as follows. Subsection
A.3.1
defines the proxy conditions and justifies that these conditions correctly identify when
M_{k}
is insufficient. Subsections
A.3.2
and
A.3.3
then verify that the progress bound of Carmon et al.
[
7
, Lemma 7]
and the outer iteration count
K
both carry over unchanged up to constants.

Since we are returning two extra pairs
(y_{j},w_{t}),(w_{t},y_{j})
from
7
, we give a straightforward justification to show that the negative curvature exploitation in Carmon et al.
[
7
, Lemmas 3 and 6]
still holds.

For Carmon et al.
[
7
, Lemma 3]
, we must show that
\|y_{j}-w_{t}\|\leq 4\tau
. Without loss of generality let
u=w_{t}
and
v=y_{j}
as the other case is symmetric. In Carmon et al.
[
7
, Lemma 3]
, we always have that
\|u-y_{0}\|\leq\tau
. But since
y_{j}
is one of the iterates, we also have
\|y_{j}-y_{0}\|\leq\tau
. By the triangle inequality, we have
\|y_{j}-w_{t}\|\leq 2\tau\leq 4\tau
.

For Carmon et al.
[
7
, Lemma 6]
, we must show that
f(v)\leq f(y_{0})+14\alpha\tau^{2}
for
v\in\{y_{j},w_{t}\}
. We first consider
v=y_{j}
. By Corollary
1
,
\hat{f}(y_{j})\leq\hat{f}(y_{0})=f(y_{0})
and since the penalty term is non-negative,
f(y_{j})\leq\hat{f}(y_{j})
. Thus,
f(y_{j})\leq f(y_{0})
holds. For the case
v=w_{t}
: if
w_{t}=y_{0}
, then we are done. If
w_{t}=w^{\min}_{t}
, then the inequality follows from the
\zeta_{t}
backtracking.

A.3.1
Proxy Conditions for
M_{k}

Fix an outer iteration
k
and a current estimate
M_{k}>0
. In the inner
while
loop we set

\alpha(M_{k})\coloneqq 2M_{k}^{1/3}\epsilon^{2/3},\qquad\tau(M_{k})\coloneqq\sqrt{\frac{\alpha(M_{k})}{32M_{k}}},\qquad\eta(M_{k})\coloneqq\sqrt{\frac{2\alpha(M_{k})}{M_{k}}}.

We run
Modified-AGD
on
\hat{f}
to obtain
(x_{0}^{t},y_{0}^{t},u,v)
. When
u,v\neq\textsc{Null}
,

b^{(1)}\leftarrow{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.5898}\textsc{Find-Best-Iterate}_{3}}(f,y_{0}^{t},u,v),\qquad b^{(2)}\leftarrow{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.5898}\textsc{Exploit-NC-Pair}_{3}}(f,u,v,\eta(M_{k})).

The algorithm accepts the current
M_{k}
in the non-convex branch only if either

f(b^{(1)})\ \leq\ f(y_{0})\ -\ \alpha(M_{k})\tau(M_{k})^{2}

(47)

or (in the
f(b^{(1)})>f(y_{0})-\alpha\tau(M_{k})^{2}
case) if both the following proxies for [
7
, Lemmas 5 and 6] hold:

\displaystyle f(v)\

\displaystyle\leq\ f(y_{0})\ +\ 14\,\alpha(M_{k})\tau(M_{k})^{2},

(48)

\displaystyle f(b^{(2)})\

\displaystyle\leq\ \max\Big\{f(v)-\frac{\alpha(M_{k})}{4}\eta(M_{k})^{2},\ \ f(u)-\frac{\alpha(M_{k})}{12}\eta(M_{k})^{2}\Big\}.

(49)

The key observation is that the conclusions of Carmon et al.
[
7
, Lemmas 5 and 6]
can be verified directly on the computed iterates, without knowing
L_{3}
explicitly. Whenever (
48
) or (
49
) fail, we can conclude that
M_{k}<L_{3}
and the algorithm updates
M_{k}\leftarrow\gamma M_{k}
for some fixed
\gamma>1
; once
M_{k}\geq L_{3}
, the conditions are guaranteed to hold and the estimate is never increased again. This gives a backtracking scheme where the total number of
L_{3}
increases is bounded by
\mathcal{O}(\log_{\min(\gamma,1/\rho)}(L_{3}/M_{0}))
.

A.3.2
Progress Bound with Adaptive
M_{k}

The following result gives us the equivalent of Carmon et al.
[
7
, Lemma 7]
with
M_{k}
replacing
L_{3}
.

Lemma 8
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be
L_{1}
-smooth and have
L_{3}
-Lipschitz continuous third-order derivatives, let
\epsilon,\alpha(M_{k})>0
and
p_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. If
p_{0}^{K}
is the sequence of iterates produced by
PF-AGD
(f,p_{0},L_{1}^{(0)},M_{0},\gamma,\epsilon)
, then for every
1\leq k<K
,

f(p_{k})\leq f(p_{k-1})-\min\left\{\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{5\alpha(M_{k})},\frac{\alpha(M_{k})^{2}}{32M_{k}}\right\}.

Proof.

We shall review the proof of Carmon et al.
[
7
, Lemma 7]
one argument at a time. The first case is when
u,v=\textsc{Null}
. Although our regularized objective
\hat{f}
potentially changes from iteration to iteration due to
\alpha(M_{k})
being increased, the original proof of this case from Carmon et al.
[
7
, Lemma 2]
only relied on the property that
\hat{f}(p_{k})=\hat{f}(y_{t})\leq\hat{f}(y_{0})=f(p_{k-1})
, which is still valid despite the fact that the regularization coefficient has changed.

Also, the next case where
f(b^{(1)})\leq f(y_{0})-\alpha(M_{k})\tau(M_{k})^{2}
, we are also done with
L_{3}
replaced by
M_{k}
. In our algorithm we simply return and break the line search in this case.

Now given
f(b^{(1)})>f(y_{0})-\alpha(M_{k})\tau(M_{k})^{2}
, by Carmon et al.
[
7
, Lemma 3]
(unchanged, works for arbitrary
\tau(M)
) we have that

\|u-v\|\leq 4\tau(M_{k})\leq\sqrt{\frac{\alpha(M_{k})}{2M_{k}}}=\frac{\eta(M_{k})}{2}

Therefore, we can apply inequality (
49
) (with
\eta(M_{k})
as defined above) to show that

f(b^{(2)})\ \leq\ \max\Big\{f(v)-\frac{\alpha(M_{k})^{2}}{2M_{k}},\ \ f(u)-\frac{\alpha(M_{k})^{2}}{6M_{k}}\Big\}.

(50)

Carmon et al.
[
7
, Corollary 1 (9)]
holds as usual, so
f(u)\leq\hat{f}(u)\leq\hat{f}(y_{0})=f(p_{k-1})
(see Corollary
1
for more details). Moreover, since
f(b^{(1)})\geq f(y_{0})-\alpha(M_{k})\tau(M_{k})^{2}
and
\tau(M_{k})=\sqrt{\frac{\alpha(M_{k})}{32M_{k}}}
, we may apply (
48
) to obtain

f(v)\leq f(y_{0})+14\alpha(M_{k})\tau(M_{k})^{2}\leq f(p_{k-1})+\frac{7\alpha(M_{k})^{2}}{16M_{k}}

Combining this with (
50
), we find that

f(p_{k})\leq f(b^{(2)})\leq f(p_{k-1})-\min\left\{\frac{\alpha(M_{k})^{2}}{2M_{k}}-\frac{7\alpha(M_{k})^{2}}{16M_{k}},\frac{\alpha(M_{k})^{2}}{6M_{k}}\right\}=f(p_{k-1})-\frac{\alpha(M_{k})^{2}}{16M_{k}},

which concludes the case
v,u\neq\textsc{Null}
under third-order smoothness.
∎

A.3.3
Bounding the Outer Iterations

We wish to obtain a bound on the total number of outer iterations in terms of our original problem parameter
L_{3}
and
M_{0}
. We begin by showing that our estimate always remains bounded.

Lemma 9

(Boundedness of
M_{k}
)
.

Let
\{M_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
be the sequence of estimates produced by

PF-AGD
(f,p_{0},L_{1}^{(0)},M_{0},\gamma,\epsilon)
, with initial estimate
M_{0}>0

and multiplicative update factor
\gamma>1
. Then for all
k\geq 0
,

M_{k}\leq\bar{M}\coloneqq\max\{M_{0},\,\gamma L_{3}\}.

In particular, the estimate
M_{k}
is increased at most

\mathcal{O}(\log_{\gamma}(L_{3}/M_{0}))
times in total.

Proof.

If
M_{0}\geq L_{3}
, we know the check in line 11 of
PF-AGD
will always be
False
so the estimate will never be incremented. Otherwise,
M_{0}<L_{3}
. Suppose after some point
M_{k}\geq L_{3}
, by the same reasoning as above, we know that
M_{k}
will not be incremented further. Hence,
M_{k}<\gamma L_{3}
, and at most
\mathcal{O}(\log_{\gamma}(L_{3}/M_{0}))
increments occur. Combining both cases we have
M_{k}\leq\max\{M_{0},\gamma L_{3}\}
.
∎

With the boundedness of
M_{k}
in mind, we split our analysis into cases. If
M_{0}\leq\gamma L_{3}
, we have

\displaystyle f(p_{k})\leq f(p_{k-1})-\min\left\{\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{5\alpha(M_{k})},\frac{\alpha(M_{k})^{2}}{32M_{k}}\right\}

\displaystyle=f(p_{k-1})-\min\left\{\frac{\epsilon^{4/3}}{10M_{k}^{1/3}},\frac{\epsilon^{4/3}}{8M_{k}^{1/3}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq f(p_{k-1})-\min\left\{\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{5\alpha(\gamma L_{3})},\frac{\alpha(\gamma L_{3})^{2}}{32\gamma L_{3}}\right\}

\displaystyle=f(p_{k-1})-\min\left\{\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{5\gamma^{1/3}\alpha(L_{3})},\frac{\alpha(L_{3})^{2}}{32\gamma^{1/3}L_{3}}\right\}

(51)

Otherwise,
M_{k}=M_{0}
and we have

f(p_{k})\leq f(p_{k-1})-\min\left\{\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{5\alpha(M_{0})},\frac{\alpha(M_{0})^{2}}{32M_{0}}\right\}=f(p_{k-1})-\frac{\epsilon^{4/3}}{10M_{0}^{1/3}}

(52)

With these new progress bounds we can derive the upper bound
K
of the number of iterations of
PF-AGD
by telescoping (
51
)

\displaystyle\Delta_{f}\geq f(p_{0})-f(p_{K-1})=\sum_{k=1}^{K-1}\left(f(p_{k-1})-f(p_{k})\right)

\displaystyle\geq(K-1)\gamma^{-1/3}\cdot\min\left\{\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{5\alpha(L_{3})},\frac{\alpha(L_{3})^{2}}{32L_{3}}\right\}

\displaystyle\geq(K-1)\frac{\epsilon^{4/3}}{10\gamma^{1/3}L_{3}^{1/3}}.

In the case of (
52
)

\displaystyle\Delta_{f}\geq f(p_{0})-f(p_{K-1})=\sum_{k=1}^{K-1}\left(f(p_{k-1})-f(p_{k})\right)\geq(K-1)\frac{\epsilon^{4/3}}{10M_{0}^{1/3}}.

We therefore conclude that

K\leq 1+10\epsilon^{-4/3}\Delta_{f}\bar{M}^{1/3},

(53)

where
\bar{M}:=\max\{\gamma L_{3},M_{0}\}
.

A.4
Global Convergence Rate for PF-AGD

With the Lyapunov analysis of Section
A.2
and the adaptive

estimation of Section

in hand, we are almost ready to assemble the main complexity bound. The argument proceeds in three stages. We first justify in Section

that the partial-momentum restarts introduced in Algorithm

do not break acceleration. Section

then translates these progress guarantees into a bound on the inner iteration count

, verifies that a non-convexity certificate

implies violation of a pathwise strong convexity condition, and confirms the iterates satisfy the boundedness property of Carmon et al.
[
7
, Corollary 1 (9)]
throughout. Finally, Section
A.4.3
combines the outer iteration bound

from Section

with the inner bound

to obtain the main result:

finds an
\epsilon
-stationary point in

A.4.1

Lemma 10
.

Let

At iteration

\displaystyle V^{(w)}_{t+1}(y_{t},x_{t})\;\leq

\displaystyle\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{Q_{t}}}\Bigr)^{t}\frac{Q_{t}^{3/2}}{Q_{0}^{3/2}}\,V^{(w)}_{0}(y_{0},x_{0})\;+\;\frac{2\|\nabla f(w)\|^{2}}{\sigma}\;\sum_{i=0}^{t}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{Q_{t}}}\Bigr)^{t-i}\frac{Q_{t}^{3/2}}{Q_{i-1}^{3/2}}

\displaystyle\;\leq

\displaystyle\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{Q_{t}}}\Bigr)^{t}\frac{Q_{t}^{3/2}}{Q_{0}^{3/2}}\,V^{(w)}_{0}(y_{0},x_{0})\;+\;\frac{2\|\nabla f(w)\|^{2}}{\sigma}\;\frac{Q_{t}^{3/2}}{Q_{0}^{3/2}}\sum_{i=0}^{t}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{Q_{t}}}\Bigr)^{t-i}

\displaystyle\;\leq

\displaystyle\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{Q_{t}}}\Bigr)^{t}\frac{Q_{t}^{3/2}}{Q_{0}^{3/2}}\,V^{(w)}_{0}(y_{0},x_{0})\;+\;\frac{2\|\nabla f(w)\|^{2}}{\sigma}\;\frac{Q_{t}^{3/2}}{Q_{0}^{3/2}}\sqrt{Q_{t}}\Bigl(1-\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{Q_{t}}}\Bigr)^{t+1}\Bigr)

Proposition 6

Discussion of the Complexity Bound.

Theorem
2
provides an explicit upper bound on the total number of gradient evaluations, which depends on the initialization of the
L_{1}
-Lipschitz constant estimate of the regularized objective
L^{(0)}
. In particular, the method requires at most

27\frac{\Delta_{f}L_{1}^{1/2}\bar{M}^{1/3}}{M_{0}^{1/6}\epsilon^{5/3}}\log\left(\frac{1473L^{5/2+m}_{1}(9/2)^{m}\,\Delta_{f}}{M_{0}^{1/2+m/3}\epsilon^{3+2m/3}}\right)

when
L^{(0)}\leq 2(L_{1}+2\alpha(\bar{M}))
, and

16\frac{\Delta_{f}L^{(0)^{1/2}}\bar{M}^{1/3}}{M_{0}^{1/6}\epsilon^{5/3}}\log\left(\frac{193L^{(0)^{5/2}}(3Q_{0})^{m}\,\Delta_{f}}{M_{0}^{1/2}\epsilon^{3}}\right)

when
L^{(0)}\geq 2(L_{1}+2\alpha(\bar{M}))
. Ignoring quantities independent of
\epsilon
(
\Delta_{f},L_{1},L^{(0)},M_{0},\bar{M},m
), the method achieves a convergence rate of
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-5/3})
in both cases. The two-case structure arises because when
L^{(0)}
is sufficiently large, it already serves as an adequate upper bound on the Lipschitz constant of the true objective, and the bound depends directly on
L^{(0)}
; for smaller initializations, it reduces to a bound expressed in terms of the true smoothness parameter
L_{1}
.

For comparison, Carmon et al.
[
7
, Theorem 2]
establish the bound

20\cdot\frac{\Delta_{f}L_{1}^{1/2}L_{3}^{1/6}}{\epsilon^{5/3}}\log\left(\frac{500L_{1}\Delta_{f}}{\epsilon^{2}}\right).

Remark (constants).

The larger constants in Theorem
2
relative to Carmon et al.
[
7
, Theorem 2]
arise from three sources: (i) the factor
L_{3}^{1/6}
is replaced by the adaptive quantity
\bar{M}^{1/3}/M_{0}^{1/6}
; (ii) the additional
(9/2)^{m}
and
(3Q_{t})^{m}
terms in the inner bound, along with dependence on
m
in the exponents, account for condition number increments during backtracking; and (iii) the adaptive condition number introduces an additional
\bar{Q}^{3/2}
factor inside the logarithm of the inner iteration bound, inflating the logarithmic constant from 500 to 1473. While the parameter-free approach may incur larger constants and logarithmic overhead, it retains the same
\epsilon^{-5/3}
scaling without requiring prior knowledge of
L_{1}
or
L_{3}
.

Appendix B
Implementation Details and Further Experiments

B.1
Implementation Details

All algorithms are implemented in Python with
\epsilon=10^{-4}
. Oracle calls count only evaluations of
\nabla f
. Unless otherwise stated, single-iterate methods are initialized at
x_{0}=\mathbf{0}
and AGD-style methods at
p_{0}=\mathbf{0}
(outer loop iterates), with
N=1{,}000
independent trials per experiment. For reproducibility, each single seed experiment uses a random seed of
0
. The index
k
denotes iterates for single-iterate methods; for AGD-style methods,
k
and
t
denote outer and inner iterates, respectively.

Simple gradient descent (GD).

Simple GD uses iterates of the form

x_{k}=x_{k-1}-\frac{1}{L_{1}}\nabla f(x_{k-1}),

(75)

where
L_{1}
is the true Lipschitz constant of the gradient.

Armijo steepest descent (SD).

In practice,
L_{1}
is often unknown and non-uniform, and therefore needs to be estimated adaptively. A standard approach is Armijo backtracking line search, initializing
L^{(0)}_{1}=1
. Given a current estimate
L^{(k)}_{1}
, we try the Simple GD step (
76
) with
L_{1}:=L^{(k)}_{1}
and accept it if the Armijo sufficient decrease condition holds:

f(x_{k})\ \leq\ f(x_{k-1})-\,\frac{c}{L^{(k)}_{1}}\|\nabla f(x_{k-1})\|^{2},

(76)

where
c\in(0,1]
; in our experiments
c=10^{-4}
. If (
76
) fails, we reject the step and increase the curvature estimate by doubling
L^{(k)}_{1}\leftarrow 2L^{(k)}_{1}
, and retry. When a step is accepted, we keep the resulting
L^{(k)}_{1}
as the starting estimate for the next iteration.

Semi-adaptive
AGD-Until-Guilty
.

We remark here that within the experiments section of
7
, they do not use the algorithm for which they proved theoretical convergence but instead switch to a practical variant.
As
L_{1}
and
L_{3}
are unknown,
7
estimate
L_{1}
via line search as above with
c=1
, and double the estimate
L_{1}^{(t)}
within an inner iteration whenever (
76
) fails. Following
7
, we want the algorithm implementation to be independent of the final desired accuracy
\epsilon
and to avoid using
L_{3}
. Hence, we set
\epsilon^{\prime}=\|\nabla f(p_{k-1})\|/10
and use
\alpha=\sigma=C_{1}\|\nabla f(p_{k-1})\|^{2/3}
, where
C_{1}=0.01
is a hyperparameter. To avoid computing
\|\nabla f(y_{t})\|
at every iteration, we move the
\|\nabla f(y_{t})\|<\epsilon
check into
Certify-Progress
and perform it only once every 5 iterations. In
Certify-Progress
, they also check
\hat{f}(x_{t})+\nabla\hat{f}(x_{t})^{T}(y_{t}-x_{t})>\hat{f}(y_{t})
and find that this substantially increases the method’s capability of detecting negative curvature; most of the non-convexity detection is due to this check. In addition to what Carmon et al. have implemented, for AGD-style methods, we initialize
L^{(0)}_{1}
using a local finite-difference estimate of gradient variation at
x_{0}
: for random unit vectors
u
, we compute
\frac{\|\nabla f(x_{0}+hu)-\nabla f(x_{0})\|}{h}
with
h\ll 1
and take the maximum.

PF-AGD.

As in
AGD-Until-Guilty
, we wish to remove the dependence on
\epsilon
. Hence, we set
\epsilon^{\prime}=\|\nabla f(p_{k-1})\|/2
with
\sigma=\alpha(M_{k-1})=C_{1}M_{k-1}^{1/3}\|\nabla f(p_{k-1})\|^{2/3}
, where
C_{1}=0.01
is a hyperparameter. Instead of checking
Certify-Progress
every iteration, we invoke it every 5 iterations. For backtracking, we take
c=0.5,\rho=0.8
with
\gamma=2
and
M_{0}=10^{-5}
.

Nonlinear Conjugate Gradient (CG).

The method is given by the following recursion
[
34
]
:

\delta_{k}=-\nabla f(x_{k})+\max\left\{\frac{\nabla f(x_{k})^{\top}\bigl(\nabla f(x_{k})-\nabla f(x_{k-1})\bigr)}{\left\lVert\nabla f(x_{k-1})\right\rVert^{2}},\;0\right\}\delta_{k-1},\qquad x_{k+1}=x_{k}+\eta_{k}\delta_{k},

where
\delta_{0}=0
and
\eta_{k}
is found via backtracking line search, as follows. If
\delta_{k}^{\top}\nabla f(x_{k})\geq 0
we set
\delta_{k}=-\nabla f(x_{k})
(truncating the recursion). We set
\eta_{k}=2\eta_{k-1}
and then check whether

f(x_{k}+\eta_{k}\delta_{k})\leq f(x_{k})+\frac{\eta_{k}\,\delta_{k}^{\top}\nabla f(x_{k})}{2}

holds. If it does, we keep the value of
\eta_{k}
; otherwise, we set
\eta_{k}=\eta_{k}/2
and repeat. The key difference from the semi-adaptive scheme used for the rest of the methods is the initialization
\eta_{k}=2\eta_{k-1}
, which allows the step size to grow. Performing line search is crucial for conjugate gradient to succeed, as otherwise it cannot produce approximately conjugate directions. If we use the semi-adaptive step size above, performance becomes very similar to that of gradient descent.

B.2.1
Regularized Quadratic Functions

Instead of
H
positive definite, we induce negative curvature via the regularized quadratic form:

f(x)=\frac{1}{2}x^{\top}Hx+b^{\top}x+\mu\|x\|_{2}^{4},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
b=[1,1,\dots,1]^{\top}
and
\mu>0
. We sample an indefinite Hessian
H\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
as
H=\bm{Q}D\bm{Q}^{\top}
, with
\bm{Q}
Haar-distributed orthogonal as above and
D
diagonal, containing both positive, negative, and zero eigenvalues. Given the negative eigenvalues of
H
, the quartic regularization term
\mu\|x\|_{2}^{4}
ensures that the objective is bounded below. We choose
\mu=1
for the objective to be sufficiently regularized so the algorithms can make good progress. The triple
(p,n,0)
indicates the minimum number of positive, negative, and zero eigenvalues respectively, and
\mathcal{U}[\lambda_{min},\lambda_{max}]
indicates the distribution of the remaining eigenvalues.

Results.

As illustrated in Figure
5
, nonlinear CG remains the most efficient approach, followed by
PF-AGD
. Despite the presence of negative curvature, which should theoretically favor its design,
AGD-Until-Guilty
proves to be the least effective baseline, plateauing below 75% convergence across all four configurations. Notably, while Armijo SD matches the performance of
PF-AGD
and
28
in the
U[-10,10]
setting, this gap widens significantly as the spectral range expands to
U[-100,100]
. The inclusion of a zero eigenvalue, denoted by the
(p,n,0)
configurations, appears to have a negligible impact on relative performance, suggesting that the zero eigenvalue (degenerate) direction is quickly identified and avoided by all methods.

B.2.2
Qing Function

For dimension
d\geq 1
, the Qing function
[
36
]
is

f(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}(x_{i}^{2}-i)^{2},\quad d\in\{4,20,60,100\}.

The global minimum
f(x^{*})=0
is attained at
x_{i}^{*}\in\{\pm\sqrt{i}\}
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,d\}
, resulting in
2^{d}
global minimizers. We set
x^{*}=[\sqrt{1},\dots,\sqrt{d}]^{\top}
and initialize
x_{0},p_{0}\sim\mathcal{N}(x^{*},10^{-1}I_{d})
.

Results.

As shown in Figure
6
, all evaluated methods converged within 800 oracle calls. Nonlinear CG and
PF-AGD
proved most efficient, necessitating approximately 100 and 180 calls, respectively; whereas
28
required 400 calls, and
AGD-Until-Guilty
and Armijo SD exhibited the slowest convergence with near-identical trajectories. Single-seed analysis, illustrated in Figure
7
, corroborates these findings. At
d=100
, the performance of
AGD-Until-Guilty
and Armijo SD remains closely aligned, which is consistent with the aggregate CDF results. However, at
d=4
, Armijo SD marginally outperforms the accelerated variants. This suggests that the overhead associated with acceleration may be counterproductive in low-dimensional regimes. As dimensionality increases,
PF-AGD
recovers its advantage, trailing nonlinear CG by a margin of at most 30 oracle calls. Upon termination, all methods reached similar objective values, successfully converging to the global minimizer at zero.

B.2.3
Rosenbrock Test Function

The Rosenbrock function
[
38
]
is a non-convex function whose global minimum lies inside a narrow, parabolic-shaped (flat) valley. Finding the valley is straightforward but converging to the global minimum is difficult. Notably, the Rosenbrock function is not (globally)
L_{1}
-smooth. This makes the function a good performance test for the robustness of our
L_{1}
estimates:

f(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}\Bigl[100\bigl(x_{i+1}-x_{i}^{2}\bigr)^{2}+\bigl(1-x_{i}\bigr)^{2}\Bigr],\qquad x=[x_{1},\dots,x_{d}]^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

The global minimizer is
x^{*}=[1,1,\dots,1]^{\top}
, where
f(x^{*})=0
. For
d=2
, we use the standard (challenging) initialization
x_{0},p_{0}=[-1.2,1]^{\top}
; for
d\in\{10,20,50\}
, we set
x_{0},p_{0}=[-1.2,1,\ldots,-1.2,1]^{\top}
. We report both
f(x_{t})
and
\|\nabla f(x_{t})\|
as a function of the number of oracle calls, stopping once
\|\nabla f(x_{t})\|\leq\epsilon
.

Results.

Figure
8
illustrates that nonlinear CG remains the most efficient method across all evaluated dimensions; however, the performance gap between it and the AGD-style methods narrows as the dimensionality increases. Overall,
PF-AGD
performs slightly worse than
AGD-Until-Guilty
, although it is notable that negative curvature is only detected and exploited in the
d=2
case. In contrast, steepest descent proves to be the least efficient approach followed by
28
, both exhibiting prolonged stagnation phases despite an Armijo SD having an initial rate of progress that matches nonlinear CG. Interestingly, the relative performance of the algorithms appears robust to dimensionality, with negligible impact on their respective convergence rates.

Performance on the Rosenbrock function.

B.2.4
Ackley Test Function

We evaluate the algorithms on the Ackley function
[
1
]
:

f(x)=-20\exp\!\left(-0.2\sqrt{\frac{1}{d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}^{2}}\right)-\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\cos(2\pi x_{i})\right)+e+20,\quad x=[x_{1},\dots,x_{d}]^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

The origin is the global minimizer, with
f(x^{*})=0
, but the landscape around it features a near-flat outer region with high-frequency oscillations that induce many local optima where optimization methods may become trapped. As such, we initialize all methods near the global minimizer with a small perturbation to break the symmetry, setting
x_{0},p_{0}=[-1,-1,0,\dots,0]^{\top}+\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},10^{-4}I)
.

Results.

As shown in Figure
9
, AGD-based methods achieve rapid initial reductions
in gradient norm for
d\in\{2,10,20\}
; however, the high terminal function values
suggest convergence to local minima rather than the global optimum. Among these,
PF-AGD
is the most oracle-efficient. Armijo SD fails to attain
\epsilon=10^{-4}
accuracy for
d\in\{10,20\}
, likely due to vanishing gradients in the flat regions characteristic of the Ackley landscape. At
d=50
, the additional degrees of freedom enable all methods to locate the global minimum. All algorithms encounter an initial plateau, though the AGD variants exhibit the most prolonged stagnation. Notably,
PF-AGD
and
28
escapes this plateau after approximately
10^{2}
oracle calls, substantially earlier than
AGD-Until-Guilty
, which requires around
3\times 10^{4}
calls, indicating considerably greater robustness. The eventual descent of
AGD-Until-Guilty
coincides with several instances of negative curvature detection, as indicated by the markers.

SCosine
Function

For dimension
d\geq 1
, the
SCosine

f(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}\cos^{2}\left(x_{i}^{2}-\frac{x_{i+1}}{2}\right),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

There are infinitely many global minimizers, with
f(x^{*})=0
. One particular minimizer can be generated via the recursion,
x_{1}=0,x_{i+1}=2x^{2}_{i}-\pi
, where each
x_{i}
is a polynomial in
\pi
. We initialize the iterates at
x_{0},p_{0}=[1,\dots,1]^{\top}
.

Results.

Figure

2\times 10^{3}
oracle calls merely to approach
\epsilon
. Conversely, all other evaluated methods achieve function values below
10^{-8}
within 40 oracle calls, with the gradient norm reaching the desired accuracy.
