Introduction

1.1.
Background and motivation

We consider the Cauchy problem of the nonlinear Schrödinger equation(NLS)

(1.1)

\begin{cases}(i\partial_{t}+\Delta_{g})u=F(u),&(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times M,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)\in H^{s}(M),\end{cases}

where
u:I\times M\to\mathbb{C}
is the unknown function. The function
F
satisfies
F(0)=0
. Then there exists a gauge-invariant function
V\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{R})
such that
F=\frac{\partial V}{\partial\bar{z}}
. Moreover,
V(z)
obeys

(1.2)

\displaystyle V(e^{i\theta}z)=V(z),\,\,\theta\in\mathbb{R},\,\,z\in\mathbb{C},|\partial_{z}^{k_{1}}\partial_{\bar{z}}^{k_{2}}V(z)|\leq C_{k_{1},k_{2}}(1+|z|)^{1+\alpha-k_{1}-k_{2}}.

The number
\alpha
usually measures the growth of nonlinear term and refers to the degree of
F
.

The nonlinear Schrödinger equation (
1.1
) has been extensively investigated over the decades on various manifolds, including Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
, and the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d}
(see, e.g.,
[
2
,
6
,
7
,
10
,
20
,
22
,
24
,
26
,
29
]
and the references therein). Here, we focus on the case where
M=\mathbb{S}^{2}
, the two-dimensional sphere endowed with the canonical metric
g
.
In the local coordinate system of
M
, the Laplace-Beltrami operator takes the form

\displaystyle\Delta_{g}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{|g|}}\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{2}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\Big(g^{ij}\sqrt{|g|}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\Big).

The Cauchy problem (
1.1
) with
M=\mathbb{S}^{2}
has a Hamiltonian structure and its solution satisfies the following two conservation laws:

(1.3)

\displaystyle M(u)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\left|u\right|^{2}\,dx\equiv M(u)(0),

(1.4)

\displaystyle E(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\left|\nabla_{g}u\right|^{2}\,dx+{\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}V(u)\,dx}\equiv E(u)(0).

When
M=\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the equation (
1.1
) with
F(u)=|u|^{\alpha-1}u
is invariant under the following scaling transformation

\displaystyle u_{\lambda}(t,x)=\lambda^{\frac{2}{\alpha-1}}u(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x).

A direct computation shows that
\|u_{\lambda}(0)\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}=\|u(0)\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}
for
s_{c}:=1-\frac{2}{\alpha-1}
. The problem is called
\dot{H}^{s_{c}}
-critical if
s=s_{c}
;
\dot{H}^{s_{c}}
-subcritical if
s>s_{c}
;
\dot{H}^{s_{c}}
-supercritical if
s<s_{c}
. For other nonlinearities with the same degree as
|u|^{\alpha-1}u
, we also refer to
s_{c}
as the critical regularity.

Next, we recall the definitions of local uniform well-posedness and ill-posedness for (
1.1
) on
M
.

Definition 1.1

(Local uniform well-posedness,
[
12
]
)
.

The Cauchy problem (
1.1
) is well-posed in
H^{s}(M)
if for any bounded subset
B
in
H^{s}(M)
, there exists
T>0
such that the solution map

\displaystyle u_{0}\in C^{\infty}(M)\cap B\mapsto u\in C(\left[-T,T\right],H^{s}(M))

is uniformly continuous when the source space is endowed with the
H^{s}
norm and when the target space is endowed with

\|u\|_{C(\left[-T,T\right],H^{s}(M))}=\sup_{\left|t\right|\leqslant T}\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}(M)}.

In contrast to the local well-posedness, we define two kinds of ill-posedness.

Definition 1.2

(Not uniformly continuous)
.

The solution map of (
1.1
) is not uniformly continuous in
H^{s}(M)
if for any
0<\delta<1
and
t>0
, there exist solutions
u,v
to (
1.1
) with initial data
u_{0},v_{0}
such that the following statements hold:

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}(M)}+\|v_{0}\|_{H^{s}(M)}

\displaystyle\lesssim 1,

\displaystyle\|u_{0}-v_{0}\|_{H^{s}(M)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta,

\displaystyle\sup_{0<\tau<t}\|u(\tau)-v(\tau)\|_{H^{s}(M)}

\displaystyle\gtrsim 1.

Next we define a stronger instability phenomenon describing the rapid growth of norms.

Definition 1.3

(Norm inflation)
.

Let
s>0
, for any
\epsilon,t>0
, there exists solution
u
to (
1.1
) with initial data
u_{0}\in H^{s}(M)
such that the following hold,

\begin{gathered}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}(M)}\lesssim\epsilon,\\
\sup_{0<\tau<t}\|u(\tau)\|_{H^{s}(M)}\gtrsim\epsilon^{-1}.\end{gathered}

Since
u\equiv 0
solves (
1.1
) with
u_{0}=0
, norm inflation implies that the solution map is not uniformly continuous directly. Therefore, it can be regarded as a stronger notion of ill-posedness.

Let us briefly review the well-posedness theory for (
1.1
) on different manifolds
M
, where the underlying geometry plays a crucial role. In the study of local well-posedness, Strichartz estimates

(1.5)

\displaystyle\big\|e^{it\Delta_{g}}u_{0}\big\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{r}(I\times M)}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}(M)}

are crucial in establishing the local well-posedness, where

and

. When

, the Strichartz estimates hold globally in time for

and

satisfying the following admissible condition

(1.6)

\displaystyle\frac{2}{q}=2\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r}\right),\,\,2<q<\infty,\,\,2\leq r<\infty.

These estimates rely heavily on the dispersive estimate

\displaystyle\big\|e^{it\Delta}u_{0}\big\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim|t|^{-1}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

Combining Strichartz estimates with the fractional chain rule, Cazenave-Weissler
[
10
]
established the

local well-posedness for

and

. This result is sharp: for

, Christ-Colliander-Tao
[
12
]
showed that the equation (
1.1
) is ill-posed in

with

and
\alpha\leq 1+k

is an integer. For the global well-posedness and scattering for cubic NLS on

On compact manifolds, however, the dispersive estimate fails in general due to the presence of trapped geodesics, which inhibit dispersion over long times. For the torus

, Bourgain
[
2

utilized the circle method with the Stein-Tomas argument to derive the

-Strichartz estimate for

(1.7)

\displaystyle\big\|e^{it\Delta_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}}P_{N}f\big\|_{L_{t,x}^{p}(I\times\mathbb{T}^{2})}\lesssim N^{1-\frac{4}{p}+\varepsilon}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})},

where

1.1
) with initial data

. In the cubic case (

. On the other hand, Kishimoto
[

showed the solution map is not

in
L^{2}
. On irrational tori
\mathbb{T}_{\theta}^{2}:=\prod_{j=1}^{2}\mathbb{R}/(\alpha_{j}\mathbb{Z})
, Guo-Oh-Wang
[

obtained similar results to those in
[

]
. For the hyperbolic Schrödinger case, i.e.

, Wang
[

proved the sharp local well-posedness of cubic hyperbolic NLS for
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
with

. Very recently, Shen-Wang
[

proved the norm inflation phenomenon for

with

. In the semi-periodic setting

established the

Strichartz estimates without loss of derivatives and then showed the local well-posedness for initial data

For general compact manifolds without boundary, Burq-Gérard-Tzvetkov
[
6

constructed a parametrix for

and derived a semiclassical dispersive estimate. Applying the abstract Strichartz estimate of Keel-Tao, the following holds for

\displaystyle\big\|e^{it\Delta_{g}}f\big\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{r}(I\times M)}\leq C(I,M)\|f\|_{H^{\frac{1}{q}}(M)},

which incurs a derivative loss of order

. Compared to the Euclidean space, the Strichartz estimate must be local in time. As an application, they showed that the solution to (
1.1
) is locally well-posed in

with

. On the sphere

, however, the loss in (

, and showed that the

-order loss of derivative is optimal in

]

, they further established bilinear eigenfunction estimates capturing high-low frequency interactions, leading to local well-posedness for the cubic NLS on

for

. This

, the flow map fails to be uniformly continuous. For sub-cubic nonlinearity, that is

Remark 1.8
.

Compared to the Euclidean space, an interesting phenomenon is that the ill-posedness results exhibit a turning point at
\alpha=11/3
. In fact, this can be explained by combining the recent result obtained by Huang–Sogge
[
21
]
. In their paper, they proved a sharp
L^{\alpha+1}_{t,x}
-type Strichartz estimates on Zoll manifolds with
\alpha\geq 1
:

(1.10)

\|e^{it\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}u_{0}\|_{L^{\alpha+1}_{t,x}([0,1]\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s_{0}(\alpha)+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{S}^{2})},

with

s_{0}(\alpha)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\alpha+1}),\,\,&\alpha\in(1,\frac{11}{3}),\\
1-\frac{4}{\alpha+1},\,\,&\alpha\in[\frac{11}{3},\infty].\end{cases}

Moreover,
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\beta\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
is a Littlewood-Paley cut off functions and denote
e_{j}(x)
be the eigenfunction associated with the
j
-th eigenspace. They proved that the maximizer of (
1.10
) is

u_{n}=\begin{cases}n^{\frac{1}{4}}(x_{1}+ix_{2})^{n},\,\,&\alpha\in(1,\frac{11}{3}),\\
\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{N}}n^{-1}\beta(n_{j}/n)e_{j}(x)\overline{e_{j}(x_{0})},\,\,&\alpha\in[\frac{11}{3}{,\infty}].\end{cases}

Here,
x_{0}
denotes a fixed point in
\mathbb{S}^{2}
and
n_{j}
is the
j
-th eigenvalue associated with
\sqrt{-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}
.

In fact, the instability phenomenon is governed by the potential energy
\|u\|^{\alpha+1}_{L^{\alpha+1}}
. Heuristically, on a short time interval, the contribution of the Duhamel term is negligible in the sense of space-time averaging. Hence, we may approximate the solution

u
by its linear part.
Therefore, to find the initial data
u_{0}
that most likely to cause instability, we require it to satisfy:

\|e^{it\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}u_{0}\|_{L^{\alpha+1}_{t,x}([0,1]\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}\gtrsim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s_{0}(\alpha)}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}.

Therefore, we may reasonably conjecture that the turning point is induced by the properties of eigenfunctions on the spheres, rather technical reasons.

We end up this section by recalling some basic definitions of Laplace-Beltrami operator and the associated Sobolev spaces.
Denote the spherical harmonic function by
Y_{k}^{l}
, which is also known as the eigenfunction of Laplace-Beltrami operator on spheres, it satisfies the following eigen-equation associated with the eigenvalue
\lambda_{k}=k(k+1)
,

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}Y_{k}^{l}(x)=k(k+1)Y_{k}^{l}(x),\,-k\leq\ell\leq k,\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{S}^{2}.

By using the spherical harmonic decomposition, we can decompose all

with

\displaystyle f=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}{\pi_{k}f},

where
\pi_{k}
denotes the
k
-th projector on eigenspace
E_{k}=\operatorname{span}\{Y_{k}^{\ell}\}_{-k\leq\ell\leq k}
.

Let

, denote

the Sobolev space associated with the operator

equipped with the norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}=\Big(\sum_{k}\langle\lambda_{k}\rangle^{2s}\|\pi_{k}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
\lambda_{k}=\sqrt{k(k+1)}
and
\langle\cdot\rangle=(1+|\cdot|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

Proof of Theorem

Let

For convenience, we define the time parameter by

(2.1)

t_{n}:=(\log n)n^{-(\alpha-1)(1-s)},\,\,0<\delta<\frac{s}{1+s(\alpha-1)}.

Performing the change of variable
y=nx
, we get the estimate

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{x}v_{n}(t)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}

\displaystyle=\kappa_{n}n^{1-s}\big\|\nabla_{y}\big(\varphi(y)\exp\big(-itf(\kappa_{n}n^{1-s}\varphi(y))\big)\big)\big\|_{L^{2}_{y}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}

\displaystyle\geq\kappa_{n}n^{1-s}\Big((\alpha-1)t\kappa_{n}^{\alpha-1}n^{(\alpha-1)-s)}\||\varphi(y)|^{k}\nabla_{y}\varphi(y)\|_{L^{2}_{y}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}-\|\nabla_{y}\varphi(y)\|_{L^{2}_{y}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\Big)

(2.2)

\displaystyle\geq c(\alpha-1)t\kappa_{n}^{\alpha}n^{\alpha(1-s)}-C\kappa_{n}n^{1-s}.

From the choice of
t_{n},\kappa_{n}
and
\delta
, one can verify that for sufficiently large
n

{c(\alpha-1)t\kappa_{n}^{\alpha}n^{\alpha(1-s)}\gg C\kappa_{n}n^{1-s},\,\,\forall\,\,0<t<t_{n}.}

As a consequence, we have

{\sup_{0<t<t_{n}}\|v_{n}(t)\|_{H^{1}_{x}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\sim\kappa_{n}^{\alpha}(\log n)n^{1-s}.}

Similarly, we also get

{\sup_{0<t<t_{n}}\|v_{n}(t)\|_{H^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\sim\kappa_{n}^{2\alpha-1}(\log n)^{2}n^{2-s}.}

By using the interpolation inequality and (
2.1
), for all
0<s<1
there exists
c=c(\alpha,\delta)>0
such that

(2.3)

\sup_{0<t<t_{n}}\|v_{n}(t)\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\geq\sup_{0<t<t_{n}}\|v_{n}(t)\|^{2-s}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\cdot\sup_{0<t<t_{n}}\|v_{n}(t)\|^{s-1}_{H^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\sim(\log n)^{c}.

Next, we aim to show that
v_{n}(t)
serves as a good approximation to the solution
u_{n}(t)
. To this end, we introduce a modified energy

E_{n}(u):=\Big(n^{2s}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+n^{2(s-2)}\|\Delta_{g}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

A direct computation shows that
\|u\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\lesssim E_{n}(u)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and

(2.4)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}E^{2}_{n}(u)

\displaystyle=2n^{2s}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\Re(u_{t}\cdot\overline{u})\,dx+2n^{2(s-2)}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\Re(\Delta_{g}u_{t}\cdot\Delta_{g}\overline{u})\,dx

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{2s}\|u_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+n^{2(s-2)}\|\Delta_{g}u_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\|\Delta_{g}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}.

We claim that there exists
\epsilon>0
such that for all
t\in(0,t_{n})
there holds

(2.5)

\sup_{0<t<t_{n}}E_{n}(u_{n}(t)-v_{n}(t))\lesssim n^{-\epsilon}.

Combining this with (
2.2
) will complete the proof of Theorem
1.5
.

It remains to show the claim (
2.5
). Let
w_{n}:=u_{n}-v_{n}
, then it satisfies
w_{n}(0)=0
and

(2.6)

(i\partial_{t}+\Delta_{g})w_{n}=F(u_{n})-F(v_{n})-\Delta_{g}v_{n}.

By taking the derivatives on both sides of the above equation, we obtain

(2.7)

(i\partial_{t}+\Delta_{g})\Delta_{g}w_{n}=\Delta_{g}\big(F(u_{n})-F(v_{n})\big)-\Delta_{g}^{2}v_{n}.

For
m=1,2,\cdots,\lfloor\alpha-2\rfloor
, using the explicit formula of
v_{n}
, we have the pointwise bound

(2.8)

\sup_{0<t<t_{n}}\|\nabla^{m}v_{n}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\lesssim n^{1-s+m}\big(\log n\big)^{m(1-\delta\alpha)}.

The Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality yields

(2.9)

\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\lesssim\|h\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{H^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\|h\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\lesssim n^{1-s}E_{n}(h).

(
2.6
) and the fact

F(u_{n})-F(v_{n})=\mathcal{O}(1+|v_{n}|^{\alpha-1}+|u_{n}|^{\alpha-1}),

imply

\displaystyle n^{s}\|\mathcal{O}(1+|v_{n}|^{\alpha-1}+|u_{n}|^{\alpha-1})w\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{s}\big(1+\|v_{n}\|^{\alpha-1}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\|u_{n}\|^{\alpha-1}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\big)\|w_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}

(2.10)

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{(1-s)(\alpha-1)}\big(E_{n}(w_{n})+E^{\alpha}_{n}(w_{n})\big).

Next, to estimate the difference term appeared in (
2.6
), we further expand it to

\displaystyle\Delta_{g}\big(F(u_{n})-F(v_{n})\big)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{g}w_{n}\cdot\mathcal{O}\big(1+|w_{n}|^{\alpha-1}+|v_{n}|^{\alpha-1}\big)

\displaystyle+\nabla w_{n}\cdot\mathcal{O}\Big(\big(1+|w_{n}|^{\alpha-2}+|v_{n}|^{\alpha-2}\big)\big(1+|w_{n}|+|v_{n}|+|\nabla w_{n}|+|\nabla v_{n}|\big)\Big)

\displaystyle+w_{n}\cdot\mathcal{O}\Big(\big(1+|w_{n}|^{\alpha-3}+|v_{n}|^{\alpha-3}\big)\big(1+|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}+(|w_{n}|+|v_{n}|)|\nabla^{2}v_{n}|\big)\Big).

By Minkowski’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\big\|\Delta_{g}\big(F(u_{n})-F(v_{n})\big)\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\Big\|\Delta w_{n}\cdot\mathcal{O}\big(1+|w_{n}|^{\alpha-1}+|v_{n}|^{\alpha-1}\big)\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}

\displaystyle+\Big\|\nabla w_{n}\cdot\mathcal{O}\Big(\big(1+|w_{n}|^{\alpha-2}+|v_{n}|^{\alpha-2}\big)\big(1+|w_{n}|+|v_{n}|+|\nabla w_{n}|+|\nabla v_{n}|\big)\Big)\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}

\displaystyle+\Big\|w_{n}\cdot\mathcal{O}\Big(\big(1+|w_{n}|^{\alpha-3}+|v_{n}|^{\alpha-3}\big)\big(1+|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}+(|w_{n}|+|v_{n}|)|\nabla^{2}v_{n}|\big)\Big)\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.

Thanks to (
2.8
) and (
2.9
), we get a bound similar to (
2.10
):

n^{s-2}(I_{1}+I_{3})\lesssim n^{(1-s)(\alpha-1)}(\log(n))^{2(1-\delta\alpha)}\big(E_{n}(w_{n})+E^{\alpha}_{n}(w_{n})\big).

For the term in
I_{2}
involving
|\nabla w_{n}|^{2}
, it can be bounded by

\displaystyle\big(1+\|v_{n}\|^{\alpha-2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\|u_{n}\|^{\alpha-2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\big)\|\nabla w_{n}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\big(1+n^{(\alpha-2)(1-s)}+n^{(\alpha-2)(1-s)}E^{\alpha-2}_{n}(w_{n})\big)\|\nabla w_{n}\|^{2}_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\big(1+n^{(\alpha-2)(1-s)}+n^{(\alpha-2)(1-s)}E^{\alpha-2}_{n}(w_{n})\big)n^{\frac{3}{2}-2s}E^{2}_{n}(w_{n})

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle n^{\alpha(1-s)+1}(E_{n}^{2}(w_{n})+E_{n}^{\alpha}(w_{n})).

Similarly, we get

(2.11)

n^{s-2}\big\|\Delta_{g}\big(F(u_{n})-F(v_{n})\big)\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\lesssim n^{(\alpha-1)(1-s)}(\log(n))^{2(1-\delta\alpha)}(E_{n}^{2}(w_{n})+E_{n}^{\alpha}(w_{n})).

For the source terms
v_{n}
, we have

(2.12)

n^{s}\|\Delta_{g}v_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+n^{s-2}\|\Delta_{g}^{2}v_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\lesssim n^{2}(\log n)^{4(1-\delta\alpha)}.

Since
E_{n}(w_{n}(0))=0
and
E_{n}(w_{n})(t)
is continuous with respect
t
, we have
E_{n}(w_{n}(t))\leq 1
for
t
sufficiently small.

Therefore, we combine (
2.10
), (
2.11
) with (
2.12
) to obtain the energy increment estimate:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}E_{n}^{2}(w_{n})

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{(1-s)(\alpha-1)}(\log(n))^{2(1-\delta\alpha)}(E_{n}^{2}(w_{n})+E_{n}^{\alpha}(w_{n}))+n^{2}(\log n)^{4(1-\delta\alpha)}E_{n}(w_{n}(t))

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{(1-s)(\alpha-1)}(\log(n))^{2(1-\delta\alpha)}(E_{n}^{2}(w_{n})+E_{n}^{\alpha}(w_{n}))

\displaystyle+\Big(n^{\frac{(1-s)(\alpha-1)}{2}}(\log n)^{1-\delta\alpha}E_{n}(w_{n})\cdot n^{2-\frac{(1-s)(\alpha-1)}{2}}(\log(n))^{3(1-\delta\alpha)}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{(1-s)(\alpha-1)}(\log(n))^{2(1-\delta\alpha)}E_{n}^{2}(w_{n})+\frac{n^{4}(\log n)^{8(1-\delta\alpha)}}{n^{(1-s)(1-\alpha)}(\log n)^{2(1-\delta\alpha)}}.

Let

\widetilde{E}_{n}(w_{n}):=\exp{\big(-tn^{(1-s)(\alpha-1)}(\log n)^{2(1-\delta\alpha)}\big)}E^{2}_{n}(w_{n}),

then the energy estimate becomes

\frac{d}{dt}\widetilde{E}_{n}(w_{n})\lesssim n^{4-(1-s)(\alpha-1)}(\log n)^{6(1-\delta\alpha)}\exp{\big(tn^{(1-s)(\alpha-1)}(\log n)^{2(1-\delta\alpha)}\big)}.

Integrating from
0
to
t
on both sides gives

E_{n}(w_{n}(t))\lesssim n^{2-(1-s)(\alpha-1)}(\log n)^{2(1-\delta\alpha)}\exp{\big(tn^{(1-s)(\alpha-1)}(\log n)^{2(1-\delta\alpha)}\big)}.

Since
0\leq t\leq t_{n}
and
s<1-\frac{2}{\alpha-1}
, for sufficiently large
n
there exists
\epsilon>0
such that

\sup_{0<t<t_{n}}E_{n}(w_{n}(t))\lesssim n^{-\epsilon}.

Recall the lower bound we have obtained in (
2.3
), then it follows that:

\sup_{t<t<t_{n}}\|u_{n}(t)\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\gtrsim(\log n)^{c}-n^{-\epsilon}\to\infty,

as
n\to\infty
. So far, we have obtained (
3
) in Theorem
1.5
.

Thus, we conclude the proof of Theorem
1.5
.
∎

3.
Not uniformly continuous: the proof of Theorem
1.6

In this section, we show that the solution map is not uniformly continuous for
s<\frac{1}{4}
, which extends the work of
[
4
]
.

For
n\geq 0
, we denote by
\pi_{n}
the spectral projection on eigenspace
E_{n}
. For a function
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
, we say that
\operatorname{degree}(f)>m
if for any
n>m
there holds
\pi_{n}f=0
. We also denote
{\psi(x)=(x_{1}+ix_{2})^{n}}
by the highest weight spherical harmonic functions.

For any
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the rotation matrix
R_{\theta}
on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and the operator
R_{\theta}^{*}
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
by

R_{\theta}:=\left(\begin{aligned} &\cos\theta&~~-\sin\theta&\quad 0~~\\
&\sin\theta&~~\cos\theta~&\quad 0~~\\
&\quad 0&0\quad&\quad 1~~\end{aligned}\right)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\begin{aligned} R_{\theta}^{*}:L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})&~\to L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})\\
f&~\mapsto R_{\theta}^{*}(f)(x):=f(R_{\theta}(x)).\end{aligned}

Then,
R_{\theta}^{*}
is an unitary operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
from the two facts below that
\det(R_{\theta})\equiv 1
and

\displaystyle\langle R_{\theta}^{*}(f),R_{\theta}^{*}(g)\rangle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}R_{\theta}^{*}(f)(x)\overline{R_{\theta}^{*}(g)(x)}~dx=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}f(R_{\theta}(x))\overline{g(R_{\theta}(x))}~dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}f(y)\overline{g(y)}~|\det(R_{\theta})|^{-1}dy=\langle f,g\rangle.

We recall a decomposition lemma of Burq-Gérard-Tzvetkov
[
4
]
, in which the operator
R^{*}_{\theta}
is used to exclude low frequency for a given function
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
.

Lemma 3.1

(Decomposition of function,
[
4
]
)
.

Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
such that for all
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
there holds

R^{*}_{\theta}f=e^{in\theta}f.

Then, there exists
\alpha_{1}\in\mathbb{C}
and
g
with
\pi_{m}g=0
for all
m>n
such that

f=\alpha_{1}\psi+g.

For
\psi(x)=(x_{1}+ix_{2})^{n}
, one can compute that

\displaystyle R_{\theta}^{*}(\psi)(x)=

\displaystyle\psi(R_{\theta}(x))=\psi(x_{1}\cos\theta-x_{2}\sin\theta,x_{1}\sin\theta+x_{2}\cos\theta,x_{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[(x_{1}\cos\theta-x_{2}\sin\theta)+i(x_{1}\sin\theta+x_{2}\cos\theta)]^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[x_{1}(\cos\theta+i\sin\theta)+ix_{2}(\cos\theta+i\sin\theta)]^{n}=e^{in\theta}\psi(x).

Furthermore, let
f(u):=|u|^{\alpha-1}u
, one can verify that

R_{\theta}^{*}(f(\psi))(x)=|\psi(R_{\theta}(x))|^{\alpha-1}\psi(R_{\theta}(x))=|e^{in\theta}\psi(x)|^{\alpha-1}(e^{in\theta}\psi(x))=e^{in\theta}(f(\psi))(x).

By Lemma
3.1
, there exists
\alpha_{1}\in\mathbb{C}
and
g
with
\operatorname{degree}(g)>n
such that

f(\psi)=\alpha_{1}\psi+g,

with

\alpha_{1}=\frac{\|\psi\|^{\alpha+1}_{L^{\alpha+1}}}{\|\psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}}.

As a further application of Lemma
3.1
, we can decompose the solution to (
1.1
) with initial data which belongs to highest weight spherical harmonic functions.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
\alpha_{1}\in\mathbb{C}
and
\psi(x)=(x_{1}+ix_{2})^{n}
and assume
u\in C([-T,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{2}))
satisfies (
1.1
) with
u_{0}=\alpha_{1}\psi
for some
\alpha_{1}>0
. Then, for every
t\in[-T,T]

u(t,x)=\alpha_{1}(t)\psi+r(t),

with
\operatorname{deg}(r)>n
.

Using the above decomposition, we construct the ansatz to (
1.1
) as follows.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
T>0
,
\kappa\in(0,1)
,
s<\frac{1}{4}
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, denote that
\phi_{n}(x)=n^{\frac{1}{4}-s}\psi(x)
and
\omega_{n}=\|\phi_{n}\|_{L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}/\|\phi_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\sim n^{(\alpha-1)(\frac{1}{4}-s)}
. Then, suppose that

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\omega_{n}T_{n}=\infty,

the solution
u_{n}(t)
to (
1.1
) with initial data
\kappa\phi_{n}(x)
can be decomposed into

(3.1)

u(t,x)=\kappa\exp\big(-it(n(n+1)+\kappa^{\alpha-1}\omega_{n})\big)(\phi_{n}+r_{n}(t)),\quad\forall t\in[0,T_{n}].

Here, the remainder
r_{n}(t)
satisfies

(3.2)

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_{n}}\|r_{n}(t)\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\to 0,\,\,\text{as}\,\,n\to\infty.

Moreover, there exists uniform constant
C>0
such that

(3.3)

\|u_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};H^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{2}))}\leq C\kappa.

Proof.

For convenience, we abbreviate
u_{n}
,
\phi_{n}
and
\omega_{n}
to
u
,
\phi
and
\omega
respectively.

We prove (
3.3
) first. Thanks to the mass and energy conservation laws (
1.3
) and (
1.4
), we have

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2}))}=\kappa\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\lesssim\kappa n^{-s},

and

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2}))}\lesssim\kappa\big(\|\phi\|_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\|\phi\|_{L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\big)\lesssim\kappa n^{1-s}.

By interpolation, we can find

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{2}))}\lesssim\|u\|^{1-s}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2}))}\|u\|^{s}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2}))}\lesssim\kappa,

which implies (
3.3
).

It remains to prove (
3.2
). For convenience, we denote by

h(t)=\exp(it\big(n(n+1)+\kappa^{\alpha-1}\omega\big)),

with

\omega=\frac{\|\phi\|^{\alpha+1}_{L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}}{\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}}\sim n^{(\alpha-1)(\frac{1}{4}-s)}.

Thus,
h(t)
satisfies

\begin{cases}ih_{t}+n(n+1)h=\kappa^{\alpha-1}\omega|h|^{\alpha-1}h,\\
h(0)=1.\end{cases}

We write the solution to (
1.1
) of the form

(3.4)

\displaystyle u(t)=\kappa h(t)(\phi+w(t)).

From Corollary
3.2
, we find that
w(t)
satisfies
w(0)=0
and

(3.5)

iw_{t}+(\Delta_{g}+n(n+1)+\kappa^{\alpha-1}\omega)w=\kappa^{\alpha-1}\big(|\phi+w|^{\alpha-1}(\phi+w)-|\phi|^{\alpha-1}\phi+r\big),

where
\operatorname{deg}r>n
. We can verify that for every
\theta\in\mathbb{R}

R^{*}_{\theta}(|\phi+w|^{\alpha-1}(\phi+w)-|w|^{\alpha-1}w+r)=e^{in\theta}(|\phi+w|^{\alpha-1}(\phi+w)-|w|^{\alpha-1}w+r).

By Corollary
3.2
, we conclude that

w(t)=z(t)\phi+q(t),

with
z(t)\in\mathbb{C}
and
\operatorname{deg}q(t)>n
.

Next, we turn to control
\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|q(t)\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}
and
\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|z(t)|
with fixed
T>0
.

Bound for
\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|q(t)\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}
.
Since
q(t)
is orthogonal to
\phi
in
\dot{H}^{k}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, inserting (
3.4
) to (
1.3
) and (
1.4
), we deduce

(3.6)

|1+z(t)|^{2}\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\|q(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}=\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})},

and

(3.7)

\displaystyle|1+z(t)|^{2}\|\phi\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\|q(t)\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\frac{2}{(\alpha+1)\kappa^{2}}\|u(t)\|^{\alpha+1}_{L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}=\|\phi\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\frac{2\kappa^{\alpha-1}}{\alpha+1}\|\phi\|^{\alpha+1}_{L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})},

respectively. Notice that
\phi\in E_{n}
, thus for all
k\in\mathbb{N}

\|\phi\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{k}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}=n^{k}(n+1)^{k}\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}.

Using this fact to (
3.6
) and (
3.7
), we can eliminate the terms contain
\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}
, thus we obtain

\displaystyle\|q(t)\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}-n^{2}(n+1)^{2}\|q(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\kappa^{\alpha-1}}{\alpha+1}\|\phi\|^{\alpha+1}_{L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\lesssim n^{(\alpha-1)(\frac{1}{4}-s)-2s}.

From spectral resolution, we expand
q(t)
as

q(t)=\sum_{m>n}q_{m}(t),

where
\operatorname{degree}q_{m}(t)=m
. Therefore, it gives that

\displaystyle\|q(t)\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}-n^{2}(n+1)^{2}\|q(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}=\sum_{m\geq n+1}\big[m(m+1)-n(n+1)\big]\|q_{m}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}

A direct calculation implies that there exists a constant
C
independent of
n
such that

\displaystyle m(m+1)-n(n+1)

\displaystyle\geq Cn,\,\,\frac{m(m+1)-n(n+1)}{m(m+1)}\geq Cn^{-1}.

Thus, we get

(3.8)

\|q(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\lesssim n^{\frac{\alpha-5}{4}-(\alpha+1)s},\,\,\|q(t)\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\lesssim n^{\frac{\alpha+3}{4}-(\alpha+1)s}.

By interpolation, we conclude

\|q(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\lesssim\|q(t)\|^{1-s}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\|q(t)\|^{s}_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\lesssim n^{\frac{\alpha-5}{8}-(\alpha-1)\frac{s}{2}},

which is acceptable.

Bound for
\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|z(t)|
. Since
r(t)
and
q(t)
are orthogonal to
\phi
, multiplying
\phi
and taking the inner product on both sides of (
3.5
), we obtain

(3.9)

\displaystyle iz_{t}+\omega\kappa^{\alpha-1}z

\displaystyle=\kappa^{\alpha-1}\|\phi\|^{-2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\big(\langle F(\phi+w)-F(\phi),\phi\rangle\big).

Note that
\alpha\geq 3
and
|\phi+w|^{\alpha-1}
has second-order smoothness, then we expand it as follows:

\displaystyle|\phi+w|^{\alpha-1}

\displaystyle=|\phi|^{\alpha-1}+\frac{(\alpha-1)}{2}|\phi|^{\alpha-3}(\phi+\overline{\phi})w+(\alpha-1)(\alpha-2)\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)|\phi+sw|^{\alpha-3}w^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle=|\phi|^{\alpha-1}+\frac{(\alpha-1)}{2}|\phi|^{\alpha-3}(\phi+\overline{\phi})w+\mathcal{O}(|\phi|^{\alpha-3}|w|^{2}+|w|^{\alpha-1}).

Recall that
w(t)=z(t)\phi+q(t)
and
|\phi|^{\alpha-1}\phi=\omega\phi+r
, we rewrite the nonlinearity satisfies (
3.9
) by

\displaystyle\Big\langle\big(|\phi+w|^{\alpha-1}(\phi+w)-|\phi|^{\alpha-1}\phi\big),\phi\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\Big(\frac{\alpha+1}{2}\omega z+\frac{\alpha-1}{2}\omega\overline{z}\Big)

\displaystyle+O\Big((|z|^{2}+|z|^{3}+|z|^{\alpha-1})\|\phi\|^{\alpha+1}_{L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(|q|^{3}|\phi|^{\alpha-2}+|q|^{2}|\phi|^{\alpha-1}+|q|^{\alpha-1}|\phi|^{2}+|q||r|)d\sigma(x)\Big).

Since
\alpha-1\in(2,3)
,
|z|^{p-1}
can be absorbed into
|z|^{2}+|z|^{3}
. Moreover, we also get

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|q|^{\alpha-1}|\phi|^{2}\,dx\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|q|^{3}|\phi|^{\alpha-2}+|q|^{2}|\phi|^{\alpha-1}\,dx.

Therefore, (
3.9
) becomes

\displaystyle iz_{t}-\frac{\alpha-1}{2}\Big(\omega\kappa^{\alpha-1}z+\omega\kappa^{\alpha-1}\overline{z}\Big)=

\displaystyle\kappa^{\alpha-1}\|\phi\|^{-2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}O\Big((|z|^{2}+|z|^{3})\|\phi\|^{\alpha+1}_{L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(|q|^{3}|\phi|^{\alpha-2}+|q|^{2}|\phi|^{\alpha-1}+|q||r|)d\sigma(x)\Big).

Invoking the
L^{2}
estimate of
q(t)
, we have

\displaystyle\kappa^{\alpha-1}\|\phi\|^{-2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|q|^{3}|\phi|^{\alpha-2}d\sigma(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{2s}\|q\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\|q\|_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\|\phi\|^{\alpha-2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{2s}n^{\frac{\alpha-5}{4}-(\alpha+1)s}n^{\frac{\alpha+3}{8}-\frac{(\alpha+1)s}{2}}n^{(\alpha-2)(\frac{1}{4}-s)}

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{\frac{5\alpha-11}{8}-\frac{5}{2}(\alpha-1)s},

and

\displaystyle\kappa^{2}\|\phi\|^{-2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|q|^{2}|\phi|^{\alpha-1}d\sigma(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{2s}\|q\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\|\phi\|^{\alpha-1}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\lesssim n^{\frac{\alpha-3}{2}-2(\alpha-1)s}.

Note that
|\phi|^{\alpha-1}\phi=\omega\phi+r
, multiplying
r
and taking the inner product implies that

\|r\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|\phi|^{\alpha-1}\phi\overline{r}d\sigma(x)\lesssim\|\phi\|^{\alpha}_{L^{2\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\|r\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}.

By using the eigenfunction estimate, we have

\|r\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\lesssim\|\phi\|^{\alpha}_{L^{2\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\lesssim n^{\frac{\alpha-1}{4}-s\alpha}.

Consequently, the last source term enjoys the bound

\displaystyle\kappa^{2}\|\phi\|^{-2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|q||r|d\sigma(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{2s}\|q\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\|r\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{2s}n^{\frac{\alpha-5}{8}-\frac{(\alpha+1)s}{2}}n^{\frac{\alpha-1}{4}-s\alpha}

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{\frac{3\alpha-7}{8}-\frac{3(\alpha-1)}{2}s}.

Therefore ,we rewrite the equation for
z(t)
with initial data
z(0)=0
as follows

\displaystyle iz_{t}=

\displaystyle(\alpha-1)\omega\kappa^{\alpha-1}\Re(z)+\mathcal{O}(\omega|z|^{2}+\omega|z|^{3})

\displaystyle\hskip 86.11084pt+\begin{cases}\mathcal{O}\big(n^{\frac{3\alpha-7}{8}-\frac{3\alpha-1)}{2}s}\big),\quad s\in\big(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{4(\alpha-1)},\frac{1}{4}\big)\\
\mathcal{O}\big(n^{\frac{5\alpha-11}{8}-\frac{5}{2}(\alpha-1)s}\big),\quad s\in\big(0,\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{4(\alpha-1)}\big].\end{cases}

First, we consider the case
s>\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{4(\alpha-1)}
. From mass conservation (
3.6
), we estimate

\Big|\frac{\alpha-1}{2}|z|^{2}+(\alpha-1)\Re(z)\Big|=\frac{\alpha-1}{2}\Big|1-|1+z|^{2}\Big|=\frac{\alpha-1}{2}\|q\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\|\phi\|^{-2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}=\mathcal{O}(n^{\frac{\alpha-5}{4}-(\alpha-1)s}).

It follows that
(\alpha-1)\Re(z)\omega\lesssim n^{\frac{\alpha-3}{2}-2(\alpha-1)s}
, which can be absorbed into
n^{\frac{3\alpha-7}{8}-\frac{3(\alpha-1)}{2}s}
. Hence,

satisfies

iz_{t}=\mathcal{O}(\omega|z|^{2}+\omega|z|^{3}+n^{\frac{3\alpha-7}{8}-\frac{3(\alpha-1)}{2}s}),

with
z(0)=0
. Let
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
, we take

T_{n}=n^{\frac{5(\alpha-1)}{4}s-\frac{5\alpha-9}{16}-\varepsilon},

and denote that

M(T):=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|z(t)|.

It is clear that

M(T_{n})\lesssim T_{n}\big(\omega M^{2}(T_{n})+\omega M^{3}(T_{n})+n^{\frac{3\alpha-7}{8}-\frac{3(\alpha-1)}{2}s}\big).

Next, we define

\widetilde{M}(T_{n}):=T_{n}^{-1}n^{\frac{3(\alpha-1)}{2}s-\frac{3\alpha-7}{8}}M(T_{n}).

Recall that
\omega=\mathcal{O}(n^{(\alpha-1)(\frac{1}{4}-s)})
and we arrive at

\widetilde{M}(T_{n})\lesssim\big(T_{n}^{2}n^{\frac{5\alpha-9}{8}-\frac{5(\alpha-1)}{2}s}\widetilde{M}^{2}(T_{n})+T_{n}^{3}n^{(\alpha-2)-4(\alpha-1)s}M^{3}(T_{n})+1\big),

Using the bound for

and

, we conclude that for some

Since

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\omega_{n}T_{n}=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}n^{\frac{5-\alpha}{16}+\frac{\alpha-1}{4}s-\varepsilon}=\infty,
