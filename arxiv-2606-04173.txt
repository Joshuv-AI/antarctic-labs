Cubic graphs, S S -minor s and conformal minors
Nishad Kothari, Orlando Lee, Cláudio L. Lucchesi, Cândida Nunes da Silva

Abstract
It is well-known that any class of simple graphs, say 𝒢 \mathcal{G} , that is characterized by finitely many forbidden minors, also admits a characterization by finitely many forbidden topological minors; furthermore, the list of forbidden topological minors may be derived from the list of forbidden minors. We prove a similar result in Matching Theory. Our Main Theorem states that any class of matching covered graphs, say 𝒢 \mathcal{G} , that is characterized by finitely many forbidden S S -minor s that are cubic, also admits a characterization by finitely many forbidden conformal minors that are cubic as well; once again, the list of forbidden conformal minors may be derived from the list of S S -minor s. In order to establish the above, we first prove that every matching covered graph has one of two graphs as a conformal minor — either K 4 K_{4} , or the Θ \Theta graph (i.e., two vertices joined by three edges). (In fact, we need and prove a much stronger statement.) This is reminescent of a theorem due to Lovász: every nonbipartite matching covered graph has one of two graphs as a conformal minor — either K 4 K_{4} , or the triangular prism C 6 ¯ \overline{C_{6}} . As applications of our Main Theorem, we deduce known ‘forbidden conformal minor characterizations’ of pfaffian near-bipartite graphs, and of pfaffian solid graphs, using their respective known ‘forbidden S S -minor characterizations’.

1
Main Story

A connected graph, of order two or more, is
matching covered

if each edge lies in some perfect matching. For several problems
that pertain to the perfect matchings of a graph (such as counting
the number of perfect matchings, or the related problem
of deciding whether a graph admits a pfaffian orientation),
one may restrict attention to matching covered graphs without
entailing any loss of generality.

A vertex of a graph is
cubic
if it has degree precisely three.
A graph is
subcubic

if each of its vertices has degree at most three,
and it is
cubic
if each of its vertices is cubic.
(Note that a cubic graph is also subcubic.)
Using Tutte’s
1
-factor theorem, one may deduce that a cubic graph
is matching covered if and only if it is
2
-connected. The smallest
2
-connected cubic
graph is the
\Theta
graph (i.e., two vertices joined by three edges);
whereas the smallest nonbipartite
2
-connected cubic graphs
are
K_{4}
and the triangular prism
\overline{C_{6}}
.

Graph containment notions of
minors

and
topological minors
play crucial roles in graph theory.
The latter is a stricter notion than the former — in the sense that —
if a graph
H
is a topological minor of a graph
G

then
H
is also a minor of
G
; however, the converse is not true in general.
That being said, the two notions coincide whenever the “smaller graph”
is subcubic. That is, a subcubic graph
H
is a minor of a graph
G

if and only if
H
is a topological minor of
G
.
The following is a well-known theorem (see
[
Die05
, pg. 352, ex. 33]
).

Theorem 1.1

Any class of simple
graphs, say
\mathcal{G}
,
that is characterized by finitely many forbidden minors,
also admits a characterization by finitely many forbidden topological minors.

Furthermore, the list of forbidden topological minors may be derived from
the list of forbidden minors.

On the other hand, graph containment notions of
S
-minor
s
(defined in Section
1.6
),

matching minors
(defined in
1.2
),
and
conformal minors
(defined in
1.1
) —
listed here in increasing order of strictness — play key roles in the theory of matching covered graphs,
and especially in the study of
pfaffian orientations
(defined in
3
). The
latter two coincide whenever the “minor” is subcubic. That is,
a subcubic matching covered graph
H
is a matching minor of a matching covered graph
G
if and only if
H

is a conformal minor of
G
. However, even in the restricted case when the “minor” is cubic,
both of these notions are stricter than that of
S
-minor
s.

Interestingly,
in most known theorems characterizing various classes of matching covered graphs,
the “forbidden minors” happen to be cubic.
Our Main Theorem is a result similar to Theorem
1.1

for this restricted case.

Theorem 1.2

[Main Theorem]

Any class of matching covered graphs, say
\mathcal{G}
, that is characterized by finitely many forbidden
S
-minor
s that are cubic,
also admits a characterization by finitely many forbidden conformal minors that are cubic as well.

Our proof is constructive. In particular, one may derive the list
of forbidden
conformal minors
(defined in Section
1.1
)
from the list of forbidden
S
-minor
s

(defined in Section
1.6
).

In the rest of this section, we provide the necessary background, and state
other results that lead to our proof of the Main Theorem (
1.2
).

1.1
Ear decompositions and conformal minors

A path is
odd
if it has an odd number of edges; otherwise, it is
even
.
As in the case of
2
-connected
graphs, there is an ear decomposition theory underlying matching covered graphs.

By a
removable single ear
of a matching covered graph
G
, we mean an odd path, say
P
, each of whose internal vertices is
of degree two (in
G
) such that the graph
G-P
is matching covered. (Note that, unless
G
is a cycle, the ends of
P
have degree
three or more in
G
.) It is well-known, and not too difficult to prove, that every bipartite matching covered graph,
except
K_{2}
, has a removable single ear. However, the same does not hold for nonbipartite matching covered graphs. For instance,
neither
K_{4}
nor
\overline{C_{6}}
, has a removable single ear. (In order to deal with nonbipartite graphs,
we need the flexibility to simultaneously remove “two single ears”.)

By a
removable double ear
of a matching covered graph
G
, we mean a pair of vertex-disjoint odd paths, say
(P,Q)
,
each of whose internal vertices is of degree two (in
G
) such that: (i) neither
G-P
nor
G-Q
is matching covered,
and (ii)
G-P-Q
is matching covered. For the sake of brevity, by a
removable ear
,
we mean either a
removable single ear
or a
removable double ear
.
A fundamental result in matching theory (see
[
LP86
]
) states that every matching covered graph, except
K_{2}
,
has a removable ear.

To put it differently, given any matching covered graph
G
, there exists a sequence
(G_{1},G_{2},\dots,G_{r})

of matching covered subgraphs of
G
such that: (i)
G_{1}:=K_{2}
, (ii)
G_{r}:=G
, and (iii) for each
2\leq i\leq r
,
the graph
G_{i-1}
is obtained from
G_{i}
by deleting a removable ear.
We refer to such a sequence as an
ear decomposition of the graph
G
.
We now proceed to state a further strengthening of this result.

A graph is
matchable
if it has a perfect matching,
and a subgraph
H
of a graph
G
is
conformal
if the graph
G-V(H)
is matchable.
It is easily observed that every subgraph, in an ear decomposition of a matching covered graph
G
, is a conformal
subgraph of
G
. The stronger version (mentioned above) says that, for any conformal matching covered subgraph
H
of a
matching covered graph
G
, there is an ear decomposition, say
(G_{1},G_{2},\dots,G_{r})
, of
G
such that
H
appears
in this sequence. We now proceed to state a different strengthening applicable to nonbipartite
graphs that will immediately lead us to the notion of a
conformal minor
.

To
bi-subdivide
an edge means to subdivide it by
inserting an even number of subdivision vertices.
A graph
H
is a
bi-subdivision
of a graph
J

if the former may be obtained from the latter by means of bi-subdividing
each edge in some subset of
E(J)
. It is easily proved
that, for a matching covered graph
J
distinct from
K_{2}
,
each
bi-subdivision
of
J
is also matching covered.
Lovász
[
Lov83
]
proved the following deep result.

Theorem 1.3

[Lovász’s Theorem]

Every nonbipartite matching covered graph
G
admits an ear decomposition, say
(G_{1},G_{2},\dots,G_{r})
,
such that either
G_{3}
is a bi-subdivision of
K_{4}
, or otherwise
G_{4}
is a bi-subdivision of
\overline{C_{6}}
.

A matching covered graph
J
is a
conformal minor
of a matching covered graph
G
if the
latter has a conformal subgraph
H
that is a
bi-subdivision
of
J
.
For the sake of convenience,
if a matching covered graph
J
is a conformal minor of a matching covered graph
G
then we say that
G
is
J
-based
;
otherwise,
G
is
J
-free
.
Lovász’s Theorem (
1.3
) may thus be restated as follows.

Theorem 1.4

[Lovász’s Theorem]

Every nonbipartite matching covered graph is either
K_{4}
-based
or
\overline{C_{6}}
-based
(or both).

In Section
2.2
, we will prove the following result that is reminescent of Lovász’s Theorem, but is
easier to prove and applies to all matching covered graphs. (In fact, we will prove something much stronger
that will help us in proving our Main Theorem.)

Theorem 1.5

[
\Theta
-
K_{4}
Theorem]

Every matching covered graph is either
\Theta
-based
or
K_{4}
-based
(or both).

We now introduce a notation that we will find useful later.
Given a finite set of matching covered graphs, say
\mathcal{J}
, we say that a matching covered graph
G
is
\mathcal{J}
-free
if
G
is
J
-free
for each graph
J\in\mathcal{J}
; otherwise, we say that
G
is
\mathcal{J}
-based.
For instance, Lovász’s Theorem (
1.4
)
states that every nonbipartite matching covered graph is
\{K_{4},\overline{C_{6}}\}
-based,
whereas Theorem
1.5
states
that there is no
\{\Theta,K_{4}\}
-free matching covered graph.

1.3
Tight cuts, bricks and braces

For a graph
G
and a subset
X\subseteq V(G)
, we use
\partial_{G}(X)
,
or simply
\partial(X)
when there is no ambiguity,
to denote the corresponding cut
(that is, the set of edges with one end in
X
and the other end in
\overline{X}:=V(G)-X
);
we refer to
X
and
\overline{X}
as the
shores
of the cut.
A cut is
trivial
if either of its shores is a singleton; otherwise, it is
nontrivial
.
For a trivial cut (i.e., edges incident at a vertex
v
),
we simplify notation as follows:
\partial(v):=\partial(\{v\})
.
By a
k
-cut
, we mean a cut that comprises precisely
k
edges.

For a cut
C:=\partial(X)
, we use
G/X\rightarrow x
, or simply
G/X
,
to denote the graph obtained from
G
by shrinking the shore
X
to a single vertex
x
; all edges
with both ends in
X
are deleted. The graph
G/\overline{X}
is defined analogously.
We refer to
G/X
and
G/\overline{X}
as the
C
-contractions of
G
.

A cut
C
of a matching covered graph
G
is a
tight cut
if it meets each perfect matching
M
exactly once (i.e.,

|M\cap C|=1
). It may be easily verified that if
C
is a tight cut of a matching covered graph
G

then each
C
-contraction of
G
is matching covered as well.
A matching covered graph devoid of nontrivial tight cuts is called a
brick
if it is nonbipartite, and a
brace
otherwise.
For instance:
\Theta,C_{4}
and
K_{3,3}
are braces whereas
K_{4}
and
\overline{C_{6}}
are bricks.

Using tight cuts, one may now define the well-known
tight cut decomposition procedure
— for any
matching covered graph
G
— as follows. If
G
has a nontrivial tight cut, say
C
, then we obtain
two smaller matching covered graphs by considering the
C
-contractions of
G
;
if either of them has a nontrivial tight cut then we
repeat the same process to obtain even smaller matching covered graphs. We do this recursively until we obtain a list of
bricks and braces. Note that, since one may choose any nontrivial tight cut at each step, a matching covered graph may admit many different applications of the tight cut decomposition procedure.
However, Lovász
[
Lov87
]
proved the following remarkable result.

Theorem 1.9

[Unique Tight Cut Decomposition Theorem]

Any two applications of the tight cut decomposition procedure on a matching covered graph
G

yield the same list of bricks and braces (up to multiplicities of edges).

In light of the above theorem, for any matching covered graph
G
, we may refer to —
the underlying simple graphs produced by any application of the tight cut decomposition procedure on
G
—
as the
bricks and braces of
G
.
It is worth noting that nontrivial tight cuts may be discovered in polynomial-time; this follows, for instance,
from the work of Edmonds, Lovász and Pulleyblank
[
ELP82
]
.
Consequently, the bricks and braces of a matching covered graph may be computed in polynomial-time.

A graph
G
is
bicritical
if the graph
G-u-v
is matchable for each pair
u,v
of distinct vertices.
They
[

also proved the following characterization of bricks that we will find useful later.

Theorem 1.10

A graph

, of order four or more, is a brick if and only if
G

3
-connected
and bicritical.

1.6
Proof of the Main Theorem

Before proving the Main Theorem (which applies to a finite set of
2
-connected cubic graphs), we
prove the key lemma that applies to a fixed

-connected cubic graph.

Lemma 1.18

Let
H
denote any
2
-connected cubic graph, and let
G
be a matching covered graph. If

is an
S
-minor
of
G

then some
K_{4}
-decoration
of
H
is a conformal minor of
G
.

Proof
:
Assume that
H
is an
S
-minor
of
G
. Thus there exists a sequence
(G_{1},G_{2},\dots,G_{r})
,
where
G_{1}:=H
and
G_{r}:=G
, so that, for each
2\leq i\leq r
,
the graph
G_{i-1}
is obtained from
G_{i}
either by deleting a removable ear,
or by contracting the shore of a separating cut.
Note that
H
is an
S
-minor
of each graph in the sequence.
If

then
H
is isomorphic to

; ergo a conformal minor of
G
.

Now suppose that
r\geq 2
, and
assume inductively that the desired conclusion holds for
G_{r-1}
. Thus,
some
K_{4}
-decoration
of
H
, say
J
, is a conformal minor of
G_{r-1}
. If
G_{r-1}
is obtained from
G_{r}

by deleting a removable ear then clearly
J
is a conformal minor of
G_{r}
.
Otherwise,
G_{r-1}
is obtained from
G_{r}
by contracting the shore of a separating cut, say

;
in particular,
G_{r-1}
is a

-contraction of

.
By the
K_{4}
-decoration
Theorem (

),
some
K_{4}
-decoration
of

, say

, is a conformal minor of
G_{r}
.
In either case, we have shown that some
K_{4}
-decoration
of

is a conformal minor of
G
.

\Box

We are now ready to prove the Main Theorem (
1.2
).
In fact, we prove a refined version (
1.19
)
that also explains how the finite list of forbidden conformal minors
is derived from the finite list of forbidden
S
-minor
s. In order to do so, we generalize the notation

K_{4}(\cdot)
from a single graph to a finite set of graphs. For a finite set of
2
-connected cubic graphs,
say

as follows.

K_{4}(\mathcal{H}):=\displaystyle\bigcup_{H\in\mathcal{H}}K_{4}(H)

Theorem 1.19

[Main Theorem]

Let
\mathcal{H}
denote a finite set of
2
-connected cubic graphs, and
let
\mathcal{G}
denote a class of matching covered graphs such that
G\in\mathcal{G}

if and only if, for each
H\in\mathcal{H}
, the graph
H
is not an
S
-minor
of
G
.
Then
G\in\mathcal{G}
if and only if
G
is
K_{4}(\mathcal{H})
-free.

Proof
:
We let
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{G}
be as described in the theorem statement.

First suppose that
G
is
K_{4}(\mathcal{H})
-based; i.e.,
there exists a graph
J\in K_{4}(\mathcal{H})
that is a conformal minor of
G
.
By Proposition
1.14
,
J
is an
S
-minor
of
G
.
By definition,

is a

-decoration
of some graph

.
By Corollary

,

is an
S
-minor
of

It follows from transitivity that

is an

-minor
of

.

Conversely, suppose that
G\notin\mathcal{G}
. By hypothesis, there exists a graph
H\in\mathcal{H}

that is an

-minor
of

. By Lemma

-decoration
of

This completes the proof of Theorem

.

\Box

Observe that, for a finite set of
2
-connected cubic graphs, say

,
the set

has at most

\sum_{H\in\mathcal{H}}2^{|V(H)|}

Consequently, in theory, the number of forbidden conformal minors may be exponential
in terms of the number of forbidden

-minor

,
in practice, the numbers are somewhat comparable due to two reasons (as follows). Firstly, most
of the forbidden minors encountered have various symmetries;
consequently, many of their

-decoration

Secondly, the particular class of graphs (one seeks to characterize) may impose additional constraints, and
often allows one to omit many of the

-decoration

this point will become clearer in Section
3
via concrete examples.

3
Applications

It was shown by Valiant
[
Val79
]
that,
in general,
it is not possible to compute the number of perfect matchings of a graph in polynomial-time
(unless
P

=

NP
).
(It is easily observed that one may restrict attention to matching covered graphs.)

An orientation
D
of a matching covered graph
G
is a
pfaffian orientation
if each conformal cycle
has an odd number of edges oriented clockwise (and, consequently, an odd number of edges oriented anticlockwise).
The significance of pfaffian orientations arises from the fact that, whenever a graph
G
is known to admit a pfaffian orientation,
such an orientation may be computed in polynomial-time, and it may consequently be used to compute the
number of perfect matchings of
G
in polynomial-time. However, not all graphs admit a pfaffian orientation.
For instance, it is easily argued that
K_{3,3}
does not admit one.

A matching covered graph
G
is
pfaffian
if
G
admits a pfaffian orientation; otherwise,
G
is
non-pfaffian
.
It was shown by Kasteleyn
[
Kas63
]
that all planar graphs are pfaffian.
A natural question arises: is it possible to characterize
the class of pfaffian graphs (or subclasses of interest therein) in terms of ‘forbidden minors’?
The following seminal result of Little
[
Lit75
]
achieved precisely that
for the class of pfaffian bipartite graphs.

Theorem 3.1

[Little’s Theorem]

A bipartite matching covered graph
G
is pfaffian if and only if

G
is
K_{3,3}
-free
.

Thus, Little’s Theorem provides a characterization of pfaffian bipartite matching covered graphs in terms of forbidden conformal minors,
and laid the foundation of the quest for more such results.

A few decades later, Fischer and Little
[
FL01
]
characterized pfaffian ‘near-bipartite’ graphs in terms
of forbidden
S
-minor
s, as well as in terms of forbidden conformal minors.
More recently, Carvalho, Lucchesi and Murty
[
CLM12
]
generalized Little’s Theorem (
3.1
)
and characterized pfaffian ‘solid’ graphs in terms of forbidden
S
-minor
s; in their paper, they also stated
a characterization in terms of forbidden conformal minors; however, they omitted the proof.
In the next two sections, we use our Main Theorem (
1.19
)
to deduce the ‘conformal minor version’ from the ‘
S
-minor
version’ — for both of these results.

In summary, if
J
has bipartite index one or two, then
J
is in fact near-bipartite (and
has bipartite index two). This completes the proof of Proposition
3.12
.

\Box

This leads us to the desired result applicable
to near-bipartite graphs (that is similar to Theorem
3.6
which applied to solid graphs).

Corollary 3.13

Every
3
-connected cubic conformal minor of a near-bipartite graph is either bipartite or near-bipartite.

Proof
:
Let
G
denote a near-bipartite graph that has a conformal minor
J
that is
3
-connected and cubic.
In particular,
G
has a subgraph
H
that is a bi-subdivision of
J
.
Since
G
is near-bipartite,
{\sf bi}(G)=2
.
By Lemma
3.10
,
{\sf bi}(J)={\sf bi}(H)\leq 2
.
By Proposition
3.12
, we conclude
that
J
is either bipartite or near-bipartite.

\Box

Lemma 3.17

Each

We are now ready to deduce (from Theorem
3.9
)
the characterization of pfaffian near-bipartite graphs
in terms of forbidden conformal minors due to Fischer and Little
[
FL01
]
.

Theorem 3.18

A near-bipartite graph
G
is pfaffian if and only if
G
is
(\{K_{3,3}\}\cup\mathcal{N}
)-free.

Proof
:
It follows immediately from Theorem
3.9
and
our Main Theorem (
1.19
)
that a near-bipartite
G
is pfaffian if and only if
G
is
K_{4}(\{K_{3,3},\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}\})
-free.
By Lemma
3.17
, each of the graphs
in
K_{4}(\{K_{3,3},\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}\})
is
3
-connected and cubic.
By invoking Corollary
3.13

and Proposition
3.16
,
we conclude that a near-bipartite graph
G
is pfaffian if and only if
G
is
\{K_{3,3}\}\cup\mathcal{N}
-free.

\Box
