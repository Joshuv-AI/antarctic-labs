Institutions, Inputs, and Agricultural Growth in China: Revisiting Several Controversies, 1949–1986
Jiyuan Lyu

Abstract
Scholarly debates on China’s agricultural growth between 1949 and 1986 continue to differ over the extent of the price scissors, the effect of heavy industrial investment, the role of the 1978 reforms, and the impact of decollectivization on irrigation. Using a single dataset and complementary econometric methods, this paper addresses each of these controversies. The results show that 1952–1957 was the only net extraction period across all three channels, after which the state channelled a net inflow of about 168.6 billion yuan into agriculture via fiscal and credit instruments. Heavy industrial investment exerted a significant positive lagged effect on agriculture, while the contemporaneous negative correlation stemmed from the zero-sum nature of the investment share indicator. The input-output elasticity shifted abruptly in 1970, and collective agricultural loans broke in 1971, both pointing to the rectification effects of the North China Agricultural Conference. Disaster prevention capacity fell from 0.70 under the collective era to 0.53 after household contracting, mainly because the collective maintenance system collapsed rather than because state investment declined. After 1979 the price elasticity of agricultural supply approached zero, suggesting that the 1979 procurement price increase acted more like a one-off recalibration than a sustained marginal incentive.



1  Introduction

Between 1949 and 1986 the nominal value of China’s agricultural output grew about sixteen-fold. Over the same period chemical fertiliser use rose from 13,000 to 19.2 million tons, large and medium tractors increased from virtually zero to 866,000 units, and the effectively irrigated area more than doubled. Institutionally, the countryside moved through land reform, cooperativisation, the people’s communes, the Great Leap Forward, the Cultural Revolution, and the household responsibility system. Despite this rich historical record, several core disagreements persist about the driving forces behind the growth.

The first controversy concerns the direction and timing of resource flows between the state and agriculture. The notion of the “price scissors” has long shaped perceptions, yet
Sheng 1993
and
Knight and Song 1999
already noted that focusing solely on price channels misses fiscal transfers and credit rationing.
Wen 2013
argued that the Mao era completed primitive accumulation through the scissors and collectivisation, after which the state shifted to supporting agriculture. A quantitative, channel-by-channel periodisation is still lacking.

A second dispute revolves around heavy industrial investment. The common claim is that priority for heavy industry squeezed agricultural resources, generating a contemporaneous negative effect. Yet
Stone 1988
documented the decisive contribution of the chemical fertiliser industry, and
Bramall 2009
pointed out that investment effects operate with a lag. If construction cycles are taken into account, heavy industry’s contribution may appear as a “feedback” rather than a “crowding out”.

A third debate centres on the institutional changes around 1978.
Lin 1992
estimated that the household responsibility system accounted for about 47% of output growth from 1978 to 1984, and
McMillan et al. 1989
also attributed the jump in total factor productivity to improved incentives. However,
Kung and Lin 2007
questioned whether price effects and institutional effects can be separated when they overlap so closely in time.
Wen 2013
and
Xiao 2014
adopted a longer perspective, arguing that post-reform growth largely released material foundations accumulated during the preceding three decades. Was 1978 a discontinuous institutional miracle, or just one link in an accumulation-release chain?

The fourth controversy involves household contracting and public goods such as irrigation.
Nickum 1998
and
Oi 1999
observed a visible weakening of rural public goods provision after decollectivisation.
Bramall 2009
estimated that roughly 70% of the farmland water conservancy stock added between 1950 and 1978 came from commune-brigade self-organised labour. If the collective organisation dissolved, would existing facilities depreciate faster for want of maintenance? And how large was the effect?

These questions have usually been addressed separately in the literature, yet they overlap heavily in time, data, and mechanisms. It is both possible and useful to examine them within a single framework. That is the starting point of this paper: instead of imposing one grand narrative, I translate each controversy into a testable statistical proposition, apply the same annual dataset covering 1949–1986, and use collinearity diagnostics, ridge regression, principal component analysis, Chow breakpoint scanning, and lag-effect scanning to see which account the data support.

The rest of the paper proceeds as follows. Section 2 reviews the related literature and formulates the propositions. Section 3 describes the data and methods. Section 4 analyses input indivisibility. Section 5 presents the three-channel resource flow accounting. Section 6 examines several facets of institutional change. Section 7 focuses on the intertemporal effects of heavy industrial investment. Section 8 reports auxiliary findings on disasters, electrification, mechanisation, and credit. Section 9 provides a comprehensive discussion, and Section 10 concludes.

2  Literature and Testable Propositions

2.1  Resource flows: the scope and limits of the price scissors

The “scissors” thesis, rooted in
Preobrazhensky 1965
model of primitive accumulation, has been widely used to explain how Chinese agriculture supplied surpluses for industrialisation.
Sheng 1993
produced an early systematic estimate of transfers through the price channel.
Knight and Song 1999
, however, pointed out that neglecting fiscal and credit channels seriously distorts the conclusion.
Wen 2013
“eight crises” narrative stresses that the first thirty years completed primitive accumulation and then shifted to supporting agriculture, but this claim requires quantitative support disaggregated by channel and period. Accordingly:

Proposition 1 (periodisation of resource flows):
1952–1957 constituted the only period of net extraction through all three channels. After 1958 the fiscal channel turned to net inflow, and after 1978 all three channels registered large net inflows. The “price scissors” as an encompassing description applies only to the first five years.

2.2  Heavy industrial investment: crowding out or feedback

The heavy-industry-first strategy is often criticised for squeezing agricultural investment. Yet
Stone 1988
demonstrated the decisive role of the chemical fertiliser build-up, and
Fan 1991
provided elasticity estimates for various inputs. The key may lie in time lags. Small nitrogenous fertiliser plants and large ammonia complexes typically required three to seven years from investment to operation. A simple contemporaneous regression may misinterpret temporal mismatch as crowding out. Hence:

Proposition 2 (lagged feedback from heavy industrial investment):
Heavy industrial investment exerts a significant positive lagged effect on agricultural growth, while contemporaneous negative effects arise from the zero-sum property of the investment share indicator and should disappear when absolute investment volume is used.

2.3  Institutional change: the statistical imprint of 1970–1971

The existing literature concentrates its attention to institutional breaks at 1958 and 1978. However, reports by
Perkins 1969
and
Wen 1993
hint that total factor productivity underwent an important shift around 1970. The North China Agricultural Conference (1970) reaffirmed the “Sixty Articles”, private plots, and production-team accounting, and called for large additional investments in chemical fertiliser, farm machinery, and water conservancy. If these policies had real effects, they should leave statistical marks on input-output relationships and credit behaviour. Therefore:

Proposition 3 (structural break in 1970–1971):
The input-output elasticity broke in 1970 and the collective agricultural loan series broke in 1971, with statistical significance exceeding that of 1958 and 1978.

2.4  Irrigation as a public good: construction versus maintenance

Vermeer 1977
and
Perkins and Yusuf 1984
early noted the advantages of collectivisation in mobilising labour for water conservancy.
Nickum 1998
and
Oi 1999
later documented the deterioration of irrigation facilities after the reforms. It is necessary to distinguish two possibilities: the state reduced investment, or the collective maintenance system collapsed. The former would imply a significant lagged effect of capital construction grants on disaster prevention capacity; the latter would imply no such effect but a clear decline in prevention capacity after decollectivisation. Thus:

Proposition 4 (institutional roots of disaster prevention):
The decline in disaster prevention capacity after household contracting stems mainly from the collapse of the collective maintenance system, not from reduced state investment in water conservancy.

2.5  Price incentives: one-off recalibration or continuous driver

The 22% increase in agricultural procurement prices in 1979 was a landmark event.
Lin 1992
and
McMillan et al. 1989
incorporated it within the improvement of incentives, but
Kung and Lin 2007
questioned whether price and institutional effects can be disentangled. If the 1979 adjustment was a one-off recalibration, the price elasticity of agricultural supply should drop sharply as subsequent price hikes taper off. Given the very small number of post-reform observations, this paper treats this point only as an auxiliary observation rather than a formal test.

Auxiliary observation (price signal):
After 1979 the price elasticity of agricultural supply approached zero.

3.4  Honest methodological statement

Inherent limitations include: the extreme collinearity of modern inputs prevents exact separation of the contributions of fertiliser, machinery, and irrigation; the price indices are weighted averages that blur differences among compulsory procurement prices, above-quota premiums, and negotiated prices; the 1967–1969 disaster and investment data are irretrievably missing; the nationwide annual time series has limited statistical power; in the absence of exogenous instrumental variables, causal inference rests essentially on time-series correlations. The following sections adhere strictly to what the data can support.

7.3  Micro mechanisms

The self-sufficiency ratio for chemical fertiliser leapt from 22.2% in 1962 to 75.0% in 1965 (Table
18
), coinciding with the early-1960s shift of heavy industrial investment from steel toward agricultural support. Between 1966 and 1975 the number of small nitrogenous fertiliser plants grew from about 200 to over 1,500, and domestic production rose at an average annual rate of 14.1%. After the large ammonia plants imported under the “Four-Three Plan” came on stream in 1976–1979, actual output was even below the trend extrapolation, indicating that the real leap in the fertiliser industry came from the small-plant expansion period. The demand-pull effect of light industry on agriculture is very weak, while the supply-push effect of agriculture on light industry is significantly lagged by 1–4 years; the feedback operates mainly through the supply side.

8  Disasters, Electrification, Mechanisation, and Credit

8.1  Changes in the disaster structure

Flood-affected area dropped from an annual average of about 160 million mu in 1960–1964 to 80 million mu in 1970–1974, reflecting the stock effect of reservoirs and dykes. Drought-affected area rebounded from the 1970–1974 trough to 150 million mu in 1980–1984, as irrigation canals and pumping stations deteriorated from insufficient maintenance. Individual disaster categories are not significant when entered separately into the difference regression.

8.2  Electrification and mechanisation

Rural electricity’s share of total national consumption rose from 0.69% to 13.05%. Its univariate elasticity is 0.32 but turns negative once the time trend is controlled, mirroring the fertiliser pattern. Threshers grew from 15,000 units in 1962 to 3.676 million in 1986, a 245-fold increase, matching the need for decentralised household operations after contracting. All machinery series are highly collinear.

8.3  Institutional inertia in credit

The sharp drop in collective loans in 1971 (
F=121.6
) was an internal rectification within the financial system. Loans surged 61% in 1984, yet the collective share never fell below 85%. After the people’s communes were abolished, the intermediary structure of credit cooperatives–village organisations–households remained practically unchanged; genuine direct lending to individual farming households had yet to appear. The time lag between the marketisation of agricultural production and the marketisation of rural finance was at least five to ten years.

9  Comprehensive Discussion

9.1  Interconnections among the controversies

Viewed together, resource flows, investment effects, institutional breaks, and public goods provision share a common temporal thread. 1952–1957 was the only period of net extraction through all three channels; thereafter the fiscal channel reversed first, and the price and credit channels followed. Heavy industrial investment shifted toward agricultural support from the 1960s onward, and after a 3–7-year construction cycle released a wave of fertiliser and machinery capacity in the 1970s. The 1970 North China Agricultural Conference confirmed this input priority and, together with the credit rectification, left a clear statistical break. By the time reforms were launched in 1978, the material foundations were in place. Household contracting and the responsibility system provided the incentives that fully released this potential, while the collapse of the collective maintenance system exacted a cost in disaster prevention capacity. These links conditioned one another and together shaped the trajectory of Chinese agriculture from 1949 to 1986.

9.2  Dialogue with the existing literature

This paper confirms the important contribution of inputs
(
Perkins 1969
;
Stone 1988
)
and the output-enhancing effect of the 1978 reforms
(
Lin 1992
)
, but it emphasises that the latter was built on prior accumulation. The three-channel accounting provides
Wen 2013
“accumulation then feedback” narrative with channel- and period-specific quantitative support. The evidence on the pure lagged feedback of heavy industrial investment explains why contemporaneous regressions tend to find “crowding out”: it is a statistical artefact, not a real economic relationship. On water conservancy, the paper uses a dose-response approach to distinguish the institutional logic of construction from that of maintenance, directly addressing the qualitative observations of
Nickum 1998
and
Bramall 2009
. The finding that the price elasticity vanishes after reform is consistent with
Kung and Lin 2007
critique that price and institutional effects are hard to separate, and it cautions against attributing post-1978 growth solely to property rights reform.

9.3  Methodological boundaries

The core analytical tools used here—lag-effect scanning and Chow breakpoint tests—essentially capture statistical associations. In the absence of exogenous instruments, causal inference remains directional. Institutional changes partly responded endogenously to prior economic performance: the 1970 conference was a reaction to Cultural Revolution chaos, and the 1979 price increase was a reaction to prolonged price suppression. The paper partially mitigates endogeneity concerns through lag structures and cross-variable break consistency checks, but cannot eliminate them entirely. Input collinearity limits factor separation, and the 1967–1969 data gap together with some small-sample estimates introduces additional uncertainty. The conclusions should be read within these boundaries.

9.4  Policy implications

A genuine tension exists between institutional incentives and public goods provision. The success of household contracting in solving the incentive problem and its failure in maintaining irrigation infrastructure are two sides of the same institutional change. Policy design cannot focus on efficiency alone; it must create alternative institutional arrangements for public goods supply. The effectiveness of price reform is highly contingent on the institutional environment and material conditions: the success of the 1979 price increase was inseparable from the simultaneous advance of property rights reform and the expansion of fertiliser capacity. The timing and structure of resource inputs also matter: the accumulation of the 1970s laid the foundation for the release after 1978, but the persistent collective-intermediated structure of rural credit meant that financial resources did not reach smallholders directly for a long time.

10  Conclusion

Using annual data on Chinese agriculture from 1949 to 1986, this paper has conducted systematic empirical tests motivated by several long-standing controversies. The main findings are as follows.

First, 1952–1957 was the only period of net extraction through fiscal, price, and credit channels combined. Thereafter the state channelled a cumulative net inflow of approximately 168.6 billion yuan into agriculture. The “price scissors” as a blanket concept should be restricted to the initial five years.

Second, the contribution of heavy industrial investment to agriculture was purely lagged and involved no crowding out. Absolute investment volume is consistently and significantly positive at lags of 3–7 years. The contemporaneous negative effect arises from the zero-sum nature of the investment share indicator and is a statistical artefact.

Third, the most pronounced structural shift in agricultural growth occurred in 1970–1971, when a mutation in the input-output elasticity and a rupture in the credit order both pointed to the rectification effects of the North China Agricultural Conference. The 1978 reforms released the potential accumulated earlier, but their statistical imprint on the input-output relationship is no more prominent than that of 1970.

Fourth, after household contracting, disaster prevention capacity fell from 0.70 to 0.53. The root cause was the collapse of the collective maintenance system, not a reduction in state investment. The lagged effects of capital construction grants on prevention capacity are insignificant at all lags.

Fifth, the price elasticity of agricultural supply approached zero after 1979, suggesting that the procurement price increase of that year functioned more like a one-off recalibration than a sustained marginal incentive. This observation is constrained by sample size and awaits further verification.

Taken together, these findings reveal a clear temporal sequence: primitive accumulation was completed in the 1950s, the 1960s and 1970s shifted toward material inputs and institutional rectification, and the post-1978 period released the accumulated potential while paying a price in the maintenance of public goods. Every turn in this process was not an isolated institutional miracle but the lagged echo of previous rounds of investment and policy.
