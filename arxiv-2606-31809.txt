Quantum Stokes matrices and quantum Riemann-Hilbert-Birkhoff maps
Xiaomeng Xu

Abstract
In this paper, we introduce quantum Stokes matrices for a noncommutative version of meromorphic linear systems of ordinary differential equations with a pole of order p + 1 p+1 . We prove that these quantum Stokes matrices satisfy natural quantum exchange relations. These relations allow us to interpret the quantum Stokes matrices as an associative algebra homomorphism, which may be viewed as a deformation quantization of the Riemann-Hilbert-Birkhoff map, regarded as a Poisson map, for meromorphic connections with a pole of order p + 1 p+1 .



1
Introduction

The Stokes phenomenon describes the fact that a solution of a meromorphic
linear ordinary differential equation may have different asymptotic expansions
as
z
approaches an irregular singular point from different sectors. We refer to
[
4
,
14
,
22
]
for detailed introductions
to the Stokes phenomenon. The jumps between these sectorial asymptotic
expansions are measured by Stokes matrices.

Over the past decades, Stokes matrices for meromorphic
linear systems have played important roles in many areas of mathematics and
physics. However, they are highly transcendental objects and are
therefore difficult to study directly. In
[
24
]
and subsequent works, we
obtained algebraic characterizations of several analytic problems concerning
Stokes matrices and isomonodromy deformations of meromorphic linear systems
with a pole of order two. These results rely on the algebraic structures,
in particular Yang-Baxter relations, underlying the Stokes phenomenon at
a second order pole, see for example
[
23
,
19
]
. In the present paper, we
derive analogous quantum algebras hidden behind the Stokes phenomenon for
poles of arbitrary order, with the hope that the representation theory of the quantum algebras can be used to understand various asymptotic analysis problems for general meromorphic linear systems.

Main theorem

Let
p>1
be an integer, and
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}
be
n
distinct complex numbers.
Let
\mathfrak{U}_{p,\hbar}
be the associative

\mathbb{C}[\hbar]
-algebra generated by

\hbar e_{ij},\qquad\hbar e_{ij}^{(a)},\qquad 1\leq i,j\leq n,\quad 1\leq a\leq p-1,

subject to the relations for all
i,j,k,l=1,...,n
and
a,b=1,...,p-1

\displaystyle[\hbar e_{ij}^{(a)},\hbar e_{kl}^{(b)}]

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\hbar(\delta_{jk}\hbar e_{il}^{(a+b)}-\delta_{li}\hbar e_{kj}^{(a+b)}),&\text{ if }a+b\leq p-1\\
\hbar\delta_{jk}\delta_{il}(u_{i}-u_{j}),&\text{ if }a+b=p,\\
0,&\text{ if }a+b\geq p+1.\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle[\hbar e_{ij},\hbar e_{kl}]

\displaystyle=\hbar(\delta_{jk}\hbar e_{il}-\delta_{li}\hbar e_{kj}),\quad\text{and}\quad[\hbar e_{ij}^{(a)},\hbar e_{kl}]=0.

(4)

We introduce a two-sided ideal

\mathfrak{m}=\bigl(\hbar,\hbar e_{ij},\ \hbar e_{ij}^{(a)}\mid 1\leq i,j\leq n,\ 1\leq a\leq p-1\bigr)\subset\mathfrak{U}_{p,\hbar}.

We then introduce the
\mathfrak{m}
-adic completion
\widehat{\mathfrak{U}}_{p,\hbar}=\varprojlim_{N\geq 1}\mathfrak{U}_{p,\hbar}/\mathfrak{m}^{N}
of the algebra, in which holomorphic valued functions are defined by finite dimensional truncations. For more details, see Section
2
.

Let
\mathfrak{h}_{\rm reg}
denote the set of
n\times n
diagonal matrices with distinct eigenvalues. Given
u\in\mathfrak{h}_{\rm reg}
, let us consider the linear differential equation for holomorphic function
F(z)\in{\widehat{\mathfrak{U}}_{p,\hbar}}{\otimes}\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})

\frac{dF}{dz}=\left(\frac{u}{z^{p+1}}+\frac{\hbar T_{[p]}}{z^{p}}+\cdots+\frac{\hbar T_{[2]}}{z^{2}}-\frac{\hbar T}{z}\right)\cdot F,

(5)

where
u={\rm diag}(u_{1},..,u_{n})
is regarded as an
n\times n
matrix with scalar entries in
\mathfrak{U}_{p,\hbar}
, and
T,T_{[m]}
are
n\times n
matrices with entries

\displaystyle(T_{[m]})_{ij}

\displaystyle=e_{ij}^{(m-1)},\ \ \ \text{for}\ 1\leq i,j\leq n,\ \ 2\leq m\leq p,

\displaystyle(T)_{ij}

\displaystyle=e_{ij},\ \ \ \ \ \text{for}\ 1\leq i,j\leq n.

Thus
u,\hbar T
and
\hbar T_{[m]}
are elements in
\mathfrak{U}_{p,\hbar}\otimes\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
and the product
\cdot
in (
5
) is just the product in the tensor algebra.

Equation (
5
) admits a formal solution
\hat{F}
at

z=0
(see Theorem
2.2
). The standard theory of resummation (see e.g,
[
4
,
14
,
22
]
) states that there exist certain sectorial regions around
z=0
, such that on each of these sectors there is a unique (therefore canonical) holomorphic solution with the prescribed asymptotic expansion
\hat{F}
. These solutions are in general different (a phenomenon known as the Stokes phenomenon), and the transition between them are measured by
2p
(quantum) Stokes matrices
S_{1},...,S_{2p}\in\widehat{\mathfrak{U}}_{p,\hbar}\otimes{\rm End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
. Further details are provided in Section
2.1
.

Theorem 1.1
.

For
p>1
and any
u\in\mathfrak{h}_{\rm reg}
, the Stokes matrices satisfy the commutation relations (as identities in
\widehat{\mathfrak{U}}_{p,\hbar}\otimes{\rm End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\otimes{\rm End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
)

\displaystyle e^{\pi\mathrm{i}\hbar\delta P}S_{i+k}^{(2)}(u)e^{-\pi\mathrm{i}\hbar\delta P}S^{(1)}_{i}(u)

\displaystyle=S^{(1)}_{i}(u)e^{\pi\mathrm{i}\hbar\delta P}S^{(2)}_{i+k}(u)e^{-\pi\mathrm{i}\hbar\delta P},\ \ \text{for }1<k<2p-1,

\displaystyle R^{12}S^{(1)}_{i}(u)e^{-\pi\mathrm{i}\hbar\delta P}S^{(2)}_{i}(u)

\displaystyle=S^{(2)}_{i}(u)e^{-\pi\mathrm{i}\hbar\delta P}S^{(1)}_{i}(u)R^{12},\ \ \text{for $i$ odd}

\displaystyle R^{12}S^{(2)}_{i}(u)e^{-\pi\mathrm{i}\hbar\delta P}S^{(1)}_{i}(u)

\displaystyle=S^{(1)}_{i}(u)e^{-\pi\mathrm{i}\hbar\delta P}S^{(2)}_{i}(u)R^{12},\ \ \text{for $i$ even}

\displaystyle S^{(1)}_{i-1}(u)R^{12}S^{(2)}_{i}(u)e^{-\pi\mathrm{i}\hbar\delta P}

\displaystyle=e^{-\pi\mathrm{i}\hbar\delta P}S^{(2)}_{i}(u)S^{(1)}_{i-1}(u),\ \ \text{for $i$ odd}.

Here the matrix
R\in{\rm End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\otimes{\rm End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\llbracket\hbar\rrbracket
is defined by

R=\sum_{i\neq j,i,j=1}^{n}E_{ii}\otimes E_{jj}+e^{\pi\mathrm{i}\hbar}\sum_{i=1}^{n}E_{ii}\otimes E_{ii}+(e^{{\pi\mathrm{i}\hbar}}-e^{-{\pi\mathrm{i}\hbar}})\sum_{1\leq j<i\leq n}E_{ij}\otimes E_{ji},

(6)

and
\delta P=\sum_{k=1}^{n}1\otimes E_{kk}\otimes E_{kk}
. We take the convention
S^{(1)}_{i+k}(u):=S^{(1)}_{i+k-2p}(u)\cdot e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}D_{1}}
if
i+k-2p\geq 1
, and

S^{(1)}_{k}(u):=\sum_{i,j}S_{k}(u)_{ij}\otimes E_{ij}\otimes 1,\ S^{(2)}_{k}(u):=\sum_{i,j}S_{k}(u)_{ij}\otimes 1\otimes E_{ij},\text{ and }\ R^{12}:=1\otimes R

as elements in
\widehat{\mathfrak{U}}_{p,\hbar}\otimes{\rm End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\otimes{\rm End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
.

Remark 1.2
.

We remark that the matrix
R
is the standard R-matrix for the quantum group
U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
, see
[
12
]
[
18
]
. In the analogous second order case
p=1
, the same construction shows that the (quantum) Stokes matrices associated with a second order pole give the FRT realization of the quantum group
U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
. Further details can be found in
[
23
]
, see also
[
15
]
for a generalization to the super setting.

For general
p>1
, the exchange relations in Theorem
1.1
are reminiscent of the

R
-matrix exchange relations in the lattice current algebras introduced in
[
1
,
2
]
. The lattice
current variables
J_{n}
are full matrix (not just triangular) variables and satisfy

J^{(1)}_{i}J^{(1)}_{j}=J^{(1)}_{j}J^{(1)}_{i}\text{ for }|i-j|>1,\text{ and }\quad J^{(1)}_{i}R_{12}J^{(2)}_{i+1}=J^{(2)}_{i+1}J^{(1)}_{i},\quad R_{21}J^{(1)}_{i}J^{(2)}_{i}=J^{(2)}_{i}J^{(1)}_{i}R_{12}.

In the present paper,
our triangular Stokes matrices
S_{1},\ldots,S_{2p}
play an analogous role: they form a
cyclic family of
R
-matrix exchange variables attached to the Stokes
rays of an irregular singularity. In some sense, the Stokes matrices play a role analogous to half current variables.

One motivation for studying the algebraic structures underlying the (quantum) Stokes matrices is to solve the hard WKB approximation problem of meromorphic linear differential equations. For instance, in the case
p=1
, the algebraic structures allow us to show that the WKB approximation is governed by the crystal structures of quantum groups, see
[
24
]
. Another motivation is to elucidate the Poisson geometric nature of the Riemann–Hilbert–Birkhoff (RHB) maps, as explained in the following.

Quantum Riemann-Hilbert-Birkhoff maps

We interpret Theorem
1.1
as a quantization of the RHB maps. However, this interpretation is presented only as a perspective and is not essential to the content of the paper.

Let us first recall the Poisson geometric nature of the RHB maps, developed in a series of works
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
.
Consider the differential equations for a function
f(z)\in{\rm GL}(n,\mathbb{C})

\displaystyle\frac{df}{dz}=\left(\frac{u}{z^{p+1}}+\frac{A_{p}}{z^{p}}+\cdots+\frac{A_{2}}{z^{2}}-\frac{B}{z}\right)\cdot f,

(7)

where
u={\rm diag}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n})\in\mathfrak{h}_{\rm reg}
, and
A_{i},B\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
for
i=2,...,p
. The equation has a pole of order
p+1
at
z=0
.

For fixed
u
, the moduli space of differential equations (
7
) can be identified with the product space

M_{dR}^{(p)}=\{(A_{p},...,A_{2},B)\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}\times\cdots\times\mathfrak{gl}_{n}\}.

(8)

The space carries a natural Poisson structure. In terms of the coordinate functions
\{b_{ij}\}_{i,j=1,...,n}
of
B
and
\{a^{(m-1)}_{ij}\}_{i,j=1,...,n}
of
A_{m}
for
m=2,...,p
, the Poisson brackets are given by

\{a^{(m)}_{ij},a^{(s)}_{kl}\}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\delta_{jk}a_{il}^{(m+s)}-\delta_{li}a_{kj}^{(m+s)},&\text{ if }m+s\leq p\\
\delta_{jk}\delta_{il}(u_{i}-u_{j}),&\text{ if }m+s=p+1,\end{array}\right.

(9)

and

\{b_{ij},b_{kl}\}=\delta_{jk}b_{il}-\delta_{li}b_{kj},\hskip 8.53581pt\ \{b_{ij},a^{(m)}_{kl}\}=0,\text{ for }i,j,k,l=1,...,n,\text{ and }m=2,...,p.

(10)

Define

A(z)=\left(\frac{u}{z^{p+1}}+\frac{A_{p}}{z^{p}}+\cdots+\frac{A_{2}}{z^{2}}-\frac{B}{z}\right)\in{\rm End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})[z^{-1}].

If
u
is regular, then there exists a unique formal power series
\hat{h}(z)\in{\rm End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\llbracket z\rrbracket
, such that the formal gauge transform

\hat{h}(z)[A(z)]=\hat{h}(z)A(z)\hat{H}(z)^{-1}+d\hat{h}(z)\cdot\hat{h}(z)^{-1}=\frac{u}{z^{p+1}}+\frac{d_{p}}{z^{p}}+\cdots+\frac{d_{2}}{z^{2}}+\frac{d_{1}}{z},

(11)

where
d_{p},d_{p-1},...,d_{1}
is a series of
n\times n
diagonal matrices. The irregular part
d(z):=\frac{u}{z^{p+1}}+\frac{d_{p}}{z^{p}}+\cdots+\frac{d_{2}}{z^{2}}
is called the irregular type of equation (
7
).
Fix an irregular type
d(z)
parameterized by
(u,d_{p},...,d_{2})
, and let
M_{dR}^{(k)}(u,d_{p},...,d_{2})
denote the subspace of
M_{dR}^{(k)}
with the given irregular type.

Theorem 1.3
.

[
7
]

The Riemann-Hilbert-Birkhoff (also known as irregular Riemann-Hilbert) map

\nu:M_{dR}^{(p)}(u,d_{p},...,d_{2})\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{B}^{(p)}~;~(A_{p},...,A_{2},B)\mapsto(s_{1},...,s_{2p},d_{1})

associating the classical Stokes matrices to the differential equation (
7
), is a locally analytic Poisson isomorphism.
Here, the Poisson structure on the space of Stokes matrices

\mathcal{M}_{B}^{(p)}=(U_{-}\times U_{+})^{p}\times\mathfrak{h}

is induced from the irregular Atiyah-Bott construction
[
7
]
, and admits an explicit form described by the quasi-Hamiltonian approach
[
3
]
.

We now clarify the sense in which (
5
) quantizes (
7
). The
classical equation (
7
) admits a formal solution

\widehat{f}(z)=\left(1+h_{1}z+h_{2}z^{2}+\cdots\right)\cdot\exp\int\left(\frac{u}{z^{p+1}}+\frac{d_{p}}{z^{p}}+\cdots+\frac{d_{1}}{z}\right)dz,

where
d_{p},\ldots,d_{1}
are diagonal matrices. The entries of the matrices

h_{m}
and
d_{r}
are polynomial functions on
M_{dR}^{(p)}
, with
coefficients depending on the fixed regular diagonal matrix
u
.

The quantum equation (
5
) has an analogous formal solution (see Theorem
2.2
)

\widehat{F}(z)=\left(1+H_{1}z+H_{2}z^{2}+\cdots\right)\cdot\exp\int\left(\frac{u}{z^{p+1}}+\frac{D_{p}}{z^{p}}+\cdots+\frac{D_{1}}{z}\right)dz,

where
D_{p},\ldots,D_{1}
are diagonal matrices with entries in
\mathfrak{U}_{p,\hbar}
, and

H_{m}\in\mathfrak{U}_{p,\hbar}\otimes\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
. Thus the formal
solution
\widehat{F}(z)
for the quantum equation gives a canonical lift of the
formal solution
\widehat{f}(z)
of the classical equation from
polynomial functions on
M_{dR}^{(p)}
to
\mathfrak{U}_{p,\hbar}
.

After Borel-Laplace resummation, the classical formal solution gives sectorial holomorphic
solutions and hence classical Stokes matrices. These Stokes matrices are holomorphic functions of the coefficients of (
7
), equivalently
the classical Riemann-Hilbert-Birkhoff map gives an analytic Poisson map. On the
quantum side, the same resummation procedure is understood in the

\mathfrak{m}
-adic sense: the resummation of
\widehat{F}(z)
produces sectorial holomorphic matrix solutions with entries in every finite truncation
\mathfrak{U}_{p,\hbar}/\mathfrak{m}^{N}
, and the inverse limit defines holomorphic matrix solutions and hence quantum Stokes matrices with entries in
\widehat{\mathfrak{U}}_{p,\hbar}
.

If one ignores the transcendental origin of these matrices and keeps only
their algebraic relations as in Theorem
1.1
, then one can define an associative algebra

\mathcal{A}_{\hbar}^{(p)}
, with generators collecting in triangular matrices
L_{1},...,L_{2p}
subject to the relations, that quantizes the completed local coordinate ring

\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{M}_{B}^{(p)},\mathbf{1}}
, at the base point
\mathbf{1}=(s_{1}=1,...,s_{2p}=1,d_{1}=0)\in M_{B}^{(p)}
, equipped with the Poisson bracket (like the
RLL
realization of the quantum group for the
p=1
case). The quantum Stokes matrices then define
an algebra homomorphism

\nu_{\hbar}(u):\mathcal{A}_{\hbar}^{(p)}\longrightarrow\widehat{\mathfrak{U}}_{p,\hbar}~;~L_{i}\mapsto S_{i}(u),

whose semiclassical limit coincides with the pullback of the classical
RHB map (in the completed local ring
sense). Thus we have a diagram of quantizations and their semiclassical limits (s.c.l):

\begin{CD}\mathcal{A}_{\hbar}^{(p)}@>{\nu_{\hbar}(u)}>{}>\widehat{\mathfrak{U}}_{p,\hbar}\\
@V{}V{s.c.l}V@V{}V{s.c.l}V\\
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{M}_{B}^{(p)},\mathbf{1}}@>{\nu(u)^{*}}>{}>\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{M_{dR}^{(p)},0}.\end{CD}

Here
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{M_{dR}^{(p)},0}
is the completed local ring at the origin point of
M_{dR}^{(p)}
, see also Remark
2.1
. The

{\nu(u)}^{*}
is understood as the pullback of the Taylor expansion of the
analytic RHB map.

Since the semiclassical limit of the associative algebra homomorphism
\nu_{\hbar}(u)
automatically yields a Poisson map, it interprets the Poisson geometric nature of the classical RHB map given in Theorem
1.3
. This motivates viewing the map
\nu_{\hbar}(u)
, or equivalently the quantum Stokes matrices, as the quantization of the highly transcendental RHB maps. And our main theorem reveals the quantum algebraic nature of the quantum RHB map.

To prove Theorem
1.1
, in Section
3
we study the Stokes phenomenon of the linear system (
35
)-(
36
) of PDEs, a Knizhnik–Zamolodchikov (KZ) type equation with
p+1
-th order poles.
Let us mention that the KZ type equations with irregular singularities have been introduced from various perspectives. For example, the irregular KZ equation was first introduced in
[
17
]
, and was given a
representation-theoretic interpretation in
[
11
]
.
These resulting differential equations can have poles of arbitrary order and hence exhibit Stokes phenomenon. In the second order pole case, the Stokes phenomenon of the irregular KZ equation was first studied in
[
20
]
. However, to the best of the author’s knowledge, the Stokes phenomenon of the KZ type equations with higher order poles has not been studied yet. The present paper is an attempt in this direction.

Formal solutions, canonical holomorphic solutions and quantum Stokes matrices

In this section, we introduce the formal solutions, canonical holomorphic solutions and quantum Stokes matrices of the equation (

).

2.1
Coefficient algebras

In this subsection we fix the coefficient algebra and the completion in which
the quantum formal solutions and their resummation take values.

Let
\mathfrak{U}_{p,\hbar}^{\operatorname{loc}}(u)
be the associative

\mathbb{C}[\hbar,\hbar^{-1}]
-algebra generated by

e_{ij},\qquad e_{ij}^{(a)},\qquad 1\leq i,j\leq n,\quad 1\leq a\leq p-1,

subject to the relations for all
i,j,k,l=1,...,n
and
a,b=1,...,p-1

\displaystyle[e_{ij}^{(a)},e_{kl}^{(b)}]

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\delta_{jk}e_{il}^{(a+b)}-\delta_{li}e_{kj}^{(a+b)},&\text{ if }a+b\leq p-1\\
\frac{1}{\hbar}\delta_{jk}\delta_{il}(u_{i}-u_{j}),&\text{ if }a+b=p,\\
0,&\text{ if }a+b\geq p+1.\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle[e_{ij},e_{kl}]

\displaystyle=\delta_{jk}e_{il}-\delta_{li}e_{kj},\quad\text{and}\quad[e_{ij}^{(a)},e_{kl}]=0.

(15)

Then the associative algebra
\mathfrak{U}_{p,\hbar}
, defined in the introduction, can be seen as the

\mathbb{C}[\hbar]
-subalgebra of
\mathfrak{U}_{p,\hbar}^{\operatorname{loc}}(u)
generated by all

\hbar e_{ij}
and
\hbar e_{ij}^{(a)}
. We remark that all
coefficients of the differential equations lie in the subalgebra
\mathfrak{U}_{p,\hbar}
. The localized algebra is used only as an ambient algebra for notation.

Let

\mathfrak{m}=\bigl(\hbar,\hbar e_{ij},\ \hbar e_{ij}^{(a)}\mid 1\leq i,j\leq n,\ 1\leq a\leq p-1\bigr)\subset\mathfrak{U}_{p,\hbar}

be the two-sided ideal generated by
\hbar
and by all coefficient
generators.
There is an augmentation homomorphism (well-defined because every defining relation maps to zero)

\epsilon:\mathfrak{U}_{p,\hbar}\to\mathbb{C},\qquad\epsilon(\hbar)=\epsilon(\hbar e_{ij})=\epsilon(\hbar e_{ij}^{(a)})=0,

so that
\mathfrak{m}\subset\ker\epsilon
. In particular,

\mathfrak{m}
is a proper ideal and
\mathfrak{U}_{p,\hbar}/\mathfrak{m}\simeq\mathbb{C}
.

We define the coefficient algebra used for holomorphic solutions to be the

\mathfrak{m}
-adic completion

\widehat{\mathfrak{U}}_{p,\hbar}=\varprojlim_{N}\mathfrak{U}_{p,\hbar}/\mathfrak{m}^{N}.

For each
N
, the quotient
\mathfrak{U}_{p,\hbar}/\mathfrak{m}^{N}
is finite dimensional.
Indeed, since
\mathfrak{U}_{p,\hbar}
is generated by the finite set

\{\hbar,\hbar e_{ij},\hbar e_{ij}^{(a)}\}
, every element of
\mathfrak{U}_{p,\hbar}
is a linear
combination of words in these generators. Every word of length at least

N
belongs to
\mathfrak{m}^{N}
. Hence
\mathfrak{U}_{p,\hbar}/\mathfrak{m}^{N}
is spanned by
the images of the finitely many words of length less than
N
.

Let
\mathbb{D}\subset\mathbb{C}
be a domain. A function

f:\mathbb{D}\longrightarrow{\widehat{\mathfrak{U}}_{p,\hbar}}

is called holomorphic if for every
N\geq 1
, the induced finite truncation

f_{N}:\mathbb{D}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{U}_{p,\hbar}/\mathfrak{m}^{N}

is holomorphic in the usual sense. In other words, the sheaf of

\widehat{\mathfrak{U}}_{p,\hbar}
-valued holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{D}
is
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{D},\widehat{\mathfrak{U}}_{p,\hbar}):=\varprojlim_{N\geq 1}\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{D},\mathfrak{U}_{p,\hbar}/\mathfrak{m}^{N}).

The same convention is used in the paper for functions with values in

\widehat{\mathfrak{U}}_{p,\hbar}\otimes\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{C}^{n}),\qquad\widehat{\mathfrak{U}}_{p,\hbar}\otimes\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\otimes\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{C}^{n}).

Remark 2.1
.

To relate to the Poisson geometric setting, we take the filtration by the degree in the generators

\hbar e_{ij}
and
\hbar e_{ij}^{(a)}
, assigning degree zero to

\hbar
. Since defining relations are PBW type relations, the
associated graded algebra is the commutative polynomial algebra over

\mathbb{C}[\hbar]
generated by the images of
\hbar e_{ij}
and
\hbar e_{ij}^{(a)}
.
Consequently, after setting
\hbar=0
, no further relations remain. Thus, we have that the quotient modulo

\hbar
is

\widehat{\mathfrak{U}}_{p,\hbar}/\overline{\hbar\mathfrak{U}_{p,\hbar}}\cong\mathbb{C}\llbracket\overline{\hbar e_{ij}},\overline{\hbar e_{ij}^{(a)}}\rrbracket\cong\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{M_{dR}^{(p)},0},

where the completion on the right is taken at the origin of the affine space

M_{dR}^{(p)}\cong\mathfrak{gl}_{n}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathfrak{gl}_{n},

and
\overline{\hbar e_{ij}}
,
\overline{\hbar e_{ij}^{(a)}}
are formal local coordinates.
The commutator is divisible by
\hbar
, and therefore it induces the
Poisson bracket

\{\bar{x},\bar{y}\}=\overline{\hbar^{-1}[x,y]}

on

\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{M_{dR}^{(p)},0}
in the usual way.
Thus
\widehat{\mathfrak{U}}_{p,\hbar}
is a formal local quantization of
the completed local ring
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{M_{dR}^{(p)},0}

equipped with the Poisson bracket.

Formal solutions

Theorem 2.2
.

For any fixed

, there exists a unique formal power series

\widehat{H}(z)=1+H_{1}z+H_{2}z^{2}+\cdot\cdot\cdot\in\mathfrak{U}_{p,\hbar}\otimes{\rm End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\llbracket z\rrbracket

with commutative diagonal elements

[(D_{l})_{ii},(D_{s})_{jj}]=0\in\mathfrak{U}_{p,\hbar},\text{ for }l,s=1,...,p,i,j=1,...,n,

(16)

such that

\displaystyle\widehat{F}(z)=\widehat{H}(z)\cdot e^{\int D(z)}z^{D_{1}}

(17)

is a formal solution of (
5
). Here the element

D(z):=\frac{u}{z^{p+1}}+\frac{D_{p}}{z^{p}}+\frac{D_{p-1}}{z^{p-1}}+\cdots+\frac{D_{2}}{z^{2}}\in\mathfrak{U}_{p,\hbar}\otimes{\rm End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})[z^{-1}]

Proof.

First, let us prove that there exists an off-diagonal matrix formal power series

\widehat{H}^{o}(z)=1+\sum_{i\geq 1}H^{o}_{i}z^{i}\in\mathfrak{U}_{p,\hbar}\otimes{\rm End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\llbracket z\rrbracket,

(here off-diagonal means that if we write

, then

for
i=1,....n
) that diagonalizes

under the gauge transformation

\widehat{H}^{o}(z)[T(z)]=\widehat{H}^{o}(z)T(z)\widehat{H}^{o}(z)^{-1}+d\widehat{H}^{o}(z)\cdot\widehat{H}^{o}(z)^{-1}.

That is there exist diagonal matrices
D_{k}\in\mathfrak{U}_{p,\hbar}\otimes{\rm End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
for
k\leq p
such that

\widehat{H}^{o}(z)[T(z)]=\frac{u}{z^{p+1}}+\frac{D_{p}}{z^{p}}+\cdots+\frac{D_{1}}{z}+D_{0}+D_{-1}z^{1}+\cdots.

(19)

Plugging
\widehat{H}^{o}(z)=1+\sum_{k\geq 1}H^{o}_{k}z^{k}
into the above equation gives (here
H^{o}_{0}=1
and by convention
H^{o}_{m}=0

T_{[m+2]}=0

\displaystyle[u,H^{o}_{m+1}]=(m-p+1)H^{o}_{m-p+1}+\sum_{l=p-m}^{p}H^{o}_{m-p+l}D_{l}-\sum_{l=p-m}^{p}\hbar T_{[l]}H^{o}_{m-p+l}.

(20)

On the one hand,
since
H^{o}_{m+1}
is off-diagonal and
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}
are distinct, it is uniquely determined by the recursion relations. On the other hand, the left hand side is off-diagonal, which forces the diagonal matrices
D_{l}
to satisfy

\displaystyle D_{p-m}

\displaystyle=\text{diagonal part of }\sum_{l=2}^{p}\hbar T_{[l]}H^{o}_{m+l-p}-\hbar TH^{o}_{m+1-p}-\sum_{l=p-m+1}^{p}H^{o}_{m+l-p}D_{l}.

(21)

In this way, the above recurrence relations uniquely determine
D_{m}
for all
m\leq p
and the off-diagonal matrices
H^{o}_{m}
for
m\geq 1
.

Second, by using Lemma
2.4
and by applying the above recurrence relations repeatedly, we obtain for
i,j,k=1,...,n
,

[(D_{1})_{kk},(H^{o}_{m})_{ij}]=\delta_{ki}(H^{o}_{m})_{ij}-\delta_{jk}(H^{o}_{m})_{ij},

(22)

and then

[(D_{1})_{kk},(D_{m})_{ii}]=0\text{ for }m=p,...,1,0,-1,....

(23)

To complete the proof, let
f(z)=1+\sum_{k=1}f_{k}z^{k}\in\mathfrak{U}_{p,\hbar}\otimes{\rm End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\llbracket z\rrbracket
be a diagonal matrix formal power series with the coefficients
f_{k}
determined by (
f_{0}:=1)

(k+1)f_{k+1}=-\sum_{i=0}^{k}(f_{i}\cdot D_{i-k}).

(24)

One checks by induction that
[f_{k},D_{1}]=0
for all
k
, thus we have

(k+1)f_{k+1}=[D_{1},f_{k+1}]-\sum_{i=0}^{k}(f_{i}\cdot D_{i-k}),

(25)

i.e.,
f(z)
is a diagonal matrix formal power series solution of the equation

f^{\prime}(z)=\frac{D_{1}}{z}\cdot f(z)-f(z)\cdot\left(\frac{D_{1}}{z}+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}D_{-k}z^{k}\right).

(26)

In the end, the gauge transform of
T(z)
under the formal power series
\widehat{H}(z):=f(z)\cdot\widehat{H}^{o}(z)
is

\widehat{H}(z)[T(z)]=D(z)+\frac{D_{1}}{z},

and

\widehat{F}(z):=\widehat{H}(z)\cdot e^{\int D(z)}z^{D_{1}}

is the formal solution as required in Theorem
2.2
.

Remark 2.3
.

From the proof we see that the coefficients of the regular part of the formal solution are finite algebraic
expressions, i.e.,

H_{k}\in\mathcal{U}_{p,\hbar}(u)\otimes\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{C}^{n}).

Thus

\widehat{H}(z)=1+\sum_{k\geq 1}H_{k}z^{k}\in\left(\mathcal{U}_{p,\hbar}(u)\otimes\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\right)\llbracket z\rrbracket.

The completion of the coefficient algebra is needed only when one passes from
the formal series
\widehat{H}
to its Borel-Laplace resummation.
The full formal solution is written in the form

\widehat{F}(z)=\widehat{H}(z)\cdot e^{\int D(z)dz}z^{D_{1}},

where the second factor is regarded as the prescribed diagonal singular factor. It is not an element in
\mathfrak{U}_{p,\hbar}\otimes\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\llbracket z\rrbracket
, but after resummation it becomes an
\widehat{\mathfrak{U}}_{p,\hbar}\otimes\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
valued holomorphic function.

2.3
Quantum Stokes matrices

Note that the
leading exponential term in the formal irregular type
D(z)
of the equation (
5
) is
D_{p}=\frac{-u}{pz^{p}}
.

Definition 2.6
.

The
anti-Stokes rays
of the equation (
5
) are the directions along which
{\rm exp}(\frac{u_{i}-u_{j}}{pz^{p}})
decays most rapidly as
z\rightarrow 0
for some
i\neq j
, i.e., the directions along which
\frac{u_{i}-u_{j}}{pz^{p}}
is real and negative. Denote by

\displaystyle{\rm aSR}(u)

\displaystyle:=\left\{\frac{1}{p}\mathrm{Arg}(u_{j}-u_{i})+\frac{2k\pi}{p}~|~\text{ for all }k\in\mathbb{Z},\text{ and }i\neq j\right\}

the set of anti-Stokes rays/directions.

In this paper we denote a direction/ray by its argument. Let us choose an initial anti-Stokes direction
\tau_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and then
arrange the anti-Stokes directions into a strictly monotonically increasing sequence

\displaystyle\cdots<\tau_{-1}<\tau_{0}<\tau_{1}<\cdots.

Definition 2.7
.

For any admissible direction
d\in(\tau_{j},\tau_{j+1})
, the
Stokes sector

{\rm Sect}_{d}
is defined as

\displaystyle{\rm Sect}_{d}:=\left\{z\in\bar{\mathbb{C}}:\mathrm{arg}(z)\in\bigg(\tau_{j}-\frac{\pi}{2p},\tau_{j+1}+\frac{\pi}{2p}\bigg)\right\}.

Proposition 2.8
.

Given any fixed
u\in\mathfrak{h}_{\rm reg}
and an admissible direction
d
, there exists a unique
\widehat{\mathfrak{U}}_{p,\hbar}\otimes{\rm End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
valued holomorphic solution
{F}_{d}(z)

on
\tilde{\mathbb{C}}
of the equation (
5
) with the asymptotics

{F}_{d}(z)\cdot e^{-\int D(z)}z^{-D_{1}}\sim 1,\ \text{ as }z\rightarrow 0\text{ within }{{\rm Sect}}_{d}.

(31)

Since
{F}_{d}(z)
is the unique solution with the prescribed asymptotics (
31
), it is called a
canonical solution
at
z=0
.

The Borel-Laplace resummation is understood as follows.
For every
N
reducing the equation modulo
\mathfrak{m}^{N}
gives a
meromorphic linear system with coefficients in the finite dimensional algebra

\mathfrak{U}_{p,\hbar}/\mathfrak{m}^{N}\otimes\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
.
Let

\widehat{F}_{N}(z)=\widehat{H}_{N}(z)\cdot e^{\int D_{N}(z)dz}z^{D_{1,N}}

be the formal solution of the finite dimensional system over
\mathfrak{U}_{p,\hbar}/\mathfrak{m}^{N}
.
Note that the coefficient matrices of these reduced systems are compatible with the
natural projections

\pi_{N}^{N+1}:\mathfrak{U}_{p,\hbar}/\mathfrak{m}^{N+1}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{U}_{p,\hbar}/\mathfrak{m}^{N}.

Thus, applying

\pi_{N}^{N+1}
termwise to the formal solution over

\mathfrak{U}_{p,\hbar}/\mathfrak{m}^{N+1}
gives a normalized formal solution over
\mathfrak{U}_{p,\hbar}/\mathfrak{m}^{N}
, that is

\pi_{N}^{N+1}(\widehat{H}_{N+1}(z))=\widehat{H}_{N}(z),\qquad\pi_{N}^{N+1}(D_{r,N+1})=D_{r,N}.

The standard Borel-Laplace resummation theorem applies in each finite
truncation. Thus, for every admissible direction
d
, the Borel-Laplace sum of
\widehat{H}_{N}(z)
gives a holomorphic function

H_{d,N}(z)\in(\mathfrak{U}_{p,\hbar}/\mathfrak{m}^{N})\otimes\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{C}^{n}).

Since the Borel-Laplace sum is unique in each finite dimensional
truncation, the compatibility of the formal series implies the compatibility
of the holomorphic functions:

\pi_{N}^{N+1}(H_{d,N+1})=H_{d,N}.

Therefore the inverse limit defines a holomorphic function

H_{d}(z)\in\widehat{\mathfrak{U}}_{p,\hbar}\otimes\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{C}^{n}).

Note that the
diagonal singular factor
e^{\int D(z)dz}z^{D_{1}}

is also a holomorphic

\widehat{\mathfrak{U}}_{p,\hbar}\otimes\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
-valued function (on the covering of
\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
). Hence we can define the holomorphic solution

F_{d}(z):=H_{d}(z)\cdot e^{\int D(z)}z^{D_{1}}\in\widehat{\mathfrak{U}}_{p,\hbar}\otimes\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{C}^{n}).

Holomorphic functions below, as well as the quantum Stokes matrices, are understood in this completed

\mathfrak{m}
-adic sense.

Definition 2.9
.

(1)

For any fixed

where

\displaystyle F_{d^{\prime}}(z,t)=F_{d}(z,t)\cdot S_{i}(u),

(33)

We remark that the Stokes matrices depend on the chosen initial anti-Stokes direction
\tau_{0}

3
Stokes phenomenon of a system of partial differential equations with two variables: proof of Theorem
1.1

The proof of Theorem
1.1
relies on the study of the Stokes phenomenon of a compatible system of partial differential equations (
35
)-(
36
) with two variables. The proof is organized in three steps. First, we construct the formal solution

\widehat{Y}
of the two variable system and show that the coefficients
of its formal power series part have poles only at
t=0,1,\infty
. Second,
near each of these three points we factor
\widehat{Y}
into a first factor
which is holomorphic in the corresponding local coordinate and a second factor
which carries the local singular behavior. Third, after Borel-Laplace
resummation, the connection matrices of the holomorphic solutions
Y_{d,\mathcal{D}}
, depending on the certain domains
\mathcal{D}
of
t
-plane, are computed from
the Stokes matrices of these second factors. The connection formula among the solutions, assigned to some particular choices of domains
\mathcal{D}
, then provides a proof of our main theorem.

3.1
A compatible system of partial differential equations with two variables

Consider a system of equations, for a
\widehat{\mathfrak{U}}_{p,\hbar}\otimes{\rm End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\otimes{\rm End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
valued function

of two complex variables,

\displaystyle\frac{{\partial Y}}{\partial z_{1}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{u^{(1)}}{z_{1}^{p+1}}+\frac{\hbar T_{[p]}^{(1)}}{z_{1}^{p}}+\cdots+\frac{\hbar T_{[2]}^{(1)}}{z_{1}^{2}}-\frac{\hbar T^{(1)}}{z_{1}}+\frac{\hbar P}{z_{1}}+\frac{\hbar P}{z_{2}-z_{1}}\right)\cdot Y,

(35)

\displaystyle\frac{{\partial Y}}{\partial z_{2}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{u^{(2)}}{z_{2}^{p+1}}+\frac{\hbar T_{[p]}^{(2)}}{z_{2}^{p}}+\cdots+\frac{\hbar T_{[2]}^{(2)}}{z_{2}^{2}}-\frac{\hbar T^{(2)}}{z_{2}}+\frac{\hbar P}{z_{2}}+\frac{\hbar P}{z_{1}-z_{2}}\right)\cdot Y,

(36)

Here

, and

\displaystyle u^{(1)}=\sum_{i}1\otimes u_{i}E_{ii}\otimes 1,\ u^{(2)}=\sum_{i}1\otimes 1\otimes u_{i}E_{ii},

\displaystyle\hbar T^{(1)}=\sum_{k,l}\hbar e_{kl}\otimes E_{kl}\otimes 1,\ \hbar T^{(2)}=\sum_{k,l}\hbar e_{kl}\otimes 1\otimes E_{kl},

\displaystyle\hbar T_{[j]}^{(1)}=\sum_{k,l}\hbar e_{kl}^{(j-1)}\otimes E_{kl}\otimes 1,\ \hbar T_{[j]}^{(2)}=\sum_{k,l}\hbar e_{kl}^{(j-1)}\otimes 1\otimes E_{kl},\ \hbar P=\hbar\sum_{k,l}1\otimes E_{kl}\otimes E_{lk},

\mathfrak{U}_{p,\hbar}\otimes{\rm End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\otimes{\rm End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})

To show the system is compatible, we need the following lemma, whose proof follows by direct computation.

.

We have the identities

\displaystyle[T_{[m]}^{(1)}+T_{[m]}^{(2)},P]

Lemma 3.16
.

The transition matrix
X_{d-\frac{\pi}{p}}(\omega)^{-1}\cdot X_{d}(\omega)
, associated to the admissible direction

, of the equation (
84
) is equal to

Proof.

Under the coordinate change
\eta=\omega^{p}=(t^{-p}-1)^{-1}z^{p}
, the equation (
84
) becomes

\displaystyle\frac{{dX}}{d\eta}=\left(\frac{u^{(2)}}{p\eta^{2}}+\frac{\hbar P}{\eta}\right)X.

(122)

The transition matrix
X_{d-\frac{\pi}{p}}(\omega)^{-1}\cdot X_{d}(\omega)
is then the Stokes matrix
X_{pd-\pi}(\eta)^{-1}X_{pd}(\eta)
of (
122
).
The Stokes matrices of the equation (
122
) were computed in
[
23
]
, see also
[
15
]
. We include a brief proof here for the convenience of the reader.

The coefficient of the equation (
122
) is in
\mathfrak{U}_{p,\hbar}\otimes{\rm End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\otimes{\rm End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
. Hence the equation may be viewed as an
n^{2}\times n^{2}
block linear system. The equation is then decomposed to multiple
2\times 2
and
1\times 1
block systems: let
\{v_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq n}
be the standard basis of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, and
\{v_{i}\otimes v_{j}\}
the basis of
\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n}
, we have for any
v\in V

\displaystyle P(v\otimes v_{i}\otimes v_{j})

\displaystyle=v\otimes v_{j}\otimes v_{i},

(123)

\displaystyle u^{(2)}(v\otimes v_{i}\otimes v_{j})

\displaystyle=v\otimes v_{i}\otimes u_{j}v_{j},\ \text{for }i,j=1,...,n

(124)

Thus, for fixed index
i\neq j
, the equation (
122
) can be restricted on the subspace spanned by the two vectors
v_{i}\otimes v_{j}
and
v_{j}\otimes v_{i}
, reducing to a
2\times 2
block system of the form

\frac{d\mathcal{M}}{d\eta}=\left({\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}u_{j}/p&0\\
0&u_{i}/p\end{array}\right)}\frac{1}{\eta^{2}}+{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\hbar\\
\hbar&0\end{array}\right)}\frac{1}{\eta}\right)\mathcal{M}.

(125)

This equation can be solved exactly and (under the assumption that we made on
u
and the choice of
\tau_{il}<d<\tau_{il+1}
with
i

{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&e^{{\pi\mathrm{i}\hbar}}-e^{-{\pi\mathrm{i}\hbar}}\\

Remark 3.17
.

The Stokes matrices have ones along the diagonal. Sometimes it is more convenient to use the normalized Stokes matrices
\mathcal{S}_{i}(u)=e^{\hbar\pi\mathrm{i}D_{1}}S_{i}(u)
by adding the formal monodromy part.
Note that we have the commutativity conditions

\big[S_{i}^{(2)}(u),\;D_{1}^{(1)}+\hbar\delta P\big]=0,\qquad\big[S_{i}^{(1)}(u),\;D_{1}^{(2)}+\hbar\delta P\big]=0,\qquad\big[R_{0},\;D_{1}^{(1)}+D_{1}^{(2)}\big]=0.

(126)

For example, the first identity follows from the fact that

{D^{(1)}_{1}+\hbar\delta P}
commutes with the coefficient matrix of the equation (
51
).
Multiply
e^{\pi\mathrm{i}(D_{1}^{(1)}+D_{1}^{(2)}+\hbar\delta P)}
to both sides of (
121
), and applying the commutativity conditions (
126
) gives the identity

R^{12}\mathcal{S}^{(1)}_{i}(u)\mathcal{S}^{(2)}_{i}(u)=\mathcal{S}^{(2)}_{i}(u)\mathcal{S}^{(1)}_{i}(u)R^{12}.

In the special case
p=1
, the above identity gives the FRT realization of the quantum group
U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
