The top Yau–Yang conjecture for Kähler manifolds with positive sectional curvature
Ved V. Datar, Vamsi Pritham Pingali, Harish Seshadri

Abstract
. We prove that the top wedge power of the Ricci form of a complete non-compact Kähler manifold with positive sectional curvature has finite integral. Using a result of Chen-Zhu, an immediate consequence is the quasiprojectivity of such manifolds under the assumption of bounded sectional curvature. A key new idea to prove Bézout estimates along with a Lipschitz weight with finite Monge-Ampère mass is used in the proof of the main result.

Primary 53C55; Secondary 32Q15, 32U40, 32W20

1.
Introduction

If
(M,g)
is a complete Riemannian
2
-manifold with Gaussian curvature
K>0
, the classical Cohn–Vossen theorem
[
2
]
implies that
\int_{M}KdV<\infty
. In higher dimensions, a Cohn–Vossen type problem, posed by Yau
[
17
]
and subsequently formulated in the Kähler setting by Yang
[
16
]
, asks if the following statement is true: Let
(X^{n},\omega)
be a complete Kähler manifold with nonnegative holomorphic bisectional curvature. If
\operatorname{Ric}_{\omega}
denotes the Ricci form and
r
denotes the distance from a fixed point
o\in X
, then the quantities

r^{2k-2n}\int_{B(o,r)}\operatorname{Ric}_{\omega}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k},\qquad 1\leq k\leq n,

are bounded independently of
r
. When the sectional curvatures are nonnegative, the case
k=1
follows from a more general result of Petrunin
[
14
]
. The case
k=n
, equivalent to global finiteness of the top Ricci mass

\int_{X}\operatorname{Ric}_{\omega}^{n},

is of particular interest, as it plays a role in Yau’s uniformisation conjecture
[
3
,
5
,
6
]
and in compactification results for non-compact Kähler manifolds
[
11
]
. Liu
[
7
]
proved the finiteness of
\int_{X}\operatorname{Ric}_{\omega}^{n}
when the volume growth is maximal.

In
[
5
]
, we proved the surface case of this finiteness statement under positive sectional curvature (without any volume growth hypotheses). The key result on which our proof depended is actually valid in all dimensions: if
(X^{n},\omega)
,
n\geq 1
, has positive holomorphic bisectional curvature and
X
admits a smooth strictly plurisubharmonic exhaustion function with bounded gradient, then there exists a uniformly Lipschitz plurisubharmonic function
\varphi
such that

(1.1)

\displaystyle 0<\displaystyle\int_{X}(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi)^{n}<\infty.

The hypotheses needed for this construction, together with the heat-flow and cut-off inputs are satisfied in the positive sectional curvature case by the results used in
[
5
,
6
]
and by Greene–Wu
[
8
]
. The purpose of this work is to show that the top-degree Yau–Yang estimate holds in every dimension:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(X^{n},\omega)
be a complete non-compact Kähler manifold with positive sectional curvature. Then

\int_{X}\operatorname{Ric}_{\omega}^{n}<\infty.

More generally, the same conclusion holds if
(X,\omega)
has positive holomorphic bisectional curvature and
X
admits a smooth strictly plurisubharmonic exhaustion function with uniformly bounded gradient.

By a theorem of Chen–Zhu
[
3
]
, a complete non-compact Kähler manifold with positive and bounded sectional curvature and finite top Ricci mass is biholomorphic to a quasiprojective variety. Thus Theorem
1.1
immediately gives the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(X^{n},\omega)
be a complete non-compact Kähler manifold with positive and bounded sectional curvature. Then
X
is biholomorphic to a quasiprojective variety.

In fact, in
[
6
]
we established Theorem
1.2
without the boundedness assumption on sectional curvature. Note that in this dimension, it follows by a result of Ramanujam
[
15
]
that
X
is actually biholomorphic to
\mathbb{C}^{2}
. In work in progress, we shall use the techniques in this paper to remove the boundedness assumption in Theorem
1.2
. As remarked in
[
6
]
, a Ramanujam-type result for higher
n
under curvature hypotheses is a challenging open problem.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
takes inspiration (just as in
[
5
]
) from
[
4
]
. Just as in
[
5
]
, the weight
\varphi
satisfying (
1.1
) is related to the Ricci form using a Poincaré-Lelong type formula. Such a relationship is delicate because of the non-smoothness of the weight and we use Bedford-Taylor theory in an essential way. The main innovation arises in the Bézout-type estimates for Poincaré-Lelong integrals. In
[
5
]
, a delicate integration-by-parts argument involving a Bochner identity was used. We bypass that argument in this paper whilst noting that it may still be useful as an independent argument. Instead, as we explain in Section
3
, we introduce a linear growth function
H
, and an induction argument to prove the desired estimates. This argument was produced after discussion with ChatGPT 5.5 Pro. To our knowledge, this is perhaps the first instance of AI coming up with a truly original idea in geometric analysis. After probing further, we realised that it may have been motivated by the ODE-type methods used in Ohsawa-Takegoshi-type theorems. That being said, our experiments (by asking it to prove the results of
[
5
]
without using literature after 2024) showed that it is still unable to come up with the road-map of the proof of
[
5
]
on its own.

2.
Preliminaries and background

In this section we summarise some important results used in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

In
[
5
]
we proved the following important result by solving a Monge-Ampère equation.

Proposition 2.1

(Datar–Pingali–Seshadri)
.

Let
(X^{n},\omega)
be either a complete non-compact Kähler manifold with positive sectional curvature, or a complete non-compact Kähler manifold with
\operatorname{BK}_{\omega}>0
admitting a smooth strictly plurisubharmonic exhaustion function with uniformly bounded gradient. Then there exists a uniformly Lipschitz strictly plurisubharmonic function
\varphi
on
X
such that, with

\alpha:=\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi,

one has

0<A_{0}:=\int_{X}\alpha^{n}<\infty.

This weight was used in
[
5
,
6
]
to produce weighted-
L^{2}
holomorphic sections of
K_{X}
. The non-smoothness of this weight is problematic and was addressed in
[
5
,
6
]
by heat-flow regularization which we summarize below.

Under either curvature hypothesis in Theorem
1.1
,
\operatorname{Ric}_{\omega}\geq 0
; hence there exists a unique, positive, symmetric and stochastically complete heat kernel
H(x,y,t)
. If
\varphi
is a Lipschitz function on
X
, we let

u(x,t)=\int_{X}H(x,y,t)\varphi(y)\,dy.

Then
u(x,t)
is a solution to the heat equation with initial condition
u(x,0)=\varphi(x)
. The heat-flow regularization used in
[
5
,
12
,
13
]
gives that
u_{t}(\cdot):=u(\cdot,t)
is strictly psh for each
t>0
, and there exists a constant
A>0
such that

\displaystyle|\nabla u_{t}|,~t|\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}u_{t}|\leq A.

Note however that the smooth weights
u_{t}
may no longer have finite Monge-Ampère mass. To switch back and forth between the smooth and non-smooth weights, we need the following crucial estimate
[
5
]
(which was incidentally proven by ChatGPT 5.0 in the thinking mode, and was inspired from the techniques in
[
9
]
).

Lemma 2.2
.

There exists a dimensional constant
c(n)
such that for any
0\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}
,

u_{t_{2}}(x)\leq u_{t_{1}}(x)+Ac\sqrt{t_{2}-t_{1}}

for all
x\in X
, where
A
is the Lipschitz constant for
\varphi
.

To compare the Ricci form with
\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi
, we need
L^{2}
-holomorphic sections. To this end we need the following standard result
[
5
]
.

Proposition 2.3

(
L^{2}
canonical section)
.

Let
u_{t}
,
0\leq t\leq 1
, be the heat-flow smoothing of
\varphi
, with
u_{0}=\varphi
. There exist
q\gg 1
, a non-zero holomorphic section

s\in H^{0}(X,K_{X}),

and a constant
C
such that

\int_{X}\|s\|^{2}_{qu_{t}}\,\omega^{n}\leq C,\qquad 0\leq t\leq 1.

Here

\|s\|^{2}_{qu}:=|s|_{\omega}^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-qu}.

Lastly, we will use the following construction (cf.
[
5
, Lemma 8]
) of cutoff functions to integrate-by-parts.

Lemma 2.4
.

Assume
(X,\omega)
satisfies either curvature hypothesis in Theorem
1.1
. Fix
o\in X
. Then there exist
0<\theta<1
,
A>0
and
a_{0}>0
such that the following holds: for all
a>a_{0}
there exist a smooth function
\chi_{a}:X\rightarrow[0,1]
having the following properties:

(1)

\chi_{a}\equiv 1
on
B(o,\theta a)
and
\mathrm{Supp}(\chi_{a})\subset B(o,\theta^{-1}a).

(2)

There exists a constant
A
such that

|\nabla\chi_{a}|,|\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\chi_{a}|\leq\frac{A}{a}.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

If
n=1
, the assertion is Cohn–Vossen’s theorem, since the Ricci form is a constant multiple of the Gaussian curvature form. We therefore assume
n\geq 2
.
By Proposition
2.1
, there exists a uniformly Lipschitz strictly plurisubharmonic function
\varphi
with

0<\int_{X}\alpha^{n}<\infty,\qquad\alpha=\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi.

Choose
q
and
s\in H^{0}(X,K_{X})\setminus\{0\}
as in Proposition
2.3
.

For
\varepsilon>0
set

f_{\varepsilon}:=\frac{\|s\|_{q\varphi}^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}},\qquad V_{\varepsilon}:=\log(1+f_{\varepsilon}),\qquad\zeta_{\varepsilon}(s):=\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}V_{\varepsilon}+q\alpha.

For
0<t\leq 1
we also set

f_{\varepsilon,t}:=\frac{\|s\|_{qu_{t}}^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}},\qquad V_{\varepsilon,t}:=\log(1+f_{\varepsilon,t}),\qquad\alpha_{t}:=\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}u_{t},

\zeta_{\varepsilon,t}(s):=\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}V_{\varepsilon,t}+q\alpha_{t}.

On compact subsets,
u_{t}\to\varphi
,
f_{\varepsilon,t}\to f_{\varepsilon}
, and

V_{\varepsilon,t}+qu_{t}\to V_{\varepsilon}+q\varphi
locally uniformly. Hence, for every compactly supported smooth function
\eta
, Bedford–Taylor continuity gives

(3.1)

\int_{X}\eta\,f_{\varepsilon,t}\zeta_{\varepsilon,t}(s)^{k}\alpha_{t}^{m}\omega^{l}\longrightarrow\int_{X}\eta\,f_{\varepsilon}\zeta_{\varepsilon}(s)^{k}\alpha^{m}\omega^{l}

whenever
k+m+l=n
; the same convergence holds without the factor
f_{\varepsilon,t}
. We use this regularisation below for integrations by parts involving
V_{\varepsilon}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

\zeta_{\varepsilon}(s)
is a closed, positive
(1,1)
current satisfying

(3.2)

\zeta_{\varepsilon}(s)\geq\frac{\|s\|_{q\varphi}^{2}}{\|s\|_{q\varphi}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}\,\operatorname{Ric}_{\omega}.

Moreover,

(3.3)

\int_{X}\operatorname{Ric}_{\omega}^{n}\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{X}\zeta_{\varepsilon}(s)^{n},

where the wedge product on the right is interpreted in the Bedford–Taylor sense.

Proof.

For
t>0
, the forms are smooth. Away from the zero set of
s
,

\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\log f_{\varepsilon,t}=\operatorname{Ric}_{\omega}-q\alpha_{t},

so

\zeta_{\varepsilon,t}(s)=\frac{f_{\varepsilon,t}}{1+f_{\varepsilon,t}}\operatorname{Ric}_{\omega}+\frac{q}{1+f_{\varepsilon,t}}\alpha_{t}+\frac{\sqrt{-1}\,\partial f_{\varepsilon,t}\wedge\bar{\partial}f_{\varepsilon,t}}{f_{\varepsilon,t}(1+f_{\varepsilon,t})^{2}}\geq\frac{f_{\varepsilon,t}}{1+f_{\varepsilon,t}}\operatorname{Ric}_{\omega}.

The quotient term extends smoothly across
Z(s)
: locally
f_{\varepsilon,t}=\rho|g|^{2}
with
\rho>0
smooth and
g
holomorphic. Passing
t\to 0
gives (
3.2
).

Let
\eta\geq 0
be compactly supported and smooth. By pointwise algebraic monotonicity for smooth semipositive forms,

\int_{X}\eta\,\zeta_{\varepsilon,t}(s)^{n}\geq\int_{X}\eta\left(\frac{f_{\varepsilon,t}}{1+f_{\varepsilon,t}}\right)^{n}\operatorname{Ric}_{\omega}^{n}.

Letting
t\to 0
and using (
3.1
) gives

\int_{X}\eta\,\zeta_{\varepsilon}(s)^{n}\geq\int_{X}\eta\left(\frac{f_{\varepsilon}}{1+f_{\varepsilon}}\right)^{n}\operatorname{Ric}_{\omega}^{n}.

As
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
, the coefficient on the right increases to
1
off the divisor
Z(s)
, which has zero Riemannian measure. Fatou’s lemma therefore gives

\int_{X}\eta\,\operatorname{Ric}_{\omega}^{n}\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{X}\eta\,\zeta_{\varepsilon}(s)^{n}\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{X}\zeta_{\varepsilon}(s)^{n}.

Taking
\eta=\chi_{a}^{4n}
and letting
a\to\infty
proves (
3.3
).
∎

We next estimate the right-hand side of (
3.3
). By Fatou’s lemma and the cutoffs,

\displaystyle\int_{X}\zeta_{\varepsilon}(s)^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{a\to\infty}\int_{X}\chi_{a}^{4n}\zeta_{\varepsilon}(s)^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{a\to\infty}\int_{X}\left(V_{\varepsilon}\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}(\chi_{a}^{4n})+q\alpha\chi_{a}^{4n}\right)\zeta_{\varepsilon}(s)^{n-1}

(3.4)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{a\to\infty}\int_{X}\left(\frac{C}{a}V_{\varepsilon}\omega\chi_{a}^{4n-2}+q\alpha\chi_{a}^{4n}\right)\zeta_{\varepsilon}(s)^{n-1}.

Here the integration by parts is first done for the smooth
t>0
regularizations and then
t\to 0
is taken; also

|\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}(\chi_{a}^{N})|\leq Ca^{-1}\chi_{a}^{N-2}\omega
for the exponents used below.

One can now hope that inductively the only surviving term is the Monge-Ampère mass of
\alpha
and that every other term goes to
0
for fixed
\varepsilon
. The problem with such an expectation is seen right away for the term

\displaystyle\int_{X}\chi_{a}^{4n-2}V_{\varepsilon}\omega\zeta_{\varepsilon}(s)^{n-1}=\int_{X}V_{\varepsilon}\chi_{a}^{4n-2}(\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}V_{\varepsilon}+q\alpha)\omega\zeta_{\varepsilon}(s)^{n-2}.

Pretending
V_{\varepsilon}
is smooth, if we integrate-by-parts twice, it is not helpful because we end up with two derivatives of
V_{\varepsilon}
. If we transfer only one derivative, we end up with a term involving
V_{\varepsilon}\bar{\partial}V_{\varepsilon}
. In
[
5
]
, using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and a delicate
W^{1,2}
-type estimate on
s
, we could bound such a term. However, when
n>2
, another iteration of this argument will run into difficulties involving higher-order derivatives of
s
. Here, motivated by Ohsawa-Takegoshi type arguments, ChatGPT 5.5 Pro produced a function
H
satisfying good properties that allow an induction argument. Indeed, define

(3.5)

H(r):=r-\log(1+r),\qquad r\geq 0.

For every smooth function
h\geq 0
,

(3.6)

0\leq H(h)\leq h,\qquad h\,\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\log(1+h)\leq\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}H(h),

indeed the difference in the second inequality is

\sqrt{-1}\,\partial h\wedge\bar{\partial}h/(1+h)
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
. If
1\leq l\leq n
,
0\leq k,m\leq n-1
, and
k+l+m=n
, then there is a constant
C_{k,l,m}(\varepsilon)
, independent of
a
, such that

(3.7)

\int_{X}\chi_{a}^{4n-2l}f_{\varepsilon}\zeta_{\varepsilon}(s)^{k}\alpha^{m}\omega^{l}\leq C_{k,l,m}(\varepsilon).

Moreover, for
0\leq p\leq n-1
there is a constant
D_{p}(\varepsilon)
, independent of
a
, such that

(3.8)

\int_{X}\chi_{a}^{4n}\zeta_{\varepsilon}(s)^{p}\alpha^{n-p}\leq\frac{D_{p}(\varepsilon)}{a}+q^{p}\int_{X}\alpha^{n}.

Assuming Lemma
3.2
, (
3.4
) gives

\displaystyle\int_{X}\zeta_{\varepsilon}(s)^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{a\to\infty}\left\{\frac{C}{a}\int_{X}\chi_{a}^{4n-2}f_{\varepsilon}\omega\zeta_{\varepsilon}(s)^{n-1}+q\int_{X}\chi_{a}^{4n}\alpha\zeta_{\varepsilon}(s)^{n-1}\right\}

(3.9)

\displaystyle\leq q^{n}\int_{X}\alpha^{n}.

The first term vanishes by (
3.7
) with
(k,m,l)=(n-1,0,1)
, and the second term uses (
3.8
) with
p=n-1
. Combining (
3.9
) with Lemma
3.1
proves Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Lemma
3.2
.

Assume first that (
3.7
) has been proved. We prove (
3.8
) by induction on
p
. The case
p=0
is immediate, with
D_{0}(\varepsilon)=0
. Suppose the estimate is known at level
p
. Using regularisation and
V_{\varepsilon}\leq f_{\varepsilon}
,

\displaystyle\int_{X}\chi_{a}^{4n}\zeta_{\varepsilon}(s)^{p+1}\alpha^{n-p-1}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\chi_{a}^{4n}\zeta_{\varepsilon}(s)^{p}(\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}V_{\varepsilon}+q\alpha)\alpha^{n-p-1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{a}\int_{X}\chi_{a}^{4n-2}f_{\varepsilon}\zeta_{\varepsilon}(s)^{p}\alpha^{n-p-1}\omega+q\int_{X}\chi_{a}^{4n}\zeta_{\varepsilon}(s)^{p}\alpha^{n-p}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{D_{p+1}(\varepsilon)}{a}+q^{p+1}\int_{X}\alpha^{n},

where the first term is absorbed into
D_{p+1}(\varepsilon)/a
by (
3.7
). This proves (
3.8
).

It remains to prove (
3.7
). We prove the following regularized estimate, uniform in the smoothing parameter: for
0<t\leq 1
and
k+m+l=n
,

(3.10)

\int_{X}\chi_{a}^{4n-2l}f_{\varepsilon,t}\zeta_{\varepsilon,t}(s)^{k}\alpha_{t}^{m}\omega^{l}\leq C_{k,l,m}(\varepsilon),

with
C_{k,l,m}(\varepsilon)
independent of
a
and
t
. Then (
3.7
) follows from (
3.1
).

We induct on
j=k+m=n-l
. When
j=0
,

\int_{X}\chi_{a}^{2n}f_{\varepsilon,t}\omega^{n}\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{X}\|s\|_{qu_{t}}^{2}\omega^{n}\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{2}}

by Proposition
2.3
, uniformly in
a
and
t
.
Assume (
3.10
) is known for all triples with
k+m\leq j
. Let
(k,m,l)
satisfy
k+m=j
and
k+m+l=n
. If
l=1
there is no level
j+1
estimate to prove, so assume
l\geq 2
.

First consider the triple
(k,m+1,l-1)
. Put

N:=4n-2l+2,\qquad T_{t}:=\zeta_{\varepsilon,t}(s)^{k}\alpha_{t}^{m}\omega^{l-1},

and

J_{a,t}:=\int_{X}\chi_{a}^{N}f_{\varepsilon,t}\alpha_{t}\wedge T_{t}.

Since
\alpha_{t}=\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}u_{t}
and
T_{t}
is closed, integration by parts and absolute values give

(3.11)

\displaystyle J_{a,t}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{a}\int_{X}\chi_{a}^{N-2}f_{\varepsilon,t}\zeta_{\varepsilon,t}(s)^{k}\alpha_{t}^{m}\omega^{l}+\left|\int_{X}\chi_{a}^{N}\sqrt{-1}\,\partial f_{\varepsilon,t}\wedge\bar{\partial}u_{t}\wedge T_{t}\right|.

The first term is bounded by the induction hypothesis. For the second term, Cauchy–Schwarz gives

\displaystyle\left|\int_{X}\chi_{a}^{N}\sqrt{-1}\,\partial f_{\varepsilon,t}\wedge\bar{\partial}u_{t}\wedge T_{t}\right|

(3.12)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{X}\chi_{a}^{N}\frac{\sqrt{-1}\,\partial f_{\varepsilon,t}\wedge\bar{\partial}f_{\varepsilon,t}}{f_{\varepsilon,t}}\wedge T_{t}\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{X}\chi_{a}^{N}f_{\varepsilon,t}\sqrt{-1}\,\partial u_{t}\wedge\bar{\partial}u_{t}\wedge T_{t}\right)^{1/2}.

The quotient is interpreted by its smooth extension across
Z(s)
; locally
f_{\varepsilon,t}=\rho|g|^{2}
with
\rho>0
smooth and
g
holomorphic. Since
|\nabla u_{t}|\leq C
,

\sqrt{-1}\,\partial u_{t}\wedge\bar{\partial}u_{t}\leq C\omega
, and since
0\leq\chi_{a}\leq 1
,

\chi_{a}^{N}\leq\chi_{a}^{N-2}=\chi_{a}^{4n-2l}
. Hence the second factor in (
3.12
) is bounded by the induction hypothesis, and

(3.13)

\left|\int_{X}\chi_{a}^{N}\sqrt{-1}\,\partial f_{\varepsilon,t}\wedge\bar{\partial}u_{t}\wedge T_{t}\right|\leq C\left(\int_{X}\chi_{a}^{N}\frac{\sqrt{-1}\,\partial f_{\varepsilon,t}\wedge\bar{\partial}f_{\varepsilon,t}}{f_{\varepsilon,t}}\wedge T_{t}\right)^{1/2}.

The Bochner identity for the canonical section
s
is

\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}f_{\varepsilon,t}=f_{\varepsilon,t}\operatorname{Ric}_{\omega}-qf_{\varepsilon,t}\alpha_{t}+\frac{\sqrt{-1}\,\partial f_{\varepsilon,t}\wedge\bar{\partial}f_{\varepsilon,t}}{f_{\varepsilon,t}}.

Since
\operatorname{Ric}_{\omega}\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\int_{X}\chi_{a}^{N}\frac{\sqrt{-1}\,\partial f_{\varepsilon,t}\wedge\bar{\partial}f_{\varepsilon,t}}{f_{\varepsilon,t}}\wedge T_{t}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}\chi_{a}^{N}\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}f_{\varepsilon,t}\wedge T_{t}+qJ_{a,t}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}f_{\varepsilon,t}\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}(\chi_{a}^{N})\wedge T_{t}+qJ_{a,t}

(3.14)

\displaystyle\leq C+qJ_{a,t},

where the last line uses the cutoff estimate and the induction hypothesis. Combining (
3.11
), (
3.13
), and (
3.14
),

J_{a,t}\leq C+C(C+qJ_{a,t})^{1/2}\leq C+C\sqrt{J_{a,t}},

so
J_{a,t}\leq C
, uniformly in
a
and
t
.

Now consider the triple
(k+1,m,l-1)
. Using (
3.6
) at the smooth level,

\displaystyle\int_{X}\chi_{a}^{N}f_{\varepsilon,t}\zeta_{\varepsilon,t}(s)^{k+1}\alpha_{t}^{m}\omega^{l-1}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}\chi_{a}^{N}\zeta_{\varepsilon,t}(s)^{k}\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}H(f_{\varepsilon,t})\alpha_{t}^{m}\omega^{l-1}+q\int_{X}\chi_{a}^{N}f_{\varepsilon,t}\zeta_{\varepsilon,t}(s)^{k}\alpha_{t}^{m+1}\omega^{l-1}

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{X}H(f_{\varepsilon,t})\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}(\chi_{a}^{N})\wedge\zeta_{\varepsilon,t}(s)^{k}\alpha_{t}^{m}\omega^{l-1}\right|+q\int_{X}\chi_{a}^{N}f_{\varepsilon,t}\zeta_{\varepsilon,t}(s)^{k}\alpha_{t}^{m+1}\omega^{l-1}

(3.15)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{a}\int_{X}\chi_{a}^{N-2}f_{\varepsilon,t}\zeta_{\varepsilon,t}(s)^{k}\alpha_{t}^{m}\omega^{l}+q\int_{X}\chi_{a}^{N}f_{\varepsilon,t}\zeta_{\varepsilon,t}(s)^{k}\alpha_{t}^{m+1}\omega^{l-1}\leq C.

The last line uses the induction hypothesis for the first term and the already proved
(k,m+1,l-1)
estimate for the second. This completes the induction, proves (
3.10
), and hence proves (
3.7
).
∎
