Random Tensor Estimates and Deterministic Diagonal Resolutions for Mixed Paracontrolled Operators
Guangqian Zhao

Abstract
We prove an abstract operator-level convergence criterion for mixed paracontrolled random operators T Λ i ; j , k ​ ( w ) = I i ​ ( w < Ψ j , Λ ) ∘ Ψ k , Λ T^{i;j,k}_{\Lambda}(w)=I_{i}(w<\Psi_{j,\Lambda})\circ\Psi_{k,\Lambda} on 𝕋 d \mathbb{T}^{d} . These operators are the second-level mixed blocks generated by quadratic paracontrolled expansions, and are singular in the range where the deterministic resonant-product threshold fails. At finite cutoff we separate T Λ τ = D Λ τ + B Λ τ , T^{\tau}_{\Lambda}=D^{\tau}_{\Lambda}+B^{\tau}_{\Lambda}, where D Λ τ D^{\tau}_{\Lambda} is the Fourier-diagonal covariance contraction supported on the external diagonal n = q n=q , and B Λ τ B^{\tau}_{\Lambda} is a centered second homogeneous Gaussian chaos with incidence n = q + ℓ + r n=q+\ell+r . The centered component is controlled by a finite second-order random tensor estimate, a Banach-valued time lift, and Sobolev–Besov dyadic summation. The deterministic component is treated through a diagonal-resolution interface: retained raw branches or model-determined orbit sums are required only to satisfy scalar Volterra dyadic bounds after the relevant finite cancellations have been formed. The result applies in arbitrary dimension, for finite color/label families, Fourier-diagonal weak covariance, random initial data, colored-in-time Gaussian profiles, and frequency-dependent amplitudes. It abstracts the reusable mixed-operator mechanism underlying concrete color-phase diagonal analyses, while leaving model-specific diagonal classification and scalar Volterra estimates as explicit inputs.



Keywords.
Random operators; operator-level criteria; centered Gaussian tensors; deterministic diagonal resolutions; Fourier-diagonal covariance; Gaussian time profiles; frequency envelopes; random initial data; colored-in-time forcing; paracontrolled distributions; stochastic dispersive equations.

Introduction

The construction of singular stochastic PDEs uses enhanced random objects in place of ill-defined products. This is the role of the Da Prato–Debussche decomposition, regularity structures and paracontrolled distributions in parabolic problems
[
2
,
7
,
5
]
. In dispersive equations the enhanced objects may be operator-valued: the Duhamel multiplier contributes oscillatory structure, while the deterministic resonant product may fall below its classical threshold. In the three-dimensional quadratic stochastic wave equation, Gubinelli, Koch and Oh construct such random operators inside a complete paracontrolled solution theory
[
6
]
. Random tensor estimates of Deng, Nahmod and Yue
[
4
]
, and finite-dimensional proofs such as
[
9
]
, provide the probabilistic mechanism for estimating operator-valued Gaussian chaoses.

The present paper isolates one operator-level mechanism which appears systematically in quadratic paracontrolled expansions. If the first paracontrolled correction contains a low-high block
I_{i}(w<\Psi_{j})
, then reinserting this correction into a later quadratic source and placing the next stochastic factor in the resonant position generates

I_{i}(w<\Psi_{j})\circ\Psi_{k}.

(1.1)

This is not a model-specific remainder. It is the mixed second-level operator attached to an ordered triple
(i;j,k)
in the coupling graph:
i
is the outer Duhamel channel,
j
is the high stochastic leg inside the paraproduct, and
k
is the stochastic leg in the resonant product. A finite system therefore generates a finite family of such triples, and the same operator estimate is needed label by label whenever the deterministic resonant-product threshold is unavailable.

The purpose of the paper is to give a reusable convergence criterion for these mixed operators. The finite-cutoff expression is a finite Fourier matrix, and the problem is operator-topology convergence of these matrices in the singular range

\lambda_{i}+\alpha_{j}+\alpha_{k}\leq d.

(1.2)

Indeed, if
\Psi_{a}
has spatial size
\alpha_{a}-d/2-\delta
and
I_{i}
has multiplier gain
\lambda_{i}
, then
I_{i}(w<\Psi_{j})
has deterministic regularity at most
\lambda_{i}+\alpha_{j}-d/2-\delta
, and the resonant product with
\Psi_{k}
would classically require
\lambda_{i}+\alpha_{j}+\alpha_{k}>d
. The theorem below constructs the mixed operator below this deterministic threshold by combining a centered random tensor estimate with a deterministic diagonal interface.

The estimate has two independent inputs. The first is the centered second-chaos estimate, where a finite tensor inequality is combined with the paracontrolled incidence relation, the Volterra time kernel and Sobolev–Besov dyadic source summation. The second is the retained diagonal estimate, where a deterministic scalar contraction, or a model-determined orbit sum of such contractions, satisfies a dyadic Volterra bound. The random tensor estimates in
[
4
,
9
]
supply the finite probabilistic inequality. The present argument identifies the Fourier incidence and the eight flattening counts needed to apply that inequality to the mixed paracontrolled block, and it records the diagonal resolution as a deterministic scalar interface. This separation is essential: the probabilistic tensor estimate is universal, while phase gaps, color cancellations, conjugation symmetries and orbit sums are model-specific scalar facts.

The relation with the two-speed Klein–Gordon paper
[
13
]
is complementary rather than a simple specialization. That paper is a concrete equation-level theory for the two-component pure-cross system on
\mathbb{T}^{3}
: it carries out color–phase separation for the four generated mixed blocks, proves the phase-difference Volterra estimates for the same-color diagonal contractions, treats the covariance-weighted lower-chaos branches under Fourier-diagonal weak covariance, constructs the stochastic enhanced data, closes the deterministic paracontrolled solution map, and identifies the canonical Galerkin limit. In particular, its diagonal analysis is part of the equation-level proof, not merely an example attached after the fact.

The present paper extracts from that model analysis the reusable mixed-operator theorem. Its object is no longer a specific two-speed SPDE but the abstract block
I_{i}(w<\Psi_{j})\circ\Psi_{k}
attached to a finite family of labels. The centered part is treated by a dimension-dependent random-tensor and incidence-count mechanism, while the lower-chaos part is encoded as a deterministic diagonal-resolution interface. This abstraction keeps the information needed to reuse the estimate in other systems: arbitrary spatial dimension, arbitrary finite color and label families, weak Fourier-diagonal color covariance, local normalization of possibly rank-defective Gaussian covariances, random initial data and other finite-rank time profiles, colored-in-time forcing, frequency-dependent amplitudes, and frequency-envelope summation with an explicit
B_{2,\infty}
output-tail condition.

Thus
[
13
]
and the present paper have different scopes. The former proves a full noise-to-solution and Galerkin-convergence theorem for one two-speed pure-cross Klein–Gordon system, including its diagonal Volterra and covariance branches. The latter does not attempt to reprove a complete solution theory for every coupling graph and does not automatically classify all deterministic diagonal cancellations. Instead, it gives the operator-level criterion that an application can call once the finite coupling graph, the retained or subtracted scalar diagonal orbits, and the corresponding Volterra estimates have been supplied. This is the level at which the same mixed operator can be reused in random initial data problems, colored-in-time noise models, multi-component systems, and other quadratic paracontrolled expansions.

The Gaussian input is therefore formulated at the level of Fourier-diagonal time profiles. The basic representation is
\widehat{\Psi}_{a}(n,t)=\mathbb{W}(h_{a,n,t})
. Stochastic convolutions driven by space-time noise, colored-in-time forcing, random initial data evolved by a linear flow, finite-rank wave/Klein–Gordon initial data, and frequency-dependent amplitudes are all instances of this representation once the dyadic amplitude and increment envelopes are verified. Different random inputs enter only through their local covariance, their dyadic envelopes and their time-oscillation scales; the Wick split, local whitening and centered tensor estimate are unchanged at the abstract level.

The finite Wick identity separates the finite kernel into a deterministic lower-chaos part and a centered random part. At cutoff
\Lambda
,

\Psi_{j,\Lambda}(\ell,s)\Psi_{k,\Lambda}(r,t)=:\Psi_{j,\Lambda}(\ell,s)\Psi_{k,\Lambda}(r,t):+\mathbb{E}[\Psi_{j,\Lambda}(\ell,s)\Psi_{k,\Lambda}(r,t)].

(1.3)

Fourier-diagonal covariance forces
\ell+r=0
in the scalar contraction. Inside the mixed block this gives the external diagonal
n=q
, so the lower-chaos branch is a deterministic Fourier-diagonal Volterra multiplier acting on the low input. The Wick-centered branch keeps the paracontrolled incidence

n=q+\ell+r

(1.4)

and is a second homogeneous Gaussian chaos. The proof keeps these two branches separate: the centered branch is estimated by a probabilistic tensor argument, and the diagonal branch is controlled by a deterministic scalar condition.

For a mixed label
\tau=(i;j,k)
, set
\Gamma_{\tau}=\lambda_{i}+\alpha_{j}+\alpha_{k}
. The centered tensor estimate gives, up to logarithmic losses,

\|B_{N,Q,M}^{\tau}\|_{\ell_{q}^{2}\to\ell_{n}^{2}}\lesssim N^{d/2-\Gamma_{\tau}}\bigl(M^{d/2}+Q^{d/2}\bigr),

(1.5)

where
N
is the high stochastic frequency,
Q
the low input frequency and
M
the output frequency. The two terms in (
1.5
) are the output-count and input-column contributions. They give the centered summability window in Definition
2.2
. The cutoff limit is obtained in operator norm for the mixed block.

For the diagonal branch, a deterministic resolution is fixed at cutoff level:

D^{\tau}_{\Lambda}=\mathsf{R}^{\tau}_{\Lambda}+\mathsf{C}^{\tau}_{\Lambda},\qquad T^{\tau,\mathfrak{P}}_{\Lambda}:=T^{\tau}_{\Lambda}-\mathsf{C}^{\tau}_{\Lambda}=\mathsf{R}^{\tau}_{\Lambda}+B^{\tau}_{\Lambda}.

(1.6)

The retained operator
\mathsf{R}^{\tau}_{\Lambda}
is a scalar diagonal Volterra multiplier. Its admissibility is the dyadic estimate in Definition
2.4
. The random tensor estimate is used only for
B^{\tau}_{\Lambda}
. The scalar contraction estimate is used only for
\mathsf{R}^{\tau}_{\Lambda}
. The assembled convergence follows from the finite identity (
1.6
).

In applications a retained diagonal may be obtained only after finitely many scalar branches have been grouped by a model-determined orbit relation, conjugation, time parity, Fourier involution or phase grouping. If
\mathfrak{o}\subset\mathfrak{J}_{\tau}
is such an orbit, its retained scalar kernel is the direct sum
\sum_{\nu\in\mathfrak{o}}D^{\tau}_{\Lambda,\nu}
. The numerical signs and complex coefficients are already contained in the branch definitions. The scalar estimate is imposed after this orbit sum is formed; no separate estimate is required for the individual summands. This is the boundary between the abstract theorem and a concrete equation: the theorem assembles the operator once the finite scalar diagonal data have been supplied.

The frequency-envelope version replaces the powers
N^{-\lambda_{i}}
,
N^{-\alpha_{j}}
,
N^{-\alpha_{k}}
by dyadic envelopes. The Gaussian input is an isonormal family
\widehat{\Psi}_{a}(n,t)=\mathbb{W}(h_{a,n,t})
, and the proof uses local Fourier-diagonal covariance, dyadic amplitude bounds and time-increment estimates. In the
B_{2,\infty}
component the assumptions include an output-tail condition, which is the hypothesis used in the cutoff Cauchy argument.

For each generated label, set
Y^{\tau}_{T}=C_{T}H^{s-\lambda_{i}}\cap L_{T}^{1}B^{\sigma-\lambda_{i}}_{2,\infty}
. The two inputs of the theorem can be read as the implications

\mathcal{H}_{\rm cen}^{\tau}(\varepsilon,s,\sigma)\Longrightarrow B^{\tau}_{\Lambda}\to B^{\tau}\quad\hbox{in }\mathcal{L}(X_{T}^{\sigma},Y^{\tau}_{T}),

\mathcal{H}_{\rm diag}^{\tau,\mathfrak{P}}(s)\Longrightarrow\mathsf{R}^{\tau}_{\Lambda}\to\mathsf{R}^{\tau}\quad\hbox{in }\mathcal{L}(E^{2,\sigma}_{T,\mathcal{M}},Y^{\tau}_{T}),

with
\mathcal{H}_{\rm cen}
and
\mathcal{H}_{\rm diag}
defined in Section
2.4
. The finite identity
T^{\tau,\mathfrak{P}}_{\Lambda}=\mathsf{R}^{\tau}_{\Lambda}+B^{\tau}_{\Lambda}
gives the assembled convergence.

In this form an application supplies the following finite data:

\begin{gathered}\tau=(i;j,k),\qquad(\lambda_{i},\alpha_{j},\alpha_{k},R_{jk}),\qquad(s,\sigma),\qquad c_{\rm ap},\\
D^{\tau}_{\Lambda}=\mathsf{R}^{\tau}_{\Lambda}+\mathsf{C}^{\tau}_{\Lambda},\qquad\|\mathsf{R}^{\tau}_{\Lambda,N,Q}P_{Q}w\|_{C_{T}L^{2}}\leq CN^{-\delta_{\tau}}\bigl(\|P_{Q}w\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L^{2}}+\|P_{Q}\partial_{t}w\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L^{2}}\bigr).\end{gathered}

(1.7)

The first line fixes the centered tensor summation. The second line fixes the retained diagonal summation. If a retained branch is an orbit with several scalar branches, the estimate in the second line is imposed after forming the direct orbit sum determined by the scalar expansion. These data are the finite interface used by Theorem
2.8
.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section
2
states the Gaussian model, the centered window, the retained diagonal interface, the main theorem and the frequency-envelope variant. Section
3
proves the finite Wick split, the Volterra criterion, the centered tensor estimate, the time lift, the dyadic summation and the cutoff convergence. Section

2
Formulation and main theorem

2.1
Littlewood–Paley blocks and Bony products

Let
P_{N}=\Delta_{N}
be smooth dyadic Littlewood–Paley projectors on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
, with
N\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
; we use the standard notation and estimates of paradifferential calculus, see
[
1
]
. All Fourier variables lie in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. We use the shell count

\#\{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:|n|\sim N\}\lesssim N^{d}

(2.1)

and the standard inhomogeneous convention at low frequency. Low-frequency blocks are absorbed into constants. Once a finite family of dispersive channels and deterministic diagonal resolutions has been fixed, choose a proportional aperture
c_{\rm ap}\in(0,1)
. This aperture is part of the finite diagonal data. Phase lower bounds and finite orbit identities for retained diagonals are verified only on the region
|q|\leq c_{\rm ap}N
, so the admissible value of
c_{\rm ap}
may depend on the finite list of phase relations. After it is chosen, it is fixed throughout the proof. Define

f<g=\sum_{N}S_{<c_{\rm ap}N}f\,P_{N}g,\qquad f>g=g<f,\qquad f\circ g=\sum_{N\sim M}P_{N}f\,P_{M}g,

(2.2)

where
S_{<c_{\rm ap}N}=\sum_{Q<c_{\rm ap}N}P_{Q}
, with the usual smooth transition near the cutoff edge. We write
\chi_{Q},\chi_{M}
for the smooth Fourier cutoffs associated with
P_{Q},P_{M}
,
\rho_{N}
for the high stochastic-leg cutoff, and
\chi_{\rm res}
for the fixed smooth resonant cutoff in the channel
|q+\ell|\sim|r|
. These cutoffs have the standard finite-overlap bounds of Littlewood–Paley theory, and their precise profile is immaterial after the aperture has been fixed. Thus every paraproduct block satisfies

Q\leq c_{\rm ap}N

(2.3)

up to constants fixed by the cutoff profile. All implicit constants may depend on the selected aperture, but never on dyadic scales, time grids or Galerkin cutoffs.

In the kernel formulae below,
\rho_{N}
denotes the smooth Fourier multiplier of
P_{N}
, and
\chi_{Q},\chi_{M}
denote the corresponding low-input and output-shell cutoffs. The factor
\chi_{\rm res}(a,b)
denotes the fixed smooth resonant cutoff used in the product
\circ
: it is supported where the two displayed frequencies are comparable, and it is equal to one on the slightly smaller comparable-frequency region. Its precise profile is part of the fixed Littlewood–Paley convention and has uniformly bounded overlap. No estimate below depends on its shape beyond these support properties.

We use the dyadic Galerkin cutoffs
\Lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, with multiplier
c_{\Lambda}(n)=\mathbf{1}_{|n|\leq\Lambda}
. Thus every cutoff operator is a finite Fourier operator, and each fixed dyadic block is eventually independent of
\Lambda
.

For a dispersive channel
i
, write

I_{i}F(t)=\int_{0}^{t}K_{i}(t-s,D)F(s)\,\mathrm{d}s.

(2.4)

The mixed block attached to a triple
(i;j,k)
is

T^{i;j,k}(w)=I_{i}(w<\Psi_{j})\circ\Psi_{k}.

(2.5)

The index
i
is the Duhamel channel; the indices
j,k
are the colors of the stochastic legs.

2.2
Gaussian Hilbert space and Fourier-diagonal covariance

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a finite set of colors. The Gaussian variables are realized on a real separable Hilbert space
\mathfrak{H}
with isonormal process
\mathbb{W}:\mathfrak{H}\to L^{2}(\Omega)
. For each color
a
, Fourier label
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, and time
t\in[0,T_{0}]
, the Gaussian Fourier coefficient is written

\widehat{\Psi}_{a}(n,t)=\mathbb{W}(h_{a,n,t}),\qquad h_{a,n,t}\in\mathfrak{H}_{\mathbb{C}},

(2.6)

with the real-valued convention

\overline{\widehat{\Psi}_{a}(n,t)}=\widehat{\Psi}_{a}(-n,t).

(2.7)

Thus the complex notation is shorthand for the corresponding real sine-cosine coordinates on each involution class
\{n,-n\}
. The variables indexed by
n
and
-n
are not independent; they are two complex encodings of the same real sine-cosine pair. The covariance convention below is written in non-conjugated Fourier form, hence Fourier diagonality appears as the constraint
n+m=0
.

The covariance is Fourier diagonal: for all
a,b\in\mathcal{C}
, all
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, and all
s,t\in[0,T_{0}]
,

\mathbb{E}\bigl[\widehat{\Psi}_{a}(n,t)\widehat{\Psi}_{b}(m,s)\bigr]=\mathbf{1}_{n+m=0}R_{ab}(n)\sigma_{ab}(n;t,s).

(2.8)

The noise-level shorthand corresponding to (
2.8
) is

\mathbb{E}\bigl[\,\mathrm{d}W_{a}(n,t)\,\mathrm{d}W_{b}(m,t)\bigr]=\mathbf{1}_{n+m=0}R_{ab}(n)\,\mathrm{d}t.

(2.9)

The compatibility conditions are

R_{ba}(-n)=\overline{R_{ab}(n)},\qquad\sigma_{ba}(-n;s,t)=\overline{\sigma_{ab}(n;t,s)}.

(2.10)

For each involution class
\{n,-n\}
, the induced covariance matrix on color and real sine-cosine coordinates is positive semidefinite. We normalize the diagonal by
R_{aa}(n)=1
. For
a\neq b
, assume

|R_{ab}(n)|\leq C_{R}\langle n\rangle^{-\kappa_{ab}},\qquad\kappa_{ab}\geq 0.

(2.11)

We set
\kappa_{aa}=0
. If
R_{ab}\equiv 0
for
a\neq b
, the corresponding cross-color diagonal is absent.

The spatial cutoff makes the Fourier sums finite. The time-indexed Gaussian process is handled through the isonormal Hilbert space above. Every probabilistic estimate below is first applied to a finite set of times and Fourier labels, hence to a finite Gaussian vector obtained from
\mathbb{W}
; the continuous-time version is then obtained from the increment bounds in Assumption
2.1
by the Banach-valued time-lift lemma. The centered tensor estimate uses only the following coefficient bounds.

General Gaussian time profiles.

The notation
\Psi_{a}
will often be realized by a stochastic convolution, but the arguments below use only the finite Gaussian Hilbert-space structure and the Fourier-diagonal covariance. The common object is the isonormal representation

\widehat{\Psi}_{a}(n,t)=\mathbb{W}(h_{a,n,t}).

Space-time white-noise forcing and random initial data are different special cases of this representation; neither construction contains the other. For a stochastic convolution driven by a Brownian Fourier mode one may take, for each fixed
n
,

h_{a,n,t}(r)=\mathbf{1}_{0\leq r\leq t}K_{a}(t-r,n)b_{a}(n)\quad\hbox{in }L^{2}([0,T_{0}]),

so that
\mathbb{W}(h_{a,n,t})=\int_{0}^{t}K_{a}(t-r,n)b_{a}(n)\,\,\mathrm{d}\beta_{a,n}(r)
. For random initial data evolved by a linear flow one may instead take

h_{a,n,t}=e^{\mathrm{i}t\phi_{a}(n)}a_{a}(n)e_{a,n},\qquad\widehat{\Psi}_{a}(n,t)=e^{\mathrm{i}t\phi_{a}(n)}a_{a}(n)g_{a,n},

or the analogous finite sum of sine and cosine profiles in wave variables. Thus the stochastic convolution case has a Volterra time covariance, while random initial data have rank-one or finite-rank time covariance. The Wick split, the local whitening and the centered tensor estimate are unchanged once the dyadic amplitude and increment estimates below are verified.

Assumption 2.1

(Channel-dependent coefficient bounds)
.

Fix
0<T_{0}\leq 1
. To every Duhamel channel
i
assign a gain
0<\lambda_{i}<\infty
, and to every stochastic color
a
assign a gain
0<\alpha_{a}<\infty
. For a mixed triple
(i;j,k)
, set

\Gamma_{i;j,k}:=\lambda_{i}+\alpha_{j}+\alpha_{k},\qquad\chi_{i;j,k}:=\max\{\lambda_{i},\alpha_{j},\alpha_{k}\}.

(2.12)

There exists
\eta_{0}>0
such that, for every
0<\theta\leq\eta_{0}
, for
|n|\sim N
and
0\leq t,t^{\prime}\leq T_{0}
,

\displaystyle|K_{i}(t,n)|

\displaystyle\leq C_{K}N^{-\lambda_{i}},

(2.13)

\displaystyle|K_{i}(t,n)-K_{i}(t^{\prime},n)|

\displaystyle\leq C_{K,\theta}|t-t^{\prime}|^{\theta}N^{-\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{i}\theta},

(2.14)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|\widehat{\Psi}_{a}(n,t)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\Psi}N^{-2\alpha_{a}},

(2.15)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|\widehat{\Psi}_{a}(n,t)-\widehat{\Psi}_{a}(n,t^{\prime})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\Psi,\theta}|t-t^{\prime}|^{2\theta}N^{-2\alpha_{a}+2\alpha_{a}\theta}.

(2.16)

The constants may depend on
T_{0}
,
\theta
and the finite channel family, but not on the dyadic shell or on the cutoff. The small exponent
\theta
is chosen after the strict dyadic loss
\varepsilon
has been fixed, so that factors
N^{\chi_{i;j,k}\theta}
are absorbed by the available margins. This power-law formulation is the main one used in Sections
3.1
–
3.4
. Section
2.6
gives the corresponding statement with general dyadic envelopes
K_{i}(N)
and
A_{a}(N)
.

2.3
Input and output spaces

Let
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{M})
be the finite set of outer Duhamel labels appearing in the mixed family
\mathcal{M}
. For
0<T\leq T_{0}
and
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
, set

X_{T}^{\sigma}:=C_{T}L^{2}\cap L_{T}^{\infty}B^{\sigma}_{2,\infty},\qquad\|w\|_{X_{T}^{\sigma}}:=\|w\|_{C_{T}L^{2}}+\|w\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}B^{\sigma}_{2,\infty}}.

(2.17)

For a small fixed
\varepsilon>0
, define

\displaystyle E_{T,\mathcal{M}}^{2,\sigma}:=\{w:

\displaystyle w\in X_{T}^{\sigma},

(2.18)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}w\in\bigcap_{i\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{M})}\bigl(C_{T}H^{-\lambda_{i}-\varepsilon}\cap L_{T}^{\infty}B^{\sigma-\lambda_{i}}_{2,\infty}\bigr)\}.

The norm is

\displaystyle\|w\|_{E_{T,\mathcal{M}}^{2,\sigma}}:=

\displaystyle\|w\|_{X_{T}^{\sigma}}

(2.19)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{M})}\left(\|\partial_{t}w\|_{C_{T}H^{-\lambda_{i}-\varepsilon}}+\|\partial_{t}w\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}B^{\sigma-\lambda_{i}}_{2,\infty}}\right).

For a single triple, this is the corresponding space with the one outer exponent
\lambda_{i}
. The derivative
\partial_{t}w
is understood in the distributional sense in time. In the Volterra integration by parts used below, this means that, after applying each spatial dyadic projector,
P_{Q}w(t)-P_{Q}w(0)=\int_{0}^{t}P_{Q}\partial_{s}w(s)\,\,\mathrm{d}s
in the indicated Sobolev–Besov topology. The derivative component of this norm is used only by retained deterministic diagonals after this integration by parts. The centered second-chaos estimates use the source space
X_{T}^{\sigma}
.

Definition 2.2

(Centered-admissible exponents)
.

Given a mixed triple
(i;j,k)
and
\varepsilon>0
, a pair
(s,\sigma)
is
(i;j,k,\varepsilon)
-centered admissible if

\Gamma_{i;j,k}>\frac{d}{2}+10\varepsilon,\qquad s<\lambda_{i}+\Gamma_{i;j,k}-d-10\varepsilon,\qquad\max\{0,d-\Gamma_{i;j,k}\}+10\varepsilon<\sigma<\lambda_{i}+\Gamma_{i;j,k}-d-10\varepsilon.

(2.20)

The
\sigma
-interval is non-empty precisely when

\max\{0,d-\Gamma_{i;j,k}\}<\lambda_{i}+\Gamma_{i;j,k}-d.

(2.21)

In the singular subrange
\Gamma_{i;j,k}\leq d
, this is the stochastic summability condition

2\Gamma_{i;j,k}+\lambda_{i}>2d.

(2.22)

The first condition in (
2.20
) is the finite random-matrix summability requirement coming from the high-loop factor
N^{d/2-\Gamma_{i;j,k}}
. In the equal-gain case it is
3\alpha>d/2
, which is weaker than the non-empty singular-window condition
\alpha>2d/7
. The gap comes from the operator summation after insertion of the low input. The
Q^{d/2}
-branch forces the lower bound on
\sigma
, while the
M^{d/2}
-branch forces the upper bound; the two inequalities overlap in the singular range exactly when
2\Gamma+\lambda>2d
.

2.4
Deterministic diagonal resolutions

Let
\tau=(i;j,k)
. The finite Wick split in Section
3.1
produces a deterministic diagonal operator
D^{\tau}_{\Lambda}
and a centered second-chaos operator
B^{\tau}_{\Lambda}
. On dyadic shells, the diagonal kernel is

\displaystyle D_{\Lambda,N,Q}^{\tau}(q;t,s):=

\displaystyle\chi_{Q}(q)\sum_{\ell}\rho_{N}(\ell)\rho_{N}(-\ell)\chi_{\rm res}(q+\ell,-\ell)

(2.23)

\displaystyle\times K_{i}(t-s,q+\ell)c_{\Lambda}(\ell)c_{\Lambda}(-\ell)R_{jk}(\ell)\sigma_{jk}(\ell;s,t),

with
D_{\Lambda,N,Q}^{\tau}=0
when the covariance channel
(j,k)
is absent. It acts by

\widehat{D^{\tau}_{\Lambda,N,Q}w}(q,t)=\int_{0}^{t}D^{\tau}_{\Lambda,N,Q}(q;t,s)\widehat{w}(q,s)\,\mathrm{d}s.

(2.24)

The full finite diagonal is the finite sum over
(N,Q)
.

For the target channel
i

Definition 2.4

(Admissible deterministic diagonal resolution)
.

A deterministic diagonal resolution for a finite family
\mathcal{M}
assigns to each label
\tau\in\mathcal{M}
deterministic diagonal Volterra operators
\mathsf{R}^{\tau}_{\Lambda}
and
\mathsf{C}^{\tau}_{\Lambda}
such that

(2.26)

The operator
\mathsf{R}^{\tau}_{\Lambda}
is the retained diagonal and
\mathsf{C}^{\tau}_{\Lambda}
is the finite diagonal subtracted from the raw mixed block.

The resolution is dyadically admissible if, for each label with
\mathsf{R}^{\tau}_{\Lambda}\neq 0
, there exists
\delta_{\tau}>0
such that, for every dyadic pair
(N,Q)
with
Q\leq c_{\rm ap}N
,

\|\mathsf{R}^{\tau}_{\Lambda,N,Q}P_{Q}w\|_{C_{T}L^{2}}\leq C_{\mathfrak{P}}N^{-\delta_{\tau}}\bigl(\|P_{Q}w\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L^{2}}+\|P_{Q}\partial_{t}w\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L^{2}}\bigr)

, uniformly in
\Lambda

\mathsf{R}^{\tau}_{\Lambda,N,Q}

for every fixed dyadic pair
(N,Q)
. If

A resolution may be specified after the finite contraction has been decomposed into finitely many deterministic scalar diagonal branches

D^{\tau}_{\Lambda}=\sum_{\nu\in\mathfrak{J}_{\tau}}D^{\tau}_{\Lambda,\nu}.

(2.28)

The branches

are the scalar pieces produced by the finite model after the relevant algebraic reductions of the diagonal branch have been made. Their numerical signs, complex coefficients, conjugation factors, sine–cosine factors, covariance weights and trigonometric constants are fixed branch data.

A retained orbit is a finite subset
\mathfrak{o}\subset\mathfrak{J}_{\tau}
, often a singleton or a two-element subset, determined by the scalar orbit relation, conjugation relation, time-parity relation, Fourier involution or phase grouping present in the concrete model. Its retained scalar kernel is the direct orbit sum

\mathfrak{D}^{\tau}_{\Lambda,\mathfrak{o}}:=\sum_{\nu\in\mathfrak{o}}D^{\tau}_{\Lambda,\nu}.

(2.29)

For a two-element orbit
\mathfrak{o}=\{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}\}
, this is
D^{\tau}_{\Lambda,\nu_{1}}+D^{\tau}_{\Lambda,\nu_{2}}
, where each summand is the scalar branch supplied by the finite expansion.

Choose a finite collection
\mathfrak{O}^{\rm ret}_{\tau}
of disjoint retained orbits, with each raw index used at most once inside a retained orbit, and set

\mathsf{R}^{\tau,\mathfrak{O}}_{\Lambda}:=\sum_{\mathfrak{o}\in\mathfrak{O}^{\rm ret}_{\tau}}\mathfrak{D}^{\tau}_{\Lambda,\mathfrak{o}},\qquad\mathsf{C}^{\tau,\mathfrak{O}}_{\Lambda}:=D^{\tau}_{\Lambda}-\mathsf{R}^{\tau,\mathfrak{O}}_{\Lambda}.

(2.30)

Then
D^{\tau}_{\Lambda}=\mathsf{R}^{\tau,\mathfrak{O}}_{\Lambda}+\mathsf{C}^{\tau,\mathfrak{O}}_{\Lambda}
. The admissibility condition is imposed on the retained scalar orbit sums. When
\mathfrak{o}
has several elements, the dyadic bound is a statement about the direct orbit sum (
2.29
); no separate bound for the individual summands is required. Any cancellation in such an orbit is therefore a property of the scalar kernel (
2.29
), not a sign choice made by the resolution.

The raw resolution retains all singleton branches of
D^{\tau}_{\Lambda}
. The fully Wick-subtracted resolution retains no orbit, so
\mathsf{R}^{\tau}_{\Lambda}=0
and
\mathsf{C}^{\tau}_{\Lambda}=D^{\tau}_{\Lambda}
. Intermediate choices retain only those singleton branches or model-determined orbit sums whose scalar kernels satisfy (
2.27

B^{\tau}_{\Lambda}
.

Only the retained raw kernels and retained orbit sums are required to have cutoff limits in Definition

. The term
\mathsf{C}^{\tau}_{\Lambda}
denotes the finite diagonal subtracted from the raw block. The finite resolved cutoff operator is

(2.31)

For each label
\tau=(i;j,k)
, the centered interface is

\mathcal{H}_{\rm cen}^{\tau}(\varepsilon,s,\sigma):\quad\text{the covariance model, Assumption~\ref{ass:coeff}, and Definition~\ref{def:admissible}}.

(2.32)

The retained-diagonal interface is

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\rm diag}^{\tau,\mathfrak{P}}(s):

\displaystyle D^{\tau}_{\Lambda}=\mathsf{R}^{\tau}_{\Lambda}+\mathsf{C}^{\tau}_{\Lambda},

(2.33)

\displaystyle\mathsf{R}^{\tau}_{\Lambda,N,Q}\text{ satisfies }\eqref{eq:retained-block-bound}\text{ and has fixed-block convergence for every }(N,Q),

\displaystyle\text{and, if }\mathsf{R}^{\tau}_{\Lambda}\neq 0,\text{ the retained gain obeys }s<\delta_{\tau}-10\varepsilon.

The centered proof uses only

Thus the raw choice retains all singleton branches of

Proposition 2.7

(Retained scalar diagonal criterion)
.

Let a deterministic diagonal resolution be chosen for one label
\tau=(i;j,k)
, and let
\mathsf{R}_{\Lambda,N,Q}^{\tau}
be the retained diagonal Volterra block. Suppose that the scalar kernel of
\mathsf{R}_{\Lambda,N,Q}^{\tau}
, after all retained orbit sums have first been formed, is a finite sum of components of the following two types.

(a)

Absolute kernels

satisfying

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{0}^{t}\sup_{|q|\sim Q}|r_{N,Q}(q;t,s)|\,\,\mathrm{d}s\leq CN^{-\delta}.

(2.41)

(b)

Oscillatory loop kernels of the form

r_{N,Q}(q;t,s)=\sum_{|\ell|\sim N}\sum_{\nu\in\mathfrak{V}}e^{\mathrm{i}(t-s)\Phi_{\nu}(q,\ell)}a_{\nu}(q,\ell;t,s),

(2.42)

where
\mathfrak{V}
is finite,
|\Phi_{\nu}(q,\ell)|>0
on the retained support, and

\sup_{0\leq s\leq t\leq T}\sup_{|q|\sim Q}\sum_{|\ell|\sim N}\sum_{\nu\in\mathfrak{V}}\frac{|a_{\nu}(q,\ell;t,s)|+|\partial_{s}a_{\nu}(q,\ell;t,s)|}{|\Phi_{\nu}(q,\ell)|}\leq CN^{-\delta}.

(2.43)

A post-loop phase
\Phi(q)
is the special case in which the
\ell
-sum has already been absorbed into
a
.

Then
\mathsf{R}_{\Lambda,N,Q}^{\tau}
satisfies the dyadic retained bound (
2.27
) with gain
\delta
, uniformly in
\Lambda
. In particular, a retained orbit sum is admissible once the model-fixed scalar branches in the orbit have first been directly summed and the resulting scalar kernel satisfies (
2.41
) or (
2.43
); no estimate is imposed on the two summands before the orbit sum is formed.

Proof.

The absolute kernels are immediate from Minkowski’s inequality. For an oscillatory loop kernel, fix
t
,
q
,
\ell
, and
\nu
. Write
a_{s}=a_{\nu}(q,\ell;t,s)
and
\Phi=\Phi_{\nu}(q,\ell)
. Integration by parts in the lower time variable gives

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}e^{\mathrm{i}(t-s)\Phi}a_{s}\widehat{w}(q,s)\,\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{\mathrm{i}t\Phi}a_{\nu}(q,\ell;t,0)\widehat{w}(q,0)-a_{\nu}(q,\ell;t,t)\widehat{w}(q,t)}{\mathrm{i}\Phi}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{e^{\mathrm{i}(t-s)\Phi}}{\mathrm{i}\Phi}\bigl(\partial_{s}a_{\nu}(q,\ell;t,s)\widehat{w}(q,s)+a_{\nu}(q,\ell;t,s)\partial_{s}\widehat{w}(q,s)\bigr)\,\,\mathrm{d}s.

The estimate (

and

\ell
and the finite index set

This is (

2.6
Frequency envelopes and general Gaussian time profiles

The power-law assumptions in Assumption

Proof of the theorem

3.1
Finite Wick split and deterministic diagonals

Throughout the proof the spatial cutoff is finite unless explicitly stated otherwise. Hence every dyadic Fourier matrix has finite spatial index sets. The Gaussian Hilbert space in time may be infinite-dimensional; whenever a Wick identity, whitening, decoupling, or tensor inequality is applied, we first restrict to the finite collection of time kernels that occurs in the corresponding fixed-time, increment, or cutoff-tail estimate. This gives a finite Gaussian vector inside the isonormal space of Section
2.2
. Uniform continuous-time estimates are obtained only after Lemma
3.11
.

Dyadic kernel formula.

Fix a dyadic triple
(N,Q,M)
with
Q\leq c_{\rm ap}N
. The kernel of the block
P_{M}T_{\Lambda}^{i;j,k}(P_{Q}w)
is

\displaystyle A_{\Lambda,N,Q,M}^{i;j,k}(n,q;t,s):=

\displaystyle\chi_{M}(n)\chi_{Q}(q)\sum_{\ell+r=n-q}\rho_{N}(\ell)\rho_{N}(r)\chi_{\rm res}(q+\ell,r)

(3.1)

\displaystyle\times K_{i}(t-s,q+\ell)\widehat{\Psi}_{j,\Lambda}(\ell,s)\widehat{\Psi}_{k,\Lambda}(r,t).

The operator acts by

P_{M}T_{\Lambda}^{i;j,k}(P_{Q}w)(n,t)=\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{q}A_{\Lambda,N,Q,M}^{i;j,k}(n,q;t,s)\widehat{w}(q,s)\,\mathrm{d}s.

(3.2)

The support conditions imply

|q|\sim Q,\qquad|\ell|\sim|r|\sim N,\qquad|n|\sim M,\qquad M\lesssim N.

(3.3)

The case
M\ll N
is permitted and corresponds to high-high cancellation in
q+\ell+r=n
.

The finite
R
-Wick identity.

For fixed colors, modes and times, set
X=\widehat{\Psi}_{j,\Lambda}(\ell,s)
and
Y=\widehat{\Psi}_{k,\Lambda}(r,t)
. The second Wick product is defined in the Gaussian Hilbert space by

XY=:XY:_{R}+\mathbb{E}[XY].

(3.4)

For every finite list of modes and time variables this is the ordinary finite-dimensional Wick identity on the span of the corresponding kernels.
The Fourier-diagonal covariance gives

\mathbb{E}\bigl[\widehat{\Psi}_{j,\Lambda}(\ell,s)\widehat{\Psi}_{k,\Lambda}(r,t)\bigr]=\mathbf{1}_{\ell+r=0}c_{\Lambda}(\ell)c_{\Lambda}(-\ell)R_{jk}(\ell)\sigma_{jk}(\ell;s,t).

(3.5)

Inserting (
3.4
) into (
3.1
) yields

A_{\Lambda,N,Q,M}^{i;j,k}=B_{\Lambda,N,Q,M}^{i;j,k}+\mathbf{1}_{n=q}\chi_{M}(q)D_{\Lambda,N,Q}^{i;j,k}(q;t,s),

(3.6)

where
D=0
if the covariance channel is absent, and otherwise

\displaystyle D_{\Lambda,N,Q}^{i;j,k}(q;t,s):=

\displaystyle\chi_{Q}(q)\sum_{\ell}\rho_{N}(\ell)\rho_{N}(-\ell)\chi_{\rm res}(q+\ell,-\ell)

(3.7)

\displaystyle\times K_{i}(t-s,q+\ell)c_{\Lambda}(\ell)c_{\Lambda}(-\ell)R_{jk}(\ell)\sigma_{jk}(\ell;s,t).

The relation
\ell+r=0
forces
n=q
. The factor
\chi_{M}(q)
in (
3.6
) is the output projection inherited from
P_{M}
; equivalently, the full diagonal operator is
D_{\Lambda,N,Q}^{i;j,k}
and its dyadic output block is
P_{M}D_{\Lambda,N,Q}^{i;j,k}P_{Q}
. Hence the lower-chaos branch is a scalar Fourier multiplier in the low input variable. The centered part
B
is still supported by
n=q+\ell+r
, including the centered Wick-square sector with
r=-\ell
.

Lemma 3.1

(Finite color split)
.

For every cutoff, dyadic triple and mixed label
(i;j,k)
, (
3.6
) is an exact algebraic identity. The branch
D
is deterministic and Fourier diagonal in
(q,n)
; the branch

is a finite sum of second homogeneous centered Gaussian chaoses.

Proof.

Fix the dyadic block and the two times
(s,t)
. The family of Fourier coefficients appearing in (

\mathfrak{H}
. For each pair
X,Y
in this finite vector, the degree-two Wick projection is
XY-\mathbb{E}[XY]
, so

; this is the elementary finite-vector form of Gaussian Hilbert space and Wiener chaos calculus
[
8
,
11
,
12
]
. Formula (
3.5

Lemma 3.2

(Finite assembly identity)
.

Let
\mathfrak{P}
be a deterministic diagonal resolution, so that
D^{\tau}_{\Lambda}=\mathsf{R}^{\tau}_{\Lambda}+\mathsf{C}^{\tau}_{\Lambda}
. Then the finite
\mathfrak{P}
-resolved operator satisfies, block by block,

P_{M}T_{\Lambda}^{\tau,\mathfrak{P}}P_{Q}=P_{M}\mathsf{R}^{\tau}_{\Lambda}P_{Q}+P_{M}B^{\tau}_{\Lambda}P_{Q}.

(3.8)

In particular, if

, then

is a centered second-chaos operator.

Proof.

The finite Wick split gives

∎

Diagonal Volterra estimate.

We prove the deterministic Volterra criterion for retained diagonals.

Proposition 3.3

(Volterra criterion for an admissible diagonal)
.

Assume Assumption
2.5
for
\tau=(i;j,k)
, and let
s<\Delta_{\tau}-10\varepsilon
. Then the choice
\mathsf{R}^{\tau}_{\Lambda}=D^{\tau}_{\Lambda}
,
\mathsf{C}^{\tau}_{\Lambda}=0
is a dyadically admissible deterministic diagonal resolution for this label with any retained gain
\delta_{\tau}<\Delta_{\tau}
. The cutoff diagonals converge to a deterministic operator
D^{\tau}
, and (
2.52
) holds with
\mathsf{R}^{\tau}=D^{\tau}
.

Proof.

We suppress the mixed label and write
\Delta=\Delta_{\tau}
,
\lambda=\lambda_{i}
, and
\Gamma=\Gamma_{\tau}
. The proof has three parts: a scalar dyadic multiplier estimate, summation in the Sobolev and Besov targets, and cutoff convergence.

For the absolutely summable remainder in Assumption
2.5
, the diagonal kernel is already bounded after the high loop:

\sup_{q,t,s}\left|\sum_{|\ell|\sim N}e_{N,Q}(q,\ell;s,t)\right|\lesssim N^{-\Delta}.

(3.9)

It therefore acts on the input mode
q
by a scalar multiplier of size
N^{-\Delta}
. We next treat one oscillatory summand; the number of such summands is fixed independently of the dyadic scales. For fixed
q,\ell,t
, set
a_{s}=a(q,\ell;s,t)
and
\Phi=\Phi(q,\ell)
. The contribution to the Volterra operator is

\mathcal{V}_{q,\ell}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}e^{\mathrm{i}(t-s)\Phi}a_{s}\widehat{w}(q,s)\,\mathrm{d}s.

(3.10)

If the branch has
\vartheta>0
, integration by parts in
s
gives the identity

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{q,\ell}(t)=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{\mathrm{i}t\Phi}a(q,\ell;0,t)\widehat{w}(q,0)-a(q,\ell;t,t)\widehat{w}(q,t)}{\mathrm{i}\Phi}

(3.11)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{e^{\mathrm{i}(t-s)\Phi}}{\mathrm{i}\Phi}\bigl(\partial_{s}a(q,\ell;s,t)\widehat{w}(q,s)+a(q,\ell;s,t)\partial_{s}\widehat{w}(q,s)\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}s.

Here
t
is treated as an external parameter; no derivative in
t
is used. By (
2.38
) and (
2.37
), the summand is bounded by

N^{-\Gamma-\kappa_{jk}-\vartheta}\Bigl(\|P_{Q}w\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L^{2}}+\|P_{Q}\partial_{t}w\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L^{2}}\Bigr)

(3.12)

at the level of the scalar multiplier acting on the
q
-shell. Summing the high loop, which has
O(N^{d})
lattice points, gives

N^{d}N^{-\Gamma-\kappa_{jk}-\vartheta}\lesssim N^{-\Delta},

(3.13)

by (
2.39
). Branches with
\vartheta=0
are estimated by their high-loop bound, which is required to satisfy the same
N^{-\Delta}
estimate. Consequently, for every dyadic diagonal block,

\|D_{N,Q}P_{Q}w\|_{C_{T}L^{2}}\lesssim N^{-\Delta}\|P_{Q}w\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L^{2}}+N^{-\Delta}\|P_{Q}\partial_{t}w\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L^{2}},

(3.14)

with the derivative term omitted for purely absolute branches.

We now sum this estimate. Because the diagonal branch is supported by
n=q
, the output frequency is
Q
, not
N
. For the Sobolev target,

\displaystyle\|D_{N,Q}P_{Q}w\|_{C_{T}H^{s-\lambda}}

\displaystyle\lesssim Q^{s-\lambda}N^{-\Delta}\|P_{Q}w\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L^{2}}

(3.15)

\displaystyle+Q^{s-\lambda}N^{-\Delta}\|P_{Q}\partial_{t}w\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L^{2}}.

The first term is summed by Cauchy–Schwarz, or by an arbitrary logarithmic loss:

\sum_{Q\leq c_{\rm ap}N}Q^{s-\lambda}\|P_{Q}w\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}N^{(s-\lambda)_{+}+\varepsilon}\|w\|_{L^{2}}.

(3.16)

The corresponding high-shell factor is
N^{-\Delta+(s-\lambda)_{+}+\varepsilon}
, which is summable under
s<\Delta-10\varepsilon
because either
s\leq\lambda
, in which case
(s-\lambda)_{+}=0
, or
s>\lambda
, in which case
(s-\lambda)_{+}<s<\Delta
. The derivative term uses

\|P_{Q}\partial_{t}w\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L^{2}}\leq Q^{\lambda+\varepsilon}\|\partial_{t}w\|_{C_{T}H^{-\lambda-\varepsilon}},

(3.17)

so that

\sum_{Q\leq c_{\rm ap}N}Q^{s-\lambda}N^{-\Delta}\|P_{Q}\partial_{t}w\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L^{2}}\lesssim N^{-\Delta+(s)_{+}+2\varepsilon}\|\partial_{t}w\|_{C_{T}H^{-\lambda-\varepsilon}}.

(3.18)

The high-shell sum is finite because
s<\Delta-10\varepsilon
; if
s<0
, the positive part is absent and the estimate is even easier. This proves the
C_{T}H^{s-\lambda}
diagonal bound.

For the Besov target, fix the output/input block
Q
. From (
3.14
),

\displaystyle Q^{\sigma-\lambda}\|D_{N,Q}P_{Q}w\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-\Delta}Q^{-\lambda}\|w\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}B^{\sigma}_{2,\infty}}

(3.19)

\displaystyle+N^{-\Delta}\|\partial_{t}w\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}B^{\sigma-\lambda}_{2,\infty}}.

The sum over
N\gtrsim Q
is finite because
\Delta>0
, uniformly in
Q
. Hence

\|Dw\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}B^{\sigma-\lambda}_{2,\infty}}\lesssim\|w\|_{E_{T,\mathcal{M}}^{2,\sigma}}.

The
L_{T}^{1}
estimate follows immediately with the factor
T
.

It remains only to justify the cutoff limit. For a fixed dyadic pair
(N,Q)
, the diagonal matrix is finite and its entries converge because the cutoff symbols converge pointwise. The estimates above give a summable majorant independent of the cutoff. Lemma
3.12
therefore proves convergence in the same operator norms. This proves the proposition.
∎

3.2
Checking deterministic diagonal resolutions

The deterministic hypothesis in Definition
2.4
is scalar and is checked before the centered tensor estimate is used. If a scalar orbit relation is available, the finite branches are first grouped into model-determined orbits and each orbit is replaced by its direct orbit sum. The resolution uses the branch coefficients already present in the scalar expansion. The following reductions record this order.

Lemma 3.4

(Elementary diagonal resolutions)
.

Let
D^{\tau}_{\Lambda}=\sum_{\nu\in\mathfrak{J}_{\tau}}D^{\tau}_{\Lambda,\nu}
be the finite diagonal decomposition in (
2.28
).

(a)

If a subset of singleton components is retained raw and each retained component satisfies (
2.27
) with fixed-block convergence, then their finite sum is an admissible retained diagonal.

(b)

If no component and no orbit is retained, then
\mathsf{R}^{\tau}_{\Lambda}=0
,
\mathsf{C}^{\tau}_{\Lambda}=D^{\tau}_{\Lambda}
, and the resolution is admissible without a deterministic estimate.

(c)

Suppose that a finite orbit
\mathfrak{o}\subset\mathfrak{J}_{\tau}
is retained through the scalar kernel

\mathcal{R}^{\tau}_{\Lambda,\mathfrak{o}}:=\sum_{\nu\in\mathfrak{o}}D^{\tau}_{\Lambda,\nu}.

If
\mathcal{R}^{\tau}_{\Lambda,\mathfrak{o}}
satisfies (
2.27
) and fixed-block convergence, then this orbit may be included in
\mathsf{R}^{\tau}_{\Lambda}
. No estimate of the individual summands
D^{\tau}_{\Lambda,\nu}
,
\nu\in\mathfrak{o}
, is used separately.

Consequently any finite retained diagonal formed from admissible singleton components and admissible orbit sums is an admissible deterministic diagonal resolution.

Proof.

Part (a) is the triangle inequality applied to finitely many scalar Volterra multipliers. Part (b) has no retained diagonal. For part (c), the scalar kernel is first replaced by the orbit sum
\mathcal{R}^{\tau}_{\Lambda,\mathfrak{o}}
. The assumed estimate is exactly (
2.27
) for that retained scalar kernel, and fixed-block convergence is part of the hypothesis. Since the number of retained orbits and singleton components is finite, summing the resulting bounds gives the retained estimate for
\mathsf{R}^{\tau}_{\Lambda}
. The discarded diagonal is
\mathsf{C}^{\tau}_{\Lambda}=D^{\tau}_{\Lambda}-\mathsf{R}^{\tau}_{\Lambda}
and enters through the finite subtraction identity.
∎

3.3
Centered second-chaos estimate

Local whitening, real Fourier variables and Wick-square decoupling.

We first record the finite skeleton used for the local covariance normalization. The normal form lemma then combines the real Fourier convention, weak color correlation and same-class Wick-square decoupling in one statement.

Finite local Gaussian skeletons.

The local normalization below is always performed for one estimate at a time. Fix a Galerkin cutoff, a dyadic triple
(N,Q,M)
, and one estimate type: fixed-time, one-time increment, or cutoff tail. For each real Fourier involution class
[m]=\{m,-m\}
, let
\mathcal{K}_{[m]}
be the finite list of normalized first-Wiener kernels with spatial label in
[m]
that occur in that estimate. A fixed-time centered block uses the two high-leg kernels at the displayed times; an increment block adds only the corresponding difference kernel; and a cutoff-tail block replaces one of these kernels by its cutoff-difference version. Fourier-diagonal covariance gives the orthogonal decomposition

\mathfrak{H}_{\rm block}=\bigoplus_{[m]}\mathfrak{H}_{[m]},\qquad\mathfrak{H}_{[m]}=\operatorname{span}\mathcal{K}_{[m]}/\ker\Sigma_{[m]},

where
\Sigma_{[m]}
is the positive semidefinite covariance matrix of
\mathcal{K}_{[m]}
. On each quotient one chooses a standard Gaussian coordinate vector
G_{[m]}
and a deterministic covariance square root
U_{[m]}
such that the normalized local Gaussian vector is
U_{[m]}G_{[m]}
. The dimension of these local coordinate spaces depends only on the number of colors, the real sine–cosine convention, and the number of kernels in the chosen estimate type. It is independent of
N,Q,M
, of the cutoff, of the actual time values, and of later rational-grid refinements. The maps
U_{[m]}
act only inside the class
[m]
, so they do not mix the Fourier labels entering the incidence relation. No inverse covariance matrix is used; rank-defective local covariance matrices are handled by quotienting out zero-norm directions before coordinates are chosen.

Lemma 3.7

(Local normalization and decoupled normal form)
.

Fix a cutoff and a dyadic block
(N,Q,M)
. For each estimate under consideration choose the finite time skeleton that appears in it: for a fixed-time bound this consists of the two Gaussian kernels
h_{j,\ell,s}
and
h_{k,r,t}
; for a
t
- or
s
-increment it also contains the corresponding differences
h_{k,r,t}-h_{k,r,t^{\prime}}
or
h_{j,\ell,s}-h_{j,\ell,s^{\prime}}
; and for a cutoff-tail estimate the same kernels are multiplied by the appropriate cutoff difference. After passing to real sine-cosine coordinates on each Fourier involution class, the centered kernel
B_{\Lambda,N,Q,M}^{i;j,k}
admits a representation

B_{\Lambda,N,Q,M}^{i;j,k}(n,q;t,s)=\sum_{\nu}\sum_{\ell,r}\sum_{\mu,\lambda\leq d_{0}}H_{\nu}(\ell,\mu,r,\lambda,q,n;t,s):G_{\nu,\ell,\mu}G^{\prime}_{\nu,r,\lambda}:,

(3.20)

where the sum over
\nu
ranges over a finite set of real-Fourier sign, color and involution patterns. The number of such patterns and the integer
d_{0}
depend only on the number of colors, the real Fourier coordinate convention and the number of time kernels in the chosen skeleton. In particular, they are independent of
N,Q,M,\Lambda
, of the particular time variables, and of any later refinement of the rational time grid. The auxiliary labels
\mu,\lambda
are local Hilbert-coordinate labels, not additional Fourier variables. The support is

n=q+\ell+r,\qquad|\ell|\sim|r|\sim N,\qquad|q|\sim Q,\qquad|n|\sim M,\qquad Q\leq c_{\rm ap}N,

(3.21)

up to the fixed overlap of the Littlewood–Paley cutoffs. Moreover

|H_{\nu}(\ell,\mu,r,\lambda,q,n;t,s)|\lesssim N^{-\Gamma_{i;j,k}}.

(3.22)

If one of the time variables is replaced by an increment, the same representation holds with the additional factor supplied by Assumption
2.1
. If the two Gaussian legs lie in the same local Gaussian family, the Wick-square part satisfies the Banach-valued decoupling estimate

\left\|\sum_{a,b}C_{a,b}:G_{a}G_{b}:\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;X)}\lesssim_{p}\left\|\sum_{a,b}C_{a,b}G_{a}\widetilde{G}_{b}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega\times\widetilde{\Omega};X)}

(3.23)

for every finite Banach space target
X
, where
(\widetilde{G}_{b})
is an independent copy. The scalar expectation removed in the Wick split is not present in (
3.23
).

Proof.

For each spatial involution class
\{m,-m\}
, collect the stochastic first-Wiener kernels from the finite skeleton described in the statement. Thus a fixed-time estimate uses
h_{j,m,s}
and
h_{k,m,t}
, while an increment estimate adds only the relevant differences, for instance
h_{k,m,t}-h_{k,m,t^{\prime}}
when the upper time changes and
h_{j,m,s}-h_{j,m,s^{\prime}}
when the lower time changes. Deterministic factors such as
K_{i}(t-s,q+\ell)
, the Littlewood–Paley cutoffs and the Galerkin cutoff difference are not whitened; they remain in the scalar coefficient tensor. The covariance matrix of the collected first-Wiener kernels, written in real sine-cosine and color coordinates, is positive semidefinite by the Gaussian Hilbert construction. Quotient by its null space and choose an orthonormal basis. Equivalently, represent the local vector as
U_{m}G_{m}
, where
G_{m}
is a standard Gaussian vector on
\operatorname{Ran}\Sigma_{m}
and
U_{m}U_{m}^{*}=\Sigma_{m}
.

This normalization is local in the involution class. More explicitly, after the dyadic size factors have been removed, let
\mathcal{K}_{m}
be the finite list of normalized color/time kernels attached to the class
\{m,-m\}
in the block under consideration, and let

\Sigma_{m}=(\langle\bar{h}_{\rho},\bar{h}_{\rho^{\prime}}\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}})_{\rho,\rho^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K}_{m}}.

This matrix is positive semidefinite. We replace
\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{K}_{m}}
by the quotient Hilbert space
\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{K}_{m}}/\ker\Sigma_{m}
, choose an orthonormal basis there, and write the normalized Gaussian vector as
U_{m}G_{m}
, with
\Sigma_{m}=U_{m}U_{m}^{*}
on the quotient. The map
U_{m}
is the covariance square root on the quotient Hilbert space. Zero-norm directions have been removed, and all estimates use the operator norm of
U_{m}
. The uniform bound is elementary. The normalized diagonal variances in
\Sigma_{m}
are

, and positive semidefiniteness gives
|(\Sigma_{m})_{\rho,\rho^{\prime}}|\leq(\Sigma_{m})_{\rho,\rho}^{1/2}(\Sigma_{m})_{\rho^{\prime},\rho^{\prime}}^{1/2}
. Since the number of indices
\rho
in the skeleton is bounded by a constant
C_{\rm skel}
,

\|\Sigma_{m}\|_{\operatorname{op}}\leq\operatorname{tr}\Sigma_{m}\leq C_{\rm skel},\qquad\|U_{m}\|_{\operatorname{op}}\leq C_{\rm skel}^{1/2}.

This bound is independent of
N,Q,M,\Lambda
, the chosen times, and any later rational-grid refinement.

The construction acts on color, sine-cosine and time-kernel coordinates attached to a fixed spatial label. It preserves the spatial labels
q,\ell,r,n
, and the convolution constraint in the dyadic kernel remains

. The dyadic sizes are factored out before the finite orthonormalization: on

one writes the first-Wiener kernels in the form

with

, and the increment kernels are normalized in the same way with the additional factor from (

). Expanding the two stochastic factors in the local orthonormal coordinates gives (
3.20

N^{-\lambda_{i}}
comes from the Duhamel multiplier and the two factors
N^{-\alpha_{j}}
,
N^{-\alpha_{k}}
come from the stochastic convolution kernels. This gives (
3.22
). The increment version follows from the normalized increment bounds (
2.14
) and (
2.16
); if both deterministic and stochastic factors change, a finite telescope decomposition is used and each summand has one displayed increment factor.

When

and

\mathbb{E}[G_{a}G_{b}]
is already assigned to the diagonal branch. The remaining centered second chaos is then handled by the standard decoupling inequality for homogeneous Gaussian chaoses, applied with values in the finite-dimensional Banach space

A finite second-order tensor estimate.

We state the finite probabilistic estimate in the form used below and give the proof to fix the dependence on the flattenings. This is the second-order finite form of the abstract random tensor mechanism; compare with
[
4
,
9
]
. The proof is included to fix the precise row and column flattenings used by the later paracontrolled incidence count. The logarithmic factor comes only from passing through Schatten norms and finite nets, and it is absorbed later by the strict dyadic losses.

Proposition 3.8

(Finite second-order tensor estimate)
.

Let
A,B,C,E
be finite sets and set

D_{\mathrm{fin}}=2+|A|+|B|+|C|+|E|.

The symbol
D_{\mathrm{fin}}
is a finite matrix-size parameter and is unrelated to the spatial dimension
d
. Let
(g_{a})_{a\in A}
and
(h_{b})_{b\in B}
be independent standard real Gaussian families. For a deterministic tensor
H=(H_{a,b,c,e})
, define the random matrix

Z_{e,c}=\sum_{a\in A}\sum_{b\in B}H_{a,b,c,e}g_{a}h_{b}.

(3.24)

Let
\mathfrak{R}(H)
be the maximum of the eight flattening norms

\displaystyle\|H\|_{\ell^{2}_{a,b,c}\to\ell^{2}_{e}},\quad\|H\|_{\ell^{2}_{a,b,e}\to\ell^{2}_{c}},\quad\|H\|_{\ell^{2}_{a,b}\to\ell^{2}_{c,e}},\quad\|H\|_{\ell^{2}_{c,e}\to\ell^{2}_{a,b}},

\displaystyle\|H\|_{\ell^{2}_{a,c}\to\ell^{2}_{b,e}},\quad\|H\|_{\ell^{2}_{b,c}\to\ell^{2}_{a,e}},\quad\|H\|_{\ell^{2}_{a,e}\to\ell^{2}_{b,c}},\quad\|H\|_{\ell^{2}_{b,e}\to\ell^{2}_{a,c}}.

(3.25)

Then, for every
p\geq 2
,

\bigl\|\|Z\|_{\ell^{2}_{c}\to\ell^{2}_{e}}\bigr\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C\bigl(p+\log D_{\mathrm{fin}}\bigr)^{C}\mathfrak{R}(H),

(3.26)

where
C
is an absolute constant. The same bound holds for complex Gaussian families after realification, and for same-family Wick-square coefficients
:g_{a}g_{b}:
after decoupling into an independent copy. It is also stable under the finite sign, real-Fourier and auxiliary-coordinate patterns produced by Lemma
3.7
: a bounded enlargement of the Gaussian legs or a finite sum of patterns changes only the implicit constant.

Proof.

We give the proof in real coordinates. Complex coordinates are first realified; this only multiplies the finite index sets by an absolute factor and is already the convention used in Lemma
3.7
. Choose

r=\left\lceil C_{0}\bigl(p+\log D_{\mathrm{fin}}\bigr)\right\rceil

with
C_{0}
large. Then
r\geq p
, and it is enough to estimate
\|Z\|_{L^{r}(\Omega;S_{r})}
, because
\|Z\|_{\operatorname{op}}\leq\|Z\|_{S_{r}}
. The Schatten spaces below act on one of the one- or two-factor spaces

\ell^{2}_{c},\quad\ell^{2}_{e},\quad\ell^{2}_{b}\otimes\ell^{2}_{c},\quad\ell^{2}_{b}\otimes\ell^{2}_{e},\quad\ell^{2}_{a}\otimes\ell^{2}_{c},\quad\ell^{2}_{a}\otimes\ell^{2}_{e}.

Their dimensions are at most
D_{\mathrm{fin}}^{2}
. Hence every passage from an operator norm to an
S_{r}
-norm costs at most
D_{\mathrm{fin}}^{2/r}
, which is uniformly bounded by the choice of
r
. This is the only finite-dimensional size loss in the argument.

We use the rectangular non-commutative Khintchine inequality
[
10
]
: for finite matrices
A_{b}:H_{1}\to H_{2}
and independent standard Gaussians
(\gamma_{b})
,

\left\|\sum_{b}\gamma_{b}A_{b}\right\|_{L^{r}(\Omega;S_{r}(H_{1},H_{2}))}\lesssim\sqrt{r}\left(\left\|\Big(\sum_{b}A_{b}A_{b}^{*}\Big)^{1/2}\right\|_{S_{r}(H_{2})}+\left\|\Big(\sum_{b}A_{b}^{*}A_{b}\Big)^{1/2}\right\|_{S_{r}(H_{1})}\right).

(3.27)

The displayed rectangular form follows from the usual self-adjoint form by placing
A_{b}
in the off-diagonal block of
H_{1}\oplus H_{2}
; this is the standard self-adjoint block trick.

Condition first on the
g
-variables and write

Z=\sum_{b\in B}h_{b}S_{b}(g),\qquad S_{b}(g)_{e,c}=\sum_{a\in A}H_{a,b,c,e}g_{a}.

(3.28)

Applying (
3.27
) to the Gaussian sum of operators
S_{b}(g):\ell^{2}_{c}\to\ell^{2}_{e}
gives

\displaystyle\|Z\|_{L_{h}^{r}S_{r}}\lesssim\sqrt{r}\Bigg(

\displaystyle\left\|\Big(\sum_{b}S_{b}(g)S_{b}(g)^{*}\Big)^{1/2}\right\|_{S_{r}(\ell^{2}_{e})}

(3.29)

\displaystyle+\left\|\Big(\sum_{b}S_{b}(g)^{*}S_{b}(g)\Big)^{1/2}\right\|_{S_{r}(\ell^{2}_{c})}\Bigg).

We estimate the two square functions in
L_{g}^{r}
.

For the first square function define

\mathcal{S}(g):\ell^{2}_{b}\otimes\ell^{2}_{c}\longrightarrow\ell^{2}_{e},\qquad\mathcal{S}(g)_{e,(b,c)}=S_{b}(g)_{e,c}.

Then
\|\mathcal{S}(g)\|_{\operatorname{op}}=\|(\sum_{b}S_{b}(g)S_{b}(g)^{*})^{1/2}\|_{\operatorname{op}}
. Write
\mathcal{S}(g)=\sum_{a}g_{a}T_{a}
, where
(T_{a})_{e,(b,c)}=H_{a,b,c,e}
. A second application of (
3.27
) yields

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{S}(g)\|_{L_{g}^{r}S_{r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{r}\left(\left\|\Big(\sum_{a}T_{a}T_{a}^{*}\Big)^{1/2}\right\|_{S_{r}}+\left\|\Big(\sum_{a}T_{a}^{*}T_{a}\Big)^{1/2}\right\|_{S_{r}}\right)

(3.30)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{r}\left(\|H\|_{\ell^{2}_{a,b,c}\to\ell^{2}_{e}}+\|H\|_{\ell^{2}_{b,c}\to\ell^{2}_{a,e}}\right).

Indeed,
\|\sum_{a}T_{a}T_{a}^{*}\|_{\operatorname{op}}^{1/2}
is the norm of the flattening
(a,b,c)\to e
, while
\|\sum_{a}T_{a}^{*}T_{a}\|_{\operatorname{op}}^{1/2}
is the norm of
(b,c)\to(a,e)
.

For the second square function define

\mathcal{T}(g):\ell^{2}_{c}\longrightarrow\ell^{2}_{b}\otimes\ell^{2}_{e},\qquad\mathcal{T}(g)_{(b,e),c}=S_{b}(g)_{e,c}.

Then
\|\mathcal{T}(g)\|_{\operatorname{op}}=\|(\sum_{b}S_{b}(g)^{*}S_{b}(g))^{1/2}\|_{\operatorname{op}}
. With
\mathcal{T}(g)=\sum_{a}g_{a}T^{\prime}_{a}
, the same argument gives

\|\mathcal{T}(g)\|_{L_{g}^{r}S_{r}}\lesssim\sqrt{r}\left(\|H\|_{\ell^{2}_{a,c}\to\ell^{2}_{b,e}}+\|H\|_{\ell^{2}_{c}\to\ell^{2}_{a,b,e}}\right).

(3.31)

The last norm is the adjoint of the listed flattening
(a,b,e)\to c
. Combining (
3.29
), (
3.30
) and (
3.31
) gives a bound by
r
times four of the flattenings. Conditioning first on the
h
-variables and repeating the identical proof gives the four flattenings with the two Gaussian legs exchanged. Together with adjoints, these are exactly the eight norms in (
3.8
). Hence

\|Z\|_{L^{r}(\Omega;S_{r})}\lesssim r\mathfrak{R}(H),

and (
3.26
) follows from
r\lesssim p+\log D_{\mathrm{fin}}
.

For same-family Wick squares, the decoupling inequality for homogeneous Gaussian chaoses with values in
X=\mathcal{L}(\ell_{c}^{2},\ell_{e}^{2})
gives

\left\|\sum_{a,b}C_{a,b}:g_{a}g_{b}:\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;X)}\lesssim\left\|\sum_{a,b}C_{a,b}g_{a}h_{b}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{g}\times\Omega_{h};X)},

(3.32)

where
(h_{b})
is an independent copy. The scalar expectation is absent because the Wick contraction has already been placed in the deterministic diagonal. Applying the decoupled estimate above proves the Wick-square statement.
∎

Time lift and cutoff tails.

The fixed-time matrix estimate must be upgraded to a uniform estimate in the two Volterra time variables. We use the following elementary form of Kolmogorov’s argument, stated separately to make clear where the small loss in

Lemma 3.11

(Banach-valued time lift)
.

Let
X(t,s)
,
0\leq s\leq t\leq T
, be a random field with values in a finite-dimensional Banach space
B
. Assume that for some
A_{N}
, all
p_{0}\geq 2
, some
\eta\in(0,1]
, and some
\chi\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\|\|X(t,s)\|_{B}\|_{L^{p_{0}}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p_{0}}A_{N},

(3.35)

\displaystyle\|\|X(t,s)-X(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\|_{B}\|_{L^{p_{0}}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p_{0}}A_{N}N^{\chi\eta}(|t-t^{\prime}|^{\eta}+|s-s^{\prime}|^{\eta}),

(3.36)

whenever both pairs
(t,s)
and
(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})
lie in the Volterra simplex. Then, for every fixed
p\geq 2
and every
\varepsilon>0
, if
\chi\eta<\varepsilon/4
,

\|\|X\|_{C_{t,s}B}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C_{p,T,\varepsilon}A_{N}N^{\varepsilon}.

(3.37)

The same statement holds for a separable Banach-valued modification. In the application below
B=\mathcal{L}(\ell_{q}^{2},\ell_{n}^{2})
, a finite-dimensional matrix space; the continuous-time norm is obtained from rational time skeletons.

Proof.

We prove the estimate on dyadic rational grids and then pass to the separable modification. Let

\Delta_{T}=\{(t,s):0\leq s\leq t\leq T\},\qquad\Gamma_{L}=2^{-L}\mathbb{Z}\cap[0,T],\qquad\Delta_{L}=\Delta_{T}\cap\Gamma_{L}^{2}.

The cardinality of
\Delta_{L}
is
O_{T}(2^{2L})
. Choose

p_{0}>\max\{p,4/\eta\}+4/\eta.

This leaves room for the two-dimensional grid count.

The grid at level zero is finite, so (
3.35
) gives

\left\|\sup_{(t,s)\in\Delta_{0}}\|X(t,s)\|_{B}\right\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim_{p,T}A_{N}.

For
L\geq 0
, choose for each
(t,s)\in\Delta_{L+1}
a point
\pi_{L}(t,s)=(\bar{t},\bar{s})\in\Delta_{L}
with

|t-\bar{t}|+|s-\bar{s}|\lesssim_{T}2^{-L}.

If a nearest grid point lies outside the simplex, first project to the boundary
s=t
and then to the grid; the same distance bound holds. By (
3.36
),

\|X(t,s)-X(\pi_{L}(t,s))\|_{L^{p_{0}}(\Omega;B)}\lesssim_{p_{0},T}A_{N}N^{\chi\eta}2^{-L\eta}.

Since
\Delta_{L+1}
has
O_{T}(2^{2L})
points,

\displaystyle\left\|\sup_{(t,s)\in\Delta_{L+1}}\|X(t,s)-X(\pi_{L}(t,s))\|_{B}\right\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\sup_{(t,s)\in\Delta_{L+1}}\|X(t,s)-X(\pi_{L}(t,s))\|_{B}\right\|_{L^{p_{0}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{2L/p_{0}}A_{N}N^{\chi\eta}2^{-L\eta}.

The series
\sum_{L}2^{-L(\eta-2/p_{0})}
converges. The dyadic chaining estimate gives

\left\|\sup_{(t,s)\in\bigcup_{L}\Delta_{L}}\|X(t,s)\|_{B}\right\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim_{p,T,\eta}A_{N}(1+N^{\chi\eta}).

For a separable continuous modification, the supremum over the rational skeleton equals the
C(\Delta_{T};B)
-norm. In the finite matrix application this modification is obtained from the increment estimates for the matrix entries and the finite-dimensional operator norm. Finally
\chi\eta<\varepsilon/4
gives
N^{\chi\eta}\leq N^{\varepsilon}
for
N\geq 1
, and finitely many low dyadic shells are absorbed into the constant. This proves (
3.37
).
∎

We apply Lemma
3.11
with
B=\mathcal{L}(\ell_{q}^{2},\ell_{n}^{2})
. Fix the strict dyadic loss
\varepsilon
. Choose
0<\theta\leq\eta_{0}
so that

, and set

. The fixed-time estimate is (

A centered summand has the form

K_{t,s}:X_{s}Y_{t}:_{R},\qquad K_{t,s}=K_{i}(t-s,q+\ell),\quad X_{s}=\widehat{\Psi}_{j}(\ell,s),\quad Y_{t}=\widehat{\Psi}_{k}(r,t).

The degree-two Wick projection is bilinear after the scalar expectation has been removed. Hence

:X_{s}Y_{t}:_{R}-:X_{s}Y_{t^{\prime}}:_{R}=:X_{s}(Y_{t}-Y_{t^{\prime}}):_{R},\qquad:X_{s}Y_{t}:_{R}-:X_{s^{\prime}}Y_{t}:_{R}=:(X_{s}-X_{s^{\prime}})Y_{t}:_{R}.

The upper-time increment is

\displaystyle K_{t,s}:X_{s}Y_{t}:_{R}-K_{t^{\prime},s}:X_{s}Y_{t^{\prime}}:_{R}

\displaystyle=(K_{t,s}-K_{t^{\prime},s}):X_{s}Y_{t}:_{R}

\displaystyle+K_{t^{\prime},s}:X_{s}(Y_{t}-Y_{t^{\prime}}):_{R},

and the lower-time increment is

\displaystyle K_{t,s}:X_{s}Y_{t}:_{R}-K_{t,s^{\prime}}:X_{s^{\prime}}Y_{t}:_{R}

\displaystyle=(K_{t,s}-K_{t,s^{\prime}}):X_{s}Y_{t}:_{R}

\displaystyle+K_{t,s^{\prime}}:(X_{s}-X_{s^{\prime}})Y_{t}:_{R}.

For two arbitrary Volterra pairs
(t,s),(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\in\Delta_{T}:=\{0\leq s\leq t\leq T\}
, the telescope is taken along a path that stays inside the simplex. Put

and write, for

(t,s),(t,u),(t^{\prime},u),(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})
belong to

and

. The first and third terms are lower-time increments, while the middle term is an upper-time increment at the fixed legal lower time

. Their increment lengths are bounded by
|s-s^{\prime}|
,

and
|s-s^{\prime}|

Assumption

|t-t^{\prime}|^{\theta}N^{\chi_{i;j,k}\theta}

(3.38)

The estimate is an operator-norm estimate before any deterministic input vector is inserted.

For cutoff tails, fix

We now insert the deterministic input and sum (

(3.40)

Using
\|P_{Q}w\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L^{2}}\leq Q^{-\sigma}\|w\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}B^{\sigma}_{2,\infty}}
, the Sobolev block is bounded by

N^{d/2-\Gamma+\varepsilon}M^{s-\lambda}\bigl(M^{d/2+\varepsilon}+Q^{d/2+\varepsilon}\bigr)Q^{-\sigma}\|w\|_{X_{T}^{\sigma}}.

(3.41)

For the Besov source target,

M^{\sigma-\lambda}\|P_{M}B_{N,Q,M}^{i;j,k}P_{Q}w\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;L_{T}^{1}L^{2})}\lesssim_{T}N^{d/2-\Gamma+\varepsilon}M^{\sigma-\lambda}\bigl(M^{d/2+\varepsilon}+Q^{d/2+\varepsilon}\bigr)Q^{-\sigma}\|w\|_{X_{T}^{\sigma}}.

(3.42)

Lemma 3.12

(Dyadic finite-set/tail assembly)
.

Let
\mathfrak{D}
be the countable set of dyadic triples
\mathbf{d}=(N,Q,M)
satisfying
Q\leq c_{\rm ap}N
and
M\lesssim N
. Let
X
and
Y
be Banach spaces, and let
U_{\Lambda,\mathbf{d}}:X\to Y
be finite-rank operators. Assume the following two conditions.

(i)

For each fixed
\mathbf{d}
, the family
U_{\Lambda,\mathbf{d}}
is Cauchy in
\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
as
\Lambda\to\infty
.

(ii)

There exist deterministic numbers
a_{\mathbf{d}}\geq 0
with
\sum_{\mathbf{d}\in\mathfrak{D}}a_{\mathbf{d}}<\infty
such that

\sup_{\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime}}\|U_{\Lambda,\mathbf{d}}-U_{\Lambda^{\prime},\mathbf{d}}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}\leq a_{\mathbf{d}}\qquad\text{for every }\mathbf{d}\in\mathfrak{D}.

Then
\sum_{\mathbf{d}\in\mathfrak{D}}U_{\Lambda,\mathbf{d}}
is Cauchy in
\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
, and the limit is independent of the order in which finite dyadic sets exhaust
\mathfrak{D}
.

For a
B_{2,\infty}^{\zeta}
-target, the same conclusion follows from output-shell majorants. Suppose that the contribution of all triples with output shell
M
is bounded by
b_{M}\geq 0
, with
\sum_{M}b_{M}<\infty
. Then, for
\|x\|_{X}\leq 1
,

\sup_{M\geq M_{0}}M^{\zeta}\left\|P_{M}\sum_{(N,Q):(N,Q,M)\in\mathfrak{D}}U_{\Lambda,N,Q,M}x\right\|_{L_{T}^{1}L^{2}}\leq\sum_{M\geq M_{0}}b_{M}\longrightarrow 0.

(3.43)

The same implications hold in
L^{p}(\Omega)
if
a_{\mathbf{d}}
and
b_{M}
dominate the corresponding
L^{p}(\Omega)
-norms of the block operator differences. If there are random majorants
A_{\mathbf{d}}(\omega)
with
\sum_{\mathbf{d}}A_{\mathbf{d}}(\omega)<\infty
almost surely and

\sup_{\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime}}\|U_{\Lambda,\mathbf{d}}(\omega)-U_{\Lambda^{\prime},\mathbf{d}}(\omega)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}\leq A_{\mathbf{d}}(\omega),

then the same finite-set/tail argument gives almost sure operator-norm Cauchy convergence. In particular, summable
L^{1}(\Omega)
majorants imply this pathwise hypothesis by Fubini.

Proof.

Let
\delta>0
. Choose a finite set
F\subset\mathfrak{D}
such that
\sum_{\mathbf{d}\notin F}a_{\mathbf{d}}<\delta
. The tail satisfies

\left\|\sum_{\mathbf{d}\notin F}(U_{\Lambda,\mathbf{d}}-U_{\Lambda^{\prime},\mathbf{d}})\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}\leq\sum_{\mathbf{d}\notin F}a_{\mathbf{d}}<\delta,

uniformly in
\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime}
. On the finite set
F
, condition (i) gives Cauchy convergence of the finite sum. This proves the Cauchy property. Since the proof uses only tails of an
\ell^{1}
-family, the limit is unconditional with respect to the chosen finite exhaustion.

For the Besov target, the norm is the supremum over output shells after multiplication by the weight
M^{\zeta}
. Estimate (
3.43
) is just the inequality
\sup_{M\geq M_{0}}x_{M}\leq\sum_{M\geq M_{0}}x_{M}
for non-negative shell bounds. Thus an
\ell^{1}_{M}
output tail gives norm Cauchy convergence in
B_{2,\infty}^{\zeta}
. A uniform shell bound
\sup_{M}b_{M}<\infty
would give boundedness of each output shell but would not force the high-output tail to vanish.

The
L^{p}
-version is the same deterministic proof applied to
L^{p}(\Omega)
-norms, using Minkowski’s inequality for finite sums and the
\ell^{1}
tail. The almost sure version is pathwise. If
\sum_{\mathbf{d}}\mathbb{E}A_{\mathbf{d}}<\infty
, then Fubini gives
\sum_{\mathbf{d}}A_{\mathbf{d}}(\omega)<\infty
for almost every
\omega
; applying the deterministic finite-set/tail proof on that event proves the assertion.
∎

Sobolev target.

We prove the dyadic summability in detail, because this is the only place where the spatial dimension enters after the tensor estimate. Write

\lambda=\lambda_{i},\qquad\Gamma=\Gamma_{i;j,k},\qquad\beta=s-\lambda.

The centered window gives

\Gamma>\frac{d}{2}+10\varepsilon,\qquad s<\lambda+\Gamma-d-10\varepsilon,\qquad\max\{0,d-\Gamma\}+10\varepsilon<\sigma<\lambda+\Gamma-d-10\varepsilon.

(3.44)

The first inequality gives the summability of the bare high-loop factor
N^{d/2-\Gamma}
, with room for the logarithmic and time-lift losses.

Consider first the
M^{d/2}
-part of (
3.41
). Since
\sigma>0
, the dyadic sum in
Q\leq c_{\rm ap}N
contributes at most a logarithmic factor, absorbed by
N^{\varepsilon}
. The sum in
M\lesssim N
is

\sum_{M\lesssim N}M^{s-\lambda+d/2+\varepsilon}\lesssim\begin{cases}1,&s-\lambda+d/2<-2\varepsilon,\\
N^{s-\lambda+d/2+2\varepsilon},&s-\lambda+d/2\geq-2\varepsilon.\end{cases}

If the first case occurs, the remaining high-shell power is
N^{d/2-\Gamma+O(\varepsilon)}
, which is summable because
\Gamma>d/2
. In the second case the high-shell power is

N^{d/2-\Gamma}N^{s-\lambda+d/2+O(\varepsilon)}=N^{s-\lambda+d-\Gamma+O(\varepsilon)},

which is summable by the upper bound
s<\lambda+\Gamma-d-10\varepsilon
. Thus the
M^{d/2}
-branch maps into
C_{T}H^{s-\lambda}
.

For the
Q^{d/2}
-part of (
3.41
), we use the positive-part form of the same calculation:

\sum_{M\lesssim N}M^{s-\lambda+\varepsilon}\lesssim N^{(s-\lambda)_{+}+2\varepsilon},\qquad\sum_{Q\leq c_{\rm ap}N}Q^{d/2-\sigma+\varepsilon}\lesssim N^{(d/2-\sigma)_{+}+2\varepsilon}.

(3.45)

The high-shell exponent is therefore

d/2-\Gamma+(s-\lambda)_{+}+(d/2-\sigma)_{+}+O(\varepsilon).

(3.46)

We check that it is negative under (
3.44
). If
\Gamma\leq d
, then
s<\lambda+\Gamma-d\leq\lambda
, hence
(s-\lambda)_{+}=0
, and
\sigma>d-\Gamma
. When
\sigma<d/2
, this gives

d/2-\Gamma+d/2-\sigma=d-\Gamma-\sigma<0;

when
\sigma\geq d/2
, the exponent is at most
d/2-\Gamma<0
. If
\Gamma>d
, then
(s-\lambda)_{+}<\Gamma-d
and
\sigma>0
. If
\sigma<d/2
, the exponent is strictly smaller than

d/2-\Gamma+(\Gamma-d)+d/2-\sigma=-\sigma<0;

if
\sigma\geq d/2
, it is smaller than
-d/2
. The strict losses absorb the logarithmic factors. This proves the centered bound into
C_{T}H^{s-\lambda_{i}}
.

Besov source target.

We next prove the
L_{T}^{1}B^{\sigma-\lambda}_{2,\infty}
estimate. For each output scale
M
, let

denote the deterministic majorant obtained from (
3.42

N\gtrsim M
and

with the Besov weight
M^{\sigma-\lambda}
. We prove more than a uniform bound: the strict window gives
\sum_{M}C_{M}<\infty
. This summability is what justifies taking the

-norm of the

supremum, since

\left\|\sup_{M}X_{M}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\left\|\sum_{M}X_{M}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\sum_{M}\|X_{M}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

for nonnegative shell norms
X_{M}
.

For the
M^{d/2}
-part, the
Q
-sum contributes only a logarithmic or bounded factor because
\sigma>0
. Since
\Gamma>d/2
,

M^{\sigma-\lambda+d/2+\varepsilon}\sum_{N\gtrsim M}N^{d/2-\Gamma+\varepsilon}\lesssim M^{\sigma-\lambda+d-\Gamma+O(\varepsilon)}.

(3.47)

The exponent is negative by
\sigma<\lambda+\Gamma-d-10\varepsilon
, so this branch is uniformly bounded and has a high-output tail.

For the
Q^{d/2}
-part, there are two cases. If
\sigma<d/2
, then

\sum_{Q\leq c_{\rm ap}N}Q^{d/2-\sigma+\varepsilon}\lesssim N^{d/2-\sigma+2\varepsilon}.

The
N
-tail is summable because
\sigma>d-\Gamma+10\varepsilon
in this case, and it gives

M^{\sigma-\lambda}\sum_{N\gtrsim M}N^{d-\Gamma-\sigma+O(\varepsilon)}\lesssim M^{d-\Gamma-\lambda+O(\varepsilon)}.

(3.48)

The last exponent is negative because the upper end of the window is positive, hence
\lambda+\Gamma>d
. If
\sigma\geq d/2
, then the inner
Q
-sum costs only a logarithm and

M^{\sigma-\lambda}\sum_{N\gtrsim M}N^{d/2-\Gamma+O(\varepsilon)}\lesssim M^{\sigma-\lambda+d/2-\Gamma+O(\varepsilon)}.

(3.49)

This is even better than required, because
\sigma<\lambda+\Gamma-d
implies

\sigma-\lambda+d/2-\Gamma<-d/2.

The estimates (
3.47
), (
3.48
) and (
3.49
) are negative powers of

, and the displayed

domination gives both the
L^{p}
-bound of the Besov supremum and the high-output tail needed for cutoff convergence. Thus (
2.51
) follows.

Cutoff convergence and completion of the proof.

Let
\mathfrak{D}
denote the countable set of dyadic triples
(N,Q,M)
satisfying

and

. For each

F
is eventually independent of
\Lambda

after

to the dyadic series

We now prove almost sure convergence. Run the block estimates above with a preliminary loss

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\|B_{N,Q,M}^{i;j,k}\|_{C_{t,s}\mathcal{L}(\ell_{q}^{2},\ell_{n}^{2})}>N^{\rho_{*}}Q^{\rho_{*}}M^{\rho_{*}}A_{N,Q,M}^{i;j,k}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}N^{-p\rho_{*}}Q^{-p\rho_{*}}M^{-p\rho_{*}},

where

The dyadic index set is countable, and the last probability bound is summable over

\|B_{N,Q,M}^{i;j,k}\|_{C_{t,s}\mathcal{L}(\ell_{q}^{2},\ell_{n}^{2})}\leq C_{\omega}N^{\rho_{*}}Q^{\rho_{*}}M^{\rho_{*}}A_{N,Q,M}^{i;j,k}

(3.50)

for all dyadic triples and all labels in the finite family. Since

Measurability follows from the finite-time skeleton construction. For every rational pair

|H_{\nu}(\ell,\mu,r,\lambda,q,n;t,s)|\lesssim G_{\tau}(N)

(3.51)

Third, Proposition

\Bigl\|\,\|B_{\Lambda,N,Q,M}^{\tau}(t,s)\|_{\ell_{q}^{2}\to\ell_{n}^{2}}\Bigr\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim_{p,\varepsilon}N^{d/2+\varepsilon}G_{\tau}(N)\bigl(M^{d/2+\varepsilon}+Q^{d/2+\varepsilon}\bigr).

(3.52)

The logarithmic factor in Proposition
3.8

(3.53)

\|P_{Q}w\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L^{2}}\leq Q^{-\sigma}\|w\|_{X_{T}^{\sigma}}

\|P_{M}B_{N,Q,M}^{\tau}P_{Q}w\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;C_{T}L^{2})}\lesssim N^{d/2+\varepsilon}G_{\tau}(N)\bigl(M^{d/2+\varepsilon}+Q^{d/2+\varepsilon}\bigr)Q^{-\sigma}\|w\|_{X_{T}^{\sigma}}.

Verification and proof summary

This section records deterministic checks used by the theorem and summarizes the proof.

4.1
Volterra verification for distinct speeds

The following proposition checks the retained-diagonal criterion for a distinct-speed wave or Klein–Gordon diagonal.

Proposition 4.1

(Distinct-speed wave/Klein–Gordon diagonals)
.

Let
\tau=(i;j,j)
and work in the standard high-frequency wave/Klein–Gordon normalization: the outer Duhamel factor has gain
\lambda_{i}=1
, the stochastic convolution leg has gain
\alpha_{j}=1
, and hence
\Gamma_{i;j,j}=3
in Assumption
2.1
. Assume that, at high frequency,

\omega_{a}(n)=(m_{a}^{2}+c_{a}^{2}|n|^{2})^{1/2},\qquad m_{a}>0,\qquad c_{i}\neq c_{j}.

Suppose that

K_{i}(t,n)=\frac{\sin(t\omega_{i}(n))}{\omega_{i}(n)}

and that
\Psi_{j}
is the corresponding stochastic convolution, with any additional same-color covariance weight satisfying the dyadic bound encoded by
\kappa_{jj}
. Then the same-color diagonal kernel satisfies Assumption
2.5
on a sufficiently small proportional aperture
|q|\leq c_{\rm ap}N
with phase gain
\vartheta=1
. More precisely, after the trigonometric expansion the principal phases are

\Phi_{\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}}(q,\ell)=\epsilon_{1}\omega_{i}(q+\ell)+\epsilon_{2}\omega_{j}(\ell),\qquad\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}\in\{+1,-1\},

(4.1)

and

|\Phi_{\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}}(q,\ell)|\gtrsim N,\qquad|\ell|\sim N,\qquad|q|\leq c_{\rm ap}N.

(4.2)

Consequently, in this normalization, a retained same-color diagonal has loop gain

\Delta<\kappa_{jj}+1+\Gamma_{i;j,j}-d=\kappa_{jj}+4-d.

(4.3)

In the three-dimensional equal wave-gain case
\Gamma_{i;j,j}=3
and
\kappa_{jj}=0
, any
\Delta<1
is available.

Proof.

The covariance of the wave/Klein–Gordon stochastic convolution is, for
\omega_{j}=\omega_{j}(\ell)
,

\displaystyle\sigma_{j}(\ell;s,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{s}\frac{\sin((s-r)\omega_{j})}{\omega_{j}}\frac{\sin((t-r)\omega_{j})}{\omega_{j}}\,\,\mathrm{d}r

(4.4)

\displaystyle=\frac{s}{2\omega_{j}^{2}}\cos((t-s)\omega_{j})+\frac{\sin((t-s)\omega_{j})-\sin((t+s)\omega_{j})}{4\omega_{j}^{3}}.

The last two terms in (
4.4
) are absolutely bounded by
\lesssim N^{-3}
, uniformly for
0\leq s\leq t\leq T_{0}
. After multiplication by the Duhamel factor
\omega_{i}(q+\ell)^{-1}\sim N^{-1}
and by a covariance weight
N^{-\kappa_{jj}}
, their high-loop contribution is
\lesssim N^{d-4-\kappa_{jj}}
, which is included in the absolutely summable remainder whenever the retained gain is chosen below the available loop exponent.

For the principal term, expanding
\sin((t-s)\omega_{i}(q+\ell))\cos((t-s)\omega_{j}(\ell))
into exponentials gives the phases (
4.1
). The amplitude has the form

a(q,\ell;s,t)=c_{0}s\,\omega_{i}(q+\ell)^{-1}\omega_{j}(\ell)^{-2}

up to constants and signs independent of the dyadic scales. Hence, before inserting the same-color covariance weight,

|a(q,\ell;s,t)|+|\partial_{s}a(q,\ell;s,t)|\lesssim_{T}N^{-3}=N^{-\Gamma_{i;j,j}}

under the standard wave-gain normalization
\lambda_{i}=\alpha_{j}=1
. Multiplication by a channel weight bounded by
N^{-\kappa_{jj}}
gives the full amplitude bound
N^{-\Gamma_{i;j,j}-\kappa_{jj}}
required in (
2.38
). It remains to prove the phase lower bound. The sum phases satisfy
|\omega_{i}(q+\ell)+\omega_{j}(\ell)|\gtrsim N
. For the difference phase, using
\omega_{a}(n)=c_{a}|n|+O(|n|^{-1})
and the mean value theorem,

|\omega_{i}(q+\ell)-\omega_{j}(\ell)|\geq|c_{i}-c_{j}|\,|\ell|-C(1+c_{i})|q|-CN^{-1}.

Choosing
c_{\rm ap}
sufficiently small in terms of
c_{i},c_{j},m_{i},m_{j}
gives (
4.2
) for all large
N
. The finitely many low dyadic shells are not treated by a phase denominator; their contribution is absorbed into the finite low-frequency constant in the deterministic diagonal estimate. Thus
\vartheta=1
, and the loop gain condition in Assumption
2.5
becomes
\kappa_{jj}+1+\Gamma_{i;j,j}-d\geq\Delta
.
∎

Remark 4.2

(Retained orbit diagonals)
.

Proposition
4.1
gives the raw retained estimate for one diagonal branch. If a finite decomposition
D_{\Lambda}=\sum_{\nu}D_{\Lambda,\nu}
contains branches that become summable only after a model-specific orbit summation, one first identifies a finite orbit

4.3
General Gaussian time profiles

The frequency-envelope theorem uses a common Fourier-diagonal isonormal representation for several time covariance structures:

\widehat{\Psi}_{a}(n,t)=\mathbb{W}(h_{a,n,t})

with dyadic amplitude and increment bounds. Equivalently, such profiles may be written as

\widehat{\Psi}_{a}(n,t)=\int_{\Theta_{a}}m_{a}(n,t,\theta)\,\,\mathrm{d}W_{a,n}(\theta),

(4.5)

provided the covariance remains supported on
n+m=0
and satisfies the finite local positivity assumptions of Section
2.2
. A stochastic convolution driven by space-time noise is obtained by taking
\Theta_{a}=[0,T_{0}]
and

h_{a,n,t}(r)=\mathbf{1}_{0\leq r\leq t}S_{a}(t-r,n)b_{a}(n),\qquad\mathbb{W}(h_{a,n,t})=\int_{0}^{t}S_{a}(t-r,n)b_{a}(n)\,\,\mathrm{d}\beta_{a,n}(r),

so its covariance is Volterra in time. Colored-in-time forcing corresponds to replacing the Lebesgue covariance in
r
by a nontrivial covariance kernel; the centered proof only uses the resulting envelopes
A_{a}(N)
and oscillation scales
\Theta_{a}(N)
.

Random initial data give a different, finite-rank time covariance. For a first-order dispersive channel one may take

\widehat{\Psi}_{a}(n,t)=e^{\mathrm{i}t\phi_{a}(n)}a_{a}(n)g_{a,n},\qquad\mathbb{E}[g_{a,n}g_{b,m}]=\mathbf{1}_{n+m=0}R_{ab}(n).

(4.6)

Then
h_{a,n,t}=e^{\mathrm{i}t\phi_{a}(n)}a_{a}(n)e_{a,n}
, and the increment bound follows from

|e^{\mathrm{i}t\phi_{a}(n)}-e^{\mathrm{i}t^{\prime}\phi_{a}(n)}|\lesssim|t-t^{\prime}|^{\theta}|\phi_{a}(n)|^{\theta}.

Wave or Klein–Gordon random initial data are finite sums of the same form after decomposing into sine and cosine profiles. Thus stochastic convolutions, colored-time inputs and random initial data are handled by the same local Gaussian Hilbert-space reduction once their envelopes are verified. Frequency-dependent amplitudes such as
A_{a}(N)=N^{-\alpha_{a}}L_{a}(N)
, with slowly varying or band-dependent factors, enter only through the product

G_{\tau}(N)=K_{i}(N)A_{j}(N)A_{k}(N)

and through the two envelope summability conditions.

4.4
Proof summary

The argument consists of two independent estimates joined by the finite algebraic identity

T^{\tau,\mathfrak{P}}_{\Lambda}=\mathsf{R}^{\tau}_{\Lambda}+B^{\tau}_{\Lambda}.

For the centered component, the finite Wick split removes the scalar contraction, and local covariance normalization gives a second Gaussian tensor supported on

n=q+\ell+r.

The finite tensor estimate, the eight incidence flattenings and the Banach-valued time lift give a dyadic operator bound for
B^{\tau}_{N,Q,M}
. The Sobolev and Besov summations then yield

B^{\tau}:X_{T}^{\sigma}\longrightarrow C_{T}H^{s-\lambda_{i}}\cap L_{T}^{1}B^{\sigma-\lambda_{i}}_{2,\infty}

under the centered window. For a nonzero retained diagonal, the estimate is on
E^{2,\sigma}_{T,\mathcal{M}}
; its time-derivative component is used in the Volterra integration by parts for the deterministic retained diagonal.

For the diagonal component, the contraction relation
\ell+r=0
gives
n=q
. The retained part is therefore a scalar Volterra multiplier in the low Fourier label. The deterministic interface is (
2.33
): either the retained scalar kernel satisfies the dyadic bound directly, or an orbit sum determined by the scalar expansion is formed first and that orbit sum is estimated. Full Wick subtraction is the case
\mathsf{R}^{\tau}_{\Lambda}=0
. Once the retained diagonal converges, the assembled convergence follows from the displayed finite identity and the centered convergence.

The frequency-envelope theorem keeps the finite algebra unchanged and changes the summation layer. The Wick split, local covariance reduction and tensor estimate are the same after replacing
N^{-\Gamma_{\tau}}
by
G_{\tau}(N)=K_{i}(N)A_{j}(N)A_{k}(N)
. The Sobolev majorant and the
\ell^{1}_{M}
Besov output-tail majorant are the deterministic tails used in the finite-set argument. In the three-dimensional equal-gain specialization they give

s_{2}<4\alpha-3,\qquad 3-3\alpha<\sigma<4\alpha-3,

with strict losses.

4.5
Further problems

The deterministic diagonal resolution leads to a finite scalar classification problem: for a given coupling graph, Fourier-diagonal covariance and dispersion relation, determine which generated diagonals are absolutely summable, which admit a phase denominator or an orbit-sum estimate, and which are assigned to the counterterm branch.

Two extensions concern the centered part. Higher-order expansions would replace the second homogeneous chaos by a top chaos together with lower pairings, requiring higher-order operator-valued tensor estimates and an induction over lower-chaos diagonal interfaces. A separate extension is to replace the absolute Sobolev–Besov summation by a modulation, Bourgain, or
U^{p}/V^{p}
summation. In that case the finite Wick split and local covariance reduction remain unchanged, while the final assembly is supplied by the chosen time-frequency estimate. Endpoint questions for
\Gamma_{\tau}=d/2
,
\sigma=d-\Gamma_{\tau}
, and
\sigma=\lambda_{i}+\Gamma_{\tau}-d
also belong to this summation layer.
