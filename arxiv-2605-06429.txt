𝖦𝖫 N ​ ( ℂ ) \mathsf{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C}) BROWNIAN MOTION AND STOCHASTIC PDE ON ENTIRE FUNCTIONS
THEODOROS ASSIOTIS AND ZAHRA SADAT MIRSAJJADI

Abstract
We construct the full edge scaling limit of the singular values of Brownian motion on the general linear group 𝖦𝖫 N ​ ( ℂ ) \mathsf{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C}) starting from general conditions. We show that the limiting paths solve an infinite system of SDE with log-interaction and have a Gibbs resampling property with exponential Brownian bridges. Moreover, we show that the evolution of the limiting rescaled reverse characteristic polynomial solves a stochastic partial differential equation with a non-linear multiplicative noise and linear drift. From a special initial condition the resulting line ensemble coincides, in logarithmic coordinates, with a line ensemble constructed by Ahn which arises as a universal scaling limit of singular values of products of random matrices. We prove some analogous results on the evolution of limiting characteristic polynomials for two models whose stationary measures are given by the Hua-Pickrell and Bessel stochastic zeta functions respectively.

We need to introduce some notation and terminology. First define,

\mathbf{W}_{N;+}\left\{\mathbf{x}=(x_{i})_{i=1}^{N}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:x_{1}\geq x_{2}\geq\cdots\geq x_{N}\geq 0\right\},

and write

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}_{\infty;+}=\left\{\mathbf{x}=(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}:x_{1}\geq x_{2}\geq x_{3}\geq\cdots\geq 0\right\},

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}_{\infty;+}^{\circ}=\left\{\mathbf{x}=(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}:x_{1}>x_{2}>x_{3}>\cdots>0\right\}.

The following infinite-dimensional spaces, in some sense extended versions of the

Definition 1.1
.

We define the following spaces:

\displaystyle\Upsilon

\displaystyle=\left\{\boldsymbol{\upsilon}=(\mathbf{x}^{+},\mathbf{x}^{-},\gamma,\delta)\in\mathbf{W}_{\infty;+}\times\mathbf{W}_{\infty;+}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}:\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(x_{i}^{+})^{2}+(x_{i}^{-})^{2}\leq\delta\right\},

(1)

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{+}

\displaystyle=\left\{\boldsymbol{v}=(\mathbf{x},\gamma)\in\mathbf{W}_{\infty;+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}:\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}x_{i}\leq\gamma\right\},

(2)

endowed with product topology as subsets of

\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}

Note that,
\Upsilon,\Upsilon_{+}
are locally compact Polish spaces. These spaces are in fact intimately related to a distinguished class of entire functions called the Laguerre-Polya class. These entire functions are exactly the ones that arise as uniform limits in
\mathbb{C}
of polynomials with only real (or only non-negative real) zeroes. Given
\boldsymbol{v}\in\Upsilon_{+}
and
\boldsymbol{u}\in\Upsilon

\displaystyle\mathfrak{E}_{\boldsymbol{v}}^{+}(z)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-(\gamma-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}x_{i})z}\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(1-zx_{i}\right),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{E}_{\boldsymbol{u}}(z)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-\gamma z-(\delta-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(x_{i}^{+})^{2}+(x_{i}^{-})^{2})z^{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}(1-zx_{i}^{+})\mathrm{e}^{zx_{i}^{+}}\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}(1+zx_{i}^{-})\mathrm{e}^{-zx_{i}^{-}}.

These functions are well-defined by virtue of the inequalities in the definitions of
\Upsilon

\Upsilon_{+}
in (

) and (

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\mathsf{Y}_{N}^{\theta}(t)=\mathsf{Y}_{N}^{\theta}(t)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{B}_{N}(t)+\theta\mathsf{Y}_{N}^{\theta}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{x}_{i}^{(N)}(t)=2\mathfrak{x}_{i}^{(N)}(t)\,\mathrm{d}\mathsf{w}_{i}(t)+2\mathfrak{x}_{i}^{(N)}(t)\left((1+\theta)+\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^{N}\frac{\mathfrak{x}_{i}^{(N)}(t)+\mathfrak{x}_{j}^{(N)}(t)}{\mathfrak{x}_{i}^{(N)}(t)-\mathfrak{x}_{j}^{(N)}(t)}\right)\mathrm{d}t,\ \ i\in\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket,

with the

Theorem 1.4

we have, as

\left((N^{-1}\mathsf{x}_{i}^{(N)}(\bullet))_{i\in\mathbb{N}},N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathsf{x}_{i}^{(N)}(\bullet)\right)\overset{\mathrm{d}}{\longrightarrow}\left((\mathsf{x}_{i}(\bullet))_{i\in\mathbb{N}},\boldsymbol{\gamma}(\bullet)\right)\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=}\mathbf{X}_{GL}^{\bullet}

where

, and

Furthermore, for any
\gamma\geq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}x_{i}

The dynamical Cauchy model

Assumption 1.7

We assume that the ordered zeroes

\mathfrak{hp}_{t}
satisfy almost surely for all

\cdots<\mathfrak{y}_{-3}(t)<\mathfrak{y}_{-2}(t)<\mathfrak{y}_{-1}(t)<0<\mathfrak{y}_{1}(t)<\mathfrak{y}_{2}(t)<\mathfrak{y}_{3}(t)<\cdots,

with no explosions to
\pm\infty

We believe, but cannot prove, that Assumption
1.7

Let us write
(\mathsf{y}_{i}(t))_{i\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\},t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
for the reciprocals of the zeros of

\mathsf{y}_{i}(t)\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=}\frac{1}{\mathfrak{y}_{i}(t)},\ \ \forall t\geq 0,\ i\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}.

(21)

We then have the following equations describing their dynamics.

Proposition 1.8
.

Let
\mathfrak{s}\in\mathbb{C}
.
Under Assumption
1.7
for the zeroes of
(\mathfrak{hp}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\mathsf{y}_{i}(t)=\sqrt{2}\mathsf{y}_{i}(t)\mathrm{d}\mathsf{w}_{i}(t)-2\left(\Re{(\mathfrak{s})}+1\right)\mathsf{y}_{i}(t)\mathrm{d}t+2\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0,i\}}\frac{\mathsf{y}_{j}^{2}(t)}{\mathsf{y}_{i}(t)-\mathsf{y}_{j}(t)}\mathrm{d}t+2\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{HP}(t)\mathrm{d}t,i\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\},

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{HP}(t)=-\partial_{z}\mathrm{d}\mathsf{M}_{t}(z;\mathfrak{hp})\big|_{z=0}+2\left(\Im{(\mathfrak{s})}-\Re{(\mathfrak{s})}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{HP}(t)\right)\mathrm{d}t.

For
\mathfrak{s}\in\mathbb{R}
,
\mathfrak{s}>-1/2

from the invariant measure, for any fixed time
t\geq 0

has the same distribution as the principal value sum of the
\mathsf{y}_{i}

\mathsf{y}_{i}

Conjecture 1.9
.

Let
\mathfrak{s}>-\tfrac{1}{2}
and
\mathfrak{hp}_{0}\overset{\mathrm{d}}{=}\mathbb{HP}_{\mathfrak{s}}
. Then, the reciprocals
(\mathsf{y}_{i}(\bullet))_{i\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}}
of zeroes of
\mathfrak{hp}_{\bullet}
satisfy the equation

\mathrm{d}\mathsf{y}_{i}(t)=\sqrt{2}\mathsf{y}_{i}(t)\mathrm{d}\mathsf{w}_{i}(t)-2\mathfrak{s}\mathsf{y}_{i}(t)\mathrm{d}t+2\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\mathsf{y}_{j}(t)|>\varepsilon\\
j\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0,i\}\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathsf{y}_{i}(t)\mathsf{y}_{j}(t)}{\mathsf{y}_{i}(t)-\mathsf{y}_{j}(t)}\mathrm{d}t,\ \ \forall{i}\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}.

It is plausible that the conjecture is true also from general initial conditions which qualitatively look like the equilibrium measure. For initial conditions far from equilibrium or for non-real parameter

1.2

On the proof

Let us finally comment on the proof. The starting point in our analysis is the observation that singular values of multiplicative Brownian motion on
\mathsf{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
satisfy a certain consistency relation as
N
varies which allows us to make use of the method of intertwiners of Borodin and Olshanski
[
27
,
79
,
80
]
. This is a general algebraic formalism which constructs an abstract Feller-Markov process on the boundary of a projective system from a tower of consistent Feller processes on each layer, see
[
27
,
80
,
12
]
for detailed expositions. The method was initially applied to discrete dynamics on partitions
[
27
,
28
,
25
,
78
,
80
,
79
]
and more recently it was realised that it can be successfully applied to random matrix dynamics
[
16
,
12
,
35
,
34
]
. Once this abstract process is constructed, the difficulty becomes describing it explicitly. In the random matrix context this was first achieved in
[
12
]
for the Rider-Valkó model (
13
) and in this paper we go one step further for Brownian motion on
\mathsf{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
for which an additional property in Gibbs resampling can be established. The description of the dynamics of the limiting rescaled characteristic polynomials in terms of a closed spde is new for all three models we consider in this paper.

Ahn in his proof of convergence of (
12
) with the special initial condition of the identity matrix for the
\mathsf{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
Brownian motion uses the Gibbs property (also present for general initial conditions) and explicit computations for the Laplace transform of the finite-dimensional distributions of (
12
) and associated determinantal correlation kernel. The correlation kernel from the identity is quite special as one can relate it, see
[
60
,
64
,
96
]
, to the correlation kernel of standard Dyson Brownian motion from equidistant initial condition
[
57
,
58
,
63
]
which is very amenable to asymptotic analysis
[
2
,
86
]
. In fact, it is an open problem to obtain the limit of the correlations from other initial conditions. Our proof, on the other hand requires minimal computations but granted it relies on the machinery built in
[
12
]
which is used as a black box.

Regarding obtaining the singular log-interacting SDE (
8
) for the dynamics of
(\mathsf{x}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
we do that via the equation for
(\mathfrak{gl}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
or more precisely its logarithmic derivative. Schematically, we follow

\textnormal{Eq. }\eqref{GLequation}\textnormal{ for }\mathfrak{gl}_{t}\overset{\psi^{GL}_{t}(z)=\partial_{z}\log\mathfrak{gl}_{t}(z)}{\longrightarrow}\textnormal{Eq. }\eqref{psiEqu}\textnormal{ for }\mathfrak{\psi}_{t}^{GL}\overset{\textnormal{Non-col.}\&\textnormal{Res.calc.}}{\longrightarrow}\textnormal{Eq. }\eqref{ISDEGL}\textnormal{ for }(\mathsf{x}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}.

(22)

A crucial non-intersection property of the paths is obtained from certain Lyapunov functions exactly as in
[
12
]
or in principle the Gibbs resampling could be used, see Remarks
2.6
and
2.12
for some subtleties. Our computations, based on residue calculus considerations (by virtue of non-intersection we can pick out individual poles), in following the scheme in display (
22
) are inspired by the remarkable work
[
53
]
which realises the Airy line ensemble
[
38
]
as the poles of a stochastic evolution on Nevanlinna functions. Alternatively we could have derived the desired equations as a limit of the finite-dimensional dynamics as we did in
[
12
]
for the Rider-Valkó model (
13
). The same argument could be applied here, see Section 4 therein. However, deriving them already in the limit directly from the bigger object
(\mathfrak{rv}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is conceptually more attractive and showcases the usefulness of having an equation like (

Infinite-dimensional dynamics from Brownian motion on
\mathsf{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})

2.1

Convergence on path space and non-intersection

In this subsection we apply our general convergence framework from
[
12
]
to the model (
7
). The material here is in some sense standard as virtually identical arguments, for the individual intermediate results, have appeared before and so we will be brief. The main contribution is the non-trivial observation that this framework can also be applied to Brownian motion on
\mathsf{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
.

We need some standard preliminaries. We refer to Section 2 of
[
12
]
for more details. The parameter
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
will be fixed throughout this section and will be suppressed from the notation. Consider the following geometric Brownian motion
(\boldsymbol{g}_{N}(t))_{t\geq 0}
, the unique strong solution in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
of the SDE

\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{g}_{N}(t)=\boldsymbol{g}_{N}(t)\mathrm{d}\mathsf{w}(t)+\left(1+\frac{\theta}{2}-N\right)\boldsymbol{g}_{N}(t)\mathrm{d}t.

(23)

Its transition density with respect to Lebesgue measure is given by, for
t,x,y>0
,

\mathsf{q}_{t}^{(N)}(x,y)=\frac{1}{y\sqrt{2\pi t}}\exp\left(-\frac{\left(\log y-\log x-\left(\frac{1+\theta}{2}-N\right)t\right)^{2}}{2t}\right).

(24)

We collect the following basic facts about the model (
7
).

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
. The SDE (
7
) has a unique strong solution from any initial condition in
\mathbf{W}_{N;+}
. Moreover, for
\mathsf{x}^{(N)}(0)\in\mathbf{W}_{N;+}^{\circ}
we have almost surely for all
t\geq 0
,
\mathsf{x}^{(N)}(t)\in\mathbf{W}_{N;+}^{\circ}
. Furthermore, the semigroup
(\mathsf{P}_{t;N})_{t\geq 0}
associated to
\mathsf{x}^{(N)}
is
\mathrm{C}_{0}
-Feller continuous on
\mathbf{W}_{N;+}
and the space of smooth symmetric functions with compact support on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
restricted to
\mathbf{W}_{N;+}
forms a core for the generator
\mathsf{L}_{N}
of
(\mathsf{P}_{t;N})_{t\geq 0}
and is moreover invariant under the action of
\mathsf{L}_{N}
. Finally, the transition kernel of
\mathsf{P}_{t;N}
is given explicitly by, for
t>0,\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{W}_{N;+}^{\circ},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbf{W}_{N;+}
,

\mathsf{P}_{t;N}(\mathbf{x},\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y})=\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_{N}t}\frac{\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}(y_{i}-y_{j})}{\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}(x_{i}-x_{j})}\det\left(\mathsf{q}_{t}^{(N)}(x_{i},y_{j})\right)_{i,j=1}^{N}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y},

(25)

where
\lambda_{N}=N(N-1)(3\theta+2-4N)/12
.

Proof.

This is rather standard and an identical result (modulo a different one-dimensional transition kernel in place of
\mathsf{q}_{t}^{(N)}
and constant
\lambda_{N}
) and proof holds for the Rider-Valkó model (
13
), see Section 3 of
[
12
]
for details. The only model specific computation is the following Doob
h
-transform structure of the generator:

\displaystyle[\mathsf{L}_{N}f](x_{1},\dots,x_{N})

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{-1}\left[\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathsf{G}_{x_{i}}^{(N)}\right)\left(f(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}(x_{i}-x_{j})\right)\right]

\displaystyle-\lambda_{N}f(x_{1},\dots,x_{N}),

where
\mathsf{G}_{x}^{(N)}=\frac{x^{2}}{2}\partial_{x}^{2}+(1+\frac{\theta}{2}-N)\partial_{x}
is the generator of (
23
).
∎

Remark 2.2
.

By virtue of the connection of (
5
) to (radial) Heckman-Opdam processes
[
93
]
the proposition above could in principle be derived by translating the results of
[
93
]
to this setting after exponentiation of the coordinates.

Moving on, for each
N\in\mathbb{N}
, define the corners maps
\mathsf{Corners}_{N}^{N+1}
from
(N+1)\times(N+1)
matrices to
N\times N
matrices by
\mathsf{Corners}_{N}^{N+1}([\mathbf{H}_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{N+1})=[\mathbf{H}_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{N}

and

we let

be the

diagonal matrix with entries
\mathbf{x}
. Define the Markov kernels
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{N}^{N+1}(\mathbf{x},\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y})
from

to
\mathbf{W}_{N;+}
as the law of the eigenvalues of the

matrix
\mathsf{Corners}_{N}^{N+1}(\mathbf{U}^{\dagger}\mathrm{diag}(\mathbf{x})\mathbf{U})
, where
\mathbf{U}
is a random Haar distributed
N\times N

\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{W}_{N+1;+}^{\circ}

\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{N}^{N+1}(\mathbf{x},\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y})

\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{N}^{N+1}(\mathbf{x},\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y})=\frac{N!\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}(y_{i}-y_{j})}{\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N+1}(x_{i}-x_{j})}\mathbf{1}_{\mathbf{y}\prec\mathbf{x}}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y},

With all this notation in place we can state the key consistency relation of the dynamics (

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, we have the intertwining,

\mathsf{P}_{t;N+1}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{N}^{N+1}=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{N}^{N+1}\mathsf{P}_{t;N},\ \forall t\geq 0,\ \forall N\in\mathbb{N}.

(27)

Proof.

This is implicit in
[
14
]
and could also be derived from intertwining results
[
85
]
for Brownian motions with drifts after a change of variables. The most direct way to prove it is the following: use the explicit determinant expressions (
25
) and (
26
) for
\mathsf{P}_{t;N},\mathsf{P}_{t;N+1}
and
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{N}^{N+1}
(valid for non-coinciding coordinates, then extend by continuity using the Feller property; for the Feller property of

see
[
12
]
), a determinant representation for
\mathbf{1}_{\mathbf{y}\prec\mathbf{x}}

[

, apply the Andreief identity for integrals of products of determinants and some elementary linear algebraic manipulations to both sides of (

\mathsf{q}_{t}^{(N)}(x,y)=-\mathrm{e}^{-(\theta/2-N)t}\int_{y}^{\infty}\partial_{x}\mathsf{q}_{t}^{(N+1)}(x,z)\mathrm{d}z,

and also observing

Proposition 2.4

\left((N^{-1}\mathsf{x}_{i}^{(N)}(\bullet))_{i\in\mathbb{N}},N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathsf{x}_{i}^{(N)}(\bullet)\right)\overset{\mathrm{d}}{\longrightarrow}\left((\mathsf{x}_{i}(\bullet))_{i\in\mathbb{N}},\boldsymbol{\gamma}(\bullet)\right)=\mathbf{X}_{GL}^{\bullet}

2.2

Gibbs property

In this section we prove that the paths of
(\mathsf{x}_{i}(\bullet))_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
from general initial condition satisfy a Gibbs resampling property, see the seminal work
[
38
]
, with respect to the law of exponential Brownian bridges. It is more convenient to work in logarithmic coordinates and we will equivalently prove that
(\log\mathsf{x}_{i}(\bullet))_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
has the Brownian Gibbs property
[
38
]
.

The results and arguments in this section are not particularly surprising. However, the general framework of considering Gibbs resampling properties with respect to essentially arbitrary diffusion bridges in a unified way should be useful for future applications to other examples of random matrix dynamics.

We will work with line-ensembles with paths taking values in
\mathbb{R}
for simplicity but the arguments below can be extended to any interval
(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})\subseteq\mathbb{R}
. So let us define,

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}_{\infty;+}=\left\{\mathbf{x}=(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}:x_{1}\geq x_{2}\geq x_{3}\geq\cdots\geq 0\right\},

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}_{\infty;+}^{\circ}=\left\{\mathbf{x}=(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}:x_{1}>x_{2}>x_{3}>\cdots>0\right\}.

The basic object will be a time-homogeneous one-dimensional diffusion process in
\mathbb{R}
with transition probability density with respect to Lebesgue measure given by
\mathfrak{p}_{t}(x,y)
that we call the
\mathscr{A}
-diffusion. We assume that for any interval
[a,b]\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
and endpoints
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
, the diffusion bridge of the
\mathscr{A}
-diffusion from
x
at time
a
to
y
at time
b
exists, see
[
46
]
. We call this the
\mathsf{A}
-bridge. The most natural example is simply the Brownian bridge.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
[a,b]\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbf{W}_{k}
.
Denote by

\mathbf{P}^{a,b;\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}
the law of
k
independent
\mathsf{A}
-bridges
started at
\mathbf{x}
at time
a
and ending at
\mathbf{y}
at time
b
and write

\mathbf{E}^{a,b;\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}
for the expectation with respect to

\mathbf{P}^{a,b;\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}
.

Observe that, dependence on
k\in\mathbb{N}
of
\mathbf{P}^{a,b;\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}
is implicit in the length of
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{W}_{k}
. To ease notation write
\mathrm{C}(\mathbb{R}_{+})=\mathrm{C}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathbb{R})
(analogously for an interval
[a,b]\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
) and
\mathrm{C}(\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket\times\mathbb{R}_{+})=\prod_{i\in\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket}\mathrm{C}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
where
N\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
, with
\llbracket 1,\infty\rrbracket\equiv\mathbb{N}
. One could also replace the target space
\mathbb{R}
with a general connected open interval.

Definition 2.8
.

A line ensemble is a
\mathrm{C}(\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket\times\mathbb{R}_{+})
-valued random variable.

We will need the following assumptions on the
\mathsf{A}
-bridge which we enforce throughout this section. We them to be true quite generally. It is well-known that Brownian bridge satisfies these, see
[
38
]
. The Bessel bridge does so as well (in
(0,\infty)
instead of
\mathbb{R}
), see
[
102
]
.

Assumption 2.9
.

Let
[a,b]\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
be arbitrary. We assume that the

\mathbf{P}^{a,b;x,y}
-distributed random variable
\mathsf{A}
satisfies:

1.

For any
f,g\in\mathrm{C}\left([a,b]\right)
with
f(a)>x,f(b)>y
and
g(a)<x,g(b)<y
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}^{a,b;x,y}\left(\left\{\inf_{t\in[a,b]}\left(f(t)-\mathsf{A}(t)\right)=0\right\}\cap\left\{\inf_{t\in[a,b]}\left(\mathsf{A}(t)-g(t)\right)=0\right\}\right)=0.

(28)

2.

For any open set
\mathscr{O}\subset\mathrm{C}\left([a,b]\right)
containing
f
with
f(a)=x
and
f(b)=y
, we have

\mathbf{P}^{a,b;x,y}\left(\mathsf{A}\in\mathscr{O}\right)>0.

By virtue of Assumption
2.9
we can define.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
[a,b]\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbf{x}=(x_{i})_{i=1}^{k}
,
\mathbf{y}=(y_{i})_{i=1}^{k}\in\mathbf{W}_{k}^{\circ}
. Let
u,l\in\mathrm{C}([a,b])
be such that:
u(t)>l(t)
for all
t\in[a,b]
and
u(a)>x_{1},u(b)>y_{1},l(a)<x_{k},l(b)<y_{k}
. We define
\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{avoid}}^{a,b;\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y};u,l}
to be the law of the process
(\mathsf{A}(t;i))_{i\in\llbracket 1,k\rrbracket,t\in[a,b]}
distributed according to
\mathbf{P}^{a,b;\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}
and conditioned on the non-zero probability event

\left\{u(r)>\mathsf{A}(r;1)>\mathsf{A}(r;2)>\cdots>\mathsf{A}(r;k)>l(r),\ \ \forall r\in[a,b]\right\}.

Write
\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{avoid}}^{a,b;\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y};u,l}
for the corresponding expectation.

We call
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},u,l
from Definition
2.10
the boundary data.

We can now define the Gibbs resampling property. It will be convenient to write
\mathsf{Law}(\mathsf{X})
for the law of a random variable
\mathsf{X}
and
\mathsf{Law}(\mathsf{X}|\mathscr{F})
for its conditional law given a sigma algebra
\mathscr{F}
.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
. We say that a line ensemble
\mathcal{L}\in\mathrm{C}(\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket)
satisfies the Gibbs resampling property with respect to the law of the
\mathsf{A}
-bridge if for all
\llbracket\ell,\ell+k-1\rrbracket\subset\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket
with
\ell,k\geq 1
and any
[a,b]\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
we have

\mathsf{Law}\left(\mathcal{L}|_{\llbracket\ell,\ell+k-1\rrbracket\times[a,b]}|\mathscr{F}_{\mathrm{ext}}\left(\llbracket\ell,\ell+k-1\rrbracket\times(a,b)\right)\right)=\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{avoid}}^{a,b;\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y};u,l},\ \ \textnormal{ almost surely,}

(29)

where the external sigma-algebra
\mathscr{F}_{\mathrm{ext}}\left(\llbracket\ell,\ell+k-1\rrbracket\times(a,b)\right)
is defined as

\mathscr{F}_{\mathrm{ext}}\left(\llbracket\ell,\ell+k-1\rrbracket\times(a,b)\right)=\sigma\left(\mathcal{L}_{j}(t);(j,t)\notin\llbracket\ell,\ell+k-1\rrbracket\times(a,b)\right),

(30)

and the (random) boundary data
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},u,l
given by,

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}=\left(\mathcal{L}_{\ell}(a),\mathcal{L}_{\ell+1}(a),\dots,\mathcal{L}_{k+\ell-1}(a)\right),\ \mathbf{y}=\left(\mathcal{L}_{\ell}(b),\mathcal{L}_{\ell+1}(b),\dots,\mathcal{L}_{k+\ell-1}(b)\right),\ u=\mathcal{L}_{\ell-1},\ l=\mathcal{L}_{\ell+k},

with the convention
\mathcal{L}_{0}\equiv\infty
,
\mathcal{L}_{N+1}\equiv-\infty
.

Remark 2.12
.

Observe that, in our definition of the Gibbs property it is implicit that for all fixed
t\geq 0
, almost surely
(\mathcal{L}_{i}(t))_{i\in\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket}\in\mathbf{W}_{N}^{\circ}
in order for the right hand side of (
29
) to be well-defined. In fact, a stronger statement holds: almost surely for all
t\geq 0
we have
(\mathcal{L}_{i}(t))_{i\in\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket}\in\mathbf{W}_{N}^{\circ}
as we spell out now. Write
\mathbf{P}
and
\mathbf{E}
for
\mathsf{Law}(\mathcal{L})
and expectation with respect to it. It suffices to show that for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and times
T\geq 0
we have,

\mathbf{P}\left(\inf_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\mathcal{L}_{k}(t)-\mathcal{L}_{k+1}(t)\right|>0\right)=1.

From (
29
) we have for any bounded measurable function
\mathsf{F}:\mathrm{C}(\{k,k+1\}\times[0,T])\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

\mathbf{E}\left[\mathsf{F}\left(\mathcal{L}|_{\{k,k+1\}\times[0,T]}\right)\big|\mathscr{F}_{\mathrm{ext}}\left(\{k,k+1\}\times(0,T)\right)\right]=\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{avoid}}^{0,T;\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y};u,l}\left[\mathsf{F}(\mathsf{Z})\right],\ \textnormal{ almost surely},

where
\mathsf{Z}
is distributed according to
\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{avoid}}^{0,T;\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y};u,l}
, and
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},u,l
as in Definition
2.11
. Taking expectations we get,

\mathbf{E}\left[\mathsf{F}\left(\mathcal{L}|_{\{k,k+1\}\times[0,T]}\right)\right]=\mathbf{E}\left[\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{avoid}}^{0,T;\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y};u,l}\left[\mathsf{F}(\mathsf{Z})\right]\right].

Choosing
\mathsf{F}(f,g)=\mathbf{1}_{f(t)>g(t),\forall t\in[0,T]}
we get the desired statement since the inner expectation is 1.

We next point out that finite-dimensional line ensembles arising from a Doob
h
-transform of a Karlin-McGregor semigroup
[
62
]
have a Gibbs property. Namely, assume there exists an
N
-dimensional Markov process
(\mathsf{v}^{(N)}(t))_{t\geq 0}
in
\mathbf{W}_{N}^{\circ}
with continuous sample paths with transition probabilities given by the following Doob
h
-transform of
\mathscr{A}
-diffusions,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{P}_{N}^{(\mathrm{h})}(t)(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\mathrm{e}^{-\mu_{N}t}\frac{\mathrm{h}(\mathbf{y})}{\mathrm{h}(\mathbf{x})}\det\left(\mathfrak{p}_{t}(x_{i},y_{j})\right)_{i,j=1}^{N},\ \ \mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{W}_{N}^{\circ},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbf{W}_{N}^{\circ},\ t>0,

(31)

for some function
\mathrm{h}
strictly positive in
\mathbf{W}_{N}^{\circ}
and
\mu_{N}\in\mathbb{R}
. We note that, the process
(\mathsf{v}^{(N)}(t))_{t\geq 0}
gives rise to a
\mathrm{C}(\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket\times\mathbb{R}_{+})
-valued line ensemble.

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, the line ensemble associated to the process
(\mathsf{v}^{(N)}(t))_{t\geq 0}
satisfies the Gibbs resampling property with respect to the law of the
\mathsf{A}
-bridge.

Proof.

Let us take an arbitrary
interval
[a,b]\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
partitioned as follows

a=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{k}=b.

Denote by
\mathbf{x}^{(m)}=(x_{1}^{(m)},\dots,x_{N}^{(m)})\in\mathbf{W}_{N}
the positions
at time
t_{m}
, for
m=0,1,\dots,k
,
with

\mathbf{x}^{(0)}=\mathbf{x}=\left(x_{1},\dots,x_{N}\right)
,
\mathbf{x}^{(m)}=\mathbf{y}=\left(y_{1},\dots,y_{N}\right)
. Applying the Markov property, the corresponding finite-dimensional distribution of

\mathsf{v}^{(N)}(\bullet)
conditional on
\mathsf{v}^{(N)}(a)=\mathbf{x}
and
\mathsf{v}^{(N)}(b)=\mathbf{y}

is given by the following joint density function with respect to product Lebesgue measure,

\displaystyle\frac{\prod_{m=0}^{k-1}\mathfrak{P}^{(h)}_{t_{m+1}-t_{m}}\left(\mathbf{x}^{(m)},\mathbf{x}^{(m+1)}\right)}{\mathfrak{P}^{(h)}_{b-a}\left(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\right)},

which, using (
31
) and after cancellation of common multiplicative terms, simplifies to

\displaystyle\frac{\prod_{m=0}^{k-1}\det\left(\mathfrak{p}_{t_{m+1}-t_{m}}\left(x_{i}^{(m)},x_{j}^{(m+1)}\right)\right)_{i,j=1}^{N}}{\det\left(\mathfrak{p}_{b-a}(x_{i},y_{j})\right)_{i,j=1}^{N}}.

(32)

Note that, this is exactly the density function corresponding to the finite-dimensional distribution of
N
non-colliding

\mathsf{A}
-bridges
on the interval
[a,b]
with endpoints
\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{y}
, as obtained by the Karlin–McGregor formula.
Hence, by virtue of continuity of the paths, the conditional law of
\mathsf{v}^{(N)}(\bullet)
on any interval
[a,b]
, given
\mathsf{v}^{(N)}(a)
and
\mathsf{v}^{(N)}(b)
, coincides with the law of
N
non-intersecting
\mathsf{A}
-bridges on
[a,b]
, with the same endpoints. It is then obvious (but tedious to write down formally and we omit this) that non-intersecting
\mathsf{A}
-bridges satisfy the Gibbs resampling property with respect to the law of the
\mathsf{A}
-bridge which completes the proof.
∎

We require one final lemma.

Lemma 2.14
.

Assume that for all
[a,b]\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
, and
x_{N},y_{N}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
x_{N}\to x
,
y_{N}\to y
as
N\to\infty
, we have the weak convergence of probability measures,

\mathbf{P}_{N}^{a,b;x_{N},y_{N}}\longrightarrow\mathbf{P}^{a,b;x,y},\ \textnormal{ as }N\longrightarrow\infty,

(33)

where
\mathbf{P}_{N}^{a,b;x_{N},y_{N}}
corresponds to the law of a
\mathsf{A}_{N}
-bridge and
\mathbf{P}^{a,b;x,y}
to the law of a
\mathsf{A}
-bridge. Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbf{x}_{N},\mathbf{y}_{N},\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbf{W}_{k}^{\circ}
with
\mathbf{x}_{N}\to\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{N}\to\mathbf{y}
as
N\to\infty
. Moreover, suppose that
u_{N},u\in\mathrm{C}([a,b];\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\})
and
l_{N},l\in\mathrm{C}([a,b];\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\})
are such that

u(t)>l(t),\ \forall t\in[a,b],\ u(a)>x_{1},\ u(b)>y_{1},\ l(a)<x_{k},\ l(b)<y_{k},

and also, as
N\to\infty
, uniformly in
[a,b]
,

u_{N}\longrightarrow u,\ \ l_{N}\longrightarrow l.

(34)

Then, for
N\in\mathbb{N}
large enough, the conditional law
\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{avoid};N}^{a,b;\mathbf{x}_{N},\mathbf{y}_{N};u_{N},l_{N}}
is well-defined and we have the following weak convergence of probability measures, as
N\longrightarrow\infty
,

\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{avoid};N}^{a,b;\mathbf{x}_{N},\mathbf{y}_{N};u_{N},l_{N}}\longrightarrow\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{avoid}}^{a,b;\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y};u,l}.

(35)

Proof.

Observe that, by virtue of Assumption
2.9
, the following event

\mathsf{Ord}=\left\{u(t)>\mathsf{A}(t;1)>\mathsf{A}(t;2)>\cdots>\mathsf{A}(t;k)>l(t),\ \forall t\in[a,b]\right\},

where
(\mathsf{A}(t;i))_{i\in\llbracket 1,k\rrbracket,t\in[a,b]}
is distributed according to
\mathbf{P}^{a,b;\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}
(
k
independent
\mathsf{A}
-bridges) has positive probability and for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
also consider the event
\mathsf{Ord}_{N}
given by,

\mathsf{Ord}_{N}=\left\{u_{N}(t)>\mathsf{A}_{N}(t;1)>\mathsf{A}_{N}(t;2)>\cdots>\mathsf{A}_{N}(t;k)>l_{N}(t),\ \forall t\in[a,b]\right\},

(36)

where
(\mathsf{A}_{N}(t;i))_{i\in\llbracket 1,k\rrbracket,t\in[a,b]}
is distributed according to
\mathbf{P}_{N}^{a,b;\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}

(
k
independent
\mathsf{A}_{N}
-bridges). On the other hand, from (
33
) and by independence of the coordinates we have

(\mathsf{A}_{N}(t;i))_{i\in\llbracket 1,k\rrbracket,t\in[a,b]}\overset{\mathrm{d}}{\longrightarrow}(\mathsf{A}(t;i))_{i\in\llbracket 1,k\rrbracket,t\in[a,b]}.

(37)

By virtue of Skorokhod’s representation theorem we can assume this convergence takes place almost surely in a coupling given by a probability measure
\mathbf{Q}
. Hence, also recalling (
34
), we obtain that for
N
large enough
\mathbf{Q}(\mathsf{Ord}_{N})>0
and
\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{avoid};N}^{a,b;\mathbf{x}_{N},\mathbf{y}_{N};u_{N},l_{N}}
is well-defined. To finally prove (
35
) we need to show that for any continuous bounded functional
\mathsf{F}:\mathrm{C}(\llbracket 1,k\rrbracket\times[a,b])\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

\frac{\mathbf{Q}\left[\mathsf{F}\left((\mathsf{A}_{N}(t;i))_{i\in\llbracket 1,k\rrbracket,t\in[a,b]}\right)\mathbf{1}_{\mathsf{Ord}_{N}}\right]}{\mathbf{Q}\left(\mathsf{Ord}_{N}\right)}\longrightarrow\frac{\mathbf{Q}\left[\mathsf{F}\left((\mathsf{A}(t;i))_{i\in\llbracket 1,k\rrbracket,t\in[a,b]}\right)\mathbf{1}_{\mathsf{Ord}}\right]}{\mathbf{Q}\left(\mathsf{Ord}\right)},\ \textnormal{ as }N\to\infty.

This convergence then follows from (
37
) by virtue of the bounded convergence theorem and the fact that the topological boundary of the set
\mathsf{Ord}
has probability zero by virtue of Assumption
2.9
.
∎

We have the following natural result.

Proposition 2.15
.

Let
(\mathcal{L}^{(N)})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of line ensembles such that for each
N\in\mathbb{N}
, with
\mathcal{L}^{(N)}\in\mathrm{C}(\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket\times\mathbb{R}_{+})
satisfies the Gibbs resampling property with respect to the law of a
\mathsf{A}_{N}
-bridge. Let
\mathcal{L}
be a
\mathrm{C}(\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})
-valued line ensemble. Moreover, assume that as
N\longrightarrow\infty
, for all
M\in\mathbb{N}
(when
N\geq M
),

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{L}^{(N)}_{i}(t)\right)_{i\in\llbracket 1,M\rrbracket,t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}\overset{\mathrm{d}}{\longrightarrow}\left(\mathcal{L}_{i}(t)\right)_{i\in\llbracket 1,M\rrbracket,t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}\ \textnormal{ in }\;\mathrm{C}\left(\llbracket 1,M\rrbracket\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\right),

and that for any fixed
t\geq 0
, almost surely
(\mathcal{L}_{i}(t))_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathbf{W}_{\infty}^{\circ}
. Finally, we assume that for any
[a,b]\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
and any
x_{N},y_{N}\in\mathbb{R}
, whenever
x_{N}\to x
and
y_{N}\to y
, we have the weak convergence of probability measures

\mathbf{P}_{N}^{a,b;x_{N},y_{N}}\longrightarrow\mathbf{P}^{a,b;x,y},\ \ \textnormal{ as }N\longrightarrow\infty,

where
\mathbf{P}_{N}^{a,b;x_{N},y_{N}}
and
\mathbf{P}^{a,b;x,y}
correspond to the law of a
\mathsf{A}_{N}
-bridge and
\mathsf{A}
-bridge respectively. Then,
\mathcal{L}
satisfies the Gibbs resampling property with respect to the law of an
\mathsf{A}
-bridge.
In particular, the paths of
\mathcal{L}
are almost surely non-intersecting.

Proof.

We fix an index set

\llbracket\ell,\ell+k-1\rrbracket\subset\mathbb{N}
, with
\ell,k\geq 1
,
and an arbitrary time interval
[a,b]\subset{\mathbb{R}_{+}}
.
Recall the definition of the external sigma-algebra
\mathscr{F}_{\mathrm{ext}}\left(\llbracket\ell,\ell+k-1\rrbracket\times(a,b)\right)
from (
30
). To prove the result, we need to show that
for any bounded Borel-measurable function

\mathsf{F}:\mathrm{C}\left(\llbracket\ell,\ell+k-1\rrbracket\times[a,b]\right)\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
we have, with
\mathbf{E}
denoting expectation with respect to
\mathsf{Law}(\mathcal{L})
,

\mathbf{E}\left[\mathsf{F}\left(\mathcal{L}|_{\llbracket\ell,\ell+k-1\rrbracket\times[a,b]}\right)\big|\mathscr{F}_{\mathrm{ext}}\left(\llbracket\ell,\ell+k-1\rrbracket\times(a,b)\right)\right]=\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{avoid}}^{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y};u,l}\left[\mathsf{F}(\mathsf{Z})\right],\ \ \textnormal{almost surely},

(38)

where
\mathsf{Z}
has the law

\mathbf{P}_{\textnormal{avoid}}^{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y};u,l}
,
and, the random boundary data is given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathcal{L}_{\ell}(a),\mathcal{L}_{\ell+1}(a),\dots,\mathcal{L}_{\ell+k-1}(a)\right),\ \ \mathbf{y}=\left(\mathcal{L}_{\ell}(b),\mathcal{L}_{\ell+1}(b),\dots,\mathcal{L}_{\ell+k-1}(b)\right),

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{\ell-1}\ \ (u=+\infty,\textnormal{ if }\ell=1),\hskip 19.91692pt\ \ l=\mathcal{L}_{\ell+k}.

Observe that the right hand side of (
38
) is well-defined almost surely by virtue of the assumption that for any fixed
t\geq 0
, almost surely
(\mathcal{L}_{i}(t))_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathbf{W}_{\infty}^{\circ}
. Here and below, we omit the parameters
a,b
from the notation for brevity.

By the definition and since

\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{avoid}}^{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y};u,l}\left[\mathsf{F}(\mathsf{Z})\right]

is
\mathscr{F}_{\mathrm{ext}}\left(\llbracket\ell,\ell+k-1\rrbracket\times(a,b)\right)
-measurable,
it suffices to verify that

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[\mathbf{1}_{\mathscr{B}}\mathsf{F}\left(\mathcal{L}|_{\llbracket\ell,\ell+k-1\rrbracket\times[a,b]}\right)\right]=\mathbf{E}\left[\mathbf{1}_{\mathscr{B}}\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{avoid}}^{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y};u,l}\left[\mathsf{F}(\mathsf{Z})\right]\right],\ \ \forall\mathscr{B}\in\mathscr{F}_{\mathrm{ext}}\left(\llbracket\ell,\ell+k-1\rrbracket\times(a,b)\right).

Consider now the following
\pi
-system of cylinder sets,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{C}\overset{\textnormal{def}}{=}\bigg\{\mathscr{B}

\displaystyle=\left\{\left(\mathcal{L}_{i_{1}}(t_{1}),\ldots,\mathcal{L}_{i_{m}}(t_{m})\right)\in A\right\}:

\displaystyle(i_{j},t_{j})\notin\llbracket\ell,\ell+k-1\rrbracket\times(a,b),\,j\in\llbracket 1,m\rrbracket,\,m\in\mathbb{N},\;A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}\textnormal{ Borel}\bigg\}.

From standard measure-theoretic results, we have

\displaystyle\sigma\left(\mathfrak{C}\right)=\mathscr{F}_{\mathrm{ext}}\left(\llbracket\ell,\ell+k-1\rrbracket\times(a,b)\right).

Next, define

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}\overset{\textnormal{def}}{=}\left\{\mathscr{B}\in\mathscr{F}_{\mathrm{ext}}\left(\llbracket\ell,\ell+k-1\rrbracket\times(a,b)\right):\mathbf{E}\left[\mathbf{1}_{\mathscr{B}}\mathsf{F}\left(\mathcal{L}|_{\llbracket\ell,\ell+k-1\rrbracket\times[a,b]}\right)\right]=\mathbf{E}\left[\mathbf{1}_{\mathscr{B}}\,\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{avoid}}^{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y};u,l}\left[\mathsf{F}(\mathsf{Z})\right]\right]\right\}.

We show in the following that

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[\mathbf{1}_{\mathscr{B}}\mathsf{F}\left(\mathcal{L}|_{\llbracket\ell,\ell+k-1\rrbracket\times[a,b]}\right)\right]=\mathbf{E}\left[\mathbf{1}_{\mathscr{B}}\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{avoid}}^{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y};u,l}\left[\mathsf{F}(\mathsf{Z})\right]\right],\ \ \forall\mathscr{B}\in\mathfrak{C}.

(39)

Assuming (
39
), it is straightforward to verify, using the monotone convergence theorem, that
\mathfrak{D}
is a (Dynkin)
\lambda
-system containing
\mathfrak{C}
. Hence, the desired result follows by
applying the
\pi-\lambda
theorem.

We now proceed to proving the identity
(
39
). To establish (
39
), we begin by considering the case where
\mathsf{F}
is continuous (and bounded). By a density argument it moreover suffices to prove the following. For any
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
(i_{j},t_{j})\notin\llbracket\ell,\ell+k-1\rrbracket\times(a,b),\,j\in\llbracket 1,m\rrbracket
and any continuous bounded function
\mathfrak{H}
from
\mathbb{R}^{m}
to
\mathbb{R}
we have:

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[\mathfrak{H}\left(\mathcal{L}_{i_{1}}(t_{1}),\ldots,\mathcal{L}_{i_{m}}(t_{m})\right)\mathsf{F}\left(\mathcal{L}|_{\llbracket\ell,\ell+k-1\rrbracket\times[a,b]}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}\left[\mathfrak{H}\left(\mathcal{L}_{i_{1}}(t_{1}),\ldots,\mathcal{L}_{i_{m}}(t_{m})\right)\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{avoid}}^{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y};u,l}\left[\mathsf{F}(\mathsf{Z})\right]\right].

(40)

By the Skorokhod representation theorem, we realize
(\mathcal{L}^{(N)})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\mathcal{L}
on a common probability space
(\Omega,\mathbf{Q})
such that,
\mathbf{Q}
-almost surely, as
N\longrightarrow\infty
, for all
M\in\mathbb{N}
(with
N\geq M
),

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{L}^{(N)}_{i}(t)\right)_{i\in\llbracket 1,M\rrbracket,t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}\longrightarrow\left(\mathcal{L}_{i}(t)\right)_{i\in\llbracket 1,M\rrbracket,t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}\ \textnormal{ in }\;\mathrm{C}\left(\llbracket 1,M\rrbracket\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\right).

(41)

Hence,
\mathbf{Q}
-almost surely, as
N\longrightarrow\infty
we have,

\mathfrak{H}\left(\mathcal{L}^{(N)}_{i_{1}}(t_{1}),\ldots,\mathcal{L}^{(N)}_{i_{m}}(t_{m})\right)\longrightarrow\mathfrak{H}\left(\mathcal{L}_{i_{1}}(t_{1}),\ldots,\mathcal{L}_{i_{m}}(t_{m})\right).

(42)

Moreover, for each
N\in\mathbb{N}

with
N\geq\ell+k
, by virtue of the Gibbs property of
\mathcal{L}^{(N)}
one obtains with
\mathfrak{H}
as above

2.3

The evolution of the reverse characteristic polynomial

We begin with the following result.

Proposition 2.17
.

For any
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
,

\mathfrak{gl}_{t;N}(z)
satisfies the following equation

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{gl}_{t;N}(z)=\mathrm{d}\mathsf{M}^{N}_{t}(z;\mathfrak{gl})+\left[\frac{\theta}{2}z\partial_{z}\mathfrak{gl}_{t;N}(z)\mathrm{d}t-\frac{z^{2}}{2}\partial_{z}^{2}\mathfrak{gl}_{t;N}(z)\right]\mathrm{d}t,

(46)

where
\mathsf{M}^{N}\in\mathrm{C}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathsf{H}(\mathbb{C}))
and for all
z\in\mathbb{C}
,
t\mapsto\mathsf{M}_{t}^{N}(z;\mathfrak{gl})
are continuous local martingales with covariation, for
z,w\in\mathbb{C}

\langle\mathsf{M}_{t}^{N}(z;\mathfrak{gl}),\mathsf{M}_{t}^{N}(w;\mathfrak{gl})\rangle=\frac{zw}{z-w}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\mathfrak{gl}_{s;N}(z)\partial_{w}\mathfrak{gl}_{s;N}(w)-\mathfrak{gl}_{s;N}(w)\partial_{z}\mathfrak{gl}_{s;N}(z)\right)\mathrm{d}s.

(47)

Proof.

It will be more convenient to consider the rescaled process
\hat{\mathsf{x}}^{(N)}_{i}(t)=N^{-1}\mathsf{x}_{i}^{(N)}
so that
\mathfrak{gl}_{t;N}(z)=\prod_{i=1}^{N}(1-\hat{\mathsf{x}}_{i}^{(N)}(t)z)
. Observe that, the
(\hat{\mathsf{x}}_{i}^{(N)})_{i=1}^{N}
is the unique solution to the exact same equation (
7
) but with the scaled by
N^{-1}
initial condition. Thus abusing notation in the computations below we still write
\mathsf{x}_{i}^{(N)}
in place of
\hat{\mathsf{x}}_{i}^{(N)}
.

Hence, using Itô’s formula applied to
\mathfrak{gl}_{t;N}(z)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{gl}_{t;N}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{-z\mathfrak{gl}_{t;N}(z)}{1-\mathsf{x}_{i}^{(N)}(t)z}\mathrm{d}\mathsf{x}_{i}^{(N)}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{d}\mathsf{M}_{t}^{N}(z;\mathfrak{gl})+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{-z\mathfrak{gl}_{t;N}(z)}{1-\mathsf{x}_{i}^{(N)}(t)z}\left[\frac{\theta}{2}\mathsf{x}_{i}^{(N)}(t)+\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^{N}\frac{\mathsf{x}_{i}^{(N)}(t)\mathsf{x}^{(N)}_{j}(t)}{\mathsf{x}^{(N)}_{i}(t)-\mathsf{x}^{(N)}_{j}(t)}\right]\mathrm{d}t,

where the local martingale term
\mathsf{M}_{t}^{N}(z;\mathfrak{gl})
is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\mathsf{M}_{t}^{N}(z;\mathfrak{gl})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{-z\mathsf{x}_{i}^{(N)}(t)\mathfrak{gl}_{t;N}(z)}{1-\mathsf{x}_{i}^{(N)}(t)z}\mathrm{d}\mathsf{w}_{i}(t).

(48)

Note that, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{z}\mathfrak{gl}_{t;N}(z)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\mathsf{x}_{i}^{(N)}(t)}{1-\mathsf{x}_{i}^{(N)}(t)z}\mathfrak{gl}_{t;N}(z),

(49)

\displaystyle\partial_{z}^{2}\mathfrak{gl}_{t;N}(z)

\displaystyle=\left(\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\mathsf{x}_{i}^{(N)}(t)}{1-\mathsf{x}_{i}^{(N)}(t)z}\right)^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\frac{\mathsf{x}_{i}^{(N)}(t)}{1-\mathsf{x}_{i}^{(N)}(t)z}\right)^{2}\right)\mathfrak{gl}_{t;N}(z)

(50)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\underset{i\neq j}{i,j=1}}^{N}\frac{\mathsf{x}_{i}^{(N)}(t)\mathsf{x}_{j}^{(N)}(t)}{\left(1-\mathsf{x}_{i}^{(N)}(t)z\right)\left(1-\mathsf{x}_{j}^{(N)}(t)z\right)}\mathfrak{gl}_{t;N}(z).

(51)

Moreover, observe that we can write,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{1-\mathsf{x}_{i}^{(N)}(t)z}\sum_{\underset{i\neq j}{i,j=1}}^{N}\frac{\mathsf{x}_{i}^{(N)}(t)\mathsf{x}_{j}^{(N)}(t)}{\mathsf{x}^{(N)}_{i}(t)-\mathsf{x}^{(N)}_{j}(t)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\underset{i\neq j}{i,j=1}}^{N}\frac{\mathsf{x}_{i}^{(N)}(t)\mathsf{x}_{j}^{(N)}(t)}{\mathsf{x}^{(N)}_{i}(t)-\mathsf{x}^{(N)}_{j}(t)}\left[\frac{1}{1-\mathsf{x}_{i}^{(N)}(t)z}-\frac{1}{1-\mathsf{x}_{j}^{(N)}(t)z}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{z}{2}\sum_{\underset{i\neq j}{i,j=1}}^{N}\frac{\mathsf{x}_{i}^{(N)}(t)\mathsf{x}_{j}^{(N)}(t)}{\left(1-\mathsf{x}_{i}^{(N)}(t)z\right)\left(1-\mathsf{x}_{j}^{(N)}(t)z\right)}.

Thus, making use of all the above, we obtain (
46
).
Next, from
(
48
),
(
49
)
and
(
51
)
we can compute the quadratic covariation of
\mathsf{M}_{t}^{N}(z;\mathfrak{gl})
and
\mathsf{M}_{t}^{N}(w;\mathfrak{gl})
, for
z,w\in\mathbb{C}
,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\langle\mathsf{M}_{t}^{N}(z;\mathfrak{gl}),\mathsf{M}_{t}^{N}(w;\mathfrak{gl})\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{zw\left(\mathsf{x}_{i}^{(N)}(t)\right)^{2}}{\left(1-\mathsf{x}_{i}^{(N)}(t)z\right)\left(1-\mathsf{x}_{i}^{(N)}(t)w\right)}\mathfrak{gl}_{t;N}(z)\mathfrak{gl}_{t;N}(w)

\displaystyle=\frac{zw}{z-w}\left(\mathfrak{gl}_{t;N}(z)\partial_{w}\mathfrak{gl}_{t;N}(w)-\mathfrak{gl}_{t;N}(w)\partial_{z}\mathfrak{gl}_{t;N}(z)\right),

which completes the proof.
∎

.

Let

2.4

The Stieltjes transform and equations for the zeros

We will derive equations for the zeros of
(\mathfrak{gl}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
or more precisely the reciprocals of them. Towards this end, define the following analogue of the Stieltjes transform in the infinite setting:

\displaystyle\psi_{t}^{GL}(z)

\displaystyle\overset{\textnormal{def}}{=}\partial_{z}\log\mathfrak{gl}_{t}(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{-\mathsf{x}_{j}(t)}{1-\mathsf{x}_{j}(t)z}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathsf{x}_{j}(t)-\boldsymbol{\gamma}(t),\ \ z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\left\{\mathsf{x}_{j}^{-1}(t),j\in\mathbb{N}\right\},\ \,t\geq 0.

(52)

We have the following result.

Proposition 2.19
.

The process
(\psi_{t}^{GL}(z))_{t\geq 0}
satisfies the following Burgers-type equation in
\mathbb{C}\backslash\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\psi_{t}^{GL}(z)=

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\mathsf{m}_{t}\left(z\right)+\left[\frac{\theta}{2}\partial_{z}\left(z\psi_{t}^{GL}(z)\right)-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{z}\left(z^{2}\left(\psi_{t}^{GL}(z)\right)^{2}\right)\right]\mathrm{d}t,

(53)

where, for
z\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\mathbb{R}
,

t\mapsto\mathsf{m}_{t}(z)
is a continuous local martingale
with quadratic covariation, for
z,w\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\ \left\langle\mathsf{m}_{t}(z),\mathsf{m}_{t}(w)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}-\partial_{z}\partial_{w}\left(zw\frac{\psi_{s}^{GL}(z)-\psi_{s}^{GL}(w)}{z-w}\right)\mathrm{d}s.

(54)

Proof.

From Proposition
2.18
we can calculate for
z\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\log\mathfrak{gl}_{t}(z)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\mathsf{M}}_{t}(z)+\frac{\theta}{2}z\partial_{z}\log\mathfrak{gl}_{t}(z)\mathrm{d}t-\frac{z^{2}}{2}\frac{\partial_{z}^{2}\mathfrak{gl}_{t}(z)}{\mathfrak{gl}_{t}(z)}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\ \ -\frac{1}{2\mathfrak{gl}_{t}^{2}(z))}z^{2}\left(\left(\partial_{z}\mathfrak{gl}_{t}(z)\right)^{2}-\mathfrak{gl}_{t}(z)\partial_{z}^{2}\mathfrak{gl}_{t}(z)\right)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\mathsf{M}}_{t}(z)+\frac{\theta}{2}z\partial_{z}\log\mathfrak{gl}_{t}(z)\mathrm{d}t-\frac{z^{2}}{2}\frac{\partial_{z}^{2}\mathfrak{gl}_{t}(z)}{\mathfrak{gl}_{t}(z)}\mathrm{d}t-\frac{z^{2}}{2}\left(\partial_{z}\log\mathfrak{gl}_{t}(z)\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\mathsf{M}}_{t}(z)+\frac{\theta}{2}z\mathfrak{gl}_{t}(z)\mathrm{d}t-\frac{z^{2}}{2}\left(\mathfrak{gl}_{t}(z)\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}t,

(55)

where we have defined the local martingale term
\widetilde{\mathsf{M}}_{t}(z)
by (note that this is well-defined as
(\mathfrak{gl}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
has only real zeros)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathsf{M}}_{t}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathsf{M}_{s}(z;\mathfrak{gl})}{\mathfrak{gl}_{s}(z)}.

Taking the derivative in
z
, one then obtains (
53
) with

\displaystyle\mathsf{m}_{t}\left(z\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{z}\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\mathsf{M}}_{s}(z).

(56)

The identity (
54
) for the quadratic covariation
can then be derived, in view of (
56
), from the corresponding expression for
\mathsf{M}_{t}(z;\mathfrak{gl})
.
Alternatively, one could
take the limit
N\to\infty
of the expressions in the finite
N
case, by first defining
\psi_{t;N}^{GL}(z)
as

\displaystyle\psi_{t;N}^{GL}(z)\overset{\textnormal{def}}{=}\partial_{z}\log\mathfrak{gl}_{t;N}(z)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{-\mathsf{x}_{i}^{(N)}(t)}{1-\mathsf{x}_{i}^{(N)}(t)z},

and noting that by virtue of
\mathfrak{gl}_{\bullet;N}\to\mathfrak{gl}_{\bullet}
in
\mathrm{C}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathfrak{LP}_{+})
we have that
\psi_{\bullet;N}^{GL}\to\psi_{\bullet}^{GL}
uniformly in time and compact sets in
\mathbb{C}\backslash\mathbb{R}
.
∎

We can now derive the equations, or more precisely a penultimate version in (
60
), for the
\mathsf{x}_{i}
via the equation for
\psi_{t}^{GL}
. This equation can alternatively be obtained in a different way by following the scheme of proof in Section 4 of
[

]
.

Proposition 2.20
.

For any
\gamma\geq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}x_{i}
, whenever
(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathbf{W}_{\infty;+}^{\circ}
, the projection of
\mathbf{X}_{GL}^{\bullet}
on the

(\mathsf{w}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}

Proof.

Let us define, by virtue of Proposition

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{i}(t)\overset{\textnormal{def}}{=}\frac{1}{\mathsf{x}_{i}(t)},\ \ t\geq 0,\ \ i\in\mathbb{N}.

First, observe that from (

\displaystyle\psi_{t}^{GL}(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{z-\mathcal{X}_{j}(t)}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\mathcal{X}_{j}(t)}-\boldsymbol{\gamma}(t),

(57)

and thus,
\mathcal{X}_{i}(t)
’s are the poles of
\psi_{t}^{GL}(z)
.

Let
t\geq 0
be arbitrary. Now, suppose

\mathscr{C}_{t}^{i}
is a contour enclosing

\mathcal{X}_{i}(t)

but not

\mathcal{X}_{j}(t)
for
i\neq j
. We note that such a (time-evolving) contour can be taken piecewise constant in time, in the sense that it is always possible to split any interval
[0,t)
into a finite number of sub-intervals
[t_{\ell}^{(i)},t_{\ell+1}^{(i)})
so that
\mathscr{C}_{t}^{i}\equiv\mathscr{C}^{i;\ell}
on

\mathcal{X}_{i}

.

Then, by using Cauchy’s residue formula, emulating the type of computations from
[

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\mathcal{X}_{i}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi\textnormal{i}}\oint_{\mathscr{C}_{t}^{i}}z\mathrm{d}\psi^{GL}_{t}(z)\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi\textnormal{i}}\oint_{\mathscr{C}_{t}^{i}}z\mathrm{d}\mathsf{m}_{t}(z)\mathrm{d}z+\frac{1}{2\pi\textnormal{i}}\oint_{\mathscr{C}_{t}^{i}}\left(\frac{\theta}{2}z\partial_{z}\left(z\psi^{GL}_{t}(z)\right)-\frac{1}{2}z\partial_{z}\left(z^{2}\left(\psi^{GL}_{t}(z)\right)^{2}\right)\right)\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}z.

Elementary manipulations give the following:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi\textnormal{i}}\oint_{\mathscr{C}_{t}^{i}}z\partial_{z}\left(z\psi^{GL}_{t}(z)\right)\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\pi\textnormal{i}}\oint_{\mathscr{C}_{t}^{i}}z\psi^{GL}_{t}(z)\mathrm{d}z=-\mathcal{X}_{i}(t),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi\textnormal{i}}\oint_{\mathscr{C}_{t}^{i}}z\partial_{z}\left(z^{2}\left(\psi^{GL}_{t}(z)\right)^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\pi\textnormal{i}}\oint_{\mathscr{C}_{t}^{i}}z^{2}\left(\psi^{GL}_{t}(z)\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\hskip-85.35826pt=-\frac{1}{2\pi\textnormal{i}}\oint_{\mathscr{C}_{t}^{i}}z^{2}\left[\frac{1}{\left(z-\mathcal{X}_{i}(t)\right)^{2}}+\frac{2}{z-\mathcal{X}_{i}(t)}\left(\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^{\infty}\frac{1}{z-\mathcal{X}_{j}(t)}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\mathcal{X}_{j}(t)}-\boldsymbol{\gamma}(t)\right)\right]\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\hskip-85.35826pt=-2\left[\mathcal{X}_{i}(t)+\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{\left(\mathcal{X}_{i}(t)\right)^{2}}{\mathcal{X}_{i}(t)-\mathcal{X}_{j}(t)}+\left(\mathcal{X}_{i}(t)\right)^{2}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\mathcal{X}_{j}(t)}-\boldsymbol{\gamma}(t)\right)\right].

To characterize the local martingale term, let us define (abusing notation as these are not the same Brownian motions from before)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\mathsf{w}_{i}(t)\overset{\textnormal{def}}{=}\frac{1}{2\pi\textnormal{i}\mathcal{X}_{i}(t)}\oint_{\mathscr{C}_{t}^{i}}z\mathrm{d}\mathsf{m}_{t}(z)\mathrm{d}z.

(58)

Note that,

\left(\mathsf{w}_{i}(\cdot)\right)_{i\in\mathbb{N}}

is a sequence of continuous local martingales adapted to the natural filtration of the process
(\mathbf{X}_{GL}^{t})_{t\geq 0}
.
We claim, and prove shortly below, that the

\mathsf{w}_{i}
’s are in fact independent
standard Brownian motions. Then, putting everything together, it follows that, for
i\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\mathcal{X}_{i}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{X}_{i}(t)\mathrm{d}\mathsf{w}_{i}(t)+\left(1-\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\mathcal{X}_{i}(t)\mathrm{d}t+\left[\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{\left(\mathcal{X}_{i}(t)\right)^{2}}{\mathcal{X}_{i}(t)-\mathcal{X}_{j}(t)}+\left(\mathcal{X}_{i}(t)\right)^{2}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\mathcal{X}_{j}(t)}-\boldsymbol{\gamma}(t)\right)\right]\mathrm{d}t.

(59)

We now prove the claim on the
\left(\mathsf{w}_{i}(\cdot)\right)_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
.
Using (

. Putting everything together we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{y}_{i}(t)=

\displaystyle\sqrt{2}\mathfrak{y}_{i}(t)\mathrm{d}\mathsf{w}_{i}(t)+2\left(\Re{(\mathfrak{s})}+2\right)\mathfrak{y}_{i}(t)\mathrm{d}t+2\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0,i\}}\left(\frac{\left(\mathfrak{y}_{i}(t)\right)^{3}}{\mathfrak{y}_{j}(t)\left(\mathfrak{y}_{i}(t)-\mathfrak{y}_{j}(t)\right)}\right)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle-2\left[\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{HP}(t)\left(\mathfrak{y}_{i}(t)\right)^{2}+\left(\boldsymbol{\delta}(t)-\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}\left(\frac{1}{\mathfrak{y}_{j}(t)}\right)^{2}\right)\left(\mathfrak{y}_{i}(t)\right)^{3}\right]\mathrm{d}t,\ \ i\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}.

Then,
an application of Itô’s formula yields the following equation for the
\mathsf{y}_{i}
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\mathsf{y}_{i}(t)=

\displaystyle\sqrt{2}\mathsf{y}_{i}(t)\mathrm{d}\mathsf{w}_{i}(t)-2\left(\Re{(\mathfrak{s})}+1\right)\mathsf{y}_{i}(t)\mathrm{d}t+2\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0,i\}}\frac{\mathsf{y}_{j}^{2}(t)}{\mathsf{y}_{i}(t)-\mathsf{y}_{j}(t)}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+2\left[\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{HP}(t)+\frac{1}{\mathsf{y}_{i}(t)}\left(\boldsymbol{\delta}(t)-\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}\mathsf{y}_{j}^{2}(t)\right)\right]\mathrm{d}t,\ \ i\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}.

(70)

We next obtain the SDE satisfied by
(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{HP}(t))_{t\geq 0}
. It is easy to check from the definition that
\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{HP}(t)=-\partial_{z}\mathfrak{hp}_{t}(z)\big|_{z=0}
. Therefore, we can use
(
17
) to obtain the equation

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{HP}(t)=

\displaystyle-\partial_{z}\mathrm{d}\mathsf{M}_{t}(z;\mathfrak{hp})\big|_{z=0}+2\left(\Im{(\mathfrak{s})}-\Re{(\mathfrak{s})}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{HP}(t)\right)\mathrm{d}t.

(71)

Moving on, we have using Itô’s formula that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\mathrm{d}\mathsf{y}_{i}^{2}(t)=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\mathsf{y}_{i}^{2}(t)\mathrm{d}\mathsf{w}_{i}(t)-\left(\Re{(\mathfrak{s})}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\mathsf{y}_{i}^{2}(t)\mathrm{d}t+\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0,i\}}\frac{\mathsf{y}_{i}(t)\mathsf{y}_{j}^{2}(t)}{\mathsf{y}_{i}(t)-\mathsf{y}_{j}(t)}\mathrm{d}t+\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{HP}(t)\mathsf{y}_{i}(t)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\left(\boldsymbol{\delta}(t)-\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}\mathsf{y}_{j}^{2}(t)\right)\mathrm{d}t,\ \ i\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}.

(72)

Writing the equations in the integral form, and taking the sum over the first
K\in\mathbb{N}
coordinates, we obtain, for any
t>0
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathsf{y}_{i}^{2}(t)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{K}y_{i}^{2}+\mathsf{MG}_{K}(t)-\left(\Re{(\mathfrak{s})}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathsf{y}_{i}^{2}(s)\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0,i\}}\frac{\mathsf{y}_{i}(s)\mathsf{y}_{j}^{2}(s)}{\mathsf{y}_{i}(s)-\mathsf{y}_{j}(s)}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{HP}(s)\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathsf{y}_{i}(s)\mathrm{d}s+K\int_{0}^{t}\left(\boldsymbol{\delta}(s)-\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}\mathsf{y}_{j}^{2}(s)\right)\mathrm{d}s.

(73)

Here, the
non-coinciding initial condition
\mathbf{y}
is such that

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}y_{i}^{2}<\infty
,
and
(\mathsf{MG}_{K}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is the corresponding local martingale given by

\mathsf{MG}_{K}(t)=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathsf{y}_{i}^{2}(s)\mathrm{d}\mathsf{w}_{i}(s),\ \forall t\geq 0.

Observe that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0,i\}}\frac{\mathsf{y}_{i}(s)\mathsf{y}_{j}^{2}(s)}{\mathsf{y}_{i}(s)-\mathsf{y}_{j}(s)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\underset{i\neq j}{i,j=1,}}^{K}\ \frac{\mathsf{y}_{i}(s)\mathsf{y}_{j}^{2}(s)}{\mathsf{y}_{i}(s)-\mathsf{y}_{j}(s)}+\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0,\cdots,K\}}\frac{\mathsf{y}_{i}(s)\mathsf{y}_{j}^{2}(s)}{\mathsf{y}_{i}(s)-\mathsf{y}_{j}(s)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\underset{i\neq j}{i,j=1,}}^{K}\ \mathsf{y}_{i}(s)\mathsf{y}_{j}(s)+\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0,\cdots,K\}}\frac{\mathsf{y}_{i}(s)\mathsf{y}_{j}^{2}(s)}{\mathsf{y}_{i}(s)-\mathsf{y}_{j}(s)},\ \ s\in[0,t],

and that,

\displaystyle\sum_{\underset{i\neq j}{i,j=1}}^{K}\ \mathsf{y}_{i}(s)\mathsf{y}_{j}(s)\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathsf{y}_{i}(s)\right)^{2},\ \ \sum_{i=1}^{K}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0,\cdots,K\}}\frac{\mathsf{y}_{i}(s)\mathsf{y}_{j}^{2}(s)}{\mathsf{y}_{i}(s)-\mathsf{y}_{j}(s)}>0,\ \ s\in[0,t].

Thus, we have,

\displaystyle K\int_{0}^{t}\left(\boldsymbol{\delta}(s)-\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}\mathsf{y}_{j}^{2}(s)\right)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathsf{y}_{i}^{2}(t)-\mathsf{MG}_{K}(t)+\left(\Re{(\mathfrak{s})}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathsf{y}_{i}^{2}(s)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\ \ +\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathsf{y}_{i}(s)\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}s-\int_{0}^{t}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{HP}(s)\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathsf{y}_{i}(s)\mathrm{d}s.

(74)

Note that, all terms on the right hand side above except the last two terms are uniformly bounded as
K\to\infty
. In particular, for
\mathsf{MG}_{K}
one can check that the quadratic variation converges and then use the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality
[
61
]
. As for the last two terms, we claim that after dividing by
K
they converge to
0
almost surely. By the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality this would be implied by the almost sure convergence:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{K^{\frac{1}{2}}}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathsf{y}_{i}(s)\overset{K\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

We now prove this claim. Let
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary and by virtue of the convergence of
\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\sum_{i=n+1}^{\infty}\mathsf{y}_{i}^{2}(s)
to
0
pick
n
random large enough so that
\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\sum_{i=n+1}^{\infty}\mathsf{y}_{i}^{2}(s)<\varepsilon^{2}
. Then, using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we can write for all
K>n

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathsf{y}_{i}(s)

\displaystyle=\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathsf{y}_{i}(s)+\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\sum_{i=n+1}^{K}\mathsf{y}_{i}(s)\leq\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathsf{y}_{i}(s)+\sqrt{K-n}\sqrt{\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\sum_{i=n+1}^{K}\mathsf{y}_{i}^{2}(s)}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathsf{y}_{i}(s)+\varepsilon\sqrt{K-n}.

Hence, we get that almost surely

\limsup_{K\to\infty}\frac{1}{K^{\frac{1}{2}}}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathsf{y}_{i}(s)<\varepsilon,

and since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary this proves the claim. Thus, putting everything together in the bound in
(
3.3
), we conclude that almost surely

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left(\boldsymbol{\delta}(s)-\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}\mathsf{y}_{j}^{2}(s)\right)\mathrm{d}s=0,

and hence the SDE reduces to the desired form of Proposition
1.8
.
∎

3.4

A PDE for Dyson Brownian motion in
1/N
scaling

In this final short section we point out a curious fact about the characteristic polynomial of Dyson Brownian motion. It is well-known
[
100
,
87
,
14
,
16
]
that the (generalised) Ornstein-Uhlenbeck Dyson Brownian motion with
\mathsf{c}\in\mathbb{R}
(for
\mathsf{c}=0
this is standard Dyson Brownian motion while for
\mathsf{c}>0
it is the standard stationary Ornstein-Uhlenbeck version),

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{d}_{i}^{(N)}(t)=\mathrm{d}\mathsf{w}_{i}(t)-\mathsf{c}\mathfrak{d}^{(N)}_{i}(t)\mathrm{d}t+\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^{N}\frac{1}{\mathfrak{d}^{(N)}_{i}(t)-\mathfrak{d}^{(N)}_{j}(t)}\mathrm{d}t,\ \ i\in\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket,

(75)

is consistent i.e. the semigroups are intertwined for different
N
in the sense of Section
2.1
, see in particular (
27
) from Proposition
2.3
. Moreover, the relevant regularity properties such as the Feller property, continuity of paths and smooth symmetric function generator core are known
[
92
]
. Hence, using the theory of
[
12
]
after embedding into
\Upsilon
one has convergence in
\mathrm{C}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\Upsilon)
. Let us then define

\mathfrak{D}_{t;N}(z)=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\left(1-\frac{\mathfrak{d}_{i}^{(N)}(t)z}{N}\right).

Note that, dividing by
N
is too big of a scaling in order to see non-trivial limiting stochastic behaviour for
\mathfrak{D}_{t;N}
, as in for example the edge scaling case towards the Airy line ensemble
[
53
]
. We have the following result.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\mathsf{c}\in\mathbb{R}
. Suppose
\mathfrak{D}_{0;N}\overset{N\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\mathfrak{D}_{0}\in\mathfrak{LP}
. Then, as
N\to\infty
,
\mathfrak{D}_{\bullet;N}\overset{\mathrm{d}}{\longrightarrow}\mathfrak{D}_{\bullet}
in
\mathrm{C}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathfrak{LP})
with the (deterministic) evolution
t\mapsto\mathfrak{D}_{t}
solving the equation

\partial_{t}\mathfrak{D}_{t}(z)=-\frac{z^{2}}{2}\mathfrak{D}_{t}(z)-\mathsf{c}z\partial_{z}\mathfrak{D}_{t}(z).

(76)

Moreover, for
\mathfrak{D}_{0}\in\mathfrak{LP}
, the unique solution to this equation is given by,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}_{t}(z)=\mathfrak{D}_{0}\left(z\mathrm{e}^{-\mathsf{c}t}\right)\exp\left(\frac{-z^{2}}{4\mathsf{c}}\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{-2\mathsf{c}t}\right)\right),

which for
\mathsf{c}=0
simplifies to
\mathfrak{D}_{t}(z)=\mathfrak{D}_{0}(z)\mathrm{e}^{-tz^{2}/2}
.

Proof.

The convergence statement for
\mathfrak{D}_{\bullet;N}
follows by virtue of consistency while the fact that
\mathfrak{D}_{\bullet}
solves (
76
) follows by deriving an equation for
\mathfrak{D}_{\bullet;N}
as before and taking the limit. It is tedious but elementary so we omit the details. The linear first order pde (
76
) can be solved explicitly using the method of characteristics. Alternatively, one can solve at the level of the
\mathfrak{LP}
-class representation of the function with parameters
((\mathsf{x}_{i}^{+}(\bullet))_{i\in\mathbb{N}},(\mathsf{x}_{i}^{-}(\bullet))_{i\in\mathbb{N}},\boldsymbol{\gamma}(\bullet),\boldsymbol{\delta}(\bullet))
as follows (one can also prove convergence in this way, see
[
16
]
where this was discussed):

\mathsf{x}_{i}^{\pm}(t)=\mathsf{x}^{\pm}_{i}(0)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathsf{c}t},\ \boldsymbol{\gamma}(t)=\boldsymbol{\gamma}(0)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathsf{c}t},\ \boldsymbol{\delta}(t)=\frac{1}{2\mathsf{c}}\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{-2\mathsf{c}t}\right)+\boldsymbol{\delta}(0)\mathrm{e}^{-2\mathsf{c}t}

and recombine to get
\mathfrak{D}_{t}
.
∎
