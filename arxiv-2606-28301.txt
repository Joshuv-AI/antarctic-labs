VGB for Masked Diffusion Model: Efficient Test-time Scaling for Reward Satisfaction and Sample Editing
Kijung JeonGeorgia Tech kjeon@gatech.edu, Thuy-Duong VuongUCSD thvuong@ucsd.edu, Molei TaoGeorgia Tech mtao@gatech.edu

Abstract
Inference-time scaling is a promising paradigm to improve generative models, especially when outputs must satisfy structural constraints or optimize downstream rewards. We consider Masked Diffusion Model (MDM) and introduce MDM-VGB, a discrete diffusion sampler that augments unmasking generation with theoretically principled reward-guided remasking. Inspired by the recent success of the classical Jerrum-Sinclair backtracking Markov chain [ 25 ] in reward-tilted generation [ 23 ] , MDM-VGB extends the backtracking random walk from a fixed prefix tree to a masked-state graph, allowing tokens to be unmasked and remasked at arbitrary positions. The resulting sampler favors unmasking and remasking moves that lead to higher-value partial configurations, enabling both effective high-reward generation and efficient repair of low-reward samples. We prove that MDM-VGB is robust to process-verifier noise and achieves quadratic complexity, while popular test-time heuristics such as best-of- N N can incur exponential complexity due to error accumulation. Our theoretical findings are corroborated by strong empirical performance, particularly on popular constraint-satisfaction and scientific benchmarks such as Sudoku and QM9 [ 22 ] . 1 1 1 All code is provided in the following repository: https://github.com/KraitGit/MDM-VGB .

Introduction

Many sequence-generation tasks require outputting configurations that satisfy global structural properties. Such properties are often easy to verify for complete sequences, but difficult to reliably extrapolate from partially generated configurations. Examples include mathematical proofs, computer programs, Sudoku puzzles, protein sequences for designing drug candidates: a completed configuration can often be accurately evaluated by a theorem prover, compiler, (Sudoku) constraint checker, or drug-property oracle, while evaluating whether a partial configuration can be extended to a valid full configuration is much harder.

In this work, we introduce
Masked-Diffusion-Model Value-Guided Backtracking
(MDM-VGB), a verifier-guided discrete diffusion sampler with remasking. MDM-VGB generalizes the classical Jerrum-Sinclair backtracking Markov chain
[
25
]
from fixed-order generation to any-order generation.
The Markov chain underlying MDM-VGB operates on partial configurations, i.e., masked states, where some coordinates are revealed, and the remaining coordinates are masked. Given an outcome reward on fully revealed configurations, we define the value of a partial configuration as the expected reward of compatible full configurations.
At each step, the Markov chain either reveals masked token(s) or remasks already revealed token(s), with transition probabilities biased toward configurations with larger estimated value. This rule gives a principled randomized-greedy way to reveal and remask tokens: tokens whose removal increases the value of the partial configuration are treated as unreliable and are more likely to be revised; similarly, unmasking decisions that lead to higher-value configurations are prioritized (see
Definition

4.2
).

This Markov chain supports both generation and editing. For generation, the chain starts at the fully masked root state (see
Figure

1(a)

While MDM-VGB also supports flexible block updates in the sense that each transition may reveal or re-mask
an arbitrary block of coordinates, we will place
particular emphasis on the
singleton-block case as in the original masked diffusion model, which we call Any-Order-Auto-Regressive VGB (AOAR-VGB). This simplest
variant already yields strong empirical gains while admitting the cleanest theoretical guarantees. This Markov chain provably targets the reward-tilted target distribution in the asymptotic time limit. In particular, in hard-constraint settings with a perfect outcome verifier, where invalid full configurations are certifiably rewardless, the sampler is guaranteed to never produce an invalid full configuration. Moreover, as in the fixed-order case, the chain mixes rapidly, even when subjected to substantial multiplicative error in the intermediate value estimates provided by the process verifiers. In particular, we show that AOAR-VGB converges to the target reward-tilted distribution within a number of Markov steps

Other related works

Recent works, such as
26
,
15
,
have considered alternative formulations of masked diffusion models (MDM) with remasking. Even
without an additional reward signal, remasking can empirically improve sample
quality by correcting errors introduced by MDM-style parallel decoding
[
26
]
. However, it is less clear how to remask in a principled way with quantitatively significant benefits. In fact,
the Markov-chain formulation of

15
also targets a reward-tilted stationary distribution, but unlike our method, it does not provide mixing-time or error-tolerance guarantees. Moreover,
15
remasks coordinates uniformly at random rather than by confidence, which reduces its ability to correct earlier mistakes. Remasking diffusion (ReMDM)

[
26
]
can use confidence scores to guide remasking decisions, but its updates follow a
factorized parallel decoding structure: all coordinates are resampled in
parallel from coordinate-wise conditionals. This can
be limiting for structured outputs with strong cross-coordinate dependencies.
For example, changing
1+2=4
to
1+2=3
gives a valid expression, whereas
simultaneously changing
4\to 3
and
2\to 1
does not. ReMDM does not target the reward-tilted distribution, and as with
15
, does not provide mixing-time or robustness guarantees.

Fixed-order backtracking (AR-VGB)
[
25
,
23
]
comes with strong theoretical guarantees, as MDM-VGB does, but it does not allow earlier mistakes to be edited without undoing a long suffix. Likewise, sampling-and-search methods like Sequential Monte Carlo
[
20
]
and Monte-Carlo Tree-Search

[
20
,
19
]
, as well as standard masked diffusion
[
3
,
24
,
2
,
14
]
, do not allow backtracking, and thus cannot repair earlier mistakes in the generative process. This is particularly problematic for structured languages, where an early error can make every subsequent extension invalid (see
Figure

7
).

\displaystyle\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(Y_{B}=a_{B}\mid x,z)

Masked diffusion model (MDM).

Following
[
24
]
, if a pretrained MDM is unavailable for a task, we train a discrete masked diffusion model
\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(\cdot|x)\equiv\pi_{\theta}
over
{\mathcal{Y}}
using a continuous masking process by minimizing the following objective function:

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MDM}}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{\begin{subarray}{c}(x,y)\sim\mathcal{D},t\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,1],z_{t}\sim q_{t}(\cdot\mid y)\end{subarray}}\left[\frac{\alpha_{t}^{\prime}}{1-\alpha_{t}}\sum_{i:\,z_{t,i}=\mathtt{[mask]}}\log\pi_{\theta}(Y_{i}=y_{i}\mid x,z_{t})\right].

where
\mathcal{D}
is the training data,
\alpha_{t}\in[0,1]
is a decreasing masking schedule,
\alpha^{\prime}_{t}
is its time derivative, and
z_{t}
is sampled from the distribution
q_{t}(\cdot|y)
over
\mathcal{Z}
defined by:

q_{t}(z_{t}\mid y)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(\alpha_{t}\mathbf{1}\{z_{t,i}=y_{i}\}+(1-\alpha_{t})\mathbf{1}\{z_{t,i}=\mathtt{[mask]}\}\right).

At inference time, MDM samples from
\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(\cdot|x)
by starting from
\varnothing
and iteratively revealing a new block of tokens according to the marginal of
\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(\cdot|x)
conditioned on the revealed tokens. In other words, at a current masked configuration

z
, the process reveals a new block
B\subset[n]\setminus R(z)
by sampling from
\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(Y_{B}=\cdot\mid x,z)
.

Reward-tilted sampling.

Given a base reference model
\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(\cdot\mid x)
and a nonnegative terminal score, i.e., reward function,
\tau(x,\cdot):{\mathcal{Y}}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},

define the
reward-tilted target distribution
\pi^{\star}(\cdot\mid x)
over
{\mathcal{Y}}
where:

\pi^{\star}(y\mid x):=\frac{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\tau(x,y)}{Z(x)},\qquad Z(x):=\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\tau(x,y).

We also define the marginal of
\pi^{\star}
at
z\in{\mathcal{Z}}
by
\pi^{\star}(z\mid x):=\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{C}}(z)}\pi^{\star}(y\mid x).
In particular, binary reward
\tau
(i.e.,
\tau(x,y)\in\{0,1\},\ \forall y\in{\mathcal{Y}}
) corresponds to the hard-constraint case.

Our goal

is to sample from
\pi^{*}
using a given base model
\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}
and terminal score
\tau
at inference time, without accessing or changing the base model’s parameters, i.e., via test-time scaling rather than RL fine-tuning.

Value of partial configurations.

For a given terminal score
\tau
, the ideal value of a partial configuration or masked state
z\in({\mathcal{V}}\cup\{\texttt{[mask]}\})^{n}
is

V^{\star}(x,z):=\mathbb{E}_{Y\sim\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(\cdot\mid x)}\!\left[\tau(x,Y)\mid Y\in{\mathcal{C}}(z)\right].

In practice,
V^{*}
is not available, and therefore we train a process verifier
\widehat{V}
to approximate
V^{*}
by fitting
V_{\theta}
with
mean squared error
, as in
23
:

{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{reg}}(\theta):=\mathbb{E}_{x,z,y}\left[\left(V_{\theta}(x,z)-\tau(x,y)\right)^{2}\right],

where
z
is a partial configuration sampled from the reference model
\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}
and
y
is a completion of
z
. After training, we use
\widehat{V}=V_{\theta}

\widehat{V}(x,z)
to be the indicator that the partial assignment

Value-guided Masked Diffusion Model (MDM-VGR).

Given a base MDM
\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}
and the value function
V^{\star}
with respect to a terminal reward
\tau

In practice, MDM-VGR uses a process verifier

Methods and Theoretical Guarantees

4.1
Formulation of MDM-VGB

To address the goal of
Section

3
, we now describe the MDM-VGB algorithm. At a high level, MDM-VGB is a random walk on the weighted masked state graph with appropriately chosen edge weights.
The edge weights depend geometrically on the estimated values of endpoint states, so that remasking or unmasking moves that lead to higher-value states are prioritized. We also rescale each edge weight by a scalar depending on the depths of
its endpoint states. This depth-dependent normalization is designed to balance forward moves that unmask new coordinates and backward moves that remask coordinates at each state and is crucial for ensuring the chain’s fast convergence.

The MDM-VGB Markov chain is defined by either of the following equivalent definitions.

Definition 4.1
.

Fix
\lambda\geq 0
and depth coefficients
s_{k,r}:=\binom{n-r}{k}^{-1}
, which satisfy
\binom{n-k}{r}s_{k,r}=\binom{k}{r}s_{k-r,r}
.
The MDM-VGB Markov chain is the random walk on the weighted masked state graph, where the edge weights are as follows: for neighboring states
u\sim v
, let
p
denote the lower-depth endpoint and
c
denote the higher-depth endpoint among
u,v
. Let the symmetric edge weight between
u,v
be

f(u,v)=f(v,u):=\begin{cases}s_{k(p),k(c)-k(p)}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(c\mid x)\widehat{V}(x,c)\widehat{V}(x,p)^{\lambda}&\text{ if }c\notin{\mathcal{Y}}\\
s_{k(p),k(c)-k(p)}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(c\mid x)\widehat{V}(x,c)&\text{ if }c\in{\mathcal{Y}}\end{cases}.

Equivalently, the same chain can be specified through its local forward and backward move weights. In both views,
s_{k,r}
is the depth-dependent normalization factor that balances forward moves revealing
r
new coordinates with backward moves re-masking
r
revealed coordinates.

Definition 4.2
.

For a state
z
at depth
k,
a child
c=z^{B\rightarrow a_{B}}\in C(z)
where
B\subseteq[n]\setminus R(z)
with
|B|=r,
and a backward parent
p=z^{-B^{\prime}}\in P(z)
where
B^{\prime}\subseteq R(z)
and
|B^{\prime}|=r^{\prime},
set:

\displaystyle w_{\rm fwd}(z\to c)

\displaystyle:=\begin{cases}s_{k,r}\widehat{V}(x,z)^{\lambda}\widehat{V}(x,c)\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(Y_{B}=a_{B}\mid x,z),&c\notin{\mathcal{Y}},\\
s_{k,r}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(Y_{B}=a_{B}\mid x,z)\widehat{V}(x,c),&c\in{\mathcal{Y}},\end{cases}

\displaystyle w_{\rm bwd}(z\to p)

\displaystyle:=\begin{cases}s_{k-r^{\prime},r^{\prime}}\widehat{V}(x,z)\widehat{V}(x,p)^{\lambda},&z\notin{\mathcal{Y}},\\
s_{k-r^{\prime},r^{\prime}}\widehat{V}(x,z),&z\in{\mathcal{Y}},\end{cases}

Let
W(z):=\sum_{c\in C(z)}w_{\rm fwd}(z\to c)+\sum_{p\in P(z)}w_{\rm bwd}(z\to p)
be the normalization factor at
z
. The MDM-VGB Markov chain’s transition probabilities at
z
are given by:

\displaystyle\forall c\in C(z):\mathbb{P}[z\to c]=\frac{w_{\rm fwd}(z\to c)}{W(z)}\text{ and }\forall p\in P(z):\quad\mathbb{P}[z\to p]=\frac{w_{\rm bwd}(z\to p)}{W(z)}

The first definition is more helpful for proving the theoretical properties of the Markov chain, while the second is more helpful for implementation.

Remark 4.1

(Role of
\lambda
)
.

The parameter
\lambda\geq 0
controls how strongly re-masking decisions depend on the value of the resulting parent state: larger
\lambda
makes the chain more likely to erase a block that yields a higher-value parent state. Our ablation study found that a moderate
\lambda
yields the best performance, as the chain can effectively edit problematic tokens while still retaining sufficient flexibility to explore alternative edits (see
Section

5.3
).

In the appendix, we further distinguish between the case
\lambda=0,
which we call
Balanced MDM-VGB
(see
Section

C.3
), and the general case
\lambda\geq 0,
which we call
Geometric Balanced MDM-VGB
(see
Sections

C.5
and
E.2
). The
\lambda=0
chain is closer to existing backtracking walks
[
25
,
23
]
, but it selects a uniformly random block for re-masking and thus is less effective at editing problematic tokens. In
Section

C.2
, we also show that the direct generalization of AR-VGB to the diffusion setting, which corresponds to setting
\lambda=0
and having no depth-normalization factor
s_{k,r}
, has exponentially slow convergence.

Remark 4.2

(Implementation details: shortlisting rule)
.

To improve efficiency, instead of exploring the entire neighborhood of a masked state, we follow the practical shortlisting convention of
23
: each MDM-VGB (or MDM-VGB-Momentum) step considers at most
L_{f}

choices of blocks to unmask,
L_{b}
choices of blocks to re-mask, and
K

\gamma(P_{\mathrm{AOAR}}):=1-\max\{|\lambda|:\lambda\in\Lambda(P_{\mathrm{AOAR}}),\ \lambda\neq 1\},

where
\Lambda(P_{\mathrm{AOAR}})
denotes the set of eigenvalues of
P_{\mathrm{AOAR}}
. Then

\gamma(P_{\mathrm{AOAR}})=\Omega\left(\left(\frac{m}{M}\right)^{2\lambda}\frac{1}{\kappa^{4}n^{2}}\right).

By standard Markov chain theory, this together with
Theorem

4.2
gives an upper bound on the mixing time to the target reward-tilted distribution
\pi^{\star}(\cdot|x)
over
{\mathcal{Y}}
, starting from any initial state.

Corollary 4.1
.

Assume
4.1
.
For any target accuracy level
\delta>0
, the distribution
Z_{T}
obtained after
T

satisfies
d_{\rm TV}\left(\mathcal{L}(Z_{T}\mid Z_{T}\in{\mathcal{Y}}),\pi^{\star}(\cdot\mid x)\right)\leq\delta
and
\mathbb{P}[Z_{T}\in{\mathcal{Y}}]\geq\Omega\left(\frac{1}{M^{\lambda}\kappa n}\right),

when

T\geq\Theta\!\left(\left(\frac{M}{m}\right)^{2\lambda}\kappa^{4}n^{2}\log\left(\frac{M^{\lambda}\kappa n}{\delta\mu(z)}\right)\right)

In particular, when
z
is the root
\varnothing,
it suffices to take
T=\Theta\!\left(\left(\frac{M}{m}\right)^{2\lambda}\kappa^{4}n^{2}\log\left(\frac{(M/m)^{\lambda}\kappa n}{\delta}\right)\right)

Remark 4.3
.

The bound above controls the target law conditioned on reaching a leaf, but the the stationary distribution
\mu
of the current chain’s mass on the leaves, i.e.
\mu({\mathcal{Y}}),
is only of order
1/n
. Thus, obtaining a leaf with constant probability would require running the chain
O(n)
times. By using the modification from
[
11
,
23
]
, i.e. increasing the holding probabilities at the leaves, we can instead make
\mu({\mathcal{Y}})
at least a constant, and obtain a quadratic-time sampler for
\pi^{\star}(\cdot|x)
. This modification is mainly a technical step to improve the theoretical analysis; in practice, the sampler simply stops after seeing a few leaves, so we keep the simpler construction of the Markov chain.

Proof sketch.

We discuss the analysis of AOAR-VGB. The proof is by relating the properties of AOAR-VGB to those of AR-VGB.
The key observation is that the weighted masked-state graph defining AOAR-VGB can be decomposed as a sum of weighted prefix-tree graphs that define AR-VGB. Consequently, the properties of AOAR-VGB can be compared with those of the corresponding AR-VGB walks. The stationary distribution probability at a state
z
is proportional to the total weight of the edges incident to
z.
First, the stationary distribution of AOAR-VGB is the average of the stationary distributions of the AR-VGB walks, and thus induces the same target distribution
\pi^{\star}
on the leaves as the AR-VGB walks. Second, the weight on the leaves under the stationary distribution of AOAR-VGB can be lower bounded by those of the AR-VGB chains. Third, the conductance of the masked-state graph, which corresponds to the relaxation time and mixing time of AOAR-VGB, can be lower bounded by the conductance of the prefix-tree graphs, which in turn can be lower bounded via the existing analysis in
[
25
,
23
]
. For details, see
Theorem

C.5
.

4.3
Formulation of MDM-VGB-Momentum

We introduce MDM-VGB-Momentum, a
momentum lift of MDM-VGB in the spirit of
[
11
]
that targets the same distribution over the full configurations, while reducing oscillatory behaviors. The lift augments each masked state
z
with a direction variable, yielding
two lifted states
(z,\downarrow)
and
(z,\uparrow)
. In reveal mode

(z,\downarrow)
, the chain can only reveal tokens, while in re-mask mode

(z,\uparrow)
, it can only re-mask tokens. At each state, the chain switches between these
two modes with a probability governed
by a parameter
\chi
chosen to reduce reveal/re-mask oscillations. See
Figure

3
for an illustration.

For AR-VGB, momentum has been shown
empirically to improve performance
[
23
]
; theoretical work by
[
11
]

also shows mixing-time improvements in the low-error regime, where the approximation parameter

\kappa
is sufficiently close to
1
. On the theory side, we prove that at a sufficiently low error–when
\kappa=1+O(1/n)
–MDM-VGB-Momentum reaches a leaf in expected
O(n)
time, achieving a quadratic speedup over the non-momentum version, which requires
\Omega(n^{2})
time
5
5

5

We suppress dependency on other parameters
M,m,\lambda.
. We expect analogous mixing-time improvements in this low error regime, but leave a full mixing time analysis to future work. In our experiments, MDM-VGB-Momentum tends to perform better on generation tasks,
whereas the non-momentum version performs slightly better on editing tasks.

Definition 4.3

(MDM-VGB-Momentum)
.

For a masked state
z
, a child
c\in C(z)
, and a parent
p\in P(z)
, let

and
w_{\rm bwd}(z\to p)
be as in
Definition

4.2
. Let

\displaystyle F(z)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{c\in C(z)}w_{\rm fwd}(z\to c),\quad B(z):=\sum_{p\in P(z)}w_{\rm bwd}(z\to p),

\displaystyle M(z)

\displaystyle:=\min\left\{F(z),B(z)\right\},\quad W(z):=F(z)+B(z).

Fix a parameter
\chi\in[0,1]
. The MDM-VGB-Momentum with parameter
\chi
is a Markov chain
on the lifted state space
\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}={\mathcal{Z}}\times\{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\downarrow},{\color[rgb]{0.7,0,0}\uparrow}\}

.

At

the Markov chain can either transition to

The transition probabilities are given by:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[(z,{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\downarrow})\to(c,{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\downarrow})]=\frac{w_{\rm fwd}(z\to c)}{W(z)},\,\mathbb{P}[(z,{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\downarrow})\to(z,{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\downarrow})]=\frac{\chi M(z)}{W(z)},\,\mathbb{P}[(z,{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\downarrow})\to(z,{\color[rgb]{0.7,0,0}\uparrow})]=\frac{B(z)-\chi M(z)}{W(z)}

At

The transition probabilities are given by:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[(z,{\color[rgb]{0.7,0,0}\uparrow})\to(p,{\color[rgb]{0.7,0,0}\uparrow})]=\frac{w_{\rm bwd}(z\to p)}{W(z)},\,\mathbb{P}[(z,{\color[rgb]{0.7,0,0}\uparrow})\to(z,{\color[rgb]{0.7,0,0}\uparrow})]=\frac{\chi M(z)}{W(z)},\,\mathbb{P}[(z,{\color[rgb]{0.7,0,0}\uparrow})\to(z,{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\downarrow})]=\frac{F(z)-\chi M(z)}{W(z)}

The following theorem characterizes asymptotic behavior of AOAR-VGB-Momentum.

Theorem 4.4

(Stationary distribution of AOAR-VGB-Momentum)
.

AOAR-VGB-Momentum with parameter
\chi\in[0,1]
as defined in
Definition

4.3
is an ergodic Markov chain, with stationary distribution
\widetilde{\mu}
defined by

\widetilde{\mu}(z,\downarrow)=\widetilde{\mu}(z,\uparrow)=\frac{1}{2}\mu(z)

where
\mu
is the stationary distribution of the AOAR-VGB chain. Hence the projection
\mathrm{proj}_{\#}\widetilde{\mu}
of
\tilde{\mu}
on
{\mathcal{Z}}
satisfies
\mathrm{proj}_{\#}\widetilde{\mu}=\mu
. In particular, if
\mu(\cdot\mid{\mathcal{Y}})=\pi^{\star}(\cdot\mid x)
then

\mathrm{proj}_{\#}\widetilde{\mu}(\cdot\mid{\mathcal{Y}})=\pi^{\star}(\cdot\mid x).

The following theorem shows that in the low error regime
\kappa=1+O(1/n)

O(n)
steps to hit a leaf, whereas AOAR-VGB requires
\Theta(n^{2})
steps.

Theorem 4.5
.

Consider the AOAR-VGB-Momentum with parameter

.

p_{\downarrow}(z)=\frac{F(z)}{F(z)+B(z)},\qquad p_{\uparrow}(z)=\frac{B(z)}{F(z)+B(z)},\qquad\Delta:=\sup_{z\in\tilde{{\mathcal{Z}}}_{+}^{\circ}(x)}\left|p_{\downarrow}(z)-p_{\uparrow}(z)\right|,

aims to design drug-like molecules using the QM9 benchmark

[

evaluates regulatory-sequence generation. We use

[
33

[
8
]

[
9
]
as the terminal score
R
. The terminal reward is the Pass@95 indicator defined from DeepSTARR developmental activity. The goal is to generate DNA sequences with high
predicted developmental enhancer activity. We learn the values of partial configurations using a small Transformer over D3LM tokens. We test multiple verifier model sizes (see

Protein motif scaffolding

1.94

Sudoku

For the base model, we use DiT-MDM, i.e., a masked discrete diffusion model

[
24

21

[
23

[
10

[
23

[()]

]

[)

12.9

6
Conclusion and Future Work

We introduced MDM-VGB, a reward-guided backtracking sampler for masked diffusion and any-order discrete generation. By extending value-guided backtracking from fixed prefix-tree to masked-state graph, MDM-VGB can reveal and re-mask arbitrary coordinates, allowing it to effectively repair faulty tokens. Theoretically, MDM-VGB efficiently target the reward-tilted law on full configurations and tolerate process verifiers noise. Empirically, MDM-VGB and MDM-VGB-Momentum improve the quality–cost frontier over baselines such as best-of-
N
and action level sampling (MDM-VGR) for reward-tilting generation and editing across scientific-design and constraint-satisfaction tasks, with especially large gains when the base model performs poorly.

A key limitation of our work lies in the cost of training high-quality process verifiers, especially in domains with long sequence length and large vocabulary. We plan to improve verifier training through distillation, or to directly learn the MDM-VGB transition weights via fine-tuning. On the inference-time side, we plan to incorporate hierarchical update and combine our method with parallel Monte Carlo sampling
14
. We hope these improvements will make MDM-VGB applicable on larger reasoning and coding benchmarks such as GSM8K, MATH500, HumanEval, and SWE-bench.

Appendix B
Shared Setup and Verifier Training

We first collect the notation shared by all VGB variants. Fix a conditioning context
x\sim\rho
, a sequence length
n
, and a finite vocabulary
{\mathcal{V}}
. The fully revealed state space and the masked state space are

{\mathcal{Y}}:={\mathcal{V}}^{n},\qquad{\mathcal{Z}}:=\left({\mathcal{V}}\cup\left\{\texttt{[mask]}\right\}\right)^{n},

where
\texttt{[mask]}\notin{\mathcal{V}}
is the masking symbol. For
z\in{\mathcal{Z}}
, let

R(z):=\left\{i\in\left[n\right]\mid z_{i}\neq\texttt{[mask]}\right\},\qquad k(z):=\left|R(z)\right|.

Here
R(z)
is the set of revealed coordinates and
k(z)
is the depth of
z
. The compatible completions of
z
are

{\mathcal{C}}(z):=\left\{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}\mid y_{i}=z_{i}\ \text{for all}\ i\in R(z)\right\}.

Equivalently, observing the partial state
z
restricts the unknown terminal sequence
Y
to the completion set
{\mathcal{C}}(z)
. As more coordinates are revealed, this set becomes more informative.

Tilted target.

Let
\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)
be a reference sequence model and let
\tau(x,y)\geq 0
be a terminal reward. These two objects define the
tilted target distribution

\pi^{*}(y\mid x):=\frac{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\tau(x,y)}{Z(x)},\qquad Z(x):=\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\tau(x,y).

The goal of the VGB procedures is to sample from
\pi^{*}(\cdot\mid x)
while using
\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}
as the proposal backbone.

Completion values.

To describe local moves on masked states, we need to know both how much reference probability remains under a partial assignment and how promising that partial assignment is under the reward. The first quantity is the
reference completion mass

M_{\mathrm{ref}}(x,z):=\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{C}}(z)}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)=\mathbb{P}_{Y\sim\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}(Y\in{\mathcal{C}}(z)\mid x).

This mass determines which masked states are reachable under the reference model:

{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathrm{reach}}(x):=\left\{z\in{\mathcal{Z}}:M_{\mathrm{ref}}(x,z)>0\right\}.

If
z\in{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathrm{reach}}(x)
, we say that
z
is
\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}
-reachable
. When the context is fixed, we write
{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathrm{reach}}
for
{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathrm{reach}}(x)
.

The second quantity is the conditional-expectation value

V^{*}(x,z):=\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}\left[\tau(x,Y)\mid x,\ Y\in{\mathcal{C}}(z)\right],

which is the object approximated by the verifier. For the analysis, it is also convenient to introduce the unnormalized tilted completion mass

U^{*}(x,z):=\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{C}}(z)}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\tau(x,y).

We note that these two views are related by

U^{*}(x,z)=M_{\mathrm{ref}}(x,z)V^{*}(x,z).

The quantity
U^{*}
is useful for stationarity proofs, but learning it directly would also require estimating the completion mass
M_{\mathrm{ref}}
. The VGB framework discussed in later sections avoids this extra estimation problem: its local transitions can be implemented using reference conditionals together with a learned approximation
\widehat{V}
of
V^{*}
.

Verifier training.

When a learned verifier is used, we train a masked-state verifier
\widehat{V}(x,z)
. During training, we fit a parametric model
V_{\theta}(x,z)
and then set
\widehat{V}
to the trained model.

For a sampled context
x
, we run several reference rollouts, each producing a terminal completion
y
. Along the way, the sampler reveals coordinates, producing a trajectory of intermediate masked states. We subsample several snapshots from each trajectory and assign all of them the same terminal label
r=\tau(x,y)
.

The verifier is trained with the Monte Carlo (MC) rollout regression objective

{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{reg}}(\theta):=\mathbb{E}_{x,z,y}\left[\left(V_{\theta}(x,z)-\tau(x,y)\right)^{2}\right],

where
z
is a sampled intermediate state from a rollout that terminates at
y
. The following proposition reveals the population target of this regression.

Proposition B.1

(MC regression recovers
V^{\star}
)
.

Consider the population rollout law
\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{ref}}
over triples
(x,z,y)
. Suppose this law is reference-consistent: whenever
z\in{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathrm{reach}}(x)
,

\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{ref}}(Y=y\mid x,z)=\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x,\ Y\in{\mathcal{C}}(z)).

Then the population regression objective

{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{reg}}(V):=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{ref}}}\left[\bigl(V(x,z)-\tau(x,Y)\bigr)^{2}\right]

recovers
V^{\star}
as its population optimum:

V^{\star}=\argmin_{V}{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{reg}}(V)\qquad\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{ref}}\text{-a.e.}

Proof.

For fixed
(x,z)
, the squared loss is minimized by the conditional mean

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{ref}}}\left[\tau(x,Y)\mid x,z\right].

Therefore, reference consistency gives

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{ref}}}\left[\tau(x,Y)\mid x,z\right]=\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}\left[\tau(x,Y)\mid x,\ Y\in{\mathcal{C}}(z)\right]=V^{\star}(x,z),

which proves the claim.
∎

Proposition

B.1
, under a reference-consistent population rollout law
\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{ref}}
, the population target of MC rollout regression is exactly
V^{\star}(x,z)
. Thus,
\widehat{V}
plays the role of an intermediate-state value function, analogous to the prefix-state value function in
23
, but lifted here to any-order partial states. The training procedure is summarized in

.

Appendix C
AOAR-VGB

We now extend the VGB perspective of
23
from left-to-right prefix trees to any-order masked states. The key difference is that the state is no longer a prefix. Instead, a state is a masked partial sequence
z\in{\mathcal{Z}}
, and a forward move reveals one currently masked coordinate. A backward move re-masks one currently revealed coordinate. This gives a natural random walk on the graph of masked states.

denotes its depth, and
{\mathcal{C}}(z)
denotes the set of full sequences compatible with
z
.

We write

for the fully masked state, so

. The fully revealed states are exactly the leaves
{\mathcal{Y}}={\mathcal{V}}^{n}
.

and token

, define the single-site reveal update
z^{j\leftarrow a}
by

\left(z^{j\leftarrow a}\right)_{i}=\begin{cases}a,&i=j,\\
z_{i},&i\neq j.\end{cases}

For a revealed coordinate
i\in R(z)
, define the single-site re-masking update
z^{-i}
by

\left(z^{-i}\right)_{\ell}=\begin{cases}\texttt{[mask]},&\ell=i,\\
z_{\ell},&\ell\neq i.\end{cases}

The AOAR neighbor sets are

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{AOAR}}(z)

\displaystyle:=\left\{z^{j\leftarrow a}\mid j\notin R(z),\ a\in{\mathcal{V}},\ M_{\text{ref}}(x,z^{j\leftarrow a})>0\right\},

(
Child neighborhood
)

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{AOAR}}(z)

\displaystyle:=\left\{z^{-i}\mid i\in R(z)\right\},

(
Parent neighborhood
)

and

N_{\mathrm{AOAR}}(z):=C_{\mathrm{AOAR}}(z)\cup P_{\mathrm{AOAR}}(z).

(
AOAR neighborhood
)

Thus,
C_{\mathrm{AOAR}}(z)
contains one-step reveal states, and
P_{\mathrm{AOAR}}(z)
contains one-step re-masked states.

The AOAR oracle assumption is that for every reachable state
z
and every
j\notin R(z)
, we can evaluate the single-coordinate reference conditional

\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(Y_{j}=a\mid x,z):=\mathbb{P}_{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}\left(Y_{j}=a\mid x,\ Y\in{\mathcal{C}}(z)\right),\qquad a\in{\mathcal{V}}.

Equivalently,

\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(Y_{j}=a\mid x,z)=\frac{M_{\text{ref}}(x,z^{j\leftarrow a})}{M_{\text{ref}}(x,z)}.

We first record the basic identities that will be used in the weighted-graph construction and the stationary analysis below.

Lemma C.1

(Single-site partition identity)
.

Fix a reachable state
z
and coordinate
j\notin R(z)
. Then

{\mathcal{C}}(z)=\bigsqcup_{a\in{\mathcal{V}}}{\mathcal{C}}(z^{j\leftarrow a}).

Consequently,

\sum_{a\in{\mathcal{V}}}U^{*}(x,z^{j\leftarrow a})=U^{*}(x,z),\qquad\sum_{a\in{\mathcal{V}}}M_{\text{ref}}(x,z^{j\leftarrow a})=M_{\text{ref}}(x,z).

Proof.

Every full sequence
y\in{\mathcal{C}}(z)
has a unique value
y_{j}=a
at coordinate
j
. Hence
y
belongs to exactly one set
{\mathcal{C}}(z^{j\leftarrow a})
. This proves the disjoint union. Summing the masses
\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\tau(x,y)
over this partition gives the identity for
U^{*}
. Setting
\tau\equiv 1
gives the identity for
M_{\text{ref}}
.
∎

Proposition C.1

(AOAR Bellman identity)
.

For every reachable state
z
and every
j\notin R(z)
,

V^{*}(x,z)=\sum_{a\in{\mathcal{V}}}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(Y_{j}=a\mid x,z)V^{*}(x,z^{j\leftarrow a}).

Proof.

By the law of total expectation,

V^{*}(x,z)=\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}\left[\tau(x,Y)\mid x,\ Y\in{\mathcal{C}}(z)\right].

Conditioning further on the value of
Y_{j}
, we obtain

V^{*}(x,z)=\sum_{a\in{\mathcal{V}}}\mathbb{P}_{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}(Y_{j}=a\mid x,Y\in{\mathcal{C}}(z))\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}\left[\tau(x,Y)\mid x,\ Y\in{\mathcal{C}}(z),\ Y_{j}=a\right].

The event
\left\{Y\in{\mathcal{C}}(z),\ Y_{j}=a\right\}
is exactly
\left\{Y\in{\mathcal{C}}(z^{j\leftarrow a})\right\}
. Therefore the inner conditional expectation is
V^{*}(x,z^{j\leftarrow a})
.
∎

Proposition C.2

(Exact target conditional)
.

For every reachable state
z
with
U^{*}(x,z)>0
, every
j\notin R(z)
, and every
a\in{\mathcal{V}}
,

\pi^{*}(Y_{j}=a\mid x,z)=\frac{U^{*}(x,z^{j\leftarrow a})}{U^{*}(x,z)}=\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(Y_{j}=a\mid x,z)\frac{V^{*}(x,z^{j\leftarrow a})}{V^{*}(x,z)}.

Proof.

By definition of conditional probability under the tilted target,

\pi^{*}(Y_{j}=a\mid x,z)=\frac{\mathbb{P}_{\pi^{*}}(Y\in{\mathcal{C}}(z^{j\leftarrow a})\mid x)}{\mathbb{P}_{\pi^{*}}(Y\in{\mathcal{C}}(z)\mid x)}.

Since

\mathbb{P}_{\pi^{*}}(Y\in{\mathcal{C}}(z^{j\leftarrow a})\mid x)=\frac{U^{*}(x,z^{j\leftarrow a})}{Z(x)}

and

\mathbb{P}_{\pi^{*}}(Y\in{\mathcal{C}}(z)\mid x)=\frac{U^{*}(x,z)}{Z(x)},

the first equality follows. For the second equality, use

U^{*}(x,z)=M_{\text{ref}}(x,z)V^{*}(x,z)

and

M_{\text{ref}}(x,z^{j\leftarrow a})=M_{\text{ref}}(x,z)\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(Y_{j}=a\mid x,z).

∎

C.2
Primitive AOAR-VGB

The most direct AOAR extension of AR-VGB uses the same local rule as the prefix-tree AR-VGB walk: forward moves are weighted by the reference conditional times the verifier value of the child, and backward moves are weighted by the verifier value of the current state.

Let
\widehat{V}(x,z)
denote the trained verifier. We use
terminal anchoring
: whenever the terminal reward is available at a leaf, the verifier is fixed to the true reward,

\widehat{V}(x,y):=\tau(x,y),\qquad y\in{\mathcal{Y}}.

Definition C.1

(
\gamma
-held primitive AOAR-VGB local kernel)
.

Fix a holding parameter
\gamma\geq 0
. For a reachable state
z
, define the primitive AOAR-VGB local weights by

Forward:

\displaystyle w(z\to z^{j\leftarrow a})

\displaystyle:=\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(Y_{j}=a\mid x,z)\,\widehat{V}(x,z^{j\leftarrow a}),\qquad

\displaystyle j\notin R(z),

Backward:

\displaystyle w(z\to z^{-i})

\displaystyle:=\widehat{V}(x,z),\qquad

\displaystyle i\in R(z).

Let

W_{\mathrm{PM}}(z):=\sum_{v\in N_{\mathrm{AOAR}}(z)}w(z\to v).

The associated non-self move kernel is

K_{\mathrm{PM}}(z,u):=\frac{w(z\to u)}{W_{\mathrm{PM}}(z)},\qquad u\in N_{\mathrm{AOAR}}(z).

The
\gamma
-held primitive AOAR-VGB kernel is

P_{\mathrm{PM}}^{(\gamma)}(z,z)=\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma},

and, for
u\in N_{\mathrm{AOAR}}(z)
,

P_{\mathrm{PM}}^{(\gamma)}(z,u)=\frac{1}{1+\gamma}K_{\mathrm{PM}}(z,u)=\frac{1}{1+\gamma}\frac{w(z\to u)}{\sum_{v\in N_{\mathrm{AOAR}}(z)}w(z\to v)}.

When
\gamma=1
, this recovers the usual
1/2
-lazy kernel. When
\gamma=0
, it is the non-lazy local random walk.

Although
M_{\text{ref}}
is not needed to implement the transition, it is useful to temporarily reintroduce it in the analysis. Multiplying the learned value by the reference completion mass
M_{\text{ref}}
gives the plug-in tilted mass

\widehat{U}(x,z):=M_{\text{ref}}(x,z)\widehat{V}(x,z).

Theorem C.1

(Weighted-graph representation and exact leaf law)
.

Construct an undirected graph on reachable masked states by connecting
z
and
z^{j\leftarrow a}
whenever
j\notin R(z)
and
z^{j\leftarrow a}
is reachable. Assign the symmetric edge weight

f_{\mathrm{PM}}(z,z^{j\leftarrow a}):=\widehat{U}(x,z^{j\leftarrow a})=M_{\text{ref}}(x,z^{j\leftarrow a})\widehat{V}(x,z^{j\leftarrow a}).

For a state
z
, let its weighted degree be

D_{\mathrm{PM}}(z):=\sum_{u\sim z}f_{\mathrm{PM}}(z,u),

where
u\sim z
means that
u
and
z
are connected by an edge in the AOAR weighted graph.

Then, for every
\gamma\geq 0
, the
\gamma
-held primitive AOAR-VGB kernel
P_{\mathrm{PM}}^{(\gamma)}
from
Definition

C.1
is exactly the
\gamma
-held weighted random walk on this graph. Also, its stationary distribution is

\mu_{\mathrm{PM}}(z)=\frac{D_{\mathrm{PM}}(z)}{\sum_{z^{\prime}}D_{\mathrm{PM}}(z^{\prime})}.

Moreover, if
\widehat{V}(x,y)=\tau(x,y)
on all leaves
y\in{\mathcal{Y}}
, then the stationary law conditioned on the leaves is exactly the tilted target:

\mu_{\mathrm{PM}}(y\mid y\in{\mathcal{Y}})=\pi^{*}(y\mid x).

Proof.

Fix a reachable state
z
. For a forward neighbor
z^{j\leftarrow a}
, we have

\frac{f_{\mathrm{PM}}(z,z^{j\leftarrow a})}{M_{\text{ref}}(x,z)}=\frac{M_{\text{ref}}(x,z^{j\leftarrow a})}{M_{\text{ref}}(x,z)}\widehat{V}(x,z^{j\leftarrow a})=\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(Y_{j}=a\mid x,z)\widehat{V}(x,z^{j\leftarrow a}).

This is exactly the forward local weight.

For a backward neighbor
z^{-i}
, the edge between
z^{-i}
and
z
has weight

f_{\mathrm{PM}}(z^{-i},z)=\widehat{U}(x,z)=M_{\text{ref}}(x,z)\widehat{V}(x,z).

Dividing by the same state-dependent factor
M_{\text{ref}}(x,z)
gives the backward local weight

\widehat{V}(x,z).

Thus, from every state
z
, the local weights in
Definition

C.1
are exactly the incident edge weights divided by the common positive factor
M_{\text{ref}}(x,z)
. This common factor cancels under normalization. Therefore the non-self move kernel is the weighted random walk on the graph with edge weights
f_{\mathrm{PM}}
:

K_{\mathrm{PM}}(z,u)=\frac{f_{\mathrm{PM}}(z,u)}{D_{\mathrm{PM}}(z)},\qquad u\sim z.

This is the standard random walk on a network with conductances
f_{\mathrm{PM}}(z,u)
in the sense of
16
.

We now verify stationarity directly. Let

D_{\mathrm{tot}}:=\sum_{z^{\prime}}D_{\mathrm{PM}}(z^{\prime}),\qquad\mu_{\mathrm{PM}}(z):=\frac{D_{\mathrm{PM}}(z)}{D_{\mathrm{tot}}}.

For two distinct neighboring states
z\sim u
, the
\gamma
-held kernel satisfies

P_{\mathrm{PM}}^{(\gamma)}(z,u)=\frac{1}{1+\gamma}\frac{f_{\mathrm{PM}}(z,u)}{D_{\mathrm{PM}}(z)}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathrm{PM}}(z)P_{\mathrm{PM}}^{(\gamma)}(z,u)

\displaystyle=\frac{D_{\mathrm{PM}}(z)}{D_{\mathrm{tot}}}\cdot\frac{1}{1+\gamma}\frac{f_{\mathrm{PM}}(z,u)}{D_{\mathrm{PM}}(z)}

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{\mathrm{PM}}(z,u)}{(1+\gamma)D_{\mathrm{tot}}}.

Since
f_{\mathrm{PM}}(z,u)=f_{\mathrm{PM}}(u,z)
, the same calculation gives

\mu_{\mathrm{PM}}(u)P_{\mathrm{PM}}^{(\gamma)}(u,z)=\frac{f_{\mathrm{PM}}(z,u)}{(1+\gamma)D_{\mathrm{tot}}}.

Thus detailed balance holds on every non-self edge. For the self-loop terms,

\mu_{\mathrm{PM}}(z)P_{\mathrm{PM}}^{(\gamma)}(z,z)=\mu_{\mathrm{PM}}(z)\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma},

so detailed balance is immediate. Therefore
P_{\mathrm{PM}}^{(\gamma)}
is reversible with respect to
\mu_{\mathrm{PM}}
, and hence
\mu_{\mathrm{PM}}
is stationary.

Now consider a leaf
y\in{\mathcal{Y}}
. It has exactly
n
parents, one for each coordinate that can be re-masked. For each parent
y^{-i}
, the edge weight is

f_{\mathrm{PM}}(y^{-i},y)=M_{\text{ref}}(x,y)\widehat{V}(x,y)=\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\tau(x,y).

Here we used terminal anchoring. Hence

D_{\mathrm{PM}}(y)=n\,\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\tau(x,y).

The factor
n
is constant across leaves. Therefore

\mu_{\mathrm{PM}}(y\mid y\in{\mathcal{Y}})=\frac{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\tau(x,y)}{\sum_{y^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{Y}}}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y^{\prime}\mid x)\tau(x,y^{\prime})}=\pi^{*}(y\mid x).

∎

When the verifier is exact, every
\gamma
-held primitive AOAR-VGB chain has a particularly simple stationary law.

Corollary C.1

(Exact primitive stationary law)
.

For any
\gamma\geq 0
, if
\widehat{V}=V^{*}
, then the stationary distribution of
P_{\mathrm{PM}}^{(\gamma)}
satisfies

\mu_{\mathrm{PM}}^{*}(z)\propto U^{*}(x,z).

Proof.

When
\widehat{V}=V^{*}
, we have
\widehat{U}=U^{*}
. For a state
z
of depth
k=k(z)
, every backward edge contributes
U^{*}(x,z)
, and there are
k
such edges. For each unrevealed coordinate
j\notin R(z)
,
Lemma

C.1
gives

\sum_{a\in{\mathcal{V}}}U^{*}(x,z^{j\leftarrow a})=U^{*}(x,z).

There are
n-k
unrevealed coordinates, so the total forward incident weight is
(n-k)U^{*}(x,z)
. Hence

D_{\mathrm{PM}}(z)=kU^{*}(x,z)+(n-k)U^{*}(x,z)=nU^{*}(x,z).

By
Theorem

C.1
, for every
\gamma\geq 0
,

\mu_{\mathrm{PM}}^{*}(z)\propto D_{\mathrm{PM}}(z).

Therefore

\mu_{\mathrm{PM}}^{*}(z)\propto U^{*}(x,z).

∎

However, we note that the same stationary law also exposes a depth-allocation problem: although the conditional law on leaves is correct, the primitive chain assigns exponentially small marginal mass to the leaf layer, as follows:

\Needspace

8

Proposition C.3

(Exponential leaf-mass dilution of primitive AOAR-VGB)
.

For any
\gamma\geq 0
, assume
\widehat{V}=V^{*}
. Then the stationary mass of depth
k
under
P_{\mathrm{PM}}^{(\gamma)}
is

\mu_{\mathrm{PM}}^{*}(k(z)=k)=\frac{\binom{n}{k}}{2^{n}}.

In particular,

\mu_{\mathrm{PM}}^{*}({\mathcal{Y}})=2^{-n}.

Proof.

For any fixed depth
k
, double-counting identity gives

\displaystyle\sum_{z:\,k(z)=k}U^{*}(x,z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{z:k(z)=k}\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{C}}(z)}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\tau(x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\tau(x,y)\sum_{z:k(z)=k}\mathbf{1}\left\{y\in{\mathcal{C}}(z)\right\}=\binom{n}{k}Z(x).

Indeed, each full sequence
y\in{\mathcal{Y}}
contributes the mass
\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\tau(x,y)
to exactly
\binom{n}{k}
masked states of depth
k
, one for each choice of
k
revealed coordinates. Therefore

\sum_{z\in{\mathcal{Z}}}U^{*}(x,z)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{z:k(z)=k}U^{*}(x,z)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}Z(x)=2^{n}Z(x).

By
Corollary

C.1
, the primitive stationary law is proportional to
U^{*}
, so

\mu_{\mathrm{PM}}^{*}(k(z)=k)=\frac{\binom{n}{k}Z(x)}{2^{n}Z(x)}=\frac{\binom{n}{k}}{2^{n}}.

Taking
k=n
gives
\mu_{\mathrm{PM}}^{*}({\mathcal{Y}})=2^{-n}
.
∎

Thus, primitive AOAR-VGB has the correct leaf-conditioned distribution, but the stationary chain spends exponentially little mass on leaves. We therefore introduce Balanced AOAR-VGB, a depth-balanced variant designed to resolve this leaf-mass dilution problem.

C.3
Balanced AOAR-VGB

Primitive AOAR-VGB treats every single-site edge symmetrically. In the AOAR graph, however, the number of states at each depth grows as
\binom{n}{k}
. As a result, the primitive stationary law puts most of its mass around the middle depths. Balanced AOAR-VGB rescales forward and backward moves as a function of depth to suppress this combinatorial depth bias.

Definition C.2

(
\gamma
-held depth-rescaled AOAR-VGB local kernel)
.

Fix a holding parameter
\gamma\geq 0
and positive coefficients

\beta_{0},\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{n-1}>0,\qquad\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{n}>0.

For a reachable state
z
with
k=k(z)
, define the depth-rescaled local weights by

Forward:

\displaystyle w^{(k)}(z\to z^{j\leftarrow a})

\displaystyle:=\beta_{k}\,\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(Y_{j}=a\mid x,z)\widehat{V}(x,z^{j\leftarrow a}),\qquad

\displaystyle j\notin R(z),

Backward:

\displaystyle w^{(k)}(z\to z^{-i})

\displaystyle:=\alpha_{k}\,\widehat{V}(x,z),\qquad

\displaystyle i\in R(z).

Let

W_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z):=\sum_{u\in N_{\mathrm{AOAR}}(z)}w^{(k)}(z\to u).

The associated non-self move kernel is

K_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z,u):=\frac{w^{(k)}(z\to u)}{W_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z)},\qquad u\in N_{\mathrm{AOAR}}(z).

The
\gamma
-held balanced AOAR-VGB kernel is

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{BAL}}^{(\gamma)}(z,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma},

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{BAL}}^{(\gamma)}(z,u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+\gamma}K_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z,u)=\frac{1}{1+\gamma}\frac{w^{(k)}(z\to u)}{\sum_{v\in N_{\mathrm{AOAR}}(z)}w^{(k)}(z\to v)},\quad u\in N_{\mathrm{AOAR}}(z).

When
\gamma=1
, this recovers the usual
1/2
-lazy kernel. When
\gamma=0
, it is the non-lazy local random walk.

In the
Proposition

C.4
below, we use the word “move” to mean a non-self transition. Thus, conditioning on
\mathrm{move}
means conditioning on the event that the kernel does not take the holding self-loop and instead chooses a neighbor in
N_{\mathrm{AOAR}}(z)
. The events
\mathrm{forward}
and
\mathrm{backward}
mean that the selected neighbor lies in
C_{\mathrm{AOAR}}(z)
or
P_{\mathrm{AOAR}}(z)
, respectively. Since the holding probability is common to all non-self moves, the conditional directional probabilities below do not depend on
\gamma
.

Proposition C.4

(Exact directional masses and balanced condition)
.

Assume
\widehat{V}=V^{*}
. Let
z
be a reachable state of interior depth
1\leq k\leq n-1
with
V^{*}(x,z)>0
. Then the exact total forward and backward local masses are

W_{\mathrm{fwd}}^{(k),*}(x,z)=(n-k)\beta_{k}V^{*}(x,z),\qquad W_{\mathrm{bwd}}^{(k),*}(x,z)=k\alpha_{k}V^{*}(x,z).

Therefore, conditional on making a non-self move,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{forward}\mid x,z,\mathrm{move})

\displaystyle=\frac{(n-k)\beta_{k}}{(n-k)\beta_{k}+k\alpha_{k}},

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{backward}\mid x,z,\mathrm{move})

\displaystyle=\frac{k\alpha_{k}}{(n-k)\beta_{k}+k\alpha_{k}}.

In particular, the forward and backward directions are exactly balanced at depth
k
if and only if

(n-k)\beta_{k}=k\alpha_{k}.

Proof.

For each unrevealed coordinate
j\notin R(z)
,
Proposition

C.1
gives

\sum_{a\in{\mathcal{V}}}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(Y_{j}=a\mid x,z)V^{*}(x,z^{j\leftarrow a})=V^{*}(x,z).

There are
n-k
unrevealed coordinates, so the total forward mass is

\displaystyle W_{\mathrm{fwd}}^{(k),*}(x,z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\notin R(z)}\sum_{a\in{\mathcal{V}}}\beta_{k}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(Y_{j}=a\mid x,z)V^{*}(x,z^{j\leftarrow a})=(n-k)\beta_{k}V^{*}(x,z).

There are
k
revealed coordinates, and each backward move has weight
\alpha_{k}V^{*}(x,z)
. Hence

W_{\mathrm{bwd}}^{(k),*}(x,z)=\sum_{i\in R(z)}\alpha_{k}V^{*}(x,z)=k\alpha_{k}V^{*}(x,z).

The conditional directional probabilities follow by normalizing the forward and backward masses among non-self moves. The common holding factor
1/(1+\gamma)
cancels after conditioning on
\mathrm{move}
. Exact balance is therefore equivalent to

(n-k)\beta_{k}V^{*}(x,z)=k\alpha_{k}V^{*}(x,z),

or simply
(n-k)\beta_{k}=k\alpha_{k}
.
∎

Remark C.1

(Primitive AOAR-VGB as the unrescaled case)
.

Primitive AOAR-VGB corresponds to the unrescaled choice

\beta_{k}=1,\qquad\alpha_{k}=1

at every interior depth. Under this choice,
Proposition

C.4
gives

\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{forward}\mid x,z,\mathrm{move})=\frac{n-k}{n},\qquad\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{backward}\mid x,z,\mathrm{move})=\frac{k}{n}.

Thus, the primitive chain strongly favors forward moves near the root and strongly favors backward moves near the leaves.

This local depth bias is consistent with the global pathology in
Proposition

C.3
: under the exact primitive stationary law, the depth marginal is binomial and the leaf layer receives only
2^{-n}
stationary mass. Balanced AOAR-VGB removes this local directional bias by choosing
\alpha_{k},\beta_{k}
so that

(n-k)\beta_{k}=k\alpha_{k},

making the forward and backward directions equally likely after conditioning on a non-self move.

We next give the stationary law for the balanced chain. The following edge-coefficient representation is the cleanest way to see that terminal exactness is preserved even when
\widehat{V}
is imperfect.

Let
g_{1}>0
be arbitrary, and define
g_{2},\dots,g_{n}
recursively by

g_{k+1}=g_{k}\frac{\beta_{k}}{\alpha_{k}},\qquad k=1,\dots,n-1.

We set
\beta_{0}=\alpha_{n}=1
. These boundary choices do not affect the normalized transition, since only forward moves are available at the root and only backward moves are available at the leaves.

Theorem C.2

(Weighted-graph representation of balanced AOAR-VGB)
.

Construct an undirected graph on reachable masked states with single-site AOAR edges. For an edge between a depth-
k
parent
z
and its child
z^{j\leftarrow a}
, assign the symmetric edge weight

f_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z,z^{j\leftarrow a}):=g_{k+1}\,M_{\text{ref}}(x,z^{j\leftarrow a})\widehat{V}(x,z^{j\leftarrow a}).

Then, for every
\gamma\geq 0
, the balanced AOAR-VGB kernel from
Definition

C.2
is exactly the
\gamma
-held weighted random walk on this graph. Hence its stationary distribution is

\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z)=\frac{D_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z)}{\sum_{z^{\prime}}D_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z^{\prime})},\qquad D_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z):=\sum_{u\sim z}f_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z,u).

If
\widehat{V}(x,y)=\tau(x,y)
for all leaves
y\in{\mathcal{Y}}
, then

\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(y\mid y\in{\mathcal{Y}})=\pi^{*}(y\mid x).

Proof.

Let
z
be a state of depth
k
. First consider a forward edge
z\to z^{j\leftarrow a}
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{f_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z,z^{j\leftarrow a})}{M_{\text{ref}}(x,z)}

\displaystyle=g_{k+1}\frac{M_{\text{ref}}(x,z^{j\leftarrow a})}{M_{\text{ref}}(x,z)}\widehat{V}(x,z^{j\leftarrow a})

\displaystyle=g_{k+1}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(Y_{j}=a\mid x,z)\widehat{V}(x,z^{j\leftarrow a}).

For a backward edge from
z
to
z^{-i}
, the child in the edge is
z
, so

\frac{f_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z^{-i},z)}{M_{\text{ref}}(x,z)}=g_{k}\widehat{V}(x,z).

For an interior state, the recursion

\frac{g_{k+1}}{g_{k}}=\frac{\beta_{k}}{\alpha_{k}}

implies
g_{k+1}/\beta_{k}=g_{k}/\alpha_{k}
. Thus, at every interior depth
k
, the incident edge weights divided by
M_{\text{ref}}(x,z)
are proportional to the local depth-rescaled weights in
Definition

C.2
. The proportionality factor is common across all outgoing neighbors of
z
, and hence cancels under normalization.

At the root and leaves, we use the boundary convention
\beta_{0}=\alpha_{n}=1
. Since only forward moves are available at the root and only backward moves are available at the leaves, these boundary choices do not change the normalized transition. Therefore
P_{\mathrm{BAL}}^{(\gamma)}
is exactly the
\gamma
-held weighted random walk on the graph with edge weights
f_{\mathrm{BAL}}
:

P_{\mathrm{BAL}}^{(\gamma)}(z,z)=\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma},\qquad P_{\mathrm{BAL}}^{(\gamma)}(z,u)=\frac{1}{1+\gamma}\frac{f_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z,u)}{D_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z)},\quad u\sim z.

We now verify the stationary distribution directly. This is the standard random walk on a network with conductances
f_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z,u)
, for which the stationary distribution is proportional to the vertex conductance
D_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z)

[
16
]
. For completeness, we check detailed balance. Let

D_{\mathrm{tot}}:=\sum_{z^{\prime}}D_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z^{\prime}),\qquad\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z):=\frac{D_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z)}{D_{\mathrm{tot}}}.

For two distinct neighboring states
z\sim u
,

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z)P_{\mathrm{BAL}}^{(\gamma)}(z,u)=\frac{D_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z)}{D_{\mathrm{tot}}}\cdot\frac{1}{1+\gamma}\frac{f_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z,u)}{D_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z)}=\frac{f_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z,u)}{(1+\gamma)D_{\mathrm{tot}}}.

Since the edge weight is symmetric,
f_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z,u)=f_{\mathrm{BAL}}(u,z)
, the same calculation gives

\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(u)P_{\mathrm{BAL}}^{(\gamma)}(u,z)=\frac{f_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z,u)}{(1+\gamma)D_{\mathrm{tot}}}.

Thus, detailed balance holds on every non-self edge. For the self-loop terms,

\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z)P_{\mathrm{BAL}}^{(\gamma)}(z,z)=\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z)\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma},

so detailed balance is trivial. Therefore
P_{\mathrm{BAL}}^{(\gamma)}
is reversible with respect to
\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}
, and hence
\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}
is stationary.

Now let
y\in{\mathcal{Y}}
be a leaf. Each of its
n
incident edges has weight

g_{n}M_{\text{ref}}(x,y)\widehat{V}(x,y)=g_{n}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\tau(x,y).

Thus

D_{\mathrm{BAL}}(y)=ng_{n}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\tau(x,y).

The factor
ng_{n}
is constant over leaves, so conditioning on
{\mathcal{Y}}
gives

\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(y\mid y\in{\mathcal{Y}})=\frac{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\tau(x,y)}{\sum_{y^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{Y}}}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y^{\prime}\mid x)\tau(x,y^{\prime})}=\pi^{*}(y\mid x).

∎

Remark C.2

(Zero holding in the practical sampler)
.

The holding parameter
\gamma
is useful for connecting the balanced AOAR-VGB transition to the standard lazy random-walk formalism, but explicit self-loop steps have limited algorithmic value in implementation and directly add computational cost.

Following the practical convention in
23
, we therefore set
\gamma=0
in our AOAR-VGB implementation and use the non-lazy local move kernel. The stationarity argument in
Theorem

C.2
is stated for every
\gamma\geq 0
, so this practical choice does not change the leaf-conditioned stationary law.

When the verifier is exact, the weighted-degree expression from
Theorem

C.2
simplifies to the tilted mass
U^{*}
multiplied by a depth-dependent coefficient.

Corollary C.2

(Exact balanced stationary law)
.

Assume
\widehat{V}=V^{*}
. Define

d_{0}:=ng_{1},\qquad d_{n}:=ng_{n},\qquad d_{k}:=kg_{k}+(n-k)g_{k+1}\quad(1\leq k\leq n-1).

Then, for every
\gamma\geq 0
, the exact balanced stationary law satisfies

\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}^{*}(z)\propto d_{k(z)}\,U^{*}(x,z).

Proof.

Let
z
have depth
k
. If
1\leq k\leq n-1
, its backward incident edges contribute

kg_{k}U^{*}(x,z).

For each unrevealed coordinate
j\notin R(z)
,
Lemma

C.1
gives

\sum_{a\in{\mathcal{V}}}g_{k+1}U^{*}(x,z^{j\leftarrow a})=g_{k+1}U^{*}(x,z).

There are
n-k
unrevealed coordinates, so the forward incident weight is

(n-k)g_{k+1}U^{*}(x,z).

Thus

D_{\mathrm{BAL}}^{*}(z)=\left(kg_{k}+(n-k)g_{k+1}\right)U^{*}(x,z).

The root and leaf cases give
d_{0}=ng_{1}
and
d_{n}=ng_{n}
, respectively. By
Theorem

C.2
, the stationary distribution is proportional to
D_{\mathrm{BAL}}^{*}
for every
\gamma\geq 0
, which proves the claim.
∎

Corollary C.3

(Canonical balanced AOAR-VGB)
.

Set

\beta_{k}=k,\qquad\alpha_{k}=n-k,\qquad 1\leq k\leq n-1.

Then, the exact forward and backward directional probabilities are both
1/2
at every interior depth. Choosing
g_{1}=1
, the corresponding edge coefficients are

g_{k}=\frac{1}{\binom{n-1}{k-1}},\qquad 1\leq k\leq n.

Moreover, under
\widehat{V}=V^{*}
, for every
\gamma\geq 0
,

\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}^{*}({\mathcal{Y}})=\frac{1}{2n}.

Proof.

The balanced condition follows immediately:

(n-k)\beta_{k}=(n-k)k=k(n-k)=k\alpha_{k}.

The recursion for
g_{k}
becomes

g_{k+1}=g_{k}\frac{k}{n-k}.

Starting from
g_{1}=1
, this gives

g_{k}=\prod_{r=1}^{k-1}\frac{r}{n-r}=\frac{(k-1)!(n-k)!}{(n-1)!}=\frac{1}{\binom{n-1}{k-1}}.

For
1\leq k\leq n-1
,

d_{k}=kg_{k}+(n-k)g_{k+1}=kg_{k}+kg_{k}=2kg_{k}=\frac{2n}{\binom{n}{k}}.

At the boundaries,

d_{0}=d_{n}=n.

Using the double-counting identity

\displaystyle\sum_{z:\,k(z)=k}U^{*}(x,z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{z:\,k(z)=k}\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{C}}(z)}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\tau(x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\tau(x,y)\sum_{z:\,k(z)=k}\mathbf{1}\left\{y\in{\mathcal{C}}(z)\right\}=\binom{n}{k}Z(x),

we can compute the unnormalized stationary weight layer by layer. For the root layer,
\sum_{z:\,k(z)=0}U^{*}(x,z)=Z(x)
and
d_{0}=n
, so its total stationary weight is
nZ(x)
. For the leaf layer,
\sum_{z:\,k(z)=n}U^{*}(x,z)=Z(x)
and
d_{n}=n
, so its total stationary weight is also
nZ(x)
. For each interior depth
1\leq k\leq n-1
, the total stationary weight is

d_{k}\sum_{z:\,k(z)=k}U^{*}(x,z)=\frac{2n}{\binom{n}{k}}\binom{n}{k}Z(x)=2nZ(x).

Hence the total weight over all depths is

nZ(x)+nZ(x)+(n-1)2nZ(x)=2n^{2}Z(x).

The leaf layer has weight
nZ(x)
, so

\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}^{*}({\mathcal{Y}})=\frac{nZ(x)}{2n^{2}Z(x)}=\frac{1}{2n}.

∎

Thus, canonical balanced AOAR-VGB keeps the exact target law on leaves while increasing the stationary leaf mass from
2^{-n}
to order
1/n
.

C.4
Theoretical Framework and Inference Algorithms

We now state the mixing framework for canonical balanced AOAR-VGB. The argument follows the same high-level principle as VGB on a prefix tree: stochastic backtracking defines a reversible walk whose leaf-conditioned stationary law is exact, and whose conductance can be controlled under a multiplicative value-approximation assumption.

Assumption C.1

(Uniform multiplicative verifier accuracy)
.

There exists
\kappa\geq 1
such that for every reachable non-leaf state
z
,

\kappa^{-1}V^{*}(x,z)\leq\widehat{V}(x,z)\leq\kappa V^{*}(x,z).

At leaves, we use terminal anchoring:

\widehat{V}(x,y)=\tau(x,y),\qquad y\in{\mathcal{Y}}.

The canonical balanced AOAR graph can be viewed as an average of fixed-order VGB trees. Let
\mathsf{Perm}_{n}
denote the set of coordinate orders, i.e., permutations of
[n]
. For
\omega=(\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{n})\in\mathsf{Perm}_{n}
, define the fixed-order state set

{\mathcal{Z}}_{\omega}:=\left\{z\in{\mathcal{Z}}\mid R(z)=\left\{\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{k}\right\}\text{ for some }k\in\left\{0,\dots,n\right\}\right\}.

This is a tree whose forward move at depth
k
reveals coordinate
\omega_{k+1}
. On this tree, define the edge weight

f_{\omega}(z,z^{\omega_{k+1}\leftarrow a}):=M_{\text{ref}}(x,z^{\omega_{k+1}\leftarrow a})\widehat{V}(x,z^{\omega_{k+1}\leftarrow a}).

Lemma C.2

(Fixed-order tree conductance)
.

We call

D_{\omega}(z):=\sum_{u\sim z}f_{\omega}(z,u),\qquad\mu_{\omega}(z):=\frac{D_{\omega}(z)}{\sum_{z^{\prime}}D_{\omega}(z^{\prime})}.

the weighted degree and the degree-normalized stationary law, respectively. For any nonempty set
A\subsetneq{\mathcal{Z}}_{\omega}
, define its set conductance and the tree conductance by

\Phi_{\omega}(A):=\frac{\sum_{z\in A,\ u\notin A}f_{\omega}(z,u)}{\sum_{z\in A}D_{\omega}(z)},\qquad\Phi_{\omega}:=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\subseteq{\mathcal{Z}}_{\omega}\\
0<\mu_{\omega}(A)\leq 1/2\end{subarray}}\Phi_{\omega}(A).

Assuming
C.1
, the random walk on each fixed-order tree
{\mathcal{Z}}_{\omega}
satisfies

\Phi_{\omega}\geq\frac{1}{4\kappa^{2}n}.

Furthermore, its stationary leaf mass satisfies

\mu_{\omega}({\mathcal{Y}})=\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}\mu_{\omega}(y)\geq\frac{1}{4\kappa n}.

Proof.

Write

\widehat{U}(x,z):=M_{\text{ref}}(x,z)\widehat{V}(x,z).

For non-leaf states,
C.1
gives

\kappa^{-1}U^{*}(x,z)\leq\widehat{U}(x,z)\leq\kappa U^{*}(x,z).

On leaves, terminal anchoring gives
\widehat{U}(x,y)=U^{*}(x,y)
.

For a fixed-order tree, the weighted degree
D_{\omega}(z)
is the sum of the parent edge and the child edges incident to
z
. If
k=k(z)
, then

D_{\omega}(z)=\mathbf{1}\left\{k>0\right\}\widehat{U}(x,z)+\mathbf{1}\left\{k<n\right\}\sum_{a\in{\mathcal{V}}}\widehat{U}(x,z^{\omega_{k+1}\leftarrow a}).

Using
\widehat{U}\leq\kappa U^{*}
and the partition identity for the next reveal coordinate,

\displaystyle D_{\omega}(z)

\displaystyle\leq\kappa\mathbf{1}\left\{k>0\right\}U^{*}(x,z)+\kappa\mathbf{1}\left\{k<n\right\}\sum_{a\in{\mathcal{V}}}U^{*}(x,z^{\omega_{k+1}\leftarrow a})

\displaystyle=\kappa\bigl(\mathbf{1}\left\{k>0\right\}+\mathbf{1}\left\{k<n\right\}\bigr)U^{*}(x,z)\leq 2\kappa U^{*}(x,z).

Let
D_{\omega,\mathrm{tot}}:=\sum_{z\in{\mathcal{Z}}_{\omega}}D_{\omega}(z)
denote the total unnormalized stationary weight of the fixed-order tree. Then

D_{\omega,\mathrm{tot}}\leq 2\kappa\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{z:\,k(z)=k,\ z\in{\mathcal{Z}}_{\omega}}U^{*}(x,z).

For each fixed depth in a fixed-order tree, the compatible states partition the leaves, so

\sum_{z:\,k(z)=k,\ z\in{\mathcal{Z}}_{\omega}}U^{*}(x,z)=Z(x).

Hence
D_{\omega,\mathrm{tot}}\leq 2\kappa(n+1)Z(x)
. Let

D_{\omega,\mathrm{leaf}}:=\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}D_{\omega}(y)

denote the total degree of the leaf layer. In the fixed-order tree, each leaf
y
has exactly one incident non-self edge, namely its parent edge, whose weight is
\widehat{U}(x,y)
. From the terminal anchoring in
C.1
,

D_{\omega,\mathrm{leaf}}=\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}U^{*}(x,y)=Z(x).

Since the stationary distribution of the fixed-order weighted walk is degree-normalized,

\mu_{\omega}({\mathcal{Y}})=\frac{D_{\omega,\mathrm{leaf}}}{D_{\omega,\mathrm{tot}}}\geq\frac{Z(x)}{2\kappa(n+1)Z(x)}=\frac{1}{2\kappa(n+1)}\geq\frac{1}{4\kappa n},

where the last inequality uses
n\geq 1
.

We next lower bound the conductance. Let
A
be a connected subset of the fixed-order tree that does not contain the root. Let
v\in A
be the unique minimum-depth node in
A
. Since
A
does not contain the root,
v
has a parent
p(v)
. By the minimality of
k(v)
, we have
p(v)\notin A
, and hence the edge
\left\{p(v),v\right\}
leaves
A
. By
C.1
, its weight is

\widehat{U}(x,v)\geq\kappa^{-1}U^{*}(x,v).

For
\ell\in\left\{0,\dots,n-k(v)\right\}
, let

C_{\omega}^{\ell}(v):=\left\{u\in{\mathcal{Z}}_{\omega}\mid u\text{ is a descendant of }v,\ k(u)=k(v)+\ell\right\}

be the set of descendants of
v
at relative depth
\ell
. Define the total weighted degree of the descendant subtree of
v
by

D_{\omega,\mathrm{sub}}(v):=\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-k(v)}\sum_{u\in C_{\omega}^{\ell}(v)}D_{\omega}(u).

Applying the pointwise degree bound above to the descendant subtree of
v
, we have

D_{\omega,\mathrm{sub}}(v)\leq 2\kappa\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-k(v)}\sum_{u\in C_{\omega}^{\ell}(v)}U^{*}(x,u).

For every descendant level
\ell
,

\displaystyle\sum_{u\in C_{\omega}^{\ell}(v)}U^{*}(x,u)

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in C_{\omega}^{\ell}(v)}\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{C}}(u)}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\tau(x,y)=\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{C}}(v)}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\tau(x,y)=U^{*}(x,v),

because the completion sets
\left\{{\mathcal{C}}(u):u\in C_{\omega}^{\ell}(v)\right\}
partition
{\mathcal{C}}(v)
. Therefore

D_{\omega,\mathrm{sub}}(v)\leq 2\kappa(n-k(v)+1)U^{*}(x,v)\leq 2\kappa(n+1)U^{*}(x,v).

Since
A
is connected in the tree and
v
is its unique minimum-depth node,
A
is contained in the descendant subtree of
v
. Hence
\sum_{z\in A}D_{\omega}(z)\leq D_{\omega,\mathrm{sub}}(v)
. The cut numerator
\sum_{z\in A,\ u\notin A}f_{\omega}(z,u)
in the tree conductance
\Phi_{\omega}(A)
contains the single boundary edge from
v
to its parent, because
v\in A
and
p(v)\notin A
. Therefore

\sum_{z\in A,\ u\notin A}f_{\omega}(z,u)\geq f_{\omega}(v,p(v))=f_{\omega}(p(v),v)=\widehat{U}(x,v)\geq\kappa^{-1}U^{*}(x,v).

Combining this boundary lower bound with the descendant-subtree volume upper bound gives

\Phi_{\omega}(A):=\frac{\sum_{z\in A,\ u\notin A}f_{\omega}(z,u)}{\sum_{z\in A}D_{\omega}(z)}\geq\frac{\kappa^{-1}U^{*}(x,v)}{2\kappa(n+1)U^{*}(x,v)}=\frac{1}{2\kappa^{2}(n+1)}\geq\frac{1}{4\kappa^{2}n}.

This proves the bound for connected sets that do not contain the root.

If
A
is disconnected and does not contain the root, decompose it into connected components
A_{1},\dots,A_{m}
. Let

B_{r}:=\sum_{z\in A_{r},\ u\notin A_{r}}f_{\omega}(z,u),\qquad V_{r}:=\sum_{z\in A_{r}}D_{\omega}(z).

Since the fixed-order graph is a tree, there are no edges between distinct components of
A
, and therefore

\Phi_{\omega}(A)=\frac{\sum_{r=1}^{m}B_{r}}{\sum_{r=1}^{m}V_{r}}=\sum_{r=1}^{m}\frac{V_{r}}{\sum_{s=1}^{m}V_{s}}\Phi_{\omega}(A_{r})\geq\min_{r\in\left[m\right]}\Phi_{\omega}(A_{r}).

If an admissible set
A
contains the root, then
A^{c}
does not. Since
\mu_{\omega}(A)\leq 1/2
, we have
\sum_{z\in A}D_{\omega}(z)\leq\sum_{z\in A^{c}}D_{\omega}(z)
, and therefore
\Phi_{\omega}(A)\geq\Phi_{\omega}(A^{c})
. Applying the previous argument to the connected components of
A^{c}
proves
\Phi_{\omega}\geq 1/(4\kappa^{2}n)
.
∎

The next two statements show that conductance survives summing weighted graphs, and that canonical balanced AOAR is exactly such a sum over fixed coordinate orders.

Lemma C.3

(Conductance of a sum of weighted graphs)
.

Let
G_{1},\dots,G_{m}
be weighted graphs on the same vertex set with nonnegative symmetric weights
f_{G_{1}},\dots,f_{G_{m}}
. Let
G
be the weighted graph with edge weight

f_{G}(z,u):=\sum_{r=1}^{m}f_{G_{r}}(z,u).

Then for every set
A
,

\Phi_{G}(A)\geq\min_{r\in\left[m\right]}\Phi_{G_{r}}(A).

Consequently,

\Phi_{G}\geq\min_{r\in\left[m\right]}\Phi_{G_{r}}.

Proof.

Write

D_{G_{r}}(z):=\sum_{u\sim_{G_{r}}z}f_{G_{r}}(z,u),\qquad D_{G}(z):=\sum_{u\sim_{G}z}f_{G}(z,u)=\sum_{r=1}^{m}D_{G_{r}}(z).

For any set
A
,

\Phi_{G}(A)=\frac{\sum_{z\in A,\ u\notin A}f_{G}(z,u)}{\sum_{z\in A}D_{G}(z)}.

Using
f_{G}=\sum_{r=1}^{m}f_{G_{r}}
and
D_{G}=\sum_{r=1}^{m}D_{G_{r}}
, this becomes

\Phi_{G}(A)=\frac{\sum_{r=1}^{m}\sum_{z\in A,\ u\notin A}f_{G_{r}}(z,u)}{\sum_{r=1}^{m}\sum_{z\in A}D_{G_{r}}(z)}.

We use the elementary fact that for
a_{r}\geq 0
and
b_{r}>0
,

\frac{\sum_{r=1}^{m}a_{r}}{\sum_{r=1}^{m}b_{r}}\geq\min_{r\in\left[m\right]}\frac{a_{r}}{b_{r}}.

Therefore

\Phi_{G}(A)\geq\min_{r\in\left[m\right]}\frac{\sum_{z\in A,\ u\notin A}f_{G_{r}}(z,u)}{\sum_{z\in A}D_{G_{r}}(z)}=\min_{r\in\left[m\right]}\Phi_{G_{r}}(A).

Taking the infimum over admissible
A
gives the conductance bound.
∎

Proposition C.5

(Canonical balanced AOAR as a sum of fixed-order VGB trees)
.

Consider the canonical balanced AOAR-VGB weighted graph whose depth-
k
parent-child edge coefficient is

g_{k+1}=\frac{1}{\binom{n-1}{k}},\qquad 0\leq k\leq n-1.

Then, the sum of the fixed-order tree graphs over all
\omega\in\mathsf{Perm}_{n}
is exactly
(n-1)!
times this canonical balanced AOAR-VGB weighted graph.

Proof.

Consider an AOAR edge between a depth-
k
state
z
and a child
z^{j\leftarrow a}
, where
j\notin R(z)
. This edge appears in exactly those fixed orders
\omega
for which the first
k
coordinates are precisely
R(z)
, in any order, and the
(k+1)
-st coordinate is
j
. The number of such permutations is

k!(n-k-1)!.

Therefore, in the sum of fixed-order tree graphs, the coefficient multiplying
M_{\text{ref}}(x,z^{j\leftarrow a})\widehat{V}(x,z^{j\leftarrow a})
is

k!(n-k-1)!=\frac{(n-1)!}{\binom{n-1}{k}}.

This is exactly
(n-1)!g_{k+1}
for the canonical balanced coefficient in the proposition.
∎

Combining the fixed-order conductance bound with the sum-of-graphs representation gives the main canonical balanced mixing guarantee.

Theorem C.3

(Mixing guarantee for canonical balanced AOAR-VGB)
.

Assume
C.1
. Let
P_{\mathrm{BAL}}
be the canonical balanced AOAR-VGB lazy kernel, and let
\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}
be its stationary distribution. For a set
A
, write

Q_{\mathrm{BAL}}(A,A^{c}):=\sum_{z\in A}\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z)P_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z,A^{c}),\qquad\Phi(P_{\mathrm{BAL}}):=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\subseteq{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathrm{reach}}\\
0<\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(A)\leq 1/2\end{subarray}}\frac{Q_{\mathrm{BAL}}(A,A^{c})}{\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(A)}

for conductance. We write

\gamma(P_{\mathrm{BAL}}):=1-\max\{|\lambda|:\lambda\in\Lambda(P_{\mathrm{BAL}}),\ \lambda\neq 1\}

for the spectral gap, where
\Lambda(P_{\mathrm{BAL}})
denotes the set of eigenvalues of
P_{\mathrm{BAL}}
. Then

\displaystyle\Phi(P_{\mathrm{BAL}})

\displaystyle=\Omega\left(\frac{1}{\kappa^{2}n}\right),

\displaystyle\gamma(P_{\mathrm{BAL}})

\displaystyle=\Omega\left(\frac{1}{\kappa^{4}n^{2}}\right),

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}({\mathcal{Y}})

\displaystyle=\Omega\left(\frac{1}{\kappa n}\right).

Moreover, the stationary law conditioned on the leaves is exactly

\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(\cdot\mid{\mathcal{Y}})=\pi^{*}(\cdot\mid x).

Consequently, if
Z_{T}
is the state after
T
lazy balanced AOAR-VGB Markov chain steps initialized at
Z_{0}\in{\mathcal{Z}}
with
\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(Z_{0})>0
, then for any
\delta>0
,

T=O\!\left(\kappa^{4}n^{2}\log\frac{\kappa n}{\delta\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(Z_{0})}\right)

suffices to guarantee

d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left(\mathcal{L}(Z_{T}\mid Z_{T}\in{\mathcal{Y}}),\pi^{*}(\cdot\mid x)\right)\leq\delta\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{P}[Z_{T}\in{\mathcal{Y}}]=\Omega\left(\frac{1}{\kappa n}\right).

For example, if
Z_{0}=\varnothing
, then

T=O\!\left(\kappa^{4}n^{2}\log\frac{\kappa n}{\delta}\right).

Proof.

By
Proposition

C.5
, the canonical balanced AOAR weighted graph is a sum of fixed-order VGB tree graphs. By
Lemma

C.2
, each fixed-order graph has conductance
\Omega(1/(\kappa^{2}n))
.
Lemma

C.3
therefore implies

\Phi(P_{\mathrm{BAL}})=\Omega\left(\frac{1}{\kappa^{2}n}\right).

Since the chain is lazy and reversible, Cheeger’s inequality
[
16
]
gives

\gamma(P_{\mathrm{BAL}})\geq\frac{1}{2}\Phi(P_{\mathrm{BAL}})^{2}=\Omega\left(\frac{1}{\kappa^{4}n^{2}}\right).

The leaf-mass lower bound follows by the same ratio-of-sums argument. Indeed, let

D_{\omega,\mathrm{leaf}}:=\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}D_{\omega}(y),\qquad D_{\omega,\mathrm{tot}}:=\sum_{z\in{\mathcal{Z}}_{\omega}}D_{\omega}(z).

Then
\mu_{\omega}({\mathcal{Y}})=D_{\omega,\mathrm{leaf}}/D_{\omega,\mathrm{tot}}
, or equivalently
D_{\omega,\mathrm{leaf}}=D_{\omega,\mathrm{tot}}\mu_{\omega}({\mathcal{Y}})
.
The global factor
(n-1)!
in
Proposition

C.5
cancels after normalization, so the leaf mass of the canonical balanced AOAR graph satisfies

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}({\mathcal{Y}})

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{\omega\in\mathsf{Perm}_{n}}D_{\omega,\mathrm{leaf}}}{\sum_{\omega\in\mathsf{Perm}_{n}}D_{\omega,\mathrm{tot}}}=\frac{\sum_{\omega\in\mathsf{Perm}_{n}}D_{\omega,\mathrm{tot}}\,\mu_{\omega}({\mathcal{Y}})}{\sum_{\omega\in\mathsf{Perm}_{n}}D_{\omega,\mathrm{tot}}}

\displaystyle\geq\min_{\omega\in\mathsf{Perm}_{n}}\mu_{\omega}({\mathcal{Y}})\geq\frac{1}{4\kappa n}=\Omega\left(\frac{1}{\kappa n}\right).

The exact leaf law follows from
Theorem

C.2
, since terminal anchoring gives
\widehat{V}(x,y)=\tau(x,y)
on leaves.

Finally, since
P_{\mathrm{BAL}}
is lazy and reversible, the standard spectral estimate for reversible chains
[
16
]
gives the mixing time bound from
Z_{0}\in{\mathcal{Z}}
as follows:

\lVert P_{\mathrm{BAL}}^{t}(Z_{0},\cdot)-\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq\frac{1}{2}e^{-t\gamma(P_{\mathrm{BAL}})}\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(Z_{0})^{-1/2}.

In particular, the mixing time from
Z_{0}
is,

T_{\mathrm{mix}}^{(Z_{0})}(\varepsilon)=O\left(\frac{1}{\gamma(P_{\mathrm{BAL}})}\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon\sqrt{\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(Z_{0})}}\right).

(1)

Let
\nu_{T}:=P_{\mathrm{BAL}}^{T}(Z_{0},\cdot)
. If

d_{\mathrm{TV}}(\nu_{T},\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}})\leq\varepsilon
and

\varepsilon\leq\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}({\mathcal{Y}})/2
, then
\nu_{T}({\mathcal{Y}})\geq\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}({\mathcal{Y}})/2=\Omega\left(\frac{1}{\kappa n}\right)
; this follows from

|\nu_{T}({\mathcal{Y}})-\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}({\mathcal{Y}})|\leq d_{\mathrm{TV}}(\nu_{T},\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}})
.
Then, from the definition of total variation (TV) distance,

\displaystyle d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left(\nu_{T}(\cdot\mid{\mathcal{Y}}),\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(\cdot\mid{\mathcal{Y}})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}\left|\frac{\nu_{T}(y)}{\nu_{T}({\mathcal{Y}})}-\frac{\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(y)}{\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}({\mathcal{Y}})}\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}\left|\frac{\nu_{T}(y)-\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(y)}{\nu_{T}({\mathcal{Y}})}+\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(y)\left(\frac{1}{\nu_{T}({\mathcal{Y}})}-\frac{1}{\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}({\mathcal{Y}})}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\nu_{T}({\mathcal{Y}})}\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}\left|\nu_{T}(y)-\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(y)\right|+\frac{\left|\nu_{T}({\mathcal{Y}})-\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}({\mathcal{Y}})\right|}{2\nu_{T}({\mathcal{Y}})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\varepsilon}{\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}({\mathcal{Y}})}+\frac{\varepsilon}{\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}({\mathcal{Y}})}\leq\frac{3\varepsilon}{\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}({\mathcal{Y}})}.

Here the partial-sum bound used above is

\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}\left|\nu_{T}(y)-\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(y)\right|\leq\sum_{z\in{\mathcal{Z}}}\left|\nu_{T}(z)-\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z)\right|=2d_{\mathrm{TV}}(\nu_{T},\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}})\leq 2\varepsilon.

Thus, taking
\varepsilon:=\delta\,\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}({\mathcal{Y}})/3
ensures that the leaf-conditioned law is within
\delta
of the stationary leaf-conditioned law.
Plugging this choice into (
1
), and using

\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}({\mathcal{Y}})=\Omega(1/(\kappa n))
, gives

T=O\left(\kappa^{4}n^{2}\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon\sqrt{\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(Z_{0})}}\right)=O\left(\kappa^{4}n^{2}\log\frac{\kappa n}{\delta\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(Z_{0})}\right)

(2)

Since
\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(\cdot\mid{\mathcal{Y}})=\pi^{*}(\cdot\mid x)
, the claim follows.

Next, consider the case
Z_{0}=\varnothing.

It remains to lower bound its stationary mass
\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(\varnothing)
. For a fixed order
\omega
, the root degree satisfies

\displaystyle D_{\omega}(\varnothing)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in{\mathcal{V}}}\widehat{U}(x,\varnothing^{\omega_{1}\leftarrow a})\geq\kappa^{-1}\sum_{a\in{\mathcal{V}}}U^{*}(x,\varnothing^{\omega_{1}\leftarrow a})=\kappa^{-1}\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\tau(x,y)=\kappa^{-1}Z(x),

where the middle equality uses that the first-reveal completion sets

\{{\mathcal{C}}(\varnothing^{\omega_{1}\leftarrow a})\}_{a\in{\mathcal{V}}}
partition
{\mathcal{Y}}
.
The total-degree bound in
Lemma

C.2
gives
D_{\omega,\mathrm{tot}}\leq 2\kappa(n+1)Z(x)
. Therefore
\mu_{\omega}(\varnothing)\geq 1/(2\kappa^{2}(n+1))
. Averaging over
\omega
as above, with the global factor
(n-1)!
canceling after normalization, yields

\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(\varnothing)=\Omega\left(\frac{1}{\kappa^{2}n}\right).

Plugging into (
2
) finishes the proof.

∎

C.5
Geometric Balanced AOAR-VGB

Geometric balanced AOAR-VGB formulation.

We take one further step beyond the depth-balanced AOAR-VGB local kernel in
Definition

C.2
. In that kernel, a backward move from a non-leaf state
z
has weight proportional to
\widehat{V}(x,z)
for every revealed coordinate
i\in R(z)
. Hence, once the chain decides to move backward, the index to re-mask is selected uniformly among revealed coordinates. This is a natural depth-balancing rule, but it ignores an extra degree of freedom that is absent in AR prefix trees: in AOAR, the sampler can choose which revealed coordinate should be erased.

This motivates a value-gated remasking rule. When considering a backward move
z\to z^{-i}
, we would like to favor coordinates whose remasked parent
z^{-i}
remains promising under the verifier. We implement this through a clean geometric edge-weight construction: the child side carries the tilted completion mass
\widehat{U}(x,z^{j\leftarrow a})
, while the parent side contributes through
\widehat{V}(x,z^{-i})^{\lambda}
. This preserves the usual VGB forward ranking and makes the backward index choice depend on the verifier signal of the remasked parent.

Figure

11
illustrates the resulting local transition rule. The geometric gate leaves the forward candidate ranking unchanged up to the state-wise factor
\widehat{V}(x,z)^{\lambda}
, while the backward weights distinguish revealed coordinates through the verifier values of their remasked parents.

Figure 11
:

Illustration of one geometric balanced AOAR-VGB transition from a masked state
z
.
Forward reveal moves use the reference conditional times the child verifier value, with the common gate
\widehat{V}(x,z)^{\lambda}
.
Backward re-masking moves use the current verifier value and the parent gate
\widehat{V}(x,z^{-i})^{\lambda}
.
Setting
\lambda=0
removes the geometric gates and recovers balanced AOAR-VGB.

Definition C.3

(Geometric balanced AOAR edge weights)
.

Fix
\lambda\geq 0
and positive depth coefficients

s_{0},s_{1},\dots,s_{n-1}>0.

For neighboring states
u\sim v
, let
p(u,v)
denote the lower-depth endpoint and
c(u,v)
denote the higher-depth endpoint. Thus
c(u,v)=p(u,v)^{j\leftarrow a}
for a unique revealed coordinate and token.

If
c(u,v)\notin{\mathcal{Y}}
, define the non-leaf edge weight by

f_{\mathrm{BG}}(u,v)=f_{\mathrm{BG}}(v,u):=s_{k(p(u,v))}\widehat{U}(x,c(u,v))\widehat{V}(x,p(u,v))^{\lambda}.

If
c(u,v)=y\in{\mathcal{Y}}
, define the leaf-corrected edge weight by

f_{\mathrm{BG}}(u,v)=f_{\mathrm{BG}}(v,u):=s_{n-1}U^{*}(x,y)=s_{n-1}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\tau(x,y).

Local implementation weights.

Although
Definition

C.3
is written in terms of
\widehat{U}
and
U^{*}
, the local weights below do not require evaluating these tilted masses directly. Let
z
have depth
k=k(z)
.

For a non-leaf child
c=z^{j\leftarrow a}\notin{\mathcal{Y}}
, we have

\widehat{U}(x,c)=M_{\mathrm{ref}}(x,z)\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(Y_{j}=a\mid x,z)\widehat{V}(x,c).

Dividing all incident non-leaf edge weights at current state
z
by the common factor
M_{\mathrm{ref}}(x,z)
gives

w_{\mathrm{BG,fwd}}(z\to c)=s_{k}\widehat{V}(x,z)^{\lambda}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(Y_{j}=a\mid x,z)\widehat{V}(x,c).

The factor
\widehat{V}(x,z)^{\lambda}
is common across all forward candidates from the same current state
z
. Hence the forward candidate ranking is the same as in ordinary VGB:

w_{\mathrm{BG,fwd}}(z\to c)\propto\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(Y_{j}=a\mid x,z)\widehat{V}(x,z^{j\leftarrow a}).

For a backward move from a non-leaf state
z
to
u=z^{-i}
, the relevant edge has child endpoint
z
and parent endpoint
u
. Thus

f_{\mathrm{BG}}(u,z)=s_{k-1}\widehat{U}(x,z)\widehat{V}(x,u)^{\lambda}=s_{k-1}M_{\mathrm{ref}}(x,z)\widehat{V}(x,z)\widehat{V}(x,u)^{\lambda}.

Dividing by the current-state common factor
M_{\mathrm{ref}}(x,z)
gives

w_{\mathrm{BG,bwd}}(z\to z^{-i})=s_{k-1}\widehat{V}(x,z)\widehat{V}(x,z^{-i})^{\lambda}.

Thus backward moves prefer revealed coordinates whose remasked parent has high verifier value.

At the leaf boundary, the leaf-corrected edge gives the local forward weight directly. If
y=z^{j\leftarrow a}\in{\mathcal{Y}}
, then

f_{\mathrm{BG}}(z,y)=s_{n-1}U^{*}(x,y)=s_{n-1}M_{\mathrm{ref}}(x,z)\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(Y_{j}=a\mid x,z)\tau(x,y),

and dividing by
M_{\mathrm{ref}}(x,z)
gives

w_{\mathrm{BG,fwd}}(z\to y)=s_{n-1}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(Y_{j}=a\mid x,z)\tau(x,y).

Similarly, from a leaf
y
to a parent
y^{-i}
,

w_{\mathrm{BG,bwd}}(y\to y^{-i})=s_{n-1}\tau(x,y).

Remark C.3

(
\lambda=0
recovers balanced AOAR-VGB)
.

When
\lambda=0
, the geometric gate disappears and the local weights reduce to the balanced AOAR-VGB weights with depth coefficients
s_{k}
.

Theorem C.4

(Stationarity and exact leaf law of geometric balanced AOAR)
.

Let
P_{\mathrm{BG}}
be the random walk on the reachable AOAR graph with edge weights
f_{\mathrm{BG}}
from
Definition

C.3
. That is,

P_{\mathrm{BG}}(z,u)=\frac{f_{\mathrm{BG}}(z,u)}{D_{\mathrm{BG}}(z)},\qquad D_{\mathrm{BG}}(z):=\sum_{v\sim z}f_{\mathrm{BG}}(z,v).

Then
P_{\mathrm{BG}}
is reversible with stationary distribution

\mu_{\mathrm{BG}}(z)=\frac{D_{\mathrm{BG}}(z)}{\sum_{z^{\prime}}D_{\mathrm{BG}}(z^{\prime})}.

Moreover, under terminal anchoring, its leaf-conditioned stationary law is exact:

\mu_{\mathrm{BG}}(y\mid y\in{\mathcal{Y}})=\pi^{*}(y\mid x).

Proof.

Since
f_{\mathrm{BG}}
is a nonnegative symmetric edge weight, the random walk is a standard weighted-graph random walk. For neighboring distinct states
z\sim u
,

\mu_{\mathrm{BG}}(z)P_{\mathrm{BG}}(z,u)=\frac{f_{\mathrm{BG}}(z,u)}{\sum_{z^{\prime}}D_{\mathrm{BG}}(z^{\prime})}=\mu_{\mathrm{BG}}(u)P_{\mathrm{BG}}(u,z),

so detailed balance holds.

Now let
y\in{\mathcal{Y}}
be a leaf. Its AOAR parents are
y^{-i}
for
i\in\left[n\right]
. By the leaf correction,

f_{\mathrm{BG}}(y^{-i},y)=s_{n-1}U^{*}(x,y)

for every
i
. Hence

D_{\mathrm{BG}}(y)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{\mathrm{BG}}(y^{-i},y)=ns_{n-1}U^{*}(x,y).

The factor
ns_{n-1}
is constant over leaves, and therefore

\mu_{\mathrm{BG}}(y\mid y\in{\mathcal{Y}})=\frac{U^{*}(x,y)}{\sum_{y^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{Y}}}U^{*}(x,y^{\prime})}=\pi^{*}(y\mid x).

∎

Mixing analysis of geometric balanced VGB.

We next transfer the canonical balanced mixing guarantee to the geometric
kernel. The only care point is that zero target-mass states should not create
spurious components. We therefore work on the positive target-support component,
which is stable under all positive-weight moves.

Lemma C.4

(Positive target support is closed under AOAR moves)
.

Assume
Z(x)>0
and
C.1
. Define the positive
target support

{\mathcal{Z}}_{+}(x):=\bigl\{z\in{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathrm{reach}}:U^{\star}(x,z)>0\bigr\}.

Then
\varnothing\in{\mathcal{Z}}_{+}(x)
. Moreover,
{\mathcal{Z}}_{+}(x)
is closed under
all positive-weight balanced and geometric AOAR moves. More precisely:

1.

If
z\in{\mathcal{Z}}_{+}(x)
and
i\in R(z)
, then

z^{-i}\in{\mathcal{Z}}_{+}(x)
.

2.

If
z\in{\mathcal{Z}}_{+}(x)
,
j\notin R(z)
, and

c=z^{j\leftarrow a}\notin{\mathcal{Z}}_{+}(x)
, then the AOAR edge

\left\{z,c\right\}
has zero balanced edge weight and zero geometric edge weight.

Proof.

Since
Z(x)>0
,

U^{\star}(x,\varnothing)=Z(x)>0,

so
\varnothing\in{\mathcal{Z}}_{+}(x)
.

For the first claim, let
p=z^{-i}
. Since
p
is obtained by re-masking one
revealed coordinate of
z
, we have

{\mathcal{C}}(z)\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}(p).

Thus

U^{\star}(x,p)=\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{C}}(p)}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\tau(x,y)\geq\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{C}}(z)}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\tau(x,y)=U^{\star}(x,z)>0.

Hence
p\in{\mathcal{Z}}_{+}(x)
.

For the second claim, let
c=z^{j\leftarrow a}\notin{\mathcal{Z}}_{+}(x)
. If
c
is
not
\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}
-reachable, then it is not an admissible child edge. Otherwise,

M_{\rm ref}(x,c)>0
and
U^{\star}(x,c)=0
, hence

V^{\star}(x,c)=0
. By
C.1
,

\widehat{V}(x,c)=0,\qquad\widehat{U}(x,c):=M_{\rm ref}(x,c)\widehat{V}(x,c)=0.

Therefore the balanced edge weight, which is proportional to

\widehat{U}(x,c)
for non-leaf children and to
U^{\star}(x,c)
for leaf
children, is zero. The same is true for the geometric edge weight: for non-leaf
children it is proportional to

\widehat{U}(x,c)\widehat{V}(x,z)^{\lambda},

and for leaf children it is proportional to
U^{\star}(x,c)
. Hence no
positive-weight edge leaves
{\mathcal{Z}}_{+}(x)
.
∎

On this component, the geometric graph differs from the canonical balanced
graph only through the parent value gate
\widehat{V}(x,p)^{\lambda}
on
non-leaf edges. The next assumption prevents this gate from creating an
additional bottleneck.

Assumption C.2

(Bounded geometric verifiers)
.

Consider the canonical geometric balanced AOAR-VGB graph with

s_{k}=\frac{1}{\binom{n-1}{k}},\qquad 0\leq k\leq n-1.

Fix
\lambda>0
. Assume there exist constants

0<m\leq 1\leq M<\infty

such that, for every non-leaf state
z\in{\mathcal{Z}}_{+}(x)
,

m\leq\widehat{V}(x,z)\leq M.

Theorem C.5

(Mixing stability for geometric balanced AOAR-VGB)
.

Assume
C.1
and
C.2
. Let
P_{\mathrm{BG}}
be the lazy canonical geometric balanced AOAR-VGB kernel on
{\mathcal{Z}}_{+}(x)
with
Z(x)>0
, and let
\mu_{\mathrm{BG}}
be its stationary distribution. Then

\mu_{\mathrm{BG}}(\cdot\mid{\mathcal{Y}})=\pi^{*}(\cdot\mid x).

Moreover, its conductance satisfies

\Phi(P_{\mathrm{BG}})=\Omega\!\left(\left(\frac{m}{M}\right)^{\lambda}\frac{1}{\kappa^{2}n}\right),

and hence, by Cheeger’s inequality for lazy reversible chains, its spectral gap satisfies

\gamma(P_{\mathrm{BG}})=\Omega\!\left(\left(\frac{m}{M}\right)^{2\lambda}\frac{1}{\kappa^{4}n^{2}}\right).

The stationary leaf and root masses satisfy

\mu_{\mathrm{BG}}({\mathcal{Y}})=\Omega\!\left(\frac{1}{M^{\lambda}\kappa n}\right),\qquad\mu_{\mathrm{BG}}(\varnothing)=\Omega\!\left(\left(\frac{m}{M}\right)^{\lambda}\frac{1}{\kappa^{2}n}\right).

Consequently, if
Z_{T}
is the state after
T
lazy geometric AOAR-VGB transitions initialized at
Z_{0}\in{\mathcal{Z}}_{+}(x)
with
\mu_{\mathrm{BG}}(Z_{0})>0
, then for any
\delta>0
,

T=O\!\left(\left(\frac{M}{m}\right)^{2\lambda}\kappa^{4}n^{2}\log\frac{M^{\lambda}\kappa n}{\delta\mu_{\mathrm{BG}}(Z_{0})}\right)

suffices to guarantee

d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left(\mathcal{L}(Z_{T}\mid Z_{T}\in{\mathcal{Y}}),\pi^{*}(\cdot\mid x)\right)\leq\delta\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{P}[Z_{T}\in{\mathcal{Y}}]=\Omega\!\left(\frac{1}{M^{\lambda}\kappa n}\right).

For example, if
Z_{0}=\varnothing
, then

T=O\!\left(\left(\frac{M}{m}\right)^{2\lambda}\kappa^{4}n^{2}\log\frac{(M/m)^{\lambda}\kappa n}{\delta}\right).

In particular, if
m,M
are constants independent of
n
and
\lambda
is fixed, the geometric chain’s mixing time is of the same order as canonical balanced AOAR-VGB.

Proof.

Let
f_{\mathrm{BAL}}
be the canonical balanced AOAR edge weight and let
f_{\mathrm{BG}}
be the canonical geometric balanced AOAR edge weight. For an edge
e=\left\{p,c\right\}
, let
p
be the lower-depth endpoint and
c
the higher-depth endpoint. If
c\notin{\mathcal{Y}}
, then by
Definition

C.3
,

f_{\mathrm{BG}}(e)=\widehat{V}(x,p)^{\lambda}f_{\mathrm{BAL}}(e).

If
c\in{\mathcal{Y}}
, the leaf correction gives

f_{\mathrm{BG}}(e)=f_{\mathrm{BAL}}(e).

Therefore, using
C.2
and
m\leq 1\leq M
, every positive-weight edge in the component satisfies

m^{\lambda}f_{\mathrm{BAL}}(e)\leq f_{\mathrm{BG}}(e)\leq M^{\lambda}f_{\mathrm{BAL}}(e).

For any set
A
, write

Q_{\mathrm{BG}}(A,A^{c}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e=\left\{u,v\right\}\\
u\in A,\ v\notin A\end{subarray}}f_{\mathrm{BG}}(e),\qquad\mathrm{vol}_{\mathrm{BG}}(A):=\sum_{u\in A}\sum_{v\sim u}f_{\mathrm{BG}}(u,v),

and define
Q_{\mathrm{BAL}}
and
\mathrm{vol}_{\mathrm{BAL}}
analogously.
The edge comparison gives

Q_{\mathrm{BG}}(A,A^{c})\geq m^{\lambda}Q_{\mathrm{BAL}}(A,A^{c}),\qquad\mathrm{vol}_{\mathrm{BG}}(A)\leq M^{\lambda}\mathrm{vol}_{\mathrm{BAL}}(A).

Thus, for any admissible
A
,

\Phi_{\mathrm{BG}}(A)=\frac{Q_{\mathrm{BG}}(A,A^{c})}{\mathrm{vol}_{\mathrm{BG}}(A)}\geq\left(\frac{m}{M}\right)^{\lambda}\frac{Q_{\mathrm{BAL}}(A,A^{c})}{\mathrm{vol}_{\mathrm{BAL}}(A)}=\left(\frac{m}{M}\right)^{\lambda}\Phi_{\mathrm{BAL}}(A).

Taking the infimum over admissible
A
and applying
Theorem

C.3
gives

\Phi(P_{\mathrm{BG}})=\Omega\!\left(\left(\frac{m}{M}\right)^{\lambda}\frac{1}{\kappa^{2}n}\right).

Since the lazy geometric chain is reversible, Cheeger’s inequality gives

\gamma(P_{\mathrm{BG}})=\Omega\!\left(\left(\frac{m}{M}\right)^{2\lambda}\frac{1}{\kappa^{4}n^{2}}\right).

The exact leaf-conditioned law follows from
Theorem

C.4
, since laziness does not change the stationary distribution. For the leaf mass, leaf-corrected edges have the same weight as in the canonical balanced graph. Writing
D_{\mathrm{BG}}
and
D_{\mathrm{BAL}}
for weighted degrees,

\mu_{\mathrm{BG}}({\mathcal{Y}})=\frac{\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}D_{\mathrm{BAL}}(y)}{\sum_{z}D_{\mathrm{BG}}(z)}\geq\frac{\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}D_{\mathrm{BAL}}(y)}{M^{\lambda}\sum_{z}D_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z)}=\frac{1}{M^{\lambda}}\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}({\mathcal{Y}})=\Omega\!\left(\frac{1}{M^{\lambda}\kappa n}\right).

Similarly, the root degree is at least
m^{\lambda}
times the balanced root degree, while the total degree is at most
M^{\lambda}
times the balanced total degree, so

\mu_{\mathrm{BG}}(\varnothing)\geq\left(\frac{m}{M}\right)^{\lambda}\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(\varnothing)=\Omega\!\left(\left(\frac{m}{M}\right)^{\lambda}\frac{1}{\kappa^{2}n}\right).

Finally, the standard start-state spectral bound for lazy reversible chains
[
16
]
gives

\left\|P_{\mathrm{BG}}^{T}(Z_{0},\cdot)-\mu_{\mathrm{BG}}\right\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq\frac{1}{2}e^{-T\gamma(P_{\mathrm{BG}})}\mu_{\mathrm{BG}}(Z_{0})^{-1/2}.

As in the proof of
Theorem

C.3
, taking the full-chain TV error to be at most

\varepsilon:=\frac{\delta\,\mu_{\mathrm{BG}}({\mathcal{Y}})}{3}

implies

d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left(\mathcal{L}(Z_{T}\mid Z_{T}\in{\mathcal{Y}}),\mu_{\mathrm{BG}}(\cdot\mid{\mathcal{Y}})\right)\leq\delta\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{P}[Z_{T}\in{\mathcal{Y}}]\geq\mu_{\mathrm{BG}}({\mathcal{Y}})/2=\Omega\!\left(\frac{1}{M^{\lambda}\kappa n}\right).

Substituting the bounds on
\gamma(P_{\mathrm{BG}})
and
\mu_{\mathrm{BG}}({\mathcal{Y}})
, while retaining the start-state factor
\mu_{\mathrm{BG}}(Z_{0})
, yields the general mixing time. The lower bound on
\mu_{\mathrm{BG}}(\varnothing)
gives the root-start example. Since
\mu_{\mathrm{BG}}(\cdot\mid{\mathcal{Y}})=\pi^{*}(\cdot\mid x)
, the claim follows.
∎

Forward/backward mass analysis.

We now return from global mixing to the local behavior of the geometric
transition. The next proposition measures how the
forward-versus-backward move probabilities change under the exact verifier setup.

Proposition C.6

(Directional masses under the exact verifier)
.

Assume
\widehat{V}=V^{*}
on all non-leaf states. Let
z
be a non-leaf interior state of depth
1\leq k\leq n-2
with
V^{*}(x,z)>0
, so that its one-step forward children are also non-leaf. Define the parent-value ratio

r_{i}^{*}(z):=\frac{V^{*}(x,z^{-i})}{V^{*}(x,z)},\qquad i\in R(z).

Then the exact forward and backward masses are

F_{\lambda}^{*}(z)=s_{k}(n-k)V^{*}(x,z)^{1+\lambda},

and

B_{\lambda}^{*}(z)=s_{k-1}V^{*}(x,z)^{1+\lambda}\sum_{i\in R(z)}r_{i}^{*}(z)^{\lambda}.

Consequently,

\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{forward}\mid x,z,\mathrm{move})=\frac{s_{k}(n-k)}{s_{k}(n-k)+s_{k-1}\sum_{i\in R(z)}r_{i}^{*}(z)^{\lambda}},

and

\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{backward}\mid x,z,\mathrm{move})=\frac{s_{k-1}\sum_{i\in R(z)}r_{i}^{*}(z)^{\lambda}}{s_{k}(n-k)+s_{k-1}\sum_{i\in R(z)}r_{i}^{*}(z)^{\lambda}}.

Proof.

For the forward mass, using the AOAR Bellman identity,

\displaystyle F_{\lambda}^{*}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\notin R(z)}\sum_{a\in{\mathcal{V}}}s_{k}V^{*}(x,z)^{\lambda}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(Y_{j}=a\mid x,z)V^{*}(x,z^{j\leftarrow a})

\displaystyle=s_{k}V^{*}(x,z)^{\lambda}\sum_{j\notin R(z)}V^{*}(x,z)=s_{k}(n-k)V^{*}(x,z)^{1+\lambda}.

For backward moves,

\displaystyle B_{\lambda}^{*}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in R(z)}s_{k-1}V^{*}(x,z)V^{*}(x,z^{-i})^{\lambda}

\displaystyle=s_{k-1}V^{*}(x,z)^{1+\lambda}\sum_{i\in R(z)}\left[\frac{V^{*}(x,z^{-i})}{V^{*}(x,z)}\right]^{\lambda}.

Normalizing the forward and backward masses gives the probabilities in the proposition.
∎

Remark C.4

(Reference-target mismatch interpretation)
.

By the exact target conditional,

\pi^{*}(Y_{i}=z_{i}\mid x,z^{-i})=\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(Y_{i}=z_{i}\mid x,z^{-i})\frac{V^{*}(x,z)}{V^{*}(x,z^{-i})},\qquad r_{i}^{*}(z)=\frac{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(Y_{i}=z_{i}\mid x,z^{-i})}{\pi^{*}(Y_{i}=z_{i}\mid x,z^{-i})}.

Thus
r_{i}^{*}(z)>1
means
\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}
favors
z_{i}
more than
\pi^{*}
and increases
the remasking weight of coordinate
i
, while
r_{i}^{*}(z)<1
means
\pi^{*}
gives
stronger support and the remasking weight is reduced.

In other words, the chain preferentially erases tokens that look base-plausible
but target-weak, and keeps tokens that are target-supported.

Remark C.5

(Penultimate-depth directional masses)
.

When
z
is a penultimate-depth state with
k(z)=n-1
, any forward move reaches a leaf. The leaf correction resets the forward edge to
s_{n-1}U^{*}(x,y)
rather than the non-leaf product edge. Hence, under the exact verifier,

F_{\lambda}^{*}(z)=s_{n-1}V^{*}(x,z),

whereas

B_{\lambda}^{*}(z)=s_{n-2}V^{*}(x,z)\sum_{i\in R(z)}V^{*}(x,z^{-i})^{\lambda}.

This boundary rule is intentional: it keeps the leaf degree proportional to
U^{*}(x,y)
, and therefore preserves the exact leaf-conditioned stationary law.

Remark C.6

(Canonical depth anchor versus exact
1/2
balance)
.

The linear balanced choice

s_{k}(n-k)=ks_{k-1}

does not give exact
1/2
–
1/2
balance for
\lambda>0
in the clean geometric chain. Under this choice, away from the leaf boundary,

\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{backward}\mid x,z,\mathrm{move})=\frac{\sum_{i\in R(z)}r_{i}^{*}(z)^{\lambda}}{k+\sum_{i\in R(z)}r_{i}^{*}(z)^{\lambda}},\qquad k=\left|R(z)\right|.

Thus
s_{k}(n-k)=ks_{k-1}
is best interpreted as the canonical
depth-neutral anchor
: it recovers exact balance when
\lambda=0
, while the
\lambda>0
correction changes the backward probability according to the verifier value of the remasked parent states.

More quantitatively, for small
\lambda
,

r_{i}^{*}(z)^{\lambda}=\exp\!\left(\lambda\log r_{i}^{*}(z)\right)=1+\lambda\log r_{i}^{*}(z)+O\!\left(\lambda^{2}\log^{2}r_{i}^{*}(z)\right).

Define the first empirical log-moment of the parent-value ratios

\bar{m}_{1}(z):=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i\in R(z)}\log\frac{V^{*}(x,z^{-i})}{V^{*}(x,z)}.

Then

\sum_{i\in R(z)}r_{i}^{*}(z)^{\lambda}=k+\lambda k\bar{m}_{1}(z)+O\!\left(\lambda^{2}k\bar{m}_{2}(z)\right),

where

\bar{m}_{2}(z):=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i\in R(z)}\log^{2}\frac{V^{*}(x,z^{-i})}{V^{*}(x,z)}.

Substituting this into the backward probability gives

\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{backward}\mid x,z,\mathrm{move})=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\lambda}{4}\bar{m}_{1}(z)+O\!\left(\lambda^{2}\bar{m}_{2}(z)+\lambda^{2}\bar{m}_{1}(z)^{2}\right).

Equivalently,

\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{forward}\mid x,z,\mathrm{move})=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\lambda}{4}\bar{m}_{1}(z)+O\!\left(\lambda^{2}\bar{m}_{2}(z)+\lambda^{2}\bar{m}_{1}(z)^{2}\right).

Therefore, the leading-order deviation from
1/2
is not a combinatorial depth bias. It is the value-gated remasking signal induced by the geometric parent factor.

Appendix D
Momentum-Balanced AOAR-VGB

Balanced AOAR-VGB fixes the depth-allocation pathology of primitive AOAR-VGB by balancing forward and backward directions at each interior depth in the ideal exact-value case. However, the resulting walk can still be locally diffusive: after revealing a coordinate, the chain may immediately re-mask a coordinate, and after re-masking it may immediately reveal again. Such re-masking/revealing oscillations waste Markov steps and can be computationally inefficient, especially for long sequences where each transition may require evaluating many candidate child states.

This issue is closely related to the diffusive behavior of reversible random walks studied in the lifting literature. A lifting replaces each state by several copies and defines a larger Markov chain whose projection preserves the desired stationary law, while using non-reversible momentum to reduce unnecessary direction changes
[
11
]
. In the present AOAR setting, the two natural directions are downward reveal moves and upward re-masking moves. We therefore introduce a two-copy momentum lift of balanced AOAR-VGB. The construction is inspired by the momentum version of VGB in
23
and by the flow-cancellation principle of
11
, but is adapted here to the any-order masked-state graph.

D.1
Setup and Notation

Throughout this section, we use the balanced AOAR-VGB notation from
Section

. In particular,
K_{\mathrm{BAL}}
denotes the self-loop-free balanced AOAR-VGB move kernel induced by the local weights in
Definition

C.2
with
\gamma=0
, and
\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}
denotes its stationary distribution from
Theorem

C.2
.

Directional neighborhoods and masses.

Recall the AOAR child and parent neighborhoods

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{AOAR}}(z)

\displaystyle:=\left\{z^{j\leftarrow a}:j\notin R(z),\ a\in{\mathcal{V}},\ M_{\mathrm{ref}}(x,z^{j\leftarrow a})>0\right\},

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{AOAR}}(z)

\displaystyle:=\left\{z^{-i}:i\in R(z)\right\}.

A move to

is called a
downward

Lifted tuple state space.

Momentum is represented by augmenting the masked state with a direction variable. We use tuple states

As introduced above, the label

means that the chain is currently in a downward, forward-revealing momentum mode, while

Let

We write

for the projection from a lifted state to its current masked state. For a probability distribution

Stationary split.

The goal of the momentum lift is to change path geometry without changing the stationary law after projection. Thus the lifted chain should have a stationary distribution on the two-copy space whose projection is exactly the balanced AOAR-VGB stationary law

. We take the simplest such target: an equal split of the base stationary mass across the two momentum modes,

This choice does not bias the chain toward either momentum direction at stationarity, and projecting out the momentum label immediately recovers the desired base stationary law:

\textsf{proj}_{\#}\widetilde{\mu}_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z)=\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z).

Proposition D.1

(Markov property)
.

For every

, the kernel

Proof.

and

Theorem D.1

(Stationarity of flow-cancelled momentum-balanced AOAR-VGB)
.

Assume terminal anchoring,

\widehat{V}(x,y)=\tau(x,y),\qquad y\in{\mathcal{Y}}.

Let
K_{\mathrm{BAL}}
be the self-loop-free (
\gamma=0
) balanced AOAR-VGB move kernel from
Definition

C.2
, restricted to a reachable component where
W_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z)>0
for every state
z
. Then, from
Theorem

C.2

K_{\mathrm{BAL}}
is reversible with respect to
\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}
, and
\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(\cdot\mid{\mathcal{Y}})=\pi^{*}(\cdot\mid x)
.

For every
\chi\in[0,1]
, the lifted measure

\widetilde{\mu}_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z,\downarrow)=\widetilde{\mu}_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z,\uparrow)=\frac{1}{2}\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z)

is stationary for
\widetilde{K}_{\mathrm{BAL}}^{\mathrm{fc},\chi}
. Moreover,

\textsf{proj}_{\#}\widetilde{\mu}_{\mathrm{BAL}}=\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}.

Consequently, the flow-cancelled lifted chain has the exact tilted target as its leaf-conditioned current-state law:

\textsf{proj}_{\#}\widetilde{\mu}_{\mathrm{BAL}}(\cdot\mid{\mathcal{Y}})=\pi^{*}(\cdot\mid x).

Proof.

We verify stationarity by checking incoming flow into each lifted copy. First consider
(z,\downarrow)
. Incoming flow comes from three sources: parents of
z
in downward mode, the switch from
(z,\uparrow)
, and the self-loop at
(z,\downarrow)
.

The incoming move-flow from parent states
u\in P_{\mathrm{AOAR}}(z)
is

\frac{1}{2}\sum_{u\in P_{\mathrm{AOAR}}(z)}\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(u)K_{\mathrm{BAL}}(u,z).

Using reversibility of
K_{\mathrm{BAL}}
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{u\in P_{\mathrm{AOAR}}(z)}\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(u)K_{\mathrm{BAL}}(u,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z)\sum_{u\in P_{\mathrm{AOAR}}(z)}K_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z,u)=\frac{1}{2}\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z)p_{\uparrow}(z).

The incoming switch-flow from
(z,\uparrow)
is

\frac{1}{2}\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z)\bigl(p_{\downarrow}(z)-\chi m(z)\bigr),

and the self-loop flow at
(z,\downarrow)
is

\frac{1}{2}\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z)\chi m(z).

Therefore the total incoming flow into
(z,\downarrow)
is

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z)p_{\uparrow}(z)+\frac{1}{2}\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z)\bigl(p_{\downarrow}(z)-\chi m(z)\bigr)+\frac{1}{2}\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z)\chi m(z)

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{2}\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z)\bigl(p_{\uparrow}(z)+p_{\downarrow}(z)\bigr)=\frac{1}{2}\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z)=\widetilde{\mu}_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z,\downarrow).

The proof for
(z,\uparrow)
is symmetric. Incoming move-flow comes from children
v\in C_{\mathrm{AOAR}}(z)
:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v\in C_{\mathrm{AOAR}}(z)}\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(v)K_{\mathrm{BAL}}(v,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z)\sum_{v\in C_{\mathrm{AOAR}}(z)}K_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z,v)=\frac{1}{2}\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z)p_{\downarrow}(z),

where the first equality uses reversibility of
K_{\mathrm{BAL}}
. The incoming switch-flow from
(z,\downarrow)
is

\frac{1}{2}\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z)\bigl(p_{\uparrow}(z)-\chi m(z)\bigr),

and the self-loop flow at
(z,\uparrow)
is

\frac{1}{2}\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z)\chi m(z).

Thus the total incoming flow into
(z,\uparrow)
is

\frac{1}{2}\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z)p_{\downarrow}(z)+\frac{1}{2}\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z)\bigl(p_{\uparrow}(z)-\chi m(z)\bigr)+\frac{1}{2}\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z)\chi m(z)=\frac{1}{2}\mu_{\mathrm{BAL}}(z).

Therefore

is stationary.

Finally,

, terminal anchoring implies

. Since the lifted current-state marginal is

, the same leaf-conditioned law holds after projection.
∎

D.4
Theoretical Analysis: Depth-Speed Gain with Momentum

We now focus on the depth-wise behavior of Balanced AOAR-VGB (the
\lambda=0
, non-geometric chain) and its flow-cancelled momentum lift. We note that this theoretical analysis is restricted to this balanced chain, rather than the geometric variants above.

In AOAR-VGB without momentum, even when forward and backward moves are locally balanced, the reversible chain still behaves like a diffusive random walk along the depth axis, leading to a quadratic first-leaf hitting time. We show that, under a near-balance condition on the realized forward and backward masses, the no-momentum chain remains diffusive with first-leaf hitting time of order
n^{2}
, whereas the maximally flow-cancelled momentum lift can accelerate this depth traversal to order
n
steps.

This is the quantity most aligned with practical VGB-style implementations, which often stop the Markov chain once it first reaches a leaf.

Definition D.3

(Depth process and hitting time)
.

For a masked state
z
, let

. For the balanced chain with current masked state

\tau_{t}^{\mathrm{BAL}}:=k(Z_{t}^{\mathrm{BAL}}),\qquad T_{n}^{\mathrm{BAL}}:=\inf\{t\geq 0:\tau_{t}^{\mathrm{BAL}}=n\}.

For a lifted momentum chain
\widetilde{Z}_{t}=(Z_{t}^{\mathrm{BAL,Mo}},D_{t})

Assumption D.1

(Uniform directional near-balance)
.

There exists
\varepsilon_{n}\in[0,1)
such that for every reachable interior state

,

\left|p_{\downarrow}(z)-p_{\uparrow}(z)\right|\leq\varepsilon_{n}.

Equivalently, since
p_{\downarrow}(z)+p_{\uparrow}(z)=1
,

\frac{1-\varepsilon_{n}}{2}\leq p_{\downarrow}(z),p_{\uparrow}(z)\leq\frac{1+\varepsilon_{n}}{2}.

The exact canonical balanced case corresponds to
\varepsilon_{n}=0
. The near-balanced speedup regime is

\varepsilon_{n}=O(1/n).

Intuitively, this says that the plug-in verifier may break exact
1/2,1/2
balance, but only by the same order as the inverse sequence length
1/n
.

Lemma D.1

(Biased reflecting walk hitting time)
.

Consider a birth-death chain on
\left\{0,1,\dots,n\right\}
with absorbing state
n
, deterministic transition
0\to 1
, and for
1\leq k\leq n-1
,

k\to k+1\text{ with probability }p,\qquad k\to k-1\text{ with probability }q:=1-p.

Let
(K_{t})_{t\geq 0}
denote this birth-death chain, and define the hitting time of depth
n
by

T_{n}^{(p)}:=\inf\left\{t\geq 0:K_{t}=n\right\}.

Set

H_{0}^{(p)}:=\mathbb{E}\left[T_{n}^{(p)}\mid K_{0}=0\right].

If
p\neq q
, then

H_{0}^{(p)}=\frac{n}{p-q}-\frac{2pq}{(p-q)^{2}}\left(1-\left(\frac{q}{p}\right)^{n}\right).

If
p=q=1/2
, then

H_{0}^{(1/2)}=n^{2}.

Moreover, for every fixed
c>0
, if

\left|p-\frac{1}{2}\right|\leq\frac{c}{2n},

then

H_{0}^{(p)}=\Theta_{c}(n^{2}).

Proof.

Let

h_{k}:=\mathbb{E}\left[T_{n}^{(p)}\mid K_{0}=k\right].

Then
h_{n}=0
, and the reflecting root gives
h_{0}=1+h_{1}
. For
1\leq k\leq n-1
,

h_{k}=1+ph_{k+1}+qh_{k-1}.

Define finite differences
\Delta_{k}:=h_{k}-h_{k-1}
for
1\leq k\leq n
. The boundary equation gives
\Delta_{1}=-1
, and the interior recurrence is equivalent to

p\Delta_{k+1}-q\Delta_{k}=-1.

If
p\neq q
, set

r:=\frac{q}{p},\qquad S_{n}(r):=\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-1}r^{\ell}.

Then the solution is

\Delta_{k}=\left(-1+\frac{1}{p-q}\right)r^{k-1}-\frac{1}{p-q}.

Since
h_{n}=0
,

-h_{0}=h_{n}-h_{0}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Delta_{k}=\left(-1+\frac{1}{p-q}\right)S_{n}(r)-\frac{n}{p-q}.

Rearranging and using
S_{n}(r)=\left(1-r^{n}\right)/\left(1-r\right)
gives

h_{0}=S_{n}(r)+\frac{n-S_{n}(r)}{p-q}=\frac{n}{p-q}-\frac{2pq}{(p-q)^{2}}\left(1-\left(\frac{q}{p}\right)^{n}\right).

If
p=q=1/2
, the recurrence becomes
h_{k}=1+\frac{1}{2}h_{k+1}+\frac{1}{2}h_{k-1}
, and the solution satisfying
h_{n}=0
and
h_{0}=1+h_{1}
is
h_{k}=n^{2}-k^{2}
. Thus
h_{0}=n^{2}
.

Finally suppose
\left|p-1/2\right|\leq c/(2n)
. Then
\left|p-q\right|\leq c/n
. If
p=q
, the claim follows from
h_{0}=n^{2}
. It remains to consider
p\neq q
. Recall that
r=q/p
; equivalently,

r=\frac{q}{p}=\frac{1-(p-q)}{1+(p-q)}

because
p+q=1
. For
n\geq 2c
, we have
\left|p-q\right|\leq 1/2
. The mean-value theorem then gives

\left|\log r\right|=\left|\log\left(1-(p-q)\right)-\log\left(1+(p-q)\right)\right|\leq 4\left|p-q\right|.

Therefore, for
0\leq\ell\leq n-1
,

e^{-4c}\leq e^{-4\left|p-q\right|n}\leq r^{\ell}\leq e^{4\left|p-q\right|n}\leq e^{4c}.

Summing these bounds gives

ne^{-4c}\leq S_{n}(r)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-1}r^{\ell}\leq ne^{4c},

so
S_{n}(r)=\Theta_{c}(n)
. The finitely many cases
n<2c
are absorbed into the constants hidden by
\Theta_{c}
.
Moreover,

n-S_{n}(r)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-1}(1-r^{\ell})=(1-r)\sum_{\ell=1}^{n-1}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}r^{j},\qquad\left|1-r\right|=\Theta_{c}(\left|p-q\right|).

The double sum is
\Theta_{c}(n^{2})
, again because all powers
r^{j}
stay within constant factors. Thus
\left|n-S_{n}(r)\right|=\Theta_{c}(\left|p-q\right|n^{2})
, and
(n-S_{n}(r))/(p-q)=\Theta_{c}(n^{2})
. Substituting into
h_{0}=S_{n}(r)+(n-S_{n}(r))/(p-q)
gives
h_{0}=\Theta_{c}(n^{2})
.
∎

Proposition D.2

(No-momentum remains diffusive under near-balance)
.

Assume
D.1
with
\varepsilon_{n}\leq c/n
for a fixed constant
c>0
. Consider the self-loop-free no-momentum balanced AOAR-VGB depth process, started from depth
0
. Then

\mathbb{E}\left[T_{n}^{\mathrm{BAL}}\mid\tau_{0}^{\mathrm{BAL}}=0\right]=\Theta_{c}(n^{2}).

Proof.

At every interior state, the probability of increasing the depth is between

p_{-}:=\frac{1-\varepsilon_{n}}{2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad p_{+}:=\frac{1+\varepsilon_{n}}{2}.

Because the conditional probability of increasing depth at each interior step is always in
[p_{-},p_{+}]
, the no-momentum depth process can be coupled between two reflecting birth-death chains using common uniform random variables at each step. The favorable chain increases depth with probability
p_{+}
and reaches depth
n
no later than the AOAR depth process, while the unfavorable chain increases depth with probability
p_{-}
and reaches depth
n
no earlier. Therefore,

H_{0}^{(p_{+})}\leq\mathbb{E}\left[T_{n}^{\mathrm{BAL}}\mid\tau_{0}^{\mathrm{BAL}}=0\right]\leq H_{0}^{(p_{-})}.

Since
|p_{\pm}-1/2|\leq c/(2n)
,
Lemma

D.1
gives
H_{0}^{(p_{+})}=\Theta_{c}(n^{2})
and
H_{0}^{(p_{-})}=\Theta_{c}(n^{2})
. Hence
\mathbb{E}[T_{n}^{\mathrm{BAL}}\mid\tau_{0}^{\mathrm{BAL}}=0]=\Theta_{c}(n^{2})
.
∎

Theorem D.3

(First-leaf hitting for near-maximal flow cancellation)
.

Assume
D.1
. Run the flow-cancelled momentum
chain
\widetilde{K}_{\mathrm{BAL}}^{\mathrm{fc},\chi}
from the initial
lifted state
(\varnothing,\downarrow)
, where
\chi\in[0,1]
. Then

\mathbb{E}\left[T_{n}^{\mathrm{BAL,Mo}}\mid\widetilde{Z}_{0}=(\varnothing,\downarrow)\right]\leq\left(\frac{4n}{1-\varepsilon_{n}}+1\right)\left(\frac{2-\chi(1-\varepsilon_{n})}{1-\varepsilon_{n}}\right)^{n}.

In particular, if
\varepsilon_{n}\leq\min\left\{c/n,1/2\right\}
and

1-\chi\leq c_{\chi}/n
for fixed constants
c,c_{\chi}>0
, then

\mathbb{E}\left[T_{n}^{\mathrm{BAL,Mo}}\mid\widetilde{Z}_{0}=(\varnothing,\downarrow)\right]=O_{c,c_{\chi}}(n).

For
\chi=1
, this recovers the maximally flow-cancelled bound.

Proof.

Let
q_{\varepsilon}:=(1-\varepsilon_{n})/2
. Under

D.1
, at every interior state
z
,

p_{\downarrow}(z),p_{\uparrow}(z)\geq q_{\varepsilon}
. From

(z,\downarrow)
, the flow-cancelled kernel has downward move probability

p_{\downarrow}(z)
, switch-up probability

p_{\uparrow}(z)-\chi m(z)
, and same-copy holding probability

\chi m(z)
, where

m(z):=\min\{p_{\downarrow}(z),p_{\uparrow}(z)\}
. Thus, while in downward
mode, the probability of a downward depth increment is at least

q_{\varepsilon}
.

We decompose the trajectory into downward attempts. A downward attempt starts
in downward mode. It succeeds if the chain reaches depth
n
while remaining
in downward mode, and it fails if the chain switches from downward mode to
upward mode before reaching depth
n
.

Same-copy holding steps do not change either the depth or the momentum label.
For the success-probability calculation, condition them out and look only at
decisive steps, namely steps on which either a downward move or an upward switch
occurs. Given the current lifted state
(z,\downarrow)
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\text{downward move}\mid\text{decisive},z,\downarrow)

\displaystyle=\frac{p_{\downarrow}(z)}{p_{\downarrow}(z)+p_{\uparrow}(z)-\chi m(z)}

\displaystyle=\frac{p_{\downarrow}(z)}{1-\chi m(z)}\geq\frac{q_{\varepsilon}}{1-\chi q_{\varepsilon}}:=\alpha_{\chi,\varepsilon}=\frac{1-\varepsilon_{n}}{2-\chi(1-\varepsilon_{n})}.

To succeed from depth
k
, an attempt only needs the next
n-k
decisive
outcomes to be downward moves. Therefore, with

s_{\chi,\varepsilon,n}:=\alpha_{\chi,\varepsilon}^{\,n}
,

\mathbb{P}(\text{attempt succeeds from depth }k)\geq\alpha_{\chi,\varepsilon}^{\,n-k}\geq\alpha_{\chi,\varepsilon}^{\,n}.

Thus every downward attempt succeeds with probability at least

s_{\chi,\varepsilon,n}
, uniformly over the past.

Next, we bound the expected duration of one attempt cycle, counting Markov
transitions including same-copy holding steps. During the downward part, every
transition in downward mode increases the depth with probability at least

q_{\varepsilon}
. To upper-bound the time cost, ignore early switch failures
and wait until
n
downward depth increments have occurred. If
G_{r}
is the
waiting time for the
r
-th such increment after the
(r-1)
-st one, then

\mathbb{P}(G_{r}>t)\leq(1-q_{\varepsilon})^{t}
and

\mathbb{E}[G_{r}]\leq 1/q_{\varepsilon}
. Hence the expected duration of the downward
part is at most
n/q_{\varepsilon}
.

If the attempt fails, the chain has switched to upward mode before reaching
depth
n
. We upper-bound the cost of preparing the next attempt by a
conservative reset: move upward until the root and then take the boundary
switch back to downward mode. Returning to the root requires at most
n

upward depth decrements, each occurring with probability at least

q_{\varepsilon}
. Thus the reset duration is at most
n/q_{\varepsilon}+1

in expectation, and one complete attempt cycle has expected duration at most

L_{\varepsilon}:=2n/q_{\varepsilon}+1
.

Let
N_{\mathrm{att}}
be the number of attempts until the first successful
one. Since each attempt succeeds with conditional probability at least

s_{\chi,\varepsilon,n}
, we have

\mathbb{P}(N_{\mathrm{att}}>m)\leq(1-s_{\chi,\varepsilon,n})^{m}
and therefore

\mathbb{E}[N_{\mathrm{att}}]\leq 1/s_{\chi,\varepsilon,n}
.

Let
C_{j}
denote the duration of the
j
-th attempt cycle. The cycle-length
bound above is uniform over the past, so

\mathbb{E}[C_{j}\mid{\mathcal{H}}_{j-1}]\leq L_{\varepsilon}
whenever the
j
-th cycle
starts. Since

T_{n}^{\mathrm{BAL,Mo}}\leq\sum_{j\geq 1}\mathbf{1}\{N_{\mathrm{att}}\geq j\}C_{j}
,
we obtain

\mathbb{E}[T_{n}^{\mathrm{BAL,Mo}}]\leq L_{\varepsilon}\sum_{j\geq 1}\mathbb{P}(N_{\mathrm{att}}\geq j)=L_{\varepsilon}\mathbb{E}[N_{\mathrm{att}}]\leq\left(\frac{2n}{q_{\varepsilon}}+1\right)\alpha_{\chi,\varepsilon}^{-n}.

Substituting
q_{\varepsilon}=(1-\varepsilon_{n})/2
and

\alpha_{\chi,\varepsilon}=(1-\varepsilon_{n})/(2-\chi(1-\varepsilon_{n}))

gives

\mathbb{E}[T_{n}^{\mathrm{BAL,Mo}}]\leq\left(\frac{4n}{1-\varepsilon_{n}}+1\right)\left(\frac{2-\chi(1-\varepsilon_{n})}{1-\varepsilon_{n}}\right)^{n}.

Finally, suppose
\varepsilon_{n}\leq\min\left\{c/n,1/2\right\}
and

1-\chi\leq c_{\chi}/n
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{2-\chi(1-\varepsilon_{n})}{1-\varepsilon_{n}}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{1-\chi+\varepsilon_{n}(1+\chi)}{1-\varepsilon_{n}}=1+O_{c,c_{\chi}}\!\left(\frac{1}{n}\right).

Therefore its
n
-th power is
O_{c,c_{\chi}}(1)
, while

(4n)/(1-\varepsilon_{n})+1=O_{c}(n)
. Hence

\mathbb{E}\left[T_{n}^{\mathrm{BAL,Mo}}\mid\widetilde{Z}_{0}=(\varnothing,\downarrow)\right]=O_{c,c_{\chi}}(n).

∎

Corollary D.1

(Depth-axis speedup with near-maximal momentum)
.

Assume
D.1
with

\varepsilon_{n}\leq\min\left\{\frac{c}{n},\frac{1}{2}\right\}

for a fixed constant
c>0
. Let the flow-cancelled momentum parameter satisfy

1-\chi\leq\frac{c_{\chi}}{n}

for a fixed constant
c_{\chi}>0
. Then the no-momentum balanced AOAR-VGB depth
process satisfies

\mathbb{E}\left[T_{n}^{\mathrm{BAL}}\mid\tau_{0}^{\mathrm{BAL}}=0\right]=\Theta_{c}(n^{2}),

while the flow-cancelled momentum lift satisfies

\mathbb{E}\left[T_{n}^{\mathrm{BAL,Mo}}\mid\widetilde{Z}_{0}=(\varnothing,\downarrow)\right]=O_{c,c_{\chi}}(n).

Thus, under the same near-balance condition and near-maximal cancellation,

\frac{\mathbb{E}[T_{n}^{\mathrm{BAL}}\mid\tau_{0}^{\mathrm{BAL}}=0]}{\mathbb{E}[T_{n}^{\mathrm{BAL,Mo}}\mid\widetilde{Z}_{0}=(\varnothing,\downarrow)]}=\Omega_{c,c_{\chi}}(n).

The maximally flow-cancelled case
\chi=1
is included by taking

c_{\chi}=0
.

Proof.

This follows immediately from
Proposition

D.2
and
Theorem

D.3
.
∎

Corollary D.2

(Ratio form of the near-balance condition)
.

Suppose there exists
\rho_{n}\geq 1
such that for every reachable interior state
z
,

\frac{1}{\rho_{n}}\leq\frac{p_{\downarrow}(z)}{p_{\uparrow}(z)}\leq\rho_{n}.

Then

\left|p_{\downarrow}(z)-p_{\uparrow}(z)\right|\leq\frac{\rho_{n}-1}{\rho_{n}+1}.

Consequently, if there are fixed constants
c,c_{\chi}>0
such that

\frac{\rho_{n}-1}{\rho_{n}+1}\leq\min\left\{\frac{c}{n},\frac{1}{2}\right\},\qquad 1-\chi\leq\frac{c_{\chi}}{n},

then the depth-axis speedup in

Corollary

D.1
applies. In particular, the ratio condition
holds asymptotically when
\rho_{n}=1+O(1/n)
, provided the cancellation is
near-maximal.

Proof.

Let
p=p_{\downarrow}(z)
and
q=p_{\uparrow}(z)
. Since
p+q=1
and
p/q\leq\rho_{n}
, we have
p\leq\rho_{n}q=\rho_{n}(1-p)
, so
p\leq\rho_{n}/(1+\rho_{n})
. Similarly,
q/p\leq\rho_{n}
gives
p\geq 1/(1+\rho_{n})
. Therefore,

\left|p-\frac{1}{2}\right|\leq\frac{\rho_{n}-1}{2(\rho_{n}+1)}.

Since
|p-q|=|2p-1|
, we obtain
|p-q|\leq(\rho_{n}-1)/(\rho_{n}+1)
. The displayed condition above gives
D.1
with
\varepsilon_{n}\leq\min\left\{c/n,1/2\right\}
and the near-maximal cancellation condition
1-\chi\leq c_{\chi}/n
, so
Corollary

D.1
applies. If
\rho_{n}=1+O(1/n)
and
1-\chi=O(1/n)
, then these conditions hold for all sufficiently large
n
.
∎

Corollary D.3

(Implication of multiplicative verifier accuracy)
.

Assume the canonical balanced coefficients
\beta_{k}=k
and
\alpha_{k}=n-k
for
1\leq k\leq n-1
. Suppose the plug-in verifier satisfies the multiplicative accuracy condition

\kappa^{-1}V^{*}(x,z)\leq\widehat{V}(x,z)\leq\kappa V^{*}(x,z)

on all relevant non-leaf states. Then, for every reachable interior state
z
,

\frac{1}{\kappa^{2}}\leq\frac{p_{\downarrow}(z)}{p_{\uparrow}(z)}\leq\kappa^{2}.

Therefore, by
Corollary

D.2
, the linear first-leaf
hitting guarantee is certified whenever, for fixed constants
c,c_{\chi}>0
,

\frac{\kappa^{2}-1}{\kappa^{2}+1}\leq\min\left\{\frac{c}{n},\frac{1}{2}\right\},\qquad 1-\chi\leq\frac{c_{\chi}}{n}.

Asymptotically, certifying this condition from
\kappa
alone requires

\kappa^{2}=1+O(1/n)
, equivalently
\kappa=1+O(1/n)
, together with
near-maximal cancellation.

Proof.

Let
F(z)
and
B(z)
denote the plug-in forward and backward total masses at
z
. Under the exact value
V^{*}
, the canonical balanced choice gives
F^{*}(z)=B^{*}(z)
by
Proposition

C.4
. Under the multiplicative verifier condition, the plug-in forward total satisfies

\kappa^{-1}F^{*}(z)\leq F(z)\leq\kappa F^{*}(z),

because each child value
\widehat{V}(x,c)
is within a factor
\kappa
of
V^{*}(x,c)
. Similarly,

\kappa^{-1}B^{*}(z)\leq B(z)\leq\kappa B^{*}(z).

Since
F^{*}(z)=B^{*}(z)
, it follows that

\frac{1}{\kappa^{2}}\leq\frac{F(z)}{B(z)}\leq\kappa^{2}.

But

\frac{p_{\downarrow}(z)}{p_{\uparrow}(z)}=\frac{F(z)/(F(z)+B(z))}{B(z)/(F(z)+B(z))}=\frac{F(z)}{B(z)}.

Applying
Corollary

D.2
with
\rho_{n}=\kappa^{2}
gives the stated sufficient condition. In particular, to certify the linear first-leaf hitting guarantee from this multiplicative bound alone, it is enough asymptotically that
\kappa^{2}=1+O(1/n)
, equivalently
\kappa=1+O(1/n)
, together with
1-\chi=O(1/n)
.
∎

Appendix E
Balanced Parallel VGBs for Masked Diffusion

The previous AOAR sections study single-site reveal and re-mask moves. In this section, we extend the same VGB principle to parallel masked-diffusion style updates, motivated by discrete and masked diffusion language models
[
3
,
24
]
. The resulting MDM-VGB family allows a move to reveal or re-mask multiple coordinates at once.

The construction proceeds in three steps. First, we define a leaf-corrected balanced MDM-VGB, which is the direct block-update analogue of Balanced AOAR-VGB. Second, we introduce a clean geometric balanced MDM-VGB whose backward remasking rule is value-gated while avoiding any explicit
M_{\mathrm{ref}}
correction in the local implementation. Third, we add a unified flow-cancelled momentum framework that applies to either base kernel once its local forward and backward weights have been specified.

In particular, AOAR-VGB is recovered by the singleton-block restriction

{\mathcal{M}}=\left\{1\right\}
. Under this restriction, the MDM algorithms below also serve
as the implementation templates for the AOAR samplers.

E.1
Balanced MDM-VGB

Setup and notation.

We reuse the shared notation from
Appendices

B
and
C
. In particular,
x
is the conditioning context,
{\mathcal{Y}}={\mathcal{V}}^{n}
is the set of fully revealed sequences,
{\mathcal{Z}}=\left({\mathcal{V}}\cup\left\{\texttt{[mask]}\right\}\right)^{n}
is the masked state space,
R(z)
is the set of revealed coordinates,
k(z)=\left|R(z)\right|
is the depth of
z
, and
{\mathcal{C}}(z)
is the compatible completion set. Recall that

U^{*}(x,z)=M_{\mathrm{ref}}(x,z)V^{*}(x,z)

is the tilted completion mass, and recall the reachable masked-state set

{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathrm{reach}}:=\left\{z\in{\mathcal{Z}}:M_{\mathrm{ref}}(x,z)>0\right\}.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}\subseteq\left\{1,\dots,n\right\}
be a nonempty set of admissible update sizes. We assume
1\in{\mathcal{M}}
, so every non-root state has at least one possible backward update and every non-leaf state has at least one possible forward update.

For a reachable state
z
and a block size
r\in{\mathcal{M}}
, define

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}_{r}^{+}(z)

\displaystyle:=\left\{A\subseteq\left[n\right]\setminus R(z):\left|A\right|=r\right\},\qquad{\mathcal{B}}_{r}^{-}(z):=\left\{A\subseteq R(z):\left|A\right|=r\right\}.

For a forward block
A\in{\mathcal{B}}_{r}^{+}(z)
and assignment
a_{A}\in{\mathcal{V}}^{A}
, write
z^{A\leftarrow a_{A}}
for the state obtained by filling all coordinates in
A
with
a_{A}
. For a backward block
A\in{\mathcal{B}}_{r}^{-}(z)
, write
z^{-A}
for the state obtained by re-masking all coordinates in
A
.

The MDM oracle assumption is that, for every reachable
z
, every
A\subseteq\left[n\right]\setminus R(z)
, and every
a_{A}\in{\mathcal{V}}^{A}
, we can evaluate the joint reference conditional

\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(Y_{A}=a_{A}\mid x,z):=\mathbb{P}_{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}\left[Y_{A}=a_{A}\mid x,\ Y\in{\mathcal{C}}(z)\right].

Equivalently,

\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(Y_{A}=a_{A}\mid x,z)=\frac{M_{\mathrm{ref}}(x,z^{A\leftarrow a_{A}})}{M_{\mathrm{ref}}(x,z)}.

Remark E.1

(Practical joint conditionals)
.

In a practical masked diffusion model, the exact joint conditional
\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(Y_{A}=a_{A}\mid x,z)
may not be directly available. One may replace it by a factorized approximation, for example

\widetilde{\pi}_{\mathrm{ref}}(Y_{A}=a_{A}\mid x,z):=\prod_{i\in A}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(Y_{i}=a_{i}\mid x,z).

This factorized plug-in is a practical approximation: the exact stationarity statements below use the exact joint conditional.

Block identities.

We first collect the block analogues of the single-coordinate partition and Bellman identities.

Lemma E.1

(Block partition identity)
.

Fix a reachable state
z
and a block
A\subseteq\left[n\right]\setminus R(z)
. Then

{\mathcal{C}}(z)=\bigsqcup_{a_{A}\in{\mathcal{V}}^{A}}{\mathcal{C}}(z^{A\leftarrow a_{A}}).

Consequently,

\sum_{a_{A}\in{\mathcal{V}}^{A}}U^{*}(x,z^{A\leftarrow a_{A}})=U^{*}(x,z),\qquad\sum_{a_{A}\in{\mathcal{V}}^{A}}M_{\mathrm{ref}}(x,z^{A\leftarrow a_{A}})=M_{\mathrm{ref}}(x,z).

Proof.

Every full sequence
y\in{\mathcal{C}}(z)
has a unique restriction
y_{A}\in{\mathcal{V}}^{A}
. Hence
y
belongs to exactly one completion set
{\mathcal{C}}(z^{A\leftarrow a_{A}})
, namely the one with
a_{A}=y_{A}
. Summing
\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\tau(x,y)
over this disjoint partition gives the
U^{*}
identity. Setting
\tau\equiv 1
gives the
M_{\mathrm{ref}}
identity.
∎

Proposition E.1

(Block Bellman identity)
.

For every reachable state
z
and every block
A\subseteq\left[n\right]\setminus R(z)
,

V^{*}(x,z)=\sum_{a_{A}\in{\mathcal{V}}^{A}}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(Y_{A}=a_{A}\mid x,z)V^{*}(x,z^{A\leftarrow a_{A}}).

Proof.

By the law of total expectation,

\displaystyle V^{*}(x,z)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}\left[\tau(x,Y)\mid x,\ Y\in{\mathcal{C}}(z)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{a_{A}\in{\mathcal{V}}^{A}}\mathbb{P}_{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}\left[Y_{A}=a_{A}\mid x,z\right]\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}\left[\tau(x,Y)\mid x,\ Y\in{\mathcal{C}}(z),\ Y_{A}=a_{A}\right].

The event
\left\{Y\in{\mathcal{C}}(z),Y_{A}=a_{A}\right\}
is exactly
\left\{Y\in{\mathcal{C}}(z^{A\leftarrow a_{A}})\right\}
. Therefore the inner conditional expectation equals
V^{*}(x,z^{A\leftarrow a_{A}})
.
∎

Proposition E.2

(Exact block target conditional)
.

For every reachable state
z
with
U^{*}(x,z)>0
, every block
A\subseteq\left[n\right]\setminus R(z)
, and every
a_{A}\in{\mathcal{V}}^{A}
,

\pi^{*}(Y_{A}=a_{A}\mid x,z)=\frac{U^{*}(x,z^{A\leftarrow a_{A}})}{U^{*}(x,z)}=\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(Y_{A}=a_{A}\mid x,z)\frac{V^{*}(x,z^{A\leftarrow a_{A}})}{V^{*}(x,z)}.

Proof.

The first equality follows from conditional probability under the tilted target:

\pi^{*}(Y_{A}=a_{A}\mid x,z)=\frac{\mathbb{P}_{\pi^{*}}(Y\in{\mathcal{C}}(z^{A\leftarrow a_{A}})\mid x)}{\mathbb{P}_{\pi^{*}}(Y\in{\mathcal{C}}(z)\mid x)}=\frac{U^{*}(x,z^{A\leftarrow a_{A}})}{U^{*}(x,z)}.

For the second equality, use
U^{*}(x,z)=M_{\mathrm{ref}}(x,z)V^{*}(x,z)
and

M_{\mathrm{ref}}(x,z^{A\leftarrow a_{A}})=M_{\mathrm{ref}}(x,z)\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(Y_{A}=a_{A}\mid x,z).

∎

Leaf-corrected balanced MDM edge weights.

For each update size
r\in{\mathcal{M}}
, define the depth-size edge coefficient

s_{k,r}:=\frac{1}{\binom{n-r}{k}},\qquad 0\leq k\leq n-r.

Then

\binom{n-k}{r}s_{k,r}=\binom{k}{r}s_{k-r,r}

whenever
r\leq k\leq n-r
. This is the multi-token analogue of the single-site balanced coefficient
s_{k}=1/\binom{n-1}{k}
.

For a reachable state
z
, define the MDM child, parent, and full neighborhoods by

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{MDM}}(z)

\displaystyle:=\left\{z^{A\leftarrow a_{A}}:r\in{\mathcal{M}},\ A\in{\mathcal{B}}_{r}^{+}(z),\ a_{A}\in{\mathcal{V}}^{A},\ z^{A\leftarrow a_{A}}\in{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathrm{reach}}\right\},

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{MDM}}(z)

\displaystyle:=\left\{z^{-A}:r\in{\mathcal{M}},\ A\in{\mathcal{B}}_{r}^{-}(z)\right\},\qquad N_{\mathrm{MDM}}(z):=C_{\mathrm{MDM}}(z)\cup P_{\mathrm{MDM}}(z).

Definition E.1

(Leaf-corrected balanced MDM edge weights)
.

Consider a parent state
z
of depth
k=k(z)
and a child
u=z^{A\leftarrow a_{A}}
, where
A\in{\mathcal{B}}_{r}^{+}(z)
and
r=\left|A\right|
. Thus
u
has depth
k+r
. Define the symmetric edge weight

f_{\mathrm{MDM}}(z,u):=\begin{cases}s_{k,r}\,M_{\mathrm{ref}}(x,u)\widehat{V}(x,u),&u\notin{\mathcal{Y}},\\[2.84526pt]
s_{n-r,r}\,M_{\mathrm{ref}}(x,u)\tau(x,u),&u\in{\mathcal{Y}}.\end{cases}

Equivalently, using
\widehat{U}(x,u):=M_{\mathrm{ref}}(x,u)\widehat{V}(x,u)
and
U^{*}(x,y)=M_{\mathrm{ref}}(x,y)\tau(x,y)
for leaves,

f_{\mathrm{MDM}}(z,u)=\begin{cases}s_{k,r}\widehat{U}(x,u),&u\notin{\mathcal{Y}},\\
s_{n-r,r}U^{*}(x,u),&u\in{\mathcal{Y}}.\end{cases}

The edge weight is understood symmetrically on the undirected block-update graph.

Local implementation weights.

The edge weights contain
M_{\mathrm{ref}}
, but the local implementation does not require estimating it. From a fixed current state
z
, all incident edge weights share the common factor
M_{\mathrm{ref}}(x,z)
, which cancels after normalization.

Definition E.2

(
\gamma
-held balanced MDM-VGB local kernel)
.

Let
z
be a reachable state of depth
k=k(z)
. We use terminal anchoring on leaves,

\widehat{V}(x,y)=\tau(x,y),\qquad y\in{\mathcal{Y}}.

Define the local implementation weights by

\displaystyle\textsc{Forward:}\quad w_{\mathrm{MDM,fwd}}(z\to u)

\displaystyle:=s_{k,r}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(Y_{A}=a_{A}\mid x,z)\widehat{V}(x,u),

\displaystyle\textsc{Backward:}\quad w_{\mathrm{MDM,bwd}}(z\to u)

\displaystyle:=s_{k-r,r}\widehat{V}(x,z),

where

\displaystyle u=z^{A\leftarrow a_{A}},\quad A\in{\mathcal{B}}_{r}^{+}(z)

\displaystyle\text{for forward moves},

\displaystyle u=z^{-A},\quad A\in{\mathcal{B}}_{r}^{-}(z)

\displaystyle\text{for backward moves}.

Let
w_{\mathrm{MDM}}(z\to u)
denote the corresponding forward or backward weight, and set

W_{\mathrm{MDM}}(z):=\sum_{u\in C_{\mathrm{MDM}}(z)}w_{\mathrm{MDM,fwd}}(z\to u)+\sum_{u\in P_{\mathrm{MDM}}(z)}w_{\mathrm{MDM,bwd}}(z\to u).

The non-lazy move kernel is

K_{\mathrm{MDM}}(z,u):=\frac{w_{\mathrm{MDM}}(z\to u)}{W_{\mathrm{MDM}}(z)},\qquad u\in N_{\mathrm{MDM}}(z).

This formula is used only at states with
W_{\mathrm{MDM}}(z)>0
.
The
\gamma
-held kernel is obtained by setting

P_{\mathrm{MDM}}^{(\gamma)}(z,z)=\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma},\qquad P_{\mathrm{MDM}}^{(\gamma)}(z,u)=\frac{1}{1+\gamma}K_{\mathrm{MDM}}(z,u).

Theorem E.1

(Weighted-graph representation and exact leaf law)
.

The local kernel in
Definition

E.2
is the weighted random walk on the graph with symmetric edge weights from
Definition

E.1
. Therefore, its stationary distribution is

\mu_{\mathrm{MDM}}(z)=\frac{D_{\mathrm{MDM}}(z)}{\sum_{z^{\prime}}D_{\mathrm{MDM}}(z^{\prime})},\qquad D_{\mathrm{MDM}}(z):=\sum_{u\sim z}f_{\mathrm{MDM}}(z,u).

Moreover, the leaf-conditioned stationary law is exact:

\mu_{\mathrm{MDM}}(y\mid y\in{\mathcal{Y}})=\pi^{*}(y\mid x).

Proof.

Fix a state
z
and a forward child
u=z^{A\leftarrow a_{A}}
of size
r
. If
u\notin{\mathcal{Y}}
, then

\displaystyle\frac{f_{\mathrm{MDM}}(z,u)}{M_{\mathrm{ref}}(x,z)}

\displaystyle=s_{k(z),r}\frac{M_{\mathrm{ref}}(x,u)}{M_{\mathrm{ref}}(x,z)}\widehat{V}(x,u)

\displaystyle=s_{k(z),r}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(Y_{A}=a_{A}\mid x,z)\widehat{V}(x,u),

which is the forward local weight. If
u\in{\mathcal{Y}}
, the same calculation gives

\frac{f_{\mathrm{MDM}}(z,u)}{M_{\mathrm{ref}}(x,z)}=s_{n-r,r}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(Y_{A}=a_{A}\mid x,z)\tau(x,u).

For a backward move from
z
to
u=z^{-A}
, the edge is the same edge with child endpoint
z
. Dividing its edge weight by
M_{\mathrm{ref}}(x,z)
gives
s_{k(z)-r,r}\widehat{V}(x,z)
if
z\notin{\mathcal{Y}}
, and
s_{n-r,r}\tau(x,z)
if
z\in{\mathcal{Y}}
. Thus the local weights are proportional to incident edge weights by a state-dependent common factor, which cancels under normalization.

The stationary law of a random walk on a symmetric weighted graph is proportional to weighted degree. It remains to check the leaf law. For any leaf
y\in{\mathcal{Y}}
, every admissible backward block
A\subseteq\left[n\right]
of size
r\in{\mathcal{M}}
gives a parent
y^{-A}
, and the corresponding leaf edge has weight

f_{\mathrm{MDM}}(y^{-A},y)=s_{n-r,r}U^{*}(x,y).

Therefore

D_{\mathrm{MDM}}(y)=U^{*}(x,y)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\in{\mathcal{M}}\\
r\leq n\end{subarray}}\binom{n}{r}s_{n-r,r}.

The multiplicative factor is constant over leaves. Since
U^{*}(x,y)=\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\tau(x,y)
, conditioning on
{\mathcal{Y}}
gives

\mu_{\mathrm{MDM}}(y\mid y\in{\mathcal{Y}})=\frac{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\tau(x,y)}{\sum_{y^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{Y}}}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y^{\prime}\mid x)\tau(x,y^{\prime})}=\pi^{*}(y\mid x).

∎

Proposition E.3

(Exact directional balance under exact values)
.

Assume
\widehat{V}=V^{*}
on all non-leaf states and terminal anchoring on leaves. Let
z
be an interior state of depth
k
. For a fixed block size
r\in{\mathcal{M}}
with
r\leq\min\left\{k,n-k\right\}
, the exact forward and backward masses of size
r
satisfy

W_{\mathrm{fwd},r}^{*}(z)=W_{\mathrm{bwd},r}^{*}(z).

Consequently, if at an interior state only paired sizes

{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{pair}}(z):=\left\{r\in{\mathcal{M}}:r\leq\min\left\{k(z),n-k(z)\right\}\right\}

are used, then the exact move-conditioned forward and backward probabilities are both
1/2
.

Proof.

Fix
r\leq\min\left\{k,n-k\right\}
. Using
Proposition

E.1
, for each
A\in{\mathcal{B}}_{r}^{+}(z)
,

\sum_{a_{A}\in{\mathcal{V}}^{A}}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(Y_{A}=a_{A}\mid x,z)V^{*}(x,z^{A\leftarrow a_{A}})=V^{*}(x,z).

There are
\binom{n-k}{r}
forward blocks of size
r
, so

W_{\mathrm{fwd},r}^{*}(z)=\binom{n-k}{r}s_{k,r}V^{*}(x,z).

For backward blocks of size
r
, there are
\binom{k}{r}
choices, and each backward weight is
s_{k-r,r}V^{*}(x,z)
. Hence

W_{\mathrm{bwd},r}^{*}(z)=\binom{k}{r}s_{k-r,r}V^{*}(x,z).

The coefficient identity in
Section

E.1
gives equality. Summing over paired sizes preserves equality.
∎

Geometric Balanced MDM-VGB

Balanced MDM-VGB gives the block-update analogue of the balanced AOAR walk. We now add the same value-gated remasking idea used in the geometric AOAR variant. The goal is to preserve the forward VGB ranking while making the backward block choice depend on the verifier value of the remasked parent.

Figure

A\in{\mathcal{M}}

Setting

recovers balanced MDM-VGB.

Definition E.3

(Clean geometric balanced MDM edge weights)
.

Fix
\lambda\geq 0
. For every admissible size
r\in{\mathcal{M}}
, let

s_{k,r}:=\binom{n-r}{k}^{-1},\qquad 0\leq k\leq n-r,

be the canonical balanced depth-size coefficient. Consider neighboring states
z,u
with
\left|k(u)-k(z)\right|=r
. Let
p(z,u)
be the lower-depth endpoint and
c(z,u)
be the higher-depth endpoint, so that

c(z,u)=p(z,u)^{A\leftarrow a_{A}}

for a unique block
A
of size
r
and assignment
a_{A}
.

If
c(z,u)\notin{\mathcal{Y}}
, define

f_{\mathrm{BGM}}(z,u)=f_{\mathrm{BGM}}(u,z):=s_{k(p(z,u)),r}\widehat{U}(x,c(z,u))\widehat{V}(x,p(z,u))^{\lambda}.

If
c(z,u)=y\in{\mathcal{Y}}
, define the leaf-corrected edge by

f_{\mathrm{BGM}}(z,u)=f_{\mathrm{BGM}}(u,z):=s_{n-r,r}U^{*}(x,y).

Local implementation weights.

Let
z
have depth
k
. For a non-leaf forward child
u=z^{A\leftarrow a_{A}}\notin{\mathcal{Y}}
with
\left|A\right|=r
,

w_{\mathrm{BGM,fwd}}(z\to u)=s_{k,r}\widehat{V}(x,z)^{\lambda}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(Y_{A}=a_{A}\mid x,z)\widehat{V}(x,u).

The factor
\widehat{V}(x,z)^{\lambda}
is common across all forward candidates from the same current state
z
, so the forward ranking remains

\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(Y_{A}=a_{A}\mid x,z)\widehat{V}(x,z^{A\leftarrow a_{A}}).

For a backward move from a non-leaf state
z
to
u=z^{-A}
,

w_{\mathrm{BGM,bwd}}(z\to z^{-A})=s_{k-r,r}\widehat{V}(x,z)\widehat{V}(x,z^{-A})^{\lambda}.

Thus backward moves prefer blocks whose remasked parent has high verifier value.

At the leaf boundary, if
y=z^{A\leftarrow a_{A}}\in{\mathcal{Y}}
, then

w_{\mathrm{BGM,fwd}}^{\mathrm{leaf}}(z\to y)=s_{n-r,r}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(Y_{A}=a_{A}\mid x,z)\tau(x,y),

and from a leaf
y
to a parent
y^{-A}
,

w_{\mathrm{BGM,bwd}}(y\to y^{-A})=s_{n-r,r}\tau(x,y).

Remark E.2

(
\lambda=0
recovers balanced MDM-VGB)
.

When
\lambda=0
, the geometric gate disappears, and the local weights in
Sections

E.2
and
E.2
reduce to the balanced MDM-VGB weights in

E.2
.

Theorem E.2

(Stationarity and exact leaf law for clean geometric MDM)
.

Let
P_{\mathrm{BGM}}
be the weighted random walk on the block-update graph with edge weights from
Definition

E.3
. Then

\mu_{\mathrm{BGM}}(z)=\frac{D_{\mathrm{BGM}}(z)}{\sum_{z^{\prime}}D_{\mathrm{BGM}}(z^{\prime})},\qquad D_{\mathrm{BGM}}(z):=\sum_{u\sim z}f_{\mathrm{BGM}}(z,u),

is stationary. Moreover,

\mu_{\mathrm{BGM}}(y\mid y\in{\mathcal{Y}})=\pi^{*}(y\mid x).

Proof.

The stationarity proof is the standard detailed-balance proof for a symmetric weighted graph. For neighboring states
z\sim u
,

\mu_{\mathrm{BGM}}(z)P_{\mathrm{BGM}}(z,u)=\frac{D_{\mathrm{BGM}}(z)}{\sum_{z^{\prime}}D_{\mathrm{BGM}}(z^{\prime})}\frac{f_{\mathrm{BGM}}(z,u)}{D_{\mathrm{BGM}}(z)}=\frac{f_{\mathrm{BGM}}(z,u)}{\sum_{z^{\prime}}D_{\mathrm{BGM}}(z^{\prime})}.

This expression is symmetric in
z
and
u
, hence detailed balance holds.

Now take a leaf
y\in{\mathcal{Y}}
. For every admissible block size
r\in{\mathcal{M}}
and every block
A\subseteq\left[n\right]
with
\left|A\right|=r
, the parent is
y^{-A}
and the leaf edge has weight

f_{\mathrm{BGM}}(y^{-A},y)=s_{n-r,r}U^{*}(x,y).

Therefore

D_{\mathrm{BGM}}(y)=U^{*}(x,y)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\in{\mathcal{M}}\\
r\leq n\end{subarray}}\binom{n}{r}s_{n-r,r}.

The multiplier is independent of
y
, so conditioning on leaves yields

\mu_{\mathrm{BGM}}(y\mid y\in{\mathcal{Y}})=\frac{U^{*}(x,y)}{\sum_{y^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{Y}}}U^{*}(x,y^{\prime})}=\pi^{*}(y\mid x).

∎

Proposition E.4

(Exact block directional masses)
.

Assume
\widehat{V}=V^{*}
on non-leaf states. Let
z
have depth
k
with
V^{*}(x,z)>0
, and fix an admissible size
r
with
r\leq k
and
k+r<n
, so that the size-
r
forward children are non-leaf. For a backward block
A\subseteq R(z)
with
\left|A\right|=r
, define

r_{A}^{*}(z):=\frac{V^{*}(x,z^{-A})}{V^{*}(x,z)}.

Then the exact size-
r
forward and backward masses are

F_{\lambda,r}^{*}(z)=s_{k,r}\binom{n-k}{r}V^{*}(x,z)^{1+\lambda},

and

B_{\lambda,r}^{*}(z)=s_{k-r,r}V^{*}(x,z)^{1+\lambda}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\subseteq R(z)\\
\left|A\right|=r\end{subarray}}r_{A}^{*}(z)^{\lambda}.

Proof.

For each forward block
A\in{\mathcal{B}}_{r}^{+}(z)
, the block Bellman identity gives

\sum_{a_{A}\in{\mathcal{V}}^{A}}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(Y_{A}=a_{A}\mid x,z)V^{*}(x,z^{A\leftarrow a_{A}})=V^{*}(x,z).

There are
\binom{n-k}{r}
forward blocks, hence

F_{\lambda,r}^{*}(z)=s_{k,r}V^{*}(x,z)^{\lambda}\binom{n-k}{r}V^{*}(x,z)=s_{k,r}\binom{n-k}{r}V^{*}(x,z)^{1+\lambda}.

For backward blocks,

\displaystyle B_{\lambda,r}^{*}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\subseteq R(z)\\
\left|A\right|=r\end{subarray}}s_{k-r,r}V^{*}(x,z)V^{*}(x,z^{-A})^{\lambda}

\displaystyle=s_{k-r,r}V^{*}(x,z)^{1+\lambda}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\subseteq R(z)\\
\left|A\right|=r\end{subarray}}\left[\frac{V^{*}(x,z^{-A})}{V^{*}(x,z)}\right]^{\lambda}.

∎

Remark E.3

(Canonical MDM depth anchor)
.

For the balanced MDM chain, the canonical coefficient

s_{k,r}^{\mathrm{bal}}=\frac{1}{\binom{n-r}{k}}

satisfies

\binom{n-k}{r}s_{k,r}^{\mathrm{bal}}=\binom{k}{r}s_{k-r,r}^{\mathrm{bal}}.

For the clean geometric MDM chain with
\lambda>0
, this coefficient no longer gives exact
1/2
–
1/2
balance in general. Instead, it is the canonical depth-neutral anchor: it recovers exact balance at
\lambda=0
, while the
\lambda>0
correction changes backward probabilities according to the verifier values of remasked parent states.

The sampler in
Algorithm

3
specializes to the AOAR sampler
when
{\mathcal{M}}=\left\{1\right\}
, since each admissible block is a singleton coordinate.

E.3
Momentum Framework for Balanced and Geometric MDM-VGB

We now add momentum after both base MDM kernels have been defined. This separation is useful because the same lifted transition only needs the local forward and backward weights of the chosen base kernel.

Figure

15
illustrates the momentum construction for the geometric MDM base kernel. The same diagram also applies to balanced MDM-VGB after setting
\lambda=0
in the local block weights.

Figure 15
:

Illustration of the flow-cancelled momentum lift for geometric MDM-VGB.
The downward copy keeps block reveal moves, the upward copy keeps block re-mask moves, and residual opposite-direction mass switches momentum; the cancelled common mass may be retained as an optional stay probability.

Let

\mathsf{B}\in\{\mathrm{MDM},\mathrm{BGM}\}

denote either the balanced MDM-VGB base kernel or the clean geometric balanced MDM-VGB base kernel. Let

w_{\mathsf{B},\mathrm{fwd}}(z\to c),\qquad w_{\mathsf{B},\mathrm{bwd}}(z\to p)

be its local forward and backward weights, as defined in
Definition

E.2
for
\mathsf{B}=\mathrm{MDM}
and in
Sections

E.2
,
E.2
,
E.2
and
E.2
for
\mathsf{B}=\mathrm{BGM}
.

Define the forward and backward total masses

F_{\mathsf{B}}(z):=\sum_{c\in C_{\mathrm{MDM}}(z)}w_{\mathsf{B},\mathrm{fwd}}(z\to c),\qquad B_{\mathsf{B}}(z):=\sum_{p\in P_{\mathrm{MDM}}(z)}w_{\mathsf{B},\mathrm{bwd}}(z\to p).

The non-lazy base move kernel is

K_{\mathsf{B}}(z,u):=\frac{w_{\mathsf{B}}(z\to u)}{F_{\mathsf{B}}(z)+B_{\mathsf{B}}(z)},\qquad u\in N_{\mathrm{MDM}}(z),

where
w_{\mathsf{B}}
denotes the appropriate forward or backward local weight. Let
\mu_{\mathsf{B}}
be its stationary law. By
Theorem

E.1
for
\mathsf{B}=\mathrm{MDM}
and
Theorem

E.2
for
\mathsf{B}=\mathrm{BGM}
, the projected leaf-conditioned law is exact:

\mu_{\mathsf{B}}(\cdot\mid{\mathcal{Y}})=\pi^{*}(\cdot\mid x).

Let
{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathsf{B},+}
be the positive-weight component of
K_{\mathsf{B}}
, i.e., the component on which
F_{\mathsf{B}}(z)+B_{\mathsf{B}}(z)>0
.

For
z\in{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathsf{B},+}
, define directional probabilities

p_{\downarrow}^{\mathsf{B}}(z):=\sum_{c\in C_{\mathrm{MDM}}(z)}K_{\mathsf{B}}(z,c)=\frac{F_{\mathsf{B}}(z)}{F_{\mathsf{B}}(z)+B_{\mathsf{B}}(z)},

and

p_{\uparrow}^{\mathsf{B}}(z):=\sum_{p\in P_{\mathrm{MDM}}(z)}K_{\mathsf{B}}(z,p)=\frac{B_{\mathsf{B}}(z)}{F_{\mathsf{B}}(z)+B_{\mathsf{B}}(z)}.

Thus
p_{\downarrow}^{\mathsf{B}}(z)+p_{\uparrow}^{\mathsf{B}}(z)=1
whenever
F_{\mathsf{B}}(z)+B_{\mathsf{B}}(z)>0
.

Definition E.4

(Flow-cancelled momentum MDM kernel)
.

Fix a base kernel
\mathsf{B}\in\{\mathrm{MDM},\mathrm{BGM}\}
and a cancellation strength
\chi\in[0,1]
. Set

m_{\mathsf{B}}(z):=\min\left\{p_{\downarrow}^{\mathsf{B}}(z),p_{\uparrow}^{\mathsf{B}}(z)\right\}.

On the lifted state space

\widetilde{{\mathcal{Z}}}_{\mathsf{B}}:={\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathsf{B},+}\times\left\{\downarrow,\uparrow\right\},

define
\widetilde{K}_{\mathsf{B}}^{\mathrm{fc},\chi}
as follows.

Downward copy.

From
(z,\downarrow)
, the chain moves to a child while keeping downward momentum with probability

\widetilde{K}_{\mathsf{B}}^{\mathrm{fc},\chi}\bigl((z,\downarrow),(c,\downarrow)\bigr):=K_{\mathsf{B}}(z,c),\qquad c\in C_{\mathrm{MDM}}(z).

(
Child Move
)

It switches to upward momentum with residual opposite-direction probability

\widetilde{K}_{\mathsf{B}}^{\mathrm{fc},\chi}\bigl((z,\downarrow),(z,\uparrow)\bigr):=p_{\uparrow}^{\mathsf{B}}(z)-\chi m_{\mathsf{B}}(z),

(
Switch Up
)

and stays in the same lifted state with cancelled crossing mass

\widetilde{K}_{\mathsf{B}}^{\mathrm{fc},\chi}\bigl((z,\downarrow),(z,\downarrow)\bigr):=\chi m_{\mathsf{B}}(z).

(
Stay Down, optional
)

Upward copy.

From
(z,\uparrow)
, the chain moves to a parent while keeping upward momentum with probability

\widetilde{K}_{\mathsf{B}}^{\mathrm{fc},\chi}\bigl((z,\uparrow),(p,\uparrow)\bigr):=K_{\mathsf{B}}(z,p),\qquad p\in P_{\mathrm{MDM}}(z).

(
Parent Move
)

It switches to downward momentum with residual opposite-direction probability

\widetilde{K}_{\mathsf{B}}^{\mathrm{fc},\chi}\bigl((z,\uparrow),(z,\downarrow)\bigr):=p_{\downarrow}^{\mathsf{B}}(z)-\chi m_{\mathsf{B}}(z),

(
Switch Down
)

and stays in the same lifted state with cancelled crossing mass

\widetilde{K}_{\mathsf{B}}^{\mathrm{fc},\chi}\bigl((z,\uparrow),(z,\uparrow)\bigr):=\chi m_{\mathsf{B}}(z).

(
Stay Up, optional
)

All other transition probabilities are zero.

Proposition E.5

(Markov property)
.

For every
\chi\in[0,1]
and every
\mathsf{B}\in\{\mathrm{MDM},\mathrm{BGM}\}
,
\widetilde{K}_{\mathsf{B}}^{\mathrm{fc},\chi}
is a Markov kernel.

Proof.

All transition probabilities are nonnegative because
m_{\mathsf{B}}(z)\leq p_{\downarrow}^{\mathsf{B}}(z)
and
m_{\mathsf{B}}(z)\leq p_{\uparrow}^{\mathsf{B}}(z)
. For the downward copy,

\displaystyle\sum_{c\in C_{\mathrm{MDM}}(z)}\widetilde{K}_{\mathsf{B}}^{\mathrm{fc},\chi}\bigl((z,\downarrow),(c,\downarrow)\bigr)+\widetilde{K}_{\mathsf{B}}^{\mathrm{fc},\chi}\bigl((z,\downarrow),(z,\uparrow)\bigr)+\widetilde{K}_{\mathsf{B}}^{\mathrm{fc},\chi}\bigl((z,\downarrow),(z,\downarrow)\bigr)

\displaystyle\qquad=p_{\downarrow}^{\mathsf{B}}(z)+\left(p_{\uparrow}^{\mathsf{B}}(z)-\chi m_{\mathsf{B}}(z)\right)+\chi m_{\mathsf{B}}(z)=1.

The upward row is identical.
∎

Theorem E.3

(Momentum preserves the MDM leaf law)
.

Fix
\mathsf{B}\in\{\mathrm{MDM},\mathrm{BGM}\}
and
\chi\in[0,1]
. Define

\widetilde{\mu}_{\mathsf{B}}(z,\downarrow)=\widetilde{\mu}_{\mathsf{B}}(z,\uparrow):=\frac{1}{2}\mu_{\mathsf{B}}(z).

Then
\widetilde{\mu}_{\mathsf{B}}
is stationary for
\widetilde{K}_{\mathsf{B}}^{\mathrm{fc},\chi}
. Moreover,

\textsf{proj}_{\#}\widetilde{\mu}_{\mathsf{B}}=\mu_{\mathsf{B}},

and hence

\textsf{proj}_{\#}\widetilde{\mu}_{\mathsf{B}}(\cdot\mid{\mathcal{Y}})=\pi^{*}(\cdot\mid x).

Proof.

We verify stationarity for the downward copy; the upward copy is symmetric. Incoming flow into
(z,\downarrow)
comes from three sources: parents of
z
in downward mode, the switch from
(z,\uparrow)
, and the self-loop at
(z,\downarrow)
.

The incoming move-flow from parents is

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{u\in P_{\mathrm{MDM}}(z)}\mu_{\mathsf{B}}(u)K_{\mathsf{B}}(u,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mu_{\mathsf{B}}(z)\sum_{u\in P_{\mathrm{MDM}}(z)}K_{\mathsf{B}}(z,u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mu_{\mathsf{B}}(z)p_{\uparrow}^{\mathsf{B}}(z),

where reversibility of
K_{\mathsf{B}}
was used. The switch-flow from
(z,\uparrow)
is

\frac{1}{2}\mu_{\mathsf{B}}(z)\left(p_{\downarrow}^{\mathsf{B}}(z)-\chi m_{\mathsf{B}}(z)\right),

and the self-loop flow is

\frac{1}{2}\mu_{\mathsf{B}}(z)\chi m_{\mathsf{B}}(z).

The total incoming flow is therefore

\frac{1}{2}\mu_{\mathsf{B}}(z)\left(p_{\uparrow}^{\mathsf{B}}(z)+p_{\downarrow}^{\mathsf{B}}(z)\right)=\frac{1}{2}\mu_{\mathsf{B}}(z)=\widetilde{\mu}_{\mathsf{B}}(z,\downarrow).

The upward-copy argument is identical after exchanging parents and children. Projection gives

\widetilde{\mu}_{\mathsf{B}}(z,\downarrow)+\widetilde{\mu}_{\mathsf{B}}(z,\uparrow)=\mu_{\mathsf{B}}(z).

The leaf law follows from the corresponding base-chain leaf law.
∎

The sampler in
Algorithm

4
also specializes to the
momentum AOAR sampler when
{\mathcal{M}}=\left\{1\right\}
.

Experimental Setting and Supplementary Results

This section gives the full task definitions, data sources, backbone models, reward functions, root-start and leaf-start protocols, and hyperparameter grids used in our experiments. Across tasks, the reference model is denoted by
\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}
and the masked-state verifier by
\widehat{V}(x,z)
, following the notation in
Appendix

A
. For hard-constraint tasks, the terminal reward
\tau(x,y)
is binary. For QM9 and DNA, we first define a real-valued terminal score
R(y)
and then use the corresponding Pass@95 indicator as the terminal reward when a binary reward is needed. Protein reports Success@1Å and motif RMSD, while its learned verifier uses the smoothed training target defined below. Unless otherwise stated, VGB uses terminal anchoring: at a complete leaf
y
,
\widehat{V}(x,y)
is replaced by the corresponding exact terminal reward or score. Experiments were run in an Ubuntu PyTorch environment on NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell 96GB and NVIDIA H200 141GB GPUs.

Common evaluation protocol.

For root-start sampling, every method starts from the fully masked state

\varnothing
and produces a completed sample under a fixed step-limit
budget. For leaf-start repair, every method starts from the same set of low-reward or invalid completed leaves
y_{0}
. All comparisons use the same reference model
\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}
and the same task-specific terminal score/reward convention. In matched-budget root-start comparisons,
BoN
uses
N

step budget before forward-only completion.

Adjusted NFE.

Raw NFE counts realized reference-model forward calls. For tasks with learned verifiers, we additionally report FLOP-adjusted NFE to account for verifier scoring cost. Let
N_{\rm base}
be the number of reference-model forward calls,
N_{\rm verif}
the number of verifier evaluations, and
C_{\rm base}
and
C_{\rm verif}
the FLOPs of one base-model and verifier forward pass. We define

\mathrm{NFE}_{\rm adj}=N_{\rm base}+N_{\rm verif}\frac{C_{\rm verif}}{C_{\rm base}}.

Thus each verifier query is charged as its equivalent fraction of a base-model forward call. For task-provided exact checkers, this adjustment is not applied unless otherwise stated.

Pass@
\alpha
normalization.

For tasks with continuous rewards, let
R(y)
denote the target task-specific score. To make success rates comparable across tasks, we convert the continuous score into a rare-event indicator using a fixed threshold from an independent

be the
\alpha

on this calibration set, where

\mathrm{Pass@\alpha}(y):=\begin{cases}\mathbf{1}\{R(y)\geq q_{\alpha}^{\rm Base}(R)\},&\text{if higher is better},\\
\mathbf{1}\{R(y)\leq q_{1-\alpha}^{\rm Base}(R)\},&\text{if lower is better}.\end{cases}

The reported
\mathrm{Pass@95}
metric corresponds to
\alpha=0.95
.

Dyck suffix-edit repair.

The Dyck language is the canonical language of well-balanced brackets studied in formal-language theory
[
6
]
. It is simple enough to serve as a controlled toy problem, but it still requires hierarchical dependency tracking: for example,
[()]
is valid, whereas

has the right local symbols in the wrong nesting order. We use this structure to illustrate the difference between prefix-tree backtracking and any-order re-masking.

The terminal reward is binary: reward
1

0
means that this well-balanced-bracket condition fails.

Each repair example is initialized from the fixed corrupted leaf template

Here

and

0
. Thus every example starts from an invalid fully revealed leaf.

E
are editable. The suffix is not manually restored: if a sampler re-masks or changes a suffix token, it must also repair that token before the exact Dyck checker accepts the sequence. Equivalently, the reward is the exact bracket-validity checker implemented as membership in the accepted terminal set. The base model is a 12.9M-parameter BERT-style masked language model, and the process verifier is a 3.24M-parameter token-state Transformer.

[
10
]

]

Sudoku is a structured constraint-satisfaction benchmark. Each puzzle is a flattened

\left\{1,\ldots,9\right\}
. The primary metric is exact solve rate. We also report the normalized mean violation count, shown as a percentage:

\mathrm{Viol}(y)=\frac{1}{27}\sum_{U\in\mathcal{U}}\left(9-\left|\mathrm{unique}(y_{U})\right|\right),

where
\mathcal{U}
is the set of the
27
Sudoku units:
9
rows,
9
columns, and
9
boxes. Thus, for a fully filled board with fixed clues preserved,

\mathrm{Solve}(y)=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mathrm{Viol}(y)=0.

We use the Sudoku data provided by
13
, with 48,000 training puzzles and 2,000 test puzzles. Our base model is a 28.6M-parameter DiT-MDM, i.e., a masked discrete diffusion model
[
24
]
with a Diffusion Transformer backbone
[
21
]
, trained on this data. For partial configurations, we use a heuristic process verifier that returns the indicator that no Sudoku constraint has yet been violated.

QM9 molecule generation.

QM9 is a small-molecule generation benchmark over organic molecules with up to nine heavy atoms
[
22
]
. We use a split with 127,190 training molecules and 6,695 test molecules, and train a QM9 MDLM 92.4M base model with the MDLM objective using the UDLM-QM9 architecture. The terminal score
R_{\rm QM9}
uses QED
[
4
]
, a quantitative estimate of drug-likeness:

R_{\rm QM9}(y)=\begin{cases}\mathrm{QED}(y),&y\text{ is a valid molecule},\\
0,&y\text{ is invalid}.\end{cases}

Since higher QED is better, the terminal reward used for rare-success guidance and evaluation is
\mathrm{Pass@95}
from the common definition above with
R(y)=R_{\rm QM9}(y)
. We also report mean QED over valid molecules, validity, and unique Pass@95 where appropriate.

DNA enhancer design.

DNA is a regulatory-sequence design task. The reference model is D3LM
[
33
]
, a DNA diffusion language model initialized from Nucleotide Transformer
[
8
]
. The terminal scorer is DeepSTARR
[
9
]
, which predicts developmental and housekeeping enhancer activity from a
249
bp DNA sequence. The terminal score uses the developmental enhancer activity:

R_{\rm DNA}(y)=s_{\rm Dev}(y).

For verifier training, we use a smoothed target rather than the sparse Pass@95 indicator. Let

z_{\rm Dev}(y)=\mathrm{clip}\left(\frac{s_{\rm Dev}(y)-\mu_{\rm Dev}}{\sigma_{\rm Dev}},-3,3\right),\qquad\tilde{r}_{\rm train}(y)=\exp(\eta z_{\rm Dev}(y)).

Here
\mu_{\rm Dev}
and
\sigma_{\rm Dev}
are computed from base-model calibration samples, and we use
\eta=1
. This target is used only for process-verifier regression. Since higher developmental enhancer activity is better, the evaluation terminal reward is the Pass@95 indicator
\mathbf{1}\{s_{\rm Dev}(y)\geq q_{0.95}^{\rm Base}(s_{\rm Dev})\}
.

Protein motif scaffolding.

Protein is a motif-scaffolding sequence-design task. The reference model is EvoDiff OADM 38M
[
1
]
. We use official EvoDiff motif-scaffolding task metadata whenever available: PDB code, motif ranges, fixed motif residues, scaffold length range, and generated motif positions. In our task, all reported Protein experiments use the 1YCR motif-scaffolding target. The generated sequence is folded with OmegaFold
[
30
]
. We compute motif
C_{\alpha}
root-mean-square deviation (RMSD) against the reference motif:

r_{\rm motif}(y)=\mathrm{RMSD}_{C_{\alpha}}\left(\mathrm{Fold}(y)_{\mathcal{M}},\mathrm{PDB}_{\mathcal{M}}\right).

Here
\mathcal{M}
denotes the motif residue positions,
\mathrm{Fold}(y)
is the OmegaFold-predicted structure,
\mathrm{PDB}_{\mathcal{M}}
contains the reference motif
C_{\alpha}
coordinates, and RMSD is computed after optimal rigid alignment.
The hard success metric is

\text{Success@1\AA{}}(y)=\mathbf{1}\{r_{\rm motif}(y)\leq 1.0\text{\AA}\}.

For verifier training, we use a smoothed RMSD-derived target

\tau_{\rm train}(y)=\exp\left[-\left(\frac{r_{\rm motif}(y)}{4.0\text{\AA}}\right)^{2}\right],

where
r_{\rm motif}(y)
is the motif
C_{\alpha}
RMSD. In implementation, the verifier regresses
\log\tau_{\rm train}(y)
for numerical stability, and we exponentiate its output at inference time to obtain the positive value used by MDM-VGB. This verifier-training target is distinct from the reported Protein metrics: Success@1Å and mean motif RMSD.

F.3

We use task-specific process verifiers. Letter and Sudoku use heuristic process verifiers with no learned parameters, while Dyck, QM9, DNA, and Protein use learned lightweight Transformer verifiers. The learned verifiers are trained by regression to the task-specific targets described in the task descriptions above.

•

3.24

•

F.4
Additional Ablations

The main text focuses on geometric re-masking and verifier amortization. Here we collect additional ablations on shortlisting and the block size.

Shortlisting budget.

We ablate the local shortlisting budget for
MDM-VGB-Momentum
by increasing
L_{f}=L_{b}
while holding the task-specific token budget
K
fixed. On QM9, increasing the shortlist from
1
to
4
substantially improves Pass@95 while also reducing realized search cost, and larger budgets give smaller additional gains. On DNA, Pass@95 peaks around
L_{f}=L_{b}=4
, whereas
L_{f}=L_{b}=8
substantially increases adjusted NFE without improving quality. Thus the gains are not explained by simply evaluating an arbitrarily large local neighborhood; a moderate shortlist is sufficient for the verifier to expose useful moves.

Block size / MDM precision.

We next study how MDM block size affects the finite-budget reward–compute tradeoff for
MDM-VGB-Momentum
. Singleton AOAR updates give the finest local control, while larger MDM blocks can reduce the number of sequential decisions by revealing or re-masking multiple coordinates at once. This creates a precision–parallelism tradeoff: moderate block sizes may amortize verifier and proposal costs, but overly coarse blocks can make the local edit decisions too blunt.
