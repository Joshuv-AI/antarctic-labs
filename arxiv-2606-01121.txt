On Jean-Marie Souriau’s geometric quantization of the relativistic electron
G. de Saxcé

Abstract
The aim of this paper is to revisit, in Souriau’s book ”Structure of Dynamical Systems”, the chapter devoted to the geometric quantization where the justifications of important results and formulae are not given and are difficult to prove. After recalling the coadjoint orbit method and its application to the relativistic particle with spin, we state and prove two keystone theorems that allow to equip its prequantum manifold with the symplectic and contact structures. We apply them to the relativistic particle with spin 1/2, leading to the Dirac equation and the conservation of the probability current. We propose also an identity of conservation of the spin current and, invoking the Kaluza-Klein theory, a systematic construction of the symmetries of charge conjugation, parity transformation and time reversal which seems to us more convenient and readable than that of the classical presentations.



Keywords:
symplectic mechanics, geometric quantization, Dirac equation, Kaluza-Klein theory

1
Introduction

In a seminal work, Jean-Marie Souriau proposed for the classical description of an elementary particle to study a family of homogeneous spaces for a symmetry group, the coadjoint orbits, and to classify them (
Souriau 1970
,
Souriau 1997b
). On this basis, he introduced in
Souriau 1966
the geometric quantization which is a refinement of the representation theory based on the classical-quantum correspondance. The reader not familiar with this topic can consult, in addition to Souriau’s references previously cited,
Kostant 1970
who is with Souriau the other pioneer of this method,
Kijowski 1977
and
Woodhouse 1991
. The pre-quantization step consists in constructing a
\mathsf{U}(1)
-principal bundle of which the base space is a symplectic manifold, a coadjoint orbit of the symmetry group. By classifying coadjoint orbits, one classifies the elementary particles known to physicists, whether elementary such as quarks and leptons or composite such as hadrons. On this basis, he proposed an intrinsic method of quantization that gives a precise sense to the correspondence principle.

As he says in the introduction of ”Structure of Dynamical Systems” (
Souriau 1970
,
Souriau 1997b
), this method ”leaves open many mathematical problems and questions of interpretation. We present this method of quantization above all as a subject of reflection for the researcher”. Our goal in this work is to revisit his quantization of the relativistic particle with spin
\frac{1}{2}
leading to Dirac equation for the electron. The justifications of important results and formulae are not given and are difficult to prove. For example, the proof of (
Souriau 1970
,
Souriau 1997b
, Formula (18.71)) seems requiring the spin group which is not considered in this book. On the other hand, Souriau developped an original theory of spinors with a purely algebraic construction of the spin group in ”Calcul linéaire” (
Souriau 1965
) and in ”Géométrie et Relativité” (
Souriau 1964
). This theory seemed to us also worthy of interest and to be made available to the specialists in an English version. It will be our starting point. Although the two approaches, that of ”Structure of Dynamical Systems” on the one hand and that of ”Calcul linéaire” and ”Géométrie et Relativité” on the other, are consistent each of them, it must be stressed, however, that the Dirac matrices of the former one are biquaternionic (
Breban 2018
) while that of the last one are quaternionic. After presenting the aforementioned spinor theory, we shall developped the electron quantization with quaternionic Dirac matrices for consistency although following the frame of ”Structure of Dynamical Systems”. Another difference with this book is, in the axioms of the prequantum manifold, the re-introduction as in
Souriau 1975
and
Duval 2011
of the Sommerfeld quantization condition, only referred in (
Souriau 1970
,
Souriau 1997b
, (18.6)) but not used and replaced by (18.19). Many tools developped in this paper are not restricted to the relativistic particle with mass and spin but can be useful to study other kinds of particles, for instance the neutrinos (see
Souriau 1970
,
Souriau 1997b
, (19.122) to (19.134)).

This paper is a preliminary work of the author needed to tackle the four dimensional Dirac equation in the footstep of a previous article (
de Saxcé 2025
) where he proposed a new symmetry group and a modified version of Kaluza-Klein theory in which a variational principle allows to find new coupled equations of the gravitation and electromagnetic field. After considering the 5D approach at the Universe scale, the aim will be to zoom in to understand with this new point of view what happens at the quantum scale.

Generalized Hermitian spaces

Let us begin with a brief reminder of definitions and notations used by Souriau and which stray from standard ones. Their interest is to ensure the frame independent, intrinsic feature of the approach, even if calculations are often made in a particular basis.

In this paper, an
Euclidean space
(called by other authors a pseudo-Euclidean space) is a real vector space
\mathcal{T}
of finite dimension

\bm{G}
,

If

is associated one and only one linear form

such that

, called its

and represented by the row

. More generally, if

is a linear map from an Euclidean space

from
\mathcal{T}

\forall\bm{X}\in\mathcal{T},\quad\forall\bm{X}_{0}\in\mathcal{T}_{0},\qquad\bm{G}(\bm{X},\bm{A}\,\bm{X}_{0})=\bm{G}_{0}(\bm{A}^{*}\bm{X},\bm{X}_{0})\

If
\bm{A}
is represented by the matrix
A
in bases of
\mathcal{T}_{0}
and
\mathcal{T}
,

is represented by the matrix

A^{*}=G_{0}^{-1}A^{T}G\ .

(1)

We verify that:

(\bm{A}+\bm{A}^{\prime})^{*}=\bm{A}^{*}+\bm{A}^{\prime*},\qquad(\bm{A}\,\bm{B})^{*}=\bm{B}^{*}\bm{A}^{*},\qquad(\bm{A}^{*})^{*}=\bm{A}

(2)

The linear map is self-adjoint (resp. anti-self-adjoint or skew-adjoint) if

\bm{A}=\bm{A}^{*}\qquad(\mbox{resp.}\quad\bm{A}=-\bm{A}^{*})\ .

If

is a complexification of a

(called ”Euclidean space” in

Souriau 1997b
).
As

adjoint
of

(called ”transpose” by Souriau) as the unique
\mathbb{C}
-linear form
\bm{Z}^{\dagger}
such that

\bm{G}^{\mathbb{C}}(\bm{Z},\bm{Z}^{\prime})=\Re(\bm{Z}^{\dagger}\bm{Z}^{\prime})

(3)

\mathbb{C}
is a Hermitian space for the metric

\bm{G}^{\mathbb{C}}(z,z^{\prime})=\bm{G}^{\mathbb{C}}(x+i\,y,x^{\prime}+i\,y^{\prime})=x\,x^{\prime}+y\,y^{\prime}=\Re(\bar{z}\,z^{\prime})

then, owing to (

of

\bar{z}
. If
\bm{Z}
is represented in a
\mathbb{C}
-basis of
\mathcal{T}^{\mathbb{C}}
by the column
Z
, its adjoint
\bm{Z}^{\dagger}
is represented by the row

Z^{\dagger}=\bar{Z}^{T}G

The adjoint of a
\mathbb{C}
-linear map
\bm{A}
between Hermitian spaces will be denoted
\bm{A}^{\dagger}
. If
\bm{A}
is represented by the matrix
A
in
\mathbb{C}
-bases,
\bm{A}^{\dagger}
is represented by the matrix

A^{\dagger}=G_{0}^{-1}\bar{A}^{T}G

(4)

The rules concerning the adjoint for Hermitian spaces are similar to the corresponding ones for Euclidean spaces, replacing
*
by
{\dagger}
in (
2
).
\Re(\bm{Z}^{\dagger}\bm{Z}^{\prime})
is symmetric with respect to
Z
and
Z^{\prime}
but it is worth to note that

is skew-symmetric with respect to
Z
and
Z^{\prime}

\Im(\bm{Z}^{\dagger}\bm{Z}^{\prime})=-\Im(\overline{\bm{Z}^{\dagger}\bm{Z}^{\prime}})=-\Im((\bm{Z}^{\dagger}\bm{Z}^{\prime})^{\dagger})

\Im(\bm{Z}^{\dagger}\bm{Z}^{\prime})=-\Im(\bm{Z}^{\prime{\dagger}}\bm{Z})

(5)

A linear map
\bm{A}
is Hermitian (resp. anti-Hermitian) if:

\bm{A}=\bm{A}^{\dagger}\qquad(\mbox{resp.}\quad\bm{A}=-\bm{A}^{\dagger})

In particular, if

and the adjoint of the matrix

Spin group

Conclusions

In classical mechanics, an electron is considered as a particle while in quantum mechanics it exhibits particle or wave properties according to the experimental circumstances. In this paper, we followed Souriau’s approach, the merit of which is to propose a new way to deduce the wave equation of the quantum particle from the symplectic representation of the motion of the classical particle. The main developments are algebraic from which the Dirac wave equation is deduced only in the last section. In particular, the construction of the spin group is purely algebraic without resorting to the matrix exponential. This approach sheds light on the link between the velocity of the classical particle and the probability current of the quantum particle.

In Souriau’s geometric quantization of the electron, there are four key formulae:

•

The definition (
41
) of the 4-vector
I

\forall dX\in\mathbb{R}^{4},\qquad I^{*}dX=\psi^{\dagger}\gamma(dX)\,\psi

which turned out to be the 4-velocity
U
of the classical particles and later the model of the probability current of the quantum particle that satisfies a well-known conservation identity.

•

The definition (
47
) of the 4-vector
J

\forall dX\in\mathbb{R}^{4},\qquad J^{*}dX=i\,\psi^{\dagger}\gamma(dX)\,\gamma_{5}\,\psi

which proved to be the 4-spin vector of the classical particle and next a spin current of the quantum particle that satisfies also a conservation identity.

•

The link between the spin part of the symplectic form of the classical particle and the quantum counterpart in terms of spinors in the form of the identity (
64
)

-\Im(d\psi^{\dagger}\delta\psi)=\frac{1}{4}\,Tr(d\Omega\,\Omega\,\delta\Omega)

•

The contact structure defined by the 1-form (
82
)

\alpha_{\mathcal{W}}(d\eta)=-2\,i\,s\,\psi^{\dagger}d\psi-m_{0}\,\psi^{\dagger}\gamma(dX)\,\psi

To throw some light on them, we supported the two definitons on the basis of the decomposition (
8
) of the space
\Gamma
of quaternionic matrices and we proved two main results, Theorems
8.1
and
65
.

To characterize the classical particles, Jean-Marie Souriau proposes in (
Souriau 1970
,
Souriau 1997b
, (13.1)) axioms of mechanics, the first of these being: ”the space of motions of a dynamical system is a connected symplectic manifold”. He does not give explicitly a similar formal definition for the quantum particle but he represents the quantum electron with the set of spinors such that
\psi^{\dagger}\psi=1
, which seems to indicate that he considers the space of a quantum particle is also connected. However Dirac claims that his equation allows to represent two distinct particles, the electron and the positron. In the present formalism, we considered also the set of spinors such that
\psi^{\dagger}\psi=-1
which can be associated to the positron. Therefore, there are two distinct quantum particles in correspondance with a single classical particle but, unlike Dirac, these two particles have the same spin and rest mass (not of opposite sign). It is the sign of the electric charge and not that of the rest mass that distinguishes the positron from the electron. Our opinion is that this ambiguity in Dirac theory on the sign of the rest mass (then of the energy) results from the absence of the charge in the formalism, ambiguity which might be resolved by introducing the Kaluza-Klein fifth dimension of the Universe that allows to identify naturally the charge as an extra invariant of the coadjoint orbit as proposed in
de Saxcé 2025
. It is also worth noting that it is troubling to observe that the space
\Gamma_{+}
of Dirac matrices is of dimension 5 and is only used in a reduced version of dimension 4. All these considerations could be a topic of investigation which we hope to tackle in the future.
