 Executive Summary of CRADA Work:

 This project will focus on diagnostics and quality control understandings of continuously
 produced FFI membranes [both Anion Exchange Membrane (AEM) and Proton Exchange
 Membrane (PEM)] and continuously produced FFI electrodes. Optimization of methods and
 novel quality control techniques will be developed as needed.

 Summary of Research Results:

 Topic 3: Diagnostics and metrics of membranes and MEA production

 Task 3.1- Diagnostics and quality control understandings of continuously produced
 membranes

 FFI will provide continuously produced AEM and PEM membranes to NLR. NLR will apply in-
 line inspection techniques for full-roll characterization, defect detection and classification. NLR
 will test continuously produced membranes, initially utilizing the test methods (e.g., optical
 vision, thermography, hyperspectral imaging) already readily available at the QV laboratory.
 Next, if needed and depending on funding left, NLR will carry out optimization of the methods
 and development of novel QC techniques specifically for AEMs and PEMs materials provided
 by FFI. The main objective of the task is to provide testing methodology and algorithms for
 quality control and consistent production output. (i.e., methods to find pin holes, gelified regions,
 thickness control etc.)

 Membrane specimens received from the partner are listed in the table below.

 Name Short Description
 FFI2409FK28 GRC Membrane
 FFI2409FK24 GRC Membrane
 FFI2405FM12 PFSA mem with GRC

 Initially a high-precision quality control experiment was conducted using NLR's quality control
 web-line to analyze a ~2.5 m long roll of PFSA membrane with GRC. The examination utilized a
 Piranha P3 line camera equipped with 12,000 pixels, achieving a 12-inch field of view. The scan
 was performed in transmission mode at a scanning speed of 5 ft/min, delivering a physical
 resolution of 25 µm and an optical resolution of 63 µm. This setup ensured accurate evaluation of
 the membrane's quality and uniformity. Later on, in the course of the project similar scans were
 also carried out for additional two membrane specimens.

 2

 Figure 1a illustrates the experimental setup used to evaluate the ~2.5 m long roll of PFSA
 membrane with GRC. Figure 1b displays the optical transmittance map of the membrane roll,
 providing detailed visual insights into its consistency and uniformity. Additionally, Figure 1c
 highlights selected point defects identified in the captured image, showcasing the capability of
 the setup to detect imperfections effectively. These results underscore the precision and
 reliability of NLR's quality control web-line methodology.

 Figure 1. a) Experimental setup showing NLR's web-line with membrane roll specimen, b) optical
 transmittance image of the entire roll, c) selected defects identified in the image

 3

 The analyzed image was evaluated for uniformity across multiple length scale levels to ensure a
 comprehensive assessment of the PFSA membrane's quality. The analysis revealed both large-
 scale and small-scale variations within the membrane, demonstrating the system's capability to
 detect inconsistencies at varying magnitudes. These variations were further examined in
 subsequent studies to better understand their nature and implications for the membrane's
 performance and production quality.

 Selected spots on the PFSA membrane roll were analyzed using X-ray fluorescence to measure
 GRC loading and with thickness gauging to evaluate membrane uniformity. The analysis
 concluded that, although the optical transmission web-line scan detected variations in the
 transmission signal, these variations were not significantly driven by differences in thickness or
 Pt loading (see Figure 2). Both thickness and Pt loading were found to be highly uniform
 throughout the roll sample. It was determined that the optically captured variations most likely
 correspond to surface roughness and chemical composition differences, rather than structural
 inconsistencies.

 Figure 2. GRC loading at selected locations as measured using X-ray fluorescent method

 4

 The next experimental steps focused on further investigating the factors influencing optical
 transmission variations in the PFSA membrane. Optical microscopy was employed to study
 samples with differing optical transmission using both bright field and dark field imaging
 techniques, aiming to identify visual patterns or anomalies. Laser confocal microscopy was
 utilized to examine the surface profiles and roughness of high and low transmission samples,
 providing detailed insights into surface characteristics. Additionally, chromatic confocal probes
 were tested for in-line thickness monitoring, with initial results offering promising data for real-
 time thickness analysis and its potential integration into quality control processes. Example
 results from this study are shown in Figure 3.

 Figure 3. Example micrograph (top left), surface topography (top-right) and thickness profile
 (bottom) of a membrane specimen

 Rolls of membranes were analyzed to assess their uniformity and to identify potential defects,
 demonstrating both fast in-line and detailed off-line quality control methods. While a larger set
 of samples had originally been planned for evaluation, the partner ultimately decided to limit the
 scope and stop the analysis after three samples.

 Task 3.2: Diagnostics and quality control understandings of continuously produced
 electrodes

 FFI will provide continuously produced electrodes to NLR. NLR will apply in-line inspection
 techniques for full-roll characterization, defect detection and classification. NLR will test
 continuously produced electrodes, initially utilizing the test methods (e.g., optical vision,
 thermography) already readily available at the QC laboratory. Next, if needed and depending on
 funding left, NLR will carry out optimization of the methods and development of novel QV
 techniques specifically for electrode materials provided by FFI. The main objective of the task is
 to provide testing methodology and algorithms for quality control and consistent production
 output. (i.e., thickness, porosity, catalyst/ionomer loading, catalyst/ionomer distribution etc.)

 24 months after receipt of funds or 9 months after receipt of additional materials whichever

 additional samples (for membranes investigating pin holes, gelified regions, and/or

 The test report on each QC sample roll will include (but not limited to) digital data of

 A series of experiments were conducted on roll specimens provided by the partner, including
 web-line optical scanning, chromatic confocal analysis, metallurgical cross-sectioning, optical
 microscopy, and X-ray fluorescence.

 Initially, four electrode samples (three cathodes and one anode) were scanned at a speed of 5
 ft/min. Due to the high opacity of the samples, no transmission signal could be obtained using
 the standard optical setup with a linear camera and LED light source; only voids and pinholes in
 the coating were visible. To address this issue, a modified transmission setup was implemented,
 utilizing a higher-sensitivity camera and a significantly brighter flashlight, capturing images of
 the samples at 2-inch intervals. One experiment was performed using the flashlight setup, while
 two additional experiments were conducted using the line camera setup.

 Transmittance images of all tested specimens were generated and analyzed at both the micro and
 millimeter length scales. For the high-loading cathode, no signal was detected from the coating
 except in the void areas (Figure 4). A measurable transmittance signal was observed for another
 cathode specimen in the 0.2-loaded segment, with signal saturation in the 0.17-loaded segment
 (Figure 5). These results suggest potential sensitivity to variations in lower loading levels.
 Analysis of the anode results revealed noticeably more void spots, pinholes, and larger features
 on the macro scale compared to the cathode rolls.

 The thickness distribution of the coating in the motion direction was measured using chromatic
 confocal probes. Significant thickness variations were observed for Pt loadings of 0.20, 0.25, and
 0.30 mg/cm². From a thickness perspective, no distinction was found between loading levels of
 0.17 and 0.20 mg/cm². This novel method demonstrates potential for in-line coating thickness
 monitoring, a capability not previously available. Currently, destructive cross-sectioning
 followed by microscopy is required to obtain coating thickness measurements.

 Figure 4 Cathode coating optical transmittance response for high loading cathode at macro (top)
 and mm (bottom) length scales

 7

 Figure 5. Optical transmittance response of another cathode roll where Pt loading changes from
 0.3 to 0.17 mg/cm2 in four discrete steps as captured using the high sensitivity imaging system.

 An X-ray fluorescence mapping experiment was conducted to analyze the catalyst loading
 distribution of the anode specimen. Two maps, covering a total length of approximately 0.4
 meters, were captured in the area where larger optical transmittance variations were previously
 observed. It was found that regions with high to low transmittance corresponded to changes in
 loading from 1.3 to 1.6 mg/cm². The results indicate that reductions in anode electrode loading
 can be successfully detected using the demonstrated high-sensitivity imaging system.

 Although more extensive study on larger number of samples and with larger set of potential in-
 line quality control techniques was originally planned, the work has stopped per request of the
 partner.
