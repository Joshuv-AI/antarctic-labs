3. Executive Summary:
Motivation, Importance, and Goals
 This project is motivated by the need to develop a materials engineering solution to
reduce solar module degradation caused by cell cracks. The cell-crack-induced power
loss is a long-term degradation mechanism and one of the main causes of solar panel
field failures. Cell cracks can occur during module fabrication, transportation, installation,
and long-term operation due to thermomechanical stressors. Our team’s internal
estimation – based on the national weather pattern, frequency of severe weather, and 39-
GW asset survey by Heliolytics – reveals that the cell-crack-induced module degradation
translates to lost revenues >$17B over an average 5-year period for solar farm owners
and to reduced reliability (<25 years panel lifetime) for solar energy consumers. In
response to this challenge, the prime recipient offers a metal matrix composite (MMC)
silver paste that is tailor-engineered for screen-printing gridlines and busbars, which serve
as the electrical contacts on solar cells. We formulate our MMC paste by adding low-cost
(∼0.02¢/WDC for research grade), surface-engineered carbon nanotubes to commercially
available silver paste. The MMC paste offers a drop-in, cost-effective solution to cell
cracks for solar cell and module manufacturers. The main goal of this project was to
conduct field-relevant, module-level analysis and qualification of MMC metallization.
Major Accomplishments
 In comparison to standard commercial metallization, the prime recipient has
accomplished the following with MMC metallization:
 Enhanced fracture toughness by 9×, increased ductility by 7×, and electrical bridging
 of cell cracks ≥ 40 µm wide
 Increased mini-module lifetime against cyclic three-point-bending load test by >1.5×
 Reduced cell cracks in full-sized solar modules and reduced module degradation rate
 upon IEC 61215-2:2021 stress testing: mechanical load test (-0.25%) and humidity
 freeze test (-2.39%) compared to standard commercial metallization (-1.70 % and -
 3.53% respectively)
 Increased cell efficiency by ≥ 0.02% from 21.62% to 21.64% statistically averaged
 over ∼1000 cells with 83 µm gridline width
 Increased cell efficiency by ≥ 0.03% from 22.29 to 23.35% statistically averaged over
 60 cells with 40 µm gridline width
 Technoeconomic analysis showing a 2.5% increase in net present value (thus
 “bankability”) of utility scale PV farms
 Completion of bankability study by Black & Veatch
 Engagement with Solamet, Heraeus, and Trina.
 In summary, we have effectively demonstrated the viability of MetZilla MMC paste
through a comprehensive set of tests and validation, ranging from materials-level testing
and optimization to mini-module accelerated stress testing, to full-sized module
accelerated stress testing in accordance with IEC standards. The MMC metallization is
expected to prolong the lifetime of solar modules by reducing cell crack formation and by
electrically bridging the cell cracks when cell cracks eventually occur. These benefits
translate to increased revenue, increased net present value, and reduced insurance
premium for long-term field owners; reduced financial exposure for insurance companies;
and reduced price of electricity for consumers.

5. Background:
 While multi-busbar technology and glass/glass module designs have reduced the risk
of cell cracks, the industry's shift toward thinner silicon substrates and increasingly thinner
glass (from 4 mm to 3.2 mm to 2 mm) in large-format modules continue to make cell
cracking a leading contributor to module degradation.1 Meanwhile, cell architecture is
evolving rapidly from Tunnel Oxide Passivated Contact (TOPCon) and Silicon
Heterojunction (SHJ) to hybrid-format TOPCon-Interdigitated Back Contact (IBC), and
much of the metallization community’s attention is currently centered on Laser Enhanced
Contact Optimization (LECO) technology.2 Despite concerns over long-term reliability due
to copper diffusion in silicon, rising silver cost and its instability are also accelerating
interest in copper-based metallization.3 In recognition of these industry trends, and though
outside the original scope of this project, the primary recipient has formulated MMC
recipes compatible with TOPCon front and rear contacts, as well as copper and aluminum
pastes. However, none of the current metallization approaches directly address cell
cracking through a cost-effective, plug-and-play solution that integrates seamlessly into
standard manufacturing. Given this landscape, competition for the prime recipient’s
MetZilla MMC technology remains limited, and no widely adopted, process-compatible
solution to mitigate crack-induced degradation has emerged. While several companies
have explored alternative strategies, none offer a viable drop-in replacement for existing
solar cell manufacturing lines. Multiwire interconnects may present an alternative to
MMC-enhanced metallization, but the long-term crack resilience of both technologies still
requires validation through statistically significant testing. The prime recipient’s
technology is specifically designed as a simple, cost-effective, drop-in solution to address
crack-related degradation.

6. Project Objectives †: 0F

 The prime recipient will conduct field-relevant, module-level analysis and
qualification of advanced composite metallization. The composite metallization is
expected to prolong the lifetime of modules by electrically bridging the cell cracks that
appear during module fabrication, transportation, installation, and operation due to
thermo-mechanical stress and weather elements. The objectives were to (1) continuously
improve the composite paste formulation along with screen-printing and firing steps to
match the beginning-of-life performance of standard passivated-emitter and rear-contact
(PERC) cells; (2) establish cell and module integration schemes for the advanced
metallization, (3) compare the level of crack formation in the modules constructed from
conventional metallization vs. advanced metallization, as the modules go through
accelerated quality assurance and field tests beyond the requirements for certification,
and (4) implement a non-destructive characterization technique to visualize and measure
the distribution of cracks and their widths appearing in the cells within a full-size module.
During the award, the prime recipient will engage with strategic partners, downstream
customers (i.e., module manufacturers), and investors.
6.1. Technical Scope Summary
 During Budget Period 1, the prime recipient will focus on (1) materials engineering
for metal matrix composite (MMC) silver paste formulation, using high aspect-ratio
multiwalled carbon nanotubes, (2) materials characterization to demonstrate desired
fracture strength/toughness, electrical gap-bridging, and self-healing from the MMC
paste, (3) fabricating passivated-emitter and rear-contact (PERC) cells with standard
metallization to establish baseline cell performance and with MMC metallization to
demonstrate beginning-of-life performance matching that of standard metallization,
(4) demonstration of Digital Image Correlation (DIC) technique at the mini-module level,
and (5) fabrication of a set of 60-cell, full-sized modules with standard metallization and
another set with MMC metallization for comparison.
 During Budget Period 2, the prime recipient will focus on (1) qualification of full-sized
modules before commencing certification and extended stress tests, (2) full-sized module
certification and extended stress tests to demonstrate increased fracture toughness of
MMC metallization, (3) demonstration of DIC technique at the full-sized module level to
characterize cell cracks, and (4) commencing field testing of full-sized modules that
underwent module certification and extended stress testing.
6.2. Tasks to Be Performed
Budget Period 1
Task 1.0: Characterization of Metal Matrix Composites
Task Summary: Within this Task, the prime recipient will determine to what degree the
weight percentage (wt%) of multiwalled carbon nanotubes (MW-CNTs) in the composite
silver paste enhances mechanical and electromechanical properties of printed and fired
gridlines against cell and gridline fracture. The prime recipient will also examine the
impact of CNT wt% on 2 inch x 2 inch small cell performance against cell and gridline
fracture.

Subtask 1.1: Screen Printing Characterization
Subtask Summary: The objective is to determine the impact of CNT wt% on viscosity,
edge definition, and adhesion strength of printed gridlines.
Subtask 1.2: Mechanical and Electromechanical Characterization
Subtask Summary: The prime recipient will use dynamic mechanical analysis (DMA) to
measure elastic modulus and modulus of toughness of free-standing composite gridlines
after firing. The objective is to gauge the increased fracture toughness of the MMC
gridlines. The maximum electrically bridgeable gap, the number of self-healing cycles,
and the current density that can be maintained through the fractured but electrically
bridged gridlines will be measured by Resistance Across Cleaves and cracKs (RACK)
testing. The RACK is an automated strain test setup in which resistance along the
gridlines is measured to monitor the electrical continuity while the gridlines are strained
to failure. The expectation is that the CNT wt% that yields the maximum bridgeable gap
and self-healing cycles would also maximize the modulus of toughness.
Subtask 1.3: Cell Characterization
Subtask Summary: The objective is to determine how CNT wt% impacts the 2 inch x 2
inch small cell performance, such as open circuit voltage (Voc), short circuit current (Jsc),
fill factor (FF), and efficiency (η), before and after the cracks form. The expectation is that
a proper CNT incorporation will maintain > 90% of the beginning-of-life cell performance
against cracks.
Task 2.0: Increased Throughput of Functionalized MW-CNTs
Task Summary: Within this Task, the prime recipient will scale up the functionalized MW-
CNT production to meet the immediate demand of fabricating >30 full-sized modules with
consistent material quality (i.e., solubility in diluent and CNT length distribution) as well
as the future product demand, while minimizing the production cost by reducing the
concentration of acid reagents and time required to fully functionalize the CNTs. The
prime recipient will make use of filter-dryer technique to reduce the time to isolate
functionalized CNTs from the acid reagents and dry them. This process engineering will
further reduce the CNT materials cost (well below $2.69 per module), while substantially
increasing the throughput of functionalized MW-CNT production to kg-level.
Task 3.0: Fabrication of PERC Cells
Task Summary: The awardee will fabricate a set of 6 inch x 6 inch, 5-busbar (5-BB),
passivated-emitter and rear-contact (PERC) cells (> 1000 cells) with standard
metallization and another set of PERC cells (> 1000 cells) with metal matrix composite
(MMC) metallization and will demonstrate that the MMC cells show beginning-of-life
performance matching that of standard PERC cells.
Subtask 3.1: Establish printing and firing conditions to produce standard PERC cells
whose efficiency is consistently > 21% with a standard deviation < 1%, randomly sampled
over 50 cells from a batch of >1000 cells.
Subtask Summary: The objective is to ensure that the pilot cell manufacturing line can
achieve volume production of commercial-level efficiency cells with a high level of
repeatability.
Subtask 3.2: Establish printing and firing conditions to produce MMC PERC cells whose
efficiency is consistently > 21% with a standard deviation < 1%, randomly sampled over
50 cells from a batch of >1000 cells. Electroluminescence (EL) and other optical/electrical

techniques will be used to enhance understanding of the silver-silicon (Ag/Si) interface
and to facilitate a stabilized process that would be needed for the MMC paste.
Subtask Summary: The objective is to ensure that the pilot cell manufacturing line can
achieve volume production of commercial-level-efficiency MMC cells with a high level of
repeatability. This objective entails printing narrow gridlines with the MMC paste and
establishing the optimum firing temperature to fabricate 5-busbar (5-BB) PERC cells. In
addition to establishing temperature profiles, EL will be used to make sure the series
resistance is uniform on the cells. A proper study will also be carried out to interrogate the
Ag/Si interface, using scanning electron microscopy (SEM), transmission electron
microscopy (TEM), conductive atomic force microscopy (c-AFM), and Raman
spectroscopy. The purpose is to understand the nature of contact with the MMC paste.
Task 4.0: Fabrication of Full-Size Modules
Task Summary: Within this Task, the awardee will fabricate a set of at least 17 sixty-cell
PERC modules with standard metallization and a set of at least 17 sixty-cell PERC
modules with MMC metallization to demonstrate that the beginning-of-life performance of
MMC modules matches that of standard modules. For the Digital Image Correlation (DIC)
technique, the awardee will also fabricate at least 2 full-sized, reference-dotted, sixty-cell
PERC modules with standard metallization and at least 2 full-sized, reference-dotted,
sixty-cell PERC modules with MMC metallization.
Subtask 4.1: Integration testing for fabrication of mini and full-size modules
Subtask Summary: The objective is to determine soldering conditions which provide
peel strength equivalent or better than control cells (typically >1N/mm for PERC) that can
be used for fabrication of 10-12 cell strings. Mini-modules with 2 ×1 cells will be fabricated
and tested through thermal shock and HAST for comparison of performance change with
thermo-mechanical and high temperature/humidity stresses prior to scaling of the process
to full-size modules.
Task 5.0: Mini-Module Demonstration of Digital Image Correlation (DIC) Technique
Task Summary: Within this Task, the awardee will explore the possibility of utilizing the
DIC technique at the mini-module level before testing it on full-sized modules.
Subtask 5.1: Establish equipment and materials requirements for the DIC technique
Subtask Summary: The objective is to select camera equipment and IR-transparent
paint for the DIC technique and to calculate the density and size of dots (used as position
references) to match the minimum displacement sensitivity requirements of DIC
Subtask 5.2: Test DIC analysis on mini-modules
Subtask Summary: The objective is to demonstrate at the mini-module level that the DIC
technique can be used to non-destructively analyze cell crack characteristics (i.e., crack
location and orientation) and their width distribution.
Task 6.0: Development of Cost Model
Task Summary: The prime recipient will develop a cost model validated by a 3rd party,
showing both the total cost and the marginal cost for MMC paste. The goal is to show
market acceptance by module manufacturers and their interest in this technology at the
cost target developed under this award.

Budget Period 2
Task 8.0: Fabrication of full-size modules for certification and field testing
Task Summary: The objective is to produce full-size modules qualified and ready for
certification and extended stress testing with identical bill of materials (BOM) except for
the paste. At least six modules with standard metallization and at least six modules with
MMC metallization will be tested for median module power under standard test conditions.
The purpose is threefold: (1) to assess whether full-size MMC modules can be fabricated
using commercial module fabrication equipment, (2) to demonstrate that the beginning-
of-life performance of MMC modules matches that of standard modules, and (3) to qualify
that the full-size modules are ready for certification and rigorous stress testing.
Task 9.0: Full-Size Module Testing
Task Summary: Within this Task, the awardee will establish the baseline performance
of standard modules and compare it with MMC modules as both types of modules
undergo module certification procedures as well as a rigorous quality assurance test (a
customized version CSA/ANSI C450 extended reliability test protocol, which is a
consolidation of existing private and IEC test protocols). This test is beyond the
requirements for module certification with environmental stresses similar to 25 years of
field exposure. The test includes a protocol where static load testing is followed by
dynamic load testing to create cell cracks and induce large areas of electrically isolated
regions. The purpose is to clearly establish the improved crack-tolerance of MMC
modules over that of standard modules.
Task 10.0: Cell Crack Analysis of Full-Sized Modules by Digital Image Correlation (DIC)
Task Summary: Within this Task, the awardee will conduct experiments aimed at non-
destructively analyzing cell cracks at the full-sized module level. The DIC technique will
be used to categorize the nature of cells cracks (i.e., location and orientation) and their
width distribution. The expectation is that both standard and MMC modules would show
very similar cell crack characteristics, but the MMC modules would show far less
electrically isolated “dark” areas under EL imaging after module certification and rigorous
stress test.
Task 11.0: Development of Cost Model
Task Summary: The prime recipient will develop a cost model validated by a 3rd party,
showing both the total cost and the marginal cost for MMC paste. The goal is to show
market acceptance by module manufacturers and their interest in this technology at the
cost target developed under this award.
Task 12.0: Stakeholder Engagement
Task Summary: The prime recipient will identify and engage with relevant stakeholders
(key independent engineering firms, financing firms, module manufacturers, and paste
developers) to collect feedback on the technology being developed and its business /
market acceptance.

7. Project Results and Discussion:
      During the 05/01/2020 – 06/30/2022 period, we met Milestones 1.1, 1.2, 1.3, 1.4,
1.5, 1.6, 1.7, 1.8, and 1.9 (Tasks 1 to 7). With our metal matrix composite (MMC) paste,
we have demonstrated electrical gap bridging of cell cracks that are ∼65-µm-wide on the
average upon initial fracture and “self-healing” capability to bridge ∼30 µm gaps after a
series of open- and closed-gap cycles. The modulus of toughness for MMC (i.e., area
under strain vs. stress curve and a measure of fracture toughness) is greater than that of
standard paste by a factor of 9. The cost of carbon nanotubes (CNTs) incorporated into
metallization to achieve these materials characteristics is $0.0098/WDC in comparison to
$0.0084/WDC for standard silver paste, $0.0014/WDC difference. – This difference has
been further reduced in 2025 to $0.0002/WDC with reduction in CNT bulk pricing. – The
additional materials cost in PV module construction is virtually negligible. The
incorporation of CNTs has no impact on beginning-of-life cell and module performance.
The performance parameters of full-sized (245.9 cm2) Passivated Emitter and Rear
Contact (PERC) cells with MMC metallization (statistically averaged over 87 cells) match
those of PERC cells with standard metallization (statistically averaged over 66 cells). The
efficiency of MMC-enhanced cells registers at 22.31 ± 0.2%, slightly exceeding the
efficiency of baseline cells that registers at 22.28 ± 0.2%. The cell efficiency distribution
shows this slight advantage for MMC paste. We have also demonstrated Digital Image
Correlation (DIC) technique at the full-sized module level. The DIC technique will be used
during Budget Period 2 to characterize the critical cell crack width beyond which the
electrical continuity would be lost, leading to module power degradation.

      During the 05/01/2020 – 06/30/2022 period, we also met all Go-No-Go (GNG)
Milestones 1A, 1B, 1C, and 1D. The beginning-of-life performance of MMC-enhanced
mini-modules matches that of standard baseline paste, tracking the cell-level observation
(Milestones 1.5 and 1.6). After Temperature Cycling (TC, 200 cycles of -40 to +85 °C
temperature excursion) and Highly Accelerated Stress Testing (HAST, 100 hours in damp
heat 120 °C/100% relative humidity), the degradation in maximum power (Pmax) of MMC-
enhanced mini-modules matches that of standard baseline mini-modules. The
degradation rates for MMC-enhanced and standard-paste mini-modules are both much
less than 5%. We have engaged more than 70 stakeholders (e.g., independent engineers,
financiers, paste manufacturers, cell manufacturers, and module manufacturers) and
secured altogether 5 letters of support for our MMC technology. We have been closely
engaging Solamet (formerly DuPont) and Heraeus, two main silver paste manufacturers
as well as Silfab, one of very few North American module manufacturers. We have also
established our cost model (courtesy of Dr. Michael Woodhouse at National Renewable
Energy Laboratory), where the addition of CNTs would cost only $0.0014/W ($0.0002/W
in 2025 with bulk CNT pricing) over the standard silver paste module construction, while
adding $2.5M in Net Present Value (NPV) to a $100M PV project.
 e.g., $2.5M for a 100-MWdc PV power plant investment (roughly $100M). This is a
significant increase at the cost of adding only $0.0002/WDC (i.e., 10¢ for a 500W PV
panel).

 During the 07/01/2022 – 09/30/2022 period, we conducted mini-module stress
testing that consisted of Temperature Stress 200 (TS200, 200 cycles of -70 to 120 °C
temperature excursion to open up cell cracks) and Highly Accelerated Stress Testing
(HAST100, 100 hours in 85% relative humidity at 120 °C). The purpose was first (1) to
ensure that mini-modules constructed with MMC metallization show equal or better
beginning-of-life performance than mini-modules constructed with standard commercial
metallization, and second (2) to demonstrate that both modules degrade less than 5% in
Pmax after the two stress tests to meet typical degradation standards for commercial
modules. TS200 typically catches early failures, such as ribbon solder failure. HAST100
typically catches metal corrosion. The details of mini-module stress testing results are
included and discussed in the RPPR-1 report for the 4th quarter of 2022. Here, we share
the summary of our key findings.

may indicate an early sign of metal corrosion in both standard metallization and MMC

paste manufacturers to long-term field owners and insurance companies. To support this
pricing effort, we recently hired a marketing firm to develop a thoughtful pricing structure
and guide our market entry strategy.
