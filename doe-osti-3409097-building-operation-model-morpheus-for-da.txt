 Provide support to Dallas Fort Worth Airport (DFW) by utilizing Morpheus to optimize DFW
 building design and operations.

 CRADA benefit to DOE, Participant, and US Taxpayer:

 Summary of Research Results:

 The primary objective of this project was to leverage digital twin technology to enhance the
 design and operation of DFW Airport terminals and their associated energy systems. To achieve
 this, Morpheus, a building digital twin, was developed to guide improvements in airport
 operations, specifically targeting reductions in peak power demand and overall energy
 consumption.

 As illustrated in Figure 1, Morpheus integrates a commercial digital twin platform with a grey-
 box predictive energy model developed by NLR. This combination enhances the standard digital
 twin by incorporating automatic fault detection and diagnostics (AFDD) and advanced control
 capabilities.

 The effectiveness of the Morpheus tool was successfully demonstrated in two pilot areas:
 Terminal D and Energy Plaza. In Terminal D, the system was used to automatically detect
 operational faults in the pilot air handling units. At Energy Plaza, an advanced control strategy
 for optimizing the central plant was tested in both simulation and a live field trial, demonstrating
 potential cost savings of up to 10%. Furthermore, the project identified additional energy-saving
 measure, which has already yielded considerable savings for DFW following its implementation.

 Below is a summary of the tasks outlined in the Statement of Work and the results that were
 generated.

 Task 1: Project Scoping

 The project will begin with a set of video conferences with the DFW to discuss project scope and
 the details of the project plan with various airport stakeholders. It should be noted that
 efficiencies and travel restrictions may require elements of the work to be performed remotely. 6
 NLR will work with DFW to plan for potential remote engagements, when required. Stakeholder
 engagement will be critical to the success of the project. Following these meetings, NLR will
 refine the project plan to address any concerns raised in feedback from DFW staff. Once the
 project plan is confirmed, a data and information gathering process will commence to meet the
 requirements of Tasks 1.2 and 1.3. The data and information include existing and/or future
 central utility plant operational characteristics, other onsite power generation operational
 characteristics, facility drawings, Building Automation System (BAS) characterization, baseline
 energy data, potential output from separately funded energy optimization studies
 (REopt/Resilience), vehicle electrification efforts, ground power efforts, and maintenance logs,
 among others.

 Task 1 Results: Following thorough discussions with representatives from DFW Airport and a
 review of previous energy efficiency projects, the project scope was defined to focus on two
 primary goals:

 • Evaluate the value propositions of a commercial off-the-shelf digital twin (COTS-DT) to
 improve critical airport operations, including work order tracking, asset management,
 predictive maintenance, and energy efficiency.
 • Develop a tool, hereafter named Morpheus, that leverages the COTS DT to enhance the
 performance of the airport’s HVAC system. As shown in Figure 1, the Morpheus tool,
 developed in Python, integrates data from the building automation system and the Athena
 digital twin (for flight-related information) to provide two core functionalities:

 o Automatic Fault Detection and Diagnostic (AFDD) for pilot air handling units
 (AHUs) in Terminal D
 o Advanced control for the central plant at the Energy Plaza.

 ̶ The Energy Plaza hosts the central plant that support the heating and
 cooling demand of the terminals and other facilities at DFW with a
 combined area of 6.6 million sq.ft. The central plant has an annual energy
 consumption of 77 million kWh and 400,000 MMBtu. A six million-
 gallon 90,000 ton-hr thermal storage tank works in parallel with six 5,500
 ton centrifugal chillers and provides load flexibility during peak demand
 hours.

 Task 1.2: Pilot Install of Indoor Mobile Wayfinding, Asset and Work Order Management
 Digital Twin aspect of Morpheus

 Improved smart building management, including wayfinding, asset management, and work order
 management, is a key element of COTS DT value proposition. Rather than developing a new
 digital twin product, NLR will leverage an existing commercial product designed specifically for
 this purpose, Esri’s ArcGIS Indoors, Willow or similar COTS DT and build predictive
 algorithms focused upon desired outcomes of the project. The first step in demonstrating the
 value of digital twins for airports, then, is to install the COTS DT at DFW and quantify cost
 savings through improved wayfinding, asset management, operations, and maintenance. NLR
 will develop a set of criteria that define desirable functional areas in DFW to install COTS DT.
 NLR will then work with partners from DFW to identify representative portion of the airport that
 best meets those priorities and sensitivities. Concurrently NLR will acquire from DFW data
 needed to define a pre-digital twin O&M baseline and also the Autodesk Revit BIM model
 corresponding to the portion of DFW selected for the pilot. It is assumed that an existing Revit
 model for the facility areas proposed can be provided by DFW and that the COTS DT will be
 available temporarily as a pilot for free or provided by DFW to NLR’s specifications. Initial
 discussions with at least one vendor indicate this approach is feasible. The Revit model will be a
 key input for the COTS DT visualization. The COTS DT vendor and NLR will help DFW install
 and configure the COTS DT and collect both quantitative data on cost impacts and qualitative
 reporting about DFW’s experiences using the new software and lessons learned.

 4

 In a high-stress environment such as an airport, effective wayfinding systems contribute to a
 sense of well-being, safety, and security.3 Indoor mobile wayfinding will help passengers
 navigate to the right gate on time, minimizing the risk of missing/delaying flights which will cost
 both the airlines and the airport. Indoor mobile wayfinding also provides a seamless transition to
 local transport services. Through this effort, NLR proposes to extract other indirect benefits from
 this indoor mobile wayfinding capability, where the resulting detailed knowledge about the flow
 of travelers through the airport will allow optimization of both ventilation rates and the
 charging/discharging of thermal and battery energy storage. Key scope would include interfacing
 ArcGIS Indoor with airport BAS systems, with NLR supporting predictive algorithm
 development. For installation, DFW’s preferred controls vendor would be on the front lines, with
 NLR providing a support role.

 Task 1.2 Results: As outlined in the original proposal, NLR developed a set of criteria to
 compare COTS DT platforms and conducted a landscaping study based on this framework.

 This study provided valuable input for DFW’s COTS DT procurement and decision-making
 process. Furthermore, NLR assisted in commissioning the selected platform, focusing on areas
 directly related to the pilot sites selected for this study. Specific tasks included:

 • Verification of proper representation of the pilot AHUs and central plant in the Building
 Information Model (BIM) of the COTS DT, and
 • Verification of proper building control points mapping from the Building Management
 System (BMS) to the COTS DT

 The procured COTS DT platform lacks mobile wayfinding functionality; therefore, work on this
 component was excluded from the project scope.

 Task 1.3: Develop and Install Grey-Box Aspect of Morpheus – Controls Mockup

 Often for complex, very large buildings or campuses like DFW, owners require a controls
 mockup in their Architecture, Engineering and Construction Request for Proposals, with specific
 evaluation criteria. This step reduces the risk of expensive and disruptive building controls
 malfunctions and controls commissioning delays when facilities are first occupied.

 In this case the Morpheus algorithms and control scenarios will be developed and a working
 mockup will be deployed at NLR's Energy Systems Integration Facility (ESIF) as a key step to
 validate potential benefits for DFW’s multibillion-dollar facility.

 5

 Building controls visualization, optimization capabilities, and automated fault detection and
 diagnostics (AFDD) envisioned for this project are novel additions to the value proposition of
 developing and maintaining digital twins. There are several key work efforts required to develop
 and deploy this additional capability. The first order of business will be for NLR to configure the
 grey-box control model and calibrate it with data from DFW. Assuming terminal facilities are
 highly efficient, primary drivers of loads, and therefore power consumption, are occupancy,
 processes, and external loads, such as Electrical Ground Support Equipment (EGSE), vehicles.
 Athena will provide key occupancy data through information exchange to Morpheus, which will
 then influence HVAC control algorithm actions and other desired outputs. The other important
 inputs for calibrating the grey-box model will come from DFW’s existing Energy Management
 & Information System (EMIS) and BAS. A preliminary step will be for NLR to work with DFW
 to characterize all of the available measurement points, to make sure they are adequate for the
 grey-box calibration and provide recommendations for infrastructure retrofits during normal
 capital planning. Following the grey-box model configuration and calibration, the next step is to
 develop optimal model-predictive HVAC control algorithms using Athena’s highly granular data
 on flow of people through the airport, and including AFDD algorithms to identify
 malfunctioning equipment and controls. Operations and maintenance data from COTS DT will
 be integrated with Morpheus’ grey-box model to understand the energy and performance impacts
 of faulty equipment operation, savings from AFDD, and to verify that repairs to the system or
 energy-saving retrofits are performing as expected.

 The HVAC control algorithms developed for Morpheus will be tested and de-risked via a
 controls-mockup at NLR’s ESIF. Building upon previous efforts, executing a successful mockup
 will involve several subtasks, including: developing a design document for integrating
 Morpheus’ algorithms into DFW’s preferred BAS; writing and supporting a detailed example
 RFP for a controls vendor; and summarizing the results of the NLR ESIF experiments. The
 ultimate goal of the testing will be to report on relevant use cases and scenarios for implementing
 Morpheus for HVAC and Load optimization with emphasis on potential value it could deliver,
 based on the controls-mockup experiments. One key advantage of the controls-mockup is that
 virtual thermal and electrical storage resources can be incorporated into the grey-box controls
 model, allowing NLR to explore the value of energy storage for DFW before having to install
 any hardware. Even better, NLR could use the existing energy storage infrastructure in their
 Commercial Buildings Research Infrastructure (CBRI) or partner with battery and/or thermal
 energy storage providers to bring storage hardware into the lab to do hardware-in-the-loop
 testing that incorporates not only the BAS but also real physical storage assets, for a more
 comprehensive and realistic evaluation.

 6

 NLR’s CBRI in the ESIF is proposed to be utilized to implement the system mock-up. This
 unique facility is a nationally recognized asset that makes it easier and less expensive to
 construct comprehensive controls mockups. The facility, shown in Figure 2 as well as Figures 5
 and 6, consists of the following:

 • Electrical infrastructure for 3 buildings
 • Integrated data acquisition system, which collects data, controls equipment, and delivers
 real-time visualization
 • Connections to grid and PV simulators and other DERs for grid interactivity research
 • Communication/control link to HVAC Lab enables hardware-in-the-loop testing of
 HVAC equipment
 • Thermal storage systems
 • BAS Testbed
 • Connection to building energy simulations platform
 • Integration with other ESIF labs and resources, including smart, fast electric vehicle
 chargers (i.e., with up to 350kW capacity, which could fully charge a 300-mile range
 electric car within 5 minutes)

 Task 1.3 Results: In addition to leveraging the COTS DT for its native features, such as asset
 and work order management, Morpheus extended its application to improve the airport's HVAC
 system by integrating automatic fault detection and advanced controls.

 Development of AFDD tool

 It is estimated that 5%–30% of the energy used in commercial buildings is wasted due to faults
 in the operation of the control system (Granderson et al. 2017). AFDD tools play a big role in
 reducing energy losses, while the timely detection of faults also prolongs equipment service life
 and avoids possible service disruptions.

 Developed in Python, the AFDD tool integrates with the COTS DT platform via an Application
 Programming Interface (API). This connection enables live interaction and helps to capture both
 mechanical and control faults. Six AHUs were selected for the demonstration, and their available
 control data were used to populate a fault library. The platform is designed to be extensible,
 allowing for the future inclusion of new fault rules and additional equipment.

 Every 15 minutes, the AFDD tool requests for new data from the COTS-DT, run each fault rule
 in the library for possible faults, and report the fault status back to the COTS-DT through API.
 The COTS-DT assigns a unique ID to each fault instances, which is then used to access and
 update fault status (active to inactive and vice versa). Figure 3 shows an instance in the COTS-
 DT insight notification window. The window provides users with important information about
 active faults, such as fault name, fault type, priority, status, equipment name, and short
 description about the fault.

 Figure 3. Sample of insights displayed in the Willow DT platform

 Development of advanced control for the central plant

 The Central plant is responsible for the heating and cooling of all terminals and other supporting
 facilities. The plant also precools the glycol solutions that are used in the pre-conditioned air
 (PCA) chillers, which provide cooling for the PCA systems at various terminal gates.

 This study demonstrated the advantage of a model predictive control (MPC) over the existing
 rule-based control for the central plant's cooling system. Studies indicated that MPC could
 provide a cost reduction of up to 32% for a campus system with Thermal Storage Tank (TES)
 (Campos et.al 2021). The MPC predicts optimal control outputs (chillers on/off and secondary
 pumps flow) in a moving horizon fashion by using grey-box models to predict system
 performance and disturbances. MPCs can be optimized for various target parameters, including
 energy and cost. This study used the reduction of electricity cost as the basis for optimization.
 The total electricity cost includes a time-of-use electricity consumption charge and a peak
 demand charge. The MPC workflow is shown in 4.

 In order to develop models for the major components of the cooling system, including chillers,
 cooling towers, pumps, and chilled water thermal energy storage, one year of historical data was
 used. The performance of the MPC was first estimated using a simulation in Python. The
 simulation methodology that was developed in Python used historical operational data, including
 cooling load and utility tariff information, to simulate how the system would perform with an
 MPC system in place. The results of the simulation indicated an average cost saving of 10%
 during the summer months. More details on this approach can be found in a journal article
 published as part of this project (Venkatesh et al.)

 Task 2.1: Phase II – Pilot Install of Morpheus – subject to remaining funds

 As stated earlier, the Morpheus digital twin for DFW operations and planning aims to:

 • Combine historical data streams and simulation-based analysis capability across
 buildings, mobility, passengers, energy supply, onsite generation, and onsite electrical
 and thermal storage
 • Enable scenario-based analysis and elucidation of the impact of GEB services on
 reliability, resilience, and costs at scale.

 Following rigorous mockup controls testing of Morpheus at NLR to understand its capabilities
 and improve its implementation, DFW could choose to install Morpheus onsite in the part of the
 airport selected for the ArcGIS Indoors pilot. Ideally, the new controls algorithms would be
 accompanied by installation of battery and/or thermal energy storage in order for Morpheus to
 demonstrate the broadest possible range of services. Note that the existing DFW central plant
 provides chilled water and it is envisioned that the existing and new plant will have additional
 thermal energy storage. A phased approach might also make sense, starting simply with energy
 efficiency and AFDD as the focus, followed by using flexible building loads themselves to shape
 power demand, and only later bringing in active energy storage technologies.

 Since the DFW installation will represent the first onsite implementation of Morpheus, the
 installation of its algorithms in DFW’s EMIS and BAS and commissioning to verify proper
 installation and operation are crucial to a successful pilot. NLR will develop detailed prototype
 RFPs for both of these steps in order to clearly state the project requirements and evaluation
 criteria (to be tailored by DFW before dissemination) and also help review the proposals
 submitted in response to the RFPs. NLR will review the commissioning agents work and write
 up feedback and lessons learned from that process.

 Once Morpheus has been installed and commissioned, NLR will collect performance data and
 DFW’s testimonials regarding the value of digital twins for AFDD, advanced HVAC (e.g.
 demand control ventilation) and lighting controls, and energy storage management as well as
 reduced operational and maintenance impacts. After analyzing the resulting data, NLR will
 publish a measurement and verification report to share its findings and recommendations with
 industry.

 Task 2.1 Results: After successful performance validation in a simulated environment, the MPC
 method was implemented for field testing. The effectiveness of the MPC is heavily reliant on the
 quality of the data and the stability of communication between the Morpheus platform and the
 Building Management System (BMS). To address this, the original MPC method was upgraded
 to include asset availability, data quality verification, and safe fallback procedures.

 10

 To account for assets being out of service from mechanical failures or scheduled maintenance,
 the MPC was updated to be configurable based on the number of available assets. The data
 quality methods confirm that all data is within the expected range and that no data is missing. If a
 communication failure or out-of-range data is detected, the tool flags the issue and automatically
 switches to a conventional control method until the problem is resolved.

 Additionally, the tool features a mechanism to continuously assess the accuracy of its model
 predictions against measured data. As mechanical equipment degrades over time, the model used
 in the MPC must be consistently checked to ensure it accurately represents the current condition
 of the actual equipment. When the model's prediction deviates from the measured data by a pre-
 defined threshold, the tool automatically initiates a new model training routine and updates the
 MPC with the latest model.

 Figure 5 shows the communication architecture used during the filed demonstration. The
 DataLake is a central data repository that collects data from the Building Management System
 (BAS). The Digital Twin collects data from DataLake and passes it to the MPC using an
 application program interface (API). One of the drawbacks of the suggested data communication
 structure is that it is a one-way communication system and MPC cannot write directly to the
 BAS. For actual implementation of MPC, this architecture needs to be updated so that the
 recommendations from the MPC could be directly applied to the BAS without operators in the
 loop. During the demonstration, the MPC recommendations were provided to the central plant
 operators through automated email system and the operator implements the recommendations
 directly into the BAS.

 The field test result for the test that was performed between August 15 and September 15, 2022
 is shown in Figure 6. The comparison shows the performance of the cooling system for multiple
 outdoor air temperature bins, indicating a kW/ton reduction from 0.75 to 0.70 which is
 equivalent to cost savings of 7%.
