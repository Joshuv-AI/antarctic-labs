Inferring Phylogenetic Networks from Required and Forbidden LCA-Constraints
Patricia A. Ebert, Marc Hellmuth

Abstract
Phylogenetic networks provide a framework for representing evolutionary histories involving reticulate events such as hybridization or horizontal gene transfer. A central problem is to infer such networks from local structural information. In this paper, we study network inference from least common ancestor (LCA) constraints, which specify relative ancestral relationships between pairs of taxa. While previous work has characterized when a set of required LCA constraints can be realized by a phylogenetic network, practical applications may also involve constraints that must be explicitly avoided, for example due to biological prior knowledge. We therefore consider the realization problem for pairs ( R , F ) (R,F) , where R R is a set of required LCA-constraints and F F is a set of forbidden ones. Since there are several natural ways to formalize what it means for a network to avoid a forbidden LCA-constraint, we study three such variants. For each of them, we characterize exactly when there exists a phylogenetic network that realizes all constraints in R R while avoiding all constraints in F F in the respective sense. Based on these characterizations, we derive polynomial-time algorithms that decide the existence of such networks and construct one whenever it exists.



Keywords:
DAG, lowest common ancestor, forbidden relations,
BUILD algorithm, evolutionary histories

Introduction

Phylogenetic networks are commonly used to describe evolutionary histories that cannot be
represented by trees. This is particularly relevant in the presence of reticulate events, such as
hybridization or horizontal gene transfer, where a purely tree-like model may fail to faithfully
represent the underlying evolutionary history. Since such networks are rarely observed directly, a
central task in computational biology is to infer such networks from “local information” about
their structure. Classical examples of such local information include small induced substructures
such as triplets

[
16
,
29
,
30
,
28
,
15
,
23
]
and
trinets
[
31
,
27
,
32
]
, relations derived from best
matches
[
24
,
9
,
8
]
, orthology and paralogy relations

[
20
,
12
,
14
]
or constraints formulated in terms of relative positions
of least common ancestors (LCAs)
[
18
,
1
]
. In all these settings, the guiding
principle is that global evolutionary histories should be recovered from small, biologically
interpretable pieces of evidence.

Here, we follow the ideas of recovering such evolutionary histories from LCA-constraints

[
18
,
1
]
. To make this more precise, let
N
be a phylogenetic network on a set of
taxa
X
, where the elements of
X
may, for instance, represent genes or species. A least common
ancestor (LCA) of two taxa

is a vertex

of

that is an ancestor of both

and

y
, and such that no proper descendant of

has this property. We write
\operatorname{lca}_{N}(xy)
whenever the

and

is uniquely determined in

(ab,cd)
of pairs of taxa. Generalizing the idea of Aho et al.
[
1
]

specifies that, in any network

\operatorname{lca}_{N}(ab)
must be a strict
descendant of the LCA
\operatorname{lca}_{N}(cd)
in
N
. In this case, we say that

strictly realizes

the pair
(ab,cd)
. Thus, LCA-constraints encode relative ancestral relations: they specify that one
pair of taxa shares a more recent common ancestor than another, without requiring exact divergence
times, branch lengths, or a fully resolved network topology.

This type of realization problem for LCA-constraints was first studied by Aho et al.
[
1
]

in the tree setting. More precisely, they considered the following question: given a collection
R

of LCA-constraints, can one construct a phylogenetic tree

X
that strictly realizes every

(ab,cd)\in R
, or decide that no such tree exists? As shown in
[

, this
problem can be solved in polynomial time by the now classical

algorithm. The idea of
realizing LCA-constraints was just recently generalized from phylogenetic trees to phylogenetic
networks by Lindeberg et al.
[
18
]
. In particular, they provided a characterization of
strictly realizable and, more generally, realizable relations, together with a polynomial-time
algorithm that decides whether there exists a phylogenetic network realizing all constraints in
R
.
In the affirmative case, the algorithm also constructs such a network in polynomial time.

However, in many applications, one is often faced with the problem that, in addition to constraints
that are supported by the data, one may also have constraints that should explicitly be avoided. For
example, a particular ancestral ordering may be incompatible with prior biological knowledge,
temporal information, or previously established evolutionary scenarios. Information about such
forbidden configurations has already proved useful in related settings, for instance in the
inference of evolutionary histories avoiding forbidden rooted triplets
[
11
]
. This
motivates the study of a more expressive realization problem in which one is given not only a
relation
R
of required LCA-constraints, but also a relation
F
of forbidden LCA-constraints. The
task is then to determine whether there exists a phylogenetic network that realizes all constraints
in
R
while avoiding all constraints in
F
.

In this contribution, we provide a characterization of pairs
(R,F)
, for which there exists a
phylogenetic network that is compatible with all constraints in
R
and displays none of the
constraints in
F
. This characterization, in turn, allows us to present a polynomial-time algorithm
that decides whether such a phylogenetic network exists for
(R,F)
and, in the affirmative case,
constructs one. This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we provide the
basic definition needed throughout this paper. We then recall some of the established results about
sets
R
of required LCA-constraints and their realization by DAGs or networks in
Section
3
. In Section
4
, we extend these results to also
deal with sets
F
of forbidden LCA-constraints. Finally, in Section
5
, we
provide two alternative definitions of realizability of forbidden LCA-constraints and
characterizations of realizable pairs of relations
(R,F)
under these definitions. We conclude our
contribution with a short outlook in Section
7
.

3
Realization of Required LCA-constraints

We begin by recalling some of the main definitions and results needed throughout this paper
concerning “realizable” relations introduced by Lindeberg et al.
[
18
]
. Our starting
point is a classical result due to Aho et al.
[
1
]
who considered the following problem:
given a collection of constraints of the form
(ab,cd)\in R
, where
a\neq b
,
c\neq d
, and

a,b,c,d\in X
, can one construct a phylogenetic tree
T
on
X
such that, whenever
(ab,cd)\in R
, it holds that
\operatorname{lca}_{T}(ab)\prec_{T}\operatorname{lca}_{T}(cd)
, or determine that no such tree exists? This
notion gives rise to the following definition.

Definition 3.1

(Strict Realization,
[
18
, Def 3]
)
.

A relation
R
on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
is
strictly realizable
if there is a DAG
G
on
X
such that
for all
ab,cd\in\operatorname{supp}_{R}^{+}
, the vertices
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)
and
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(cd)
are well-defined and
the following implication holds:

(I0)

(ab,cd)\in R
implies that
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)\prec_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(cd)
.

In this case, we say that
R

is strictly realized by

G
.

One may relax this definition by requiring that
R
is “realizable” if there exists a DAG
G
such
that
(ab,cd)\in R
implies
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)\preceq_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(cd)
. However, this notion is too
permissive: the star tree would always realize
R
, provided there are no pairs
(ab,xx)\in R
with

ab\neq xx
. Therefore, the following definition generalizing “strict realization” was introduced
by Lindeberg et al.
[
18
]
.

Definition 3.2

(Realization,
[
18
, Def 4]
)
.

A relation
R
on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
is
realizable
if there is a DAG
G
on
X
such that for all

ab,cd\in\operatorname{supp}^{+}_{R}
, the vertices
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)
and
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(cd)
are well-defined and the
following implications hold:

(I1)

(ab,cd)\in R
and
(cd,ab)\notin\operatorname{tc}(R)
implies that
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)\prec_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(cd)
.

(I2)

(ab,cd)\in R
and
(cd,ab)\in\operatorname{tc}(R)
implies that
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)=\operatorname{lca}_{G}(cd)
.

In this case, we say that
R

is realized by

G
.

Indeed the notion of strictly realizability of Aho et al. is just a special case of realizability
as shown by the next result.

Lemma 3.3

(
[
18
, L 5]
)
.

A relation
R
is strictly realized by
G
if and only if
R
is realized by
G
and
\operatorname{tc}(R)
is asymmetric.

Central to this theory is the closure operator
\operatorname{cl}(R)
, which admits both a characterization as the
intersection over all realizations of
R
and a combinatorial characterization via a small set of
inference rules. To be more precise, for a relation
R
on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
, we define
\mathfrak{R}_{R}

as the set of all relations
R^{\prime}
on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
that are
\operatorname{supp}^{+}_{R}
-reflexive, transitive,
cross-consistent, and satisfy
R\subseteq R^{\prime}
and let
\mathbb{G}
denote the set of all DAGs
G
on

X
that realize
R
. As shown in
[
18
, Thm 45]
, it holds that

\operatorname{cl}(R)\coloneqq\bigcap_{R^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{R}_{R}}R^{\prime}=\bigcap_{G\in\mathbb{G}}\trianglelefteq_{G}

and, in particular,
\operatorname{cl}
is a closure operator, i.e., it satisfies the classical closure axioms

extensivity
,
monotonicity
, and
idempotency
. Somewhat surprisingly,
\operatorname{cl}(R)
can
be determined in polynomial time using the three simple rules
(R1)
,
(R2)
, and
(R3)

as specified in Definition
2.1
. Moreover, realizability of a relation
R
can be
characterized in terms of two conditions,
(X1)
and
(X2)
, which also yield a
polynomial-time recognition and construction procedure.

Theorem 3.4

(
[
18
, Thm 45, 32 & 39]
)
.

For any realizable relation
R
on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
,
\operatorname{cl}(R)
can be computed in polynomial time in
|X|

as follows: Put
S=R
and first apply the rule
(R1)
to
S
. Afterwards,
repeatedly apply one of the two rules
(R2)
and
(R3)
to
S
in any order, until
none of the rules can be applied. Then,
S=\operatorname{cl}(R)
.

Furthermore, the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

R
is realizable.

(2)

R
satisfies the following two conditions:

(X1)

For all
a,b,x\in X
:
ab\neq xx
implies

(ab,xx)\notin R
.

(X2)

For all
a,b,x,y\in X
:

(ab,xy)\in R

and
(xy,ab)\not\in\operatorname{tc}(R)
implies
(xy,ab)\notin\operatorname{cl}(R)
.

Moreover, in polynomial time in
|X|
, it can be verified if
R
is realizable and, in the
affirmative case, a DAG realizing
R
can be constructed.

4
Realization of Required and Forbidden LCA-constraints

We now introduce a second relation
F
and consider pairs
(R,F)
of relations on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
, i.e.,
both
R
and
F
are relations on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
. In what follows, given a pair
(R,F)
, the relation

R
represents “required (
R
)” LCA-constraints, while
F
represents “forbidden
(
F
)” LCA-constraints, which motivates the following definition of realization.

Definition 4.1

(
RF
-Realization)
.

Let
(R,F)
be a pair of relations on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
. Then,
(R,F)
is
RF
-realizable
if there
is a DAG
G
on
X
such that
R
is realized by
G
and the following implication holds:

(F)

If
(ab,cd)\in F
and
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)
and
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(cd)
are well-defined, then
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)\nprec_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(cd)
.

In this case, we say that
(R,F)

is
RF
-realized by

G
.

In Section
5
, we discuss two alternative definitions, one to forbid
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)\preceq_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(cd)
rather than
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)\prec_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(cd)
for elements
(ab,cd)\in F
and
one to ensure that
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)
is well-defined for all
ab\in\operatorname{supp}_{F}
.

Note that in a DAG
G
that
RF
-realizes a given pair
(R,F)
of relations, it can occur that

\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)
is not well-defined. In this case, it must hold that
ab\notin\operatorname{supp}^{+}_{R}
. In
particular, if
ab\notin\operatorname{supp}^{+}_{R}
but
ab\in\operatorname{supp}_{F}
, we can exploit this by constructing
a DAG in which
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)
is not well-defined; then, none of the forbidden LCA-constraints

(ab,cd)\in F
or
(cd,ab)\in F
are realized, as they trivially satisfy
(F)
. On the other
hand, if
ab\in\operatorname{supp}^{+}_{R}
, then
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)
must be well-defined in any DAG
G
realizing
R
,
regardless of whether
ab\in\operatorname{supp}_{F}
holds or not. Hence, to construct a DAG
G
that

RF
-realizes
(R,F)
, it is sufficient to ensure that
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)
and
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(cd)
are well-defined
only for those pairs
(ab,cd)\in F
with
ab,cd\in\operatorname{supp}^{+}_{R}
. To formalize the latter idea,
let
(R,F)
be a pair of relations on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
Then, we define the following relation on

\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
:

F_{|R}=\{(ab,cd)\in F\mid ab,cd\in\operatorname{supp}^{+}_{R}\}.

The
F_{|R}
-extension of a DAG
G
on
X
is obtained from
G
by stepwise application of

xy
-extensions for all
xy\in\operatorname{supp}_{F}\setminus\operatorname{supp}^{+}_{R}
. Applying an
xy
-extension to

G
yields a DAG
G^{\prime}
in which, by Observation
2.3
,
\operatorname{lca}_{G^{\prime}}(xy)
is not
well-defined. In this case, Condition
(F)
is satisfied for
G^{\prime}
and all elements
(ab,xy)
and

(xy,ab)
in
F
. We emphasize that, if
(R,F)
is a pair of relations on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
, then
xx\in\operatorname{supp}_{R}^{+}
for all
x\in X
. Hence, we obtain

Observation 4.2
.

If
(R,F)
is a pair of relations on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
,
then in any
F_{|R}
-extension of a DAG
G
on
X
and, thus,
in the application of
xy
-extensions for all

xy\in\operatorname{supp}_{F}\setminus\operatorname{supp}^{+}_{R}
,
it holds that
x\neq y
.

We are now in the position to provide a first characterization of
RF
-realizable pairs
(R,F)
in
terms of
R
and the restriction
F_{|R}
of
F
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
(R,F)
be a pair of relations on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
.
Then, the following statements hold.

(1)

If
(R,F)
is
RF
-realized by a DAG
G
, then
(R,F_{|R})
is also
RF
-realized by
G
.

(2)

If
(R,F_{|R})
is
RF
-realized by a DAG
G
, then
(R,F)
is
RF
-realized by the
F_{|R}
-extension of
G
.

In particular,
(R,F)
is
RF
-realizable if and only if
(R,F_{|R})
is
RF
-realizable.

Proof.

Let
(R,F)
be a pair of relations on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
. Clearly, if
(R,F)
is
RF
-realized by a DAG

G
, then
F_{|R}\subseteq F
implies that
G
also
RF
-realizes
(R,F_{|R})
. This, in particular,
proves Statement (1).

We show now that Statement (2) holds. Hence, suppose that
(R,F_{|R})
is
RF
-realized by the DAG

G
. Let
(ab,cd)\in F\setminus F_{|R}
. By the definition of
F_{|R}
, we have
ab\notin\operatorname{supp}_{R}^{+}
or
cd\notin\operatorname{supp}_{R}^{+}
. In other words, none of the elements in
R
enforces
both
\operatorname{lca}_{H}(ab)
and
\operatorname{lca}_{H}(cd)
to be well-defined in any DAG
H
that
RF
-realizes
(R,F_{|R})
.
Let
pq\in\{ab,cd\}
be such that
pq\notin\operatorname{supp}_{R}^{+}
and let
G^{\prime}
be the graph obtained from

G
by applying a
pq
-extension. Observation
2.3
implies that
G^{\prime}
is a DAG
and, moreover, that
\operatorname{lca}_{G^{\prime}}(pq)
is not well-defined. In addition,
Observation
2.3
(ii) and (iii) and the definition of
F_{|R}
implies that
G^{\prime}

RF
-realizes
(R,F_{|R})
. Together with
\operatorname{lca}_{G^{\prime}}(pq)
not being well-defined and
pq\in\{ab,cd\}
, it follows that
G^{\prime}

RF
-realizes
(R,F_{|R}\cup\{(ab,cd)\})
. Now let
p^{\prime}q^{\prime}\in\{ab,cd\}\setminus\{pq\}
and assume that
p^{\prime}q^{\prime}\notin\operatorname{supp}^{+}_{R}
. In this case, we would not
only apply a
pq
-extension but also a
p^{\prime}q^{\prime}
-extension. However, since
G^{\prime}
is a DAG that

RF
-realizes
(R,F_{|R}\cup\{(ab,cd)\})
, we can re-use an analogous argument as used for
G
and

G^{\prime}
to conclude that the
p^{\prime}q^{\prime}
-extension
G^{\prime\prime}
of
G^{\prime}

RF
-realizes
(R,F_{|R}\cup\{(ab,cd)\})
.
Repeating this procedure for all
(ab,cd)\in F\setminus F_{|R}
, results in a DAG
H
that

RF
-realizes
(R,F)
. Note that
H
corresponds to the
F_{|R}
-extension of
G
and, therefore,
Statement (2) holds.

Finally Statement (1) and (2) together imply that
(R,F)
is
RF
-realizable if and only if

(R,F_{|R})
is
RF
-realizable.
∎

In the following, we want to determine further constraints to characterize
RF
-realizable pairs

(R,F)
. Note that
(R,F)
can be
RF
-realizable even if
R\cap F\neq\emptyset
which is covered by
the following result.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
(R,F)
be a pair of relations on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
. If
(R,F)
is
RF
-realized by
G
and

(ab,xy)\in R\cap F
, then
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)=\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)
and it holds that
(xy,ab)\in\operatorname{tc}(R)
.

Proof.

Suppose that
(R,F)
is
RF
-realized by
G
and that
(ab,xy)\in R\cap F
. Assume, for
contradiction, that
(xy,ab)\notin\operatorname{tc}(R)
. Since
R
is realized by
G
, the vertices

\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)
and
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)
are well-defined and
(I1)
implies that
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)\prec_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)
. However, since
G

RF
-realizes
(R,F)
and
(ab,xy)\in F
, Condition
(F)
must
hold and, therefore,
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)\nprec_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)
; a contradiction. Hence,
(xy,ab)\in\operatorname{tc}(R)
. Thus,
(I2)
must hold and we can conclude that
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)=\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)
.
∎

In particular, Lemma
4.4
motivates the definition of

F
-conditional-symmetry as specified in Section
2
. To recall, for a pair
(R,F)
of
relations on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
,
R
is
F
-conditional-symmetric (
F
-csym
, for short) if for all

a,b,x,y\in X
(not necessarily distinct) the following statement holds:

(ab,xy)\in R\cap F\text{ implies }(xy,ab)\in R.

Application of rule
(R4)
enforces
(xy,ab)\in R
whenever
(ab,xy)\in R\cap F
and ensures,
according to Lemma
4.4
, that
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)=\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)
for any DAG
G
that

RF
-realizes
(R,F)
. The latter guarantees that the forbidden pair
(ab,xy)\in F
is not shown in

G
.

As proven in the following results, repeated application of the rules
(R1)
–
(R4)
to

(R,F)
yields the closure
\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
of
R
w.r.t.
F
, analogous to the closure
\operatorname{cl}(R)
defined
in Section
3
. This closure plays a crucial role in characterizing

RF
-realizable relations and constructing a DAG
RF
-realizing
(R,F)
if one exists.

Definition 4.5

(The set
\mathfrak{R}_{R,F}
and the relation
\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
)
.

Let
(R,F)
be a pair of relations on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
. We define
\mathfrak{R}_{R,F}
as the set of all
relations
R^{\prime}
on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
that are
\operatorname{supp}^{+}_{R}
-reflexive, transitive, cross-consistent,

F
-csym, and satisfy
R\subseteq R^{\prime}
. Moreover, we define

\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)\coloneqq\bigcap_{R^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{R}_{R,F}}R^{\prime}.

Note that if
R
and
F
are relations on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
, then
\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
is considered as a relation on

\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
. Furthermore, observe that if
F=\emptyset
, the latter intersection is taken among
those relations that contain
R
and are
\operatorname{supp}^{+}_{R}
-reflexive, transitive, and
cross-consistent and, thus,
\operatorname{cl}_{\emptyset}(R)=\operatorname{cl}(R)
. In particular, for all relations
F
it
holds that
\mathfrak{R}_{R,F}\subseteq\mathfrak{R}_{R,\emptyset}
. We summarize this discussion
into

Observation 4.6
.

For all relations
R
and
F
on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
, it holds that
\operatorname{cl}(R)=\operatorname{cl}_{\emptyset}(R)\subseteq\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
.

As we shall see in Proposition
4.8
,
\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
is, indeed a closure operator.
To prove this result, we first show that
\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
can be computed in polynomial time by using the
four simple rules
(R1)
,
(R2)
,
(R3)
, and
(R4)
as specified in
Definition
2.1
which generalizes
[
18
, Thm 16]
.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
(R,F)
be a pair of relations on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
. Let
S
be a relation obtained from
(R,F)
by
starting with
S=R
and first applying the rule
(R1)
to
S
. Afterwards, repeatedly
apply one of the three rules
(R2)
,
(R3)
, and
(R4)
to
(S,F)
in any order,
until none of the rules can be applied. Then,
S=\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
and
\operatorname{supp}^{+}_{R}=\operatorname{supp}_{\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)}=\operatorname{supp}_{S}
. Furthermore,
\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
can be constructed in polynomial time in

|X|
.

Proof.

We follow here some of the ideas used in the proof of Theorem 16 in
[
18
]
. Let
(R,F)

be a pair of relations on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
, and suppose that
S
is obtained as described in the
statement. To simplify notation, put
\mathfrak{R}\coloneqq\mathfrak{R}_{R,F}
and
\operatorname{cl}_{F}\coloneqq\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
. We first show that
\operatorname{cl}_{F}\subseteq S
. Since the rules
(R1)
-
(R4)
are
exhaustively applied, the final relation
S
must be
\operatorname{supp}^{+}_{R}
-reflexive, transitive,
cross-consistent, and
F
-csym and satisfies, by definition,
R\subseteq S
. Thus,
S\in\mathfrak{R}
holds. This together with
\operatorname{cl}_{F}=\cap_{R^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{R}}R^{\prime}
implies
\operatorname{cl}_{F}\subseteq S
.

We now show
S\subseteq\operatorname{cl}_{F}
by considering the sequence
R=S_{0},S_{1},\dots,S_{n}=S
of
relations on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
, where
S_{i+1}
is constructed from
S_{i}
by application of exactly one
of the rules
(R1)
,
(R2)
,
(R3)
, or
(R4)
for all
i\in\{0,\dots,n-1\}
.

Note that all relations
R^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{R}
satisfy
R\subseteq R^{\prime}
and it, thus, holds that
R\subseteq\bigcap_{R^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{R}}R^{\prime}=\operatorname{cl}_{F}
. Hence,
R=S_{0}\subseteq\operatorname{cl}_{F}
holds. The
relation
S_{1}
is obtained from
S_{0}
by applying
(R1)
, i.e.,
S_{1}\coloneqq S_{0}\cup\{(p,p)\mid p\in\operatorname{supp}^{+}_{R}\}
. The latter equation together with
R\subseteq\operatorname{cl}_{F}
and the
fact that
\operatorname{cl}_{F}
is
\operatorname{supp}^{+}_{R}
-reflexive, implies that
S_{1}\subseteq\operatorname{cl}_{F}
.

Now suppose
S_{i}\subseteq\operatorname{cl}_{F}
for some fixed
i
with
1\leq i\leq n-1
. The relation

S_{i+1}
is constructed from
S_{i}
by applying one of the rules
(R2)
,
(R3)
, or

(R4)
, and we distinguish these three cases. If
(R2)
or
(R3)
were applied to

S_{i}
, then
S_{i+1}\subseteq\operatorname{cl}_{F}
by the same argument as given in the proof of Theorem 16 in

[
18
]
. Suppose now that
S_{i+1}
is obtained from
S_{i}
by applying
(R4)
. By
construction,
S_{i+1}=S_{i}\cup\{(q,p)\}
holds for some
(p,q)\in F\cap S_{i}
. By assumption,

S_{i}\subseteq\operatorname{cl}_{F}
holds and, thus,
(p,q)\in\operatorname{cl}_{F}
. Hence,
(p,q)\in R^{\prime}
holds for all
R^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{R}
. Since every
R^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{R}
is
F
-csym and
(p,q)\in F
, we can
conclude that
(q,p)\in R^{\prime}
for all
R^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{R}
. Hence,
(q,p)\in\operatorname{cl}_{F}
and,
therefore,
S_{i+1}\subseteq\operatorname{cl}_{F}
. By induction,
S=S_{n}\subseteq\operatorname{cl}_{F}
holds. This together
with
\operatorname{cl}_{F}\subseteq S
implies that
S=\operatorname{cl}_{F}
.

Therefore, we have, in particular,
\operatorname{supp}_{S}=\operatorname{supp}_{\operatorname{cl}_{F}}
. Furthermore, observe that

\operatorname{supp}_{S_{1}}=\operatorname{supp}^{+}_{R}
and by construction,
\operatorname{supp}_{S_{i}}=\operatorname{supp}_{S_{i+1}}
for
all
i\in\{0,\dots,n-1\}
. Consequently,
\operatorname{supp}^{+}_{R}=\operatorname{supp}_{S}
holds.

Finally, we show that
\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
can be constructed in polynomial time in
|X|
. By the arguments
above,
\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
can be obtained by repeatedly applying rules
(R1)
,
(R2)
,
(R3)
,
and
(R4)
starting with
S=R
and extending it step by step. Since
S,F\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
and
|\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)|=\binom{|X|}{2}+|X|\in O(|X|^{2})
, it follows that
|S|\in O(|X|^{4})
at each step and
|F|\in O(|X|^{4})
. Thus, checking whether one of the rules
(R1)
,

(R2)
,
(R3)
, or
(R4)
can be applied to
S
can be done in polynomial time, and
adding a pair
(p,q)
to
S
requires only constant time. Clearly, the process of constructing

\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
by applying rules
(R1)
,
(R2)
,
(R3)
, or
(R4)
must terminate,
since at most
O(|X|^{4})
pairs can be added to
S
. In summary,
\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
can be constructed in
polynomial time in
|X|
.
∎

\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)

to

as outlined in Theorem

is illustrated in

. We continue with showing that
\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
is indeed a closure operator.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
F
be a relation on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
. Then,
\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
is a closure operator for all relations
R
on

\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
, i.e., it satisfies the following three conditions.

(i)

Extensivity:
R\subseteq\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
.

(ii)

Monotonicity:
\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R^{\prime})\subseteq\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
for all relations
R^{\prime}
on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
with
R^{\prime}\subseteq R
.

(iii)

Idempotency:
\operatorname{cl}_{F}(\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R))=\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
.

Proof.

Let
R
and
F
be relations on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
. Note that all relations
R^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{R}_{R,F}

satisfy
R\subseteq R^{\prime}
and it, thus, holds that
R\subseteq\bigcap_{R^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{R}_{R,F}}R^{\prime}=\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
. Hence, extensivity holds.

Suppose now that
R^{\prime}
and
R
are relations on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
such that
R^{\prime}\subseteq R
. Hence,

\operatorname{supp}_{R^{\prime}}\subseteq\operatorname{supp}_{R}
holds. Since
R,R^{\prime}
are both relations on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
,
also
\operatorname{supp}_{R^{\prime}}^{+}\subseteq\operatorname{supp}_{R}^{+}
is satisfied. Now, let
\tilde{R}\in\mathfrak{R}_{R,F}
. Since
\operatorname{supp}_{R^{\prime}}^{+}\subseteq\operatorname{supp}_{R}^{+}
and since
\tilde{R}
is

\operatorname{supp}_{R}^{+}
-reflexive, it follows that
\tilde{R}
is, in particular,

\operatorname{supp}_{R^{\prime}}^{+}
-reflexive. Moreover, since
\tilde{R}\in\mathfrak{R}_{R,F}
, the relation

\tilde{R}
is, in addition, transitive, cross-consistent,
F
-csym, and satisfies
R^{\prime}\subseteq R\subseteq\tilde{R}
. Taking the latter two arguments together,
\tilde{R}\in\mathfrak{R}_{R^{\prime},F}

follows. Consequently,
\mathfrak{R}_{R,F}\subseteq\mathfrak{R}_{R^{\prime},F}
holds and we obtain

\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R^{\prime})=\bigcap_{\tilde{R}\in\mathfrak{R}_{R^{\prime},F}}\tilde{R}\subseteq\bigcap_{\tilde{R}\in\mathfrak{R}_{R,F}}\tilde{R}=\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
. Hence, monotonicity holds.

To prove idempotency, note that, by Theorem
4.7
and

(R1)
,
\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
is
\operatorname{supp}^{+}_{R}
-reflexive and
\operatorname{supp}^{+}_{R}=\operatorname{supp}_{\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)}=\operatorname{supp}^{+}_{\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)}
. Therefore,
\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
is
\operatorname{supp}_{\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)}^{+}
-reflexive. Moreover, by
Theorem
4.7
, to obtain
\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
from
R
the rules

(R1)
,
(R2)
,
(R3)
, and
(R4)
are exhaustively applied until none of these
rules can be applied anymore. Consequently,
\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
is transitive, cross-consistent,
F
-csym,
and clearly
\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)\subseteq\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
. In summary,
\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)\in\mathfrak{R}_{\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R),F}
holds.
Hence,
\operatorname{cl}_{F}(\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R))=\bigcap_{\tilde{R}\in\mathfrak{R}_{\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R),F}}\tilde{R}\subseteq\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
. Moreover, by extensivity,
\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)\subseteq\operatorname{cl}_{F}(\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R))
holds, and it follows that

\operatorname{cl}_{F}(\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R))=\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
, i.e., idempotency holds.
∎

By definition,
\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
is
\operatorname{supp}^{+}_{R}
-reflexive. By
Theorem
4.7
and
(R1)
, we have
\operatorname{supp}^{+}_{R}=\operatorname{supp}_{\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)}=\operatorname{supp}^{+}_{\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)}
. Hence,
\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
is reflexive. Moreover,
Theorem
4.7
implies that
\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
is transitive and, thus,

\operatorname{tc}(\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R))=\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
. This and
R\subseteq\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
implies
\operatorname{tc}(R)\subseteq\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
. We
summarize the latter discussion for later reference into

Observation 4.9
.

For all pairs
(R,F)
of relations on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
, it holds that
\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
is reflexive and
that
\operatorname{tc}(R)\subseteq\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
.

Moreover,
\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
also reveals some insights into any DAG that
RF
-realizes
(R,F_{|R})
.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
(R,F)
be a pair of relations on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
and let
G
be a DAG on
X
that
RF
-realizes

(R,F_{|R})
. Then, for all
a,b,x,y\in X
, it holds that

(ab,xy)\in\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)\implies\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)\preceq_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy).

In particular, it holds that
\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)\subseteq\trianglelefteq_{G}
.

Proof.

We follow here some of the ideas used in the proof of Lemma 21 in
[
18
]
. Suppose that

(R,F)
is a pair of relations on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
and that
G
is a DAG on
X
that
RF
-realizes

(R,F_{|R})
. Let
R=S_{0},S_{1},\dots,S_{n}=\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
be a sequence of relations, where
S_{i+1}
is
obtained from
S_{i}
by applying one of the rules
(R1)
,
(R2)
,
(R3)
, or
(R4)

for all
i\in\{0,\dots,n-1\}
(w.r.t. the two relations
S_{i}
and
F
as specified in
Definition
2.1
). Such a sequence exists by
Theorem
4.7
. We now prove that, for all
i\in\{0,\dots,n\}
, it holds that

\displaystyle(ab,xy)\in S_{i}\implies\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)\preceq_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy).

(1)

For
S_{0}=R
, let
(ab,xy)\in S_{0}
. Since
G

RF
-realizes
(R,F_{|R})
and
(ab,xy)\in R
, we can
conclude that
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)
and
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)
are well-defined. Thus, one of the axioms
(I1)
and

(I2)
must be satisfied, and we obtain
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)\prec_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)
and
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)=\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)
, respectively. In both case, it holds that
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)\preceq_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)
as desired.
To obtain
S_{1}
, we apply
(R1)
to
S_{0}
, i.e.,
S_{1}=S_{0}\cup\{(p,p)\mid p\in\operatorname{supp}^{+}_{R}\}
. Clearly, all elements
(p,q)\in S_{0}\cap S_{1}
satisfy
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(p)\preceq_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(q)
. Moreover, all
(p,q)\in S_{1}\setminus S_{0}
must satisfy
p=q
and trivially
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(p)=\operatorname{lca}_{G}(q)
holds. Thus, Eq.
1
is satisfied for
S_{0}
and
S_{1}
.

Suppose that Eq.
1
holds for
S_{i}
for some fixed
i
with
1\leq i\leq n-1
. By construction,
S_{i+1}=S_{i}\cup\{(p,q)\}
for some
p,q\in\operatorname{supp}^{+}_{R}
. Since
Eq.
1
is correct for
S_{i}
, it suffices to show that
Eq.
1
holds for
(p,q)\in S_{i+1}
and we distinguish three cases for the
rules that were used to add
(p,q)
. In case of
(R2)
and
(R3)
, the correctness follows
by the same arguments used in the proof of Lemma 21 in
[
18
]
. Suppose now that rule

(R4)
was applied to obtain
S_{i+1}=S_{i}\cup\{(p,q)\}
. Hence,
(q,p)\in F\cap S_{i}

holds. Since
(q,p)\in S_{i}
, Eq.
1
implies that
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(q)\preceq_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(p)
. Moreover, since
(q,p)\in S_{i}
, it follows by the same arguments as in the proof of
Theorem
4.7
that
q,p\in\operatorname{supp}^{+}_{R}
. This and
(q,p)\in F
implies that
(q,p)\in F_{|R}
. Hence,
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(q)\nprec_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(p)
, as
G

RF
-realizes

(R,F_{|R})
. Taking the latter two arguments together,
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(q)=\operatorname{lca}_{G}(p)
must hold. Therefore,
Eq.
1
is satisfied for
(p,q)\in S_{i+1}
. Since
S_{i}=S_{i+1}\setminus(p,q)
and since, by induction assumption, Eq.
1
holds for
S_{i}
it follows
that Eq.
1
is satisfied for all elements in
S_{i+1}
.

In summary, Eq.
1
holds for all elements in
S_{i}
with
i\in\{0,\dots,n\}
, and, thus, in particular for all elements in
S_{n}=\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
. The latter arguments together
with the definition of
\trianglelefteq_{G}
imply
\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)\subseteq\trianglelefteq_{G}
.
∎

As we shall see in Theorem
4.18
,
RF
-realizable relations are
characterized in terms of two conditions,
(Y1)
and
(Y2)
, as specified next.

Definition 4.11

(Condition
(Y1)
and
(Y2)
)
.

For a pair
(R,F)
of relations on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
, we define the
following two conditions:

(Y1)

For all
a,b,x\in X
:
ab\neq xx
implies

(ab,xx)\notin\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
.

(Y2)

For all
a,b,x,y\in X
:

(ab,xy)\in R
and
(xy,ab)\not\in\operatorname{tc}(R)
implies
(xy,ab)\notin\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
.

Note that
(Y1)
and
(Y2)
form a natural generalization of Conditions
(X1)
and

(X2)
, which characterize realizable relations.
(Y1)
differs from
(X1)
by the
condition
(ab,xx)\notin\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
instead of
(ab,xx)\notin R
. Moreover,
(Y2)
differs from

(X2)
in the chosen closures
\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
and
\operatorname{cl}(R)
. In particular, all these conditions
coincide whenever
F=\emptyset
, as shown next.

Lemma 4.12
.

For a pair
(R,\emptyset)
of relations on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
it holds that:

(1)

(R,\emptyset)
satisfies
(Y1)
if and only if
R
satisfies
(X1)
.

(2)

(R,\emptyset)
satisfies
(Y2)
if and only if
R
satisfies
(X2)
.

Proof.

Consider a pair
(R,\emptyset)
of relations on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
. As shown in
[
18
, L 24]
,

R
satisfies
(X1)
if and only if
\operatorname{cl}(R)
satisfies
(X1)
. This combined with
Observation
4.6
implies that
R
satisfies
(X1)
if and only if

\operatorname{cl}_{\emptyset}(R)
satisfies
(X1)
or, equivalently, if
(R,\emptyset)
satisfies

(Y1)
. Hence, Statement (1) holds. Statement (2) is an immediate consequence of
Observation
4.6
and the definition of
(Y2)
and
(X2)
.
∎

To provide a characterization of
RF
-realizable relations
(R,F)
, we first show that those
relations satisfy
(Y1)
and
(Y2)
which generalizes
[
18
, L 25]
.

Lemma 4.13
.

If a pair
(R,F)
of relations on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
is
RF
-realizable, then
(R,F)
satisfies
(Y1)

and
(Y2)
.

Proof.

Let
(R,F)
be a pair of relations on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
which is
RF
-realized by a DAG
G
on
X
. By
Proposition
4.3
,
G

RF
-realizes
(R,F_{|R})
. Assume, for contradiction,
that
(Y1)
is not satisfied for
(R,F)
. Hence, there exists
a,b,x\in X
with
ab\neq xx

such that
(ab,xx)\in\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
. This together with Lemma
4.10
implies

\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)\preceq_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xx)
. Since
x
is a leaf,
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)=\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xx)=x
must hold.
Hence,
a=b=x
; a contradiction to
ab\neq xx
.

To show that
(Y2)
is satisfied, suppose there are
a,b,x,y\in X
such that
(ab,xy)\in R
and

(xy,ab)\not\in\operatorname{tc}(R)
. This and the fact that
G
realizes
R
together with
(I1)
implies
that
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)\prec_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)
. Contraposition of Lemma
4.10
thus
implies that
(xy,ab)\notin\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
. Hence,
(Y2)
holds.
∎

To prove that
(Y1)
and
(Y2)
in fact characterize
RF
-realizable relations
(R,F)
, we
construct a
canonical DAG

\mathscr{G}_{R,F}
based on properties of
\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
. As we shall see in
Theorem
4.18
, if
(R,F)
is
RF
-realizable, then a slightly
modified version of
\mathscr{G}_{R,F}

RF
-realizes
(R,F)
. The idea of a canonical DAG derived from
properties of an underlying closure is not new, and we largely follow the approach
of
[
18
]
.

For the definition of the canonical DAG associated with
(R,F)
, we use a standard procedure that
associates a poset with a reflexive and transitive relation (also known as a
preorder
or

quasi-order
) by identifying precisely those elements that violate anti-symmetry. Here, we use
the reflexive and transitive relation
\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
as initial relation. More precisely, we put two
elements
p
and
q
in relation
\sim_{\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)}
whenever both
(p,q)\in\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
and
(q,p)\in\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
hold. This, in turn yields an equivalence relation
\sim_{\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)}
whose equivalence
classes will correspond to the vertices of
\mathscr{G}_{R,F}
. We then define a partial order

\leq_{\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)}
on these equivalence classes which is used to define the arcs of
\mathscr{G}_{R,F}
.

Definition 4.14

(The quotient poset
(Q,\leq_{\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)})
of
\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
)
.

Let
(R,F)
be a pair of relations on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
. We define the equivalence relation

\sim_{\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)}
on
\operatorname{supp}^{+}_{R}
by putting, for all
p,q\in\operatorname{supp}^{+}_{R}
,

p\sim_{\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)}q\iff(p,q)\in\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)\text{ and }(q,p)\in\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R).

Let
[p]
denote the equivalence class
of
\sim_{\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)}
that contains
p\in\operatorname{supp}_{R}^{+}
, and let
Q
denote the set of all such
equivalence classes. Define the partial order
\leq_{\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)}
on
Q
by putting, for all classes

[p]
and
[q]
in
Q
,

[p]\leq_{\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)}[q]\iff(p,q)\in\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R).

We refer to the poset

(Q,\leq_{\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)})
as the
quotient poset of
\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
.

The well-definedness and the facts that
\sim_{\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)}
is an equivalence relation and that

(Q,\leq_{\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)})
is a poset follows from the standard “quotient” construction that turns a
preorder into a partial order; see
[
25
, Prop 5.2.4]
for a full proof. Based on the
equivalence relation
\sim_{\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)}
and partial order
\leq_{\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)}
, we define the canonical
DAG
\mathscr{G}_{R,F}
, similar to the definition of a canonical DAG given in
[
18
, Def 27]
.

Definition 4.15

(Canonical DAG)
.

Let
(R,F)
be a pair of relations on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
and let
(Q,\leq_{\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)})
be the quotient poset
of
\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
. The
canonical DAG

\mathscr{G}_{R,F}
is defined as the DAG obtained from the Hasse
diagram
\mathscr{H}(Q,\leq_{\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)})
by relabeling all vertices
[aa]\in Q
with
a
.

We emphasize that if
(R,F)
does not satisfy
(Y1)
, then
\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
may contain constraints of
the form
(ab,xx)
. In this case, it is even possible that, for example,
[aa]=[bb]\in Q
. Hence, in
the construction of
\mathscr{G}_{R,F}
, the vertex
[aa]
would have to be relabeled by
a
, while the same
vertex, viewed as
[bb]
, would have to be relabeled by
b
. Thus, the construction of
\mathscr{G}_{R,F}

would no longer be well-defined. One can circumvent this ambiguity by choosing, for each such class,
a single representative leaf by which it is replaced (e.g. by imposing a linear order on
X
and
choosing the representative as the smallest element). Nevertheless, we will only explicitly
construct
\mathscr{G}_{R,F}
for pairs
(R,F)
of relations on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
that satisfy
(Y1)
. Under
this assumption, the ambiguity described above cannot occur. Consequently,
\mathscr{G}_{R,F}
is a
well-defined DAG and, as we shall see in Proposition
4.17
, has
leaf set
X
.

We further note in passing that
\mathscr{G}_{R,\emptyset}
coincides with the canonical DAG
\mathscr{G}_{R}
as
defined in
[
18
]
. Furthermore, observe that the canonical DAG
\mathscr{G}_{R,F}
is only based on
properties of the relation
\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
and similarly, the construction of
\mathscr{G}_{\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R),F}
depends
only on the relation
\operatorname{cl}_{F}(\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R))
. By Proposition
4.8
,
\operatorname{cl}_{F}(\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R))=\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
and we obtain

Observation 4.16
.

For all pairs
(R,F)
of relations on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
, we have
\mathscr{G}_{R,F}=\mathscr{G}_{\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R),F}
.

Proposition 4.17
.

Let
(R,F)
be a pair of relations on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
that satisfies
(Y1)
or that is

RF
-realizable. Then, the canonical DAG
\mathscr{G}_{R,F}
is a well-defined DAG on
X
such that
[ab]=\operatorname{lca}_{\mathscr{G}_{R,F}}(ab)
for all vertices
[ab]
of
\mathscr{G}_{R,F}
with
a\neq b
and
x=\operatorname{lca}_{\mathscr{G}_{R,F}}(xx)
for all
x\in X
. In particular,
\mathscr{G}_{R,F}
is 2-lca-relevant, phylogenetic,
and
RF
-realizes
(\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R),F_{|R})
. Moreover, the
F_{|R}
-extension of
\mathscr{G}_{R,F}
is phylogenetic.

Proof.

We closely follow the proof of
[
18
, Prop 29]
. It differs, in essence, only from the
part showing that
\mathscr{G}_{R,F}

RF
-realizes
(\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R),F_{|R})
. Let
(R,F)
be a pair of relations on

\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
that satisfies
(Y1)
or that is
RF
-realizable. In the latter case,
Lemma
4.13
implies that
(R,F)
satisfies
(Y1)
. To simplify
writing, let
\mathscr{G}\coloneqq\mathscr{G}_{R,F}
,
\sim\coloneqq\sim_{\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)}
,
\leq\coloneqq\leq_{\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)}
, and
\operatorname{cl}_{F}\coloneqq\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
. Moreover, let
Q
be the set of all
\sim
-classes.

We start by showing that
\mathscr{G}
has leaf set
X
. By
(Y1)
, no class
[xx]\in Q
can coincide
with a class
[ab]\in Q
for which
ab\neq xx
. Moreover, by
(R1)
,
[xx]\in Q
for all
x\in X
. Hence, the two sets
Q_{1}=\{[xx]\colon x\in X\}
and
Q_{2}=\{[ab]\in Q\colon a\neq b\}
form a
bipartition of
Q
, i.e.,
Q=Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}
and
Q_{1}\cap Q_{2}=\emptyset
. By the latter arguments,
we have
x,y\in X
and
x\neq y
if and only if
[xx]\neq[yy]
and
[xx],[yy]\in Q_{1}\subseteq Q
.
Thus, there is a 1-to-1 correspondence between the vertices in
X
and the classes in
Q_{1}
.
Moreover, by
(Y1)
,
(ab,xx)\not\in\operatorname{cl}_{F}
for all
a,b,x\in X
with
ab\neq xx
. Hence,

[ab]\not\leq[xx]
for all
a,b,x\in X
with
ab\neq xx
and by construction,
\mathscr{G}
does not
contain arcs
(v,v)
for any
v\in V(\mathscr{G})
. Therefore, one never adds an arc of the form
x\to v

for any
x\in X
and
v\in V(\mathscr{G})
in
\mathscr{G}
. In summary, the set
X
forms a subset of leaves in

Classical Closure

In the preceding sections, we introduced the closure
\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
of a relation

w.r.t. to a
relation
F
and used it to construct the canonical DAG associated with an
RF
-realizable relation.
For realizable relations, however, there is another, arguably more intrinsic, way to define closure.
This alternative mirrors the standard notion of closure for triplets and quartets on trees

[

,
17
,

,

]
. Namely, one may define the closure
\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
of

as
the intersection of all relations
\trianglelefteq_{G}
induced by DAGs

that

-realize
(R,F)

\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
consists precisely of those constraints that are forced by

and
F
across every
possible
RF
-realization. The main result of this section shows that these two notions of a closure
coincide; see Theorem
6.4
. We start by giving the formal definition of this
alternative closure.

Definition 6.1

(Classical Closure)
.

Let
(R,F)
be an

-realizable pair of relations on

, and let

of all DAGs on

-realize
(R,F)

\operatorname{CL}_{F}(R)\coloneqq\bigcap_{G\in\mathfrak{G}}\trianglelefteq_{G}.

By definition,
\operatorname{CL}_{F}(R)
is a relation on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
. To show that
\operatorname{CL}_{F}(R)=\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
for all

RF
-realizable pairs
(R,F)
, we first need some auxiliary results.

Lemma 6.2
.

(R,F)

-realizable pair of relations on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)

be the

. Then, for all

7
Summary and Outlook

In this contribution, we defined and studied three variants of the realization problem for pairs

(R,F)
of required and forbidden LCA-constraints, namely,
RF
-realizability, RF
⋠
-realizability,
and RF
{}^{\operatorname{lca}}
-realizability. For each of these variants, we provided a characterization of pairs of
relations that are realizable under the respective definition and designed polynomial-time
algorithms to decide whether such a realization exists. In the affirmative case, a phylogenetic
network that realizes all constraints in
R
while avoiding those in
F
can be constructed in
polynomial time. The latter construction is heavily based on the closure
\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
. We showed
that this closure can be constructed in polynomial time based on a set of simple rules. In addition, if

(R,F)
is
RF
-realizable, then
\operatorname{cl}_{F}(R)
coincides with the intersection of all relations
\trianglelefteq_{G}

induced by DAGs
G
that
RF
-realize
(R,F)
.

While our results settle the general realization problem for required and forbidden LCA-constraints,
the networks constructed in this framework are not required to belong to any restricted network
class and may therefore be structurally complex. Nevertheless, this work lays the foundation for a
systematic study of restricted realization problems, for instance by strengthening or supplementing
the conditions
(Y1)
and
(Y2)
. In particular, future work will focus on understanding
when a given pair
(R,F)
can be realized by more specific and biologically constrained classes of
phylogenetic networks, such as normal networks
[
33
,
21
]
, level-
1

networks
[
7
,
13
]
, or galled trees
[
10
]
.
