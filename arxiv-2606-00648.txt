Asymptotic estimates for multiple point ranges of transient random walks on graphs
Kazuki Okamura

Abstract
. We study multiple point ranges for random walks on graphs, extending known asymptotic results obtained for random walks on groups. A distinctive feature is that algebraic and translation-invariance assumptions are replaced by a uniform tail condition on the first return time to the starting point. Under this condition, we obtain upper and lower bounds of linear order for the expectations of the number of sites visited at least a given number of times and the number of sites visited exactly that number of times. We also prove the corresponding almost sure bounds under a stronger condition. In spatially homogeneous transient cases, these bounds coincide and yield a strong law of large numbers. We apply these estimates to derive asymptotic results for functions of the local times.



1.
Introduction and main results

The range of a random walk is one of the basic quantities measuring how much of the state space the walk has explored.
It counts sites without distinguishing how many times they have been visited.
In order to describe the occupation structure more finely, we count sites according to their number of visits.
For each
\displaystyle j\geq 1
, we consider the number
\displaystyle R_{n}^{j}
of sites visited at least
\displaystyle j
times by time
\displaystyle n
, and the number
\displaystyle R_{n}^{(j)}
of sites visited exactly
\displaystyle j
times by time
\displaystyle n
.
Here
\displaystyle R_{n}^{1}
is the single point range.
These quantities are closely related to self-intersections and to the local times of the walk.

The asymptotic behavior for the multiple point ranges
\displaystyle R_{n}^{j}
and
\displaystyle R_{n}^{(j)}
has been investigated by several authors.
For transient random walks, Erdős and Taylor
[
5
]
showed the strong law of large numbers for the simple random walk on the integer lattice
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}^{d},d\geq 3
.
Pitt
[
14
]
showed the strong law of large numbers for a transient random walk on a countable Abelian group.
Derriennic
[
4
]
considered a more general class of random walks including the case of random walks on countable non-Abelian groups.
Multiple point ranges for the two-dimensional random walk have also been considered by Flatto
[
6
]
and Hamana
[
7
,
8
]
.
These works rely on the homogeneous structure of the underlying space.
In particular, group structures make it possible to use ergodic methods.
For random walks on general graphs, such tools do not apply.
This motivates the use of assumptions formulated in terms of return probabilities, rather than algebraic structure.

The aim of this paper is to obtain analogues of known asymptotic results for multiple point ranges for random walks on graphs.
Instead of relying on translation invariance or ergodic arguments, we assume a uniform tail condition for the first return time to the starting point.
Under this condition, we prove upper and lower bounds of linear order for the expectations of
\displaystyle R_{n}^{j}
and
\displaystyle R_{n}^{(j)}
.
Furthermore, under a stronger uniform tail condition, we obtain the corresponding almost sure bounds.
If the probability of returning to the starting point is independent of the starting point and is strictly less than one,
then the upper and lower bounds coincide and a strong law of large numbers holds.

The case of sites visited at least once corresponds to the single point range.
Our results also recover estimates for the single point range, and the uniform tail condition here is weaker than the one used by the author
[
13
, Corollary 2.3]
and by Kumagai and Nakamura
[
10
, Proposition 5.7]
.
As discussed in Section
4
, this condition can be verified through on-diagonal heat kernel upper bounds.
It is therefore applicable to a broad class of transient random walks on graphs.

Multiple point ranges are closely related to functions of the local times.
For transient random walks on lattices and groups, asymptotic properties of local time functionals have been studied by several authors.
Becker and König
[
2
]
investigated moments of the local times of transient random walks on
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
Asymont and Korshunov
[
1
]
considered a more general class of functions of the local times.
Recently, Chang, Chen, Meng and Peng
[
3
]
obtained further limit results for such functionals.
These results are proved for integer lattices and groups.

We consider the corresponding problem for random walks on graphs in Section
3
.
For a non-negative function
\displaystyle f
with
\displaystyle f(0)=0
, let
\displaystyle G_{n}(f)
be the sum obtained by applying
\displaystyle f
to the local time of each site visited up to time
\displaystyle n
.
Combining a decomposition by local time levels with our estimates for exact multiple point ranges
\displaystyle R_{n}^{(j)}
, we obtain asymptotic results for
\displaystyle G_{n}(f)
under suitable summability conditions on
\displaystyle f
.
The local time results are graph analogues of known results in the lattice and group settings and include an extension of
[
3
, Theorem 2]
and of the
\displaystyle L^{2}
-convergence part of
[
1
, Theorem 1]
.

1.1.
Framework and main results

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{X}
be a countable set.
Let
\displaystyle(X_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
be an irreducible Markov chain on
\displaystyle\mathcal{X}
.
If
\displaystyle P(X_{0}=x)=1
, then we denote the law of the Markov chain
\displaystyle(X_{n})_{n}
by
\displaystyle P^{x}
.
We denote the expectation with respect to
\displaystyle P^{x}
by
\displaystyle E^{x}
.

Let
\displaystyle\ell(n,x)\coloneqq|\{i\in\{1,\dots,n\}:X_{i}=x\}|
.
For
\displaystyle j\geq 1
and
\displaystyle n\geq 1
, let

R_{n}^{j}\coloneqq\left|\left\{x\in\mathcal{X}:\ell(n,x)\geq j\right\}\right|

and

R_{n}^{(j)}\coloneqq\left|\left\{x\in\mathcal{X}:\ell(n,x)=j\right\}\right|.

We remark that
\displaystyle R_{n}\coloneqq R_{n}^{1}
is the range of the random walk and
\displaystyle R_{n}^{j}=R_{n}^{(j)}+R_{n}^{j+1}
.

For
\displaystyle x\in\mathcal{X}
, let

\displaystyle T_{x}\coloneqq\inf\{n\geq 1:X_{n}=x\}
.
Let

F_{\inf}\coloneqq\inf_{x\in\mathcal{X}}P^{x}(T_{x}<\infty),\ \textup{ and }\ F_{\sup}\coloneqq\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}P^{x}(T_{x}<\infty).

We introduce the following uniform tail condition for the first return time to the starting point.
We say that
\displaystyle(U_{0})
holds if

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}P_{x}(n<T_{x}<\infty)=0,

and that
\displaystyle(U_{1})
holds if

\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}P_{x}(n<T_{x}<\infty)=O\left((\log n)^{-1-\delta}\right),\ n\to\infty.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
\displaystyle(U_{0})
holds, then, for every
\displaystyle j\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle(F_{\inf})^{j-1}(1-F_{\sup})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\inf_{x\in\mathcal{X}}E^{x}\left[R_{n}^{j}\right]}{n}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}E^{x}\left[R_{n}^{j}\right]}{n}\leq(F_{\sup})^{j-1}(1-F_{\inf})

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle(F_{\inf})^{j-1}(1-F_{\sup})^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\inf_{x\in\mathcal{X}}E^{x}\left[R_{n}^{(j)}\right]}{n}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}E^{x}\left[R_{n}^{(j)}\right]}{n}\leq(F_{\sup})^{j-1}(1-F_{\inf})^{2}.

If the Markov chain
\displaystyle(X_{n})_{n}
is the simple random walk on a vertex-transitive graph or the random walk on a countable group, then
\displaystyle F_{\sup}=F_{\inf}
.
If the Markov chain
\displaystyle(X_{n})
is recurrent, then
\displaystyle F_{\sup}=F_{\inf}=1
.

\displaystyle(U_{0})
is identical with the condition in
[
13
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

If
\displaystyle(U_{1})
holds, then, for every
\displaystyle j\geq 1
and every
\displaystyle x\in\mathcal{X}
,
the following statements hold
\displaystyle P^{x}
-a.s.:

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{R_{n}^{j}}{n}\leq(F_{\sup})^{j-1}(1-F_{\inf}).\

(1.1)

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{R_{n}^{(j)}}{n}\leq(F_{\sup})^{j-1}(1-F_{\inf})^{2}.

(1.2)

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{R_{n}^{j}}{n}\geq(F_{\inf})^{j-1}(1-F_{\sup}).\

(1.3)

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{R_{n}^{(j)}}{n}\geq(F_{\inf})^{j-1}(1-F_{\sup})^{2}.

(1.4)

In Section
4
, we give a sufficient condition for
\displaystyle(U_{1})
in terms of heat kernel.
To our knowledge, the Nash-type on-diagonal heat kernel upper bounds needed to verify
\displaystyle(U_{1})
are not known to hold for all transient random walks on countable groups.
However, under additional assumptions, Nash-type bounds and related estimates have been studied extensively; see Saloff-Coste and Zheng
[
15
]
for example.
We remark that there exist infinite vertex-transitive graphs which are not Cayley graphs of groups, as shown by Woess
[
18
]
.

2.
Proofs

In this section, we prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.
As an outline, we follow
[
14
]
; in particular, we use induction on
\displaystyle j
in the estimates of expectations.
However, we cannot apply the ergodic theorem and several parts of the argument have to be changed significantly.
The main difficulty is to estimate
\displaystyle E_{m,n}
defined in (
2.9
) below.
In
[
14
]
, this part is handled by the ergodic theorem; here we use the uniform tail condition
\displaystyle(U_{1})
instead.

2.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{k}^{n}\coloneqq\left\{X_{k}\neq X_{\ell},k+1\leq\ell\leq n\right\}
for
\displaystyle k<n
and
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{n}^{n}
be the whole event.
Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{k}^{(j)}\coloneqq\left\{|\{i\in\{1,\dots,k\}:X_{i}=X_{k}\}|=j\right\}
.
We remark that
\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{k}^{(j)}=\emptyset
if
\displaystyle j>k
.
Then,

R^{(j)}_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\bf 1}_{\mathcal{B}_{k}^{(j)}\cap\mathcal{A}_{k}^{n}},\textup{ and }R^{j}_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\bf 1}_{\mathcal{B}_{k}^{(j)}}.

Hence, for every
\displaystyle x\in\mathcal{X}
,

E^{x}\left[R^{(j)}_{n}\right]=\sum_{k=1}^{n}P^{x}\left(\mathcal{B}_{k}^{(j)}\cap\mathcal{A}_{k}^{n}\right)\textup{ and }E^{x}\left[R^{j}_{n}\right]=\sum_{k=1}^{n}P^{x}\left(\mathcal{B}_{k}^{(j)}\right).

(2.1)

For
\displaystyle n\geq 0
, let

F_{\inf}(n)\coloneqq\inf_{x\in\mathcal{X}}P^{x}(T_{x}\leq n)\ \textup{ and }\ F_{\sup}(n)\coloneqq\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}P^{x}(T_{x}\leq n).

Then, by the Markov property,

1-F_{\sup}(n-k)\leq P^{x}\left(\mathcal{A}_{k}^{n}\,\middle|\,\mathcal{B}_{k}^{(j)}\right)\leq 1-F_{\inf}(n-k),\ x\in\mathcal{X}.

(2.2)

First, we deal with the upper bounds.
By (
2.1
) and (
2.2
),

E^{x}\left[R^{(j)}_{n}\right]\geq\sum_{k=1}^{n}P^{x}\left(\mathcal{B}_{k}^{(j)}\right)(1-F_{\sup}(n-k)),\ x\in\mathcal{X}.

Using this, (
2.1
), and the fact that
\displaystyle F_{\sup}(m)\leq F_{\sup}
for each
\displaystyle m\geq 1
,
it holds that

E^{x}\left[R^{(j)}_{n}\right]\geq(1-F_{\sup})E^{x}\left[R^{j}_{n}\right],\ x\in\mathcal{X}.

(2.3)

Since
\displaystyle R_{n}^{j}-R_{n}^{j+1}=R_{n}^{(j)}
,

E^{x}\left[R^{j+1}_{n}\right]\leq F_{\sup}E^{x}\left[R^{j}_{n}\right],\ x\in\mathcal{X},

and hence,

\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}E^{x}\left[R^{j+1}_{n}\right]\leq F_{\sup}\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}E^{x}\left[R^{j}_{n}\right].

(2.4)

We see that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}E^{x}\left[R_{n}\right]}{n}\leq 1-F_{\inf}.

Hence, by induction on
\displaystyle j
,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}E^{x}\left[R_{n}^{j}\right]}{n}\leq(F_{\sup})^{j-1}(1-F_{\inf}).

(2.5)

We consider
\displaystyle E^{x}\left[R^{(j)}_{n}\right]
.
By (
2.1
) and (
2.2
),

E^{x}\left[R^{(j)}_{n}\right]\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}P^{x}\left(\mathcal{B}_{k}^{(j)}\right)(1-F_{\inf}(n-k)),\ x\in\mathcal{X}.

(2.6)

By
\displaystyle(U_{0})
, it holds that

\lim_{n\to\infty}F_{\inf}(n)=F_{\inf}\ \textup{ and }\ \lim_{n\to\infty}F_{\sup}(n)=F_{\sup}.

Let
\displaystyle\epsilon>0
.
Let
\displaystyle N_{\epsilon}
be an integer such that for every
\displaystyle n>N_{\epsilon}
,
\displaystyle F_{\inf}(n)\geq F_{\inf}-\epsilon
.
Then, for every
\displaystyle n>N_{\epsilon}
and
\displaystyle x\in\mathcal{X}
,

\sum_{k=1}^{n}P^{x}\left(\mathcal{B}_{k}^{(j)}\right)(1-F_{\inf}(n-k))\leq(1-F_{\inf}+\epsilon)\sum_{k=1}^{n-N_{\epsilon}}P^{x}\left(\mathcal{B}_{k}^{(j)}\right)+N_{\epsilon}.

Combining this with (
2.6
) and (
2.1
),

E^{x}\left[R^{(j)}_{n}\right]\leq(1-F_{\inf}+\epsilon)E^{x}\left[R^{j}_{n}\right]+N_{\epsilon},\ n>N_{\epsilon},\ x\in\mathcal{X}.

(2.7)

By this and (
2.5
),

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}E^{x}\left[R_{n}^{(j)}\right]}{n}\leq(1-F_{\inf}+\epsilon)(F_{\sup})^{j-1}(1-F_{\inf}).

Letting
\displaystyle\epsilon\to+0
,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}E^{x}\left[R_{n}^{(j)}\right]}{n}\leq(F_{\sup})^{j-1}(1-F_{\inf})^{2}.

We now deal with the lower bound.
By (
2.7
) and
\displaystyle R_{n}^{j}-R_{n}^{j+1}=R_{n}^{(j)}
,

(F_{\inf}-\epsilon)E^{x}\left[R^{j}_{n}\right]\leq E^{x}\left[R^{j+1}_{n}\right]+N_{\epsilon},\ n>N_{\epsilon},\ x\in\mathcal{X}.

Hence,

(F_{\inf}-\epsilon)\inf_{x\in\mathcal{X}}E^{x}\left[R^{j}_{n}\right]\leq\inf_{x\in\mathcal{X}}E^{x}\left[R^{j+1}_{n}\right]+N_{\epsilon},\ n>N_{\epsilon}.

Dividing by
\displaystyle n
and letting
\displaystyle n\to\infty
,

(F_{\inf}-\epsilon)\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\inf_{x\in\mathcal{X}}E^{x}\left[R_{n}^{j}\right]}{n}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\inf_{x\in\mathcal{X}}E^{x}\left[R_{n}^{j+1}\right]}{n}.

Letting
\displaystyle\epsilon\to+0
,

F_{\inf}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\inf_{x\in\mathcal{X}}E^{x}\left[R_{n}^{j}\right]}{n}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\inf_{x\in\mathcal{X}}E^{x}\left[R_{n}^{j+1}\right]}{n}.

By using the last-exit decomposition as in the proof of
[
13
, Theorem 1.2]
,
we obtain that

E^{x}\left[R_{n}\right]\geq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\inf_{y\in\mathcal{X}}P^{y}(T_{y}\geq k)\geq n\inf_{y\in\mathcal{X}}P^{y}(T_{y}=\infty)=n(1-F_{\sup}).

(2.8)

It follows that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\inf_{x\in\mathcal{X}}E^{x}\left[R_{n}\right]}{n}\geq 1-F_{\sup}.

Therefore, by induction on
\displaystyle j
,

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\inf_{x\in\mathcal{X}}E^{x}\left[R_{n}^{j}\right]}{n}\geq(F_{\inf})^{j-1}(1-F_{\sup}).

By this and (
2.3
),

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\inf_{x\in\mathcal{X}}E^{x}\left[R_{n}^{(j)}\right]}{n}\geq(F_{\inf})^{j-1}(1-F_{\sup})^{2}.

This completes the proof.

2.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

We first deal with the upper estimates.

Let

T^{j}_{k,n}\coloneqq\left|\left\{x\in\mathcal{X}\colon\ell(kn,x)-\ell((k-1)n,x)\geq j\right\}\right|

and

T^{(j)}_{k,n}\coloneqq\left|\left\{x\in\mathcal{X}\colon\ell(kn,x)-\ell((k-1)n,x)=j\right\}\right|.

Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{k,n}\coloneqq\left\{X_{i}\colon(k-1)n+1\leq i\leq kn\right\}

and

E_{m,n}\coloneqq\left|\bigcup_{k_{1},k_{2}\in\{1,\dots,m\},k_{1}\neq k_{2}}\mathcal{R}_{k_{1},n}\cap\mathcal{R}_{k_{2},n}\right|.

(2.9)

Then,

R_{mn}^{j}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{m}T^{j}_{k,n}+E_{m,n},\ \textup{ and }\ R_{mn}^{(j)}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{m}T^{(j)}_{k,n}+E_{m,n}.

For notational convenience, let

F^{(u)}_{j}\coloneqq(F_{\sup})^{j-1}(1-F_{\inf}),\ \textup{ and }\ F^{(u)}_{(j)}\coloneqq(F_{\sup})^{j-1}(1-F_{\inf})^{2}.

Then, for every
\displaystyle\epsilon>0
,

P^{x}\left(R_{mn}^{j}\geq mn(F_{j}^{(u)}+\epsilon)\right)\leq P^{x}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}T^{j}_{k,n}\geq mn\left(F_{j}^{(u)}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right)\right)+P^{x}\left(E_{m,n}\geq mn\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right).

We first give an upper bound for
\displaystyle\displaystyle P^{x}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}T^{j}_{k,n}\geq mn\left(F_{j}^{(u)}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right)\right)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For every
\displaystyle\epsilon>0
, there exists
\displaystyle\theta(\epsilon)>0
such that
for every random variable
\displaystyle Y
on a probability space satisfying
\displaystyle|Y|\leq 1
,

E\left[\exp(\theta(\epsilon)Y)\right]\leq 1+\theta(\epsilon)(E[Y]+\epsilon)\leq\exp\left(\theta(\epsilon)(E[Y]+\epsilon)\right).

(2.10)

Proof.

By the Lebesgue convergence theorem and the fact that
\displaystyle E[Y^{n}]\leq 1
,

E\left[\exp(\theta(\epsilon)Y)\right]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\theta^{n}}{n!}E[Y^{n}]\leq 1+\theta E[Y]+\frac{\theta^{2}}{2}\exp(\theta).

Let
\displaystyle\theta(\epsilon)
be a positive constant such that

\displaystyle\theta\exp(\theta)=\epsilon
.
Then, using the inequality
\displaystyle 1+x\leq\exp(x)
,
we have (
2.10
).
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

For every
\displaystyle\epsilon>0
, there exists
\displaystyle N(\epsilon)\in\mathbb{N}
such that
for every
\displaystyle n\geq N(\epsilon)
,

\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}E^{x}\left[\exp\left(\frac{\theta(\epsilon/2)}{n}R_{n}^{j}\right)\right]\leq\exp\left(\theta(\epsilon/2)(F_{j}^{(u)}+\epsilon)\right),

(2.11)

where
\displaystyle\theta(\cdot)
is the function appearing in Lemma
2.1
.

Proof.

Since
\displaystyle 0\leq R^{j}_{n}\leq n
,
by Lemma
2.1
,

\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}E^{x}\left[\exp\left(\frac{\theta(\epsilon/2)}{n}R_{n}^{j}\right)\right]\leq\exp\left(\theta(\epsilon/2)\left(\frac{E^{x}\left[R_{n}^{j}\right]}{n}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right)\right).

By Theorem
1.1
,
there exists
\displaystyle N(\epsilon)\in\mathbb{N}
such that
for every
\displaystyle n\geq N(\epsilon)
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{E^{x}\left[R_{n}^{j}\right]}{n}\leq F_{j}^{(u)}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}
.
Hence,
(
2.11
) holds for every
\displaystyle n\geq N(\epsilon)
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

For every
\displaystyle\epsilon>0
, there exist
\displaystyle C(\epsilon)>0
and
\displaystyle N_{1}(\epsilon)\in\mathbb{N}
such that
for every
\displaystyle n\geq N_{1}(\epsilon)
and every
\displaystyle m\in\mathbb{N}
,

\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}P^{x}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}T^{j}_{k,n}\geq mn\left(F_{j}^{(u)}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right)\right)\leq\exp\left(-C(\epsilon)m\right).

Proof.

By the Markov property, for every
\displaystyle\lambda>0
and
\displaystyle x\in\mathcal{X}
,

P^{x}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}T^{j}_{k,n}\geq mn\left(F_{j}^{(u)}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right)\right)\leq\exp\left(-\lambda mn\left(F_{j}^{(u)}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right)\right)E^{x}\left[\prod_{k=1}^{m}\exp\left(\lambda T^{j}_{k,n}\right)\right]

\leq\left(\exp\left(-\lambda n\left(F_{j}^{(u)}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right)\right)\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}E^{x}\left[\exp(\lambda R^{j}_{n})\right]\right)^{m}.

Let
\displaystyle n\geq N(\epsilon/4)
and
\displaystyle\lambda=\frac{1}{n}\theta(\epsilon/8)
.
Then, by Lemma
2.2
,

\exp\left(-\lambda n\left(F_{j}^{(u)}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right)\right)\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}E^{x}\left[\exp(\lambda R^{j}_{n})\right]\leq\exp\left(-\frac{\epsilon}{4}\theta(\epsilon/8)\right).

Thus the assertion holds for
\displaystyle\displaystyle C(\epsilon)\coloneqq\frac{\epsilon}{4}\theta(\epsilon/8)
and
\displaystyle N_{1}(\epsilon)\coloneqq N(\epsilon/4)
.
∎

Second, we give an upper bound for
\displaystyle\displaystyle P^{x}\left(E_{m,n}\geq mn\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right)
.
Let

\mathcal{A}_{k}^{\infty}\coloneqq\left\{X_{\ell}\neq X_{k},\ell\geq k+1\right\}=\bigcap_{n\geq k+1}\mathcal{A}_{k}^{n}.

Then, by the same argument as in
[
14
, Proof of Lemma]
,
it holds that for
\displaystyle n^{\prime}<n
,

E_{m,n}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{mn}{\bf 1}_{\mathcal{A}_{k}^{k+n^{\prime}}\setminus\mathcal{A}_{k}^{\infty}}+mn^{\prime}.

Henceforth, we denote the integer part of a real number
\displaystyle x
by
\displaystyle\lfloor x\rfloor
.
For
\displaystyle n\geq 2
, let

n^{\prime}\coloneqq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{\log n}\right\rfloor.

(2.12)

Then, there exists
\displaystyle N_{2}(\epsilon)\in\mathbb{N}
such that for every
\displaystyle n\geq N_{2}(\epsilon)
,
\displaystyle n^{\prime}/n<\epsilon/4
.
Hence, it holds that for every
\displaystyle n\geq N_{2}(\epsilon)
and every
\displaystyle m\geq 1
,

P^{x}\left(E_{m,n}\geq mn\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right)\leq P^{x}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{mn}{\bf 1}_{\mathcal{A}_{k}^{k+n^{\prime}}\setminus\mathcal{A}_{k}^{\infty}}\geq mn\frac{\epsilon}{4}\right)\leq\frac{4}{\epsilon}\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}P^{x}(n^{\prime}<T_{x}<\infty).

(2.13)

By
\displaystyle(U_{1})
, there exists a constant
\displaystyle C_{0}
such that

\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}P^{x}(n<T_{x}<\infty)\leq C_{0}(\log n)^{-1-\delta},\ \ n\geq 1.

(2.14)

Thus,
for every
\displaystyle\epsilon>0
, every
\displaystyle n\geq N_{1}(\epsilon)+N_{2}(\epsilon)
and every
\displaystyle m\in\mathbb{N}
,
it follows that

P^{x}\left(R_{mn}^{j}\geq mn(F_{j}^{(u)}+\epsilon)\right)\leq\exp\left(-C(\epsilon)m\right)+\frac{4C_{0}}{\epsilon}(\log n-\log\log n)^{-1-\delta}.

Let
\displaystyle\eta>0
.

For
\displaystyle m_{k}=r_{k}=\lfloor(1+\eta)^{k/2}\rfloor
,

\exp\left(-C(\epsilon)m_{k}\right)+\frac{4C_{0}}{\epsilon}(\log r_{k}-\log\log r_{k})^{-1-\delta}=O\left(k^{-1-\delta}\right),\ k\to\infty.

Let
\displaystyle a_{k}\coloneqq m_{k}r_{k}
.
Then,

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}P^{x}\left(R_{a_{k}}^{j}\geq a_{k}(F_{j}^{(u)}+\epsilon)\right)<+\infty,

and by the Borel-Cantelli lemma,

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{R_{a_{k}}^{j}}{a_{k}}\leq F_{j}^{(u)}+\epsilon,\ \textup{ $\displaystyle P^{x}$-a.s.}

(2.15)

For
\displaystyle a_{k}\leq n\leq a_{k+1}
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{R^{j}_{n}}{n}\leq\frac{a_{k+1}}{a_{k}}\frac{R_{a_{k+1}}^{j}}{a_{k+1}}
.
By this estimate and the equality
\displaystyle\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{a_{k+1}}{a_{k}}=1+\eta
,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{R_{n}^{j}}{n}\leq(1+\eta)(F_{j}^{(u)}+\epsilon),\ \textup{ $\displaystyle P^{x}$-a.s.}

By letting
\displaystyle\eta\to 0
and
\displaystyle\epsilon\to 0
,
we see that (
1.1
) holds
\displaystyle P^{x}
-a.s.

We now deal with
\displaystyle R^{(j)}_{n}
.
In the same manner as in the derivation of (
2.15
), we obtain that

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{R_{a_{k}}^{(j)}}{a_{k}}\leq F_{(j)}^{(u)}+\epsilon,\ \textup{ $\displaystyle P^{x}$-a.s.}

Since

R_{n}^{(j)}\leq R_{a_{k+1}}^{(j)}+a_{k+1}-a_{k},\ a_{k}\leq n\leq a_{k+1},

it holds that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{R_{n}^{(j)}}{n}\leq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{a_{k+1}}{a_{k}}\frac{R_{a_{k+1}}^{(j)}}{a_{k+1}}+\frac{a_{k+1}-a_{k}}{a_{k}}\leq(1+\eta)(F_{(j)}^{(u)}+\epsilon)+\eta,\ \textup{ $\displaystyle P^{x}$-a.s.}

By letting
\displaystyle\eta\to 0
and
\displaystyle\epsilon\to 0
,
we see that (
1.2
) holds
\displaystyle P^{x}
-a.s.

We now deal with the lower estimates.

We see that

R_{mn}^{j}\geq\sum_{k=1}^{m}(T^{j}_{k,n}-E_{m,n}),\ \textup{ and }\ R_{mn}^{(j)}\geq\sum_{k=1}^{m}(T^{(j)}_{k,n}-E_{m,n}).

For ease of notation, let

F^{(l)}_{j}\coloneqq(F_{\inf})^{j-1}(1-F_{\sup}),\ \textup{ and }\ F^{(l)}_{(j)}\coloneqq(F_{\inf})^{j-1}(1-F_{\sup})^{2}.

Then, for every
\displaystyle\epsilon>0
,

P^{x}\left(R_{mn}^{j}\leq mn(F_{j}^{(l)}-\epsilon)\right)\leq P^{x}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}T^{j}_{k,n}\leq mn\left(F_{j}^{(l)}-\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right)\right)+P^{x}\left(E_{m,n}\geq n\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right).

We first give an upper bound for
\displaystyle\displaystyle P^{x}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}T^{j}_{k,n}\leq mn\left(F_{j}^{(l)}-\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right)\right)
.
By Theorem
1.1
,
it holds that
for every
\displaystyle\epsilon>0
,
there exists
\displaystyle N(\epsilon)\in\mathbb{N}
such that
for every
\displaystyle n\geq N(\epsilon)
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{E^{x}\left[R_{n}^{j}\right]}{n}\geq F_{j}^{(l)}-\frac{\epsilon}{4}
, which is equivalent with
\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{E^{x}\left[n-R_{n}^{j}\right]}{n}\leq 1-F_{j}^{(l)}+\frac{\epsilon}{4}
.

In the same manner as in the proof of Lemma
2.2
,
we can show that
for every
\displaystyle\epsilon>0
, there exists
\displaystyle N(\epsilon)\in\mathbb{N}
such that
for every
\displaystyle n\geq N(\epsilon)
,

\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}E^{x}\left[\exp\left(\frac{\theta(\epsilon/8)}{n}(n-R_{n}^{j})\right)\right]\leq\exp\left(\theta(\epsilon/8)\left(1-F_{j}^{(l)}+\frac{3\epsilon}{8}\right)\right).

Since
\displaystyle T^{j}_{k,n}\leq n
,
by the Markov property, for every
\displaystyle\lambda>0
and
\displaystyle x\in\mathcal{X}
,

P^{x}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}T^{j}_{k,n}\leq mn\left(F_{j}^{(l)}-\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right)\right)=P^{x}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}(n-T^{j}_{k,n})\geq mn\left(1-F_{j}^{(l)}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right)\right)

\leq\exp\left(-\lambda mn\left(1-F_{j}^{(l)}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right)\right)E^{x}\left[\prod_{k=1}^{m}\exp\left(\lambda(n-T^{j}_{k,n})\right)\right]

\leq\left(\exp\left(-\lambda n\left(1-F_{j}^{(l)}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right)\right)\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}E^{x}\left[\exp\left(\lambda(n-R^{j}_{n})\right)\right]\right)^{m}.

In the same manner as in the proof of Lemma
2.3
,
we can show that
for every
\displaystyle\epsilon>0
, there exist
\displaystyle C(\epsilon)>0
and
\displaystyle N_{3}(\epsilon)\in\mathbb{N}
such that
for every
\displaystyle n\geq N_{3}(\epsilon)
and every
\displaystyle m\in\mathbb{N}
,

\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}P^{x}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}T^{j}_{k,n}\leq mn\left(F_{j}^{(l)}-\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right)\right)\leq\exp\left(-C(\epsilon)m\right).

Second, we give an upper bound for
\displaystyle\displaystyle P^{x}\left(E_{m,n}\geq n\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right)
.
Let
\displaystyle n^{\prime}
be as in (
2.12
).
As in (
2.13
), by using
\displaystyle(U_{1})
,
we see that there exists
\displaystyle N_{4}(\epsilon)\in\mathbb{N}
such that for every
\displaystyle n\geq N_{4}(\epsilon)
and
\displaystyle m\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying
\displaystyle mn^{\prime}/n<\epsilon/4
,

P^{x}\left(E_{m,n}\geq n\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right)\leq\frac{2m}{\epsilon-2mn^{\prime}/n}\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}P^{x}(n^{\prime}<T_{x}<\infty).

Recall (
2.14
).
Thus we obtain that
for every
\displaystyle\epsilon>0
,
for every
\displaystyle n\geq N_{3}(\epsilon)+N_{4}(\epsilon)
and every
\displaystyle m\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying
\displaystyle mn^{\prime}/n<\epsilon/4
,

P^{x}\left(R_{mn}^{j}\leq mn(F_{j}^{(l)}-\epsilon)\right)\leq\exp\left(-C(\epsilon)m\right)+\frac{4C_{0}}{\epsilon}m(\log n-\log\log n)^{-1-\delta}.

Let
\displaystyle\eta>0
.
For
\displaystyle m_{k}=\lfloor(\log k)^{2}\rfloor
and
\displaystyle r_{k}=\lfloor(1+\eta)^{k}\rfloor
,
it holds that
\displaystyle m_{k}(r_{k})^{\prime}/r_{k}<\epsilon/4
for large
\displaystyle k
, and,

\exp\left(-C(\epsilon)m_{k}\right)+\frac{4C_{0}}{\epsilon}m_{k}(\log r_{k}-\log\log r_{k})^{-1-\delta}=O\left(k^{-1-\frac{\delta}{2}}\right),\ k\to\infty.

Let
\displaystyle b_{k}\coloneqq m_{k}r_{k}
.
Then,

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}P^{x}\left(R_{b_{k}}^{j}\leq b_{k}(F_{j}^{(l)}-\epsilon)\right)<+\infty.

Hence, by the Borel-Cantelli lemma,

\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{R_{b_{k}}^{j}}{b_{k}}\geq F_{j}^{(l)}-\epsilon,\ \textup{ $\displaystyle P^{x}$-a.s.}

It holds that for
\displaystyle b_{k}\leq n\leq b_{k+1}
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{R^{j}_{n}}{n}\geq\frac{b_{k}}{b_{k+1}}\frac{R_{b_{k}}^{j}}{b_{k}}
.
By this estimate and the equality
\displaystyle\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{b_{k}}{b_{k+1}}=\frac{1}{1+\eta}
,

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{R_{n}^{j}}{n}\geq\frac{F_{j}^{(l)}-\epsilon}{1+\eta},\ \textup{ $\displaystyle P^{x}$-a.s.}

(2.16)

Letting
\displaystyle\eta\to 0
and
\displaystyle\epsilon\to 0
,
we see that (
1.3
) holds
\displaystyle P^{x}
-a.s.

We now deal with
\displaystyle R^{(j)}_{n}
.
In the same manner as in the derivation of (
2.16
),

\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{R_{b_{k}}^{(j)}}{b_{k}}\geq F_{(j)}^{(l)}-\epsilon,\ \textup{ $\displaystyle P^{x}$-a.s.}

Since

R_{n}^{(j)}\geq R_{b_{k}}^{(j)}-(b_{k+1}-b_{k}),\ b_{k}\leq n\leq b_{k+1},

it holds that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{R_{n}^{(j)}}{n}\geq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{b_{k}}{b_{k+1}}\frac{R_{b_{k}}^{(j)}}{b_{k}}-\frac{b_{k+1}-b_{k}}{b_{k}}\geq\frac{F_{(j)}^{(l)}-\epsilon}{1+\eta}-\eta,\ \textup{ $\displaystyle P^{x}$-a.s.}

Letting
\displaystyle\eta\to 0
and
\displaystyle\epsilon\to 0
,
we see that (
1.4
) holds
\displaystyle P^{x}
-a.s.

3.
Functions of the local times

Let
\displaystyle f
be a non-negative function on
\displaystyle\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
such that
\displaystyle f(0)=0
.
Let

G_{n}(f)\coloneqq\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}f(\ell(n,x)).

Let

C_{\inf}(f)\coloneqq\sum_{j\geq 1}f(j)(F_{\inf})^{j-1}(1-F_{\sup})^{2}

and

C_{\sup}(f)\coloneqq\sum_{j\geq 1}f(j)(F_{\sup})^{j-1}(1-F_{\inf})^{2}.

Since
\displaystyle f(0)=0
, we see that

G_{n}(f)=\sum_{j\geq 1}f(j)R_{n}^{(j)}.

The following theorem extends
[
3
, Theorem 2]
and the
\displaystyle L^{2}
-convergence part of
[
1
, Theorem 1]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\displaystyle p\geq 1
.
Assume that
\displaystyle(U_{1})
holds and

\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{f(j)^{p}}{j^{p-1}}(F_{\sup})^{j}<\infty.

Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}E^{x}\left[\left(\left(\frac{G_{n}(f)}{n}-C_{\sup}(f)\right)_{+}\right)^{p}\right]=0

(3.1)

and

\lim_{n\to\infty}E^{x}\left[\left(\left(\frac{G_{n}(f)}{n}-C_{\inf}(f)\right)_{-}\right)^{p}\right]=0.

(3.2)

Proof.

We remark that for every
\displaystyle a,b\in\mathbb{R}
,

((a+b)_{+})^{p}\leq(a_{+}+b_{+})^{p}\leq 2^{p-1}((a_{+})^{p}+(b_{+})^{p}).

Let
\displaystyle f_{N}(j)\coloneqq f(j){\bf 1}_{\{1,\dots,N\}}(j)
.
Then, using
\displaystyle C_{\sup}(f)\geq C_{\sup}(f_{N})
,

\left(\frac{G_{n}(f)}{n}-C_{\sup}(f)\right)_{+}\leq\left(\frac{G_{n}(f_{N})}{n}-C_{\sup}(f)\right)_{+}+\frac{G_{n}(f)-G_{n}(f_{N})}{n}.

By (
2.4
),

\sup_{x}E^{x}\left[R_{n}^{(j)}\right]\leq\sup_{x}E^{x}\left[R_{n}^{j}\right]\leq(F_{\sup})^{j-1}\sup_{x}E^{x}\left[R_{n}\right]\leq n(F_{\sup})^{j-1}.

(3.3)

Let
\displaystyle q
be the conjugate exponent of
\displaystyle p
, i.e.,
\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
, with the convention
\displaystyle q=\infty
if
\displaystyle p=1
.
Using the Hölder inequality and
\displaystyle n=\sum_{j\geq 1}jR_{n}^{(j)}
,

\sum_{j\geq N+1}f(j)R_{n}^{(j)}=\sum_{j\geq N+1}\frac{f(j)}{j}(jR_{n}^{(j)})^{1/p}\cdot(jR_{n}^{(j)})^{1/q}\leq n^{1/q}\left(\sum_{j\geq N+1}\frac{f(j)^{p}}{j^{p-1}}R_{n}^{(j)}\right)^{1/p}.

Hence,

\left(\frac{G_{n}(f)}{n}-\frac{G_{n}(f_{N})}{n}\right)^{p}\leq\sum_{j\geq N+1}\frac{f(j)^{p}}{j^{p-1}}\frac{R_{n}^{(j)}}{n}.

Using this and (
3.3
),

E^{x}\left[\left(\frac{G_{n}(f)}{n}-\frac{G_{n}(f_{N})}{n}\right)^{p}\right]\leq\sum_{j\geq N+1}\frac{f(j)^{p}}{j^{p-1}}(F_{\sup})^{j-1}.

Since
\displaystyle\frac{G_{n}(f_{N})}{n}\leq\max_{1\leq j\leq N}f(j)
,
(
1.2
) and the Lebesgue convergence theorem yield that

\lim_{n\to\infty}E^{x}\left[\left(\left(\frac{G_{n}(f_{N})}{n}-C_{\sup}(f_{N})\right)_{+}\right)^{p}\right]=0.

Using this and
\displaystyle C_{\sup}(f_{N})\leq C_{\sup}(f)
, we obtain that

\lim_{n\to\infty}E^{x}\left[\left(\left(\frac{G_{n}(f_{N})}{n}-C_{\sup}(f)\right)_{+}\right)^{p}\right]=0.

Hence,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}E^{x}\left[\left(\left(\frac{G_{n}(f)}{n}-C_{\sup}(f)\right)_{+}\right)^{p}\right]\leq 2^{p-1}\sum_{j\geq N+1}\frac{f(j)^{p}}{j^{p-1}}(F_{\sup})^{j-1}.

Letting
\displaystyle N\to\infty
, we have (
3.1
).
The same argument gives (
3.2
).
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Assume that
\displaystyle(U_{1})
holds and
\displaystyle F_{\inf}<1
.
Then for every
\displaystyle x\in\mathcal{X}
,

C_{\inf}(f)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{G_{n}(f)}{n}\leq C_{\sup}(f),\ \textup{ $\displaystyle P^{x}$-a.s.}

(3.4)

Proof.

The lower bound follows from the non-negativity of
\displaystyle f
and Theorem
1.2
.
If
\displaystyle C_{\sup}(f)=\infty
, the upper bound is obvious.
Assume that
\displaystyle C_{\sup}(f)<\infty
.
By Theorem
3.1
for
\displaystyle p=1
,
there exists a sequence
\displaystyle(n_{k})_{k}
such that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\left(\frac{G_{n_{k}}(f)}{n_{k}}-C_{\sup}(f)\right)_{+}=0,\ \textup{ $\displaystyle P^{x}$-a.s.}

This is equivalent to

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{G_{n_{k}}(f)}{n_{k}}\leq C_{\sup}(f),\ \textup{ $\displaystyle P^{x}$-a.s.}

This implies the upper bound.
∎

If
\displaystyle f(j)=j
and
\displaystyle F_{\inf}=1
, then
\displaystyle G_{n}(f)=n
and
\displaystyle C_{\sup}(f)=0
so that (
3.4
) fails.

The following lower bound on the expectation plays a role similar to that of
[
3
, Lemma 3]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\displaystyle x\in\mathcal{X},n\geq 1
.
Then,

(i)
\displaystyle E^{x}\left[R_{n}^{(1)}\right]\geq(1-F_{\sup})^{2}n
.

(ii) If
\displaystyle j\geq 2
, then for every
\displaystyle\ell\in\{1,\dots,n\}
,

E^{x}\left[R_{n}^{(j)}\right]\geq(1-F_{\sup})^{2}\ell F_{\inf}\left(\left\lfloor\frac{n-\ell}{j-1}\right\rfloor\right)^{j-1}.

Proof.

For
\displaystyle y\in\mathcal{X}
,
let
\displaystyle T_{y}^{(1)}\coloneqq T_{y}
and

T_{y}^{(j)}\coloneqq\inf\left\{n>T_{y}^{(j-1)}\colon X_{n}=y\right\},\ j\geq 2.

By the Markov property, we obtain that

P^{y}\left(T_{y}^{(j)}\leq n\right)\geq P^{y}\left(T_{y}\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{j}\right\rfloor\right)^{j},\ j\geq 1,n\geq 1,y\in\mathcal{X}.

(3.5)

We also see that

E^{x}\left[R_{n}^{j}\right]=\sum_{y\in\mathcal{X}}P^{x}\left(T_{y}^{(j)}\leq n\right).

By this and the Markov property,

E^{x}\left[R_{n}^{j}\right]=\sum_{y\in\mathcal{X}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}P^{x}\left(T_{y}^{(j)}\leq n,T_{y}=k\right)=\sum_{y\in\mathcal{X}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}P^{x}\left(T_{y}=k\right)P^{y}\left(T_{y}^{(j-1)}\leq n-k\right).

By this and (
3.5
),

E^{x}\left[R_{n}^{j}\right]\geq\sum_{y\in\mathcal{X}}P^{x}\left(T_{y}\leq\ell\right)P^{y}\left(T_{y}^{(j-1)}\leq n-\ell\right)\geq E^{x}\left[R_{\ell}\right]F_{\inf}\left(\left\lfloor\frac{n-\ell}{j-1}\right\rfloor\right)^{j-1}.

Applying (
2.3
) and (
2.8
), we obtain the assertion.
∎

We now consider the following stronger uniform tail condition
\displaystyle(U_{2})
:

\sup_{x}P^{x}(n<T_{x}<\infty)=O(n^{-\delta}),\ n\to\infty,\delta>0.

This condition was considered in
[
10
, Proposition 5.7]
.
The simple random walk on
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}^{d},d\geq 3
, satisfies this condition.

The following corresponds to
[
3
, Remark 3]
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Assume that
\displaystyle(U_{2})
holds, that
\displaystyle 0<F_{\inf}\leq F_{\sup}<1
, and that
\displaystyle p\geq 1
.
Let
\displaystyle\alpha>p-2+\frac{p-1}{\delta}
.
Let
\displaystyle f(0)\coloneqq 0
and
\displaystyle f(j)\coloneqq j^{\alpha/p}F_{\inf}^{-j/p}
for
\displaystyle j\geq 1
.
Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}E^{x}\left[\left(\frac{G_{n}(f)}{n}\right)^{p}\right]=\infty.

Proof.

We first remark that

E^{x}\left[\left(\frac{G_{n}(f)}{n}\right)^{p}\right]\geq\frac{1}{n^{p}}\sum_{j\geq 1}f(j)^{p}E^{x}\left[\left(R_{n}^{(j)}\right)^{p}\right]\geq\frac{1}{n^{p}}\sum_{j\geq 2}f(j)^{p}E^{x}\left[R_{n}^{(j)}\right].

By Lemma
3.3
,

\frac{1}{n^{p}}\sum_{j\geq 2}f(j)^{p}E^{x}\left[R_{n}^{(j)}\right]\geq\frac{(1-F_{\sup})^{2}}{n^{p}}\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor\sum_{j\geq 2}j^{\alpha}F_{\inf}^{-j}F_{\inf}\left(\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2(j-1)}\right\rfloor\right)^{j-1}.

By
\displaystyle(U_{2})
,
there exists
\displaystyle C>0
such that for every
\displaystyle n\geq 1
and every
\displaystyle j\geq 2
,

F_{\inf}\left(\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2(j-1)}\right\rfloor\right)\geq\left(1-C\left(\frac{j-1}{n}\right)^{\delta}\right)_{+}F_{\inf}.

Hence it suffices to show that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n^{p-1}}\sum_{j\geq 2}j^{\alpha}\left(\left(1-C\left(\frac{j-1}{n}\right)^{\delta}\right)_{+}\right)^{j-1}=\infty.

(3.6)

Let
\displaystyle a>0
such that
\displaystyle Ca^{\delta+1}<1/2
.
If
\displaystyle j-1\leq\lfloor an^{\delta/(1+\delta)}\rfloor
, then

\left(1-C\left(\frac{j-1}{n}\right)^{\delta}\right)^{j-1}\geq 1-C\frac{(j-1)^{1+\delta}}{n^{\delta}}\geq\frac{1}{2}.

Hence

\sum_{j\geq 2}j^{\alpha}\left(\left(1-C\left(\frac{j-1}{n}\right)^{\delta}\right)_{+}\right)^{j-1}\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=2}^{\lfloor an^{\delta/(1+\delta)}\rfloor+1}j^{\alpha}

and the right-hand side grows on the scale of
\displaystyle n^{(\alpha+1)\delta/(\delta+1)}
.
By the assumption,
\displaystyle(\alpha+1)\delta>(p-1)(\delta+1)
and we obtain (
3.6
).
∎

Remark 3.5
.

Suppose that the assumptions of Proposition
3.4
hold.
If, additionally,
\displaystyle p>1
and
\displaystyle F_{\sup}<F_{\inf}^{1/p}
,
then
\displaystyle C_{\sup}(f)<\infty
and

\lim_{n\to\infty}E^{x}\left[\left(\frac{G_{n}(f)}{n}-C_{\sup}(f)\right)_{+}\right]=\lim_{n\to\infty}E^{x}\left[\left(\frac{G_{n}(f)}{n}-C_{\inf}(f)\right)_{-}\right]=0.

If
\displaystyle F_{\inf}=F_{\sup}<1
and
\displaystyle p>1
, then
\displaystyle F_{\sup}<F_{\inf}^{1/p}
.

In general, the strict inequality
\displaystyle F_{\inf}<F_{\sup}
can occur.
See
[
13
, Theorem 1.3]
.
By the same argument as in the proof of
[
13
, Theorem 1.3]
,
one can show that

(F_{\inf})^{j-1}(1-F_{\sup})=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\inf_{x\in\mathcal{X}}E^{x}\left[R_{n}^{j}\right]}{n}

<\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}E^{x}\left[R_{n}^{j}\right]}{n}=(F_{\sup})^{j-1}(1-F_{\inf})

for each
\displaystyle j
.

4.
Examples

We give a sufficient condition for
\displaystyle(U_{1})
.
Let
\displaystyle p_{n}(x,y)\coloneqq P^{x}(X_{n}=y)
.
Then we have the trivial estimate

\displaystyle P^{x}(n<T_{x}<\infty)\leq\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}p_{k}(x,x)
.

Specifically, if
there exists a constant
\displaystyle C_{0}
such that

p_{n}(x,x)\leq\frac{C_{0}}{n(\log(n+1))^{2+\delta}}

(4.1)

holds for every
\displaystyle x\in\mathcal{X},n\geq 1
,
then
\displaystyle(U_{1})
holds.

Proposition 4.1
.

Assume that
\displaystyle(X_{n})_{n}
is the simple random walk on an infinite connected simple graph with bounded degrees.
Then the Nash-type inequality (
4.1
) is stable under rough isometries between graphs.

Proof.

We apply results of Tessera
[
16
]
.
Let
\displaystyle\gamma(t)\coloneqq C_{0}t^{-1}(\log(t+1))^{-2-\delta}
.
Then there exists an increasing positive function
\displaystyle\varphi
such that

\displaystyle t=\int_{0}^{1/\gamma(t)}\varphi(v)^{2}\frac{dv}{v}
for every
\displaystyle t\geq 1
.
Moreover,

\displaystyle\varphi(v)\sim\frac{\sqrt{C_{0}v}}{\log(v+1)^{1+\delta/2}}
as
\displaystyle v\to\infty
.

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{1}
and
\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{2}
be two infinite connected simple graphs with bounded degrees.
Assume that (
4.1
) holds for the simple random walk on
\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{1}
.
By
[
16
, Theorem 3.5 (ii)]
,
the Sobolev inequality associated with
\displaystyle\varphi
holds for
\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{1}
.
By
[
16
, Theorem 8.1]
,
the Sobolev inequality is stable under rough isometries, and hence,
the Sobolev inequality associated with
\displaystyle\varphi
also holds for
\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{2}
.
By
[
16
, Theorem 3.5 (i)]
,
(
4.1
) also holds for the simple random walk on
\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{2}
.
∎

The Laakso-type graph constructed in Murugan
[
11
, Theorem 5.13]
is an infinite connected simple graph with bounded degrees such that
there exist two constants
\displaystyle C_{1}
and
\displaystyle C_{2}
such that the inequalities

\frac{C_{1}}{n(\log(n+1))^{2+\delta}}\leq p_{n}(x,x)+p_{n+1}(x,x)\leq\frac{C_{2}}{n(\log(n+1))^{2+\delta}}

(4.2)

hold for every
\displaystyle x\in\mathcal{X},n\geq 1
.

Every infinite, connected, locally finite, vertex-transitive graph with polynomial volume growth does not satisfy (
4.2
),
because every such graph is roughly isometric to a Cayley graph of a virtually nilpotent group by Trofimov
[
17
]
and the claim follows from Hebisch and Saloff-Coste
[
9
]
.

There are examples of random walks on
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}
with long-range jumps satisfying (
4.2
).
By Murugan and Saloff-Coste
[
12
, Theorem 1.1]
,
if there exist two constants
\displaystyle C_{3}
and
\displaystyle C_{4}
such that the inequalities

C_{3}\frac{(\log(e+|x-y|))^{2+\delta}}{(1+|x-y|)^{2}}\leq p(x,y)=p(y,x)\leq C_{4}\frac{(\log(e+|x-y|))^{2+\delta}}{(1+|x-y|)^{2}}

hold for every
\displaystyle x,y\in\mathcal{X}
,
then (
4.2
) holds.
