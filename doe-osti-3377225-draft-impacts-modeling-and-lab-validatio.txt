Executive Summary of CRADA Work:
Chimney draft is a significant source of variability in real-world wood heater performance, yet
laboratory tests conducted for certification do not capture this variability. Because draft varies
with climate, chimney height, and home conditions, a heater that performs well in the lab can
perform quite differently when installed in a home. Until now, wood heater manufacturers and
installers have lacked the tools needed to anticipate how draft will vary across real installations
or to advise on corrective measures when draft is too low or too high.

The goal of this project was to develop and validate an open-source draft prediction tool for
cordwood heater chimney systems. Over 18 months, Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory
(LBNL) and the Hearth, Patio & Barbecue Association (HPBA) collaborated to build a physics-
based, Python tool that predicts how chimney draft evolves during operation, from ignition
through steady burning. HPBA convened a stakeholder group of manufacturers and venting
experts who provided feedback throughout development. LBNL validated the tool against
laboratory measurements from a catalytic and a non-catalytic cordwood heater operated
across a range of chimney heights and room-pressure conditions.

This research advances knowledge in this area by providing a modern, validated, openly
available tool for predicting transient chimney draft in cordwood heater installations. Across all
operating conditions, the tool reproduced the measured draft and accurately predicted the
flue-gas temperatures. The tool is freely available as open-source software, with the source
code at https://github.com/LBNL-Combustion/ and an interactive web-based version at
https://draft.lbl.gov/. This tool aims to support manufacturers with improving their designs to
anticipate potential draft conditions across climates and installation geometries, and support
installers with evaluating whether an existing or proposed venting configuration is likely to
perform well.

Summary of Research Results:

Overview
Wood heaters remain an important source of residential heat across much of the United States,
and their performance depends heavily on their chimney. Draft, the pressure difference that
helps move combustion gases up the chimney, is notoriously difficult to predict at the design or
installation stage. It depends on chimney geometry, indoor and outdoor temperatures, wind,
and the operating state of the heater. Wood heater installers have historically relied on rules of
thumb, manufacturer guidance, laboratory data, or trial-and-error adjustments to design and
optimize chimney systems. Existing chimney design tools are either proprietary, limited to gas
appliances, or assume the heater operates at a constant heat output, which fails to capture the
startup period most relevant to establishing good chimney draft.

This project set out to demonstrate that a chimney draft prediction tool for cordwood heaters
could be developed using a transient, physics-based model to provide accurate draft
predictions across the wide range of installation conditions encountered in U.S. homes. The
primary goal of this project was to develop and validate an interactive, open-source draft
prediction tool for residential cordwood heaters, and to make it freely available to
manufacturers, installers, and the broader community through a public-facing web interface.

Approach and Activities Over the Period of Performance
The 18-month project was conducted following the Statement of Work (SOW) between HPBA
and LBNL, and included a project management task and six technical tasks.

 1. Project management and monthly meetings (Task 1)
 2. Establish a stakeholder group (Task 2)
 a. HPBA convened a stakeholder group of manufacturers and leading installers that
 met on a recurring basis throughout the project. The group reviewed the
 proposed modeling approach and supplied feedback on tool design.
 3. Review published draft models and determine the best approach (Task 3)
 a. LBNL reviewed existing draft prediction frameworks, including VENT-II, FLUESIM,
 WOODSIM, and other simplified published models. The benefits and limitations
 of each were evaluated and presented to HPBA and the stakeholder group.
 b. LBNL proposed a hybrid, transient, one-dimensional approach that combines
 time-marching conservation equations for mass, momentum, and energy with
 multilayer wall conduction, internal radiation, and wind-induced outlet forcing.
 4. Develop a preliminary draft prediction model (Task 4)

All milestones and deliverables across the six technical tasks were completed, with one
modification. The original SOW called for publishing an easy-to-use chimney sizing table
alongside the prediction tool. As model development progressed and stakeholder engagement
deepened, it became clear that a single generic table could not reliably represent the diversity

of modern cordwood heaters. Each heater has a unique firebox geometry, air intake
configuration, and combustion temperature profile, all of which influence draft as strongly as
the chimney design. In place of static tables, we built additional flexibility into the tool so users
can upload their own measured or manufacturer-supplied combustion temperature profile and
air intake conditions, preserving the original intent of broad accessibility while ensuring results
remain physically meaningful for any given heater.

Technical Results and Outcomes
The model was evaluated against seven validation experiments spanning two heater types (one
catalytic and one non-catalytic cordwood heater), two chimney heights, and one imposed
room-depressurization condition. Validation data were drawn from laboratory measurements
collected at the LBNL test facility, supplemented by published literature and stakeholder-
supplied field measurements.

Across these tests, the model successfully reproduced how draft develops over the course of a
burn: the sharp pressure drop that occurs as the fire ignites and hot gases begin rising through
the chimney, the strongest draft reached shortly after, and the gradual settling toward stable
operation as the burn matures (see Figure 3). Direct comparison of predicted and measured
draft confirmed consistent performance across both heaters and chimney heights, with
agreement scores (R²) ranging from approximately 0.66 to 0.87 across the baseline tests. For
the depressurization test, in which the room was depressurized before startup to induce a
downward flow (downdraft), the model maintained close agreement throughout operation (R²
= 0.92).

Gas-temperature predictions near the chimney entrance and exit provided an independent
check on the underlying physics, capturing the main timing and magnitude of the measured
thermal response. The model treats the chimney as a one-dimensional system with prescribed
inlet gas temperatures rather than a coupled combustion simulation, and the largest
discrepancies occurred during the first few minutes after ignition, where rapidly evolving
combustion and chimney wall thermal mass are not fully resolved by this formulation. These
limitations represent natural avenues for future development. A full description of the
validation methodology, governing equations, and quantitative results will be provided in the
accompanying journal manuscript, which is being prepared for submission in June 2026.

The validation results from this project provide a strong foundation for continued refinement,
reinforcing the value of the underlying model. By making the resulting tool freely available, the
project equips American manufacturers and installers with a practical resource for improving
wood heater performance and customer satisfaction, supporting an industry that delivers
affordable, domestically sourced heat to households nationwide.

Products, Applications, and Technology Transfer
This project demonstrated that a transient, physics-based draft prediction model can reproduce
the dominant behavior of chimney draft in residential cordwood heaters with sufficient
accuracy to support design, installation, and troubleshooting decisions. The MC Chimney Draft
Tool is the first openly available transient draft prediction software tailored to residential
cordwood heaters and is suitable for use by manufacturers, installers, and researchers.

The primary application of the tool is to help manufacturers and installers anticipate draft
behavior across the diversity of climates and installation conditions encountered in U.S. homes,
thereby reducing the risk of backdraft and poor heater performance. Secondary applications
include support for heater design improvements, installer training, and research on residential
wood heaters.

The HPBA–LBNL partnership supported effective technology transfer from the national
laboratory to the wood heater industry. Specific products and transfer activities from the
project include the following:

 • Publicly accessible web-based draft prediction tool. The MC Chimney Draft Tool is
 hosted on a public-facing website and provides an interactive canvas on which users
 assemble a chimney system from standard components (straight pipe, elbows, tees,
 chimney sections, and terminations), specify indoor and outdoor temperatures, and run
 a transient draft simulation (see Figures 4 and 5). The interface was designed in
 consultation with the stakeholder group to match the workflow used by installers and
 manufacturers.

 • Open-source Python model. The underlying transient draft model is implemented in
 Python with a modular architecture and documentation. The code has been approved
 for open-source release by LBNL's Intellectual Property Office under a modified BSD-3-

 Clause license and is copyrighted by The Regents of the University of California, through
 Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory. This release enables continued use, extension,
 and independent verification by the research community while preserving the ability of
 LBNL and its collaborators to incorporate community-contributed enhancements.

 • Journal manuscript (in preparation). A peer-reviewed manuscript describing the model
 formulation, validation methodology, and quantitative results is in preparation for
 submission. Publication will provide the research community with a documented and
 citable basis for the tool.

 • Stakeholder engagement and outreach. Recurring stakeholder meetings convened by
 HPBA throughout the project served as an ongoing technology-transfer channel
 between LBNL and the wood heater industry, with manufacturers and installers
 providing feedback that shaped both the model and the interface. HPBA-supported
 presentations to the wood heater community further disseminated project results.

 • Foundation for future work. The model and tool provide a foundation on which future
 enhancements, such as integration of heater-specific combustion sub-models, expanded
 validation across additional heaters and climates, and coupling to whole-building airflow
 models, can be built.

Together, these products establish a durable capability that extends well beyond the period of
performance and positions HPBA and LBNL to continue supporting the wood heater community
as the tool is adopted, refined, and extended.
