A high solar cell efficiency of 32%, water-splitting for hydrogen generation, optoelectronics, and super-exchange coupling in Ba2MnTiO6: a DFT and AIMD study
Authors: Hafsa Malik, Arslan Zulfiqar, Abdullah Algethami, Hafiz Tauqeer Ali, S. Nazir
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Recently, double perovskite oxides (DPO) have gained much interest due to their wide range of technological applications in various demanding fields of energy conversion. Here, we present a comprehensive theoretical study of several physical properties of a lead-free Ba2MnTiO6 DPO. The structural stability is validated by computing the formation enthalpy, elastic parameters, and phonon dispersion curves. Additionally, the ab initio molecular dynamics simulations carried out indicate stable energy oscillations without any drifting and structural degradation, further confirming the thermal stability of the structure. Alongside this, a quasi-harmonic Debye approximation shows that the material is thermodynamically stable. Interestingly, a direct energy gap of 1.236 eV is predicted, rendering the compound appropriate for visible light-driven applications. The magnetic ground state of the motif is antiferromagnetic, characterized by strong superexchange coupling in the partially occupied Mn t2g–t2g orbitals having a spin-moment of 2.554µB, which is attributed to the 3d3 state with . The onset of the optical absorption at around 1.23 eV coincides well with the calculated Eg value, while there is a sharp optical absorption peak within the visible/visible–ultraviolet range up to about 23 × 104/63 × 104 cm−1 at 2.2/3.2 eV. Strikingly, the extraordinary Spectroscopic Limited Maximum Efficiency (SLME) of 32.28% is based on the optimized conditions of important device parameters, which are characterized by significant open-circuit voltage (Voc = 0.945 V), accompanied by a large fill factor (FF = 0.877), which are backed up by the small saturation current density (J0 = 5.17 × 10−18 A cm−2) and enhanced short circuit current density (Jsc = 38.95 mA cm−2), reflecting its strong photovoltaic performance. Moreover, band edge alignment illustrates that the system displays hydrogen production for a higher pH state. Ultimately, the thermoelectric analysis shows that there is a significant power factor of 6.54 W mK−2 s−1 and a figure of merit value of 0.63 at 1200 K, which enhances its potential for energy conversion applications. In conclusion, these results demonstrate that Ba2MnTiO6 is a versatile and efficient material for solar energy conversion, hydrogen generation by photocatalysis, and energy harvesting technologies.

Introduction

Exploration of material features continues to remain a prime area of concern within the subject of materials science, especially in searching for innovative ideas for technological advancements.1 In this light, perovskite oxides (POs) have captured great interest across different scientific fields due to their various compositions, synthesis feasibilities, and their captivating aspects.2 From the point of view of diverse features, double POs (DPOs) show a range of physical behaviours, making them suitable candidates for a variety of applications, including photoabsorbers for solar cell technology, photovoltaic (PV) cells, photodetectors, and energy storage devices.3–5 In DPOs, an A or B site is occupied by two distinct cations, having the formula A′A″B2O6 or A2BB′O6. In the A2BB′O6 structure, B and B′ represent transition metals (TM), while A represents an alkaline material. Generally, A-site cations are the electron donor to the BO6 framework6 and the electronic traits of DPOs are mainly affected by the B-site cation.7 Among the various magnetic behaviors displayed by DPOs, antiferromagnetic ordered behavior (AFM) is a result of superexchange coupling (SEC) via B–O–B′ bonds between the TM ions, resulting in zero magnetization having high Néel temperatures (temp.), and display stability against external fields.8 Gd2MgTiO2 shows B-site ordered AFM that allows for a giant cryogenic magnetocaloric effect (MCE) with −ΔSM = 46.21 J kg−1 K−1 around 3.3 K under 7 T and is highly promising for efficient low-temp. magnetic refrigeration applications.9 Y2AlCrO6 exhibits a G-type AFM state due to SEC between two Cr3+ ions, with insulating behavior having a bandgap (Eg) of 2.57 eV, and thus is suitable as a tunnel barrier material in AFM spintronic devices and magnetic sensors.10

The cubic DPO exerts excellent structural robustness and composition versatility, which makes for effective modulation in its electronic band structure and optical response. Owing to these advantages, material crystallizing in the cubic phases is considered highly attractive for optoelectronic (OE), thermoelectric (TE), PV, and spintronics applications.11,12 Materials with direct Eg are beneficial for the occurrence of momentum-conserving optical transitions and enhanced light absorption.13 This is a notable characteristic of cubic materials, indicating their high suitability for application in solar cells, LEDs, and photodetectors.14,15 For example, cubic X2AlBiO6 (where X = Mg, Ca, and Ba) systems guarantee structural stability and isotropic electronic responses, whereas the inherent direct Eg help in efficient optical absorption, making them appropriate for OE applications.16 Similarly, K2YIO6 and Rb2YIO6 demonstrate appreciable optical and TE features, illustrating their potential for OE and energy harvesting.17

The TE potential of the DPOs with well-defined stoichiometries can be traced to their Eg, effective masses (m*), and density of states (DOS) at the Fermi level (EF).18–20 For example, first-principles calculations indicated that Ba2CoNiO6 possesses a narrow Eg and a high dimensionless figure of merit (ZT) of 0.8 at room temp., suitable for the regeneration of wasted energies.21 In contrast first-principles calculations on Ba2ZrTiO6 predict a wide Eg of about 3.5 eV and estimate the ZT 0.76 at 800 K, signifying the potential of the compound for high-temp. energy conversion.22 On the other hand, compounds such as Ba2ZnMoO6/Ba2CdMoO6 are identified as p-type semiconductors (SC) with large Eg of 2.81/2.98 eV, while TE coefficients increase significantly with temp., emphasizing their potential in energy harvesting under high-heat conditions.23 Ba2SbXO6 compounds with X = Nb and Ta are known to have good TE features, where Ba2SbNbO6 was found to have a ZT as high as 0.85 at 300 K with p-type conduction well ahead of Ba2SbTaO6 with a ZT = 0.38. The temp.-dependent enhancement of ZT is observed in both materials and is applicable to high-temp. energies.24

Furthermore, DPOs have also been identified as promising materials for next-generation PV materials due to their exceptional chemical stability, non-toxicity, and tunable OE aspect, which provide enhanced environmental stability compared to lead(Pb)-halide PO.25,26 Density functional theory (DFT) and SCAPS-1D calculations indicate that Pb-free La2NiMnO6, with a Eg of 0.58 eV and strong infrared (IR), visible, and ultraviolet (UV) absorption arising from hybridization of the Ni/Mn–O orbitals, can be used to fabricate efficient solar cells with 20% efficiency using optimized charge transport layers.27 Similarly, DFT analysis shows that Sm2NiMnO6 has a Eg of 1.0–1.2 eV with maximum absorption in the visible region due to Ni/Mn d-orbital hybridization, making it a potential compound for a Pb-free solar cell absorber.28 Alongside, Ba2SbNbO6 (26.8% at 293.15 K),24 and Ba2SbTaO6 (12.9% at 293.15 K)24 tend to display strong light absorption in the UV and visible spectrum, with the Spectroscopic Limited Maximum Efficiency (SLME) being an essential factor in determining the theoretical PV potential beyond the Shockley–Queisser limit (SQL).

Following the latest developments in the computational investigation of DPOs, our research utilizes spin polarized (SP) DFT to extensively investigate the underlying physical traits of the recently synthesized lead-free Ba2MnTiO6 (BMTO). The primary objective of our work is the study of its electronic structure, which exhibits a direct Eg, which is important for high PV efficiency due to its ability to promote strong absorption and achieve an SLME. Further, the direct Eg semiconductor (SC) material with favorable band-edge positions can be used for photo-induced catalytic water splitting reactions that lead to hydrogen formation. Besides OE aspects, interaction between transition metal ions in the B-site is expected to induce magnetic ordering, along with the TE energy conversion features, which are also investigated. Hence, this study attempts to demonstrate the potential of BMTO as a lead free multifunctional material with various solar related applications.

Computational and structural details

Electronic structure calculations were performed using the full-potential linearized augmented plane-wave method within the framework of SP-DFT as implemented in the WIEN2k code.29,30 The exchange–correlation part was treated using the generalized gradient approximation in the Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof (GGA-PBE) scheme,31,32 while the strong on-site Coulomb interactions were treated using the GGA + U method, where U (Hubbard parameter) varied from 0 to 6 eV for the correlated 3d-orbitals of the Mn and Ti ions to concur with the experimental Eg.33 The wave function expansion within the atomic spheres was set to lmax = 12, while the plane-wave cutoff was defined by Rmt × Kmax = 7 and Gmax = 24. The Brillouin-zone integrations were carried out using a dense Monkhorst–Pack k-mesh of 8 × 8 × 8 to properly converge the total energy (Et). The structural optimization was performed until the residual forces on each atom were reduced to <5 mRy bohr−1 and self-consistency was obtained, when the Et and charge convergence criteria reach 10−4 Ry and 10−5 C, respectively.

BMTO has a cubic crystal system with a space group of Fm3̄m (no. 225) having the experimental lattice parameters of a = b = c = 8.0414 Å.34 Ba, Mn, Ti, and O ions reside in the Wyckoff positions 4a (0, 0, 0), , 4c (0, 0, 0), and 24e (0.67839, 0, 0), respectively. The primitive unit cell holds 40 atoms, consisting of 8 Ba, 4 Mn, 4 Ti, and 24 O ions. The fully relaxed crystal structure of the BMTO is illustrated in Fig. 1 along with the computed 3D spin magnetization density plotted with an isovalue of 0.26 e Å−3. The cubic structure consists of corner-sharing TiO6 and MnO6 octahedra, with Ba ions occupying the interstitial points to ensure structural stability and charge balance. The cubic structure offers a three-dimensional network with equal distances from each point, ideal for investigating the physical properties of the material.

Magnetic ground state and electronic structure

First, the magnetic ground state of the system is analyzed by estimating two types of spin ordering, such as FM and AFM between Mn ions. In the FM spin state all four Mn ions are aligned in one direction, while two of them lie in a opposite direction for the AFM case. Then the magnetic state is established by taking the Et difference of the FM and AFM ones as ΔE = EAFM − EFM. Our calculated ΔE value of −56 meV verifies that the AFM phase is energetically stable as compared to FM ones due to the negative sign, which is in excellent accordance with the experiment.34 Next, the electronic traits of the system in the stable AFM state are analyzed by investigating the dependence of computed Eg on U, which controls the level of electron correlation for the TM 3d states. To do this, we plotted the calculated Eg of the BMTO against UMn/Ti3d in Fig. 5(a) in its stable AFM state, where both Mn ions are aligned antiparallel to each other as described in the computational and structural details section.34 One can see that for a certain U range, Eg increases monotonically, then reaches a maximum value of about 1.224 eV for U = 1.0 eV and then slightly decreases when U further increases. This behavior indicates that a certain level of correlation is favorable for localization of the Mn/Ti 3d states, thus maximizing the Eg, while too strong a correlation results in band renormalization and a decrease in Eg. For a more precise determination, a finer U scan was performed for a range of 0.6–1.4 eV as depicted in Fig. 5(b). The maximum Eg of 1.236 eV occurs at U = 0.7 eV, which is recognized as the critical point. This optimized U value provides a Eg in a reasonable agreement with the experimental value,34 indicating an accuracy of approximately 86.4%, validating the reliability of the applied computational framework.

Variation of the calculated band gap (Eg) against the Hubbard parameter of the Mn/Ti 3d states (UMn/Ti3d) having ranged from (a) 0 to 6 eV and (b) 0.6 to 1.4 eV for the Ba2MnTiO6 structure.

For a qualitative analysis, the corresponding SP total DOS (TDOS) of the system for different U values ranging from 0 to 6 eV is shown in Fig. 1S(a–g) of the SI. At U = 0 eV (see Fig. 1S(a) of the SI), a small Eg is observed with considerable spectral intensity at the EF. As U increases from 1 to 3 eV (see Fig. 1S(b–d) of the SI), the conduction band minimum (CBM) shifts towards the higher energy side, resulting in the widening of the Eg. These observations are because of the localization of the 3d orbitals. As the values of U further increase from 4 to 6 eV (see Fig. 1S(e–g) of the SI), Eg does not show considerable variation. The features in the TDOS display a band renormalization effect (CBM slightly moves towards EF) for higher U-values. Alongside, we plotted TDOS for a scan U in the range of 0.6 to 1.4 eV in Fig. 2S of the SI. It can be observed that with increasing U, ranging from 0.6 to 0.7 eV (see Fig. 2S(a and b) of the SI), CBE shifts to higher energies, causing the Eg to increase, indicating sensitivity to correlation effects. However, with further increasing U, ranging from 0.7 to 1.4 eV (see Fig. 2S(b)–(h) of the SI), CMB solely starts to move towards EF (reducing Eg), indicating non-monotonic behavior of the electronic structure with respect to U.

For clarity, Fig. 6(a) exhibits the SP TDOS of BMTO, which is calculated using the optimized value of U = 0.7 eV. It is seen that the valence band (VB) and conduction band (CB) edges are separated by an Eg of 1.236 eV, very close to the experimental value of 1.43 eV.34 The sharpness of the CBE is due to the localized nature of the Mn/Ti 3d states, as described by the Hubbard correction. Further, the corresponding electronic band structure along the principal high-symmetry directions of the Brillouin zone is depicted in Fig. 6(b). This indicates a semiconducting nature of the material, which is in good agreement with the TDOS analysis. It can be noted that the material has a direct Eg as both the VB maximum (VBM) and CBM occur at the Γ-point. A direct Eg in a material is highly favorable for OE devices because of the possibility of direct recombination of electron–hole pairs and the probability of optical transitions.28 To understand the orbital contributions near the EF, we plotted the calculated SP partial DOS (PDOS) for the Mn 3d, Ti 3d, and O 2p states in Fig. 3S of the SI. From the PDOS, we found that the VB region, ranging from −5 eV to the EF, consists of Mn 3d and O 2p states, showing strong hybridization and hence significant covalency in the MnO6 and TiO6 octahedra. In particular, the states near the VBM are found to be Mn 3d states. On the other hand, the CB states are related to Ti 3d orbitals, indicating that Ti also plays an important role in the conduction process. However, O 2p states contribute to both VB and CB states. It shows that the Mn 3d states dominate the upper VB region and the lower CB region, while the Ti 3d states dominate the CBM. Also, O 2p states display strong overlap with Mn 3d and Ti 3d states over the entire range, indicating strong p–d orbitals hybridization. Alongside, we employed the one of the most accurate DFT methods of HSE06 to confirm the Eg value. Our calculated TDOS and band structure using the HSE06 functional clearly indicates that system exhibits a large direct Eg of 1.78 eV (see Fig. 7(a) and (b), respectively), which is 24.5% overestimated from the experimental value of 1.43 eV. Thus, in this case, the GGA + U method predicted a more accurate system electronic structure than that of the HSE06 ones, which affirms the method reliability for the present work.

Next, the study of the Bader charge and Electron Localization Function (ELF) results provides a coherent picture of the bonding of the BMTO. As illustrated in Fig. 8, the Bader charge analysis indicates a considerable amount of charge redistribution amongst individual atoms. The Ba atom has a charge transfer of −0.4745e, which means it donates slight electrons and confirms it as a cation due to its value which is near Ba2+. In a like manner, the positive charge transfer associated with Mn (+1.9706e) as well as Ti (+2.0682e) indicates considerable depletion of electrons and suggests an oxidation state of around Mn3+/Mn4+ and Ti4+, respectively. Conversely, the energy gained by the O atom is about −1.1816e, confirming its strong electron-acceptor role and functioning as an anion in the lattice. The data reveals that metal electrons mainly go to oxygen according to the quality of the bonding.

Computed electron localization function of the Ba2MnTiO6 structure, where its 0/1 value shows very low/high electron density.

The ELF graph further supports the charge-transfer mechanism with strong localization of the electrons at the oxygen sites, which is associated with the high ELF value, and suggests the accumulation of localized valence electrons. However, the regions of Mn and Ti exhibit relatively low ELF values, which signify electron depletion and involvement of their valence d-electrons in bonding. Localized zones that are large, non-directional, and located around Ba are entirely associated with core or semicore electrons. In addition, the ELF accumulations along the Mn–O and Ti–O bond paths are arguably not pronounced, indicating that the bonding is primarily ionic in nature. However, it must be acknowledged that some weak degree of p–d hybridization between O and the TM atoms is present, which endorses a partial covalent character of the bond. The significantly low-ELF areas in the interstitial space also suggest non-metal-like electron delocalization. As such, both the Bader charge and ELF data demonstrate that BMTO mainly features ionic bonding with a small component of covalent bonding due mainly to Mn/Ti–O. Thus, one can conclude that the bonding is primarily ionic, as signified by substantial charge transfer between Mn/Ti 3d and oxygen 2p states. However, remarkable orbital-hybridization among the Mn 3d, O 2p, and Ti 3d states predicts a notable covalent aspect to the bonding along with the ionic, characteristic of TM DPOs.

Solar cell efficiency

The PV applicability of the material is analyzed using the SLME theory formulated by Yu and Zunger.51 Contrary to the SQL, where the calculation of solar cell efficiency is based on the Eg of the material with ideal absorption conditions, the SLME theory takes into account the actual optical absorption aspects of the material, the thickness of the absorber layer, and radiative losses. As a result, SLME theory is now recognized as a trustworthy theoretical tool for determining the PV efficiency of the newly discovered materials before their experimental realization. It also has a very high efficiency of 31.85% when the absorber thickness is at 1 µm, indicating the feasibility of using the material in thin film solar cells. The plot in Fig. 11(a–d) demonstrates the impact of absorber thickness on some important PV parameters like FF, Voc, Jsc, and J0. In Fig. 11(a), the FF is decreased with increasing thickness from about 0.883 to 0.877. This can be related to an increase in the value of series resistance and carrier recombination rate in absorber layers, resulting in a low value of J–V curve squareness. Once the above result is achieved, further changes in thickness have a negligible effect on FF. In Fig. 11(b), it is evident that the Voc is reduced from almost 1.01 to 0.95 volts. This decrease is due to an increase in recombination and trapping effects of the carrier in thick layers, making the quasi-EF separation smaller. Once the thickness crosses a certain point, Voc remains constant. In Fig. 11(c), Jsc shows a considerable increase with an increase in thickness from 8.23 mA cm−2 to approximately 38.95 mA cm−2. Such behavior happens because thick absorbers absorb more photons, resulting in greater photogenerated carrier concentration and higher current production. At greater thickness values, Jsc increases less and tends to approach a constant value since most of the incident light gets absorbed. Fig. 11(d) depicts an increase in J0, varying from 9.48 × 10−20 to 5.18 × 10−18 A cm−2 with increasing thickness values. An increase in J0 means that there will be an increase in dark currents and recombination activities, which explains the reduction in Voc values. After the increase, J0 also tends to approach a constant value with greater thickness values.

While the SLME model offers a more practical estimation compared to the traditional SQL by taking into account the absorption coefficient that varies with materials and thickness of the absorber layer, it still relies on some unrealistic assumptions. Magnetism

The magnetic traits of the structure are further studied, where the calculated spin magnetic moment (ms) of two Mn ions is 2.554µB, while the remaining two Mn ions exhibit −2.554µB. The “−” sign indicates that their spins are aligned antiparallel to each other, which confirms the AFM coupling between them. This is further supported by the 3D spin density isosurface diagram as shown in Fig. 1 with an iso-value of 0.26 e Å−3. It is found that the structure exhibits strong localization of the spin density around the Mn ions, thus affirming that the magnetic features mainly depend on them. It is also seen that the two Mn ions possess the same intensity of spin density but with opposite color codes, further confirming the AFM state. It is also seen that the ms arises mainly from the Mn 3d orbitals, with minimal contributions from Ti and the oxygen ions. The different directions of the spins of the Mn ions provide compelling evidence of the strong AFM interactions in the structure.

Further, the magnetic interactions in BMTO can be described based on the SEC involving the exchange of electrons between neighboring Mn4+ ions via the non-magnetic oxygen ion, which gives us some idea about the electron transfer process in the material. In the BMTO, the Mn4+ ions have a 3d3 electron configuration, whereby the crystal field causes splitting in such a way that the configuration is t2g3 e0g. This means that all three electrons are in the t2g orbitals (dxy,dxz, and dyz), where each electron has the same spin orientation as dictated by Hund's rule,65 leading to a high-spin state with , whereas the eg orbitals are vacant. Here, there is no direct contact between the two Mn atoms, rather, they interact through virtual hopping of an electron via the bridging O 2p orbital as shown in Fig. 13. Hence, the SEC is primarily between t2g orbitals as the eg orbitals are empty and thus not involved in the process of exchange. The electrons in the half-filled t2g orbitals of Mn4+ can virtually transfer to an oxygen 2p orbital and then hop to another t2g orbital of a neighboring Mn4+. The hopping process is indirect in nature, which results in an SEC that favors the antiparallel alignment of spins between neighboring Mn ions according to the Pauli exclusion principle and Hund's rules, ensuring minimum energy for the system. Moreover, the structure of the Mn–O–Mn bond is also important in determining the interaction: the linear or 180° arrangement of the bond improves the overlap between Mn t2g and O 2p orbitals, resulting in an AFM SE interaction. In accordance with the Goodenough–Kanamori–Anderson rules,66 the coupling among the half-filled t2g orbitals via O 2p, leads to a highly preferred AFM coupling. It is completely compatible with the magnetic traits exhibited by BMTO. As such, the orbital occupation of the Mn4+ ions along with the virtual hopping of electrons within the Mn–O–Mn configuration explains, on a microscopic level, the origin of AFM in this material. In other words, BMTO exhibits an AFM ordering due to the strong Mn–O–Mn SEC.

Conclusion

In this work, we present a detailed first-principles investigation of the lead-free double perovskite oxide Ba2MnTiO6 to assess its potential performance in solar cells, photocatalysis, and thermoelectric energy conversion. It is noted that the material is thermodynamically, dynamically, and mechanically stable. Further, ab initio molecular dynamics simulations study show that the system is thermally stable even at higher temperatures. Also, the thermodynamic results show stable lattice behaviour under different temperatures and pressures. Electronic structure calculations indicate that the material has a direct band gap (Eg) of 1.236 eV, which is in close agreement with the experimental value (1.43 eV) with an accuracy of 86.4%. Also, an antiferromagnetic (AFM) semiconducting ground state is favourable, which arises from the AFM superexchange interaction between partially filled Mn t2g orbitals. The calculated spin magnetic moment on the Mn ion is 2.554µB, which confirms its +3 state having . Furthermore, the optical response demonstrates high absorption from the visible to the ultraviolet range, thus suggesting excellent photon harvesting. Generally, the computed real dielectric constant of 8.0 lies in the infrared regime and rises to 11.5 in the visible regime, implying that there is high polarization behaviour, whereas the imaginary dielectric constant is close to zero in the infrared regime but attains a sharp peak of 10.8 at 3.1 eV. Moreover, the spectroscopic limited maximum efficiency of 32.28% further proves its excellent potential for photovoltaic applications because of the optimised Voc, FF, Jsc, and J0 values. Furthermore, Eg alignment of the structure exhibits a desirable pH-dependent nature, with an increase in pH causing a shift of the conduction band edge from −4.628 eV to −3.802 eV and the valence band edge from −5.864 eV to −5.038 eV, thereby facilitating better alignment with the redox potential and promoting hydrogen generation. Also, the thermoelectric properties show a reasonable value of the figure of merit of 0.63 at elevated temperature, indicating its application in high-temperature energy conversion. In conclusion, the combination of direct Eg, high optical absorbance, promising photoelectrical efficiency, adjustable photocatalysis capability, and good thermoelectric properties renders Ba2MnTiO6 an excellent material choice for solar energy conversion and energy harvesting applications.
