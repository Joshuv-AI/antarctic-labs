Towards Non-Monotonic Entailment in Propositional Defeasible Standpoint Logic
Nicholas Leisegang, Thomas Meyer, Ivan Varzinczak

Abstract
Recent work in defeasible reasoning has seen notions of preferential semantics and entailment in the style of Kraus et al. applied to modal logics. However, work in this field has focussed primarily on satisfiability checking, and monotonic notions of entailment, which may be inferentially weak. One particular modal logic where this has been introduced is propositional standpoint logics, where modalities can express the views of different viewpoints. This has resulted in the formalisation of propositional defeasible standpoint logic (PDSL). In this paper, we propose a means of lifting the class of (non-monotonic) rational entailment relations from traditional KLM-style reasoning to a fragment of PDSL. In order to do so, we extend the expressivity of PDSL via situated standpoint conditionals, allowing us to talk about a defeasible conditional holding in the context of a given standpoint. This allows us to re-characterise the syntax of PDSL in terms of situated conditionals, and shows that a large fragment of PDSL is expressible as a set of situated conditionals. We then focus on characterising non-monotonic entailment in this fragment, defining a method to transport any ranking-based entailment relation from the propositional case into the PDSL case. This is first described in the general case and then considered in the specific cases of rational and lexicographic closures, providing a faithful translation of each inference into PDSL. We also show that entailment-checking in this fragment of PDSL can be done largely using algorithms from the propositional case, while preserving complexity bounds.

Introduction

Standpoint modalities were introduced in the propositional case by Gómez Álvarez and Rudolph
12
, in order to integrate (potentially conflicting) viewpoints into a single logical framework while maintaining consistency. However, this framework could only talk about the unequivocal and possible beliefs which a standpoint holds. Leisegang et al.
19
introduced preferential semantics to classical standpoint logics which added the ability to reason about the beliefs which are
typically held
by or
distinctly possible
to a standpoint. In their work, they consider a Tarskian notion of entailment which reasons monotonically over all models. This however, often becomes unnessecarily inferentially weak when reasoning over defeasible statements, as can be seen in the following example.

Example 1
.

Within the standpoint of physics, Newton’s laws are usually taken to hold. This can be represented within defeasible standpoint logics as
\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip-1.07639pt\hbox{$\shortmid$}}}}\hskip-0.12915pt\joinrel\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip 0.21529pt\hbox{$\sim$}\vskip 1.07639pt\hbox{$\sim$}}}}\hskip-0.21529pt\joinrel\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip-3.01385pt\hbox{$\shortmid$}}}_{\mathsf{Phy}}\mathsf{NL}
, which states that “the physics standpoint typically believes in Newton’s laws (NL)”. One of Newton’s laws is the law of universal gravitation, which states that gravitational force acting between two objects is a function of the mass of the objects and the distance between them. Thus, Newton’s laws usually treat gravitational force as variable. This can expressed using the defeasible implication
\mathsf{NL}\leadsto\mathsf{VG}
where VG stands for “variable gravity”. Taking these two pieces of information into account, it seems reasonable to conclude that from the physics perspective, gravitational force should be typically treated as variable. That is, we should conclude
\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip-1.07639pt\hbox{$\shortmid$}}}}\hskip-0.12915pt\joinrel\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip 0.21529pt\hbox{$\sim$}\vskip 1.07639pt\hbox{$\sim$}}}}\hskip-0.21529pt\joinrel\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip-3.01385pt\hbox{$\shortmid$}}}_{\mathsf{Phy}}\mathsf{VG}
. Moreover, assume we are introduced to a different standpoint, such as that of astronomy or engineering, which bases its views on ideas from physics. We can express this in PDSL by
\mathsf{Astro}\lesssim\mathsf{Phy}
and
\mathsf{Eng}\lesssim\mathsf{Phy}
, stating that typically the astronomy and engineering standpoints can be seen as more specific cases of the physics standpoint. Based on this, it is reasonable that these sub-standpoints inherit the views of the greater standpoint and we conclude that
\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip-1.07639pt\hbox{$\shortmid$}}}}\hskip-0.12915pt\joinrel\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip 0.21529pt\hbox{$\sim$}\vskip 1.07639pt\hbox{$\sim$}}}}\hskip-0.21529pt\joinrel\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip-3.01385pt\hbox{$\shortmid$}}}_{\mathsf{Astro}}\mathsf{NL}
and
\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip-1.07639pt\hbox{$\shortmid$}}}}\hskip-0.12915pt\joinrel\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip 0.21529pt\hbox{$\sim$}\vskip 1.07639pt\hbox{$\sim$}}}}\hskip-0.21529pt\joinrel\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip-3.01385pt\hbox{$\shortmid$}}}_{\mathsf{Eng}}\mathsf{NL}
. However, from the perspective of a (typical) engineer who works on Earth-based projects, it is practical to treat gravity not as a variable, but as a constant value that the Earth exerts on materials. Hence, we may want to add that although
\mathsf{Eng}\lesssim\mathsf{Phy}
holds, we have that
\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip-1.07639pt\hbox{$\shortmid$}}}}\hskip-0.12915pt\joinrel\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip 0.21529pt\hbox{$\sim$}\vskip 1.07639pt\hbox{$\sim$}}}}\hskip-0.21529pt\joinrel\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip-3.01385pt\hbox{$\shortmid$}}}_{\mathsf{Eng}}\neg\mathsf{VG}
. That is, from an engineering perspective, gravitational force is usually not considered a variable.

Leisegang et al.
19
introduce Propositional Defeasible Standpoint Logic (PDSL) as a means to increase the expressivity of propositional standpoint logics via the introduction of defeasible standpoint modalities, defeasible implications and defeasible standpoint sharpenings. The above example shows a situation where the increased expressivity can allow the logic to incorporate increased nuance through defeasibility. For example, the classical knowledge base
\{\Box_{\mathsf{Phy}}\mathsf{VG},\mathsf{Eng}\leq\mathsf{Phy},\Box_{\mathsf{Eng}}\neg\mathsf{VG}\}
is unsatisfiable, while its defeasible variant in PDSL
\{\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip-1.07639pt\hbox{$\shortmid$}}}}\hskip-0.12915pt\joinrel\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip 0.21529pt\hbox{$\sim$}\vskip 1.07639pt\hbox{$\sim$}}}}\hskip-0.21529pt\joinrel\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip-3.01385pt\hbox{$\shortmid$}}}_{\mathsf{Phy}}\mathsf{VG},\mathsf{Eng}\lesssim\mathsf{Phy},\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip-1.07639pt\hbox{$\shortmid$}}}}\hskip-0.12915pt\joinrel\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip 0.21529pt\hbox{$\sim$}\vskip 1.07639pt\hbox{$\sim$}}}}\hskip-0.21529pt\joinrel\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip-3.01385pt\hbox{$\shortmid$}}}_{\mathsf{Eng}}\neg\mathsf{VG}\}
can be satisfied. In previous work
19
, entailment for PDSL was restricted to the Tarskian notion of preferential entailment, denoted
\vDash_{P}
. However, this notion of entailment in PDSL severely restricts the inferences which follow from a PDSL knowledge base. For instance, we have that,

\{{\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip-1.07639pt\hbox{$\shortmid$}}}}\hskip-0.12915pt\joinrel\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip 0.21529pt\hbox{$\sim$}\vskip 1.07639pt\hbox{$\sim$}}}}\hskip-0.21529pt\joinrel\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip-3.01385pt\hbox{$\shortmid$}}}_{\mathsf{Phy}}}\mathsf{NL},\mathsf{NL}\leadsto\mathsf{VG}\}\nvDash_{P}{\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip-1.07639pt\hbox{$\shortmid$}}}}\hskip-0.12915pt\joinrel\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip 0.21529pt\hbox{$\sim$}\vskip 1.07639pt\hbox{$\sim$}}}}\hskip-0.21529pt\joinrel\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip-3.01385pt\hbox{$\shortmid$}}}_{\mathsf{Phy}}}\mathsf{VG}\quad\textit{ and }\quad\{{\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip-1.07639pt\hbox{$\shortmid$}}}}\hskip-0.12915pt\joinrel\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip 0.21529pt\hbox{$\sim$}\vskip 1.07639pt\hbox{$\sim$}}}}\hskip-0.21529pt\joinrel\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip-3.01385pt\hbox{$\shortmid$}}}_{\mathsf{Phy}}\mathsf{NL}},\mathsf{Astro}\lesssim\mathsf{Phy}\}\nvDash_{P}{\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip-1.07639pt\hbox{$\shortmid$}}}}\hskip-0.12915pt\joinrel\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip 0.21529pt\hbox{$\sim$}\vskip 1.07639pt\hbox{$\sim$}}}}\hskip-0.21529pt\joinrel\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip-3.01385pt\hbox{$\shortmid$}}}_{\mathsf{Astro}}}\mathsf{NL}

which is shown in detail in Example
2
. Hence, our ability to reason prototypically about standpoints falls at the first hurdle when considering
\vDash_{P}
. In the first case, we are not able to extract any of the usual conclusions which our standpoints ought to make from their beliefs even though there is no known reason to prevent this. In the second, sub-standpoints are unable to inherit any of the typical believes of their greater standpoint, even though we are given no reason to believe they behave atypically as a sub-standpoint. In order to combat this, we aim to lift methods of non-monotonic defeasible reasoning from the traditional KLM literature into the PDSL case. Specifically, we consider the class of rational defeasible inference operations, including rational closure and lexicographic closure. The paper is organised as follows. In Section
2
we give preliminaries on PDSL and traditional (propositional) KLM defeasible reasoning. In Section
3
we introduce standpoint situated conditionals and show how we can increase the expressivity of PDSL, as well as rephrase the syntax of PDSL in terms of situated conditionals. In Section
4
we define a fragment of PDSL which can be translated into propositional KLM knowledge bases, while in Section
5
we show how this translation can lift the class of rational defeasible entailment relations from the propositional case into the PDSL case.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce Propositional Defeasible Standpoint Logics, as well as some basic definitions from defeasible reasoning in the tradition of Kraus, Lehmann and Magidor (KLM)
15
. In the KLM tradition of defeasible reasoning, the language
\mathcal{L}^{\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim}
consists of conditionals or defeasible implications of the form
\alpha\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim\beta
where
\alpha
and
\beta
are Boolean formulas. We refer to (finite) subsets
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim}
as propositional conditional knowledge bases. The semantics for
\mathcal{L}^{\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim}
are given by
preferential
interpretations.

Definition 1
.

15
A preferential interpretation over a set of propositional atoms
\mathcal{P}
is a triple
\mathcal{I}=(W,l,\prec)
where
W
is a set of states,
l:W\longrightarrow 2^{\mathcal{P}}
is a mapping from the set of states to the set of classical valuations on
\mathcal{P}
, and
\prec\subseteq W\times W
is a strict partial order such that for every Boolean formula
\alpha
, the set
\llbracket\alpha\rrbracket_{\mathcal{I}}:=\{w\in W\mid l(w)\Vdash\alpha\}
has a minimal element with respect to
\prec
.

We say that
\mathcal{I}\Vdash\alpha\leadsto\beta
, or
\mathcal{I}
satisfies
\alpha\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim\beta
if
min_{\prec}(\llbracket\alpha\rrbracket)_{\mathcal{I}})\subseteq\llbracket\beta\rrbracket_{\mathcal{I}}
. We extend this to propositional conditional knowledge bases in the usual way. Given a knowledge base
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim}
, we say that
\mathcal{K}
preferentially entails
\alpha\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim\beta
, written
\mathcal{K}\vDash_{pref}\alpha\leadsto\beta
, if
\mathcal{I}\Vdash\mathcal{K}
implies
\mathcal{I}\Vdash\alpha\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim\beta
for all preferential interpretations
\mathcal{I}
. However, as in the PDSL case in Example
1
,
\vDash_{pref}
is often considered too weak an inference operator for defeasible reasoning. This has led to several different notions of defeasible entailment to be defined for propositional conditional knowledge bases. An important class of these are rational defeasible entailment relations, defined by Lehmann and Magidor
16
. These are the class of defeasible entailment relations defined by
ranking functions
. Ranking functions were originally introduced by Spohn
22
. For our purposes, we consider a slightly restricted definition.

Definition 2
.

5

A
ranking function

\mathcal{R}
is a function
\mathcal{R}:2^{\mathcal{P}}\longrightarrow\mathds{N}\cup\{\infty\}
, satisfying the following property: if
\mathcal{R}(u)<\infty
, then for every
0\leq j<r(u)
there exists
v\in 2^{\mathcal{P}}
such that
r(v)=j
.

Each ranking function
\mathcal{R}
induces a preferential interpretation
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{R}}=(W_{\mathcal{R}},l_{\mathcal{R}},\prec_{\mathcal{R}})
where
W_{\mathcal{R}}=2^{\mathcal{P}}\setminus\mathcal{R}^{-1}(\infty)
,
l_{\mathcal{R}}
is the identity and
u\prec_{\mathcal{R}}v
iff.
\mathcal{R}(u)<\mathcal{R}(v)
. We write
\mathcal{R}\Vdash\alpha\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim\beta
to denote
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{R}}\Vdash\alpha\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim\beta
.

Definition 3
.

A rational defeasible entailment relation is an entailment relation
\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx
such that for any knowledge base
\mathcal{K}
, there exists some ranking function
\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{K}}
such that
\mathcal{K}\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx\alpha\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim\beta
iff
\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{K}}\Vdash\alpha\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim\beta
.

Many well-known entailment relations in defeasible reasoning are rational entailment relations. Later, we consider two of the most well-known in the literature: rational closure
16
;
20
and lexicographic closure
17
.
Casini et al.
5
study rational defeasible entailment in the general setting. They define a
basic (rational) defeasible entailment
as a rational entailment relation
\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx
which satisfies
inclusion
and
classical preservation
.
\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx
satisfies inclusion if for all
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim}
and all
\alpha\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim\beta\in\mathcal{K}
,
\mathcal{K}\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx\alpha\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim\beta
.
\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx
satisfies classical preservation if:
\mathcal{K}\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx\alpha\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim\bot
if and only if
\mathcal{K}\vDash_{pref}\alpha\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim\bot
for every Boolean formula
\alpha
. This is called classical preservation since
\mathcal{I}=(W,l,\prec)\Vdash\neg\alpha\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim\bot
iff
w\Vdash\alpha
for all
w\in W
.

Definition 4
.

A ranking strategy is a mapping
r:2^{\mathcal{L}^{\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim}}\longrightarrow\mathscr{R}
, where
\mathscr{R}
is the set of ranked interpretations. That is, a ranking strategy assigns each propositional conditional knowledge base a ranking function.

Each ranking strategy
r:2^{\mathcal{L}^{\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim}}\longrightarrow\mathscr{R}
defines a rational defeasible entailment relation
\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx_{r}
by:
\mathcal{K}\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx_{r}\alpha\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim\beta
iff
r(\mathcal{K})\Vdash\alpha\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim\beta
. We say a ranking strategy is well-defined if
\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx_{r}
is a basic defeasible entailment, and assume all ranking strategies considered in this paper are well-defined, unless otherwise stated. Casini et al. also define an algorithm
\mathtt{DefeasibleEntail}
such that, given a knowledge base
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim}
, a query
\alpha\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim\beta\in\mathcal{L}^{\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim}
, and a ranking strategy
r
, we have that
\mathcal{K}\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx_{r}\alpha\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim\beta
iff
\mathtt{DefeasibleEntail}(\mathcal{K},r(\mathcal{K}),\alpha\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim\beta)=\textit{True}
. In Section
5
we show that this algorithm can be used for entailment-checking in PDSL. Inspired by the preferential semantics in propositional KLM, Propositional Defeasible Standpoint Logic (PDSL) was introduced by Leisegang et al.
19
. It extends (classical) Propositional Standpoint Logic
12
with defeasible versions of both material implication
15
and standpoint modalities in order to express not only the necessary or possible views of standpoints, but describes those views which are
typical
or
distinctly possible
for a given standpoint. These are drawn from the more general notion of defeasible modalities given by Britz and Varzinczak
3
. The syntax for PDSL is defined below.

Definition 5
.

19

Given a vocabulary
\mathcal{V=(P,S)}
where
\mathcal{P}
is a set of propositional atoms and
\mathcal{S}
is a set of standpoints, we define the set of standpoint expressions
\mathcal{E}
over
\mathcal{S}
as

e::=*\mid s\mid-e\mid e\cap e

where
s\in\mathcal{S}
. We define
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathds{S}}

\mathcal{V}
(where

and

\alpha::=\top\mid p\mid e\lesssim d\mid\neg\alpha\mid\alpha\wedge\alpha\mid\Box_{e}\alpha\mid{\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip-1.07639pt\hbox{$\shortmid$}}}}\hskip-0.12915pt\joinrel\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip 0.21529pt\hbox{$\sim$}\vskip 1.07639pt\hbox{$\sim$}}}}\hskip-0.21529pt\joinrel\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip-3.01385pt\hbox{$\shortmid$}}}}_{e}\alpha\mid\alpha\leadsto\alpha

We introduce the supplementary dual modalities as
\Diamond_{e}\alpha:=\neg\Box_{e}\neg\alpha
,
\scalebox{0.8}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{\rotatebox{57.0}{$\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip-1.07639pt\hbox{$\shortmid$}}}}\hskip-0.12915pt\joinrel\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip 0.21529pt\hbox{$\sim$}\vskip 1.07639pt\hbox{$\sim$}}}}\hskip-0.21529pt\joinrel\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip-3.01385pt\hbox{$\shortmid$}}}$}}}\hskip-1.29167pt_{e}\alpha:=\neg\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip-1.07639pt\hbox{$\shortmid$}}}}\hskip-0.12915pt\joinrel\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip 0.21529pt\hbox{$\sim$}\vskip 1.07639pt\hbox{$\sim$}}}}\hskip-0.21529pt\joinrel\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip-3.01385pt\hbox{$\shortmid$}}}_{e}\neg\alpha
and classical “standpoint sharpening” statements as
e\leq d:=\Box_{e\cap-d}\bot

\Box_{e}\alpha
and
\Diamond_{e}\alpha

“ it is unequivocal to
e

\alpha
”
and
“ it is possible to
e

\alpha
”
. On the other hand, sentences with defeasible modalities
\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip-1.07639pt\hbox{$\shortmid$}}}}\hskip-0.12915pt\joinrel\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip 0.21529pt\hbox{$\sim$}\vskip 1.07639pt\hbox{$\sim$}}}}\hskip-0.21529pt\joinrel\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip-3.01385pt\hbox{$\shortmid$}}}_{e}\alpha

\scalebox{0.8}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{\rotatebox{57.0}{$\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip-1.07639pt\hbox{$\shortmid$}}}}\hskip-0.12915pt\joinrel\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip 0.21529pt\hbox{$\sim$}\vskip 1.07639pt\hbox{$\sim$}}}}\hskip-0.21529pt\joinrel\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip-3.01385pt\hbox{$\shortmid$}}}$}}}\hskip-1.29167pt_{e}\alpha

“
\alpha

“ it is distinctly possible to
e
that
\alpha
”

“the standpoint

Definition 6
.

19

A state-preferential standpoint structure (SPSS) is a quadruple
M=(\Pi,\sigma,\tau,\prec)
where,

•

\Pi
is a set of precisifications (possible worlds).

•

\sigma:\mathcal{E}\longrightarrow 2^{\Pi}
is a function such that
\sigma(*)=\Pi
,
\sigma(e\cap d)=\sigma(e)\cap\sigma(d)
, and
\sigma(-e)=\Pi\setminus\sigma(e)
. Moreover, we require that
\sigma(s)\neq\emptyset
for all
s\in\mathcal{S}
.

•

\tau:\Pi\longrightarrow 2^{\mathcal{P}}
is a map which assigns a classical valuation to each precisification.

•

\prec
is a strict partial order on
\Pi
such that for every subset
X
of
\Pi
, and every
\pi\in X
, either
\pi
is a
\prec
-minimal element of
X
, or there is a
\pi^{\prime}\in X
such that
\pi^{\prime}
is a
\prec
-minimal element of
X
, and
\pi^{\prime}\prec\pi
(well-foundedness).

Here, each precisification represents a possible precise set of views which may occur within the possible ways to interpret a standpoint. The map
\sigma
allocates a set of precisifications to each standpoint, with the allocation being extended to standpoint expressions using set-theoretic operators. The preference ordering on
\Pi

\pi_{1}\prec\pi_{2}

\pi_{1}

\pi_{2}

\prec

.

\beta

\alpha

\beta

\beta

\beta

\alpha

M,\pi\Vdash\alpha\leadsto_{e}\beta\textit{ iff }\pi\notin\min_{\prec}(\sigma(e)\cap\llbracket\alpha\rrbracket)\textit{ or }\pi\in\llbracket\beta\rrbracket.

(1)

Conditional Knowledge Bases

In this section, we describe a fragment of of PDSL which can be translated into a traditional KLM-style conditional knowledge base. This translation then can be utilised in order to characterise non-monotonic systems of inference or entailment for PDSL. The fragment of PDSL which we consider are those in which the formulas are some form of positive defeasible conditionals.

Definition 8
.

The fragment
\textbf{Cond}(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathds{S}})\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathds{S}}
(for
e,d\in\mathcal{E}
and
\alpha,\beta
are Boolean) is the fragment of
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathds{S}}
consisting of all formulas of the form:

\phi::=\alpha\leadsto_{e}\beta\mid e\lesssim d

A PDSL knowledge base
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathds{S}}
is called a conditional knowledge base if
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\textbf{Cond}(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathds{S}})

We should note here that although this logical fragment looks fairly restricted, it is sufficient enough to cover a fair portion of our language, especially since for entailment purposes we are interested in models which satisfy our knowledge base globally. In particular any Boolean formula
\alpha
can be represented by
\neg\alpha\leadsto_{*}\bot
. Any defeasible implication, or Boolean formula bound by defeasible or classical necessity can be expressed, by Propositon
2
. This includes classical standpoint sharpenings.
Furthermore, conjunctions of statements can be expressed by including each conjunct in the set. Therefore, our framework includes many of the core features of PDSL. The notable omissions stem from the absence of negation on the outer level. We cannot express distinctly in such a knowledge base that a defeasible implication, or a modal statement ought to
not
hold. As a result, we also cannot describe non-Boolean disjunctive statements, and furthermore, we lose the ability to express both defeasible and classical diamond statements of the form
\Diamond_{e}\alpha
or
\scalebox{0.8}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{\rotatebox{57.0}{$\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip-1.07639pt\hbox{$\shortmid$}}}}\hskip-0.12915pt\joinrel\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip 0.21529pt\hbox{$\sim$}\vskip 1.07639pt\hbox{$\sim$}}}}\hskip-0.21529pt\joinrel\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip-3.01385pt\hbox{$\shortmid$}}}$}}}\hskip-1.29167pt_{e}\alpha
. As the name suggests, conditional knowledge bases in PDSL are constructed to be analogously structured to conditional knowledge bases in the propositional case
16
. In fact, the central idea within this section is to show that we can translate conditional knowledge bases in PDSL to conditional knowledge bases in propositional logic.

Definition 9
.

Consider the conditional language
\textbf{Cond}(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathds{S}})
over the vocabulary
\mathcal{V}=(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{S})
. Then consider the language of propositional KLM
\mathcal{L}^{\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim}
defined over the atomic variables
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}=\mathcal{P}\cup\mathcal{S}
. We define the translation
T:\textbf{Cond}(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathds{S}})\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}^{\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim}
as follows.

•

We define the standpoint index translation
tr
inductively over standpoint expressions by
t(s)=s
for all
s\in\mathcal{S}
,
t(*)=\top
,
t(e\cap d)=t(e)\wedge t(d)
, and
t(-e)=\neg tr(e)
.

•

T(e\lesssim d)=tr(e)\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim tr(d)
.

•

T(\alpha\leadsto_{e}\beta)=\alpha\wedge tr(e)\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim\beta
.

We extend
T
to knowledge bases in
\textbf{Cond}(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathds{S}})
via the usual means. That is,
T(\mathcal{K}):=\{T(\phi)\mid\phi\in\mathcal{K}\}
for
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\textbf{Cond}(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathds{S}})

Note here that the translated sentence is no longer than the original sentence and computable in linearly many steps in the size of the original sentence. Abusing notation, we translate PDSL models into KLM propositional models with the same function
T
. Notice here how this translation essentially treats standpoint symbols as propositional atoms which may or may not be true at each precisification.

Definition 10
.

Given an SPSS
M=(\Pi,\sigma,\tau,\prec)
, we define the propositional translated model of
M
as
T(M)=(W^{\prime},l^{\prime},\prec^{\prime})
where

•

W^{\prime}=\Pi
.

•

l(\pi)=\tau(\pi)\cup\{s\in\mathcal{S}\mid\pi\in\sigma(s)\}
.

•

\prec^{\prime}=\prec
.

Our next result tells us that our translations between language and models correspond as expected.

Proposition 4
.

For any
\phi\in\textbf{Cond}(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathds{S}})
and any SPSS
M
we have that
M\Vdash\phi\textit{ iff. }T(M)\Vdash T(\phi)
.

This translation provides us with a context in which defining non-monotonic entailment relations for conditional knowledge bases in PDSL can be extrapolated from their propositional counterpart. The translation function
T
is also injective on models, meaning that no two non-equivalent SPSSes induce identical preferential models.

Proposition 5
.

T
is injective on models.

In fact, the image of
T
in the set of preferential interpretations are exactly the set preferential interpretations
\mathcal{I}
such that
\llbracket s\rrbracket_{\mathcal{I}}\neq\emptyset
for all
s\in\mathcal{S}
. Clearly, it follows that
T
is not surjective on models, since there are preferential interpretations over
\mathcal{P^{\prime}}=\mathcal{P}\cup\mathcal{S}
such that
\llbracket s\rrbracket_{\mathcal{I}}=\emptyset
for some
s\in\mathcal{S}
. Moreover, for any preferential interpretation
\mathcal{I}=(W,l,\prec)
in the image of
T
we can construct its preimage
T^{-1}(\mathcal{I}):=(\Pi^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime},\tau^{\prime},\prec^{\prime})
by:
\Pi^{\prime}=W
;
\prec^{\prime}=\prec
; for each
w\in W
,
\tau^{\prime}(w)=l(w)\cap\mathcal{P}
; and for each
s\in\mathcal{S}
,
\sigma^{\prime}(s)=\{w\in W\mid s\in l(w)\}
and
\sigma^{\prime}(*)=W
.
\sigma^{\prime}
is defined inductively on complex standpoint expressions. In our next section, we use this translation to lift methods of non-monotonic entailment from the propositional defeasible reasoning setting to the PDSL setting.

5
Non-Monotonic Entailment

In this section, we attempt to answer in the PDSL setting the same question posed for the propositional setting by Lehmann and Magidor in
16
: “
What does a conditional knowledge base entail?
”

In particular, rational defeasible entailments, which are defined semantically via a
single ranked interpretation
. We first consider the general case of this question as investigated by et al.
5
, and then highlight two well-known forms of inference in the propositional case: rational closure
16
;
20
and lexicographic closure
17
. Given Proposition
4
, a clear intuition is to build an SPSS model which characterises a non-monotonic entailment
\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx
via the translated model
T(M)
. That is, for a knowledge base
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\textbf{Cond}(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathds{S}})
we choose a model
M
to characterise our entailment in the PDSL case, such that
T(M)
induces the corresponding entailment result in the propositional case. Therefore, if we are given
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\textbf{Cond}(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathds{S}})
and propositional defeasible entailment
\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx_{r}
with an associated ranking strategy
r:2^{\mathcal{L}^{\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim}}\longrightarrow\mathscr{R}
which we wish to define in the conditional-PDSL case, we proceed as follows:

1.

Translate
\mathcal{K}
into its propositional form
T(\mathcal{K})
.

2.

Compute the semantic structure for
\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx_{r}
in the propositional case via
r(T(\mathcal{K}))
.

3.

Using the fact that nature of
T
is injective on models, we define
T^{-1}(r(T(\mathcal{K}))
as the semantic model which characterises
\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx
for
\textbf{Cond}(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathds{S}})
. This relies on the following Lemma.

Lemma 1
.

If
\mathcal{K}
is a satisfiable conditional PDSL knowledge base and
r
is a well-defined ranking strategy, the preferential interpretation
r(T(\mathcal{K}))
is in the semantic image of
T
.

We define
\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx_{r}
for conditional-PDSL as follows.

Definition 11
.

Consider
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\textbf{Cond}(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathds{S}})
,
\phi\in\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathds{S}}
and a ranking strategy
r
. We say

{\mathcal{K}\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx_{r}\phi}
iff
T^{-1}(r(T(\mathcal{K}))\Vdash\phi
.

It is worth noting here that the equivalence of models in the conditional-PDSL case and the original KLM case means that algorithms for this case can be used equivalently in the PDSL case. In the most general case for ranked interpretations, this is given by the
DefeasibleEntail
algorithm
5
which takes a ranking-based model in the propositional case and uses SAT-solving methods to compute whether a conditional is satisfied by this model. We therefore obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 1
.

Let
\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx_{r}
be the entailment associated with the ranking strategy
r
, and let
\mathcal{K}
be a
\textbf{Cond}(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathds{S}})
knowledge base and
\phi\in\textbf{Cond}(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathds{S}})
. Then,
\mathcal{K}\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx_{r}\phi\text{ iff. }\mathtt{DefeasibleEntail}(T(\mathcal{K}),r(T(\mathcal{K})),T(\phi))=\text{True}
.

Another upshot of this is that checking a query in
\textbf{Cond}(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathds{S}})
is in the same complexity class as the translated query for conditionals in propositional logic. However, although the entailment we have defined can only be computed with knowledge bases in
\textbf{Cond}(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathds{S}})
, we are able to compute queries for larger fragments of
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathds{S}}
. Specifically, we want to consider query-answering for a fragment of PDSL where we can answer queries with defeasible and classical diamond standpoint modalities. Using the syntax proposed in
2
this can be formulated by:

Definition 12
.

The fragment
\textbf{NegCond}(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathds{S}})\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathds{S}}
is the fragment of
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathds{S}}
consisting of all formulas of the form

\phi::=\alpha\leadsto_{e}\beta\mid e\lesssim d\mid\ \mathrm{not}\ \phi

where
\mathrm{not}:=\neg\Box_{*}
,
\alpha
and
\beta
are Boolean formulas, and
e,d\in\mathcal{E}
are standpoint expressions.

Note that the compound unary connective
\mathrm{not}
allows us to introduce classical or defeasible diamond statements into our language by Proposition
2
, where
\Diamond_{e}\alpha
is equivalent to
\mathrm{not}(\alpha\leadsto_{e}\bot)
and
\scalebox{0.8}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{\rotatebox{57.0}{$\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip-1.07639pt\hbox{$\shortmid$}}}}\hskip-0.12915pt\joinrel\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip 0.21529pt\hbox{$\sim$}\vskip 1.07639pt\hbox{$\sim$}}}}\hskip-0.21529pt\joinrel\mathrel{\vbox{\vskip-3.01385pt\hbox{$\shortmid$}}}$}}}\hskip-1.29167pt_{e}\alpha
is equivalent to
\mathrm{not}(\top\leadsto_{e}\alpha)
. The syntax chosen for
\mathrm{not}
is used due to the similarity with negation-as-failure in the logic programming tradition. We note that
\mathrm{not}
is a strictly weaker notion than
\neg
and that
\mathrm{not}
behaves according to the principle of negation as failure in our semantics.

Proposition 6
.

Given an SPSS
M
and
\phi\in\textbf{Cond}(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathds{S}})
then we have:

1.

M\Vdash\neg\phi
implies
M\Vdash\mathrm{not}\phi
, and the converse does not hold in general.

2.

M\Vdash\mathrm{not}\phi
iff.
M\nVdash\phi
.

Since for each ranking strategy
r

\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx_{r}
relative to a single SPSS, the symbol
\mathrm{not}
acts as negation as failure on the level of non-monotonic entailment as well.

Corollary 2
.

Given a ranking strategy
r
, a formula
\phi\in\textbf{Cond}(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathds{S}})
and a knowledge base
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\textbf{Cond}(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathds{S}})
:
\mathcal{K}\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx_{r}\mathrm{not}\phi
iff
\mathcal{K}\not\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx_{r}\phi
iff
\mathtt{DefeasibleEntail}(T(\mathcal{K}),r_{T(\mathcal{K})},T(\phi))=\text{False}
.

This in turn shows that
\mathrm{not}\phi

DefeasibleEntail
algorithm, and that the query language can be expanded in this way with no effects to the complexity. We also note that we can compute queries which are conjunctions of formulas in
\textbf{NegCond}(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathds{S}})
by just considering a conjunction as a set of its conjuncts and computing
DefeasibleEntail
for each such conjunct. Disjunctions of conditionals in general cannot be computed by evaluating each disjunct. For example, it may be the case, for an SPSS

M\nVdash\phi_{1}
and
M\nVdash\phi_{2}
. This is not a feature specific to standpoint logics, but to the more general defeasible modal logic

The last thing we note in the general case is how queries of diamond-statements behave in a more “permissive” way than in classical propositional standpoint logic. In particular, in our definition of non-monotonic entailment for a ranking strategy
r
, we essentially include in a standpoint
e
every valuation which is allowable from the knowledge base. That is, we conclude
\Diamond_{e}\alpha
(for a Boolean statement
\alpha
) whenever
\Diamond_{e}\alpha
is not directly precluded from the knowledge base. Therefore, although for classical Boolean statements and box statements our logic is supraclassical, when it comes to classical diamond queries, our logic answers queries more in the style of brave reasoning. This is formalised by the propositions below. We recall here that
\vDash_{P}
is the Tarskian notion of preferential entailment defined at the end of Section
2
.

Proposition 7
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a conditional PDSL knowledge base, and let
\alpha
be a Boolean statement, and
r
be a well-defined ranking strategy. Then
\mathcal{K}\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx_{r}\alpha
iff
\mathcal{K}\vDash_{P}\alpha
. Furthermore,
\mathcal{K}\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx_{r}\Box_{e}\alpha
iff
\mathcal{K}\vDash_{P}\Box_{e}\alpha
.

Proposition 8
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a conditional PDSL knowledge base, and let
\alpha
be a Boolean statement, and
r
be a (valid) ranking strategy. Then
\mathcal{K}\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx_{r}\Diamond_{e}\alpha
iff there exists a model of
\mathcal{K}
,
M
, such that
M\Vdash\Diamond_{e}\alpha
.

We note that this is a departure from convention in conditional reasoning, where supraclassicality holds for all non-defeasible parts of the logic. However, we believe that brave reasoning for diamond bound statements is in the spirit of non-monotonic prototypical reasoning, since we assume standpoints to hold all beliefs as possible, unless we learn evidence which shows otherwise.

Related Work and Conclusions

Another approach to integrating KLM-style defeasibility and standpoint logics is given by Defeasible Restricted Standpoint logic (DRSL), introduced by Leisegang et al.
18
. DRSL captures standpoints which hold beliefs that are defeasible conditionals in nature, while PDSL captures classical beliefs which are held defeasibly by standpoints. In particular, the preference ordering in PDSL semantics is defined over the set of precisifications
\Pi
, whereas in DRSL semantics, there is no ordering on
\Pi
and instead each precisification
\pi\in\Pi
is mapped to a preferential interpretation. As such, DRSL semantics do not express defeasible modalities, while PDSL was constructed explicitly in order to express such modalities. Gorczyca and Straß
13
consider another method of non-monotonic reasoning with standpoint modalities, by integrating standpoints and the non-monotonic modal logic S4F. Preferential semantics and defeasible modalities have been considered in the modal logic
K
by Britz et al.
3
and in linear temporal logic by Chafik et al.
8
, although these focus on monotonic entailment. Outside of modal logics, KLM-style rational defeasible entailments have been also been lifted to Description Logics. Britz et al.
2
consider the general case, while rational closure
11
;
2
;
21
and lexicographic closure
7
;
4
have also been considered specifically.

In this paper, we have proposed a systematic means to lift rational defeasible entailment relations from the propositional case to a fragment of PDSL. In particular, we made the case that the preferential entailment introduced in PDSL by Leisegang et al.
19
was unnecessarily inferentially weak, and sought to remedy this by introducing non-monotonic inferences from the propositional case into PDSL. In doing so, we enhanced the expressivity of PDSL through
standpoint situated conditionals
, showing that the syntax of PDSL may be rephrased in terms of these conditionals. We then identified the fragment
\textbf{Cond}(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathds{S}})
of PDSL consisting of just conditionals and showed that this fragment can be translated into traditional KLM conditional knowledge bases, while preserving model satisfaction. Using this translation, we lifted the class of rational defeasible entailment relations to
\textbf{Cond}(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathds{S}})
in the general case, before focussing on the well-known systems of rational and lexicographic closure in the conditional-PDSL case. In all these cases, we showed that propositional algorithms can be used to compute the conditional-PDSL case, and hence that the complexity of reasoning in conditional-PDSL is the same as in the propositional case.

Proposition

Lemma

.

\mathcal{K}
is a satisfiable conditional PDSL knowledge base and

Since

T(\mathcal{K})

\mathcal{K}

\sigma(s)\neq

Proposition

Proposition
8
.
Let
\mathcal{K}
be a conditional PDSL knowledge base, and let
\alpha
be a Boolean statement, and
r
be a (valid) ranking strategy. Then
\mathcal{K}\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx_{r}\Diamond_{e}\alpha
iff there exists a model of
\mathcal{K}
,
M
, such that
M\Vdash\Diamond_{e}\alpha
.

Proof.

Clearly, if
\mathcal{K}\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx_{r}\Diamond_{e}\alpha

T^{-1}(r(T(\mathcal{K}))
is a model of
\mathcal{K}
such that
T^{-1}(r(T(\mathcal{K}))\Vdash\Diamond_{e}\alpha
.

We then prove the converse. Assume that there is some SPSS

such that

and
M\Vdash\Diamond_{e}\alpha
. Then, it follows that
M\nVdash\Box_{e}\neg\alpha
, or equivalently
M\nVdash\alpha\leadsto_{e}\bot
. By Proposition
4
we have that
T(M)\nVdash\alpha\wedge t(e)\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim\bot
. Since
T(M)
is a model of
T(\mathcal{K})
(by Proposition
4
) then
T(\mathcal{K})\nvDash_{pref}\alpha\wedge t(e)\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim\bot

.
