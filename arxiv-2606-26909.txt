Quasiclassical theory of nonlinear response in d d -wave superconductors
Aidan Steineman, Samuel Awelewa, Maxim Dzero

Abstract
We use a self-consistent Keldysh–Nambu quasiclassical theory to study two related nonlinear phenomena in clean d d -wave superconductors: the photo-induced static correction to the order parameter – the Eliashberg effect – and third-harmonic generation. Both follow from a systematic perturbative solution of the out-of-equilibrium Eilenberger equation for the Keldysh propagator. For the steady-state correction to the pairing amplitude we find that at temperatures close to the critical temperature and to leading order in the gap magnitude Δ \Delta , the photo-induced change of the order parameter is zero at all drive frequencies: in contrast to s s -wave superconductors, a clean d d -wave superconductor exhibits no Eliashberg enhancement at this order. The gap-enhancing quasiparticle-redistribution channel that drives the effect in the s s -wave case is suppressed by an additional power of Δ \Delta in the d d -wave case. For third-harmonic generation we find that the charge-density-fluctuation (particle–hole) channel significantly dominates the Schmid–Higgs amplitude-mode contribution over a broad frequency range, the two becoming comparable only in a narrow window near the resonance frequency ω ≈ 2 ​ 2 ​ Δ \omega\approx 2\sqrt{2}\,\Delta if one neglects the diamagnetic part of the current in the normal state. We trace this to the nonequilibrium dynamics of nodal quasiparticles, which must be retained explicitly and which also makes the response sensitive to the orientation of the driving field.



I
Introduction

Optical probes of correlated electronic matter beyond linear response have recently become one of the major experimental tools for studying collective excitations. The nonlinear electromagnetic response of superconductors allows one to probe their collective modes, associated with the amplitude (Schmid–Higgs)
Schmid 1968
;
Sooryakumar and Klein 1980
;
Littlewood and Varma 1981
;
Littlewood and Varma 1982
;
Méasson et al. 2014
;
Anderson 2015
;
Shimano and Tsuji 2020
;
Volovik and Zubkov 2014
;
Pekker and Varma 2015
and phase (Carlson–Goldman) fluctuations of the order parameter
Carlson and Goldman 1973
;
Artemenko and Volkov 1975
;
Artemenko and Volkov 1979
;
Schmid and Schön 1975
;
Schmid and Schön 1979
;
Kulik et al. 1981
. Even though the Schmid–Higgs mode is arguably not a true collective mode — for small deviations from equilibrium it exhibits power-law Landau damping — its dynamics can still be probed on relatively short time scales
Volkov and Kogan 1974
. As a side remark we would like to add that only for sufficiently strong deviations from equilibrium the amplitude mode exhibits undamped periodic oscillations
Barankov et al. 2004
;
Yuzbashyan et al. 2005a
;
Yuzbashyan et al. 2005b
;
Barankov and Levitov 2006
;
Yuzbashyan et al. 2006
;
Yuzbashyan 2008
;
Yuzbashyan et al. 2015
. In any case, it is well established that the amplitude mode is most directly accessed through third-harmonic generation, where it produces a resonant response when the drive frequency matches the order-parameter scale
Silaev 2019
;
Moor et al. 2017
;
Schwarz et al. 2020
.

In conventional
s
-wave superconductors with the order parameter
\Delta_{0}
this resonance appears at frequency
\omega=2\Delta_{0}
and is sharp, making the third-harmonic signal a clean probe of the amplitude mode. One should also keep in mind that in addition to the third harmonic response from the Schmid-Higgs mode there is also a contribution from the charge density fluctuations, i.e. from the particle-hole channel, so that the third harmonic response of the current density in a superconductor can be represented as a sum of two distinct contributions

{\mathbf{j}}^{(3\omega)}(t)={\mathbf{j}}_{\textrm{SH}}^{(3\omega)}(t)+{\mathbf{j}}_{\textrm{CDF}}^{(3\omega)}(t),

(1)

where
{\mathbf{j}}_{\textrm{SH}}^{(3\omega)}\propto\delta{\Delta}_{\omega}^{L}
accounts for the response due to the amplitude (Schmid-Higgs) mode fluctuations, while
{\mathbf{j}}_{\textrm{CDF}}^{(3\omega)}
is governed mainly by charge density fluctuations in a superconductor. Here
\delta{\Delta}_{\omega}^{L}
is proportional to the product of the longitudinal pair susceptibility
\chi_{\textrm{SH}}(\omega)
and the square of external vector potential,
{\mathbf{A}}^{2}
.
Whether the first term in (
1
) dominates over the second one or not mainly depends on the frequency of external electromagnetic pulse. For the case of
s
-wave superconductor the imaginary part of
\chi_{\textrm{SH}}(\omega)
is singular at
\omega=2\Delta_{0}
and it is therefore expected that measurements of the third harmonic response would provide a direct experimental probe of the amplitude mode fluctuations
Tsuji and Aoki 2015
;
Cea et al. 2016
. These theoretical predictions have indeed been confirmed experimentally
Matsunaga et al. 2014
;
Matsunaga et al. 2017
;
Matsunaga et al. 2013
.

In the case of the
d
-wave superconductors, by contrast, the nodes in the order parameter remove this singularity. This has a major implication for the frequency dependence of the longitudinal pair susceptibility since its imaginary part remains finite in the immediate vicinity of the Schmid-Higgs resonance

\textrm{Im}\left[\chi_{\textrm{SH}}(\nu)\right]\sim\nu\log\nu
,
where dimensionless parameter
\nu=|\omega-2\sqrt{2}\Delta_{0}|/2\sqrt{2}\Delta_{0}
has been assumed to be small
Yuan et al. 2024
;
Islam et al. 2026
. The extra factor of
\sqrt{2}
appears here due to the normalized
d
-wave spectral factor
\sqrt{2}(n_{x}^{2}-n_{y}^{2})
(
\mathbf{n}=\mathbf{p}_{F}/p_{F}
and
\mathbf{p}_{F}
is the Fermi momentum).

In their work Schwarz and Manske
Schwarz and Manske 2020
studied the third-harmonic response in unconventional superconductors, with particular focus on
d
- and
d+s
-wave order parameters. For the

d
-wave case they found that, for a certain polarization of the external field, the Schmid–Higgs contribution to (
1
) significantly dominates the charge-density-fluctuation one. At first sight this is surprising, since — as noted above — the

d
-wave Schmid–Higgs susceptibility is not singular in frequency and provides no obvious mechanism for such a pronounced enhancement. The resolution is that the dominance reflects a suppression of the charge density channel rather than an enhancement of the amplitude one: it appears at the special polarization at which the charge-fluctuation contribution is geometrically suppressed.

In Ref.
Schwarz and Manske 2020
the third-harmonic contribution to the current density was computed using the following expression

{\mathbf{j}}(t)=e\sum\limits_{\mathbf{k}}{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathbf{k}-\frac{e}{c}{\mathbf{A}}(t)}n_{\mathbf{k}}(t),

(2)

where
{\mathbf{A}}(t)
is an external vector potential,
\mathbf{k}
is the single particle momentum,
{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathbf{k}}
is the band velocity and
n_{\mathbf{k}}(t)
is the charge density. Computed this way, the response further underestimates the charge-fluctuation channel, reinforcing the apparent dominance noted above. The physical reason for such result stems from the fact that in the case of the
d
-wave superconductor there are significant charge density fluctuations associated with the scattering of the nodal quasiparticles. On a technical level it follows that in the calculation of the third harmonic current in
d
-wave superconductors one needs to explicitly take into account single-particle excitations especially near the nodal points of the order parameter. At low temperatures these processes will be exponentially suppressed in an
s
-wave case which may justify the use of (
2
) in this case
Tsuji and Aoki 2015
;
Cea et al. 2016
;
Seibold et al. 2021
;
Wang et al. 2026
. In contrast, these processes are only suppressed in a power-law fashion according to
\sim(T/\Delta_{0})^{2}
in
d
-wave superconductors. Thus, while the use of (
2
) in the
s
-wave case can be justified and one can work with an equilibrium single-particle distribution function due to the separation of the corresponding time-scales,
\hbar/\Delta_{0}\ll\hbar\varepsilon_{F}/\Delta_{0}^{2}
(
\varepsilon_{F}
is the Fermi energy), it is strictly speaking not justified in a transport calculation for nodal superconductors. Lastly, (
2
) also unjustifiably ignores the presence of the vector potential in the momentum dependence of the order parameter, which not only leads to a change in the relative contribution to (
1
), but also produces the sensitivity of the response function to a direction of external field due to anisotropy of the
d
-wave order parameter.

A second, closely related manifestation of the nonlinear response is the Eliashberg effect — the steady-state modification of the order parameter by an external field, originally predicted as a microwave-induced enhancement of superconductivity in

s
-wave superconductors
Eliashberg 1970
;
Ivlev and Eliashberg 1971
;
Tikhonov et al. 2020
;
Curtis et al. 2019
;
Arora et al. 2025
. To the best of our knowledge, its fate in the

d
-wave case, where nodal quasiparticles dominate the low-energy absorption, has received far less attention. Since both third-harmonic generation and the Eliashberg effect are controlled by the same out-of-equilibrium quasiparticle dynamics, it is natural to treat them within a single theoretical framework.

In this paper we formulate self-consistent Keldysh-Nambu microscopic theory of third harmonic generation in clean
d
-wave superconductors. Our theory is based on a systematic perturbative solution for the Keldysh propagator of the out-of-equilibrium quasiclassical Eilenberger equation. Using the resulting expression for the Keldysh propagator in the first part we evaluate the static correction to the order parameter and show that it remains zero to the lowest order in powers of the order parameter at temperatures close to the critical temperature. This result implies that for the Eliashberg effect to emerge in clean
d
-wave superconductors one needs to retain terms
O(\Delta^{3})
and also to explicitly take into account the inelastic scattering processes.

In the second part we compute the third harmonic contribution to the current density. In general agreement with the conclusion of Ref.
Schwarz and Manske 2020
we find that in a broad range of frequencies, the charge density fluctuations contribution
{\mathbf{j}}_{\textrm{CDF}}^{(3\omega)}
significantly dominates the Schmid-Higgs one. Only in the fairly narrow region of frequencies
\omega\sim 2\sqrt{2}\Delta_{0}
do we find that both of these contributions, when they originate from the pairing (i.e. condensate) degrees of freedom, are of the same order.

Our paper is organized as follows. In the next Section we provide a brief summary of the quasiclassical formalism which we then use to determine the static correction to the pairing amplitude which is proportional to the second power of the external vector potential. In Section III we use our results from Section II to compute the third harmonic contribution to the current density. Section IV is devoted to the discussion of our results and conclusions. Lastly, main technical details of the calculations are summarized in Appendices
A
–
E
.

II
Nonlinear response

The main object of our study will be quasiclassical Green’s function defined in Keldysh and Nambu spaces:
Serene and Rainer 1983
;
Kopnin 2001

\check{g}=\left[\begin{matrix}\hat{g}^{R}&\hat{g}^{K}\\
0&\hat{g}^{A}\end{matrix}\right].

(3)

This function can be found by solving the Eilenberger equation
Eilenberger 1968
;
Larkin 1965
;
Larkin and Ovchinnikov 1977
;
Belzig et al. 1999
:

\displaystyle[\varepsilon\check{\tau}_{3}+\check{\Delta}_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathbf{r},t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{,}}\check{g}]+\frac{i}{2}\left\{\check{\tau}_{3},\partial_{t}\check{g}\right\}

(4)

\displaystyle+\frac{ev_{F}}{c}[\mathbf{n}{\mathbf{A}}(\mathbf{r},t)\check{\tau}_{3}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{,}}\check{g}]+i{v}_{F}(\mathbf{n}\mbox{\boldmath$\nabla$}_{\mathbf{r}})\check{g}=0.

Here

{\mathbf{A}}(\mathbf{r},t)=\left(\frac{c{\mathbf{E}}}{i\omega}\right)e^{i(\mathbf{k}\mathbf{r}-\omega t)}+\textrm{c.c.}

(5)

is an external vector potential,
{\mathbf{E}}
is an electric field,
\check{\tau}_{3}
is the third Pauli matrix diagonal in Keldysh space and
\check{\Delta}_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathbf{r},t)
is the
d
-wave pairing field. Eilenberger equation (
4
) must be supplemented by the normalization condition

\check{g}\circ\check{g}=\check{{\mathbbm{1}}}.

(6)

The convolution should be understood as follows

\begin{split}\left[\check{A}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{,}}\check{B}\right]&=\int\frac{d\epsilon}{2\pi}\left\{\check{A}(\mathbf{r},t)e^{-\frac{i}{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\partial}}_{t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rightarrow}}{{\partial}}_{\epsilon}}\check{B}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon;\mathbf{r},t)\right.\\
&\left.-\check{B}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon;\mathbf{r},t)e^{\frac{i}{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\partial}}_{\epsilon}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rightarrow}}{{\partial}}_{t}}\check{A}(\mathbf{r},t)\right\}e^{-i\epsilon(t_{1}-t_{2})}.\end{split}

(7)

In the ground state the Eilenberger equation (
4
) can be easily solved with the order parameter represented by

\hat{\Delta}(\mathbf{n};\mathbf{r},t)=\left(i\hat{\tau}_{2}\right){\cal Y}_{\mathbf{n}}\Delta
,
{\cal Y}_{\mathbf{n}}=\sqrt{2}(n_{x}^{2}-n_{y}^{2})
is a normalized
d
-wave form factor,
\mathbf{n}=\mathbf{p}_{F}/p_{F}

and

\hat{g}_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R(A)}=\hat{\tau}_{3}g_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R(A)}+i\hat{\tau}_{2}f_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R(A)}.

(8)

Given the normalization condition (
6
) the Keldysh component is a simple parametrization

\hat{g}_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{K}=\left(\hat{g}_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R}-\hat{g}_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{A}\right)t_{\epsilon}

(9)

and we used the shorthand notation
t_{\epsilon}=\tanh\left({\epsilon}/{2T}\right)
.
After simple algebra for the retarded components of the correction function we find

g_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R}={\epsilon}/{\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R}}
and

f_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R}={\Delta_{\mathbf{n}}}/{\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R}}
,
where functions
\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R(A)}
are defined as follows:

\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R(A)}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\pm\mathrm{sgn}(\epsilon)\sqrt{(\epsilon\pm i\delta)^{2}-\Delta_{\mathbf{n}}^{2}},~|\epsilon|\geq|\Delta_{\mathbf{n}}|,\\
&i\sqrt{\Delta_{\mathbf{n}}^{2}-\epsilon^{2}},\quad|\epsilon|<|\Delta_{\mathbf{n}}|\end{aligned}\right.

(10)

and
\Delta_{\mathbf{n}}={\cal Y}_{\mathbf{n}}\Delta
.
We can now use these expressions to determine the perturbative corrections to
\check{g}
in powers of
{\mathbf{A}}
.
The pairing gap in equilibrium is determined by solving the self-consistency equation

\frac{1}{\lambda}=\frac{1}{\Delta}\int\limits_{-\omega_{D}}^{\omega_{D}}d\epsilon\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\phi_{\mathbf{n}}}{2\pi}{\cal Y}_{\mathbf{n}}\left(f_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R}-f_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{A}\right)t_{\epsilon},

(11)

where
\lambda
is the dimensionless pairing strength.

II.1
Second order response

Calculation of the first order corrections to
\check{g}
is described in Appendix
A
so here we start with the calculation of the second order corrections which determine the variation of the order parameter.
The second order correction to the function
\check{g}
is formally given by

\displaystyle\check{g}_{2}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon;\mathbf{r},t)

\displaystyle=\check{g}_{2}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon;\mathbf{k}\omega)+\check{g}_{2,+}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon;\mathbf{k}\omega)e^{2i(\mathbf{k}\mathbf{r}-\omega t)}

(12)

\displaystyle+\check{g}_{2,-}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon;\mathbf{k}\omega)e^{-2i(\mathbf{k}\mathbf{r}-\omega t)}.

As it has been already mentioned above there is a correction to the longitudinal component of the pairing field which we represent as

\displaystyle\delta\hat{\Delta}_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathbf{r},t)=i\hat{\tau}_{2}

\displaystyle\left\{\delta\Delta_{\mathbf{n}}+\delta\Delta_{\mathbf{n}}^{L}(\mathbf{q},\nu)e^{i(\mathbf{q}\mathbf{r}-\nu t)}\right.

\delta\Delta_{\omega}=\frac{\lambda}{2}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\epsilon\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\phi_{\mathbf{n}}}{2\pi}{\cal Y}_{\mathbf{n}}\textrm{Tr}\left\{(-i\hat{\tau}_{2})\hat{g}_{2}^{K}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon;\omega)\right\}.

We will look for second order correction to the Keldysh function using the standard ansatz:

{\hat{g}_{2}^{K}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon;\omega)=\left(\hat{g}_{2}^{R}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon;\omega)-\hat{g}_{2}^{A}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon;\omega)\right)t_{\epsilon}+\delta\hat{g}_{2}^{K}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon;\omega).}

\displaystyle\hat{g}_{2}^{R(A)}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon;\omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(\delta\hat{\Delta}_{\mathbf{n}}-\hat{g}_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R(A)}\delta\hat{\Delta}_{\mathbf{n}}\hat{g}_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R(A)}\right)}{2\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R(A)}}

(17)

\displaystyle+{\hat{g}_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R(A)}}\hat{Y}_{2}^{R(A)}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon;\omega).

Similarly, we find the following expression for the function
\delta\hat{g}_{2}^{K}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon;\omega)

\displaystyle\delta\hat{g}_{2}^{K}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon;\omega)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{A}}{\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R}+\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{A}}\right)\hat{g}_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R}\hat{Y}_{2}^{K}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon;\omega)

(18)

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{1}{\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R}+\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{A}}\right)\hat{g}_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R}\hat{Z}_{2}^{K}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon;\omega).

\hat{Y}_{2}^{K}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon;\omega)

II.2
Suppression of superconductivity by external radiation

Having expressed
\hat{g}_{2}^{K}
in terms of the ground state propagators we return to the self-consistency condition (
15
). We can cast (
15
) into the following form

\left(\frac{2}{\lambda}\right)\delta\Delta_{\omega}=F_{\textrm{reg.}}(\omega)+F_{\textrm{anom.}}(\omega).

(19)

Here function
F_{\textrm{reg.}}(\omega)
is determined by

\displaystyle F_{\textrm{reg.}}(\omega)=\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\phi_{\mathbf{n}}}{2\pi}{\cal Y}_{\mathbf{n}}

(20)

\displaystyle\times\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\textrm{Tr}\left\{(-i\hat{\tau}_{2})[\hat{g}_{2}^{R}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon;\omega)-\hat{g}_{2}^{A}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon;\omega)]\right\}t_{\epsilon}{d\epsilon}.

while function
F_{\textrm{anom.}}(\omega)
is given by

\displaystyle F_{\textrm{anom.}}(\omega)=\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}{d\epsilon}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\phi_{\mathbf{n}}}{2\pi}\frac{{\cal Y}_{\mathbf{n}}}{\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R}+\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{A}}

(21)

\displaystyle\times\textrm{Tr}\left\{(-i\hat{\tau}_{2})\hat{g}_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R}\left[\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{A}\hat{Y}_{2}^{K}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon;\omega)+\hat{Z}_{2}^{K}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon;\omega)\right]\right\}.

It can be easily checked that the resulting frequency dependence of
F_{\textrm{reg.}}(\omega)
depends on the ultraviolet cutoff. However, some of the terms generated after the trace has been evaluated can be recombined with the term on the left hand side (
19
) which yields the ultraviolet convergent result. The details of this procedure are given in Appendix
B
.

As it is well known, the Eliashberg effect is governed by redistribution of the single particles to higher energies
Dayem and Wiegand 1967
;
Wyatt et al. 1966
;
Eliashberg 1970
;
Tikhonov et al. 2020
;
Derendorf et al. 2024
;
Ivlev and Eliashberg 1971
;
Klapwijk and de
Visser 2020
. Since
F_{\textrm{reg.}}(\omega)
contains the Fermi-Dirac distribution function via the relation
t_{\epsilon}=1-2n_{F}(\epsilon)
, its contribution accounts for the processes which lead to an increase in the value of the pairing amplitude. In contrast, the remaining contribution describes the effects associated with out-of-equilibrium single particle distribution. Specifically,
F_{\textrm{anom.}}(\omega)
determines the deviations of the single-particle distribution function from equilibrium and, as such, must account for both pair breaking processes and processes which may lead to enhancement of the order parameter.

III
Third harmonic generation

The third harmonic contribution to the current density defined by

{\mathbf{j}}^{(3\omega)}=\frac{e\nu_{F}v_{F}}{4}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\mathbf{n}\frac{d\phi_{\mathbf{n}}}{2\pi}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\textrm{Tr}\left\{\hat{\tau}_{3}\hat{g}_{3}^{K}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon;\omega)\right\}d\epsilon.

(24)

In this expression
\nu_{F}
is the density of states at the Fermi level and
v_{F}=p_{F}/m
is the Fermi velocity. It is important for us to note here that (
24
) does not take into account the effects associated with the curvature of the single particle spectrum
{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathbf{k}}=\partial\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}/\partial\mathbf{k}

24

2
) by setting
\Delta=0

Since the calculation of
\hat{g}_{3}^{K}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon;\omega)
is fully analogous to the one above we delegate the technical details to Appendix
C

The first step consists in representing
\hat{g}_{3}^{K}

\hat{g}_{3}^{K}=\hat{g}_{3}^{R}t_{\epsilon-\frac{3\omega}{2}}-t_{\epsilon+\frac{3\omega}{2}}\hat{g}_{3}^{A}+\delta\hat{g}_{3}^{K}.

(25)

Here
\delta\hat{g}_{3}^{K}
accounts for the variations in the single-particle distribution function. For the functions
\hat{g}_{3}^{R(A)}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon;\omega)
we find

\displaystyle\hat{g}_{3}^{R(A)}=\frac{\hat{g}_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\frac{3\omega}{2}}^{R(A)}\left[\hat{Q}_{\delta\Delta}^{R(A)}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon;\omega)+\hat{Q}_{{\mathbf{E}}}^{R(A)}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon;\omega)\right]}{\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\frac{3\omega}{2}}^{R(A)}+\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\frac{3\omega}{2}}^{R(A)}}.

(26)

and functions entering into this expressions are given by (
89
) and (

\delta\hat{g}_{3}^{K}
is given by (
97
) in Appendix

.

The subsequent calculation of traces using the analytical expressions obtained above produces very large number of terms which all contribute to the same order in both Schmid-Higgs and charge density fluctuation channel. This fact makes our calculation barely tractable. Since many experiments are performed at temperatures not too far below the critical temperature we will limit our analysis here to the case when
T\sim T_{c}
. To the linear order in
\Delta

g_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R(A)}\approx\pm 1

IV
Discussion

The absence of radiation-stimulated superconductivity established above has a
limited range of validity, and we believe that it needs to be discussed further. Our calculation has only shown
that near
T_{c}
and at leading order in
\Delta
the anomalous, out-of-equilibrium
contribution to the gap equation is positive—pair-breaking—at all drive
frequencies and cancels exactly the boost effect, so that the net effect is zero,
\delta\Delta_{\omega}=0
. The redistribution term that produces
the Eliashberg effect in
s
-wave superconductors, parametrically enhanced by the
inelastic scattering rate
\gamma_{\textrm{in}}
and gap-enhancing for

\omega<2\Delta
, has vanishing contribution in the
d
-wave case at
O(\Delta)
and we expect it to
re-enter only at next order
O(\Delta^{3})
. A radiation-induced enhancement is therefore not
excluded in clean
d
-wave superconductors, but it is parametrically smaller than in
the
s
-wave case and requires both a higher order in
\Delta
and an explicit
treatment of inelastic processes which are needed to prevent the pair-breaking processes.

A central feature of our analysis of third-harmonic generation is the role of the
normal,
O(\Delta^{0})
, part of the charge channel. The quasiclassical current
(
24
) is evaluated with the Fermi-surface velocity
v_{F}\mathbf{n}
and hence
possesses no
\Delta
-independent piece: it vanishes in the normal state, and both
condensate channels—Schmid–Higgs and charge-density-fluctuation—enter at

O(\Delta^{2})
. The dominance of the charge channel near
T_{c}
is thus not visible at
the level of these condensate contributions, which are comparable and become of the
same order only near
\omega=2\sqrt{2}\,\Delta
; it originates instead in the
normal-state response recovered from the full current (
2
) by setting

\Delta=0
, which is generated by the curvature of the single-particle spectrum,

\partial\mathbf{v}/\partial\mathbf{k}
, that is absent from the linearized quasiclassical
current. This is at once the physical origin of the charge-channel dominance and a
limitation of the quasiclassical scheme: a complete quantitative account of the
relative weight of the two channels requires restoring the band curvature in the
current operator. However, it is clear that the
\Delta=0
part of the charge density fluctuation channel will be
at least of the order of
O(\varepsilon_{F}/T_{c})
larger.

The two channels are governed by two distinct frequency scales, which suggests a way
to disentangle them experimentally
Katsumi et al. 2018
;
Chu et al. 2020
;
Katsumi et al. 2023
. The amplitude-mode response is controlled by the
longitudinal pair susceptibility
\chi_{\textrm{SH}}(\omega)
, whose (smeared) feature
sits at
\omega=2\sqrt{2}\,\Delta
and therefore tracks the gap as the temperature is
varied. The nonequilibrium quasiparticle response that feeds the charge channel, by
contrast, is set by the thermal scale: the kernel
I(\omega,T)
in
(
32
) is a universal function of
\omega/T
, so that its characteristic
frequency—including the sign change of its real part—scales with temperature
rather than with
\Delta
. A third-harmonic measurement able to resolve a

\Delta
-tracking scale from a
T
-tracking one would directly separate the
amplitude-mode and charge-fluctuation contributions.

V
Conclusions

We have formulated a self-consistent Keldysh–Nambu quasiclassical theory of the
nonlinear electromagnetic response of clean
d
-wave superconductors and, using a single framework, applied it to the steady-state correction to the order
parameter (Eliashberg effect) and to third-harmonic generation. We found that near
T_{c}
and to leading
order in
\Delta
the photo-induced correction to the pairing amplitude is zero at
all drive frequencies, so that no Eliashberg enhancement occurs at this order. Compared to the
s
-wave case the
gap-enhancing redistribution channel is therefore suppressed by an
additional power of
\Delta
. For third-harmonic generation we found that the
Schmid–Higgs and charge-density-fluctuation contributions arising from the condensate
are both
O(\Delta^{2})
and comparable only in a narrow window around

\omega=2\sqrt{2}\,\Delta
, while over a broad range of frequencies the charge channel
dominates through its normal,
O(\Delta^{0})
, component. We identify the microscopic mechanism that drives this response as
the non-equilibrium dynamics of nodal quasiparticles. Our results are in general agreement with the
clean-limit conclusion of Ref.
Schwarz and Manske 2020
.

We expect that our results remain valid far below
T_{c}
, where the nodal quasiparticles
that control the charge channel are suppressed only as a power law,

\sim(T/\Delta_{0})^{2}
, rather than exponentially. A quantitative treatment at

T\ll T_{c}
, the inclusion of inelastic scattering and disorder, the restoration of
band curvature in the current operator, and the experimental signatures of the
predicted polarization and temperature dependence of the third-harmonic response
remain natural directions for future work.

A.1
First order correction: retarded and advanced components

Given (
5

Equation which determines the first order correction to the retarded and advanced components of

[\varepsilon\hat{\tau}_{3}+\hat{\Delta}_{\mathbf{n}},\hat{g}_{1}]+\frac{1}{2}\left\{{\omega}\hat{\tau}_{3}-{v}_{F}(\mathbf{n}\mathbf{k})\hat{\tau}_{0},\hat{g}_{1}\right\}=\left(\frac{ev_{F}}{i\omega}\right)(\mathbf{n}{\mathbf{E}})\left[\hat{g}_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega/2}\hat{\tau}_{3}-\hat{\tau}_{3}\hat{g}_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\omega/2}\right]

(37)

Note that the first term here is approximate. The reason is that
\hat{\Delta}_{\mathbf{n}}=\hat{\Delta}{\cal Y}_{\mathbf{n}}
with
\mathbf{n}=\mathbf{p}_{F}/p_{F}
is which is a function of momentum. Specifically, I recall Groenewold-Moyal product rule:

\hat{A}\circ\hat{B}=\hat{A}_{\mathbf{p}\epsilon}(\mathbf{r},t)\exp\left[\frac{i}{2}\left(\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\partial}}_{\mathbf{r}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rightarrow}}{{\partial}}_{\mathbf{p}}-\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\partial}}_{t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rightarrow}}{{\partial}}_{\epsilon}-\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\partial}}_{\mathbf{p}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rightarrow}}{{\partial}}_{\mathbf{r}}+\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\partial}}_{\epsilon}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rightarrow}}{{\partial}}_{t}\right)\right]\hat{B}_{\mathbf{p}\epsilon}(\mathbf{r},t),

(38)

so that formally we have

\begin{split}&\hat{\Delta}_{\mathbf{p}}\exp\left[\frac{i}{2}\left(\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\partial}}_{\mathbf{r}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rightarrow}}{{\partial}}_{\mathbf{p}}-\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\partial}}_{t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rightarrow}}{{\partial}}_{\epsilon}-\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\partial}}_{\mathbf{p}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rightarrow}}{{\partial}}_{\mathbf{r}}+\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\partial}}_{\epsilon}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rightarrow}}{{\partial}}_{t}\right)\right]\hat{g}_{1}(\mathbf{p}\epsilon;\mathbf{r},t)=\hat{\Delta}_{\mathbf{p}}\exp\left[-\frac{i}{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\partial}}_{\mathbf{p}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rightarrow}}{{\partial}}_{\mathbf{r}}\right]\hat{g}_{1}(\mathbf{p}\epsilon;\mathbf{r},t)\\

(39)

is evaluated at

\mathbf{k}
. Furthermore, the first normalization condition is

\begin{split}&[\varepsilon\hat{\tau}_{3}+\hat{\Delta}_{\mathbf{n}},\hat{g}_{1}]+\frac{1}{2}\left\{{\omega}\hat{\tau}_{3}-{v}_{F}(\mathbf{n}\mathbf{k})\hat{\tau}_{0},\hat{g}_{1}\right\}=\left[\left(\epsilon+\frac{\omega}{2}\right)\hat{\tau}_{3}+\hat{\Delta}_{\mathbf{n}}\right]\hat{g}_{1}-\hat{g}_{1}\left[\left(\epsilon-\frac{\omega}{2}\right)\hat{\tau}_{3}+\hat{\Delta}_{\mathbf{n}}\right]-{v}_{F}(\mathbf{n}\mathbf{k})\hat{g}_{1}\\

(41)

It allows us to re-write (
37

\left[\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\frac{\omega}{2}}+\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\frac{\omega}{2}}-{v}_{F}(\mathbf{n}\mathbf{k})\hat{g}_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\frac{\omega}{2}}\right]\hat{g}_{1}=\left(\frac{ev_{F}}{i\omega}\right)(\mathbf{n}{\mathbf{E}})\left(\hat{\tau}_{3}-\hat{g}_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\frac{\omega}{2}}\hat{\tau}_{3}\hat{g}_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\omega/2}\right),

where I took into account

(43)

Then we have

\hat{g}_{2}^{K}
is of course the same as (
54
):

\begin{split}[\epsilon\hat{\tau}_{3}+\hat{\Delta}_{\mathbf{n}},\hat{g}_{2}^{K}]+\omega\left\{\hat{\tau}_{3},\hat{g}_{2}^{K}\right\}&-2{v}_{F}(\mathbf{n}\mathbf{k})\hat{g}_{2}^{K}=-\left[\delta\hat{\Delta}_{\mathbf{n}}^{L}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{,}}\hat{g}_{0}^{K}\right]\\
&+\left(\frac{ev_{F}}{i\omega}\right)(\mathbf{n}{\mathbf{E}})\left[\hat{g}_{1}^{K}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega/2;\mathbf{k}\omega)\hat{\tau}_{3}-\hat{\tau}_{3}\hat{g}_{1}^{K}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\omega/2;\mathbf{k}\omega)\right].\end{split}

(109)

The solution for
\hat{g}_{2}^{K}
is different from
\hat{g}_{2}^{R(A)}
because it satisfies the different normalization condition:

\begin{split}&\hat{g}_{\epsilon+\omega}^{R}\hat{g}_{2}^{K}+\hat{g}_{\epsilon+\omega}^{K}\hat{g}_{2}^{A}+\hat{g}_{2}^{K}\hat{g}_{\epsilon-\omega}^{A}+\hat{g}_{2}^{R}\hat{g}_{\epsilon-\omega}^{K}\\
&+\hat{g}_{1}^{R}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega/2;\mathbf{k}\omega)\hat{g}_{1}^{K}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\omega/2;\mathbf{k}\omega)+\hat{g}_{1}^{K}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega/2;\mathbf{k}\omega)\hat{g}_{1}^{A}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\omega/2;\mathbf{k}\omega)=0.\end{split}

(110)

Similar to the ansatz (
48
) we will look for the solution of Eq. (
109
) in the following form

{\hat{g}_{2}^{K}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon;\mathbf{k}\omega)=\hat{g}_{2}^{R}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon;\mathbf{k}\omega)t_{\epsilon-\omega}-t_{\epsilon+\omega}\hat{g}_{2}^{A}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon;\mathbf{k}\omega)+\delta\hat{g}_{2}^{K}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon;\mathbf{k}\omega).}

(111)

Inserting this ansatz into (
110
) yields

\hat{g}_{\epsilon+\omega}^{R}\delta\hat{g}_{2}^{K}+\delta\hat{g}_{2}^{K}\hat{g}_{\epsilon-\omega}^{A}=0.

(112)

Furthermore, equation for the function
\delta\hat{g}_{2}^{K}
is

\begin{split}&[\epsilon\hat{\tau}_{3}+\hat{\Delta}_{\mathbf{n}},\delta\hat{g}_{2}^{K}]+\omega\left\{\hat{\tau}_{3},\delta\hat{g}_{2}^{K}\right\}-2{v}_{F}(\mathbf{n}\mathbf{k})\delta\hat{g}_{2}^{K}=\left[\delta\hat{\Delta}_{\mathbf{n}}^{L}\hat{g}_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\omega}^{A}-\hat{g}_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R}\delta\hat{\Delta}_{\mathbf{n}}^{L}\right](t_{\epsilon-\omega}-t_{\epsilon+\omega})\\
&+\left(\frac{ev_{F}}{i\omega}\right)(\mathbf{n}{\mathbf{E}})\left[\hat{g}_{1}^{R}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega/2;\mathbf{k}\omega)\hat{\tau}_{3}(t_{\epsilon}-t_{\epsilon-\omega})-\hat{\tau}_{3}\hat{g}_{1}^{A}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\omega/2;\mathbf{k}\omega)(t_{\epsilon}-t_{\epsilon+\omega})\right]\\
&+\left(\frac{ev_{F}}{i\omega}\right)(\mathbf{n}{\mathbf{E}})\left[\delta\hat{g}_{1}^{K}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega/2;\mathbf{k}\omega)\hat{\tau}_{3}-\hat{\tau}_{3}\delta\hat{g}_{1}^{K}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\omega/2;\mathbf{k}\omega)\right].\end{split}

(113)

Introducing functions

\begin{split}\hat{\Lambda}_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{K}(\mathbf{k}\omega)&=\left(\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R}+\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\omega}^{A}\right)\hat{\tau}_{0}+2v_{F}(\mathbf{n}\mathbf{k})\hat{g}_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R},\\
\hat{\cal R}_{\mathbf{E}}^{K}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon,\mathbf{k}\omega)&=\left(\frac{ev_{F}}{i\omega}\right)(\mathbf{n}{\mathbf{E}})\left[\hat{g}_{1}^{R}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega/2;\mathbf{k}\omega)\hat{\tau}_{3}(t_{\epsilon}-t_{\epsilon-\omega})-\hat{\tau}_{3}\hat{g}_{1}^{A}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\omega/2;\mathbf{k}\omega)(t_{\epsilon}-t_{\epsilon+\omega})\right]\\
&+\left(\frac{ev_{F}}{i\omega}\right)(\mathbf{n}{\mathbf{E}})\left[\delta\hat{g}_{1}^{K}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega/2;\mathbf{k}\omega)\hat{\tau}_{3}-\hat{\tau}_{3}\delta\hat{g}_{1}^{K}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\omega/2;\mathbf{k}\omega)\right].\end{split}

(114)

the resulting expression for the function
\delta\hat{g}_{2}^{K}
reads

{\delta\hat{g}_{2}^{K}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon;\mathbf{k}\omega)=\frac{\hat{\Lambda}_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{K}(\mathbf{k}\omega)\left(\delta\hat{\Delta}_{\mathbf{n}}^{L}-\hat{g}_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R}\delta\hat{\Delta}_{\mathbf{n}}^{L}\hat{g}_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\omega}^{A}\right)(t_{\epsilon+\omega}-t_{\epsilon-\omega})}{\left(\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R}+\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\omega}^{A}\right)^{2}-4{v}_{F}^{2}(\mathbf{n}\mathbf{k})^{2}}+\frac{\hat{g}_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R}\hat{\Lambda}_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{K}(\mathbf{k}\omega)\hat{\cal R}_{\mathbf{E}}^{K}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon,\mathbf{k}\omega)}{\left(\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R}+\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\omega}^{A}\right)^{2}-4{v}_{F}^{2}(\mathbf{n}\mathbf{k})^{2}}.}

(115)

Expressions (
17
,
16
) and (
18
) are the main results of this Section.

Appendix E
Correction to the pairing amplitude

In this Section we will derive the expression for the susceptibility of the amplitude Schmid-Higgs (SH) mode in the clean
d
-wave superconductor.
The expression for the SH susceptibility can be derived from the self-consistency equation

\delta\Delta_{\omega}^{L}=\frac{\lambda}{2}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\phi_{\mathbf{n}}}{2\pi}{\cal Y}(\mathbf{n})\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}{d\epsilon}\textrm{Tr}\left\{-i\hat{\tau}_{2}\hat{g}_{2}^{K}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon;\omega)\right\},

(116)

where
\lambda
is the dimensionless pairing strength. We will consider
\delta\Delta_{\mathbf{n}}^{L}(\omega)
in the form

\delta\Delta_{\mathbf{n}}^{L}(\omega)=\delta\Delta_{\omega}^{L}{\cal Y}(\mathbf{n}).

(117)

Our goal here is to derive an expression for the function
\delta\Delta_{\omega}^{L}
which we will use in the calculation of the third harmonic contribution to the current density. In order to do that we are going to consider separately terms which are proportional to
\delta\Delta_{\omega}^{L}
and those proportional to external electromagnetic field. The equations we are looking for can be formally written as follows:

\chi_{\textrm{SH}}^{-1}(\omega)\delta\Delta_{\omega}^{L}=F_{\textrm{reg.}}(\omega)+F_{\textrm{anom.}}(\omega).

(118)

The expression for
\chi_{\textrm{SH}}^{-1}(\omega)
has been derived in
Islam et al. 2026
and we refer the reader to that reference.

E.1
Contributions from the electric field terms

Now in the expressions for
\hat{g}_{2}^{R(A)}
and
\hat{g}_{2}^{K}
we set
\delta\Delta_{\mathbf{k}\omega}^{L}
to zero. Furthermore mainly for simplicity we will compute the electric field terms by neglecting their momentum dependence, i.e. the resulting momentum dependence of the function
\delta\Delta_{\mathbf{k}\omega}
will be primarily determined by the momentum dependence of the function
\chi_{\textrm{SH}}^{-1}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)
. On purely physics grounds, this is allowed since
k=\omega/c\ll 1/\xi
where
\xi
is the superconducting coherence length.

We start by considering the following trace:

\displaystyle\textrm{Tr}\left\{(-i\hat{\tau}_{2})\hat{\cal R}_{\mathbf{E}}^{R(A)}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon,\mathbf{k}\omega)\right\}_{\textrm{a}}

\displaystyle=\textrm{Tr}\left\{(-i\hat{\tau}_{2})\hat{g}_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}\left[\hat{g}_{1}^{R(A)}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega/2;\mathbf{k}\omega)\hat{\tau}_{3}-\hat{\tau}_{3}\hat{g}_{1}^{R(A)}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\omega/2;\mathbf{k}\omega)\right]\right\}

(119)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{2ev_{F}}{i\omega}\right)(\mathbf{n}{\mathbf{E}})\left(\frac{f_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R(A)}B_{\epsilon\epsilon-\omega}^{R(A)}+g_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R(A)}C_{\epsilon\epsilon-\omega}^{R(A)}}{\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R(A)}+\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\omega}^{R(A)}}+\frac{f_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R(A)}+f_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R(A)}}{\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R(A)}+\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R(A)}}\right).

For the remaining trace in the definition (
56
) it obtains:

\displaystyle\textrm{Tr}\left\{(-i\hat{\tau}_{2})\hat{\cal R}_{\mathbf{E}}^{R(A)}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon,\mathbf{k}\omega)\right\}_{\textrm{b}}

\displaystyle=\textrm{Tr}\left\{(-i\hat{\tau}_{2})\hat{g}_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}\hat{g}_{1}^{R(A)}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega/2;\mathbf{k}\omega)\hat{g}_{1}^{R(A)}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\omega/2;\mathbf{k}\omega)\right\}

(120)

\displaystyle=2\left(\frac{ev_{F}}{\omega}\right)^{2}{(\mathbf{n}{\mathbf{E}})^{2}}\left\{\frac{f_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R(A)}B_{\epsilon\epsilon-\omega}^{R(A)}+g_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R(A)}C_{\epsilon\epsilon-\omega}^{R(A)}-f_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R(A)}-f_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\omega}^{R(A)}}{\left(\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R(A)}+\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\omega}^{R(A)}\right)\left(\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R(A)}+\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R(A)}\right)}\right\}.

Here we functions
B_{\epsilon\epsilon^{\prime}}^{R(A)}
and
C_{\epsilon\epsilon^{\prime}}^{R(A)}
have been defined in (
63
). Combining (
119
) and (
120
) I have

\displaystyle\textrm{Tr}\left\{(-i\hat{\tau}_{2})\hat{\cal R}_{\mathbf{E}}^{R(A)}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon,\mathbf{k}\omega)\right\}=2\left(\frac{ev_{F}}{i\omega}\right)^{2}{(\mathbf{n}{\mathbf{E}})^{2}}\left\{\frac{f_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R(A)}B_{\epsilon\epsilon-\omega}^{R(A)}+g_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R(A)}C_{\epsilon\epsilon-\omega}^{R(A)}}{\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R(A)}+\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\omega}^{R(A)}}+\frac{f_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R(A)}+f_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R(A)}}{\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R(A)}+\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R(A)}}\right\}

(121)

\displaystyle+2\left(\frac{ev_{F}}{i\omega}\right)^{2}{\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\omega}^{R(A)}(\mathbf{n}{\mathbf{E}})^{2}}\left\{\frac{f_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R(A)}B_{\epsilon\epsilon-\omega}^{R(A)}+g_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R(A)}C_{\epsilon\epsilon-\omega}^{R(A)}-f_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R(A)}-f_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\omega}^{R(A)}}{\left(\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R(A)}+\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\omega}^{R(A)}\right)\left(\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R(A)}+\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R(A)}\right)}\right\}.

Given the expressions above, we define following retarded and advanced functions

\displaystyle{\cal F}_{\textrm{reg.}}^{R(A)}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{ev_{F}}{\omega}\right)^{2}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}t_{\epsilon\mp\omega}d\epsilon\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\phi_{\mathbf{n}}}{2\pi}\frac{{\cal Y}(\mathbf{n})(\mathbf{n}{\mathbf{E}})^{2}}{\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\omega}^{R(A)}+\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R(A)}}\left\{\frac{f_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R(A)}B_{\epsilon\epsilon-\omega}^{R(A)}+g_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R(A)}C_{\epsilon\epsilon-\omega}^{R(A)}}{\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R(A)}+\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\omega}^{R(A)}}+\frac{f_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R(A)}+f_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R(A)}}{\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R(A)}+\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R(A)}}\right\}

(122)

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{ev_{F}}{\omega}\right)^{2}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}t_{\epsilon\mp\omega}d\epsilon\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\phi_{\mathbf{n}}}{2\pi}\frac{{\cal Y}(\mathbf{n})(\mathbf{n}{\mathbf{E}})^{2}\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\omega}^{R(A)}}{\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\omega}^{R(A)}+\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R(A)}}\left\{\frac{f_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R(A)}B_{\epsilon\epsilon-\omega}^{R(A)}+g_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R(A)}C_{\epsilon\epsilon-\omega}^{R(A)}-f_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R(A)}-f_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\omega}^{R(A)}}{\left(\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R(A)}+\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\omega}^{R(A)}\right)\left(\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R(A)}+\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R(A)}\right)}\right\}.

Then, function
F_{\textrm{reg.}}(\omega)
in (
118
) on account of my definition (
67
) is simply given by

{F_{\textrm{reg.}}(\omega)={\cal F}_{\textrm{reg.}}^{R}(\omega)-{\cal F}_{\textrm{reg.}}^{A}(\omega).}

(123)

We continue with the calculation of the function
F_{\textrm{anom.}}(\omega)
which accounts for the electric field contribution from the Keldysh part. This function is determined by the field dependent part of
\delta\hat{g}_{2}^{K}
:

\displaystyle{}_{\delta\Delta=0}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{ev_{F}}{i\omega}\right)\frac{(\mathbf{n}{\mathbf{E}})}{\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R}+\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\omega}^{A}}\hat{g}_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R}\left[\hat{g}_{1}^{R}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega/2;\mathbf{k}\omega)\hat{\tau}_{3}(t_{\epsilon}-t_{\epsilon-\omega})-\hat{\tau}_{3}\hat{g}_{1}^{A}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\omega/2;\mathbf{k}\omega)(t_{\epsilon}-t_{\epsilon+\omega})\right]

(124)

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{ev_{F}}{i\omega}\right)\frac{(\mathbf{n}{\mathbf{E}})}{\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R}+\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\omega}^{A}}\hat{g}_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R}\left[\delta\hat{g}_{1}^{K}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega/2;\mathbf{k}\omega)\hat{\tau}_{3}-\hat{\tau}_{3}\delta\hat{g}_{1}^{K}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\omega/2;\mathbf{k}\omega)\right].

After we insert (
16
) into (
116
) we see that (
124
) will generate several contributions which we will consider in what follows.
We will need the following expressions:

\displaystyle\textrm{Tr}\left[(-i\hat{\tau}_{2})\hat{g}_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R}\hat{g}_{1}^{R}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega/2;\mathbf{k}\omega)\hat{\tau}_{3}\right]=2\left(\frac{ev_{F}}{i\omega}\right)\left(\frac{f_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R}+f_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R}}{\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R}+\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R}}\right)(\mathbf{n}{\mathbf{E}}),

(125)

\displaystyle\textrm{Tr}\left[(-i\hat{\tau}_{2})\hat{g}_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R}\hat{\tau}_{3}\hat{g}_{1}^{A}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\omega/2;\mathbf{k}\omega)\right]=-2\left(\frac{ev_{F}}{i\omega}\right)\left(\frac{f_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R}B_{\epsilon\epsilon-\omega}^{A}+g_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R}C_{\epsilon\epsilon-\omega}^{A}}{\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{A}+\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\omega}^{A}}\right)(\mathbf{n}{\mathbf{E}}).

Given the expression

\delta\hat{g}_{1}^{K}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon;\mathbf{k}\omega)=\left(\frac{ev_{F}}{i\omega}\right)\frac{\left(\hat{\tau}_{3}-\hat{g}_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\frac{\omega}{2}}^{R}\hat{\tau}_{3}\hat{g}_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\omega/2}^{A}\right)(\mathbf{n}{\mathbf{E}})}{\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\frac{\omega}{2}}^{R}+\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\frac{\omega}{2}}^{A}}\left(t_{\epsilon+\frac{\omega}{2}}-t_{\epsilon-\frac{\omega}{2}}\right)

(126)

and we also have

\displaystyle\textrm{Tr}\left[(-i\hat{\tau}_{2})\hat{g}_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R}\delta\hat{g}_{1}^{K}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega/2;\mathbf{k}\omega)\hat{\tau}_{3}-(-i\hat{\tau}_{2})\hat{g}_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R}\hat{\tau}_{3}\delta\hat{g}_{1}^{K}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\omega/2;\mathbf{k}\omega)\right]

(127)

\displaystyle=2\left(\frac{ev_{F}}{i\omega}\right)(\mathbf{n}{\mathbf{E}})\left[\frac{f_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R}\tilde{B}_{\epsilon\epsilon-\omega}^{K}+g_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R}\tilde{C}_{\epsilon\epsilon-\omega}^{K}}{\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R}+\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\omega}^{A}}(t_{\epsilon}-t_{\epsilon-\omega})+\frac{f_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R}+f_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{A}}{\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R}+\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{A}}(t_{\epsilon+\omega}-t_{\epsilon})\right].

Here we introduced functions

\tilde{B}_{\epsilon\epsilon^{\prime}}^{K}=g_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R}g_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon^{\prime}}^{A}+f_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R}f_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon^{\prime}}^{A}-1,\quad\tilde{C}_{\epsilon\epsilon^{\prime}}^{K}=g_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R}f_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon^{\prime}}^{A}+f_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R}g_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon^{\prime}}^{A}.

(128)

Collecting all these contributions together we have

\displaystyle F_{\textrm{anom.}}(\omega)=-\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\epsilon\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\phi_{\mathbf{n}}}{2\pi}{\cal Y}(\mathbf{n})\textrm{Tr}\left[(-i\hat{\tau}_{2})\delta\hat{g}_{2}^{K}(\mathbf{n}\epsilon;\mathbf{k}=0,\omega)\right]=\left(\frac{ev_{F}}{\omega}\right)^{2}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\epsilon\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\phi_{\mathbf{n}}}{2\pi}\frac{{\cal Y}(\mathbf{n})(\mathbf{n}{\mathbf{E}})^{2}}{\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R}+\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\omega}^{A}}

(129)

\displaystyle\times\left\{\frac{(f_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R}+f_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R})(t_{\epsilon}-t_{\epsilon-\omega})}{\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R}+\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R}}+\frac{(f_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R}B_{\epsilon\epsilon-\omega}^{A}+g_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R}C_{\epsilon\epsilon-\omega}^{A})(t_{\epsilon}-t_{\epsilon+\omega})}{\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{A}+\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\omega}^{A}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{(f_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R}+f_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{A})(t_{\epsilon+\omega}-t_{\epsilon})}{\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R}+\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{A}}+\frac{(f_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R}\tilde{B}_{\epsilon\epsilon-\omega}^{K}+g_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon+\omega}^{R}\tilde{C}_{\epsilon\epsilon-\omega}^{K})(t_{\epsilon}-t_{\epsilon-\omega})}{\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon}^{R}+\eta_{\mathbf{n}\epsilon-\omega}^{A}}\right\}.

Thus, we write

{\chi_{\textrm{SH}}^{-1}(\omega)\delta\Delta_{\omega}^{L}=F_{\textrm{reg.}}(\omega)+F_{\textrm{anom.}}(\omega).}

(130)

Assuming that
T\sim T_{c}
and expanding the right hand side of this equation up to the linear order in

yields equation (
