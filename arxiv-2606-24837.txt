From L ∞ L_{\infty} algebroids to L ∞ L_{\infty} spaces: Part I
Alberto S. Cattaneo, Shuhan Jiang

Abstract
. The notion of L ∞ L_{\infty} spaces over dg manifolds is developed. An equivalence between the category of transitive L ∞ L_{\infty} algebroids and that of L ∞ L_{\infty} spaces is established, and this equivalence detects weak equivalences. Moreover, a faithful functor from L ∞ L_{\infty} algebroids to L ∞ L_{\infty} spaces is constructed, which also detects weak equivalences.



1.
Introduction

L_{\infty}
spaces were introduced by Costello as an approach to derived stacks motivated by problems in quantum field theory
[
costello2011geometric
]
. In combination with the Batalin–Vilkovisky formalism
[
Batalin1977
,
Batalin1981
]
,
L_{\infty}
spaces provide a convenient framework for describing quantization in the formal neighborhood of a family of classical solutions parameterized by a smooth manifold
[
costello2011renormalization
]
. Applications to non-linear sigma models recover classical invariants of the target manifold, such as the
\hat{A}
-genus
[
grady2014one
]
and the Witten genus
[
costello2010geometric
,
costello2011geometric
]
. In this case, the relevant family of classical solutions consists of constant maps, parameterized by the target manifold.

In many quantum field theory applications, however, the family of classical solutions of interest is not parameterized by a smooth manifold, but rather by a derived smooth manifold. Such situations are not directly covered by Costello’s original formulation: the functor of points of an
L_{\infty}
space over a smooth manifold can encode derived structure only in the nilpotent directions
[
grady2015spaces
]
. The present paper addresses this limitation by adapting Costello’s notion of
L_{\infty}
spaces to the setting of derived manifolds, or more precisely, to that of dg manifolds
[
Behrend2020thx
,
carchedi2023derivedmanifoldsdifferentialgraded
]
.

L_{\infty}
algebroids serve as a homotopical generalization of dg Lie algebroids in which the differential
l_{1}
, the binary Lie bracket
l_{2}
, and the unary anchor map
\rho_{1}
are extended into hierarchies of higher multilinear operations
\{l_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
and
\{\rho_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
satisfying higher coherent identities, just as
L_{\infty}
algebras generalize dg Lie algebras
[
stasheff1993introduction
,
lada1995strongly
]
. They appear in geometry as models of singular foliations and higher Dirac structures
[
severa2001some
,
zambon2012algebras
,
laurent2020universal
]
, in string theory as geometric formulations of higher gauge field content
[
sati2012twisted
]
, and in AKSZ theories as target spaces
[
alexandrov1997geometry
]
.
L_{\infty}
algebroids also generalize Lie
n
-algebroids
[
bonavolontaponcin2013
,
shengzhu2017
]
. The latter form a special class of
L_{\infty}
algebroids whose underlying graded vector bundles are concentrated in degrees
1-n,\dots,0
.
More generally,
L_{\infty}
algebroids can be defined over a dg manifold
\mathcal{M}
by allowing a zeroth anchor map
\rho_{0}
, which coincides with the cohomological vector field on
\mathcal{M}

[
bruce2011algebroids
,
bandiera2020shifted
]
.

There exists a close parallel between
L_{\infty}
spaces and Lie-theoretic structures in higher differential geometry, as revealed by a result of Grady and Gwilliam
[
grady2020lie
]
. They show that to any dg Lie algebroid one can associate an
L_{\infty}
space in a functorial manner compatible with weak equivalences. It is therefore natural to expect that a theory of
L_{\infty}
spaces over dg manifolds should develop in correspondence with the existing notion of
L_{\infty}
algebroids over dg manifolds. The main results of our paper confirm this expectation.

1.1.
Main results

To study
L_{\infty}
algebroids and
L_{\infty}
spaces over dg manifolds in a unified way, we introduce the intermediate notion of
quasi-dg manifolds
from a commutative algebraic perspective. Roughly speaking, a quasi-dg manifold is a ringed space
\mathcal{C}=(C,\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}})
whose structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}
is a sheaf of commutative differential graded algebras, such that, modulo a proper dg ideal
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{C}}\subset\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}
,
\mathcal{C}
reduces to an ordinary dg manifold. Using Chevalley–Eilenberg type functors, we establish the following equivalences between categories:

(1)

\mathbf{L_{\infty}Algd}\cong\mathbf{QdgM}^{\mathrm{bp}}
between the category of
L_{\infty}
algebroids over dg manifolds and that of quasi-dg manifolds with base-preserving morphisms;

(2)

\mathbf{L_{\infty}Algd}_{\mathrm{fib}}\cong\mathbf{QdgM}_{\mathrm{fib}}^{\mathrm{bp}}
between the category of transitive
L_{\infty}
algebroids over dg manifolds and that of fibrant quasi-dg manifolds with base-preserving morphisms;

(3)

\mathbf{L_{\infty}Sp}\cong\mathbf{QdgM}_{\mathrm{fib}}^{\mathrm{bp}}
between the category of
L_{\infty}
spaces over dg manifolds and that of fibrant quasi-dg manifolds with base-preserving morphisms.

Moreover, we prove that all these equivalences detect weak equivalences. Combining the last two, we obtain our first main result:

Theorem 1.1

(Corollary
4.14
)
.

There is an equivalence between the category of transitive
L_{\infty}
algebroids over dg manifolds and that of
L_{\infty}
spaces over dg manifolds, which detects weak equivalences.

Another advantage of working with quasi-dg manifolds is that they naturally admit the language of infinite jets and
D
-modules. In the appendices, we provide a thorough account of these notions in the setting of graded manifolds. With these tools in hand, we introduce the notion of the
jet space
of a quasi-dg manifold, which gives rise to a functor

\mathbf{J}^{\infty}\colon\mathbf{QdgM}\longrightarrow\mathbf{QdgM}_{\mathrm{fib}},

called the
jet space functor
. The infinite jet prolongation
j^{\infty}
defines a natural transformation from
\mathbf{J}^{\infty}
to the identity functor. Since
j^{\infty}
is a weak equivalence of quasi-dg manifolds,
\mathbf{J}^{\infty}
detects weak equivalences. Together with the preceding categorical equivalences, this yields our second main result:

Theorem 1.2

(Corollary
5.22
)
.

One can construct a faithful functor

\mathbf{Fib}\colon\mathbf{L_{\infty}Algd}\longrightarrow\mathbf{L_{\infty}Sp}\cong\mathbf{L_{\infty}Algd}_{\mathrm{fib}},

which detects weak equivalences.

We refer to
\mathbf{Fib}
as a
fibrant replacement functor
. The terminology is due to the fact that
L_{\infty}
spaces over a fixed dg manifold form a category of fibrant objects.
We prove this result in a companion paper
[
jiangCFO
]
.

1.2.
Future work and applications

As the title suggests, the present paper is the first in a series. In subsequent work, we will construct a fibrant replacement functor for representations of
L_{\infty}
algebroids and apply it to the study of their characteristic classes and shifted symplectic structures. The advantage of passing to
L_{\infty}
spaces is that these structures become more transparent and computationally tractable. We will also study the Maurer–Cartan functor of
L_{\infty}
spaces over dg manifolds.

One of the main motivations for our work comes from applications to the problem of globalizing the partition function and observables of a quantum field theory over its moduli space of classical solutions within the Batalin–Vilkovisky formalism. This problem has been studied, in a language different from that of
L_{\infty}
spaces but closely related to it, in the context of AKSZ sigma models, where one considers the moduli space of constant maps
[
cattaneofelder2001
,
bonechi2012poisson
,
grady2017batalin
,
cattaneo2019globalization
]
, as well as in two-dimensional Yang–Mills theory and Chern–Simons theory, where one considers the smooth locus of the moduli space of flat connections
[
irasomnev2019
,
mnevwernli2025
]
. The framework developed in the present paper, together with its sequels, is intended to provide an approach to the globalization problem in the presence of both derived and stacky singularities.

The two main classes of field theories we have in mind for applications are cohomological field theories, as discussed in
[
jiangjost2026cohft
]
in a formulation suited to applications of
L_{\infty}
spaces over dg manifolds, and AKSZ theories with cotangent derived targets. Both classes admit one-loop exact quantization. The latter is technically more tractable and is expected to produce interesting invariants of dg manifolds
[
jiangAKSZ
]
.

2.
Dg vector bundles over dg manifolds

In this section, we review dg manifolds and dg vector bundles over them. In particular, we prove a Whitehead-type theorem for dg vector bundles.

2.1.
Differential graded manifolds

Let
V=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}V_{i}
be a graded vector space of finite total rank. Suppose that
V
is concentrated in non-negative degrees, i.e.,
V_{i}=0
for all
i<0
. We denote by
(V_{0},\mathcal{O}_{V})
the ringed space whose structure sheaf assigns each open
U\subset V_{0}
the graded commutative algebra

\mathcal{O}_{V}(U)=C^{\infty}(U)\otimes_{\mathrm{Sym}(V_{0}^{\vee})}\mathrm{Sym}(V^{\vee}),

where
V^{\vee}
denotes the dual graded vector space of
V
, and
\mathrm{Sym}(V^{\vee})
denotes the free graded commutative algebra generated by
V^{\vee}
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
(non-positively) graded manifold
is a ringed space
\mathcal{M}=(M,\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}})
, where
M
is a smooth manifold and
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}
is a sheaf of graded commutative algebras, which is locally isomorphic to
(V_{0},\mathcal{O}_{V})
for some graded vector space
V
as above.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a graded manifold. For each open
U\subset M
, define

\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{M}}(U)\coloneqq\operatorname{Der}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}(U)),

where
\operatorname{Der}(\cdot)
denotes the graded Lie algebra of derivations of a graded commutative algebra. The assignment
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{M}}
defines a sheaf, called the
tangent sheaf
of
\mathcal{M}
. Global sections of
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{M}}
are called
vector fields
on
\mathcal{M}
.

Definition 2.2
.

A
dg manifold
is a graded manifold
\mathcal{M}
together with a degree
1
vector field
Q_{\mathcal{M}}
over
\mathcal{M}
satisfying
[Q_{\mathcal{M}},Q_{\mathcal{M}}]=0
.

The vector field
Q_{\mathcal{M}}
defines a differential on
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}
, making it into a sheaf of commutative differential graded algebras (cdgas).

Definition 2.3
.

A
morphism
of dg manifolds
\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{N}
is a morphism of ringed spaces

(f,f^{\sharp})\colon(M,\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}})\longrightarrow(N,\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}}),

where
f\colon M\to N
is smooth,
f^{\sharp}\colon\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}}\to f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}
is a morphism of sheaves of cdgas. Here
f_{*}
denotes the direct image functor associated to
f
.

By a slight abuse of notation, we denote a dg manifold morphism
(f,f^{\sharp})
simply by
f
.

Remark 2.4
.

By Batchelor’s theorem, dg manifolds are essentially curved
L_{\infty}
bundles in disguise. They form a category of fibrant objects
[
Behrend2020thx
]
: a morphism
f\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{N}
is called

•

a
weak equivalence
if

f^{\sharp}_{N}\colon\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}}(N)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}(M)

is a quasi-isomorphism;

•

a
fibration
if it is a submersion of graded manifolds.

Formally inverting weak equivalences in this category produces an
\infty
-category equivalent to the
\infty
-category of derived manifolds
[
carchedi2023derivedmanifoldsdifferentialgraded
]
.

2.2.
Differential graded vector bundles

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a graded manifold.

Definition 2.5
.

A
graded vector bundle
over
\mathcal{M}
is a locally free graded (left)
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}
-module of finite total rank.

The following result for graded vector bundles over
\mathcal{M}
is not trivial, despite first appearances, since
\mathcal{M}
is not a locally ringed space.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{1}
and
\mathcal{E}_{2}
be graded vector bundles over
\mathcal{M}
. Let
\rho\colon\mathcal{E}_{1}\to\mathcal{E}_{2}
be a surjective morphism of graded
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}
-modules. Then
\ker\rho
is a graded vector bundle over
\mathcal{M}
.

We begin by stating a super version of the Quillen–Suslin theorem needed for the proof, which we were unable to locate in the literature.

Lemma 2.7

(Super Quillen–Suslin theorem)
.

Every finitely generated projective module over the super polynomial ring
\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n},\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{m}]
is free, where
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
denote the even variables and
\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{m}
denote the odd variables.

Proof.

For convenience, we denote the exterior algebra
\mathbb{R}[\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{m}]
by
\Lambda_{m}
. The super polynomial ring can then be identified with the tensor product

R\coloneqq\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\Lambda_{m}.

Let
I\subset R
denote the nilpotent ideal
\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\otimes\Lambda_{m}^{>0}
, where
\Lambda_{m}^{>0}
is the maximal ideal of
\Lambda_{m}
generated by the odd variables. Then

R/I\cong\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}].

Let
M
be a finitely generated projective
R
-module. Then
M/IM\cong M\otimes_{R}R/I
is a finitely generated projective
R/I
-module. The classical Quillen–Suslin theorem then implies that
M/IM
is a free
R/I
-module
[
quillen1976projective
,
suslin1976projective
]
.

Since
I
is nilpotent,
I\subset\operatorname{rad}(R)
. A lemma of Bass (see Lemma 2.4 in
[
bass1961projective
]
) states that for projective
R
-modules
P
and
P^{\prime}
,

P\cong P^{\prime}\quad\text{if and only if}\quad P/IP\cong P^{\prime}/IP^{\prime}.

In particular, a projective
R
-module
P
is free if and only if
P/IP
is a free
R/I
-module. Applying this criterion to
M
completes the proof.
∎

To apply the super Quillen–Suslin theorem, we use the geometric language of graded vector bundles. By Batchelor’s theorem, there exists a positively graded vector bundle
E
over
M
such that

\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}\cong\mathrm{Sym}(E^{\vee}),

where
\mathrm{Sym}(E^{\vee})
is understood as the corresponding locally free sheaf on
M
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{1}
and
\mathcal{E}_{2}
be graded vector bundles over
\mathcal{M}
. Then there exist graded vector bundles
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
over
M
such that

(2.1)

\mathcal{E}_{i}\cong\mathrm{Sym}(E^{\vee})\otimes E_{i},\quad i=1,2.

Moreover,

\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{E}_{1},\mathcal{E}_{2})\cong\mathrm{Hom}\bigl(\mathrm{Sym}(E^{\vee})\otimes E_{1},\,\mathrm{Sym}(E^{\vee})\otimes E_{2}\bigr),

where the first
\mathrm{Hom}
denotes the set of morphisms of graded
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}
-modules over
\mathcal{M}
, and the second
\mathrm{Hom}
denotes the set of morphisms of graded
\mathrm{Sym}(E^{\vee})
-modules over
M
.

Proof.

The graded vector bundles
E_{i}
are obtained by restricting
\mathcal{E}_{i}
to
M
. The isomorphisms (
2.1
) then follow from homotopy invariance of graded vector bundles over split graded manifolds; see Proposition A.6 in
[
seol2025atiyah
]
.

We can then identify a graded
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}
-module morphism
\rho\colon\mathcal{E}_{1}\to\mathcal{E}_{2}
with a family of
C^{\infty}
-linear sheaf morphisms

\rho^{n}\colon E_{1}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(E^{\vee})\otimes E_{2},\qquad n\geq 0,

where all bundles are understood as the corresponding locally free sheaves on
M
. Since each
E_{i}
has finite total rank, we can further identify these sheaf morphisms with morphisms of graded vector bundles on
M

\underline{\rho}^{n}\colon E_{1}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(E^{\vee})\otimes E_{2},\qquad n\geq 0.

Consequently,
\rho
can be identified with a morphism of graded vector bundles on
M

\underline{\rho}\colon\mathrm{Sym}(E^{\vee})\otimes E_{1}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Sym}(E^{\vee})\otimes E_{2},

which is
\mathrm{Sym}(E^{\vee})
-linear.
∎

We can now prove Proposition
2.6
.

Proof of Proposition
2.6
.

It follows from Lemma
2.8
that the short exact sequence of graded
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}
-modules

0\longrightarrow\ker\rho\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}_{1}\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}_{2}\longrightarrow 0.

can be identified with the short exact sequence of graded
\mathrm{Sym}(E^{\vee})
-modules

0\longrightarrow\ker\underline{\rho}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Sym}(E^{\vee})\otimes E_{1}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Sym}(E^{\vee})\otimes E_{2}\longrightarrow 0.

Thus,
\ker\underline{\rho}
is a graded vector bundle on
M
equipped with an induced graded
\mathrm{Sym}(E^{\vee})
-module structure from
\mathrm{Sym}(E^{\vee})\otimes E_{1}
. For each
x\in M
, the fiber
(\ker\underline{\rho})_{x}
is a direct summand of the free graded
\mathrm{Sym}(E^{\vee}_{x})
-module
\mathrm{Sym}(E^{\vee}_{x})\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}(E_{1})_{x}
of finite total rank, and hence finitely generated and projective over the super polynomial ring
\mathrm{Sym}(E^{\vee}_{x})
. By Lemma
2.7
,
(\ker\underline{\rho})_{x}
is a free graded
\mathrm{Sym}(E^{\vee}_{x})
-module of finite total rank.

Let
\{e_{x,i}\}_{i=1}^{p}
be a basis of
(\ker\underline{\rho})_{x}
over
\mathrm{Sym}(E_{x}^{\vee})
, where
p
denotes the total rank of
(\ker\underline{\rho})_{x}
. Since
\ker\underline{\rho}
is a graded vector bundle over
M
, we may extend
\{e_{x,i}\}_{i=1}^{p}
to sections
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{p}
of
\ker\underline{\rho}|_{U}
for some open neighborhood
U\ni x
satisfying

e_{i}(x)=e_{x,i},\qquad i=1,\dots p.

For convenience, set

R\coloneqq\mathrm{Sym}(E^{\vee}|_{U}),\qquad P\coloneqq\ker\underline{\rho}|_{U}.

Then
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{p}
induces a graded
R
-module morphism

\phi\colon\bigoplus_{i=1}^{p}R[-d_{i}]\longrightarrow P,

where
d_{i}=\deg(e_{i})
,
i=1,\dots,p
. After shrinking
U
further if necessary, choose a local frame
\{e_{i}\}_{i=p+1}^{p+q}
of the graded
R
-module
R\otimes E_{2}|_{U}
, where
q
is the total rank of
E_{2}
. This induces a graded
R
-module isomorphism

P\oplus\bigoplus_{i=p+1}^{p+q}R[-d_{i}]\cong P\oplus\left(R\otimes E_{2}|_{U}\right)\cong R\otimes E_{1}|_{U}\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{p+q}R[-d_{i}],

where
d_{i}=\deg(e_{i})
,
i=p+1,\dots,p+q
.

Now define

\widetilde{\phi}\colon\bigoplus_{i=1}^{p+q}R[-d_{i}]\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{p}R[-d_{i}]\oplus\bigoplus_{i=p+1}^{p+q}R[-d_{i}]\xrightarrow{(\phi,\mathrm{id})}P\oplus\bigoplus_{i=p+1}^{p+q}R[-d_{i}]\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{p+q}R[-d_{i}].

By construction, the fiber map
\widetilde{\phi}_{x}
is invertible. Hence, after shrinking
U
once more if necessary, the determinant
\det(\widetilde{\phi}_{y})\in R_{y}
is invertible for all
y\in U
, and therefore
\widetilde{\phi}
is an isomorphism over
U
. It follows that
\phi
is also an isomorphism over
U
. ∎

Lemma 2.9
.

The tensor product
\mathcal{E}_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}\mathcal{E}_{2}
of two graded vector bundles
\mathcal{E}_{1}
and
\mathcal{E}_{2}
is again a graded vector bundle. Moreover,

\bigl(\mathcal{E}_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}\mathcal{E}_{2}\bigr)(M)\cong\mathcal{E}_{1}(M)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}(M)}\mathcal{E}_{2}(M).

Proof.

The first statement is obvious. For the second, recall that on smooth manifolds the global sections functor on vector bundles commutes with tensor products; see Theorem 12.39 of
[
nestruev2020
]
. The claim then follows from the identifications in (
2.1
).
∎

Let
f\colon\mathcal{M}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{2}
be a graded manifold morphism. Let
\mathcal{E}_{2}
be a graded vector bundle over
\mathcal{M}_{2}
. We call the graded
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}_{1}}
-module

f^{*}\mathcal{E}_{2}\coloneqq\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}_{1}}\otimes_{f^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}_{2}}}f^{-1}\mathcal{E}_{2}

the
pullback
of
\mathcal{E}_{2}
along
f
, where
f^{-1}
denotes the inverse image functor associated to
f
.

Lemma 2.10
.

f^{*}\mathcal{E}_{2}
is a graded vector bundle over
\mathcal{M}_{1}
. Moreover,

(2.2)

f^{*}\mathcal{E}_{2}(M_{1})\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}_{1}}(M_{1})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}_{2}}(M_{2})}\mathcal{E}_{2}(M_{2}).

Proof.

The first statement is obvious. The second holds in the setting of smooth manifolds; see Theorem 12.43 of
[
nestruev2020
]
. The result then follows from the identifications in (
2.1
).
∎

Remark 2.11
.

Using (
2.2
), one obtains a canonical identification

(2.3)

f^{*}\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{M}_{2}}(M_{1})\cong\mathrm{Der}\left(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}_{2}}(M_{2}),\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}_{1}}(M_{1})\right),

where
\mathrm{Der}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}_{2}}(M_{2}),\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}_{1}}(M_{1}))
denotes the space of vector fields along
f
, i.e.,
\mathbb{R}
-linear maps

X\colon\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}_{2}}(M_{2})\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}_{1}}(M_{1})

satisfying the Leibniz rule

X(gh)=X(g)\,f^{\sharp}_{M_{2}}(h)+(-1)^{|X||g|}f^{\sharp}_{M_{2}}(g)\,X(h),

for all
g,h\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}_{2}}(M_{2})
. See
[
seol2025atiyah
]
.

From now on, let
\mathcal{M}
be a dg manifold.

Definition 2.12
.

A
dg vector bundle
over
\mathcal{M}
is a graded vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
equipped with a differential
d_{\mathcal{E}}
making
\mathcal{E}
into a dg module over
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}
.

We denote a dg vector bundle by
\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{M}
, or simply by
\mathcal{E}
when its base is clear from context.

Example 2.13
.

The tangent sheaf
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{M}}
is naturally a dg vector bundle, with differential given by the adjoint action of the cohomological vector field:

d_{\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{M}}}\coloneqq[Q_{\mathcal{M}},\cdot].

Given a dg manifold morphism
f\colon\mathcal{M}_{1}\to\mathcal{M}_{2}
and a dg vector bundle
\mathcal{E}_{2}
over
\mathcal{M}_{2}
, the pullback graded vector bundle
f^{*}\mathcal{E}_{2}
carries a canonical dg vector bundle structure. Its differential is given by

d_{f^{*}\mathcal{E}_{2}}(g\otimes e)=f^{*}d_{\mathcal{E}_{2}}(g\otimes e)=Q_{\mathcal{M}_{1}}(g)\otimes e+(-1)^{|g|}g\otimes f^{-1}d_{\mathcal{E}_{2}}(e),

for all
g\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}_{1}}
and
e\in f^{-1}\mathcal{E}_{2}
.

Definition 2.14
.

A
morphism
between dg vector bundles
\mathcal{E}_{1}/\mathcal{M}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}_{2}/\mathcal{M}_{2}
consists of a pair
\phi=(f,\phi^{\sharp})
, where
f\colon\mathcal{M}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{2}
is a dg manifold morphism and

\phi^{\sharp}\colon\mathcal{E}_{1}\longrightarrow f^{*}\mathcal{E}_{2}

is a morphism of dg
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}_{1}}
-modules. Such
\phi
is called
base-fixing
if
f=\mathrm{id}
.

Example 2.15
.

Given any dg manifold morphism
f\colon\mathcal{M}_{1}\to\mathcal{M}_{2}
, there exists a canonical dg vector bundle morphism
Tf\colon\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{M}_{1}}\to\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{M}_{2}}
, with
(Tf)^{\sharp}\colon\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{M}_{1}}\rightarrow f^{*}\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{M}_{2}}
defined by

(Tf)^{\sharp}(X)(\cdot)=X(f^{\sharp}(\cdot)),

where we use the identification (
2.3
).

Example 2.16
.

We define the following dg vector bundles over
\mathcal{M}
associated to a dg vector bundle
\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{M}
:

•

the
suspension
:

\mathcal{E}[1]\coloneqq\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{E}_{i+1},

equipped with the differential

d_{\mathcal{E}[1]}\coloneqq d_{\mathcal{E}};

•

the
dual
:

\mathcal{E}^{\vee}\coloneqq\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}),

where
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}
denotes the internal Hom in the category of graded (left)
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}
-modules, equipped with the differential

d_{\mathcal{E}^{\vee}}(\alpha)(X)=Q_{\mathcal{M}}\bigl(\alpha(X)\bigr)-(-1)^{|\alpha|}\alpha\bigl(d_{\mathcal{E}}(X)\bigr);

•

the
n
-th tensor power
:

\mathcal{E}^{\otimes n}\coloneqq\underbrace{\mathcal{E}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}\cdots\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}\mathcal{E}}_{n\text{ times}},

equipped with the differential

\displaystyle d_{\mathcal{E}^{\otimes n}}(X_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes X_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{|X_{1}|+\cdots+|X_{i-1}|}X_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes d_{\mathcal{E}}(X_{i})\otimes\cdots\otimes X_{n};

•

the
n
-th symmetric power
:

\mathrm{Sym}^{n}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}(\mathcal{E})\coloneqq\{X\in\mathcal{E}^{\otimes n}\mid\sigma(X)=X\ \text{for all }\sigma\in\Sigma_{n}\},

where
\Sigma_{n}
denotes the symmetric group on
n
letters, equipped with the differential

d_{\mathrm{Sym}^{n}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}(\mathcal{E})}\coloneqq d_{\mathcal{E}^{\otimes n}}\big|_{\mathrm{Sym}^{n}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}(\mathcal{E})}.

All of these constructions are functorial in the category of dg vector bundles over
\mathcal{M}
.

Definition 2.17
.

A dg vector bundle morphism
\phi\colon\mathcal{E}_{1}/\mathcal{M}_{1}\to\mathcal{E}_{2}/\mathcal{M}_{2}
is a
weak equivalence
if
f
is a weak equivalence of dg manifolds and

\phi^{\sharp}_{M_{1}}\colon\mathcal{E}_{1}(M_{1})\rightarrow f^{*}\mathcal{E}_{2}(M_{1})

is a quasi-isomorphism.

A dg vector bundle
\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{M}
is called
weakly contractible
if the canonical morphism from the zero dg vector bundle over
\mathcal{M}
to
\mathcal{E}
is a weak equivalence; equivalently, the dg
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}(M)
-module
\mathcal{E}(M)
is acyclic.

Example 2.18
.

Let
\phi\colon\mathcal{E}_{1}\to\mathcal{E}_{2}
be a base-fixing morphism of dg vector bundles. The
mapping cone
of
\phi
is the dg vector bundle

C(\phi)\coloneqq\left(\mathcal{E}_{1}[1]\oplus\mathcal{E}_{2},\quad\begin{pmatrix}-d_{\mathcal{E}_{1}[1]}&0\\
\phi^{\sharp}&d_{\mathcal{E}_{2}}\end{pmatrix}\right).

A standard computation shows that
\phi
is a weak equivalence if and only if
C(\phi)
is weakly contractible.

A dg vector bundle
\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{M}
is called
contractible
if there exists an
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}
-linear map
h\colon\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}
of degree
-1
satisfying

d_{\mathcal{E}}h+hd_{\mathcal{E}}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{E}}.

We refer to such
h
as a
(homotopy) contraction
.

Proposition 2.19
.

Every weakly contractible dg vector bundle is contractible.

To prove this proposition, we need the following useful lemma.

Lemma 2.20

(Lemma 2.3 in
[
seol2025atiyah
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{M}
be a dg vector bundle.
Then
\mathcal{E}(M)
is a homotopy flat dg
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}(M)
-module; that is, for any acyclic dg
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}(M)
-module
A
, the tensor product
A\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}(M)}\mathcal{E}(M)
is again acyclic.

Remark 2.21
.

Using the mapping cone argument, one sees that tensoring with
\mathcal{E}(M)
preserves quasi-isomorphisms of dg
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}(M)
-modules.

Sketch of proof.

The graded commutative algebra
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}(M)
is canonically a
C^{\infty}(M)
-algebra.
Recall that there exists a graded vector bundle
E=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}E_{i}
on
M
such that

\mathcal{E}(M)\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}(M)\otimes_{C^{\infty}(M)}\Gamma(E).

This allows us to define a complete descending filtration on
\mathcal{E}(M)
by

F^{p}\mathcal{E}(M)\coloneqq\bigoplus_{i\geq p}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}(M)\otimes_{C^{\infty}(M)}\Gamma(E_{i}).

Since
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}(M)
is non-positively graded, the differential
d_{\mathcal{E}}
preserves this filtration, and
\mathrm{Gr}^{p}d_{\mathcal{E}}=Q_{\mathcal{M}}\otimes_{C^{\infty}(M)}1
for all
p
.
It follows that
\mathrm{Gr}^{p}\big(A\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}(M)}\mathcal{E}(M)\big)\cong A\otimes_{C^{\infty}(M)}\Gamma(E_{p})
is acyclic for all
p
, and hence
A\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}(M)}\mathcal{E}(M)
is acyclic.
∎

Proof of Proposition
2.19
.

Let
\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{M}
be a dg vector bundle. The graded vector bundle

\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{E})\coloneqq\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E})

carries a canonical dg vector bundle structure, with differential

d_{\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{E})}(h)=[d_{\mathcal{E}},h],\qquad h\in\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{E}).

One can see that
\mathcal{E}
is contractible if and only if
\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{E}}\in\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{E})
is exact. Now assume that
\mathcal{E}
is weakly contractible. Note that

\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{E})\cong\mathcal{E}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}\mathcal{E}^{\vee}.

By Lemma
2.9
, there is an isomorphism

\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{E})(M)\cong\mathcal{E}(M)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}(M)}\mathcal{E}^{\vee}(M).

The latter complex is acyclic by Lemma
2.20
. It follows that
\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{E}}
must be exact.
∎

We conclude this section with three lemmas that will be used later.

Lemma 2.22
.

Let
f\colon\mathcal{M}_{1}\to\mathcal{M}_{2}
be a weak equivalence of dg manifolds. Let
\mathcal{E}_{i}/\mathcal{M}_{i}
,
i=1,2
, be dg vector bundles. Under the natural isomorphism

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}_{1}}}(f^{*}\mathcal{E}_{2},\mathcal{E}_{1})\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}_{2}}}(\mathcal{E}_{2},f_{*}\mathcal{E}_{1}),

a morphism
f^{*}\mathcal{E}_{2}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}_{1}
is a weak equivalence of dg vector bundles if and only if the induced map on global sections

\mathcal{E}_{2}(M_{2})\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}_{1}(M_{1})

is a quasi-isomorphism.

Proof.

Since
f^{\sharp}\colon\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}_{2}}(M_{2})\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}_{1}}(M_{1})
is a quasi-isomorphism, it follows from Lemmas
2.10
and
2.20
that the canonical map

\mathcal{E}_{2}(M_{2})\cong\mathcal{E}_{2}(M_{2})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}_{2}}(M_{2})}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}_{2}}(M_{2})\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}_{2}(M_{2})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}_{2}}(M_{2})}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}_{1}}(M_{1})\cong f^{*}\mathcal{E}_{2}(M_{1})

is a quasi-isomorphism. The claim then follows from the fact that
\mathcal{E}_{2}(M_{2})\rightarrow\mathcal{E}_{1}(M_{1})
is obtained as the composition

\mathcal{E}_{2}(M_{2})\longrightarrow f^{*}\mathcal{E}_{2}(M_{1})\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}_{1}(M_{1}),

and the 2-out-of-3 property of quasi-isomorphisms.
∎

Lemma 2.23
.

The operations listed in Example
2.16
preserve base-fixing weak equivalences.

Proof.

For the suspension, the claim is immediate. For the tensor power (and consequently for the symmetric power construction), the result follows directly from Lemma
2.20
.

More precisely, given a base-fixing weak equivalence
\phi\colon\mathcal{E}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}_{2}
, the induced morphism
\phi\otimes\phi\colon\mathcal{E}_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}\mathcal{E}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}_{2}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}\mathcal{E}_{2}
can be factored as

\mathcal{E}_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}\mathcal{E}_{1}\xlongrightarrow{\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{E}_{1}}\otimes\phi}\mathcal{E}_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}\mathcal{E}_{2}\xrightarrow{\phi\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{E}_{2}}}\mathcal{E}_{2}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}\mathcal{E}_{2}.

Since each factor is a weak equivalence, their composition is a weak equivalence. By induction, the induced morphism
\mathcal{E}_{1}^{\otimes n}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}_{2}^{\otimes n}
is a weak equivalence for all
n
. Consequently, the claim also holds for the symmetric power, since the symmetrization projector realizes the symmetric power as a direct summand of the tensor power, compatible with the induced morphisms.

We now turn to the dual. Since the dual commutes with the mapping cone (up to suspension), it suffices to show that it preserves weak contractibility. By Proposition
2.19
, every weakly contractible dg vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
admits a contraction
h\colon\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}
. One readily verifies that its dual map
h^{\vee}\colon\mathcal{E}^{\vee}\to\mathcal{E}^{\vee}
satisfies

d_{\mathcal{E}^{\vee}}h^{\vee}+h^{\vee}d_{\mathcal{E}^{\vee}}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{E}^{\vee}}.

Therefore,
\mathcal{E}^{\vee}
is contractible, hence weakly contractible.
∎

Lemma 2.24

(Section 3 in
[
seol2025atiyah
]
)
.

If
f\colon\mathcal{M}_{1}\to\mathcal{M}_{2}
is a weak equivalence of dg manifolds, then
Tf\colon\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{M}_{1}}\to\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{M}_{2}}
is a weak equivalence of dg vector bundles.

3.
L_{\infty}
algebroids over dg manifolds

In this section, we review the notion of
L_{\infty}
algebroids over dg manifolds from the Lie algebraic picture. We then introduce a commutative algebraic picture and prove its equivalence to the Lie algebraic one.

3.1.
Lie algebraic perspective

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a graded manifold and
\mathcal{L}
a graded vector bundle over
\mathcal{M}
. Set

\Omega^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{L}}\coloneqq\mathrm{Sym}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}\!\big(\mathcal{L}[1]^{\vee}\big).

This graded commutative algebra carries a canonical descending filtration

F^{p}\Omega_{\mathcal{L}}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{n\geq p}\Omega^{n}_{\mathcal{L}},\qquad p\in\mathbb{Z}.

Its completion with respect to this filtration is

\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathcal{L}}\coloneqq\widehat{\mathrm{Sym}}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}\!\big(\mathcal{L}[1]^{\vee}\big),

where
\widehat{\mathrm{Sym}}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}
denotes the completed symmetric power. When
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{M}}
, we write
\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{M}}}
simply as
\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathcal{M}}
. In this case,
\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathcal{M}}
is the sheaf of completed differential forms on
\mathcal{M}
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a dg manifold. An
L_{\infty}
algebroid over
\mathcal{M}
is a graded vector bundle
\mathcal{L}
over
\mathcal{M}
, together with a filtration preserving differential
D_{\mathcal{L}}
on
\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathcal{L}}
, whose associated graded operator satisfies

\mathrm{Gr}D_{\mathcal{L}}|_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}=Q_{\mathcal{M}}.

The filtered cdga
\mathrm{CE}(\mathcal{L})\coloneqq(\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathcal{L}},D_{\mathcal{L}})
is called the
Chevalley–Eilenberg algebra
of
\mathcal{L}
.

By a slight abuse of notation, we denote an
L_{\infty}
algebroid by
\mathcal{L}/\mathcal{M}
, or simply by
\mathcal{L}
when the base
\mathcal{M}
is clear from context.

The differential
D_{\mathcal{L}}
determines

•

a sequence of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}
-linear maps of degree
0

\rho_{n}\colon\mathrm{Sym}^{n}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}(\mathcal{L}[1])\longrightarrow\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{M}}[1],\qquad n\geq 0,

called the
multi-anchors
of
\mathcal{L}
, with
\rho_{0}=Q_{\mathcal{M}}
;

•

a sequence of
\mathbb{R}
-linear maps of degree
1

l_{n}\colon\mathrm{Sym}^{n}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}(\mathcal{L}[1])\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}[1],\qquad n\geq 1,

called the
multi-brackets
of
\mathcal{L}
.

More precisely, these maps are determined by the action of
D_{\mathcal{L}}
on the generators of
\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathcal{L}}
:

\displaystyle\Omega^{0}_{\mathcal{L}}\longrightarrow\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathcal{L}},\quad f\mapsto D_{\mathcal{L}}f=\sum_{n\geq 0}\rho_{n}^{\vee}(\mathrm{d}f),

\displaystyle\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}\longrightarrow\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathcal{L}},\quad\alpha\mapsto D_{\mathcal{L}}\alpha=\sum_{n\geq 1}l_{n}^{\vee}(\alpha),

where
\rho_{n}^{\vee}\colon\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{M}}\rightarrow\Omega^{n}_{\mathcal{L}}
is specified by

\displaystyle\rho_{n}^{\vee}(\alpha)(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})+(-1)^{|\alpha|}\alpha\left(\rho_{n}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})\right)=0,

and
l_{n}^{\vee}\colon\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}\rightarrow\Omega^{n}_{\mathcal{L}}
is specified by

\displaystyle l_{n}^{\vee}(\alpha)(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})+(-1)^{|\alpha|}\alpha\left(l_{n}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{|X_{i}|(|X_{1}|+\cdots+|X_{i-1}|)}d(\alpha(X_{i}))\left(\rho_{n-1}(X_{1},\dots,\widehat{X_{i}},\dots,X_{n})\right).

They are characterized by the following conditions:

•

The failure of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}
-linearity of the
n
-th multi-bracket
l_{n}
is measured by the
(n-1)
-th multi-anchor
\rho_{n-1}
:

l_{n}(X_{1},\dots,fX_{n})=\rho_{n-1}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n-1})(f)X_{n}+(-1)^{|f|(\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}|X_{i}|+1)}fl_{n}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n}).

For
n=1
, one has

l_{1}(fX)=Q_{\mathcal{M}}(f)X+(-1)^{|f|}fl_{1}(X).

•

The multi-brackets
\{l_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
satisfy the
strong homotopy Jacobi identities
:

\sum_{p+q=n}\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{n}}\frac{\epsilon(\sigma)}{p!q!}l_{q+1}(l_{p}(X_{\sigma(1)},\dots),\dots,X_{\sigma(n)})=0.

For
n=1
, one has

l_{1}\circ l_{1}=0.

Thus, equipped with
l_{1}
,
\mathcal{L}
becomes a dg vector bundle over
\mathcal{M}
.

•

The multi-anchors
\{\rho_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
intertwine the multi-brackets
l_{n}
on
\mathcal{L}[1]
with the Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]
on
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{M}}[1]
:

\displaystyle\sum_{p+q=n}\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{n}}\frac{\epsilon(\sigma)}{p!q!}\rho_{q+1}\big(l_{p}(X_{\sigma(1)},\dots),\dots,X_{\sigma(n)}\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{p+q=n}\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{n}}\frac{\epsilon(\sigma)}{p!q!}\big[\rho_{p}(X_{\sigma(1)},\dots),\rho_{q}(\dots,X_{\sigma(n)})\big],

where
\epsilon(\sigma)
is the Koszul sign of the permutation
\sigma
.
For
n=0
, this reduces to
[Q_{\mathcal{M}},Q_{\mathcal{M}}]=0
.
For
n=1
, one has

\rho_{1}(l_{1}(X))=[Q_{\mathcal{M}},\rho_{1}(X)].

In other words,
\rho_{1}\colon\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{M}}
is a dg vector bundle morphism.

In fact, the first condition follows from the fact that
D_{\mathcal{L}}
is a derivation, while the second and third follow from the vanishing of
D_{\mathcal{L}}^{2}
on
\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}
and
\Omega^{0}_{\mathcal{L}}
, respectively.

Example 3.2
.

A finite-dimensional
L_{\infty}
algebra can be seen as an
L_{\infty}
algebroid over a point.

Example 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a dg manifold. There are two canonical
L_{\infty}
algebroids over
\mathcal{M}
:

•

The zero vector bundle
0_{\mathcal{M}}
, equipped with zero multi-brackets and zero multi-anchors, defines an
L_{\infty}
algebroid, called the
zero algebroid
on
\mathcal{M}
. Its Chevalley–Eilenberg algebra is given by
(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}},Q_{\mathcal{M}})
.

•

Recall that the de Rham differential
\mathrm{d}_{dR}
is the unique differential on
\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathcal{M}}
extending

\Omega^{0}_{\mathcal{M}}\longrightarrow\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{M}},\qquad f\mapsto\mathrm{d}f,

where
\mathrm{d}f(X)=(-1)^{|X||f|}X(f)
for all vector fields
X
. The Lie derivative
L_{X}
is the unique derivation on
\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathcal{M}}
extending

\Omega^{0}_{\mathcal{M}}\longrightarrow\Omega^{0}_{\mathcal{M}},\qquad f\mapsto X(f).

Now consider the total differential

D_{\mathcal{M}}\coloneqq\mathrm{d}_{dR}+L_{Q_{\mathcal{M}}}

on
\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathcal{M}}
. Obviously,
D_{\mathcal{M}}
preserves the filtration on
\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathcal{M}}
. Moreover,

\mathrm{Gr}D_{\mathcal{M}}|_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}=Q_{\mathcal{M}}.

Thus,
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{M}}
is an
L_{\infty}
algebroid, called the
tangent algebroid
of
\mathcal{M}
. In terms of multi-anchors and multi-brackets, we have

\rho_{0}=Q_{\mathcal{M}},\quad\rho_{1}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{M}}},\quad l_{1}=[Q_{\mathcal{M}},\cdot],\quad l_{2}=[\cdot,\cdot],

and
\rho_{n}=l_{n+1}=0
for all
n\geq 2
.

Definition 3.4
.

An
L_{\infty}
algebroid is called
strict
if
\rho_{n}=0
for all
n\geq 2
. A strict
L_{\infty}
algebroid is called an
L_{\infty}
algebra bundle
if, in addition,
\rho_{1}=0
.

The compatibility conditions between the multi-brackets
\{l_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
and the unary anchor
\rho_{1}
of a strict
L_{\infty}
algebroid
\mathcal{L}/\mathcal{M}
simplify considerably due to the vanishing of the higher multi-anchors. One has

\displaystyle\rho_{1}(l_{1}(X))=[Q_{\mathcal{M}},\rho_{1}(X)],\quad\rho_{1}\big(l_{2}(X_{1},X_{2})\big)=\big[\rho_{1}(X_{1}),\rho_{1}(X_{2})\big],

and for all
n\geq 3
,

\rho_{1}\big(l_{n}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})\big)=0.

Definition 3.5
.

A
(base-preserving) morphism
between two
L_{\infty}
algebroids
\mathcal{L}_{1}/\mathcal{M}_{1}
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}/\mathcal{M}_{2}
consists of a pair
\Phi=(f,\Phi^{\sharp})
where

(f,f^{\sharp})\colon\mathcal{M}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{2}

is a dg manifold morphism, and

\Phi^{\sharp}\colon\mathrm{CE}(\mathcal{L}_{2})\longrightarrow f_{*}\mathrm{CE}(\mathcal{L}_{1})

is a filtered cdga morphism satisfying

\Phi^{\sharp}|_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}_{2}}}=f^{\sharp}.

Such
\Phi
is called
base-fixing
if
f=\mathrm{id}
.

The filtered cdga morphism
\Phi^{\sharp}
is uniquely determined by a sequence
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}_{1}}
-linear maps of degree
0

\phi^{\sharp}_{n}\colon\mathrm{Sym}^{n}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}_{1}}}(\mathcal{L}_{1}[1])\longrightarrow f^{*}\mathcal{L}_{2}[1],\quad n\geq 1.

Here, we use the canonical identifications

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}_{2}}}\bigl(\mathcal{L}_{2}[1]^{\vee},f_{*}\mathrm{Sym}^{n}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}_{1}}}(\mathcal{L}_{1}[1]^{\vee})\bigr)

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}_{1}}}\bigl(f^{*}\mathcal{L}_{2}[1]^{\vee},\mathrm{Sym}^{n}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}_{1}}}(\mathcal{L}_{1}[1]^{\vee})\bigr)

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}_{1}}}\bigl(\mathrm{Sym}^{n}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}_{1}}}(\mathcal{L}_{1}[1]),f^{*}\mathcal{L}_{2}[1]\bigr).

The second isomorphism follows from the fact that pullback commutes with duals for graded vector bundles, together with the canonical identification of a graded vector bundle with its double dual. The latter can be proved using Proposition
2.6
.

One can easily check that the condition

\Phi^{\sharp}\circ D_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}|_{\Omega^{0}_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}}=f_{*}D_{\mathcal{L}_{1}}\circ\Phi^{\sharp}|_{\Omega^{0}_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}}

is equivalent to the compatibility of
\{\phi^{\sharp}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
with the multi-anchors on
\mathcal{L}_{1}
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}
:

Tf^{\sharp}(\rho_{n}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n}))=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{j}=n}\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{n}}\frac{\epsilon(\sigma)}{j!k_{1}!\cdots k_{j}!}f^{*}\rho_{j}\big(\phi^{\sharp}_{k_{1}}(X_{\sigma(1)},\dots),\dots,\phi^{\sharp}_{k_{j}}(\dots,X_{\sigma(n)})\big).

For
n=0
, this reduces to
Tf^{\sharp}(Q_{\mathcal{M}_{1}})=f^{*}Q_{\mathcal{M}_{2}}
. For
n=1
, one has

Tf^{\sharp}(\rho_{1}(X))=f^{*}\rho_{1}(\phi_{1}^{\sharp}(X)).

The condition

\Phi^{\sharp}\circ D_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}|_{\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}}=f_{*}D_{\mathcal{L}_{1}}\circ\Phi^{\sharp}|_{\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}}

is more delicate to interpret. If
\mathcal{L}_{1}
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}
are both
L_{\infty}
algebra bundles, or if
f=\mathrm{id}
, then it is equivalent to the compatibility of
\{\phi_{n}^{\sharp}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
with the multi-brackets on
\mathcal{L}_{1}
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}
, namely

\displaystyle\sum_{p+q=n}\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{n}}\frac{\epsilon(\sigma)}{p!\,q!}\,\phi^{\sharp}_{q+1}\bigl(l_{p}(X_{\sigma(1)},\dots),\dots,X_{\sigma(n)}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{j}=n}\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{n}}\frac{\epsilon(\sigma)}{j!\,k_{1}!\cdots k_{j}!}\,f^{*}l_{j}\bigl(\phi^{\sharp}_{k_{1}}(X_{\sigma(1)},\dots),\dots,\phi^{\sharp}_{k_{j}}(\dots,X_{\sigma(n)})\bigr).

For
n=1
, one has

\phi_{1}^{\sharp}\bigl(l_{1}(X)\bigr)=f^{*}l_{1}\bigl(\phi_{1}^{\sharp}(X)\bigr).

In general, however, for
f\neq\mathrm{id}
and
n>1
, the pullback multi-brackets
f^{*}l_{n}
are not well defined due to the presence of multi-anchors.

Definition 3.6
.

An
L_{\infty}
algebroid morphism
\Phi\colon\mathcal{L}_{1}/\mathcal{M}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}_{2}/\mathcal{M}_{2}
is a
weak equivalence
if the linear term
\phi_{1}=(f,\phi_{1}^{\sharp})
is a weak equivalence of the underlying dg vector bundles.

Example 3.7
.

Let
f\colon\mathcal{M}_{1}\to\mathcal{M}_{2}
be a morphism of dg manifolds. There is a canonical
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}_{2}}
-linear map

f_{dR}^{\sharp}\colon\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathcal{M}_{2}}\longrightarrow f_{*}\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathcal{M}_{1}},

defined by

f_{dR}^{\sharp}(g)=f^{\sharp}(g),\qquad f_{dR}^{\sharp}(dg)=f_{*}d(f^{\sharp}(g))

for
g\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}_{2}}
.
One can easily check that
f_{dR}^{\sharp}
defines a filtered cdga morphism from
(\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathcal{M}_{2}},D_{\mathcal{M}_{2}})
to
f_{*}(\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathcal{M}_{1}},D_{\mathcal{M}_{1}})
. It follows that
f_{dR}\coloneqq(f,f_{dR}^{\sharp})
defines an
L_{\infty}
algebroid morphism between the tangent algebroids.

By Lemma
2.24
,
f_{dR}
is a weak equivalence of tangent algebroids whenever
f
is a weak equivalence of dg manifolds.

Definition 3.8
.

An
L_{\infty}
algebroid morphism
\Phi\colon\mathcal{L}_{1}/\mathcal{M}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}_{2}/\mathcal{M}_{2}
is called
strict
if
\phi_{n}^{\sharp}=0
for all
n\geq 2
.

The multi-anchors
\{\rho_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
of an
L_{\infty}
algebroid
\mathcal{L}/\mathcal{M}
define a base-fixing
L_{\infty}
algebroid morphism from
\mathcal{L}
to the tangent algebroid
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{M}}
, which is strict if and only if
\mathcal{L}
is strict. In other words, there is a canonical morphism

\mathrm{CE}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{M}})=(\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathcal{M}},D_{\mathcal{M}})\longrightarrow\mathrm{CE}(\mathcal{L})=(\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathcal{L}},D_{\mathcal{L}})

between filtered cdgas.

Definition 3.9
.

An
L_{\infty}
algebroid
\mathcal{L}/\mathcal{M}
is called
transitive (or fibrant)
if its unary anchor

\rho_{1}\colon\mathcal{L}\longrightarrow\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{M}}

is surjective.

Lemma 3.10
.

Every transitive
L_{\infty}
algebroid is strict up to an isomorphism.

The proof follows by adapting a standard argument from the
L_{\infty}
algebra literature.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{L}/\mathcal{M}
be a transitive
L_{\infty}
algebroid. We need to construct an
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}
-linear automorphism
\Phi^{\sharp}
of the filtered graded commutative algebra
\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathcal{L}}
such that

(\Phi^{\sharp}\circ D_{\mathcal{L}}\circ(\Phi^{\sharp})^{-1})(f)\in\Omega^{0}_{\mathcal{L}}\oplus\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}

for all
f\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}
.

For
n\geq m\geq 1
, let
\phi_{n}^{m}
denote the composition

\Omega^{m}_{\mathcal{L}}\xrightarrow{\Phi^{\sharp}|_{\Omega^{m}_{\mathcal{L}}}}\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathcal{L}}\longrightarrow\Omega^{n}_{\mathcal{L}}.

Recall that
D_{\mathcal{L}}(f)=Q_{\mathcal{M}}(f)+\sum_{m\geq 1}\rho_{m}^{\vee}(\mathrm{d}f)
. Then

\Phi^{\sharp}\circ D_{\mathcal{L}}(f)=Q_{\mathcal{M}}(f)+\sum_{m\geq 1}\sum_{n\geq m}\phi_{n}^{m}\big(\rho_{m}^{\vee}(\mathrm{d}f)\big).

Note that each
\phi_{n}^{m}
is uniquely determined by
\phi_{1}^{1},\dots,\phi_{n+1-m}^{1}
. We are therefore led to solve the following equations for
\phi^{1}_{k}
’s

\sum_{m=1}^{n}\phi_{n}^{m}(\phi_{1}^{1},\dots,\phi_{n+1-m}^{1})\circ\rho_{m}^{\vee}=0,\qquad n\geq 2.

We impose the initial condition
\phi_{1}^{1}=\mathrm{Id}_{\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}}
, which implies
\phi_{n}^{n}=\mathrm{Id}_{\Omega^{n}_{\mathcal{L}}}
for all
n\geq 2
. The equations then become

\phi_{n}^{1}\circ\rho_{1}^{\vee}+\sum_{1<m<n}\phi_{n}^{m}\circ\rho_{m}^{\vee}=-\rho_{n}^{\vee},\qquad n\geq 2.

In particular, for
n=2
, this reduces to

\phi_{2}^{1}\circ\rho_{1}^{\vee}=-\rho_{2}^{\vee}.

Since
\mathcal{L}
is transitive, the injective map
\rho_{1}^{\vee}\colon\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{M}}\to\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}
admits a splitting
h_{1}\colon\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}\to\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{M}}
. Thus

\phi_{2}^{1}=-\rho_{2}^{\vee}\circ h_{1}

solves the equation.
Proceeding inductively, one constructs
\phi_{n}^{1}
for all
n
, and hence
\Phi^{\sharp}
.
∎

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a strict transitive
L_{\infty}
algebroid over
\mathcal{M}
, and let

\mathcal{L}_{red}\coloneqq\ker\rho_{1}.

By Proposition
2.6
,
\mathcal{L}_{red}
is a graded vector bundle over
\mathcal{M}
.

Lemma 3.11
.

For all
n\geq 1
, one has

\rho_{1}(l_{n}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n}))=0

whenever at least one of the elements
X_{i}\in\mathcal{L}
belongs to
\mathcal{L}_{red}
.

Proof.

Recall that for all
n\geq 3
, one has
\rho_{1}\circ l_{n}=0
. While for
n=1,2
, we have

\displaystyle\rho_{1}(l_{1}(X))=[Q_{\mathcal{M}},\rho_{1}(X)],\quad\rho_{1}\big(l_{2}(X_{1},X_{2})\big)=\big[\rho_{1}(X_{1}),\rho_{1}(X_{2})\big].

These expressions vanish whenever
X
or at least one of
X_{1},X_{2}
belongs to
\mathcal{L}_{red}
.
∎

In particular, equipped with the induced multi-brackets from
\mathcal{L}
,
\mathcal{L}_{red}
is an
L_{\infty}
algebra bundle over
\mathcal{M}
, called the
isotropy
L_{\infty}
algebra bundle
.

3.2.
Commutative algebraic perspective

Let
V
be a non-positively graded vector space of finite total rank. Let
(V_{0},\mathcal{O}_{V})
denote the associated ringed space as defined in the previous section. Let
\mathbb{V}
be a graded
\mathcal{O}_{V}
-module free of finite total rank. We denote by
(V_{0},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{V}})
the ringed space whose structure sheaf is given by

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{V}}\coloneqq\widehat{\mathrm{Sym}}_{\mathcal{O}_{V}}(\mathbb{V}^{\vee}),

where
\widehat{\mathrm{Sym}}_{\mathcal{O}_{V}}(\mathbb{V}^{\vee})
is the completion of
\mathrm{Sym}_{\mathcal{O}_{V}}(\mathbb{V}^{\vee})
with respect to its canonical descending filtration by symmetric degree.

Definition 3.12
.

A
(projected) filtered graded manifold
is a ringed space
\mathcal{C}=(C,\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}})
, where
C
is a smooth manifold and
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}
is a sheaf of graded commutative algebras on
C
, together with an ideal
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{C}}
and a splitting of the short exact sequence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{C}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}/\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{C}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

of sheaves of graded commutative algebras.

These data are required to satisfy the following condition: for every
x\in C
, there exists an open neighborhood
U\ni x
and a commutative diagram of ringed spaces

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(U,\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}|_{U})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(V_{0},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{V}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(U,(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}/\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{C}})|_{U})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(V_{0},\mathcal{O}_{V})}

where the horizontal arrows are isomorphisms.

By definition,
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}
is complete with respect to the
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{C}}
-adic filtration

F^{p}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}=\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{C}}^{p},\qquad p\in\mathbb{N}.

Moreover, the ringed space

\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{C}\coloneqq(C,\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}/\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{C}})

is a graded manifold, called the
base
of
\mathcal{C}
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a filtered graded manifold. For each open
U\subset C
, define

\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{C}}(U)\coloneqq\operatorname{Der}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}(U)).

The assignment
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{C}}
defines a sheaf, called the
tangent sheaf
of
\mathcal{C}
. Global sections of
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{C}}
are called
vector fields
on
\mathcal{C}
. Note that
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{C}}
carries a natural filtration given by

F^{p}\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{C}}=\{X\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{C}}\colon X(F^{\bullet}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}})\subset F^{\bullet+p}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\},\qquad p\in\mathbb{Z}.

Definition 3.13
.

A
quasi-dg manifold
is a filtered graded manifold
\mathcal{C}
equipped with a degree
1
vector field
Q_{\mathcal{C}}
that satisfies

•

[Q_{\mathcal{C}},Q_{\mathcal{C}}]=0
;

•

Q_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{C}})\subseteq\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{C}}
, or equivalently,
Q_{\mathcal{C}}\in F^{0}\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{C}}(C)
.

Equipped with
Q_{\mathcal{C}}
and the
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{C}}
-adic filtration,
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}
becomes a sheaf of filtered cdgas. It follows that
\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{C}
, endowed with
\mathrm{Gr}^{0}Q_{\mathcal{C}}
, is a dg manifold. Note that the morphism of graded commutative algebras
\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}
is not, in general, a morphism of cdgas.

Example 3.14
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=(M,\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}})
be a dg manifold.

•

\mathcal{M}
equipped with the trivial dg ideal defines a quasi-dg manifold over itself.

•

The ringed space
\mathcal{M}_{dR}\coloneqq(M,\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathcal{M}})
, equipped with the differential
D_{\mathcal{M}}=\mathrm{d}_{dR}+L_{Q_{\mathcal{M}}}
and the dg ideal
\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathcal{M}}^{>0}
of completed differential forms whose projection to functions vanishes, defines a quasi-dg manifold over
\mathcal{M}
, called the
de Rham space
of
\mathcal{M}
.

Definition 3.15
.

A
morphism
between quasi-dg manifolds
\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\mathcal{C}_{2}
consists of a pair

F=(f,F^{\sharp})
, where
f\colon C_{1}\to C_{2}
is a smooth map and
F^{\sharp}\colon\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}_{2}}\longrightarrow f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}_{1}}
is a morphism of sheaves of filtered cdgas.

Such
F
is called
base-preserving
if the following diagram of sheaves of graded commutative algebras

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}_{2}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}_{1}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}_{2}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{*}\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}_{1}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F^{\sharp}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Gr}^{0}F^{\sharp}}

commutes. A base-preserving

is called
base-fixing
if
\mathrm{Gr}^{0}F^{\sharp}=\mathrm{id}
.

We denote by
\mathrm{Gr}^{0}F=(f,\mathrm{Gr}^{0}F^{\sharp})
the dg manifold morphism between
\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{C}_{2}
.

Definition 3.16
.

A quasi-dg manifold morphism
F\colon\mathcal{C}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}_{2}
is called a
weak equivalence
if
F^{\sharp}_{C_{2}}\colon\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}_{2}}(C_{2})\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}_{1}}(C_{1})
is a filtered quasi-isomorphism; that is, if
\mathrm{Gr}\,F^{\sharp}_{C_{2}}\colon\mathrm{Gr}\,\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}_{2}}(C_{2})\rightarrow\mathrm{Gr}\,\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}_{1}}(C_{1})
is a quasi-isomorphism.

Lemma 3.17
.

There exists a unique base-fixing morphism between quasi-dg manifolds

H\colon\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow(\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{C})_{dR}.

Moreover, for any base-preserving morphism
F\colon\mathcal{C}_{1}\to\mathcal{C}_{2}
between quasi-dg manifolds, the following diagram of quasi-dg manifolds

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{C}_{1})_{dR}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{C}_{2})_{dR}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces dR(\mathrm{Gr}^{0}F)}

commutes.

Proof.

As a filtered graded commutative algebra,
\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{C}}
is generated by functions and exact
1
-forms. We define
H^{\sharp}\colon\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{C}}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}
by specifying its action on the generators:

H^{\sharp}(f)=f,\qquad H^{\sharp}(\mathrm{d}f)=(Q_{\mathcal{C}}-\mathrm{Gr}^{0}Q_{\mathcal{C}})f,

for all
f\in\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}
. It is straightforward to verify that
H
satisfies the desired properties.
∎

Definition 3.18
.

A quasi-dg manifold
\mathcal{C}
is called
fibrant
if

\mathrm{Gr}\,H^{\sharp}\colon\Omega_{\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{C}}\to\mathrm{Gr}\,\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}

is injective.

Let
\mathbf{L_{\infty}Algd}
denote the category of
L_{\infty}
algebroids over dg manifolds. Let
\mathbf{QdgM}
denote the category of quasi-dg manifolds, and
\mathbf{QdgM}^{\mathrm{bp}}\subset\mathbf{QdgM}
denote the subcategory with the same objects and base-preserving morphisms.

Consider the assignment

\mathbf{CE}_{\mathbf{Algd}}\colon\mathbf{L_{\infty}Algd}\longrightarrow\mathbf{QdgM}^{\mathrm{bp}}

defined on objects
\mathcal{L}/\mathcal{M}
by

\mathbf{CE}_{\mathbf{Algd}}(\mathcal{L}/\mathcal{M})=(M,\mathrm{CE}(\mathcal{L})),

and on morphisms
\Phi=((f,f^{\sharp}),\Phi^{\sharp})\colon\mathcal{L}_{1}/\mathcal{M}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}_{2}/\mathcal{M}_{2}
by

\mathbf{CE}_{\mathbf{Algd}}(\Phi)=(f,\Phi^{\sharp})\colon(M_{1},\mathrm{CE}(\mathcal{L}_{1}))\longrightarrow(M_{2},\mathrm{CE}(\mathcal{L}_{2})).

\mathbf{CE}_{\mathbf{Algd}}
is obviously a well-defined functor, called the
Chevalley–Eilenberg functor
.

Theorem 3.19
.

The Chevalley–Eilenberg functor
\mathbf{CE}_{\mathbf{Algd}}
yields an equivalence of categories. Moreover, it detects weak equivalences and fibrant objects.

Proof.

By definition,
\mathbf{CE}_{\mathbf{Algd}}
is fully faithful. We need to show that it is essentially surjective. Let
\mathcal{C}
be a quasi-dg manifold. Since we are in the smooth setting, the short exact sequence of locally free graded
\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}
-modules

0\longrightarrow F^{2}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\longrightarrow F^{1}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Gr}^{1}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\longrightarrow 0

admits a splitting
\mathrm{Gr}^{1}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow F^{1}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}
. Let
\mathcal{L}=(\mathrm{Gr}^{1}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}[1])^{\vee}
. By the universal property of the symmetric algebra, the splitting induces a graded commutative
\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}
-algebra morphism

\Omega_{\mathcal{L}}=\mathrm{Sym}_{\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}}(\mathcal{L}[1]^{\vee})\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}},

which extends uniquely to a morphism of filtered graded commutative algebras

\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathcal{L}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}.

This morphism is locally an isomorphism and therefore an isomorphism globally. We can use it to transfer the differential
Q_{\mathcal{C}}
on
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}
to a differential
D_{\mathcal{L}}
on
\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathcal{L}}
.
\mathcal{L}
is then an
L_{\infty}
algebroid over
\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{C}
.

Let
\Phi=(f,\{\phi_{n}^{\sharp}\}_{n=1}^{\infty})\colon\mathcal{L}_{1}/\mathcal{M}_{1}\to\mathcal{L}_{2}/\mathcal{M}_{2}
be a morphism of
L_{\infty}
algebroids. The functor detects weak equivalences: by Lemmas
2.22
and
2.23
, the map

\mathrm{Gr}\,\Phi^{\sharp}_{M_{2}}\colon\mathrm{Sym}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}_{2}}(M_{2})}\!\big(\mathcal{L}_{2}[1]^{\vee}(M_{2})\big)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Sym}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}_{1}}(M_{1})}\!\big(\mathcal{L}_{1}[1]^{\vee}(M_{1})\big)

is a quasi-isomorphism if and only if both

f^{\sharp}_{M_{2}}\colon\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}_{2}}(M_{2})\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}_{1}}(M_{1})\quad\text{and}\quad\phi^{\sharp}_{M_{1}}\colon\mathcal{L}_{1}[1](M_{1})\rightarrow f^{*}\mathcal{L}_{2}[1](M_{1})

are quasi-isomorphisms.

The functor detects fibrant objects:
\mathrm{Gr}\,H^{\sharp}\colon\Omega_{\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{C}}\to\mathrm{Gr}\,\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}
is injective if and only if

\mathrm{Gr}^{1}H^{\sharp}\colon\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{C}}\cong(\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{C}}[1])^{\vee}\to\mathrm{Gr}^{1}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\cong\mathcal{L}[1]^{\vee}

is injective, which holds precisely when the dual map
\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{C}}
is surjective.
∎

4.
L_{\infty}
spaces over dg manifolds

In this section, we adapt Costello’s notion of
L_{\infty}
spaces
[
costello2011geometric
]
to the dg manifold setting and prove an equivalence of the categories of transitive
L_{\infty}
algebroids and
L_{\infty}
spaces over dg manifolds.

4.1.
Curved
L_{\infty}
algebras

Let
(R,D_{R})
be a cdga and
I\subset R
a proper dg ideal. Assume that
R
is complete with respect to the
I
-adic filtration
F^{p}R=I^{p}
. Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a graded
R
-module, endowed with the induced filtration

F^{p}\mathfrak{g}\coloneqq I^{p}\cdot\mathfrak{g}.

Assume further that
\mathfrak{g}
is complete with respect to this filtration.

Definition 4.1
.

A
curved
L_{\infty}
algebra
structure on
\mathfrak{g}
consists of the following data:

•

an element of degree
1

l_{0}\in F^{1}\mathfrak{g}[1];

•

a filtration-preserving
\mathbb{R}
-linear map of degree
1

l_{1}\colon\mathfrak{g}[1]\to\mathfrak{g}[1];

•

for each
n\geq 2
, a filtration-preserving
R
-linear map of degree
1

l_{n}\colon\mathrm{Sym}_{R}^{n}(\mathfrak{g}[1])\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}[1].

These operations satisfy the following identities:

•

(Leibniz rule) for every
r\in R
and
x\in\mathfrak{g}[1]
,

l_{1}(rx)=D_{R}(r)\,x+(-1)^{|r|}r\,l_{1}(x);

•

(Strong homotopy Jacobi identities) for every
n\geq 0
and
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in\mathfrak{g}[1]
,

\sum_{p+q=n}\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{n}}\frac{\epsilon(\sigma)}{p!\,q!}\,l_{q+1}\big(l_{p}(x_{\sigma(1)},\dots,x_{\sigma(p)}),x_{\sigma(p+1)},\dots,x_{\sigma(n)}\big)=0.

In particular, one has

l_{1}(l_{0})=0,\quad\text{and}\quad l_{2}(l_{0},x)+l_{1}(l_{1}(x))=0.

Accordingly,
l_{0}
is called the
curvature
of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

The associated
\mathbb{N}
-graded operator

\mathrm{Gr}\,l_{1}\colon\mathrm{Gr}\,\mathfrak{g}[1]\longrightarrow\mathrm{Gr}\,\mathfrak{g}[1]

is a differential.

Proof.

Since
l_{0}\in F^{1}\mathfrak{g}[1]
and
l_{n}
preserves the filtration on
\mathfrak{g}[1]
, we have

l_{1}(l_{1}(x))=-\,l_{2}(l_{0},x)\in F^{p+1}\mathfrak{g}[1]

for any
x\in F^{p}\mathfrak{g}[1]
. Thus,
\mathrm{Gr}\,l_{1}\circ\mathrm{Gr}\,l_{1}=0
.
∎

Let
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}
denote the
R
-dual of
\mathfrak{g}
. The filtration on
\mathfrak{g}
induces a filtration on
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}
:

F^{p}\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}=\{\alpha\in\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}\mid\alpha(F^{q}\mathfrak{g})\subset F^{p+q}R\text{ for all }q\}.

Lemma 4.3
.

If
\mathfrak{g}
is projective and finitely generated over
R
, then the filtration on
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}
is also induced by the filtration on
R
, i.e.,

F^{p}\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}=F^{p}R\cdot\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}.

Proof.

We only need to show that
F^{p}\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}\subset F^{p}R\cdot\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}
.
Without loss of generality, assume that
\mathfrak{g}
is free of finite rank with basis
\{v_{i}\}
. If
\alpha\in F^{p}\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}
, then
\alpha(v_{i})\in F^{p}R
for all
i
, so

\alpha=\sum_{i}\alpha(v_{i})\cdot v_{i}^{\vee}\in F^{p}R\cdot\mathfrak{g}^{\vee},

where
\{v_{i}^{\vee}\}
denotes the dual basis.
∎

For our purposes, we will henceforth assume that
\mathfrak{g}
is finitely generated and projective. Consider the following two natural filtrations on
\mathrm{Sym}_{R}(\mathfrak{g}[1]^{\vee})
:

•

Internal filtration defined by

F_{I}^{p}\mathrm{Sym}_{R}(\mathfrak{g}[1]^{\vee})\coloneqq F^{p}R\cdot\mathrm{Sym}_{R}(\mathfrak{g}[1]^{\vee})

•

Symmetric filtration defined by

F_{S}^{p}\mathrm{Sym}_{R}(\mathfrak{g}[1]^{\vee})\coloneqq\bigoplus_{q\geq p}\mathrm{Sym}_{R}^{q}(\mathfrak{g}[1]^{\vee}).

These two filtrations can be combined into a single filtration by convolution:

F^{p}\mathrm{Sym}_{R}(\mathfrak{g}[1]^{\vee})\coloneqq\sum_{i+j=p}F_{I}^{i}\mathrm{Sym}_{R}(\mathfrak{g}[1]^{\vee})\odot F_{S}^{j}\mathrm{Sym}_{R}(\mathfrak{g}[1]^{\vee}),

which is the smallest filtration containing both. We use
\widehat{\mathrm{Sym}}_{R}(\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}[-1])
to denote the completion with respect to this filtration.

The multi-brackets
\{l_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
on
\mathfrak{g}
determine a filtration-preserving differential

D_{\mathfrak{g}}\colon\widehat{\mathrm{Sym}}_{R}(\mathfrak{g}[1]^{\vee})\longrightarrow\widehat{\mathrm{Sym}}_{R}(\mathfrak{g}[1]^{\vee}).

More precisely,
D_{\mathfrak{g}}
is determined by its action on the generators

D_{\mathfrak{g}}(r)=D_{R}(r),\qquad D_{\mathfrak{g}}(\alpha)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}l_{n}^{\vee}(\alpha)

for
r\in R
and
\alpha\in\mathfrak{g}[1]^{\vee}
. Here
l_{n}^{\vee}
denotes the
R
-linear dual of
l_{n}
for
n\neq 1
, while for
n=1
we define

(l_{1}^{\vee}(\alpha))(x)=D_{R}(\alpha(x))-(-1)^{|\alpha|}\alpha(l_{1}(x)).

The condition
D_{\mathfrak{g}}^{2}=0
is equivalent to the strong homotopy Jacobi identities of
\{l_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
.

Definition 4.4
.

We call the filtered cdga

\mathrm{CE}(\mathfrak{g})\coloneqq(\widehat{\mathrm{Sym}}_{R}(\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}[-1]),D_{\mathfrak{g}})

the
Chevalley–Eilenberg algebra
of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Definition 4.5
.

A
(base-fixing) morphism
between curved
L_{\infty}
algebras
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
over
(R,D_{R})
is an
R
-linear morphism between filtered cdgas

\Phi\colon\mathrm{CE}(\mathfrak{g}_{2})\longrightarrow\mathrm{CE}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}).

Such
\Phi
is uniquely determined by a degree
0
element

\phi_{0}\in F^{1}\mathfrak{g}_{2}[1],

and a family of degree
0

R
-linear maps

\phi_{n}\colon\mathrm{Sym}_{R}^{n}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}[1])\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{2}[1],\quad n\geq 1.

\{\phi_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
intertwines the multi-brackets on
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
:

\displaystyle\sum_{p+q=n}\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{n}}\frac{\epsilon(\sigma)}{p!q!}\,\phi_{q+1}\big(l_{p}(x_{\sigma(1)},\dots),\dots,x_{\sigma(n)}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{j}=n}\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{n}}\frac{\epsilon(\sigma)}{j!k_{1}!\cdots k_{j}!}\,l_{j}\big(\phi_{k_{1}}(x_{\sigma(1)},\dots),\dots,\phi_{k_{j}}(\dots,x_{\sigma(n)})\big).

In particular, for
n=0
and
n=1
, one has

\displaystyle\phi_{1}(l_{0})=l_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j!}l_{j}(\phi_{0}^{\otimes j}),

\displaystyle\phi_{1}(l_{1}(x))+\phi_{2}(l_{0},x)=l_{1}(\phi_{1}(x))+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j!}\,l_{j+1}(\phi_{0}^{\otimes j},\phi_{1}(x)).

Lemma 4.6
.

The associated
\mathbb{N}
-graded morphism

\mathrm{Gr}\,\phi_{1}\colon\mathrm{Gr}\,\mathfrak{g}_{1}[1]\longrightarrow\mathrm{Gr}\,\mathfrak{g}_{2}[1]

commutes with the differentials
\mathrm{Gr}\,l_{1}
.

Proof.

Since
l_{0}\in F^{1}\mathfrak{g}_{1}[1]
and
\phi_{0}\in F^{1}\mathfrak{g}_{2}[1]
, we have

\phi_{1}(l_{1}(x))-l_{1}(\phi_{1}(x))\in F^{p+1}\mathfrak{g}_{2}[1]

for any
x\in F^{p}\mathfrak{g}_{1}[1]
. Thus,
\mathrm{Gr}\,\phi_{1}\circ\mathrm{Gr}\,l_{1}=\mathrm{Gr}\,l_{1}\circ\mathrm{Gr}\,\phi_{1}
.
∎

Definition 4.7
.

\Phi
is called a
weak equivalence
if

\mathrm{Gr}\,\phi_{1}\colon(\mathrm{Gr}\,\mathfrak{g}_{1}[1],\mathrm{Gr}\,l_{1})\longrightarrow(\mathrm{Gr}\,\mathfrak{g}_{2}[1],\mathrm{Gr}\,l_{1})

is a quasi-isomorphism.

Equivalently, a curved
L_{\infty}
algebra morphism
\Phi\colon\mathfrak{g}_{1}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{2}
can be regarded as a degree
0
element

\alpha\in F^{1}\mathrm{CE}(\mathfrak{g}_{1})\,\widehat{\otimes}_{R}\,\mathfrak{g}_{2},

where
\widehat{\otimes}_{R}
denotes the completed tensor product of filtered graded
R
-modules.
\alpha
satisfies the
Maurer–Cartan equation
:

\mathrm{MC}(\alpha)\coloneqq 1\otimes l_{0}+(D_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}\otimes 1+1\otimes l_{1})(\alpha)+\sum_{n\geq 2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}(1\otimes l_{n})(\alpha^{\otimes n})=0.

The following definition (and Definition
4.12
) will not be used in the rest of the paper and may be skipped on a first reading.

Definition 4.8
.

Let
(S,D_{S})
be a cdga equipped with a dg ideal
J\subset S
, complete with respect to the
J
-adic filtration, together with a cdga morphism
(R,D_{R})\rightarrow(S,D_{S})
sending the dg ideal
I
of
R
into
J
.

The set of
S
-points of
\mathfrak{g}
is defined as

\mathrm{MC}(\mathfrak{g};S)\coloneqq\left\{\alpha\in(J\,\widehat{\otimes}_{R}\,\mathfrak{g})_{0}\big|\mathrm{MC}(\alpha)=0\right\},

where
\mathrm{MC}(\alpha)\coloneqq 1\otimes l_{0}+(D_{S}\otimes 1+1\otimes l_{1})(\alpha)+\sum_{n\geq 2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}(1\otimes l_{n})(\alpha^{\otimes n})
.

4.2.
L_{\infty}
spaces

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a dg manifold.

Definition 4.9
.

An
L_{\infty}
space
over
\mathcal{M}
is a pair
B\mathfrak{g}=(\mathcal{M},\mathfrak{g})
, where
\mathfrak{g}
is a sheaf of graded
\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathcal{M}}
-modules, locally free of finite total rank, equipped with a curved
L_{\infty}
algebra structure over
(\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathcal{M}},D_{\mathcal{M}})
with dg ideal
\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathcal{M}}^{>0}
.

Example 4.10
.

An
L_{\infty}
space over a point is simply a finite-dimensional
L_{\infty}
algebra.

Unlike
L_{\infty}
algebroids over dg manifolds,
L_{\infty}
spaces over dg manifolds admit pullbacks along morphisms of dg manifolds. Let
f\colon\mathcal{M}_{1}\to\mathcal{M}_{2}
be a morphism of dg manifolds, and let
B\mathfrak{g}_{2}
be an
L_{\infty}
space over
\mathcal{M}_{2}
. We define the pullback
L_{\infty}
space
Bf^{*}\mathfrak{g}_{2}=(\mathcal{M}_{1},f^{*}\mathfrak{g}_{2})
over
\mathcal{M}_{1}
by

f^{*}\mathfrak{g}_{2}\coloneqq\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathcal{M}_{1}}\,\widehat{\otimes}_{f^{-1}\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathcal{M}_{2}}}\,f^{-1}\mathfrak{g}_{2}.

Definition 4.11
.

A
morphism

B\mathfrak{g}_{1}\to B\mathfrak{g}_{2}
of
L_{\infty}
spaces is a pair
\Phi=(f,\Phi^{\sharp})
, where

•

f\colon\mathcal{M}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{2}
is a dg manifold morphism;

•

\Phi^{\sharp}\colon\mathfrak{g}_{1}\rightarrow f^{*}\mathfrak{g}_{2}
is a morphism of sheaves of curved
L_{\infty}
algebras.

Such
\Phi
is a
weak equivalence
if
f
is a weak equivalence of dg manifolds and

\Phi^{\sharp}_{M_{1}}\colon\mathfrak{g}_{1}(M_{1})\rightarrow f^{*}\mathfrak{g}_{2}(M_{1})

is a weak equivalence of curved
L_{\infty}
algebras.

A morphism of
L_{\infty}
spaces
\Phi\colon B\mathfrak{g}_{1}\to B\mathfrak{g}_{2}
is equivalent to a degree
0
element

\alpha\in F^{1}\mathrm{CE}(\mathfrak{g}_{1})\,\widehat{\otimes}_{\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathcal{M}_{1}}}\,f^{*}\mathfrak{g}_{2},

satisfying the Maurer–Cartan equation.

Definition 4.12
.

Let
\mathcal{C}=(C,\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}})
be a quasi-dg manifold. Let
B\mathfrak{g}=(\mathcal{M},\mathfrak{g})
be an
L_{\infty}
space. The set of
\mathcal{C}
-points of
B\mathfrak{g}
is

\mathrm{MC}(B\mathfrak{g};\mathcal{C})\coloneqq\bigsqcup_{f\colon\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{M}}\mathrm{MC}(f^{*}\mathfrak{g};\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}),

where the disjoint union is taken over all dg manifold morphisms
f\colon\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{M}
.

Let
\mathbf{L_{\infty}Sp}
denote the category of
L_{\infty}
spaces over dg manifolds. Let
\mathbf{QdgM}^{\mathrm{bp}}_{\mathrm{fib}}
denote the category of fibrant quasi-dg manifolds with base-preserving morphisms.

Consider the assignment

\mathbf{CE}_{\mathbf{Sp}}\colon\mathbf{L_{\infty}Sp}\longrightarrow\mathbf{QdgM}^{\mathrm{bp}}_{\mathrm{fib}}

defined on objects
B\mathfrak{g}=(\mathcal{M},\mathfrak{g})
by

\mathbf{CE}_{\mathbf{Sp}}(B\mathfrak{g})=(M,\mathrm{CE}(\mathfrak{g})),

and on morphisms
\Phi=((f,f^{\sharp}),\Phi^{\sharp})\colon B\mathfrak{g}_{1}\rightarrow B\mathfrak{g}_{2}
by

\mathbf{CE}_{\mathbf{Sp}}(\Phi)=(f,\Phi^{\sharp})\colon(M_{1},\mathrm{CE}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}))\longrightarrow(M_{2},\mathrm{CE}(\mathfrak{g}_{2})),

where we use the identification

\mathrm{Hom}_{\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathcal{M}_{1}}}\bigl(f^{*}\mathfrak{g}_{2}[1]^{\vee},\mathrm{CE}(\mathfrak{g}_{1})\bigr)\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathcal{M}_{2}}}\bigl(\mathfrak{g}_{2}[1]^{\vee},f_{*}\mathrm{CE}(\mathfrak{g}_{1})\bigr).

\mathbf{CE}_{\mathbf{Sp}}
is obviously a well-defined functor, called the
Chevalley–Eilenberg functor
.

Theorem 4.13
.

\mathbf{CE}_{\mathbf{Sp}}
yields an equivalence of categories and detects weak equivalences.

Proof.

By definition,
\mathbf{CE}_{\mathbf{Sp}}
is fully faithful. We need to show that it is essentially surjective. Let
\mathcal{C}
be a fibrant quasi-dg manifold. By Theorem
3.19
and Lemma
3.10
, we know that there exists a strict transitive
L_{\infty}
algebroid
\mathcal{L}
over
\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{C}
such that

(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}},Q_{\mathcal{C}})\cong(\mathrm{CE}(\mathcal{L}),D_{\mathcal{L}})

as filtered cdga over
(\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{C}},D_{\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{C}})
. We now set

\mathfrak{g}\coloneqq\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{C}}\,\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}}\,\mathcal{L}_{red},

where
\mathcal{L}_{red}=\ker\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{1}\colon\mathcal{L}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{C}}
is the unary anchor of
\mathcal{L}
. Fix a splitting

\sigma\colon\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{C}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}

of the short exact sequence of graded vector bundles

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{red}\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}\longrightarrow\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{C}}\longrightarrow 0.

We can use
\sigma
to define a
\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}
-linear map

\mathcal{L}[1]^{\vee}\cong\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{C}}\oplus\mathcal{L}_{red}[1]^{\vee}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Sym}_{\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{C}}}(\mathfrak{g}[1]^{\vee})

By the universal property of the symmetric algebra, this map induces a graded commutative
\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}
-algebra morphism

\Omega_{\mathcal{L}}=\mathrm{Sym}_{\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}}(\mathcal{L}[1]^{\vee})\longrightarrow\mathrm{Sym}_{\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{C}}}(\mathfrak{g}[1]^{\vee})

which extends uniquely to a morphism of filtered graded commutative algebras

\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathcal{L}}=\widehat{\mathrm{Sym}}_{\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}}(\mathcal{L}[1]^{\vee})\longrightarrow\widehat{\mathrm{Sym}}_{\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{C}}}(\mathfrak{g}[1]^{\vee}),

which is locally an isomorphism and therefore an isomorphism globally. We can use it to transfer the differential
D_{\mathcal{L}}
on
\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathcal{L}}
to a differential on
\widehat{\mathrm{Sym}}_{\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{C}}}(\mathfrak{g}[1]^{\vee})
. Since all higher anchor maps of
\mathcal{L}
vanish, the induced differential
D_{\mathfrak{g}}
satisfies the desired Leibniz rule

D_{\mathfrak{g}}(\omega\alpha)=D_{\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{C}}(\omega)\,\alpha+(-1)^{|\omega|}\,\omega\,D_{\mathfrak{g}}(\alpha),

where
\omega\in\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{C}}
and
\alpha\in\widehat{\mathrm{Sym}}_{\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{C}}}(\mathfrak{g}[1]^{\vee})
. Thus,
\mathfrak{g}
is a sheaf of curved
L_{\infty}
algebras over
(\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{C}},D_{\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{C}})
, and
B\mathfrak{g}\coloneqq(\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{C},\mathfrak{g})
defines an
L_{\infty}
space over
\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{C}
.

Let
\Phi\colon B\mathfrak{g}_{1}\to B\mathfrak{g}_{2}
be a morphism of
L_{\infty}
spaces, and let
\widetilde{\Phi}\colon\mathcal{L}_{1}/\mathcal{M}_{1}\to\mathcal{L}_{2}/\mathcal{M}_{2}
be the corresponding morphism of
L_{\infty}
algebroids. By Theorem
3.19
, to show that the functor
\mathbf{CE}_{\mathbf{Sp}}
detects weak equivalences, it suffices to show that
\widetilde{\Phi}
is a weak equivalence of
L_{\infty}
algebroids whenever
\Phi
is a weak equivalence of
L_{\infty}
spaces, which follows from Lemma
2.24
.

More precisely, let
\phi_{1}^{\sharp}
denote the linear component of
\Phi^{\sharp}
. Using the identifications

\mathfrak{g}_{i}\cong\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathcal{M}_{i}}\,\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}_{i}}}\,(\mathcal{L}_{i})_{red},\qquad i=1,2,

one sees that

\mathrm{Gr}\,\phi_{1}^{\sharp}\colon\mathrm{Gr}\,\mathfrak{g}_{1}[1]\longrightarrow\mathrm{Gr}\,f^{*}\mathfrak{g}_{2}[1]

is a quasi-isomorphism on global sections if and only if

(\mathcal{L}_{1})_{red}\longrightarrow(\mathcal{L}_{2})_{red}

is a weak equivalence of dg vector bundles. Note that
\Phi
induces a morphism of short exact sequences of dg vector bundles

Corollary 4.14
.

There is an equivalence between the category of
L_{\infty}
spaces over dg manifolds and that of transitive
L_{\infty}
algebroids over dg manifolds, which detects weak equivalences.

At first glance, Corollary
4.14
may seem surprising, since
L_{\infty}
spaces and
L_{\infty}
algebroids appear to be of quite different nature. In particular, one may wonder how the curvature of an
L_{\infty}
space arises under this equivalence. The following example helps dispel the mystery.

Example 4.15
.

Let
M
be a manifold. Let
L
be a transitive Lie algebroid over
M
; that is, a strict transitive
L_{\infty}
algebroid over
M
whose only non-vanishing multi-bracket is the binary bracket

[\cdot,\cdot]_{L}\colon L\times_{M}L\rightarrow L.

Let
L_{red}
be the isotropy Lie algebra bundle of
L
, i.e., the kernel of
\rho_{L}\colon L\rightarrow T_{M}
.

To construct the corresponding
L_{\infty}
space over
M
, we follow the proof of Theorem
4.13
to choose a vector bundle splitting

\sigma\colon T_{M}\longrightarrow L

of the short exact sequence of Lie algebroids

0\longrightarrow L_{red}\longrightarrow L\xlongrightarrow{\rho_{L}}T_{M}\longrightarrow 0.

This splitting induces a connection on
L_{red}
by

\nabla^{\sigma}_{X}(s)\coloneqq[\sigma(X),s]_{L}.

The connection
\nabla^{\sigma}
is well-defined since

[\nabla^{\sigma}_{X},f](s)=\rho_{L}\circ\sigma(X)(f)s=X(f)s,\quad\rho_{L}(\nabla^{\sigma}_{X}(s))=[X,\rho_{L}(s)]=0.

Moreover, it is compatible with the bracket

\nabla^{\sigma}_{X}([s_{1},s_{2}]_{L})=[\nabla^{\sigma}_{X}(s_{1}),s_{2}]_{L}+[s_{1},\nabla^{\sigma}_{X}(s_{2})]_{L}.

Its curvature
R_{\nabla^{\sigma}}
measures the failure of
\sigma
to be a Lie algebroid splitting. Explicitly,

R_{\nabla^{\sigma}}(X,Y)=[\sigma(X),\sigma(Y)]_{L}-\sigma([X,Y]).

We are now ready to describe the corresponding
L_{\infty}
space
B\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma}=(M,\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma})
over
M
. For an open
U\subset M
, we set

\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma}(U)\coloneqq\Omega_{M}(U)\otimes_{C^{\infty}(U)}\Gamma\bigl(L_{red}|_{U}\bigr).

The non-vanishing multi-brackets on
\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma}
are

l_{0}=R_{\nabla^{\sigma}}\in F^{1}\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma}[1],\qquad l_{1}=\mathrm{d}_{\nabla^{\sigma}}\colon\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma}[1]\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma}[1],

and

l_{2}\colon\mathrm{Sym}^{2}_{\Omega_{M}}(\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma}[1])\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma}[1],

defined by

l_{2}(\alpha\otimes s,\beta\otimes t)=(\alpha\wedge\beta)\otimes[s,t]_{L}.

One can readily verify that the strong homotopy Jacobi identities for
\{l_{n}\}_{n=0}^{2}
hold precisely by virtue of the second Bianchi identity for
R_{\nabla^{\sigma}}
, the compatibility between
\nabla^{\sigma}
and
[\cdot,\cdot]_{L}
, and the Jacobi identity for
[\cdot,\cdot]_{L}
.

More generally, let
\mathcal{L}/\mathcal{M}
be a strict transitive
L_{\infty}
algebroid, and let
\sigma\colon\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{M}}\to\mathcal{L}
be a graded vector bundle splitting of the short exact sequence of strict
L_{\infty}
algebroids

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{red}\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}\xlongrightarrow{\rho_{1}}\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{M}}\longrightarrow 0.

Then the curvature of the associated
L_{\infty}
space is precisely the obstruction to
\sigma
being a strict morphism of strict
L_{\infty}
algebroids.

5.
The fibrant replacement functor

In this section, we construct a faithful functor

\mathbf{Fib}\colon\mathbf{L_{\infty}Algd}\longrightarrow\mathbf{L_{\infty}Sp}

satisfying the following properties:

•

it restricts to the identity on
L_{\infty}
algebras;

•

it assigns to each dg manifold its Fedosov resolution;

•

it detects weak equivalences.

More precisely,
\mathbf{Fib}
is obtained as the composition

\mathbf{L_{\infty}Algd}\xrightarrow{\mathbf{CE}_{\mathbf{Algd}}}\mathbf{QdgM}^{\mathrm{bp}}\xrightarrow{~\mathbf{J}^{\infty}}~\mathbf{QdgM}^{\mathrm{bp}}_{\mathrm{fib}}\xrightarrow{\mathbf{CE}_{\mathbf{Sp}}^{-1}}\mathbf{L_{\infty}Sp},

where
\mathbf{J}^{\infty}
is the main functor we will construct in this section, called the
jet space functor
.

5.1.
Infinite jets and
D
-modules

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a dg manifold.

Definition 5.1
.

A
filtered graded vector bundle
on
\mathcal{M}
is a graded (left)
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}
-module
\mathcal{E}
equipped with a descending filtration

\mathcal{E}\supset\cdots\supset F^{p-1}\mathcal{E}\supset F^{p}\mathcal{E}\supset F^{p+1}\mathcal{E}\supset\cdots\supset 0,

satisfying the following conditions:

•

the filtration is exhaustive, separated, and complete, i.e.,

\bigcup_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}F^{p}\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E},\qquad\bigcap_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}F^{p}\mathcal{E}=0,\qquad\mathcal{E}\cong\varprojlim_{p}\mathcal{E}/F^{p}\mathcal{E};

•

for each
p\in\mathbb{Z}
, the graded
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}
-module

\mathrm{Gr}^{p}\mathcal{E}=F^{p}\mathcal{E}/F^{p+1}\mathcal{E}

is a graded vector bundle, i.e., locally free of total finite rank.

We recommend that our readers consult the appendices to familiarize themselves with our notions of differential operators, infinite jet bundles, and
D
-modules on graded manifolds before reading the rest of this section.

Definition 5.2
.

A
quasi-dg vector bundle
on
\mathcal{M}
is a filtered graded vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
equipped with a differential operator

D_{\mathcal{E}}\colon\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E},

of cohomological degree
1
, order
1
, and filtration degree
0
, such that

•

D_{\mathcal{E}}
is a differential, i.e.,
D_{\mathcal{E}}\circ D_{\mathcal{E}}=0
;

•

the principal symbol

\sigma_{1}^{0}(D_{\mathcal{E}})\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{M}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{E}}

of
D_{\mathcal{E}}
is given by
Q_{\mathcal{M}}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathrm{Gr}\,\mathcal{E}}
; or equivalently,

[D_{\mathcal{E}},f]s=Q_{\mathcal{M}}(f)s\mod F^{p+1}\mathcal{E}

for all
f\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}
and
s\in F^{p}\mathcal{E}
.

Such
\mathcal{E}
is called a
filtered dg vector bundle
if

[D_{\mathcal{E}},f]=Q_{\mathcal{M}}(f)

for all
f\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}
.

By a slight abuse of notation, we denote a quasi-dg vector bundle by
\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{M}
, or simply by
\mathcal{E}
when the base
\mathcal{M}
is clear from context.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a quasi-dg vector bundle. Then

\mathrm{Gr}^{\bullet}D_{\mathcal{E}}\colon\mathrm{Gr}^{\bullet}\mathcal{E}\rightarrow\mathrm{Gr}^{\bullet}\mathcal{E}

makes
\mathrm{Gr}^{\bullet}\mathcal{E}
into a dg vector bundle.

Proof.

By definition, one has
(\mathrm{Gr}^{\bullet}D_{\mathcal{E}})^{2}=0
, and

[\mathrm{Gr}^{\bullet}D_{\mathcal{E}},f]=Q_{\mathcal{M}}(f)

for all
f\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}
.
∎

Example 5.4
.

A dg vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
can be regarded as a filtered dg vector bundle with the trivial filtration

F^{p}\mathcal{E}=\begin{cases}\mathcal{E},&p\leq 0,\\
0,&p>0.\end{cases}

Example 5.5
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a quasi-dg manifold. The structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}
of
\mathcal{C}
defines a quasi-dg vector bundle over its base dg manifold
\mathrm{Gr}^{0}\mathcal{C}
.

Definition 5.6
.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{1}
and
\mathcal{E}_{2}
be quasi-dg vector bundles over a dg manifold
\mathcal{M}
. A
(base-fixing) morphism

\Phi\colon\mathcal{E}_{1}\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}_{2}

is a morphism of graded
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}
-modules which preserves the filtrations and the differentials.

Such
\Phi
is called a
weak equivalence
if
\mathrm{Gr}^{\bullet}\Phi
is a weak equivalence of dg vector bundles.

One can further introduce a category of quasi-dg vector bundles over a quasi-dg manifold. This level of generality becomes necessary when constructing fibrant replacements for representations of
L_{\infty}
algebroids. We will not pursue this direction in the present paper.

Example 5.7
.

Every base-fixing morphism of dg vector bundles is a morphism of quasi-dg vector bundles (with respect to the trivial filtration).

Example 5.8
.

Viewing the structure sheaf of a quasi-dg manifold as a quasi-dg vector bundle over its base dg manifold, every base-fixing morphism
\mathcal{C}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}_{2}
of quasi-dg manifolds yields a morphism of quasi-dg vector bundles
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}_{2}}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}_{1}}
in the opposite direction.

Let
\mathbf{QdgM}(\mathcal{M})
and
\mathbf{QdgVB}(\mathcal{M})
denote the categories of quasi-dg manifolds and quasi-dg vector bundles over
\mathcal{M}
with base-fixing morphisms, respectively. By Examples
5.5
and
5.8
, there is a faithful functor

\mathbf{QdgM}(\mathcal{M})^{op}\longrightarrow\mathbf{QdgVB}(\mathcal{M}).

Lemma 5.9
.

\mathbf{QdgM}(\mathcal{M})^{op}\rightarrow\mathbf{QdgVB}(\mathcal{M})
detects weak equivalences.

Proof.

This is tautological from the definitions.
∎

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a quasi-dg vector bundle over
\mathcal{M}
. Let
\mathcal{J}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{E}}
be the infinite jet bundle of
\mathcal{E}
(viewed as a filtered graded vector bundle). By Proposition
B.18
, the differential
D_{\mathcal{E}}
lifts to a differential
j^{\infty}(D_{\mathcal{E}})
on
\mathcal{J}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{E}}
. Moreover, since
D_{\mathcal{E}}
is of order
1
and filtration degree
0
,
j^{\infty}(D_{\mathcal{E}})
decreases the filtration on
\mathcal{J}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{E}}
by one level.

Let
\nabla^{G}
denote the Grothendieck connection on
\mathcal{J}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{E}}
. Since
\nabla^{G}
is flat, we have

[\nabla^{G}_{Q_{\mathcal{M}}},\nabla^{G}_{Q_{\mathcal{M}}}]=\nabla^{G}_{[Q_{\mathcal{M}},Q_{\mathcal{M}}]}=0.

Thus,
\nabla^{G}_{Q_{\mathcal{M}}}
is a differential on
\mathcal{J}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{E}}
, which also lowers the filtration by one level. Since
j^{\infty}(D_{\mathcal{E}})
is compatible with the left
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{M}}
-action on
\mathcal{J}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{E}}
, we have

[\nabla^{G}_{Q_{\mathcal{M}}},j^{\infty}(D_{\mathcal{E}})]=0.

Proposition 5.10
.

Equipped with the total differential

D_{\mathcal{J}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{E}}}\coloneqq\nabla^{G}_{Q_{\mathcal{M}}}+j^{\infty}(D_{\mathcal{E}}),

\mathcal{J}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{E}}
becomes a filtered dg vector bundle, called the
infinite jet bundle
of
\mathcal{E}
.

Proof.

\nabla^{G}_{Q_{\mathcal{M}}}
is a differential operator of order
1
, while
j^{\infty}(D_{\mathcal{E}})
is of order
0
. Their sum
D_{\mathcal{J}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{E}}}
is therefore a differential operator of order
1
.

We now show that
D_{\mathcal{J}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{E}}}
preserves the filtration on
\mathcal{J}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{E}}
. Since both
\nabla^{G}_{Q_{\mathcal{M}}}
and
j^{\infty}(D_{\mathcal{E}})
lower the filtration by one level, it suffices to show that the sum

\mathrm{Gr}\,\nabla^{G}_{Q_{\mathcal{M}}}+\mathrm{Gr}\,j^{\infty}(D_{\mathcal{E}})\colon\mathrm{Gr}^{\bullet}\mathcal{J}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{E}}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Gr}^{\bullet-1}\mathcal{J}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{E}}

vanishes.

Recall from Appendix
A
that

\mathrm{Gr}\,\mathcal{J}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{E}}\cong\mathrm{Sym}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{M}}^{\vee})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}\mathrm{Gr}\,\mathcal{E},

and that the associated graded operator
\mathrm{Gr}\,\mathrm{d}_{\nabla^{G}}
of the exterior covariant derivative
\mathrm{d}_{\nabla^{G}}
on

\mathrm{Sym}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{M}}[1]^{\vee})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}\mathrm{Sym}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{M}}^{\vee})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}\mathrm{Gr}\,\mathcal{E},

is given by minus the universal Koszul differential

\mathrm{Gr}\,\mathrm{d}_{\nabla^{G}}=-\delta_{K}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathrm{Gr}\mathcal{E}}.

It follows that

\mathrm{Gr}\,\nabla^{G}_{Q_{\mathcal{M}}}=-\iota_{Q_{\mathcal{M}}}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathrm{Gr}\mathcal{E}},

where
\iota_{Q_{\mathcal{M}}}\colon\mathrm{Sym}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{M}}^{\vee})\rightarrow\mathrm{Sym}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{M}}^{\vee})
denotes the contraction with
Q_{\mathcal{M}}
. On the other hand, by Proposition
B.18
,
\mathrm{Gr}\,j^{\infty}(D_{\mathcal{E}})
is given by contraction with the principal symbol
\sigma_{1}^{0}(D_{\mathcal{E}})
of
D_{\mathcal{E}}
, which precisely cancels
\mathrm{Gr}\,\nabla^{G}_{Q_{\mathcal{M}}}
.

To complete the proof, observe that

[D_{\mathcal{J}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{E}}},f]=[\nabla^{G}_{Q_{\mathcal{M}}},f]=Q_{\mathcal{M}}(f)

for all
f\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}
.
∎

Definition 5.11
.

A
(left)
D
-module
on
\mathcal{M}
is a filtered dg vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
\mathcal{M}
, together with a
D
-module structure on
\mathcal{E}
(viewed as a filtered graded vector bundle) satisfying

D_{\mathcal{E}}(D\cdot s)=L_{Q_{\mathcal{M}}}(D)\cdot s+(-1)^{|D|}D\cdot D_{\mathcal{E}}(s),

for all
D\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{M}}
and
s\in\mathcal{E}
, where
L_{Q_{\mathcal{M}}}(D)=[Q_{\mathcal{M}},D]
is the Lie derivative of
D
along
Q_{\mathcal{M}}
.

Remark 5.12
.

Equivalently, the compatibility between the left
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{M}}
-action and
D_{\mathcal{E}}
reads

[D_{\mathcal{E}},f]=L_{Q_{\mathcal{M}}}(f)

for
f\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}
, which holds automatically by the definition of a filtered dg vector bundle, together with the condition

[D_{\mathcal{E}},X\cdot]=[Q_{\mathcal{M}},X]\cdot

for
X\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{M}}
, since
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{M}}
is generated by functions and vector fields.

Recall from Appendix
B
that the de Rham complex of a
D
-module
\mathcal{E}
on
\mathcal{M}
(viewed as a graded manifold) is given by

dR(\mathcal{E})=\bigl(\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathcal{M}}\,\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}\,\mathcal{E},\;\mathrm{d}_{\nabla}\bigr),

where
\nabla
is the flat connection on
\mathcal{E}
induced by left
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{M}}
-action. Explicitly,

\nabla_{X}(s)=X\cdot s.

We now adapt this construction from the graded manifold setting to the dg manifold setting.

Definition 5.13
.

The
de Rham complex
of a
D
-module
\mathcal{E}
on
\mathcal{M}
is the following quasi-dg vector bundle on
\mathcal{M}
:

dR(\mathcal{E})\coloneqq\bigl(\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathcal{M}}\,\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}\,\mathcal{E},\;\mathrm{d}_{\nabla}+L_{D_{\mathcal{E}}}\bigr),

where
\nabla
is the flat connection on
\mathcal{E}
induced by left
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{M}}
-action, and
L_{D_{\mathcal{E}}}
is given by

L_{D_{\mathcal{E}}}(\alpha\otimes s)=L_{Q_{\mathcal{M}}}(\alpha)\otimes s+(-1)^{|\alpha|}\alpha\otimes D_{\mathcal{E}}(s).

Proposition 5.14
.

The de Rham complex
dR(\mathcal{E})
is well-defined.

Proof.

By definition,
L_{D_{\mathcal{E}}}
is well-defined and squares to zero. Moreover,

[L_{D_{\mathcal{E}}},f]s=Q_{\mathcal{M}}(f)s,\quad\mathrm{d}_{\nabla}(s)=0\mod F^{p+1}dR(\mathcal{E}),

for all
s\in F^{p}dR(\mathcal{E})
. It remains to verify that
[\mathrm{d}_{\nabla},L_{D_{\mathcal{E}}}]=0
. Since
[\mathrm{d}_{\nabla},L_{Q_{\mathcal{M}}}^{\nabla}]=0
, it suffices to show that

\nabla(D_{\mathcal{E}}(s))+L_{D_{\mathcal{E}}}\nabla(s)=0.

Let
x^{\mu}
be local coordinates on
\mathcal{M}
. We compute

\displaystyle dx^{\mu}\otimes\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}}(D_{\mathcal{E}}(s))+L_{D_{\mathcal{E}}}(dx^{\mu}\otimes\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}}(s))

\displaystyle=L_{Q_{\mathcal{M}}}(dx^{\mu})\otimes\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}}(s)-dx^{\mu}\otimes[D_{\mathcal{E}},\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}}](s)

\displaystyle=L_{Q_{\mathcal{M}}}(dx^{\mu})\otimes\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}}(s)-dx^{\mu}\otimes\nabla_{[Q_{\mathcal{M}},\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}]}(s)

\displaystyle=0,

which completes the proof.
∎

One readily sees that the infinite jet bundle
\mathcal{J}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{E}}
of a quasi-dg vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
carries a compatible
D
-module structure. More precisely, we have

[D_{\mathcal{J}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{E}}},X\cdot]=[D_{\mathcal{J}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{E}}},\nabla^{G}_{X}]=[\nabla^{G}_{Q_{\mathcal{M}}},\nabla^{G}_{X}]=\nabla^{G}_{[Q_{\mathcal{M}},X]}=[Q_{\mathcal{M}},X]\cdot,

where we use Proposition
B.18
and the flatness of
\nabla^{G}
. For simplicity, we denote by

Appendix A

Let
\mathcal{M}=(M,\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}})
be a graded manifold. In this appendix, we collect and prove some basic facts about differential operators and jets on
\mathcal{M}
.

Definition A.1
.

The
sheaf of differential operators

\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{M}}
on
\mathcal{M}
is defined by assigning to each open set
U\subset M
the subalgebra of
\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}(U))
generated by left multiplications by
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}(U)
and by vector fields
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{M}}(U)
.

\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{M}}
is naturally a graded
(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}})
-algebra. It carries a compatible descending filtration

\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{M}}\supset\cdots\supset F^{-1}\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{M}}\supset F^{0}\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{M}}\supset F^{1}\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{M}}=0,

given by
F^{0}\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{M}}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}
, and inductively,

F^{-k}\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{M}}=\left\{D\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{M}}\,\middle|\,[D,f]\in F^{-k+1}\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{M}}\text{ for all }f\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}\right\}

for
k>0
, where
[D,f]=D\circ f-(-1)^{|f||D|}f\circ D
. In other words,
F^{-k}\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{M}}
consists of differential operators
of order at most
k
. There is a canonical isomorphism of graded
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}
-modules

\mathrm{Gr}^{-k}\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{M}}\cong\mathrm{Sym}^{k}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{M}}).

This identification induces a map

\sigma_{k}\colon F^{-k}\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{M}}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Sym}^{k}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{M}}),

called the
symbol map
. For
D\in F^{-k}\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{M}}
, if
\sigma_{k}(D)\neq 0
, then
D
is said to be
of order
k
. The element
\sigma_{k}(D)\in\mathrm{Sym}^{k}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{M}}
is called the
principal symbol
of
D
.

\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{M}}
is also a graded cocommutative
(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}})
-coalgebra. The counit map
\epsilon\colon\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{M}}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}
is defined by

\epsilon(D)\coloneqq D(1),

\Delta(D)(f\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}g)\coloneqq D(fg)

where we identify
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{M}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{M}}
with the set of bi-differential operators. Equivalently,

is characterized by the following formulas
[
xu2001quantum
]

(A.1)

\displaystyle\Delta(1)=1\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}1,

(A.2)

\displaystyle\Delta(X)=1\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}X+X\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}1,\quad\text{for~all~}X\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{M}},

\displaystyle\Delta(D_{1}\circ D_{2})=\Delta(D_{1})\circ\Delta(D_{2}),\quad\text{for~all~}D_{1},D_{2}\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{M}}.

\displaystyle(\nabla^{G}_{X}J)(D)=X(J(D))-(-1)^{|X||J|}J(X\circ D).

For any function
f
on
\mathcal{M}
, we have

\displaystyle\nabla^{G}_{X}(j^{\infty}(f))(D)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|X||f|}\bigl(X(D(f))-(\nabla^{G}_{X}D)(f)\bigr)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|X||f|}\bigl(X(D(f))-(X\circ D)(f)\bigr)

\displaystyle=0.

We use this observation to determine a local formula for
\nabla^{G}
. Indeed,

\nabla^{G}\bigl(j^{\infty}(x^{\mu})\bigr)=\nabla^{G}(x^{\mu}+y^{\mu})=dx^{\mu}+\nabla^{G}(y^{\mu})=0.

Since
\nabla^{G}
is a derivation, it follows that it must take the local form

(B.1)

\nabla^{G}=dx^{\mu}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}-dx^{\mu}\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{\mu}}.

Lemma B.11
.

The associated
\mathbb{N}
-graded operator of the differential
\mathrm{d}_{\nabla^{G}}
on
dR(\mathcal{J}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{M}})
is minus the
universal Koszul differential

\deltaK\colon\Omega^{k}_{\mathcal{M}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}\mathrm{Sym}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{M}}^{\vee})\longrightarrow\Omega^{k+1}_{\mathcal{M}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}\mathrm{Sym}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}^{l-1}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{M}}^{\vee}).

Proof.

The associated
\mathbb{N}
-graded algebra of
dR(\mathcal{J}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{M}})
is given by the universal Koszul algebra of the graded
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}
-module
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{M}}
:

\mathrm{Gr}\,dR(\mathcal{J}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{M}})\cong\Omega_{\mathcal{M}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}\mathrm{Sym}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{M}}^{\vee}).

Note that
dx^{\mu}\wedge
increases the filtration degree by
1
, while
\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{\mu}}
decreases it by
1
. Using (
B.1
), we obtain the local formula

-\mathrm{Gr}\,\mathrm{d}_{\nabla^{G}}=dx^{\mu}\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{\mu}},

which is independent of local coordinates and is exactly the universal Koszul differential.
∎

Using (
A.2
) and (
A.3
), one can easily check that
\nabla^{G}_{X}
is a derivation of
\mathcal{J}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{M}}
. It follows that
\mathrm{d}_{\nabla^{G}}
, as well as any covariant Lie derivative on
dR(\mathcal{J}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{M}})
, is also a derivation. Consequently,
dR(\mathcal{J}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{M}})
is a filtered cdga over
(\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathcal{M}},\mathrm{d}_{dR})
.

Proposition B.12
.

The infinite jet prolongation

j^{\infty}\colon\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}\longrightarrow dR(\mathcal{J}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{M}})

is a filtered quasi-isomorphism of cdgas. More precisely, a stronger statement holds: on the
E_{0}
pages of the associated spectral sequences,

\mathrm{Gr}\,j^{\infty}\colon\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Gr}\,dR(\mathcal{J}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{M}})

is already a quasi-isomorphism of cdgas.

Proof.

It is straightforward to verify that
\mathrm{Gr}\,j^{\infty}
is given by the canonical inclusion

\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}\hookrightarrow\Omega_{\mathcal{M}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}\mathrm{Sym}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{M}}^{\vee}).

The statement then follows from the fact that the cohomology of the universal Koszul algebra of a finitely generated projective
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}
-module is the base
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}
.
∎

Proposition B.13
.

Every differential operator
D
of order
k
on
\mathcal{M}
uniquely lifts to a morphism of
D
-modules

j^{\infty}(D)\colon F^{\bullet}\mathcal{J}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{M}}\longrightarrow F^{\bullet-k}\mathcal{J}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{M}},

whose associated
\mathbb{N}
-graded operator

\mathrm{Gr}\,j^{\infty}(D)\colon\mathrm{Sym}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{M}}^{\vee})\longrightarrow\mathrm{Sym}^{\bullet-k}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{M}}^{\vee})

is given by contraction with the principal symbol
\sigma_{k}(D)\in\mathrm{Sym}^{k}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{M}})
of
D
.

Moreover, the following diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{J}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{M}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{J}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{M}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j^{\infty}(D)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j^{\infty}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j^{\infty}}

commutes.

Proof.

Since the image
j^{\infty}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}})
generates
\mathcal{J}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{M}}
, we define
j^{\infty}(D)
simply by setting

j^{\infty}(D)\bigl(j^{\infty}(f)\bigr)\coloneqq j^{\infty}\bigl(D(f)\bigr).

We need to verify that this is well-defined, i.e.,

\sum_{i\in I}g_{i}\,j^{\infty}\bigl(D(f_{i})\bigr)=0\quad\text{whenever}\quad\sum_{i\in I}g_{i}\,j^{\infty}(f_{i})=0,

where
I
is some finite index set.

Recall that
j^{\infty}(f_{i})=\sum_{\alpha}\frac{1}{\alpha!}\,\frac{\partial^{\alpha}}{\partial x^{\alpha}}(f_{i})\,y^{\alpha}
. It follows that

\sum_{i\in I}g_{i}\,\frac{\partial^{\alpha}}{\partial x^{\alpha}}(f_{i})=0

for all multi-indices
\alpha
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}g_{i}\,j^{\infty}\bigl(D(f_{i})\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha}\frac{y^{\alpha}}{\alpha!}\sum_{i\in I}g_{i}\,\frac{\partial^{\alpha}}{\partial x^{\alpha}}\bigl(D(f_{i})\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\beta|\leq k}\sum_{\alpha}\frac{y^{\alpha}}{\alpha!}\sum_{i\in I}g_{i}\,\frac{\partial^{\alpha}}{\partial x^{\alpha}}\bigl(D_{\beta}\partial_{\beta}(f_{i})\bigr)

\displaystyle=0,

where
D_{\beta}
is some local function. In local coordinates,
j^{\infty}(D)
is given by

j^{\infty}(D)=\sum_{|\beta|\leq k}\sum_{\alpha}\frac{\partial^{\alpha}D_{\beta}}{\partial x^{\alpha}}\frac{y^{\alpha}}{\alpha!}\frac{\partial^{\beta}}{\partial y^{\beta}}.

It follows that
j^{\infty}(D)
lowers the filtration degree by
k
and

\mathrm{Gr}\,j^{\infty}(D)=\sum_{|\beta|=k}D_{\beta}\frac{\partial^{\beta}}{\partial y^{\beta}},

which is exactly contraction with
\sigma_{k}(D)
.

To see that
j^{\infty}(D)
is a morphism of
D
-modules, we compute

j^{\infty}(D)(\nabla^{G}_{X}(gj^{\infty}(f)))=j^{\infty}(D)(X(g)j^{\infty}(f))=\pm X(g)j^{\infty}(D(f))=\pm\nabla^{G}_{X}(gj^{\infty}(D(f))),

which completes the proof.
∎

Corollary B.14
.

Let
X
be a vector field on
\mathcal{M}
, viewed as a differential operator. Then

L^{v}_{X}\coloneqq 1\otimes j^{\infty}(X)

is a derivation of
dR(\mathcal{J}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{M}})
, called the
vertical Lie derivative
on
dR(\mathcal{J}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{M}})
.

We refer to the covariant Lie derivative
L^{\nabla^{G}}_{X}
on
dR(\mathcal{J}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{M}})
as the
horizontal Lie derivative
and denote it by
L^{h}_{X}
. The sum

L_{X}\coloneqq L^{h}_{X}+L^{v}_{X},

is called the
(total) Lie derivative
on
dR(\mathcal{J}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{M}})
. Note that both
L^{v}_{X}
and
L^{h}_{X}
, and hence also
L_{X}
, commute with
\mathrm{d}_{\nabla^{G}}
.

Remark B.15
.

The
Lie derivative

L_{X}
on
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{M}}
along
X
is defined by

L_{X}D\coloneqq[X,D].

L_{X}
is a coderivation of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{M}}
. It extends to
dR(\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{M}})=\Omega_{\mathcal{M}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{M}}
via the Leibniz rule:

L_{X}(\alpha\otimes D)=L_{X}\alpha\otimes D+(-1)^{|X||\alpha|}\alpha\otimes L_{X}D.

We leave it to the reader as an exercise to verify that the Lie derivative
L_{X}
on
dR(\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{M}})
is dual to the Lie derivative
L_{X}
on
dR(\mathcal{J}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{M}})
under the identification

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}(\Omega_{\mathcal{M}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{M}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}})\cong\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathcal{M}}\,\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}\,\mathcal{J}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{M}}.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a filtered graded vector bundle on
\mathcal{M}
.

.

The infinite jet bundle
\mathcal{J}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{E}}=\mathcal{J}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{M}}\,\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}\,\mathcal{E}
of

carries a canonical flat connection

is called the

of
\mathcal{J}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{E}}
.

We need to verify that (
B.2
) is well-defined. We have

\nabla^{G}_{X}(J\otimes fs)=\nabla^{G}_{X}(J)\otimes fs=(\nabla^{G}_{X}(J)j^{\infty}(f))\otimes s

for all functions
f
. Using Lemma
A.8
, we have

\nabla^{G}_{X}(J)j^{\infty}(f)=\nabla^{G}_{X}(Jf).

Thus,
\nabla^{G}_{X}(J\otimes fs)=\nabla^{G}_{X}(Jf\otimes s)
.

.

The infinite jet prolongation

is a filtered quasi-isomorphism. That is, on the

pages of the associated spectral sequences,

\mathrm{Gr}\,j^{\infty}\colon\mathrm{Gr}\,\mathcal{E}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Gr}\,dR(\mathcal{J}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{E}})

is a quasi-isomorphism.

.
