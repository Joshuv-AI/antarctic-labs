ABSTRACT
In order to perform post mortem analysis on materials launched in gas guns and powder guns,
various methods have been explored to soft catch these materials. The majority of the deformation
profile must be from the initial impact during the experiment, not from the subsequent impact
with the energy absorption material in the catch tank. This process becomes increasingly more
difficult with increased velocities. This report explores a method to soft catch materials launched
at velocities 3000 𝑚𝑠 .

1. INTRODUCTION

In the fields of shock physics, terminal ballistics, and metalography it is of paramount importance
to capture the sample of interest softly and slowly after impact. This can be quite difficult at high
speeds with weak materials. Several methods have been explored over the years including using
sand, clay, ballistic gels, water, corn, and several types of foam. Shock physicists have explored
methods of designing and manufacturing high tolerance (+ − 1𝜇𝑚) momentum trapping rings, and
spallation preventing plates. All of this effort has been expended in search of the best way to keep
the sample intact after the initial impact of interest occurs, so one can perform vital postmortem
analysis upon the material to derive failure mechanisms and material parameters. This report is a
brief recap of a attempt to catch a projectile launched from a two-stage gas gun. To the author’s
limited knowledge, there currently is no successful "soft-catching" experiment at these velocities.

2. FLYING PIG PROJECT

A symmetric impact shot of a 5 mm thick 22 mm diameter steel flyer hitting a 2.5 mm thick 22 mm
diameter steel target at 2.8 km/s was conducted in the STAR 2 stage gas gun. The steel flyer was
mounted to the front of a Lexan sabot resulting in a total projectile weight of 36.18 grams.
In an attempt to recover the steel target and projectile for postmortem analysis, a soft-catch system
was built. It consisted of a hollow cylinder of aluminum with a internal diameter of 13" and an
external diameter of 15". Plates of various materials were cut via a band saw to slide into the
hollow cylinder, a conical nose piece was bolted to the front, and a solid plate of 2" thick aluminum
was attached to the rear. The materials inside the cylinder were arranged in increasing impedance
with a few exceptions to distribute the momentum transfer across a large surface area. Table 2-1
describes the order of the materials and the thickness of each layer.
These types of cylinders filled with energy absorption materials are often referred to as "pigs" due
to their shape and weight. The steel projectile and target penetrated through the Ballistic gel, TPX,
Aluminum Honeycomb, TPX, Aluminum Honeycomb, Aluminum Plate, and embedded themselves
into the Aluminum Honeycomb just before the carbon foam. Each of the Aluminum Honeycombs
were fully compressed to the point where no space was between the aluminum struts. The pig itself
was launched from it’s initial point of origin, a few inches behind the target, to about 9 ft behind
the target in the catch tank.

3. OBR

The two-stage gas gun at the STAR facility at Sandia National Laboratories utilizes an Optical
Barrel Probe (OBR). Three laser send/receive probes are fashioned to the side of the barrel extension
shining perpendicular to the flight path of the projectile. As the projectile passes the laser probe,
light is reflected off the side of the projectile, back into the probe, onto the optical sensor, generating
an electrical pulse, which is recorded on an oscilloscope. The data is then plotted in Matlab and
the flyer velocity is calculated using the code written at the end of chapter 3.

5. FUTURE CONCEPTS

The author proposes a method of soft catching materials launched at high velocities by having a
two-stage gas gun with a target tank under vacuum, but no catch tank. Instead of a catch tank,
the gun would be pointed straight up into the atmosphere, normal to the surface of the earth. The
projectile would be slowed down by the drag cause by the friction force of the air, as well as the force
of gravity. Sufficient area surrounding the gun facility would be required to capture the target upon
it’s return to the surface of the earth. A salt flat or large body of water may suffice. An on board
system such as a parachute with a location beacon would be ideal, however such a device would
have to survive the g-forces of the initial launch and subsequent impact during the experiment.

 34

6. CONCLUSION

The target and flyer material were recovered from the energy absorption material. Unfortunately,
the resulting deformation of the samples impacting the energy absorption material was too great
to be able to perform useful post-mortem analysis upon the samples. One key take way from
this experiment is the projectile and target did not penetrate the entirety of the energy absorption
material, therefore, lower impedance material could be used. This may result in a "softer" catch
and less deformation upon the sample from the subsequent impact.
