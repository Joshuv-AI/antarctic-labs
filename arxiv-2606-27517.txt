Korovkin type theorems for operators acting on functions of polynomial and exponential growth on [ 0 , ∞ ) [0,\infty) Journal: …
Ulrich Abel, Ana Maria Acu, Margareta Heilmann, Ioan Raşa

Abstract
We prove two Korovkin-type approximation theorems for sequences of positive linear operators acting on continuous functions on [ 0 , ∞ ) [0,\infty) . Under the assumption of pointwise convergence on suitable test functions, we establish pointwise convergence for all functions with polynomial or exponential growth. As direct applications, we obtain convergence results for the classical Baskakov and Szász–Mirakjan operators. The proposed method offers an elementary framework that can be applied to a broad class of positive linear operators.

1
Introduction

Let
L_{n}:D\to C[0,\infty)
be a sequence of positive linear operators, where
D
is a linear subspace of
C[0,\infty)
containing the space
\Pi
of all polynomial functions. For example, if
V_{n}
is the sequence of Baskakov operators, then
D
can be taken as the subspace of
C[0,\infty)
consisting of all functions with at most polynomial growth. In the case of Szász–Mirakjan operators
S_{n}
, polynomial growth should be replaced by exponential growth.

For operators
L_{n}
as above, many results are known, guaranteeing a certain type of convergence of
L_{n}f
toward
f
, for
f
in suitably restricted subspaces of
D
. In this paper we give sufficient conditions entailing the pointwise convergence of
L_{n}f
toward
f
, for
all
functions
f\in D
.

2
Convergence for functions with polynomial growth

Theorem 2.1

Let
D
be a linear subspace of
C[0,\infty)
,
\Pi\subset D
,
L_{n}:D\to C[0,\infty)
positive linear operators,
L_{n}p(x)\to p(x)
, for all
p\in\Pi
,
x\geq 0
. Let
f\in D
such that there exists
p\in\Pi
with
|f(x)|\leq p(x)
,
x\geq 0
. Then
L_{n}f(a)\to f(a)
,
a\geq 0
.

For the proof we need the following lemma.

Lemma 2.2

Let
f\in C[0,\infty)
,
p\in\Pi
,
p(x)\geq f(x)
,
x\in[0,\infty)
.
Let
a\geq 0
,
\varepsilon>0
. Then, there exists
P\in\Pi
:

P(a)=f(a)+\varepsilon,\quad P(x)\geq f(x),\quad x\geq 0.

(2.1)

Proof
Let
a>0
. Then, there exists
0<\ell\leq\dfrac{a}{2}
, such that

f(a)+\varepsilon\geq f(x),\,\,x\in(a-2\ell,a+2\ell).

(2.2)

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
2m>\deg p
. Then, there exists
M>0
with

\left(\frac{x-a}{\ell}\right)^{2m}+f(a)+\varepsilon\geq p(x),\quad x>M,

which implies that

\left(\frac{x-a}{\ell}\right)^{2m}+f(a)+\varepsilon\geq f(x),\quad x>M.

(2.3)

Set
U:=[0,a-2\ell]\cup[a+2\ell,M]
. Take
K\geq 1
with

\min\left\{K\left(\frac{x-a}{\ell}\right)^{2m}+f(a)+\varepsilon\mid x\in U\right\}\geq\max\left\{p(x)\mid x\in U\right\}.

(2.4)

Define

P(x):=K\left(\frac{x-a}{\ell}\right)^{2m}+f(a)+\varepsilon.

Using (
2.2
)–(
2.4
) we see that
P
satisfies (
2.1
). The proof is similar for
a=0
.

Proof of Theorem
2.1

Let
a\geq 0
,
\varepsilon>0
. Lemma
2.2
implies the existence of
P,Q\in\Pi
, such that

Q(x)\leq f(x)\leq P(x),\quad x\geq 0;\quad Q(a)=f(a)-\varepsilon,\ \ P(a)=f(a)+\varepsilon.

Now

L_{n}Q(a)\leq L_{n}f(a)\leq L_{n}P(a);\quad f(a)-\varepsilon=Q(a)=\lim_{n\to\infty}L_{n}Q(a)

\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}L_{n}f(a)\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}L_{n}f(a)\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}L_{n}P(a)=P(a)=f(a)+\varepsilon.

Therefore, for each
\varepsilon>0
we have

f(a)-\varepsilon\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}L_{n}f(a)\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}L_{n}f(a)\leq f(a)+\varepsilon.

It follows that
L_{n}f(a)\to f(a)
as
n\to\infty
.

Corollary 2.3

Let
V_{n}
be the classical Baskakov operators and
S_{n}
the Szász-Mirakjan operators. Let
f\in C[0,\infty)
such that there exists
p\in\Pi
with
|f(x)|\leq p(x)
,
x\geq 0
.
Then,
V_{n}f\to f
and
S_{n}f\to f
, pointwise on
[0,\infty)
.

Proof
The Baskakov and the Szász–Mirakjan operators are exponential-type operators (see
[
1
, Theorem 3.3]
). From
[
1
, Proposition 2.9]
it follows that
V_{n}p\to p
and
S_{n}p\to p
pointwise on
[0,\infty)
, for all
p\in\Pi
. An application of Theorem
2.1
concludes the proof.

3
Convergence for functions with exponential growth

Let
\exp_{\lambda}(x)=e^{\lambda x}
,
\lambda,x\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Consider also the function
\alpha(x)=\cosh(x)-1
,
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

Theorem 3.1

Let
D
be a linear subspace of
C[0,\infty)
such that
\exp_{\lambda}\in D
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.
Let
L_{n}:D\to C[0,\infty)
be positive linear operators,
L_{n}\exp_{\lambda}\to\exp_{\lambda}
pointwise on
[0,\infty)
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.
Let
f\in C[0,\infty)
,
|f(x)|\leq A\exp_{\omega}(x),\,x\geq 0
for some
A>0
and
\omega>0
.
Then
L_{n}f\to f
pointwise on
[0,\infty)
.

For the proof we need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.2

Let
A>0
,
\omega>0
,
f\in C[0,\infty)
, such that

A\exp_{\omega}(x)\geq f(x),\quad x\geq 0.

Fix
a\geq 0
,
\varepsilon>0
. Then there exist
\ell>0
,
K\in\mathbb{N}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that

K\,\alpha^{m}\!\left(\frac{x-a}{\ell}\right)+f(a)+\varepsilon\geq f(x),\quad x\geq 0.

(3.1)

Proof
Let
a>0
. There exists
0<\ell\leq\dfrac{a}{2}
such that

f(a)+\varepsilon\geq f(x),\quad x\in(a-2\ell,a+2\ell).

(3.2)

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
m>\omega\ell
. Then

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\alpha^{m}\!\left(\frac{x-a}{\ell}\right)+f(a)+\varepsilon}{A\exp_{\omega}(x)}=\infty.

So, there exists
M>0
such that

\alpha^{m}\!\left(\frac{x-a}{\ell}\right)+f(a)+\varepsilon\geq f(x),\quad x>M.

(3.3)

Set

U:=[0,a-2\ell]\cup[a+2\ell,M].

Take
K\in\mathbb{N}
with

\min\left\{K\,\alpha^{m}\!\left(\frac{x-a}{\ell}\right)+f(a)+\varepsilon\;\middle|\;x\in U\right\}\geq\max\left\{A\exp_{\omega}(x)\;\middle|\;x\in U\right\}.

(3.4)

Then (
3.1
) is a consequence of (
3.2
)–(
3.4
). The proof is similar for
a=0
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1

Fix
a\geq 0
and
\varepsilon>0
.

Since

f(x)\leq A\exp_{\omega}(x),\qquad x\geq 0,

Lemma 3.2 yields a function

G(x)=K\alpha^{m}\!\left(\frac{x-a}{\ell}\right)+f(a)+\varepsilon

such that
G(x)\geq f(x)
for all
x\geq 0
.

Since
\alpha(x)=\cosh(x)-1
, the function
G
is a finite linear combination of exponentials, hence
G\in D
. By positivity,

L_{n}f(a)\leq L_{n}G(a).

Using the assumption
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}L_{n}\exp_{\lambda}(a)=\exp_{\lambda}(a)
for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}L_{n}G(a)=G(a)=f(a)+\varepsilon,

and therefore

\limsup_{n\to\infty}L_{n}f(a)\leq f(a)+\varepsilon.

Applying Lemma 3.2 to
-f
, we similarly obtain

\liminf_{n\to\infty}L_{n}f(a)\geq f(a)-\varepsilon.

Hence

f(a)-\varepsilon\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}L_{n}f(a)\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}L_{n}f(a)\leq f(a)+\varepsilon.

Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}L_{n}f(a)=f(a).

Since
a\geq 0
was arbitrary, the convergence is pointwise on
[0,\infty)
.

Corollary 3.3

Let
f\in C[0,\infty)
,
|f(x)|\leq A\exp_{\omega}(x)
,
x\geq 0
, for some
A>0
and
\omega>0
. Then
S_{n}f\to f
pointwise on
[0,\infty)
.

Proof
For all
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
x\geq 0
, we have

S_{n}\exp_{\lambda}(x)=e^{nx(e^{\lambda/n}-1)}\to\exp_{\lambda}(x)\quad(n\to\infty).

An application of Theorem
3.1
concludes the proof.
