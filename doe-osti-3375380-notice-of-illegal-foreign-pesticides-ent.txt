I. Scope Note
 This information bulletin is intended to generate a heightened awareness of illicit pesticide
 importation, distribution, and use in the United States. This bulletin focuses on solid, fumigant
 materials which are primarily used in the production of marijuana growth and cultivation.
 Exposure to illicit pesticides, fumigant fumes, or residual materials may pose a public health
 and officer safety concern. Many illegal fumigant pesticide products have been seized and
 determined to consist of a mixture of several pesticides and is often in contrast with the
 pesticide labelling.

 This information bulletin is not a complete account of all illegal pesticides found in country; it
 serves to highlight frequently encountered materials and present patterns in product
 presentation, packaging, and shipment containment which are intended to aid in identification
 and intervention. Other varieties of illegal pesticide fumigants are known to exist, and
 additional variations are expected to be encountered in policing intervention. This bulletin
 includes information on the presentation of illegal pesticides, label review recommendations, a
 field reference guide, which includes example photographs of seized pesticide/fumigants and
 their labels, and related United States federal regulation information, including relevant tariff
 code information for reference.

II. Illegal Foreign Pesticides Background
 Illegal foreign pesticides are being smuggled into the United States (U.S.) and being
 redistributed across the country for agricultural application including use at licensed and
 unlicensed cannabis cultivation sites. The use case of these illegal pesticide materials has
 strong ties to transnational criminal organizations (TCOs) who utilize these illegal foreign
 pesticide materials in the process of cultivating marijuana within the United States 1. The
 interdiction of these materials has the ability to (a) remove illegal toxic chemicals from public
 distribution, (b) disrupt the illegal cannabis cultivation market, and (c) cutoff an illegal revenue
 stream (the sale and distribution of illegal foreign pesticides) which is currently benefitting
 TCOs.

 The illegal importation and distribution of foreign-manufactured pesticides circumvent federal
 regulatory oversight and constitutes a transnational criminal threat. Furthermore, these illegally
 imported pesticide materials are being domestically distributed through organized domestic
 distribution networks. These illegal foreign pesticides often contain compounds which are not
 approved for use in the United States, posing harm to the health and safety of the public due to
 adverse health and environmental effects 2. The following pesticides are not registered for
 approved for use in the United States, but have been found by law enforcement in the country:

III. Pesticide Importation per FIFRA
 The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) regulates the production, distribution
 (including importation) and use of pesticides and pesticide devices in the United States under
 the Federal Insecticide, Fungicide, and Rodenticide Act (FIFRA). All imported pesticides
 intended for use in the United States must be registered, as required by Section 3 of FIFRA,
 before being permitted entry into the United States 4. Section 17 of FIFRA and the U.S. Customs
 and Border Protection (CBP)-promulgated regulations set forth at 19 C.F.R. §§ 12.110-12.117
 require that any importer of a “pesticide” or “device” subject to FIFRA must file an EPA Notice of
 Arrival (NOA) of Pesticides and Devices 4,5. Importers are responsible for determining whether
 their goods are “pesticides” or “devices” subject to these import filing requirements.

 Section 2 of FIFRA defines “device”, “pest,” and “pesticide” (see FIFRA sections 2(h), 2(t), and
 2(u)). The Agency issued regulations to help determine what products or substances meet
 those definitions, and whether a given pesticide is regulated by FIFRA. 40 C.F.R. Part 152,
 Subparts A and B. FIFRA section 25(b) authorizes the EPA to exempt from FIFRA requirements
 pesticides that it determines are adequately regulated by another federal agency, and
 pesticides that have been determined to be of a character which is unnecessary to be subject
 to FIFRA 6. For the specific conditions that must be met for a product to meet an exemption, see
     40 C.F.R. §§ 152.20 and 152.25.

 If the importer or their authorized agent is unsure whether they are required to file an EPA NOA
 after reading the information provided, they should contact the appropriate EPA regional
 pesticide imports contact for the state/territory for the port of entry. The EPA regional pesticide
 import contacts can be found at https://www.epa.gov/compliance/regional-contacts-
 questions-concerning-notices-arrival-epa-form-3540-1

IV. Pesticide Labeling Requirements per Code of Federal Regulations
 This section contains a summarized accounting of the Code of Federal Regulations 40 C.F.R. §§
 156.10. For a full accounting of the regulation and the specific verbiage please review the Code
 of Federal Regulations (eCFR :: 40 CFR 156.10 -- Labeling requirements) 7.

 The Code of Federal Regulations states that every pesticide product shall bear a label
 containing the information specified by the Act and the associated regulations. The contents of
 a label must show clearly and prominently the following:

 Contents of the Label

 (a)(1)(i) The name, brand, or trademark under which the product is sold; this branding
 information must be associated with a registered Administrator or supplemental
 registration (recorded distributor);
 (a)(1)(ii) The name and address of the producer, registrant, or person for whom the pesticide
 was produced;
 (a)(1)(iii) The net contents;
 (a)(1)(iv) The product registration number of the pesticide;
 (a)(1)(v) The producing establishment’s registration number;
 (a)(1)(vi) An ingredient statement to include:

 and the inert ingredients by the term “inert ingredients,”
 (g)(1) A statement which contains the name and percentage by weight of each
 active ingredient, the total percentage by weight of all inert ingredients.
 (g)(4) The total percentage of active and inert ingredients must sum to 100.

 (a)(1)(vii) Hazard and precautionary statements for human and domestic animal hazards and

 Prominence and legibility

 (a)(2)(i) All words, statements, graphic representations, designs or other information required
 on the labeling must be clearly legible to a person with normal vision (ex. Text at 6-
 point or larger type, on a clear contrasting background, not obscured or crowded.)

 Language to be used

 (a)(3) All required label or labeling text shall appear in the English language. When
 additional text in another language is necessary, all labeling requirements will be
 applied equally to both the English and other-language versions of the labeling.

V. Harmonized Tariff System (HTS)
 The contents of these illegal foreign pesticides are categorized under the HTS under several
 codes depending on the contents of the packet; see Harmonized Tariff Schedule of the United
 States Revision 13 (2025) Chapter 29 and Chapter 38 8,9.

VI. Examples of Illegal Foreign Pesticides
 Illegal foreign pesticides have most commonly been encountered as solid materials. These
 products appear as both commercially packaged bags and labeled mylar packages.

 illegal pesticides are being brought into the country without any indicating labels (Figure 2). The
 interior contents of the package may resemble sawdust or fertilizer packaged into small
 cellophane bags (Figure 2).
