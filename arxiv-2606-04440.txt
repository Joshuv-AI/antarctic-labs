This document provides in-depth details for the derivation of the univariate splitting

1

L^{2}

This text presents asymptotic analyses of the proposed

h

[

[

. For this

“B”, as it would be in Appendix A or B,
for example,
Eq.

A.1
for the very first equation below. On the contrary, all

[

, e.g.,

2.1
Odd case

In our splitting method, the mixand weights
\bm{w}
are found by solving the linear system of
equations
\bm{A}\bm{w}=\bm{b}
, where the elements of the matrix
\bm{A}
and the rhs vector

\bm{b}
are given by
and
. At
h=0
, all functions
d_{\alpha}
,

c_{\alpha,\beta}
in
become identical, and therefore, the elements
a_{m,k}
,

b_{m}
in
turn to zero, rendering the weights undefined.

To obtain the solution for small but finite
h
,
we decompose
\bm{A}
and
\bm{b}
into Maclaurin series over
h_{*}\vcentcolon=h/\sigma
.
The derivatives of
d_{\alpha}
and

c_{\alpha,\beta}
from
with respect to
h_{*}
at
h_{*}=0
are

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2n}d_{\alpha}}{\partial h_{*}^{2n}}\Bigr|_{h_{*}=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{n}(2n)!\alpha^{2n}\sigma^{2n}}{n!\,(2(1+\sigma^{2}))^{n+1/2}\sqrt{\pi}}\text{,}

(A.1)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2n}c_{\alpha,\beta}}{\partial h_{*}^{2n}}\Bigr|_{h_{*}=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{n}(2n)!(\alpha-\beta)^{2n}}{n!\,2^{2n+1}\sigma\sqrt{\pi}}\text{.}

(A.2)

By symmetry, all odd derivatives at
h_{*}=0
are zero. Substituting
Eqs.

A.1
and
A.2
into

, we obtain for the derivatives of
b_{m}
and
a_{m,k}
:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2n)!}\frac{\partial^{2n}b_{m}}{\partial h_{*}^{2n}}\Bigr|_{h_{*}=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!\,2^{2n}\sigma\sqrt{\pi}}\left(\frac{2^{n+1/2}\sigma^{2n+1}}{(1+\sigma^{2})^{n+1/2}}-1\right)m^{2n}\text{,}

(A.3)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2n)!}\frac{\partial^{2n}a_{m,k}}{\partial h_{*}^{2n}}\Bigr|_{h_{*}=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{n}\left[(k-m)^{2n}+(k+m)^{2n}-2k^{2n}-2m^{2n}\right]}{n!\,2^{2n}\sigma\sqrt{\pi}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!\,2^{2n-1}\sigma\sqrt{\pi}}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\binom{2n}{2j}k^{2j}m^{2(n-j)}\text{.}

(A.4)

The second order derivative of
a_{m,k}
(
n=1
in
Eq.

A.4
) is zero. We moved
the factors of
(2n)!
to the lhs because this is how they would appear in the Maclaurin series.

Using
Eq.

A.3
, we write the decomposition of the rhs vector
\bm{b}
in compact vector
form as (reproducing
here)

\bm{b}=\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\beta_{2n}\bm{p}_{2n}h_{*}^{2n}\text{,}

(A.5)

where

\bm{b}_{2n}=\beta_{2n}\bm{p}_{2n}\text{, $\forall n\geq 1$,}

(A.6)

\beta_{2n}=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!\,2^{2n}\sigma\sqrt{\pi}}\left(\frac{2^{n+1/2}\sigma^{2n+1}}{(1+\sigma^{2})^{n+1/2}}-1\right)\!\text{,}

(A.7)

and

\bm{p}_{2n}=\bigl[1,2^{2n},\dots,M^{2n}\bigr]\text{.}

(A.8)

The vectors
\bm{p}_{2n}
for
n\leq M
are independent, although not mutually orthogonal.

Similarly, using
Eq.

A.4
we decompose the matrix
\bm{A}
as (repeating

)

\bm{A}=\sum_{n=2}^{+\infty}\bm{A}_{2n}h_{*}^{2n}\text{,}

(A.9)

where

\bm{A}_{2n}=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\alpha^{(2n)}_{2j,2(n-j)}\left(\bm{p}_{2j}\otimes\bm{p}_{2(n-j)}\right)\text{, $\forall n\geq 2$,}

(A.10)

the symbol
\otimes
denotes the outer product of vectors, and

\alpha^{(2n)}_{2j,2(n-j)}=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!\,2^{2n-1}\sigma\sqrt{\pi}}\binom{2n}{2j}\text{.}

(A.11)

The second of the lower indices in
\alpha^{(2n)}_{2j,2(n-j)}
is technically unnecessary, as it
is fully defined by the first lower and the upper indices. However, it makes for convenient notation
relating the indices of
\alpha
with the indices of
\bm{p}
in the outer product, for example,

\bm{A}_{8}=\alpha^{(8)}_{2,6}\left(\bm{p}_{2}\otimes\bm{p}_{6}\right)+\alpha^{(8)}_{4,4}\left(\bm{p}_{4}\otimes\bm{p}_{4}\right)+\alpha^{(8)}_{6,2}\left(\bm{p}_{6}\otimes\bm{p}_{2}\right)\text{.}

When
h_{*}\to 0
,
the matrix
\bm{A}
is decreasing faster than the rhs

\bm{b}
, and therefore, the solution
\bm{w}
must grow on the order of at least
h_{*}^{-2}
. The
sum of these increasing weights equals to one, and therefore, some of them must be negative. The
weights
\bm{w}
may grow faster than
h_{*}^{-2}
if such rapidly growing terms are orthogonal to

\bm{b}
, to keep the
L^{2}
finite. In Subsection
2.1.4.3
we demonstrate that the
lowest power of
h_{*}
is
-2M
. Thus, the weights are decomposed as:

\bm{w}=\sum_{m=-M}^{+\infty}\bm{w}_{2m}h_{*}^{2m}\text{.}

(A.12)

As the vector set
\bm{p}_{2m}
does not form an orthonormal basis, we introduce complementary
vectors
\tilde{\bm{p}}_{2m}
,
m=1,...,M
, that are orthogonal to all
\bm{p}_{2j}
for
j<M
and
j\neq m
. The complementary vectors are normalised such that
\langle\tilde{\bm{p}}_{2m},\bm{p}_{2m}\rangle=1
, where the angular brackets denote the inner product. Like
\bm{p}_{2m}
, the
\tilde{\bm{p}}_{2m}
vectors are linearly
independent, but not mutually orthogonal. They are no longer orthogonal to
\bm{p}_{2j}
when
j>M
. For example, if
M=3
, the space of weight vectors is
3
-D, the vectors
\bm{p}_{2k}
,
k=1,2,3
, are not parallel, and vectors
\tilde{\bm{p}}_{2k}
,
k=1,2,3
, always exist. However, a
vector
\tilde{\bm{p}}_{8}
orthogonal to all
\bm{p}_{k}
,
k=2,4,6
, cannot exist.

We represent the coefficients
\bm{w}_{2m}
in the power series
A.12
of
mixand weights as linear combinations of the complementary vectors:

\bm{w}_{2m}=\sum_{j=1}^{M}C_{2j}^{(2m)}\tilde{\bm{p}}_{2j}\text{.}

(A.13)

This form of decomposition is instrumental in combination with matrices expressed through outer products in

Eq.

A.10
because for arbitrary vectors
\bm{x}
,
\bm{y}
, and
\bm{z}
we have

\left(\bm{x}\otimes\bm{y}\right)\bm{z}=\bm{x}\langle\bm{y},\bm{z}\rangle
.
If
\bm{y}
is one the vectors from

Eq.

A.8
and
\bm{z}
is one of the complementary vectors
\tilde{\bm{p}}_{2m}
, we can use
their orthogonality to simplify the product of
\bm{A}
and
\bm{w}
.

We substitute the decompositions

A.9
,
A.5
,
A.12
and
A.13
into the linear system

\bm{A}\bm{w}=\bm{b}
, equate the terms with the same powers of
h_{*}
on the lhs and the rhs, and then solve the
obtained linear systems of equations to find
C_{2j}^{(2m)}
. Using these results
in the product
\bm{w}{{}^{\mkern-1.5mu\mathsf{T}}}\bm{b}
, we then find the coefficients in the power series of
L^{2}
(repeating

here for completeness):

L^{2}=\sum_{j=0}^{+\infty}L^{2}_{2j}h_{*}^{2j}\text{.}

(A.14)

We first present
the solution up to the second-order terms in
L^{2}
for the
simplest cases of
M=1,2
and
3
, then generalise to arbitrary
M
and arbitrary order of
decomposition in
h_{*}
.

2.1.1
M=1

In this degenerate case
\bm{A}
is not a matrix, but a scalar denoted as
A
. Likewise,
\bm{b}\Rightarrow b
and
\bm{w}\Rightarrow w
. All vectors
\bm{p}_{2j}
become the scalar
1
, while
the “orthogonal” vectors
\tilde{\bm{p}}_{2j}
cannot be defined, making the
M=1
case an
exception in terms of notation. Applying the series decomposition, the
h_{*}^{2}
terms yield

A_{4}w_{-2}=\alpha^{(4)}_{2,2}w_{-2}=\beta_{2}

(A.15)

to find
w_{-2}
. We skip the notation
C^{(2m)}_{2}
because these values are equal to
w_{2m}
,

\forall m\geq-1
. From the
h_{*}^{4}
terms we obtain

\alpha^{(4)}_{2,2}w_{0}+\left(\alpha^{(6)}_{2,4}+\alpha^{(6)}_{4,2}\right)w_{-2}=\beta_{4}

(A.16)

and solve for
w_{0}
. Then the lowest coefficients in the series of
L^{2}
are found as

L^{2}_{0}={\lVert F\rVert}_{2}-\beta_{2}w_{-2}

(A.17)

and

L^{2}_{2}=-\beta_{2}w_{0}-\beta_{4}w_{-2}\text{.}

(A.18)

In
Eq.

A.17
,
{\lVert F\rVert}_{2}
is the
L^{2}
norm squared of the standard Gaussian
function
\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}=\mathcal{N}(x;0,1)
minus the central mixand
\mathcal{N}_{0}=\mathcal{N}(x;0,\sigma)
, see
:

{\lVert F\rVert}_{2}=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}{\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}-\mathcal{N}_{0}\right)}^{2}dx=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}-\frac{2}{\sqrt{2\pi(1+\sigma^{2})}}+\frac{1}{2\sigma\sqrt{\pi}}\text{.}

(A.19)

2.1.2
M=2

We define the system of equations from the series decomposition of
\bm{A}\bm{w}=\bm{b}
:

\displaystyle\text{the $h_{*}^{0}$ terms}\quad\quad

\displaystyle\bm{A}_{4}\bm{w}_{-4}

\displaystyle=\bm{0}

(A.20)

\displaystyle\text{the $h_{*}^{2}$ terms}\quad\quad

\displaystyle\bm{A}_{4}\bm{w}_{-2}

\displaystyle+\bm{A}_{6}\bm{w}_{-4}

\displaystyle=\bm{b}_{2}

\displaystyle\text{the $h_{*}^{4}$ terms}\quad\quad

\displaystyle\bm{A}_{4}\bm{w}_{0}

\displaystyle+\bm{A}_{6}\bm{w}_{-2}

\displaystyle+\bm{A}_{8}\bm{w}_{-4}

\displaystyle=\bm{b}_{4}

\displaystyle\text{the $h_{*}^{6}$ terms}\quad\quad

\displaystyle\bm{A}_{4}\bm{w}_{2}

\displaystyle+\bm{A}_{6}\bm{w}_{0}

\displaystyle+\bm{A}_{8}\bm{w}_{-2}

\displaystyle+\bm{A}_{10}\bm{w}_{-4}

\displaystyle=\bm{b}_{6}\text{.}

Expanding the
h_{*}^{0}
equation gives

\displaystyle\bm{A}_{4}\bm{w}_{-4}=\alpha^{(4)}_{2,2}(\bm{p}_{2}\otimes\bm{p}_{2})(C_{2}^{(-4)}\tilde{\bm{p}}_{2}+C_{4}^{(-4)}\tilde{\bm{p}}_{4})

(A.21)

\displaystyle=\alpha^{(4)}_{2,2}(C_{2}^{(-4)}\langle\bm{p}_{2},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{2}\rangle\bm{p}_{2}+C_{4}^{(-4)}\langle\bm{p}_{2},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{4}\rangle\bm{p}_{2})

\displaystyle=\alpha^{(4)}_{2,2}(C_{2}^{(-4)}(1)\bm{p}_{2}+C_{4}^{(-4)}(0)\bm{p}_{2})

\displaystyle=\alpha^{(4)}_{2,2}C_{2}^{(-4)}\bm{p}_{2}=\bm{0}\text{.}

Hence,
C_{2}^{(-4)}=0
, while
C_{4}^{(-4)}
is undefined. Then, the
h_{*}^{2}
equation is:

\displaystyle\bm{A}_{4}\bm{w}_{-2}+\bm{A}_{6}\bm{w}_{-4}

\displaystyle=\alpha^{(4)}_{2,2}(C_{2}^{(-2)}\langle\bm{p}_{2},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{2}\rangle\bm{p}_{2}+C_{4}^{(-2)}\langle\bm{p}_{2},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{4}\rangle\bm{p}_{2})+

\displaystyle\alpha^{(6)}_{2,4}C_{4}^{(-4)}\langle\bm{p}_{4},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{4}\rangle\bm{p}_{2}+\alpha^{(6)}_{4,2}C_{4}^{(-4)}\langle\bm{p}_{2},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{4}\rangle\bm{p}_{4}

\displaystyle=\alpha^{(4)}_{2,2}C_{2}^{(-2)}\bm{p}_{2}+\alpha^{(6)}_{2,4}C_{4}^{(-4)}\bm{p}_{2}=\beta_{2}\bm{p}_{2}\text{,}

which yields

\alpha^{(4)}_{2,2}C_{2}^{(-2)}+\alpha^{(6)}_{2,4}C_{4}^{(-4)}=\beta_{2}\text{.}

(A.22)

Now, moving on to the
h_{*}^{4}
equation, and noting that for
M=2
, the vectors

\tilde{\bm{p}}_{2}
and
\tilde{\bm{p}}_{4}
are only orthogonal to
\bm{p}_{4}
and
\bm{p}_{2}
,
respectively, not
\bm{p}_{2m}
for
m\geq 3
, we obtain

\displaystyle\bm{A}_{4}\bm{w}_{0}+\bm{A}_{6}\bm{w}_{-2}+\bm{A}_{8}\bm{w}_{-4}

\displaystyle\begin{split}\!\begin{aligned} b]{2}&=\ &&\alpha^{(4)}_{2,2}(C_{2}^{(0)}\langle\bm{p}_{2},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{2}\rangle\bm{p}_{2}+C_{4}^{(0)}\langle\bm{p}_{2},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{4}\rangle\bm{p}_{2})+\\
&&&\alpha^{(6)}_{2,4}(C_{2}^{(-2)}\langle\bm{p}_{4},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{2}\rangle\bm{p}_{2}+C_{4}^{(-2)}\langle\bm{p}_{4},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{4}\rangle\bm{p}_{2})+\\
&&&\alpha^{(6)}_{4,2}(C_{2}^{(-2)}\langle\bm{p}_{2},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{2}\rangle\bm{p}_{4}+C_{4}^{(-2)}\langle\bm{p}_{2},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{4}\rangle\bm{p}_{4})+\\
&&&\alpha^{(8)}_{2,6}C_{4}^{(-4)}\langle\bm{p}_{6},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{4}\rangle\bm{p}_{2}+\alpha^{(8)}_{4,4}C_{4}^{(-4)}\langle\bm{p}_{4},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{4}\rangle\bm{p}_{4}+\alpha^{(8)}_{6,2}C_{4}^{(-4)}\langle\bm{p}_{2},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{4}\rangle\bm{p}_{6}\\
\end{aligned}\end{split}

\displaystyle\begin{split}\!\begin{aligned} b]{2}&=\ &&\alpha^{(4)}_{2,2}C_{2}^{(0)}\bm{p}_{2}+\alpha^{(6)}_{2,4}C_{4}^{(-2)}\bm{p}_{2}+\alpha^{(6)}_{4,2}C_{2}^{(-2)}\bm{p}_{4}+\\
&&&\alpha^{(8)}_{2,6}C_{4}^{(-4)}\langle\bm{p}_{6},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{4}\rangle\bm{p}_{2}+\alpha^{(8)}_{4,4}C_{4}^{(-4)}\bm{p}_{4}=\beta_{4}\bm{p}_{4}\text{.}\end{aligned}\end{split}

(A.23)

Multiplying both sides by
\tilde{\bm{p}}_{4}
yields

\alpha^{(6)}_{4,2}C_{2}^{(-2)}+\alpha^{(8)}_{4,4}C_{4}^{(-4)}=\beta_{4}\text{.}

(A.24)

Combined with
Eq.

A.22
, this gives a linear system for
C_{2}^{(-2)}
and
C_{4}^{(-4)}
.
Solving it, we find the term of order
h_{*}^{0}
in the power series decomposition of
L^{2}
:

\displaystyle L^{2}_{0}

\displaystyle={\lVert F\rVert}_{2}-\bm{w}_{-2}^{\mkern-1.5mu\mathsf{T}}\bm{b}_{2}-\bm{w}_{-4}^{\mkern-1.5mu\mathsf{T}}\bm{b}_{4}

(A.25)

\displaystyle={\lVert F\rVert}_{2}-\beta_{2}(C_{2}^{(-2)}\langle\bm{p}_{2},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{2}\rangle+C_{4}^{(-2)}\langle\bm{p}_{2},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{4}\rangle)-\beta_{4}C_{4}^{(-4)}\langle\bm{p}_{4},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{4}\rangle

\displaystyle={\lVert F\rVert}_{2}-\beta_{2}C_{2}^{(-2)}-\beta_{4}C_{4}^{(-4)}\text{,}

where
{\lVert F\rVert}_{2}
is given in
Eq.

A.19
.
To get the coefficients for the second-order term
L^{2}_{2}
, we first multiply
Eq.

A.23
by

\tilde{\bm{p}}_{2}
to obtain

\alpha^{(4)}_{2,2}C_{2}^{(0)}+\alpha^{(6)}_{2,4}C_{4}^{(-2)}=-\alpha^{(8)}_{2,6}C_{4}^{(-4)}\langle\bm{p}_{6},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{4}\rangle\text{,}

(A.26)

Then we consider the
h_{*}^{6}
equation:

\displaystyle\bm{A}_{4}\bm{w}_{2}+\bm{A}_{6}\bm{w}_{0}+\bm{A}_{8}\bm{w}_{-2}+\bm{A}_{10}\bm{w}_{-4}

\displaystyle\begin{split}\!\begin{aligned} b]{2}&=\ &&\alpha^{(4)}_{2,2}(C_{2}^{(2)}\langle\bm{p}_{2},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{2}\rangle\bm{p}_{2}+C_{4}^{(2)}\langle\bm{p}_{2},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{4}\rangle\bm{p}_{2})+\\
&&&\alpha^{(6)}_{2,4}(C_{2}^{(0)}\langle\bm{p}_{4},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{2}\rangle\bm{p}_{2}+C_{4}^{(0)}\langle\bm{p}_{4},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{4}\rangle\bm{p}_{2})+\\
&&&\alpha^{(6)}_{4,2}(C_{2}^{(0)}\langle\bm{p}_{2},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{2}\rangle\bm{p}_{4}+C_{4}^{(0)}\langle\bm{p}_{2},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{4}\rangle\bm{p}_{4})+\\
&&&\alpha^{(8)}_{2,6}(C_{2}^{(-2)}\langle\bm{p}_{6},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{2}\rangle\bm{p}_{2}+C_{4}^{(-2)}\langle\bm{p}_{6},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{4}\rangle\bm{p}_{2})+\\
&&&\alpha^{(8)}_{4,4}(C_{2}^{(-2)}\langle\bm{p}_{4},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{2}\rangle\bm{p}_{4}+C_{4}^{(-2)}\langle\bm{p}_{4},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{4}\rangle\bm{p}_{4})+\\
&&&\alpha^{(8)}_{6,2}(C_{2}^{(-2)}\langle\bm{p}_{2},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{2}\rangle\bm{p}_{6}+C_{4}^{(-2)}\langle\bm{p}_{2},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{4}\rangle\bm{p}_{6})+\\
&&&\alpha^{(10)}_{2,8}C_{4}^{(-4)}\langle\bm{p}_{8},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{4}\rangle\bm{p}_{2}+\alpha^{(10)}_{4,6}C_{4}^{(-4)}\langle\bm{p}_{6},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{4}\rangle\bm{p}_{4}+\\
&&&\alpha^{(10)}_{6,4}C_{4}^{(-4)}\langle\bm{p}_{4},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{4}\rangle\bm{p}_{6}+\alpha^{(10)}_{8,2}C_{4}^{(-4)}\langle\bm{p}_{2},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{4}\rangle\bm{p}_{8}\\
\end{aligned}\end{split}

\displaystyle\begin{split}\!\begin{aligned} b]{2}&=\ &&\alpha^{(4)}_{2,2}C_{2}^{(2)}\bm{p}_{2}+\alpha^{(6)}_{2,4}C_{4}^{(0)}\bm{p}_{2}+\alpha^{(6)}_{4,2}C_{2}^{(0)}\bm{p}_{4}+\\
&&&\alpha^{(8)}_{2,6}(C_{2}^{(-2)}\langle\bm{p}_{6},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{2}\rangle\bm{p}_{2}+C_{4}^{(-2)}\langle\bm{p}_{6},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{4}\rangle\bm{p}_{2})\\
&&&\alpha^{(8)}_{4,4}C_{4}^{(-2)}\bm{p}_{4}+\alpha^{(8)}_{6,2}C_{2}^{(-2)}\bm{p}_{6}+\alpha^{(10)}_{2,8}C_{4}^{(-4)}\langle\bm{p}_{8},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{4}\rangle\bm{p}_{2}+\\
&&&\alpha^{(10)}_{4,6}C_{4}^{(-4)}\langle\bm{p}_{6},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{4}\rangle\bm{p}_{4}+\alpha^{(10)}_{6,4}C_{4}^{(-4)}\bm{p}_{6}=\beta_{6}\bm{p}_{6}\text{.}\end{aligned}\end{split}

(A.27)

Multiplying by
\tilde{\bm{p}}_{4}
and combining the common terms, we simplify the above to

\displaystyle\begin{split}\!\begin{aligned} b]{2}&\alpha^{(6)}_{4,2}C_{2}^{(0)}+\alpha^{(8)}_{4,4}C_{4}^{(-2)}\\
&=\left(\beta_{6}-\alpha^{(10)}_{4,6}C_{4}^{(-4)}-\alpha^{(8)}_{6,2}C^{(-2)}_{2}-\alpha^{(10)}_{6,4}C_{4}^{(-4)}\right)\langle\bm{p}_{6},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{4}\rangle\text{,}\end{aligned}\end{split}

(A.28)

Together with

A.26
, we solve for
C_{2}^{(0)}
and
C_{4}^{(-2)}
. Finally,
we take the

terms in
\bm{w}{{}^{\mkern-1.5mu\mathsf{T}}}\bm{b}
to obtain

\displaystyle L^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle=-\bm{w}_{0}^{\mkern-1.5mu\mathsf{T}}\bm{b}_{2}-\bm{w}_{-2}^{\mkern-1.5mu\mathsf{T}}\bm{b}_{4}-\bm{w}_{-4}^{\mkern-1.5mu\mathsf{T}}\bm{b}_{6}

(A.29)

\displaystyle=-\beta_{2}(C_{2}^{(0)}\langle\bm{p}_{2},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{2}\rangle+C_{4}^{(0)}\langle\bm{p}_{2},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{4}\rangle)

\displaystyle-\beta_{4}(C_{2}^{(-2)}\langle\bm{p}_{4},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{2}\rangle+C_{4}^{(-2)}\langle\bm{p}_{4},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{4}\rangle)

\displaystyle-\beta_{6}C_{4}^{(-4)}\langle\bm{p}_{6},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{4}\rangle

2.1.4
The solution for general
M

Having analysed the special cases of
M=1,2,3
, we now seek a solution for arbitrary dimensionality
M
.
Furthermore, we seek to prove the statement made before
Eq.

A.12
that the highest power
of the decomposition of the weights in
Eq.

A.12
is
-2M
.

2.1.4.1  Solution for the weights

Using decompositions
A.5
,
A.9
and
A.12
and matching the multipliers of

h_{*}^{2q}
on both sides of
\bm{A}\bm{b}=\bm{w}
for
q=-M+2,-M+3,\dots
, we obtain

\sum_{j=2}^{q+M}\bm{A}_{2j}\bm{w}_{2(q-j)}=\beta_{2q}\bm{p}_{2q}[q\geq 1]\text{,}

(A.46)

where
[\ldots]
denotes the Iverson bracket
[
2
, p. 24]
; it equals
1
if
q\geq 1
and zero otherwise.
The rhs of
Eq.

A.48
uses non-existing

\beta_{2q}
factors for
q<1
; however, for such cases the Iverson bracket evaluates to zero, so,
we ignore this inconsistency.

After expanding
\bm{A}_{2j}
and
\bm{w}_{2(q-j)}
into their own summations by
Eqs.

A.10
and
A.12
,
the lhs of
Eq.

A.46
becomes

\displaystyle\sum_{j=2}^{q+M}\bm{A}_{2j}\bm{w}_{2(q-j)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=2}^{q+M}\sum_{k=1}^{j-1}\alpha^{(2j)}_{2k,2(j-k)}(\bm{p}_{2k}\otimes\bm{p}_{2(j-k)})\sum_{l=1}^{M}C^{(2(q-j))}_{2l}\tilde{\bm{p}}_{2l}

(A.47)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=2}^{q+M}\sum_{k=1}^{j-1}\sum_{l=1}^{M}\alpha^{(2j)}_{2k,2(j-k)}C^{(2(q-j))}_{2l}\langle\bm{p}_{2(j-k)},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{2l}\rangle\bm{p}_{2k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=2}^{q+M}\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}\sum_{l=1}^{M}\alpha^{(2j)}_{2(j-i),2i}C^{(2(q-j))}_{2l}\langle\bm{p}_{2i},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{2l}\rangle\bm{p}_{2(j-i)}\text{,}

where on the last line we replaced the middle sum index as
k=j-i
.

Multiplying both sides of
Eq.

A.46
by some
\tilde{\bm{p}}_{2n}
, we
finally get

\sum_{j=2}^{q+M}\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}\sum_{l=1}^{M}\alpha^{(2j)}_{2(j-i),2i}C^{(2(q-j))}_{2l}\langle\bm{p}_{2i},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{2l}\rangle\langle\bm{p}_{2(j-i)},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{2n}\rangle\\
=\beta_{2q}\langle\bm{p}_{2q},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{2n}\rangle[q\geq 1]\text{.}

(A.48)

We refer to
Eq.

A.48
for some
q,n
as “the equation
2q/2n
”: it is the
h_{*}^{2q}
-th term in the power series of
\bm{A}\bm{b}=\bm{w}
projected onto

\tilde{\bm{p}}_{2n}
.

Consider the groups of
2q/2n
equations with
q=-M+s+n
for
s\geq 1
and
n
running from

1
to
\min{(s,M)}
. The rhs of
Eq.

A.48
is non-zero when either
q=n\leq M
, which means
s=M
(for all
n
), or
q>M
, which means
s+n>2M
; the latter is
possible only when
s>M
. We will see that the
s=M
group yields the equations for the
coefficients
C^{(2m)}_{2j}
that are used in
L^{2}_{0}
. Similarly, solving the
s=M+1
group
produces the coefficients used in
L^{2}_{2}
. But before we get there, consider the groups for smaller

s
.

2.1.4.1.1  Group
s=1

The simplest group for
s=1
has a single equation with
n=1
,
q=-M+2
(the lowest value
possible), and, based on the sum limits in
Eq.

A.48
,
j=2
and
i=1
. The first
inner product in
Eq.

A.48
becomes the Kronecker delta function
\delta_{1,l}
, and
the entire triple sum reduces to a single term:

\alpha^{(4)}_{2,2}C^{(-2M)}_{2}=\beta_{2(-M+2)}\delta_{-M+2,1}[-M+2\geq 1]\text{.}

(A.49)

The rhs is non-zero only when
M=1
, in which case we reproduce
Eq.

A.15
.
Otherwise, if
M>1
, we get
C^{(-2M)}_{2}=0
, also familiar from
Eq.

A.21
for
M=2
and
Eq.

A.31
for
M=3
.

2.1.4.1.2  Groups
s<M

Now consider more general
s<M
.
By definitions of

\bm{p}_{2k}
and
\tilde{\bm{p}}_{2k}
, the inner products in
Eq.

A.48
satisfy the following conditions:

\displaystyle\langle\bm{p}_{2i},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{2l}\rangle

\displaystyle=\delta_{i,l}

\displaystyle\text{ if }1\leq i\leq M\text{,}

(A.50a)

\displaystyle\langle\bm{p}_{2i},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{2l}\rangle

\displaystyle>1

\displaystyle\text{ if }i>M\text{.}

(A.50b)

The second index is

1\leq l\leq M
.
We aim to use the Kronecker delta functions from
Eq.

A.50a
to
simplify the summation in
Eq.

A.48
. However, according to
Eq.

A.50b
, the
deltas might not be available. If we ignore this complication for a moment (it is reviewed below),
then the only non-zero terms in
Eq.

A.48
are those with
j=n+i
and
l=i
. As

i\geq 1
, we must have
j\geq n+1
, increasing the lower limit of the first sum. Also, by
definition of
s
and
n
, we have
q+M=n+s
. Together this yields

\sum_{j=n+1}^{n+s}\biggl.\alpha^{(2j)}_{2(j-i),2i}C^{(2(q-j))}_{2l}\biggr|_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.0pt}{$\displaystyle\begin{subarray}{l}l=i\\
i=j-n\end{subarray}$}}{\raisebox{3.0pt}{$\textstyle\begin{subarray}{l}l=i\\
i=j-n\end{subarray}$}}{\raisebox{3.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\begin{subarray}{l}l=i\\
i=j-n\end{subarray}$}}{\raisebox{3.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\begin{subarray}{l}l=i\\
i=j-n\end{subarray}$}}}=0\text{,}

or, using a new summation index
m=j-n
,

\sum_{m=1}^{s}\alpha^{(2(m+n))}_{2n,2m}C^{(2(-M+s-m))}_{2m}=0\quad\text{ for $n=1,\ldots,s$.}

(A.51)

Note that the indices of the
C^{(\ldots)}_{2m}
variables in
Eq.

A.51
depend only on
m
,

1\leq m\leq s
, but not on
n
, so we have
s
linear equations for the same
s
unknowns. The
coefficients
\alpha^{(2(m+n))}_{2n,2m}
of this linear system are given in
Eq.

A.11

and do not exhibit any linear dependencies across the rows or columns. Thus, the system is solvable,
and given that all the right-hand sides are zeros, so are the solutions.

For
M=3
, the system
A.51
at
s=2
yields
Eqs.

A.32
and
A.34
,
while for
s=1
and
M\neq 1
it becomes
Eq.

A.49
, which was considered separately because
of the special case of non-zero rhs at
M=1
.

So far, however, the derivation tacitly omitted a possible hurdle: the indices of the
\bm{p}_{2i}

and
\bm{p}_{2(j-i)}
vectors in
Eq.

A.48
may exceed
M
, and if so, the compaction
of the two inner sums by Kronecker deltas would not apply. Now we examine if such indices may occur.

If we have large
n
,
j
could be large enough so that the index
i
in the first inner product in

Eq.

A.48
exceeds
M
. Then the product would fall into the category of

Eq.

A.50b
, not
Eq.

A.50a
. However, when
i>M
, we would have
j-i<j-M\leq s+n-M<n
, as we consider
1\leq s<M
. Therefore, the second inner product would be
zero, so the overall contribution of such terms to the sum would be nil. Note that for
s=M
we
would have
s+n-M=n
, but still
j-i<n
. However, for
s>M
, this condition would no
longer hold.

Consider now the second inner product. May we have
j-i>M
? Indeed,
\max{(j-i)}=s+n-1
,
and it may exceed
M
for sufficiently large
s>(M+1)/2
, as
\max{(n)}=s
.
1
1

1

Given
that
s
is an integer and
s<M
, the condition may be satisfied only when
M\geq 4
.
The total
contribution of such terms to the lhs of
Eq.

A.48
would be

\sum_{j=M+2}^{s+n}\sum_{i=1}^{j-M-1}\alpha^{(2j)}_{2(j-i),2i}C^{(2(q-j))}_{2i}\langle\bm{p}_{2(j-i)},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{2n}\rangle\text{.}

(A.52)

We adjusted the sum limits because to have
j-i>M
(and
i\geq 1
), one has to have
j\geq M+2
and
i\leq j-M-1
. Also, we already know that we may not have
i>M
and
j-i>M
at the
same time. Therefore, the first inner product is
\delta_{i,l}
. Expressing
q
through
s
and
n
,

Eq.

A.52
contains the variables

C^{(2(-M+s+n-j))}_{2i}\quad\text{ for $M+2\leq j\leq s+n$, $1\leq i\leq j-M-1$.}

(A.53)

If we solve the length-
s
groups of equations sequentially from smaller to larger
s
, we would
already know all these values by solving
Eq.

A.51
for some
s^{\prime}<s
. More formally, for all

i,j,s,n
such that

\displaystyle M+2

\displaystyle\leq j\leq s+n\text{,}\quad

\displaystyle 1\leq s\leq M-1\text{,}

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\leq i\leq j-M-1\text{,}\quad

\displaystyle 1\leq n\leq s\text{,}

we can find
s^{\prime}
and
m=i\leq s^{\prime}
that satisfy

1\leq s^{\prime}<s\quad\text{and}\quad-M+s+n-j=-M+s^{\prime}-m\text{.}

The proof is straightforward. We explicitly have

s^{\prime}=s+n-j+i\text{.}

Then

\min\limits_{i,j,n}{(s+n-j+i)}=\min\limits_{j,n}{(s+n-j+1)}=1\text{,}

(A.54a)

as
\max{(j)}=s+n
, while

\max\limits_{i,j,n}{(s+n-j+i)}=\max\limits_{i,j,n}{(s+n-(j-i))}=\\
\max\limits_{n}{(s+n-(M+1))}=2s-(M+1)<s\text{,}

(A.54b)

as
s<M
. Therefore,
1\leq s^{\prime}<s
. At the same time,
m=i=s^{\prime}-(s+n-j)\leq s^{\prime}
, because

s+n\geq j
. Thus, all variables in
Eq.

A.53
would already appear in the linear systems

A.51
for
s=s^{\prime}
, and consequently, would be zeros. This completes the
proof of
Eq.

A.51
validity: when some of the inner products in
Eq.

A.48
do
not evaluate to Kronecker delta functions, the corresponding terms are equal to zero for other reasons.

Also note that the conclusions given by
Eq.

A.54
hold for arbitrary
s\geq M
. The minimal
value in
Eq.

A.54a
remains the same, while for the maximal value we obtain a slightly
modified expression

\max\limits_{i,j,n}{(s+n-j+i)}=\max\limits_{n}{(s+n-(M+1))}=s-1<s\text{,}

(A.54c)

because the maximal value of
n
would now be
M
.

2.1.4.1.3  Group
s=M

This happens to be a minor extension of the earlier derivation for
s<M
. The main difference is
that for
s=M
we have
q=n
, and therefore, both the inner product and the Iverson bracket in
the rhs of the
2q/2n
equation
A.48
are equal to one. The
presented earlier analysis of the inner products in the lhs remains the same: the
derivation applies verbatim for
s=M
. Thus, the
s=M
group yields
M
linear equations

\sum_{m=1}^{M}\alpha^{(2(m+n))}_{2n,2m}C^{(-2m)}_{2m}=\beta_{2n}\quad\text{ for $n=1,\ldots,M$.}

(A.55)

This linear system generalises the earlier obtained
Eqs.

A.22
and
A.24
for
M=2
, and
Eqs.

A.35
,
A.37
and
A.39
for
M=3
.

2.1.4.1.4  Group
s=M+1

Now both inner products in
Eq.

A.48
may fall into the category of
Eq.

A.50b
.
Thus, we need to consider three types of terms in the lhs of
Eq.

A.48
:

•

The “regular” terms with
i\leq M
and
j-i\leq M
; for these, the inner products
would evaluate to Kronecker deltas.

•

The terms with
i>M
and
j-i\leq M
, so that the second inner product evaluates to

\delta_{j-i,n}
for some
n
.

•

The terms with
j-i>M
and arbitrary
i
.

Also, the rhs of
Eq.

A.48
could be non-zero.

The first type of terms is simple and would eventually yield the lhs of a linear system
similar to
Eq.

A.51
. It is written out below.

For the second type, we have
j=n+i>M+n
. Given that the upper limit of
j
is
M+n+1
,
this leaves only
j=M+n+1
, and therefore,
i=M+1
,
j-i=n
, and
q-j=-M
. This
eliminates the first two summations leaving

\sum_{l=1}^{M}\alpha^{(2(M+1+n))}_{2n,2(M+1)}C^{(-2M)}_{2l}\langle\bm{p}_{2(M+1)},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{2l}\rangle\text{.}

As we know that
C^{(-2M)}_{2l}=0
unless
l=M
, the sum reduces to a single term

\alpha^{(2(M+1+n))}_{2n,2(M+1)}C^{(-2M)}_{2M}\langle\bm{p}_{2(M+1)},\tilde{\bm{p}}_{2M}\rangle\text{.}

This component exists for all
n
.

The contribution of the third type of terms is given by
Eq.

A.52
. It was proven above
that all the variables
C^{(\ldots)}_{2i}
in that expression are already known from solving

Eq.

A.51
for some
s^{\prime}<s
. Furthermore, only the variables
C^{(-2m)}_{2m}
with matching
lower and upper indices could be non-zero. Thus, we set
-2(q-j)=2i
in
Eq.

A.52
. For
the current group
q=n+1
, and therefore,
i=j-(n+1)
. However, the upper limit of
i
in

Eq.
