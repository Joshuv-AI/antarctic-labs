Relaxation dynamics of the Inertial Winfree model
Caiman Moreno-Earle, Seung-Yeon Ryoo, Grace To

Abstract
. We prove two synchronization theorems for the second-order (inertial) Winfree model of coupled oscillators. The first result is a pathwise oscillator-death theorem with explicit smallness thresholds on the natural frequencies, initial velocities, and inertia, scaling as R 0 3 / 2 R_{0}^{3/2} in the initial order parameter R 0 R_{0} . The second result is a qualitative zero-inertia synchronization statement: under generic initial data, if the intrinsic and initial velocity spreads are small compared to κ \kappa and the inertia m m is small, then the limiting order parameter can be made arbitrarily close to 2. The proof of the first result is organized around three mechanisms, namely inertial gradient flow and the Łojasiewicz theorem, an initial layer argument, and an order-parameter bootstrapping argument. The proof of the second result involves approximation to the first-order case via a quantitative Tikhonov theorem.

2.1.
Inertial gradient-flow structure and Łojasiewicz theorem

The inertial Winfree model (
4
) possesses a real-analytic
inertial gradient-flow formulation. Define the real-analytic potential

P\colon\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
by

P(\Theta)\coloneqq-\sum_{k=1}^{N}\nu_{k}\theta_{k}-\frac{\kappa N}{2}R(\Theta)^{2}=-\sum_{k=1}^{N}\nu_{k}\theta_{k}-\frac{\kappa}{2N}\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}(1+\cos\theta_{k})\Bigr)^{\!2}.

(13)

A direct computation yields

\partial_{\theta_{i}}P=-\nu_{i}-\kappa NR\cdot\Bigl(-\tfrac{1}{N}\sin\theta_{i}\Bigr)=-\nu_{i}+\kappa R(\Theta)\sin\theta_{i}=-F_{i}(\Theta),

so that (
6
) takes the inertial gradient-flow form

m\ddot{\Theta}+\dot{\Theta}\ =\ -\nabla_{\Theta}P(\Theta).

(14)

We denote the set of critical
points of the real-analytic potential
P
by

\mathcal{S}\coloneqq\{(\Theta,{\mathbf{0}})\in\mathbb{R}^{2N}\colon\nabla_{\Theta}P(\Theta)=0\},

whose projection to the first factor is precisely the set of

death states
, i.e., equilibria of the first-order Winfree model
(
2
)–(
3
).

The Łojasiewicz gradient theorem (
[
Loj63
,
Loj82
]
) asserts that a bounded solution to a
first-order gradient flow of a real-analytic potential converges to a
critical point of the potential. This was extended to inertial
gradient flows in
[
HJ98
, Theorem 1.1]

and
[
BBJ15
, Corollary 5.1]
, and was observed
in
[

, Proposition 2.1]
to apply to the inertial
Kuramoto model.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
HJ98
,
BBJ15
]
)
.

Let
(\Theta,\Omega)
be a global solution to (

) satisfying

\|\Theta\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\coloneqq\|\Theta\|_{L^{\infty}[0,\infty)}+\|\Omega\|_{L^{\infty}[0,\infty)}<\infty.

Then there exists

such that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\bigl(\|\Theta(t)-\Theta^{\infty}\|_{\infty}+\|\Omega(t)\|_{\infty}\bigr)=0.

Moreover, the convergence is algebraic: there exist constants

We can rewrite (

\begin{cases}\dot{\theta}_{i}(t)=\omega_{i}(t),\\[2.0pt]

Viewing the equation for

\dot{\theta}_{i}(t)=\omega_{i}^{0}e^{-t/m}+\frac{1}{m}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)/m}F_{i}(\Theta(s))\,ds,

\theta_{i}(t)=\theta_{i}^{0}+m\omega_{i}^{0}(1-e^{-t/m})+\int_{0}^{t}F_{i}(\Theta(s))\bigl(1-e^{-(t-s)/m}\bigr)\,ds.

Lemma 2.5

(Speed limit)
.

For every solution to (
4

i\in[N]
, and every

|\dot{\theta}_{i}(t)|\leq|\omega_{i}^{0}|e^{-t/m}+(|\nu_{i}|+2\kappa)(1-e^{-t/m})\leq\|\Omega^{0}\|_{\infty}+M_{F},

\Bigl|\frac{d}{dt}(R(t)\sin\theta_{i}(t))\Bigr|\leq 2\|\Omega^{0}\|_{\infty}e^{-t/m}+2M_{F}(1-e^{-t/m}).

For
\mathcal{B}\subset[N]
and

\Bigl|\frac{d}{dt}(R_{\mathcal{B}}(t)\sin\theta_{i}(t))\Bigr|\leq\frac{2|\mathcal{B}|}{N}\left(\|\Omega_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}\|_{\infty}e^{-t/m}+M_{F,\mathcal{B}}(1-e^{-t/m})\right).

The first bound follows term by term from (

) (recalling (

|F_{i}|\leq|\nu_{i}|+2\kappa\leq M_{F}

\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)/m}\,ds=m(1-e^{-t/m})

\displaystyle=\omega_{i}^{0}e^{-t/m}+\nu_{i}(1-e^{-t/m})-\frac{\kappa}{m}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)/m}R(s)\sin\theta_{i}(s)\,ds

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\eqref{eq:order-parameter-split}}}}{{=}}\omega_{i}^{0}e^{-t/m}+\nu_{i}(1-e^{-t/m})-\frac{\kappa}{m}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)/m}R_{\mathcal{B}}(s)\sin\theta_{i}(s)ds

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{\kappa}{m}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)/m}R_{[N]\setminus\mathcal{B}}(s)\sin\theta_{i}(s)\,ds

\displaystyle=\omega_{i}^{0}e^{-t/m}+\nu_{i}(1-e^{-t/m})-\kappa R_{\mathcal{B}}(t)\sin\theta_{i}(t)(1-e^{-t/m})

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\kappa}{m}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)/m}(R_{\mathcal{B}}(t)\sin\theta_{i}(t)-R_{\mathcal{B}}(s)\sin\theta_{i}(s))ds

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\kappa}{m}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)/m}R_{[N]\setminus\mathcal{B}}(s)\sin\theta_{i}(s)\,ds

so that we may bound

\displaystyle\Bigl|\dot{\theta}_{i}(t)-\omega_{i}^{0}e^{-t/m}-\nu_{i}(1-e^{-t/m})+\kappa R_{\mathcal{B}}(t)\sin\theta_{i}(t)(1-e^{-t/m})\Bigr|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\Bigl|\frac{\kappa}{m}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(R_{\mathcal{B}}(t)\sin\theta_{i}(t)-R_{\mathcal{B}}(s)\sin\theta_{i}(s)\bigr)e^{-(t-s)/m}\,ds\Bigr|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\left|\frac{\kappa}{m}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)/m}R_{[N]\setminus\mathcal{B}}(s)\sin\theta_{i}(s)ds\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{\kappa}{m}\int_{0}^{t}|R_{\mathcal{B}}(t)\sin\theta_{i}(t)-R_{\mathcal{B}}(s)\sin\theta_{i}(s)|e^{-(t-s)/m}\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\frac{N-|\mathcal{B}|}{N}2\kappa(1-e^{-t/m})

where in the second inequality we used the triangle inequality,
R_{[N]\setminus\mathcal{B}}\leq 2\frac{N-|\mathcal{B}|}{N}
, and
|\sin|\leq 1
.

By the third bound of Lemma
2.5
,

\displaystyle|R_{\mathcal{B}}(t)\sin\theta_{i}(t)-R_{\mathcal{B}}(s)\sin\theta_{i}(s)|

\displaystyle\leq 2\frac{|\mathcal{B}|}{N}\int_{s}^{t}\bigl(\|\Omega_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}\|_{\infty}e^{-\tau/m}+M_{F,\mathcal{B}}(1-e^{-\tau/m})\bigr)d\tau

\displaystyle=2\frac{|\mathcal{B}|}{N}\left(m\|\Omega_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}\|_{\infty}(e^{-s/m}-e^{-t/m})+M_{F,\mathcal{B}}(t-s-me^{-s/m}+me^{-t/m})\right).

Substituting, integrating, and using the elementary inequalities

xe^{-x}-e^{-x}+e^{-2x}\leq xe^{-x}(1-e^{-x}),\quad 1-2xe^{-x}-e^{-2x}\leq(1-e^{-x})^{3},\quad x\geq 0,

(with
x=t/m
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa}{m}\int_{0}^{t}|R_{\mathcal{B}}(t)\sin\theta_{i}(t)-R_{\mathcal{B}}(s)\sin\theta_{i}(s)|e^{-(t-s)/m}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq 2\frac{|\mathcal{B}|}{N}\kappa\left(\|\Omega_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}\|_{\infty}e^{-t/m}\int_{0}^{t}\left(1-e^{-(t-s)/m}\right)ds+\frac{M_{F,\mathcal{B}}}{m}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s-me^{-s/m}+me^{-t/m})e^{-(t-s)/m}ds\right)

\displaystyle=2\frac{|\mathcal{B}|}{N}\kappa\left(\|\Omega_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}\|_{\infty}e^{-t/m}\left(t-m+me^{-t/m}\right)+M_{F,\mathcal{B}}\left(m-2te^{-t/m}-me^{-2t/m}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\frac{|\mathcal{B}|}{N}\kappa\left(\|\Omega_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}\|_{\infty}te^{-t/m}(1-e^{-t/m})+mM_{F,\mathcal{B}}(1-e^{-t/m})^{3}\right).

Combining estimates, we get the stated result.
∎

Lemma 2.7

(Initial layer effect on
R
)
.

If
\delta\in(0,1)
and
\eta>0
satisfy

m\|\Omega^{0}\|_{\infty}(1-e^{-\eta})+M_{F}\cdot m(\eta-1+e^{-\eta})<(1-\delta)R_{0},

then
R(t)>\delta R_{0}
for
t\in[0,\eta m]
.

Proof.

By (
18
) in the proof of Lemma
2.5
,

|\dot{R}(t)|\leq M(t)=\|\Omega^{0}\|_{\infty}e^{-t/m}+M_{F}(1-e^{-t/m})
,
so for
t\in[0,\eta m]
,

\displaystyle R(t)

\displaystyle\geq R_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}M(s)\,ds

\displaystyle\geq R_{0}-\int_{0}^{\eta m}M(s)\,ds

\displaystyle=R_{0}-m\|\Omega^{0}\|_{\infty}(1-e^{-\eta})-M_{F}\cdot m(\eta-1+e^{-\eta})

\displaystyle>\delta R_{0}.\qed

3.
Quantitative higher-order Tikhonov theorem

We mentioned earlier in the introduction that solutions to (
2
) with (
3
) are formally obtained from those of (
1
) when passing to the limit
m\to 0
.
Tikhonov’s classical theorem
[
Tik52
,
Vas63
]
makes this precise: it asserts that, for fixed initial data, the solution
\Theta(m,t)
of the
inertial Winfree problem converges, as
m\to 0
, to the solution

\Theta(0,t)
of the first-order Winfree problem (
2
)
with (
3
), in the Fréchet topologies
C^{0}[0,\infty)
and
C^{1}(0,\infty)
. The classical theorem is qualitative. In this section we
derive a
quantitative
and
higher-order
version, namely
Proposition
3.5
below, giving explicit rates of
convergence in the topologies
C^{0}[0,\infty)
and
C^{\infty}(0,\infty)
.
This is the Winfree analogue of
[
CDHR25a
, Proposition 3.2]
.

3.1.
Qualitative Tikhonov theorem

Let us first restate the classical theorem for the reader’s convenience.

Proposition 3.1

(Classical Tikhonov theorem)
.

Fix initial data
(\Theta^{0},\Omega^{0})
, intrinsic velocities
\mathcal{V}
,
and a coupling strength
\kappa>0
. For each
m>0
, let
\Theta(m,t)

denote the solution to (
4
) and
\Theta(0,t)
the solution
to (
2
)–(
3
). Then for every
i\in[N]
,

\lim_{m\to 0}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\theta_{i}(m,t)-\theta_{i}(0,t)|=0\quad(\forall T>0),

and

\lim_{m\to 0}\sup_{t\in[T_{1},T_{2}]}|\omega_{i}(m,t)-\omega_{i}(0,t)|=0\quad(\forall T_{2}>T_{1}>0).

Proof.

System (
4
) in the form (
15
) fits the
standard hypotheses of
[
Vas63
, Theorem 1.1]
: the
“fast” variable
\omega
has a unique globally stable equilibrium

\omega=F(\Theta)
for each frozen
\Theta
, and the “slow” variable

\Theta
evolves via
\dot{\Theta}=\omega
. The conclusion follows.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Proposition
3.1
cannot give uniform-in-time bounds
(convergence in
L^{\infty}[0,\infty)
) because it is not true. Indeed, even for
N=1
, the two
systems
\dot{\theta}=1-\tfrac{1}{2}(1+\cos\theta)\sin\theta
starting at
\theta^{0}=0
and

\tfrac{1}{2}\ddot{\theta}+\dot{\theta}=1-\tfrac{1}{2}(1+\cos\theta)\sin\theta

starting at
\theta^{0}=\dot{\theta}^{0}=0
have phase difference growing
linearly in
t
. Nor does Proposition
3.1
give convergence in
C^{1}
on a
neighborhood of
t=0
, because again it is not true: the inertial system prescribes the arbitrary
value
\dot{\theta}_{i}(0)=\omega_{i}^{0}
, while the first-order system
mandates
\dot{\theta}_{i}(0)=F_{i}(\Theta^{0})
. Any approximation of the
second-order by the first-order model in the
C^{1}
-topology, such as Lemma
2.6
, must
therefore be carried out after an initial time layer of the form

[0,\eta m]
.

The drawback of Tikhonov’s theorem is that it does not give the quantitative bounds needed in this paper. By working with the ODEs of the Winfree model directly, we obtain explicit bounds as below in Proposition
3.5
, where we also show
C^{\infty}(0,\infty)
convergence. One could verify these bounds directly by adapting the Gronwall arguments, but to streamline the exposition we import the results of
[
CDHR25a
]
for the Kuramoto model via an embedding of the Winfree system into the Kuramoto system.

3.2.
Embedding the Winfree model into the Kuramoto model

The main observation of this section is that the first-order/inertial Winfree
system of
N
oscillators is exactly an invariant submanifold of a
first-order/inertial Kuramoto system of
4N
oscillators, obtained by adjoining
phase-reflected copies and a cluster of spectator oscillators frozen
at the origin. We are not aware of prior work that observes this fact. This reduces the quantitative Tikhonov problem for
Winfree to the one already treated in
[
CDHR25a
]
for
Kuramoto, at the negligible cost of a constant factor in the rate.

Lemma 3.3

(Winfree-to-Kuramoto embedding)
.

Let
\Theta(t)=(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{N})
solve (
4
)
with natural frequencies
\mathcal{V}=(\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{N})
, coupling

\kappa
, inertia
m
, and initial data
(\Theta^{0},\Omega^{0})
. Define

\Phi(t)=(\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{4N})
by

\varphi_{i}(t)=\theta_{i}(t),\quad\varphi_{N+i}(t)=-\theta_{i}(t),\quad\varphi_{2N+k}(t)\equiv 0,\qquad i\in[N],\ k\in[2N],

with natural frequencies

\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}=(\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{N},-\nu_{1},\dots,-\nu_{N},0,\dots,0)
,
coupling strength
\tilde{\kappa}=2\kappa
, inertia
m
, and initial
data
(\Phi^{0},\dot{\Phi}^{0})
inherited from the embedding. Then
\Phi

solves the inertial Kuramoto system

m\ddot{\varphi}_{k}+\dot{\varphi}_{k}=\tilde{\nu}_{k}+\frac{\tilde{\kappa}}{4N}\sum_{l=1}^{4N}\sin(\varphi_{l}-\varphi_{k}),\qquad k\in[4N].

(19)

Likewise, if
\Theta(t)
solves the first-order Winfree system (
2
) under (
3
) with natural frequencies
\mathcal{V}=(\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{N})
, coupling

\kappa
, and initial data
\Theta^{0}
, then
\Phi
defined as above with natural frequencies

\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}=(\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{N},-\nu_{1},\dots,-\nu_{N},0,\dots,0)
,
coupling strength
\tilde{\kappa}=2\kappa
, and initial
data
\Phi^{0}
inherited from the embedding solves the first-order Kuramoto system

\dot{\varphi}_{k}=\tilde{\nu}_{k}+\frac{\tilde{\kappa}}{4N}\sum_{l=1}^{4N}\sin(\varphi_{l}-\varphi_{k}),\qquad k\in[4N].

(20)

Proof.

We check each block separately. For
k=i\in[N]
, the Kuramoto right-hand
side equals

\displaystyle\nu_{i}+\frac{2\kappa}{4N}\sum_{l=1}^{4N}\sin(\theta_{l}-\varphi_{i})

\displaystyle=\nu_{i}-\frac{\kappa}{2N}\Bigl[\sum_{j}\sin(\theta_{i}-\theta_{j})+\sum_{j}\sin(\theta_{i}+\theta_{j})+2N\sin\theta_{i}\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\nu_{i}-\frac{\kappa}{2N}\Bigl[2\sin\theta_{i}\sum_{j}\cos\theta_{j}+2N\sin\theta_{i}\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\nu_{i}-\frac{\kappa}{N}\sin\theta_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(1+\cos\theta_{j}),

which matches the Winfree right-hand side. For

k=N+i
the computation is the same with
\theta_{i}\mapsto-\theta_{i}

and
\nu_{i}\mapsto-\nu_{i}
, so the ansatz
\varphi_{N+i}=-\theta_{i}
is
preserved. For
k=2N+j
, one checks that both the intrinsic frequency
and the coupling sum vanish by odd symmetry, so

\varphi_{2N+j}\equiv 0
is an invariant solution.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

Because the embedded system contains
2N
oscillators pinned at
0
,
the sup-norm
\|\Theta\|_{\infty}
of the Winfree configuration equals
half the
phase diameter

\max_{k,l}|\varphi_{k}-\varphi_{l}|
of the
embedded Kuramoto configuration. Hence any
diameter-based (equivalently, Galilean-invariant) norm bound for the
Kuramoto system (
19
) translates verbatim into a
sup-norm bound for the Winfree system (
4
). The same holds
for velocities since
\dot{\varphi}_{2N+j}\equiv 0
.

3.3.
The quantitative Tikhonov theorem

We present a quantitative and higher-order version of Tikhonov’s theorem, namely a
C[0,\infty)\cap C^{\infty}(0,\infty)
convergence statement with explicit bounds. Using this, we will prove Theorem
1.3
later in §
5
by a comparison argument.

Proposition 3.5

(Quantitative higher-order Tikhonov theorem)
.

Fix initial data
(\Theta^{0},\Omega^{0})
, intrinsic velocities
\mathcal{V}
,
and a coupling strength
\kappa>0
. For each
m>0
, let
\Theta(m,t)
denote the solution to (
4
) with initial position
\Theta^{0}
and initial velocity
\Omega^{0}
, and let
\Theta(0,t)
denote the solution to (
2
)–(
3
), with the same
initial position
\Theta^{0}
. Then:

(1)

(Phase convergence,
C^{0}[0,\infty)
)
For all
t\geq 0
,

\|\Theta(m,t)-\Theta(0,t)\|_{\infty}<\frac{1}{2}m\Big(\|\Omega^{0}-\mathcal{V}\|_{\infty}+2\kappa\Big)e^{4\kappa t}.

(21)

(2)

(Velocity convergence,
C^{0}(0,\infty)
)
For all
t\geq 0
,

\|\dot{\Theta}(m,t)-\dot{\Theta}(0,t)\|_{\infty}<\frac{1}{2}\Big(\|\Omega^{0}-\mathcal{V}\|_{\infty}+2\kappa\Big)e^{-t/m}+2m\kappa(\|\mathcal{V}\|_{\infty}+2\kappa)+2m\kappa\Big(\|\Omega^{0}-\mathcal{V}\|_{\infty}+2\kappa\Big)e^{4\kappa t}.

(22)

(3)

(Higher derivatives,
C^{\infty}(0,\infty)
)
For every
integer
n\geq 1
and every
t\geq 0
,

\|\Theta^{(n)}(m,t)-\Theta^{(n)}(0,t)\|_{\infty}\leq C_{n}\left[m^{-n}\Bigl(1+\tfrac{t}{m}\Bigr)^{\!n}e^{-t/m}+m^{1-n}\kappa\Bigl(1+\tfrac{t}{m}\Bigr)^{\!n}e^{4\kappa t-t/m}\right]+C_{n}^{\prime}m\kappa^{n+1}e^{4\kappa t}

(23)

where
C_{n}
depends polynomially on
m\kappa
,

m\|\mathcal{V}\|_{\infty}
,
m\|\Omega^{0}\|_{\infty}
, and
C_{n}^{\prime}
depends polynomially on
\frac{\|\Omega^{0}\|_{\infty}}{\kappa},\frac{\|\mathcal{V}\|_{\infty}}{\kappa}
(with the polynomial dependence depending on
n
).

Proof.

Lemma
3.3
reduces the proof to a direct application
of
[
CDHR25a
, Proposition 3.2]
. We spell out the reduction.

Fix the Winfree initial data
(\Theta^{0},\Omega^{0})
and let

(\Phi^{0},\dot{\Phi}^{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{4N}\times\mathbb{R}^{4N}
be the embedded Kuramoto
initial data defined by Lemma
3.3
. Write
\Phi(m,t)

and
\Phi(0,t)
for the solutions of the second-order Kuramoto
system (
19
) and (
20
) respectively, both starting from

\Phi^{0}
(for the first-order system, use
\Phi^{0}
as the initial
phase; for the second-order system, use
(\Phi^{0},\dot{\Phi}^{0})
), with
\tilde{\kappa}=2\kappa
.
By Lemma
3.3
, for both
m>0
and
m=0
the components of

\Phi
satisfy
\varphi_{i}=\theta_{i}
,
\varphi_{N+i}=-\theta_{i}
, and

\varphi_{2N+k}\equiv 0
for all
t\geq 0
. In particular, similar to
Remark
3.4
,

\|\Theta^{(n)}(m,t)-\Theta^{(n)}(0,t)\|_{\infty}=\frac{1}{2}\|\Phi^{(n)}(m,t)-\Phi^{(n)}(0,t)\|_{\infty}.

(24)

By
[
CDHR25a
, Proposition 3.2]
, we have (denoting
\mathcal{D}(X)\coloneqq\max_{i,j\in[4N]}|x_{i}-x_{j}|
for
X\in\mathbb{R}^{4N}
):

(1)

(Convergence of
\Phi(m,\cdot)
):

\displaystyle\|\Phi(m,t)-\Phi(0,t)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt<m\left|\frac{\max_{i}(\tilde{\omega}_{i}^{0}-\tilde{\nu}_{i})+\min_{i}(\tilde{\omega}_{i}^{0}-\tilde{\nu}_{i})}{2}\right|+\frac{1}{2}m\Big(\mathcal{D}(\tilde{\Omega}^{0}-\tilde{\mathcal{V}})+2\tilde{\kappa}\Big)e^{2\tilde{\kappa}t}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=0+m\Big(\|\Omega^{0}-\mathcal{V}\|_{\infty}+2\kappa\Big)e^{4\kappa t}

so that by (
24
),

\|\Theta(m,t)-\Theta(0,t)\|_{\infty}<\frac{1}{2}m\Big(\|\Omega^{0}-\mathcal{V}\|_{\infty}+2\kappa\Big)e^{4\kappa t}.

(2)

(Convergence of
{\dot{\Phi}}(m,\cdot)
):

\displaystyle\|\dot{\Phi}(m,t)-\dot{\Phi}(0,t)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt<\Big(\|\tilde{\Omega}^{0}-\tilde{\mathcal{V}}\|_{\infty}+\tilde{\kappa}\Big)e^{-t/m}+m\tilde{\kappa}(\mathcal{D}(\tilde{\mathcal{V}})+2\tilde{\kappa})+m\tilde{\kappa}\Big(\mathcal{D}(\tilde{\Omega}^{0}-\tilde{\mathcal{V}})+2\tilde{\kappa}\Big)e^{2\tilde{\kappa}t}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=\Big(\|\Omega^{0}-\mathcal{V}\|_{\infty}+2\kappa\Big)e^{-t/m}+4m\kappa(\|\mathcal{V}\|_{\infty}+2\kappa)+4m\kappa\Big(\|\Omega^{0}-\mathcal{V}\|_{\infty}+2\kappa\Big)e^{4\kappa t}

so that by (
24

\|\dot{\Theta}(m,t)-\dot{\Theta}(0,t)\|_{\infty}<\frac{1}{2}\Big(\|\Omega^{0}-\mathcal{V}\|_{\infty}+2\kappa\Big)e^{-t/m}+2m\kappa(\|\mathcal{V}\|_{\infty}+2\kappa)+2m\kappa\Big(\|\Omega^{0}-\mathcal{V}\|_{\infty}+2\kappa\Big)e^{4\kappa t}.

(3)

(Convergence of
\Phi^{(n)}(m,\cdot)
with

For

, we have

\displaystyle\|\Phi^{(n)}(m,t)-\Phi^{(n)}(0,t)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\leq(n-1)!\left(2\tilde{\kappa}+\|\tilde{\Omega}^{0}\|_{\infty}+\|\tilde{\mathcal{V}}\|_{\infty}+\frac{9}{8m}\right)^{n}\left(1+\frac{t}{m}\right)^{n}e^{-t/m}

\displaystyle\hskip 19.91684pt+\frac{9}{8}m\tilde{\kappa}\cdot n!e^{2\tilde{\kappa}t}\left(2\tilde{\kappa}+\mathcal{D}(\tilde{\Omega}^{0})+\mathcal{D}(\tilde{\mathcal{V}})+\frac{9}{8m}\right)^{n}\left(1+\frac{t}{m}\right)^{n}e^{-t/m}

\displaystyle\hskip 19.91684pt+\frac{3}{4}m\tilde{\kappa}\cdot(n+1)!e^{2\tilde{\kappa}t}\Big(2\tilde{\kappa}+\mathcal{D}(\tilde{\Omega}^{0})+\mathcal{D}(\tilde{\mathcal{V}})\Big)^{n}(1-e^{-t/m})

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\leq(n-1)!\left(4\kappa+\|\Omega^{0}\|_{\infty}+\|\mathcal{V}\|_{\infty}+\frac{9}{8m}\right)^{n}\left(1+\frac{t}{m}\right)^{n}e^{-t/m}

\displaystyle\hskip 19.91684pt+9m\kappa\cdot n!2^{n-2}e^{4\kappa t}\left(2\kappa+\|\Omega^{0}\|_{\infty}+\|\mathcal{V}\|_{\infty}+\frac{9}{16m}\right)^{n}\left(1+\frac{t}{m}\right)^{n}e^{-t/m}

\displaystyle\hskip 19.91684pt+3m\kappa\cdot(n+1)!2^{n-1}e^{4\kappa t}\Big(2\kappa+\|\Omega^{0}\|_{\infty}+\|\mathcal{V}\|_{\infty}\Big)^{n}(1-e^{-t/m}),

so that

\displaystyle\|\Theta^{(n)}(m,t)-\Theta^{(n)}(0,t)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\eqref{eq:equal-norms}}}}{{\leq}}\frac{1}{2}\|\Phi^{(n)}(m,t)-\Phi^{(n)}(0,t)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\leq\frac{1}{2}(n-1)!m^{-n}\left(4m\kappa+m\|\Omega^{0}\|_{\infty}+m\|\mathcal{V}\|_{\infty}+\frac{9}{8}\right)^{n}\left(1+\frac{t}{m}\right)^{n}e^{-t/m}

\displaystyle\hskip 19.91684pt+9m\kappa\cdot n!2^{n-3}e^{4\kappa t}m^{-n}\left(2m\kappa+m\|\Omega^{0}\|_{\infty}+m\|\mathcal{V}\|_{\infty}+\frac{9}{16}\right)^{n}\left(1+\frac{t}{m}\right)^{n}e^{-t/m}

\displaystyle\hskip 19.91684pt+3m\kappa\cdot(n+1)!2^{n-2}e^{4\kappa t}\kappa^{n}\Big(2+\frac{\|\Omega^{0}\|_{\infty}}{\kappa}+\frac{\|\mathcal{V}\|_{\infty}}{\kappa}\Big)^{n}(1-e^{-t/m}).

One can check that this is in the stated form.

∎

4.

1.1

Throughout this section
\Theta(t)
denotes the solution

R(t)=R(\Theta(t))
. The proof of

1.1
is organized into three stages, namely initial

4.1.
A priori partial trapping

We first begin by proving that under an a priori lower bound on the order parameter, one can control a partial cluster. Below,
\mathcal{B}\subset[N]
denotes a subset of oscillators which we wish to control.

Lemma 4.1

(A priori partial trapping)
.

Let
\mathcal{B}\subset[N]
,
\eta>0
,
\rho>0
, and
T\in(\eta m,\infty]
, and suppose
R_{\mathcal{B}}(t)\geq\rho
for

t\in[\eta m,T)
. Let
x\in(-1,1)
satisfy

1-x^{2}\geq\left(\frac{\|\Omega_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}\|_{\infty}e^{-\eta}+\|\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{B}}\|_{\infty}+2\kappa\left(\frac{|\mathcal{B}|}{N}m\left(\|\Omega_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}\|_{\infty}(\eta\vee 1)e^{-(\eta\vee 1)}+M_{F,\mathcal{B}}\right)+\frac{N-|\mathcal{B}|}{N}\right)}{\kappa\rho(1-e^{-\eta})}\right)^{\!2},

(25)

where
z\vee w\coloneqq\max\{z,w\}
.
Let
y\in[-|x|,|x|]
.

(1)

If
i\in\mathcal{B}
is such that
\cos\theta_{i}(t_{0})\geq y
for
some
t_{0}\in[\eta m,T)
, then
\cos\theta_{i}(t)\geq y
for all

t\in[t_{0},T)
. In particular,
\mathrm{osc}_{[\eta m,T)}\theta_{i}<2\pi
, where
\mathrm{osc}_{I}f(t)\coloneqq\sup_{t\in I}f(t)-\inf_{t\in I}f(t)
for an interval
I
and a function
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
.

(2)

For all
i\in\mathcal{B}
, we have
\mathrm{osc}_{[\eta m,T)}\theta_{i}<4\pi

4.2.
Criterion for partial oscillator death

We now state and prove the main criterion for partial oscillator death. This involves an order parameter bootstrapping argument of
[
Ryo26
]
. Below, now there are two subsets
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{B}\subset[N]
. The smaller set
\mathcal{A}
is a subset that we know that the oscillators are in some neighborhood of

and which we wish to have detailed control over, and the larger set
\mathcal{B}
are oscillators whose locations we do not know precisely but which we wish to have some loose control over.

Proposition 4.3

(Criterion for partial oscillator death)
.

Let
\Theta(t)
be the solution to (
4
), and let

\eta>0
,
t_{0}\geq\eta m
,
\rho\in(0,2]
,

, and

\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{B}\subset[N]
. Assume (
25
) and assume that at time

,

\cos\theta_{i}(t_{0})\geq x\ \ \text{for every }i\in\mathcal{A},\qquad\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{A}}\bigl(1+\cos\theta_{i}(t_{0})\bigr)>\frac{2\rho}{1+|x|}.

Then:

(1)

for every

;

(2)

\cos\theta_{i}(t)\geq x
for every
i\in\mathcal{A}
and

, so that
\operatorname{osc}_{[t_{0},\infty)}\theta_{i}<2\pi
for every

(3)

\operatorname{osc}_{[t_{0},\infty)}\theta_{i}<4\pi
for every

.

Proof.

Items (1) and (2).
Define

\mathcal{T}:=\{T>t_{0}\colon R_{\mathcal{A}}(t)\geq\rho\text{ for all }t\in[t_{0},T)\}
.
By (

),

R_{\mathcal{A}}(t_{0})\geq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{A}}(1+\cos\theta_{i}(t_{0}))>\frac{2\rho}{1+|x|}>\rho
,
so by continuity
\mathcal{T}\neq\emptyset
; set
T^{\star}:=\sup\mathcal{T}\in(t_{0},\infty]
.
For
t\in[t_{0},T^{\star})
we have
R_{\mathcal{B}}(t)\geq R_{\mathcal{A}}(t)\geq\rho
, so
Lemma
4.1
(1) applies; it yields

\cos\theta_{i}(t)\geq x
for
i\in\mathcal{A}
,
t\in[t_{0},T^{\star})
, and
\operatorname{osc}_{[t_{0},T^{\star})}\theta_{i}<2\pi
, which
is item (2) but restricted to
[t_{0},T^{\star})
. So to prove items (1) and (2), it is enough to prove

.

We observe that for

and

.

If
\cos\theta_{i}(t_{0})\leq|x|
, then

4.1
applied with

gives

, so

1+\cos\theta_{i}(t)\geq 1+\cos\theta_{i}(t_{0})\geq\frac{1+|x|}{2}(1+\cos\theta_{i}(t_{0}))

since
\frac{1+|x|}{2}\leq 1
.
If
\cos\theta_{i}(t_{0})>|x|
, then Lemma

with

gives

, so

\displaystyle R_{\mathcal{A}}(t)\geq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{A}}(1+\cos\theta_{i}(t))\geq\frac{1+|x|}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{A}}(1+\cos\theta_{i}(t_{0}))>\frac{1+|x|}{2}\cdot\frac{2\rho}{1+|x|}=\rho,\quad t\in[t_{0},T^{\star}).

, then by definition of
\mathcal{T}
and continuity of

R_{\mathcal{A}}(T^{\star})=\lim_{t\to T^{\star}-}R_{\mathcal{A}}(t)\geq\frac{1+|x|}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{A}}(1+\cos\theta_{i}(t_{0}))>\frac{1+|x|}{2}\cdot\frac{2\rho}{1+|x|}=\rho,

a contradiction. Hence

, and items (1) and (2) hold for all

.

Item (3).
This follows from Lemma
4.1
(2).

6.
Comments on volumetric and Lyapunov functional arguments

In the first-order model (
2
)-(
3
), it is known that for Lebesgue-a.e. initial data
\Theta^{0}
,
\kappa>2\|\mathcal{V}\|_{\infty}
guarantees oscillator death
[
Ryo26
, Theorem 1]
. There, the proof was to first establish a bound on the pathwise critical coupling strength
[
Ryo26
, Corollary 16]
, as done in Theorem
1.1
, and then to invoke volumetric arguments regarding the divergence of the vector field defining the first-order Winfree model. We were unable to replicate this result in the inertial Winfree model (
4
) and will describe some possible approaches.

The first-order Winfree model (
2
)–(
3
) can be viewed as a dynamical system on the compact torus
\mathbb{T}^{N}
. Likewise, the inertial Winfree model (
4
) can be viewed as a dynamical system on the compact phase space given by the
cylinder

\mathcal{M}\ \coloneqq\ \mathbb{T}^{N}\times\overline{B_{\lambda}},\qquad\overline{B_{\lambda}}\coloneqq\{p\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:\|p\|_{\infty}\leq\lambda\},\qquad\lambda\coloneqq\|\Omega^{0}\|_{\infty}+\|\mathcal{V}\|_{\infty}+2\kappa,

to which the flow of (
4
) descends in the
\theta
-variables
and which is invariant in the
p
-variables by the speed-limit
Lemma
2.5
.

6.1.
A volumetric argument

The first-order model (
2
)–(
3
) gives the flow of the vector field

X\ =\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bigl(\nu_{i}-\kappa R(\theta)\sin\theta_{i}\bigr)\partial_{\theta_{i}},

the divergence of which is

\operatorname{div}X=\kappa\left(NR(1-R)+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sin^{2}\theta_{i}\right)\geq\kappa NR(1-R).

So the flow expands in the region
R<1
. Therefore, excluding a measure-zero set of initial data, the flow must eventually reach a state at which
R\geq 1
, at which we can use the pathwise oscillator death theorem
[
Ryo26
, Corollary 16]
. This gives
[
Ryo26
, Theorem 1]
.

However, for the second-order model (
4
), on the phase space
\mathbb{T}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
the vector field is given by

X\ =\ \sum_{i=1}^{N}p_{i}\,\partial_{\theta_{i}}\ +\ \sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{m}\bigl(-p_{i}+\nu_{i}-\kappa R(\theta)\sin\theta_{i}\bigr)\partial_{p_{i}},

where
p_{i}\coloneqq\dot{\theta}_{i}
, the divergence of which is
-\frac{N}{m}
. Since this is nowhere positive, we can only see that the flow is contractive. Thus we cannot deduce instability of states with small order parameter; it might even by contracting onto a smaller-dimensional submanifold. One may imagine modifying the volume form or Riemannian metric to obtain a better divergence, then restrict to the compact cylinder
\mathcal{M}\ \coloneqq\ \mathbb{T}^{N}\times\overline{B_{\lambda}}
to prove almost-sure convergence results.

We provide one obstruction: we show that we cannot work with volume forms depending only on
R
that give divergences that depend only on
R
, except for the trivial case of Lebesgue measure with constant negative divergence.

Lemma 6.1

(Obstruction for
R
-only volume forms)
.

Let
\mu=h(R(\theta))\,d\theta\wedge dp
with
h\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0};\mathbb{R}_{>0})
.
Then

\operatorname{div}_{\mu}X\ =\ -\frac{N}{m}\ -\ \frac{h^{\prime}(R)}{h(R)}\,S(\theta,p),\qquad S(\theta,p)\coloneqq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}p_{i}\sin\theta_{i}.

(40)

In particular,
\operatorname{div}_{\mu}X
is a function of
R
alone
if and only if
h
is constant, in which case
\operatorname{div}_{\mu}X\equiv-N/m
.

Proof.

For a volume form
\mu=h\,d\theta\wedge dp
with
h>0
, one has

\operatorname{div}_{\mu}X=\tfrac{1}{h}\,\nabla\!\cdot\!(hX)
, where

\nabla\!\cdot
is the Euclidean divergence in the coordinates

(\theta,p)
. Since
h(R)
is independent of
p
,

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\partial_{p_{i}}\!\Bigl[h(R)\cdot\tfrac{1}{m}(-p_{i}+\nu_{i}-\kappa R\sin\theta_{i})\Bigr]\ =\ -\frac{N}{m}h(R).

For the
\theta
-derivatives,

\partial_{\theta_{j}}R=-\tfrac{1}{N}\sin\theta_{j}
, and so

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\partial_{\theta_{i}}\bigl[h(R)p_{i}\bigr]\ =\ \sum_{i=1}^{N}p_{i}\,h^{\prime}(R)\,\partial_{\theta_{i}}R\ =\ -h^{\prime}(R)\,S(\theta,p).

Dividing by
h(R)
gives (
40
). The last assertion is
immediate: for fixed
\theta
with some
\sin\theta_{j}\neq 0
, the
map
p\mapsto S(\theta,p)
is a nonzero linear function of
p
, so
(
40
) depends only on
R
iff
h^{\prime}(R)/h(R)\equiv 0
, i.e.
h
is constant.
∎

Motivated by the first-order case, one might ask if
\operatorname{div}_{\mu}X
could be
lower bounded
by a function of
R
alone. However, this might not be possible if the dynamics of (
4

7.
Concluding remarks and open problems

We have proved two synchronization theorems for the second-order
Winfree model: a pathwise oscillator-death theorem
(Theorem
1.1

R_{0}^{3/2}
scaling, and a
qualitative zero-inertia synchronization theorem
(Theorem

first-order Winfree results of

Ryo26
]
and the

of

]
. Along the way we proved
a quantitative higher-order Tikhonov theorem
(Proposition
3.5

(1)

R_{0}
-independent pathwise constants.

The constants

not sharp. More importantly, the

-dependence is limiting, as Theorem
1.1
does not give a uniform bound that works for all initial data. We pose the following conjecture.

Conjecture 7.1

There exist absolute constants
a,b,c>0
with the following property. For any initial data

(\{\theta_{i}^{0}\}_{i=1}^{N},\{\omega_{i}^{0}\}_{i=1}^{N})

(\{\nu_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N},\kappa,m)

\frac{\|\mathcal{V}\|_{\infty}}{\kappa}<a,\qquad m\kappa<b,\qquad\frac{\|\Omega^{0}\|_{\infty}}{\kappa}<c,

(43)

the solution

(2)

Of course, Conjecture
