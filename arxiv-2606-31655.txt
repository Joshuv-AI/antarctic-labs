Local magnon modes studied by dynamic magnetic pair-density function analysis
Shin-ichi Shamoto, Yukio Yasui, Kazuki Iida, Yasuhiro Inamura, Motoyuki Ishikado, Mitsutaka Nakamura, Lieh-Jeng Chang

Abstract
The dynamic magnetic pair-density function (DymPDF) D M ​ ( r , E ) D_{\rm M}(r,E) is obtained via the Fourier transform of the dynamic magnetic structure factor, S M ​ ( Q , E ) S_{\rm M}(Q,E) , which is measured using nonpolarized inelastic neutron scattering. While there is a long history of magnetic excitation studies with S M ​ ( Q , E ) S_{\rm M}(Q,E) , there are no reports on D M ​ ( r , E ) D_{\rm M}(r,E) . In this study, we examine simple magnet models and representative magnet examples, such as FeTiO 3 and YBa 2 Cu 3 O 6 , to investigate the real-space dynamics of D M ​ ( r , E ) D_{\rm M}(r,E) . We derive the D M ​ ( r , E ) D_{\rm M}(r,E) equations for simple magnet models in a low energy limit. By comparing these equations to the simulations, we demonstrate the characteristic energy dependence of real-space local magnon modes, including the transition of the magnon mode from acoustic to optical. Our novel analysis reveals the local magnon modes accompanied by a sign change in each spin-pair correlation at a given energy in nanoscale real space even under non-periodic conditions. This method is unique for studying local magnetic dynamics. Subject Areas: dynamic magnetic pair-density function, inelastic neutron scattering, real-space spin dynamics, magnon mode transition



I
Introduction

A magnon is described as a collective excitation with a dispersion in reciprocal space. Inelastic neutron scattering is a powerful tool for directly observing the magnon dispersion as a function of momentum and energy. However, this method has not been widely used to study local spin dynamics, despite the long-standing use of the Fourier transform method of pair distribution function analysis
Egami2019
to study local structures in liquids and amorphous materials. We developed the dynamic magnetic pair-density function (DymPDF) analysis
Iida2022
using the state-of-the-art inelastic neutron scattering spectrometer, 4SEASONS
Kajimoto2011
;
Nakamura2009
, at the Materials and Life Science Experimental Facility (MLF). The MLF is a modern high-intensity pulsed neutron source at the Japan Proton Accelerator Research Complex (J-PARC).

Studying the local dynamics is essential for understanding the physical properties of clustered materials at the nanoscale level. A small cluster domain may evolve in a medium where competing phases degenerate in energy. This degeneracy can be lifted by altering the physical conditions, such as temperature and pressure. For instance, local lattice dynamics have been examined using the Fourier transform of the lattice dynamic structure factor,
S_{\rm L}(Q,E)
, also referred to as dynamic pair density function (DyPDF) analysis
Egami2019
. This analysis has revealed local phonon properties in various materials. These have been observed in silica glass
Hannon1992
, high-
T_{c}
cuprates
Arai1995
, relaxor ferroelectrics
Dmowski2008
, and the superfluid of
4
He
Dmowski2017
. A technical review of DyPDF is also available to check the worldwide availability of inelastic neutron scattering (INS) spectrometers
Acosta2023
. The magnetic version is the dynamic magnetic pair-density function (DymPDF) analysis
Iida2022
. The obtained DymPDF reveals the local dynamic spin-pair correlations at the corresponding bond lengths in real space. These correlations include information about the spin exchange interaction of the static magnetic structure. This method can also easily access the static magnetic pair-distribution function in a low energy limit. In this limit, it is usually difficult to separate the magnetic component from the atomic lattice component without polarized neutron scattering, because these components were simultaneously observed using nonpolarized INS. In this study, the components were separated using the
Q
-dependence difference between the magnon and phonon at finite transfer energy. In the DyPDF analysis, a sign change in the correlation peak corresponds to a phonon mode transition from acoustic to optical modes. Similarly, the DymPDF sign changes at the magnon mode transition from acoustic to optical modes.

Meanwhile, the meaning of the DymPDF
D_{\rm M}(r,E)
is not well known. To improve understanding, we present simple examples of magnet models for the DymPDF analysis. The first is the one-, two-, and three-dimensional ferromagnetic and antiferromagnetic simple magnet models. The second is an in-plane ferromagnet of FeTiO
3

Iida2022
. The third is a two-dimensional (2D) antiferromagnet YBa
2
Cu
3
O
6
. Finally, we will discuss its capabilities and limitations under the present approximation.

Dynamic magnetic pair-density function

The minimum component of elastic nuclear neutron scattering is composed of two atoms, ex. O and M. The phase difference
\Delta\phi
between incident (
\bf{k_{i}}
) and final (
\bf{k_{f}}
) neutrons is given by the vector

from O to M as,

\displaystyle\Delta\phi=(\bf{k_{f}-k_{i}})\cdot\bf{r}.

(1)

A Bragg reflection is observed as a peak when the phase difference,
\Delta\phi
becomes
2\pi n
, where
n
is an integer. Standard diffraction can be considered the sum of the pair reflections.
In the case of inelastic neutron scattering, the atomic pair correlation becomes a vector pair correlation for both of phonons and magnons at a finite energy transfer
E
. The scattering length of a nucleus is accompanied by a moving direction vector. In magnetic scattering, the magnetic moment is a vector, even in elastic scattering. Furthermore, in magnetic INS, the magnetic moment vector is accompanied by a fluctuating direction vector. In the acoustic phonon mode, the nearest-neighbor nuclei move in the same direction. In the optical phonon mode, nearest-neighbor nuclei move in opposite directions. The magnon situation is the same as the phonon situation in the classical limit. In the acoustic magnon mode, the nearest-neighbor magnetic moments fluctuate in the same direction. In the optical magnon mode, the nearest-neighbor magnetic moments fluctuate in the opposite direction
Iida2022
. This difference in modes is important for the local spin dynamics.

Summary

Using the dynamic magnetic pair-density function analysis, we studied simple Heisenberg model magnets in one-dimensional chains, two-dimensional square-lattices, and three-dimensional cubic-lattices, as well as ferromagnets, antiferromagnets, and representative magnets such as in-plane ferromagnet FeTiO
3
and two-dimensional square-lattice antiferromagnet YBa
2
Cu
3
O
6
.

We derived the dynamic magnetic pair-density functions (DymPDFs) of these magnets in a low energy limit. Our focus was on the phase factor of
D_{\rm M}(r,E)
. For simple model magnets, we can discuss the magnon stiffness constant using the simulated DymPDF. Although the simple approximation has limitations when estimating the magnon parameters for real magnet measurements, the magnon top energy and magnon mode transition energy provide meaningful information.

The DymPDF reveals unique features, such as the magnon mode transition from acoustic to optical modes. According to the magnetic model simulations, the magnon mode transition is exclusively related to ferromagnetic spin-pair correlations, regardless of dimensionality, except for the top energy of the antiferromagnet dispersion. Under the approximation of the 0th-order Bessel function
J_{0}(x)
for
x\gg 1
, the magnon phase factor exhibits a unique
-\pi/4
phase shift in
D_{\rm M}(r,E)
depending on dimensionality for ferromagnets. A one-dimensional ferromagnet does not exhibit the phase shift. A two-dimensional ferromagnet shows a
-\pi/4
phase shift, whereas three-dimensional ferromagnet shows a
-\pi/2
phase shift. Additionally, the magnon phase factor also exhibits systematic
r
-dependence of
D_{\rm M}(r,E)
depending on dimensionality for ferromagnets after including the
C(r)
coordination number.
The whole
D_{\rm M}(r,E)
increases with 1,
\sqrt{r}
, and
r
from one, two, to three-dimensions, respectively. This
r
-dependence may be imaged as a magnetic form factor in a magnetic diffraction pattern. Although we do not take into account the local atomic structure, the
r
-dependence appears in the middle of the magnon band energy in our simulations.
Due to the current low-energy approximation, the calculated equations may not apply to spin gap magnets.
Further improving the approximation is an interesting way to study the
D_{\rm M}(r,E)
function. Here, we discussed only single magnetic moment in a system. Since there are many multiple magnetic moment systems, it is also interesting way to improve the present discussion with multiple magnetic moments.

As with representative magnets, the derived
D_{\rm M}(r,E)
function can be used to fit the observed
D_{\rm M}(r,E)
in the case of FeTiO
3
. The fittings successfully reproduce the exchange sign and energy dependence for nearest neighbor spin-pair correlation. However, the estimated parameters using two-dimensional ferromagnet equation are not reasonably matches with the expected values, which can be attributed to insufficient approximation in the present analysis. If we apply a correction factor to the magnon stiffness constant based on the current simulation, the estimated value becomes fairly consistent to the expected value. On the other hand, one-dimensional ferromagnet equation fit to FeTiO
3
result yields good consistent parameters.
In the case of YBa
2
Cu
3
O
6
, the nearest neighbor correlation is detected to be antiferromagnetic even for the quantum spin system with a wide magnetic excitation energy.

We applied a classical calculation to simple Heisenberg models. Quantum effects such as the spin excitation continuum were not considered. These effects are intriguing when considering the present DymPDF as a local mode.
Anyway, this DymPDF analysis provides a basic foundation for studying local spin-pair correlations. This makes it attractive for interdisciplinary research on exotic, dynamic magnetic cluster states, such as superconducting spin resonance modes. Our DymPDF analysis is an indispensable tool for studying these emergent magnetic clusters in various materials, even under nonperiodicity.
