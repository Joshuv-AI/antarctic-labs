Comprehensive Consideration of Ethics in AI-assisted Scientific Writing and Peer Review
Authors: Jin-Hong Yoo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe emergence of large language models and generative artificial intelligence (AI) is driving fundamental transformations in the ecosystem of scholarly publishing and peer review. As manuscript production enters an era of sophisticated technological assistance, it has become imperative to transition from traditional approaches focused on misconduct prevention toward a more proactive ethical framework. We propose a new standard centered on transparency, accountability, and confidentiality, presenting a clear solution to challenges associated with integrating AI into academic discourse. Regarding authorship, AI cannot be credited as an author; the final accountability for academic integrity lies only with human authors. Transparency is maintained through a tiered disclosure framework that mandates reporting based on the extent of artificial intelligence utilization. In the context of peer review, while the potential of artificial intelligence to optimize efficiency is recognized, its application must be restricted to a closed security system to safeguard against data breaches. Furthermore, this review highlights new risk factors such as algorithmic sycophancy and prompt injection attacks, emphasizing that a final verification through human expertise is essential to ensuring the integrity of the peer review process. In conclusion, we present a comprehensive regulatory revision roadmap integrating the authors’ obligations for transparent information disclosures, reviewers’ commitment to confidentiality and security, and editors’ ethical oversight. This framework does not regard AI as an object of absolute prohibition but rather positions it as an advanced scholarly aid rooted in human intellectual accountability. The perspectives in this Special Issue will provide a practical framework to maintain academic rigor and enhance institutional trust in the era of AI.Keywords: Artificial Intelligence, AI, Large Language Model (LLM)

PARADIGM SHIFTS IN THE SCHOLARLY ECOSYSTEM AND NEW ETHICAL DIMENSIONS OF ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE (AI)

The rapid development of large language models and generative AI is transforming the production and verification of academic research. Scientific records, once the sole product of human intellectual labor, have transitioned into a stage of significant machine-assisted collaboration in knowledge sharing. In response to these changes, the Journal of Korean Medical Science (JKMS) presents this Special Issue to re-evaluate ethical standards for AI-assisted scientific writing. This review clarifies core concepts and establishes outlines for subsequent scholarly discourse in this evolving field.

Traditional research ethics prioritized the prevention of fabrication, falsification, and plagiarism; however, the integration of AI shifts the focus toward ensuring transparency, accountability, and confidentiality.1 Specifically, this Special Issue addresses the use of AI by peer reviewers—a widespread yet tacitly overlooked practice that has remained outside the scope of formal ethical discussion.2

It is evident that many peer reviewers already incorporate AI to analyze logical structures, verify technical accuracy, and draft evaluative comments. Such practices should not be dismissed as inherently detrimental; rather, they may represent a pragmatic approach to enhancing efficiency within demanding schedules and an effort to leverage technological advancements to improve the quality of peer review. Consequently, instead of imposing categorical bans, we think that the academic community should prioritize establishing institutional frameworks to ensure the safe and ethical integration of AI.

JKMS collaborated with the Korean Association of Medical Journal Editors (KAMJE) in a dedicated workshop to examine these issues thoroughly. Based on this, we present the following points of view to inform the ongoing refinement of submission guidelines for JKMS and other medical journals in Korea.

ETHICAL RESPONSIBILITY IN AI-ASSISTED AUTHORSHIP

AI supports the research process across multiple stages, including hypothesis formulation, data visualization, and linguistic refinement. Guidelines from the researcher's perspective are based on the Science, Technical and Medical (STM) guidelines and focus on balancing transparency and accountability. The STM framework categorizes AI-assisted tasks into nine distinct types, requiring authors to disclose the specific scope of AI integration in their manuscripts.3 This classification system ensures that AI remains a supportive tool rather than a substitute for human intellect, thereby facilitating the validation of an author's original contribution. Owing to its inherent architecture, generative AI is susceptible to risks such as hallucination, algorithmic bias, and potential copyright infringement through unauthorized citations. Therefore, as AI lacks legal personhood and cannot be held accountable for the accuracy or integrity of research, it does not qualify for authorship; the human author retains full accountability for the integrity of the final manuscript.4,5 Authors are responsible for manually verifying the accuracy of all citations and the logical validity of statistical results within AI-generated content. The non-disclosure of AI-assistance or a failure to correct AI-induced errors that result in the distortion of scientific data will be classified as research misconduct, which may lead to disciplinary actions, including manuscript retraction.6

The use of AI in writing academic papers must be strictly categorized according to its purpose and scope, with disclosure requirements determined by the nature and extent of the AI’s contribution. Mandatory disclosure is required for activities that directly impact research originality, including hypothesis formulation, drafting core manuscript sections, data collection and analysis, and the generation of tables or figures. In contrast, linguistic support—such as grammar correction and sentence refinement—is subject to optional disclosure, provided the author retains full oversight of the final manuscript. Technical tasks, including typographical checks and reference formatting, could be exempt from disclosure obligations.7,8

The boundary between mandatory and optional disclosure depends on whether the AI altered the core logical substance or merely adjusted the linguistic expression. If the AI tool introduces new logical connections, conceptual frameworks, or interpretations not present in the original draft, disclosure is mandatory.

Because linguistic refinement and logical structuring often occur simultaneously within a single prompt, authors must evaluate their disclosure requirements based on the depth of the AI’s influence on the research substance, rather than the literal phrasing of their prompts. In practice, this evaluation is guided by three distinct levels:

1) Technical tasks (exempt from disclosure): Purely routine copyediting processes—such as formatting references to match style guides or correcting typographical and spacing errors—do not alter the content or logic of the manuscript and are entirely exempt from disclosure.

2) Linguistic support (optional): Utilizing AI to correct grammatical errors or refine text into sophisticated academic prose remains at the author’s discretion, provided that the original logical sequence, core arguments, and causal relationships established by the author remain entirely intact.

3) Impact on originality (mandatory): Disclosure is strictly compulsory if the AI actively participates in developing or advancing the scientific claims and core arguments. This includes instances where authors task the AI with structuring a logical conclusion from unrefined findings, or rely on its reasoning capabilities to bridge logical gaps within the Discussion section. Even if introduced inadvertently during a refinement process, any new hypotheses, logical connections, or interpretive frameworks generated by the AI must be transparently declared upon submission.

Transparent AI utilization requires more than a simple acknowledgment of its use; authors must detail the specific nature of the AI’s contribution. To enhance research reproducibility, authors must distinguish between the descriptive and analytical roles of AI. While simple generative tasks require general disclosure, analytical AI applications should be documented extensively. Recommended technical documentation includes the AI tool’s name and version, execution timestamps, and the exact prompts used to generate results.1 These requirements provide a framework for editors to verify procedural transparency, while the author remains strictly accountable for plagiarism and copyright compliance.

AI AND EDITORIAL INTEGRITY IN PEER REVIEW

Establishing AI-related publication ethics for authors is relatively straightforward, as the principle that ‘AI cannot be an author’ serves as a definitive benchmark for most ethical evaluations. However, the ethical framework for peer reviewers presents unique complexities. Since reviewers do not claim authorship, they are not bound by the primary constraints applicable to authors. The peer review process entails labor-intensive tasks, such as verifying reference authenticity and ensuring copyright compliance. In this context, utilizing AI as an automated agent offers a pragmatic appeal by significantly enhancing efficiency and reducing the burden of repetitive evaluation.

This leads to a critical inquiry regarding the permissible limits of AI utilization in peer review and the specific threshold: At what point does it become a “crossing the line” that compromises integrity?

Despite ongoing efforts to reach an academic consensus, significant ambiguities (the gray zone) remain between the adoption of technological advancements and the preservation of evaluative autonomy. Addressing ethical ambiguities and resolving the controversies inherent in this transition—this is the primary objective of the current Special Issue.

While academic consensus is emerging regarding AI use by authors, its application by reviewers presents more intricate challenges related to security and expertise. Peer review remains an unpaid professional contribution, often demanding significant intellectual labor without formal compensation. In an era of rapidly expanding scientific information, relying exclusively on manual evaluation is increasingly inefficient and may compromise review quality. Thus, leveraging AI to enhance procedural efficiency is viewed by many as a pragmatic necessity. However, these benefits are offset by critical concerns regarding confidentiality. Manuscripts are the intellectual property of the authors and must remain strictly confidential prior to publication. Uploading a manuscript to external, server-based generative AI constitutes a fundamental breach of the confidentiality agreement.2 Data uploaded to AI may be incorporated into training sets, posing a significant risk of unauthorized disclosure or the inadvertent inclusion of an author’s original ideas in future AI-generated outputs. Therefore, reviewers should only utilize AI within secure, local environments or through proprietary, closed-loop review platforms established by the journal to ensure data integrity. In the absence of such institutional infrastructure, reviewers must strictly refrain from uploading any unpublished manuscript text, figures, or data into public or commercially open AI models.

AI models, optimized to align with user preferences, frequently exhibit sycophancy—a tendency to generate overly favorable responses rather than objective, critical evaluations.9,10 This propensity poses a significant threat to the rigor of the peer review process. Reviewers must avoid uncritical reliance on AI-generated feedback. Instead, they should employ expert judgment to identify flaws in originality or methodology that AI may overlook.

Furthermore, emerging prompt injection attacks compromise the integrity of academic evaluation, as adversarial commands embedded within manuscript files can subvert AI-assisted analysis to generate biased or fraudulent reviews.11,12

These considerations underscore the indispensable role of expert judgment in the final verification of AI-generated feedback, ensuring that human intuition remains central to the review process. Navigating the AI era requires leveraging automated tools for technical efficiency, while ensuring that the evaluation of research significance remains a human-led endeavor.

Ensuring transparency in peer review requires a systematic disclosure framework. Journals should implement structured checklists for reviewers to categorize the scope of AI use, distinguishing between linguistic support and substantive content generation. Rigorous transparency is essential to delineate the boundary between stylistic editing and evaluative input. Furthermore, to ensure reproducibility, reviewers must document their specific input prompts and the methodologies employed during the AI-assisted process.

Admittedly, technological constraints currently preclude the definitive verification of AI utilization by reviewers, and the limitations of existing detection tools remain evident.8,13,14,15 Nevertheless, codified guidelines are essential as a benchmark for community self-regulation. They establish the ethical and legal grounds for accountability regarding potential data breaches or biased evaluations, while affirming the normative principle that the ultimate responsibility for academic confidentiality remains with the human reviewer.

Although exhaustive codification of every scenario is impractical, reaching a consensus on the core principles of efficiency, confidentiality, and accountability offers the most viable path toward resolving the ethical ambiguities of the AI era.

Ultimately, peer review in the AI era necessitates a paradigm shift: prioritizing the establishment of a culture of transparency over an obsession with imperfect technical detection. Acknowledging the inherent limits of verification while permitting secure AI assistance based on mutual trust—underpinned by a rigorous ethical mandate where reviewers assume full liability for any breach of integrity—represents the most pragmatic and sophisticated framework for the future of academic publishing.

ROADMAP FOR REVISING JKMS SUBMISSION AND PEER REVIEW GUIDE

To safeguard academic integrity, the journal’s submission guidelines have transitioned from elective recommendations to mandatory protocols. Central to this reform is the institutionalization of transparency through the disclosure of AI utilization, requiring authors to specify the tool’s name, version, manufacturer, and date of use. The guidelines preclude AI from authorship, assigning full accountability for the accuracy and ethical compliance of the content to the human author, confirmed via a formal signature. Furthermore, the fabrication or unauthorized manipulation of research data is strictly prohibited. To ensure data integrity, editors are now empowered to request original source data both before and after AI-assisted image processing, effectively mitigating any potential for data manipulation.

The implementation of formal reviewer guidelines is a cornerstone of this reform. While reviewers may utilize AI to enhance efficiency, they are mandated to provide a transparent declaration at the outset of the process. Disclosure requirements are differentiated by the nature of the task—distinguishing between routine linguistic support and substantive analysis—and facilitated through a structured checklist to document input prompts and ensure reproducibility. The primary mandate, however, is preserving the confidentiality of manuscripts. Ultimately, reviewers must refrain from automatically replicating AI outputs, synthesizing all findings based on their own expertise.

Finally, the editorial board’s oversight must be reinforced to address the evolving challenges of the AI era. Editors are mandated to preclude automated decision-making and avoid uncritical reliance on AI-generated recommendations, upholding their ethical responsibilities as the ultimate arbiters of scholarly judgment. These multifaceted reforms will establish a critical precedent, positioning JKMS as a global benchmark for trust and integrity within an AI-integrated academic ecosystem.

CONCLUDING REMARK

While the integration of AI may challenge academic traditions, it serves as a powerful enhancer for progress by redirecting intellectual labor toward more significant endeavors. For clinicians balancing rigorous research with demanding patient care, AI emerges as an invaluable asset that optimizes the research workflow. Technical responsibilities, such as enhancing manuscript quality and identifying minor errors, should be delegated to automated systems. This strategic transition allows human experts to focus on the true essence of research: its clinical significance and societal impact. For this synergy to flourish, the principles of openness and transparency must be firmly established as the foundational cultural values of the modern academic community.

We anticipate this Special Issue as a landmark milestone for both the Korean medical community and the global academic publishing landscape. By addressing the complexities of AI utilization by reviewers, we seek to establish a model of 'progressive publication ethics' that strategically integrates machine intelligence. AI should no longer be viewed as a clandestine tool to be concealed, but rather as a transformative asset to be leveraged for enhancing academic rigor. Nevertheless, it remains an immutable truth that inherent security risks and hallucinations must be governed by responsible human oversight. Even if a future singularity allows AI to fully replicate human cognition, the intellectual contribution and accountability that define the essence of scholarship must remain intact.

The detailed suggestions from the four topics16,17,18,19 following this review will be organically integrated within the comprehensive framework we have presented. When the transparent disclosure of authors, the security compliance of reviewers, and the technical defense systems of editors achieve a tripartite synergy, JKMS will remain a trusted academic journal even in the era of AI. Rather than rejecting AI, we must leverage it to catalyze the evolution of more sophisticated and efficient scholarly communication. This commitment to transparency and accountability represents the hallmark of genuine ethical progress to which we aspire.

Embracing AI’s efficiency must be balanced with a strict adherence to confidentiality and transparency, and disclosure is the core of this reform. This is the strategic trajectory we present to the global academic publishing landscape, as well as the Korean medical community, through this Special Issue.
