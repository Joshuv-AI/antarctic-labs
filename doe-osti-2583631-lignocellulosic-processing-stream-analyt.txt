Task 1: Identify and quantify major components in a set of biomass slurries and solids.

NREL will perform compositional analysis for herbaceous samples consisting of one feedstock
sample and 15 acidic process slurry samples (12 slurries and 3 lignin solids) provided by
ExxonMobil Research and Engineering Company. Details of the standard laboratory analytical
procedures (LAPs) that will be used for these analyses can be found at:
https://www.nrel.gov/bioenergy/biomass-compositional-analysis.html. NREL has published the
history of these methods and the typical uncertainties associated with these methods.1,2

NREL will provide a report detailing the results of the analysis for the samples analyzed. NREL
will also report any unusual results of these analyses.

Compositional Analysis of Feedstock Sample(s)

NREL will perform compositional analysis of herbaceous feedstock samples. In summary, the
standard analysis procedure involves a multi-step analytical procedure involving two-stage
solvent extraction of the biomass samples followed by two-stage acid hydrolysis of the extracted
biomass. Alliance/NREL will measure the following values (all with units of % dry matter): total
water extractives, ethanol extractives, structural carbohydrate (glucan, xylan, galactan, arabinan,
and mannan when appropriate), acetate, protein, lignin (both acid soluble and acid insoluble
(Klason)), and ash. Alliance/NREL will measure the nonstructural carbohydrates sucrose,
fructose, and glucose in the water extractives fraction.

 2

Compositional Analysis of Process Samples

NREL will perform compositional analysis of process slurry samples. In summary, slurry
separation and fraction insoluble solids will be calculated. Separated solids and liquors will be
analyzed as described below.

Fraction Insoluble Solids

NREL will perform a fraction insoluble solids (FIS) analysis on slurries following the NREL
LAPs found here (https://www.nrel.gov/docs/gen/fy08/42621.pdf). Each FIS measurement will
be performed in triplicate. NREL will separate process slurries provided by EMRE into a solid
and liquor portion and wash the solid fraction to remove any entrained liquor. This is required if
the composition of the liquor or solid fraction of the slurry is to be determined. In summary, the
slurry is separated into washed solids and liquid fraction, and the total solids content of the two
phases is used to calculate the fractions of the liquid phase and solids phase so that as-received
composition can be calculated for the separated phases.

Slurry Solids

NREL will perform compositional analysis on solid process samples or the solid fraction of
process slurries. Each sample will be analyzed in duplicate. Details of the standard LAPs that
will be used for these analyses can be found at: https://www.nrel.gov/bioenergy/biomass-
compositional-analysis.html. In summary, the standard analysis involves a multi-step analytical
procedure like “Compositional Analysis of Feedstock Sample” described above, but omitting the
two-stage solvent extraction of the biomass samples. NREL will measure the following values
(all with units of % dry matter): carbohydrates (glucan, xylan, galactan, arabinan), acetate,
protein, lignin (both acid soluble and acid insoluble), and ash.

Slurry Liquids

NREL will perform compositional analysis on the liquor fraction of process slurries. Each
sample will be analyzed in duplicate. Details of the standard LAPs that will be used for these
analyses can be found at: https://www.nrel.gov/bioenergy/biomass-compositional-analysis.html.
In summary, NREL will measure the as-received pH of the samples, neutralize if necessary, and
filter the samples and measure the monomeric carbohydrates (glucose, xylose, arabinose,
galactose, and mannose when appropriate). NREL will also perform an analytical hydrolysis of
the samples using 4% sulfuric acid to hydrolyze oligomeric carbohydrates to their monomeric
forms. The samples will then be neutralized and analyzed. The difference in the measured
monomeric sugar concentration with and without analytical hydrolysis is a measure of
carbohydrate oligomer concentration of a sample. NREL will also analyze the samples for select
organic acids (lactic acid and acetic acid), other analytes and degradation products (glycerol,
ethanol, 5-Hydroxymethylfurfural, and furfural), and measure the acid soluble lignin content of
the liquor samples.

NREL performed compositional analysis on three feedstocks that were provided by EMRE.
EMRE provided 10 slurries, which were analyzed for fraction insoluble solids and separated into
10 liquors and 10 intermediate solids, which were also analyzed. Because these analyses were

Task 3: Thioacidolysis of feedstock and isolated lignin samples.

NREL will perform thioacidolysis analysis on herbaceous samples consisting of one feedstock
sample and three lignin solid samples provided by EMRE. Details of the analysis are provided
below. It is important to note that this method was developed for biomass feedstock samples.
Isolated lignin is not representative of native lignin, as the isolation process almost always
induces changes to the structure (particularly condensation). In addition, there may be other
species present from the isolation process that can hinder the reaction; therefore, it can have a
two-fold impact on the results. That doesn’t mean that the results aren’t meaningful for isolated
lignin, but that they need to be interpreted with caution.

Thioacidolysis of Feedstock and Lignin Solids

Thioacidolysis is a chemical degradation method used to measure the relative abundance of
syringyl (S), coniferyl (or guaiacyl, G), and p-coumaryl (H) units bound by labile linkages (i.e.,
β-O-4) in lignin. Lignin monomers bound by labile linkages undergo thioethylation in the
presence of boron trifluoride and ethanthiol to produce thioethylated S, G, and H monomers that
are separated from the reaction media and quantitated by GC/MS after derivatization. 3,4 NREL
will provide results indicating the relative abundance of labile linkages present in lignin and the
relative abundance of the monomers, S/G/H ratio, and results are reported as µmol/g material
(material being ground biomass or isolated lignin), but can also be expressed per g Klason lignin
content if known.

Thioacidolysis was performed on 2 mg of ground samples as received, as reported in Harman-
Ware et al.:

 Harman-Ware, A. E.; Foster, C.; Happs, R. M.; Doeppke, C.; Meunier, K.; Gehan, J.; Yue,
 F.; Lu, F.; Davis, M., Quantitative Analysis of Lignin Monomers by a Thioacidolysis Method
 Tailored for Higher-Throughput Analysis. Biotechnology Journal 2016, 11, 1268-1273.

 5

Notes: Feedstock 19_161262 sample was wet with microbial growth and had a pungent smell
and was not milled to a fine size. Approximately 10 mg of sample without visible microbial
growth was selected, dried under a nitrogen stream for 2 h at room temperature, and hand-
chopped to a particle size of approximately 1 mm. No further pretreatment on the feedstock
sample was performed. The “wet cake” samples appeared dry and were already milled to fine
particle size and analyzed as received without further pretreatment. Results from the feedstock
sample should be interpreted with caution due to the condition of the sample, and wet cake
sample results may not be reflective of native lignin structure, content, or composition.
Reference standard (poplar) had yield and S/G within historical limits. Typical thioacidolysis
total monomer yields for hardwoods are on the order of 400 μmol/g biomass sample (or around
1000 μmol/g Klason lignin), and grass thioacidolysis yields are typically 100-200 μmol/g
biomass.
