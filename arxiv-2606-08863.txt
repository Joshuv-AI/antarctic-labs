Monotone convergence theorems equivalent to Markov’s principle
Douglas S. Bridges

Abstract
The notions of provisional, negative, and apparent convergence to 0 0 are introduced. It is then shown that Markov’s principle is equivalent, in Bishop-style constructive mathematics, to the statement ‘every decreasing sequence of real numbers provisionally convergent to 0 0 actually converges to 0 0 ’, and that this equivalence holds with ‘provisionally’ replaced by ‘negatively’. Finally, apparent convergence and convergence are related by means of the anti-Specker principle.

Proposition 1

A sequence in
\mathbb{R}
converges to
0
if and only if it
converges to a limit and is provisionally convergent to
0
.

Proof.
Suppose that
\mathbf{x}\equiv\left(x_{n}\right)_{n\geqslant 1}
converges
to
0
, that
\varepsilon>0
, and that
\left|x_{n}-t\right|<\varepsilon
for all sufficiently large
n
. If
\left|t\right|>\varepsilon
, then for all sufficiently large
n
we have
\left|x_{n}\right|<\left|t\right|-\varepsilon
and therefore

\left|t\right|\leqslant\left|x_{n}-t\right|+\left|x_{n}\right|<\varepsilon+\left(\left|t\right|-\varepsilon\right)=\left|t\right|,

which is absurd. Hence
\left|t\right|\leqslant\varepsilon
, and
therefore
\mathbf{x}
is provisionally convergent.

Now let
\mathbf{x}
converge to a limit
t\in\left[0,1\right]
and be
provisionally convergent to
0
. Suppose that
\left|t\right|>0
;
then
\left|x_{n}-t\right|<\left|t\right|/2
eventually,
so, by the provisional convergence of
\mathbf{x}
to
0
,
\left|t\right|\leqslant\left|t\right|/2
, which is absurd. Hence

t=0
.

In the recursive model of

there exists a sequence
\mathbf{s}\equiv\left(s_{n}\right)_{n\geqslant 1}

Lemma 2

Let
x\in\left[0,1\right]
satisfy
\lnot\left(x=1\right)

\left(x^{n}\right)_{n\geqslant 1}
is
provisionally convergent to
0
. If it converges to
0

x<1

Proof.
Let
\varepsilon>0
, and suppose that
\left|x^{n}-t\right|<\varepsilon
for all
n\geqslant N
. If
x\neq 1
, then
x^{n}\rightarrow 0
,
so
\left|t\right|\leqslant\varepsilon
; from which it follows that
if
\left|t\right|>\varepsilon
, then
\lnot\left(x\neq 1\right)
, and therefore
x=1
, a contradiction. Hence, in fact,
\left|t\right|\leqslant\varepsilon
. On the other hand, if
x^{n}\rightarrow 0

then there exists

x^{n}<1
, whence
x=\sqrt[n]{x^{n}}<1

Proposition 3

The following are equivalent over

(i)

(ii)

Every decreasing sequence of nonnegative real numbers that

Proof.
Assume

\mathbf{x\equiv}\left(x_{n}\right)_{n\geqslant 1}
be a decreasing sequence of nonnegative numbers converging provisionally to

0
. Given
\varepsilon>0

x_{n}>\varepsilon

Setting

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}=0

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\forall_{k\leqslant n}\left(\left|x_{k}-x_{1}\right|<\varepsilon\right),

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}=1-\lambda_{n-1}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\left|x_{n}-x_{1}\right|>\varepsilon/2.

Since
\mathbf{x}

, we obtain

such that

\left|x_{n_{2}}-x_{n_{1}}\right|>\varepsilon/2
. Carrying on in
this way, we construct a strictly increasing sequence
\left(n_{k}\right)_{k\geqslant 1}
such that
\left|x_{n_{k}}-x_{n_{k-1}}\right|>\varepsilon/2
for each

is decreasing we now see that

\displaystyle x_{n_{K}}

\displaystyle=x_{n_{1}}+\sum_{k=2}^{K}\left(x_{n_{k}}-x_{n_{k-1}}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant x_{1}-\sum_{k=2}^{K}\left|x_{n_{k}}-x_{n_{k-1}}\right|<x_{1}-\left(K-2\right)\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\leqslant 0,

a contradiction from which we conclude that

\lnot\forall_{n}\left(x_{n}>\varepsilon\right).

Applying

There is another weak notion of convergence that, under

Proposition 4

If a sequence in
\mathbb{R}
is negatively convergent to
0
,
then it is provisionally convergent to
0
.

Proof.
Let the sequence
\mathbf{x}
be negatively convergent to
0
. Given

\varepsilon>0
, suppose that
\left|x_{n}-t\right|<\varepsilon
for
all
n\geqslant N
. If
\left|t\right|>\varepsilon
, then for

n\geqslant N
we have
\left|x_{n}\right|>\left|t\right|-\varepsilon>0
, which contradicts the negative convergence of
\mathbf{x}
to

0

\left|t\right|\leqslant\varepsilon

\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary, we conclude that
\mathbf{x}
is provisionally
convergent to
0

To see that the converse of Proposition
4
is false, take

x_{n}\equiv\left(-1\right)^{n-1}

\left|x_{n}\right|=1

\mathbf{x}
is not negatively convergent to
0

is provisionally convergent to
0

\left|x_{n}-t\right|<\varepsilon<\left|t\right|

\left|1-t\right|<\varepsilon

\left|1+t\right|<\varepsilon
, so

1<\min\left\{\varepsilon-t,\varepsilon+t\right\}<0,

a contradiction.

Proposition 5

A sequence of real numbers converges to
0
if and only if it
converges to a limit and is negatively convergent to
0

Proof.
It is trivial that if
\mathbf{x}\equiv\left(x_{n}\right)_{n\geqslant 1}

converges to
0

0

\mathbf{x}
converges to a limit

0

then it follows from Propositions

{}_{\left[0,1\right]}\vdash

Note that in the recursive model of

Corollary 8

MP

{}_{\left[0,1\right]}\vdash\
A decreasing sequence of nonnegative numbers converges to

Proof.
Let

\mathbf{x}

Conversely, if
