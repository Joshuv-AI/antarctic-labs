Research Traineeship for Diversity in Nuclear Physics at Yale Wright Laboratory

Principal Investigators
Karsten M. Heeger (Principal Investigator)
Reina H. Maruyama (Co-Investigator)
Yale University, Wright Laboratory

Final Report

This report pertains to the grant titled “Research Traineeship for Diversity in Nuclear Physics at
Yale Wright Laboratory.” The award aimed to train and retain a diverse group of future
scientists, including underrepresented, first-generation, and low-income undergraduates, as
prospective nuclear physicists and leaders in science. Yale’s undergraduate demographics are
changing, offering a unique opportunity to identify and support future leaders from this group.
Yale has also invested heavily in training local middle and high school students who then
progress to colleges in the surrounding area and beyond. The program introduced students to the
environment of a leading research university in nuclear science, utilizing existing infrastructure
and collaborations with other campus support organizations to assist students through academics
and research. This program continued the longstanding tradition of excellence in education,
mentoring, and hands-on training at Wright Lab. Participating students engaged in cutting-edge
nuclear physics research within a diverse community, developed professional skills through
workshops and training, enhanced their personal growth, and received personalized mentoring to
pursue careers in STEM or graduate studies. As part of this traineeship, we offered a year-long
research experience, a summer research and training program, and opportunities for participation
and networking within the national nuclear physics community.

This award supported two postbac students in their studies at the Yale Wright Laboratory: Sergio
Nunez Silva and Celin Hildago.
Celine Hildago was a postbac trainee at Yale University from summer 2023 through summer
2024. She was one of two postbac students in the Yale DOE traineeship program. Celine earned
her undergraduate degree from Rutgers University and was interested in gaining research
experience before applying to graduate school. As an immigrant to the US, Celine immersed
herself in her studies and was motivated to become an expert scientist and share her experience
as a new American. She demonstrated great potential to make a lasting impact on US science and
society. Her initiative to seek new experiences and build new skills, along with her
accomplishments during her undergraduate studies at Rutgers, made her stand out and led to her
application to the DOE traineeship at Yale Wright Laboratory.
At Yale, Celine worked in my research group on Project 8, an experiment to measure the mass of
the neutrino. Project 8 aims to determine the absolute neutrino mass by precisely measuring the

beta decay spectrum of tritium using Cyclotron Radiation Emission Spectroscopy (CRES). As
part of her research, Celine conducted simulations to optimize the magnetic trap for electrons in
this decay and carried out measurements to assess the magnetic field requirements for the
experiment. She contributed through simulations and hardware planning for the next phase of
Project 8. During her trainee project, Celine learned new software tools, utilized the High
Performance Computing (HPC) cluster at Yale, and began taking measurements of magnetic
background fields in our lab. She worked daily with scientists in my research group at Yale and
with other collaborators. At the end of her research traineeship, she presented her work to the
research staff and students at the Yale Wright Laboratory. Celine was eager to continue her
studies and applied to graduate school. She was accepted into the Gran Sasso Science Institute, a
leading graduate studies institute in L’Aquila, Italy, where she works on the DarkSide dark
matter search experiment located at Gran Sasso National Laboratory.

The second postbac in the traineeship program was Sergio Nunez Silva. He studied at the
Universidad Nacional de Ingeniería (UNI), Peru, and the Colorado School of Mines. During his
time at Yale, he conducted R&D on the Project 8 neutrino mass experiment. His work focused
on implementing a Mask Region-Based Convolutional Neural Network (Mask R-CNN) to
improve the current reconstructed track algorithm and event classification of low-frequency
electron cyclotron radiation. He generated training data using LOCUST software based on C++,
and he became familiar with High Performance Computing at Yale. He utilized Yale’s
computing resources for creating machine learning training data. He was also involved in
operating and collecting data with magnetometers to characterize the magnetic field inside his
laboratory at Yale, where a test setup for Project 8 was built. After his postbac experience at
Yale, Sergio applied to graduate school and is now pursuing a Master’s degree in theoretical
physics and computing at the University of Bologna, Italy.

Both trainees completed successful traineeships at Yale, where they gained hands-on research
experience, became familiar with simulations and high-performance computing, and contributed
to the design studies and R&D for the Project 8 neutrino mass. They built professional resumes,
improved their presentation skills, and learned about the work of nuclear scientists in the US.
Both students successfully applied to graduate school and are continuing their academic and
research careers at leading institutions.
