1 Introduction
This purpose of this document is to provide a clear derivation of the symmetry determining equations (SDEs)
of the Euler equations to supplement the lecture document [1]. The Euler equations are a coupled system of
three first-order quasilinear hyperbolic partial differential equations (PDEs).
 The Euler equations are a system of partial differential equations (PDEs) for fluid velocity v(t, r), fluid
density ρ(t, r), and fluid pressure P (t, r), where t is time and r is the spatial coordinate. In one-dimension,
the Euler equations are:
  
 ∂ρ ∂ρ ∂v gv
                                        +v      +ρ        +       =0                                        (1a)
 ∂t ∂r ∂r r
  
 ∂v ∂v ∂P
                                    ρ       +v       +       =0                                            (1b)
 ∂t ∂r ∂r
  
 ∂P ∂P ∂v gv
                                         +v      + As (ρ, P )      +      = 0,                              (1c)
 ∂t ∂r ∂r r
where As is the isentropic bulk modulus and g =0, 1, or 2 for planar, cylindrical, or spherical geometry,
respectively. Equation 1a is a statement of the conservation of mass, Eq. 1b states conservation of momentum,
and Eq. 1c states the conservation of energy of the fluid.
 This document is organized as follows: Section 2 demonstrates the derivation of the symmetry determining
equations (SDEs) of the Euler equations, Sec. 3 provides the known solutions to the SDEs, Sec. 4 shows how
to use one of the Lie groups to reduce the Euler equations from a couple set of PDEs to a coupled system of
ODEs, and Sec. 5 provides closing remarks.
