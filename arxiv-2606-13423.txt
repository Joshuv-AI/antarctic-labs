Abstract
The class-breadth conjecture of Leedham-Green, Neumann and Wiegold states that the nilpotency class of any p p -group is at most b ⁡ ( G ) + 1 b(G)+1 , where b ( G ) = max g ∈ G log p [ G : Z G ( g ) ] \displaystyle{b(G)=\max_{g\in G}\log_{p}[G:Z_{G}(g)]} denotes the breadth of G G . While several counter-examples to this conjecture have been found for p = 2 p=2 , it is still open in general for p > 2 p>2 . This article is dedicated to the general case p > 2 p>2 of the conjecture. We propose a generalization for the case p > 2 p>2 , which we prove under some additional conditions.

Introduction

Let
G
be a finite
p
-group. The breadth
b(x)
of an element
x
of a group
G
is defined as
b(x):=\log_{p}|G:Z_{G}(x)|
, where
Z_{G}(x)
denotes the centralizer of
x
in
G
. The breadth
b(G)
of
G
is defined as
\displaystyle{b(G):=\max_{g\in G}b(g)}
.

C. Leedham-Green, P. Neumann, and J. Wiegold in
[
9
]
stated the following conjecture:
\operatorname{cl}(G)\leq b(G)+1
for all
p
-groups
G
(
\operatorname{cl}(G)
denotes the nilpotency class of
G
).

W. Felsch, J. Neubüser, and W. Plesken in
[
4
]
provided a series of counterexamples to the conjecture for
p=2
. Further counterexamples were found by B. Eick, M. Newman, and E. O’Brien in
[
3
]
. In
[
3
]
, they also proved that the analogue of the conjecture for finite-dimensional nilpotent Lie algebras is false for all finite fields; i.e., for every finite field
\mathbb{F}
, there exists a finite-dimensional nilpotent Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
over
\mathbb{F}
such that
\operatorname{cl}(\mathfrak{g})>b(\mathfrak{g})+1
, where
b(\mathfrak{g}):=\max_{a\in\mathfrak{g}}(\dim\mathfrak{g}-\dim Z_{\mathfrak{g}}(a))
and
Z_{\mathfrak{g}}(a):=\{x\in\mathfrak{g}\mid[a,x]=0\}
.

The conjecture has been proved in some special cases; see, for example: if
b(G)\leq 4
in
[
8
,
10
]
, if
b(G)\leq p+1
in
[
5
]
, if
G
is a
p
-group of maximal class in
[
1
]
, if
G
is metabelian in
[
9
]
, and if
G
is not covered by its two-step centralizers in
[
9
]
. See also
[
3
]
for more classes of
p
-groups satisfying the conjecture. A more detailed description of the results related to the class-breadth conjecture can be found in the introduction of
[
4
]
.

In this paper, we study the general case
p>2
of the conjecture.

Let

-group

Consider any

Definition 1

For each subgroup

(

-invariant subgroup

-group, equipped with the restricted action of

Definition 2
.

For each
G
-group

\gamma_{2}^{G}(P):=P^{\prime}[G,P]
.

Definition 3
.

For each
G
-group

, denote
\gamma_{1}^{G}(P):=P
and
\gamma_{i}^{G}(P):=\gamma_{2}^{G}(\gamma_{i-1}^{G}(P))

. We will call
\gamma_{i}^{G}(P)

-lower central series of

. Define

We will call
\operatorname{cl}^{G}(P)

G
-nilpotency class of

.

Definition 4
.

Let
P
be a

-group. A finite sequence of subgroups

,

1.

C_{i+1}\subseteq C_{i}
,

2.

[G,C_{i}]\subseteq C_{i+1}
.

The following two propositions are straightforward, and we omit their proofs.

Proposition 1
.

For each
G
-group
P
and each its
G
-central series
C_{i}
,
0\leq i\leq N
:

1.

C_{i}
are
G
-invariant subgroups of
P
,

2.

\gamma_{i}^{G}(P)\subseteq C_{i-1}
,
\forall 1\leq i\leq N+1
.

Proposition 2
.

In the case
G=\{e\}
, we have that for each
G
-invariant subgroup
H
of
P
,
\gamma_{2}^{G}(H)=H^{\prime}
,
\gamma_{i}^{G}(H)=H^{(i-1)}
, and
\operatorname{cl}^{G}(H)=d(H)
. In the case

, we have that for each
G
-invariant subgroup
H
of

,
\gamma_{2}^{G}(H)=[G,H]
,
\gamma_{i}^{G}(P)=\gamma_{i}(P)
, and
\operatorname{cl}^{G}(P)=\operatorname{cl}(P)
.

Definition 5
.

For each element
x\in P
, define
b^{G}(x):=b_{P_{1}}(x)
. We will call
b^{G}(x)
the
G
-breadth of

.

Definition 6
.

Define
\displaystyle{b^{G}(P):=\max_{x\in P}b^{G}(x)}
. We will call
b^{G}(P)
the
G
-breadth of
P
.

The following lemma is well-known, and we omit its proof.

.

Consider any group
A
and any its subgroups

such that

is a normal subgroup of

. Then
BC=CB=\{bc\mid b\in B,c\in C\}=\{cb\mid b\in B,c\in C\}

The following lemma is straightforward to verify, and its proof is omitted:

For each
G
-invariant subgroup

of

induces an action of

H

.

Definition 7
.

For each

-invariant subgroup

of

and each element

Definition 8

Define

\mathcal{M}^{G}(P):=\min\biggl\{k\biggm|\begin{aligned} &\text{the set }\{x\in P\mid b^{G}(x)>k\}\text{ can be covered}\\

Strengthened Class-Breadth Conjecture for
G
-groups

The class-breadth conjecture for
p>2
-groups can be generalized via the following statement that also generalizes
[
12
, Theorem 1.2]
:

Conjecture 1
.

Consider any prime number
p>2
and any
p
-group
G
. Then for each
G
-group
P
there exists a
G
-central series
C_{i}
,
0\leq i\leq N
of
P
such that:

1.

C_{0}=P
,
C_{1}=\gamma_{2}^{G}(P)
,
C_{N}=\{e\}
,

2.

\log_{p}[C_{i}:C_{i+1}]\leq\mathcal{M}^{G}(P)-i+1
,
\forall 1\leq i\leq N-1
.

Proposition 3
.

Conjecture
1
implies both the class-breadth conjecture and
[
12
, Theorem 1.2]
for
p>2
-groups.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
C_{N-1}\not=\{e\}
. From Properties 1, 2, it follows that
1\leq\log_{p}|C_{N-1}|=\log_{p}|C_{N-1}/C_{N}|\leq\mathcal{M}^{G}(P)-N+2
, therefore,
N\leq\mathcal{M}^{G}(P)+1
and, from Proposition
1
,
\gamma_{\mathcal{M}^{G}(P)+2}^{G}(P)\subseteq\gamma_{N+1}^{G}(P)\subseteq C_{N}=\{e\}
, i.e.
\operatorname{cl}^{G}(P)\leq\mathcal{M}^{G}(P)+1
. In the case
G=Int(P)
, from Proposition
2
we obtain the class-breadth conjecture for
p>2
-groups. To prove
[
12
, Theorem 1.2]
, notice that Properties 1, 2 imply
\log_{p}|\gamma_{2}^{G}(P)|=\log_{p}|C_{1}|=\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\log_{p}[C_{i}:C_{i+1}]\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\max(0,\mathcal{M}^{G}(P)-i+1)\leq\mathcal{M}^{G}(P)(\mathcal{M}^{G}(P)+1)/2
. In the case
G=Int(P)
, from Proposition
2
we obtain
[
12
, Theorem 1.2]
for
p>2
-groups.
∎

In this paper, we prove the following special case of Conjecture
1
:

Theorem 1
.

Consider any
p>2
-group
G
and any
G
-group
P
, let
P_{1}:=G\ltimes P
denotes the respective semidirect product. Assume that there exists a normal and

-invariant subgroup

of

1.

\log_{p}[P:M]\leq 1
,

2.

[G,M]=\{e\}
.

Then

has a
G
-central series

,

as in Conjecture

.

Remark 1
.

When

and

(the statement is trivially true when

) in Theorem

, we can replace

(for example,

Remark 2

Theorem

(b)

Proof.

Part (a) follows from Lemma

, and the fact that

Part (b) follows from
[a,bc]=[a,b][a,c]^{b}

Lemma 4
.

Consider any group
X
and any its subgroups
A,B
, such that
B\trianglelefteq X
. Then
[\langle\!\langle A\rangle\!\rangle^{X},B]=\llbracket A,B\rrbracket^{X}
.

Proof.

From

[\langle\!\langle A\rangle\!\rangle^{X},B]\trianglelefteq X
and, therefore, from

Lemma 5
.

For each
