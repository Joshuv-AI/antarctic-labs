 ABSTRACT

This project investigated the use of a Centrifugal Particle Separation (CPS) system to improve the
durability and operational reliability of outdoor heat exchangers in vapor-compression based heating,
ventilation, air conditioning, and refrigeration (HVACR) systems. The developed method introduced a
novel dust and fouling mitigation approach by integrating an ambient air pre-cleaner that employs
centrifugal forces to remove particulate matter from incoming air before it reaches the heat exchanger.
By reducing the accumulation of dust and debris on coil surfaces, the CPS system aims to lower
maintenance requirements and extend equipment lifespan. The project specifically involves coupling a
centrifugal air pre-cleaner with a single-zone ductless mini-split system and evaluating its effectiveness
in maintaining cleaner coil surfaces. Additionally, the study examined the impact of CPS integration
on system energy performance. Through controlled experimental testing and performance monitoring,
this work seeks to validate CPS-based air pre-cleaning as a practical, efficient solution for mitigating
outdoor coil dust accumulation and fouling in both residential and commercial HVACR applications.

 1. INTRODUCTION

Heating, ventilation, air conditioning, and refrigeration (HVACR) systems represent a major
component of building energy consumption in the United States, accounting for approximately 50
percent of total energy use in commercial and public buildings [1]. These systems operate in a wide
range of environmental conditions, often exposed to outdoor air that contains dust, debris, and other
airborne particulates [2-4]. Over time, the accumulation of these contaminants on outdoor heat
exchanger surfaces leads to fouling, which reduces the operational lifespan of the equipment.
Addressing outdoor coil fouling therefore presents an important opportunity to enhance system
reliability and extend equipment service life.

To address this challenge, the present study explores the use of a CPS apparatus as a novel fouling
prevention technology for HVAC heat exchangers. The CPS concept developed and patented by
Coilpod (U.S. Patent No. 10,816,260) [5], utilizes centrifugal forces to remove dust and particulate
matter from ambient air before it reaches the condenser coil. By pre-cleaning the incoming air stream,
the CPS system aims to minimize contaminant deposition on coil surfaces and maintain stable thermal
performance under real-world conditions.

The primary objective of this project is to integrate the CPS air pre-cleaner with a single zone ductless
mini split heat pump system and to evaluate its influence on system performance. Specifically, the study
aims to:

  Demonstrate visible reductions in coil dust accumulation and fouling compared to conventional
 systems.

  Assess the resulting effects on energy consumption and system efficiency.

Through controlled experimental testing and comparative performance analysis, this work seeks to
demonstrate the CPS-based air pre-cleaning system as a practical and effective solution for outdoor coil
dust accumulation and fouling mitigation in both residential and commercial HVAC applications.

 2. METHODOLOGY

In most HVACR systems, outdoor coils (whether operating as condensers or evaporators) are designed
to permit unrestricted airflow across the coil surfaces to facilitate effective heat transfer between the
working fluid and the surrounding air. While this design enhances thermal performance, it also
increases the coil’s exposure to environmental contaminants. As a result, the accumulation of dust and
debris on coil surfaces is a widespread problem in the HVACR industry, often leading to coil fouling
and a higher frequency of maintenance.

To address this problem, a new filter configuration is implemented as a pre-cleaner for the outdoor coil
in order to evaluate potential improvements in component durability. The pre-cleaner operates based
on the principle of CPS, using centrifugal forces to separate clean air from atmospheric dust and debris,
as illustrated in Fig. 1:

Figure 1: Filtering device utilizing centrifugal forces to separate clean air from atmospheric dust [5].

This effort utilizes the expertise and facilities of BTRIC at ORNL to provide a validation of the
described patented technology. The pre-cleaner conceptual model utilized is the 6” Turbo II (Fig. 2)
that can handle up to 500 CFM.

 Figure 2: The 6” Turbo II pre-cleaners utilized in this work for proof of concept.

A duct is built around the inlet air path of the outdoor coil for mounting the pre-cleaners as shown in

To simulate the dust accumulation, synthetic dust has been prepared using non-carcinogenic mate-
rials. It is composed of bentonite powder <50 μm of diameter to simulate fine dust, ceramic sand with
diameters ranging from 500 - 2000 μm for coarse particles, and cotton liners to account for fibers that
might accumulate on the outdoor coil. The synthetic dust components are shown in Fig. 4.

Multiple tests were performed to assess and validate the performance of the current filter design on the
outdoor coil of the residential units in both cooling and heating modes. For the cooling mode, the tests
were performed at an outdoor temperature between 30 and 31℃. All the tests are consistent in the
room’s starting temperature of 20℃ as well as the temperature setpoint of 28℃. For the heating tests,
the outdoor air temperature was close to 10℃ for all the experiments. Each time the room’s temperature
starts at 15℃ and the heating setpoint is 29℃. The outdoor and indoor units of the vapor compression
system utilized for the experimental tests are shown in Fig. 5:

In the cooling mode, several cases were investigated. The baseline case involved no dust application and
no pre-cleaner. Additional cases included applying 250 grams of dust to the heat exchanger without a pre-
cleaner and applying 500 grams of dust without a pre-cleaner. Further configurations involved duct with
openings at the top or at the front, both without pre-cleaners. Finally, cases were tested with pre-cleaners
installed at either the top or the front of the duct, with and without the application of 500 grams of dust.

Overall, the power consumption when the duct is installed is higher compared to the no-duct scenario,
and more time is required to overcome the initial cooling load. This increase can be attributed to higher
pressure drop across the condensing coil caused by air entering through small duct openings, as well
as temperature rise within the duct. The elevated duct temperature increases the condensing
temperature and reduces the coefficient of performance (COP).

The front opening configuration exhibits lower energy consumption during the initial cooling load
period due to relatively better air distribution across the condenser coil compared to the top opening
configuration. The corresponding profiles are visually explained in Fig. 10.

 4. CONCLUSION

Based on the experimental results and performance analysis, the following conclusions can be drawn:

 1. The application of 500 grams of dust to the heat exchanger did not result in a significant reduction
 in heat pump performance. This outcome is likely attributed to the oversized capacity of the tested
 unit, which was able to compensate for moderate levels of fouling without noticeable degradation
 in performance.

 2. The pre-cleaner effectively prevented a substantial portion of airborne debris and dust particles
 from reaching the heat exchanger surface, demonstrating its potential to mitigate outdoor coil
 fouling and reduce maintenance requirements.

 3. According to the manufacturer’s specifications, the pre-cleaners produce a pressure drop of
 approximately 1 to 2 inches of water column at an airflow rate of 400 cubic feet per minute (CFM).
 This added resistance reduced airflow to the heat exchanger, which in turn negatively affected the
 overall energy performance of the heat pump, as observed in the experimental results.

 4. The current pre-cleaner configuration, featuring a six-inch diameter tube with an open area of
 approximately 20 square inches, may not be suitable for typical residential heat pump systems that
 generally employ coil designs with an open area of ~ 324 square inches. This mismatch in airflow
 capacity can result in excessive pressure losses and reduced efficiency.

 5. Larger systems such as rooftop units (RTUs) and cooling towers appear to be more compatible with
 the tested pre-cleaner design. These systems often operate in industrial or dust-laden environments
 where air pre-cleaning offers clear advantages and where duct connections can be more easily
 accommodated, as shown in Fig. 22.

 6. In heating mode, only the configuration with the pre-cleaner mounted at the front exhibited slightly
 lower energy consumption compared to the baseline case without dust.

 12

 Figure 22: Cooling towers are a better fit for the investigated precleaner.

Overall, the results suggest that while the centrifugal pre-cleaner concept is effective in mitigating dust
deposition and protecting coil surfaces, its integration into small residential systems may introduce
undesirable airflow restrictions. Future work should focus on optimizing the pre-cleaner geometry to
reduce pressure losses and evaluating its performance in larger commercial systems where its benefits
are expected to be more pronounced.
