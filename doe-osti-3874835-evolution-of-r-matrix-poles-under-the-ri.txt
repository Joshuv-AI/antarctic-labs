In R-matrix theory [1,2] the channel radius a is an auxiliary construction: it is chosen for convenience, and the physical
scattering matrix cannot depend on it. In practice, however, parameter sets evaluated at different radii are treated as
unrelated fits. This talk asked what happens if the radius is instead promoted to a continuous flow parameter and the induced
motion of the resonance parameters followed explicitly.

Finite-dimensional projection. Because the exact flow does not preserve a finite pole set, the Riccati velocity field is
projected orthogonally onto the tangent space of the truncated manifold, giving a Gram system G(p)ṗ = b with an a priori
error bound.
Outlook. The picture that emerges is that parameter sets extracted at different channel radii are not independent: they are
points on a single trajectory, related by a known differential law. Hale, Brown and Paris [4] examined this radius dependence
previously, holding low-energy observables fixed and solving algebraically as a → 0; the present formulation instead imposes
invariance of the full collision matrix and yields a differential law for any number of levels. Work since the workshop has applied
the framework to evaluated 5 He J π = 3/2+ parameters and clarified the relationship between the finite-pole representation
and the analytic background. Natural extensions are to higher partial waves and charged channels, where the flow keeps its
Riccati form with (ka)2 → (ka)2 − 2ηka − ℓ(ℓ + 1), and to the multichannel case.
