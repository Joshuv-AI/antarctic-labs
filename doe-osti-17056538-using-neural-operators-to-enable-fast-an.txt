Using Neural Operators to Enable Fast and Accurate Scientific Simulations

 Qingkai Kong (24-ERD-012)

Project Overview
Earthquake simulations are essential for understanding ground shaking and identifying what
caused a seismic event, but high-quality simulations can require substantial computing time. This
cost makes it difficult to quickly explore many possible earthquake sources or Earth models,
especially when observations are incomplete or noisy. This project aimed to develop a new,
neural-operator-based simulation capability that learns from trusted physics simulations and can
then rapidly predict how seismic waves travel through the Earth. The San Francisco Bay Area
(SFBA) provided a realistic and challenging test environment because of its complex geology,
dense seismic network, and frequent small-to-moderate earthquakes. Therefore, we
demonstrate our results on real data from SFBA for estimating the seismic source mechanisms.
We developed and tested models that learn to simulate three-dimensional earthquake
wavefields and use those simulations to estimate earthquake source properties. We evaluated
both time- and frequency-domain approaches; the frequency-domain approach became the
most mature approach. It successfully estimated source mechanisms for 22 of 28 Bay Area
earthquakes within 30 degrees of established catalog solutions, while also improving on a
conventional grid-search comparison. We also identified and reduced an important accuracy
problem in the AI models—their tendency to reproduce lower-frequency waves better than
higher-frequency waves. Finally, we also experimented using the trained neural operator model
for subsurface structure imaging on synthetic data, and a companion full-waveform imaging
study produced a new Bay Area subsurface model that improves agreement between simulated
and observed waveforms. Together, these results demonstrate a practical path toward faster and
accurate seismic simulation that enabling event characterization and imaging Earth structure
through inversions.

Mission Impact
The project strengthens LLNL capabilities at the intersection of high-performance computing,
simulation, and data science. It demonstrates a credible route from high-fidelity spectral-element
simulations to a reusable learned forward operator for seismic applications that would otherwise
require many expensive wave solves. The frequency-domain results show that this operator can
be used with real regional data for source characterization. The resulting capability is relevant to
Threat Preparedness and Response and Multi-Domain Deterrence, as described in the proposal.
Faster source estimation and repeated forward modeling can support the analysis of natural and
man-made seismic events, while the same operator-learning methodology can enable sensitivity
analysis and future uncertainty quantification that are difficult with conventional simulation
alone. Beyond seismology, the workflow—high-fidelity simulation to generate training data,
scalable neural-operator training, systematic error diagnosis, and differentiation through a

learned forward model—is transferable to other PDE-governed LLNL applications. The work
therefore contributes both a seismic capability and experience relevant to broader scientific
simulation and inverse problems.
