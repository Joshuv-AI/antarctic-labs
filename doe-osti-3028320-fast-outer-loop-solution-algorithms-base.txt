I. ACCOMPLISHMENTS

Major goals of the project

Important questions such as uncertainty quantification (UQ), inverse problems (IP), and data
assimilation (DA), are solved by “outer loop” algorithms that require repeated runs of “inner loop”
(forward) simulations. In many applications of interest to the DOE the inner loop involves large
simulations of complex multiphysics systems modeled by differential equations. The cost of solving
outer loop problems by conventional algorithms is at least one order of magnitude larger than the
(already sizeable) cost of running the inner loop simulations.

In order to overcome the resulting computational challenges, this project seeks to develop efficient
solution algorithms for outer loop applications by augmenting traditional numerical analysis and
statistical approaches with machine learning (ML) methodologies. The specific goals are to:
1. Construct efficient surrogates for multiphysics high-fidelity (inner-loop) models, by augmenting
 dynamical-based reduced order modeling with learning-based closure models.
2. Accelerate inner loop simulations using the mathematically rigorous MRI-GARK framework to couple
 a large number of surrogate model steps with a small number of high-fidelity model calls.
3. Accelerate the outer loop dynamical optimization solution using specially trained surrogates in the
 ARRA coupling framework, such as to ensure the accuracy of the optimal solutions.
4. Accelerate outer loop ensemble Kalman filter data assimilation by augmenting small ensembles of
 the high-fidelity model with large ensembles of inexpensive surrogate models, and coupling them in
 the mathematically rigorous MSEnKF framework.
5. Ensure that the accuracy of the computed outer-loop solutions does not depend on surrogate
 accuracy. This is achieved by using mathematically rigorous frameworks to couple surrogate
 models, inner-loop high-fidelity simulations, and outer loop solvers. The overall computational
 efficiency does depend on the tradeoff between cost and accuracy of ML-based surrogates.
6. Implement and validate the new methodologies on an array of test problems.

Accomplishments

The project developed new “numerical+learning” algorithms that can accelerate inner and outer loop
computations. We studied the construction of ML-based surrogates for dynamical models, and their use
to augment full order model simulations in inner and outer loop computations. We seek to build
algorithms with provable accuracy guarantees; the quality of the surrogates impacts the overall
efficiency, but not the size of numerical approximation errors.

In the report below the publications are referenced by their titles.

Activity 1. Accelerate outer loop simulations using physics informed neural network surrogate models for
open and closed loop control.

Accomplishments: A fundamental problem of science is designing optimal control policies that
manipulate a given environment into producing a desired outcome. In the paper “Physics-informed
neural networks for PDE-constrained optimization and control” we develop Control Physics-Informed
Neural Networks (Control PINNs), which simultaneously solve a given system state, and its respective
optimal control, in a one-stage framework that conforms to physical laws of the system. Prior
approaches use a two-stage framework that models and controls a system sequentially, whereas

 2

Control PINNs incorporates the required optimality conditions in its architecture and loss function. The
paper was published.

Activity 2: Accelerate outer loop ensemble Kalman filter data assimilation by augmenting small
ensembles of the high-fidelity model with large ensembles of inexpensive surrogate models.

Accomplishments:
In the paper “Multifidelity Ensemble Kalman Filtering Using Surrogate Models Defined by Theory-guided
Autoencoders” we extend MFEnKF to work with non-linear couplings between the models. Optimal
nonlinear projection and interpolation operators are obtained by appropriately trained physics-informed
autoencoders, and this approach allows to construct reduced order surrogate models with less error
than conventional linear methods. Numerical experiments with the canonical Lorenz '96 model illustrate
that nonlinear surrogates perform better than linear projection-based ones in the context of multifidelity
filtering. The paper was accepted by Frontiers in Applied Mathematics and Statistics.

In the paper “The Model Forest Ensemble Kalman Filter” we provide a possible solution by generalizing
the idea of model hierarchies into model forests - collections of high fidelity and low fidelity models
organized in a groping of model trees. We generalize the multifidelity ensemble Kalman filter that
previously operated on model hierarchies into the model forest ensemble Kalman filter through a
generalized theory of linear control variates.

Activity 3: Accelerate the outer loop dynamical data assimilation solution using the novel concept of
particle flow filters.

Accomplishments: Particle flow filters smoothly transform particles from being samples of a prior
distribution to samples of a posterior. In the paper “Ensemble Variational Fokker-Planck Methods for
Data Assimilation” we introduce a generalized data assimilation framework called the Variational
Fokker-Planck method for filtering and smoothing whose specific implementations are the previously
known methods such as the mapping particle filter and Langevin-based filters. Using the properties of
an optimal Ito process that drives the corresponding Fokker-Planck equation, we derive natural
dynamics for known heuristics such as particle rejuvenation and regularization to fit into the said
framework. We also extend our framework to higher dimensions using localization and covariance
shrinkage, and provide a robust implicit-explicit method for evolving the Ito process, as a stochastic
initial value problem.

Activity 4: Perform theoretical analyses of machine learning autoencoders to explain current drawbacks,
and improve them by developing novel formulations.

Accomplishments: A rapidly growing area of research is the use of machine learning approaches such
as autoencoders for dimensionality reduction of data and models in scientific applications. In the paper
“A Meta-learning Formulation of the Autoencoder Problem for Non-linear Dimensionality Reduction” we
show that the canonical formulation of autoencoders suffers from several deficiencies that can hinder
their performance. Using a meta-learning approach, we reformulate the autoencoder problem as a bi-
level optimization procedure that explicitly solves the dimensionality reduction task. We prove that the
new formulation corrects the identified deficiencies with canonical autoencoders, provide a practical way
to solve it, and showcase the strength of this formulation with a simple numerical illustration.

III. IMPACT

Impact on the development of the principal discipline(s) of the project

Many applications of interest to the DOE, such as making decisions based on model predictions, are
solved by “outer loop” algorithms that require repeated runs of “inner loop” (forward) simulations. This
project helps to overcome the challenges faced by conventional outer loop algorithms by developing
computationally light surrogate models that approximate the dynamics of the high-fidelity (inner-loop)
models. The surrogate models are obtained by augmenting conventional numerical analysis and
statistical approaches with machine learning methodologies and are used to accelerate both the inner
loop simulations and the outer loop calculations. A mathematically rigorous framework is developed to
couple inner-loop surrogate and high-fidelity simulations, and outer loop solvers, such as to ensure the
accuracy of the computed outer-loop solutions at minimal overall computational cost. Novel algorithmic
developments include: Control PINNs, using reinforcement learning to guide adaptive numerical solvers,
constructing adjoint matching neural network surrogates for dynamical systems, using multiple fidelity
models (including learning models) in data assimilation in a rigorous manner, nonlinear model order
reduction via autoencoders, machine learning based algorithms for uncertainty quantification, stochastic
covariance shrinkage approaches.

Impact on other disciplines

The approach of constructing hybrid “numerical+learning” computational algorithms is a fruitful
paradigm that can impact simulation approaches in many fields of sciences.
