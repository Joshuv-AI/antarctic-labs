Constraining Lorentz symmetry breaking in bumblebee gravity with extreme mass-ratio inspirals
Sheng Long 1 , Zhong-wu Xia 2 , Huajie Gong 2 , Zhoujian Cao 1,3,∗ , Qiyuan Pan 2,4,∗ , and Jiliang Jing 2,4,∗

Abstract
Abstract Extreme mass-ratio inspirals (EMRIs), with their long-lived and highly relativistic orbital evolution, can probe strong-field spacetime geometry and provide an important means to test general relativity. In this work, we investigate EMRI waveforms in a Schwarzschild-like black hole spacetime arising in bumblebee gravity, where Lorentz symmetry breaking (LSB) is characterized by a dimensionless parameter ℓ \ell . We construct EMRI waveforms within the Augmented Analytic Kludge (AAK) framework using the modified orbital frequencies and fluxes. We find that ℓ \ell significantly affects the orbital evolution and thereby modifies the waveform. These modifications grow with increasing ℓ \ell and are further enhanced for more eccentric orbits. Furthermore, using Bayesian analysis, we obtain the posterior distributions of EMRI with the parameter ℓ \ell included. Our results show that all injected source parameters are recovered within their 1 ​ σ 1\,\sigma credible intervals. We find that the bumblebee parameter ℓ \ell can be constrained with an uncertainty of order 𝒪 ⁡ ( 10 − 4 ) \mathcal{O}(10^{-4}) by LISA.

II
Spacetime and orbital dynamics in bumblebee gravity

We consider a Schwarzschild-like black hole solution in bumblebee gravity
Colladay and Kostelecky 1998
;
Kostelecky 2004
.
In this framework, a vector field acquires a nonzero vacuum expectation value,
which modifies the gravitational field equations. We adopt the static, spherically symmetric solution obtained in Ref.
Casana et al. 2018
,
corresponding to a radial bumblebee background. The resulting metric takes the form

ds^{2}=-f(r)\,dt^{2}+\frac{1+\ell}{f(r)}\,dr^{2}+r^{2}\left(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta\,d\varphi^{2}\right),

(1)

where

f(r)=1-\frac{2M}{r},

(2)

and
M
is the black hole mass. The dimensionless parameter
\ell

originates from the nonzero vacuum expectation value of the bumblebee field.

The dynamics of a test particle with mass
m
in the spacetime (
1
) is governed by the Lagrangian
Chandrasekhar 1998

\mathcal{L}=\frac{1}{2}m\,g_{\mu\nu}\dot{x}^{\mu}\dot{x}^{\nu},

(3)

where an overdot denotes differentiation with respect to the proper time
\tau
along timelike geodesics. Owing to the time-translation and rotational symmetries, the orbital energy
E
and axial angular momentum
L_{z}
are conserved quantities associated with the Killing vectors
\partial_{t}
and
\partial_{\varphi}
, respectively. Restricting the motion to the equatorial plane
\theta=\pi/2
, the geodesic equations give the radial equation of motion

\dot{r}^{2}=\frac{1}{(1+\ell)}\left[\frac{E^{2}}{m^{2}}-f(r)\left(1+\frac{L_{z}^{2}}{m^{2}r^{2}}\right)\right],

(4)

together with

\dot{t}=\frac{E}{mf(r)},\qquad\dot{\varphi}=\frac{L_{z}}{mr^{2}}.

(5)

For bound orbits, we introduce the semilatus rectum
p
, eccentricity
e
, and parameterize the radius in terms of
\chi

Babak et al. 2007

r(\chi)=\frac{p\,M}{1+e\cos\chi}.

(6)

The periapsis and apoapsis are

r_{\rm p}=\frac{p\,M}{1+e},\qquad r_{\rm a}=\frac{p\,M}{1-e}.

(7)

The conserved quantities
E
and
L_{z}
follow from imposing
\dot{r}(r_{\rm p})=\dot{r}(r_{\rm a})=0
in Eq. (
4
), yielding

\displaystyle\frac{E^{2}}{m^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(p-2-2e)(p-2+2e)}{p\left(p-3-e^{2}\right)},

(8)

\displaystyle\frac{L_{z}^{2}}{m^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{M^{2}p^{2}}{p-3-e^{2}}.

(9)

These expressions have the same form as in the Schwarzschild case.

The radial and azimuthal orbital frequencies are defined by

\Omega_{r}=\frac{2\pi}{T_{r}},\qquad\Omega_{\varphi}=\frac{\Delta\varphi}{T_{r}},

(10)

where
T_{r}
denotes the radial period and
\Delta\varphi
is the azimuthal phase accumulated during one radial cycle. In terms of
\chi
, they are given by

T_{r}=2\int_{r_{\rm p}}^{r_{\rm a}}\frac{dt}{dr}\frac{dr}{d\chi}\,d\chi,\qquad\Delta\varphi=2\int_{r_{\rm p}}^{r_{\rm a}}\frac{d\varphi}{dt}\frac{dt}{dr}\frac{dr}{d\chi}\,d\chi.

(11)

These quantities admit weak-field expansions in powers of
p
:

\displaystyle\frac{T_{r}}{\sqrt{1+\ell}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi M}{X^{3}}p^{3/2}+\frac{6\pi M}{X}p^{1/2}+\frac{3\pi M(5+4/X)}{p^{1/2}}+\frac{\pi M\left[3(16+25X+4X^{2})\right]}{p^{3/2}X}+\mathcal{O}(p^{-5/2}),

\displaystyle\Omega_{r}\sqrt{1+\ell}

\displaystyle=\frac{X^{3}}{Mp^{3/2}}-\frac{3X^{5}}{Mp^{5/2}}+\frac{3X^{5}(-4-5X+6X^{2})}{2Mp^{7/2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{-3X^{5}(16+25X-20X^{2}-30X^{3}+18X^{4})}{2Mp^{9/2}}+\mathcal{O}(p^{-11/2}),

(12)

\displaystyle\Omega_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=\frac{X^{3}}{Mp^{3/2}}+\frac{-6X^{3}(-1+X^{2})}{2Mp^{5/2}}+\frac{6X^{3}(19-21X^{2}-10X^{3}+12X^{4})}{8Mp^{7/2}}+\mathcal{O}(p^{-9/2}).

(13)

where
X=\sqrt{1-e^{2}}
. Within the AAK framework, it is necessary to incorporate the corrections to the orbital frequencies induced by the bumblebee parameter. Interestingly, the above results indicate that the bumblebee parameter leaves the azimuthal frequency
\Omega_{\varphi}
unchanged, while the radial frequency receives only an overall correction in the form of a constant rescaling factor
1/\sqrt{1+\ell}
.

III
Gravitational-wave fluxes and orbital evolution

GW emission causes the orbit to shrink and gradually circularize over time. Within the adiabatic approximation
Isoyama et al. 2022
, the inspiral can be described as a sequence of geodesics, with the orbital parameters
(p,e)
evolving slowly due to the loss of energy and angular momentum.

At leading order, the energy and angular-momentum fluxes are computed using the quadrupole formula
Thorne 1980
;
Maggiore 2018
,

-\frac{dE}{dt}=\frac{1}{5}\left\langle\dddot{Q}_{ij}\dddot{Q}^{ij}\right\rangle,\quad-\frac{dL_{i}}{dt}=\frac{2}{5}\epsilon_{ijk}\left\langle\ddot{Q}^{jm}\dddot{Q}^{k}_{\hskip 8.19447pt\!\!\!\!m}\right\rangle,

(14)

using the trajectory
x^{i}(t)=(r\cos\varphi,r\sin\varphi,0)
, where the indices
i
,
j
,
k
, and
m
are summed over spatial components only. Here
Q_{ij}=m\left(x_{i}x_{j}-\frac{1}{3}\delta_{ij}x^{k}x_{k}\right)
is the mass quadrupole moment,
\epsilon_{ijk}
is the Levi-Civita symbol, and
\langle\cdot\rangle
denotes averaging over one orbital period.

After time-averaging, we can express the fluxes as expansions in powers of
1/p
and the eccentricity. The bumblebee correction enters the fluxes through the modified orbital trajectory used in the quadrupole radiation formula. We obtain

\displaystyle\left.+6\left(16+120e^{2}+90e^{4}+5e^{6}\right)\ell^{3}\right]+\frac{(1-e^{2})^{3/2}m^{2}}{5p^{6}M^{2}}\left[e^{2}\left(176+450e^{2}+53e^{4}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+e^{2}\left(-216+732e^{2}+437e^{4}\right)\ell+2e^{2}\left(-592-600e^{2}+190e^{4}+15e^{6}\right)\ell^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+6e^{2}\left(-128-240e^{2}+5e^{6}\right)\ell^{3}\right]+\mathcal{O}(p^{-7}),

(15)

\displaystyle\left\langle\frac{dL_{z}}{dt}\right\rangle=

\displaystyle\frac{4(1-e^{2})^{3/2}m^{2}}{5p^{7/2}M(1+\ell)^{2}}\left[8+7e^{2}+\left(16+28e^{2}+e^{4}\right)\ell+\left(8+24e^{2}+3e^{4}\right)\ell^{2}\right]+\frac{4e^{2}(1-e^{2})^{3/2}m^{2}}{5p^{9/2}M(1+\ell)^{2}}

The orbital evolution equations for
(p,e)

\displaystyle\frac{dE}{dt}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial E}{\partial p}\frac{dp}{dt}+\frac{\partial E}{\partial e}\frac{de}{dt},

(17)

\displaystyle\frac{dL_{z}}{dt}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial L_{z}}{\partial p}\frac{dp}{dt}+\frac{\partial L_{z}}{\partial e}\frac{de}{dt},

(18)

Solving Eqs.

and

dp/dt
and

IV
EMRI waveforms in the bumblebee-modified spacetime

IV.1
Augmented Analytic Kludge Waveform

A variety of EMRI waveform models have been developed over the past decades, including the Analytic Kludge (AK)
Peters and Mathews 1963
;
Peters 1964
, Numerical Kludge (NK)
Babak et al. 2007
, and Teukolsky waveform
Hughes et al. 2021
;
Isoyama et al. 2022
;
Nasipak 2024
, with continuous efforts aimed at improving accuracy and computational efficiency
Khalvati et al. 2025
. Among these, the AAK model
Chua et al. 2017
is particularly effective: by incorporating frequency corrections derived from the NK model into the AK framework, it achieves relatively high accuracy at low computational cost, making it well suited for large-scale LISA template generation.

Although fully relativistic self-force waveforms provide the highest precision
Katz et al. 2021
;
Chapman-Bird et al. 2025
, their computation is extremely challenging. As a result, self-force studies in modified gravity are still at an early stage
Roy et al. 2025
, motivating the continued use of PN-based kludge expansions. These approximations capture the essential strong-field dynamics relevant for EMRI systems. An advantage of the AAK model is that it allows the rapid generation of a large number of templates, making it suitable for Bayesian analyses of the constraining capability of EMRI observations on bumblebee gravity.

Following Refs.
Babak et al. 2007
;
Sopuerta and Yunes 2009
, we use the multipolar expansion of metric perturbations and retain only the leading mass quadrupole term. The transverse–traceless metric perturbation is then

h_{ij}=\frac{2}{D}\left(P_{ik}P_{jl}-\frac{1}{2}P_{ij}P_{kl}\right)\ddot{I}^{kl}

(21)

where
I_{ij}
is the mass quadrupole,
D
is the luminosity distance and
P_{ij}
is the projection operator defined as
P_{ij}=\delta_{ij}-\hat{n}_{i}\hat{n}_{j}
. For a point particle, the quadrupole moment is

I^{ij}=\left[\int d^{3}x\,x^{i}x^{j}\,T^{tt}(t,x^{k})\right]^{\mathrm{STF}},

(22)

with the energy density given by

T^{tt}(t,x^{i})=m\,\delta^{(3)}\!\left[x^{i}-z^{i}(t)\right],

(23)

where
z^{i}(t)
is the position of particle.

The NK approach treats the motion as a “bead on a wire,” using a curved-spacetime trajectory as input to a flat-space wave-generation formula. Despite its formal inconsistency, it is generally quite accurate, with overlaps exceeding
95\%
against Teukolsky-based waveforms over a significant part of the relevant parameter space
Babak et al. 2007
.
While the NK provides a much more faithful approximation to EMRI waveforms, its construction is still significantly more expensive than that of the analytic kludge. This motivates the AAK, which incorporates key information from NK to improve waveform faithfulness without sacrificing the speed that makes kludge models attractive for data analysis.
Then, in AAK the GW signal is written as a sum over harmonics of the radial and azimuthal motions
Chua et al. 2017

h_{+,\times}(t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A^{+,\times}_{n}(t),

(24)

The harmonic amplitudes
A_{n}^{+}
and
A_{n}^{\times}
are given by

\displaystyle A_{n}^{+}

\displaystyle=\left[1+(\hat{L}\cdot\hat{n})^{2}\right]\left[b_{n}\sin(2\gamma)-a_{n}\cos(2\gamma)\right]+\left[1-(\hat{L}\cdot\hat{n})^{2}\right]c_{n},

(25)

\displaystyle A_{n}^{\times}

\displaystyle=2(\hat{L}\cdot\hat{n})\left[b_{n}\cos(2\gamma)+a_{n}\sin(2\gamma)\right],

(26)

where

\displaystyle\gamma=\varphi_{\varphi}(t)-\varphi_{r}(t),\qquad\varphi_{r}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\Omega_{r}(t^{\prime})\,dt^{\prime},\qquad\varphi_{\varphi}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\Omega_{\varphi}(t^{\prime})\,dt^{\prime},

(27)

\displaystyle\hat{L}\cdot\hat{n}=\cos\theta_{K}\cos\theta_{S}+\sin\theta_{K}\sin\theta_{S}\cos(\varphi_{K}-\varphi_{S}).

In following calculations we set
\theta_{S}=\pi/3
,
\varphi_{S}=\pi/2
,
\theta_{K}=\pi/4
and
\varphi_{K}=\pi/4
.
For eccentric motion, the coefficients take the form

\displaystyle a_{n}

\displaystyle=-n\mathcal{A}\left[J_{n-2}(ne)-2eJ_{n-1}(ne)+2J_{n}(ne)/n+2eJ_{n+1}(ne)-J_{n+2}(ne)\right]\cos(n\varphi_{r}(t)),

\displaystyle b_{n}

\displaystyle=-n\mathcal{A}\sqrt{1-e^{2}}\left[J_{n-2}(ne)-2J_{n}(ne)+J_{n+2}(ne)\right]\sin(n\varphi_{r}(t)),

(28)

\displaystyle c_{n}

\displaystyle=2\mathcal{A}J_{n}(ne)\cos(n\varphi_{r}(t)),

where
J_{n}(ne)
is a Bessel function of the first kind, the amplitude
\mathcal{A}=(M\Omega_{\varphi})^{2/3}m/D
and luminosity distance
D=1\,\mathrm{Gpc}
.

The radial frequency
\Omega_{r}
and the orbital parameters
(p,e)
are directly modified by the bumblebee gravity, while the azimuthal frequency
\Omega_{\varphi}
retains unchanged.

IV.2
Parameter Estimation

To connect with observations, it is necessary to convert the plus and cross polarizations of the GW into the LISA detector response.
The response of LISA to the incident GW is modeled by two independent Michelson-type channels
\alpha=I,II
, with time-domain strain
Barack and Cutler 2004

\displaystyle h_{\alpha}(t)=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\left[F^{+}_{\alpha}(t)h_{+}(t)+F^{\times}_{\alpha}(t)h_{\times}(t)\right]\,,

(29)

where
F^{+,\times}_{\alpha}(t)
are the antenna pattern functions.

To quantify how distinguishable two EMRI signals are, we compute the
noise–weighted overlap between two LISA responses
h_{a}(t)
and

h_{b}(t)
. Working in the frequency domain, the corresponding inner
product is defined as

(h_{a}|h_{b})=2\int\frac{h_{a}^{*}(f)\,h_{b}(f)+h_{a}(f)\,h_{b}^{*}(f)}{S_{n}(f)}\,{\rm d}f\,,

(30)

where
S_{n}(f)
is the one–sided power spectral density of the
detector noise
Amaro-Seoane et al. 2017
.
Then, the normalized overlap between the two waveforms is

\mathcal{O}(h_{a},h_{b})=\frac{(h_{a}|h_{b})}{\sqrt{(h_{a}|h_{a})\,(h_{b}|h_{b})}}\,,

(31)

and we define the corresponding mismatch as

\mathcal{M}(h_{a},h_{b})=1-\mathcal{O}(h_{a},h_{b})\,.

(32)

A mismatch of
\mathcal{M}=0
indicates that the signal and template are perfectly matched, whereas
\mathcal{M}=1
indicates that they are completely mismatched.

To evaluate the constraining power of EMRI observations more rigorously, we go beyond mismatch and infer the posterior distribution of the source parameters. This is particularly important for EMRI signals, whose long-lived and highly structured waveforms generally give rise to a high-dimensional likelihood surface with nontrivial parameter correlations and possible multiple local maxima. In such situations, a full Bayesian analysis is therefore required in order to obtain reliable parameter uncertainties, identify degeneracies among parameters, and assess whether the bumblebee parameter can be distinguished from the detected signal
Chua et al. 2018
;
Ali et al. 2012
.

In a Bayesian framework, the information on the source parameters is encoded in the posterior probability distribution, which is given by Bayes’ theorem,

p(\Theta|D)=\frac{p(D|\Theta)\,\pi(\Theta)}{p(D)},

(33)

where
\pi(\Theta)
is the prior distribution,
p(D|\Theta)
is the likelihood, and
p(D)
is the evidence. For GW data with stationary Gaussian noise, the likelihood is usually constructed from the noise-weighted inner product between the injected waveform and the template waveform. In practice, the posterior is explored numerically using MCMC sampling, from which we can obtain marginalized distributions and credible intervals for both the standard source parameters and the bumblebee parameter.

The basic procedure is straightforward. For each parameter vector, we generate the corresponding waveform and detector response, and construct the likelihood by using the noise-weighted inner product

\mathcal{L}(D|\Theta)\propto\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}(D-h(\Theta)\,|\,D-h(\Theta))\right],

(34)

Combined with suitable prior distributions listed in Tab.
1
, Bayes’ theorem yields the posterior probability density of the model parameters. We then use MCMC sampling to explore this posterior in the five-dimensional parameter space. Once the chains have converged, the resulting samples are used to construct corner plots and marginalized posterior distributions, from which we obtain the credible intervals.

V
Conclusion

In this work, we have investigated GW signals from eccentric EMRI in a Schwarzschild-like black hole spacetime arising in bumblebee gravity with a bumblebee parameter
\ell
. Starting from the geodesic motion of a test particle in this modified background, we analyzed how the bumblebee gravity modifies the conservative orbital dynamics and the corresponding orbital frequencies. We find that the LSB parameter affects only the radial frequency
\Omega_{r}
, entering as an overall rescaling factor, while leaving the azimuthal frequency
\Omega_{\phi}
unchanged.
We then constructed the GW energy and angular-momentum fluxes based on the multipolar radiation formula for the eccentric orbit. We find that the LSB parameter modifies both the energy flux and the angular momentum flux, and that these corrections already enter at the leading order.
To assess the detectability of bumblebee gravity, these modifications were subsequently incorporated into the AAK framework to generate EMRI waveforms.

Our results show that, within the parameter range considered here, although the bumblebee and Schwarzschild waveforms are nearly indistinguishable over short timescales, a phase difference gradually accumulates during the inspiral and becomes more pronounced at late times. This behavior reflects a characteristic feature of EMRI systems: since the small object evolves gradually in the strong-field region and accumulates a large number of orbital cycles, even small deviations in the background spacetime can leave observable imprints on the waveform. Consistent with this picture, our mismatch analysis indicates that the distinguishability between the modified and GR waveforms increases monotonically with
\ell
. In addition, larger eccentricity
e
can significantly increase the sensitivity to
\ell
, since more eccentric orbits probe stronger-field regions.

Our posterior results show that the intrinsic source parameters are recovered accurately, while the bumblebee parameter
\ell
is also tightly constrained around its injected value. For the representative configuration studied here, we obtain
\ell=0.002499\,^{+1.46\times 10^{-4}}_{-1.43\times 10^{-4}}
, demonstrating that the LSB correction leaves a measurable imprint on the EMRI waveform. This indicates that EMRI observations can not only resolve the standard binary parameters, but also place meaningful constraints on bumblebee gravity in the strong-field regime.

Overall, our analysis shows that EMRIs provide an effective framework for probing bumblebee gravity in the strong-field regime. The waveform dephasing induced by the parameter
\ell
is sufficiently large to be resolved in the representative configurations considered here, and the posterior inference confirms that
\ell
can be constrained together with the standard intrinsic source parameters. In particular, high-eccentricity systems exhibit enhanced sensitivity to the bumblebee correction, suggesting that they may offer the best prospects for testing LSB effects with future space-based GW observations.
