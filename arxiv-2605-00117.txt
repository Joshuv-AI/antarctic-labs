Topological Charge of Causality at a PT-Symmetric Exceptional Point
Kejun Liu

Abstract
Causality in linear response is conventionally treated as a binary property: a response function is either analytic in the upper half-plane or it is not. We show that in a PT-symmetric open dimer it instead carries a topological charge. As the gain–loss parameter crosses the exceptional point, a single pole of the reflection coefficient migrates into the upper half-plane, the Blaschke winding number jumps from 0 0 to 1 1 , and standard Kramers–Kronig reconstruction acquires a Lorentzian residual fixed by the pole residue. The transition is sharp, protected by the codimension-one structure of the exceptional point, and directly measurable in a one-port reflection experiment. Most strikingly, the violation magnitude scales as Δ KK ∼ | γ − γ c | ν \Delta_{\rm KK}\sim|\gamma-\gamma_{c}|^{\nu} with ν ≈ − 1.08 \nu\approx-1.08 in the single-port geometry: the breakdown of standard KK is strongest at threshold and weakens deeper in the broken phase. We derive the exact reflection coefficient, verify the residue-corrected dispersion relation, and propose a THz time-domain spectroscopy protocol that detects the topological charge through the residual itself.



Causality, in the form of analyticity of the response function in the upper half-plane
\mathbb{H}_{+}
, is one of the most universal constraints in linear-response physics
Toll 1956
;
Nussenzveig 1972
. It is usually treated as a binary property: a response either obeys the upper-half-plane analyticity needed for standard Kramers–Kronig (KK) relations, or it fails to do so. In passive systems this binary picture is consistent with thermodynamics. In non-Hermitian systems with engineered gain, from
\mathcal{PT}
-symmetric photonic dimers
Rüter et al. 2010
;
Peng et al. 2014
to driven–dissipative cold-atom and superconducting platforms
Özdemir et al. 2019
;
El-Ganainy et al. 2018
, it is not.

Recent work has shown two ways in which reduced descriptions can display apparent acausality. Zhang
et al.
found internal causality breaking in a reduced subsystem driven by initial-state structure rather than by gain/loss dynamics
Zhang et al. 2024
. Gavassino showed, in a classical coarse-graining setting, that causal microscopic dynamics can generate effective equations with acausal form
Gavassino 2026
. Our question is sharper: can the failure of standard KK itself be organised as a topological transition in an open non-Hermitian system, with an integer invariant that counts how many poles have crossed into the upper half-plane?

For the simplest solvable model, a PT-symmetric dimer coupled to a one-port waveguide, the upper-half-plane pole count of the reflection coefficient is a Blaschke winding number
\mathcal{N}_{B}
. It jumps from
0
to
1
as the gain–loss parameter crosses the exceptional point and is therefore a parameter-space topological charge of causality. This is not just another exceptional-point story: exceptional points can coexist with causality in driven bound-state systems
Kulkarni and Joglekar 2025
, so the mechanism must be traced to the open gain–loss dynamics rather than to spectral degeneracy alone. Below we draw this distinction and make it measurable.

Model.
—The bare PT-symmetric dimer Hamiltonian is

Experimental proposal.
—The topological charge is directly testable with THz time-domain spectroscopy on a PT-symmetric plasmonic metasurface
Bhardwaj et al. 2025
. The protocol: (1) fabricate samples spanning the EP; (2) measure complex transmission and extract
\chi_{\rm eff}(\omega)
; (3) compute the standard-KK prediction; (4) fit the residual to the Blaschke ansatz
\Delta(\omega)\approx 2\Real[\rho/(\omega-z_{0})]
to extract the UHP pole; (5) plot
\|\Delta\|_{2}
vs
|S-S_{\rm EP}|
to verify
\nu<0
. A passive reference sample serves as null control. A faster proof-of-concept on an electronic RLC dimer with a negative impedance converter
Schindler et al. 2011
can validate the pipeline before metasurface fabrication.

Discussion.
—The Blaschke winding number
\mathcal{N}_{B}
is a topological invariant in parameter space, in contrast to the spectral winding numbers and discriminant numbers of non-Hermitian band theory
Bergholtz et al. 2021
;
Kawabata et al. 2019
;
Yang et al. 2021
, which live in momentum space. The two are not the same object but they share the same mathematical scaffolding (argument principle on a contour in the complex plane) and are protected by the same codimension-one structure of the EP.

The mechanism of causality breaking here is dynamical, not kinematic. Zhang
et al.

Zhang et al. 2024
showed that initial-state structure alone can produce apparent acausality in a reduced subsystem; Gavassino
Gavassino 2026
showed that causal microscopic dynamics can generate effective equations with acausal form. Our result is distinct: the upper-half-plane pole is driven by the gain–loss dynamics of the open system, not by initial conditions or coarse-graining. Kulkarni and Joglekar
Kulkarni and Joglekar 2025
demonstrated that exceptional points can coexist with causality in driven bound-state systems, confirming that the EP itself is not the causal agent; it is the open gain channel that forces the pole across the real axis.

Concurrent work has uncovered the complementary side of this problem in the spectral structure of the memory-kernel generator
Liu 2026b
: the Nakajima–Zwanzig projected Liouvillian
QLQ
of the Jaynes–Cummings model is pseudo-Hermitian with a positive-definite metric, protecting its spectrum on the real axis and thereby guaranteeing the Hardy-space analyticity that underlies standard Kramers–Kronig relations. While the present Letter demonstrates
r(\omega)
-side breakdown when a UHP pole appears, Ref.
Liu 2026b
shows
\eta
-protection of the
QLQ
spectrum as a spectral-side mechanism that ensures standard KK remains intact. The two results together frame causality in open quantum systems as a property of both the memory-kernel generator and the physical response, with symmetry-protected transitions on each side.

The negative scaling exponent
\nu<0
is a robust feature of the open PT-dimer. The physical reason is that the uncorrected KK residual is set by the
L^{2}
norm of the Lorentzian
2\,\Real[\rho_{+}/(\omega-z_{+})]
, which scales as
|\rho_{+}|/\sqrt{\Imag z_{+}}
. As
\gamma
increases above
\gamma_{c}
, the pole migrates further into
\mathbb{H}_{+}
and its residue decreases, causing the residual to decay rather than grow. The violation is thus largest immediately above threshold — a “critical peak” at the phase boundary. A cross-check on a four-site PT-symmetric SSH chain shows oscillatory rather than monotonic scaling (
R^{2}=0.04
), arising from interference between multiple UHP poles; the simple single-pole scaling is specific to the
2\times 2
dimer. Universality, if it exists, is at the level of the single-pole class of codimension-one EPs.

The residue-corrected KK relation also has an inverse-problem reading: given a measured pair
(\Real\chi,\Imag\chi)
over a finite band, the residual after standard KK reconstruction encodes the locations and strengths of any UHP poles, regardless of whether the underlying Hamiltonian is known explicitly. This is a model-independent diagnostic of non-Hermitian gain in materials.

Conclusion.
—We have shown that causality in linear response carries a topological charge: the Blaschke winding number
\mathcal{N}_{B}
, counting upper-half-plane poles of the open-system propagator, jumps from
0
to
1
at the PT-symmetric exceptional point and is protected by its codimension-one structure. The standard Kramers–Kronig identity acquires a calculable Lorentzian residual whose location and amplitude are set by the residue at the new UHP pole. The scaling exponent
\nu\approx-1.08
is negative—the violation peaks at threshold and decays deeper in the broken phase, correcting the heuristic expectation of
\nu=+1/2
. The transition is directly testable in THz time-domain spectroscopy on PT-symmetric metasurfaces. The topological charge framework extends naturally to multi-pole systems and higher-order EPs, where the winding number can take values
\mathcal{N}_{B}>1
.

Heisenberg–Langevin derivation

Let
a_{1},a_{2}
be the bosonic site annihilation operators. We couple site
1
to a continuum bus waveguide via the standard Gardiner–Collett interaction
Gardiner and Collett 1985
;
Walls and Milburn 2008

\hat{H}_{SB}\;=\;i\!\int d\omega\,\sqrt{\frac{\gamma_{\rm ex}}{2\pi}}\,\bigl[b^{\dagger}(\omega)\,a_{1}\;-\;a_{1}^{\dagger}\,b(\omega)\bigr],

(6)

giving Heisenberg equations
\dot{a}_{i}=-i[a_{i},\hat{H}_{\rm tot}]
. Solving the bus equation formally and applying the Markov approximation
\int d\omega\,e^{-i\omega(t-s)}\to 2\pi\delta(t-s)
yields the quantum Langevin equations

\displaystyle\dot{a}_{1}

\displaystyle=-i(H_{11}a_{1}+H_{12}a_{2})-\tfrac{\gamma_{\rm ex}}{2}\,a_{1}-\sqrt{\gamma_{\rm ex}}\,b_{\rm in}(t),

\displaystyle\dot{a}_{2}

\displaystyle=-i(H_{21}a_{1}+H_{22}a_{2}),

(7)

with input field
b_{\rm in}(t)\equiv(2\pi)^{-1/2}\int d\omega\,b(\omega,0)\,e^{-i\omega t}
. Equivalently
\dot{\vec{a}}=-i\,H_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{SP}}\,\vec{a}-\sqrt{\gamma_{\rm ex}}\,|1\rangle\,b_{\rm in}(t)
with the single-port effective non-Hermitian Hamiltonian

H_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{SP}}\;=\;H(\gamma)\;-\;i\,\frac{\gamma_{\rm ex}}{2}\,|1\rangle\!\langle 1|\;=\;\begin{pmatrix}-i\gamma-i\gamma_{\rm ex}/2&\kappa\\
\kappa&i\gamma\end{pmatrix}.

(8)

The decay term
-i(\gamma_{\rm ex}/2)\,|1\rangle\!\langle 1|
is asymmetric: it lives entirely on the port site.

The standard input–output relation
b_{\rm out}(t)=b_{\rm in}(t)+\sqrt{\gamma_{\rm ex}}\,a_{1}(t)
, combined with the Langevin equations in the frequency domain, gives

r(\omega;\gamma)\;=\;1\;+\;i\,\gamma_{\rm ex}\,G_{11}^{\rm eff}(\omega;\gamma),\qquad G^{\rm eff}(\omega)\equiv(\omega I-H_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{SP}})^{-1}.

(9)

Computing the (1,1) cofactor and the determinant explicitly,

\displaystyle G_{11}^{\rm eff}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega-i\gamma}{D^{\rm SP}(\omega;\gamma,\gamma_{\rm ex})},

(10)

\displaystyle D^{\rm SP}(\omega;\gamma,\gamma_{\rm ex})

\displaystyle=\omega^{2}+i\,\frac{\gamma_{\rm ex}}{2}\,\omega+\gamma^{2}+\frac{\gamma\gamma_{\rm ex}}{2}-\kappa^{2}.

Symmetric two-port coupling

For two ports with equal coupling rates, the Markov self-energy is proportional to the identity:

H_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{SYM}}=H(\gamma)-i(\gamma_{\rm ex}/2)\,I
,
with denominator
D^{\rm SYM}=(\omega+i\gamma_{\rm ex}/2)^{2}+\gamma^{2}-\kappa^{2}
. The roots are
\omega_{\pm}^{\rm SYM}=-i\gamma_{\rm ex}/2\pm\sqrt{\kappa^{2}-\gamma^{2}}
.
For
\gamma>\kappa
,
\Imag\,\omega_{+}^{\rm SYM}=\sqrt{\gamma^{2}-\kappa^{2}}-\gamma_{\rm ex}/2
, which is exactly the heuristic form. The UHP-pole threshold is
\gamma_{\rm ex}<2\sqrt{\gamma^{2}-\kappa^{2}}
, and the EP remains pinned at
\gamma_{c}^{\rm SYM}=\kappa
. The SYM scaling exponent is
\nu_{\rm SYM}=-1.195\pm 0.005
.

Correction to previously stated residue-corrected KK

The residue-corrected KK relations (Eqs. (3)–(4) of the main text) differ from the form

\Real

\Imag

\omega=2

Gain–bandwidth bound

For an infinite collection of UHP poles
\{z_{j}=\omega_{j}+i\gamma_{j}\}
, the Blaschke product converges on compact subsets of
\mathbb{H}_{+}
if and only if
\sum_{j}\gamma_{j}/(1+\omega_{j}^{2}+\gamma_{j}^{2})<\infty
. Within a bounded strip this reduces to
\sum_{j}\gamma_{j}<\infty
: the total gain rate is finite. This is the non-Hermitian analogue of the Bode integral theorem.

\gamma-\gamma_{c}
rather than decaying monotonically (

). The oscillation arises from interference between multiple UHP poles; the simple single-pole scaling is specific to the
2\times 2
dimer.
