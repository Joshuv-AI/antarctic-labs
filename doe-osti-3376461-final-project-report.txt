Final Project Report
Uncertainty Quantification for Neutrino Reconstruction in DUNE

Project Overview
This project addressed key data-analysis and scientific challenges for the Deep Underground
Neutrino Experiment (DUNE) by developing machine-learning methods for neutrino
reconstruction with an emphasis on uncertainty quantification (UQ). The original goals focused
on introducing UQ into classification and regression problems relevant to DUNE, including
neutrino interaction classification, neutrino energy reconstruction, and neutrino interaction
localization.

The project was positioned at the intersection of high-energy physics, statistics, computer
science, and machine learning. It explored the use of probabilistic and Bayesian machine-
learning approaches to improve the interpretability and reliability of neural-network-based
reconstruction tools for neutrino experiments.

Major Scientific Achievements
A major achievement of the project was the development, training, and deployment of a
Convolutional Visual Network (CVN) for atmospheric-neutrino interaction classification in
DUNE’s Far Detector horizontal-drift module. This work is particularly important because
atmospheric neutrinos are expected to be among the first physics samples collected by the DUNE
experiment.

The CVN was trained using a large simulated atmospheric-neutrino dataset consisting of:

 • 1.84 million pixel maps for training
 • 80,000 pixel maps for validation
 • 80,000 pixel maps for testing

This represented a significant milestone in demonstrating that deep-learning-based image-
classification methods can be applied to atmospheric-neutrino reconstruction in the DUNE Far
Detector. The results from this effort were published in the paper:“Reconstruction of
atmospheric neutrinos in DUNE’s horizontal-drift far-detector module.” See list of
products. This publication documents the successful application of the CVN approach to
atmospheric-neutrino classification and represents one of the primary scientific outputs of the
project.

Development of Bayesian Neural Networks for Classification
In addition to the DUNE atmospheric-neutrino CVN work, the project explored the use of
Bayesian neural networks for classification problems using the MicroBooNE open dataset. This

effort focused on understanding how uncertainty quantification can be incorporated directly into
neural-network classification outputs.

The neural-network architecture explored in this part of the project used multiple detector-plane
views as input. The U, V, and Y wire-plane images were each processed through a sparse
encoder based on two-dimensional convolutional layers and residual blocks. The resulting latent
representations were combined through a cross-plane multi-head attention mechanism, followed
by dense flipout layers to produce a categorical classification output together with uncertainty
information.

This architecture demonstrated how Bayesian neural-network layers can provide both
classification probabilities and associated predictive uncertainties. The work showed that
Bayesian methods can be used to move beyond point-estimate classification and provide a more
complete statistical description of model predictions.

Progress Toward Uncertainty Quantification Goals
Overall, the project achieved its central goal of demonstrating the incorporation of uncertainties
into classification problems using Bayesian neural networks. The project showed that
uncertainty-aware neural-network architectures can be constructed and applied to neutrino-
interaction classification tasks.

The work also identified important practical challenges for incorporating these methods into a
full DUNE analysis workflow. In particular, the integration of uncertainty-quantification libraries
into the LArSoft-based DUNE software ecosystem remains challenging. Current machine-
learning libraries are provided through LArSoft, and adding tools capable of supporting
uncertainty quantification, such as TensorFlow Probability, requires additional software
integration work. Performing inference with these tools directly within the LArSoft framework
also remains a technical challenge.

These findings are important because they clarify the gap between demonstrating uncertainty-
aware methods in standalone machine-learning studies and deploying them in production-level
DUNE reconstruction and analysis workflows.

Challenges in Regression Applications
The project also examined the broader use of convolutional visual networks for regression
problems, such as neutrino energy reconstruction. This remains an open challenge. One
important issue is the dependence of energy-regression models on final-state interactions, which
can introduce model-dependent effects into the reconstructed energy. There were also
discussions within the collaboration about how to properly include uncertainties in regression-
based reconstruction. In particular, there is an open question of whether introducing machine-
learning-based uncertainty estimates could lead to double counting, given that neutrino analyses
already include many sources of systematic uncertainty. This remains an important area for
future work.

Dissemination and Broader Impacts
The PI disseminated the results of this research at a major international conference, presenting
the work at the 2025 edition of Neutrino Physics and Machine Learning, held at the University of
Tokyo in Japan. This provided an opportunity to share the project’s progress with the broader
neutrino-physics and machine-learning communities.

The project also contributed to workforce development. During each budget period, an
undergraduate student was supported for summer research. This included support for a senior
thesis, providing hands-on training in machine learning, neutrino reconstruction, and data-
intensive high-energy physics analysis.
