Abstract
. We prove a sharp quantitative stability result for Alexandrov’s theorem in arbitrary dimension for bounded C 1 C^{1} open sets in a small-excess regime. More precisely, if E ⊂ ℝ n E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n} is a bounded C 1 C^{1} open set with the same volume as the unit ball B B , small excess, and scalar distributional mean curvature ℋ ∂ E ∈ L 2 ​ ( ∂ E ) \mathcal{H}_{\partial E}\in L^{2}(\partial E) , then, up to a translation, Exc ⁡ ( E ) + | E ​ Δ ​ B | 2 + | μ − ( n − 1 ) | 2 ≤ C ⁡ ( n ) ​ ‖ ℋ ∂ E − μ ‖ L 2 ​ ( ∂ E ) 2 ∀ μ ∈ ℝ . \operatorname{Exc}(E)+|E\Delta B|^{2}+|\mu-(n-1)|^{2}\leq C(n)\|\mathcal{H}_{\partial E}-\mu\|_{L^{2}(\partial E)}^{2}\qquad\forall\,\mu\in\mathbb{R}. In other words, both the excess and the symmetric difference from the ball are controlled by the optimal L 2 L^{2} -oscillation of the mean curvature. This yields a sharp stability estimate in a genuinely non-parametric regime. The proof combines a B ​ V BV version of Fuglede’s spectral-gap argument, a star-shaped rearrangement for sets of finite perimeter, quantitative estimates for the part of the boundary contained in the tentacles, and a polyhedral approximation argument for the non-graphical region. Introduction

Alexandrov’s Soap Bubble Theorem has been the subject of extensive investigation. In its most basic form, the theorem states that the only compact, connected, embedded hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with constant mean curvature is the
(n-1)
-dimensional sphere
\partial B=\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
up to translation and scaling.

A natural and fundamental question arises regarding the stability of this statement: if a compact, connected, embedded hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
has nearly constant mean curvature, is it close to a sphere? The answer is known to be negative if the control over the mean curvature is insufficiently strong, as bubbling can occur, resulting in the hypersurface resembling a union of disjoint spheres. For a detailed discussion on this phenomenon, refer to
[
25
,
26
,
7
,
8
,
12
]
, and for applications of these types of results, see
[
38

.

Following a series of foundational results that elucidated the stability of this bubbling behavior
[
12
,
27
,
15
]
, several works have focused on understanding the stability of Alexandrov’s theorem in the absence of bubbling. The first significant result in this direction was achieved in
[
27
, Theorems 1.8 and 1.10]
, where sets that are Lipschitz-close to a sphere are considered and precise stability estimates are established in terms of the
L^{2}
-oscillations of the mean curvature around the “natural” value
n-1
(corresponding to the mean curvature of the unit ball
B
). This result was improved in
[
32
]
(see also
[
17
]
), where the
L^{2}
oscillations of the mean curvature are taken around its average.

To state these results more precisely, we need to recall the following definition of nearly spherical sets. In the whole paper,

\mathbb{R}^{n}

For every function

Remark 1.4
.

Theorem

under the additional hypotheses required there. More precisely, if one assumes

together with a uniform diameter bound and a pointwise positive lower bound on the mean curvature, then the compactness result of
[

implies that any sequence with vanishing

-oscillation of the mean curvature converges, up to translations, to a single unit ball. Theorem

then yields the corresponding quantitative estimate for all sufficiently small oscillations.

If instead one has control of the critical norm of the oscillation, namely

in our notation (the

-norm in the notation of

[
24

36
]
, where the ambient space is
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
), then one can
use the bubbling results of
[
24
]
and

[

]
. These results give convergence, under the natural perimeter
and diameter bounds, to a finite union of equal balls (more generally, to Wulff
shapes in the anisotropic setting of
[
36
]
) without imposing a

argument then excludes more than one ball.

Remark 1.5
.

We note that the constant

The proof of Theorem

proceeds in four main steps. First, in Section

we collect the basic consequences of small excess: We relate

to both the perimeter deficit and the symmetric difference from the ball, show that only a small amount of perimeter can lie in the tentacular region away from

Second, in Section
3
we establish the main analytic input of the paper, namely a

is more natural here, since it is preserved by the star-shaped rearrangement.

Third, in Section

3
applies. The remaining difficulty is the non-graphical region, which is handled by the leveling argument of Proposition

Finally, still in Section

Preliminaries

In this section, we establish several preliminary results that will be crucial for the proof of our main theorem.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},n\geq 2
be a set of finite volume.
Then

\int_{E}\frac{1}{|x|}\,dx\leq C(n)|E|^{\frac{n-1}{n}}.

Assume in addition that
|E|=|B|
. Then

(2.1)

\int_{E\setminus B}\left||x|-1\right|\,dx\geq c(n)|E\Delta B|^{2}.

Proof.

Let
r>0
be such that
|B_{r}|=|E|
. Since
\frac{1}{|x|}
is radially decreasing,
it follows that

\int_{E\setminus B_{r}}\frac{1}{|x|}\,dx\leq\frac{1}{r}|E\setminus B_{r}|=\frac{1}{r}|B_{r}\setminus E|\leq\int_{B_{r}\setminus E}\frac{1}{|x|}\,dx,

therefore

\int_{E}\frac{1}{|x|}\,dx=\int_{E\cap B_{r}}\frac{1}{|x|}\,dx+\int_{E\setminus B_{r}}\frac{1}{|x|}\,dx\\
\leq\int_{E\cap B_{r}}\frac{1}{|x|}\,dx+\int_{B_{r}\setminus E}\frac{1}{|x|}\,dx=\int_{B_{r}}\frac{1}{|x|}\,dx=C(n)r^{n-1}=C(n)|E|^{\frac{n-1}{n}}.

Assume now that
|E|=|B|
. Since
\left||x|-1\right|
is radial and it is increasing away from
\partial B
, arguing similarly as above one can prove that the minimum of
\int_{E\setminus B}\left||x|-1\right|\,dx
among sets with prescribed exterior volume is attained when
E\setminus B
coincides with the annulus
A_{1,1+\epsilon}
, with
\epsilon\in(0,1)
so that

|A_{1,\,1+\epsilon}|=|E\setminus B|.

Therefore

(2.2)

\int_{E\setminus B}\left||x|-1\right|\,dx\geq c(n)\int_{1}^{1+\epsilon}|r-1|r^{n-1}\,dr=\frac{(1+\epsilon)^{n}(n\epsilon-1)+1}{n(n+1)}\sim\epsilon^{2}.

Noticing that
\epsilon\sim|E\setminus B|=\frac{1}{2}|E\Delta B|
(recall that
|E|=|B|
),
this concludes the proof of the lemma.

Lemma 2.2

Let

be a set of finite perimeter such that

\frac{1}{C(n)}|E\Delta B|^{2}+P(E)-P(B)\leq{\rm Exc}(E)\leq P(E)-P(B)+C(n)|E\Delta B|^{\frac{n-1}{n}}.

Proof.

As shown in

{\rm Exc}(E)=P(E)-P(B)+\int_{B\setminus E}\frac{n-1}{|x|}\,dx-\int_{E\setminus B}\frac{n-1}{|x|}\,dx.

Also, it follows from the proof of

\int_{B\setminus E}\frac{n-1}{|x|}\,dx-\int_{E\setminus B}\frac{n-1}{|x|}\,dx\geq c(n)|E\Delta B|^{2}.

This proves the first inequality.
To obtain the second inequality, it suffices to note that applying Lemma

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a set of finite perimeter such that
|E|=|B|

\displaystyle P(E\setminus B_{r})\leq C(n,\gamma){\rm Exc}(E)^{\frac{1}{2}}\qquad\text{ for any }\ r\geq 1+\gamma

\displaystyle P(B_{r}\setminus E)\leq C(n,\gamma){\rm Exc}(E)^{\frac{1}{2}}\qquad\text{ for any }\ r\in(1/2,1-\gamma),

Proof.

We first consider the part of
\partial^{*}E
outside the unit ball.
By the coarea formula
[
16

|E\Delta B|\geq\left|E\setminus B\right|\geq\int_{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}^{1+\gamma}\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\partial B_{s}\cap E)\,ds.

Hence, there exists

\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\partial B_{\rho}\cap E)\leq 2\gamma^{-1}|E\Delta B|.

In particular, by the bound above, the definition of excess, and the divergence theorem, we have

\begin{split}\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}E\setminus B_{\rho})&\leq 2{\rm Exc}(E)+\int_{\partial^{*}E\setminus B_{\rho}}\frac{x}{|x|}\cdot\nu_{E}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\\

&\leq 2{\rm Exc}(E)+(n-1)|E\setminus B_{\rho}|+2\gamma^{-1}|E\Delta B|\\
&\leq 2{\rm Exc}(E)+C(n,\gamma)|E\Delta B|,\end{split}

where we used that

&\leq 2{\rm Exc}(E)+\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\partial B_{\rho}\setminus E)\\
&\leq 2{\rm Exc}(E)+\gamma^{-1}|E\Delta B|,\end{split}

which still implies (as it was for (

is small for all

Concerning (

in

on

, the divergence theorem

\begin{split}\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\partial B_{r}\setminus E)&=\int_{\partial B_{r}\setminus E}\frac{x}{|x|}\cdot\nu_{B_{r}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\\
&\leq\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}E\cap B_{r})+\int_{\partial^{*}(B_{r}\setminus E)}\frac{x}{r}\cdot\nu_{B_{r}\setminus E}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\\
&=\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}E\cap B_{r})+\frac{n}{r}|B_{r}\setminus E|\\

We now show that we can slightly translate a set close to the ball to ensure that its “weighted barycenter” coincides with the origin.
We write

Lemma 2.4
.

There exists a dimensional constant

such that the following is true:
Let

satisfy

Then there exists

, with
|x_{0}|\leq 2|x_{1}|
, such that

\int_{F+x_{0}}\frac{x}{|x|}\,dx=0.

Proof.

Consider the continuous map

\Psi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n},\qquad y\mapsto y-\,-\hskip-10.80003pt\int_{F+y}\frac{x}{|x|}\,dx.

By a change of variable and the fact that
|x|^{-1}\in L^{1}_{{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),n\geq 2

\,-\hskip-10.80003pt\int_{F+t\theta}\frac{x}{|x|}\,dx=\,-\hskip-10.80003pt\int_{F}\frac{x+t\theta}{|x+t\theta|}\,dx=x_{1}+t\,-\hskip-10.80003pt\int_{F}\bigg(\frac{\theta}{|x|}-\frac{(x\cdot\theta)x}{|x|^{3}}\bigg)\,dx+o(t)

uniformly with respect to
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
.
Also, since

\,-\hskip-10.80003pt\int_{B}\frac{1}{|x|}\left({\rm Id}-\frac{x\otimes x}{|x|^{2}}\right)\,dx={\rm Id},\,

if
|F\Delta B|\leq\eta
then the symmetric matrix

A_{F}:=\,-\hskip-10.80003pt\int_{F}\frac{1}{|x|}\left(Id-\frac{x\otimes x}{|x|^{2}}\right)\,dx\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}

satisfies

, where
o_{\eta}(1)\to 0
as
\eta\to 0
.
Thus, if
|y|\leq 2|x_{1}|\leq 2\eta\ll 1
, we get

|\Psi(y)|=|y-x_{1}-A_{F}y+o(|y|)|\leq|x_{1}|+|y-A_{F}y|+o(|y|)\leq|x_{1}|+o_{\eta}(1)|y|+o(|y|)\leq 2|x_{1}|.

This implies that

\,-\hskip-10.80003pt\int_{F+x_{0}}\frac{x}{|x|}\,dx=0.

Lemma 2.5

Let

|\mathbf{H}_{\partial G_{r}}|\leq|\mathcal{H}_{\partial^{*}E}|\,\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\lfloor_{\partial^{*}E\setminus B_{r}}+\frac{n-1}{r}\,\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\lfloor_{E^{(1)}\cap\partial B_{r}}+C\mathscr{H}^{n-2}\lfloor_{\partial^{*}E\cap\partial B_{r}},

and the constant

is absolute.

Proof.

By coarea formula, we may assume that

\mathscr{H}^{n-2}(\partial^{*}E\cap\partial B_{r})<\infty.

By the definition of scalar distributional mean curvature, for
X\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\,\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
|X|\leq 1
and
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have

\int_{\partial^{*}E}\big(\varphi\,{\rm div}_{\tau}X-\varphi\,\mathcal{H}_{\partial^{*}E}\,X\cdot\nu_{E}\big)\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}=-\int_{\partial^{*}E}X\cdot\nabla_{\tau}\varphi\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}.

By approximation, we can consider the Lipschitz function

\varphi(x):=\phi\left(\frac{|x|}{\epsilon}+1-\frac{r}{\epsilon}\right)\qquad\text{where }\quad\phi(t):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\text{for }t\leq 1,\\
t-1&\text{for }1\leq t\leq 2,\\
1&\text{for }t\geq 2,\end{array}\right.\qquad

and
\epsilon>0
is small. This, together with the coarea formula
[

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\partial^{*}E\setminus B_{r}}\varphi\,{\rm div}_{\tau}X-\varphi\,\mathcal{H}_{\partial^{*}E}X\cdot\nu_{E}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|-\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int_{\partial^{*}E\cap(B_{r+\epsilon}\setminus\overline{B}_{r})}\phi^{\prime}\left(\frac{|x|}{\epsilon}+1-\frac{r}{\epsilon}\right)\left(\frac{x}{|x|}-\frac{x\cdot\nu_{E}}{|x|}\nu_{E}\right)\cdot X\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon^{-1}\int_{r}^{r+\epsilon}\int_{\partial^{*}E\cap\partial B_{s}}\frac{\left|\frac{x}{|x|}-\frac{x\cdot\nu_{E}}{|x|}\nu_{E}\right|}{\sqrt{1-\left(\frac{x}{|x|}\cdot\nu_{E}\right)^{2}}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-2}\,ds.

and letting
\epsilon\to 0
, we obtain that

Likewise, for

with

and

, we also have

Now we choose, for

and applying a similar argument on the smooth hypersurface

, with

, leads to

Since, up to

-negligible sets,

\partial^{*}G_{r}=\big(\partial^{*}E\setminus B_{r}\big)\cup\big(E^{(1)}\cap\partial B_{r}\big),

and the outer unit normal of

3.
A spectral gap estimate on sets of finite perimeter

In his celebrated work
[
19
]
, Fuglede proved a sharp stability result for the isoperimetric inequality on
\sigma
-spherical sets by exploiting a spectral gap for the Laplacian on the sphere for functions.
Our aim is to find a suitable generalization of his estimate for sets of finite perimeter contained in a small annulus around the sphere. The main result of this section, Proposition
3.7
, is based on a delicate interplay between compactness estimates on BV functions and suitable rearrangement techniques.

Recall that a function
u\in L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
is of bounded variation (BV) if its distributional tangential gradient
\nabla_{\tau}u
is a finite measure.
By the decomposition of measure, one can write
D_{\tau}u=\nabla_{\tau}u\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}+D^{s}_{\tau}u
, where
D^{s}_{\tau}u
is singular with respect to
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}
. Also,
\|D_{\tau}u\|(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
denotes the total mass of
D_{\tau}u
, that is,

\|D_{\tau}u\|(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\nabla_{\tau}u|\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|D^{s}_{\tau}u|.

We begin by proving the following inequality.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\xi\in{BV(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}
satisfy, for some
0<\sigma=\sigma(n)\ll 1
,

(3.1)

\left|\frac{1}{n|B|}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\xi\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\right|\leq\sigma\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon^{2}|\xi|^{2}}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Then, for any
0<\epsilon<\frac{1}{2},

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon^{2}|\xi|^{2}}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\leq C(n)\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{|\nabla_{\tau}\xi|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon^{2}|\nabla_{\tau}\xi|^{2}}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}+\epsilon^{-1}|D^{s}_{\tau}\xi|(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})\right).

In addition, assume that there exists a sequence
\xi_{i}\in BV(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
satisfying, for every

,

(3.2)

\left|\frac{1}{n|B|}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\xi_{i}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\right|\leq\sigma\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{|\xi_{i}|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon_{i}^{2}|\xi_{i}|^{2}}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

and

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{|\nabla_{\tau}\xi_{i}|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon_{i}^{2}|\nabla_{\tau}\xi_{i}|^{2}}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}+\epsilon_{i}^{-1}|D^{s}_{\tau}\xi_{i}|(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})\leq M

for some
\epsilon_{i}\to 0
and
M>0
.
Then, up to passing to a subsequence,

\xi_{i}\to\xi
strongly in
L^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
for some
1<p=p(n)<2
(below the BV-Sobolev compactness exponent on
S^{n-1}
),
and

(3.3)

\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{|\xi_{i}|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon_{i}^{2}|\xi_{i}|^{2}}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}{|\xi|^{2}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}.

Proof.

Step 1: Energy inequalities.

Given
\xi\in BV(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
, we set for simplicity

\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon^{2}|\xi|^{2}}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1},

\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}(\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{|\nabla_{\tau}\xi|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon^{2}|\nabla_{\tau}\xi|^{2}}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}+\epsilon^{-1}|D^{s}_{\tau}\xi|(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}).

We first prove the estimate for smooth functions. The passage to
BV
follows by strict approximation on the sphere. Indeed, after subtracting constants, one may choose

\xi_{h}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
with

\int\xi_{h}=\int\xi
,
\xi_{h}\to\xi
in

, and

D_{\tau}\xi_{h}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}D_{\tau}\xi
with

. Since the
recession function of

is

\epsilon^{-1}|p|
, Reshetnyak’s theorem gives

\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}(\xi_{h})\to\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}(\xi)
and

\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(\xi_{h})\to\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(\xi)
. We may therefore work
in the smooth setup.

We first consider the case where
\sigma=0
in (
3.1
).

Case 1
:

(3.4)

\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}{|\xi|^{2}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\leq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon^{2}|\xi|^{2}}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\leq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}{|\xi|^{2}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}.

We claim that, for any

(3.5)

|\xi||\nabla_{\tau}\xi|\leq C(\beta)\frac{|\nabla_{\tau}\xi|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon^{2}|\nabla_{\tau}\xi|^{2}}}+\beta|\xi|^{2}.

Indeed, if

When

SubCase 2.1

We first assume that

(3.13)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\eta|\,\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\leq\epsilon^{-1}|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap\{\epsilon|\tilde{\xi}|>1\}|.

Therefore, as
|\psi|=\epsilon^{-1}
on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap\{\epsilon|\tilde{\xi}|>1\}
,

(3.14)

\epsilon^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\eta|\,\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\leq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap\{\epsilon|\tilde{\xi}|>1\}}\epsilon^{-2}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\leq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}{|\psi|^{2}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}.

Thus, (
3.10
) directly gives (
3.12
).

SubCase 2.2
:
When

(3.15)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\eta|\,\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\geq\epsilon^{-1}|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap\{\epsilon|\tilde{\xi}|>1\}|,

we write

\mathcal{E}=\{\epsilon|\nabla_{\tau}\xi|<1\}\cap\{\epsilon|\tilde{\xi}|>1\}\cap\mathbb{S}^{n-1},\quad\mathcal{F}=\{\epsilon|\nabla_{\tau}\xi|\geq 1\}\cap\{\epsilon|\tilde{\xi}|>1\}\cap\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.

Then, since
\nabla_{\tau}\eta=0

\mathscr{H}^{n-1}
-a.e. on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap\{\epsilon|\tilde{\xi}|\leq 1\}
, the
(1,1)
-Poincaré inequality implies

(3.16)

\begin{split}\epsilon^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\eta|\,\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}&\leq C(n)\epsilon^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\nabla_{\tau}\eta|\,\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\\
&=C(n)\epsilon^{-1}\int_{\mathcal{E}}|\nabla_{\tau}\eta|\,\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}+C(n)\epsilon^{-1}\int_{\mathcal{F}}|\nabla_{\tau}\eta|\,\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}.\end{split}

SubCase 2.2.1
:
Suppose first that

(3.17)

\int_{\mathcal{F}}|\nabla_{\tau}\eta|\,\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\geq\int_{\mathcal{E}}|\nabla_{\tau}\eta|\,\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}.

Then, by the definition of
\mathcal{F}
and (
3.16
),

(3.18)

\epsilon^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\eta|\,\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\leq C(n)\epsilon^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\nabla_{\tau}\eta|\,\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\\
\leq 2C(n)\epsilon^{-1}\int_{\mathcal{F}}|\nabla_{\tau}\eta|\,\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\leq 2\sqrt{2}C(n)\int_{\mathcal{F}}\frac{|\nabla_{\tau}\xi|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon^{2}|\nabla_{\tau}\xi|^{2}}}\,\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1},

where we used that
\nabla_{\tau}\eta=\nabla_{\tau}\xi

\mathscr{H}^{n-1}
-a.e. on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap\{\epsilon|\tilde{\xi}|>1\}
together with the definition of
\mathcal{F}
.
This gives (
3.12
).

SubCase 2.2.2
: Now the remaining case is

(3.19)

\int_{\mathcal{F}}|\nabla_{\tau}\eta|\,\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\leq\int_{\mathcal{E}}|\nabla_{\tau}\eta|\,\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}.

Notice that (
3.15
) yields

|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap\{\epsilon|\tilde{\xi}|>1\}|=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap\{\epsilon|\tilde{\xi}|>1\}}1\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\leq\epsilon\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\eta|\,\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}.

Therefore, since
\tilde{\xi}=\psi+\eta
, the definition of
\mathcal{E}
gives

(3.20)

|\mathcal{E}|\leq\int_{\mathcal{E}}\epsilon|\tilde{\xi}|\,\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap\{\epsilon|\tilde{\xi}|>1\}}(1+\epsilon|\eta|)\,\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\leq 2\epsilon\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\eta|\,\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}.

Thus, by (
3.16
), (
3.19
), Hölder’s inequality, the definition of
\mathcal{E}
, and (
3.20
), we get

\displaystyle\epsilon^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\eta|\,\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}

\displaystyle\leq 2C(n)\epsilon^{-1}\int_{\mathcal{E}}|\nabla_{\tau}\eta|\,\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}=2C(n)\epsilon^{-1}\int_{\mathcal{E}}|\nabla_{\tau}\xi|\,\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}

\displaystyle\leq 2C(n)\epsilon^{-1}\left(\int_{\mathcal{E}}\frac{|\nabla_{\tau}\xi|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon^{2}|\nabla_{\tau}\xi|^{2}}}\,\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\mathcal{E}}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon^{2}|\nabla_{\tau}\xi|^{2}}}\,\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sqrt{2}C(n)\epsilon^{-1}|\mathcal{E}|^{1/2}\left(\int_{\mathcal{E}}\frac{|\nabla_{\tau}\xi|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon^{2}|\nabla_{\tau}\xi|^{2}}}\,\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq 4C(n)\epsilon^{-1/2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\eta|\,\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\mathcal{E}}\frac{|\nabla_{\tau}\xi|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon^{2}|\nabla_{\tau}\xi|^{2}}}\,\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\right)^{1/2}

which yields (
3.12
) and

(3.21)

\epsilon^{-1}\int_{\mathcal{E}}|\nabla_{\tau}\eta|\,\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\leq C(n)\int_{\mathcal{E}}\frac{|\nabla_{\tau}\xi|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon^{2}|\nabla_{\tau}\xi|^{2}}}\,\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}.

This concludes the proof of the first part of the lemma when
\sigma=0
.

We now treat the general case in (
3.1
). Let

m:=\frac{1}{n|B|}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\xi\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1},\qquad\bar{\xi}:=\xi-m.

Then
\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\bar{\xi}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}=0
and
\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}(\bar{\xi})=\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}(\xi)
.
Set

g_{\epsilon}(t):=\min\{|t|^{2},\epsilon^{-1}|t|\}.

One easily checks that

\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,g_{\epsilon}(t)\leq\frac{|t|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon^{2}|t|^{2}}}\leq g_{\epsilon}(t)\qquad\forall\,t\in\mathbb{R},

and that

g_{\epsilon}(a+b)\leq 2g_{\epsilon}(a)+2g_{\epsilon}(b)\qquad\forall\,a,b\in\mathbb{R}.

Hence

\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(\xi)=\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(\bar{\xi}+m)\leq C\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(\bar{\xi})+C\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(m)\leq C\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(\bar{\xi})+C|m|^{2}.

Applying the already proved case
\sigma=0
to
\bar{\xi}
, we get

\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(\bar{\xi})\leq C(n)\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}(\bar{\xi})=C(n)\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}(\xi).

On the other hand, (
3.1
) gives

|m|^{2}\leq\sigma^{2}\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(\xi).

Choosing
\sigma=\sigma(n)>0
sufficiently small, we can absorb the
|m|^{2}
term into the left-hand side and conclude the first part of the lemma in full generality.

Step 2: Energy convergence.
In this second part, we argue directly in
BV
.
By the first part, the quantities

\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon_{i}}(\xi_{i})

are uniformly bounded. In particular, (
3.2
) yields a uniform bound on the averages

m_{i}:=\frac{1}{n|B|}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\xi_{i}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}.

Also, since
\epsilon_{i}<1
for
i
large,

|a|\leq 1+\frac{2|a|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+|a|^{2}}}\leq 1+\frac{2|a|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon_{i}^{2}|a|^{2}}}\qquad\forall\,a\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

and therefore

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\nabla_{\tau}\xi_{i}|\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}+|D^{s}_{\tau}\xi_{i}|(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})\leq C(n,M).

Hence, up to passing to a subsequence,

\xi_{i}\to\xi\qquad\text{strongly in }L^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})

for some
1<p=p(n)<2
, and then a.e. on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
.

For each
i
, let
c_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
be such that

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}T_{\epsilon_{i}}(\xi_{i}-c_{i})\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}=0.

Set

\tilde{\xi}_{i}:=\xi_{i}-c_{i},\qquad\psi_{i}:=T_{\epsilon_{i}}(\tilde{\xi}_{i}),\qquad\eta_{i}:=\tilde{\xi}_{i}-\psi_{i}.

Then the argument of Case 2 above applies verbatim to
(\tilde{\xi}_{i},\psi_{i},\eta_{i})
, and gives the analogues of (
3.9
), (
3.10
), (
3.11
), (
3.12
), (
3.18
), and (
3.21
), with constants independent of
i
.

In particular, from (
3.10
), (
3.11
), and the
BV
chain
rule for the truncation, we obtain

\sup_{i}\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\psi_{i}|^{2}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\psi_{i}\nabla_{\tau}\psi_{i}|\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}+\epsilon_{i}^{-1}|D^{s}_{\tau}\psi_{i}|(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})\right)<\infty.

Moreover
\|D_{\tau}\psi_{i}\|(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})\leq\|D_{\tau}\xi_{i}\|(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
. Hence
\psi_{i}
is compact in
L^{1}
, while

\psi_{i}^{2}
is compact in
L^{1}
, since

|D_{\tau}(\psi_{i}^{2})|\leq C|\psi_{i}\nabla_{\tau}\psi_{i}|\,\mathscr{H}^{n-1}+C\epsilon_{i}^{-1}|D^{s}_{\tau}\psi_{i}|
. Thus, up to extracting a further subsequence,

(3.22)

\psi_{i}
converges strongly in
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
to some function
v
.

Since
\epsilon_{i}\psi_{i}\to 0
a.e. on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and

\frac{|\psi_{i}|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon_{i}^{2}|\psi_{i}|^{2}}}\leq|\psi_{i}|^{2},

Vitali’s convergence theorem yields

(3.23)

\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{|\psi_{i}|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon_{i}^{2}|\psi_{i}|^{2}}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}{|v|^{2}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}.

Let

E_{i}:=\{\eta_{i}\neq 0\}=\{\epsilon_{i}|\tilde{\xi}_{i}|>1\}\subset\{|\psi_{i}|=\epsilon_{i}^{-1}\}.

Then, by (
3.22
),

(3.24)

\epsilon_{i}^{-2}|E_{i}|=\int_{E_{i}}|\psi_{i}|^{2}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\leq\int_{\{|\psi_{i}|\geq\epsilon_{i}^{-1}\}}|\psi_{i}|^{2}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\longrightarrow 0.

Moreover, the analogue of (
3.12
) gives

\epsilon_{i}^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\eta_{i}|\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\leq C(n,M),

hence

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\eta_{i}|\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\to 0.

Since

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\xi_{i}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}=c_{i}\,\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\eta_{i}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1},

and the averages of
\xi_{i}
are uniformly bounded, it follows that
\{c_{i}\}
is bounded. Up to a subsequence,

c_{i}\to c\in\mathbb{R}.

Then, by (
3.22
),

\psi_{i}+c_{i}\to v+c\qquad\text{strongly in }L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}).

Since
\eta_{i}\to 0
in
L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
and
\xi_{i}=\psi_{i}+c_{i}+\eta_{i}
, while
\xi_{i}\to\xi
in measure, we conclude that

\xi=v+c\qquad\text{a.e. on }\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.

We now show that

\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon_{i}}(\xi_{i})=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\xi|^{2}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}.

On
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\setminus E_{i}
, one has
\eta_{i}=0
, hence
\xi_{i}=\psi_{i}+c_{i}
there. Therefore

|\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon_{i}}(\xi_{i})-\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon_{i}}(\psi_{i}+c_{i})|\leq\int_{E_{i}}\frac{|\xi_{i}|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon_{i}^{2}|\xi_{i}|^{2}}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}+\int_{E_{i}}\frac{|\psi_{i}+c_{i}|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon_{i}^{2}|\psi_{i}+c_{i}|^{2}}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}.

Since
|\psi_{i}|=\epsilon_{i}^{-1}
on
E_{i}
and
c_{i}
is bounded, for
i
large enough

|\psi_{i}+c_{i}|\leq 2\epsilon_{i}^{-1}\qquad\text{on }E_{i},

and therefore

\int_{E_{i}}\frac{|\psi_{i}+c_{i}|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon_{i}^{2}|\psi_{i}+c_{i}|^{2}}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\leq C\,\epsilon_{i}^{-2}|E_{i}|\to 0

by (
3.24
). Also, on
E_{i}
,

\xi_{i}=(\psi_{i}+c_{i})+\eta_{i}.

Since
|\psi_{i}+c_{i}|\geq\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{i}^{-1}
on
E_{i}
for
i
large, one has

\frac{|\xi_{i}|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon_{i}^{2}|\xi_{i}|^{2}}}\leq C\frac{|\psi_{i}+c_{i}|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon_{i}^{2}|\psi_{i}+c_{i}|^{2}}}+C\epsilon_{i}^{-1}|\eta_{i}|\qquad\text{on }E_{i}.

Hence

\int_{E_{i}}\frac{|\xi_{i}|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon_{i}^{2}|\xi_{i}|^{2}}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\leq C\int_{E_{i}}\frac{|\psi_{i}+c_{i}|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon_{i}^{2}|\psi_{i}+c_{i}|^{2}}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}+C\epsilon_{i}^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\eta_{i}|\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}.

If SubCase 2.1 occurs along a subsequence, then by the analogue of (
3.14
) and (
3.22
),

\epsilon_{i}^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\eta_{i}|\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\to 0.

If SubCase 2.2.1 or SubCase 2.2.2 occurs along a subsequence, then the analogues of (
3.18
) or (
3.21
) imply that
\epsilon_{i}^{-1}\eta_{i}
is uniformly bounded in
BV(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
; since its support is contained in
E_{i}
and
|E_{i}|\to 0
, compactness in
L^{1}
implies

\epsilon_{i}^{-1}\eta_{i}\to 0\qquad\text{strongly in }L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}).

Thus, in all cases,

\epsilon_{i}^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\eta_{i}|\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\to 0,

and consequently

\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon_{i}}(\xi_{i})-\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon_{i}}(\psi_{i}+c_{i})\to 0.

Finally, since
\psi_{i}+c_{i}\to\xi
strongly in
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
and

\frac{|\psi_{i}+c_{i}|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon_{i}^{2}|\psi_{i}+c_{i}|^{2}}}\leq|\psi_{i}+c_{i}|^{2},

Vitali’s convergence theorem again yields

\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon_{i}}(\psi_{i}+c_{i})=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\xi|^{2}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}.

Combining the last two formulae, we obtain (
3.3
). This concludes the proof.

Now we are ready to generalize (and improve, as needed in our setting) the spectral gap result of Fuglede
[
19
]
, originally proved for Lipschitz perturbations, to BV perturbations of the sphere.
In the following,
\{Y_{0},\ldots,Y_{n}\}
denote the first
n+1
eigenfunctions of the Laplace–Beltrami operator on the sphere, namely

(3.25)

Y_{0}(\theta)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n|B|}},\qquad Y_{i}(\theta)=\frac{\theta_{i}}{\sqrt{|B|}}\quad i=1,\ldots,n,

see for instance
[
20
, Proof of Theorem 3.1, Step 3]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Given any
N\geq 2n
, there exist
\zeta=\zeta(n)>0
,
\sigma_{0}=\sigma_{0}(n,N)>0
, and
\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}(n,N)>0
so that the following holds.

For each
0<\sigma<\sigma_{0}
, any set Borel
K\subset\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
with
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(K)\leq\delta_{0}
, and any function
\xi\in BV(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
satisfying both

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{|\nabla_{\tau}\xi|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla_{\tau}\xi|^{2}}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|D^{s}_{\tau}\xi|\leq\sigma,

and

(3.26)

\biggl|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}Y_{i}\,\xi\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\biggr|\leq C(n)\sigma\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+|\xi|^{2}}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\qquad\text{for }i=0,\ldots,n,

we have:

either

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\setminus K}\frac{|\nabla_{\tau}\xi|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla_{\tau}\xi|^{2}}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\setminus K}|D^{s}_{\tau}\xi|\geq\left(n-1+3\zeta\right)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+|\xi|^{2}}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1},

or

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{|\nabla_{\tau}\xi|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla_{\tau}\xi|^{2}}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|D^{s}_{\tau}\xi|\geq 2N\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+|\xi|^{2}}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}.

In particular, if in addition
\|\xi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\leq\sigma
, then:

either

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\setminus K}\frac{|\nabla_{\tau}\xi|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla_{\tau}\xi|^{2}}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\setminus K}|D^{s}_{\tau}\xi|\geq\left(n-1+2\zeta\right)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\xi|^{2}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1},

or

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{|\nabla_{\tau}\xi|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla_{\tau}\xi|^{2}}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|D^{s}_{\tau}\xi|\geq N\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\xi|^{2}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}.

Proof.

First of all, it follows from
[
20
, Proof of Theorem 3.1, Step 3]
that, when
\sigma\ll 1
in (
3.27
), the Poincaré constant for functions in
W^{1,\,2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
is arbitrarily close to
2n
. More precisely, for any
\xi\in W^{1,\,2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
satisfying

(3.27)

\biggl|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}Y_{i}\,\xi\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\biggr|\leq C(n)\sigma\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\xi|^{2}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\qquad\text{for }i=0,\ldots,n,

it holds that,

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}{|\nabla_{\tau}\xi|^{2}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\geq\left(2n-C(n)\sigma\right)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\xi^{2}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1},

In particular, given
\zeta=\zeta(n)\in\left(0,\frac{n-1}{4}\right)
, one can choose
\sigma
sufficiently small so that

(3.28)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}{|\nabla_{\tau}\xi|^{2}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\geq(n-1+4\zeta)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\xi^{2}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\quad\forall\,\xi\in W^{1,\,2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})\text{ satisfying \eqref{almost orthogonal}}.

We prove the result by contradiction. If it fails, Lemma
3.1
gives numbers
\sigma_{i}\downarrow 0
,
sets
K_{i}\subset\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
with

\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(K_{i})=:\delta_{i}\to 0
, and functions

\xi_{i}\in BV(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
satisfying (
3.26
),
with

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{|\nabla_{\tau}\xi_{i}|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla_{\tau}\xi_{i}|^{2}}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}+|D^{s}_{\tau}\xi_{i}|(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})\leq\sigma_{i},

such that both inequalities

(3.29)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\setminus K_{i}}\frac{|\nabla_{\tau}\xi_{i}|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla_{\tau}\xi_{i}|^{2}}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}+|D^{s}_{\tau}\xi_{i}|(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\setminus K_{i})\leq\left(n-1+3\zeta\right)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{|\xi_{i}|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+|\xi_{i}|^{2}}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1},

and

(3.30)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{|\nabla_{\tau}\xi_{i}|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla_{\tau}\xi_{i}|^{2}}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}+|D^{s}_{\tau}\xi_{i}|(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})\leq 2N\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{|\xi_{i}|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+|\xi_{i}|^{2}}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}

hold.

Define

\alpha_{i}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{|\nabla_{\tau}\xi_{i}|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla_{\tau}\xi_{i}|^{2}}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}+|D^{s}_{\tau}\xi_{i}|(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})\right)^{1/2}\to 0,

We may assume

and consider

indeed, if

, then

\xi_{i}
is constant, and the condition (
3.26
) with

forces

for

Then according to (

\hat{\xi}_{i}
satisfies

\biggl|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}Y_{i}\,\hat{\xi}_{i}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\biggr|\leq C(n)\sigma_{i}\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{|\hat{\xi}_{i}|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+\alpha_{i}^{2}|\hat{\xi}_{i}|^{2}}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\qquad\text{for }i=0,\ldots,n,

and

(3.31)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{|\nabla_{\tau}\hat{\xi}_{i}|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+\alpha_{i}^{2}|\nabla_{\tau}\hat{\xi}_{i}|^{2}}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}+\alpha_{i}^{-1}|D^{s}_{\tau}\hat{\xi}_{i}|(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})=1.

Also, it follows from (
3.29
), (
3.30
), (
3.31
), and Lemma

that

(3.32)

\max\left\{\frac{1}{n-1+3\zeta}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\setminus K_{i}}\frac{|\nabla_{\tau}\hat{\xi}_{i}|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+\alpha_{i}^{2}|\nabla_{\tau}\hat{\xi}_{i}|^{2}}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1},\frac{1}{2N}\right\}\\
\leq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{|\hat{\xi}_{i}|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+\alpha_{i}^{2}|\hat{\xi}_{i}|^{2}}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\leq C(n).

In particular, using Lemma
3.1
again, up to passing to a subsequence, the functions
\hat{\xi}_{i}
converge strongly in
L^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
,
p>1
,
to some function
\hat{\xi}_{\infty}
satisfying (
3.27
).
Furthermore,

(3.33)

\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{|\hat{\xi}_{i}|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+\alpha_{i}^{2}|\hat{\xi}_{i}|^{2}}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}{|\hat{\xi}_{\infty}|^{2}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}.

In particular
\hat{\xi}_{\infty}\not\equiv 0
as a consequence of (

).

Now, given
\epsilon>0
small, define the sets

\mathcal{R}_{i,\epsilon}=\{x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\colon|\nabla_{\tau}\hat{\xi}_{i}|\leq\epsilon\alpha_{i}^{-1}\},\qquad\mathcal{S}_{i,\epsilon}=\{x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\colon|\nabla_{\tau}\hat{\xi}_{i}|\geq\epsilon\alpha_{i}^{-1}\}.

Since

(3.34)

1\geq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{|\nabla_{\tau}\hat{\xi}_{i}|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+\alpha_{i}^{2}|\nabla_{\tau}\hat{\xi}_{i}|^{2}}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{R}_{i,\epsilon}}|\nabla_{\tau}\hat{\xi}_{i}|^{2}\,\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}+\epsilon\alpha_{i}^{-1}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i,\epsilon}}|\nabla_{\tau}\hat{\xi}_{i}|\,\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}

and
|\nabla_{\tau}\hat{\xi}_{i}|
is uniformly bounded away from
0
inside
\mathcal{S}_{i,\epsilon}
, we deduce that

(3.35)

\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\mathcal{S}_{i,\epsilon})+\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i,\epsilon}}|\nabla_{\tau}\hat{\xi}_{i}|\,\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\leq C(n)\epsilon^{-1}\alpha_{i}\to 0\quad\text{ as }i\to\infty.

Moreover, (
3.34
) also gives

(3.36)

\|\nabla_{\tau}\hat{\xi}_{i}\,\chi_{\mathcal{R}_{i,\epsilon}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\leq C(n).

Also
|D^{s}_{\tau}\hat{\xi}_{i}|(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})\leq\alpha_{i}
by (
3.31
).
Thanks to (
3.35
) and (
3.36
), the sequence

\hat{\xi}_{i}
is uniformly bounded in
BV
, the singular parts vanish, and

\nabla_{\tau}\hat{\xi}_{i}
is uniformly integrable. Hence, according to Dunford–Pettis theorem (see e.g.
[
6
]
),
\hat{\xi}_{\infty}\in W^{1,1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
and

\nabla_{\tau}\hat{\xi}_{i}\,\text{ converges weakly to }\,\nabla_{\tau}\hat{\xi}_{\infty}\ \text{ in $L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1},\mathbb{R}^{n})$}.

Combining the uniform integrability of
\nabla_{\tau}\hat{\xi}_{i}

with the fact that
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\mathcal{S}_{i,\epsilon})+\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(K_{i})\to 0
, we actually deduce that

\nabla_{\tau}\hat{\xi}_{i}\chi_{\mathcal{R}_{i,\epsilon}\setminus K_{i}}\,\text{ converges weakly to }\,\nabla_{\tau}\hat{\xi}_{\infty}\ \text{ in $L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1},\mathbb{R}^{n})$}.

Noticing now that the left-hand side above is uniformly bounded in
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
(see (
3.36
)), it follows that
\nabla_{\tau}\hat{\xi}_{\infty}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1},\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and that the weak convergence above actually holds in
L^{2}
:

\nabla_{\tau}\hat{\xi}_{i}\chi_{\mathcal{R}_{i,\epsilon}\setminus K_{i}}\,\text{ converges weakly to }\,\nabla_{\tau}\hat{\xi}_{\infty}\ \text{ in }L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1},\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Therefore, by weak lower-semicontinuity we obtain

\displaystyle\liminf_{i\to\infty}\int_{\mathcal{R}_{i,\epsilon}\setminus K_{i}}\frac{|\nabla_{\tau}\hat{\xi}_{i}|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+\alpha_{i}^{2}|\nabla_{\tau}\hat{\xi}_{i}|^{2}}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}

\displaystyle\geq(1+\epsilon^{2})^{-1/2}\liminf_{i\to\infty}\int_{\mathcal{R}_{i,\epsilon}\setminus K_{i}}|\nabla_{\tau}\hat{\xi}_{i}|^{2}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}

(3.37)

\displaystyle\geq(1+\epsilon^{2})^{-1/2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\nabla_{\tau}\hat{\xi}_{\infty}|^{2}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}.

Thus, recalling (
3.32
) and (
3.33
), we get

\frac{1}{(1+\epsilon^{2})^{1/2}\left(n-1+3\zeta\right)}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}{|\nabla_{\tau}\hat{\xi}_{\infty}|^{2}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\leq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\hat{\xi}_{\infty}|^{2}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}.

Since
\hat{\xi}_{\infty}
satisfies (
3.27
),
this contradicts (
3.28
) for
\epsilon>0
sufficiently small. This concludes the proof of the first part of the lemma.

The second part of the lemma follows immediately, since

\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|\xi|^{2}}}\geq 1-\sigma

when
\|\xi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\leq\sigma\ll 1.
Hence, it suffices to choose
\sigma
sufficiently small, depending on
n
and
\zeta=\zeta(n)
.
∎

Our next step is to generalize Lemma
3.2
to general sets of finite perimeter. We first prove two auxiliary lemmas.
In the following, given
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we denote by

\mathcal{C}_{U}:=\big\{t\theta\colon t>0,\theta\in U\big\}

the cone over it.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
0<\sigma=\sigma(n)<\frac{1}{4}
small, and
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a set of finite perimeter with
|G|=|B|
and

B_{1-\sigma}\subset G\subset B_{1+\sigma}.

Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}:=\big\{\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\colon\#\big\{(\partial^{*}G\setminus\big\{x\in\partial^{*}G\,:\,x\cdot\nu_{G}=0\big\})\cap\{t\theta\colon t>0\}\big\}\geq 2\big\}.

Suppose that

B_{1/2}\subset G\subset B_{2}
.
Then, for
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}
-a.e.
\theta\in\mathcal{B}
, there are numbers

0<a_{0}(\theta)<b_{1}(\theta)<a_{1}(\theta)<\cdots<b_{k(\theta)}(\theta)<a_{k(\theta)}(\theta),

with
k(\theta)\geq 1
, such that

(3.38)

G_{\theta}:=G\cap\{t\theta:t>0\}=(0,a_{0}(\theta))\cup\bigcup_{j=1}^{k(\theta)}(b_{j}(\theta),a_{j}(\theta))

up to one-dimensional negligible sets.
Moreover,

(3.39)

\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\big\{x\in\partial^{*}G\,:\,x\cdot\nu_{G}=0\big\})+\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\mathcal{B})+\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}G\cap\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{B}})\leq C(n){\rm Exc}(G).

In particular,

(3.40)

\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}G\cap\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{B}})\leq C(n)\int_{\partial^{*}G\cap\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{B}}}\left(1-\frac{x}{|x|}\cdot\nu_{G}\right)\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}.

Proof.

By the definition of excess,

(3.41)

\begin{split}{\rm Exc}(G)&\geq\int_{\big\{x\in\partial^{*}G\,:\,x\cdot\nu_{G}=0\big\}}\left(1-\frac{x}{|x|}\cdot\nu_{G}\right)\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}=\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\big\{x\in\partial^{*}G\,:\,x\cdot\nu_{G}=0\big\}),\end{split}

(3.42)

\begin{split}{\rm Exc}(G)&\geq\int_{\partial^{*}G\cap\mathcal{C}_{U}}\left(1-\frac{x}{|x|}\cdot\nu_{G}\right)\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\qquad\forall\,U\subset\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\text{ Borel.}\end{split}

Set

\mathcal{T}_{G}:=\big\{x\in\partial^{*}G:\ x\cdot\nu_{G}=0\big\}.

Applying the area formula
[
16
, Theorem 1, Section 3.3.3]
to

(\partial^{*}G\cap\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{B}})\setminus\mathcal{T}_{G}
, it follows from
(
3.42
) with
U=\mathcal{B}
that

(3.43)

\displaystyle{\rm Exc}(G)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\partial^{*}G\cap\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{B}}}\left(1-\frac{x}{|x|}\cdot\nu_{G}\right)\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\geq\int_{\mathcal{B}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\partial^{*}G,\\
x\cdot\nu_{G}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{x}{|x|}\cdot\nu_{G}\right)\frac{|x|^{n}}{|x\cdot\nu_{G}|}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\theta),

and

(3.44)

\mathscr{H}^{n-1}((\partial^{*}G\cap\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{B}})\setminus\mathcal{T}_{G})=\int_{\mathcal{B}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\partial^{*}G,\\
x\cdot\nu_{G}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{|x|^{n}}{|x\cdot\nu_{G}|}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\theta).

We next observe that, thanks to
[
3
, Chapters 3.2 and 3.11]
and
[
10
, Theorem G]
, for
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}
-a.e.
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
the set

\{t\theta\colon t>0\}\cap G

is of finite perimeter in
(0,\infty)
. In particular, by the definition of
\mathcal{B}
, for
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}
-a.e.
\theta\in\mathcal{B}
there exist
\{a_{i}=a_{i}(\theta)\}_{0\leq i\leq k(\theta)}
and
\{b_{i}=b_{i}(\theta)\}_{1\leq i\leq k(\theta)}
, with
0<a_{0}<b_{1}<a_{1}<\cdots<b_{k}<a_{k}
and
k=k(\theta)\geq 1
, such that

\{t\theta\colon t>0\}\cap G=(0,\,a_{0})\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}(b_{i},\,a_{i}).

This proves (
3.38
).

Moreover, since
\partial G\subset A_{1-\sigma,\,1+\sigma}
, we have

a_{i},\,b_{i}\in(1-\sigma,\,1+\sigma).

Now let us fix such a
\theta
. By the structure of (
3.38
), we always have

(3.45)

x\cdot\nu_{G}\leq 0\quad\text{ when $x=b_{i}\theta$},

and hence at this point

(3.46)

\left(1-\frac{x}{|x|}\cdot\nu_{G}\right)\frac{|x|^{n}}{|x\cdot\nu_{G}|}\geq\frac{|x|^{n}}{|x\cdot\nu_{G}|}.

Now we
discuss in two cases depending on the normal of
G
at
x=a_{i}\theta,

0\leq i\leq k
.

Case 1:

\frac{x}{|x|}\cdot\nu_{G}(x)<\frac{7}{8}.

Then

1-\frac{x}{|x|}\cdot\nu_{G}(x)\geq\frac{1}{8},

and hence

(3.47)

\left(1-\frac{x}{|x|}\cdot\nu_{G}(x)\right)\frac{|x|^{n}}{|x\cdot\nu_{G}(x)|}\geq\frac{1}{8}\frac{|x|^{n}}{|x\cdot\nu_{G}(x)|}.

Case 2:

\frac{x}{|x|}\cdot\nu_{G}(x)\geq\frac{7}{8}.

In this case, since
|x|\in(1-\sigma,\,1+\sigma)
,

\frac{|x|^{n}}{|x\cdot\nu|}\leq\frac{8}{7}(1+C(n)\sigma).

Let
\hat{x}=b_{i}\theta
with
b_{0}:=b_{1}.
Then by (
3.45
) one has

\left(1-\frac{\hat{x}}{|\hat{x}|}\cdot\nu_{G}(\hat{x})\right)\frac{|\hat{x}|^{n}}{|\hat{x}\cdot\nu_{G}(\hat{x})|}\geq 1-C(n)\sigma.

Therefore, for
\sigma=\sigma(n)>0
small we get

(3.48)

\left(1-\frac{\hat{x}}{|\hat{x}|}\cdot\nu_{G}(\hat{x})\right)\frac{|\hat{x}|^{n}}{|\hat{x}\cdot\nu_{G}(\hat{x})|}\geq c(n)\frac{|x|^{n}}{|x\cdot\nu_{G}(x)|}.

Combining now (
3.46
) with (
3.47
) and (
3.48
), we eventually conclude that, for
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}
-a.e.
\theta\in\mathcal{B}
,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\{t\theta\colon t>0\}\cap\partial^{*}G,\\
x\cdot\nu_{G}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{|x|^{n}}{|x\cdot\nu_{G}|}\leq C(n)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\{t\theta\colon t>0\}\cap\partial^{*}G,\\
x\cdot\nu_{G}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{x}{|x|}\cdot\nu_{G}\right)\frac{|x|^{n}}{|x\cdot\nu_{G}|}.

Recalling (
3.41
), (
3.43
), and
(
3.44
), we get

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}G\cap\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{B}})

\displaystyle\leq\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\mathcal{T}_{G}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{B}})

\displaystyle\quad+C(n)\int_{\mathcal{B}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\partial^{*}G,\\
x\cdot\nu_{G}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{x}{|x|}\cdot\nu_{G}\right)\frac{|x|^{n}}{|x\cdot\nu_{G}|}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\theta)

(3.49)

\displaystyle\leq C(n){\rm Exc}(G),

which also yields (
3.40
).

The estimate on
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(B)
follows from a similar (but simpler) argument. Indeed, for
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}
-a.e.
\theta\in\mathcal{B}
, choose the first non-tangential reentry point
b_{\theta}\theta
in (
3.38
). Since
|b_{\theta}|>\frac{1}{2}
,

(3.50)

{\rm Exc}(G)\geq\int_{\mathcal{B}}\frac{b_{\theta}^{n}}{b_{\theta}|\theta\cdot\nu_{G}(b_{\theta}\theta)|}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\theta)\geq\int_{\mathcal{B}}b_{\theta}^{n-1}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\theta)\geq 2^{-(n-1)}\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\mathcal{B}).

Combining (
3.41
), (
3
), and (
3.50
), we deduce the validity of (
3.39
).

∎

To proceed further, given a set of finite perimeter
G
, we consider its star-shaped rearrangement
G^{S}
defined as follows: for
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, define
G_{\theta}:=G\cap\{t\theta\colon t>0\}

and

h(\theta)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\int_{G_{\theta}}r^{n-1}\,d\mathscr{H}^{1}(r)&\text{when }\ \mathscr{H}^{1}(G_{\theta})>0,\\
0&\text{when }\ \mathscr{H}^{1}(G_{\theta})=0.\end{array}\right.

Then, set
R(\theta)=(nh(\theta))^{\frac{1}{n}}
and

G^{S}=\{t\theta\colon\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1},\,0\leq t<R(\theta)\}.

We observe that

(3.51)

|G^{S}|=|G|,

see
[
34
,
35
]
.
In addition, since by construction

\displaystyle\int_{G_{\theta}}r^{n-1}\,dr=h(\theta)=\int_{0}^{R(\theta)}r^{n-1}\,dr,

we deduce that

\left|\int_{G^{S}}\frac{x}{|x|}\,dx-\int_{G}\frac{x}{|x|}\,dx\right|\leq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left|\int_{G_{\theta}}r^{n-1}\,dr-\int_{0}^{R(\theta)}r^{n-1}\,dr\right|\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\theta)=0,

that is,
G
and
G^{S}
have the same weighted barycenter:

(3.52)

\int_{G^{S}}\frac{x}{|x|}\,dx=\int_{G}\frac{x}{|x|}\,dx.

However it is not generally true that

P(G^{S})\leq P(G),

see
[
35
, Lemma 1.2]
.
Nevertheless, we can show the following:

Lemma 3.4
.

For any
\sigma\in(0,1/2)
, let
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a set of finite perimeter satisfying

B_{1-\sigma}\subset G\subset B_{1+\sigma}.

Then

\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}G^{S}\cap\mathcal{C}_{U})\leq(1+C(n)\sigma)\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}G\cap\mathcal{C}_{U})\qquad\forall\,U\subset\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\text{ Borel.}

In particular,
G^{S}=B+u
with
u\in BV(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
.

Proof.

Consider a family of open disjoint subsets
\{\mathcal{V}_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq M=M(n,\sigma)}
whose closures cover
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and such that

(3.53)

\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\big((\partial^{*}G\cup\partial^{*}G^{S})\cap\partial\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}_{i}}\big)=0\qquad\forall\,i=1,\ldots,M.

We also assume that each set
\mathcal{V}_{i}
has diameter at most
\sigma
,
so that it can be written as a
C(n)\sigma
-Lipschitz graph over
\mathcal{W}_{i}\subset\Pi_{i}
, where
\Pi_{i}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a
(n-1)
-dimensional linear subspace.

Fix
i\in\{1,\ldots,M\}
and assume, without loss of generality, that
\Pi_{i}=\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\{0\}
and that
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}_{i}}
points in the
e_{n}
direction. Then, writing
x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}
, we define

\Psi_{i}:\left(B_{1+\sigma}\setminus B_{1-\sigma}\right)\cap\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}_{i}}\to\mathcal{W}_{i}\times[0,3\sigma],\qquad x\mapsto\left(\frac{x^{\prime}}{|x|},\int_{1-\sigma}^{|x|}r^{n-1}\,dr\right).

In this way

\Psi_{i}\colon\left(B_{1+\sigma}\setminus B_{1-\sigma}\right)\cap\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}_{i}}\to\mathcal{W}_{i}\times[0,3\sigma]

is a
\big(1+C(n)\sigma\big)
-biLipschitz map such that:

-
\Psi_{i}
sends radial lines to lines parallel to
e_{n}
;

-
\mathcal{W}_{i}\times\{0\}=\Psi_{i}\left(\partial B_{1-\sigma}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}_{i}}\right)
;

- composing
\Psi_{i}
with the two-valued mapping

\mathcal{R}\colon\mathcal{W}_{i}\times[0,3\sigma]\to\mathcal{W}_{i}\times[-3\sigma,3\sigma],\qquad\mathcal{R}(x^{\prime},x_{n})=\big\{(x^{\prime},-x_{n}),(x^{\prime},x_{n})\big\}

our star-shaped rearrangement of
G
corresponds, inside
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}_{i}}
, to the Steiner symmetrization of
\mathcal{R}\circ\Psi_{i}\big((G\cap\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}_{i}})\setminus B_{1-\sigma}\big)
with respect to the hyperplane
\Pi_{i}
.

Since the Steiner symmetrization locally decreases the perimeter
[
28
, Theorem 14.4]
, it follows that

2\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\left(\Psi_{i}(\partial^{*}G^{S}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}_{i}\cap U})\right)=\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\left(\mathcal{R}\circ\Psi_{i}(\partial^{*}G^{S}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}_{i}\cap U})\right)\\
\leq\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\left(\mathcal{R}\circ\Psi_{i}(\partial^{*}G\cap\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}_{i}\cap U})\right)=2\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\left(\Psi_{i}(\partial^{*}G\cap\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}_{i}\cap U})\right).

Note that the comparison is made for relative perimeters in the corresponding cylinders.
Thus, since
\Psi_{i}
is
\big(1+C(n)\sigma\big)
-biLipschitz,

\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\left(\partial^{*}G^{S}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}_{i}\cap U}\right)\leq\big(1+C(n)\sigma\big)\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\left(\partial^{*}G\cap\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}_{i}\cap U}\right).

Recalling (
3.53
), the result follows by summing the inequalities above over
i\in\{1,\ldots,M\}

∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a set of finite perimeter with

\partial^{*}G\subset A_{r,R}
for some
0<r<R<\infty
, and set

(3.54)

\Pi(x):=\frac{x}{|x|},\qquad\mathcal{T}_{G}:=\{x\in\partial^{*}G:\ x\cdot\nu_{G}(x)=0\}.

Then

\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\Pi(\mathcal{T}_{G}))=0.

Moreover, if
Z\subset A_{r,R}
is Borel with

\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\Pi(Z))=0
, then

\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\big((\partial^{*}G\setminus\mathcal{T}_{G})\cap\Pi^{-1}(\Pi(\mathcal{T}_{G}\cup Z))\big)=0.

Proof.

Since
\partial^{*}G
is countably
(n-1)
-rectifiable and lies in an annulus,

\Pi
is Lipschitz on
\partial^{*}G
. Its tangential Jacobian is

J_{\partial G}\Pi(x)=\frac{|x\cdot\nu_{G}(x)|}{|x|^{n}}\qquad\text{for }\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\text{-a.e. }x\in\partial^{*}G.

Hence
J_{\partial G}\Pi=0
on
\mathcal{T}_{G}
, and the area formula gives

\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\Pi(\mathcal{T}_{G}))=0.

Now let
Z
be as in the statement and set

A_{m}:=\big((\partial^{*}G\setminus\mathcal{T}_{G})\cap\Pi^{-1}(\Pi(\mathcal{T}_{G}\cup Z))\big)\cap\{J_{\partial G}\Pi\geq m^{-1}\}.

By the area formula again,

\frac{1}{m}\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(A_{m})\leq\int_{A_{m}}J_{\partial G}\Pi\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}=\int_{\Pi(A_{m})}N(A_{m},\theta)\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\theta)=0,

because
\Pi(A_{m})\subset\Pi(\mathcal{T}_{G}\cup Z)
and this last set is

\mathscr{H}^{n-1}
-negligible. Since

(\partial^{*}G\setminus\mathcal{T}_{G})\cap\Pi^{-1}(\Pi(\mathcal{T}_{G}\cup Z))=\bigcup_{m=1}^{\infty}A_{m},

the second assertion follows.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
G
be a set of finite perimeter satisfying

B_{1-\sigma}\subset G\subset B_{1+\sigma}
, and let

G^{S}=B+u
be its star-shaped rearrangement. Let
\mathcal{B}
be as in
Lemma
3.3
, and
\mathcal{T}_{G}
as in (
3.54
).
Then, up to

\mathscr{H}^{n-1}
-negligible sets,

\partial^{*}G\setminus(\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{B}}\cup\mathcal{T}_{G})=\partial^{*}G^{S}\setminus\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{B}},\qquad\text{with}\quad\nu_{G}=\nu_{G^{S}}\quad\text{there.}

Proof.

By Lemma
3.5
applied with
Z=\emptyset
, we have

\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\Pi(\mathcal{T}_{G}))=0
and

\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\big((\partial^{*}G\setminus\mathcal{T}_{G})\cap\Pi^{-1}(\Pi(\mathcal{T}_{G}))\big)=0.

It is therefore enough to argue away from this negligible set.
By the slicing theorem used in Lemma
3.3
, for a.e.

\theta\notin\mathcal{B}\cup\Pi(\mathcal{T}_{G})
the one-dimensional section
G_{\theta}
has exactly one
non-tangential boundary point. Since
B_{1-\sigma}\subset G\subset B_{1+\sigma}
,
this section is
(0,a(\theta))
, up to negligible sets. The rearranged radius is
therefore determined by

\int_{0}^{R(\theta)}r^{n-1}\,dr=\int_{0}^{a(\theta)}r^{n-1}\,dr,

hence
R(\theta)=a(\theta)
. The equality of the reduced boundaries and of the
normals follows from the area formula on the non-tangential part.
∎

We are now ready to show the following stability result.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a set of finite perimeter satisfying

(3.55)

|G|=|B|\quad\text{ and }\quad\int_{G}\frac{x}{|x|}\,dx=0.

Then, for any
\kappa>0
there exist
\delta_{1}=\delta_{1}(n,\,\kappa)>0
and
0<\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{1}(n,\,\kappa)<\frac{1}{n}
such that the following hold: Given
\sigma\in(0,\sigma_{1})
, assume that

{\rm Exc}(G)\leq\delta_{1}\qquad\text{and}\qquad B_{1-\sigma}\subset G\subset B_{1+\sigma}.

Then

(3.56)

\kappa\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}G\cap\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{B}})+\int_{\partial^{*}G}\left(1-\frac{(x\cdot\nu_{G})^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\right)\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\\
\geq(n-1+\zeta)\int_{\partial^{*}G}\left(\frac{1}{|x|}+|x|-2\right)\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1},

and

\int_{\partial^{*}G}\left(\frac{1}{|x|}+|x|-2\right)\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\geq c(n)|G\Delta B|^{2}.

Proof.

Let
G^{S}
denote the star-shaped rearrangement defined before. Then also
G^{S}
satisfies the same inclusion. In particular,

G^{S}=B+u

for some
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\leq\sigma.
Moreover,
u\in BV(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
by Lemma
3.4
. Its graph energy is small when
\sigma
and
{\rm Exc}(G)
are small: indeed, Lemma
3.4
applied with
U=\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and (
1.2
) give
P(G^{S})-P(B)\leq C(n)\big({\rm Exc}(G)+\sigma P(G)\big)
, and the area formula for
G^{S}=B+u
yields

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{|\nabla_{\tau}u|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla_{\tau}u|^{2}}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\,+\,|D^{s}_{\tau}u|(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})\leq\omega_{n}(\sigma,{\rm Exc}(G)),

with
\omega_{n}(\sigma,t)\to 0
as
\sigma,t\to 0
. Decreasing
\sigma_{1}
and
\delta_{1}
if necessary, we may therefore apply Lemma

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}E_{r})^{1-\eta}\leq\|R\|_{L^{2}(\partial^{*}E\setminus B_{r})}^{2}-C\frac{d}{dr}\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\left(\partial^{*}E\setminus B_{r}\right).

In particular, if we consider the non-increasing function
F(r):=\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\left(\partial^{*}E\setminus B_{r}\right)
, we proved that, for some
\rho\in(1+\sigma/8,\,1+\sigma/4)
,

F(r)^{1-\eta}\leq\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}E_{r})^{1-\eta}\leq\|R\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial^{*}E)}-CF^{\prime}(r)\qquad\text{for almost every }\ r\geq\rho.

Recalling that
\rho\leq 1+\frac{\sigma}{4}
, we make the following:

Claim:

If
Exc(E)\leq\delta
is small enough, then

(4.10)

F(r)^{1-\eta}\leq 2\|R\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial^{*}E)}\qquad\text{for almost every }\ r\geq 1+\sigma/2.

Indeed, if not, since
F
is non-increasing we would have

F(r)^{1-\eta}\leq\frac{1}{2}F(r)^{1-\eta}-CF^{\prime}(r)\qquad\text{for almost every }\ r\in(1+{\sigma}/4,\,1+\sigma/2),

or equivalently

\frac{d}{dr}F(r)^{\eta}=\eta F^{\prime}(r)F(r)^{\eta-1}\leq-\frac{\eta}{2C}\qquad\text{for almost every }r\in(1+{\sigma}/4,\,1+\sigma/2).

Integrating this ODE over
(1+\sigma/4,\,1+\sigma/2)
one obtains

\frac{\eta}{8C}\sigma\leq F\left(1+\frac{\sigma}{4}\right)^{\eta}-F\left(1+\frac{\sigma}{2}\right)^{\eta}\leq F\left(1+\frac{\sigma}{4}\right)^{\eta}=\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\left(\partial^{*}E\setminus B_{1+\sigma/4}\right)^{\eta},

a contradiction to (
2.3
) when
\delta>0
is sufficiently small.

This proves (
4.10
), which, combined with (
2.4
), implies the bound

\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}(E\setminus B_{r}))\leq C(n)\|R\|_{L^{2}(\partial^{*}E)}^{\frac{2}{1-\eta}}\qquad\text{for almost every }\ r\geq 1+\sigma/2,

concluding the estimate for the part of
\partial^{*}E

outside the unit ball.

The interior estimate is similar, with the signs reversed. Indeed, set

\hat{E}_{r}:=B_{r}\setminus E
and

F_{\rm in}(r):=\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}E\cap B_{r})
. For a.e.
r
,

\frac{d}{dr}F_{\rm in}(r)=\int_{\partial^{*}E\cap\partial B_{r}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\big|\frac{x}{|x|}\cdot\nu_{E}\big|^{2}}}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-2}\geq\mathscr{H}^{n-2}(\partial^{*}E\cap\partial B_{r}),

|\mathbf{H}_{\partial\hat{E}_{r}}|\leq|\mathcal{H}_{\partial^{*}E}|\,\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\lfloor_{\partial^{*}E\cap B_{r}}+\frac{n-1}{r}\,\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\lfloor_{E^{(0)}\cap\partial B_{r}}+C\mathscr{H}^{n-2}\lfloor_{\partial^{*}E\cap\partial B_{r}}.

Thus, Michael–Simon, Hölder, the preceding coarea identity, and
Lemma
2.3
give

F_{\rm in}(r)^{1-\eta}\leq\|R\|_{L^{2}(\partial^{*}E)}^{2}+CF_{\rm in}^{\prime}(r)\qquad\text{for a.e. }r\in(1-\sigma/2,1-\sigma/4).

If the desired bound failed at some
r\leq 1-\sigma/2
, the monotonicity of

F_{\rm in}
would give

F_{\rm in}^{1-\eta}\leq\frac{1}{2}F_{\rm in}^{1-\eta}+CF_{\rm in}^{\prime}

on
(1-\sigma/2,1-\sigma/4)
. Hence

\frac{d}{dr}F_{\rm in}(r)^{\eta}\geq c(n)
there, contradicting the smallness of

F_{\rm in}(1-\sigma/4)
from Lemma
2.3
.
Finally the
same divergence estimate as in Lemma
2.3
, together with
the relative isoperimetric inequality in
B_{r}
, controls

\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(E^{(0)}\cap\partial B_{r})
by
F_{\rm in}(r)
.
This proves

P(B_{r}\setminus E)\leq C(n)\|R\|_{L^{2}(\partial^{*}E)}^{\frac{2}{1-\eta}}\qquad\text{for all }r\in(1/2,1-\sigma/2),

after decreasing
\delta(n,\sigma)
if necessary.
The bounds for all radii follow by approximating an arbitrary radius by regular radii and using lower semicontinuity of perimeter under
L^{1}_{\rm loc}
convergence of the truncations.
∎

4.3.
Approximation results

We collect here the approximation results for the original set. The first
lemma records the local second-order regularity that follows from the weak
mean-curvature equation.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded Lipschitz open set, and assume that its scalar distributional mean curvature satisfies

\mathcal{H}_{\partial E}\in L^{2}(\partial E).

Then
\partial E
is of class
W^{2,2}
and there exists a sequence
\{E_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of bounded open sets with smooth boundary
such that

E_{j}\supset E,\qquad|E_{j}\Delta E|\to 0,\qquad P(E_{j})\to P(E),

and

\int_{\partial E_{j}}|\mathcal{H}_{\partial E_{j}}|^{2}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\to\int_{\partial E}|\mathcal{H}_{\partial E}|^{2}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}.

Proof.

Since
\partial E
is compact and Lipschitz, we may cover it by finitely many coordinate cylinders in which
\partial E
is represented as the graph of a function

u\colon B^{\prime}_{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R}

with
u\in W^{1,\infty}(B^{\prime}_{R})
. In such a chart, writing the mean curvature with respect to the graph orientation, we have

(4.11)

-{\rm div}\bigg(\frac{Du}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}}\bigg)=f\qquad\text{in }B^{\prime}_{R}

in the weak sense, where
f
is the mean curvature read in the chart. Since the surface measure on the graph is comparable to Lebesgue measure on
B^{\prime}_{R}
, our assumption
\mathcal{H}_{\partial E}\in L^{2}(\partial E)
yields
f\in L^{2}(B^{\prime}_{R})
.

Set

A(p):=\frac{p}{\sqrt{1+|p|^{2}}},\qquad p\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1},

so that (
4.11
) is of the form
-{\rm div}(A(Du))=f
. Since
u
is Lipschitz,
DA
is uniformly elliptic on the range of
Du
. Thus, by
[
11
, Theorem 2.1]
we obtain

A(Du)\in W^{1,2}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(B^{\prime}_{R};\mathbb{R}^{n-1}).

Since
A^{-1}:B_{1}\to\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
is a diffeomorphism and
A(Du)
is contained in a compact subset of
B_{1}
, it follows that

Du=A^{-1}(A(Du))\in W^{1,2}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(B^{\prime}_{R}).

Thus
\partial E
is a
W^{2,2}
-boundary in the sense of
[
2
, Theorem 2(3)]
. Applying that result, we obtain smooth inner and outer approximating domains
E^{-}_{j}\subset\subset E\subset\subset E^{+}_{j}
such that, in each fixed chart,

u^{\pm}_{j}\to u\qquad\text{in }W^{2,2}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(B^{\prime}_{R}).

Moreover, the approximating charts remain uniformly Lipschitz, and therefore, up to a subsequence,

Du^{\pm}_{j}\to Du\qquad\text{a.e. on }B^{\prime}_{R}.

Setting
E_{j}:=E_{j}^{+}
, we obtain

|E_{j}\Delta E|\to 0,\qquad P(E_{j})\to P(E).

Let
a_{kl}(p):=\partial_{p_{l}}A_{k}(p)
. In each chart, with the convention of
(
4.11
), the mean curvature of the graph of
u
is given by

\mathcal{H}(u)=-{\rm div}(A(Du))=-a_{kl}(Du)\,\partial_{kl}u,

and similarly for
u^{\pm}_{j}
. Since
u^{\pm}_{j}\to u
in
W^{2,2}
on every smaller ball, we have

\|\mathcal{H}(u^{\pm}_{j})-\mathcal{H}(u)\|_{L^{2}(B^{\prime}_{R-\epsilon})}\leq\ \|a_{kl}(Du^{\pm}_{j})(\partial_{kl}u^{\pm}_{j}-\partial_{kl}u)\|_{L^{2}(B^{\prime}_{R-\epsilon})}\\
\ +\|(a_{kl}(Du^{\pm}_{j})-a_{kl}(Du))\partial_{kl}u\|_{L^{2}(B^{\prime}_{R-\epsilon})}\to\ 0,

because the coefficients
a_{kl}
are bounded on bounded sets,
D^{2}u^{\pm}_{j}\to D^{2}u
in
L^{2}
, and
Du^{\pm}_{j}\to Du
a.e. with a uniform
L^{\infty}
-bound. Since also

\sqrt{1+|Du^{\pm}_{j}|^{2}}\to\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}

pointwise a.e. and with a uniform
L^{\infty}
-bound, the area formula yields the convergence of the
L^{2}
-norms on each chart. Summing over the finite atlas gives the
L^{2}
-convergence.
∎

We shall use the following polyhedral approximation result.
We use the notation introduced in (
2.5
); in
particular, for polyhedral boundaries the measure
\mathbf{H}_{\partial F}

contains the codimension-two contributions.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set of
C^{1}
-class and assume that

\mathcal{H}_{\partial E}\in L^{2}(\partial E)
. Then there exists a sequence of
bounded open polyhedral sets
\{E_{h}\}_{h\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

E_{h}\to E\quad\text{in measure},\qquad P(E_{h})\to P(E),

and

|\mathbf{H}_{\partial E_{h}}|(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\int_{\partial E}|\mathcal{H}_{\partial E}|\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1},

where
|\mathbf{H}_{\partial E_{h}}|
denotes the total variation of the vector-valued measure
\mathbf{H}_{\partial E_{h}}
.
Moreover,

\mathbf{H}_{\partial E_{h}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\mathcal{H}_{\partial E}\nu_{E}\,\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\llcorner\partial E

as vector-valued Radon measures. In addition, away from the

(n-2)
-skeletons of the polyhedral boundaries, the corresponding graphs and
normals converge uniformly to those of
\partial E
.

Proof.

By the local regularity proved in Lemma
4.2
, the
C^{1}
graph functions representing
\partial E
belong to
W^{2,2}_{{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}}
. We fix a finite atlas of coordinate cylinders and then shrink the cylinders so that the smaller cylinders still cover
\partial E
and the
W^{2,2}
regularity holds up to the boundary of each cylinder used below. Approximating the graph functions on the larger cylinders by smooth functions converging both in
W^{2,2}
on the smaller cylinders and in
C^{1}
, and gluing the local normal displacements by a partition of unity, gives smooth domains
\widetilde{E}_{j}
with

|\widetilde{E}_{j}\Delta E|\to 0,\qquad P(\widetilde{E}_{j})\to P(E),

and

\mathcal{H}_{\partial\widetilde{E}_{j}}\nu_{\widetilde{E}_{j}}\,\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\llcorner\partial\widetilde{E}_{j}\to\mathcal{H}_{\partial E}\nu_{E}\,\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\llcorner\partial E

as vector-valued Radon measures, with convergence of the total variations.
Then, for each fixed smooth
\widetilde{E}_{j}
, using
[
4
]
(see also
[
18
,
14
]
) we can find polyhedral domains
P_{j,h}
such that

|P_{j,h}\Delta\widetilde{E}_{j}|\to 0,\qquad P(P_{j,h})\to P(\widetilde{E}_{j}),

\mathbf{H}_{\partial P_{j,h}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\mathcal{H}_{\partial\widetilde{E}_{j}}\nu_{\widetilde{E}_{j}}\,\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\llcorner\partial\widetilde{E}_{j},\qquad|\mathbf{H}_{\partial P_{j,h}}|(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\int_{\partial\widetilde{E}_{j}}|\mathcal{H}_{\partial\widetilde{E}_{j}}|\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1},

and the normals are close to those of
\partial\widetilde{E}_{j}.

Choosing
h=h(j)
sufficiently large gives a diagonal sequence; after
relabeling it as
E_{h}
, the corollary follows.
∎

4.4.
The fixed truncation and leveling

Throughout this subsection, we say that
F
is a
truncation
if there
exist a bounded
C^{1}
open set
E
with

\mathcal{H}_{\partial E}\in L^{2}(\partial E)
, a point
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and radii

0<\rho_{0}<\rho_{1}
such that

F=(E\cap B_{\rho_{1}}(x_{0}))\cup B_{\rho_{0}}(x_{0}),

and

\mathscr{H}^{n-2}\bigl(\partial E\cap\partial B_{\rho_{0}}(x_{0})\bigr)+\mathscr{H}^{n-2}\bigl(\partial E\cap\partial B_{\rho_{1}}(x_{0})\bigr)<\infty.

For such a set we denote by
\mathcal{S}_{F}
the union of the two interfaces
above. In this case, Lemma
2.5
implies that the measure
\mathbf{H}_{\partial F}
defined by
(
2.5
) satisfies

\mathbf{H}_{\partial F}=\mathcal{H}_{F}\nu_{F}\,\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\llcorner\partial^{*}F+\mathbf{H}^{s}_{\partial F},

where the singular part
\mathbf{H}^{s}_{\partial F}
of
\mathbf{H}_{\partial F}
is concentrated on

\mathcal{S}_{F}
.

We now isolate the approximation property required of the two cutting radii.
Write
d_{x_{0}}(x):=|x-x_{0}|
. A truncation

F=(E\cap B_{\rho_{1}}(x_{0}))\cup B_{\rho_{0}}(x_{0})

is called
admissible
if there are smooth domains
\widetilde{E}_{j}

approximating
E
as in the proof of
Corollary
4.3
, and regular values

\rho_{0,j}\to\rho_{0}
,
\rho_{1,j}\to\rho_{1}
of
d_{x_{0}}
on

\partial\widetilde{E}_{j}
, such that
\rho_{0,j}<\rho_{1,j}
and, setting

F_{j}=(\widetilde{E}_{j}\cap B_{\rho_{1,j}}(x_{0}))\cup B_{\rho_{0,j}}(x_{0}),

one has
|F_{j}\Delta F|\to 0
,
P(F_{j})\to P(F)
, and
the vector-valued curvature measures and their total variations converge
weakly:

\mathbf{H}_{\partial F_{j}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\mathbf{H}_{\partial F},\qquad|\mathbf{H}_{\partial F_{j}}|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}|\mathbf{H}_{\partial F}|

as Radon measures on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
We call such a sequence
\{F_{j}\}
a good smooth cutting approximation of
F
.

The next lemma shows that almost every pair of radii gives an admissible
truncation.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded
C^{1}
open set with

\mathcal{H}_{\partial E}\in L^{2}(\partial E)
, let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and let

\widetilde{E}_{j}
be a smooth approximation of
E
as in the proof of
Corollary
4.3
. Then, for a.e. pair

0<\rho_{0}<\rho_{1}
, the truncation

F=(E\cap B_{\rho_{1}}(x_{0}))\cup B_{\rho_{0}}(x_{0})

is admissible.

Proof.

We first compute the density of the curvature measure carried by a single
spherical interface. Let

M
be a smooth hypersurface with unit normal
\nu
, and let
t
be a regular
value of
d_{x_{0}}\llcorner M
. On
M\cap\partial B_{t}(x_{0})
set

\omega:=\frac{x-x_{0}}{|x-x_{0}|},\qquad a:=\nu\cdot\omega,\qquad s:=\sqrt{1-a^{2}}.

For the cut
E\cap B_{t}(x_{0})
, the exterior conormal of the
M
-piece is

\eta_{M}=\frac{\omega-a\nu}{s},

while the exterior conormal of the spherical piece is

\eta_{B}=\frac{\nu-a\omega}{s}.

Thus the total variation density of this interface contribution is

(4.12)

q(\nu,\omega):=|\eta_{M}+\eta_{B}|=\left|\frac{\omega-a\nu}{s}+\frac{\nu-a\omega}{s}\right|=\sqrt{2(1-\nu\cdot\omega)}.

For the cut
E\cup B_{t}(x_{0})
both conormals are reversed, so the interface
measure changes sign but its total variation density is again
(
4.12
). We therefore use the continuous extension

q(\nu,\omega)=\sqrt{2(1-\nu\cdot\omega)}
for all pairs of unit vectors. Thus,
if

m_{j}(t):=\int_{\partial\widetilde{E}_{j}\cap\partial B_{t}(x_{0})}q(\nu_{\widetilde{E}_{j}},\omega)\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-2},

then
m_{j}(t)
is the total variation of the curvature measure carried by the
interface of the approximating cut at radius
t
. The analogous function
m(t)

for
\partial E
gives the total variation of the corresponding interface
contribution in the limiting cut for a.e.
t
.

We claim that the measures
m_{j}(t)\,dt
converge weakly to
m(t)\,dt
on
compact intervals of radii. Indeed, by the coarea formula on

\partial\widetilde{E}_{j}
, for every
\varphi\in C_{c}((0,\infty))
,

\int\varphi(t)m_{j}(t)\,dt=\int_{\partial\widetilde{E}_{j}}\varphi(d_{x_{0}}(x))q(\nu_{\widetilde{E}_{j}},\omega)|\nabla_{\tau_{j}}d_{x_{0}}|\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}.

The right-hand side converges to the corresponding integral on
\partial E
,
because the approximating hypersurfaces converge in
C^{1}
, with uniformly
converging normals, on the finite atlas used in
Corollary
4.3
.
Applying the coarea formula on
\partial E
gives the claimed weak convergence
of the slice measures.

Analogously, the mean curvature measures of the spherical pieces are
controlled by the same slicing argument. On compact intervals of radii contained
in
(0,\infty)
set

a_{1,j}(t):=\frac{n-1}{t}\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\bigl(\partial B_{t}(x_{0})\cap\widetilde{E}_{j}\bigr),\qquad a_{0,j}(t):=\frac{n-1}{t}\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\bigl(\partial B_{t}(x_{0})\setminus\widetilde{E}_{j}\bigr),

and define
a_{1},a_{0}
analogously with
E
in place of

\widetilde{E}_{j}
. These are precisely the total variations of the spherical
curvature contributions for the outer and inner cuts. Since

\chi_{\widetilde{E}_{j}}\to\chi_{E}
in
L^{1}_{\rm loc}
, the coarea formula in

\mathbb{R}^{n}
gives

a_{i,j}(t)\,dt\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}a_{i}(t)\,dt,\qquad i=0,1,

on every such compact interval.

Set

\lambda_{j}:=|\mathcal{H}_{\partial\widetilde{E}_{j}}|\,\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\llcorner\partial\widetilde{E}_{j},\qquad\lambda:=|\mathcal{H}_{\partial E}|\,\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\llcorner\partial E.

By Corollary
4.3
,

\lambda_{j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\lambda
. Now, let

0<\rho_{0}<\rho_{1}
be such that
\rho_{i}
is a Lebesgue point of both
m
and
a_{i}
,
i=0,1
,
and such that

\lambda(\partial B_{\rho_{0}}(x_{0}))=\lambda(\partial B_{\rho_{1}}(x_{0}))=0.

This holds for a.e. pair
0<\rho_{0}<\rho_{1}
. Choose shrinking intervals

I_{0,h}\downarrow\{\rho_{0}\}
and
I_{1,h}\downarrow\{\rho_{1}\}
with

\sup I_{0,h}<\inf I_{1,h}
. Since

(m_{j}+a_{i,j})(t)\,dt
converges weakly to
(m+a_{i})(t)\,dt
, and since

\rho_{i}
is a Lebesgue point of
m+a_{i}
, a diagonal argument gives indices

h(j)\to\infty
such that, setting
I_{i,j}=I_{i,h(j)}
,

\frac{1}{|I_{i,j}|}\int_{I_{i,j}}(m_{j}(t)+a_{i,j}(t))\,dt\to m(\rho_{i})+a_{i}(\rho_{i}).

Since the regular values of
d_{x_{0}}\llcorner\partial\widetilde{E}_{j}
have full
measure, we may then choose regular values
\rho_{i,j}\in I_{i,j}
with

m_{j}(\rho_{i,j})+a_{i,j}(\rho_{i,j})\leq\frac{1}{|I_{i,j}|}\int_{I_{i,j}}(m_{j}(t)+a_{i,j}(t))\,dt+\frac{1}{j},\qquad i=0,1.

In this way
\rho_{i,j}\to\rho_{i}
,
\rho_{0,j}<\rho_{1,j}
for all large
j
,
and

(4.13)

\limsup_{j\to\infty}\sum_{i=0}^{1}\bigl(m_{j}(\rho_{i,j})+a_{i,j}(\rho_{i,j})\bigr)\leq\sum_{i=0}^{1}\bigl(m(\rho_{i})+a_{i}(\rho_{i})\bigr).

Now set

F_{j}=(\widetilde{E}_{j}\cap B_{\rho_{1,j}}(x_{0}))\cup B_{\rho_{0,j}}(x_{0}),\qquad F=(E\cap B_{\rho_{1}}(x_{0}))\cup B_{\rho_{0}}(x_{0}),

K_{j}:=\overline{B}_{\rho_{1,j}}(x_{0})\setminus B_{\rho_{0,j}}(x_{0}),\qquad K:=\overline{B}_{\rho_{1}}(x_{0})\setminus B_{\rho_{0}}(x_{0}),

and, for
\varepsilon>0
small enough,

K_{\varepsilon}:=\overline{B}_{\rho_{1}+\varepsilon}(x_{0})\setminus B_{\rho_{0}-\varepsilon}(x_{0}).

For every such
\varepsilon
and all
j
large enough,

K_{j}\subset K_{\varepsilon}
. Hence, by the weak
convergence
\lambda_{j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\lambda
,

\limsup_{j\to\infty}\lambda_{j}(K_{j})\leq\limsup_{j\to\infty}\lambda_{j}(K_{\varepsilon})\leq\lambda(K_{\varepsilon}).

Letting
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
and using

\lambda(\partial B_{\rho_{i}}(x_{0}))=0
,
i=0,1
, gives

\limsup_{j\to\infty}\lambda_{j}(K_{j})\leq\lambda(K).

Combining this with (
4.13
), we proved

(4.14)

\limsup_{j\to\infty}|\mathbf{H}_{\partial F_{j}}|(\mathbb{R}^{n})\leq|\mathbf{H}_{\partial F}|(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

On the other hand, since

\partial F_{j}\to\partial F
as varifolds,
(
2.5
) gives

\mathbf{H}_{\partial F_{j}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\mathbf{H}_{\partial F}
.
The opposite inequality follows from lower semicontinuity of total variation
under weak-
*
convergence, combined with (
4.14
). Hence the total
masses converge, which implies that

|\mathbf{H}_{\partial F_{j}}|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}|\mathbf{H}_{\partial F}|,

as desired.
∎

With the good pairs of cutting levels available, we now choose the two cuts
used in the main proof. The small-tentacle estimate supplies annuli where the
interfaces have small
(n-2)
-measure, and Lemma
4.4

lets us choose the corresponding radii as a good pair.

Lemma 4.5
.

There exists
\sigma_{2}=\sigma_{2}(n)>0
such that the following holds. Let

0<\sigma<\sigma_{2}
,
|x_{0}|\leq\sigma/8
, and let
E
be a
C^{1}
-domain satisfying the
hypotheses of Lemma
4.1
. Then one can find

s^{\pm}\in[0,\sigma/8]
so that, setting

G:=(E\cap B_{1+\sigma/2+s^{+}}(x_{0}))\cup B_{1-\sigma/2-s^{-}}(x_{0}),

one has
\partial G\subset A_{1-2\sigma,1+2\sigma}
and

(4.15)

|\mathbf{H}^{s}_{\partial G}|(\partial G)\leq C(n,\sigma)\|R\|_{L^{2}(\partial^{*}E)}^{\frac{2}{1-\eta}}.

In addition,
G
is an admissible truncation.

Proof.

The inclusion
\partial G\subset A_{1-2\sigma,1+2\sigma}
follows from

|x_{0}|\leq\sigma/8
and the definitions of the two cutting radii. We choose the
radii by coarea. Since
|\nabla_{\tau}|x-x_{0}||\leq 1
on
\partial E
and the
translation by
x_{0}
is absorbed by the margins in the annuli, Lemma
4.1

applied with parameter
3\sigma/4
gives

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\sigma/8}\mathscr{H}^{n-2}\bigl(\partial E\cap\partial B_{1+\sigma/2+s}(x_{0})\bigr)\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C(n)\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\bigl(\partial E\setminus B_{1+3\sigma/8}\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq C(n,\sigma)\|R\|_{L^{2}(\partial^{*}E)}^{\frac{2}{1-\eta}},

and, analogously,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\sigma/8}\mathscr{H}^{n-2}\bigl(\partial E\cap\partial B_{1-\sigma/2-s}(x_{0})\bigr)\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C(n)\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\bigl(\partial E\cap B_{1-3\sigma/8}\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq C(n,\sigma)\|R\|_{L^{2}(\partial^{*}E)}^{\frac{2}{1-\eta}}.

Since the good pairs of Lemma
4.4
have full measure
in the square
[0,\sigma/8]^{2}
, removing the bad pairs does not affect the
average of the sum of the two interface terms over that square. Thus we may
choose
s^{\pm}\in[0,\sigma/8]
so that the two radii below form a good pair and

(4.16)

\mathscr{H}^{n-2}\bigl(\partial E\cap\partial B_{1+\sigma/2+s^{+}}(x_{0})\bigr)+\mathscr{H}^{n-2}\bigl(\partial E\cap\partial B_{1-\sigma/2-s^{-}}(x_{0})\bigr)\leq C(n,\sigma)\|R\|_{L^{2}(\partial^{*}E)}^{\frac{2}{1-\eta}}.

Let

\rho_{0}=1-\sigma/2-s^{-},\qquad\rho_{1}=1+\sigma/2+s^{+}.

By Lemma
4.4
, the corresponding truncation
G
