Split intron-exon system for circular RNA synthesis
Authors: Lei Wang, Qiaoli Zhai, Chunbo Dong, Weibing Zhang, Xu Ma, Kai Yang, Shaolong Qi, Yarong Wu, Guocan Yu, Lian-Qing Wang, George Fu Gao, Zhida Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractRibozyme-based approaches have been extensively employed for in vitro circular RNA (circRNA) synthesis. However, several critical challenges are impeding the progress in this field, including the relatively low ribozyme utilization efficiency, limited synthetic flexibility, and complicated downstream purification procedures. Here, we introduce a split intron-exon (SIE) system for circRNA synthesis by physically separating the ribozyme from its substrates. This design ensures efficient circRNA synthesis by overcoming the inherent limitations of current ribozyme-based techniques, in which each ribozyme can only catalyse the synthesis of at most one circRNA molecule. Moreover, the SIE system enables the efficient synthesis of both unmodified and chemically modified circRNAs. Furthermore, the recyclability and immobilization of the ribozyme within the SIE system reduce reaction impurities and improves circRNA enrichment and yield. Overall, the SIE system holds great potential to advance the diversification and large-scale development of circRNA therapeutics.Subject terms: Nucleic-acid therapeutics, Ribozymes

Introduction

CircRNAs are biologically active single-stranded RNA molecules characterised by closed-loop structures. Unlike linear mRNAs, they do not have 5’ caps or 3’ polyadenylated tails. Moreover, the closed-loop structures of circRNAs endow them with high stability as they are resistant to degradation mediated by exonucleases1,2. In eukaryotic cells, although circRNAs were once considered byproducts of aberrant RNA splicing, ongoing research has shown that back-splicing is the main mechanism for the formation of circRNAs3. These molecules play crucial roles in various biological processes, including acting as miRNA or protein sponges, modulating transcriptional processes, and influencing mRNA splicing, among others4–7. In particular, the discovery of their translational capability has significantly broadened our understanding of circRNA functions8–13. These features make circRNAs attractive candidates for therapeutic and diagnostic applications14,15. For instance, circRNAs have been explored as potential biomarkers for cancer and other diseases due to their abundance and stability in body fluids16,17. Moreover, their protein-encoding properties rendered circRNAs increasingly appealing candidates for substituting mRNA in the development of protein-based therapeutics18–22.

Recent advances in RNA engineering have enabled the synthesis of circRNAs through both in vivo and in vitro methods23. While in vivo synthesis within cells can yield potent therapeutic effects, it often requires the exogenous introduction of DNA templates, raising concerns about genomic integration and variability in synthesis efficiency across different cell types24–26. In contrast, in vitro synthesis followed by targeted delivery circumvents these issues, making it the preferred approach for circRNA-based drug development27,28. Current in vitro synthesis methods include chemical ligation, enzyme-mediated ligation, and ribozyme-based approaches. Chemical ligation is restricted to short RNA sequences29, whereas enzyme-mediated ligation using T4 RNA ligase often faces challenges such as low ligation efficiency and prominent intermolecular side reactions30. In contrast, ribozyme-based approaches can efficiently achieve the circularisation of longer RNA fragments. However, conventional ribozyme-based methods typically introduce scar sequences into circRNAs, which may raise concerns regarding their potential immunogenicity31,32. This has motivated the development of optimised systems to produce scarless circRNAs, with the goal of minimising unnecessary immune responses33. Nevertheless, accumulating evidence suggests that residual linear RNA and 5’-triphosphate groups are key factors contributing to the immunogenicity of circRNAs34,35. A stringent purification process can significantly reduce impurities in circRNA preparations, thereby enabling circRNAs to exhibit lower immunogenicity34,35.

Group I and group II intron-based PIE (Permuted intron–exon) systems have been extensively studied, they exhibit distinct mechanisms during the self-catalytic splicing process, resulting in variations in the required reaction conditions, splicing products, and efficiencies36,37. Currently, the group I intron-based PIE system is the most widely adopted technology for in vitro circRNA synthesis. Researchers have developed various modification strategies to enhance circularisation efficiency, such as designing auxiliary sequences, substituting new introns, screening optimal circularisation sites, and adding spacer sequences38. However, the final products often contain the concatenated RNA with high molecular weight (HMW), linear precursor RNA and excised introns, which require subsequent complex purification processes to remove these byproducts. Although several circRNA purification techniques, such as RNase R or phosphatase treatment, size-exclusion chromatography, reversed-phase HPLC, and cellulose-based filtration, either individually or in combination, have been established, they remain insufficient to fully meet the current demands31,33,35,39. Additionally, the existing PIE methods generally require the ribozyme and the corresponding substrate fragments to be on the same precursor RNA molecule. Once the catalytic reaction is completed, the ribozyme is inactivated and cannot initiate new catalytic reactions like proteinaceous enzymes31,33,40. Moreover, the presence of chemically modified ribonucleotides in the precursor RNA molecule will interfere with ribozyme activity in the PIE system, thereby affecting circularisation efficiency, which greatly restricts the flexibility and diversity of the RNA molecules to be circularized34,35.

In the present study, we developed an innovative engineered SIE system for the synthesis of circRNAs. This system comprises two distinct RNA components: a universal ribozyme and a substrate that harbours the target sequence. The ribozyme specifically recognises the substrate via a defined recognition sequence (RS) and catalyses the splicing reaction. A key feature of this system is the ribozyme’s ability to undergo multiple catalytic cycles, akin to proteinaceous enzymes. This significantly boosts its utilisation rate, leading to efficient and cost-effective circRNA synthesis. Additionally, the split ribozyme from substrates facilitates the synthesis of circRNAs with the chemically modified ribonucleotides, which expands the flexibility and diversity of circularised RNAs. Our system also enables the recycling of the ribozymes via immobilisation, which not only reduces impurities in the reaction products and simplifies the subsequent separation and purification of circRNAs but also offers substantial advantages in large-scale production of circRNAs. Collectively, these findings represent a significant advancement in the in vitro synthesis of circRNAs, paving the way for the development of circRNA-based therapeutics and expanding their applications in biotechnology and medicine.

Results

Design of SIE for circRNA synthesis

The PIE system represents a conventional approach for the synthesis of circRNAs41. Typically, this method involves incorporating ribozyme and gene of interest (GOI) sequences within an RNA precursor42. Once the catalytic reaction is completed, the ribozyme becomes inactive and is rendered non-reusable. Although these RNA molecules are designated as ribozymes, they still exhibit significant differences in their catalytic behaviour compared to proteinaceous enzymes. Furthermore, during the preparation of circRNAs using PIE technology, the intact ribozyme is typically divided into two halves and positioned on either side of the GOI38. For circRNA formation to proceed, these two halves of the ribozyme must interact with each other to assemble a complete and functionally effective structure31,38. Thus, the fully assembled ribozyme structure is crucial for circRNA formation and represents a key rate-limiting step in the PIE-based circRNA synthesis process (Supplementary Fig. 1).

To mimic the substrate catalytic behaviour similar to that of a proteinaceous enzyme, we have developed an innovative SIE system for circRNA synthesis (Fig. 1). This SIE system consists of two RNA molecules generated through in vitro transcription (IVT): the ribozyme and the substrate. Within this system, the core sequence of the ribozyme, with minor deletions at the 5’ and 3’ ends, is located on one RNA molecule. Unlike the PIE system, the ribozyme sequences are no longer interrupted by GOI sequences and are spatially closer, which facilitates the formation of higher-order structures essential for ribozyme catalysis. Regarding the substrate, the GOI sequences along with a small amount of 5’ and 3’ end ribozyme sequences are placed on another RNA molecule. The specificity of the ribozyme relies on RS, which can be designed to enable complementary pairing with the substrate, forming ribozyme-substrate complexes that undergo two transesterification reactions to complete the catalytic process. Importantly, the ribozyme remains intact and active after each reaction cycle, enabling it to participate in multiple rounds of catalysis, akin to the behaviour of proteinaceous enzymes (Fig. 1). This SIE design will significantly enhance the utilisation efficiency of the ribozyme and benefit the overall efficacy of circRNA synthesis.

Ribozyme cleavage at a distinct loop alters circRNA synthesis

To ensure the optimal preservation of the intact ribozyme structure within the SIE system, we selected a sequence interval spanning from the L1/L2 loop at the 5’ end to the L9.1/L9.2 loop at the 3’ end as our ribozyme core43–46, based on the structural characteristics observed in group I self-splicing introns found in Anabaena (Ana) pre-tRNALeu or T4 phage thymidylate synthase gene (Fig. 2a, b). To identify which loop regions are appropriate for ribozyme reconstitution in the context of circRNA synthesis within the SIE system, we employed the previously established PIE system for preliminary screening. Initially, the T4 phage and Anabaena-derived ribozymes were cleaved at the L1, L2, L9.1, and L9.2 loops, respectively, to construct distinct PIE systems. Given that cleaving the ribozyme at the L6 loop is a conventional strategy in most PIE systems, we designated this as the control group (Fig. 2a, b). The 5’ and 3’ ends of these PIE systems were equipped with homology arms (HA) to facilitate the reconstruction of the disrupted loop resulting from the ribozyme cleavage (Supplementary Fig. 2a, b).

Subsequently, we evaluated the circRNA synthesis capability of these PIE systems using gel electrophoresis and RT-qPCR analyses. Our findings demonstrate that, in PIE systems derived from T4 phage ribozyme, cleavage at any position within the L1, L2, L9.1, or L9.2 loop can produce circRNAs (Fig. 2c–e). However, the synthesis efficiency varies depending on the cleavage positions and is significantly lower than that observed when cleavage occurs at the L6 loop (Fig. 2d, e). In contrast, in PIE systems derived from the Ana ribozyme, only those configured for cleavage at the L1 or L9.1 loop demonstrated significant circRNA synthesis capability (Fig. 2f–h). Notably, the system designed for cleavage at the L9.1 loop exhibited circRNA synthesis efficiency comparable to that observed with cleavage at the L6 loop. Conversely, cleavage at the L2 or L9.2 loop resulted in negligible circRNA production (Fig. 2g, h). These observations indicate that the L1 and L9.1 loop regions of the Ana ribozyme were potential cleavage sites for ribozyme reconstitution in the SIE system.

CircRNA synthesis via the SIE system

Based on the efficiency of circRNA synthesis observed in the engineered PIE systems, the Ana-derived ribozyme was selected to develop the SIE system, with the L1 and L9.1 loop regions chosen as the cleavage and reconstitution sites (Fig. 2g, h). Given that the cleavage site at the L1 loop region in the Ana ribozyme-derived PIE system showed relatively low circRNA synthesis efficiency (Fig. 2g, h), it is crucial to enhance the functionality of the L1 loop reconstituted via RS interactions within the SIE system to improve the catalytic efficiency of the split ribozyme. Previous studies have demonstrated that RS, which is alternatively referred to as HA in other studies, plays a critical role in enhancing circRNA synthesis within PIE systems31. Therefore, we utilized the PIE system to investigate whether increasing the RS length (designated as enhanced L1, eL1) could improve RNA circularization when ribozyme cleavage occurs at the L1 loop (Fig. 3a and Supplementary Fig. 3a). The results revealed a substantial improvement in circRNA synthesis capacity, which was even comparable to that of PIE systems designed with ribozyme cleavages at the L6 or L9.1 loop regions (Fig. 3b, c).

Building on the insights from the aforementioned research findings, we engineered an Ana ribozyme (designated AnaR) for the SIE system, in which the substrate is separated from the catalytic sequence. Specifically, this ribozyme was designed with cleavage sites at the L1 and L9.1 loop regions. An extended RS was incorporated at the terminus of the L1 loop, while a short RS was appended to the end of the L9.1 loop to facilitate substrate recognition. According to the design principles of SIE as described in Fig. 1, the 5’ R and 3’ R segments of the Ana ribozyme were inserted into the substrate. Meanwhile, an extended reverse complementary RS was attached to the 3’ end, and a short reverse complementary RS was appended to the 5’ end of the substrate to enable recognition by AnaR (Fig. 3d and Supplementary Fig. 3b).

Next, we evaluated the ability of AnaR to circularise a linear RNA substrate (named SEGFP) that contained the CVB3 IRES and EGFP coding sequences. Electrophoresis, RT-qPCR, and RNase R digestion experiments demonstrated that AnaR could efficiently catalysed the circularization of linear substrates into their circular forms (Fig. 3e, f and Supplementary Fig. 4a). To further characterize the reaction products, we implemented a two-step approach consisting of high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) combined with RNase R treatment, which enabled the successful isolation of circRNA samples (Supplementary Fig. 4b). The collected circRNA was subsequently subjected to reverse transcription-polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR), following which it was cloned into a vector and underwent sequencing analysis. The sequencing results confirmed that splicing reactions occurred precisely at predetermined positions during circRNA synthesis (Supplementary Fig. 4c). Furthermore, upon transfection of the collected circRNAs into Expi293F cells for 24 h, significant expression of EGFP was observed (Fig. 3g, h). All these findings suggest that our designed SIE system is capable of effectively synthesising functional circRNAs.

Investigation of catalytic parameters for the SIE system

Investigating the potential factors within the context of this innovative SIE system is essential for optimising catalytic reaction conditions and maximising circRNA synthesis efficiency. Such optimisation will further advance the application of this system in the development of circRNA-based therapeutics.

Molar ratio of ribozyme to substrate

Compared to the previous PIE system, the most prominent feature of the SIE system is the decoupling of ribozyme and substrate into two independent components, enabling catalytic reactions with different ribozyme-to-substrate ratios. Ribozyme-to-substrate ratios ranging from 0.5 to 5 were examined; electrophoresis and RT-qPCR analyses revealed that a two-fold excess of ribozyme relative to substrates resulted in the optimal synthesis efficacy of circRNA, further increasing the ribozyme ratio did not improve the circularisation efficiency (Fig. 4a, b).

Reaction time

The PIE system-mediated circRNA synthesis typically involves a brief heat denaturation, followed by a catalytic reaction at 55 °C31,32,34. However, prolonged exposure to high temperatures may result in RNA degradation. Thus, we investigated the effect of varying reaction durations (ranging from 5 to 30 min) on circRNA synthesis in the SIE system. The results revealed that circRNA synthesis reached near-maximal levels within 5 min, with no significant increase observed with longer reaction times (Fig. 4c, d). Further reduction of the reaction time revealed that circRNA synthesis plateaued after 3 min (Supplementary Fig. 5).

Concentration of magnesium ions (Mg2+)

For group I intron-derived ribozymes, the presence of Mg2+ is crucial for completing the transesterification reaction47. However, there is currently no standardised concentration for Mg2+, and its specific influence on ribozyme catalytic efficiency remains poorly characterised. Recent studies suggest that elevated Mg2+ concentrations enable efficient co-circularisation of linear RNA during IVT48. To systematically evaluate the effect of Mg²⁺ concentration on circRNA synthesis in the SIE system, we tested a range of 0–20 mM Mg²⁺. Our results revealed that higher Mg²⁺ concentrations were associated with increased RNA degradation (Fig. 4e), the precise mechanisms underlying this phenomenon remain unclear. Importantly, we identified an optimal Mg²⁺ concentration range of 8–10 mM for efficient SIE-mediated circRNA synthesis (Fig. 4e, f).

Reaction temperature

RNA stability is inversely correlated with temperature, as lower temperatures generally reduce the rate of RNA degradation. While most studies on ribozyme-mediated RNA circularisation conduct catalytic reactions at 55 °C, the specific rationale for this temperature selection remains unclear. To establish an optimal reaction temperature for the SIE system and provide a reference for its application in circRNA-based therapeutics, we investigated the circRNA synthesis efficiency of the SIE system across a temperature range of 30–60 °C. Our findings revealed that the SIE system consistently achieved high circRNA synthesis efficiency within the temperature range of 42–55 °C (Fig. 4g, h).

RS length

Previous studies have demonstrated that intact ribozyme structure is necessary for efficient catalytic reactions within the PIE system21,31. Similarly, the SIE system also requires two sets of RSs in both the L1 loop region and the L9.1 loop region to facilitate substrate recognition and subsequent transesterification reactions. However, it is crucial to emphasise that a greater RS length does not invariably correspond to superior outcomes. Indeed, in specific instances, these auxiliary sequences may exert a detrimental effect on RNA transcription or splicing processes33. Therefore, it is crucial to evaluate the impact of RS length on circRNA synthesis efficiency. We further investigated the circRNA synthesis efficiency of the SIE system under conditions where the RS lengths in both the L1 and L9.1 loop regions were simultaneously extended from 30 to 100 nt (Supplementary Fig. 6). The findings indicated that the synthesis efficiency of circRNA gradually increased as the length of the RS was extended up to 60 nt (Fig. 4i, j). Nevertheless, extending the RS from 60 nt to 100 nt did not lead to further significant improvements in circRNA synthesis (Fig. 4j).

Versatility and advantages of the SIE system in circRNA synthesis

To assess the versatility of the SIE system, we evaluated its ability to circularise RNA substrates of varying lengths using AnaR under the optimal reaction conditions. The results demonstrated that the SIE system efficiently catalysed the formation of circRNAs up to 5.7 kb in length (Supplementary Fig. 7a, b), underscoring its considerable potential for broad applications in circRNA synthesis. Comparative analyses with the PIE system and other recently reported approaches, such as STS and TRIC33,35, indicated that the SIE system achieves superior circRNA production efficiency (Supplementary Fig. 7c, d). Despite the absence of co-transcriptional circularization, which is available in some alternative systems, the performance of the SIE system indicates potentially favourable catalytic kinetics (Supplementary Fig. 7e). Furthermore, the system maintained high circularization activity following storage at –80 °C for 30 days (Supplementary Fig. 8a), and reproducibility was confirmed across multiple independent batches of SIE components (Supplementary Fig. 8b), thereby demonstrating robust stability under the tested conditions.

It is well-established that natural RNA transcripts are extensively modified within cells. This principle underpins RNA therapeutics, wherein synthetic modifications are introduced to confer desirable properties such as enhanced expression and stability, as well as reduced immunogenicity40,49,50. However, existing systems, including PIE and TRIC, encounter limitations in generating modified circRNAs35. In these systems, the ribozyme and substrate are covalently linked within a single RNA molecule, such that modifications introduced into the ribozyme may impair its activity, thereby reducing the efficiency of circRNA synthesis. In contrast, the SIE system physically separates the ribozyme from the substrate, allowing their independent preparation via IVT and enabling the efficient production of modified circRNAs using an unmodified ribozyme. To validate this assumption, different concentrations of N⁶-methyl-ATP (m⁶ATP) or N¹-methyl-pseudo-UTP (m1ψTP) were incorporated into the substrate during IVT. Splicing assays revealed that circRNA synthesis by the PIE and TRIC systems was markedly impaired in a dose-dependent manner as the proportion of m⁶ATP or m1ψTP increased (Fig. 5a–h). When incorporating more than 50% of m⁶ATP or m1ψTP, the PIE system exhibits an almost complete inability to synthesise circRNA (Fig. 5a, b, e, f). Under the same conditions, TRIC retains only a minimal synthesis capability, which is also abolished upon incorporation of 100% m⁶ATP or m1ψTP (Fig. 5c, d, g, h). In contrast, the SIE system maintained robust circRNA synthesis activity, outperforming PIE and TRIC even when m⁶ATP or m¹ψTP fully replaced ATP or UTP (Fig. 5a–h). Collectively, these findings underscore the distinct advantages of the SIE system in enabling the efficient synthesis of chemically modified circRNAs.

Ribozyme immobilisation for circRNA synthesis

In the field of industrial production, immobilised enzyme technology in proteinaceous enzyme-mediated catalytic reactions offers several advantages over free enzymes, such as improved stability, ease of recovery, enhanced controllability, reusability, and cost-effectiveness51,52. However, in RNA circularisation mediated by ribozyme technology, this achievement has not yet been realised. The prior PIE system or other derived approaches led to a loss of ribozyme activity following each catalytic cycle, as the ribozyme and substrate were co-located within the same RNA precursor. Consequently, the recycling and reuse of ribozymes held no practical significance. In contrast, the SIE system facilitates this possibility by effectively segregating ribozymes from substrates.

As a proof of concept, we immobilised 3’-biotinylated ribozymes on streptavidin-coated magnetic beads and performed a ribozyme-catalysed reaction at a ribozyme-to-substrate ratio of 0.25. Following the completion of each reaction cycle, the ribozyme was removed, and an equivalent amount of fresh ribozyme was replenished to sustain the catalytic process (Fig. 6a). This setup effectively mimics large-scale circRNA synthesis using ribozyme immobilisation with ribozyme excess. The results from electrophoresis and RT-qPCR consistently demonstrate that as the number of reaction cycles increases, there is a gradual accumulation of circRNA within the reaction products (Fig. 6b, c). These findings significantly contribute to the advancement of ribozyme immobilisation technology for scale-up production of circRNA synthesis.

To assess ribozyme reusability in the SIE system, we performed 20 consecutive circRNA synthesis cycles using immobilised ribozymes in excess (Fig. 6d). All splicing reactions were conducted at 37 °C to maintain the stability of ribozymes. Following each cycle, ribozymes were regenerated via water elution at room temperature. After 20 cycles, immobilised ribozymes retained robust circRNA synthesis activity, though with a moderate reduction in yield (Fig. 6e, f). Additionally, following the application of immobilised ribozyme technology, the splicing products exhibit negligible ribozyme contamination after magnetic bead separation, thereby facilitating downstream purification procedures for ribozyme removal (Fig. 6b, e). This further reinforces the advantages of the SIE system in circRNA synthesis when compared with PIE, STS, and TRIC.

Translational capacity of SIE-synthesized circRNA

CircRNAs exhibit enhanced stability compared to linear RNAs due to their covalently closed circular structure with the absence of free 5’ and 3’ ends, which confers resistance to nuclease-mediated degradation, and achieves extended half-life and prolonged protein expression relative to linear mRNAs31,35. To assess the expression profile of circRNAs synthesised with the SIE system, two distinct circRNAs were generated: one encoding a recombinant PD-L1 antibody (anti-PD-L1), comprising the anti-PD-L1 single-chain variable fragment (scFv) fused with the human IgG1 Fc domain, and another encoding human erythropoietin (hEpo) (Fig. 7a, c).

Following the transfection of equimolar amounts of mRNA and circRNA into HEK293T cells, their expression levels were monitored over a six-day period. The results revealed that the expression of circRNAanti-PD-L1 was initially lower than that of mRNAanti-PD-L1 during the first two days. However, starting from the third day, circRNAanti-PD-L1 exhibited significantly higher expression levels compared to mRNAanti-PD-L1 (Fig. 7b). While circRNAhEPO demonstrated consistently no lower expression levels than mRNAhEPO from the outset (Fig. 7d). Moreover, we evaluated the half-life of circRNA expression based on expression levels relative to those measured on the first day. Both circRNAanti-PD-L1 and circRNAhEPO expression exhibited substantially longer half-lives compared to their respective mRNAs (Supplementary Fig. 9). These findings suggest that appropriately designed circRNAs can achieve robust and sustained protein expression.

The potential application of circRNA prepared by the SIE system for circRNA therapy in vivo was validated through the encapsulation of circRNAhEPO using lipid nanoparticles (LNP) (Supplementary Fig. 10). The resulting formulation was administered via retro-orbital (r.o.) injection into mice (Fig. 7e). Given that hEPO can stimulate reticulocyte production in vivo53, the proportion of reticulocytes in the blood was analysed six days post-injection. The results demonstrated that mice injected with circRNAhEPO or mRNAhEPO exhibited a significantly higher reticulocyte ratio compared to the control group (Fig. 7f, Supplementary Fig. 11). Notably, the circRNAhEPO group exhibited a significantly higher reticulocyte ratio compared to the mRNAhEPO group (Fig. 7f), consistent with the in vitro expression profile of circRNAhEPO. Additionally, analysis of serum levels of alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), creatinine (CREA) and TNFα showed no significant differences among the circRNAhEPO, mRNAhEPO and control groups, confirming the safety profile of the treatments (Fig. 7g–j). These findings support the potential of SIE-generated circRNA for applications in protein replacement therapy.

Discussion

CircRNA is increasingly recognised as a promising alternative to linear RNA, owing to its superior stability and diminished immunogenicity, as evidenced by extensive studies34,54. For instance, Qu et al. have developed a circRNA-based vaccine for SARS-CoV-2 that elicits more robust B-cell and T-cell responses compared to mRNA vaccines, thereby providing enhanced protection18,55. Some studies employed engineered circRNA as single-guide RNA (sgRNA) within the DNA or RNA editing in vivo, leading to improved efficiency56–61. Moreover, several circRNA drugs have recently been approved for clinical trials. These advancements unequivocally highlight the substantial potential of circRNA in RNA therapy. Among previous circRNA synthesis techniques, the ribozyme method has been widely favoured for its simplicity. However, most existing methods integrate the ribozyme and substrate into a single RNA molecule31,40. Despite continuous updates and improvements in technology that have enhanced circRNA synthesis, purity, and protein expression levels, these methods are fundamentally limited by the principle that one ribozyme molecule can only circularise one substrate molecule21,33,48.

In this study, we have developed a circRNA synthesis system named SIE, which employs ribozymes in a manner distinct from existing technologies. This innovative system facilitates the placement of ribozymes and substrates on two separate RNA molecules. Unlike other cis-acting ribozymes, we separated the ribozyme from domains L1 to L9.1 of the Ana ribozyme, which exhibits high activity, recognises the substrate via the RS, and circularises it through transesterification reactions. We delved deeper into the reaction conditions within the SIE system. Additionally, we adjusted the molar ratio of ribozyme to substrate and the length of RS to fully tap into the catalytic potential of the Ana ribozyme. However, it is inaccurate to assume a longer RS inherently enhances circRNA synthesis. An extended RS may interact with ribozyme or substrate sequences, potentially interfering with the circRNA synthesis process.

Based on our findings, the SIE demonstrates a more remarkable RNA circularisation ability. The main reasons are presumably as follows: Firstly, within the SIE system, the ribozyme active domains remain mostly intact and rely on the RS for substrate recognition. This leads to greater structural stability, which may, in turn, guarantee a high efficiency of circRNA synthesis. In contrast, the PIE system requires the ribozyme to be split into two parts and reassembled via HA interactions, which may serve as the rate-limiting step in catalysis. This observation is further supported by the excellent RNA circularisation efficiency demonstrated by other RNA circularisation systems, in which the ribozyme is almost entirely positioned on one side of the RNA precursor33,35,48,62. Secondly, the separation of ribozyme and substrate in the SIE system increases the system’s flexibility. This enables a single ribozyme to catalyse multiple substrate reactions and achieve catalytic behaviours comparable to those of proteinaceous enzymes. Although the RS sequence of the ribozyme in the SIE system may be bound by the free introns, a plausible explanation is that the substrate can competitively bind to the ribozyme with the free intron. This enables the SIE system to exhibit an RNA circularisation efficiency that surpasses that of the PIE system. Moreover, in the SIE system, the quantity of ribozyme utilised can be adjusted. This adjustment allows for better recognition between the substrate and the ribozyme, thus facilitating RNA circularisation.

Although the SIE system is required to generate two independent RNA molecules, the PIE system only necessitates one. Even when aiming to ensure optimal circRNA synthesis efficiency, an excessive amount of ribozyme has to be employed, which might appear wasteful and high-cost. However, once established, the SIE system offers controllable expenses without substantial increases. This is largely attributable to the generality of ribozymes produced in a single batch, which can be applied to circularise different GOIs. Moreover, ribozyme immobilisation not only facilitates reagent recycling but also enables circRNA enrichment, thereby streamlining downstream purification and potentially reducing both time and cost. It is worth noting that despite the development of various purification technologies specifically for circRNA33,35,39,63,64, challenges remain substantial. These technologies have yet to fully meet the requirements for obtaining high-purity circRNA, leading to inconsistent conclusions regarding its performance, particularly in terms of immunogenicity, across numerous studies32,34. Impurities present in circRNAs, or even the scar sequences that persist on circRNAs during their synthesis, have the potential to induce unnecessary immunogenicity. Consequently, rigorous purification is imperative. In the SIE system, ribozyme recycling undoubtedly will facilitate the execution of the purification process, although the extent of this benefit requires further detailed investigation.

The SIE system exhibits distinct advantages in enabling the incorporation of specific RNA modifications. In circRNA therapy, synthesising circRNA with modifications can enhance circRNA expression, stability, or diminish its immunogenicity40,49,50. However, utilising the PIE and TRIC system to synthesise these modified circRNAs remains challenging because it is not feasible to add modifications exclusively to the circularisation sequence without also modifying the ribozyme. Modifications introduced to the ribozyme may substantially impair its activity, thereby hindering the synthesis of circRNA. Conversely, the SIE system enables RNA modifications solely on the substrate, thus preserving the catalytic activity of the ribozyme and promoting the synthesis of circRNA with modifications. This further highlights the versatility and adaptability of the SIE system in circRNA synthesis. Furthermore, the modular design of SIE-decoupling catalytic ribozymes from substrates suggests a broad applicability that extends beyond the Ana ribozyme. This principle could extend to other ribozymes, such as hairpin ribozymes and group II introns65,66, by reengineering their RS to operate in a trans-acting manner. Even these engineered ribozymes can achieve specific in vivo RNA editing through the proper design of RS sequences67. Such adaptability positions SIE as a versatile platform for RNA engineering, potentially enabling scalable, cost-effective synthesis of therapeutic circRNA across diverse ribozyme systems.

The separation of the Ana ribozyme from the system creates favourable conditions for its immobilisation on a solid matrix. The application of immobilised enzyme technology has substantially reduced production costs in enzyme-catalysed industries51,68,69. Similarly, the reusable nature of ribozymes in the SIE system enhances the feasibility of scale-up circRNA production through immobilisation. However, it is important to acknowledge that a gap still exists between the catalytic behaviour of ribozymes in SIE systems and that of proteinaceous enzymes. According to the ‘bind-catalyse-release’ model observed in proteinaceous enzymes, the enzyme returns to its original state after each reaction, allowing it to continuously catalyse the conversion of new substrate molecules into products. While the catalytic behaviour of ribozymes in SIE systems can be broadly described using this model, free introns from substrate molecules may remain bound to the ribozyme via RS interactions after catalysis. This necessitates ribozyme regeneration through transient heat treatment or other strategies to restore its catalytic activity for subsequent reactions. Another critical consideration is that the thermal denaturation in the SIE system may disrupt the coupling between ribozymes and magnetic beads. In this study, to strike a balance between ribozyme immobilisation and circRNA synthesis, we utilised a relatively low temperature (37 °C) to carry out the splicing reaction without heat denaturation. Although this approach entails a certain loss in circRNA synthesis efficiency, such reduction is compensated by extending the splicing reaction duration. Furthermore, as the number of reaction cycles increases, the circRNA synthesis efficiency progressively declines with the use of immobilised ribozymes. This phenomenon may indicate a gradual deterioration of ribozyme stability over successive reaction iterations. Moreover, it could even give rise to off-target effects and an increase in by-products. To overcome this limitation, achieving covalent coupling of ribozymes to carrier beads would withstand harsher denaturation or washing conditions during ribozyme regeneration, thereby likely improving its stability and reusability for circRNA synthesis. More significantly, we also examined the stability of ribozymes following 20 thermal denaturation (70 °C) cycles. The findings indicated that the ribozymes still retained a relatively high level of activity (Supplementary Fig. 8c). This implies that once the ribozymes in the SIE system are covalently linked, there is a good prospect of achieving the desired industrial-scale circRNA synthesis.

In summary, a synthetic route of circRNA was proposed using the SIE system. This system can not only effectively synthesise modified or unmodified circRNAs, but also reduce the consumption of synthetic raw materials through the recycling of ribozymes. Meanwhile, the splicing products after ribozyme recycling contain fewer impurities, which will facilitate the separation and purification of circRNA. In addition, the SIE system offers significant advantages in terms of scale-up circRNA synthesis via immobilisation, making it highly promising for applications in circRNA therapy.

Methods

Mice and cell lines

All mice were housed under specific pathogen-free conditions in the animal care facilities at Tsinghua University. All animal experiments strictly complied with the standards (21-YGC1.G24-1) of the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Tsinghua University. The four BALB/c female mice, aged 6–8 weeks, utilised for the assessment of hEPO expression in vivo were procured from Beijing Vital River Laboratory Animal Technology Co., Ltd. All mice were housed in groups of four per cage under a 12-h light–dark cycle, with unrestricted access to food and water. The temperature was maintained at approximately 20 °C, and the humidity levels were kept around 50%.

Expi293F cells (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) were cultured in SMM 293-TII medium (Sino Biological, China) and maintained in an incubator shaker (135 rpm) at 37 °C with 5% CO2. HEK293T (Cell Bank of Chinese Academy of Sciences) cells were cultured statically in DMEM medium (Gibco, USA) with 10% foetal bovine serum and maintained in an incubator shaker at 37 °C with 5% CO2. The Expi293F cells were utilised for assessing EGFP expression from circRNAs, whereas the HEK293T cells were employed for evaluating anti-PD-L1 and hEPO expression from mRNA or circRNAs. During the continuous monitoring of anti-PD-L1 and hEPO expression, HEK293T cells were passaged at a 1:4 ratio after three days of culture.

Plasmid templates for IVT

The IVT templates containing the T7 promoter for the PIE, STS, TRIC and SIE systems were either chemically synthesised (Logenbio, China), or artificially constructed in accordance with the specified design. The IVT template for linear mRNA synthesis was prepared following the protocol of the Takara IVTproTM mRNA Synthesis System (Takara, Japan). For both mRNA and circRNA synthesis, it is essential to conduct precise restriction enzyme-mediated linearization of plasmid templates prior to IVT. The sequences of all IVT templates and the corresponding primer pairs used for template preparation are summarised in Supplementary Data 1. Escherichia coli DH5α cells were cultivated in LB medium at 37 °C for the purpose of plasmid construction and amplification.

CircRNA synthesis

The plasmid templates were linearised using a designated restriction enzyme, followed by extraction with a phenol/chloroform/isoamylol (25:24:1) solution. The RNA transcripts required for the PIE, STS, TRIC and SIE systems were obtained through IVT using the T7 High Yield RNA Synthesis Kit (New England Biolabs, USA). When necessary, m6ATP or m1ψTP at varying concentrations was incorporated during IVT to obtain RNA transcripts modified with m6A or m1ψ. The resulting IVT products were then treated with DNase I (Takara, Japan) at 37 °C for 30 min and subsequently were column-purified using a HiPure RNA Pure Micro kit (Magen, China).

For PIE-mediated circRNA synthesis in Figs. 2d, g and 3b, the precursor RNA was heat-treated at 70 °C for 5 min. Subsequently, it was incubated at 55 °C for 10 min in the presence of 2 mM GTP and a buffer containing magnesium (50 mM Tris-HCl, 10 mM MgCl2, 1 mM DTT, pH 7.5). To investigate the catalytic parameters of the SIE system, a single-variable method was adopted under basic conditions (55 °C, 10 min, 10 mM Mg²⁺) in the presence of 2 mM GTP. During the immobilised ribozyme-mediated circRNA synthesis process illustrated in Fig. 6b, thermal denaturation was performed at 65 °C (5 min), followed by a splicing reaction at 55 °C (10 min, 10 mM Mg²⁺, 2 mM GTP). In Fig. 6e, the splicing reaction was conducted at 37 °C without thermal denaturation, with the splicing reaction duration extended to 30 min. The immobilised ribozyme was regenerated to remove free introns through water elution at room temperature. The synthetic efficiency of modified circRNA utilising PIE, TRIC, and SIE was evaluated by denaturing the reaction mixture at 70 °C for 5 min, followed by incubation at 55 °C for 10 min (2 mM GTP, 10 mM Mg²⁺). A comparative assessment of circRNA synthesis efficiencies among PIE, STS, TRIC, and SIE was conducted by denaturing the reaction mixture at 70 °C for 5 min, followed by incubation at 55 °C for 3 min (2 mM GTP, 10 mM Mg²⁺).

The RNase R degradation assay was conducted by adding 1 U of RNase R (Vazyme, China) to 1 μg of splicing products at 37 °C for 10 min. All RNA samples were separated on 1 ~ 2% agarose gel and analysed using Tanon 3500 Gel Image System. The unprocessed images of the agarose gels are provided in Supplementary Figs. 12 and 13. Bands in Fig. 5a, c, e, g and Supplementary Fig. 7c were quantified using Image J, and circularisation efficiency was calculated quantitatively according to the following formula: ((Integrated density of circRNA)/(length of circRNA))/(((Integrated density of precursor)/(length of precursor)) + ((Integrated density of circRNA)/(length of circRNA))). In other assays, circularisation efficiency was quantified via RT-qPCR.

Linear mRNA synthesis

The linear m1ψ-modified mRNA was synthesised using EasyCap T7 Co-transcription Kit with CAG Trimer (Vazyme, China) as per the manufacturer’s instructions with complete replacement of uridine with m1ψTP. After the DNA template was removed by DNase I (Takara, Japan) at 37 °C for 30 min, the linear mRNA products were purified using a packed column containing POROS™ Oligo (dT)25 Affinity Resin (Thermo Scientific, USA).

CircRNA purification and characterization

To eliminate impurities of circRNAs, the HPLC assay was conducted using a 4.6 × 300 mm size exclusion column with a particle size of 5 µm and a pore size of 1000 Å (Sepax Technologies, USA) on an Elite EClassical 3200 Series HPLC system (Elite, China). RNA samples were separated in RNase-free phosphate buffer (150 mM sodium phosphate, pH 7.0) at a flow rate of 0.6 mL/min. Detection and collection of RNA were performed through UV absorbance at 260 nm. The RNA fractions obtained were subjected to desalting using VAHTS RNA Clean Beads (Vazyme, China) and were subsequently dissolved in RNase-free water. The RNase R digestion assay was performed subsequent to HPLC purification to further remove linear RNA from the circRNA. To verify the splice junction of circRNAs, reverse transcription was performed to synthesise complementary DNA (cDNA) using the EasyScript One-Step gDNA Removal and cDNA Synthesis SuperMix (TransGen, China). Then, a specific primer pair crossing the splice junction of the putative circRNA was utilised in a standard PCR procedure to amplify PCR products from the synthetic cDNA template. These PCR products were then cloned into the pUC57 vector for subsequent sequencing analysis.

RNA transfection

In order to analyse RNA expression, an equimolar quantity of circRNAs or m1ψ-modified mRNA was introduced into each well containing 4 × 106 Expi293F cells/2 mL or 2 × 106 HEK293T cells/2 mL in a six-well plate using the polyethylenimine (PEI)-mediated transfection technique. Following a transfection duration of 6 h, the serum-free DMEM medium in HEK293T cells was substituted with fresh DMEM medium supplemented with 10% foetal bovine serum. After being cultured for a specified duration, the cells or culture supernatant were harvested and utilised to assess the expression levels of circRNA and mRNA through appropriate methodologies, including fluorescence microscopy, flow cytometry, or enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA). Notably, the expression levels of anti-PD-L1 and hEPO were continuously monitored over a six-day period to evaluate their sustained presence.

RT-qPCR

After the catalysis of the ribozyme, the reaction products undergo reverse transcription. Random or specific primers are used to synthesise cDNA with the EasyScript One-Step gDNA Removal and cDNA Synthesis SuperMix (TransGen, China). Quantitative PCR analysis was conducted on an Applied Biosystems 7500 system (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA), employing the PerfectStart Green qPCR SuperMix (TransGen, China) as per the manufacturer’s instructions. The synthesis efficiency of circRNAs was calculated and normalised with the substrate. The primers employed for amplification in RT-qPCR assays were provided in Supplementary Data 1.

AnaR 3’-biotinylation and streptavidin capture

AnaR with 3’ end biotinylation was prepared by using the PierceTM RNA 3’ End Biotinylation Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, the in vitro transcribed AnaR was heated for 3 min at 85 °C. Subsequently, the T4 RNA ligase-mediated labelling reaction was set up by mixing the AnaR with an adequate excess of biotinylated cytidine (bis)phosphate in the reaction buffer (15% PEG, 1 U/μL RNase inhibitor, 50 mM Tris-HCl, 10 mM MgCl2, 10 mM DTT, 1 mM ATP, pH 7.8). The reaction was incubated at 16 °C overnight. Then, a chloroform/isoamylol solution was utilised to remove RNA ligase, and the aqueous phase was carefully transferred to a nuclease-free tube. RNA precipitation was conducted for 1 h at −20 °C in ice-cold ethanol with the assistance of NaCl and glycogen. Subsequently, centrifugation was carried out for 15 min at 13,000×g at 4 °C. The supernatant was carefully removed, and the pellet was washed with 70% (v/v) ice-cold ethanol. After the pellet was air-dried for approximately 5 min, it was resuspended using nuclease-free water.

Transfer streptavidin-coated magnetic beads into a 1.5 mL microcentrifuge tube. Separate the beads using a magnetic stand and discard the supernatant. Wash the beads twice with an equal volume of 20 mM Tris-HCl (pH 7.5). Add 1× RNA capture buffer (20 mM Tris-HCl, 1 M NaCl, 1 mM EDTA, pH 7.5) to resuspend the beads, mix with the labelled RNA, and incubate at room temperature with gentle agitation for 30 min. Following incubation, reapply the magnetic stand to remove the supernatant and repeat the washing steps with the Tris-HCl buffer twice. Finally, collect the beads, ensuring removal of any residual liquid, to complete the binding and purification process.

LNP encapsulation of circRNAs

LNP-circRNAs were prepared by combining an aqueous phase containing circRNAs with an ethanol phase containing the lipids and cholesterol components via microfluidic mixing devices. The devices utilised chaotic mixing features to induce fluid folding in a state of laminar flow to reproducibly form homogeneous LNPs. The aqueous phase was composed of 100 mM citrate buffer (pH 5.0) and circRNAs. The ethanol phase contained DLin-MC3-DMA, dioleoylphosphatidylethanolamine, cholesterol, DMG-PEG, and 18PA at the molar ratio of 35:7:26.5:1.5:3070. The aqueous and ethanol phases were then mixed in the microfluidic device at a 1:3 (v/v) ratio. After preparation, the LNP-circRNAs were dialysed against PBS for 4 h (MWCO = 3.5 kDa). To measure size, the LNP-circRNAs were 10 times diluted with PBS and analysed using dynamic light scattering (DLS) performed on a Zetasizer Nano (Malvern Instruments, UK). The diameter and polydispersity index (PDI) of the LNP were measured in triplicate. To measure the morphology, LNP-circRNAs were dialysed against deionised water for 2 h (MWCO = 3.5 kDa) twice. Then the dialysed LNP-circRNAs were diluted 100-fold using deionised water, and 10 μL of the sample was dropped onto a carbon-coated copper grid. Samples were dried under a vacuum to remove water. Imaging was performed using a Hitachi HT-7700 transmission electron microscope (Hitachi, Japan). The LNP encapsulation of mRNAs was carried out by referring to the above method.

Detection of anti-PD-L1 expression

ELISA plates (Corning, USA) were coated overnight with a PBS buffer (pH 7.0), containing 2 µg/mL of goat anti-human IgG polyclonal antibody (bs-0279G, Bioss, China). After blocking with 5% skim milk in PBS, the cell culture supernatant was moderately diluted and added to each well. Subsequently, the plates were incubated with HRP-coupled goat anti-human IgG-Fc polyclonal antibody (SSA001, SinoBiological, China) and developed using 3,3’,5,5’-tetramethyl-benzidine (TMB) substrate. The reactions were terminated by adding 2 M H2SO4, and the absorbance at 450 nm was measured. The content of anti-PD-L1 in the culture supernatant was determined through a standard curve.

Quantification of hEPO expression

The concentrations of hEPO were determined using an hEPO ELISA kit (FineTest, China). Specifically, 100 μL of the diluted cell culture supernatant was added to each well and incubated at 37 °C for 90 min. The plate was subsequently washed twice with wash buffer. Next, 100 μL of biotin-antibody working solution was added and incubated at 37 °C for 60 min, followed by three washes. Then, 100 μL of HRP-streptavidin conjugate working solution was added and incubated at 37 °C for 30 min, after which the plate was washed five times. Subsequently, 90 μL of TMB substrate solution was added and incubated at 37 °C for 15 min. Finally, 50 μL of stop solution was added, and absorbance was measured immediately at 450 nm. The hEPO concentration was calculated based on a standard curve.

Reticulocyte counting

To evaluate the expression of circRNA and mRNA following in vivo delivery, LNPs encapsulating 20 μg of circRNAhEPO or equimolar amounts of mRNAhEPO were administered to mice via r.o. injection. LNPs without RNA served as the control group. After a 6-day period, blood samples were collected from the mice for reticulocyte enumeration. Specifically, a 5 µL blood sample was mixed with 1 mL of 1 × 10−4 mg/mL thiazole orange and incubated at room temperature in the dark for 1 h. Following two washes, the samples were analysed using a Beckman CytoFLEX S flow cytometer, with 100,000 events recorded for each sample. Cells positive for thiazole orange were defined as reticulocytes.

In vivo toxicity assay

To evaluate the in vivo toxicity and immune response, whole blood samples were collected on day 6 after the administration of circRNAhEPO and mRNAhEPO. After being placed at room temperature for 2 h, they were centrifuged at 3000 rpm for 15 min at 4 °C, and the serum was taken for detection. The levels of ALT, AST, and CREA were measured using an automatic biochemical analyser (Rayto, China). The TNF-ɑ was measured using Mouse TNF alpha Uncoated ELISA Kit (Invitrogen, USA) according to the manual.

Statistical analysis

Unpaired two-tailed t-tests (*p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, ***p < 0.001, ****p < 0.0001) were employed for all data analyses. When variances were unequal, Welch’s correction was implemented. Error bars represent the mean ± standard deviation (SD) of replicates.
