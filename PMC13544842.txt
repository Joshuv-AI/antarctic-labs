Introduction

Primary hyperparathyroidism (PHPT) is the most common cause of hypercalcemia and is usually caused by a benign parathyroid adenoma located in the cervical region, adjacent to the thyroid glands[1]. However, in up to 20% of cases, the adenoma may be ectopic, which poses a diagnostic challenge when conventional neck imaging fails[2]. Embryologically, the inferior parathyroid glands and thymus originate from the third pharyngeal pouch and migrate into the mediastinum, making the thymus a potential ectopic site for parathyroid adenomas[3,4].

Ectopic parathyroid adenomas located entirely within the mediastinum are rare and often lead to a delayed diagnosis due to their atypical location and nonspecific presentation[5]. Nuclear imaging, particularly SPECT/CT with technetium-99m sestamibi, has significantly enhanced the localization and successful resection of these lesions[6]. Here, we present a case of an intrathymic parathyroid adenoma in a 19-year-old female with a delayed diagnosis, highlighting the role of advanced imaging and surgical planning in managing such cases. This case is reported in accordance with the SCARE guidelines[7,8].

Case presentation

This work has been reported in line with the SCARE 2025 criteria[7,8]. A 19-year-old Iranian woman presented with persistent bone pain, especially in the spine and knees, which began around the age of 15. She was initially evaluated by a neurosurgeon and treated for suspected osteoarthritis with NSAIDs and physiotherapy, but her symptoms did not improve. She reported multiple episodes of severe pain over the following 2 years. A follow-up bone densitometry scan revealed severe osteoporosis (Fig. 1). Over time, she developed additional complaints, including unintentional weight loss, palpitations, abdominal discomfort, constipation, polyuria, insomnia, and depression. She had a family history of thyroid disorder in her mother, but no other notable medical, surgical, or social history. Routine laboratory evaluations were largely normal, but her serum calcium was elevated at 13 mg/dL, and PTH was markedly increased at 614 pg/mL. Neck ultrasound and soft tissue neck CT scan showed no abnormalities. However, a technetium-99m sestamibi SPECT/CT scan revealed a 38 × 40 mm nodular lesion in the left anterior mediastinum with moderate uptake, consistent with an ectopic parathyroid adenoma (Fig. 2). A minimally invasive VATS approach was attempted but was unsuccessful. The adenoma was ultimately removed through a partial median sternotomy. The resected tissue mass was irregular and lobulated, grossly resembling a soft tumor (Fig. 3). Histopathological examination confirmed an intrathymic parathyroid adenoma with typical parathyroid cell populations surrounded by thymic and adipose tissue (Fig. 3). Postoperatively, the patient’s calcium level normalized to 10 mg/dL and PTH dropped to 14 pg/mL. She was discharged in good condition with complete resolution of her symptoms. Follow-up and outcomes

Follow-up details

Timing: Follow-up visits were scheduled at 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, and 6 months post-surgery. Location: Follow-up occurred at the outpatient clinic, with telephone or virtual consultations for subsequent visits. With Whom: Follow-up was conducted by the operating surgeon and an endocrinologist. Methods: Clinical examinations, blood tests (calcium and PTH), and a chest X-ray were performed to monitor recovery. Long-term Surveillance: Annual blood tests and chest X-rays were recommended to monitor for recurrence.

Intervention adherence and compliance

The patient adhered well to postoperative instructions, including activity restrictions and medications (calcium and vitamin D). Adherence was measured through follow-up exams and patient self-reporting. Impact on Long-Term Effectiveness: Full adherence contributed to a successful recovery with no complications.

Outcomes

Expected Outcome: Normalization of calcium and PTH, symptom relief, and improved quality of life (according to the literature on parathyroid adenoma resection). Achieved Outcome: Calcium and PTH levels normalized, and symptoms (bone pain and limited mobility) resolved. Patient-Reported Outcomes: Significant improvement in quality of life, with reduced pain and improved mobility.

Discussion

Ectopic parathyroid adenomas are an uncommon but important cause of PHPT, particularly when standard cervical imaging fails to localize the source of hormone excess. Among ectopic sites, the mediastinum – and specifically the thymus – is clinically relevant due to the shared embryologic origin of the inferior parathyroid glands and thymic tissue from the third pharyngeal pouch, which explains the occurrence of intrathymic parathyroid adenomas[1–3].

Delayed diagnosis is frequently reported in mediastinal parathyroid adenomas, especially in young patients with atypical or nonspecific manifestations such as bone pain, osteoporosis, neuropsychiatric symptoms, and gastrointestinal complaints[2,4]. In the present case, these features led to an initial focus on more common musculoskeletal and endocrine conditions, contributing to diagnostic delay and disease progression.

When neck ultrasonography and cervical CT are nondiagnostic, functional imaging becomes essential. Tc-99m sestamibi SPECT/CT provides combined functional and anatomical localization and has demonstrated high sensitivity for detecting ectopic mediastinal parathyroid adenomas[5,6]. In this patient, SPECT/CT was decisive in identifying the hyperfunctioning intrathymic lesion and guiding the surgical approach.

Surgical excision remains the definitive treatment for PHPT. Minimally invasive techniques such as VATS are often preferred for mediastinal lesions; however, their success depends on lesion size, depth, and accessibility[3,4]. In this case, the deep intrathymic location limited thoracoscopic access, necessitating conversion to a partial median sternotomy to achieve complete excision. Postoperatively, rapid normalization of serum calcium and parathyroid hormone levels with complete symptom resolution confirmed a biochemical cure, consistent with previously reported outcomes[1,6,8].

Strengths and limitations

Strengths

This case highlights the successful diagnosis and definitive surgical management of a rare intrathymic ectopic parathyroid adenoma. Accurate localization using Tc-99m sestamibi SPECT/CT and close collaboration between thoracic surgery, endocrinology, and radiology were key strengths that enabled timely intervention and complete biochemical and clinical resolution.

Limitations

The atypical presentation led to an initial misdiagnosis, resulting in delayed recognition of the underlying endocrine disorder. Because this is a single case of a rare condition, the findings have limited generalizability. Additionally, the need for conversion from a minimally invasive approach to open surgery reflects anatomical constraints that may not apply to all patients with mediastinal parathyroid adenomas.

Patient perspective

The patient reported significant relief after receiving a definitive diagnosis that explained her long-standing symptoms. Despite initial anxiety regarding surgery, she felt well supported by the multidisciplinary care team. Following surgical treatment, she experienced marked improvement in pain, mobility, and overall quality of life.

Conclusions

Intrathymic parathyroid adenomas should be considered in patients with persistent hyperparathyroidism and negative cervical imaging. Tc-99m sestamibi SPECT/CT enables accurate mediastinal localization and guides surgical planning. When minimally invasive approaches are unsuccessful, tailored open surgery can result in complete biochemical and clinical resolution.
