of the USSR to meet the Seven Year Plan goals for the production of
rolled steel and pipe. At the same time that the USSR is sut plying or
making commitments to supply metallurgical equipment to underdeveloped
countries inside and outside the bloc, she is relying heavily on
Czechoslovakia and East Germany for metallurgical equipment to meet
domestic production requirements. Imports of metallurgical equipment
from these 2 countries equalled about 20# of domestic Soviet production
in 1955, 15 $o in 195*5, and 21 $o in 1957* An even larger percentage is
obtained when comparing imports and domestic production of railing Mil

Germany and Czechoslovakia to the USSR represented about 83 # of their
combined domestic production, in 1956 , 69 #, and in 1957 , almost 90 #.

A major factor in recent failures to meet the planned goals for the
production of rolling mill equipment has been the lack of sufficient
productive capacity. The Ural Heavy Machine Building Plant at
Sverdlovsk and the Novo-Kramatorsk Heavy Machine Building Plait at

mills, have reportedly been overburdened with orders for heavy machinery

production of 111,000 metric tons of rolling mill equipment in the peak

220,000 metric tons in 1965 . Meanwhile there is little evidence that a
significant amount of new production capacity is scheduled unler the
plan. The Alma-Ata Heavy Machine Building Plant Is scheduled to be

capacity will increase has not been announced. Construction )f the new
rolling mill equipment plant at Petropavlovsk is now schedule 1 to begin
in 1961 although preliminary construction work was reported t) be in
progress in 1956 following announcement of the Sixth Five Yea.- Plan.
Because the plans call for completion of only several preparatory and
auxiliary shops by 1965 , it is not likely that substantial be lefits in
the form of new productive capacity will be realized from thi ; project
