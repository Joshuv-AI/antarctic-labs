On some generalizations of Gödel’s second incompleteness theorem
YUDAI SUZUKI, KEITA YOKOYAMA

Abstract
. In this note, we give some generalizations of Gödel’s second incompleteness theorem and study their surroundings. We revisit it from two perspectives. One perspective is the relationship between the definable complexity of a theory and unprovability of its soundness. We clarify the relationship between this perspective and induction axioms. We also determine the logical strength of Craig’s trick, which is important for studying the definability of a theory, from the point of view of reverse mathematics. The other perspective is semantic incompleteness. The second incompleteness theorem may be seen as the unprovability of the existence of a model. It is known that ‘model’ is replaced with ‘ ω \omega -model’ or ‘ β n \beta_{n} -model’. We give a new and unified proof of the ω \omega -model and β n \beta_{n} -model versions of the incompleteness theorem.



1.
Introduction

In this note, we give two generalizations of the second incompleteness theorem and study their surroundings.

The first one focuses on the relationship between the definability and the soundness of a theory.
In the context of reflection principles, the second incompleteness can be written as follows.

Theorem 1.1

(The second incompleteness theorem)
.

Let
T

\Sigma_{1}
-definable extension of
\mathsf{PA}

\Pi_{1}
-sound if and only if

An analogue of this theorem for

[
3

[
1
]

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
T
be a
\Sigma_{k+1}
-definable extension of
\mathsf{PA}
.
Then,
T
is
\Pi_{k+1}
-sound if and only if
T\not\vdash\mathsf{RFN}_{\Pi_{k+1}}(T)

In this note, we give a refinement of this theorem.
We consider extensions of

rather than

. By this refinement, we clarify the role of the induction axiom in this equivalence.
We also give a second-order analogue of Theorem

Our second generalization is an extension of the semantic incompleteness theorem.
By completeness theorem, the consistency of a theory is equivalent to the existence of a model of it. Thus, the second incompleteness theorem can be rephrased as follows for suitable theories.

If a theory

On the one hand, in Simpson’s monograph

In
