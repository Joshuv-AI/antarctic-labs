A geometric correspondence for reparameterizations of geodesic flows
Stephen Cantrell, Dídac Martínez-Granado, Eduardo Reyes

Abstract
. For any non-elementary, torsion-free hyperbolic group, we provide a correspondence between the left-invariant Gromov-hyperbolic metrics on the group that are quasi-isometric to a word metric, and continuous reparameterizations of the associated Mineyev’s flow space. From this correspondence, we produce the first examples of continuous reparameterizations of geodesic flows on negatively curved manifolds with all periodic orbits having integer lengths. For surface and free groups, this also yields isometric actions on Gromov-hyperbolic spaces on which loxodromic elements are precisely the non-simple elements. Key ingredients in our proof are an analysis of the geometry of Mineyev’s flow space (such as the metric-Anosov property recently proven by Dilsavor), and the density of Green metrics in the moduli space of (symmetric) metrics on the group. We further establish continuity of the Bowen–Margulis–Sullivan geodesic current map on the moduli space of metrics, as well as a Bowen-type description of these currents as limits of sums of appropriately normalized atomic geodesic currents. For surface groups, we apply this continuity result to show that the Bowen–Margulis–Sullivan map restricts to a topological embedding on Hitchin components (up to contragradient involution) when equipped with their Hilbert lengths.

1.1.
A dictionary between metrics and reparameterizations

Our first theorem gives a map from
\Par(\mathsf{T}^{1}M,\mathsf{g})
into
\MC({H}_{\Gamma}^{++})
, the metric completion of
{H}_{\Gamma}^{++}
with respect to
\Delta
. This map extends the embeddings of spaces of Anosov representations into both spaces. In the statement below,
\MC(X)
denotes the completion of a subspace
X\subset{H}_{\Gamma}^{++}
(see also
Definition

3.2
for the related spaces
\MC(\mathcal{D}_{\Gamma})
,
\MC(\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{++})
, and the translation lengths for
\psi\in\MC(\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{++})
). Also, we let
\Par^{s}(\mathsf{T}^{1}M,\mathsf{g})
and
\Par^{h}(\mathsf{T}^{1}M,\mathsf{g})
denote the subspaces of
\Par(\mathsf{T}^{1}M,\mathsf{g})
represented by the reparameterizations that are reversible and Hölder respectively; see
Section

5.1
for the details.

Theorem A

(Dictionary metrics-reparameterizations, manifold case)
.

Let
M
be a closed negatively curved Riemannian manifold with fundamental group
\Gamma
, and let
\mathsf{g}
be the geodesic flow on its unit tangent bundle
\mathsf{T}^{1}M
. Then there exists an injection

\Theta:\Par(\mathsf{T}^{1}M,\mathsf{g})\rightarrow\MC({H}^{++}_{\Gamma})

satisfying the following:

(1)

(Duality) If
\mathsf{\Psi}
is a reparameterization of
\mathsf{g}
, then
\Theta([\mathsf{\Psi}])=[\psi]
for some
\psi\in\MC(\mathcal{H}^{++}_{\Gamma})
such that

\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{\Psi}}[g]=\ell_{\psi}[g]\quad\text{ for any non-trivial }g\in\Gamma.

(2)

(Bijection on symmetric metrics)
\Theta(\Par^{s}(\mathsf{T}^{1}M,\mathsf{g}))=\MC({D}_{\Gamma})
.

(3)

(Hölder reparameterizations in the interior)
\Theta(\Par^{h}(\mathsf{T}^{1}M,\mathsf{g}))\subset{H}_{\Gamma}^{++}
.

That is, for any reparameterization
\mathsf{\Psi}
of the geodesic flow there is pseudometric
\psi
on
\Gamma
whose stable length
\ell_{\psi}
corresponds to the periods on closed orbits
\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{\Psi}}
of
\mathsf{\Psi}
. This dictionary is summarized in the following diagram.

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\Par(\mathsf{T}^{1}M,\mathsf{g})&\lhook\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow&\MC({H}^{++}_{\Gamma})\\
\scriptstyle\text{reparameterizations}&&\scriptstyle\text{pseudometrics}\\[3.99994pt]
\cup&&\cup\\[3.99994pt]
\Par^{s}(\mathsf{T}^{1}M,\mathsf{g})&\longleftrightarrow&\MC({D}_{\Gamma})\\
\text{\scriptsize\begin{tabular}[]{c}reversible\\
reparameterizations\end{tabular}}&&\scriptstyle\text{symmetric pseudometrics}\end{array}

This result is especially notable in that it does not require any a priori regularity of the reparameterization, extending beyond the Hölder framework.
In fact, there are at least two known families of metric structures in
{H}_{\Gamma}^{++}
that cannot be encoded by Hölder reparameterizations.

Example 1.1
.

Suppose

is a left-invariant metric on

with

translation length: there exists

such that

satisfies

(cf.

and
[

). This is the case when

is a word metric for a finite generating set of
\Gamma

[

]
or

is induced by the geometric action of

on a

[

]
.

Example 1.2
.

Suppose
M
is a surface and
\mu
is a filling (symmetric)

on
\Gamma

[
8
]
; see

2.6

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
M
be and
\Gamma
be as in

, and let
\ell:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be either:

(1)

the stable translation length of a word metric for a finite generating set;

(2)

the stable translation length for a geometric action on a

(3)

Then there exists a reparameterization

By rescaling the reparameterization in items (1) or (2) above, we obtain continuous reparameterizations of the geodesic flow of a closed negatively curved manifold all whose periods are integers. To the best of the authors’ knowledge, these are the first examples of reparameterizations with this property.

1.2.
Bowen–Margulis–Sullivan geodesic currents

The relation between reparameterizations and pseudometrics also holds for their associated spaces of measures. Suppose
\Gamma
is a non-elementary torsion-free hyperbolic group
\Gamma
, with associated
Mineyev’s flow space
(\mathsf{U}\Gamma,\mathsf{g})
. Then any Hölder reparameterization of
\mathsf{g}
corresponds to a unique
\mathsf{g}
-invariant probability measure on
\mathsf{U}\Gamma
, its
Gibbs measure
.
This follows from
[
85
,
46
]
, whereas the result for geodesic flows on closed negatively curved manifolds goes back to
[

,
12
]
. This Gibbs measure completely determines the class of the reparameterization in

On the other hand, each hyperbolic metric potential

induces a geodesic current
\Lambda_{\psi}
, i.e., a Radon measure on the double Gromov boundary

that is invariant under the diagonal action of

(see Section

).
This geodesic current
\Lambda_{\psi}
is ergodic and lies in the measure class of

for

a
quasi-conformal
measure for
\psi
and
\hat{\nu}
a quasi-conformal measure for
opposite
hyperbolic metric potential

2.5

[
20
]
, based on the work of Furman
[
50
]
for pseudometrics in

The projective class of

Theorem B

(Continuity of
\mathsf{BMS}
)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a non-elementary torsion-free hyperbolic group. Then the Bowen–Margulis–Sullivan map
\mathsf{BMS}:{H}^{++}_{\Gamma}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}
is continuous and has dense image.

Note that
\mathsf{BMS}
cannot be a proper map. Indeed, BMS geodesic currents do not form a closed subset, since they have full support and form a dense subset. If
\mathsf{BMS}
was proper, and since the space of projective currents is compact Hausdorff
[
9
]
,
\mathsf{BMS}
would be a closed map and hence its image would be closed, a contradiction.

The proof of this theorem relies on the relation between positive continuous functions on
\mathsf{U}\Gamma
with the
Bowen property
(

5.7
) and
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{++}
; see

and the space of

[

9

The geodesic current
\Lambda_{\psi}
in the conclusion satisfies
\ell_{\psi}(\Lambda_{\psi})=1
, where
\ell_{\psi}
denotes the continuous extension of
\ell_{\psi}
to
\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}
(
Corollary

6.13
). This result was claimed by Furman
[
50
, Equation 10]
, but no proof has been available since. The proof of this proposition makes crucial use of
Theorem

6.1
, which describes the stable translation lengths of BMS currents.

1.3.
Related work

A
is inspired by
Ledrappier’s correspondence

[
72
]
, which for a closed negatively curved manifold
M
with
\Gamma=\pi_{1}(M)
establishes correspondences between

(1)

conjugacy classes of Hölder reparameterizations in
\Par^{h}(\mathsf{T}^{1}M,\mathsf{g})
;

(2)

cohomology classes of positive real-valued Hölder continuous potentials
F:\mathsf{T}^{1}M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
; and,

(3)

cohomology classes of Hölder cocycles
\mathsf{c}:\Gamma\times\partial\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with positive periods and finite entropy.

See
Section

4.4
for the detailed definitions. Note that the cocycles in item (3) above are only defined in terms of
\Gamma
, and therefore can be obtained from geometric information that is not necessarily related to
M
. In the setting of Anosov representations, this correspondence was first used by Sambarino

[
90
]
to prove counting results for strictly convex (for instance, Hitchin) representations. See also
[
92
]
for a version of Ledrappier’s correspondence for Mineyev’s flow space
\mathsf{U}\Gamma
.

For compact locally
\CAT(-1)
spaces, some distance-like functions on
\Gamma
have been produced from Hölder functions on the geodesic flow space, similar to
A
.
This is the case of the works
[
31
,
32
]
on harmonicity of Patterson–Sullivan measures, and
[
52
]
on singularity of hitting measures (for random walks with superexponential moment) with respect to Gibbs measures.

In our setting, we obtain points in
{H}_{\Gamma}^{++}
from positive continuous potentials
F:\mathsf{U}\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfying the
Bowen condition
, see
Definition

5.7
and
Proposition

5.9
. This condition is strictly weaker than being Hölder, and it also appears in the recent work of Carrasco and Rodríguez-Hertz
[
26
]
. For compact, locally
\CAT(-1)
spaces, they show a correspondence between
measurable
Bowen potentials on the flow space
\mathsf{U}\Gamma
and
quasimorphisms
of
\Gamma
, in a similar spirit to
A
.
Our work is disjoint from theirs in two aspects. First, we are able to construct
continuous
potentials from geometric data on
\Gamma
, which is not immediately accessible from their methods. Second, for a hyperbolic group
\Gamma
, its space of quasimorphisms is disjoint from
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{++}
(up to bounded functions) and is “orthogonal” to
\mathcal{D}_{\Gamma}
(see Section
2.4
).

Beyond the continuous setting, there are also recent developments in the Patterson–Sullivan theory for
coarse
cocycles associated to convergence group actions by Blayac–Canary–Zhu–Zimmer
[
7
,
6
]
. See also Dilsavor’s thesis
[
46
]
for related notions and Kim–Zimmer’s work on PS systems
[
68
]
. We wonder if the continuous cocycles obtained from our work (or a modification thereof) are instances of continuous GPS-systems in the sense of
[
6
]
, allowing for refinements of
Proposition

1.7
.

1.4.
Green metrics

To obtain continuous reparameterizations in
A
, we make use of
Green metrics
on hyperbolic groups. These metrics were introduced by Blachère and Brofferio in
[
3
]
and encode probabilistic information about random walks on the group.

A probability measure
\mu
on the (non-necessarily hyperbolic group)
\Gamma
is
admissible
if its support generates
\Gamma
as a semi-group. If the random walk
(Z_{k})_{k}
on
\Gamma
with transition probabilities given by
\mu
is transient, the Green metric
d_{\mu}
on
\Gamma
is given by

d_{\mu}(g,h):=-\log\mathbb{P}(\exists k\text{ s.t. }gZ_{k}=h).

Note that
d_{\mu}
is not necessarily symmetric. For
\Gamma
a non-elementary hyperbolic group, we have that
d_{\mu}
belongs to
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{++}
as long as
\mu
has superexponential moment and
d_{\mu}\in\mathcal{D}_{\Gamma}
if in addition
\mu
is symmetric
[
5
,
54
,
51
]
. These results are based on the work of Ancona
[
1
]
.

For a non-elementary hyperbolic group, we show that Green metrics induce a dense subspace of
{D}_{\Gamma}
. More precisely, by considering suitably chosen random walks supported on large spheres, we construct sequences of Green metrics whose induced metric structures converge to an arbitrary point in
{D}_{\Gamma}
.

Theorem C

(Density of Green metrics)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a non-elementary hyperbolic group and
d\in\mathcal{D}_{\Gamma}
. Then there exists a constant
\delta_{1}>0
satisfying the following. Let
\delta>\delta_{1}
and
\mu_{l}
be the uniform probability measure supported on
S_{l}=\{g\in\Gamma\colon l-\delta<d(o,g)\leq l\}
. Then
S_{l}
generates
\Gamma
for
l
large enough and
[d_{\mu_{l}}]
converges to
[d]
in
{D}_{\Gamma}
.

The theorem above also has the following consequence, which partially recovers a result of Gouëzel–Mathéus–Maucourant
[
53
, Theorem 1.4]
. For a probability measure
\mu
on
\Gamma
and
d\in\mathcal{D}_{\Gamma}
,
h(\mu)
and
\ell(\mu)
denote the
entropy
and the
drift
(with respect to
d\in\mathcal{D}_{\Gamma}
) of
\mu
, respectively.

Corollary 1.8
.

Given
d\in\mathcal{D}_{\Gamma}
with exponential growth rate
v
, let
(\mu_{l})_{l}
be a sequence of probability measures as in
C
. Then we have that

h(\mu_{l})/\ell(\mu_{l})\to v\quad\text{ as }l\to\infty.

Proof.

The convergence
[d_{\nu_{l}}]\to[d]
from
C
implies that the dilations
{\rm Dil}(vd,d_{\nu_{l}})
(see
Section

2.3
) converge to 1. Here we use
[
89
, Lemma 3.6]
and the fact that each
d_{\mu_{l}}
has exponential growth rate 1
[
4
, Remark 3.2]
. If
\tau(d/d_{\mu_{l}})
denotes the
mean distortion
of
d
with respect to
d_{\mu_{l}}
(see
Equation

2.5
), we have that
\tau(d/d_{\mu_{l}})\to v^{-1}
since
v^{-1}\leq\tau(d/d_{\mu_{l}})\leq{\rm Dil}(d,d_{\mu_{l}})

[
24
, Theorem 1.2]
. The result then follows since
\tau(d/d_{\mu_{l}})
also equals
\ell(\mu_{l})/h(\mu_{l})
.
∎

When
\Gamma
is the fundamental group of a closed hyperbolic surface, Green metrics are also related to the
singularity conjecture

[
62
]
. This conjecture predicts that if
\mu
is an admissible finitely supported probability measure on
\Gamma
and
\rho:\Gamma\rightarrow\PSL(2,\mathbb{R})
is a discrete and faithful representation, then the hitting measure of
\mu
on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
is mutually singular to the Lebesgue measure. As proven in
[
5
]
, this is equivalent to
[d_{\mu}]\neq\mathbf{x}_{\rho}\in{D}_{\Gamma}
, where
\mathbf{x}_{\rho}
is the point induced by the action of
\Gamma
on
\mathbb{H}^{2}
given by
\rho
. In a sense,
C
reveals that the singularity conjecture is as hard as it can be from the point of view of the length functions: for any point
\mathbf{x}
in the Teichmüller space of
\Gamma
, any neighborhood of
\mathbf{x}
in
{D}_{\Gamma}
contains a point induced by a Green metric for a finitely supported measure.

The geometry of Green metrics and
C
are crucial in the proof of
A
(2). The main fact is that Green metrics are strongly hyperbolic
[
81
]
, hence they have Hölder continuous Busemann cocycles. For these cocycles, Ledrappier’s correspondence applies, and then a limiting argument using
C
upgrades this correspondence to continuous potentials on
\mathsf{U}\Gamma
that are dual to metrics in
\mathcal{D}_{\Gamma}
, see
Proposition

5.13
.

Preliminaries

2.1.
Pseudometric spaces and quasi-isometries

Let
(X,d)
be a
pseudometric
space; that is,
d\colon X\times X\to\mathbb{R}
is a non-negative function satisfying
d(x,x)=0
and the triangle inequality. This is weaker than being a metric: in particular, we may have
d(x,y)=0
for distinct points
x,y\in X
, and
d
need not be symmetric.
The
double difference
in
X
is the function
\langle\cdot,\cdot\mid\cdot,\cdot\rangle\colon X^{4}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

\langle a,a^{\prime}\mid b,b^{\prime}\rangle\coloneq\frac{1}{2}\left(d(a,b)-d(a^{\prime},b)-d(a,b^{\prime})+d(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})\right).

This extends the notion introduced
by Paulin in
[
83
]
when
d
is a symmetric distance. The function

\langle a\mid b\rangle_{c}\coloneqq\langle a,c\mid c,b\rangle=\frac{1}{2}(d(a,c)+d(c,b)-d(a,c))

is the
Gromov product
with respect to the pseudometric
d
, and we have the relation

\langle a,b\mid x,y\rangle=\langle b\mid x\rangle_{a}-\langle b\mid y\rangle_{a}.

If we want to emphasize the dependence of
d
, we will write
\langle a,b\mid a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\rangle=\langle a,b\mid a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\rangle^{d}
and
\langle a\mid b\rangle_{c}=\langle a\mid b\rangle^{d}_{c}
.

Given an interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
and constants
L,C>0
, we
say that a map
\gamma\colon I\to X
is an
(L,C,d)
-quasigeodesic

if

L^{-1}|s-t|-C\leq d(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))\leq L|s-t|+C\quad\text{for all }s,t\in I,

and a
(C,d)
-rough geodesic
if

|s-t|-C\leq d(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))\leq|s-t|+C\quad\text{for all }s,t\in I.

A
(0,d)
-rough geodesic is referred to as a
d
-geodesic
. Unless necessary, we will drop the dependence on
d
and refer, respectively, to
(L,C)
-quasigeodesics,

C
-rough geodesics, or geodesics.
The pseudometric space
(X,d)
is called
C
-roughly geodesic
if for
each
x,y\in X
, there is a
C
-rough geodesic connecting
x
and
y
.
We simply say
(X,d)
is
roughly geodesic
if it is
C
-roughly geodesic
for some
C\geq 0
.

Given two pseudometric spaces
(X,d)
and
(Y,\hat{d})
,
a map
F\colon X\to Y
is a
quasi-isometric embedding
if there exist
constants
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},C>0
such that

\lambda_{1}d(x,y)-C\leq\hat{d}(F(x),F(y))\leq\lambda_{2}d(x,y)+C\quad\text{for all }x,y\in X.

If, moreover, there is a constant
C^{\prime}>0
such that every
y\in Y
is
within
C^{\prime}
of some element of
F(X)
, we say that
F
is a
quasi-isometry
.
A quasi-isometry
F
satisfying
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}
is called a

rough similarity
, and when
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=1
it is called
a
rough isometry
. If
d_{1},d_{2}
are two pseudometrics on the same set
X
, we say that
d_{1},d_{2}
are
quasi-isometric/roughly similar/roughly isometric
if the identity map
\Id_{X}:(X,d_{1})\rightarrow(X,d_{2})
is a quasi-isometry/rough similarity/rough isometry.

2.2.
Gromov hyperbolic spaces

A pseudometric space
(X,d)
is
\delta
-hyperbolic

(\delta\geq 0)
if

\langle x\mid z\rangle_{w}\geq\min\{\langle x\mid y\rangle_{w},\langle y\mid z\rangle_{w}\}-\delta\quad\text{ for all }x,y,z,w\in X,

and
(X,d)
is
hyperbolic
if it is
\delta
-hyperbolic for some
\delta\geq 0
. Equivalently, we say that
d
is
(\delta
-)hyperbolic.
A pseudometric space
(X,d)
is
strongly hyperbolic
with parameter
\varepsilon>0
if

(2.1)

e^{-\varepsilon\langle x\mid z\rangle_{w}}\leq e^{-\varepsilon\langle x\mid y\rangle_{w}}+e^{-\varepsilon\langle y\mid z\rangle_{w}}\quad\text{ for all }x,y,z,w\in X.

Any strongly hyperbolic space with parameter
\varepsilon
is
\delta
-hyperbolic with
\delta=(\log 2)/\varepsilon

[
81
, Theorem 4.2]
.

Hyperbolic pseudometric spaces have a natural completion at infinity: its Gromov boundary. A sequence
\{x_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
of points in
X

diverges

if
\langle x_{k}\mid x_{m}\rangle^{d}_{o}
tends to infinity as
\min\{k,m\}
tends to infinity, for
o
a fixed base point in
X
. Two divergent sequences
\{x_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
and
\{y_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
are said to be
equivalent
if
\langle x_{k}\mid y_{m}\rangle^{d}_{o}
tends to infinity as
\min\{k,m\}
tends to infinity. The notions of divergence and equivalence are independent of the base point
o\in X
. The
Gromov boundary

\partial X
of
X
is the space of equivalence classes of divergent sequences in
X
. If
a\in\partial X
is represented by the divergent sequence
\{x_{k}\}_{k}
we say that
x_{k}

converges
to
a
. We denote
\overline{X}\coloneqq X\cup\partial X
.

Using hyperbolicity, we form a quasi-extension of the Gromov product
\langle\cdot\mid\cdot\rangle^{d}_{o}:\overline{X}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

in such a way that for some constant
C\geq 0
we have that

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\langle x_{k}\mid y_{k}\rangle^{d}_{o}-C\leq\langle a\mid b\rangle^{d}_{o}\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\langle x_{k}^{\prime}\mid y_{k}^{\prime}\rangle^{d}_{o}+C

for all
a,b\in\overline{X}
and for all
\{x_{k}\}_{k\geq 0},\{x_{k}^{\prime}\}_{k\geq 0}\in a
and
\{y_{k}\}_{k\geq 0},\{y_{k}^{\prime}\}_{k\geq 0}^{\infty}\in b
(if
a\in X
we interpret
\{x_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}\in a
as as sequence
\{x_{k}\}_{k}
converging to
a
).

For
R>0
, the
shadow set
of
x\in X
with radius
R
is

O(x,R)=O_{d}(x,R)=\{a\in\partial X:\langle x\mid a\rangle_{o}\geq d(o,x)-R\}\subset\partial X.

The
Busemann function

\beta^{d}_{o}:X\times\partial X
is defined according to

(2.2)

\beta^{d}_{o}(x,a):=\sup\big\{\limsup_{k\to\infty}(d(x,x_{k})-d(o,x_{k}))\ :\ \{x_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}\in a\big\}.

When
(X,d)
is strongly hyperbolic, the Busemann function is obtained as a genuine limit (i.e.
\beta^{d}_{o}(x,a):=\lim_{k\to\infty}d(x,x_{k})-d(o,x_{k})
). Similarly, the Gromov product extends uniquely to
\overline{X}
, and is continuous for the corresponding topology on
\overline{X}
. Moreover, we have the following criterion for strong hyperbolicity.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
81
, Lemma 6.2]
)
.

A hyperbolic pseudometric space
(X,d)
is strongly hyperbolic with parameter
\varepsilon
if and only if there exist constants
C,R_{0}>0
such that if
x,y,z,w\in\overline{X}
satisfy

\langle x,w\mid y,z\rangle\geq R\geq R_{0},

then

|\langle x,z\mid y,w\rangle|\leq Ce^{-\varepsilon R/2}.

2.3.
Hyperbolic groups and the space of symmetric pseudometrics

Recall that a non-elementary hyperbolic group is a finitely generated group
\Gamma
that is not virtually cyclic and such that any word metric
d_{S}
on
\Gamma
associated to a finite, symmetric generating set
S
is hyperbolic. Throughout this work we use
o
to denote the identity element of
\Gamma
and
[\Gamma]
(resp.
[\Gamma]^{\prime}
) to denote the set of (resp. non-torsion) conjugacy classes of
\Gamma
.

Given a hyperbolic group
\Gamma
we write
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
for the collection of pseudometrics
d
on
\Gamma
that are

•

hyperbolic;

•

quasi-isometric to a (equivalent any) word metric
d_{S}
on
\Gamma
; and,

•

are (left)
\Gamma
-invariant:
d(gx,gy)=d(x,y)
for all
g,x,y\in\Gamma
.

The pseudometrics in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
are roughly geodesic
[
11
]
.

We are interested in the space
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
up to rough similarity (recall Section
2.1
). Let
{D}_{\Gamma}
denote the quotient of
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
by the equivalence relation of rough similarity
[
50
]
. Given
d\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
we write
[d]\in{D}_{\Gamma}
for its corresponding equivalence class. There is a natural metric
\Delta
on
{D}_{\Gamma}
which is the
symmetrized Thurston metric
:
for
d_{1},d_{2}\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
let

{\rm Dil}(d_{1},d_{2})=\sup_{[g]\in[\Gamma]^{\prime}}\frac{\ell_{d_{1}}[g]}{\ell_{d_{2}}[g]}.

Then given
[d_{1}],[d_{2}]\in{D}_{\Gamma}
, define its distance according to

\Delta([d_{1}],[d_{2}])=\log(\Dil(d_1,d_2) \, \Dil(d_2,d_1)).

Here
\ell_{d}
denotes the
stable translation length
of
d
:

\ell_{d}[g]:=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{d(o,g^{k})}{k}\quad\text{ for }[g]\in[\Gamma].

The metric properties of
({D}_{\Gamma},\Delta)
were studied in
[
89
]
and
[
21
]
.

For a hyperbolic group, its Gromov boundary can be defined using any metric
d\in\mathcal{D}_{\Gamma}
, and the resulting space
\partial\Gamma
is independent of this choice by the Morse lemma. Moreover,
\partial\Gamma
is compact and metrizable.

2.4.
Hyperbolic metric potentials

As discussed in the introduction, we are interested in a natural non-symmetric extension of
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
. Let
\Gamma
be a non-elementary hyperbolic group with identity element
o\in\Gamma
as above.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}
denote the space of all functions

\psi\colon\Gamma\times\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying:

(1)

\Gamma
-invariance:
\psi(gx,gy)=\psi(x,y)
for all
x,y,g\in\Gamma
; and,

(2)

for any
d_{0}\in\mathcal{D}_{\Gamma}
there exists
\lambda>0
so that for every

x,y,w\in\Gamma
we have

\bigl|\langle x\mid y\rangle_{w}^{\psi}\bigr|\leq\lambda\langle x\mid y\rangle_{w}^{d_{0}}+\lambda,

where

\langle x\mid y\rangle_{w}^{\psi}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\bigl(\psi(x,w)+\psi(w,y)-\psi(x,y)\bigr)

denotes the
\psi
-
Gromov product
of
x,y
based at
w
.

The elements of
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}
are called
hyperbolic metric potentials
.
We also let
\mathcal{H}^{++}_{\Gamma}
be the set of proper maps
\psi\in\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}

such that
\psi(x,y)>-C
for all
x,y\in\Gamma
, which we refer to as
properly positive hyperbolic metric potentials
.

Hyperbolic metric potentials were introduced it
[
20
]
.
In that work, the following characterization of hyperbolic metric potentials was provided
[
20
, Proposition 3.1]
.

Proposition 2.3
.

A function
\psi:\Gamma\times\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}
is a hyperbolic metric potential if and only if it is
\Gamma
-invariant and the following holds. Given
d\in\mathcal{D}_{\Gamma}
and all large enough
C,R\geq 0
, there exists
C_{0}\geq 0
such that for all
g,h\in\Gamma
and any
(C,d)
-rough geodesic
\gamma
between
g
and
h
, we have

|\langle g\mid h\rangle_{w}^{\psi}|\leq C_{0}

for all
w
in the
R
-neighborhood of
\gamma
for the metric
d
.

Generalizing the situation for
\mathcal{D}_{\Gamma}
, given
\psi\in\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}
we define its
stable translation length function

\ell_{\psi}:[\Gamma]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
according to

\ell_{\psi}[g]=\lim_{k\to\infty}{\frac{\psi(o,g^{k})}{k}}.

This function is well-defined and finite
[
20
, Lemma 3.4]
.

Even though
\mathcal{D}_{\Gamma}
and
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{++}
play a prominent role as subspaces of
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}
, there are other subspaces of interest that will appear throughout this work. These are:

•

\partial\mathcal{D}_{\Gamma}
: the subspace of
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}
consisting of symmetric pseudo-metrics on
\Gamma
that are unbounded but do not belong to
\mathcal{D}_{\Gamma}
.

•

\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}:=\mathcal{D}_{\Gamma}\cup\partial\mathcal{D}_{\Gamma}
.

•

\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{+}
: the space of all
\psi\in\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}
such that
\ell_{\psi}\geq 0
.

•

\overline{\mathcal{H}^{++}_{\Gamma}}
: the subspace of
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{+}
such that
\ell_{\psi}
is not identically zero.

•

\partial\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{++}:=\overline{\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{++}}\backslash\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{++}
.

•

\mathcal{Q}_{\Gamma}
: the space of
\psi\in\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}
such that
\ell_{\psi}[g]=-\ell_{\psi}[g^{-1}]
for all
g\in\Gamma
.

By
[
20
, Lemma 3.22]
, the space
\mathcal{Q}_{\Gamma}
is in natural correspondence with the space of quasimorphisms on
\Gamma
. This space intersects
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{+}
in the subspace
{\mathcal{B}}_{\Gamma}
of bounded
\Gamma
-invariant functions on

2.6.
Geodesic currents

The
double boundary

We equip

A

\Gamma

We let

denote the space of all geodesic currents on
\Gamma

equipped with the weak
∗

(i.e. invariant under the flip

.

Geodesic currents were introduced by Bonahon
[
8
,
10
]
as a completion of the space of weighted conjugacy classes in
\Gamma
. Indeed, if
g
is a primitive non-torsion element, i.e. if

for some

then

, let
g^{-},g^{+}
be its repelling and attracting fixed points
in
\partial\Gamma
, respectively. Let
\eta_{[g]}
be the sum of Dirac measures
supported on the
\Gamma
-translates of
(g^{-},g^{+})
. If
g=h^{m}
for
h
primitive and
m\geq 1
, we let
\eta_{[g]}\coloneqq m\cdot\eta_{[h]}
.
Since
\Gamma\cdot(g^{-},g^{+})
is countable and discrete,
\eta_{[g]}
is a locally finite Borel measure on
\partial^{2}\Gamma
, hence Radon,
and it is clearly
\Gamma
-invariant.
We call
\eta_{[g]}
the
rational current
associated to
g
, which actually only depends on the conjugacy class
[g]\in[\Gamma]^{\prime}
. Bonahon also showed that positive real multiples of rational currents
are dense in
\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}

[
10

.

Let

\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}\coloneqq(\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}\setminus\{0\})/\mathbb{R}^{+}

denote the space of
projective geodesic currents
equipped
with the quotient topology. The space
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}

is compact and metrizable

8
]
. We let
[\Lambda]
denote the projective class of the non-zero current
\Lambda
.

In
[
50
]
, Furman noted that pseudometrics in
\mathcal{D}_{\Gamma}
induce geodesic currents in a natural way, and this was further extended in
[

to properly positive hyperbolic metric potentials.
Given
\psi\in{\mathcal{H}}^{++}_{\Gamma}
, let
\hat{\psi}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{++}_{\Gamma}
be defined as
\hat{\psi}(g,h)\coloneqq\psi(h,g)
. We have that
\hat{\psi}
also belongs to
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{++}
and
v_{\psi}=v_{\hat{\psi}}
. Consider quasi-conformal measures

for
\psi
and
\hat{\psi}

there exists a geodesic current

satisfying

where

.
This induces a map

of
\Lambda_{\psi}

the

of
\psi
or
[\psi]

Lemma 3.5

\widetilde{\nu}

This function is well-defined

\psi_{r_{1}}
, and assuming we have found
\hat{\psi}_{1},\hat{\psi}_{2},\dots\hat{\psi}_{k}
, we use
Corollary

3.6
to find a pseudometric
\hat{\psi}^{\prime}_{k+1}
that is roughly isometric to
\psi_{r_{k+1}}
and such that
\psi^{\prime}_{k+1}\leq{\rm Dil}(\psi_{r_{k+1}},\psi_{r_{k}})\hat{\psi}_{k}
. Then we define
\hat{\psi}_{k+1}:=\max\{\psi^{\prime}_{k+1},{\rm Dil}(\psi_{r_{k}},\psi_{r_{k+1}})^{-1}\psi^{\prime}_{k}\}.

By construction and a simple induction, our choice of subsequence
(\mathbf{x}_{r_{k}})_{k}
implies that
\hat{\psi}_{k+1}\leq 2\hat{\psi}_{1}
for each
k
. In particular, up to taking a further subsequence and reindexing, we can assume that
(\hat{\psi}_{k})_{k}
point-wise converges to a pseudometric
\psi
, which we claim satisfies all the required properties.

First, from the construction of
(\hat{\psi}_{k})_{k}
we have

(3.1)

2^{-1/2^{m-1}}\hat{\psi}_{m}\leq\hat{\psi}_{m+k+1}\leq 2^{1/2^{m-1}}\hat{\psi}_{m}\quad\text{ for all }m,k,

and the same inequalities hold if we replace
\hat{\psi}_{m+k+1}
by
\psi
. In particular, we have

2^{-1/2^{m-1}}\ell_{\psi_{r_{m}}}\leq\ell_{\psi}\leq 2^{1/2^{m-1}}\ell_{\psi_{r_{m}}}

for all
m
, implying that
\log\ell_{\psi_{m}}
uniformly converges to
\log\ell_{\psi}
. It is not hard to see that this convergence holds for any sequence in
{H}_{\Gamma}^{++}
converging to
\mathbf{x}
, thus we have proved item (1). Item (2) then follows from
Equation

3.1
, and item (3) follows from item (1) and
Corollary

6.13
.
∎

We can also use
Proposition

3.1
to define a “completion” of
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}
. In virtue of
[
20
, Proposition 3.8]
,
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}
is spanned as a real vector space by
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{++}
. Hence we make the following definitions.

Definition 3.7
.

We define
\MC(\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma})
as the set of linear combinations of elements in
\MC(\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{++})
. If
\psi=\psi^{+}-\psi^{-}
for
\psi^{+},\psi^{-}\in\MC(\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{++})
, we define its stable translation length function as
\ell_{\psi}:=\ell_{\psi^{+}}-\ell_{\psi^{-}}
. We also let
\MC(\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{+})
be the subset of elements of
\psi
in
\MC(\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma})
such that
\ell_{\psi}\geq 0
.

It turns out that the translation function
\ell_{\psi}
is independent of the decomposition
\psi=\psi^{+}-\psi^{-}
as above. Also, in virtue of
Proposition

3.1
, we define
\MC({H}_{\Gamma})
as the sets of equivalence classes in
\MC(\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma})
, in which we identify
\psi
and
\psi^{\prime}
if
\ell_{\psi}=\lambda\ell_{\psi^{\prime}}
for some
\lambda>0
.

3.2.
The strong length topology

We continue by defining the strong length topology on
\overline{{H}_{\Gamma}^{++}}
.
For that we fix an auxiliary hyperbolic metric potential
\psi_{0}\in\mathcal{H}^{++}_{\Gamma}
.

Definition 3.8
.

Given
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\overline{{H}^{++}_{\Gamma}}
, we define

D_{\psi_{0}}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}):=\inf\{\varepsilon>0:|\ell_{\psi}-\ell_{\varphi}|\leq\varepsilon\ell_{\psi_{0}}\},

where
\mathbf{x}=[\psi],\mathbf{y}=[\varphi]
are representatives such that
{\rm Dil}(\psi,\psi_{0})={\rm Dil}(\varphi,\psi_{0})=1
.

Lemma 3.9
.

D_{\psi_{0}}
is a metric on
\overline{{H}_{\Gamma}^{++}}
whose underlying topology is independent of the base potential
\psi_{0}\in\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{++}
.

The underlying topology of the metric
D_{\psi_{0}}
is the
strong length topology
on
\overline{{H}_{\Gamma}^{++}}
.

Proof.

Since the homothety class of
\ell_{\psi}
determines
[\psi]
, it follows that
D_{\psi_{0}}
is a metric. To show that it is independent of
\psi_{0}
, let
\hat{\psi}_{0}\in\mathcal{H}^{++}_{\Gamma}
be such that
\alpha^{-1}\ell_{\psi_{0}}\leq\ell_{\hat{\psi}_{0}}\leq\beta\ell_{\psi_{0}}
for some
\alpha,\beta>0
. We let
\mathbf{x}=[\psi],\mathbf{y}=[\varphi]\in\overline{{H}^{++}_{\Gamma}}
be such that
{\rm Dil}(\psi,\psi_{0})={\rm Dil}(\varphi,\psi_{0})=1
, and let
A>0
be such that
\ell_{\psi}\leq A\ell_{\psi_{0}}
. From this we have

\beta^{-1}\leq{\rm Dil}(\psi,\hat{\psi}_{0})\leq\alpha.

If
\varepsilon:=D_{\psi_{0}}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
is such that
\varepsilon\alpha\beta<1
, then we also have

\ell_{\psi}-\varepsilon\alpha\ell_{\hat{\psi}_{0}}\leq\ell_{\psi}-\varepsilon\ell_{\psi_{0}}\leq\ell_{\varphi}\leq\ell_{\psi}+\varepsilon\ell_{\psi_{0}}\leq\ell_{\psi}+\varepsilon\alpha\ell_{\hat{\psi}_{0}}\leq\alpha(A+\varepsilon)\ell_{\hat{\psi}_{0}}.

In particular we get

|{\rm Dil}(\psi,\hat{\psi}_{0})-{\rm Dil}(\varphi,\hat{\psi}_{0})|\leq\varepsilon\alpha,

and hence
{\rm Dil}(\varphi,\hat{\psi}_{0})\geq{\rm Dil}(\psi,\hat{\psi}_{0})-\varepsilon\alpha\geq\beta^{-1}-\varepsilon\alpha
. Using these inequalities we obtain

\displaystyle|{\rm Dil}(\psi,\hat{\psi}_{0})^{-1}\ell_{\psi}-{\rm Dil}(\varphi,\hat{\psi}_{0})^{-1}\ell_{\varphi}|

\displaystyle\leq{\rm Dil}(\psi,\hat{\psi}_{0})^{-1}|\ell_{\psi}-\ell_{\varphi}|+\frac{|{\rm Dil}(\psi,\hat{\psi}_{0})-{\rm Dil}(\varphi,\hat{\psi}_{0})|}{{\rm Dil}(\psi,\hat{\psi}_{0}){\rm Dil}(\varphi,\hat{\psi}_{0})}\ell_{\varphi}

\displaystyle\leq{\rm Dil}(\psi,\hat{\psi}_{0})^{-1}\varepsilon\ell_{\psi_{0}}+\varepsilon\alpha({\rm Dil}(\psi,\hat{\psi}_{0}){\rm Dil}(\varphi,\hat{\psi}_{0}))^{-1}\ell_{\varphi}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\alpha\beta\ell_{\hat{\psi}_{0}}+\frac{\varepsilon\alpha\beta^{2}}{1-\varepsilon\alpha\beta}\cdot\alpha(A+\varepsilon)\ell_{\hat{\psi}_{0}}

\displaystyle=\left(\varepsilon\alpha\beta+\frac{\varepsilon\alpha^{2}\beta^{2}(A+\varepsilon)}{1-\varepsilon\alpha\beta}\right)\cdot\ell_{\hat{\psi}_{0}}.

In particular, if
\mathbf{y}
converges to
\mathbf{x}
with respect to the metric
D_{\psi_{0}}
(i.e.
\varepsilon\to 0
), then
D_{\hat{\psi}_{0}}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
tends to 0, as desired.
∎

The strong length topology recovers the metric topology induced by
\Delta
on
{H}_{\Gamma}^{++}
.

Lemma 3.10
.

On
{H}_{\Gamma}^{++}
, the topology induced by
D_{\psi_{0}}
coincides with that induced by
\Delta
.

Proof.

Let
\psi,\varphi\in\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{++}
with
{\rm Dil}(\psi,\psi_{0})={\rm Dil}(\varphi,\psi_{0})=1
. First we prove that if
[\varphi]\rightarrow[\psi]
for the metric
D_{\psi_{0}}
then
\Delta([\psi],[\varphi])
tends to 0. From the definition of
D_{\psi_{0}}
, we have that

\displaystyle\ell_{\varphi}\leq\ell_{\psi}+D_{\psi_{0}}([\psi],[\varphi])\ell_{\psi_{0}}\leq(1+D_{\psi_{0}}([\psi],[\varphi]){\rm Dil}(\psi_{0},\psi))\ell_{\psi},

and

\displaystyle\ell_{\varphi}\geq\ell_{\psi}-D_{\psi_{0}}([\psi],[\varphi])\ell_{\psi_{0}}\geq(1-D_{\psi_{0}}([\psi],[\varphi]){\rm Dil}(\psi_{0},\psi))\ell_{\psi}.

This implies the expected convergence if
D_{\psi_{0}}([\psi],[\varphi])
tends to 0.

On the other hand, we have
1={\rm Dil}(\psi,\psi_{0})\leq{\rm Dil}(\psi,\varphi){\rm Dil}(\varphi,\psi_{0})={\rm Dil}(\psi,\varphi)
, and similarly
1\leq{\rm Dil}(\varphi,\psi)
. This implies

\displaystyle\ell_{\varphi}-\ell_{\psi}

\displaystyle\leq({\rm Dil}(\varphi,\psi)-1)\ell_{\psi}\leq{\rm Dil}(\psi,\psi_{0})({\rm Dil}(\varphi,\psi)-1)\ell_{\psi_{0}}

and

\displaystyle\ell_{\varphi}-\ell_{\psi}

\displaystyle\geq-(1-{\rm Dil}(\psi,\varphi)^{-1})\ell_{\psi}\geq-{\rm Dil}(\psi,\psi_{0})(1-{\rm Dil}(\psi,\varphi)^{-1})\ell_{\psi_{0}}.

These two inequalities yield

D_{\psi_{0}}([\psi],[\varphi])\leq{\rm Dil}(\psi,\psi_{0})\cdot\max\left({\rm Dil}(\varphi,\psi)-1,1-{\rm Dil}(\psi,\varphi)^{-1}\right).

Therefore, if
[\varphi]
converges to
[\psi]
for
\Delta
then both
{\rm Dil}(\psi,\varphi)
and
{\rm Dil}(\varphi,\psi)
tend to 1, and hence
D_{\psi_{0}}([\psi],[\varphi])
tends to zero.
∎

Given
\mathbf{x}\in{H}^{++}_{\Gamma}
and
r>0
, we consider the sphere

S_{r}(\mathbf{x}):=\{\mathbf{y}\in{H}^{++}_{\Gamma}:\Delta(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=r\}.

If
\mathbf{y}\in S_{r}(\mathbf{x})
, we let
\pi_{\mathbf{x},r}(\mathbf{y})\in\partial{H}^{++}_{\Gamma}
be the limit of the unique Manhattan geodesic ray based at
\mathbf{x}
and containing
\mathbf{y}
, recall
Equation

2.7
. Then
\pi_{\mathbf{x},r}
is a bijection (cf.
[
21
, Corollary 5.4]
in the case of
{D}_{\Gamma}
).

Lemma 3.11
.

For any
\mathbf{x}\in{H}^{++}_{\Gamma}
and
r>0
, the bijection

\pi_{\mathbf{x},r}:S_{r}(\mathbf{x})\rightarrow\partial{H}^{++}_{\Gamma}

is a homeomorphism, when both
S_{r}(\mathbf{x})
and
\partial{H}^{++}_{\Gamma}
are equipped with the induced strong length topology.

Proof.

By
Lemma

3.10
we can use
D_{\mathbf{x}}
to compute the strongly length topology on
\overline{{H}^{++}_{\Gamma}}
. Given
\mathbf{y}\in{H}_{\Gamma}^{++}
, we use the notation
\psi_{\mathbf{y}}
for any representative of
\mathbf{y}
with exponential growth rate 1. Then we have
\mathbf{y}_{\infty}=\pi_{\mathbf{x},r}(\mathbf{y})=[\psi_{\mathbf{y}_{\infty}}]
for
\mathbf{y}\in S_{r}(\mathbf{x})
and

\psi_{\mathbf{y}_{\infty}}={\rm Dil}(\psi_{\mathbf{x}},\psi_{\mathbf{y}})\psi_{\mathbf{y}}-\psi_{\mathbf{x}},

see
Equation

2.7
. From this description, and using the notation
\ell_{\mathbf{z}}=\ell_{\psi_{\mathbf{z}}}
, we compute

{\rm Dil}(\mathbf{y}_{\infty},\mathbf{x})={\rm Dil}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}){\rm Dil}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{x})-1=e^{r}-1.

In particular, for
\mathbf{y},\mathbf{z}\in S_{r}(\mathbf{x})
we get

\displaystyle D_{\mathbf{x}}(\pi_{\mathbf{x},r}(\mathbf{y}),\pi_{\mathbf{x},r}(\mathbf{z}))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{e^{r}-1}\cdot\left\|\frac{\ell_{\mathbf{y}_{\infty}}-\ell_{{\mathbf{z}}_{\infty}}}{\ell_{\mathbf{x}}}\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{e^{r}-1}\cdot\left\|\frac{{\rm Dil}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\ell_{\mathbf{y}}-{\rm Dil}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{z})\ell_{\mathbf{z}}}{\ell_{\mathbf{x}}}\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{r}}{e^{r}-1}\cdot\left\|\frac{{\rm Dil}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{x})^{-1}\ell_{\mathbf{y}}-{\rm Dil}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{x})^{-1}\ell_{\mathbf{z}}}{\ell_{\mathbf{x}}}\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{r}}{e^{r}-1}\cdot D_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{z}).

Therefore the map
\pi_{\mathbf{x},r}
is a homothety with respect to the metric
D_{\mathbf{x}}
, and hence a homeomorphism.
∎

From the lemma above we get the immediate corollary below, which shows that
({H}_{\Gamma}^{++},\Delta)
is topologically homogeneous in a somewhat strong sense.

Corollary 3.12
.

All metric spheres in
({H}^{++}_{\Gamma},\Delta)
are homeomorphic.

Remark 3.13
.

(1)

The same discussion above holds if we replace
{H}^{++}_{\Gamma}
by
{D}_{\Gamma}
. In particular, all metric spheres in
({D}_{\Gamma},\Delta)
are homeomorphic.

(2)

The discussion above also holds for the completion
\MC({H}_{\Gamma}^{++})
.

3.3.
Non-local compactness

In this subsection we prove the following result, which asserts that the strong length topology on
{H}_{\Gamma}^{++}
is not locally compact. Indeed, it is already the case for
{D}_{\Gamma}
.

Theorem 3.14
.

({H}^{++}_{\Gamma},\Delta)
is not locally compact. Indeed, for any
\mathbf{x}_{0}\in{D}_{\Gamma}
and
r>0
the sphere
S_{r}(\mathbf{x}_{0})\cap{D}_{\Gamma}
is not compact. In particular,
({D}_{\Gamma},\Delta)
is not locally compact.

Let us fix
\mathbf{x}_{0}\in{H}^{++}_{\Gamma}
and
r>0
. Then
Theorem

3.14
will follow from the next proposition, which is also useful in
Section

7.2
.

Proposition 3.15
.

Let
(\mathbf{x}_{k})_{k}\in{H}^{++}_{\Gamma}
be a sequence such that
\Delta(\mathbf{x}_{k},\mathbf{x}_{0})\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
but
({\rm Dil}(\mathbf{x}_{k},\mathbf{x}_{0}))_{k}
is bounded. For
k
large enough let
\mathbf{y}_{k}
be the point in the Manhattan segment
[\mathbf{x}_{0},\mathbf{x}_{k}]
such that
{\rm Dil}(\mathbf{x}_{0},\mathbf{y}_{k})=r
. Then
(\mathbf{y}_{k})_{k}
has no convergent subsequence.

Proof of
Theorem

3.14
.

By
Corollary

3.12
it is enough to prove the result for
\mathbf{x}_{0}=[d_{S}]
for
d_{S}
a word metric with generating set
S
. Fix a loxodromic
g\in\Gamma
, and for
k>0
let
S_{k}:=S\cup\{g^{k},g^{-k}\}
and
\mathbf{x}_{k}=[d_{S_{k}}]
. Then
(\mathbf{x}_{k})_{k}
diverges in
({D}_{\Gamma},\Delta)
by the proof of
[
89
, Proposition 4.3]
. In addition, since
S\subset S_{k}
we have
d_{S_{k}}\leq d_{S}
for all
k
, and hence

\displaystyle{\rm Dil}(\mathbf{x}_{k},\mathbf{x}_{0})=\frac{v_{S_{k}}}{v_{S}}{\rm Dil}(d_{S_{k}},d_{S})\leq\frac{v_{S_{k}}}{v_{S}}\leq\frac{\log{(\#{S_k} -1 )}}{v_{S}}\leq\frac{\log{(\#{S} +1 )}}{v_{S}}

(here
v_{T}
denotes the exponential growth rate for the word metric
d_{T}
). Therefore the sequence
(\mathbf{x}_{k})_{k}
satisfies the assumptions of
Proposition

3.15
, so we can construct a sequence
(\mathbf{y}_{k})_{k}
in
S_{r}(\mathbf{x}_{0})\cap{D}_{\Gamma}
with no convergent subsequence.
∎

Proof of
Proposition

3.15
.

For the proof, we abuse notation and denote any representative of
\mathbf{z}\in{H}^{++}_{\Gamma}
with exponential growth rate 1 by
\mathbf{z}
. Let
\theta_{k}
be the Manhattan curve for
(\mathbf{x}_{0},\mathbf{y}_{k})
, so that for
k
large enough we have
\mathbf{x}_{k}=t_{k}\mathbf{y}_{k}+\theta_{k}(t_{k})\mathbf{x}_{0}
for some
t_{k}>1
.
We can compute

(3.2)

{\rm Dil}(\mathbf{x}_{k},\mathbf{x}_{0})=t_{k}{\rm Dil}(\mathbf{y}_{k},\mathbf{x}_{0})+\theta_{k}(t_{k})

and

(3.3)

{\rm Dil}(\mathbf{x}_{0},\mathbf{x}_{k})^{-1}=t_{k}{\rm Dil}(\mathbf{x}_{0},\mathbf{y}_{k})^{-1}+\theta_{k}(t_{k})

for all
k\geq 1
, see
[
21
, Proposition 4.8]
.

For the sake of contradiction, suppose (up to extracting a subsequence) that
\mathbf{y}_{k}
converges to some
\mathbf{y}
(and hence
\theta_{k}
converges to
\theta=\theta_{\mathbf{y}/\mathbf{x}_{0}}
by the proof of
[
89
, Theorem 1.9]
). We consider two cases:

Case 1:

(t_{k})_{k}
is bounded. Then, up to a subsequence, we assume that
t_{k}
converges to
t
. In addition, we have

\theta_{k}(t_{k})\to\theta(t),\quad{\rm Dil}(\mathbf{x}_{0},\mathbf{y}_{k})\to{\rm Dil}(\mathbf{x}_{0},\mathbf{y})\quad\text{ and }{\rm Dil}(\mathbf{y}_{k},\mathbf{x}_{0})\to{\rm Dil}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{x}_{0}).

Our assumptions imply
{\rm Dil}(\mathbf{x}_{0},\mathbf{x}_{k})\to\infty
, and hence by
Equation

3.3
we get

t{\rm Dil}(\mathbf{x}_{0},\mathbf{y})^{-1}+\theta(t)=0,

which is impossible since
t{\rm Dil}(\mathbf{x}_{0},\mathbf{y})^{-1}+\theta(t)>0
for
t\geq 1

[
21
, Remark 4.14]
.

Case 2:
up to a subsequence,
t_{k}
tends to infinity as
t\to\infty
. We know that

0<t_{k}{\rm Dil}(\mathbf{x}_{0},\mathbf{y}_{k})^{-1}+\theta_{k}(t_{k})\leq{\rm Dil}(\mathbf{x}_{0},\mathbf{y}_{k})^{-1}

for all
k
, and hence

0<{\rm Dil}(\mathbf{x}_{0},\mathbf{y}_{k})^{-1}+t_{k}^{-1}\theta_{k}(t_{k})\leq\frac{{\rm Dil}(\mathbf{x}_{0},\mathbf{y}_{k})^{-1}}{t_{k}}\to 0

as
k\to\infty
. From this we deduce that
t_{k}^{-1}\theta_{k}(t_{k})
tends to
-{\rm Dil}(\mathbf{x}_{0},\mathbf{y})^{-1}
as
k
diverges. In addition, by
Equation

3.2
we have

t_{k}^{-1}{\rm Dil}(\mathbf{x}_{k},\mathbf{x}_{0})={\rm Dil}(\mathbf{y}_{k},\mathbf{x}_{0})+t_{k}^{-1}\theta_{k}(t_{k})\to{\rm Dil}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{x}_{0})-{\rm Dil}(\mathbf{x}_{0},\mathbf{y})^{-1},

which is positive since
\Delta(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{x}_{0})=r>0
. On the other hand, if
({\rm Dil}(\mathbf{x}_{k},\mathbf{x}_{0}))_{k}
is bounded then
t_{k}^{-1}{\rm Dil}(\mathbf{x}_{k},\mathbf{x}_{0})
tends to 0, which is a contradiction.
∎

4.
Mineyev’s flow space

For this section we fix a non-elementary, torsion-free hyperbolic group
\Gamma
and a strongly hyperbolic metric
\hat{d}\in\mathcal{D}_{\Gamma}
. Our goal of is to describe the construction of Mineyev’s flow space
[
78
]
and establish some technical properties that will be needed in the sequel. In Mineyev’s original construction, the input metric
\hat{d}
is defined in terms of a geometric action of
\Gamma
on a graph. However, any strongly hyperbolic metric in
\mathcal{D}_{\Gamma}
suffices, see e.g.
[
94
, Section 3]
and
[
46
, Section 5]
.

As a continuous dynamical system, the flow space
\mathsf{U}\Gamma
goes back to Gromov’s seminal paper
[
55
]
(see also
[
77
,
28
]
) and is orbit equivalent to the geodesic flow on
\mathsf{T}^{1}M
when
M
is a closed negatively curved manifold and
\Gamma=\pi_{1}(M)
. For arbitrary
\Gamma
, Mineyev
[
78
]
was the first to introduce a metric on
\mathsf{U}\Gamma
(and an appropriate parameterization of the flow) resembling hyperbolic behavior (see e.g.
Theorem

4.1
).

4.1.
Construction of the flow

We consider the space
\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma:=\partial^{2}\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}
equipped with the product topology induced by the inclusion into
\partial\Gamma\times\partial\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}
. A
line
is a set of form
[a,b]:=\{(a,b)\}\times\mathbb{R}
for
a,b\in\partial^{2}\Gamma
. There are two natural actions on
\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma
:

(1)

The
\mathbb{R}
-action: for
t\in\mathbb{R}
we define
\widetilde{\mathsf{g}}_{t}:\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma
according to

\widetilde{\mathsf{g}}_{t}(a,b,s):=(a,b,s+t)\quad\text{ for }(a,b,s)\in\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma.

The action
\widetilde{\mathsf{g}}=(\widetilde{\mathsf{g}}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is the
geodesic flow
on
\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma
.

(2)

The
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-action: we define an involution
\widetilde{\iota}:\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma
according to

\widetilde{\iota}(a,b,s):=(b,a,-s)\quad\text{ for }(a,b,s)\in\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma.

Additionally, the metric
\hat{d}
allows us to define a third action:

(3)

The
\Gamma
-action: for
g\in\Gamma
and
(a,b,s)\in\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma
we define

g(a,b,s):=(ga,gb,s-\frac{1}{2}(\hat{\beta}(g^{-1},a)-\hat{\beta}(g^{-1},b))),

where
\hat{\beta}=\beta^{\hat{d}}_{o}
is the Busemann function associated to
\hat{d}
.

All these actions are continuous on
\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma
. Moreover, they satisfy the following properties
[
78
, Lemma 6]
:

(a)

The
\Gamma
and
\mathbb{R}
-actions commute.

(b)

The
\Gamma
and
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-actions commute.

(c)

The
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-action anti-commutes with the
\mathbb{R}
-action:

\widetilde{\iota}(\widetilde{\mathsf{g}_{t}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}))=\widetilde{\mathsf{g}}_{-t}(\widetilde{\iota}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}))
for
\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}\in\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

(d)

The three actions map lines onto lines.

(e)

The
\Gamma
-action is free, properly discontinuous, and cocompact.

Using the double difference
\langle\cdot,\cdot\mid\cdot,\cdot\rangle
in
(\Gamma,\hat{d})
, we define a
Busemann cocycle

\beta^{\times}:\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma\times\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma\times\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
according to

\beta^{\times}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}},\widetilde{\mathbf{w}};g):=\langle a\mid b\rangle_{g}+|s-\langle a,b\mid g,o\rangle|-\langle a^{\prime}\mid b^{\prime}\rangle_{g}-|s^{\prime}-\langle a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\mid g,o\rangle|

for
g\in\Gamma
and
\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}=(a,b,s),\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}=(a^{\prime},b^{\prime},s^{\prime})\in\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma
; see
[
78
, Definition 10 & Section 8.4]
. This cocycle satisfies the following properties
[
78
, Theorem 11]
:

(a)

For
\widetilde{\mathbf{v}},\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}\in\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma
, the map
g\mapsto\beta^{\times}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}},\widetilde{\mathbf{w}};g)
is Lipschitz on
(\Gamma,\hat{d})
.

(b)

\beta^{\times}(\cdot,\cdot;g)
satisfies the cocycle condition:

\beta^{\times}(\widetilde{\mathbf{u}},\widetilde{\mathbf{w}};g)=\beta^{\times}(\widetilde{\mathbf{u}},\widetilde{\mathbf{v}};g)+\beta^{\times}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}},\widetilde{\mathbf{w}};g)\quad\text{ for }g\in\Gamma\text{ and }\widetilde{\mathbf{u}},\widetilde{\mathbf{v}},\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}\in\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma.

(c)

\beta^{\times}(\cdot,\cdot;g)
is
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-invariant in each variable:

\beta^{\times}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}},\widetilde{\mathbf{w}};g)=\beta^{\times}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}},\widetilde{\iota}(\widetilde{\mathbf{w}});g)=\beta^{\times}(\widetilde{\iota}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}),\widetilde{\mathbf{w}};g)\quad\text{ for }\widetilde{\mathbf{v}},\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}\in\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma.

(d)

\beta^{\times}
is
\Gamma
-invariant:

\beta^{\times}(g\widetilde{\mathbf{v}},g\widetilde{\mathbf{w}};gh)=\beta^{\times}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}},\widetilde{\mathbf{w}};h)\quad\text{ for }g,h\in\Gamma\text{ and }\widetilde{\mathbf{v}},\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}\in\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma.

Indeed, the cocycle
\beta^{\times}
continuously extends to
\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma\times\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma\times\overline{\Gamma}
while still satisfying properties (a)-(d) above
[
78
, Theorem 55 (a’)]
. For example, if
a,b,b^{\prime}\in\partial\Gamma
are pairwise distinct and
s,s^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
, then

(4.1)

\beta^{\times}((a,b,s),(a,b^{\prime},s^{\prime});a)=s-s^{\prime}+\langle b,b^{\prime}\mid a,o\rangle.

Similarly, for
a,a^{\prime},b\in\partial\Gamma
pairwise distinct and
s,s^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\beta^{\times}((a,b,s),(a^{\prime},b,s^{\prime});b)=s^{\prime}-s+\langle a,a^{\prime}\mid b,o\rangle.

We use the cocycle
\beta^{\times}
to define metrics
d^{\times}
and
d_{\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma}
on
\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma
as follows. First, we define

d^{\times}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}},\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}):=\sup_{g\in\Gamma}|\beta^{\times}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}},\widetilde{\mathbf{w}};g)|.

This metric satisfies that

is a unit speed isometric embedding into
(\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma,d^{\times})
for each
\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}\in\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma
. Next, we fix a strong hyperbolicity parameter

for
\hat{d}
(see

) and a number

. Then we set

(4.2)

\qquad d_{\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}},\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}):=1/C_{\lambda^{\prime}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d^{\times}(\widetilde{\mathsf{g}}_{t}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}),\widetilde{\mathsf{g}_{t}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}))e^{-\lambda^{\prime}|t|}dt,

where

is chosen so that

is again a unit speed isometric embedding into
(\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma,d_{\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma})
. We call

the

rate for

.

The metric

satisfies the following properties.

Theorem 4.1

(
[

)
.

The pair

equipped with the geodesic flow

(5)

For any non-trivial

.

There exists

such that for any triplet

of pairwise distinct points and

we have

.

.

4.2.
Properties of
(\widetilde{\mathsf{U}\Gamma},\widetilde{\mathsf{g}},d_{\widetilde{\mathsf{U}\Gamma}})

In this subsection we record some further properties about the geometry of the space
(\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma,d_{\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma})
. These are well-known for the usual geodesic flow on the universal cover of a closed negatively curved manifold.

Lemma 4.4
.

There exists a compact set
K\subset\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma
such that for any
g\in\Gamma
we can find
\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}\in K
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
with
\widetilde{\mathsf{g}}_{t}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}})\in gK
.

Proof.

For the space
(\Gamma,\hat{d})
we use the following well-known fact: there exists
\alpha>0
such that
any two points
g,h\in\Gamma
belong to a bi-infinite
(\alpha,\hat{d})
-rough geodesic path. This is true for word metrics on
\Gamma
, and the result for an arbitrary
\hat{d}
follows from the Morse lemma.

Now, let
K_{0}\subset\partial^{2}\Gamma
be the set of pairs
(a,b)
such that
\langle a\mid b\rangle_{o}\leq 3\alpha/2
. We claim that for any
g\in\Gamma
the intersection
K_{0}\cap g^{-1}K_{0}
is non-empty. Indeed, let
a,b
be the endpoints of a bi-infinite
(\alpha,\hat{d})
-rough geodesic in
\Gamma
containing
o
and
g^{-1}
. Since
\hat{d}
is strongly hyperbolic, we have
\langle a\mid b\rangle_{o}\leq 3\alpha/2
and
\langle ga\mid gb\rangle_{o}=\langle a\mid b\rangle_{g^{-1}}\leq 3\alpha/2
, so that
(a,b)
and
(ga,gb)
belong to
K_{0}
.
To finish the proof, let
K=K_{0}\times\{0\}\subset\partial^{2}\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}=\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma
. Given
g\in\Gamma
, by our previous claim there exists
(a,b)\in K_{0}\cap g^{-1}K_{0}
. We define
\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}:=(ga,gb,0)\in K
so for
t=-\frac{1}{2}(\hat{\beta}(g^{-1},a)-\hat{\beta}(g^{-1},b))
we have

\widetilde{\mathsf{g}}_{t}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}})=(ga,gb,-\frac{1}{2}(\hat{\beta}(g^{-1},a)-\hat{\beta}(g^{-1},b)))=g\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}\in gK.\qed

Our next result states that if two flow-line segments are close at their endpoints, then they are exponentially close in their interiors.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\lambda^{\prime}
the expansion/contraction rate for
d_{\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma}
. Then for any
\varepsilon>0
there exist
\delta,T_{0}>0
such that if
\widetilde{\mathbf{v}},\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}\in\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma
and
T>T_{0}
satisfy

(4.6)

d_{\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}},\widetilde{\mathbf{w}})<\delta\quad\text{ and }\quad d_{\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma}(\widetilde{\mathsf{g}}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}),\widetilde{\mathsf{g}}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}))<\delta,

then there exists
t_{0}\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
such that

d_{\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma}(\widetilde{\mathsf{g}}_{t}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}),\widetilde{\mathsf{g}}_{t+t_{0}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}))<\varepsilon e^{-\lambda^{\prime}\min\{t,T-t\}}\quad\text{ for all }t_{0}\leq t\leq T-t_{0}.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\lambda^{\prime}
be the expansion/contraction constant for
d_{\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma}
. Then for any
T>0
there exists
\delta_{0}
satisfying the following. Let
a,b,c\in\partial\Gamma
be pairwise distinct, and let
\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}=p_{[b,c]}(a)
and
\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}=p_{[a,c]}(b)
. Then for any
t\leq T
we have
d_{\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma}(\widetilde{\mathsf{g}}_{t}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}),\widetilde{\mathsf{g}}_{t}(\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}))\geq\delta_{0}
.

Proof.

Suppose the result is not true and let
(a_{k})_{k},(b_{k})_{k},(c_{k})_{k}
be sequences of pairwise distinct points in
\partial\Gamma
, and a sequence of real numbers
(t_{k})_{k}
such that
t_{k}\leq T
for all
k
, but

d_{\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma}(\widetilde{\mathsf{g}}_{t_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}_{k}),\widetilde{\mathsf{g}}_{t_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}_{k}))\to 0
as
k\to\infty
for
\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}_{k}=p_{[b_{k},c_{k}]}(a_{k})
and
\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}_{k}=p_{[a_{k},c_{k}]}(b_{k})
.

Since
\Gamma
acts cocompactly on the space of distinct triples of
\partial\Gamma
, after translating elements of this sequence by elements of
\Gamma
and taking a subsequence, we can assume that
a_{k}\to a
,
b_{k}\rightarrow b
and
c_{k}\rightarrow c
for some pairwise distinct points
a,b,c\in\partial\Gamma
. Therefore, by the definition of the projection (Eq. (
4.3
)) and the continuity of
\langle\cdot,\cdot\mid\cdot,\cdot\rangle
we have that
\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}_{k}\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}=p_{[b,c]}(a)
and
\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}_{k}\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}=p_{[a,c]}(b)
.

If the sequence
(t_{k})
is bounded, using
Theorem

4.1
(1) and (3), up to taking a subsequence we can assume that
t_{k}\to t\in(-\infty,T]
and that
\widetilde{\mathsf{g}}_{t}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}})=\widetilde{\mathsf{g}}_{t}(\widetilde{\mathbf{w}})
. This is a contradiction since
a\neq b
.

On the other hand, if up to a subsequence we have
t_{k}\to-\infty
, then
\widetilde{\mathsf{g}}_{t_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}_{k})\to b
and
\widetilde{\mathsf{g}}_{t_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}_{k})\to a
, again a contradiction.
∎

Given
\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}=(a,b,s)\in\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma
, its
stable horosphere
is defined as the set

H^{s}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}})=\{\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}^{\prime}=(a^{\prime},b,s-\langle a,a^{\prime}\mid b,x_{0}\rangle)|a^{\prime}\in\partial\backslash\{b\}\}.

Similarly, its
unstable horosphere
is

H^{u}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}})=\{\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}^{\prime}=(a,b^{\prime},s+\langle b,b^{\prime}\mid a,x_{0}\rangle)|b^{\prime}\in\partial X\backslash\{a\}\}.

A
stable
(resp.

)

is a set of form
H^{s}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}})
(resp.
H^{u}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}})
) for some
\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}\in\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma
.

From
Lemma

4.6
we immediately deduce the following.

Corollary 4.7
.

For any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta_{0}>0
satisfying the following. Let
\widetilde{\mathbf{v}},\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}\in\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma
be points on the same stable (resp. unstable) horosphere. If
d_{\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}},\widetilde{\mathbf{w}})<\delta_{0}

(resp.

).

Proof.

Let
M
be the constant from
Theorem

4.1
(6) and

such that

. Then the lemma for the stable case follows by choosing
\delta_{0}
as in
Lemma

4.6
for

4.1
(6) (see
Equation

4.4
and
Equation

∎

Proposition

4.3.
Invariant measures and currents

We continue with the notation from the previous subsection and denote the space of real-valued continuous functions on
\mathsf{U}\Gamma
by
C(\mathsf{U}\Gamma)
. Sometimes, we will refer to functions in
C(\mathsf{U}\Gamma)
as
potentials
.

A Borel measure
\mathfrak{m}
on
\mathsf{U}\Gamma
is
\mathsf{g}
-
invariant
if
(\mathsf{g}_{t})_{\ast}\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{m}
for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Similarly, we can talk of
\widetilde{\mathsf{g}}
-invariant measures on
\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma
. We denote by
\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{g}}(\mathsf{U}\Gamma)
the space of
\mathsf{g}
-invariant Borel probability measures on
\mathsf{U}\Gamma
equipped with the weak
∗
topology. Note that
\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{g}}(\mathsf{U}\Gamma)
is compact and metrizable.

Invariant measures supported on periodic orbits correspond to conjugacy classes in
\Gamma
. Namely, for a non-trivial conjugacy class
[g]\in[\Gamma]^{\prime}
there is a corresponding periodic orbit
\gamma_{[g]}
on
\mathsf{U}\Gamma
, given by the image of the curve
t\mapsto(g^{-},g^{+},t)
in
\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma
. By
Theorem

4.1
(5), the length of the periodic orbit
\gamma_{[g]}
is equal to
\ell_{\hat{d}}[g]
. Then
[g]
determines a
\mathsf{g}
-invariant Borel probability measure
\mathfrak{m}_{[g]}
on
\mathsf{U}\Gamma
given by the normalized integral along
\gamma_{[g]}
:

(4.10)

\int Fd\mathfrak{m}_{[g]}=\frac{1}{\ell_{\hat{d}}[g]}\int_{\gamma_{[g]}}{F}:=\frac{1}{\ell_{\hat{d}}[g]}\int_{0}^{\ell_{\hat{d}}[g]}F(\mathsf{g}_{t}(\mathbf{v}))dt\quad\text{ for all }F\in C(\mathsf{U}\Gamma),

where
\mathbf{v}
is any point on
\gamma_{[g]}
. Invariant measures supported on periodic orbits are dense in
\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{g}}(\mathsf{U}\Gamma)
. This follows, for instance, from
Lemma

4.10
below and the density of rational currents on
\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}

[
10
]
.

The correspondence above extends to a natural bijection between geodesic currents and Borel
\mathsf{g}
-measures on
\mathsf{U}\Gamma

[
94
, Section 3.2]
. Given a geodesic current
\Lambda\in\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}
, let
\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}_{\Lambda}:=\Lambda\otimes dt
be the product measure on
\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma=\partial^{2}\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}
, where

is the (normalized) Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}
. Then
\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}_{\Lambda}
is a Radon
\widetilde{\mathsf{g}}
-invariant measure. Moreover,
\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}_{\Lambda}
is also invariant under the
\Gamma

\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma
, hence it descends to a
\mathsf{g}
-invariant measure
\mathfrak{m}_{\Lambda}
on
\mathsf{U}\Gamma
. We have the following:

Lemma 4.9

(
[
94
, Lemma 3.4]
)
.

The measure
\mathfrak{m}_{\Lambda}
is the unique
\mathsf{g}
-invariant Borel measure on
\mathsf{U}\Gamma
such that if
\widehat{F}:\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a compactly supported continuous function and

(4.11)

F(\mathbf{v}):=\sum_{\pi(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}})=\mathbf{v}}{\widehat{F}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}})}\quad\text{ for }\mathbf{v}\in\mathsf{U}\Gamma,

then

(4.12)

\int_{\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma}{\widehat{F}}{d\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}_{\Lambda}}=\int_{\mathsf{U}\Gamma}{F}{d\mathfrak{m}_{\Lambda}}.

Since for any
F\in C(\mathsf{U}\Gamma)
there exists a compactly supported continuous function
\widehat{F}:\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfying
Equation

4.11
, the identity from
Equation

4.12
gives us that the convergence
\Lambda_{k}\xrightharpoonup{\ast}\Lambda

in
\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}
implies the weak
∗
convergence
\mathfrak{m}_{\Lambda_{k}}\xrightharpoonup{\ast}\mathfrak{m}_{\Lambda}
in the space of
\mathsf{g}
-invariant measures on
\mathsf{U}\Gamma
.

Given a projective geodesic current
[\Lambda]\in\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}
, we let
\mathfrak{m}_{[\Lambda]}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{g}}(\mathsf{U}\Gamma)
be the unique probability measure in the projective class of the measure
\mathfrak{m}_{\Lambda}
. It is not hard to see that for the rational current
\eta_{[g]}
associated to
[g]\in[\Gamma]^{\prime}
, we have that
\mathfrak{m}_{[\eta_{[g]}]}
is equal to the measure
\mathfrak{m}_{[g]}
from
Equation

4.10
.

Lemma 4.10
.

The map
\mathsf{m}:\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{g}}(\mathsf{U}\Gamma)
that maps
[\Lambda]
to
\mathfrak{m}_{[\Lambda]}
is a homeomorphism.

Proof.

From our discussion above, it follows that
\mathsf{m}
is continuous. Since both
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}
and
\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{g}}(\mathsf{U}\Gamma)
are compact and metrizable, it is enough to prove that

is a bijection.

To produce an inverse of
\mathsf{m}
, we follow the construction by Kaimanovich
[
63
, Thm. 2.2]
. Let
\mathfrak{m}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{g}}(\mathsf{U}\Gamma)
, and lift it to the unique
\widetilde{\mathsf{g}}
-invariant Radon measure
\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}
on
\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma
satisfying

4.12
. If
p:\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma=\partial^{2}\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\partial^{2}\Gamma
denotes the projection map, then there exists a unique probability measure
\Lambda
on
\partial^{2}\Gamma
such that for every Borel subset

we have

\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathcal{A})=\int_{p(\mathcal{A})}{\mathrm{Leb}_{(a,b)}(p^{-1}((a,b)))}{d\Lambda(a,b)},

where

is the normalized Lebesgue measure along the flow line

\Lambda
is clearly Radon, and
\Gamma

implies that

-invariant. Hence

[

, Theorem 2.2]
, it can be checked that

. We leave the details to the reader.
∎

4.4.
Metric-Anosov property and Ledrappier correspondence

A crucial property that we will use is that Mineyev’s flow
(\mathsf{U}\Gamma,\mathsf{g},d_{\mathsf{U}\Gamma})
is
metric-Anosov
—a broad extension of the notion of Anosov flow—, as was proven recently in Disalvor’s thesis
[
46
]
.

For
\CAT(-1)
groups, this was first studied in
[
33
,
34
]
(see also
[
45
]
for non-necessarily convex cocompact
\CAT(-1)
groups). In particular,
[
33
]
proves that Mineyev’s flow for
\CAT(-1)
groups is metric-Anosov, which was also proven for groups admitting Anosov representations in
[
16
]
(up to Hölder orbit equivalence). A strong coding for Mineyev’s flow space was proven in
[
23
]
without relying on the metric-Anosov property (see also
[
94
,
24
]
).
In Proposition
8.6
we show that Mineyev’s flow space for any non-elementary torsion-free hyperbolic group satisfies the specification property.

Theorem 4.11

(
[
46
, Theorem D]
)
.

For any strongly hyperbolic metric
\hat{d}\in\mathcal{D}_{\Gamma}
, then Mineyev’s flow space
\mathsf{U}\Gamma=(\mathsf{U}\Gamma,\mathsf{g},d_{\mathsf{U}\Gamma})
is metric-Anosov.

The metric-Anosov property will not be used directly, so we refer the reader to
[
85
]
,
[
92
, Section 2]
,
[
33
, Section 3]
. However, the metric-Anosov property has some remarkable consequences. For instance, it will allow us to establish versions of Ledrappier correspondence between potentials on
\mathsf{U}\Gamma
and cocycles on
\Gamma

[
72
, Théorème 3]
, as was proven by Sambarino
[
92
]
.

On the side of
\Gamma
, a
cocycle
on
\Gamma
is a continuous map
\mathsf{c}:\Gamma\times\partial\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

\mathsf{c}(gh,a)=\mathsf{c}(g,ha)+\mathsf{c}(h,a)\quad\text{ for }g,h\in\Gamma,a\in\partial\Gamma.

Such a cocycle is
Hölder
if each function
a\mapsto\mathsf{c}(g,a)
is Hölder with the same exponent for all
g\in\Gamma
, when
\partial\Gamma
is equipped with some (hence, any) visual metric.

For a cocycle
\mathsf{c}
, its
period map
is the function
\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{c}}:[\Gamma]^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{c}}[g]=\mathsf{c}(g,g^{+})\quad\text{ for }[g]\in[\Gamma]^{\prime}.

We say that
\mathsf{c}
is
properly positive
if there exists
d\in\mathcal{D}_{\Gamma}
such that
\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{c}}\geq\ell_{d}
.

Example 4.12
.

If
d^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}_{\Gamma}
is any strongly hyperbolic pseudometric and
\beta^{\prime}=\beta^{d^{\prime}}_{o}:\Gamma\times\partial\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the Busemann function, then
\mathsf{c}=\mathsf{c}_{d^{\prime}}:\Gamma\times\partial\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
given by

\mathsf{c}(g,a)=\beta^{\prime}(g^{-1},a)

is a Hölder cocycle satisfying
\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{c}}[g]=\ell_{d^{\prime}}[g]
for all
[g]\in[\Gamma]^{\prime}
. Hence
\mathsf{c}
is properly positive.

Proof.

Let
\langle\cdot\mid\cdot\rangle
denote the Gromov product on
(\Gamma,d^{\prime})
based at the identity
o
, and let
\varepsilon>0
be a strong hyperbolicity parameter for
d^{\prime}
. Then
\rho(a,b)=e^{-\varepsilon\langle a\mid b\rangle}
defines a visual metric on
\partial\Gamma
. By
Equation

2.1
and the mean value theorem, for all
g\in\Gamma
and
a,b\in\partial\Gamma
we have

\displaystyle|\mathsf{c}(g,a)-\mathsf{c}(g,b)|

\displaystyle=|\beta^{\prime}(g^{-1},a)-\beta^{\prime}(g^{-1},b)|

\displaystyle=2|\langle g^{-1}\mid a\rangle-\langle g^{-1}\mid b\rangle|

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\varepsilon}|\log(e^{-\ep\gpr{g^{-1}}{a}})-\log(e^{-\ep\gpr{g^{-1}}{b}})|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\varepsilon}\max\{e^{\varepsilon\langle g^{-1}\mid a\rangle},e^{\varepsilon\langle g^{-1}\mid b\rangle}\}\cdot|e^{-\varepsilon\langle g^{-1}\mid a\rangle}-e^{-\varepsilon\langle g^{-1}\mid b\rangle}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\varepsilon}e^{\varepsilon d^{\prime}(g^{-1},o)}e^{-\varepsilon\langle a\mid b\rangle}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\varepsilon}e^{\varepsilon d^{\prime}(o,g)}\rho(a,b).

Hence, with respect to
\rho
, the assignment
a\mapsto\mathsf{c}(g,a)
is Lipschitz for any
g\in\Gamma
. This proves the Hölder assertion about
\mathsf{c}
. The identity
\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{c}}=\ell_{d^{\prime}}
follows from standard arguments using the cocycle property of
\mathsf{c}
, see e.g. the proof of
[
78
, Theorem 60 (i)]
.
∎

Two cocycles
\mathsf{c},\mathsf{c}^{\prime}
on
\Gamma
are
cohomologous
if there exists a continuous function
\alpha:\partial\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

\mathsf{c}(g,a)-\mathsf{c}^{\prime}(g,a)=\alpha(a)-\alpha(ga)\quad\text{ for all }g\in\Gamma,a\in\partial\Gamma.

On the side of
\mathsf{U}\Gamma
, we say that two potentials
F,F^{\prime}\in C(\mathsf{U}\Gamma)
are
cohomologous
if there exists a continuous potential
U:\mathsf{U}\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
that is
C^{1}
in the direction of the flow (that is,
t\mapsto U(\mathsf{g}_{t}(\mathbf{v}))
is
C^{1}
for any
\mathbf{v}\in\mathsf{U}\Gamma
), and such that

F(\mathbf{v})-F^{\prime}(\mathbf{v})=\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right|_{t=0}U(\mathsf{g}_{t}(\mathbf{v}))\quad\text{ for }\mathbf{v}\in\mathsf{U}\Gamma.

It follows that if
F
and
F^{\prime}
are cohomologous then they are
weakly cohomologous
, in the sense that

\int{F}d\mathfrak{m}=\int{F^{\prime}}d\mathfrak{m}\quad\text{ for any }\mathfrak{m}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{g}}(\mathsf{U}\Gamma).

On the other hand, and using that the flow
(\mathsf{U}\Gamma,\mathsf{g})
is transitive (see e.g.
[
16
, Proposition 5.1]
) and metric-Anosov, we have that these two notions coincide among Hölder continuous potentials. Using the closing lemma (available in this setting by the metric-Anosov property) and
Proposition

4.5
, one can prove
Livšic’s theorem
in this setting
[
74
]
.

Theorem 4.13

(Sambarino,
[
92
, Theorem 2.2.2]
)
.

If
F,F^{\prime}:\mathsf{U}\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
are Hölder continuous, then they are cohomologous if and only if they are weakly cohomologous.

Using the theorem above, Sambarino proved in
[
92
]
the following version of Ledrappier correspondence for metric-Anosov Mineyev’s flow spaces. Combining this result with
Theorem

4.11
, we have the following.

Proposition 4.14

(Sambarino,
[
92
, Proposition 3.1.1]
)
.

If
\mathsf{c}:\Gamma\times\partial\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a Hölder cocycle then there exists a Hölder continuous function
F=F_{\mathsf{c}}:\mathsf{U}\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{c}}[g]=\int_{\gamma_{[g]}}F\quad\text{ for }[g]\in[\Gamma]^{\prime}.

Moreover, if
\mathsf{c}
and
\mathsf{c}^{\prime}
are cohomologous, then
F_{\mathsf{c}}
and
F_{\mathsf{c}^{\prime}}
are cohomologous.

.
Our goal is to prove

, which implies

We establish a dictionary between the (appropriate equivalence classes) of the following objects:

(1)

Properly positive cocycles

(
Section

4.4
).

(2)

.

(3)

5.1.
The space of reparameterizations and dictionary
\Par(\mathsf{U}\Gamma)\Rightarrow C(\mathsf{U}\Gamma)

In this section we define the space of reparameterizations of the geodesic flow.

Definition 5.1

(Flow reparameterizations)
.

A
reparameterization
of Mineyev’s flow space
(\mathsf{U}\Gamma,\mathsf{g})
is a continuous flow
\mathsf{\Psi}=(\mathsf{\Psi}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
on
\mathsf{U}\Gamma
whose flow lines coincide with the flow lines for
\mathsf{g}
and respect the orientation. The reparameterization
\mathsf{\Psi}
is
Hölder
if the map
\mathsf{U}\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathsf{U}\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}
that maps
(\mathbf{v},t)
to
(\mathsf{\Psi}_{t}(\mathbf{v}),t)
is bi-Hölder for
\mathsf{U}\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}
equipped with the metric
d_{\mathsf{U}\Gamma}+d_{\mathbb{R}}
for
d_{\mathbb{R}}
the usual distance on
\mathbb{R}
. We denote the pair
(\mathsf{U}\Gamma,\mathsf{\Psi})
by
\mathsf{U}\Gamma^{\mathsf{\Psi}}
.

If
\mathsf{U}\Gamma^{\mathsf{\Psi}}
is a reparameterization and
[g]\in[\Gamma]^{\prime}
, then
\gamma_{[g]}
is also a periodic orbit for
\mathsf{U}\Gamma^{\mathsf{\Psi}}
. We denote its
period
by
\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{\Psi}}[g]
. Equivalently, if
[g]
is primitive then
\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{\Psi}}[g]
is the minimal positive number such that
\mathsf{\Psi}_{\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{\Psi}}[g]}(\mathbf{v})=\mathbf{v}
for any
\mathbf{v}\in\gamma_{[g]}
.

For any reparameterization
\mathsf{U}\Gamma^{\mathsf{\Psi}}
there exists a continuous function
\kappa=\kappa_{\mathsf{\Psi}}:\mathsf{U}\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}
satisfying

\mathsf{g}_{t}(\mathbf{v})=\mathsf{\Psi}_{\kappa(\mathbf{v},t)}(\mathbf{v})\quad\text{ for }\mathbf{v}\in\mathsf{U}\Gamma,t\in\mathbb{R}.

Such a function
\kappa
is a
translation cocycle
as it satisfies

\kappa(\mathbf{v},s+t)=\kappa(\mathsf{g}_{t}(\mathbf{v}),s)+\kappa(\mathbf{v},t)\quad\text{ for }\mathbf{v}\in\mathsf{U}\Gamma\text{ and }s,t\in\mathbb{R}.

Moreover, if
\mathsf{\Psi}
is Hölder, then
\mathbf{v}\mapsto\kappa(\mathbf{v},t)
is Hölder with exponent independent of
t
and multiplicative constant that is bounded for
t
in a bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}
. We call
\kappa
the
translation cocycle
of
\mathsf{U}\Gamma^{\mathsf{\Psi}}
. Using the translation cocycle and standard arguments relying on Livšic’s
Theorem

4.13
, any reparameterization determines a potential in
C(\mathsf{U}\Gamma).

Proposition 5.2

(cf.
[
96
, Proposition 1.11]
,
[
92
, Corollary 2.2.3]
)
.

If
\mathsf{U}\Gamma^{\mathsf{\Psi}}
is a continuous reparameterization of
\mathsf{U}\Gamma
, then there exists a positive continuous function
F=F_{\mathsf{\Psi}}:\mathsf{U}\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

\int_{\gamma_{[g]}}{F}=\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{\Psi}}[g]\quad\text{ for any primitive }[g]\in[\Gamma]^{\prime}.

Moreover, if
\mathsf{\Psi}
is Hölder then we can choose
F
to be Hölder.

Two reparameterizations
\mathsf{U}\Gamma^{\mathsf{\Psi}}
and
\mathsf{U}\Gamma^{\mathsf{\Psi}^{\prime}}
are
conjugate
if there exists a homeomorphism
\Phi:\mathsf{U}\Gamma^{\mathsf{\Psi}}\rightarrow\mathsf{U}\Gamma^{\mathsf{\Psi}^{\prime}}
such that
\mathsf{\Psi}\circ\mathsf{\Psi}_{t}=\mathsf{\Psi}^{\prime}_{t}\circ\Phi
for all
t
. Such a conjugating homeomorphism must be of the form
\mathbf{v}\mapsto\Phi(\mathbf{v})=\Phi_{F}(\mathbf{v})=\mathsf{\Psi}_{F(\mathbf{v})}(\mathbf{v})
for some
F>0
in
C(\mathsf{U}\Gamma)
, and in that case
\mathsf{\Psi}
and
\mathsf{\Psi}^{\prime}
are related by the identity

\mathsf{\Psi}_{t}(\mathbf{v})=\mathsf{\Psi}^{\prime}_{t+F(\mathbf{v})-F(\mathsf{\Psi}_{t}(\mathbf{v}))}(\mathbf{v}).

We say that
\mathsf{\Psi}
and
\mathsf{\Psi}^{\prime}
are
weakly conjugate
if
\mathsf{\Psi}^{\prime}
is the uniform limit of reparameterizations conjugate to
\mathsf{\Psi}
. That is, there exists a sequence
(\mathsf{\Psi}_{m})_{m}
of flow reparameterizations conjugate to
\mathsf{\Psi}
such that the maps
(\mathbf{v},t)\mapsto(\mathsf{\Psi}_{m})_{t}(\mathbf{v})
converge to
(\mathbf{v},t)\mapsto\mathsf{\Psi}^{\prime}_{t}(\mathbf{v})
uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathsf{U}\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}
. It turns out that the correspondence from
Proposition

5.2
maps weak conjugacy classes of reparameterizations to weak conjugacy classes of potentials.

Given a reparameterization
\mathsf{U}\Gamma^{\mathsf{\Psi}}
, its
rescaling
by
\lambda>0
is the reparameterization
\mathsf{U}\Gamma^{\lambda\mathsf{\Psi}}
such that
\lambda\mathsf{\Psi}_{t}(\mathbf{v})=\mathsf{\Psi}_{\lambda t}(\mathbf{v})
for all
\mathbf{v}\in\mathsf{U}\Gamma
. Two reparameterizations are (
weakly
)
equivalent
if they have (
weak
) conjugate rescalings.

A reparameterization
\mathsf{U}\Gamma^{\mathsf{\Psi}}
is
reversible
if it anti-commutes with the involution
\iota
on
\mathsf{U}\Gamma
:
\mathsf{\Psi}_{t}\circ\iota=\iota\circ\mathsf{\Psi}_{-t}
for all
t
.

Definition 5.3
.

We define
\Par(\mathsf{U}\Gamma)=\Par(\mathsf{U}\Gamma,\mathsf{g})
as the set of continuous reparameterizations of
(\mathsf{U}\Gamma,\mathsf{g})
, modulo weak equivalence. This is the
space of reparameterizations
of Mineyev’s flow space
\mathsf{U}\Gamma
. The equivalence class of
\mathsf{U}\Gamma^{\mathsf{\Psi}}
in
\Par(\mathsf{U}\Gamma)
is denoted by
[\mathsf{\Psi}]
.

We let
\Par^{s}(\mathsf{U}\Gamma)
be the subspace of
\Par(\mathsf{U}\Gamma)
of points represented by reversible reparameterizations, and let
\Par^{h}(\mathsf{U}\Gamma)\subset\Par(\mathsf{U}\Gamma)
be the subspace of points represented by Hölder reparameterizations.

It turns out that points in
\Par(\mathsf{U}\Gamma)
are completely determined by the homothety classes of their periods. The next lemma follows from the fact that two reparameterizations are conjugate if and only if their corresponding translation cocycles are cohomologous
[
96
, Proposition 1.17]
.

Lemma 5.4

(cf.
[
96
, Corollary 1.19]
)
.

Two reparameterizations
\mathsf{U}\Gamma^{\mathsf{\Psi}}
and
\mathsf{U}\Gamma^{\mathsf{\Psi}^{\prime}}
are weakly conjugate if and only if
\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{\Psi}}[g]=\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{\Psi}^{\prime}}[g]
for all primitive
[g]\in[\Gamma]^{\prime}
. ∎

The following is one of the main results of this paper.

Theorem 5.5

(Dictionary metrics-reparameterizations)

Let
\Gamma
be the a non-elementary torsion-free hyperbolic group and consider a Mineyev’s flow space

Definition 5.6
.

Let

\widetilde{\mathsf{g}}=(\widetilde{\mathsf{g}}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
denote the flows on
\mathsf{U}\Gamma
and
\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma
respectively. We also let
d_{\mathsf{U}\Gamma}
and
d_{\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma}
denote the corresponding metrics on
\mathsf{U}\Gamma
and
\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma
.

The core of the proof is to describe
\ell_{\varphi}
as a drift function for an almost cocycle defined on
\mathsf{U}\Gamma
. This is inspired by the construction of the continuous extension of
\ell_{d}
to
\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}
when
d
is a word metric by Erlandsson–Parlier–Souto
[
48
, Theorem 1.5]
. Incidentally, this reproves the fact that
\ell_{\varphi}
admits a (unique) continuous extension to
\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}
, recovering the result of Kapovich and Martinez-Granado
[
65
]
.

To begin the description of
\ell_{\varphi}
, we consider a precompact Borel fundamental domain
\mathcal{B}\subset\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma
for the action of
\Gamma
, and for
\mathbf{v}\in\mathsf{U}\Gamma
we let
\widehat{\mathbf{v}}
denote its unique lift in
\mathcal{B}
. For each
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbf{v}\in\mathsf{U}\Gamma
, we let
g(\mathbf{v},t)=g_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbf{v},t)\in\Gamma
be the unique group element such that
\widetilde{\mathsf{g}}_{t}(\widehat{\mathbf{v}})\in g_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbf{v},t)\mathcal{B}
. It follows from its definition that
g_{\mathcal{B}}
satisfies the cocycle relation

(6.1)

g_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbf{v},t+s)=g_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbf{v},t)g_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathsf{g}_{t}(\mathbf{v}),s)\quad\text{ for }\mathbf{v}\in\mathsf{U}\Gamma\text{ and }s,t>0.

Definition 6.2
.

We define
\Psi=\Psi_{\mathcal{B}}:\mathsf{U}\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
according to
\Psi(\mathbf{v},t)=\varphi(o,g_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbf{v},t))
.

Since
\mathcal{B}
is Borel,
\Psi
is a Borel function, and bounded backtracking of
\varphi
implies that
\Psi
is an almost cocycle:

Lemma 6.3
.

There exists
C>0
depending only on
\mathcal{B}
such that for
\Psi=\Psi_{\mathcal{B}}
we have

|\Psi(\mathbf{v},t+s)-\Psi(\mathbf{v},t)-\Psi(\mathsf{g}_{t}(\mathbf{v}),s)|\leq C\quad\text{ for }\mathbf{v}\in\mathsf{U}\Gamma\text{ and }s,t>0.

Proof.

Let
C_{1}>0
be the diameter of
\mathcal{B}
in the metric
d_{\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma}
. Fixed a point
\mathbf{p}\in\mathsf{U}\Gamma
, the metric
d^{\widehat{\mathbf{p}}}_{\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma}
on
\Gamma
given by
(g,h)\mapsto d_{\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma}(g\widehat{\mathbf{p}},h\widehat{\mathbf{p}})
belongs to
\mathcal{D}_{\Gamma}
by
Theorem

4.1
(3). Also, let
R>0
be such that

(6.2)

|\langle g\mid h\rangle^{\varphi}_{o}|\leq R\langle g\mid h\rangle_{o}^{d_{\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma}^{\widehat{\mathbf{p}}}}+R

for all
g,h\in\Gamma
.

Now let
\mathbf{v}\in\mathsf{U}\Gamma
, and for
s,t>0
note that the distances

d_{\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma}(\widehat{\mathbf{p}},\widehat{\mathbf{v}}),\qquad d_{\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma}(g_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbf{v},t)\widehat{\mathbf{p}},\widetilde{\mathsf{g}}_{t}(\widehat{\mathbf{v}})),\qquad d_{\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma}(g_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbf{v},t+s)\widehat{\mathbf{p}},\widetilde{\mathsf{g}}_{t+s}(\widehat{\mathbf{v}}))

are bounded above by
C_{1}
. Since
r\mapsto\widetilde{\mathsf{g}}_{r}(\widehat{\mathbf{v}})
is a geodesic in
(\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma,d_{\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma})
by
Theorem

4.1
(3),
Equation

6.2
implies that

|\varphi(o,g_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbf{v},t))+\varphi(g_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbf{v},t),g_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbf{v},t+s))-\varphi(o,g_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbf{v},t+s))|\leq C

for
C=3C_{1}R+2R
. The conclusion then follows from the cocycle relation in
Equation

6.1
and the definition of
\Psi
.
∎

Definition 6.4
.

Given a measure
\mathfrak{m}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{g}}(\mathsf{U}\Gamma)
, we define

L_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathfrak{m})=L_{\varphi,\mathcal{B}}(\mathfrak{m}):=\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int{\Psi_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbf{v},t)}{d\mathfrak{m}(\mathbf{v})}.

The limit above exists by
Lemma

6.3
and Kingman’s subadditive ergodic theorem. In fact, from the almost cocycle relation we have the following stronger property. If
\mathfrak{m}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{g}}(\mathsf{U}\Gamma)
, then
\mathsf{g}
-invariance implies that

\left|\int{\Psi_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbf{v},s+t)}{d\mathfrak{m}}-\int{\Psi_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbf{v},t)}{d\mathfrak{m}}-\int{\Psi_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbf{v},s)}{d\mathfrak{m}}\right|\leq C\mathfrak{m}(\mathsf{U}\Gamma)

for all
s,t>0
,
where
C>0
is the constant from
Lemma

6.3
. In particular, Fekete’s lemma implies that

(6.3)

\left|L_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathfrak{m})-\frac{1}{n}\int\Psi_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbf{v},n)d\mathfrak{m}\right|\leq\frac{C\mathfrak{m}(\mathsf{U}\Gamma)}{n}

for all natural numbers
n\geq 1
.

In addition, the quantity
L_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathfrak{m})
is independent of the precompact Borel fundamental domain
\mathcal{B}
. To show this, let
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
be another precompact Borel fundamental domains, so that there exists a finite set
A\subset\Gamma
such that
\mathcal{B}\subset A\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\subset A\mathcal{B}
. Then for all
\mathbf{v}\in\mathsf{U}\Gamma
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
we have
g_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{v},t)=ag_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbf{v},t)a^{\prime}
for
a,a^{\prime}\in A
(depending on
t
and
\mathbf{v}
). From
[
20
, Lemma 3.3(2)]
, this implies that

|\Psi_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{v},t)-\Psi_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbf{v},t)|\leq C^{\prime}
, for
C^{\prime}
independent of
\mathbf{v}
and
t
, which implies that
L_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{m})=L_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathfrak{m})
for all
\mathfrak{m}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{g}}(\mathsf{U}\Gamma)
.

Definition 6.5
.

Let
L=L_{\varphi}:\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{g}}(\mathsf{U}\Gamma)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be the function such that
L(\mathfrak{m})=L_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathfrak{m})
for any precompact Borel fundamental domain
\mathcal{B}\subset\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma
.

Our next goal is to prove the continuity of
L
. To do this, we fix
\mathfrak{m}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{g}}(\mathsf{U}\Gamma)
, for which we want to construct the appropriate fundamental domain
\mathcal{B}
so that
L_{\mathcal{B}}
is continuous at
\mathfrak{m}
. This amounts to understanding the discontinuity points of
\Psi_{\mathcal{B}}
.

To find the convenient domain
\mathcal{B}
we require the notion of
transversals
from
[
48
, Section 3.2]
. For the rest of the section we let
\Phi:\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma
denote the flow map
\Phi(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}},t)=\widetilde{\mathsf{g}}_{t}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}})
.

Definition 6.6
.

A
transversal
is a compact set
\tau\subset\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma
satisfying:

•

The restriction
\Phi:\tau\times[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma
is an embedding for some
\varepsilon>0
.

•

The restriction of the covering map
\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma\rightarrow\mathsf{U}\Gamma
to
\tau
is an embedding.

•

For every
\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}\in\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma
there exist
t>0
and
g\in\Gamma
with
\widetilde{\mathsf{g}}_{t}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}})\in g\tau
.

As discussed in
[
48
, Section 3.2]
, transversals always exist. For such a transversal
\tau
, its
interior
(denoted
\dot{\tau}
) is defined as the set of all
\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}\in\tau
such that, for all
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small,
\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}
is an interior point of
\Phi(\tau\times[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon])
. Non-interior points of
\tau
are called
boundary points
, and the set of all such points is denoted by
\partial\tau
.

The
first return time
of
\tau
is the function
T_{\tau}:\tau\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that
\widetilde{\mathsf{g}}_{T(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}})}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}})\in g\tau
for some
g\in\Gamma
(necessarily non-trivial) and
\widetilde{\mathsf{g}}_{t}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}})\notin\Gamma\tau
for any
0<t<T_{\tau}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}})
. It follows that
T_{\tau}
is Borel, and in fact continuous inside the set
T_{\tau}^{-1}(\Gamma\dot{\tau})
. We also set
R_{\tau}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}):=\widetilde{\mathsf{g}}_{T_{\tau}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}})}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}})
for
\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}\in\tau
.

Given a transversal
\tau
and
\varepsilon>0
small enough,
\widetilde{\mathsf{g}}_{\varepsilon}(\tau)
is also a transversal and
\partial\tau_{\varepsilon}=\widetilde{\mathsf{g}}_{\varepsilon}(\partial\tau)
. Moreover, if we choose
\varepsilon
small enough so that the image of
\Phi(\tau\times[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon])
under
\pi:\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma\rightarrow\mathsf{U}\Gamma
is an embedding, we have that

(6.4)

(\tau_{\varepsilon_{1}}\cup R_{\tau_{\varepsilon_{1}}}(\tau_{\varepsilon_{1}}))\cap(\tau_{\varepsilon_{2}}\cup R_{\tau_{\varepsilon_{2}}}(\tau_{\varepsilon_{2}}))=\emptyset\quad\text{ for }\varepsilon_{1}\neq\varepsilon_{2}\text{ in }[0,\varepsilon].

The advantage of using transversals is justified by the next definition.

Definition 6.7
.

For a transversal
\tau
we define

\mathcal{B}_{\tau}:=\{\widetilde{\mathsf{g}}_{t}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}):\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}\in\tau,0\leq t<T_{\tau}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}})\}\subset\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma.

The definition of transversal easily implies:

Lemma 6.8
.

For any transversal
\tau
, the set
\mathcal{B}_{\tau}
is a Borel fundamental domain for the action of
\Gamma
on
\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma
.

In addition, the interior of
\mathcal{B}_{\tau}
in
\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma
contains the points of form
\widetilde{\mathsf{g}}_{t}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}})
for
\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}
in
T_{\tau}^{-1}(\Gamma\dot{\tau})
and
0<t<T_{\tau}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}})
. This is equivalent to saying that the boundary
\partial\mathcal{B}_{\tau}
is contained in the union

\tau\cup R_{\tau}(\tau)\cup\{\widetilde{\mathsf{g}}_{t}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}):\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}\in T^{-1}_{\tau}(\Gamma\cdot\partial\tau),t\in\mathbb{R}\}.

For our fixed measure
\mathfrak{m}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{g}}(\mathsf{U}\Gamma)
, we let
\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}
denote its
\Gamma
-invariant lift to
\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma
. The conclusion of
[
48
, Lemma 3.1]
implies that there exists a transversal
\tau
such that
\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}(\Gamma\cdot\{\widetilde{\mathsf{g}}_{t}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}):\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}\in\partial\tau,t\in\mathbb{R}\})=0
.
For such a
\tau
,
Equation

6.4
implies that the (uncountably many) sets
(\tau_{\varepsilon}\cup R_{\tau_{\varepsilon}}(\tau_{\varepsilon}))
are pairwise disjoint among all
\varepsilon\geq 0
small enough. Therefore, we can find such
\varepsilon
satisfying
\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}((\tau_{\varepsilon}\cup R_{\tau_{\varepsilon}}(\tau_{\varepsilon})))=0
. Since

\{\widetilde{\mathsf{g}}_{t}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}):\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}\in T^{-1}_{\tau}(\Gamma\cdot\partial\tau),t\in\mathbb{R}\}\subset\Gamma\cdot\{\widetilde{\mathsf{g}}_{t}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}):\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}\in\partial\tau,t\in\mathbb{R}\},

from this discussion we deduce the following lemma.

Lemma 6.9
.

There exists a transversal
\tau
such that
\mathfrak{m}(\pi(\partial\mathcal{B}_{\tau}))=0
.

Our efforts in the construction of
\mathcal{B}_{\tau}
are justified by the next lemma.

Lemma 6.10
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a precompact Borel fundamental domain for
\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma
. If
\mathfrak{m}(\pi(\partial\mathcal{B}))=0
then
L_{\mathcal{B}}
is continuous at
\mathfrak{m}
.

Proof.

First, we note that for each
t
, the function
\mathbf{v}\mapsto g_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbf{v},t)
is continuous (in fact, locally constant) on the open set
\mathsf{U}\Gamma\backslash\mathsf{g}_{-t}(\pi(\partial\mathcal{B}))
. Hence,
\mathsf{g}
-invariance of
\mathfrak{m}
and the assumption
\mathfrak{m}(\pi(\partial\mathcal{B}))=0
imply that

\mathfrak{n}\mapsto\int{\Psi_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbf{v},t)}{d\mathfrak{n}(\mathbf{v})}

is continuous at
\mathfrak{m}
. This fact combined with
Equation

6.3
gives us the continuity of
L_{\mathcal{B}}
at
\mathfrak{m}
.
∎

As a consequence of
Lemma

6.9
and
Lemma

6.10
, we deduce the following.

Corollary 6.11
.

L:\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{g}}(\mathsf{U}\Gamma)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is continuous.

Our next goal is to show that
L
is a continuous extension of
\ell_{\varphi}
.

Lemma 6.12
.

Let
[g]\in[\Gamma]^{\prime}
be a non-trivial conjugacy class and
\mathfrak{m}_{[g]}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{g}}(\mathsf{U}\Gamma)
supported on
\gamma_{[g]}
as in
Equation

4.10
. Then

L(\mathfrak{m}_{[g]})=\frac{\ell_{\varphi}[g]}{\ell_{\hat{d}}[g]}.

Proof.

Recall that
\hat{d}
is the strongly hyperbolic metric used to define
\mathsf{U}\Gamma
. Without loss of generality suppose that
g
is primitive, and consider a precompact Borel fundamental domain
\mathcal{B}
containing
\{(g^{-},g^{+})\}\times[0,\ell_{\hat{d}}[g])
. Then for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathbf{v}\in\mathsf{U}\Gamma
with lift
\widehat{\mathbf{v}}=(g^{-},g^{+},s)
and
0\leq s<\ell_{\hat{d}}[g]
, we get
g_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbf{v},k\ell_{\hat{d}}[g])=g^{k}
. This observation and
Equation

4.10
imply

\int{\Psi_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbf{v},k\ell_{\hat{d}}[g])}{d\mathfrak{m}_{[g]}}(\mathbf{v})=\frac{1}{\ell_{\hat{d}}[g]}\int_{\gamma_{[g]}}{\varphi(o,g^{k})dt}=\varphi(o,g^{k}),

and hence

L(\mathfrak{m}_{[g]})=\lim_{k\to\infty}{\frac{1}{k\ell_{\hat{d}}[g]}}{\int{\Psi_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbf{v},k\ell_{\hat{d}}[g])}{d\mathfrak{m}_{[g]}(\mathbf{v})}}=\lim_{k\to\infty}{\frac{\varphi(o,g^{k})}{k\ell_{\hat{d}}[g]}}=\frac{\ell_{\varphi}[g]}{\ell_{\hat{d}}[g]},

as desired.
∎

Since real multiples of rational currents are dense in
\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}
,
\ell_{\varphi}
has at most one continuous extension from conjugacy classes in
\Gamma
to
\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}
. Therefore, from
Corollary

6.11
and
Lemma

6.12
we get the following, reproving a result of Kapovich and Martinez-Granado
[
65
]
.

Corollary 6.13
.

For any
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}
there exists a unique continuous extension of
\ell_{\psi}
from
[\Gamma]^{\prime}
to
\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}
. Such an extension satisfies

\ell_{\varphi}(\Lambda)=L(\mathfrak{m}_{\Lambda})

for any
\Lambda\in\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}
.

Proof.

From
Equation

4.12
it follows that
\mathfrak{m}_{\eta_{[g]}}=\ell_{\hat{d}}[g]\mathfrak{m}_{[g]}
for any
[g]\in[\Gamma]^{\prime}
. Then the result follows immediately from
Lemma

6.12
and
Corollary

6.11
.
∎

Recall that we have fixed
\psi\in\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{++}
. We now recover the mean distortion
\tau(\varphi/\psi)
from
L_{\varphi}
.

Lemma 6.14
.

Let
\Lambda_{\psi}\in\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}
be a BMS current for
\psi
, normalized so that
\mathfrak{m}_{\psi}:=\mathfrak{m}_{\Lambda_{\psi}}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{g}}(\mathsf{U}\Gamma)
. Then

(6.5)

L_{\varphi}(\mathfrak{m}_{\psi})=\tau(\varphi/\psi)/\tau(\hat{d}/\psi).

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{B}\subset\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma
be any precompact fundamental domain and fix
\mathbf{p}\in\mathsf{U}\Gamma
with lift
\widehat{\mathbf{p}}\in\mathcal{B}
. Then by
Theorem

4.1
(3), there is a constant
C^{\prime}>0
such that for any
\mathbf{v}\in\mathsf{U}\Gamma
we have

|d_{\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma}(\widehat{\mathbf{p}},g_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbf{v},t)\widetilde{\mathbf{p}})-t|\leq C^{\prime}
. The same happens if we replace
d_{\widetilde{\mathsf{U}}\Gamma}(\widehat{\mathbf{p}},g_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbf{v},t)\widetilde{\mathbf{p}})
with
\hat{d}(o,g_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbf{v},t))
by
Theorem

4.1
(3). This implies that for
\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}=(a,b,s)
with
a\neq b
in
\partial\Gamma
, the map
t\mapsto g_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbf{v},t)
is a uniform quasigeodesic ray converging in
\Gamma
to
b
.

In addition,
\Lambda_{\psi}
is in the measure class of a product of quasi-conformal measures, and hence by
Lemma

2.9
, for
\Lambda_{\psi}
-almost every
(a,b)\in\partial^{2}\Gamma
and
\mathbf{v}\in\mathsf{U}\Gamma
with lift
\widehat{\mathbf{v}}=(a,b,s)
we have

\frac{\varphi(o,g_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbf{v},t))}{\psi(o,g_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbf{v},t))}\to\tau(\varphi/\psi)\ \ \text{ and }\ \ \frac{\psi(o,g_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbf{v},t))}{t}\to\tau(\hat{d}/\psi)^{-1}\quad\text{ as }t\to\infty.

In consequence, we have that

\Psi_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbf{v},t)/t\to\tau(\varphi/\psi)/\tau(\hat{d}/\psi)\quad\text{as }t\to\infty

for
\mathfrak{m}_{\psi}
-almost every
\mathbf{v}
. Then by
Lemma

6.3
, Kingman’s subadditive ergodic theorem and the fact that
\mathfrak{m}_{\psi}
is a probability measure, this implies
Equation

6.5
, as desired.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

6.1
.

After rescaling
\Lambda_{\psi}
, we can assume without loss of generality that
\mathfrak{m}_{\psi}:=\mathfrak{m}_{\Lambda_{\psi}}
is a probability measure on
\mathsf{U}\Gamma
. Then, applying
Lemma

6.14
to both
L_{\varphi}
and

we get

(6.6)

\tau(\varphi/\psi)=\frac{\tau(\varphi/\psi)/\tau(\hat{d}/\psi)}{\tau(\psi/\psi)/\tau(\hat{d}/\psi)}=\frac{L_{\varphi}(\mathfrak{m}_{\psi})}{L_{\psi}(\mathfrak{m}_{\psi})}.

By

, the right hand side of

7.
The Bowen–Margulis–Sullivan map

In this section we prove
B
; stating that
\mathsf{BMS}:{H}_{\Gamma}^{++}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}
is continuous and has dense image, as well as
Proposition

1.7
. Here we also
Proposition

1.6
, asserting that this map restricts to an embedding on Hitchin components (up to contragradient) for surface groups. In this section we consider Mineyev’s flow space
\mathsf{U}\Gamma=(\mathsf{U}\Gamma,\mathsf{g})
for a strongly hyperbolic metric
\hat{d}\in\mathcal{D}_{\Gamma}
,

7.1.
Continuity of the
\mathsf{BMS}
map

Here we prove
B
. We begin with the following lemma, for which we recall the notion of duality from
Definition

5.6
. Its proof requires
Theorem

6.1
.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
\psi\in\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}
be dual to
F\in C(\mathsf{U}\Gamma)
. Then for any geodesic current
\Lambda\in\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}
we have

\int{F}{d\mathfrak{m}_{[\Lambda]}=\frac{\ell_{\psi}(\Lambda)}{\ell_{\hat{d}}(\Lambda)}}.

In particular, if
\mathsf{BMS}(\varphi)\in\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}
is the BMS current for
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}
, then we have

\int{F}{d\mathfrak{m}_{\mathsf{BMS}(\varphi)}}=\frac{\tau(\psi/\varphi)}{\tau(\hat{d}/\varphi)}.

Proof.

Since
\psi
and
F
are dual, the first identity follows immediately from
Equation

5.1
,
Corollary

6.13
, and the density of rational currents in
\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}
. The second identity is a particular case of the first identity, since by
Theorem

6.1
we have

\displaystyle\int{F}{d\mathfrak{m}_{\mathsf{BMS}(\varphi)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\ell_{\psi}(\mathsf{BMS}(\varphi))}{\ell_{\hat{d}}(\mathsf{BMS}(\varphi))}=\frac{\tau(\psi/\varphi)}{\tau(\hat{d}/\varphi)}/\frac{\tau(\hat{d}/\varphi)}{\tau(\hat{d}/\varphi)}=\frac{\tau(\psi/\varphi)}{\tau(\hat{d}/\varphi)}.\qed

Corollary 7.2
.

For two geodesic currents
\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}\in\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}
we have

\Lambda_{1}=\Lambda_{2}\ \text{ if and only if }\ \ell_{\psi}(\Lambda_{1})=\ell_{\psi}(\Lambda_{2})\quad\text{ for all }\psi\in\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{+}.

Proof.

Suppose that
\ell_{\psi}(\Lambda_{1})=\ell_{\psi}(\Lambda_{2})
for all
\psi\in\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{+}
. By
Lemma

7.1
and
Proposition

5.9
, we deduce that

(7.1)

\int{F}{d\mathfrak{m}_{[\Lambda_{1}]}}=\int{F}{d\mathfrak{m}_{[\Lambda_{2}]}}

for all nonnegative
F\in C^{B}(\mathsf{U}\Gamma)
. By additivity and
Lemma

5.8
, we then have that
Equation

7.1
holds for all
F\in C(\mathsf{U}\Gamma)
and hence
\mathfrak{m}_{[\Lambda_{1}]}=\mathfrak{m}_{[\Lambda_{2}]}
.
Lemma

4.10
then implies that
\Lambda_{1}=c\Lambda_{2}
for some
c>0
. Our assumptions on
\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}
imply
c=1
, concluding that
\Lambda_{1}=\Lambda_{2}
.
∎

We are ready to prove
Proposition

1.7
from the introduction.

Proof of
Proposition

1.7
.

Let
\psi\in\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{++}
and
\Lambda_{T}\in\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}
be as in the statement of the proposition, and let
(T_{k})_{k}
be a diverging sequence of real numbers such that

converges to
\Lambda_{\infty}\in\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}
. Then given
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{+}
, by

2.5

\tau(\varphi/\psi)=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{\#\{[g]\in[\Gamma]^{\prime}:\ell_{\psi}[g]<T\}}\sum_{[g]\in[\Gamma]^{\prime}:\ell_{\psi}[g]<T}{\frac{\ell_{\varphi}[g]}{\ell_{\psi}[g]}}=\lim_{k\to\infty}{\ell_{\varphi}(\Lambda_{T_{k}})}=\ell_{\varphi}(\Lambda_{\infty}).

On the other hand, if
\Lambda_{\psi}\in\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}
is the BMS current for
\psi
such that
\ell_{\psi}(\Lambda_{\psi})=1
, then by

6.1

Now we prove

.

Proof of
B
.

First we prove the continuity statement, so let
(\mathbf{x}_{m})_{m}
be a sequence in
{H}_{\Gamma}^{++}
converging to
\mathbf{x}_{\infty}
. Since
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}
is compact and metrizable, we only need to prove that the BMS currents of some subsequence of the
\mathbf{x}_{m}
’s converges to
\mathsf{BMS}(\mathbf{x}_{\infty})
.

Let
\mathfrak{m}_{m}=\mathfrak{m}_{\mathsf{BMS}(\mathbf{x}_{m})}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{g}}(\mathsf{U}\Gamma)
, and extract a subsequence (also denoted
\mathfrak{m}_{m}
) converging to the probability measure
\mathfrak{m}_{\infty}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{g}}(\mathsf{U}\Gamma)
. By
Lemma

4.10
, it is enough to show that
\mathfrak{m}_{\infty}=\mathfrak{m}_{\mathsf{BMS}(\mathbf{x}_{\infty})}
. Suppose that
\mathbf{x}_{m}=[\psi_{m}]
with
\psi_{m}\in\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{++}
with exponential growth rate 1 for each
m
, and similarly
\mathbf{x}_{\infty}=[\psi_{\infty}]
for
\psi_{\infty}
with exponential growth rate 1.

Let
F\in C(\mathsf{U}\Gamma)
be a continuous function, and suppose it is dual to a hyperbolic metric potential
\psi_{F}\in\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}
. By
Lemma

7.1
we know that

\int{F}{d\mathfrak{m}_{m}}=\frac{\tau(\psi_{F}/\psi_{m})}{\tau(\hat{d}/\psi_{m})}\quad\text{ for all }m.

Therefore, the convergence
\mathbf{x}_{m}\to\mathbf{x}_{\infty}
in
{H}_{\Gamma}^{++}
and
Lemma

2.7
then imply

\displaystyle\int{F}{d\mathfrak{m}_{\infty}}=\lim_{m\to\infty}{\int{F}{d\mathfrak{m}_{m}}}=\lim_{m\to\infty}{\frac{\tau(\psi_{F}/\psi_{m})}{\tau(\hat{d}/\psi_{m})}}=\frac{\tau(\psi_{F}/\psi_{\infty})}{\tau(\hat{d}/\psi_{\infty})}=\int{F}{d\mathfrak{m}_{\mathsf{BMS}(\mathbf{x}_{\infty})}},

and hence

(7.2)

\int{F}{d\mathfrak{m}_{\infty}}=\int{F}{d\mathfrak{m}_{\mathsf{BMS}(\mathbf{x}_{\infty})}}

for all
F\in C(\mathsf{U}\Gamma)
with a dual hyperbolic metric potential in
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}
. By
Proposition

5.9
and
Lemma

5.8
we deduce that
Equation

7.2
holds for any
F
in
C(\mathsf{U}\Gamma)
. This implies that
\mathfrak{m}_{\infty}=\mathfrak{m}_{\mathsf{BMS}(\mathbf{x}_{\infty})}
, as desired.

Next, we prove that the set
\{\mathsf{BMS}(\mathbf{x}):\mathbf{x}\in{H}_{\Gamma}^{++}\}
is dense in
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}
, for which we consider the homeomorphism
\mathsf{m}
from
Lemma

4.10
. Then by the density of rational projective currents in
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}
, it is enough to show that
\mathfrak{m}_{[g]}
belongs to the closure of
\mathsf{m}(\{\mathsf{BMS}(\mathbf{x}):\mathbf{x}\in{H}_{\Gamma}^{++}\})
for
[g]\in[\Gamma]^{\prime}
. To show this, let
F:\mathsf{U}\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be the distance function to the periodic orbit
\gamma_{[g]}
. Since
F
is Lipschitz, let
\psi\in\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{++}
be a dual potential given by
Proposition

5.9
. Let
\theta=\theta_{\psi/\hat{d}}
be the Manhattan curve for the pair
\psi,\hat{d}
, and let
\psi_{t}=t\psi+\theta(t)\hat{d}
, which belongs to
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{++}
and has exponential growth rate 1
[
20
, Proposition 5.1]
. Let
\mathfrak{m}_{t}=\mathfrak{m}_{\mathsf{BMS}(\psi_{t})}
, and consider a sequence
t_{k}\to\infty
so that
\mathfrak{m}_{t_{k}}
converges to some
\mathfrak{m}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{g}}(\mathsf{U}\Gamma)
. We claim that
\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{m}_{[g]}
.

By
Lemma

7.1
, for each
k
large enough we have that

\int{F}{d\mathfrak{m}_{t_{k}}}=\frac{\tau(\psi/\psi_{t_{k}})}{\tau(\hat{d}/\psi_{t_{k}})}=\frac{\tau(({t_{k}}^{-1}\psi_{t_{k}}-(\theta(t_{k})/t_{k})\hat{d})/\psi_{t_{k}})}{\tau(\hat{d}/\psi_{t_{k}})}=\frac{1}{t_{k}\tau(\hat{d}/\psi_{t_{k}})}-\frac{\theta(t_{k})}{t_{k}}.

Since
\psi\in\partial\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{++}
, we also have that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\theta(t)}{t}=-\inf_{[g]\in[\Gamma]^{\prime}}\frac{\ell_{\psi}[g]}{\ell_{\hat{d}}[g]}=0,

see e.g.
[
24
, Corollary 3.3]
. Finally, since each
\psi_{t}
has exponential growth rate 1 and the same holds for
\hat{d}
, we have
\tau(\hat{d}/\psi_{t})\geq 1
for all
t
(cf.
[
24
, Theorem 1.2]
). Combining these observations, we have that

\int{F}{d\mathfrak{m}}=\lim_{k\to\infty}{\int{F}{d\mathfrak{m}_{t_{k}}}}=0.

The definition of
F
then forces
\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{m}_{[g]}
, proving our claim and concluding the proof of the theorem.
∎

7.2.
Embedding Hitchin components

In this section we let
\Gamma
be the fundamental group of a closed hyperbolic surface and consider the map
\mathsf{BMS}
restricted to
\overline{\Hit_{\Gamma}^{n}}
, the space of
n
-Hitchin representations up to
contragradient
, i.e., the relation
\rho(g)^{\ast}\coloneqq{}^{t}\rho(g)^{-1}
. Here we identify
\overline{\Hit_{\Gamma}^{n}}
with a subspace of
{H}_{\Gamma}^{++}
via the Hilbert metric from
Equation

1.2
. We prove
Proposition

1.6
, stating that the restriction
\mathsf{BMS}:\overline{\Hit_{\Gamma}^{n}}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}
is an embedding. For
n\geq 3
, also note in
Corollary

7.6
that this embedding differs the embedding into geodesic currents via positively ratioed cross-ratios provided in
[
76
]
.

In order to prove these results, we first restrict to the subspace
\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{s}\subset\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}
of
symmetric geodesic currents
, i.e. those geodesic currents that are invariant under the involution
(a,b)\mapsto(b,a)
on
\partial^{2}\Gamma
. Then the projective quotient
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}^{s}_{\Gamma}
is naturally a subspace of
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}
and hence is equipped with the quotient of the weak
∗
topology (which we also call the weak
∗
topology). For a conjugacy class
[g]\in[\Gamma]^{\prime}
, its
symmetric rational current
is
\alpha_{[g]}=\frac{1}{2}(\eta_{[g]}+\eta_{[g^{-1}]})\in\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{s}
; see
[
21
, Corollary 6.11]
.

A symmetric geodesic current
\mu
is
filling
if
i(\mu,\alpha)>0
for all non-zero currents
\alpha\in\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{s}
. Let
\mathbb{P}_{f}\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{s}
(resp.
\mathbb{P}_{nf}\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{s}
) denote the subset of
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}^{s}_{\Gamma}
of projective classes of filling (resp. non filling) geodesic currents.

By
[
18
,
21
,
41
]
, there is a natural injection of
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}^{s}_{\Gamma}
into
\overline{{H}_{\Gamma}^{++}}
, which maps the projective class
[\mu]
to the rough similarity class of
d_{\mu}\in\overline{\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{++}}
satisfying

(7.3)

\ell_{d_{\mu}}[g]=i(\mu,\alpha_{[g]})\quad\text{ for all }[g]\in[\Gamma]^{\prime}.

For the rest of the section, for each non-zero geodesic current
\mu\in\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{s}
we fix a hyperbolic metric potential
d_{\mu}
satisfying this identity. If
\mu
is filling, we can choose
d_{\mu}\in\mathcal{D}_{\Gamma}

[
21
, Corollary 6.11]
, and in this case we let
v_{\mu}
denote the exponential growth rate of
d_{\mu}
.

Our first step to prove
Proposition

1.6
is to show that inclusion
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{s}\subset\overline{{H}_{\Gamma}^{++}}
is an embedding for the strong length topology on
\overline{{H}_{\Gamma}^{++}}
from
Section

3.2
.

Lemma 7.3
.

The strong length topology on
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}^{s}_{\Gamma}
coincides with the weak
∗
topology.

Proof.

By
[
47
, Theorem 11]
(cf.
[
82
, Théorème 2]
), the weak
∗
topology on
\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{s}
coincides with the restriction of the product topology on
\mathbb{R}^{[\Gamma]^{\prime}}
induced by the injection

\mathcal{C}^{s}_{\Gamma}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{[\Gamma]^{\prime}},\qquad\mu\longmapsto i(\mu,\cdot).

Through the proof we use this characterization of the weak
∗
topologies on
\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{s}
and
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}^{s}_{\Gamma}
. We also fix a filing geodesic current
\mu_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{s}
. For each projective current
[\mu]\in\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{s}
, we choose a representative
\mu\in\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{s}
satisfying
{\rm Dil}(d_{\mu},d_{\mu_{0}})=1
.

First, we show that the strong length topology on
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{s}
is finer than the weak
∗
topology. Given
[\mu],[\nu]\in\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{s}
, by
Equation

7.3
and
Definition

3.8
we have

(7.4)

D_{d_{\mu_{0}}}([d_{\mu}],[d_{\nu}])=\sup_{[g]\in[\Gamma]^{\prime}}\frac{|i(\mu,\alpha_{[g]})-i(\nu,\alpha_{[g]})|}{i(\mu_{0},\alpha_{[g]})}.

For each
[g]\in[\Gamma]^{\prime}
, define
p_{[g]}:\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}^{s}_{\Gamma}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
according to

p_{[g]}([\mu])=i(\mu,\alpha_{[g]})
(for our choice of representative
\mu
as above). Then
Equation

7.4
implies that each function
p_{[g]}
is continuous with respect to strong length topology. Since the product topology is the coarsest topology making all projection maps continuous, it follows that the strong length topology is finer than the weak
∗
topology.

We now prove the reverse inclusion. Recall that
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}^{s}_{\Gamma}
is compact
[
8
]
and the functions

[\mu]\longmapsto\frac{i(\mu,\alpha)}{i(\mu_{0},\alpha)}

are well-defined and continuous in the weak
∗
topology. By density of (projective) symmetric rational geodesic currents in
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{s}
, for any
\mu\in\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{s}
we can write

{\rm Dil}(d_{\mu},d_{\mu_{0}})=\max_{[\alpha]\in\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}^{s}_{\Gamma}}\frac{i(\mu,\alpha)}{i(\mu_{0},\alpha)}.

Thus, it follows that
\mu\mapsto{\rm Dil}(d_{\mu},d_{\mu_{0}})
is continuous for the weak
∗
topology on
\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{s}
, as the maximum of a continuous family of functions indexed by a compact space. Similarly, we can write

D_{d_{\mu_{0}}}([d_{\mu}],[d_{\nu}])=\max_{[\alpha]\in\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}^{s}_{\Gamma}}\frac{\left|{\rm Dil}(d_{\mu},d_{\mu_{0}})^{-1}i(\mu,\alpha)-{\rm Dil}(d_{\nu},d_{\mu_{0}})^{-1}i(\nu,\alpha)\right|}{i(\mu_{0},\alpha)}.

Hence,
([\mu],[\nu])\mapsto D_{d_{\mu_{0}}}([d_{\mu}],[d_{\nu}])
is continuous in the weak
∗
topology. Since the strong length topology is precisely the metric topology induced by
D_{d_{\mu_{0}}}
—that is, the coarsest topology making
D_{d_{\mu_{0}}}
continuous—we conclude that the weak
∗
topology is finer than the strong length topology on
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{s}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Our next lemma describes the behavior of the dilation along divergent sequences on
\mathbb{P}_{f}\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{s}
.

Lemma 7.4
.

Let
([\alpha_{k}])_{k}\in\mathbb{P}_{f}\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{s}
be a sequence converging in
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{s}
to
[\alpha_{\infty}]\in\mathbb{P}_{nf}\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{s}
, and for each
k
choose a representatives such that
v_{\alpha_{k}}=1
. Then for any
d\in\mathcal{D}_{\Gamma}
we have that
{\rm Dil}(d_{\alpha_{k}},d)\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
.

Proof.

We see
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{s}
as a subspace of
\overline{{H}_{\Gamma}^{++}}
via the identification
[\mu]=[d_{\mu}]
, so that
\mathbb{P}_{f}\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{s}\subset{D}_{\Gamma}
. Without loss of generality we can assume
d=d_{\mu}
for some filling geodesic current
\mu\in\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{s}
. By
Lemma

3.10
and
Lemma

7.3
, closed balls with respect to
\Delta
in
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{s}
are compact, and hence we must have that
\Delta([d_{\alpha_{k}}],[d_{\mu}])\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
.

If the sequence
({\rm Dil}(d_{\alpha_{k}},d_{\mu}))_{k}
is bounded, then by
Proposition

3.15
we can find
r>0
and a sequence
(\mathbf{y}_{k})_{k}
in
{D}_{\Gamma}
with each
\mathbf{y}_{k}
in a Manhattan segment from
[d_{\mu}]
to
[d_{\alpha_{k}}]
, and so that
\Delta(\mathbf{y}_{k},[d_{\mu}])=r
and
(\mathbf{y}_{k})_{k}
has no convergent subsequence in
{D}_{\Gamma}
. However, from
Equation

2.6
it follows that
\mathbf{y}_{k}=[d_{\beta_{k}}]
for some
\beta_{k}\in\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{s}
that is a convex combination of
\mu
and
\alpha_{k}
. Then the compactness of balls with respect to
\Delta
in
\mathbb{P}_{f}\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{s}
gives us a convergent subsequence for
(\mathbf{y}_{k})_{k}
, which is a contradiction. Thus the sequence
{\rm Dil}(d_{\alpha_{k}},d)
must diverge.
∎

In what follows, for a filling geodesic current
\mu\in\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{s}
we denote
\mathsf{BMS}([\mu])=\mathsf{BMS}(\mu)=\mathsf{BMS}(d_{\rho})\in\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{s}
.

Proposition 7.5
.

If
([\alpha_{k}])_{k}\in\mathbb{P}_{f}\mathcal{C}^{s}_{\Gamma}
is a sequence of projective currents converging to
[\alpha_{\infty}]\in\mathbb{P}^{s}_{nf}\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}
and
[\Lambda_{k}]=\mathsf{BMS}([\alpha_{k}])
converges to
[\Lambda_{\infty}]
in
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}^{s}_{\Gamma}
, then

i(\alpha_{\infty},\Lambda_{\infty})=0.

Proof.

Suppose
\hat{d}\in\mathcal{D}_{\Gamma}
is a strongly hyperbolic metric representing a point in Teichmüller space, so that
\hat{d}=d_{\mu}
for
\mu
a Liouville current. We consider representatives
\alpha_{k}
so that
v_{\alpha_{k}}=1
for each
k
, and let
t_{k}>0
satisfy
{\rm Dil}(t_{k}d_{\alpha_{k}},\hat{d})=1
. Then
Lemma

7.4
implies that
t_{k}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
. In addition, the set of geodesic currents
\nu\in\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{s}
such that
{\rm Dil}(d_{\nu},d_{\mu})=1
is compact
[
9
, Proposition 4]
, and hence we can assume that
t_{k}\alpha_{k}\to\alpha_{\infty}
.

For each
k
, let
F_{k}\in C(\mathsf{U}\Gamma)
be dual to
d_{\alpha_{k}}
given by
Proposition

5.13
, and let
\mathfrak{m}_{k}=\mathfrak{m}_{[\Lambda_{k}]}
for
1\leq k\leq\infty
.
We claim that
\int{t_{k}F_{k}}{d\mathfrak{m}_{k}}\to\int{F}{d\mathfrak{m}_{\infty}}
.
Indeed, fix an arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
. Using
Equation

5.1
for all
k
and the fact that measures supported on periodic orbits are dense in
\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{g}}(\mathsf{U}\Gamma)
, we have

(7.5)

\displaystyle\sup_{\mathfrak{m}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{g}}(\mathsf{U}\Gamma)}\left|\int t_{k}F_{k}\,d\mathfrak{m}-\int F\,d\mathfrak{m}\right|=\sup_{[g]\in[\Gamma]^{\prime}}\frac{|t_{k}\ell_{d_{\alpha_{k}}}[g]-\ell_{d_{\alpha}}[g]|}{\ell_{\hat{d}}[g]}.

By
Lemma

7.3
, we also have
[t_{k}d_{\alpha_{k}}]\to[d_{\alpha_{\infty}}]
in the strong length topology of
\overline{{H}_{\Gamma}^{++}}
. Therefore, by
Equation

7.5
, there exists
k_{1}
such that for all
k\geq k_{1}
,

\left|\int t_{k}F_{k}\,d\mathfrak{m}_{k}-\int F\,d\mathfrak{m}_{k}\right|\leq\sup_{\mathfrak{m}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{g}}(\mathsf{U}\Gamma)}\left|\int t_{k}F_{k}\,d\mathfrak{m}-\int F\,d\mathfrak{m}\right|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Since
\mathfrak{m}_{k}\to\mathfrak{m}_{\infty}
weak
∗
and
F
is continuous, there exists
k_{2}
such that for all
k\geq k_{2}
,

\left|\int F\,d\mathfrak{m}_{\infty}-\int F\,d\mathfrak{m}_{k}\right|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Hence, for all
k\geq\max\{k_{1},k_{2}\}
,

\left|\int F\,d\mathfrak{m}_{\infty}-\int t_{k}F_{k}\,d\mathfrak{m}_{k}\right|\leq\left|\int F\,d\mathfrak{m}_{\infty}-\int F\,d\mathfrak{m}_{k}\right|+\left|\int F\,d\mathfrak{m}_{k}-\int t_{k}F_{k}\,d\mathfrak{m}_{k}\right|<\varepsilon,

proving the claim.

Finally, by
Lemma

7.1
, for every
k
we have

\int F_{k}\,d\mathfrak{m}_{[\Lambda]}=\frac{\ell_{d_{\alpha_{k}}}(\Lambda)}{\ell_{\hat{d}}(\Lambda)}\qquad\text{for all }\Lambda\in\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{i(\alpha_{\infty},\Lambda_{\infty})}{\ell_{\hat{d}}(\Lambda_{\infty})}=\frac{\ell_{d_{\alpha_{\infty}}}(\Lambda_{\infty})}{\ell_{\hat{d}}(\Lambda_{\infty})}

\displaystyle=\int F\,d\mathfrak{m}_{\infty}

\displaystyle(\text{by \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{lem:dualintegral}})

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}t_{k}\int F_{k}\,d\mathfrak{m}_{k}

(by Claim)

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}t_{k}\frac{\ell_{d_{\alpha_{k}}}(\Lambda_{k})}{\ell_{\hat{d}}(\Lambda_{k})}

\displaystyle(\text{by \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{lem:dualintegral}})

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}t_{k}\frac{\tau(d_{\alpha_{k}}/d_{\alpha_{k}})}{\tau(\hat{d}/d_{\alpha_{k}})}

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{t_{k}}{\tau(\hat{d}/d_{\alpha_{k}})}

\displaystyle(\text{since }\tau(\hat{d}/d_{\alpha_{t}})\geq v_{\hat{d}}^{-1}\text{ by \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{cantrell-tanaka.manhattan}{}{}, Theorem~1.2]}})

\displaystyle\leq v_{\hat{d}}\lim_{k\to\infty}t_{k}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle(\text{since }t_{k}\to 0).

This yields
i(\alpha_{\infty},\Lambda_{\infty})=0
, as desired.
∎

Given a Hitchin representation
\rho:\Gamma\rightarrow\PSL(n,\mathbb{R})
, let
d_{\rho}=d_{\rho,H}\in\mathcal{D}_{\Gamma}
denote the corresponding Hilbert metric as in
Equation

1.2
, and let
\ell_{\rho}:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be the associated translation length:

\ell_{\rho}[g]=\log\lambda_{1}(\rho(g))\lambda_{1}(\rho(g^{-1}))\quad\text{ for }[g]\in[\Gamma]^{\prime}.

We let
[\rho]=[d_{\rho}]\in\overline{\Hit_{\Gamma}^{n}}\subset{D}_{\Gamma}
be the metric structure induced by
\rho
. The work of Martone–Zhang
[
76
]
gives a (necessarily unique) symmetric geodesic current
\alpha_{\rho}\in\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{s}
such that

\ell_{\rho}[g]=i(\alpha_{\rho},\alpha_{[g]})\quad\text{ for all }[g]\in[\Gamma]^{\prime},

where
i:\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{s}\times\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{s}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
denotes Bonahon’s intersection number
[
8
]
. This induces a
positively ratioed
map
\mathsf{PR}:\overline{\Hit_{\Gamma}^{n}}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{s}
that send
[\rho]
to
[\alpha_{\rho}]
. This map is injective (for instance, by
[
16
]
), continuous by
[
49
, Corollary 1.10]
, and proper (and hence an embedding) by
[
73
, Corollary 1.5]
.

By
Equation

7.3
, note that each
d_{\rho}
is roughly isometric to
d_{\alpha_{\rho}}
, so that
\mathsf{PR}
induces a compatible chain of inclusions

\overline{\Hit_{\Gamma}^{n}}\subset\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{s}\subset\overline{{H}^{++}_{\Gamma}}

that identifies
[\rho]=[\alpha_{\rho}]=[d_{\rho}]
.
By
Lemma

7.3
, these inclusions correspond to topological embeddings. For a Hitchin representation
\rho
, we also denote
\mathsf{BMS}(\rho)=\mathsf{BMS}(d_{\rho})\in\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{s}
.

Proof of
Proposition

1.6
.

Given
n\geq 2
, we know that
\mathsf{BMS}:\overline{\Hit^{n}_{\Gamma}}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}
is injective, and it is continuous by
B
and the fact that
\mathsf{PR}
is continuous. To show that
\mathsf{BMS}
is a topological embedding, suppose
([\rho_{m}])_{m}\in\overline{\Hit_{\Gamma}^{n}}
is a sequence such that
\mathsf{BMS}(\rho_{m})\to\mathsf{BMS}(\rho_{\infty})
for some
[\rho_{\infty}]\in\overline{\Hit^{n}_{\Gamma}}
. We claim that
[\rho_{m}]\to[\rho_{\infty}]
in
\overline{\Hit_{\Gamma}^{n}}
. By
Equation

7.3
, it is enough to show that
\mathsf{PR}([\rho_{m}])\to\mathsf{PR}([\rho_{\infty}])
in
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{s}
. In order to prove this, for each
m
suppose
\mathsf{PR}([\rho_{m}])=[\alpha_{m}]
for
\alpha_{m}\in\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{s}
with
v_{\alpha_{m}}=1
, and up to a subsequence assume that
[\alpha_{m}]\to[\alpha_{\infty}]\in\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{s}
. By metrizability of
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}
, it is enough to prove that
\mathsf{PR}([\rho_{\infty}])=[\alpha_{\infty}]
. First, we note that
\alpha_{\infty}
is filling, since otherwise by
Proposition

7.5
we would have that
\mathsf{BMS}(\rho_{\infty})
is not filling, contradicting that it has full support. If
\alpha_{m}\in\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{s}
is chosen so that
v_{\alpha_{\infty}}=1
, by
Lemma

3.10
and
Lemma

7.3
we have that
\alpha_{m}\to\alpha_{\infty}
in
\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{s}
. In addition, if
\mathsf{BMS}(\rho_{m})=[\Lambda_{m}]
for
\Lambda_{m}\in\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{s}
such that
i(\alpha_{m},\Lambda_{m})=1
and
\mathsf{BMS}(\rho_{\infty})=[\Lambda_{\infty}]
for
\Lambda_{\infty}
such that
i(\alpha_{\infty},\Lambda_{\infty})=1
, then the continuity of the intersection number and
[
9
, Proposition 4]
imply that
\Lambda_{m}\to\Lambda_{\infty}
in
\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{s}
.
If
\hat{d}_{\rho}
represents the normalized Hilbert distance of the Hitchin representation
\rho
with exponential growth rate 1, then these facts imply that

\tau(d_{\alpha_{\infty}}/\hat{d}_{\rho_{\infty}})=i(\alpha_{\infty},\Lambda_{\infty})=\lim_{m\to\infty}{i(\alpha_{m},\Lambda_{m})}=\lim_{m\to\infty}{\tau(d_{\alpha_{m}}/\hat{d}_{\rho_{m}})}=1.

Hence
\tau(d_{\alpha_{\infty}}/\hat{d}_{\rho_{\infty}})=1
, and since both
d_{\alpha_{\infty}}
and
\hat{d}_{\rho_{\infty}}
have exponential growth rate 1,
[

, Theorem 1.2]
implies that
[d_{\alpha_{\infty}}]=[d_{\rho_{\infty}}]
in
{D}_{\Gamma}
. Therefore,
\mathsf{PR}([\rho_{\infty}])=[\alpha_{\infty}]
, as expected.
∎

.

Corollary 7.6
.

Suppose that
n\geq 3
. Then the maps
\mathsf{PR}
and
\mathsf{BMS}
are distinct.

Proof.

Suppose that the maps
\mathsf{PR}
and
\mathsf{BMS}
coincide on
\overline{\Hit_{\Gamma}^{n}}
. By
[
88
]
, there exist sequences
[\rho_{m}]\in\overline{\Hit_{\Gamma}^{n}}
so that

Definition 8.1

For a potential

It follows immediately that an equilibrium state for

Lemma 8.2
.

Suppose

is dual to a potential in

. Then

has a unique equilibrium state.

Proof.

Let

be dual to

. In the language of

\Gamma

For this family

\mathsf{P}_{\mathsf{g}}(f):=\sup_{\mathfrak{m}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{g}}(\mathsf{U}\Gamma)}\left(h(\mathfrak{m})+\int_{\mathsf{U}\Gamma}fd\mathfrak{m}\right),

where we have

\int_{\mathsf{U}\Gamma}fd\mathfrak{m}:=\lim_{t\to\infty}{\frac{1}{t}\int_{\mathsf{U}\Gamma}}{f_{t}d\mathfrak{m}}.

In addition,
[
46

tells us that

Moreover, if we follow the proof of

Definition 8.5
.

Let

represent a self-intersecting figure eight curve in
P
, and
gh^{-1}
represent a simple closed curve corresponding to the third
boundary component of
P
.
In
[
40
, Section 1.11]
it is shown that the translation length

\Gamma\ni k\mapsto\|k\|\coloneqq\inf_{p\in T}d_{T}(p,k\cdot p)

of an isometric action of
\Gamma
on a real tree

must satisfy that for every

, that
\|k_{1}k_{2}\|=\|k_{1}k_{2}^{-1}\|
, or

\|k_{1}\|+\|k_{2}\|\geq\max\{\|k_{1}k_{2}\|,\|k_{1}k_{2}^{-1}\|\}
.
We also note that the hypothesis can be preserved under equivariant rough isometry, by
[
40
, Theorem 3.7]
and from the fact that for a real tree the translation length and the stable length are equal.
We see that, for this pair
(g,h)
,
\ell_{d}
violates Culler-Morgan’s axioms. Hence, since for a real tree translation length and stable length are equal, it follows that
\ell_{d}
cannot be the stable length of a real tree, proving the third item.

In
[
75
, Theorem A’]
it is shown that for a stable length to be an intersection number with a geodesic current,
a necessary condition is that for any pair of elements
g,h\in\Gamma
whose hyperbolic axes cross on the hyperbolic disk, one must have

\ell_{d}[g]+\ell_{d}[h]\geq\ell_{d}[gh]
. This, again, is violated by

, so it cannot arise as an intersection number with a geodesic current.
∎

The analog of
Proposition

8.8
for free groups is the following. An element in a free group
\Gamma
is

if it is not contained in a proper free factor of
\Gamma
.

Proposition 8.11
.

Let
\Gamma
be a free group of rank at least

. Then there exists a pseudometric

such that:

(1)

\ell_{d}[g]=0
for any

representing a simple element.

(2)

\ell_{d}[g]>0
for any

representing a non-simple element.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be the closure in

of the set

, so that

is a flip-invariant algebraic lamination.
Then

8.10
gives us a pseudometric

such that a geodesic current

9.
Density of Green metrics

In this section we prove
C
. For the reader’s convenience, we restate the result here. Recall that for a probability measure
\mu
on the group
\Gamma
,
d_{\mu}
denotes the associated Green metric on
\Gamma
.

Theorem 9.1

(Density of Green metrics)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a non-elementary hyperbolic group and
d\in\mathcal{D}_{\Gamma}
. Then there exists a constant
\delta_{1}>0
satisfying the following. Let
\delta>\delta_{1}
and
\mu_{l}
be the uniform probability measure supported on
S_{l}=\{g\in\Gamma\colon l-\delta<d(o,g)\leq l\}
. Then
S_{l}
generates
\Gamma
for
l
large enough and
[d_{\mu_{l}}]
converges to
[d]
in
{D}_{\Gamma}
.

Each set
S_{l}
as in the above statement is a “thickened sphere” of radius
l
around
o
in
(\Gamma,d)
, and
\mu_{l}
is the uniform probability measure on
l
. A sketch of this approximation result is the following.

(1)

We give an upper bound of
d_{\mu_{l}}
in terms of
d_{S_{l}}
, the word metric with respect to
S_{l}
(
Lemma

9.4
). This only requires mild assumptions on
\Gamma
.

(2)

We give a lower bound of
d_{\mu_{l}}
in terms of
d
and
d_{S_{l}}
(
Proposition

9.5
). This uses hyperbolicity of
\Gamma
in a crucial way, as we rely on counting results from
[
39
]
.

(3)

For
l
large enough, we show that
d
is well-approximated (in
{D}_{\Gamma}
) by
d_{S_{l}}
(
Lemma

9.2
). Again, this result does not require hyperbolicity of
\Gamma
, but uses that
d
is roughly geodesic. This approximation upgrades the bounds in (1) and (2) to bounds of
d_{\mu_{l}}
in terms of
d
only.

(4)

As
l
tends to infinity, these bounds become more precise (up to additive errors), implying the convergence
[d_{\mu_{l}}]\to[d]
in
{D}_{\Gamma}
.

We begin by showing that word metrics on thickened spheres approximate roughly geodesic metrics. This is analogous to
[
22
, Lemma 2.5 (2)]
, where it is shown that word metrics on large balls approximate roughly geodesic metrics.

Lemma 9.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be any group and let
d
be an unbounded left-invariant
\alpha
-roughly geodesic (non-necessarily symmetric) metric on
\Gamma
. Then for any
l>\delta>2(\lceil\alpha\rceil+\alpha+2)
the set
S_{l}=\{g\in\Gamma:l-\delta<d(o,g)\leq l\}
generates
\Gamma
as a semi-group and there exists
M=M_{l,\alpha}>0
such that

(9.1)

(l-\lceil{\alpha\rceil})|g|_{S_{l}}-M\leq d(o,g)\leq l|x|_{S_{l}}\quad\text{ for all }g\in\Gamma.

Proof.

Since
d
is
\alpha
-rough geodesic, the ball
B=\{g\in\Gamma\colon d(o,g)\leq 2+\alpha\}
generates
\Gamma
as a semi-group and satisfies

(9.2)

(\lceil{\alpha\rceil}-\alpha+1)|g|_{B}\leq d(o,g)+\lceil{\alpha\rceil}+1

for all
g\in\Gamma
, see
[
22
, Lemma 2.5 (2)]
. Therefore, since
d
is unbounded and
\alpha
-roughly geodesic and
l>\delta>2(\alpha+\lceil\alpha\rceil+1)
, we can find
h\in\Gamma
with
|d(o,h)-(l-2\lceil{\alpha\rceil}-2)|\leq\alpha
. In particular, if
b\in B
, then both
h,hb
belong to
S_{l}
and
|b|_{S_{l}}\leq 2
, implying

(9.3)

|g|_{S_{l}}\leq 2|g|_{B}\quad\text{ for all }g\in\Gamma.

Now we prove
Equation

9.1
, for which the second inequality holds easily by the triangle inequality. To prove the first inequality in
Equation

9.1
, we let
o=g_{0},\dots,g_{m}=g
be an
\alpha
-rough geodesic joining
o
and
g\in\Gamma
, and write
m=q(l-\lceil{\alpha\rceil})+r
with
q,r
integers and
0\leq r<l-\lceil{\alpha\rceil}
. We set
z_{1}=g_{l-\lceil{\alpha\rceil}},z_{j}=g_{(j-1)(l-\lceil{\alpha\rceil})}^{-1}g_{j(l-\lceil{\alpha\rceil})}
for
2\leq j\leq q
, and
\hat{z}=z_{q}^{-1}g=g_{q(l-\lceil{\alpha\rceil})}^{-1}g_{q(l-\lceil{\alpha\rceil})+r}
. The definition of the
g_{i}^{\prime}s
implies that

d(o,z_{j})\in[l-\lceil{\alpha\rceil}-\alpha,l-\lceil{\alpha\rceil}+\alpha]\subset(l-\delta,l]

for all
j
, where we used
l-\alpha>2(\lceil\alpha\rceil+\alpha+2)
. This yields

\displaystyle|g|_{S_{l}}\leq q+|\hat{z}|_{S_{l}}\leq(l-\lceil{\alpha\rceil})^{-1}m+|\hat{z}|_{S_{l}}\leq(l-\lceil{\alpha\rceil})^{-1}(d(o,g)+\alpha)+|\hat{z}|_{S_{l}}.

In addition, we also have
d(o,\hat{z})=d(g_{q(l-\lceil{\alpha\rceil})},g_{m})\leq l
, and so from
Equation

9.2
and
Equation

9.3
we deduce

|\hat{z}|_{S_{l}}\leq 2|\hat{z}|_{B}\leq(l+\lceil{\alpha\rceil}+1)(\lceil{\alpha\rceil}-\alpha+1)^{-1}.

Combining the last two inequalities we obtain the first inequality in
Equation

9.1
with
M=(l+\lceil{\alpha\rceil}+1)(l-\lceil{\alpha\rceil})(\lceil{\alpha\rceil}-\alpha+1)^{-1}+\alpha
.
∎

For
\Gamma
hyperbolic and
d\in\mathcal{D}_{\Gamma}
, the lemma above has the following corollary.

Corollary 9.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be non-elementary hyperbolic and
d\in\mathcal{D}_{\Gamma}
be
\alpha
-roughly geodesic. Then there exists a constant
\beta>0
depending only on
d
such that for any
l>\delta>2(\lceil\alpha\rceil+\alpha+2)
we have

l^{-1}(\log(\# S_l)-\beta)\leq v_{d}\leq(l-\lceil\alpha\rceil)^{-1}\log(\# S_l).

Proof.

Given a (symmetric) finite generating set
S\subset\Gamma
, let
v_{S}
denote the exponential growth rate of the word metric
d_{S}
. Then we have the immediate bound

(9.4)

v_{S}\leq\log(\# S).

On the other hand, by
[
2
, Theorem 1]
there exists a constant
\beta
depending only on
\Gamma
, such that for any such
S
we have

(9.5)

v_{S}\geq\log(\# S)-\beta.

Now, given
l>\delta>2(\lceil\alpha\rceil+\alpha+2)
,
from
Lemma

9.2
we obtain the inequalities

l^{-1}v_{S_{l}}\leq v_{d}\leq(l-\lceil\alpha\rceil)^{-1}v_{S_{l}}.

These inequalities together with
Equation

9.4
and
Equation

9.5
imply the corollary.
∎

We continue by giving an upper bound for Green metrics in terms of the word metrics defined by their supports. If
d_{\mu}
is the Green metric on
\Gamma
associated to the probability measure
\mu
, we have the formula

d_{\mu}(g,h)=-\log\left(\frac{G_{\mu}(g,h)}{G_{\mu}(o,o)}\right)\quad\text{ for }g,h\in\Gamma,

where
G_{\mu}(g,h)=\sum_{k\geq 0}{\mu^{\ast k}(g^{-1}h)}
is the
Green function
and
\mu^{\ast k}
denotes the
k
th-convolution of
\mu

[
5
, p. 686]
.

Lemma 9.4
.

Let
\Gamma
be an infinite finitely generated group that is not virtually
\mathbb{Z}
or
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
and let
S\subset\Gamma
be a finite set generating
\Gamma
as a semi-group. If
\mu=\mu_{S}
denotes the uniform probability measure supported on
S
, then for all
g\in\Gamma
we have

d_{\mu}(o,g)\leq\log\left(\#S\right)|g|_{S}+\log\left(G_{\mu}(o,o)(1-(\#S)^{-1})\right).

Proof.

The assumptions on
\Gamma
imply that
G_{\mu}(o,o)
is finite and
\#S\geq 2
. Then for an arbitrary
g\in\Gamma
we have

\displaystyle e^{-d_{\mu}(o,g)}

\displaystyle=G_{\mu}(o,o)^{-1}G_{\mu}(o,g)=G_{\mu}(o,o)^{-1}\cdot\sum_{k\geq 0}{\mu^{\ast k}(g)}

\displaystyle=G_{\mu}(o,o)^{-1}\cdot\sum_{k\geq|g|_{S}}{\mu^{\ast k}(g)}

\displaystyle\geq G_{\mu}(o,o)^{-1}\cdot\sum_{k\geq|g|_{S}}{\frac{1}{(\#S)^{k}}}=G_{\mu}(o,o)^{-1}\frac{(\#S)^{-|g|_{S}}}{1-(\#S)^{-1}}.

This last inequality implies the lemma.
∎

Our next proposition is the key inequality in the proof of
Theorem

9.1
, and relies crucially on the hyperbolicity of
\Gamma
.

Proposition 9.5
.

Let
\Gamma
be a non-elementary hyperbolic group and consider
d\in\mathcal{D}_{\Gamma}
. Then there exists
\delta_{0}
satisfying the following. Let
\delta>\delta_{0}
and for
l
large enough define
S_{l}=S_{l}^{\delta}:=\{g\in\Gamma\colon l-\delta<d(o,g)\leq l\}
. Then
S_{l}
generates
\Gamma
(as a group) and if
\mu_{l}
is the uniform probability measure supported on
S_{l}
, then there exists
\kappa_{l}>0
such that for all
g\in\Gamma
we have

(9.6)

d_{\mu_{l}}(o,g)\geq\frac{v_{d}d(o,g)}{2}+|g|_{S_{l}}\cdot\left(\log\left(\frac{\#S_{l}}{D_{0}\left(\frac{l}{\delta}+1\right)}\right)-\frac{v_{d}l}{2}\right)-\kappa_{l}.

Proof.

Given
l,n,\delta>0
and
g\in\Gamma
, we consider the set

\mathcal{O}_{l}^{\delta}(g,n)=\{(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})\in(S_{l}^{\delta})^{n}\colon g_{1}\cdots g_{n}=g\}.

From
[
39
, Lemma B.3]
we can find
\delta_{0}^{\prime}>0
such that if
l>\delta>\delta_{0}^{\prime}
, then there exists
C=C_{\delta}>0
satisfying

(9.7)

\#\mathcal{O}_{l}(g,n)\leq C^{n}\left(\frac{l}{\delta}+1\right)^{n}e^{v_{d}\left(\frac{nl-d(o,g)}{2}\right)}

for all
g\in\Gamma
. The proof of
[
39
, Lemma B.3]
assumes that
d
is induced by a geometric action of
\Gamma
on a geodesic metric space
X
. However, cocompactness can be relaxed to coboundedness and the space

only requires to be roughly geodesic. Therefore, we can apply that lemma to the left action of
\Gamma
on
X=(\Gamma,d)
.

Now we set
\delta_{0}:=\max\{2(\lceil{\alpha\rceil}+\alpha+2),\delta_{0}^{\prime}\}+1
, where
\alpha
is a rough geodesic constant for
d
. Hence
Lemma

9.2
implies that
S_{l}=S_{l}^{\delta}
generates
\Gamma
for
\delta>\delta_{0}
. Moreover, for any
l,n>0
and
g\in\Gamma
we have

\mu_{l}^{\ast n}(g)=\sum_{(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})\in\mathcal{O}_{l}(g,n)}{\mu_{l}(g_{1})\cdots\mu_{l}(g_{n})}=\#\mathcal{O}_{l}(g,n)(\#S_{l})^{-n}.

Let
C=C_{\delta}
be the constant from
Equation

9.7
. By
Corollary

9.3
we have that
\log(\# S_l)/l
converges to
v_{d}
as

tends to infinity, so that for all
l

C(\frac{l}{\delta}+1)e^{\frac{v_{d}l}{2}}(\#S_{l})^{-1}<1
. Therefore, there exists
\kappa=\kappa_{l}>0
such that for all
l
large enough and all
g\in\Gamma
we have

\displaystyle e^{-d_{\mu_{l}}(o,g)}

\displaystyle=G_{\mu_{l}}(o,o)^{-1}\cdot\sum_{n\geq|g|_{S_{l}}}{\mu_{l}^{\ast n}(g)}

\displaystyle\leq G_{\mu_{l}}(o,o)^{-1}\cdot\sum_{n\geq|g|_{S_{l}}}{C^{n}\left(\frac{l}{\delta}+1\right)^{n}e^{v_{d}\left(\frac{nl-d(o,g)}{2}\right)}(\#S_{l})^{-n}}

\displaystyle\leq e^{\kappa_{l}}e^{\frac{v_{d}d(o,g)}{2}}{\left(C\left(\frac{l}{\delta}+1\right)e^{\frac{v_{d}l}{2}}(\#S_{l})^{-1}\right)^{|g|_{S_{l}}}}.

After rearranging, this inequality becomes
Equation

9.6
.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

9.1
.

Let
d\in\mathcal{D}_{\Gamma}
, which we assume to be
\alpha
-roughly geodesic. We fix
\delta>\max(\delta_{0},2(\lceil\alpha\rceil+\alpha+2)),
so that both

9.2
and

9.5
apply. For

large enough and

\displaystyle d_{\mu_{l}}(o,g)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{v_{d}d(o,g)}{2}+|g|_{S_{l}}\cdot\left(\log\left(\frac{\#S_{l}}{C\left(\frac{l}{\delta}+1\right)}\right)-\frac{v_{d}l}{2}\right)-\kappa_{l}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{v_{d}d(o,g)}{2}+d(o,g)\cdot\left(\frac{\log(\# S_l)}{l}-\frac{\log(C\left(\frac{l}{\del}+1\right))}{l}-\frac{v_{d}}{2}\right)-\kappa_{l}

\displaystyle=d(o,g)\cdot\left(\frac{\log(\# S_l)}{l}-\frac{\log(C\left(\frac{l}{\del}+1\right))}{l}\right)-\kappa_{l}.

In particular, we obtain

(9.8)

{\rm Dil}(d,d_{\mu_{l}})\leq\left(\frac{\log{\# S_l}}{l}-\frac{\log(C(\frac{l}{\del}+1))}{l}\right)^{-1},

and the right hand side of

10.
Questions

We conclude by formulating several natural problems.

10.1.
Sharpening the dynamical–metric dictionary

Our dictionary identifies a correspondence between geometric and dynamical structures. A natural next step is to sharpen this correspondence by relating intrinsic dynamical properties of a reparameterization (or continuous potential) to geometric properties of the associated hyperbolic metric potential.

For instance, recall from
Example

4.12
and
Theorem

5.5
that any strongly hyperbolic metric
d\in\mathcal{D}_{\Gamma}
induces a Hölder continuous positive potential
F:\mathsf{U}\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}
. This raises the following question:

Question 10.1
.

Given a positive Hölder continuous potential
F:\mathsf{U}\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, does it have a dual
strongly hyperbolic metric potential

\psi\in\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{++}
? That is, we require
\psi
to be a (non-necessarily symmetric) pseudometric such that for some
\varepsilon>0
we have

e^{-\varepsilon\langle x\mid z\rangle^{\psi}_{w}}\leq e^{-\varepsilon\langle x\mid y\rangle_{w}^{\psi}}+e^{-\varepsilon\langle y\mid z\rangle_{w}^{\psi}}\quad\text{ for all }x,y,z,w\in\Gamma.

As established by our dictionary, every continuous Bowen potential determines a dual hyperbolic metric potential, and in particular has
C^{1}
Manhattan curves. This naturally leads to the question of whether the converse holds.

Question 10.2
.

Is every hyperbolic metric potential in
{\mathcal{H}}_{\Gamma}
with
C^{1}
Manhattan curves dual to a continuous Bowen potential?

In Lemma
8.2
we established a necessary condition for an element in
\MC({\mathcal{H}}_{\Gamma}^{++})
to lie in
{\mathcal{H}}_{\Gamma}^{++}
. A natural problem is to determine whether this condition can be strengthened to a characterization.

Question 10.3
.

Are the points in
{\mathcal{H}}_{\Gamma}^{++}
precisely those
\psi\in\MC({\mathcal{H}}_{\Gamma}^{++})
whose Manhattan curves are
C^{1}
and which admit a corresponding continuous potential with unique equilibrium states?

10.2.
Geometry and topology of the space of metric potentials

As discussed in the introduction, the study of the space of reparameterizations of Anosov and metric-Anosov flows permeates a wide range of geometric contexts. This naturally leads to the problem of refining the structure of the corresponding geometric space.

For instance, in the setting of surface groups, the space of reparameterizations was studied by Tholozan
[
96
]
, building on earlier work of Sullivan
[
93
]
. In the Hölder setting, he established a bijective correspondence with several other spaces, including that of
Hölder foliated conformal structures
. A natural problem is to extend this correspondence to the full space of reparameterizations using the methods developed here.

Question 10.4
.

Let
\Gamma
be a hyperbolic surface group. Does Tholozan’s bijection between Hölder foliated conformal structures and Hölder reparameterizations for the geodesic flow extend to the full space of reparameterizations?

In Theorem
1.4
, we showed that the metric
\Delta
on
{H}_{\Gamma}^{++}
is incomplete by constructing an element of
\MC({H}_{\Gamma}^{++})
dual to a potential with uncountably many equilibrium states. A natural question is whether these examples can be leveraged to produce symmetric potentials.

Question 10.5
.

Is
{D}_{\Gamma}
equipped with
\Delta
non-complete?

We showed that the map
\mathsf{BMS}\colon{H}_{\Gamma}^{++}\to\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}
is a continuous injection (Theorem
B
), and that it restricts to an embedding on Hitchin representations (
Proposition

1.6
). It is an interesting problem to determine whether it is an embedding more generally.

Question 10.6
.

Is
\mathsf{BMS}\colon{H}_{\Gamma}^{++}\to\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}
a topological embedding?

10.3.
Green metrics

A key ingredient in the construction of our dictionary (Theorem
5.5
) is Theorem
C
, whose proof relies on approximating by uniform probability measures supported on spheres. This can likely be strengthened by allowing the approximating measures to be supported on balls.

Question 10.7
.

Let
\Gamma
be a non-elementary hyperbolic group and let
d\in\mathcal{D}_{\Gamma}
. For
l\geq 0
, let
\mu_{l}
be the uniform probability measure on the ball
B_{l}=\{g\in\Gamma\colon d(o,g)\leq l\}
. Does
[d_{\mu_{l}}]\to[d]
in
{D}_{\Gamma}
?

Theorem
C
yields a bijection between
\Par^{s}(\mathsf{U}\Gamma,\mathsf{g})
and
\MC({D}_{\Gamma})
. Establishing density of asymmetric Green metrics in
{H}^{++}_{\Gamma}
would upgrade the injection in Theorem
C
(1) to a bijection.

Question 10.8
.

Is the set of hyperbolic metric potentials
\psi_{\mu}
arising from admissible non-symmetric probability measures on
\Gamma
dense in
{H}^{++}_{\Gamma}
?

More generally, one may ask whether the admissibility hypothesis in Theorem
C
can be removed; see
[
53
, Lemma 2.13]
.

Question 10.9
.

For which sequences of probability measures
\mu_{l}
does one have
[d_{\mu_{l}}]\to[d]
for a given
d\in{D}_{\Gamma}
? Is convergence of the associated hitting measures
\nu_{l}
to a quasi-conformal measure for
d
sufficient?

Beyond hyperbolic groups, the Green metric still reflects the large-scale behavior of some “large” groups. For instance, let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated non-amenable group and
d_{\mu}
be the Green metric on
\Gamma
associated to a probability measure
\mu
whose support is finite, symmetric, and generates
\Gamma
. Then
d_{\mu}
is quasi-isometric to any word metric on
\Gamma
(with respect to a finite generating set)
[
3
, p. 328]
. Under these conditions on
\Gamma
we wonder whether there are enough Green metrics to approximate word metrics in the following sense.

Question 10.10
.

Suppose
\Gamma
is a finitely generated non-amenable group and let
d
be a word metric on
\Gamma
associated to a symmetric finite generating set. Does there exist a sequence
\mu_{l}
of probability measures on
\Gamma
such that for the corresponding Green metrics
d_{\mu_{l}}
we can find constants
\lambda^{+}_{l},\lambda_{l}^{-},A_{l}>0
with
\lambda^{+}_{l}/\lambda_{l}^{-}\to 1
as
l\to\infty
and such that

\lambda_{l}^{-}d_{\mu_{l}}(g,h)-A_{l}\leq d(g,h)\leq\lambda_{l}^{+}d_{\mu_{l}}(g,h)+A_{l}

for all
g,h\in\Gamma
and
l
.

For hyperbolic groups, the convergence above is equivalent to
[d_{\mu_{l}}]
converging to
[d]
in
{D}_{\Gamma}

[
21
, Theorem 1.1]
.

10.4.
Geodesic currents on surfaces

As described in
Section

7.2
, the
\mathsf{BMS}
map yields an embedding of the Hitchin components (up to contragradient) of a surface group
\Gamma
into the space of geodesic currents. The image of this embedding lies on the set
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{s}
of symmetric projective currents. By
[
70
]
, for a point
[\rho]\in\Hit_{\Gamma}^{n}
there is an asymmetric
Liouville
geodesic current
\omega_{\rho}\in\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{s}
defined in terms of cross ratios. This defines an injective map
\omega:\Hit_{\Gamma}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}
for any
n\geq 2
.

Question 10.11
.

Is the Liouville geodesic current map
