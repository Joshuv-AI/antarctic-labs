1.
Introduction

In this paper, all rings considered are commutative, Noetherian, local with unity and all modules considered are finitely generated, unless staed otherwise. We use terminology from
[
6
]
. Unless otherwise specified,
A
denotes a local ring with unique maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
and residue field
k
. The aim of this paper is to study the degree of Hilbert polynomials of torsion and extension functors. If
X
is an
A
-module of finite length, we denote its length by
\lambda(X)
. Let
M,N
be finitely generated
A
-modules
I
an ideal of
A
. It is well known that, if the lengths
\lambda(M/I^{n}M)
are finite for all sufficiently large
n
, then they are given by a polynomial function in
n
, called the
Hilbert–Samuel polynomial
, whose degree equals
\dim M
. Let
i\geq 1
be an integer. In his paper, Kodiylam proved a generalized version of this result, by showing that
\lambda(\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{A}(M,N/I^{n}N))
is eventually polynomial, provided
\lambda(M\otimes N)
is finite; see
[
17
, Theorem 2]
. Later, Theodorescu proved that, if the lengths
\lambda(\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{A}(M,N/I^{n+1}N))
and
\lambda(\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{A}(M,N/I^{n+1}N))
are finite for all sufficiently large
n
, then these lengths are eventually given by polynomial functions in
n
. Moreover, he derived bounds for the degrees of the corresponding Hilbert–Samuel polynomials and provided a condition under which these bounds are attained; see
[
29
, Corollary 4]
. However, verifying whether this condition holds is often a difficult task. Also the degrees of the corresponding Hilbert-Samuel polynomials are not as easy to determine; see
[
14
]
,
[
16
]
. There are some results which show under certain contains the maximal degree is attained; see
[
11
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
19
]
and
[
25
]
. On the other hand, this function can also be identically zero; see
[
19
, Remark 20]
,
[
25
, Example 2.6]
. In this paper, we improve the existing bounds, characterize the cases in which they are achieved, and analyze the situations where they fail to be attained.

Recall, a non-zero
A
-module
N
is said to be
perfect
if
\operatorname{pdim}_{A}N=\min\{j|\operatorname{Ext}^{j}_{A}(M,A)\neq 0\}
. Note that, if the ring
A
is Cohen-Macaulay, then
N
is perfect if and only if
N
is a Cohen-Macaulay module and
\operatorname{pdim}_{A}N<\infty
(see
[
6
, Theorem 2.1.5]
).

Throughout this paper we work with the following setup, unless stated otherwise.

Setup 1.1
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a non-regular Gorenstein local of dimension
d\geq 1
, with the residue field
A/\mathfrak{m}=k

N
be a perfect
A
-module of dimension

and

an ideal of definition of

-module

\displaystyle r_{i}^{I,N}(M)=\deg(n\mapsto\lambda(\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{A}(M,N/I^{n}N))),

\displaystyle s^{i}_{I,N}(M)=\deg(n\mapsto\lambda(\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{A}(M,N/I^{n}N))).

Recall
A
is said to be a

ring if its completion
\widehat{A}=Q/(f)
where
(Q,\mathfrak{n})
be a regular local ring and

is non-zero. Motivated by the above discussion, the second author investigated the degrees of the corresponding Hilbert–Samuel polynomials over hypersurface rings. More precisely, he proved the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

[
23
, Theorem 9.12]
Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a hypersurface local ring,
N
a perfect
A
-module and
I
an ideal of definition

. Then there exist integers

depending only on

and

-module

(i)

,

(ii)

,

(iii)

.

He has also explored the case when

is complete intersection (see
[
21
, Theorem 1.1]
).

Our motivation is to investigate these results over Gorenstein rings. Our first objective is to improve the known estimates for the numerical invariants
r_{i}^{I,N}(M)
and
s_{I,N}^{i}(M)
. To this end, we show

Theorem I.
(Theorem
3.3
) [With hypothesis as in
1.1
]
Let
M
be a MCM
A
-module
M
. Then for an integer
i\geq 1
, we have

(i)

r_{i}^{I,N}(\Omega^{d}k)=r_{1}^{I,N}(\Omega^{d}k)
,

(ii)

s^{i}_{I,N}(\Omega^{d}k)=s^{1}_{I,N}(\Omega^{d}k)
,

(iii)

r_{1}^{I,N}(\Omega^{d}k)=s^{1}_{I,N}(\Omega^{d}k)
,

(iv)

r_{i}^{I,N}(M)\leq r_{1}^{I,N}(\Omega^{d}k)\leq t-1
,

(v)

s^{i}_{I,N}(M)\leq s^{1}_{I,N}(\Omega^{d}k)\leq t-1
.

As a consequence of the preceding theorem, the MCM
A
-module
\Omega^{d}k
dominates all other MCM
A
-modules in terms of the degrees of the associated Hilbert-Samuel polynomials. It is therefore natural to ask what happens when one considers the higher syzygies and cosyzygies of

\Omega^{d}k
. We use spectral sequences and techniques from MCM approximation (see
[

Theorem II.
(Theorem
4.4
) [With hypothesis as in
1.1
]
Let

be an integer, then we have

(i)

r_{1}^{I,N}(\Omega^{d}k)=r_{1}^{I,N}(\Omega^{n}\Omega^{d}k)
,

(ii)

s^{1}_{I,N}(\Omega^{n}\Omega^{d}k)=s^{1}_{I,N}(\Omega^{d}k).

We next show that, in the case

, the inequalities of

become equalities.

Theorem III.
(Theorem
5.2
,
5.3
) [With hypothesis as in
1.1
]
Let
M
be a non-free MCM
A
-module. Then for any integer

, we have
r_{i}^{\mathfrak{m},N}(M)=t-1=s^{i}_{\mathfrak{m},N}(M)
.

We exhibit an example (see Remark

\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideals.

Next, we study the stable category MCM
A
-modules
\operatorname{\underline{CM}}(A)
and various thick subcategories of

[

]
for notation on triangulated categories. For a MCM

-module

-module. More precisely, we prove

Theorem IV.
(Theorem
6.11
)
Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})

\geq 1
and

-module of dimension
\geq 1
and

We continue our discussion by considering the case where the bound in
Theorem III
is not attained. To this end, we take the perfect module
N
to be
A
itself. Let
G_{I}(A)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}I^{n}/I^{n+1}
be the
associated graded ring
of

with respect to

and

of

with respect to

, considered as a graded

-module. Then for each integer

, we have
H^{i}_{G_{I}(A)_{+}}(G_{I}(M))_{n}=0

), where

. Set

. For any arbitrary Noetherian local ring

Recall that for a maximal Cohen–Macaulay

-module

is defined as the intersection of all thick subcategories of

that contain

Theorem V.
(Theorem
7.7
)
Let
(A,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a non-regular Gorenstein local ring of dimension

and
I

\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal. Let

be a MCM

-module such that

-module

We also examine the case when

has a unique graded maximal ideal

Theorem VI.
(Theorem

If

Definition 1.3
.

We now recall two definitions.

(i)

An
A
-module

is said to be a

, if for all

-modules

have finite projective dimension.

(ii)

We note that test modules are abundant; see

Theorem VII.
(Theorem
8.4
) [With hypothesis be as in

]
Assume further that the residue field

More generally, if

Techniques used to prove the results:
Most of the results in this paper are established by induction on
t=\dim N
. The case
t=1
serves as the foundation of our approach, while the higher-dimensional cases are handled using the well-known technique of common superficial and filter-regular elements. For the base case, we make use the notion of the cosyzygy of a MCM module over a Gorenstein rings. We study the associated graded module
G_{I}(M)
via the
\mathcal{R}(I)
-module
L^{I}(M)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}M/I^{n+1}M
. Recall a graded module
T
over a graded ring
R
is said to be
*-Artinian
if every descending chain of graded submodules of
T
terminates. The second author in his paper
[
20
]
proved that
L^{I}(M)
is a module over the Rees algebra
\mathcal{R}(I)
(not finitely generated)
and the local cohomology modules
H^{i}_{\mathfrak{M}}(L^{I}(M))
is *-Artinian for
0\leq j\leq 0ptM-1
, where
\mathfrak{M}=\mathfrak{m}\oplus\mathcal{R}(I)_{+}
, the *maximal ideal of
\mathcal{R}(I)
(see
[
20
, Proposition 4.4]
). For some properties of *-Aritinian modules we refer to
[
20
, 1.10]
. We use the short exact sequence of
\mathcal{R}(I)
-modules

0\longrightarrow G_{I}(M)\longrightarrow L^{I}(M)\longrightarrow L^{I}(M)(-1)\longrightarrow 0

to have control over the numbers
a_{j}(G_{I}(M))
for
j=0,\ldots,0ptM-1
.

Setup 2.1
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m},k)
be Gorenstein local ring, which is not regular. Assume
N
is a perfect module of dimension
t\geq 1
and
I
is an ideal of definition of

. For a non-free MCM
A
-module
M
and a positive integer

\displaystyle r_{i}^{I,N}(M)=\deg(n\mapsto\lambda(\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{A}(M,N/I^{n}N))),

\displaystyle s^{i}_{I,N}(M)=\deg(n\mapsto\lambda(\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{A}(M,N/I^{n}N))).

When

{\operatorname{i^{t}h}}

Remark 2.2

Let

be an integer. Then we have

\displaystyle r_{i}^{I,N}(M)=r_{i-1}^{I,N}(\Omega M),

\displaystyle s^{i}_{I,N}(M)=s^{i-1}_{I,N}(\Omega M).

Therefore, often we can assume

.

2.3
.

If the residue field

of

is finite,
we make use of the following faithfully flat extension

(A,\mathfrak{m},k)\longrightarrow(A[X]_{\mathfrak{m}A[x]},\mathfrak{m}A[X]_{\mathfrak{m}A[x]},k(X))

to assume the residue field of

is infinite. We also consider the extension

in order to obtain an uncountable residue field.

2.4
.

Cosyzygy:

Let
M
be a non-free MCM over a Gorenstein local ring
(A,\mathfrak{m})
. We will define the
cosyzygy
of
M
. Set
(-)^{*}=\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(-,A)
. Consider a minimal presentation

0\longrightarrow\Omega(M^{*})\longrightarrow A^{\tau}\longrightarrow M^{*}\longrightarrow 0
of
M^{*}
, where
\tau=\operatorname{type}(M)
. Since
M
is MCM and
A
is Gorenstein, therefore we have
M^{**}\cong M
and
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{i}(M^{*},A)=0
for
i\geq 1
(see
[
6
, Theorem 3.3.10]
). Hence applying the contra-variant functor
(-)^{*}
, we obtain the following exact sequence
0\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow A^{\tau}\longrightarrow(\Omega(M^{*}))^{*}\longrightarrow 0
. We set
\Omega^{-1}M:=(\Omega(M^{*}))^{*}
, it is called the
1^{\operatorname{st}}
-
cosyzygy
of
M
. Recursively, we can define the
{\operatorname{i^{t}h}}
-
cosyzygy
of

as
\Omega^{-i}(M):=\Omega^{-1}(\Omega^{-(i-1)}(M))
.
Note that
\Omega^{i}M
is non-free MCM for any integer

and
\Omega\Omega^{-1}M\cong M
.

Next, we establish a few results that will be used in the subsequent discussion.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a
d
-dimensional Gorenstein local ring and
M
a MCM
A
-module and
N
a finite
A
-module with finite projective dimension (equivalently, finite injective dimension), then for any positive integer
i
, we have
\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{A}(M,N)=0=\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{i}(M,N).

Proof.

For
i\geq 1
, we have
\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{A}(M,N)=\operatorname{Tor}_{i+d}^{A}(\Omega^{-d}M,N)=0
. As
A
is Gorenstein and
\operatorname{pdim}_{A}N<\infty
, we get
\operatorname{idim}_{A}N<\infty
(see
[
6
, Exercise 3.1.25]
). So for
i\geq 1
, we have
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{i}(M,N)=\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{i+d}(\Omega^{-d}M,N)=0.

∎

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be Gorenstein local ring and
M
a MCM
A
-module and
N
a perfect module of dimension
t
. Then
M\otimes_{A}N
and
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N)
are Cohen-Macaulay
A
-module of dimension
t
.

Proof.

If
\dim A=0
, then the assertions are trivial. Let
\dim A\geq 1
. We note that if
M,N
are nonzero then so is
M\otimes_{A}N
. Also note that
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N)=0
if and only if
\operatorname{ann}_{A}M
contains an
N
-regular element. Since
\operatorname{pdim}_{A}N<\infty
, so by new intersection theorem, an
N
-regular element is also
A
-regular (see
[
6
, Theorem 9.4.7 and Remark 9.4.8]
). As
M
is MCM, it implies that
0pt(\operatorname{ann}_{A}M,A)=\operatorname{ht}(\operatorname{ann}_{A}M)=0
. So
\operatorname{ann}_{A}(M)
does not contain an
N
-regular element and hence
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N)\neq 0
.

We prove the results by induction on
t=\dim N
. When
t=0
, there is nothing to prove. Assume
t\geq 1
. Let
x\in\mathfrak{m}
be
N
-regular. Then
N/xN
is a perfect module of dimension
t-1
. By
2.5
we have

\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{A}(M,N/xN)=0=\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{1}(M,N/xN)
. The exact sequence

0\longrightarrow N\xlongrightarrow{x}N\longrightarrow N/xN\longrightarrow 0

yields the following two exact sequences

0\longrightarrow M\otimes_{A}N\xlongrightarrow{x}M\otimes_{A}N\longrightarrow M\otimes_{A}(N/xN)\longrightarrow 0,

0\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N)\xlongrightarrow{x}\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N/xN)\longrightarrow 0,

which implies that
x
is both
M\otimes_{A}N
and
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N)
-regular and

\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,\frac{N}{xN})\cong\frac{\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N)}{x\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N)}.

Also
M\otimes_{A}N/xN
and
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N/xN)
are Cohen-Macaulay of dimension (
t-1
) by induction hypothesis. The result follows.
∎

r_{1}^{I,N}(\Omega^{d}k)
and
r_{i}^{I,N}(M)
is
-1
. Let
x\in I
be such that it is
N\oplus((\Omega^{d}k\oplus M)\otimes N)
-superficial and
xt
is
L_{p}^{N}(M\oplus\Omega^{d}k)
-filter regular for
p=1,i,i-1
. Using the short exact sequence in
2.16
, we obtain

\displaystyle r_{1}^{I,N/xN}(M)=r_{1}^{I,N}(M)-1,

\displaystyle r_{1}^{I,N/xN}(\Omega^{d}k)=r_{1}^{I,N}(\Omega^{d}k)-1.

Since
N/xN
is perfect of dimension
t-1
, so by induction hypothesis the result follows.

(v) is proved in similar lines as in (iv).
∎

4.
Proof of Theorem II.

In this section we give a proof of Theorem II. Before proceeding, we establish a few preliminary results that will be essential for the proof of our theorem.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a
d
-dimensional Gorenstein local ring and
M
a finite
A
-module of depth
s
and dimension
r
. Let
i\geq 1
be an integer such that
\{i,i+1\}\cap[d-r,d-s]=\emptyset
, then for any
A
-module
N
(not necessarily finitely generated), we have
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{A}(M,N)\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{A}(\Omega M,\Omega N)
.

Proof.

Apply
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(-,\Omega N)
to the exact sequence
0\longrightarrow\Omega M\longrightarrow A^{\mu(M)}\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow 0
to obtain

\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{A}(\Omega M,\Omega N)\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{i+1}_{A}(M,\Omega N).

Next, we apply
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,-)
to the exact sequence
0\longrightarrow\Omega N\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow 0
, where
G
is free, to obtain the exact sequence

\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{A}(M,G)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{A}(M,N)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{i+1}_{A}(M,\Omega N)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{i+1}_{A}(M,G).

By our assumption on
i
, we have
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{A}(M,G)=0=\operatorname{Ext}^{i+1}_{A}(M,G)
(see
[
6
, Corollary 3.5.11]
), therefore we get

\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{A}(M,N)\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{i+1}_{A}(M,\Omega N).

The result follows.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a
d
-dimensional non-regular Gorenstein local ring and
E
a MCM
A
-module. Suppose for an integer
n
, if we have
\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{A}(\Omega^{n}\Omega^{d}k,E)=0
, then
E
is free.

Proof.

The assertion is trivial for
n\geq 0
. Assume
n<0
. There are isomorphisms in the derived category of
A
:
\mathbf{R}\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(X\otimes_{A}^{\mathbf{L}}M,A)\cong\mathbf{R}\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(X,M^{*})
and
\mathbf{R}\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(\mathbf{R}\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(X,M^{*}),A)\cong X\otimes_{A}^{\mathbf{L}}\mathbf{R}\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M^{*},A)\cong X\otimes_{A}^{\mathbf{L}}M
(see
[
9
, A.4.21 and A.4.24]
). These gives rise to a spectral sequence

E_{2}^{p,q}=\operatorname{Ext}^{p}_{A}(\operatorname{Tor}_{q}^{A}(\Omega^{n}\Omega^{d}k,E),A)\implies H^{p+q}=\operatorname{Ext}^{p+q}_{A}(\Omega^{n}\Omega^{d}k,E^{*}).

Since
\Omega^{n}\Omega^{d}k
is locally free on the punctured spectrum, so
\lambda(\operatorname{Tor}_{q}^{A}(\Omega^{-1}\Omega^{d}k,E))<\infty
for
q>0
. So by
[
6
, Corollary 3.5.11]
, we have
\operatorname{Ext}^{p}_{A}(\operatorname{Tor}_{q}^{A}(\Omega^{n}\Omega^{d}k,E),A)=0
for
p\neq d
and
q\neq 0
. So the above spectral sequence collapses at
2^{\operatorname{nd}}
-page. By hypothesis,
\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{A}(\Omega^{n}\Omega^{d}k,E)=0
, thus we obtain

\operatorname{Ext}^{d+1}_{A}(\Omega^{n}\Omega^{d}k,E^{*})=0
. By repeatedly applying
4.1
, we obtain (as
n<0
)

0=\operatorname{Ext}^{d+1}_{A}(\Omega^{n}\Omega^{d}k,E^{*})=\operatorname{Ext}^{d+1}_{A}(\Omega^{-n}\Omega^{n}\Omega^{d}k,\Omega^{-n}E^{*})=\operatorname{Ext}^{d+1}_{A}(\Omega^{d}k,\Omega^{-n}E^{*}).

So we have
\operatorname{Ext}^{2d+1}_{A}(k,\Omega^{-n}E^{*})=0
, which implies that
\Omega^{-n}E^{*}
is free, and thus
E^{*}
is free and so is
E^{**}\cong E
.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a
d
-dimensional non-regular Gorenstein local ring,
E
a finite
A
-module. Suppose for some integer
n
, one of the following two conditions holds:

(i)

\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{A}(\Omega^{n}\Omega^{d}k,E)=0
,

(ii)

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{A}(\Omega^{n}\Omega^{d}k,E)=0
,

then we have
\operatorname{pdim}_{A}(E)<\infty
.

Proof.

The assertions are trivial for
n\geq 0
. We assume
n<0
.

(i) By a maximal Cohen-Macaulay approximation of
E
, we have an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow Y\longrightarrow X\longrightarrow E\longrightarrow 0,

where
X
is MCM and
\operatorname{pdim}_{A}(Y)<\infty
(see
[
2
, Theorem A]
). Tensoring this exact sequence with
\Omega^{n}\Omega^{d}k
, we obtain

\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{A}(\Omega^{n}\Omega^{d}k,Y)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{A}(\Omega^{n}\Omega^{d}k,X)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{A}(\Omega^{n}\Omega^{d}k,E).

As
\operatorname{pdim}_{A}(Y)<\infty
, so by
2.5
we get
\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{A}(\Omega^{n}\Omega^{d}k,Y)=0
which implies
\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{A}(\Omega^{n}\Omega^{d}k,X)=0
. Since
X
is MCM, so by
4.2
we have
X
is free and hence
\operatorname{pdim}_{A}(E)<\infty
.

(ii) We apply
4.1
repeatedly to obtain (as
n<0
)

0=\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{A}(\Omega^{n}\Omega^{d}k,E)=\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{A}(\Omega^{-n}\Omega^{n}\Omega^{d}k,\Omega^{-n}E)=\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{A}(k,\Omega^{-n}E),

so we have
\operatorname{pdim}_{A}(\Omega^{-n}E)<\infty
, and thus
\operatorname{pdim}_{A}(E)<\infty
.
∎

With the preceding results in place, we can now prove Theorem II.

Theorem 4.4
.

(with hypothesis as in
3.1
) Let
n
be an integer, then we have

(i)

r_{1}^{I,N}(\Omega^{d}k)=r_{1}^{I,N}(\Omega^{n}\Omega^{d}k)
,

(ii)

s^{1}_{I,N}(\Omega^{n}\Omega^{d}k)=s^{1}_{I,N}(\Omega^{d}k).

Proof.

(i) If
n\geq 0
, the assertions follow from
3.3
. Assume
n<0
. We claim that

r_{1}^{I,N}(\Omega^{d}k)=-1\ \operatorname{iff}\ r_{1}^{I,N}(\Omega^{n}\Omega^{d}k)=-1.

The only if part directly follows from
3.2
. If
r_{1}^{I,N}(\Omega^{n}\Omega^{d}k)=-1
, then
\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{A}(\Omega^{n}\Omega^{d}k,N/I^{n}N)=0
for
n\gg 0
and so by
4.3
we have
\operatorname{pdim}_{A}(N/I^{n}N)<\infty
for
n\gg 0
. Thus by
2.5
we have
\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{A}(\Omega^{d}k,N/I^{n}N)=0
for
n\gg 0
, which implies
r_{1}^{I,N}(\Omega^{d}k)=-1
.

We apply induction on
t=\dim N
. The case t=1 follows from
2.10
together with the above argument. Assume
t\geq 2
. we can assume that none of
r_{1}^{I,N}(\Omega^{d}k)
and
r_{1}^{I,N}(\Omega^{n}\Omega^{d}k)
is
-1
. Let
x\in I
be such that it is
N\oplus((\Omega^{d}k\oplus\Omega^{n}\Omega^{d}k)\otimes N)
-superficial and
xt\in A[It]_{1}
is
L_{1}^{I,N}(\Omega^{d}k\oplus\Omega^{n}\Omega^{d}K)
-filter regular. Using the exact sequence in
2.16
, we obtain

r_{1}^{I,N/xN}(\Omega^{d}k)=r_{1}^{I,N}(\Omega^{d}k)-1,

r_{1}^{I,N/xN}(\Omega^{n}\Omega^{d}k)=r_{1}^{I,N}(\Omega^{n}\Omega^{d}k)-1.

Since
N/xN
is perfect of dimension
t-1
, hence by induction hypothesis the result follows.

(ii) is proved similarly.
∎

5.
The case when
I=\mathfrak{m}

We continue with the notation introduced in the previous sections.
In the preceding section, we proved that for every integer
i\geq 1
,

r_{i}^{I,N}(M)\leq\dim N-1\quad\text{and}\quad s_{i}^{I,N}(M)\leq\dim N-1.

In this section, we shall show that both of these inequalities become equalities when
I
is the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
. Using
2.3
we can assume the residue field
k
is infinite.

Setup 5.1
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m},k)
be Gorenstein local ring, which is not regular and
N
a perfect module of dimension
t\geq 1
. For any non-free MCM
A
-module
M
and a positive integer
i
, we set

\displaystyle r_{i}^{N}(M)=\deg(n\mapsto\lambda(\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{A}(M,N/\mathfrak{m}^{n}N))),

\displaystyle s^{i}_{N}(M)=\deg(n\mapsto\lambda(\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{A}(M,N/\mathfrak{m}^{n}N))).

Theorem 5.2
.

(With hypothesis as in
5.1
)
Let
M
be a non-free MCM
A
-module, then for any integer
i\geq 1
, we have
r_{i}^{N}(M)=t-1
.

Proof.

In view of
2.2
, it suffices to prove the statement for
i=1
. For this we apply induction on
t=\dim N
. Let
t=1
. By
3.3
we have
\lambda(\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{A}(M,N/\mathfrak{m}^{n}N))=c
for
n\gg 0
, where
c\geq 0
is a constant. We need to prove that
c>0
.

We tensor the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathfrak{m}^{n}N/\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}N\longrightarrow N/\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}N\longrightarrow N/\mathfrak{m}^{n}N\longrightarrow 0

with
M
to get the exact sequence

\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{A}(M,N/\mathfrak{m}^{n}N)\longrightarrow\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{n}N}{\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}N}\otimes M\longrightarrow\frac{M\otimes N}{\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}(M\otimes N)}\longrightarrow\frac{M\otimes N}{\mathfrak{m}^{n}(M\otimes N)}\longrightarrow 0.

Since
\dim N=1
, so
\mathfrak{m}^{n}N/\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}N\cong k^{e(N)}
. Therefore we have

\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{n}N}{\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}N}\otimes M\cong(\frac{M}{\mathfrak{m}M})^{e(N)}.

Computing lengths, for
n\gg 0
we have

c\geq\mu(M)e(N)-\lambda(\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{n}(M\otimes N)}{\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}(M\otimes N)}).

Since
\dim M\otimes N=1
(see
2.6
), so for
n\gg 0

\lambda(\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{n}(M\otimes N)}{\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}(M\otimes N)})=e(M\otimes N),

therefore we obtain

(1)

c\geq\mu(M)e(N)-e(M\otimes N).

Next we tensor the exact sequence
0\longrightarrow\Omega M\longrightarrow A^{\mu(M)}\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow 0

with
N
to get the exact sequence

0=\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{A}(M,N)\longrightarrow\Omega M\otimes N\longrightarrow N^{\mu(M)}\longrightarrow M\otimes N\longrightarrow 0.

By
2.6
all the above nonzero modules have dimension
1
, therefore we get

(2)

\mu(M)e(N)-e(M\otimes N)=e(\Omega M\otimes N)

Now
\dim\Omega M\otimes N=1
(see
2.6
), therefore
e(\Omega M\otimes N)>0
, and finally combining this with (
1
) and (
2
), we obtain

c\geq e(\Omega M\otimes N)>0.

So for
t=1
the result holds.

Assume
t\geq 2
. Let
x\in\mathfrak{m}
be such that it is
N\oplus(M\otimes N)
-superficial and
xt\in A[\mathfrak{m}t]_{1}
is
L_{1}^{N}(M)
-filter regular. Using the exact sequence in
2.16
we obtain
r_{1}^{N/xN}(M)=r_{1}^{N}(M)-1
. Since
N/xN
is perfect of dimension
t-1
, so by induction hypothesis the result follows.
∎

We now proceed to prove an analogous result for Ext.

Theorem 5.3
.

(With hypothesis as in
5.1
)
Let
M
be a non-free MCM
A
-module, then for any integer
i\geq 1
we have
s^{i}_{N}(M)=t-1
.

Proof.

In view of
2.2
it suffices to prove the statement for
i=1
. For this we apply induction on
t=\dim N
. Let
t=1
. We need to show
s^{1}_{N}(M)=0
. Suppose
s^{1}_{N}(M)<0
, then

(3)

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{A}(M,N/\mathfrak{m}^{n}N)=0\ \operatorname{for}n\gg 0.

Consider the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathfrak{m}^{n}N/\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}N\cong k^{e^{(}N)}\longrightarrow N/\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}N\longrightarrow N/\mathfrak{m}^{n}N\longrightarrow 0,

applying
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,-)
, we obtain for
n\gg 0

\begin{split}0\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(&M,k)^{e(N)}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N/\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}N)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N/\mathfrak{m}^{n}N)\\
&\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{A}(M,k)^{e(N)}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{A}(M,N/\mathfrak{m}^{n}N)=0\end{split}

Note that
\beta_{i}(M)=\dim_{k}\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{A}(M,k)=\lambda(\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{A}(M,k))
. Therefore computing lengths, for
n\gg 0
we have

(4)

\lambda(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N/\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}N))-\lambda(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N/\mathfrak{m}^{n}N))=e(N)(\mu(M)-\beta_{1}(M)).

Since
\dim\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N)=1=\dim N
(see
2.6
), so by
2.9
we have (
n\mapsto\lambda(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N/\mathfrak{m}^{n}N))
) is a polynomial function of degree
1
, say for
n\gg 0

(5)

\lambda(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N/\mathfrak{m}^{n}N))=c_{0}n+c_{1}

with
c_{0}>0
. We claim that
c_{0}=e(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N))
. Assume this for now. Then from (
4
) we have

(6)

e(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N))=e(N)(\mu(M)-\beta_{1}(M)).

Now we apply
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(-,N)
to the exact sequences

0\longrightarrow\Omega M\longrightarrow A^{\mu(M)}\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow 0,

0\longrightarrow\Omega^{2}M\longrightarrow A^{\beta_{1}(M)}\longrightarrow\Omega M\longrightarrow 0,

and use
2.5
to obtain the following exact sequences

0\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N)\longrightarrow N^{\mu(M)}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(\Omega M,N)\longrightarrow 0,

0\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(\Omega M,N)\longrightarrow N^{\beta_{1}(M)}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(\Omega^{2}M,N)\longrightarrow 0.

Since all nonzero modules in the above two exact sequences have dimension
1
, therefore we obtain

\mu(M)e(N)=e(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N))+e(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(\Omega M,N))

\beta_{1}(M)e(N)=e(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(\Omega M,N))+e(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(\Omega^{2}M,N))

Subtracting we get

e(N)(\mu(M)-\beta_{1}(M))=e(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N))-e(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(\Omega^{2}M,N)),

and from (
6
), we have
e(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(\Omega^{2}M,N))=0
, this is not possible, as
\dim\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(\Omega^{2}M,N)=1
. Hence
s^{1}_{N}(M)=0
.

Now we prove our claim that
c_{0}=e(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N))
. Let
x\in\mathfrak{m}
such that
x
is
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N)\oplus N
-superficial. So we have
\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}N:_{N}x=\mathfrak{m}^{n}N\operatorname{for}n\gg 0

and

(7)

e(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N))=e(\frac{\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N)}{x\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N)})=\lambda(\frac{\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N)}{x\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N)}).

Since
\dim N=1
, for
n\gg 0
we have an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow N/\mathfrak{m}^{n}N\xlongrightarrow{\phi_{n}}N/\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}N\longrightarrow N/xN\longrightarrow 0

where
\phi_{n}(\zeta+\mathfrak{m}^{n}N)=x\zeta+\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}N
for
\zeta\in N
. By applying the functor
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,-)
to the preceding short exact sequence and making use of (
3
), we deduce that the following sequence is exact for all

0\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N/\mathfrak{m}^{n}N)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N/\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}N)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N/xN)\longrightarrow 0.

Computing lengths and using (
5
) we obtain

(8)

c_{0}=\lambda(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N/xN)).

By assumption,
x
is a regular element on both
N
and
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N)
, therefore

(9)

\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,\frac{N}{xN})\cong\frac{\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N)}{x\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N)}.

Finally, putting together (
7
), (
8
) and (
9
), we conclude the proof of the claim.

So we have proved the assertion for the case
t=1
. Assume
t\geq 2
. Let
y\in\mathfrak{m}
be such that it is
N
-superficial and

is

-filter regular. Using the exact sequence in

we obtain

. Since

is perfect of dimension

, so by induction hypothesis the result follows.
∎

6.
The Stable category
\operatorname{\underline{CM}}(A)

Throughout this section we work with the following setup, unless stated otherwise.

6.1
.

Setup:
Let
(A,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a non-regular Gorenstein ring of dimension
d\geq 1
and
N
a perfect module of dimension
t\geq 1
and
I
is an ideal of definition of
N
.

6.2
.

For a MCM
A
-module
M
, we define

r^{I,N}_{\infty}(M):=\operatorname{sup}\{r^{I,N}_{1}(\Omega^{s}M)|s\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

By
4.4
, we have
r^{I,N}_{\infty}(\Omega^{d}k)=r_{1}^{I,N}(\Omega^{d}k).

We now proceed to explore some properties. To that end, we first state a lemma that will play a crucial role (see
[
28
, Lemma 4.1.]
).

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
0\longrightarrow U\longrightarrow V\longrightarrow W\longrightarrow 0
be an exact sequence of
A
-modules. Then there exists an exact sequence
0\longrightarrow\Omega W\longrightarrow U\oplus F\longrightarrow V\longrightarrow 0
, where
F
is free.

This lemma is easily proved by taking an exact sequence
0\longrightarrow\Omega W\longrightarrow A^{\mu(W)}\longrightarrow W\longrightarrow 0
and making a pullback diagram.

6.4
.

Some properties:
(With hypothesis as in
6.1
) For any MCM
A
-module
M
, we have

(I)

r^{I,N}_{\infty}(M)=r^{I,N}_{\infty}(\Omega^{i}M)
for any integer
i
.

(II)

If
W|M
, that is,
W
is a direct summand of
M
, then
r^{I,N}_{\infty}(W)\leq r^{I,N}_{\infty}(M)
.

(III)

If
0\longrightarrow U\longrightarrow V\longrightarrow W\longrightarrow 0
is an exact sequence of MCM
A
-modules, then we have

(i)

r^{I,N}_{\infty}(V)\leq\operatorname{max}\{r^{I,N}_{\infty}(U),r^{I,N}_{\infty}(W)\},

(ii)

r^{I,N}_{\infty}(U)\leq\operatorname{max}\{r^{I,N}_{\infty}(V),r^{I,N}_{\infty}(W)\}
,

(iii)

r^{I,N}_{\infty}(W)\leq\operatorname{max}\{r^{I,N}_{\infty}(U),r^{I,N}_{\infty}(V)\}

Proof.

(I)
Follows directly from definition.

(II)
There exists an
A
-module
V
such that
M\cong W\oplus V
. Applying Schanuel’s lemma to the following two exact sequences

0\longrightarrow\Omega W\oplus\Omega V\longrightarrow A^{\mu(W)+\mu(V)}\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow 0

and
0\longrightarrow\Omega M\longrightarrow A^{\mu(M)}\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow 0
,
we obtain
\Omega W\oplus\Omega V\oplus A^{\mu(M)}\cong\Omega M\oplus A^{\mu(W)+\mu(V)}
. In particular, there exists a free module
F_{1}
such that
\Omega W|(\Omega M\oplus F_{1})
. By iterating this process repeatedly, for each integer
s\geq 0
, we obtain a free module
F_{s}
such that
\Omega^{s}W|(\Omega^{s}M\oplus F_{s})
.

For
s<0
, note that
W^{*}|M^{*}
, where
(-)^{*}=\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(-,A)
. So there exists a free module
G
such that
\Omega(W^{*})|(\Omega(M^{*})\oplus G)
, which implies
(\Omega(W^{*}))^{*}|((\Omega(M^{*}))^{*}\oplus G^{*})
. Recall for a MCM
A
- module
M
, the cosyzygy of
M
is defined to be as
\Omega^{-1}(M)=(\Omega(M^{*}))^{*}
(see
2.4
). So there exists a free module
F_{-1}=G^{*}
such that
\Omega^{-1}W|((\Omega^{-1}M\oplus F_{-1})
. By proceeding inductively for each integer
s
, we obtain a free module
F_{s}
such that
\Omega^{s}W|(\Omega^{s}M\oplus F_{s})
, which implies
r_{1}^{I,N}(\Omega^{s}W)\leq r_{1}^{I,N}(\Omega^{s}M)\leq r_{\infty}^{I,N}(M)
for each
s\in\mathbb{Z}
. Hence we have
r_{\infty}^{I,N}(W)\leq r_{\infty}^{I,N}(M)
.

(III)
For each integer
s\geq 0
, we have an exact sequence
0\longrightarrow\Omega^{s}U\longrightarrow\Omega^{s}V\oplus F_{s}\longrightarrow\Omega^{s}W\longrightarrow 0
, where
F_{s}
is free. For
s<0
, we apply
(-)^{*}
to obtain the following exact sequence

0\longrightarrow W^{*}\longrightarrow V^{*}\longrightarrow U^{*}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{A}(W,A)=0
.
So we have an exact sequence
0\longrightarrow\Omega(W^{*})\longrightarrow\Omega(V^{*})\oplus G\longrightarrow\Omega(U^{*})\longrightarrow 0
, where
G
is free. Again we apply
(-)^{*}
to obtain the exact sequence
0\longrightarrow(\Omega(U^{*}))^{*}\longrightarrow(\Omega(V^{*}))^{*}\oplus G^{*}\longrightarrow(\Omega(W^{*}))^{*}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{A}(\Omega(U^{*}),A)=0
, that is, we have an exact sequence
0\longrightarrow\Omega^{-1}U\longrightarrow\Omega^{-1}V\oplus F_{-1}\longrightarrow\Omega^{-1}W\longrightarrow 0
, where
F_{-1}=G^{*}
is free. By proceeding inductively for each integer
s
, we obtain an exact sequence
0\longrightarrow\Omega^{s}U\longrightarrow\Omega^{s}V\oplus F_{s}\longrightarrow\Omega^{s}W\longrightarrow 0
, where
F_{s}
is free. Tensoring with
N/I^{n}N
, for each integer
s
, we obtain an exact sequence
\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{A}(\Omega^{s}U,N/I^{n}N)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{A}(\Omega^{s}V,N/I^{n}N)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{A}(\Omega^{s}W,N/I^{n}N)
, which yields
r_{1}^{I,N}(\Omega^{s}V)\leq\operatorname{max}\{r_{1}^{I,N}(\Omega^{s}U),r_{1}^{I,N}(\Omega^{s}W)
for each
s\in\mathbb{Z}
. Thus we have (i).

By
6.3
, there exists an exact sequence
0\longrightarrow\Omega W\longrightarrow U\oplus H\longrightarrow V\longrightarrow 0
, where
H
is free. Thus (ii) follows from (i) and (iii) follows from (ii).
∎

We now apply the above properties to a distinguished triangle in
\underline{\operatorname{CM}}(A)
to obtain similar inequalities.

Lemma 6.5
.

(With hypothesis as in
6.1
)
Let
X\longrightarrow Y\longrightarrow Z\longrightarrow\Omega^{-1}X
be a distinguished triangle in
\underline{\operatorname{CM}}(A)
, then we have

(i)

r_{\infty}^{I,N}(X)\leq\operatorname{max}\{r_{\infty}^{I,N}(Y),r_{\infty}^{I,N}(Z)\}
,

(ii)

r_{\infty}^{I,N}(Y)\leq\operatorname{max}\{r_{\infty}^{I,N}(X),r_{\infty}^{I,N}(Z)\}
,

(iii)

r_{\infty}^{I,N}(Z)\leq\operatorname{max}\{r_{\infty}^{I,N}(X),r_{\infty}^{I,N}(Y)\}
.

Proof.

By the properties of a distinguished triangle in
\underline{\operatorname{CM}}(A)
, we have an exact sequence
0\longrightarrow Y\longrightarrow Z\oplus F\longrightarrow\Omega^{-1}X\longrightarrow 0
, where
F
is free. Therefore, we apply
6.4
to obtain the desired inequalities.
∎

6.6
.

Construction of
thick
(E)
:

Let
E\in\underline{\operatorname{CM}}(A)
. Recall that,
thick
(E)
is the thick subcategory of
\underline{\operatorname{CM}}(A)
defined as the intersection of all thick subcategories of
\underline{\operatorname{CM}}(A)
that contains
E
. We now recall a concrete construction of thick
(E)
. To this end, we define the following notations; for two additive subcategories

\chi
and
\chi^{\prime}
of
\underline{\operatorname{CM}}(A)
, we set
\chi*\chi^{\prime}=\{D\in\underline{\operatorname{CM}}(A):
there exists a triangle
M\longrightarrow D\longrightarrow M^{\prime}\longrightarrow\Omega^{-1}M
, where
M\in\chi
and
M^{\prime}\in\chi^{\prime}\}
and
<\chi*\chi^{\prime}>=\{W\in\underline{\operatorname{CM}}(A):
there exists
s\in\mathbb{Z}
and
D\in\chi*\chi^{\prime}
, such that
W|\Omega^{s}D\}
.

Now, we consider the additive subcategory

\operatorname{add}(E)=\{V\in\underline{\operatorname{CM}}(A):V|E^{m}\ \operatorname{for\ some}\ m\in\mathbb{N}\}

of
\underline{\operatorname{CM}}(A)
. We set
\chi_{1}=\chi_{2}=\operatorname{add}(E)
, and for
i\geq 3
, define recursively

\chi_{i}=<\chi_{i-1}*\chi_{i-2}>.

Then we have

\operatorname{thick}(E)=\bigcup_{i\geq 1}\chi_{i}.

Proposition 6.7
.

(With hypothesis as in
6.1
) Let
E\in\underline{\operatorname{CM}}(A)
. Then for any
M\in\textit{thick}(E)
, we have
r_{\infty}^{I,N}(M)\leq r_{\infty}^{I,N}(E)
.

Proof.

From the construction of
\operatorname{thick}(E)
, there exists an integer
i\geq 1
, such that
M\in\chi_{i}
. We proceed by induction on
i
. If
i=1
or
2
, there exists an integer
m\geq 1
such that
M|E^{m}
, which implies
r_{\infty}^{I,N}(M)\leq r_{\infty}^{I,N}(E^{m})=r_{\infty}^{I,N}(E)
. Assume that
i\geq 3
and that the inequality holds for all integers
j<i
. We have
\chi_{i}=<\chi_{i-1}*\chi_{i-2}>
. If
D\in\chi_{i-1}*\chi_{i-2}
, by definition there exists a triangle
V\longrightarrow D\longrightarrow W\longrightarrow\Omega^{-1}V
in
\underline{\operatorname{CM}}(A)
, where
V\in\chi_{i-1}
and
W\in\chi_{i-2}
. Thus, by induction hypothesis and
6.5
, we have
r_{\infty}^{I,N}(D)\leq r_{\infty}^{I,N}(E)
. Finally, there exists an integer
s
and
D\in\chi_{i-1}*\chi_{i-2}
such that
M|\Omega^{s}D
. Hence, applying
6.4
yields the desired inequality.
∎

We proceed to study additional thick subcategories of
\underline{\operatorname{CM}}(A)
. To this end, for each integer

, we define

K_{q}=\{M\in\underline{\operatorname{CM}}(A):r_{\infty}^{I,N}(M)\leq q\}.

First, we show that it is indeed a thick subcategory of
\underline{\operatorname{CM}}(A)
.

Lemma 6.8
.

(With hypothesis as in
6.1
)
K_{q}
is a thick subcategory
\underline{\operatorname{CM}}(A)
for each integer

.

Proof.

We have

(i)

0\in K_{q}
.

(ii)

If
X\cong Y
and

, then clearly

.

(iii)

If

, then by

we have

.

(iv)

Let

be a distinguished triangle in

. If two of

belong to

.

(v)

Let

\operatorname{max}\{r_{\infty}^{I,N}(X),r_{\infty}^{I,N}(Y)\}=r_{\infty}^{I,N}(X\oplus Y)\leq q,

which yields

.

Thus,

is a thick subcategory of
\underline{\operatorname{CM}}(A)
.
∎

Next, we prove that the thick subcategory

contains a non-free maximal Cohen–Macaulay module of minimal complexity, when

Lemma 6.9
.

(With hypothesis as in
6.1
) Let

be a local complete intersection. Then, with the above notation, for each integer

, either

or

contains an MCM

-module

Proof.

Follows from the above lemma and

6.10
.

We have a decreasing chain of thick subcategories of

\underline{\operatorname{CM}}(A)=K_{t-1}\supseteq K_{t-2}\supseteq\ldots\ldots\supseteq K_{0}\supseteq K_{-1}\supseteq 0.

Set

Note that for every non-zero MCM

-module

. Therefore, for each

r_{\infty}^{I,N}(E)=\operatorname{inf\{r_{\infty}^{I,N}(M)|0\neq M\in\underline{CM}(A)\}}.

As a corollary of the above results, we obtain the following.

Theorem 6.11
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a complete intersection local ring of dimension
\geq 1
and

Let

Now there exists an integer

Let

Regularity and Depth

In this section, we investigate situations in which the upper bound in

is not attained. We also explore further applications within the stable category
\operatorname{\underline{CM}}(A)
. Throughout we work with the following setup.

Setup 7.1
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a non-regular Gorenstein ring of dimension
d\geq 1
and
N
a perfect module of dimension
t\geq 1
and
I
is an ideal of definition of
N
. Recall, for a MCM
A
-module

r^{I,N}_{\infty}(M)=\operatorname{sup}\{r^{I,N}_{1}(\Omega^{s}M)|s\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

7.2
.

Let
M
be a MCM
A
-module, then
M\otimes N
is a CM
A
-module of dimension
t
(see
2.6
). Therefore invoking
2.20
, for all
j=0,\ldots,t-1
we have

(11)

\operatorname{end}(H^{j}_{\mathfrak{M}}(L^{I}(M\otimes N)))\leq\operatorname{end}(H^{j+1}_{\mathfrak{M}}(G_{I}(M\otimes N))).

We tensor the surjective map
A^{\mu(M)}\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow 0
with
N
to obtain the surjective map
N^{\mu(M)}\longrightarrow M\otimes N\longrightarrow 0
. So for each
n\geq 0
, we have a surjective map

(\frac{I^{n}N}{I^{n+1}N})^{\mu(M)}\longrightarrow\frac{I^{n}(M\otimes N)}{I^{n+1}(M\otimes N)}\longrightarrow 0,

which induces a surjective map of
\mathcal{R}(I)
-modules

G_{I}(N)^{\mu(M)}\longrightarrow G_{I}(M\otimes N)\longrightarrow 0.

Since
\operatorname{dim}N=t=\operatorname{dim}M\otimes N

H^{t}_{\mathfrak{M}}(G_{I}(N))^{\mu(M)}\longrightarrow H^{t}_{\mathfrak{M}}(G_{I}(M\otimes N))\longrightarrow 0,

which implies
\operatorname{end}(H^{t}_{\mathfrak{M}}(G_{I}(M\otimes N)))\leq\operatorname{end}(H^{t}_{\mathfrak{M}}(G_{I}(N))).
Thus from equation (
11

\operatorname{end}(H^{t-1}_{\mathfrak{M}}(L^{I}(M\otimes N)))\leq\operatorname{end}(H^{t}_{\mathfrak{M}}(G_{I}(N))).

Note the right-hand side of the above equation depends only on
I,N
. We now generalize this pattern in the following result.

Lemma 7.3
.

(With hypothesis as in
7.1
) Let
M
be a MCM
A
-module such that
r_{i}^{I,N}(M)\leq t-q
for some integer
q\geq 1
and for every integer

. There exits an integer
\eta
, depending only on
I
and
N
, such that for every integer

we have

\operatorname{end}(H^{j}_{\mathfrak{M}}(L^{I}(M\otimes N)))\leq\eta.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
q
. For
q=1
, the assertion precisely from the discussion above. Assume
q\geq 2
. Suppose that the result holds for
q-1
, that is, there exists an integer
\eta^{\prime}
(depending only on
I
and
N
) such that for every integer
j=t-q+1,\ldots,t-1
, we have

(12)

\operatorname{end}(H^{j}_{\mathfrak{M}}(L^{I}(W\otimes N)))\leq\eta^{\prime}.

We tensor the exact sequence
0\longrightarrow\Omega M\longrightarrow A^{\mu(M)}\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow 0
with
N/I^{n+1}N
, for each
n\geq 0
to obtain the following exact sequence of
\mathcal{R}(I)
-modules

0\longrightarrow L_{1}^{I,N}(M)\longrightarrow L^{I}(\Omega M\otimes N)\xlongrightarrow{\phi}L^{I}(N)^{\mu(M)}\longrightarrow L^{I}(M\otimes N)\longrightarrow 0.

Set
C=\operatorname{Im}\phi
. By hypothesis we have
r_{1}^{I,N}(M)\leq t-q
. Now
L_{1}^{I,N}(M)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{A}(M,N/I^{n+1}N)
is a finitely generated graded
\mathcal{R}(I)
-module, so
\dim L_{1}^{I,N}(M)=r_{1}^{I,N}(M)+1\leq t-q+1
, thus
H^{j}_{\mathfrak{M}}(L_{1}^{I,N}(M))=0
for all
j\geq t-q+2
. From
the above exact sequence of
\mathcal{R}(I)
-modules, we have the following exact sequence of graded local cohomology
H^{t-q+1}_{\mathfrak{M}}(L^{I}((\Omega M)\otimes N))\longrightarrow H^{t-q+1}_{\mathfrak{M}}(C)\longrightarrow H^{t-q+2}_{\mathfrak{M}}(L^{I,N}_{1}(M))=0
. Note that
r_{i}^{I,N}(\Omega M)=r_{i+1}^{I,N}(M)\leq t-q\leq t-q+1
for every integer

. Hence by induction hypothesis we have

\operatorname{end}(H^{t-q+1}_{\mathfrak{M}}(C))\leq\operatorname{end}(H^{t-q+1}_{\mathfrak{M}}(L^{I}((\Omega M)\otimes N))\leq\eta^{\prime}.

We also have another exact sequence of graded local cohomologies

H^{t-q}_{\mathfrak{M}}(L^{I}(N))^{\mu(M)}\longrightarrow H^{t-q}_{\mathfrak{M}}(L^{I}(M\otimes N))\longrightarrow H^{t-q+1}_{\mathfrak{M}}(C),

which yields

\operatorname{end}(H^{t-q}_{\mathfrak{M}}(L^{I}(M\otimes N)))\leq\operatorname{end}(H^{t-q}_{\mathfrak{M}}(L^{I}(N)))+\eta^{\prime}.

Thus,
\eta=\operatorname{end}(H^{t-q}_{\mathfrak{M}}(L^{I}(N)))+\eta^{\prime}
suffices.
∎

, under additional assumptions on

Definition 8.2
.

A finite

, if for all
A
-modules

\operatorname{Tor}_{i\gg 0}^{A}(X,Y)=0
have finite projective dimension.

Example 8.3
.

Test modules are abundant.

(i)

If
M
is a test module, then so is
\Omega^{n}M
for each integer
n\geq 0
.

(ii)

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a local ring, then the residue field
k
is a test module. So
\Omega^{n}k
is a test module for each integer
n\geq 0
.

(iii)

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a local ring with infinite residue field
k
. Let
M
be a finite
A
-module with
\operatorname{depth}(M)>0
. Then
\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}^{n}M}
is a test module for all
n\geq 1
. As
\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}^{n}M}=\mathfrak{m}^{n}M
for
n\gg 0
, so
\mathfrak{m}^{n}M
is a
test module
for all
n\gg 0
(see
[
1
, 1.5.1, Definition 2.1, Proposition 2.2 and Proposition 2.3(5)]
.

(iv)

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a local complete intersection. Then a finite
A
-module
M
is a test module if and only if
\operatorname{cx}_{A}M=\operatorname{codim}A
(see
[
8
, Proposition 2.7]
). Therefore, if
\operatorname{codim}A=1
, then every finite
A
-module

of infinite projective dimension, is a test module. In particular, every non-free MCM

-module is a test module, if

is hypersurface.

(v)

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a local ring and

-primary, integrally closed ideal. Then

[

).

Theorem 8.4
.

(With hypothesis as in

We now give the definition of

of a ring.

Definition 8.5
.

A Noetherian ring

if for finite
A

\operatorname{Tor}_{i\gg 0}^{A}(M,N)=0
implies that either

or

has finite projective dimension.

Example 8.6
.

Let

)

For more examples, we refer to

]

Remark 8.7

If the ring

A

Let
