Ξ c ​ c + ⁣ + − Ξ c ​ c + \Xi_{cc}^{++}-\Xi_{cc}^{+} Transitions as a Two-Charm-Selective Portal to Ultra-Low- Q Q Charged Currents
Yong Du

Abstract
The recent LHCb observation of Ξ c ​ c + \Xi_{cc}^{+} opens the ultra-low- Q Q transition Ξ c ​ c + ⁣ + → Ξ c ​ c + \Xi_{cc}^{++}\to\Xi_{cc}^{+} as an experimentally motivated null test of charged-current new physics. Our two- and three-body analyses show sensitivity to effective baryon-level couplings of 𝒪 ⁡ ( 10 − 6 − 10 − 7 ) \mathcal{O}(10^{-6}-10^{-7}) for MeV-scale recoil momenta. We establish a practical no-go result for a universal light charged scalar ϕ + \phi^{+} : with a first-generation ϕ + ​ u ¯ ​ d \phi^{+}\bar{u}d coupling, existing electroweak-precision and beta-decay constraints are parametrically stronger than the projected LHCb sensitivity. We then identify a two-charm-selective charged-current portal whose leading operator has a nonzero matrix element in doubly charmed baryons but vanishes at leading order in pions, nucleons, nuclei, and singly charmed mesons. In this class of models, Ξ c ​ c + ⁣ + → Ξ c ​ c + \Xi_{cc}^{++}\to\Xi_{cc}^{+} transitions can provide the leading direct probe of the portal interaction.



I
Introduction

The doubly charmed isodoublet

T_{u}\equiv\Xi_{cc}^{++}(ccu),\qquad T_{d}\equiv\Xi_{cc}^{+}(ccd)

(1)

provides a heavy-hadron analogue of beta decay in which the compact
cc
pair is a spectator. The LHCb Collaboration
Aaij et al. 2026
recently observed
\Xi_{cc}^{+}
in Run-3 data and measured

M(\Xi_{cc}^{+})-M(\Xi_{cc}^{++})=-1.77\pm 0.84\pm 0.15{}^{+1.90}_{-1.30}\,\mathrm{MeV}.

(2)

Thus, at the baseline lifetime correction used by LHCb,

Q_{\Xi}\equiv M(\Xi_{cc}^{++})-M(\Xi_{cc}^{+})=1.77\,\mathrm{MeV},

(3)

The final asymmetric uncertainty in Eq. (
2
) arises from the assumed
\Xi_{cc}^{+}
lifetime,
\tau(\Xi_{cc}^{+})
, which enters the correction for a selection-induced mass bias. LHCb adopts
\tau(\Xi_{cc}^{+})=45\,{\rm fs}
as its baseline, motivated by the heavy-quark-expansion (HQE) estimate of Ref.
Cheng and Shi 2018
, and scans the range
15
–
160\,{\rm fs}
. More recent HQE calculations
Cheng and Liu 2026
;
Dulibić et al. 2026
provide updated lifetime predictions that can be used to sharpen the corresponding uncertainty in
Q_{\Xi}
.

Independent calculations also favor a positive, MeV-scale mass difference. Hwang and Chung obtain
Q_{\Xi}=2.3\pm 1.7\,\mathrm{MeV}
from heavy-quark-symmetry phenomenology
Hwang and Chung 2008
; Brodsky
et al.
find
1.5\pm 2.7\,\mathrm{MeV}
using heavy-quark–diquark symmetry
Brodsky et al. 2011
; Wei
et al.
obtain
0.4\pm 0.3\,\mathrm{MeV}
from Regge phenomenology
Wei et al. 2015
; and Karliner and Rosner find
1.41\pm 0.12{}^{+0.76}_{-0.00}\,\mathrm{MeV}
in a constituent-quark fit to isospin splittings
Karliner and Rosner 2017
. A fully dynamical QCD+QED lattice calculation by the BMW Collaboration gives
Borsanyi et al. 2015

\left(Q_{\Xi}\right)^{\rm BMW}=2.16(11)(17)\,\mathrm{MeV}.

(4)

Taken together, these results favor a positive value of
Q_{\Xi}
at the MeV scale. We therefore use the LHCb central value,
Q_{\Xi}=1.77\,\mathrm{MeV}
, as our benchmark, while retaining the explicit
Q_{\Xi}
dependence wherever it is phenomenologically relevant.

Because
Q_{\Xi}\ll m_{\pi}
, ordinary hadronic transitions are kinematically forbidden. The only open Standard Model (SM) channel,
\Xi_{cc}^{++}\to\Xi_{cc}^{+}e^{+}\nu_{e}
, is strongly suppressed by
Q_{\Xi}^{4}/M_{\Xi_{cc}^{++}}^{4}
and is expected to have a branching fraction of
\mathcal{O}(10^{-15})
. We therefore consider the charged-recoil transitions

\Xi_{cc}^{++}\to\Xi_{cc}^{+}{\cal P},\qquad{\cal P}=X^{+}\ \text{or}\ e^{+}N,

(5)

where
X^{+}
is a new charged state and
N
is a light neutral fermion. Observation of the two-body mode, or of a three-body rate or kinematic distribution inconsistent with the tiny SM beta-decay contribution, would constitute a null test of the SM.

II
Model-independent reach

At the baryon level, we parameterize emission of a spin-0 charged state by

\mathcal{L}_{X}=X^{+}\,\overline{\Xi_{cc}^{+}}(g_{\Xi}+ig_{P}\gamma_{5})\Xi_{cc}^{++}+{\rm h.c.},

(6)

where
g_{\Xi}
and
g_{P}
are scalar and pseudoscalar baryonic couplings, respectively. Near threshold,

\Gamma(\Xi_{cc}^{++}\to\Xi_{cc}^{+}X^{+})\simeq\frac{|g_{\Xi}|^{2}}{2\pi}\,\sqrt{Q_{\Xi}^{2}-m_{X}^{2}}.

(7)

Using
\tau(\Xi_{cc}^{++})=0.256^{+0.024}_{-0.022}\pm 0.014\,{\rm ps}

Aaij et al. 2018
, one finds

\mathcal{B}_{X}\simeq 6.2\times 10^{7}|g_{\Xi}|^{2}\left(\frac{\sqrt{Q_{\Xi}^{2}-m_{X}^{2}}}{1\,\mathrm{MeV}}\right),

(8)

where
\mathcal{B}_{X}\equiv\mathcal{B}(\Xi_{cc}^{++}\to\Xi_{cc}^{+}X^{+})
is the branching fraction. The left panel of Fig.
1
shows the projected LHCb sensitivity for the two benchmark reaches defined in the next section. The Run-3 benchmark probes
g_{\Xi}
at the
\mathcal{O}(10^{-6})
level in this model-independent two-body parametrization, with an improvement of approximately one order of magnitude at Upgrade II.

for illustration and assume that

The same ultra-low-
Q
phase-space window also permits the three-body semileptonic decay

\Xi_{cc}^{++}\to\Xi_{cc}^{+}e^{+}N,

(9)

where
N
is a generic light neutral fermion. Introducing a charged scalar mediator
\phi^{+}
provides a unified description of the heavy-mediator, resonant, and light-mediator regimes:

\mathcal{L}_{\phi eN}=g_{\Xi}\phi^{+}\overline{\Xi_{cc}^{+}}\Xi_{cc}^{++}+y_{N}\phi^{+}\bar{N}P_{R}e+{\rm h.c.},

(10)

where
g_{\Xi}
is the same effective
\Xi_{cc}^{++}

\Xi_{cc}^{+}

6

\frac{d\Gamma_{eN}^{(\phi)}}{ds}=\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{\sqrt{s}\,\Gamma_{\Xi\to\Xi\phi^{*}(s)}\,\Gamma_{\phi^{*}(s)\to eN}}{(s-m_{\phi}^{2})^{2}+m_{\phi}^{2}\Gamma_{\phi}^{2}},

(11)

where
s\equiv(p_{e}+p_{N})^{2}
. For
m_{N}=0
, the off-shell partial widths are

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\Xi\to\Xi\phi^{*}(s)}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{|g_{\Xi}|^{2}}{2\pi}\sqrt{Q_{\Xi}^{2}-s},

(12)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\phi^{*}(s)\to eN}

\displaystyle=\frac{|y_{N}|^{2}\sqrt{s}}{16\pi}\left(1-\frac{m_{e}^{2}}{s}\right)^{2}.

(13)

The right panel of Fig.
1
shows the corresponding LHCb sensitivity. For
m_{\phi}<m_{e}
, the Run-3 benchmark probes
|g_{\Xi}y_{N}|
at the
10^{-5}
level, with an improvement of approximately one order of magnitude at Upgrade II. In the on-shell region,
m_{e}+m_{N}<m_{\phi}<Q_{\Xi}
, the narrow-width approximation factorizes the rate into
\Xi_{cc}^{++}\to\Xi_{cc}^{+}\phi^{+}
followed by
\phi^{+}\to e^{+}N
; the plotted reach assumes
\mathcal{B}(\phi^{+}\to e^{+}N)=1
. For
m_{\phi}>Q_{\Xi}
, the mediator is off shell and the sensitivity degrades rapidly. The ultra-low-
Q

\Xi_{cc}^{++}\to\Xi_{cc}^{+}
transition is therefore most sensitive to light charged-current mediators.

III
Experimental observables at LHCb

If
X^{+}
is reconstructed, the natural visible-recoil observable is the reconstructed mass difference

\Delta m_{X}=m(\Xi_{cc}^{+}X^{+})-m(\Xi_{cc}^{+}).

(14)

For a correctly reconstructed two-body signal, this observable peaks at
M(\Xi_{cc}^{++})-M(\Xi_{cc}^{+})=Q_{\Xi}
. The kinematically small quantities are instead the kinetic-energy release
Q_{\Xi}-m_{X}
and the recoil momentum in the
\Xi_{cc}^{++}
rest frame. Direct reconstruction of a singly charged MeV-scale state is, however, severely efficiency-suppressed at LHCb: even a
50\,\mathrm{GeV}

\Xi_{cc}^{++}
boosts a MeV-scale recoil only to momenta of
\mathcal{O}(10\,\mathrm{MeV})
, far below the
\sim 1.5\,\mathrm{GeV}
scale required for a charged particle to reach the downstream tracking stations
Aaij et al. 2015
. A more promising strategy is therefore to search for a feed-down component in the reconstructed
\Xi_{cc}^{+}\to\Lambda_{c}^{+}K^{-}\pi^{+}
sample. Promptly produced
\Xi_{cc}^{+}
baryons and cascade
\Xi_{cc}^{+}
baryons from
\Xi_{cc}^{++}\to\Xi_{cc}^{+}X^{+}
populate the same
\Lambda_{c}^{+}K^{-}\pi^{+}
mass peak, but differ in impact-parameter and decay-time-proxy distributions because the cascade component contains the additional upstream
\Xi_{cc}^{++}
flight. A multicomponent template fit to the
\Xi_{cc}^{+}
mass and topology variables, normalized to the abundant
\Xi_{cc}^{++}
control mode, can therefore constrain
R_{X}
even when
X^{+}
is not reconstructed. For
X^{+}\to e^{+}\nu
or the decay in Eq. (
9
), the visible invariant-mass spectrum is continuous because the neutral fermion is unobserved. If the parent flight direction can be inferred, a corrected-mass analysis can nevertheless provide an endpoint observable, as in standard LHCb semileptonic analyses
Aaij et al. 2020
.

Accordingly, we define product-ratio observables normalized to established LHCb reconstruction modes:

R_{\cal P}=\frac{\mathcal{B}(\Xi_{cc}^{++}\to\Xi_{cc}^{+}{\cal P})\mathcal{B}(\Xi_{cc}^{+}\to\Lambda_{c}^{+}K^{-}\pi^{+})}{\mathcal{B}(\Xi_{cc}^{++}\to\Lambda_{c}^{+}K^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{+})},

(15)

with
{\cal P}=\{X^{+},\,e^{+}N\}
. Let
N_{++}
denote the reconstructed yield in the normalization mode
\Xi_{cc}^{++}\to\Lambda_{c}^{+}K^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{+}
and
\epsilon_{\rm rel}
the signal-to-normalization efficiency ratio. The expected signal yield is

s_{\cal P}=N_{++}\,R_{\cal P}\,\epsilon_{\rm rel},\qquad R_{\cal P}^{95}\simeq\frac{3.0}{N_{++}\epsilon_{\rm rel}}

(16)

for a zero-background 95% C.L. estimate. The publicly reported Run-3 normalization yield is
N_{++}=8712\pm 160
in
6.9\,{\rm fb}^{-1}

Han 2026
.

To estimate Eq. (
16
), we perform two fast-simulation studies based on standalone
GenXicc

Chang et al. 2010
: one for the two-body topology
{\cal P}=X^{+}
and one for the semileptonic topology
{\cal P}=e^{+}N
. Both use the same
10^{8}
-event
\Xi_{cc}^{++}
production sample, the LHCb forward acceptance
2<\eta<5
, public kinematic inputs for
\Xi_{cc}^{++}
and
\Xi_{cc}^{+}
, and a template fit that separates prompt
\Xi_{cc}^{+}
production from cascade
\Xi_{cc}^{+}
feed-down
Aaij et al. 2017
;
Aaij et al. 2026
.

IV
No-go result for universal light charged scalars

The preceding discussion treats
X^{+}
as a generic charged state. We now show that, if
X^{+}
is identified with a light scalar
\phi^{+}
carrying a universal first-generation charged-current coupling, LHCb cannot provide the leading constraint. We write

\mathcal{L}_{ud\phi}=\phi^{+}\bar{u}(y_{L}P_{L}+y_{R}P_{R})d+{\rm h.c.}\,,

(21)

as the simplest microscopic realization of Eq. (
5
). First, an elementary charged scalar in a conventional electroweak representation with
m_{\phi}<M_{Z}/2
contributes to the
Z
width,

\displaystyle\Gamma(Z\to\phi^{+}\phi^{-})\simeq\frac{G_{F}M_{Z}^{3}}{6\sqrt{2}\pi}q_{Z}^{2}\left(1-\frac{4m_{\phi}^{2}}{M_{Z}^{2}}\right)^{3/2},

(22)

with
q_{Z}\equiv T_{3}-Qs_{W}^{2}
. Here,
G_{F}
is the Fermi constant,
T_{3}
and
Q
are the weak isospin and electric charge of
\phi^{+}
in units of the proton charge, and
s_{W}\equiv\sin\theta_{W}
. For conventional electroweak-singlet or -doublet assignments, Eq. (
22
) gives an additional partial width of
\mathcal{O}(10\,\mathrm{MeV})
, which is incompatible with the measured total
Z
width
Schael et al. 2006
;
Navas et al. 2024
. Independently, a charged scalar below a few MeV is excluded in standard thermal cosmology
Escudero 2019
;
Du and Yu 2021
;
Du 2024
.

Second, even if a nonstandard completion evades the electroweak and cosmological bounds, the same universal
\bar{u}d
interaction induces zero-charm processes:
\pi^{+}\to\pi^{0}\phi^{+}
,
n\to p\phi^{-}
when kinematically allowed, and exotic two-body decays of superallowed parent nuclei. We express these processes in terms of a common effective zero-charm coupling
g_{\rm ord}
,

\Gamma_{i}^{\phi}=\frac{C_{i}|g_{\rm ord}|^{2}p_{i}}{2\pi},\qquad p_{i}\simeq\sqrt{Q_{i}^{2}-m_{\phi}^{2}},

(23)

where
C_{i}
encodes the channel-dependent spin, isospin, and hadronic or nuclear matrix elements. We construct a joint profile likelihood from the PIBETA measurement of pion beta decay, the neutron lifetime, and the 15 best-measured superallowed
0^{+}\!\to 0^{+}
transitions in the Hardy–Towner survey
Pocanic et al. 2004
;
Navas et al. 2024
;
Hardy and Towner 2020
. A common
V_{ud}
parameter and inner radiative correction
\Delta_{R}^{V}
are profiled across the three data sets, while the superallowed-transition likelihood retains the correlated
\delta_{R}^{\prime}
and
\delta_{NS}
uncertainties. Details and numerical inputs are given in Appendix B.

C_{i}=\alpha_{i}=1
, the combined fit gives

(24)

and the best fit is statistically consistent with the null hypothesis. Below the neutron threshold, the pion, neutron, and nuclear data contribute jointly; above threshold, the correlated superallowed-transition likelihood dominates. Comparing Eq. (
24

g_{\Xi}\sim 10^{-6}
–

, shows that the zero-charm constraint lies approximately six to seven orders of magnitude below the coupling accessible in
\Xi_{cc}^{++}\to\Xi_{cc}^{+}\phi^{+}
, provided
g_{\rm ord}\sim g_{\Xi}
up to hadronic matching factors. The numerical result is conditional on the response coefficients
\alpha_{i}
and matrix-element normalizations
C_{i}
, but the hierarchy is sufficiently large that LHCb cannot provide the leading constraint on a universal light scalar.

V
Two-charm-selective benchmark

The no-go result can be avoided if the
u\to d
current is proportional to a local two-charm structure. In a model-independent effective field theory (EFT), we write the leading interactions as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{cc}\supset

\displaystyle\,\frac{C_{X}}{\Lambda_{cc}^{6}}X^{+}(\bar{u}\Gamma d){\cal G}_{cc}

\displaystyle\,+\frac{C_{eN}}{\Lambda_{cc}^{8}}(\bar{u}\Gamma d)(\bar{e}\Gamma_{N}N){\cal G}_{cc}+{\rm h.c.},

(25)

where
N
is a light neutral fermion,
\Gamma
and
\Gamma_{N}
denote Lorentz structures, and
\mathcal{G}_{cc}
is a local two-charm density operator whose leading matrix element is nonzero only in states containing a compact
cc
pair. One realization is

{\cal G}_{cc}=:{\cal D}_{cc,\mu}^{\dagger}{\cal D}_{cc}^{\mu}:,\qquad{\cal D}_{cc}^{i\mu}=\epsilon^{ijk}c^{jT}C\gamma^{\mu}c^{k},

(26)

where
i,j,k
are color indices, normal ordering removes vacuum contractions,
C
is the charge-conjugation matrix, and
T
denotes transposition. Because Eq. (
26
) has vanishing leading matrix elements in pions, nucleons, and nuclei, the zero-charm constraints discussed above do not apply at leading order. The same structure suppresses singly charmed transitions such as
D^{+}\to D^{0}e^{+}\nu_{e}

Ablikim et al. 2017
. The decays
\Xi_{cc}^{++}\to\Xi_{cc}^{+}X^{+}
and
\Xi_{cc}^{++}\to\Xi_{cc}^{+}e^{+}N
therefore become direct probes of a two-charm-selective interaction.

It is useful to define the following hierarchy parameters:

\displaystyle g_{0}

\displaystyle\,=g_{\rm ord},\quad g_{1}=g_{D},\quad g_{2}=g_{\Xi},

(27)

\displaystyle\epsilon_{i}

\displaystyle\,=\left|\frac{g_{i}}{g_{2}}\right|,\quad(i=0,1),

(28)

where
g_{0}
is the zero-charm coupling probed by pions, nucleons, and nuclei;
g_{1}
is the coupling probed by singly charmed transitions such as
D^{+}\to D^{0}X^{+}
; and
g_{2}
is the coupling tested by
\Xi_{cc}^{++}\to\Xi_{cc}^{+}X^{+}
or
\Xi_{cc}^{++}\to\Xi_{cc}^{+}e^{+}N
at LHCb.

For LHCb to be more sensitive than the lower-charm probes, the leakage ratios must satisfy

\displaystyle\epsilon_{0}<\epsilon_{0}^{\rm crit}\equiv\frac{g_{\rm ord}^{\rm lim}}{g_{\Xi}^{\rm reach}},\qquad\epsilon_{1}<\epsilon_{1}^{\rm crit}\equiv\frac{g_{D}^{\rm lim}}{g_{\Xi}^{\rm reach}}.

(29)

Here
g_{\Xi}^{\rm reach}
is the projected LHCb coupling sensitivity shown in Fig.
1
, and
g_{\rm ord}^{\rm lim}
is the profile-likelihood limit shown in Fig.
3
. We estimate the singly charmed bound
g_{D}^{\rm lim}
by translating a branching-fraction limit into a two-body soft-recoil coupling:

g_{D}^{\rm lim}(m_{X})=\left(\frac{2\pi\Gamma_{D^{+}}\mathcal{B}_{D}^{\rm lim}}{\sqrt{Q_{D}^{2}-m_{X}^{2}}}\right)^{1/2},

(30)

with
Q_{D}=M_{D^{+}}-M_{D^{0}}
. We adopt
\mathcal{B}_{D}^{\rm lim}=10^{-4}
as a proxy, based on the current BESIII limit on
D^{+}\to D^{0}e^{+}\nu_{e}

Ablikim et al. 2017
.

For
m_{X}=1\,{\rm MeV}
, the combined zero-charm likelihood gives
\epsilon_{0}^{\rm crit}\simeq 4\times 10^{-8}
for the Run-3 benchmark
\mathcal{B}_{\Xi}^{\rm lim}=10^{-3}
and
\epsilon_{0}^{\rm crit}\simeq 4\times 10^{-7}
for the Upgrade-II benchmark
\mathcal{B}_{\Xi}^{\rm lim}=10^{-5}
. The corresponding singly charmed conditions are much weaker,
\epsilon_{1}^{\rm crit}\simeq 0.09
and
0.9
, respectively. Figure
4
shows the resulting dominance plane for
m_{X}=1\,\mathrm{MeV}
. The solid and dashed shaded regions correspond to the Run-3 and Upgrade-II benchmarks, respectively. The universal (
U
), singly charmed (
C_{1}
), and two-charm-selective (
C_{2}
) cases are represented schematically by

\displaystyle{\rm U}:

\displaystyle\ (\epsilon_{0},\epsilon_{1})\simeq(1,1),

\displaystyle{\rm C}_{1}:

\displaystyle\ \epsilon_{0}\ll 1,\ \epsilon_{1}\sim 1,\qquad{\rm C}_{2}:\ \epsilon_{0}\ll 1,\ \epsilon_{1}\ll 1,

(31)

which are indicated by framed symbols in Fig.
4
.

VI
UV interpretation and naturalness

To interpret the hierarchy in Eq. (
31
), we first write the low-energy EFT obtained after integrating out the heavy mediators. A convenient spurion parametrization is

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\cal P}=

\displaystyle\,\eta_{0}\,\hat{g}_{0}^{i}\,{\cal P}^{i}\left(\bar{u}_{R}d_{R}\right)+\eta_{1}\,\hat{g}_{1}^{i}\,{\cal P}^{i}(\bar{u}_{R}d_{R})\mathcal{G}_{1c}

\displaystyle\,+\eta_{2c}\,\hat{g}_{2}^{i}\,{\cal P}^{i}(\bar{u}_{R}d_{R})\mathcal{G}_{cc}+{\rm h.c.}\,.

(32)

Here the index
i
labels the recoil and semileptonic operator structures,
{\cal P}^{X}=X^{+}
and
{\cal P}^{eN}=\bar{e}_{R}N
, and repeated
i
indices are summed. The first two terms parameterize zero- and one-charm leakage, whereas the third is the desired two-charm-selective interaction. At the hadron level this structure becomes

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\cal P}^{\rm had.}=

\displaystyle\,g_{cc}X^{+}\bar{T}_{u}T_{d}+G_{cc}\left(\bar{T}_{u}T_{d}\right)\left(\bar{e}_{R}N\right)+y_{N}X^{+}\bar{N}e_{R}

\displaystyle\,+\eta_{0}\,\left[g_{0}X^{+}\bar{p}n+G_{0}\left(\bar{p}n\right)\left(\bar{e}_{R}N\right)\right]

\displaystyle\,+\eta_{1}\,\left[g_{1c}X^{+}\bar{D}^{+}D^{0}+G_{1c}\left(\bar{D}^{+}D^{0}\right)\left(\bar{e}_{R}N\right)\right]

\displaystyle\,+{\rm h.c.}\,.

(33)

Under a global
U(1)_{\rm sel}
symmetry, assign charges
+1
,
+1
, and
-1
to
X^{+}
,
N
, and
T_{d}
, respectively, and take the remaining fields to be neutral. The first line of Eq. (
33
) is then allowed, corresponding to
\Xi_{cc}^{++}\to\Xi_{cc}^{+}+{\cal P}
and, when kinematically open,
X^{+}\to e^{+}N
. The lower-charm terms require the compensating spurions
\eta_{0}
and
\eta_{1}
. Consequently, the
C_{2}
hierarchy is technically natural at the hadronic-EFT level: the limit
\eta_{0},\eta_{1}\to 0
restores the selection symmetry while preserving the two-charm-selective interaction.

Finally, we outline a UV completion of the two-charm-selective operators in Eq. (
25
). Before electroweak symmetry breaking, the local two-charm current in Eq. (
26
) may be embedded as

J_{cc,\mu}^{ia}=\epsilon^{ijk}c_{R}^{jT}C\gamma_{\mu}Q_{2L}^{ka},\qquad Q_{2L}=(c_{L},s_{L})^{T},

(34)

where
\epsilon^{ijk}
projects the two color triplets in
{\bf 3}\otimes{\bf 3}=\bar{\bf 3}_{A}\oplus{\bf 6}_{S}
onto the antisymmetric color-antitriplet channel. A gauge-invariant completion can employ a heavy spin-1 diquark-like mediator
V_{a\mu}\sim({\bf 3},{\bf 2},-5/6)
, whose lower component couples to
J_{cc,\mu}
. The index
a=1,2
labels two copies of the same SM representation, with gauge quantum numbers listed in Table
1
. We also introduce charged scalars
H_{qa}^{+}
coupled to
\bar{u}_{R}d_{R}
and, for the semileptonic channel,
H_{\ell a}^{+}
coupled to
\bar{N}e_{R}
.

Explicitly, the UV Lagrangian is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rm UV}\supset\sum_{a=1}^{2}\Big(

\displaystyle y_{a}H_{qa}^{+}\bar{u}_{R}d_{R}+\kappa_{a}V_{a\mu}J_{cc}^{\mu}

\displaystyle+\lambda_{a}X^{+}H_{qa}^{-}V_{a\mu}^{\dagger}V_{a}^{\mu}\Big)+{\rm h.c.}

(35)

where the leptonic mediator
H_{\ell a}
can be added similarly to generate the
e^{+}N
operator in Eq. (
25
). The fields transform under a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
exchange symmetry as

\displaystyle\,H_{q1}\leftrightarrow H_{q2},\quad H_{\ell 1}\leftrightarrow H_{\ell 2},

\displaystyle\,V_{1}\leftrightarrow V_{2},\quad X^{+}\to-X^{+}.

(36)

In the symmetric limit, this implies degenerate mediator masses and

\displaystyle\,y_{1}=y_{2},\quad y_{N1}=y_{N2},

\displaystyle\,\lambda_{1}=-\lambda_{2},\quad\lambda_{q\ell,1}=-\lambda_{q\ell,2},

(37)

As a consequence, the one-loop threshold correction that mixes
X^{+}
with
H_{qa}^{+}
cancels analogously to the Glashow–Iliopoulos–Maiani mechanism
Glashow et al. 1970
:

C_{0}^{\rm loop}\propto\sum_{a=1}^{2}{y_{a}\lambda_{a}M_{V_{a}}^{2}\over 16\pi^{2}M_{H_{qa}}^{2}}=0,

(38)

so lower-charm operators such as
X^{+}\bar{u}_{R}d_{R}
and
(\bar{u}_{R}d_{R})(\bar{e}_{R}N)
arise only through insertions of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-breaking spurions.

The two-charm-selective amplitude need not vanish because it contains the additional current insertions
\kappa_{a}V_{a}J_{cc}
. Figure
5
shows the tree-level matching topology for one branch of the doubled mediator sector. Integrating out the heavy fields gives

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{C_{X}}{\Lambda_{2c}^{6}}&\sim\sum_{a=1}^{2}\frac{\lambda_{a}\,y_{a}\,|\kappa_{a}|^{2}}{M_{H_{qa}}^{2}\,M_{V_{a}}^{4}},\\[5.69054pt]
\frac{C_{eN}}{\Lambda_{2c}^{8}}&\sim\sum_{a=1}^{2}\frac{\lambda_{q\ell,a}\,y_{a}\,y_{N_{a}}^{*}\,|\kappa_{a}|^{2}}{M_{H_{qa}}^{2}\,M_{H_{\ell a}}^{2}\,M_{V_{a}}^{4}}.\end{split}

(39)

An exchange-odd two-charm spurion, such as
|\kappa_{1}|^{2}-|\kappa_{2}|^{2}
, can therefore generate
\Xi_{cc}^{++}\to\Xi_{cc}^{+}
transitions while preserving the cancellation of the leading lower-charm scalar mixing. Relating an LHCb measurement to the UV parameters requires the hadronic matrix element

\beta_{\Xi}^{\Gamma}\equiv\langle\Xi_{cc}^{+}|(\bar{u}\Gamma d){\cal G}_{cc}|\Xi_{cc}^{++}\rangle,

(40)

which should be determined from lattice QCD or estimated in heavy-diquark effective theory.

VII
Summary

The observation of
\Xi_{cc}^{+}
establishes the doubly charmed isodoublet as an ultra-low-
Q
laboratory for charged-current searches. We have shown that a minimal MeV-scale charged scalar is overconstrained by electroweak precision data and by pion, neutron, and nuclear beta-decay observables. A two-charm-selective interaction suppresses lower-charm transitions, allowing
\Xi_{cc}^{++}\to\Xi_{cc}^{+}X^{+}
and
\Xi_{cc}^{++}\to\Xi_{cc}^{+}e^{+}N
to become the leading direct probes of the corresponding charged-current portal. A dedicated LHCb feed-down analysis would test interactions whose leading matrix elements vanish in pion, nuclear, and singly charmed systems. Our standalone-
GenXicc
fast simulations support this strategy: direct reconstruction of a MeV-scale recoil or soft positron is strongly efficiency-suppressed, whereas a feed-down template fit reaches
R_{\cal P}^{95}\sim 10^{-3}
with the public Run-3 yield and approximately
4\times 10^{-5}
under a simple Upgrade-II luminosity extrapolation for both
{\cal P}=X^{+}
and
{\cal P}=e^{+}N
.

Appendix B: Combined likelihood for ordinary-sector recasts

This appendix gives the individual likelihoods and their combination for the hidden-branch recast used in Sec.

.

.1
Pion beta decay

Let
\mathcal{B}_{\pi\beta}^{\rm exp}
denote the measured branching fraction for
\pi^{+}\to\pi^{0}e^{+}\nu(\gamma)
and
\mathcal{B}_{\pi\beta}^{\rm SM}
the SM prediction. The PIBETA measurement gives
Pocanic et al. 2004

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\pi\beta}^{\rm exp}=\big[

\displaystyle\,1.036\pm 0.004_{\rm stat}\pm 0.004_{\rm syst}

\displaystyle\,\pm 0.003_{\pi e2}\big]\times 10^{-8},

(50)

while the SM prediction and radiative-correction systematics are discussed in Ref.
Czarnecki et al. 2020
. If an exotic two-body mode
\pi^{+}\to\pi^{0}\phi^{+}
contributes to the same selected event class with relative acceptance
\epsilon_{\pi\phi}
, a one-bin likelihood is

\displaystyle-2\ln{\cal L}_{\pi}={}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\Delta_{\pi}^{2}\over\sigma_{\pi,{\rm exp}}^{2}+\sigma_{\pi,{\rm unc}}^{2}}+\vartheta_{V}^{2}+\vartheta_{R}^{2},

(51)

\displaystyle\Delta_{\pi}={}

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\pi\beta}^{\rm exp}-\mathcal{B}_{\pi\beta}^{\rm SM}(\vartheta_{V},\vartheta_{R})-\epsilon_{\pi\phi}\mathcal{B}_{\pi\phi}.

Here
\mathcal{B}_{\pi\phi}=\mathcal{B}(\pi^{+}\to\pi^{0}\phi^{+})
,
\vartheta_{V}
is a nuisance parameter for the CKM normalization, and
\vartheta_{R}
is a nuisance parameter for the short-distance radiative input. A convenient linearized form is

\mathcal{B}_{\pi\beta}^{\rm SM}(\vartheta_{V},\vartheta_{R})=\mathcal{B}_{\pi\beta,0}^{\rm SM}\left(1+2\sigma_{V}\vartheta_{V}+\sigma_{R}\vartheta_{R}\right),

(52)

where
\sigma_{V}
and
\sigma_{R}
are the fractional
1\sigma
uncertainties assigned to
|V_{ud}|
and the radiative correction, respectively. For a scalar coupling normalized as in Eq. (
23
) and
m_{\phi}<m_{\pi^{+}}-m_{\pi^{0}}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\pi\phi}(g_{\rm ord},m_{\phi})

\displaystyle={\tau_{\pi^{+}}C_{\pi}|g_{\rm ord}|^{2}\over 2\pi}p_{\pi},

(53)

\displaystyle p_{\pi}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{(m_{\pi^{+}}-m_{\pi^{0}})^{2}-m_{\phi}^{2}}.

The one-sided 95% bound is obtained by profiling the nuisance parameters and solving

\Delta\chi^{2}(g_{\rm ord})=-2\ln{\cal L}_{\pi}(g_{\rm ord})+2\ln{\cal L}_{\pi,{\rm max}}=2.71.

(54)

.2
Neutron-decay likelihood

For the neutron likelihood, the observable is the total decay rate. The measured lifetime and beta-decay parameters are taken from Ref.
Navas et al. 2024
, while the neutron–proton mass difference is also tabulated by CODATA/NIST
National Institute of Standards and
Technology 2022
. The likelihood is given by

\displaystyle-2\ln{\cal L}_{n}={}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\Delta_{n}^{2}\over\sigma_{\Gamma_{n},{\rm exp}}^{2}+\sigma_{\Gamma_{n},{\rm unc}}^{2}}+\vartheta_{V}^{2}+\vartheta_{R}^{2}+\vartheta_{A}^{2},

(55)

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}={}

\displaystyle\Gamma_{n}^{\rm exp}-\Gamma_{n}^{\rm SM}(\vartheta_{V},\vartheta_{R},\vartheta_{A})-\Gamma_{n\phi}(g,m_{\phi}).

Here
\vartheta_{A}
denotes the neutron-specific nuisance associated with the axial-vector coupling
g_{A}
and beta-correlation inputs. For the SM prediction of the neutron decay rate, we use

\Gamma_{n}^{\rm SM}\propto G_{F}^{2}|V_{ud}|^{2}(1+3g_{A}^{2})f_{n}(1+\Delta_{R}^{V}+\delta_{R}^{n}),

(56)

Thus
\vartheta_{V}
and
\vartheta_{R}
are shared with the pion and nuclear likelihoods. The exotic two-body contribution is

\displaystyle\Gamma_{n\phi}(g_{\rm ord},m_{\phi})

\displaystyle={C_{n}|g_{\rm ord}|^{2}\over 2\pi}p_{n},

(57)

\displaystyle\quad p_{n}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{(m_{n}-m_{p})^{2}-m_{\phi}^{2}}.

.3
Superallowed nuclear beta-like transitions

For the nuclear likelihood, we use the corrected
{\cal F}t
formalism for superallowed
0^{+}\to 0^{+}
transitions. The required
Q_{EC}
values, half-lives, branching fractions, and nuclear and radiative corrections are tabulated in Ref.
Hardy and Towner 2020
. For an isotope
i
,

{\cal F}t_{i}=f_{i}t_{i}(1+\delta_{R,i}^{\prime})(1+\delta_{NS,i}-\delta_{C,i}),

(58)

and the SM prediction is

\displaystyle{\cal F}t_{\rm SM}(\vartheta_{V},\vartheta_{R})

\displaystyle={K\over 2G_{F}^{2}|V_{ud}|^{2}(1+\Delta_{R}^{V})}

(59)

\displaystyle\simeq{\cal F}t_{0}\left(1-2\sigma_{V}\vartheta_{V}-\sigma_{R}\vartheta_{R}\right).

The effect of a hidden branch on a published superallowed branching fraction depends on how the parent activity and the known beta branches were normalized. We therefore introduce a channel response coefficient
\alpha_{i}
. If the exotic branch is omitted from a normalization that constrains the known beta branches to sum to unity, then
\alpha_{i}=1
and

{\cal F}t_{i}^{\rm obs}={\cal F}t_{0}\,[1-\mathcal{B}_{i\phi}]+{\cal O}(\mathcal{B}_{i\phi}^{2}).

(60)

If the branching fraction is instead measured absolutely with respect to the total number of parent decays, the added width is already included and
\alpha_{i}\simeq 0
. A compact interpolation is given by

\displaystyle h_{i}(g_{\rm ord},m_{\phi})

\displaystyle\equiv 1-\alpha_{i}\mathcal{B}_{i\phi}(g_{\rm ord},m_{\phi}),

(61)

\displaystyle\qquad\mathcal{B}_{i\phi}

\displaystyle={\tau_{i}C_{i}|g_{\rm ord}|^{2}\over 2\pi}\sqrt{Q_{i}^{2}-m_{\phi}^{2}},

for
m_{\phi}<Q_{i}
, with
\tau_{i}=t_{1/2,i}/\ln 2
. The sign in Eq. (
60
) follows directly from
t_{i}=t_{1/2,i}(1+P_{EC,i})/R_{i}
: an unobserved additional width shortens
t_{1/2,i}
and, when omitted from the branching normalization, lowers the inferred partial half-life.

We perform a correlated, table-level recast of the 15 transitions entering the Hardy–Towner average. Let
\bm{y}
denote the corrected
{\cal F}t
values in their Table XVI and
\sigma_{i,{\rm tab}}
the quoted transition-specific uncertainties. The covariance matrix is

V_{ij}=\delta_{ij}\sigma_{i,{\rm tab}}^{2}+r_{i}r_{j}+n_{i}n_{j},

(62)

where

r_{i}={\cal F}t_{i}{\sigma_{\delta_{R}^{\prime},i}\over 100},\qquad n_{i}={\cal F}t_{i}{\sigma_{\delta_{NS},i}^{\rm sys}\over 100}.

(63)

The
\delta_{R}^{\prime}
uncertainties arise from the
Z^{2}\alpha^{3}
term in Table V of Ref.
Towner and Hardy 2008
and are treated as a single fully correlated nuisance, following Ref.
Hardy and Towner 2020
. The vector
\bm{n}
uses the second (systematic) uncertainty quoted in Table XI of Ref.
Hardy and Towner 2020
. Note that the diagonal Table-XVI uncertainties already contain the propagated
Q_{EC}
, half-life, branching-fraction,
\delta_{C}
, and transition-specific
\delta_{NS}
contributions, so adding them again would double count those errors.

For fixed
(g_{\rm ord},m_{\phi})
, the common conserved-vector-current (CVC) value
{\cal F}t_{0}
is profiled rather than fixed. With
\bm{h}=(h_{1},\ldots,h_{15})
,

\displaystyle\chi^{2}(g_{\rm ord},m_{\phi};{\cal F}t_{0})

\displaystyle=(\bm{y}-{\cal F}t_{0}\bm{h})^{T}V^{-1}(\bm{y}-{\cal F}t_{0}\bm{h}),

(64)

\displaystyle\widehat{{\cal F}t}_{0}(g_{\rm ord},m_{\phi})

\displaystyle={\bm{h}^{T}V^{-1}\bm{y}\over\bm{h}^{T}V^{-1}\bm{h}}.

(65)

The one-sided limit is defined by
\Delta\chi^{2}=2.71
. For the benchmark choice
\alpha_{i}=C_{i}=1
, the fit reproduces the CVC consistency of the survey, with
\chi^{2}(g=0)=6.70
for approximately 14 degrees of freedom and
\widehat{{\cal F}t}_{0}=3072.30\,\mathrm{s}
, and yields
g_{\rm nuc}^{95}(m_{\phi}=0)=2.1\times 10^{-13}
.

.4
Combined likelihood

The combined analysis uses the same 15-transition data vector, response function, and Hardy–Towner covariance as the standalone nuclear fit. In particular, the nuclear likelihood is not approximated by independent isotope-by-isotope Gaussian constraints. Using common values of
V_{ud}
and the inner radiative correction, the predicted nuclear data vector is

\displaystyle\bm{y}_{A}^{\rm pred}(g_{\rm ord},m_{\phi})

\displaystyle={K\,\bm{h}(g_{\rm ord},m_{\phi})\over 2G_{F}^{2}|V_{ud}|^{2}(1+\Delta_{R}^{V})},

(66)

\displaystyle\qquad h_{i}

\displaystyle=1-\alpha_{i}B_{i\phi},

\chi_{A}^{2}=(\bm{y}_{A}-\bm{y}_{A}^{\rm pred})^{T}V_{\rm HT}^{-1}(\bm{y}_{A}-\bm{y}_{A}^{\rm pred}),

(67)

where

is the correlated matrix in Eq. (

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\pi\beta}^{\rm pred}={}

\displaystyle\left[{1\over\tau_{n}^{\rm SM}(V_{ud},\Delta_{R}^{V},\lambda,K_{n})}+{\Gamma_{n\phi}}\right]^{-1}.

(69)

The corresponding joint chi-square is

\displaystyle\chi_{\rm comb}^{2}={}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\bigl(B_{\pi\beta}^{\rm exp}-B_{\pi\beta}^{\rm pred}\bigr)^{2}\over\sigma_{\pi}^{2}}+{\bigl(\tau_{n}^{\rm exp}-\tau_{n}^{\rm pred}\bigr)^{2}\over\sigma_{\tau_{n}}^{2}}+\chi_{A}^{2}

(70)

\displaystyle+z_{R}^{2}+z_{K}^{2}+z_{\lambda}^{2}+z_{\pi,\rm th}^{2},

where

is the single shared nuisance for
\Delta_{R}^{V}
, while

,

, and

\overline{{\cal F}t}
in Eq. (
65

\delta_{R}^{\prime}
and

and are not added a second time.

\displaystyle\Delta\chi^{2}(g_{\rm ord},m_{\phi})=

\displaystyle\chi_{\rm comb}^{2}(g_{\rm ord},m_{\phi};\widehat{\bm{\vartheta}}_{g_{\rm ord}})

\displaystyle-\chi_{\rm comb}^{2}(\widehat{g}_{\rm ord},m_{\phi};\widehat{\bm{\vartheta}}),

\displaystyle g_{\rm ord}^{95}

\displaystyle g_{\pi+n}^{95}
