Poster 130. Similar Hip-RSI Scores in Active Patients First Year After Hip Impingement vs Hip Dysplasia Surgery
Authors: Alfred A. Mansour, Abbey Laurel, Philip Sheedy, Jacob Jelmini, Nicole Lemaster, Brian Duncan, Alexis H. Aboulafia
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Objective:The Hip-Return to Sport after Injury (Hip-RSI) scale is a validated tool used to assess psychological readiness to return to sport (RTS) following hip surgery. The Hip Outcome Score SSS subscale (HOS-SSS) is an instrument to assess self-reported sport function in individuals with musculoskeletal hip disorders. Hip-RSI scores after isolated hip arthroscopy (I-HA) for femoroacetabular impingement or hip arthroscopy with concomitant periacetabular osteotomy (HA+PAO) for hip dysplasia have yet to be compared across each procedure's respective functional recovery timelines. The aim of this study was to compare Hip-RSI and HOS-SSS scores at 3-, 6-, and 12-months postoperatively between patients who underwent I-HA with those who underwent HA+PAO. We hypothesized there would be differences in Hip-RSI and HOS-SSS scores between patients after I-HA and HA+PAO groups at 3- and 6- months but no differences by 12 months.Methods:Patients who underwent either I-HA or HA+PAO from January 2021 to July 2024 from a single surgeon registry were identified. Patients completed the Hip-RSI and HOS-SSS post-operatively at 3-, 6-, and 12-months. Patients who were inactive, as confirmed by the Hip Sports Activity Scale (HSAS), or did not complete the Hip-RSI or HOS-SSS at all time points were excluded. Repeated-measures ANOVA were utilized to compare Hip-RSI and HOS-SSS scores at 3-, 6-, and 12-months postoperatively for both groups. Hip-RSI and HOS-SSS scores were reported as mean ± standard deviation and statistical significance was accepted at an alpha level of <0.05.Results:Sixty-nine subjects were included, 34 I-HA (23 females, 27.7 ± 12.8 years, BMI 24.5 ± 3.7) and 35 HA+PAO (33 females, 27.0 ± 9.0 years, BMI 23.8 ± 3.1), with no significant differences in age or BMI between groups. Group means and standard deviations for both Hip-RSI and HOS-SSS are presented in Table 1. Overall, there were significant differences in Hip-RSI scores across time, F(2,134)=36.418, p<.001. Surgical type did not have a significant effect on Hip-RSI scores over time, F(2,134)=1.198, p=.305. There were no significant differences in Hip-RSI scores between surgical groups at any time point. The I-HA group demonstrated significant improvements in Hip-RSI scores from 3- to 12-months and 6- to 12-months, but the improvement from 3- to 6- months was not significant. The HA+PAO group demonstrated significant improvements in Hip-RSI scores across all time points. All pairwise comparisons of Hip-RSI scores are presented in detail in Table 2. There were also differences in HOS-SSS scores across time, F(2,126)=101.514, p<.001. The effect of surgical type was not found to be significant on HOS-SSS scores over time, F(2,126)=2.313, p=.103. However, I-HA demonstrated significantly higher HOS-SSS scores compared to HA+PAO at 3- and 6-months, while the difference between I-HA and HA+PAO at 12-months was not found to be significant. Both surgical groups demonstrated significant differences between all timepoints with the exception of 6- to 12-months in the I-HA group. All pairwise comparisons of HOS-SSS scores are presented in detail in Table 3.Conclusions:Two discrete patterns of improvement of functional recovery and psychological readiness over the first year exist between groups. Regarding patient function, I-HA group demonstrates a rapid initial improvement while the HA+PAO group has a gradual, steady improvement in functional recovery throughout the first year with similar results achieved by 12-months. Psychological readiness in the I-HA group demonstrates slow progress initially before rapidly improving by 12-months, while the HA+PAO group again demonstrates a gradual, steady improvement ultimately resulting in similar results by 12-months.
