Executive Summary of CRADA Work:

National Technology & Engineering Solutions of Sandia, LLC (NTESS) in collaboration with
the National Renewable Energy Laboratory (NREL), and with guidance from Ramboll, will
perform fluid dynamics simulations to support of The Ocean Energy Systems (OES) Energy
Technology Collaboration Program Task 10 Wave Energy Converters (WEC) Modelling
Verification and Validation effort. Specific numerical simulations include the fixed device wave
impingement studies to benchmark the performance of simulation techniques against physical
testing results.

 • Assists laboratory in achieving programmatic scope
 • Adds new capability to the laboratory’s core competencies
 • Enhances the laboratory’s core competencies

Summary of Research Results:

Task 1: OpenFOAM Simulations

Discussion: NTESS will work with Ramboll to perform full 3-dimenstional (3D) computational
fluid dynamics (CFD) simulations, using two-phase models in the open source software
OpenFOAM. Each simulation will be performed twice for the scenario with and without the test
body in place. The first simulation will consist of a baseline wave measurement without the test
article, recording the wave height elevation at the sphere center location, and the second simulation
will include the fixed sphere and record the forces and moments on the test article for inclusion
and comparison in the OES test report.

Deliverable: Completed OpenFOAM simulations

Task 1 Results:

The open-source, finite-volume based computational fluid dynamics (CFD) package Open Field
Operation and Manipulation (OpenFOAM) version 2106 consists of a variety of built-in solvers
covering a wide range of application domains. OpenFOAM is also extensible with third-party
solvers, such as the waveFoam solver utilized in this study to model the wave absorption. The
waveFoam models the wave absorption through the use of a relaxation zone and solves the two-
phase Navier-Stokes equations for incompressible, isothermal, and immiscible fluids. The free
surface is captured in waveFoam using the volume of fluid (VOF) method and relies on the
multidimensional universal limiter for explicit solution (MULES) and an interface compression
scheme. A laminar simulation setup—which omits the use of a turbulence model—was specified
for the cases analyzed.

 2

A slightly modified version of the physical tank at Aalborg University described in Section 5 was
utilized for the 3D numerical simulations. A schematic of the numerical wave tank and key features
are shown in Figure 1, while the dimensions utilized for the 3D simulations of both the empty tank
(wave only) and the tank with the test article present are listed in Table 1. To reduce the large cost
of simulating the full width of the physical wave tank (13 m), a reduced tank width of 2.4 m was
utilized. The revised width of 2.4 m is equivalent to eight diameters of the sphere test article. A
symmetry condition was utilized along the mid-line of the numerical wave tank. The length of the
absorption relaxation zone was specified to match the length of the absorption section of the
physical tank.

Each of the test cases utilize a unique mesh configuration consisting of a prescribed number of
cells per wave height (CPH), number of cells per wavelength (CPL), and number of refinement
layers adjacent to the sphere test article. CPH specifies the number of cells in the region of
propagation in the z-direction while CPL specifies the number of cells in the x-direction. The
number of cells in the y-direction was specified to match the number of cells in the x-direction.
Refinement levels were also specified to coarsen the mesh outside the region of wave
propagation. These configurations were determined by studying a range of CPH, CPL, and layer
ranges. Figure 3 shows the mesh for the R12 that is zoomed in with and without (wave only) the
sphere test article area. This mesh is representative of the other test cases. The features shown in
Figure 3 are representative of all test cases. Each layer that is adjacent to the sphere test article
was specified to be at least 8 cells thick.

Task 2: DualSPHhysic Simulations

Discussion: NREL will work with Ramboll to perform similar simulations in task 1 using an
opensource smoothed particle hydrodynamics code, DualSPHysic. Spatial (or particle resolution)
and temporal discretization convergence studies will be conducted for the model at a specific
wave condition. This will help the team quantify the numerical uncertainty for the body (herein,
the sphere geometry which tends to be sensitive with particle resolution around the body). 3-D
simulation domains will be performed to provide hydrodynamic force outputs which are then
validated against the experimental data.

Deliverable: Completed DualSPHysic simulations

 7

Task 2 Results:

The DualSPHysics code is an open-source Smoothed Particle Hydrodynamics (SPH) model
which is a Lagrangian meshless method to discretize a continuum using a set of material points
or particles. Specifically, the discretized Navier-Stokes equations are locally integrated at the
location of each of the particles in the case of a fluid dynamic simulation. In this study, we
applied both DBC and mDBC for the numerical wave tank simulation. A variable time step and
the second-order accurate Symplectic time integrator scheme were applied with a CFL-based
value of 0.2. The Wendland model was selected for the smoothing kernel. An artificial viscosity
of 0.01 was applied in this study. Four different mesh resolutions with the ratio of wave height
and particle distances (H/Dp) of 7, 10, 15, and 20 were conducted for uncertainty quantification.
The paddle motion in the physical tank testing was modeled by the piston motion via the
experimental time history input (Same as in Figure2).

Task 3: Final reporting

Discussion: NTESS and NREL will work with Ramboll to provide detailed numerical models
based on the open-source codes, OpenFOAM and DualSphysics so that researchers of both
academia and industry can use it as a baseline model for their WEC design. An uncertainty
quantification resulting from the study (possibly via conference/journal paper) in this proposal
will provide a numerical guideline for researchers to understand the numerical accuracy of each
open-source, high-fidelity simulation code and to inform their decisions on future modeling
efforts.

Deliverable: The reported data will be in the form of hydrodynamic forces and moments on a
fixed sphere subjected to impinging waves. The reported datasets will consist of time series of
the surface elevation and the resulting forces and moments. Surface elevation is the incident,
regular wave measured at the location of sphere center (without the sphere) in a baseline case,
and forces and moments are reported on the simulated test article in a subsequent, time-aligned,
simulation which includes the sphere device. All results will be uploaded to a publicly available
repository hosted at NREL as well as uploaded to the marine and hydrokinetic data repository
(MHKDR).
