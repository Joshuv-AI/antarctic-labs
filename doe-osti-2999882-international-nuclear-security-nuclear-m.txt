1. Purpose
This document describes the use of exercise kits (called NMAC Kits) developed to support
training and technical engagements sponsored by DOE/NNSA Office of Nuclear Security
(INS) in the use of nuclear material accounting and control (NMAC) methodologies in support
of nuclear security. INS has previously used other similar kits that were originally designed to
support NMAC training for international safeguards purposes. These new kits are specifically
designed to be used in nuclear security training and emphasis the role NMAC plays in
nuclear security as well as security against the insider threat. Planning for the development
of these kits is described in LA-UR-24-30063, FY24 NMAC Kits Upgrade and served as the
initial framework for the development of the new NMAC Kit material in FY25.
The newly developed INS NMAC Kits are designed to readily and fully support flexible and
customizable exercises and demonstration tools for INS engagements. The NMAC Kits
provide all required documentation (user guides, procedures, forms, etc) and materials
necessary to illustrate NMAC good practices described in International Atomic Energy Agency
(IAEA) Nuclear Security Series documents NSS 25-G and NSS 32-T via hands-on exercises
and discussions. 1 2

1 Use of Nuclear Material Accounting and Control for Nuclear Security Purposes at Facilities. IAEA Nuclear

Security Series No. 25-G. Vienna 2015.
2 Establishing a System for Control of Nuclear Material for Nuclear Security Purposes at a Facility during Use,

Storage and Movement. IAEA Nuclear Security Series No. 32-T. Vienna 2019.

The NMAC Kit’s procedures and materials are designed for flexible use—both within the
structure outlined in the exercise documentation and independently in alternative
configurations that better align with the user’s specific instructional goals and operational
needs. Multiple kits can be combined to conduct exercises with larger groups.

 2.2 Procedure Key Terms and Navigation
To mimic facility practices, the procedures are written following recommended good
procedural practices and formats as recommended by IAEA and other professional
organizations. All NMAC Kit procedures provide an overview of important terms and
methodology of operations to align with typical facility practices.
The format of the procedures includes realistic cover pages and sections and detail
important operational information, including:
 1. Each procedure has a unique numerical identifier.
 2. Types of procedures can include:
 a. Technical Procedure: “a controlled document that outlines a series of steps
 for the operation, maintenance, or testing of a system, structure, or
 component” 3
 b. Administrative Procedure: “a controlled document that specifies actions and
 processes necessary to implement a program” 4
 c. Emergency and alarm response procedures: “define the action steps to take
 when an abnormal condition exists. Emergency procedures address
 conditions that require immediate and absolute attention to mitigate
 problems, reestablish safety boundaries, and bring operations and materials
 back within established operating parameters. Alarm response procedures
 address the actions to be performed in response to annunciators and other
 types of facility displays that indicate an off-normal condition.” 5
 3. Procedure use level 6 7
 a. Level of use: “a procedure classification that designates the minimum
 requirements for procedure use during the activity”
 i. Continuous Use: “Required for complex or infrequent work activities for
 which the consequences of an improper action could have an
 immediate, possibly irreversible adverse impact on safety, production,
 or reliability. Each step is read before that step is performed.”
 ii. Information Use: “Allowed for activities, usually administrative in
 nature, that do not involve direct contact with plant equipment, are
 performed frequently, have no immediate consequences if performed
 improperly, and are within the knowledge and skills of experienced

3. Exercise Materials
 3.1 Key Assumptions
The NMAC Kit provides 12 prelabeled, weighed and TID’d containers for direct use in the
exercises The nuclear material selected for example purposes within all NMAC Kit exercises
with simulated material has been modeled after HALEU (> 5% and < 20% NM) ‘Candium
Oxide’. The material is in loose form and procedures are developed to be robust enough to
use in a nuclear facility that contains Category 1 levels of NM. When able, the
documentation is structured to allow for conversations centered on graded approach, for
potentially considerations and adaptations for differing materials and facility setups. The
higher level of facility was decided to fully convey the depth and intricacies of NMAC
controls. The material in this exercise is in metal containers and with adhesive TIDs. The
NMAC Kit provides 12 prelabeled, weighed and TID’s containers for direct use in the
exercises. When referring to and handling these containers and the material housed inside,
the measures and controls set in place reflect the simulated category 1 nature. The
procedures allow for clauses and statements to handle as required or per work instructions
etc. This can allow for flexibility in the event differing categories of facilities or environments
are preferred with other material items. Specifics to the container set ups and the intentions
employed are described later in 4. Materials and Data Generated.
As written in all documentation and background information for exercise details and
prompts, the assumed date of the activity is June 9, 2425. This assumption allows for
repopulation of all attachment documentation, leading to the ability of printing materials
without necessary date updates and adjustments for every NMAC Kit use. It is important
that the participants are aware of this, especially in the event an irregularity investigation is
required; all investigatory documentation provided is structured and in reference to this set
date in order to allow participants to create a timeline of events without documentation
updates before every use.

 3.2 Instructor Guide
 A. Instructor Guide Overview
The intention of the NMAC Kit exercises is to provide a detailed exercise backstory, setup
and navigation through the required procedures. The procedures are purposefully written to
be vague and apply to many other exercises than the ones provided with the NMAC Kits.
Thus, the Instructor Exercise Guides provide the blueprint for the NMAC Kit User to set up,
facilitate, wrap up and reset the materials, using those procedures throughout only when
called upon.
The exercises are intended to navigate the instructors through the activity so that no matter
where or by whom the training is conducted, the performance of the exercise is the same.
When there is uncertainty in the selection or conduction of an exercise, the Instructor
Exercise Guides provide the most detailed information.
Important Note: During the course of the exercise as described and set up by the Instructor
Exercise Guide, accessing the stand-only procedures AND their subsequent datasheets in
the quantity needed for each group are necessary. The procedures and blank datasheets
are not provided in the Instructor Exercise Guide as to allow for updating procedures without
requiring update to ALL Instructor Exercise Guide in which the update procedure is required.

The NMAC Kit exercise table, Table 3, provides an overview of how to navigate the NMAC Kit
exercises. The table can be used in a variety of ways, depending on the starting place of the
NMAC Kit user. The table provides the following information for each of the 12 exercises:
 1) Exercise Description: Overall key activities housed within the exercise;
 Designed to allow for easy communication of activity intentions and a more
 specific reference than assigned number indicators.
 2) Time (hour): The approximate time in hours required to complete the activity
 with the participants. This time is an estimate and does not include setup or
 NMAC Kit reset time required. Furthermore, due to the limited quantity of
 groups per exercise, the time it the estimate to complete a single evolution of
 the intended exercise.
 3) Recommended Number of Participants per Group: The quantity of
 participants recommended to be in each group as designated by the exercise,
 detailed in the Instructor Guide. This quantity is a suggestion. Some exercises
 have specific participant role assignments which, if the quantity is exceeded,
 would require adaptation and a share of participant’s roles and
 responsibilities. Consideration for each exercise should be made before
 exceeding the suggested quantity.
 4) Groups per Kit: The quantity of total groups able to participate in the exercise
 during a single evolution using all available NMAC Kit items. This quantity
 takes into consideration the materials provided in each NMAC Kit to
 determine the quantity of groups per NMAC Kit able to be accommodated. It is
 strongly recommended that if more participants are expected, the exercise be
 completed either in multiple evolutions using the single NMAC Kit or in
 conjunction with another INS NMAC Kit.
 As noted previously, when properly maintained, the NMAC Kits are identical
 and, thus, can be used concurrently to accommodate a larger number of
 participants. It should be noted that not all exercises allow for more than a
 single group; this limitation stems from the material provided in the NMAC Kit
 in order to balance the size/weight constraints with conveyance of NMAC
 controls and methodologies appropriately.
 5) Procedures and Concepts Utilized: The procedural concepts actively used
 during the exercise. In general, all the columns under this heading represent a
 single procedure; a notable exception being ‘Offsite Shipment’ and ‘Offsite
 Receipt’. There is a single procedure covering both activities, due to the
 similarity of activities and use of the same datasheets. However, in the table
 these are separated to highlight the difference in the exercises, as these are

 17

 not performed together in any of the current NMAC Kit exercises (though the
 exercise could be run back-to-back to convey this theme easily).
 Procedure selection indicators:
 • Use of an ‘X’ indicates the of the participants to actively use the
 procedure.
 • Use of ‘Reference’ indicates portions of the exercise covering the
 procedural topic. It is recommended to have this procedure available
 for discussion or questions that may arise.
 • Use of ‘Optional’ indicates the ability of the NMAC Kit user to add an
 additional element to the exercise. Adding complexity and additional
 NMAC controls practice but also length to each evolution. Currently,
 this is in reference to a vault type room setup as locations vary.
The NMAC Kit exercise table can be navigated in a variety of methods into order to select an
appropriate exercise. Some examples of ways in which to use this table are given below:
 1) An NMAC controls event is being planned. The team requires an exercise that fits into
 less than a 2 hour time slot showcasing TIDs for beginners.
 - Using the table, starting with TIDs, there are a variety of options, potentially all.
 Concentrating on TIDs, you narrow your focus significantly to Ex. 1,2 and potentially 3
 if vaults are appropriate within the scope of the event. Deciding against the inclusion
 of vault as that is covered after this exercise would take place, Ex. 1 and 2 are left.
 Ex. 1 is suitable for beginners getting a sense of TIDs and their purpose, it is listed as
 no procedure required. Ex. 2, however, still only practicing TIDs at an introductory
 level (its sole focus is TIDs) and requires the use of the TID procedure. Therefore, the
 requirement to complete paperwork and procedural step adherence should be
 considered when deciding between the two exercises. Both can accommodate the
 same quantity of participants, so looking into the exercise objectives would help
 narrow the focus. Ultimately, the team decided upon Ex. 1 to familiarize the
 participants with TIDs and Ex. 2 later in the week to practice the lessons taught using
 a structured procedure to highlight record generation and the importance of
 procedures in the NMAC controls.
 2) A NMAC course is being planned. The team already has basic TID exercises, and the
 participants are not beginners. The team would like to introduce an exercise to
 convey more complex themes relating to the importance of item monitoring and/or
 physical inventory taking. The physical inventory taking module is before the item
 monitoring module. The irregularity investigation and resolution module can be given
 in a variety of locations throughout the event.
 - Using the table, the exercises that cover either item monitoring or physical inventory
 taking include Ex. 5-9. If the activity is placed before item monitoring, the team could
 choose between Ex. 5, 6, 8 ,9. If the irregularity module has been completed, then

 18

 Ex. 8 or 9 is applicable, if not then Ex. 5 must be used. If the activity is placed after
 item monitoring and irregularity investigation, then Ex. 8 could be used covering all
 three themes. The team has flexible exercise contents as their schedule takes form.
 They must consider the participant quantity as exercises involving PITs use all the
 NMAC Kit materials. For those exercises mentioned above, either evolutions where
 perhaps a different activity or lunch is during the time of the group performance or
 having multiple NMAC Kits should be considered and planned out.
 Ultimately, the team decided to have 3 NMAC Kits to cover the 24 participants
 simultaneously performing Ex. 6, after the PIT and irregularity investigation module.
 They decided to do larger group performances of Ex. 7 item monitoring without an
 irregularity after the item monitoring module, to practice the concept without needing
 to spend as much time with the irregularity scenario as it was covered previously.

 3.3 Participant Guide
The Participant Guide is designed with the intention of being provided directly to each for the
participants in the exercise. Each Instructor Exercise Guide has an accompanying
Participant Exercise Guide. Only a few sections are provided for the participants to use to
start and during the exercise, including the discussion questions with associated recording
space. The Participant Guide purposefully does not state when an irregularity should be
discovered. Thus, the exercises which cover the same basic NMAC controls and procedures
look very similar in the Participant Exercise Guide, but not the Instructor Exercise Guide,
such as Ex. 5 / 6 and also 7 / 8.
It is essential that the NMAC Kit user, using the Instructor Exercise Guide, prepare in
advance all necessary documentation as stated in the guide in the quantity necessary to
complete the exercise with the specified amount of participants. The Participant Exercise
Guides do not contain any material content (procedures, datasheets, report templates etc.)
required.

 4.2 Data Generation and Distribution
To ensure all are the same and the documentation functions across ALL NMAC Kits, the pre-
filled item containers are carefully constructed to be identical; All names, TID IDs, container
conditions and container gross weights are identical. While all prelabeled, empty containers
have the same assigned Item ID. All of these properties are utilized throughout all NMAC Kit
