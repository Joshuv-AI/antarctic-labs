Parameterized Verification of Asynchronous Round-Based Distributed Algorithms via Reduction to Finite-Counter Systems
Nathalie Bertrand, Pranav Ghorpade, Sasha Rubin

Abstract
Traditional model-checking techniques typically verify distributed algorithms only for a fixed number of finite-state processes. Parameterized model checking generalizes this to any number of processes, while still typically assuming that each process is finite-state. In this work, we consider asynchronous round-based distributed algorithms in which each process is infinite-state since it can execute for an infinite number of rounds. We show that the parameterized verification problem for asynchronous round-based distributed algorithms is undecidable, already for simple specifications. Nevertheless, as our main contribution, we provide a reduction to 𝖫𝖳𝖫 \mathsf{LTL} model checking over finite-counter systems and prove that it is sound and complete. This enables the use of off-the-shelf, mature symbolic model checkers for finite-counter systems. We demonstrate the practical applicability of this reduction by verifying safety and liveness properties of several asynchronous round-based consensus and leader-election algorithms using the nuXmv model checker.

Introduction

Asynchronous distributed algorithms consist of a parameterized number of identical processes that asynchronously exchange messages with other processes and update their local states.
A subclass of these is that of asynchronous round-based distributed algorithms (
\mathsf{ARDAs}
), in which each process progresses through an unbounded sequence of rounds and each message is tagged with the sender’s current round number. Typical examples include Ben-Or’s randomized consensus algorithms
[
4
]
, Bracha’s consensus algorithm
[
9
]
, and Raft’s leader-election algorithm
[
35
]
. In the absence of synchronization, processes in such algorithms may drift across rounds, so each process must store its current round index as part of its local state. Since this index is unbounded, each process has infinitely many possible states. Consequently, the global system exhibits two orthogonal sources of unboundedness: the parameterized number of processes and the unbounded round index of each process.

Parameterized verification of distributed algorithms refers to checking their correctness for
all numbers
n
of processes, provided that a resilience condition relating
n
and the maximum number
t
of faulty processes is satisfied (e.g.,
3t<n
).
Parameterized model checking provides techniques that cope with all admissible valuations of the parameters
n
and
t
, namely those satisfying the resilience condition. However, it typically assumes that processes are finite-state (see, for instance, the book
[
8
]
or the recent survey
[
25
]
) and is therefore not directly applicable to asynchronous round-based distributed algorithms. Moreover, even for fixed parameter values, the resulting system is infinite-state because of the round indices and cannot be directly handled by finite-state model checkers such as SPIN, NuSMV, or TLA+.

In this work, we introduce a modeling formalism for a broad class of
\mathsf{ARDAs}
, together with a specification logic designed to express their standard correctness properties. For this formalism, we show that the parameterized verification problem is undecidable, whereas the fixed-instance verification problem is decidable. To address the parameterized case despite this undecidability, we give a sound and complete reduction to
\mathsf{LTL}
model checking over finite-counter systems. Although this target problem is itself undecidable, it is well studied, and sound, necessarily incomplete, symbolic model-checking techniques for it have been developed and implemented, for example in nuXmv
[
10
]
. Thus, our reduction enables the use of existing symbolic model checkers for parameterized verification of
\mathsf{ARDAs}
, while also potentially benefiting from future advances in such tools.
To assess the practical applicability of the reduction, we apply it to four case studies: (i) Ben-Or’s consensus with crash faults
[
4
, Algo A]
, (ii) Ben-Or’s consensus with Byzantine faults
[
4
, Algo B]
, (iii) Bracha’s consensus with Byzantine faults
[
9
, Fig. 4]
, and (iv) Raft’s leader-election
[
35
, Sec. 5.2]
. Using nuXmv as the back end, we verify safety and liveness properties, namely agreement, validity, termination (under some condition), and leader uniqueness.
These case studies suggest that existing verification engines can efficiently handle the resulting finite-counter systems.

Related Work.

Round-based distributed algorithms belong to the broader class of fault-tolerant distributed algorithms, which also includes algorithms in which processes are finite-state, such as broadcast algorithms.
Threshold automata
[
27
,
26
]
provide a successful framework for parameterized verification of such finite-state algorithms by abstracting them to finite-counter systems that can be analyzed by symbolic model checkers.
This line of work was later extended to synchronous round-based distributed algorithms through synchronous threshold automata
[
40
]
.
For asynchronous round-based distributed algorithms (
\mathsf{ARDAs}
), a compositional approach was proposed in
[
6
]
. It reduces the verification to proof obligations for individual rounds, which are handled within the threshold-automata framework. A limitation is that the target property is not established directly: one must first identify suitable round invariants and then argue separately that these invariants imply the desired property.

Layered threshold automata
[
7
]
, together with the PyLTA prototype
[
42
]
, were later proposed as a more direct framework for verifying
\mathsf{ARDAs}
.
However, this framework is restricted to algorithms that admit a
layered
structure, thereby excluding, for instance, most leader-election algorithms.
More importantly, the corresponding verification procedure does not retain the main tooling advantage of standard threshold automata. It first gives a sound and complete abstraction to counter systems with
infinitely
many counters, and then applies a further abstraction to finite-state systems that is
incomplete
. As a result, the approach relies on a bespoke CEGAR loop rather than on off-the-shelf symbolic model checking.
In contrast, our work targets a broader class of
\mathsf{ARDAs}
, including leader-election algorithms, and gives a sound and complete reduction to finite-counter systems. This enables the use of mature general-purpose symbolic model checkers, as illustrated in Fig.
1

Traditionally, parameterized verification of

has largely relied on human-guided methods.
Interactive theorem provers such as Rocq, Isabelle/HOL, and TLA+ have been used to formalize distributed algorithms and establish their correctness through mechanized, expert-driven proofs

.
Deductive verification frameworks such as Ivy

[

For fixed-instance verification, prior work
[

applies finite-state model-checking tools to prove correctness for a fixed number of processes executing a bounded number of rounds.

The work of

[

However, their reduction is tailored to TLA+/TLC, whose specifications are written in the Temporal Logic of Actions, whereas our reduction targets standard
\mathsf{LTL}
model checking.
Moreover, our framework handles a broader class of algorithms, including Byzantine fault-tolerant consensus algorithms and leader-election algorithms.

Modeling Asynchronous Round-Based Distributed Algorithms

This section introduces the process templates that serve as our formal model, together with the specification language and verification problems studied in the paper.

2.1
Round-based Process Templates

The syntax of our model is motivated by the typical structure of
\mathsf{ARDAs}
; see Fig.
2
(left) for an example. These algorithms are parameterized; processes communicate via broadcast, with choices governed by threshold guards; and the overall behavior of each process is composed of identical, bounded, communication-closed rounds.
We now expand on these three aspects.
(i)
\mathsf{ARDAs}
are parameterized by integer variables (typically
n
for number of processes and
t
for an upper-bound on the number of faulty processes) and are designed to work when these variables satisfy a
resilience condition
, typically expressed as a linear arithmetic formula (e.g.,
t<n/2
).
(ii) In
\mathsf{ARDAs}
, communication is typically by
broadcast
. Moreover, since processes may be faulty, an
\mathsf{ARDA}
cannot rely on receiving messages from specific processes.
Accordingly, process updates are governed by
threshold guards
, which depend only on the number of received messages of each type, rather than on the identities of the senders; see step 3 in Fig.
2
(left).
(iii) In
\mathsf{ARDAs}
, the behavior of a process in each round follows the same high-level structure: a finite sequence of broadcast, receive, and compute steps, potentially followed by a progress step to a future round. As a result, rounds are
structurally identical
, and the control-flow within each round is
bounded
. Messages from different rounds are distinguished using
round tags
,
which enforce
communication closure
within rounds: messages tagged with round
r
are used exclusively during computations in round
r
.

Exploiting the identical structure of rounds in
\mathsf{ARDAs}
, our round-based process template provides a round-independent representation capturing the behavior of a
single process
within a
single round
, together with the rules governing round progress. Similar representations for
\mathsf{ARDAs}
have appeared in prior work, though in more restrictive forms
[
7
]
or in a less direct form requiring additional modeling components (e.g., auxiliary locations)
[
6
]
.

While a template describes the symbolic parameters and the behavior of a single process, the semantics of several interacting processes is represented by an infinite-state transition system. We first define the fixed-instance semantics, for a given parameter valuation, and then lift it to the parameterized semantics over all admissible parameter valuations.

Given a template
\mathcal{T}
and a parameter valuation
\nu:\mathsf{P}\to\mathbb{N}
such that
\nu\models\mathsf{rc}
,
the system of
\nu(n)
identical processes executing
\mathcal{T}
is represented by the action-labeled transition system
\mathsf{RDTS}(\mathcal{T},\nu)=\langle S_{\nu},I_{\nu},Act_{\nu},Tr_{\nu}\rangle
.
Its semantics is sketched here and detailed in App.
A.1
. Elements of
S_{\nu}
are
global states
(or configurations), each composed of a process state and a network state.
The
process state

\mathsf{PState}:\llbracket 1,\nu(n)\rrbracket\rightarrow\langle\mathsf{loc}:\mathcal{L},\mathsf{rd}:\mathbb{N},\mathsf{rcvMsg}:\mathcal{M}\times\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}\rangle
stores for each process: its current round, location, and multiset of received message types.
The latter records how many messages of a given type were received with a given round tag. The
network state

\mathsf{NState}:\mathcal{M}\times\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
collects the number of messages of each type and round tag that were broadcast.
In every configuration, the number of received messages (in process state) is pointwise bounded by the number of broadcast messages (in network state). Initial global states
I_{\nu}
are those where all processes are in initial locations at round 0 with empty set of received messages, and the network state contains no
message.
Actions in
Act_{\nu}
are of two types: (i) a reception
\textsf{Receive}(i,\langle m,r\rangle)
, where process
i
receives a message of type
m
tagged with round
r
, or (ii) an update
\mathsf{Update}(i,\rho,r)
, where process
i
updates its state according to rule
\rho
of the template at round
r
. A message reception is enabled for a process if its count of received messages (of that type and round tag) is strictly less than the number of similar broadcast messages in the network state; the process then increments its message count accordingly. An update
\mathsf{Update}(i,\rho,r)
is enabled if process
i
is at location
\rho.\mathsf{frm}
in round
r
, and its received messages with round tag
r
satisfy the guard
\rho.\mathsf{guard}
; the process location and its round are then updated to
\rho.\textsf{to}
and
r+\rho.\mathsf{type}
, and the process broadcasts a message
\textsf{Bcast}(\rho.\textsf{to})
(unless
\rho.\textsf{to}=\perp
) tagged with round
r+\rho.\mathsf{type}
on the network state. An
execution
of
\mathsf{RDTS}(\mathcal{T},\nu)
is a finite or infinite alternating sequence of configurations and transitions starting from an initial configuration.

To reason about all admissible parameter valuations simultaneously, we lift the fixed-instance transition system
\mathsf{RDTS}
to its parameterized counterpart
\mathsf{PRDTS}(\mathcal{T})
, which takes the disjoint union of all
\mathsf{RDTS}(\mathcal{T},\nu)
for
\nu\models\mathsf{rc}
(see App.
A.2
).

2.2
History State-Count Properties and Logic

The choice of a property class is central to any verification framework, as it determines both the expressiveness of the specifications and the feasibility of the reductions.
We therefore need a specification formalism that balances three requirements:
(i) expressiveness, to capture standard correctness conditions for
\mathsf{ARDAs}
, as enumerated in Fig.
3
(left); (ii) reduction-compatibility, to establish a sound and complete reduction from parameterized
\mathsf{RDTS}
to finite-counter systems; (iii)
\mathsf{LTL}
-translatability, so that the final verification problem can be expressed as standard
\mathsf{LTL}
model checking.

Previously introduced specification formalisms for threshold automata fall short in expressing standard correctness properties of
\mathsf{ARDAs}
. Specifically,
ELTL
FT

[
26
]
lacks quantification over rounds and thus cannot express standard properties such as agreement.
The extension with universal round quantifiers
[
6
]
enables reasoning about properties local to individual rounds, but it still cannot express consensus properties that inherently span multiple rounds, such as agreement.
Finally,
[
7
]
does not define a formal specification logic and relies on assumptions about its successive abstractions that guarantee preservation of correctness.

These limitations motivate our choice of specification formalism. We propose a semantic class of
history state-count
(
\mathsf{HSC}
) properties, chosen to satisfy expressiveness and reduction-compatibility. We also introduce
\mathsf{HSC}
logic (
\mathsf{HSCL}
), a syntactic fragment of temporal logic whose formulas denote a subset of
\mathsf{HSC}
properties and are designed to be
\mathsf{LTL}
-translatable while retaining expressiveness. In contrast to prior state-based approaches
[
26
,
7
]
, the
\mathsf{HSC}
properties are action-based. This distinction is important for our reduction: although relevant action histories could, in principle, be encoded by auxiliary state predicates, the specifications and reduction are most naturally formulated in terms of the occurrence of transitions. Thus, instead of taking a restricted fragment of state-based
\mathsf{LTL}
as the specification language, we use action-based
\mathsf{HSCL}
and later translate it into
\mathsf{LTL}
over the reduced finite-counter system.

2.2.1
History State-Count Properties

A History State-Count (
\mathsf{HSC}
) property applies to history state-count traces of executions. For an execution
\pi
, its
history state-count trace
(
\mathsf{hsc}
-trace) is a function
\mathsf{hsc}(\pi):\mathcal{L}\times\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
that records, along
\pi
, the aggregate number of process visits to each location in every round. Formally, for a location
\ell
and a round tag
r
,
\mathsf{hsc}(\pi)(\ell,r)
denotes the number of update actions, with some rule
\rho
and round tag
r^{\prime}
taken along
\pi
such that
\rho.\textsf{to}=\ell
and
r^{\prime}+\rho.\textsf{type}=r
. Hence, the
\mathsf{hsc}
-trace is entirely determined by the multiset of pairs
(\rho,r)
corresponding to the update actions occurring along the execution. Since each process can visit any location at most once per round (Condition
1
, Def.
1
), the
\mathsf{hsc}
-trace equivalently captures, for every pair of a location and a round tag, the number of processes that have visited it.

For a template
\mathcal{T}
with locations
\mathcal{L}
, a
history state-count
(
\mathsf{HSC}
) property is a family
\mathcal{P}=\{\mathcal{P}_{\nu}\subseteq\mathsf{Dom}\ \mid\ \nu:\mathsf{P}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}\}\text{, where }\mathsf{Dom}=\{\,f:\mathcal{L}\times\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}\,\}.
We write
\textsf{Traces}(\mathsf{RDTS}(\mathcal{T},\nu))=\{\textsf{hsc}(\pi)\ |\ \pi\text{ is an execution of $\mathsf{RDTS}(\mathcal{T},\nu)$}\}
. We write
\mathsf{RDTS}(\mathcal{T},\nu)\models\mathcal{P}

The first step exploits the fact that the

Since

While abstraction of received messages has appeared in prior work

The completeness of

As a result, in the abstract enabledness relation, the witness for one update may be

Step 3. Semi-Synchronous Restriction (

The third step exploits the fact that the

To achieve this, we impose a

The

As a result, since round jumps are bounded by

An execution

The equivalence follows from the observation that, for every transition

r_{\mathsf{max}}(\mathsf{cfg}^{\prime})=\max(r_{\mathsf{max}}(\mathsf{cfg}),\mathsf{tgt}(a))

Lemma 6
.

In

\pi

that starts from the same initial configuration as

The proof proceeds by transforming any execution

Each swap is justified by the
commutativity property
: two consecutive updates commute whenever the source round of the later differs from the target round of the earlier.
To ensure convergence, swaps are performed in a disciplined manner that gives a monotone sequence of prefixes converging to a well-defined limiting execution, which is semi-synchronous.

The same commutativity property gives a stronger result that further reduces nondeterminism in semi-synchronous executions, which we exploit in the experiments reported in §
4

For every execution, there exists a semi-synchronous execution (with the same initial configuration and the same multiset of update actions) with the additional property that we call

(whose source round is smaller than

(whose source round equals

Moreover, all jump updates are executed as a multiset in a fixed order which can alternatively be viewed as occurring synchronously in a single step.

\mathsf{BWA}

The fourth abstraction step exploits the observation that configurations need not maintain counters that can no longer influence future updates.
Since in
\mathsf{RDTS}^{\mathsf{SSR}}(\mathcal{T},\nu)
the size of the relevant window is bounded by
b+1
across all configurations, the
\mathsf{BWA}
forgets counters outside this window and encodes the relevant portion as a sliding window of width
b+1
.
In the resulting abstract system
\mathsf{RDTS}^{\mathsf{BWA}}(\mathcal{T},\nu)
, each configuration in
S_{\nu}^{\mathsf{BWA}}
is a triple consisting of a frontier round
r_{\mathsf{max}}\in\mathbb{N}
, a process state
\mathsf{PState}:\mathcal{L}\times\llbracket 0,b\rrbracket\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
and a network state
\mathsf{NState}:\mathcal{M}\times\llbracket 0,b\rrbracket\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
.
The process and network states record counters only for rounds ranging from the current frontier round down to
b
rounds below.
The
\mathsf{BWA}
induces an
action-based bisimulation

[
2
, Def. 7.15]
between
\mathsf{RDTS}^{\mathsf{SSR}}(\mathcal{T},\nu)
and
\mathsf{RDTS}^{\mathsf{BWA}}(\mathcal{T},\nu)
, establishing the following result:

Proposition 8

(Soundness and Completeness of
\mathsf{BWA}
)
.

For every template
\mathcal{T}
and parameter valuation
\nu
,

\mathsf{Traces}(\mathsf{RDTS}^{\mathsf{BWA}}(\mathcal{T},\nu))=\mathsf{Traces}(\mathsf{RDTS}^{\mathsf{SSR}}(\mathcal{T},\nu)).

The system
\mathsf{RDTS}^{\mathsf{BWA}}(\mathcal{T},\nu)
is a finite-counter system. Indeed, it maintains a single frontier-round counter
r_{\mathsf{max}}
, and for each depth
d\in\llbracket 0,b\rrbracket
, there are
|\mathcal{L}|+|\mathcal{M}|
counters: a location counter
\mathsf{PState}(\ell,d)
for every
\ell\in\mathcal{L}
and a message counter
\mathsf{NState}(m,d)
for every
m\in\mathcal{M}
.
Moreover,
\mathsf{RDTS}^{\mathsf{BWA}}(\mathcal{T},\nu)
can be viewed as a
one-counter system
with a
nondecreasing
counter. This is because the total number of processes
\nu(n)
is bounded, and each process can send only a bounded number of messages (
\nu(n)\times|\mathcal{L}|
) per round.
Consequently, all location and message counters are bounded, and the only unbounded counter is
r_{\mathsf{max}}
, which increases monotonically.
Thus, from Prop.
4
,
5
,
7
, and
8
, we have:

Theorem 9
.

For every template
\mathcal{T}
, parameter valuation
\nu
, and
\mathsf{HSC}
property
\mathcal{P}
, the system
\mathsf{RDTS}^{\mathsf{BWA}}(\mathcal{T},\nu)
is a one-counter system with a nondecreasing counter
r_{\mathsf{max}}
, and
\mathsf{RDTS}^{\mathsf{BWA}}(\mathcal{T},\nu)\models\mathcal{P}
iff

\mathsf{RDTS}(\mathcal{T},\nu)\models\mathcal{P}
.

We define the parameterized
\mathsf{BWA}
semantics, denoted
\mathsf{PRDTS}^{\mathsf{BWA}}(\mathcal{T})
, as the disjoint union of all fixed-instance systems
\mathsf{RDTS}^{\mathsf{BWA}}(\mathcal{T},\nu)
over admissible parameter valuations
\nu
.
Since the set of counter variables in
\mathsf{RDTS}^{\mathsf{BWA}}(\mathcal{T},\nu)
is independent of
\nu
, the states of
\mathsf{PRDTS}^{\mathsf{BWA}}(\mathcal{T})
can be represented using finitely many counters: the frontier-round counter; the location and message counters inherited from
\mathsf{RDTS}^{\mathsf{BWA}}(\mathcal{T},\nu)
; and an additional set of
|\mathsf{P}|
counters encoding the parameter valuation
\nu
.
Note, however, that the location and message counters in parameterized states need not be bounded, since the parameter
\nu(n)
itself may range over unbounded values.
Recall from §
2.2.1
that the parameterized semantics satisfies an
\mathsf{HSC}
property iff all its admissible fixed-instance semantics do. Hence, from Thm.
9
, we have:

Theorem 10
.

For every template
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathsf{HSC}
property
\mathcal{P}
, the system
\mathsf{PRDTS}^{\mathsf{BWA}}(\mathcal{T})
is a finite-counter system, and
\mathsf{PRDTS}^{\mathsf{BWA}}(\mathcal{T})\models\mathcal{P}
iff
\mathsf{PRDTS}(\mathcal{T})\models\mathcal{P}
.

Step 5. History-Record Extension (
\mathsf{HRE}
)

The fifth step evaluates
\mathsf{HSCL}
formulas from the state point of view by extending each
\mathsf{BWA}
configuration with a “history record” that retains exactly those fragments of the execution history that are relevant to
\mathsf{HSCL}
.
Given an
\mathsf{HSCL}
formula
\varphi
over locations
\mathcal{X}
and parameters
\mathcal{Y}
, we construct an extended system
\mathsf{RDTS}^{\mathsf{HRE}}(\mathcal{T},\nu,\varphi)
by augmenting every state in
S_{\nu}^{\mathsf{BWA}}
with two counters for each
\ell\in\mathcal{X}
:
a cumulative counter
\mathsf{cumul}(\ell)
, recording the number of visits to
\ell
across all rounds; and
a local counter
\mathsf{local}(\ell)
, recording the number of visits to
\ell
in the current frontier round. Only the frontier round requires a dedicated local counter, since under
\mathsf{BWA}
no update targets a round strictly below the frontier round. Thus, once a round is left behind, its local counts of process visits become fixed.

Observe that local counters are bounded by the total number of processes.
In contrast, cumulative counters may grow unboundedly. However, for a fixed parameter valuation
\nu
and formula
\varphi
, each cumulative counter can be capped at one more than the largest threshold appearing in the cumulative atoms of
\varphi
.
Counts beyond this bound are irrelevant to satisfaction of
\mathsf{HSCL}
formulas and can therefore be safely truncated.
As a result, the system
\mathsf{RDTS}^{\mathsf{HRE}}(\mathcal{T},\nu,\varphi)
remains a single-counter system with a nondecreasing counter
r_{\mathsf{max}}
.

Next, we give a recursive procedure to translate the
\mathsf{HSCL}
formula
\varphi
into an
\mathsf{LTL}
formula
\mathsf{LTL}(\varphi)
. We use a variant of the standard
\mathsf{LTL}
syntax in which the only temporal operator is "globally", written
\mathbf{G}
, and atomic propositions are linear-arithmetic formulas over the counter variables; see App.
A.3
for the formal syntax and semantics in our setting.
For universal round local atoms
\forall_{r}.\alpha_{r}
where
\alpha_{r}=\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{X}}c_{\ell}\cdot\kappa(\ell,r)\leq t
, define the translation
\mathsf{LTL}(\forall_{r}.\alpha_{r})=\mathbf{G}(\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{X}}c_{\ell}\cdot\mathsf{local}(\ell)\leq t)
. For cumulative atoms
\beta=\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{X}}c_{\ell}\cdot\sum_{r}\kappa(\ell,r)\leq t
, define the translation
\mathsf{LTL}(\beta)=\mathbf{G}(\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{X}}c_{\ell}\cdot\mathsf{cumul}(\ell)\leq t)
. The Boolean cases are handled compositionally:
\mathsf{LTL}(\neg\varphi)=\neg\mathsf{LTL}(\varphi)
and
\mathsf{LTL}(\varphi\wedge\varphi^{\prime})=\mathsf{LTL}(\varphi)\wedge\mathsf{LTL}(\varphi^{\prime})
. Fig.
3
provides
\mathsf{LTL}

\mathsf{HSCL}

Proposition 11

(Soundness and Completeness of
\mathsf{HRE}

.

For every
\mathsf{HSCL}
formula
\varphi
,

\mathsf{RDTS}^{\mathsf{HRE}}(\mathcal{T},\nu,\varphi)\models\mathsf{LTL}(\varphi)\text{ iff }\mathsf{RDTS}^{\mathsf{BWA}}(\mathcal{T},\nu)\models\varphi.

Step 6. Round-Identity Abstraction (
\mathsf{RIA}
)

The sixth and final step exploits the fact that the properties expressible in
\mathsf{HSCL}
are insensitive to the round identifiers. Consequently, the frontier-round counter
r_{\max}
can be dropped from
\mathsf{HRE}
configurations.
The resulting abstract system
\mathsf{RDTS}^{\mathsf{RIA}}(\mathcal{T},\nu,\varphi)
induces a

[
2

with respect to
\mathsf{RDTS}^{\mathsf{HRE}}(\mathcal{T},\nu,\varphi)
, giving:

Proposition 12

(Soundness and Completeness of
\mathsf{RIA}
)
.

For every
\mathsf{HSCL}
formula
\varphi
,

\mathsf{RDTS}^{\mathsf{RIA}}(\mathcal{T},\nu,\varphi)\models\mathsf{LTL}(\varphi)
iff
\mathsf{RDTS}^{\mathsf{HRE}}(\mathcal{T},\nu,\varphi)\models\mathsf{LTL}(\varphi)
.

Note that the system
\mathsf{RDTS}^{\mathsf{RIA}}(\mathcal{T},\nu,\varphi)
is a finite-counter system, all of whose counters are bounded by a finite value determined by the parameter valuation
\nu
, and is therefore a finite-state system. By Thm.
9
and Prop.
11
and
12
, we have:

Theorem 13
.

For every template
\mathcal{T}
, parameter valuation
\nu
, and an
\mathsf{HSCL}
formula
\varphi
,
the system
\mathsf{RDTS}^{\mathsf{RIA}}(\mathcal{T},\nu,\varphi)
is a finite-state system, and

\mathsf{RDTS}^{\mathsf{RIA}}(\mathcal{T},\nu,\varphi)\models\mathsf{LTL}(\varphi)
iff
\mathsf{RDTS}(\mathcal{T},\nu)\models\varphi
.

The parameterized
\mathsf{RIA}
semantics, denoted
\mathsf{PRDTS}^{\mathsf{RIA}}(\mathcal{T},\varphi)
, is defined as the disjoint union of all fixed-instance systems
\mathsf{RDTS}^{\mathsf{RIA}}(\mathcal{T},\nu,\varphi)
over all admissible parameter valuations
\nu
. Since the set of counters in
\mathsf{RDTS}^{\mathsf{RIA}}(\mathcal{T},\nu,\varphi)
does not depend on
\nu
, each state of
\mathsf{PRDTS}^{\mathsf{RIA}}(\mathcal{T},\varphi)
can be represented with finitely many counters: those of
\mathsf{RDTS}^{\mathsf{RIA}}(\mathcal{T},\nu,\varphi)
together with an additional
|\mathsf{P}|
counters encoding the parameter valuation
\nu
. Hence,
\mathsf{PRDTS}^{\mathsf{RIA}}(\mathcal{T},\varphi)
is a finite-counter system. The atoms of the
\mathsf{LTL}
formula
\mathsf{LTL}(\varphi)
are interpreted over these parameter counters and over the local/cumulative visit counters of
\mathsf{PRDTS}^{\mathsf{RIA}}(\mathcal{T},\varphi)

By the definition of the disjoint union,
\mathsf{PRDTS}^{\mathsf{RIA}}(\mathcal{T},\varphi)\models\mathsf{LTL}(\varphi)
iff, for all
\nu\models\textsf{rc}
,
\mathsf{RDTS}^{\mathsf{RIA}}(\mathcal{T},\nu,\varphi)\models\mathsf{LTL}(\varphi).
Therefore, by Thm.
13
, we obtain:

Theorem 14
.

For every template
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathsf{HSCL}
formula
\varphi
, the system
\mathsf{PRDTS}^{\mathsf{RIA}}(\mathcal{T},\varphi)
is a finite-counter system, and
\mathsf{PRDTS}^{\mathsf{RIA}}(\mathcal{T},\varphi)\models\mathsf{LTL}(\varphi)
iff
\mathsf{PRDTS}(\mathcal{T})\models\varphi.

and

\mathsf{PRDTS}^{\mathsf{RIA}}(\mathcal{T},\varphi)

from the input template
\mathcal{T}
and formula
\varphi

5
Conclusion

Asynchronous round-based distributed algorithms underpin many modern distributed systems, including many consensus algorithms at the core of blockchain technologies
[
44
]
. Our reduction offers a formal step toward their verification via mature symbolic model checkers and suggests a number of challenging extensions.

First, an important direction is to extend our reduction to incorporate probabilistic behavior and so allow verification of almost-sure termination.
While existing probabilistic model checkers
[
29
,
15
]
can efficiently verify almost-sure reachability conditions on
finite
MDPs, obtaining such a model is far from immediate, even for a fixed parameter valuation. The main challenge arises in the
\mathsf{SSR}
step as a probabilistic semantics requires to reorder not only a trace, but the whole execution tree.

Second, a compelling challenge is to extend our reduction to handle algorithms whose number of locations within a round grows with the round index, as is the case for variants of Paxos
[
30
,
31
]
and DAG-based consensus
[
3
,
22
,
23
,
19
,
18
]
(where the number of locations also grows with the number of processes).
Interestingly, the reduction we presented in this paper does not assume that the set of locations within each round is finite; however, the reduced system would then have infinitely many counters.

A.1
Fixed-instance semantics

Let
\mathcal{T}=\langle\mathsf{P},\mathsf{rc},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{I},\mathcal{M},\mathsf{Bcast},\mathsf{Rules}\rangle
be a template
and
\nu:\mathsf{P}\to\mathbb{N}
a
parameter valuation

such that
\nu\models\mathsf{rc}
. In the system consisting of

\nu(n)
identical processes, each process execute the behavior
specified by

\langle\mathcal{L},\mathcal{I},\mathcal{M},\mathsf{Bcast},\mathsf{Rules}\rangle
.

The
fixed-instance semantics
of the template
\mathcal{T}
for a parameter valuation
\nu
, denoted
\mathsf{RDTS}(\mathcal{T},\nu)
, is an action-labeled transition system:

\mathsf{RDTS}(\mathcal{T},\nu)\;=\;\langle S_{\nu},\;I_{\nu},\;Act_{\nu},\;Tr_{\nu}\rangle,

where
S_{\nu}
denotes the set of
configurations
,
I_{\nu}\subseteq S_{\nu}
the set of
initial configurations
,
Act_{\nu}
the
action set
, and
Tr_{\nu}\subseteq S_{\nu}\times Act_{\nu}\times S_{\nu}
the
transition relation
.

Configurations.
A
configuration
is a pair
\mathsf{cfg}=\langle\mathsf{PState},\mathsf{NState}\rangle
consisting of a process state and a network state.
The process state is a function
\mathsf{PState}:\llbracket 1,\nu(n)\rrbracket\rightarrow\langle\mathsf{loc}:\mathcal{L},\mathsf{rd}:\mathbb{N},\mathsf{rcvMsg}:\mathcal{M}\times\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}\rangle
that assigns to each process a location in
\mathcal{L}
, a round number in
\mathbb{N}
, and a multiset of received messages without sender identities. Specifically,
\mathsf{rcvMsg}(m,r)
records how many times a message of type
m\in\mathcal{M}
was received with round tag
r
.
This representation abstracts away sender identities and retains only message counts — a standard abstraction known to preserve the temporal properties typically verified for fault-tolerant distributed algorithms
[
28
]
. The reason is that correctness properties typically depend on the control locations occupied by processes, rather than on the specific messages they send or receive. Moreover, the guards that determine these locations are threshold-based and independent of process identifiers.

The network state
\mathsf{NState}:\mathcal{M}\times\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
records how many messages of each type and round tag were broadcast, irrespective of the sender’s identity.
The acyclicity assumption in Definition
1
ensures that no process visits the same location more than once per round. Consequently, each process broadcasts at most
|\mathcal{L}|
messages in a round, and the range of
\mathsf{NState}
is therefore bounded above by
\nu(n)\times|\mathcal{L}|
. Moreover, if all processes are in a round with number less than
r
(formally,
\forall i:\mathsf{PState}(i).\mathsf{rd}<r
), then
\mathsf{NState}(\cdot,r)
must be the zero function
{\bf 0}
, mapping every argument to
0
. Finally, in every configuration, the number of received messages is
pointwise bounded
by the number of broadcast messages:

\forall i,\ \forall(m,r)\in\mathcal{M}\times\mathbb{N}:\quad\mathsf{PState}(i).\mathsf{rcvMsg}(m,r)\ \leq\ \mathsf{NState}(m,r).

(1)

A configuration
\langle\mathsf{PState},\mathsf{NState}\rangle
is
initial
if every process is in an initial location at round 0 with an empty set of received messages, and the network contains no broadcasts. Formally:
for all
i\in\llbracket 1,\nu(n)\rrbracket
,
\mathsf{PState}(i).\mathsf{loc}\in\mathcal{I}
,
\mathsf{PState}(i).\mathsf{rd}=0
, and
\mathsf{PState}(i).\mathsf{rcvMsg}=\bf 0
; and
\mathsf{NState}=\bf 0
.

Actions and Transitions.

A process
i\in\llbracket 1,\nu(n)\rrbracket
can perform two types of actions: (i)
\mathsf{Recv}(i,\langle m,r\rangle)
, which represents the reception of a message of type
m
tagged with round
r
; (ii)
\mathsf{Update}(i,\rho,r)
, which represents updating its location by applying rule
\rho\in\mathsf{Rules}
at round
r\in\mathbb{N}
.
These form the action set
Act_{\nu}
, in which each action represents a step executed by a single process.
The transition relation
Tr_{\nu}
consists of all triples
(\mathsf{cfg},a,\mathsf{cfg}^{\prime})
such that

a\in Act_{\nu}
is
enabled
in the configuration
\mathsf{cfg}\in S_{\nu}
(denoted
\mathsf{enabled}(\mathsf{cfg},a)
), and executing
a
at
\mathsf{cfg}
yields
\mathsf{cfg}^{\prime}
(denoted
\mathsf{effect}(\mathsf{cfg},a)=\mathsf{cfg}^{\prime}
). We now formally define
\mathsf{enabled}
and
\mathsf{effect}
for
receive
and
update
actions.

Receive
. In a configuration
\mathsf{cfg}=\langle\mathsf{PState},\mathsf{NState}\rangle
, the action
\mathsf{Recv}(i,\langle m,r\rangle)
is enabled when process
i
has not yet received all broadcasts of type
m
tagged with round
r
. Formally,

\mathsf{enabled}(\mathsf{cfg},\mathsf{Recv}(i,\langle m,r\rangle)):=\mathsf{PState}(i).\mathsf{rcvMsg}(m,r)<\mathsf{NState}(m,r).

Executing
\mathsf{Recv}(i,\langle m,r\rangle)
delivers one message of type
m
tagged with round
r
to process
i
and leaves all other components unchanged. Formally,

\mathsf{effect}(\mathsf{cfg},\mathsf{Recv}(i,\langle m,r\rangle)):=\langle\mathsf{PState}^{\prime},\mathsf{NState}^{\prime}\rangle
, where:

•

For every process
j\neq i
, the state remains unchanged:
\mathsf{PState}^{\prime}(j)=\mathsf{PState}(j)
.

•

Location and round component of process
i
state remain unchanged:

\mathsf{PState}^{\prime}(i).\mathsf{loc}=\mathsf{PState}(i).\mathsf{loc}
and
\mathsf{PState}^{\prime}(i).\mathsf{rd}=\mathsf{PState}(i).\mathsf{rd}
.

•

The receive component of process
i
state is incremented at
\langle m,r\rangle
:

\mathsf{PState}^{\prime}(i).\mathsf{rcvMsg}(m,r)=\mathsf{PState}(i).\mathsf{rcvMsg}(m,r)+1
and for all

\langle m^{\prime},r^{\prime}\rangle\neq\langle m,r\rangle
,
\mathsf{PState}^{\prime}(i).\mathsf{rcvMsg}(m^{\prime},r^{\prime})=\mathsf{PState}(i).\mathsf{rcvMsg}(m^{\prime},r^{\prime})
.

•

The network state remains unchanged:
\mathsf{NState}^{\prime}=\mathsf{NState}
.

Update.
In a configuration
\mathsf{cfg}=\langle\mathsf{PState},\mathsf{NState}\rangle
, the action
\mathsf{Update}(i,\rho,r)
is enabled when process
i
is in location
\rho.\mathsf{frm}
at round
r
, and its received messages in round
r
satisfy the guard
\rho.\mathsf{guard}
. Formally,

\displaystyle\mathsf{enabled}(\mathsf{cfg},\mathsf{Update}(i,\rho,r)):=\

\displaystyle\mathsf{PState}(i).(\mathsf{loc},\mathsf{rd})=(\rho.\mathsf{frm},r)\ \text{and}

\displaystyle\mathsf{PState}(i).\mathsf{rcvMsg}(\_,r)\models\rho.\mathsf{guard}[\mathsf{P}\leftarrow\nu].

Executing
\mathsf{Update}(i,\rho,r)
has two effects. First, process
i
moves from location
\rho.\mathsf{frm}
to
\rho.\mathsf{to}
and updates its round counter to
r+\rho.\mathsf{type}
. Second, it broadcasts a message of type
\mathsf{Bcast}(\rho.\mathsf{to})
, tagged with round
r+\rho.\mathsf{type}
. Formally,

\mathsf{effect}(\mathsf{cfg},\mathsf{Update}(i,\rho,r))=\langle\mathsf{PState}^{\prime},\mathsf{NState}^{\prime}\rangle
, where:

•

For every process
j\neq i
, the state remains unchanged:
\mathsf{PState}^{\prime}(j)=\mathsf{PState}(j)
.

•

The location component of process
i
is updated to
\rho.\mathsf{to}
:

\mathsf{PState}^{\prime}(i).\mathsf{loc}=\rho.\mathsf{to}
.

•

The round component of process
i
state is incremented by
\rho.\mathsf{type}
:

\mathsf{PState}(i)^{\prime}.\mathsf{rd}=r+\rho.\mathsf{type}

•

The receive component of process
i
remains unchanged:

\mathsf{PState}^{\prime}(i).\mathsf{rcvMsg}(m,r)=\mathsf{PState}(i).\mathsf{rcvMsg}(m,r)
.

•

The network state is incremented at
\langle\mathsf{Bcast}(\rho.\mathsf{to}),r+\rho.\mathsf{type}\rangle
:

\mathsf{NState}^{\prime}(m^{\prime},r^{\prime})=\mathsf{NState}(m^{\prime},r^{\prime})+1
if
\langle m^{\prime},r^{\prime}\rangle=\langle\mathsf{Bcast}(\rho.\mathsf{to}),r+\rho.\mathsf{type}\rangle
, and
\mathsf{NState}^{\prime}(m^{\prime},r^{\prime})=\mathsf{NState}(m^{\prime},r^{\prime})
otherwise.

An
execution
of
\mathsf{RDTS}(\mathcal{T},\nu)
is a finite or infinite alternating sequence
\pi=\mathsf{cfg}_{0}a_{1}\mathsf{cfg}_{1}a_{2}\dots,
such that
\mathsf{cfg}_{0}\in I_{\nu}
is an initial configuration and, for all
k
,
(\mathsf{cfg}_{k},a_{k+1},\mathsf{cfg}_{k+1})\in Tr_{\nu}
. We adopt this definition of executions uniformly for all subsequent semantics and do not restate it.

A.2
Parameterized semantics

The parameterized semantics is a disjoint union of all fixed-instance semantics
\mathsf{RDTS}(\mathcal{T},\nu)
for
\nu\models\mathsf{rc}
.
Fix a template
\mathcal{T}
, and let
\mathsf{Val}=\{\nu:\mathsf{P}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}\ |\ \nu\models\mathsf{rc}\}
denote the set of all admissible parameter valuations.
The parameterized semantics of
\mathcal{T}
is an action-labeled transition system, called the
parameterized

\mathsf{RDTS}
(
\mathsf{PRDTS}
), defined as

\mathsf{PRDTS}(\mathcal{T})=\langle S,\;I,\;Act,\;Tr\rangle,

which is the
disjoint union
of
\mathsf{RDTS}(\mathcal{T},\nu)
over all

A.3
\mathsf{LTL}
Model Checking over Finite-Counter Systems

We use a notion of finite-counter system that can be directly represented as a
nuXmv model. A
finite-counter system

\mathcal{C}
is a tuple

(V,\mathsf{INVAR},\mathsf{INIT},\mathsf{TRAN})
, where

V=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\}
is a finite set of variables (also called counters) ranging over

\mathbb{N}
,
\mathsf{INVAR}
and
\mathsf{INIT}
are linear-arithmetic formulas over
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}
, and

\mathsf{TRAN}
is a linear-arithmetic formula over

x_{1},\ldots,x_{k},x^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,x^{\prime}_{k}
. Here,
x^{\prime}_{i}
denotes the next-state copy
of
x_{i}
.

A finite-counter system
\mathcal{C}=(V,\mathsf{INVAR},\mathsf{INIT},\mathsf{TRAN})

induces a transition system
\mathcal{S}_{C}:=(Q,Q_{0},\to)
, where
Q
is the set of
valuations
s:V\to\mathbb{N}
satisfying
\mathsf{INVAR}
,

Q_{0}\subseteq Q
is the set of valuations satisfying
\mathsf{INIT}
, and

\to\subseteq Q\times Q
contains exactly the pairs
(s,s^{\prime})
satisfying

\mathsf{TRAN}
, i.e., those for which

\mathsf{TRAN}
is true after substituting each
x_{i}
by
s(x_{i})
and
each
x^{\prime}_{i}
by
s^{\prime}(x_{i})
. Intuitively,
\mathsf{INVAR}
defines
the admissible counter values,
\mathsf{INIT}
defines the admissible initial
counter values, and
\mathsf{TRAN}
relates current counter values to their possible next values.

We use a fragment of the syntax of
\mathsf{LTL}

[
2
]
, defined according to the following grammar:

\varphi::=\mathit{true}\mid p\mid\varphi\land\varphi\mid\lnot\varphi\mid\mathbf{G}\varphi

where
\mathbf{G}
is the "globally" operator (we do not use the "next" or "until" operators), and
p
varies over the
atomic propositions
which are taken to be the linear-arithmetic formulas over
V
(rather than abstract symbols). Note that we can interpret atoms in states of the transition system
\mathcal{S_{C}}
(this implicitly
defines a labeling function mapping each state to the set of
linear-arithmetic formulas true in that state, but we will not use this labeling function explicitly).

We inductively define what it means for an infinite sequence
\pi=\pi_{0}\pi_{1}\cdots
, with
\pi_{i}:V\to\mathbb{N}
for all
i
, to
satisfy
an
\mathsf{LTL}
formula, as usual
[
2
]
:

•

\pi\models\mathit{true}
;

•

\pi\models p
if the atom
p
is true under the valuation
\pi_{0}
;

•

\pi\models\varphi_{1}\land\varphi_{2}
if
\pi\models\varphi_{1}
and
\pi\models\varphi_{2}
;

•

\pi\models\lnot\varphi
if
\pi\not\models\varphi
;

•

\pi\models\mathbf{G}\varphi
if for all
j\geq 0
, it holds that
\pi_{j}\pi_{j+1}\cdots\models\varphi
.

A
path
of
\mathcal{S_{C}}
is a sequence
\pi=s_{0}s_{1}s_{2}\cdots
such that
s_{i}\in{Q}
for all
i
,
s_{0}\in Q_{0}
, and

(s_{i},s_{i+1})\in\to
for every
i
. A
maximal
path is either an infinite path or a finite path that ends in a
terminal
state
s
, i.e., for which there is no state
s^{\prime}\in Q
such that
(s,s^{\prime})\in\to
. A finite maximal path

s_{0}s_{1}\cdots s_{n}
is said to
satisfy

\varphi
iff its infinite stuttering extension

s_{0}s_{1}\cdots s_{n}s_{n}s_{n}\cdots
satisfies
\varphi
.

Finally, given a finite-counter system

\mathcal{C}
and an
\mathsf{LTL}
formula

\varphi
, we write
\mathcal{C}\models\varphi
, and say that
\mathcal{C}

satisfies

\varphi
, iff every maximal path of the induced transition system

\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{C}}
satisfies
\varphi
.

Undecidability of Parameterized

-Verification

We give the detailed construction of
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{C}}
and

used in the proof of Theorem

.

The template
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{C}}
has jump bound

and is defined as follows.

•

Controller locations and messages.

For each control state

, there is a location

, with no broadcast message.
For each command

If

Non-controller locations and messages.

There are three main non-controller locations

,

, and

m_{\mathsf{rdy},c_{1}}

For each

and each

l_{x,c_{i}}
, which broadcasts
m_{\mathsf{ack}}
.

•

Initial locations.

The initial locations are
l_{s_{\mathsf{init}}}
and
l_{\mathsf{res}}
.

•

Type-
0
rules.

For each command
\mathsf{cmd}=(s,\mathsf{op},s^{\prime})\in\Delta
, the template has a rule
l_{s}\to l_{\mathsf{cmd}}
with guard
m_{\mathsf{rdy},c_{1}}+m_{\mathsf{rdy},c_{2}}+m_{\mathsf{rdy},\mathsf{res}}=n-1
.
Additionally, if
\mathsf{op}=\mathsf{iszero}(c_{i})
, then the guard is conjugated with
m_{\mathsf{rdy},c_{i}}=0
.
For each
i\in\{1,2\}
, there is a rule
l_{\mathsf{res}}\to l_{\mathsf{inc},c_{i}}
with guard
m_{\mathsf{inc},c_{i}}>0

•

Type-
1

All type-
1

For each command
\mathsf{cmd}=(s,\mathsf{op},s^{\prime})\in\Delta

.
For each

Here,

enforces that at most one process visits a controller location in each round,
\varphi_{\mathsf{step}}
enforces that at most one process performs an increment/decrement move in each round, and

B.1
Receive-message abstraction

The system
\mathsf{RDTS}\textsuperscript{$\mathsf{RMA}$}(\mathcal{T},\nu)
removes the per-process records of received messages and eliminates
\mathsf{Receive}
actions, while deeming
\mathsf{Update}(i,\rho,r)
enabled iff there exists a receive multiset
R:\mathcal{M}\times\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
such that (i)
0\leq R\leq\mathsf{NState}
(pointwise), and (ii) if process
i
had received exactly
R
, the same update would be permitted in the concrete semantics. Equivalently, guards are evaluated
existentially
with respect to subset of the
\mathsf{NState}
.

Projection.

For a configuration
\mathsf{cfg}=\langle\mathsf{PState},\mathsf{NState}\rangle
, we define the
\mathsf{RMA}
projection
\alpha_{\mathsf{RMA}}(\mathsf{cfg})=\langle\mathsf{PState}_{\mathsf{RMA}},\ \mathsf{NState}_{\mathsf{RMA}}\rangle,
where

\mathsf{PState}_{\mathsf{RMA}}:\llbracket 1,\nu(n)\rrbracket\rightarrow\langle\mathsf{loc}:\mathcal{L},\mathsf{rd}:\mathbb{N}\rangle

is given, for each process
i
, by

\mathsf{PState}_{\mathsf{RMA}}(i)=\langle\mathsf{loc}\mapsto\mathsf{PState}(i).\mathsf{loc},\ \mathsf{rd}\mapsto\mathsf{PState}(i).\mathsf{rd}\rangle,

and
\mathsf{NState}_{\mathsf{RMA}}=\mathsf{NState}
.

The
\mathsf{RMA}
semantics
\mathsf{RDTS}\textsuperscript{$\mathsf{RMA}$}(\mathcal{T},\nu)=\langle S_{\nu}^{\mathsf{RMA}},I_{\nu}^{\mathsf{RMA}},Act_{\nu}^{\mathsf{RMA}},Tr_{\nu}^{\mathsf{RMA}}\rangle
, where

S_{\nu}^{\mathsf{RMA}}=\{\alpha_{\mathsf{RMA}}(\mathsf{cfg})\mid\mathsf{cfg}\in S_{\nu}\}
,

I_{\nu}^{\mathsf{RMA}}=\{\alpha_{\mathsf{RMA}}(\mathsf{cfg})\mid\mathsf{cfg}\in I_{\nu}\}
,
the actions in
Act_{\nu}^{\mathsf{RMA}}
are exactly the
\mathsf{Update}(i,\rho,r)
actions of the concrete system,
and the transition relation
Tr_{\nu}^{\mathsf{RMA}}
is determined by the following enabledness and effect conditions. For
\mathsf{cfg}_{\mathsf{RMA}}=\langle\mathsf{PState}_{\mathsf{RMA}},\mathsf{NState}_{\mathsf{RMA}}\rangle
and
a=\mathsf{Update}(i,\rho,r)
,

\displaystyle\mathsf{enabled}_{\mathsf{RMA}}\big(\mathsf{cfg}_{\mathsf{RMA}},a\big)=\

\displaystyle\mathsf{PState}_{\mathsf{RMA}}(i).(\textsf{loc, rd})=(\rho.\mathsf{frm},r)\ \ \wedge

\displaystyle\exists R:\ 0\leq R\leq\mathsf{NState}_{\mathsf{RMA}}\ \wedge\ R(\cdot,r)\models\rho.\mathsf{guard}[\mathsf{P}\leftarrow\nu].

The abstract effect updates the process’s location and round exactly as in the corresponding concrete effect, and modifies
\mathsf{NState}_{\mathsf{RMA}}
in the same way (see Appendix
A.1
).
For
\pi=\mathsf{cfg}_{0}a_{1}\mathsf{cfg}_{1}a_{2}\dots
an execution of
\mathsf{RDTS}^{\mathsf{RMA}}(\mathcal{T},\nu)
, we define its
hsc
-trace exactly as in §
2.2.1
, i.e.,

\textsf{hsc}(\pi)(\ell,r)\;=\;\bigl|\{\,k\mid a_{k}=\mathsf{Update}(i,\rho,r^{\prime}),\ \rho.\mathsf{to}=\ell,\ r=r^{\prime}+\rho.\mathsf{type}\,\}\bigr|.

Below we establish a forward simulation between the concrete semantics and the
\mathsf{RMA}
semantics.

Lemma 15

(Forward simulation)
.

For every configuration
\mathsf{cfg}\in S_{\nu}
, if
\mathsf{cfg}\in I_{\nu}
then
\alpha_{\mathsf{RMA}}(\mathsf{cfg})\in I_{\nu}^{\mathsf{RMA}}
. Moreover if
(\mathsf{cfg},a,\mathsf{cfg}^{\prime})\in Tr_{\nu}
, the following hold:

1.

If
a=\mathsf{Recv}(i,\langle m,r\rangle)
, then
\alpha_{\mathsf{RMA}}(\mathsf{cfg})=\alpha_{\mathsf{RMA}}(\mathsf{cfg}^{\prime})
.

2.

If
a=\mathsf{Update}(i,\rho,r)
, then
(\alpha_{\mathsf{RMA}}(\mathsf{cfg}),a,\alpha_{\mathsf{RMA}}(\mathsf{cfg}^{\prime}))\in Tr_{\nu}^{\mathsf{RMA}}
.

Proof.

1. A receive transition changes only the received messages (
rcvMsg
) by process
i
;
\alpha_{\mathsf{RMA}}
forgets exactly this component thus
\alpha_{\mathsf{RMA}}(\mathsf{cfg})=\alpha_{\mathsf{RMA}}(\mathsf{cfg}^{\prime})
.

2. In the
\mathsf{RDTS}(\mathcal{T},\nu)
,
a=\mathsf{Update}(i,\rho,r)
enabledness requires at
cfg
:

\mathsf{PState}(i).(\textsf{loc},\textsf{rd})=(\rho.\mathsf{frm},r)\text{ and }\mathsf{PState}(i).\mathsf{RecMsg}(\cdot,r)\models\rho.\mathsf{guard}[\mathsf{P}\leftarrow\nu].

By equation
1
,
\mathsf{PState}(i).\mathsf{RecMsg}\leq\mathsf{NState}
. Since the
\alpha_{\mathsf{RMA}}
projection preserves
\textsf{PState}.(\textsf{loc},\textsf{rd})
and
\mathsf{NState}
,
R=\mathsf{PState}(i).\mathsf{RecMsg}
(at
cfg
) witnesses abstract enabledness. The abstract effect mirrors the concrete one on the components it keeps. Therefore
(\alpha_{\mathsf{RMA}}(\mathsf{cfg}),a,\alpha_{\mathsf{RMA}}(\mathsf{cfg}^{\prime}))\in Tr_{\nu}^{\mathsf{RMA}}
.
∎

Next, we establish Refinement between the
\mathsf{RMA}
semantics and the concrete semantics using Condition

from Def.

.

Lemma 16

(Refinement)
.

For every template
\mathcal{T}
, there exists
a mapping
\beta_{\mathsf{RMA}}:S_{\nu}^{\mathsf{RMA}}\to S_{\nu}

1.

For every

.

2.

For every
(\mathsf{cfg}_{\mathsf{RMA}},a,\mathsf{cfg}^{\prime}_{\mathsf{RMA}})\in Tr_{\nu}^{\mathsf{RMA}}

there exists a concrete fragment

\mathsf{cfg}_{1}\xrightarrow{a_{1}}\cdots\xrightarrow{a_{n}}\mathsf{cfg}_{n+1}

with
\beta_{\mathsf{RMA}}(\mathsf{cfg}_{\mathsf{RMA}})=\mathsf{cfg}_{1}
,

\beta_{\mathsf{RMA}}(\mathsf{cfg}^{\prime}_{\mathsf{RMA}})=\mathsf{cfg}_{n+1}

1\leq j\leq n:

a_{j}=a
and for all

,

is
\mathsf{Recv}

Sketch.

For an
\mathsf{RMA}
configuration
\mathsf{cfg}_{\mathsf{RMA}}=\langle\mathsf{PState}_{\mathsf{RMA}},\mathsf{NState}_{\mathsf{RMA}}\rangle
, define the refinement mapping
\beta_{\mathsf{RMA}}(\mathsf{cfg}_{\mathsf{RMA}})=\mathsf{cfg}=\langle\mathsf{PState},\mathsf{NState}\rangle
with (i)
\mathsf{NState}=\mathsf{NState}_{\mathsf{RMA}}
, (ii) for process

\mathsf{PState}(i).(\mathsf{loc},\mathsf{rd})=\mathsf{PState}_{\mathsf{RMA}}(i).(\mathsf{loc},\mathsf{rd})

•

\mathsf{PState}(i).\mathsf{rcvMsg}(\cdot,r^{\prime})=\mathsf{NState}_{\mathsf{RMA}}(\cdot,r^{\prime})

\mathsf{PState}(i).\mathsf{rcvMsg}(\cdot,r^{\prime})=0

•

\mathsf{PState}(i).\mathsf{rcvMsg}(m,\mathsf{PState}_{\mathsf{RMA}}(i).\mathsf{rd})=\mathsf{NState}_{\mathsf{RMA}}(m,\mathsf{PState}(i).\mathsf{rd})
if

and
0
otherwise, where

\mathsf{PState}_{\mathsf{RMA}}(i).\mathsf{loc}

To see that
\beta_{\mathsf{RMA}}
satisfies Part 1, note that by Def.

.
The key observation for Part

\mathsf{RMA}

\mathsf{cfg}_{\mathsf{RMA}}=\langle\mathsf{PState}_{\mathsf{RMA}},\,\mathsf{NState}_{\mathsf{RMA}}\rangle

where
S_{\nu}^{\mathsf{PIA}}=\{\,\alpha_{\mathsf{PIA}}(\mathsf{cfg})\mid\mathsf{cfg}\in S_{\nu}^{\mathsf{RMA}}\,\}
,
I_{\nu}^{\mathsf{PIA}}=\{\,\alpha_{\mathsf{PIA}}(\mathsf{cfg})\mid\mathsf{cfg}\in I_{\nu}^{\mathsf{RMA}}\,\},

(no process ids), and the transition relation

is determined by the following enabledness and effect conditions.

For
\mathsf{cfg}_{\mathsf{PIA}}=\alpha_{\mathsf{PIA}}(\mathsf{cfg}_{\mathsf{RMA}})

for some
\mathsf{RMA}
configuration
\mathsf{cfg}_{\mathsf{RMA}}
, the abstract action

is enabled iff there exists a process
i
at
\mathsf{cfg}_{\mathsf{PIA}}

(\rho.\mathsf{frm},r)
in
\mathsf{cfg}_{\mathsf{RMA}}
such that
\mathsf{Update}(i,\rho,r)
is enabled
in
\mathsf{cfg}_{\mathsf{RMA}}
. Equivalently, for
\mathsf{cfg}_{\mathsf{PIA}}=\langle\mathsf{PState}_{\mathsf{PIA}},\mathsf{NState}_{\mathsf{PIA}}\rangle

\displaystyle\mathsf{enabled}_{\mathsf{PIA}}\!\big(\mathsf{cfg}_{\mathsf{PIA}},a\big)=\

\displaystyle\mathsf{PState}_{\mathsf{PIA}}(\rho.\mathsf{frm},\,r)>0\ \ \wedge

\displaystyle\exists R:\ 0\leq R\leq\mathsf{NState}_{\mathsf{PIA}}\ \wedge\ R(\cdot,r)\models\rho.\mathsf{guard}[\mathsf{P}\leftarrow\nu].

Upon executing

at
\mathsf{cfg}_{\mathsf{PIA}}
, the

is updated to reflect move of one process from
(\rho.\mathsf{frm},r)
to
(\rho.\mathsf{to},\,r+\rho.\mathsf{type})

Formally,

\mathsf{effect}_{\mathsf{PIA}}(\mathsf{cfg}_{\mathsf{PIA}},\,\mathsf{Update}(\rho,r))=\langle\mathsf{PState}^{\prime}_{\mathsf{PIA}},\,\mathsf{NState}^{\prime}_{\mathsf{PIA}}\rangle
, where:

\mathsf{PState}^{\prime}_{\mathsf{PIA}}(\ell,r^{\prime})=\mathsf{PState}_{\mathsf{PIA}}(\ell,r^{\prime})+1

For
\pi=\mathsf{cfg}_{0}a_{1}\mathsf{cfg}_{1}a_{2}\dots
an execution of

\textsf{hsc}(\pi)(\ell,r)\;=\;\bigl|\{\,k\mid a_{k}=\mathsf{Update}(\rho,r^{\prime}),\ \rho.\mathsf{to}=\ell,\ r=r^{\prime}+\rho.\mathsf{type}\,\}\bigr|.

Lemma 17

(Action-based bisimulation)

\forall\ \mathsf{cfg}\in I_{\nu}^{\mathsf{RMA}}:

For all

\Rightarrow

(\mathsf{cfg},a,\mathsf{cfg}^{\prime})\in Tr_{\nu}^{\mathsf{RMA}}

(\alpha_{\mathsf{PIA}}(\mathsf{cfg}),\delta(a),\alpha_{\mathsf{PIA}}(\mathsf{cfg}^{\prime}))\in Tr_{\nu}^{\mathsf{PIA}}

\Leftarrow

B.3
Semi-synchronous restriction

B.3.1
\mathsf{SSR}

The system
\mathsf{RDTS}^{\mathsf{SSR}}(\mathcal{T},\nu)=\langle S_{\nu}^{\mathsf{SSR}},I_{\nu}^{\mathsf{SSR}},Act_{\nu}^{\mathsf{SSR}},Tr_{\nu}^{\mathsf{SSR}}\rangle,
where
S_{\nu}^{\mathsf{SSR}}=S_{\nu}^{\mathsf{PIA}}
,
I_{\nu}^{\mathsf{SSR}}=I_{\nu}^{\mathsf{PIA}}
,
Act_{\nu}^{\mathsf{SSR}}=Act_{\nu}^{\mathsf{PIA}}
, and

Tr_{\nu}^{\mathsf{SSR}}=\{(\mathsf{cfg},a,\mathsf{cfg}^{\prime})\in Tr_{\nu}^{\mathsf{PIA}}\ |\ \mathsf{tgt}(a)\geq r_{\mathsf{max}}(\mathsf{cfg})\}.

Equivalently, enabledness and effect are given by

\mathsf{enabled}_{\mathsf{SSR}}(\mathsf{cfg},a)=\mathsf{enabled}_{\mathsf{PIA}}(\mathsf{cfg},a)\wedge\mathsf{tgt}(a)\geq r_{\mathsf{max}}(\mathsf{cfg})

and
\mathsf{effect}_{\mathsf{SSR}}(\mathsf{cfg},a)=\mathsf{effect}_{\mathsf{PIA}}(\mathsf{cfg},a)
.

The
hsc
-trace of executions of
\mathsf{RDTS}^{\mathsf{SSR}}(\mathcal{T},\nu)
is defined identically to that of executions of
\mathsf{RDTS}^{\mathsf{PIA}}(\mathcal{T},\nu)
(see Appendix
B.2
). Since the action sets of the two systems coincide, this is well defined.

Recall that every rule
\rho\in\mathsf{Rules}
satisfies
\rho.\mathsf{type}\leq b
, where
b\in\mathbb{N}
is the
jump bound
of the template
\mathcal{T}
. Hence, in a configuration
\mathsf{cfg}
, no update
\mathsf{Update}(\rho,r)
with
r<r_{\mathsf{max}}(\mathsf{cfg})-b
is enabled under
\mathsf{SSR}
. Consequently, in configuration
\mathsf{cfg}
all counters below
r_{\mathsf{max}}(\mathsf{cfg})-b
can be discarded, while counters above
r_{\mathsf{max}}(\mathsf{cfg})
are zero by definition. Thus the relevant window of any
\mathsf{SSR}
configuration
\mathsf{cfg}
is
\llbracket r_{\mathsf{max}}(\mathsf{cfg})-b,r_{\mathsf{max}}(\mathsf{cfg})\rrbracket
, of fixed size
b+1
, uniformly bounded across all configurations.
For an update action
a=\mathsf{Update}(\rho,r)
, let
\mathsf{tgt}(a)=r+\rho.\mathsf{type}
.

An execution
\pi=\mathsf{cfg}_{1}\,a_{1}\,\mathsf{cfg}_{2}\,a_{2}\cdots
of the
\mathsf{RDTS}^{\mathsf{PIA}}(\mathcal{T},\nu)
is called
semi-synchronous
if its target sequence
\mathsf{tgt}(a_{1})\mathsf{tgt}(a_{2})\dots
is non-decreasing. This notion precisely characterizes executions of
\mathsf{RDTS}^{\mathsf{SSR}}(\mathcal{T},\nu)
, as formalized and proved in following lemma.

Lemma 18
.

An execution
\pi=\mathsf{cfg}_{1}a_{1}\mathsf{cfg}_{2}a_{2}\dots
of
\mathsf{RDTS}^{\mathsf{PIA}}(\mathcal{T},\nu)
is semi synchronous iff it is also an execution of
\mathsf{RDTS}^{\mathsf{SSR}}(\mathcal{T},\nu)
.

Proof.

To prove the lemma we first prove following claim:

Claim 19
.

For any
(\mathsf{cfg},a,\mathsf{cfg}^{\prime})\in Tr_{\nu}^{\mathsf{PIA}}
,
r_{\mathsf{max}}(\mathsf{cfg}^{\prime})=\max\bigl(r_{\mathsf{max}}(\mathsf{cfg}),\,\mathsf{tgt}(a)\bigr).

Proof of claim.
By construction of
\mathsf{PIA}
an action
a=\mathsf{Update}(\rho,r)
modifies only counters at rounds
r
and
\mathsf{tgt}(a)>r
. If
\mathsf{tgt}(a)<r_{\mathsf{max}}(\mathsf{cfg})
, then
r_{\mathsf{max}}(\mathsf{cfg}^{\prime})=r_{\mathsf{max}}(\mathsf{cfg})
. If
\mathsf{tgt}(a)\geq r_{\mathsf{max}}(\mathsf{cfg})
, the transition increments the
\rho.\mathsf{to}
location counter at round
\mathsf{tgt}(a)
, making it positive, so
r_{\mathsf{max}}(\mathsf{cfg}^{\prime})=\mathsf{tgt}(a)
. In both cases the claim holds.

Now suppose
\pi
is semi-synchronous execution of
\mathsf{RDTS}^{\mathsf{PIA}}(\mathcal{T},\nu)
, i.e.,
\mathsf{tgt}(a_{1})\leq\mathsf{tgt}(a_{2})\leq\cdots
. Since every
\mathsf{cfg}\in I_{\nu}^{\mathsf{PIA}}
satisfies
r_{\mathsf{max}}(\mathsf{cfg})=1
, we have
\mathsf{tgt}(a_{1})\geq r_{\mathsf{max}}(\mathsf{cfg}_{1})
. By the claim
r_{\mathsf{max}}(\mathsf{cfg}_{2})=\mathsf{tgt}(a_{1})
. Inductively, if
r_{\mathsf{max}}(\mathsf{cfg}_{i})=\mathsf{tgt}(a_{i-1})
then
\mathsf{tgt}(a_{i})\geq\mathsf{tgt}(a_{i-1})=r_{\mathsf{max}}(\mathsf{cfg}_{i})
, so the
\mathsf{SSR}
enabledness restriction holds at step
i
. Thus
\pi
is an execution of
\mathsf{RDTS}^{\mathsf{SSR}}(\mathcal{T},\nu)
.

Conversely, suppose
\pi
is an execution of
\mathsf{RDTS}^{\mathsf{SSR}}(\mathcal{T},\nu)
. Then by definition
\mathsf{tgt}(a_{i})\geq r_{\mathsf{max}}(\mathsf{cfg}_{i})
for all
i
. The above claim gives
r_{\mathsf{max}}(\mathsf{cfg}_{i+1})=\mathsf{tgt}(a_{i})
, which implies
\mathsf{tgt}(a_{i+1})\geq r_{\mathsf{max}}(\mathsf{cfg}_{i+1})=\mathsf{tgt}(a_{i})
. Therefore
\mathsf{tgt}(a_{1})\leq\mathsf{tgt}(a_{2})\leq\cdots
, i.e., the target sequence is non-decreasing, so
\pi
is semi-synchronous.
∎

Claim 20

Let

B.3.4
Strong
SSR

The
\mathsf{SSR}
restriction is sufficient to obtain a reduced finite-counter semantics. However, we can further decrease non-determinism on interleaving by introducing a stronger restriction while preserving soundness.

Let
\theta
be a total order on the set of jump rules
\{\rho\in\mathsf{Rules}\ |\ \rho.\mathsf{type}>0\}
. An execution
\pi=\mathsf{cfg}_{0},a_{1},\mathsf{cfg}_{1},a_{2}\dots
of
\mathsf{RDTS}^{\mathsf{PIA}}(\mathcal{T},\nu)
with
a_{i}=\mathsf{Update}(\rho_{i},r_{i})
is called
\theta
-strong semi-synchronous
if:
(i) it is semi-synchronous, i.e., for all
i<j
,
\mathsf{tgt}(a_{i})\leq\mathsf{tgt}(a_{j})
, and
(ii) whenever
\mathsf{tgt}(a_{i})=\mathsf{tgt}(a_{j})
and
\rho_{j}.\mathsf{type}>0
, we have
\rho_{i}.\mathsf{type}>0
and
\rho_{i}\leq_{\theta}\rho_{j}
.
Clearly, every
\theta
-strong semi-synchronous execution is semi-synchronous. The converse is also true:

Lemma 21

(Soundness of strong
\mathsf{SSR}
)
.

Let
\theta
be a total order on jump rules. For every semi-synchronous execution
\pi
of
\mathsf{RDTS}^{\mathsf{PIA}}(\mathcal{T},\nu)
there exists a
\theta
-strong semi-synchronous execution
\pi^{\prime}
of
\mathsf{RDTS}^{\mathsf{PIA}}(\mathcal{T},\nu)
that starts from the same initial configuration as
\pi
and contains the same multiset of update actions.

Proof.

The proof follows from the Commutativity claim. In a semi-synchronous execution, within the fragment whose updates targets round
r
, a local update targeting round
r
can always be commuted past a jump update targeting
r
, so all jumps can be placed before locals. Furthermore, jump updates targeting the same round commute among themselves. Thus, by reordering them according to
\theta
, we obtain the desired execution.
∎

The corresponding strong
\mathsf{SSR}
semantics extends the
\mathsf{SSR}
semantics. Informally, configurations additionally record the last executed rule, and enabledness is further restricted as follows: (i) if the last rule was local, then no jump to the current frontier round is permitted; (ii) if the last rule was a jump, then no jump to the current frontier round with a smaller (w.r.t.
\theta
) rule is permitted.
Strong
\mathsf{SSR}
substantially reduces nondeterminism:
(i) it enforces that all jumps to a round
r
occur before any locals at
r
, and
(ii) it quotients away permutations of jump updates targeting the same round by fixing their order according to
\theta
.
Since soundness holds for any choice of
\theta
, the strong
\mathsf{SSR}
semantics can be viewed as if all processes
synchronously jump
to round
r
, followed by an
asynchronous execution of local updates
at
r
, before moving on to the next round.

B.4
Bounded-window abstraction

Recall that in
\mathsf{SSR}
the relevant window of a configuration
\mathsf{cfg}
is
\llbracket r_{\mathsf{max}}(\mathsf{cfg})-b,\ r_{\mathsf{max}}(\mathsf{cfg})\rrbracket
, of fixed size
b{+}1
. The bounded-window abstraction (
\mathsf{BWA}
) drops all counters outside this window and encodes the remainder as a sliding window of width
b{+}1
anchored at the frontier
r_{\mathsf{max}}(\mathsf{cfg})
; that is, it stores
r_{\mathsf{max}}(\mathsf{cfg})
together with the counters for rounds
r_{\mathsf{max}}(\mathsf{cfg})-b\leq r\leq r_{\mathsf{max}}(\mathsf{cfg})
.

Projection.

For a
\mathsf{SSR}
configuration

\mathsf{cfg}_{\mathsf{SSR}}=\langle\mathsf{PState}_{\mathsf{SSR}},\,\mathsf{NState}_{\mathsf{SSR}}\rangle
, we define the
\mathsf{SSR}
projection

\alpha_{\mathsf{BWA}}(\mathsf{cfg}_{\mathsf{SSR}})=\big\langle\mathsf{PState}_{\mathsf{BWA}},\,\mathsf{NState}_{\mathsf{BWA}},\,r_{\mathsf{max}}\big\rangle,

where
\mathsf{PState}_{\mathsf{BWA}}:\mathcal{L}\times\llbracket 0,b\rrbracket\to\mathbb{N}
is given by
\mathsf{PState}_{\mathsf{BWA}}(\ell,d)=\mathsf{PState}_{\mathsf{SSR}}(\ell,r_{\mathsf{max}}(\mathsf{cfg})-d),

\mathsf{NState}_{\mathsf{BWA}}:\mathcal{M}\times\llbracket 0,b\rrbracket\to\mathbb{N}
is given by
\mathsf{NState}_{\mathsf{BWA}}(m,d)=\mathsf{NState}_{\mathsf{SSR}}(m,r_{\mathsf{max}}(\mathsf{cfg})-d),
and
r_{\mathsf{max}}\in\mathbb{N}
is given by
r_{\mathsf{max}}=r_{\mathsf{max}}(\mathsf{cfg}_{\mathsf{SSR}})
.

The
\mathsf{BWA}
semantics

\mathsf{RDTS}^{\mathsf{BWA}}(\mathcal{T},\nu)=\langle S_{\nu}^{\mathsf{BWA}},I_{\nu}^{\mathsf{BWA}},Act_{\nu}^{\mathsf{BWA}},Tr_{\nu}^{\mathsf{BWA}}\rangle,

where
S_{\nu}^{\mathsf{BWA}}=\{\alpha_{\mathsf{BWA}}(\mathsf{cfg})\mid\mathsf{cfg}\in S_{\nu}^{\mathsf{SSR}}\}
,
I_{\nu}^{\mathsf{BWA}}=\{\alpha_{\mathsf{BWA}}(\mathsf{cfg})\mid\mathsf{cfg}\in I_{\nu}^{\mathsf{SSR}}\}
, and
Act_{\nu}^{\mathsf{BWA}}=Act_{\nu}^{\mathsf{SSR}}
.
The transition relation is the existential lift of
\mathsf{SSR}
semantics:
(\mathsf{cfg}_{\mathsf{BWA}},a,\mathsf{cfg}^{\prime}_{\mathsf{BWA}})\in Tr_{\nu}^{\mathsf{BWA}}
iff there exist
\mathsf{cfg}_{\mathsf{SSR}},\mathsf{cfg}^{\prime}_{\mathsf{SSR}}\in S_{\nu}^{\mathsf{SSR}}
with

\alpha_{\mathsf{BWA}}(\mathsf{cfg}_{\mathsf{SSR}})=\mathsf{cfg}_{\mathsf{BWA}},\ \alpha_{\mathsf{BWA}}(\mathsf{cfg}^{\prime}_{\mathsf{SSR}})=\mathsf{cfg}^{\prime}_{\mathsf{BWA}},\text{ and }(\mathsf{cfg}_{\mathsf{SSR}},a,\mathsf{cfg}^{\prime}_{\mathsf{SSR}})\in Tr_{\nu}^{\mathsf{SSR}}.

The
hsc
-trace of executions of
\mathsf{RDTS}^{\mathsf{BWA}}(\mathcal{T},\nu)
is defined identically to that of executions of
\mathsf{RDTS}^{\mathsf{PIA}}(\mathcal{T},\nu)
(see Appendix
B.2
). Since the action sets of the two systems coincide, this is well defined.

Before establishing action based bisimulation between
\mathsf{BWA}
and
\mathsf{SSR}
semantics, we establish following property of projection
\alpha_{\mathsf{BWA}}:

Claim 22
.

Let
\mathsf{cfg},\hat{\mathsf{cfg}}\in S_{\nu}^{\mathsf{SSR}}
and

If
\alpha_{\mathsf{BWA}}(\mathsf{cfg})=\alpha_{\mathsf{BWA}}(\hat{\mathsf{cfg}})

\mathsf{SSR}
semantics:

1.

a
is enabled at
\mathsf{cfg}
iff it is enabled at
\hat{\mathsf{cfg}}
.

2.

if
a
is enabled, then
\alpha_{\mathsf{BWA}}\!\big(\mathsf{effect}_{\mathsf{SSR}}(\mathsf{cfg},a)\big)\;=\;\alpha_{\mathsf{BWA}}\!\big(\mathsf{effect}_{\mathsf{SSR}}(\hat{\mathsf{cfg}},a)\big).

Proof.

Assume
\alpha_{\mathsf{BWA}}(\mathsf{cfg})=\alpha_{\mathsf{BWA}}(\hat{\mathsf{cfg}})
. Then
r_{\mathsf{max}}(\mathsf{cfg})=r_{\mathsf{max}}(\hat{\mathsf{cfg}})
and, for all
r\geq r_{\mathsf{max}}(\mathsf{cfg})-b
, the round

counters,

\mathsf{PState}(\ell,r)
and
\mathsf{NState}(m,r)
coincide in
\mathsf{cfg}
and
\hat{\mathsf{cfg}}
.

1.
For any

,

is enabled at
\mathsf{cfg}
implies

, Since the enabledness of

B.5
History-record extension

Formally,
\mathsf{RDTS}^{\mathsf{HRE}}(\mathcal{T},\nu,\varphi)=\langle S_{\nu}^{\mathsf{HRE}},I_{\nu}^{\mathsf{HRE}},Act_{\nu}^{\mathsf{HRE}},Tr_{\nu}^{\mathsf{HRE}}\rangle,
where
S_{\nu}^{\mathsf{HRE}}
is the set of
\mathsf{HRE}
configurations,
I_{\nu}^{\mathsf{HRE}}
the set of initial
\mathsf{HRE}
configurations,
Act_{\nu}^{\mathsf{HRE}}=Act_{\nu}^{\mathsf{BWA}}
, and
Tr_{\nu}^{\mathsf{HRE}}
, the transition relation obtained by extending
Tr_{\nu}^{\mathsf{BWA}}
with the corresponding updates to the history record.

\mathsf{HRE}
Configurations.

An
\mathsf{HRE}
configuration is of the form

\mathsf{cfg}_{\mathsf{HRE}}=\langle\mathsf{cfg}_{\mathsf{BWA}},\mathsf{local},\mathsf{cumul}\rangle

with
\mathsf{cfg}_{\mathsf{BWA}}\in S_{\nu}^{\mathsf{BWA}}
and
\mathsf{local},\mathsf{cumul}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{N}
. For
\ell\in\mathcal{X}
,
\mathsf{local}(\ell)
counts visits to
\ell
in the frontier round
r_{\mathsf{max}}
of
\mathsf{cfg}_{\mathsf{BWA}}
, and
\mathsf{cumul}(\ell)
counts total visits to
\ell
up to
r_{\mathsf{max}}
. We refer to counters
\mathsf{local}
and
\mathsf{cumul}
as history record counters.

For a formula
\varphi
, define
t_{\max}(\varphi,\nu)
as the largest numeric value attained by any threshold in
\varphi
under
\nu
. Formally, let
T(\varphi)\subseteq\textsf{LinTrm}(\mathcal{Y})
denote the set of thresholds occurring in
\varphi
(for example, in Figure
3
-Left, we have
T(\texttt{A})=\{0\}
and
T(\texttt{T})=\{\mathsf{N_{c}}\}
), then

t_{\max}(\varphi,\nu)=\max\{\,t[\mathcal{Y}\leftarrow\nu]\mid t\in T(\varphi)\,\}.

In
\mathsf{HRE}
configurations we require that for every
\ell\in\mathcal{X}
, both
\mathsf{local}(\ell)
and
\mathsf{cumul}(\ell)
are bounded above by
B_{\nu}^{\varphi}=1+\max\{0,\,t_{\max}(\varphi,\nu)\}.

This bounding is sufficient because every atomic constraint in
\varphi
has the form
\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}c_{x}A_{x}\leq t
where
c_{x}\in\mathbb{N}
and
A_{x}
denote either a local or cumulative visit count. If
c_{x}=0
, the value of
A_{x}
is irrelevant. If
c_{x}>0
and
A_{x}
takes value
B_{\nu}^{\varphi}
, then the atom is permanently false regardless of further increases.
Thus exact values of
A_{x}
beyond
B_{\nu}^{\varphi}
never affect the truth of
\varphi
.

An
\mathsf{HRE}
configuration
\mathsf{cfg}_{\mathsf{HRE}}=\langle\mathsf{cfg}_{\mathsf{BWA}},\mathsf{local},\mathsf{cumul}\rangle
is initial iff
\mathsf{cfg}_{\mathsf{BWA}}\in I_{\nu}^{\mathsf{BWA}}
and, the history record counters are initialized to match the number of processes at location
\ell
in round 0, respecting the bound of
B_{\nu}^{\varphi}
.
Formally, for every
\ell\in\mathcal{X}
,

\mathsf{local}(\ell)=\mathsf{cumul}(\ell)=\min\bigl(\mathsf{PState}_{\mathsf{BWA}}(\ell,0),B_{\nu}^{\varphi}\bigr),

where
\mathsf{PState}_{\mathsf{BWA}}
is the process-state component of
\mathsf{cfg}_{\mathsf{BWA}}
.

\mathsf{HRE}
transitions.

For
\mathsf{HRE}
configurations
\mathsf{cfg}_{\mathsf{HRE}}=\langle\mathsf{cfg}_{\mathsf{BWA}},\mathsf{local},\mathsf{cumul}\rangle
and
\mathsf{cfg}_{\mathsf{HRE}}^{\prime}=\langle\mathsf{cfg}_{\mathsf{BWA}}^{\prime},\mathsf{local}^{\prime},\mathsf{cumul}^{\prime}\rangle
, and an action
a=\mathsf{Update}(\rho,r)
, we have

(\mathsf{cfg}_{\mathsf{HRE}},a,\mathsf{cfg}_{\mathsf{HRE}})\in Tr_{\nu}^{\mathsf{HRE}}
iff
(\mathsf{cfg}_{\mathsf{BWA}},a,\mathsf{cfg}_{\mathsf{BWA}}^{\prime})\in Tr_{\nu}^{\mathsf{BWA}}
and the history record counters are related as follows.
Let
r_{\mathsf{max}}
,
r_{\mathsf{max}}^{\prime}
be the frontier rounds of
\mathsf{cfg}_{\mathsf{BWA}}
and
\mathsf{cfg}_{\mathsf{BWA}}^{\prime}
respectively. Then, for each
\ell\in\mathcal{X}
:

•

The cumulative record
\mathsf{cumul}
is incremented at location
\rho.\mathsf{to}
subject to the saturation at
B_{\nu}^{\varphi}
; other locations are unchanged. Formally,
\mathsf{cumul}^{\prime}(\ell)=\min(\mathsf{cumul}(\ell)+1,B_{\nu}^{\varphi})
if
\ell=\rho.\mathsf{to}
and
\mathsf{cumul}^{\prime}(\ell)=\mathsf{cumul}(\ell)
otherwise.

•

Recall that the record
\mathsf{local}
counts visits in the frontier round.

–

If the frontier-round does not advance (
r_{\mathsf{max}}^{\prime}=r_{\mathsf{max}}
), then only the target location increments, subject to the saturation. Formally,
\mathsf{local}^{\prime}(\ell)=

\min(\mathsf{local}(\ell)+1,B_{\nu}^{\varphi})
if
\ell=\rho.\mathsf{to}
and
\mathsf{local}^{\prime}(\ell)=\mathsf{local}(\ell)
otherwise.

–

If the frontier-round advances (
r_{\mathsf{max}}^{\prime}>r_{\mathsf{max}}
), all local counters reset to
0
except at the new target, which is set to
1
. Formally,
\mathsf{local}^{\prime}(\ell)=1
if
\ell=\rho.\mathsf{to}
and
\mathsf{local}^{\prime}(\ell)=0
otherwise.

–

Note that frontier-round is never decremented in
\mathsf{BWA}
semantics.

Appendix D
Explicit Encoding of Finite-Counter System

This appendix gives the explicit finite-counter-system construction obtained
after the reduction presented in Section
3
. The input is a process template

\mathcal{T}=\langle\mathsf{P},\mathsf{rc},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{I},\mathcal{M},\mathsf{Bcast},\mathsf{Rules}\rangle

and an
\mathsf{HSCL}
formula
\varphi
. For fixed-instance verification, the input
also includes a parameter valuation
\nu:\mathsf{P}\to\mathbb{N}
.

The output is a finite-counter system

(V,\mathsf{INVAR},\mathsf{INIT},\mathsf{TRAN})

together with the
\mathsf{LTL}
formula obtained from
\varphi
, whose atomic
propositions are linear-arithmetic formulas over
V
.
Thus, the construction
produces an instance of
\mathsf{LTL}
model checking over a finite-counter system,
which can be encoded in symbolic model checkers such as nuXmv.

In the fixed-instance case, the invariant formula
INVAR
additionally fixes the parameter variables
according to
\nu
. This implies that only finitely many valuations satisfy
\mathsf{INVAR}
. Hence, for fixed-instance verification, the generated finite-counter system is in fact finite-state.

For a rule
\rho\in\mathsf{Rules}
, write

\rho=(\ell,\ell^{\prime},k,g),

where
\ell=\rho.\mathsf{from}
,
\ell^{\prime}=\rho.\mathsf{to}
,

k=\rho.\mathsf{type}
, and
g=\rho.\mathsf{guard}
. Let

b=\max\{\rho.\mathsf{type}\mid\rho\in\mathsf{Rules}\}.

Further, let
p_{1},\ldots,p_{q}
be the parameter variables from
\mathsf{P}
that occur in
g
, and let
m_{1},\ldots,m_{s}
be the message variables from

\mathcal{M}
that occur in
g
. Let

\widehat{g}(p_{1},\ldots,p_{q},m_{1},\ldots,m_{s})

be a quantifier-free Presburger formula equivalent to

\exists r_{1},\ldots,r_{s}.\;\left(\bigwedge_{i=1}^{s}0\leq r_{i}\leq m_{i}\right)\wedge g[m_{1}\mapsto r_{1},\ldots,m_{s}\mapsto r_{s}].

Let
X\subseteq\mathcal{L}
be the set of locations mentioned in
\varphi
and let
K
be one plus the largest threshold appearing in cumulative atoms of
\varphi
, see Def.
2
(
K=0
if no such
atom exists).

Below, we describe each of the components of the obtained finite-counter system and the
\mathsf{LTL}
formula, including some brief explanations to help with understanding.

Variables
V
.

The set
V
consists of the following non-negative integer variables:

\mathsf{par}_{p}\qquad(p\in\mathsf{P}),

\mathsf{loc}_{\ell,d}\qquad(\ell\in\mathcal{L},\ d\in\{0,\ldots,b-1\}),

\mathsf{msg}_{m,d}\qquad(m\in\mathcal{M},\ d\in\{0,\ldots,b-1\}),

\mathsf{local}_{\ell},\mathsf{cumul}_{\ell}\qquad(\ell\in X).

These are called, respectively,
parameter variables, location variables, message variables, and history variables.

Invariant formula
\mathsf{INVAR}
.

The formula
\mathsf{INVAR}
is the conjunction of the constraints below.

1. The parameter variables satisfy the resilience condition:

\mathsf{rc}\big[p\mapsto\mathsf{par}_{p}\big].

For fixed-instance verification with parameter valuation

\nu:\mathsf{P}\to\mathbb{N}
,
\mathsf{INVAR}
additionally includes the
constraints

\mathsf{par}_{p}=\nu(p)\qquad(p\in\mathsf{P}).

2. The location variables are bounded by the total number of processes:

H\leq\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}\sum_{d=0}^{b-1}\mathsf{loc}_{\ell,d}\leq\mathsf{par}_{n}

where
H=n
when failures are modeled explicitly by dedicated fault locations,
and
H=n-t
when failures are modeled implicitly by allowing processes to stop participating.

3. The message variables are bounded by the maximum number of messages that
can be broadcast in one round:

0\leq\mathsf{msg}_{m,d}\leq|\mathcal{L}|\cdot\mathsf{par}_{n}\qquad(m\in\mathcal{M},\ d\in\{0,\ldots,b-1\}).

4. The history variables are bounded as follows:

(0\leq\mathsf{local}_{\ell}\leq\mathsf{par}_{n})\wedge(0\leq\mathsf{cumul}_{\ell}\leq K)\qquad(\ell\in X).

Initial formula
\mathsf{INIT}
.

The formula
\mathsf{INIT}
is the conjunction of the constraints below.

1. The initial location variables satisfy

\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{I}}\mathsf{loc}_{\ell,0}=\mathsf{par}_{n},

\mathsf{loc}_{\ell,0}=0\qquad(\ell\notin\mathcal{I}),\qquad\text{and}

\mathsf{loc}_{\ell,d}=0\qquad(\ell\in\mathcal{L},\ d\in\{1,\ldots,b-1\}).

2. The initial message variables satisfy

\mathsf{msg}_{m,d}=0\qquad(m\in\mathcal{M},\ d\in\{0,\ldots,b-1\}).

3. The initial history variables satisfy

\mathsf{local}_{\ell}=\begin{cases}\mathsf{loc}_{\ell,0}&\text{if }\ell\in\mathcal{I},\\
0&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}\qquad\mathsf{cumul}_{\ell}=\begin{cases}\mathsf{loc}_{\ell,0}&\text{if }\ell\in\mathcal{I},\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Transition formula
\mathsf{TRAN}
.

The transition formula is the disjunction of the local-transition formula and
the jump-transition formula:

\mathsf{TRAN}=\mathsf{TRAN}_{\mathsf{loc}}\vee\mathsf{TRAN}_{\mathsf{jump}}.

Local-transition formula
\mathsf{TRAN}_{\mathsf{loc}}
.

Intuitively, the formula
\mathsf{TRAN}_{\mathsf{loc}}
encodes one process taking a
type-
0
rule in the current frontier round. For each type-
0
rule

\rho=(\ell,\ell^{\prime},0,g),

we define a clause
\mathsf{LocClause}_{\rho}
, and set

\mathsf{TRAN}_{\mathsf{loc}}=\bigvee_{\rho=(\ell,\ell^{\prime},0,g)\in\mathsf{Rules}}\mathsf{LocClause}_{\rho}.

The clause
\mathsf{LocClause}_{\rho}
is the conjunction of the
following constraints.

1. The enabledness constrain:

\mathsf{loc}_{\ell,0}>0\;\wedge\;\widehat{g}\big[p\mapsto\mathsf{par}_{p},\;m\mapsto\mathsf{msg}_{m,0}\big].

2. The location variables are updated by moving one process from
\ell

to
\ell^{\prime}
at depth
0
:

\mathsf{loc}^{\prime}_{\ell,0}=\mathsf{loc}_{\ell,0}-1,\qquad\mathsf{loc}^{\prime}_{\ell^{\prime},0}=\mathsf{loc}_{\ell^{\prime},0}+1,

and all other location variables are unchanged.

3. If
\mathsf{Bcast}(\ell^{\prime})=m\in\mathcal{M}
, then the corresponding
message variable at depth
0
is incremented:

\mathsf{msg}^{\prime}_{m,0}=\mathsf{msg}_{m,0}+1,

and all other message variables are unchanged. If

\mathsf{Bcast}(\ell^{\prime})=\bot
, then all message variables are unchanged.

4. If
\ell^{\prime}\in X
, then the history variables for
\ell^{\prime}
is incremented as follows:

\mathsf{local}^{\prime}_{\ell^{\prime}}=\mathsf{local}_{\ell^{\prime}}+1,\qquad\mathsf{cumul}^{\prime}_{\ell^{\prime}}=\begin{cases}\mathsf{cumul}_{\ell^{\prime}}+1&\text{if }\mathsf{cumul}_{\ell^{\prime}}<K,\\
\mathsf{cumul}_{\ell^{\prime}}&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

and all other history variables are unchanged. If
\ell^{\prime}\notin X
, then all
history variables are unchanged.

5. Finally, all parameter variables are unchanged.

Jump-transition formula
\mathsf{TRAN}_{\mathsf{jump}}
.

Intuitively, the formula
\mathsf{TRAN}_{\mathsf{jump}}
encodes a jump of the frontier
round. For each jump length

h\in\{1,\ldots,b\},

we define a clause
\mathsf{JumpClause}_{h}
, and set

\mathsf{TRAN}_{\mathsf{jump}}=\bigvee_{h=1}^{b}\mathsf{JumpClause}_{h}.

The clause
\mathsf{JumpClause}_{h}
uses auxiliary non-negetive integer variables

J_{d,\rho}\qquad(d\in\{0,\ldots,b-1\},\ \rho\in\mathsf{Rules},\ \rho.\mathsf{type}>0).

The variable
J_{d,\rho}
denotes the number of processes at depth
d
that
take the positive-type rule
\rho
during the jump.

Formally,
\mathsf{JumpClause}_{h}
can be viewed as existentially
quantifying the auxiliary variables
J_{d,\rho}
; the resulting formula can then be made linear-arithmetic by Presburger quantifier elimination. In our encoding, we use the equivalent and more direct presentation: the variables
J_{d,\rho}
are included in
V
as auxiliary variables and are updated nondeterministically by jump transitions, so that their current values serve as witnesses for the jump.

The clause

\mathsf{JumpClause}_{h}
is the conjunction of the following constraints.

1. The auxiliary variables cannot move more
processes than are available. For every
\lambda\in\mathcal{L}
and
d\in\{0,\ldots,b-1\}
writing

J^{\mathsf{out}}_{\lambda,d}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho=(\lambda,\ell^{\prime},k,g)\in\mathsf{Rules}\\
k>0\end{subarray}}J_{d,\rho},

we require

J^{\mathsf{out}}_{\lambda,d}\leq\mathsf{loc}_{\lambda,d}\qquad(\lambda\in\mathcal{L},\ d\in\{0,\ldots,b-1\}).

2. A positive-type rule may be taken only if it lands exactly in the
new frontier round. Thus, for every

\rho=(\ell,\ell^{\prime},k,g)
with
k>0
, and every

d\in\{0,\ldots,b-1\}
, we require

J_{d,\rho}>0\implies d+h=k.

3. Whenever some processes take a rule, its guard must be enabled at
the corresponding depth.

J_{d,\rho}>0\implies\widehat{g}\big[p\mapsto\mathsf{par}_{p},\;m\mapsto\mathsf{msg}_{m,d}\big],

4. The location variables are updated by shifting the old window by length

h
and placing the processes that jump in the new frontier
round. For every
\lambda\in\mathcal{L}
, let

J^{\mathsf{in}}_{\lambda}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\in\{0,\ldots,b-1\}\\
\rho=(\ell,\lambda,k,g)\in\mathsf{Rules}\\
k>0\end{subarray}}J_{d,\rho}.

Then

\mathsf{loc}^{\prime}_{\lambda,0}=J^{\mathsf{in}}_{\lambda}\qquad(\lambda\in\mathcal{L}).

For depths skipped by the jump, we set

\mathsf{loc}^{\prime}_{\lambda,d}=0\qquad(\lambda\in\mathcal{L},\ 1\leq d<h).

For the remaining depths, we shift the old variables and remove the processes
that jumped:

\mathsf{loc}^{\prime}_{\lambda,d}=\mathsf{loc}_{\lambda,d-h}-J^{\mathsf{out}}_{\lambda,d-h}\qquad(\lambda\in\mathcal{L},\ h\leq d\leq b-1).

5. The message variables are updated analogously. The messages in the
new frontier round are exactly those broadcast by the processes that jump into
that round. Thus, for every
m\in\mathcal{M}
,

\mathsf{msg}^{\prime}_{m,0}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in\mathcal{L}\\
\mathsf{Bcast}(\lambda)=m\end{subarray}}J^{\mathsf{in}}_{\lambda}.

For depths skipped by the jump, we set

\mathsf{msg}^{\prime}_{m,d}=0\qquad(m\in\mathcal{M},\ 1\leq d<h).

For the remaining depths, we shift the old message variables:

\mathsf{msg}^{\prime}_{m,d}=\mathsf{msg}_{m,d-h}\qquad(m\in\mathcal{M},\ h\leq d\leq b-1).

6. The history variables are updated according to the processes that
enter locations mentioned in the formula. For every
\lambda\in X
,

\mathsf{local}^{\prime}_{\lambda}=J^{\mathsf{in}}_{\lambda},\quad\text{and}\qquad\mathsf{cumul}^{\prime}_{\lambda}=\begin{cases}\mathsf{cumul}_{\lambda}+J^{\mathsf{in}}_{\lambda}&\text{if }\mathsf{cumul}_{\lambda}+J^{\mathsf{in}}_{\lambda}\leq K,\\[5.69054pt]
K&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

7. Finally, all parameter variables are unchanged.

Remark.

The nuXmv encoding additionally requires the transition relation to be total, i.e., to have no deadlock states. We ensure this manually by adding stuttering transitions wherever necessary.

\mathsf{LTL}
Specification.

For completeness, we recall the
\mathsf{HSCL}
-to-
\mathsf{LTL}
translation presented
Section
3.2
. From
\mathsf{HSCL}
formula
\varphi
, we
construct an
\mathsf{LTL}
formula
\mathsf{LTL}(\varphi)
recursively. The atoms of the translated formula are linear-arithmetic formulas over the history variables of the finite-counter system, namely formulas of the form

\sum_{\ell\in X}c_{\ell}\cdot\mathsf{local}_{\ell}\leq t\qquad\text{or}\qquad\sum_{\ell\in X}c_{\ell}\cdot\mathsf{cumul}_{\ell}\leq t,

where
c_{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}
are constants,
t\in\mathsf{LinTrm}(\mathsf{P})
, and
\mathsf{local}_{\ell},\mathsf{cumul}_{\ell}
are history variables.

The translation is defined as follows. See Def.
2
for the syntax of
\mathsf{HSCL}
. For a universal round-local atom

\forall r.\ \alpha_{r}\qquad\text{where}\qquad\alpha_{r}=\sum_{\ell\in X}c_{\ell}\cdot\kappa(\ell,r)\leq t,

we define

\mathsf{LTL}(\forall r.\ \alpha_{r})=\mathbf{G}\!\left(\sum_{\ell\in X}c_{\ell}\cdot\mathsf{local}_{\ell}\leq t\right).

For a cumulative atom

\beta=\sum_{\ell\in X}c_{\ell}\cdot\sum_{r}\kappa(\ell,r)\leq t,

we define

\mathsf{LTL}(\beta)=\mathbf{G}\!\left(\sum_{\ell\in X}c_{\ell}\cdot\mathsf{cumul}_{\ell}\leq t\right).

Boolean connectives are translated compositionally:

\mathsf{LTL}(\neg\psi)=\neg\mathsf{LTL}(\psi),\qquad\mathsf{LTL}(\psi\wedge\psi^{\prime})=\mathsf{LTL}(\psi)\wedge\mathsf{LTL}(\psi^{\prime}).
