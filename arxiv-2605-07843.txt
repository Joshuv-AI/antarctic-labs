Shape theory for condensed anima
Catrin Mair

Abstract
We give different perspectives on the notion of shape for condensed anima. We prove that it recovers more classical notions of shape for topological spaces in the cases of all paracompact compactly generated spaces and all locally contractible spaces. These recovering statements imply and extend comparison results on sheaf and condensed cohomology by Clausen-Scholze and Haine [ 28 , 14 ] . Another homotopy-theoretical direction for condensed anima are their condensed homotopy groups. Connected to this, we give a description of the underlying topological group functor on condensed groups via quasi-topological groups.

Introduction: Overview and main results

In Algebraic Topology, one studies topological spaces by means of algebraic invariants like homotopy and cohomology.
The theory of condensed mathematics suggests that topological spaces should be replaced by condensed sets or anima.
It seems natural to ask how one can translate classical concepts from algebraic topology developed for topological spaces into this setting.

2.14 Proposition

Thus also

This indeed shows that in the case of a homotopy equivalence

Condensed sets and condensed anima

The basic objects in condensed mathematics are
condensed sets
. Originally, these are defined as sheaves of sets on the
pro-étale site

\mathrm{Pro\acute{E}t}_{*}
of a point, i.e., the spectrum of a separably closed field. See
[
3
]
for an introduction to the
pro-étale topology of schemes.
The category of condensed sets is denoted by

\Cond(\textbf{{Set}})\coloneq\Sh(\mathrm{Pro\acute{E}t}_{*},\textbf{{Set}})\hbox to0.0pt{\ {.}\hss}

Sheaves on
\mathrm{Pro\acute{E}t}_{*}
can be identified with sheaves on certain subcategories of topological spaces. The notion of condensed sets extends in the
\infty
-categorical world to the notion of
condensed anima
, denoted by
\Cond(\textbf{{Ani}})
, by considering
hypersheaves
with values in the
\infty
-category
Ani
of
anima
(or spaces).

2.15 Notation
.

We denote by
\ProFin
the category of
profinite sets
. By Stone duality, the category
\ProFin
can be identified with the full subcategory of totally disconnected spaces in the category
Comp
of
compact Hausdorff spaces
.
By a result by Gleason, stated in
[
17
, p. III 3.7]
, the projective objects in
Comp
are the
extremally disconnected
profinite sets
\textbf{{Extr}}\subset\textbf{{Comp}}
.

2.16 Recollection
.

There are at least four different defining sites for the
\infty
-category
\Cond(\textbf{{Ani}})
of
condensed anima
defining the same
\infty
-topos of hypercomplete
\infty
-sheaves:

(i)

The pro-étale site
\mathrm{Pro\acute{E}t}_{\mathrm{Spec}(k)}
for
k
any separably closed field,

(ii)

the site
Comp
of compact Hausdorff spaces,

(iii)

the site
\ProFin
of profinite sets,

(iv)

the site
Extr
of extremally disconnected profinite sets.

Here, coverings for the sites (ii)-(iv) are given by jointly surjective families of continuous maps of topological spaces.
All of these sites define the same
\infty
-topos on hypercomplete
\infty
-sheaves: Every compact Hausdorff space admits a surjection from an extremally disconnected compact Hausdorff space, namely from the Čech–Stone compactification of its underlying discrete space. Moreover,
\ProFin
identifies with the subcategory of affine schemes in
\mathrm{Pro\acute{E}t}_{\mathrm{Spec}(k)}
. Thus there are hierarchies of bases

\textbf{{Extr}}\subset\ProFin\subset\textbf{{Comp}}\text{ and }\ProFin\subset\mathrm{Pro\acute{E}t}_{\mathrm{Spec}(k)}.

By
[
arXiv:2001.00319
, Corollary A.7]

\infty
-topoi

(2.17)

\Sh^{\hyp}(\mathrm{Pro\acute{E}t}_{\mathrm{Spec}(k)})\similarrightarrow\Sh^{\hyp}(\Pro(\textbf{{Set}}_{\textup{fin}}))\similarrightarrow\Sh^{\hyp}(\textbf{{Extr}})\hbox to0.0pt{\ {.}\hss}

The
\infty
-topos
\Cond(\textbf{{Ani}})
of
condensed anima
is any of the equivalent
\infty
-topoi (
2.17
).

2.18 Recollection
.

The category
\Cond(\textbf{{Set}})
of condensed sets embeds fully faithfully into the
\infty
-category
\Cond(\textbf{{Ani}})

2.19 Recollection

On extremally disconnected profinite sets, hypersheafification can be omitted and the

Condensed homotopy groups of condensed anima

For the definition of condensed homotopy groups, note that we have an identification on pointed objects

Further recall that for all

2.32 Definition
.

Condensed homotopy groups

We define the
condensed homotopy group functors
on pointed condensed anima

\pi_{n}^{\mathrm{cond}}\colon\Cond(\textbf{{Ani}})_{*}\rightarrow\Cond(\textbf{{Grp}})

by level-wise postcomposition with the simplicial homotopy group functors on pointed anima

\pi_{n}\colon\textbf{{Ani}}_{*}\rightarrow\textbf{{Grp}},
for all

In other words, the
n

\pi_{n}^{\mathrm{cond}}(X,x)
of a pointed condensed anima
(X,x\colon{\mathbin{*}}\rightarrow X)
is the condensed group with value

\pi_{n}(X(K),x(K))\smallin\textbf{{Grp}}

at every
K\smallin\textbf{{Extr}}
.
For all
n\geq 2
, it is even a condensed abelian group by the corresponding statement for simplicial homotopy groups.

2.33 Remark
.

[
2
, Example 2.4.7]
Together with the underlying condensed set functor
\pi_{0}^{\mathrm{cond}}\colon\Cond(\textbf{{Ani}})\rightarrow\Cond(\textbf{{Set}})
, defined by post-composition with the connected components functor
\pi_{0}\colon\textbf{{Ani}}\rightarrow\textbf{{Set}}
, the condensed homotopy group functors are collectively conservative: A morphism of condensed anima

is an equivalence if and only if for every

, the corresponding morphism on

is an isomorphism in condensed sets, condensed groups or condensed abelian groups, respectively.

2.34 Example
.

Discrete condensed anima have discrete homotopy groups

Let
(A,a)\smallin\Cond(\textbf{{Ani}})_{*}
be a pointed discrete condensed anima.
For a cofiltered limit representation
K=\lim_{i\smallin I}K_{i}\smallin\textbf{{Extr}}
by finite sets
K_{i}\smallin\textbf{{Set}}_{\textup{fin}}
, we have

\displaystyle\pi_{n}(A(K),a(K))

\displaystyle=\pi_{n}(\colim_{i\smallin I^{\mathrm{op}}}A^{K_{i}},a(K))=\colim_{i\smallin I^{\mathrm{op}}}\prod_{k\smallin K_{i}}\pi_{n}(A,a({\mathbin{*}}))

\displaystyle=\colim_{i\smallin I^{\mathrm{op}}}\Hom_{\textbf{{Set}}}(K_{i},\pi_{n}(A,a({\mathbin{*}})))=\pi_{n}(A,a({\mathbin{*}}))^{\mathrm{disc}}(K)

where
(\cdot)^{\mathrm{disc}}\colon\textbf{{Grp}}\hookrightarrow\Cond(\textbf{{Grp}})
. We used that simplicial homotopy group functors commute with finite products and filtered colimits. The above means that for all discrete condensed anima, the condensed homotopy are discrete and can be identified with the classical simplicial homotopy groups of the corresponding anima.

The example applies to the singular simplicial complex
\Sing(T)\smallin\textbf{{Ani}}
of a topological space
T
. However, viewing a topological space as a condensed anima via the topological direction, gives a trivial result on condensed homotopy groups.

2.35 Remark
.

Vanishing condensed homotopy groups

As the category
\Cond(\textbf{{Set}})
of condensed sets forms the sub-
\infty
-category of
0
-truncated objects inside
\Cond(\textbf{{Ani}})
,
the condensed homotopy groups vanish for all these. In other words, condensed homotopy groups loose all the information on homotopy groups of topological spaces.

The remark provides one reason to study the notion of shape for condensed anima and its relation to the shape of topological spaces. In the main part, we will construct and study a ’shape’ functor

\Pi_{\infty}^{c}\colon\Cond(\textbf{{Ani}})\rightarrow\Pro(\textbf{{Ani}}).

Our results there compared with the previous remark then imply that
the following square does not commute for
n\geq 1
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Cond(\textbf{{Ani}})_{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pro(\textbf{{Ani}})_{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Cond(\textbf{{Grp}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pro(\textbf{{Grp}}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{n}^{\mathrm{cond}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pro(\pi_{n})}

This is illustrated by the following simple example:

2.36 Example
.

Consider the static condensed anima corresponding to the circle
S^{1}
. Its image under
\Cond(\textbf{{Ani}})\rightarrow\Pro(\textbf{{Ani}})
then coincides with its homotopy type
\Sing(S^{1})
, see
Example

3.6
, and its homotopy pro-groups thus with its classical homotopy groups. By truncatedness, all the condensed homotopy groups of

are trivial but the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(S^{1})=\mathbf{Z}
is not. For

, we can argue in a similar way by choosing an arbitrary CW-space admitting non-trivial higher homotopy groups.

As an upshot, condensed homotopy groups assign a notion of homotopy groups carrying the additional structure of a topology in a broader sense to a condensed anima. However, this is not very meaningful in the context of viewing condensed sets as a replacement of topological spaces.

3
Shapes of condensed anima and topological spaces

How to possibly do homotopy theory for condensed anima, has already been studied in
[
22
]
.
There we asked to what extent the discrete functor

\textbf{{Ani}}\hookrightarrow\Cond(\textbf{{Ani}})

has a left adjoint assigning a homotopy type to every condensed anima.
This was motivated by the fact that a hypothetical left adjoint would reflect the situation of the topological direction, where we have the left adjoint

\mathrm{\pi}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{0}\colon\Cond(\textbf{{Ani}})\rightarrow\Cond(\textbf{{Set}})

by which a condensed anima can be provided with an underlying topological space.

3.1
Definition of shapes of condensed anima

Via the restricted shape functor
\Pi_{\infty}\colon\textbf{{Top}}\rightarrow\Pro(\textbf{{Ani}})
on topological spaces, we can assign a shape
\Pi_{\infty}(K)\smallin\Pro(\textbf{{Ani}})
to every extremally disconnected profinite set
K\smallin\textbf{{Extr}}
.
As these spaces constitute the building blocks of the
\infty
-topos
\Cond(\textbf{{Ani}})
in the sense that
\Cond(\textbf{{Ani}})
is freely generated under sifted colimits by
Extr
, we can extend this to all condensed anima.

3.1 Definition
.

Shapes of condensed anima

We define the
shape functor
on condensed anima

\Pi_{\infty}^{c}\colon\Cond(\textbf{{Ani}})\rightarrow\Pro(\textbf{{Ani}})

as the unique hypercomplete cosheaf extending the restricted shape functor on topological spaces
\Pi_{\infty}\colon\textbf{{Extr}}\rightarrow\Pro(\textbf{{Ani}})
.
For every
X\smallin\Cond(\textbf{{Ani}})
, we refer to the image

\Pi_{\infty}^{c}(X)\smallin\Pro(\textbf{{Ani}})

as its
shape
.

Being a hypercomplete cosheaf is the same as being a functor sending colimits in the hypercomplete
\infty
-topos
\Cond(\textbf{{Ani}})
to colimits in
\Pro(\textbf{{Ani}})
.
As every condensed anima can be represented by a sifted colimit of objects in
\textbf{{Extr}}\subset\Cond(\textbf{{Ani}})
, the construction in the definition of the shape is the extension of the restricted shape functor
\Pi_{\infty}\colon\textbf{{Extr}}\rightarrow\Pro(\textbf{{Ani}})
by sifted colimits along the Yoneda embedding
\textbf{{Extr}}\hookrightarrow\Cond(\textbf{{Ani}})
.

3.2 Remark
.

Let
X\smallin\Cond(\textbf{{Ani}})
be represented by a sifted colimit
X=\colim_{i\smallin I}K_{i}
of (extremally disconencted) profinite sets
K_{i}\smallin\textbf{{Extr}}
.
Then the shape of
X
is given by

\Pi_{\infty}^{c}(X)=\colim_{i\smallin I}\Pi_{\infty}(K)\smallin\Pro(\textbf{{Ani}})\hbox to0.0pt{\ {.}\hss}

By definition, it is clear that for every
K\smallin\textbf{{Extr}}
, viewed as a condensed anima, the shape
\Pi_{\infty}^{c}(K)
coincides with the shape
\Pi_{\infty}(K)
.
As mentioned in
Example

2.13
, this shape can be identified with the pro-anima corresponding to the profinite set
K
itself under the inclusion
\ProFin\subset\Pro(\textbf{{Ani}})
.
Thus, by abuse of notation, we can simplify to

\Pi_{\infty}^{c}(X)=\colim_{i\smallin I}K\smallin\Pro(\textbf{{Ani}})\hbox to0.0pt{\ {.}\hss}

Note that the colimit is taken in the
\infty
-category
\Pro(\textbf{{Ani}})
and thus corresponds to a limit of finite limit preserving functors
\textbf{{Ani}}\rightarrow\textbf{{Ani}}
. The shape of
X\smallin\Cond(\textbf{{Ani}})
is independent of the choice of the colimit realizing
X
.

3.2
Recovering shapes of topological spaces

We are going to explore topological spaces
T
for which the shapes
\Pi_{\infty}^{c}(T)
and
\Pi_{\infty}(T)
can be identified.
A natural assumption on
T
is to lie in the category
CG
of compactly generated spaces, consisting exactly of those spaces whose topology is already determined by all (extremally disconnected) compact Hausdorff spaces mapping into them.
We show that we indeed get an identification under the additional restriction to paracompact spaces.

3.3 Recollection
.

A topological space
T
is
paracompact
if every open cover can be refined by a locally finite cover, i.e., a cover
\{U_{i}\subset T\}_{i\smallin I}
of open subsets such that for every
x\smallin T
, there exists some neighborhood
x\smallin U_{x}
having non-empty intersection with only finitely many of the
U_{i}
.

3.4 Remark
.

This class of spaces still contains all CW-complexes, compact Hausdorff spaces and second countable locally compact Hausdorff spaces, for references see
[
15
, Proposition 1.18, Proposition 1.20]
and
[
10
, Theorem 2.6]
.

3.5 Theorem
.

For all paracompact compactly generated spaces
T
, there is an equivalence

\Pi_{\infty}^{c}(T)\simeq\Pi_{\infty}(T)\smallin\Pro(\textbf{{Ani}})\hbox to0.0pt{\ {.}\hss}

Proof.

The result follows from the explicit description of the shape
\Pi_{\infty}(T)
of a paracompact space
T
due to Lurie and the fact that for a compactly generated space
T\smallin\textbf{{CG}}
the expression
T=\colim_{i\smallin I}K_{i}
as a colimit of extremally disconnected profinite sets is not only true as a colimit inside
\Cond(\textbf{{Ani}})
but also in
Top
:
It follows from
[
18
, Section 7.1.1, especially Corollary 7.1.4.4, Proposition 7.1.5.1]
, that for a paracompact space
T
the shape
\Pi_{\infty}(T)
identifies with

\textbf{{Ani}}\rightarrow\textbf{{Ani}},\hskip 11.74988ptA\mapsto\Hom_{\textbf{{Top}}}(T,|A|)\hbox to0.0pt{\ {.}\hss}

Here,
|A|
denotes the geometric realization of the anima
A
, i.e., the image under the left adjoint
|\cdot|\colon\textbf{{Ani}}\rightarrow\textbf{{Top}}
of the functor
\textbf{{Top}}\rightarrow\textbf{{Ani}}
assigning to a topological space
T
its fundamental
\infty
-groupoid
\mathrm{Sing}(T)\smallin\textbf{{Ani}}
.
As
T=\colim_{i\smallin I}K_{i}\smallin\Cond(\textbf{{Ani}})
is also assumed to be compactly generated and
\textbf{{CG}}\hookrightarrow\Cond(\textbf{{Ani}})
is fully faithful, its image under the left adjoint functor
\Cond(\textbf{{Ani}})\rightarrow\textbf{{Top}}
, assigning the underlying topological space to a condensed anima, is given by
\colim_{i\smallin I}K_{i}\smallin\textbf{{Top}}
and coincides with
T\smallin\textbf{{CG}}
. Thus, one has

\Pi_{\infty}(T)=\colim_{i\smallin I}\Hom_{\textbf{{Top}}}(K_{i},|\cdot|)\smallin\Pro(\textbf{{Ani}})\hbox to0.0pt{\ {.}\hss}

As every profinite set
K_{i}
is a paracompact space, we know by the same reasoning as above that

\Pi_{\infty}(K_{i})=\Hom_{\textbf{{Top}}}(K_{i},|\cdot|)\smallin\Pro(\textbf{{Ani}})

By
Remark

3.2
, this already leads to

\Pi_{\infty}(T)=\colim_{i\smallin I}\Pi_{\infty}(K_{i})=\Pi_{\infty}^{c}(T)\smallin\Pro(\textbf{{Ani}}).

∎

We do not know whether the statement is even valid for arbitrary compactly generated spaces as in that general case we are not in possession of a more explicit description of the shape.

3.6 Example
.

As every CW-space
T
is paracompact compactly generated, by combining
Theorem

3.5
with
Example

2.7
, it can be deduced that the shape
\Pi_{\infty}^{c}(T)
of its associated condensed anima
T\smallin\Cond(\textbf{{Ani}})
is again the homotopy type
\Sing(T)
.

We can extend the example to all locally contractible spaces using that the shape functor on condensed anima
\Pi_{\infty}^{c}
likewise satisfies homotopy invariance.
Therefore, we define the concept of homotopy equivalences of condensed anima along the lines of topological spaces.

3.7 Definition
.

Homotopy equivalences of condensed anima

Let
J=\mathopen{[}0,1\mathclose{]}\smallin\Cond(\textbf{{Ani}})
be the condensed anima associated with the unit interval and

\displaystyle*_{0}\colon*\rightarrow J\text{ and }*_{1}\colon*\rightarrow J

the morphisms of condensed anima corresponding to the points
0,1\smallin J=\mathopen{[}0,1\mathclose{]}\smallin\textbf{{Top}}
.

A map
f\colon T\rightarrow Y
of condensed anima is a
homotopy equivalence
if there exists a morphism
g\colon Y\rightarrow T
, the
homotopy inverse
, and
homotopies

\displaystyle H_{1}\colon T\times J\rightarrow T\text{ and }H_{2}\colon Y\times J\rightarrow Y

such that the compositions of
H_{1},H_{2}
with the maps

\displaystyle T\times*\xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}\times*_{i}}T\times J\text{ and }Y\times*\xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}\times*_{i}}Y\times J

are given by
g\circ f\colon T\rightarrow T
for
i=0
and, respectively,
f\circ g\colon Y\rightarrow Y
for
i=1
.

3.8 Remark
.

As
\textbf{{Top}}\rightarrow\Cond(\textbf{{Ani}})
is a right adjoint, for every homotopy equivalence of topological spaces
f\colon T\rightarrow Y\smallin\textbf{{Top}}
, the induced map
f\smallin\Cond(\textbf{{Ani}})
is a homotopy equivalence of condensed anima in the sense of the definition.
Moreover, just as in the case of topological spaces, for an
T\smallin\Cond(\textbf{{Ani}})
the projection map

\mathrm{pr}\colon T\times J\rightarrow T

is a common homotopy inverse of the inclusions
i_{0},i_{1}\colon T\simeq T\times{\mathbin{*}}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}\times{\mathbin{*}}_{0,1}}T\times J
.

3.9 Proposition
.

The shape functor
\Pi_{\infty}^{c}\colon\Cond(\textbf{{Ani}})\rightarrow\Pro(\textbf{{Ani}})
is homotopy invariant, i.e., inverts homotopy equivalences of condensed anima.

Proof.

By the same argument as in
Proposition

2.14
, it is enough to show that for all
T\smallin\Cond(\textbf{{Ani}})
the shape functor
\Pi_{\infty}^{c}
inverts the projection map
T\times[0,1]\rightarrow T
.
So let us show that the induced map

\Pi_{\infty}^{c}(T\times\mathopen{[}0,1\mathclose{]})\rightarrow\Pi_{\infty}^{c}(T)

is an equivalence in
\Pro(\textbf{{Ani}})
.
As sifted colimits commute with finite products in every
\infty
-topos, a representation of

with
I
sifted yields a representation

T\times[0,1]=\colim_{i\smallin I}(K_{i}\times[0,1])\hbox to0.0pt{\ {.}\hss}

We can reduce to spaces of the form

with

an extremally disconnected profinite set.
For these spaces, we know by

that

\Pi_{\infty}^{c}(K)=\Pi_{\infty}(K)=\Pi_{\infty}(K\times[0,1])\hbox to0.0pt{\ {.}\hss}

So it remains to deduce the equivalence

This follows from

∎

3.10 Remark
.

In other words, the shape functor factors as a composition

where

3.5
Comparison results for cohomology theories

We have studied how the passage from topological spaces to condensed anima is compatible with the notion of shape on topological spaces. Here, we will summarize how this has consequences on other important invariants of topological spaces to be detected on the associated condensed anima, namely cohomology.

3.37 Recollection
.

In the lecture notes on condensed mathematics by Clausen-Scholze
[
28

, there is a comparison result on condensed and sheaf cohomology of topological spaces based on work by Dyckoff
[
28

\displaystyle H^{*}_{\mathrm{sheaf}}(T;A)\xrightarrow{\simeq}H^{*}_{\mathrm{cond}}(T;A).

Haine extended this result to locally compact Hausdorff spaces and to very general coefficients (living in the

using that the comparison map between sheaf and condensed cohomology is induced by the comparison geometric morphism

which he showed to have a fully faithful pullback after Postnikov completion and tensoring with any compactly assembled
\infty
-category
\mathcal{E}

Our results on the shape

provide a further extension of (

is a group

with a topology such that

(i)

the inversion
G\rightarrow G,\ g\mapsto g^{-1}
, is continuous and

(ii)

the group operation
G\times G\rightarrow G
is continuous in each variable.

The category of quasi-topological groups with continuous group homomorphisms is
\mathrm{q}\textbf{{Top}}\textbf{{Grp}}
.

Note that the second condition in the definition is equivalent to the translations
g\mapsto gh
and
h\mapsto hg
being homeomorphisms for every
h\smallin G
.

4.4 Remark
.

Every topological group is a quasi-topological group and there exists a fully faithful inclusion functor

\textbf{{Top}}\textbf{{Grp}}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{q}\textbf{{Top}}\textbf{{Grp}}\hbox to0.0pt{\ {.}\hss}

A locally compact Hausdorff quasi-topological group is a topological group
[
11
, Theorem 2]
.

4.5 Remark
.

There are interesting examples of topologized groups which are not topological but quasi-topological. For instance, this phenomenon occurs when equipping fundamental groups of topological spaces in a canonical way with the quotient topology of the loop space, as for example studied by Brazas in

[
5
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
4
]
. It is an open question to us whether it is possible to recover these from a construction internal to condensed mathematics.

4.6 Proposition
.

[
6
, Lemma 3.2., Proposition 3.3., Corollary 3.9]

The fully faithful inclusion
\textbf{{Top}}\textbf{{Grp}}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{q}\textbf{{Top}}\textbf{{Grp}}
has a left adjoint
\tau\colon\mathrm{q}\textbf{{Top}}\textbf{{Grp}}\rightarrow\textbf{{Top}}\textbf{{Grp}}
, such that for
G\smallin\mathrm{q}\textbf{{Top}}\textbf{{Grp}}
the topological group
\tau(G)
has the same underlying group structure but a coarser topology such that

(i)

G
and
\tau(G)
share the same lattice of open subgroups,

(ii)

for every topological group
G\smallin\mathrm{q}\textbf{{Top}}\textbf{{Grp}}
, we find
G=\tau(G)\smallin\textbf{{Top}}\textbf{{Grp}}
.

Involving this left adjoint, we can describe the underlying topological group functor.

4.7 Proposition
.

Restriction of the functor

(\cdot)^{\textbf{{Top}}}\colon\Cond(\textbf{{Set}})\rightarrow\textbf{{Top}},\hskip 11.74988ptX\mapsto X({\mathbin{*}})^{\textbf{{Top}}}

to group objects in
\Cond(\textbf{{Set}})
lands in the category of quasi-topological groups, i.e., it restricts to

\Cond(\textbf{{Grp}})\rightarrow\mathrm{q}\textbf{{Top}}\textbf{{Grp}}\hbox to0.0pt{\ {.}\hss}

Then the left adjoint functor
(\cdot)^{\textbf{{Top}}\textbf{{Grp}}}\colon\Cond(\textbf{{Grp}})\rightarrow\textbf{{Top}}\textbf{{Grp}}
is given as the composition

(4.8)

\displaystyle(\cdot)^{\tau}\colon\Cond(\textbf{{Grp}})\rightarrow\mathrm{q}\textbf{{Top}}\textbf{{Grp}}\xrightarrow{\tau}\textbf{{Top}}\textbf{{Grp}}\hbox to0.0pt{\ {.}\hss}

Proof.

First (
1.
), we prove that restriction of the functor

(\cdot)^{\textbf{{Top}}}\colon\Cond(\textbf{{Set}})\rightarrow\textbf{{Top}},\hskip 11.74988ptX\mapsto X({\mathbin{*}})^{\textbf{{Top}}}

to group objects in
\Cond(\textbf{{Set}})
lands in the category
\mathrm{q}\textbf{{Top}}\textbf{{Grp}}
.
Second (
2.
), we show that the described composition (
4.8
) takes over the role as the left adjoint functor
(\cdot)^{\textbf{{Top}}\textbf{{Grp}}}\colon\Cond(\textbf{{Grp}})\rightarrow\textbf{{Top}}\textbf{{Grp}}
, i.e., for all condensed groups
G\smallin\Cond(\textbf{{Grp}})
and topological groups
F\smallin\textbf{{Top}}\textbf{{Grp}}
there is a bijection of hom-sets natural in
G
and
F

(4.9)

\displaystyle\Hom_{\Cond(\textbf{{Grp}})}(G,\underline{F})\simeq\Hom_{\textbf{{Top}}\textbf{{Grp}}}(G^{\tau},F)\hbox to0.0pt{\ {.}\hss}

(1.)
We need to show that for every condensed group
G
the underlying group
G({\mathbin{*}})
equipped with the quotient topology carries the structure of a quasi-topological group and that the induced morphisms between the underlying groups of two condensed groups are continuous group homomorphisms.
As
G
is a condensed group, every set
G(K)
carries a group structure and we have morphisms
G\rightarrow G
of condensed sets representing the inverse and translation maps.
More precisely, there is a morphism
(\cdot)^{-1}\colon G\rightarrow G
of condensed sets such that for all
K
the map
G(K)\rightarrow G(K)
sends a group element
g_{K}\smallin G(K)
to its inverse
(g_{K})^{-1}\smallin G(K)
.
Moreover, for all
h\smallin G({\mathbin{*}})
we have morphisms
(h\cdot)\colon G\rightarrow G
and
(\cdot h)\colon G\rightarrow G
sending an element
g_{K}\smallin G(K)
to the translation
h_{K}\cdot g_{K}
or
g_{K}\cdot h_{K}
, respectively. Here,
h_{K}
denotes the image of
h\smallin G({\mathbin{*}})
under the map
G({\mathbin{*}})\rightarrow G(K)
induced by the unique morphism
K\rightarrow{\mathbin{*}}
.
These morphisms are sent to continuous morphisms
G({\mathbin{*}})^{\textbf{{Top}}}\rightarrow G({\mathbin{*}})^{\textbf{{Top}}}
on the topological space
G({\mathbin{*}})^{\textbf{{Top}}}
, whose underlying set carries the structure of a group.
Hence,
G({\mathbin{*}})^{\textbf{{Top}}}
is a topologized group with continuous inverse
g\mapsto g^{-1}
as well as continuous translations
g\mapsto hg
and
g\mapsto gh
for all
h\smallin G({\mathbin{*}})
. By definition, it thus is a quasi-topological group.
If
G\rightarrow H
is a morphism of condensed groups, the map
G({\mathbin{*}})^{\textbf{{Top}}}\rightarrow H({\mathbin{*}})^{\textbf{{Top}}}
is a morphism of quasi-topological groups if it is a continuous group homomorphism.
The map is clearly continuous as it lives in the category
Top
. It is also a group homomorphism by the definition of morphisms of condensed groups as level-wise group homomorphisms (satisfying certain compatibility conditions).

(2.)
We already know about isomorphisms natural in
C\smallin\Cond(\textbf{{Set}})
and
T\smallin\textbf{{Top}}

(4.10)

\displaystyle\Hom_{\Cond(\textbf{{Set}})}(C,\underline{X})\simeq\Hom_{\textbf{{Top}}}(C({\mathbin{*}})^{\textbf{{Top}}},X),

as well as natural in
Q\smallin\mathrm{q}\textbf{{Top}}\textbf{{Grp}}
and
F\smallin\textbf{{Top}}\textbf{{Grp}}

(4.11)

\displaystyle\Hom_{\mathrm{q}\textbf{{Top}}\textbf{{Grp}}}(Q,F)\simeq\Hom_{\textbf{{Top}}\textbf{{Grp}}}(\tau(Q),F).

If we are able to show that the isomorphism (
4.10
) restricts on group objects in
\Cond(\textbf{{Set}})
and
Top
to an isomorphism

(4.12)

\displaystyle\Hom_{\Cond(\textbf{{Grp}})}(G,\underline{F})\simeq\Hom_{\mathrm{q}\textbf{{Top}}\textbf{{Grp}}}(G({\mathbin{*}})^{\textbf{{Top}}},F),

which is then automatically natural in
G\smallin\Cond(\textbf{{Grp}})
and
F\smallin\textbf{{Top}}\textbf{{Grp}}
, we can deduce the claim by combining (
4.12
) and (
4.11
) to obtain (
4.9
). Note that one has
G^{\tau}=\tau(G({\mathbin{*}})^{\textbf{{Top}}})
by definition.
So let
G\rightarrow\underline{F}
be a morphism of condensed groups, where
F
is a topological group. In particular, it is a morphism on the level of condensed sets and thus under (
4.10
) corresponds to a morphism
G({\mathbin{*}})^{\textbf{{Top}}}\rightarrow F
of topological spaces. We already argued above, that
G({\mathbin{*}})^{\textbf{{Top}}}\rightarrow\underline{F}({\mathbin{*}})^{\textbf{{Top}}}
is a morphism of quasi-topological groups. This remains through after postcomposition with the continuous counit
\underline{F}({\mathbin{*}})^{\textbf{{Top}}}\rightarrow F
which is on the underlying groups just the identity and thus clearly a morphism on the level of quasi-topological groups, i.e., a continuous group homomorphism. On the other hand, we have to show that every continuous group homomorphism
G({\mathbin{*}})^{\textbf{{Top}}}\rightarrow F
corresponds under (
4.10
) to a morphism
G\rightarrow\underline{F}
of condensed groups and not just of condensed sets. Indeed, we need to prove that
for all
K\smallin\textbf{{Extr}}
the morphism
G(K)\rightarrow\underline{F}(K)
of sets is indeed a homomorphism of groups such that the square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G(K^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G(K)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{F}(K)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{F}(K^{\prime})}

commutes for all
f\colon K\rightarrow K^{\prime}
in
Extr
.
The commutativity condition is already satisfied by
G\rightarrow\underline{F}
being a morphism of condensed sets.
For the group homomorphism condition, note that the morphism
G(K)\rightarrow\underline{F}(K)
is given as the composition
G(K)\rightarrow\underline{G({\mathbin{*}})^{\textbf{{Top}}}}(K)\rightarrow\underline{F}(K)
of the unit of the adjunction with the map induced by the given morphism
G({\mathbin{*}})^{\textbf{{Top}}}\rightarrow F
.
The set
\underline{G({\mathbin{*}})^{\textbf{{Top}}}}(K)
is in general not a group as we do not have a continuous multiplication on the quasi-topological group
G({\mathbin{*}})^{\textbf{{Top}}}
.
Nevertheless, it formally follows that for every
K
the composition above is a homomorphism of groups: For an element
g\smallin G(K)
, its image under the unit
G(K)\rightarrow\underline{G({\mathbin{*}})^{\textbf{{Top}}}}(K)=\Hom_{\textbf{{Top}}}(K,G({\mathbin{*}})^{\textbf{{Top}}})
is given by sending
g
to the morphism

K\rightarrow G({\mathbin{*}})^{\textbf{{Top}}},\ (k=({\mathbin{*}}\xrightarrow{k}K))\mapsto(G(k)(g))\hbox to0.0pt{\ {.}\hss}

This image is just the evaluation of the morphism
g\colon h_{k}\rightarrow G
, corresponding to the element
g\smallin G(K)
under the Yoneda identification
G(K)=\Hom(h_{k},G)
, at the point
{\mathbin{*}}
.
If we consider two elements
g,h\smallin G(K)
and their product
g\cdot h
in
G(K)
, then by the above its image under
G(K)\rightarrow\underline{G({\mathbin{*}})^{\textbf{{Top}}}}(K)=\Hom_{\textbf{{Top}}}(K,G({\mathbin{*}})^{\textbf{{Top}}})
is given by the morphism

K\rightarrow G({\mathbin{*}})^{\textbf{{Top}}},\ (k=({\mathbin{*}}\xrightarrow{k}K))\mapsto(G(k)(g\cdot h))

which is continuous by definition of the topology on
G({\mathbin{*}})^{\textbf{{Top}}}
. As for all
k\colon{\mathbin{*}}\rightarrow K
, the induced morphism
G(K)\xrightarrow{G(k)}G({\mathbin{*}})
is a group homomorphism, we have for all these
k

(G(k)(g\cdot h))=G(k)(g)\cdot G(k)(h)\hbox to0.0pt{\ {.}\hss}

Further, the morphism
\underline{G({\mathbin{*}})^{\textbf{{Top}}}}(K)\rightarrow\underline{F}(K)
is given by postcomposition with the continuous map
G({\mathbin{*}})^{\textbf{{Top}}}\rightarrow F
of quasi-topological groups, which indeed preserves the group structure. All in all, the map
G(K)\rightarrow\underline{G({\mathbin{*}})^{\textbf{{Top}}}}(K)\rightarrow\underline{F}(K)
is compatible with the group structures on
G(K)
and
\underline{F}(K)
and thus a group homomorphism.
∎

Note that the underlying quasi-topological group will carry a compactly generated topology as this is true for the underlying topological space of every condensed set. Its image under the functor

\tau\colon\mathrm{q}\textbf{{Top}}\textbf{{Grp}}\rightarrow\textbf{{Top}}\textbf{{Grp}}

will then, in general, carry a coarser topology.

4.13 Corollary
.

If the underlying topological space of a condensed group
G
is locally compact Hausdorff, the image of
G
under

(\cdot)^{\textbf{{Top}}\textbf{{Grp}}}\colon\Cond(\textbf{{Grp}})\rightarrow\textbf{{Top}}\textbf{{Grp}}

agrees with
G({\mathbin{*}})^{\textbf{{Top}}}
inherited with the group structure of
G({\mathbin{*}})
.

Proof.

By
Remark

4.4
, every locally compact Hausdorff quasi-topological group is already a topological group and thus
G^{\textbf{{Top}}\textbf{{Grp}}}=\tau(G({\mathbin{*}})^{\textbf{{Top}}})=G({\mathbin{*}})^{\textbf{{Top}}}
.
∎
