A case of pause-dependent atrioventricular block with ventricular arrhythmias following redo Bentall procedure
Authors: Mohammed Mhanna, Denice Hodgson-Zingman, Brian Olshansky
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
A 63-year-old male with prior aortic valve replacement and Bentall procedure for E. faecalis endocarditis developed paroxysmal atrioventricular (AV) block after repeat Bentall surgery. Telemetry demonstrated PVC-triggered phase 4 block leading to high-grade AV block, prolonged pauses, and polymorphic ventricular tachycardia. A temporary permanent pacemaker was implanted, followed by ventricular leadless pacemaker placement. This case highlights the malignant potential of phase 4 block, particularly when combined with postoperative conduction system disease, and underscores the importance of early recognition and pacing support.

Case presentation

The patient was a 63-year-old male with a history of hypertension, peripheral vascular disease, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, paroxysmal atrial fibrillation on apixaban, and severe aortic stenosis status post aortic valve replacement (2013). In May 2024 he underwent redo aortic valve explant with Bentall procedure. He later presented with E. faecalis bacteremia complicated by prosthetic valve endocarditis, splenic infarct, and right middle cerebral artery stroke requiring thrombectomy. Workup revealed aortic root abscess with left ventricular outflow tract fistula, for which a repeat Bentall procedure was performed on January 9, 2025.

Postoperatively he developed intermittent complete heart block. Initial telemetry showed sinus rhythm with right bundle branch block, first-degree AV block, and left axis deviation. On day 4, transient high-grade AV block with pauses was noted, often following premature ventricular complex (PVC). On day 5, prolonged post-PVC pauses were associated with polymorphic VT/torsades. A temporary permanent pacemaker was implanted (VVI 40, later increased to 60 bpm to reduce pause length), stabilizing the rhythm. The patient was discharged with the device in place and later underwent successful leadless pacemaker implantation.

Discussion

This case demonstrates pause-dependent (phase 4) AV block triggered by PVCs in the setting of extensive postoperative conduction system disease. The phenomenon, first described by Rosenbaum et al. [1] and later elaborated by Uhm et al. among others [2], is due to abnormal His-Purkinje automaticity and diastolic depolarization that predispose to block after long pauses or ectopy.

Our case highlights several key teaching points:1.Triggers of Phase 4 Block – PVCs are classic initiators of phase 4 block. In our case, post-PVC pauses precipitated high-grade AV block.2.Association with Ventricular Arrhythmias – Prolonged pauses created by phase 4 block can facilitate pause-dependent polymorphic VT/torsades, as observed here. Additionally, transient ventricular pacing may further modulate repolarization by altering the activation sequence and regional dispersion of refractoriness. In the presence of postoperative conduction disease, a V-paced beat can transiently enhance repolarization heterogeneity, thereby lowering the threshold for pause-dependent polymorphic ventricular tachycardia (VT).3.Diagnostic Challenge – Surface ECG showed trifascicular disease with intermittent complete heart block. The absence of intracardiac electrograms was a limitation; however, the temporal relation of PVCs, AV block, and pause-dependent VT strongly supported the diagnosis.4.Therapeutic Considerations – Temporary pacing stabilized the rhythm by shortening pauses, preventing recurrent torsades. Ultimately, leadless pacemaker implantation was favored given the patient's infection risk profile.

Prior series of phase 4 block have documented various triggers—sinus slowing, premature atrial complexes, PVCs, and termination of supraventricular tachycardia. Most patients require permanent pacing due to high recurrence risk [[3], [4], [5]]. Our case extends these observations by illustrating malignant ventricular arrhythmias superimposed on phase 4 block in a postoperative setting.

Conclusion

Pause-dependent AV block is an important postoperative complication in patients with structural conduction disease. When triggered by PVCs, it can result in malignant ventricular arrhythmias. Prompt recognition, pacing support, and long-term device therapy are essential for preventing sudden cardiac death (see Fig. 1).

Editorial commentary (Bernard Belhassen)

I thank Drs Mhanna, Zingman and Olshansky for this article dealing with “phase 4 block” which was one of the subjects of expertise of my mentor in Paris, the late Professor Gilbert Motté [6]. Paroxysmal AV block is closely related to severe conduction disturbance in relation with a spontaneous diastolic depolarization of diseased cells at a time when sodium channels are inactive (“phase 4 block”). It has been described only at the infranodal level (His bundle and bundle branches). This phenomenon is based on Weidmann's rule [7] closely relating the transmembrane action potential with conduction velocity (a less negative diastolic potential in working myocardial cells contributes to the slow conduction of the electrical impulse) as well as observations of Singer et al. associating automaticity and conduction [8]. As nicely shown in the present case, phase 4 block may be initiated by a simple critical prolongation of the diastolic interval, such the one achieved here by the post PVC interval prolongation (Fig. 2, Fig. 3, Fig. 4). Also, very important in these patients is what happens once complete AV block is installed. One would easily imagine that patient's death might occur following irreversible complete AV block or sustained bradycardia-induced polymorphic ventricular tachycardia/fibrillation. Fortunately, this seems not to be the typical scenario in many instances such as in the present case due to two reasons: a) a ventricular escape (Fig. 2, Fig. 3) or nonsustained polymorphic VT (Fig. 4) spontaneously occur that reset the transmembrane potential at its maximal resting value and b) a sinus beat falling at an appropriately time after the escape beat is present to enable 1 to 1 AV conduction.
