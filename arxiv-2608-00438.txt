Homogenization of Viscous Sublinear Hamilton–Jacobi Equations with u / ϵ u/\epsilon -Dependence
Guyu Jin

Abstract
We study the periodic homogenization of a class of viscous Hamilton–Jacobi equations with fast dependence on the unknown. This problem combines features of first-order Hamilton–Jacobi equations with u ϵ / ϵ u^{\epsilon}/\epsilon -periodic Hamiltonians and semilinear heat equations with rapidly oscillating positive potentials. In this paper, we prove qualitative homogenization results for H ⁡ ( y , s , p ) = F ⁡ ( s , p ) + η ​ W ​ ( y , s , p ) H(y,s,p)=F(s,p)+\eta W(y,s,p) when | η | |\eta| is sufficiently small, with the smallness threshold depending on the Lipschitz constant of the initial datum, and establish a large-time averaging result for a general class of evolutionary cell problems.

Introduction

1.1
Setting of the problem

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\epsilon>0
be a small parameter. We consider a continuous Hamiltonian

H:\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R},

and an initial datum
u_{0}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}.

The unknown is a function
u^{\epsilon}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R},

and we denote by
u_{t}^{\epsilon}
,
Du^{\epsilon}
and
\Delta u^{\epsilon}
its time derivative, spatial gradient and Laplacian, respectively. The equation studied in this paper is of the form

\begin{cases}u_{t}^{\epsilon}+H\left(\dfrac{x}{\epsilon},\dfrac{u^{\epsilon}}{\epsilon},Du^{\epsilon}\right)=\epsilon\Delta u^{\epsilon}&\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,\infty),\\[5.69054pt]
u^{\epsilon}(x,0)=u_{0}(x)&\text{on }\mathbb{R}^{N},\end{cases}

(1.1)

where

u_{0}\in BUC(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Here
H
is periodic in the first two variables and belongs to
C(\mathbb{T}^{N}\times\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

In order to understand the contribution of this work, we recall that the theory of homogenization for first-order Hamilton–Jacobi equation started with the famous unpublished work of Lions, Papanicolaou and Varadhan
[
18
]
who completely solved the problem in the case of periodic and coercive Hamiltonians. Since the literature on homogenization theory for Hamilton–Jacobi equations is vast, we focus here on the works most relevant to the present paper. For the first-order Hamilton-Jacobi equation with
u^{\epsilon}/\epsilon
-periodic Hamiltonians, we first refer to Imbert and Monneau
[
13
]
and Barles
[
3
]
. Achdou and Patrizi
[
1
]
obtain a rate of convergence for contact-type Hamilton-Jacobi equations, and then Mitake, Ni and Tran
[
19
]
establish the optimal convergence rate for convex superlinear contact-type Hamilton-Jacobi equation. Ni
[
21
]
obtain a quantitative homogenization result for first-order ODEs with a form related to our model, and Ni, Wang and Yan
[
20
]
are the first to use the method of contact Hamilton systems and implicit variational principle to study the contact-type Hamilton-Jacobi equation with
u
-periodicity. We also refer to
[
24
,
25
,
26
]
for related works particularly from the viewpoint of weak KAM theory. The viscous Hamilton–Jacobi equation has a different structure, since the associated cell problem contains an elliptic operator and the coercivity is not always necessary. Related results include the work of Barles and Souganidis
[
2
]
, and the recent quantitative homogenization result of Qian, Sprekeler, Tran and Yu
[
22
]
. In
[
22
]
, the obtained convergence rate
O(\epsilon^{1/2})
is optimal. For further results on second-order Hamilton–Jacobi homogenization, we refer to
[
16
,
17
,
9
,
23
]
.

The distinguishing feature of (
1.1
) is the simultaneous fast dependence on the spatial variable
x/\epsilon
and on the phase
u^{\epsilon}/\epsilon
. If the viscous term is removed, one recovers the contact-type first-order Hamilton–Jacobi equation. On the other hand, if the Hamiltonian has no gradient dependence and is a positive periodic function of
u^{\epsilon}/\epsilon
, the equation is closely related to the semilinear heat equation studied by Cesaroni, Dirr and Novaga
[
6
]
. In that setting the effective speed is described by a one-dimensional cell ODE, or equivalently by a travelling wave speed. Related periodic travelling waves in heterogeneous media were studied, for instance, by Chen and Namah
[
7
]
. The cell problem considered here is closely related to the equations satisfied by travelling-wave solutions of reaction–diffusion equations , see
[
4
,
5
,
27
]
.

The presence of the viscous term in (
1.1
) creates a new difficulty. For a general Hamiltonian
H(y,s,p)
, the heuristic explanations lead to the cell problem

\displaystyle c(p)(\chi_{p})_{z}

\displaystyle+H\left(y,\chi_{p},D_{y}\chi_{p}+p(\chi_{p})_{z}\right)

(1.2)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{y}\chi_{p}+2p\cdot D^{2}_{yz}\chi_{p}+|p|^{2}(\chi_{p})_{zz},

where
c(p)
is a constant depending on
p\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. If a periodic solution of (
1.2
) is available, and
c(p)
is continuous, then Evans’ perturbed test function method
[
10
]
can be used to prove homogenization. However, solving (
1.2
) for a general Hamiltonian is highly nontrivial. It is worth noting that this equation is degenerate when
p=0
. So far, for general
H
, it remains unclear whether the cell problem (
1.2
) admits a solution.

In this paper we therefore focus on the class

H_{\eta}(y,s,p)=F(s,p)+\eta W(y,s,p),

(1.3)

where
F
is the principal part and
\eta W
is a spatially oscillatory perturbation. The smallness of
\eta
is local in the gradient variable: if
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is the compact set on which the correctors are needed, then the construction gives a number
\eta_{K}>0
. In the homogenization theorem, this compact set is chosen from the Lipschitz constant of
u_{0}
, see Section 3 for more details.

The main novelty of this work lies in the treatment of a second-order contact-type Hamilton–Jacobi homogenization problem. In the first-order theory with
u^{\epsilon}/\epsilon
-periodic Hamiltonians, the viscous term is absent, while in the semilinear heat equation setting the oscillatory forcing depends only on the fast phase
u^{\epsilon}/\epsilon
and has no Hamilton–Jacobi gradient dependence. By contrast, here the equation contains the viscous term
\epsilon\Delta u^{\epsilon}
, and the oscillatory Hamiltonian may depend simultaneously on the fast space variable, the fast phase, and the gradient. This leads from the one-dimensional travelling-wave ODE of the semilinear model to a periodic-parabolic corrector equation, see Section 3 (
3.4
). Parabolic equations with gradient-dependent nonlinearities have been studied in other contexts, for instance in
[
11
]
; to the best of our knowledge, this is the first perturbative construction of periodic-parabolic correctors for viscous Hamilton–Jacobi equations with simultaneous
x/\epsilon
- and
u^{\epsilon}/\epsilon
-oscillations and a gradient-dependent spatial perturbation.

(F4).

–

\begin{cases}u_{t}+\overline{H}(Du)=0&\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,\infty),\\

(1.11)

We next consider the perturbed Hamiltonian (
1.3
).For each fixed

\chi_{p}^{\eta}(y,z)=z+w_{p}^{\eta}(y,z)

\displaystyle c_{\eta}(p)(\chi_{p}^{\eta})_{z}

\displaystyle+F\left(\chi_{p}^{\eta},D_{y}\chi_{p}^{\eta}+p(\chi_{p}^{\eta})_{z}\right)

\displaystyle+\eta W\left(y,\chi_{p}^{\eta},D_{y}\chi_{p}^{\eta}+p(\chi_{p}^{\eta})_{z}\right)

(Homogenization in the perturbative case)
.

Let

(1.12)

converges locally uniformly to the viscosity solution

\begin{cases}u_{t}+\overline{H}_{\eta}(Du)=0&\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,\infty),\\

Finally, we discuss the large-time average of the cell evolution associated with a general periodic Hamiltonian satisfying the assumptions (H1)–(H2) in Section 4. If

\begin{cases}w_{\tau}+H(\zeta,p\cdot\zeta+w,p+D_{\zeta}w)=\Delta_{\zeta}w,&\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,\infty),\\[2.84526pt]

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we study the unperturbed Hamiltonian
F(s,p)
. We derive the cell ODE, construct the monotone corrector, prove the uniqueness and continuity of the cell speed, and establish homogenization in this case. In Section 3, we treat
F(s,p)+\eta W(y,s,p)
and construct correctors using periodic-parabolic Fredholm theory and a fixed point argument. In Section 4, we prove the large-time averaged cell result and identify the averaged constant with the corrector speed in the perturbative class. Section 5 gives examples. The appendices contain the comparison principle, a uniform bound and initial-trace lemma, and the phase-shift contact lemma used in the perturbed test function argument.

2.1

v_{n}
gives

\mu_{n}v_{n}(s_{n}^{M})=F(s_{n}^{M},p_{n}v_{n}(s_{n}^{M})).

Using the Lipschitz continuity of
F
in the
p
-variable, we obtain

F(s_{n}^{M},p_{n}v_{n}(s_{n}^{M}))\leq A+L|p_{n}|v_{n}(s_{n}^{M}).

Moreover, since
v_{n}(s_{n}^{M})\geq 1
and
F\geq c_{0}
, we have

\mu_{n}=\frac{F(s_{n}^{M},p_{n}v_{n}(s_{n}^{M}))}{v_{n}(s_{n}^{M})}\geq\frac{c_{0}}{v_{n}(s_{n}^{M})}.

A simpler lower bound follows from a minimum point. Let
s_{n}^{m}
be a minimum point of
v_{n}
. Then
v_{n}^{\prime}(s_{n}^{m})=0
and

\mu_{n}v_{n}(s_{n}^{m})=F(s_{n}^{m},p_{n}v_{n}(s_{n}^{m})).

Since
v_{n}(s_{n}^{m})\leq 1
and
F\geq c_{0}
, we get
\mu_{n}\geq c_{0}.

On the other hand, from the maximum point identity and the estimate above,

\mu_{n}v_{n}(s_{n}^{M})\leq A+L|p_{n}|v_{n}(s_{n}^{M}).

Hence, for
n
large enough,

(\mu_{n}-L|p_{n}|)v_{n}(s_{n}^{M})\leq A.

Since
\mu_{n}\geq c_{0}
, choosing
n
large so that
L|p_{n}|\leq c_{0}/2
, we obtain

v_{n}(s)\leq v_{n}(s_{n}^{M})\leq\frac{2A}{c_{0}}\quad\text{for all }s.

(2.4)

Similarly, using the minimum point identity,

\mu_{n}v_{n}(s_{n}^{m})=F(s_{n}^{m},p_{n}v_{n}(s_{n}^{m}))\geq a-L|p_{n}|v_{n}(s_{n}^{m}).

Thus

(\mu_{n}+L|p_{n}|)v_{n}(s_{n}^{m})\geq a.

Since
\mu_{n}\leq A+L|p_{n}|
, we get, for
n
large,

v_{n}(s)\geq v_{n}(s_{n}^{m})\geq C^{-1}\quad\text{for all }s,

(2.5)

where
C>0
is independent of
n
. Therefore both
v_{n}
and
a_{n}=1/v_{n}
are uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}(0,1)
.

By passing to a subsequence, we may assume that

\mu_{n}\to\mu_{*}

and

a_{n}\rightharpoonup a_{*}\quad\text{weakly-* in }L^{\infty}(0,1).

We claim that

a_{*}(s)F(s,0)=\mu_{*}\quad\text{a.e. in }(0,1).

(2.6)

Indeed, dividing the ODE

|p_{n}|^{2}v_{n}(s)v_{n}^{\prime}(s)=-\mu_{n}v_{n}(s)+F(s,p_{n}v_{n}(s))

by
v_{n}(s)
gives

|p_{n}|^{2}v_{n}^{\prime}(s)=-\mu_{n}+\frac{F(s,p_{n}v_{n}(s))}{v_{n}(s)}=-\mu_{n}+a_{n}(s)F(s,p_{n}v_{n}(s)).

Let
\varphi\in C^{1}_{\rm per}([0,1])
. Multiplying by
\varphi
and integrating by parts, we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left(-\mu_{n}+a_{n}(s)F(s,p_{n}v_{n}(s))\right)\varphi(s)\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad=|p_{n}|^{2}\int_{0}^{1}v_{n}^{\prime}(s)\varphi(s)\,ds=-|p_{n}|^{2}\int_{0}^{1}v_{n}(s)\varphi^{\prime}(s)\,ds.

Since
v_{n}
is uniformly bounded and
p_{n}\to 0
, the right-hand side tends to
0
. Moreover,

\left|a_{n}(s)F(s,p_{n}v_{n}(s))-a_{n}(s)F(s,0)\right|\leq L|p_{n}|

because
a_{n}(s)v_{n}(s)=1
. Hence

\int_{0}^{1}\left(-\mu_{n}+a_{n}(s)F(s,0)\right)\varphi(s)\,ds\to 0.

Letting
n\to\infty
, we obtain

\int_{0}^{1}\left(-\mu_{*}+a_{*}(s)F(s,0)\right)\varphi(s)\,ds=0

for every
\varphi\in C^{1}_{\rm per}([0,1])
. Therefore
a_{*}(s)F(s,0)=\mu_{*}
a.e.

On the other hand, since

\int_{0}^{1}a_{n}(s)\,ds=1,

we also have
\int_{0}^{1}a_{*}(s)\,ds=1.

Using

a_{*}(s)=\frac{\mu_{*}}{F(s,0)},

we get

1=\mu_{*}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{F(s,0)}\,ds.

Thus

\mu_{*}=\left(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{F(s,0)}\,ds\right)^{-1}=\mu(0).

Since every convergent subsequence of
\mu(p_{n})
has the same limit
\mu(0)
, we conclude that
\mu(p_{n})\to\mu(0)\text{ as }p_{n}\to 0.

Therefore

c(p_{n})=-\mu(p_{n})\to-\mu(0)=c(0).

This proves the continuity of
c(p)
at
p=0
.
∎

We end this subsection with uniform estimates for the correctors.

Proposition 2.3
.

Suppose that
K
is a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Then there exist constants

0<m_{K}\leq M_{K}<\infty,\qquad C_{K}<\infty,

such that

m_{K}\leq\chi_{p}^{\prime}(z)\leq M_{K}

for each
z\in\mathbb{R}
, and

\|\chi_{p}-z\|_{C^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\leq C_{K}.

Proof.

We first consider the case where
K
is contained in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
. In this case, the equation

v^{\prime}(s)=\frac{-\mu v(s)+F(s,pv(s))}{|p|^{2}v(s)}

is a regular periodic ODE with parameters
(p,\mu)
. Hence the desired estimates follow from the continuous dependence of ODE solutions on parameters and the compactness of
K
.

It remains to prove the estimates near
p=0
. Let

\mu_{0}:=\mu(0)=\left(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{F(s,0)}\,ds\right)^{-1}.

By Proposition
2.2
,

\mu(p)\to\mu_{0}\text{ as }p\to 0.

Hence, there exist
\delta_{0}>0
and constants
0<\mu_{-}<\mu_{+}
such that
\mu_{-}\leq\mu(p)\leq\mu_{+}
for
|p|\leq\delta_{0}
.

Let
p\neq 0
and
|p|\leq\delta_{0}
. Write

v_{p}:=v_{\mu(p)}.

We first prove that
v_{p}
is uniformly bounded from above and below. By (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), there exist constants
0<m_{0}\leq M_{0}<\infty
such that

m_{0}\leq v_{p}(s)\leq M_{0}

for all
s\in\mathbb{T}
and all
0<|p|\leq\delta_{0}
.

Next, we prove a uniform bound for
v_{p}^{\prime}
. For fixed
p
and
s
, define
V_{p}(s)>0
by the algebraic equation

\mu(p)V_{p}(s)=F(s,pV_{p}(s)).

(2.7)

This positive solution is unique. Indeed,

v\to\frac{F(s,pv)}{v}

is strictly decreasing by (F2), tends to
+\infty
as
v\to 0^{+}
, and tends to
F_{\infty}(p)
as
v\to+\infty
. Since
\mu(p)>F_{\infty}(p)
, the equation has a unique positive solution.

We claim that
V_{p}
is uniformly bounded from above and below for
|p|
small. By (
2.7
) and the Lipschitz continuity of
F
,

\mu(p)V_{p}(s)\leq A+L|p|V_{p}(s).

Since
\mu(p)\geq c_{0}
for
|p|
small, we obtain

V_{p}(s)\leq C.

(2.8)

Similarly,

\mu(p)V_{p}(s)=F(s,pV_{p}(s))\geq a-L|p|V_{p}(s),

(2.9)

and since
\mu(p)
is bounded from above, we obtain

V_{p}(s)\geq C^{-1}.

Thus
V_{p}
is uniformly bounded above and below. In particular,

pV_{p}(s)\to 0

uniformly as
p\to 0
.

We also have a uniform bound for
V_{p}^{\prime}
. Differentiating (
2.7
) with respect to
s
, we obtain

\left(\mu(p)-D_{p}F(s,pV_{p}(s))\cdot p\right)V_{p}^{\prime}(s)=F_{s}(s,pV_{p}(s)).

Since
\mu(p)\to\mu_{0}>0
and
D_{p}F
is bounded, after decreasing
\delta_{0}
if necessary,

\mu(p)-D_{p}F(s,pV_{p}(s))\cdot p\geq\gamma_{0}>0.

Therefore

\|V_{p}^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})}\leq C.

Set

G_{p}(s,v):=F(s,pv)-\mu(p)v.

Then
G_{p}(s,V_{p}(s))=0.

Moreover, for
v
in a fixed compact interval containing the ranges of
v_{p}
and
V_{p}
, we have

\partial_{v}G_{p}(s,v)=D_{p}F(s,pv)\cdot p-\mu(p)\leq-\gamma_{1}<0

for all
0<|p|\leq\delta_{0}
, after decreasing
\delta_{0}
if necessary.
We now show that

\|v_{p}-V_{p}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})}\leq C|p|^{2}.

Let

h_{p}:=v_{p}-V_{p}.

Assume that
h_{p}
has a positive maximum at
s_{0}
. Then

h_{p}^{\prime}(s_{0})=0,

and hence

v_{p}^{\prime}(s_{0})=V_{p}^{\prime}(s_{0}).

Since
v_{p}
satisfies

|p|^{2}v_{p}(s)v_{p}^{\prime}(s)=G_{p}(s,v_{p}(s)),

(2.10)

we get

G_{p}(s_{0},v_{p}(s_{0}))=|p|^{2}v_{p}(s_{0})V_{p}^{\prime}(s_{0}).

On the other hand, since
v_{p}(s_{0})>V_{p}(s_{0})
and
\partial_{v}G_{p}\leq-\gamma_{1}
,

G_{p}(s_{0},v_{p}(s_{0}))\leq-\gamma_{1}h_{p}(s_{0}).

Therefore

\gamma_{1}h_{p}(s_{0})\leq|p|^{2}|v_{p}(s_{0})|\,|V_{p}^{\prime}(s_{0})|\leq C|p|^{2}.

Hence

\max_{\mathbb{T}}h_{p}\leq C|p|^{2}.

Similarly, assume that
h_{p}
has a negative minimum at
s_{1}
. Then

h_{p}^{\prime}(s_{1})=0,

and hence

v_{p}^{\prime}(s_{1})=V_{p}^{\prime}(s_{1}).

Since
v_{p}(s_{1})<V_{p}(s_{1})
and
\partial_{v}G_{p}\leq-\gamma_{1}
, we have

G_{p}(s_{1},v_{p}(s_{1}))\geq\gamma_{1}\bigl(V_{p}(s_{1})-v_{p}(s_{1})\bigr)=-\gamma_{1}h_{p}(s_{1}).

On the other hand,

G_{p}(s_{1},v_{p}(s_{1}))=|p|^{2}v_{p}(s_{1})V_{p}^{\prime}(s_{1}).

Thus

-\gamma_{1}h_{p}(s_{1})\leq|p|^{2}|v_{p}(s_{1})|\,|V_{p}^{\prime}(s_{1})|\leq C|p|^{2}.

Therefore

-\min_{\mathbb{T}}h_{p}\leq C|p|^{2}.

Combining the two estimates, we obtain

\|v_{p}-V_{p}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})}\leq C|p|^{2}.

Finally, using (
2.10
) and the mean value theorem,

|G_{p}(s,v_{p}(s))|=|G_{p}(s,v_{p}(s))-G_{p}(s,V_{p}(s))|\leq C|v_{p}(s)-V_{p}(s)|\leq C|p|^{2}.

Since
v_{p}\geq m_{0}>0
, we conclude that

|v_{p}^{\prime}(s)|\leq C

uniformly for all
s\in\mathbb{T}
and all
0<|p|\leq\delta_{0}
.

Now recall that
\chi_{p}^{\prime}(z)=v_{p}(\chi_{p}(z)).

Therefore
m_{0}\leq\chi_{p}^{\prime}(z)\leq M_{0}.

Moreover,
\chi_{p}^{\prime\prime}(z)=v_{p}^{\prime}(\chi_{p}(z))\chi_{p}^{\prime}(z).

The uniform bounds on
v_{p}^{\prime}
and
v_{p}
give

|\chi_{p}^{\prime\prime}(z)|\leq C.

Since we choose the normalization

\chi_{p}(0)=0,

and since

\chi_{p}(z+1)=\chi_{p}(z)+1,

the function
\chi_{p}(z)-z
is periodic. The bounds on
\chi_{p}^{\prime}
and
\chi_{p}^{\prime\prime}
imply

\|\chi_{p}-z\|_{C^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\leq C.

Combining the estimates near
p=0
with the standard compactness argument on

K\cap\{|p|\geq\delta_{0}\},

we obtain constants

0<m_{K}\leq M_{K}<\infty,

C_{K}<\infty,

such that

m_{K}\leq\chi_{p}^{\prime}(z)\leq M_{K}

and

\|\chi_{p}-z\|_{C^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\leq C_{K}

for all
p\in K
and
z\in\mathbb{R}
.
∎

2.3
Homogenization for the unperturbed case

Before proving the homogenization theorem, we recall Lemma
C.1
. This phase-shift lemma will also be used in the perturbative case, and its underlying idea goes back to
[
13
]
. In the unperturbed case, the test function
\Phi^{\epsilon}_{s}
is independent of the fast spatial variable
y
.

We now prove the homogenization theorem for
H(y,s,p)=F(s,p)
.

Theorem 4
.

Assume that
F(s,p)
satisfies (F1)-(F4). Consider

\begin{cases}u_{t}^{\epsilon}+F\left(\dfrac{u^{\epsilon}}{\epsilon},Du^{\epsilon}\right)=\epsilon\Delta u^{\epsilon}&\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,\infty),\\[5.69054pt]
u^{\epsilon}(x,0)=u_{0}(x)&\text{on }\mathbb{R}^{N}.\end{cases}

(2.11)

Suppose that

u_{0}\in BUC(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

and
F
satisfies (F1)–(F4). Then

u^{\epsilon}\to u

locally uniformly, where
u
is the viscosity solution of

\begin{cases}u_{t}-c(Du)=0&\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,\infty),\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)&\text{on }\mathbb{R}^{N}.\end{cases}

(2.12)

Proof.

By Lemma
B.1
, the family
\{u^{\epsilon}\}
is locally uniformly bounded on finite time intervals, and the half-relaxed limits satisfy the initial condition.
Define

u^{*}:={\limsup}_{\epsilon\to 0}^{*}u^{\epsilon},\qquad u_{*}:={\liminf}_{\epsilon\to 0,*}u^{\epsilon}.

It suffices to show that
u^{*}
is a viscosity subsolution. The proof that
u_{*}
is the viscosity supersolution is similar.

Pick

\phi\in C^{2}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N}),

and suppose that
u^{*}-\phi
attains its strict maximum at
(x_{0},t_{0})
, where
t_{0}>0
. Denote

a:=\phi_{t}(x_{0},t_{0}),

p:=D\phi(x_{0},t_{0}).

We only need to show

a\leq c(p).

Pick the corrector
\chi_{p}
. Consider the perturbed test function

\Phi_{s}^{\epsilon}(t,x)=\epsilon\chi_{p}\left(\frac{\phi(t,x)}{\epsilon}+s\right).

Then

\Phi_{s+1}^{\epsilon}=\Phi_{s}^{\epsilon}+\epsilon.

By Lemma
C.1
, applied to

\chi(z)=\chi_{p}(z),

there exist
s_{\epsilon}
and maximum points
(x_{\epsilon},t_{\epsilon})\to(x_{0},t_{0})
such that
M_{\epsilon}(s_{\epsilon})=0,

and
\frac{u^{\epsilon}(x_{\epsilon},t_{\epsilon})}{\epsilon}=\chi_{p}\left(\frac{\phi(x_{\epsilon},t_{\epsilon})}{\epsilon}+s_{\epsilon}\right).

Let

z_{\epsilon}:=\frac{\phi(x_{\epsilon},t_{\epsilon})}{\epsilon}+s_{\epsilon},

z:=\frac{\phi(x,t)}{\epsilon}+s_{\epsilon}.

Then

\frac{u^{\epsilon}(x_{\epsilon},t_{\epsilon})}{\epsilon}=\frac{\Phi_{s_{\epsilon}}^{\epsilon}(x_{\epsilon},t_{\epsilon})}{\epsilon}=\chi_{p}(z_{\epsilon}).

Moreover,

(\Phi_{s_{\epsilon}}^{\epsilon})_{t}=\chi_{p}^{\prime}(z)\phi_{t},

D\Phi_{s_{\epsilon}}^{\epsilon}=\chi_{p}^{\prime}(z)D\phi,

and

\epsilon\Delta\Phi_{s_{\epsilon}}^{\epsilon}=\chi_{p}^{\prime\prime}(z)|D\phi|^{2}+\epsilon\chi_{p}^{\prime}(z)\Delta\phi.

Suppose that
u^{\epsilon}-\Phi_{s_{\epsilon}}^{\epsilon}
attains its maximum at
(x_{\epsilon},t_{\epsilon})
. The viscosity subsolution inequality gives

0\geq(\Phi_{s_{\epsilon}}^{\epsilon})_{t}+F\left(\chi_{p}(z_{\epsilon}),D\Phi_{s_{\epsilon}}^{\epsilon}\right)-\epsilon\Delta\Phi_{s_{\epsilon}}^{\epsilon}.

By the cell ODE,

\displaystyle 0\geq\;

\displaystyle\chi_{p}^{\prime}(z_{\epsilon})\bigl(\phi_{t}-c(p)\bigr)

\displaystyle+\left[F\left(\chi_{p}(z_{\epsilon}),\chi_{p}^{\prime}(z_{\epsilon})D\phi\right)-F\left(\chi_{p}(z_{\epsilon}),\chi_{p}^{\prime}(z_{\epsilon})p\right)\right]

\displaystyle-\chi_{p}^{\prime\prime}(z_{\epsilon})\left(|D\phi|^{2}-|p|^{2}\right)-\epsilon\chi_{p}^{\prime}(z_{\epsilon})\Delta\phi.

Letting
\epsilon\to 0
, we have

D\phi\to p,

\phi_{t}\to a.

Therefore

\liminf_{\epsilon\to 0}\chi_{p}^{\prime}(z_{\epsilon})(a-c(p))\leq 0.

Because

\inf_{z}\chi_{p}^{\prime}(z)>0,

we obtain

a\leq c(p).

Similarly,
u_{*}
is the viscosity supersolution. With the comparison principle,

u^{*}\leq u_{*}.

Since

u_{*}\leq u^{*},

we conclude that

u:=u^{*}=u_{*}

is the viscosity solution of the effective equation. This proves the local uniform convergence.
∎

The proof is based on Evans’ perturbed test function method
[
10
]
.

3
Homogenization for the perturbative case

We now introduce a spatially oscillatory perturbation of the unperturbed case.

Consider

H(y,s,p)=F(s,p)+\eta W(y,s,p),

(3.1)

where
\eta
will be chosen sufficiently small on the relevant compact set of gradients. We assume that
F
satisfies (F1)–(F4),
that

W\in C_{\mathrm{loc}}^{3,\alpha}(\mathbb{T}^{N}\times\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}),

and that there exists
L>0
such that

|W(x,r,p)-W(y,s,q)|\leq L\big((1+|p|+|q|)|x-y|+|r-s|+|p-q|\big)

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
r,s\in\mathbb{R}
, and
p,q\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

We write

\chi_{p}^{\eta}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}

for the corrector associated with

H(y,s,p)=F(s,p)+\eta W(y,s,p).

The corresponding cell equation is

\displaystyle c_{\eta}(p)(\chi_{p}^{\eta})_{z}

\displaystyle+F\left(\chi_{p}^{\eta},D_{y}\chi_{p}^{\eta}+p(\chi_{p}^{\eta})_{z}\right)

(3.2)

\displaystyle+\eta W\left(y,\chi_{p}^{\eta},D_{y}\chi_{p}^{\eta}+p(\chi_{p}^{\eta})_{z}\right)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{y}\chi_{p}^{\eta}+2p\cdot D^{2}_{yz}\chi_{p}^{\eta}+|p|^{2}(\chi_{p}^{\eta})_{zz}.

Set

w_{p}^{\eta}(y,z):=\chi_{p}^{\eta}(y,z)-z.

We seek a corrector of the form
\chi_{p}^{\eta}(y,z)=z+w_{p}^{\eta}(y,z)
with
w_{p}^{\eta}
periodic in
(y,z)
. This motivates the following definition.

Definition 3.1
.

We say that
\chi_{p}^{\eta}
is a corrector associated with
p
if

\chi_{p}^{\eta}(y,z)=z+w_{p}^{\eta}(y,z),\qquad w_{p}^{\eta}\in C^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{N+1}),

and there exists a constant
c_{\eta}(p)\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\chi_{p}^{\eta}(y,z+1)=\chi_{p}^{\eta}(y,z)+1,\qquad(\chi_{p}^{\eta})_{z}(y,z)>0,\\[5.69054pt]
&c_{\eta}(p)(\chi_{p}^{\eta})_{z}+F\left(\chi_{p}^{\eta},D_{y}\chi_{p}^{\eta}+p(\chi_{p}^{\eta})_{z}\right)\\
&\quad+\eta W\left(y,\chi_{p}^{\eta},D_{y}\chi_{p}^{\eta}+p(\chi_{p}^{\eta})_{z}\right)\\
&\quad=\Delta_{y}\chi_{p}^{\eta}+2p\cdot D^{2}_{yz}\chi_{p}^{\eta}+|p|^{2}(\chi_{p}^{\eta})_{zz}.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.3)

The constant
c_{\eta}(p)
is called the cell speed associated with
p
.

Let
\alpha_{0}\in(0,1)
denote the Hölder exponent in the regularity assumptions on
F
and
W
. Throughout this section, we fix an exponent
\alpha\in(0,\alpha_{0})
.

3.1
Construction of a corrector

Let

w_{p}^{0}(z):=\chi_{p}^{0}(z)-z,\qquad w(y,z):=w_{p}^{0}(z)+\psi(y,z),\qquad c:=c_{0}(p)+\kappa.

We define the nonlinear operator

\mathcal{N}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\times C^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{T}^{N+1})\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\to C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{T}^{N+1})

by

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(p,\psi,\kappa,\eta):=

\displaystyle(c_{0}+\kappa)(1+w_{z})+F\left(z+w,D_{y}w+p(1+w_{z})\right)

\displaystyle+\eta W\left(y,z+w,D_{y}w+p(1+w_{z})\right)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{y}w-2p\cdot D^{2}_{yz}w-|p|^{2}w_{zz}.

We require

\mathcal{N}(p,\psi,\kappa,\eta)=0,\qquad\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N+1}}\psi=0.

Clearly,

\mathcal{N}(p,0,0,0)=0.

Denote

a_{p}(z):=(\chi_{p}^{0})^{\prime}(z)=1+(w_{p}^{0})^{\prime}(z)>0.

We linearize
\mathcal{N}
at
(\psi,\kappa,\eta)=(0,0,0)
in the variables
(\psi,\kappa)
.

For
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(p,\theta\psi,\theta\kappa,0)=

\displaystyle\;c_{0}a_{p}+\theta(c_{0}\psi_{z}+\kappa a_{p})+O(\theta^{2})+F(\chi_{p}^{0},pa_{p})

\displaystyle+\theta\left[F_{s}(\chi_{p}^{0},pa_{p})\psi+D_{p}F(\chi_{p}^{0},pa_{p})\cdot(D_{y}\psi+p\psi_{z})\right]+O(\theta^{2})

\displaystyle-\theta\left(\Delta_{y}\psi+2p\cdot D^{2}_{yz}\psi+|p|^{2}\psi_{zz}\right)-|p|^{2}(w_{p}^{0})^{\prime\prime}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d}{d\theta}\mathcal{N}(p,\theta\psi,\theta\kappa,0)\right|_{\theta=0}=

\displaystyle c_{0}(p)\psi_{z}+F_{s}(\chi_{p}^{0},pa_{p})\psi

\displaystyle+D_{p}F(\chi_{p}^{0},pa_{p})\cdot(D_{y}\psi+p\psi_{z})

\displaystyle-\Delta_{y}\psi-2p\cdot D^{2}_{yz}\psi-|p|^{2}\psi_{zz}+\kappa a_{p}

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle L_{p}\psi+\kappa a_{p},

where
L_{p}
is a linear operator.
Whenever necessary, we decrease the fixed Hölder exponent
\alpha
without changing the notation.

For a function
\psi=\psi(y,z)
, we introduce the change of variables

r=z-p\cdot y,\qquad\widetilde{\psi}(y,r):=\psi(y,p\cdot y+r).

Then

\psi_{z}=\widetilde{\psi}_{r},

D_{y}\psi+p\psi_{z}=D_{y}\widetilde{\psi},

and

\Delta_{y}\psi+2p\cdot D^{2}_{yz}\psi+|p|^{2}\psi_{zz}=\Delta_{y}\widetilde{\psi}.

Therefore, in the variables
(y,r)
, the linearized operator
L_{p}
becomes the periodic-parabolic operator

\widetilde{L}_{p}\widetilde{\psi}=c_{0}(p)\widetilde{\psi}_{r}+F_{s}(\chi_{p}^{0},pa_{p})\widetilde{\psi}+D_{p}F(\chi_{p}^{0},pa_{p})\cdot D_{y}\widetilde{\psi}-\Delta_{y}\widetilde{\psi},

(3.4)

where

a_{p}=(\chi_{p}^{0})^{\prime}.

Here
\chi_{p}^{0}
and
a_{p}
are evaluated at
p\cdot y+r
.

For fixed
p
, we define the skew torus
\mathbb{T}_{p}^{N+1}
by the identifications

(y,r)\sim(y+k,r-p\cdot k),\qquad(y,r)\sim(y,r+\ell),\qquad k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N},\ \ell\in\mathbb{Z}.

Equivalently, this is the usual periodicity of
\psi(y,z)
in the variables
(y,z)
.

We say that

\widetilde{\psi}\in C_{\rm per}^{2+\alpha,1+\alpha/2}(\mathbb{T}_{p}^{N+1})

if
\widetilde{\psi}
satisfies the above skew-periodicity and

\widetilde{\psi},\quad D_{y}\widetilde{\psi},\quad D_{y}^{2}\widetilde{\psi},\quad\widetilde{\psi}_{r}

are continuous and skew-periodic, with
D_{y}^{2}\widetilde{\psi}
and
\widetilde{\psi}_{r}
Hölder continuous with respect to the parabolic distance

d_{\rm par}\bigl((y,r),(y^{\prime},r^{\prime})\bigr)=|y-y^{\prime}|+|r-r^{\prime}|^{1/2}.

The corresponding norm is

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\psi}\|_{C^{2+\alpha,1+\alpha/2}}:=

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\psi}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|D_{y}\widetilde{\psi}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|D_{y}^{2}\widetilde{\psi}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\widetilde{\psi}_{r}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle+[D_{y}^{2}\widetilde{\psi}]_{\alpha,\alpha/2}+[\widetilde{\psi}_{r}]_{\alpha,\alpha/2}.

Here

[f]_{\alpha,\alpha/2}:=\sup_{(y,r)\neq(y^{\prime},r^{\prime})}\frac{|f(y,r)-f(y^{\prime},r^{\prime})|}{|y-y^{\prime}|^{\alpha}+|r-r^{\prime}|^{\alpha/2}}.

We also define

\mathcal{X}_{p}:=\left\{\widetilde{\psi}\in C_{\rm per}^{2+\alpha,1+\alpha/2}(\mathbb{T}_{p}^{N+1}):\int_{\mathbb{T}_{p}^{N+1}}\widetilde{\psi}=0\right\}\times\mathbb{R}

and

\mathcal{Y}_{p}:=C_{\rm per}^{\alpha,\alpha/2}(\mathbb{T}_{p}^{N+1}).

We define
\widetilde{A}_{p}:\mathcal{X}_{p}\to\mathcal{Y}_{p}

by

\widetilde{A}_{p}(\widetilde{\psi},\kappa)=\widetilde{L}_{p}\widetilde{\psi}+\kappa a_{p}.

Before proving invertibility, we first record a lemma for parabolic equations on skew tori. For the periodic-parabolic Schauder estimates and Fredholm alternative used in the construction of the correctors, we refer to Lieberman
[
15
]
, Ladyzhenskaya–Solonnikov–Ural’tseva
[
14
]
, and Hess
[
12
]
.

Lemma 3.1

(
Uniform periodic-parabolic estimates on skew tori
)
.

Let
K\Subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
. For each
p\in K
, let
\mathbb{T}_{p}^{N+1}
be the skew torus defined by the identifications

(y,r)\sim(y+k,r-p\cdot k),\qquad(y,r)\sim(y,r+\ell),\qquad k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N},\ \ell\in\mathbb{Z}.

Consider the linear periodic-parabolic operator

\mathcal{P}_{p}u=c_{p}u_{r}+b_{p}(y,r)\cdot D_{y}u+d_{p}(y,r)u-\Delta_{y}u

on
\mathbb{T}_{p}^{N+1}
. Assume that
|c_{p}|\geq c_{K}>0

for all
p\in K
, and that

\|b_{p}\|_{C^{\alpha,\alpha/2}(\mathbb{T}_{p}^{N+1})}+\|d_{p}\|_{C^{\alpha,\alpha/2}(\mathbb{T}_{p}^{N+1})}\leq C_{K}

uniformly for
p\in K
. Then there exists a constant
C_{K}>0
, independent of
p\in K
, such that every skew-periodic solution
u
of

\mathcal{P}_{p}u=f

satisfies

\|u\|_{C^{2+\alpha,1+\alpha/2}(\mathbb{T}_{p}^{N+1})}\leq C_{K}\left(\|f\|_{C^{\alpha,\alpha/2}(\mathbb{T}_{p}^{N+1})}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}_{p}^{N+1})}\right).

Moreover, the periodic problem for
\mathcal{P}_{p}
satisfies the Fredholm alternative with index
0
.

Proof.

We regard functions on
\mathbb{T}_{p}^{N+1}
as functions on
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}
satisfying the skew-periodicity

u(y+k,r-p\cdot k)=u(y,r),\qquad u(y,r+1)=u(y,r).

The local parabolic Schauder estimate for

c_{p}u_{r}+b_{p}\cdot D_{y}u+d_{p}u-\Delta_{y}u=f

is applied on ordinary parabolic cylinders in the variables
(y,r)
. Its constant depends only on the lower bound of
|c_{p}|
, the Hölder norms of
b_{p},d_{p}
, the Hölder exponent, and the dimension. By the assumptions, these quantities are uniformly bounded for
p\in K
.

It remains to explain why the local estimates can be patched together with constants independent of
p
. The skew-periodicity is generated by the translations

(y,r)\mapsto(y+e_{i},r-p_{i}),\qquad i=1,\ldots,N,

and

(y,r)\mapsto(y,r+1).

These transition maps are translations in the variables
(y,r)
; in particular, they do not mix the parabolic time variable
r
with the spatial derivatives in the local equation. Hence the local parabolic Schauder estimates are invariant under these transitions.

Since
p
ranges in the compact set
K
, the fundamental parallelepipeds associated with the lattice generated by

(e_{i},-p_{i}),

i=1,\ldots,N,

and

(0,1)

have uniformly bounded diameter and fixed volume. Therefore one can cover each fundamental domain by a number of parabolic cylinders bounded only in terms of
K
. Using the skew-periodicity, the local estimates on this finite covering give the global estimate on
\mathbb{T}_{p}^{N+1}
with a constant independent of
p\in K
.

The Fredholm property follows from the usual periodic-parabolic theory. Indeed, the solution operator over one period in the time variable
r
is compact on Hölder spaces by parabolic smoothing. Therefore the periodic solvability problem is equivalent to an equation of the form
I-P_{p}
with
P_{p}
compact. Hence
\mathcal{P}_{p}
is Fredholm of index
0
, and the standard Fredholm alternative holds.
∎

Proposition 3.1
.

For every fixed
p\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, the operator

\widetilde{A}_{p}:(\widetilde{\psi},\kappa)\longmapsto\widetilde{L}_{p}\widetilde{\psi}+\kappa a_{p}

is an isomorphism from
\mathcal{X}_{p}
to
\mathcal{Y}_{p}
.

Proof.

First, differentiating the unperturbed cell ODE

|p|^{2}(\chi_{p}^{0})^{\prime\prime}=c_{0}(p)(\chi_{p}^{0})^{\prime}+F(\chi_{p}^{0},p(\chi_{p}^{0})^{\prime})

with respect to
z
, we obtain
L_{p}a_{p}=0.

Equivalently,
\widetilde{L}_{p}a_{p}=0.

We now prove that

\ker\widetilde{L}_{p}=\operatorname{span}\{a_{p}\}.

Let
\widetilde{L}_{p}\widetilde{\psi}=0
and write

\widetilde{\psi}=a_{p}h.

Using
\widetilde{L}_{p}a_{p}=0
, a direct computation gives

c_{0}(p)h_{r}+\widetilde{b}_{p}(y,r)\cdot D_{y}h-\Delta_{y}h=0

on
\mathbb{T}_{p}^{N+1}
, where
\widetilde{b}_{p}
is a bounded Hölder continuous vector field. Since
c_{0}(p)\neq 0
, this is a linear periodic -parabolic equation with no zero-order term. By the strong maximum principle, every periodic solution is constant. Hence

\widetilde{\psi}=Ca_{p}.

Therefore

\ker\widetilde{L}_{p}=\operatorname{span}\{a_{p}\}.

By Lemma
3.1
, applied with

\mathcal{P}_{p}=\widetilde{L}_{p},

the operator

\widetilde{L}_{p}:C_{\rm per}^{2+\alpha,1+\alpha/2}(\mathbb{T}_{p}^{N+1})\to C_{\rm per}^{\alpha,\alpha/2}(\mathbb{T}_{p}^{N+1})

is Fredholm of index
0
. Let
\rho_{p}^{*}>0
be the positive normalized generator of the adjoint kernel:

\widetilde{L}_{p}^{*}\rho_{p}^{*}=0,\qquad\int_{\mathbb{T}_{p}^{N+1}}\rho_{p}^{*}=1.

Then

\widetilde{L}_{p}\widetilde{\psi}=f

is solvable if and only if
\int_{\mathbb{T}_{p}^{N+1}}f\rho_{p}^{*}=0.

Therefore

\widetilde{L}_{p}\widetilde{\psi}+\kappa a_{p}=f

is solvable if and only if

\int_{\mathbb{T}_{p}^{N+1}}(f-\kappa a_{p})\rho_{p}^{*}=0.

Thus the unique admissible value of
\kappa
is

\kappa=\frac{\int_{\mathbb{T}_{p}^{N+1}}f\rho_{p}^{*}}{\int_{\mathbb{T}_{p}^{N+1}}a_{p}\rho_{p}^{*}}.

The denominator is strictly positive because
a_{p}>0
and
\rho_{p}^{*}>0
.

For this value of
\kappa
, the equation for
\widetilde{\psi}
is solvable. The solution is unique after imposing the normalization

\int_{\mathbb{T}_{p}^{N+1}}\widetilde{\psi}=0.

Indeed, the kernel is exactly
\operatorname{span}\{a_{p}\}
and

\int_{\mathbb{T}_{p}^{N+1}}a_{p}>0.

Hence
\widetilde{A}_{p}
is an isomorphism.
∎

We next prove the required uniform invertibility.

Proposition 3.2

(
Uniform invertibility on compact sets
)
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a compact set. There exists
B_{K}>0
such that, for every
p\in K
and every
f\in\mathcal{Y}_{p}
, the equation

\widetilde{L}_{p}\widetilde{\psi}+\kappa a_{p}=f,\qquad\int_{\mathbb{T}_{p}^{N+1}}\widetilde{\psi}=0,

has a unique solution
(\widetilde{\psi},\kappa)\in\mathcal{X}_{p}
, and

\|\widetilde{\psi}\|_{C^{2+\alpha,1+\alpha/2}(\mathbb{T}_{p}^{N+1})}+|\kappa|\leq B_{K}\|f\|_{C^{\alpha,\alpha/2}(\mathbb{T}_{p}^{N+1})}.

Proof.

For each fixed
p
, the existence and uniqueness follow from Proposition
3.1
. We prove that the inverse estimates are uniform for
p\in K
.

Suppose by contradiction that the estimate is false. Then there exist

p_{n}\in K,

f_{n}\in\mathcal{Y}_{p_{n}},

and solutions

(\widetilde{\psi}_{n},\kappa_{n})\in\mathcal{X}_{p_{n}}

of

\widetilde{L}_{p_{n}}\widetilde{\psi}_{n}+\kappa_{n}a_{p_{n}}=f_{n},\qquad\int_{\mathbb{T}_{p_{n}}^{N+1}}\widetilde{\psi}_{n}=0

such that

\|\widetilde{\psi}_{n}\|_{C^{2+\alpha,1+\alpha/2}(\mathbb{T}_{p_{n}}^{N+1})}+|\kappa_{n}|=1,

while

\|f_{n}\|_{C^{\alpha,\alpha/2}(\mathbb{T}_{p_{n}}^{N+1})}\to 0.

By taking a subsequence, we may assume that

p_{n}\to p_{\infty}\in K

and

\kappa_{n}\to\kappa_{\infty},
as
n\to\infty
.

We view each
\widetilde{\psi}_{n}
as a function on
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
satisfying the skew-periodicity

\widetilde{\psi}_{n}(y+k,r-p_{n}\cdot k)=\widetilde{\psi}_{n}(y,r),\qquad\widetilde{\psi}_{n}(y,r+1)=\widetilde{\psi}_{n}(y,r).

By the normalization

\|\widetilde{\psi}_{n}\|_{C^{2+\alpha,1+\alpha/2}(\mathbb{T}_{p_{n}}^{N+1})}+|\kappa_{n}|=1,

the functions
\widetilde{\psi}_{n}
are uniformly bounded in

C_{\rm loc}^{2+\alpha,1+\alpha/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}).

Therefore, by the compact embedding of parabolic Hölder spaces, after passing to a further subsequence, there exists a function
\widetilde{\psi}_{\infty}
such that

\widetilde{\psi}_{n}\to\widetilde{\psi}_{\infty}

locally in
C^{2+\beta,1+\beta/2}
for every
0<\beta<\alpha
as
n\to\infty
.

Since

p_{n}\to p_{\infty},

the skew-periodicity passes to the limit. Thus, for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
,

\widetilde{\psi}_{\infty}(y+k,r-p_{\infty}\cdot k)=\widetilde{\psi}_{\infty}(y,r),\qquad\widetilde{\psi}_{\infty}(y,r+1)=\widetilde{\psi}_{\infty}(y,r).

Hence
\widetilde{\psi}_{\infty}
descends to a function on
\mathbb{T}_{p_{\infty}}^{N+1}
.

The normalization also passes to the limit. Indeed, using the parametrization

(y,r)=(\theta,\sigma-p_{n}\cdot\theta),\qquad(\theta,\sigma)\in[0,1]^{N}\times[0,1],

of a fundamental domain of
\mathbb{T}_{p_{n}}^{N+1}
, we have

0=\int_{\mathbb{T}_{p_{n}}^{N+1}}\widetilde{\psi}_{n}=\int_{[0,1]^{N+1}}\widetilde{\psi}_{n}(\theta,\sigma-p_{n}\cdot\theta)\,d\theta d\sigma.

Letting
n\to\infty
, we get

\int_{\mathbb{T}_{p_{\infty}}^{N+1}}\widetilde{\psi}_{\infty}=0.

We now pass to the limit in the equation. Since
p_{n}\to p_{\infty}
and the unperturbed correctors depend continuously on
p
, the coefficients of
\widetilde{L}_{p_{n}}
converge locally uniformly to those of
\widetilde{L}_{p_{\infty}}
. Since

f_{n}\to 0

and

\widetilde{\psi}_{n}\to\widetilde{\psi}_{\infty}\quad\text{locally in }C^{2+\beta,1+\beta/2},

we obtain

\widetilde{L}_{p_{\infty}}\widetilde{\psi}_{\infty}+\kappa_{\infty}a_{p_{\infty}}=0,\qquad\int_{\mathbb{T}_{p_{\infty}}^{N+1}}\widetilde{\psi}_{\infty}=0.

By Proposition
3.1
applied to
p_{\infty}
, we conclude that
\widetilde{\psi}_{\infty}=0,

\kappa_{\infty}=0.

We next show that this convergence is strong in the full parabolic Hölder norm. From the local uniform convergence and the skew-periodicity, we have

\|\widetilde{\psi}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}_{p_{n}}^{N+1})}\to 0.

Indeed, using the same parametrization of the fundamental domains,

(y,r)=(\theta,\sigma-p_{n}\cdot\theta),

this follows from the uniform convergence of

\widetilde{\psi}_{n}(\theta,\sigma-p_{n}\cdot\theta)

to

\widetilde{\psi}_{\infty}(\theta,\sigma-p_{\infty}\cdot\theta)=0

on the compact set
[0,1]^{N+1}
.

Applying Lemma
3.1
to

\widetilde{L}_{p_{n}}\widetilde{\psi}_{n}=f_{n}-\kappa_{n}a_{p_{n}},

we obtain

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\psi}_{n}\|_{C^{2+\alpha,1+\alpha/2}(\mathbb{T}_{p_{n}}^{N+1})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{K}\left(\|f_{n}-\kappa_{n}a_{p_{n}}\|_{C^{\alpha,\alpha/2}(\mathbb{T}_{p_{n}}^{N+1})}+\|\widetilde{\psi}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}_{p_{n}}^{N+1})}\right)

(3.5)

\displaystyle\leq C_{K}\left(\|f_{n}\|_{C^{\alpha,\alpha/2}(\mathbb{T}_{p_{n}}^{N+1})}+|\kappa_{n}|+\|\widetilde{\psi}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}_{p_{n}}^{N+1})}\right).

The right-hand side tends to
0
. Hence

\|\widetilde{\psi}_{n}\|_{C^{2+\alpha,1+\alpha/2}(\mathbb{T}_{p_{n}}^{N+1})}+|\kappa_{n}|\to 0,

which contradicts

\|\widetilde{\psi}_{n}\|_{C^{2+\alpha,1+\alpha/2}(\mathbb{T}_{p_{n}}^{N+1})}+|\kappa_{n}|=1.

Therefore, the desired uniform estimate holds.
∎

We now solve the nonlinear cell problem by a fixed point argument. For

\widetilde{\psi}\in\mathcal{X}_{p},

we write

\psi(y,z):=\widetilde{\psi}(y,z-p\cdot y).

Equivalently,

\widetilde{\psi}(y,r)=\psi(y,p\cdot y+r).

We define

\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}(p,\widetilde{\psi},\kappa,\eta)

to be the expression of

\mathcal{N}(p,\psi,\kappa,\eta)

in the variables
(y,r)
. Then

\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}(p,0,0,0)=0,

and

D_{(\widetilde{\psi},\kappa)}\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}(p,0,0,0)(\widetilde{\psi},\kappa)=\widetilde{A}_{p}(\widetilde{\psi},\kappa).

Set

Z:=(\widetilde{\psi},\kappa).

Taylor’s formula gives

\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}(p,Z,\eta)=\widetilde{A}_{p}Z+\eta b_{p}+\widetilde{R}_{p}(Z,\eta),

where
b_{p}(y,r)=W\left(y,\chi_{p}^{0}(p\cdot y+r),p\,a_{p}(p\cdot y+r)\right).

Here

a_{p}=(\chi_{p}^{0})^{\prime}.

For every compact set
K\Subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, define

M_{K}:=\sup_{p\in K}\|b_{p}\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{p}}.

Then
M_{K}<\infty
.

Moreover, since
F
and
W
are
C^{3,\alpha}
on the relevant compact set, there exist constants

r_{K}>0,

C_{K}^{(R)}>0

such that, whenever

\|Z_{i}\|_{\mathcal{X}_{p}}\leq r_{K},\qquad|\eta|\leq 1,\qquad i=1,2,

we have

\|\widetilde{R}_{p}(Z_{1},\eta)-\widetilde{R}_{p}(Z_{2},\eta)\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{p}}\leq C_{K}^{(R)}\left(\|Z_{1}\|_{\mathcal{X}_{p}}+\|Z_{2}\|_{\mathcal{X}_{p}}+|\eta|\right)\|Z_{1}-Z_{2}\|_{\mathcal{X}_{p}}.

In particular,

\|\widetilde{R}_{p}(Z,\eta)\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{p}}\leq C_{K}^{(R)}\left(\|Z\|_{\mathcal{X}_{p}}^{2}+|\eta|\|Z\|_{\mathcal{X}_{p}}\right).

The fixed point argument yields the following local solution branch for the nonlinear cell problem.

Proposition 3.3
.

For every compact set
K\Subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, there exist constants

\eta_{K}>0,

C_{K}>0

such that, for all
p\in K
and
|\eta|\leq\eta_{K}
, there exists a unique small solution

Z_{p}^{\eta}=(\widetilde{\psi}_{p}^{\eta},\kappa_{p}^{\eta})\in\mathcal{X}_{p}

of

\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}(p,Z_{p}^{\eta},\eta)=0.

Moreover,

\|\widetilde{\psi}_{p}^{\eta}\|_{C^{2+\alpha,1+\alpha/2}}+|\kappa_{p}^{\eta}|\leq C_{K}|\eta|.

Proof.

The equation

\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}(p,Z,\eta)=0

is equivalent to
\widetilde{A}_{p}Z=-\eta b_{p}-\widetilde{R}_{p}(Z,\eta).

Therefore,

Z=-\widetilde{A}_{p}^{-1}\left[\eta b_{p}+\widetilde{R}_{p}(Z,\eta)\right].

Define

\mathcal{T}_{p}(Z):=-\widetilde{A}_{p}^{-1}\left[\eta b_{p}+\widetilde{R}_{p}(Z,\eta)\right].

By the previous proposition,

\|\mathcal{T}_{p}(Z)\|_{\mathcal{X}_{p}}\leq B_{K}\left[M_{K}|\eta|+C_{K}^{(R)}\left(\|Z\|_{\mathcal{X}_{p}}^{2}+|\eta|\|Z\|_{\mathcal{X}_{p}}\right)\right].

Set

R_{0}:=2B_{K}M_{K}.

For
|\eta|>0
, define

\mathcal{B}_{\eta}^{p}:=\left\{Z\in\mathcal{X}_{p}:\|Z\|_{\mathcal{X}_{p}}\leq R_{0}|\eta|\right\}.

We choose
\eta_{K}>0
small enough such that
R_{0}\eta_{K}\leq r_{K},

and
B_{K}C_{K}^{(R)}(R_{0}^{2}+R_{0})\eta_{K}\leq B_{K}M_{K}.

Then, if
Z\in\mathcal{B}_{\eta}^{p}
and
|\eta|\leq\eta_{K}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}_{p}(Z)\|_{\mathcal{X}_{p}}

\displaystyle\leq B_{K}M_{K}|\eta|+B_{K}C_{K}^{(R)}\left(R_{0}^{2}|\eta|^{2}+R_{0}|\eta|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2B_{K}M_{K}|\eta|

\displaystyle=R_{0}|\eta|.

Thus
\mathcal{T}_{p}
maps
\mathcal{B}_{\eta}^{p}
into itself.

Next, for
Z_{1},Z_{2}\in\mathcal{B}_{\eta}^{p}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}_{p}(Z_{1})-\mathcal{T}_{p}(Z_{2})\|_{\mathcal{X}_{p}}

\displaystyle\leq B_{K}\|\widetilde{R}_{p}(Z_{1},\eta)-\widetilde{R}_{p}(Z_{2},\eta)\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{p}}

\displaystyle\leq B_{K}C_{K}^{(R)}\left(\|Z_{1}\|_{\mathcal{X}_{p}}+\|Z_{2}\|_{\mathcal{X}_{p}}+|\eta|\right)\|Z_{1}-Z_{2}\|_{\mathcal{X}_{p}}

\displaystyle\leq B_{K}C_{K}^{(R)}(2R_{0}|\eta|+|\eta|)\|Z_{1}-Z_{2}\|_{\mathcal{X}_{p}}.

We choose
\eta_{K}
smaller if necessary such that

B_{K}C_{K}^{(R)}(2R_{0}+1)\eta_{K}\leq\frac{1}{2}.

Then
\mathcal{T}_{p}
is a contraction on
\mathcal{B}_{\eta}^{p}
.

By the contraction mapping theorem, there exists a unique fixed point

Z_{p}^{\eta}=(\widetilde{\psi}_{p}^{\eta},\kappa_{p}^{\eta})\in\mathcal{B}_{\eta}^{p}.

Therefore

\|\widetilde{\psi}_{p}^{\eta}\|_{C^{2+\alpha,1+\alpha/2}}+|\kappa_{p}^{\eta}|\leq R_{0}|\eta|.

This proves the proposition.
∎

Returning to the original variables, we set

\psi_{p}^{\eta}(y,z):=\widetilde{\psi}_{p}^{\eta}(y,z-p\cdot y).

Then define

w_{p}^{\eta}:=w_{p}^{0}+\psi_{p}^{\eta},\qquad c_{\eta}(p):=c_{0}(p)+\kappa_{p}^{\eta},\qquad\chi_{p}^{\eta}(y,z):=z+w_{p}^{\eta}(y,z).

We next derive the corrector estimates needed in the homogenization argument.

Lemma 3.2

(
Higher regularity of the corrector
)
.

Let
K\Subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Then there exists
C_{K}>0
such that

\|\chi_{p}^{\eta}-z\|_{C^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{N+1})}\leq C_{K}

for all
p\in K
and
|\eta|\leq\eta_{K}
.

Proof.

We write the corrector in the variables

r=z-p\cdot y,

\Xi(y,r):=\chi_{p}^{\eta}(y,p\cdot y+r).

Then

\chi_{p}^{\eta}(y,z)=\Xi(y,z-p\cdot y).

In the variables
(y,r)
, the cell equation becomes

c_{\eta}(p)\Xi_{r}+F(\Xi,D_{y}\Xi)+\eta W(y,\Xi,D_{y}\Xi)=\Delta_{y}\Xi.

By the construction of the corrector, we already know that

\Xi-\Xi^{0}\in C_{\rm per}^{2+\alpha,1+\alpha/2}(\mathbb{T}_{p}^{N+1}).

In particular,

\Xi,D_{y}\Xi,D_{y}^{2}\Xi,\Xi_{r}

are bounded.

We now differentiate the equation with respect to
r
. Set

U:=\Xi_{r}.

Then
U
satisfies the linear periodic-parabolic equation

c_{\eta}(p)U_{r}+\left[F_{s}(\Xi,D_{y}\Xi)+\eta W_{s}(y,\Xi,D_{y}\Xi)\right]U+\left[D_{p}F(\Xi,D_{y}\Xi)+\eta D_{p}W(y,\Xi,D_{y}\Xi)\right]\cdot D_{y}U=\Delta_{y}U.

The coefficients of this equation are uniformly Hölder continuous for
p\in K
and
|\eta|\leq\eta_{K}
. Moreover, since
c_{0}(p)<0
and
c_{\eta}(p)=c_{0}(p)+O(\eta)
, by choosing
\eta_{K}
smaller if necessary, we have

|c_{\eta}(p)|\geq c_{K}>0

for all
p\in K
. Hence this is a uniformly parabolic periodic equation with respect to the time variable
r
.

By the periodic-parabolic Schauder estimate applied to the equation for
U
, we obtain
U=\Xi_{r}\in C_{\rm per}^{2+\alpha,1+\alpha/2}(\mathbb{T}_{p}^{N+1}),

with a norm bounded by a constant depending only on
K
. Consequently,

U_{r}=\Xi_{rr}

is bounded, and

D_{y}U=D^{2}_{yr}\Xi

is bounded.

We now return to the variables
(y,z)
. Since

\chi_{p}^{\eta}(y,z)=\Xi(y,z-p\cdot y),

we have

(\chi_{p}^{\eta})_{z}=\Xi_{r},

D_{y}\chi_{p}^{\eta}=D_{y}\Xi-p\Xi_{r},

and

(\chi_{p}^{\eta})_{zz}=\Xi_{rr}.

Moreover,

D^{2}_{yz}\chi_{p}^{\eta}=D^{2}_{yr}\Xi-p\Xi_{rr},

and

D^{2}_{yy}\chi_{p}^{\eta}=D^{2}_{yy}\Xi-p\otimes D_{y}\Xi_{r}-D_{y}\Xi_{r}\otimes p+p\otimes p\,\Xi_{rr}.

All terms on the right-hand sides are bounded uniformly for
p\in K
, because
p
ranges in a compact set and we have already bounded

D_{y}^{2}\Xi,

D_{y}\Xi_{r},

\Xi_{rr}.

Therefore
\|\chi_{p}^{\eta}-z\|_{C^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{N+1})}\leq C_{K}.

∎

Remark 3.1
.

The proof of Lemma 3.2 actually gives a slightly stronger estimate. After decreasing the Hölder exponent if necessary, there exist

0<\beta<\frac{\alpha}{2}

and a constant
C_{K}>0
such that

\|\chi_{p}^{\eta}-z\|_{C^{2,\beta}(\mathbb{T}^{N+1})}\leq C_{K}

for all
p\in K
and
|\eta|\leq\eta_{K}
.

Indeed, after setting

r=z-p\cdot y,

\Xi(y,r)=\chi_{p}^{\eta}(y,p\cdot y+r),

one first applies the uniform parabolic Schauder estimate on the skew torus to the equation for

Z(y,r):=\Xi(y,r)-p\cdot y-r.

Then one applies the same estimate to the differentiated equation for

U:=\Xi_{r}.

This gives Hölder bounds for

D_{y}^{2}Z,

D_{y}Z_{r},

Z_{rr}

in the variables
(y,r)
. Since the change of variables

(y,z)\mapsto(y,z-p\cdot y)

is linear and
p
ranges in the compact set
K
, these estimates imply the stated uniform
C^{2,\beta}
estimate in the variables
(y,z)
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Assume that
K
is a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Then there exist constants

m_{K},\ M_{K},\ C_{K},\ \eta_{K}>0

such that

m_{K}\leq(\chi_{p}^{\eta})_{z}(y,z)\leq M_{K}

for
(y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}
, and

\|\chi_{p}^{\eta}-z\|_{C^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{N+1})}\leq C_{K}

for
|\eta|\leq\eta_{K}
and
p\in K
.

Proof.

We use the variables

r=z-p\cdot y,\qquad\Xi_{p}^{\eta}(y,r):=\chi_{p}^{\eta}(y,p\cdot y+r).

Similarly, set

\Xi_{p}^{0}(y,r):=\chi_{p}^{0}(p\cdot y+r).

By the construction in Proposition 3.3,

\Xi_{p}^{\eta}-\Xi_{p}^{0}=\widetilde{\psi}_{p}^{\eta},

and

\|\widetilde{\psi}_{p}^{\eta}\|_{C_{\rm per}^{2+\alpha,1+\alpha/2}(\mathbb{T}_{p}^{N+1})}+|\kappa_{p}^{\eta}|\leq C_{K}|\eta|.

In particular,

\|(\widetilde{\psi}_{p}^{\eta})_{r}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{K}|\eta|.

Since

(\chi_{p}^{\eta})_{z}(y,z)=(\Xi_{p}^{\eta})_{r}(y,z-p\cdot y),

and

(\Xi_{p}^{0})_{r}(y,r)=(\chi_{p}^{0})^{\prime}(p\cdot y+r)=a_{p}(p\cdot y+r),

we have

By Lemma

Lemma 3.3

4
Large-time average in the general case

For a general contact-type Hamiltonian, we do not construct correctors in this paper. Nevertheless, the associated cell evolution has a well-defined large-time average under the assumptions below, following the viewpoint of
[
13
]
.
We impose the following assumptions on the Hamiltonian
H
.

(H1).

Regularity:
The Hamiltonian

H:\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}

is Lipschitz continuous, and there exists
\gamma\geq 0
such that, for almost every
(y,v,p)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

|D_{y}H(y,v,p)|\leq\gamma(1+|p|),\qquad|\partial_{v}H(y,v,p)|\leq\gamma,\qquad|D_{p}H(y,v,p)|\leq\gamma.

(H2).

Periodicity:
for any
(y,v,p)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
:

H(y+k,v+l,p)=H(y,v,p)\quad\text{for any }l\in\mathbb{Z},k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N};

4.1
The cell equation from an embedded viewpoint

For a general contact-type Hamiltonian, the corrector problems constructed in Sections 2 and 3 are not available. We therefore return to the higher-dimensional formulation introduced by Imbert and Monneau
[
13
]
.

As explained in
[
13
]
, the usual ansatz is obstructed by an uncontrolled phase. We first present the formal computation. Suppose
u^{\epsilon}(x,t)\approx u(x,t)+\epsilon v(y,\tau)
, where
y=\frac{x}{\epsilon},\tau=\frac{t}{\epsilon}
. Here
\epsilon>0
is small. Then

u^{\epsilon}_{t}(x,t)\approx u_{t}(x,t)+v_{\tau}(y,\tau),

D_{x}u^{\epsilon}(x,t)\approx D_{x}u(x,t)+D_{y}v(y,\tau),

\Delta_{x}u^{\epsilon}(x,t)\approx\Delta_{x}u(x,t)+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\Delta_{y}v(y,\tau).

Let
\lambda=u_{t}(0,0)
and
p=D_{x}u(0,0)
. Using the Taylor expansion
u(x,t)\approx u(0,0)+\lambda t+p\cdot x
and substituting the formal ansatz into (1.1), we obtain

\lambda+v_{\tau}(y,\tau)+H\left(y,\frac{u(0,0)}{\epsilon}+\lambda\tau+py,p+D_{y}v(y,\tau)\right)\approx\epsilon\Delta_{x}u(0,0)+\Delta_{y}v(y,\tau).

However, when we let
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
,
\frac{u(0,0)}{\epsilon}
cannot be controlled if
u(0,0)\neq 0
. Therefore the usual ansatz does not yield an appropriate corrector.

Imbert and Monneau introduced an embedding method in one additional space variable. Let
U^{\epsilon}(x,x_{N+1},t)
be the viscosity solution of

\begin{cases}U^{\epsilon}_{t}+H\left(\frac{x}{\epsilon},\frac{U^{\epsilon}}{\epsilon},D_{x}U^{\epsilon}\right)=\epsilon\Delta_{x}U^{\epsilon}&\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N+1}\times(0,\infty),\\
U^{\epsilon}(x,x_{N+1},0)=u_{0}(x)+x_{N+1}&\text{on }\mathbb{R}^{N+1}.\end{cases}

(4.1)

Assume formally that
U^{\epsilon}
converges locally uniformly to
U
. Then
U(x,x_{N+1},0)=u_{0}(x)+x_{N+1}
, and
U(X,t)=u(x,t)+x_{N+1}
. We now consider the following ansatz:

U^{\epsilon}(x,x_{N+1},t)=U(x,x_{N+1},t)+\epsilon V\left(\frac{x}{\epsilon},\frac{U(t,x,x_{N+1})-\lambda t-px}{\epsilon},\frac{t}{\epsilon}\right),

where
\lambda=U_{t}(0,0,0)
and
p=D_{x}U(0,0,0)
. A formal substitution gives

\displaystyle U_{t}+V_{\tau}+V_{y_{N+1}}\cdot(U_{t}-\lambda)

\displaystyle+H\left(y,V+\lambda\tau+py+y_{N+1},p+D_{y}V+\frac{\partial V}{\partial y_{N+1}}\cdot(D_{x}U-p)\right)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{y}V+\frac{\partial^{2}V}{\partial y_{N+1}^{2}}|D_{x}U-p|^{2}+\epsilon\frac{\partial V}{\partial y_{N+1}}\Delta U.

Here
y_{N+1}:=\frac{U(t,x,x_{N+1})-\lambda t-px}{\epsilon}
. Letting
\epsilon\to 0
and
(x,x_{N+1},t)\to(0,0,0)
formally yields

\lambda+V_{\tau}+H(y,V+\lambda\tau+py+y_{N+1},p+D_{y}V)=\Delta_{y}V.

This motivates the following cell evolution:

\begin{cases}w_{\tau}+H(\zeta,p\cdot\zeta+w,p+D_{\zeta}w)=\Delta_{\zeta}w,&(\tau,\zeta)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
w(\zeta,0)=0,&\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.\end{cases}

This evolution will be used to define the ergodic constant.

4.2
The ergodic constant

Following Barles and Souganidis
[
2
]
, we first establish an oscillation estimate for the cell evolution.
Assumption (H1) implies that

|H(\zeta,s,q)|\leq\chi(|q|)\quad\text{for all }(\zeta,s,q),\qquad\int^{\infty}\frac{dr}{\chi(r)}=+\infty.

In the present setting we may take
\chi
to be linear.

Proposition 4.1

(
Oscillation estimate
)
.

Assume that
H
satisfies (H1)(H2). Consider

\begin{cases}w_{\tau}+H(\zeta,p\cdot\zeta+w,p+D_{\zeta}w)=\Delta_{\zeta}w,&(\tau,\zeta)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
w(\zeta,0)=0,&\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.\end{cases}

Then there exists a constant
C_{p}>0
, such that

\operatorname{osc}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}w(\cdot,\tau)\leq C_{p},\quad\text{for each}\quad\tau\geq 0.

Proof.

Suppose
R>\sqrt{N}
and take a function

\psi\in C^{2}((0,R])\cap C([0,R])

such that

\psi(0)=0,

\psi^{\prime}>0,

\psi^{\prime\prime}<0,

and

4\psi^{\prime\prime}(r)+2\chi(|p|+\psi^{\prime}(r))<0\quad\text{for }0<r<R.

Let

z(r)=\psi^{\prime}(r).

Consider

4z^{\prime}(r)=-3\chi(|p|+z(r)).

Since

\int^{\infty}\frac{dr}{\chi(r)}=+\infty,

we choose
z(0)
sufficiently large so that

z(r)>0\quad\text{on }[0,R).

Then

4\psi^{\prime\prime}+2\chi(|p|+\psi^{\prime})=-\chi(|p|+\psi^{\prime})<0.

Local Lipschitz estimate.

We show that

w(\zeta,\tau)-w(\eta,\tau)\leq\psi(|\zeta-\eta|)\quad\text{for }|\zeta-\eta|\leq R.

Suppose that

\max_{[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\left\{w(\zeta,\tau)-w(\eta,\tau)-\psi(|\zeta-\eta|)\right\}>0.

Consider

\Phi(\zeta,\eta,\tau)=w(\zeta,\tau)-w(\eta,\tau)-\psi(|\zeta-\eta|)-\alpha(|\zeta|^{2}+|\eta|^{2})-\frac{\rho}{T-\tau}.

Assume that

\max_{[0,T]\times\{|\zeta-\eta|\leq R\}}\Phi(\zeta,\eta,\tau)>0.

Then
\Phi
attains its maximum at

(\tau_{\alpha},\zeta_{\alpha},\eta_{\alpha}).

At this maximum point,

\tau_{\alpha}>0,\qquad\zeta_{\alpha}\neq\eta_{\alpha}.

By Ishii’s lemma
[
8
]
, there exist
a\in\mathbb{R}
and
X,Y\in\mathbb{S}^{N}
such that

\left(a+\frac{\rho}{(T-\tau_{\alpha})^{2}},\psi^{\prime}(r_{\alpha})e_{\alpha}+2\alpha\zeta_{\alpha},X\right)\in\overline{J}^{2,+}w(\zeta_{\alpha},\tau_{\alpha}),

\left(a,\psi^{\prime}(r_{\alpha})e_{\alpha}-2\alpha\eta_{\alpha},Y\right)\in\overline{J}^{2,-}w(\eta_{\alpha},\tau_{\alpha}),

and

\operatorname{tr}X-\operatorname{tr}Y\leq 4\psi^{\prime\prime}(r_{\alpha})+C\alpha,

where

r_{\alpha}:=|\zeta_{\alpha}-\eta_{\alpha}|,

e_{\alpha}:=\frac{\zeta_{\alpha}-\eta_{\alpha}}{|\zeta_{\alpha}-\eta_{\alpha}|}.

Using the subsolution and supersolution inequalities,

a+\frac{\rho}{(T-\tau_{\alpha})^{2}}+H\left(\zeta_{\alpha},p\cdot\zeta_{\alpha}+w(\zeta_{\alpha},\tau_{\alpha}),p+\psi^{\prime}(r_{\alpha})e_{\alpha}+2\alpha\zeta_{\alpha}\right)\leq\operatorname{tr}X,

and

a+H\left(\eta_{\alpha},p\cdot\eta_{\alpha}+w(\eta_{\alpha},\tau_{\alpha}),p+\psi^{\prime}(r_{\alpha})e_{\alpha}-2\alpha\eta_{\alpha}\right)\geq\operatorname{tr}Y.

Therefore

\displaystyle\frac{\rho}{(T-\tau_{\alpha})^{2}}+H\left(\zeta_{\alpha},p\cdot\zeta_{\alpha}+w(\zeta_{\alpha},\tau_{\alpha}),p+\psi^{\prime}(r_{\alpha})e_{\alpha}+2\alpha\zeta_{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-H\left(\eta_{\alpha},p\cdot\eta_{\alpha}+w(\eta_{\alpha},\tau_{\alpha}),p+\psi^{\prime}(r_{\alpha})e_{\alpha}-2\alpha\eta_{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{tr}X-\operatorname{tr}Y.

Since

\displaystyle H\left(\zeta_{\alpha},p\cdot\zeta_{\alpha}+w(\zeta_{\alpha},\tau_{\alpha}),p+\psi^{\prime}(r_{\alpha})e_{\alpha}+2\alpha\zeta_{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-H\left(\eta_{\alpha},p\cdot\eta_{\alpha}+w(\eta_{\alpha},\tau_{\alpha}),p+\psi^{\prime}(r_{\alpha})e_{\alpha}-2\alpha\eta_{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle\geq-2\chi\left(|p|+\psi^{\prime}(r_{\alpha})+O_{\alpha}(1)\right),

letting
\alpha\to 0
, we have

0\leq 4\psi^{\prime\prime}(r)+2\chi(|p|+\psi^{\prime}(r)),

which is a contradiction. Therefore

|w(\zeta,\tau)-w(\eta,\tau)|\leq\psi(|\zeta-\eta|)\quad\text{when }|\zeta-\eta|\leq R.

Global Lipschitz estimate.

Pick
\zeta,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
such that

|\zeta-(\eta+k)|\leq\sqrt{N}<R.

Moreover, by the periodicity in the second variable of
H
,

w(\eta+k,\tau)+p\cdot k-\lfloor p\cdot k\rfloor\leq w(\eta,\tau)+1.

Therefore

\displaystyle w(\zeta,\tau)-w(\eta,\tau)

\displaystyle\leq w(\zeta,\tau)-w(\eta+k,\tau)+1\leq C_{p}.

Finally,

\operatorname{osc}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}w(\cdot,\tau)\leq C_{p}\quad\text{for all }\tau\geq 0.

∎

The oscillation estimate yields the following large-time behavior.

Theorem 6
.

Assume that
H
satisfies (H1)(H2). Consider

\begin{cases}w_{\tau}+H(\zeta,p\cdot\zeta+w,p+Dw)=\Delta w&\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,\infty),\\
w(\zeta,0)=0&\text{on }\mathbb{R}^{N}.\end{cases}

Then

\frac{w(\zeta,\tau)}{\tau}\to\lambda(p)\quad\text{as }\tau\to+\infty\quad\text{locally uniformly}.

Moreover, there exists a constant
C>0
, independent of
\zeta
and
\tau
, such that

|w(\zeta,\tau)-\lambda(p)\tau|\leq C

for all
(\zeta,\tau)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,\infty)
.

Before we prove our result, we need to introduce a lemma.

Lemma 4.1

(
Almost additive functions
)
.

Let
f:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be locally bounded. Assume that there exists a constant
C_{0}>0
such that

|f(t+s)-f(t)-f(s)|\leq C_{0}\qquad\text{for all }t,s\geq 0.

Then there exists a constant
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{f(t)}{t}=\lambda.

Moreover,

|f(t)-\lambda t|\leq C_{0}\qquad\text{for all }t\geq 0.

Proof.

For each
t\geq 0
, define

A(t):=\lim_{n\to\infty}2^{-n}f(2^{n}t),

provided that the limit exists. We first show that this limit is well-defined.

For
n\geq 0
, by the almost-additivity assumption with
s=2^{n}t
, we have
\left|f(2^{n+1}t)-2f(2^{n}t)\right|\leq C_{0}.

Hence

\displaystyle\left|2^{-(n+1)}f(2^{n+1}t)-2^{-n}f(2^{n}t)\right|

\displaystyle=2^{-(n+1)}\left|f(2^{n+1}t)-2f(2^{n}t)\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-(n+1)}C_{0}.

Therefore the sequence

\left\{2^{-n}f(2^{n}t)\right\}_{n\geq 0}

is Cauchy. Thus
A(t)
is well-defined for every
t\geq 0
. Moreover,

\displaystyle|A(t)-f(t)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left|2^{-(n+1)}f(2^{n+1}t)-2^{-n}f(2^{n}t)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}2^{-(n+1)}C_{0}=C_{0}.

Thus

|A(t)-f(t)|\leq C_{0}\qquad\text{for all }t\geq 0.

Next, we prove that
A
is additive. For
t,s\geq 0
, by the almost-additivity assumption,

\left|f(2^{n}(t+s))-f(2^{n}t)-f(2^{n}s)\right|\leq C_{0}.

Multiplying by
2^{-n}
and letting
n\to\infty
, we obtain

A(t+s)=A(t)+A(s)\qquad\text{for all }t,s\geq 0.

Since
f
is locally bounded and
|A-f|\leq C_{0}
, the function
A
is also locally bounded. A locally bounded additive function on
[0,\infty)
is linear. Indeed, if we set

\lambda:=A(1),

then additivity gives

A(q)=\lambda q\text{ for all }q\in\mathbb{Q}_{+}.

Local boundedness implies continuity of
A
at
0
. Therefore, by density of
\mathbb{Q}_{+}
in
[0,\infty)
,

A(t)=\lambda t\qquad\text{for all }t\geq 0.

Combining this with
|A(t)-f(t)|\leq C_{0}
, we obtain

|f(t)-\lambda t|\leq C_{0}\qquad\text{for all }t\geq 0.

In particular,

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{f(t)}{t}=\lambda.

This completes the proof.
∎

We now complete the proof of Theorem
6
.

Proof of Theorem
6
.

Let

m(\tau):=\inf_{\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}w(\zeta,\tau),\qquad M(\tau):=\sup_{\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}w(\zeta,\tau).

Then by Proposition
4.1
,

M(\tau)-m(\tau)\leq C_{p},

and

m(s)\leq w(\zeta,s)\leq m(s)+C_{p}.

Therefore there exist an integer
N_{p}
and

n_{s}=\lfloor m(s)\rfloor

such that

n_{s}\leq w(\zeta,s)\leq n_{s}+N_{p}\quad\text{for all }\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

By the comparison principle, we have

w(\zeta,\tau)+n_{s}\leq w(\zeta,s+\tau)\leq w(\zeta,\tau)+n_{s}+N_{p}.

Taking the infimum with respect to
\zeta
, we obtain

m(\tau)+n_{s}\leq m(s+\tau)\leq m(\tau)+n_{s}+N_{p}.

Moreover,

m(\tau)+m(s)-1\leq m(s+\tau)\leq m(\tau)+m(s)+N_{p}.

Therefore
m
is an almost-additive function. By Lemma
4.1
, there exists
\lambda(p)
such that

|w(\zeta,\tau)-\lambda(p)\tau|\leq C.

∎

4.3
Identification of the cell speed with the ergodic constant

We now identify the corrector speed obtained from (
3.2
) with the ergodic constant defined by the large-time average in Theorem 6.

Suppose that
H(y,s,p)=F(s,p)+\eta W(y,s,p)
, and we assume that
F
satisfies (F1)–(F4), and

W\in C^{3,\alpha}(\mathbb{T}^{N}\times\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Assume that, for each
p\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, there exist
c_{\eta}(p)\in\mathbb{R}
and a corrector
\chi_{p}^{\eta}
satisfying

\chi_{p}^{\eta}(y+k,z)=\chi_{p}^{\eta}(y,z),\qquad\chi_{p}^{\eta}(y,z+1)=\chi_{p}^{\eta}(y,z)+1,

and

\displaystyle c_{\eta}(p)(\chi_{p}^{\eta})_{z}

\displaystyle+F\left(\chi_{p}^{\eta},D_{y}\chi_{p}^{\eta}+p(\chi_{p}^{\eta})_{z}\right)

\displaystyle+\eta W\left(y,\chi_{p}^{\eta},D_{y}\chi_{p}^{\eta}+p(\chi_{p}^{\eta})_{z}\right)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{y}\chi_{p}^{\eta}+2p\cdot D^{2}_{yz}\chi_{p}^{\eta}+|p|^{2}(\chi_{p}^{\eta})_{zz}.

For fixed
p\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, consider the cell evolution

\begin{cases}w_{\tau}+F\left(p\cdot\zeta+w,p+D_{\zeta}w\right)\\
\qquad+\eta W\left(\zeta,p\cdot\zeta+w,p+D_{\zeta}w\right)=\Delta_{\zeta}w,&(\tau,\zeta)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N},\\[2.84526pt]
w(\zeta,0)=0,&\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.\end{cases}

Theorem 7
.

For every fixed
p\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, the solution
w=w(\zeta,\tau)
of the above cell evolution satisfies

\frac{w(\zeta,\tau)}{\tau}\to c_{\eta}(p)\quad\text{as }\tau\to+\infty,

locally uniformly in
\zeta
. In particular, the ergodic constant defined by the long-time average is equal to the cell speed
c_{\eta}(p)
.

Proof.

Define

\widetilde{w}(\zeta,\tau):=\chi_{p}^{\eta}\left(\zeta,p\cdot\zeta+c_{\eta}(p)\tau\right)-p\cdot\zeta.

We first verify that
\widetilde{w}
solves the same cell evolution.

Set
z=p\cdot\zeta+c_{\eta}(p)\tau.

Then
\widetilde{w}_{\tau}=c_{\eta}(p)(\chi_{p}^{\eta})_{z},

and
p+D_{\zeta}\widetilde{w}=D_{y}\chi_{p}^{\eta}+p(\chi_{p}^{\eta})_{z}.

Moreover,
p\cdot\zeta+\widetilde{w}=\chi_{p}^{\eta}(\zeta,z).

Finally,

\Delta_{\zeta}\widetilde{w}=\Delta_{y}\chi_{p}^{\eta}+2p\cdot D^{2}_{yz}\chi_{p}^{\eta}+|p|^{2}(\chi_{p}^{\eta})_{zz}.

Therefore, by the cell equation satisfied by
\chi_{p}^{\eta}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\widetilde{w}_{\tau}

\displaystyle+F\left(p\cdot\zeta+\widetilde{w},p+D_{\zeta}\widetilde{w}\right)

\displaystyle+\eta W\left(\zeta,p\cdot\zeta+\widetilde{w},p+D_{\zeta}\widetilde{w}\right)=\Delta_{\zeta}\widetilde{w}.

Thus
\widetilde{w}
is a solution of the cell evolution, with initial data

\widetilde{w}(\zeta,0)=\chi_{p}^{\eta}(\zeta,p\cdot\zeta)-p\cdot\zeta.

Since
\chi_{p}^{\eta}(y,z)=z+w_{p}^{\eta}(y,z)

with
w_{p}^{\eta}
periodic in
(y,z)
, the function
\widetilde{w}(\zeta,0)
is bounded. Hence there exists an integer
M_{p}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

-M_{p}\leq\widetilde{w}(\zeta,0)\leq M_{p}\quad\text{for all }\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Let
w
be the solution of the cell evolution with initial data
0
. Since
F
and
W
are periodic in the phase variable, if
u
is a solution, then
u+m
is also a solution for every
m\in\mathbb{Z}
. Therefore
w-M_{p}
and
w+M_{p}
are also solutions of the same cell equation. At
\tau=0
, we have

w(\zeta,0)-M_{p}\leq\widetilde{w}(\zeta,0)\leq w(\zeta,0)+M_{p}.

By the comparison principle,

w(\zeta,\tau)-M_{p}\leq\widetilde{w}(\zeta,\tau)\leq w(\zeta,\tau)+M_{p}

for all
\tau\geq 0
and
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Hence

|w(\zeta,\tau)-\widetilde{w}(\zeta,\tau)|\leq M_{p}.

On the other hand, since
\chi_{p}^{\eta}(y,z)=z+w_{p}^{\eta}(y,z),

we have
\widetilde{w}(\zeta,\tau)=c_{\eta}(p)\tau+w_{p}^{\eta}\left(\zeta,p\cdot\zeta+c_{\eta}(p)\tau\right).

The function
w_{p}^{\eta}
is bounded. Therefore

\left|\frac{\widetilde{w}(\zeta,\tau)}{\tau}-c_{\eta}(p)\right|\leq\frac{\|w_{p}^{\eta}\|_{L^{\infty}}}{\tau}.

Combining this estimate with (
4.3
),
we obtain

\left|\frac{w(\zeta,\tau)}{\tau}-c_{\eta}(p)\right|\leq\frac{M_{p}+\|w_{p}^{\eta}\|_{L^{\infty}}}{\tau}.

Letting
\tau\to+\infty
, we conclude that

\frac{w(\zeta,\tau)}{\tau}\to c_{\eta}(p).

The convergence is locally uniform in
\zeta
, and in fact uniform in
\zeta
.
∎

5
Examples

In this section we provide examples satisfying the assumptions above.

Example 5.1

(Semilinear heat equation)
.

Let

F(s,q)=g(s),\quad s\in\mathbb{R},

where
g\in C^{3,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
and has a positive lower bound. Then
F
satisfies
(F1)–(F4)
.

Example 5.2

(An admissible unperturbed Hamiltonian)
.

Let

F(s,q)=4+\sqrt{1+|q|^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{\sin(2\pi s)}{\sqrt{1+|q|^{2}}},\qquad(s,q)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Then
F
satisfies
(F1)–(F4)
.

Indeed,
F
is
1
-periodic in
s
and is smooth in
(s,q)
. Moreover,

F(s,q)\geq 4+1-\frac{1}{2}=\frac{9}{2}>0.

Thus
(F1)
holds.

Next, we compute the recession function. For
\rho>0
,

\displaystyle\frac{F(s,\rho q)}{\rho}

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{\rho}+\frac{\sqrt{1+\rho^{2}|q|^{2}}}{\rho}+\frac{1}{2\rho}\frac{\sin(2\pi s)}{\sqrt{1+\rho^{2}|q|^{2}}}.

Since

\frac{\sqrt{1+\rho^{2}|q|^{2}}}{\rho}=\sqrt{|q|^{2}+\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}}\to|q|

locally uniformly in
q
, and the other two terms converge to
0
uniformly in
s
, we obtain

F_{\infty}(q)=|q|.

Hence
(F3)
holds.

We now verify the strict radial subhomogeneity condition. Let

R:=\sqrt{1+\rho^{2}|q|^{2}},\qquad S:=\sin(2\pi s).

Then

F(s,\rho q)=4+R+\frac{1}{2}\frac{S}{R}.

A direct computation gives

\rho D_{q}F(s,\rho q)\cdot q-F(s,\rho q)=-4-\frac{1}{R}-\frac{S}{2}\frac{2R^{2}-1}{R^{3}}.

Since
R\geq 1
and
|S|\leq 1
, we have

\left|\frac{S}{2}\frac{2R^{2}-1}{R^{3}}\right|\leq 1.

Therefore

\rho D_{q}F(s,\rho q)\cdot q-F(s,\rho q)\leq-4+1=-3<0.

Thus
(F2)
holds.

Finally,

D_{q}F(s,q)=\frac{q}{\sqrt{1+|q|^{2}}}-\frac{1}{2}\sin(2\pi s)\frac{q}{(1+|q|^{2})^{3/2}}.

Hence

|D_{q}F(s,q)|\leq\frac{3}{2}.

Also

|F_{s}(s,q)|=\left|\pi\frac{\cos(2\pi s)}{\sqrt{1+|q|^{2}}}\right|\leq\pi.

Therefore
F
is globally Lipschitz. Since
F
is also smooth,
(F4)
holds.

We finally give an explicit example with nontrivial
y
-dependence.

Example 5.3

(A perturbative Hamiltonian with an explicit nonzero perturbation)
.

Let
F
be the Hamiltonian in the previous example. Fix

\eta_{0}=\frac{1}{10},\qquad\varphi(y)=-\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\sin(2\pi y_{1}).

Then

\Delta_{y}\varphi(y)=\sin(2\pi y_{1}),\qquad D_{y}\varphi(y)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\cos(2\pi y_{1})e_{1}.

Define

W_{\eta_{0}}(y,s,q)=\Delta_{y}\varphi(y)+\frac{F(s-\eta_{0}\varphi(y),q-\eta_{0}D_{y}\varphi(y))-F(s,q)}{\eta_{0}}.

Equivalently,

W_{\eta_{0}}(y,s,q)=\sin(2\pi y_{1})+10\left[F\left(s+\frac{\sin(2\pi y_{1})}{40\pi^{2}},q+\frac{\cos(2\pi y_{1})}{20\pi}e_{1}\right)-F(s,q)\right].

Then

H(y,s,q)=F(s,q)+\eta_{0}W_{\eta_{0}}(y,s,q)

is a Hamiltonian belongs to the perturbative class..

The function
W_{\eta_{0}}
is
1
-periodic in
y
and
s
. Moreover, since
F
is smooth and
\varphi
is smooth and periodic, we have

W_{\eta_{0}}\in C^{\infty}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{T}^{N}\times\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}).

In particular,
W_{\eta_{0}}
satisfies the regularity assumptions required in the perturbative case.

We now verify that the explicit value

\eta_{0}=\frac{1}{10}

is admissible. Let
(c_{0}(p),\chi_{p}^{0})
be the unperturbed corrector associated with
F
, namely

|p|^{2}(\chi_{p}^{0})^{\prime\prime}=c_{0}(p)(\chi_{p}^{0})^{\prime}+F\bigl(\chi_{p}^{0},p(\chi_{p}^{0})^{\prime}\bigr).

Define

\chi_{p}^{\eta_{0}}(y,z)=\chi_{p}^{0}(z)+\eta_{0}\varphi(y).

Then
(\chi_{p}^{\eta_{0}})_{z}=(\chi_{p}^{0})^{\prime}(z)>0.

Moreover,
D_{y}\chi_{p}^{\eta_{0}}=\eta_{0}D_{y}\varphi(y),

and
D_{y}\chi_{p}^{\eta_{0}}+p(\chi_{p}^{\eta_{0}})_{z}=\eta_{0}D_{y}\varphi(y)+p(\chi_{p}^{0})^{\prime}(z).

Set
s=\chi_{p}^{\eta_{0}}(y,z),

q=D_{y}\chi_{p}^{\eta_{0}}+p(\chi_{p}^{\eta_{0}})_{z}.

Then
s-\eta_{0}\varphi(y)=\chi_{p}^{0}(z),

and
q-\eta_{0}D_{y}\varphi(y)=p(\chi_{p}^{0})^{\prime}(z).

By the definition of
W_{\eta_{0}}
,

F(s,q)+\eta_{0}W_{\eta_{0}}(y,s,q)=F\bigl(s-\eta_{0}\varphi(y),q-\eta_{0}D_{y}\varphi(y)\bigr)+\eta_{0}\Delta_{y}\varphi(y).

Therefore,

F(s,q)+\eta_{0}W_{\eta_{0}}(y,s,q)=F\bigl(\chi_{p}^{0},p(\chi_{p}^{0})^{\prime}\bigr)+\eta_{0}\Delta_{y}\varphi(y).

On the other hand,

\Delta_{y}\chi_{p}^{\eta_{0}}=\eta_{0}\Delta_{y}\varphi(y),\qquad D^{2}_{yz}\chi_{p}^{\eta_{0}}=0,\qquad(\chi_{p}^{\eta_{0}})_{zz}=(\chi_{p}^{0})^{\prime\prime}.

Hence

\displaystyle\Delta_{y}\chi_{p}^{\eta_{0}}+2p\cdot D^{2}_{yz}\chi_{p}^{\eta_{0}}+|p|^{2}(\chi_{p}^{\eta_{0}})_{zz}

\displaystyle=\eta_{0}\Delta_{y}\varphi(y)+|p|^{2}(\chi_{p}^{0})^{\prime\prime}.

Using the unperturbed cell ODE, we obtain
c_{0}(p)(\chi_{p}^{0})^{\prime}+F\bigl(\chi_{p}^{0},p(\chi_{p}^{0})^{\prime}\bigr)=|p|^{2}(\chi_{p}^{0})^{\prime\prime}.

Thus

\displaystyle c_{0}(p)(\chi_{p}^{\eta_{0}})_{z}+F\left(\chi_{p}^{\eta_{0}},D_{y}\chi_{p}^{\eta_{0}}+p(\chi_{p}^{\eta_{0}})_{z}\right)

\displaystyle+\eta_{0}W_{\eta_{0}}\left(y,\chi_{p}^{\eta_{0}},D_{y}\chi_{p}^{\eta_{0}}+p(\chi_{p}^{\eta_{0}})_{z}\right)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{y}\chi_{p}^{\eta_{0}}+2p\cdot D^{2}_{yz}\chi_{p}^{\eta_{0}}+|p|^{2}(\chi_{p}^{\eta_{0}})_{zz}.

Consequently,
c_{\eta_{0}}(p)=c_{0}(p),

\chi_{p}^{\eta_{0}}(y,z)=\chi_{p}^{0}(z)+\eta_{0}\varphi(y)

is an exact corrector for the perturbed cell problem. Hence the explicit value
\eta_{0}=\frac{1}{10}

is admissible.

Appendix A
Comparison principle for equations with variable diffusion

In the appendix, we prove the comparison principle used in the paper, following
[
8
]
.

Proposition A.1

(Comparison principle in the linear growth class)
.

Let
T>0
. Assume that

A(x)=\sigma(x)\sigma(x)^{T},\qquad\sigma\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}).

Assume that
H=H(x,r,p)
is continuous and satisfies the following Lipschitz condition: there exists
L>0
such that

|H(x,r,p)-H(y,s,q)|\leq L\Bigl((1+|p|+|q|)|x-y|+|r-s|+|p-q|\Bigr)

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
r,s\in\mathbb{R}
, and
p,q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
u\in C(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[0,T])
be a viscosity subsolution and
v\in C(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[0,T])
be a viscosity supersolution of

w_{t}+H(x,w,Dw)=\operatorname{tr}(A(x)D^{2}w)\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,T).

Assume that

u(x,0)\leq v(x,0)\quad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

and that
u,v
have at most linear growth in
x
, uniformly in
t\in[0,T]
. Then

u(x,t)\leq v(x,t)\quad\text{for all }(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[0,T].

Proof.

Choose
k>L
and set

\widetilde{u}(x,t):=e^{-kt}u(x,t),\qquad\widetilde{v}(x,t):=e^{-kt}v(x,t).

Then
\widetilde{u}
and
\widetilde{v}
are respectively a viscosity subsolution and supersolution of

w_{t}+G(t,x,w,Dw)=\operatorname{tr}(A(x)D^{2}w),

where

G(t,x,r,p):=kr+e^{-kt}H(x,e^{kt}r,e^{kt}p).

Moreover, if
r\geq s
, then

\displaystyle G(t,x,r,p)-G(t,x,s,p)

\displaystyle=k(r-s)+e^{-kt}\Bigl[H(x,e^{kt}r,e^{kt}p)-H(x,e^{kt}s,e^{kt}p)\Bigr]

\displaystyle\geq(k-L)(r-s).

Set
\lambda:=k-L>0.

We prove
\widetilde{u}\leq\widetilde{v}
. Suppose by contradiction that
M:=\sup_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[0,T]}(\widetilde{u}-\widetilde{v})>0.

We first introduce a localization function. Since
\widetilde{u},\widetilde{v}
have at most linear growth, there exists
C_{0}>0
such that

\widetilde{u}(x,t)-\widetilde{v}(y,s)\leq C_{0}(1+|x|+|y|)\quad\text{for all }x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\ t,s\in[0,T].

Choose a family
\{\beta_{R}\}_{R>1}\subset C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

\beta_{R}\geq 0,\qquad\beta_{R}(x)=0\quad\text{for }|x|\leq R,

\liminf_{|x|\to\infty}\frac{\beta_{R}(x)}{|x|}\geq 4C_{0},

\|D\beta_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|D^{2}\beta_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C,

and

\beta_{R}(x)\to 0\quad\text{as }R\to\infty\quad\text{for every fixed }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Step 1:

Let
\zeta(z):=\sqrt{1+|z|^{2}}.

We first prove the auxiliary estimate

\widetilde{u}(x,t)-\widetilde{v}(y,t)\leq C(1+|x-y|)\quad\text{for all }x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\ t\in[0,T].

Indeed, fix
\Theta>0
,
\mu>0
, and
R>1
, and consider

\Phi_{R}(x,y,t):=\widetilde{u}(x,t)-\widetilde{v}(y,t)-\Theta e^{\mu t}\zeta(x-y)-\beta_{R}(x)-\beta_{R}(y).

Because of the growth of
\beta_{R}
,
\Phi_{R}
attains its supremum on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[0,T]
. We claim that for
\Theta,\mu
sufficiently large, independent of
R
,
\sup_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[0,T]}\Phi_{R}\leq 0.

Assume otherwise. By the initial condition and by choosing
\Theta
larger if necessary, a positive maximum cannot occur at
t=0
. To avoid the common time variable, for
\nu>0
consider

\Psi_{R,\nu}(x,y,t,s):=\widetilde{u}(x,t)-\widetilde{v}(y,s)-\Theta e^{\mu t}\zeta(x-y)-\beta_{R}(x)-\beta_{R}(y)-\frac{|t-s|^{2}}{2\nu}.

Let
(x_{\nu},y_{\nu},t_{\nu},s_{\nu})
be a maximum point. As
\nu\to 0
, up to a subsequence,
(x_{\nu},y_{\nu},t_{\nu},s_{\nu})\to(\bar{x},\bar{y},\bar{t},\bar{t}),

where
(\bar{x},\bar{y},\bar{t})
is a positive maximum point of
\Phi_{R}
.

We now derive the key estimate used in Step 1. Let

p_{\nu}:=\Theta e^{\mu t_{\nu}}D\zeta(x_{\nu}-y_{\nu}),\qquad Z_{\nu}:=\Theta e^{\mu t_{\nu}}D^{2}\zeta(x_{\nu}-y_{\nu}).

By Ishii’s lemma, there exist
X_{\nu},Y_{\nu}\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
such that

\left(\mu\Theta e^{\mu t_{\nu}}\zeta(x_{\nu}-y_{\nu})+\frac{t_{\nu}-s_{\nu}}{\nu},p_{\nu}+D\beta_{R}(x_{\nu}),X_{\nu}+D^{2}\beta_{R}(x_{\nu})\right)\in\overline{J}^{2,+}\widetilde{u}(x_{\nu},t_{\nu}),

and

\left(\frac{t_{\nu}-s_{\nu}}{\nu},p_{\nu}-D\beta_{R}(y_{\nu}),Y_{\nu}-D^{2}\beta_{R}(y_{\nu})\right)\in\overline{J}^{2,-}\widetilde{v}(y_{\nu},s_{\nu}).

Using the viscosity subsolution inequality for
\widetilde{u}
, we get

\displaystyle\mu\Theta e^{\mu t_{\nu}}\zeta(x_{\nu}-y_{\nu})+\frac{t_{\nu}-s_{\nu}}{\nu}+G\left(t_{\nu},x_{\nu},\widetilde{u}(x_{\nu},t_{\nu}),p_{\nu}+D\beta_{R}(x_{\nu})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{tr}\left(A(x_{\nu})\bigl(X_{\nu}+D^{2}\beta_{R}(x_{\nu})\bigr)\right).

Similarly, using the viscosity supersolution inequality for
\widetilde{v}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{t_{\nu}-s_{\nu}}{\nu}+G\left(s_{\nu},y_{\nu},\widetilde{v}(y_{\nu},s_{\nu}),p_{\nu}-D\beta_{R}(y_{\nu})\right)

\displaystyle\geq\operatorname{tr}\left(A(y_{\nu})\bigl(Y_{\nu}-D^{2}\beta_{R}(y_{\nu})\bigr)\right).

Subtracting the two inequalities yields

\displaystyle\mu\Theta e^{\mu t_{\nu}}\zeta(x_{\nu}-y_{\nu})+G\left(t_{\nu},x_{\nu},\widetilde{u}(x_{\nu},t_{\nu}),p_{\nu}+D\beta_{R}(x_{\nu})\right)

\displaystyle-G\left(s_{\nu},y_{\nu},\widetilde{v}(y_{\nu},s_{\nu}),p_{\nu}-D\beta_{R}(y_{\nu})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{tr}\left(A(x_{\nu})\bigl(X_{\nu}+D^{2}\beta_{R}(x_{\nu})\bigr)\right)-\operatorname{tr}\left(A(y_{\nu})\bigl(Y_{\nu}-D^{2}\beta_{R}(y_{\nu})\bigr)\right).

We split the difference of the
G
-terms as

\displaystyle G\left(t_{\nu},x_{\nu},\widetilde{u}(x_{\nu},t_{\nu}),p_{\nu}+D\beta_{R}(x_{\nu})\right)-G\left(s_{\nu},y_{\nu},\widetilde{v}(y_{\nu},s_{\nu}),p_{\nu}-D\beta_{R}(y_{\nu})\right)

\displaystyle=\Bigl[G\left(t_{\nu},x_{\nu},\widetilde{u}(x_{\nu},t_{\nu}),p_{\nu}+D\beta_{R}(x_{\nu})\right)-G\left(t_{\nu},x_{\nu},\widetilde{v}(y_{\nu},s_{\nu}),p_{\nu}+D\beta_{R}(x_{\nu})\right)\Bigr]

\displaystyle+\Bigl[G\left(t_{\nu},x_{\nu},\widetilde{v}(y_{\nu},s_{\nu}),p_{\nu}+D\beta_{R}(x_{\nu})\right)-G\left(s_{\nu},y_{\nu},\widetilde{v}(y_{\nu},s_{\nu}),p_{\nu}-D\beta_{R}(y_{\nu})\right)\Bigr].

Since
G
is strictly increasing in the
r
-variable, we have

\displaystyle G\left(t_{\nu},x_{\nu},\widetilde{u}(x_{\nu},t_{\nu}),p_{\nu}+D\beta_{R}(x_{\nu})\right)-G\left(t_{\nu},x_{\nu},\widetilde{v}(y_{\nu},s_{\nu}),p_{\nu}+D\beta_{R}(x_{\nu})\right)

\displaystyle\geq\lambda\bigl(\widetilde{u}(x_{\nu},t_{\nu})-\widetilde{v}(y_{\nu},s_{\nu})\bigr),

where
\lambda=k-L>0
. Therefore

\displaystyle\mu\Theta e^{\mu t_{\nu}}\zeta(x_{\nu}-y_{\nu})

\displaystyle\leq-\lambda\bigl(\widetilde{u}(x_{\nu},t_{\nu})-\widetilde{v}(y_{\nu},s_{\nu})\bigr)

\displaystyle+E_{\nu}+\mathcal{T}_{\nu},

where

\displaystyle E_{\nu}

\displaystyle:=\left|G\left(t_{\nu},x_{\nu},\widetilde{v}(y_{\nu},s_{\nu}),p_{\nu}+D\beta_{R}(x_{\nu})\right)-G\left(s_{\nu},y_{\nu},\widetilde{v}(y_{\nu},s_{\nu}),p_{\nu}-D\beta_{R}(y_{\nu})\right)\right|,

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\nu}

\displaystyle:=\operatorname{tr}\left(A(x_{\nu})\bigl(X_{\nu}+D^{2}\beta_{R}(x_{\nu})\bigr)\right)-\operatorname{tr}\left(A(y_{\nu})\bigl(Y_{\nu}-D^{2}\beta_{R}(y_{\nu})\bigr)\right).

We now estimate
E_{\nu}
and
\mathcal{T}_{\nu}
. First, since
|D\zeta|\leq 1,

|p_{\nu}|\leq\Theta e^{\mu T},

and since
\|D\beta_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C,

the gradient variables in
E_{\nu}
are bounded by
C(1+\Theta e^{\mu T})
. Moreover,

|D\beta_{R}(x_{\nu})+D\beta_{R}(y_{\nu})|\leq C.

Using the Lipschitz continuity of
G
in
x
and
p
, we obtain

E_{\nu}\leq C\Theta e^{\mu t_{\nu}}\zeta(x_{\nu}-y_{\nu})+C+\omega_{R}(|t_{\nu}-s_{\nu}|),

where
\omega_{R}(r)\to 0
as
r\to 0
. The modulus
\omega_{R}
comes from the time-dependence of
G
; for fixed
R
, the variables are confined to a compact set by the localization
\beta_{R}
.

Next, using
A=\sigma\sigma^{T}
and the matrix inequality in Ishii’s lemma, we estimate the second-order term as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\nu}

\displaystyle\leq C\Theta e^{\mu t_{\nu}}\|D^{2}\zeta(x_{\nu}-y_{\nu})\|\|\sigma(x_{\nu})-\sigma(y_{\nu})\|^{2}

\displaystyle+C\bigl(\|D^{2}\beta_{R}(x_{\nu})\|+\|D^{2}\beta_{R}(y_{\nu})\|\bigr).

Since
\sigma
is Lipschitz and

\|D^{2}\zeta(z)\|\leq C(1+|z|^{2})^{-1/2},

we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\nu}

\displaystyle\leq C\Theta e^{\mu t_{\nu}}\frac{|x_{\nu}-y_{\nu}|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+|x_{\nu}-y_{\nu}|^{2}}}+C

\displaystyle\leq C\Theta e^{\mu t_{\nu}}\zeta(x_{\nu}-y_{\nu})+C.

Combining the estimates for
E_{\nu}
and
\mathcal{T}_{\nu}
, we arrive at

\displaystyle\mu\Theta e^{\mu t_{\nu}}\zeta(x_{\nu}-y_{\nu})

\displaystyle\leq-\lambda\bigl(\widetilde{u}(x_{\nu},t_{\nu})-\widetilde{v}(y_{\nu},s_{\nu})\bigr)

\displaystyle+C\Theta e^{\mu t_{\nu}}\zeta(x_{\nu}-y_{\nu})+C+\omega_{R}(|t_{\nu}-s_{\nu}|).

Finally, since
t_{\nu}-s_{\nu}\to 0
as
\nu\to 0
, we may let
\nu\to 0
and obtain

\displaystyle\mu\Theta e^{\mu\bar{t}}\zeta(\bar{x}-\bar{y})

\displaystyle\leq-\lambda\bigl(\widetilde{u}(\bar{x},\bar{t})-\widetilde{v}(\bar{y},\bar{t})\bigr)

\displaystyle+C\Theta e^{\mu\bar{t}}\zeta(\bar{x}-\bar{y})+C.

Moreover,

\mu\Theta e^{\mu\bar{t}}\zeta(\bar{x}-\bar{y})\leq C\Theta e^{\mu\bar{t}}\zeta(\bar{x}-\bar{y})+C.

Choosing
\mu>2C
and then
\Theta
sufficiently large yields a contradiction. Therefore
\Phi_{R}\leq 0.

Letting
R\to\infty
, we obtain

\widetilde{u}(x,t)-\widetilde{v}(y,t)\leq\Theta e^{\mu T}\zeta(x-y)\leq C(1+|x-y|).

Step 2:

We now prove the comparison. Let
\eta>0
, and assume that

\sup_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[0,T]}\bigl(\widetilde{u}-\widetilde{v}-\eta t\bigr)>0.

For
\alpha,\kappa,\nu>0
, consider

\displaystyle M_{\alpha,\kappa,\nu}:=\sup_{x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\ t,s\in[0,T]}\Bigl\{\widetilde{u}(x,t)-\widetilde{v}(y,s)-\eta t-\alpha|x|^{2}-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{2\kappa}-\frac{|t-s|^{2}}{2\nu}\Bigr\}.

Let
(\bar{x},\bar{y},\bar{t},\bar{s})
be a maximum point. The estimate just proved implies

|\bar{x}-\bar{y}|\leq C\kappa,

and the standard doubling argument gives

\frac{|\bar{t}-\bar{s}|^{2}}{\nu}\to 0\quad\text{as }\nu\to 0.

Moreover,
\alpha|\bar{x}|^{2}

is bounded, hence

\alpha|\bar{x}|\to 0\quad\text{as }\alpha\to 0.

For small parameters, the positivity of the above supremum and the initial condition imply

\bar{t}>0,\qquad\bar{s}>0.

Set

p:=\frac{\bar{x}-\bar{y}}{\kappa}+2\alpha\bar{x},\qquad q:=\frac{\bar{x}-\bar{y}}{\kappa}.

By the parabolic Ishii lemma, there exist
X,Y\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
such that

\left(\eta+\frac{\bar{t}-\bar{s}}{\nu},p,X\right)\in\overline{J}^{2,+}\widetilde{u}(\bar{x},\bar{t}),

and

\left(\frac{\bar{t}-\bar{s}}{\nu},q,Y\right)\in\overline{J}^{2,-}\widetilde{v}(\bar{y},\bar{s}).

We estimate the second-order term. Since

A(x)=\sigma(x)\sigma(x)^{T},\qquad\sigma\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}),

we write

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr}(A(\bar{x})X)-\operatorname{tr}(A(\bar{y})Y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left[\left\langle X\sigma(\bar{x})e_{j},\sigma(\bar{x})e_{j}\right\rangle-\left\langle Y\sigma(\bar{y})e_{j},\sigma(\bar{y})e_{j}\right\rangle\right].

By the matrix inequality in Ishii’s lemma,

\begin{pmatrix}X&0\\
0&-Y\end{pmatrix}\leq\frac{C}{\kappa}\begin{pmatrix}I&-I\\
-I&I\end{pmatrix}+C\alpha\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\
0&I\end{pmatrix}.

Applying this inequality to the vector

\binom{\sigma(\bar{x})e_{j}}{\sigma(\bar{y})e_{j}}

and summing over
j=1,\dots,m
, we get

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr}(A(\bar{x})X)-\operatorname{tr}(A(\bar{y})Y)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\kappa}\|\sigma(\bar{x})-\sigma(\bar{y})\|^{2}+C\alpha\left(\|\sigma(\bar{x})\|^{2}+\|\sigma(\bar{y})\|^{2}\right).

Since
\sigma
is bounded and Lipschitz continuous, this yields

\operatorname{tr}(A(\bar{x})X)-\operatorname{tr}(A(\bar{y})Y)\leq C\frac{|\bar{x}-\bar{y}|^{2}}{\kappa}+C\alpha.

Using the viscosity inequalities, we obtain

\displaystyle\eta

\displaystyle+G(\bar{t},\bar{x},\widetilde{u}(\bar{x},\bar{t}),p)-G(\bar{s},\bar{y},\widetilde{v}(\bar{y},\bar{s}),q)

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{|\bar{x}-\bar{y}|^{2}}{\kappa}+C\alpha.

By the strict monotonicity of
G
in the
r
-variable,

\displaystyle G(\bar{t},\bar{x},\widetilde{u}(\bar{x},\bar{t}),p)-G(\bar{s},\bar{y},\widetilde{v}(\bar{y},\bar{s}),q)

\displaystyle\geq\lambda\bigl(\widetilde{u}(\bar{x},\bar{t})-\widetilde{v}(\bar{y},\bar{s})\bigr)-E_{\alpha,\kappa,\nu},

where
E_{\alpha,\kappa,\nu}:=\left|G(\bar{t},\bar{x},\widetilde{v}(\bar{y},\bar{s}),p)-G(\bar{s},\bar{y},\widetilde{v}(\bar{y},\bar{s}),q)\right|.

Since

|\bar{x}-\bar{y}|\leq C\kappa,\qquad|p-q|=2\alpha|\bar{x}|\to 0,

and
p,q
remain bounded, we have
E_{\alpha,\kappa,\nu}\to 0

as
\nu\to 0
, then
\kappa\to 0
, and finally
\alpha\to 0
. Hence

\eta\leq C\frac{|\bar{x}-\bar{y}|^{2}}{\kappa}+C\alpha+E_{\alpha,\kappa,\nu}.

Letting
\nu\to 0
, then
\kappa\to 0
, and then
\alpha\to 0
, we obtain
\eta\leq 0,

which is impossible. Therefore, for every
\eta>0
,

\widetilde{u}(x,t)-\widetilde{v}(x,t)-\eta t\leq 0\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[0,T].

Letting
\eta\to 0
, we obtain

\widetilde{u}\leq\widetilde{v}.

Since
e^{kt}>0
, this gives

u\leq v\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[0,T].

∎

Appendix B
Uniform local bound for the equation

Lemma B.1
.

Assume that
H
satisfies
(H1)
–
(H2)
, and let
u^{\epsilon}
be the viscosity solution of

\begin{cases}u_{t}^{\epsilon}+H\left(\dfrac{x}{\epsilon},\dfrac{u^{\epsilon}}{\epsilon},Du^{\epsilon}\right)=\epsilon\Delta u^{\epsilon}&\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,\infty),\\[5.69054pt]
u^{\epsilon}(x,0)=u_{0}(x)&\text{on }\mathbb{R}^{N}.\end{cases}

Assume that

u_{0}\in BUC(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Then, for every
T>0
, there exists a constant
C_{T}>0
, independent of
\epsilon
, such that

|u^{\epsilon}(x,t)|\leq C_{T}

for all
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T]
.

Moreover, if

u^{*}:={\limsup}_{\epsilon\to 0}^{*}u^{\epsilon},\qquad u_{*}:={\liminf}_{\epsilon\to 0,*}u^{\epsilon},

then

u^{*}(x,0)=u_{*}(x,0)=u_{0}(x)

for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Proof.

Let

M_{0}:=\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

By (H1)–(H2), we have
B_{0}:=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{T}^{N},\ s\in\mathbb{T}}|H(y,s,0)|<\infty.

Define

\overline{u}(x,t):=M_{0}+B_{0}t,\qquad\underline{u}(x,t):=-M_{0}-B_{0}t.

Then

D\overline{u}=D\underline{u}=0,\qquad\Delta\overline{u}=\Delta\underline{u}=0.

Moreover,

\overline{u}_{t}+H\left(\dfrac{x}{\epsilon},\dfrac{\overline{u}}{\epsilon},D\overline{u}\right)-\epsilon\Delta\overline{u}=B_{0}+H\left(\dfrac{x}{\epsilon},\dfrac{\overline{u}}{\epsilon},0\right)\geq 0,

and

\underline{u}_{t}+H\left(\dfrac{x}{\epsilon},\dfrac{\underline{u}}{\epsilon},D\underline{u}\right)-\epsilon\Delta\underline{u}=-B_{0}+H\left(\dfrac{x}{\epsilon},\dfrac{\underline{u}}{\epsilon},0\right)\leq 0.

Since

\underline{u}(x,0)\leq u_{0}(x)\leq\overline{u}(x,0),

the comparison principle gives

-M_{0}-B_{0}t\leq u^{\epsilon}(x,t)\leq M_{0}+B_{0}t.

This proves the uniform boundedness on every finite time interval.

We now prove the initial trace. Let

L_{0}:=\operatorname{Lip}(u_{0}).

For
\delta>0
, let
u_{0}^{\delta}
be a standard smooth mollification of
u_{0}
. Then

\|u_{0}^{\delta}-u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq CL_{0}\delta,

\|Du_{0}^{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq L_{0},

and

\|\Delta u_{0}^{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\frac{CL_{0}}{\delta}.

By (H1)–(H2), the quantity

B_{L_{0}}:=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{T}^{N},\ s\in\mathbb{T},\ |q|\leq L_{0}}|H(y,s,q)|

is finite.

Define

\overline{u}^{\epsilon,\delta}(x,t):=u_{0}^{\delta}(x)+CL_{0}\delta+\left(B_{L_{0}}+\epsilon\|\Delta u_{0}^{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)t.

Then

D\overline{u}^{\epsilon,\delta}=Du_{0}^{\delta},\qquad\Delta\overline{u}^{\epsilon,\delta}=\Delta u_{0}^{\delta}.

Since

|Du_{0}^{\delta}|\leq L_{0},

we have

\displaystyle(\overline{u}^{\epsilon,\delta})_{t}+H\left(\dfrac{x}{\epsilon},\dfrac{\overline{u}^{\epsilon,\delta}}{\epsilon},D\overline{u}^{\epsilon,\delta}\right)-\epsilon\Delta\overline{u}^{\epsilon,\delta}

\displaystyle\geq B_{L_{0}}+\epsilon\|\Delta u_{0}^{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}}-B_{L_{0}}-\epsilon\|\Delta u_{0}^{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}}=0.

Thus
\overline{u}^{\epsilon,\delta}
is a supersolution. Moreover,

u_{0}(x)\leq u_{0}^{\delta}(x)+CL_{0}\delta=\overline{u}^{\epsilon,\delta}(x,0).

By the comparison principle,

u^{\epsilon}(x,t)\leq u_{0}^{\delta}(x)+CL_{0}\delta+\left(B_{L_{0}}+\epsilon\|\Delta u_{0}^{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)t.

Using

\|u_{0}^{\delta}-u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq CL_{0}\delta

and

\|\Delta u_{0}^{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{CL_{0}}{\delta},

we obtain

u^{\epsilon}(x,t)\leq u_{0}(x)+CL_{0}\delta+\left(B_{L_{0}}+\frac{C\epsilon L_{0}}{\delta}\right)t.

Similarly, define

\underline{u}^{\epsilon,\delta}(x,t):=u_{0}^{\delta}(x)-CL_{0}\delta-\left(B_{L_{0}}+\epsilon\|\Delta u_{0}^{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)t.

Then
\underline{u}^{\epsilon,\delta}
is a subsolution and

\underline{u}^{\epsilon,\delta}(x,0)\leq u_{0}(x).

By the comparison principle,

u^{\epsilon}(x,t)\geq u_{0}(x)-CL_{0}\delta-\left(B_{L_{0}}+\frac{C\epsilon L_{0}}{\delta}\right)t.

Therefore,

|u^{\epsilon}(x,t)-u_{0}(x)|\leq CL_{0}\delta+\left(B_{L_{0}}+\frac{C\epsilon L_{0}}{\delta}\right)t.

Let
(x_{\epsilon},t_{\epsilon})\to(x,0)
as
\epsilon\to 0
. Then

|u^{\epsilon}(x_{\epsilon},t_{\epsilon})-u_{0}(x)|\leq CL_{0}\delta+L_{0}|x_{\epsilon}-x|+\left(B_{L_{0}}+\frac{C\epsilon L_{0}}{\delta}\right)t_{\epsilon}.

Taking
\limsup^{*}
and
\liminf_{*}
as
\epsilon\to 0
, then letting
\delta\to 0
, we obtain

u^{*}(x,0)\leq u_{0}(x)\leq u_{*}(x,0).

Since always

u_{*}(x,0)\leq u^{*}(x,0),

we conclude that

u^{*}(x,0)=u_{*}(x,0)=u_{0}(x).

This completes the proof.
∎

Appendix C
Phase-shift contact lemma

Lemma C.1

(
Phase-shift contact lemma
)
.

Let
Q
be a compact cylinder and let
\phi\in C^{2}(Q)
. Let
\chi
be a corrector of the form

\chi(y,z)=z+w(y,z),

where
w
is bounded and periodic in
(y,z)
, and

\chi(y,z+1)=\chi(y,z)+1.

For
s\in\mathbb{R}
, define

\Phi_{s}^{\epsilon}(t,x)=\epsilon\chi\left(\frac{x}{\epsilon},\frac{\phi(t,x)}{\epsilon}+s\right).

Then

\Phi_{s+1}^{\epsilon}=\Phi_{s}^{\epsilon}+\epsilon.

Assume that
u^{*}-\phi
has a strict local maximum at
(x_{0},t_{0})
, with
t_{0}>0
. Then, after replacing
Q
by a smaller cylinder around
(x_{0},t_{0})
, for every sufficiently small
\epsilon
there exists
s_{\epsilon}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

M_{\epsilon}(s_{\epsilon}):=\max_{\overline{Q}}\left(u^{\epsilon}-\Phi_{s_{\epsilon}}^{\epsilon}\right)=0.

Moreover,
\epsilon s_{\epsilon}\to 0

as
\epsilon\to 0
, and every maximum point
(x_{\epsilon},t_{\epsilon})
of
u^{\epsilon}-\Phi_{s_{\epsilon}}^{\epsilon}
in
\overline{Q}
satisfies
(x_{\epsilon},t_{\epsilon})\to(x_{0},t_{0}).

In particular, for such a maximum point,

\frac{u^{\epsilon}(x_{\epsilon},t_{\epsilon})}{\epsilon}=\chi\left(\frac{x_{\epsilon}}{\epsilon},\frac{\phi(x_{\epsilon},t_{\epsilon})}{\epsilon}+s_{\epsilon}\right).

Proof.

Since
\chi(y,z)=z+w(y,z)
and
w
is bounded, we have
\Phi_{s}^{\epsilon}(t,x)=\phi(t,x)+\epsilon s+\epsilon w\left(\frac{x}{\epsilon},\frac{\phi(t,x)}{\epsilon}+s\right).

Hence

\|\Phi_{s}^{\epsilon}-\phi-\epsilon s\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}\leq C\epsilon.

(C.1)

Also, from
\chi(y,z+1)=\chi(y,z)+1
, we get
\Phi_{s+1}^{\epsilon}=\Phi_{s}^{\epsilon}+\epsilon.

Therefore
M_{\epsilon}(s+1)=M_{\epsilon}(s)-\epsilon.

Since
s\mapsto M_{\epsilon}(s)
is continuous, there exists
s_{\epsilon}
such that
M_{\epsilon}(s_{\epsilon})=0.

We now show that
\epsilon s_{\epsilon}\to 0
. Set

A_{\epsilon}:=\max_{\overline{Q}}(u^{\epsilon}-\phi).

By the definition of the half-relaxed limit and by the strict maximum assumption, after subtracting a constant from
\phi
if necessary, we may assume

A_{\epsilon}\to 0.

Using (
C.1
),
we obtain

A_{\epsilon}-\epsilon s-C\epsilon\leq M_{\epsilon}(s)\leq A_{\epsilon}-\epsilon s+C\epsilon.

Putting
s=s_{\epsilon}
and using
M_{\epsilon}(s_{\epsilon})=0
, we get

|\epsilon s_{\epsilon}-A_{\epsilon}|\leq C\epsilon.

Thus
\epsilon s_{\epsilon}\to 0.

Finally, since
u^{*}-\phi
has a strict local maximum at
(x_{0},t_{0})
, we may choose
Q
so small that

\sup_{\partial Q}(u^{*}-\phi)<(u^{*}-\phi)(x_{0},t_{0}).

Because
\Phi_{s_{\epsilon}}^{\epsilon}-\phi=\epsilon s_{\epsilon}+O(\epsilon)\to 0
uniformly on
Q
, the maximum points of

u^{\epsilon}-\Phi_{s_{\epsilon}}^{\epsilon}

remain in the interior of
Q
and converge to
(x_{0},t_{0})
. At a maximum point
(x_{\epsilon},t_{\epsilon})
, the identity

M_{\epsilon}(s_{\epsilon})=0

gives

u^{\epsilon}(x_{\epsilon},t_{\epsilon})=\Phi_{s_{\epsilon}}^{\epsilon}(x_{\epsilon},t_{\epsilon}).

Dividing by
\epsilon
yields the desired phase identity.
∎
