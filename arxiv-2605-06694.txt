Discontinuity at the fixed point in suprametric spaces
Nicola Fabiano

Abstract




Abstract.

The aim of this paper is to generalize some fixed point theorems in the class of convex contraction of order
m
on a complete suprametric space.
Then, we will prove that the class of convex contraction of order m is
strong enough to generate a fixed point on a complete suprametric spaces
but do not force the mapping to be continuous at the fixed point, and it can be replaced by relatively weaker
conditions of
k
-continuity or
T
-orbitally lower semi-continuous. On this way a new and distinct
solution to the open problem of Rhoades (Contemp Math 72:233-245,
1988) is found. In sequel, we will prove some fixed point results in the setting suprametric spaces which are generalizations of the results regarding Sehgal,
Ćirić and Fisher’s quasi-contraction. Some examples and application will be approved our results.

M.S.C.2000:
47H10,47H09.

Keywords:
Suprametric space, convex contraction, Sehgal’s quasi-contraction, Ćirić’s quasi-contraction, Fisher’s quasi-contraction.

1
Introduction

The concept of a suprametric space was introduced by
Berzig in
[
2
]
.

Definition 1
.

[
2
]

Let
X
be a nonempty set. A function

d:X\times X\longrightarrow[0,\infty)
is called a suprametric on

X
if it satisfies the following properties:

(1)
d(x,y)=0
if and only if
x=y
for any
x,y\in X
;

(2)
d(x,y)=d(y,x)
for any
x,y\in X
;

(3)
d(x,y)\leq d(x,z)+d(z,y)+\rho d(x,z)d(z,y)
for some constant
\rho\in[0,\infty)
and for any
x,y,z\in X
.
The pair
(X,d)
is called a suprametric space.

Example 1
.

[
2
]
Let
(X,d)
be a metric space. Then a function
\delta^{\lambda}:X\times X\to[0,\infty)
defined by

\delta^{\lambda}(x,y)=d(x,y)(d(x,y)+\lambda)\quad\mbox{ for every $x,y\in X$}

is a
suprametric on
X
with the constant
\rho=\frac{2}{\lambda}
.

Suppose that
(X,d)
is a suprametric space. Then the set
B(a,r)=\{x\in X\,|\,d(a,x)<r\}
is an open ball in
X
which is open set in the topology
\tau
contain of the family of all open subsets. This topology is Hausdorff; see
[
2
]
for more details.

Berzig in 2022 generalized the Banach–Picard–Caccioppoli contraction principle in complete suprametric spaces.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
2
]

Suppose
T
is a self-mapping of a complete suprametric space
(X,d)
. Also let
T
be a contraction; i.e. there exists a
\alpha\in[0,1)
such that
d(Tx,Ty)\leq\alpha d(x,y)
. Then it has a unique fixed point.

Convex contractions have been studied by many authors. Some fixed point theorems for convex contraction mappings on cone metric spaces proved by Alghamdi et al.. Then, these results was generalized by Ghorbanian et al. in the setting complete ordered metric spaces. Next, the concept of generalized convex contractions was introduced by Miandaragh et al. and they generalized the main results. Convex contractions are also natural extension of the
Kannan contraction and the Reich contraction. The following theorem was proved by Istrǎtescu for convex contraction mapping of type
2.

The concept of convex contractions of order 2 was introduced by Istrǎtescu

[
9
]
.

Definition 2
.

[
9
]

A continuous mapping
T:X\rightarrow X
is said to be a convex
contraction of order 2 if there exist
a,b\in(0,1)
such that for
all
x,y\in X
,

d(T^{2}x,T^{2}y)\leq ad(Tx,Ty)+bd(x,y)

where
a+b<1
.

The existence of the fixed point for convex contraction mappings
requires the continuity of the mappings in the whole domain.
The following example showed that convex contraction mapping of
order 2 is not a contraction mapping.

Example 2
.

[
9
]

Let
X=[0,1]
with the usual metric and define
T:X\rightarrow X
by
the relation

T(x)=(x^{2}+\frac{1}{2})^{2}.

It is obvious that this is
continuous and not a contraction.

If
x,y\in X
are arbitrary chosen, then

|T^{2}(x)-T^{2}(y)|=\frac{(x^{2}+y^{2})}{4}|T(x)-T(y)|+\frac{(x+y)}{8}|x-y|

Thus
T
is a convex contraction of order 2.

Istrǎtescu generalized the Banach–Picard–Caccioppoli contraction principle by introducing convexity contraction and then proved that each
convex contraction has a unique fixed point on a complete metric
space.

Theorem 1.2
.

[
9
]

Suppose
T
is a continuous self-mapping of a complete metric space
(X,d)
. Also let
T
be convex contraction of order two. Then it has a unique fixed point.

In 2017, Pant introduced the following notion of
k-continuity for the metric space.

Definition 3
.

Suppose that
(X,d)
is a metric space and
T
is a self-mapping.

T
is said to be
k
-continuous, (k = 1, 2, 3,
\cdots
) if for every
sequence
\{x_{n}\}
in
X
such that
T^{k-1}x_{n}\to z
implies that

T^{k}x_{n}\to Tz
.

Definition 4
.

[
5
]

A continuous function
T:X\rightarrow X
where
(X,d)
is a metric space is called generalized convex contraction of order
m
if there exist
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
a_{0},a_{1},...,a_{m-1}\geq 0
such that
\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}a_{j}<1
and

d(T^{m}x,T^{m}y)\leq\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}a_{j}d(T^{j}x,T^{j}y),

for each
x,y\in X
, where by
T^{j}
is called the composition of
T
by itself
j
items.

Theorem 1.3
.

[
5
]

Let
T:X\rightarrow X
be a generalized convex contraction of order
m
of a complete metric space
(X,d)
. If
T
is orbitally continuous or
k
-continuous for
k\geq 1
then
T
possesses a unique fixed point in
X
.

In 1988, B. E. Rhoades in
[
11
]
, expressed an open problem as follows:

Is there a contractive definition which is strong enough to generate a fixed point but does not
force the mapping to be continuous at the fixed point?

The purpose of this paper is to establish some fixed point theorems
defined on a complete suprametric space, and using
general contractive conditions. These results extend some previous fixed point
theorems in this field to convex contractive and more general convex
contractive conditions in the setting suprametric spaces. We prove
that the assumption of continuity condition can be replaced by a
relatively weaker condition of k-continuity under various settings.
On this way a new and distinct solution to the open problem of
Rhoades
[
11
]
is found, and some examples will be approved our results.

Convex contractions of order

In this section, we will prove some fixed point theorems for the selfmappings satisfy in convex
contractions of order
m
(
m\geq 2
), two-sided convex contractions and

These results generalize fixed point results by Bishet et al in
[
5
]
, Istrǎtescu result in
[
9
]
,

[
4
, Theorem 2.1]
, and
[
13
, Theorem 2]
and a generalization of the
Banach-Picard-Caccioppoli contraction principle in
[
1
]
.

Definition 5
.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space.
For
x\in X
,
T:X\rightarrow X
,
O(x;\infty)=\{x,Tx,T^{2}x,...\}
is called the
T
-orbit of
x
.
The mapping
T
is
orbitally continuous at
x
if for any sequence
\{x_{n}\}
in

O(x;\infty)
which is convergent to
z
, then
Tx_{n}
is convergent to
Tz
, as
n\to\infty
.
A function
f:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
is called
T
-orbitally
lower semi-continuous at x if
\{x_{n}\}
is a sequence in

O(x;\infty)
and
x_{n}\rightarrow z
implies
f(z)\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}f(x_{n})
.

Definition 6
.

The mapping
T:X\to X
on a metric space
(X,d)
obeys the condition
(C;k)
if there is a constant
k\geq 0
such that for every sequence
x_{n}\in X
,

x_{n}\to x_{0}\in X\quad\Rightarrow\quad\mathfrak{D}(x_{0})\leq k\cdot\limsup\mathfrak{D}(x_{n})\quad s.t.\quad\mathfrak{D}(x)=d(x,Tx),x\in X.

The main results in this paper is to prove the following fixed point theorem in the class of convex contraction of order
m
on a complete suprametric space. This theorem is a generalization of

[
3
, Theorem 2.1]
and
[
5
, Theorem 2.3]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
(X,d)
be a complete suprametric space with constant
\rho\geq 0
, i.e.,

d(x,y)\leq d(x,z)+d(z,y)+\rho\,d(x,z)d(z,y)\quad\text{ for all }x,y,z\in X.

(2.1)

Let
T:X\to X
satisfy for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
and non-negative constants
a_{0},\dots,a_{m-1}
with

\alpha=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}a_{i}<1

(2.2)

the convex contraction condition of order
m
:

d(T^{m}x,T^{m}y)\leq\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}a_{i}\,d(T^{i}x,T^{i}y)\quad\text{ for all }x,y\in X.

(2.3)

Then
T
possesses a unique fixed point
x^{*}\in X
. Moreover, for every
x_{0}\in X
, the Picard iteration
x_{n}=T^{n}x_{0}
converges to
x^{*}
in
(X,d)
, provided at least one of the following conditions holds:

(i)

T
is
k
-continuous for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
, i.e., for any sequence
\{y_{n}\}\subseteq X
,

T^{k-1}y_{n}\to z\quad\text{ implies }\quad T^{k}y_{n}\to Tz;

(ii)

The displacement function
\mathfrak{D}(x)=d(x,Tx)
is
T
-orbitally lower semicontinuous at
x_{0}
, i.e., for any sequence
\{y_{n}\}\subseteq O(x_{0};\infty)
with
y_{n}\to z
, we have

\mathfrak{D}(z)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathfrak{D}(y_{n});

(iii)

T
satisfies condition
(C)
: if a sequence
\{y_{n}\}\subseteq O(x_{0};\infty)
satisfies
y_{n}\to z
and
\mathfrak{D}(y_{n})\to 0
, then
Tz=z
.

Proof.

Fix an arbitrary
x_{0}\in X
and define the Picard iteration sequence
\{x_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
by
x_{n}=T^{n}x_{0}
for all
n\geq 0
.

Step 1: Bounding successive iterates.

If
x_{n}=x_{n+1}
for some
n
, then
x_{n}
is a fixed point and the proof is complete. Henceforth assume
x_{n}\neq x_{n+1}
for all
n\geq 0
. Define

\mu=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}d(x_{i},x_{i+1})>0.

(2.4)

For
n=m
, applying (
2.3
) with
x=x_{0}
and
y=x_{1}
yields

d(x_{m},x_{m+1})=d(T^{m}x_{0},T^{m}x_{1})\leq\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}a_{i}d(T^{i}x_{0},T^{i}x_{1})=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}a_{i}d(x_{i},x_{i+1})\leq\alpha\mu.

(2.5)

We claim that for all
n\geq m
,

d(x_{n},x_{n+1})\leq\alpha^{\lfloor n/m\rfloor}\mu.

(2.6)

We prove this by strong induction on
n
. The base case
n=m
holds by (
2.5
) since
\lfloor m/m\rfloor=1
. Now assume (
2.6
) holds for all integers
k
with
m\leq k<n
for some
n>m
. Then

\displaystyle d(x_{n},x_{n+1})

\displaystyle=d(T^{m}x_{n-m},T^{m}x_{n-m+1})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}a_{i}d(T^{i}x_{n-m},T^{i}x_{n-m+1})\quad\text{(by \eqref{convex})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}a_{i}d(x_{n-m+i},x_{n-m+i+1})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}a_{i}\alpha^{\lfloor(n-m+i)/m\rfloor}\mu\quad\text{(by induction hypothesis, since $n-m+i<n$)}

\displaystyle\leq\alpha^{\lfloor(n-m)/m\rfloor}\mu\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}a_{i}\quad\text{(since $\lfloor(n-m+i)/m\rfloor\geq\lfloor(n-m)/m\rfloor$)}

\displaystyle=\alpha^{\lfloor n/m\rfloor-1}\mu\cdot\alpha=\alpha^{\lfloor n/m\rfloor}\mu,

which establishes (
2.6
) for
n
. Since
\alpha<1
by (
2.2
), it follows immediately that

\lim_{n\to\infty}d(x_{n},x_{n+1})=0.

(2.7)

Step 2: The sequence
\{x_{n}\}
is Cauchy.

From (
2.6
), we have
d(x_{n},x_{n+1})\leq\mu\alpha^{\lfloor n/m\rfloor}
with
0\leq\alpha<1
. Hence the series
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}d(x_{n},x_{n+1})
converges. Define the tail sums

R_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}d(x_{k},x_{k+1}),\quad n\geq 0.

Clearly
R_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. We now show that
\{x_{n}\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
(X,d)
. Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
p\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
\Sigma_{n,p}=\sum_{k=n}^{n+p-1}d(x_{k},x_{k+1})
. Applying the suprametric inequality (
2.1
) repeatedly yields

1+\rho d(x_{n},x_{n+p})\leq\prod_{k=n}^{n+p-1}\bigl(1+\rho d(x_{k},x_{k+1})\bigr).

Taking the natural logarithm of both sides and using the elementary inequality
\ln(1+u)\leq u
for
u\geq 0
, we obtain

\ln\bigl(1+\rho d(x_{n},x_{n+p})\bigr)\leq\sum_{k=n}^{n+p-1}\ln\bigl(1+\rho d(x_{k},x_{k+1})\bigr)\leq\rho\sum_{k=n}^{n+p-1}d(x_{k},x_{k+1})=\rho\Sigma_{n,p}.

Exponentiating and rearranging gives

d(x_{n},x_{n+p})\leq\frac{e^{\rho\Sigma_{n,p}}-1}{\rho}\quad\left(\text{with }\frac{e^{\rho u}-1}{\rho}:=u\text{ if }\rho=0\right).

(2.8)

Since
\Sigma_{n,p}\leq R_{n}
, inequality (
2.8
) yields

d(x_{n},x_{n+p})\leq\frac{e^{\rho R_{n}}-1}{\rho}\quad\text{for all }p\geq 1.

Given
\varepsilon>0
, choose
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\frac{e^{\rho R_{N}}-1}{\rho}<\varepsilon
. Then for all
n\geq N
and all
p\geq 1
, we have
d(x_{n},x_{n+p})<\varepsilon
. Thus
\{x_{n}\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
(X,d)
. By completeness of
(X,d)
, there exists
x^{*}\in X
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}d(x_{n},x^{*})=0.

(2.9)

Step 3:
x^{*}
is a fixed point of
T
.
We verify this under each of the three conditions.

(i)

k
-continuity
: Since
x_{n}\to x^{*}
by (
2.9
), we have
T^{k-1}x_{n}=x_{n+k-1}\to x^{*}
. By
k
-continuity of
T
, it follows that
T^{k}x_{n}=x_{n+k}\to Tx^{*}
. But
x_{n+k}\to x^{*}
as
n\to\infty
by (
2.9
). Since suprametric spaces are Hausdorff (Berzig
[
2
]
, Proposition 1.3), limits are unique, hence
Tx^{*}=x^{*}
.

(ii)

Orbital lower semicontinuity
: The sequence
\{x_{n}\}
lies in the
T
-orbit
O(x_{0};\infty)
and converges to
x^{*}
by (
2.9
). By orbital lower semicontinuity of
\mathfrak{D}
at
x_{0}
,

\mathfrak{D}(x^{*})\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathfrak{D}(x_{n})=\liminf_{n\to\infty}d(x_{n},x_{n+1})=0,

where the last equality follows from (
2.7
). Since
\mathfrak{D}(x^{*})=d(x^{*},Tx^{*})\geq 0
, we conclude
d(x^{*},Tx^{*})=0
, i.e.,
Tx^{*}=x^{*}
.

(iii)

Condition
(C)
: From (
2.7
) we have
\mathfrak{D}(x_{n})=d(x_{n},x_{n+1})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Since
\{x_{n}\}\subseteq O(x_{0};\infty)
and
x_{n}\to x^{*}
by (
2.9
), condition
(C)
directly implies
Tx^{*}=x^{*}
.

Step 4: Uniqueness of the fixed point.
Suppose
y^{*}\in X
is another fixed point of
T
with
y^{*}\neq x^{*}
. Then
T^{i}x^{*}=x^{*}
and
T^{i}y^{*}=y^{*}
for all
i\geq 0
. Applying (
2.3
) with
x=x^{*}
and
y=y^{*}
gives

d(x^{*},y^{*})=d(T^{m}x^{*},T^{m}y^{*})\leq\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}a_{i}d(T^{i}x^{*},T^{i}y^{*})=\left(\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}a_{i}\right)d(x^{*},y^{*})=\alpha\,d(x^{*},y^{*}).

Since
\alpha<1
by (
2.2
) and
d(x^{*},y^{*})>0
(as
x^{*}\neq y^{*}
), this yields the contradiction
d(x^{*},y^{*})<d(x^{*},y^{*})
. Hence
x^{*}=y^{*}
, proving uniqueness.

Step 5: Global convergence.
The argument in Steps 1–3 shows that for
any
initial point
x_{0}\in X
, the Picard iteration
\{T^{n}x_{0}\}
converges to
some
fixed point of
T
. Step 4 establishes that
T
has exactly one fixed point
x^{*}
. Therefore
T^{n}x_{0}\to x^{*}
for every
x_{0}\in X
.

This completes the proof.
∎

3
Ćirić quasi-contraction in suprametric spaces

In this section we will generalize Ćirić and Sehal’s fixed point theorems in the setting suprametric spaces.

Many authors have defined contractive type mappings on a complete metric space
X
which are generalizations of the well-known Banach contraction, and have the property that each such mapping has a unique fixed point.
The concept of quasi-contractions, in 1983 in
[
6
]
, is defined by
Ćirić in metric spaces as follows:

The selfmapping
T:X\to X
is said to be quasi-contraction if
for each
x\in X
there exists
a positive integer
n=n(x)
and
\lambda\in[0,1)
such that

d(T^{n}x,T^{n}y)\leq\lambda\cdot\max\Big\{d(x,y),d(x,Ty),\dots,d(x,T^{n}y),d(x,T^{n}x)\Big\},

(3.10)

holds for all
y\in X
.

In 1970, Guseman
[
8
]
generalized the result of Sehgal to mappings which
are both necessarily continuous and have a contractive iterate at each point
in a (possibly proper) subset of the space. In 1983, Ćirić
[
6
]
, among other
things, proved the following interesting generalization of Sehgal result.

In the following result, we will give a generalization of Ćirić’s theorem in
[
6
]
in the setting suprametric space.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(X,d)
be a complete suprametric space with constant
\rho\geq 0
, i.e.,

d(x,y)\leq d(x,z)+d(z,y)+\rho\,d(x,z)d(z,y)\quad\text{ for all }x,y,z\in X.

(3.11)

Suppose
T:X\to X
satisfies: for each
x\in X
, there exist a positive integer
n(x)
and a constant
\lambda\in[0,1)
such that for all
y\in X
,

d(T^{n(x)}x,T^{n(x)}y)\leq\lambda\cdot M(x,y),

(3.12)

where

M(x,y)=\max\Big\{d(x,T^{i}y):0\leq i\leq n(x)\Big\}\cup\Big\{d(x,T^{j}x):0\leq j\leq n(x)\Big\}.

(3.13)

Then
T
possesses a unique fixed point
x^{*}\in X
. Moreover, for every
x_{0}\in X
, the Picard iteration
x_{n}=T^{n}x_{0}
converges to
x^{*}
in
(X,d)
.

Proof.

Fix an arbitrary
x_{0}\in X
. We proceed in four steps: (1) boundedness of the orbit, (2) Cauchy property via metric equivalence, (3) fixed point verification, and (4) uniqueness.

Step 1: The orbit
\mathcal{O}(x_{0})=\{T^{k}x_{0}:k\geq 0\}
is
d
-bounded.

Define the finite set of initial iterates

F=\{x_{0},Tx_{0},T^{2}x_{0},\dots,T^{m-1}x_{0}\},\quad\text{where }m=\max\{n(z):z\in F\}.

(3.14)

Such an
m
exists because
F
is finite and
n(\cdot)
takes positive integer values. Set

C_{0}=\max\Big\{d(z,T^{j}z):z\in F,\ 0\leq j\leq m\Big\}<\infty.

(3.15)

We claim that for all
k\geq 0
,

d(x_{0},T^{k}x_{0})\leq C:=\frac{C_{0}(1+\rho C_{0})}{1-\lambda}<\infty.

(3.16)

We prove this by strong induction on
k
. The claim holds for
0\leq k<m

Assume (

\displaystyle d(x_{0},T^{k}x_{0})

\displaystyle\leq C+\lambda\big[C_{0}(1+\rho C)+C\big]+\rho C\lambda\big[C_{0}(1+\rho C)+C\big]

\displaystyle=C+\lambda C_{0}(1+\rho C)+\lambda C+\rho\lambda CC_{0}(1+\rho C)+\rho\lambda C^{2}

Fisher’s quasi-contraction in suprametric spaces

In this section we will prove some fixed point results in the setting suprametric spaces which are generalizations of the results regarding Ćirić and Sehgal Fisher’s quasi-contraction.

In 1979, Fisher in
[
7
]
extended the
definition of Ćirić quasi-contraction as follow and he show if
T
is a continuous mapping from complete metric space
(X,d)
into itself satisfying the inequality

d(T^{p}x,T^{q}y)\leq\lambda\cdot\max\Big\{d(T^{r}x,T^{s}y),d(T^{r}x,T^{r^{\prime}}x),d(T^{s}y,T^{s^{\prime}}y):0\leq r,r^{\prime}\leq p,0\leq s,s^{\prime}\leq q\Big\}

for some fixed positive integers
p
and
q
and constant
\lambda\in(0,1)
, then
T
has a unique fixed point.
Also, it is shown that the condition that
T
be continuous is unnecessary if
q
(or
p
) =1.

Now, we study Fisher’s results in the setting of a suprametric space.

The following theorem extends this result in the framework of suprametric spaces.

(X,d)

k

5
Application

In this section, we present an application of our main results to the existence and uniqueness of solutions for a Fredholm integral equation.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
K:[a,b]\times[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous kernel such that
|K(x,t)|\leq M
for all
x,t\in[a,b]
. Consider the integral equation

f(x)=\int_{a}^{b}K(x,t)f(t)\,dt,\quad x\in[a,b].

(5.32)

If
L=M(b-a)<1
, then (
5.32
) has a unique solution in
C[a,b]
.

Proof.

Let
X=C[a,b]
be equipped with the supremum norm
\|f\|_{\infty}=\sup_{x\in[a,b]}|f(x)|
. Fix
\lambda>0
and define
d:X\times X\to[0,\infty)
by

d(f,g)=\|f-g\|_{\infty}\bigl(\|f-g\|_{\infty}+\lambda\bigr).

By Example
1
,
(X,d)
is a complete suprametric space with constant
\rho=2/\lambda
.

Define the operator
T:X\to X
by
(Tf)(x)=\int_{a}^{b}K(x,t)f(t)\,dt
. Since
K
is continuous,
T
maps
C[a,b]
into itself. For any
f,g\in X
, we estimate

\|Tf-Tg\|_{\infty}\leq\sup_{x\in[a,b]}\int_{a}^{b}|K(x,t)|\,|f(t)-g(t)|\,dt\leq M(b-a)\|f-g\|_{\infty}=L\|f-g\|_{\infty}.

Iterating this bound yields
\|T^{2}f-T^{2}g\|_{\infty}\leq L^{2}\|f-g\|_{\infty}
. We now verify that
T
satisfies the convex contraction condition of order
m=2
from Theorem
2.1
. Set
a_{0}=L^{2}
and
a_{1}=0
. Since
L<1
, we have
a_{0}+a_{1}=L^{2}<1
. Then,

\displaystyle d(T^{2}f,T^{2}g)

\displaystyle=\|T^{2}f-T^{2}g\|_{\infty}\bigl(\|T^{2}f-T^{2}g\|_{\infty}+\lambda\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq L^{2}\|f-g\|_{\infty}\bigl(L^{2}\|f-g\|_{\infty}+\lambda\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq L^{2}\|f-g\|_{\infty}\bigl(\|f-g\|_{\infty}+\lambda\bigr)

\displaystyle=a_{0}d(f,g)+a_{1}d(Tf,Tg).

Thus,
T
is a convex contraction of order
2
on the complete suprametric space
(X,d)
. Furthermore, the boundedness of
K
implies that
T
is Lipschitz continuous, and hence
T
is
k
-continuous for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
(satisfying condition (i) of Theorem
2.1
). By invoking Theorem
2.1
,
T
possesses a unique fixed point
f^{*}\in X
, which corresponds to the unique solution of the integral equation (
5.32
).
∎
