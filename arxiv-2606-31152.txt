A consistent-splitting generalized scalar auxiliary variable scheme for the perturbed Boussinesq system
M Nader Alhomsi, Jiahong Wu, Xiaoming Zheng

Abstract
We propose and analyze a second-order consistent-splitting scheme, based on the generalized scalar auxiliary variable (GSAV) approach, for the two-dimensional perturbed Boussinesq system. The system is obtained by subtracting a stable, linearly-stratified hydrostatic equilibrium from the standard Boussinesq system. The time discretization extends the consistent-splitting generalized BDF2 framework of Huang and Shen [ 17 ] for the Navier–Stokes equations, treating the nonlinear convection and advection together with the linear buoyancy and stratification couplings explicitly, so that each time step reduces to a small number of decoupled linear systems. We prove an unconditional weak stability theorem for the GSAV scheme and derive optimal second-order error estimates for the velocity, pressure, and temperature. A careful tracing reveals that the error constant depends on the inverse viscosity and inverse thermal diffusivity through a quadruply-nested exponential, so the scheme is not robust as either tends to zero. Numerical experiments confirm the second-order convergence and reproduce the expected internal-wave dynamics and exponential relaxation toward hydrostatic balance in a long-time stratified-flow simulation.



Keywords:
Perturbed Boussinesq system, Generalized scalar auxiliary variable (GSAV), Consistent-splitting scheme, Unconditional stability, Robustness on viscosity, Stratified flow.

Introduction

Buoyancy-driven flows lie at the heart of countless geophysical and engineering processes, from atmospheric circulation and oceanic dynamics to convective heat transfer, internal wave propagation, and mantle convection
[
13
,
27
,
24
,
35
,
32
]
.
A widely-used mathematical model for such phenomena is the Boussinesq approximation, in which density variations are assumed small and are retained only through the gravity-coupled buoyancy term, while the velocity field remains divergence-free.
Stably stratified environments—such as the atmosphere, the ocean thermocline, and stellar interiors—support hydrostatic equilibria in which a vertical temperature gradient balances gravity.
In this work, we adopt the classical linearly-stratified equilibrium, with constant lapse rate measured by the (squared) Brunt–Väisälä frequency
[
13
,
35
]
.
Subtracting this equilibrium from the full Boussinesq system yields the
perturbed
Boussinesq system studied here, in which buoyancy drives vertical motion in the momentum equation and vertical motion advects the linear background gradient as a constant-coefficient stratification term in the temperature equation.
This coupling is the source of internal gravity waves and supports the relaxation of disturbances toward hydrostatic balance
[
32
,
35
]
.

To make this precise, let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a bounded domain with sufficiently smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
.
Denote the velocity vector as
\mathbf{U}
, the pressure as
P
, and the
temperature as
\Theta
. The full Boussinesq system is

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathbf{U}+\mathbf{U}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{U}

\displaystyle=-\nabla P+\nu\,\Delta\mathbf{U}+\Theta\,\mathbf{e}_{2},\qquad\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\Omega,\ t>0,

(1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\Theta+\mathbf{U}\cdot\nabla\Theta

\displaystyle=\gamma\,\Delta\Theta,

(2)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\mathbf{U}

\displaystyle=0,

(3)

where
\nu>0
is the kinematic viscosity and
\gamma>0
is the thermal
diffusivity, and
\mathbf{e}_{2}
is the unit vector in the vertical direction.
Denote a hydrostatic equilibrium as

(\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{he}},\,P_{\mathrm{he}},\,\Theta_{\mathrm{he}})=\Bigl(0,\;\int_{0}^{x_{2}}\mathcal{T}(z)dz,\;\mathcal{T}(x_{2})=\alpha x_{2}+\overline{\theta}\Bigr),

(4)

where
\overline{\theta}=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\Theta dx_{1}dx_{2}
is the spatial average.
Define the perturbation as

(\mathbf{u},\,p,\,\theta)=(\mathbf{U}-\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{he}},\;P-P_{\mathrm{he}},\;\Theta-\Theta_{\mathrm{he}}).

(5)

Substituting the perturbation decomposition (
5
) into the full system (
1
)–(
3
) yields the perturbed Boussinesq system for
\mathbf{u}=(u,v)
,

, and
\theta

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u}+\nabla p-\nu\Delta\mathbf{u}=\theta\,\mathbf{e}_{2}+\mathbf{f},\\[5.69054pt]
\partial_{t}\theta+\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\theta+\alpha\,v-\gamma\Delta\theta=g,\\[2.84526pt]
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}=0,\end{cases}

(6)

The Dirichlet boundary conditions are imposed:

\mathbf{u}=0
,
\theta=0
on
\partial\Omega
.

The mathematical analysis of the Boussinesq system has attracted intense interest. The fully dissipative system in two space dimensions—with both kinematic viscosity and thermal diffusion—is globally well-posed and develops no finite-time singularities; the substantial difficulties arise once the dissipation is reduced. In the partial-dissipation setting, Hou and Li
[
16
]
established global well-posedness of the 2D system with full velocity viscosity but zero thermal diffusivity, and Chae
[
7
]
proved global regularity when either the kinematic viscosity or the thermal diffusivity is absent. Subsequent works substantially extended these results to systems with only anisotropic or directional dissipation. For vertical dissipation, Adhikari, Cao, and Wu
[
1
,
2
]
obtained global regularity and well-posedness results for the 2D Boussinesq equations with vertical viscosity and vertical diffusivity, and Cao and Wu
[
5
]
proved global regularity for the 2D anisotropic Boussinesq equations with only vertical dissipation. For horizontal dissipation, Danchin and Paicu
[
9
]
constructed global solutions for the anisotropic Boussinesq system in two dimensions in which the diffusion acts in the horizontal direction in only one equation, exploiting the fact that the dissipation occurs in a direction perpendicular to the buoyancy force. We refer to these works and the references therein for the now-extensive theory of partially dissipative Boussinesq systems.

A second line of investigation, which is the one directly relevant to the perturbed system (
6
), concerns the stability of small perturbations of a stable hydrostatic equilibrium and the rate at which they decay to zero. Doering, Wu, Zhao, and Zheng
[
10
]
initiated a quantitative study of the long-time behavior of the 2D Boussinesq equations
without
buoyancy diffusion and demonstrated algebraic decay of the velocity perturbation. Tao, Wu, Zhao, and Zheng
[
33
]

subsequently solved several problems left open in
[
10
]
and established the nonlinear stability of perturbations near hydrostatic equilibrium together with explicit algebraic decay rates. Closely related stability and exponential-decay
results in the anisotropic setting have been obtained by Dong, Wu, Xu, and Zhu
[
11
]
for the 2D Boussinesq equations with only horizontal dissipation, and by Ji, Yan, and Wu
[
21
]
who derived
optimal
decay rates for the 3D anisotropic Boussinesq system near the hydrostatic balance. Earlier
H^{1}
-stability results
with partial dissipation are due to Ji, Li, Wei, and
Wu
[
20
]
. In a different but related direction, Castro, Córdoba, and Lear
[
6
]
proved asymptotic
stability of stratified solutions of the 2D Boussinesq equations with a velocity damping term. These works collectively establish that, under suitable dissipation, smooth perturbations of a stable hydrostatic
equilibrium remain regular for all time and decay to zero, with algebraic or exponential rates depending on the dissipation structure. We emphasize, however, that each of the references above treats a
partial
or
anisotropic
dissipation regime, whereas the
system (
6
) considered in the present paper is

fully dissipative
, with full kinematic viscosity

\nu\Delta\mathbf{u}
and full thermal diffusion
\gamma\Delta\theta
, posed on a bounded two-dimensional domain with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions. For this fully dissipative case, exponential decay of unforced perturbations to zero is proved in Lemma
2
. The present paper verifies this analytic theory in Section
5
.

On the numerical side, the Boussinesq system inherits the classical difficulties of incompressible Navier-Stokes solvers: the divergence-free constraint and the resulting saddle-point structure. The direct monolithic time discretization solves a nonlinear, coupled velocity-pressure system at each time step, whose numerical methods can be found in a review paper
[
12
]
.
In contrast, the splitting schemes divide the time step into sequential stages. The projection methods, originating with the work of Chorin
[
8
]
and Temam
[
34
]
, decouple velocity and pressure through a Helmholtz projection; we refer to
[
14
]
for a comprehensive overview and
[
39
]
for recent development in an iterative projection method. The projection methods typically fail to achieve optimal accuracy due to inconsistent boundary conditions imposed on the pressure or velocity
[
14
]
.
In contrast, gauge formulations
[
37
]
and consistent-splitting schemes
[
15
,
23
,
31
,
17
,
18
,
3
]

retain the optimal second-order accuracy while keeping the algebraic cost per step low.

The scalar auxiliary variable (SAV) approach, introduced by Shen, Xu, and Yang
[
29
,
30
]
for gradient flows, augments the system with a scalar variable updated to mimic the continuous energy law, yielding unconditional stability of a modified discrete energy at the cost of only a few decoupled linear solves per time step.
Huang and Shen
[
19
]
subsequently introduced a new class of SAV schemes for general dissipative systems, referred to in the literature as GSAV, a framework that covers the perturbed Boussinesq system (
6
).
The same authors then combined the GSAV method with an innovative time-shifted second-order BDF consistent-splitting scheme (with shift parameter
k
) for the Navier–Stokes equations
[
17
]
, which forms the foundation of the present work.

Several recent papers have applied SAV-type approaches to the Boussinesq system.
Zhang, Yuan, and Chen
[
38
]
proposed a fully decoupled first-order SAV pressure-correction scheme and established rigorous error estimates.
Li, Li, and Luo
[
25
]
analyzed a two-step BDF2 scheme based on the exponential SAV (E-SAV) variant, which decouples velocity from temperature.
Jiang and Yang
[
22
]
developed stabilized SAV-BDF2 and Crank–Nicolson–leapfrog
ensemble
schemes for the evolutionary Boussinesq equations.
Most directly comparable to the present work, Wagner, Wohlmuth, and Zawallich
[
36
]
extended the second-order consistent-splitting GSAV-BDF2 (with time shift
k
) framework of Huang and Shen
[
17
]
from the Navier–Stokes equations to the standard Boussinesq equation, employing an exponential time integrator for the auxiliary-variable update and two independent extrapolation widths for velocity and temperature; their scheme is reformulated for an
H^{1}
-conforming finite element discretization (e.g., Taylor–Hood), with two-dimensional error analysis and three-dimensional numerical experiments.

Compared with these existing works, the present paper differs in three essential respects.
First, all four of the above references analyze the
standard
Boussinesq system, whereas the present paper analyzes the
perturbed
Boussinesq system (
6
) carrying a linear stratification coupling
\alpha v
in the temperature equation.
Second, among these works only
[
36
]
combines the GSAV approach with the time-shifted second-order BDF consistent-splitting framework of
[
17
]
; we adopt the same time-stepping framework but retain the standard GSAV update as in
[
17
]
.
Third, our error analysis explicitly tracks the dependence of the final error constant on the viscosity
\nu
and the thermal diffusivity
\gamma
, and shows that this constant contains negative powers of
\nu
and
\gamma
(see Remark
3
); consequently, the explicit treatment of the convection terms adopted here may not be robust in the vanishing-viscosity limit
\nu,\gamma\to 0^{+}
, and a fully implicit or stabilized treatment of the convection would be required to obtain bounds uniform in
\nu
and
\gamma
.
The combination of (i) the perturbed Boussinesq system with its additional stratification coupling, (ii) a consistent extrapolation of all coupling terms at the BDF time level
t^{n+k}
that preserves full second-order accuracy in both the velocity and temperature equations, and (iii) an explicit accounting of the
\nu^{-1}
and
\gamma^{-1}
dependence of the error constant is, to the best of our knowledge, new.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows.
Section

collects preliminary lemmas, including the energy law for the continuous Boussinesq system and the exponential decay of its energy in the unforced case.
Section
3
introduces the proposed consistent-splitting GSAV scheme and establishes the weak stability theorem.
Section
4
carries out the error analysis.
Section

reports the numerical results.
Section

n
, such that

\nu\delta t\sum_{j=0}^{n}\xi^{j}\|\nabla\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{\,j}\|^{2}+\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}\delta t\sum_{j=0}^{n}\xi^{j}\|\nabla\overline{\theta}^{\,j}\|^{2}+\|\mathbf{u}^{n}\|+\|\theta^{n}\|+r^{n}\leq M_{T},

(35)

for all
1\leq n\leq N
.

Remark 2
.

The proof uses only the GSAV relations (
31
)–(
33
), and does not invoke the discrete update equations (
28
), (
29
), or (
30
). The result is therefore independent of the spatial and temporal discretizations and holds for any time step
0<\delta t<\delta t_{\max}
.
The bound is termed
weak
because it remains trivially true when
\|\overline{\bm{u}}^{n}\|
becomes unbounded: in such a blow-up scenario,
r^{n}
,
\xi^{n}
, and
\eta^{n}
all vanish, and the inequality (
35
) reduces to
0\leq M_{T}
. Blow-up of this kind is indeed observed for the Navier–Stokes equations in
[
3
]
.

Proof.

Part (a).
First,
r^{1}=E(\mathbf{u}^{1},\theta^{1})+\overline{C}\geq\overline{C}\geq 1>0
based on the choice of
\overline{C}
.
Assume
r^{n}\geq 0
for any
1\leq n\leq N-1
.
By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and
Young’s inequality with the choice
(
34
), one obtains

\left|\frac{\delta t\,(\mathbf{f}^{n+1},\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{\,n+1})}{\overline{E}^{n+1}+\overline{C}}\right|\leq\frac{1}{4},\qquad\left|\frac{\frac{\delta t}{\alpha}(g^{n+1},\overline{\theta}^{\,n+1})}{\overline{E}^{n+1}+\overline{C}}\right|\leq\frac{1}{4},

(36)

and, without the factor
\delta t
,

\left|\frac{(\mathbf{f}^{n+1},\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{\,n+1})}{\overline{E}^{n+1}+\overline{C}}\right|\leq\frac{1}{4},\qquad\left|\frac{\frac{1}{\alpha}(g^{n+1},\overline{\theta}^{\,n+1})}{\overline{E}^{n+1}+\overline{C}}\right|\leq\frac{1}{4}.

(37)

Rearranging (
31
)
gives

r^{n+1}=\frac{r^{n}}{1+\delta t\,D^{n+1}},

(38)

where

D^{n+1}=\frac{\nu\|\nabla\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{\,n+1}\|^{2}+\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}\|\nabla\overline{\theta}^{\,n+1}\|^{2}}{\overline{E}^{n+1}+\overline{C}}-\frac{(\mathbf{f}^{n+1},\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{\,n+1})+\frac{1}{\alpha}(g^{n+1},\overline{\theta}^{\,n+1})}{\overline{E}^{n+1}+\overline{C}}.

(39)

Applying (
36
)
to the forcing part of
D^{n+1}
:

\delta t\,D^{n+1}\geq 0-\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{4}=-\frac{1}{2},

so
1+\delta t\,D^{n+1}\geq\frac{1}{2}>0
.
Therefore
r^{n+1}\geq 0
. By induction,
r^{n}\geq 0
for all
1\leq n\leq N
.
Because
\overline{E}^{n}+\overline{C}\geq\overline{C}\geq 1
,
we have

\xi^{n}=r^{n}/(\overline{E}^{n}+\overline{C})\geq 0
for all
1\leq n\leq N
.

Part (b).
Rewriting (
31
)
in increment form yields, for
1\leq j\leq N-1
:

r^{j+1}-r^{j}=-\delta t\,\xi^{j+1}\mathcal{L}^{j+1}+\delta t\,\xi^{j+1}\!\left((\mathbf{f}^{j+1},\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{\,j+1})+\frac{1}{\alpha}(g^{j+1},\overline{\theta}^{\,j+1})\right),

(40)

where the dissipative term

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{j+1}\triangleq\nu\|\nabla\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{\,j+1}\|^{2}+\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}\|\nabla\overline{\theta}^{\,j+1}\|^{2}\geq 0.

Summing up over
j=1,\cdots,n-1
and dropping the dissipative terms yields

\displaystyle r^{n}-r^{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\delta t\,\xi^{n}\Big((\mathbf{f}^{n},\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{\,n})+\frac{1}{\alpha}(g^{n},\overline{\theta}^{\,n})\Big)+\delta t\sum_{j=2}^{n-1}\xi^{j}\Big((\mathbf{f}^{j},\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{\,j})+\frac{1}{\alpha}(g^{j},\overline{\theta}^{\,j})\Big)

(41)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta t\,r^{n}\frac{\Big((\mathbf{f}^{n},\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{\,n})+\frac{1}{\alpha}(g^{n},\overline{\theta}^{\,n})\Big)}{\overline{E}^{n}+\overline{C}}+\delta t\,\sum_{j=2}^{n-1}r^{j}\frac{\Big((\mathbf{f}^{j},\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{\,j})+\frac{1}{\alpha}(g^{j},\overline{\theta}^{\,j})\Big)}{\overline{E}^{j}+\overline{C}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}r^{n}+\frac{\delta t}{2}\sum_{j=2}^{n-1}r^{j}.

By the Grönwall Lemma
4
, it implies that

r^{n}\leq 2\exp(T)\cdot r^{1}\triangleq C_{T}\cdot r^{1},\qquad 1\leq n\leq N.

(42)

Summing (
40
) over
j=1,\ldots,n
:

\delta t\sum_{j=1}^{n}\xi^{j+1}\mathcal{L}^{j+1}=r^{1}-r^{n+1}+\delta t\sum_{j=1}^{n}\xi^{j+1}\!\left((\mathbf{f}^{j+1},\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{\,j+1})+\frac{1}{\alpha}(g^{j+1},\overline{\theta}^{\,j+1})\right).

(43)

Using the estimate (
37
),
the identity

\xi^{j+1}(\overline{E}^{j+1}+\overline{C})=r^{j+1}\geq 0
,
and the bound (
42
)
yields

\delta t\sum_{j=1}^{n}\xi^{j+1}\left(|(\mathbf{f}^{j+1},\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{\,j+1})|+\frac{1}{\alpha}|(g^{j+1},\overline{\theta}^{\,j+1})|\right)\leq\frac{\delta t}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}r^{j+1}\leq\frac{T}{2}\cdot C_{T}r^{1}.

Substituting into (
43
) and using
r^{n+1}\geq 0
implies

\nu\delta t\sum_{j=1}^{n}\xi^{j+1}\|\nabla\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{\,j+1}\|^{2}+\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}\delta t\sum_{j=1}^{n}\xi^{j+1}\|\nabla\overline{\theta}^{\,j+1}\|^{2}\leq r^{1}\!\left(1+\frac{T}{2}C_{T}\right)\triangleq D_{T}.

(44)

From Part (a), (
42
), and

\overline{E}^{n+1}+\overline{C}\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{\,n+1}\|^{2}+\overline{C}
, one obtains

0\leq\xi^{n+1}=\frac{r^{n+1}}{\overline{E}^{n+1}+\overline{C}}\leq\frac{C_{T}\,r^{1}}{\frac{1}{2}\|\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{\,n+1}\|^{2}+\overline{C}}\leq\frac{2C_{T}\,r^{1}}{\|\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{\,n+1}\|^{2}+2},

(45)

where
\overline{C}\geq 1
is used in the last step.
Similarly, from

\overline{E}^{n+1}+\overline{C}\geq\frac{1}{2\alpha}\|\overline{\theta}^{\,n+1}\|^{2}+\overline{C}
, it follows that

0\leq\xi^{n+1}\leq\frac{2\alpha C_{T}r^{1}}{\|\overline{\theta}^{\,n+1}\|^{2}+2\alpha}.

(46)

Since
\eta^{n+1}=\xi^{n+1}(2-\xi^{n+1})
and
\xi^{n+1}\geq 0
,
we have

|\eta^{n+1}|=\xi^{n+1}|2-\xi^{n+1}|\leq\xi^{n+1}(2+\xi^{n+1}).

(47)

Combining (
33
), (
47
), and (
45
) yields

\|\mathbf{u}^{n+1}\|=\|\eta^{n+1}\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{\,n+1}\|\leq\xi^{n+1}(2+\xi^{n+1})\|\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{\,n+1}\|\leq\frac{2C_{T}r^{1}}{\|\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{\,n+1}\|^{2}+2}\!\left(2+\frac{2C_{T}r^{1}}{\|\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{\,n+1}\|^{2}+2}\right)\|\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{\,n+1}\|.

(48)

Setting
s=\|\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{\,n+1}\|\geq 0

and noting that the function

h(s)=\frac{s}{s^{2}+2}
satisfies

h(s)\leq\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}
for all
s\geq 0

(the maximum is attained at
s=\sqrt{2}
),
we obtain

\|\mathbf{u}^{n+1}\|\leq 2C_{T}\,r^{1}\cdot\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\cdot\!\left(2+C_{T}\,r^{1}\right)=\frac{C_{T}\,r^{1}}{\sqrt{2}}\cdot\!\left(2+C_{T}\,r^{1}\right)\triangleq M_{T,u}.

(49)

Similarly, we can get from
\theta^{n+1}=\eta^{n+1}\overline{\theta}^{\,n+1}
that

\|\theta^{n+1}\|\leq\xi^{n+1}(2+\xi^{n+1})\|\overline{\theta}^{\,n+1}\|.

Setting
y=\|\overline{\theta}^{\,n+1}\|
and
substituting (
46
),
we use
\frac{y}{y^{2}+2\alpha}\leq\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2\alpha}}

(maximum at
y=\sqrt{2\alpha}
) to get

\|\theta^{n+1}\|\leq 2\alpha C_{T}\,r^{1}\cdot\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2\alpha}}\cdot\!\left(2+\alpha C_{T}\,r^{1}\right)=\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\,C_{T}\,r^{1}(2+\alpha C_{T}r^{1})\triangleq M_{T,\theta}.

(50)

Define

M_{T}=D_{T}+M_{T,u}+M_{T,\theta}+C_{T}\,r^{1}+\nu\,\delta t_{\max}\sum_{j=0}^{1}\xi^{j}\|\nabla\overline{\bf u}^{j}\|^{2}+\frac{\gamma\,\delta t_{\max}}{\alpha}\sum_{j=0}^{1}\xi^{j}\|\nabla\overline{\theta}^{j}\|^{2}.

(51)

Here,
\overline{\bf u}^{0}
,
\overline{\bf u}^{1}
,

\overline{\theta}^{0}
,
\overline{\theta}^{1}
are defined as the initial condition and the solution at the first time step respectively, and
\xi^{0}=\xi^{1}=1
.
Collecting (
42
),
(
44
),
(
49
),
and (
50
)
establishes (
35
).
The constants
C_{T}
,
D_{T}
,
M_{T,u}
,

M_{T,\theta}
, and
M_{T}
are independent of
\delta t
and
n
.
This completes the proof.

∎

\displaystyle\;2k\Lambda^{u}\,\delta t\sum_{i=0}^{m}\|\nabla\overline{\mathbf{e}}^{\,i}\|^{2}+2kC_{\mathrm{abs}}^{\theta}\,\delta t\sum_{i=0}^{m}H_{\theta}^{\,i}\,\|\nabla\overline{\mathbf{e}}^{\,i}\|^{2}+2kC_{\mathrm{Gr}}^{u}\,\delta t\sum_{i=0}^{m}\|\widetilde{e}_{\theta}^{\,i}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+2k\bigl(C_{\mathrm{Gr}}^{u}+4(k+1)^{2}C_{\mathrm{reg}}^{u}C_{\mathrm{abs}}^{\theta}\bigr)\,\delta t\sum_{i=0}^{m}\|\nabla\overline{e}_{\theta}^{\,i}\|^{2}+2k\bigl(C_{2,\mathrm{trunc}}^{u}+C_{2,\mathrm{trunc}}^{\theta}\bigr)\delta t^{4}.

(181)

Define

\Phi_{i}=\|\nabla\overline{\mathbf{e}}^{\,i}\|^{2}+\|\nabla\overline{e}_{\theta}^{\,i}\|^{2},\quad\Psi_{i}=4\nu\|\Delta\overline{\mathbf{e}}^{\,i}\|^{2}+4\gamma\|\Delta\overline{e}_{\theta}^{\,i}\|^{2}.

(182)

Note

\|\widetilde{e}_{\theta}^{\,i}\|^{2}\leq 2(k+1)^{2}(\|\overline{e}_{\theta}^{\,i}\|^{2}+\|\overline{e}_{\theta}^{\,i-1}\|^{2})\leq 2C^{2}(k+1)^{2}(\|\nabla\overline{e}_{\theta}^{\,i}\|^{2}+\|\nabla\overline{e}_{\theta}^{\,i-1}\|^{2}),

where the Poincare inequality is used in the last step.
Then (
181
) takes the Grönwall form

\Phi_{m+1}+\delta t\sum_{i=1}^{m+1}\Psi_{i}\leq\delta t\sum_{i=0}^{m}G_{i}^{*}\,\Phi_{i}+C_{2}^{*}\,\delta t^{4},

(183)

where

\displaystyle G_{i}^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2kC_{\mathrm{abs}}^{\theta}\,H_{\theta}^{\,i}+\Lambda^{*}_{\mathrm{fix}},\qquad C_{2}^{*}=2k\bigl(C_{2,\mathrm{trunc}}^{u}+C_{2,\mathrm{trunc}}^{\theta}\bigr),

(184)

\displaystyle\Lambda^{*}_{\mathrm{fix}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2k\Lambda^{u}+8k(k+1)^{2}C_{\mathrm{Gr}}^{u}C^{2}+2k(C_{\mathrm{Gr}}^{u}+4(k+1)^{2}C_{\mathrm{reg}}^{u}C_{\mathrm{abs}}^{\theta}).

(185)

By (
180
), the Grönwall coefficient satisfies the bound

\delta t\sum_{i=0}^{m}G_{i}^{*}\leq\Lambda_{*}^{\mathrm{fix}}T+2kC_{\mathrm{abs}}^{\theta}\,M_{\theta}=:M_{*}.

(186)

Then Grönwall Lemma
4
gives

\Phi_{m+1}+\delta t\sum_{i=1}^{m+1}\Psi_{i}\leq\exp(M_{*})\,C_{2}^{*}\delta t^{4}
,

\forall 1\leq m\leq n
.
Setting

C_{2}=\exp(M_{*})\,C_{2}^{*},

(187)

we obtain

\|\nabla\overline{\mathbf{e}}^{\,m+1}\|^{2}+\|\nabla\overline{e}_{\theta}^{\,m+1}\|^{2}+4\delta t\sum_{i=1}^{m+1}(\nu\|\Delta\overline{\mathbf{e}}^{\,i}\|^{2}+\gamma\|\Delta\overline{e}_{\theta}^{\,i}\|^{2})\leq C_{2}\delta t^{4},\quad 1\leq m\leq n.

(188)

From

\|\nabla\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{\,m+1}\|^{2}\leq 2\|\nabla\overline{\mathbf{e}}^{\,m+1}\|^{2}+2\|\nabla\mathbf{u}(t^{m+1})\|^{2}
,
(
188
),
and (
88
), one deduces

\|\nabla\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{\,m+1}\|^{2}\leq 2C_{2}\delta t^{4}+2C_{1}^{u}\leq 2C_{2}+2C_{1}^{u}\leq C_{3},\quad 1\leq m\leq n,

(189)

where
C_{3}
is defined as

C_{3}=\max\{2C_{2}+2C^{u}_{1},2C_{2}+2C^{\theta}_{1}\}.

(190)

Here, the condition
\delta t\leq\frac{1}{1+2C_{0}^{2}}<1
is applied in (
189
).
Similarly, one derives

\|\nabla\overline{\theta}^{\,m+1}\|^{2}\leq C_{3},\quad 1\leq m\leq n.

(191)

4.3
Proof Step 3: Estimate for

The goal of this step is to close the induction by proving

and derive the error bounds for the unbarred velocity and temperature, and the pressure.

4.3.1
Step 3A. The SAV error equation.

Define the auxiliary error

s^{\,i}=r^{\,i}-r(t^{i}).

(192)

Note there exists a general relation

r(t^{i+1})-r(t^{i})=\delta t\,r_{t}(t^{i+1})+T^{i},

(193)

where the remainder is

T^{i}=r(t^{i+1})-r(t^{i})-\delta t\,r_{t}(t^{i+1})=-\int_{t^{i}}^{t^{i+1}}(s-t^{i})\,r_{tt}(s)\,ds.

(194)

Recall
r(t)=E({\bf u}(t),\theta(t))+\bar{C}
from (
23
) and Lemma
1
implies

r_{t}(t)=-\nu\|\nabla\mathbf{u}(t)\|^{2}-\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}\|\nabla\theta(t)\|^{2}+(\mathbf{f}(t),\mathbf{u}(t))+\frac{1}{\alpha}(g(t),\theta(t)).

(195)

Differentiating
r(t)=E({\bf u}(t),\theta(t))+\bar{C}
twice in time gives

r_{tt}(t)=\|\mathbf{u}_{t}(t)\|^{2}+(\mathbf{u}(t),\mathbf{u}_{tt}(t))+\frac{1}{\alpha}\bigl(\|\theta_{t}(t)\|^{2}+(\theta(t),\theta_{tt}(t))\bigr).

(196)

Substituting (
196
) into (
194
)
and applying Cauchy–Schwarz to the inner products,

|T^{i}|\leq\delta t\int_{t^{i}}^{t^{i+1}}\!\left(\|\mathbf{u}_{t}(s)\|^{2}+\|\mathbf{u}(s)\|\|\mathbf{u}_{tt}(s)\|+\frac{1}{\alpha}\|\theta_{t}(s)\|^{2}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\|\theta(s)\|\|\theta_{tt}(s)\|\right)\,ds.

(197)

The discrete GSAV update
(
31
) can be rewritten as

r^{\,i+1}-r^{\,i}=\delta t\,R^{i+1}_{\bar{E}}\!\left(-\nu\|\nabla\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{\,i+1}\|^{2}-\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}\|\nabla\overline{\theta}^{\,i+1}\|^{2}+(\mathbf{f}^{\,i+1},\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{\,i+1})+\frac{1}{\alpha}(g^{\,i+1},\overline{\theta}^{\,i+1})\right).

(198)

where we denote

R^{i+1}_{\overline{E}}=\frac{r^{\,i+1}}{E(\overline{\bf u}^{i+1},\overline{\theta}^{i+1})+\overline{C}},\qquad R^{i+1}_{E}=\frac{r^{\,i+1}}{E({\bf u}(t^{i+1}),\theta(t^{i+1}))+\overline{C}}.

(199)

Subtracting (
193
) from
(
198
) and rearranging,
the error
s^{\,i+1}
satisfies

\displaystyle s^{\,i+1}-s^{\,i}=-T^{i}+\nu\delta t\!\left(\|\nabla\mathbf{u}(t^{i+1})\|^{2}-R^{i+1}_{\overline{E}}\|\nabla\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{\,i+1}\|^{2}\right)+\frac{\gamma\delta t}{\alpha}\!\left(\|\nabla\theta(t^{i+1})\|^{2}-R^{i+1}_{\overline{E}}\|\nabla\overline{\theta}^{\,i+1}\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\delta t\!\left(R^{i+1}_{\overline{E}}(\mathbf{f}^{\,i+1},\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{\,i+1})-(\mathbf{f}^{i+1},\mathbf{u}(t^{i+1}))\right)+\frac{\delta t}{\alpha}\!\left(R^{i+1}_{\overline{E}}(g^{\,i+1},\overline{\theta}^{\,i+1})-(g^{i+1},\theta(t^{i+1}))\right).

(200)

4.3.2
Step 3B. Preliminary estimates for
s^{n+1}

We bound each of the four main terms in
(
200
).
Throughout, we use the following facts
from the weak stability Theorem
1

and the induction hypothesis: for
j=1,\ldots,N
,

\|\mathbf{u}^{\,j}\|,\,\|\theta^{\,j}\|,\,r^{\,j}\leq M_{T},\qquad\overline{E}^{\,j+1}+\overline{C}\geq\overline{C}\geq 1,

together with the uniform bounds on the exact solution

\|\mathbf{u}(t)\|^{2},\,\|\nabla\mathbf{u}(t)\|^{2},\,\|\theta(t)\|^{2},\,\|\nabla\theta(t)\|^{2}\leq C_{301}:=\max\{C_{\mathrm{reg}}^{u},\,C_{\mathrm{reg}}^{\theta}\},\qquad t\in[0,T].

(201)

For the velocity dissipation, we write

\displaystyle\nu\!\left(\|\nabla\mathbf{u}(t^{i+1})\|^{2}-R_{\overline{E}}^{\,i+1}\|\nabla\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{\,i+1}\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu\|\nabla\mathbf{u}(t^{i+1})\|^{2}\bigl(1-R_{\overline{E}}^{\,i+1}\bigr)+\nu R_{\overline{E}}^{\,i+1}\!\left(\|\nabla\mathbf{u}(t^{i+1})\|^{2}-\|\nabla\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{\,i+1}\|^{2}\right)=:\nu W_{1}^{u,i}+\nu W_{2}^{u,i}.

(202)

An identical splitting applies to the
temperature dissipation:

\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}\!\left(\|\nabla\theta(t^{i+1})\|^{2}-R_{\overline{E}}^{\,i+1}\|\nabla\overline{\theta}^{\,i+1}\|^{2}\right)=:\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}W_{1}^{\theta,i}+\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}W_{2}^{\theta,i}.

(203)

Here
W_{1}^{\theta,i}:=\|\nabla\theta(t^{i+1})\|^{2}\bigl(1-R_{\overline{E}}^{\,i+1}\bigr)

and
W_{2}^{\theta,i}:=R_{\overline{E}}^{\,i+1}\bigl(\|\nabla\theta(t^{i+1})\|^{2}-\|\nabla\overline{\theta}^{\,i+1}\|^{2}\bigr)
.

We decompose

1-R_{\overline{E}}^{\,i+1}=\bigl(1-R_{E}^{\,i+1}\bigr)+\bigl(R_{E}^{\,i+1}-R_{\overline{E}}^{\,i+1}\bigr).

(204)

Noting
1-R_{E}^{\,i+1}=-s^{\,i+1}/(E(\mathbf{u}(t^{i+1}),\theta(t^{i+1}))+\overline{C})

since
r(t^{i+1})=E(\mathbf{u}(t^{i+1}),\theta(t^{i+1}))+\overline{C}
,
and using
E(\mathbf{u}(t^{i+1}),\theta(t^{i+1}))+\overline{C}\geq 1

\bigl|1-R_{\overline{E}}^{i+1}\bigr|\leq|s^{i+1}|+M_{T}\bigl|E(\mathbf{u}(t^{i+1}),\theta(t^{i+1}))-\overline{E}^{i+1}\bigr|.

(205)

For the energy difference, using

|E(\mathbf{u},\theta)-E(\overline{\mathbf{u}},\overline{\theta})|\leq\frac{1}{2}(\|\mathbf{u}\|+\|\overline{\mathbf{u}}\|)\|\mathbf{u}-\overline{\mathbf{u}}\|+\frac{1}{2\alpha}(\|\theta\|+\|\overline{\theta}\|)\|\theta-\overline{\theta}\|

the Poincare inequality, and

\left|E(\mathbf{u}(t^{i+1}),\theta(t^{i+1}))-\overline{E}^{i+1}\right|\leq C_{302}\!\left(\|\overline{\mathbf{e}}^{i+1}\|+\|\overline{e}_{\theta}^{i+1}\|\right)\leq C_{302}C\!\left(\|\nabla\overline{\mathbf{e}}^{i+1}\|+\|\nabla\overline{e}_{\theta}^{i+1}\|\right),

(206)

where

C_{302}=\max\left\{\frac{1}{2},\,\frac{1}{2\alpha}\right\}\;(\sqrt{C_{301}}+M_{T}).

(207)

Inserting (
206

205

\bigl|1-R_{\overline{E}}^{i+1}\bigr|\leq\max\{1,M_{T}CC_{302}\}\left(|s^{i+1}|+\|\nabla\overline{\mathbf{e}}^{i+1}\|+\|\nabla\overline{e}_{\theta}^{i+1}\|\right).

Therefore, defining

C_{303}=(\nu+\frac{\gamma}{\alpha})C_{301}\max\{1,M_{T}CC_{302}\},

we can easily obtain

\displaystyle|\nu W_{1}^{u,i}|+\left|\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}W_{1}^{\theta,i}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{303}\!\left(|s^{i+1}|+\|\nabla\overline{\mathbf{e}}^{i+1}\|+\|\nabla\overline{e}_{\theta}^{i+1}\|\right).

(210)

The same method together with
\|\nabla\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{i+1}\|\leq\sqrt{C_{3}}

from (

\displaystyle\nu|W_{2}^{u,i}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\nu M_{T}\!\left(\|\nabla\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{i+1}\|+\|\nabla\mathbf{u}(t^{i+1})\|\right)\|\nabla\overline{\mathbf{e}}^{i+1}\|\leq C_{304}^{u}\|\nabla\overline{\mathbf{e}}^{i+1}\|,

(211)

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}|W_{2}^{\theta,i}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{304}^{\theta}\|\nabla\overline{e}_{\theta}^{i+1}\|,

(212)

where

C_{304}^{u}=\nu M_{T}(\sqrt{C_{3}}+\sqrt{C_{301}}),\qquad C_{304}^{\theta}=\frac{\gamma M_{T}}{\alpha}(\sqrt{C_{3}}+\sqrt{C_{301}}).

(213)

We split the velocity forcing difference as

\displaystyle\left|R^{i+1}_{\overline{E}}(\mathbf{f}^{i+1},\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{i+1})-(\mathbf{f}^{i+1},\mathbf{u}(t^{i+1}))\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|(\mathbf{f}^{i+1},\mathbf{u}(t^{i+1}))|\left|1-R^{i+1}_{\overline{E}}\right|+R^{i+1}_{\overline{E}}\left|(\mathbf{f}^{i+1},\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{i+1})-(\mathbf{f}^{i+1},\mathbf{u}(t^{i+1}))\right|=:W_{3}^{u,i}+W_{4}^{u,i}.

(214)

For

, using

(

W_{3}^{u,i}\leq C_{305}^{u}\!\left(|s^{i+1}|+\|\nabla\overline{\mathbf{e}}^{i+1}\|+\|\nabla\overline{e}_{\theta}^{i+1}\|\right),\qquad C_{305}^{u}=C_{f}\sqrt{C_{301}}\max\{1,M_{T}C_{302}C\}.

(215)

For

, using

(216)

An identical argument applies to the temperature
forcing to generate

\displaystyle\bigl|R^{i+1}_{\overline{E}}(g^{i+1},\overline{\theta}^{i+1})-(g^{i+1},\theta(t^{i+1}))\bigr|\leq C^{\theta}_{305}\bigl(|s^{i+1}|+\|\nabla\overline{e}^{i+1}\|+\|\nabla\overline{e}^{i+1}_{\theta}\|\bigr)+C^{\theta}_{306}\|\nabla\overline{e}^{i+1}_{\theta}\|,

(217)

where

Defining

(218)

the sum of all terms in
(

Substituting (

4.3.6
Step 3F. Pressure error estimate

We return to the pressure error equation

(239)

6
Discussion

We have proposed and analyzed a second-order consistent-splitting GSAV scheme for the perturbed Boussinesq system (
6
) arising from a stable linear hydrostatic equilibrium with constant lapse rate.
The time discretization adopts the time-shifted BDF2 framework of Huang and Shen
[
17
]
.
The nonlinear convection
\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u}
and advection
\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\theta
, together with the linear buoyancy
\theta\,\mathbf{e}_{2}

and stratification
\alpha v
couplings, are all treated explicitly through a second order extrapolation at the time level
t^{n+k}
, so that each time step
reduces to a small number of decoupled linear systems with constant coefficients.

Theorem
1
establishes an
unconditional weak
stability
property: provided the stabilization constant
\overline{C}
exceeds
an explicit threshold determined by the magnitudes of the forcing terms,
the solution
{\bf u}^{n}
and
\theta^{n}
remain uniformly bounded for all
n
, with no restriction on the time step.
Second, Theorem
2
establishes optimal second-order temporal convergence.
Numerical experiments in Section
5
confirm the predicted
second-order rate on a manufactured solution and illustrate the long-time
relaxation toward hydrostatic balance in a stratified-flow simulation.

A careful tracing shows that the error constant
C_{\mathrm{Bou}}
contains negative powers of
\nu
and
\gamma
, arising through a chain of estimates: the Stokes pressure bound
C_{S}(\varepsilon)\sim\varepsilon^{-3}
, the stability conditions
\kappa^{u}\geq 0
and
\kappa^{\theta}\geq 0
, and the discrete Grönwall inequality. The cumulative effect is a quadruply-exponential dependence of
C_{\mathrm{Bou}}
on
\nu^{-1}
and
\gamma^{-1}
(see Remark
3
). A similar negative-power dependence was observed by Alhomsi, Wu, and Zheng
[
3
]
for the consistent-splitting GSAV scheme of Huang and Shen
[
17
]
applied to the Navier–Stokes equations, and traced to the explicit treatment of the convection term. Their numerical experiments show that this scheme indeed blows up as
\nu\to 0^{+}
, whereas a fully implicit discretization solved by Newton’s method remains accurate. For the perturbed Boussinesq system considered here, the situation is expected to be even less favorable, since the temperature equation introduces an additional explicit advection
\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\theta
together with the bidirectional buoyancy–stratification coupling.

In Example 2 of Section
5.2
, we also tested smaller values of the viscosity and thermal diffusivity, with these runs blowing up at the present spatial and temporal resolutions. Although further mesh and time-step refinement may rescue them, this failure shows that the scheme is not robust: it requires an increasingly fine mesh and a smaller time step size to remain stable as the diffusion is reduced. A natural direction for future work is therefore to develop a more robust scheme that handles all viscosity regimes without requiring a prohibitively fine mesh or time step size.
