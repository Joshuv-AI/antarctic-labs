Theorem of Wantzel
J. Mainik

Abstract
In 1796, Gauss succeeded in solving the problem of constructing the regular 17-gon with compass and straightedge. Later he proved that, using a compass and straightedge, it is possible to construct the regular polygons with n = 2 m n 1 ⋯ n l n=2^{m}n_{1}\cdots n_{l} sides if n 1 , ⋯ , n l n_{1},\cdots,n_{l} are different prime numbers of the form n k = 2 2 ν k + 1 n_{k}=2^{2^{\nu_{k}}}+1 . Gauss also knew that only these regular polygons can be constructed but did not prove it.P. Wantzel completed the result of Gauss and proved it in 1837. The present paper provides a new proof for Wantzel’s theorem.



Keywords:
Regular polygon, compass and straightedge,
Fermat prime, constructability, Wantzel

The author hopes that the new proof of Wantzel’s theorem will find interest

The numbers of the form
n=2^{2^{\nu}}+1
are named Fermat numbers.
Fermat thought that they are prime numbers, but this is not correct.
The numbers

2^{2^{0}}+1=3,2^{2^{1}}+1=5,2^{2^{2}}+1=17,2^{2^{3}}+1=257
and
2^{2^{4}}+1=65537

are indeed primes, but the numbers

are not.
It is currently unknown whether other Fermat primes exist.
This problem is not trivial:
the smallest Fermat number for which it is not known whether it is prime or not is

2\,585\,827\,973

It is clear that the regular

can be constructed exactly when every single

-gon can be constructed.
Therefore the proof of Wantzel’s theorem consists of the following two parts:

1) Show that the regular

-gon cannot be constructed
if the prime

is not a Fermat number.

2) Show that for a Fermat prime

the regular

-gon
cannot be constructed. It is sufficient to analyze only this case.
If we could construct the

-gon, then we could also construct the

Primes that are not Fermat numbers

Proposition 1.
If the prime number
n
is not a Fermat number,
then the regular
n
-gon cannot be constructed using a compass and straightedge.

Proof.
Suppose the prime number
n>3
is not a Fermat number. Then
the even number
n-1
has an odd divisor
k
,
k>1
, so that

(n-1)=n_{0}\cdot k
. Using the primitive root
g
for
n
,
we form
n-1
distinct numbers
r_{k}=\mathrm{rest}(g^{k-1};n)
,
and split the ordered set
V
of these numbers

V=\{r_{1},r_{2},\cdots,r_{n-1}\}
into
k
disjunct and ordered
parts
V_{j}
,
1\leq j\leq k
,

V_{j}=\{r_{j},r_{j+k},r_{j+2k},\dots,r_{j+n-1-k}\}

Thus,
V_{j}
contains the
j
-th number and every
k
-th number thereafter.
For the sets
V_{j}
defined in this way,
the following cyclic property clearly holds. If we multiply the numbers
of the sets by the factor
g
and calculate modulo
n
,
we obtain for
1\leq j<k
from
V_{j}
the set
V_{j+1}
and from
V_{k}

the set
V_{1}
.

Each set
V_{j}
consists of
n_{0}=(n-1)/k
numbers, and it follows from
(
4
)
that the set of the
n_{0}
values

v^{r_{j}},v^{r_{j+k}},v^{r_{j+2k}},\cdots,v^{r_{j+(n_{0}-1)\cdot k}}

with
degrees from
V_{j}
consists of pairs of inverse elements.
The value
v^{r_{j}+(m-1)k}

for the
m
-th number from the first half in
V_{j}

(i.e. for
1\leq m\leq n_{0}/2
)
is inverse to the value
v^{r_{L/2+j+(m-1)k}}
for the
(n_{0}/2+m)
-th number
from the second half
in
V_{j}
.

We define the values
x_{j}
,
1\leq j\leq k
, as follows:

x_{j}=\sum_{m\in V_{j}}v^{m}=\sum_{0\leq m<n_{0}}e^{r_{j+km}\cdot 2\pi i/n}.

(5)

Here and in what follows, we use the agreed notation
v=e^{2\pi i/n}
.
Due to the property of the values
v^{m}
of degree
m
in
V_{j}
it immediately follows
that the values
x_{j}
are real numbers,

x_{j}=\sum_{0\leq m<n_{0}/2}2\cos(r_{j+km}\cdot 2\pi/n).

(6)

In
(
6
)
only the first half of the numbers in
V_{j}
is used.

We now consider the polynomial
F(x)=(x-x_{1})(x-x_{1})\cdots(x-x_{k})
.
This is a polynomial of odd degree
k
with real roots.

F(x)
has the form

F(x)=x^{k}+c_{1}x^{k-1}+c_{2}x^{k-2}+\cdots+c_{k-1}x+c_{k},

(7)

and we want to show that the coefficients
c_{p}
,
1\leq p\leq k
,
are integers.
The coefficient
c_{1}
is
1
here:

c_{1}=-(x_{1}+\cdots+x_{k})=-\sum_{1\leq m\leq n-1}v^{m}=1,

(8)

and hence only the coefficients
c_{p}
,
2\leq p\leq k
, need to be considered.

For integers
k
and
p
such that
2\leq p\leq k
let
M(k,p)
denote
the set of all distinct sequences
\{j_{1},j_{2},\cdots,j_{p}\}
of integers,
formed in such a way that
1\leq j_{1}<j_{2}<\cdots<j_{p}\leq k
.
For
c_{p}

the following representation clearly holds:

c_{p}=(-1)^{p}\sum_{M(k,p)}x_{j_{1}}x_{j_{2}}\cdots x_{j_{p}}

(9)

and due to
(
9
) and (
5
)
we obtain the representation of

in terms of the elements

v^{1},\cdots,v^{n-1}

c_{p}=\mu_{p,0}+\mu_{p,1}v^{r_{1}}+\mu_{p,2}v^{r_{2}}+\cdots\mu_{p,n-1}v^{r_{n-1}}

(10)

with integer coefficients
\mu_{p,0},\mu_{p,1},\dots,\mu_{p,n-1}
.
The constant
\mu_{p,0}
can be obtained here using products
of inverse elements, due to

.

We now multiply the numbers in each set

,

,
by the factor

(calculating modulo

).
With this change of the degrees in
(
9
)
for each factor in the product

the degrees

of the corresponding summands in (
5
)
are multiplied by

.
If the degrees in the summands of the individual factors

x_{j_{1}},x_{j_{2}},\dots,x_{j_{p}}
are multiplied by the factor
g
,
it is clear that in the result
for the product
x_{j_{1}}x_{j_{2}}\cdots x_{j_{p}}

the degrees

of the corresponding summands are also multiplied by the factor
g
.
Thus it follows that in the representation for

in
(
10
)
all degrees of

must be multiplied by the factor
g
.
This means that in
(
10
)
for

, we must get
\mu_{k,j}v^{r_{j+1}}

\mu_{k,n-1}v^{r_{n-1}}

\mu_{k,n-1}v^{r_{1}}
.
However, when multiplying the degrees by the factor

,

, and since

.

(11)

When calculating the coefficient

in

When calculating the coefficient

.
Because of

Thus, when calculating

Lemma.

\displaystyle x_{i}x_{j}=c_{i,j,1}x_{1}+c_{i,j,2}x_{2}+\cdots+c_{i,j,k}x_{k}+c_{i,j,0}

1) The property that the proposed approach yields a polynomial

For values of
