Executive Summary

The major goal of this project is to de-risk single-crystal CdTe solar cells grown on
inexpensive silicon templates without post-growth halide treatments as a prototype to
more stable, high efficiency CdTe solar cells. A fast deposition, minimal post
processing, single-crystal CdTe solar cell should also result in a cell with higher Voc1,3.
CdTe has similar bandgap to GaAs, yet GaAs solar cells have a higher V oc by 150mV-
250mV. It maybe that surface defects in the junction of polycrystalline CdTe cells are
responsible7. Single-crystal CdTe solar cells have achieved Voc > 1V6. A high Voc, thin
CdTe cell could serve as a top cell in a high efficiency Silicon/CdTe tandem solar cell1,2.

High deposition rate close-space sublimation (CSS) of single-crystal CdTe on a silicon
substrate is the first key objective toward low cost tandem solar cells 3,8. In parallel,
molecular beam epitaxy (MBE) growth of single-crystal CdTe on silicon substrates
provides a baseline for the final solar cell architecture and achievable doping. The team
will explore doping the CSS-grown CdTe by post-deposition diffusion doping and in situ
CSS doping5.

The first steps in achieving this goal are to grow good quality single-crystal thin epitaxial
ZnTe and CdTe layers on inexpensive silicon substrate. That goal was achieved in the
first quarter and is a prerequisite for doping the layers required to develop a high-
efficiency solar cell. All scheduled doping of p-type and n-type CdTe has been achieved
in the second quarter.

The details of Task 3.0, the main task of the second quarter as specified in the SOPO,
are listed below. The milestone to be achieved at the end of the second quarter was: P-
type doping of single crystal CdTe absorber with a carrier concentration cc ≥ 1.0e16/cm3.
In order to achieve this goal, it is necessary to grow good quality, relatively thin single-
crystal ZnTe layer on 12% lattice mismatched silicon substrate or a good quality CdTe
layer on 19% mismatched silicon substrate. Doping efficiency requires that the strain
release layer enables the growth of a CdTe layer of sufficient quality to allow high
carrier concentration with minimum defect compensation. This milestone is included in
Task 2.0 through Task 4 and has been achieved with CdTe doping efficiency > 80%.

Given the good crystalline quality of the epitaxial ZnTe buffer layers grown by MBE 4 in
the first quarter of the project, the task of doping the ZnTe buffer has been achieved. In
the second quarter, January through April 2023, doping experiments with cracked As
were performed on ZnTe and CdTe layers grown by MBE on Semi-Insulating (SI)
Si<211B> and Si<100> substrate. P-type in-situ doping of both MBE and CSS CdTe
was achieved. P-type and N-type diffusion doping of both MBE and CSS grown CdTe
was also achieved. Several of the resulting samples were measured by Hall to have p-
type carrier concentrations that exceed milestones: 1.1.1, 1.2.1 and 1.3.1, as can be
seen in Tables 1 and 2 of this report. Both in-situ and diffusion n-type doping of CdTe is
reported in Table 3 of this report. Both ZnTe and CdTe doping levels specified in
milestones 1.0 through 4.0 have been achieved and exceeded.

We were, however, unable to achieve milestone 5.2 EOP-B, Epi-CdTe solar cell on Si
with Voc ≥ 0.9V. When we transitioned to a highly conductive p-type silicon substrate
with resistivity 0.001Ω-cm for a device back-contact, we were unable to consistently
temperature calibrate the silicon substrate for nucleation layer deposition. We had no
such problem with semi-insulating silicon substrate and concluded that radiative heating
of highly conductive silicon is not stable. Using the same recipes as those for semi-
insulating substrate resulted in no deposition of the MEE nucleation layer. After
searching for temperature corrections for the p+ silicon that resulted in MEE deposition,
we found corrections as large as 60°C that varied from substrate to substrate. Following
the same doping methodologies we used for semi-insulating substrate, we found doping
efficiencies for p-type CdTe on p+ substrate dropped significantly. We believe this is due
to temperature fluctuations in growing the nucleation layer which result in a higher
density of compensating defects. The devices produced suffered from low Fill-Factors,
although one device had Voc = 0.883V. Even with high series resistence, we saw
several devices with Jsc > 13mA/cm2 with no CdCl post growth heat treatment. This is
discussed further in the Path Forward section of this report.

Background
Epitaxial growth of single crystal CdTe on lattice-matched substrate is too expensive for
commercial solar cells as are large crystals grown by the Bridgman process. However,
good-quality single crystal CdTe grown on cheap, lattice-mismatched silicon offers a
viable route to high efficiency, single crystal CdTe solar cells. Silicon and CdTe are the
two most successful solar technologies on the market today. They constitute almost
98% of global PV shipments, have module costs < $0.25/W with weighted average
production module efficiencies of 18.5-20.5%, and compete favorably with all other
electricity generation sources. CdTe solar panels have proven to be a commercial
success and have made steady improvement in both cost and efficiency. Currently,
CdTe solar modules have reached approximate efficiency parity with multi-crystalline
silicon and are now purported to be the lowest cost per watt PV technology, have the
shortest energy payback time, and be the least carbon intensive in production.
However, production scale polycrystalline CdTe material deposited at temperatures of
400-600℃ via closed space sublimation (CSS) or vapor transport deposition (VTD) has
consistently experienced performance limitations; specifically low open circuit voltage
(Voc). The highest efficiency CdTe commercial panels are about 19%, while the most
efficient lab cells have reached an efficiency of 22%9. Despite these efficiency
achievements, many questions remain about extrinsic doping and material limitations. A
key complication involved in the manufacture of polycrystalline CdTe cells is the halide
treatment (typically CdCl2) used to passivate grain boundaries and interfaces, but which
may also compensate p-type doping, preventing higher Voc. Copper has historically
been diffused into the CdTe absorber post deposition since polycrystalline CdTe is hard
to dope extrinsically and only a few groups have successfully doped polycrystalline
CdTe p-type in-situ. However, copper diffusion into the junction region can cause device
degradation and is a major source of CdTe cell instability over time. Low doping, in the
range of 1-2∙1014/cm3, results in lower open circuit voltages, degraded fill factor, and
nearly fully depleted devices. An electron reflector layer such as ZnTe, which is more
easily doped p-type, at the back contact can improve this situation, but may contribute
to recombination due to defects induced by lattice mismatch at the CdTe/ZnTe
interface5,7. In principle, CdTe, with a direct band-gap of 1.5eV, is an optimal solar
absorber for single junction cells, much like GaAs in III-V. Crystalline GaAs has reached
a single junction efficiency of 28.8% with a Voc of 1.12V. However, the best commercial
poly-crystalline CdTe devices have Voc’s in the range of 0.85V.

Single-crystal cells offer a wide variety of potential benefits including higher dopant
activation ratios and no grain boundaries to passivate with compensating halide
treatments or enhance degradation-related diffusion mechanisms. This means that
single-crystal CdTe cells could be thinner, more efficient, and more stable. Moreover,
only single-crystal CdTe based cells without halide treatments have demonstrated high
Voc (≥ 1V)1,6.

As Si, CdTe, and other technologies approach single-junction efficiency limit, next-
generation efficiency will require transitioning to dual junction cells while retaining low
costs. In light of the current market and technology presented above, the most obvious
combination to retain viable manufacturability, cost, and stability is Si/CdTe or

 Page 6 of 27

Si/CdZnTe. There has been very little work done on Si/CdTe-based tandem PV. Several
projects have worked towards the development of polycrystalline CdZnTe and CdMgTe
top cells, but a key obstacle has remained halide treatments which lead to Mg or Zn
migration, low Voc, and ultimately performance limitations. Several other projects have
focused on various aspects of single crystal material and device development. Tuning
carrier concentration, dopant incorporation, and defect density to maintain high lifetimes
was found to be a key challenge in that work. An outcome of one of those projects
(FPACE 2), was the determination of the effective material limitation on carrier
concentration to be much lower than originally targeted by that project (mid 10 17cm-3).
Building on the knowledge from previous experimental and theoretical work, positions
us to target a different doping/lifetime regime that should be both more readily
achievable and efficient.

Project Objectives

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first project whose goal is to develop
commercially viable single-crystal thin film technology to produce high-efficiency one-
sun solar cells. The material set we are using has been proven and accepted in the
marketplace and represents the material set of the largest panel manufacturer in the
US. The success of this project opens the door to the development of stable high-
efficiency tandem solar cells.

Task 1 Milestone 1.1.1:

First Quarter milestone (1.1.1) is to demonstrate thin, p-doped ZnTe layer on 12%
lattice mismatched silicon <211B> substrate with charge concentration (cc) cc ≥
5.0x1017/cm3.

 Figure 1) ZnTe:As layers were deposited by MBE with thickness 0.8µm and are shown in the
 table below (blue/green) and 1.05µm(red).
The silicon substrate is etched to remove oxides using a modified RCA proceedure
outlined by Chen4, then thermally cleaned in the MBE growth chamber at 900°C (5 min)
until a streaky RHEED (Reflection High Energy Electron Diffraction) pattern is obtained.
Nucleation is done using a 25 to 50 cycle MEE (Migration Enhanced Epitaxy) process.
MEE (dusting) requires alternating thin depositions of Zn and Te by depositing on the
oxide free silicon surface a ten second exposure of Zinc followed by a ten second
pause, then exposing the surface to ten seconds of Tellurium, pausing for ten seconds,
then beginning the cycle again. The RHEED pattern of the MEE surface shown in
Figure 1 above indicates a bright 2D crystalline surface.

Task 5 Milestone 1.5.1:
Close-Space Sublimation used to deposit single-crystal CdTe (Thickness ≤8um)on p+
Silicon.
Single crystal deposition by CSS was shown in Milestone 2. For task 5 CSS, deposition
was repeated on conductive rather than insulating silicon wafer. When switching from a
semi-insulating silicon wafer to a p+ Silicon wafer with resistivity 0.001Ω-cm for a solar
cell back contact, it was determined that we could not calibrate the temperature of the
silicon substrate. Inconsistent results were measured via O2 blow-off from the silicon
and the melt temperature of Indium from the silicon surface. The MBE platen heater
does not come into direct contact with a material the silicon is attached to (like
Molybdenum) or the silicon itself. The silicon is radiantly heated and the conductive
properties of the p+ wafer changed the temperature profile of the sample in an

When the MEE recipe used in previous growths on semi-insulating substrate was
repeated, no MEE layer remained at the end of the process (as seen by RHEED),
indicating the deposition did not stick due to the temperature being too high. Through
trial and error, a temperature off-set was determined that yielded an MEE layer at the
end of the deposition at the growth temperature, as measured by RHEED. Different off-
sets for different Si wafers ranged from 50°C-60°C. The MEE process and a resulting
p+Si/ZnTe:As/CdTe(6μm) deposition are shown above in Figure 6. Due to this
temperature anomaly on p+ silicon as compared with semi-insulating silicon, it is not
clear that heating set by PIDs is maintained uniformly over the sample and over the
growth period.

Significant Accomplishments and Conclusions
A prerequisite to achieving the goal of commercially viable single-crystal CdTe solar
cells is to grow good quality crystalline CdTe (XRD < 300 arc-secs) on lattice miss-
matched (19% lattice miss-match between Silicon/CdTe), cheap silicon substrate at a
large growth rate. We have achieved that prerequisite:

  ZnTe 0.6μm grown on SI-Si<211B> substrate with XRD FWHM ≤ 260” by MBE.
  (ZnTe 0.7μm)/CdTe 8μm grown on SI-Si<211B> substrate with XRD FWHM =
 180” by CSS at a growth rate (GR) of 16μm/min.
  (CdTe 2.3μm)/CdTe 4.1μm grown on SI-Si<211B> substrate with XRD FWHM =
 110” by CSS at a growth rate of 0.8μm/min. At this GR, it takes 5min to grow a
 4μmCdTe layer.
  P-type ZnTe:As grown on Si<211B> by MBE with carrier concentration (cc) =
 8.28x1017/cm3, hole mobility = 67.7cm2/(V*s), and a pair resistance = 4kΩ - 6kΩ.
  P-type CdTe:As grown on Si<211B>/ZnTe by MBE, As by diffusion with FWHM =
       87”, cc = 1.0x1018, μ = 84cm2/V*s.
  P-type CdTe:As grown on Si<211B>/ZnTe by CSS at 16μ/min, As by diffusion
       with FWHM = 222”, cc = 1.88x1017, μ = 57.5cm2/V*s.

In order to build a device using single-crystal silicon substrate, an ohmic metallic back
contact must be developed on p+Si substrate. Also, both the ZnTe buffer layer and
CdTe absorber layer must be doped to levels greater than 1.0xe17/cm3 and 5.0xe15/cm3
respectively to guarantee a high efficiency solar cell. That doping was achieved using
Arsenic on semi-insulating substrate with doping efficiency > 60%. However, this was
not achieved on p+Si substrate due to as yet unidentified doping compensation.
  3μm Al layer annealed onto p+Si wafer to form a highly conductive, ohmic
 metallic back contact.

  Al/p+Si/MEE/ZnTe:As back contact and buffer layer with ohmic series resistance
 < 2.0Ω
  3μm epitaxially solar cell grown on p+Si, single-crystal ZnTe/CdTe device with
 Jsc= 13.9mA/cm2 with no post growth (CdCl2) halide treatment.
  6μm epitaxially solar cell grown on p+Si, single-crystal ZnTe/CdTe device with
 Voc = 0.88V with no post growth (CdCl2) halide treatment.

Path Forward
The primary problem to be overcome in producing a high efficiency single-crystal CdTe
solar cell on silicon substrate is to discover why we see high As doping activation on
semi-insulating silicon substrate, but high compensation on highly (Boron) doped silicon
substrate. Due to the discrepancy in doping efficiency between highly conductive p+
and semi-insulating silicon substrate, we conclude that radiative heating of the highly
conductive substrate is unstable and results in a nucleation layer that promotes a higher
density of compensating defects. Chen et. al. have documented temperature
differences between thermocouple readings and silicon surface temperatures as high as
90°C. As noted, our temperature offsets were of order 60°C, but we could not
consistently calibrate the temperature of our p+ silicon substrate. Avenues to explore
include uneven (in time and space) radiant heating of p+ silicon and miscut or damaged
p+ silicon substrate. More experimentation must be done on less conductive p+ silicon,
i.e. 0.01 ≤ ρ ≤ 1.0, and, or attach the substrate to a sample holder with direct contact
heating rather than radiative heating. This could be accomplished by attaching the
silicon substrate to a molly-block and held in place by clips so that the substrate
remains attached during the high-temperature oxide-desorption step of the silicon
surface. Direct contact to the molly-block can be made by a spring-loaded finger to the
thermocouple. We believe that Carmody did not employ direct heater contact to the
silicon in growing his champion CdTe device, which had repeatability problems.
Carmody did use extrinsic p-type As doping in the absorber layer of that device, which
was grown on Si<111> 3” substrate. Once stable temperature calibration is achieved on
the p+Si(MEE)/ZnTe layer, RTP (Rapid Thermal Process) anneals should further
reduce the compensating dislocation density10. We believe that once a high quality
nucleation layer is deposited on silicon using fast deposition CSS growth on Si/ZnTe
substrate/buffer with consistent temperature calibration, high quality CdTe devices can
be reliably produced.
