Energetics of non-Gaussianity in single mode cavities
Sahil Sardar Jafar, Kian Hwee Lim, Kiarn T. Laverick, Samyak P. Prasad, Maria Maffei, Alexia Auffèves

Abstract
Non-Gaussian states play a central role in quantum technologies, making the ability to quantify non-Gaussianity essential. We introduce an energetic framework to characterize non-Gaussianity in single-mode bosonic states by decomposing the total energy into Gaussian and non-Gaussian contributions. For pure states, we show that the non-Gaussian component defines a valid measure of non-Gaussianity and establish its connection to the relative entropy of non-Gaussianity. As an illustration, we compare this measure with Wigner negativity and find that both are maximized in closely related parameter regimes. For mixed states, we demonstrate that the non-Gaussian contribution acts as a faithful witness of non-Gaussianity. Our results reveal an energetic fine structure underlying non-Gaussianity and may provide practical insights for the efficient generation of non-Gaussian states.



I
Introduction

Bosonic systems, such as single mode cavities, are simple and textbook systems of quantum physics. As they can support states with a clear classical limit, they provide appealing platforms to explore the quantum-classical boundary – through, for example, the generation of Schrödinger cat states, and the study of their decoherence
Brune et al. 1996
;
Haroche and Raimond 2006
;
He et al. 2023
, measurement theory
Wiseman and Milburn 2010
and the emergence of classicality
Schlosshauer 2007
;
Zurek 2003
. They have also emerged as a promising paradigm for quantum technologies, especially in the context of quantum computing with continuous variables
Lloyd and Braunstein 1999
;
Chabaud and Walschaers 2023
;
Upreti et al. 2026
;
Braunstein and van
Loock 2005
, quantum communication
Usenko et al. 2026
;
Joshi et al. 2021
;
Weedbrook et al. 2012
, and quantum sensing
Pirandola et al. 2018
.

Non-Gaussian states are key to capture the features of non-classical physics, and have been shown to lead to quantum advantages
Eisert et al. 2002
;
Niset et al. 2009
;
Ahmed and Smith 2026
;
Mari and Eisert 2012
;
Veitch et al. 2012
;
Banaszek and Wódkiewicz 1998
;
Booth et al. 2022
, making their detection and quantification a crucial ability for both foundations and applications. Accordingly, several measures of non-Gaussianity have been proposed. These include information-theoretic measures such as relative entropy of non-Gaussianity
Genoni et al. 2008
;
Genoni and Paris 2010
;
Marian and Marian 2013
;
Park et al. 2019
and Hilbert–Schmidt distance
Genoni et al. 2007
, phase-space based measures such as Wigner negativity
Kenfack and Życzkowski 2004
;
Albarelli et al. 2018
and maximum negentropy of quadrature distributions
Park et al. 2021
, as well as operational measures such as the robustness of non-Gaussianity
Regula et al. 2021
;
Lami et al. 2021
. Experimentally motivated measures include stellar rank
Chabaud et al. 2020
and non-Gaussian control parameters
Hanamura et al. 2026
.

Recently, energy has emerged as an interesting physical quantity to measure and detect quantum features such as entanglement, both in continuous variables
Polo-Rodríguez et al. 2026
;
Friis et al. 2016
;
Serafini 2007
;
Hyllus and Eisert 2006
and in discrete variables
Laverick et al. 2026
;
Yang et al. 2024
;
Alimuddin et al. 2020
;
Perarnau-Llobet et al. 2015
. Energetic measures of quantum resources are constrained by conservation laws, providing a direct route to understanding fundamental trade-offs
Laverick et al. 2026
. Moreover, energetic quantities offer operational advantages, since they can typically be determined without requiring the knowledge of the full quantum state. They have recently been proposed as cost-functions to optimize the generation of non-Gaussian states in the scattering of coherent light by a two-level atom
Lim et al. 2026
.

In this work, we show that the total energy of a single bosonic mode can be decomposed into energetic quantities accounting for displacement, squeezing and non-Gaussianity. We show that for pure states, the non-Gaussian contribution to energy, which we dub the
non-Gaussian energy
, is a measure of non-Gaussianity which can be easily accessed in experiments. We further establish its connection with the relative entropy of non-Gaussianity, thereby reconciling our energetic approach with the entropic approach to quantifying non-Gaussianity.
As an example, we study the non-Gaussian energy of states generated by SNAP (Selective Number Dependent Arbitrary Phase) operations
Heeres et al. 2015
on coherent states and compare it with Wigner negativity
Kenfack and Życzkowski 2004
. For mixed states, we introduce an energetic quantity that
serves as a faithful witness of non-Gaussianity, and present an energetic decomposition analogous
to that for pure states, but modified by an additional contribution accounting the mixedness of the state. We further show that this witness is functionally independent of the relative entropy of non-Gaussianity – however, we demonstrate that it provides upper and lower bounds on the latter.

In Sec.
II
,
we present the decomposition of energy for pure states, introduce the non-Gaussian energy, explore its properties and relate it to the relative entropy of non-Gaussianity. In Sec
III
, we use our measure to quantify the non-Gaussianity of states generated by applying SNAP gates on coherent states of light, and compare the behavior of the non-Gaussian energy with the one of the Wigner negativity. In Sec.
IV
, we extend our energetic approach to mixed states by introducing an energetic witness of non-Gaussianity and explore how it characterizes mixed non-Gaussian states.

II
Non-Gaussian energy for pure states

II.1
Energetic decomposition for pure states

We consider a single bosonic mode described by the annihilation and creation operators
\hat{a}
and
\hat{a}^{\dagger}
, satisfying the commutation relation
[\hat{a},\hat{a}^{\dagger}]=1
. The total energy of the system in state
\ket{\psi}
is counted in numbers of photon and reads
E=\langle{\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}}\rangle
. The primary question we consider here is the following: can we distinguish, purely from an energetic perspective, whether a pure state
\ket{\psi}
is Gaussian, that is, whether its Wigner function is a Gaussian distribution
Serafini 2017
;
Weedbrook et al. 2012
? Such states are fully characterized by the first moment
\langle{\hat{a}}\rangle
, and second moments, which are collected in the covariance matrix
V
, given by

V=\left(\langle\delta\hat{a}^{\dagger}\delta\hat{a}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\right)I_{2}+\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{Re}\,\langle(\delta\hat{a})^{2}\rangle\,&\mathrm{Im}\,\langle(\delta\hat{a})^{2}\rangle\,\\
\mathrm{Im}\,\langle(\delta\hat{a})^{2}\rangle\,&-\mathrm{Re}\,\langle(\delta\hat{a})^{2}\rangle\end{pmatrix}\,,

(1)

where
\delta\hat{O}=\hat{O}-\langle{\hat{O}}\rangle
and
I_{2}
denotes the
2\times 2
identity matrix. To answer the question, we decompose the total energy as

E=E_{\cal C}+E_{\cal S}+E_{\cal NG}\,,

(2)

where we define

\displaystyle E_{\cal C}

\displaystyle:=|\langle{\hat{a}}\rangle|^{2}\,,

(3)

\displaystyle E_{\cal S}

\displaystyle:=\langle{\delta\hat{a}^{\dagger}\delta\hat{a}}\rangle-\bigg(\sqrt{\det V}-\frac{1}{2}\bigg)\,,

(4)

\displaystyle E_{\cal NG}

\displaystyle:={\sqrt{\det V}}-\frac{1}{2}\,.

(5)

The
coherent energy

E_{\cal C}
, captures the maximum amount of energy that can be extracted from the state
\ket{\psi}
using displacement unitaries. Specifically, one can extract
E_{\cal C}
by applying the displacement operator

\hat{D}^{\dagger}(\alpha)=e^{\alpha^{*}\hat{a}-\alpha\hat{a}^{\dagger}}
,
with amplitude

\alpha=\langle{\hat{a}}\rangle.
The extraction of coherent energy is pictorially depicted in Fig.
1
. In the standard energetic decomposition based on the mean-field decomposition
Laverick et al. 2026
;
Prasad et al. 2026
, the energy remaining in the state after deducting the coherent energy is known as the
incoherent energy
,
E_{\cal I}=E-E_{\cal C}=\langle{\delta\hat{a}^{\dagger}\delta\hat{a}}\rangle
, which is the energy associated with the quantum fluctuations about the
mean field. Here, we have further resolved the incoherent energy into two parts,
i.e.
,
E_{\cal I}=E_{\cal S}+E_{\cal NG}
.

Figure 1:
A diagram of our energetic approach to quantifying non-Gaussianity compared to the standard entropic approach. In our energetic approach, we systematically extract energy through Gaussian unitaries (displacements and squeezing) and quantify the remaining energy of the state extractable only through non-Gaussian unitaries as our measure of non-Gaussianity. The states we obtain thereby have the same entropy but different energies. In contrast, the entropic approach involves relative entropy to the reference Gaussian state
\rho_{V}
, which has same energy but different entropy. We have shown that these two approaches are equivalent for pure states, since the entropic measure is related to
E_{\cal NG}
through the function
h
[see Eq. (
11
)].

E_{\cal S}
corresponds to the maximum amount of energy extractable from the state
\hat{D}^{\dagger}(\alpha)\ket{\psi}
using squeezing unitaries. It has been proved in
Brown et al. 2016
;
Rodriguez et al. 2025
that the optimal squeezing unitary is
\hat{S}^{\dagger}(\xi)=e^{\xi\hat{a}^{2}-\xi^{*}{\hat{a}^{\dagger}}^{2}}
, with squeezing parameter
\xi
, which transforms the covariance matrix to its Williamson form,
i.e.
,

\sqrt{\det V}\,{I}_{2}

Williamson 1936
.
It is particularly illustrative to express
E_{\cal S}
in terms of the principal quadratures,

\hat{q}_{\phi}=\hat{a}^{\dagger}e^{i\phi}+\hat{a}e^{-i\phi}
and

\hat{p}_{\phi}=i(\hat{a}^{\dagger}e^{i\phi}-\hat{a}e^{-i\phi})
,
where the phase
\phi
is chosen so that the covariance matrix is diagonal. In this representation,
E_{\cal S}
takes the following form:

E_{\cal S}=\frac{1}{4}\bigg(\sqrt{\langle{(\delta\hat{q}_{\phi})^{2}}\rangle}-\sqrt{\langle{(\delta\hat{p}_{\phi})^{2}}\rangle}\bigg)^{2}\,.

(6)

The above equation shows that
E_{\cal S}
is a non-negative quantity and originates from the difference between the standard deviations of the principal quadratures. Importantly, the squeezed energy is zero for isotropic states,
i.e.
, states satisfying
\langle{\delta\hat{q}_{\phi}^{2}}\rangle=\langle{\delta\hat{p}_{\phi}^{2}}\rangle
. Following the naming convention established by the coherent energy to name the energetic contribution after the state that only contains this energy (
i.e.
, coherent states), we refer to
E_{\cal S}
as the
squeezed energy
, since squeezed vacuum states contain only this energetic contribution.

We have accounted for the energetic contribution arising due to displacement and squeezing which are single mode Gaussian unitaries. The resulting state,
\hat{S}^{\dagger}(\xi)\hat{D}^{\dagger}(\alpha)\ket{\psi}
is the minimum-energy state that can be prepared from
\ket{\psi}
using Gaussian unitaries, and is called the Gaussian-passive state
Brown et al. 2016
;
Rodriguez et al. 2025
. This further extraction of the squeezed energy is depicted in Fig.
1
. Note that the order of displacement and squeezing is important for this energetic interpretation. If squeezing is applied before displacement, the extracted energies do not correspond to
E_{\cal C}
and
E_{\cal S}
individually. Nevertheless, the total,
i.e.
,
E_{\cal C}+E_{\cal S}
, is the maximum energy that can be extracted via Gaussian unitaries.

For a pure Gaussian state, its corresponding Gaussian-passive state is the vacuum, meaning that the coherent and squeezed energies account for all the energy in the state. In contrast, for a non-Gaussian state, the corresponding Gaussian-passive state is not the vacuum state and has an energy equal to
\sqrt{\det V}-\tfrac{1}{2}
, which we identify as the
non-Gaussian energy
,
E_{\cal NG}
. Finally, the removal of this energy from the Gaussian-passive state, which necessarily requires a non-Gaussian unitary, eliminates all remaining energy, thereby leaving the state in the vacuum [see Fig.
1
].

II.2
E_{\cal NG}
as a measure of non-Gaussianity

The central result of our work is that
E_{\cal NG}
is a measure of non-Gaussianity for pure states. To show this, we must demonstrate the following properties of
E_{\cal NG}

Albarelli et al. 2018
:

1.

It is a well-defined, non-negative quantity,
i.e.
,
E_{\cal NG}\geq 0
.

2.

(Faithfulness)
E_{\cal NG}=0
, iff
\ket{\psi}
is pure Gaussian state.

3.

(Invariant under Gaussian unitaries)
E_{\cal NG}[\ket{\psi}]=E_{\cal NG}[\hat{U}_{G}\ket{\psi}]
, where
\hat{U}_{G}
is a Gaussian unitary,
i.e.
,
\hat{U}_{G}=e^{i\hat{G}}
where
\hat{G}
is a Hermitian operator at most quadratic in annihilation and creation operators.

Properties
1
and
2
follow directly from the Robertson-Schrödinger uncertainty relation
Weedbrook et al. 2012
;
Serafini 2017
, which states that,

{\det V}\geq\frac{1}{4}\,.

(7)

The equality is satisfied iff the state is a pure Gaussian, in which case,
E_{\cal NG}=0
. To prove property
3
, we use the fact that Gaussian unitaries preserve the symplectic eigenvalues of the covariance matrix
Serafini 2017
;
Weedbrook et al. 2012
. For a single mode covariance,
\sqrt{\det V}
is the only symplectic eigenvalue, which implies that
E_{\cal NG}
is invariant under Gaussian unitaries. Thus, properties
1
,
2
, and
3
make
E_{\cal NG}
a valid measure of non-Gaussianity for pure states
Albarelli et al. 2018
.

Having shown that the non-Gaussian energy serves as a measure of non-Gaussianity for pure states, we investigate its relationship with another well-known measure of non-Gaussianity, namely the relative entropy of non-Gaussianity,
\Sigma_{\cal NG}[\rho]

Albarelli et al. 2018
;
Genoni and Paris 2010
;
Genoni et al. 2008
;
Marian and Marian 2013
, which is defined as

\Sigma_{\cal NG}[\rho]=\min_{\sigma\in{\mathbb{G}}}D[\rho||\sigma]\,,

(8)

where
{\mathbb{G}}
denotes the set of Gaussian quantum states and quantum relative entropy is
D[\rho_{1}||\rho_{2}]:=\text{Tr}\!\left[\rho_{1}(\ln\rho_{1}-\ln\rho_{2})\right]
. Computing the minimum over the set of Gaussian state in Eq. (
8
), one finds
Marian and Marian 2013
the simpler form

\Sigma_{\cal NG}[\rho]=S[\rho_{V}]-S[\rho]\,,

(9)

where the von-Neumann entropy is
S[\rho]=-\text{Tr}[\rho\ln\rho]
and
\rho_{V}
, called the reference Gaussian state, is the Gaussian state with the same value of first moment and covariance matrix as
\rho
.

In contrast to our energetic approach, which compares the state with Gaussian state with same entropy but different energy, the relative entropy of non-Gaussianity constitutes an entropic approach to quantifying non-Gaussianity, as depicted in Fig.
1
, which compares the state with the reference Gaussian state which has same energy but different entropy.
Interestingly, the two approaches can be reconciled as we now show.

The von Neumann entropy of the Gaussian state
\rho_{V}
is given by
Demarie 2018
;
Serafini 2017

S[\rho_{V}]=h(\sqrt{\det V})\,,

(10)

where the function
h(x)
is

h(x)=\bigg(x+\frac{1}{2}\bigg)\ln{\bigg(x+\frac{1}{2}\bigg)}-\bigg(x-\frac{1}{2}\bigg)\ln{\bigg(x-\frac{1}{2}\bigg)}\,.

(11)

For a pure state
\rho=\outerproduct{\psi}{\psi}
, since
S[\rho]=0
, the measure simplifies to

\Sigma_{\cal NG}[\outerproduct{\psi}{\psi}]=h(E_{\cal NG}+1/2)\,.

(12)

The derivative of the function
h(x)
, denoted by
h^{\prime}(x)
is

h^{\prime}(x)=\ln({\frac{2x+1}{2x-1}})\,.

(13)

h^{\prime}(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in[1/2,\infty)
, which implies that
h(x)
increases monotonically with
x
. As a result, increasing
E_{\cal NG}
necessarily implies increasing
\Sigma_{\cal NG}[\rho]
. This result shows that for pure states of a single mode bosonic system,
E_{\cal NG}
and
\Sigma_{\cal NG}[\rho]
are equivalent quantifiers of non-Gaussianity, thereby reconciling the entropic approach and energetic approach to quantifying non-Gaussianity.

III
Maximising non-Gaussianity in pure states

For a fixed total energy
E
, the maximum possible value of non-Gaussian energy is
E_{\cal NG}=E
. This maximum is attained for states with
\langle\hat{a}\rangle=0
and
\langle\hat{a}^{2}\rangle=0
.
To show this, we express
\det V
using Eq. (
1
) as

\det V=\bigg(\langle\delta\hat{a}^{\dagger}\delta\hat{a}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\bigg)^{2}-|\langle(\delta\hat{a})^{2}\rangle|^{2}\,,

(14)

from which we obtain the inequality

\det V\leq\bigg(\langle\delta\hat{a}^{\dagger}\delta\hat{a}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\bigg)^{2}\leq\bigg(\langle\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\bigg)^{2}\,,

(15)

Here, the first inequality is saturated when
\langle(\delta\hat{a})^{2}\rangle=0
and the second inequality is saturated when
\langle{\hat{a}}\rangle=0
. Rearranging Eq. (
15
), we obtain

E_{\cal NG}\leq E_{\cal I}\leq E\,.

(16)

The maximum value,
i.e.
,
E_{\cal NG}=E
is satisfied when both the first and second inequalities are saturated,
i.e.
, when
\langle\hat{a}^{2}\rangle=0
and
\langle{\hat{a}}\rangle=0
.
We can identify a class of states with exactly this property: a superposition of Fock states
\ket{n}
which differ from each other by at least three excitations,
i.e.
,
\ket{\psi}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{K}}c_{n}\ket{n}
, where
\mathbb{K}
is the set of non-negative integers such that
|n^{\prime}-n^{\prime\prime}|\geq 3\quad\forall\quad n^{\prime}\neq n^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{K}
. These states are “maximally non-Gaussian” for a fixed energy according to our measure. Since the non-Gaussian energy and the relative entropy of non-Gaussianity are functionally equivalent, these states also maximize the relative entropy of non-Gaussianity at fixed energy, as previously identified in
Genoni et al. 2008
. A simple example of such states are the Fock states
\ket{n}
, where
n\neq 0
. This means that, under a fixed total energy budget, the class of pure states with zero first and second moment uniquely define the most non-Gaussian states.

In the same spirit, one may ask: given a class of non-Gaussian unitaries, which Gaussian input state yields an output state with maximum non-Gaussianity? As an example, consider the Selective Number-dependent Arbitrary Phase (SNAP) gate, which is energy-preserving
Heeres et al. 2015
;
Krastanov et al. 2015
;
Kudra et al. 2022
.
SNAP gates, along with displacements, allows for arbitrary state generation in a bosonic cavity. For simplicity, we consider an initial coherent state
\ket{\alpha_0}=\hat{D}(\alpha_{0})\ket{0}
, and apply a SNAP gate that imparts a phase of
\pi
to one of the Fock component
\ket{n}
. Without loss of generality,
\alpha_{0}
is taken to be real and positive. The output state is given by

\ket{\psi(\alpha_0,n)}=\hat{\Phi}_{n}\ket{\alpha_0}\,,

(17)

where
\hat{\Phi}_{n}=e^{i\pi\ket{n}\bra{n}}
.
The initial coherent state stores all of its energy in the coherent contribution,
i.e.
,
E_{\cal C}=|\alpha_{0}|^{2}
. From an energetic perspective, the SNAP gate redistributes the input coherent energy into output coherent, squeezed and non-Gaussian energy. For fixed
n
, the optimization problem is to determine the value of
\alpha_{0}
that maximizes the non-Gaussianity of
\ket{\psi(\alpha_0,n)}
. The problem reduces to finding the value of
\alpha_{0}
that maximizes the non-Gaussian energy of the output state.

In Fig.
2
, we plot the non-Gaussian energy as a function of
\alpha_{0}
for different values of
n
. We observe that there exists an optimal value of
\alpha_{0}

that maximizes the non-Gaussian energy. By tuning the input coherent amplitude to this value, one can generate the most non-Gaussian state within this setting. For comparison, we also plot the Wigner negativity of the output states as a function of
\alpha_{0}
for the same values of
n
. Wigner negativity is another measure of non-Gaussianity for pure states and is given by
Kenfack and Życzkowski 2004
;
Albarelli et al. 2018

\mathcal{N}[\rho]=\int d^{2}\alpha\,\big|W(\alpha)\big|-1\,,

(18)

where

W(\alpha):=\frac{2}{\pi}\text{Tr}[\rho\,\hat{D}(\alpha)\hat{\Pi}\hat{D}^{\dagger}(\alpha)]

is the Wigner function of the state and

\hat{\Pi}=(-1)^{\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}}
is the parity operator
Wigner 1932
;
Royer 1977
.
We normalize Wigner negativities with respect to the Wigner negativity of a single photon,
i.e.
,
{\cal N}_{\ket{1}}:=\mathcal{N}[\outerproduct{1}{1}]=4e^{-1/2}-2
.

Firstly, we observe that the region of
E_{\cal NG}>0
overlaps with
{\cal N}>0
, as expected. This is due to Hudson’s theorem
Hudson 1974
which states that a pure non-Gaussian state is necessarily Wigner negative. Secondly, we observe that the values of
\alpha_{0}

that maximize the Wigner negativity closely coincide with those that maximize the non-Gaussian energy. This is noteworthy because distinct resource monotones generally need not induce the same ordering on states unless they are related by a monotonic function
Virmani and Plenio 2000
. While the Wigner negativity and the non-Gaussian energy are not related by a monotonic function, we nevertheless observe that their behavior is qualitatively similar, with their maxima occurring at remarkably similar values of
\alpha_{0}
. This observation suggests that, for pure states, non-Gaussian energy can act as an effective probe to maximising Wigner negativity, enabling to approximate optimal parameters and reduce the search space. This is desirable because Wigner negativity is both analytically and computationally difficult to evaluate and optimise. In contrast, the non-Gaussian energy is significantly more tractable analytically and demands less computational resources, making it useful for optimization tasks. Ref.
Lim et al. 2026
successfully applies this strategy to the scattering of a coherent pulse by a two-level atom. In addition to providing a more tractable metric for optimization, the non-Gaussian energy is more experimentally accessible than the negativity, the former only requiring knowledge of the first two moments which can be obtained through photon detection and homodyne detection and the latter requiring full state tomography of the output field.

IV
Extension to mixed states

IV.1
Energetic decomposition for mixed states

We now extend the energetic decomposition to mixed states of single bosonic modes. As in the pure-state case, we seek an energetic quantity that determines whether a given state
\rho
is a mixed Gaussian state. A single mode mixed Gaussian state,
\rho_{G}
, can most generally be expressed as

\rho_{G}\equiv\rho_{G}(\alpha,\xi,\beta)=\hat{D}(\alpha)\hat{S}(\xi)\,\tau(\beta)\,\hat{S}^{\dagger}(\xi)\hat{D}^{\dagger}(\alpha)\,,

(19)

where
\tau(\beta)=e^{-\beta\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}}/\text{Tr}[e^{-\beta\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}}]
is a thermal state with temperature
\beta^{-1}
(in units of
\hbar\omega
).

Figure 3:
A diagrammatic representation of our energetic witness of non-Gaussianity compared with the entropic measure of non-Gaussianity. As in the pure state case [Fig.
1
], our energetic approach first removes energy from the system via Gaussian unitaries. The non-Gaussian energy is then removed via transforming the remaining state to the completely passive state. The entropic approach proceeds as in Fig.
1
.

For an arbitrary single-mode state
\rho
, we decompose the total energy as

E=E_{\cal C}+E_{\cal S}+E_{{\cal T}h}+\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}\,,

(20)

where
E_{\cal C}
and
E_{\cal S}
are given in
Eqs. (
3
) and (
4
) and account for displacement and squeezing of the state, respectively. We quantify the energetic contribution due to the statistical mixedness of the state by the
thermal energy

E_{{\cal T}h}
, defined as

E_{{\cal T}h}:=n_{th}\,,

(21)

where
n_{th}
is the energy of a thermal state
\tau(\beta_{th})
with temperature
\beta_{th}^{-1}
chosen such that
\tau(\beta_{th})
has the same von Neumann entropy as
\rho
,
i.e.
,
S[\tau(\beta_{th})]=S[\rho]
.
\tau(\beta_{th})
is known in the literature
Lenard 1978
;
Pusz and Woronowicz 1978
;
Alicki and Fannes 2013
as the completely passive state. For a general Gaussian state
\rho_{G}
as expressed in Eq. (
19
),
E_{{\cal T}h}
is equal to the energy of
\tau({\beta})
, quantifying the thermal component. Thus,
E_{\cal C}+E_{\cal S}+E_{{\cal T}h}
accounts for all the energy of a Gaussian state.
The energetic contribution due to non-Gaussianity of the state,
\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}
is

\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}:=\left(\sqrt{\det V}-\frac{1}{2}\right)-n_{th}\,.

(22)

Note, here we have chosen to use the symbol
\mathcal{W}
(denoting ‘witness’ as well as ‘work’ for reasons that will become clear) to distinguish this quantity from the pure state quantity.

Just as in the pure state case, for a mixed state
\rho
,
E_{\cal C}
can be extracted by a displacement unitary
\hat{D}(\alpha)
that sets the first moment of
\rho
to zero and
E_{\cal S}
can be extracted by a squeezing unitary
\hat{S}(\xi)
that sets the covariance matrix into its Williamson form. These operations will then bring
\rho
to its corresponding Gaussian-passive state
Brown et al. 2016
;
Rodriguez et al. 2025
, denoted by
\rho^{p}_{G}
,

\rho^{p}_{G}=\hat{S}^{\dagger}(\xi)\hat{D}^{\dagger}(\alpha)\rho\hat{D}(\alpha)\hat{S}(\xi)\,.

(23)

It has been shown in
Lenard 1978
;
Pusz and Woronowicz 1978
;
Alicki and Fannes 2013

that if one further allows for arbitrary unitaries and global entangling unitaries on multiple copies of
\rho_{G}^{p}
, the energy of the state can at most be further reduced to that of the completely passive state,
\tau(\beta_{th})
.
\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}
corresponds to the amount of energy that can be further extracted from
\rho_{G}^{p}
[see Fig.
3
],
i.e.
,

\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}(\rho)=E[\rho_{G}^{p}]-E[\tau(\beta_{th})]\,.

(24)

Note that for pure states, the completely passive state is vacuum. In this case,
\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}
reduces to the non-Gaussian energy as defined in Eq. (
5
) and
E_{{\cal T}h}=0
, recovering the energetic decomposition for pure states in Eq. (
2
).

IV.2
Faithful witness of non-Gaussianity

We now show that
\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}
is a faithful energetic witness of non-Gaussianity. This primarily relies on the extremality property of Gaussian states
Wolf et al. 2006
. Specifically, among all states with a fixed first moment and covariance matrix, the Gaussian state has the maximum von Neumann entropy,
i.e.
,

S[\rho_{V}]\geq S[\rho]\,,

(25)

where we remind that
\rho_{V}
is the reference Gaussian state with the same first moment and covariance matrix as
\rho
.
Equality holds iff
\rho
is Gaussian.
Using the relation for
S[\rho_{V}]
in Eq. (
10
), we get

h(\sqrt{\det V})\geq S[\rho]\,,

(26)

which implies

\sqrt{\det V}\geq h^{-1}(S[\rho])\,.

(27)

For pure states,
S[\rho]=0
and since
h^{-1}(0)=\frac{1}{2}
, we recover the inequality
\sqrt{\det V}\geq\tfrac{1}{2}
, which is the Robertson-Schrödinger uncertainty relation. Thus,
Eq. (
27
) can be viewed as providing a tighter lower bound on
\det V
depending on the mixedness of the state, with Gaussian states saturating the lower bound. Using the relation
S[\rho]=S[\tau(\beta_{th})]=h(n_{th}+1/2)
, we see that Eq. (
27
) implies that

\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}\geq 0\,.

(28)

Thus, we summarise the key properties of
\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}
below:

1.

It is a well defined non-negative quantity,
i.e.
,
\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}\geq 0
.

2.

(Faithfulness)
\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}=0
, iff
\rho
is Gaussian.

3.

(Invariant under Gaussian unitaries)
\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}[\rho]=\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}[\hat{U}_{G}\rho\hat{U}_{G}^{\dagger}]
, where
\hat{U}_{G}
is a Gaussian unitary. This follows from the property (
3
) of non-Gaussian energy for pure states and the fact that
n_{th}
, which depends on the von Neumann entropy, is invariant under unitaries.

These properties make
\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}
a faithful witness of non-Gaussianity. Physically, it quantifies the extent to which
\sqrt{\det V}
, which characterizes the phase-space spread of the state, exceeds the contribution expected from the thermal state of same entropy,
n_{{th}}
, which captures the spread arising solely from the statistical mixedness of the state.

,

\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}
characterizes the non-Gaussianity of single-mode states through the plot shown in Fig.

n_{{th}}+\frac{1}{2}
, while the vertical axis represents

IV.3
From Witness to Measure

To be a valid measure of non-Gaussianity for mixed states
Albarelli et al. 2018
, we need to prove that the non-Gaussian energy is non-increasing under Gaussian
channels
,
i.e.
,
\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}[{\cal G}(\rho)]\leq\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}[\rho]
where
{\cal G}(\rho)=\text{Tr}_{E}[\hat{U}_{G}(\rho\otimes\sigma_{E})\hat{U}_{G}^{\dagger}]
,
\sigma_{E}
is a Gaussian state and
\hat{U}_{G}
is a Gaussian unitary. In the absence of an explicit proof of the monotonicity under Gaussian channels, we provide numerical evidence by sampling states subjected to randomly generated Gaussian channels (see Appendix
B
for details).

The change in
\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}

\Delta\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}=\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}[{\cal G}(\rho)]-\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}[\rho]
, is non-increasing. The data plotted here is a representative 2000 data points of a larger set (50000 points) consider, none of which showed
\Delta\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}>0
.

For the 50,000 examples of states and Gaussian channels we considered,
\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}
was found to be non-increasing. Results for a representative amount of samples are shown here in Fig.
5
. Additionally, in Appendix
C
we provide a route towards analytically proving this result, where we show that
\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}
is non-increasing under thermalization channels for isotropic states (states with zero squeezed energy).

Based on this numerical evidence and the proof for isotropic states under thermalization channels, we conjecture that the
\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}
is a monotone for Gaussian channels.

IV.4
Connection to relative entropy of non-Gaussianity

We now investigate the relationship between
\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}
and the relative entropy of non-Gaussianity
\Sigma_{\cal NG}[\rho]
for a mixed state
\rho
, which is given by [see Eq. (
9
)]

\Sigma_{\cal NG}[\rho]=h(\sqrt{\det V})-h\!\left(n_{th}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\,.

(29)

Both
\Sigma_{\cal NG}[\rho]
and
\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}
are functions of the variables
(\sqrt{\det V},\,n_{th}+\tfrac{1}{2})\equiv(x_{1},x_{2})
which corresponds to a point in the plot shown in Fig.
4
.
The gradient of
\Sigma_{\cal NG}[\rho]
with respect to these variables is

\nabla\Sigma_{\cal NG}=\bigl(h^{\prime}(x_{1}),-h^{\prime}(x_{2})\bigr)\,,

(30)

where
\nabla:=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}},\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}\right)
. Likewise, the gradient of
\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}
is

\nabla\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}=(1,-1)\,.

(31)

The vectors
\nabla\Sigma_{\cal NG}
and
\nabla\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}
are not proportional implying that
\Sigma_{\cal NG}[\rho]
and
\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}
are not monotonic functions of one another. In other words, for mixed states, increasing
\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}
does not necessarily imply an increase in
\Sigma_{\cal NG}[\rho]
. In Fig.
4
, we plot contours of constant
\Sigma_{\cal NG}[\rho]
along with contours of constant
\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}
as functions of
\sqrt{\det V}
and
n_{{th}}+\tfrac{1}{2}
. As expected, the constant lines of these quantities are not parallel. However, they exhibit qualitatively similar behavior, in the sense that both the quantities increase in the regime of large
\sqrt{\det V}
and small
n_{{th}}+\tfrac{1}{2}
.

Although
\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}
and
\Sigma_{\cal NG}[\rho]
are not equivalent quantifiers for mixed states, in Appendix
A
, we derive a necessary and sufficient condition for the increase of
\Sigma_{\cal NG}[\rho]
in terms of energetic quantities. In an infinitesimal transformation which changes the state from
\rho\rightarrow\rho+{\rm d}\rho
, the relative entropy of non-Gaussianity will increase,
i.e.
,
{\rm d}\Sigma_{\cal NG}[\rho]=\Sigma_{\cal NG}[\rho+{\rm d}\rho]-\Sigma_{\cal NG}[\rho]>0
, iff the corresponding change in the energetic quantities
\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}
and
E_{{\cal T}h}
satisfy the following condition:

{\rm d}\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}>\left(\frac{\beta_{th}}{\beta_{V}}-1\right){\rm d}E_{{\cal T}h}\,.

(32)

where
{\rm d}\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}=\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}[\rho+{\rm d}\rho]-\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}[\rho]
,
{\rm d}E_{{\cal T}h}=E_{{\cal T}h}[\rho+{\rm d}\rho]-E_{{\cal T}h}[\rho]
and
\beta_{V}
is the inverse temperature corresponding to the Gaussian-passive state of
\rho_{V}
, which is a thermal state.

In Appendix
D
, we show that for any state
\rho
,
\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}
provides upper and lower bounds on
\Sigma_{\cal NG}[\rho]
, namely,

\beta_{V}\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}\leq\Sigma_{\cal NG}[\rho]\leq\beta_{th}\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}\,.

(33)

The above inequality provides a quantitative connection between the energetic and entropic approaches to non-Gaussianity for mixed states. The upper and lower bound are equal only when the state is Gaussian,
i.e.
,
\Sigma_{\cal NG}[\rho]=\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}=0
. Moreover, as it bounds a measure of non-Gaussianity, it must at least contain some information about the ‘size’ of the non-Gaussianity present in the field, providing slightly more evidence for our conjecture that
\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}
is a valid monotone.

V
Conclusion

In this work, we introduced energetic quantities that characterise the non-Gaussianity of single-mode bosonic states. For pure states, we provide a decomposition of the total energy into coherent, squeezed, and non-Gaussian contributions. We showed that the non-Gaussian contribution,
i.e.
, non-Gaussian energy, constitutes a valid measure of non-Gaussianity and elucidated its relationship with the relative entropy of non-Gaussianity. Furthermore, unlike many existing non-Gaussianity measures, the non-Gaussian energy can be determined experimentally without requiring full state tomography. For mixed states, we demonstrated that the energetic contribution associated with non-Gaussianity serves as a faithful witness of non-Gaussianity. Motivated by extensive numerical evidence, we conjecture that it may also be a valid measure of non-Gaussianity in the mixed-state regime. In addition, we derive upper and lower bounds on the relative entropy of non-Gaussianity in terms of the energetic witness, and identify necessary and sufficient conditions for its increase in terms of energetic quantities. More broadly, our results establish a connection between energetic concepts such as ergotropy and information-theoretic quantifiers of non-Gaussianity. By providing an energetic framework, this work offers new tools for the detection, characterization, and quantification of non-Gaussianity in single mode bosonic systems.

It would be an interesting avenue for future work to extend the non-Gaussian energy to the multimode case. Such an extension could also help reveal energetic signatures of entanglement between the modes
Laverick et al. 2026
. Another important open problem is to prove whether
\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}
is monotonically decreasing under Gaussian channels, for which a multimode formulation may prove useful. Furthermore, understanding the precise role of non-Gaussianity in work extraction by continuous-variable quantum engines and in the cooling of quantum systems
Png et al. 2026
remains an important direction for future research, where our energetic approach may provide a useful framework.

Appendix A
Proof of inequality (
32
)

Let us consider an infinitesimal operation on the state
\rho
which changes the state from
\rho\rightarrow\rho+{\rm d}\rho
. Beginning from Eq. (
29
), and simplifying the notation so that let
x_{1}=\sqrt{\det V}
and
x_{2}=n_{th}+\frac{1}{2}
, we can take the total differential the relative entropy if non-Gaussianity, yielding
{\rm d}\Sigma_{\cal NG}[\rho]=h^{\prime}(x_{1}){\rm d}x_{1}-h^{\prime}(x_{2}){\rm d}x_{2}

i.e.
,
{\rm d}\Sigma_{\cal NG}[\rho]>0

h^{\prime}(x_{1}){\rm d}x_{1}>h^{\prime}(x_{2}){\rm d}x_{2}

({\rm d}x_{1}-{\rm d}x_{2})>\left(\frac{h^{\prime}(x_{2})}{h^{\prime}(x_{1})}-1\right){\rm d}x_{2}\,.

The left-hand side of this equation is simply the change in non-Gaussian energy is
{\rm d}\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}=({\rm d}x_{1}-{\rm d}x_{2})
, while the change in thermal energy is
{\rm d}E_{{\cal T}h}={\rm d}x_{2}
.
Furthermore, using the relation
h^{\prime}(x)=\beta
, where
\beta
denotes the inverse temperature of a thermal state whose symplectic eigenvalue is
x
, we identify
h^{\prime}(\sqrt{\det V})=\beta_{V}
and
h^{\prime}(2n_{th}+1)=\beta_{th}
. Substituting these expressions into Eq. (
34

{\rm d}\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}\geq\left(\frac{\beta_{th}}{\beta_{V}}-1\right){\rm d}E_{{\cal T}h}\,.

(35)

Appendix B
Numerical results for the monotonicity of Non-Gaussian Energy

We describe the numerical procedure used to produce the data in Fig.
5
. Any arbitrary Gaussian channel on a single mode bosonic state
\rho
can be described by
Serafini 2017

\mathcal{E}[\rho]=\text{Tr}_{2}[{\hat{U}_{G}(\rho\otimes\sigma_{E})\hat{U}^{\dagger}_{G}}]\,,

(36)

where
\hat{U}_{G}
is a two-mode Gaussian unitary, and
\sigma_{E}
is a Gaussian state. For our numerical calculations, we take
\sigma_{E}
to be the vacuum state. As for the Gaussian unitary, the Bloch-Messiah decomposition
Weedbrook et al. 2012
of
\hat{U}_{G}
states that
\hat{U}_{G}=\hat{U}_{\rm BS,1}\hat{S}(r_{1}e^{i\phi_{1}})\otimes\hat{S}(r_{2}e^{i\phi_{2}})\hat{U}_{\rm BS,1}
where
\hat{S}_{j}:=\hat{S}(r_{j}e^{i\phi_{j}})
are single mode squeezing operations with real squeezing parameter
r_{j}
and phase
\phi_{j}
and
\hat{U}_{\rm BS,1}
,
\hat{U}_{\rm BS,2}
are passive two-mode linear optical unitaries.

Our numerical procedure first begins by sampling a random mixed state
\rho
according to the Hilbert-Schmidt distribution
Życzkowski and Sommers 2001
by calculating
\rho=\text{Tr}_{2}[\outerproduct{\psi_{12}}{\psi_{12}}]
where
\ket{\psi_{12}}
is a Haar random pure two-mode state. Thereafter, we construct
\hat{U}_{{\rm BS},\ell}
through

\hat{U}_{{\rm BS},\ell}=\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{2}-i\mathcal{H}^{\ell}_{jk}\hat{a}_{j}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{k}\right)\,,

(37)

where
\mathcal{H}^{\ell}=-i\log(\mathcal{U}^\ell)
, and
\mathcal{U}^{\ell}
is a
U(2)
Haar random unitary. Finally, the single mode squeezers
\hat{S}(r_{j}e^{i\phi_{j}})
are generated by sampling
\phi_{j}
and
r_{j}
according to the invariant volume measure on the squeezing manifold,
{\rm d}\mu\propto\sinh{2r}{\rm d}r{\rm d}\phi
. Since the squeezing manifold is non-compact, we introduce a cut-off
r_{\rm max}
, and hence we sample
\phi_{j}
uniformly from
[0,2\pi)
and
r
from the probability distribution function
p(r)=\frac{2\sinh(2r)}{\cosh(2r_{\max})-1}

so that the result squeezing unitaries
\hat{S}
are uniformly distributed on
{\rm SU}(1,1)/{\rm U}(1)
. In our numerics, we start with
\rho
that is supported on up to Fock state
\ket{15}
, and we set
r_{\rm max}=0.5
. All our simulations are done in a truncated single mode Hilbert space with dimension much larger than
15
, so that there are no errors in
\mathcal{E}[\rho]
arising from a truncated Hilbert space.

We sample up to
50000
random initial states
\rho
and Gaussian unitaries
\hat{U}_{G}
using the method described above, and found no increase in
\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}
under the induced Gaussian channel on
\rho
. Our numerical results suggests that
\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}
does not increase under Gaussian channels, which makes it a monotone for non-Gaussianity. More details on our numerical implementation can be found in

Appendix C
Proof that
\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}
is non-increasing under thermalization channel for isotropic states

Here we show that
\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}
is non-increasing under thermalization channels
\mathcal{G}_{th}
for the set of state with zero squeezed energy. Consider a Gaussian state
\rho
with zero coherent and squeezed energy undergoing thermalization with a bath having
\overline{n}
thermal excitations. Let the spontaneous emission rate of
\rho
be
\gamma
and the energy of its reference Gaussian state
\rho_{V}
be
E_{V}=\sqrt{\det V}-1/2=\langle\delta a^{\dagger}\delta a\rangle
. Under the action of the thermalization channel,
E_{V}
evolves as

E_{V}(t)=e^{-\gamma t}E_{V}+(1-e^{-\gamma t})\overline{n}\,.

(38)

We now use the fact that among all input states with a fixed input entropy, the output entropy of a thermalization channel is minimized by a thermal input state
De Palma et al. 2017
. This implies the inequality

S[\mathcal{G}_{th}(\tau(\beta_{th}))]\leq S[\mathcal{G}_{th}(\rho_{V})]\,,

(39)

where
\tau(\beta_{th})
is the completely passive state of
\rho
that describes its thermal energy
E_{{\cal T}h}
. After interacting for time
t
,
\mathcal{G}_{th}
transforms the thermal excitations of
\tau(\beta_{th})
to
e^{-\gamma t}E_{{\cal T}h}+(1-e^{-\gamma t})\overline{n}
, whereas the mixed energy of
\rho
becomes
E_{{\cal T}h}(t)
. As entropy is a monotonic function of the thermal excitations, from the entropy inequality Eq. (
39
), it follows that

e^{-\gamma t}E_{{\cal T}h}+(1-e^{-\gamma t})\overline{n}\leq E_{{\cal T}h}(t).

(40)

Subtracting
E_{V}(t)
from the above inequality and using Eq. (
38
), we find

E_{V}(t)-E_{{\cal T}h}(t)\leq e^{-\gamma t}(E_{V}-E_{{\cal T}h}).

(41)

As
0\leq e^{-\gamma t}\leq 1
and
\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}[\rho]=E_{V}-E_{{\cal T}h}
, we arrive to the result
\Delta\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}=\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}[\mathcal{G}_{th}(\rho)]-\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}[\rho]=(E_{V}(t)-E_{{\cal T}h}(t))-(E_{V}-E_{{\cal T}h})\leq 0
,
i.e.
,
W_{\cal NG}
decreases under the action of the Gaussian channel
{\cal G}_{th}
. When the state
\rho
also has coherent energy, then the proof follows similarly as the coherent energy also decays exponentially as
e^{-\gamma t}
.

Appendix D
Proof of Eq. (
33
)

Let us begin by first showing that
\Sigma_{\cal NG}[\rho]\leq\beta_{th}\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}
. Focussing on the left-hand side, we have, by definition,

\beta_{th}^{-1}\Sigma_{\cal NG}[\rho]=\beta_{th}^{-1}(S[\rho_{V}]-S[\rho])\,.

(42)

Since the state
\tau(\beta_{th})
has the same entropy as
\rho
,
i.e.
,
S[\rho]=S[\tau(\beta_{th})]
and using the fact that the entropy of a thermal state is

S[\tau(\beta)]=\beta E[\tau(\beta)]+\ln Z(\beta)\,,

(43)

where
Z(\beta)=\text{Tr}[e^{\beta\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}}]
, we have

\beta_{th}^{-1}\Sigma_{\cal NG}[\rho]=\beta_{th}^{-1}(S[\rho_{V}]-\ln Z(\beta_{th}))-E_{{\cal T}h}\,,

(44)

where we have recognised that
E[\tau(\beta_{th})]=E_{{\cal T}h}
. By definition, we have that
S[\rho_{V}]=S[\tau(\beta_{V})]
and using the fact that the relative entropy

D[\rho||\tau(\beta)]=\beta E[\rho]-S[\rho]+\ln Z(\beta)\,,

(45)

we have

\beta_{th}^{-1}\Sigma_{\cal NG}[\rho]=\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}-D[\tau(\beta_{V})||\tau(\beta_{th})]\,.

(46)

The right-hand side follow by noticing that
E[\tau(\beta_{V})]=E[\rho_{G}^{p}]
, which can be seen from the fact that
E[\rho_{V}]=E[\rho]
and the unitaries that transform
\rho_{V}\to\tau(\beta_{V})
also transform
\rho\to\rho_{G}^{p}
. Thus,
E[\tau(\beta_{V})]-E_{{\cal T}h}=\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}
. Finally, since
D[\rho_{V}||\tau(\beta_{th})]\geq 0
, we have

\Sigma_{\cal NG}[\rho]\leq\beta_{th}\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}\,.

(47)

For the other bound,
\beta_{V}\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}\leq\Sigma_{\cal NG}[\rho]
, let us consider Eq. (
24
). Using the fact that
E[\rho_{G}^{p}]=E[\tau(\beta_{V})]
as well as Eq. (
43
), we have

\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}=\beta_{V}^{-1}S[\tau(\beta_{V})]-\ln Z(\beta_{V})-E[\tau(\beta_{th})]\,.

(48)

Making use of Eq. (
45
), and that
S[\tau(\beta_{V})]=S[\rho_{V}]
and
S[\tau(\beta_{th})]=S[\rho]
, we obtain

\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}=\beta_{V}^{-1}(S[\rho_{V}]-S[\rho])-D[\tau(\beta_{th})||\tau(\beta_{V})]\,.

(49)

Finally, once again using the fact that
D[\tau(\beta_{th})||\tau(\beta_{V})]\geq 0
, we have

\beta_{V}\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}\leq\Sigma_{\cal NG}[\rho]\,.

(50)

The lower and upper bounds of Eq. (
33
) coincide only when the state is Gaussian,
i.e.
, when
\Sigma_{\cal NG}=\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{NG}}=0
. Note that for a Gaussian state,
\tau(\beta_{{th}})=\tau(\beta_{V})
.
