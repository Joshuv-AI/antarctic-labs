PROJECT SCOPE AND OBJECTIVE

The Lab MATCH prize, funded by the Office of Technology Commercialization through the
Technology Commercialization Fund, was designed to accelerate the commercialization
of national laboratory intellectual property (IP) by incentivizing for-profit companies and
startups to license lab IP and that enable more affordable, available, reliable and secure
energy solutions.

The project sought to develop a repeatable, high-impact prize program designed to accelerate the
commercialization of national laboratory intellectual property (IP) by:

 1. Leveraging the support of industry partnerships/sponsors to contribute towards the prize
 pool.
 2. Engaging national laboratory researcher/inventor engagement to support the process. Lab
 MATCH prize paired national lab staff from NLR, SNL and ORNL with innovators to
 collaborate on and accelerate commercialization plans and action steps.
 3. Incentivizing external innovators to accelerate their plans and activities to license
 technologies, build partnerships, get them market ready and commercialize the
 technologies. The prize focused on awarding prize money to the companies that made the
 most significant advancements in their commercialization plans throughout the prize, and
 who could prove that they had either been successful with their commercialization efforts
 or have a clear pathway for doing so.
 4. Recruiting innovators with varying backgrounds and expertise.
 5. Leveraging templates, licensing/commercialization processes, and partnership
 agreements established by the NLR Foundation and national lab Tech Transfer Offices.
 6. Educating the prize cohort to ensure that we build strong commercialization expertise in a
 growing number of emerging innovators.

BENEFIT TO DOE MISSION
The Lab MATCH prize program offers both technical and commercial benefits, aligning with the
Department of Energy’s (DOE) mission to advance energy solutions through innovation,
commercialization, and market-ready deployment. Below is a detailed summary of these benefits:

Technical Benefits
 1. Accelerate National Lab IP Licensing:
 a. The program drives the licensing and commercialization of national laboratory
 intellectual property (IP), ensuring that emerging technologies advance from
 research to real-world applications.
 b. It facilitates collaboration between labs, researchers, and innovators, increasing
 the likelihood of technical success in bringing breakthroughs to market.
 2. Focus on U.S.-based innovation:
 a. Prize activities must occur within the United States, ensuring that the technologies
 benefit the domestic market and align with DOE’s strategic goals for U.S.-based
 energy dominance.

 Page 4 of 12

 b. Teams must demonstrate both technical expertise and a solid understanding of
 U.S. market fundamentals, supporting the DOE’s emphasis on advancing economic
 prosperity through innovation.
Commercial Benefits
 1. Commercialization of new technologies inventions: By collaborating with
 entrepreneurial teams with varying backgrounds and expertise, the program has the
 potential to foster the commercialization of new and derivative technologies.
 2. Strengthened industry partnerships: Through collaboration with industry partners and
 sponsors, Lab MATCH encourages public-private partnerships, contributing additional
 resources, expertise, and market access to the commercialization process. This aligns with
 TCF’s goal to support partnerships between DOE facilities and industry, and other
 organizations to advance technologies with strong market potential. The prize program
 strengthened partnerships between labs and teams, advancing technology
 commercialization efforts.
 3. Establishment of viable U.S.-based businesses: Competitors are evaluated based on
 their ability to establish viable businesses in the U.S., contributing to economic growth, job
 creation, and domestic energy sector development.
 4. Acceleration of licensing efforts: Proposed commercialization plans must focus on
 licensing, ensuring that technologies are quickly adopted and developed into market-ready
 solutions, shortening the time between innovation and implementation. This aligns with the
 Technology Commercialization Fund’s objective to helps move innovations developed at
 DOE national laboratories, plants, and sites into the marketplace.
Advancing the DOE’s Mission
 1. Market-ready energy solutions: The Lab MATCH Prize directly supports DOE’s focus on
 driving advanced energy technologies into the market, addressing critical technical and
 commercialization challenges to strengthen U.S. competitiveness.
 2. Innovation ecosystem: The prize builds an ecosystem of commercialization expertise,
 connecting labs, entrepreneurs, and industry stakeholders to strengthen ongoing
 innovation and technology transfer.

In summary, the Lab MATCH Prize promotes DOE’s objectives by marrying technical innovation
with commercialization expertise, resulting in scalable, market-ready solutions that strengthen the
U.S. economy, advance energy technologies, and reaffirm U.S. leadership in energy innovation.

  LESSONS LEARNED

  Following are some of the lessons learned over the course of the Lab MATCH prize
  program:

  1. Engaging reviewers with varied backgrounds: Engaging reviewers from different
 areas of expertise (i.e., venture capital, accelerators, university technology
 transfer offices, etc.) is beneficial to get a well-rounded review of submissions and
 ensures a more comprehensive review that accounts for perspectives across the
 innovation and commercialization landscape.
  2. Challenges in securing cash sponsors: Identifying and securing cash
 sponsorships from corporate entities proved challenging, as their interests are
 primarily aligned with supporting activities providing direct benefits to their
 business. However, securing in-kind partnerships presented a more viable
 alternative. This approach successfully enabled substantial non-monetary

                                         Page 10 of 12

 support and opened doors for potential future financial collaborations and
 partnerships between sponsors and prize competitors. We were able to secure a
 Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) for potential future financial
 collaborations from Faurecia Automotive Seating, LLC when the intellectual
 property (IP) selected by prize competitors aligned with their strategic portfolio.
3. Involvement of Technology Transfer Offices (TTOs): Technology Transfer Offices
 (TTOs) from national lab partners were critical in the submission review process.
 Their evaluations ensured teams were assessed properly and provided important
 feedback on the commercial availability of intellectual property (IP), identifying
 potential constraints early in the process. Engaging TTOs from the outset proved
 highly beneficial as they play a key role in the commercialization of lab IP. Their
 involvement in the prize program whether in developing a pre-approved list of
 available IP for commercialization or reviewing submissions was essential in
 identifying roadblocks early, as ultimately, TTOs must approve which lab IP can or
 cannot be commercialized
4. Multiple Applications: Allowing competitors to submit multiple applications
 leveraging different IPs proved beneficial. They had the option to select IP from a
 pre-approved list provided at the start of the prize and/or to submit IP outside the
 list (if the pre-approved options were no longer available for commercialization at
 a later point). This approach helped expand commercialization opportunities and
 provided greater flexibility to participants.
5. Standardized rules document and engaging national labs: We developed a
 rules document, approved by our national lab partners, that can be reused for
 future similar prize efforts. Engaging the labs during the creation of the rules
 document was highly beneficial, as it allowed us to ensure the prize structure
 effectively supported the commercialization of lab IP. Additionally, this process
 helped raise awareness about prize programs among labs that may not have been
 familiar with them, serving as both an educational and collaborative opportunity.
6. Interactive webinars and knowledge sharing: Hosting webinars with TTO
 representatives from different labs facilitated knowledge sharing by highlighting
 what labs look for in agreements and providing sample templates. Bringing TTOs
 closer to competing teams helped educate participants about the
 commercialization process. Additionally, anecdotal experiences, like a
 presentation by an entrepreneur engaged by ADL on successful NLR IP
 commercialization, provided practical insights and inspiration to the teams.
7. Optimal researcher engagement: Allocating additional project funding for
 greater researcher engagement would have been advantageous. In Phase II,
 researchers were allocated five hours. A balance should be struck between
 respecting the researcher’s time and enabling meaningful participation. Future
 programs should aim to maintain this balance while providing sufficient flexibility
 for researchers to engage at levels necessary to maximize commercialization

prospects. The allocated hours should adequately reflect the project's needs and
align with the researcher's goals, interests, and availability.
