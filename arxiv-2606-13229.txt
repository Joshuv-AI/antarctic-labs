Embedded Trefftz DG method for steady Navier–Stokes flow Part I: Oseen linearization
Paul Stocker, Igor Voulis, Christoph Lehrenfeld, Philip L. Lederer

Abstract
We develop an embedded Trefftz-DG method for the Oseen problem and prove a complete stability and quasi-optimality theory in standard DG norms. The key ingredient is a construction of a suitable local complement space to the Trefftz space, on which the Oseen operator is stably invertible. We also derive a reduced formulation of the method, the resulting system is posed in terms of the velocity unknown only, a crucial step in the analysis especially for the nonlinear Navier–Stokes problem in Part II. Keywords : embedded Trefftz methods, discontinuous Galerkin methods, Oseen problem MSC2020 : 65N30, 65N12, 65N15, 76D07, 76M10

Introduction

The present paper is the first part of a two-part work on embedded Trefftz discontinuous Galerkin methods for time-harmonic incompressible flow problems.
In this first part we consider the steady Oseen problem, which forms the linear building block for the Navier–Stokes analysis developed in the second part.
A very detailed comparison with other methods in terms of computational cost is given in
[
19
]
for the Stokes problem, and the exact same comparison applies to the Oseen setting as well.

Trefftz methods, dating back to Trefftz
[
40
]
, are based on approximation spaces contained in the kernel of the underlying differential operator.
By building the PDE locally into the discrete space, they can deliver high approximation quality with substantially fewer degrees of freedom than standard polynomial methods, and are therefore particularly appealing in combination with discontinuous Galerkin (DG) discretizations.
Trefftz-DG methods have by now been developed for a broad range of problems; see, for instance,
[
15
,
14
,
8
,
16
,
10
,
9
,
28
,
1
,
2
,
17
,
18
,
32
]
.
For a detailed comparison to other methods on general meshes, see
[
23
]
.
At the same time, classical Trefftz constructions remain most natural for homogeneous linear equations with piecewise-constant coefficients, while variable coefficients and nonzero right-hand sides are well known to be significantly more challenging.

The embedded Trefftz-DG approach introduced in
[
26
,
21
]
avoids the explicit construction of local Trefftz bases.
Instead, one starts from an underlying polynomial DG space and imposes the PDE only through local projection constraints, thereby selecting a PDE-adapted subspace inside the full DG space.
This point of view makes Trefftz techniques accessible for variable-coefficient operators and inhomogeneous right-hand sides in a generic way.
The approach has already been investigated for a wide range of problems
[
37
,
13
,
27
,
34
,
12
,
31
,
11
]
.
Most importantly for the present work is the study of the Stokes problem presented in
[
19
]
, which is the direct predecessor of the present paper.
There, higher-order pressure components are locally tied to the velocity unknowns, so that only piecewise-constant pressures remain globally coupled, and full stability and quasi-optimality were established.
A unified abstract local-global framework for embedded Trefftz methods was subsequently formulated in
[
20
]
.

The extension from Stokes to Oseen is, however, far from formal.
The convection term introduces variable coefficients through the prescribed convection field, so the constant-coefficient arguments used in the Stokes analysis no longer apply directly.
In particular, the approximation arguments in
[
19
]
rely on Taylor-polynomial constructions on affinely shifted Trefftz spaces, and these are tied to the constant-coefficient structure of the Stokes operator.
For the Oseen operator, a different route is needed.
The key idea of the present paper is therefore to exploit the abstract local-global splitting from
[
20
]
and to construct suitable local complement spaces on which the Oseen operator is stably invertible.
This leads naturally to a reduced formulation for the velocity unknown, in which the higher-order pressure components are again locally determined by the velocity and only the piecewise-constant pressure component remains globally coupled.
The resulting analysis removes the saddle-point structure at the critical local step and allows us to transfer coercivity from the Stokes setting to reduced Oseen-Trefftz spaces under a suitable smallness condition on the convection field.

At the DG level, our construction is also connected to the discrete functional analysis framework of Di Pietro and Ern
[
6
,
7
]
, in particular to the treatment of convective terms and to energy-stable DG formulations for incompressible flow problems.
More broadly, the Oseen problem sits at the interface between classical DG discretizations of viscous incompressible flows and Trefftz-type model reduction ideas.
While Trefftz techniques for Stokes problems do exist, they are much less developed than for wave propagation problems; see, for instance, the spectral-type constructions in
[
33
,
3
]
, the spectral method of
[
25
]
, and the collocation Trefftz approach of
[
24
]
.
As well as approximations via Solenoidal functions, i.e. element-wise divergence-free, a partial requirement of the PDE, in
[
30
,
29
]
.
Another important and complementary strategy for reducing globally coupled degrees of freedom is provided by hybridizable DG methods
[
4
]
.
However, hybridization and Trefftz reduction act in rather different ways, and their benefits are to a large extent competing rather than cumulative.

Contributions.

This paper extends the embedded Trefftz-DG methodology from the Stokes problem
[
19
]
to the steady Oseen equations and provides the linear foundation for the nonlinear Navier–Stokes analysis in Part II
[
38
]
.
Compared with the Stokes predecessor, the present work contains three main new ingredients:

•

We construct suitable local complement spaces
\mathbb{L}(T)
for the Oseen operator.
In contrast to the Stokes case, where the construction relied on constant-coefficient Taylor-polynomial arguments, the convection term leads to a variable-coefficient local operator and requires a more delicate construction.

•

We treat inhomogeneous right-hand sides through a local problem built directly into the formulation, rather than through a correction argument as in the Stokes analysis.
This is better adapted to the Oseen setting and fits naturally into the abstract local-global splitting framework of
[
20
]
.

•

We derive a reduced formulation for the velocity unknown and prove reduced inf–sup stability for the full Oseen operator.
More precisely, after eliminating the higher-order pressure components locally, we establish stability of the reduced Oseen problem and transfer it to the full embedded Trefftz-DG discretization, leading to well-posedness and quasi-optimality in standard DG norms.

These linear results provide the key analytical groundwork for the nonlinear Navier–Stokes theory developed in Part II,
[
38
]
.

1.1
The Oseen problem

The Oseen problem is the canonical linearization of the steady incompressible Navier–Stokes equations around a given convection field.
It is obtained by freezing the advecting velocity in the nonlinear term, replacing
(u\cdot\nabla)u
by
(w\cdot\nabla)u
for a prescribed field
w
.
As such, the Oseen problem serves as the fundamental linear building block for Navier–Stokes theory and, in particular, for fixed-point and iterative linearization arguments.

Consider an open bounded Lipschitz domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
d=2,3
.
The central model problem of this paper is the
Oseen problem
, obtained by linearizing the steady Navier–Stokes equations around a given convection field
w\in[L^{4}(\Omega)]^{d}
:
Find velocity
u\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and pressure
p\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle-\nu\Delta u+(w\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p

\displaystyle=f,\quad\text{in }\Omega,

(1)

\displaystyle\Div u

\displaystyle=0,\quad\text{in }\Omega,

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=0,\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega,

where
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the external body force and
\nu>0
is the dynamic viscosity.
For the ease of presentation we only consider homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions.
The Navier–Stokes problem (obtained by setting
w=u
in (
1
)) is treated in Part II,
[
38
]
.

1.

We drop the customary subscript

3.

The superscript

,

,

{\mathcal{F}_{h}}={\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\text{i}}}\cup{\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\text{b}}}

With abuse of notation, we write

Thus

\displaystyle a_{h}^{\!\texttt{d}}(u_{h},v_{h})\!

where we used the notation

The interior penalty parameter

2.2
Local operator and Trefftz condition

We now introduce our Trefftz space as the local kernel of a relaxed PDE operator.
Rather than requiring functions to lie in the kernel of the strong PDE operator—which may be trivial for
w\neq 0
—we relax the condition by testing only against a suitable lower-order polynomial space.
We therefore first introduce the test space

\mathbb{Q}(T)=[\mathbb{P}^{k-2}(T)]^{d}\times\mathbb{P}^{k-1}(T),\quad\mathbb{Q}=\mathbb{Q}({\mathcal{T}_{h}})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\prod_{T\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}\mathbb{Q}(T),

(
\mathbb{Q}
)

where we write elements as
\sigma_{h}=(r_{h},s_{h})\in\mathbb{Q}(T)
.

With
\mathbb{X}^{\texttt{reg}}(T)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=[H^{2}(T)]^{d}\times H^{1}(T)
, we define the local PDE operator
K_{T,w}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{X}^{\texttt{reg}}(T)\to\mathbb{Q}(T)^{\prime}
directly via the duality pairing
for
\sigma_{h}=(r_{h},s_{h})\in\mathbb{Q}(T)
and

\xi=(u,p)\in\mathbb{X}^{\texttt{reg}}(T)
:

\langle K_{T,w}\xi,\,\sigma_{h}\rangle_{T}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=h\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big(-\nu\Delta u+w\cdot\nabla u+\nabla p,\,r_{h}\big)_{T}+\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(-\Div u,\,s_{h}\big)_{T}.

(
K_{T,w}
)

Accordingly, we set
K_{w}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{X}^{\texttt{reg}}\to\mathbb{Q}^{\prime}
via
\langle K_{w}\xi_{h},\,\sigma_{h}\rangle_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}=\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}\langle K_{T,w}\xi_{h},\,\sigma_{h}\rangle_{T}
. Here
\mathbb{X}^{\texttt{reg}}=\mathbb{X}^{\texttt{reg}}({\mathcal{T}_{h}})=[H^{2}({\mathcal{T}_{h}})]^{d}\times H^{1}({\mathcal{T}_{h}})
.
The scaling factor
h\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}
in the first component balances the scaling with respect to our choice of norms, as we will see in the analysis.

With this PDE operator defined, we set the Trefftz space as the subspace of
\mathbb{X}
satisfying the Trefftz condition element-wise:

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}_{w}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=

\displaystyle\{\xi_{h}\in\mathbb{X}\mid\langle K_{w}\xi_{h},\sigma_{h}\rangle_{T}=0\quad\forall\sigma_{h}\in\mathbb{Q}({\mathcal{T}_{h}})\}.

(
\mathbb{T}_{w}
)

For
w=0
(the Stokes case),
the residuals
-\nu\Delta u_{h}+\nabla p_{h}
and
-\Div u_{h}
already lie in
\mathbb{Q}(T)
for any
\xi_{h}\in\mathbb{X}(T)
,
T\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}
, so
K_{0}
coincides with the (element-wise) strong Stokes operator.
For
w\neq 0
, the pairing
(w\cdot\nabla u_{h},r_{h})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}
implicitly involves only the
L^{2}
-projection of
w\cdot\nabla u_{h}
into
[\mathbb{P}^{k-2}]^{d}
, i.e. functions in
\mathbb{T}_{w}
fulfill the PDE only in a relaxed sense.

2.4
The convection trilinear form

To discretize the convective term

3.1

While an explicit construction of the complement space to the Trefftz space is not needed for the implementation of the method,

Stokes case

We start off with the following continuity result for the local Stokes operator

Lemma 3.3

(Continuity of
K_{T,0}
)
.

For all
\xi=(u,p)\in\mathbb{X}^{\texttt{reg}}
there holds

\|K_{0}\xi\|_{\mathbb{Q}^{\prime}}^{2}=\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}\|K_{T,0}\xi\|_{\mathbb{Q}(T)^{\prime}}^{2}\leq\beta_{\scalebox{0.6}{$K_{0}$}}^{2}\,\,\lVert\xi\rVert_{\mathbb{X},\ast}^{2},

(9)

with
\beta_{\scalebox{0.6}{$K_{0}$}}>0
depending only on the shape-regularity of
{\mathcal{T}_{h}}
and on

.

Proof.

The
L^{2}
representation of
K_{0}\xi
in
\mathbb{Q}
is
(h_{T}\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Pi^{k-2}(-\nu\Delta u+\nabla p),-\Pi^{k-1}\Div u)
.
With the continuity of the
L^{2}
projection we then have on each element

\|K_{T,0}\xi\|_{\mathbb{Q}(T)^{\prime}}^{2}\leq\|h_{T}\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}(-\nu\Delta u+\nabla p)\|_{T}^{2}+\nu\|\Div u\|_{T}^{2},

Using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality
and
\|\Div u_{h}\|_{T}\leq\|\nabla u_{h}\|_{T}
we bound

\displaystyle\|K_{T,0}\xi_{h}\|_{\mathbb{Q}(T)^{\prime}}^{2}\leq C\Big(\nu\,\|\nabla u\|_{T}^{2}+\nu\,\|h_{T}\Delta u\|_{T}^{2}+\nu^{-1}\|h_{T}\nabla p_{h}\|_{T}^{2}\Big).

Summing over
T\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}
gives (
9
).
∎

We now wish to verify that the local Stokes operator
K_{0}
is invertible on a suitable subspace of
\mathbb{X}
. As a intermediate result, we use the following estimate for functions in the bubble space
[\mathbb{B}^{k}_{0}(T)]^{d}=[\mathbb{P}^{k}(T)\cap H^{1}_{0}(B_{T})]^{d}
enriched by
\Span\{\chi\}
.

Lemma 3.4

(Auxiliary estimate for a bubble space)
.

There exists a constant
C>0
depending only on
k
(and
d
) such that for all

u_{h}\in[\mathbb{B}^{k}_{0}(T)]^{d}\oplus\Span\{\chi\}
and all
p_{h}\in\mathbb{P}^{k-1}(T)
there holds

\|\nabla u_{h}\|_{T}^{2}\leq C\Big(\frac{h_{T}^{2}}{\nu^{2}}\|-\nu\Delta u_{h}+\nabla p_{h}\|_{T}^{2}+\|\Div u_{h}\|_{T}^{2}\Big).

(10)

Proof.

We will show (
10
) w.r.t. to the
L^{2}(B_{T})
-norms, which is sufficient since the norms on
T
and
B_{T}
are equivalent on the finite-dimensional space
[\mathbb{B}^{k}_{0}(T)]^{d}\oplus\Span\{\chi\}
.
In the following we will derive a splitting
u_{h}=u_{\ell}+u_{0}=u_{\ell}+u_{\perp}+u_{\Div}
for which the obvious estimate

\|\nabla u_{h}\|_{B_{T}}^{2}\leq C\big(\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{B_{T}}^{2}+\|\nabla u_{\ell}\|_{B_{T}}^{2}\big)=C\big(\|\nabla u_{\Div}\|_{B_{T}}^{2}+\|\nabla u_{\perp}\|_{B_{T}}^{2}+\|\nabla u_{\ell}\|_{B_{T}}^{2}\big)

holds. Subsequently, we will derive the splitting and a bound for each of these summands.

1. Split off the affine part.

Set

u_{\ell}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{\Pi^{0}(\Div u_{h})}{\Div(\chi)}\,\chi=d^{-1}\Pi^{0}(\Div u_{h})\,\chi\in\Span\{\chi\},\qquad u_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=u_{h}-u_{\ell}\in\mathbb{B}^{k}_{0},

so that
\Pi^{0}\Div u_{h}=\Pi^{0}\Div u_{\ell}
and
\Pi^{0}\Div u_{0}=0
and with
\nabla\chi=I
we find

\|\nabla u_{\ell}\|_{B_{T}}\leq d^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\Div u_{h}\|_{B_{T}}
.

2.
H^{1}_{0}
-orthogonal decomposition of
u_{0}
into
u_{\Div}
and
u_{\perp}
.
Let

\mathbb{B}_{\Div}(T)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{v\in\mathbb{B}^{k}_{0}(T)\mid\Div v=0\},

and let
\mathbb{B}_{\Div}^{\perp}(T)
be its orthogonal complement in
\mathbb{B}^{k}_{0}(T)
with respect to

(\nabla\cdot,\nabla\cdot)_{B_{T}}
.
Then
u_{0}=u_{\Div}+u_{\perp}
with
u_{\Div}\in\mathbb{B}_{\Div}(T)
,
u_{\perp}\in\mathbb{B}_{\Div}^{\perp}(T)
, and

\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{B_{T}}^{2}=\|\nabla u_{\Div}\|_{B_{T}}^{2}+\|\nabla u_{\perp}\|_{B_{T}}^{2},\qquad\Div u_{0}=\Div u_{\perp}.

Since
v\mapsto\|\Div v\|_{B_{T}}
is a norm on the finite-dimensional space
\mathbb{B}_{\Div}^{\perp}(T)
, there exists

c_{\perp}>0
depending only on
k
such that

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{\perp}\|_{B_{T}}

\displaystyle\leq c_{\perp}\|\Div u_{\perp}\|_{B_{T}}=c_{\perp}\|\Div u_{0}\|_{B_{T}}\leq\|\Div u_{h}\|_{B_{T}}+\|\Div u_{\ell}\|_{B_{T}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Div u_{h}\|_{B_{T}}+\|\nabla u_{\ell}\|_{B_{T}}\leq(1+d^{-\frac{1}{2}})\|\Div u_{h}\|_{B_{T}}.

3. Stokes estimate on the divergence-free part.

Since
u_{\Div}\in[H^{1}_{0}(B_{T})]^{d}
and
\Div u_{\Div}=0
, we have
(\nabla p_{h},u_{\Div})_{B_{T}}=0
.
Moreover
u_{\ell}
is affine, hence
\Delta u_{h}=\Delta u_{0}
.
Thus, integrating by parts, Cauchy–Schwarz and Poincaré on

\displaystyle\nu\|\nabla u_{\Div}\|_{B_{T}}^{2}

\displaystyle=(\nu\nabla(u_{0}-u_{\perp}),\nabla u_{\Div})_{B_{T}}=(\nu\nabla u_{0},\nabla u_{\Div})_{B_{T}}=(-\nu\Delta u_{0},u_{\Div})_{B_{T}}

\displaystyle=(-\nu\Delta u_{h}+\nabla p_{h},u_{\Div})_{B_{T}}\leq\|-\nu\Delta u_{h}+\nabla p_{h}\|_{B_{T}}\,\|u_{\Div}\|_{B_{T}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|-\nu\Delta u_{h}+\nabla p_{h}\|_{B_{T}}\,h\,\|\nabla u_{\Div}\|_{B_{T}}.

Hence,

\|\nabla u_{\Div}\|_{B_{T}}^{2}\leq C^{2}\,\frac{h^{2}}{\nu^{2}}\,\|-\nu\Delta u_{h}+\nabla p_{h}\|_{B_{T}}^{2}.

∎

on

Theorem 3.5

(Stable invertibility of
K_{0}
on
\mathbb{L}
)
.

For
T\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}
, the local Stokes operator
K_{T,0}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{L}(T)\to\mathbb{Q}(T)^{\prime}
is a bijection. Moreover, there exists
\alpha_{\scalebox{0.6}{$K_{0}$}}>0
,
depending only on
k
and the shape-regularity of
T
(in particular through the constant in

\xi_{h}\in\mathbb{L}(T)
there holds

\|K_{T,0}\xi_{h}\|_{T}\geq\alpha_{\scalebox{0.6}{$K_{0}$}}\Big(\nu\|\nabla u_{h}\|_{T}^{2}+\nu h_{T}^{-1}\|u_{h}\|_{\partial T}^{2}+\nu^{-1}\|h_{T}\nabla p_{h}\|_{T}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\!

(11)

Hence,
K_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{L}\to\mathbb{Q}^{\prime}
is also a bijection and
\|K_{0}\xi_{h}\|_{\mathbb{Q}^{\prime}}\geq\alpha_{\scalebox{0.6}{$K_{0}$}}\lVert\xi_{h}\rVert_{\mathbb{X}}
for all
\xi_{h}\in\mathbb{L}
.

Proof.

We set
u_{h}=\tilde{u}_{h}-u_{c}
with
\tilde{u}_{h}\in[\mathbb{B}_{0}^{k}(T)]^{d}+\Span\{\chi\}
,
u_{c}\in[\mathbb{P}^{0}(T)]^{d}=\Span\{n_{T,1},\dots,n_{T,d}\}
.
Explicitly
u_{c}
is the unique vector such that
\int_{F_{T,i}}\tilde{u}_{h}\cdot n_{T,i}=|F_{T,i}|u_{c}\cdot n_{T,i}
for
i=1,\ldots,d
, which is well-defined by the linear independence of
n_{T,1},\ldots,n_{T,d}
.

The bounds for the three contributions
\nu\|\nabla u_{h}\|_{T}^{2}
,
\nu h_{T}^{-1}\|u_{h}\|_{\partial T}^{2}
and
\nu^{-1}\|h_{T}\nabla p_{h}\|_{T}^{2}
are then shown one after another.

1. Control of
\nu\|\nabla u_{h}\|_{T}^{2}
.

Applying (
10
) yields

\nu\|\nabla u_{h}\|_{T}^{2}=\nu\|\nabla\tilde{u}_{h}\|_{T}^{2}\leq C\Big(\|h_{T}\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}(-\nu\Delta u_{h}+\nabla p_{h})\|_{T}^{2}+\|\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}\Div u_{h}\|_{T}^{2}\Big)=C\,\|K_{T,0}\xi_{h}\|_{T}^{2}.

2. Control of the boundary term
\nu h_{T}^{-1}\|u_{h}\|_{\partial T}^{2}
.

Due to
\int_{F_{T,i}}\tilde{u}_{h}\cdot n_{T,i}=|F_{T,i}|u_{c}\cdot n_{T,i}
for
i=1,\ldots,d
, we have

\|u_{h}\|_{\partial T}\leq\|u_{c}\|_{\partial T}+\|\tilde{u}_{h}\|_{\partial T}\leq C\|\tilde{u}_{h}\|_{\partial T}.

It hence suffices to show the bound for
\tilde{u}_{h}
. By the trace inequality on
T
and a Poincaré estimate on
[\mathbb{B}_{0}^{k}(T)]^{d}
(exploiting that it does not contain constants),

\displaystyle\nu h^{-1}\|\tilde{u}_{h}\|_{\partial T}^{2}\leq C\nu\|\nabla\tilde{u}_{h}\|_{T}^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:ball_est}}}{{\leq}}C\|K_{T,0}\xi_{h}\|_{T}^{2}.

3. Control of pressure contribution
\nu^{-1}\|h_{T}\nabla p_{h}\|_{T}^{2}
.

By the triangle inequality and the inverse bound
h_{T}\|\Delta u_{h}\|_{T}\leq C\|\nabla u_{h}\|_{T}
,

\|h_{T}\nabla p_{h}\|_{T}\leq\|h_{T}(-\nu\Delta u_{h}+\nabla p_{h})\|_{T}+\nu\,\|h_{T}\Delta u_{h}\|_{T}\leq\|h_{T}(-\nu\Delta u_{h}+\nabla p_{h})\|_{T}+C\,\nu\|\nabla u_{h}\|_{T}.

Multiplying by
\nu^{-1}
and using step 1 yields

\nu^{-1}\|h_{T}\nabla p_{h}\|_{T}^{2}\leq C\,\|K_{T,0}\xi_{h}\|_{T}^{2}.

4. Conclusion and bijectivity.

Combining the above estimates, we obtain (
11
).
In particular,
K_{T,0}\xi_{h}=0
implies
\xi_{h}=0
, so
K_{T,0}
is injective on
\mathbb{L}(T)
.
Since
\mathbb{L}(T)
and
\mathbb{Q}(T)
are finite-dimensional and have the same dimension (by construction),
K_{T,0}
is bijective.
∎

\lVert u-\underline{u}_{h}\rVert_{\underline{\mathbb{X}}}\leq\left(1+\frac{C_{\rm app}(w)}{\alpha_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\underline{c}_{w}$}}}\right)\lVert u-\underline{v}_{h}\rVert_{\underline{\mathbb{X}},\ast}+\frac{C_{p}}{\alpha_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\underline{c}_{w}$}}}\,\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}h^{\ell}\|p\|_{H^{\ell}({\mathcal{T}_{h}})}.

Taking the infimum over all
\underline{v}_{h}\in\underline{\mathbb{X}}
with
u-\underline{v}_{h}\in\mathbb{X}_{\rm ps}({\mathcal{T}_{h}})
proves (
31
).

For (
32
), the standard BDM estimate gives

\Pi^{\mathbb{B}\mathbb{D}\mathbb{M}}_{k}u\in[\mathbb{P}^{k}]^{d}
with

\Pi^{0}(\Pi_{k}^{\mathbb{B}\mathbb{D}\mathbb{M}}u)=\Pi^{0}u
elementwise for
k\geq 2
,

\Div\Pi^{\mathbb{B}\mathbb{D}\mathbb{M}}_{k}u|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}=0
(for
\Div u=0
) and
[\![\Pi^{\mathbb{B}\mathbb{D}\mathbb{M}}_{k}u\cdot n]\!]=0
on all

. Further,

\lVert u-\Pi^{\mathbb{B}\mathbb{D}\mathbb{M}}_{k}u\rVert_{\underline{\mathbb{X}},\ast}=\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert u-\Pi^{\mathbb{B}\mathbb{D}\mathbb{M}}_{k}u\rVert_{1,h,\ast}\lesssim\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}h^{k}|u|_{H^{k+1}({\mathcal{T}_{h}})}.

Inserting this choice into (
31
) yields (

).

\displaystyle+\;\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}h^{\ell_{1}+1}\|f\|_{H^{\ell_{1}}({\mathcal{T}_{h}})}

(34)

\displaystyle+\;\left(\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}+\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}|w|_{W^{\ell_{2},\infty}({\mathcal{T}_{h}})}h\right)h^{\ell_{2}}\|u\|_{H^{\ell_{2}+1}({\mathcal{T}_{h}})},

B.3

In the LDG framework of
[
5

, convection is discretized by an upwind numerical flux.
In the mixed (primal) setting, the convective contribution can be written (for a given convecting field

\displaystyle a_{h,w}^{\!\texttt{c}}(u_{h},v_{h})

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=-((\mathcal{P}_{\Div}w_{h}\cdot\nabla)u_{h},v_{h})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\big\langle(\mathcal{P}_{\Div}w_{h}\cdot n_{F})u_{h}^{w},[\![v_{h}]\!]\big\rangle_{{\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\text{i}}}}+\big\langle(\mathcal{P}_{\Div}w_{h}\cdot n)\,u_{h}^{w},v_{h}\big\rangle_{{\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\text{b}}}}

(46)

where
\mathcal{P}_{\Div}
is a suitable projection onto the space of

-conforming element-wise divergence-free vector fields, for example the BDM-interpolation operator.
And
u_{h}^{w}
is the standard upwind trace

u_{h}^{w}=\begin{cases}u_{h}^{+}&\text{if }\mathcal{P}_{\Div}w\cdot n_{F}>0,\\
u_{h}^{-}&\text{if }\mathcal{P}_{\Div}w\cdot n_{F}<0.\end{cases}

A key observation in
