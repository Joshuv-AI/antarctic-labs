A Note on the Construction of Trial States for the Dilute Bose Gas
Morris Brooks, Jakob Oldenburg, Diane Saint Aubin

Abstract
We review how the local particle number cutoff introduced in [ 11 ] is used to build trial states for the dilute Bose gas that capture the substantial correlation structure of the ground state in the thermodynamic limit. In particular, we provide a simplified derivation of the Lee-Huang-Yang correction as an upper bound for the ground state energy.



1
Introduction

We consider a Bose gas in the thermodynamic limit, described by the Hamilton operator

H_{L,N}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}-\Delta_{i}+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq N}V(x_{i}-x_{j}),

acting on the Hilbert space of permutation symmetric
L^{2}
-functions
L^{2}_{s}(\Lambda_{L}^{N})
, where
\Lambda_{L}=[-L/2,L/2]^{3}
is the thermodynamic box and we impose Dirichlet boundary conditions. In this article we study the dilute regime, i.e. we let the length of the box
L
and the number of particles
N
go jointly to infinity at a fixed but small value
\rho:=N/L^{3}
. We further assume the interaction
0\leq V\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
to be radial and compactly supported.

It is well understood that correlations play a crucial role when it comes to low-energy properties of the operator
H_{L,N}
. In particular, it has been verified in
[
18
,
30
]
that the (ground state) energy per unit volume

e(\rho)=\lim_{N,L\to\infty,\rho=N/L^{3}}\frac{E(N,L)}{L^{3}}

is asymptotically given in terms of the scattering length as

\displaystyle e(\rho)=4\pi\mathfrak{a}\rho^{2}+o_{\rho\rightarrow 0}(\rho^{2}).

(1)

Notably, for
V\neq 0
, the scattering length satisfies
8\pi\mathfrak{a}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(x)V(x)dx<\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}V(x)dx
, where
f:\mathbb{R}^{3}\longrightarrow[0,1]
denotes the solution of the zero-energy scattering equation

\left[-\Delta+\frac{1}{2}V\right]f=0

(2)

with the boundary condition
f(x)\to 1
as
|x|\to\infty
and
\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}V(x)dx
is the optimal constant that can be obtained with a pure product state. Beyond the leading order asymptotics in (
1
), it has been predicted by Lee-Huang-Yang
[
28
]
, based on previous work of Bogoliubov
[
10
]
,

\displaystyle e(\rho)=4\pi\mathfrak{a}\rho^{2}\left(1+\frac{128}{15\sqrt{\pi}}\sqrt{\rho\mathfrak{a}^{3}}\right)+o_{\rho\rightarrow 0}\!\left(\rho^{\frac{5}{2}}\right),

(3)

which has been verified at the Gross-Pitaevskii scale
\rho\sim N^{-2}
in
[
8
]
as well as in the thermodynamic limit by
[
35
,
22
,
23
]
, see also
[
19
,
4
]
for a second order upper bound. Following these landmark results, simplified proofs and improvements of (
3
) have been presented in e.g.
[
2
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
26
,
20
,
16
,
3
]
. Extensions have been obtained in
[
15
,
32
]
for more general trapping potentials, spectral properties beyond the ground state energy have been analyzed in intermediate regimes
[
13
]
and the free energy at low temperatures has been investigated in
[
24
,
25
,
21
]
. Furthermore, the ground state and its condensation have been studied in
[
1
,
7
,
12
,
14
,
29
,
31
]
.

The third-order correction to the asymptotics in (
3
)

\displaystyle e(\rho)=4\pi\mathfrak{a}\rho^{2}\left(1+\frac{128}{15\sqrt{\pi}}\sqrt{\rho\mathfrak{a}^{3}}+8\left(\frac{4\pi}{3}-\sqrt{3}\right)\rho\mathfrak{a}^{3}\log(\rho\mathfrak{a}^{3})+C\rho\mathfrak{a}^{3}\right)

(4)

has been predicted by Wu
[
36
]
in 1959, and then also by Hugenholtz-Pines
[
27
]
and Sawada
[
34
]
, and has been verified at the Gross-Pitaevskii scale in
[
17
]
. An upper bound matching (
4
) was recently established in the thermodynamic limit in
[
11
]
. In this manuscript, we review the method employed in
[
11
]
, and provide a simplified proof of the upper bound in (
3
) as the content of our main Theorem
1
. Since, in contrast to the third order asymptotics (
4
), only correlations on comparatively small length scales are relevant in (
3
), our proof of Theorem
1
focuses on the conceptual novelties in
[
11
]
and bypasses many technical complications.

Theorem 1
.

Let
0\leq V\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
be radial and with compact support and let
\mathfrak{a}
be its scattering length. Then, there are constant
C,h>0
such that

e(\rho)\leq 4\pi\mathfrak{a}\rho^{2}\left(1+\frac{128}{15\sqrt{\pi}}\sqrt{\rho\mathfrak{a}^{3}}+C\left(\rho\mathfrak{a}^{3}\right)^{1/2+h}\right)

for all
\rho\mathfrak{a}^{3}>0
small enough.

The proof of Theorem
1
follows immediately by the equivalences of ensembles, once we can provide a grand canonical trial state with the correct energy and particle density. By a well-known localization technique, it is furthermore sufficient to consider trial states on a periodic box of size
L=\rho^{-\gamma}
, given that
\gamma
is large enough. In order to reach a precision that allows us to detect the Lee-Huang-Yang term, we require
\gamma>1
.

It is the content of Theorem
2
to provide an upper bound on the energy in this setting (grand canonical and periodic box of size
L=\rho^{-\gamma}
), by construction of an appropriate trial state. Since it can be a delicate task to find a state with the exact right number of particles, we first construct a trial state that has slightly less and one that has slightly more particles, which are then convex combined to a trial state with the correct density, see also
[
9
]
for a similar argument. Before we state our main technical Theorem
2
, let us introduce the parameter
\kappa=(2\gamma-1)/(3\gamma-1)
, which allows us to express
L=N^{1-\kappa}
. By rescaling to the unit torus
\Lambda\equiv\Lambda_{1}\simeq[-1/2;1/2]^{3}
, we observe that the grand-canonical operator
\bigoplus_{N=0}^{\infty}H_{L,N}
, acting on the Fock space
\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)=\bigoplus_{N\geq 0}L^{2}_{s}(\Lambda_{L}^{N})
, is unitarily equivalent to the operator
L^{-2}\mathcal{H}_{N}
acting on
\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)=\bigoplus_{N\geq 0}L^{2}_{s}(\Lambda^{N})
, where

\mathcal{H}_{N}=\sum_{p\in\Lambda^{*}}p^{2}a_{p}^{*}a_{p}+\frac{N^{\kappa}}{2N}\sum_{p,q,r\in\Lambda^{*}}\hat{V}(r/N^{1-\kappa})a_{p+r}^{*}a_{q-r}^{*}a_{q}a_{p},

and
a_{p}^{*}
and
a_{p}
, for
p\in\Lambda^{*}:=2\pi\mathbb{Z}^{3}
, are the bosonic creation and annihilation operators.

Theorem 2
.

Let
0\leq V\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
be radial and with compact support. Then, for any
\kappa\in(0,2/3)
,
\epsilon>0
and
N
large enough there exists
\Psi^{\pm}_{N}\in\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
with
\lVert\Psi^{\pm}_{N}\rVert=1
and constants
C,h>0
, such that

{\langle\Psi^{-}_{N},\mathcal{N}\Psi^{-}_{N}\rangle+N^{\frac{3\kappa}{2}-\epsilon}\leq N\leq\langle\Psi^{+}_{N},\mathcal{N}\Psi^{+}_{N}\rangle-N^{\frac{3\kappa}{2}-\epsilon}\leq 2N},

(5)

and the energy of
\Psi^{\pm}_{N}
is bounded by

\begin{split}\langle\Psi^{\pm}_{N},\mathcal{H}_{N}\Psi^{\pm}_{N}\rangle\leq\;&4\pi\mathfrak{a}N^{1+\kappa}+\frac{512\sqrt{\pi}}{15}\mathfrak{a}^{5/2}N^{5\kappa/2}+CN^{5\kappa/2-h}.\end{split}

(6)

With Theorem
2
, we can prove our main result.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Rescaling to a box of length
L_{0}=\rho^{-\gamma}
, with
\gamma:=\frac{1-\kappa}{2-3\kappa}
, we obtain states
\Psi^{\pm}_{L_{0}}
on the periodic box
\Lambda_{L_{0}}
satisfying

\displaystyle L_{0}^{-3}\langle\Psi^{-}_{L_{0}},\mathcal{N}\Psi^{-}_{L_{0}}\rangle+\rho^{3/2+\frac{\epsilon}{2-3\kappa}}

\displaystyle\leq\rho\leq L_{0}^{-3}\langle\Psi^{+}_{L_{0}},\mathcal{N}\Psi^{+}_{L_{0}}\rangle-\rho^{3/2+\frac{\epsilon}{2-3\kappa}}\leq 2\rho,

(7)

\displaystyle L_{0}^{-3}\langle\Psi^{\pm}_{L_{0}},\mathcal{H}_{N}\Psi^{\pm}_{L_{0}}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq 4\pi\mathfrak{a}\rho^{2}+\frac{512\sqrt{\pi}}{15}\mathfrak{a}^{5/2}\rho^{5/2}+C\rho^{5/2+\frac{h}{2-3\kappa}},

Utilizing the methods in
[
6
, Appendix A]
, we find states
\Phi^{\pm}_{L}
on the thermodynamic box
\Lambda_{L}
with

\displaystyle|L^{-3}\langle\Phi^{\pm}_{L},\mathcal{N}\Phi^{\pm}_{L}\rangle-L_{0}^{-3}\langle\Psi^{\pm}_{L_{0}},\mathcal{N}\Psi^{\pm}_{L_{0}}\rangle|\leq\rho^{3/2+\frac{\epsilon}{2-3\kappa}},

(8)

and
L^{-3}\langle\Phi^{\pm}_{L},\mathcal{H}_{N}\Phi^{\pm}_{L}\rangle\leq L_{0}^{-3}\langle\Psi^{\pm}_{L_{0}},\mathcal{H}_{N}\Psi^{\pm}_{L_{0}}\rangle+C\rho^{1/2+2\gamma-\frac{\epsilon}{2-3\kappa}}
. Consequently, (
7
) and (
8
) guarantee that there exists a convex combination
\rho L^{3}=t\langle\Phi^{-}_{L},\mathcal{N}\Phi^{-}_{L}\rangle+(1-t)\langle\Phi^{+}_{L},\mathcal{N}\Phi^{+}_{L}\rangle
,
t\in[0,1]
, or put in a different way there exists a
t\in[0,1]
such that

\displaystyle\Gamma_{L}:=t\Pi_{\Phi^{-}_{L}}+(1-t)\Pi_{\Phi^{+}_{L}}

defined via projections
\Pi_{\Phi^{\pm}_{L}}
satisfies
\mathrm{Tr}[\mathcal{N}\,\Gamma_{L}]=\rho L^{3}
. According to the equivalences of ensembles
[
33
]
we conclude

\displaystyle e(\rho)\leq\liminf_{L}L^{-3}\mathrm{Tr}[\mathcal{H}_{L}\Gamma_{L}]\leq 4\pi\mathfrak{a}\rho^{2}+\frac{512\sqrt{\pi}}{15}\mathfrak{a}^{5/2}\rho^{5/2}+C\rho^{5/2+\frac{h}{2-3\kappa}}+C\rho^{1/2+2\gamma-\frac{\epsilon}{2-3\kappa}},

where we choose e.g.
\epsilon:=1/3
and
\kappa<\frac{2}{3}
large enough such that
1/2+2\gamma-\frac{1}{3(2-3\kappa)}
is larger than
\frac{5}{2}
.

2
Construction and properties of the trial state

We sketch the construction of the trial state
\Psi_{N}\in\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
satisfying (
5
) and (
6
).

It is well-understood that the correct leading order
4\pi\mathfrak{a}N^{1+\kappa}
in the ground-state energy of
\mathcal{H}_{N}
arises from a perfect Bose-Einstein condensate dressed by correlations between particle pairs on the short length-scale
N^{\kappa-1}
, which is proportional to the range of the potential. While a perfect condensate is simply a product function in the canonical picture, there are more ambiguities in the grand-canonical setting and we decide to model the condensate by a coherent state, i.e. we use the Weyl operator
W_{N_{0}}
satisfying

W_{N_{0}}^{*}a_{p}W_{N_{0}}=a_{p}+\delta_{p,0}\sqrt{N_{0}}

(9)

for all
p\in\Lambda^{*}=2\pi\mathbb{Z}^{3}
, which creates (in average)
N_{0}
particles with zero momentum. The short-range correlations are then implemented using the Bogoliubov transformation

\displaystyle e^{B-B^{*}}:=e^{\frac{1}{2}\sum_{p\in\Lambda^{*}\backslash\{0\}}\mu_{p}a_{p}a_{-p}-\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{p\in\Lambda^{*}\backslash\{0\}}\mu_{p}a_{p}a_{-p}\right)^{*}},

(10)

with sensibly chosen coefficients
\mu_{p}
, which actually creates excitations on all length-scales up to
N^{-\kappa/2}
. We will then consider a trial state of the form

\Psi_{N}=W_{N_{0}}e^{B-B^{*}}\xi,

(11)

where the state
\xi
encodes additional correlations on the healing length
N^{-\kappa/2}
that contribute to the Lee-Huang-Yang term. By a formal Duhamel expansion, it is evident that the excitation vector should be of the form

\displaystyle\xi_{\mathrm{formal}}:=e^{A^{*}-A}\Omega

(12)

with
\Omega
being the vacuum state and
A
given as the operator

A=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{p,q\in\Lambda^{*}}\eta_{p}\sigma_{q}\,a_{p+q}a_{-p}a_{-q},

where the correct coefficients
\eta_{p}
and
\sigma_{q}
are introduced in the following Subsection
2.1
. The formal definition in (
12
) suffers from multiple issues. First of all since
A
is cubic in creation and annihilation operators we expect that
A^{*}-A
is not essentially selfadjoint on the finite-particle subspace, hence there might be an ambiguity in the choice of a selfadjoint extension. Even more important, and related to the first point, it is not possible to (formally) close a Grönwall argument, which would be necessary to control the remainder in a Duhamel expansion.

Both issues with
\xi_{\mathrm{formal}}
are addressed in this manuscript, using a cutoff operator
\Theta
that allows us to control the local particle density. With
A
and
\Theta
at hand, we introduce the excitation vector
\xi
in the definition of our trial state
\Psi_{N}
in (
11
) as

\displaystyle\xi:=e^{\Theta A^{*}-A\Theta}\Omega.

Notably, we choose
\Theta
such that
A
is bounded on the range of
\Theta
and in particular we observe that the unique anti-selfadjoint extension of
\Theta A^{*}-A\Theta
is given by
(A\Theta)^{*}-(A\Theta)
.
The relevant contributions of
\xi
to the energy are then extracted via Duhamel expansion of order up to two.

2.1
Coefficients

The coefficients appearing in the Bogoliubov transformation (
10
) are chosen via the scattering problem (
2
) as follows: define first for
p\in\Lambda^{*}_{+}=2\pi\mathbb{Z}^{3}\backslash\{0\}

\eta_{\infty,p}=\frac{-N^{\kappa}\widehat{Vf}(p/N^{1-\kappa})}{2p^{2}}

(13)

and let
\eta_{\infty,0}=0
.
Note that the solution of (
2
) can be rewritten as
1-f=(-2\Delta)^{-1}Vf
emphasizing that these coefficients are the Fourier coefficients of a rescaled version of
1-f
. However,
1-f
only decays as
\frac{\mathfrak{a}}{\lvert x\rvert}
on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and hence is not a function on the torus. The torus implicitly imposes a long-distance cutoff and thereby (with the choice
\eta_{\infty,0}=0
) also regularizes the scattering solution in the sense that
\eta_{\infty}\in L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}
whereas this is not true for
1-f
. We show in Lemma
3
below that
\eta_{\infty}
satisfies a discrete version of the scattering equation with a subleading finite size error.
Next, define for
p\in\Lambda^{*}

\mu_{\infty,p}=-\frac{1}{4}\log(1-4\eta_{\infty,p}).

Observe

\tanh(2\mu_{\infty,p})=-\frac{\widehat{Vf}(p/N^{1-\kappa})}{p^{2}+\widehat{Vf}(p/N^{1-\kappa})},

and hence, these coefficients would be the right choice to diagonalize a quadratic Hamiltonian consisting of kinetic energy and an interaction
Vf
.
As the action of a Bogoliubov transformation contains the
\sinh
and
\cosh
of the kernels we define

\sigma_{\infty,p}=\sinh\mu_{\infty,p}\textrm{ and }\gamma_{\infty,p}=\cosh\mu_{\infty,p}.

We do not directly use these coefficients but rather impose a momentum cutoff as it turns out that momenta
\lvert p\rvert\ll N^{\kappa/2}
corresponding to the healing length do not matter to the precision we require. Pick
\epsilon>0
and define the length scale
\ell_{\sigma}=N^{-\kappa/2+\epsilon}
slightly bigger than the healing length. We will remove all momenta below
\ell_{\sigma}^{-1}
. Note that this implies that the correlations we implement live on short scales as the Fourier transforms of the various kernels should decay rapidly in position space at distances much larger than
\ell_{\sigma}
. This decay will be crucial when analyzing the cubic transformation. In order to make this statement rigorous, we use that the kernels in momentum space can be extended to
\mathbb{R}^{3}
in an obvious manner and use a smooth cutoff as we need to take derivatives in momentum space in order to prove the decay estimates.
Thus, first define for every
0\neq p\in\mathbb{R}^{3}

\begin{split}&\eta_{\infty}(p)=-\frac{N^{\kappa}\widehat{Vf}(p/N^{1-\kappa})}{2p^{2}},\quad\mu_{\infty}(p)=-\frac{1}{4}\log(1-4\eta_{\infty}(p)),\\
&\sigma_{\infty}(p)=\sinh\mu_{\infty}(p),\quad\gamma_{\infty}(p)=\cosh\mu_{\infty}(p).\end{split}

Now we introduce the low momentum cutoff: Pick
\chi_{l}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
with
0\leq\chi_{l}\leq 1
,
\chi_{l}(x)=0
if
x\leq 1
,
\chi_{l}(x)=1
if
x\geq 2
, and we set
\chi(p)=\chi_{l}(\ell_{\sigma}\lvert p\rvert)
and
\tilde{\chi}(p)=\chi_{l}(\ell_{\eta}\lvert p\rvert)
for
\ell_{\eta}=N^{-1+\kappa+\epsilon}\ll\ell_{\sigma}
.
We can now define the kernels with cutoff as

\displaystyle\mu(p)\coloneqq\mu_{\infty}(p)\chi(p),\quad\eta(p)=\eta_{\infty}(p)\tilde{\chi}(p),\quad\sigma(p)=\sinh\mu(p),\quad\gamma(p)=\cosh\mu(p).

(14)

For all lattice points
p\in\Lambda^{*}
we write

\mu_{p}=\mu(p)
,
\sigma_{p}=\sigma(p)
,
\gamma_{p}=\gamma(p)
,
\eta_{p}=\eta(p)
. In position space, we set

\check{\sigma}(x)=\sum_{p\in\Lambda^{*}}\sigma_{p}e^{ip\cdot x},\quad\widecheck{\gamma-1}(x)=\sum_{p\in\Lambda^{*}}(\gamma_{p}-1)e^{ip\cdot x},\quad\text{and}\quad\check{\eta}(x)=\sum_{p\in\Lambda^{*}}\eta_{p}e^{ip\cdot x}.

The discrete scattering equation and some further useful properties of
\eta_{\infty,p}
,
\sigma_{\infty,p}
and
\gamma_{\infty,p}
are contained in Lemma
3
.

Lemma 3
.

The coefficients
\eta_{\infty,p}
satisfy the discrete scattering equation

\begin{split}p^{2}\eta_{\infty,p}+\frac{N^{\kappa}}{2}\hat{V}(p/N^{1-\kappa})+\frac{N^{\kappa}}{2N}\sum_{q\in\Lambda_{+}^{*}}\hat{V}((p-q)/N^{1-\kappa})\eta_{\infty,q}=O(N^{2\kappa-1})\\
\frac{N^{\kappa}}{2N}\sum_{q\in\Lambda_{+}^{*}}\hat{V}(\frac{p-q}{N^{1-\kappa}})\eta_{\infty,q}=-\frac{N^{\kappa}}{2}\widehat{Vw}(\frac{p}{N^{1-\kappa}})+O(N^{2\kappa-1})\end{split}

(15)

uniformly in
p\in\Lambda^{*}_{+}
. Furthermore,

\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{+}^{*}}N^{\kappa}\hat{V}(p/N^{1-\kappa})\eta_{\infty,p}=(8\pi\mathfrak{a}-\hat{V}(0))N^{1+\kappa}+O(N^{2\kappa}).

(16)

Additionally,
\gamma_{\infty,p}
and
\sigma_{\infty,p}
satisfy the bounds

\lvert\gamma_{\infty,p}-1\rvert\leq\lvert\sigma_{\infty,p}\rvert\leq C\lvert\widehat{Vf}(p/N^{1-\kappa})\rvert\,\min\Big(\frac{N^{\kappa/4}}{\lvert p\rvert^{1/2}},\frac{N^{\kappa}}{\lvert p\rvert^{2}}\Big),

(17)

and therefore,

\lVert\sigma_{\infty}\rVert_{2}^{2},\lVert\gamma_{\infty}-1\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq CN^{3\kappa/2},\quad\lVert\sigma_{\infty}\rVert_{1},\lVert\gamma_{\infty}-1\rVert_{1}\leq CN.

Lastly, we have

\lVert\gamma_{\infty}\sigma_{\infty}-\eta_{\infty}\rVert_{1},\lVert\gamma_{\infty}-1\rVert_{1}\leq CN^{3\kappa/2}.

(18)

The proof can be found in
[
11
, Lemma 3]
, we also include a simplified version in the appendix for completeness.
In the following we show properties of the kernels containing the momentum cutoff and in particular make the decay estimates in position space rigorous.
The proof is a straightforward adaptation of
[
11
, Lemma 5]
, we give the details in the appendix.

Lemma 4
.

The kernels
\gamma,\sigma
and
\eta
satisfy

\begin{split}\lvert\gamma(p)-1\rvert\leq\lvert\sigma(p)\rvert&\leq C\lvert\widehat{Vf}(p/N^{1-\kappa})\rvert\min(N^{\kappa/4}\lvert p\rvert^{-1/2},N^{\kappa}\lvert p\rvert^{-2})\\
\lvert\eta(p)\rvert&\leq C\lvert\widehat{Vf}(p/N^{1-\kappa})\rvert N^{\kappa}\lvert p\rvert^{-2},\end{split}

and thus also

\lVert\sigma\rVert_{\infty},\lVert\gamma-1\rVert_{\infty}\leq CN^{\epsilon/2},\quad\lVert\sigma\rVert_{2}^{2},\lVert\gamma-1\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq CN^{3\kappa/2},\quad\lVert\sigma\rVert_{1},\lVert\gamma-1\rVert_{1}\leq CN

\lVert\eta\rVert_{\infty}\leq CN^{3\kappa-2+2\epsilon},\quad\lVert\eta\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq CN^{3\kappa-1+\epsilon},\quad\lVert\eta\rVert_{1}\leq CN.

(19)

Moreover

\lVert\eta-\eta_{\infty}\rVert_{1}\leq CN^{1-\epsilon},\lVert\sigma-\sigma_{\infty}\rVert_{1}\leq N^{3\kappa/2-\epsilon}.

(20)

Furthermore, for any
m\geq 1
, there exists
C_{m}>0
such that

\lvert\check{\sigma}(x)\rvert,\lvert\widecheck{\gamma-1}(x)\rvert\leq C_{m}N\left(\frac{\ell_{\sigma}}{\lvert x\rvert}\right)^{m}

(21)

where
\ell_{\sigma}=N^{-\kappa/2+\epsilon}
.
The previous bounds also imply

\lVert\check{\sigma}\rVert_{1},\lVert\widecheck{\gamma-1}\rVert_{1}\leq CN^{2\epsilon},\quad\lVert\check{\sigma}\rVert_{\infty},\lVert\widecheck{\gamma-1}\rVert_{\infty}\leq CN

(22)

Similarly, with
\ell_{\eta}=N^{-(1-\kappa)+\epsilon}
, we have for any
m\geq 1

\lvert\check{\eta}(x)\rvert\leq C_{m}N\left(\frac{\ell_{\eta}}{\lvert x\rvert}\right)^{m}.

(23)

In addition we have for
r>0

\displaystyle\int_{|x|\geq r}dx|\nabla\check{\sigma}(x)|^{2}\leq C_{m}N^{1+\kappa+2\epsilon}\left(\frac{\ell_{\sigma}}{r}\right)^{2m}.

(24)

2.2
The Local Particle Number Cutoff

We now construct the local particle number cutoff
\Theta
used in the trial state in (
11
). We impose conditions on the number of excitations on balls of size
\ell_{B}=N^{-\kappa/2+2\epsilon}
with
\epsilon>0
small enough as before, and in particular we will assume
0<\epsilon<\frac{2-3\kappa}{7}
to ensure that the expected number of excitations in a ball of radius
\ell_{B}
is of the order
O(1)
. As we will see, on the support of the cutoff the number of excitations in such balls is bounded by
N^{\delta}
for a suitably chosen small
\delta>0
.
Observe that
\ell_{\eta}\ll\ell_{\sigma}\ll\ell_{B}
, and hence, the transformations create correlations on scales much shorter than the ones on which restrict the particle number. This allows us to estimate error terms as follows: at short scales we use the local particle number cutoff, whereas at large scales, we use the rapid decay of the kernels.

In order to construct
\Theta
, let
\chi_{\ell_{B}}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
be such that
0\leq\chi_{\ell_{B}}\leq 1
,
\chi_{\ell_{B}}(x)=1
if
\lvert x\rvert\leq\ell_{B}
and
\chi_{\ell_{B}}(x)=0
if
\lvert x\rvert\geq 2\ell_{B}
. We then define

\mathcal{N}_{\chi_{\ell_{B}}}(w)\coloneqq\int dv\,\chi_{\ell_{B}}(w-v)a_{v}^{*}a_{v},

which essentially counts the number of particle close to a given point
w
, and for
n\in\mathbb{N}
we introduce

\mathcal{L}_{n}\coloneqq\int dw\,(\mathcal{N}_{\chi_{\ell_{B}}}(w)+1)^{n}.

The cut-off operator is then defined as

\Theta(\mathcal{L}_{n}/N^{\frac{1}{\delta}})\,,

where
\Theta\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
is a smooth function with
0\leq\Theta\leq 1
,
\Theta(x)=1
if
x\leq 1
and
\Theta(x)=0
if
x\geq 2
. Note that in the following, we choose
n=n(\delta)
large enough such that on the support of the cutoff operator
\Theta(\mathcal{L}_{n}/N^{\frac{1}{\delta}})
the value of the local particle number operators
\mathcal{N}_{\chi_{\ell}}(w)
is of the magnitude
N^{\delta}
. To be precise, we fix
n(\delta):=\lceil\frac{1}{\delta}+\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\rceil
, which is sufficiently large as is explained in the proof of Lemma
5
.

With the cutoff
\Theta(\mathcal{L}_{n}/N^{\frac{1}{\delta}})
at hand, we introduce the unitary cubic transformation

\exp\Big(\Theta(\mathcal{L}_{n}/N^{\frac{1}{\delta}})\int dxdydz\check{\eta}(x-y)\check{\sigma}(x-z)a_{x}^{*}a_{y}^{*}a_{z}^{*}-\text{h.c.}\ \Big)\eqqcolon\exp(\Theta A^{*}-\text{h.c.}\ ),

(25)

where, on the right side of (
25
), we have omitted the argument
\mathcal{L}_{n}/N^{\frac{1}{\delta}}
in the operator

if

K^{(4)}(y,z)&=N_{0}^{1/2}\int dw\,[V_{N}*\check{\sigma}](w)\check{\sigma}(w+y)(\widecheck{\gamma-1})(w+z)\end{split}

where

denotes
N^{2-2\kappa}V(N^{1-\kappa}x)
.
The bounds from Lemma
4
imply
\|K^{(j)}\|_{2}\leq CN^{(5\kappa-1)/2}
, for all
j=1,\dots,4
. Moreover, for
|x|\geq\ell_{\sigma}
and for any
m\geq 1

\begin{split}|V_{N}\ast\check{\sigma}(x)|&\leq\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}|y|\leq cN^{\kappa-1}\end{subarray}}|V_{N}(y)||\check{\sigma}(x-y)|dy\leq C_{m}N^{\kappa}\left(\frac{\ell_{\sigma}}{\lvert x\rvert}\right)^{m}.\end{split}

Here

(V)\subset B_{c}(0)
, and we used (

|x-y|\geq|x|/2

\check{\sigma}
is replaced by
\check{\gamma}
or
(\widecheck{\gamma-1})
.
It follows that, for

, and for any

\begin{split}|K^{(4)}(x,y)|&\leq N^{1/2}|\int_{|w|\geq|x|/2}dw\,[V_{N}*\check{\sigma}](w)\check{\sigma}(w+x)(\widecheck{\gamma-1})(w+y)|\\
&+N^{1/2}|\int_{|w|\leq|x|/2}dw\,[V_{N}*\check{\sigma}](w)\check{\sigma}(w+x)(\widecheck{\gamma-1})(w+y)|\\
&\leq C_{m}N^{3/2}\left(\frac{\ell_{\sigma}}{\lvert x\rvert}\right)^{m}\int dw\Big(N^{\kappa-1}|\check{\sigma}(w+x)|+|[V_{N}*\check{\sigma}]|(w)\Big)|(\widecheck{\gamma-1})(w+y)|\end{split}

A similar estimate holds when
|y|\geq\ell_{B}
and for
|K^{(2)}(x,y)|
.
Hence, for
j=1,..,4
and any
m\geq 1

\int_{\{|x|\geq\ell_{B}\}\cup\{|y|\geq\ell_{B}\}}dxdy|K^{(j)}(x,y)|^{2}\leq C_{m}N^{-m}.

(39)

To estimate the expectation of

, we first write

\begin{split}\langle\xi,\tilde{\mathcal{C}}^{(j)}_{N}\xi\rangle=\langle\Theta\xi,\tilde{\mathcal{C}}^{(j)}_{N}\Theta\xi\rangle+\langle\xi,\tilde{\mathcal{C}}^{(j)}_{N}(1-\Theta)\xi\rangle+\langle(1-\Theta)\xi,\tilde{\mathcal{C}}^{(j)}_{N}\Theta\xi\rangle.\end{split}

\begin{split}|\langle(1-\Theta)\xi,\tilde{\mathcal{C}}^{(j)}_{N}\Theta\xi\rangle|\leq\|(1-\Theta)\xi\|\|K^{(j)}\|_{2}\|(\mathcal{N}+1)^{3/2}\xi\|\leq CN^{-1/(4\delta)}\end{split}

if

For the main term on the r.h.s of (

\begin{split}&|\langle\Theta\xi,\tilde{\mathcal{C}}^{(j)}_{N}\Theta\xi\rangle|\\
&\leq C\Big(\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x-y|,\\
|x-z|\leq\ell_{B}\end{subarray}}dxdydz\|a_{x}a_{y}a_{z}\Theta\xi\|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\|K^{(j)}\|_{2}\|\xi\|+C_{m}N^{-m}\langle\xi,(\mathcal{N}+1)^{3/2}\xi\rangle,\end{split}

having
in particular used (

and

. Using the bound (
28
) and Lemma

for the first term, and the bound (

∎

Lemma 11

Proof.

We will bound the expectation of

\gamma-1

\sigma

\begin{split}\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(0)}_{N}=\;&\frac{1}{2}\int dxdy\,V_{N}(x-y)\,a^{*}(\check{\theta}_{x})a^{*}(\check{\theta}_{y})a(\check{\theta}_{x})a(\check{\theta}_{y})\\

&+\int dxdy\,V_{N}(x-y)\,a^{*}_{x}a^{*}(\check{\theta}_{y})a_{x}a(\check{\theta}_{y})\\
&+\int dxdy\,V_{N}(x-y)\,a^{*}_{x}a^{*}(\check{\theta}_{y})a(\check{\theta}_{x})a_{y}\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\int dxdy\,V_{N}(x-y)a^{*}_{x}a^{*}_{y}a(\check{\theta}_{x})a(\check{\theta}_{y})+\text{h.c.}\\
&+\int dxdy\,V_{N}(x-y)\,a^{*}_{x}a^{*}_{y}a_{x}a(\check{\theta}_{y})+\text{h.c.}\end{split}

(41)

where

\check{\theta}(x-s)

\Theta

. For the last two terms, this is done differently and, as an example, we show it for the last one. We write

\begin{split}\int&dxdy\,V_{N}(x-y)\langle\xi,a_{x}^{*}a_{y}^{*}a_{x}a(\check{\theta}_{y})\xi\rangle\\
=\;&\int dxdy\,V_{N}(x-y)\langle\Theta\xi,a_{x}^{*}a_{y}^{*}a_{x}a(\check{\theta}_{y})\Theta\xi\rangle+\int dxdy\,V_{N}(x-y)\langle\xi,a_{x}^{*}a_{y}^{*}a_{x}a(\check{\theta}_{y})(1-\Theta)\xi\rangle\\

(42)

The second (and similarly the third) term on the r.h.s. can be bounded by

\begin{split}\Big|\int&dxdy\,V_{N}(x-y)\langle\xi,a_{x}^{*}a_{y}^{*}a_{x}a(\check{\theta}_{y})(1-\Theta)\xi\rangle\Big|\\

With Lemma
6
, we obtain, for
\delta
small enough,

\Big|\int dxdy\,V_{N}(x-y)\langle\xi,a_{x}^{*}a_{y}^{*}a_{x}a(\check{\theta}_{y})(1-\Theta)\xi\rangle\Big|\leq CN^{-1/(4\delta)}

Therefore, all that remains is to evaluate the first term on the r.h.s. of (

It can be bounded, for any

\begin{split}&\Big|\int dxdy\,V_{N}(x-y)\langle\Theta\xi,a(\check{\theta}_{x})^{*}a(\check{\theta}_{y})^{*}a(\check{\theta}_{x})a(\check{\theta}_{y})\Theta\xi\rangle\Big|\\

having used (

\Big|\int dxdy\,V_{N}(x-y)\langle\Theta\xi,a(\check{\theta}_{x})^{*}a(\check{\theta}_{y})^{*}a(\check{\theta}_{x})a(\check{\theta}_{y})\Theta\xi\rangle\Big|\leq CN^{5\kappa/2-3/2(2-3\kappa)+7\epsilon+3\delta}.

\begin{split}&\Big|\int dxdy\,V_{N}(x-y)\langle\Theta\xi,a_{x}^{*}a_{y}^{*}a(\check{\theta}_{x})a(\check{\theta}_{y})\Theta\xi\rangle\Big|\\

A similar bound holds for the last term of (

3.3

In this subsection, we prove bounds on the expectations
\langle\xi,(\mathcal{C}_{N}^{*}+\mathcal{C}_{N})\xi\rangle

\Psi_{N}=W_{N_{0}}e^{B^{*}-B}\xi
by inserting them in (
37

\check{A}^{\mathrm{sym}}
of the kernel
\check{A}(u,v):=N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\check{\eta}(u)\check{\sigma}(v)
as

\displaystyle\check{A}^{\mathrm{sym}}(u,v):=\!\check{A}(u,v)\!+\!\check{A}(v,u)\!+\!\check{A}(-u,v\!-\!u)\!+\!\check{A}(v\!-\!u,-u)+\check{A}(-v,u\!-\!v)\!+\!\check{A}(u\!-\!v,-v),

(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\Lambda^{3}

\displaystyle\check{A}^{\mathrm{sym}}(x_{1}-x_{2},x_{1}-x_{3})=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{3}}\check{A}(x_{\sigma(1)}-x_{\sigma(2)},x_{\sigma(1)}-x_{\sigma(3)}).

In the following we denote
\check{A}^{\text{sym}}_{\ell_{B}}(u,v):=\mathbbm{1}(|u|,|v|\leq\ell_{B})\check{A}^{\text{sym}}(u,v)
and
\check{A}^{\text{sym}}_{\infty}(u,v):=\check{A}^{\text{sym}}(u,v)-\check{A}^{\text{sym}}_{\ell_{B}}(u,v)

4
, for any
m\geq 1
, we can find a constant

\|\check{A}_{\infty}\|_{2}\leq C_{m}N^{-m}
.

Lemma 12
.

Recall the definition of the cubic operator from (
37
)

\mathcal{C}_{N}=N_{0}^{1/2}\int dxdydzN^{2-2\kappa}V(N^{1-\kappa}(x-y))\check{\sigma}(x-z)a^{*}_{x}a^{*}_{y}a^{*}_{z}.

For
\delta>0
small enough, we have

\begin{split}\langle e^{t\Theta A^{*}-\text{h.c.}\ }\Omega,&(\mathcal{C}_{N}+\mathcal{C}_{N}^{*})e^{t\Theta A^{*}-\text{h.c.}\ }\Omega\rangle\\
&=2tN_{0}^{1/2}\int dxdyN^{2-2\kappa}V(N^{1-\kappa}x)\check{\sigma}(y)\check{A}^{\mathrm{sym}}(x,y)+\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{C}}\end{split}

where

\pm\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{C}}\leq CN^{-(2-3\kappa)+15\epsilon/2+2\delta}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}ds\;\langle e^{s\Theta A^{*}-\text{h.c.}\ }\Omega,\mathcal{V}_{N}e^{s\Theta A^{*}-\text{h.c.}\ }\Omega\rangle+N^{5\kappa/2}\Big)

for all

Proof.

Writing
\xi_{t}=e^{t\Theta A^{*}-\text{h.c.}\ }\Omega
, we expand with Duhamel

\begin{split}\langle&\xi_{t},(\mathcal{C}_{N}+\mathcal{C}_{N}^{*})\xi_{t}\rangle\\
&=\int_{0}^{t}ds\;\langle\Theta\xi_{s},[\mathcal{C}_{N},A^{*}]\Theta\xi_{s}\rangle+\langle\Theta\xi_{s},[\mathcal{C}_{N},A^{*}](1-\Theta)\xi_{s}\rangle\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt+\langle\xi_{s},\big(\mathcal{C}_{N}(\Theta-1)A^{*}-(\Theta-1)\mathcal{C}_{N}A^{*}-A\mathcal{C}_{N}(\Theta-1)+A(\Theta-1)\mathcal{C}_{N}\big)\xi_{s}\rangle+\text{h.c.}\ \end{split}

The terms containing a
(\Theta-1)

, if

is chosen small enough, by using Lemma
6
, in particular (
30
), (
31
), and (

[\mathcal{C}_{N},A^{*}]=T_{0}+T_{2}+T_{4}

\begin{split}T_{0}&=2N_{0}^{1/2}\int dxdyV_{N}(x)\check{\sigma}(y)\check{A}^{\mathrm{sym}}(x,y)\\
T_{2}&=N_{0}^{1/2}\int dxdydzdz^{\prime}\check{A}^{\mathrm{sym}}(x-y,x-z^{\prime})\times\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt\Big[V_{N}(x-y)\check{\sigma}(x-z)+V_{N}(x-z)\big(\check{\sigma}(x-y)+\check{\sigma}(z-y)\big)\Big]a^{*}_{z^{\prime}}a_{z}\\
T_{4}&=\frac{N_{0}^{1/2}}{2}\int dxdydzdx^{\prime}dy^{\prime}\check{A}^{\mathrm{sym}}(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime},x^{\prime}-z)\times\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt\Big[V_{N}(x-y)\check{\sigma}(x-z)+V_{N}(x-z)\big(\check{\sigma}(x-y)+\check{\sigma}(z-y)\big)\Big]a^{*}_{x^{\prime}}a^{*}_{y^{\prime}}a_{x}a_{y}.\end{split}

We find,

\begin{split}&|\langle\Theta\xi_{s},T_{4}\Theta\xi_{s}\rangle|\\
&\leq CN^{1/2}\|V_{N}\|_{1}^{1/2}\|\sigma\|_{2}\Big(\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|,\\
|x^{\prime}-z|\leq\ell_{B}\end{subarray}}dx^{\prime}dy^{\prime}dz\|a_{x^{\prime}}a_{y^{\prime}}\Theta\xi_{s}\|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt\times\|\check{A}^{\mathrm{sym}}\|_{2}\Big(\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x-y|,\\

Here, we used the bound (
21

\|\check{A}_{\infty}\|_{2}\leq C_{m}N^{-m}
,

, for some constant

and (
31
) to show that the contribution associated to
1-\mathbbm{1}(|x-y|,|x-z|,|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|,|x^{\prime}-z|\leq\ell_{B})

4
, Lemma

\begin{split}|\langle\Theta\xi_{s},T_{2}\Theta\xi_{s}\rangle|&\leq CN^{1/2}\ell_{B}^{3}\|V_{N}\|_{2}\|\sigma\|_{2}\|\check{A}^{\mathrm{sym}}\|_{2}\langle\xi_{s},\mathcal{N}\xi_{s}\rangle\\

Lemma 13
.

Recall

\begin{split}[\mathcal{V}_{N},A^{*}]+\text{h.c.}\ =&\;\frac{1}{2}\int dxdydz\,V_{N}(x-y)\check{A}^{\mathrm{sym}}(x-y,x-z)a_{x}^{*}a_{y}^{*}a_{z}^{*}+\text{h.c.}\ \\

Lastly, we turn to the kinetic energy operator.

Lemma 14

Let

\begin{split}c^{m}&\lvert x_{j}\rvert^{m}\lvert\check{\sigma}(x)\rvert\leq\lvert(e^{2\pi ix_{j}}-1)^{m}\check{\sigma}(x)\rvert=\lvert(e^{2\pi ix_{j}}-1)^{m}\sum_{p\in\Lambda^{*}}\sigma(p)e^{ip\cdot x}\rvert\\
