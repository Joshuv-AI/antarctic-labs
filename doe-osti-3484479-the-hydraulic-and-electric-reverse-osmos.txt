Introduction

 Introduction

Hydraulic and Electric Reverse Osmosis Wave Energy Converter
• The HERO WEC is a
  desalination device to create
  fresh water from near-shore
  ocean waves.
• It is designed for quick
  deployment in disaster
  scenarios when water supply
  is compromised.

Origin of HERO WEC

Design

How does it work?
• The Point-absorber WEC
 ◦ Heave motion pulls on mooring
 line, creating rotational motion
 through a gearbox

 ◦ Rotational motion spins either a
 generator or a hydraulic pump

 ◦ Winch winds line back with
 pressure from air spring.

Hydraulic System
• The rotary, six-diaphragm pump was
 originally intended for agricultural
 applications.

• Two pressure relief valves keep the
 system from over-pressurization.
• The pump is connected to a hose
 running across the seabed to provide
 the necessary pressure to the reverse
 osmosis system on shore. Photo by Andrew Simms, NLR

                                                                         National Laboratory of the Rockies   |   10

  Reverse-Osmosis System
  • Two off-the-shelf Spectra reverse
 osmosis units—commonly used on
 boats—desalinate seawater into fresh
 drinking water.
  • Clark pumps used for energy recovery
 and intensification.
  • Hydraulic accumulators smooth
 pressure spikes.

  Challenges
  • Inconsistent and pulse-based power delivery.
  • Renewable power delivery changes over
 hours, days, and seasons. A wave changes
 over seconds.
  • High peak-to-average power ratio.
  • Peak-to-average power ratios for waves can
 exceed 10x those of wind.

                                                     National Laboratory of the Rockies   |   14

Cut-in Voltage
• Buck converters can only
 step down DC voltage,
 limiting range.
• Battery is only able to
 charge at input voltages
 greater than the battery
 charge voltage.

                               National Laboratory of the Rockies   |   15

Overshoot
• Under moderately aggressive
 conditions, the current delivered
 to the battery exceeded the
 command by >40 A.
• Shorter wave periods and higher
 amplitudes exaggerate overshoot.
• Caused by improperly tuned
 controller applied to wave

                                        National Laboratory of the Rockies   |   16

  New Custom Controller
  A custom charge controller was built in-house to improve:
  • Cut-in voltage by replacing the buck topology with buck/boost
  • System response by building firmware to quickly read voltage/current
 and tuned for quick wave inputs
  • Future research potential by including onboard measurements and
 utilizing an FPGA for system control with plenty of I/O expansion.

Software
• Software processes voltage and current
 measurements, monitors for faults, and
 commands the MOSFET gate.
• Simple linear damping control is
 implemented:
 ◦ Output battery current is linearly
 proportional to input DC voltage
 (bulk charging stage).
 ◦ Control is deliberately simple.

In-Lab Results

Microgrid Needs
• Remote communities rely on traditional generation sources at higher cost
 and challenging logistics.
• Diversity in generation increases resiliency and reliability.
• Storable energy is critical for maintaining year-round energy demand for
 far-north communities.

Wave Energy Meets Microgrid Needs
• Wave/tidal energy is consistent and requires no consumables.
• Coastal communities can add an abundant energy source to their portfolio
 of generation.
• Hydrogen storage with wave energy is an emerging field of research to provide
 long-term energy storage.

Future of HERO WEC

• Dump load:

 Photo by Taylor Mankle, NLR
