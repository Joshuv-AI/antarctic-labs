Ceralasertib Plus Durvalumab in Chinese Patients with Advanced Solid Tumors: A Nonrandomized Clinical Trial
Authors: Yuping Sun, Jia Zhong, Huiwen Sun, Graeme Parr, Edit Lukacs, Daniel Slade, Yizhao Zhou, Xiaoyu Ren, David Kirk, Aleksandra Kmieciak, Tingting Yao, Jie Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionThis phase I, multicenter, nonrandomized open-label study is the first study to investigate the safety, pharmacokinetics, and antitumor activity of ceralasertib, an oral ATR kinase inhibitor, in combination with durvalumab in Chinese patients with advanced solid tumors who are refractory or resistant to prior anti-PD-(L)1 immunotherapy or for whom no standard of care exists.MethodsPatients were enrolled into one of two cohorts. Cohort 1 received ceralasertib 240 mg twice daily on days 1‒7 of cycle 0 then on days 22‒28 from cycle 1 onwards (28-day cycle). Cohort 2 received ceralasertib 160 mg twice daily on days 1‒14 of cycle 0 then on days 15‒28 from cycle 1 onwards (28-day cycle). All patients received durvalumab 1500 mg on day 1 (28-day cycle). Patients were treated until disease progression, discontinuation due to adverse events, or patient or investigator’s decision to withdraw. The primary endpoint was safety and tolerability of ceralasertib combined with durvalumab. Secondary endpoints included ceralasertib pharmacokinetics and antitumor activity.ResultsOverall, 14 patients were enrolled (cohort 1, n = 8; cohort 2, n = 6). All patients were Chinese and the majority (85.7%) had received > 4 prior lines of therapy. Most patients (13/14; 92.9%) had an adverse event, which were grade ≥ 3 in 7 (50.0%) patients. No dose-limiting toxicities occurred (12 evaluable patients; six in each cohort). Pharmacokinetics for ceralasertib were comparable to that reported in other studies for monotherapy or in combination with durvalumab. Among 13 response-evaluable patients, 3/13 had a confirmed partial response (one with lung adenocarcinoma, one with squamous cell carcinoma of the cervix uteri, and one with lung squamous cell carcinoma); the objective response rate was 23.1% [80% CI 8.8‒44.4%]).ConclusionsCeralasertib plus durvalumab had antitumor activity in Chinese patients with advanced solid tumors; safety and pharmacokinetics were consistent with studies in Western patient populations.Trial RegistrationClinicalTrials.gov identifier, NCT05514132.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1007/s40487-026-00458-3.

Introduction

In 2022, there were nearly 5 million new cases of cancer in China, which resulted in more than 2.5 million deaths [1]. In recent years, the use of immune checkpoint inhibitors targeting the programmed cell death 1 (PD-1)/programmed cell death ligand 1 (PD-L1) axis have transformed the treatment paradigm and these agents are now the standard-of-care for several cancers and are approved in the USA, Europe, and China [2]. However, there is an unmet medical need for new treatments for patients who have become refractory or resistant to anti-PD-(L)1 therapy [3].

Ceralasertib is an oral, selective, potent inhibitor of ATR, a key serine/threonine kinase involved in the DNA damage response [4]. In the phase I, first-in-human, dose-escalation and expansion PATRIOT study (NCT02223923), ceralasertib monotherapy demonstrated antitumor activity in patients with advanced solid tumors who had no standard treatment options, with durable responses seen in patients with DNA damage repair mutations [5]. The safety profile was manageable with most adverse events (AEs) being predominantly hematological [5].

Preclinical evidence suggests that ATR inhibition may modulate PD-1 and PD-L1 expression and that the failure to repair S-phase DNA damage, such as DNA fragmentation, cytosolic DNA/RNA, chromosome mis-segregation, and micronuclei, can activate an innate immune response through antiviral nucleic acid sensing pathways [6, 7]. Here, activation of either of the cGAS-STING-dependent cytosolic DNA or RIG-I-MAVS-dependent RNA sensing pathways can induce type 1 interferon signaling and sensitize cancer cells to T-cell-mediated immune destruction [8–12]. This suggests ceralasertib may reshape the tumor immune microenvironment to an immune-effective state, thus providing a rationale for combining with anti-PD-(L)1 immune checkpoint inhibitors, even in the immunotherapy resistant or relapsed settings.

Durvalumab, an anti-PD-L1 antibody, was first approved in China on the basis of the results from the phase III PACIFIC study (NCT02125461) [13–15], which showed that durvalumab monotherapy significantly improved progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) compared with placebo in patients with stage III unresectable non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) who did not have disease progression after concurrent chemoradiotherapy [14, 15].

In several phase II studies, the combination of intermittent weekly dosing of ceralasertib plus durvalumab every 4 weeks has shown encouraging antitumor activity and a manageable safety profile in patients with NSCLC [16], melanoma [17], or gastric cancer [18], including patients who had progressed on or after prior treatment with anti-PD-(L)1 therapy. The safety profile was consistent with either agent given as monotherapy, and no new safety signals were observed. Additionally, in the phase II HUDSON study, patients with NSCLC who received ceralasertib plus durvalumab had a higher objective response rate (ORR) and longer PFS and OS compared with those who received durvalumab in combination with other agents [16].

Here, we report the safety and tolerability, pharmacokinetics (PK), and antitumor activity of ceralasertib in combination with durvalumab in Chinese patients with advanced solid malignancies who are refractory or resistant to prior anti-PD-(L)1 therapy or for which no appropriate standard-of-care therapy exists.

Methods

Study Design

This phase I, multicenter, open-label study (NCT05514132) enrolled patients from two centers in China. Patients were enrolled and treated in one of two cohorts; in each cohort, a monotherapy lead-in period (cycle 0 of 7 or 14 days), prior to dosing with durvalumab, was included to investigate the PK profile and safety/tolerability of ceralasertib in Chinese patients. In cohort 1 (7-day regimen), patients received ceralasertib 240 mg orally twice daily (BID) on days 1‒7 of cycle 0; from cycle 1 onwards, patients received ceralasertib on days 22‒28 of a 28-day cycle. In cohort 2 (14-day regimen), ceralasertib 160 mg was administered orally BID on days 1‒14 of cycle 0; from cycle 1 onwards, patients received ceralasertib on days 15‒28 of a 28-day cycle. A single dose of durvalumab 1500 mg was given intravenously on day 1 of each 28-day cycle to each cohort.

A minimum of three and a maximum of six dose-limiting toxicity (DLT) evaluable patients were enrolled in each cohort, using a “rolling six design” [19]. If no more than one DLT was observed in six DLT-evaluable patients in cohort 1, then patients would be enrolled in cohort 2.

Patients

Key inclusion criteria included adults (≥ 18 years of age) with histologically or cytologically confirmed advanced solid tumors that are refractory or resistant following a prior line of anti-PD-(L)1 therapy or for whom no standard of care exists; Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status of 0–1; treatment-free interval of ≥ 3 weeks from any prior therapy before the first dose of study treatment; no toxicity that led to permanent discontinuation of prior anti-PD-(L)1 immunotherapy.

The protocol and patient informed consent form were approved by the institutional review board or independent ethics committee at each study site. The study was performed in accordance with the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki, the International Council for Harmonization Good Clinical Practice guidelines, and applicable regulatory authorities. All patients provided written informed consent.

Study Endpoints

The primary endpoint was to assess the safety and tolerability (incidence of DLTs and AEs) of ceralasertib in combination with durvalumab. DLTs were defined as any toxicity not attributable to the disease or disease-related processes under investigation, that met the definition of DLT and occurred during cycle 0 or cycle 1 (i.e., from first dose day 1 cycle 0 until day 28 of cycle 1) for ceralasertib monotherapy and for combination therapy with durvalumab. A list of DLTs is given in the electronic supplementary material.

Secondary endpoints included characterizing the PK of ceralasertib and antitumor activity of ceralasertib in combination with durvalumab. PK was assessed by determining plasma ceralasertib concentrations and derived PK parameters such as area under the concentration–time curve from time 0 to the last quantifiable concentration (AUClast), maximum observed concentration (Cmax), time to reach maximum observed plasma drug concentration (tmax), terminal elimination half-life (t1/2λz), and temporal change parameter. Antitumor activity was assessed by evaluating ORR, duration of response (DoR), percentage change in tumor size, and PFS by investigator assessment (defined as the start of treatment [first dose of ceralasertib] until the date of progressive disease or death due to any cause according to Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors v1.1).

Study Assessments and Procedures

All patients received treatment until disease progression, discontinuation due to AEs, or patient or investigator’s decision to withdraw. The incidence rates of AEs, serious AEs (SAEs), and AEs of special interest (defined in the electronic supplementary methods) were collected throughout the treatment period and until the follow-up period was completed.

For PK, venous blood samples were collected during cycle 0 within 10 min prior to dosing of ceralasertib at day 1 and at days 7 or 14, depending on the cohort. After dosing with ceralasertib, blood samples were collected at 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 7, and 11 h; additional blood samples were also collected at 24, 48, and 72 h post-dose on days 7 or 14, depending on the cohort.

Antitumor activity was evaluated using computed tomography and magnetic resonance imaging scans. Tumors were assessed at baseline and every 8 weeks (± 1 week) after the start of the combination treatment (cycle 1, day 1) up to 18 months, then every 12 weeks (± 1 week) until disease progression or end of treatment. Responses were confirmed at least 4 weeks later.

Statistical Analyses

The safety analysis set included all patients who received at least one dose of any study intervention. The DLT-evaluable analysis set included patients enrolled in the dose study who completed the DLT evaluation period with sufficient dosing (i.e., 75% of the specified dose of ceralasertib) or who experienced any DLT. The PK analysis set included all dosed patients with reportable ceralasertib plasma concentrations and no AEs or protocol deviations that may have impacted PK. The efficacy analysis set included all dosed patients who had measurable disease at baseline.

The efficacy endpoints for ORR, DoR, and percentage change in tumor size were summarized using the efficacy analysis set, while PFS was summarized using the safety analysis set. Descriptive statistics were used for all variables. ORR was assessed using the Clopper–Pearson methods to calculate point estimates and bilateral 80% confidence interval (CI). DoR and PFS were assessed using the Kaplan‒Meier method. For median DoR, 95% CIs were calculated using the Kaplan‒Meier method. For median PFS, 95% CIs were calculated using the Brookmeyer and Crowley method. For PFS, the percentages of patients who were progression free at 3 and 6 months were calculated; 95% CIs were calculated using the Greenwood method.

Results

Baseline Characteristics and Demographics

A total of 21 patients were screened and 14 were enrolled; eight (including two patients who were not evaluable for DLTs) and six patients were assigned to cohort 1 (7-day regimen) and cohort 2 (14-day regimen), respectively (Fig. 1). Patient Disposition

At data cutoff (20 October 2023), seven patients (50.0%) had completed the study (Fig. 1). Of the remaining patients, two had died (14.3%, one patient in each cohort) and five patients were ongoing in the study (35.7%). At data cutoff, two patients were still receiving treatment in cohort 1 and six patients had discontinued (AEs, n = 2; progressive disease, n = 3; investigator decision, n = 1). In cohort 2, one patient was still receiving both treatments, and five patients had discontinued (progressive disease, n = 4; investigator decision, n = 1).

Safety

The median duration of ceralasertib treatment was 5.2 months (range 0.2‒12.2 months) for cohort 1 and 2.3 months (range 1.4‒6.9 months) for cohort 2; median duration of treatment with durvalumab was 7.5 months (range 0.9‒12.1 months) for cohort 1 and 1.9 months (range 0.9–6.9 months) for cohort 2. The median relative dose intensity of ceralasertib and durvalumab is given in Table S1 in the electronic supplementary material.

In total, most patients (13/14; 92.9%) had at least one AE; grade ≥ 3 AEs occurred in 7/14 patients (50.0%) (Table 2). Twelve patients (six in each cohort) were evaluable for DLTs; no DLTs were observed and no AEs leading to death were reported. One patient in cohort 1 discontinued both study treatments due to an AE of ascites, which was not considered to be related to either study drug. Dose interruptions due to AEs occurred in eight patients (cohort 1, n = 5; cohort 2, n = 3); ceralasertib treatment was interrupted as a result of AEs in five patients and durvalumab treatment was interrupted due to AEs in three patients. The most common AEs across both cohorts were anemia (11/14, 78.6%), platelet count decreased (8/14, 57.1%), white blood cell count decreased (7/14, 50.0%), and hyperglycemia (6/14, 42.9%) (Table 3). Individual patient plots for hematological laboratory analyses are given in Figs. S1 and S2 in the electronic supplementary material. The most common grade ≥ 3 AEs occurring in ≥ 10% of patients across both cohorts were anemia (5/14, 35.7%), lymphocyte count decreased (2/14, 14.3%), thrombocytopenia (2/14, 14.3%), and white blood cell count decreased (2/14, 14.3%) (Table 3). In total, 6/14 patients experienced SAEs (42.9%, n = 3 in each cohort); a summary of treatment-related SAEs is given in Table S2 in the electronic supplementary material. During the ceralasertib monotherapy lead-in (i.e., cycle 0), 11/14 patients (78.6%) experienced an AE; grade ≥ 3 AEs occurred in 1/14 patients (7.1%). The most common AEs were nausea (4/14, 28.6%) and vomiting (4/14, 28.6%) (Table S3 in the electronic supplementary material).

Pharmacokinetics

Following single-dose administration of ceralasertib, concentrations were detectable for 11 h, i.e., the last sampling timepoint within the dose interval following the first dose (Figs. S3a, b in the electronic supplementary material). Ceralasertib was detectable in all patients following multiple-dose administration, including up to 72 h post-dose (Figs. S3c, d in the electronic supplementary material). Additional PK results are given in the Supplementary Results.

Antitumor Activity

At data cutoff of 20 October 2023, 13 patients were evaluable for antitumor activity, and three patients had a confirmed partial response (PR); ORR was 23.1% (3/13, 80% CI 8.8‒44.4%). Among the seven response-evaluable patients in cohort 1 (one patient was excluded due to the absence of measurable disease at baseline), two patients had insufficient exposure to the investigational drugs; one patient had no durvalumab exposure during the study and one patient had insufficient ceralasertib exposure (i.e., less than 75%) in cycle 0. The two patients with PRs were in cohort 1 (2/7 patients. ORR, 28.6%; 80% CI 7.9‒59.6%); one patient had lung adenocarcinoma and the other patient had squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) of the cervix uteri. The third patient with PR had lung squamous cell carcinoma and was in cohort 2 (1/6 patients. ORR 16.7%; 80% CI 1.7‒51.0%). The responder with cervical cancer had acquired resistance and the other two responders had primary resistance to prior PD-(L)1 immunotherapies. Four patients (30.8%) had stable disease for ≥ 6 months.

In cohort 1, DoR was 5.7 months in the patient with SCC of the cervix uteri, and 9.4 months in the patient with lung adenocarcinoma. In cohort 2, DoR was 2.7 months for the patient with lung squamous carcinoma. Median PFS in cohort 1 was 5.6 months (95% CI 2.0‒not calculable [NC]); in cohort 2, median PFS was 2.7 months (95% CI 0.8‒NC).

Discussion

There are limited studies reporting clinical data for ceralasertib and this is the first study to investigate the safety, PK, and antitumor activity of ceralasertib in combination with durvalumab in Chinese patients with advanced solid tumors who are refractory or resistant to prior anti-PD-(L)1 immunotherapy or for whom no standard of care exists.

The most commonly observed toxicities with ceralasertib plus durvalumab treatment were hematologic, with the top three AEs being anemia, platelet count decreased, and white blood cell count decreased, which is consistent with those reported for ceralasertib monotherapy in the phase I PATRIOT study [5]. Hematologic toxicities were not unexpected with the use of ceralasertib, given its mechanism of action as an ATR inhibitor, but were well tolerated and effectively managed in Chinese patients, irrespective of the dosing regimen (240 mg BID for 7 days or 160 mg BID 14 days). The safety profile in Chinese patients was also comparable to that reported for ceralasertib plus durvalumab in previous studies, conducted primarily in Europe and the USA, suggesting there is no overlap in the toxicity profile between ceralasertib and durvalumab and no differences in tolerability between European and Asian patients [16, 20]. No deaths due to AEs were reported and no new safety signals were identified.

The study investigated two dosing schedules of ceralasertib, 240 mg BID in a 7-day regimen and 160 mg BID in a 14-day regimen, in combination with durvalumab. The intermittent dosing schedules were selected on the basis of preclinical findings, which showed that maximal efficacy with ceralasertib plus durvalumab was achieved with an on–off treatment schedule [21]. Ceralasertib suppresses proliferating CD8+ T cells on treatment, but this effect is rapidly reversed off treatment; short, intermittent ceralasertib dosing therefore facilitates antitumor immunity by transiently depleting exhausted T cells during treatment and enabling repopulation of the tumor immune microenvironment with non-activated T cells during off-treatment, resulting in upregulation of type I interferon (IFN) and increased CD8 T cell-dependent and IFN-dependent antitumor activity, especially in combination with anti-PD-L1 therapy [21]. In the current study, ceralasertib was rapidly absorbed following single- and multiple-dose administration; with median time to reach maximum drug concentration in plasma in Chinese patients consistent with previously reported data [5, 20, 22]. Terminal elimination half-life was also similar to data reported for ceralasertib monotherapy or in combination with durvalumab in studies conducted at centers in Europe, the USA [5, 20], and South Korea [22]. Overall, the PK profile in Chinese patients was consistent with previous studies and suggests there are no meaningful differences in PK between patients of European and Asian ancestry [5, 20].

Patients in this study were heavily pretreated with the majority (85.7%) receiving > 4 prior lines of therapy. However, preliminary antitumor activity was observed with the combination of ceralasertib and durvalumab: three patients with PRs (two patients with NSCLC, one with uterine cervix cancer) and four patients with stable disease for ≥ 6 months. Of the three patients who responded, all had primary or acquired resistance to prior anti-PD-(L)1 immunotherapy. As the study protocol did not mandate assessment of PD-L1 expression, we were not able to evaluate responses in patients with high versus low PD-L1 expression. Overall, while noting that the small sample size and nonrandomized study design precluded formal assessment or adjustment for potential confounding variables, the activity observed in Chinese patients is comparable to that reported in previous studies of ceralasertib plus durvalumab in patients with advanced solid tumors [16–18]. For example, in the multicenter HUDSON study (NCT03334617), among 79 response-evaluable patients with NSCLC, the ORR was 13.9% (11/79 patients) and median PFS was 5.8 months (80% CI 4.6‒7.4 months) [16]. The population in HUDSON was heavily pretreated, unselected for PD-L1 status, and all were refractory or resistant to previous anti-PD-(L)1 immunotherapy or platinum-doublet therapy [16]. Furthermore, in the single-arm, open-label, phase II VIKTORY study in patients with histologically confirmed metastatic melanoma who were refractory or who had relapsed on anti-PD-1/PD-L1 therapy (monotherapy or in combination), the ORR was 30.0% (9/30 patients, 95% CI 13.6‒46.4%) and response to ceralasertib plus durvalumab was independent of the response to prior immunotherapy [17]. Overall, these clinical data suggest that ceralasertib may be able to modulate the tumor microenvironment to a state which is sensitive to immunotherapy, and therefore, ceralasertib may have the potential to reverse anti-PD-(L)1 resistance and benefit patients with solid tumors which are refractory to or have progressed on anti-PD-(L)1 treatments and enable effective tumor cell killing when used in combination with durvalumab. However, additional studies are needed to confirm these preliminary antitumor effects in larger patient populations.

The strong mechanistic rationale for combining ceralasertib with anti-PD-L1 treatment, together with promising findings from early-phase clinical trials such as HUDSON [16], led to the inception of the global, phase III LATIFY study, which evaluated ceralasertib plus durvalumab compared with docetaxel, in patients with locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC (without actionable genomic alterations) whose disease had progressed on or after anti-PD-(L)1 treatment and platinum-based chemotherapy, including patients in China [23]. Despite the combination being generally well tolerated, the primary endpoint of OS was not met, and there were no improvements in PFS or ORR [23]. Further translational work will be needed to fully explore why promising efficacy signals seen in early-phase studies failed to translate into a statistically significant survival benefit in the phase III setting.

Conclusions

The results from this study demonstrate that ceralasertib in combination with durvalumab in Chinese patients with advanced solid tumors who are refractory or resistant to prior anti-PD-(L)1 therapy has a safety and PK profile and preliminary antitumor activity that is similar to that reported in other studies conducted in primarily Western patient populations.
