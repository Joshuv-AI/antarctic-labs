On the Schur multiplier of p p -groups with abelianization s s -elementary abelian
Sumana Hatui, Tony Nixon Mavely, Sahanawaj Sabnam

Abstract
Let p p be an odd prime. We describe a method to compute the Schur multiplier of finite p p -groups G G of nilpotency class 2 2 such that G / [ G , G ] ≅ ( ℤ p s ) k , s , k ∈ ℕ {G/[G,G]\cong(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{s}})^{k},s,k\in\mathbb{N}} , generalizing a method of Blackburn and Evens, who treated the case s = 1 s=1 . As an application, we investigate which abelian p p -groups can occur as the Schur multiplier of a non-abelian p p -group, a problem posed in Kourakava’s notebook. We further introduce the notions of s s -special p p -groups of rank k k generalizing the notion of special p p -groups of rank k k . We study the structural properties, compute the Schur multipliers of s s -special p p -groups of rank 1 1 .

Introduction

In this article, we develop an approach to determine the Schur multiplier for an extensive family of finite

p
-groups where
p
is an odd prime. Let
G
be a finite
p
-group and
G^{\prime}
denotes the commutator subgroup of
G
. The Schur multiplier
M(G)
of a group
G
is the second cohomology group
H^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
.
In
[
2
]
, Blackburn and Evens determined the Schur multiplier of
p
-groups
G
of nilpotency class
2
whose abelianization
G/G^{\prime}
is
elementary abelian, and as a notable consequence, computed the Schur
multiplier of extraspecial
p
-groups.
Motivated by this work, we generalize these results to a broader class of groups
G
of
nilpotency class
2
satisfying
G/G^{\prime}\cong(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{s}})^{k}
with
s,k\in\mathbb{N}
. We call a finite abelian
p
-group an
s
-elementary abelian
p
-group

if it is isomorphic to
(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{s}})^{k}
; such groups are
precisely the homocyclic
p
-groups of exponent
p^{s}
.
This notion generalizes that of elementary abelian
p
-groups.

The following notation will be used throughout the paper. For two abelian groups
A
and
B
,
A\otimes B
denotes the tensor product of
A
and
B
as
\mathbb{Z}
-modules and
A\wedge A
is the group defined by
\frac{A\otimes A}{\langle a\otimes a\mid a\in A\rangle}
. For
{a_{1},a_{2}\in A}
, let
a_{1}\wedge a_{2}
denote the image of
a_{1}\otimes a_{2}
in
A\wedge A
.
For a
p
-group
G
, we define
{V:=G/G^{\prime}}
and
W:=G^{\prime}
. Denote
{(v_{1},v_{2})=[g_{1},g_{2}]}
for
{v_{1}=g_{1}G^{\prime}}
and
{v_{2}=g_{2}G^{\prime}}
. Let
X_{1}
be the subgroup of
{V\otimes W}
generated by the elements

(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}):=v_{1}\otimes(v_{2},v_{3})+v_{2}\otimes(v_{3},v_{1})+v_{3}\otimes(v_{1},v_{2}),v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}\in V,

and
X_{2}
be the subgroup of
V\otimes W
generated by the elements
v\otimes f(v)
for
v\in V
, where
f:V\to W
is defined by
f(v)=g^{p^{s}}
for
v=gG^{\prime}
. Let
X
be the subgroup of
V\otimes W
generated by
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a
p
-group with nilpotency class
2
, and
G/G^{\prime}
be an
s
-elementary abelian
p
-group. Define a homomorphism

\rho:V\wedge V\longrightarrow W\text{ by }\rho(v_{1}\wedge v_{2})=(v_{1},v_{2})\quad\text{for all }v_{1},v_{2}\in V.

The following holds:

1.

There exists an abelian group
M^{*}
with a subgroup
N
such that

N\cong\frac{V\otimes W}{X},\qquad\frac{M^{*}}{N}\cong V\wedge V.

2.

Let
M
be the subgroup of
M^{*}
containing
N
such that
M/N

corresponds to
\ker\rho
. Then

M(G)\cong M.

The proof of this result is presented in Section
3
.
As a further consequence of Theorem
1.1
, we address a problem posed in the Kourovka notebook
[
9
, Problem 15.30]
, which is given below.

Problem.

Is it true that every finite abelian
p
-group is isomorphic to the Schur
multiplier of some non-abelian finite
p
-group?

Recently, Rai
[
14
, Theorem 6]
, proved that every elementary abelian
p
-group arises as the Schur multiplier of some
non-abelian finite
p
-group. Generalizing this result, we prove the following
theorem in Section
4
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{N}
. The following abelian groups occur as the Schur multiplier of some non-abelian
p
-groups.

(i)

(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{s}})^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{m_{1}}}
, where
m_{1},n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and
m_{1}<s
.

(ii)

(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{s}})^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{m_{1}}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{m_{2}}}
, where
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{2\}
and
m_{1},m_{2}<s
.

(iii)

(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{s}})^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{m_{1}}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{m_{2}}}\times\ldots\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{m_{r}}}
, where
3\leq r
,
m_{i}<s
for
1\leq i\leq r
and
n\geq\frac{1}{2}(a-1)(a-2)
for
a=\lceil\frac{3r}{2}+2\rceil
.

In particular, the group
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n_{1}}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n_{2}}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n_{3}}}
is also obtained as the Schur multiplier of some non-abelian
p
-group, for any
n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
.

A proof of this result is given in Section
4
.

Remark 1.3
.

For distinct primes
p_{i}
, let
H(p_{i})
denote a group listed in Theorem
1.2
. By
[
8
, Theorem 2.2.10]
,
\prod_{i=1}^{n}H({p_{i}})
is also the Schur multiplier of some non-abelian group.

A finite
p
-group

is called an

-special

-group of rank k
if

is an
s
-elementary abelian
p
-group minimally generated by

and

is an
s
-elementary abelian
p
-group. We say that a group is an
s
-extraspecial

-group if it is an
s
-special
p
-group of rank

. These groups genralize the notion of special
p
-groups of rank

and extraspecial
p
-groups. The structure of extraspecial

-groups was subsequently described in

[
8
, Theorem 3.3.4]
, where it was shown that every such group is a central product
of extraspecial
p
-groups of order
p^{3}
. Recall that a group

is said to be a central product of subgroups

,

, amalgamating

if
G_{i}
generate

and for all

in

G_{i}\cap G_{j}=D

-extraspecial
p
-groups and compute their Schur multiplier in the following result. This generalizes the result for extraspecial
p
-groups.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
G
be an
s
-extraspecial
p
-group, where
s\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then there exists an integer
r\geq 1
such that
G
is the central product
of
r

s
-extraspecial
p
-subgroups of order
p^{3s}
, amalgamating
the center
Z(G)
.
In particular,

|G|=p^{(2r+1)s}
.
Moreover, if
r>1
, then
M(G)\cong(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{s}})^{\,2r^{2}-r-1}
.

A proof of this result is given in Section
5
.
Furthermore, we show that for

, these groups are unicentral (c.f. Definition
2.8
).

Theorem 5.1

(Chapter I, Corollary I. 30 of

]
)
.

Let

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

The case
|G|=p^{3s}
is trivial. Now we assume that
{|G|>p^{3s}}
. Since
G
is
s

\{g_{1},\ldots,g_{d}\}

and
{G/G^{\prime}\cong\langle g_{1}G^{\prime}\rangle\times\ldots\times\langle g_{d}G^{\prime}\rangle}

Without loss of generality,

can be chosen such that

and
{[g_{1},g_{i}]=[g_{2},g_{i}]=1}

{i\in\{3,\ldots,d\}}
.
We have that
{\langle w\rangle=Z(G)=G^{\prime}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p^{s}}}
. Let

to be the centralizer of

in

.
We now show that

is an
s
-extraspecial
p
-group of order

.
We have
{G_{1}^{\prime}=G^{\prime}=\langle w\rangle}
and hence,

{G_{1}/G_{1}^{\prime}=\langle g_{1}G^{\prime},g_{2}G^{\prime}\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p^{s}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{s}}}
.
Therefore,
|G_{1}|=p^{3s}
,
{G_{1}^{\prime}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p^{s}}}
, and
{G_{1}/G_{1}^{\prime}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p^{s}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{s}}}
. We are left to show that
{Z(G_{1})\cong G_{1}^{\prime}}
. Note that any element of

can be written as

{b\equiv 0}

{p^{s}}

[g_{1}^{a}g_{2}^{b}w^{c},g_{2}]=1

is an

{[G,G_{1}]\subseteq G^{\prime}=Z(G)=Z(G_{1})}

.

. We have

{\psi(g_{i})=g_{i}w^{m_{i}}}

which act trivially on

that act trivially on

{\langle w\rangle\subseteq G_{1}\cap K\subseteq Z(K)=Z(G)=\langle w\rangle}
