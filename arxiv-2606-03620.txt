Physics-Guided Policy Optimization with Self-Distillation
Ke Wang ∗, Yuning Wu ∗, Haoran Liu, Chaoqun Jia, Devin Chen, Kai Wei

Abstract
Self-distilled policy optimization (SDPO) has become a popular paradigm for LLM post-training, where a model learns from its own predictions conditioned on privileged information. SDPO, however, is sensitive to how much each update step should be trusted: corrections from a self-teacher can be highly informative on some batches and misleading on others, and applying them uniformly with a fixed step size can destabilize training. Drawing inspiration from viscous-fluid dynamics and formalizing the analogy at the SDE level, we propose Physics-Guided Policy Optimization (PGPO), which introduces an information-modulated step-size multiplier derived from a mutual-information estimate between the student’s predictions and the feedback-conditioned teacher. We show that this modulation preserves the order-1 weak-approximation guarantees of vanilla SGD, and incurs negligible overhead per iteration. We evaluate PGPO on the Science-QA dataset, where it outperforms SDPO on 3 of the 4 domains with gains of up to +4.5 points, while remaining stable in a setting where SDPO collapses late in training.

Introduction

On-policy distillation (OPD)
(
Lu and Lab 2025
;
Song and Zheng 2026
;
Li et al. 2026b
;
Team et al. 2026
;
DeepSeek-AI 2026
)
has emerged as a popular training paradigm for large language model post-training, utilizing a teacher model to generate dense, token-level guidance for supervising student model training. A recent variant, self-distilled policy optimization (SDPO)
(
Hübotter et al. 2026
)
, has attracted community attention by treating a single model as both teacher and student: the teacher receives privileged information (e.g., correct solutions) to generate signals, while the student learns from these signals without such access. This method eliminates the need for a separate, larger teacher model and facilitates iterative self-improvement. Existing studies
(
Shenfeld et al. 2026
;
Song et al. 2026
;
Zhao et al. 2026
;
Ye et al. 2026
;
Kim et al. 2026
)
have shown that combining SDPO with reinforcement learning with verified rewards (RLVR)
(
Lambert et al. 2025
)
leads to efficient performance gains.

However, SDPO faces critical challenges during training. While dense token-level supervision enables rapid early gains, it becomes unstable as the student model evolves. As generated prefixes drift from the teacher’s conditioning distribution, corrections become unreliable or directionally misleading. Moreover, applying corrections uniformly across all samples, including correct ones, introduces ambiguity and corrupts learned behaviors. Extreme token-level rewards further destabilize training by dominating gradient updates, ultimately leading to collapse. Behind these symptoms lies a fundamental credit assignment problem:
treating all tokens and samples uniformly fails to account for their varying informativeness and reliability.

To alleviate these issues, several methodologies have been proposed: routing corrections based on sample correctness
(
Li et al. 2026a
)
, filtering signals by model confidence
(
Yang et al. 2026
)
, and anchoring updates to environment rewards
(
Ko et al. 2026
)
. In contrast to these approaches, we propose
Physics-Guided Policy Optimization
(PGPO), a method grounded in physical intuition from fluid mechanics
(
Cengel and Cimbala 2013
)
. PGPO interprets the optimization process as a particle moving through a viscous medium, where viscosity adaptively modulates step sizes based on local mutual information. This mechanism mirrors non-Newtonian fluids, where viscosity adapts to flow conditions, providing a principled alternative to uniform correction strategies that preserves informative signals while suppressing noise. By scaling each update step according to the informativeness of the current self-teacher signal, PGPO addresses the credit assignment problem and prevents the instability that plagues training.

Our contributions are as follows:

•

We propose Physics-Guided Policy Optimization (PGPO), a method inspired by fluid mechanics that addresses credit assignment in self-distilled policy optimization (SDPO).

•

We show that PGPO admits an order-1 weak SDE approximation and demonstrate its effectiveness in preliminary experiments.

Preliminaries

2.1
Problem Formulation

Consider a dataset
\mathcal{D}=\{(\mathbf{x}_{i},\mathbf{y}_{i})\}_{i=1}^{N}
consisting of
N
prompt-response pairs, where
\mathbf{x}_{i}
denotes the input prompt and
\mathbf{y}_{i}
the target response from offline datasets or online rollouts. We aim to optimize a large language model (LLM) parameterized by
\mathbf{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, denoted as
\pi_{\mathbf{\theta}}
, by solving the empirical risk minimization problem:

\min_{\mathbf{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{\theta}):=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\ell_{i}(\mathbf{\theta}),

(1)

where
\ell_{i}(\mathbf{\theta}):=\ell(\mathbf{\theta};\mathbf{x}_{i},\mathbf{y}_{i})
represents the training objective on the
i
-th example. We use this notation throughout the paper for simplicity.

In self-distilled policy optimization (SDPO)
(
Hübotter et al. 2026
)
, the student
\pi_{\mathbf{\theta}}
generates responses, receives feedback
f_{i}
, and distills from a self-teacher that retrospectively corrects mistakes:

\ell_{i}(\mathbf{\theta})=\sum_{t}\mathbb{D}_{\text{KL}}\left(\pi_{\mathbf{\theta}}(\cdot|\mathbf{x}_{i},\mathbf{y}_{<t})\,\|\,\text{stopgrad}(\pi_{\mathbf{\theta}}(\cdot|\mathbf{x}_{i},f_{i},\mathbf{y}_{<t}))\right).

(2)

2.2
Stochastic Gradient Descent

To minimize
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{\theta})
, we can employ stochastic gradient descent (SGD) and its adaptive variants (e.g., Adam
(
Kingma and Ba 2017
)
). At each iteration
k
, SGD samples a mini-batch
\mathcal{B}_{k}\subset\{1,...,N\}
and updates with learning rate
\eta>0
:

\mathbf{\theta}_{k+1}=\mathbf{\theta}_{k}-\eta\cdot\nabla\widehat{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{\theta}_{k}),\quad\text{where}\quad\nabla\widehat{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{\theta}_{k})=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{B}_{k}|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{B}_{k}}\nabla_{\mathbf{\theta}}\ell_{i}(\mathbf{\theta}_{k}).

(3)

2.3
Stochastic Differential Equation

The dynamics of SGD can be interpreted as stochastic differential equations (SDEs)
(
Li et al. 2017
)
. Rewrite the SGD update rule as:

\mathbf{\theta}_{k+1}-\mathbf{\theta}_{k}=-\eta\cdot\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{\theta}_{k})+\sqrt{\eta}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{k},

(4)

where
\mathbf{v}_{k}=\sqrt{\eta}\cdot(\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{\theta}_{k})-\nabla\widehat{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{\theta}_{k}))
is a
d
-dimensional random vector representing the gradient noise. By the Central Limit Theorem
(
Wasserman 2004
)
, for sufficiently large mini-batch sizes,
\mathbf{v}_{k}
is approximately Gaussian with mean zero and covariance matrix
\eta\cdot\mathbf{\Sigma}(\mathbf{\theta}_{k})
, where

\mathbf{\Sigma}(\mathbf{\theta})=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{\theta})-\nabla_{\mathbf{\theta}}\ell_{i}(\mathbf{\theta})\right)\,\left(\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{\theta})-\nabla_{\mathbf{\theta}}\ell_{i}(\mathbf{\theta})\right)^{\top}

(5)

captures the variance of stochastic gradients across dataset.

To derive the continuous-time limit, we identify the discrete update with the Euler-Maruyama discretization of an SDE
(
Kloden and Platen 1992
)
. Setting the time step
\Delta t=\eta
and recognizing that
\mathbf{v}_{k}\approx\sqrt{\eta}\cdot\mathbf{\Sigma}^{1/2}(\mathbf{\theta}_{k})\,\mathbf{z}_{k}
, where
\mathbf{z}_{k}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{I}_{d})
and
\mathbf{\Sigma}^{1/2}
denotes the matrix square root.

\mathbf{\theta}_{k+1}=\mathbf{\theta}_{k}-\eta\cdot\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{\theta}_{k})+\eta\cdot\mathbf{\Sigma}^{1/2}(\mathbf{\theta}_{k})\,\mathbf{z}_{k}.

(6)

Taking the continuous-time limit yields the SGD-SDE:

d\mathbf{\theta}_{t}=\underbrace{-\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{\theta}_{t})}_{\text{drift}}\,dt+\sqrt{\eta}\cdot\underbrace{\mathbf{\Sigma}^{1/2}(\mathbf{\theta}_{t})}_{\text{diffusion}}\,d\mathbf{w}_{t}.

(7)

where
\mathbf{w}_{t}
is a standard
d
-dimensional Brownian motion. The learning rate
\eta
sets the time scale: one unit of SDE time corresponds to
1/\eta
SGD iterations. This SGD-SDE provides a continuous-time approximation of SGD in the
weak sense
: their statistical properties (e.g., marginal distributions, expectations) converge as
\eta\to 0
(see Appendix
A
).

3
Physics-Guided Policy Optimization

The SGD-SDE can be interpreted through fluid mechanics
(
Cengel and Cimbala 2013
)
, where the parameter trajectory
\mathbf{\theta}_{t}
represents a particle moving through a viscous fluid. The drift term
-\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{\theta}_{t})
acts as a pressure gradient driving the particle toward loss minima, while the diffusion term
\mathbf{\Sigma}^{1/2}(\mathbf{\theta}_{t})d\mathbf{w}_{t}
captures stochastic fluctuations from mini-batch sampling, analogous to Brownian motion. This perspective suggests a natural mechanism for adaptive step-size control: modulating viscosity based on how informative the self-teacher’s correction is. In physical systems, high viscosity slows motion while low viscosity permits faster movement; we let an uninformative correction keep baseline viscosity, and an informative correction reduce it.

We quantify the informativeness of the correction using the mutual information between predictions and feedback on mini-batch
\mathcal{B}_{k}
:

I_{k}:=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{B}_{k}|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{B}_{k}}\left[H(\mathbf{y}_{i}|\mathbf{x}_{i},\mathbf{y}_{<t})-H(\mathbf{y}_{i}|\mathbf{x}_{i},f_{i},\mathbf{y}_{<t})\right],

(8)

where high
I_{k}
indicates that the feedback is highly informative; the student’s prediction without feedback diverges from the feedback-conditioned teacher, so the correction carries a strong, reliable signal. Conversely, low
I_{k}
indicates that the student already agrees with the teacher, making the correction small. This motivates an adaptive viscosity that decreases (taking larger steps) when the correction is informative, and stays at baseline when it is not.

With the physical interpretation and a quantified informativeness signal in hand, we introduce Physics-guided Policy Optimization (PGPO), where a multiplier
\rho(I_{k})
modulates the gradient step:

\mathbf{\theta}_{k+1}=\mathbf{\theta}_{k}-\eta\cdot\rho(I_{k})\cdot\nabla\widehat{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{\theta}_{k}),\quad\text{where}\quad\rho(I_{k})=\min\left(\exp\left(\alpha\cdot I_{k}\right),\rho_{\max}\right),

(9)

with
\alpha>0
controlling sensitivity and
\rho_{\max}\geq 1
capping acceleration. This yields:

•

High
I_{k}
(informative correction):
\rho_{k}>1
→ reduced viscosity → larger steps

•

Low
I_{k}
(uninformative correction):
\rho_{k}\approx 1
→ baseline viscosity → standard steps

The cap
\rho_{\max}
mirrors a physical safety constraint, limiting acceleration when the information signal spikes, while
\rho_{k}\geq 1
keeps each update at least as large as the baseline SGD step.

Algorithm
2.3
presents the complete PGPO framework. The key innovation is the physics-guided multiplier
\rho_{k}
, which modulates step size based on the per-batch information signal
I_{k}
, with only
O(1)
overhead per iteration, since
I_{k}
reuses the forward pass used for gradient computation. The framework extends to adaptive optimizers (e.g., Adam
(
Kingma and Ba 2017
)
) by modulating the final update without altering moment accumulation: for Adam with update direction
\mathbf{h}_{k}
, the viscosity-modulated update becomes
\mathbf{\theta}_{k+1}=\mathbf{\theta}_{k}-\eta\cdot\rho_{k}\cdot\mathbf{h}_{k}
.

4
Conclusion

We proposed Physics-Guided Policy Optimization (PGPO), a method that addresses the credit assignment problem in self-distilled policy optimization (SDPO) through adaptive step-size modulation motivated by an analogy with fluid mechanics. We showed that PGPO is an order-1 weak approximation of a modulated-drift SDE, and demonstrated through preliminary experiments that PGPO achieves consistent improvements over SDPO across multiple scientific reasoning domains. The current instantiation modulates step size at the batch level and is sensitive to the choice of sensitivity parameter
\alpha
, which we observed in the Biology setting where no single
\alpha
matched SDPO; finer-grained (e.g., per-token) modulation and adaptive
\alpha
schedules are natural directions for future work. These results suggest that PGPO offers an alternative approach to stabilizing self-distillation in large language model post-training.

Appendix A
Weak Convergence Guarantees

We now show that PGPO admits an order-1 weak approximation by an SDE with a modulated drift. We emphasize that this is a consistency-style result rather than a convergence-rate guarantee.

Definition 1

(Weak Approximation)
.

(
Mannella 1997
)

Let
0<\eta<1
,
T>0
, and set
N=\lfloor T/\eta\rfloor
. Let
\mathcal{G}
denote the set of functions with polynomial growth, i.e.,
g\in\mathcal{G}
if there exist constants
K,\kappa>0
such that
|g(\mathbf{x})|\leq K(1+\|\mathbf{x}\|^{\kappa})
. We say that the SDE

d\mathbf{\theta}_{t}=\mathbf{b}(\mathbf{\theta}_{t})\,dt+\mathbf{\sigma}(\mathbf{\theta}_{t})\,d\mathbf{w}_{t},\quad\mathbf{\theta}_{0}=\mathbf{\theta}_{\text{init}}

(10)

is an
order
\alpha
weak approximation
to SGD if for every
g\in\mathcal{G}
, there exists
C>0
independent of
\eta
such that for all
k=0,1,\ldots,N
,

\left|\mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{\theta}_{k\eta})]-\mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{\theta}_{k}^{\text{SGD}})]\right|\leq C\eta^{\alpha}.

(11)

Theorem 2

(Weak Convergence of PGPO)
.

Let
T>0
and define the gradient covariance
\mathbf{\Sigma}(\mathbf{\theta})
as in Eq. (
5
). Assume
\mathcal{L}
and
\ell_{i}(\mathbf{\theta})
are Lipschitz continuous with at most linear growth, and possess sufficiently smooth derivatives in
\mathcal{G}
. Further assume the physics-guided multiplier
\rho(I)
is bounded and Lipschitz continuous in
I
. Then:

(i) Vanilla SGD:

(
Li et al. 2017
)
The SDE
d\mathbf{\theta}_{t}=-\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{\theta}_{t})\,dt+\mathbf{\Sigma}^{1/2}(\mathbf{\theta}_{t})\,d\mathbf{w}_{t}
is an order-1 weak approximation of SGD, where
t=k\cdot\eta
is iteration time.

(ii) PGPO:
The modified SDE
d\mathbf{\theta}_{t}=-\rho(I_{t})\cdot\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{\theta}_{t})\,dt+\mathbf{\Sigma}^{1/2}(\mathbf{\theta}_{t})\,d\mathbf{w}_{t}
is an order-1 weak approximation of PGPO, where
I_{t}
is the information measure at time
t
and the diffusion term remains unchanged.

Proof sketch for (ii).
Since
\rho
is bounded and Lipschitz in
I
, the modified drift
-\rho(I_{t})\nabla\mathcal{L}(\theta_{t})
inherits the Lipschitz and linear-growth conditions of
-\nabla\mathcal{L}
. The result follows by applying
(
Li et al. 2017
)
to the modified drift, with the diffusion coefficient unchanged.

Theorem
2
states that the discrete PGPO updates are tracked, in the weak sense, by the modulated-drift SDE as
\eta\to 0
; it does not by itself imply that PGPO converges faster or to a better stationary point than SDPO. We use it solely to confirm that information-based step-size modulation does not break the SDE picture motivating the design. We also note that the form
\rho(I_{k})=\min(\exp(\alpha I_{k}),\rho_{\max})
is a design choice: any bounded, Lipschitz function of
I_{k}
would yield the same conclusion, and we treat
\alpha
and
\rho_{\max}
as ordinary hyperparameters.
