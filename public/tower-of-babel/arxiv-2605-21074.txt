A Sine-Quadrature Search Channel for Off-Diagonal Lunar Tidal Residuals
Muhittin Cenk Eser

Abstract
The Newtonian quadrupolar tidal tensor accurately describes the dominant Earth–Moon tide. In its principal frame, this tensor is diagonal and produces the familiar 90 ∘ 90^{\circ} stretching–squeezing pattern. A 45 ∘ 45^{\circ} projection of the Newtonian field is possible, but it is not an independent cross-tidal residual. We formulate a residual test for off-diagonal lunar tidal geometry. The key observation is that the diagonal Newtonian principal-frame tensor produces a plus-type cos ⁡ ( 2 ​ β ) \cos(2\beta) angular channel, whereas an off-diagonal tidal residual would appear in the sine-quadrature channel sin ⁡ ( 2 ​ β ) \sin(2\beta) . This gives a clean harmonic target for residual analysis after the dominant Newtonian, solar, geophysical, loading, instrumental, and frame-dependent contributions have been removed. To motivate the structure of such a residual, we use Halilsoy’s cross-polarized cylindrical gravitational waves as a relativistic proof of principle. Their weak-field tidal sector contains an off-diagonal component capable of rotating a local tidal eigenframe. By matching this eigenframe rotation to that of an effective lunar residual tensor, we define an alpha-dependent coefficient χ H ​ ( α , t , ρ ) \chi_{H}(\alpha,t,\rho) . This coefficient preserves the 90 ∘ 90^{\circ} orthogonality of the principal axes but rotates the whole eigenframe and produces a 45 ∘ 45^{\circ} -type signature through the sin ⁡ ( 2 ​ β ) \sin(2\beta) acceleration channel. The corresponding residual acceleration scale is Δ ​ a 45 H = a 0 ​ χ H \Delta a_{45}^{H}=a_{0}\chi_{H} , where a 0 = G ​ M M ​ R E / D 3 ≃ 5.5 × 10 − 7 ​ m ​ s − 2 a_{0}=GM_{M}R_{E}/D^{3}\simeq 5.5\times 10^{-7}\ {\rm m\,s^{-2}} . The construction is not a replacement for standard lunar tidal theory and does not model the Earth–Moon system as a Halilsoy spacetime. It provides a mathematically explicit and observationally constrainable residual framework, and it identifies the associated back-action problem as the next dynamical closure: whether such an off-diagonal sector can be coupled self-consistently to curvature, orbital motion, and terrestrial tidal response.



I
Introduction

The dominant Earth–Moon tide is accurately described by the Newtonian quadrupolar tidal tensor.
In a local principal frame aligned with the Earth–Moon direction, the leading two-dimensional tidal block has the diagonal form

\mathcal{E}_{N}=\kappa_{M}\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad\kappa_{M}=\frac{GM_{M}}{D^{3}},

(1)

where
M_{M}
is the lunar mass and
D
is the Earth–Moon distance.
This tensor produces stretching along the Earth–Moon axis and squeezing along a transverse direction.
Its principal axes are orthogonal, giving the familiar
90^{\circ}
tidal geometry
(
14
;
12
;
2
;
1
;
17
)
.

The present work does not challenge this standard description.
Instead, it asks a more specific residual question: if an off-diagonal cross-tidal component existed after the dominant Newtonian tide and standard environmental corrections had been removed, what would its tensorial and harmonic signature be?
This framing is important.
A diagonal tensor can be projected along any direction, including
45^{\circ}
.
Such a projection, however, is not an independent cross-tidal residual.
It is only the value of the same diagonal plus-type field along a chosen direction.

For the diagonal Newtonian tensor, the projected surface acceleration along a direction making an angle
\beta
with the Earth–Moon axis is

a_{N}(\beta)=a_{0}\left[\frac{1}{2}+\frac{3}{2}\cos(2\beta)\right],\qquad a_{0}=\frac{GM_{M}R_{E}}{D^{3}}.

(2)

Thus the ordinary Newtonian principal-frame projection occupies the plus-type harmonic channel
\cos(2\beta)
.
There is no independent
\sin(2\beta)
channel in Eq. (
2
).
An off-diagonal residual would instead appear in the sine-quadrature channel.
This harmonic separation is the central observational idea of the paper:

\boxed{\begin{aligned} \text{diagonal plus channel}&\sim\cos(2\beta),\\
\text{off-diagonal cross channel}&\sim\sin(2\beta).\end{aligned}}

(3)

The simplest local residual tensor realizing this distinction is

\mathcal{E}_{\chi}=\kappa_{M}\begin{pmatrix}2&\chi\\
\chi&-1\end{pmatrix},

(4)

where
\chi
is a dimensionless off-diagonal residual coefficient.
Because this matrix is symmetric, its eigenvectors remain orthogonal whenever the eigenvalues are distinct.
The off-diagonal sector therefore does not destroy the
90^{\circ}
separation of the principal axes.
Instead, it rotates the entire eigenframe.

For Eq. (
4
), the eigenframe rotation angle is

\Theta(\chi)=\frac{1}{2}\arctan\left(\frac{2\chi}{3}\right).

(5)

For small
|\chi|
, one has

\Theta(\chi)\simeq\frac{\chi}{3}.

(6)

For large
|\chi|
, the rotated eigenframe approaches a
45^{\circ}
-type orientation relative to the original Newtonian frame:

\Theta(\chi)\rightarrow\pm\frac{\pi}{4}.

(7)

The two principal axes nevertheless remain separated by
90^{\circ}
.
This is the basic
90^{\circ}
–
45^{\circ}
distinction used throughout the paper:
90^{\circ}
is the mutual separation of the principal axes, while
45^{\circ}
is the limiting orientation of a cross-dominant rotated eigenframe.
This distinction is essential for separating the orientation of a cross-dominant eigenframe from the mutual orthogonality of the principal tidal axes.

The tensorial language of tidal deformation is common to Newtonian gravity, relativistic geodesic deviation, and gravitational-wave physics.
In general relativity, the local relative acceleration of neighboring freely falling particles is governed by

\ddot{\xi}^{i}=-\mathcal{E}^{i}{}_{j}\xi^{j},\qquad\mathcal{E}_{ij}=R_{i0j0},

(8)

where
\mathcal{E}_{ij}
is the electric part of the curvature tensor in a local inertial frame
(

;

;
22
;
17
)
.
For weak gravitational waves, the plus and cross polarizations are most directly interpreted through their tidal action on test particles
(

.

The relativistic motivation used here comes from Halilsoy’s cross-polarized cylindrical gravitational-wave solution, introduced as a second-polarization extension of the Einstein–Rosen cylindrical wave family

;

.
In its weak-field tidal sector, the second polarization generates an off-diagonal transverse tidal component.
This makes the Halilsoy geometry a useful exact relativistic proof of principle for how an off-diagonal tidal sector can rotate a local eigenframe.
Related studies of exact, cylindrical, and standing gravitational waves provide the broader context for interpreting such off-diagonal sectors as genuine polarization structures rather than mere coordinate artifacts

The goal is not to identify the Earth–Moon system with a Halilsoy spacetime.
That would be a different and much stronger claim.
Rather, the Halilsoy solution is used to motivate the structure of a residual coefficient
\chi_{H}(\alpha,t,\rho)
that can be inserted into the local lunar tidal tensor.
The resulting effective tensor is

\mathcal{E}_{M,H}=\kappa_{M}\begin{pmatrix}2&\chi_{H}(\alpha,t,\rho)\\
\chi_{H}(\alpha,t,\rho)&-1\end{pmatrix}.

(9)

The associated projected acceleration is

a_{\parallel}^{M,H}(\beta)=a_{0}\left[\frac{1}{2}+\frac{3}{2}\cos(2\beta)+\chi_{H}(\alpha,t,\rho)\sin(2\beta)\right].

(10)

The final term is the proposed off-diagonal residual channel.
Its extrema occur at

\beta=45^{\circ},\ 135^{\circ},\ 225^{\circ},\ 315^{\circ}.

(11)

The observational consequence of the model is therefore not a modification of the dominant Newtonian lunar tide, but the possible appearance of a residual sine-quadrature component after standard tidal corrections.
If residual data are decomposed as

a_{\rm residual}(\beta)=A_{c}\cos(2\beta)+A_{s}\sin(2\beta),

(12)

then the proposed cross-tidal coefficient is associated with

A_{s}=a_{0}\chi_{H},\qquad\chi_{H}=\frac{A_{s}}{a_{0}}.

(13)

This gives a direct residual target: the model is constrainable through the sine-quadrature component, not through the ordinary Newtonian plus-channel amplitude.

The formulation also identifies a second, deeper problem: back-action.
Once an off-diagonal tidal sector is written as a local residual operator, one can ask whether it remains an externally prescribed test coefficient or whether it must be coupled dynamically to the background curvature, the Earth–Moon orbital motion, and the terrestrial tidal response.
The present paper does not solve this coupled problem.
It provides the kinematic residual structure needed before such a dynamical closure can be attempted.
In this sense, the proposed model has two layers: an immediately testable harmonic residual signature and a future self-consistency problem involving back-action.

The main contributions of the paper are the construction of four linked quantities:

\boxed{\begin{gathered}\chi_{H}(\alpha,t,\rho),\qquad\Theta_{M,H}(\alpha,t,\rho),\\
a_{\rm cross}^{H}(\beta;\alpha,t,\rho),\qquad\Delta a_{45}^{H}(\alpha,t,\rho).\end{gathered}}

(14)

They describe, respectively, the Halilsoy-inspired residual coefficient, the rotated eigenframe angle, the sine-quadrature acceleration channel, and the physical
45^{\circ}
-direction residual scale.

The paper is organized as follows.
We first review the Newtonian lunar tidal tensor and its projected harmonic content.
We then extract the weak-field Halilsoy tidal block that motivates the off-diagonal sector.
Next, we derive the alpha-dependent residual coefficient
\chi_{H}
.
We then obtain the projected acceleration, the
45^{\circ}
-type residual scale, and the amplitude ratios relative to the Newtonian tide.
The visual diagnostics summarize the tensorial and observational content of the model.
Finally, we discuss the physical interpretation, observational meaning, limitations, and back-action problem.

II
Newtonian lunar tidal tensor and harmonic separation

We begin with the standard local Newtonian description of the lunar tide.
Let
\bm{D}
be the vector from the Earth’s center to the Moon, let
D=|\bm{D}|
, and define the unit direction

\bm{n}_{M}=\frac{\bm{D}}{D}.

(15)

For a local displacement
\bm{r}
from the Earth’s center with
|\bm{r}|\ll D
, the Newtonian lunar potential is

\Phi_{M}(\bm{r})=-\frac{GM_{M}}{|\bm{D}-\bm{r}|}.

(16)

Expanding about the Earth’s center and retaining the quadrupolar term gives the tidal potential

\Phi_{\rm tide}=-\frac{GM_{M}}{2D^{3}}\left[3(\bm{n}_{M}\cdot\bm{r})^{2}-r^{2}\right].

(17)

This is the standard leading-order construction of the lunar tide in celestial mechanics and geophysical tidal theory
(
14
;
12
;
2
;
1
;
17
)
.

The corresponding tidal operator is the Hessian of the tidal potential, up to the conventional sign used in the relative-acceleration equation.
In the principal frame aligned with
\bm{n}_{M}
, the three-dimensional Newtonian tidal tensor is

\mathcal{E}_{N}^{(3)}=\kappa_{M}\begin{pmatrix}2&0&0\\
0&-1&0\\
0&0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad\kappa_{M}=\frac{GM_{M}}{D^{3}}.

(18)

It is trace-free:

\operatorname{tr}\mathcal{E}_{N}^{(3)}=2\kappa_{M}-\kappa_{M}-\kappa_{M}=0.

(19)

This trace-free quadrupolar structure is the familiar Newtonian tidal stretching–squeezing geometry.

Restricting to the local two-dimensional plane spanned by the Earth–Moon axis and one transverse direction gives

\boxed{\mathcal{E}_{N}=\kappa_{M}\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}.}

(20)

The eigenvalues are

2\kappa_{M},\qquad-\kappa_{M},

(21)

with eigenvectors aligned with the Earth–Moon axis and the transverse direction.
Thus the Newtonian tide stretches along the Earth–Moon direction and squeezes in the transverse direction.
The two principal axes are separated by
90^{\circ}
.

The absence of an off-diagonal term in Eq. (
20
) is the key point.
In the Newtonian principal frame,

(\mathcal{E}_{N})_{xy}=0.

(22)

Therefore the standard tensor contains no independent cross-tidal sector in that frame.
Any off-diagonal residual must appear only after the dominant plus-type Newtonian tide has been modeled and subtracted.

II.1
Projected Newtonian acceleration

Let

\bm{n}(\beta)=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\beta\\
\sin\beta\end{pmatrix}

(23)

be a unit direction making an angle
\beta
with the Earth–Moon axis.
At the Earth’s surface,

\bm{r}(\beta)=R_{E}\bm{n}(\beta).

(24)

The projected Newtonian tidal acceleration along this direction is

a_{N}(\beta)=R_{E}\,\bm{n}^{T}\mathcal{E}_{N}\bm{n}.

(25)

Substituting Eq. (
20
) gives

\displaystyle a_{N}(\beta)

\displaystyle=\kappa_{M}R_{E}\left(2\cos^{2}\beta-\sin^{2}\beta\right)

(26)

\displaystyle=\kappa_{M}R_{E}\left[\frac{1}{2}+\frac{3}{2}\cos(2\beta)\right].

(27)

Equivalently, with

a_{0}=\kappa_{M}R_{E}=\frac{GM_{M}R_{E}}{D^{3}},

(28)

one has

\boxed{a_{N}(\beta)=a_{0}\left[\frac{1}{2}+\frac{3}{2}\cos(2\beta)\right].}

(29)

Equation (
29
) contains only the
\cos(2\beta)
harmonic.
This is the plus-type channel.
There is no
\sin(2\beta)
term in the diagonal Newtonian principal-frame expression.

At
\beta=45^{\circ}
, the Newtonian projection is

a_{N}(45^{\circ})=\frac{1}{2}a_{0}.

(30)

This is not a cross-tidal residual.
It is merely the projection of the diagonal Newtonian tensor along a chosen direction.
The distinction matters because the proposed residual channel is not defined by evaluating the Newtonian field at
45^{\circ}
; it is defined by the appearance of an additional sine-quadrature harmonic absent from Eq. (
29
).

II.2
What an off-diagonal residual would look like

To see this explicitly, consider a local symmetric residual extension

\mathcal{E}_{\chi}=\kappa_{M}\begin{pmatrix}2&\chi\\
\chi&-1\end{pmatrix}.

(31)

The projected acceleration becomes

\displaystyle a_{\chi}(\beta)

\displaystyle=R_{E}\,\bm{n}^{T}\mathcal{E}_{\chi}\bm{n}

(32)

\displaystyle=a_{0}\left[2\cos^{2}\beta-\sin^{2}\beta+2\chi\sin\beta\cos\beta\right]

(33)

\displaystyle=a_{0}\left[\frac{1}{2}+\frac{3}{2}\cos(2\beta)+\chi\sin(2\beta)\right].

(34)

Thus the diagonal Newtonian part and the off-diagonal residual occupy different quadrature channels:

\displaystyle a_{\chi}(\beta)

\displaystyle=a_{N}(\beta)+a_{\rm cross}(\beta),

(35)

\displaystyle a_{N}(\beta)

\displaystyle=a_{0}\left[\frac{1}{2}+\frac{3}{2}\cos(2\beta)\right],

(36)

\displaystyle a_{\rm cross}(\beta)

\displaystyle=a_{0}\chi\sin(2\beta).

(37)

This decomposition is the operational reason for focusing on the
\sin(2\beta)
term.
A correction to the amplitude of the ordinary lunar tide changes the coefficient of
\cos(2\beta)
.
An off-diagonal cross residual instead appears as a sine-quadrature term.
Therefore, in residual data one should distinguish between

A_{c}\cos(2\beta)

(38)

and

A_{s}\sin(2\beta).

(39)

The first can represent an ordinary plus-channel residual or modeling error.
The second is the natural harmonic location of a cross-tidal residual.

The extrema of the off-diagonal residual occur when

|\sin(2\beta)|=1.

(40)

Hence

\beta=45^{\circ},\ 135^{\circ},\ 225^{\circ},\ 315^{\circ}.

(41)

This is the sense in which the proposed channel is
45^{\circ}
-type.
It is not because Newtonian gravity cannot be projected at
45^{\circ}
.
It is because the cross harmonic itself has extrema along those directions.
This distinction prevents an ordinary Newtonian projection from being confused with an independent off-diagonal residual channel.

II.3
Eigenframe interpretation

The same distinction appears at the eigenframe level.
For a real symmetric matrix

M=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
b&c\end{pmatrix},

(42)

the principal-axis rotation angle satisfies

\tan(2\Theta)=\frac{2b}{a-c}.

(43)

For Eq. (
31
),

a=2,\qquad b=\chi,\qquad c=-1.

(44)

Therefore,

\boxed{\tan(2\Theta)=\frac{2\chi}{3},\qquad\Theta(\chi)=\frac{1}{2}\arctan\left(\frac{2\chi}{3}\right).}

(45)

For
|\chi|\ll 1
,

\Theta(\chi)\simeq\frac{\chi}{3}.

(46)

For
|\chi|\gg 1
,

\Theta(\chi)\rightarrow\pm\frac{\pi}{4}.

(47)

The eigenvectors remain orthogonal because the tensor is symmetric.
Thus the cross sector rotates the whole eigenframe while preserving the
90^{\circ}
separation of the principal axes.

This section fixes the purely tensorial target.
A possible off-diagonal lunar residual should be identified not by the existence of a Newtonian projection at
45^{\circ}
, but by the appearance of a sine-quadrature residual harmonic and a corresponding eigenframe rotation.
The next section introduces the Halilsoy tidal block as a relativistic mechanism that naturally supplies such an off-diagonal sector.

III
Halilsoy weak-field tidal sector

The previous section identified the purely tensorial target: an off-diagonal residual in a local tidal tensor appears as a sine-quadrature
\sin(2\beta)
harmonic and rotates the local eigenframe.
We now introduce the relativistic mechanism used to motivate the structure of such a residual coefficient.
The role of the Halilsoy solution in this paper is not to model the Earth–Moon spacetime.
Its role is more limited and more useful: it provides an exact relativistic example in which a cross-polarized gravitational wave produces an off-diagonal tidal sector.

Halilsoy’s cross-polarized cylindrical gravitational-wave solution was introduced as a second-polarization extension of the Einstein–Rosen cylindrical wave family
(
3
;
23
;
21
;
7
)
.
The Einstein–Rosen family already provides a canonical setting for cylindrical gravitational waves, while Halilsoy’s extension introduces a second polarization through an off-diagonal metric sector.
This makes the solution a useful laboratory for the relation between gravitational-wave polarization, local curvature, and tidal eigenframe rotation.
Related work on exact cylindrical and standing gravitational waves further supports the interpretation of such sectors as genuine polarization structures rather than merely coordinate effects
(
18
;
6
;
19
;
5
;
15
;
20
)
.

In general relativity, the local tidal field is encoded in the electric part of the curvature tensor.
For neighboring freely falling test particles, the geodesic-deviation equation gives

\ddot{\xi}^{i}=-\mathcal{E}^{i}{}_{j}\xi^{j},\qquad\mathcal{E}_{ij}=R_{i0j0},

(48)

where the components are evaluated in a local orthonormal frame
(
16
;
13
;
22
;
17
)
.
In weak-field transverse-traceless language, one may equivalently regard the tidal matrix as the second time derivative of the transverse metric perturbation, up to convention-dependent signs:

\mathcal{E}_{ij}=-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}^{2}h^{\rm TT}_{ij}.

(49)

Thus plus and cross gravitational-wave polarizations have direct tidal meaning
(
10
;
11
;
4
)
.

For the weak-field Halilsoy standing-wave sector, choose a local cylindrical orthonormal frame

(e_{\rho},e_{\phi},e_{z}).

(50)

The transverse plane relevant to the second polarization is the
(e_{\phi},e_{z})
plane.
In this plane the weak-field tidal block can be written as

\boxed{\mathcal{E}_{\perp}^{H}=\frac{\varepsilon}{2\lambda^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}-Q_{0}+\dfrac{\lambda}{\rho}Q_{1}&-\sinh\alpha\,W\\[8.00003pt]
-\sinh\alpha\,W&Q_{0}\end{pmatrix}.}

(51)

Here
\varepsilon
is a weak-field amplitude parameter,
\lambda
is the wavelength scale, and
\alpha
controls the second polarization sector.
The functions entering Eq. (
51
) are

Q_{0}(t,\rho)=J_{0}(\rho/\lambda)\cos(t/\lambda),

(52)

Q_{1}(t,\rho)=J_{1}(\rho/\lambda)\cos(t/\lambda),

(53)

and

W(t,\rho)=J_{1}(\rho/\lambda)\sin(t/\lambda).

(54)

The off-diagonal tidal component is therefore

\boxed{\mathcal{E}_{\phi z}^{H}=-\frac{\varepsilon}{2\lambda^{2}}\sinh\alpha\,W(t,\rho).}

(55)

This term vanishes when

\alpha=0,

(56)

because

\sinh\alpha=0.

(57)

Thus the
\alpha=0
limit removes the Halilsoy cross sector in this transverse block.
For
\alpha\neq 0
, the off-diagonal component is generally nonzero and rotates the local tidal eigenframe.

The diagonal contrast of the transverse block is

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\phi\phi}^{H}-\mathcal{E}_{zz}^{H}

\displaystyle=\frac{\varepsilon}{2\lambda^{2}}\left(-Q_{0}+\frac{\lambda}{\rho}Q_{1}-Q_{0}\right)

(58)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\varepsilon}{2\lambda^{2}}\left(2Q_{0}-\frac{\lambda}{\rho}Q_{1}\right).

(59)

It is therefore useful to define the effective plus-sector factor

\boxed{P_{H}(t,\rho)=2Q_{0}(t,\rho)-\frac{\lambda}{\rho}Q_{1}(t,\rho).}

(60)

Substituting Eqs. (
52
) and (
53
) gives

\boxed{P_{H}(t,\rho)=\left[2J_{0}(\rho/\lambda)-\frac{\lambda}{\rho}J_{1}(\rho/\lambda)\right]\cos(t/\lambda).}

(61)

For compactness, define the radial factor

\boxed{D_{H}(\rho)=2J_{0}(\rho/\lambda)-\frac{\lambda}{\rho}J_{1}(\rho/\lambda).}

(62)

Then

P_{H}(t,\rho)=D_{H}(\rho)\cos(t/\lambda).

(63)

Similarly, define the Halilsoy cross-sector numerator

\boxed{C_{H}(\alpha,t,\rho)=\sinh\alpha\,W(t,\rho)=\sinh\alpha\,J_{1}(\rho/\lambda)\sin(t/\lambda).}

(64)

The two quantities

C_{H}(\alpha,t,\rho)\qquad\text{and}\qquad P_{H}(t,\rho)

(65)

are the local cross and plus-sector amplitudes controlling the rotation of the Halilsoy tidal eigenframe.

Because Eq. (
51
) is a real symmetric
2\times 2
matrix, its principal directions are orthogonal wherever the eigenvalues are distinct.
The off-diagonal term does not break orthogonality.
It rotates the orthogonal eigenframe in the
(e_{\phi},e_{z})
plane.
For a real symmetric matrix

M=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
b&c\end{pmatrix},

(66)

the principal-axis angle satisfies

\tan(2\Theta)=\frac{2b}{a-c}.

(67)

Applying Eq. (
67
) to Eq. (
51
), one finds

\tan(2\Theta_{H})=\frac{2C_{H}(\alpha,t,\rho)}{P_{H}(t,\rho)}.

(68)

In explicit Bessel form,

\boxed{\tan(2\Theta_{H})=\frac{2\sinh\alpha\,J_{1}(\rho/\lambda)\sin(t/\lambda)}{\left[2J_{0}(\rho/\lambda)-\dfrac{\lambda}{\rho}J_{1}(\rho/\lambda)\right]\cos(t/\lambda)}.}

(69)

For numerical or diagnostic use, it is better to preserve quadrant information by writing the angle with a two-argument arctangent.
Define

N_{H}(\alpha,t,\rho)=2\sinh\alpha\,J_{1}(\rho/\lambda)\sin(t/\lambda).

(70)

Then

\boxed{\Theta_{H}(\alpha,t,\rho)=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{atan2}\left(N_{H}(\alpha,t,\rho),D_{H}(\rho)\cos(t/\lambda)\right).}

(71)

This is the local Halilsoy tidal eigenframe angle.

The cross-dominant regime is obtained when the off-diagonal sector dominates the effective plus-sector contrast:

\left|2\sinh\alpha\,J_{1}(\rho/\lambda)\sin(t/\lambda)\right|\gg\left|D_{H}(\rho)\cos(t/\lambda)\right|.

(72)

In this limit,

|\tan(2\Theta_{H})|\gg 1,

(73)

and therefore

\boxed{\Theta_{H}\rightarrow\pm\frac{\pi}{4}.}

(74)

Again, this does not mean that the principal axes cease to be orthogonal.
It means that the whole orthogonal eigenframe is rotated toward a
45^{\circ}
-type orientation relative to the reference plus frame.

The parameter
\alpha
controls the strength of this cross sector.
For small
\alpha
,

\sinh\alpha=\alpha+O(\alpha^{3}),

(75)

so the cross-sector amplitude is linear in
\alpha
at leading order:

C_{H}(\alpha,t,\rho)=\alpha\,J_{1}(\rho/\lambda)\sin(t/\lambda)+O(\alpha^{3}).

(76)

Thus
\alpha
plays the role of a relativistic polarization parameter controlling the leading off-diagonal tidal effect.

The Halilsoy block therefore supplies exactly the tensorial ingredient needed for the residual lunar construction: a symmetric tidal operator with a controlled off-diagonal sector, a rotated eigenframe, and a cross-dominant
45^{\circ}
-type limit.
The next step is to convert the Halilsoy ratio
C_{H}/P_{H}
into a dimensionless residual coefficient
\chi_{H}
appropriate for the Newtonian lunar tidal scale.

IV
Effective Halilsoy-induced cross ratio

We now convert the Halilsoy tidal eigenframe ratio into a dimensionless off-diagonal residual coefficient suitable for the local lunar tidal tensor.
The purpose is not to match two spacetimes.
The purpose is to match the local tensorial mechanism by which an off-diagonal tidal sector rotates a symmetric tidal operator.

The lunar residual tensor introduced above has the form:

\mathcal{E}_{\chi}=\kappa_{M}\begin{pmatrix}2&\chi\\
\chi&-1\end{pmatrix}.

(77)

Its principal-axis angle satisfies

\tan(2\Theta)=\frac{2\chi}{3},

(78)

where the factor
3
is the diagonal contrast of the Newtonian two-dimensional tidal block:

2-(-1)=3.

(79)

The weak-field Halilsoy transverse tidal block, on the other hand, has the eigenframe ratio

\tan(2\Theta_{H})=\frac{2C_{H}(\alpha,t,\rho)}{P_{H}(t,\rho)},

(80)

where

C_{H}(\alpha,t,\rho)=\sinh\alpha\,J_{1}(\rho/\lambda)\sin(t/\lambda)

(81)

and

P_{H}(t,\rho)=D_{H}(\rho)\cos(t/\lambda),\qquad D_{H}(\rho)=2J_{0}(\rho/\lambda)-\frac{\lambda}{\rho}J_{1}(\rho/\lambda).

(82)

The effective residual coefficient
\chi_{H}
is defined by requiring that the lunar residual tensor inherit the same eigenframe rotation ratio as the Halilsoy tidal block:

\frac{2\chi_{H}}{3}=\frac{2C_{H}}{P_{H}}.

(83)

This gives

\boxed{\chi_{H}(\alpha,t,\rho)=3\frac{C_{H}(\alpha,t,\rho)}{P_{H}(t,\rho)}.}

(84)

Substituting Eqs. (
81
) and (
82
), we obtain

\boxed{\chi_{H}(\alpha,t,\rho)=3\frac{\sinh\alpha\,J_{1}(\rho/\lambda)\sin(t/\lambda)}{D_{H}(\rho)\cos(t/\lambda)}.}

(85)

Equivalently, in fully expanded Bessel form,

\boxed{\chi_{H}(\alpha,t,\rho)=3\frac{\sinh\alpha\,J_{1}(\rho/\lambda)\sin(t/\lambda)}{\left[2J_{0}(\rho/\lambda)-\dfrac{\lambda}{\rho}J_{1}(\rho/\lambda)\right]\cos(t/\lambda)}.}

(86)

Equation (
86
) is the central bridge formula of the paper.
It translates the off-diagonal tidal sector of the Halilsoy weak-field block into a dimensionless residual coefficient for the local lunar tidal tensor.
The coefficient
\chi_{H}
is not introduced as a free angular fitting parameter.
It is fixed by matching the eigenframe rotation ratio of two symmetric tidal operators: the Halilsoy transverse block and the effective lunar residual tensor.

IV.1
Compact form and small-
\alpha
limit

When
\cos(t/\lambda)\neq 0
and
D_{H}(\rho)\neq 0
, Eq. (
85
) can be written as

1.

IV.2
Zeros, enhancement regions, and regularized angle

The coefficient

(92)

These are cross-dominant regions.

They indicate that the ratio
\chi_{H}=C_{H}/P_{H}
is not the best variable near a plus-sector zero.
Near such regions the eigenframe angle itself is the better diagnostic.

For that reason, the regularized angle form should be retained:

\boxed{\Theta_{H}(\alpha,t,\rho)=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{atan2}\left(N_{H}(\alpha,t,\rho),D_{H}(\rho)\cos(t/\lambda)\right),}

(94)

\boxed{\Theta_{H}=\frac{1}{2}\arctan\left(\frac{2\chi_{H}}{3}\right).}

VII
Discussion

The central result of this work is not a new leading-order theory of lunar tides.
The dominant Earth–Moon tide is already accurately described by the Newtonian quadrupolar tensor.
Rather, the present work identifies the tensorial and harmonic form of a possible off-diagonal residual channel after the ordinary diagonal tide has been modeled.
The distinction is important: a Newtonian tidal tensor can be projected along
45^{\circ}
, but this is not the same as an independent
45^{\circ}
-type cross residual.
The proposed residual is defined by the appearance of a sine-quadrature term,

a_{\rm cross}^{H}(\beta)=a_{0}\chi_{H}(\alpha,t,\rho)\sin(2\beta),

(138)

which is absent from the diagonal Newtonian principal-frame tensor.

VII.1
What is new

The new element is the construction of a dimensionless residual coefficient

\chi_{H}(\alpha,t,\rho)=3\frac{\sinh\alpha\,J_{1}(\rho/\lambda)\sin(t/\lambda)}{D_{H}(\rho)\cos(t/\lambda)},

(139)

obtained by matching the eigenframe rotation ratio of the weak-field Halilsoy tidal block to that of an effective off-diagonal lunar residual tensor.
This coefficient is then inserted into

\mathcal{E}_{M,H}=\kappa_{M}\begin{pmatrix}2&\chi_{H}\\
\chi_{H}&-1\end{pmatrix}.

(140)

The resulting tensor has three linked signatures:

\boxed{\begin{aligned} \chi_{H}&\Longrightarrow\Theta_{M,H}=\frac{1}{2}\arctan\left(\frac{2\chi_{H}}{3}\right),\\
\Theta_{M,H}&\Longrightarrow a_{\rm cross}^{H}(\beta)=a_{0}\chi_{H}\sin(2\beta).\end{aligned}}

(141)

Thus the proposed residual sector appears simultaneously as an off-diagonal tensor component, a rotated eigenframe, and a sine-quadrature acceleration channel.
This is the main mathematical content of the model.
The Halilsoy solution supplies the relativistic cross-to-plus tidal ratio; the local lunar tensor supplies the physical Newtonian tidal scale.

The phrase “beyond Newtonian” should therefore be understood in a restricted and precise sense.
The model does not go beyond Newtonian gravity by replacing the dominant lunar tide.
It goes beyond the diagonal Newtonian principal-frame tensor by adding a possible off-diagonal residual sector whose angular harmonic is orthogonal to the ordinary plus-type channel:

\boxed{\begin{aligned} \text{Newtonian plus channel}&:\quad\cos(2\beta),\\
\text{cross-residual channel}&:\quad\sin(2\beta).\end{aligned}}

(142)

This formulation separates the proposed off-diagonal residual from ordinary amplitude corrections to the Newtonian plus channel.

VII.2
Relation to the original
45^{\circ}
tidal idea

The motivation for the present construction is the idea that gravitational cross-polarization should generate a
45^{\circ}
-type tidal effect.
In the language of gravitational waves, plus and cross polarizations are related by a
45^{\circ}
rotation in the transverse plane.
In tidal-tensor language, this means that an off-diagonal sector rotates the local eigenframe and produces extrema in the sine-quadrature channel.

However, the statement must be sharpened.
It is not correct to say that Newtonian gravity has no acceleration at
45^{\circ}
.
The diagonal Newtonian tidal tensor has a perfectly well-defined projection at every angle, including
45^{\circ}
:

a_{N}(45^{\circ})=\frac{1}{2}a_{0}.

(143)

What is absent in the Newtonian principal-frame tensor is an independent off-diagonal residual coefficient multiplying
\sin(2\beta)
.
Therefore the proper residual statement is

\boxed{\begin{gathered}45^{\circ}\text{ projection: Newtonian},\\
45^{\circ}\text{-type residual: }\sin(2\beta).\end{gathered}}

(144)

This refinement is essential for making the proposal physically defensible.
The observable target is not simply “a tide at
45^{\circ}
.”
The target is a residual sine-quadrature amplitude
A_{s}
after the standard modeled tide and environmental corrections have been removed:

a_{\rm residual}(\beta)=A_{c}\cos(2\beta)+A_{s}\sin(2\beta).

(145)

The model predicts that an off-diagonal residual should contribute to
A_{s}
, with

A_{s}=a_{0}\chi_{H}.

(146)

VII.3
Physical interpretation of the Halilsoy-inspired coefficient

The Halilsoy weak-field sector is used as a relativistic proof of principle for off-diagonal tidal structure.
Its transverse tidal block contains a cross term proportional to

(147)

and an effective plus-sector contrast proportional to

The effective lunar coefficient

In the small-
\alpha

\chi_{H}\simeq 3\alpha\frac{J_{1}(\rho/\lambda)}{D_{H}(\rho)}\tan(t/\lambda).

(149)

Thus the proposed cross-residual amplitude is linear in the Halilsoy polarization parameter at leading order.
This gives

The construction should not be read as a claim that the Earth–Moon system is literally a Halilsoy cylindrical wave spacetime.
The Earth–Moon field is generated by a localized orbital source, whereas the Halilsoy solution belongs to the class of exact cylindrical gravitational waves.
The connection is therefore tensorial, not global.
Both systems contain local symmetric tidal operators, and the Halilsoy operator provides an explicit relativistic example of how an off-diagonal sector rotates a tidal eigenframe.

A discovery-level signal would not be a generic mismatch in the tidal record, but a robust nonzero sine-quadrature coefficient
A_{s}
that persists after the removal of Newtonian lunar and solar tides, solid-Earth response, ocean loading, atmospheric and hydrological effects, instrumental drift, local site effects, and reference-frame systematics.
Such a residual would correspond to an effective off-diagonal tidal coefficient

\chi_{H}=\frac{A_{s}}{a_{0}},

(156)

and would provide the first empirical target for testing whether an off-diagonal lunar tidal channel exists.

VII.5
Back-action and dynamical closure

The residual construction also identifies the natural back-action problem associated with an off-diagonal tidal sector.
In the present work, the effective tensor is prescribed at the level of a local tidal operator:

\mathcal{E}_{M,H}=\mathcal{E}_{N}+\mathcal{E}_{\rm cross}^{H},\qquad\mathcal{E}_{\rm cross}^{H}=\kappa_{M}\begin{pmatrix}0&\chi_{H}\\
\chi_{H}&0\end{pmatrix}.

(157)

This is a test-residual approximation.
The off-diagonal sector is allowed to rotate the local eigenframe and generate a sine-quadrature acceleration channel, but it is not inserted back into a self-consistent solution of the field equations or into a full Earth–Moon dynamical model.

This does not mean that back-action is physically absent.
It means that the present work neglects its dynamical effect at the current level of approximation.
A complete treatment would have to determine how the residual sector modifies the background curvature, perturbs orbital motion, and propagates through the terrestrial tidal response.
Schematically, the missing closure problem is

\mathcal{E}_{\rm cross}^{H}\longrightarrow\delta g_{\mu\nu}\longrightarrow\delta R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\longrightarrow\delta\mathcal{O}_{\rm EM}\longrightarrow\delta a_{\rm obs},

(158)

where
\delta g_{\mu\nu}
denotes the induced metric perturbation,
\delta R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
the associated curvature correction,
\delta\mathcal{O}_{\rm EM}
a perturbation of the Earth–Moon orbital and tidal response system, and
\delta a_{\rm obs}
the final observable residual acceleration.

There are at least three distinct back-action channels.
The first is gravitational: an off-diagonal tidal sector may correspond to a curvature perturbation or, in a wave setting, to an effective stress-energy contribution of gravitational radiation
(
8
;
9
;
10
)
.
The second is orbital: a persistent or phase-correlated cross-tidal component would have to be incorporated into a consistent perturbative treatment of the Earth–Moon dynamics
(
17
)
.
The third is geophysical: even a small local residual acceleration would be filtered by the elastic Earth, oceans, atmosphere, hydrological loading, and measurement apparatus before appearing in observed data.

The present paper does not solve this dynamical closure problem.
Its role is more basic: it identifies the residual operator, its angular harmonic, and its acceleration scale.
The back-action problem is therefore not excluded from the physics.
It is deferred as the next level of analysis.
In this sense,
\chi_{H}
should be interpreted as a first-order test parameter.
A future self-consistent theory would determine whether
\chi_{H}
remains externally prescribed, becomes dynamically sourced, or is constrained by the coupled gravitational–orbital–geophysical response.

VII.6
Limitations

Several limitations should be stated explicitly.
First, the model is local and two-dimensional.
It uses a selected section spanned by the Earth–Moon direction and one transverse direction.
A complete terrestrial model would require a full three-dimensional tensor field on the rotating Earth, including latitude, longitude, lunar declination, Earth rotation, and station-dependent projection effects.

Second, the Halilsoy-inspired coefficient is not derived from a matched Earth–Moon spacetime.
It is obtained by transferring a local tidal ratio from an exact relativistic wave solution into a lunar residual tensor.
This is defensible as a residual ansatz, but it is not a global solution-matching procedure.
This limitation is important: the construction is based on a local tensorial analogy, not on a self-consistent Earth–Moon spacetime solution.

Third, the present work does not use observational data.
It does not claim that a nonzero
A_{s}
has been detected.
It provides the harmonic form and scale conversion needed for such a search:

A_{s}=a_{0}\chi_{H}.

(159)

The observational question remains open.

Fourth, environmental and geophysical systematics are likely to be severe at the relevant scales.
Sub-
\mu{\rm Gal}
signals can be contaminated by ocean loading, atmospheric pressure, hydrological effects, local geological structure, instrument drift, and reference-frame errors.
Therefore a future observational analysis must be designed as a residual-harmonic search, not as a simple comparison of measured tides at
45^{\circ}
.

Despite these limitations, the framework has a clear value.
It converts a qualitative cross-polarization idea into a specific tensorial residual model.
It separates the ordinary Newtonian plus channel from the off-diagonal sine-quadrature channel.
It provides the coefficient
\chi_{H}(\alpha,t,\rho)
, the eigenframe rotation
\Theta_{M,H}
, the residual acceleration
a_{\rm cross}^{H}
, and the scale
\Delta a_{45}^{H}
.
These are the ingredients needed before either an observational residual search or a dynamical back-action closure can be attempted.

VIII
Conclusion

We have formulated a sine-quadrature residual test for off-diagonal lunar tidal geometry.
The starting point is the standard Newtonian quadrupolar tidal tensor,

\mathcal{E}_{N}=\frac{GM_{M}}{D^{3}}\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix},

(160)

which accurately describes the dominant Earth–Moon tide in the local principal frame.
Its projected acceleration contains the plus-type harmonic

a_{N}(\beta)=a_{0}\left[\frac{1}{2}+\frac{3}{2}\cos(2\beta)\right],\qquad a_{0}=\frac{GM_{M}R_{E}}{D^{3}}.

(161)

A projection of this field at
45^{\circ}
is possible, but it is not an independent cross-tidal residual.

The central observation of the paper is that an off-diagonal residual sector appears in the sine-quadrature channel:

a_{\rm cross}^{H}(\beta)=a_{0}\chi_{H}(\alpha,t,\rho)\sin(2\beta).

(162)

This channel is absent from the diagonal Newtonian principal-frame tensor.
Its extrema occur along the
45^{\circ}
-type directions,

45^{\circ},\ 135^{\circ},\ 225^{\circ},\ 315^{\circ}.

(163)

Thus the proposed residual is not defined by the ordinary Newtonian value at
45^{\circ}
.
It is defined by a distinct sine-quadrature harmonic.

To motivate the form of the off-diagonal coefficient, we used the weak-field tidal sector of Halilsoy’s cross-polarized cylindrical gravitational waves.
The Halilsoy transverse tidal block contains a cross component proportional to

\sinh\alpha\,J_{1}(\rho/\lambda)\sin(t/\lambda),

(164)

and an effective plus-sector contrast proportional to

D_{H}(\rho)\cos(t/\lambda),

(165)

where

D_{H}(\rho)=2J_{0}(\rho/\lambda)-\frac{\lambda}{\rho}J_{1}(\rho/\lambda).

(166)

Matching the Halilsoy eigenframe rotation ratio to that of an effective lunar residual tensor gives the alpha-dependent coefficient

\boxed{\chi_{H}(\alpha,t,\rho)=3\frac{\sinh\alpha\,J_{1}(\rho/\lambda)\sin(t/\lambda)}{D_{H}(\rho)\cos(t/\lambda)}.}

(167)

The corresponding effective lunar residual tensor is

\boxed{\mathcal{E}_{M,H}=\frac{GM_{M}}{D^{3}}\begin{pmatrix}2&\chi_{H}(\alpha,t,\rho)\\
\chi_{H}(\alpha,t,\rho)&-1\end{pmatrix}.}

(168)

This tensor remains symmetric.
Therefore the principal axes remain orthogonal.
The off-diagonal sector does not replace the ordinary
90^{\circ}
stretching–squeezing geometry.
It rotates the whole eigenframe by

\boxed{\Theta_{M,H}=\frac{1}{2}\arctan\left(\frac{2\chi_{H}}{3}\right).}

(169)

For small
|\chi_{H}|
, the rotation is perturbative:

\Theta_{M,H}\simeq\frac{\chi_{H}}{3}.

(170)

For
|\chi_{H}|\gg 1
, the eigenframe approaches the cross-dominant orientation

\Theta_{M,H}\rightarrow\pm\frac{\pi}{4}.

(171)

The
45^{\circ}
-type effect is therefore an eigenframe-orientation and residual-harmonic effect, not a loss of orthogonality.

The physical residual scale is

\boxed{\Delta a_{45}^{H}=a_{0}\chi_{H}(\alpha,t,\rho).}

(172)

Using the mean Earth–Moon scale,

\boxed{\begin{aligned} \Delta a_{45}^{H}&\simeq 5.5\times 10^{-7}\chi_{H}\ {\rm m\,s^{-2}}\\
&=55\,\chi_{H}\,\mu{\rm Gal}.\end{aligned}}

(173)

Thus
|\chi_{H}|=10^{-2}
corresponds to a residual scale of approximately
0.55\,\mu{\rm Gal}
, while
|\chi_{H}|=10^{-3}
corresponds to approximately
0.055\,\mu{\rm Gal}
.
These are target scales for residual analysis, not detections.

The observational bridge is the residual decomposition

a_{\rm residual}(\beta)=A_{c}\cos(2\beta)+A_{s}\sin(2\beta).

(174)

The proposed off-diagonal sector contributes to the sine-quadrature coefficient,

\boxed{A_{s}=a_{0}\chi_{H}.}

(175)

Hence a measurement or upper bound on
A_{s}
gives

\boxed{\chi_{H}=\frac{A_{s}}{a_{0}}.}

(176)

This is the operational test supplied by the model.
The strongest possible empirical outcome would be the detection of a statistically robust nonzero
A_{s}
after all known Newtonian, geophysical, instrumental, and reference-frame contributions have been removed.
In that case, the residual would not merely indicate a modeling mismatch; it would define an effective off-diagonal tidal coefficient through
\chi_{H}=A_{s}/a_{0}
.
Absent such a residual, the same framework gives an upper bound on
\chi_{H}
.

The construction does not replace standard lunar tidal theory and does not model the Earth–Moon system as a Halilsoy spacetime.
Newtonian gravity explains the dominant diagonal lunar tide.
The Halilsoy-inspired sector supplies a mathematically explicit residual ansatz for an off-diagonal cross channel.
The appropriate compact interpretation is

\boxed{\mathcal{E}_{M,H}=\mathcal{E}_{N}+\mathcal{E}_{\rm cross}^{H}.}

(177)

The first term is the standard diagonal Newtonian tide.
The second term is the proposed alpha-dependent off-diagonal residual.

The work also identifies the natural back-action problem.
At present,
\mathcal{E}_{\rm cross}^{H}
is treated as a prescribed test-residual operator.
A complete dynamical extension would have to determine whether this sector can be coupled self-consistently to the background curvature, Earth–Moon orbital motion, and terrestrial tidal response.
That future problem can be summarized schematically as

\mathcal{E}_{\rm cross}^{H}\rightarrow\delta g_{\mu\nu}\rightarrow\delta R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\rightarrow\delta\mathcal{O}_{\rm EM}\rightarrow\delta a_{\rm obs}.

(178)

Thus the present paper supplies the kinematic residual structure; the dynamical back-action closure remains open.

The main contribution is therefore the explicit construction of the linked residual forms

\boxed{\begin{gathered}\chi_{H}(\alpha,t,\rho),\qquad\Theta_{M,H}(\alpha,t,\rho),\\
a_{\rm cross}^{H}(\beta;\alpha,t,\rho),\qquad\Delta a_{45}^{H}(\alpha,t,\rho).\end{gathered}}

(179)

Together they define a mathematically explicit and observationally constrainable framework for a possible
45^{\circ}
-type off-diagonal lunar tidal residual.

Future work should proceed in two directions.
First, the residual test should be applied to high-precision tidal or gravimetric data after subtracting standard lunar, solar, solid-Earth, ocean-loading, atmospheric, hydrological, instrumental, and reference-frame effects.
Second, the corresponding back-action problem should be developed as a coupled gravitational–orbital–geophysical perturbation problem.
Until such analyses are performed, the present model should be understood as a residual framework and not as a detection claim.
