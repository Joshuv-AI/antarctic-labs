Kalman-aided uplink channel estimation for time-varying multi-user vehicular STAR-RIS systems
Authors: Aswiniya Ambigapathy, Sriharipriya KC
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Accurate uplink channel estimation in high-mobility vehicular networks remains challenging due to rapid channel variations and Doppler effects, especially in systems assisted by simultaneously transmitting and reflecting reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (STAR-RIS). Most existing studies focus on limited user scenarios and static or low-mobility conditions, which restricts their applicability to realistic vehicular environments. This work proposes a Kalman-aided channel estimation framework for multi-user uplink vehicular STAR-RIS systems operating under high mobility. The proposed approach integrates least squares (LS) estimation with discrete Fourier transform (DFT)-based orthogonal pilot design to obtain initial channel estimates, followed by a Kalman filter to continuously track time-varying Rician fading channels modeled with Jakes temporal correlation. Path-loss scaling is incorporated to improve composite channel tracking accuracy. The framework effectively mitigates Doppler-induced estimation degradation and supports scalable multi-user operation. Simulation results show that the proposed estimator consistently outperforms conventional methods in high-mobility scenarios. In particular, the normalized mean square error is reduced by up to 25 dB for the time-switching protocol and approximately 22 dB for the energy-splitting protocol compared with the corresponding baseline estimation schemes. These results indicate that the proposed estimation and tracking framework maintains stable accuracy across a wide range of vehicular operating conditions.

Introduction

The growth of vehicle-to-everything (V2X) communications aims to reduce accidents and make everyday travel safer and more convenient. The evolution of sixth-generation (6G) wireless networks and intelligent transportation systems (ITS) has significantly increased the demand for ultra-reliable, high-throughput, and low-latency communication in vehicular environments. These systems form the backbone of emerging applications such as autonomous driving, real-time traffic control, and cooperative vehicular safety. However, vehicular communication links are severely degraded by complicated propagation environments, particularly due to building blockages in metropolitan areas and rapidly time-varying channels caused by high vehicle mobility1. Reconfigurable Intelligent Surfaces (RIS), also referred to as Intelligent Reflecting Surfaces (IRS), have recently emerged as a promising technology to enhance wireless coverage, spectral efficiency, and energy efficiency2. By intelligently adjusting signal propagation using a large number of low-cost passive elements, RIS enables the creation of a reconfigurable radio environment. However, conventional RIS architectures can only reflect signals within a single half-space, thereby serving users located on one side of the surface only3. To overcome this limitation, a brand-new idea called Simultaneously Transmitting and Reflecting Reconfigurable Intelligent Surfaces (STAR-RIS) has been introduced4. It can transmit and reflect the incident signals, allowing users on both sides of the RIS to achieve a full-space smart radio environment. The benefits of implementing STAR-RIS in automotive networks, such as increased system throughput, reduced power consumption, and extended network coverage, have sparked growing research interest5. To enable full-space signal manipulation, STAR-RIS operates under three practical protocols, namely energy splitting (ES), mode switching (MS), and time switching (TS), each offering distinct trade-offs between design flexibility, implementation complexity, and transmission-reflection control: ES allows simultaneous transmission and reflection with fine-grained amplitude and phase tuning; MS partitions elements into either transmission or reflection mode for simpler hardware; and TS alternates all elements between transmission and reflection over time slots, enabling independent optimization at the cost of synchronization overhead4.

In practical wireless systems, hardware imperfections are inevitable and may noticeably influence system performance, particularly in high-mobility and high-frequency scenarios. Typical impairments include I/Q imbalance, limited phase resolution, and mutual coupling among antenna and surface elements. In reconfigurable intelligent surface systems, such non-idealities lead to deviations between the designed and actual phase and amplitude responses, resulting in configuration mismatches. Previous studies have reported that hardware impairments at both the transceiver and RIS sides can degrade beamforming performance, channel estimation accuracy, and achievable data rates, especially when a large number of surface elements is employed6–8. These effects become more pronounced in vehicular environments, where Doppler shifts and rapid channel variations further aggravate the impact of hardware non-idealities. For this reason, incorporating hardware impairments is necessary to develop realistic and reliable STAR-RIS-assisted channel estimation frameworks for high-mobility vehicular communication systems.

The fundamental protocols of STAR-RIS can be efficiently integrated with several emerging technologies. For example, combining STAR-RIS with MIMO or massive MIMO systems improves spatial multiplexing and beamforming performance, which supports reliable multi-user communication in high-mobility vehicular scenarios5. The use of millimeter-wave (mmWave) or terahertz (THz) communications helps overcome line-of-sight blockages that commonly occur in urban vehicular environments, thereby extending coverage and improving link reliability9. Combining STAR-RIS with non-orthogonal multiple access (NOMA) can improve spectral efficiency and allow multiple vehicles to be served simultaneously in dense traffic conditions10. In addition, STAR-RIS has been investigated within integrated sensing and communication (ISAC) frameworks, where vehicular nodes carry out radar sensing and data transmission in parallel5,10. These works indicate that STAR-RIS can support joint communication sensing operations and improve coverage and link quality in next-generation V2X systems.

Although STAR-RIS has demonstrated promising gains in coverage and throughput, most existing works still examine it under static or slowly changing conditions. Its performance and effectiveness in fast vehicular environments have not yet been sufficiently examined. In particular, the challenges related to time-varying channel estimation, multi-user interference, and protocol optimization between the TS and ES modes under Doppler effects remain largely unaddressed. To address these gaps, this paper investigates a STAR-RIS-assisted multi-user vehicular communication system and develops a channel estimation framework based on Least Squares (LS), discrete Fourier transform-based orthogonal pilot design, and Kalman filtering. Both the TS and ES protocols are considered under Rician fading channels with Jakes temporal correlation. Closed-form LS estimators and normalized mean square error (NMSE) expressions are derived, and a Kalman filter is employed to track the temporal variation of the composite direct and cascaded channels. The performance of the proposed framework is evaluated in terms of NMSE and achievable channel capacity under different system parameters, including pilot length, transmit power, and vehicle speed. The main objective of this work is to improve channel estimation accuracy for STAR-RIS-assisted vehicular communication systems operating in realistic high-mobility environments. This study provides a practically implementable channel estimation framework for STAR-RIS-assisted vehicular communication systems under high mobility, with explicit computational complexity and pilot overhead considerations.

We note that this work considers a narrowband channel model, primarily to keep the analysis tractable and focus on the core estimation and tracking problem in STAR-RIS-assisted vehicular systems. We recognize that real-world vehicular standards like 5G NR-V2X use wideband OFDM, where the channel must be handled across multiple subcarriers. The proposed LS-Kalman framework could potentially be applied to such systems by running the estimator on each subcarrier separately, though this would naturally increase the computational load depending on the number of subcarriers involved. How to handle this efficiently in resource-limited vehicular platforms is a practical challenge that we leave open for future work.

Motivated by the above observations, this paper investigates uplink channel estimation for STAR-RIS-assisted multi-user vehicular communication systems under realistic high-mobility conditions. The main contributions of this work are summarized as follows. First, we develop a unified LS-Kalman channel estimation framework for both TS and ES protocols by combining DFT-based orthogonal pilot design with Kalman filtering to track time-varying Rician channels modeled using Jakes temporal correlation. Second, we extend existing STAR-RIS channel estimation studies from static or two-user scenarios to a general multi-user uplink vehicular setting and analyze the impact of the number of users, vehicle speed, and pilot length on estimation accuracy. Third, closed-form NMSE expressions are derived for both TS and ES protocols, and the achievable uplink channel capacity under imperfect CSI is evaluated by explicitly incorporating the residual estimation error. Extensive simulation results show that the proposed framework performs well under Doppler effects and multi-user interference, and that the TS protocol provides lower NMSE and higher spectral efficiency than the ES protocol in high-mobility vehicular scenarios.

Related work

This section reviews the related literature on STAR-RIS in vehicular and high-mobility communications scenarios. In5, Li et al. explored STAR-RIS-aided Integrated Sensing and Communication (ISAC) for high-mobility vehicles, demonstrating channel parameter extraction and vehicle localization using multidimensional orthogonal matching pursuit (MOMP) algorithms. In11, Li et al. proposed an energy-harvesting relay-assisted STAR-RIS-enhanced vehicular NOMA network. The authors derive outage probabilities for near and far vehicles using Gamma-distributed channel approximations and demonstrate that adjusting power allocation and STAR-RIS coefficients under the energy splitting (ES) protocol improves fairness and reliability without increasing surface complexity. In12, Li et al. deployed STAR-RIS in a high-mobility ISAC framework to simultaneously serve indoor users and track outdoor dynamic scatterers in the environment. A GM-PHD filter is used for multi-target tracking, and beam prediction strategies are developed to enhance communication performance, showcasing STAR-RIS potential for mobility-aware sensing and beamforming.

In13, Wu et al. investigated channel estimation schemes for STAR-RIS under the two fundamental protocols: Time Switching (TS) and Energy Splitting (ES). Results demonstrated that TS yields lower estimation error compared to ES due to the absence of power leakage, and they proposed a near-optimal training pattern design under practical phase-shift constraints. However, their analysis was restricted to generic wireless scenarios, without considering the challenges of vehicular communication where high mobility, Doppler effects, and multi-user interference significantly complicate CSI acquisition. Different from the STAR-RIS channel estimation method in13, which considers a generic static or slowly varying scenario and mainly focuses on training pattern optimization under practical phase-shift constraints, this work explicitly addresses high-mobility vehicular environments with time-varying Rician fading and Jakes temporal correlation. In addition, while13 mainly investigates two-user setups without temporal tracking, the proposed framework extends the analysis to a general multi-user uplink scenario and incorporates a Kalman filter to continuously track Doppler-induced channel variations. Furthermore, this paper provides a unified LS-Kalman estimation framework for both TS and ES protocols and evaluates their performance in terms of NMSE and achievable channel capacity under realistic vehicular mobility, which is not considered in13.

In14, Aung et al. optimized a STAR-RIS-assisted V2X using a deep reinforcement learning (DRL) framework. The authors jointly optimize spectrum allocation, STAR-RIS configuration, and beamforming to maximize V2I data rates while satisfying V2V latency and reliability constraints. Their DDQN-attention based solution demonstrates STAR-RIS’s adaptability in complicated vehicular networks by outperforming traditional RIS and vanilla DRL techniques. In15, Shang et al. proposed an AGSM-assisted STAR-RIS scheme that integrates generalized spatial modulation (GSM) with Alamouti space-time block coding (STBC). In the first design, STAR-RIS is partitioned into reflective and transmissive blocks, to enable simultaneous multi-user communication, thereby improving BER performance. In a second design, STAR-RIS itself functions as an access point (AP), which simplifies hardware implementation and enhances spectral efficiency. Likewise, in16, Ghadi et al. evaluated a dual RIS/STAR-IOS-assisted Vehicle-to-Vehicle NOMA/OMA system under composite Fisher-Snedecor F fading and demonstrated that STAR-IOS greatly enhances performance, primarily under NOMA schemes, by obtaining closed-form formulas for outage probability, ergodic capacity, underscoring its promise for vehicular communication.

Although the above works demonstrate the potential of STAR-RIS in vehicular, ISAC, NOMA, and multi-user communication scenarios, most of them focus on system-level performance optimization, sensing-communication integration, or outage and capacity analysis under quasi-static or slowly varying channels. Moreover, the majority of existing studies either consider single-user or two-user setups, or do not explicitly address the challenges of channel estimation in high-mobility vehicular environments. In particular, the joint impact of Doppler effects, Jakes temporal correlation, and multi-user interference of STAR-RIS-assisted uplink channel estimation under both TS and ES protocols remains largely unexplored. System model

As shown in Fig. 1, we consider a narrow-band wireless channel in a vehicular communication scenario, where a STAR-RIS with N passive elements is installed to assist uplink data transmission from K single-antenna vehicular users to a base station (BS) equipped with m antennas. To minimize the channel estimation cost, we recall the element-grouping technique in17,18, which divides the STAR-RIS elements into M sub-surfaces (M < < N), each consisting of N/M neighboring elements that share a common transmission/reflection coefficient. This grouping strategy reduces the channel estimation overhead while preserving adequate beamforming flexibility. The Jakes temporal correlation model is employed to describe the time variation of the wireless channels caused by vehicular motion. To account for both line-of-sight (LOS) and non-line-of-sight (NLOS) components, the complex channel amplitudes are modeled according to a Rician fading distribution, while their temporal variations are governed by the Jakes temporal correlation model.

STAR-RIS channel estimation under energy splitting for multi-user vehicular communication

In this part, we introduce the channel estimation scheme for the ES protocol. Under ES, every STAR-RIS element concurrently forwards part of the incident energy to the transmission side and part to the reflection side; all K vehicular users transmit pilots simultaneously, and the BS (with m antennas) receives a superposition of direct and cascaded signals. We present the per-slot signal model, the stacked LS estimator, the MSE/error covariance, and the Kalman filter used to track Jakes time variations.

Kalman filter for time-varying (Jakes) channels

To track the Jakes time variations across pilot frames, the channel dynamics and measurements are cast into a state-space form and processed using a Kalman filter.

State model (common for TS and ES)

For the composite channel vector \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{x}_{k}\left(\mathrm{t}\right)$$\end{document} of user k at discrete pilot frame index t, we use a first-order auto-regressive approximation consistent with the Jakes temporal correlation:34\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{x}_{k}\left(\mathrm{t}+1\right)=\:{F}_{k}{x}_{k}\left(\mathrm{t}\right)+\:{w}_{k}\left(\mathrm{t}\right)$$\end{document}

Where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{F}_{k}=\:{\alpha\:}_{k}{I}_{m+M}$$\end{document} with \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\:{\alpha\:}_{k}={J}_{0}\left(2\pi\:{f}_{D,k}\varDelta\:t\right)$$\end{document}, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{w}_{k}\left(t\right)\sim\:CN(0,{Q}_{k})$$\end{document} is the process noise, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{f}_{D,k}$$\end{document} is the Doppler frequency associated with user k and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\varDelta\:t$$\end{document} is the interval between pilot frames.

It should be noted that the state transition matrix \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{F}_{k}$$\end{document} is constructed using Jakes temporal correlation, which treats the channel statistics as stationary over consecutive pilot frames. In practice, as vehicles move along their paths, the geometry of the scattering environment changes, causing the AoA and AoD to shift gradually over time. For short to moderate observation windows, this stationarity assumption holds reasonably well, as the angular changes between consecutive frames are small relative to the Doppler-induced temporal variations. However, in prolonged high-speed trajectories, the gradual evolution of spatial angles could eventually cause the Kalman tracker to diverge if the state model is not updated. Incorporating geometry-based AoA/AoD tracking into the state transition model is an interesting extension that we leave for future work.

In summary, the proposed STAR-RIS-assisted framework employs both TS and ES protocols for uplink channel estimation in multi-user vehicular systems. Under TS, the STAR-RIS alternates between transmission and reflection modes, whereas under ES each sub-surface simultaneously forwards energy to both sides. In both cases, the frame-wise LS estimator provides unbiased measurements of the composite channels, and the Kalman filter exploits the Jakes temporal correlation to track their evolution across pilot frames. This two-stage LS-Kalman approach enables accurate tracking of fast time-varying channels in STAR-RIS-aided multi-user vehicular environments.

Overall computational complexity and practical feasibility

By combining LS estimation and Kalman-based channel tracking, the total computational complexity per frame of the proposed framework scales as53\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:O\left(K(m+M)^3\right)$$\end{document}

Compared to conventional LS-only channel estimation, the proposed LS-Kalman framework introduces additional computational overhead due to the Kalman filtering stage. This additional overhead is justified by the significant NMSE reduction and the improved tracking capability for time-varying channels observed in the simulation results.

Furthermore, the element-grouping strategy (M < < N) significantly reduces the effective channel dimension, making the proposed approach computationally feasible for real-time implementation. Since channel estimation is performed at the BS, which typically has sufficient processing capability, the proposed framework remains scalable for moderate numbers of users and STAR-RIS sub-surfaces in practical vehicular communication systems.

Complexity comparison between TS and ES protocols

The LS and Kalman filtering operations under the TS and ES protocols share the same computational structure. The primary difference between the two protocols lies in the measurement noise covariance matrices due to power splitting in the ES protocol, which does not affect the asymptotic computational complexity. As a result, both TS and ES protocols exhibit identical computational complexity orders, while their performance differences stem from protocol-specific power allocation and signal coupling effects rather than computational cost.

Results and discussion

Simulation setup

In this portion, we present numerical results to verify the effectiveness of the suggested channel estimation schemes for both Time Switching (TS) and Energy Splitting (ES) protocols in a vehicular STAR-RIS-assisted communication system. The system consists of K = 4 single-antenna vehicular users as the baseline configuration, and additional simulations are conducted by varying the number of users from K = 2 to K = 16 to evaluate the impact of multi-user interference on channel estimation performance. A STAR-RIS is equipped with N=80 passive elements, which are grouped into M = 20 sub-surfaces, each sharing a common transmission and reflection coefficient. All wireless channels are modeled as Rician fading to incorporate both line-of-sight (LOS) and non-line-of-sight (NLOS) components. The Rician factor is set to 10 dB, which indicates a moderately strong LOS component. The NLOS temporal variation of each channel coefficient follows the Jakes temporal correlation, represented as a sum of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{N}_{s}=8$$\end{document} sinusoids with independent random phases. In this paper, these benchmark schemes are employed to provide a fair and consistent reference for performance comparison under the same training protocols and system assumptions. Different from13, which focuses on static or slowly varying scenarios with limited user configurations, this work extends these baseline estimators to a high-mobility multi-user uplink vehicular framework with time-varying Rician fading and Jakes temporal correlation, and integrates Kalman filtering to track Doppler-induced channel variations. In addition, this paper evaluates their impact not only on NMSE but also on achievable uplink channel capacity under realistic vehicular mobility, which is not investigated in13. It is worth noting that most existing compressed-sensing-based and learning-based channel estimation methods for RIS or STAR-RIS are developed for downlink transmission, static or slowly varying channels, or single-user scenarios, and typically rely on sparse angular-domain channel models and long coherence times. In high-mobility vehicular uplink systems with fast time variations and short coherence intervals, these approaches require prohibitive training overhead and frequent retraining, and are not directly applicable to the considered multi-user LS-Kalman tracking framework. Therefore, in this work we focus on representative training-based STAR-RIS baseline schemes under the TS and ES protocols to ensure a fair and practically meaningful comparison.

Where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\widehat{h}}_{\mathrm{k}}$$\end{document} is the estimated composite channel (direct and STAR-RIS-assisted), \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{P}_{k}$$\end{document} is the transmit power of user k, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\sigma\:}^{2}$$\end{document} denotes the noise variance. Both thermal noise and the residual estimation error power, which is directly related to the NMSE, are included in the denominator. Hence, more accurate channel estimation (lower NMSE) results in a higher effective SNR and improved system throughput.

Conclusion

In this work, we proposed efficient channel estimation schemes for a STAR-RIS-assisted multi-user vehicular communication system under the TS and ES protocols. Closed-form LS estimators and NMSE expressions were derived for each protocol, and a Kalman-based tracking model exploiting Jakes’ temporal correlation was incorporated to handle high-mobility channel variations. A practical and two-phase ES training design and a full-power TS training strategy were analyzed using the unified composite channel model. Numerical results demonstrated that TS achieves lower NMSE and higher uplink capacity than ES under the considered system settings, while ES performance depends strongly on the energy splitting ratio. The Kalman filter improves estimation quality in dynamic vehicular environments. Moreover, the proposed framework exhibits robustness against multi-user interference and maintains stable performance as the number of vehicular users increases. The computational complexity analysis confirms that the LS-Kalman framework remains feasible for practical implementation at the base station, even in moderate multi-user and high-mobility scenarios. By considering practical phase-shift imperfections, the proposed framework partially reflects hardware non-idealities in realistic STAR-RIS deployments. This work assumes perfect synchronization between the base station and STAR-RIS and considers narrowband channel estimation under fixed vehicular geometry. One limitation of the current Kalman model is that the state transition matrix relies on Jakes temporal correlation, which assumes the channel statistics remain roughly stable between frames. This works well when the vehicle travels over short distances between pilot transmissions, but over longer trajectories the AoA and AoD can shift noticeably as the surrounding scattering geometry changes. Handling this kind of slow geometric drift in the state model is something we plan to address in future work. Hardware impairments such as phase noise, mutual coupling, and synchronization errors are not fully modeled. In addition, the study focuses on uplink channel estimation and does not explicitly consider downlink estimation and beamforming optimization. The developed method therefore provides an effective channel estimation solution for STAR-RIS-enabled vehicular systems. Future work will investigate joint channel estimation and robust beamforming, as well as downlink training design for STAR-RIS-assisted vehicular networks under hardware impairments and imperfect synchronization. The sensitivity of the Kalman tracker to Doppler frequency mismatch is another practical concern, and joint Doppler estimation and channel tracking remains an open problem for highly dynamic scenarios, which we identify as a direction for future work.

Looking ahead, the Kalman-aided estimation approach developed here could also be relevant beyond terrestrial vehicular settings. LEO satellites move at very high speeds, which creates severe Doppler conditions that share some similarity with the high-mobility vehicular problem studied in this work. At the same time, STAR-RIS is well suited for satellite scenarios because it can serve ground users on both sides of the surface simultaneously, which is difficult to achieve with conventional RIS. That said, the scale of the problem is much larger - coordinating massive numbers of ground terminals and designing satellite constellations under heavy Doppler dispersion are challenges that go well beyond what is addressed here21,22. We see this as a worthwhile direction to pursue in future work.
