MicroRNA-425-5p as a diagnostic biomarker and ox-LDL-induced VSMC regulator in atherosclerosis
Authors: Jinguang Chen, Bin Lin, Zhenbin Zhan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundMicroRNAs (miRNAs) are key regulators of atherosclerotic (AS) development. This study aimed to evaluate miR-425-5p expression patterns and their clinical relevance in patients with AS.Methods131 patients with AS and 112 controls were enrolled. Serum miR-425-5p and Krüppel-like factor 7 (KLF7) levels were quantified using RT-qPCR. ROC analysis assessed the diagnostic value of miR-425-5p for AS, and logistic regression identified risk factors. An in vitro AS model was established using ox-LDL-stimulated HVSMC cells. Cell viability, apoptosis, inflammatory cytokine secretion, and migration were assessed by CCK-8, flow cytometry, ELISA, and Transwell assays, respectively. Bioinformatics was used to predict the downstream target genes of miR-425-5p, and their targeting bindings were verified by DLR and RIP assays.ResultsmiR-425-5p was significantly upregulated, while KLF7 was downregulated, in serum of AS patients and in ox-LDL-stimulated HVSMCs. Serum miR-425-5p was positively correlated with CIMT, TG, and LDL-C, and negatively correlated with HDL-C. It showed favorable diagnostic performance for AS (85.50% sensitivity, 85.71% specificity) and was identified as an independent risk factor for AS. Moreover, ox-LDL-induced HVSMC dysfunction, including reduced viability, increased apoptosis, elevated migration, and excessive inflammation, was attenuated by miR-425-5p inhibition and further attenuated by KLF7 knockdown.ConclusionsSerum miR-425-5p demonstrates diagnostic potential for AS and correlates with HVSMC proliferation, apoptosis, migration, and inflammatory responses.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13019-026-04307-2.

Introduction

Atherosclerosis (AS) is a chronic vascular disorder characterized by dysregulated lipid metabolism and oxidative stress, resulting in arterial plaque formation [1, 2]. AS often progresses asymptomatically for decades, but, when symptomatic, it becomes a major cause of mortality due to ischemic stroke, peripheral vascular disease, and related complications. AS pathogenesis is multifaceted, with vascular smooth muscle cell (VSMC) survival, migration, and proliferation playing key roles in stable plaque formation during disease progression [3]. Despite significant advancements in AS diagnosis and management over recent decades, its global incidence continues to rise. Thus, identifying reliable biomarkers and therapeutic targets remains critical for improving early intervention and treatment outcomes in AS patients.

MicroRNA (miRNAs) are short (19–24 nucleotide), non-coding, single-stranded RNAs that post-transcriptionally regulate gene expression. Their stable expression in peripheral blood, resistance to pH and temperature, and cost-effective, straightforward detection methods make miRNAs promising biomarkers for diverse diseases [4, 5]. For instance, lower expression of miR-637 serves as a biomarker for identifying patients with AS [6], while elevated miR-211-5p levels correlate with poor progression in AS [7]. miR-425, a member of the miRNA family, is predominantly situated on human chromosome 3p21.31. Prior research has elucidated its involvement in cardiovascular and cerebrovascular disorders. For instance, miR-425-5p shows marked upregulation in clinical samples of glioblastoma multiforme [8]. The depletion of miR-425-5p impedes the functions of vascular endothelial cells, including proliferation, migration, and tube formation. Conversely, its overexpression can counteract the anti - angiogenic effects induced by arsenic [9]. Moreover, miR-425-5p is a crucial miRNA in a predictive model for coronary artery atherosclerosis in patients with rheumatoid arthritis and can serve as a predictor of high coronary artery calcification [10]. Notably, miRNA microarray analysis has revealed that miR-425–5p is among the several miRNAs that are upregulated in severe atherosclerotic lesions [11]. However, its clinical significance and underlying mechanisms in AS remain to be elucidated.

Building on prior evidence, we hypothesized that miR-425-5p is aberrantly expressed in patients with AS and holds potential as a diagnostic biomarker. To test this, we enrolled asymptomatic AS patients, analyzed serum miR-425-5p expression patterns, and investigated its mechanistic role in AS using an in vitro model with oxidized low-density lipoprotein (ox-LDL)-treated cells. This study aims to address current knowledge gaps regarding miR-425-5p’s involvement in AS pathogenesis.

Materials and methods

Ethical approval

The study received approval from the First Hospital of Putian City Medical Ethics Committee, and written informed consent was obtained from all participants before enrollment. All procedures adhered strictly to the ethical principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki.

Participants

A total of 131 asymptomatic patients with atherosclerosis (AS) were enrolled at The First Hospital of Putian City from June 2021 to December 2023. Inclusion criteria required: (1) carotid ultrasound-confirmed plaque formation indicative of atherosclerosis, and (2) complete clinical documentation. Exclusion criteria included: prior cerebrovascular disease, myocardial infarction, percutaneous coronary intervention, uncontrolled malignant arrhythmias, severe hepatic/renal dysfunction, acute/chronic infections, malignancy, or pregnancy. An age- and sex-matched control group of 112 healthy individuals undergoing routine health screenings during the same period was also recruited. Carotid intima-media thickness (CIMT) was assessed using an ATL HDI 3000 ultrasound system.

Peripheral venous blood (5 mL) was collected from each participant. The sample was allowed to clot naturally at room temperature for 15 min, followed by centrifugation at 4,000 rpm for 15 min. The supernatant serum was then collected and stored at −80°C. An aliquot of the blood was simultaneously analyzed for routine clinical biochemical parameters using a Beckman LX20 automated biochemical analyzer.

Cell culture and construction of cell models

Human Vascular Smooth Muscle Cells (HVSMCs) were obtained from BeNa Culture Collection and cultured in DMEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum and incubated at 37°C in a humidified 5% CO2 incubator. To model AS, HVSMCs were exposed to varying concentrations of ox-LDL (0, 25, 50, 100 µg/mL) for 24 h. For subsequent experiments, a concentration of 100 µg/mL ox-LDL was selected based on prior studies [12].

Cell transfection

To investigate the roles of miR-425-5p and KLF7 in AS, their expression in HVSMCs was modulated using a miR-425-5p inhibitor and small interfering RNA (siRNA) targeting KLF7 (si-KLF7). HVSMCs were seeded into 6-well plates. A transfection mixture containing 50 nM miR-425-5p inhibitor or negative control (NC), together with si-KLF7 or its negative control (si-NC), using Lipofectamine 3000. The mixture was incubated at room temperature for 20 min, and then added to the cells. After 6 h, the medium was replaced with fresh DMEM, and the cells were further incubated to allow inhibitor uptake.

The oligonucleotide miR-425-5p mimic (5’-CTGCUAGAGGCAGCGCTGT-3’), miR-425-5p inhibitor (5’-AGGCGAAGGAUGACAAAGGGAA-3’), mimic NC (5’-ACUACUTGGUGACGCTGAGCA-3’), inhibitor NC (5’-UUCUCCGAACGUGUCACGUdTdT-3’),and si-NC (5’-UUCUCCGAACGUGUCACGU-3’), si-KLF7#1 (5’-GGGUUCUUCCAAAGAACAUdTdT-3’) and si-KLF7#2 (5’-GGGCGUACCATGGGAUUAUAUdTdT-3’) were obtained from GenePharm.

Cell viability assessment using CCK-8 assay

HVSMCs (1 × 104 cells/100 µL) treated with ox-LDL and transfected with oligonucleotides were seeded into 96-well plates. After 24 h, the culture medium was replaced with a 10% CCK-8 solution in DMEM (10 µl CCK-8 + 90 µl DMEM per well). Following a 1-hour incubation, absorbance at 450 nm was measured using a microplate reader.

Apoptosis assessment via flow cytometry

Apoptosis was assessed using the Annexin V-FITC/propidium iodide (PI) staining kit combined with flow cytometry. Ox-LDL-treated and transfected cells (1 × 105 cells) were rinsed with pre-chilled PBS, detached using EDTA-free trypsin, and resuspended in binding buffer. For analysis, 5 µl each of Annexin V-FITC and PI reagents were added, followed by a 5-minute incubation at room temperature in the dark, before flow cytometric detection of apoptosis rates.

Real-time quantitative reverse transcription PCR (RT-qPCR)

RNA was extracted from serum samples and cells using the miRNeasy kit, and its concentration and purity were assessed with the NanoDrop 2000. RNA was deemed pure when the A260/A280 ratio fell within the range of 1.8–2.0. Using the miRCURY LNA RT kit or iScript Select cDNA synthesis kit, 500 ng of RNA was reverse transcribed into complementary DNA (cDNA). The PCR amplification system was then prepared with 5 µl of cDNA, components from the miRCURY LNA SYBR Green PCR Kit (for miR-425-5p) or Power SYBR Green Master Mix kit (for KLF7), 0.4 µl each of forward and reverse primers, and sterile ultrapure water to reach a final volume of 25 µl. The PCR amplification system was loaded into an ABI Prism 7900HT Thermal Cycler for amplification. U6 was used as an internal reference for the normalization of miR-425-5p expression, and GAPDH was employed as the internal reference for DHAC4. Relative expression levels were determined using the 2−ΔΔCt method. The primer sequences are shown in Supplementary Table S1.

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

Supernatants were collected from ox-LDL-treated HVSMCs and HVSMCs transfected with a miR-425-5p inhibitor. Levels of IL-6 and IL-1β were quantified using commercial ELISA kits. Briefly, diluted supernatants and protein standards were added to antibody-coated ELISA plates and incubated at 37°C for 90 min. After washing, biotinylated detection antibodies were added and incubated for 1 h, followed by HRP-conjugated streptavidin for 30 min. Substrate solution (90 µL) was then added and incubated for 15 min. The reaction was stopped, and the absorbance was measured at 450 nm.

Cell migration analysis using the transwell assay

HVSMCs, pre-treated with ox-LDL and/or transfected, were serum-starved in 1% FBS for 12 h. Cells (3 × 10⁴/200 µL) were then seeded into the upper chamber of a Transwell insert (0.8 μm pore size), with DMEM containing 20% FBS added to the lower chamber as a chemoattractant. After 24 h, migrated cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde, stained with 0.25% crystal violet at room temperature for 20 min, and counted under a microscope. Relative migration was calculated by normalizing the control groups.

Dual luciferase reporter (DLR) assay

The bioinformatics tool miRmap was employed to predict the target genes of miR-425-5p, and KLF7 was identified as a potential target harboring a binding site for miR-425-5p. Subsequently, the KLF7 sequence containing this potential miR-425-5p binding site was inserted into the pmirGLO dual luciferase reporter vector to construct the wild-type KLF7 recombinant luciferase reporter plasmid (WT-KLF7). In contrast, the sequence with a mutated binding site was inserted to generate the mutant KLF7 plasmid (MUT-KLF7).

HVSMCs were seeded into 48-well plates, and the aforementioned recombinant luciferase reporter plasmids were co-transfected with mimics (negative control [NC] and miR-425-5p) into HVSMCs using Lipofectamine 3000 transfection reagent. After 48 h of transfection, the cells were lysed, and the luciferase activity of the dual-luciferase system was detected. Renilla luciferase was used as an internal reference to normalize the experimental results.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses and data visualization were conducted using GraphPad Prism 9.0 and SPSS 23.0. The normality of continuous data was evaluated using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. Normally distributed data were presented as mean ± standard deviation and compared between two groups using Student’s t-test. Non-normally distributed data were presented as median (interquartile range, P50 [p25-p75]) and analyzed using the Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical data were expressed as counts (n, %) and analyzed using the chi-square test. Pearson correlation analysis was used for normally distributed variables, while Spearman’s rank correlation was applied for non-normally distributed data to assess the relationship. For comparison among three or more groups, one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s multiple comparison test was used. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves analysis was used to evaluate the diagnostic performance of miR-425-5p for AS. Logistic regression was performed to identify independent risk factors for AS.

Results

Comparison of clinical baseline characteristics among subjects

Baseline characteristics of the two groups were compared (Table 1). Patients with AS exhibited significantly higher LDL-C, triglyceride, CRP levels and CIMT, along with lower HDL-C, compared to controls (P < 0.05). No significant differences were observed in age, gender, BMI, total cholesterol, systolic blood pressure (SBP), or diastolic blood pressure (DBP) between groups. These findings confirm comparable baseline profiles between the study cohorts.

Serum miR-425-5p elevation serves as a diagnostic biomarker for AS

Serum miR-425-5p levels were significantly elevated in AS patients compared to controls (Fig. 1A). Correlation analysis revealed inverse associations with HDL-C (r = −0.708) and positive associations with LDL-C (r = 0.804), triglycerides (r = 0.690), and CRP (r = 0.732; Table 2). Notably, miR-425-5p demonstrated a strong positive correlation with carotid intima-media thickness (CIMT; r = 0.716, Fig. 1B), a validated subclinical marker of AS. ROC analysis revealed that miR-425-5p exhibited a sensitivity of 85.50% and a specificity of 85.71% at the optimal cutoff value of 1.210, with an AUC of 0.917 (95% CI: 0.878–0.951) for differentiating AS patients from healthy controls, indicating its promising diagnostic value (Fig. 1C). As presented in Table 3, multivariate logistic regression analysis incorporating baseline characteristics and miR-425-5p revealed that miR-425-5p (OR = 9.157, 95%CI: 3.295–25.44, P < 0.001) and LDL-C (OR = 2.108, 95% CI: 1.029–4.318, P = 0.042) were independent risk factors for AS.

miR-425-5p expression is markedly upregulated in ox-LDL-treated HVSMCs

To model AS, HVSMCs were exposed to graded ox-LDL concentrations. After 24 h, miR-425-5p expression exhibited a dose-dependent increase with ox-LDL treatment (P < 0.001, Fig. 2A). Furthermore, ox-LDL also significantly reduced cell viability and enhanced apoptosis in HVSMCs (P < 0.001, Figs. 2B-C). Compared to controls, ox-LDL stimulation markedly promoted the secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines IL-6 and IL-1β in HVSMCs (P < 0.01, Fig. 2D).

miR-425-5p inhibition attenuates ox-LDL-induced HVSMCs injury

To investigate miR-425-5p’s role in AS, HVSMCs were transfected with a miR-425-5p inhibitor. As shown in Fig. 3A, ox-LDL-induced miR-425-5p upregulation was significantly suppressed by the inhibitor (P < 0.001). Furthermore, miR-425-5p knockdown attenuated ox-LDL-mediated reductions in cell viability and increases in apoptosis (P < 0.001, Figs. 3B-C). Ox-LDL-induced secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines IL-6 and IL-1β was also significantly attenuated by miR-425-5p inhibition (P < 0.001, Fig. 3D). Finally, while ox-LDL promoted HVSMCs migration, miR-425-5p suppression significantly reduced this effect (P < 0.001, Fig. 3E).

KLF7 is a target of miR-425-5p

To further explore the molecular mechanism underlying miR‑425‑5p function in AS, a putative binding site between KLF7 and miR‑425‑5p was predicted using the miRmap bioinformatics database (Fig. 4A). Clinical analysis demonstrated that serum KLF7 levels were markedly lower in patients with AS than in controls (P < 0.001, Fig. 4B). In addition, serum miR‑425‑5p expression was negatively correlated with KLF7 levels (r = 0.703, P < 0.0001, Fig. 4C). In vitro experiments also showed that KLF7 expression was gradually downregulated with increasing concentrations of ox‑LDL (P < 0.001, Fig. 4D). Additionally, DLR assay demonstrated that overexpression of miR‑425‑5p markedly suppressed luciferase activity in cells transfected with the WT‑KLF7 reporter plasmid, whereas no significant change was observed in cells carrying the MUT‑KLF7 plasmid (Fig. 4E).

Silencing miR-425-5p contributes to ox-LDL-induced injury in HVSMCs by targeting KLF7

In comparison with the si-NC group, all KLF7-targeting siRNAs effectively reduced KLF7 expression levels. Among them, si-KLF7#2 showed the most prominent inhibitory effect and was therefore chosen for subsequent experimental studies (P < 0.001, Fig. 5A). Additionally, the suppressive effect of ox-LDL on KLF7 was notably weakened by miR-425-5p downregulation, and this weakening effect could be significantly reversed via KLF7 silencing (P < 0.001, Fig. 5B). Meanwhile, KLF7 silencing also remarkably abrogated the restorative effect of reduced miR-425-5p expression on cell viability (P < 0.001, Fig. 5C). Furthermore, the inhibitory effects of miR-425-5p downregulation on cell apoptosis rate and the secretion of inflammatory cytokines IL-6 and IL-1β were both attenuated by KLF7 silencing (P < 0.001, Fig. 5D-E). Similarly, the inhibition of HVSMC migration induced by decreased miR-425-5p levels was also significantly impaired following KLF7 silencing (P < 0.001, Fig. 5F).

Discussion

AS is clinically silent in its early stages and progresses insidiously, often advancing to coronary artery disease and increasing the risk of sudden cardiac death if left untreated. Thus, identifying reliable biomarkers for early diagnosis and developing targeted therapies are critical priorities. While coronary angiography remains the gold standard for AS diagnosis, its invasive nature limits its utility for large-scale screening. In this study, we observed significantly elevated serum miR-425-5p levels in AS patients, suggesting a potential association between miR-425-5p dysregulation and AS progression.

MiRNAs have garnered significant interest as diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers in cardiovascular diseases [13]. For instance, miR-199a-5p demonstrates reliable diagnostic potential for AS [1], while elevated miR-18a-5p levels in AS patients correlate with increased cardiovascular risk and independently predict adverse clinical outcomes [14]. Additionally, miR-127-3p expression has been linked to macrophage infiltration and plaque stability in atherosclerotic lesions [15]. The involvement of miR-425-5p in cerebrovascular disease has captured our interest. It shows promise as a prognostic marker for heart failure [16]. In primary microglia and cerebral endothelial cells under OGD and in MCAO mice brain tissue, miR-425-5p expression rose [17]. Its plasma level in acute venous thromboembolism patients was higher than that of controls, indicating diagnostic potential [18]. A lack of miR-425-5p hampers the normal functions of vascular endothelial cells, whereas its overexpression can counteract the ati-angiogenic effects caused by arsenic [9]. Furthermore, miR-425-5p is a pivotal miRNA within a predictive model for coronary artery atherosclerosis in patients with rheumatoid arthritis [10]. Importantly, miRNA microarray analysis indicates that miR-425-5p is among several miRNAs that are upregulated in severe atherosclerotic lesions [11]. Consistent with prior studies, we observed a marked elevation of miR-425-5p in AS patients, suggesting its aberrant expression may contribute to AS progression.

AS is strongly associated with lipid profile alterations. LDL-C accumulation in the arterial endothelium represents a critical initiating event in plaque formation [19]. Ox-LDL, derived from endothelial-infiltrated LDL-C, is engulfed by macrophages to form foam cells—the lipid core of atherosclerotic plaques. Ox-LDL also sustains plaque inflammation by promoting pro-inflammatory cytokine release. Conversely, impaired HDL-C function reduces cholesterol efflux capacity, exacerbating necrotic core expansion after macrophage apoptosis [20]. TG-rich lipoproteins directly deposit in vessel walls, accelerating plaque progression [21]. In this study, AS patients exhibited significantly elevated LDL-C and TG levels, with reduced HDL-C. Notably, serum miR-425-5p showed positive correlations with LDL-C and TG, while inversely correlating with HDL-C. These findings suggest miR-425-5p may serve as a potential biomarker reflecting lipid-driven inflammatory processes in AS pathogenesis, warranting further investigation into its diagnostic and therapeutic implications.

The American Heart Association and the American College of Cardiology endorse CIMT as a validated subclinical marker for AS detection and disease progression monitoring [14], owing to its non-invasive, reproducible, and clinically accessible nature [22]. In this study, serum miR-425-5p levels demonstrated a significant positive correlation with CIMT, suggesting its potential utility in AS identification. To further evaluate miR-425-5p as a non-invasive biomarker, ROC curve analysis was performed. The results revealed an 85.50% sensitivity and 85.71% specificity for distinguishing AS patients from healthy controls, supporting its diagnostic potential. Our study identified a significant positive correlation between serum miR-425-5p levels and CIMT, suggesting its potential for AS detection. Subsequent ROC analysis demonstrated that miR-425-5p achieved 85.50% sensitivity and 85.71% specificity in distinguishing AS patients from healthy controls, supporting its viability as a non-invasive diagnostic biomarker. Furthermore, after further adjusting for complex confounding factors, it was found that miR‑425‑5p remains a potential risk factor for the development of AS. This observation highlights the potential clinical value of miR‑425‑5p as a promising biomarker for risk stratification and early assessment of AS.

VSMCs contribute critically to atherosclerotic progression through phenotypic modulation, losing contractile function and acquiring macrophage-like ox-LDL uptake capacity, leading to foam cell formation [23]. This transition is driven by lipoprotein accumulation and pro-inflammatory plaque microenvironments, which disrupt signaling pathways regulating insulin sensitivity, inflammation, and cell survival [24]. Ox-LDL stimulation significantly elevated miR-425-5p expression in HVSMCs, while miR-425-5p inhibition attenuated ox-LDL-induced cytotoxicity and enhanced apoptosis resistance. Ox-LDL is known to exacerbate vascular injury by upregulating inflammatory cytokines and amplifying cellular inflammation, thereby promoting AS development [25]. Notably, miR-425-5p knockdown suppressed ox-LDL-driven inflammatory cytokine overproduction in VSMCs, suggesting its regulatory role in atherogenic inflammation.

KLF7, a member of the Krüppel-like transcription factor family, participates in a spectrum of biological processes including cell survival, inflammatory response, and metabolic regulation across the cardiovascular, central nervous, and immune systems [26]. Emerging evidence has positioned KLF7 as a promising biomarker and therapeutic target for AS. For instance, genome-wide association studies have implicated KLF7 as a causal gene in patients with coronary artery disease, a major clinical manifestation of AS [27]. In the context of ox-LDL-induced AS, elevated KLF7 expression has been shown to drive ferroptosis in microvascular endothelial cells, exacerbating vascular injury [28]. Conversely, KLF7 expression is downregulated in aortic tissues of AS mouse models and in ox-LDL-stimulated macrophages; forced overexpression of KLF7 attenuates atherosclerotic lesion formation and ameliorates metabolic dysfunction in vivo [29]. In contrast, silencing KLF7 mitigates oxidative stress, inflammatory activation, and apoptosis in ox-LDL-treated human umbilical vein endothelial cells [30]. Additionally, extracellular vesicles carrying miR-210-3p suppress KLF7 expression in macrophages, thereby attenuating inflammatory responses and retarding atherosclerotic plaque progression [31]. In line with prior research, our results demonstrated significantly decreased KLF7 expression both in patients with atherosclerosis and in HVSMCs exposed to ox-LDL. Notably, we identified the direct regulatory interaction between miR-425-5p and KLF7 for the first time. Moreover, knockdown of miR-425-5p effectively reversed the downregulation of KLF7 induced by ox-LDL in VSMCs. Collectively, these findings provide novel perspectives and potential therapeutic targets for the clinical intervention and management of atherosclerosis.

Our study showed that miR‑425‑5p was significantly elevated in patients with asymptomatic atherosclerosis, could effectively distinguish patients from controls, and may serve as a potential risk factor for atherosclerosis. In terms of clinical translational significance, miR‑425‑5p represents a promising circulating biomarker for the early screening and risk stratification of asymptomatic atherosclerosis. As the asymptomatic stage is a critical window for cardiovascular prevention, early detection of abnormal miR‑425‑5p levels allows identification of high‑risk individuals before clinical events occur, which supports the shift toward proactive prevention and precision cardiovascular medicine. Timely intervention may delay vascular lesion progression and reduce the risk of major cardiovascular complications. Moreover, silencing miR‑425‑5p attenuated ox‑LDL‑induced apoptosis, inflammation, and migration in HVSMCs by targeting KLF7, suggesting that miR‑425‑5p may act as a novel therapeutic target for halting early atherosclerosis. Collectively, our findings provide dual translational value in early noninvasive screening and targeted intervention for asymptomatic atherosclerosis, which greatly strengthens the clinical relevance of this study. Nevertheless, several limitations should be acknowledged in the present study. First, all experiments were performed at clinical and cellular levels without in vivo verification using animal models. Second, the clinical component was restricted by a relatively small sample size, single-center design, and lack of an independent validation cohort. Third, the precise downstream signaling cascades mediating the effects of the miR-425-5p/KLF7 axis on atherosclerotic progression remain to be further explored.

Elevated serum miR-425-5p demonstrates diagnostic potential for AS, while its inhibition may mitigate ox-LDL-induced HVSMC dysfunction by preserving cell viability, reducing apoptosis, and suppressing inflammatory cytokine overproduction by upregulated the KFL7. Collectively, these findings support miR-425-5p as both a promising biomarker for AS detection and a viable therapeutic target to attenuate atherogenic progression.
