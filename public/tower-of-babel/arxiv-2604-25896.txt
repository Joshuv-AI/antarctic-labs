Thermodynamic Identification of the Internal Superconducting Phase Boundary in UTe 2 for H | b H\parallel b
Michal Vališka, Tetiana Haidamak, Andrej Cabala, Petr Proschek, Andreas Hausprug, Sergei Zherlitsyn, Vladimír Sechovský

Abstract
The H H – T T phase diagram of UTe 2 for magnetic field along the hard b b axis contains an unresolved internal boundary near μ 0 ​ H ∼ 14 \mu_{0}H\sim 14 –15 T, previously inferred from ac susceptibility and transport experiments but lacking thermodynamic evidence. We report ultrasound results for several elastic modes in an ultraclean UTe 2 single crystal with T c > 2 T_{c}>2 K for H | b H\parallel b down to 0.33 K and up to 18 T. A pronounced anomaly in the longitudinal C 33 C_{33} mode, with a weaker response in C 44 C_{44} and no resolvable anomaly in C 55 C_{55} , establishes this feature as a bulk thermodynamic phase boundary and reveals a symmetry-selective coupling to lattice strain. The phase line remains nearly constant in field near 14 T and terminates near 13.5 T and 1.25 K at a tetracritical point, providing the thermodynamic evidence for the fourth phase boundary in the H H – T T phase diagram. The results constrain the order-parameter structure of the high-field phase and support field-induced multicomponent superconductivity in UTe 2 .

The phase boundary sought in this work is examined through its coupling to lattice strain
\varepsilon_{ij}
. Ultrasound and magnetostriction are particularly suitable for this purpose, because both are thermodynamic probes of the same strain-dependent free-energy landscape
Lüthi 2005
;
Sigrist and Ueda 1991
. Elastic constants are determined by the strain dependence of the free-energy density
f
as

C_{ij}=\left(\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial\varepsilon_{i}\partial\varepsilon_{j}}\right)_{T,H}.

(1)

An anomaly in
C_{ij}
therefore reflects a change in the local stiffness of the strain-dependent free-energy landscape and represents a bulk thermodynamic signature of a phase transition or phase boundary
Lüthi 2005
;
Sigrist and Ueda 1991
.

Magnetostriction provides a complementary probe of the strain-dependent thermodynamic potential
f
. In the absence of external stress, the equilibrium strain is determined by the minimum condition

\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial\varepsilon_{i}}\right)_{T,H}=0,

(2)

so that the measured length change
\Delta l_{i}/l_{i}\equiv\varepsilon_{i}(H)
corresponds to the field evolution of the equilibrium strain. In this sense, ultrasound and magnetostriction probe the same free-energy landscape in complementary limits: ultrasound accesses its local stiffness dynamically through the adiabatic elastic response, whereas magnetostriction tracks the field evolution of the equilibrium lattice distortion in the static limit
Lüthi 2005
.

For an acoustic mode, the elastic constant is related to the sound velocity by

C_{ij}=\rho v^{\,2},

(3)

where
\rho
is the mass density and
v
is the sound velocity of the corresponding acoustic mode
Lüthi 2005
. In an ultrasound experiment, the measured elastic mode is specified by the propagation direction
\mathbf{q}
and the polarization
\mathbf{u}
of the acoustic wave. In the present work all measured modes propagate along the
c
axis: the longitudinal geometry
\mathbf{q}\parallel c
,
\mathbf{u}\parallel c
probes
C_{33}
, while the transverse geometries
\mathbf{q}\parallel c
,
\mathbf{u}\parallel b
and
\mathbf{q}\parallel c
,
\mathbf{u}\parallel a
probe
C_{44}
and
C_{55}
, respectively.

In the pulse-echo experiment, the directly measured quantity is the relative frequency shift
\Delta f/f
. For small changes,

\frac{\Delta v}{v}=\frac{\Delta f}{f}+\frac{\Delta l}{l}-\frac{\Delta\varphi}{\varphi}.

(4)

Here
\varphi
is the phase of the acoustic wave, which is kept constant in our ultrasound experiments, so that
\Delta\varphi/\varphi=0
. As shown below, the magnetostriction term in Eq. (
4
) is only of order
10^{-6}
, two orders of magnitude smaller than
\Delta f/f
, and is therefore neglected without affecting the anomaly positions.

The geometry was chosen to maximize sensitivity to the high-field boundary: superconductivity in UTe

C_{33}

H\parallel b

\Delta l_{c}/l_{c}

\Delta l_{b}/l_{b}

Figure

C_{33}
at selected temperatures. A clear softening anomaly, marked by arrows, is resolved inside the superconducting state at a nearly temperature-independent field of about 14 T. Since
C_{33}
is a bulk elastic constant, this anomaly establishes the missing thermodynamic signature of the internal high-field phase boundary previously inferred only from ac susceptibility and transport measurements
Sakai et al. 2023
;
Tokiwa et al. 2023
;
Matsumura et al. 2026
. The anomaly is visible from the lowest measured temperature up to 1.1 K, and its amplitude increases substantially on approaching the multicritical region where the line terminates at the TetCP. At higher temperatures, the feature weakens and shifts to lower fields as the system moves away from the tetracritical regime. For the 0.9 and 1.1 K isotherms, an additional kink is observed at higher field, consistent with the transition into the upper high-field superconducting phase
Knebel et al. 2019
;
Knafo et al. 2021
;
Wu et al. 2025a
.

The enhancement of the
C_{33}
anomaly on approaching the TetCP is consistent with the known sensitivity of elastic constants to critical and multicritical fluctuations in correlated uranium systems, including metamagnetic critical endpoints in UTe
2

Yoshizawa et al. 2023

A weaker anomaly is also observed in the transverse mode

Because both elastic constants and magnetostriction derive from the same strain-dependent free energy, the anomaly cannot be attributed merely to vortex dynamics or to a transport-specific feature.

The mode selectivity also has symmetry implications. In orthorhombic
D_{2h}

C_{44}

\varepsilon_{bc}

\varepsilon_{ab}

T_{c}

C_{44}

C_{55}

C_{66}

;
Kappler et al. 1995
;
Wu et al. 2025b
;
Kamat et al. 2026

The anomaly positions extracted from the thermodynamic data are summarized in Fig.

The newly resolved, nearly field-constant internal boundary near 14 T separates SC1 from an intermediate region naturally identified as SC1+SC2 and terminates near 13.5 T and 1.25 K at a tetracritical point (TetCP), where four phase boundaries meet. Thus, the ultrasound anomaly supplies the missing thermodynamic phase line required to complete the
H\parallel b

The local geometry near the TetCP also helps explain why this boundary has been difficult to detect calorimetrically. The four phase boundaries have approximate slopes
dH/dT\sim-15
,
-11
,
-0.44
, and
+30
T/K, with the discussed boundary being almost horizontal. For a field-tuned second-order boundary
H^{\ast}(T)
, the Ehrenfest relation gives, up to conventional prefactors,

\Delta\!\left(\frac{C_{p}}{T}\right)\propto\Delta\chi\left(\frac{dH^{\ast}}{dT}\right)^{2},

(5)

where
\Delta\chi
is the susceptibility discontinuity across the boundary. Thus, the calorimetric anomaly is quadratically suppressed when
dH^{\ast}/dT
is small
Yip et al. 1991
;
Adenwalla et al. 1992
;
Boukhny et al. 1994
. With
dH^{\ast}/dT\approx-0.44
T/K, the expected specific-heat anomaly is smaller by factors of order
10^{-3}
–
10^{-4}
compared with the steeper neighboring boundaries, for comparable
\Delta\chi
. This naturally explains why the internal 14–15 T phase boundary may remain essentially unresolved in specific heat, even if it is a genuine thermodynamic transition
Machida 2024
;
Sakai et al. 2023
;
Vasina et al. 2025
.

The elastic response, however, is governed by a different derivative of the same field-tuned boundary. For
H^{\ast}(T,\varepsilon)
, the corresponding Ehrenfest relation may be written, again up to conventional prefactors, as

\Delta C_{ij}\propto-\Delta\chi\left(\frac{\partial H^{\ast}}{\partial\varepsilon_{i}}\right)_{T}\left(\frac{\partial H^{\ast}}{\partial\varepsilon_{j}}\right)_{T}.

(6)

Equations (
5
) and (
6
) therefore highlight why the same transition can be nearly invisible in
C_{p}
but prominent in ultrasound: the calorimetric anomaly is suppressed by the small slope
dH^{\ast}/dT
, whereas the elastic anomaly remains large if the critical field is strongly strain dependent. The clear
C_{33}
anomaly and the corresponding feature in
\Delta l_{c}/l_{c}
indicate precisely such a strong coupling to
c
-axis strain, making ultrasound and magnetostriction particularly sensitive probes of this otherwise elusive phase line
Lüthi 2005
;
Sigrist and Ueda 1991
.

Taken together, the present results establish the internal boundary near 14–15 T for
H\parallel b
as a genuine bulk thermodynamic phase line. Its inclusion confirms the fourth boundary at the ambient-pressure TetCP and supplements the
H
–
T
phase diagram. The resulting topology is not merely geometrical: tetracriticality naturally implies the simultaneous relevance of two superconducting order-parameter sectors and the stabilization of a mixed phase rather than a simple continuation of a single superconducting state
Sigrist and Ueda 1991
;
Kamat et al. 2026
;
Machida 2024
. The strong
C_{33}
response, the weaker but finite
C_{44}
anomaly, the absence of a resolvable
C_{55}
anomaly, and the accompanying kink in
\Delta l_{c}/l_{c}
further show that this high-field phase couples to lattice strain in a symmetry-selective manner. Thus, crossing the internal 14–15 T boundary drives UTe
2
from the low-field SC1 state into a symmetry-distinct mixed SC1+SC2 phase, linking the ambient-pressure high-field phase diagram directly to the pressure-induced multicomponent regime
Theuss et al. 2024
;
Vasina et al. 2025
;
Kamat et al. 2026
.
