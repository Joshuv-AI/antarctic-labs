Activity of datopotamab deruxtecan in TROP2-expressing low-grade serous ovarian cancer: a preclinical study
Authors: Sarah Ottum, Orazio De Tommasi, Cem Demirkiran, Stefania Bellone, Natalia Buza, Na Niu, Namrata Sethi, Tobias M. Hartwich, Alessandro D. Santin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionLow-grade serous ovarian carcinoma (LGSOC) is a rare ovarian cancer subtype with limited sensitivity to chemotherapy and modest benefit from endocrine and MEK-directed therapy. New therapeutic targets are needed. We evaluated trophoblast cell-surface antigen 2 (TROP2) expression in LGSOC and the activity of datopotamab deruxtecan (Dato-DXd), a TROP2-directed antibody-drug conjugate, in an LGSOC patient-derived xenograft (PDX) model.MethodsTROP2 expression was assessed by immunohistochemistry in a retrospective single-center cohort of 29 LGSOC cases treated at Yale University. Antitumor activity of Dato-DXd was evaluated in a TROP2-expressing PDX model derived from a heavily pretreated patient with LGSOC resistant to chemotherapy, aromatase inhibitors, and MEK inhibitors.ResultsTROP2 expression was detected in 29 of 29 cases, and 23 tumors showed moderate-to-strong expression. In vivo, Dato-DXd significantly inhibited tumor growth versus vehicle/saline control in a treatment-resistant LGSOC PDX model (p < 0.0001) without relevant toxicity. Median survival was 22 days in control animals and was not reached by day 50 in the Dato-DXd group.ConclusionTROP2 is frequently expressed in LGSOC and may represent a therapeutically relevant target. Dato-DXd showed marked antitumor activity in a treatment-resistant LGSOC PDX model. Clinical evaluation in patients with recurrent LGSOC is warranted.

Introduction

Low-grade serous ovarian carcinoma (LGSOC) is a rare and biologically distinct subtype of epithelial ovarian cancer, accounting for a small proportion of cases. Compared with high-grade serous ovarian carcinoma (HGSOC), LGSOC is characterized by a more indolent clinical course and a younger age at diagnosis. A defining feature of LGSOC is its intrinsic resistance to conventional cytotoxic chemotherapy, resulting in limited efficacy of standard platinum-based regimens (Zwimpfer et al., 2023). Surgical cytoreduction remains a cornerstone of initial management. Alternative therapeutic strategies have been explored, including endocrine therapy with aromatase inhibitors and targeted approaches such as MEK inhibitors, reflecting the frequent activation of the MAPK signaling pathway in LGSOC (Gershenson et al., 2009). Nevertheless, response rates to these treatments are generally modest, and durable disease control is achieved in only a subset of patients. In addition, no standard treatment sequencing has been established, and most patients ultimately develop resistance to available therapies. Given the limited effectiveness of current treatment options and the unique biological characteristics of LGSOC, there remains a critical need to develop more effective, personalized therapeutic strategies for patients with recurrent and treatment-resistant disease (Santin et al., 2024).

Antibody–drug conjugates (ADCs) are an emerging class of targeted therapeutics that combine a monoclonal antibody with a cytotoxic payload, enabling selective delivery of chemotherapy to tumor cells while minimizing off-target toxicity. In ovarian cancer, ADCs have shown promising clinical activity, particularly in platinum-resistant disease, representing a novel therapeutic strategy capable of improving outcomes beyond conventional chemotherapy (Narayana and Gupta, 2025).

Trophoblast cell-surface antigen 2 (TROP2) is a transmembrane glycoprotein composed of an extracellular domain, a transmembrane region, and a short cytoplasmic tail containing phosphorylation sites. Functionally, TROP2 acts as a calcium signal transducer, modulating key cell cycle–related signaling pathways through interactions with multiple proteins, including insulin-like growth factor 1, claudins, cyclin D1, and protein kinase C. These interactions contribute to the regulation of cellular proliferation, survival, and apoptosis. Notably, TROP2 is frequently overexpressed in a wide range of epithelial malignancies and is associated with poor clinical outcomes. Its limited expression in normal tissues, together with high expression rates reported in certain tumor types, supports its role as an attractive and clinically relevant therapeutic target (Goldenberg et al., 2018).

TROP2-targeting strategies have already been explored in LGSOC using sacituzumab govitecan, a TROP2-directed antibody–drug conjugate. In preclinical models, sacituzumab govitecan demonstrated marked antitumor activity, with significant tumor growth inhibition and improved survival in patient-derived xenografts, including models resistant to chemotherapy, endocrine therapy, and MEK inhibitors (Ettorre et al., 2025).

Datopotamab deruxtecan (DS-1062; Dato-DXd) is a next-generation ADC targeting TROP2, consisting of a humanized monoclonal antibody linked to a potent topoisomerase I inhibitor (DXd) through a cleavable tetrapeptide linker. This linker, based on a maleimide glycine–phenylalanine–glycine (GGFG) sequence, is identical to that used in trastuzumab deruxtecan and is designed to remain stable in circulation while enabling efficient intracellular drug release. Following internalization, the linker is selectively cleaved by lysosomal proteases that are highly active in tumor cells, thereby promoting targeted cytotoxicity and reducing systemic exposure (Greenman, Bellone, et al., 2025).

Dato-DXd has received regulatory approval from the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for the treatment of selected advanced solid tumors. In January 2025, the FDA granted approval for Dato-DXd for adult patients with unresectable or metastatic hormone receptor (HR)–positive, HER2-negative breast cancer who have previously received endocrine-based therapy and at least one prior line of chemotherapy. Subsequently, in June 2025, the FDA granted accelerated approval for Dato-DXd for adult patients with locally advanced or metastatic non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) harboring activating EGFR mutations, whose disease has progressed on prior EGFR-targeted therapy and platinum-based chemotherapy (Lawrence, 2025).

Within the context of gynecologic malignancies, Dato-DXd has demonstrated significant preclinical antitumor activity across multiple tumor types, including high-grade serous ovarian carcinoma (HGSOC) and uterine serous carcinoma (USC) (Greenman et al., 2025a, Greenman et al., 2025b; McNamara et al., 2024). Building upon this evidence, our study aimed to evaluate, for the first time, the in vivo activity of Dato-DXd in a patient-derived xenograft (PDX) model of LGSOC.

Methods

Patient selection

To establish TROP2 expression in LGSOC, we identified 29 patients with pathology collected within the Yale Health System between 2009 and 2026 who were diagnosed with LGSOC. The pathologic diagnosis was confirmed after review of all available cases by the Yale study pathologist (N.B.). This retrospective study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the Yale University Institutional Review Board.

IHC expression testing

Unstained 5-μm sections were obtained from formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded tumor specimens and subjected to immunohistochemical analysis for TROP2 expression. Slides were stained using a rabbit monoclonal anti-TROP2 antibody (clone EPR20043; Abcam, Waltham, MA) at a dilution of 1:2000, following standard deparaffinization, rehydration, and antigen retrieval in Tris–EDTA buffer (pH 9.0). Signal detection was performed using 3,3′-diaminobenzidine as chromogen, and appropriate positive and negative controls were included in each staining run. TROP2 expression was evaluated based on membranous staining and scored according to previously described criteria. The proportion of positive tumor cells was estimated, and staining intensity was graded on a scale from 0 (negative) to 3+ (Bychkov et al., 2016; Liu et al., 2024; Santin et al., 2024). A composite score was calculated by multiplying the percentage of positive tumor cells by the corresponding intensity score, generating four categories: 0–9 (negative), 10–99 (Kang et al., 2023), 100–199 (moderate), and 200–300 (high expression).

Drug

Dato-DXd was provided by Daiichi Sankyo Co., Ltd. (Tokyo, Japan). All study drug was stored at −80 °C until use. For in vivo studies, dosing solutions were prepared weekly using a vehicle composed of polyethylene glycol 400 (PEG 400), ethanol, and water, and stored at 4 °C protected from light. Dosing was adjusted to deliver 10 mg/kg per administration via retro-orbital injection. In this regard, while retro-orbital (RO) and tail vein (TV) injections are functionally equivalent for systemic delivery of antibodies, RO injections are technically faster to perform and are widely recognized to cause less procedural stress than traditional TV methods. (Steel et al., 2008).

PDX model formation, treatment, and evaluation

The in vivo antitumor activity of Dato-DXd was evaluated in a TROP2-expressing low-grade serous ovarian cancer (LGSOC) patient-derived xenograft (PDX) model established from a surgical biopsy obtained from a heavily pretreated patient with recurrent LGSOC. This PDX model has recently been used to evaluate the preclinical activity of Sacituzumab Govitecan, in LGSOC(Ettorre et al., 2025). Fresh tumor tissue was collected during secondary debulking surgery for malignant small bowel obstruction and immediately implanted subcutaneously into 6–8-week-old severe combined immunodeficient (SCID) mice to establish the PDX line. The PDX was subsequently serially passed in vivo before treatment studies. When tumors reached a volume of approximately 0.2 cm3, animals were randomized to receive Dato-DXd (10 mg/kg; n = 4) or vehicle/saline control (n = 6). Treatment was administered as a single retro-orbital injection on day 0. Tumor growth was assessed twice weekly, and tumor volume was calculated using the formula (A2 × B)/2, where A represents the shortest diameter and B the longest perpendicular diameter. Animals were euthanized when tumor burden reached 1.0 cm3 in accordance with humane endpoints established by institutional guidelines. All animal procedures were conducted in compliance with Yale University Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) regulations and were approved before study initiation (Number of protocol: 2025–11,205). Overall survival was the primary endpoint. Tumor growth differences were analyzed using two-way ANOVA, and percent changes from baseline at the last common study time point were evaluated using an unpaired t-test. Statistical significance was defined as a p value <0.05.

In vivo LGSOC PDX testing of Dato-DXd

To evaluate the efficacy of TROP2-directed therapy in LGSOC, a novel PDX model was established using a surgical biopsy from a patient with LGSOC who had experienced disease progression following multiple regimens of chemotherapy, aromatase inhibitors (AI), and MEK inhibitors (MEKi). The biopsy showed a TROP2 H-score of 285. The LGSOC xenografts were treated with Dato-DXd. As outlined in the Methods, tumor tissue from a consented LGSOC patient was collected during a secondary debulking procedure prompted by malignant small bowel obstruction and immediately divided for subcutaneous engraftment into SCID mice. This process was repeated to expand the PDX line in ten animals. Once the tumors reached a volume of 0.2cm3, the animals were randomized into either the saline control group (n = 6) or the Dato-DXd group (n = 4). Differences in tumor growth were noticeable between the two treatment groups by the first day of measurement (Fig. 3A). By day 11, tumors in the control group had mean volumes of about 0.54 cm3, compared to about 0.37 cm3 in the treatment group. This pattern persisted, with the treated mice showing reduced/arrested tumor growth while the control mice showed linear tumor growth. On day 24, the mean tumor volume in the mice treated with Dato-DXd was about 0.34 cm3, while in the control group it reached 1 cm3. At this time, control mice were euthanized as per IACUC protocol, while the Dato-DXd treated mice were followed until day 50 to assess tumor growth, which remained essentially unchanged. We also assessed the percent change in tumor size from baseline to day 24 in all treated animals. As reported in Fig. 3B, the control group tumor size increased by 356% from baseline, while mice treated with Dato-DXd showed a 34% increase, which is significantly smaller than baseline (i.e., less than 100%, p < 0.0001). When we examined overall survival between the two groups (Fig. 3C), the median survival in the control mice was 22 days. The median survival for the Dato-DXd group was not reached by the end of our experiment at day 50 as all ADC-treated mice remained alive. Finally, to assess the treatment's tolerability in vivo, we monitored each animal's weight throughout the study. Discussion

In this study, we demonstrate that Dato-DXd, a TROP2-directed ADC, exhibits significant antitumor activity in a preclinical model of LGSOC, supporting its potential as a therapeutic strategy in this challenging disease. To evaluate the activity of Dato-DXd in a clinically relevant setting, we generated a PDX model from tumor tissue from a patient with LGSOC who had progressed on platinum-based chemotherapy, liposomal doxorubicin, endocrine therapy and MEK inhibitor. The tumor was successfully engrafted and expanded in SCID mice, allowing the development of an in vivo model representative of treatment-resistant disease. Treatment with Dato-DXd resulted in a marked antitumor effect, with early divergence of tumor growth curves observed within the first days of therapy. This was associated with a significant improvement in overall survival compared with control animals (p < 0.0001). Notably, treatment was well tolerated, with no evidence of significant weight loss or treatment-related toxicity.

The identification of actionable surface antigens is critical for the development of ADCs in LGSOC since low grade ovarian tumors are slow-growing tumors notoriously poorly responsive to chemotherapy. Accordingly, ADCs may potentially bypass the limitations of traditional chemotherapy in LGSOC by targeting specific cell-surface receptors rather than relying on rapid cell division. This enables ADCs to effectively internalize and deliver cytotoxic payloads into slow-growing tumors, regardless of their mitotic rate.In our retrospective single-center analysis, which included a cohort of LGSOC patients surgically treated at our institution between 2009 and 2026, TROP2 expression was identified in 29 of 29 cases, with 23 tumors demonstrating moderate-to-strong expression. A similarly high prevalence of expression has also been reported for folate receptor alpha (FRα), a clinically validated ADC target in ovarian cancer. In the analysis by Ettorre et al., FRα expression was detected in 100% of cases, with approximately 78% of tumors showing moderate-to-strong positivity by immunohistochemistry, supporting its potential as a broadly applicable therapeutic target in this setting (Ettorre et al., 2026). By contrast, HER2 expression appears to be uncommon in low-grade serous ovarian tumors. In a retrospective analysis of 75 LGSOC cases conducted by Němejcová et al., genomic alterations were identified in approximately 5% of tumors, while protein expression was generally low. HER2 1+ expression was observed in approximately 5–10% of cases, with rare tumors demonstrating an “ultra-low” phenotype; notably, no tumors met criteria for HER2 overexpression (3+), indicating a limited role for HER2-directed ADC strategies in this disease (Nemejcova et al., 2025). This differential expression profile may have important therapeutic implications, positioning TROP2-directed ADCs as a particularly promising strategy for patients with LGSOC, especially in the setting of treatment-resistant disease.

Sacituzumab govitecan and Dato-DXd are both TROP2-directed ADCs that have demonstrated promising antitumor activity across multiple solid tumors; however, they differ in key structural and pharmacologic features that may influence efficacy and safety. Sacituzumab govitecan delivers SN-38, the active metabolite of irinotecan, through a moderately stable linker and a relatively high drug-to-antibody ratio (DAR = 7.6), resulting in potent cytotoxicity but also a higher rate of off-target toxicity due to payload release in circulation (Cardillo et al., 2024). In contrast, Dato-DXd utilizes a more stable, cleavable tetrapeptide linker and a topoisomerase I inhibitor payload (deruxtecan, DXd) with higher membrane permeability and potency, coupled with a lower DAR (DAR = 4), which may enhance tumor selectivity and reduce systemic exposure (Okajima et al., 2021). Preclinical studies have shown that Dato-DXd induces strong DNA damage, apoptosis, and a pronounced bystander effect, allowing killing of both TROP2-positive and adjacent TROP2-negative cells, while maintaining favorable tolerability profiles across different gynecologic malignancies, including HGSOC and USC. Overall, while both agents are effective TROP2-targeting ADCs, Dato-DXd may offer advantages in pharmacokinetics, payload potency, and therapeutic index, although direct comparative clinical data are currently lacking.

Conclusion

TROP2 is widely expressed in LGSOC, supporting its role as a relevant therapeutic target in this rare histologic subtype. Dato-DXd, similarly to Sacituzumab Govitecan(Ettorre et al., 2025), demonstrated significant antitumor activity in preclinical models, highlighting its potential as a novel treatment strategy for LGSOC. Further clinical studies are warranted to evaluate the safety and efficacy of TROP2-targeting ADCs in patients.
