Large deviations for sums of multivariate stretched-exponential random variables: the few-big-jumps principle
Nina Gantert nina.gantert@tum.de Joscha Prochno joscha.prochno@uni-passau.de Philipp Tuchel philipp.tuchel@ruhr-uni-bochum.de

Abstract
Large deviations for sums of i.i.d. random variables with stretched-exponential tails (also called Weibull or semi-exponential tails) have been well understood since the 60’s, going back to Nagaev’s seminal work. Many extensions in the 1 1 -dimensional setting have been developed since then, showing that such deviations are typically governed by a single big jump. In higher dimensions, a corresponding theory has remained largely undeveloped. This work provides such a multivariate extension and establishes large deviation results for sums of i.i.d. random vectors in ℝ k \mathbb{R}^{k} under fairly general assumptions. Roughly speaking, for some α ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) \alpha\in(0,1) , the log-probability of one random vector divided by x x exceeding a threshold t t in all components behaves asymptotically, for large x x , as x α x^{\alpha} times a negative infimum of a function 𝒥 \mathcal{J} . We prove large deviation results for sums of i.i.d. copies, where the rate function is given by a minimization of at most k k summands of 𝒥 \mathcal{J} . This establishes a few-big-jumps principle that generalizes the classical 1 1 -dimensional phenomenon: the deviation is typically realized by at most k k independent vectors. The results are applied to absolute powers of multivariate Gaussian vectors as well as to various other examples. They also allow us to study random projections of high-dimensional ℓ p N \ell_{p}^{N} -balls, revealing interesting insights about the appearance of light- and heavy-tailed distributions in high-dimensional geometry. Keywords . Large deviations, stretched exponential random variables, Weibull-type distributions, empirical mean, limit theorems, multivariate random variables MSC . Introduction

In probability, the theory of large deviations provides a quantitative description of rare events. It originated in Cramér’s work on sums of i.i.d. random variables from 1938, where he proved that if the moment generating function of the summands is finite in a neighborhood of the origin, then the empirical mean satisfies a large deviation principle with an exponential speed and a convex rate function given by the Legendre transform of the logarithmic moment generating function (see
[
5
]
). Later developments by Donsker and Varadhan and the subsequent formulation of the large deviation framework by Varadhan and others have elevated large deviations to a cornerstone of modern probability theory (see
[
5
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
35
]
and the book by
[
7
]
). In this Cramér regime, the probability that the empirical mean deviates from its expectation decays at an exponential speed, i.e., much faster than a straight forward application of the law of large numbers suggests. For heavy-tailed distributions, however, the moment generating functions do not exist and the classical Cramér theory does not apply. The limiting behavior in this case is entirely different and is typically governed by large but rare summands. In one dimension, the empirical mean of i.i.d. heavy-tailed variables typically deviates, since a single summand causes the deviation. By writing
a(x)\sim b(x)
if
a(x)/b(x)\to 1
as
x\to\infty
, a random variable
Y
with values in
\mathbb{R}
has a
1
-dimensional
stretched-exponential tail
if there exist
c>0
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that

\log\mathbb{P}(Y\geq t)\sim-c\,t^{\alpha}\qquad(t\to\infty).

These random variables are also called
Weibull-like
or
semi-exponential
random variables. Their tails do not decay exponentially fast, but faster than any inverse power. This tail behavior lies just below the light-tailed case: at the endpoint
\alpha=1
the asymptotics become exponential, corresponding to a setting with finite exponential moments, and hence to the classical Cramér theorem. In this natural regime, it follows from Nagaev’s result (
[
30
,
31
,
32
]
) that if
(Y_{i})_{i\geq 1}
are i.i.d. centered with
Y_{1}
having stretched-exponential tail as above, then for every
t>0
,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N^{\alpha}}\log\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}Y_{i}\geq t\right)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N^{\alpha}}\log\mathbb{P}(Y_{1}\geq Nt)=-c\,t^{\alpha}.

This phenomenon is well known and often referred to as
one-big-jump principle
or
principle of a single big jump
. The upper tail of the sum is, on the logarithmic scale, the same as the tail of the maximum of the summands. Subsequent work has shown that this principle persists in a variety of settings. Denisov, Dieker, and Shneer proved that for general sub-exponential distributions one has
\mathbb{P}(\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}>t)\sim N\mathbb{P}(X_{1}>t)
,
t\to\infty
, so that a rare event in the sum is caused by one large jump, see
[
8
]
. Gantert, Ramanan, and Rembart established large deviations for certain weighted sums of stretched-exponential random variables, see
[
12
]
. Lehtomaa later obtained a large deviation principle (LDP) for the sample mean of i.i.d. heavy-tailed variables with finite moments of all orders, see (
[
28
]
). Brosset et al. identified the transition speed between the Gaussian/moderate deviation regime and the sub-exponential one-big-jump regime for stretched-exponential tails, providing asymptotics across regimes and an explicit rate function exactly at the transition scale, see
[
3
]
. Aurzada extended in
[
2
]
the weighted-sum theory to infinite weighted series of independent stretched-exponential variables, which generalizes
[
12
]
. A comprehensive survey of large deviations for
1
-dimensional heavy-tailed sums may be found in
[
8
]
.

Surprisingly, beyond the
1
-dimensional setting, much less is known, even though vectors with heavy-tailed components arise naturally in many applications ranging from insurance and finance to telecommunications and statistical mechanics. The case of independent components is easy to obtain from the
1
-dimensional setting, but dependence between components poses significant challenges. For heavy-tailed risk processes, Hägele and Lehtomaa established multivariate large deviation principles ( see
[
15
]
) under the assumption that
\mathbf{X}=R\mathbf{U}
, where
R
is
1
-dimensional stretched-exponential and (in a certain sense) asymptotically independent of
\mathbf{U}
, which is distributed on the unit sphere. In this setup, the problem can essentially be reduced to the
1
-dimensional setting by using the tails of
R
. However, a general description of large deviations for empirical means of stretched-exponential random vectors remained open. We found no unified definition of multivariate stretched-exponential tails in the literature. Nevertheless, we propose a natural notion that extends the
1
-dimensional definition and is applicable across a broad range of settings.

In this paper, we generalize the
1
-dimensional results to some multivariate random vectors with stretched-exponential tails. We consider an i.i.d. sequence
(\mathbf{X}_{i})_{i\geq 1}
of centered
\mathbb{R}^{k}
-valued random vectors whose marginal tails decay like
\exp(-c\,t^{\alpha})
for some
\alpha\in(0,1),c>0
, and whose joint tail is governed by a function
\mathcal{J}
, see Definition
2.1
. Our main result, Theorem
A
, shows that if
x_{N}\to\infty
with
x_{N}/N^{1/(2-\alpha)}\to\infty
, then the vector
\frac{1}{x_{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{X}_{i}
is (componentwise) larger than
\mathbf{t}>0
with probability
\sim\exp(-x_{N}^{\alpha}\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}(\mathbf{t}))
, where the rate function
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}
is obtained by minimizing at most
k
summands of
\mathcal{J}
. In contrast to the
1
-dimensional case, the deviation need not be realized by a single sample vector. At most
k
summands contribute, each corresponding to a single vector deviation while each vector may contribute to the deviation of multiple coordinates. This
few-big-jumps principle
extends the large deviation results for stretched-exponential random variables to the multivariate setting. We summarize the behavior of
\frac{1}{x_{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{X}_{i}
depending on the growth of the scale

We obtain a condition on the density, which guarantees that a random vector satisfies our definition, and we apply our general theory to absolute powers of multivariate Gaussian vectors, multivariate Weibull distributions in the sense of

\ell_{p}^{N}

2.1
Large deviations and a few-big-jumps principle

We give the definition of multivariate stretched-exponential tails and state the main result.

Definition 2.1

(Multivariate stretched-exponential tails)
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
. A centered random vector
\mathbf{Y}=(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{k})
with values in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
has
stretched-exponential tail
of rate
(\mathcal{J},\alpha)
if there exists a lower semicontinuous function
\mathcal{J}:\mathbb{R}_{+}^{k}\to[0,\infty)
such that

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{1}{x^{\alpha}}\log\mathbb{P}\big(\mathbf{Y}\geq x\mathbf{t}\big)=-\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{t}_{+})

for all
\mathbf{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
with at least one (strictly) positive component. Further, assume that
\mathcal{J}
is non-degenerate in the sense that
\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{t})>0
for all
\mathbf{t}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{k}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
.

Often,
\mathcal{J}
is given by the infimum of some function
\overline{\mathcal{J}}
, i.e.
\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{t})=\inf_{\mathbf{s}\geq\mathbf{t}}\overline{\mathcal{J}}(\mathbf{s})
. In the above definition, the function
\mathcal{J}
is necessarily
\alpha
-homogeneous (see Lemma
5.1
) and componentwise nondecreasing. We further remark that
\mathbf{Y}
satisfying Definition
2.1
and Assumption
(A)
has finite second moments in each marginal (see Lemma
5.2
).

For
\mathcal{J}:\mathbb{R}_{+}^{k}\to[0,\infty)
, we define the rate function
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}:\mathbb{R}_{+}^{k}\to[0,\infty)
by

\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}(\mathbf{t}):=\min\Big\{\sum_{r=1}^{k}\mathcal{J}\big(\mathbf{t}^{(r)}\big):\mathbf{t}^{(r)}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{k},\sum_{r=1}^{k}\mathbf{t}^{(r)}=\mathbf{t}\Big\}.

(2.2)

Theorem A

(Large deviations for sums of stretched-exponential random vectors)
.

Fix
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Let
(\mathbf{X}_{i})_{i\geq 1}
be i.i.d. random vectors with values in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
and assume that
\mathbf{X}_{1}
satisfies
Definition

2.1
with rate
(\mathcal{J},\alpha)
and Assumption
(A)
. Let
(x_{N})_{N\geq 1}
be a sequence of positive numbers such that
x_{N}\to\infty
and
x_{N}N^{-1/(2-\alpha)}\to\infty
. Then, for any
\mathbf{t}\in(0,\infty)^{k}
,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{x_{N}^{\alpha}}\log\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{x_{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{X}_{i}\geq\mathbf{t}\right)=-\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}(\mathbf{t})

The above can be rewritten as

\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{x_{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{X}_{i}\geq\mathbf{t}\right)=\exp(-x_{N}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}(\mathbf{t})+o(1))).

This theorem generalizes the
1
-dimensional result to the multivariate setting and for
k=1
, the theorem reduces to the classical
1
-dimensional result. From the rate function, it can be seen that a typical deviation is realized by at most
k
independent deviations, where the size of each of these deviations is such that the variational problem in (
2.2
) is minimized. Each of the non-zero summands corresponds typically to one deviation of a random vector in the sum. Note however, that one vector may contribute to the deviation of multiple coordinates. In some situations, the deviation is realized by only one vector as in the
1
-dimensional case, but we give also examples where the deviation is typically caused by multiple vectors (see Lemma
5.8
). In the above setting, we can choose
x_{N}=N
for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

Remark 2.2
.

The rate function
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}
is not necessarily convex nor concave even in simple examples (see Lemma
5.7
). This contrasts with the light tailed case in Cramér’s theorem, where the rate function is convex, and with the
1
-dimensional stretched-exponential case, where the rate function is concave.

2.2
Moderate deviation principles

When
x_{N}
grows slower than
N^{1/(2-\alpha)}
, one is in the Gaussian or moderate deviation range. The following statement is known, but we could not find it in the literature as stated. We include it for completeness as this, together with Theorem
A
, gives a full picture of the large deviations for sequences
(x_{N})_{N\geq 1}
with
x_{N}N^{-1/2}\to\infty
and either
x_{N}N^{-1/(2-\alpha)}\to\infty
or
x_{N}N^{-1/(2-\alpha)}\to 0
. For the case where
x_{N}\sim cN^{1/(2-\alpha)}
for some
c>0
, the situation seems to be more involved. For the
1
-dimensional case, this case is understood and the rate function is given by a combination of the rate functions of the both regimes (see
[
3
]
). In the multivariate case, this question remains open.

For the definition of a large deviation principle, see Definition
4.1
and for a result on Banach space valued random variables, see Proposition
4.4
.

Theorem B

(Moderate deviations)
.

Let
(x_{N})_{N\geq 1}
satisfy
\sqrt{N}/x_{N}\to 0
and
x_{N}N^{-1/(2-\alpha)}\to 0
. Let
\mathbf{X},\mathbf{X}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{X}_{N}
be i.i.d. centered with values in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
and assume that for some
c>0
and all large
t
,

\mathbb{P}(\|\mathbf{X}\|\geq t)\leq\exp(-c\,t^{\alpha}).

If
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}=\operatorname{Cov}(\mathbf{X})
is invertible, then
\frac{1}{x_{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{X}_{i}
satisfies an LDP on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
with speed
x_{N}^{2}/N
and good rate function

I(\mathbf{z})=\frac{1}{2}\,\mathbf{z}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1}\mathbf{z}.

In particular, for every
\mathbf{t}\in(0,\infty)^{k}
,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{N}{x_{N}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{x_{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{X}_{i}\geq\mathbf{t}\right)=-\inf_{\mathbf{z}\geq\mathbf{t}}\ \frac{1}{2}\,\mathbf{z}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1}\mathbf{z}.

Remark 2.3
.

If
\mathbf{X}
satisfies
Definition

2.1
with rate
(\mathcal{J},\alpha)
and Assumption
(A)
then for large
t
,

\mathbb{P}(\|\mathbf{X}\|\geq t)\leq k\max_{1\leq j\leq k}\mathbb{P}\big(|X_{j}|\geq\frac{t}{\sqrt{k}}\big)\leq k\max_{j}\big\{\mathbb{P}\big(X_{j}\geq\frac{t}{\sqrt{k}}\big)+\mathbb{P}\big(X_{j}\leq-\frac{t}{\sqrt{k}}\big)\big\}\leq\exp(-c^{\prime}t^{\alpha})

for some
c^{\prime}>0
. Here the last inequality follows by Lemma
5.2
. Thus, the hypothesis of
B
holds.

3.2
Geometry of high-dimensional
\ell_{p}^{N}

This section uses our analysis of multivariate stretched-exponential vectors to study the behavior of high-dimensional objects. We will see that an analogue to light- and heavy-tailed distributions exists for high-dimensional objects. A concrete class of high-dimensional objects is the family of
\ell_{p}^{N}
-balls, the unit balls in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
for the usual
\ell_{p}
-norm. This norm is given by

\|\mathbf{x}\|_{p}=\begin{cases}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}|x_{i}|^{p}\right)^{1/p},&\text{if }p\in[1,\infty),\\
\max\{|x_{1}|,\ldots,|x_{N}|\},&\text{if }p=\infty,\end{cases}

and
\mathbb{B}_{p}^{N}=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:\|\mathbf{x}\|_{p}\leq 1\}
. Even though this class is only parametrized by the value of
p
, it gives rise to interesting and unexpected behavior in high dimensions and is an active area of research (see for instance
[
1
,
11
,
19
,
21
,
22
,
24
,
33
]
or
[
34
]
).

For a matrix
\mathbf{M}
write
\mathbf{M}^{\top}
for the transpose. We denote by
\operatorname{Id}_{m}
the
m
-dimensional identity matrix. For
N\geq m
the
Stiefel manifold
is defined as

\mathbb{V}_{m,N}:=\Big\{\mathbf{M}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times N}:\mathbf{M}\mathbf{M}^{\top}=\operatorname{Id}_{m}\Big\}

and carries a unique Haar probability measure, denoted
\operatorname{Unif}(\mathbb{V}_{m,N})
, invariant under left and right multiplication by orthogonal matrices. Elements of
\mathbb{V}_{m,N}
correspond, up to an orthogonal transformation, to orthogonal projections. More precisely, for any convex body
C\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
\mathbf{V}\in\mathbb{V}_{m,N}
,

\mathbf{V}C=\mathbf{V}\big|_{E}\,(C\big|E),

where
E=\operatorname{Range}(\mathbf{V}^{\top})
and
C\big|E
is the orthogonal projection of
C
onto
E
. If
\mathbf{x}\in E
, then
\mathbf{x}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}\mathbf{v}_{i}
for some scalars
\lambda_{i}
, where
\{\mathbf{v}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
are the orthonormal rows of
\mathbf{V}
. Hence,
\mathbf{V}\big|_{E}\,\mathbf{x}=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m})
, so
\mathbf{V}\big|_{E}
maps the orthonormal basis
\{\mathbf{v}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
to the standard basis of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. In particular, for any convex body
C\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

\operatorname{vol}_{m}(\mathbf{V}C)\ \overset{d}{=}\ \operatorname{vol}_{m}(C\big|E),

with
\mathbf{V}\sim\operatorname{Unif}(\mathbb{V}_{m,N})
and
E
a uniformly chosen
m
-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, i.e. according to the Haar measure. The
support function
of a convex body
C\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
is

h(C,\mathbf{u}):=\sup_{\mathbf{x}\in C}\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u}\rangle,\qquad\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{S}^{m-1},

and determines
C
uniquely. We write
q
for the Hölder conjugate of
p
, such that
1/p+1/q=1
. It is known that the support function
h(N^{1/p-1/2}\mathbf{V}_{N}\mathbb{B}_{p}^{N},\cdot)
converges almost surely in the uniform norm to the constant
\sqrt{2}\,\Gamma\big(\frac{q+1}{2}\big)^{1/q}\pi^{-1/(2q)}
for fixed
m
as
N\to\infty
which is the support function of the Euclidean ball of radius
\sqrt{2}\,\Gamma\big(\frac{q+1}{2}\big)^{1/q}\pi^{-1/(2q)}
(see
[
33
]
). We will quantify this convergence and give rate function as well as the speed of the convergence (which is of different nature for
p\in(1,2)
and
p\in(2,\infty]
) in the following. We show (see below) that for every

f\in C\Big(\mathbb{S}^{m-1},\ \Big[\frac{\sqrt{2}\,\Gamma\big(\frac{q+1}{2}\big)^{1/q}}{\pi^{1/(2q)}},\,\infty\Big)\Big),

one has for
p\in(1,2)
,

\mathbb{P}\big(h(N^{1/p-1/2}\mathbf{V}_{N}\mathbb{B}_{p}^{N},\cdot)\geq f\big)\approx\exp\big(-N^{2/q}\,\mathcal{J}_{p}(f)\big),

and for
p\in(2,\infty]
,

\mathbb{P}\big(h(N^{1/p-1/2}\mathbf{V}_{N}\mathbb{B}_{p}^{N},\cdot)\geq f\big)\approx\exp\big(-N\,\mathcal{I}_{p}(f)\big),

for certain rate functions
\mathcal{J}_{p}
and
\mathcal{I}_{p}
. Here
\mathcal{I}_{p}
arises from transformations of the Legendre–Fenchel transform in Cramér’s theorem, while
\mathcal{J}_{p}
is a transformed quadratic form which originates from Theorem
A
. Since support functions determine convex bodies, these asymptotics encode all geometric information. Thus, for large
N
, the speed at which the rescaled projections
N^{1/p-1/2}\mathbf{V}_{N}\mathbb{B}_{p}^{N}
approach the Euclidean ball of radius
\sqrt{2}\,\Gamma\big(\frac{q+1}{2}\big)^{1/q}\pi^{-1/(2q)}
is of order
\exp(-c\,N^{2/q})
for
p\in(1,2)
with explicit
c>0
, and depends on the shape of
\mathbb{B}_{p}^{N}
. As
p
is closer to
1
the speed decreases, while for
p
being closer to
2
it increases. In contrast, for
p\in(2,\infty]
the speed is always of order
\exp(-c\,N)
with a constant
c>0
where the speed does not depend on the shape of
\mathbb{B}_{p}^{N}
.

We begin with an extension of the result for absolute powers of multivariate Gaussians. On the large deviation scale at speed
N^{2/q}
, the columns of a Haar-Stiefel matrix behave like standard Gaussian vectors.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\mathbf{V}_{N}\sim\operatorname{Unif}(\mathbb{V}_{m,N})
and let
\mathbf{v}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{v}_{N}
be the columns of
\mathbf{V}_{N}
. Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
q>2
,
t_{1},\ldots,t_{k}>0
, and pairwise distinct
\mathbf{u}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{u}_{k}\in\mathbb{S}^{m-1}
. Then

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N^{2/q}}\log\mathbb{P}\Big(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\langle\sqrt{N}\,\mathbf{v}_{i},\mathbf{u}_{1}\rangle|^{q}-M_{q}\geq t_{1},\ \ldots,\ \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\langle\sqrt{N}\,\mathbf{v}_{i},\mathbf{u}_{k}\rangle|^{q}-M_{q}\geq t_{k}\Big)

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{k}),

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the Euclidean inner product,
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}
is the rate from Theorem
3.3
with
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}=(\langle\mathbf{u}_{i},\mathbf{u}_{j}\rangle)_{1\leq i,j\leq k}
,
M_{q}=\mathbb{E}|G|^{q}=\frac{2^{q/2}\Gamma(\frac{q+1}{2})}{\sqrt{\pi}}
for
G\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
.

The next result gives the large deviation behavior for projected
\ell_{p}^{N}
-balls for
p\in(1,2)
.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
p\in(1,2)
and
\mathbf{V}_{N}\sim\operatorname{Unif}(\mathbb{V}_{m,N})
for all
N\geq m
. Let
q>2
with
1/p+1/q=1
, and let
\{\mathbf{u}_{1},\mathbf{u}_{2},\ldots\}\subseteq\mathbb{S}^{m-1}
be a dense countable set. For every
f\in C(\mathbb{S}^{m-1},[M_{q}^{1/q},\infty))
,

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N^{2/q}}\log\mathbb{P}\big(h(N^{1/p-1/2}\mathbf{V}_{N}\mathbb{B}_{p}^{N},\cdot)\geq f\big)=-\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{J}_{k}\big(f(\mathbf{u}_{1}),\ldots,f(\mathbf{u}_{k})\big),

where for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
we set

\mathcal{J}_{k}(\mathbf{s}):=\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}^{(k)}}\big((\mathbf{s}^{q}-M_{q}\mathbf{1})\big),\qquad\mathbf{s}\in[M_{q}^{1/q},\infty)^{k},

and
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}^{(k)}}
is the rate from Theorem
3.3
with covariance matrix

\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{(k)}:=(\langle\mathbf{u}_{r},\mathbf{u}_{s}\rangle)_{1\leq r,s\leq k}
.

The rate in Theorem
3.6
equals
0
for the constant function
f\equiv M_{q}^{1/q}
and is strictly positive otherwise. By
[
33
, Theorem 2.4]
we have the following: let
p\in(2,\infty]
and
\mathbf{V}_{N}\sim\operatorname{Unif}(\mathbb{V}_{m,N})
. Let
q\in[1,2)
with
1/p+1/q=1
. Then,
(h(N^{1/p-1/2}\mathbf{V}_{N}\mathbb{B}_{p}^{N},\cdot))_{N\geq 1}
satisfies an LDP on
C(\mathbb{S}^{m-1})
with speed
N
and good rate function
\mathcal{I}_{p}
. One representation is

\mathcal{I}_{p}(f)=\inf\Big\{J(\nu):f(\mathbf{u})=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}|\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u}\rangle|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\mathbf{x})\Big)^{1/q}\ \text{for all }\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{S}^{m-1}\Big\},

where
J
is a good convex rate function on probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
(see
[
33
, Theorem 2.4]
; see also
[
22
, Theorem 1]
).

3.3
Further applications and examples

The class of multivariate stretched-exponentials in Definition
2.1
contains several standard models from
reliability, signal processing, extreme-value theory, and high-dimensional statistics. In this section, we give some examples that can be applied to our theory. In each example below, after centering, one checks Definition
2.1
(typically via Lemma
3.1
or by direct evaluation of upper-orthant tails) together with Assumption
(A)
.

•

(Multivariate Weibull tails).

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k}>0
. Following
[
16
]
, a canonical type of multivariate Weibull distribution is specified by the survival function

\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{X}>\mathbf{t})=\exp\Big(-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}t_{j}^{\alpha}-\lambda_{0}\max_{1\leq j\leq k}t_{j}^{\alpha}\Big),\qquad\mathbf{t}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{k}.

Such distributions arise naturally in competing-risks, reliability shock models and have a long
history in dependence modelling through the Marshall–Olkin scheme
[
18
,
29
]
.

•

(Multivariate generalized Gaussian).

Fix
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}
symmetric positive definite, and define the density by

f_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{x})=c_{\alpha,k}|\boldsymbol{\Sigma}|^{-1/2}\exp\Big(-\big(\mathbf{x}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1}\mathbf{x}\big)^{\alpha/2}\Big),\qquad\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{k},

where
c_{\alpha,k}
is a normalization constant. This class is often called multivariate generalized Gaussian distribution and is widely used in model-based clustering and as a parametric model for
wavelet statistics in image processing
[
6
,
14
,
36
]
.

•

(Gaussian scale mixtures).

Let
\mathbf{Z}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\boldsymbol{\Sigma})
and
R\geq 0
independent of
\mathbf{Z}
, and set
\mathbf{X}=R\mathbf{Z}
.
If
R
has Weibull-type tail
\log\mathbb{P}(R>r)\sim-cr^{\alpha}
with
\alpha\in(0,1)
, then
\mathbf{X}
inherits
stretched-exponential right tails (with a rate
\mathcal{J}
determined by the minimal radial scaling needed
to enter the orthant
x\mathbf{t}
). Gaussian scale mixtures are a standard tool in sparse signal modelling
and appear in finance models
[
17
,
37
]
. A related case is covered in
[
15
]
for
\mathbf{Z}
being a random vector on the unit sphere.

•

(Polynomial images of Gaussians).

Let
\mathbf{G}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\operatorname{Id}_{m})
and define

\mathbf{X}=(P_{1}(\mathbf{G}),\dots,P_{k}(\mathbf{G})),

where each
P_{j}
is a non-degenerate homogeneous polynomial of total degree
d\geq 3
. Tail estimates for Gaussian chaoses imply stretched-exponential tails of order
2/d\in(0,1)

for such polynomial observables
[
27
]
. These objects appear in stochastic
analysis (Wiener chaos expansions) and in random matrix theory.

•

(Sub-Weibull vectors in high-dimensional covariance problems).

In high-dimensional statistics one may assume coordinates with Weibull-type tails

\mathbb{P}(|X_{j}|>t)=\exp(-(t/K)^{\alpha})
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
, in order to go beyond sub-Gaussianity (see for example
[
26
]
). Then centered quadratic and bilinear forms (e.g., entries of sample covariance matrices) are sums of i.i.d. stretched-exponential terms, which is a key input for concentration and moderate/large deviation arguments in covariance estimation and regression under sub-Weibull tails.

4
Preliminaries

We introduce some notation and definitions. For a random vector
\mathbf{X}
that is distributed according to a probability measure
\mu
, we write
\mathbf{X}\sim\mu
. The mean of
\mathbf{X}
is denoted by
\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{X}]
and the covariance by
\operatorname{Cov}[\mathbf{X}]
. An open ball of radius
r>0
centered at
\mathbf{x}
in a metric space is denoted by
\mathbb{B}_{r}(\mathbf{x})
. We use both
\mathbf{1}_{A}(\mathbf{x})
and
\mathbf{1}\{\mathbf{x}\in A\}
for the indicator function over some set
A
. For a square matrix
\mathbf{A}
,
\operatorname{Tr}(\mathbf{A})
denotes the trace of
\mathbf{A}
and for a measurable set
B
,
\operatorname{vol}_{m}(B)
is the
m
-dimensional Lebesgue measure of
B
.

Let
\mathbb{X}
be a topological space. A function
I\colon\mathbb{X}\to[0,\infty]
is a
rate function
if all sublevel sets
\{x\in\mathbb{X}:I(x)\leq y\}
are closed for every
y\in\mathbb{R}
. A rate function is
good
if all sublevel sets are compact.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(Z_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
be random elements in a topological space
\mathbb{X}
. The sequence
(Z_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies a
large deviation principle
with speed
s_{N}\to\infty
and rate function
I\colon\mathbb{X}\to[0,\infty]
if

\displaystyle\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{s_{N}}\log\mathbb{P}(Z_{N}\in O)

\displaystyle\geq-\inf_{x\in O}I(x)\quad\text{for all open }O\subseteq\mathbb{X},

(4.1)

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{s_{N}}\log\mathbb{P}(Z_{N}\in C)

\displaystyle\leq-\inf_{x\in C}I(x)\quad\text{for all closed }C\subseteq\mathbb{X}.

(4.2)

Proposition 4.2

(Cramér’s theorem,
[
23
, Chapter 24]
)
.

Let
\mathbf{X},\mathbf{X}_{1},\mathbf{X}_{2},\ldots
be i.i.d. in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Assume the
log-moment generating function

\Lambda(\boldsymbol{\lambda}):=\log\mathbb{E}\big[e^{\langle\boldsymbol{\lambda},\mathbf{X}\rangle}\big],\qquad\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

is finite in a neighborhood of
\mathbf{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then
\big(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{X}_{i}\big)_{N\geq 1}
satisfies an LDP on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with speed
N
and good, convex rate function
\Lambda^{*}
, the
Fenchel–Legendre transform
of
\Lambda
,

\Lambda^{*}(\mathbf{x}):=\sup_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big(\langle\boldsymbol{\lambda},\mathbf{x}\rangle-\Lambda(\boldsymbol{\lambda})\big),\qquad\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Proposition 4.3

(Contraction principle,
[
7
, Theorem 4.2.1]
)
.

Let
\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y}
be Hausdorff topological spaces and
f\colon\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{Y}
continuous. If
(Z_{N})_{N\geq 1}
satisfies an LDP on
\mathbb{X}
with speed
s_{N}
and good rate function
I\colon\mathbb{X}\to[0,\infty]
, then
(f(Z_{N}))_{N\geq 1}
satisfies an LDP on
\mathbb{Y}
with speed
s_{N}
and good rate function
J\colon\mathbb{Y}\to[0,\infty]
given by

J(y)=\inf\{I(x):\ f(x)=y\},\qquad y\in\mathbb{Y}.

By convention,
J(y)=+\infty
if
f^{-1}(\{y\})=\varnothing
.

We state the moderate deviation principle in our notation. See
[
13
, Theorem 4.7]
.

Proposition 4.4

(Gaussian range for the empirical mean, general)
.

Let
\mathbb{X}
be a measurable space and let
X
take values in
\mathbb{X}
with law
\mu
. Let
\mathbb{T}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a parameter set and let
\psi:\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be measurable. Let
(a_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
be such that
a_{N}\to\infty
and
a_{N}/\sqrt{N}\to 0
. For
\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\mathbb{T}
set

\boldsymbol{\Psi}(\boldsymbol{\theta}):=\mathbb{E}[\psi(X,\boldsymbol{\theta})],\qquad\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{N}(\boldsymbol{\theta}):=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\psi(X_{i},\boldsymbol{\theta}),

where
X_{1},\dots,X_{N}
are i.i.d. with law
\mu
. Assume:

(C1) For each
x\in\mathbb{X}
, the map
\boldsymbol{\theta}\mapsto\psi(x,\boldsymbol{\theta})
is continuous. For each
\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\mathbb{T}
, the map
x\mapsto\psi(x,\boldsymbol{\theta})
is measurable.

(C2) There exist
\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0}\in\mathbb{T}
and
\eta>0
such that:

•

\boldsymbol{\Psi}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0})=0
and
\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0}
is the unique zero of
\boldsymbol{\Psi}
in
\mathbb{T}
,

•

\mathbb{B}_{\eta}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0})\subseteq\mathbb{T}
,

•

\boldsymbol{\Psi}
is differentiable at
\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0}
with nonsingular derivative
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
,

•

\boldsymbol{\Psi}
is a homeomorphism on
\mathbb{B}_{\eta}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0})
,

•

\mathbb{E}[\|\psi(X,\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0})\|^{2}]<\infty
.

(C3)

\frac{\sqrt{N}}{a_{N}}\sup_{\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\mathbb{B}_{\eta}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0})}\|\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{N}(\boldsymbol{\theta})-\boldsymbol{\Psi}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\|\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}0,\,\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{a_{N}^{2}}\log\Big(N\,\mathbb{P}\big(\sup_{\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\mathbb{B}_{\eta}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0})}\|\psi(X,\boldsymbol{\theta})\|\geq\sqrt{N}\,a_{N}\big)\Big)=-\infty.

Let
\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{0N}
be the restriction of
\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{N}
to
\mathbb{B}_{\eta}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0})
. Let
\Phi
be the inverse map functional from
[
13
, Lemma 4.1]
and set
\boldsymbol{\theta}_{N}:=\Phi(\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{0N})
. Then

\frac{\sqrt{N}}{a_{N}}\,(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{N}-\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0})

satisfies an LDP with speed
a_{N}^{2}
and good rate function

I(\mathbf{z})=\frac{1}{2}\langle\mathbf{A}\mathbf{z},\boldsymbol{\Gamma}^{-1}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{z}\rangle,

where
\boldsymbol{\Gamma}=\operatorname{Cov}(\psi(X,\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0}))
is invertible.

We deduce the following simplified version of Proposition
4.4
.

Corollary 4.5

(Gaussian range in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
)
.

Let
\mathbf{X},\mathbf{X}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{X}_{N}
be i.i.d. in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
with mean
\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{X}]
and invertible covariance matrix

\boldsymbol{\Sigma}=\operatorname{Cov}(\mathbf{X})
. Let
(a_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
be such that
a_{N}\to\infty
and
a_{N}/\sqrt{N}\to 0
. Assume

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{a_{N}^{2}}\log\Big(N\,\mathbb{P}\big(\|\mathbf{X}\|\geq\sqrt{N}\,a_{N}\big)\Big)=-\infty.

(4.3)

Then

\frac{1}{a_{N}\sqrt{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\mathbf{X}_{i}-\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{X}])

satisfies an LDP with speed
a_{N}^{2}
and good rate function
I(\mathbf{z})=\frac{1}{2}\langle\mathbf{z},\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1}\mathbf{z}\rangle
.

Condition (
4.3
) is weaker than the existence of exponential moments and thus suited for stretched-exponential tails.

5
Proofs

In this section, we provide all the proofs. We start with some preliminary lemmas. After that we give the proof of the main result and we end the section with proofs of the applications.

5.1
Preliminary lemmas

We first show that the function
\mathcal{J}
in Definition
2.1
is always
\alpha
-homogeneous.

Lemma 5.1
.

If
\mathbf{Y}
satisfies Definition
2.1
with rate
(\mathcal{J},\alpha)
, then
\mathcal{J}
is
\alpha
-homogeneous.

Proof.

Fix
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{k}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
and
\lambda>0
. By Definition
2.1
, we get

\displaystyle-\mathcal{J}(\lambda\mathbf{u})

\displaystyle=\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{1}{x^{\alpha}}\log\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{Y}\geq x\lambda\mathbf{u})=\lim_{y\to\infty}\frac{1}{(y/\lambda)^{\alpha}}\log\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{Y}\geq y\mathbf{u})

\displaystyle=\lambda^{\alpha}\lim_{y\to\infty}\frac{1}{y^{\alpha}}\log\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{Y}\geq y\mathbf{u})=-\lambda^{\alpha}\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{u}),

hence
\mathcal{J}(\lambda\mathbf{u})=\lambda^{\alpha}\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{u})
.
∎

As the following lemma shows, the random variables that have stretched-exponential tails in the sense of Definition
2.1
always have second moments.

Lemma 5.2

(Marginal tails and finite second moments)
.

If
\mathbf{Y}
satisfies
Definition

2.1
and Assumption
(A)
, then there exist constants
c,C>0
such that for all
j\in\{1,\dots,k\}
,
\mathbb{P}(|Y_{j}|>t)\leq ce^{-Ct^{\alpha}}
for
t
large enough and, in particular,
\mathbb{E}[Y_{j}^{2}]<\infty
.

Proof of Lemma
5.2
.

For
t\to\infty
and
\mathbf{e}_{j}
the
j
-th unit vector, consider

\mathbf{v}^{(j)}(t):=(-t,\ldots,-t,t,-t,\ldots,-t)\in\mathbb{R}^{k}.

Then
(\mathbf{v}^{(j)}(t))_{+}=t\mathbf{e}_{j}
and
\mathbf{v}^{(j)}(t)=t\,\mathbf{v}^{(j)}(1)
. Hence, by
Definition

2.1
,

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t^{\alpha}}\log\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{Y}\geq\mathbf{v}^{(j)}(t))=-\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{e}_{j}).

Set
d_{j}:=\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{e}_{j})>0
. Therefore, for all large
t
,

\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{Y}\geq\mathbf{v}^{(j)}(t))\leq\exp\Big(-\frac{d_{j}}{2}\,t^{\alpha}\Big).

Moreover,

\mathbb{P}(Y_{j}\geq t)\leq\mathbb{P}\Big(Y_{j}\geq t,\ \forall\ell\neq j:\ Y_{\ell}\geq-t\Big)+\sum_{\ell\neq j}\mathbb{P}(Y_{\ell}\leq-t).

The first term is bounded by
\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{Y}\geq\mathbf{v}^{(j)}(t))
, and Assumption
(A)
yields

\mathbb{P}(Y_{\ell}\leq-t)\leq\exp(-c_{\ell}t^{\alpha})
for all large
t
. Since
k<\infty
, there exist
t_{0}\geq 1
and
constants
C_{+},C_{-}>0
such that for all
t\geq t_{0}
and all
j
,

\mathbb{P}(Y_{j}\geq t)\leq e^{-C_{+}t^{\alpha}},\qquad\mathbb{P}(Y_{j}\leq-t)\leq e^{-C_{-}t^{\alpha}}.

Hence for
t\geq t_{0}
and all
j
,
\mathbb{P}(|Y_{j}|>t)\leq 2e^{-Ct^{\alpha}}
with
C:=\min\{C_{+},C_{-}\}
, which implies

\mathbb{E}[Y_{j}^{2}]<\infty
.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{J}:\mathbb{R}^{k}_{+}\to[0,\infty)
be as in Definition
2.1
and recall the definition of
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}
in (
2.2
). Then, for every

\mathbf{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}_{+}
,

\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}(\mathbf{t})=\min_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\inf\Big\{\sum_{r=1}^{m}\mathcal{J}\big(\mathbf{t}^{(r)}\big):\mathbf{t}^{(r)}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}_{+},\ \sum_{r=1}^{m}\mathbf{t}^{(r)}\geq\mathbf{t}\Big\}

(5.1)

Further,
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}
is nondecreasing in each component.

Proof.

First, we note that
\mathcal{J}
is lower semicontinuous, componentwise nondecreasing, and
\alpha
-homogeneous (for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
, see Lemma
5.1
). Given
\mathbf{t}^{(1)},\dots,\mathbf{t}^{(m)}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}_{+}
with
\sum_{r=1}^{m}\mathbf{t}^{(r)}\geq\mathbf{t}
, define recursively

\mathbf{l}^{(1)}:=\mathbf{t}
and

\mathbf{u}^{(r)}:=\min\{\mathbf{t}^{(r)},\mathbf{l}^{(r)}\},\qquad\mathbf{l}^{(r+1)}:=\mathbf{l}^{(r)}-\mathbf{u}^{(r)},\qquad r=1,\dots,m,

where minima are componentwise. Then
\mathbf{u}^{(r)}\leq\mathbf{t}^{(r)}
and
\sum_{r=1}^{m}\mathbf{u}^{(r)}=\mathbf{t}
by construction (using an induction argument), hence

\sum_{r=1}^{m}\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{u}^{(r)})\leq\sum_{r=1}^{m}\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{t}^{(r)})
.
Therefore we can assume equality in the r.h.s. of (
5.1
).
Now, fix
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbf{t}^{(1)},\dots,\mathbf{t}^{(m)}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}_{+}
with
\sum_{r=1}^{m}\mathbf{t}^{(r)}=\mathbf{t}
.
Let
\widehat{a}_{r}:=\|\mathbf{t}^{(r)}\|_{1}
and set
\mathbf{e}_{1}:=(1,0,\dots,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
. Define

\mathbf{u}^{(r)}:=\mathbf{t}^{(r)}/\widehat{a}_{r}
if
\widehat{a}_{r}>0
, and
\mathbf{u}^{(r)}:=\mathbf{e}_{1}
if
\widehat{a}_{r}=0
.
Then
\|\mathbf{u}^{(r)}\|_{1}=1
and
\mathbf{t}^{(r)}=\widehat{a}_{r}\mathbf{u}^{(r)}
for all
r
, hence by
\alpha
-homogeneity,

\sum_{r=1}^{m}\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{t}^{(r)})=\sum_{r=1}^{m}\widehat{a}_{r}^{\alpha}\,\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{u}^{(r)})
. Consider the convex polytope

P:=\Big\{\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\in[0,\infty)^{m}:\ \sum_{r=1}^{m}a_{r}\mathbf{u}^{(r)}=\mathbf{t}\Big\},

and
F:P\to[0,\infty)
given by
F(\mathbf{a}):=\sum_{r=1}^{m}a_{r}^{\alpha}\,\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{u}^{(r)})
.
Note that
\widehat{\mathbf{a}}:=(\widehat{a}_{1},\dots,\widehat{a}_{m})\in P
and
F(\widehat{\mathbf{a}})=\sum_{r=1}^{m}\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{t}^{(r)})
.
The function
F
is concave, hence there exists an extreme point
\mathbf{a}^{\star}\in P
with
F(\mathbf{a}^{\star})\leq F(\widehat{\mathbf{a}})
.
If
\mathbf{a}^{\star}
has more than
k
positive components, then the corresponding vectors
\mathbf{u}^{(r)}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}

(with
a_{r}^{\star}>0
) are linearly dependent, so there exists a nonzero
\mathbf{d}=(d_{1},\dots,d_{m})
with

d_{r}=0
whenever
a_{r}^{\star}=0
and
\sum_{r=1}^{m}d_{r}\mathbf{u}^{(r)}=0
. For any
\varepsilon>0
such that

\mathbf{a}^{\star}\pm\varepsilon\mathbf{d}\in[0,\infty)^{m}
, we have

\mathbf{a}^{\star}\pm\varepsilon\mathbf{d}\in P
, contradicting extremality of
\mathbf{a}^{\star}
. Hence
\mathbf{a}^{\star}
has at most
k

positive components. Writing these indices as
r_{1},\dots,r_{\ell}
with
\ell\leq k
and setting

\widetilde{}\mathbf{t}^{(j)}:=a_{r_{j}}^{\star}\,\mathbf{u}^{(r_{j})}
, we obtain
\mathbf{t}=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\widetilde{}\mathbf{t}^{(j)}
and

\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\mathcal{J}(\widetilde{}\mathbf{t}^{(j)})=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}(a_{r_{j}}^{\star})^{\alpha}\,\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{u}^{(r_{j})})=F(\mathbf{a}^{\star})\leq F(\widehat{\mathbf{a}})=\sum_{r=1}^{m}\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{t}^{(r)}).

Since
\mathcal{J}
is lower semicontinuous, it attains its infimum on the compact set. The fact that
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}
is nondecreasing follows from the fact that
\mathcal{J}
is nondecreasing and by (
2.2
).
∎

5.2
Proof of Theorem
A

In this section, we provide the proof of the main result, starting with the lower bound.

Lemma 5.4

(Lower bound of Theorem
A
)
.

In the setup of Theorem
A
,

\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{x_{N}^{\alpha}}\log\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{x_{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{X}_{i}\geq\mathbf{t}\right)\geq-\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}(\mathbf{t}).

Proof.

Write
\mathbf{S}_{N}:=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{X}_{i}
.
Fix
\delta\in(0,1)
.
We prove

\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{x_{N}^{\alpha}}\log\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{S}_{N}\geq x_{N}\mathbf{t})\geq-\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}((1+\delta)\mathbf{t}),

(5.2)

and then let
\delta\downarrow 0
using the
\alpha
-homogeneity of
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}
. For every fixed
\mathbf{t}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{k}
,

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{1}{x^{\alpha}}\log\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{X}_{1}\geq x\mathbf{t})=-\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{t}).

(5.3)

Fix
\varepsilon>0
. By Lemma
5.3
, there exist
m\in\mathbb{N}
and vectors

\mathbf{u}^{(1)},\dots,\mathbf{u}^{(m)}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{k}
such that

\sum_{r=1}^{m}\mathbf{u}^{(r)}\geq(1+\delta)\mathbf{t},\qquad\sum_{r=1}^{m}\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{u}^{(r)})\leq\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}((1+\delta)\mathbf{t})+\varepsilon.

(5.4)

Define the events

A_{N}:=\bigcap_{r=1}^{m}\{\mathbf{X}_{r}\geq x_{N}\mathbf{u}^{(r)}\},\qquad B_{N}:=\Big\{\sum_{i=m+1}^{N}\mathbf{X}_{i}\geq-\delta x_{N}\mathbf{t}\Big\}.

On
A_{N}\cap B_{N}
,

\mathbf{S}_{N}=\sum_{r=1}^{m}\mathbf{X}_{r}+\sum_{i=m+1}^{N}\mathbf{X}_{i}\geq x_{N}\sum_{r=1}^{m}\mathbf{u}^{(r)}-\delta x_{N}\mathbf{t}\geq x_{N}\mathbf{t}

by (
5.4
). Hence,

\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{S}_{N}\geq x_{N}\mathbf{t})\geq\mathbb{P}(A_{N}\cap B_{N})=\mathbb{P}(A_{N})\mathbb{P}(B_{N}),

(5.5)

where the last equality uses independence. By (
5.3
),

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{x_{N}^{\alpha}}\log\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{X}_{1}\geq x_{N}\mathbf{u}^{(r)})=-\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{u}^{(r)}),\qquad r=1,\dots,m.

Therefore,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{x_{N}^{\alpha}}\log\mathbb{P}(A_{N})=\sum_{r=1}^{m}\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{x_{N}^{\alpha}}\log\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{X}_{1}\geq x_{N}\mathbf{u}^{(r)})=-\sum_{r=1}^{m}\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{u}^{(r)}).

(5.6)

By Slutsky’s theorem and the central limit theorem,
\mathbb{P}(B_{N})\to 1
, and so

\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{x_{N}^{\alpha}}\log\mathbb{P}(B_{N})=0,

(5.7)

since the random variables are centered, have finite second moments (see Lemma
5.2
) and
x_{N}/\sqrt{N}\to\infty
.
Combining (
5.5
), (
5.6
) and (
5.7
),

\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{x_{N}^{\alpha}}\log\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{S}_{N}\geq x_{N}\mathbf{t})\geq-\sum_{r=1}^{m}\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{u}^{(r)}).

Using (
5.4
) and then letting
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
yields (
5.2
). Finally, by
\alpha
-homogeneity of
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}
, letting
\delta\downarrow 0
and taking the supremum over
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbf{u}^{(1)},\dots,\mathbf{u}^{(m)}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{k}
such that
\sum_{r=1}^{m}\mathbf{u}^{(r)}\geq(1+\delta)\mathbf{t}
gives the claimed lower bound (see Lemma
5.3
).
∎

We continue with the proof of the upper bound and start with a preliminary lemma.

Lemma 5.5
.

Fix
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\mathbf{t}\in(0,\infty)^{k}
. Let
(\mathbf{X}_{i})_{i\geq 1}
be i.i.d. centered random vectors with values in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
and assume that
\mathbf{X}_{1}
satisfies
Definition

2.1
with rate
(\mathcal{J},\alpha)
and Assumption
(A)
. Let
(x_{N})_{N\geq 1}
be a sequence of positive numbers such that
x_{N}\to\infty
and
x_{N}N^{-1/(2-\alpha)}\to\infty
. Then

\limsup_{\varepsilon_{0}\downarrow 0}\ \limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{x_{N}^{\alpha}}\log\mathbb{P}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{X}_{i}\geq x_{N}\mathbf{t},\ \forall i\leq N:\ \mathbf{X}_{i}\leq\varepsilon_{0}x_{N}\mathbf{t}\Big)=-\infty.

Proof.

Fix
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,1)
and set

A_{N}(\varepsilon_{0}):=\Big\{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{X}_{i}\geq x_{N}\mathbf{t},\ \forall i\leq N:\ \mathbf{X}_{i}\leq\varepsilon_{0}x_{N}\mathbf{t}\Big\}.

Then
A_{N}(\varepsilon_{0})\subseteq\bigcap_{j=1}^{k}A_{N,j}(\varepsilon_{0})
, where

A_{N,j}(\varepsilon_{0}):=\Big\{\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i,j}\geq x_{N}t_{j},\ \forall i\leq N:\ X_{i,j}\leq\varepsilon_{0}x_{N}t_{j}\Big\}.

Hence,
\mathbb{P}(A_{N}(\varepsilon_{0}))\leq\min_{1\leq j\leq k}\mathbb{P}(A_{N,j}(\varepsilon_{0}))
.
It suffices to show that for each fixed
j
there exists
c_{*}=c_{*}(j)>0
such that

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{x_{N}^{\alpha}}\log\mathbb{P}(A_{N,j}(\varepsilon_{0}))\leq-\frac{c_{*}}{\varepsilon_{0}^{1-\alpha}}.

(5.8)

Letting
\varepsilon_{0}\downarrow 0
then yields the claim. Fix
j
and write
\xi_{i}:=X_{i,j}
and
t:=t_{j}
. Set
b_{N}:=\varepsilon_{0}x_{N}t
, so that

\mathbb{P}(A_{N,j}(\varepsilon_{0}))=\mathbb{P}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\xi_{i}\geq x_{N}t,\ \forall i\leq N:\ \xi_{i}\leq b_{N}\Big).

By Lemma
5.2
, there exist constants
c,C>0
such that

\mathbb{P}(\xi_{1}>x)\leq c\,e^{-Cx^{\alpha}},\qquad x\geq 0.

(5.9)

Fix
\eta\in(0,C)
. By Markov’s inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(A_{N,j}(\varepsilon_{0}))

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\Big(\exp\Big(\eta b_{N}^{\alpha-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\xi_{i}\Big)\geq\exp\big(\eta b_{N}^{\alpha-1}x_{N}t\big),\ \forall i\leq N:\ \xi_{i}\leq b_{N}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\big(-\eta b_{N}^{\alpha-1}x_{N}t\big)\Big(\mathbb{E}\big[\exp(\eta b_{N}^{\alpha-1}\xi_{1})\mathbf{1}_{\{\xi_{1}\leq b_{N}\}}\big]\Big)^{N}.

(5.10)

Moreover,

\eta b_{N}^{\alpha-1}x_{N}t=\eta(\varepsilon_{0}x_{N}t)^{\alpha-1}x_{N}t=\frac{\eta t^{\alpha}}{\varepsilon_{0}^{1-\alpha}}\,x_{N}^{\alpha}.

(5.11)

We bound the truncated moment generating function. Using the identity

e^{y}=1+y+y^{2}\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)e^{sy}\,ds,

with
y=\eta b_{N}^{\alpha-1}\xi_{1}
,
multiplying by
\mathbf{1}_{\{\xi_{1}\leq b_{N}\}}
and taking expectations gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[e^{\eta b_{N}^{\alpha-1}\xi_{1}}\mathbf{1}_{\{\xi_{1}\leq b_{N}\}}\big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\big[\mathbf{1}_{\{\xi_{1}\leq b_{N}\}}\big]+\eta b_{N}^{\alpha-1}\mathbb{E}\big[\xi_{1}\mathbf{1}_{\{\xi_{1}\leq b_{N}\}}\big]

\displaystyle\quad+\eta^{2}b_{N}^{2\alpha-2}\mathbb{E}\Big[\xi_{1}^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\{\xi_{1}\leq b_{N}\}}\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)e^{s\eta b_{N}^{\alpha-1}\xi_{1}}\,ds\Big].

(5.12)

Since
\mathbb{E}[\xi_{1}]=0
,

\mathbb{E}\big[\xi_{1}\mathbf{1}_{\{\xi_{1}\leq b_{N}\}}\big]=-\mathbb{E}\big[\xi_{1}\mathbf{1}_{\{\xi_{1}>b_{N}\}}\big]\leq 0,

hence

\mathbb{E}\big[e^{\eta b_{N}^{\alpha-1}\xi_{1}}\mathbf{1}_{\{\xi_{1}\leq b_{N}\}}\big]\leq 1+\eta^{2}b_{N}^{2\alpha-2}\mathbb{E}\Big[\xi_{1}^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\{\xi_{1}\leq b_{N}\}}\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)e^{s\eta b_{N}^{\alpha-1}\xi_{1}}\,ds\Big].

(5.13)

For
\xi_{1}\leq 0
we have
e^{s\eta b_{N}^{\alpha-1}\xi_{1}}\leq 1
, hence

\mathbb{E}\Big[\xi_{1}^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\{\xi_{1}\leq 0\}}\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)e^{s\eta b_{N}^{\alpha-1}\xi_{1}}\,ds\Big]\leq\mathbb{E}[\xi_{1}^{2}]<\infty.

For
0<\xi_{1}\leq b_{N}
, fix
s\in[0,1]
and use integration by parts for Stieltjes integrals to obtain

\mathbb{E}\big[\xi_{1}^{2}e^{s\eta b_{N}^{\alpha-1}\xi_{1}}\mathbf{1}_{\{0<\xi_{1}\leq b_{N}\}}\big]\leq\int_{0}^{b_{N}}\big(2x+s\eta b_{N}^{\alpha-1}x^{2}\big)e^{s\eta b_{N}^{\alpha-1}x}\,\mathbb{P}(\xi_{1}>x)\,dx.

(5.14)

Since
\alpha-1<0
and
0<x\leq b_{N}
, we have
b_{N}^{\alpha-1}\leq x^{\alpha-1}
, hence

s\eta b_{N}^{\alpha-1}x\leq\eta x^{\alpha},\qquad s\eta b_{N}^{\alpha-1}x^{2}\leq\eta x^{\alpha+1}.

Using (
5.9
) in (
5.14
) yields

\mathbb{E}\big[\xi_{1}^{2}e^{s\eta b_{N}^{\alpha-1}\xi_{1}}\mathbf{1}_{\{0<\xi_{1}\leq b_{N}\}}\big]\leq c\int_{0}^{\infty}(2x+\eta x^{\alpha+1})e^{-(C-\eta)x^{\alpha}}\,dx=:C_{1}<\infty,

uniformly in
N
and
s\in[0,1]
. Therefore, for some
C_{2}<\infty
and all large
N
,

\mathbb{E}\Big[\xi_{1}^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\{\xi_{1}\leq b_{N}\}}\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)e^{s\eta b_{N}^{\alpha-1}\xi_{1}}\,ds\Big]\leq C_{2},

and (
5.13
) gives

\mathbb{E}\big[e^{\eta b_{N}^{\alpha-1}\xi_{1}}\mathbf{1}_{\{\xi_{1}\leq b_{N}\}}\big]\leq 1+C_{2}\eta^{2}b_{N}^{2\alpha-2},\qquad\log\mathbb{E}\big[e^{\eta b_{N}^{\alpha-1}\xi_{1}}\mathbf{1}_{\{\xi_{1}\leq b_{N}\}}\big]\leq C_{2}\eta^{2}b_{N}^{2\alpha-2},

for all large
N
. Combining with (
5.10
) and (
5.11
) yields

\frac{1}{x_{N}^{\alpha}}\log\mathbb{P}(A_{N,j}(\varepsilon_{0}))\leq-\frac{\eta t^{\alpha}}{\varepsilon_{0}^{1-\alpha}}+C_{2}\eta^{2}\frac{Nb_{N}^{2\alpha-2}}{x_{N}^{\alpha}}.

Since
b_{N}^{2\alpha-2}=(\varepsilon_{0}t)^{2\alpha-2}x_{N}^{2\alpha-2}
,

\frac{Nb_{N}^{2\alpha-2}}{x_{N}^{\alpha}}=(\varepsilon_{0}t)^{2\alpha-2}\frac{N}{x_{N}^{2-\alpha}}\longrightarrow 0\qquad(N\to\infty),

because
x_{N}N^{-1/(2-\alpha)}\to\infty
. Thus (
5.8
) holds with
c_{*}=\eta t_{j}^{\alpha}
.
Letting
\varepsilon_{0}\downarrow 0
completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.6

(Upper bound of Theorem
A
)
.

In the setup of Theorem
A
,

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{x_{N}^{\alpha}}\log\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{x_{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{X}_{i}\geq\mathbf{t}\right)\leq-\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}(\mathbf{t}).

Proof.

Fix
\mathbf{t}=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{k})\in(0,\infty)^{k}
and recall the rate function
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}
from (
2.2
) and (
5.1
). Write

\mathbb{P}\Big(\frac{1}{x_{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{X}_{i}\geq\mathbf{t}\Big)=\mathbb{P}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{X}_{i}\geq x_{N}\mathbf{t}\Big).

By Lemma
5.2
there exist
c>0
and, for each
j
, some
u_{j}>0
such that for all
u\geq u_{j}
,

\mathbb{P}(|X_{1,j}|\geq u)\leq 2e^{-cu^{\alpha}}
.
Moreover,
x_{N}N^{-1/(2-\alpha)}\to\infty
implies
\log(Nk)=o(x_{N}^{\alpha})
.
Hence we can choose numbers
\lambda_{j}>1
such that for all large
N
,

Nk\max_{1\leq j\leq k}\mathbb{P}\Big(\frac{|X_{1,j}|}{t_{j}}\geq\lambda_{j}x_{N}\Big)\leq e^{-\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}(\mathbf{t})\,x_{N}^{\alpha}}.

(5.15)

Let
\lambda:=\max_{1\leq j\leq k}\lambda_{j}
and define the event

\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{box}}:=\Big\{\forall\,i\leq N,\ \forall\,j\leq k:\ \frac{X_{i,j}}{t_{j}}\in[-\lambda x_{N},\lambda x_{N}]\Big\}.

Then

\mathbb{P}\Big(\frac{1}{x_{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{X}_{i}\geq\mathbf{t}\Big)\leq\mathbb{P}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{X}_{i}\geq x_{N}\mathbf{t},\ \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{box}}\Big)+\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{box}}^{\mathrm{c}}).

(5.16)

By (
5.15
) and a union bound,

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{x_{N}^{\alpha}}\log\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{box}}^{\mathrm{c}})\leq-\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}(\mathbf{t}).

(5.17)

Hence, it remains to bound the first term in (
5.16
).
Fix
\delta\in(0,1/4)
and
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,1)
, and set

\mathcal{E}_{\leq}:=\Big\{\forall i\leq N:\ -\lambda x_{N}\mathbf{t}\leq\mathbf{X}_{i}\leq\varepsilon_{0}x_{N}\mathbf{t}\Big\}.

Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{X}_{i}\geq x_{N}\mathbf{t},\ \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{box}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{X}_{i}\geq x_{N}\mathbf{t},\ \mathcal{E}_{\leq}\Big)+\mathbb{P}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{X}_{i}\geq x_{N}\mathbf{t},\ \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{box}}\cap\mathcal{E}_{\leq}^{\mathrm{c}}\Big).

(5.18)

By Lemma
5.5
,

\limsup_{\varepsilon_{0}\downarrow 0}\ \limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{x_{N}^{\alpha}}\log\mathbb{P}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{X}_{i}\geq x_{N}\mathbf{t},\ \mathcal{E}_{\leq}\Big)=-\infty.

(5.19)

For the second term in (
5.18
), on
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{box}}\cap\mathcal{E}_{\leq}^{\mathrm{c}}
define

\Gamma:=\Big\{i\leq N:\ \mathbf{X}_{i}\not\leq\varepsilon_{0}x_{N}\mathbf{t}\Big\}\text{ and note }|\Gamma|\geq 1.

Fix
\varepsilon_{0}
and choose
M=M(\varepsilon_{0})\in\mathbb{N}
so large that

(M+1)c(\varepsilon_{0}\min_{1\leq j\leq k}t_{j})^{\alpha}\ \geq\ \mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}(\mathbf{t})+1,

(5.20)

where
c>0
is as in Lemma
5.2
. For
i\in\Gamma
, we have that there is
j
such that
X_{i,j}>\varepsilon_{0}x_{N}t_{j}
, and so we obtain for all large
N
,

\mathbb{P}(|\Gamma|\geq M+1)\leq\binom{N}{M+1}\Big(k\max_{1\leq j\leq k}\mathbb{P}(X_{1,j}>\varepsilon_{0}x_{N}t_{j})\Big)^{M+1},

hence, using
\log N=o(x_{N}^{\alpha})
, Lemma
5.2
and (
5.20
),

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{x_{N}^{\alpha}}\log\mathbb{P}(|\Gamma|\geq M+1)\leq-\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}(\mathbf{t})-1.

(5.21)

Consequently,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{X}_{i}\geq x_{N}\mathbf{t},\ \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{box}}\cap\mathcal{E}_{\leq}^{\mathrm{c}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}(|\Gamma|\geq M+1)+\sum_{m=1}^{M}\mathbb{P}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{X}_{i}\geq x_{N}\mathbf{t},\ \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{box}},\ |\Gamma|=m\Big).

(5.22)

Fix
m\in\{1,\ldots,M\}
and a set
A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}
with
|A|=m
.
On
\{\Gamma=A\}\cap\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{box}}
we have
\forall i\notin A:\ -\lambda x_{N}\mathbf{t}\leq\mathbf{X}_{i}\leq\varepsilon_{0}x_{N}\mathbf{t}
.
Write

\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{sm}}:=\sum_{i\notin A}\mathbf{X}_{i},\qquad\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{bg}}:=\sum_{i\in A}\mathbf{X}_{i}.

If
\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{sm}}+\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{bg}}\geq x_{N}\mathbf{t}
and
(\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{sm}})_{j}<\delta x_{N}t_{j}
for all
j
, then

\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{bg}}=(\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{sm}}+\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{bg}})-\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{sm}}\geq x_{N}\mathbf{t}-\delta x_{N}\mathbf{t}=(1-\delta)x_{N}\mathbf{t}.

Hence,

\{\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{sm}}+\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{bg}}\geq x_{N}\mathbf{t}\}\subseteq\Big(\bigcup_{j=1}^{k}\{(\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{sm}})_{j}\geq\delta x_{N}t_{j}\}\Big)\ \cup\ \{\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{bg}}\geq(1-\delta)x_{N}\mathbf{t}\}.

(5.23)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{X}_{i}\geq x_{N}\mathbf{t},\ \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{box}},\ \Gamma=A\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mathbb{P}\Big(\sum_{i\notin A}X_{i,j}\geq\delta x_{N}t_{j},\ \forall i\notin A:\ -\lambda x_{N}t_{j}\leq X_{i,j}\leq\varepsilon_{0}x_{N}t_{j}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{P}\Big(\sum_{i\in A}\mathbf{X}_{i}\geq(1-\delta)x_{N}\mathbf{t},\ \forall i\leq N:\ -\lambda x_{N}\mathbf{t}\leq\mathbf{X}_{i}\leq\lambda x_{N}\mathbf{t}\Big).

(5.24)

For each fixed
j
, apply Lemma
5.5
to the
j
-th probability above to obtain (using
[
7
, Lemma 1.2.15]
and
|A^{c}|=N-m
),

\limsup_{\varepsilon_{0}\downarrow 0}\ \limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{x_{N}^{\alpha}}\log\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mathbb{P}\Big(\sum_{i\notin A}X_{i,j}\geq\delta x_{N}t_{j},\ \forall i\notin A:\ -\lambda x_{N}t_{j}\leq X_{i,j}\leq\varepsilon_{0}x_{N}t_{j}\Big)=-\infty.

(5.25)

We now treat the second term in (
5.24
).
Fix
\rho>0
such that

M\rho\,\mathbf{1}\ \leq\ \delta\mathbf{t}.

(5.26)

Let

\mathcal{D}_{\rho}:=\prod_{j=1}^{k}\{-\lambda t_{j}+\ell\rho:\ \ell=0,1,\ldots,\lfloor 2\lambda t_{j}/\rho\rfloor\}\subseteq[-\lambda\mathbf{t},\lambda\mathbf{t}].

For
\mathbf{y}\in[-\lambda\mathbf{t},\lambda\mathbf{t}]
define

by

so that
\pi_{\rho}(\mathbf{y})\leq\mathbf{y}<\pi_{\rho}(\mathbf{y})+\rho\mathbf{1}
.
If

where we used (
5.26
). Consequently,

\displaystyle\Big\{\sum_{i\in A}\mathbf{X}_{i}\geq(1-\delta)x_{N}\mathbf{t},\ \forall i\leq N:\ -\lambda x_{N}\mathbf{t}\leq\mathbf{X}_{i}\leq\lambda x_{N}\mathbf{t}\Big\}

\displaystyle\subseteq\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\mathbf{d}^{(i)})_{i\in A}\in\mathcal{D}_{\rho}^{\,m}\\
\sum_{i\in A}\mathbf{d}^{(i)}\geq(1-2\delta)\mathbf{t}\end{subarray}}\ \bigcap_{i\in A}\Big\{\mathbf{X}_{i}\geq x_{N}\mathbf{d}^{(i)},\ \forall\ell\leq N:\ -\lambda x_{N}\mathbf{t}\leq\mathbf{X}_{\ell}\leq\lambda x_{N}\mathbf{t}\Big\}.

(5.27)

Since
\mathcal{D}_{\rho}
is finite,
\log|\mathcal{D}_{\rho}|=O(1)
. Fix
m\in\{1,\ldots,M\}
and a choice
(\mathbf{d}^{(i)})_{i\in A}\in\mathcal{D}_{\rho}^{\,m}
.
Dropping the box constraint and using independence, we get

\mathbb{P}\Big(\bigcap_{i\in A}\big\{\mathbf{X}_{i}\geq x_{N}\mathbf{d}^{(i)},\ \forall\ell\leq N:\ -\lambda x_{N}\mathbf{t}\leq\mathbf{X}_{\ell}\leq\lambda x_{N}\mathbf{t}\big\}\Big)\leq\prod_{i\in A}\mathbb{P}\big(\mathbf{X}_{1}\geq x_{N}\mathbf{d}^{(i)}\big).

By Definition

,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{x_{N}^{\alpha}}\log\mathbb{P}\big(\mathbf{X}_{1}\geq x_{N}\mathbf{d}^{(i)}\big)=-\mathcal{J}\big((\mathbf{d}^{(i)})_{+}\big),

whenever

, while if

we trivially have

.
Thus, for every

and all

large enough,

\mathbb{P}\Big(\bigcap_{i\in A}\big\{\mathbf{X}_{i}\geq x_{N}\mathbf{d}^{(i)},\ \forall\ell\leq N:\ -\lambda x_{N}\mathbf{t}\leq\mathbf{X}_{\ell}\leq\lambda x_{N}\mathbf{t}\big\}\Big)\leq\exp\Big(-(1-\eta)x_{N}^{\alpha}\sum_{i\in A}\mathcal{J}\big((\mathbf{d}^{(i)})_{+}\big)\Big).

Applying this bound in (

), taking logarithms, dividing by
x_{N}^{\alpha}
, and using

and
\log\binom{N}{m}=o(x_{N}^{\alpha})
yields, after letting

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{x_{N}^{\alpha}}\log\mathbb{P}\Big(\sum_{i\in A}\mathbf{X}_{i}\geq(1-\delta)x_{N}\mathbf{t},\ \forall i\leq N:\ -\lambda x_{N}\mathbf{t}\leq\mathbf{X}_{i}\leq\lambda x_{N}\mathbf{t}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq-\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\mathbf{d}^{(i)})_{i\in A}\in\mathcal{D}_{\rho}^{\,m}\\
\sum_{i\in A}\mathbf{d}^{(i)}\geq(1-2\delta)\mathbf{t}\end{subarray}}\ \sum_{i\in A}\mathcal{J}\big((\mathbf{d}^{(i)})_{+}\big).

(5.28)

If

. Therefore,

\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\mathbf{d}^{(i)})_{i\in A}\in\mathcal{D}_{\rho}^{\,m}\\

Taking the infimum over
m\in\mathbb{N}
yields by Lemma
5.3
,

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{x_{N}^{\alpha}}\log\sum_{m=1}^{M}\ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}\\
|A|=m\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\Big(\sum_{i\in A}\mathbf{X}_{i}\geq(1-\delta)x_{N}\mathbf{t},\ \forall i\leq N:\ -\lambda x_{N}\mathbf{t}\leq\mathbf{X}_{i}\leq\lambda x_{N}\mathbf{t}\Big)

(5.29)

\displaystyle\leq-\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}\big((1-2\delta)\mathbf{t}\big).

(5.30)

Combining (
5.18

5.19
), (
5.22
), (
5.21
), (
5.24

5.25
) and (

\limsup_{\varepsilon_{0}\downarrow 0}\ \limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{x_{N}^{\alpha}}\log\mathbb{P}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{X}_{i}\geq x_{N}\mathbf{t},\ \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{box}}\Big)\leq-\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}\big((1-2\delta)\mathbf{t}\big).

(5.31)

Since

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

Fix
\mathbf{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
with at least one strictly positive component and set

m(\mathbf{t}):=\inf_{\mathbf{s}\geq\mathbf{t}_{+}}\overline{\mathcal{J}}(\mathbf{s})\in(0,\infty].

Lower bound.

Fix
\delta\in(0,1)
. Choose
\mathbf{s}^{\delta}\geq\mathbf{t}_{+}
such that
\overline{\mathcal{J}}(\mathbf{s}^{\delta})\leq m(\mathbf{t})+\delta
and, in addition,
\mathbf{s}^{\delta}
is a continuity point of
\overline{\mathcal{J}}
(this is possible since
\overline{\mathcal{J}}
is lower semicontinuous, hence continuous on a dense set).
By continuity of
\overline{\mathcal{J}}
at
\mathbf{s}^{\delta}
, there exists
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{\delta}>0
such that for the box
B^{\delta}:=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{k}:\mathbf{s}^{\delta}\leq\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{s}^{\delta}+\varepsilon\}

\sup_{\mathbf{u}\in B^{\delta}}\overline{\mathcal{J}}(\mathbf{u})\leq\overline{\mathcal{J}}(\mathbf{s}^{\delta})+\delta
. For
x
large enough (so that
\|(x\mathbf{u})_{+}\|\geq R
for all

\alpha
-homogeneity,

\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{Y}\geq x\mathbf{t})\ \geq\ \mathbb{P}(\mathbf{Y}\in xB^{\delta})\ \geq\ \int_{xB^{\delta}}p_{1}(\mathbf{x})\,e^{-\overline{\mathcal{J}}(\mathbf{x})}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}=x^{k}\int_{B^{\delta}}p_{1}(x\mathbf{u})\,e^{-x^{\alpha}\overline{\mathcal{J}}(\mathbf{u})}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{u}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{Y}\geq x\mathbf{t})

\displaystyle\geq x^{k}\,\mathrm{vol}(B^{\delta})\,\inf_{\mathbf{u}\in B^{\delta}}p_{1}(x\mathbf{u})\,\exp\Big(-x^{\alpha}\sup_{\mathbf{u}\in B^{\delta}}\overline{\mathcal{J}}(\mathbf{u})\Big)

\displaystyle\geq x^{k}\,\mathrm{vol}(B^{\delta})\,\inf_{\mathbf{u}\in B^{\delta}}p_{1}(x\mathbf{u})\,e^{-x^{\alpha}(\overline{\mathcal{J}}(\mathbf{s}^{\delta})+\delta)}.

Dividing by
x^{\alpha}
and letting

, the assumptions on
p_{1}
imply

\liminf_{x\to\infty}\frac{1}{x^{\alpha}}\log\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{Y}\geq x\mathbf{t})\ \geq\ -(\overline{\mathcal{J}}(\mathbf{s}^{\delta})+\delta)\ \geq\ -(m(\mathbf{t})+2\delta).

Letting

\liminf_{x\to\infty}\frac{1}{x^{\alpha}}\log\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{Y}\geq x\mathbf{t})\ \geq\ -m(\mathbf{t}).

Upper bound.

Fix
M>1
and define the box
K_{M,x}:=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}:\forall j,\ |x_{j}|\leq Mx\|\mathbf{t}\|\}
and

D_{M,x}:=\{\mathbf{x}\in K_{M,x}:\mathbf{x}\geq x\mathbf{t}\}
. Then

\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{Y}\geq x\mathbf{t})\ \leq\ \mathbb{P}(\mathbf{Y}\in D_{M,x})+\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{Y}\notin K_{M,x}).

(5.32)

Considering the second term on the r.h.s. of (

), by similar arguments as in the proof of Lemma

for the lower bound and the upper density bound for the right tails, there exist
c,C>0
such that
\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{Y}\notin K_{M,x})\leq Ce^{-cM^{\alpha}x^{\alpha}}
for all
x
.
Choose
M
so large that

, then

\limsup_{x\to\infty}\frac{1}{x^{\alpha}}\log\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{Y}\notin K_{M,x})\ \leq\ -cM^{\alpha}\ <\ -m(\mathbf{t}).

For the first term on the r.h.s. of (
5.32
), for
x
large enough the upper density bound applies on
D_{M,x}
, hence

\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{Y}\in D_{M,x})=\int_{D_{M,x}}f(\mathbf{x})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\ \leq\ \int_{D_{M,x}}p_{2}(\mathbf{x}_{+})\,e^{-\overline{\mathcal{J}}(\mathbf{x}_{+})}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}.

\mathbf{x}_{+}\geq x\mathbf{t}_{+}

\overline{\mathcal{J}}(\mathbf{x}_{+})\geq\inf_{\mathbf{s}\geq x\mathbf{t}_{+}}\overline{\mathcal{J}}(\mathbf{s})=x^{\alpha}m(\mathbf{t})
by

-homogeneity. Therefore,

\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{Y}\in D_{M,x})\ \leq\ \mathrm{vol}(D_{M,x})\,\sup_{\mathbf{x}\in D_{M,x}}p_{2}(\mathbf{x}_{+})\,e^{-x^{\alpha}m(\mathbf{t})}.

Since
\mathrm{vol}(D_{M,x})\leq(2Mx\|\mathbf{t}\|)^{k}

(\mathbf{x}_{+}/x)_{\mathbf{x}\in D_{M,x}}

give

\limsup_{x\to\infty}\frac{1}{x^{\alpha}}\log\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{Y}\in D_{M,x})\ \leq\ -m(\mathbf{t}).

Combining the two terms on the r.h.s. of (

) yields

\limsup_{x\to\infty}\frac{1}{x^{\alpha}}\log\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{Y}\geq x\mathbf{t})\ \leq\ -m(\mathbf{t}).

Together with the lower bound, this proves the claim.

Proof of Theorem

.

\mathbf{G}_{1},\mathbf{G}_{2},\ldots

\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\boldsymbol{\Sigma})
in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
and set

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}_{i}

\displaystyle=(|G_{i,1}|^{q},\ldots,|G_{i,k}|^{q}),\quad\boldsymbol{\mu}_{q}:=\big(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{11}^{q/2}M_{q},\ldots,\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{kk}^{q/2}M_{q}\big),\quad\mathbf{X}_{i}:=\mathbf{W}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_{q},

\displaystyle\mathbf{Y}_{N}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{X}_{i}.

\boldsymbol{\Sigma}
is positive definite. By Lemma
3.2
and (
3.2
),
\mathbf{X}_{1}=\mathbf{W}_{1}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_{q}
has a Lebesgue density
f_{\mathbf{X}_{1}}

for some positive polynomials

and

\overline{\mathcal{J}}(\mathbf{z})=\min_{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\in\{\pm 1\}^{k}}\frac{1}{2}\,(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\odot\mathbf{z}^{1/q})^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\odot\mathbf{z}^{1/q}),\qquad\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{k}.

Each marginal
W_{1,j}
has
1
-dimensional stretched-exponential tail with index
\alpha=2/q\in(0,1)
.
Lemma
3.1
applied to
\mathbf{X}_{1}
with this
\overline{\mathcal{J}}
and
\alpha
yields that
\mathbf{X}_{1}
has stretched-exponential tail of rate
(\mathcal{J},\alpha)
in the sense of Definition
2.1
, where
\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{t})=\inf_{\mathbf{s}\geq\mathbf{t}}\overline{\mathcal{J}}(\mathbf{s})

A
with

This proves the theorem in the positive definite case.

Now assume that
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}
is only positive semidefinite. For
\delta>0

\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\delta}:=\boldsymbol{\Sigma}+\delta\,\operatorname{Id}_{k}
, which is positive definite. Let
\mathbf{G}_{i}^{(\delta)}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\delta})

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}_{i}^{(\delta)}

\displaystyle=(|G_{i,1}^{(\delta)}|^{q},\ldots,|G_{i,k}^{(\delta)}|^{q}),\quad\boldsymbol{\mu}_{q,\delta}:=\big((\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{11}+\delta)^{q/2}M_{q},\ldots,(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{kk}+\delta)^{q/2}M_{q}\big),

\displaystyle\mathbf{X}_{i}^{(\delta)}

\displaystyle:=\mathbf{W}_{i}^{(\delta)}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_{q,\delta},\qquad\mathbf{Y}_{N}^{(\delta)}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{X}_{i}^{(\delta)}.

By the first part applied with
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\delta}
, for every
\mathbf{t}\in(0,\infty)^{k}
,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N^{2/q}}\log\mathbb{P}\big(\mathbf{Y}_{N}^{(\delta)}\geq\mathbf{t}\big)=-\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}_{\delta}}(\mathbf{t}),

(5.33)

where

\overline{\mathcal{J}_{\delta}}(\mathbf{z}):=\min_{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\in\{\pm 1\}^{k}}\frac{1}{2}\,(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\odot\mathbf{z}^{1/q})^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\delta}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\odot\mathbf{z}^{1/q}),\qquad\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{k},

to
\mathbf{Y}_{N}

\mathbf{G}_{i}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\boldsymbol{\Sigma})

\mathbf{X}_{i},\mathbf{X}_{i}^{(\delta)},\mathbf{Y}_{N},\mathbf{Y}_{N}^{(\delta)}

\mathbf{G}_{i},\mathbf{G}_{i}^{(\delta)}
as above. We show that for every
\varepsilon>0

\lim_{\delta\downarrow 0}\ \limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N^{2/q}}\log\mathbb{P}\big(\|\mathbf{Y}_{N}^{(\delta)}-\mathbf{Y}_{N}\|_{\infty}>\varepsilon\big)=-\infty.

For the first term,

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N^{2/q}}\log\mathbb{P}\Big(\Big|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(X_{i,j}^{(\delta)}-X_{i,j})\Big|>\varepsilon\Big)\leq-c(\varepsilon)\gamma(\delta).

Taking a union bound over
j
and letting
\delta\downarrow 0
confirms (
5.34
). Fix
\mathbf{t}\in(0,\infty)^{k}

By the exponential bound for

and (

), for every fixed

, we can choose

\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}_{\delta}}(\mathbf{t}-\varepsilon\mathbf{1})
. Consequently,

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N^{2/q}}\log\mathbb{P}\big(\mathbf{Y}_{N}\geq\mathbf{t}\big)\leq-\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}_{\delta}}(\mathbf{t}-\varepsilon\mathbf{1}).

Letting
\delta\downarrow 0
and then
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
(using lower semicontinuity of
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}
) yields

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N^{2/q}}\log\mathbb{P}\big(\mathbf{Y}_{N}\geq\mathbf{t}\big)\leq-\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}(\mathbf{t}).

For the lower bound, fix again
\mathbf{t}\in(0,\infty)^{k}
and
\eta\in(0,1)
, and set
\varepsilon:=\eta\,\min_{j}t_{j}
so that
(1+\eta)\mathbf{t}-\varepsilon\mathbf{1}\geq\mathbf{t}
. For every
\delta>0
and
N
,

\mathbb{P}\big(\mathbf{Y}_{N}\geq\mathbf{t}\big)\geq\mathbb{P}\big(\mathbf{Y}_{N}^{(\delta)}\geq(1+\eta)\mathbf{t},\ \|\mathbf{Y}_{N}^{(\delta)}-\mathbf{Y}_{N}\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon\big)\geq\mathbb{P}\big(\mathbf{Y}_{N}^{(\delta)}\geq(1+\eta)\mathbf{t}\big)-\mathbb{P}\big(\|\mathbf{Y}_{N}^{(\delta)}-\mathbf{Y}_{N}\|_{\infty}>\varepsilon\big).

By (
5.33

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N^{2/q}}\log\mathbb{P}\big(\mathbf{Y}_{N}^{(\delta)}\geq(1+\eta)\mathbf{t}\big)=-\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}_{\delta}}\big((1+\eta)\mathbf{t}\big).

By (
5.34
), for sufficiently small
\delta
, the first term dominates:

\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N^{2/q}}\log\mathbb{P}\big(\mathbf{Y}_{N}\geq\mathbf{t}\big)\geq-\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}_{\delta}}\big((1+\eta)\mathbf{t}\big).

Letting
\delta\downarrow 0
yields

\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N^{2/q}}\log\mathbb{P}\big(\mathbf{Y}_{N}\geq\mathbf{t}\big)\geq-\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}\big((1+\eta)\mathbf{t}\big),

and finally letting
\eta\downarrow 0
and using the
\alpha

\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}
gives

\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N^{2/q}}\log\mathbb{P}\big(\mathbf{Y}_{N}\geq\mathbf{t}\big)\geq-\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}(\mathbf{t}).

Combining the upper and lower bounds completes the proof.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
q>2
and
\rho\in(0,1)
. Define

\overline{\mathcal{J}}(z_{1},z_{2}):=\frac{z_{1}^{2/q}+z_{2}^{2/q}-2\rho\,z_{1}^{1/q}z_{2}^{1/q}}{2(1-\rho^{2})},\qquad(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2},

\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{t}):=\inf_{\mathbf{s}\geq\mathbf{t}}\overline{\mathcal{J}}(\mathbf{s}),\qquad\mathbf{t}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2},

and

\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}(\mathbf{t}):=\min\Big\{\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{u})+\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{v}):\ \mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2},\ \mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{t}\Big\},\qquad\mathbf{t}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}.

Then
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}
is neither convex nor concave on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
.

Proof.

Fix
t\geq 0
and let
s_{1}\geq t
,
s_{2}\geq 0
. Writing
a:=s_{1}^{1/q}
and
b:=s_{2}^{1/q}
,

a^{2}+b^{2}-2\rho ab=(b-\rho a)^{2}+(1-\rho^{2})a^{2}\geq(1-\rho^{2})a^{2},

and so
\overline{\mathcal{J}}(s_{1},s_{2})\geq a^{2}/2=s_{1}^{2/q}/2
. Therefore,

\mathcal{J}(t,0)=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}s_{1}\geq t\\
s_{2}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\overline{\mathcal{J}}(s_{1},s_{2})\geq\frac{t^{2/q}}{2}.

Choosing
(s_{1},s_{2})=(t,\rho^{q}t)
gives
b=\rho a
and thus
\overline{\mathcal{J}}(t,\rho^{q}t)=t^{2/q}/2
, so

\mathcal{J}(t,0)=\frac{t^{2/q}}{2},\qquad\mathcal{J}(0,t)=\frac{t^{2/q}}{2}.

Set
p:=2/q\in(0,1)
. For
t\geq 0
,

\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}(t,0)=\min_{u\in[0,t]}\Big(\mathcal{J}(u,0)+\mathcal{J}(t-u,0)\Big)=\frac{1}{2}\min_{u\in[0,t]}\Big(u^{p}+(t-u)^{p}\Big)=\frac{t^{p}}{2},

since
(x+y)^{p}\leq x^{p}+y^{p}
for all
x,y\geq 0
, with equality at
u\in\{0,t\}
.

Not convex.
Using
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}(0,0)=0
and
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}(1,0)=1/2
,

\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}\Big(\frac{1}{2},0\Big)=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\Big)^{p}=2^{-(1+p)}>\frac{1}{4}=\frac{1}{2}\,\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}(1,0)+\frac{1}{2}\,\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}(0,0),

because
p<1
. Hence,
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}
is not convex.

Not concave.
Let
\varepsilon:=\rho^{q}/(1+\rho^{q})\in(0,1)
, so that
\varepsilon^{1/q}=\rho(1-\varepsilon)^{1/q}
. Then

\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}(\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon)\leq\mathcal{J}(\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon)\leq\overline{\mathcal{J}}(\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon)=\frac{(1-\varepsilon)^{p}}{2}<\frac{1}{2}.

But
\varepsilon(1,0)+(1-\varepsilon)(0,1)=(\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon)
and

\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}(1,0)=\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}(0,1)=1/2
, hence concavity would imply

\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}(\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon)\geq\varepsilon\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}(1,0)+(1-\varepsilon)\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}(0,1)=\frac{1}{2},

a contradiction. Thus
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}
is not concave.
∎

One can show that in the multivariate Weibull example, a deviation is typically realized by exactly one vector. The next lemma gives an example where a deviation is realized by two vectors.

Lemma 5.8

(A deviation induced by two big jumps)
.

Let
R
be Weibull with parameter
1/2
, i.e.
\mathbb{P}(R\geq t)=\exp(-t^{1/2})
for
t\geq 0
.
Fix
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Let
\mathbf{V}\in\{(1,\varepsilon),(\varepsilon,1)\}
with

\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{V}=(1,\varepsilon))=\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{V}=(\varepsilon,1))=\frac{1}{2}
.
Let
S_{1},S_{2}
be i.i.d. Rademacher variables, independent of
(R,\mathbf{V})
, and set

\mathbf{X}=(X_{1},X_{2}):=(RV_{1}S_{1},\ RV_{2}S_{2}).

Then
\mathbf{X}
satisfies
Definition

2.1
with
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
and Assumption
(A)
with

\mathcal{J}(t_{1},t_{2})=\min\Big\{(\max\{t_{1},t_{2}/\varepsilon\})^{1/2},\ (\max\{t_{1}/\varepsilon,t_{2}\})^{1/2}\Big\},\qquad(t_{1},t_{2})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}.

In particular,
\mathcal{J}(1,1)=\varepsilon^{-1/2}
.
Let
(\mathbf{X}_{i})_{i\geq 1}
be i.i.d. copies of
\mathbf{X}
and take
x_{N}=N
. Then

\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N^{1/2}}\log\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{X}_{i}\geq(1,1)\right)\geq-2.

Consequently, whenever
\varepsilon<1/4
, the lower bound
-2
is strictly larger than
-\mathcal{J}(1,1)=-\varepsilon^{-1/2}
, so
\mathcal{J}
is not the correct rate for this deviation.

Proof.

For
j=1,2
and
t
large,

\mathbb{P}(X_{j}\geq t)=\frac{1}{4}\,\mathbb{P}(R\geq t)+\frac{1}{4}\,\mathbb{P}(R\geq t/\varepsilon)\leq\exp(-c\,t^{1/2})

for some
c=c(\varepsilon)>0
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(X_{1}\geq t_{1},\ X_{2}\geq t_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8}\,\mathbb{P}\left(R\geq\max\{t_{1},t_{2}/\varepsilon\}\right)+\frac{1}{8}\,\mathbb{P}\left(R\geq\max\{t_{1}/\varepsilon,t_{2}\}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8}\,\exp\Big(-(\max\{t_{1},t_{2}/\varepsilon\})^{1/2}\Big)+\frac{1}{8}\,\exp\Big(-(\max\{t_{1}/\varepsilon,t_{2}\})^{1/2}\Big).

Hence,
\mathbf{X}
satisfies
Definition

2.1
with
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
and

\mathcal{J}(t_{1},t_{2})=\min\Big\{(\max\{t_{1},t_{2}/\varepsilon\})^{1/2},\ (\max\{t_{1}/\varepsilon,t_{2}\})^{1/2}\Big\},

so
\mathcal{J}(1,1)=\varepsilon^{-1/2}
. Assumption
(A)
holds by symmetry (thus
Equation

2.1
holds). Now let
\big((R_{i},\mathbf{V}_{i},S_{i,1},S_{i,2})\big)_{i\geq 1}
be i.i.d. copies of
(R,\mathbf{V},S_{1},S_{2})
and set

\mathbf{X}_{i}=(X_{i,1},X_{i,2}):=\big(R_{i}(\mathbf{V}_{i})_{1}S_{i,1},\ R_{i}(\mathbf{V}_{i})_{2}S_{i,2}\big),\qquad i\geq 1.

Fix
\delta\in(0,1)
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i,1}\geq 1,\ \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i,2}\geq 1\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=3}^{N}X_{i,1}\geq-\delta,\ \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=3}^{N}X_{i,2}\geq-\delta\right)\times\mathbb{P}\left(S_{1,1}=1,\ S_{1,2}=1,\ \mathbf{V}_{1}=(1,\varepsilon),\ R_{1}\geq N(1+\delta)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\mathbb{P}\left(S_{2,1}=1,\ S_{2,2}=1,\ \mathbf{V}_{2}=(\varepsilon,1),\ R_{2}\geq N(1+\delta)\right).

The first factor tends to
1
by the law of large numbers. Each of the last two factors equals

\frac{1}{8}\,\mathbb{P}\big(R\geq N(1+\delta)\big)=\frac{1}{8}\,\exp\big(-(1+\delta)^{1/2}N^{1/2}\big).

Therefore,

\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N^{1/2}}\log\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{X}_{i}\geq(1,1)\right)\geq-2(1+\delta)^{1/2}.

Letting
\delta\downarrow 0
yields the asserted bound
-2
. If
\varepsilon<1/4
, then
\varepsilon^{-1/2}>2
, hence
-2>-\varepsilon^{-1/2}=-\mathcal{J}(1,1)
, showing that
\mathcal{J}
alone cannot be the correct rate for this event; the correct rate is given by
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}
as in
A
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.5
.

Let
\mathbf{g}_{1},\mathbf{g}_{2},\ldots
be i.i.d. standard Gaussian vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
and let
\mathbf{G}_{N}
be the
m\times N
matrix with columns
\mathbf{g}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{g}_{N}
. It is well known (see for instance
[
20
, Lemma 3.1]
) that

\mathbf{V}_{N}\ \overset{d}{=}\ \Big(\frac{1}{N}\mathbf{G}_{N}\mathbf{G}_{N}^{\top}\Big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,\mathbf{G}_{N},

hence
\sqrt{N}\,\mathbf{v}_{i}\overset{d}{=}\mathbf{A}_{N}\mathbf{g}_{i}
with

\mathbf{A}_{N}:=\Big(\frac{1}{N}\mathbf{G}_{N}\mathbf{G}_{N}^{\top}\Big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Hence, for
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\langle\sqrt{N}\,\mathbf{v}_{i},\mathbf{u}_{1}\rangle|^{q}-M_{q}\geq t_{1},\ldots,\ \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\langle\sqrt{N}\,\mathbf{v}_{i},\mathbf{u}_{k}\rangle|^{q}-M_{q}\geq t_{k}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\mathbf{D}\in B_{\varepsilon}(\operatorname{Id}_{m})}\ \mathbb{P}\Big(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\langle\mathbf{D}\mathbf{g}_{i},\mathbf{u}_{1}\rangle|^{q}-M_{q}\geq t_{1},\ldots,\ \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\langle\mathbf{D}\mathbf{g}_{i},\mathbf{u}_{k}\rangle|^{q}-M_{q}\geq t_{k}\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\ \mathbb{P}\big(\mathbf{A}_{N}\notin B_{\varepsilon}(\operatorname{Id}_{m})\big).

By Cramér’s theorem (Proposition
4.2
) for
\frac{1}{N}\mathbf{G}_{N}\mathbf{G}_{N}^{\top}
(at speed
N
) and contraction under
\mathbf{A}\mapsto\mathbf{A}^{-1/2}
on positive definite matrices,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N^{2/q}}\log\mathbb{P}\big(\mathbf{A}_{N}\notin B_{\varepsilon}(\operatorname{Id}_{m})\big)=-\infty.

(5.35)

Upper bound.

Fix
\mathbf{D}\in B_{\varepsilon}(\operatorname{Id}_{m})
. For each
j\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
set

\sigma_{j}(\mathbf{D}):=\|\mathbf{D}^{\top}\mathbf{u}_{j}\|,\qquad\widehat{}\mathbf{u}_{j}(\mathbf{D}):=\frac{\mathbf{D}^{\top}\mathbf{u}_{j}}{\|\mathbf{D}^{\top}\mathbf{u}_{j}\|}\in\mathbb{S}^{m-1}.

Then
\langle\mathbf{D}\mathbf{g}_{i},\mathbf{u}_{j}\rangle=\sigma_{j}(\mathbf{D})\langle\mathbf{g}_{i},\widehat{}\mathbf{u}_{j}(\mathbf{D})\rangle
and hence

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\big(|\langle\mathbf{D}\mathbf{g}_{i},\mathbf{u}_{j}\rangle|^{q}-M_{q}\big)\geq t_{j}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\langle\mathbf{g}_{i},\widehat{}\mathbf{u}_{j}(\mathbf{D})\rangle|^{q}-M_{q}\ \geq\ \frac{t_{j}+M_{q}}{\sigma_{j}(\mathbf{D})^{q}}-M_{q}.

Let
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}(\mathbf{D}):=\big(\langle\widehat{}\mathbf{u}_{r}(\mathbf{D}),\widehat{}\mathbf{u}_{s}(\mathbf{D})\rangle\big)_{1\leq r,s\leq k}
. The matrix
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}(\mathbf{D})
is positive semidefinite and
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}(\mathbf{D})\to\boldsymbol{\Sigma}
as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
. By Theorem
3.3
with the corresponding rate function
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}^{\,\boldsymbol{\Sigma}(\mathbf{D})}
,

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N^{2/q}}\log\mathbb{P}\Big(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\langle\mathbf{D}\mathbf{g}_{i},\mathbf{u}_{\cdot}\rangle|^{q}-M_{q}\geq\mathbf{t}\Big)\ \leq\ -\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}^{\,\boldsymbol{\Sigma}(\mathbf{D})}(\mathbf{t}^{(\varepsilon)}(\mathbf{D})),

where
\mathbf{t}^{(\varepsilon)}(\mathbf{D})
has coordinates
t_{j}^{(\varepsilon)}(\mathbf{D}):=\frac{t_{j}+M_{q}}{\sigma_{j}(\mathbf{D})^{q}}-M_{q}
. Continuity of
(\boldsymbol{\Sigma},\mathbf{t})\mapsto\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}^{\,\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}(\mathbf{t})
and
\sigma_{j}(\mathbf{D})\to 1
give

\sup_{\mathbf{D}\in B_{\varepsilon}(\operatorname{Id}_{m})}\ \mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}^{\,\boldsymbol{\Sigma}(\mathbf{D})}(\mathbf{t}^{(\varepsilon)}(\mathbf{D}))\ \xrightarrow[\varepsilon\downarrow 0]{}\ \mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}^{\,\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}(\mathbf{t}).

With (
5.35
) this yields

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N^{2/q}}\log\mathbb{P}\Big(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\langle\sqrt{N}\,\mathbf{v}_{i},\mathbf{u}_{\cdot}\rangle|^{q}-M_{q}\geq\mathbf{t}\Big)\leq-\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}}^{\,\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}(\mathbf{t}).

Lower bound.

Define

\mathbf{Y}_{N}:=\Big(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\langle\sqrt{N}\,\mathbf{v}_{i},\mathbf{u}_{\ell}\rangle|^{q}-M_{q}\Big)_{\ell=1}^{k},\qquad\mathbf{Z}_{N}:=\Big(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\langle\mathbf{g}_{i},\mathbf{u}_{\ell}\rangle|^{q}-M_{q}\Big)_{\ell=1}^{k}.

We prove exponential equivalence at speed
N^{2/q}
. Fix
\delta\in(0,1)
. Whenever
\mathbf{A}_{N}\in B_{\delta}(\operatorname{Id}_{m})
, we have

|\sigma_{\ell}(\mathbf{A}_{N})^{q}-1|\leq C\delta,\quad\|\widehat{}\mathbf{u}_{\ell}(\mathbf{A}_{N})-\mathbf{u}_{\ell}\|_{2}\leq C\delta,

for a constant
C=C(m,q)
. Indeed, the map
\mathbf{A}\mapsto\sigma_{\ell}(\mathbf{A})^{q}
is smooth near
\operatorname{Id}_{m}
with value
1
at
\operatorname{Id}_{m}
, and similarly for
\mathbf{A}\mapsto\widehat{}\mathbf{u}_{\ell}(\mathbf{A})
with value
\mathbf{u}_{\ell}
.
Using the inequality
|x^{q}-y^{q}|\leq q(|x|^{q-1}+|y|^{q-1})|x-y|
, we obtain

\displaystyle\big||\langle\mathbf{A}_{N}\mathbf{g}_{i},\mathbf{u}_{\ell}\rangle|^{q}-|\langle\mathbf{g}_{i},\mathbf{u}_{\ell}\rangle|^{q}\big|

\displaystyle\qquad=\big|\sigma_{\ell}(\mathbf{A}_{N})^{q}|\langle\mathbf{g}_{i},\widehat{}\mathbf{u}_{\ell}(\mathbf{A}_{N})\rangle|^{q}-|\langle\mathbf{g}_{i},\mathbf{u}_{\ell}\rangle|^{q}\big|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq|\sigma_{\ell}(\mathbf{A}_{N})^{q}-1|\,|\langle\mathbf{g}_{i},\widehat{}\mathbf{u}_{\ell}(\mathbf{A}_{N})\rangle|^{q}+\big||\langle\mathbf{g}_{i},\widehat{}\mathbf{u}_{\ell}(\mathbf{A}_{N})\rangle|^{q}-|\langle\mathbf{g}_{i},\mathbf{u}_{\ell}\rangle|^{q}\big|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C\delta\|\mathbf{g}_{i}\|_{2}^{q}+q\big(|\langle\mathbf{g}_{i},\widehat{}\mathbf{u}_{\ell}(\mathbf{A}_{N})\rangle|^{q-1}+|\langle\mathbf{g}_{i},\mathbf{u}_{\ell}\rangle|^{q-1}\big)\,|\langle\mathbf{g}_{i},\widehat{}\mathbf{u}_{\ell}(\mathbf{A}_{N})-\mathbf{u}_{\ell}\rangle|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C\delta\|\mathbf{g}_{i}\|_{2}^{q}+q(1+\delta)^{q-1}(\|\mathbf{g}_{i}\|_{2}^{q-1}+\|\mathbf{g}_{i}\|_{2}^{q-1})\,\|\mathbf{g}_{i}\|_{2}\,\|\widehat{}\mathbf{u}_{\ell}(\mathbf{A}_{N})-\mathbf{u}_{\ell}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C^{\prime}\delta\,\|\mathbf{g}_{i}\|_{2}^{q},

where we used
|\langle g,u\rangle|\leq\|g\|_{2}\|u\|_{2}
and
\|u\|_{2}=1
. The constant
C^{\prime}
depends only on
m
and
q
.
Summing over
i
gives

\|\mathbf{Y}_{N}-\mathbf{Z}_{N}\|_{\infty}\ \leq\ \frac{C^{\prime}\delta}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\mathbf{g}_{i}\|_{2}^{q}

if
\mathbf{A}_{N}\in B_{\delta}(\operatorname{Id}_{m})
. Therefore, for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\mathbb{P}(\|\mathbf{Y}_{N}-\mathbf{Z}_{N}\|_{\infty}>\varepsilon)\leq\mathbb{P}\Big(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\mathbf{g}_{i}\|_{2}^{q}>\frac{\varepsilon}{C^{\prime}\delta}\Big)\ +\ \mathbb{P}(\mathbf{A}_{N}\notin B_{\delta}(\operatorname{Id}_{m})).

The second term is negligible at speed
N^{2/q}
. For the first term, using the norm equivalence
\|x\|_{2}^{q}=(\sum_{r=1}^{m}x_{r}^{2})^{q/2}\leq m^{q/2}\sum_{r=1}^{m}|x_{r}|^{q}
(valid for
q\geq 2
by Jensen’s inequality),

\mathbb{P}\Big(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\mathbf{g}_{i}\|_{2}^{q}>u\Big)\ \leq\ \sum_{r=1}^{m}\mathbb{P}\Big(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\langle\mathbf{g}_{i},\mathbf{e}_{r}\rangle|^{q}>\frac{u}{m^{q/2+1}}\Big).

Each summand decays like
\exp(-c(u)\,N^{2/q})
. With
u=\varepsilon/(C^{\prime}\delta)
, the rate goes to
\infty
as
\delta\downarrow 0
. Thus
\mathbf{Y}_{N}
and
\mathbf{Z}_{N}
are exponentially equivalent. Since
\mathbf{Z}_{N}
satisfies the LDP lower bound, the same holds for
\mathbf{Y}_{N}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.6
.

By
[
33
, Lemma 4.3]
,

\mathbb{P}\big(h(N^{1/p-1/2}\mathbf{V}_{N}\mathbb{B}_{p}^{N},\cdot)\geq f\big)=\mathbb{P}\Big(\forall\,\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{S}^{m-1}:\ \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\big|\langle\sqrt{N}\,\mathbf{v}_{i},\mathbf{u}\rangle\big|^{q}\geq f(\mathbf{u})^{q}\Big).

Fix a dense sequence without repetitions
\{\mathbf{u}_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}\subseteq\mathbb{S}^{m-1}
. For each
N
the map
\mathbf{u}\mapsto N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\langle\sqrt{N}\,\mathbf{v}_{i},\mathbf{u}\rangle|^{q}
is continuous, hence

\Big\{\forall\,\mathbf{u}:\ \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\langle\sqrt{N}\,\mathbf{v}_{i},\mathbf{u}\rangle|^{q}\geq f(\mathbf{u})^{q}\Big\}=\bigcap_{j\geq 1}\Big\{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\langle\sqrt{N}\,\mathbf{v}_{i},\mathbf{u}_{j}\rangle|^{q}\geq f(\mathbf{u}_{j})^{q}\Big\}.

For
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
j\geq 1
set

A_{j}^{(N)}:=\Big\{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\big|\langle\sqrt{N}\,\mathbf{v}_{i},\mathbf{u}_{j}\rangle\big|^{q}\geq f(\mathbf{u}_{j})^{q}\Big\}.

Then
\bigcap_{j\geq 1}A_{j}^{(N)}\subseteq\bigcap_{j=1}^{k}A_{j}^{(N)}
for all
k
, so

\mathbb{P}\Big(\bigcap_{j\geq 1}A_{j}^{(N)}\Big)\leq\mathbb{P}\Big(\bigcap_{j=1}^{k}A_{j}^{(N)}\Big)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathbb{P}\Big(\bigcap_{j\geq 1}A_{j}^{(N)}\Big)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\Big(\bigcap_{j=1}^{k}A_{j}^{(N)}\Big).

Upper bound.
For fixed
k
apply Theorem
3.5
to the
k
-tuple
(\mathbf{u}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{u}_{k})
with
t_{j}=f(\mathbf{u}_{j})^{q}-M_{q}
. Writing
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{r,s}=\langle\mathbf{u}_{r},\mathbf{u}_{s}\rangle
and
\mathbf{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{k})
, define

\mathcal{J}_{k}(\mathbf{s}):=\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}^{(k)}}\big((\mathbf{s}^{q}-M_{q}\mathbf{1})\big),\qquad\mathbf{s}\in[M_{q}^{1/q},\infty)^{k},

where
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{J}^{(k)}}
is the rate from Theorem
3.3
with covariance matrix

\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{(k)}=(\langle\mathbf{u}_{r},\mathbf{u}_{s}\rangle)_{1\leq r,s\leq k}
. Therefore,

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N^{2/q}}\log\mathbb{P}\Big(\bigcap_{j=1}^{k}A_{j}^{(N)}\Big)\leq-\,\mathcal{J}_{k}\big(f(\mathbf{u}_{1}),\ldots,f(\mathbf{u}_{k})\big).

Since
\mathbb{P}(\bigcap_{j\geq 1}A_{j}^{(N)})\leq\mathbb{P}(\bigcap_{j=1}^{k}A_{j}^{(N)})
for all
k
, we obtain

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N^{2/q}}\log\mathbb{P}\big(h(N^{1/p-1/2}\mathbf{V}_{N}\mathbb{B}_{p}^{N},\cdot)\geq f\big)\leq-\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{J}_{k}\big(f(\mathbf{u}_{1}),\ldots,f(\mathbf{u}_{k})\big),

which implies the desired bound.

Lower bound.

For fixed
k\in\mathbb{N}
, apply Theorem
3.5
to
(\mathbf{u}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{u}_{k})
with

t_{j}=f(\mathbf{u}_{j})^{q}-M_{q}
. With
\mathcal{J}_{k}
as defined above, this gives

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N^{2/q}}\log\mathbb{P}\Big(\bigcap_{j=1}^{k}A_{j}^{(N)}\Big)=-\,\mathcal{J}_{k}\big(f(\mathbf{u}_{1}),\ldots,f(\mathbf{u}_{k})\big).

Since
\bigcap_{j\geq 1}A_{j}^{(N)}=\bigcap_{k\geq 1}\bigcap_{j=1}^{k}A_{j}^{(N)}
and the events decrease in
k
,

\mathbb{P}\Big(\bigcap_{j\geq 1}A_{j}^{(N)}\Big)=\inf_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{P}\Big(\bigcap_{j=1}^{k}A_{j}^{(N)}\Big),

hence

\frac{1}{N^{2/q}}\log\mathbb{P}\Big(\bigcap_{j\geq 1}A_{j}^{(N)}\Big)=\inf_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{N^{2/q}}\log\mathbb{P}\Big(\bigcap_{j=1}^{k}A_{j}^{(N)}\Big).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N^{2/q}}\log\mathbb{P}\Big(\bigcap_{j\geq 1}A_{j}^{(N)}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\ \liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N^{2/q}}\log\mathbb{P}\Big(\bigcap_{j=1}^{k}A_{j}^{(N)}\Big)

\displaystyle=\inf_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\Big(-\mathcal{J}_{k}\big(f(\mathbf{u}_{1}),\ldots,f(\mathbf{u}_{k})\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=-\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{J}_{k}\big(f(\mathbf{u}_{1}),\ldots,f(\mathbf{u}_{k})\big).

Combining the bounds yields

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N^{2/q}}\log\mathbb{P}\big(h(N^{1/p-1/2}\mathbf{V}_{N}\mathbb{B}_{p}^{N},\cdot)\geq f\big)=-\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{J}_{k}\big(f(\mathbf{u}_{1}),\ldots,f(\mathbf{u}_{k})\big).

∎

Proof of Corollary
4.5
.

Set
\psi(\mathbf{x},\boldsymbol{\theta}):=\mathbf{x}-\boldsymbol{\theta}
. Then
\boldsymbol{\Psi}(\boldsymbol{\theta})=\mathbb{E}[\psi(\mathbf{X},\boldsymbol{\theta})]=\boldsymbol{\mu}-\boldsymbol{\theta}
and
\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{N}(\boldsymbol{\theta})=N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{X}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\theta}
. Condition (C1) holds. For (C2):
\boldsymbol{\Psi}
has the unique zero
\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0}=\boldsymbol{\mu}
;
\mathbb{B}_{\eta}(\boldsymbol{\mu})\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
for every
\eta>0
; the derivative at
\boldsymbol{\mu}
is
-\operatorname{Id}_{d}
(nonsingular);
\boldsymbol{\Psi}
is a homeomorphism. Moreover, by
[
10
, Lemma 2.5]
and (
4.3
),
\mathbb{E}\|\mathbf{X}\|^{2}<\infty
. For (C3)(i),

\frac{\sqrt{N}}{a_{N}}\sup_{\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\mathbb{B}_{\eta}(\boldsymbol{\mu})}\|\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{N}(\boldsymbol{\theta})-\boldsymbol{\Psi}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\|=\Big\|\frac{1}{a_{N}\sqrt{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\mathbf{X}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mu})\Big\|\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}0,

since
a_{N}\to\infty
and by the CLT and Slutsky’s theorem.
For (C3)(ii), using that
\sup_{\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\mathbb{B}_{\eta}(\boldsymbol{\mu})}\|\mathbf{X}-\boldsymbol{\theta}\|\leq\|\mathbf{X}-\boldsymbol{\mu}\|+\eta
,

N\,\mathbb{P}\big(\sup_{\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\mathbb{B}_{\eta}(\boldsymbol{\mu})}\|\psi(\mathbf{X},\boldsymbol{\theta})\|\geq\sqrt{N}\,a_{N}\big)\leq N\,\mathbb{P}\big(\|\mathbf{X}-\boldsymbol{\mu}\|\geq\sqrt{N}\,a_{N}-\eta\big)\leq N\,\mathbb{P}\big(\|\mathbf{X}\|\geq\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{N}\,a_{N}\big)

for all large
N
. Hence, (
4.3
) implies (C3)(ii). Proposition
4.4
gives an LDP with speed
a_{N}^{2}
and rate function

I(\mathbf{z})=\frac{1}{2}\langle\mathbf{z},\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1}\mathbf{z}\rangle,\qquad\boldsymbol{\Sigma}=\operatorname{Cov}(\mathbf{X}).

Finally,

\frac{1}{a_{N}\sqrt{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\mathbf{X}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mu})=\frac{\sqrt{N}}{a_{N}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{N}-\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0}),

∎

Proof of Theorem
B
.

Write
x_{N}=\sqrt{N}\,a_{N}
with
a_{N}\to\infty
and
a_{N}/\sqrt{N}\to 0
. It suffices, by Corollary
4.5
applied to

, to verify (
4.3
) with

.
