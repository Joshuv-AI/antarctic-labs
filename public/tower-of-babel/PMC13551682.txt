Successful management of hemolysis after transcatheter mitral valve implantation by valve re-tensioning
Authors: Si-Wen Wang, Qian-Hui Sun, Zi-Ming Rao, Jian-Bin Gao, Da Zhu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTranscatheter mitral valve implantation (TMVI) device featuring an apical fixation design is now the most mature TMVI system and widely applied in routine clinical practice. Nevertheless, severe unique complications including left ventricular outflow tract (LVOT) obstruction, paravalvular leakage (PVL), and hemolysis can still occur with this device.Case summaryA 75-year-old frail elderly female with severe mitral regurgitation (MR) and recurrent heart failure was admitted to our institution for treatment. TMVI was successfully completed. The patient was subsequently rehospitalized owing to decompensated heart failure, PVL and severe hemolysis due to late valve dislodgement. We then performed valve re-tensioning via the prior thoracotomy, which resolved PVL and relieved hemolysis.DiscussionSubacute dislodgement of the prosthesis can still occur following TMVI with apical fixation design, leading to severe PVL and hemolysis. Accurate diagnosis using transesophageal echocardiography (TEE) combined with prompt re-tensioning is critical for the management of this complication.

Background

TMVI is now an established therapeutic option for selected patients presenting with symptomatic severe MR [1, 2]. Severe complications including LVOT obstruction, valve dislodgement, PVL and hemolysis may still develop post-procedure [3–5]. Herein, we present a case in which PVL and hemolysis due to late valve dislodgement after TMVI were successfully managed with valve re-tensioning.

History of presentation

A 75-year-old frail female patient (160 cm in height, 50 kg in weight) with severe MR was transferred to our center due to recurrent heart failure episodes, despite receiving optimal medical treatment.

Past medical history

The patient’s past medical history was notable for prior percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) for coronary artery disease, cardiac resynchronization therapy with defibrillator (CRT-D) implantation for chronic heart failure, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), peripheral vascular disease (PVD), and chronic renal dysfunction.

Investigation

Transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) demonstrated severe MR and a reduced left ventricular ejection fraction of 38%. The Society of Thoracic Surgeons (STS) risk score for open cardiac surgery was 9.72%. The patient was further assessed for TEER. TEE identified diffuse mitral valve leaflet lesions of mixed origin: the entire posterior leaflet was restricted and the anterior leaflet prolapsed, rendering TEER contraindicated (Fig. 1A-C). TMVI was therefore evaluated. Pre-procedural cardiac computed tomography provided the following measurements (systole/diastole): mitral annular area 9.6 cm²/10.9 cm², perimeter 118.4 mm / 121.6 mm, commissure-to-commissure diameter 37.9 mm/37 mm, and anteroposterior diameter 32.5 mm/36.1 mm (Fig. 2A-B). Preoperative CT implantation simulation with TruDelta™ TMVI system revealed low risk for LVOT obstruction (a systolic neo-LVOT area of 468 mm² with the 35 L transcatheter mitral valve)(Fig. 2C-D).

Device used

The TruDelta™ system (Zhenyi Medical, Jiangsu, China) (Fig. 1H) is composed of a self-expanding D-shaped nitinol outer frame stent and round-shaped inner frame stent which is sutured with anti-calcified bovine pericardium. The apical fixation of the TruDelta™ system consists of tether cords that are connected to the outer frame and apical anchoring pads, sharing a similar design with the Tendyne system (Abbott Vascular, Santa Clara, California, USA). This prosthetic valve system is available in 15 sizes and paired with a 33 F transapical delivery system, which can accommodate the anatomical characteristics of the vast majority of patients. Distinctively, the TruDelta™ prosthesis features an innovative inward retraction of the ventricular segment of the outer frame after implantation, which effectively reduces the risk of LVOT obstruction.

Management

After informed consent was obtained, TMVI was carried out under general anesthesia, guided solely by TEE, via a left mini-thoracotomy at the fifth intercostal space. A 35 L TruDelta™ transcatheter mitral valve was used. Because of poor acoustic visualization, 3D multi-planar imaging was used to confirm proper device positioning (Fig. 1D–E). The bioprosthesis was deployed accurately, and the apical tether was firmly secured. No PVL or LVOT obstruction was observed post-operatively and optimal valve function and position (Fig. 1F–G). The patient had an uncomplicated post-procedural course and was discharged successfully.

However, the patient presented with signs of decompensated heart failure and was readmitted two weeks after discharge. Hemoglobin decreased significantly to a nadir of 67 g/L despite transfusion. Upon admission, plasma free hemoglobin increased markedly to 2565 mg/L, along with gross hemoglobinuria (Fig. 3). Progressive hemolytic anemia was identified as the primary contributor to persistent dyspnea and repeated cardiac decompensation. Suboptimal acoustic windows rendered TTE non-diagnostic. Follow-up TEE demonstrated PVL secondary to mild atrial migration of the mitral bioprosthesis, with a high-velocity regurgitant jet of 4–5 m/s. The 3D en-face imaging clearly visualized a gap between the valve skirt and left atrium (Fig. 4A–C). Although PVL appeared mild on 2D images, 3D color Doppler confirmed moderate to severe regurgitation.

Valve re-tensioning via the previous thoracotomy incision was performed to reduce PVL, resolve hemolysis and alleviate clinical symptoms. This reoperation was carried out in the hybrid operating room with a primed cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB) circuit on standby to address potential valve dislocation. Prior to the intervention, the operators practiced the transapical valve re-tensioning process using the two hemostatic forceps method. During the operation, the apical pad was unlocked, and the residual tether length was secured with the two forceps. Under TEE guidance, the tether was then manually withdrawn approximately 2 cm out of the left ventricle gradually by pulling the two forceps sequentially (Fig. 4D). This maneuver improved the apposition of the valve frame and nearly eliminated the anterior PVL (Fig. 4E-F). Postoperatively, the patient’s hemolytic anemia resolved, and the plasma free hemoglobin level returned to normal. The patient was discharged without complications.

Follow-up

At the 3-month follow-up, the patient remained in good condition with no signs or symptoms of heart failure.

Discussion

To date, the Tendyne system (Abbott Vascular, Santa Clara, California) — a TMVI device with an apical fixation design — remains the most mature TMVI system worldwide. Its efficacy and feasibility for treating primary and secondary MR, as well as for patients with severe mitral annular calcification, have been well documented [1, 2]. In the present case, we adopted the domestically developed TruDelta™ device. This system features a self-expanding D-shaped double-layer nitinol stent and a round-shaped inner stent equipped with bovine pericardial leaflets, which is anchored to the left ventricular apex via a tether pad. It bears similarities to the Tendyne system, which is not available in China.

The first challenge of this procedure was poor TEE image quality caused by cardiac transposition. Due to deviation of the sampling line in 3D X-plane imaging, TEE failed to obtain a clear bi-commissural view. In this case, we adopted 3D multi-view imaging and adjusted the sampling line in the bi-commissural view to acquire optimal LVOT images. With the guidance of both 3D en-face and 3D multi-view imaging, the device was precisely deployed at the mitral annulus. Advanced 3D imaging tools are helpful in cases with suboptimal acoustic windows to maintain coaxiality during valve implantation.

LVOT obstruction is a potentially severe complication of TMVI. In this patient, due to marked left ventricular (LV) enlargement and a favorable aortomitral angle, the neo-LVOT remained unobstructed with adequate patency after valve implantation [3]. Hemolysis is another serious complication following TMVI. In this case, due to left ventricular remodeling and potential elongation of the fixation suture, subacute prosthesis dislodgement occurred approximately two weeks post-procedure, resulting in significant PVL and hemolysis. Also in the Tendyne Global Feasibility Study, clinically relevant hemolysis complicated by symptomatic anemia was rare, occurring in 3 out of 100 patients. Sporadic cases have also indicate that late valve dislodgement is the primary cause of severe hemolysis after TMVI [4–6]. For this complication, accurate diagnosis using 3D TEE combined with timely re-tensioning via the original transthoracic access is key to successful management of a TMVI device with an apical fixation design [6].

Previous literature has also reported a rare case of late dislocation of a transcatheter mitral prosthesis (Tendyne system) after implantation, in which attempted valve re-tensioning failed and the patient ultimately underwent open surgical repair [7]. Learning from this case, two potential causes for the failure of secondary valve re-tensioning can be summarized. First, annular calcification within the PVL region may hinder the downward displacement of the prosthetic valve toward the ventricular side. Second, the re-tensioning procedure was performed nearly one year after the initial TMVI, by which time complete endothelialization of the prosthetic valve and the PVL interface had already been achieved [7]. In contrast, valve re-tensioning was performed at two weeks postoperatively in the present case, allowing for easier re-fixation of the prosthesis via the original operative access. Accordingly, we speculate that the timing of secondary intervention is a critical determinant of procedural success for salvage valve re-tensioning. However, given the inherent limitations of single-case reports, larger-scale clinical investigations are warranted in the future to verify the above-mentioned speculations.

Conclusions

TMVI serves as a viable therapeutic strategy for patients with MR and complex mitral valve anatomy; however, close monitoring for postoperative complications (e.g., hemolysis, PVL) is indispensable. Combining detailed follow-up information (e.g., new-onset fatigue, oliguria, or dyspnea) with 3D multi-view TEE imaging facilitates early detection of significant PVL and hemolysis. Meanwhile, valve re-tensioning for a TMVI device with an apical fixation design is an effective salvage treatment method for valve dislodgement.

Take home messages

3D multi-view imaging can effectively ensure precise positioning and implantation of the prosthetic valve during TMVI. Valve re-tensioning for a TMVI device with an apical fixation design is an effective salvage treatment for late valve dislodgement.
