Visualizing conjugation in affine Coxeter groups
Amy Herron, Anne Thomas

Abstract
. Affine Coxeter groups are fundamental objects in mathematics and in crystallography. If two group elements are conjugate, then they have very similar algebraic and geometric properties. Using recent structural results of Milićević, Schwer and the second author, we develop an app to visualize conjugation in affine Coxeter groups in dimensions 2 and 3. The resulting pictures exhibit beautiful geometric symmetries.



1.
Introduction

We describe an app we have developed to visualize important structures in affine Coxeter groups. These collections of symmetries are also known as affine Weyl groups and Euclidean reflection groups. Affine Coxeter groups have long been studied in algebra and geometry, and they are a key family in crystallography: in dimensions 2 and 3, every crystallographic group has finite index in some affine Coxeter group (see, for example,
[
MST25a
, Appendix A]
).

An affine Coxeter group
W
is, by definition, a discrete group of Euclidean isometries generated by reflections in the faces of a convex polytope
P
in Euclidean space. The action of
W
induces a tiling of Euclidean space by copies of
P
, and the elements of
W
can then be identified with the tiles. A first example is
W
of type
\tilde{A}_{2}
, which induces the tiling of the Euclidean plane by equilateral triangles, as depicted in Figure
1
.

Figure 1.
For
W
of type
\tilde{A}_{2}
and
x=s_{0120102}=t^{2\alpha_{1}^{\vee}+2\alpha_{2}^{\vee}}s_{1}\in W
, the conjugacy class
[x]
consists of the “lines” of pink, purple, and yellow tiles depicted on the left. For this same
x
and
y=s_{21021021020}=t^{-2\alpha_{1}^{\vee}-3\alpha_{2}^{\vee}}s_{2}\in[x]
, the coconjugation set
\operatorname{C}(x,y)
consists of the “lines” of purple and green tiles shown on the right. The tile
x
is outlined in red on both left and right, and
y
is outlined in blue on the right.

Two elements
x
and
y
of
W
are
conjugate
if there is some
z\in W
such that
zxz^{-1}=y
, and the
conjugacy class
of
x\in W
is the set of all conjugates of
x
:

[x]=\{zxz^{-1}\mid z\in W\}.

Elements of the same conjugacy class have closely related properties. Algebraically, if
\varphi:W\to A
is a homomorphism from
W
to an abelian group
A
, then for every
y\in[x]
we have
\varphi(y)=\varphi(x)
. Geometrically, every conjugate of a reflection (respectively, translation) in
W
is another reflection (respectively, translation). The set of elements which conjugate
x
to itself is called the
centralizer
of
x
:

\operatorname{C}(x)=\{z\in W\mid zxz^{-1}=x\}.

More generally, if
x
and
y
are conjugate then the corresponding
coconjugation set
is given by

\operatorname{C}(x,y)=\{z\in W\mid zxz^{-1}=y\}.

In this paper we describe an app
AffineCoxeterExplorer.java

[
Her26
]
that we have developed to visualize conjugacy classes and coconjugation sets in affine Coxeter groups in dimensions 2 and 3. Using the identification of the elements of
W
with the tiles in the induced tesselation, we shade all tiles which are in the desired conjugacy class or coconjugation set (and which lie inside a bounding box of size chosen by the user). For example, in type
\tilde{A}_{2}
, Figure
1
shows the output from our app for a conjugacy class
[x]
and a coconjugation set
\operatorname{C}(x,y)
. (All figures appearing in this paper were generated using version 1.0.1 of our app.)

Our first goal with this visualization is to illustrate how both conjugacy classes and coconjugation sets lie along certain classically-defined subspaces.
For example, in both parts of Figure
1
, the shaded tiles lie along certain lines. To make this more precise, write
\mathbb{E}
for the Euclidean space tesselated by the action of
W
. Then the conjugacy class
[x]
lies along certain images of an affine subspace of
\mathbb{E}
called the
move-set
:

\textsc{Mov}(x)=\{q\in\mathbb{E}\mid q=xp-p\mbox{ for some }p\in\mathbb{E}\}=\operatorname{range}(x-I)=(x-I)\mathbb{E},

where
I
is the identity map on
\mathbb{E}
.
Now any
y\in W
can be decomposed uniquely as the product of a translation
t^{\lambda}
and an element
w\in W
which fixes the origin. Given
y=t^{\lambda}w
, the coconjugation set
\operatorname{C}(x,y)
then lies along certain images of a linear subspace of
\mathbb{E}
called the
fix-set
:

\textsc{Fix}(w)=\{p\in\mathbb{E}\mid wp=p\}=\ker(w-I).

Figure
2
illustrates this in type
\tilde{A}_{3}
.

Figure 2.
For
W
of type
\tilde{A}_{3}
and

, the conjugacy class
[x]
consists of the sheets of pink, green, and yellow tiles depicted in two different views on the left and in the center. The centralizer
\operatorname{C}(x)
consists of the lines of pink, green, yellow, and blue tiles shown on the right. Here,
\textsc{Mov}(x)
is
2
-dimensional and
\textsc{Fix}(x)=\textsc{Fix}(w)
is
1
-dimensional.

Our second goal is to illustrate some finer geometry for conjugacy classes. The
coroot lattice

L
is the set of vectors
\lambda\in\mathbb{E}
so that
W
contains the translation by
\lambda
; we indicate the elements of
L
by heavy black dots in our
2
-dimensional figures. The
mod-set
for an element
x\in W
is then the
L
-analog of the move-set:

\textsc{Mod}(x)=\{\mu\in L\mid\mu=x\lambda-\lambda\mbox{ for some }\lambda\in L\}=(x-I)L.

Only the tiles along the (images of the) move-set which have translation part in the (images of the) mod-set are actually in the conjugacy class
[x]
. For example, in Figure
3
, along the horizontal and vertical lines of tiles, only every second translation by an element of
L
is in
[x]
.

Figure 3.
The conjugacy class of the element
x=s_{201210121}=t^{2\alpha_{1}^{\vee}+2\alpha_{2}^{\vee}}s_{2}
in
W
of type
\tilde{C}_{2}
consists of the horizontal lines of purple tiles, and the vertical lines of yellow tiles. Notice the “gaps” between shaded alcoves here.

The geometric description of conjugacy classes and coconjugation sets that we have just sketched is established in recent work of Milićević, Schwer and the second author
[
MST25a
,
MST25b
]
. This description actually holds for many other Euclidean isometry groups, including the full isometry group of Euclidean space (a non-discrete group). Thus our app illustrates, for an important family of groups, a much wider connection between algebra and geometry. It also, we believe, results in some beautiful figures.

2.1.
Affine Coxeter groups

We recall some key notions concerning affine Coxeter groups. A concise reference for this material is
[
Ron09
, Chapter 2]
, and the standard reference is
[
Hum90
]
. As in
[
MST25a
,
MST25b
]
, we follow the conventions of
[
Bou02
]
.

A
Coxeter group
is a group
W
with presentation of the form

W=\langle S\mid(st)^{m_{st}}\mbox{ for all }s,t\in S\rangle

where
m_{ss}=1
for all
s\in S
, and
m_{st}=m_{ts}\in\{2,3,4,\dots\}\cup\infty
if
s,t\in S
are distinct. Here,
m_{st}=\infty
means that the product
st
has infinite order. Given
x\in W
, an expression
x=s_{i_{1}}\dots s_{i_{k}}
where
s_{i_{1}},\dots,s_{i_{k}}\in S
is called a
word
for
x
, and is a
reduced
word if no shorter product of elements of
S
equals
x
. We often abbreviate a product
s_{i_{1}}\dots s_{i_{k}}
to
s_{i_{1}\dots i_{k}}
, as in Figures
1
–
3
. We write
e
for the identity element of
W
, which is represented by the empty word.

For
W
and
S
as in the previous paragraph, the pair
(W,S)
is called a
Coxeter system
.
A Coxeter system
(W,S)
is
irreducible
if there is no nontrivial partition of
S
into two disjoint commuting subsets,
spherical
if
W
is finite, and
affine
if
S
is the set of reflections in the faces of a compact polytope
P
in some Euclidean space
\mathbb{E}
. For convenience, if
\dim(\mathbb{E})=n
, we define the corresponding affine Coxeter system
(W,S)
to be
n
-dimensional
(this terminology is not standardized).

The irreducible affine Coxeter systems were classified by Coxeter in the 1930s, and correspond to the case that
P
is a simplex. Hence if
(W,S)
is
n
-dimensional and irreducible affine, the generating set
S
has
n+1
elements; these are denoted
s_{0},s_{1},\dots,s_{n}
. For all affine Coxeter systems, the action of
W
induces a tesselation of
\mathbb{E}
by copies of the polytope
P
. We call these copies of
P
the
alcoves
, and identify the elements of
W
with the alcoves in the induced tesselation.

Example 2.1
.

The
2
-dimensional affine Coxeter systems
(W,S)
are those where
W
is generated by the set
S
of reflections in the sides of a polygon
P
in the Euclidean plane. We list all such systems below by their classical type, and record the corresponding polygon
P
. These Coxeter systems are irreducible except for that of type
\tilde{A}_{1}\times\tilde{A}_{1}
, where
W
is the direct product of two copies of the infinite dihedral group.

(1)

Type
\tilde{A}_{2}
:
P
is an equilateral triangle, as seen in Figure
1
.

(2)

Type
\tilde{B}_{2}
and type
\tilde{C}_{2}
: in both these types,
P
is a right-angled isosceles triangle. (The coroot lattices in these types are not the same.) See Figure
3
for type
\tilde{C}_{2}
.

(3)

Type
\tilde{G}_{2}
:
P
is a triangle with vertex angles
\frac{\pi}{2}
,
\frac{\pi}{3}
, and
\frac{\pi}{6}
.

(4)

Type
\tilde{A}_{1}\times\tilde{A}_{1}
:
P
is a square.

.

The
3
-dimensional irreducible affine Coxeter systems
(W,S)
are those of types
\tilde{A}_{3}
,
\tilde{B}_{3}
and
\tilde{C}_{3}
. The corresponding simplex
P
(a tetrahedron) is different in each of these types. See Figure

.

Let
(W,S)
be an irreducible affine Coxeter system, with its natural action on
\mathbb{E}
. As in the introduction, the
coroot lattice

L
is the set of all vectors
\lambda\in\mathbb{E}
such that the translation by
\lambda
is in
W
. Write
t^{\lambda}
for the translation by
\lambda
, so that
W
has translation subgroup
\{t^{\lambda}\mid\lambda\in L\}
. The coroot lattice has canonical basis elements called the
simple coroots
and denoted
\alpha_{1}^{\vee},\dots,\alpha_{n}^{\vee}
, where
n=\dim(\mathbb{E})
. For example, the simple coroots
\alpha_{1}^{\vee}
and
\alpha_{2}^{\vee}
are depicted in Figures
1
and
3
.

Write
W_{0}
for the stabilizer of the origin in
W
, and define
S_{0}=W_{0}\cap S
. Then
(W_{0},S_{0})
is a spherical Coxeter system, and
W_{0}
is called the
finite Weyl group
. The alcoves containing the origin are exactly those corresponding to the elements of
W_{0}
, and every element
x\in W
can be expressed uniquely in the form
x=t^{\lambda}w
where
\lambda\in L
and
w\in W_{0}
. We then call
t^{\lambda}
the
translation part
of
x
, and
w\in W_{0}
its
spherical direction
. In fact,
W
is the semidirect product of its translation subgroup
\{t^{\lambda}\mid\lambda\in L\}
and its finite Weyl group
W_{0}
, with the action of
W_{0}
on translations given by
wt^{\lambda}w^{-1}=t^{w\lambda}
. If
(W,S)
is irreducible affine of type
\tilde{X}_{n}
, with
S=\{s_{0},s_{1},\dots,s_{n}\}
, then
(W_{0},S_{0})
is irreducible spherical of type
X_{n}
, with
S_{0}=\{s_{1},\dots,s_{n}\}
.

(2)

(3)

Type

is the dihedral group of order

.

(4)

Type
A_{1}\times A_{1}
: the group
W_{0}=\langle s_{1},s_{2}\mid s_{1}^{2}=s_{2}^{2}=(s_{1}s_{2})^{2}\rangle
is the Klein

-group i.e. the direct product of two groups of order

.

Example 2.4
.

The finite Weyl group of type
A_{3}
has
24
elements, and can be identified with the symmetric group
\operatorname{Sym}(4)
. The finite Weyl groups in type

and type

are isomorphic groups with

2.2.
The geometry of conjugacy classes and coconjugation sets

We now briefly recall the relevant results of Milićević, Schwer and the second author
[
MST25a
,
MST25b
]
. See the introduction for the definitions and our notation for conjugacy classes and coconjugation sets, together with move-sets, fix-sets, and mod-sets.

Let
(W,S)
be an
n
-dimensional affine Coxeter system, with coroot lattice
L
and finite Weyl group
W_{0}
. We first recall some relationships between the mod-set
\textsc{Mod}(w)
, the move-set
\textsc{Mov}(w)
, and the coroot lattice
L
, for elements
w\in W_{0}
. It is immediate from definitions that
\textsc{Mod}(w)
is contained in
\textsc{Mov}(w)
and in
L
. Hence
\textsc{Mod}(w)\subseteq\textsc{Mov}(w)\cap L
. Moreover, the lattice
L
can be viewed as a free abelian
\mathbb{Z}
-module (of rank
n
), and then both
\textsc{Mod}(w)
and the intersection
\textsc{Mov}(w)\cap L
are submodules of
L
. The next statement is a special case of Corollary 1.9(a) of
[
MST25a
]
.

Theorem 2.5
.

For any
w\in W_{0}
, the mod-set
\textsc{Mod}(w)
has finite index in
\textsc{Mov}(w)\cap L
.

In general,
\textsc{Mod}(w)
is a proper submodule of
\textsc{Mov}(w)\cap L
. For example, in type
\tilde{C}_{2}
, if
w=s_{2}
then
\textsc{Mod}(w)
has index
2
in
\textsc{Mov}(w)\cap L
(this explains the “gaps” between alcoves in the conjugacy class depicted in Figure
3
).

To fully describe conjugacy classes in
W
we draw on the following statement, which is a special case of Theorem 1.2 of
[
MST25b
]
.

Theorem 2.6

(Conjugacy classes)
.

Let
x=t^{\lambda}w\in W
, where
\lambda\in L
and
w\in W_{0}
. Then the conjugacy class of
x
in
W
satisfies

[x]=\bigcup_{u\in W_{0}}t^{u(\lambda+\textsc{Mod}(w))}uwu^{-1}\subseteq\bigcup_{u\in W_{0}}t^{u(\lambda+\textsc{Mov}(w))}uwu^{-1}=\bigcup_{u\in W_{0}}t^{u\textsc{Mov}(x)}uwu^{-1}.

That is, for any
x=t^{\lambda}w\in W
the conjugacy class
[x]
is found by, for each
u\in W_{0}
, translating
uwu^{-1}
by all elements of
u(\lambda+\textsc{Mod}(w))
. Since
\textsc{Mod}(w)\subseteq\textsc{Mov}(w)
, we get the stated containment, and as
\textsc{Mov}(x)=\lambda+\textsc{Mov}(w)
(by
[
LMPS19
, Proposition 1.21]
), we obtain the final equality.

In order to describe coconjugation sets in
W
, we need some additional definitions. For
w,w^{\prime}\in W_{0}
, the associated
spherical coconjugation set
is given by

\operatorname{C}_{W_{0}}(w,w^{\prime})=\{u\in W_{0}\mid uwu^{-1}=w^{\prime}\}=\operatorname{C}(w,w^{\prime})\cap W_{0}.

Now let
x=t^{\lambda}w
and
x^{\prime}=t^{\lambda^{\prime}}w^{\prime}
be elements of
W
, where
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in L
and
w,w^{\prime}\in W_{0}
. The corresponding
translation-compatible part
of
\operatorname{C}_{W_{0}}(w,w^{\prime})
is the set

\operatorname{C}_{W_{0}}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(w,w^{\prime})=\{u\in\operatorname{C}_{W_{0}}(w,w^{\prime})\mid\lambda^{\prime}-u\lambda\in\textsc{Mod}(w^{\prime})\}.

The next theorem describes coconjugation sets, and is a special case of Theorem 1.13 of
[
MST25b
]
.

Theorem 2.7

(Coconjugation sets)
.

Let
x=t^{\lambda}w
and
x^{\prime}=t^{\lambda^{\prime}}w^{\prime}
be elements of
W
, where
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in L
and
w,w^{\prime}\in W_{0}
. Then

(1)

\operatorname{C}(x,x^{\prime})\neq\emptyset\;\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\operatorname{C}_{W_{0}}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(w,w^{\prime})\neq\emptyset.

Moreover, if these sets are nonempty, then

(2)

\operatorname{C}(x,x^{\prime})=\bigsqcup_{u\in\operatorname{C}_{W_{0}}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(w,w^{\prime})}t^{\eta_{u}+(\textsc{Fix}(w^{\prime})\cap L)}u

where for each
u
, the element
\eta_{u}\in L
is a solution to the equation

(3)

(I-w^{\prime})\eta=\lambda^{\prime}-u\lambda.

That is, the coconjugation set
\operatorname{C}(x,x^{\prime})
has a closed-form description involving
\operatorname{C}_{W_{0}}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(w,w^{\prime})
and hence
\textsc{Mod}(w^{\prime})
, and its shape is described by translates of
\textsc{Fix}(w^{\prime})
. The reason
\textsc{Fix}(w^{\prime})
appears in this statement is that, given one solution
\eta=\eta_{u}
to Equation (
3
), all solutions to this equation are of the form
\eta_{u}+\eta
where
\eta
is a solution to the homogeneous equation
(I-w^{\prime})\eta=0
, equivalently,
\eta\in\textsc{Fix}(w^{\prime})
.

Remark 2.8
.

For all irreducible affine Coxeter systems, further results of
[
MST25a
]
give type-by-type descriptions of mod-sets, and so determine in particular when
\textsc{Mod}(w)=\textsc{Mov}(w)\cap L
. We do not use these descriptions in our visualization, instead computing mod-sets directly, so do not recall these results from
[
MST25a
]
here.

•

. The first method corresponds to the presentation of

as a group generated by

. The elements of the generating set

for

are indexed by SageMath as

or

for the cases we consider. Now given

from Figure

are

,
0121012
(both of which are reduced), or

(which is not reduced). The identity element is input by leaving the entry box empty.

The second method for inputting elements of
W
corresponds to
W
being the semidirect product of its translation subgroup by its finite Weyl group
W_{0}
. For any
x\in W
, we have
x=t^{\lambda}w
for a unique
\lambda\in L
and
w\in W_{0}
. Then
\lambda=c_{1}\alpha_{1}^{\vee}+\dots+c_{n}\alpha_{n}^{\vee}
for unique coefficients
c_{1},\dots,c_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
, where
\alpha_{1}^{\vee},\dots,\alpha_{n}^{\vee}

(again, this word for

3.2.
Output and visual features

In this section we describe the output of our app, which comprises both text and graphics. The visual features of the app have been chosen carefully, as we explain.

The text output, which appears in the right-hand column of the app, is the SageMath output. It provides coordinates for the simple coroots, and then a list of all alcoves in the conjugacy class (respectively, coconjugation set) which lie inside the selected bounding box. These alcoves are listed using both their reduced word which is first in lexicographic order, and their translation part (with respect to the basis of simple coroots) and spherical direction. Thus once a conjugacy class
[x]
is computed, we have easy access to a list of elements to which
x
is conjugate. Any of these elements can then be used in addition to
x
to visualize a nonempty coconjugation set.

The graphical output shows the tesselation of the Euclidean plane by alcoves in the
2
-dimensional cases, and by wireframe alcoves (the 1-skeleton of the induced tessselation) in the
3
-dimensional cases. In dimension
2
this output is displayed in the center of the app, while in dimension
3
a browser window will open, and the user can then use the mouse to zoom in or out and rotate the wireframe around, in order to better understand the image. In dimension
2
, the coroot lattice vertices are indicated by heavy black dots, and the coroot basis vectors
\alpha_{1}^{\vee}
and
\alpha_{2}^{\vee}
are drawn in bold red and bold blue, respectively (see Figures
1
and
3
). We decided not to display this coroot lattice information in dimension
3
, so as to avoid cluttering the image.

In the graphical output, certain alcoves are decorated to indicate mathematical information. The alcoves in the computed conjugacy class (respectively, coconjugation set) which lie within the chosen bounding box are shaded, with each spherical direction assigned a different color as explained further in Section
3.3
. This choice of shading helps the user to see that conjugacy classes lie along images of move-sets, and that coconjugation sets lie along images of fix-sets. We decided not to depict the move-set or fix-set itself, because the pictures became too cluttered, especially in the 3-dimensional cases, and by Theorem
2.5
the move-set can be inferred from the mod-set.

To help locate the computed conjugacy class (respectively, coconjugation set), in both dimensions
2
and
3
, the identity alcove is shaded and labeled
e
. If
e
is an element of the given conjugacy class (respectively, coconjugation set), then
e
is also striped. This can be seen in Figure
4
, which depicts the conjugacy class
[e]=\{e\}
in the
3
-dimensional cases. In dimension
2
, we additionally shade and label all elements of the finite Weyl group
W_{0}
, and stripe them if they are in the computed conjugacy class (respectively, coconjugation set). In dimension
3
, since the finite Weyl group
W_{0}
has either 24 or 48 elements (see Example
2.4
), such shading and labeling of the entire group
W_{0}
is not feasible. Instead, we indicate the origin in dimension
3
by a red dot.

So that the user can easily compare input and output, the alcove(s) for the user-supplied element(s) are labeled by the corresponding reduced word
s_{i_{1}\dots s_{i_{k}}}
which is first in lexicographic order, and shaded according to their spherical direction. In addition, for the conjugacy class
[x]
and the coconjugation set
\operatorname{C}(x,y)
, the alcove
x
is outlined in bold red, and for the coconjugation set
\operatorname{C}(x,y)
, the alcove
y
is outlined in bold blue.

3.3.
Color choices

As explained in Section
3.2
, certain alcoves are shaded in our graphical output, according to their spherical direction. In this section, we explain these color choices in more detail.

The
2
-dimensional affine Coxeter groups of types
\tilde{A}_{1}\times\tilde{A}_{1}
,
\tilde{A}_{2}
,
\tilde{B}_{2}
,
\tilde{C}_{2}
, and
\tilde{G}_{2}
have finite Weyl groups of orders 4, 6, 8, 8, and 12, respectively (see Example
2.3
). For these finite Weyl groups, we simply assign colors chosen for maximum contrast to each element of
W_{0}
.

In dimension
3
, the finite Weyl groups corresponding to types
\tilde{A}_{3}
,
\tilde{B}_{3}
, and
\tilde{C}_{3}
contain 24, 48, and 48 elements, respectively (see Example
2.4
). This makes it infeasible to assign a visually distinct color to each finite Weyl group element. To manage this, we observe that if
x=t^{\lambda}w
and
x^{\prime}=t^{\lambda^{\prime}}w^{\prime}
are conjugate elements of
W
, with
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in L
and
w,w^{\prime}\in W_{0}
, then
w
and
w^{\prime}
must be conjugate in
W_{0}
. Thus it is enough to assign visually distinct colors to the elements of each conjugacy class in
W_{0}
. Now it is well known that two permutations are conjugate in a symmetric group if and only if they have the same cycle type. Thus in order to determine conjugacy classes in
W_{0}
in dimension
3
, it makes sense to find an injective homomorphism from
W_{0}
to a symmetric group.

For all
n\geq 1
, the finite Coxeter group of type
A_{n}
is isomorphic to the symmetric group
\operatorname{Sym}(n+1)
. Thus in particular, if
(W,S)
is of type
\tilde{A}_{3}
, then the finite Weyl group
W_{0}
is isomorphic to
\operatorname{Sym}(4)
.
For
W_{0}
of type
B_{3}
or
C_{3}
, let
t_{1},\dots,t_{5}
be the Coxeter generators for the finite Weyl group of type
A_{5}
, which is isomorphic to
\operatorname{Sym}(6)
. Then there is an injective homomorphism
\phi:W_{0}\to\operatorname{Sym}(6)
induced by
s_{1}\mapsto t_{1}t_{5}
,
s_{2}\mapsto t_{2}t_{4}
, and
s_{3}\mapsto t_{3}
. We use these identifications of the finite Weyl groups in types
\tilde{A}_{3}
,
\tilde{B}_{3}
, and
\tilde{C}_{3}
with a subgroup of a symmetric group to assign visually distinct colors to elements of
W_{0}
that have the same cycle type.

3.4.
Outline of code

We conclude by giving an overview of the code for our app.

The code for the
2
-dimensional affine Coxeter groups differs substantially from that for the
3
-dimensional cases. This is in part because built-in graphics are available for the
2
-dimensional tesselations, but not the
3
-dimensional ones. Also, we display coroot lattice information in dimension
2
but not in dimension
3
, and our method of assigning colors to spherical directions differs between these dimensions, as explained in Section
3.3
. We are, however, able to obtain reduced words and the translation parts and spherical directions for elements of conjugacy classes and coconjugation sets using existing SageMath code for extended affine Weyl groups, in both dimensions
2
and
3
. In both dimensions, we build labels for alcoves by hand, in order to modify the label font depending on the size of the individual alcoves.

For the
2
-dimensional cases, we first find Euclidean coordinates for the simple coroots
\alpha_{1}^{\vee}
and
\alpha_{2}^{\vee}
in each type. We then use this basis for
L
to find all the coroot lattice vertices which are contained within the user-specified bounding box.

To find the elements of the conjugacy class of
x=t^{\lambda}w\in W
, we start by computing
(w-I)\alpha_{i}^{\vee}
for each simple coroot
\alpha_{i}^{\vee}
. The mod-set
\textsc{Mod}(w)=(w-I)L
will be the
\mathbb{Z}
-span of these vectors. We then apply each
u\in W_{0}
to the user-supplied vector
\lambda
and to each

(w-I)\alpha_{i}^{\vee}
, and restrict the resulting values of
t^{u\lambda+u\textsc{Mod}(w)}
to those within the user-specified bounding box.
Then for all
u\in W_{0}
, we conjugate the spherical direction
w
of the user-supplied element by
u
as well, to obtain
uwu^{-1}
. Pairing these, we obtain

\displaystyle[x]=[t^{\lambda}w]=\bigcup_{u\in W_{0}}t^{u(\lambda+\textsc{Mod}(w))}uwu^{-1},

as in the statement of Theorem
2.6
.

To find the elements of the coconjugation set
\operatorname{C}(x,x^{\prime})
in dimension
2
, where
x=t^{\lambda}w
and
x^{\prime}=t^{\lambda^{\prime}}w^{\prime}
, we first compute the spherical coconjugation set
\operatorname{C}_{W_{0}}(w,w^{\prime})
, and then determine its translation-compatible part
\operatorname{C}_{W_{0}}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(w,w^{\prime})
. Assuming that
\operatorname{C}_{W_{0}}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(w,w^{\prime})
is nonempty, for each
u
in
\operatorname{C}_{W_{0}}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(w,w^{\prime})
we find all solutions
\eta_{u}
to the equation
(I-w^{\prime})\eta=\lambda^{\prime}-u\lambda
which lie within our bounding box.
These
\eta_{u}
become the translation part and the
u
becomes the spherical direction of the elements that are in the coconjugation set
\operatorname{C}(x,x^{\prime})
, as given by Theorem
2.7
.

In
3
dimensions, we need to build the tesselations for our graphical output by hand. For this, we get the coordinates for the identity alcove from SageMath, then act by the finite Weyl group
W_{0}
on this alcove to obtain all alcoves containing the origin. We now take these finite Weyl group alcoves and translate them across
3
-dimensional space, using the coroot lattice
L
. The balance of the logic remains the same for the 3-dimensional cases as it does for the 2-dimensional cases.

In both dimensions
2
and
3
, we used Claude (version Opus 4.6) by Anthropic, an artificial intelligence source, to provide basic programming assistance and to consolidate the SageMath code into the Java app. Claude also tweaked the SageMath code to make the app display better. We tested the app to ensure that its output matches that produced directly by SageMath. Claude also provided most of the 3-dimensional graphics code, and we tested this code to ensure that it has the correct output. Lastly, Claude wrote the README file.
