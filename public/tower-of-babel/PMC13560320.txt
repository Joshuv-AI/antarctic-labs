The critical role of the RUNX1/NF-κB transcriptional complex–mediated PVAT–VSMC axis in aortic dissection
Authors: Xin Guo, Baohui Liu, Yuexin Han, Xizeng Si, Yifan Zhou, Yujiu Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Aortic dissection (AD) is a devastating vascular disorder pathologically characterized by pathological phenotypic switching of medial vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs) and progressive breakdown of extracellular matrix (ECM) architecture. Conventional paradigms have primarily emphasized unidirectional injury originating within the vessel wall. Nevertheless, accumulating evidence indicates that perivascular adipose tissue (PVAT), which directly envelops the adventitia and is increasingly regarded as the “fourth layer” of the vascular wall, exerts a critical outside-in influence on vascular homeostasis and microenvironmental imbalance. This review systematically delineates how perivascular adipose tissue (PVAT) shifts from a vasoprotective component to a pathogenic source under conditions of local inflammation or metabolic stress. We particularly focus on the pathological state in which PVAT releases a broad array of pro-inflammatory mediators through paracrine signaling, most notably transforming growth factor-β1 (TGF-β1), which crosses tissue boundaries to orchestrate microenvironmental crosstalk between the adventitia and the media.Under persistent TGF-β1 stimulation, vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs) may develop a cooperative transcriptional regulatory network centered on Runt-related transcription factor 1 (RUNX1) and nuclear factor-κB (NF-κB). This review explores the hypothesis that PVAT-derived inflammatory stimuli may induce post-receptor TGF-β1 signal shunting, thereby favoring the formation of a RUNX1/NF-κB regulatory axis in VSMCs. Such a network may contribute to aortic wall destabilization by suppressing contractile phenotype-associated genes while promoting inflammatory and extracellular matrix remodeling programs. From an “outside-in” perspective, we discuss the potential role of PVAT–VSMC microenvironmental crosstalk in AD pathogenesis and critically evaluate the current evidence supporting this model, as well as its limitations. Furthermore, we propose that future therapeutic strategies targeting this transcriptional axis may require localized delivery approaches to minimize systemic adverse effects, including bleeding and immunosuppression, thereby offering a potential avenue for precision treatment of AD.

Introduction

Traditionally, cardiovascular pathology has focused primarily on the intima, media, and adventitia of the vascular wall, with aortic remodeling generally considered to originate from hemodynamic alterations or intimal injury.This traditional view follows the “inside-out” model of vascular pathology, in which disease is thought to originate at the luminal surface of the vessel and subsequently extend outward into the media and adventitia [1]. However, accumulating evidence in recent years suggests that the perivascular microenvironment can also actively contribute to disease initiation and progression. In contrast, the “outside-in” paradigm proposes that pathological alterations first occur in extravascular components, including perivascular adipose tissue (PVAT), adventitial immune cells, and the vasa vasorum, which then influence medial vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs) and vascular wall homeostasis through inflammatory mediators and paracrine signaling.However, as insights into the vascular microenvironment continue to expand, perivascular adipose tissue (PVAT), which is anatomically adjacent to the adventitia, has gained growing attention and is increasingly recognized as the “fourth vascular layer” with an important role in maintaining vascular stability and homeostasis [2]. Accumulating evidence has demonstrated that PVAT not only modulates vascular tone and inflammatory responses via endocrine and paracrine mechanisms, but also contributes to vascular remodeling and the development of cardiovascular diseases. Notably, PVAT dysfunction is closely linked to pathological conditions such as hypertension and atherosclerosis [3, 4].

Under physiological conditions, perivascular adipose tissue (PVAT) acts as an active perivascular endocrine organ, releasing protective factors such as adiponectin and nitric oxide (NO) to maintain vascular dilation, anti-inflammatory effects, and overall vascular homeostasis. However, under pathological conditions such as obesity, hypertension, or abnormal biomechanical stress, PVAT can become dysfunctional (PVAT dysfunction), characterized by reduced protective adipokines and increased infiltration of pro-inflammatory cells, gradually transforming it from a vascular-protective tissue into a source of pro-inflammatory and pro-fibrotic signals [5–7].

Under pathological conditions, PVAT progressively transitions from a vascular-protective tissue to a source of pro-inflammatory and pro-fibrotic mediators, with TGF-β1 being recognized as a pivotal paracrine regulator. Through an “outside-in” mode of signaling, these mediators influence the vascular wall microenvironment and contribute to inflammatory responses and vascular remodeling [8, 9]. Sustained TGF-β1 stimulation can promote VSMC phenotypic switching and activate downstream inflammatory signaling pathways, thereby facilitating pathological vascular remodeling and potentially establishing a molecular context for subsequent transcriptional reprogramming [10, 11].

Therefore, this review aims to systematically explore the hypothesis that PVAT-derived pro-inflammatory mediators, particularly transforming growth factor-β1 (TGF-β1), may drive pathological phenotypic switching of vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs) through RUNX1- and NF-κB-associated signaling networks under inflammatory stress. In addition, we discuss the potential contribution of PVAT–VSMC spatial crosstalk to the development of vascular remodeling diseases, including aortic dissection (AD) and aortic aneurysm.Although previous studies have independently demonstrated the importance of PVAT dysfunction, aberrant TGF-β1 activation, and VSMC phenotypic switching in the pathogenesis of aortic dissection (AD), these mechanisms have largely been investigated in isolation. Consequently, a coherent mechanistic framework explaining how extravascular microenvironmental signals drive sustained transcriptional reprogramming within the vascular wall remains lacking. Building upon recent advances in understanding the role of RUNX1 in vascular remodeling and inflammatory regulation, this review proposes a working hypothesis in which coordinated RUNX1–NF-κB transcriptional regulation serves as a central mechanistic hub linking PVAT-derived inflammatory signals to pathological VSMC remodeling [12, 13].

Inflammatory remodeling of perivascular adipose tissue (PVAT) and the TGF-β1-mediated outside-in vascular signaling mechanism

PVAT Phenotypic Remodeling and Macrophage Polarization–Driven Inflammatory Microenvironment Formation

PVAT dysfunction is often initiated by phenotypic remodeling and deterioration of the local microenvironment. Under pathological stimuli, aortic PVAT undergoes a process commonly referred to as “whitening,” which is characterized by reduced uncoupling protein 1 (UCP1) expression, adipocyte hypertrophy, and metabolic dysregulation [14–17]. Damaged adipocytes release damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) and chemokines such as CCL2/MCP-1, thereby promoting monocyte recruitment and inflammatory cell infiltration [18, 19]. Concurrently, local hypoxia, oxidative stress, and the accumulation of oxidized low-density lipoprotein (ox-LDL) further drive macrophage polarization toward a pro-inflammatory phenotype, resulting in sustained production of inflammatory mediators, including tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) and interleukin-6 (IL-6), and the establishment of a chronic inflammatory microenvironment [20, 21]. This inflammatory state not only suppresses the expression of vasculoprotective factors such as adiponectin but also progressively impairs the ability of PVAT to maintain vascular homeostasis [22]. However, persistent cytokine release alone is insufficient to fully explain the long-term structural remodeling of the aortic wall. Emerging evidence suggests that inflammatory stimuli must ultimately be converted into stable gene expression programs through specific transcriptional regulatory mechanisms, thereby driving sustained VSMC phenotypic switching and pathological vascular remodeling.

Paracrine Amplification of TGF-β1 Secretion from PVAT and the Development of a Fibrotic Microenvironment

Under persistent inflammatory stimulation, dysfunctional PVAT gradually becomes an important paracrine source of transforming growth factor-β1 (TGF-β1) [7]. This process involves the coordinated contribution of multiple cellular components within PVAT rather than a single cell type. In particular, the stromal vascular fraction (SVF), which contains fibroblasts, preadipocytes, and immune cells, exhibits enhanced profibrotic activity under conditions of oxidative stress, thereby promoting TGF-β1 expression and secretion [23]. In addition, adipocyte hypertrophy and microvascular dysfunction contribute to local hypoxia, leading to activation of hypoxia-inducible factor-1α (HIF-1α) signaling. This response further exacerbates adipose tissue inflammation and fibrosis while reinforcing TGF-β signaling pathways [24]. Moreover, reactive oxygen species (ROS) and matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) generated during inflammatory and oxidative stress conditions facilitate the conversion of latent TGF-β1 into its biologically active form, thereby amplifying its paracrine effects [23]. Collectively, these mechanisms drive the transition of PVAT from a passive amplifier of inflammation to a pathogenic microenvironment that actively promotes vascular fibrosis and remodeling [25–27]. Importantly, the pathogenic effects of TGF-β1 may not be solely attributable to its well-established profibrotic properties. More importantly, TGF-β1 has the potential to activate downstream transcriptional regulatory networks that establish sustained pathological gene expression programs. Therefore, during the development and progression of AD, TGF-β1 may function not only as a mediator of inflammation and fibrosis but also as a critical molecular link between PVAT dysfunction and VSMC transcriptional reprogramming.

Furthermore, the biological output of TGF-β1 signaling is not fixed but is dynamically shaped by the local cellular microenvironment.Under physiological conditions, TGF-β1 predominantly activates the canonical Smad2/3 pathway to preserve vascular homeostasis. In contrast, under conditions of PVAT dysfunction, chronic inflammatory stimuli—including TNF-α, IL-6, oxidative stress, and immune cell infiltration—may reshape post-receptor signaling networks, promoting the engagement of non-canonical pathways such as the TRAF6–TAK1 cascade. This context-dependent signal shunting may redirect TGF-β1 signaling away from homeostatic Smad-dependent responses toward NF-κB-driven inflammatory transcriptional programs [23, 28, 29]. Consequently, TGF-β1 may contribute not only to tissue repair and fibrosis but also to sustained pathological transcriptional reprogramming in VSMCs.

Inter-tissue Paracrine Signaling of TGF-β1 Derived from PVAT

Elevated levels of active TGF-β1 are not confined to PVAT but can function as important paracrine mediators that transmit outside-in signals to the vascular wall [30]. On the one hand, bioactive factors released from PVAT can diffuse inward through the relatively loose extracellular matrix of the adventitia. On the other hand, the extensive vasa vasorum network located within the adventitia and outer media may facilitate the transport of inflammatory mediators and immune cells into deeper layers of the vascular wall under inflammatory conditions [31–33]. As local inflammation intensifies and microvascular permeability increases, TGF-β1 and other pro-inflammatory and pro-fibrotic factors gain greater access to the medial layer, thereby exacerbating vascular inflammation and promoting pathological remodeling of the aortic wall [34].

Upon entering the vascular wall, TGF-β1 engages its receptors on VSMCs and activates signaling cascades that promote phenotypic remodeling [35–37]. In doing so, PVAT-derived inflammatory cues are converted into intracellular molecular responses, potentially creating a permissive environment for the activation of transcriptional regulators such as RUNX1 and NF-κB [38]. Nevertheless, although the effects of PVAT-derived factors on vascular inflammation have been widely documented, the molecular mechanisms through which these extracellular signals establish persistent transcriptional programs in VSMCs remain largely unresolved.

Although accumulating evidence has demonstrated that PVAT-derived TGF-β1 can cross tissue boundaries and influence vascular wall cells, the molecular mechanisms linking transient cytokine stimulation to sustained pathological remodeling remain incompletely understood. Given the progressive nature of AD, characterized by persistent structural instability and long-term vascular remodeling, transient cytokine activation alone may be insufficient to explain disease progression. We therefore propose that PVAT-derived TGF-β1 may convert outside-in inflammatory cues into sustained transcriptional reprogramming in VSMCs through the activation of specific transcriptional regulatory networks. Among the candidate regulators, RUNX1 has recently attracted increasing attention because of its emerging roles in inflammation, vascular remodeling, and phenotypic switching, supporting its potential involvement as a central transcriptional hub in PVAT–VSMC communication.

Current limitations and key mechanistic gaps

Although previous studies have implicated PVAT inflammation, aberrant TGF-β1 activation, and NF-κB signaling in aortic disease progression, these mechanisms have largely been investigated in isolation. How PVAT-derived inflammatory cues are integrated by VSMCs and subsequently translated into sustained pathological transcriptional programs remains poorly understood. In particular, the key transcriptional regulators linking microenvironmental alterations to VSMC phenotypic remodeling have yet to be clearly defined. This knowledge gap provides the rationale for the proposed RUNX1/NF-κB regulatory model. Compared with other transcription factors involved in vascular inflammation and remodeling, such as KLF4 and STAT3, RUNX1 possesses additional roles in chromatin remodeling, cell fate regulation, and phenotypic switching, supporting its potential involvement as a candidate transcriptional hub connecting PVAT dysfunction to persistent VSMC reprogramming.

Despite recent advances, several limitations remain in both the mechanistic interpretation and experimental approaches used in this field. The biological effects of TGF-β1 are likely to be highly dependent on disease stage and cellular context. Indeed, some studies have suggested that systemic inhibition of TGF-β signaling may paradoxically increase the risk of aortic dissection progression or rupture. In addition, the widely used angiotensin II (Ang II)-induced animal model primarily reflects inflammation-driven vascular remodeling, which may differ from the pathological features of primary medial degeneration observed in human disease [39]. Furthermore, vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs) cultured in vitro are prone to spontaneous phenotypic switching in response to environmental conditions, potentially introducing confounding factors into mechanistic studies. Therefore, careful interpretation of existing findings within their specific temporal and biological contexts will be essential for advancing our understanding of this field [40].

RUNX1: a potential transcriptional hub in PVAT–VSMC communication

Transcriptional regulatory roles of RUNX1 in vascular inflammation and remodeling

RUNX1 was initially identified as a key regulator of hematopoiesis and is well known for its involvement in acute myeloid leukemia (AML) [41]. More recently, accumulating evidence has demonstrated its roles in cardiovascular biology, particularly in the regulation of cellular plasticity and tissue remodeling [42]. Under physiological conditions, RUNX1 expression is relatively low in vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs). However, its expression is markedly increased in pathological settings, including vascular injury, aortic aneurysm, and aortic dissection (AD) [43, 44]. Emerging studies have linked RUNX1 activation to VSMC phenotypic switching, enhanced inflammatory responses, and vascular remodeling [45, 46]. These findings suggest that RUNX1 may function not only as a regulator of vascular remodeling but also as a potential transcriptional hub linking microenvironmental stress signals to sustained transcriptional reprogramming in VSMCs.

Collectively, current evidence suggests that many downstream targets of RUNX1 are involved in inflammation, extracellular matrix (ECM) remodeling, and phenotypic switching. These processes are not only hallmarks of vascular remodeling but also central features of AD pathogenesis. Therefore, rather than viewing RUNX1 as merely a component of a single signaling pathway, it may be more informative to consider whether RUNX1 functions as a transcriptional hub that integrates multiple extracellular stimuli and ultimately drives pathological phenotypic reprogramming of VSMCs.

RUNX1 activation induced by abnormal vascular microenvironment and its signaling regulatory mechanisms

Aberrant RUNX1 upregulation in VSMCs is unlikely to occur as an isolated event. Rather, it may represent a cellular response to alterations in the vascular microenvironment, particularly to inflammatory and pro-fibrotic signals originating from PVAT [43]. Under pathological conditions, dysfunctional PVAT becomes an important source of TGF-β1, which may contribute to the activation of downstream signaling pathways associated with vascular remodeling.Upon activation of TGF-β receptors on VSMCs, TGF-β1 can initiate transcriptional programs associated with phenotypic remodeling and may contribute to the upregulation of RUNX1 expression and activity [47]. However, RUNX1 activation is unlikely to be driven by TGF-β1 alone. Pro-inflammatory cytokines within the PVAT microenvironment, including TNF-α and IL-6, may further potentiate RUNX1-associated transcriptional responses through STAT3- and NF-κB-dependent pathways [48]. In parallel, hypertension-related hemodynamic stress and mechanical stretch can increase the sensitivity of VSMCs to pathological stimuli through mechanotransduction mechanisms involving Piezo1, integrins, and downstream signaling networks [49, 50]. Collectively, these findings suggest that RUNX1 may function as a convergent transcriptional node integrating inflammatory, fibrotic, and mechanical cues within the diseased vascular microenvironment.Notably, many of the pathological stimuli implicated in RUNX1 activation are also characteristic features of the dysfunctional PVAT microenvironment in AD. Chronic inflammation, aberrant TGF-β1 signaling, oxidative stress, and immune cell infiltration may collectively contribute to sustained RUNX1 activation. These observations raise the possibility that RUNX1 serves as a molecular link between PVAT-derived outside-in signaling and persistent transcriptional reprogramming in VSMCs. In this context, the importance of RUNX1 may lie not only in its upregulation, but also in its potential role as a transcriptional hub capable of integrating diverse pathological signals and coordinating downstream responses together with inflammatory regulators such as NF-κB, thereby contributing to aortic wall remodeling (Fig. 1).

NF-κB: the classical engine of inflammation and phenotypic transformation

Non-classical TGF-β1-TAK1-NF-κB signaling axis mediated inflammation amplification

Traditionally, TGF-β1 has been considered to regulate VSMC behavior primarily through the canonical Smad2/3 pathway, thereby contributing to the maintenance of the contractile phenotype and fibrotic responses. However, under conditions of persistent inflammation and oxidative stress, TGF-β1 signaling may shift from the canonical Smad2/3 pathway toward TAK1–NF-κB signaling, a process that has been proposed as a potential mechanism underlying the transition from tissue repair to pathological remodeling [51].

The molecular determinants of this signaling shift remain incompletely understood but are likely influenced by changes in the local microenvironment. On the one hand, chronic inflammatory stimulation may induce compensatory upregulation of inhibitory Smads, such as Smad7, thereby attenuating canonical Smad2/3 signaling [23, 28]. On the other hand, ROS and pro-inflammatory mediators released from dysfunctional PVAT can disrupt redox homeostasis and potentially lower the activation threshold of TAK1 [52]. Under such conditions, a portion of TGF-β1 signaling may be redirected toward non-canonical inflammatory pathways.

TAK1 serves as a central signaling node in this process. Its activation promotes IKK complex activation and NF-κB nuclear translocation, leading to enhanced transcription of inflammation-related genes [52]. In the pathological setting of AD, dysfunctional PVAT continuously releases pro-inflammatory mediators, including TNF-α and IL-6, which may further promote inflammatory responses and phenotypic switching in VSMCs through NF-κB-, STAT3-, and related signaling pathways [48, 53]. Moreover, TGF-β signaling is known to interact extensively with other signaling networks. Depending on the cellular context and microenvironmental conditions, these pathways may act cooperatively to amplify inflammatory signaling [54].

Sustained inflammatory stimulation may therefore establish positive-feedback interactions among multiple signaling pathways, contributing to persistent NF-κB activation. Importantly, the role of NF-κB may extend beyond the amplification of inflammatory responses. By converting transient extracellular stimuli into relatively stable transcriptional programs, NF-κB may provide a mechanistic basis for long-term VSMC phenotypic remodeling and progressive aortic wall destabilization [55].

NF-κB-mediated transcriptional reprogramming and the synthetic phenotypic transition of VSMCs

Persistent nuclear translocation of NF-κB heterodimers, predominantly p65/p50, can profoundly alter the transcriptional landscape of VSMCs [55, 56]. Activated NF-κB has been shown to reshape gene expression programs by promoting the transcription of genes involved in inflammation, cell migration, and extracellular matrix (ECM) remodeling, while suppressing transcriptional networks associated with the contractile phenotype [38]. This process is typically accompanied by reduced expression of contractile markers, including ACTA2, MYH11, and TAGLN, together with increased expression of synthetic phenotype–associated genes such as osteopontin (OPN) and matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) [11, 57]. These molecular changes are indicative of a phenotypic shift toward a more proliferative, migratory, and secretory state.Sustained activation of these transcriptional programs may progressively alter the functional phenotype of VSMCs. Rather than primarily serving as structural and contractile cells, VSMCs may become active contributors to inflammation and ECM remodeling. This phenotypic transition disrupts the balance among collagen deposition, elastic fiber homeostasis, and matrix degradation, ultimately compromising extracellular matrix integrity and increasing susceptibility to aortic wall destabilization [58, 59]. Although NF-κB activation is capable of rapidly inducing inflammatory gene expression, its ability to independently sustain long-term pathological transcriptional programs remains debated. Accumulating evidence indicates that persistent phenotypic remodeling is more likely driven by the coordinated activity of multiple transcriptional regulators rather than by a single signaling pathway alone.Taken together, NF-κB-mediated transcriptional reprogramming serves not only as the critical molecular foundation for the acquisition of the synthetic phenotype, but also as the pivotal juncture at which the vascular wall transitions from adaptive remodeling to decompensatory damage [60].

RUNX1-NF-κB synergistic transcriptional regulation in driving VSMC phenotypic reprogramming

Persistent exposure of medial VSMCs to PVAT-derived TGF-β1 and inflammatory mediators may progressively establish a transcriptional landscape favoring inflammation and phenotypic remodeling. In this setting, RUNX1 and NF-κB may function as complementary transcriptional regulators that cooperate in shaping pathological gene expression programs. Although RUNX1 has been reported to interact with the NF-κB subunit p50 and enhance inflammatory transcriptional activity, evidence for such interactions is currently derived mainly from immune and cancer-related studies. Whether a comparable mechanism operates in aortic disease remains to be determined [61].

Based on their overlapping roles in inflammation, ECM remodeling, and VSMC phenotypic switching, we propose that RUNX1 and NF-κB may cooperate at specific regulatory regions of disease-associated genes, thereby reinforcing transcriptional programs that promote inflammation, migration, and vascular remodeling [44, 54, 62].

Mechanistically, this cooperative regulation is more likely to occur at the level of promoters and enhancers rather than through simple direct protein-protein interactions. RUNX1 may increase transcriptional accessibility at certain pathogenic gene loci by recruiting co-activators and modulating chromatin openness. Building on this, persistently activated NF-κB could further enhance transcription of genes associated with inflammation and phenotypic switching [63]. For genes such as SPP1, MMPs, and POSTN, which are involved in synthetic phenotype, matrix degradation, and tissue remodeling, this “transcriptional synergistic amplification” may provide a plausible explanation for their sustained high expression under pathological conditions. However, direct evidence from aortic dissection tissues remains lacking.

In addition, the potential cooperation between RUNX1 and NF-κB may extend to epigenetic regulation [64]. Previous studies have shown that RUNX1 can form functional complexes with p300 and promote a more permissive chromatin state at target gene loci [65]. Under conditions of persistent inflammatory stimulation, RUNX1-mediated chromatin remodeling may therefore facilitate the recruitment of NF-κB and other inflammatory transcription factors, thereby supporting the maintenance of pathological gene expression programs.

It should be emphasized that there is currently no direct evidence demonstrating the formation of a stable RUNX1–NF-κB transcriptional complex in AD tissues. The proposed RUNX1/NF-κB cooperative model is primarily based on existing evidence showing that both factors participate in inflammatory signaling, vascular remodeling, and phenotypic regulation. Accordingly, this model should be regarded as a biologically plausible working hypothesis rather than an established mechanism. Further validation will require approaches such as ChIP-seq, ChIP-reChIP, CUT&Tag, and protein interaction studies to determine whether RUNX1 and NF-κB directly cooperate at disease-relevant regulatory regions.

From broad-spectrum signal blockade to precision molecular intervention: targeted therapeutic strategies for the RUNX1-NF-κB Axis

Limitations of NF-κB-targeted therapies and strategies for precision modulation

Over the past decades, considerable efforts have been devoted to the direct targeting of TGF-β1 and NF-κB. However, the overall success of these strategies in clinical translation has remained limited. One major reason is that both pathways play dual roles in physiological homeostasis and pathological remodeling. TGF-β1 is involved in the maintenance of VSMC phenotype, tissue repair, and extracellular matrix (ECM) homeostasis, whereas NF-κB is broadly required for immune defense and cell survival. Consequently, although broad inhibition of these pathways may attenuate inflammation and fibrosis, it may also lead to impaired immune function, defective tissue repair, and off-target toxicities [66–71].

In light of these challenges, current research has gradually shifted from broad pathway blockade toward selective modulation of disease-associated signaling branches. The goal is to preserve essential physiological functions while selectively targeting pathogenic networks involved in PVAT inflammation, VSMC phenotypic switching, and aortic wall destabilization [72, 73].

This perspective is consistent with the emerging trend in TGF-β- and NF-κB-targeted therapies toward greater spatiotemporal precision, cell type-specific intervention, and selective modulation of pathogenic signaling pathways. Given that the pathological mechanisms underlying acute and chronic AD may differ substantially, future therapeutic approaches will likely require stage-specific optimization to achieve maximal efficacy while minimizing adverse effects.

Precision intervention strategies targeting the RUNX1-NF-κB protein-protein interaction

If RUNX1–NF-κB cooperation plays a role in VSMC phenotypic reprogramming, selective disruption of this regulatory interaction may provide a more specific therapeutic strategy than broad inhibition of TGF-β1 or NF-κB signaling [74]. In recent years, advances in protein–protein interaction (PPI)-targeted therapies have demonstrated the feasibility of modulating transcription factor complexes through interface-specific interventions [75]. Supporting this concept, the small molecule Ro5-3335 has been reported to attenuate cardiovascular remodeling by disrupting the interaction between RUNX1 and CBFβ, suggesting that RUNX1-associated transcriptional complexes may possess therapeutic tractability [74]. Consequently, within the inflammatory aortic microenvironment, leveraging high-throughput screening, structural modeling, or de novo peptide design to identify and perturb the critical binding interfaces between RUNX1 and NF-κB may enable the targeted decoupling of synergistic transcriptional amplification for pathogenic genes—such as OPN, MMPs, and POSTN. Crucially, this strategy aims to achieve therapeutic efficacy without compromising the fundamental immune defense functions of NF-κB or abrogating the homeostatic TGF-β1/Smad signaling cascades [76]. In this context, the potential therapeutic rationale may not be to broadly inhibit RUNX1 or NF-κB themselves, but rather to selectively interfere with their pathological cooperation at disease-relevant regulatory regions. Such an approach could potentially suppress inflammatory activation, migratory responses, and matrix-remodeling programs in VSMCs while minimizing disruption of their physiological functions [77, 78]. Nevertheless, this concept should currently be regarded as a hypothesis-generating framework rather than an established therapeutic strategy. Its validity will require further investigation through aortic tissue–specific structural studies, ChIP-reChIP analyses, and functional intervention experiments.Given the established roles of RUNX1 in hematopoiesis and tissue repair, the safety and long-term consequences of targeting this pathway will require careful evaluation. Moreover, systemic inhibition of RUNX1 or NF-κB may carry risks of bleeding complications and immune suppression. Considering that controlled inflammatory responses are involved in tissue repair during the acute phase of AD, therapeutic intervention targeting the RUNX1–NF-κB axis may be more appropriate during the chronic remodeling stage. In addition, such approaches will likely require localized delivery strategies, such as targeted nanocarriers, to minimize systemic toxicity and selectively inhibit pathological matrix degradation.

Targeted nanodelivery system-mediated precision intervention of the RUNX1 gene

In terms of targeted delivery, the molecular heterogeneity of early-stage aortic dissection lesions provides a strategic entry point for precision guidance. Research indicates that VCAM-1, activated on the surface of the inflamed endothelium, along with OPN (SPP1), which is highly expressed in phenotypically switched VSMCs, can both serve as molecular ‘navigation markers’ for the diseased vascular wall [79]. Building upon this rationale, antibodies, peptide ligands, or aptamers can be conjugated onto the surface of liposomes, polymeric nanoparticles, or biomimetic nanocarriers to enhance their capacity for recognition, adhesion, and localized enrichment within the diseased aortic wall during systemic circulation. Specifically, VCAM-1-targeted strategies have been extensively employed for the directional identification of inflamed vascular endothelium. Meanwhile, OPN-targeted nanosystems have demonstrated significant targeting potential in models of atherosclerosis and restenosis, enabling precise delivery to vascular regions characterized by aberrant VSMCs lesions [80, 81].

Regarding the therapeutic cargo, nanovectors facilitate the delivery of gene-intervention tools, including RUNX1-siRNA and CRISPR/Cas9, enabling the selective regulation of pivotal pathogenic transcriptional nodes [82, 83]. This strategy is firmly supported by recent advances in vessel-targeted RNA delivery and non-viral gene editing systems, which offer the necessary technical infrastructure for clinical translation [84].

These findings indicate that coupling disease-site targeting moieties with RUNX1 nucleic acid interventions could, in theory, improve localized therapeutic accuracy while mitigating systemic exposure and off-target effects [85]. Nevertheless, the direct utilization of RUNX1-siRNA or RUNX1-CRISPR for targeted AD therapy is currently best viewed as a forward-looking hypothesis grounded in existing vascular nanodelivery technologies. Rigorous investigations in aortic disease models are required to further substantiate their delivery efficacy, long-term biosafety, and translational viability [86].

Mechanistic integration and future perspectives of RUNX1-NF-κB transcriptional regulation on the PVAT–VSMC Axis

Regarding AD and vascular remodeling, TGF-β1 represents a long-standing conundrum, balancing a delicate dichotomy between tissue protection and disease promotion. While TGF-β signaling fundamentally maintains aortic architecture and extracellular matrix (ECM) stability [40], its dysregulation within distinct pathological contexts directly fuels aneurysmal dilation, dissection propagation, and maladaptive wall remodeling [87]. Crucially, recent advances in perivascular adipose tissue (PVAT) biology demonstrate that PVAT does not merely wrap blood vessels as an inert accessory, but actively orchestrates aortic lesions through “outside-in” paracrine loops and inflammatory crosstalk. This understanding provides a critical microenvironmental framework for re-evaluating the ambivalent actions of TGF-β1 in aortic disorders, suggesting that its phenotypic switch from homeostatic guardian to pathogenic driver is governed by the local inflammatory landscape and cellular context.

Building on this, we propose a mechanistic framework where the pathological impact of TGF-β1 is dictated not merely by the ligand itself, but by post-receptor signaling dynamics and the local inflammatory milieu. Amidst persistent PVAT inflammation and medial stress, RUNX1 and NF-κB may converge into a pathogenic, synergistic transcriptional hub. Specifically, the stress-induced transcription factor RUNX1 facilitates chromatin accessibility to upregulate pathogenic genes, while the NF-κB network amplifies pro-inflammatory, pro-migratory, and matrix-remodeling programs [13]. Theoretically, this synergy offers a compelling rationale for the phenotypic shift of VSMCs from a contractile to a synthetic and pro-inflammatory state [88].

Importantly, the concept of RUNX1–NF-κB coregulation in aortic VSMCs currently rests largely on mechanistic inference and indirect evidence, warranting direct experimental validation. Furthermore, neither RUNX1 nor NF-κB is VSMC-specific, as both mediate inflammatory remodeling in endothelial cells, fibroblasts, and immune cells. We specifically highlight the PVAT–VSMC axis because VSMC phenotypic switching drives the core pathology of aortic dissection, and evidence supporting RUNX1’s role in VSMC remodeling is the most robust. Ultimately, single-cell and spatial omics technologies will be essential to precisely delineate their spatiotemporal dynamics across diverse cell populations.

Despite its potential, this mechanistic framework has limitations. Research on RUNX1 in aortic diseases remains sparse compared to established inflammatory and TGF-β pathways, leaving its consistency across VSMC subpopulations and disease stages unclear. While bulk RNA-seq captures overall transcriptional changes, it masks the profound cellular heterogeneity of the aortic wall, particularly transient cell states that drive disease progression. Recent single-cell transcriptomics has confirmed that VSMCs in aneurysms and dissections comprise distinct inflammatory and stress-responsive subclusters [89]. Furthermore, spatial transcriptomics and single-molecule spatial imaging have identified rare but biologically pivotal VSMC subpopulations in human thoracic aortic lesions, providing new avenues to trace the spatiotemporal trajectories of phenotypic switching [88].

Consequently, future studies must integrate high-resolution single-cell multi-omics and spatial technologies. Combining scRNA-seq, scATAC-seq, and spatial transcriptomics in authentic AD animal models and clinical specimens will not only help identify transitional VSMC subsets—where contractile programs decline before pathogenic programs fully manifest—but also delineate their spatial relationships with PVAT, the adventitia, and infiltrating immune cells [39]. Such approaches will directly address whether PVAT-derived inflammatory signals propagate microenvironmentally from the adventitia to the media, and whether RUNX1 and NF-κB synergistically drive pathological transcriptional reprogramming within specific VSMC subclusters.

In conclusion, aortic dissection pathogenesis represents a multivalent breakdown of the local microenvironment, mechanobiology, and transcriptional regulatory networks. By integrating PVAT-derived “outside-in” inflammatory crosstalk with persistent intracellular VSMC reprogramming, the proposed RUNX1/NF-κB coregulatory model offers a unified framework. Distinct from isolated discussions of PVAT inflammation, aberrant TGF-β1 activation, or NF-κB signaling, this model highlights transcriptional hubs as critical bridges connecting microenvironmental shifts to vascular remodeling, providing a novel spatiotemporal lens on aortic dissection progression (Fig. 2).

These modulate gene expression, decreasing contractile genes and increasing remodeling genes, inducing VSMC phenotypic switching. Abbreviations: PVAT, perivascular adipose tissue; VSMC, vascular smooth muscle cell; TGF-β1, transforming growth factor-β1; TAK1, TGF-β-activated kinase 1; NF-κB, nuclear factor kappa B; p-Smad2/3, phosphorylated Smad2/3.
