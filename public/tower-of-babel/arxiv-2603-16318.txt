The Wulff crystal of self-dual FK-percolation becomes round when approaching criticality
Ioan Manolescu †, Maran Mohanarangan ∗

Abstract
The study of the phase transition in planar FK-percolation on the square lattice has seen significant recent breakthroughs. The model undergoes a change in the nature of its phase transition at q = 4 q=4 , transitioning from a continuous to a discontinuous regime. The aim of this article is to investigate the behaviour of the model in the discontinuous regime as q > 4 q>4 approaches the continuous transition point 4 4 from above, while maintaining the critical parameter p = p c ​ ( q ) p=p_{c}(q) . We prove that in this limit, the correlation length becomes isotropic. The core of the proof builds upon the recently established rotational invariance of the large-scale features of the model at q = 4 q=4 [ 6 ] .

Introduction

1.1
Definition of the model and main result

The
random-cluster model
, introduced by Fortuin and Kasteleyn and hence also referred to as
Fortuin–Kasteleyn (FK) percolation
,
is a central model of statistical mechanics. We consider it here on the two-dimensional square lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, where it exhibits a first-order or higher-order phase transition, depending on the cluster-weight
q
.
We recall its definition and a few basic properties below. For more background, we refer the reader to the monograph
[
19
]
and, for an exposition of more recent results, to the lecture notes
[
20
]
.

We consider the square lattice
(\mathbb{Z}^{2},\mathbb{E})
, that is, the graph with vertex set
\mathbb{Z}^{2}=\{(n,m)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}n,m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\}
and edges between nearest neighbours. We will slightly abuse notation and denote the graph itself by
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Let
G=(V,E)
be a subgraph of the square lattice. A percolation configuration
\omega
on
G
is an element of
\{0,1\}^{E}
. An edge
e\in E
is said to be
open
in
\omega
if
\omega_{e}=1
and
closed
otherwise. A configuration
\omega
can be seen as a subgraph of
G
with vertex set
V
and edge set
\{e\in E\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\omega_{e}=1\}
. When speaking of
connections
in
\omega
, we view
\omega
as a graph. A
cluster
is a maximal connected component of
\omega
(it may be an isolated vertex). When
G
is finite, let
o(\omega)
and
c(\omega)
denote the number of open and closed edges in
\omega
, respectively. Furthermore, let
k(\omega)
denote the number of clusters in
\omega
. Then the random-cluster measure on
G
with parameters
p\in[0,1]
and
q>0
, and free boundary conditions is a measure on percolation configurations given by

\displaystyle\phi^{0}_{G,p,q}[\omega]=\frac{1}{Z^{0}_{G,p,q}}p^{o(\omega)}(1-p)^{c(\omega)}q^{k(\omega)},

(1.1)

where
Z^{0}_{G,p,q}
is a normalising constant called the
partition function
.

For
q\geq 1
, the model can be extended to infinite volume where it exhibits a phase transition. More precisely, the family of measures
\phi^{0}_{G,p,q}
converges weakly as
G
tends to
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. The limiting measure, denoted by
\phi^{0}_{\mathbb{Z}^{2},p,q}
, undergoes a phase transition in the sense that there exists a critical threshold
p_{c}\coloneqq p_{c}(q)\in(0,1)
such that the probability that there exists an infinite cluster under
\phi^{0}_{\mathbb{Z}^{2},p,q}
is 0 for all
p<p_{c}
and 1 if
p>p_{c}
. It has been shown
[
1
]
that the critical threshold is
p_{c}=\sqrt{q}/(1+\sqrt{q})
.

The behaviour at the critical parameter
p_{c}
is of great interest. One important question is whether the phase transition is
continuous
or
discontinuous
, meaning here whether the probability that
0
is contained in an infinite cluster is continuous or discontinuous as a function of
p
. The phase transition was shown to be continuous for
1\leq q\leq 4

[
10
]

and discontinuous when
q>4

[
5
]
. Alternative proofs of these two regimes were obtained in
[
16
]
and
[
23
]
, respectively.

The behaviour at criticality differs drastically between the two regimes. In the regime
1\leq q\leq 4
, the measure at the point of phase transition is unique and exhibits properties indicative of scale invariance, such as the Russo–Seymour–Welsh property (
RSW
); it is expected to have a non-trivial, conformally invariant scaling limit.
In contrast, for
q>4
, there are multiple measures at the point of phase transition, with either sub- or super-critical behaviour, but no critical measure exists
[
17
]
.
In particular, in the first case the correlation length of the model diverges when approaching the critical point, while in the second case it remains bounded.

It was recently proved in
[
6
]
that for
1\leq q\leq 4
, the model exhibits asymptotic rotational invariance at criticality,
in line with its conjectured conformal invariance.
More precisely, the model and its rotation by an arbitrary angle
\theta
may be coupled so that, at large scales, they produce similar configurations with high probability.
In particular, any subsequential scaling limit of the critical model is invariant under all rotations.
This result will be instrumental in the proof of our main theorem.

In this paper, we will be particularly interested in the discontinuous phase transition,
q>4
.
For the exposition of our results, we need to introduce two central quantities.
Fix
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
and define
e_{\theta}
as the unit vector with angle
\theta
to the horizontal axis.
For
q\geq 1
and
p\leq p_{c}
, the
correlation length
of the model in direction
\theta
is defined as the limit

\displaystyle\xi_{p,q}(\theta)\coloneqq\Big(\lim_{n\to\infty}-\tfrac{1}{n}\log\phi^{0}_{\mathbb{Z}^{2},p,q}\big[0\xleftrightarrow{\>\>\>}\lfloor ne_{\theta}\rfloor\big]\Big)^{-1},

(1.2)

where
\lfloor ne_{\theta}\rfloor
is the vertex of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
closest to
ne_{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Similarly, we define the
point-to-hyperplane decay rate
by

\displaystyle\zeta_{p,q}(\theta)\coloneqq\Big(\lim_{n\to\infty}-\tfrac{1}{n}\log\phi^{0}_{\mathbb{Z}^{2},p,q}\big[0\xleftrightarrow{\>\>\>}\mathcal{H}_{\geq n}^{\theta}\big]\Big)^{-1},

(1.3)

where
\mathcal{H}_{\geq n}^{\theta}
is the half-space defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\geq n}^{\theta}\coloneqq\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\langle x,e_{\theta}\rangle\geq n\}\quad\text{ for $n\geq 0$}.

(1.4)

The existence of both limits follows from standard sub-additivity arguments.
The two quantities are related via a so-called
convex duality
(see e.g.
[
21
]
). More precisely, it holds that

\displaystyle\big(\xi_{p,q}(\theta)\big)^{-1}=\sup_{\theta^{\prime}\in[0,2\pi)}\big(\zeta_{p,q}(\theta^{\prime})\big)^{-1}\langle e_{\theta},e_{\theta^{\prime}}\rangle.

(1.5)

A hallmark of the regime
q>4
is the finiteness of
\xi_{p_{c},q}(\theta)
and
\zeta_{p_{c},q}(\theta)
for all
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
.
It should be mentioned that
\xi_{p,q}(\theta)
and
\zeta_{p,q}(\theta)
are both finite for all
p<p_{c}
and
q\geq 1

[
1
,
9
]
.
However, when
p=p_{c}
and
q\in[1,4]
, both quantities are infinite
[
10
]
.

The asymptotic rotational invariance of the model in the regime
1\leq q\leq 4
is a strong indication that
\xi_{p_{c},q}
and
\zeta_{p_{c},q}
become isotropic as
q\searrow 4
.
Our main result confirms this. Note that while both
\xi_{p_{c},q}(\theta)
and
\zeta_{p_{c},q}(\theta)
diverge for all
\theta
as
q\searrow 4
, our result states that these quantities become asymptotically isotropic when renormalised.

Theorem 1.1
.

For all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
q_{0}>4
such that for all
q\in(4,q_{0}]
and any
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in[0,2\pi)
, we have

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{\xi_{p_{c},q}(\theta_{1})}{\xi_{p_{c},q}(\theta_{2})}-1\bigg|<\varepsilon\quad\text{and}\quad\bigg|\frac{\zeta_{p_{c},q}(\theta_{1})}{\zeta_{p_{c},q}(\theta_{2})}-1\bigg|<\varepsilon.

(1.6)

Theorem
1.1
may also be expressed in terms of the
Wulff shape
, the polar set of the inverse correlation length:

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{q}\coloneqq\bigcap_{\theta\in[0,2\pi)}\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\langle x,e_{\theta}\rangle\leq\xi_{p_{c},q}(\theta)^{-1}\}.

(1.7)

Then Theorem
1.1
implies the following result.

Corollary 1.2
.

When
q\searrow 4
,
\mathcal{W}_{q}/\sqrt{{\rm Vol}(\mathcal{W}_{q})}
tends to the unit disk
\mathbb{U}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\left|x\right|\leq 1\}
.

The Wulff shape describes the asymptotic shape of a cluster when conditioned to have a large volume.
Indeed, write
\mathsf{C}
for the cluster of the origin and denote its cardinality by
\left|\mathsf{C}\right|
. Then, for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\phi_{\mathbb{Z}^{2},p_{c},q}\bigg[{\rm d}_{\rm Hausdorff}\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\cdot\mathsf{C},\frac{1}{\sqrt{{\rm Vol}(\mathcal{W}_{q})}}\cdot\mathcal{W}_{q}\Big)>\varepsilon\,\Big|\,\left|\mathsf{C}\right|\geq n\bigg]\to 0

(1.8)

as
n\to\infty
, where
{\rm d}_{\rm Hausdorff}
denotes the Hausdorff distance
[
3
]
.
Thus, Corollary
1.2
states that, as
q\searrow 4
, the typical shape of a cluster conditioned to be large becomes round.

1.2
Strategy of the proof

Similar to the rotational invariance proved in
[
6
]
, our result is accompanied by a universality result relating random-cluster models on different isoradial graphs.
To that end, we briefly describe an inhomogeneous FK-percolation model on some distorted embedding of the square lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. A more detailed discussion of this topic will be postponed until Section
2
.

Fix
q>4
and
\alpha\in(0,\pi)
. Let
\mathbb{L}(\alpha)
be the embedding of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
in which horizontal edges have length
2\cos(\alpha/2)
, vertical edges have length
2\sin(\alpha/2)
, and which is rotated by an angle of
\alpha/2
— see Figure
1
. Define the inhomogeneous random-cluster model on finite subgraphs of
\mathbb{L}(\alpha)
with cluster weight
q
and edge-parameters
p_{\rm h}
and
p_{\rm v}
for the horizontal and vertical edges, respectively, given by

\displaystyle\frac{p_{\rm h}}{1-p_{\rm h}}=\frac{1-p_{\rm v}}{p_{\rm v}}=\sqrt{q}\frac{\sinh(r\alpha)}{\sinh(r(\pi-\alpha))}\quad\text{with}\quad r\coloneqq\tfrac{1}{\pi}\cosh^{-1}\big(\frac{\sqrt{q}}{2}\big),

(1.9)

in the same way as the model on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
; we also refer to (
2.2
) for a more general definition.

Write
\phi^{0}_{\mathbb{L}(\alpha),q}
for the infinite-volume measure on
\mathbb{L}(\alpha)
with the edge weights
p_{\rm h}
and
p_{\rm v}
as above and free boundary conditions. We call
\mathbb{L}(\alpha)
an
isoradial rectangular lattice
and
\phi_{\mathbb{L}(\alpha),q}^{0}
its associated random-cluster model.

Below, we will use estimates on lattices
\mathbb{L}(\alpha)
for different values of
\alpha\in(0,\pi)
.
We will use the phrase “uniform in
\alpha
on compacts of
(0,\pi)
” to mean that estimates are uniform on any interval of the type
(\varepsilon,\pi-\varepsilon)
with
\varepsilon>0
, but potentially not on
(0,\pi)
.
All constants hereafter will be uniform in
\alpha
on compacts of
(0,\pi)
. The main results may be shown to be uniform over the whole interval
(0,\pi)
, but this requires more care and is not essential for our purposes; for further details, see
[
7
, Sec. 5]
.

It was shown in
[
7
]
that, for any
q\geq 1
fixed, the random-cluster models exhibit the same type of phase transition across all isoradial rectangular lattices
\mathbb{L}(\alpha)
.
In particular, as for the critical random-cluster model on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, the phase transition is discontinuous for
q>4
. Thus, for any direction
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
and any angle
\alpha\in(0,\pi)
,
the correlation length and the point-to-hyperplane decay rate may be defined and are bounded, uniformly in
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
and
\alpha
on compacts of
(0,\pi)
:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \xi_{\alpha,q}(\theta)&=\Big(\lim_{n\lx@nobreakspace\to\infty}-\tfrac{1}{n}\log\phi^{0}_{\mathbb{L}({\alpha}),q}\big[0\xleftrightarrow{\>\>\>}\lfloor ne_{\theta}\rfloor\big]\Big)^{-1}<\infty\quad\text{and}\\
\zeta_{\alpha,q}(\theta)&=\Big(\lim_{n\to\infty}-\tfrac{1}{n}\log\phi^{0}_{\mathbb{L}(\alpha),q}\big[0\xleftrightarrow{\>\>\>}\mathcal{H}_{\geq n}^{\theta}\big]\Big)^{-1}<\infty.\end{aligned}

(1.10)

Moreover, while the convex duality in
[
21
]
is stated for the square lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, the same proof still applies to isoradial rectangular lattices
\mathbb{L}(\alpha)
, i.e., the relation

\displaystyle\big(\xi_{\alpha,q}(\theta)\big)^{-1}=\sup_{\theta^{\prime}\in[0,2\pi)}\big(\zeta_{\alpha,q}(\theta^{\prime})\big)^{-1}\langle e_{\theta},e_{\theta^{\prime}}\rangle.

(1.11)

still holds.
Lastly, notice that for
\alpha=\tfrac{\pi}{2}
, one obtains a slight modification

\mathbb{L}(\pi/2)
is the rotation by
\pi/4
of
\sqrt{2}\mathbb{Z}^{2}

\xi_{p_{c},q}

The models
\phi^{0}_{\mathbb{L}(\alpha),q}
for different values of
\alpha
may be related by modifying the lattice step by step via the so-called

. Features that are stable under these transformations can then be transferred from one measure to the other. This strategy was used successfully for FK-percolation in

Theorem 1.3
.

For all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
q_{0}>4
such that for
q\in(4,q_{0}]
, all
\alpha\in(\varepsilon,\pi-\varepsilon)
and any
\theta\in[0,2\pi)

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{\xi_{\alpha,q}(\theta)}{\xi_{\pi/2,q}(\theta)}-1\bigg|<\varepsilon\quad\text{and}\quad\bigg|\frac{\zeta_{\alpha,q}(\theta)}{\zeta_{\pi/2,q}(\theta)}-1\bigg|<\varepsilon.

(1.12)

We will focus on proving the statement for the point-to-hyperplane decay rate
\zeta
.
Indeed, this quantity is simpler to control under the local modifications we apply to the model.
The analogous statement for the correlation length
\xi
will then be obtained via the convex duality (

We close this section by observing that Theorem

directly implies Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We prove the statement for the correlation length
\xi
— the analogous result for
\zeta
is obtained in the same way.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
and
0<\varepsilon^{\prime}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2+\varepsilon}
. Choose
q_{0}
as in Theorem
1.3
according to
\varepsilon^{\prime}
and let
q\in(4,q_{0}]
. Fix
\theta_{1}\in(0,\tfrac{\pi}{4}]
and
\theta_{2}\in[\tfrac{\pi}{4},\tfrac{\pi}{2}]
, then set
\alpha=\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}\in[\frac{\pi}{4},\frac{3\pi}{4}]
. Observe that
e^{i\alpha/2}\mathbb{R}
is an axis of symmetry of
\mathbb{L}(\alpha)
and therefore,
\phi^{0}_{\mathbb{L}(\alpha),q}
is invariant under orthogonal reflections with respect to said axis. In particular, we have
\xi_{\alpha,q}(\theta_{1})=\xi_{\alpha,q}(\theta_{2})
. Theorem
1.3
then implies

\displaystyle\frac{\xi_{\pi/2,q}(\theta_{1})}{\xi_{\pi/2,q}(\theta_{2})}=\frac{\xi_{\pi/2,q}(\theta_{1})/\xi_{\alpha,q}(\theta_{1})}{\xi_{\pi/2,q}(\theta_{2})/\xi_{\alpha,q}(\theta_{2})}\in\bigg(\frac{1-\varepsilon^{\prime}}{1+\varepsilon^{\prime}},\frac{1+\varepsilon^{\prime}}{1-\varepsilon^{\prime}}\bigg).

(1.13)

In particular,

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{\xi_{\pi/2,q}(\theta_{1})}{\xi_{\pi/2,q}(\theta_{2})}-1\bigg|<\frac{2\varepsilon^{\prime}}{1-\varepsilon^{\prime}}<\varepsilon.

(1.14)

The above extends to all angles
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in[0,2\pi)
using the invariance of
\phi^{0}_{\mathbb{L}({\pi/2}),q}
under reflections with respect to the horizontal, vertical, and diagonal axes.
Finally, note that
\xi_{p_{c},q}(\theta)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\xi_{\pi/2,q}(\theta+\frac{\pi}{4})
.

1.3
Near-critical FK-percolation with
q\leq 4

One may view the topic of the present paper in the context of near-critical FK-percolation on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.
Most commonly, the near-critical regime refers to FK-percolation on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
with fixed
q\in[1,4]
and
p\neq p_{c}
, at scales where the model transitions from a critical to an off-critical behaviour.
We direct the reader to
[
8
]
for details
and only mention here that the near-critical FK percolation with
1\leq q\leq 4
exhibits similar features as the critical one, most importantly (
RSW
).

It is expected that, as for the critical phase, the near-critical regime exhibits a form of asymptotic invariance under rotations.
In particular, we expect that, for any
1\leq q\leq 4
and any two angles
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}
,

\displaystyle\frac{\xi_{p,q}(\theta_{1})}{\xi_{p,q}(\theta_{2})}\to 1\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\zeta_{p,q}(\theta_{1})}{\zeta_{p,q}(\theta_{2})}\to 1\qquad\text{ as $p\nearrow p_{c}(q)$}.

(1.15)

Theorem
1.1
may be viewed as a manifestation of (
1.15
) when the critical regime is approached along the line
q\searrow 4
and
p=p_{c}(q)
.
The authors have not managed to adapt the strategy below to also prove (
1.15
) and we believe a key ingredient is missing.
Indeed, the
exact
validity of the star-triangle transformation (or its manifestation as track-exchanges; see Proposition
2.2
below) is essential to the present argument.
These transformations are no longer exact when
p\neq p_{c}
, which we believe is a fundamental obstruction to adapting the argument.

It should be mentioned that
[
11
]
proved (
1.15
) for a variant of Bernoulli percolation (corresponding to
q=1
) through different means.
Indeed, this work builds on the existence and rotational invariance of the near-critical scaling limit
[
13
]
, which in turn relies on the description of the scaling limit of the critical phase
[
25
,
2
]
.
This approach has not yet been adapted to
q>1
and will likely require significant new ideas.

Organisation of the paper

Section
2
contains a review of some basic results for FK-percolation on isoradial graphs. In Section

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce isoradial graphs and the random-cluster models associated to them.

2.1
Isoradial graphs

A rhombic tiling
\mathbb{G}^{\diamond}
is a tiling of the plane by rhombi of edge-length 1. Any such graph is bipartite and we can divide its vertices in two sets of non-adjacent vertices
\mathbb{V}_{\bullet}
and
\mathbb{V}_{\circ}
. The
isoradial graph

\mathbb{G}
associated to
\mathbb{G}^{\diamond}
is the graph with vertex set
\mathbb{V}_{\bullet}
and edge set given by the diagonals of the faces of
\mathbb{G}^{\diamond}
between vertices of
\mathbb{V}_{\bullet}
. If the roles of
\mathbb{V}_{\bullet}
and
\mathbb{V}_{\circ}
are exchanged, we obtain the dual
\mathbb{G}^{*}
of
\mathbb{G}
, which is also isoradial. The term isoradial refers to the fact that each face of
\mathbb{G}
can be inscribed in a circle of radius 1. The rhombic tiling
\mathbb{G}^{\diamond}
is called the
diamond graph
of
\mathbb{G}
.

A
track
of
\mathbb{G}^{\diamond}
is a bi-infinite sequence of adjacent faces
(r_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
of
\mathbb{G}^{\diamond}
, such that each pair
r_{i}
and
r_{i+1}
shares an edge, and all such edges are parallel. The angle formed by any such edge with the horizontal axis is called the
transverse angle
of the track.

The graphs considered in this paper are of a specific type: we assume that all faces of
\mathbb{G}^{\diamond}
have horizontal top and bottom edges — we call these
isoradial rectangular lattices
. As a result, the diamond graphs consist of two families of tracks:
horizontal tracks

t_{i}
with transverse angles
\alpha_{i}\in(0,\pi)
, and
vertical tracks

s_{j}
, each with transverse angle 0. Each track of one family intersects all tracks of the other family, but no two tracks from the same family intersect.

For a sequence of track angles
\bm{\alpha}=(\alpha_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\in(0,\pi)^{\mathbb{Z}}
, denote by
\mathbb{L}(\bm{\alpha})
the graph as above whose horizontal tracks have transverse angles
\alpha_{i}
in increasing vertical order. When
\alpha_{i}=\alpha
for every
i
, we simply write
\mathbb{L}(\alpha)=\mathbb{L}(\bm{\alpha})
.
Note that
\mathbb{L}(\alpha)
corresponds to a distorted embedding of the square lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, with
e^{i\alpha/2}\mathbb{R}
and
e^{i(\alpha+\pi)/2}\mathbb{R}
as axes of symmetry; see Figure
1
for an illustration.

We will use the same notation for finite or half-infinite sequences
\bm{\alpha}
to indicate lattices
\mathbb{L}(\bm{\alpha})
covering a horizontal strip or half-plane.
For technical reasons (specifically for the results of
[
7
]
to adapt readily), we will always work with sequences
\bm{\alpha}
containing at most two values.
This restriction is not essential and may be removed by revisiting
[
7
]
.

\mathbb{L}(\alpha)
. The underlying diamond graph is drawn in thin black lines, while the actual lattice is drawn in red. The white points denote vertices of the dual lattice. Two tracks — one horizontal and one vertical — are shown as dashed grey lines. Note the different parameters

\alpha=\pi/2

by
\frac{\pi}{4}
. In this case, the model is homogenous, as

.

When considering isoradial graphs
\mathbb{G}=(\mathbb{V},\mathbb{E})
, we use the notation
\Lambda_{n}=[-n,n]^{2}
and identify it with the subgraph spanned by the vertices of
\mathbb{V}
contained in
\Lambda_{n}
. We write
\partial\Lambda_{n}
for the set of vertices
v\in\mathbb{V}\cap\Lambda_{n}
that have a neighbour in
\mathbb{V}\cap\Lambda_{n}^{c}
. Furthermore, we write
\Lambda_{n}(z)
for the translation of
\Lambda_{n}
by

.

2.2
Definition and elementary properties of the isoradial random-cluster model

The isoradial embedding of a graph
\mathbb{G}
produces different edge-weights — called
isoradial weights
— for the edges of
\mathbb{G}
as a function of their length. Indeed if
e
is an edge of
\mathbb{G}
and
\theta_{e}
is the angle of the rhombus of
\mathbb{G}^{\diamond}
containing
e
and not bisected by
e
, we set

\displaystyle p_{e}\coloneqq\begin{cases}\frac{\sqrt{q}\sin(r(\pi-\theta_{e}))}{\sin(r\theta_{e})+\sqrt{q}\sin(r(\pi-\theta_{e}))},&q<4,\\
\frac{2\pi-2\theta_{e}}{2\pi-\theta_{e}},&q=4,\\
\frac{\sqrt{q}\sinh(r(\pi-\theta_{e}))}{\sinh(r\theta_{e})+\sqrt{q}\sinh(r(\pi-\theta_{e}))},&q>4,\end{cases}\quad\text{where}\quad r\coloneqq\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\pi}\cos^{-1}\left(\frac{\sqrt{q}}{2}\right),&q\leq 4,\\
\frac{1}{\pi}\cosh^{-1}\left(\frac{\sqrt{q}}{2}\right),&q>4.\end{cases}

(2.1)

For a finite subgraph
G=(V,E)
of an isoradial graph
\mathbb{G}=(\mathbb{V},\mathbb{E})
, we define its vertex boundary
\partial G
as the set of vertices in
V
incident to a vertex in
\mathbb{V}\setminus V

RSW property.

Arguably the most useful ingredient in the study of critical planar percolation models is the

\displaystyle c\leq\phi_{\mathbb{L},4}\big[\mathscr{C}([0,\rho n]\times[0,n])\,|\,A\big]\leq 1-c,

,
which we denote by
\tilde{\mathsf{C}}^{\rm IIC}
. We then define the
IIC increment
by

\displaystyle\Delta^{\rm IIC}{\rm E}_{\theta}={\rm E}_{\theta}(\tilde{\mathsf{C}}^{\rm IIC})-{\rm E}_{\theta}(\mathsf{C}^{\rm IIC}).

(2.10)

Note that
\mathbf{S}_{\rm even}(\mathbb{L}_{\rm mix})
is also a translate of
\mathbb{L}_{\rm mix}
. As such, one might expect the application of
\mathbf{S}_{\rm even}
and the subsequent application of
\mathbf{S}_{\rm odd}
to have identical effects. This is not the case:
\mathbf{S}_{\rm even}(\mathbb{L}_{\rm mix})
and
\mathbb{L}_{\rm mix}
are translates of each other, but their partition into cells differ, which affects the definition of
{\rm Ext}_{\theta}
.
This is the reason for applying both transformations before considering the increment.

The expected increment
\mathbb{E}[\Delta^{\rm IIC}{\rm E}_{\theta}]
— where
\mathbb{E}
refers to the expectation taken with respect to the coupling between
\omega^{\rm IIC}
and
(\mathbf{S}_{\rm odd}\lx@nobreakspace\circ\mathbf{S}_{\rm even})(\omega^{\rm IIC})
— captures the
drift
of the extremum of a large cluster under the transformation
(\mathbf{S}_{\rm odd}\circ\mathbf{S}_{\rm even})
. It was proved in
[
6
]
that this drift vanishes, i.e., for all
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\Delta^{\rm IIC}{\rm E}_{\theta}]=0.

(2.11)

This fact plays a central role in establishing universality among isoradial rectangular lattices for
1\leq q\leq 4
.

3
Half-plane measures

The strategy of proving universality by successively transforming one lattice into another via a sequence of track-exchanges is faced with additional difficulties for
q>4
. In this regime, the boundary conditions influence the model at infinite distance. Indeed, suppose a lattice
\mathbb{L}
is transformed into
\mathbb{L}^{\prime}
by a sequence of track-exchanges, which we denote by
\mathbf{S}
. The transformation affects the boundary conditions in an uncontrollable manner, which, in this case, are crucial. In particular, it is unclear whether the push-forward of
\phi^{0}_{\mathbb{L},q}
by
\mathbf{S}
is
\phi^{0}_{\mathbb{L}^{\prime},q}
. This problem does not appear in the regime
1\leq q\leq 4
due to the uniqueness of the infinite-volume measure.
To circumvent this issue, we will work with half-plane measures with free boundary conditions.

From here onwards, we consider lattices
\mathbb{L}(\bm{\alpha})
where
\bm{\alpha}=(\alpha_{i})_{i\geq 1}
is a half-infinite sequence of angles; as in the previous section, these sequences will contain at most two distinct values.
These lattices cover the upper half-plane
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
.
For such a lattice, write
\partial\mathbb{L}(\bm{\alpha})
for the set of its vertices lying on the horizontal axis
\mathbb{R}\times\{0\}
.

3.1
Uniqueness of the half-plane free measure

We call a half-infinite sequence of angles
\bm{\alpha}=(\alpha_{i})_{i\geq 1}

periodic
if there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\alpha_{k+i}=\alpha_{i}
for all
i\geq 1
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Fix
q\geq 1
and a half-infinite periodic sequence of angles
\bm{\alpha}
.
For any sequence
\xi_{n}
of boundary conditions on
\mathbb{L}(\bm{\alpha})\cap\Lambda_{n}
with the property that
\xi_{n}
is free on
\partial\mathbb{L}(\bm{\alpha})
,
the measures
\phi_{\mathbb{L}(\bm{\alpha})\cap\Lambda_{n},q}^{\xi_{n}}
converge as
n\to\infty
to a measure
\phi_{\mathbb{L}(\bm{\alpha}),q}^{0}
,
which we call the
free half-plane measure
.

Proof.

Write
\phi^{1/0}_{\mathbb{L}(\bm{\alpha})\cap\Lambda_{n},q}
for the isoradial random-cluster measure on
\mathbb{L}(\bm{\alpha})\cap\Lambda_{n}
with free boundary conditions on
[-n,n]\times\{0\}
and wired boundary conditions for the rest of the boundary. Let
\phi^{1/0}_{\mathbb{L}(\bm{\alpha}),q}
be the half-plane random-cluster measure which is the weak (decreasing) limit of
\phi^{1/0}_{\mathbb{L}(\bm{\alpha})\cap\Lambda_{n},q}
for
n\to\infty
.

Similarly, write
\phi^{0}_{\mathbb{L}(\bm{\alpha})\cap\Lambda_{n},q}
for the random-cluster measure on
\mathbb{L}(\bm{\alpha})\cap\Lambda_{n}
with free boundary conditions everywhere and denote its weak (increasing) limit by
\phi^{0}_{\mathbb{L}(\bm{\alpha}),q}
.

By (
CBC
), it suffices to show that

\displaystyle\phi^{1/0}_{\mathbb{L}(\bm{\alpha}),q}=\phi^{0}_{\mathbb{L}(\bm{\alpha}),q},

(3.1)

which in turn follows from the fact that, under
\phi^{1/0}_{\mathbb{L}(\bm{\alpha}),q}
, there exists a.s. no infinite cluster.
The absence of an infinite cluster in
\phi^{1/0}_{\mathbb{L}(\bm{\alpha}),q}
follows from the arguments of
[
15
,
17
]
,
with the periodicity of the sequence of angles playing a particular role.
We give a brief sketch for completeness.

Consider the increasing limit
\phi
of the downward translates of
\phi^{1/0}_{\mathbb{L}(\bm{\alpha}),q}
.
Then
\phi
is a percolation measure on a periodic isoradial lattice
\mathbb{L}
. Furthermore,
\phi
itself is invariant under the translations which map
\mathbb{L}
to itself.
The dual of
\phi
produces configurations on the horizontal translate of
\mathbb{L}
by
1
.
Furthermore, due to the order in which the different parts of the boundary were taken to infinity,
\phi
is stochastically dominated by its dual (translated by
(-1,0)
).

Due to the planarity and periodicity of
\mathbb{L}
, the non-coexistence theorem of
[
24
,
9
]
applies, and we conclude that, under
\phi
, either the primal or the dual configuration contains no infinite cluster.
By the domination above, the primal configuration contains a.s. no infinite cluster, which extends to
\phi^{1/0}_{\mathbb{L}(\bm{\alpha}),q}
by stochastic domination.
∎

Corollary 3.2

(Track-exchange for half-plane measures)
.

Fix
q\geq 1
. Let
\bm{\alpha}
be a half-infinite periodic sequence of angles and
A\subset\mathbb{N}
be a periodic set containing no consecutive integers.
Write
\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}
for the half-infinite periodic sequence obtained from
\bm{\alpha}
by exchanging
\alpha_{i-1}
and
\alpha_{i}
for each
i\in A
.
If
\omega
is sampled according to
\phi_{\mathbb{L}(\bm{\alpha}),q}^{0}
and
\omega^{\prime}
is the configuration obtained by applying each
(\mathbf{T}_{i})_{i\in A}
to
\omega
,
then
\omega^{\prime}
is distributed according to
\phi_{\mathbb{L}(\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}),q}^{0}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{S}_{K}
be the strip of
\mathbb{L}(\bm{\alpha})
formed of the horizontal tracks
t_{1},\dots,t_{K}
and similarly
let
\mathbb{S}_{K}^{\prime}
be the strip of
\mathbb{L}(\bm{\alpha}^{\prime})
containing the tracks
t_{1},\dots,t_{K}
.
Write
\phi^{0}_{\mathbb{S}_{K},q}
and
\phi^{0}_{\mathbb{S}^{\prime}_{K},q}
for the measures on
\mathbb{S}_{K}
and
\mathbb{S}^{\prime}_{K}
, respectively, with free boundary conditions.

Write
\mathbf{S}
for the composition of all transformations
(\mathbf{T}_{i})_{i\in A}
.
Assume now that
K
is such that
K+1\notin A
. Then, we may apply all track exchanges
(\mathbf{T}_{i})_{i\in A;i\leq K}
to
\mathbb{S}_{K}
to obtain
\mathbb{S}^{\prime}_{K}
.
For simplicity, we call the composition of these transformations also
\mathbf{S}
, since the transformations
(\mathbf{T}_{i})_{i\in A;i>K}
do not apply to
\mathbb{S}_{K}
and hence are considered trivial in this setting.

If
\omega
is sampled according to
\phi^{0}_{\mathbb{S}_{K},q}
, then, by Proposition
2.2
, the law of
\mathbf{S}(\omega)
is given by
\phi^{0}_{\mathbb{S}^{\prime}_{K},q}
.
Proposition
3.1
then implies that

\displaystyle\phi_{\mathbb{L}(\bm{\alpha}),q}^{0}=\lim_{K\to\infty}\phi^{0}_{\mathbb{S}_{K},q}\quad\text{ and }\quad\phi_{\mathbb{L}(\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}),q}^{0}=\lim_{K\to\infty}\phi^{0}_{\mathbb{S}^{\prime}_{K},q}.

(3.2)

The conclusion follows.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Fix
q\geq 1
and let
\bm{\alpha}
and
\bm{\beta}
be two half-infinite periodic sequences of angles with
\alpha_{i}=\beta_{i}
for all
i\leq n
.
Then, for any event
H
depending only on the edges on the tracks
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
,

\displaystyle\phi_{\mathbb{L}(\bm{\alpha}),q}^{0}[H]=\phi_{\mathbb{L}(\bm{\beta}),q}^{0}[H].

(3.3)

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{S}(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{K})
denote the strip with
K
horizontal tracks of transverse angles
\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{K}
and vertical tracks of transverse angle
0
.
Let
\phi^{\xi}_{\mathbb{S}(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{K}),q}
denote the measure on this strip with boundary conditions
\xi
.

Fix
H
as in the statement and
\varepsilon>0
. Then, by Proposition
3.1
applied to both
\mathbb{L}(\bm{\alpha})
and
\mathbb{L}(\bm{\beta})
, there exists
K\geq n
such that

\displaystyle\big|\phi_{\mathbb{L}(\bm{\alpha}),q}^{0}[H]-\phi_{\mathbb{S}(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{K}),q}^{\xi}[H]\big|\leq\varepsilon\quad\text{ and }\quad\big|\phi_{\mathbb{L}(\bm{\beta}),q}^{0}[H]-\phi_{\mathbb{S}(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{K}),q}^{\xi}[H]\big|\leq\varepsilon

(3.4)

for any boundary conditions
\xi
that are free on the bottom of the strip.

Consider now the strips

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}\coloneqq\mathbb{S}(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{K},\beta_{n+1},\dots,\beta_{K})\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathbb{S}^{\prime}\coloneqq\mathbb{S}(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{K},\alpha_{n+1},\dots,\alpha_{K}).

(3.5)

Due to (
3.4
) and (
SMP
),

\displaystyle\big|\phi_{\mathbb{L}(\bm{\alpha}),q}^{0}[H]-\phi_{\mathbb{S},q}^{0}[H]\big|\leq\varepsilon\quad\text{ and }\quad\big|\phi_{\mathbb{L}(\bm{\beta}),q}^{0}[H]-\phi_{\mathbb{S}^{\prime},q}^{0}[H]\big|\leq\varepsilon.

(3.6)

Since
\alpha_{i}=\beta_{i}
for all
i\leq n
, there exists a sequence of track exchanges that act only above the track
t_{n}
and turn
\mathbb{S}
into
\mathbb{S}^{\prime}
.
The configuration on
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
does not change when applying this sequence of transformations, so we find

\displaystyle\phi_{\mathbb{S},q}^{0}[H]=\phi_{\mathbb{S}^{\prime},q}^{0}[H].

(3.7)

Combining this with (
3.6
), we conclude that
|\phi_{\mathbb{L}(\bm{\alpha}),q}^{0}[H]-\phi_{\mathbb{L}(\bm{\beta}),q}^{0}[H]|\leq 2\varepsilon
.
Finally, since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary, we find
\phi_{\mathbb{L}(\bm{\alpha}),q}^{0}[H]=\phi_{\mathbb{L}(\bm{\beta}),q}^{0}[H]
.
∎

3.2
Rate of decay of connection probabilities in the half-plane

For
\alpha\in(0,\pi)
, write
\mathbb{L}_{+}(\alpha)
for the half-plane isoradial lattice with constant transverse angle
\alpha
for the tracks
(t_{n})_{n\geq 1}
.
Since we will exclusively be working with half-plane measures, we now introduce the half-plane analogue of
\zeta
.
For
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
, set

\displaystyle\zeta_{\alpha,q}^{\rm hp}(\theta)=\Big(\lim_{n\to\infty}-\tfrac{1}{n}\log\phi^{0}_{\mathbb{L}_{+}(\alpha),q}\big[0\xleftrightarrow{\>\>\>}\mathcal{H}_{\geq n}^{\theta}\big]\Big)^{-1}.

(3.8)

The existence of the limit, similarly to that of
\zeta_{\alpha,q}(\theta)
, is proved using the super-additivity of the sequence

\displaystyle\max_{x\in\mathcal{H}_{\geq n}^{\theta}}\log\phi^{0}_{\mathbb{L}_{+}(\alpha),q}[0\xleftrightarrow{\>\>\>}x].

(3.9)

Finally, observe that, due to (
1.10
) and the finite energy property (
FE
),

\displaystyle 0<\zeta_{\alpha,q}^{\rm hp}(\theta)<\infty

(3.10)

for any
q>4
,
\alpha\in(0,\pi)
, and
\theta\neq 3\pi/2
.

We call a direction
\theta

upper-half-plane aiming
for
\mathbb{L}(\alpha)
if there exists an infinite number of integers
n\geq 1
such that
the point
x\in\mathcal{H}_{\geq n}^{\theta}
maximising
\phi^{0}_{\mathbb{L}(\alpha),q}[0\xleftrightarrow{\>\>\>}x\,]
lies in the upper half-plane.
The upper-half-plane aiming directions may be shown to be those dual — in the sense of (
1.11
) — to
\theta\in[0,\pi]
.
For
\mathbb{L}(\pi/2)
, all
\theta\in[0,\pi]
are upper-half-plane aiming due to the invariance of the model with respect to vertical reflections.
This is not necessarily the case for
\mathbb{L}(\alpha)
, as this particular symmetry may be lost.

We note two fundamental facts that are due to the invariance of the models
\phi^{0}_{\mathbb{L}(\alpha),q}
under rotation by
\pi
. For any
\theta\in[0,\pi)
and
\alpha\in(0,\pi)
,

\displaystyle\zeta_{\alpha,q}(\theta)=\zeta_{\alpha,q}(\pi+\theta)\quad\text{ and }\quad\text{at least one of $\theta$ and $\pi+\theta$ is upper-half-plane aiming}.

(3.11)

Finally, we state the essential link between
\zeta_{\alpha,q}^{\rm hp}(\theta)
and
\zeta_{\alpha,q}(\theta)
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Fix
\alpha\in(0,\pi)
,
q>4
, and
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
. Then

\displaystyle\zeta_{\alpha,q}(\theta)\geq\zeta_{\alpha,q}^{\rm hp}(\theta),

(3.12)

with equality if
\theta
is upper-half-plane aiming for
\mathbb{L}(\alpha)
.

Proof.

By (
CBC
) and inclusion of events,

\displaystyle\phi^{0}_{\mathbb{L}_{+}(\alpha),q}\big[0\xleftrightarrow{\>\>\>}\mathcal{H}_{\geq n}^{\theta}\big]\leq\phi^{0}_{\mathbb{L}(\alpha),q}\big[0\xleftrightarrow{\>\>\>}\mathcal{H}_{\geq n}^{\theta}\big].

(3.13)

Taking the logarithm, dividing by
-n
and taking the limit
n\to\infty
, we obtain (
3.12
).

Assume now that
\theta
is upper-half-plane aiming for
\mathbb{L}(\alpha)
.
Fix
\varepsilon>0
. Then, for
r
larger than some threshold,

\displaystyle\phi^{0}_{\mathbb{L}(\alpha),q}\big[0\xleftrightarrow{\>\>\>}\mathcal{H}_{\geq r}^{\theta}\big]\geq\exp\big(-\tfrac{r}{\zeta_{\alpha,q}(\theta)-\varepsilon}\big).

(3.14)

Consider the point
x\in\mathcal{H}_{\geq r}^{\theta}
that maximises
\phi^{0}_{\mathbb{L}(\alpha),q}[0\xleftrightarrow{\>\>\>}x\,]
.
In light of (
1.10
), there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
r
such that

\displaystyle\phi^{0}_{\mathbb{L}(\alpha),q}[0\xleftrightarrow{\>\>\>}x]\geq\frac{1}{Cr}\exp\big(-\tfrac{r}{\zeta_{\alpha,q}(\theta)-\varepsilon}\big).

(3.15)

By further increasing
r
and using the fact that
\theta
is upper-half-plane aiming, we may consider
x
to be in the upper-half-plane and such that

\displaystyle\phi^{0}_{\mathbb{L}(\alpha),q}[0\xleftrightarrow{\>\>\>}x]\geq\exp\big(-\tfrac{r}{\zeta_{\alpha,q}(\theta)-2\varepsilon}\big).

(3.16)

Finally, we may find
R\geq 1
large enough such that

\displaystyle\phi^{0}_{\Lambda_{R}\cap\mathbb{L}(\alpha),q}[0\xleftrightarrow{\>\>\>}x]\geq\exp\big(-\tfrac{r}{\zeta_{\alpha,q}(\theta)-3\varepsilon}\big).

(3.17)

Now fix
z\in\mathbb{L}_{+}(\alpha)
at a distance at least
R
from the horizontal line.
Then, for any
n
sufficiently large, by (
FKG
) and (
CBC
)

\displaystyle\phi^{0}_{\mathbb{L}_{+}(\alpha),q}\big[0\xleftrightarrow{\>\>\>}\mathcal{H}_{\geq n}^{\theta}\big]

\displaystyle\geq\phi^{0}_{\mathbb{L}_{+}(\alpha),q}[0\xleftrightarrow{\>\>\>}z]\phi^{0}_{\mathbb{L}_{+}(\alpha),q}[z\xleftrightarrow{\>\>\>}z+\ell x]

(3.18)

\displaystyle\geq\phi^{0}_{\mathbb{L}_{+}(\alpha),q}[0\xleftrightarrow{\>\>\>}z]\phi^{0}_{\Lambda_{R}\cap\mathbb{L}(\alpha),q}[0\xleftrightarrow{\>\>\>}x]^{\ell}

(3.19)

when
\ell
is a sufficiently large integer such that
z+\ell x\in\mathcal{H}_{\geq n}^{\theta}
. Due to the choice of
x
,
one may choose
\ell\leq\frac{n+C_{0}}{r}
for some constant
C_{0}
that depends on
\theta,r,x
, and
z
, but not on
n
.
Using (
3.17
), we conclude the existence of a constant
c_{1}>0
independent of
n
such that

\displaystyle\phi^{0}_{\mathbb{L}_{+}(\alpha),q}\big[0\xleftrightarrow{\>\>\>}\mathcal{H}_{\geq n}^{\theta}\big]\geq c_{1}\exp\big(-\tfrac{n}{\zeta_{\alpha,q}(\theta)-3\varepsilon}\big).

(3.20)

Taking the logarithm, dividing by
-n
and taking
n
to infinity, we conclude that

\displaystyle\zeta_{\alpha,q}^{\rm hp}(\theta)\geq\zeta_{\alpha,q}(\theta)-3\varepsilon.

(3.21)

Since
\varepsilon>0
was chosen arbitrarily and in light of (
3.12
), we conclude that the two quantities are equal.
∎

4
Universality: proof of Theorem
1.3

We briefly outline the idea behind proving Theorem
1.3
. Our aim will be to compare

\zeta_{\alpha,q}^{\rm hp}(\theta)
for different values of
\alpha
and
\theta\neq 3\pi/2
fixed. More precisely, we will show the following.

Proposition 4.1
.

For all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
q_{0}>4
such that for
q\in(4,q_{0}]
, all
\alpha,\beta\in(\varepsilon,\pi-\varepsilon)
and any
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
with
\theta\neq 3\pi/2
,

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{\zeta_{\alpha,q}^{\rm hp}(\theta)}{\zeta_{\beta,q}^{\rm hp}(\theta)}-1\bigg|<\varepsilon.

(4.1)

We will see in Section
4.3
how to pass from the half-plane connection rates to the full-plane connection rates, and hence prove Theorem
1.3
.
The rest of the section is dedicated to proving Proposition
4.1
.

The aim is to show that connection probabilities in
\omega\sim\phi^{0}_{\mathbb{L}_{+}(\alpha),q}
and
\omega^{\prime}\sim\phi^{0}_{\mathbb{L}_{+}(\beta),q}
are close to each other when
q>4
is taken sufficiently close to
4
.
To achieve this, we couple these two configurations and construct a sequence of intermediate configurations, each obtained from the previous one by a sequence of track-exchanges.

In this coupling, we keep track of the extremal coordinate
{\rm E}_{\theta}(\mathsf{C})
of the cluster
\mathsf{C}
of the origin in direction
e_{\theta}
.
By taking
q>4
close enough to
4
, we argue that the effect of track-exchanges on
{\rm E}_{\theta}(\mathsf{C})
are almost identical in law to
\Delta^{\rm IIC}{\rm E}_{\theta}
,
and therefore have vanishingly small expectation.
This will allow us to compare the probabilities in the initial and final configurations
\omega
and
\omega^{\prime}
to have
{\rm E}_{\theta}(\mathsf{C})\geq n
for large values of
n
.
Ultimately, our goal is to show that the exponential rate of decay of these two quantities is almost equal.

4.1
The coupling

We will now fix some notation necessary for the proof of Proposition
4.1
and describe the coupling in detail.
Fix two angles
\alpha,\beta\in(0,\pi)
and

All constants below will be uniform in
\alpha

\beta

.

Write
\mathbb{L}_{0}

\displaystyle\alpha_{i}=\begin{cases}\alpha&\text{ for $2kN<i\leq(2k+1)N$, $k\geq 0$,}\\

. We will associate a sequence of configurations

\displaystyle\omega_{t+1}=\mathbf{S}_{t}(\omega_{t}).

Fix an angle

. Thus, case (2) implies that the extremum is within distance

of an interface, but also at a distance at least
CR
from the horizontal axis.
Combining the deterministic bound
\Delta_{t}{\rm E}\geq-4
with (

\displaystyle\sum_{z\text{ in case (2)}}\mathbb{E}\big[\Delta_{t}{\rm E}\,|\,{\rm Ext}(\mathsf{C}_{t})=z\big]\mathbb{P}\big[{\rm Ext}(\mathsf{C}_{t})=z\,|\,{\rm E}(\mathsf{C}_{t})\in[n\varepsilon,(n+1)\varepsilon)\big]\geq-4\delta,

where the sum is taken over all possible realisations
z

{\rm Ext}(\mathsf{C}_{t})

Case (3):

The remaining points
z

R
from any interface. In this scenario, we want to relate the increment
\Delta_{t}{\rm E}
to the increment

of an IIC (see Section

To that end, we first prove that the local environment around an extremum is indistinguishable from that of an IIC extremum (up to an arbitrarily small error).

Lemma 4.6
.

For any
r\geq 1
, there exists a choice of

and

such that, for any

, any

\displaystyle d_{\rm TV}\big(\mathbb{P}\big[\cdot\,|\,{\rm Ext}(\mathsf{C}_{t})=z\big],\phi_{\mathbb{L}_{\rm mix},4}^{\rm IIC}\big)\leq\delta,

(4.39)

where the two measures refer to the configuration in
\Lambda_{r}(z)
and the latter is translated by
z
.

Note here that
R
and
q_{0}
depend on
r
and
\delta
, but not on
z
,
t
or any other quantity previously fixed.

Proof.

Fix
r\geq 1
and let
R\geq r
be a constant to be fixed below. Fix a point
z
as in the statement.

First, observe that, in
\mathbb{L}_{t}
, the lattice in
\Lambda_{R}(z)
is identical to
\mathbb{L}_{\rm mix}
— including its partition into cells.
Thus, Proposition
2.5
ensures that, by choosing

\displaystyle d_{\rm TV}\big(\phi_{\mathbb{L}_{t},4}^{0}\left[\,\cdot\,|\,\omega=\omega_{0}\text{ on }\Lambda_{R}(z)^{c},{\rm Ext}(\mathsf{C}_{t})=z\right],\phi^{\rm IIC}_{\mathbb{L}_{\rm mix},4}\big)\leq\tfrac{\delta}{2}

(4.40)

for any configuration
\omega_{0}
on
\Lambda_{R}(z)^{c}
for which the conditioning is not degenerate, where the two measures refer to the configuration in
\Lambda_{r}(z)
and the latter is translated by
z
.

Now, by continuity of the measures
\phi_{\Lambda_{R}(z),q}^{\xi}
, there exists
q_{0}=q_{0}(R,\delta)>4
such that, for any
4<q\leq q_{0}
,

\displaystyle d_{\rm TV}\big(\phi_{\mathbb{L}_{t},4}^{0}[\,\cdot\,|\,\omega=\omega_{0}\text{ on }\Lambda_{R}(z)^{c},{\rm Ext}(\mathsf{C}_{t})=z],\phi_{\mathbb{L}_{t},q}^{0}[\,\cdot\,|\,\omega=\omega_{0}\text{\lx@nobreakspace on }\Lambda_{R}(z)^{c},{\rm Ext}(\mathsf{C}_{t})=z]\big)\leq\tfrac{\delta}{2}

(4.41)

for any
\omega_{0}
as above,
with both measures referring to the configuration in the full box
\Lambda_{R}(z)
.

Combining the two displays above, and keeping in mind that the law of
\omega_{t}
is
\phi_{\mathbb{L}_{t},q}^{0}
, we obtain the desired conclusion.
∎

We now aim to control the terms
\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{t}{\rm E}\,|\,{\rm Ext}(\mathsf{C}_{t})=z]
for
z
deep within the mixed block.
Sample
\omega^{\rm IIC}
according to
\phi^{\rm IIC}_{\mathbb{L}_{\rm mix},4}
and translate it by
z
.
Include this sample under the measure
\mathbb{P}
so as to maximise the probability under
\mathbb{P}[\,\cdot\,|\,{\rm Ext}(\mathsf{C}_{t})=z]

that
\omega_{t}
and
\omega^{\rm IIC}
are identical in
\Lambda_{r}(z)
.

Write
\mathsf{C}^{\rm IIC}
for the cluster of
z
in
\omega^{\rm IIC}
and
\tilde{\mathsf{C}}^{\rm IIC}
for the corresponding cluster in
(\mathbf{S}_{t+1}\circ\mathbf{S}_{t})(\omega^{\rm IIC})
. Notice that the effect of
(\mathbf{S}_{t+1}\circ\mathbf{S}_{t})
on the local environment of
{\rm Ext}(\mathsf{C}^{\rm IIC})
is identical to that of
(\mathbf{S}_{\rm odd}\circ\mathbf{S}_{\rm even})
. In particular, we have that

\displaystyle{\rm E}(\tilde{\mathsf{C}}^{\rm IIC})-{\rm E}(\mathsf{C}^{\rm IIC})=\Delta^{\rm IIC}{\rm E}.

(4.42)

There are multiple reasons why the increment
\Delta_{t}{\rm E}
might differ from
\Delta^{\rm IIC}{\rm E}
.
We say a
coupling error
occurs if the configurations
\omega_{t}
and
\omega^{\rm IIC}
are not identical in
\Lambda_{r}(z)
.
An
increment error
occurs if the configurations
(\mathbf{S}_{t+1}\circ\mathbf{S}_{t})(\omega^{\rm IIC})
and
\omega_{t+2}=(\mathbf{S}_{t+1}\circ\mathbf{S}_{t})(\omega_{t})
are not identical on
\Lambda_{r/2}(z)
.
Finally, an
IIC error
occurs if the extremum of
\tilde{\mathsf{C}}^{\rm IIC}
is not contained in
\Lambda_{r/2}(z)
.

If none of these errors occur, then
\omega_{t+2}
is identical to
(\mathbf{S}_{t+1}\circ\mathbf{S}_{t})(\omega^{\rm IIC})
on
\Lambda_{r/2}(z)
and said box contains
{\rm Ext}(\tilde{\mathsf{C}}^{\rm IIC})
. In particular,
{\rm Ext}(\tilde{\mathsf{C}}^{\rm IIC})\in\mathsf{C}_{t+2}
and therefore

\displaystyle{\rm E}(\mathsf{C}_{t+2})\geq{\rm E}(\tilde{\mathsf{C}}^{\rm IIC}).

(4.43)

The inequality comes from the case where
\mathsf{C}_{t+2}
has an extremum outside of
\Lambda_{r/2}(z)
.
While we expect this to be unlikely under
\mathbb{P}[\,\cdot\,|\,{\rm Ext}(\mathsf{C}_{t})=z]
, we will not endeavour to bound its probability.

By collecting the error terms, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\Delta_{t}{\rm E}\,|\,{\rm Ext}(\mathsf{C}_{t})=z\big]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}\big[\Delta^{\rm IIC}{\rm E}-4\bm{1}_{\text{error}}\,|\,{\rm Ext}(\mathsf{C}_{t})=z\big]

(4.44)

\displaystyle\geq-4\mathbb{P}\big[\text{error}\,|\,{\rm Ext}(\mathsf{C}_{t})=z\big],

(4.45)

where the first inequality is due to the deterministic bound on increments and the second one to the fact that that
\mathbb{E}[\Delta^{\rm IIC}{\rm E}]\lx@nobreakspace=0
— see (
2.11
).

We will now bound the probabilities of each type of error occurring.
We start with the IIC and increment errors, as these are controlled by taking
r
large enough.
Indeed, since the track exchanges are

transformations, the probability under
\mathbb{P}[\,\cdot\,|\,{\rm Ext}(\mathsf{C}_{t})=z]

For an IIC error to occur,

By a union bound on the possible values of

This computation is identical to the corresponding one in

.
Thus, by taking

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big[\text{increment or IIC error}\,|\,{\rm Ext}(\mathsf{C}_{t})=z\big]\leq\delta.

(4.46)

Finally, with

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big[\text{coupling error}\,|\,{\rm Ext}(\mathsf{C}_{t})=z\big]\leq\delta.

Inserting the last two bounds in (

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\Delta_{t}{\rm E}\,|\,{\rm Ext}(\mathsf{C}_{t})=z\big]\geq-8\delta.

Take

as dictated by case (3), so that (

Consider now
