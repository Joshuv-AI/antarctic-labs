 CSP Update
• A 100-megawatt (MW) linear Fresnel CSP plant, with 8 hours of storage, began operation in
  Xinjiang, China, in October 2025. It was built in conjunction with a 900-MW PV plant to balance the
  PV system’s variable generation. It was reported that several innovative O&M techniques were
  applied to the project, including integrated control of PV and CSP systems; a first-ever linear Fresnel
  full-system simulator; and automated start-up and shutdown.
• In Europe, companies announced building applications that will combine thermal energy with PV. In
  November 2025, Heineken announced its Portuguese operations will deploy 7 MW of PV with 100
  MWh of thermal battery storage, from U.S.-based company Rondo Energy. In October 2025, a
  Viennese sports arena announced it was hosting Europe’s largest photovoltaic-thermal system,
  which uses U.S.-based SunMaxx Solar’s hybrid modules to produce both electricity and heat.
• It was reported in September 2025 that Glasspoint, a U.S. firm that uses lightweight concentrated
  solar collectors in glasshouses, will deploy a 750-MW solar system, with 18 hours of storage, to
  generate 250 MWth of continuous steam for a California mine. The price was reported to be lower
  than steam they have been generating from fossil fuels.

4. The median inverter loading ratio (ILR) for projects installed in 2024 was 1.33.
 Historically, ILR increased as module prices fell faster than inverter prices, causing
 developers to oversize the DC array capacity relative to the AC inverter capacity to
 enhance revenue and reduce output variability. The introduction of bifacial modules
 may be pushing ILR down slightly; however, ILRs have remained relatively consistent

 Plug-In Solar: Plug-in solar refers to small, portable PV systems that have
 lower up-front costs and easier installation than traditional

 Basics PV systems.
 • Home systems are generally smaller than 2 kWdc.
 • Larger canopy systems also have been developed.
 • Plug-in solar systems:
 – Typically consist of a few PV modules, a
 microinverter, cables, mounting hardware, and a
 connection to standard electrical outlets.
 – Ideally require no detailed permitting process.
 – Offset on-site electricity use.
 – Do not allow consumer-installed systems to export
 to the grid in most countries.
