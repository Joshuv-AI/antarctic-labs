From metabolic node to smart building block: α-Ketoglutarate-empowered biomaterials for programmable cell fate
Authors: Yongqi Ji, Zhao Chang, He Wang, Xiuyun Ren, Bin Zhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Abstractα-Ketoglutarate serves as a metabolic node in tricarboxylic acid cycle. In recent years, its role has expanded far beyond that of an energy supplier, emerging as a crucial regulator that programs cell fate by modulating the epigenetic landscape, delaying cellular aging, and maintaining immune homeostasis. However, the clinical translation of AKG has been severely hindered by the inherent pharmacokinetic deficiencies of its monomeric form, such as low bioavailability, short in vivo half-life, and poor tissue targeting. This review proposes a paradigm shift: redefining AKG from a mere "therapeutic molecule" to a "smart building block" for AKG-empowered biomaterials. The core of this concept lies in leveraging AKG's intrinsic functional groups to directly participate in biomaterial synthesis through covalent bonds, thereby integrating it as an intrinsic component of the material framework. This achieves a leap from "passive transportation" to "active construction." The review systematically delineates the main biological effects of AKG and evaluates three progressive engineering strategies for AKG-empowered biomaterials: from protected "cargo," to functionally synergistic "adjuvant," and ultimately to the material "framework" itself. Emphasis is placed on the significant application prospects of these intelligent materials in fields such as bone and cartilage regeneration, soft tissue repair, immune homeostasis regulation, and enhanced oncotherapy through specific cell fate programming. Finally, current challenges are addressed, and an outlook on future directions is provided. This review aims to offer a blueprint for bridging the gap from basic biology to advanced material design, promoting the establishment of a new paradigm: "metabolism-guided materialization of cell fate programming."Keywords: α-Ketoglutarate, Cell fate programming, Smart building block, Tissue regeneration

Introduction

α-Ketoglutarate (AKG) stands as a pivotal endogenous metabolite in the tricarboxylic acid cycle (TCA cycle) [1]. In mitochondria, AKG is enzymatically derived from the oxidative decarboxylation of isocitrate and subsequently channeled into succinyl-CoA. This process produces reduced nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NADH), providing the essential reducing equivalents that fuel mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation and ATP synthesis, thereby establishing the crucial role of AKG as the central hub of cellular energy metabolism [2]. However, recent studies have revealed that the biological functions of AKG go far beyond energy metabolism. As an obligatory co-substrate for the 2-oxoglutarate-dependent dioxygenase (2-OGDD) family, including ten-eleven translocation hydroxylases (TETs) and Jumonji C domain-containing histone demethylases (JMJDs), AKG exerts a profound influence on the epigenetic landscape, modulating gene expression and cellular functional programming [3]. Moreover, AKG exhibits extensive functions in delaying cellular aging [4], maintaining immune homeostasis [5], governing stem cell lineage commitment [6], regulating redox balance [7], and inhibiting tumor growth [8]. Therefore, AKG is essentially a bridge connecting cellular metabolism and epigenetics, and should no longer be regarded as a static metabolic intermediate, but rather a dynamic, multifunctional regulator of cellular fate. This repositioning has reformed scholars' understanding of AKG and laid a solid biological foundation for its innovative applications in the field of biomaterials.

Although AKG has profound therapeutic potential, its clinical translation faces formidable pharmacokinetic constraints. Due to the pronounced first-pass effect in the digestive tract, a considerable portion of dietary AKG is catabolized by intestinal epithelial cells into CO2, culminating in poor systemic bioavailability [9]. Moreover, after entering the systemic circulation, AKG is rapidly cleared, with a half-life of less than 5 min, making it difficult to maintain an effective blood drug concentration [1,10]. Additionally, after absorption, AKG distributes to various tissues and organs, leading to poor targeting of the disease site, which may significantly reduce the therapeutic effect and increase the risk of systemic adverse reactions [11]. These inherent defects severely limit the ability of AKG to reach and maintain an effective therapeutic concentration at the lesion site, hindering the transition of its therapeutic promise from bench to bedside.

The preparation of AKG derivatives using biochemical methods can improve the inherent defects to a certain extent. Dimethyl α-ketoglutarate (DMAKG) is a dimethyl esterified derivative of AKG [12], designed to enhance the efficiency of transcellular membrane transport by increasing lipophilicity [13]. However, the discovery by Parker, S. J [14]. caused quite a stir: DMAKG is extremely unstable in aqueous media and rapidly hydrolyzes to produce AKG and methanol, the latter of which may be potentially toxic at high concentrations. Moreover, this process is accompanied by proton release, resulting in a decrease in the pH value of the local microenvironment. More importantly, contrary to the long-standing generally accepted view in academia, Parker [14] demonstrated that the efficiency of AKG uptake by many cell lines is not significantly different from that of DMAKG, and the fates of internalized AKG and DMAKG in cells are different [14,15]. Therefore, the practice of performing esterification modification solely to solve the problem of AKG transmembrane transport deserves further discussion. In addition, diethyl α-ketoglutarate, octyl α-ketoglutarate, and trifluoromethylbenzyl α-ketoglutarate are also esterified derivatives of AKG, and researchers often use these derivatives to achieve effective cell membrane penetration. However, the cytotoxicity of the first two themselves and their hydrolysates is still a problem that cannot be ignored [[16], [17], [18]]. Besides esterified derivatives, AKG mineral salts are another common type of AKG derivatives in experiments, among which calcium alpha-ketoglutarate (CaAKG) is a calcium derivative of AKG, which is superior to AKG in terms of chemical stability and bioavailability [19]. However, excessive intake of calcium may damage the cardiovascular system [20], urinary system [21], and digestive system [22]. More importantly, neither the esterified derivatives of AKG nor its calcium salt changes the essential nature as a small-molecule substance via such simple structural modifications, nor can they endow it with functional characteristics, including environment-responsive release, precise targeting to lesions, and multimodal synergistic therapy. To fully exploit the therapeutic potential of AKG, relying solely on biochemical improvement methods is no longer sufficient. A fundamental shift in thinking is imperative. In recent years, the close integration of materials science with other disciplines has given rise to the innovative concept of "smart building block". Smart building block refers to the molecule that acts simultaneously as both therapeutic agent and material skeleton in biomaterials. The term "building" embodies that these molecules are no longer regarded as "cargo" passively transported by material carriers, but rather have their chemical functional groups directly integrated into the main chain or framework of the materials through advanced synthesis strategies. Unlike conventional prodrug design or small-molecule/material conjugation where therapeutic molecules show an insignificant regulatory effect on the overall performance of materials [23], this construction method realizes the integration of structure and function. Smart building block is an essential guarantee for the successful synthesis of biomaterials and the maintenance of their structures and properties, enabling biomaterials to acquire excellent performances such as environment-triggered precise release, more stable drug release kinetics, and higher drug loading capacity, which is the implication of the term "smart". This design concept of structure-function integration is leading the gradual transformation and evolution of biomaterial design paradigms. Furthermore, this paradigm has gained considerable traction across diverse biomedical domains. For instance, Inamdar, S. et al. incorporated succinic acid (SUC) into the main chain of polymer microspheres to achieve targeted regulation of immune cells [24]; The long-chain polymers prepared by the polymerization of itaconic acid with aliphatic diols enabled the release of itaconic monomers via the cleavage of ester bonds [25]; long chains were formed between lactic acid monomers via ester bonds, and the nanofiber scaffolds further prepared therefrom could release L-lactate [26]; Regarding bioactive molecules or drugs, Gilroy, C. A. et al.used elastin polypeptide fused with glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) and fibroblast growth factor 21 (FGF21) to collaboratively treat diabetes and overcome the problem of short half-life [27]; Additionally, Zhang, C., based on acid-sensitive bonds, constructed a doxorubicin (DOX) delivery system that achieved synergistic chemotherapy and photodynamic therapy and monitoring of release [28]. Inspired by this, this paper will systematically elaborate on how this "smart building block" paradigm can be applied to AKG, thereby transforming it from a molecule with inherent defects into a "smart building block" constituting advanced biomaterials. This fundamental transformation of identity is the key to solving its pharmacokinetic bottlenecks and releasing its full therapeutic potential.

This review seeks to establish a comprehensive theoretical basis and practical roadmap for this "paradigm shift" regarding AKG. Initially, the biological underpinnings of AKG-mediated cell fate programming are thoroughly examined, delineating AKG's core mechanisms as an "epigenetic remote control", "cellular aging brake", and "immune regulation switch". Subsequently, the review systematically summarizes the three progressive engineering strategies of AKG-empowered biomaterials, from the rudimentary physical encapsulation as "cargo", to multi-drug synergy "adjuvant", and ultimately to covalently constructed "framework". In this context, it is highlighted how the third strategy achieves "smart" properties such as responsive release and superior drug-loading capacity. Furthermore, emphasis is placed on the cutting-edge applications of these intelligent materials in programming specific cell fate (such as osteoblasts and immune cells) to promote tissue regeneration, regulate immunity, and potentiate oncotherapy. Finally, the current challenges are discussed in this field and the future perspectives are proposed, such as developing multi-responsive systems and constructing personalized treatment strategies, in order to promote the establishment of a new " metabolism-guided materialization of cell fate programming " model (Fig. 1). Previous reviews on AKG in the biomedical field mostly focused on the physiological mechanism and biological effects of its monomer as well as its applications in disease prevention and treatment. Few studies have systematically sorted out the research status of biomaterials constructed based on AKG. This review just conducts a comprehensive summary based on this research gap. Meanwhile, previous reviews on biomaterials based on metabolites rarely clearly and systematically presented such a unique framework. This review innovatively proposes a three-step progressive engineering evolution model, and more systematically and comprehensively reviews the biological functions and application value of AKG-empowered biomaterials under various pathological conditions.

The biological basis of AKG as the intrinsic blueprint for programmed cell fate

The physiological potential of AKG far goes beyond simple energy supply, which not only serves as the logical starting point for the development of AKG-empowered biomaterials, but also forms the theoretical cornerstone for the functioning of such materials. Precisely due to the highly valuable biological effects of AKG, the research and development of relevant materials possess broad interdisciplinary application prospects. Meanwhile, only by clarifying the effects of AKG at different dimensions can researchers accurately pinpoint the potential application fields of related materials.

The epigenetic metabolic remote control

Epigenetic regulation denotes the mechanism of modulating gene expression levels and cellular functions without altering the underlying DNA sequence. This is achieved through methods such as DNA methylation, histone modification, and non-coding RNA alteration, thereby enabling the stable transmission of genetic information [29]. Studies have shown that AKG exerts a pivotal influence on the programmed determination of cell fate by modulating the epigenetic landscape [30]. This regulatory effect is intrinsically linked to the 2-OGDD family. 2-OGDDs are a class of enzymes that require AKG as an obligatory co-substrate and depend on molecular oxygen and ferrous ion (Fe2+) to catalyze hydroxylation reactions of various substrates such as proteins, DNA, RNA, and fatty acids [31,32]. Therefore, their catalytic activity is exquisitely sensitive to fluctuations in the intracellular AKG concentration [33]. The key members of this family, lysine demethylases (KDMs) and TETs, govern the demethylation modifications of histones and DNA, respectively. The dysregulation of these two types of enzymes has been confirmed to be implicated in oncogenesis and cancer progression. For example, the inactivation of KDM3B (one of the lysine demethylases) is known to precipitate myelodysplastic syndrome and acute myeloid leukemia [34], while decreased KDM6A (another type of lysine demethylase) levels exacerbate the tumor-promoting effect of cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) [35]; the loss-of-function mutations of TETs are also common in various hematological malignancies [36].

Given that AKG is an obligatory co-substrate for the catalytic reactions of KDMs and TETs, investigators have been prompted to explore whether exogenous AKG supplementation could serve as a strategy to activate these key enzymes for anticancer therapy. Recent studies have provided compelling evidence supporting this hypothesis. In in vivo models of colorectal cancer, supplementing AKG attenuated the methylation levels of DNA and histone H3K4, inhibited the oncogenic Wnt signaling pathway, and upregulated the expression of differentiation-related genes, thereby inducing cancer cells to revert toward a differentiated phenotype and significantly inhibiting tumor growth (Fig. 2A) [37]. Additionally, Liu, N. et al. demonstrated that in mouse melanoma models, AKG effectively activated TET2 and TET3 (two of the TETs), elevating the DNA hydroxymethylation status of the programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) promoter region, and thereby upregulated the expression of PD-L1 in tumor tissues, augmenting the sensitivity of anti-PD-L1 immunotherapy (Fig. 2B) [38]. These findings indicate that AKG, with its epigenetic regulatory capabilities, holds significant promise in the treatment of malignant tumors and is poised for clinical translation, with the potential to benefit a broader patient population in the future.

The epigenetic regulatory effects of AKG are not restricted solely to the realm of oncotherapy. They also demonstrate profound therapeutic potential in diverse pathological conditions, such as skeletal disorders [41], kidney diseases [42], and metabolic diseases [43]. For instance, gestational AKG supplementation can partially reverse the fatty acid β-oxidation impairment caused by arsenic exposure during the fetal stage by modulating the DNA hydroxymethylation status of the carnitine palmitoyltransferase 1a (Cpt1a) gene locus (Fig. 2C) [39]. Specifically in the context of vascular calcification, Fu, M. et al. observed that exogenous AKG administration to calcified blood vessels could upregulate TET2 expression, thereby inhibiting the activation of nucleotide-binding oligomerization domain-like receptor pyrin domain-containing 3 (NLRP3) inflammasome pathway in vascular smooth muscle cells and attenuating the expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines, conferring significant vascular protection (Fig. 2D) [40].

Taken together, leveraging its epigenetic regulatory capacity, AKG can precisely regulate the gene expression programs of cells, presenting broad application prospects in various disease treatments. This provides an important theoretical basis for the development of novel intelligent biomaterials based on metabolic intervention and anticancer therapy.

The metabolic brake of cellular senescence

The unrelenting trajectory of global population aging is steadily accelerating, constituting a major social and healthcare challenge confronting global societies in the 21st century. As of 2015, the proportion of the global population aged 60 and above stood at 11%. It is predicted that this proportion will double by 2050, rising to 22% [44]. Coupled with this demographic shift, the prevalence of age-related pathologies such as Alzheimer's disease, coronary heart disease, cataracts, and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease continues to escalate [[45], [46], [47], [48]], and the consequent healthcare and socioeconomic ramifications are increasingly profound [49].

In the research on diseases related to aging, intervention strategies have witnessed substantial advancements in recent years. The early study by the Chin, R. M. group demonstrated that AKG could substantially prolong the longevity of adult Caenorhabditis elegans by inhibiting ATP synthase and the target of rapamycin (TOR) signaling pathway, and revealed its key role in mediating the anti-aging effect of dietary restriction (Fig. 3A) [50]. Subsequently, the anti-aging effect of AKG was validated across a spectrum of model organisms, including yeast and Drosophila [52,53]. However, the anti-aging potential of AKG in mammals remained elusive until its definitive demonstration in 2020 by the Asadi Shahmirzadi, A. team: exogenous supplementation of Calcium α-Ketoglutarate (CaAKG) could effectively attenuate pro-inflammatory cytokine levels and mitigate systemic frailty in mice, thereby promoting healthspan extension (Fig. 3B) [10]. ​Of greater clinical significance, a retrospective analysis by Demidenko, O. et al. revealed that in 42 subjects who underwent chronic AKG supplementation for an average of 7 months, the cohort exhibited a mean biological age reversal of 8 years (Fig. 3C) [51]. Although this study has limitations, such as the absence of a placebo-controlled cohort, these findings nonetheless provide pivotal pilot evidence for the geroprotective potential of AKG in humans. In conclusion, although the specific mechanism and long-term effect of AKG in human aging intervention warrant further validation through more large-scale and prospective clinical studies, its demonstrated potential to delay aging in various models has underscored promising new horizons for the development of anti-aging biomaterials and related intervention strategies.

Regulatory switch of immune homeostasis

Immune-mediated inflammatory diseases (IMIDs) are a group of multisystem disorders driven by aberrant and tissue-destructive immune-inflammatory responses [54]. Common disease types include inflammatory bowel disease [55], rheumatoid arthritis (RA) [56], asthma [57], and psoriasis [58], etc. These diseases typically exhibit high morbidity and disability rates, imposing a substantial burden on healthcare systems and socioeconomics [[59], [60], [61], [62]]. Epidemiological studies have shown that the incidence of diverse IMIDs has escalated substantially from 1990 to 2019 [63]. However, notably, a subset of patients exhibit intolerance or show suboptimal therapeutic responses to existing treatment regimens [64]. Consequently, it is imperative to explore novel intervention strategies to suppress the hyper-activated immune cascades in patients with IMIDs [65].

The dysregulated polarization balance between pro-inflammatory M1 and anti-inflammatory M2 phenotypes represents a hallmark of immune-inflammatory diseases [66]. Recent studies have elucidated that AKG acts as a pivotal metabolic orchestrator that regulates the phenotype of macrophages. Liu, P. S. et al. revealed that AKG skews the macrophage polarization toward a pro-resolving M2 phenotype through integrated metabolic and epigenetic mechanisms; conversely, depleted AKG levels favor their phenotypic shift toward the pro-inflammatory M1 state [67]. Subsequently, the research by Lin, Z. et al. further substantiated that exogenous AKG supplementation can trigger Janus Kinase 1/Signal Transducer and Activator of Transcription 3 (JAK1/STAT3) signaling axis, promoting the polarization of macrophages to the M2 type, and thereby mitigating post-infarction myocardial inflammatory damage (Fig. 4A) [68].

Furthermore, AKG exerts a complex, bidirectional regulatory influence on the adaptive immune system. Research has shown that AKG attenuates the aberrant hyperactivation of B cells during the lupus pathogenesis by upregulating the expression of cluster of differentiation 39 (CD39), positioning it as a promising therapeutic candidate for the treatment of autoimmune diseases (Fig. 4B) [69]. Notably, mounting evidence has established that AKG can reprogram the metabolic state and DNA methylation levels of T cells, thereby suppressing the differentiation of regulatory T cells (Tregs) endowed with pro-resolving and tissue-reparative properties [[70], [71], [72]]. This suggests that for IMIDs where the primary pathogenic driver is a T helper 17 cells (Th17)/Tregs imbalance [73], the targeted depletion of AKG levels may represent a novel therapeutic avenue.

Beyond the aforementioned core functions, AKG is also integral to the orchestration of amino acid and protein metabolism [74], antioxidation [75], and detoxification [76,77] (Fig. 5). By thoroughly elucidating these biological effects of AKG, we are expected to deliver precise instructions to specific cells using AKG-empowered biomaterials in a manner analogous to programming, thereby steering their fate trajectories.

AKG-empowered biomaterials engineering strategies from passive transportation to active construction

AKG possesses multiple excellent biological effects, all of which have extremely high practical application values. In particular, its anti-aging effect and anti-cancer function exerted through epigenetic regulation are direct and efficient, which other metabolites in TCA cycle, such as citric acid, SUC, and fumaric acid, do not possess [78,79]. On the contrary, the latter two are considered competitive inhibitors of 2-OGDDs and may play a role in promoting tumor progression [80]. In addition, in terms of chemical structure, AKG has a ketone group that citric acid, SUC, and fumarate do not possess, which creates more favorable conditions for it to undergo chemical reactions with other substances to construct more complex and diverse biomaterials [[81], [82], [83]]. In summary, AKG exhibits unique advantages at both structural and functional levels that are not possessed by other metabolites in the TCA cycle, thus attracting more attention. To maximize the therapeutic potential and structural advantages of AKG, AKG-empowered biomaterials based on different construction strategies have emerged as required.

Strategy one as the protected cargo for enhancing delivery efficiency

To overcome the inherent pharmacokinetic limitations of AKG, such as low oral bioavailability, rapid in vivo metabolism, and suboptimal targeting of pathological sites, researchers have developed various AKG delivery systems. These systems encapsulate AKG as "cargo" within specific carriers, aiming to enhance its delivery efficiency and therapeutic targeting. For instance, Chen X's team [84] developed a metal-organic framework (MOF)-based carrier (Mn-MIL-100@AKG). This carrier is constructed using high-valent manganese heptoxide (Mn2O7) and benzene-1,3,5-tricarboxylic acid (BTC), and its highly porous architecture can effectively load and stabilize AKG through adsorption. ​In preclinical models of intervertebral disc degeneration, this system is administered locally through injection, bypassing the limitations associated with systemic oral administration. Furthermore, it utilizes the intrinsic antioxidant properties of the carrier to synergistically clear ROS within the nucleus pulposus cells, while protecting AKG from being converted to 2-hydroxyglutarate (2HG) and losing its activity in the acidic microenvironment of the lesion. Experimental results demonstrated that the efficacy of this delivery platform outperformed both the empty carrier and free AKG (Fig. 6A) [84]. Similarly, Cheng, C. et al. [85] designed a bone-targeting fluorescent nanocarrier with a core-shell multilayer structure (β-NaYF4:7%Yb,60%Nd@NaLuF4@mSiO2-EDTA-AKG, abbreviated as LMEK). The core consists of a lanthanide (Yb3+, Nd3+)-doped rare-earth nanoparticle, coated with an inert NaLuF4 layer to minimize fluorescence quenching. Its outermost layer is mesoporous silica loaded with AKG, and the surface is modified with ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) to enhance its affinity for hydroxyapatite in bone tissue. AKG is loaded into the mesoporous channels through physical adsorption. The therapeutic dosage of this system is reduced to 0.2% of that of free AKG, significantly improving its bioavailability. Moreover, the system achieves theranostic integration by leveraging its near-infrared fluorescence imaging capability, demonstrating ideal efficacy in mouse models of osteoporosis (Fig. 6B) [85].

The studies in section 3.1 represent the foundational strategy for engineering of AKG-empowered biomaterials, namely the incorporation of AKG within functionalized carriers via physical loading. This strategy has mitigated the primary constraints of AKG, such as easy degradation, difficulty in tissue accumulation and heavy metabolic burden, to a certain extent, enhancing the precision and effectiveness of treatment. Short-term in vitro and in vivo experiments have validated the satisfactory biocompatibility of these carriers, progressively promoting the metamorphosis of AKG from a "physiologically disadvantaged" molecule to a "clinically applicable" drug. However, these advanced systems still face significant hurdles in clinical translation. The fabrication of both carriers necessitates harsh synthesis conditions, such as high temperature and pressure, alongside complex chemical modifications. Furthermore, LMEK requires specialized imaging equipment for effect evaluation [84,85]. These factors may hinder the scalability and broad clinical adoption of these "cargo-based" delivery platforms.

Strategy Two as the adjuvant for functional synergy towards synergistic outcomes

In addition to single-agent delivery, AKG is frequently co-administered with other bioactive moieties to elicit a synergistic "1 + 1 > 2″ effect. In this context, an "adjuvant" denotes an agent that, when administered in conjunction with an antigen, potentiates the host's immune response [86]. For example, in the research by Elumalai, P. [87], AKG functioned as an immunological adjuvant: to prevent the infection of Edwardsiellatarda in fish, the researchers leveraged the cationic amino groups of chitosan and the anionic carboxyl groups of sodium alginate to fabricate a vaccine scaffold that could stably encapsulate the inactivated Edwardsiellatarda antigen by ionotropic gelation; after mixing the vaccine with commercial feed and subsequently functionalized with a 5% AKG oil coating, engineering an oral vaccine delivery system based on the scaffolds. This system not only safeguards the scaffold against degradation by gastric acid but also exploits the mucoadhesive properties of sodium alginate to prolong its intestinal residence time, facilitating site-specific release and enhanced absorption. Owing to the adjuvant effect of AKG, the vaccine successfully augmented innate immune response and elevated specific antibody titers (Fig. 7A) [87].

Furthermore, Li, Q. et al. [88] developed a dual-drug co-delivery platform based on bioactive hydrogels (GelhMSS). The team engineered a mesoporous silica nanoparticle with a virus-like spike structure (SSiO2), leveraging their high specific surface area to physically encapsulate AKG (AKG@SSiO2) before incorporating these particles into the hydrogel pores. The other active ingredient, D-mannose, was covalently tethered to the hydrogel backbone to form a co-delivery system of AKG and D-mannose. A defining feature of this study is that the spiky structure of SSiO2 significantly enhanced cellular internalization efficiency (2.5 times higher than smooth mesoporous silica nanoparticles), thereby overcoming the bottleneck of poor membrane permeability inherent to AKG monomers. Simultaneously, the covalent conjugation of D-mannose to the hydrogels avoided the risk of leakage that might cause insulin resistance. AKG and D-mannose, acting as functional co-components, independently modulated the functions of bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells and immune cells, offering a novel paradigm for the regeneration of diabetic jawbone defects (Fig. 7B) [88].

It should be specially noted that the term "adjuvant" originates from immunology and specifically refers to substances that enhance the host's immune response to antigens [86]. However, in the specific context of AKG-empowered biomaterials, the meaning of this term has been extended to a broader sense and covers a wider range of synergistic therapeutic effects. In different research cases, AKG either acts as an immunological adjuvant in the strict sense in vaccines or serves as a functional co-component to play a role in the synergistic treatment of diseases with other active ingredients. In the study of Li, Q. [88], the role of AKG belongs to the latter.

The strategy of jointly delivering AKG with other drugs is defined as an advanced strategy in AKG-empowered biomaterial engineering. Compared with monotherapy, multi-drug co-delivery not only continues to improve the pharmacokinetic characteristics of AKG but, more importantly, transcends the therapeutic ceiling of single-drug interventions through functional synergy [89]. Given the multifaceted pathogenesis of complex diseases (such as the coexistence of metabolic disorders and immune disorders in diabetic bone defects) [90], multi-drug co-delivery can concurrently engage diverse targets and modulate multiple stages of disease progression. For example, the bioactive hydrogels dual-loaded with AKG and D-mannose can simultaneously stimulate the osteogenic differentiation of mesenchymal stem cells while stabilizing the local immune microenvironment [88], thereby maximizing therapeutic outcomes. At the same time, the synergy of multiple drugs can significantly lower the requisite dosage of individual agents, minimizing off-target toxicities and systemic metabolic burdens [91]. This integrated approach also mitigates the risk of pathogens or malignant cells developing drug resistance, ultimately extending the effective therapeutic window [92].

However, inherent limitations still persist within these two categories of materials. Regarding vaccine scaffolds, if not ingested by fish promptly, the surface-coated AKG oil layer is prone to dissolution within the aqueous environment, resulting in the uncontrolled leakage of the metabolite [87]. Meanwhile, bioactive hydrogels often necessitate stringent storage conditions, including precise temperature and humidity control, and preserving their structural and functional integrity during long-term storage remains a formidable technical bottleneck [93].

Both Strategy One and Strategy Two rely on physical encapsulation, AKG remains a passive component and foregoes active participation in material synthesis via its inherent functional moieties. This passive drug-loading mode has several drawbacks: Firstly, physical encapsulation struggles to maintain robust drug retention within the carrier, predisposing the system to premature leakage [94]; Should the drug be liberated prematurely during circulation, it may lead to off-target accumulation and potential systemic toxicity [95]. Secondly, the absence of covalent constraints frequently results in uncontrolled burst release kinetics at the lesion site [96]. For example, Mao, C. et al. [97] constructed a DMAKG delivery system, which exhibited a rapid burst release of up to 70% within the first 12 h [97]. Finally, compared with the method of connecting through covalent bonds (such as acid-sensitive bonds) that can achieve pathological environment-triggered release, physical encapsulation lacks similar precise release regulation mechanisms and is difficult to achieve on-demand drug release [98].

Strategy three as the framework of the material to create inherent intelligent properties

Given the multiple constraints faced by passive transport of AKG in practical applications, researchers have proposed a more advanced construction model for AKG-empowered biomaterials, which involves leveraging the intrinsic functional moieties of AKG to covalently anchor it to biomaterials or assimilate it directly as a structural building block during material synthesis.

Wang et al. [99] initially functionalized hyaluronic acid (HA) with carbohydrazide (CDH) to obtain the aminoated hyaluronic acid derivative (HA-NH2). Subsequently, the ketone groups in DMAKG reacted with the amino groups in HA-NH2 to undergo an imine condensation reaction, covalently grafting DMAKG onto HA-NH2. Finally, through ultrasonic treatment, they successfully prepared self-assembled hyaluronic acid-α- ketoglutarate nanoparticles with acid-sensitive hydrazone bonds (HA-AKG NPs). Characterization revealed that these nanoparticles were spherical with a diameter of approximately 60 nm, exhibiting superior monodispersity, stability, and anti-dilution properties. Under normal physiological conditions, minimal premature leaching of AKG was observed; conversely, in the acidic microenvironment of osteoarthritis (OA) lesions, the release of AKG was markedly accelerated. Release kinetics substantiated that this material could achieve sustained, gradual, and controllable drug release for up to 10 days. Meanwhile, the carrier HA could bind to cluster of differentiation 44 (CD44) receptor on the surface of chondrocytes. Therefore, this material not only surmounted the limitations of short half-life, low bioavailability, and poor chondral targeting of AKG but also possessed advantages such as the synergistic treatment of HA and AKG, pH-responsive precise sustained release, ideal residence time in the joint cavity, and facile cellular internalization. This system exhibits certain clinical transformation potential in the field of OA treatment (Fig. 8A) [99].

Wang, Z [100]. investigated the osteoinductive potential of AKG by developing AKG-based polymer microspheres (PAKG MPs): the AKG monomer underwent polycondensation with aliphatic diols (e.g., 1,8-octanediol or 1,10-decanediol) at 120 °C to form ester bonds between the carboxyl groups of AKG and the hydroxyl groups of the diols, yielding the AKG-based polymer (PAKG). It was then fabricated into biodegradable microspheres via the oil-water emulsification method. The hydrophilicity, cytotoxicity, and degradation rate of the microspheres could be tuned by introducing polyethylene glycol (PEG) or modulating the carbon chain length of the diol. This AKG-based material not only circumvents the problems of pronounced cytotoxicity and rapid hydrolysis of AKG monomers but also facilitates long-term sustained release of AKG through ester bond hydrolysis. More importantly, PAKG MPs can be internalized by cells such as bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells that originally lack phagocytic ability, effectively surmounting the limitation of suboptimal cell permeability of AKG monomers and achieving efficient intracellular drug delivery (Fig. 8B) [100].

Kang Z. et al. [101] also combined AKG with 1,10-decanediol in a 1:1 M ratio and synthesized AKG polyester with ester bond connections through a polycondensation at 130 °C. Then, they processed it with the sol-gel method to obtain smooth-surfaced spherical AKG polyester microsphere powders. In contrast, they mechanically blended the microsphere powders with calcium phosphate bone cement (CPC) powders, added the curing liquid for stirring, and injected them into molds for curing. Eventually, they constructed a calcium phosphate bone cement composite material loaded with AKG polyester microspheres (CPC/AKG), which was used to treat osteoporotic bone defects. This composite material enabled the sustained and slow release of AKG in bone defect areas through the hydrolysis of ester bonds, without any appreciable burst release effect. It could maintain an effective concentration at lesion sites and optimize the performance of traditional CPC. It established a dual treatment system combining bone repair materials and active factor delivery. Particularly ingenious was that there was a positive feedback relationship between the drug release and the material degradation of this composite material, showing a high degree of synchronization between the degradation rate and the tissue repair process (Fig. 8C) [101].

Inamdar, S [102]. developed a co-delivery system of spermine and AKG to regulate the phenotype of immune cells: similarly, PAKG was synthesized via the polycondensation of AKG with aliphatic diols, which was then combined with spermine to form emulsions. The emulsification-evaporation method was employed to prepare AKG microparticles (spermine encapsulated PAKG MPs) that could load spermine. Owing to the strong electrostatic interaction between the negatively charged PAKG and the positively charged spermine, the encapsulation efficiency of spermine in the microparticles reached up to 90%, and the particles were smooth spherical in shape with an average diameter of 2.7 ± 0.1 μm, falling within the phagocytic range of macrophages. These microparticles served dual roles as both a carrier and an active molecule. They could simultaneously release AKG and spermine through ester bond hydrolysis in an acidic environment, facilitating the synergistic delivery of the two metabolites to regulate the phenotype of immune cells. Moreover, this system offered advantages such as high drug loading capacity, facile uptake by immune cells such as macrophages, stable spermine encapsulation, etc., effectively addressing bottlenecks such as the difficulty in maintaining effective concentrations of AKG and spermine at the lesion site, poor targeting, and insufficient long-term efficacy (Fig. 8D) [102].

Mangal, J. L [103]. also used PAKG MPs to induce antigen-specific immunosuppressive response: after AKG and 1,10-decanediol were synthesized into PAKG via polycondensation at a molar ratio of 1:1, the classic water-oil-water emulsion techniques were adopted to prepare PAKG MPs encapsulated with glycolytic inhibitor (PFK15) and bovine collagen type II (bc2) (PFK15 and bc2 encapsulated PAKG MPs), thereby constructing a three-component co-delivery system of AKG, PFK15, and bc2. The microparticles exhibited a relatively constant degradation rate within the 10-day experimental observation period, with an average diameter of 1.5 ± 0.2 μm, which is highly suitable for phagocytosis by dendritic cells, and the encapsulation efficiencies for PFK15 and bc2 reached 47.4% and 54.9%, respectively. The PAKG MPs in this study also acted as both bioactive molecules with therapeutic effects and delivery carriers. This design concept not only solves the inherent defects of AKG monomers, but also ensures that multiple active ingredients can be simultaneously delivered into the same cell [103].

In summary, Strategy Three represents the evolution of the material design philosophy: researchers no longer simply physically encapsulate AKG within the carriers, but instead utilize biotechnological methods to engineer AKG as part of the material framework. Through the cleavage of chemical bonds during the material degradation process, AKG was released in situ, achieving a high degree of integration between structure and function. Compared with traditional prodrugs, HA-AKG NPs and PAKG MPs in the above-listed research cases share certain similarities but possess more essential differences. Polymer prodrugs are constructed by conjugating therapeutic drugs to polymer backbones via covalent bonds [104], and HA-AKG NPs are exactly formed through this small molecule/polymer conjugation mode. However, the difference lies in that the polymers in polymer prodrugs usually have no pharmacological activity at all, and their function is merely to improve the solubility, half-life and other properties of therapeutic molecules, while HA in HA-AKG NPs exerts a synergistic therapeutic effect with AKG. In addition, a considerable number of polymer prodrugs are linear macromolecules with poor local retention capacity, whereas the spherical morphology of HA-AKG NPs endows them with long-term retention properties [99,105]. As for the material construction mode of PAKG MPs, it is completely different from that of polymer prodrugs. AKG directly participates in the construction of the polymer backbone, and its spherical morphology enables more efficient cellular uptake, leading to a more significant improvement in bioavailability compared with polymer prodrugs. Furthermore, the excellent properties of PAKG MPs, such as being applicable for composite use with other materials, optimizing the uptake efficiency of traditional microspheres, and acting as a carrier for delivering other small-molecule drugs, are not possessed by polymer prodrugs that only function as a single drug delivery system [100]. In addition to the superior advantages of AKG-empowered biomaterials over polymer prodrugs in terms of material properties and application scope, more importantly, there exists the most essential distinction in the role definition between AKG in the former and therapeutic molecules in the latter. The covalent conjugation of AKG not only drives self-assembly but also endows HA-AKG NPs with appropriate amphipathicity and surface charge, ensuring the intact morphology and uniform particle size of nanoparticles. Similarly, AKG in PAKG MPs also regulates parameters such as zeta potential of the polymer, guaranteeing the regular morphology of microspheres. In contrast, the drugs in polymer prodrugs exert an insignificant regulatory effect on the overall performance of polymers [23,99,100]. This indicates that AKG is not only a therapeutic agent exerting pharmacological effects but also an indispensable building block that ensures the successful synthesis of biomaterials and maintains their structure and performance, which is the primary difference between the building block and the drug component in polymer prodrugs.

The material construction approach in strategy three can be regarded as the core innovative strategy for the engineering of AKG-empowered biomaterials, truly completing the transformation of AKG from a standalone "therapeutic molecule" to a "smart building block". Its advantages are mainly reflected in many aspects: precise release triggered by the environment (such as pH response, redox response, enzymatic cleavage, physical stimulus response, etc.) [106,107], more stable drug release kinetics [108], lower systemic toxicities [109], higher drug loading capacity [110], and enhancement of the performance of traditional biomaterials [111]. Specifically, in terms of the loading capacity of different material systems for AKG, the maximum drug loading efficiency of SSiO2 designed by Li, Q [88]. is 44.86 ± 2.067%, while the side chain substitution rate of DMAKG to HA-NH2 is 65%, and researchers clearly pointed out that this value is relatively high compared with previous studies. The mass proportion of AKG in PAKG MPs is close to 50%, while each milligram of LMEK can only load 0.3 mg of AKG [88,99,100]. As for the targeting performance, although LMEK achieves bone tissue targeting with the help of EDTA, it lacks the environmentally responsive precise release possessed by HA-AKG NPs and PAKG MPs, making therapeutic molecules likely to be released both in normal bone tissue and lesion bone tissue. The latter two release AKG through specific chemical bond cleavage in the lesion microenvironment, reducing systemic distribution and achieving the therapeutic effect of precise targeting of lesions [85,99,100]. In terms of release control, about 93.60% of AKG in LMEK was released within 24 h. Under pathological conditions simulating diabetes, the cumulative release amount of GelhMSS within 72 h exceeded 60% [85,88]. In contrast, the pH-sensitive release trend of HA-AKG NPs persisted throughout the 10-day observation period. Due to the differences in particle size and chemical composition, the degradation cycle of PAKG MPs ranges from several weeks to several months [99,100]. It is evident that due to the effective constraint of chemical bonds, AKG achieves longer-term release, which solves the trouble of frequent administration and can better match the treatment cycle of diseases. Moreover, GelhMSS exhibited a fast AKG release rate within the first 10 h under different pH conditions, and then tended to level off. Although the cumulative release curves of HA-AKG NPs under different pH conditions also have inflection points, the change in the AKG release rate is relatively moderate [88,99]. In terms of transformation potential, the integrated structural and functional design of strategy three simplifies the composition of biomaterials. In contrast, systems such as Mn-MIL-100@AKG and LMEK not only involve complex synthesis processes, but the components in the materials also bring potential metabolic burdens to the body [84,85]. Therefore, biomaterials developed based on the design concept of Strategy Three may have a clearer clinical translation path. The various advantages of strategy Three are precisely the embodiment of the "smart" concept. The paradigm shift from "carrying cargo" to "constructing the framework" is the key foundation for AKG-empowered biomaterials to advance towards intelligence and surmount the bottlenecks of clinical translation (Table 1).

However, the various materials involved in Strategy Three are still not flawless. Studies have indicated that the modified HA may possess immunogenicity [112], and Wang, X.'s [99] research did not fully assess the possible systemic immune response that nanoparticles might elicit, which might limit their clinical application [99]. The diol monomers released during the degradation of AKG polymer microspheres may exhibit potential cytotoxicity. For example, 1,10-decanediol can significantly inhibit cell proliferation at a relatively low concentration, exacerbating the risk of clinical application [100,113]. Inamdar, S [102]. also observed that during the initial release phase, spermine exhibited a burst release phenomenon under acidic conditions (pH 5.0). Future optimization of the loading method of spermine is still warranted to achieve more stable drug release kinetics profiles [102].

Application frontier on how enabling materials program specific cell fate

After clarifying three progressive engineering strategies, this section systematically discusses the application frontiers of AKG-empowered biomaterials in the biomedical field, aiming to demonstrate that material engineering means can largely release the biological potential of AKG, and such biomaterials have relatively remarkable clinical translation potential and multidisciplinary application prospects.

Bone and cartilage regeneration through programming osteoblast and chondrocyte fate

Bone defects arise from various factors, which not only inflict a double burden of physical injury and psychological stress on patients [114,115] but also become a critical social problem due to significant labor loss and increased medical costs [116,117]. Currently, the optimal repair of complex bone defects remains a major challenge in clinical practice [118]. It is worth noting that the emergence of AKG-empowered biomaterials provides new hope for solving this problem. Wang, Z. et al. [119] engineered a DMAKG delivery system based on a gelatin scaffold, in which DMAKG suppressed the adipogenic differentiation of multipotent mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) in aged mice and downregulated the expression levels of related genes (such as fatty acid binding protein 4 and peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma, abbreviated as Fabp4 and Pparg). Besides, DMAKG could either administer AKG alone to promote osteogenic differentiation of stem cells, or significantly elevate the expression of osteogenic markers (such as alkaline phosphatase, osteocalcin, and bone sialoprotein, abbreviated as ALP, OCN and BSP), in the presence of bone morphogenetic protein 2 (BMP2), thereby effectively augmenting the osteogenic differentiation and mineralization induced by BMP2. Research data showed that by locally delivering DMAKG through the gelatin scaffold combined with a small dose of BMP2, the new bone mass formed in aged mice is three times that of using BMP2 alone, and it also abrogated the inhibition of osteogenic differentiation of mesenchymal stem cells mediated by lipopolysaccharide (LPS), successfully achieving the lineage programming of stem cells towards the osteogenic fate. Combined with the anti-inflammatory and energy-supplementing effects of AKG, this system shifted the balance of adipogenic-to-osteogenic differentiation in the elderly mice's bodies towards the osteogenic direction, providing a feasible strategy for bone defect repair in the context of senescence [119].

Liu H [120]. designed an electrospun scaffold (Fn-TA-PFC/PCK) incorporating three biomimetic strategies to promote bone regeneration in elderly individuals: 1) The fibronectin (Fn) on the scaffold surface could locally enrich endogenous vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), expediting the vascularization of the bone defect areas; 2) Immune regulatory factors promoted the polarization of macrophages to the M2 phenotype, mitigating local inflammation levels, and creating an immune microenvironment favorable to tissue repair; 3) AKG improved the metabolic disorder of aged bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells, augmenting ATP levels to support their proliferation and differentiation, and down-regulating the expression of aging-related genes, restoring their impaired osteogenic differentiation potential. This scaffold exhibited excellent repair effects in various mouse bone defect models. Parameters such as the number of bone trabeculae, trabecular separation, and bone volume fraction in scaffold group were significantly superior to those in control group. Mechanically, multi-omics combined analysis elucidated that this scaffold could activate osteogenic and angiogenic-related pathways (such as Wnt, PI3K-Akt, etc.) and up-regulate the levels of osteogenic and angiogenic-related metabolites. This material implemented the core strategy of "metabolic repair, immune regulation, angiogenesis, promotion of osteogenesis", breaking the vicious cycle of "metabolism, inflammation, ischemia" in aging-related bone regeneration disorders, and offering new ideas for bone regeneration treatment in related pathological backgrounds (Fig. 9C) [120].

For the fracture healing disorder associated with type 2 diabetes (T2DM), Wang J [121]. employed alendronate liposomes loaded with AKG (AKG@Cy5.5@ALN-Liposome) to selectively deliver AKG to macrophages in bone tissue. It effectively replenished the decrease in intracellular AKG levels caused by diabetes, thereby elevating the activity of TETs, reducing the DNA methylation level in BMP2 gene promoter region, and restoring the function of macrophages in secreting BMP2. Eventually, this facilitated the differentiation of bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells into osteoblasts and expedited the formation of mineralization nodules. The experimental results demonstrated that, unlike the situation where systemic supplementation of AKG monomers exacerbated the fracture healing disorder in type 2 diabetic mice, the mice treated with this liposome intervention exhibited a higher number of bone trabeculae, larger bone callus index, and higher bone volume fraction at the fracture site. Moreover, the strength, stiffness, and toughness of the healed bone were significantly enhanced. This study positioned macrophages as the regulatory node, through a cascade reaction of "AKG supplementation, epigenetic regulation, secretion of osteogenic factors, osteoblast differentiation", to attain targeted and efficient fracture healing in the context of T2DM (Fig. 9A) [121].

The AKG-empowered biomaterials not only can program the fate of stem cells to promote their differentiation into osteoblasts, but also can finely regulate the behavior of chondrocytes. Xue, F. et al. [122] encapsulated AKG in nanoparticles formed by self-assembly of cowpea extracts, yielding AKG-loaded capsid protein nanoparticles (CPaKG) for the treatment of OA. This system suppressed the expression of cell cycle arrest proteins (such as P16, P21, and P53) in senescent chondrocytes, thereby restoring their proliferative ability; at the same time, it downregulated the release of senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP) factors, mitigating the local inflammatory level. Moreover, the levels of chondrocyte matrix synthesis markers (such as proteoglycans, type II collagen, etc.) significantly elevated, while the expression of matrix metalloproteinase 13 (MMP13) was significantly attenuated, combined with its scavenging effect on ROS in chondrocytes, jointly achieved the effective inhibition of the ROS-nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB)-SASP axis. In mouse OA models, this system significantly reduced the proportion of senescent chondrocytes in the lesion areas, ameliorated the grade of cartilage damage, reduced synovial inflammation, and diminished the formation of joint osteophytes. Therefore, this delivery system focused on resisting the senescence of chondrocytes and exerted multiple biological effects, such as anti-inflammatory, scavenging of ROS, and maintaining the balance of extracellular matrix, targeting multiple pathological links of OA and initially demonstrating multi-level therapeutic potential (Fig. 9B) [122].

In three studies on bone regeneration, compared with Wang, J.'s [121] research, Wang, Z.'s [119] research placed more emphasis on the promoting effect of DMAKG on the osteogenic effect of BMP2, while the former cut in from the epigenetic perspective and deeply explored the influence of macrophage glutaminolysis on bone repair under the background of T2DM, with the role of macrophages elaborated more clearly. Liu, H [120]. innovatively proposed the concept of endogenous niche engineering. The progressive aspect of his research lies in the recognition that the impairment of bone regeneration under the background of advanced age is a comprehensive outcome of metabolic disorders, vascular deficiency, and immune imbalance. Although the research results are encouraging, there are still aspects requiring improvement from a critical perspective. Though Wang, Z [119]. designed a sound non-clinical randomized controlled study and adopted highly relevant animal models, the adoption of the ectopic bone formation model reduced the credibility of the evidence. Wang, J [121]. still needs to further clarify whether long-term application of alendronate (ALN)-modified liposomes will interfere with the normal bone remodeling process, because ALN itself can inhibit the activity of osteoclasts [123]. For weight-bearing bone fractures that more commonly occur in the elderly, such as fractures of the femur and spine [124,125], it remains unclear whether the mechanical strength of Fn-TA-PFC/PCK is sufficient to support the repair process. How to guarantee the storage stability of CpaKG, which is sensitive to temperature fluctuations, is also a difficult problem to be solved. In addition, although most studies have provided detailed and high-quality evidence in aspects including biomaterial design, in vitro mechanism exploration, and animal model verification, the researches are still in the proof-of-concept stage due to factors such as the limitation of experimental models to small animals and the immaturity of biomaterials. To reach the level of clinically credible systems, it is necessary to complete large animal experiments and human clinical trials at the very least.

The AKG-empowered biomaterials, by their ability to steer the differentiation fate of mesenchymal stem cells and precisely regulate the behavior of bone lineage cells, have demonstrated substantial efficacy in the treatment of skeletal disorder models. They possess the value for further conducting relevant application exploration.

Soft tissue regeneration and repair via programming epithelial, neural, and vascular cell fate

Soft tissue defects entail diverse complications such as infection, functional impairment, and non-union of fractures [[126], [127], [128]]. To date, this remains a persistent challenge that urgently needs to be tackled in clinical practice [129]. The AKG-empowered biomaterials integrate the biological activity of metabolic molecules with the structural advantages of biomaterials, providing a new approach for efficient repair of soft tissues.

In the field of oral mucosa injury repair, Wang, Y [130]. et al. developed a polyethyleneimine (PEI)/polyacrylic acid (PAA)/AKG supramolecular network delivery system. This system utilized AKG to upregulate the activity of cyclin-dependent kinase 1-cyclin A (CDK1-cyclin A) complex and cyclin-dependent kinase 1-cyclin B (CDK1-cyclin B) complexes in oral epithelial cells, propelling cells to enter the S and M phases, and downregulating the transcriptional level of the cell cycle inhibitor P21. Moreover, AKG could directly bind to the transcription factor forkhead box M1 (FOXM1), stabilizing its structure, and thereby coordinating the expression of cell cycle proteins such as cyclin A1 (CCNA1), cyclin A2 (CCNA2), cyclin B1 (CCNB1), and cyclin B2 (CCNB2). In mouse palatal wound models, this delivery system could undergo in situ gelation upon contact with wound exudate within seconds, forming a protective layer, reducing mechanical friction and microbial stimulation, thereby expediting the healing rate, enhancing collagen deposition, and significantly reducing local inflammation and edema. This system simultaneously achieved wound protection, epithelial proliferation, and collagen deposition, and its "band-aid" design is both innovative and practical, exhibiting potential for oral mucosa injury repair (Fig. 10A) [130].

For skin injury repair, Jaggarapu, Mmcs [131] synthesized PAKG MPs using the emulsion evaporation method. In vitro scratch experiments demonstrated that these microspheres could significantly promote the migration of human keratinocytes, and the wound closure rate was superior to that of the free AKG group and the phosphate buffered saline (PBS) group. In mouse full-thickness skin defect models, the microsphere treatment group attained complete healing on the 9th day, which was faster than the control group, and there was diminished cell infiltration in the wound area. The healed skin had a higher ultimate tensile strength (UTS) and more profuse deposition of type III and type I collagen. This AKG delivery system, revolving around the synergistic effect of "promoting cell migration, accelerating healing, reducing inflammation, improving repair quality", realized both speed and quality in skin wound healing (Fig. 10B) [131].

In the research on nerve cell regeneration, Vishnoi, T [133]. administered AKG to Neuro-2a cell line (a neural cell line) via three modalities: exogenous direct addition, chitosan or gelatin microsphere delivery, and freeze gel-microsphere composite scaffolds. 3-(4,5-dimethylthiazol-2-yl)-2,5-diphenyltetrazolium bromide (MTT) assay confirmed that direct addition of AKG was the most rapid to take effect; chitosan microspheres and gelatin microspheres were more suitable for medium- and long-term cultivation, respectively, elevating the cell proliferation rate by approximately 70% and 42%; the freeze gel-microsphere composite system performed adequately in maintaining the long-term proliferation of cells. Scanning electron microscopy observation confirmed that the cells were evenly distributed on the scaffold and the neurites extended well. AKG could also convert intracellular ammonia into glutamate and glutamine, which not only alleviated the decline in cell viability caused by ammonia toxicity but also promoted the synthesis of excitatory neurotransmitter glutamate [134]. Neuro-2a cell line, exhibiting multiple characteristics of neuronal cells, is often used in tissue engineering and neural regeneration research [135]. This study designed delivery systems tailored to different cultivation scenarios based on the characteristics of neural cells being sensitive to ammonia toxicity and needing to synthesize neurotransmitters, which has guiding significance for the repair of neural tissue defects [133].

To tackle the high re-stenosis rate after small-diameter artificial vessel transplantation, Li, M [132]. engineered small-diameter artificial vessel grafts loaded with AKG. AKG controlled-release could modulate an immune microenvironment conducive to repair, reverse the inhibition of proliferation of human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs) precipitated by inflammation, and simultaneously inhibit the excessive proliferation of vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs) induced by inflammation, averting intimal thickening. In rabbit carotid artery transplantation models, the AKG-loaded grafts remained patent at 1, 2, and 3 months, with no thrombosis or blockage. HE staining revealed that the intima was more continuous, smooth, and uniform, and its thickness was significantly attenuated. This graft adhered to the principle of "inflammation regulation, promoting endothelial proliferation, inhibiting smooth muscle proliferation", and by constructing an anti-inflammatory microenvironment to regulate the behavior of vascular cells, it significantly diminished the incidence of re-stenosis (Fig. 10C) [132].

Wang, Y [130]. and Jaggarapu, Mmcs [131] both focused on the role of AKG in promoting wound healing. The former targeted mucosal injury, while the latter attached importance to skin injury. Both have keenly perceived the limitations of AKG monomers in practical application, and then demonstrated the application potential of AKG-empowered biomaterials in the field of soft tissue repair and regeneration through excellent material engineering methods. However, Wang, Y.'s [130] research offered a more in-depth mechanistic explanation, and thoroughly explored how AKG regulated cell proliferation at the molecular level by stabilizing FOXM1. In comparison, Jaggarapu, Mmcs's [131] research provided relatively insufficient elaboration on the mechanism of action. In addition, difficult healing of oral mucosal wounds in clinical practice is often associated with aging, diabetes, radiotherapy, and chemotherapy [136,137], while Wang, Y [130]. mainly used healthy mouse models to verify the therapeutic effect. If the role of the supramolecular network delivery system can be further verified in pathological models in the future, the clinical persuasiveness will be stronger. Although Vishnoi, T [133]. compared a variety of AKG delivery strategies in detail, compared with the other three studies mentioned in this section, this study is completely based on in vitro experiments and does not involve any animal experiments, which constitutes a decisive restriction on its level of evidence. Finally, despite the fact that Li, M [132]. referred to three months as "long-term" in the paper, in clinical practice, the failure of artificial vascular transplantation often occurs within a longer time span [138], and setting a three-month animal experiment is not enough to completely exclude the possibility of long-term restenosis of artificial blood vessels.

In summary, AKG-empowered biomaterials can directly regulate cell proliferation or indirectly enhance cell functions by improving the microenvironment, thereby precisely influencing the behaviors of mucosal epithelial cells, skin keratinocytes, nerve cells, vascular cells, etc. They have room for further exploration and application in the repair of soft tissue defects.

Regulation of immune homeostasis by programming the fate of immune cells

As mentioned in section 2.3, AKG possesses the ability to modulate multiple immune cell phenotypes, which provides a theoretical basis for AKG-empowered biomaterials to be employed in the treatment of immune and inflammation-related diseases. Mao, C et al. [97] utilized a thermosensitive gel as the delivery platform for DMAKG and validated its efficacy in mouse models of systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE)-related periodontitis (PD). After intervention, AKG effectively ameliorated the M1/M2 polarization imbalance of macrophages in the periodontal tissues of mice through metabolic reprogramming, accompanied by a diminution in pro-inflammatory cytokines, such as tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), interleukin-1β (IL-1β), and interleukin-6 (IL-6) in the gingival tissues and a significant depletion in the number of osteoclasts. Through micro-CT and histological analysis, it was observed that the parameters of alveolar bone, the degree of gingival tissue destruction, and the periodontal attachment level of mice in gel treatment group were superior to those in control group. This thermosensitive gel specifically designed for intraperiodontal pocket delivery of DMAKG effectively modulated macrophage polarization, inhibited bone resorption, and selectively improved the major pathological manifestations of PD associated with SLE, namely periodontal tissue inflammation and alveolar bone resorption, providing a new strategy for the treatment of this disease (Fig. 11A) [97].

Liu H [139]. developed an electrospun fiber scaffold (PFC/PCK) that could simultaneously dispense AKG and biomineralization ions (calcium phosphate, CaP) to regulate the immune microenvironment during bone formation. This scaffold could down-regulate the expression of M1-type markers (inducible nitric oxide synthase, abbreviated as iNOS; TNF-α; IL-1β) in macrophages and up-regulate the expression of M2-type markers (cluster of differentiation 206, arginase-1, interleukin-10, abbreviated as CD206, Arg-1, IL-10). Immunofluorescence results showed that M2-type macrophages clustered around the scaffold. Moreover, the scaffold could induce hyperpolarization of mitochondrial membrane potential in bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells, bolster cellular energy supply, promote osteogenic differentiation, scavenge intracellular ROS, and accelerate angiogenesis through the synergistic effect of biomineralization ions and AKG. In cranial bone defect models, the scaffold group exhibited the strongest new bone formation ability. At 8 weeks, the new bone tissue completely occupied the defect area, and the mineralization degree of the bone matrix was robust. This electrospun scaffold realized an "immune, energy, vascularization, osteogenesis" integrated bone regeneration strategy, providing a one-stop repair solution for bone defects (Fig. 11B) [139].

AKG-empowered biomaterials can not only modulate the phenotype of innate immune cells but also program adaptive immune cells. For instance, Mangal, J. L [140]. constructed PAKG MPs to achieve the sustained delivery of AKG. In vitro experiments demonstrated that after dendritic cells internalized the microspheres, their metabolic profiles changed. Among the 299 detectable metabolites, 113 showed significant changes, mainly modifying metabolic pathways such as glutathione, glutamate, and arginine; at the same time, intracellular glycolysis and mitochondrial respiration were inhibited, thereby down-regulating the expression of major histocompatibility complex class II (MHC-II) and cluster of differentiation 86 (CD86), manifesting an immunosuppressive phenotype. Dendritic cells, as the key antigen-presenting cells connecting innate immunity and adaptive immunity, in the mixed lymphocyte reaction, dendritic cells treated with microspheres could inhibit the proliferation of pro-inflammatory T cells such as T helper 1 cells (Th1 cells) and Th17 cells, while having a weaker inhibitory effect on Tregs, overall shifting the immune state towards tolerance or inhibition. This delivery system reprogrammed the metabolism of dendritic cells, induced their immunosuppressive phenotype, and indirectly governed adaptive immunity, thereby avoiding excessive activation of inflammation (Fig. 11C) [140].

Mangal, J. L [141]. also explored new treatments for rheumatoid arthritis (RA) using PAKG nanoparticles (PAKG NPs). In vitro experiments demonstrated that the combination of nanoparticles with methotrexate (MTX) could significantly reduce the activation ratio of pro-inflammatory T cells (Th1 cells, Th17 cells), elevate the proportion of anti-inflammatory T helper 2 cells (Th2 cells), and maintain the frequency of Tregs. In mouse collagen-induced arthritis (CIA) models, the arthritis score (front paw and hind paw) of the group treated with PAKG NPs and MTX in combination was the lowest, joint swelling and redness were significantly diminished, weight loss triggered by systemic inflammation was reversed, and this group was the only one whose weight was higher than the initial value at the end of the experiment. HE staining showed that the joint inflammation infiltration in the combined treatment group was alleviated. Further research disclosed that when the spleen cells of this group of mice were exposed again to the self-antigen of CIA, the antigen-specific pro-inflammatory Th17 cells response was significantly weakened, while the Tregs response was enhanced. This scheme focused on regulating antigen-specific T cell immunity, combining the potent effect of MTX with the persistent and mild effect of PAKG NPs, and arresting the progression of RA from the root (Fig. 11D) [141].

Both studies by Mao, C [97]. and Liu, H [139]. explored how to regulate macrophage phenotypes via AKG to promote the repair of damaged tissues. However, the PFC/PCK in the latter study is superior in release cycle, biological activity, and physical function. Thermosensitive gel requires administration once every two days, while PFC/PCK can sustain the release of AKG for more than 28 days. The former only acts as a drug carrier with no supporting effect, and the latter possesses both drug delivery and tissue engineering scaffold functions. Compared with single-component delivery, the biological design of PFC/PCK is more comprehensive and three-dimensional. Even so, in terms of operational convenience, thermosensitive gel can be directly perfused into the periodontal pocket through a syringe, whereas PFC/PCK needs to be implanted into bone defects via surgery. As for the two studies of Mangal, J. L. [140,141], they present a progressive evolutionary relationship from basic mechanism exploration to therapeutic transformation of specific diseases. One is based on in vitro experiments, focusing on exploring the underlying regulatory mechanism of PAKG MPs on the metabolic profile and functions of immune cells, and the other applies AKG polymer technology to mouse models of RA, placing more focus on therapeutic application. The former has laid a scientific foundation and proved that it is feasible to intervene in the metabolism and functions of immune cells through material engineering approaches, and the latter is an important step towards clinical transformation, demonstrating that this metabolic regulation strategy, when combined with existing drugs, has the potential to treat severe autoimmune diseases.

In summary, AKG-empowered biomaterials can utilize their immunomodulatory properties to program innate and adaptive immune cells, inducing them to manifest immunosuppressive or pro-healing phenotypes, thereby suppressing excessive immune responses and demonstrating potential in the treatment of IMIDs.

Supporting tumor treatment through programming the fate of tumor cells

To date, chemotherapy, targeted therapy, and immunotherapy remain the predominant therapeutic modalities for malignant tumors [142,143]. Since the anti-tumor efficacy of AKG was unveiled, studies have preliminarily explored its combination with conventional anti-tumor drugs as an auxiliary strategy for cancer treatment.

In the treatment of lung cancer, Kemchandani, R et al. [144] employed liquid-assisted grinding method, solvent evaporation method, and quench Cooling method to prepare three coamorphous systems (CAM) of lumefantrine (LMF) and AKG, intended to improve the pharmacokinetic properties of LMF. The experiments demonstrated that compared with LMF crystals, the three CAM systems achieved several-fold improvements in solubility, dissolution efficiency, and peak blood drug concentration, and still maintained robust stability under extreme storage conditions. The MTT experiment based on the A549 lung cancer cell line indicated that two of the three CAMs exhibited a lower half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) than LMF crystals; among them, the liquid-assisted grinding type CAM (CAM LAG) treatment group manifested the most pronounced apoptotic morphological characteristics (such as DNA breakage, chromatin condensation), and the intracellular ROS level was also the highest, signifying the strongest anti-tumor activity. It is worth noting that the study disclosed that both AKG and LMF possessed strong affinity for the lung cancer treatment target epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR); at the same time, AKG could also enhance the body's anti-tumor immune response by priming CD8+ T cells. In this coamorphous system, AKG not only amplified the activity of LMF but also exerted its own anti-tumor effect, and the two functioned synergistically through the multi-mechanism pathway of "EGFR targeting, ROS generation, induction of apoptosis" to combat lung cancer (Fig. 12A) [144].

In Hossain, S. M.'s [146] breast cancer research, AKG was mainly employed for functionalizing the carrier to enhance the therapeutic effect. The AKG-modified carbonate apatite nanoparticles (α-KAMCA) could preserve a small and uniform particle size and a large folded surface area, inhibiting particle size growth. This carrier significantly improved the killing effect of AZ628 on cancer cells: compared with the unmodified drug-loaded calcium phosphate nanoparticles, the AKG-modified carrier increased the uptake efficiency of breast cancer cells for the drug by approximately 21%; in a 48-h treatment, the cytotoxicity of the drug-loaded α-KAMCA was 30% greater than that of the free drug, and also superior to the unmodified group. Western blot analysis demonstrated that this delivery system could down-regulate the activation and expression of mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) pathway, phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI-3 kinase), and protein kinase B (also known as Akt), and facilitate the activation of cysteine-aspartic protease 3 (Caspase-3), thereby inhibiting the proliferation of breast cancer cells and inducing their apoptosis. This study enhanced the delivery efficiency of AZ628 by introducing AKG, but whether AKG in the carrier directly participates in killing breast cancer cells still needs further exploration [146].

One year later, Hossain, S. M [147]. further functionalized the carbonate apatite nanoparticles using AKG to potentiate the efficacy of conventional chemotherapy drugs, cyclophosphamide (CTX) and DOX, and optimize their pharmacokinetics. In mouse breast cancer models, the terminal tumor volume of CTX-loaded α-KAMCA group was merely 1/5 of the free CTX group and 1/17 of the untreated group. The therapeutic effect surpassed that of the unmodified group and the citrate-modified group. A tumor shrinkage trend was observed as early as 3 days after the first intervention. In terms of pharmacokinetics, thanks to the characteristics of nanoparticles that could evade phagocytosis by phagocytes and have an appropriate pore size for tumor blood vessels, the α-KAMCA carrying DOX had a longer circulation half-life and higher accumulation in tumor tissues. Their distribution in the spleen and kidneys was reduced, and at the same time, due to their particle size being larger than the intercellular connection pore size of cardiac capillary cells, the accumulation of the cardiotoxic drug DOX in the heart was significantly reduced. Moreover, this nanoparticle was also detected in brain tissue, providing a potential possibility for the treatment of breast cancer brain metastasis. The drug-loaded α-KAMCA prioritized "efficient delivery, tumor targeting, and low toxicity and safety", providing a viable strategy for enhancing efficacy and reducing toxicity in breast cancer treatment [147,148].

In the treatment of liver cancer, AKG also demonstrated its application value. To augment the sensitivity of liver cancer to radiotherapy, Sood, A [145]. constructed a type of iron oxide-gold core-shell nanoparticle (GNP) that could encapsulate chemotherapy drugs and was functionalized by AKG. AKG played a " navigational " role here, directing the GNP into cancer cells and preferentially localizing in mitochondria after entering the cells, targeting functionally active mitochondria that regulated cancer cell growth and invasion. Additionally, the GNP could carry the chemotherapy drug ​4-N-hydroxyphenyl retinamide (4-HPR), assisting in killing liver cancer cells and serving as a bimodal contrast agent for magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and CT. Experimental results showed that under the same dose of γ-ray irradiation, the ROS levels, nuclear and DNA damage degrees of cancer cells in GNP-treated group exceeded those in control groups, and the survival rate of cancer cells was markedly attenuated. The mitochondrial-targeted probe validated that the GNP was located within the mitochondria, which might be the key to its ability to kill tumor cells. Therefore, this nanoparticle consolidated the "targeted delivery, radiotherapy sensitization, chemotherapy assistance, imaging tracing" four functions and constituted a potential tumor diagnosis and treatment integrated platform (Fig. 12B) [145].

In the 4 studies cited in this section, only AKG in the CAM designed by Khemchandani, R [144]. was more than a co-former, and it itself directly exerted anti-lung cancer effects through multiple pathways. AKG did not produce direct anti-tumor effects in the other three studies. In two studies of Hossain, S. M. [146,147], AKG was used as a modifier to modify traditional carriers. The most essential function of AKG in GNP is serving as a targeting ligand. The reasons why AKG failed to exert its inherent anti-tumor effects are speculated as follows. First, in terms of the original intention of experimental design, AKG was initially defined as an excipient or structural unit without therapeutic effects in the latter three studies, and the dose required for it to perform such functions may be much lower than the threshold for exerting therapeutic effects. In particular, AKG also acted as a reducing agent for gold during the synthesis process of GNP, so it would be oxidized. This means that a considerable part of the substances ultimately modified on the carrier surface might have been converted into derivatives or residual groups of AKG, rather than AKG in its complete form. Therefore, the specific role played by AKG in the cases of this section should be viewed objectively.

In conclusion, AKG-empowered biomaterials exhibit profound prospects for interdisciplinary applications through their ability to program the fate of diverse cells (including mesenchymal stem cells, chondrocytes, epithelial cells, nerve cells, vascular cells, immune cells, and tumor cells) at the transcriptional, metabolic, and epigenetic levels (Fig. 13, Table 2). With the ongoing development of material engineering strategies, such materials possess certain research potential and exploration value in the fields of hard and soft tissue defect regeneration, regulation of immune-related diseases, and tumor therapy.

The AKG-empowered biomaterials program the fate of different cells: promoting differentiation of mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) into osteoblasts, inhibiting senescence of chondrocytes, promoting proliferation of epithelial cells, promoting proliferation of neuronal cells, promoting proliferation of endothelial cells (ECs), inhibiting proliferation of vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs), driving phenotypic transformation of macrophages, driving expression of immunosuppressive phenotypes in dendritic cells (DCs), inhibiting excessive activation of T cells, and killing tumor cells.

Summary and outlook

Summary of paradigm shift

This review systematically delineates a fundamental transition that is quietly emerging in the design paradigm of biomaterials: redefining the key metabolite of the TCA cycle, AKG, from its role as a "therapeutic molecule" with inherent pharmacokinetic defects, to a "smart building block" assembling the sophisticated biomaterial itself. The essence of this transition lies in leveraging the chemical functional groups of AKG and integrating it into the material framework through covalent bonds, achieving a leap from "passive transportation" to "active construction". The logical route of this review clearly outlines the process of AKG's identity transformation. Firstly, based on the severe pharmacokinetic defects of AKG monomer, this review demonstrates the inevitability of developing advanced biomaterials to overcome the bottleneck of clinical translation. Secondly, through in-depth analysis of the multi-dimensional biological effects of AKG, these effects are established as the internal blueprint for guiding material design. Subsequently, three progressive technological evolutions from "cargo" to "adjuvant" and finally to "framework" are elaborated, which are proposed to maximize the therapeutic efficacy and structural potential of AKG. Finally, application examples in multiple fields verify that material engineering approaches can release the potential of AKG, laying a solid foundation for the establishment of the new paradigm of "metabolism-guided materialized cell fate programming" (Fig. 14).

Current core challenges and limitations

Despite the encouraging prospects, this field still encounters diverse hurdles on its way to clinical application:

Firstly, from the perspective of biological safety, AKG is the most concerned component in AKG-empowered biomaterials, and its safe exposure dose has been evaluated in previous studies. In acute toxicity studies, the median lethal dose of orally administered AKG in rats was greater than 5.0 g/kg, which is much higher than the conventional dosage [149]. In terms of subacute toxicity, the no-observed-adverse-effect level (NOAEL) of oral AKG administration in rats was 1.0 g/kg [150]. However, there is still a lack of chronic toxicity studies on long-term repeated administration in the human body. Determining the safe dose window of AKG in the human body is a crucial step for AKG-empowered biomaterials to move toward clinical application. In addition, as most current studies focus on the verification of short-term therapeutic efficacy, the long-term in vivo fate and potential chronic toxicity of other components in the materials have not been fully evaluated. For example, long-term excessive exposure to manganese ions in Mn-MIL-100@AKG can cause nervous system poisoning [151], and lanthanide elements in LMEK also have multi-system toxicity to the nervous, respiratory, and reproductive systems [[152], [153], [154]]. Whether the accumulation of material degradation products will produce unknown impacts on distal organs or systemic metabolism is a key question that must be answered in preclinical studies. Furthermore, most current studies focus on the therapeutic effect of materials on lesion sites, but ignore the multifaceted interference of materials on normal tissues. Excessive AKG entering the tricarboxylic acid cycle of normal cells has the risk of causing abnormal accumulation of SUC, a downstream metabolic intermediate, as its content exceeds the metabolic capacity of tissues [155]. The latter is considered a pro-inflammatory factor, leading to inflammation-like metabolic disorders in normal tissues under non-infectious conditions [156]. Moreover, the competitive inhibition of 2-OGDDs by excessive succinate may cause drastic fluctuations in epigenetic regulation and result in unintended gene silencing in normal tissue cells [80]. In terms of immune regulation, if AKG-empowered biomaterials continuously drive the macrophage population that maintains M1/M2 polarization balance in normal tissues to polarize toward the M2 phenotype in a targeted manner, it may not only weaken the body's early ability to recognize pathogens, but also potentially cause the occurrence of fibrotic lesions due to the excessive secretion of transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β) under extreme circumstances [157,158]. Most importantly, the vast majority of research cases cited in this review only verify the safety and efficacy of experimental doses, without conducting systematic investigation of dose-dependent efficacy and toxicity gradients, nor delineating a complete safe dose window, which undoubtedly becomes an obstacle for these biomaterials, especially implantable materials, to advance into clinical application.

Current material systems still have deficiencies in application adaptation performance, which poses challenges to their clinical translation. First of all, with regard to administration routes, the puncture and injection method adopted by Mn-MIL-100@AKG and CpaKG has risks of hemorrhage, infection, headaches, and nerve damage [[159], [160], [161], [162]]. The intravenous injection administration routes used by AKG@Cy5.5@ALN-Liposome and LMEK may cause phlebitis, thrombosis, or air embolism [[163], [164], [165]]. PFC/PCK need to be implanted into the lesion site through surgical procedures, which has the limitation of excessive trauma [120,139]. It is a major challenge to develop more minimally invasive administration routes with higher patient compliance. The next aspect is the matching of controlled release and treatment duration. Most existing systems provide drug release for several weeks, but for many chronic diseases (such as osteoporosis and OA), the treatment duration may require several months or even longer [166,167]. Developing intelligent materials that can achieve ultra-long-lasting or even on-demand pulsed release is an important direction for the future. Another aspect is the balance between mechanical properties and biological activity. In the field of bone regeneration, introducing porous structures or active components to achieve excellent biological activity often results in a sacrifice of the material's mechanical strength [101,120]. How to design a composite material that can provide initial mechanical support and has a degradation rate that matches the growth rate of new bone is a significant challenge in the repair of weight-bearing bone defects. Finally, there is the issue of disease heterogeneity and personalized treatment. Currently, the material design is mostly in a "one-size-fits-all" mode. However, there are significant differences among patients in terms of age, metabolic status, and disease stage (such as tumor microenvironment, degree of osteoporosis). The future materials need to have adjustable parameters to meet the specific needs of individuals, this helps further promote the clinical translation of AKG-empowered biomaterials.

The unclear functional positioning of AKG in biomaterials has also brought great difficulties to relevant regulatory approval, especially for implantable materials. It is necessary to clarify whether AKG functions as an excipient, an active component, or a structural unit. When AKG is defined as an excipient that is mainly used to stabilize materials, improve processing performance, and does not have an independent therapeutic effect, or defined as a structural unit that contributes to the degradation performance or mechanical properties of materials, the regulatory threshold is relatively low, and the evaluation should focus on its biocompatibility. If AKG is defined as an active component that can produce a clear therapeutic effect, a large amount of pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic data must be provided, which usually means extremely expensive preclinical experiments [168,169]. Of course, AKG may also play multiple roles, which makes regulatory evaluation work more complex and challenging. Improper classification may lead to wrong selection of regulatory pathways and inadequate safety assessment. Therefore, clarifying the functional attribute of AKG is crucial for correctly evaluating its clinical translation pathway and carrying out proper regulatory classification. For example, whether AKG in GNP can directly kill tumor cells needs to be further clarified [145]. Moreover, for biomaterials as a whole, regulatory authorities are also very concerned about whether these products are classified as drugs, medical devices, or combination products, and different classifications correspond to different regulatory pathways [169]. Current researches have not conducted sufficient discussion on this issue. For instance, AKG-loaded small-diameter artificial vascular grafts have not been clearly classified by researchers as medical devices or combination products, and relevant discussions need to be further supplemented and improved [132].

Ignoring the context dependency of AKG effects may also become an obstacle for AKG-empowered biomaterials to move towards clinical application. Researchers often only pay attention to the benefits of AKG, without recognizing that such benefits can only be exerted under certain conditions. When conditions change, the beneficial effects of AKG may disappear or even lead to outcomes completely contrary to expectations. For example, although AKG can induce M2 polarization of macrophages, it can also inhibit Treg differentiation [[67], [68], [69], [70]]. Therefore, for diseases with Treg differentiation defect as the main pathological mechanism, such as type 1 diabetes and graft-versus-host disease [170,171], the application of AKG may aggravate disease progression. Hence, the same AKG can produce entirely opposite therapeutic effects under different pathological backgrounds. Dose regulation is equally critical. As a weak acid with two carboxyl groups, excessively high local concentration of AKG will disrupt acid-base homeostasis and further induce acidosis. Acidosis can subsequently trigger secondary hyperkalemia and cause multi-organ toxicity to the heart, kidneys, brain, and other organs [172]. In terms of disease staging, AKG can suppress the overactivated inflammatory response in the host by regulating macrophage polarization in the early stage of inflammation. Nevertheless, when inflammation progresses to the chronic stage and repair phase, excessive secretion of TGF-β resulting from over-differentiation of M2 macrophages may cause excessive deposition of fibrous tissue [158]. In addition, AKG can exert completely opposite effects in different cell types. Although this review mainly highlights the anti-cancer effect of AKG, it can also promote the proliferation of tumor cells, including glioma cells and prostate cancer cells [173,174]. Finally, the restrictive effect of the microenvironment can limit the functional performance of AKG. As mentioned in section 2.1, oxygen molecules are indispensable for the function of 2-OGDDs [3]. Thus, in extremely hypoxic microenvironments such as the tumor core and deep wounds [175], the epigenetic regulatory effects of AKG can hardly be exerted even with sufficient AKG supply. In conclusion, prior to the application of AKG-empowered biomaterials, advanced diagnostic techniques should be adopted to accurately determine the type and stage of diseases, which is exactly neglected in most current studies. The optimization of release control is also important to avoid rapid local accumulation of AKG.

Finally, further optimization remains to be made in terms of key processes and industrialization feasibility. Some studies have not directly verified formulation reproducibility. For instance, Jaggarapu, Mmcs [131] only described the single-batch preparation process of PAKG MPs, without arranging multi-batch repeated preparation, nor fully evaluating the reproducibility of particle size, drug loading, and release behavior between and within batches, which hinders the clinical application and industrialization advancement of the materials [131]. As for the material preparation process, improvements are also needed to realize industrial scale-up. For example, the dialysis method adopted for the purification of AKG@Cy5.5@ALN-Liposome has the disadvantages of long time consumption and large water consumption [121], and tangential flow filtration (TFF) should be adopted in industrial production to greatly improve production efficiency [176]. Meanwhile, the use of chloroform as a solvent is harmful to the environment and human body [177], and solvents with lower toxicity should be prioritized for industrial improvement. The improvement of sterilization processes can also facilitate the translation of biomaterials from laboratory research to clinical application. For example, Fn-TA-PFC/PCK adopted the form of aseptic processing and assembly, that is, grafting each component onto the sterile scaffold in a sterile environment [120], while ideal clinical products should undergo terminal sterilization after final forming [178]. Therefore, ethylene oxide sterilization can be applied to the final finished products to minimize the risk of contamination while ensuring that the components in biomaterials are not damaged [179]. As for storage stability, it is particularly crucial for gel products. The current main preservation method is low-temperature storage, which faces risks such as polymer degradation and microbial contamination [97]. However, if preserved in the form of lyophilized powder for reconstitution, namely converting the gel into solid powder via lyophilization technology and reconstituting it with normal saline before use, the storage stability will be greatly enhanced [180].

Future development directions and frontier prospects

(1)Developing multi-responsive intelligent systems: Beyond the single pH response, future materials should integrate the ability to respond to multiple specific signals in the disease microenvironment, such as temperature, specific enzymes, and magnetic fields [181]. For example, designing an AKG delivery system that simultaneously responds to the secretion of Cathepsin K enzyme by osteoclasts [182] and the high ROS levels in the inflammatory area can achieve more precise and spatially controlled release.

In addition, strengthening clinical translational research is also indispensable. It is urgent to conduct long-term (such as 6-12 months) safety and efficacy evaluations in large animal models (such as sheep and pigs) in accordance with good laboratory practice (GLP) standards. At the same time, exploring non-invasive or minimally invasive administration methods (such as injectable hydrogels, micro-needle patches) will greatly enhance patient compliance and the feasibility of clinical application.

In summary, AKG-empowered biomaterials represent a model of the integration of metabolic intervention and advanced material engineering. Through continuous interdisciplinary innovation, addressing current challenges and exploring in the aforementioned direction, these intelligent materials are expected to move from the laboratory to the clinic within the next decade, ultimately ushering in a new era of "materialized cell fate programming guided by metabolism", bringing new hope to countless patients with tissue defects and metabolic diseases.
