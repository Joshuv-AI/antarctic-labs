Introduction

Let
A_{G}
be the adjacency matrix of a simple graph
G
. Let
\lambda_{1}(G),\lambda_{2}(G),\ldots,\lambda_{n}(G)
be the eigenvalues of
A_{G}
such that
\lambda_{1}(G)\geq\lambda_{2}(G)\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}(G)
.
For an integer
p\geq 2
, the
positive
p
-energy
and
negative
p
-energy
of a graph
G
, denoted
\mathcal{E}^{+}_{p}(G)
and
\mathcal{E}^{+}_{p}(G)
, are defined as follows:

\mathcal{E}^{+}_{p}(G)=\sum_{\lambda_{i}(G)>0}|\lambda_{i}(G)|^{p},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \mathcal{E}^{-}_{p}(G)=\sum_{\lambda_{i}(G)<0}|\lambda_{i}(G)|^{p}.

Positive and negative
p
-energies are useful parameters related to other graph characteristics, such as chromatic number and fractional chromatic number; We refer interested readers to a recent work
9
.

Conjecture 1.1

(
7
)
.

For every connected
n
-vertex graph
G
,

\min\{\mathcal{E}_{2}^{+}({G}),\penalty\ \mathcal{E}_{2}^{-}({G})\}\geq n-1.

1.1
has been verified for many classes of graphs: bipartite graphs, regular graphs, complete
q
-partite graphs, hyper-energetic graphs, and barbell graphs in
7
, and extended barbell graphs, unicyclic graphs, graphs with two positive eigenvalues, graphs with a certain fraction of positive or negative eigenvalues, cactus graphs in
1
, and
d
-regular disconnected graphs with no clique component in
8
. S. Zhang
12
proved that

\min\{\mathcal{E}^{+}_{2}(G),\,\mathcal{E}^{-}_{2}(G)\}\geq n-\gamma,
where
\gamma
is the domination number of
G
. In the same paper, Zhang also showed that
\mathcal{E}^{+}_{2}(G)\geq m^{6/7-o(1)}
and
\mathcal{E}^{-}_{2}(G)=\Omega(m^{1/2})
where
m=|E(G)|
with exponents in both bounds being optimal. The best-known bound towards
1.1
was obtained by S. Akbari, H. Kumar, and B. Mohar
2
in 2025, who utilized a super-additivity property of square energies to prove that

\min\{\mathcal{E}^{+}_{2}(G),\,\mathcal{E}^{-}_{2}(G)\}\geq\frac{3n}{4}.

Nevertheless,
1.1
remains open and continues to stimulate active research interest.

Let
P_{n}
denote the path on
n
vertices,
K_{n}
denote the complete graph on
n
vertices, and
K_{1,n}
denote a star graph with
n
vertices of degree
1
.
Note that for each integer
p\geq 2
,
\mathcal{E}_{p}^{-}(K_{n})=n-1
and
\mathcal{E}_{p}^{+}(P_{n})=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}2^{p}\cos^{p}\!\Big(\frac{k\pi}{n+1}\Big)
; In particular,
\mathcal{E}_{2}^{+}(P_{n})=n-1
.
Following the same line of study, the next two conjectures can be seen as natural generalizations of
1.1
.

Conjecture 1.2

(
11
)
.

Let
n
and
p
be two positive integers with
p\geq 2
. For every connected
n
-vertex graph
G
,

\mathcal{E}^{+}_{p}({G})\geq\mathcal{E}^{+}_{p}({P_{n})}.

Conjecture 1.3

(
3
)
.

Let
n
and
p
be two positive integers with
p\geq 2
. For every connected
n
-vertex graph
G
,

\mathcal{E}^{-}_{p}({G})\geq\mathcal{E}^{-}_{p}({K_{n})}.

For connected graphs
G
and all
p\geq 2
, S. Akbari, H. Kumar, B. Mohar, and S. Pragada
3
obtained upper bounds on the positive and negative
p
-energies:
\mathcal{E}_{p}^{+}(G)\leq(n-1)^{p}
and
\mathcal{E}_{p}^{-}(G)\leq(n/2)^{p}
, showing that neither part can grow faster than a fixed polynomial in
n
. In a complementary direction, C. Elphick, Q. Tang, and S. Zhang
9
conjectured that for every connected
n
-vertex graphs
G
and every
p
with
0\leq p\leq 2
,

\min\{\mathcal{E}_{p}^{+}(G),\,\mathcal{E}_{p}^{-}(G)\}\geq(n-1)^{p/2},

and they verified this bound for
p
with
0\leq p\leq 1
. Another conjecture concerning
\mathcal{E}_{p}(G)=\mathcal{E}_{p}^{+}(G)+\mathcal{E}_{p}^{-}(G)
proposed by V. Nikiforov

is that, for every tree
T
,
\mathcal{E}_{p}(K_{1,{n-1}})\leq\mathcal{E}_{p}(T)\leq\mathcal{E}_{p}(P_{n})
holds for
1\leq p\leq 2
, while
\mathcal{E}_{p}(P_{n})\leq\mathcal{E}_{p}(T)\leq\mathcal{E}_{p}(K_{1,n-1})
holds for

; for some related results see
4
;
5
.

In a very recent work
3
by S. Akbari, H. Kumar, B. Mohar, and S. Pragada,

has been verified for

.

Theorem 1.4

(
3
)
.

Let
n
and
p
be two positive integers with
p\geq 4
. For every connected
n
-vertex graph
G
,
\mathcal{E}^{-}_{p}({G})\geq\mathcal{E}^{-}_{p}({K_{n})}.

In this paper, we take a step towards resolving
1.3
by showing that it holds for

.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
n
be a positive integer. If
G
is a connected
n
-vertex graph, not isomorphic to
K_{n}
or
P_{3}
, then
\mathcal{E}_{3}^{-}(G)\geq n.

Note that
\mathcal{E}_{3}^{-}(P_{3})=2\sqrt{2}>2=\mathcal{E}_{3}^{-}(K_{3})
, and recall that
\mathcal{E}_{p}^{-}(K_{n})=n-1
for every integer
p\geq 2
. Our result thus implies that
1.3
is true for
p=3
.

We further remark that proving
1.3
for a smaller value of
p
is more challenging than for a larger one. Note that
1.2
does not exhibit this phenomenon. For any real number
r\geq 2
, let
\mathcal{E}^{-}_{r}(G)=\sum\limits_{\lambda_{i}(G)<0}|\lambda_{i}(G)|^{r}
.

Lemma 1.6
.

Let
r
and
r^{\prime}
be two real numbers such that
2\leq r\leq r^{\prime}
. Assume that
G
is a connected graph.
If
\mathcal{E}_{r}^{-}(G)\geq\mathcal{E}_{r}^{-}(K_{n})
, then
\mathcal{E}_{r^{\prime}}^{-}(G)\geq\mathcal{E}_{r^{\prime}}^{-}(K_{n})
.

Let
\theta
denote the number of negative eigenvalues of
G
. Since
\lambda_{1}>0
, we have
\theta\leq n-1
. By Hölder’s inequality, for
r\leq r^{\prime}
, we have the following inequality for
\mathcal{E}_{r}^{-}(G)
and
\mathcal{E}_{r^{\prime}}^{-}(G)
:

(\mathcal{E}_{r^{\prime}}^{-}(G))^{\frac{r}{r^{\prime}}}\,\theta^{\frac{r^{\prime}-r}{r^{\prime}}}\;\geq\;\mathcal{E}_{r}^{-}(G)\;\geq\;\mathcal{E}_{r}^{-}(K_{n})=n-1.

Therefore, we obtain
\mathcal{E}_{r^{\prime}}^{-}(G)\geq(\frac{(n-1)^{r^{\prime}}}{\theta^{r^{\prime}-r}})^{\frac{1}{r}}.
Since
\theta\leq n-1
, it follows that
\mathcal{E}_{r^{\prime}}^{-}(G)\geq n-1.
Thus, we have shown that
\mathcal{E}_{r^{\prime}}^{-}(G)\geq\mathcal{E}_{r^{\prime}}^{-}(K_{n})
, completing the proof of the lemma.

In particular,
Lemma

1.6
holds for
r=p
where
p
is a positive integer. Combining all discussion above, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
n
and
p
be two positive integers with
p\geq 3
. For every connected
n
-vertex graph
G
,
\mathcal{E}^{-}_{p}({G})\geq\mathcal{E}^{-}_{p}({K_{n})}.

Also we note that the
p=2
case of
1.3
is the most changeling one. Together with
Lemma

1.6
, our result of

1.5
suggests a natural direction for future work: investigating the real-valued graph energy, in particular, the
r
-energy of

for rational

.

Compared to the previous work

, which established the result for

, our main effort is devoted to handling a somewhat “bad” graph: the path

. This is due to the fact that the inequality

’s. In contrast, the case

Lemma 2.1

Let

\mathcal{E}_{3}^{+}(G)\geq\mathcal{E}_{3}^{+}(P_{n})

Theorem

1.8
.

Let
n
be a positive integer. If
G
is a connected
n
-vertex graph, not isomorphic to
K_{1},K_{2}
, and
P_{3}
, then
\mathcal{E}_{3}^{+}(G)\geq\frac{\sqrt{5}}{2}n
.

Proof.

Let
n
be a positive integer. Let
G
be a connected
n
-vertex graph, and we proceed by induction on

. The assertion has been verified using a computer for all graphs up to

vertices as shown in

. From now on, we assume that

.

Let

be the maximum degree of
G
and
let

be a vertex with

. Assume that
T
is a spanning tree of

w
. If there exists an edge of

incident to the vertex
w
, say
wu
, such that each of the two connected components

and
T_{b}
of

has at least
4
vertices, then neither
G[V(T_{a})]
nor

is isomorphic to any of
\{K_{1},K_{2},P_{3}\}
. By
Theorem

1.9
and the induction hypothesis,
\mathcal{E}_{3}^{+}(G)\geq\mathcal{E}_{3}^{+}(G[V(T_{1})])+\mathcal{E}_{3}^{+}(G[V(T_{2})])\geq\frac{\sqrt{5}}{2}n
. Therefore, for each edge
wu
of

incident to

T-wu
has at most
3
vertices.

We next consider the subgraph
G-w
. Let
T_{1},\ldots,T_{s}
denote the connected trees in
T-w
.
We first claim that
G-w
contains no subgraph isomorphic to
P_{4}
. Assume, to the contrary, that such a
4
-path
Q
exists, and we choose one that intersects the minimum number of
T_{i}
’s. If there are

such that
V(Q)\subset V(T_{i})\cup V(T_{j})
, then as

,
|T_{i}|+|T_{j}|\leq\max\{3+1,3+2,3+3,2+2\}=6
; Otherwise, there are either

with
|T_{i}|=2,|T_{j}|=1,
and
|T_{k}|=1
, or
V(Q)\subset V(T_{i})\cup V(T_{j})\cup V(T_{k})\cup V(T_{\ell})
with
|T_{i}|=|T_{j}|=|T_{k}|=|T_{\ell}|=1
, in both cases,
\sum|T_{d}|\leq\max\{2+1+1,1+1+1+1\}=4
. Let
T^{*}
denote the union of trees

’s which intersect the chosen

. Since

and by the analysis above, we know that
4\leq|T^{*}|\leq 6
. Furthermore,
|T-T^{*}|\geq 11-6\geq 5
. By

1.9
and the induction hypothesis,

\mathcal{E}^{+}_{3}(G)\geq\mathcal{E}^{+}_{3}(G[V(T^{*})])+\mathcal{E}^{+}_{3}(G[V(T-T^{*})])\geq|V(T^{*})|+|V(G)-V(T^{*})|=\frac{\sqrt{5}}{2}n.

Therefore,
G-w
is a disjoint
union of
K_{1},K_{2},P_{3},
and
K_{3}
. Let
n_{i}
denote the number of
K_{i}
’s in
G-w
for
i\in[3]
, and let
n_{3^{-}}
denote the number of
P_{3}
’s in
G-w
. Let

Note that
s\leq d_{G}(w)\leq n-1
and
n=n_{1}+2n_{2}+3n_{3}+3n_{3^{-}}+1
. Note that as
n\geq 11
, we have that
s\geq 4
.
Since
G
contains
K_{1,s}
as a subgraph,
\lambda_{1}(G)\geq\lambda_{1}(K_{1,s})=\sqrt{s}
. We consider two possibilities based on the value of

.

Case 1
.
Assume that
n_{3}=0
.

In this case, since
G-w
contains no
K_{3}
,
\lambda_{1}(G-w)\leq\max\{\lambda_{1}(K_{1}),\lambda_{1}(K_{2}),\lambda_{1}(P_{3})\}=\sqrt{2}
.
By
Theorem

1.10
, we have that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{+}_{3}(G)

\displaystyle\geq\mathcal{E}^{+}_{3}(G-w)+\lambda_{1}(G)^{3}-\lambda_{1}(G-w)^{3}

\displaystyle=\big(n_{2}\mathcal{E}^{+}_{3}(K_{2})+n_{3^{-}}\mathcal{E}^{+}_{3}(P_{3}))+\lambda_{1}(G)^{3}-\lambda_{1}(G-w)^{3}

\displaystyle\geq n_{2}+2\sqrt{2}n_{3^{-}}+s\sqrt{s}-2\sqrt{2}.

Let
f_{s}(n_{1},n_{2},n_{3^{-}}):=n_{2}+2\sqrt{2}n_{3^{-}}+s\sqrt{s}-2\sqrt{2}-\frac{\sqrt{5}}{2}n
. It suffices to show that
f_{s}(n_{1},n_{2},n_{3^{-}})\geq 0
.
Substituting
n_{1}=s-n_{2}-n_{3^{-}}
into
n
gives

n=s+n_{2}+2n_{3^{-}}+1.
Thus

f_{s}(n_{1},n_{2},n_{3^{-}})=(\sqrt{s}-\frac{\sqrt{5}}{2})s+(1-\frac{\sqrt{5}}{2})n_{2}+(2\sqrt{2}-\sqrt{5})n_{3^{-}}-2\sqrt{2}-\frac{\sqrt{5}}{2}.

Note that
1-\frac{\sqrt{5}}{2}<0,
and
2\sqrt{2}-\sqrt{5}>0.
Hence, for a fixed

, a decrease in
n_{3^{-}}
leads to a corresponding increase in
n_{1}
or
n_{2}
, and consequently, a decrease in
f_{s}(n_{1},n_{2},n_{3^{-}})
; and also a decrease in
n_{1}
leads to a corresponding increase in
n_{2}
, and consequently, again a decrease in
f_{s}(n_{1},n_{2},n_{3^{-}})
.
Under the constraints of

\min\{n_{1},n_{2},n_{3^{-}}\}\geq 0
and

, we have that

f_{s}(n_{1},n_{2},n_{3^{-}})\geq f_{s}(n_{1}+n_{3^{-}},n_{2},0)\geq f_{s}(0,n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{3^{-}},0)=f_{s}(0,s,0),

where

f_{s}(0,s,0)=s\bigl(\sqrt{s}+1-\sqrt{5}\bigr)-2\sqrt{2}-\frac{\sqrt{5}}{2}.

Note that
f_{s}(0,s,0)\geq 0
when
s\geq 5
. Recall that
n\geq 11
,
s\geq 4
, and
n=n_{1}+2n_{2}+3n_{3^{-}}+1
. It remains to consider the case when

. We only have the following four possibilities:
(n_{1},n_{2},n_{3^{-}})\in\{(0,0,4),\penalty\ (0,1,3),\penalty\ (0,2,2),\penalty\ (1,0,3)\}
. Note that

f_{4}(n_{1},n_{2},n_{3^{-}})=8-2\sqrt{2}-\frac{5\sqrt{5}}{2}+(1-\frac{\sqrt{5}}{2})n_{2}+(2\sqrt{2}-\sqrt{5})n_{3^{-}}.

By the monotonicity of

and some numerical computation, we obtain the following result:
f_{4}(0,1,3)\geq f_{4}(0,2,2)=10+2\sqrt{2}-\frac{11\sqrt{5}}{2}\approx 0.53,\penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ f_{4}(0,0,4)\geq f_{4}(1,0,3)=8+4\sqrt{2}-\frac{11\sqrt{5}}{2}\approx 1.35.
Therefore, when

\mathcal{E}^{+}_{3}(G)\geq\frac{\sqrt{5}}{2}n.

.
Assume that

.

In this case,
\lambda_{1}(G-w)\leq\max\{\lambda_{1}(K_{1}),\lambda_{1}(K_{2}),\lambda_{1}(P_{3}),\lambda_{1}(K_{3})\}=2

By

, we have that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{+}_{3}(G)

\displaystyle\geq\mathcal{E}^{+}_{3}(G-w)+\lambda_{1}(G)^{3}-\lambda_{1}(G-w)^{3}

\displaystyle=\big(n_{2}\mathcal{E}^{+}_{3}(K_{2})+n_{3^{-}}\mathcal{E}^{+}_{3}(P_{3})+n_{3}\mathcal{E}^{+}_{3}(K_{3})\big)+\lambda_{1}(G)^{3}-\lambda_{1}(G-w)^{3}

\displaystyle\geq n_{2}+2\sqrt{2}n_{3^{-}}+8n_{3}+s\sqrt{s}-8.

Recall that

. Let

Lemma 3.2

Assume that

A(G)=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1&1&\cdots&1\\
0&0&1&1&\cdots&1\\

1&1&1&0&\cdots&1\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
1&1&1&1&\cdots&0\end{pmatrix}\text{\penalty\ \penalty\ and\penalty\ \penalty\ }M_{\mathcal{P}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&n-2\\

Observe that for the adjacency matrix

(0,0,1,-1,0,\ldots,0),(0,0,1,0,-1,\ldots,0),\ldots,
and

with multiple

Lemma

3.3
.

Let
n
be a positive integer with
n\geq 4
. If
G
is a graph formed from
K_{n-1}
by attaching a pendant vertex to one of its vertices, then
\mathcal{E}_{3}^{-}(G)\geq n
.

Proof.

Assume that
n\geq 4
.
Let
V(G)=\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{n-1},u\},

where
G[\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-1}\}]
is isomorphic to
K_{n-1}
and
u
is a pendant vertex adjacent only to
v_{1}
.
We consider the vertex partition
\mathcal{P}=\{\{u\},\{v_{1}\},\{v_{2},\ldots,v_{n-1}\}\}.

Let
M_{\mathcal{P}}
denote the corresponding quotient matrix of
G/\mathcal{P}
. We have that

M_{\mathcal{P}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\
1&0&n-2\\
0&1&n-3\end{pmatrix}.

A direct computation yields

f_{n}(\lambda):=\det(\lambda I-M_{\mathcal{P}})=\lambda^{3}-(n-3)\lambda^{2}-(n-1)\lambda+(n-3).

This cubic polynomial has exactly one negative root
\lambda_{0}
.

Observe that the induced subgraph
G[\{v_{2},\ldots,v_{n-1}\}]
is isomorphic to
K_{n-2}
. Hence,
-1
is an eigenvalue of the adjacency matrix of
G
with multiplicity
n-3
.
Therefore, the negative eigenvalues of
G
are
-1
(with multiplicity
n-3
) and
\lambda
.
Consequently,

\mathcal{E}_{3}^{-}(G)=n-3+|\lambda|^{3}.

It suffices to show that
|\lambda_{0}|^{3}\geq 3
for
n\geq 4
.

On the one hand,
f_{n}(-\sqrt[3]{3})=(\sqrt[3]{3}-\sqrt[3]{9}+1)n+3\sqrt[3]{9}-\sqrt[3]{3}-6,
where
\sqrt[3]{3}-\sqrt[3]{9}+1\approx 0.362>0
. So
f_{n}(-\sqrt[3]{3})
increases as
n
increases. In particular, we note that
f_{4}(-\sqrt[3]{3})=3\sqrt[3]{3}-\sqrt[3]{9}-2\approx 0.246>0
. So
f_{n}(-\sqrt[3]{3})>0
for every
n\geq 4
.

On the other hand, for a fixed
n\geq 4
,
\lim_{\lambda\to-\infty}f_{n}(\lambda)=-\infty.
By continuity, the negative root
\lambda_{0}
of
f(\lambda)
satisfies

\lambda_{0}<-\sqrt[3]{3},

which implies
|\lambda_{0}|>\sqrt[3]{3}
. Therefore,
|\lambda_{0}|^{3}>3
. This completes the proof.
∎
