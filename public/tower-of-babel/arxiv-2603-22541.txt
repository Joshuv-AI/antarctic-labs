V\sim F_{V}

2.3
Stochastic majorization of the maximum of r.v.’s with given marginals: anti-thetic coupling and the Fréchet bound

Let

P(\max_{1\leq i\leq n}X_{i}>x)\leq\min(1,\sum_{i=1}^{n}F_{i}^{*}(x))=H^{*}(x)

(2)

Let
x_{0}=\inf\{x|\sum_{i=1}^{n}F_{i}^{*}(x)<1\}
. Then
\sum_{i=1}^{n}F_{i}^{*}(x_{0})=1
. The following class of joint distributions achieve equality in inequality (

from the conditional

2.5
Convex majorization of maxima of partial sums - The method of Meilijson & Nádas
[
7
]

This subsection generalizes the previous two, and places LPP-type convex majorization in a broader context.

X_{n}\sim F_{n}
, for
1\leq n\leq N
, are non-negative r.v. with some joint distribution with the given marginal distributions.
I_{k}
, for
1\leq k\leq K
, are non-empty subsets of

S_{k}=\sum_{n\in I_{k}}X_{n}
are partial sums, and

is the maximal partial sum, such as LPP. Subsection
2.3
(the ”parallel” machine) dealt with singleton subsets, and Subsection
2.4
(the ”series” machine) dealt with a single subset, the set itself. LPP on

is a machine built by nesting series and parallel sub-machines.

A convex bound for the distribution of
{\cal M}
will be found by maximizing over the joint distributions the premium function
E[({\cal M}-x)^{+}]
, for each
x
. Take an arbitrary joint distribution, fix an arbitrary vector
(v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{N})
and express for each
k

S_{k}-x=\sum_{n\in I_{k}}(X_{n}-v_{n})+\sum_{n\in I_{k}}v_{n}-x

Increase the RHS as indicated to obtain

S_{k}-x\leq\sum_{n\in I_{k}}(X_{n}-v_{n})^{+}+(\max_{k}\sum_{n\in I_{k}}v_{n}-x)^{+}

Increase further the RHS as indicated to obtain

S_{k}-x\leq\sum_{n=1}^{N}(X_{n}-v_{n})^{+}+(\max_{k}\sum_{n\in I_{k}}v_{n}-x)^{+}

The RHS in constant wrt k, so

{\cal M}-x=\max_{k}S_{k}-x\leq\sum_{n=1}^{N}(X_{n}-v_{n})^{+}+(\max_{k}\sum_{n\in I_{k}}v_{n}-x)^{+}

The RHS is non-negative, so

({\cal M}-x)^{+}=(\max_{k}S_{k}-x)^{+}\leq\sum_{n=1}^{N}(X_{n}-v_{n})^{+}+(\max_{k}\sum_{n\in I_{k}}v_{n}-x)^{+}

(3)

This inequality holds a.s., so, taking expectations,

H_{\cal M}(x)=E[({\cal M}-x)^{+}]\leq\sum_{n=1}^{N}H_{X_{n}}(v_{n})+(\max_{k}\sum_{n\in I_{k}}v_{n}-x)^{+}

(4)

The LHS is constant in
v
, leading to

H_{\cal M}(x)=E[({\cal M}-x)^{+}]\leq B(x)=\inf_{v}\{\sum_{n=1}^{N}H_{X_{n}}(v_{n})+(\max_{k}\sum_{n\in I_{k}}v_{n}-x)^{+}\}

(5)

It should be observed that the function

for every joint distribution of the

But (

is ever less than

of

. Its essential infimum is

. For

4
On stochastically small last passage times

This is a detour from the main theme studying how large can LPP time be, to the opposite question, how small can LPP times be. As will be seen, sharp answers can be provided for distributions that are memoryless up to scaling.

Since LPP time exceeds the total weight along any path, expected LPP time is at least
NE[W]
. By making all
W(i,j)
along every anti-diagonal equal to each other, LPP time has mean
NE[W]
, regardless of the joint distribution of these anti-diagonal weights. Hence, the minimal expected LPP time in the class of all couplings of the weights
W(i,j)
is simply
NE[W]
. In the exponential case, this means that the i.i.d. LPP times have expected value at most twice the minimal possible value.

As for the variance of these LPP times with common anti-diagonal weights, there are distributions
F
, the
completely mixable
distributions (see Wang & Wang
[
11
]
and its references, Rüschendorf & Uckelman
[
9
]
in particular) for which there is a coupling of
W_{1},W_{2},\dots W_{N}
, with sum a.s. constant. So, in this case, the minimal possible variance of these LPP times is zero (even if
F
has finite mean and infinite variance). The uniform distribution and any distribution with
symmetric unimodal density are completely mixable. As an example,

The exchangeable multivariate Gaussian case
. Let
Z_{i},1\leq i\leq N
be i.i.d. standard normal, and let
Q_{N}
be their sum. Let
W_{i}={{NZ_{i}-Q_{N}}\over{\sqrt{N(N-1)}}},1\leq i\leq N
. These
W_{i}
are standard normal and add up to
0
a.s. A Gaussian copula that generates this singular multivariate Gaussian distribution is
\Phi(W_{i}),1\leq i\leq N
. This solution is not unique: giving up on exchangeability and correlation coefficient
\rho=-{1\over{N=1}}
between every pair, for
N=2k
even, letting
W_{i}
be i.i.d. for
{i\leq k}
and letting
W_{k+i}=-W_{i}
for each
{i\leq k}
, will also achieve
{\mbox{V}ar}[sum_{i-1}^{N}]=0
. For
N
odd, apply the exchangeable trivariate Gaussian solution once and complement it with bivariate pairs.

The role played by symmetry is apparent. Minimal variance of the sum is zero for
F
symmetric and
N
even, by the pair-wise construction above. The case
N=3
(and then all
N\geq 2
) for symmetric unimodal densities falls under the rubric of completely mixable distributions of Wang & Wang
[
11
]
.

The
Exp(1)
case with
N=2
. Clearly, the minimal variance is achieved by the antithetic pair
W_{1}=-log(U)
and
W_{2}=-log(1-U)
with sum
W_{1}+W_{2}=-log(U(1-U))
. By numerical integration and large sample simulation,
{\mbox{V}ar}[W_{1}+W_{2}]=0.7101
, smaller than
{\mbox{V}ar}[W_{1}]=1
. In fact, for every
F
with finite variance, a coupling with minimal variance of
W_{1}+W_{2}
is
W_{1}=F^{-1}(U),W_{2}=F^{-1}(1-U)
.

A significant difference between
N=2
and
N>2
is that there is a notion of antithetic copula for
N=2
but not for any
N>2
. In contrast, the co-monotone copula with identical uniform variables and the independent copula with i.i.d. uniform variables, naturally exist for all
N
. For any distribution
F
with finite variance, just as in the exponential case above, the minimal possible variance of the sum of two
F
-distributed r.v. is attained by the sum
F^{-1}(U)+F^{-1}(1-U)
, with
U\sim U(0,1)
, generated by the antithetic copula.

Wang & Wang
[
11
]
: Completely mixable distributions with decreasing density and bounded support
. For every distribution
F
supported by the finite interval
[0,b]
, with mean
\mu\in[{b\over N},b-{b\over N}]

N

F

This remarkable theorem by Wang & Wang provides a tool to analyze the variance of the sum of
N
identically distributed weights with distribution memoryless up to scaling. The
\beta

have bounded support and either constant or decreasing density. Hence, by the theorem of Wang & Wang, for every

NE[W]

The exponential and Pareto distributions are supported by right rays and have decreasing density.

The plan to bound from above the minimal variance of the sum
S_{N}
of
N
weights
W_{i}
that are either exponentially or Pareto distributed is as follows. Fix

. The minimal possible variance of LPP time with minimal possible mean tends to zero as

Proof

\displaystyle V_{N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mbox{V}ar}[S_{N}]=E[{\mbox{V}ar}[S_{N}|I_{H}]]+{\mbox{V}ar}[E[V_{N}|I_{H}]]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0+P(W>b)V_{N}+N^{2}P(W>b)P(W\leq b)(E[W|W>b]-E[W|W\leq b])^{2}

\displaystyle N^{2}P(W>b)(E[W|W>b]-E[W|W\leq b])^{2}

Flat random walks on
C_{N}

A

on
C_{N}

with initial state
W_{1}=(1,1)
, such that for every

is supported by the
n

is uniform on the
n

The natural random walks
on

or

have independent coordinates

. Trajectories are supported by
C_{N}
or by
P_{N,M}
.

The Pólya random walk
on
C_{N}
is built as follows.
W_{N}
is sampled from its uniform distribution, with values from
1
to
N
. Consider an urn with
W_{N}-1
”up” balls and
N-W_{N}
”right” balls. Balls are successively drawn from the urn without replacement, until the urn is empty. The balls withdrawn describe, backwards from the diagonal to the origin, the steps of the random walk. Its trajectories are supported by
L_{N}
. The name Pólya was chosen because this random walk may be built forward, from the origin to the diagonal, by the Pólya urn scheme. In spite of the elegance of the Pólya urn scheme forward construction, the backward construction is more naturally generalized to obtain rectangle point-to-point random walks.

The rectangle random walk
on
P_{N,M}
. Consider the rectangle with corners
(1,1),\linebreak(N-M+1,1),(1,M),(N-M+1,M)
, where
{{N+1}\over 2}\leq M\leq N
. Two sub-triangles are singled out in this rectangle,
C_{N-M+1}
with the right angle at
(1,1)
and its mirror image with the right angle at the opposite corner
(N-M+1,M)
. Choose a random vertex
W_{N-M+1}
in the sub-diagonal from
(1,N-M+1)
to
(N-M+1,1)
and connect it vertically via ”up” transitions to the sub-diagonal from
(1,M)
to
(N-M+1,2M-N)
. Complete the random walk by two independent samplings without replacement as in the Pólya random walk construction, to connect to the corners
(1,1)
and
(N-M+1,M)
. The trajectories of this random walk are supported by

.
