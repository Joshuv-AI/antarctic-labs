1.
Introduction and main result

1.1.
Overview

We consider the model equation

(1.1)

\partial_{t}u-\operatorname{div}\!\bigl(|Du|^{p-2}Du+a(z)|Du|^{q-2}Du\bigr)=0\qquad\text{in }\Omega_{T},

and, in greater generality, the equation

(1.2)

\partial_{t}u-\operatorname{div}A(z,u,Du)=0\qquad\text{in }\Omega_{T},

where
\Omega_{T}:=\Omega\times(0,T]
with
\Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{N}
a
bounded open set,
N\geq 2
. The Carathéodory vector field

A:\Omega_{T}\times\mathds{R}\times\mathds{R}^{N}\to\mathds{R}^{N}
is
assumed to satisfy the structural conditions

(1.3)

\nu\,H(z,|\xi|)\leq A(z,v,\xi)\cdot\xi,\qquad|A(z,v,\xi)|\,|\xi|\leq L\,H(z,|\xi|),

for all
z\in\Omega_{T}
,
v\in\mathds{R}
,
\xi\in\mathds{R}^{N}
, with
constants
0<\nu\leq L
. Here,
H:\Omega_{T}\times\mathds{R}_{+}\to\mathds{R}_{+}
is the parabolic double phase integrand

H(z,\zeta)=\zeta^{p}+a(z)\zeta^{q},\qquad z\in\Omega_{T},\quad\zeta\in\mathds{R}_{+},

where
1<p\leq q
and the modulating coefficient
a
is a non-negative
function on
\Omega_{T}
whose precise regularity will be specified below.
The choice
A(z,v,\xi)=|\xi|^{p-2}\xi+a(z)|\xi|^{q-2}\xi

recovers (
1.1
), the canonical parabolic double phase equation.

The elliptic ancestor of (
1.1
) was introduced by
Zhikov
[
34
,
35
,
36
,
37
]
as a model for
strongly anisotropic composite materials, in which the ellipticity changes
from
p
-growth on the set
\{a=0\}
to
(p,q)
-growth elsewhere. The
same model is also one of the standard examples in which the Lavrentiev
phenomenon occurs
[
12
,
13
,
35
,
36
]
and has,
for over a decade, been a focal point of the regularity theory of
non-uniformly elliptic problems. In the elliptic setting,

C^{0,\alpha}
- and
C^{1,\alpha}
-estimates, Calderón–Zygmund
estimates, and Harnack-type inequalities have been established
in
[
3
,
9
,
8
,
10
,
11
,
2
,
6
,
5
]
, among many other contributions. A
parabolic counterpart has only recently begun to develop: gradient higher integrability is treated
in
[
23
,
26
,
32
,
21
,
22
,
29
,
1
]
, Calderón–Zygmund estimates in
[
27
,
28
]
, existence and uniqueness of
weak solutions in
[
33
,
24
]
, and Hölder continuity
in
[
25
]
.

A central question in the double phase setting is how the regularity of solutions is related to
the interplay between the gap condition of
p,q
and the regularity of the
modulating coefficient
a
. Until now, the literature has assumed
a\in C^{\alpha,\alpha/2}(\Omega_{T})
for some
\alpha\in(0,1]
. We instead work with a
strictly larger class of coefficients, originally identified
in
[
4
]
in the elliptic setting and which we follow in that paper
by calling
\mathcal{Z}^{\kappa}
.

Definition 1.1
.

For
\kappa\in(0,\infty)
, a function
a:\Omega_{T}\to[0,\infty)

belongs to the class
\mathcal{Z}^{\kappa}(\Omega_{T})
if there exists a
constant
c_{a}\geq 1
such that

(1.4)

a(z_{1})\leq c_{a}\bigl(a(z_{2})+d_{p}(z_{1},z_{2})^{\kappa}\bigr)\qquad\text{for all }z_{1},z_{2}\in\Omega_{T}.

where
d_{p}
denotes the parabolic metric given by Definition
2.1
.

In the elliptic setting,
\mathcal{Z}^{\kappa}
was introduced
in
[
4
]
in connection with the Lavrentiev phenomenon, and
closely related conditions had previously appeared in the regularity theory
of non-autonomous elliptic problems
[
15
,
17
,
18
]
. To the best of
our knowledge, the present paper is the first to use this class in the
parabolic context. As a guiding example, the function
x\mapsto|x|^{\kappa}

belongs to
\mathcal{Z}^{\kappa}(\mathds{R}^{N})
for every
\kappa\in(0,\infty)
, illustrating that
\mathcal{Z}^{\kappa}
accommodates
non-Hölder behaviour of the coefficients and
\kappa

precisely measures the rate at which the coefficient is allowed to vanish on its zero set
\{a=0\}
, a rate that may be taken arbitrarily large.

Throughout this paper,
a
is assumed to lie in

\mathcal{Z}^{\kappa}(\Omega_{T})
for some
\kappa\in(0,\infty)
, and our main result is established under the gap bound

(1.5)

2\leq p\leq q\leq p+\frac{q\kappa}{q-2\gamma},

where
\gamma\in[0,1)
is the Hölder exponent of the solution
u
and

\kappa
is the constant in (
1.4
).

We also assume that the coefficient
a
satisfies a
mild monotonicity
condition, i.e.,there exist
\zeta\in\mathds{R}^{N}
and a positive constant
C_{a}
, both independent of
(x,t)
, such that either

(1.6)

a(x,t)\leq C_{a}a(x+(t-s)\zeta,s)\quad\text{for all }(x,t),(x,s)\in\Omega_{T}\quad\text{with }\,\,s\leq t.

or

(1.7)

a(x,t)\leq C_{a}a(x+(s-t)\zeta,s)\quad\text{for all }(x,t),(x,s)\in\Omega_{T}\quad\text{with }\,\,s\geq t.

This assumption is a generalization of the assumptions considered in
[
31
]
. As observed in
[
31
]
, the assumptions are not optimal in the sense that one can find functions such as
a(x,t)=\max\{-(x+t+1),0\},
that do not satisfy the assumption on a cylinder centered at a point
(x,t)
that lies on the line
x=-(t+1).
Such functions are now covered by assumptions (
1.6
)-(
1.7
).

1.2.
Main result

To state the main theorem, we first recall the notion of a weak solution and
introduce the solution space associated with (
1.2
):

W^{H}(\Omega_{T}):=\biggl\{u\in L^{1}((0,T),W^{1,1}(\Omega,\mathds{R})):\iint_{\Omega_{T}}\bigl(|Du|^{p}+a(z)|Du|^{q}\bigr)\,dz<\infty\biggr\}.

We also denote the parabolic cylinders by

Q_{r}(z_{0}):=B_{r}(x_{0})\times(t_{0}-r^{2},\,t_{0}+r^{2}).

Definition 1.2

(Weak solution)
.

A function
u:\Omega\times(0,T]\to\mathds{R}
with

u\in C_{\mathrm{loc}}\!\bigl((0,T];\,L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega,\mathds{R})\bigr)\cap W^{H}(\Omega_{T})
is a weak solution of (
1.2
) if

\iint_{\Omega_{T}}\bigl(-u\,\partial_{t}\varphi+A(z,u,Du)\cdot D\varphi\bigr)\,dz=0

holds for every nonnegative test function
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega_{T},\mathds{R})
.

Our main result of this paper is the following.

Theorem 1.3

(Gradient higher integrability)
.

Let
u
be a weak solution of (
1.2
) that is locally
\gamma
-Hölder continuous in space and
\frac{\gamma}{q}
-Hölder continuous in time. Also, assume that
a
satisfies (
1.4
) and (
1.7
). Then there exist constants
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(\texttt{data})>0

and
c=c(\texttt{data})\geq 1
such that

{{{\int\hskip-7.96674pt\int}\hskip-15.93347pt-\hskip-4.26773pt-}_{\hskip-3.69899ptQ_{r}(z_{0})}}H(z,|Du|)^{1+\varepsilon}\,dz\leq c\left(\frac{2+\|a\|_{\infty}}{r^{q-2\gamma}}\right)^{\!1+\frac{\varepsilon q}{2}}

for every
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
, whenever
Q_{3r}(z_{0})\subset\Omega_{T}
.

We close the introduction with several remarks on this result.

Remark 1.4
.

(i) The new upper bound (
1.5
) reflects the time
Hölder regularity of solutions to (
1.1
) and differs from the
classical elliptic Lavrentiev gap bound for Hölder continuous solutions. In
fact, for
p\geq 2
it is worse than the elliptic one in the sense that

\frac{q\kappa}{q-2\gamma}\leq\frac{\kappa}{1-\gamma},

where
\gamma
denotes the Hölder exponent of
u
. We do not know whether
this new upper bound is sharp. In the elliptic case, the upper bound

q-p\leq\kappa/(1-\gamma)
is known to be sharp. In the parabolic setting,
the phenomenon is governed entirely by the time regularity: in particular, if
the time regularity could be improved from
\gamma/q
to
\gamma/2
, the same
gap condition as in the elliptic case would be recovered. Note also that

q\kappa/(q-2\gamma)
blows up when
\gamma\to 1
and
q\to 2
.

(ii) Since our higher integrability proof broadly follows the
framework of
[
23
,
32
]
, it carries over to the singular
regime and to systems. Nevertheless, we restrict our attention to scalar
equations, as Hölder regularity in the singular case and for systems has
not yet been established. For the same reason, the main statements concern
the homogeneous problem. That said, our approach accommodates natural
nonhomogeneous sources of the form

\partial_{t}u-\operatorname{div}\left(|Du|^{p-2}Du+a(z)|Du|^{q-2}Du\right)=-\operatorname{div}\left(|F|^{p-2}F+a(z)|F|^{q-2}F\right),

for a given vector field
F:\Omega_{T}\to\mathds{R}
satisfying

\iint_{\Omega_{T}}H(z,|F|)\,dz=\iint_{\Omega_{T}}\bigl(|F|^{p}+a(z)|F|^{q}\bigr)\,dz<\infty.

To obtain higher integrability in the presence of such a source, an integrability assumption on
H(z,|F|)
stronger than
L^{1}(\Omega_{T})
, which is typically required in Lemma
3.2
to control the growth of
H(z,|F|)
; see
[
7
]
.

Although Hölder regularity for the parabolic double phase equation is known
in the homogeneous case, an Ishii–Lions type argument can be adapted to
handle the analogous regularity for the equation with a nonhomogeneous term,
provided that
a
is Lipschitz in space so that the viscosity framework is well
posed. In the absence of such Lipschitz continuity of
a
, no viscosity
formulation is currently available. Consequently, while our higher
integrability theory extends to systems and to the singular regime, the
existing Hölder regularity statements are confined to scalar, homogeneous
equations (with nonhomogeneous extensions available only under the additional
Lipschitz continuity of
a
).

(iii) A key distinction from the recent works
[
7
,
21
]

is that these papers assume

(1.8)

u\in L^{q}\bigl(0,T;\,W^{1,q}(\Omega)\bigr)

as an
a priori
requirement, which is not guaranteed in this setting. In
particular, the standard Lipschitz truncation method is not applicable here:
deriving an energy estimate via Lipschitz truncation requires a prescribed
relation between the scaling parameter
\lambda
and the radius of the
parabolic cylinder, while in our approach this very relation is itself
extracted from the energy estimate. Starting from a truncation would
therefore lead to a circular dependence.

In place of (
1.8
), we employ the slanted Steklov average as a time mollification; see Section
2.2
for a precise definition.

(iv) We work with
a\in\mathcal{Z}^{\kappa}(\Omega_{T})
for

\kappa\in(0,\infty)
, which is a strictly larger class than

C^{\alpha,\alpha/2}(\Omega_{T})
with
\alpha\in(0,1]
. This class was
introduced in
[
4
]
in the study of the Lavrentiev phenomenon for
the elliptic double phase functional. To our knowledge, the present paper is
the first to use this class to investigate the regularity theory of parabolic
double phase equations. Previously, the class was used in the regularity
theory of non-autonomous elliptic problems
[
15
,
17
,
18
]
.
Note that for every
\kappa\in(0,\infty)
, the function

x\mapsto|x|^{\kappa}
belongs to
\mathcal{Z}^{\kappa}(\mathds{R}^{N})
.

To give some insight into this class, we recall that, for

\Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{N}
, every function in
\mathcal{Z}^{\kappa}(\Omega)
with

\kappa\in(0,1]
is comparable to a Hölder continuous function, and

C^{1+\kappa}(\Omega)\subset\mathcal{Z}^{1+\kappa}(\Omega)
. Moreover, a function
a

belongs to
\mathcal{Z}^{\kappa}
for some
\kappa\in(0,\infty)
if and only if there
exists
\tilde{a}
comparable to
a
such that
\tilde{a}^{1/\kappa}
is
Lipschitz. However, as observed in
[
4
, Proposition 1.3]
, the
class
\mathcal{Z}^{\kappa}
does not share many features with smoothness, in general.
Indeed, the function
t\mapsto t^{2}
is
C^{\infty}
but does not belong to

\mathcal{Z}^{2+\delta}
for any
\delta>0
.

Preliminaries

Notations and definitions

Here we collect the standard notation and definitions that will be used throughout the paper:

(i)

We denote by
N
the space dimension and by
z=(x,t)
a point in
\mathds{R}^{N}\times(0,T]
. We will always assume that
N\geq 2
.

(ii)

A ball with center
x_{0}\in\mathds{R}^{N}
and radius
r
is denoted as

\displaystyle B_{r}(x_{0})=\left\{x\in\mathds{R}^{N}:|x-x_{0}|<r\right\}.

(iii)

In general, a parabolic cylinder centered at
z_{0}=(x_{0},t_{0})
is denoted as

\displaystyle U_{r,\varrho}(z_{0}):=B_{r}(x_{0})\times\left(t_{0}-\varrho,t_{0}+\varrho\right)=:B_{r}(x_{0})\times l_{\varrho}(t_{0}).

Let
\lambda\geq 1.
We shall use the following three types of cylinders for the
p
-,and
(p,q)
-phases.

Q_{\varrho,\lambda}(z_{0})
is used to denote
p
-intrinsic cylinders at
z_{0}=(x_{0},t_{0}),

\displaystyle Q_{\varrho,\lambda}(z_{0}):=B_{\varrho}(x_{0})\times\left(t_{0}-\lambda^{2-p}\varrho^{2},t_{0}+\lambda^{2-p}\varrho^{2}\right)=:B_{\varrho}(x_{0})\times I_{\varrho,\lambda}(t_{0}).

The notation
G_{\varrho,\lambda}(z_{0})
is used to denote
(p,q)
-intrinsic cylinders at
z_{0}=(x_{0},t_{0}),

\displaystyle G_{\varrho,\lambda}(z_{0}):=B_{\varrho}(x_{0})\times\left(t_{0}-\frac{\lambda^{2}\varrho^{2}}{\Lambda},t_{0}+\frac{\lambda^{2}\varrho^{2}}{\Lambda}\right)=:B_{\varrho}(x_{0})\times J_{\varrho,\lambda}(t_{0}),

where
\Lambda:=\lambda^{p}+a(z_{0})\lambda^{q}.

(v)

In the case
\lambda=1,
we denote

Q_{\varrho}(z_{0}):=B_{\varrho}(x_{0})\times(t_{0}-\varrho^{2},t_{0}+\varrho^{2})=:B_{\varrho}(x_{0})\times I_{\varrho}(t_{0}).

(vi)

Integration with respect to either space or time only will be denoted by a single integral
\int
whereas integration on
\Omega\times(0,T)
will be denoted by a double integral
\iint
.

(vii)

The notation
a\lesssim b
is shorthand for
a\leq cb
where
c
is a universal constant that depends on
``\text{{data}}"
where

\displaystyle\text{{{data}}}:=\left(N,\widetilde{M},p,q,\nu,L,[u]_{\gamma.\gamma/q},\kappa,c_{a},||a||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{T})},||H(z,|Du|)||_{L^{1}(\Omega_{T})}\right).

(viii)

We shall denote

\displaystyle(f)_{\mathcal{D}}:=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{D}|}\iint_{\mathcal{D}}f(z)\,dz={{{\int\hskip-7.96674pt\int}\hskip-15.93347pt-\hskip-4.26773pt-}_{\hskip-3.69899pt\mathcal{D}}}f(z)\,dz

as the integral average of
f
over a measurable set
\mathcal{D}\subset\Omega_{T}
with
0<|\mathcal{D}|<\infty.

We also introduce the definition of the parabolic metric and its scaled version.

Definition 2.1

(Parabolic metric)
.

Given any two points
z_{1}=(x_{1},t_{1})
and
z_{2}=(x_{2},t_{2})
in
\mathds{R}^{N+1},
we define the parabolic metric
d_{p}
as

\displaystyle d_{p}(z_{1},z_{2}):=\max\left\{|x_{1}-x_{2}|,\sqrt{|t_{1}-t_{2}|}\right\}.

A suitable adaptation of the above definition in intrinsically scaled cylinders centered at
z_{0}\in\mathds{R}^{N+1}
is as follows.

Definition 2.2

(Scaled parabolic metric)
.

Fix
z_{0}\in\mathds{R}^{N+1}.
Given any two points
z_{1}=(x_{1},t_{1}),z_{2}=(x_{2},t_{2})\in\mathds{R}^{N+1}
and
\lambda\geq 1
, we define the parabolic metric
d^{\lambda}_{p}(\cdot,\cdot)
as

\displaystyle\displaystyle d^{\lambda}_{p}(z_{1},z_{2}):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\max\left\{|x_{1}-x_{2}|,\sqrt{\lambda^{p-2}|t_{1}-t_{2}|}\right\},\,\,\,\,\text{in $p$-intrinsic case,}\\
\max\left\{|x_{1}-x_{2}|,\sqrt{\Lambda\lambda^{-2}|t_{1}-t_{2}|}\right\},\,\,\,\text{in $(p,q)$-intrinsic case,}\end{array}\right.

where
\Lambda:=\lambda^{p}+a(z_{0})\lambda^{q}.

2.2.
Slanted Steklov average

In this section, we introduce the idea of a slanted Steklov average. For any
\zeta\in\mathds{R}^{N},
we define
right slanted Steklov average
and
left slanted Steklov average
respectively.

(2.1)

\displaystyle[u]_{h}^{R}(z):=\frac{1}{h}\int_{t}^{t+h}u(x+(s-t)\zeta,s)\,ds,

and

(2.2)

\displaystyle[u]_{h}^{L}(z):=\frac{1}{h}\int_{t-h}^{t}u(x+(t-s)\zeta,s)\,ds.

As can be seen from the definition, the name “slanted” suggests a shift to the spatial direction. This makes the definition different from the usual Steklov average as the usual definition only averages in the time direction. We note some advantages and restrictions for introducing such mollification in the following.

(i) First, we note that, similar to the standard spatial mollification, one can also use a space-time mollification. This was first explicitly written in
[
30
]
and later used in
[
19
,
20
]
;
it avoids the assumptions (
1.6
)–(
1.7
) on the coefficient,
and it is enough to assume
a\in\mathcal{Z}^{\kappa}(\Omega_{T})
. The precise definition of this space-time mollification is as follows. Let
\widetilde{\Theta}_{M}\in C^{\infty}(\mathds{R})
be the
standard mollifier

\widetilde{\Theta}_{M}(s):=\begin{cases}c_{M}\,\exp\!\left(\dfrac{1}{s^{2}-1}\right)&\text{if }|s|<1,\\[5.69054pt]
0&\text{if }|s|\geq 1,\end{cases}

where
c_{M}>0
is chosen so that

\int_{\mathds{R}^{M}}\widetilde{\Theta}_{M}(|x|)\,dx=1
. We define

\Theta(z)=\Theta(x,t):=\widetilde{\Theta}_{N}(|x|)\,\widetilde{\Theta}_{1}(t),\qquad\Theta_{h}(x,t):=\frac{1}{h^{N+2}}\,\Theta\!\left(\frac{x}{h},\,\frac{t}{h^{2}}\right)\quad\text{for }h>0.

Then
\Theta_{h}\in C^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N+1})
,
\Theta_{h}\geq 0
,

\|\Theta_{h}\|_{L^{1}(\mathds{R}^{N+1})}=1
, and

\mathrm{supp}(\Theta_{h})\subset Q_{h}(0)
. Moreover, with constants
depending only on
N
, we have

0\leq\Theta_{h}\leq c\,h^{-(N+2)},\qquad|D\Theta_{h}|\leq c\,h^{-(N+3)},\qquad|\partial_{t}\Theta_{h}|\leq c\,h^{-(N+4)}.

For a bounded open set
U\subset\mathds{R}^{N+1}
and
f\in L^{1}(U)
, the
parabolic mollification of
f
is

[f]^{h}(z):=(f*\Theta_{h})(z)=\iint_{\mathds{R}^{N+1}}f(z-\sigma)\,\Theta_{h}(\sigma)\,d\sigma=\iint_{Q_{h}(0)}f(z-\sigma)\,\Theta_{h}(\sigma)\,d\sigma,

defined whenever
Q_{h}(z)\subset U
. Under this mollification, as

h\to 0
, we would have

\displaystyle\iint_{U_{R,S}(z_{0})}|[f]^{h}-f|^{p}+a(z)|[f]^{h}-f|^{q}\,dz\to 0,

\displaystyle\iint_{U_{R,S}(z_{0})}|D[f]^{h}-Df|^{p}+a(z)|D[f]^{h}-Df|^{q}\,dz\to 0,

\displaystyle\iint_{U_{R,S}(z_{0})}\left[|Df|^{p-2}Df+a(z)|Df|^{q-2}Df\right]^{h}\cdot D[f]^{h}\,dz\to\iint_{U_{R,S}(z_{0})}|Df|^{p}+a(z)|Df|^{q}\,dz.

Notice that the most crucial convergence is the third one. Once we
have these convergences, we can prove
Lemma
2.5
and Lemma
2.6
, and
completely avoid assumptions (
1.6
)–(
1.7
) in our main result. However, to use this mollification, we need the
(\kappa,\frac{\kappa}{2})
-regularity in space and time of the coefficient
a
with
\kappa\in(0,1].
Since, in our case, the exponent
\kappa
can be greater than
1
the above convergences are not entirely clear with the gap bound (
1.5
) and we need to seek a different route than the usual one.

(ii) Another important motivation for our mollification is to understand the optimal time regularity of the coefficient
a
. Our strategy is to use separate approximation lemmas in space and in time; see Lemma
2.3
and Lemma
2.4
. In this way, we do not need Hölder continuity of
a
in time; continuity in time is enough. In place of this, we assume the almost monotonicity (
1.6
)–(
1.7
), which essentially matters only on the small cylinders that meet the zero set of
a
. When
a\geq\delta>0
, assumptions (
1.6
)–(
1.7
) are trivially satisfied. The novelty of our approach is that the time regularity part of (
1.4
) is not used in the approximation lemmas and hence not in the energy estimates; only the spatial regularity of
a
is used at this stage. For parabolic double phase problems, approximation is a crucial step, and since
a
can vanish in some set, to perform the approximation in a correct way, one needs some regularity of
a
in time. Our monotonicity assumption on
a
is a direct, pointwise condition in time, used as such in the time approximation, whereas Hölder regularity in time enters only indirectly, through the density theorem in generalized Orlicz spaces. However, we use the
\frac{\kappa}{2}
-regularity in time of
a
as in (
1.4
) in the other parts of the paper.

2.3.
Approximation lemmas and energy estimate

This section begins with the time and space approximation lemmas, where we use the slanted Steklov average, and those lemmas are used to prove the Caccioppoli estimate.

Lemma 2.3

(Space approximation)
.

Let
2\leq p\leq q\leq p+\frac{q\kappa}{q-2\gamma}
and
R,S>0
. Moreover, suppose
that

a(x_{1},t)\leq c_{a}\left(a(x_{2},t)+|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{\kappa}\right)\quad\text{for}\quad(x_{1},t),(x_{2},t)\in\Omega\times\{t\},

and

u\in W^{H}(U_{R,S})\cap C^{0,\gamma}(U_{R,S})
is compactly
supported in
U_{R,S}
. For
\delta>0
, define

u_{\delta}(x,t):=\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}}u\left(x-y,t\right)\varrho_{\delta}(y)\,dy,

where
\varrho_{\delta}
is the standard mollifier and the integrand
is read as zero when
x-y\not\in U_{R,S}
. Then
\supp u_{\delta}\Subset U_{R,S}

for all small enough
\delta>0
. Furthermore,
u_{\delta}\in W^{H}(U_{R,S})
and

(2.3)

\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\iint_{U_{R,S}(z_{0})}\left|Du-Du_{\delta}\right|^{p}+a(z)\left|Du-Du_{\delta}\right|^{q}\,dz=0.

Proof.

We follow
[
31
, Theorem 3.2]
. We need to verify (
2.3
) and that

\int_{U_{R,S}(z_{0})}|Du_{\delta}|^{p}+a(z)|Du_{\delta}|^{q}\,dz<\infty.

Step 1:
Let
d:=\operatorname{dist}(\supp u,\partial_{\mathcal{P}}U_{R,S}(z_{0}))
,
\delta_{0}:=d/8

and
r:=R-d/4,\tau=S-d/4
. Then
u_{\delta}\equiv 0
in
U_{R,S}\setminus U_{r,\tau}

for all
\delta<\delta_{0}
. Fix
(x,t)\in U_{r,\tau}
, where
t
is such that
u(\cdot,t)
is
\gamma
-Hölder continuous in
B_{r}.
Further, assume that
\delta<\delta_{0}
and let
x_{t}^{\ast}\in\overline{B}_{\delta}(x)

be such that

a(x_{t}^{\ast},t)=\inf_{y\in B_{\delta}(x)}a(y,t),

which is well defined since
B_{\delta}(x)\Subset B_{r}.
Using
u\in C^{0,\gamma}_{loc}(\Omega)
, we have

\displaystyle\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|=\left|D\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}}u\left(x-y,t\right)\varrho_{\delta}(y)\,dy\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{B(x,\delta)}\left(u(y,t)-u(x,t)\right)D\varrho_{\delta}(x-y)\,dy\right|+\left|u(x,t)\int_{B(x,\delta)}D\varrho_{\delta}(x-y)\,dy\right|

\displaystyle\leq([u]_{\gamma,\gamma/q}+||u||_{\infty})\delta^{\gamma}\left\|D\varrho_{\delta}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathds{R}^{N})}\leq([u]_{\gamma,\gamma/q}+||u||_{\infty})\delta^{-1+\gamma}\left\|D\varrho\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathds{R}^{N})}=:\delta^{-1+\gamma}C_{0}.

Note that, we also used that
\left\|D\varrho_{\delta}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathds{R}^{N})}=\delta^{-1}\left\|D\varrho\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathds{R}^{N})}
and the fundamental theorem of calculus to conclude
\int_{B(x,\delta)}D\varrho_{\delta}(x-y)\,dy=0.

Using the above display, Hölder continuity of
a
in space variable,
as well as that
\left|x-x_{t}^{\ast}\right|\leq\delta
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{p}+a(x,t)\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{q}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{p}+a(x,t)\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{q}}{\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{p}+c_{a}a(x_{t}^{\ast},t)\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{q}}\left(\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{p}+c_{a}a(x^{\ast}_{t},t)\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{q}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(1+\frac{(a(x,t)-c_{a}a(x_{t}^{\ast},t))\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{q}}{\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{p}+c_{a}a(x_{t}^{\ast},t)\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{q}}\right)\left(\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{p}+c_{a}a(x^{\ast}_{t},t)\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{q}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\frac{c_{a}\delta^{\kappa}\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{q}}{\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{p}}\right)\left(\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{p}+c_{a}a(x_{t}^{\ast},t)\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{q}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+c_{a}C^{q-p}_{0}\delta^{\kappa}\left(\delta^{-1+\gamma}\right)^{q-p}\right)\left(\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{p}+c_{a}a(x_{t}^{\ast},t)\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{q}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+c_{a}C^{q-p}_{0}\delta^{\kappa}\left(\delta^{-1+\gamma}\right)^{\frac{q\kappa}{q-2\gamma}}\right)\left(\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{p}+c_{a}a(x_{t}^{\ast},t)\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{q}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+c_{a}C^{q-p}_{0}\delta^{\kappa}\left(\delta^{-1+\gamma}\right)^{\frac{\kappa}{1-\gamma}}\right)\left(\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{p}+c_{a}a(x_{t}^{\ast},t)\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{q}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(1+c_{a}C^{q-p}_{0}\right)\left(\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{p}+c_{a}a(x_{t}^{\ast},t)\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{q}\right)

\displaystyle=:C_{1}\left(\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{p}+a(x_{t}^{\ast},t)\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{q}\right)

where
C_{1}:=\max\{(1+C^{q-p}_{0}c_{a}),c_{a}(1+C^{q-p}_{0}c_{a})\}.

Further, since
\int_{B_{\delta}}\rho_{\delta}(y)\,dy=1
, we can apply Jensen’s inequality to estimate

\displaystyle\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{p}+a(x_{t}^{\ast},t)\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{q}

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{B_{\delta}}Du(x-y,t)\rho_{\delta}(y)\,dy\right|^{p}+a(x_{t}^{\ast},t)\left|\int_{B_{\delta}}Du\left(x-y,t\right)\rho_{\delta}(y)\,dy\right|^{q}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{\delta}}\left|Du(x-y,t)\right|^{p}\rho_{\delta}(y)\,dy+\int_{B_{\delta}}a(x_{t}^{\ast},t)\left|Du\left(x-y,t\right)\right|^{q}\rho_{\delta}(y)\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{\delta}}\left|Du(x-y,t)\right|^{p}\rho_{\delta}(y)\,dy+\int_{B_{\delta}}a(x-y,t)\left|Du\left(x-y,t\right)\right|^{q}\rho_{\delta}(y)\,dy,

\displaystyle=(\left|Du\right|^{p})_{\delta}(x,t)+(a\left|Du\right|^{q})_{\delta}(x,t),

where the last estimate follows from the choice of
x_{t}^{\ast}
. Combining the last two displays, we arrive at

(2.4)

\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{p}+a(x,t)\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{q}\leq C_{1}(\left|Du\right|^{p}+a\left|Du\right|^{q})_{\delta}(x,t)

for all
x\in B_{r}(x_{0})
and almost all
t\in(t_{0}-\tau,t_{0}+\tau)
.

Step 2:
We define
w:\mathds{R}^{N+1}\rightarrow\mathds{R}

by setting
w=(\left|Du\right|^{p}+a\left|Du\right|^{q})
in
U_{r,\tau}

and
w=0
in
\mathds{R}^{N+1}\setminus U_{r,\tau}.
Since
w\in L^{1}(\mathds{R}^{N+1})

with compact support, we have
w_{\delta}\rightarrow w
in
L^{1}(\mathds{R}^{N+1})

by the standard properties of the mollifier. On the other hand, by (
2.4
),
we have

\left|Du_{\delta}\right|^{p}+a\left|Du_{\delta}\right|^{q}\leq C_{1}w_{\delta}\quad\text{almost everywhere in }U_{r,\tau}.

It follows that
\left|Du_{\delta}\right|^{p}+a\left|Du_{\delta}\right|^{q}

is uniformly integrable in
U_{r,\tau}
with respect to
\delta
.
Therefore, by Vitali’s convergence theorem,

\left|Du_{\delta}\right|^{p}+a\left|Du_{\delta}\right|^{q}\rightarrow\left|Du\right|^{p}+a\left|Du\right|^{q}\quad\text{in }L^{1}(U_{r,\tau}).

Then observe that

\displaystyle\int_{U_{r,\tau}}\left|Du-Du_{\delta}\right|^{p}+a(z)\left|Du-Du_{\delta}\right|^{q}\,dz

\displaystyle\leq\int_{U_{r,\tau}}2^{p-1}(\left|Du\right|^{p}+\left|Du_{\delta}\right|^{p})+2^{q-1}a(z)(\left|Du\right|^{q}+\left|Du_{\delta}\right|^{q})\,dz,

so that
\left|Du-Du_{\delta}\right|^{p}+a\left|Du-Du_{\delta}\right|^{q}

is again uniformly integrable, and by Vitali’s convergence theorem,
the left-hand side of the above inequality tends to zero as
\delta\rightarrow 0
.
Since both
\left|Du\right|
and
\left|Du_{\delta}\right|
vanish
in
U_{R,S}\setminus U_{r,\tau}
(provided that
\delta<\delta_{0}
),
the claim follows.
∎

Next, we prove the time approximation lemma. For that, we recall the definitions (
2.1
) and (
2.2
).

Lemma 2.4

(Time approximation)
.

Let
a:U_{R,S}(z_{0})\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be bounded and measurable and
u\in W^{H}(U_{R,S})
and compactly supported in
U_{R,S}(z_{0}).
If (
1.7
) holds, then for any small
h_{0}>0
,we have

\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\iint_{B_{R}(x_{0})\times(t_{0}-S,t_{0}+S+h_{0})}\left|D[u]^{R}_{h}-Du\right|^{p}+a(z)\left|D[u]^{R}_{h}-Du\right|^{q}\,dz=0,

up to a subsequence.
Moreover, if
u\in W^{H}(U_{R,S}(z_{0}))\cap C(U_{R,S}(z_{0}))
, then
[u]_{h}^{R}\in W^{H}(U_{R,S}(z_{0}))

with
D[u]_{h}^{R}=[Du]_{h}^{R}
and the weak time
derivative of
[u]_{h}^{R}
and
[u]^{L}_{h}
are given by

-\partial_{t}[u]_{h}^{R}(x,t)=\frac{u(x,t)-u(x+h\zeta,t+h)}{h}+\zeta\cdot D[u]_{h}^{R}(x,t),

and

\partial_{t}[u]_{h}^{L}(x,t)=\frac{u(x,t)-u(x+h\zeta,t-h)}{h}+\zeta\cdot D[u]_{h}^{L}(x,t).

If (
1.6
) holds, then we have the analogous convergence result for
[u]^{L}_{h}
.

Proof.

Consider the linear mapping
\Phi:\mathds{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathds{R}^{N}
,

\Phi(x,t)=(x-t\zeta,t)\quad\text{and its inverse}\quad\Phi^{-1}(x,t)=(x+t\zeta,t).

Since
u
is compactly supported in
U_{R,S}(z_{0})
, there exists a cylinder

U_{r,\tau}(z_{0})\Subset U_{R,S}(z_{0})
such that for small enough
h>0
, we have

u\equiv 0\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,[u]_{h}^{R}\equiv 0\quad\text{in }U_{R,S}(z_{0})\setminus U_{r,\tau}(z_{0}),

and

(x+h\zeta,t+h)\in U_{R,S}(z_{0})\quad\text{for all }(x,t)\in U_{r,\tau}(z_{0}).

Denote the slanted cylinders
U^{\prime}_{r,\tau}:=\Phi(U_{r,\tau}),

U_{R,S}^{\prime}:=\Phi(U_{R,S})
.
Denote by
[\cdot]_{h}
the standard Steklov average (without slanting). Fix
h_{0}>0
and suppose that
h<h_{0}
. For
i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
, we denote
v:=\partial_{i}u
. By standard properties of Steklov averages, we have
\partial_{i}[u]_{h}=[\partial_{i}u]_{h}=[v]_{h}
.
Using (
1.7
) we have for all
(x,t)\in U^{\prime}_{r,\tau}

\displaystyle\left(a\circ\Phi^{-1}\left|[v\circ\Phi^{-1}]_{h}\right|^{q}\right)(x,t)

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{1}{h}\int_{t}^{t+h}a^{1/q}(x+t\zeta,t)v(x+s\zeta,s)\,ds\right|^{q}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\left(\frac{1}{h}\int_{t}^{t+h}a^{1/q}(x+s\zeta,s)\left|v(x+s\zeta,s)\right|\,ds\right)^{q}

(2.5)

\displaystyle=C_{0}[(a^{1/q}\left|v\right|)\circ\Phi^{-1}]_{h}^{q}(x,t).

Since
(a^{1/q}\left|v\right|)\circ\Phi^{-1}\in L^{q}(U_{r,\tau}^{\prime})
,
the above display and standard properties of Steklov averages imply
that

\iint_{U^{\prime}_{r,\tau}(z_{0})}(a\circ\Phi^{-1})\left|[v\circ\Phi^{-1}]_{h}\right|^{q}\,dz\leq C<\infty\quad\text{for all small $h>0,$}

where
C
is independent of
h
.On the other hand, for any
(x,t)\in U^{\prime}_{r,\tau}(z_{0})
, we have

\displaystyle[v\circ\Phi^{-1}]_{h}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h}\int_{t}^{t+h}v(x+s\zeta,s)\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h}\int_{t}^{t+h}v(x+t\zeta+(s-t)\zeta,s)\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h}\int_{t}^{t+h}v(\Phi^{-1}(x,t)+(s-t)\zeta,s)\,ds

(2.6)

\displaystyle=[v]_{h}^{R}\circ\Phi^{-1}(x,t).

Then by change of variables and the last two displays, we obtain (note that
\det J_{\Phi}=1
)

\displaystyle\iint_{U_{r,\tau}}a(x,t)\left|[v]_{h}^{R}(x,t)\right|^{q}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=\iint_{U_{r,\tau}^{\prime}}(a\circ\Phi^{-1}(y,\tau))\left|[v]_{h}^{R}\circ\Phi^{-1}(y,\tau)\right|^{q}\,dy\,d\tau

\displaystyle=\iint_{U_{r,\tau}^{\prime}}(a\circ\Phi^{-1}(x,t))\left|[v\circ\Phi^{-1}]_{h}(y,\tau)\right|^{q}\,dy\,d\tau<\infty

for small
h>0
. Since
[v]_{h}^{R}
vanishes outside of
U_{r,\tau}
,
it follows that
[v]_{h}^{R}\in L^{H}(U_{r,\tau})
. To show the convergence, observe that by (
2.6
) we have

\displaystyle\iint_{U_{r,\tau}}a(x,t)\left|([v]_{h}^{R}-v)(x,t)\right|^{q}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=\iint_{U^{\prime}_{r,\tau}}a\circ\Phi^{-1}(y,\tau)\left|[v]_{h}^{R}\circ\Phi^{-1}(y,\tau)-v\circ\Phi^{-1}(y,\tau)\right|^{q}\,dy\,d\tau

(2.7)

\displaystyle=\iint_{U^{\prime}_{r,\tau}}a\circ\Phi^{-1}(y,s)\left|[v\circ\Phi^{-1}]_{h}(y,\tau)-v\circ\Phi^{-1}(y,\tau)\right|^{q}\,dy\,d\tau.

Observe that

\displaystyle a\circ\Phi^{-1}\left|[v\circ\Phi^{-1}]_{h}-v\circ\Phi^{-1}\right|^{q}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{q-1}a\circ\Phi^{-1}\left(\left|[v\circ\Phi^{-1}]_{h}\right|^{q}+\left|v\circ\Phi^{-1}\right|^{q}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{q-1}\left(C_{0}[(a^{1/q}|u|)\circ\Phi^{-1}]_{h}^{q}+a\circ\Phi^{-1}|v\circ\Phi^{-1}|^{q}\right),

where in the last inequality we used (
2.3
). Since the right-hand side of the above display converges in
L^{1}(U^{\prime}_{r,\tau})
by the properties of the standard Steklov average, it follows that the left-hand side
is uniformly integrable in
U^{\prime}_{r,\tau}
. Therefore, the right-hand
side of (
2.7
) converges to zero.

Now we prove the second assertion. Let
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(Q)
. Then, using that

u\circ\Phi^{-1}
has a weak space derivative in
U^{\prime}_{r,\tau}
with

D(u\circ\Phi^{-1})=Du\circ\Phi^{-1}
, we obtain (using also the
same computation as in (
2.6
), but for
Du
)

\displaystyle\iint_{U_{r,\tau}}[u]_{h}^{R}(x,t)D\varphi(x,t)\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=\iint_{U^{\prime}_{r,\tau}}([u]_{h}^{R}\circ\Phi^{-1}(y,\tau))(D\varphi\circ\Phi^{-1}(y,\tau))\,dy\,d\tau

\displaystyle=\iint_{U^{\prime}_{r,\tau}}([u\circ\Phi^{-1}]_{h}(y,\tau))D(\varphi\circ\Phi^{-1}(y,\tau))\,dy\,d\tau

\displaystyle=\iint_{U^{\prime}_{r,\tau}}-(\varphi\circ\Phi^{-1}(y,\tau))[D(u\circ\Phi^{-1}(y,\tau))]_{h}\,dy\,d\tau

\displaystyle=\iint_{U^{\prime}_{r,\tau}}-(\varphi\circ\Phi^{-1}(y,\tau))[Du\circ\Phi^{-1}(y,\tau)]_{h}\,dy\,d\tau

\displaystyle=\iint_{U^{\prime}_{r,\tau}}-(\varphi\circ\Phi^{-1}(y,\tau))[Du]_{h}^{R}\circ\Phi^{-1}(y,\tau)\,dy\,d\tau=\iint_{U_{r,\tau}}-\varphi[Du]_{h}^{R}(x,t)\,dx\,dt.

Therefore, the weak derivative of
[u]_{h}^{R}
is
[Du]_{h}^{R}
.
It follows from the previous statement that
[u]_{h}^{R}\in W^{H}(U_{R,S}(z_{0}))
. For the
time derivative, we compute

\displaystyle\iint_{U_{r,\tau}}[u]_{h}^{R}(x,t)\partial_{t}\varphi(x,t)\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=\iint_{U^{\prime}_{r,\tau}}([u]_{h}^{R}\circ\Phi^{-1}(y,s))((\partial_{s}\varphi)\circ\Phi^{-1}((y,s))\,dy\,d\tau

\displaystyle=\iint_{U^{\prime}_{r,\tau}}[u\circ\Phi^{-1}]_{h}(y,\tau)((\partial_{s}\varphi)\circ\Phi^{-1}(y,\tau))\,dy\,d\tau

\displaystyle=\iint_{U^{\prime}_{r,\tau}}[u\circ\Phi^{-1}]_{h}(y,\tau)\partial_{s}(\varphi\circ\Phi^{-1}(y,\tau))\,dy\,d\tau

\displaystyle-\iint_{U^{\prime}_{r,\tau}}[u\circ\Phi^{-1}]_{h}(y,\tau)\zeta\cdot(D\varphi)\circ\Phi^{-1}(y,\tau)\,dy\,d\tau,

where we used that
\partial_{t}(\varphi\circ\Phi^{-1})=\partial_{t}\varphi\circ\Phi^{-1}+\zeta\cdot D\varphi\circ\Phi^{-1}
.
Further, we have

\displaystyle\iint_{U^{\prime}_{r,\tau}}[u\circ\Phi^{-1}]_{h}(y,\tau)\partial_{\tau}(\varphi\circ\Phi^{-1}(y,\tau))\,dy\,d\tau

\displaystyle=-\iint_{U^{\prime}_{r,\tau}}\partial_{\tau}[u\circ\Phi^{-1}]_{h}(y,\tau)\varphi\circ\Phi^{-1}(y,\tau)\,dy\,d\tau

\displaystyle=-\iint_{U^{\prime}_{r,\tau}}\frac{u\circ\Phi^{-1}(y,\tau+h)-u\circ\Phi^{-1}(y,\tau)}{h}\varphi\circ\Phi^{-1}(y,\tau)\,dy\,d\tau

\displaystyle=-\iint_{U^{\prime}_{r,\tau}}\frac{u\circ\Phi^{-1}\circ f(y,\tau)-u\circ\Phi^{-1}\circ id\,(y,\tau)}{h}\varphi\circ\Phi^{-1}(y,\tau)\,dy\,d\tau

where
f(y,\tau)=(y,\tau+h).

Combining the last two displays (and using again that
[u\circ\Phi^{-1}]_{h}=[u]_{h}^{R}\circ\Phi^{-1}
),
we have

\displaystyle\iint_{U_{r,\tau}}[u]_{h}^{R}(x,t)\partial_{t}\varphi(x,t)\,dz

\displaystyle=-\iint_{U^{\prime},_{r,\tau}}\frac{u\circ\Phi^{-1}\circ f(y,\tau)-u\circ\Phi^{-1}\circ id\,(y,\tau)}{h}\varphi\circ\Phi^{-1}\circ id\,(y,\tau)\,dy\,d\tau.

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-\iint_{U^{\prime}_{r,\tau}}([u]_{h}^{R}\circ\Phi^{-1}\circ id\,(y,\tau))\zeta\cdot((D\varphi)\circ\Phi^{-1}\circ id\,(y,\tau))\,dy\,d\tau

\displaystyle=\iint_{U^{\prime}_{r,\tau}}\varphi(x+t\zeta,t)\frac{u(x+t\zeta,t+h)-u(x+t\zeta,t)}{h}\,dz-\iint_{U_{r,\tau}}[u]^{R}_{h}\zeta\cdot D\varphi\,dz

\displaystyle=\iint_{U_{r,\tau}}\Phi\circ\varphi(x+t\zeta,t)\frac{\Phi\circ u(x+t\zeta,t+h)-\Phi\circ u(x+t\zeta,t)}{h}\,dz

\displaystyle-\iint_{U_{r,\tau}}(\Phi\circ[u]^{R}_{h})\zeta\cdot(\Phi\circ D\varphi)\,dz

\displaystyle=\iint_{U_{r,\tau}}\varphi(x-t\zeta+t\zeta,t)\frac{u(x-(t+h)\zeta+t\zeta,t+h)-u(x-t\zeta+t\zeta,t)}{h}\,dz

\displaystyle-\iint_{U_{r,\tau}}[u]^{R}_{h}\zeta\cdot D\varphi\,dz

\displaystyle=-\iint_{U_{r,\tau}}\varphi(x,t)\frac{u(x+h\zeta,t+h)-u(x,t)}{h}\,dx\,dt-\iint_{U_{r,\tau}}[u]_{h}^{R}(x,t)\zeta\cdot(D\varphi)(x,t)\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=\iint_{U_{r,\tau}}\varphi(x,t)\frac{u(x,t)-u(x+h\zeta,t+h)}{h}\,dx\,dt+\iint_{U_{r,\tau}}\varphi D[u]_{h}^{R}\cdot\zeta\,dz,

where we used the change of variable,

\displaystyle u\circ\Phi^{-1}\circ f(y,\tau)=u\circ\Phi^{-1}\circ f\circ\Phi(x,t)

\displaystyle=u\circ\Phi^{-1}(f(x-t\zeta,t))

\displaystyle=u(\Phi^{-1}(x-t\zeta,t+h))

\displaystyle=u((x-t\zeta)+(t+h)\zeta,t+h)=u(x+h\zeta,t+h),

and the same computation holds for
f=id.

This completes the proof of

-\partial_{t}[u]^{R}_{h}=\frac{u(x,t)-u(x+h\zeta,t+h)}{h}+D[u]_{h}^{R}\cdot\zeta

in the weak sense.
∎

\displaystyle a(x,t)\leq C_{a}a(x+(s-t)\zeta,s)\quad\text{for all }(x,t),(x,s)\in U_{R,S}(z_{0})\text{ with }t\leq s.

Lemma 2.5

(Caccioppoli inequality I)
.

Let
u
be a weak solution to (
1.1
) and let

satisfy (
2.8
). Then, for
U_{R,S}(z_{0})\subset\Omega_{T}
,

and

depending on

and

such that the following inequality holds:

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in(t_{0}-\tau.t_{0}+\tau)}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\frac{|u-(u)_{U_{r,\tau}(z_{0})}|^{2}}{\tau}\;dx+{{{\int\hskip-7.96674pt\int}\hskip-15.93347pt-\hskip-4.26773pt-}_{\hskip-3.69899ptU_{r,\tau}(z_{0})}}H(z,|Du|)\;dz

\displaystyle\qquad\leq c_{E}{{{\int\hskip-7.96674pt\int}\hskip-15.93347pt-\hskip-4.26773pt-}_{\hskip-3.69899ptU_{R,S}(z_{0})}}H\left(z,\frac{|u-(u)_{U_{R,S}(z_{0})}|}{R-r}\right)dz+c_{E}{{{\int\hskip-7.96674pt\int}\hskip-15.93347pt-\hskip-4.26773pt-}_{\hskip-3.69899ptU_{R,S}(z_{0})}}\frac{|u-(u)_{U_{R,S}(z_{0})}|^{2}}{S-\tau}\;dz.

Proof.

Step 1:
Suppose that
\varphi\in W^{H}(U_{R,S}(z_{0}))\cap C(\overline{U}_{R,S}(z_{0}))
with
\supp\varphi\Subset U_{R,S}(z_{0})
. Then the Steklov average
[\varphi]^{R}_{h}
and
[\varphi]^{L}_{h}
are well defined for small enough

. Moreover,
[\varphi]^{R}_{h}\in W^{H}(U_{R,S}(z_{0}))
by Lemma

.
Further, set

([\varphi]^{R}_{h})_{\delta}(x,t):=\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}}[\varphi]^{R}_{h}(y,t)\rho_{\delta}(y-x)\,dy.

Then by space approximation from Lemma

we have that

(2.9)

\iint_{U_{R,S}(z_{0})}\left|D([\varphi]^{R}_{h})_{\delta}-D[\varphi]^{R}_{h}\right|^{p}+a(z)\left|D([\varphi]^{R}_{h})_{\delta}-D[\varphi]^{R}_{h}\right|^{q}\,dx\,dt\rightarrow 0\quad\text{as }\delta\rightarrow 0.

Since
([\varphi]^{R}_{h})_{\delta}
is Lipschitz and compactly supported
in
U_{R,S}(z_{0})
, it can be used as a test function in the weak formulation Definition
1.2
. Thus, we obtain the following.

(2.10)

\displaystyle\iint_{U_{R,S}(z_{0})}-u\partial_{t}([\varphi]^{R}_{h})_{\delta}+A(z,u,Du)\cdot D([\varphi]^{R}_{h})_{\delta}\,dx\,dt=0.

Now, observe that by Hölder’s inequality and the growth condition (
1.3

\displaystyle\Lambda=\lambda^{p}_{\mathfrak{z}}+a(\mathfrak{z})\lambda^{q}_{\mathfrak{z}}
