Torsion cycles on Fermat varieties
Ramesh Sreekantan

Abstract
A theorem of Manin and Drinfeld [ Man72 ] , [ Dri73 ] states that any divisor of degree 0 0 on the cusps of a modular curve is torsion in the Jacobian. An elegant proof of this result was provided by Elkik [ Elk90 ] using mixed Hodge theory. Rohrlich [ Roh77 ] proved a generalization of this to Fermat curves. In this note we reprove his results along the lines of the work of Elkik. We then use the same methods to generalize it to higher codimensional null homologous cycles as well as higher Chow cycles on Fermat varieties. Mathematical Sciences Classification (2020): 14C25, 19E15, 14C30.



1
Introduction.

A well known theorem of Manin and Drinfeld
[
Man72
,
Dri73
]
states that any divisor of degree
0
supported on the cusps of a modular curve is torsion in the Jacobian. This was generalized to Fermat curves by Rohrlich
[
Roh77
]
. He proved the following theorem

Theorem 1.1

(Rohrlich)
.

Let
F_{d}=F_{d}^{1}
be the Fermat curve of degree
d
.

X_{0}^{d}+X_{1}^{d}+X_{2}^{d}=0.

Let
S_{d}
be the set of
3d
‘Fermat points’ - which are points determined by the hyperplanes
X_{i}=0
. These are the ‘trivial’ solutions to Fermats Last Theorem. Then any divisor of degree
0
supported on
S_{d}
is torsion is the Jacobian. That is, if
D=\sum_{P\in S_{d}}a_{P}P
such that
\sum_{P\in S_{d}}a_{P}=0
then there exists a function
f\in K(F_{d})
such that for some
k\in{\mathds{N}}
,

\operatorname{div}(f)=kD

There is an elegant ‘pure thought’ proof of the original Manin-Drinfeld theorem due to Deligne and Elkik
[
Elk90
]
. The idea is that the Abel-Jacobi map can be described in terms of extensions of mixed Hodge structures. Further, if
X
is a modular curve,
S
the set of cusps and
Y=X-S
the open curve, then the rational Hodge structure on
H^{1}(Y)
is a direct sum of the Hodge structure of pure weight
1
on
H^{1}(X)
and a Hodge structure of pure weight
2
. Once one knows this a short argument (Proposition
2.3
) shows that any divisor of degree
0
is torsion.

In this short paper we show how the same idea can be applied to get a new proof of Rohrlich’s theorem - though not quite as explicit as his is. However, one advantage is that this can be generalized to higher co-dimensions with only a little more work, though we get a weaker statement - only torsion in the intermediate Jacobian.

A linear subvariety of a Fermat hypersurface is a subvariety obtained by intersection with hyperplane sections. We have the following theorem (Theorem
3.1
, Corollary
3.2
).

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
F^{n}_{d}
be the Fermat hypersurface of degree
d
and dimension
n
. Let
S_{d}^{p}=\cup_{|I|=p}C_{I}
be the union of all the linear subvarieties of codimension
p
. Then the exact sequence

0\rightarrow H^{2p-1}(F^{n}_{d},{\mathds{Q}})\rightarrow H^{2p-1}(F^{n}_{d}-S_{d}^{p},{\mathds{Q}})\rightarrow H^{2p}_{S_{d}^{p}}(F_{d}^{n},{\mathds{Q}})^{0}\rightarrow 0

is a split exact sequence of Hodge structures. Here

H^{2p}_{S_{d}^{p}}(F_{d}^{n},{\mathds{Q}})^{0}=\operatorname{Ker}\{H^{2p}_{S_{d}^{p}}(F^{n}_{d},{\mathds{Q}})\rightarrow H^{2p}(F^{n}_{d},{\mathds{Q}})\}

is the kernel of the cycle class map and is generated by linear combinations of the linear subvarieties which are null-homologous.

As a result, any null-homologous cycle supported on
S_{d}^{p}
is torsion in the
p^{th}
-intermediate Jacobian.

A conjecture of Beilinson and Bloch asserts that the Abel-Jacobi map is injective for varieties defined over number fields, hence this would imply that the cycle is actually torsion in the Chow group itself. That is the case when
n=1
, which is Rohrlich’s theorem.

We then show that the same methods can be used to obtain a statement about certain higher Chow cycles having torsion regulator (Theorem
4.2
, Corollary
4.3
)

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
y
be a higher Chow cycle in the group
CH^{p}(F_{d}^{n},q)_{{\mathds{Q}}}=H^{2p-q}(F_{d}^{n},{\mathds{Q}}(p))
,

y=\sum(Z_{I},f_{I_{i}},\dots,f_{I_{q}})

where
Z_{I}
are linear subvarieties of codimension
p-q
and
f_{I_{j}}
are functions on
Z_{I}
with divisorial support on linear subvarieties of
Z_{I}
. Then the regulator
\operatorname{reg}(y)
is
0
in the Deligne cohomology group
H^{2p-q}_{{\mathcal{D}}}(F_{d}^{n},{\mathds{Q}}(p))
.

2
Hodge structures.

2.1
Mixed Hodge structures.

According to Deligne
[
Del71
]
, a mixed Hodge structure (MHS) is an Abelian group
H_{{\mathds{Z}}}
with two filtrations.

•

An increasing
weight filtration

W_{\bullet}
on
H_{{\mathds{Q}}}=H_{{\mathds{Z}}}\otimes{\mathds{Q}}
.

•

A decreasing
Hodge filtration

F^{\bullet}
on
H_{{\mathds{C}}}=H_{{\mathds{Z}}}\otimes{\mathds{C}}
.

such that the graded pieces
Gr^{W_{\bullet}}_{l}=W_{l}/W_{l-1}

have a pure Hodge structure of weight
l
.

2.2
Extensions of Mixed Hodge structures.

An extension of
B
by
A
, where
A
and
B
are MHSs, is a short exact sequence of mixed Hodge structures

e(B,A):0\rightarrow A\rightarrow E\rightarrow B\rightarrow 0

e(B,A)
determines a class in the set
\operatorname{Ext}_{MHS}(B,A)=\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{MHS}(B,A)
. The set
\operatorname{Ext}_{MHS}^{1}(B,A)
is a group under the Bär sum. A class is trivial if and only if the exact sequence splits.

An extension is called a
separated
extension of MHS if the highest weight of
A
is less than the lowest weight of
B
. Carlson
[
Car80
]
showed that in this case that group can be identified with a generalized torus

Theorem 2.1

(Carlson)
.

If
A
and
B
are mixed Hodge structures such that the highest weight of
A
is less than the lowest weight of
B
- so any
e(B,A)
in
\operatorname{Ext}(B,A)
is separated. Then

\operatorname{Ext}_{MHS}(B,A)=\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathds{C}}}(B,A)/(F^{0}\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathds{C}}}(B,A)+\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathds{Z}}}(B,A))

2.3
Extensions and the Abel-Jacobi map.

Suppose
X
is a smooth projective variety and
D=\sum a_{i}D_{i}
is a codimensional
p
cycle. Let
|D|=\cup D_{i}
denote the support of the cycle. Let
Y=X-|D|
. Then one has a long exact sequence of cohomology with support

\dots\rightarrow H^{2p-1}(X,{\mathds{Z}})\rightarrow H^{2p-1}(Y,{\mathds{Z}})\rightarrow H^{2p}_{|D|}(X,{\mathds{Z}})\rightarrow H^{2p}(X,{\mathds{Z}})\rightarrow\dots

The term
H^{2p}_{|D|}(X,{\mathds{Z}})
is pure of weight
2p
. Hence the cohomology group
H^{2p-1}
has the weight filtration

•

W_{2p-2}=\{0\}\subset W_{2p-1}=i^{*}(H^{2p-1}(X,{\mathds{Q}})\subset W_{2p}=H^{2p-1}(Y,{\mathds{Q}})

•

Gr_{2p-1}^{W_{\bullet}}=W_{2p-1}=i^{*}(H^{2p-1}(X,{\mathds{Q}})

•

Gr_{2p}^{W_{\bullet}}=H^{2p}_{|D|}(X,{\mathds{Q}})^{0}
, where

H^{2p}_{|D|}(X,{\mathds{Q}})^{0}=\operatorname{Ker}\{H^{2p}_{|D|}(X,{\mathds{Q}})\rightarrow H^{2p}(X,{\mathds{Q}})\}

If
Z
is a null-homologous cycle supported on
|D|
then it determines a copy
{\mathds{Z}}\cdot Z
of
{\mathds{Z}}(-p)\subset H^{2p}_{|D|}(X,{\mathds{Z}})
which maps to
0
in
H^{2p}(X,{\mathds{Z}})
and induces a short exact sequence

0\rightarrow H^{2p-1}(X,{\mathds{Z}})\rightarrow E_{Z}\rightarrow{\mathds{Z}}(-p)\rightarrow 0

and
E_{Z}\in\operatorname{Ext}_{MHS}({\mathds{Z}}(-p),H^{2p-1}(X))
.

This is a separated extension of mixed Hodge structures. From Carlson’s theorem we can see that
\operatorname{Ext}_{MHS}({\mathds{Z}}(-p),H^{2p-1}(X))
is the same as the
p^{th}
intermediate Jacobian.

The
(higher) Abel-Jacobi map
is given by the association

Z\rightarrow[E_{Z}]\in\operatorname{Ext}_{MHS}({\mathds{Z}}(-p),H^{2p-1}(X)).

In particular, if
p=1
this is the usual Abel-Jacobi map to the Jacobian of
X
. Hence we have

Lemma 2.2
.

A divisor
Z
of degree
0
is the divisor of a function if and only if the exact sequence

0\rightarrow H^{1}(X)\rightarrow E_{Z}\rightarrow{\mathds{Z}}(-1)\rightarrow 0

splits as an extension of MHSs. More generally it is torsion in the Jacobian if and only if the sequence of
{\mathds{Q}}
-MHSs splits.

This argument can be generalized to the following

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety and
|D|=\cup D_{i}
a union of codimensional
p
subvarieties. Let
Y=X-|D|
. Let

H^{2p}_{|D|}(X,{\mathds{Q}})^{0}=\operatorname{Ker}\{H^{2p}_{|D|}(X,{\mathds{Q}})\rightarrow H^{2p}(X,{\mathds{Q}})\}

Then any null-homologous divisor
Z
supported on
D
is torsion in the
p^{th}
intermediate Jacobian if and only if the exact sequence

0\rightarrow H^{2p-1}(X,{\mathds{Q}})\rightarrow H^{2p-1}(Y,{\mathds{Q}})\rightarrow H^{2p}_{|D|}(X,{\mathds{Q}})^{0}\rightarrow 0

splits.

Proof.

If
Z
is a null-homologous cycle supported on
|D|
then it determines a copy of
{\mathds{Q}}(-p)
in
H^{2p}_{|D|}(X,{\mathds{Q}})
. If the exact sequence is split then the same splitting implies that the class of
E_{Z}
is split. Conversely,
H^{2p}_{|D|}(X,{\mathds{Q}})^{0}
is generated by
{\mathds{Q}}\cdot(D_{i}-D_{j})|i\neq j
so if
E_{D_{i}-D_{j}}
is trivial for all
i,j
then the sequence

0\rightarrow H^{2p-1}(X,{\mathds{Q}})\rightarrow H^{2p-1}(Y,{\mathds{Q}})\rightarrow H^{2p}_{|D|}(X,{\mathds{Q}})^{0}\rightarrow 0

is split.
∎

For higher codimensional varieties it is not necessarily the case that a cycle which is trivial in the intermediate Jacobian is trivial in the Chow group. Hence from the splitting of the exact sequence one can only conclude that the cycle is
0
in the intermediate Jacobian or is torsion if the
{\mathds{Q}}
-mixed Hodge structure splits. A conjecture of Bloch
[
Blo84
]
and Beilinson asserts that if the variety is defined over a number field, such a cycle is necessarily torsion in the Chow group as well.

3
Fermat varieties.

3.1
Rohrlich’s theorem and its generalizations.

From Proposition
2.3
, in order to prove Rohrlich’s theorem we have to prove that the sequence

0\rightarrow H^{1}(F_{d},{\mathds{Q}})\rightarrow H^{1}(F_{d}-S_{d},{\mathds{Q}})\rightarrow H^{2}_{|S_{d}|}(F_{d},{\mathds{Q}})^{0}\otimes{\mathds{Q}}\rightarrow 0

is split as a sequence of mixed Hodge structures. Equivalently we need to find a complement to the image of
H^{1}(F_{d},{\mathds{Q}})
in
H^{1}(F_{d}-S_{d},{\mathds{Q}})
which is a pure Hodge structure of weight
2
.

We first need some definitions. Let
F^{n}_{d}
be the
Fermat hypersurface of dimension
n
and degree
d
in
{\mathds{P}}^{n+1}
:

F^{n}_{d}:X_{0}^{d}+X_{1}^{d}+\dots+X_{n+1}^{d}=0

For
I=\{i_{1},\dots,i_{p}\}\subset\{0,\dots,n+1\}
let
C_{I}=H_{i_{1}}\cap H_{i_{2}}\dots\cap H_{i_{p}}\cap F^{n}_{d}
, where
H_{k}
is the hyperplane
X_{k}=0
.
C_{I}
is a subvariety isomorphic to the Fermat variety
F_{d}^{n-p}
and lies in
CH^{p}(F_{d}^{n})
. We call an irreducible component of such a subvariety a
linear subvariety of codimension
p
. If
n-p>1
the linear subvarieties are irreducible so its only the
n=p
case in which there could be components. However, as we also assume that
p=\frac{n+1}{2}
this leaves us with only the case when
n=1
, namely the case of Rohrlich’s theorem that is different.

We have the following theorem

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
F^{n}_{d}
be the Fermat hypersurface of dimension
n
and degree
d
. Let
S_{d}^{p}=\cup_{|I|=p}C_{I}
be the union of all the linear subvarieties of codimension
p
. Then the exact sequence

0\rightarrow H^{2p-1}(F^{n}_{d},{\mathds{Q}})\rightarrow H^{2p-1}(F^{n}_{d}-S_{d}^{p},{\mathds{Q}})\rightarrow H^{2p}_{S_{d}^{p}}(F_{d}^{n},{\mathds{Q}})^{0}\rightarrow 0

is a split exact sequence of Hodge structures. Here

H^{2p}_{S_{d}^{p}}(F_{d}^{n},{\mathds{Q}})^{0}=\operatorname{Ker}\{H^{2p}_{S_{d}^{p}}(F^{n}_{d},{\mathds{Q}})\rightarrow H^{2p}(F^{n}_{d},{\mathds{Q}})\}

is the kernel of the cycle class map and is generated by linear combinations of the linear subvarieties which are null-homologous.

We get the following corollary:

Corollary 3.2
.

Any null homologous cycle supported on the set of linear subvarieties is torsion in the intermediate Jacobian.

In particular, when
n=1
and
p=1
this is Rohrlich’s theorem. Note that if
n>1
the linear subvarieties are all irreducible but in the case of curves they are not.

In order to prove the splitting we have to understand the cohomology of
F_{d}^{n}
. For that we follow Shioda
[
Shi79
]
, who proved the Hodge conjecture for such varieties under some conditions.

Let
\mu_{d}
denote the group of
d^{th}
roots of unity. The group
\mu_{d}^{n+2}
acts on
F_{d}^{n}
by

(\zeta_{0},\dots,\zeta_{n+1})\cdot[X_{0}:\dots:X_{n+1}]=[\zeta_{0}X_{0}:\dots:\zeta_{n+1}X_{n+1}]

The diagonal acts trivially. Let
G_{d}^{n}=\mu_{d}^{n+2}/(diag)\simeq\mu_{d}^{n+1}
. The dual group of
G_{d}^{n}
can be identified with

\hat{G}^{n}_{d}=\{\chi=(a_{0},\dots,a_{n+1})|a_{i}\in{\mathds{Z}}/d{\mathds{Z}}\text{ such that }\sum a_{i}=0\operatorname{mod}d\}

\chi((\zeta_{0},\dots,\zeta_{n+1}))=\prod\zeta_{i}^{a_{i}}.

The action of
G^{n}_{d}
on the Fermat varieties induces an action on related cohomology groups. Since
G^{n}_{d}
also preserves
S_{d}^{p}
it also induces a group action on the cohomology group
H^{2p}_{S_{d}^{p}}(F_{d}^{n},{\mathds{Z}})
.

3.2
G_{d}^{n}
-module structure of
H^{n}(F_{d}^{n},{\mathds{C}})
.

We want to understand the decomposition of the cohomology groups
H^{i}(F^{n}_{d},{\mathds{Q}})
,
H^{i}(F^{n}_{d}-S_{d}^{p},{\mathds{Q}})
and
H^{i}_{S_{d}^{p}}(F_{d}^{n},{\mathds{Q}})
as
G_{d}^{n}
representations.

From the Lefschetz hyperplane theorem we know that

H^{i}(F_{d}^{n},{\mathds{Q}})=\begin{cases}\{0\}&i\neq n\text{ odd}\\
{\mathds{Q}}&i\neq n\text{ even}\end{cases}

Hence the only ‘interesting’ cohomology is when
i=n
.

Recall that the primitive cohomology is the orthogonal complement of the hyperplane class. From above, all the primitive cohomology groups are trivial for
i\neq n
. When
n
is odd,
H^{n}_{prim}(F_{d}^{n},{\mathds{Q}})\simeq H^{n}(F_{d}^{n},{\mathds{Q}})
but when
n
is even the primitive cohomology is of codimension
1
.

For a character
\chi=\chi(a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{n+1})
, let
V(\chi)
denote corresponding
1
-dimensional representation. Shioda has the following theorem

Theorem 3.3

(Shioda)
.

Let
A_{d}^{n}\subset\hat{G}_{d}^{n}
be the subset

A_{d}^{n}=\{\chi=\chi(a_{0},\dots,a_{n+1})|\,a_{i}\neq 0\text{ for all }i\}

we have

H^{n}_{prim}(F_{d}^{n},{\mathds{C}})\simeq\bigoplus_{\chi\in A^{n}_{d}}V(\chi)

Null-homologous cycles of codimension
p
determine an extension in

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{MHS}({\mathds{Q}}(-p),H^{2p-1}(F_{d}^{n},{\mathds{Q}})).

If
2p-1\neq n
then the cohomology group is trivial. So we can restrict ourselves to the case when
n=2p-1
is odd and in this case
H^{n}_{prim}=H^{n}
. In the section on motivic cycles we will need to consider the case when
n
is even.

This describes the action on the weight
(2p-1)
part of the Hodge structure of
H^{2p-1}(F_{d}^{n}-S_{d}^{p},{\mathds{Q}})
.

3.3
G_{d}^{n}
-module structure of
H^{2p}_{S_{d}^{p}}(F_{d}^{n},{\mathds{Q}})
.

To understand the
Gr_{2p}^{W_{\bullet}}
part of the Hodge structure we have to understand the
G_{d}^{n}
action on
H^{2p}_{S_{d}^{p}}(F_{d}^{n},{\mathds{Q}})
. This is the free
{\mathds{Q}}
-vector space generated by the classes of
C_{I}
in
H^{2p}(F_{d}^{n},{\mathds{Q}})
, where
I=\{i_{0},\dots,i_{p-1}\}
is a subset of
\{0,\dots,n+1\}
of cardinality
|I|=p
. Recall that we can assume
p=(n+1)/2
.

There are two cases:

Case 1:
n=1
. Here the linear subvarieties are the cuspidal points. Let
P
be a cuspidal point in
C_{I}
for some
I
. Here
C_{I}=C_{\{i\}}
for
i\in\{0,1,2\}
so we will just use
i
.

For any
P
in
C_{i}
, the subgroup

G_{P}=1\times\dots\times\mu_{d}\times\dots 1

with
\mu_{d}
in the
i
coordinate fixes
P
. The orbit of
P
under the group
G_{d}^{1}
is
C_{i}
. Note that
G_{P}
is the same for all
P
in
C_{i}
, so we denote it by
G_{i}
.

We would like to understand the representation
V_{I}
corresponding to this orbit. Since
G_{P}=G_{i}
acts trivially on
P
, the representation is

V_{i}=\operatorname{Ind}_{G_{i}}^{G_{d}^{1}}({\mathds{1}})

From Frobenius reciprocity the representation decomposes into those characters which restrict to the trivial character of
G_{i}

V_{i}=\oplus_{\chi|_{G_{i}}={\mathds{1}}}V(\chi)

These are those characters where
a_{i}=0
.

Let
B_{i}
be the set of characters such that
a_{i}=0
.

H^{2}_{S_{d}^{1}}(F_{d}^{1},{\mathds{Q}})=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{2}\bigoplus_{\chi\in B_{i}}V(\chi)

Note that
B_{i}\cap A_{d}^{n}=\emptyset
for all
i
.

Case 2:
n>1
. In this case the
C_{I}
are irreducible.
G_{d}^{n}
preserves
C_{I}
for any
C_{I}
so the action on the class of
C_{I}
is trivial. Hence
G_{d}^{n}
acts trivially on
H^{2p}_{S_{d}^{p}}(F_{d}^{n},{\mathds{Q}})
.

3.4
Splitting of the Hodge Structure.

We can now complete the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Proof.

We first deal with the case
n>1
. Consider the element

T=\frac{1}{|G_{d}^{n}|}\sum_{g\in G_{d}^{n}}g

acting on
H^{2p-1}(F^{n}_{d}-S_{d}^{p},{\mathds{Q}})
. Since

H^{2p-1}(F_{d}^{n},{\mathds{C}})\simeq\bigoplus_{\chi\in A^{n}_{d}}V(\chi)

for
v\in V(\chi)
,

T(v)=\frac{1}{|G_{d}^{n}|}\sum_{g\in G_{d}^{n}}g\cdot v=\frac{1}{|G_{d}^{n}|}\sum_{g\in G_{d}^{n}}\chi(g)v=0

since
\chi
is a non-trivial character.

Define a map

H^{2p}_{S_{d}^{p}}(F_{d}^{n},{\mathds{Q}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{2p}_{S_{d}^{p}}(F_{d}^{n},{\mathds{Q}})

\phi(v_{0})=T(v)\text{ where }v\in\partial^{-1}(v_{0})

This is well defined as if
v,v^{\prime}\in\partial^{-1}(v_{0})
,
v-v^{\prime}\in H^{2p-1}(F_{d}^{n},{\mathds{Q}})
so
T(v-v^{\prime})=-0
.

Hence
H^{n}(F_{d}^{n},{\mathds{C}})\subset\operatorname{Ker}(T)
.
T
is also a morphism of Hodge structures as it is induced by an automorphism of
F_{d}^{n}
. Further, since
G_{d}^{n}
acts trivially on
H^{2p}_{S_{d}^{p}}(F_{d}^{n},{\mathds{Q}})
,
T
acts by multiplication by
|G_{d}^{n}|=d^{n+1}
. The image of
T
is a pure Hodge substructure of weight
2p
of
H^{2p-1}(F^{n}_{d}-S_{d}^{p},{\mathds{Q}})
and is isomorphic to
H^{2p}_{S_{d}^{p}}(F_{d}^{n},{\mathds{Q}})
and complementary to
H^{2p-1}(F^{n}_{d},{\mathds{Q}})
. Hence it provides a splitting of the exact sequence.

When
n=1
the argument is similar. Let

T=\sum_{|I|=p}\sum_{\chi\in B_{I}}p_{\chi}

where
p_{\chi}
is the projector corresponding to the character
\chi
.
Since
B_{I}\cap A_{d}^{n}
are disjoint
T
projects on to the weight two piece of the Hodge structure on
H^{1}(F_{d}^{n}-S_{d}^{1},{\mathds{C}})

∎

Remark 3.4
.

The case when
n=2p-1
is the only case where one expects potentially interesting cycles. For instance
CH^{1}_{hom}(F_{d}^{1}),CH^{2}_{hom}(F_{d}^{3}),CH^{3}_{hom}(F_{d}^{5})
and in fact there do exist interesting non-torsion cycles in these groups as we will see in Section
5
.

4
Motivic cycles.

A motivic cycle, or equivalently a higher Chow cycle, in the group
CH^{p}(X,q)=H^{2p-q}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(X,{\mathds{Z}}(p))
is a codimensional
p
cycle on
\Delta^{q}X=X\times\Delta^{q}
, where
\Delta^{q}
is the
q
-simplex

\Delta^{q}=\{(t_{0},\dots,t_{q})\in{\mathds{A}}^{q+1}|\sum t_{i}=1\}

subject to certain conditions with respect to the faces
[
Blo86
,
Sch93a
]
.

In some cases these cycles can be represented by

\sum(Z_{i},f_{i_{1}},f_{i_{2}},\dots f_{i_{q}})

where
Z_{i}
are codimensional
p
cycles on
X\times{\mathds{A}}^{q}
and
f_{i_{j}}
are functions on
Z_{i}
subject to the vanishing of the tame symbol of the functions. In general, such a cycle will determine a higher Chow cycle but it is not necessarily the case that all higher Chow cycles have such a presentation. However in what follows we will only consider cycles of this type.

A special case is when
q=1
. A cycle in the group
CH^{p}(X,1)=H^{2p-1}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(X,{\mathds{Z}}(p))
is represented by a sum

\sum(Z_{i},f_{i})

where
Z_{i}
are subvarieties of codimensional
p-1
and
f_{i}
are functions on
C_{i}
subject to the condition that
\sum\operatorname{div}(f_{i})=0
. We will primarily focus on this group though our arguments should work in general.

4.1
Fermat varieties.

In the case when
X
is a Fermat hypersurface we can use Corollary
3.2
to construct motivic cycles.

Let
Z
be a linear subvariety of the type
C_{I}
for some
I\subset\{0,1,\dots,n+1\}
as before. Let
\{J\}
be the sets such that
I\subset J
and
|J|=|I|+1
. Any null homologous divisor on
C_{I}
supported on the
C_{J}
is torsion in the Jacobian as if
\operatorname{dim}(C_{I})>1
then
H^{1}(F^{n}_{d},{\mathds{Q}})=\{0\}
and if
\operatorname{dim}(C_{I})=1
we can apply Rohrlich’s theorem. Hence it is not very difficult to find functions.

Using these functions we can construct higher Chow cycles. For instance, in the group
CH^{p}(X,1)
a cycle is represented by a sum
\sum(Z_{i},f_{i})
with
\sum\operatorname{div}(f_{i})=0
. We can construct cycles as follows.

Let
I=\{i_{1},\dots,i_{p-2}\}
be a subset of
\{0,\dots,n+1\}
. Let
a,b,c
be three elements of the complement of
I
in
\{0,\dots,n+1\}
and consider the linear subvarieties of the type
C_{a}=C_{I\cup\{a\}}
and

On

More generally one can construct cycles in

There are some trivial elements in the higher Chow groups of the form

4.2
Extensions and motivic cycles.

Analogously to the situation of null-homologous cycles one can associate an extension to a motivic cycle
[
Sch93b
]
.

A conjecture of Beilinson asserts that if
X
is a smooth projective variety then

CH^{p}(X,q)_{{\mathds{Q}}}=H^{2p-q}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(X,{\mathds{Q}}(p))=\operatorname{Ext}_{{\mathcal{M}}}({\mathds{Q}}(-p),h^{2p-q-1}(X,{\mathds{Q}}))

where
{\mathcal{M}}
is the conjectured category of mixed motives.

It is known that in the category of mixed Hodge structures,

\operatorname{Ext}_{MHS}({\mathds{Q}}(-p),H^{2p-q-1}(X,{\mathds{Q}}))=H^{2p-q}_{{\mathcal{D}}}(X,{\mathds{Q}}(p)).

The term on the right is the Deligne cohomology group which is the analogue of the Jacobian and there is a map called the regulator map from motivic cohomology to Deligne cohomology analogous to the Abel-Jacobi map.

A motivic cycle, therefore, determines an extension in the conjectural category of mixed motives as well as determines an extension in the category of mixed Hodge structures and the realization in the category of mixed Hodge structures is the regulator map.

To see how one associates an extension to a motivic cycle we follow Scholl
[
Sch93b
]
, Section 3.1. Let
\Sigma^{q}X
be the strict augmented simplicial scheme over
X
.

\Delta^{q}X\leftarrow\bigsqcup^{[0,q]}\Delta^{q-1}X\mathrel{\begin{subarray}{c}\leftarrow\\
\leftarrow\end{subarray}}\bigsqcup^{[1,q]}\cdots\mathrel{\begin{subarray}{c}\leftarrow\\
\cdot\\
\cdot\\
\leftarrow\end{subarray}}\bigsqcup^{[q-1,q]}\Delta^{0}X

Higher Chow groups or motivic cohomology groups can be defined using these schemes. For any suitable cohomology theory
h^{*}_{\_}(\_)
that extends to simplicial schemes with support, one has an exact sequence

0\rightarrow h^{2p-1}(\Sigma^{q}X)\rightarrow h^{2p-1}(\Sigma^{q}X-|D|_{\bullet})\rightarrow h^{2p}_{|D|_{\bullet}}(\Sigma^{q}X)\rightarrow h^{2p}(\Sigma^{q}X)

where for a higher Chow cycle
y
,
|D|
denotes its support and
|D|_{\bullet}
the closed simplicial subscheme of
\Sigma^{q}X
determined by
|D|
.

Scholl shows that using the tensor category of mixed realizations

of Deligne and Jannsen

]

h^{*}(\Sigma^{q}X)\simeq h^{*-q}(X)

The cycle

4.3
Motivic cycles on Fermat Varieties.

We now show that any motivic cycle supported on the linear hypersurfaces of a Fermat variety have regulator which is torsion in the Deligne cohomology. This is the higher Chow version of Rohrlich’s theorem. The idea, as before, is to show that in this case the exact sequence in Proposition
4.1
splits.

We consider cycles in the group
CH^{p}(F_{d}^{n},q)
with
p-q\geq 0
and
n>1
. The cycles
y
are of the following type:

y=\sum(Z_{I},f_{I_{1}},\dots,f_{I_{q}})

where
Z_{I}
are linear subvarieties of codimension
p-q
corresponding to a subset
I
of
\{0,\dots,n+1\}
and
f_{I_{k}}
are functions on the
Z_{I}
such that their divisors are supported on cycles of the form
Z_{I\cup\{j\}}
for some
j\in\{0,\dots,n+1\},j\notin I
and further they satisfy a co-cycle condition ensuring that the sum is in the motivic cohomology group.

We want to discount the decomposable cycles coming from linear subvarieties. In order to do that we work with primitive cohomology.

The group
G_{d}^{n}
stabilizes
Z_{I}
and the support of the divisors of
f_{I_{j}}
as they are all linear subvarieties. Hence similar to before since
n>1
the group acts trivially on the classes of a higher cycle
y=\sum_{I}(Z_{I},f_{I_{i}},\dots,f_{I_{q}})
in

, where
|D|_{\bullet}
is the simplicial subscheme determined by cycles of the type above.

.

Let

be the Fermat hypersurface of dimension

F^{n}_{d}:X_{0}^{d}+X_{1}^{d}+\dots+X_{n+1}^{d}=0

For

5
Non-torsion cycles on Fermat varieties.

The above theorems show that certain ‘natural’ constructions do not produce interesting cycles on Fermat varieties. This does not mean that the Chow groups of Fermat varieties are trivial.

There is an inductive structure on Fermat Varieties which has interesting implications for cycles on Fermat varieties. There is a rational map

F_{d}^{r}\times F_{d}^{s}\dashrightarrow F^{r+s}_{d}

defined by

[x_{0}:\dots:x_{r+1}],[y_{0}:\dots:y_{s+1}]\dashrightarrow[x_{0}y_{s+1}:\dots:x_{r}y_{s+1}:\epsilon x_{r+1}y_{0}:\dots:\epsilon x_{r+1}y_{s}]

where
\epsilon^{d}=-1
. As a result we have a map

F_{d}^{1}\times F_{d}^{1}\times F_{d}^{1}\dashrightarrow F_{d}^{3}

In the group

[
GS95

e

F_{d}^{1}

\Delta_{e}=\Delta-\Delta_{12}-\Delta_{13}-\Delta_{23}+\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}+\Delta_{3}

where
\Delta_{ij}

\Delta_{i}
is the diagonal with

This is called the Gross-Schoen modified diagonal cycles. It is well known that this cycle is in general non-trivial in the Chow group of null-homologous cycles on

[

We can consider the image of this cycle under the map induced by the inductive structure to get a null-homologous cycle in
