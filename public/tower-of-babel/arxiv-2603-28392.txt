William Graham, Minyoung Jeon, Scott Joseph Larson

Abstract
. Let G = G ​ L ​ ( n ) G=GL(n) and K = G ​ L ​ ( p ) × G ​ L ​ ( q ) K=GL(p)\times GL(q) with p + q = n p+q=n , where the groups are taken over ℂ \mathbb{C} . In this paper we study a certain family of K K -orbit closures on the flag variety X X of G G . The geometry of these orbit closures plays a central role in the infinite-dimensional representation theory of the real Lie group U ⁡ ( p , q ) U(p,q) , and has applications to degeneracy loci and combinatorics. In this paper we use small resolutions to study orbit closures in this family. We prove that the fibers of these resolutions are smooth and strongly reduced, as well as a general result that if a variety has a resolution of singularities with these properties, then its characteristic cycle is irreducible. 2.
Small resolutions and characteristic cycles

In this section we prove a general theorem showing that under certain hypotheses, characteristic cycles
are irreducible. As noted in the introduction, this theorem is closely related to recent work
of Aluffi (see
[
1
]
).

We begin by recalling some facts about characteristic cycles; see
[
21
]
and
[
12
]
for more information.
Suppose
X
is a smooth variety and
X=\bigcup S_{i}
is a Whitney stratification of
X
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be an element of the
derived category of constructible sheaves on
X
, and assume that
\mathcal{F}
is constructible with respect to the stratification
X=\bigcup S_{i}
.
The singular support
SS(\mathcal{F})
is a closed conical Lagrangian subset of
T^{*}X
whose irreducible components
are closures of certain of the conormal bundles
\overline{T^{*}_{S_{i}}X}
to the strata (see
[
12
, Section 4.3]
, where

SS(\mathcal{F})
is denoted
CV(\mathcal{F})
).
The characteristic cycle
\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{F})
is a formal linear combination of cycles
of the form
\sum n_{i}[\overline{T^{*}_{S_{i}}X}]
where
\overline{T^{*}_{S_{i}}X}\subseteq SS(\mathcal{F})
.
Let
Y=\overline{S_{i}}
and
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{I}\mathcal{C}_{Y}
. In this case,
n_{i}=1
, and

\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{I}\mathcal{C}_{Y})=[\overline{T^{*}_{S_{i}}X}]+\sum n_{j}[\overline{T^{*}_{S_{j}}X}]
, where the sum is over
j
such
that

. If
\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{I}\mathcal{C}_{Y})=[\overline{T^{*}_{S_{i}}X}]
, then we call the chararacteristic cycle of
\mathcal{I}\mathcal{C}_{Y}

irreducible or trivial. We remark that if
U_{i}
is the nonsingular locus of
\overline{S_{i}}
, then
U_{i}\supset S_{i}
and

\overline{T^{*}_{S_{i}}X}=\overline{T^{*}_{U_{i}}X}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Suppose that

is a map of smooth varieties. A smooth fiber

if for all

, we have

\ker df_{z}=T_{z}f^{-1}(x)
. If

factors as

Z\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mu}}{{\rightarrow}}Y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota}}{{\rightarrow}}X
, where
\iota
is a closed embedding,
we will sometimes abuse notation and use

to denote

.

This condition appears in
[
4

(although they do not use the term strongly reduced), where it is
observed that strongly reduced implies reduced.

Theorem 2.2
.

Suppose we have a diagram

3.
Generalities on fibers

This section contains some general results (Propositions
3.1
and
3.3
) which we will use to study the fibers
of our resolutions.

Let
G
be an algebraic group. We say a map of
G
-varieties
\xi:Y\to Z
has homogeneous fibers if for all
y\in Y
, the stabilizer group
G^{y}
of
y

acts transitively on
\xi^{-1}(y)
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
G
be an algebraic group and let
\xi:Z\rightarrow Y
be a
G
-equivariant map of
G
-varieties. Suppose that
Y
has finitely many
G
-orbits. Then

\xi
has homogeneous fibers if and only if
\#(G\backslash Z)=\#(G\backslash Y)
.

Proof.

We have
\#(G\backslash Z)=\#(G\backslash Y)
if and only if the inverse image of any
G
-orbit in
Y
is a single
G
-orbit
in
Z
. The
G
-orbits in
\xi^{-1}(G\cdot y)
are in bijection with the
G^{y}
-orbits in
\xi^{-1}(y)
, so
\xi^{-1}(G\cdot y)
is a single

G
-orbit if and only if
\xi^{-1}(y)
is a single
G^{y}
-orbit. The result follows.
∎

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ker}(d\mu_{z})=T_{z}F\mbox{ for all }z\in F

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\mathrm{Ker}(d\tilde{\mu}_{\tilde{z}^{\prime}})=T_{\tilde{z}^{\prime}}\tilde{F}^{\prime}\mbox{ for all }\tilde{z}^{\prime}\in\tilde{F}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\mathrm{Ker}(d\mu_{z^{\prime}})=T_{z^{\prime}}F^{\prime}\mbox{ for all }z^{\prime}\in F^{\prime}.

Here, the first and third equivalences hold by Lemma
3.2
. To see the middle equivalence, let

\rho
denote the map on

or

given by right multiplication by
p
, and let
z^{\prime}=zp=\rho(z)
.
Since
\tilde{\mu}\circ\rho=\rho\circ\tilde{\mu}
, the map

takes

\mathrm{Ker}(d\tilde{\mu}_{\tilde{z}})
to
\mathrm{Ker}(d\tilde{\mu}_{\tilde{z}^{\prime}})
and
T_{\tilde{z}}\tilde{F}

to
T_{\tilde{z}^{\prime}}\tilde{F}^{\prime}
. The middle equivalence follows. This proves (4).
∎

In the setting of the previous proposition, we call

a good base point of the map

is isomorphic to a fiber over a good base point. Note that

. In this case, the set of good base points is

or

,
depending on which expression for

is being considered.

Remark 3.4

In (

Let

Given

Let

Let

4.2.
K
-orbit closures for general
G

The group

with finitely many orbits (see
[
35
]
,
[
25
]
). Write

. If

\mathcal{V}=\{g\in G\;|\;g^{-1}(\theta(g))\in N_{G}(H)\}

\mathcal{V}\hookrightarrow G

Let
\dot{v}=g(v)

G
or
\mathcal{V}
for
v\in V
; by abuse of notation,
we sometimes use the same notation for an element of
V
and a representative in
G
or
\mathcal{V}
.
See
[
39
]
for representatives of

in
G
;
[
38
]
is a good reference for the case

and

Let
G_{v}=\overline{K\dot{v}B}
, and
X_{v}=\overline{K\dot{v}B}/B
. Define the length of
v
to be
\ell(v)=\dim X_{v}
.
Define
\phi:V\to W
by the rule
\phi(v)=g^{-1}(\theta(g))\mbox{ mod }H
, where
g=g(v)\in\mathcal{V}
.

Because

is
\theta
-stable, the involution
\theta
acts on the set
\Phi
of roots; if we view the root
\alpha
as a character of

\theta(\alpha)(h)=\alpha(\theta(h))
.
Given

, we define an involution
\theta_{v}
of

by the formula
\theta_{v}\cdot\alpha=\phi(v)\left(\theta(\alpha)\right)
.
Let
g=g(v)
.
We say a simple root

has

\theta_{v}\cdot\alpha=-\alpha
, (b) compact imaginary if
\theta_{v}\cdot\alpha=\alpha
and
\mathrm{Ad}(g)(Z_{\alpha})\in\mathfrak{k}
, (c) noncompact imaginary if
\theta_{v}\cdot\alpha=\alpha
and
\mathrm{Ad}(g)(Z_{\alpha})\in\mathfrak{p}
, and (d) complex if
\theta_{v}\cdot\alpha\neq\pm\alpha
.
We call
\alpha
of type (d)
a complex ascent if
\theta_{v}\cdot\alpha>0
, and a complex descent if
\theta_{v}\cdot\alpha<0
. (See
[
34
, Definition 2.5]
,
[
31
, §2.4]
, or equivalently,

[
32
]
.) If

for

v
-type of

is the
v
-type of
\alpha
. Note that if
\alpha
is
v
-compact
imaginary, and

is the corresponding standard parabolic subgroup, then for every
\dot{v}\in G
, we have

(4.1)

\dot{v}P_{\alpha}/B\subseteq K\dot{v}B/B

by
[
34
, Lemma 5.1]
(see, e.g.,
[
31
, 2.4]
).

There is a monoid product
*
on
W
given by

w*s=\begin{cases}ws&\mbox{ if }\ell(ws)>\ell(w)\\
w&\mbox{ if }\ell(ws)<\ell(w)\end{cases}

for
w\in W,s\in S
.
There is a right monoid action of
W
on
V
: if
v\in V
and

, then

if

is the unique dense

-orbit in
(K\dot{v}B)(B\dot{w}B)

(see
[
31
]
). If

-orbit in

.
Given a
K
-orbit closure
\overline{K\dot{v}B}/B
for

and

is smooth (in fact, such that

is a closed orbit), and

(see
[

).

The

-invariant of

or

is defined as

\tau(w)=\{s\in S\;|\;w*s=w\}\mbox{ and }\tau(v)=\{s\in S\;|\;v*s=v\}.

for

,

. Equivalently,

is the right descent set of

[

is the weak

Since

\overline{K\dot{v}B}=\overline{K\dot{v}P_{\tau(v)}}
.
Hence
\overline{K\dot{v}B}
is right

-invariant.

For

, there is a proper surjective morphism

(4.2)

\mu:G_{v}\times^{B}P_{s}/B\to X_{v\star s}.

If

has

-type real, compact imaginary, or complex descent, then

(that is,

). If

has

-type noncompact imaginary or complex ascent, then

; in this case, the map

is generically finite.
If the
v
-type of

is complex ascent, then

is birational.
The
v
-noncompact imaginary simple reflections (or roots) are classified into two types. For type I, the map

is birational;
for type II,

is generically 2-1.

Let
P=P_{I}
and let
\rho:K\backslash G/B\to K\backslash G/P
denote the
natural surjection of finite sets.
Let
V_{I}=\{v\in V\mid v*w_{P}=v\}
. For
v\in V_{I}
, let

.
By
[
31
, Section 3.4]
,
the map

,

, is a bijection, so

. Moreover, if

, then

\rho^{-1}(K\dot{v}P)=\sqcup_{w\in\Gamma(v)}K\dot{w}B.

Given

, we have

for

if and only if

.
(Indeed, if

, then

, so

.
Conversely, if

then

This implies that
K\dot{x}P=K\dot{y}P
if and only if

.

4.3.
K
-orbit closures for

and

Unless otherwise noted, for the remainder of Section
4
,

,
\theta
is the involution
given by conjugation by

I_{p,q}=\begin{bmatrix}I_{p}&\\
&-I_{q}\end{bmatrix},

where

indicates the

identity matrix, and

,
and

is the block diagonal subgroup of

with blocks of sizes
p
and

. Let

and

denote the subgroups of upper triangular matrices and diagonal matrices in

The simple roots are
\alpha_{i}=\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{i+1}
for

. Under the isomorphism

,

s_{i}
is the transposition
(i,i+1)
. For this

and

, each

is normal (see
[
11
]
, Theorem 6 and the discussion following Corollary 2;
cf.
[
36
]
, which refers to
[
11
]
.)

This following (known) lemma implies that for this

and
K
,
the
K
-orbits in

are equivariantly simply connected, i.e., there are no nontrivial
K
-equivariant coverings of orbits.
For lack of a precise reference we give the proof.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
G=GL(n)
and
K=GL(p)\times GL(q)
as above. The stabilizer in
K
of any point in
G/P
is connected.

Proof.

The stabilizer in
K
of
gP
is
K\cap gPg^{-1}
. Since
K=GL(p)\times GL(q)
, and a Levi factor of

is a product of
general linear groups, the groups

and
gPg^{-1}
can each be viewed as open subsets of their Lie algebras
{\mathfrak{k}}

and
\mathrm{Ad}(g){\mathfrak{p}}
. Therefore,
K\cap gPg^{-1}
is an open subspace of the vector space
{\mathfrak{k}}\cap\mathrm{Ad}(g){\mathfrak{p}}
, so it is connected.
∎

The set
V=K\backslash G/B
can be parametrized by

, which are defined as follows.
A clan
\gamma
is a sequence of symbols
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
, where each symbol is either a sign (that is
+
or
-
) or an integer, such
that each integer occuring in
\gamma
occurs exactly twice; and
if
n(\gamma,+)
(resp.
n(\gamma,-)
) are the number of occurrences of the symbol
+
(resp.
-
) in the clan
\gamma
, then

n(\gamma,+)-n(\gamma,-)=p-q
. Clans
\gamma=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
and
\gamma^{\prime}=(a_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,a_{n}^{\prime})
are considered equal if
the following hold:
a_{i}
is a sign if and only if
a^{\prime}_{i}
is a sign, and in that case

; and
a_{i}=a_{j}
are equal integers if and only if

a^{\prime}_{i}=a^{\prime}_{j}
are equal integers. For example, if
n=7
,
p=4
,

, then

(1,+,2,1,2,-,+)
and
(2,+,1,2,1,-,+)
are equal clans. If
a_{i}
is a natural number, the
a_{j}
which is equal to
a_{i}
is called the mate of
a_{i}
.
We identify
v\in V
with the corresponding clan,
and if we need to specify
p
and
q
, we will write

V_{p,q}
for

.
For brevity, we frequently omit commas, e.g. we write
(1+1)
instead of
(1,+,1)
. The closed orbits correspond to clans whose entries are all signs.
The open
K
-orbit (i.e,. the unique maximal orbit in the closure
ordering) corresponds to the clan

v=\max(V_{p,q})=\begin{cases}(1\;2\;\cdots\;q\;+\;\cdots\;+\;q\;\cdots\;2\;1)\mbox{ if }p\geq q\\
(1\;2\;\cdots\;p\;-\;\cdots\;-\;p\;\cdots\;2\;1)\mbox{ if }q\geq p.\end{cases}

Given clans
v^{i}\in V_{p_{i},q_{i}}
, we can form a clan
v=(v^{1},\ldots,v^{r})\in V=V_{p,q}
(where
p=\sum p_{i}
and
q=\sum q_{i}
)
by concatenating the clans
v^{i}
.
We refer to
v^{i}
as the
i
-th block of
v
; this depends on the particular
expression for
v
as a concatenation. We often write

to emphasize the block structure.

The map
\phi:V\to W
takes the clan
v=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
to the involution
\phi(v)\in W
which switches
i
and

whenever
a_{i}
and
a_{j}
are equal integers,
and fixes
i
if
a_{i}
is a sign. Since
\theta(\alpha)=\alpha
for all
\alpha\in\Phi
,
the action
\theta_{v}
on
\Phi

satisfies
\theta_{v}\cdot\alpha=\phi(v)(\alpha)
. Hence, a simple root
s_{i}
is imaginary if and only if
a_{i}
and
a_{i+1}
are signs.

The monoid action on clans is as follows (see
[
26
]
). Let
v=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
be a clan.
If
s_{i}\not\in\tau(v)
, then
v*s_{i}>v
.
This occurs if
\alpha_{i}
is (1) a complex ascent, or
(2) imaginary and noncompact. In terms of the entries of
v
, this means:

(1)

(a)

a_{i}
and
a_{i+1}
are unequal natural numbers, such that the mate of
a_{i}
occurs before the mate of
a_{i+1}
;

(b)

a_{i}
is a sign,
a_{i+1}
is a number, and the mate of
a_{i+1}
occurs after position
i+1
;

(c)

a_{i}
is a number,
a_{i+1}
is a sign, and the mate of
a_{i}
occurs before position
i
;

(2)

a_{i}
and
a_{i+1}
are opposite signs.

The clan

differs from

only in the

and
i+1
positions. In case (1),
v*s_{i}
is obtained by switching the
i
and
i+1
entries of
v
; in case (2),
v*s_{i}
is obtained by replacing the
i
and
i+1
entries of
v
with equal numbers.
If both
a_{i}
and
a_{i+1}
are both signs, then as noted above,
s_{i}
is imaginary. In this case, if the signs are opposite then
s_{i}
is noncompact imaginary,
since

; if the signs are the same, then
s_{i}
is compact imaginary, since

. For this

and

, all imaginary noncompact roots are of type I (cf.
[
11
, Corollary 2]
).

4.4.
Smooth orbit closures

The
K
-orbit closure
X_{v}
is smooth if and only if

v=(v^{1}\mid\cdots\mid v^{r})
, where each
v^{j}=\max(V_{p_{j},q_{j}})
(this is essentially stated in the proof of the main theorem of
[
27
]
).
Specifying such a
v
is equivalent to specifying decompositions
p=p_{1}+p_{2}+\cdots+p_{r}
and
q=q_{1}+q_{2}+\cdots q_{r}
where the

p_{i}
and
q_{i}
are nonnegative integers and each
p_{i}+q_{i}>0
.

If
v=(v^{1}\mid\cdots\mid v^{r})=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
, we say that
s_{i}
straddles two blocks if
a_{i}
is the last entry of a block and
a_{i+1}

is the first entry of the next block. If
X_{v}
is smooth and each
v_{j}=\max(V_{p_{j},q_{j}})
then

consists of
the
s_{i}
which straddle two blocks such that at least one of which has more than one entry, or such that the blocks are a pair of opposite signs.
For example, if

v=(1+1|2332|4-4)=(v^{1}\mid v^{2}\mid v^{3})
, where
v^{1}=(1+1)=\max(V_{2,1})
,
v^{2}=(2332)=\max(V_{2,2})
, and
v^{3}=(4-4)=\max(V_{1,2})
, then

S\setminus\tau(v)=\{s_{3},s_{7}\}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose that
v\in V_{p,q}
is of the form
v=(v^{1}\mid\cdots\mid v^{r})
, where each
v^{k}\in V_{p_{k},q_{k}}
. Suppose that

and
y\leq v
.
Then
y=(y^{1}\mid\cdots\mid y^{r})
, where each
y^{i}\leq v^{i}
is in
V_{p_{i},q_{i}}
.

Proof.

By
[
24
, Proposition 4.3.1]
, we have
X_{v}=K\times^{P_{K}}(X_{v^{1}}\times\cdots\times X_{v^{h}})
, where
P
is the standard parabolic subgroup corresponding to
S\smallsetminus\left\{n_{1},n_{1}+n_{2},\ldots,n-n_{h}\right\}
, and
P_{K}=P\cap K
is a parabolic subgroup of
K
.
The
K
-stable subspaces of
X_{v}
correspond to the
P_{K}
-stable subspaces of
X_{v^{1}}\times\cdots\times X_{v^{h}}
.
Since
P_{K}
contains a subgroup which acts as
GL_{p_{i}}\times GL_{q_{i}}
on

, a closed
K
-stable subspace of
X_{v}
is equal to
K\times^{P_{K}}(X_{y^{1}}\times\cdots\times X_{y^{h}})
for some
X_{y^{i}}\subseteq X_{v^{i}}
.
The claim follows.
∎

The following lemma will be used in computing tangent spaces of resolutions
(see Theorem

and Corollary

).

Lemma 4.3

If

is smooth, then
G_{v_{0}}=\overline{K\dot{v}_{0}B}=K\dot{v}_{0}P_{\tau(v_{0})}
.
Moreover, if

is a set of simple reflections containing

, then

is closed in

where

Proof.

We claim that
K\dot{v}_{0}P_{\tau(v_{0})}/P_{\tau(v_{0})}
is closed in

. Indeed, if
K\dot{y}P_{\tau(v_{0})}/P_{\tau(v_{0})}\subseteq\overline{K\dot{v}_{0}P_{\tau(v_{0})}}/P_{\tau(v_{0})}

then

. Because

is smooth,
v_{0}=(v^{1},\ldots,v^{r})
where

. Let

The element

is the longest

.
Thus,

is the only

Since

If

∎

The next proposition plays an important role in the proof of Theorem
6.2
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
G=GL(n)
and
K=GL(p)\times GL(q)
.
Suppose that
I\subset S
is a set of commuting simple reflections.
Suppose
n=n_{1}+\cdots n_{h}
and
n_{i}=p_{i}+q_{i}
for

i\in\{1,\ldots,h\}
. Let

v=(v^{1}\mid v^{2}\mid\cdots\mid v^{h})\in V_{p,q}
,
where
v^{i}\in V_{p_{i},q_{i}}
. Suppose that
(a)
\ell(v*w_{I})=\ell(v)+|I|
, and (b) each
s\in I
straddles two blocks of
v
(this follows from (a)
if each
v^{i}=\max(V_{p_{i},q_{i}})
).
Then the natural map
\rho:K\backslash\overline{KvB}/B\rightarrow K\backslash\overline{KvP_{I}}/P_{I}

is a bijection.

Proof.

Since
\pi_{v}:\overline{KvB}/B\rightarrow\overline{KvP_{I}}/P_{I}=\overline{Kv*w_{I}P_{I}}/P_{I}
is surjective, the map
\rho

is surjective. Hence, given any
w\in V_{I}
with

w\leq v*w_{I}
, there exists
x\in V
,
x\leq v
, such that
\pi_{v}(KxB/B)\subseteq KwP_{I}/P_{I}
.
We must show that this
x
is unique. Observe that
KxB/B\subseteq\pi_{v}^{-1}(KwP_{I}/P_{I})

is equivalent to the conditions
x\in\Gamma(w)
and
x\leq v
.
It suffices to show that
the entries of
x
are determined by these conditions.

Because
x\leq v
, we can write

x=(x^{1}\mid\cdots\mid x^{h})
.
The entries of
x
coincide with those of

w=x*w_{I}
except in positions
m,m+1
, for
s_{m}\in I
.
Suppose that
s_{m}\in I
. Since

consists of commuting simple reflections,
any other element
s_{m^{\prime}}
of

satisfies

. This implies that the entries
(w_{m},w_{m+1})
are determined by the entries

(x_{m},x_{m+1})
. The description of the monoid action
implies that one of the following holds, where
\epsilon
denotes a sign

or
-
, and

denote integers.

(1)

(x_{m},x_{m+1})
and
(w_{m},w_{m+1})
are pairs of equal signs, and
(x_{m},x_{m+1})=(w_{m},w_{m+1})
.

(2)

(x_{m},x_{m+1})
are opposite signs, and
(w_{m},w_{m+1})
are equal integers.

(3)

(x_{m},x_{m+1})=(a,\epsilon)
, where the mate of

occurs before position
m
, and

(w_{m},w_{m+1})=(\epsilon,a)
.

(4)

(x_{m},x_{m+1})=(\epsilon,a)
, where the mate of
a
occurs after position

, and
(w_{m},w_{m+1})=(a,\epsilon)
,

(5)

(x_{m},x_{m+1})=(a,b)
, where the mate of

occurs before position

, the mate of
b
occurs after position
m+1
,
and

From this

uniquely determines
(x_{m},x_{m+1})
. Indeed, the only possible ambiguity would
be if
(w_{m},w_{m+1})
are equal integers; then we are in case (2), so
(x_{m},x_{m}+1)
is either

(+,-)
or
(-,+)
. The number of

is

of

is the unique element in

and

.
∎

4.5.
Some results on the
\tau
-invariant

For later use, we record the following lemmas. We continue assuming

and

.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
v=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in V_{p,q}
. Suppose
s=s_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{i+1}}
and
s^{\prime}=s_{\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{j+1}}

(a_{i},a_{i+1})
and
(a_{j},a_{j+1})
are both ordered pairs of natural numbers, then

(a_{i},a_{i+1})\neq(a_{j},a_{j+1})

Then

, and
v<v*s<v*s*s^{\prime}

Proof.

The monoid action, in terms of clans, is described explicitly in Section
4.3
. The lemma follows by
examining what happens for each of the possibilities for
(a_{i},a_{i+1})
and
(a_{j},a_{j+1})
described there.
∎

Assuming the other hypotheses of the lemma, the condition that
(a_{i},a_{i+1})
and
(a_{j},a_{j+1})
are distinct ordered pairs of natural numbers is automatically
satisfied if

is a concatenation of smaller clans, and
s
and
s^{\prime}
both straddle blocks of
v
.

Example 4.8
.

Even if the other hypotheses of the lemma hold, the conclusion of the lemma can fail
if
(a_{i},a_{i+1})=(a_{j},a_{j+1})
are
equal ordered pairs of natural numbers
(e.g. for
v=(1212),I=\{s,s^{\prime}\}
where

and

.
In this example, the inequality

is strict. The next

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
v\in V
be a concatenation of clans. Suppose
I=\{s_{i_{1}},\ldots,s_{i_{k}}\}
is a set
of commuting simple reflections, each of which straddles two blocks of
v
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

I\cap\tau(v)=\emptyset
.

(2)

v<v*s_{i_{1}}<\cdots<v*s_{i_{1}}*\cdots*v*s_{i_{k}}=v*w_{I}
.

(3)

\ell(v*w_{I})=\ell(v)+|I|
.

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
: Suppose
I\cap\tau(v)=\emptyset
. Applying Lemma
4.7
repeatedly yields (2).

(2)\Rightarrow(3)
: If
v<v*s
, then
\ell(v*s)=\ell(v)+1
. Hence the chain of inequalities in (2) implies (3).

(3)\Rightarrow(1)
: We prove the contrapositive. Suppose
I\cap\tau(v)\neq\emptyset
, so
s_{i_{r}}\in\tau(v)
for some
r
.
Let
I^{\prime}=I\smallsetminus\{s_{i_{r}}\}
. Then
v*w_{I}=v*w_{I^{\prime}}
, so
\ell(v*w_{I})=\ell(v*w_{I^{\prime}})\leq\ell(v)+|I|-1
, so
(3)
does not hold.
∎

Note that
(1)\Leftarrow(2)\Leftrightarrow(3)
for general
G
and
K
. Only the implication
(1)\Rightarrow(2)
uses the hypothesis that

G=GL(n)
and
K=GL(p)\times GL(q)
. We do not know if this hypothesis is necessary.

Lemma 4.10
.

Suppose
X_{v_{0}}
is smooth, and let
y\leq v_{0}
. If
s
is a simple reflection such that
s\in\tau(y)
and
s\not\in\tau(v_{0})
, then

s
is
y
-compact imaginary.

Proof.

Since
X_{v_{0}}
is smooth,
v_{0}=(v^{1}\mid\cdots\mid v^{h})
for some
h
, where
v^{i}=\max(V_{p_{i},q_{i}})
.
By Lemma
4.2
,

y=(y^{1}\mid\cdots\mid y^{h})
, where
y_{i}\in V_{p_{i},q_{i}}
.

Now suppose

and
s\not\in\tau(v_{0})
. Because

,

must straddle the
k
and

blocks of
v_{0}
. If
y^{k}
ends with a number, then the mate of this number occurs in

(as

) – in particular, it occurs prior to the last entry of

. Similarly, if

, so

ends with a sign and

is

-compact imaginary.
∎

Let

There is a natural map

(5.1)

\mu:G_{v_{0}}\times^{B}P_{i_{1}}\times^{B}\cdots\times^{B}P_{i_{k}}/B\to X_{v}.

By induction, reasoning as in the discussion of (

Suppose that

. Then this map is generically finite.
Each

-type either (1) complex ascent, or (2) non-compact imaginary.
If in addition whenever

for

is smooth, then

Suppose

are all distinct, and write
I=\{s_{i_{1}},\ldots,s_{i_{k}}\}
.
Recall that
Y_{w}
denotes the Schubert variety
\overline{BwB}/B
.
By
[
33
, p. 50]
,
Y_{w}=\overline{BwB}/B=P_{i_{1}}P_{i_{2}}\cdots P_{i_{k}}/B=P_{I}/B

We claim that the multiplication map yields an isomorphism

8.4.
Push-forwards of Chern classes and Chern-Mather classes

By Corollary
6.4
, the
T
-equivariant Chern-Mather class of
X_{v}
is given by
c_{M}^{T}(X_{v})=\mu_{*}(c^{T}(TZ_{v})\cap[Z_{v}]_{T})
.
Localization in equivariant cohomology and Borel-Moore homology can be used to compute the right hand side.
More generally, if
\mu:Z\to Y
is a
T
-equivariant map of
T
-varieties where
Z
is nonsingular and
Z^{T}
is finite,
we explain how to calculate
\mu_{*}(c^{T}(TZ)\cap[Z]_{T})\in H^{T}_{*}(Y)
.

The
T
-equivariant Borel-Moore homology groups are a module for
H^{*}_{T}
, the
T
-equivariant cohomology of a point. For convenience,
we use complex coefficients; then
H^{*}_{T}
is isomorphic to the symmetric algebra
S({\mathfrak{t}}^{*})
, where

{\mathfrak{t}}=\mbox{Lie }T
. Let
\mathcal{Q}
denote the
quotient field of
H^{*}_{T}
.

Let
q\in Z^{T}
, and let
i_{q}:\{q\}\hookrightarrow Z
denote the inclusion. The tangent space
T_{q}Z
is a representation of

T
; suppose it has weights
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n}
. The weights are all nonzero, but they are not necessarily distinct.
Then the
n
-th Chern class and total Chern class of
T_{q}Z
are elements of
H^{*}_{T}
given, respectively, by

c_{n}^{T}(T_{q}Z)=\beta_{1}\beta_{2}\cdots\beta_{n}
and
c^{T}(T_{q}Z)=(1+\beta_{1})(1+\beta_{2})\cdots(1+\beta_{n})
.
We have
i_{q}^{*}c^{T}_{n}(TZ)=c_{n}^{T}(T_{q}Z)
and
i_{q}^{*}c^{T}(TZ)=c^{T}(T_{q}Z)
. Define

(8.12)

C(q)=\frac{c^{T}(T_{q}Z)}{c^{T}_{n}(T_{q}Z)}=\frac{\prod\beta_{i}}{\prod(1+\beta_{i})}\in\mathcal{Q}.

Proposition 8.13
.

In
H^{T}_{*}Y\otimes_{H^{*}_{T}}\mathcal{Q}
, we have

(8.13)

\mu_{*}(c^{T}(TZ)\cap[Z]_{T})=\sum_{p\in Y^{T}}(\sum_{q\in(\mu^{-1}(p))^{T}}C(q))[p].

Proof.

The localization theorem implies that in
H^{T}_{*}Z\otimes_{H^{*}_{T}}\mathcal{Q}
,

\displaystyle c^{T}(TZ)\cap[Z]_{T}

\displaystyle=\sum_{q\in Z^{T}}i_{q*}\Big(\frac{1}{c^{T}_{n}(T_{q}Z)}i_{q}^{*}(c^{T}(TZ)\cap[Z]_{T})\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{q\in Z^{T}}i_{q*}(\frac{c^{T}(T_{q}Z)}{c_{n}^{T}(T_{q}Z)}\cap[q])=\sum_{q\in Z^{T}}i_{q*}(C(q)\cap[q]).

We abuse notation and write
i_{q*}[q]=[q]
, where on the left side,
[q]
denotes the fundamental class in

H_{*}^{T}(\{q\})
, and on the right side,
[q]
is viewed as a class in
H_{*}^{T}(Z)
. With these conventions,

i_{q*}(C(q)\cap[q])=C(q)[q]
, and so, in
H^{T}_{*}Z\otimes_{H^{*}_{T}}\mathcal{Q}
, we have

c^{T}(TZ)\cap[Z]_{T}=\sum_{q\in Z^{T}}C(q)[q]

Therefore, in
H^{T}_{*}Y\otimes_{H^{*}_{T}}\mathcal{Q}
, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{*}(c^{T}(TZ)\cap[Z]_{T})

\displaystyle=\sum_{q\in Z^{T}}\mu_{*}(C(q)[q])=\sum_{q\in Z^{T}}C(q)\mu_{*}[q]

\displaystyle=\sum_{p\in Y^{T}}\Big(\sum_{q\in(\mu^{-1}(p))^{T}}C(q)\Big)[p],

proving the result.
∎

Remark 8.14
.

By the localization theorem,
[X]_{T}=\sum_{p\in X^{T}}\varepsilon_{p}(X)[p]_{T}

and
[Z]_{T}=\sum_{q\in Z^{T}}\varepsilon_{q}(Z)[q]_{T}
. The elements
\varepsilon_{p}(X)
and
\varepsilon_{q}(Z)
of
\mathcal{Q}

are called equivariant multiplicities (see
[
10
]
)).
Since
Z
is smooth,
\varepsilon_{q}(Z)=\prod_{\beta_{i}\in\Phi(T_{q}Z)}\beta_{i}
. If
\mu
is birational, then

\varepsilon_{p}(X)=\sum_{q\in(\mu^{-1}(p))^{T}}\varepsilon_{q}(Z)
.

We now return to the
K
-orbit closure
X_{v}
. We abuse
notation and view
c_{M}^{T}(X_{v})
as an element of
H^{T}_{*}(X)

by taking its image under the map
H^{T}_{*}(X_{v})\to H^{T}_{*}(X)
.
We can write
c_{M}^{T}(X_{v})=\sum b_{y}[yB]_{T}
, where, in light
of Corollary
6.4
, formulas for the coefficients

can
be obtained by
applying Corollary
8.8
and Proposition
8.13
to our resolution

.
Given
w\in W

denote the corresponding Schubert
variety. The classes

form an

-basis
of

The

]

[

Hence we can calculate the coefficient of

A similar change of basis was applied in the setting of Springer fibers in
