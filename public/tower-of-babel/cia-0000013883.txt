A U.S Pilot was captured north of HAIPHONG City in August 1966 and was
taken through the city to a prison located on the waterfront (XJ 744065).
Source was on leave in HAIPHONG at the time and watched as the small
security convoy passed by. The crowd was angry and tossed rocks and other
objects at the jeep in which the pilot was riding. His guards hunched
down to keep from being hit themselves, but they did nothing to protect
the pilot or calm the people. Because of the angry mood of the people,
Source and several other NVA soldiers who were standing among the people
were drafted by security police to help quiet the mobs.

Source caught only a cursory glance of the pilot and did not see enough
to describe him. The pilot was wearing a helmet and kept his arms locked
in front of his face to protect himself from further injury. Source
observed that his face was cut, swollen, and appeared to have been heaten.

The vehicles traveled down the road on the waterfront, and Source followed
behind. The jeep stopped at an old prison located there, and the pilot
was taken in. The security guard who asked him to help with the crowd
told Source that the pilot would be kept there awhile, interrogated and
later transferred to another prison. Source believed that all pilots
captured in the HAIPHONG area were taken first to this prison, but he did
not have any facts to substantiate this,
