Real-world persistence with antifibrotic treatments: a nationwide study on claims data in France (The REPEAT study)
Authors: Vincent Cottin, Marie Najean, Elise Arnee, Malamati Voulgaridou, Clementine Dillard, Xavier Ansolabehere, Maxime Doublet, Julien Chollet, Sylvie Leroy, Stephane Jouneau
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundLong-term persistence with antifibrotic drugs is crucial to slow disease progression in fibrosing ILDs but is challenging due to adverse events. Large-scale, real-world studies investigating modifiable healthcare system factors associated with persistence are lacking.ObjectivesThe main objectives of this study were to assess the real-life persistence with antifibrotic treatments in the overall French population and to explore factors associated with patients’ persistence with antifibrotic therapy.MethodsIn this retrospective observational study conducted using the French National Health Database System, all adults initiating nintedanib or pirfenidone between January 2019 and December 2021 were included. They were categorized by diagnosis (idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, interstitial lung disease associated with systemic sclerosis, and progressive pulmonary fibrosis) using International Classification of Diseases 10th Revision codes. Persistence with antifibrotic therapy (defined as no treatment gap longer than 60 days), adherence to antifibrotic treatments, overall survival (OS), and event-free survival (EFS) were assessed. A multivariable Fine-Gray model identified factors associated with non-persistence.ResultsAmong 4,935 incident patients, the 12-month persistence rate with antifibrotic therapy was 70.8%. The median time of follow-up for incident patients was 27 months. The median time on antifibrotic therapy was 14.1 months. Switching between antifibrotics occurred in 16.6% of patients, mainly during the first year. At 12 months, OS and EFS were 85.8% and 57.4%, respectively, with lower rates observed among patients with IPF. Regular outpatient visits, follow-up in expert centers, and early supportive interventions (day-hospitalization, temporary treatment interruptions) were associated with increased persistence whereas suboptimal follow-up (< 2 pulmonary function tests during the follow-up period) was strongly associated with non-persistence (sHR = 3.1).ConclusionStructured patient follow-up and proactive management of adverse events, including treatment interruption, could be key to optimizing long-term persistence with antifibrotic therapy.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12931-026-03754-8.Keywords: Antifibrotic treatment, Interstitial lung disease, Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, Persistence, Adherence, SNDS

Introduction

Interstitial lung diseases (ILDs) encompass a heterogeneous group of parenchymal lung disorders characterized by varying degrees of inflammation and fibrosis, which often share overlapping clinical features and patterns of outcome [1]. While some ILDs have identifiable causes, such as connective tissue diseases or environmental exposure, others may be idiopathic with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) being the most prevalent and carrying a poor prognosis [2]. IPF is a progressive fibrosing ILD marked by a gradual decline in lung function leading to early mortality with a median survival at diagnosis of 2–5 years [3, 4]. A progressive fibrosing phenotype is not exclusive to IPF and can occur in other ILDs, leading to similar trajectories of declining lung function, increased morbidity, and early mortality [4].

The introduction of the antifibrotic agents nintedanib and pirfenidone represented a major advance in the management of this progression. Initially approved for the treatment of IPF, indications for nintedanib have been extended to ILD associated with systemic sclerosis (SSc-ILD) and progressive pulmonary fibrosis (PPF) [5, 6]. Clinical trials and real-world studies have shown that antifibrotics slow the decline in lung function and reduce the risk of acute exacerbations, which are associated with substantial morbidity and elevated mortality rates in patients with IPF [3, 5, 7–11]. However, their clinical utility is tempered by a well-documented side-effect profile, including gastrointestinal disturbances for both, and photosensitivity and rash with pirfenidone [12–15], which can challenge long-term treatment continuity.

Pirfenidone and nintedanib have been available for IPF in France since 2012 and 2015 respectively. In 2021, the reimbursed indications for nintedanib were broadened to include the treatment of SSc-ILD as well as other progressive chronic fibrosing ILDs.

While real-world studies have shown that patients treated with antifibrotics experience improved clinical outcomes compared with patients without antifibrotic medication, they have also highlighted low treatment persistence rates to antifibrotics [16–22]. Notably, these persistence rates vary markedly across world regions, suggesting that geographical disparities–potentially driven by differences in public health policies and healthcare system organization–may influence antifibrotic treatment continuity, thereby contributing to greater FVC decline and higher morbidity rates in ‘non-persistent’ patients. Persistence refers to the length of time a patient continues treatment without an unacceptable gap or permanent discontinuation and should be clearly distinguished from treatment adherence, which relates to how closely the patient follows the prescribed regimen, including timing, dosing, and frequency of administration [23]. Large-scale, nationwide studies assessing persistence across all approved indications for antifibrotics are lacking, as well as studies identifying system-level and patient-level factors associated with persistence.

Here, we aimed to assess the REal-life PErsistence with Antifibrotic (REPEAT study) in a large population and to explore factors associated with patients’ persistence with antifibrotics.

Methods

Study objectives

The primary objective was to evaluate the real-life persistence rate with antifibrotic therapy, defined by the percentage of patients remaining on antifibrotic therapy at key timepoints (e.g., 6, 12, 24, 30 months) following initiation. Persistence was analyzed allowing for a permissible gap between dispensations.

Secondary objectives included (1) measuring antifibrotic treatment adherence and exposure, (2) identifying patient and system-level factors associated with antifibrotic therapy persistence, (3) describing the overall survival (OS) and event-free survival (EFS), overall and by treatment indication.

Study design and data source

We conducted a nationwide, retrospective observational study using the French healthcare database, Système National des Données de Santé (SNDS). This database provides a comprehensive collection of all healthcare related reimbursements for any given patient, covering over 99% of the French population (~ 68 million inhabitants). It provides a detailed overview of outpatient prescriptions, hospital diagnoses, mortality data and demographic information. The study was conducted in Magellan’s secure data warehouse (a secure export of the SNDS database) covering the period from January 1, 2018 to December 31, 2022.

All adult patients with at least one reimbursement for an antifibrotic treatment (nintedanib or pirfenidone) between January 1, 2019, and December 31, 2021 were included. All patients who initiate an antifibrotic in France are registered in the database, allowing an exhaustive identification of all consecutive cases. The index date was defined as the date of the first antifibrotic reimbursement within the identification period.

Patients were classified as incident (new) users if they had no record of antifibrotic dispensing in the 12 months preceding the index date. All patients were followed for a minimum of 12 months or until death, whichever occurred first, to enable survival analyses. No exclusion criteria were applied.

Patients’ indications for IPF and SSc-ILD were approximated through their diagnoses using International Classification of Diseases 10th Revision (ICD-10) codes during a hospital stay or identified by a long-term disease reimbursement status. Given that there is no specific ICD-10 code for PPF, patients were assumed to be treated for PPF if they were receiving an antifibrotic in the absence of IPF or SSc-ILD codes during the entire study period.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using Python in a secured infrastructure. Continuous variables following a normal distribution were compared by a Student t test and otherwise by a Mann-Whitney test. Statistical comparisons of qualitative variables were tested with a Chi2 or a Fisher’s exact test if appropriate. Descriptive statistical analyses included the mean and standard deviation (SD) for continuous variables, and the number and proportion of patients for categorical variables. To ensure the anonymity of patients, no results concerning a sample of less than 11 patients were presented. No data were missing for the variables reported.

Persistence with antifibrotic therapy was assessed from the time of antifibrotic initiation using a Kalbfleisch & Prentice curve to account for competing risks of lung transplantation and death.

Differences in persistence rates between pirfenidone and nintedanib and between indications were assessed using a Log rank test.

Event-free survival and overall survival were assessed using the Kaplan-Meier method, with antifibrotic initiation as index date. The events considered were death, lung transplantation, hospitalization for acute exacerbation, hospitalization for other respiratory diseases and oxygen therapy onset [25].

Univariate and multivariate Fine & Gray proportional hazards models were used to analyze factors associated with antifibrotic treatment discontinuation, accounting for competing risks of lung transplantation and death.

Ethics

This study was approved by the French Institute for Health Data (approval no. 18770554 Bis from 18 July 2024). It was conducted with anonymised data, as requested by the National Informatics and Liberty Commission [CNIL], approval no. 924,199, from 23 September 2024.

Results

Study participants

A total of 10,646 patients with at least one reimbursement for an antifibrotic treatment were identified between January 1st 2018, and December 31st 2022. Among them, we identified an incident cohort of 4,935 patients who initiated antifibrotic therapy between January 1st 2019, and December 31st 2021. IPF was the most common diagnosis (68%), followed by PPF (27%), and SSc-ILD (5%). The first antifibrotic treatment prescribed was most frequently nintedanib (Fig. 1). The median time of follow-up for patients in the incident cohort was 27 months.

Treatment switching

A total of 820 patients (16.6%) switched antifibrotic agents after the first-line initiation during the study period. This switch occurred after a mean (SD) of 328 days (222.5), and occurred in 23.2% among patients initiated on pirfenidone, and 13.5% in those initiated on nintedanib. Furthermore, the switch occurred significantly earlier in the pirfenidone group (median 231 days [IQR 137–409]) compared to the nintedanib group (median 305 days [IQR 172–506]).

Treatment adherence

At 12 months post-initiation, 64% of the overall cohort demonstrated a good adherence (Proportion of Days Covered ≥ 80%) to antifibrotic therapy (including treatment switches). This adherence rates were 65.4% for IPF, 62.0% for PPF and 56.9% for SSc-ILD. By 24 months, the overall adherence rate declined to 61% (62.8% for IPF, 59.0% for PPF and 45.7% for SSc-ILD), with the most notable decrease observed in the SSc-ILD group. When analyzed by first antifibrotic treatment prescribed, 12-month adherence was 61.4% for nintedanib and 56.0% for pirfenidone.

Overall survival

The overall survival rate for the entire cohort was 85.8% at 12 months. As expected, survival differed significantly across disease indications (p < 0.0001). Patients with PPF demonstrated the highest survival rates (93.7% at 1 year; 88.2% at 2 years), followed by patients with SSc-ILD (88.7% at 1 year; 82.5% at 2 years). Patients with IPF had the lowest survival rates (82.7% at 1 year; 64.6% at 2 years) (Fig. 5).

Kaplan-Meier curve of overall survival by treatment indication, among patients newly initiated with antifibrotic therapy

Event-Free Survival (EFS) was defined as time to death, lung transplantation, hospitalization for acute exacerbation, or hospitalization for other respiratory diseases and oxygen therapy onset. The event-free survival rate in the overall population was 70.8% at 6 months and 57.4% at 1 year. EFS rates differed significantly across the three indications (log-rank p < 0.0001; Fig. 6). Patients with PPF demonstrated markedly higher EFS (83.1% at 1 year; 76.7% at 2 years) compared to other groups. In contrast, patients with IPF and SSc-ILD experienced substantially lower and similar EFS rates (IPF: 49.2% at 1 year, 28.8% at 2 years; SSc-ILD: 42.9% at 1 year, 26.2% at 2 years). The difference in EFS between IPF and SSc-ILD alone was not statistically significant (p = 0.067).

Factors associated with antifibrotic therapy persistence

In the multivariable Fine and Gray competing risk model, several factors were significantly associated with an increased risk of non-persistence with antifibrotic therapy. These included: (1) suboptimal management, defined as performing fewer than two pulmonary function tests (PFTs) during follow-up, which demonstrated the strongest association (sHR = 3.1; p-value < 0.001), (2) patient management outside of a reference center (sHR = 1.6; p-value < 0.001), (3) hospitalization for respiratory exacerbation during follow-up (sHR = 1.4; p-value < 0.001) (Fig. 7). Conversely, several management factors were positively associated with treatment persistence, including frequent follow-up visits with a pulmonologist, day hospitalization within 2 months of antifibrotic therapy initiation, and temporary treatment interruption.

Persistence

At 12 months, the persistence rate for patients who initiated any antifibrotic treatment between 2019 and 2021 (N = 4,935) was 68.7% (suppl Fig. 3). It was 68.1% for patients initiated with nintedanib and 55.9% for patients initiated with pirfenidone at 2,403 mg/day (p < 0.001) (suppl Fig. 4).

At 12 months, the persistence rate with antifibrotic therapy was 69.2% in IPF patients, 66.4% in SSc-ILD patients, and 67.5% in patients with PPF (p = 0.655) (suppl Fig. 5).

Adherence

The overall adherence to antifibrotic therapy considering a pirfenidone dose of 2,403 mg/day was estimated at 59% at 12 months (61% for nintedanib and 42% for pirfenidone).

Persistence with first antifibrotic treatment prescribed (sensitivity analysis considering antifibrotic treatment switching as discontinuation)

In a sensitivity analysis in which treatment switching was considered as discontinuation, the persistence rate with first antifibrotic treatment prescribed at 12 months was 65.9% for patients who initiated any antifibrotic treatment between 2019 and 2021; it was 66.4% in patients with IPF, 66.4% in patients with SSc-ILD, and 64.7% in patients with PPF (suppl Fig. 6).

When performing this sensitivity analysis using the pirfenidone daily dose of 2,403 mg, the persistence rate with first treatment antifibrotic prescribed at 12 month was 64.2% for patients who initiated any antifibrotic treatment between 2019 and 2021; it was 64.5% in patients with IPF, 65.6% in patients with SSc-ILD, and 63.2% in patients with PPF (suppl Fig. 6).

Discussion

This nationwide, real-world study covering the entire French population, provides a comprehensive assessment of antifibrotic treatment persistence across all approved indications in France. The main finding is that approximately one third of antifibrotic-treated patients discontinued treatment during the first year, highlighting treatment non-persistence as a major challenge in the clinical management of fibrosing ILDs, although to a lesser extent compared to a previous study [16]. We further identified key modifiable factors at both the system and the patient level that are strongly associated with persistence, offering actionable insights for improving long-term care.

Persistence with antifibrotic treatment at 12 months was 68.1% for patients who initiated antifibrotic therapy with nintedanib and 61.3% for those who initiated antifibrotic therapy with pirfenidone. Although this difference was statistically significant (p = 0.0049), it should be interpreted with caution given the non-random treatment allocation. Patient characteristics differed between groups, particularly regarding cardiovascular comorbidities and treatment indications, and with pirfenidone used off-label for SSc-ILD and PPF. In addition, no propensity score matching was performed to ensure comparability between treatment groups. Comparative effectiveness between treatments was not a prespecified objective of the study, and the analysis was not designed to support causal inference between treatment groups. These findings should therefore be considered descriptive and reflective of real-world practices rather than indicative of comparative effectiveness.

Discontinuation of either treatment occurred predominantly within the first year, suggesting that discontinuation may be related primarily to adverse events. Additionally, consistent with the literature [18, 26], persistence tended to decrease less during the second year of treatment, suggesting that patients who tolerate the initial treatment phase may be more likely to remain on therapy in the long term. Although reasons for discontinuation were not captured in the SNDS database, adverse effects and perceived lack of efficacy are likely contributors [27]. Persistence with pirfenidone was lower in Southern France and in French overseas territories (DROM). We speculate these lower rates might be driven by phototoxicity, although the data available does not provide supportive information on this hypothesis, and the smaller sample sizes in these regions warrant cautious interpretation.

Our findings suggest that a regular follow-up of the patient and dose adaptations could improve the persistence with antifibrotic treatment. Multivariable Fine & Gray models identified follow-up outside of an expert center as a factor associated with treatment non-persistence, while limited patient management (fewer than 2 PFTs over the study period) was an even stronger predictor of discontinuation. This suggests that experience of the physician, and regular follow-up, even outside of expert centers, may improve treatment persistence compared with less regular patient visits. Day hospital following antifibrotic initiation was associated with improved persistence (HR = 0.58, p < 0.001), presumably by providing early support for adverse event management and better information to patients about what to expect from antifibrotic treatment. Temporary treatment interruptions, as recommended in the summary of product characteristics1,2, were also associated with improved persistence. Such short “treatment holidays” may help patients recover from common adverse events (e.g., diarrhea), facilitating treatment resumption and continuity.

Among patients treated with antifibrotic, 16.6% switched from one antifibrotic to the other, with most switching occurring within the first-year post-initiation. In prior studies on IPF, treatment switching occurred in approximately 14% of cases [10, 26], in line with our findings.

The demographic profile of SSc-ILD patients (notably age and sex distribution) was consistent with both real-world clinical practice and previously published data [28, 29], confirming the validity of our cohort selection. The proportion of SSc-ILD patients receiving pirfenidone was low, in line with its lack of regulatory approval for this indication. A notable proportion of patients classified as PPF received pirfenidone (32%), despite nintedanib being the only antifibrotic drug approved; this may be attributable either to misclassification or to off-label use of pirfenidone in clinical practice, as suggested by French guidelines [30].

Patients treated with pirfenidone were more likely to have a history of ischemic heart disease. This observation aligns with the summary of product characteristics for nintedanib, which advises caution in patients with elevated cardiovascular risk, including those with known coronary artery disease, whereas such a warning is absent for pirfenidone.

IPF patients demonstrated poorer overall survival compared with SSc-ILD and PPF patients, which is consistent with the known more severe prognosis of IPF. The event-free survival rates at one year were similar between patients with IPF and SSc-ILD, at 49.2% and 42.9% respectively, while the rate was greater (83.1%) for PPF.

Hospitalizations for respiratory exacerbations were associated with lower antifibrotic persistence. This finding may reflect a shift toward supportive care after an exacerbation or indicate that clinicians may perceive the antifibrotic therapy as insufficiently effective in preventing acute exacerbations and choose to discontinue antifibrotic treatment after such events, deeming its expected benefit no longer favorable.

Strengths and limitations

This study benefits from the largest French dataset and robust statistical modeling to explore persistence patterns in real-world antifibrotic use across multiple indications, taking into account the competing risks of death and lung transplantation, highlighting the proportion of patients who could benefit from interventions to help them stay on treatment. This study also assessed adherence to treatment for a more comprehensive description of antifibrotic treatment [23], and found that adherence was generally fair (80% or greater in approximately two thirds of the patients during the first year).

Several limitations should be acknowledged. As the study was conducted using the SNDS healthcare database, no clinical variables or pulmonary function test results were available, precluding assessment of disease severity or progression. Diagnoses were inferred from ICD-10 codes, which may lead to misclassification, in particular for the PPF group for which no ICD-10 code exist. The relatively small number of patients treated for SSc-ILD and PPF reflects the more recent approval of nintedanib for these indications. Channeling bias may have influenced treatment allocation, as patient characteristics and comorbidities probably guided prescribing decisions, limiting comparability between nintedanib and pirfenidone subgroups. Moreover, the database did not capture reasons for treatment discontinuation. The choice of a sixty-day period with no antifibrotic dispensation to define treatment discontinuation, although common in pharmaco-epidemiological studies, is arbitrary. To investigate factors associated with treatment persistence, we constructed a statistical model incorporating variables identified by clinical experts; nonetheless, the potential influence of unmeasured confounders cannot be excluded. The retrospective design and reliance on administrative coding may introduce biases inherent to real-world healthcare databases, including potential underestimation or overestimation of persistence rates.

Conclusion

In this large real-world analysis, a high rate of early treatment discontinuation was observed, with one-third of antifibrotic-treated patients stopping therapy within the first year. Persistence was influenced by care pathways, with regular follow-up, care in reference center, early supportive interventions such as day hospital and temporary treatment interruptions, being associated with improved antifibrotic persistence. These findings reinforce the importance of an organized, continuous follow-up to optimize long-term treatment persistence and patient outcomes, and support networks of care including pulmonologists and primary care physicians, while new treatment options, which are expected to offer improved tolerability, are under evaluation or development. Our findings also highlight areas for further investigation, including regional disparities as well as the role of early management strategies for patients treated with antifibrotics.
