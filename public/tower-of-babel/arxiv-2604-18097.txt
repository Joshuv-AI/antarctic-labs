Asymmetric Scattering-Induced Néel Spin-Orbit Torque in Antiferromagnets
Sayan Sarkar, Amit Agarwal

Abstract
Magnetic switching in antiferromagnets relies on Néel spin orbit torque (NSOT), which originates from a current-induced staggered spin polarization of itinerant electrons. In collinear antiferromagnets, such a response requires the spin susceptibility to be odd under combined space–time inversion symmetry ( 𝒫𝒯 \cal PT ), and is conventionally attributed to symmetric scattering processes. Here, we demonstrate that asymmetric impurity scattering generates an additional 𝒫𝒯 \cal PT -odd spin polarization when coupled with the anomalous spin polarizability (ASP) of Bloch electrons. This extrinsic contribution arises from the interplay between antisymmetric higher-order scattering processes and band geometry, effectively converting an otherwise 𝒫𝒯 \cal PT -even susceptibility into a staggered spin polarization. Using a minimal model of tetragonal CuMnAs , we show that this anomalous skew-scattering contribution can be comparable to, and with sufficient impurity density even exceed, the conventional symmetric scattering (Drude) contribution. Our results identify a new band-geometry-driven mechanism for NSOT and establish an efficient route for electrical control of antiferromagnets.

Asymmetric scattering contribution to spin magnetization

In the semiclassical Boltzmann framework, current-induced spin polarization arises from corrections to both the spin expectation value and the non-equilibrium distribution function in the presence of an external electric field
\bm{E}
and weak disorder. These corrections can be systematically organized according to their order in the electric field and impurity potential. The total spin polarization is obtained by combining all allowed contributions from these corrections. In this section, we identify a previously overlooked contribution originating from the interplay between disorder-induced spin correction and asymmetric impurity scattering. Combining all of these corrections to both spin expectation and the non-equilibrium distribution yields the standard contributions to the linear spin Edelstein effect (LSEE), including the Drude (
\delta S^{\nu}_{\rm Dr}
), anomalous (

summarizes these channels and their leading impurity-density scaling. These are the standard spin polarization components widely discussed in the literature
Guo et al. 2024
;
Xiao and Niu 2017
;
Xiao et al. 2019
. The corresponding spin susceptibility components
\chi^{\nu;b}_{i}
can then be obtained from the constitutive relation

\delta S^{\nu}_{i}=\chi^{\nu;b}_{i}E_{b}~~~~(i=\rm Dr,anm,sj,sk).

(12)

Among all the above contributions to the spin susceptibility, only
\chi^{\nu;b}_{\mathrm{Dr}}
is a
\cal PT
-odd quantity, while the remaining components (
\chi^{\nu;b}_{\mathrm{anm}},\chi^{\nu;b}_{\mathrm{sj}}
and
\chi^{\nu;b}_{\mathrm{sk}}
) are
\cal PT
-even (see Table
1
). At linear order in the applied electric field, the standard channels can be organized as follows:
s^{\nu,0}f^{\rm in}
gives the Drude term,
s^{\nu,\rm anm}f^{0}
gives the intrinsic anomalous term,
(s^{\nu,\rm sct}f^{\rm in}+s^{\nu,0}f^{\rm sj})
forms the side-jump class, and
s^{\nu,0}f^{\rm sk3}
gives the conventional skew-scattering response. The product
s^{\nu,\rm anm}f^{\rm sk3}
is second order in the electric field and is therefore beyond the present linear-response theory, while
s^{\nu,\rm sct}f^{\rm sj}
is found in the higher-order impurity-density sector (
\propto n_{i}^{1}
) and therefore neglected.

In addition to these known contributions, we identify a previously overlooked term arising from the cross-combination of the disorder-induced spin correction
s^{\nu,\mathrm{sct}}_{n\bm{k}}
and the asymmetric distribution function
f^{\mathrm{sk3}}_{n\bm{k}}
. This contribution is given by

\displaystyle\delta S^{\nu}_{\rm AS}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\bm{k}}s^{\nu,\mathrm{sct}}_{n\bm{k}}f^{\mathrm{sk3}}_{n\bm{k}},

(13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e\tau}{\hbar}\sum_{n\bm{k}\bm{k}^{\prime}}w^{(3),a}_{n;\bm{k}\bm{k}^{\prime}}{\gamma^{\nu,c}_{n\bm{k}}k_{c}}\left(\partial_{k_{b}}f_{n\bm{k}}^{0}+\partial_{k_{b}^{\prime}}f_{n\bm{k}^{\prime}}^{0}\right)E_{b}.

The suffix ‘AS’ denotes anomalous skew-scattering, following the terminology introduced in Ref.
Ma et al. 2023
. This term is distinct from conventional skew-scattering contributions because it is a cross term. It couples the asymmetric scattering rate
w^{(3),a}
with the anomalous spin polarizability
\gamma^{\nu,c}_{n\bm{k}}
, rather than with the uncorrected spin expectation value alone. Since
w^{(3),a}
is
\mathcal{PT}
-odd
Ma et al. 2023
while
\gamma^{\nu,c}_{n\bm{k}}
is
\mathcal{PT}
-even
Guo et al. 2024
;
Xiao et al. 2022
;
Sarkar et al. 2025
, their product yields a
\mathcal{PT}
-odd spin susceptibility (
\chi^{\nu;b}_{\mathrm{AS}}
). In physical terms, asymmetric scattering biases carriers in momentum space in a Berry-curvature-dependent way, converting the otherwise
\cal PT
-even ASP into a
\cal PT
-odd observable. The
{\cal P}{\cal T}
character of the response has a direct sublattice consequence. For the two magnetic sublattices (
A
and
B
) related by
{\cal P}{\cal T}

{\cal P}{\cal T}
-odd susceptibilities satisfy
\chi^{\nu;b}_{\mathrm{o},A}=-\chi^{\nu;b}_{\mathrm{o},B}
, whereas
{\cal P}{\cal T}
-even channels satisfy
\chi^{\nu;b}_{\mathrm{e},A}=\chi^{\nu;b}_{\mathrm{e},B}
and therefore do not generate a Néel torque. This is why the ‘AS’ term is relevant for NSOT even though it is built from an ASP ingredient that is
{\cal P}{\cal T}
-even by itself.

-odd spin-susceptibility channels in tetragonal CuMnAs and verify their staggered sublattice structure. In Sec. IV we isolate the disorder prefactor that controls when the anomalous skew scattering term can compete with the Drude channel, and in Sec. III
Néel spin polarization in
{}\mathrm{CuMnAs}

The magnetic point groups of collinear AFMs such as
\text{MnBi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
,
CuMnAs
,
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{Au}
possess combined
\cal PT
symmetry

;
Bodnar et al. 2018
. In these systems, the two opposite magnetic sublattices are related by the
\cal PT
operation. For a bipartite collinear AFM with sublattices
A
and
B
, the symmetry transformation acts as
\mathcal{PT}:\mathbf{r}_{A}\to\mathbf{r}_{B}
and
\mathcal{PT}:\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{r}_{A})\to-\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{r}_{B})
, where
\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{r})
denotes the local magnetic moment. This relation ensures the compensation of the net atomic magnetization in equilibrium.

In the presence of
\cal PT
symmetry, response tensors can be classified according to their transformation properties. In particular, for a
\cal PT
-odd spin susceptibility, the symmetry operation enforces
\mathcal{PT}:\chi^{\nu;b}_{A}\to-\chi^{\nu;b}_{B}
. As a consequence, the induced itinerant spin polarization on the two sublattices acquires opposite signs, resulting in a staggered spin response. Such a staggered spin polarization acts as the driving mechanism for the NSOT and enables electrical switching in
\cal PT
-symmetric AFMs. In the sublattice-resolved numerical results below, this distinction is realized explicitly. The Drude and anomalous skew-scattering susceptibilities are odd under sublattice exchange, while the
{\cal P}{\cal T}
-even channels remain equal on the two sublattices and therefore drop out of the Néel response.

IV
Relative impurity scaling of anomalous skew-scattering contribution

To understand the dependence of the anomalous skew-scattering contribution on impurity parameters, we analyze the scaling of the corresponding spin susceptibility. For this purpose, it is useful to express the microscopic quantities entering the spin response in dimensionless form. We introduce the characteristic energy scale
E_{s}
(
\sim 1~\rm eV

Our LLG calculation is intended as an illustrative dynamics estimate based on the torque scale extracted from the susceptibility calculation. It should not be interpreted as a complete switching phase diagram over damping, pulse shape, and chemical-potential space. Within the chosen parameter set, the asymmetric scattering-induced NSOT produces a substantial enhancement of the spin torque and supports switching on the few-picosecond timescale. An experimentally useful discriminator of this mechanism is the predicted sensitivity of the switching scale to disorder tuning or chemical-potential variation, both of which directly modify the balance between the anomalous skew scattering and Drude channels. This establishes anomalous skew-scattering as a promising and previously unexplored mechanism for efficient electrical control in antiferromagnetic spintronic devices.

VI
Conclusion

In this work, we have investigated the role of asymmetric impurity scattering in generating staggered current-induced spin polarization in
\cal PT
-symmetric antiferromagnets. Within a semiclassical Boltzmann framework, we identify an additional contribution to the spin susceptibility arising from the interplay between antisymmetric higher-order impurity scattering and the anomalous spin polarizability. We show that this contribution is
\cal PT
-odd and therefore produces a staggered spin polarization on the two magnetic sublattices, enabling a Néel spin–orbit torque.

Our analysis establishes a direct connection between this extrinsic mechanism and band-geometric properties of the electronic structure, highlighting the role of band geometry in governing asymmetric scattering processes. Using a minimal model of CuMnAs, we demonstrate that the resulting spin response can be comparable to, and for sufficient magnetic disorder can exceed, the conventional Drude contribution. The corresponding torque is shown to efficiently drive ultrafast switching of the Néel vector within a realistic parameter regime.

These results reveal that asymmetric scattering can convert an otherwise
\cal PT
-even response into a staggered spin polarization, providing a new pathway for electrical control of antiferromagnetic order. More broadly, our findings demonstrate that disorder, when combined with band geometry, can play a constructive role in antiferromagnetic spintronics. We expect that this mechanism may be relevant in a wider class of
\cal PT
-symmetric materials and could be explored through controlled disorder engineering in future experiments.

w_{ll^{\prime}}\neq w_{l^{\prime}l}
. It is therefore convenient to decompose

w_{ll^{\prime}}^{s}=\frac{w_{ll^{\prime}}+w_{l^{\prime}l}}{2},\qquad w_{ll^{\prime}}^{a}=\frac{w_{ll^{\prime}}-w_{l^{\prime}l}}{2},

(35)

where
w^{s}_{ll^{\prime}}=w^{s}_{l^{\prime}l}
and
w^{a}_{ll^{\prime}}=-w^{a}_{l^{\prime}l}
. The symmetric part governs conventional relaxation processes, while the antisymmetric part gives rise to skew-scattering effects. For weak disorder, the scattering rate can be expanded perturbatively in powers of the impurity potential. Retaining contributions up to third order
Xiao et al. 2019
, we write

w_{l^{\prime}l}\simeq w_{l^{\prime}l}^{(2)}+w_{l^{\prime}l}^{(3),a}.

(36)

The second-order term is completely symmetric under
l\leftrightarrow l^{\prime}
and can be further decomposed into normal and coordinate-shift contributions (discussed in Appendix
C
). The antisymmetric part arises at third order and is responsible for skew scattering. Symmetric higher-order corrections merely renormalize
w^{(2)}
and are neglected here.

In the presence of a uniform electric field,
\dot{\bm{k}}=-e\bm{E}/\hbar
. Restricting to the steady state (
\partial_{t}f_{l}=0
), Eq. (
31
) leads to a hierarchy of coupled equations for the different contributions to the distribution function,

\displaystyle-\frac{e\bm{E}}{\hbar}\cdot\partial_{\bm{k}}f_{l}^{\rm in}

\displaystyle=I_{\rm el}^{\rm in}(f_{l}^{\rm in}),

(37a)

\displaystyle-\frac{e\bm{E}}{\hbar}\cdot\partial_{\bm{k}}f_{l}^{\rm sj}

\displaystyle=I_{\rm el}^{\rm in}(f_{l}^{\rm sj})+I_{\rm el}^{\rm sj}(f_{l}^{\rm in}),

(37b)

\displaystyle-\frac{e\bm{E}}{\hbar}\cdot\partial_{\bm{k}}f_{l}^{\rm sk3}

\displaystyle=I_{\rm el}^{\rm in}(f_{l}^{\rm sk3})+I_{\rm el}^{\rm sk3}(f_{l}^{\rm in}).

(37c)

Here depending on the different physical origins of the impurity scattering rates, the elastic collision integral has been decomposed into distinct contributions. In particular, we separate the collision term into intrinsic, side-jump, and skew-scattering components,
I_{\mathrm{el}}(f_{l})=I_{\mathrm{el}}^{\mathrm{in}}(f_{l})+I_{\mathrm{el}}^{\mathrm{sj}}(f_{l})+I_{\mathrm{el}}^{\mathrm{sk3}}(f_{l}),

and these distinct components have the form
Du et al. 2019
;
Varshney and Agarwal 2026a
;
Sarkar et al. 2026c
,

\displaystyle I_{\mathrm{el}}^{\mathrm{in}}(f_{l})=-\sum_{l^{\prime}}w_{ll^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{2S}}(f_{l}-f_{l^{\prime}})~,~~~~~I_{\mathrm{el}}^{\mathrm{sj}}(f_{l})=-\sum_{l^{\prime}}w_{ll^{\prime}}^{(2),\mathrm{cs}}(f_{l}-f_{l^{\prime}})~,~~~~~~I_{\mathrm{el}}^{\mathrm{sk3}}(f_{l})=\sum_{l^{\prime}}w_{ll^{\prime}}^{(3),a}(f_{l}+f_{l^{\prime}})~.

(38)

The intrinsic collision integral is treated within the relaxation-time approximation,

I_{\rm el}^{\rm in}(f_{l}^{\rm in})=-\frac{f_{l}^{\rm in}-f_{l}^{0}}{\tau},

(39)

where
\tau
is the elastic scattering time. We solve these equations perturbatively by expanding the distribution function in powers of the electric field,
f_{l}=f_{l}^{(0)}+f_{l}^{(1)}+f_{l}^{(2)}+\cdots,
with
f_{l}^{(i)}\propto|\bm{E}|^{i}
. The intrinsic linear-response solution is

f_{l}^{\rm in,(1)}=\frac{e\tau}{\hbar}\,\bm{E}\cdot\partial_{\bm{k}}f_{l}^{0}.

(40)

Solving Eq. (
37
) to linear order in
\bm{E}
, the side-jump contribution is obtained as

f_{l}^{\rm sj,(1)}=-\tau\sum_{l^{\prime}}w^{(2),{\rm cs}}_{l^{\prime}l}\bigl(f_{l}^{0}-f_{l^{\prime}}^{0}\bigr).

(41)

Using the identity

v_{l}^{a}\,\partial_{\varepsilon_{l}}\delta(\varepsilon_{l}-\varepsilon_{l^{\prime}})=\hbar^{-1}\partial_{k_{a}}\delta(\varepsilon_{l}-\varepsilon_{l^{\prime}}),

and performing integration by parts, this can be recast as

f_{l}^{\rm sj,(1)}=-e\tau\,\bm{E}\cdot\bm{v}_{l}^{\rm sj}\frac{\partial f^{0}}{\partial\varepsilon_{l}},

(42)

where the side-jump velocity is defined as
\bm{v}_{l}^{\rm sj}=-\sum_{l^{\prime}}w^{(2S)}_{l^{\prime}l}\,\bm{\delta r}_{ll^{\prime}},

with the coordinate shift defined as

\delta{\bm{r}}_{l^{\prime}l}=\langle u_{l^{\prime}}|i\partial_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}|u_{l^{\prime}}\rangle-\langle u_{l}|i\partial_{\bm{k}}|u_{l}\rangle-(\partial_{\bm{k}}+\partial_{\bm{k}^{\prime}})\arg(V_{l^{\prime}l}).

(43)

Proceeding similarly, the antisymmetric third-order scattering process yield the skew-scattering corrections

\displaystyle f_{l}^{\rm sk3,(1)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{e\tau^{2}}{\hbar}\sum_{l^{\prime}}w^{(3),a}_{l^{\prime}l}\left(\bm{E}\cdot\partial_{\bm{k}}f_{l}^{0}+\bm{E}\cdot\partial_{\bm{k}}f_{l^{\prime}}^{0}\right).

(44)

These expressions provide the linear-order non-equilibrium distribution functions used in the evaluation of the extrinsic spin response discussed in the main text.

Appendix C
Asymmetric scattering rate

In this section, we derive simplified expressions for the impurity scattering rates entering the semiclassical Boltzmann equation. Our goal is to reduce the general scattering amplitudes to the forms suitable for numerical evaluation of the spin susceptibilities. The total scattering rate can be decomposed in orders of scattering potential

w_{ll^{\prime}}\simeq w_{ll^{\prime}}^{(2)}+w_{ll^{\prime}}^{(3)}+\cdots,

(45)

where
w_{ll^{\prime}}^{(\nu)}\propto V^{\nu}
represent the
\nu
-th order scattering, for
\nu=2,3...
The explicit expressions of these scattering rates are given by

w_{ll^{\prime}}^{(2)}=\frac{2\pi}{\hbar}\left\langle V_{ll^{\prime}}V_{l^{\prime}l}\right\rangle_{\text{dis}}\delta(\varepsilon_{l}-\varepsilon_{l^{\prime}})~,

(46)

w_{ll^{\prime}}^{(3)}=\frac{2\pi}{\hbar}\sum_{l^{\prime\prime}}\bigg[\frac{\langle V_{ll^{\prime\prime}}V_{l^{\prime\prime}l^{\prime}}V_{l^{\prime}l}\rangle_{\text{dis}}}{\varepsilon_{l^{\prime}}-\varepsilon_{l^{\prime\prime}}+i\eta}+\frac{\langle V_{ll^{\prime}}V_{l^{\prime}l^{\prime\prime}}V_{l^{\prime\prime}l}\rangle_{\text{dis}}}{\varepsilon_{l^{\prime}}-\varepsilon_{l^{\prime\prime}}-i\eta}\bigg]\delta(\varepsilon_{l}-\varepsilon_{l^{\prime}})~.

(47)

The second-order scattering rate
w_{ll^{\prime}}^{(2)}
is strictly symmetric under exchange of initial and final states,
l\leftrightarrow l^{\prime}
. On the other hand,
w_{ll^{\prime}}^{(3)}
and
w_{ll^{\prime}}^{(4)}
do not follow such relations. The symmetric and antisymmetric parts of these scattering rates can therefore be separated as follows:

\displaystyle w_{ll^{\prime}}^{(\nu),s}=\frac{w_{ll^{\prime}}^{(\nu)}+w_{l^{\prime}l}^{(\nu)}}{2}~,~~~w_{ll^{\prime}}^{(\nu),a}=\frac{w_{ll^{\prime}}^{(\nu)}-w_{l^{\prime}l}^{(\nu)}}{2}~.

(48)

The symmetric parts of these higher-order terms merely renormalize the leading second-order contribution and do not generate qualitatively new transport phenomena. Consequently, for the third- and fourth-order scattering processes, only their antisymmetric components are relevant for transport. With this separation, the elastic collision integral naturally decomposes into symmetric and antisymmetric parts as
I^{s}_{\text{el}}\{f_{l}\}=-\sum_{l^{\prime}}w_{l^{\prime}l}^{(2)}(f_{l}-f_{l^{\prime}})
and
I^{a}_{\text{el}}\{f_{l}\}=-\sum_{l^{\prime}}w_{l^{\prime}l}^{(3),a}(f_{l}+f_{l^{\prime}})
, respectively.

When we account for the work done by the electric field as an electron is displaced within the unit cell during the collision, the second-order impurity contribution is modified as

\displaystyle w_{ll^{\prime}}^{(2)}=\frac{2\pi}{\hbar}\left\langle V_{ll^{\prime}}V_{l^{\prime}l}\right\rangle_{\text{dis}}\delta(\varepsilon_{l}-\varepsilon_{l^{\prime}}+e{\bm{E}}\cdot\delta{\bm{r}}_{ll^{\prime}})=w^{(2S)}_{ll^{\prime}}+w^{(2),\rm cs}_{ll^{\prime}},

(49)

where
\delta{\bm{r}}_{ll^{\prime}}
is the coordinate shift defined in Eq. (
43
) in the main text. The symmetric field-independent part governs conventional momentum relaxation (
w^{(2S)}_{ll^{\prime}}
), the coordinate shift correction term gives the side jump contribution (
w^{(2),\rm cs}_{ll^{\prime}}
). Our next step is to simplify these scattering rates and express them in forms suitable for numerical evaluation of the spin conductivity. We begin with the field independent part of the second-order scattering rate
w_{ll^{\prime}}^{(2S)}
,

\displaystyle w_{ll^{\prime}}^{(2S)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi}{\hbar}\left\langle V_{ll^{\prime}}V_{l^{\prime}l}\right\rangle_{\text{dis}}\delta(\varepsilon_{l}-\varepsilon_{l^{\prime}})=\frac{2\pi}{\hbar}\left\langle V_{{\bm{k}}{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}V_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}{\bm{k}}}\right\rangle_{\text{dis}}\langle u_{n\bm{k}}|u_{n^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}\rangle\langle u_{n^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}|u_{n\bm{k}}\rangle\delta(\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}}-\varepsilon_{n^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}})~.

(50)

Here we have used the notation
V_{ll^{\prime}}=\langle l|V|l^{\prime}\rangle=\langle n\bm{k}|V|n^{\prime}{\bm{k}}^{\prime}\rangle
. To simplify the expressions above, we adopt the simplest model for disorder, consisting of short-range, randomly distributed
\delta
-function scatterers,
V(\bm{r})=\sum_{i}V_{i}\delta(\bm{r}-\bm{R}_{i})
. Within this model, the impurity matrix element between Bloch states takes the form

\displaystyle\bra{ n \bm k}V\ket{ n'\bm k'}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\int d{\bm{r}}V_{i}\delta(\bm{r}-\bm{R}_{i})e^{i(\bm{k}^{\prime}-\bm{k})\cdot\bm{r}}\langle u_{n\bm{k}}|u_{n^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}\rangle=\sum_{i}V_{i}e^{i(\bm{k}^{\prime}-\bm{k})\cdot\bm{R}_{i}}\langle u_{n\bm{k}}|u_{n^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}\rangle=V^{0}_{{\bm{k}}{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}\langle u_{n\bm{k}}|u_{n^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}\rangle~.

and the disorder average can be further simplified to

\left\langle V_{{\bm{k}}{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}V_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}{\bm{k}}}\right\rangle_{\text{dis}}=\left\langle\sum_{ij}V_{i}V_{j}\exp\{({\bm{k}}^{\prime}-{\bm{k}})(R_{i}-R_{j})\}\right\rangle_{\rm dis}=n_{i}V_{0}^{2}~.

(51)

where
n_{i}
is the impurity concentration and
V_{0}
is the zeroth moment of the impurity potential. Putting these ingredients together,
w^{(2S)}_{ll^{\prime}}
finally simplifies to

w_{ll^{\prime}}^{(2S)}=\frac{2\pi n_{i}V_{0}^{2}}{\hbar}\langle u_{n\bm{k}}|u_{n^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}\rangle\langle u_{n^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}|u_{n\bm{k}}\rangle\delta(\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}}-\varepsilon_{n^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}})~.

(52)

The coordinate-shift component of the scattering rate is given by

w_{l^{\prime}l}^{(2),\mathrm{cs}}=\frac{2\pi}{\hbar}\langle|V_{l^{\prime}l}|^{2}\rangle_{\rm dis}\,\frac{\partial\delta(\varepsilon_{l}-\varepsilon_{l^{\prime}})}{\partial\varepsilon_{l}}\,e{\bm{E}}\cdot\delta{\bm{r}}_{l^{\prime}l}~,

(53)

which captures the coordinate-shift correction induced by the external electric field
\bm{E}
. This term originates from the real-space displacement of the electron wave packet during an impurity scattering event. The coordinate shift
\delta\bm{r}_{l^{\prime}l}
of the wave packet is given by
Sinitsyn et al. 2006

\delta{\bm{r}}_{l^{\prime}l}=\langle u_{l^{\prime}}|i\partial_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}|u_{l^{\prime}}\rangle-\langle u_{l}|i\partial_{\bm{k}}|u_{l}\rangle-(\partial_{\bm{k}}+\partial_{\bm{k}^{\prime}})\arg(V_{l^{\prime}l})~.

(54)

Here, the operator “
\arg
” denotes the phase (argument) of a complex number. This coordinate shift plays a crucial role in the side-jump contribution to spin transport. We now turn to the antisymmetric third-order scattering rate,

w^{(3),a}_{ll^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{2}(w_{ll^{\prime}}^{(3)}-w_{l^{\prime}l}^{(3)}).

(55)

Substituting Eq. (
47
) into this definition, we obtain

\displaystyle w_{ll^{\prime}}^{(3),a}

\displaystyle=(\pi/\hbar)\sum_{l^{\prime\prime}}\bigg[\frac{\langle V_{ll^{\prime\prime}}V_{l^{\prime\prime}l^{\prime}}V_{l^{\prime}l}\rangle_{\text{dis}}}{\varepsilon_{l}-\varepsilon_{l^{\prime\prime}}+i\eta}+\frac{\langle V_{ll^{\prime}}V_{l^{\prime}l^{\prime\prime}}V_{l^{\prime\prime}l}\rangle_{\text{dis}}}{\varepsilon_{l}-\varepsilon_{l^{\prime\prime}}-i\eta}-\frac{\langle V_{l^{\prime}l^{\prime\prime}}V_{l^{\prime\prime}l}V_{ll^{\prime}}\rangle_{\text{dis}}}{\varepsilon_{l}-\varepsilon_{l^{\prime\prime}}+i\eta}-\frac{\langle V_{l^{\prime}l}V_{ll^{\prime\prime}}V_{l^{\prime\prime}l^{\prime}}\rangle_{\text{dis}}}{\varepsilon_{l}-\varepsilon_{l^{\prime\prime}}-i\eta}\bigg]\delta(\varepsilon_{l}-\varepsilon_{l^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=(\pi/\hbar)\sum_{l^{\prime\prime}}\bigg[\langle V_{ll^{\prime\prime}}V_{l^{\prime\prime}l^{\prime}}V_{l^{\prime}l}\rangle_{\rm dis}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{l}-\varepsilon_{l^{\prime\prime}}+i\eta}-\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{l}-\varepsilon_{l^{\prime\prime}}-i\eta}\right)-cc.\bigg]\delta(\varepsilon_{l}-\varepsilon_{l^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=(4\pi^{2}/\hbar){\rm Im}\sum_{l^{\prime\prime}}\langle V_{ll^{\prime\prime}}V_{l^{\prime\prime}l^{\prime}}V_{l^{\prime}l}\rangle_{\rm dis}\delta(\varepsilon_{l}-\varepsilon_{l^{\prime}})\delta(\varepsilon_{l}-\varepsilon_{l^{\prime\prime}})

\displaystyle=(4\pi^{2}/\hbar){\rm Im}\sum_{n^{\prime\prime}}\sum_{\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}}\langle V_{{\bm{k}}{\bm{k}}^{\prime\prime}}^{0}V_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime\prime}{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}^{0}V_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}{\bm{k}}}^{0}\rangle_{\rm dis}\langle u_{n\bm{k}}|u_{n^{\prime\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}}\rangle\langle u_{n^{\prime\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}}|u_{n^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}\rangle\langle u_{n^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}|u_{n\bm{k}}\rangle\delta(\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}}-\varepsilon_{n^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}})\delta(\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}}-\varepsilon_{n^{\prime\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}})~.

(56)

With the same approximations as before, we first evaluate the disorder-averaged third-order impurity correlator as

\langle V_{{\bm{k}}{\bm{k}}^{\prime\prime}}^{0}V_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime\prime}{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}^{0}V_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}{\bm{k}}}^{0}\rangle_{\rm dis}=\sum_{i,j,k}V_{i}V_{j}V_{k}\exp\left(({\bm{k}}^{\prime\prime}-\bm{k})R_{i}+({\bm{k}}^{\prime}-{\bm{k}}^{\prime\prime})R_{j}+(\bm{k}-{\bm{k}}^{\prime})R_{k}\right)=n_{i}V_{1}^{3}~,

(57)

with
n_{i}
being the impurity concentration and
V_{1}
being the first moment of the impurity potential. Finally the antisymmetric third-order scattering rate (non-Gaussian skew scattering) takes the compact form

\displaystyle w_{ll^{\prime}}^{(3),a}=\frac{4\pi^{2}n_{i}V_{1}^{3}}{\hbar}{\rm Im}\sum_{\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}}\langle u_{n\bm{k}}|u_{n^{\prime\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}}\rangle\langle u_{n^{\prime\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}}|u_{n^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}\rangle\langle u_{n^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}|u_{n\bm{k}}\rangle\delta(\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}}-\varepsilon_{n^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}})\delta(\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}}-\varepsilon_{n^{\prime\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}})~.

(58)

Within the present set of approximations, this represents the maximal level of simplification. To proceed further, we need to explicitly evaluate the Bloch-state overlaps entering the disorder matrix elements. We note that the Bloch wave functions satisfy the orthogonality relation
\langle n\bm{k}|n^{\prime}{\bm{k}}^{\prime}\rangle=\delta_{{\bm{k}}{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}\delta_{nn^{\prime}}
, but the periodic part of the Bloch wave function need not satisfy the same relation, i.e.
\langle u_{n\bm{k}}|u_{n^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}\rangle\neq\delta_{{\bm{k}}{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}\delta_{nn^{\prime}}
. This means there can be finite overlap between
\ket{u_{ n' \bm k'}}
and
\ket{u_{ n \bm k}}
.

Here we assume that no inter-band transition occurs during scattering. This imposes
n=n^{\prime}
for
w^{(2S)}_{ll^{\prime}}
and
n=n^{\prime}=n^{\prime\prime}
for
w^{(3),a}_{ll^{\prime}}
. This condition effectively simplifies the Bloch-state overlaps, making them finite only in the regime of small momentum transfer
q=({\bm{k}}^{\prime}-\bm{k})\to 0
. Within this limit, we expand the Bloch state
\ket{u_{ n \kb'}}
around
\bm{k}
as

\ket{u_{ n \kb'}}=\ket{u_{ n \bm{k}}}+q_{b}\ket{\partial_b u_{ n \bm{k}}}+\frac{1}{2}q_{b}q_{c}\ket{\partial_b \partial_c u_{ n \bm{k}}}+\cdots~.

(59)

Here we adopt Einstein summation over the repeated coordinate index
b
. This leads to

\langle u_{n\bm{k}}|u_{n\bm{k}^{\prime}}\rangle\approx 1-iq_{b}\mathcal{R}_{nn}^{b}(\bm{k})\approx e^{-iq_{b}\mathcal{R}^{b}_{nn}(\bm{k})}~.

(60)

Using this in Eq. (
52
), we obtain

w^{(2S)}_{ll^{\prime}}=w_{n,{\bm{k}}{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}^{(2S)}=\frac{2\pi n_{i}V_{0}^{2}}{\hbar}|\langle u_{n\bm{k}}|u_{n\bm{k}^{\prime}}\rangle|^{2}\delta(\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}}-\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}^{\prime}})\approx\frac{2\pi n_{i}V_{0}^{2}}{\hbar}\delta(\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}}-\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}^{\prime}})~.

(61)

We now consider the case of
w_{ll^{\prime}}^{(3),a}
within the same limit. It contains
\langle u_{n\bm{k}}|u_{n\bm{k}^{\prime}}\rangle
,
\langle u_{n{\bm{k}}^{\prime\prime}}|u_{n{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}\rangle
and
\langle u_{n{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}|u_{n\bm{k}}\rangle
. We also assume
q^{\prime}=({\bm{k}}^{\prime\prime}-\bm{k})\to 0
, which leads to
\ket{u_{ n \kb''}}=\ket{u_{ n \bm{k}}}+q_{b}^{\prime}\ket{\partial_b u_{ n \bm{k}}}+\frac{1}{2}q_{b}^{\prime}q_{c}^{\prime}\ket{\partial_b \partial_c u_{ n \bm{k}}}+\cdots
. The above overlaps then evaluate to

\displaystyle\langle u_{n\bm{k}}|u_{n\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-iq_{b}^{\prime}\mathcal{R}^{b}_{nn}+\frac{1}{2}q_{b}^{\prime}q_{c}^{\prime}\langle u_{n\bm{k}}|\partial_{b}\partial_{c}u_{n\bm{k}}\rangle~,

\displaystyle\langle u_{n\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}}|u_{n\bm{k}^{\prime}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+i(q_{b}^{\prime}-q_{b})\mathcal{R}_{nn}^{b}+q_{b}q_{c}^{\prime}\langle\partial_{c}u_{n\bm{k}}|\partial_{b}u_{n\bm{k}}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}q_{b}q_{c}\langle u_{n\bm{k}}|\partial_{b}\partial_{c}u_{n\bm{k}}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}q_{b}^{\prime}q_{c}^{\prime}\langle\partial_{b}\partial_{c}u_{n\bm{k}}|u_{n\bm{k}}\rangle~,

\displaystyle\langle u_{n\bm{k}^{\prime}}|u_{n\bm{k}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+iq_{b}\mathcal{R}^{b}_{nn}+\frac{1}{2}q_{b}q_{c}\langle\partial_{b}\partial_{c}u_{n\bm{k}}|u_{n\bm{k}}\rangle~.

Using the above in Eq. (
C
), we can simplify
w_{n,{\bm{k}}{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}^{(3),a}
as follows

\displaystyle w^{(3),a}_{ll^{\prime}}=w_{n,{\bm{k}}{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}^{(3),a}

\displaystyle=\frac{4\pi^{2}n_{i}V_{1}^{3}}{\hbar}{\rm Im}\sum_{\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}}\langle u_{n\bm{k}}|u_{n\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}}\rangle\langle u_{n\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}}|u_{n\bm{k}^{\prime}}\rangle\langle u_{n\bm{k}^{\prime}}|u_{n\bm{k}}\rangle\delta(\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}}-\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}^{\prime}})\delta(\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}}-\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}})

\displaystyle=\frac{4\pi^{2}n_{i}V_{1}^{3}}{\hbar}\sum_{\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}}{\rm Im}[1+(q_{b}q_{c}+q_{b}^{\prime}q_{c}^{\prime}-q_{b}q_{c}^{\prime})\mathcal{R}^{b}_{nn}\mathcal{R}^{c}_{nn}+q_{b}q_{c}^{\prime}\langle\partial_{c}u_{n\bm{k}}|\partial_{b}u_{n\bm{k}}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+(q_{b}q_{c}+q_{b}^{\prime}q_{c}^{\prime}){\rm Re}(\langle u_{n\bm{k}}|\partial_{b}\partial_{c}u_{n\bm{k}}\rangle)]\delta(\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}}-\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}^{\prime}})\delta(\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}}-\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}})

\displaystyle=\frac{4\pi^{2}n_{i}V_{1}^{3}}{\hbar}\sum_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime\prime}}q_{b}q_{c}^{\prime}{\rm Im}[\langle\partial_{c}u_{n\bm{k}}|\partial_{b}u_{n\bm{k}}\rangle]\delta(\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}}-\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}^{\prime}})\delta(\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}}-\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}})

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\pi^{2}n_{i}V_{1}^{3}}{\hbar}\sum_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime\prime}}(k_{b}^{\prime}-k_{b})(k_{c}^{\prime\prime}-k_{c})\Omega^{bc}_{n}(\bm{k})\delta(\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}}-\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}^{\prime}})\delta(\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}}-\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}})

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi^{2}n_{i}V_{1}^{3}}{\hbar}\sum_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime\prime}}[(k_{b}^{\prime}-k_{b})(k_{c}^{\prime\prime}-k_{c})]\epsilon_{bcd}\Omega^{d}_{n}(\bm{k})\delta(\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}}-\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}^{\prime}})\delta(\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}}-\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}})

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi^{2}n_{i}V_{1}^{3}}{\hbar}\sum_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime\prime}}[({\bm{k}}^{\prime}-\bm{k})\times({\bm{k}}^{\prime\prime}-\bm{k})]\cdot\bm{\Omega}_{n}(\bm{k})\delta(\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}}-\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}^{\prime}})\delta(\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}}-\varepsilon_{n{\bm{k}}^{\prime\prime}})

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\pi^{2}n_{i}V_{1}^{3}}{\hbar}\sum_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime\prime}}[({\bm{k}}^{\prime\prime}\times{\bm{k}}^{\prime})+({\bm{k}}^{\prime}\times\bm{k})+(\bm{k}\times{\bm{k}}^{\prime\prime})]\cdot\bm{\Omega}_{n}(\bm{k})\delta(\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}}-\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}^{\prime}})\delta(\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}}-\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}})~.

(62)

Here
\Omega_{n}^{bc}
is the Berry curvature of the system and is defined as
\Omega_{n}^{bc}=-\Omega_{n}^{cb}=2\mathrm{Im}[\langle\partial_{c}u_{n\bm{k}}|\partial_{b}u_{n\bm{k}}\rangle]
for the
n
-th band.
