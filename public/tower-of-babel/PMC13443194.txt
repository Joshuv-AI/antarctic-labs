Non-response adjusted mean estimation using tool wear and torque data in predictive maintenance systems
Authors: N. Venkata Lakshmi, Faizan Danish, Javid Gani Dar, Fabio Armando Fuentes Gandara
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The study provides a hybrid log-exponential estimator for estimating the finite population mean under non-response, which incorporates auxiliary information and sample size efficiently within a coherent and mathematically structured framework. Unlike traditional ratio, exponential, and logarithmic estimators, the suggested estimator combines logarithmic and exponential transformations into a single functional form, which improves flexibility and stability under changing sampling conditions. Theoretical properties, such as bias and mean squared error are derived using first-order Taylor series approximation, and optimal conditions are obtained by minimizing mean squared error, providing a clear justification for the estimator’s efficiency gains. In particular, the inclusion of the sample size parameter contributes to variance stabilization and improved performance in finite samples. To ensure methodological consistency, both theoretical research and simulation design assume a Missing Completely at Random mechanism, with missing data handled via K-nearest neighbour imputation. The suggested estimator’s performance is assessed using comprehensive Monte Carlo simulations and empirical tests on a variety of real-world datasets, including radiation data, agricultural labour data, and tool wear and torque data from predictive maintenance systems. The results show that the proposed estimator consistently achieves lower MSE and higher percentage relative efficiency than existing estimators, with improvements frequently exceeding 50%, especially under moderate to high non-response rates and strong correlation structures, making it a reliable and practical alternative for finite population mean estimation in real-world applications.

Introduction

The primary objective of sampling theory is to derive reliable estimates of population parameters from sample data, frequently by adding auxiliary information to improve precision. However, the existence of non-response affects this relationship by lowering the efficiency of conventional estimators and increasing bias and variability. This difficulty emphasizes the necessity for robust estimators that can effectively handle missing data while efficiently utilizing auxiliary information, resulting in lower MSE and more reliable statistical inference.

The inclusion of auxiliary information has significantly influenced the development of efficient estimators in sampling theory. Early contributions by Cochran1 laid the groundwork with the introduction of ratio estimators, while Hansen and Hurwitz2 expanded on this framework to address non-response by offering an estimator for the finite population mean under non-respondent sub-sampling. Rao3,4, Rao5 and Subzar et al.6 built on this key research by developing many improved ratio and regression estimators that incorporate sub-sampling technique. Khare and Srivastava7, Sharma and Kumar8, Jan et al.9, Singh and Usman10, Kumar and Chhaparwal11, and Dansawad12 offered other developments in dealing with non-response using various approaches. Rehman et al.13 also presented regression-type and factor-type estimators specifically designed for non-response circumstances. Singh and Vishwakarma14, Singh et al.15, Grover and Kaur.16, Khare and Sinha17, Rachokarn and Lawson18, Khan et al.19, Eisa et al.20, Ilyas et al.21, and Asuthosh et al.22, as well as Sajjad et al.23, have proposed generalized classes of estimators to improve estimation efficiency. Triveni et al.24 and Unal and Kadilar25 developed log-type ratio estimators and exponential-type estimators, respectively, to increase estimation efficiency under non-response.

Despite substantial research on ratio, exponential, and log-type estimators in survey sampling, many existing approaches fail to maintain efficiency and consistency in real-world situations with fluctuating levels of non-response and unstable correlations between study and auxiliary variables. Most classical estimators are based on simplified assumptions and frequently address non-response in only one variable; even when extended to handle non-response in both variables, their performance degrades in the presence of complex data structures, nonlinear relationships, and significant missingness. These constraints become more obvious when non-response impacts both the study and the auxiliary variables at the same time, resulting in a significant loss of efficiency. As a result, there is an obvious need for a more robust and adaptable estimating approach that can effectively handle dual non-response scenarios while capturing underlying nonlinear patterns. Motivated by this gap, the current study proposes a hybrid log-exponential estimator that combines logarithmic and exponential transformations within a unified framework for estimating the finite population mean.

Methodology

This study uses a simple random sampling without replacement (SRSWOR) technique to choose a sample of size n from a finite population of size N, denoted as \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$S=\left\{{\vartheta}_{1},{\vartheta}_{2},\dots ,{\vartheta}_{N}\right\}$$\end{document}. The primary goal is to estimate the finite population mean of the study variable with minimal error in the presence of non-response. Let Y represent the study variable, and X represent the auxiliary variable used to improve estimation efficiency.

The analysis addresses two practical non-response scenarios: (i) non-response occurring just in the study variable (Y) and (ii) non-response affecting both the study and auxiliary variables Y and X, respectively. Following sample selection, the sampled units are partitioned into \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${w}_{1}=\frac{{n}_{1}}{n}$$\end{document}, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${w}_{2}=\frac{{n}_{2}}{n}$$\end{document}, respectively, where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${n}_{1}$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${n}_{2}$$\end{document} denote the number of respondents and non-respondents. To address non-response, the Hansen-Hurwitz sub-sampling strategy is used, where in a sub-sample of size ε is taken from the non-respondent group such that \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${n}_{2}=k\varepsilon ,$$\end{document} with k>1 representing the inverse sampling rate in the second phase.

The study employs the KNN imputation technique to handle missing observations arising from non-response since it is data-driven and may retain local data structure and underlying correlations between variables. Under this method, each missing value is imputed with the observed value from the nearest respondent using distance measures. Case I solely applies imputation to missing values in Y, while Case II imputes missing values in both Y and X prior to further analysis. The imputation procedure is implemented under the assumption of MCAR mechanism. Following the treatment of non-response, a hybrid log-exponential estimator is proposed for estimating the finite population mean with auxiliary data. The estimator is designed within a unified functional framework, and its statistical features are investigated using first-order Taylor series linearisation. Expressions for bias and MSE are calculated up to the first-order approximation. The minimized values for the unknown constants are derived by minimizing the MSE with standard differentiation techniques, resulting in increased efficiency of the proposed estimator.

To evaluate the performance of the proposed estimator, an extensive Monte Carlo simulation study is conducted. Artificial populations are generated under controlled settings and samples of varying sizes are repeatedly drawn. Non-response is introduced using a MCAR framework at different rates, followed by KNN imputation. The proposed estimator is then compared with existing classical estimators using standard performance measures such as MSE and PRE. This simulation design allows a systematic evaluation of estimator performance across different levels of non-response.

The suggested estimator

The suggested estimator is based on a hybrid log-exponential functional structure to estimate the finite population mean. The advantage of this formulation is that it efficiently captures both absolute and relative differences between the study variable and the auxiliary variable. The logarithmic component stabilizes variability and lowers scale effects, whereas the exponential component represents proportional changes between variables. This integrated structure allows for additional flexibility in addressing nonlinear connections without relying on strict linear assumptions. The estimator relies on unknown constants ξ and δ, which are optimized by reducing the MSE by traditional differentiation procedures. The incorporation of the sample size ‘n’ within the estimator improves its adaptability to various sampling settings. The bias and MSE expressions are obtained using a first-order Taylor series approximation within the MCAR framework and missing values are deal with the help of KNN imputation technique.

Dataset-III

An application to the Industrial Predictive Maintenance Dataset under Situation-I

The AI4I 2020 Predictive Maintenance Dataset28 is a synthetic dataset mimicking real-world industrial predictive maintenance settings. It has 10,000 observations with 14 variables, including air temperature, process temperature, rotational speed, torque, tool wear, and a binary machine failure indication based on several failure modes. Temperature and rotational speed are generated using normalised random walks and power-based calculations with stochastic noise, resulting in realistic distributional features.

For this investigation, the dataset is modelled as a finite population (N = 1000), from which a simple random sample (n = 100) is drawn. This enables the evaluation of finite population mean estimators under controlled sampling settings. Although the dataset was originally designed for predictive maintenance and machine learning, its structured, well-defined, and interconnected variables make it ideal for methodological analysis in a survey sampling environment.

Tool wear is a well-known measure of machining performance and equipment deterioration in predictive maintenance systems29,30. A recent study emphasises the need for sensor-based deterioration monitoring as a means of improving dependability and maintenance planning31. As a result, tool wear is chosen as the study variable and torque as the auxiliary variable due to their strong and interpretable relationship, which provides a solid foundation for comparing classical and proposed estimators.

To simulate real-world survey conditions, non-response is artificially produced in two scenarios: (i) non-response exclusively on the study variable (tool wear), and (ii) non-response on both the study and auxiliary variables. Non-response patterns are generated using controlled random processes at predetermined proportions (15%, 20% and 25%) to ensure reproducibility. This method enables the evaluation of estimator performance under realistic missing data settings within the framework of MCAR, which reflects original constraints experienced in industrial monitoring and survey sampling. Conclusion

The current study offers a hybrid log-exponential estimator meant to handle non-response issues in survey sampling. Traditional estimators, while successful in complete response settings, frequently lose stability and efficiency when either the study or auxiliary variable is missing. The suggested estimator, which implicitly incorporates sample size into its structure and incorporates log-exponential components, presents an adaptable and robust alternative capable of managing both single-variable and dual-variable non-responses. The estimator uses KNN imputation to preserve the underlying association between variables, boosting estimating precision even at moderate missingness levels of 15%, 20%, and 25% produced by the MCAR mechanism.

The proposed estimator’s performance was evaluated using Monte Carlo simulations on radiation and agricultural labour datasets, and it consistently produced lower MSE than four classical estimators and three families of generalized estimators, each with ten members. Further validation with the Tool Wear and Torque dataset, which displays nonlinear behaviour and sensor-induced non-response, demonstrated its robustness in complicated real-world scenarios. The results show that, while conventional estimators decline as the correlation between variables weakens, the suggested estimator remains stable and performs better. Overall, the findings show that the proposed estimator enables more efficient estimation of the limited population mean under diverse non-response conditions, with consistent decreases in MSE and associated improvements in PRE, showing its practical application with incomplete data scenarios.

Limitations and future scope

The current study has some limitations, which should be acknowledged. The theoretical features of the proposed estimator are calculated using the first-order Taylor series approximation, which, while commonly employed in survey sampling, may not adequately describe the estimator’s behaviour under higher variability or nonlinear situations. The research is carried out under the premise of a MCAR mechanism with KNN imputation. However, the explicit influence of imputation on bias and MSE has not been investigated analytically. Furthermore, the empirical study relies on a synthetic dataset created to replicate real-world processes, which may restrict the findings direct generalizability. Furthermore, the estimator’s performance has not been examined using different correlation structures or alternative non-response mechanisms.

These limitations point to various avenues for future investigation. Extending the research to include higher-order approximations and asymptotic features may reveal more theoretical insights. Further research into the impact of various imputation approaches on estimator performance would strengthen the methodology. The suggested estimator’s practical usefulness would be enhanced by applying it to real-world datasets from a variety of sectors, such as agriculture, health, and industrial surveys. Furthermore, a rigorous sensitivity analysis taking into account various correlation levels and non-response mechanisms, including more complex scenarios, would provide a more complete understanding of the estimator’s performance.
