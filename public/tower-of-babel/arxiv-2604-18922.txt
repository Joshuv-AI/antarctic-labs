A spinor proof of the classification of stable minimal surfaces in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3}
Douglas Stryker

Abstract
. We give a proof that every complete two–sided stable minimal surface in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} is flat using the index theory for Dirac operators on twisted spinor bundles.



1.
Introduction

We use the index theory for Dirac operators on twisted spinor bundles over noncompact manifolds introduced by
[

\mathbb{R}^{3}

Theorem 1.1

Every complete two–sided stable minimal immersion
\Sigma^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}

Each of the original proofs

1.2.
Spectral scalar curvature of stable minimal surfaces

To make use of Theorem
1.2
, we need to show that
\Sigma
has a positive lower bound on its spectral scalar curvature (as in (
1.1

By the stability inequality and the Gauss equation, any two–sided stable minimal immersion
M^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
satisfies

(1.2)

\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{2}+R_{M}\phi^{2}\geq 0

for all smooth, compactly supported functions
\phi
on
M

R_{M}
is the scalar curvature of

1.2
) gives us nonnegative spectral scalar curvature, but not
positive

Hence, we need to strengthen the lower bound on spectral scalar curvature. For this purpose, we recall the Hardy inequality for minimal hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
(see
[
6

(1.3)

\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{2}-\frac{(n-2)^{2}}{4\mathbf{r}^{2}}\phi^{2}\geq 0

for all smooth, compactly supported functions
\phi
on
M
, where
\mathbf{r}
is the Euclidean distance from the origin in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.

Combining (
1.2
) and (
1.3

(1.4)

\int_{M}2|\nabla\phi|^{2}+\left(R_{M}-\frac{(n-2)^{2}}{4\mathbf{r}^{2}}\right)\phi^{2}\geq 0

for any smooth compactly supported
\phi
. We emphasize that the function
h=\frac{(n-2)^{2}}{4\mathbf{r}^{2}}
is identically zero when
n=2
, which is precisely the case of interest. We overcome this issue by considering
\Sigma\times\mathbb{R}
, which is a complete two–sided stable minimal hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
when
\Sigma
is a complete two–sided stable minimal surface in
\mathbb{R}^{3}

1.4
),
\Sigma\times\mathbb{R}
satisfies the assumption of Theorem
1.2
for
\alpha=2
and
h=\frac{1}{4\mathbf{r}^{2}}>0
.

2.
Spectral scalar curvature notation

Let
(M,g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold, and let
Q:M\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function. We write

(2.1)

-\alpha\triangle+Q\geq 0

to mean that the following inequality holds for any compactly supported smooth function
\phi
:

\int_{M}\alpha|\nabla\phi|^{2}+Q\phi^{2}\geq 0.

In this paper, we consider the case where
Q=R_{M}-h
, where
R_{M}
is the scalar curvature of
M
and
h
is a smooth function. In this case, we say that the manifold has
spectral scalar curvature
bounded below by
h
. To justify the terminology, note that if
\alpha=0
, then (
2.1
) implies that
R_{M}\geq h
. Since
-\triangle
is a nonnegative operator, the condition (
2.1
) becomes weaker as
\alpha
gets larger.

3.
Twisted spinors and spectral scalar curvature

We recall the basic setup and topological facts related to twisted spinor bundles on noncompact Riemannian manifolds, following
[
24
, Chapter IV, §6]

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a complete Riemannian spin manifold of even dimension

. Let
\mathbf{S}_{\mathbb{C}}\to M
denote the complex spinor bundle of

with canonical metric connection.

Let

be a Hermitian vector bundle together with a metric connection that has

, meaning that the restriction of the bundle
E
to the complement of a compact subset of
M
is a trivial bundle with the trivial de Rham connection.

Let
\mathbf{D}_{E}
be the Atiyah–Singer Dirac operator acting on
L^{2}
–sections of the tensor product
\mathbf{S}_{\mathbb{C}}\otimes E
. By
[
24
, Chapter II, Theorem 5.7]
,
\mathbf{D}_{E}
is a self-adjoint operator.

By
[
24
, Chapter II, Theorem 8.17]
, we have the twisted Lichnerowicz formula

(3.1)

\mathbf{D}_{E}^{2}=\nabla^{*}\nabla+\frac{1}{4}R_{M}+\mathcal{R}^{E},

where
\mathcal{R}^{E}
, given by
[
24
, Chapter II, (8.22)]
, depends linearly on the curvature tensor of
E
. We note that, by assumption,
\mathcal{R}^{E}
vanishes outside a compact subset
K\subset M
. Let
c>0
denote a positive constant so that
-\mathcal{R}^{E}\leq c\ \mathrm{Id}
.

Since
M
is even dimensional, we can use the complex volume element to induce a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
–grading on the complex spinor bundle
\mathbf{S}_{\mathbb{C}}=\mathbf{S}_{\mathbb{C}}^{+}\oplus\mathbf{S}_{\mathbb{C}}^{-}
(see
[
24
, Chapter II, §6]
). With respect to the orthgonal decomposition
\mathbf{S}_{\mathbb{C}}\otimes E=(\mathbf{S}_{\mathbb{C}}^{+}\otimes E)\oplus(\mathbf{S}_{\mathbb{C}}^{-}\otimes E)
, the Dirac operator decomposes as

\mathbf{D}_{E}=\left(\begin{matrix}0&\mathbf{D}_{E}^{-}\\
\mathbf{D}_{E}^{+}&0\end{matrix}\right),

where
\mathbf{D}_{E}^{\pm}
sends sections of
\mathbf{S}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\pm}\otimes E
to sections of
\mathbf{S}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\mp}\otimes E
. Since
\mathbf{D}_{E}
is self-adjoint, we have
\mathrm{ker}(\mathbf{D}_{E}^{-})=\mathrm{coker}(\mathbf{D}_{E}^{+})
. In particular, if
\mathrm{ker}(\mathbf{D}_{E})
is finite dimensional, then both
\mathrm{ker}(\mathbf{D}_{E}^{+})
and
\mathrm{coker}(\mathbf{D}_{E}^{+})
are finite dimensional and

\mathrm{ind}(\mathbf{D}_{E}^{+})=\mathrm{dim}(\mathrm{ker}(\mathbf{D}_{E}^{+}))-\mathrm{dim}(\mathrm{ker}(\mathbf{D}_{E}^{-})).

We note that, in this case, if
\mathbf{D}_{E}^{+}
has nonzero index, then there is a nonzero
L^{2}
section
\psi
of
\mathbf{S}_{C}\otimes E
satisfying
\mathbf{D}_{E}\psi=0
.

•

Suppose otherwise for contradiction, so such a map

\mathbb{S}^{2}

For ease of notation, let

4.
Stable minimal surfaces

We apply the Dirac index theory developed in the previous section to give a new proof of the classification of complete two–sided stable minimal surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

4.1.
Spectral scalar curvature of stable minimal surfaces

We prove a positive–but–decaying lower bound for the spectral scalar curvature of a two–sided stable minimal surface in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, taking advantage of the Hardy inequality for minimal hypersurfaces (see
[
6
]
). Since the Hardy inequality is only effective for minimal hypersurfaces in dimension 4 and higher, we take a product with
\mathbb{R}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a two–sided stable minimal immersion, and let
g
denote the pullback metric on
\Sigma
. Consider the product manifold

(M,\tilde{g})\coloneq(\Sigma\times\mathbb{R},g\oplus dt^{2}).

Let
\mathbf{r}
be the restriction of the Euclidean distance in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
from
\vec{0}
to the
\mathbb{R}
–invariant immersion
M\to\mathbb{R}^{4}
. For any
\beta>0
, we have

(4.1)

-(1+\beta)\triangle_{M}+R_{M}-\frac{\beta}{4\mathbf{r}^{2}}\geq 0.

Proof.

We note that
\tilde{g}
is the pullback metric on
M
for the
\mathbb{R}
–invariant immersion
M\to\mathbb{R}^{4}
.
Moreover, the immersion
M\to\mathbb{R}^{4}
is also a two–sided stable minimal immersion. Indeed, two–sided stability is equivalent to the inequality

-\triangle_{\Sigma}-|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}\geq 0.

By
[
17
, Theorem 1]
, there is a positive function
u:\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
so that

-\triangle_{\Sigma}u-|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}u=0.

Let
\tilde{u}
denote the
\mathbb{R}
–invariant extension of
u
to
\Sigma\times\mathbb{R}
. Since
|A_{M}|^{2}=|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}
, the positive function
\tilde{u}
satisfies

-\triangle_{M}\tilde{u}-|A_{M}|^{2}\tilde{u}=0.

Then by
[
17
, Theorem 1]
,
M
is stable.

By the Gauss equation and the minimality of
M
, we know that
M
has nonnegative spectral scalar curvature:

(4.2)

-\triangle_{M}+R_{M}\geq 0.

By the Hardy inequality for minimal hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
(see
[
6
, (1.5)]
), we have

(4.3)

-\triangle_{M}-\frac{1}{4\mathbf{r}^{2}}\geq 0.

Adding (
4.2
) to
\beta
times (
4.3
) gives (
4.1
).
∎

4.2.
Construction of contracting map

Here we aim to construct a degree one map from
\Sigma\times\mathbb{R}
to
\mathbb{S}^{3}
that is sufficiently contracting on 2–forms.

We begin with two preliminary propositions about Cartan–Hadamard manifolds. These results are standard in Riemannian geometry, but we include the details of the precise formulations required for our construction.

The first preliminary proposition relates to the gradient of the radial projection maps.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a complete simply connected manifold with nonpositive sectional curvature, and fix
x_{0}\in M
. Then
\exp_{x_{0}}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to M
is a diffeomorphism. For
0<\rho<R
, the radial projection map

\pi_{R,\rho}:\partial B_{R}(x_{0})\to\partial B_{\rho}(x_{0}),\ \ \pi_{R,\rho}(x)\coloneq\exp_{x_{0}}\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\exp_{x_{0}}^{-1}(x)\right)

is smooth, depends smoothly on
R
and
\rho
, and satisfies

|\nabla\pi_{R,\rho}|\leq\frac{\rho}{R}.

Proof.

The fact that
\exp_{x_{0}}
is a diffeomorphism is the classical theorem of Cartan and Hadamard (see for instance
[
30
, Theorem 6.2.2]
. The fact that
\pi_{R,\rho}
is smooth and depends smoothly on
R
and
\rho
follows from the fact that
\exp_{x_{0}}
is a diffeomorphism and dilation in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by
\frac{\rho}{R}
is a smooth map depending smoothly on
R
and
\rho
.

For the gradient bound, let us write the metric
g
in geodesic normal coordinates centered at
x_{0}
. Then
g=dr^{2}+g_{r}
. By
[
30
, Proposition 3.2.11]
, we have

\frac{\partial}{\partial r}g_{r}=L_{\partial r}g=2\mathrm{Hess}\ r.

By the Rauch comparison in nonpositive curvature (see
[
30
, Theorem 6.4.3]
), we have

\mathrm{Hess}\ r\geq\frac{1}{r}g_{r}.

Let
v
be a unit vector tangent to
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. In these polar coordinates, we have along radial geodesics

\log\left(\frac{\sqrt{g_{R}(v,v)}}{\sqrt{g_{\rho}(v,v)}}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\frac{g_{R}(v,v)}{g_{\rho}(v,v)}\right)\geq\int_{\rho}^{R}\frac{1}{r}dr=\log(R/\rho).

In other words (recalling that
\sqrt{g(v,v)}
is the length of
v
),

g_{R}\geq\frac{R}{\rho}g_{\rho}.

Since
\pi_{R,\rho}
is the identity on the spherical part of the polar coordinates, we have the desired gradient estimate.
∎

The second preliminary proposition establishes a monotonicity formula for the area of geodesic balls.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
(\Sigma^{2},g)
be a complete simply connected surface with nonpositive curvature, and fix
x_{0}\in\Sigma
. Let

a(r)\coloneq\mathrm{Area}(B_{r}(x_{0})),\ \ l(r)\coloneq\mathrm{Length}(\partial B_{r}(x_{0})).

Then

\left(\frac{a(r)}{\pi r^{2}}\right)^{\prime}\geq 0\ \ \mathrm{and}\ \ l(r)\geq\frac{2a(r)}{r}.

Proof.

The second inequality follows directly from the first inequality. For the first inequality, working in geodesics polar coordinates centered at
x_{0}
, we have

\mathrm{area}=\lambda(r,\theta)dr\wedge d\theta.

Recall by
[
30
, §2.1.3]
that we have

(\partial_{r}\lambda)dr\wedge d\theta=L_{\partial_{r}}\mathrm{area}=(\triangle r)\mathrm{area}=(\triangle r)\lambda dr\wedge d\theta.

By the Rauch comparison (see
[
30
, Theorem 6.4.3]
), we have

\triangle r\geq r^{-1}.

Hence,

\partial_{r}\lambda\geq r^{-1}\lambda.

We compute

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dr}\left(\frac{a(r)}{\pi r^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dr}\left(\frac{\int_{0}^{r}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\lambda(\rho,\theta)d\rho\wedge d\theta}{\pi r^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi r^{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\lambda(r,\theta)d\theta-2\pi r\int_{0}^{r}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\lambda(\rho,\theta)d\rho\wedge d\theta}{(\pi r^{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\int_{0}^{r}\left[(2\pi\rho\int_{0}^{2\pi}\lambda(r,\theta)d\theta-2\pi r\int_{0}^{2\pi}\lambda(\rho,\theta)d\theta\right]d\rho}{(\pi r^{2})^{2}}.

To show that this derivative is nonnegative, it suffices to check that

r\mapsto\frac{\int_{0}^{2\pi}\lambda(r,\theta)d\theta}{2\pi r}

is nondecreasing (since the limit as
r\to 0
is 1). We compute

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dr}\left(\frac{\int_{0}^{2\pi}\lambda(r,\theta)d\theta}{2\pi r}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi r\int_{0}^{2\pi}\partial_{r}\lambda d\theta-2\pi\int_{0}^{2\pi}\lambda d\theta}{(2\pi r)^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2\pi r\int_{0}^{2\pi}r^{-1}\lambda d\theta-2\pi\int_{0}^{2\pi}\lambda d\theta}{(2\pi r)^{2}}=0,

which concludes the proof.
∎
