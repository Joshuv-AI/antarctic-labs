A note on conserved worldsheet supercharges in heterotic pure spinor superstring
Osvaldo Chandia

Abstract




We study conserved worldsheet charges associated with spacetime supersymmetry in heterotic pure-spinor superstrings on curved ten-dimensional superspace backgrounds. Requiring BRST invariance and worldsheet conservation gives a covariant set of superspace constraints. In flat superspace these conditions reproduce the standard ten-dimensional supersymmetry generator. In curved superspace, they organize the requirements for global supersymmetry in terms of a normalizable spinor superfield.

Introduction

It is widely accepted that string theory or any theory of quantum gravity possesses no global symmetries
[

[

Nevertheless, the conserved worldsheet currents and the associated conserved charges are useful for assessing the symmetries of a fixed string theory background. With this idea, we will study when a general four-dimensional supergravity background admits global supersymmetry. In past works

4
Construction of the supercharge

4.1
Flat superspace

As a consistency check, consider flat ten-dimensional superspace. All curvature and torsion components beyond
T_{\alpha\beta}{}^{a}=-(\gamma^{a})_{\alpha\beta}
vanish, so the general conditions for the existence of a Grassmann-odd conserved charge reduce to

\displaystyle D_{\alpha}\chi^{\beta}+g_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}=0,

(4.1)

\displaystyle D_{(\alpha}f_{\beta)}=\gamma^{a}_{\alpha\beta}f_{a},

(4.2)

\displaystyle D_{\alpha}f_{a}-\partial_{a}f_{\alpha}=-(\gamma_{a}\chi)_{\alpha},

(4.3)

\displaystyle\partial_{a}\chi^{\alpha}=0,\qquad\partial_{a}f_{\alpha}=0,

(4.4)

\displaystyle\lambda^{\alpha}\lambda^{\beta}D_{\alpha}g_{\beta}{}^{\gamma}=0,\quad\partial_{a}g_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}=0.

(4.5)

From (
3.36
) one can immediately see that

Using this, equation (

\displaystyle\chi^{\alpha}=\epsilon^{\alpha}.

Substituting this expression into (
4.2
) fixes the quadratic term in
f_{\alpha}
. With the ansatz

\displaystyle f_{\alpha}=A(\gamma_{a}\theta)_{\alpha}(\theta\gamma^{a}\epsilon),

(4.8)

one finds
A=1/3
, namely

\displaystyle f_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{3}(\gamma_{a}\theta)_{\alpha}(\theta\gamma^{a}\epsilon).

(4.9)

Therefore, the covariant equations derived above reproduce the standard flat-space supersymmetry charge,

\displaystyle q_{\epsilon}=\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}\oint\left[\epsilon^{\alpha}d_{\alpha}+(\epsilon\gamma_{a}\theta)\Pi^{a}+\frac{1}{3}(\gamma_{a}\theta)_{\alpha}(\theta\gamma^{a}\epsilon)\Pi^{\alpha}\right].

(4.10)

This fixes the normalization of the compensating currents and provides the simplest check that the curved-background constraints have the correct flat limit.
Using the flat-space OPEs, this charge generates the standard supersymmetry transformations of flat superspace, up to normalization
conventions for
d_{\alpha}
.

4.2
Curved-superspace expansion

Constructing the explicit component expansion of the conserved supercharge is not as simple as in the flat superspace case.
The standard way of computing the component expansion in a curved superspace is to use normal-coordinate expansion in superspace
[
26
]
. Around a point
Z_{0}
one introduces tangent normal coordinates
Y^{A}
and expands superfields covariantly along the corresponding superspace geodesic. For the present purpose it is enough to use fermionic normal coordinates, namely to expand only in the Grassmann-odd directions
Y^{A}=(0,\theta^{\alpha})

[
21
,
22
]
. The background dependence of the expansion is encoded in the normal-coordinate expansion of the background tensors, such as torsion, curvature, the
H
-field, dilaton, and their covariant derivatives. Once this background geometry is fixed, the only additional information needed to determine the coefficients in the expansions of
\chi^{\alpha}
,
f_{a}
and
f_{\alpha}
is the set of multiple Grassmann-odd covariant derivatives

\displaystyle\nabla_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\nabla_{\alpha_{n}}f_{a}\big|_{\theta=0},\qquad\nabla_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\nabla_{\alpha_{n}}f_{\beta}\big|_{\theta=0},

(4.11)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\nabla_{\alpha_{n}}\chi^{\beta}\big|_{\theta=0}=-\nabla_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\nabla_{\alpha_{n-1}}g_{\alpha_{n}}{}^{\beta}\big|_{\theta=0},

(4.12)

which are obtained recursively from the constraint equations derived in Section
3
and (
3.36
).

The procedure is simplified by first considering dimensional analysis and a purely bosonic background at
\theta=0
. The supercharge
q_{\epsilon}
should have mass dimension zero. With the conventions chosen in (
2.2
), the worldsheet currents
(d_{\alpha},\Pi^{a},\Pi^{\alpha},\omega_{\alpha}\lambda^{\beta})
have mass dimensions
(-3/2,-1,-1/2,-2)
. The supersymmetry parameter
\chi^{\alpha}
has mass dimension
-1/2
,
f_{a}
has mass dimension
-1
,
f_{{\alpha}}
has mass dimension
-3/2
and
g_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}
has mass dimension
0
. The lowest mass-dimensional background tensors that can appear in
q_{\epsilon}
are
\nabla_{a}\Phi
and
H_{abc}
. These considerations lead us to choose the lowest components of
\chi^{\alpha}
,
f_{\alpha}
,
f_{a}
and
g_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}
to satisfy

\displaystyle\chi^{\alpha}\big|_{\theta=0}=\chi^{\alpha}_{0},

(4.13)

\displaystyle f_{{\alpha}}\big|_{\theta=0}=\nabla_{\gamma}f_{{\alpha}}\big|_{\theta=0}=0,

(4.14)

\displaystyle f_{a}\big|_{\theta=0}=0,

(4.15)

\displaystyle g_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}\big|_{\theta=0}=0.

(4.16)

Equations (
3.13
) and (
3.18
) and the absence of Grassmann-odd background fields imply

\displaystyle\nabla_{\gamma}f_{a}\big|_{\theta=0}=-(\chi_{0}\gamma_{a})_{\gamma},

(4.17)

Furthermore, equation (
3.12
) implies that

\displaystyle\nabla_{\beta}f_{{\gamma}}=\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{a}_{\beta\gamma}f_{a}+G_{\beta\gamma},

(4.18)

where
G_{\beta\gamma}
is antisymmetric in
\beta
and
\gamma
. Adding a total derivative to
q_{\epsilon}
changes
f_{A}
to
f_{A}+\nabla_{A}\Lambda
. Inserting this into (
4.18
), we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{\beta}f_{{\gamma}}+\nabla_{\beta}\nabla_{\gamma}\Lambda=\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{a}_{\beta\gamma}f_{a}+\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{a}_{\beta\gamma}\nabla_{a}\Lambda+G_{\beta\gamma}.

(4.19)

Using
\nabla_{\beta}\nabla_{\gamma}\Lambda=\frac{1}{2}[\nabla_{\beta},\nabla_{\gamma}]\Lambda+\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{a}_{\beta\gamma}\nabla_{a}\Lambda
, we obtain

\displaystyle\nabla_{\beta}f_{{\gamma}}+\frac{1}{2}[\nabla_{\beta},\nabla_{\gamma}]\Lambda=\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{a}_{\beta\gamma}f_{a}+G_{\beta\gamma}.

(4.20)

Locally we can choose
\Lambda
to eliminate
G_{\beta\gamma}
. Using this gauge choice, the second derivative of
f_{\gamma}
can be computed

\displaystyle\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla_{\beta}f_{\gamma}=-\frac{1}{4}\gamma^{a}_{\beta\gamma}\nabla_{\alpha}\Phi f_{a}+\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{a}_{\beta\gamma}\nabla_{\alpha}f_{a}.

(4.21)

Since
\nabla_{\alpha}\Phi\big|_{\theta=0}=0
,

\displaystyle\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla_{\beta}f_{\gamma}\big|_{\theta=0}=-\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{a}_{\beta\gamma}(\chi_{0}\gamma_{a})_{\alpha}.

(4.22)

Up to this order in the expansion, we obtain exactly the flat space result. The second-order derivative of
f_{\alpha}
in (
4.22
) is the expected result from (
4.9
) using the gamma matrix identity

\displaystyle(\gamma^{a})_{\alpha\beta}(\gamma_{a})_{\gamma\delta}+(\gamma^{a})_{\beta\gamma}(\gamma_{a})_{\alpha\delta}+(\gamma^{a})_{\gamma\alpha}(\gamma_{a})_{\beta\delta}=0.

(4.23)

The background tensors appear in the next non-trivial derivatives. The next non-vanishing derivatives of
\chi^{\alpha}
can be computed from (
3.11
) and (
3.36
):

\displaystyle\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla_{\beta}\chi^{\gamma}\big|_{\theta=0}

\displaystyle=-(\nabla_{\alpha}g_{\beta}{}^{\gamma})\big|_{\theta=0}=\delta_{\beta}^{\gamma}\nabla_{\alpha}g^{(0)}\big|_{\theta=0}-\frac{1}{4}(\gamma^{ab})_{\beta}{}^{\gamma}\nabla_{\alpha}g^{(2)}_{ab}\big|_{\theta=0},

(4.24)

where

\displaystyle\nabla_{\alpha}g^{(0)}\big|_{\theta=0}=-\frac{1}{4}\chi_{0}^{\beta}\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla_{\beta}\Phi\big|_{\theta=0},

(4.25)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\alpha}g^{(2)}_{ab}\big|_{\theta=0}=H_{ab}{}^{c}\big|_{\theta=0}(\chi_{0}\gamma_{c})_{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}\chi_{0}^{\beta}(\gamma_{ab})_{\beta}{}^{\gamma}\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla_{\gamma}\Phi\big|_{\theta=0},

(4.26)

where
\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla_{\beta}\Phi
is given by (
2.15
).

Using again (
3.13
), (
3.18
), (
4.13
) and the on-shell supergravity constraints we obtain for
f_{a}

\displaystyle\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla_{\beta}\nabla_{\gamma}f_{a}\big|_{\theta=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\gamma_{abc})_{\alpha\beta}(\gamma^{b}\chi_{0})_{\gamma}\nabla^{c}\Phi\big|_{\theta=0}-\frac{1}{48}(\gamma^{bcd})_{\alpha\beta}(\gamma_{a}\chi_{0})_{\gamma}H_{bcd}\big|_{\theta=0}

(4.27)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{8}(\gamma_{abc})_{\alpha\beta}(\gamma_{d}\chi_{0})_{\gamma}H^{bcd}\big|_{\theta=0}+\frac{1}{8}(\gamma^{bcd})_{\alpha\beta}(\gamma_{b}\chi_{0})_{\gamma}H_{acd}\big|_{\theta=0}

(4.28)

\displaystyle-(\gamma_{a})_{\gamma\delta}\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla_{\beta}\chi^{\delta}\big|_{\theta=0}.

(4.29)

Similarly, using (
4.21
) we obtain

\displaystyle\nabla_{\alpha_{1}}\nabla_{\alpha_{2}}\nabla_{\alpha_{3}}\nabla_{\alpha_{4}}f_{\beta}\big|_{\theta=0}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}\gamma^{a}_{\beta\alpha_{4}}(\nabla_{\alpha_{2}}\nabla_{\alpha_{3}}\Phi)\big|_{\theta=0}(\chi_{0}\gamma_{a})_{\alpha_{1}}

(4.30)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\gamma^{a}_{\beta\alpha_{4}}(\nabla_{\alpha_{1}}\nabla_{\alpha_{3}}\Phi)\big|_{\theta=0}(\chi_{0}\gamma_{a})_{\alpha_{2}}

(4.31)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\gamma^{a}_{\beta\alpha_{4}}(\nabla_{\alpha_{1}}\nabla_{\alpha_{2}}\Phi)\big|_{\theta=0}(\chi_{0}\gamma_{a})_{\alpha_{3}}+\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{a}_{\beta\alpha_{4}}\nabla_{\alpha_{1}}\nabla_{\alpha_{2}}\nabla_{\alpha_{3}}f_{a}\big|_{\theta=0}.

(4.32)

Higher-order terms are obtained recursively in the same way, although the expressions rapidly become lengthy. In the next order, there will be terms with
(\nabla\Phi)^{2}
,
\nabla\Phi H
,
H^{2}
, and the curvature.

To close this section, let us analyze the
|_{\theta=0}
component of (
3.45
). Since
T_{ab}{}^{\alpha}
is Grassmann odd,
T_{ab}{}^{\alpha}|_{\theta=0}=0
. Using (
4.13
) we obtain

\displaystyle-(\gamma_{a})^{\gamma\alpha}\left(\frac{1}{4}(\chi_{0}\gamma^{b})_{\alpha}\nabla_{b}\Phi\big|_{\theta=0}-\chi_{0}^{\beta}\nabla_{\alpha}\Omega_{\beta}\big|_{\theta=0}\right)

(4.33)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}(\gamma_{a}\gamma^{bc})^{\alpha\gamma}\left(-\chi_{0}^{\rho}\nabla_{(\rho}T_{\alpha)bc}\big|_{\theta=0}+2(\chi_{0}\gamma_{bc})^{\rho}\nabla_{\alpha}\Omega_{\rho}\big|_{\theta=0}\right)=0,

(4.34)

recall that
T_{\alpha ab}=2(\gamma_{ab}\Omega)_{\alpha}
and that

\displaystyle\nabla_{\alpha}\Omega_{\beta}=\frac{1}{8}\gamma^{a}_{\alpha\beta}\nabla_{a}\Phi-\frac{1}{96}\gamma^{abc}_{\alpha\beta}H_{abc},

(4.35)

we obtain that (
4.33
) is equal to

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{8}\gamma_{a}^{\alpha\gamma}\chi_{0}^{\beta}\left(\gamma^{b}_{\beta\alpha}\nabla_{b}\Phi\big|_{\theta=0}-\frac{1}{12}\gamma^{bcd}_{\beta\alpha}H_{bcd}\big|_{\theta=0}\right)

(4.36)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{16}(\gamma_{bc}\gamma_{a}\gamma^{bc})^{\alpha\gamma}\chi_{0}^{\beta}\left(\gamma^{b}_{\beta\alpha}\nabla_{b}\Phi\big|_{\theta=0}-\frac{1}{12}\gamma^{bcd}_{\beta\alpha}H_{bcd}\big|_{\theta=0}\right)

(4.37)

\displaystyle=\frac{13}{4}\gamma_{a}^{\alpha\gamma}\chi_{0}^{\beta}\left(\gamma^{b}_{\beta\alpha}\nabla_{b}\Phi\big|_{\theta=0}-\frac{1}{12}\gamma^{bcd}_{\beta\alpha}H_{bcd}\big|_{\theta=0}\right)=0,

(4.38)

which is proportional to the supersymmetry transformation of the dilatino. As we mentioned at the end of Section
3
, the combined equations (
3.11
) and (
3.17
) constrain the class of backgrounds admitting a global Grassmann odd symmetry. This final result strongly implies that the only possible symmetry of this type is indeed supersymmetry.

4.3
Four dimensional compactification

The derivation above was kept ten-dimensional and covariant. We now briefly explain how the result is interpreted after compactification to four dimensions. By a
4+6
compactification, we mean that, locally, the ten-dimensional target space is viewed as a fibration in which the non-compact directions describe four-dimensional spacetime. In contrast, the remaining six directions are internal. Correspondingly, tangent ten-dimensional vector indices are decomposed as
a=(\mu,i)
where
\mu=0,\ldots,3
labels the four-dimensional spacetime directions and

i=1,\ldots,6
labels the internal directions. We will maintain
a
as the index for all bosonic directions.

The ten-dimensional Majorana-Weyl spinor index is similarly decomposed under the corresponding subgroup of the Lorentz group,
SO(9,1)\rightarrow SO(3,1)\times SO(6).
Schematically, we write
\alpha\rightarrow({\alpha}_{I},\dot{\alpha}{}^{I}),

where
\alpha,\dot{\alpha}
are now four-dimensional Weyl spinor indices and
I
denotes an internal
SU(4)
spinor index. Note that
(\alpha_{I})^{\dagger}=(\dot{\alpha}^{I})
. This notation is only meant to indicate the representation content. We do not need an explicit choice of
4+6
gamma matrices for the derivation above.

The spinor superfield
\chi^{\alpha}
then determines
which four-dimensional supersymmetry is generated by the worldsheet charge. For a background preserving a four-dimensional
N=1
supersymmetry, one expects the corresponding ten-dimensional spinor parameter to factorize schematically as

\displaystyle\chi^{\alpha}\rightarrow\chi^{\alpha I}\oplus\bar{\chi}_{\dot{\alpha}I}=\epsilon^{\alpha}\,\eta^{I}(Z)\oplus\bar{\epsilon}_{\dot{\alpha}}\,\bar{\eta}_{I}(Z),

(4.39)

where
\epsilon^{\alpha}
and
\bar{\epsilon}_{\dot{\alpha}}
are the four-dimensional supersymmetry parameters
while
\eta^{I}(Z)
and
\bar{\eta}_{I}(Z)
are internal spinors depending on the compact bosonic coordinates and all the Grassmann-odd coordinates. If
\eta^{I}(Z)
and
\bar{\eta}_{I}(Z)
are normalizable, i.e.,
\eta^{I}\bar{\eta}_{I}\neq 0
globally, it is said that the internal manifold has
SU(3)
structure. In this interpretation, the covariant equations obtained in Section
3
are the worldsheet-current form of the supersymmetry-preservation conditions. Rather than imposing a component Killing-spinor equation from the supergravity point of view from the beginning, we characterize a preserved supersymmetry by the existence of superfields
(\eta^{I},\bar{\eta}_{I},f_{\alpha I},\bar{f}_{\dot{\alpha}}{}^{I},f_{\mu},f_{i})
satisfying the conditions derived in Section
3
.

In the case of a compactification with four-dimensional Poincaré symmetry, we will assume

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\Phi\big|_{\theta=0}=H_{\mu\bullet\bullet}\big|_{\theta=0}=R_{\mu\bullet}{}^{\bullet\bullet}\big|_{\theta=0}=R_{\bullet\bullet}{}^{\mu\bullet}\big|_{\theta=0}=0,

(4.40)

where
\bullet
means four-dimensional or six-dimensional tangent vector indices. These assumptions are imposed only on the lowest components. The higher components are constrained recursively by the superspace equations. Imposing the same conditions on the full superfields would be unnecessarily restrictive. The torsions
(T_{\mu\nu}{}^{\alpha I},T_{\mu i}{}^{\alpha I},T_{\alpha I\mu}{}^{\nu},T_{\alpha I\mu}{}^{i})
and curvature
R_{\mu\alpha I}{}^{\bullet\bullet}
, and their conjugates, are Grassmann odd; their
\theta=0
components identically vanish in a purely bosonic background.

Since we are using (
4.39
), the equation (
4.36
) gives two equations: one proportional to
\epsilon_{\alpha}
and the other proportional to
\epsilon_{\dot{\alpha}}
. The first is

\displaystyle\eta^{I}\left(\gamma^{i}_{IJ}\nabla_{i}\Phi-\frac{1}{12}(\gamma^{ijk})_{IJ}H_{ijk}\right)=0.

(4.41)

The
\theta=0
projection of the left-hand side of the equation above is the supersymmetry transformation of the dilatino, which should vanish in supersymmetric backgrounds with torsion, as first analyzed in the seminal work
[
27
]
.

The superspace equation (
3.17
) at
\theta=0
gives

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}(\epsilon\eta)\big|_{\theta=0}=0,\quad\nabla_{i}(\epsilon\eta)\big|_{\theta=0}=0.

(4.42)

The first, given the assumptions (
4.40
), is just the constancy of the supersymmetry parameter in the four-dimensional spacetime. To interpret the second one, we must remember that the connection in
\nabla_{i}
is torsionful, so it corresponds to the textbook local supersymmetry transformation for the gravitino. This condition only implies Ricci flatness if the dilaton is constant and
H=0
. Acting with
\nabla_{[\alpha}\nabla_{\beta]}
on (
3.17
), projecting to
\theta=0
and using (
4.13
), we obtain

\displaystyle\nabla_{a}\nabla_{[\alpha}g_{\beta]}{}^{\gamma}\big|_{\theta=0}+\chi^{\sigma}_{0}\nabla_{[\alpha}R_{\beta]a\sigma}{}^{\gamma}\big|_{\theta=0}+2(\chi_{0}\gamma^{b})_{[\alpha}\nabla_{\beta]}T_{ab}{}^{\gamma}\big|_{\theta=0}=0,

(4.43)

which will impose an algebraic relation between
\chi
,
\nabla^{2}\Phi
,
(\nabla\Phi)^{2}
,
\nabla H
,
\nabla\Phi H
,
H^{2}
and the curvature.

The important point is that these equations were not imposed directly as component supersymmetry variations. They arise as projections of the covariant conditions required for the existence of a conserved worldsheet charge. The full superspace system determines the higher
\theta
-components of
\chi^{\alpha}
, together with the compensating superfields
f_{A}
and
g_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}
, recursively in terms of the background torsion, curvature, flux and dilaton. A complete solution of this recursive system would give the full superspace lift of the four-dimensional preserved supercharge. In this paper we only need the lowest-component projection, which shows how the usual compactification conditions are encoded in the conserved-current construction.

5
Conclusions and prospects

In this note we formulated the existence of conserved worldsheet charges associated with four-dimensional supersymmetry directly in ten-dimensional heterotic superspace. Starting from the curved-background pure-spinor action, we imposed BRST invariance and worldsheet conservation on the general charge (
3.3
). The resulting covariant system, summarized in (
3.11
)–(
3.14
) and (
3.17
)–(
3.20
), gives the superspace conditions on the spinor superfield
\chi
and on the compensating coefficients
f_{A}
and
g_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}
.

The flat-superspace analysis provides a useful normalization and consistency check. The solution (
4.10
) reproduces the standard spacetime supersymmetry generator, with
\epsilon^{\alpha}
denoting the constant supersymmetry parameter. For curved backgrounds, the same equations provide a covariant starting point for discussing four-dimensional global supersymmetry without introducing an explicit component decomposition in the main derivation. In the compactification interpretation, the lowest component of
\chi
is naturally identified with the internal spinor that selects the preserved supersymmetry. In contrast, the higher
\theta
components and the coefficients
f_{A}
are determined recursively by the background torsion, curvature, flux, and dilaton data. A complete solution of this recursive system and its comparison with the usual component Killing-spinor equations are natural next steps.

Throughout the analysis, we have worked at the classical level in worldsheet theory; possible quantum corrections to current conservation are not addressed here. Another interesting direction is to include a gauge background and the heterotic fermions in the conserved charge. This will probably give a way to describe the heterotic string in
AdS_{4}
with a gaugino condensate
[
20
]
. A more speculative project is to use
\chi
and related superfields to construct worldsheet holomorphic currents to describe
N=2
superconformal generators, which naturally appear in RNS Calabi-Yau compactifications. The methods used to construct the
b
ghost in the pure spinor formalism

[
18
,
4
,
14
]
may help in this direction. Finally, we would like to comment about a new superstring formalism that mixes RNS, GS and pure spinors strings
[
8
]
. The corresponding versions in curved backgrounds were developed in
[
3
]
,
[
9
]
and
[
19
]
. It would be interesting to adapt our methods to this new formalism.
