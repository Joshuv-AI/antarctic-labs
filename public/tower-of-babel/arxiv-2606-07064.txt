An inverse source problem for the Monge–Ampere equation from large boundary data
Cătălin I. Cârstea, Tuhin Ghosh

Abstract
We study an inverse source problem for the Monge–Ampere equation det D 2 ​ u = f ⁡ ( x ) \det D^{2}u=f(x) on a bounded smooth uniformly convex domain. In the smooth classical regime, we prove that the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map associated with convex solutions determines the positive source uniquely. The proof uses a family of large boundary values and reduces the inverse source problem to the injectivity of the Euclidean X-ray transform.

Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, be a bounded smooth uniformly convex domain. This paper concerns the inverse source problem for the Monge–Ampere equation

\det D^{2}u=f(x)\quad\text{in }\Omega,

(1.1)

where the unknown source
f
is positive. We ask whether boundary measurements for the corresponding convex solutions determine
f
.

We use the standard classical Dirichlet theory for the Monge–Ampere equation. If

f\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}),\qquad 0<c_{f}\leq f\leq C_{f}<\infty,

and
g\in C^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)
, then the Dirichlet problem

\det D^{2}u=f\quad\text{in }\Omega,\qquad u|_{\partial\Omega}=g,

(1.2)

has a unique smooth strictly convex solution
u_{g}^{f}
. The associated Dirichlet-to-Neumann map is

\Lambda_{f}(g)=\partial_{\nu}u_{g}^{f}|_{\partial\Omega},

(1.3)

where
\nu
is the outward unit normal. The forward solvability statement follows from the classical work of Caffarelli–Nirenberg–Spruck
[
4
]
; see also Gutiérrez
[
17
]
for background on the Monge–Ampere equation.

Our main result is the following uniqueness theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, be bounded, smooth, and uniformly convex. Let

f_{1},f_{2}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}),\qquad 0<c\leq f_{j}\leq C<\infty.

If

\Lambda_{f_{1}}(g)=\Lambda_{f_{2}}(g)\qquad\text{for every }g\in C^{\infty}(\partial\Omega),

then

f_{1}=f_{2}\quad\text{in }\Omega.

Inverse boundary value problems for nonlinear equations have developed in several related directions. For semilinear elliptic and parabolic equations, early uniqueness results include
[
21
,
20
,
19
,
37
]
. Later works treat partial data, geometric settings, and power-type or gradient nonlinearities; see, for instance,
[
15
,
25
,
28
,
27
]
. A common method is to prescribe boundary values depending on small parameters and differentiate the nonlinear Dirichlet-to-Neumann map with respect to those parameters. The resulting identities are linear inverse problems for the coefficients appearing in the nonlinear terms. In elliptic problems this is the higher order linearization method. Related ideas also play an important role in hyperbolic inverse problems; see, for example,
[
26
]
.

There is a parallel literature for quasilinear elliptic equations and nonlinear conductivity-type problems. Early uniqueness results include
[

,

,

,

]
. More recent results and tools include
[

,

,

,

,

,

]
. Related nonlinear geometric inverse problems include inverse problems for minimal surface type equations
[

]
.

Inverse problems for the
p

p
-Laplacian form another important part of this development. These problems are technically different from uniformly elliptic semilinear models because the linearized operators may become degenerate or singular at critical points of the background solution. Results in this direction include boundary determination, monotonicity and enclosure methods, size estimates, and coefficient recovery; see, for instance,
[

,

,

The closest previous work to ours is
[
29
]
. It proves uniqueness for the same inverse source problem in two-dimensional convex Euclidean domains. Their method starts from the full nonlinear Dirichlet-to-Neumann map and linearizes it at non-zero solutions. If
u
solves
\det D^{2}u=f
, then the linearized equation has coefficient matrix
\operatorname{cof}(D^{2}u)
. Since
\operatorname{cof}(D^{2}u)=f(D^{2}u)^{-1}
, this equation has the same solutions as the non-divergence form equation
(D^{2}u)^{-1}_{ij}\partial_{ij}v=0
. They then recover the Hessian matrix
D^{2}u
from boundary measurements for this linear equation, and taking the determinant gives
f
.

The argument in the present paper is different. We use a large-parameter family of boundary values for which the leading term is a degenerate cylindrical profile. The first correction satisfies a one-dimensional equation on chords of
\Omega
, and the boundary normal derivative of this correction gives the corresponding chord integrals of the source. Thus the inverse source problem is reduced to injectivity of the Euclidean X-ray transform. The method works in all dimensions
n\geq 2
.

Although Theorem
1.1
is stated for the full Dirichlet-to-Neumann map, the proof uses only a restricted family of boundary values. For
\omega\in S^{n-1}
, let

\phi_{\omega}(x)=\frac{1}{2}|P_{\omega^{\perp}}x|^{2},

where
P_{\omega^{\perp}}
is orthogonal projection onto
\omega^{\perp}
. We prescribe the large cylindrical boundary values

g_{t,\omega}=t\phi_{\omega}|_{\partial\Omega},\qquad t\gg 1.

The leading profile
t\phi_{\omega}
has strong convexity in directions transverse to
\omega
, but no convexity in the
\omega
-direction. The missing longitudinal second derivative appears in the first correction. Along each chord of
\Omega
parallel to
\omega
, the correction solves a one-dimensional equation whose endpoint derivatives determine the line integral of
f
over that chord. Equality of the Dirichlet-to-Neumann maps on the large cylindrical family therefore gives equality of the X-ray transforms of the two sources. The standard Fourier-slice injectivity theorem then gives
f_{1}=f_{2}
; see
[
32
]
for the tomographic background.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
records the forward problem and the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map. Section
3
introduces the large cylindrical boundary values, the associated chord geometry, and the one-dimensional correction that appears in the asymptotic expansion. Section
4
proves the large-data asymptotic theorem. Section
5
converts the boundary asymptotic into X-ray data and recalls the injectivity needed for the final step. Section
6
proves the restricted large-data uniqueness theorem and then the main theorem.

2
Forward problem and boundary measurements

We use two standard facts about convex solutions of the Monge–Ampere equation. The first is the classical smooth Dirichlet theorem.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
4
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded domain with smooth strictly convex boundary. If
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\overline{D})
is positive and
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\partial D)
, then

\det D^{2}u=\psi\quad\text{in }D,\qquad u=\varphi\quad\text{on }\partial D

has a unique strictly convex solution
u\in C^{\infty}(\overline{D})
.

The second is the comparison principle for the Monge–Ampere measure. If
u
is convex, we write
M_{u}
for its Monge–Ampere measure; when
u\in C^{2}
, this measure is
M_{u}=(\det D^{2}u)\,dx
.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
17
, Theorem 1.4.6]
)
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open and bounded, and let
u,v\in C(\overline{D})
be convex in
D
. If
M_{u}\leq M_{v}
in
D
, then

\min_{\overline{D}}(u-v)=\min_{\partial D}(u-v).

In particular, if
u\geq v
on
\partial D
, then
u\geq v
in
D
.

We use the following convention throughout the paper. The domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is bounded, smooth, and uniformly convex. The outward unit normal is denoted by
\nu
. Sources belong to the class

\mathcal{F}=\left\{f\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}):0<c_{f}\leq f\leq C_{f}<\infty\right\}.

(2.1)

For
f\in\mathcal{F}
, Theorem
2.1
gives, for every
g\in C^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)
, a unique smooth strictly convex solution
u_{g}^{f}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
of

\det D^{2}u=f\quad\text{in }\Omega,\qquad u=g\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega.

(2.2)

The Dirichlet-to-Neumann map is

\Lambda_{f}:g\mapsto\partial_{\nu}u_{g}^{f}|_{\partial\Omega}.

(2.3)

The paper is written in this smooth framework to keep the inverse problem separate from lower-regularity trace issues. A lower-regularity formulation would require replacing (
2.3
) by a boundary trace statement justified by additional regularity.

3
Large cylindrical data and the chordwise correction

We now introduce the special boundary values used in the proof of uniqueness. The point of this section is to explain why large cylindrical data should produce chord integrals of the source. The rigorous asymptotic estimate is stated at the end of the section and proved in the next one.

3.1
Large cylindrical boundary values

For
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, let

P_{\omega^{\perp}}=\mathrm{Id}-\omega\otimes\omega.

The cylindrical profile is

\phi_{\omega}(x)=\frac{1}{2}|P_{\omega^{\perp}}x|^{2}.

(3.1)

The large cylindrical trace family is

\mathcal{C}_{T}=\{g_{t,\omega}=t\phi_{\omega}|_{\partial\Omega}:t\geq T,\ \omega\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\}.

(3.2)

For
f\in\mathcal{F}
, we write
u_{t,\omega}^{f}
for the solution of

\det D^{2}u_{t,\omega}^{f}=f\quad\text{in }\Omega,\qquad u_{t,\omega}^{f}=g_{t,\omega}\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega.

(3.3)

The corresponding measurements are

\Lambda_{f}(g_{t,\omega})=\partial_{\nu}u_{t,\omega}^{f}|_{\partial\Omega}.

(3.4)

3.2
Chord geometry

Fix
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. Let

\Pi_{\omega}=\omega^{\perp},\qquad\Omega_{\omega}=P_{\omega^{\perp}}\Omega\subset\Pi_{\omega}.

For
y\in\Omega_{\omega}
, set

\displaystyle s_{+}(y)

\displaystyle=\sup\{s\in\mathbb{R}:y+s\omega\in\Omega\},

(3.5)

\displaystyle s_{-}(y)

\displaystyle=\inf\{s\in\mathbb{R}:y+s\omega\in\Omega\}.

The corresponding endpoints of the chord through
y
are

x_{\pm}(y)=y+s_{\pm}(y)\omega\in\partial\Omega.

(3.6)

We also introduce the incoming, outgoing, and glancing boundary sets

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\omega}^{+}

\displaystyle=\{x\in\partial\Omega:\omega\cdot\nu(x)>0\},

(3.7)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\omega}^{-}

\displaystyle=\{x\in\partial\Omega:\omega\cdot\nu(x)<0\},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\omega}^{\mathrm{gl}}

\displaystyle=\{x\in\partial\Omega:\omega\cdot\nu(x)=0\}.

Thus

\Gamma_{\omega}=\Gamma_{\omega}^{+}\cup\Gamma_{\omega}^{-}=\partial\Omega\setminus\Gamma_{\omega}^{\mathrm{gl}}

is the non-glancing boundary for the direction
\omega
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be bounded, smooth, and strictly convex. Then
\Omega_{\omega}
is an open subset of
\Pi_{\omega}
. For every
y\in\Omega_{\omega}
,

\Omega\cap(y+\mathbb{R}\omega)=\{y+s\omega:s_{-}(y)<s<s_{+}(y)\},

(3.8)

with
s_{-}(y)<s_{+}(y)
. Moreover
x_{\pm}(y)\in\Gamma_{\omega}^{\pm}
, that is,

\omega\cdot\nu(x_{+}(y))>0,\qquad\omega\cdot\nu(x_{-}(y))<0.

(3.9)

The maps

y\mapsto s_{\pm}(y),\qquad y\mapsto x_{\pm}(y),

are smooth on
\Omega_{\omega}
. Equivalently, the projection map
P_{\omega^{\perp}}
restricts to smooth diffeomorphisms

\Gamma_{\omega}^{\pm}\longrightarrow\Omega_{\omega},

with inverses
y\mapsto x_{\pm}(y)
.

Proof.

Since
P_{\omega^{\perp}}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\Pi_{\omega}
is an open map,
\Omega_{\omega}
is open. For fixed
y\in\Omega_{\omega}
, the set

I_{y}=\{s\in\mathbb{R}:y+s\omega\in\Omega\}

is a nonempty bounded open interval because
\Omega
is open, bounded, and convex. This proves (
3.8
) and gives
s_{-}(y)<s_{+}(y)
. Since
\Omega
is bounded and open, the endpoints
x_{\pm}(y)=y+s_{\pm}(y)\omega
lie on
\partial\Omega
.

We next prove non-glancing and the signs. Let
\rho
be a smooth defining function with

\Omega=\{\rho<0\},\qquad\partial\Omega=\{\rho=0\},\qquad\nabla\rho=|\nabla\rho|\nu\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega.

If
\omega\cdot\nu(x_{+}(y))=0
, then the line
x_{+}(y)+\mathbb{R}\omega
is contained in the tangent hyperplane to
\partial\Omega
at
x_{+}(y)
. By strict convexity, this supporting hyperplane meets
\overline{\Omega}
only at
x_{+}(y)
. This contradicts the fact that
y+s\omega\in\Omega
for
s<s_{+}(y)
close to
s_{+}(y)
. Hence
\omega\cdot\nu(x_{+}(y))\neq 0
. Similarly
\omega\cdot\nu(x_{-}(y))\neq 0
.

The sign follows from the crossing direction. The function
s\mapsto\rho(y+s\omega)
is negative on
(s_{-}(y),s_{+}(y))
and zero at the endpoints. Hence

\frac{d}{ds}\rho(y+s\omega)\bigg|_{s=s_{+}(y)}>0,\qquad\frac{d}{ds}\rho(y+s\omega)\bigg|_{s=s_{-}(y)}<0.

Since this derivative equals
|\nabla\rho|\nu\cdot\omega
on the boundary, (
3.9
) follows.

Finally, because
\partial_{s}\rho(y+s_{\pm}(y)\omega)\neq 0
, the implicit function theorem gives smoothness of
s_{\pm}
. The diffeomorphism statement follows from the identity

P_{\omega^{\perp}}(x_{\pm}(y))=y

and uniqueness of the two endpoints on each chord.
∎

3.3
The chordwise correction

For
f\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
, define
w_{\omega}^{f}
chordwise by

\partial_{s}^{2}w_{\omega}^{f}(y,s)=f(y+s\omega),\qquad w_{\omega}^{f}(y,s_{\pm}(y))=0.

(3.10)

It is useful to introduce the chord length

\ell_{\omega}(y)=s_{+}(y)-s_{-}(y).

(3.11)

Solving the one-dimensional Dirichlet problem on each chord gives the explicit formula

\displaystyle w_{\omega}^{f}(y,s)

\displaystyle=\int_{s_{-}(y)}^{s}(s-r)f(y+r\omega)\,dr

(3.12)

\displaystyle-\frac{s-s_{-}(y)}{\ell_{\omega}(y)}\int_{s_{-}(y)}^{s_{+}(y)}(s_{+}(y)-r)f(y+r\omega)\,dr.

In particular
w_{\omega}^{f}
is smooth in the open set

\{(y,s):y\in\Omega_{\omega},\ s_{-}(y)<s<s_{+}(y)\},

after identifying
(y,s)
with
y+s\omega
. The following estimates record precisely what will be used later.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
f\in C(\overline{\Omega})
. For every
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, every
y\in\Omega_{\omega}
, and every
s\in[s_{-}(y),s_{+}(y)]
,

|w_{\omega}^{f}(y,s)|\leq\frac{1}{8}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\ell_{\omega}(y)^{2},\qquad|\partial_{s}w_{\omega}^{f}(y,s)|\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\ell_{\omega}(y),

(3.13)

and

|\partial_{s}^{2}w_{\omega}^{f}(y,s)|\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

(3.14)

Consequently
w_{\omega}^{f}=O(\ell_{\omega}^{2})
and
\partial_{s}w_{\omega}^{f}=O(\ell_{\omega})
as the chord length tends to zero.

Proof.

For fixed
y
, write
a=s_{-}(y)
,
b=s_{+}(y)
, and
\ell=b-a
. The Dirichlet Green function for
\partial_{s}^{2}
on
(a,b)
satisfies

|G(s,r)|\leq\frac{\ell}{4},\qquad\int_{a}^{b}|G(s,r)|\,dr\leq\frac{\ell^{2}}{8}.

Equivalently, these bounds follow directly from (
3.12
). Thus

|w(y,s)|\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\frac{\ell^{2}}{8}.

Differentiating (
3.12
) with respect to
s
gives

\partial_{s}w(y,s)=\int_{a}^{s}f(y+r\omega)\,dr-\frac{1}{\ell}\int_{a}^{b}(b-r)f(y+r\omega)\,dr,

which implies
|\partial_{s}w(y,s)|\leq\ell\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}
. The last bound is just the equation
\partial_{s}^{2}w=f
.
∎

If a boundary point is glancing for the direction
\omega
, then it is the limit of endpoints of chords whose lengths tend to zero. The bound (
3.13
) implies
w_{\omega}^{f}=O(\ell_{\omega}^{2})
along such chords. Thus
w_{\omega}^{f}
extends continuously to the glancing boundary by setting it equal to zero there. Whenever we write
t\phi_{\omega}+t^{1-n}w_{\omega}^{f}=t\phi_{\omega}
on all of
\partial\Omega
, this extension is understood. For smooth
f
, Lemma
4.2
below gives a stronger conclusion: for each fixed direction
\omega
, the apparent singularities of the endpoint parametrization cancel and
w_{\omega}^{f}
extends smoothly through glancing.

3.4
The large-data asymptotic

Fix
\omega\in S^{n-1}
and let
\phi_{\omega}
be defined by (
3.1
). For
t\gg 1
, let
u_{t,\omega}^{f}
be the classical convex solution of

\det D^{2}u_{t,\omega}^{f}=f\quad\text{in }\Omega,\qquad u_{t,\omega}^{f}=t\phi_{\omega}\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega.

(3.15)

We make the Ansatz

u_{t,\omega}^{f}=t\phi_{\omega}+t^{1-n}w_{\omega}^{f}+r_{t,\omega}^{f}.

(3.16)

Indeed, in coordinates
x=y+s\omega
,

D^{2}(t\phi_{\omega}+t^{1-n}w)=\begin{pmatrix}tI_{n-1}+t^{1-n}D_{y}^{2}w&t^{1-n}D_{y}\partial_{s}w\\[2.84526pt]
t^{1-n}\partial_{s}D_{y}w&t^{1-n}\partial_{s}^{2}w\end{pmatrix}.

A determinant expansion gives

\det D^{2}(t\phi_{\omega}+t^{1-n}w)=\partial_{s}^{2}w+O(t^{-n})

(3.17)

provided
w
has bounded second derivatives. Lemma
4.1
supplies such bounds for
w_{\omega}^{f}
on regions separated from glancing. Therefore the leading correction should satisfy
\partial_{s}^{2}w=f
, with zero endpoint values on every chord.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be bounded, smooth, and uniformly convex, and let
f\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
satisfy
f\geq c_{f}>0
. Fix
\omega\in S^{n-1}
. Let
u_{t,\omega}^{f}
solve (
3.15
), and let
w_{\omega}^{f}
be defined by (
3.10
). Then there are constants
C_{\omega},T_{\omega}<\infty
such that, for all
t\geq T_{\omega}
,

\|u_{t,\omega}^{f}-(t\phi_{\omega}+t^{1-n}w_{\omega}^{f})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\omega}t^{1-2n}.

(3.18)

Moreover, for every compact set
K\Subset\Gamma_{\omega}
, there are constants
C_{K},T_{K}<\infty
such that, for all
t\geq T_{K}
,

\sup_{x\in K}\left|\partial_{\nu}u_{t,\omega}^{f}(x)-t\partial_{\nu}\phi_{\omega}(x)-t^{1-n}\partial_{\nu}w_{\omega}^{f}(x)\right|\leq C_{K}t^{1-n-n/2}.

(3.19)

In particular, the error in (
3.19
) is
o(t^{1-n})
locally uniformly on
\Gamma_{\omega}
. The constants are not asserted to be uniform as
\omega
varies.

The proof of Theorem
3.3
is given in Section
4
. The bulk estimate comes from Proposition
4.8
, and the boundary normal estimate comes from Proposition
4.9
.

4
Proof of the large-data asymptotic

This section proves Theorem
3.3
for each fixed direction
\omega
. The proof first records the regularity of the chordwise correction, then uses an exact Schur-complement determinant expansion. Global comparison barriers give the
C^{0}
asymptotic, and localized boundary barriers upgrade it to the normal derivative asymptotic on compact subsets of the non-glancing boundary.

4.1
Regularity of the chordwise correction

For derivative estimates one must stay away from glancing chords. For
0<\kappa<1
, define

\displaystyle\Omega_{\omega,\kappa}=\{y\in\Omega_{\omega}:\omega\cdot\nu(x_{+}(y))\geq\kappa,\ -\omega\cdot\nu(x_{-}(y))\geq\kappa\},

(4.1)

and set

Q_{\omega,\kappa}=\{y+s\omega:y\in\Omega_{\omega,\kappa},\ s_{-}(y)\leq s\leq s_{+}(y)\}.

(4.2)

We also write

\Gamma_{\omega,\kappa}^{\pm}=x_{\pm}(\Omega_{\omega,\kappa})\subset\Gamma_{\omega}^{\pm}.

When
C^{m}
-norms are taken over
\Omega_{\omega,\kappa}
or
Q_{\omega,\kappa}
, they mean suprema of the corresponding coordinate derivatives on these sets. Equivalently, one may take the norms on a slightly larger open non-glancing neighborhood.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
m\geq 0
and
0<\kappa<1
. There is a constant

C=C(\Omega,m,\kappa)
, independent of
\omega
, such that the following hold.

First,

\|s_{+}\|_{C^{m}(\Omega_{\omega,\kappa})}+\|s_{-}\|_{C^{m}(\Omega_{\omega,\kappa})}+\|\ell_{\omega}^{-1}\|_{C^{m}(\Omega_{\omega,\kappa})}\leq C.

(4.3)

Second, if
f\in C^{m}(\overline{\Omega})
, then

\|w_{\omega}^{f}\|_{C^{m}(Q_{\omega,\kappa})}\leq C\|f\|_{C^{m}(\overline{\Omega})}.

(4.4)

Here the
C^{m}
-norm on
Q_{\omega,\kappa}
is taken in the coordinates
(y,s)\in\omega^{\perp}\times\mathbb{R}
. In particular, for every such non-glancing region,

\|D^{2}w_{\omega}^{f}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{\omega,\kappa})}\leq C\|f\|_{C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})}.

(4.5)

Moreover, after increasing the constant if necessary,

\|\partial_{\nu}w_{\omega}^{f}\|_{C^{m}(\Gamma_{\omega,\kappa}^{+}\cup\Gamma_{\omega,\kappa}^{-})}\leq C\|f\|_{C^{m+1}(\overline{\Omega})}.

(4.6)

Proof.

We may choose
\rho\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

\Omega=\{\rho<0\},\qquad\partial\Omega=\{\rho=0\},\qquad\nabla\rho\neq 0\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega.

For instance, one may take the signed distance function in a collar neighborhood of
\partial\Omega
, with negative sign in
\Omega
, and then extend it smoothly away from the boundary while preserving its sign. With this convention,
\nabla\rho
points in the outward normal direction on
\partial\Omega
, and hence

\nabla\rho=|\nabla\rho|\,\nu\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega.

The endpoint functions satisfy

\rho(y+s_{\pm}(y)\omega)=0.

For a vector
e\in\omega^{\perp}
, differentiation gives

\partial_{e}s_{\pm}(y)=-\frac{\nabla\rho(x_{\pm}(y))\cdot e}{\nabla\rho(x_{\pm}(y))\cdot\omega}=-\frac{\nabla\rho(x_{\pm}(y))\cdot e}{|\nabla\rho(x_{\pm}(y))|\,\nu(x_{\pm}(y))\cdot\omega}.

(4.7)

On
\Omega_{\omega,\kappa}
, the denominator is bounded away from zero by a constant depending only on
\Omega
and
\kappa
. Repeated differentiation of the implicit equation gives the corresponding higher derivative bounds for
s_{\pm}
, uniformly in
\omega
.

It remains to justify that the inverse chord length is also uniformly bounded on these non-glancing sets. We claim that there is
c=c(\Omega,\kappa)>0
such that

\ell_{\omega}(y)\geq c\qquad\text{for all }\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1},\ y\in\Omega_{\omega,\kappa}.

(4.8)

If not, then there exist
\omega_{j}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and
y_{j}\in\Omega_{\omega_{j},\kappa}
such that
\ell_{\omega_{j}}(y_{j})\to 0
. Passing to a subsequence,
\omega_{j}\to\omega
, and the two endpoints
x_{+,j}=x_{+}^{\omega_{j}}(y_{j})
,
x_{-,j}=x_{-}^{\omega_{j}}(y_{j})
converge to the same point
x\in\partial\Omega
, because
|x_{+,j}-x_{-,j}|=\ell_{\omega_{j}}(y_{j})\to 0
. The non-glancing inequalities give

\omega_{j}\cdot\nu(x_{+,j})\geq\kappa,\qquad-\omega_{j}\cdot\nu(x_{-,j})\geq\kappa.

Letting
j\to\infty
yields simultaneously

\omega\cdot\nu(x)\geq\kappa,\qquad\omega\cdot\nu(x)\leq-\kappa,

which is impossible. This proves (
4.8
). The estimates for
\ell_{\omega}^{-1}
and its derivatives now follow from the bounds for
s_{\pm}
, the identity
\ell_{\omega}=s_{+}-s_{-}
, and the lower bound (
4.8
).

The estimate for
w_{\omega}^{f}
follows by differentiating the explicit formula (
3.12
). Each derivative falls either on
f(y+r\omega)
, on the endpoints
s_{\pm}(y)
, or on
\ell_{\omega}(y)^{-1}
. The preceding bounds control the endpoint terms, while the derivatives of
f(y+r\omega)
are bounded by
\|f\|_{C^{m}(\overline{\Omega})}
. This proves (
4.4
), and (
4.5
) is the case
m=2
.

Finally, by Lemma
3.1
, the maps
y\mapsto x_{\pm}(y)
parametrize the non-glancing boundary pieces
\Gamma_{\omega}^{\pm}
. Since
w_{\omega}^{f}(y,s_{\pm}(y))=0
, the function
w_{\omega}^{f}
vanishes identically on each of these boundary pieces, so
\nabla w_{\omega}^{f}
is normal to
\partial\Omega
there. Hence

\partial_{\nu}w_{\omega}^{f}(x_{\pm}(y))=\frac{\partial_{s}w_{\omega}^{f}(y,s_{\pm}(y))}{\omega\cdot\nu(x_{\pm}(y))}.

The denominator is bounded away from zero on
\Gamma_{\omega,\kappa}^{\pm}
. Differentiating this formula in the boundary coordinates
y
gives (
4.6
). The right hand side is written with
\|f\|_{C^{m+1}}
because the differentiated boundary quantity contains derivatives of
\partial_{s}w_{\omega}^{f}
, hence derivatives of the chordwise solution one order beyond those used to control
w_{\omega}^{f}
itself.
∎

The non-glancing estimates above cannot be extended to glancing by differentiating the individual endpoint functions
s_{\pm}
, because (
4.7
) contains the factor
(\omega\cdot\nu)^{-1}
. This singularity is an artifact of the parametrization by the two separate roots. The combinations entering the chordwise Dirichlet problem have additional cancellation. The next lemma records the needed global statement for each fixed direction
\omega
.

We shall use the following standard elementary fact. If
U
is an open set and

H\in C^{\infty}(U\times(-\epsilon,\epsilon))
is even in the second variable, then, after possibly shrinking
\epsilon
, there is a function

\widetilde{H}
, smooth for
\mu\geq 0
and smoothly extendable to a neighborhood of
\mu=0
, such that

H(y,\lambda)=\widetilde{H}(y,\lambda^{2}).

Equivalently, a smooth even function of
\lambda
is a smooth function of
\lambda^{2}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Fix
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. If
f\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
, then the chordwise solution
w_{\omega}^{f}
, initially defined for
y\in\Omega_{\omega}
and
s_{-}(y)<s<s_{+}(y)
, extends to a function in
C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
. The chordwise quadratic barrier

b_{\omega}(y,s)=\frac{1}{2}(s-s_{-}(y))(s-s_{+}(y))

extends to a function in
C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
as well. Moreover,

w_{\omega}^{f}=0,\qquad b_{\omega}=0\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega,

and, in the coordinates
x=y+s\omega
,

\partial_{s}^{2}w_{\omega}^{f}=f,\qquad\partial_{s}^{2}b_{\omega}=1

throughout
\Omega
, with the identities extending continuously to
\overline{\Omega}
. For every
m
, the norms
\|w_{\omega}^{f}\|_{C^{m}(\overline{\Omega})}
and
\|b_{\omega}\|_{C^{m}(\overline{\Omega})}
are finite. No uniformity in
\omega
is asserted in this global glancing statement.

Proof.

Away from the glancing set this is the ordinary implicit-function argument already used in Lemma
4.1
. It remains to analyze a glancing point
x_{0}=y_{0}+s_{0}\omega\in\partial\Omega
, so
\omega\cdot\nu(x_{0})=0
. Let
\rho
be a smooth signed-distance defining function near
x_{0}
, negative in
\Omega
. At
x_{0}
,

\partial_{s}\rho(x_{0})=\nabla\rho(x_{0})\cdot\omega=0.

Since
\omega
is tangent to
\partial\Omega
at
x_{0}
and
\Omega
is uniformly convex, the second fundamental form is positive definite with respect to the outward normal. For the signed-distance defining function normalized by
\rho<0
in
\Omega
, this gives the sign

\partial_{s}^{2}\rho(x_{0})=D^{2}\rho(x_{0})[\omega,\omega]>0.

By the one-dimensional Malgrange preparation theorem for smooth functions
[
30
]
, after shrinking the coordinate neighborhood there are smooth functions
a(y)
,
\beta(y)
, and a smooth non-vanishing factor
h(y,s)
such that, with
z=s-a(y)
,

\rho(y,s)=h(y,s)\bigl(z^{2}-\beta(y)\bigr).

Since
2h(y_{0},s_{0})=\partial_{s}^{2}\rho(x_{0})>0
, we may shrink the neighborhood so that
h>0
. Hence, because
\rho<0
in
\Omega
, the part of
\Omega
in this neighborhood is represented by

z^{2}<\beta(y).

Thus the two chord endpoints are

s_{\pm}(y)=a(y)\pm\sqrt{\beta(y)}

whenever
\beta(y)>0
. Consequently

b_{\omega}(y,s)=\frac{1}{2}\bigl(z^{2}-\beta(y)\bigr)

in this neighborhood, and hence
b_{\omega}
is smooth through the glancing point.

We now treat
w_{\omega}^{f}
. Set

F(y,z)=f(y+(a(y)+z)\omega).

For fixed
y
, let

P(y,z)=\int_{0}^{z}(z-r)F(y,r)\,dr,\qquad\partial_{z}^{2}P=F.

For
\lambda>0
, define

A(y,\lambda)=\frac{P(y,-\lambda)-P(y,\lambda)}{2\lambda},\qquad B(y,\lambda)=-\frac{P(y,\lambda)+P(y,-\lambda)}{2}.

The functions
A
and
B
are smooth and even in
\lambda
after extending across
\lambda=0
. By the even-factorization fact stated above, after possibly shrinking the neighborhood, there are smooth functions
\widetilde{A}(y,\mu)
and
\widetilde{B}(y,\mu)
, defined for
\mu\geq 0
near
0
and smoothly extendable across
\mu=0
, such that

A(y,\lambda)=\widetilde{A}(y,\lambda^{2}),\qquad B(y,\lambda)=\widetilde{B}(y,\lambda^{2}).

Define

W(y,z)=P(y,z)+\widetilde{A}(y,\beta(y))z+\widetilde{B}(y,\beta(y)).

Then
W
is smooth in
(y,z)
. If
\beta(y)>0
and
\lambda=\sqrt{\beta(y)}
, then

W(y,\pm\lambda)=0,\qquad\partial_{z}^{2}W(y,z)=F(y,z).

By uniqueness for the one-dimensional Dirichlet problem on the chord,
W(y,s-a(y))
agrees with
w_{\omega}^{f}(y,s)
for the interior chords in the neighborhood. This proves smooth extension through the glancing point. On overlaps, the local extensions agree with the original chordwise solution on the interior chords, and therefore agree with each other by continuity. A finite covering of the compact glancing set, together with the non-glancing smoothness, proves the global assertion on
\overline{\Omega}
.
∎

4.2
The determinant expansion for approximate solutions

Set

U_{t}=t\phi_{\omega}+t^{1-n}w_{\omega}^{f}.

At non-glancing boundary points this satisfies
U_{t}=t\phi_{\omega}
. At glancing boundary points this equality is understood using the continuous extension of
w_{\omega}^{f}
described after Lemma
3.2
. The following lemma gives the precise determinant expansion behind the formal statement (
3.17
).

Fix an orthonormal coordinate system
(y,s)\in\omega^{\perp}\times\mathbb{R}
, where
s=x\cdot\omega
. For a function
w=w(y,s)
, write

B=D_{y}^{2}w,\qquad z=D_{y}\partial_{s}w,\qquad a=\partial_{s}^{2}w,\qquad q=t^{-n}.

Here
B
is an
(n-1)\times(n-1)
symmetric matrix,
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
, and
a\in\mathbb{R}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
w\in C^{2}
on an open set, and suppose that
I+qB
is invertible at the point under consideration. Then

\displaystyle\det D^{2}(t\phi_{\omega}+t^{1-n}w)

\displaystyle=\det(I+qB)\left(a-q\left\langle(I+qB)^{-1}z,z\right\rangle\right).

(4.9)

In particular, the identity holds for all sufficiently large
t
on any set where
D_{y}^{2}w
is bounded.

Proof.

In the coordinates
x=y+s\omega
, one has

D^{2}(t\phi_{\omega}+t^{1-n}w)=\begin{pmatrix}tI_{n-1}+t^{1-n}B&t^{1-n}z\\
t^{1-n}z^{T}&t^{1-n}a\end{pmatrix}.

(4.10)

The upper-left block is

A_{t}=tI_{n-1}+t^{1-n}B=t(I+qB).

Assuming
A_{t}
is invertible, the Schur-complement formula gives

\displaystyle\det D^{2}(t\phi_{\omega}+t^{1-n}w)

\displaystyle=\det A_{t}\left(t^{1-n}a-t^{2-2n}z^{T}A_{t}^{-1}z\right)

\displaystyle=t^{n-1}\det(I+qB)\left(t^{1-n}a-t^{2-2n}t^{-1}z^{T}(I+qB)^{-1}z\right)

\displaystyle=\det(I+qB)\left(a-q\left\langle(I+qB)^{-1}z,z\right\rangle\right),

because
q=t^{-n}
. This proves the formula.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
M\geq 1
. There exists a constant
C=C(n,M)
such that the following holds. Suppose

|a|+|z|+\|B\|\leq M,\qquad t^{-n}\|B\|\leq\frac{1}{2}.

Then

\displaystyle\det D^{2}(t\phi_{\omega}+t^{1-n}w)

\displaystyle=a+t^{-n}\left(a\operatorname{tr}B-|z|^{2}\right)+R_{t},

(4.11)

where

|R_{t}|\leq Ct^{-2n}.

(4.12)

Equivalently,

\displaystyle\det D^{2}(t\phi_{\omega}+t^{1-n}w)

\displaystyle=\partial_{s}^{2}w

(4.13)

\displaystyle+t^{-n}\left[(\partial_{s}^{2}w)\operatorname{tr}(D_{y}^{2}w)-|D_{y}\partial_{s}w|^{2}\right]+O(t^{-2n}).

The constant in the
O(t^{-2n})
term depends only on
n
and on the stated bound for the second derivatives of
w
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.3
,

\det D^{2}(t\phi_{\omega}+t^{1-n}w)=\det(I+qB)\left(a-q\left\langle(I+qB)^{-1}z,z\right\rangle\right).

For
q\|B\|\leq 1/2
, the elementary matrix expansions give

\det(I+qB)=1+q\operatorname{tr}B+O(q^{2}),\qquad(I+qB)^{-1}=I+O(q),

with constants depending only on
n
and
M
. Hence

\left\langle(I+qB)^{-1}z,z\right\rangle=|z|^{2}+O(q).

Substituting these estimates into the exact formula gives

\displaystyle\det D^{2}(t\phi_{\omega}+t^{1-n}w)

\displaystyle=(1+q\operatorname{tr}B+O(q^{2}))(a-q|z|^{2}+O(q^{2}))

\displaystyle=a+q(a\operatorname{tr}B-|z|^{2})+O(q^{2}).

Since
q=t^{-n}
, this is (
4.11
)–(
4.13
).
∎

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
w=w_{\omega}^{f}
satisfy

\partial_{s}^{2}w=f

on a region where the second derivatives of
w
are bounded. Then the approximate solution

U_{t}=t\phi_{\omega}+t^{1-n}w_{\omega}^{f}

satisfies

\displaystyle\det D^{2}U_{t}

\displaystyle=f+t^{-n}\left[f\operatorname{tr}(D_{y}^{2}w_{\omega}^{f})-|D_{y}\partial_{s}w_{\omega}^{f}|^{2}\right]+O(t^{-2n}).

(4.14)

In particular,

\det D^{2}U_{t}=f+O(t^{-n})

(4.15)

locally uniformly on every region where
\|D^{2}w_{\omega}^{f}\|
is bounded.

Proof.

Apply Lemma
4.4
with
w=w_{\omega}^{f}
and use
\partial_{s}^{2}w_{\omega}^{f}=f
.
∎

The scaling in (
3.17
) is as follows. The leading transverse block has determinant
t^{n-1}
, while the longitudinal second derivative of the correction has size
t^{1-n}\partial_{s}^{2}w
. Their product is order one. The first relative perturbation of the transverse block is

t^{-1}t^{1-n}D_{y}^{2}w=t^{-n}D_{y}^{2}w,

which is why the first error term is of order
t^{-n}
. No term of order
t^{1-n}
appears in the determinant expansion. The off-diagonal blocks contribute only through the Schur complement: before multiplication by
\det A_{t}
, their contribution is
t^{2-2n}z^{T}A_{t}^{-1}z=O(t^{1-2n})
, and after multiplication by
\det A_{t}=O(t^{n-1})
this is again of order
t^{-n}
.

4.3
Convexity of the approximate solution

We next record the convexity estimate for the approximate profile. This is a local statement in a coordinate tube on which the second derivatives of the chordwise correction are bounded. By Lemma
4.1
, this applies uniformly on every non-glancing tube
Q_{\omega,\kappa}
.

The estimate is slightly more general than needed for
U_{t}
, because the comparison argument will later use perturbations of size
t^{1-2n}
. Let
w,\eta\in C^{2}(Q)
, where
Q
is a region written in coordinates
(y,s)\in\omega^{\perp}\times\mathbb{R}
. For
|\lambda|\leq L
, set

V_{t,\lambda}=t\phi_{\omega}+t^{1-n}w+\lambda t^{1-2n}\eta=t\phi_{\omega}+t^{1-n}\bigl(w+\lambda t^{-n}\eta\bigr).

(4.16)

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
c_{0}>0
,
M<\infty
, and
L<\infty
. Suppose that on
Q

\partial_{s}^{2}w\geq c_{0},\qquad\|D^{2}w\|+\|D^{2}\eta\|\leq M.

(4.17)

Then there is
T=T(n,c_{0},M,L)
such that, for every
t\geq T
and every
|\lambda|\leq L
,

D^{2}V_{t,\lambda}>0\quad\text{on }Q.

More precisely, writing

W_{t,\lambda}=w+\lambda t^{-n}\eta,

one has

I+t^{-n}D_{y}^{2}W_{t,\lambda}\geq\frac{1}{2}I

(4.18)

and

\partial_{s}^{2}W_{t,\lambda}-t^{-n}\left\langle(I+t^{-n}D_{y}^{2}W_{t,\lambda})^{-1}D_{y}\partial_{s}W_{t,\lambda},D_{y}\partial_{s}W_{t,\lambda}\right\rangle\geq\frac{c_{0}}{2}.

(4.19)

Proof.

Put
q=t^{-n}
. The Hessian of
V_{t,\lambda}=t\phi_{\omega}+t^{1-n}W_{t,\lambda}
has block form

D^{2}V_{t,\lambda}=\begin{pmatrix}t(I+qD_{y}^{2}W_{t,\lambda})&t^{1-n}D_{y}\partial_{s}W_{t,\lambda}\\
t^{1-n}(D_{y}\partial_{s}W_{t,\lambda})^{T}&t^{1-n}\partial_{s}^{2}W_{t,\lambda}\end{pmatrix}.

(4.20)

The assumptions imply, for
t\geq 1
and
|\lambda|\leq L
,

\|D^{2}W_{t,\lambda}\|\leq M+LM=:M_{1}.

If
qM_{1}\leq 1/2
, then (
4.18
) holds and the transverse block is positive definite.

It remains to check the Schur complement. Since

\partial_{s}^{2}W_{t,\lambda}=\partial_{s}^{2}w+\lambda q\partial_{s}^{2}\eta,

we have

\partial_{s}^{2}W_{t,\lambda}\geq c_{0}-LqM.

Also, when
qM_{1}\leq 1/2
,

\|(I+qD_{y}^{2}W_{t,\lambda})^{-1}\|\leq 2,\qquad|D_{y}\partial_{s}W_{t,\lambda}|\leq M_{1}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\partial_{s}^{2}W_{t,\lambda}-q\left\langle(I+qD_{y}^{2}W_{t,\lambda})^{-1}D_{y}\partial_{s}W_{t,\lambda},D_{y}\partial_{s}W_{t,\lambda}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\geq c_{0}-LqM-2qM_{1}^{2}.

For
t
large enough, the right-hand side is at least
c_{0}/2
. The positivity of the transverse block and of the Schur complement implies
D^{2}V_{t,\lambda}>0
.
∎

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
f\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
satisfy
f\geq c_{f}>0
, and fix
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. Let
\eta\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
. For every fixed
L<\infty
, there is
T=T(f,\omega,\eta,L)
such that

t\phi_{\omega}+t^{1-n}w_{\omega}^{f}+\lambda t^{1-2n}\eta

is strictly convex in
\Omega
whenever
t\geq T
and
|\lambda|\leq L
. In particular, for every fixed
C<\infty
, the functions

U_{t}=t\phi_{\omega}+t^{1-n}w_{\omega}^{f},\qquad U_{t}\pm Ct^{1-2n}b_{\omega}

are strictly convex in
\Omega
for all sufficiently large
t
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.2
, the functions
w_{\omega}^{f}
and
b_{\omega}
have bounded second derivatives on
\overline{\Omega}
for the fixed direction
\omega
. The chordwise equation gives

\partial_{s}^{2}w_{\omega}^{f}=f\geq c_{f}.

The assertion follows directly from Lemma
4.6
, with
Q=\Omega
written in the coordinates
x=y+s\omega
. For the final statement, take
\eta=b_{\omega}
.
∎

4.4
Global comparison barriers and the
C^{0}
asymptotic

We now compare the true solution
u_{t,\omega}^{f}
with

U_{t}=t\phi_{\omega}+t^{1-n}w_{\omega}^{f}.

The global smoothness statement in Lemma
4.2
allows the chordwise barriers to be used on all of
\Omega
, including near glancing.

Define

b_{\omega}(y,s)=\frac{1}{2}\bigl(s-s_{-}(y)\bigr)\bigl(s-s_{+}(y)\bigr).

(4.21)

Then

\partial_{s}^{2}b_{\omega}=1,\qquad b_{\omega}=0\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega,

(4.22)

and

b_{\omega}\leq 0\quad\text{on each chord},\qquad|b_{\omega}(y,s)|\leq\frac{1}{8}\ell_{\omega}(y)^{2}\leq\frac{1}{8}\operatorname{diam}(\Omega)^{2}.

(4.23)

We will use Theorem
2.2
in the following classical form. If
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is bounded and
v,u\in C^{2}(\overline{D})
are convex, with

\det D^{2}v\geq\det D^{2}u\quad\text{in }D,\qquad v\leq u\quad\text{on }\partial D,

then
v\leq u
in
D
. Thus, for equal boundary values, the convex function with the larger Monge–Ampere determinant lies lower.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
f\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
satisfy
f\geq c_{f}>0
, and fix
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. There exist constants
C_{*}>0
and
T<\infty
, depending on
f
,
\omega
, and
\Omega
, such that, for every
t\geq T
, the functions

V_{t}^{\rm low}=U_{t}+C_{*}t^{1-2n}b_{\omega},\qquad V_{t}^{\rm up}=U_{t}-C_{*}t^{1-2n}b_{\omega}

(4.24)

are strictly convex in
\Omega
, agree with
t\phi_{\omega}
on
\partial\Omega
, and satisfy

V_{t}^{\rm low}\leq U_{t}\leq V_{t}^{\rm up}\quad\text{in }\Omega,

(4.25)

\det D^{2}V_{t}^{\rm low}\geq f,\qquad\det D^{2}V_{t}^{\rm up}\leq f\quad\text{in }\Omega.

(4.26)

Consequently,

U_{t}+C_{*}t^{1-2n}b_{\omega}\leq u_{t,\omega}^{f}\leq U_{t}-C_{*}t^{1-2n}b_{\omega}\quad\text{in }\Omega,

(4.27)

and hence

\|u_{t,\omega}^{f}-U_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{C_{*}}{8}\operatorname{diam}(\Omega)^{2}t^{1-2n}.

(4.28)

Proof.

The boundary equality follows from
w_{\omega}^{f}=b_{\omega}=0
on
\partial\Omega
. The order (
4.25
) follows from
b_{\omega}\leq 0
. Strict convexity for large
t
follows from Corollary
4.7
with
\eta=b_{\omega}
and
|\lambda|=C_{*}
.

It remains to prove the determinant inequalities. Put
q=t^{-n}
and write

E_{\omega}^{f}=f\,\operatorname{tr}(D_{y}^{2}w_{\omega}^{f})-|D_{y}\partial_{s}w_{\omega}^{f}|^{2}.

By Lemma
4.2
,

A:=\|E_{\omega}^{f}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}<\infty.

(4.29)

For
\sigma\in\{+1,-1\}
, define

W_{t}^{\sigma}=w_{\omega}^{f}+\sigma C_{*}qb_{\omega},\qquad V_{t}^{\sigma}=t\phi_{\omega}+t^{1-n}W_{t}^{\sigma}.

Thus
V_{t}^{+}=V_{t}^{\rm low}
and
V_{t}^{-}=V_{t}^{\rm up}
. Since

\partial_{s}^{2}W_{t}^{\sigma}=f+\sigma C_{*}q,

and since
D^{2}w_{\omega}^{f}
and
D^{2}b_{\omega}
are bounded on
\Omega
, Lemma
4.4
gives, uniformly in
\Omega
,

\det D^{2}V_{t}^{\sigma}=f+q\left(\sigma C_{*}+E_{\omega}^{f}\right)+O_{C_{*}}(q^{2}).

(4.30)

Choose
C_{*}\geq 2A+2
. After increasing
T
, the remainder in (
4.30
) is bounded in absolute value by
q
for
t\geq T
. For
\sigma=+1
,

\det D^{2}V_{t}^{\rm low}\geq f+q(C_{*}-A)-q\geq f.

For
\sigma=-1
,

\det D^{2}V_{t}^{\rm up}\leq f-q(C_{*}-A)+q\leq f.

This proves (
4.26
).

On
\partial\Omega
, both barriers equal
u_{t,\omega}^{f}=t\phi_{\omega}
. Since

\det D^{2}V_{t}^{\rm low}\geq f=\det D^{2}u_{t,\omega}^{f},

the comparison principle in the orientation recalled above gives

V_{t}^{\rm low}\leq u_{t,\omega}^{f}\quad\text{in }\Omega.

Similarly,

\det D^{2}u_{t,\omega}^{f}=f\geq\det D^{2}V_{t}^{\rm up},

and the same comparison principle applied to the pair
(u_{t,\omega}^{f},V_{t}^{\rm up})
gives

u_{t,\omega}^{f}\leq V_{t}^{\rm up}\quad\text{in }\Omega.

This proves (
4.27
). The norm bound (
4.28
) follows from (
4.23
).
∎

4.5
Boundary normal derivatives

We now derive the boundary statement (
3.19
) on compact subsets of the non-glancing boundary. The global
C^{0}
estimate from Proposition
4.8
controls the artificial boundary of small one-sided collars. Inside those collars we use barriers with a larger but still lower-order profile perturbation.

For reference, throughout this part of the argument
U_{t}=t\phi_{\omega}+t^{1-n}w_{\omega}^{f}
,
q=t^{-n}
, and
b_{\omega}
denotes the chordwise quadratic barrier from (
4.21
). The auxiliary functions
G_{x_{0}}
and collars
D_{x_{0}}^{\pm}
are introduced below at the boundary point
x_{0}
where the normal derivative is estimated.

Choose

\varepsilon_{t}=t^{-n/2},\qquad\eta_{t}=t^{1-n}\varepsilon_{t}=t^{1-n-n/2}.

Then
\varepsilon_{t}\to 0
, while
t^{n}\varepsilon_{t}\to\infty
. Thus the determinant correction generated by the local barriers dominates the
O(t^{-n})
determinant defect of
U_{t}
, and their normal derivative error is
\eta_{t}=o(t^{1-n})
.

For a boundary point
x_{0}\in\Gamma_{\omega}
, set

y_{0}=P_{\omega^{\perp}}x_{0},\qquad p_{x_{0}}(x)=|P_{\omega^{\perp}}x-y_{0}|^{2}.

(4.31)

Thus
p_{x_{0}}
is independent of the
s
-variable and

p_{x_{0}}(x_{0})=0,\qquad\partial_{\nu}p_{x_{0}}(x_{0})=0.

(4.32)

Define

G_{x_{0}}=-b_{\omega}+p_{x_{0}}.

(4.33)

Since
b_{\omega}\leq 0
, the function
G_{x_{0}}
is nonnegative, and

\partial_{s}^{2}G_{x_{0}}=-1.

(4.34)

We shall also use the sign of the outward normal derivative of
b_{\omega}
on non-glancing boundary pieces. For
y\in\Omega_{\omega}
,

\partial_{\nu}b_{\omega}(x_{\pm}(y))=\frac{\ell_{\omega}(y)}{2|\omega\cdot\nu(x_{\pm}(y))|}>0.

(4.35)

Indeed,
b_{\omega}
vanishes on each non-glancing boundary piece, so
\nabla b_{\omega}
is normal there. Moreover,

\partial_{s}b_{\omega}(y,s_{+}(y))=\frac{1}{2}\ell_{\omega}(y),\qquad\partial_{s}b_{\omega}(y,s_{-}(y))=-\frac{1}{2}\ell_{\omega}(y),

and the endpoint signs
\omega\cdot\nu(x_{+})>0
,
\omega\cdot\nu(x_{-})<0
give (
4.35
).

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
f\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
satisfy
f\geq c_{f}>0
. Fix
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, and let
K\Subset\Gamma_{\omega}
. Then there are constants
C_{K}<\infty
and
T_{K}<\infty
such that, for all
t\geq T_{K}
,

\sup_{x\in K}\left|\partial_{\nu}u_{t,\omega}^{f}(x)-\partial_{\nu}U_{t}(x)\right|\leq C_{K}t^{1-n-n/2}.

(4.36)

In particular,

\partial_{\nu}u_{t,\omega}^{f}=t\partial_{\nu}\phi_{\omega}+t^{1-n}\partial_{\nu}w_{\omega}^{f}+o(t^{1-n})

(4.37)

locally uniformly on
\Gamma_{\omega}
.

Proof.

We prove the estimate pointwise with constants uniform for
x_{0}\in K
. Write

K_{+}=K\cap\Gamma_{\omega}^{+},\qquad K_{-}=K\cap\Gamma_{\omega}^{-}.

Since
K\Subset\Gamma_{\omega}
, there are
\kappa>0
and an open set
Y\Subset\Omega_{\omega}
such that, with

Y_{+}=P_{\omega^{\perp}}(K_{+}),\qquad Y_{-}=P_{\omega^{\perp}}(K_{-}),

one has
Y_{+}\cup Y_{-}\subset Y
and

\omega\cdot\nu(x_{+}(y))\geq\kappa,\qquad-\omega\cdot\nu(x_{-}(y))\geq\kappa\qquad\text{for all }y\in Y.

Lemma
4.1
gives uniform bounds for the endpoint functions and for
\partial_{\nu}b_{\omega}
on this non-glancing set. Since
\overline{Y}\subset\Omega_{\omega}
and
\ell_{\omega}
is continuous and positive on
\Omega_{\omega}
, there is
\ell_{0}>0
such that

\ell_{\omega}(y)=s_{+}(y)-s_{-}(y)\geq\ell_{0}\qquad\text{for all }y\in Y.

Choose
\rho>0
so small that
\{y\in\omega^{\perp}:|y-y_{0}|<\rho\}\subset Y
for every
y_{0}\in Y_{+}\cup Y_{-}
, and then choose
0<\delta<\ell_{0}/4
. Reducing
\rho
and
\delta
, if necessary, we may also assume that the following collars remain inside this non-glancing tube. If
x_{0}\in K_{+}
, set
y_{0}=P_{\omega^{\perp}}x_{0}
and

D_{x_{0}}^{+}=\{y+s\omega:|y-y_{0}|<\rho,\ s_{+}(y)-\delta<s<s_{+}(y)\}.

(4.38)

If
x_{0}\in K_{-}
, set

D_{x_{0}}^{-}=\{y+s\omega:|y-y_{0}|<\rho,\ s_{-}(y)<s<s_{-}(y)+\delta\}.

(4.39)

We write
D_{x_{0}}
for either collar. The Monge–Ampere comparison principle for convex solutions is valid on bounded domains, so the corners of these collars cause no difficulty; one may also justify the same step by applying the comparison principle on a smooth exhaustion and passing to the limit.

Let
\varepsilon_{t}
and
\eta_{t}
be as above. Define

L_{t,x_{0}}=U_{t}-\eta_{t}G_{x_{0}},\qquad W_{t,x_{0}}=U_{t}+\eta_{t}G_{x_{0}}.

(4.40)

Since
G_{x_{0}}\geq 0
, these barriers are ordered around
U_{t}
. On the physical boundary part of
D_{x_{0}}
, one has
w_{\omega}^{f}=0
and
b_{\omega}=0
, so

L_{t,x_{0}}=t\phi_{\omega}-\eta_{t}p_{x_{0}}\leq t\phi_{\omega}=u_{t,\omega}^{f}\leq t\phi_{\omega}+\eta_{t}p_{x_{0}}=W_{t,x_{0}}.

At the distinguished point
x_{0}
, both inequalities are equalities.

On the artificial boundary of
D_{x_{0}}
, the function
G_{x_{0}}
is bounded below by a positive constant independent of
x_{0}
. On the tangential face
|y-y_{0}|=\rho
, one has
G_{x_{0}}\geq p_{x_{0}}=\rho^{2}
. On the inner face, for either sign,

-b_{\omega}=\frac{1}{2}\delta(\ell_{\omega}(y)-\delta)\geq\frac{1}{2}\delta(\ell_{0}-\delta).

Thus there exists
c_{0}>0
such that

G_{x_{0}}\geq c_{0}\quad\text{on }\partial D_{x_{0}}\setminus\partial\Omega,

(4.41)

for every
x_{0}\in K
.

By Proposition
4.8
, there is
M<\infty
such that

\|u_{t,\omega}^{f}-U_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq Mt^{1-2n}

(4.42)

for all sufficiently large
t
. Since

\frac{\eta_{t}}{t^{1-2n}}=t^{n/2}\to\infty,

(
4.41
) and (
4.42
) imply, for all sufficiently large
t
,

L_{t,x_{0}}\leq u_{t,\omega}^{f}\leq W_{t,x_{0}}\quad\text{on }\partial D_{x_{0}}.

We next check the determinant inequalities. The profiles in (
4.40
) are

L_{t,x_{0}}=t\phi_{\omega}+t^{1-n}(w_{\omega}^{f}-\varepsilon_{t}G_{x_{0}}),\qquad W_{t,x_{0}}=t\phi_{\omega}+t^{1-n}(w_{\omega}^{f}+\varepsilon_{t}G_{x_{0}}).

On the chosen collars,
G_{x_{0}}
has uniformly bounded second derivatives and
\partial_{s}^{2}G_{x_{0}}=-1
. Lemma
4.4
gives, uniformly for
x\in D_{x_{0}}
and
x_{0}\in K
,

\displaystyle\det D^{2}L_{t,x_{0}}

\displaystyle=f+\varepsilon_{t}+O(t^{-n}),

(4.43)

\displaystyle\det D^{2}W_{t,x_{0}}

\displaystyle=f-\varepsilon_{t}+O(t^{-n}).

Since
\varepsilon_{t}=t^{-n/2}
and
t^{-n}=o(\varepsilon_{t})
, after increasing the lower threshold for
t
,

\det D^{2}L_{t,x_{0}}\geq f,\qquad\det D^{2}W_{t,x_{0}}\leq f\quad\text{in }D_{x_{0}}.

(4.44)

The same uniform
C^{2}
-bounds and the Schur-complement convexity argument of Lemma
4.6
show that both
L_{t,x_{0}}
and
W_{t,x_{0}}
are strictly convex for all sufficiently large
t
. Indeed, their longitudinal profile second derivatives are
f+\varepsilon_{t}
and
f-\varepsilon_{t}
, both bounded below by
c_{f}/2
for large
t
, while the transverse block remains a small relative perturbation of
tI_{n-1}
.

The comparison principle, with the orientation recalled above, now gives

L_{t,x_{0}}\leq u_{t,\omega}^{f}\leq W_{t,x_{0}}\quad\text{in }D_{x_{0}}.

(4.45)

Indeed, the lower barrier has determinant at least
f=\det D^{2}u_{t,\omega}^{f}
, so it lies below
u_{t,\omega}^{f}
; the upper barrier has determinant at most
f
, so
u_{t,\omega}^{f}
lies below it.

At
x_{0}
, the three functions in (
4.45
) have the same boundary value. Since
u_{t,\omega}^{f}-L_{t,x_{0}}\geq 0
and
W_{t,x_{0}}-u_{t,\omega}^{f}\geq 0
in the one-sided collar, their outward normal derivatives at
x_{0}
are nonpositive. Hence

\partial_{\nu}W_{t,x_{0}}(x_{0})\leq\partial_{\nu}u_{t,\omega}^{f}(x_{0})\leq\partial_{\nu}L_{t,x_{0}}(x_{0}).

(4.46)

Using (
4.32
) and
G_{x_{0}}=-b_{\omega}+p_{x_{0}}
, we get

\partial_{\nu}G_{x_{0}}(x_{0})=-\partial_{\nu}b_{\omega}(x_{0}).

Therefore

\partial_{\nu}L_{t,x_{0}}(x_{0})=\partial_{\nu}U_{t}(x_{0})+\eta_{t}\partial_{\nu}b_{\omega}(x_{0}),\qquad\partial_{\nu}W_{t,x_{0}}(x_{0})=\partial_{\nu}U_{t}(x_{0})-\eta_{t}\partial_{\nu}b_{\omega}(x_{0}).

By (
4.35
),
\partial_{\nu}b_{\omega}(x_{0})>0
, and the non-glancing estimates give

\sup_{K}|\partial_{\nu}b_{\omega}|\leq C_{K}.

Thus (
4.46
) implies

\left|\partial_{\nu}u_{t,\omega}^{f}(x_{0})-\partial_{\nu}U_{t}(x_{0})\right|\leq C_{K}\eta_{t}=C_{K}t^{1-n-n/2},

uniformly for
x_{0}\in K
. Finally,

\partial_{\nu}U_{t}=t\partial_{\nu}\phi_{\omega}+t^{1-n}\partial_{\nu}w_{\omega}^{f},

and
t^{1-n-n/2}=o(t^{1-n})
. This proves the proposition.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.3
.

The estimate (
3.18
) follows from (
4.28
) in Proposition
4.8
, after absorbing the fixed factor
\operatorname{diam}(\Omega)^{2}/8
into the constant. The estimate (
3.19
) follows from (
4.36
) in Proposition
4.9
, together with

\partial_{\nu}U_{t}=t\partial_{\nu}\phi_{\omega}+t^{1-n}\partial_{\nu}w_{\omega}^{f}.

∎

The comparison argument uses global barriers for each fixed direction
\omega
. The only non-glancing restriction left in the boundary derivative statement is intrinsic to the recovery formula: at glancing points the factor
\omega\cdot\nu
vanishes, so the endpoint identity will not be used there. The estimates above are proved for each fixed
\omega
; the constants are not claimed to be uniform as
\omega
varies.

5
From boundary asymptotics to X-ray data

We now show how the boundary asymptotic proved in Theorem
3.3
determines chord integrals of the source. This is the point at which the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map enters the recovery of the source.

5.1
Boundary limits determined by the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map

For
f\in\mathcal{F}
,
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, and
x\in\Gamma_{\omega}
, define

L_{\omega}^{f}(x)=\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{n-1}\left[\Lambda_{f}(t\phi_{\omega}|_{\partial\Omega})(x)-t\partial_{\nu}\phi_{\omega}(x)\right].

(5.1)

The limit exists locally uniformly on
\Gamma_{\omega}
by Theorem
3.3
, and

L_{\omega}^{f}(x)=\partial_{\nu}w_{\omega}^{f}(x),\qquad x\in\Gamma_{\omega}.

(5.2)

Thus
L_{\omega}^{f}
is determined by the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map on the large cylindrical family.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathcal{F}
. Suppose that, for some
T_{0}>0
,

\Lambda_{f_{1}}(t\phi_{\omega}|_{\partial\Omega})=\Lambda_{f_{2}}(t\phi_{\omega}|_{\partial\Omega})

for all
t\geq T_{0}
and all
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. Then

L_{\omega}^{f_{1}}=L_{\omega}^{f_{2}}\quad\text{on }\Gamma_{\omega}

for every
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
.

Proof.

Fix
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and
x\in\Gamma_{\omega}
. For all sufficiently large
t
, the equality of the Dirichlet-to-Neumann data gives

t^{n-1}\left[\Lambda_{f_{1}}(t\phi_{\omega}|_{\partial\Omega})(x)-t\partial_{\nu}\phi_{\omega}(x)\right]=t^{n-1}\left[\Lambda_{f_{2}}(t\phi_{\omega}|_{\partial\Omega})(x)-t\partial_{\nu}\phi_{\omega}(x)\right].

Passing to the limit
t\to\infty
and using (
5.1
) gives the claim.
∎

5.2
Endpoint derivatives and chord integrals

Definition 5.2

(Chordwise X-ray transform)
.

For
f\in C(\overline{\Omega})
, define

\mathcal{X}_{\omega}f(y)=\int_{s_{-}(y)}^{s_{+}(y)}f(y+s\omega)\,ds,\qquad y\in\Omega_{\omega}.

(5.3)

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
w=w_{\omega}^{f}
be defined by (
3.10
). Then, for every
y\in\Omega_{\omega}
,

\mathcal{X}_{\omega}f(y)=\partial_{s}w(y,s_{+}(y))-\partial_{s}w(y,s_{-}(y)).

(5.4)

Moreover, since
w=0
on the boundary endpoints of the chords,

\mathcal{X}_{\omega}f(y)=(\omega\cdot\nu(x_{+}(y)))\,\partial_{\nu}w(x_{+}(y))-(\omega\cdot\nu(x_{-}(y)))\,\partial_{\nu}w(x_{-}(y)).

(5.5)

Proof.

Integrating
\partial_{s}^{2}w=f
along the chord gives

\int_{s_{-}(y)}^{s_{+}(y)}f(y+s\omega)\,ds=\partial_{s}w(y,s_{+}(y))-\partial_{s}w(y,s_{-}(y)),

which is (
5.4
). By Lemma
3.1
, the endpoints
x_{\pm}(y)
belong to the non-glancing boundary pieces
\Gamma_{\omega}^{\pm}
, and the maps
y\mapsto x_{\pm}(y)
locally parametrize those pieces. Since
w(y,s_{\pm}(y))=0
, the function
w
vanishes on these non-glancing boundary pieces. Thus the tangential derivative of
w
along
\partial\Omega
vanishes at
x_{\pm}(y)
. Therefore
\nabla w(x_{\pm}(y))
is parallel to
\nu(x_{\pm}(y))
, and

\partial_{s}w(x_{\pm}(y))=\omega\cdot\nabla w(x_{\pm}(y))=(\omega\cdot\nu(x_{\pm}(y)))\partial_{\nu}w(x_{\pm}(y)).

Substituting these identities into (
5.4
) proves (
5.5
).
∎

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{F}
. Then the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map on the large cylindrical family determines
\mathcal{X}_{\omega}f
for every
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. More precisely, for every
y\in\Omega_{\omega}
,

\mathcal{X}_{\omega}f(y)=(\omega\cdot\nu(x_{+}(y)))\,L_{\omega}^{f}(x_{+}(y))-(\omega\cdot\nu(x_{-}(y)))\,L_{\omega}^{f}(x_{-}(y)).

(5.6)

Proof.

By Lemma
3.1
, the endpoints
x_{\pm}(y)
belong to the non-glancing sets
\Gamma_{\omega}^{\pm}
. Hence (
5.2
) applies at both endpoints. Substituting
L_{\omega}^{f}=\partial_{\nu}w_{\omega}^{f}
into the endpoint identity (
5.5
) gives (
5.6
).
∎

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathcal{F}
. If there is
T_{0}>0
such that

\Lambda_{f_{1}}(t\phi_{\omega}|_{\partial\Omega})=\Lambda_{f_{2}}(t\phi_{\omega}|_{\partial\Omega})

for all
t\geq T_{0}
and all
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, then

\mathcal{X}_{\omega}f_{1}(y)=\mathcal{X}_{\omega}f_{2}(y)

for every
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and every
y\in\Omega_{\omega}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.1
, the equality of the DN maps implies equality of the limits
L_{\omega}^{f_{1}}=L_{\omega}^{f_{2}}
on
\Gamma_{\omega}
. Proposition
5.4
then gives equality of the corresponding chord integrals.
∎

5.3
Glancing points cause no loss of data

The boundary asymptotic is used only on the non-glancing boundary set
\Gamma_{\omega}
. This subsection records explicitly that this loses no information needed for the X-ray step. For a fixed direction
\omega
, the only projected lines not represented by interior chords are tangent lines, and their chord length is zero.

Lemma 5.6
.

Fix
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. Then

\overline{\Omega_{\omega}}=P_{\omega^{\perp}}\overline{\Omega},

(5.7)

and the projected glancing set is exactly the boundary of the projected domain:

P_{\omega^{\perp}}\Gamma_{\omega}^{\mathrm{gl}}=\partial\Omega_{\omega}.

(5.8)

Moreover, if
y_{j}\in\Omega_{\omega}
and
y_{j}\to y_{0}\in\partial\Omega_{\omega}
, then

\ell_{\omega}(y_{j})=s_{+}(y_{j})-s_{-}(y_{j})\longrightarrow 0.

(5.9)

Consequently, the positive-length chords
y\in\Omega_{\omega}
, whose endpoints are non-glancing by Lemma
3.1
, are dense in the set of all projected chords
\overline{\Omega_{\omega}}
. The missing boundary family consists only of zero-length tangent chords.

Proof.

Since
\overline{\Omega}
is compact and
P_{\omega^{\perp}}
is continuous,
P_{\omega^{\perp}}\overline{\Omega}
is compact. Because
\Omega
is dense in
\overline{\Omega}
, this compact set is the closure of
P_{\omega^{\perp}}\Omega=\Omega_{\omega}
. This proves (
5.7
).

Let
x\in\Gamma_{\omega}^{\mathrm{gl}}
, and set
y=P_{\omega^{\perp}}x
. Then
y\in\overline{\Omega_{\omega}}
. Since
\omega\cdot\nu(x)=0
, the line
x+\mathbb{R}\omega
lies in the tangent hyperplane to
\partial\Omega
at
x
. Uniform convexity implies strict convexity of
\overline{\Omega}
; hence this supporting hyperplane meets
\overline{\Omega}
only at
x
. Thus the line
y+\mathbb{R}\omega
does not meet
\Omega
, so
y\notin\Omega_{\omega}
. Therefore
y\in\partial\Omega_{\omega}
.

Conversely, let
y\in\partial\Omega_{\omega}
. By (
5.7
), there is
x\in\overline{\Omega}
with
P_{\omega^{\perp}}x=y
. Since
y\notin\Omega_{\omega}
, this point cannot lie in
\Omega
, and hence
x\in\partial\Omega
. If
\omega\cdot\nu(x)\neq 0
, then the line
x+\mathbb{R}\omega
crosses
\partial\Omega
transversely at
x
. The implicit function theorem, or simply the sign change of a defining function along this transverse line, then gives nearby points of
\Omega
with the same projection
y
, contradicting
y\notin\Omega_{\omega}
. Hence
x\in\Gamma_{\omega}^{\mathrm{gl}}
, proving (
5.8
).

It remains to prove (
5.9
). Suppose instead that, after passing to a subsequence,
\ell_{\omega}(y_{j})\geq\delta>0
. The endpoints

x_{\pm,j}=y_{j}+s_{\pm}(y_{j})\omega

lie in the compact set
\partial\Omega
, so after passing to a further subsequence they converge to points
x_{\pm}\in\partial\Omega
. Their projections are both
y_{0}
, and

|x_{+}-x_{-}|=\lim_{j\to\infty}\ell_{\omega}(y_{j})\geq\delta,

so
x_{+}\neq x_{-}
. By strict convexity, the open segment joining
x_{-}
to
x_{+}
lies in
\Omega
. Every point of this segment projects to
y_{0}
, so
y_{0}\in\Omega_{\omega}
, contradicting
y_{0}\in\partial\Omega_{\omega}
. Therefore
\ell_{\omega}(y_{j})\to 0
.
∎

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
F\in C(\overline{\Omega})
, and fix
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. Define

\overline{\mathcal{X}}_{\omega}F(y)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{s_{-}(y)}^{s_{+}(y)}F(y+s\omega)\,ds,&y\in\Omega_{\omega},\\[11.99998pt]
0,&y\in\omega^{\perp}\setminus\Omega_{\omega}.\end{cases}

(5.10)

Then
\overline{\mathcal{X}}_{\omega}F
is continuous on
\omega^{\perp}
. In particular, it vanishes on
\partial\Omega_{\omega}=P_{\omega^{\perp}}\Gamma_{\omega}^{\mathrm{gl}}
.

Proof.

Continuity inside
\Omega_{\omega}
follows from the smooth dependence of
s_{\pm}(y)
and the continuity of
F
. The function is identically zero on the open set
\omega^{\perp}\setminus\overline{\Omega_{\omega}}
. It remains to check continuity at
y_{0}\in\partial\Omega_{\omega}
. If
y_{j}\to y_{0}
and
y_{j}\in\Omega_{\omega}
, then

|\overline{\mathcal{X}}_{\omega}F(y_{j})|\leq\|F\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\ell_{\omega}(y_{j})\longrightarrow 0

by Lemma
5.6
. If
y_{j}\notin\Omega_{\omega}
, the value is already zero. Hence
\overline{\mathcal{X}}_{\omega}F
is continuous at the boundary as well.
∎

Proposition 5.8
.

Let
F\in C(\overline{\Omega})
. Suppose that, for every
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, the chord integrals
\mathcal{X}_{\omega}F(y)
are known for all positive-length chords
y\in\Omega_{\omega}
. Then the full Euclidean X-ray transform of the zero extension
\widetilde{F}
is known for all
y\in\omega^{\perp}
: it is
\mathcal{X}_{\omega}F(y)
on
\Omega_{\omega}
and is zero on
\omega^{\perp}\setminus\Omega_{\omega}
. In particular, if
\mathcal{X}_{\omega}F=0
on
\Omega_{\omega}
for every
\omega
, then
F=0
in
\Omega
.

Proof.

For
y\in\Omega_{\omega}
, the line
y+\mathbb{R}\omega
intersects
\Omega
in the chord
(s_{-}(y),s_{+}(y))
, so the Euclidean X-ray transform of the zero extension
\widetilde{F}
equals
\mathcal{X}_{\omega}F(y)
. If
y\notin\Omega_{\omega}
, the line does not meet the open set
\Omega
; at boundary points of
\Omega_{\omega}
it is a tangent line and contributes zero to the one-dimensional integral. Thus the missing glancing values are forced to be zero, consistently with the continuous extension in Lemma
5.7
. The final assertion follows from Lemma
5.9
.
∎

5.4
Injectivity of the X-ray transform

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
F\in C(\overline{\Omega})
. If

\mathcal{X}_{\omega}F(y)=0

for every
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and every
y\in\Omega_{\omega}
, then
F=0
in
\Omega
.

Proof.

Extend
F
by zero to a compactly supported function
\widetilde{F}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. For fixed
\omega
, define the Euclidean X-ray transform

R_{\omega}\widetilde{F}(y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{F}(y+s\omega)\,ds,\qquad y\in\omega^{\perp}.

If
y\in\Omega_{\omega}
, this is precisely
\mathcal{X}_{\omega}F(y)
, and if
y\notin\Omega_{\omega}
, the line
y+\mathbb{R}\omega
does not meet
\Omega
, so
R_{\omega}\widetilde{F}(y)=0
. Hence
R_{\omega}\widetilde{F}=0
for every
\omega
.

Taking the Fourier transform in the
y
-variable on
\omega^{\perp}
, one obtains the Fourier slice identity; this is the standard Fourier-slice proof of injectivity for the X-ray/Radon transform; see
[
32
]
:

\widehat{R_{\omega}\widetilde{F}}(\xi)=\widehat{\widetilde{F}}(\xi),\qquad\xi\in\omega^{\perp}.

Indeed,

\displaystyle\widehat{R_{\omega}\widetilde{F}}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\omega^{\perp}}e^{-iy\cdot\xi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{F}(y+s\omega)\,ds\,dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-ix\cdot\xi}\widetilde{F}(x)\,dx=\widehat{\widetilde{F}}(\xi),

because
\xi\cdot\omega=0
. Given any
\xi\neq 0
, choose
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
with
\xi\in\omega^{\perp}
. Since
R_{\omega}\widetilde{F}=0
, the identity gives
\widehat{\widetilde{F}}(\xi)=0
. Since
\widetilde{F}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, its Fourier transform is continuous; hence the same conclusion holds at
\xi=0
. Thus
\widehat{\widetilde{F}}\equiv 0
, and hence
\widetilde{F}=0
, so
F=0
in
\Omega
.
∎

6
Proof of uniqueness

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, be bounded, smooth, and uniformly convex. Let

f_{1},f_{2}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}),\qquad 0<c\leq f_{j}\leq C<\infty.

Assume that there is
T_{0}>0
such that

\Lambda_{f_{1}}(g_{t,\omega})=\Lambda_{f_{2}}(g_{t,\omega})

(6.1)

for all
t\geq T_{0}
and all
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. Then

f_{1}=f_{2}\quad\text{in }\Omega.

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.

By Corollary
5.5
, the equality of the Dirichlet-to-Neumann maps on the large cylindrical family implies

\mathcal{X}_{\omega}f_{1}(y)=\mathcal{X}_{\omega}f_{2}(y)

for every
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and every
y\in\Omega_{\omega}
. Equivalently,

\mathcal{X}_{\omega}(f_{1}-f_{2})(y)=0

for every positive-length chord of
\Omega
. Proposition
5.8
shows that the missing glancing values cause no loss for the Euclidean X-ray transform of the zero extension. Lemma
5.9
, applied to
F=f_{1}-f_{2}
, gives
f_{1}=f_{2}
in
\Omega
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

For each fixed
\omega
, the trace
g_{t,\omega}=t\phi_{\omega}|_{\partial\Omega}
is a smooth boundary value for every
t>0
. The assumed equality of the full Dirichlet-to-Neumann maps therefore gives

\Lambda_{f_{1}}(g_{t,\omega})=\Lambda_{f_{2}}(g_{t,\omega})

for all
t>0
and all
\omega\in S^{n-1}
. In particular, the hypothesis of Theorem
6.1
holds for any
T_{0}>0
, and that theorem gives
f_{1}=f_{2}
in
\Omega
.
∎
