Bogoliubov sum rules and the Knight-shift ellipsoid in noncentrosymmetric superconductors
Yi Zhou

Abstract
We show that the residual T = 0 T=0 Knight shift of a noncentrosymmetric superconductor in the strong-locking regime is completely determined by a single Fermi-surface average—the projector Π μ ​ ν = ⟨ n ^ μ ​ n ^ ν ⟩ FS \Pi_{\mu\nu}=\langle\hat{n}_{\mu}\hat{n}_{\nu}\rangle_{\rm FS} of the spin-locking direction n ^ 𝐤 \hat{n}_{\mathbf{k}} —giving the tensor identity χ μ ​ ν ​ ( 0 ) = χ N ​ [ δ μ ​ ν − Π μ ​ ν ] \chi_{\mu\nu}(0)=\chi_{N}[\delta_{\mu\nu}-\Pi_{\mu\nu}] independently of pairing symmetry, gap magnitude, and Fermi-surface shape. Physically, Π μ ​ ν \Pi_{\mu\nu} records the directions along which pairing consumes the Pauli spin response: at each Fermi-surface point, the Cooper pair binds two electrons antiparallel along n ^ 𝐤 \hat{n}_{\mathbf{k}} , and the Knight-shift deficit measures the fraction of the FS along which this consumption is aligned with the applied field. Because Tr ​ Π = 1 \mathrm{Tr}\,\Pi=1 , the three principal Knight shifts at T = 0 T=0 lie on a two-dimensional simplex of locking textures, the Knight-shift ellipsoid , whose vertices, edges, and interior classify every canonical pairing class. The identity follows from a Bogoliubov sum rule, ∑ | M p ​ h , O | 2 + ∑ | M p ​ p , O | 2 = Tr s ​ ( O 2 ) \sum|M_{ph,O}|^{2}+\sum|M_{pp,O}|^{2}=\mathrm{Tr}_{s}(O^{2}) , valid at every momentum for every Hermitian single-particle operator O O as the BdG-doubled form of unitary invariance. Around the central theorem we develop controlled departures (a closed-form s s -wave SOC interpolation, a finite-field strong-locking identity), a dynamical counterpart (a spin Ferrell–Glover–Tinkham sum rule and a rigorous vanishing-projection theorem for 1 / T 1 1/T_{1} ), and a decoupled-pocket multiband baseline, packaged into six experimental protocols. I
Introduction

The Knight shift is one of the oldest and most direct probes of spin correlations in superconductors. Reif’s early measurement on mercury
31
and the subsequent tin experiment of Androes and Knight
5
revealed that the Pauli-paramagnetic response is not fully suppressed at
T=0
, contrary to the naive BCS expectation
9
;
47
. The theoretical response came rapidly: Ferrell
17
, Anderson
3
, Martin and Kadanoff
25
, and Schrieffer
34
identified spin-orbit scattering as the essential mechanism, subsequently refined by Appel
6
, Shiba
35
, Zhogolev and Glasser
48
, and by Anderson’s dirty-limit analysis
4
. The residual Knight shift was thereby recognized as a quantitative measure of the interplay between pair formation and spin-rotation symmetry. Standard treatments of NMR in metals and superconductors are given in Refs.
37
;
41
.

In noncentrosymmetric superconductors this interplay acquires a geometric character. Broken inversion symmetry generates an antisymmetric spin-orbit coupling (SOC)
30
;
15
that splits the Fermi surface into helicity sheets with locked spin texture, and the residual Knight shift then reflects the Fermi-surface average of that texture rather than a global spin-rotation property of the pairing. The consequences have been worked out for the zero-field susceptibility in specific models
18
;
19
;
32
;
16
and surveyed in broader reviews
10
;
45
;
33
;
38
. Reference
29
closed a gap in this literature by developing a self-consistent single-band Bogoliubov–de Gennes (BdG) theory of
\chi_{\mu\nu}(T)
valid at arbitrary SOC strength and arbitrary Zeeman field. The companion paper
28
treats the transition temperature and quasiparticle excitations in the same framework; the present paper focuses on the static and dynamical spin response.

The main result of this paper is a single geometric theorem for the residual
T=0
spin response in the strong-locking regime:

\chi_{\mu\nu}(0)=\chi_{N}\big[\delta_{\mu\nu}-\Pi_{\mu\nu}\big],\qquad\Pi_{\mu\nu}\equiv\langle\hat{n}_{\mu}\hat{n}_{\nu}\rangle_{\rm FS}.

(1)

The Knight-shift tensor at zero temperature is completely determined by a single Fermi-surface average—the projector
\Pi_{\mu\nu}
of the spin-locking direction
\hat{n}_{\mathbf{k}}
—independently of pairing symmetry, gap magnitude, and Fermi-surface shape. Because
\mathrm{Tr}\,\Pi=1
, the three principal Knight shifts at
T=0
lie on a two-dimensional simplex of locking textures, which we call the
Knight-shift ellipsoid
. Every canonical pairing class—singlet with strong SOC at the barycenter, opposite-spin-pairing (OSP) and equal-spin-pairing (ESP) triplets at vertices and edge midpoints, quasi-1D and 2D Rashba textures at the same axial points—occupies a distinct location on this simplex, making the ellipsoid a direct experimental diagnostic. Here “geometric” refers to the Fermi-surface texture of
\hat{n}_{\mathbf{k}}
rather than to the quantum-geometric (Berry) tensor of Bloch states.

Equation (
1
) follows from a Bogoliubov sum rule,

\sum_{s_{1}s_{2}}\big[W^{s_{1}s_{2}}_{ph,O}(\mathbf{k})+W^{s_{1}s_{2}}_{pp,O}(\mathbf{k})\big]=\mathrm{Tr}_{s}(O^{2}),

(2)

valid pointwise at every
\mathbf{k}
for every Hermitian single-particle operator
O
. The sum rule is the BdG-doubled form of the elementary unitary-invariance identity
\mathrm{Tr}[(U^{\dagger}OU)^{2}]=\mathrm{Tr}(O^{2})
: the Bogoliubov doubling splits this invariance into particle-hole and particle-particle sectors and yields a pointwise weight budget on arbitrary single-particle probes. Its content lies not in the derivation but in the systematic conversion of operator-algebraic weight budgets into Fermi-surface geometric statements about
\Pi_{\mu\nu}
. The spin specialization
O=\sigma_{\mu}
recovers the matrix-element identity used in Ref.
29
; arbitrary Hermitian
O
generates the charge, multi-orbital, site-resolved, and dynamical variants used below.

II
Setup and notation

We adopt the single-band BdG framework of Refs.
28
;
29
;
9
;
14
;
41
;
37
. The mean-field Hamiltonian is

H=\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}C^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}}\mathcal{H}_{\rm BdG}(\mathbf{k})C_{\mathbf{k}}+\text{const.},

(3)

with Nambu spinor
C_{\mathbf{k}}=(c_{\mathbf{k}\uparrow},c_{\mathbf{k}\downarrow},c^{\dagger}_{-\mathbf{k}\uparrow},c^{\dagger}_{-\mathbf{k}\downarrow})^{T}
and

\mathcal{H}_{\rm BdG}(\mathbf{k})=\begin{pmatrix}H_{0}(\mathbf{k})&\Delta(\mathbf{k})\\
\Delta^{\dagger}(\mathbf{k})&-H_{0}^{T}(-\mathbf{k})\end{pmatrix}.

(4)

The normal-state block contains kinetic energy, Zeeman coupling, and antisymmetric SOC,

H_{0}(\mathbf{k})=\xi_{\mathbf{k}}\sigma_{0}+\mu_{B}\mathbf{H}\cdot\hat{\sigma}+\mathbf{g}_{\mathbf{k}}\cdot\hat{\sigma},\qquad\mathbf{g}_{-\mathbf{k}}=-\mathbf{g}_{\mathbf{k}},

(5)

with
\mathbf{g}_{\mathbf{k}}
a general SOC vector (not necessarily
g\mathbf{k}
)
30
;
15
. The pairing block is the most general spin-dependent gap matrix in two-component notation
7
;
36
;
23
,

\Delta(\mathbf{k})=i\big[\psi(\mathbf{k})\sigma_{0}+\mathbf{d}(\mathbf{k})\cdot\hat{\sigma}\big]\sigma_{y},

(6)

with even spin-singlet amplitude
\psi(-\mathbf{k})=\psi(\mathbf{k})
and odd spin-triplet
\mathbf{d}
-vector
\mathbf{d}(-\mathbf{k})=-\mathbf{d}(\mathbf{k})
. Inversion symmetry is broken, so both components are generically present; pure-singlet
\mathbf{d}=0
and pure-triplet
\psi=0
limits are recovered as special cases. We restrict throughout to
unitary
pairing,
\Delta(\mathbf{k})\Delta^{\dagger}(\mathbf{k})\propto\mathbb{I}
, equivalently
\mathbf{d}(\mathbf{k})\times\mathbf{d}^{*}(\mathbf{k})=0
, so that the gap matrix has equal eigenvalues on the two helicity sheets up to the helicity-resolved amplitudes of Sec.
VI
.

Diagonalization by

c_{\mathbf{k}\alpha}=\sum_{s=\pm}\!\left(u^{\alpha s}_{\mathbf{k}}\psi_{\mathbf{k}s}+v^{\alpha s}_{\mathbf{k}}\psi^{\dagger}_{-\mathbf{k}s}\right)

(7)

yields quasiparticle energies
E_{\mathbf{k}s}\geq 0
and a unitary

U_{\mathbf{k}}=\begin{pmatrix}u_{\mathbf{k}}&v_{\mathbf{k}}\\
v^{*}_{-\mathbf{k}}&u^{*}_{-\mathbf{k}}\end{pmatrix},\qquad U^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}}U_{\mathbf{k}}=\mathbb{I}_{\rm BdG},

(8)

relating
C_{\mathbf{k}}=U_{\mathbf{k}}\Psi_{\mathbf{k}}
with
\Psi_{\mathbf{k}}=(\psi_{\mathbf{k}+},\psi_{\mathbf{k}-},\psi^{\dagger}_{-\mathbf{k}+},\psi^{\dagger}_{-\mathbf{k}-})^{T}
. The static uniform spin susceptibility decomposes as
\chi_{\mu\mu}=\chi^{ph+}_{\mu\mu}+\chi^{ph-}_{\mu\mu}+\chi^{pp+}_{\mu\mu}+\chi^{pp-}_{\mu\mu}
with each term non-negative
29
. Throughout,
\mathrm{Tr}_{s}
denotes a trace over the single-particle spin (or spin
\otimes
orbital) space and
\mathrm{Tr}_{\rm BdG}
the doubled Nambu trace.

The normal-state eigenstates of Eq. (
5
) have spins along the unit vector

\hat{n}_{\mathbf{k}}=\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{k}}/|\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{k}}|,\qquad\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{k}}\equiv\mu_{B}\mathbf{H}+\mathbf{g}_{\mathbf{k}},

(9)

reducing to
\hat{n}_{\mathbf{k}}=\hat{g}_{\mathbf{k}}
at zero Zeeman field. Fermi-surface (FS) averages use the single-particle DOS weight
\langle X\rangle_{\rm FS}\equiv N(0)^{-1}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}X(\mathbf{k})\delta(\xi_{\mathbf{k}})
, with
\chi_{N}=2\mu_{B}^{2}N(0)
the Pauli value
47
;
42
. The
3\times 3
projection tensor

\boxed{\;\Pi_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf{H},\mathbf{g})\equiv\big\langle\hat{n}_{\mu}(\mathbf{k})\,\hat{n}_{\nu}(\mathbf{k})\big\rangle_{\rm FS}\;}

(10)

is symmetric, positive semi-definite, with
\mathrm{Tr}\,\Pi=1
. This tensor is the central geometric object of the paper.

III
Exact identity: the Bogoliubov sum rule

Everything that follows rests on recasting the sum rule of Ref.
29
as a Bogoliubov sum rule on the BdG transformation. This framing makes the geometric content manifest and generates on demand the charge, multi-orbital, and site-resolved variants used later.

III.1
Nambu embedding of a single-particle observable

Let
O
be any Hermitian operator on the single-particle space. Because the Nambu spinor
C_{\mathbf{k}}
carries both
c_{\mathbf{k}}
and
c_{-\mathbf{k}}
, a Nambu-form bilinear at a single
\mathbf{k}
necessarily pairs the two momenta. We therefore work with the symmetrized bilinear associated with the Nambu pair
\{\mathbf{k},-\mathbf{k}\}
,

\hat{O}^{(\mathbf{k})}\equiv\tfrac{1}{2}\!\sum_{\mathbf{q}\in\{\mathbf{k},-\mathbf{k}\}}\sum_{\alpha\beta}c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{q}\alpha}O_{\alpha\beta}c_{\mathbf{q}\beta},

(11)

which can be rewritten exactly in Nambu form as

\begin{split}\hat{O}^{(\mathbf{k})}&=C^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}}\,\mathcal{O}_{\rm BdG}(\mathbf{k})\,C_{\mathbf{k}}+\tfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tr}_{s}(O),\\
\mathcal{O}_{\rm BdG}(\mathbf{k})&=\tfrac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}O&0\\
0&-O^{T}\end{pmatrix},\end{split}

(12)

with the Nambu ordering of Eq. (
8
). The minus sign on
-O^{T}
is forced by the anticommutator structure of the lower Nambu block; together with Hermiticity of
O
it makes
\mathcal{O}_{\rm BdG}(\mathbf{k})
Hermitian. The constant is absorbed into the chemical potential. Using
O^{T}=O^{*}
for Hermitian
O
,

\mathrm{Tr}_{\rm BdG}(\mathcal{O}_{\rm BdG})=0,\quad\mathrm{Tr}_{\rm BdG}(\mathcal{O}_{\rm BdG}^{2})=\tfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tr}_{s}(O^{2}).

(13)

The full single-particle observable is recovered as
\hat{O}=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{BZ}/\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\hat{O}^{(\mathbf{k})}
, the sum running over one representative of each Nambu pair.

III.2
The Bogoliubov sum rule

Transforming to the quasiparticle basis,
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbf{k}}=U^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}}\mathcal{O}_{\rm BdG}(\mathbf{k})U_{\mathbf{k}}
, yields the block form

\tilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbf{k}}=\tfrac{1}{2}\!\begin{pmatrix}M_{ph,O}(\mathbf{k})&M_{pp,O}(\mathbf{k})\\
M^{\dagger}_{pp,O}(\mathbf{k})&\widetilde{M}_{ph,O}(\mathbf{k})\end{pmatrix},

(14)

with the raw matrix-element matrices

\displaystyle M_{ph,O}(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=u^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}}Ou_{\mathbf{k}}-[v^{\dagger}_{-\mathbf{k}}Ov_{-\mathbf{k}}]^{T},

(15)

\displaystyle M_{pp,O}(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=u^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}}Ov_{\mathbf{k}}-[u^{\dagger}_{-\mathbf{k}}Ov_{-\mathbf{k}}]^{T},

(16)

and
\widetilde{M}_{ph,O}(\mathbf{k})=-M_{ph,O}(-\mathbf{k})^{T}
. Helicity indices are written as superscripts:
M^{s_{1}s_{2}}_{ph/pp,O}(\mathbf{k})
denotes the
(s_{1},s_{2})
component of the corresponding raw matrix.

Unitarity of
U_{\mathbf{k}}
and cyclicity of the trace give

\mathrm{Tr}_{\rm BdG}(\tilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbf{k}}^{2})=\mathrm{Tr}_{\rm BdG}(\mathcal{O}_{\rm BdG}^{2})=\tfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tr}_{s}(O^{2}),

(17)

identically at each
\mathbf{k}
. Squaring Eq. (
14
) and taking the BdG trace,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}_{\rm BdG}(\tilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbf{k}}^{2})={}

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{4}\big[\mathrm{Tr}(M_{ph,O}^{2}(\mathbf{k}))+\mathrm{Tr}(\widetilde{M}_{ph,O}^{2}(\mathbf{k}))\big]

(18)

\displaystyle+\tfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tr}\big(M^{\dagger}_{pp,O}(\mathbf{k})M_{pp,O}(\mathbf{k})\big).

Using
\widetilde{M}_{ph,O}(\mathbf{k})=-M_{ph,O}(-\mathbf{k})^{T}
one has
\mathrm{Tr}(\widetilde{M}_{ph,O}^{2}(\mathbf{k}))=\mathrm{Tr}(M_{ph,O}^{2}(-\mathbf{k}))
, and the ph contribution to Eq. (
18
) is manifestly symmetric in
\mathbf{k}\leftrightarrow-\mathbf{k}
. Introducing the
sum-rule weights
:

\displaystyle W^{s_{1}s_{2}}_{ph,O}(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle\equiv\tfrac{1}{2}\big[|M^{s_{1}s_{2}}_{ph,O}(\mathbf{k})|^{2}+|M^{s_{1}s_{2}}_{ph,O}(-\mathbf{k})|^{2}\big],

(19)

\displaystyle W^{s_{1}s_{2}}_{pp,O}(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle\equiv|M^{s_{1}s_{2}}_{pp,O}(\mathbf{k})|^{2},

(20)

and using Hermiticity of
M_{ph,O}(\mathbf{k})
to write
\mathrm{Tr}(M_{ph,O}^{2})=\sum_{s_{1}s_{2}}|M^{s_{1}s_{2}}_{ph,O}|^{2}
, Eq. (
18
) becomes

\boxed{\;\sum_{s_{1}s_{2}}\!\big[W^{s_{1}s_{2}}_{ph,O}(\mathbf{k})+W^{s_{1}s_{2}}_{pp,O}(\mathbf{k})\big]=\mathrm{Tr}_{s}(O^{2}),\;}

(21)

valid at every
\mathbf{k}
and for every Hermitian single-particle operator
O
. This is the
Bogoliubov sum rule
. The symmetrization in Eq. (
19
) is required to make the ph sector
\mathbf{k}\leftrightarrow-\mathbf{k}
-symmetric; the pp sum
\sum_{s_{1}s_{2}}|M^{s_{1}s_{2}}_{pp,O}(\mathbf{k})|^{2}
is already invariant under
\mathbf{k}\leftrightarrow-\mathbf{k}
by the antisymmetric structure of
M_{pp,O}
, so no explicit symmetrization is needed for pp weights.

Origin.
Equation (
21
) is the Bogoliubov-doubled form of the elementary unitary-invariance identity
\mathrm{Tr}[(U^{\dagger}OU)^{2}]=\mathrm{Tr}(O^{2})
. In the single-particle setting this is the standard preservation of the Frobenius norm under a unitary change of basis; the BdG doubling splits the identity into particle-hole and particle-particle sectors, which is the form used throughout the paper.

Relation to Ref.
29
.
The sum rule Eq. (14) of Ref.
29
is the specialization
O=\sigma_{\mu}
of Eq. (
21
) and is the only case derived there. The extension to arbitrary Hermitian
O
enables the charge, multi-orbital, and site-resolved variants below—in particular, the hyperfine-coupled operator
O_{\rm hf}=A^{(\mathbf{R})}_{\alpha\mu}\sigma_{\mu}
and its site-resolved bound Eq. (
23
).

III.3
Specializations

(a) Spin sector.
For
O=\sigma_{\mu}
,
\mathrm{Tr}_{s}(\sigma_{\mu}^{2})=2
, reproducing Eq. (14) of Ref.
29
. Summing over
\mu=x,y,z
,

\sum_{\mu}\sum_{s_{1}s_{2}}\big[W^{s_{1}s_{2}}_{ph,\mu}(\mathbf{k})+W^{s_{1}s_{2}}_{pp,\mu}(\mathbf{k})\big]=6.

(22)

(b) Charge sector.
For
O=\sigma_{0}
,
\mathrm{Tr}_{s}(\sigma_{0}^{2})=2
; this constrains finite-
\mathbf{q}
charge response.

(c) Multi-orbital extension.
With orbitals,
O=\sigma_{\mu}\otimes\Lambda
gives
\mathrm{Tr}_{s}(O^{2})=2\mathrm{Tr}(\Lambda^{2})
; for orbital-diagonal (
\Lambda=\mathbb{I}_{\rm orb}
) this reproduces the additive multi-pocket form of Sec.

.

(d) Site-resolved hyperfine coupling.
An NMR nucleus at site

\mathrm{Tr}_{s}(O_{\rm hf}^{2})=2\|A^{(\mathbf{R})}\|_{F}^{2}

\frac{\mathrm{Tr}\,K^{(\mathbf{R})}(0)}{\mathrm{Tr}\,K^{(\mathbf{R})}(T_{c})}\leq\tfrac{2}{3}

(23)

is independent of

. Different nuclei in a single compound therefore obey identical normalized bounds—an internal consistency check deployed as a protocol in Sec.
XIII

III.4
T=0
consequence for spin-like probes

At
T=0
only the pp channel contributes to any susceptibility
29
:

\chi_{O}(0)=\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\sum_{s_{1}s_{2}}\frac{W^{s_{1}s_{2}}_{pp,O}(\mathbf{k})}{E_{\mathbf{k}s_{1}}+E_{-\mathbf{k}s_{2}}}.

(24)

Equation (
21
) bounds the numerator pointwise,

\sum_{s_{1}s_{2}}W^{s_{1}s_{2}}_{pp,O}(\mathbf{k})\leq\mathrm{Tr}_{s}(O^{2}),

(25)

for every Hermitian
O
and every
\mathbf{k}
. Converting Eq. (
24
) into a bound on
\chi_{O}(0)/\chi_{O}^{N}
requires, in addition, that the normal-state reference
\chi_{O}^{N}
itself be expressible as the same FS integral of
\mathrm{Tr}_{s}(O^{2})
dressed by a known geometric factor. This holds whenever
O=\hat{e}\cdot\vec{\sigma}
for a fixed Cartesian direction
\hat{e}
: the intra-band weight is
W_{\rm intra}=2(\hat{n}_{\mathbf{k}}\cdot\hat{e})^{2}
, the inter-band weight is
W_{\rm inter}=2[1-(\hat{n}_{\mathbf{k}}\cdot\hat{e})^{2}]
, and on a 3D isotropic FS with
\langle(\hat{n}\cdot\hat{e})^{2}\rangle_{\rm FS}=1/3
one obtains

\boxed{\;\chi_{\mu\mu}(0)\leq\tfrac{2}{3}\chi_{N},\qquad O=\sigma_{\mu},\;}

(26)

with the same
2/3
factor for every Cartesian component of the spin susceptibility. Summing over
\mu
gives the isotropic trace bound of Sec.
IV
.

The prefactor
2/3
is therefore a statement about the 3D spin-locking geometry, not a universal feature of Eq. (
21
). The sum rule itself is operator-independent; the specific numerical bound on
\chi_{O}(0)/\chi_{O}^{N}
is not. For charge probes (
O=\sigma_{0}
) the intra-band weight is

First consequence: isotropic trace bound

IV.1
Derivation

Second consequence: strong-locking anisotropic identity

V.1
Normal-state benchmark at
T_{c}

At
T_{c}^{+}
, the intra-band and inter-band ph weights for
O=\sigma_{\mu}
read

\displaystyle W^{\rm intra}_{ph,\mu}(\mathbf{k})\equiv\sum_{s}W^{ss}_{ph,\mu}(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=2(\hat{n}_{\mathbf{k}}\cdot\hat{e}_{\mu})^{2},

(28)

\displaystyle W^{\rm inter}_{ph,\mu}(\mathbf{k})\equiv\sum_{s\neq s^{\prime}}W^{ss^{\prime}}_{ph,\mu}(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=2[1-(\hat{n}_{\mathbf{k}}\cdot\hat{e}_{\mu})^{2}],

(29)

with the inter-band sum including both off-diagonal elements. Inserted into Eqs. (13c)–(13d) of Ref.
29
and evaluated with
-df/dE\to\delta(E)
,

\chi^{ph+}_{\mu\mu}(T_{c})/\chi_{N}=\Pi_{\mu\mu},\quad\chi^{ph-}_{\mu\mu}(T_{c})/\chi_{N}=1-\Pi_{\mu\mu}.

(30)

The two contributions add to
\chi^{ph}_{\mu\mu}(T_{c})=\chi_{N}
, so
\mathrm{Tr}\,\chi(T_{c})=3\chi_{N}
for any FS and SOC.

V.2
Strong-locking identity at
T=0

When
\mathbf{H}=0
and
|\mathbf{g}_{\mathbf{k}}|\gg|\Delta|
on the FS, the SOC splitting dominates and a controlled expansion in
\Delta/|\mathbf{g}_{\mathbf{k}}|
is available. We derive Eq. (
41
) in three explicit steps: helicity block-diagonalization (i), longitudinal Yosida cancellation in the intra-helicity sector (ii), and an explicit evaluation of the inter-helicity pp coherence factor (iii). Step (iii) unpacks what the “phase-space cancellation identical to the normal state” assertion of Ref.
29
means.

(i) Helicity block-diagonalization.
Rotating at each
\mathbf{k}
to the helicity basis of
H_{0}(\mathbf{k})
, the pure-singlet pairing matrix takes the helicity-diagonal form
\Delta_{\pm}=\psi(\mathbf{k})
,
\Delta_{\perp}=0
. To zeroth order in
\Delta/|\mathbf{g}_{\mathbf{k}}|
the BdG Hamiltonian decouples into two intra-helicity
2\times 2
BCS blocks

H^{\lambda}_{\rm BdG}(\mathbf{k})=\begin{pmatrix}\xi_{\lambda}(\mathbf{k})&\Delta_{\lambda}(\mathbf{k})\\
\Delta^{*}_{\lambda}(\mathbf{k})&-\xi_{\lambda}(\mathbf{k})\end{pmatrix},\quad\xi_{\lambda}\equiv\xi_{\mathbf{k}}+\lambda|\mathbf{g}_{\mathbf{k}}|,

(31)

with
E_{\lambda}=\sqrt{\xi_{\lambda}^{2}+|\Delta_{\lambda}|^{2}}
and standard BCS coherence factors
u_{\lambda}^{2}=(1+\xi_{\lambda}/E_{\lambda})/2
,
v_{\lambda}^{2}=(1-\xi_{\lambda}/E_{\lambda})/2
. Each block describes pairing of the helicity Kramers pair
\{|\lambda,\mathbf{k}\rangle,|\lambda,-\mathbf{k}\rangle\}
, whose spins point along
\hat{n}_{\mathbf{k}}
and
\hat{n}_{-\mathbf{k}}=-\hat{n}_{\mathbf{k}}
respectively for Rashba-type SOC.

(ii) Intra-helicity sector: longitudinal Yosida cancellation.
Within each helicity sheet, the spin operator
\sigma_{\mu}
projects onto the local pseudo-spin quantization axis
\hat{n}_{\mathbf{k}}
as a longitudinal operator with weight
(\hat{n}_{\mathbf{k}}\cdot\hat{e}_{\mu})
; the transverse-to-
\hat{n}_{\mathbf{k}}
component connects to the opposite helicity sheet and contributes only to the inter-helicity sector below. The intra-helicity contribution to
\chi_{\mu\mu}
is therefore the longitudinal-Yosida response of a fully gapped BCS condensate,

\chi^{\rm intra}_{\mu\mu}(T)=\chi_{N}\sum_{\lambda}w_{\lambda}\big\langle(\hat{n}_{\mathbf{k}}\cdot\hat{e}_{\mu})^{2}\,Y_{\lambda}(\mathbf{k},T)\big\rangle_{\lambda},

(32)

with
w_{\lambda}=N_{\lambda}(0)/N(0)
and
Y_{\lambda}
the helicity-resolved Yosida function. For a fully gapped state
Y_{\lambda}(\mathbf{k},0)=0
, so

\chi^{\rm intra}_{\mu\mu}(0)=0.

(33)

The vanishing is exact at this order, not parametrically small in
\Delta/|\mathbf{g}|
: it is the standard
T=0
gapping of the longitudinal spin response on a fully polarized BCS sheet.

(iii) Inter-helicity sector: coherence factor at strong locking.
The inter-helicity pp contribution to
\chi_{\mu\mu}
at
T=0
is

\chi^{\rm inter}_{\mu\mu}(0)=\mu_{B}^{2}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\sum_{\lambda\neq\lambda^{\prime}}\frac{W^{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}_{pp,\mu}(\mathbf{k})}{E_{\mathbf{k}\lambda}+E_{-\mathbf{k}\lambda^{\prime}}}.

(34)

The pp matrix element factorizes into a spin-matrix-element factor and a Bogoliubov coherence factor,

W^{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}_{pp,\mu}(\mathbf{k})=\big[1-(\hat{n}_{\mathbf{k}}\cdot\hat{e}_{\mu})^{2}\big]\,\mathcal{C}^{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}(\mathbf{k}),

(35)

with the inter-helicity coherence factor

\mathcal{C}^{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}(\mathbf{k})\equiv\big|u_{\lambda}(\mathbf{k})v_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\mathbf{k})-u_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\mathbf{k})v_{\lambda}(\mathbf{k})\big|^{2}.

(36)

For Rashba SOC,
|\mathbf{g}_{-\mathbf{k}}|=|\mathbf{g}_{\mathbf{k}}|
implies
\xi_{\lambda}(-\mathbf{k})=\xi_{\lambda}(\mathbf{k})
, so
u_{\lambda},v_{\lambda}
are even in
\mathbf{k}
. We evaluate
\mathcal{C}^{+-}
for
\mathbf{k}
on the
+
FS, where
\xi_{+}=0
and
\xi_{-}=-2|\mathbf{g}_{\mathbf{k}}|
:

\displaystyle u_{+}(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=v_{+}(\mathbf{k})=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}},

(37)

\displaystyle u_{-}(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=O(\Delta/|\mathbf{g}_{\mathbf{k}}|),\;v_{-}(\mathbf{k})=1+O(\Delta^{2}/|\mathbf{g}_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}),

(38)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{+-}(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=\big|\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\cdot 1-O(\Delta/|\mathbf{g}_{\mathbf{k}}|)\cdot\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\big|^{2}=\tfrac{1}{2}+O(\Delta/|\mathbf{g}_{\mathbf{k}}|).

(39)

Particle-hole conjugation gives
\mathcal{C}^{-+}(\mathbf{k})=\tfrac{1}{2}+O(\Delta/|\mathbf{g}_{\mathbf{k}}|)
at the same
\mathbf{k}
, and the analogous result on the
-
FS by
+\leftrightarrow-
. The denominator on either FS is
E_{\mathbf{k}\lambda}+E_{-\mathbf{k}\lambda^{\prime}}=2|\mathbf{g}_{\mathbf{k}}|+O(\Delta^{2}/|\mathbf{g}_{\mathbf{k}}|)
.

The integrand of Eq. (
34
) is concentrated near each helicity FS over a
\xi
-range
\sim|\mathbf{g}_{\mathbf{k}}|
set by the variation of
u_{\lambda}v_{\lambda^{\prime}}
, not the much smaller scale
\Delta
. The sheet-resolved
O(\Delta/|\mathbf{g}|)
deviation in Eq. (
39
) does not survive the full
\xi
-integrated response: the exact closed-form result of Sec.
VII
shows that the net susceptibility correction starts only at quadratic order in
\Delta/|\mathbf{g}|
. Performing the
\xi
-integration and matching to the normal-state benchmark Eq. (
30
)—which gives
\chi^{ph-}_{\mu\mu}(T_{c})/\chi_{N}=1-\Pi_{\mu\mu}
from precisely the same
[1-(\hat{n}\cdot\hat{e})^{2}]
matrix-element factor and the same
2|\mathbf{g}_{\mathbf{k}}|
inter-band energy scale—yields

\chi^{\rm inter}_{\mu\mu}(0)=\chi_{N}\,(1-\Pi_{\mu\mu})+O(\Delta^{2}/g_{\min}^{2}).

(40)

Physically, SC pairing on each helicity sheet is irrelevant to the inter-sheet transition at energy scale
2|\mathbf{g}_{\mathbf{k}}|\gg\Delta
: the inter-helicity pp response in the SC state reduces to the normal-state inter-helicity Van Vleck response, up to corrections of order
(\Delta/g)^{2}
.

The same derivation extends to off-diagonal
\chi_{\mu\nu}
by replacing the diagonal spin-matrix-element factor
1-(\hat{n}_{\mathbf{k}}\cdot\hat{e}_{\mu})^{2}
in Eq. (
35
) with
\delta_{\mu\nu}-\hat{n}_{\mu}(\mathbf{k})\hat{n}_{\nu}(\mathbf{k})
, the only modification being which Cartesian components of
\hat{\sigma}
enter the matrix element. Combining Eqs. (
33
) and (
40
) (and its off-diagonal analogue),

\boxed{\;\chi_{\mu\nu}(0)=\chi_{N}[\delta_{\mu\nu}-\Pi_{\mu\nu}],\;\mathbf{H}=0,\;|\mathbf{g}_{\mathbf{k}}|/\Delta\to\infty,\;}

(41)

with corrections of order
\Delta^{2}/g_{\min}^{2}
from three sources of the same parametric size: (i) the inter-helicity denominator expansion; (ii) the inter-helicity coherence factor
\mathcal{C}^{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}=\tfrac{1}{2}+O(\Delta/g)
above, whose sheet-resolved linear deviation integrates to a net
O(\Delta^{2}/g^{2})
contribution to
\chi
, in agreement with the exact closed-form expansion
F_{s}(\lambda)=1-\ln(2\lambda)/\lambda^{2}+\cdots
of Sec.
VII
; and (iii)
\Delta_{\perp}
-mediated intra-/inter-helicity sector mixing, which enters at
O(\Delta/g)
in the helicity rotation but contributes only at
O(\Delta^{2}/g^{2})
to
\chi
.

Consequences: trace universality
\mathrm{Tr}\,\chi(0)=2\chi_{N}
for any FS/SOC; non-zero off-diagonal
\chi_{\mu\nu}(0)
for tilted SOC; eigenvectors of
\Pi
define spin-lock principal axes with
\pi_{1}+\pi_{2}+\pi_{3}=1
.

Replacing

VI
Parity-mixed pairing

VI.1
Helicity-basis structure

Because inversion symmetry is absent, parity is not a good quantum number and a generic superconducting state mixes even spin-singlet and odd spin-triplet components
18
;
32
;
16
;
10
;
38
. We work with the pairing matrix Eq. (
6
) introduced in Sec.
II
, repeated here for convenience:

\Delta(\mathbf{k})=i[\psi(\mathbf{k})\sigma_{0}+\mathbf{d}(\mathbf{k})\cdot\hat{\sigma}]\sigma_{y},

(44)

with
\psi(-\mathbf{k})=\psi(\mathbf{k})
and
\mathbf{d}(-\mathbf{k})=-\mathbf{d}(\mathbf{k})
. At
\mathbf{H}=0
, the natural spin-quantization axis is the helicity direction
\hat{g}_{\mathbf{k}}=\mathbf{g}_{\mathbf{k}}/|\mathbf{g}_{\mathbf{k}}|
. Decompose the triplet part into longitudinal and transverse pieces,

\mathbf{d}(\mathbf{k})=d_{\parallel}(\mathbf{k})\hat{g}_{\mathbf{k}}+\mathbf{d}_{\perp}(\mathbf{k}),\quad d_{\parallel}(\mathbf{k})=\mathbf{d}(\mathbf{k})\cdot\hat{g}_{\mathbf{k}}.

(45)

In the helicity basis of the normal-state Hamiltonian, the pairing matrix then takes the schematic form

\tilde{\Delta}(\mathbf{k})=\begin{pmatrix}\Delta_{+}(\mathbf{k})&\Delta_{\perp}(\mathbf{k})\\
-\Delta_{\perp}(\mathbf{k})&\Delta_{-}(\mathbf{k})\end{pmatrix},\quad\Delta_{\pm}(\mathbf{k})=\psi(\mathbf{k})\pm d_{\parallel}(\mathbf{k}),

(46)

where
\Delta_{\perp}(\mathbf{k})
is set by the transverse component
\mathbf{d}_{\perp}(\mathbf{k})
and mixes the two helicity sheets. The geometrically simplest regime is therefore the
helicity-diagonal
limit
\Delta_{\perp}=0
, realized exactly when
\mathbf{d}\parallel\mathbf{g}
and approximately whenever the antisymmetric SOC splitting is large compared with transverse inter-helicity pairing. In that limit, parity mixing changes the gap amplitudes on the
\pm
sheets but not the underlying locking texture
\hat{n}_{\mathbf{k}}=\hat{g}_{\mathbf{k}}
.

VI.2
T=0
consequences and finite-
T
recovery

We now compute
\chi_{\mu\nu}(T)
in the helicity-diagonal limit
\Delta_{\perp}=0
. Rotating to the helicity basis with unitary
R_{\mathbf{k}}
diagonalizing
H_{0}(\mathbf{k})
at
\mathbf{H}=0
, the spin operator becomes

R^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}}(\hat{e}_{\mu}\cdot\hat{\sigma})R_{\mathbf{k}}=(\hat{g}_{\mathbf{k}}\cdot\hat{e}_{\mu})\tau_{3}+\sqrt{1-(\hat{g}_{\mathbf{k}}\cdot\hat{e}_{\mu})^{2}}\,\tau_{\perp}(\mathbf{k}),

(47)

with
\tau_{3}
diagonal and
\tau_{\perp}
off-diagonal in helicity. In the helicity-diagonal limit, the BdG Hamiltonian decouples into two
2\times 2
blocks per helicity sheet, each with gap
|\Delta_{\lambda}(\mathbf{k})|
and standard BCS coherence factors
u_{\lambda},v_{\lambda}
. Substituting Eq. (
47
) into the Kubo expression for
\chi_{\mu\nu}
, the intra-helicity sector (driven by
\tau_{3}
) contributes a BCS-Yosida response on each sheet, while the inter-helicity sector (driven by
\tau_{\perp}
) reproduces the strong-SOC projector calculation of Sec.
V
. Adding the two,

\begin{split}\frac{\chi_{\mu\nu}(T)}{\chi_{N}}={}&\delta_{\mu\nu}-\Pi_{\mu\nu}\\
&+\sum_{\lambda=\pm}w_{\lambda}\left\langle Y_{\lambda}(\mathbf{k},T)\hat{g}_{\mu}(\mathbf{k})\hat{g}_{\nu}(\mathbf{k})\right\rangle_{\lambda}\\
&+O(\Delta^{2}/|\mathbf{g}|^{2}),\end{split}

(48)

with
\langle\cdots\rangle_{\lambda}
the FS average over the
\lambda
-helicity sheet,
w_{\lambda}=N_{\lambda}(0)/N(0)
, and

Y_{\lambda}(\mathbf{k},T)=-\!\int\!d\xi\,\frac{\partial f}{\partial E}\bigg|_{E_{\lambda}(\mathbf{k})}

(49)

the standard Yosida function built from
|\Delta_{\lambda}(\mathbf{k})|
, and
\Pi_{\mu\nu}\equiv\sum_{\lambda}w_{\lambda}\langle\hat{g}_{\mu}\hat{g}_{\nu}\rangle_{\lambda}
is the helicity-resolved form of Eq. (
10
). The dropped
O(\Delta^{2}/|\mathbf{g}|^{2})
correction originates from the strong-SOC expansion of the inter-helicity denominator; the same three sources of correction—denominator expansion,
\Delta_{\perp}
-mediated sector mixing, and coherence-factor corrections—are catalogued in the finite-field analysis of Sec.
VIII
.

For a fully gapped state,
Y_{\lambda}\to 0
as
T\to 0
, so

\boxed{\;\chi_{\mu\nu}(0)=\chi_{N}[\delta_{\mu\nu}-\Pi_{\mu\nu}].\;}

(50)

A fully gapped helicity-diagonal parity mixture therefore does
not
generate a new
T=0
simplex point: it lands on the same projector geometry as the corresponding pure locking texture. What changes is the finite-
T
recovery, because the two helicity sheets generally carry different gaps
\Delta_{\pm}
. Taking the trace of Eq. (
48
),

\frac{\mathrm{Tr}\,\chi(T)}{\chi_{N}}=2+\sum_{\lambda=\pm}w_{\lambda}\langle Y_{\lambda}(\mathbf{k},T)\rangle_{\lambda},

(51)

using
\mathrm{Tr}\,\Pi=1
. Unequal
\Delta_{\pm}
therefore produce a broadened or two-scale return of the Knight shift with temperature even when the
T=0
ellipsoid is unchanged. The forward implication is clean: a fully gapped helicity-diagonal parity-mixed state lands on a pure-locking simplex point at
T=0
but recovers toward
K_{N}
via two distinct Yosida scales set by
\Delta_{+}
and
\Delta_{-}
, weighted by the helicity-resolved DOS fractions
w_{\pm}
.

The inverse implication is weaker. A two-scale Yosida recovery onto a pure-locking
T=0
point is consistent with helicity-diagonal parity mixture but is also produced by several alternatives within the single-band unitary BdG framework: (a) multi-band orbital-diagonal pairing, where two FS pockets with different gaps generate an analogous
\sum_{i}w_{i}Y_{i}(T)\Pi^{(i)}
structure (Sec.
X
); (b) strongly anisotropic gaps over a single FS, which spread
Y(\mathbf{k},T)
over a continuum of scales and can mimic a two-component recovery; (c) nodal pairing, whose low-
T
power-law tail superposed on a bulk Yosida contribution from antinodal regions produces an effective second scale; and (d) pair-breaking impurity broadening, which fills sub-gap states and smears the Yosida onset.

A two-scale recovery is therefore necessary, but not sufficient, to identify helicity-diagonal parity mixture from Knight-shift data alone. Positive identification additionally requires (i) independent constraints on the band structure that rule out alternative (a), (ii) a gap-anisotropy probe (specific heat, thermal conductivity, penetration depth) that constrains alternatives (b)–(c), and (iii) a sample-quality assessment for (d). Within those caveats, the ellipsoid construction upgrades from a
T=0
vertex/edge classifier to a finite-
T
consistency check on parity admixture, at no additional experimental cost beyond the temperature sweep already required to extract the
T\to 0
ellipsoid.

Once
\Delta_{\perp}
is sizable,
or
one helicity sheet is weakly paired or gapless,
or
inter-band pairing dominates, Eq. (
50
) should be treated as a baseline rather than a theorem. At finite Zeeman field, the helicity basis rotates with
\mathbf{H}
via Eq. (
9
) and the helicity-diagonal condition
\Delta_{\perp}=0
becomes field-dependent; Eq. (
50
) then holds with
\Pi
replaced by the field-dependent projector
\Pi(\mathbf{H})
of Eq. (
62
) (Sec.
VIII
), provided the strong-locking condition Eq. (
60
) is satisfied.

Controlled departure I: intermediate SOC

Up to this point, generality in FS shape was obtained by taking the strict strong-locking limit. We now relax that assumption in the opposite direction: keep
\mathbf{H}=0
, keep the pairing to the analytically tractable
s
-wave case, and allow arbitrary SOC magnitude. Throughout this section we specialize to pure
s
-wave pairing,
\psi(\mathbf{k})=\Delta
and
\mathbf{d}(\mathbf{k})=0
, and denote the singlet amplitude simply by
\Delta
.

VII.1
Reduction to a single integral

From Eq. (23c) of Ref.
29
,

\displaystyle\chi_{\mu\nu}(0)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{\chi_{N}}{2}\big\langle[\delta_{\mu\nu}-\hat{n}_{\mu}\hat{n}_{\nu}]\tilde{J}(\lambda_{\mathbf{k}})\big\rangle_{\rm FS},

(52)

\displaystyle\tilde{J}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!d\xi\,\mathcal{F}(\xi,\lambda\Delta,\Delta),

(53)

with
\mathcal{F}(\xi,\alpha,\Delta)=[E_{+}E_{-}-\xi_{+}\xi_{-}-\Delta^{2}]/[E_{+}E_{-}(E_{+}+E_{-})]
,
\xi_{\pm}=\xi\pm\alpha
,
E_{\pm}=\sqrt{\xi_{\pm}^{2}+\Delta^{2}}
, and
\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}=|\mathbf{g}_{\mathbf{k}}|/\Delta
.

VII.2
Closed-form evaluation

An ellipse parametrization
u=\xi/\Delta
,
a=(f+g)/2
with
f=\sqrt{(u+\lambda)^{2}+1}
,
g=\sqrt{(u-\lambda)^{2}+1}
gives the identity
fg-u^{2}+\lambda^{2}-1=2\lambda^{2}/(a^{2}-\lambda^{2})
. Evaluation yields

\boxed{\;F_{s}(\lambda)\equiv\tfrac{1}{2}\tilde{J}(\lambda)=1-\frac{\sinh^{-1}\lambda}{\lambda\sqrt{1+\lambda^{2}}},\;}

(54)

and

\boxed{\;\frac{\chi_{\mu\nu}(0)}{\chi_{N}}=\langle F_{s}(\lambda_{\mathbf{k}})[\delta_{\mu\nu}-\hat{n}_{\mu}\hat{n}_{\nu}]\rangle_{\rm FS}.\;}

(55)

VII.3
Geometric interpretation and SOC spectroscopy

Equation (
55
) shows that intermediate SOC does not replace the strong-locking tensor structure; it dresses each FS point by the scalar factor
F_{s}(\lambda_{\mathbf{k}})\in[0,1]
. Consequently,

\mathrm{Tr}\,\chi(0)/\chi_{N}=2\langle F_{s}(\lambda_{\mathbf{k}})\rangle_{\rm FS}\leq 2,

strict for finite SOC. For
uniform
SOC magnitude over the FS, the relation is invertible:
\lambda=F_{s}^{-1}(\mathrm{Tr}\,\chi(0)/2\chi_{N})
, yielding a pair-breaking spectroscopy of
|\mathbf{g}|/\Delta
from three-axis Knight shift.

For
non-uniform
SOC magnitude, which is the generic case, the inversion returns an effective parameter

\lambda_{\rm eff}\equiv F_{s}^{-1}\big(\langle F_{s}(\lambda_{\mathbf{k}})\rangle_{\rm FS}\big),

(56)

which is a particular FS-weighted average of
|\mathbf{g}_{\mathbf{k}}|/\Delta
rather than its value at any individual FS point. For mild FS variation, expanding around the mean
\bar{\lambda}\equiv\langle\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}\rangle_{\rm FS}
gives

\langle F_{s}(\lambda_{\mathbf{k}})\rangle_{\rm FS}\approx F_{s}(\bar{\lambda})+\tfrac{1}{2}F_{s}^{\prime\prime}(\bar{\lambda})\,\mathrm{Var}(\lambda_{\mathbf{k}})+\cdots,

(57)

so
\lambda_{\rm eff}
differs from
\bar{\lambda}
by a correction set by
F_{s}^{\prime\prime}(\bar{\lambda})
times the FS variance of
\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}
. Direct evaluation of
F_{s}^{\prime\prime}
shows that
F_{s}
has a single inflection point at
\lambda^{\ast}\approx 0.669
: it is
convex
(
F_{s}^{\prime\prime}>0
) for
\lambda\lesssim\lambda^{\ast}
and
concave
(
F_{s}^{\prime\prime}<0
) for
\lambda\gtrsim\lambda^{\ast}
. By Jensen’s inequality applied to the monotonically increasing
F_{s}
,
\lambda_{\rm eff}\geq\bar{\lambda}
at weak SOC (
\bar{\lambda}\lesssim\lambda^{\ast}
) and
\lambda_{\rm eff}\leq\bar{\lambda}
at moderate-to-strong SOC (
\bar{\lambda}\gtrsim\lambda^{\ast}
). The spectroscopic readout therefore requires either an independent estimate of FS anisotropy in
|\mathbf{g}_{\mathbf{k}}|
(e.g., from DFT+SOC) or a deliberate restriction to systems where the SOC is dominated by a single, near-isotropic FS sheet.

Operationally, this section supplies the radial contraction away from the strong-locking simplex used later in Sec.
XI
: for uniform SOC magnitude,
\Pi^{\rm eff}=F_{s}(\lambda)\Pi
, so every strong-locking point moves along a straight line toward the origin as SOC weakens; for non-uniform SOC, the more general
\Pi^{\rm eff}_{\mu\nu}=\langle F_{s}(\lambda_{\mathbf{k}})\hat{n}_{\mu}\hat{n}_{\nu}\rangle_{\rm FS}
of Eq. (
91
) applies and the contraction is direction-dependent. An independent numerical overlay against the published BdG curves of Ref.
29
, together with a brief heuristic note on anisotropic-gap unitary triplets, is deferred to Appendix
A
. The overlay confirms Eq. (
54
) at the
10^{-3}
level without interrupting the main derivation.

F_{s}(0)=0
,
F_{s}(\infty)=1
, monotonic. Small-
\lambda

F_{s}=\tfrac{2}{3}\lambda^{2}-\tfrac{8}{15}\lambda^{4}+O(\lambda^{6})
. Large-

VIII
Controlled departure II: finite Zeeman field

Section
VII
relaxed the SOC-magnitude assumption while keeping
\mathbf{H}=0
. We now take the complementary step: restore finite Zeeman field while returning to the strong-locking regime. In this section we show that Eq. (
43
) survives at finite
\mathbf{H}
in the strong-locking regime
|\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{k}}|\gg\Delta
, with a field-dependent projection tensor
\Pi(\mathbf{H})
; we quantify the leading corrections and delineate the regimes in which a closed form is inaccessible. Throughout this section we use the dimensionless ratios
\lambda\equiv|\mathbf{g}_{\mathbf{k}}|/\Delta
and
h\equiv\mu_{B}|\mathbf{H}|/\Delta
.

VIII.1
Structure of the finite-field problem

With both fields present, the normal-state quasiparticle spin is locked along the composite vector

\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{k}}=\mu_{B}\mathbf{H}+\mathbf{g}_{\mathbf{k}},\qquad\hat{n}_{\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{H})=\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{k}}/|\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{k}}|.

(58)

For Rashba-type SOC with
\mathbf{g}_{-\mathbf{k}}=-\mathbf{g}_{\mathbf{k}}
,

|\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}-|\mathbf{b}_{-\mathbf{k}}|^{2}=4\mu_{B}\mathbf{H}\cdot\mathbf{g}_{\mathbf{k}},

(59)

so any Zeeman component parallel to
\mathbf{g}_{\mathbf{k}}
breaks the Kramers symmetry of Cooper pairs. A direct characteristic-polynomial check shows that the simple two-sector factorization of the
4\times 4
BdG matrix occurs in the
collinear
case
\mathbf{H}\parallel\mathbf{g}_{\mathbf{k}}
; a noncollinear configuration retains the generic quartic structure. Accordingly, no closed-form expression for
\chi_{\mu\nu}(0,\mathbf{H})
is available at generic
(\mathbf{H},\mathbf{g}_{\mathbf{k}},\Delta)
. What survives analytically is the strong-locking regime.

VIII.2
Strong-locking identity at finite
\mathbf{H}

When the minimum locking magnitude over the FS satisfies

b_{\min}(\mathbf{H})\equiv\min_{\mathbf{k}\in\rm FS}|\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{k}}|\;\gg\;\Delta,

(60)

the BdG problem admits a controlled expansion in
\Delta/b
. We sketch the error estimate because it controls the validity range of every finite-field statement below.

Rotate at each
\mathbf{k}
to the helicity basis of
H_{0}(\mathbf{k})
, with spin quantization axis
\hat{n}_{\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{H})
. To zeroth order in
\Delta/b_{\mathbf{k}}
, BdG decouples into two intra-helicity
2\times 2
blocks with gaps
|\Delta_{\lambda}(\mathbf{k})|
and standard BCS coherence factors
u_{\lambda},v_{\lambda}
. The
T=0
spin susceptibility decomposes accordingly into intra-helicity and inter-helicity contributions.

The intra-helicity contribution is
\chi_{N}\Pi_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf{H})[1-Y_{\lambda}(0)]=0
for a fully gapped state: it vanishes identically as the longitudinal response of a fully polarized, fully gapped sheet, exactly as in the standard Yosida argument. This is
not
a smallness-in-
\Delta/b
statement; it is the usual
T=0
gapping of longitudinal spin response on a single helicity sheet.

The inter-helicity contribution reproduces the normal-state matrix-element structure of Eq. (
29
) with the zero-field locking vector replaced by
\hat{n}_{\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{H})
. Its denominator is
E_{+}+E_{-}=2b_{\mathbf{k}}+O(\Delta^{2}/b_{\mathbf{k}})
, where the subleading piece arises from expanding
E_{\lambda}=\sqrt{\xi_{\lambda}^{2}+|\Delta_{\lambda}|^{2}}
around
|\xi_{\lambda}|=b_{\mathbf{k}}
. The same three-step argument as Sec.
V
(helicity block-diagonalization, intra-helicity Yosida cancellation, inter-helicity coherence-factor evaluation) then yields

\boxed{\;\chi_{\mu\nu}(0,\mathbf{H})=\chi_{N}\big[\delta_{\mu\nu}-\Pi_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf{H})\big]+O\!\big(\Delta^{2}/b_{\min}^{2}\big),\;}

(61)

with the field-dependent projection tensor

\Pi_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf{H})\equiv\big\langle\hat{n}_{\mu}(\mathbf{k};\mathbf{H})\,\hat{n}_{\nu}(\mathbf{k};\mathbf{H})\big\rangle_{\rm FS}.

(62)

The residual
O(\Delta^{2}/b_{\min}^{2})
combines three sources of the same parametric size: (i) the denominator correction above; (ii) mixing of intra- and inter-helicity sectors by off-diagonal gap components
\Delta_{\perp}
at
O(\Delta/b)
, whose contribution to
\chi
is
O(\Delta^{2}/b^{2})
; and (iii) deviations of the coherence factors from the strong-locking limit, again at
O(\Delta^{2}/b^{2})
. For typical NMR-field Rashba superconductors with
gk_{F}\sim 10\,\Delta
, the correction is
\lesssim 1\%
and well below Knight-shift experimental resolution.

VIII.3
Worked limits

(a) Weak Zeeman on top of strong SOC.
For
\mu_{B}H\ll|\mathbf{g}_{\mathbf{k}}|
, i.e.,
h\ll\lambda
, expansion of
\hat{n}_{\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{H})
in powers of
h/\lambda
gives odd powers of
\mathbf{H}
that vanish on FS averaging by the parity
\hat{g}_{-\mathbf{k}}=-\hat{g}_{\mathbf{k}}
of Rashba-type SOC. The leading correction is therefore quadratic,

\Pi_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf{H})=\Pi_{\mu\nu}(0)+O(h^{2}/\lambda^{2}),

(63)

and the zero-field ellipsoid is
stable
against small applied fields. Practically, the zero-field diagnostic of Sec.
XI
applies without modification at measurement fields
H\ll gk_{F}/\mu_{B}
.

(b) Zeeman-dominated locking.
For
\mu_{B}H\gg|\mathbf{g}_{\mathbf{k}}|
on the FS,
\hat{n}_{\mathbf{k}}\to\hat{\mathbf{H}}
uniformly,
\Pi(\mathbf{H})\to\hat{\mathbf{H}}\otimes\hat{\mathbf{H}}
, and

\chi_{\mu\nu}(0,\mathbf{H})\to\chi_{N}[\delta_{\mu\nu}-\hat{H}_{\mu}\hat{H}_{\nu}],

(64)

an oblate-axial ellipsoid with symmetry axis along
\hat{\mathbf{H}}
: the longitudinal direction collapses while the two transverse directions respond at
\chi_{N}
. Physically, this is the field-aligned analogue of an OSP state with
\mathbf{d}\parallel\hat{\mathbf{H}}
.

(c) Vanishing locking (breakdown).
If
|\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{k}_{0}}|\to 0
at some
\mathbf{k}_{0}
on the FS, the condition Eq. (
60
) is violated and Eq. (
61
) fails locally. For 3D Rashba with
\mathbf{H}\parallel\hat{z}
and spherical FS, this occurs at the critical field

\mu_{B}H^{\ast}=gk_{F},

(65)

where SOC and Zeeman contributions cancel at a pole of the FS. At
H^{\ast}
a Bogoliubov Fermi surface forms
29
, and the full numerical BdG solution is required. The kinks reported in Fig. 5(a) of Ref.
29
locate this breakdown and its successors.

VIII.4
Regime diagram and limitations

The strong-locking regime Eq. (
60
) for Rashba SOC with
\mathbf{H}\parallel\hat{z}
and spherical FS becomes

|gk_{F}-\mu_{B}H|\gg\Delta,

(66)

valid both at low field (
\mu_{B}H\ll gk_{F}
, covered by Eq. (
63
)) and high field (
\mu_{B}H\gg gk_{F}
, Eq. (
64
)), but not in the crossover window around
H^{\ast}
. Outside the strong-locking regime, no closed form is known. We verified numerically that naive substitutions such as
\Delta\to\sqrt{\Delta^{2}-(\mu_{B}H)^{2}}
in Eq. (
55
) do not reproduce the self-consistent BdG result, departing by
O(1)
in
\chi/\chi_{N}
across the bulk of the
(g,H)
phase diagram; the problem is structural (the BdG fails to block-diagonalize) rather than a matter of substitution. Protocol C below is built precisely around this regime separation: simple ellipsoid deformation away from the crossover, full numerics near
H^{\ast}
.

For illustration, at
gk_{F}=10\Delta
and
\mu_{B}H=0.1\,gk_{F}
,
b_{\min}/\Delta\approx 9
and the leading correction to Eq. (
61
) is
\sim(\Delta/b_{\min})^{2}\approx 1\%
, comfortably below current Knight-shift resolution.

IX
Dynamic counterpart: spin FGT sum rule

The preceding sections fixed the static geometry of
\chi_{\mu\nu}(0)
. We now turn to its dynamical counterpart: whatever Knight-shift deficit is produced by pair formation must reappear as additional spectral weight at finite frequency in
\mathrm{Im}\,\chi_{\mu\mu}(\omega)
. This section supplies the only additional input needed for the
1/T_{1}
cross-check developed later: a budget relation between the static ellipsoid and dynamical spectral-weight transfer.

IX.1
Spectral sum rule

The uniform spin susceptibility satisfies Kramers–Kronig at
\omega=0
in both the normal and superconducting states:

\chi_{\mu\mu}(T)=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\frac{d\omega}{\omega}\,\mathrm{Im}\,\chi_{\mu\mu}(\omega,T),

(67)

with
\mathrm{Im}\,\chi_{\mu\mu}(\omega)
odd in
\omega
. Subtracting the two,

\boxed{\;\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\frac{d\omega}{\omega}\,\big[\mathrm{Im}\,\chi_{\mu\mu}^{N}(\omega)-\mathrm{Im}\,\chi_{\mu\mu}^{SC}(\omega)\big]=\chi_{N}-\chi_{\mu\mu}(0)=\chi_{N}\Pi_{\mu\mu},\;}

(68)

where
\chi_{\mu\mu}(0)=\chi_{N}(1-\Pi_{\mu\mu})
is the strong-locking identity Eq. (
43
). Equation (
68
) is the spin analogue of the Ferrell–Glover–Tinkham optical sum rule:
the weighted spectral weight removed from the superconducting state at each frequency equals the Knight-shift deficit
, itself a geometric quantity determined by the FS average
\Pi_{\mu\mu}=\langle(\hat{n}\cdot\hat{e}_{\mu})^{2}\rangle_{\rm FS}
.

IX.2
Origin in the generalized sum rule

Equation (
68
) traces to Eq. (
21
) through the spectral representation of
\chi_{\mu\mu}
. In the SC state at
T=0
, only the pp channels contribute:

\begin{split}\mathrm{Im}\,\chi_{\mu\mu}^{SC}(\omega>0)={}&\pi\mu_{B}^{2}\sum_{\mathbf{k},s_{1}s_{2}}W^{s_{1}s_{2}}_{pp,\mu}(\mathbf{k})\\
&\times\delta\!\big(\omega-E_{\mathbf{k}s_{1}}-E_{-\mathbf{k}s_{2}}\big).\end{split}

(69)

In the normal state at
T_{c}^{+}
, only the ph channel contributes, with spectral support at the interband splitting
2|\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{k}}|
(Van Vleck) and a Landau damping contribution at
\omega\to 0
(Pauli). Equation (
21
) fixes the
total
sum-rule weight,

\sum_{s_{1}s_{2}}\big[W^{s_{1}s_{2}}_{ph,\mu}(\mathbf{k})+W^{s_{1}s_{2}}_{pp,\mu}(\mathbf{k})\big]=2,

(70)

per
\mathbf{k}
, so the
total
oscillator strength, suitably weighted, is conserved across the superconducting transition. Spectral weight originally in the Pauli term at
\omega=0
in the normal state is partially transferred into a continuum above
\omega=2\Delta_{\min}
in the SC state, with the missing static response set by
\Pi_{\mu\mu}
.

IX.3
Spectral-weight transfer: rigorous content

Equation (
68
) has a direct spectral interpretation. In the normal state,
\mathrm{Im}\,\chi_{\mu\mu}^{N}(\omega)/\omega
concentrates at
\omega\to 0
(the Pauli-Landau piece) with a Van Vleck shoulder at the interband threshold
\omega\sim 2|\mathbf{g}_{\mathbf{k}}|
. In the superconducting state, the
\omega\to 0
weight is suppressed by the factor
1-\Pi_{\mu\mu}
, and the displaced weight reappears above
2\Delta_{\min}
as pair-breaking absorption. The Van Vleck contribution is unaffected at leading order in
\Delta/|\mathbf{g}_{\mathbf{k}}|
. The total
\mathrm{Im}\,\chi/\omega

, while the Van Vleck shoulder at

IX.4
Vanishing-projection theorem for
1/T_{1}

The spin-lattice relaxation rate probes a different slice of
\mathrm{Im}\,\chi
from the sum rule
37
;
41
;
44
,

\frac{1}{T_{1}T}=\gamma_{n}^{2}k_{B}\sum_{\mathbf{q}}|A_{\rm hf}(\mathbf{q})|^{2}\lim_{\omega\to 0}\frac{\mathrm{Im}\,\chi_{+-}(\mathbf{q},\omega)}{\omega},

(71)

integrated over
\mathbf{q}
with the hyperfine form factor and restricted to
\omega\to 0
. Equation (
68
), in contrast, integrates over all
\omega
at
\mathbf{q}=0
. The two quantities probe orthogonal slices of
\mathrm{Im}\,\chi(\mathbf{q},\omega)
, so no exact quantitative relation between them follows. What does follow is a sharp
vanishing-projection theorem
that requires no auxiliary detailed-balance assumption.

Statement.
Decompose Eq. (
71
) into
\mathbf{q}=0
and finite-
\mathbf{q}
contributions,

\frac{1}{T_{1}T}=\frac{1}{T_{1}T}\bigg|_{\mathbf{q}=0}+\frac{1}{T_{1}T}\bigg|_{\mathbf{q}\neq 0},

(72)

with the first term collecting the
\mathbf{q}=0
piece of the hyperfine-weighted integral. Then

\boxed{\;\begin{aligned} &\Pi_{\alpha\alpha}=0\;\Longrightarrow\\
&\quad(1/T_{1}T)_{\mathbf{q}=0,\alpha}^{N}=(1/T_{1}T)_{\mathbf{q}=0,\alpha}^{\rm SC}=0,\;T\leq T_{c}.\end{aligned}\;}

(73)

Proof.
Because
\Pi_{\alpha\alpha}=\langle\hat{n}_{\alpha}^{2}\rangle_{\rm FS}
and
\hat{n}_{\alpha}^{2}\geq 0
pointwise, the condition
\Pi_{\alpha\alpha}=0
forces
\hat{n}_{\alpha}(\mathbf{k})=0
on every FS point carrying spectral weight. Equation (
47
) (equivalently the diagonal part of Eq. (
28

\sigma_{\alpha}
has no diagonal helicity matrix element: the uniform
\mathbf{q}=0

\alpha
is purely inter-helicity. In the strong-locking regime the minimum inter-helicity threshold is
2b_{\min}>0
, so
\mathrm{Im}\,\chi_{\alpha\alpha}(\mathbf{q}=0,\omega)
has no support in the
\omega\to 0
limit in either the normal or superconducting state. Hence the
\mathbf{q}=0
contribution to
1/T_{1}T
vanishes identically on both sides of the transition.
\square

73

\alpha

\Pi_{\alpha\alpha}=0

IX.5
Parametric estimate at
\Pi_{\alpha\alpha}>0

When
\Pi_{\alpha\alpha}>0
, the vanishing-projection theorem of Sec.

gives no constraint and a quantitative comparison between the
1/T_{1}

1/T_{1}

\delta_{\alpha}\equiv\int_{0}^{T_{c}}\!\frac{dT}{T}\Big[1/T_{1}^{N}-1/T_{1}^{SC}\Big]_{\mathbf{H}\parallel\hat{e}_{\alpha}}

(74)

is a temperature integral of rate differences, while the spin-FGT spectral weight
\chi_{N}\Pi_{\alpha\alpha}
is a static
\mathbf{q}=0

to a temperature-broadened integral of
\mathrm{Im}\,\chi(\mathbf{q},\omega)/\omega
, and (ii) the assumption that the hyperfine-weighted spin-fluctuation spectrum is dominated by
\mathbf{q}\approx 0
, valid when
|A_{\rm hf}(\mathbf{q})|^{2}
is slowly varying and magnetic correlations are not peaked at finite
\mathbf{q}
.

Under those conditions, dimensional analysis gives the parametric estimate

\delta_{\alpha}\sim\gamma_{n}^{2}k_{B}\,\langle|A_{\rm hf}|^{2}\rangle\,\chi_{N}\Pi_{\alpha\alpha},\qquad\mathbf{q}\approx 0\text{ dominant},

(75)

with
\langle|A_{\rm hf}|^{2}\rangle
the spectrum-weighted mean and a proportionality constant of order unity that depends on the SC-state spectral shape and on the temperature window of the integral. Equation (
75
) is therefore a plausibility estimate, not a sharp inequality. We do not use it as a quantitative diagnostic; the K
2
Cr
3
As

The contrapositive of Eq. (

\mathbf{q}

(i)

Vanishing projection.
When
\Pi_{\alpha\alpha}=0

(ii)

In the intermediate-SOC regime, the parametric estimate Eq. (

X
Structural extension: decoupled multiband baseline

The last extension changes not the control parameters but the Hilbert-space structure. Because the fully general multi-orbital problem is broader than the present paper, we isolate the piece that the measurement analysis can use directly: the decoupled-pocket baseline in which each FS sheet contributes additively and deviations can be read as signatures of inter-band structure.

X.1
Disjoint-pocket form

For
N_{b}
disjoint FS pockets, additivity of the Kubo expression gives

\chi_{\mu\nu}(0)=\sum_{i}\chi_{N}^{(i)}[\delta_{\mu\nu}-\Pi^{(i)}_{\mu\nu}],

(76)

with
\chi_{N}=\sum_{i}\chi_{N}^{(i)}=\mathrm{Tr}\,\chi(T_{c})/3
and

\boxed{\;\mathrm{Tr}\,\chi(0)\leq 2\sum_{i}\chi_{N}^{(i)}=\tfrac{2}{3}\mathrm{Tr}\,\chi(T_{c}).\;}

(77)

X.2
Scope: inter-band pairing as deviation from baseline

Equation (
76
) is exact for orbital-diagonal pairing and is a baseline otherwise: deviations diagnose the inter-band structure. The trace inequality Eq. (
77
), in contrast, follows directly from the pointwise sum-rule bound
\sum_{s_{1}s_{2}}W^{s_{1}s_{2}}_{pp,O}\leq\mathrm{Tr}_{s}(O^{2})
and the isotropic-FS geometric factor
\langle\cos^{2}\theta\rangle=1/3
; it therefore survives inter-band pairing on a 3D isotropic FS. The Bogoliubov sum rule Eq. (
21
) itself is independent of the orbital-diagonal assumption. A dedicated multi-orbital derivation is required, however, before importing the anisotropic simplex interpretation of Sec.
XI
, or the normalized site/pocket bounds deployed in Sec.
XIII
, to genuine inter-band pairing.

X.3
Structural prediction for A
2
Cr
3
As
3

Density-functional calculations
21
;
43
give three FS bands in the A
2

3
As
3
family (A=Na,K,Rb,Cs)
8
;
39
;
40
;
26
;
49
: quasi-1D bands
\alpha,\beta
and a 3D band
\gamma
, with DOS fractions
w_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
satisfying
\sum w_{i}=1
. Under a
P\bar{6}m2

\hat{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}\approx\mathrm{sgn}(k_{z})\hat{c}
, giving
\Pi^{(\alpha,\beta)}\approx\mathrm{diag}(0,0,1)
, while the 3D
\gamma

XII
Quick-start guide for experimentalists

This section is a self-contained on-ramp to the measurement program of Sec.
XIII
. We first frame the physical question and the geometric object that the framework asks experiments to determine; then condense the apparatus into a decision tree and an explicit pairing-diagnostic workflow.

XII.1
The physical question

The Knight shift measures the residual spin response of conduction electrons through a small frequency shift of nuclei embedded in the metal. In a textbook BCS singlet superconductor, this response vanishes at
T=0
: all electrons are paired with antiparallel spins, and an applied field finds nothing to polarize. Real superconductors deviate from this picture in a controlled way. In noncentrosymmetric materials, the Knight shift typically remains
finite
at
T=0
along some directions, even though the electrons are still paired. The pairing has not failed; rather, the electron spins are constrained by the crystal to lie along directions set by the antisymmetric spin-orbit coupling.

The microscopic origin is the spin-locking direction
\hat{n}_{\mathbf{k}}
, the unit vector along which the normal-state quasiparticle spin at each Fermi-surface point is aligned. In the strong-locking limit, an applied field along
\hat{n}_{\mathbf{k}}
cannot reorient the spin and produces no response, while a field perpendicular to
\hat{n}_{\mathbf{k}}
allows a residual transverse response that survives pairing. The Knight-shift tensor measured along three orthogonal directions therefore reads out the Fermi-surface average of
\hat{n}_{\mathbf{k}}
in the form of a
3\times 3
projector,

\Pi_{\mu\nu}=\langle\hat{n}_{\mu}\hat{n}_{\nu}\rangle_{\rm FS},

which is the central geometric object of the paper.

Pairing information and the projector.

A reader new to the framework may reasonably ask: how can a Fermi-surface average of a unit vector contain information about the pairing state at all? The mental picture is the following. A Cooper pair has its own intrinsic spin structure—singlet pairs bind two electrons with antiparallel spins along every direction; opposite-spin-pairing triplets with
\mathbf{d}\parallel\hat{z}
bind them antiparallel only along
\hat{z}
and parallel along
\hat{x},\hat{y}
; equal-spin triplets bind them parallel along
\hat{z}
. Apply a small Zeeman field along
\hat{e}_{\mu}
: a Cooper pair contributes nothing to the
T=0
susceptibility along the axis where its two electrons are already antiparallel, because the field finds no transverse spin to polarize; it contributes fully along an axis where the two electrons are parallel or uncorrelated. The pair therefore “uses up” some directions of the spin response and leaves others alone. The locking direction
\hat{n}_{\mathbf{k}}
is the axis at FS point
\mathbf{k}
along which the Cooper pair binds its two electrons antiparallel: set by the SOC vector
\hat{g}_{\mathbf{k}}
for a singlet in a strong-SOC noncentrosymmetric system, set by the triplet
\hat{d}
-vector at zero SOC, set by a combination of both more generally. Averaging
\hat{n}_{\mu}\hat{n}_{\nu}
over the FS gives the projector
\Pi_{\mu\nu}
: the fraction of the FS along which pairing has consumed the spin response in direction
\mu\nu
. The Knight-shift deficit
\chi_{N}-\chi_{\mu\nu}(0)=\chi_{N}\Pi_{\mu\nu}
is therefore a direct, direction-resolved measurement of where pairing has done its work in spin space. The pairing information enters through which microscopic mechanism (SOC,
\hat{d}
-vector, or their combination) sets
\hat{n}_{\mathbf{k}}
, and the FS geometry then converts that microscopic input into the macroscopic Knight-shift tensor.

The eigenvalues of
\Pi
sum to one and lie on a two-dimensional simplex (Fig.
3
). Each canonical region of that simplex corresponds to a recognizable physical limit:

•

Barycenter

(\tfrac{1}{3},\tfrac{1}{3},\tfrac{1}{3})
: isotropic distribution of
\hat{n}_{\mathbf{k}}
, e.g. singlet pairing with strong 3D SOC, or a triplet with isotropic
\hat{d}
-vector.

•

Edge midpoints

(\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2},0)
: planar distribution, e.g. 2D Rashba SOC or equal-spin triplet (
\mathbf{d}
in a plane).

•

Vertices

(0,0,1)
: rigid alignment along one crystallographic axis, e.g. opposite-spin triplet with
\mathbf{d}\parallel\hat{z}
or quasi-1D SOC.

•

Interior points
: tilted or mixed locking textures.

Reading the eigenvalues off the experimental Knight-shift tensor places the material at a specific simplex point, and the location is the diagnostic.

The reason this matters beyond classification is that the simplex location constrains the pairing mechanism. A common
\hat{c}
-axis spin-locking direction across all bands of a multiband material strongly suggests a single coherent triplet
\mathbf{d}
-vector aligned with
\hat{c}
, even when band-structure calculations would predict more isotropic textures from SOC alone. That mismatch—between what the bands “should” do and what the Knight-shift tensor forces on us—is itself a measurement of the pairing physics. The K
2
Cr
3
As
3
analysis in Sec.
XIV
is a worked example.

A second handle becomes available when one combines the Knight shift with the spin-lattice relaxation rate
1/T_{1}
, which probes the dynamical part of the same susceptibility. If the static spin-locking distribution forbids any spin response along a given axis at
\mathbf{q}=0
(the vanishing-projection condition
\Pi_{\alpha\alpha}=0
), then any change in
1/T_{1}
along that axis below
T_{c}
must come from finite-momentum spin fluctuations. This converts
1/T_{1}
data into a rigorous finite-
\mathbf{q}
fingerprint and supplies an independent constraint on the spin-fluctuation structure that mediates pairing.

The framework therefore organizes the experimental program around three independent constraints on the pairing state: the static spin-locking distribution from
T\to 0
Knight shift, the parity content from finite-
T
recovery toward
K_{N}
, and the finite-
\mathbf{q}
spin-fluctuation structure from
1/T_{1}
.

XII.4
Internal consistency check on the orbital-shift subtraction

The measured Knight shift contains an orbital (Van Vleck) contribution that must be subtracted before applying any of the protocols above:

K^{\rm meas}_{\mu\nu}(T)=K^{\rm orb}_{\mu\nu}+K^{\rm spin}_{\mu\nu}(T).

(92)

K^{\rm orb}_{\mu\nu}
is temperature-independent and pairing-independent at leading order in
\Delta/W
with
W
the bandwidth, since it arises from inter-band Van Vleck matrix elements gapped by
W\gg\Delta
. The standard Clogston–Jaccarino extraction
12
;
37
;
41
fits
K^{\rm meas}(T)
against the bulk susceptibility
\chi^{\rm spin}(T)
above
T_{c}
and reads
K^{\rm orb}
off the intercept. This extraction can fail when impurity Curie tails contaminate
\chi^{\rm spin}(T)
, when multiple bands contribute with different hyperfine couplings, or when the bulk susceptibility carries an anisotropy not matched by
K^{\rm spin}
. The sum rules provide three checks that any candidate
K^{\rm orb}
extraction must pass.

(i) Trace bound as necessary condition.
Equation (
27
) applies to
K^{\rm spin}
, not
K^{\rm meas}
. After subtracting candidate
K^{\rm orb}_{\mu\mu}
on a 3D isotropic FS,

\sum_{\mu}\big[K^{\rm meas}_{\mu\mu}(0)-K^{\rm orb}_{\mu\mu}\big]\leq\tfrac{2}{3}\sum_{\mu}\big[K^{\rm meas}_{\mu\mu}(T_{c}^{+})-K^{\rm orb}_{\mu\mu}\big].

(93)

Violation indicates an underestimated
K^{\rm orb}
. Saturation indicates an acceptable extraction in the strong-locking regime.

(ii) Self-consistent
K^{\rm spin}_{N}
from temperature differences.
Because
K^{\rm orb}
is temperature-independent, the temperature change
K^{\rm meas}_{i}(T_{c}^{+})-K^{\rm meas}_{i}(0)=K^{\rm spin}_{i}(T_{c}^{+})-K^{\rm spin}_{i}(0)
cancels
K^{\rm orb}
identically. In the strong-locking regime, the spin part satisfies
\sum_{i}K^{\rm spin}_{i}(0)=2K^{\rm spin}_{N}
from the trace identity Eq. (
86
), and
\sum_{i}K^{\rm spin}_{i}(T_{c}^{+})=3K^{\rm spin}_{N}
from the normal-state benchmark of Sec.
V
. Subtracting yields

K^{\rm spin}_{N}=\sum_{i}\big[K^{\rm meas}_{i}(T_{c}^{+})-K^{\rm meas}_{i}(0)\big],

(94)

a direct determination of
K^{\rm spin}_{N}
from the SC transition itself, independent of
K^{\rm orb}
or of any normal-state Korringa fit. Once
K^{\rm spin}_{N}
is fixed by Eq. (
94
), each
K^{\rm orb}_{i}
follows by subtraction
K^{\rm orb}_{i}=K^{\rm meas}_{i}(T_{c}^{+})-K^{\rm spin}_{i}(T_{c}^{+})
, providing an internal consistency check on the Clogston–Jaccarino extraction.

(iii) Simplex constraint on the principal projections.
For any acceptable
K^{\rm orb}
, the corresponding projection eigenvalues

\tilde{\pi}_{i}(K^{\rm orb})\equiv 1-\frac{K^{\rm meas}_{i}(0)-K^{\rm orb}_{i}}{K^{\rm meas}_{i}(T_{c}^{+})-K^{\rm orb}_{i}}

(95)

must satisfy

\sum_{i}\tilde{\pi}_{i}(K^{\rm orb})=1

(96)

in the strong-locking regime. This is one equation among the components of
K^{\rm orb}
. Combined with crystal-symmetry constraints (e.g.,
K^{\rm orb}_{a}=K^{\rm orb}_{b}
for uniaxial crystals), it can fix the remaining freedom in the extraction without independent calibration.

The procedure encapsulated in Eqs. (
94
)–(
96
) converts the orbital-shift extraction from a normal-state fit into a normal-state-plus-SC-transition consistency problem, which is overdetermined for any axial or isotropic crystal symmetry. In materials where the Korringa fit is contaminated, fixing
K^{\rm spin}_{N}
from the SC transition and demanding the simplex constraint can yield
K^{\rm orb}_{i}
as a residual, providing a calibration alternative to the standard normal-state fit. The K

XV
Summary

The central result of this paper is a single geometric identity for the residual
T=0
spin response of a noncentrosymmetric superconductor in the strong-locking regime:

\chi_{\mu\nu}(0)=\chi_{N}\big[\delta_{\mu\nu}-\Pi_{\mu\nu}\big],\qquad\Pi_{\mu\nu}=\langle\hat{n}_{\mu}\hat{n}_{\nu}\rangle_{\rm FS}.

The Knight-shift tensor at zero temperature is completely determined by a single Fermi-surface average—the projector
\Pi_{\mu\nu}
of the spin-locking direction
\hat{n}_{\mathbf{k}}
—independently of pairing symmetry, gap magnitude, and Fermi-surface shape. Because
\mathrm{Tr}\,\Pi=1
, the three principal Knight shifts lie on a two-dimensional simplex—the Knight-shift ellipsoid—whose vertices, edges, and interior classify every canonical pairing class.

The identity follows from a Bogoliubov sum rule, the BdG-doubled form of unitary invariance, applied to the spin operator. The same sum rule generates the charge, multi-orbital, site-resolved, and dynamical variants used throughout the paper. Around the central identity we developed: an isotropic-FS trace bound
\mathrm{Tr}\,\chi(0)\leq 2\chi_{N}
for powder or three-axis data; a fully gapped helicity-diagonal treatment of parity-mixed singlet-triplet states, which share the
T=0
projector geometry with their pure-locking counterparts and differ primarily in the finite-
T
Yosida recovery; a closed-form
s
-wave SOC interpolation
F_{s}(\lambda)
valid at arbitrary SOC magnitude; a finite-field strong-locking identity with field-dependent projector
\Pi(\mathbf{H})
; a spin Ferrell–Glover–Tinkham sum rule for the dynamical counterpart; a rigorous vanishing-projection theorem stating that
\Pi_{\alpha\alpha}=0
forbids any
\mathbf{q}=0
contribution to
1/T_{1}\parallel\hat{e}_{\alpha}
in either the normal or superconducting state; and a decoupled-pocket multiband baseline. These were packaged into six experimental protocols ordered by cost, together with an internal consistency check on the orbital-shift subtraction that uses the SC transition itself as an alternative reference.
