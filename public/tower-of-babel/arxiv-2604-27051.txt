Local Current Algebra for the HK Universality Class
Yuting Bai 1 ,, Philip W. Phillips 1

Abstract
We show that a Hamiltonian in terms of the local real-space currents obeying an 𝔰 ​ 𝔲 1 ​ ( 2 ) \mathfrak{su}_{1}(2) affine Lie algebra eliminates the non-locality in the Hatsugai-Kohmoto model for a doped Mott insulator. We establish this local correspondence through the Bjorken-Johnson-Low prescription for anomalous commutators. With this result, we show that the charge susceptibility computed from the current Hamiltonian is identical to that with the elemental Fermionic fields. Consequently, the HK model is local in real space, though not in terms of the Fermionic fields, thereby eliminating the key criticism of this model and reinforcing the utility of current algebras for strong interactions.

U^{x}_{-q}J^{x}_{qa\sigma}+U^{y}_{-q}J^{y}_{qa\sigma}

\displaystyle(U_{x}+iU_{y})J^{\xi\eta}_{qa\sigma}\Leftrightarrow\sum_{k}(U_{x}+iU_{y})(c^{\xi})^{\dagger}_{k+qa\sigma}c^{\eta}_{ka\sigma},

(29)

which pushes electrons from the LHB to the UHB with a momentum shift
\bf{q}
. This makes it explicit that it is the mixing between the bands that produces the non-locality in terms of the original Fermionic operators. It is worth noticing that directly setting
\bf{q}
to zero makes this term vanish. This further shows that it is the spectral weight transfer between the UHB and LHB that ultimately leads to the anomalous WZW term. Thus, to obtain the Berry phase, one must consider
H[{\bf U}(x,t)]=H[{\bf U}(t)+{\bf\epsilon u}(x,t)]
with infinitesimal nonuniformity, and take the limit
\epsilon\to 0
.

To compare with previous results, we compute the charge susceptibility from our current algebra Hamiltonian. In the limit
q\to 0
, the charge susceptibility
17
of the 1D band HK model obtained from the Kubo formalism is

\displaystyle\chi(q,\omega)=\left(\rho-\frac{\lvert q\rvert}{2\pi}\right)\frac{U}{\omega^{2}-U^{2}}+\frac{v_{F}q^{2}}{\pi\omega^{2}}+o_{q\to 0}(q^{2}),

(30)

with
\rho=\frac{N}{L}
. We may then calculate the retarded correlation function
\chi^{R}_{B}

\displaystyle\chi^{R}_{B}(q,\omega)\equiv\frac{-i\theta(t)}{L}\int dte^{-i\omega t}\sum_{\sigma,a}\langle[\rho_{qa\sigma}(t),\rho_{-qa\sigma}(0)]\rangle_{B},

from Eq. (
24
), the current Hamiltonian. Here
\langle A\rangle_{B}\equiv\bra{0}A\ket{0}_{B}
. Since
\rho_{qa\sigma}=J^{\xi\xi}_{qa\sigma}+J^{\xi\eta}_{qa\sigma}+J^{\eta\xi}_{qa\sigma}
. The charge susceptibility now has two contributions,
\chi^{\text{Direct}}_{B}=\braket{[J^{\xi\xi},J^{\xi\xi}]}
and
\chi^{\text{Mix}}_{B}=\braket{[J^{\xi\eta},J^{\eta\xi}]}+\braket{[J^{\eta\xi},J^{\xi\eta}]}
. Following Appendix C, the result is

\displaystyle\chi^{R}_{B}(q,\omega)=\frac{\rho U}{\omega^{2}-U^{2}}+\frac{v_{F}q^{2}}{\pi\omega^{2}}+O\left(q^{2}\right).

(32)

Here we compare the density-density correlation functions computed from the currents, Eq.(
32
), with that obtained from the HK Hamiltonian, Eq.(
30
). Eq. (
32
) captures the behavior of charge susceptibility as
q\to 0
of the band HK model computed in
17
. The difference between the band HK Eq. (
1
) and the current algebra Eq. (
24
) is the
|q|
contribution. In the Dirac limit where we take
k_{F},m\to\infty
while keeping
v_{F}=\frac{k_{F}}{m}=\text{Const}
, when
U
is large,
|\chi_{B}-\chi|/\chi\simeq\frac{q}{k_{F}},\frac{\omega^{2}}{Uv_{F}}\to 0
.
Thus, despite being local, our current algebra Hamiltonian captures the IR behavior
(\omega,q\to 0)
of the HK model at the Dirac limit.

In conclusion, we have shown that in the 1D band HK model, due to strong repulsive interactions, the propagating degrees of freedom are currents
J^{\alpha\beta}_{qa\sigma}
. These currents form a
\mathfrak{su}_{1}(2)
affine Lie algebra, and the low-energy, long wavelength dynamics of the band HK model is captured by a local Hamiltonian Eq. (
24
) in terms of local currents. Our result suggests that the HK universality class associated with
Z_{2}
symmetry breaking could have a local description in terms of currents rather than fermionic fields
c,c^{\dagger}
. Moreover, the BJL prescription, which we used to derive the current algebra, could be applied to strongly correlated systems and is not restricted to one dimension. Further directions include the generalization of the current algebra to the Momentum Mixing HK model
21
;
19
, higher-dimensional cases, or even the Hubbard model. Moreover, exploring the relationship between the WZW term and the multivaluedness of the Luttinger-Ward function
16
in HK models remains to be completed.

Appendix A
A. Derivation of BJL Prescription

Here we present the proof of the BJL prescription
[
3
,
15
,
34
]
. For two arbitrary states,
\ket{a}
,
\ket{b}
, the BJL prescription is

\displaystyle T(\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int dte^{i\omega t}\bra{a}TA(t)B(0)\ket{b}

\displaystyle\lim_{\omega\to\infty}\omega T(\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\bra{a}[A(0),B(0)]_{-}\ket{b}.

(33)

for any two operators,
A
and
B
.
We may first absorb the
\omega
factor via an integration by parts,

\displaystyle\omega T(\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dt(-i)\partial_{t}e^{i\omega t}\bra{a}TA(t)B(0)\ket{b}

\displaystyle=i\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dte^{i\omega t}\partial_{t}\bra{a}TA(t)B(0)\ket{b}.

(34)

The time derivative of
TA(t)B(0)
consists of two parts,

\displaystyle T\dot{A}(t)B(0),

the smooth part, due to the time derivative of
A(t)
, and the singular part

\displaystyle\delta(t)[A(t)B(0)-B(0)A(t)]

generated from the time derivative of
\theta(\pm t)
.

In the limit
\omega\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\lim_{\omega\to\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dte^{i\omega t}\bra{a}T\dot{A}(t)B(0)\ket{b}=0,

(35)

because of the Lebesgue theorem. Only the singular part survives the
\omega\to\infty
limit,

\displaystyle\lim_{\omega\to\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dt\delta(t)e^{i\omega t}\bra{a}[A(t),B(0)]_{-}\ket{b}=\bra{a}[A(0),B(0)]_{-}\ket{b},

(36)

thereby completing the proof of the BJL prescription.

Appendix B
B. Calculation of
T^{\alpha\beta;\gamma\delta}_{\mu\nu,\sigma}

Here we present details of the calculation of
T^{\xi\xi;\xi\xi}_{\mu\nu\sigma}(q,\omega)
and
T^{\xi\eta;\eta\xi}_{\mu\nu\sigma}(q,\omega)
. We omit the spin index
\sigma
in this Appendix since these results are spin-independent. In all of these calculations, we adopt the following definition of the current operators
J^{\alpha\beta}_{\mu{q\sigma}}=(\rho^{\alpha\beta}_{q\sigma},J^{\alpha\beta}_{q\sigma})
:

\displaystyle\rho^{\alpha\beta}_{q\sigma}

\displaystyle=J^{\alpha\beta}_{qL\sigma}+J^{\alpha\beta}_{qR\sigma}=\sum_{k}c^{\alpha\dagger}_{k+q\sigma}c^{\beta}_{k\sigma}

(37)

\displaystyle J^{\alpha\beta}_{q\sigma}

\displaystyle=J^{\alpha\beta}_{qL\sigma}-J^{\alpha\beta}_{qR\sigma}=\sum_{k}\text{sgn}(k)c^{\alpha\dagger}_{k+q\sigma}c^{\beta}_{k\sigma}.

(38)

In general, these operators should be regularized using the standard field theory approach through the addition of a cutoff
f_{\Lambda}(k^{2}-k_{F}^{2})
to project out states deep inside the filling sea. The cutoff
f_{\Lambda}(k^{2}-k_{F}^{2})
restricts the summation over k to the range
[\pm k_{F}-\Lambda,\pm k_{F}+\Lambda]
. However, since we are working with a UV finite model, it is unnecessary to regularize these operators to obtain the correct current algebra.

We start with
T^{\xi\xi;\xi\xi}
in Eq. (
Local Current Algebra for the HK Universality Class
). For
\mu=\nu=0
,

\displaystyle T^{\xi\xi;\xi\xi}_{00}(q,\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int dt\sum_{k_{1}k_{2}}\delta_{k_{2},k_{1}+q}e^{i\left(\omega+\frac{(k_{1}+q)^{2}}{2m}-\frac{k_{1}^{2}}{2m}\right)t}[\theta(t)\braket{c^{\xi\dagger}_{k_{1}+q\sigma}c^{\xi}_{k_{1}\sigma}c^{\xi\dagger}_{k_{2}-q\sigma}c^{\xi}_{k_{2}\sigma}}+\theta(-t)\braket{c^{\xi\dagger}_{k_{2}-q\sigma}c^{\xi}_{k_{2}\sigma}c^{\xi\dagger}_{k_{1}+q\sigma}c^{\xi}_{k_{1}\sigma}}]

(39)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int dt\sum_{k_{1},k_{2}}e^{i\left(\omega+\frac{(k_{1}+q)^{2}}{2m}-\frac{k_{1}^{2}}{2m}\right)t}\delta_{k_{2},k_{1}+q}[\theta(t)\braket{n^{S}_{k_{2}}}\braket{n^{O}_{k_{1}}}+\theta(-t)\braket{n^{O}_{k_{2}}}\braket{n^{S}_{k_{1}}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\theta(q)\left[\sum_{k_{1}=-k_{F}-q}^{-k_{F}}\frac{1}{-i\left(\omega+\frac{k_{1}q}{m}+\frac{q^{2}}{2m}\right)+0^{+}}+\sum_{k_{1}=k_{F}-q}^{k_{F}}\frac{1}{i\left(\omega+\frac{k_{1}q}{m}+\frac{q^{2}}{2m}\right)+0^{+}}\right]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\theta(-q)\left[\sum_{k_{1}=k_{F}}^{k_{F}-q}\frac{1}{-i\left(\omega+\frac{k_{1}q}{m}+\frac{q^{2}}{2m}\right)+0^{+}}+\sum_{k_{1}=-k_{F}}^{-k_{F}-q}\frac{1}{i\left(\omega+\frac{k_{1}q}{m}+\frac{q^{2}}{2m}\right)+0^{+}}\right].

To extract the equal-time commutator, we only need to evaluate the residue of
\omega^{-1}
. Since in the
\omega\to\infty
limit there is no singularity in the integrand, we may expand the integrand in terms of
\omega^{-n}
before doing the integration. When
q>0
, we find that

\displaystyle T^{\xi\xi;\xi\xi}_{00}(q,\omega)\simeq\sum_{k_{1}=-k_{F}-q}^{-k_{F}}\frac{1}{-i\omega}+\sum_{k_{1}=k_{F}-q}^{k_{F}}\frac{1}{i\omega}=0.

(40)

This yields
T^{\xi\xi;\xi\xi}_{00}(\omega)=0
. The same result holds for the
q<0
case. Calculation of
T^{\xi\xi;\xi\xi}_{11}
is rather similar, which also yields 0. For
\mu=0,\nu=1
, we have

\displaystyle T^{\xi\xi;\xi\xi}_{01}(q,\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int dt\sum_{k_{1}k_{2}}\delta_{k_{2},k_{1}+q}e^{i\left(\omega+\frac{(k_{1}+q)^{2}}{2m}-\frac{k_{1}^{2}}{2m}\right)t}\text{sgn}(k_{2})[\theta(t)\braket{c^{\xi\dagger}_{k_{1}+q\sigma}c^{\xi}_{k_{1}\sigma}c^{\xi\dagger}_{k_{2}-q\sigma}c^{\xi}_{k_{2}\sigma}}+\theta(-t)\braket{c^{\xi\dagger}_{k_{2}-q\sigma}c^{\xi}_{k_{2}\sigma}c^{\xi\dagger}_{k_{1}+q\sigma}c^{\xi}_{k_{1}\sigma}}]

(41)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int dt\sum_{k_{1},k_{2}}e^{i\left(\omega+\frac{(k_{1}+q)^{2}}{2m}-\frac{k_{1}^{2}}{2m}\right)t}\delta_{k_{2},k_{1}+q}\text{sgn}(k_{2})[\theta(t)\braket{n^{S}_{k_{2}}}\braket{n^{O}_{k_{1}\sigma}}+\theta(-t)\braket{n^{O}_{k_{2}}}\braket{n^{S}_{k_{1}\sigma}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\theta(q)}{2}\left[\sum_{k_{1}=-k_{F}-q}^{-k_{F}}\frac{\text{sgn}(k_{1}+q)}{-i\left(\omega+\frac{k_{1}q}{m}+\frac{q^{2}}{2m}\right)+0^{+}}+\sum_{k_{1}=k_{F}-q}^{k_{F}}\frac{\text{sgn}(k_{1}+q)}{i\left(\omega+\frac{k_{1}q}{m}+\frac{q^{2}}{2m}\right)+0^{+}}\right]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{\theta(-q)}{2}\left[\sum_{k_{1}=k_{F}}^{k_{F}-q}\frac{\text{sgn}(k_{1}+q)}{-i\left(\omega+\frac{k_{1}q}{m}+\frac{q^{2}}{2m}\right)+0^{+}}+\sum_{k_{1}=-k_{F}}^{-k_{F}-q}\frac{\text{sgn}(k_{1}+q)}{i\left(\omega+\frac{k_{1}q}{m}+\frac{q^{2}}{2m}\right)+0^{+}}\right].

In the limit
\omega\to\infty,q\to 0
, for
q>0
case, the contribution from the
\omega^{-1}
pole is

\displaystyle T^{\text{LL;LL}}_{01}(q,\omega)\simeq\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{k_{1}=-k_{F}-q}^{-k_{F}}\frac{-1}{-i\omega}+\sum_{k_{1}=k_{F}-q}^{k_{F}}\frac{1}{i\omega}\right)=\frac{Lq}{2\pi i\omega}.

(42)

For
q<0
case, it yields the same result.

For the
T^{\xi\eta;\eta\xi}
case, the
\theta(-t)
part does not contribute. Since there is no double occupancy in the ground state,
\braket{J^{\eta\xi}J^{\xi\eta}}=0
. We also set
\mu=\nu=0
to obtain

\displaystyle T^{\xi\eta;\eta\xi}_{00}(q,\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int dt\sum_{k_{1}k_{2}}\delta_{k_{2},k_{1}+q}e^{i\left(\omega-U+\frac{k_{1}q}{m}+\frac{q^{2}}{2m}\right)t}\theta(t)\braket{c^{\xi\dagger}_{k_{1}+q\sigma}c^{\eta}_{k_{1}\sigma}c^{\eta\dagger}_{k_{2}-q\sigma}c^{\xi}_{k_{2}\sigma}}

(43)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\sum_{k_{1},k_{2}}\delta_{k_{2},k_{1}+q}\frac{\braket{n^{S}_{k_{1}}}\braket{n^{S}_{k_{2}}}}{-i\left(\omega-U+\frac{k_{1}q}{m}+\frac{q^{2}}{2m}\right)+0^{+}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{L\theta(q)}{8\pi}\int_{-k_{F}}^{k_{F}-q}dk\frac{1}{-i\left(\omega-U+\frac{k_{1}q}{m}+\frac{q^{2}}{2m}\right)+0^{+}}+\frac{L\theta(-q)}{8\pi}\int_{-k_{F}-q}^{k_{F}}dk\frac{1}{-i\left(\omega-U+\frac{k_{1}q}{m}+\frac{q^{2}}{2m}\right)+0^{+}}.

In the limit
\omega\rightarrow\infty

\displaystyle T^{\xi\eta;\eta\xi}_{00}(q,\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{L\theta(q)}{8\pi}\frac{2k_{F}-q}{-i\omega}+\frac{L\theta(-q)}{8\pi}\frac{2k_{F}+q}{-i\omega}

Since

\displaystyle T^{\xi\eta;\eta\xi}_{01}(q,\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int dt\sum_{k_{1}k_{2}}\delta_{k_{2},k_{1}+q}e^{i\left(\omega-U+\frac{k_{1}q}{m}+\frac{q^{2}}{2m}\right)t}\theta(t)\text{sgn}(k_{2})\braket{c^{\xi\dagger}_{k_{1}+q\sigma}c^{\eta}_{k_{1}\sigma}c^{\eta\dagger}_{k_{2}-q\sigma}c^{\xi}_{k_{2}\sigma}}

(45)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\sum_{k_{1},k_{2}}\delta_{k_{2},k_{1}+q}\text{sgn}(k_{2})\frac{\braket{n^{S}_{k_{1}}}\braket{n^{S}_{k_{2}}}}{-i\left(\omega-U+\frac{k_{1}q}{m}+\frac{q^{2}}{2m}\right)+0^{+}}

Appendix C
C. Calculation of Charge Susceptibility from the Current Algebra

In this section, we calculate
\chi^{\text{Direct}}_{B}
and
\chi^{\text{Mix}}_{B}
. We start with
\chi^{\text{Direct}}_{B}(q,\omega)
.

\displaystyle\chi_{B}^{\text{Direct}}(q,\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{i}{L}\int_{0}^{\infty}dte^{-i\omega t}\sum_{\sigma}[e^{iv_{F}qt}\langle[J^{\xi\xi}_{qR\sigma},J^{\xi\xi}_{-qR\sigma}]\rangle_{B}+e^{-iv_{F}qt}\langle[J^{\xi\xi}_{qL\sigma},J^{\xi\xi}_{-qL\sigma}]\rangle_{B}]

(47)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{L}\sum_{\sigma}\left(\frac{\langle[J^{\xi\xi}_{qR\sigma},J^{\xi\xi}_{-qR\sigma}]\rangle_{B}}{\omega-v_{F}q-i0^{+}}+\frac{\langle[J^{\xi\xi}_{qL\sigma},J^{\xi\xi}_{-qL\sigma}]\rangle_{B}}{\omega+v_{F}q-i0^{+}}\right).

The spectral weights are
\langle[J^{\xi\xi}_{qR\sigma},J^{\xi\xi}_{-qR\sigma}]\rangle_{B}=-\frac{qL}{4\pi}
and
\langle[J^{\xi\xi}_{qL\sigma},J^{\xi\xi}_{-qL\sigma}]\rangle_{B}=\frac{qL}{4\pi}
. In the limit
q\to 0
, the leading order terms are

\displaystyle\chi_{B}^{\text{Direct}}(q,\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma}\frac{q}{4\pi}\left(\frac{1}{\omega-v_{F}q}-\frac{1}{\omega+v_{F}q}\right)

(48)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{v_{F}q^{2}}{\pi\omega^{2}}+O(q^{2}).

We next calculate
\chi_{B}^{\text{Mix}}(q,\omega)
using the same method. In terms of the commutator, we find that

\displaystyle\chi^{\text{Mix}}_{B}(q,\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{L}\sum_{\sigma}\left[\frac{\bra{0}[J^{\xi\eta}_{qR\sigma},J^{\eta\xi}_{-qR\sigma}]\ket{0}}{(\omega-v_{F}q+U)-i0^{+}}+\frac{\bra{0}[J^{\xi\eta}_{qL\sigma},J^{\eta\xi}_{-qL\sigma}]\ket{0}}{(\omega+v_{F}q+U)-i0^{+}}+\frac{\bra{0}[J^{\eta\xi}_{qR\sigma},J^{\xi\eta}_{-qR\sigma}]\ket{0}}{(\omega-v_{F}q-U)-i0^{+}}+\frac{\bra{0}[J^{\eta\xi}_{qL\sigma},J^{\xi\eta}_{-qL\sigma}]\ket{0}}{(\omega+v_{F}q-U)-i0^{+}}\right]

(49)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{\sigma}\left[\left(\frac{\rho}{8}-\frac{q}{8\pi}\right)\left(\frac{1}{\omega-v_{F}q+U}-\frac{1}{\omega+v_{F}q-U}\right)+\left(\frac{\rho}{8}+\frac{q}{8\pi}\right)\left(\frac{1}{\omega+v_{F}q+U}-\frac{1}{\omega-v_{F}q-U}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\rho U}{\omega^{2}-U^{2}}+O(q^{2}).

Consequently, we obtain the expression Eq.(
32
) through adding
\chi^{\text{Direct}}_{B}
and
\chi^{\text{Mix}}_{B}
. This corroborates that the formulation in terms of local currents and the HK Hamiltonian in terms of fermionic operators are in agreement in the Dirac limit.
