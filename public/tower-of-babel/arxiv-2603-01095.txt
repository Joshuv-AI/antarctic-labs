X-ray and H α \alpha Superflare on an RS CVn-type Star, UX Arietis: Constraint for the Flare Location from Radial Velocity Change during the Flare
Sota Urabe, \altemailmark \orcid Yohko Tsuboi \altemailmark \orcid 0000-0001-9943-0024 Kosuke Namekata \orcid 0000-0002-1297-9485 Sakura Nawa \orcid Hiroyuki Maehara \orcid 0000-0003-0332-0811 Noboru Nemoto \orcid Yuta Notsu \orcid 0000-0002-0412-0849 and Wataru Iwakiri \orcid 0000-0002-0207-9010

Abstract
We report on a giant stellar flare from the RS CVn-type binary UX Arietis, detected with the Monitor of All-sky X-ray Image (MAXI) and followed by a 12-day optical spectroscopic campaign using the 3.8 m Seimei Telescope. The flare released 5 × 10 37 5\times 10^{37} erg in X-rays (0.1–100 keV) and ( 2 CLOSE (2 – OPEN 6 ) × 10 36 6)\times 10^{36} erg in the H α \alpha line, placing it among the most energetic events of its kind. The H α \alpha light curve showed sinusoidal modulation atop an exponential decay, consistent with reappearance of the flaring region due to binary rotation. At orbital phase 0, when the primary star is farthest from the observer, 40% of the H α \alpha flux was obscured, while at phase 0.5 the full emission was visible. This suggests the H α \alpha emitting region is located at a relatively low latitude and is comparable in size to the stellar disk. Radial velocity modulation implies that the region lies at ∼ 19 ​ R ⊙ \sim 19\,R_{\odot} from the system’s rotation axis, farther out than the stellar limb at 14.4 ​ R ⊙ 14.4\,R_{\odot} . Photometric monitoring with the Chuo-university Astronomical Telescope revealed a large low-latitude starspot covering ∼ 25 % \sim 25\% of the surface. These findings are consistent with a scenario in which the flare occurred above the starspot, and the H α \alpha -emitting plasma was magnetically confined in a loop extending at least 5 ​ R ⊙ 5\,R_{\odot} above the stellar surface. From the MAXI data and assuming a radiatively cooling plasma, the electron density and volume are estimated to be 10 10 10^{10} cm -3 and 1 × 10 35 1\times 10^{35} cm 3 , respectively. If cubic in shape, this corresponds to 7 ​ R ⊙ 7\,R_{\odot} , consistent with the H α \alpha region height. These results provide direct constraints on the geometry of the plasma and its spatial relationship with the starspot in one of the most energetic stellar flares ever observed.

1
Introduction

Solar flares are explosive phenomena in which the magnetic energy stored in the solar corona is rapidly released and converted into kinetic, thermal, and non-thermal energies (e.g.
[
41
,
40
,
42
,
24
,
30
]
).
The released energy is transported downward into the lower atmosphere, driving various plasma processes such as chromospheric evaporation.
As a result, hot plasma with temperatures of about
10^{7}
K expands upward into the corona along magnetic field loops, forming bright X-ray flare loops.
The largest solar flare ever recorded released an enormous amount of energy, on the order of
10^{32}
erg (e.g.
[
10
]
).
Stellar flares span a wide range of energies, from small-scale events to some of the most powerful stellar explosions, and are detected across wavelengths from radio to X-rays (e.g.,
[
21
,
5
,
15
]
).
By analogy with solar flares, those observed on late-type stars are also thought to be caused by magnetic reconnection occurring in their coronae, as their light curves and spectral evolution are similar to those of solar flares (e.g.
[
47
,
22
]
).

With large starspots as well as persistent H
\alpha
emission, both indicative of enhanced chromospheric activity (e.g.
[
46
]
), RS CVn-type binary systems are known to produce flares that are several orders of magnitude more energetic than solar flares, with released energies ranging from
10^{36}
to
10^{39}
erg (e.g.
[
47
,
22
,
20
,
7
]
).

The large radiative energies may be released via an extensive magnetic structures, for example magnetic loops connecting the binary components (e.g.
[
44
,
49
]
).
To understand the geometry of flare loops in RS CVn binaries, estimates of loop lengths have been made using X-ray studies, comparing them with stellar radius and binary separations (e.g.
[
47
,
39
,
22
]
). These estimates have been obtained through spectral and timing analyzes using methods such as those described by
Shibata & Yokoyama 1999
;
Shibata & Yokoyama 2002
,
Haisch 1983
,
van den Oord & Mewe 1989
,
Reale et al. 1997a
, and
Reale 2007
. For example,
Tsuboi et al. 2016
analyzed 18 MAXI X-ray flares from eight RS CVn systems and estimated loop lengths ranging from 0.4 to 20 times the binary separation, using the scaling relations of
Shibata & Yokoyama 1999
;
Shibata & Yokoyama 2002
and a pre-flare coronal density of
n_{\rm e}=10^{9}
cm
-3
.
Kawai et al. 2022
also estimated loop lengths of 3.1 to 3.3 binary separations using
Shibata & Yokoyama 1999
;
Shibata & Yokoyama 2002
’s scaling relation, and 0.6 to 1.3 binary separations using
Haisch 1983
’s scaling relation. However, despite the suggestions of the large scale of flaring loops, it is not conclusive whether the loops are connected with each binary components, since there should be systematic uncertainties in the physical parameters such as preflare electron densities or the shape of the loops.

In this case, (1) high-dispersion spectroscopic observations and/or (2) multi-wavelengths observations in giant flares may be useful; the former can capture the dynamism of the emission region and the latter can resolve the different temperature/spatial regions of the same event in the different bands, thus providing information comparable to spatial information in stellar flares that are distant from us and lack spatial resolution. However, giant flares in stars occur infrequently, and although multi-wavelength observations exist (e.g.
Kawai et al. 2022
), they are still scarce.

Here, we present follow-up mid-resolution H
\alpha
spectroscopic observations conducted using the Japanese Seimei telescope at Okayama Observatory, triggered by MAXI’s detection of a large stellar superflare from the RS CVn-type star UX Ari. The observations were carried out over 12 days, beginning on April 3, 2022. The superflare lasted several days and exhibited rotational radial velocity modulation in the H
\alpha
line during the follow-up observations, which is the primary focus of this study. Additionally, we conducted long-term photometric observations of UX Ari using the Chuo-university Astronomical Telescope (CAT). The rotational modulations of stellar brightness and H
\alpha
RV changes allow us to estimate the relationship between starspots/active regions and the giant flare. In this paper, we provide an overview of the observations in Section
2
, present the analysis and results in Section
3
, discuss the flare radiation energetics, loop length scale, and potential flare locations in Section
4
, and conclude with a summary in Section
5
.

Seimei Telescope

As can be seen, there is a clear single-peaked modulation in Figure
7
.
This can be interpreted as a large starspot or a group of starspots on the surface of one of the components, repeatedly appearing and disappearing as the star rotates (e.g.
Hummel et al. 2017
).
Hereafter, we call the area “the spot area”. Here, we are using the phase definition by
Hummel et al. 2017
. Doppler imaging based on high-resolution spectroscopy has revealed stellar spots on the surface of the subgiant primary of UX Ari (
Vogt & Hatzes 1991
;
Aarum et al. 1999
;
Gu et al. 2004
;
Hummel et al. 2017
). On the other hand, in Figure
7
, the flux maximum is near phase 0, while the minimum is near phase 0.5. These indicate that “the spot area” is on the primary star side and on the farther location from the secondary star.

Next, we estimate the occupancy of “the spot area”.
Alekseev 2014
monitored the V-band brightness over the period from 1970 to 2010,
as in Figure 4 of their paper. In the light curve, long-term variations can be seen in addition to the short-term rotational modulation.
During the active phase observed from around 1985 to 2000, particularly near 1989 and 1994, the minimum V-band magnitude of 6.35 was recorded at the brightest phase of the short-term rotational modulation.
We assume that at the flux-maximum, there are almost no star spots on the hemisphere of the primary. On the other hand, our derived least V-band magnitude is about 6.40, which is almost the same as the least V-band magnitude during the long monitoring by
Alekseev 2014
.
This means that even in our observation, there are almost no star spots on the hemisphere of the primary star during the flux-maximum phase.

Summary

(1)

A giant X-ray flare was detected from an RS CVn-type binary, UX Ari, with MAXI’s all sky survey. We performed follow-up optical spectroscopic observations of H
\alpha
line with the 3.8 m Seimei telescope for 12 days.

(2)

The light curve in the X-ray band shows gradual decay, while that in the H
\alpha
emission line band shows additional sinusoidal fluctuation on an exponential decay, which is consistent with the recurrence of the flare location due to the binary rotation. The
e
-folding decay times in both bands are located at the high-end of the common proportional relationship derived by
Kawai et al. 2022
.

(3)

From the obtained peak luminosity and the
e
-folding time, the radiative energy in the X-ray (the 0.1–100 keV) and the H
\alpha
bands are respectively obtained. The energies follow the empirical linear relationship of X-ray and H
\alpha
flares energy for solar and stellar flares
(
Kawai et al. 2022
)
, giving
one of the largest ever recorded among stellar flares observed in both X-ray and H
\alpha
bands.

(4)

The RV variation of the H
\alpha
emission line has the same period as the orbital period of the binary. The timing and the amplitude of the RV variation indicate the H
\alpha
emission region is 19
R_{\odot}
separated from the axis of the rigid rotation and located

R_{\odot}
from the rotational axis.

(5)

Like the RV, the light curve in the H
\alpha

The minimum value of the H

These results suggest that the H
