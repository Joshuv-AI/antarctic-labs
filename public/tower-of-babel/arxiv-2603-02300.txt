One-point energy correlator for deep inelastic scattering at small x x
Zhong-Bo Kang, Robert Kao, Meijian Li, Jani Penttala

Abstract
We derive the expressions for the one-point energy correlator (OPEC) in deep inelastic scattering in the high-energy (small- x x ) limit within the Color Glass Condensate framework. The OPEC is computed as a function of the angle between the energy flow and the target proton or nucleus, enabling a systematic exploration of different momentum scales in the scattering process. Owing to the momentum sum rule, the dependence on fragmentation functions cancels, leaving the dipole amplitude as the only nonperturbative input. As a result, the OPEC provides a clean and direct probe of gluon saturation dynamics at small x x . We present numerical results for representative kinematic configurations relevant to the future Electron–Ion Collider, demonstrating sizable nuclear suppression effects and highlighting the sensitivity of this observable to saturation phenomena.

Theoretical formalism

II.1
DIS one-point energy correlator

Energy-correlator observables are defined as an energy-weighted sum over all produced hadrons.
For DIS OPEC, this corresponds to measuring the angular distribution of outgoing hadrons with respect to the incoming nucleus, as defined in Ref.
Li:2021txc
,
1
1

1

In Ref.
Li:2021txc
, this observable is referred to as DIS EEC. Our definition differs in that it is not normalized to the inclusive cross section.

\frac{\differential{\Sigma}_{\lambda}}{\differential{\cos\theta}}=\sum_{h}\int\differential\sigma^{\gamma^{*}_{\lambda}+p\to h+X}\,z_{h}\,\delta(\cos\theta_{hp}-\cos\theta)\;,

(1)

where
\lambda=T,L
is the photon polarization, and
z_{h}
is the longitudinal momentum fraction of the outgoing hadron specified by

z_{h}\equiv\frac{P\cdot p_{h}}{P\cdot q},

(2)

with
P
,
p_{h}
, and
q
being the 4-momenta of the incoming proton/nucleus, outgoing hadron
h
, and intermediate virtual photon, respectively. We denote by
\theta_{hp}
the polar angle between the incoming proton/nucleus and the outgoing hadron.
To simplify the notation, we parameterize the angle into

\tau=\frac{1+\cos\theta}{2}\;.

(3)

In this parametrization, the back-to-back limit (
\theta\to\pi
) corresponds to
\tau\to 0
, while the collinear limit (
\theta\to 0
) corresponds to
\tau\to 1
.

We work in the Breit frame in which the virtual photon is moving fast along the positive
z
-direction, while the nucleon moves along the opposite direction, as illustrated in
fig.

2
.
The photon carries momentum

q^{\mu}=\frac{Q}{\sqrt{2}}(\bar{n}^{\mu}-n^{\mu})\;,

(4)

in which
Q=\sqrt{-q^{2}}
is its virtuality.
The light-cone null vectors are defined as
n^{\mu}\equiv(0,1,0,0)
and
\bar{n}^{\mu}\equiv(1,0,0,0)
.
Throughout the manuscript, we use the light-cone variables expressed as
v^{\mu}=(v^{+},v^{-},\boldsymbol{v})=((v^{0}+v^{3})/\sqrt{2},(v^{0}-v^{3})/\sqrt{2},v^{1},v^{2})
. Note that
v\dotproduct n=v^{+}
and
v\dotproduct\bar{n}=v^{-}
, and the boldface notation
\boldsymbol{v}
denotes transverse components. We also write
v_{\perp}=|\boldsymbol{v}|
for the magnitude of the transverse vector.
The incoming proton/nucleus has a 4-momentum given by

P^{\mu}=\frac{Q}{\sqrt{2}x_{B}}n^{\mu}\;,

(5)

where
x_{B}\equiv Q^{2}/(2q\cdot P)
is the Bjorken variable.

In the dipole picture that is convenient for calculations at small
x
, the incoming virtual photon fluctuates into a quark–antiquark pair that scatters off the target eikonally.
We keep track of the parton (denoted as
a
) that will further fragment into the produced hadron, while integrating out the other parton.
We define the longitudinal momentum fraction of the parton
a
with respect to the virtual photon as

\displaystyle\hat{\xi}\equiv\frac{p_{a}\cdot n}{q\cdot n}=\frac{p_{a}^{+}}{q^{+}}\;.

(6)

In the collinear factorization approach for fragmentation, the produced hadron,
h
, carries a fraction
z
of the parton’s momentum,
p_{h}=zp_{a}
.
We assume that the hadron momentum is sufficiently large such that its mass can be neglected.
By imposing the on-shell condition
p_{h}^{+}=p_{h,\perp}^{2}/(2p_{h}^{-})
, it is readily seen that the hadron transverse momentum can be determined from
\theta_{hp}
:

\absolutevalue{\pt_h}=z\hat{\xi}Q\cot\frac{\theta_{hp}}{2}.

(7)

The cross section for
\gamma^{*}+p\to h+X
can be written in terms of the partonic cross sections for
\gamma^{*}+p\to a+X
as

\frac{\differential{\sigma^{\gamma^*+p \to h+X}}}{\differential{z_h}\differential^{2}\mathbf{p}_{h}}=\int_{z_{h}}^{1}\frac{\differential{z}}{z^{3}}\sum_{a}D_{h/a}(z)\frac{\differential{\sigma^{\gamma^*+p \to a+X}}}{\differential{\hat\xi}\differential^{2}{\mathbf{p}_{a}}}\;,

(8)

where
D_{h/a}(z)
is the parton-to-hadron fragmentation function, and we note that
z_{h}=p_{h}^{+}/q^{+}=z\hat{\xi}
.
The differential partonic cross section is as follows
Marquet:2009ca
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{\differential{\sigma^{\gamma^*_\lambda+p \to a+X}}}{\differential{\hat\xi}\differential^{2}{\mathbf{p}_{a}}}=&\frac{2\alpha_{\text{em}}e_{f}^{2}N_{c}S_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int\differential^{2}{\mathbf{q}}\,\\
&\times\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{p}_{a},\hat{\xi},Q^{2})\mathcal{F}_{x}(\mathbf{q})\;,\end{split}

(9)

in which for different photon polarizations

\mathcal{H}_{L}(\mathbf{p}_{a},\mathbf{q},\hat{\xi},Q^{2})\equiv 4\hat{\xi}^{2}(1-\hat{\xi})^{2}Q^{2}\\
\times\left(\frac{1}{\mathbf{p}_{a}^{2}+\epsilon_{f}^{2}}-\frac{1}{(\mathbf{p}_{a}-\mathbf{q})^{2}+\epsilon_{f}^{2}}\right)^{2}\;,

(10a)

\mathcal{H}_{T}(\mathbf{p}_{a},\mathbf{q},\hat{\xi},Q^{2})\equiv[\hat{\xi}^{2}+(1-\hat{\xi})^{2}]\\
\times\absolutevalue{\frac{\pt_a}{\pt_a^2+\ef^2}-\frac{\pt_a-\qt}{(\pt_a-\qt)^2+\ef^2}}^{2}\;.

(10b)

with
\epsilon_{f}^{2}\equiv\hat{\xi}(1-\hat{\xi})Q^{2}
.
Here,
S_{\perp}
is the transverse size of the target, the summation over
a
runs over quarks and antiquarks, and
e_{f}
is the dimensionless fractional charge of parton
a
with flavor
f
(
\bar{f}
). The function
\mathcal{F}_{x}(\mathbf{q})
denotes the dipole amplitude in the momentum space, related to the S-matrix for dipole–target scattering by

\mathcal{F}_{x}(\mathbf{q})=\int\frac{\differential[2]{\rt}}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{-i\mathbf{q}\dotproduct\mathbf{r}}S_{x}(\mathbf{r}),

(11)

where
\mathbf{r}
is the dipole transverse separation. We employ the CGC effective field theory to model the dipole amplitude, with its dependence on the
x
-variable governed by the running-coupling Balitsky–Kovchegov (rcBK) equation
Balitsky:1995ub
;
Kovchegov:1999yj
with the Balitsky prescription
Balitsky:2006wa
. We use the same set of parameters as in our previous study of the transverse energy–energy correlator
Kang:2025vjk
.

For the initial condition of the rcBK equation we use the model
Casuga:2023dcf

S_{x_{0}}(r)=\\
1-\exp\quantity[-\frac{\qty(r^2 Q^2_{s0})^{\gamma}}{4}\log(\frac{1}{\Lambda_{\rm QCD} r} + e \cdot e_c )]\;.

(12)

For the proton target, we take the median values of the relevant parameters from the 4-parameter fit (with
\gamma=1
fixed) in Ref.
Casuga:2023dcf
.
For nuclear targets, we modify the saturation scale and the transverse size by
Kang:2023oqj

\displaystyle Q_{s0,A}^{2}=cA^{1/3}Q_{s0}^{2}\;,\qquad S_{\perp,A}=A^{2/3}S_{\perp}/c\;.

(13)

The parameter
c
takes into account the uncertainty in the nuclear geometry, and we vary it between
0.5<c<1.0

;
Tong:2022zwp
.

By inserting the above expressions for the cross section into the OPEC defined in
eq.

1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{\differential{\Sigma}_{\lambda}}{\differential{\tau}}=&\sum_{h,a}\int\differential{\hat\xi}\differential^{2}\mathbf{p}_{h}\differential{z}\frac{1}{z}D_{h/a}(z)\hat{\xi}\frac{\differential{\sigma^{\gamma^*_\lambda+p \to a+X}}}{\differential{\hat\xi}\differential^{2}{\mathbf{p}_{a}}}\\
&\times\delta\left(\frac{\absolutevalue{\pt_h}^{2}}{(z\hat{\xi}Q)^{2}+\absolutevalue{\pt_h}^{2}}-\tau\right)\;.\end{split}

(14)

Note that after the change of variables
z_{h}\to\hat{\xi}
, the lower limit of the
z
integral becomes
0
.
We apply the delta function in carrying out the
p_{h,\perp}
integral, thereby fixing the value of
p_{a,\perp}(=p_{h,\perp}/z)
as well,

\begin{split}\frac{\differential{\Sigma}_{\lambda}}{\differential{\tau}}=&\sum_{h,a}\int_{0}^{1}\differential{\hat\xi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\differential{\phi}\int_{0}^{1}\differential{z}\frac{z\hat{\xi}^{3}Q^{2}}{2(1-\tau)^{2}}D_{h/a}(z)\\
&\times\frac{\differential{\sigma^{\gamma^*+p \to a+X}}}{\differential{\hat\xi}\differential^{2}{\mathbf{p}_{a}}}\bigg|_{p_{a,\perp}=\hat{\xi}Q\sqrt{\tau/(1-\tau)}}\;.\end{split}

(15)

Here,
\phi=\arg\mathbf{p}_{h}=\arg\mathbf{p}_{a}
is the azimuthal angle of the hadron and of the parent parton.
The dependence on the fragmentation functions canceled via the momentum-sum rule

\sum_{h}\int_{0}^{1}\differential{z}zD_{h/a}(z)=1\;.

(16)

It follows that,

\begin{split}\frac{\differential{\Sigma}_{\lambda}}{\differential{\tau}}&=\frac{2\alpha_{\text{em}}N_{c}S_{\perp}Q^{2}}{(2\pi)^{2}(1-\tau)^{2}}\sum_{f}e_{f}^{2}\int\differential^{2}{\mathbf{q}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\differential{\phi}\int_{0}^{1}\differential{\hat\xi}\\
&\times\hat{\xi}^{3}\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{p}_{a},\hat{\xi},Q^{2})\mathcal{F}_{x}(\mathbf{q})\bigg|_{p_{a,\perp}=\hat{\xi}Q\sqrt{\tau/(1-\tau)}}\;,\end{split}

(17)

where we account for the antiquark contribution by introducing a factor of
2
, i.e.,
\sum_{a}\to 2\sum_{f}
, and we include only the light quark flavors
f=u,d,s
in the sum.
After performing the integrations over the remaining azimuthal angles, the resulting OPEC can be written as,

\frac{\differential{\Sigma}_{\lambda}}{\differential{\tau}}=\frac{\alpha_{\text{em}}N_{c}S_{\perp}Q^{2}}{\pi(1-\tau)^{2}}\sum_{f}e_{f}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\differential{\hat\xi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\differential{q_\perp}\,q_{\perp}\,\hat{\xi}^{3}\\
\times\mathcal{F}_{x}(q_{\perp})h_{\lambda}\left(q_{\perp},\hat{\xi}Q\sqrt{\frac{\tau}{1-\tau}},\hat{\xi},Q^{2}\right)\;,

(18)

where the functions
h_{\lambda}
for longitudinal and transverse polarizations are given by

h_{L}(q_{\perp},k_{\perp},\hat{\xi},Q^{2})\equiv\hat{\xi}^{2}(1-\hat{\xi})^{2}Q^{2}8\pi\\
\times\left[\frac{a}{(a^{2}-b^{2})^{3/2}}-\frac{2}{(a^{2}-b^{2})^{1/2}d}+\frac{1}{d^{2}}\right],

(19a)

h_{T}(q_{\perp},k_{\perp},\hat{\xi},Q^{2})\equiv[\hat{\xi}^{2}+(1-\hat{\xi})^{2}]2\pi\\
\times\left[-\frac{\epsilon_{f}^{2}a}{(a^{2}-b^{2})^{3/2}}+\frac{2\epsilon_{f}^{2}+a-d}{d(a^{2}-b^{2})^{1/2}}-\frac{\epsilon_{f}^{2}}{d^{2}}\right]\;,

(19b)

with the shorthand definitions,

\displaystyle a\equiv\epsilon_{f}^{2}+q_{\perp}^{2}+k_{\perp}^{2}\,,\quad b\equiv 2q_{\perp}k_{\perp}\,,\quad d\equiv\epsilon_{f}^{2}+k_{\perp}^{2}\,.

(20)

In analogy to the inclusive DIS cross section
Golec-Biernat:1998zce
, the OPEC can be parameterized in terms of different photon polarizations.
We define the structure-function-like OPECs as

\displaystyle\frac{\differential{\tilde\Sigma_2}}{\differential{\tau}}=\frac{Q^{2}}{4\pi^{2}\alpha_{\text{em}}}\left(\frac{\differential{\Sigma_L}}{\differential{\tau}}+\frac{\differential{\Sigma_T}}{\differential{\tau}}\right)\;,

(21)

and

\displaystyle\frac{\differential{\tilde\Sigma_\lambda}}{\differential{\tau}}=\frac{Q^{2}}{4\pi^{2}\alpha_{\text{em}}}\frac{\differential{\Sigma_\lambda}}{\differential{\tau}}\;.

(22)

This rescaling of the OPEC has the advantage of absorbing most of the
Q^{2}
-dependence and making the quantities dimensionless.
The reduced OPEC, a counterpart of the reduced cross section, can be written as

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tilde{\Sigma}_{r}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau}=\frac{\differential{\tilde\Sigma_2}}{\differential{\tau}}-\frac{y^{2}}{\quantity[1+(1-y)^2]}\frac{\differential{\tilde\Sigma_L}}{\differential{\tau}}\;.

(23)

To characterize nuclear effects relative to the proton baseline, we define the nuclear modification factor for the OPEC as

R_{A}(\tau)=\left(\derivative{\tilde\Sigma_{\lambda,A}}{\tau}\right)\bigg/\left(A\derivative{\tilde\Sigma_{\lambda,p}}{\tau}\right)\;.

(24)

Without nuclear effects, this ratio is normalized to one.

IV
Conclusion

In this work, within the framework of the Color Glass Condensate effective field theory, we have derived the one-point energy correlator (OPEC) between the produced hadron and the incoming nucleus in high-energy deep inelastic scattering in the small-
x
regime. Extending previous analyses that focused on the back-to-back limit, we obtained expressions valid at intermediate angles, enabling a systematic study of the angular dependence of energy flow in high-energy DIS. Because the dependence on fragmentation functions cancels via the momentum-sum rule, the only nonperturbative input is the dipole amplitude describing the scattering of a quark–antiquark dipole from the classical color field. The OPEC therefore provides a particularly clean and direct probe of gluon saturation in nucleons and nuclei.

We presented numerical results for representative kinematic regions relevant to the future Electron–Ion Collider. We find sizable nuclear suppression at small
\tau
, which gradually weakens as
\tau
increases or as the photon virtuality
Q
becomes larger. The angular variable
\tau
effectively scans different values of the longitudinal momentum fraction
x
, with small
\tau
probing the lowest-
x
region where saturation effects are strongest. In particular, at large angles (corresponding to small
\tau
and lower transverse momentum scales), the OPEC exhibits enhanced sensitivity to nuclear modification effects associated with gluon saturation.

Taken together, these results indicate that measuring the OPEC in DIS at the future EIC would offer a valuable and fragmentation-independent window into gluon saturation dynamics and the small-
x
structure of nucleons and nuclei across a wide angular range.
