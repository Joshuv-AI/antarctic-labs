Cosmological perturbations in the theory of gravity with non-minimal derivative coupling. I. Modes of perturbations
R. I. Kamalitdinov, S. V. Sushkov

Abstract
We consider perturbations in the isotropic and homogeneous cosmological model with the spatially flat Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker metric in the framework of the theory of gravity with non-minimal derivative coupling. The Lagrangian of the theory contains the coupling term η ​ G μ ​ ν ​ ∇ μ ϕ ​ ∇ ν ϕ \eta G^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\phi\nabla_{\nu}\phi and represents the particular example of a general Horndeski Lagrangian, which results in second-order field equations. It is known that the non-minimal derivative coupling crucially changes scenarios of the Universe evolution on early times. In particular, the η \eta -term is dominating on early times and leads to a primary quasi-de Sitter (inflationary) stage which needs no fine-tuned potential. On late times the influence of non-minimal derivative coupling on the Universe evolution completely disappears, and this naturally leads to the transition to the standard cosmological evolution (post-inflationary stage). We have derived a complete set of equations which describe an evolution of scalar, vector and tensor modes of perturbations. All modes are analyzed analytically in two asymptotic cases, and then we construct exact numerical solutions which describe an entire evolution of the modes. We show that all modes, including vector ones, are amplified in the quasi-de Sitter (inflationary) stage, and such the behavior is cardinally distinct from that in Friedmann cosmology.

III
Isotropic and homogeneous cosmological model

Let us consider isotropic homogeneous spatially flat cosmological models with the Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker metric

ds^{2}=-dt^{2}+\textrm{a}^{2}(t)d\mathbf{x}^{2},

(6)

where
d\mathbf{x}^{2}
is the Euclidean metric,

\textrm{a}(t)
is the scale factor, and
H(t)=\dot{\textrm{a}}(t)/\textrm{a}(t)
is the Hubble parameter. Hereafter, the overdot denotes derivatives with respect to the cosmic time, i.e.
\dot{\textrm{a}}(t)=d{\textrm{a}}/dt
, etc.
Denoting the present moment of time as
t_{0}
, we have
\textrm{a}_{0}=\textrm{a}(t_{0})
and
H_{0}=H(t_{0})
.
Supposing homogeneity and isotropy, we also get
\phi=\phi(t)
.

The general field equations (
2

6

\displaystyle 3H^{2}=4\pi{\dot{\phi}}^{2}\left(1-9\eta H^{2}\right),

(7a)

\displaystyle\displaystyle 2\dot{H}+3H^{2}=-4\pi\dot{\phi}^{2}-4\pi\eta\dot{\phi}^{2}\left(2\dot{H}+3H^{2}+4H{\ddot{\phi}}{\dot{\phi}^{-1}}\right),

(7b)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\textrm{a}^{3}}\frac{d}{dt}\left[\textrm{a}^{3}\dot{\phi}\left(1-3\eta H^{2}\right)\right]=0,

(7c)

Here Eq. (
7a

tt

2a
), Eq. (
7b
) is the
ii

2a

8

Integrating Eq. (

(8)

where
{\cal Q}
is a constant of integration. In fact,

is the Noether or scalar charge associated with the shift symmetry

III.1
Asymptotic cosmological stages

As we mentioned in the introduction, cosmological scenarios described by the field equations (
7
) in the theory of gravity with non-minimal derivative coupling have been intensively studied in the literature (see, for example, our works
Sushkov:2009
;
SarSus:2010
;
Sushkov:2012
;
SkuSusTop:2013
;
MatSus:2015
;
StaSusVol:2016
;
StaSusVol:2019
;
Galeev_etal:2021
;
Sushkov:2023aya
).
Here let us briefly discuss the key feature of these scenarios. For this purpose, we rewrite the modified Friedmann equation (
7a
) as follows

3H^{2}(1+12\pi\eta\dot{\phi}^{2})=4\pi{\dot{\phi}}^{2}.

(9)

From the first integral (
8
) one has
\dot{\phi}\sim\textrm{a}^{-3}
, hence
12\pi\eta\dot{\phi}^{2}\gg 1
in the limit
\textrm{a}\to 0
, and the unity in the left-hand side of Eq. (
9
) can be neglected.
As the result, one has

H^{2}=\frac{1}{9\eta}\,.

(10)

Therefore, the
\eta
-term in Eq. (
9
), dominating at early times, provides the quasi-de Sitter (inflationary) character of the cosmological evolution such that

\textrm{a}(t)=\textrm{a}_{i}e^{H_{\eta}(t-t_{i})},\quad\phi(t)=\phi_{i}e^{-3H_{\eta}(t-t_{i})},\quad\text{where}\quad H_{\eta}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{9\eta}},

(11)

and
t_{i}
is some initial value of time meaning the beginning of inflation.

At late cosmic times, when
\textrm{a}(t)
becomes large, the
\eta
-terms become small and negligible, so that
12\pi\eta\dot{\phi}^{2}\ll 1
. Now Eq. (
9
) reduces to

3H^{2}=4\pi{\dot{\phi}}^{2}.

(12)

This is nothing but the standard Friedmann equation describing the late-time evolution of the Universe with the massless scalar field.
Its solution reads

\textrm{a}(t)\propto t^{1/3},\quad H(t)=\frac{1}{3t},\quad\dot{\phi}\propto\frac{1}{t}.

(13)

Hereafter, we will call these two asymptotic stages as (i)
quasi-de Sitter (inflationary) stage
, when the non-minimal derivative coupling is dominating, so that
12\pi\eta\dot{\phi}^{2}\gg 1
, and (ii)
post-inflationary stage
, when the non-minimal derivative coupling is negligible, so that
12\pi\eta\dot{\phi}^{2}\ll 1
.

It is worth noting that the quasi-de Sitter stage is naturally ending and exchanging by the post-inflationary stage as soon as a domination of the non-minimal derivative coupling term comes decreasing during the Universe evolution.
Therefore, the non-minimal derivative coupling provides an inflationary mechanism at early stages of the Universe evolution and naturally describes transitions between various cosmological phases
without any fine-tuned potential
.

Homogeneity and isotropy also suppose that

Now, the field equations (

\displaystyle 3{\cal H}^{2}=4\pi{\phi}^{\prime 2}\left(1-9\eta\textrm{a}^{-2}{\cal H}^{2}\right),

\displaystyle 2{\cal H}^{\prime}+{\cal H}^{2}=-4\pi{\phi}^{\prime 2}\left[1+\eta\textrm{a}^{-2}\left(2{\cal H}^{\prime}-3{\cal H}^{2}+4{\cal H}\frac{{\phi}^{\prime\prime}}{{\phi}^{\prime}}\right)\right],

IV
Perturbations

In the Poisson gauge (see, for example,

;

14

(20)

Here
\Phi\equiv\Phi(\tau,{\bf x})\ll 1
and
\Psi\equiv\Psi(\tau,{\bf x})\ll 1

Q_{i}
and

are transverse, and

is a traceless tensor such that

\nabla^{i}Q_{i}=0,\quad\nabla^{i}h_{ij}=0,\quad h^{i}_{i}=0.

(21)

The homogeneous scalar field

is also perturbed as follows

(22)

where
\delta\varphi\equiv\delta\varphi(\tau,{\bf x})\ll 1
is a perturbation.

Substituting the perturbed metric (
20
) and the scalar field (
22
) into the field equations (
2
) and taking into account the background relations (
15
), one can obtain the resulting equations for perturbations. Usually, instead of perturbed scalar values

and the length

and for
short-wavelength scalar modes
, when
k\tau\gg 1
, one has

\Psi(\tau,{k})\propto\frac{1}{(k\tau)^{3/2}}\cos(k\tau+\alpha^{(s)}_{k}),

(33)

where
\alpha^{(s)}_{k}
is an arbitrary phase.

Note that the result obtained for scalar perturbation modes in the post-inflationary stage is expectable and well-known for cosmology with an ordinary massless scalar field (see, for example,
Padmanabhan:2010
). A completely novel result is expected for the modes in the early quasi-de Sitter stage.

IV.1.2
Scalar modes in the quasi-de Sitter (inflationary) stage

First, let us rewrite Eq. (
26
) as follows

(1-4\pi\eta\textrm{a}^{-2}\phi^{\prime 2})\Psi-(1+4\pi\eta\textrm{a}^{-2}\phi^{\prime 2})\Phi=8\pi\eta\textrm{a}^{-2}\phi\phi^{\prime\prime}\delta\varphi.

(34)

Taking into account that in the quasi-de Sitter (inflationary) stage
4\pi\eta\textrm{a}^{-2}\phi^{\prime 2}\gg 1
and using the solution (
19
) for
\phi(\tau)
, one can obtain from the above equation that

\delta\varphi=-\frac{3}{4}(\Psi+\Phi).

(35)

Performing analogous manipulations with Eqs. (
24
), (
25
), substituting the background expressions for
\textrm{a}(\tau)
,
\mathcal{H}(\tau)
,
\phi(\tau)
given by Eq. (
19
), and taking into account the relation (
35
), we can obtain the following equations describing the scalar perturbations in the quasi-de Sitter stage:

\displaystyle\tau\Psi^{\prime}-\Phi-\frac{k^{2}\tau^{2}}{18}(\Psi-\Phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(36)

\displaystyle\tau\Psi^{\prime}-\tau\Phi^{\prime}-6\Phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

(37)

A general solution of these equations yields

\displaystyle\Psi(\tau,{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{D^{(s)}_{1,k}}{\xi^{5}}(\xi^{3}-9\xi-9\sqrt{3})e^{-\xi/\sqrt{3}}

(38)

\displaystyle+\frac{D^{(s)}_{2,k}}{\xi^{5}}(\xi^{3}-9\xi+9\sqrt{3})e^{\xi/\sqrt{3}},

\displaystyle\Phi(\tau,{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{D^{(s)}_{1,k}}{\xi^{5}}(\xi^{3}+6\sqrt{3}\xi^{2}+45\xi+45\sqrt{3})e^{-\xi/\sqrt{3}}

(39)

\displaystyle+\frac{D^{(s)}_{2,k}}{\xi^{5}}(\xi^{3}-6\sqrt{3}\xi^{2}+45\xi-45\sqrt{3})e^{\xi/\sqrt{3}},

where the dimensionless value
\xi=k\tau
was introduced, and
D^{(s)}_{1,k}
and
D^{(s)}_{2,k}
are constants of integration. Here, let us emphasize that values of
D^{(s)}_{1,k}
and
D^{(s)}_{2,k}
have to choose so that initial scalar perturbations are small, i.e.
\Psi_{i,k}\equiv\Psi(\tau_{i},k)\ll 1
and
\Phi_{i,k}\equiv\Phi(\tau_{i},k)\ll 1
, where
\tau_{i}=-{1}/{\textrm{a}_{i}H_{\eta}}
is the initial moment of time (see Eq. (
18
)). In practice, performing numerical calculations, we fix initial values of
\Psi_{i,k}
,
\Phi_{i,k}
, and determine
D^{(s)}_{1,k}
and
D^{(s)}_{2,k}
from Eqs. (
38
), (
39
), assuming
\xi=\xi_{i}=k\tau_{i}
.

Now, let us discuss an asymptotic behavior of scalar modes (
38
) and (
39
). As was mentioned above, initially, at
\tau=\tau_{i}=-{1}/{\textrm{a}_{i}H_{\eta}}
, the scalar perturbation modes are small,
\Psi_{i,k}\ll 1
and
\Phi_{i,k}\ll 1
. During the inflationary stage
\tau
is increasing from
\tau_{i}
to
0
. In the limit
\tau\to 0
, or, equivalently,
\xi\to 0
, the scalar modes (
38
) and (
39
) behave as

\displaystyle\Psi(\tau,k)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{4}{15}(D^{(s)}_{1,k}+D^{(s)}_{2,k})-\frac{9\sqrt{3}}{\xi^{5}}(D^{(s)}_{1,k}-D^{(s)}_{2,k}),

(40)

\displaystyle\Phi(\tau,k)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{45\sqrt{3}}{\xi^{5}}(D^{(s)}_{1,k}-D^{(s)}_{2,k}).

(41)

It is seen that in the very specific case, when
D^{(s)}_{1,k}=D^{(s)}_{2,k}
, the modes
\Psi(\tau,k)
and
\Phi(\tau,k)
remain finite in the limit
\xi\to 0
. However, generally the modes are increasing in this limit and become as large as
\Psi,\Phi\sim(D^{(s)}_{1,k}-D^{(s)}_{2,k})\xi_{e}^{-5}
, where
\xi_{e}=k\tau_{e}
, and
\tau_{e}
is a time when the inflationary stage ends and the post-inflationary one begins.

IV.2
Tensor modes

As usually, an arbitrary transversal traceless tensor
h_{ij}
can be
represented as a linear combination of two basic tensors
e^{(+)}_{ij}
and
e^{(\times)}_{ij}
with helicities
+2
and
-2
, respectively, so that

h_{ij}=\sum_{A=+,\times}e^{(A)}_{ij}h^{(A)},

(42)

where
h^{(A)}
are amplitudes.
Applying the Fourier transformation (
23
) for
h^{(A)}
, one can obtain the following equation for tensor perturbation modes
h^{(A)}(\tau,k)
:

(1+4\pi\eta\textrm{a}^{-2}\phi^{\prime 2})h^{\prime\prime}+2\left(\mathcal{H}+4\pi\eta\textrm{a}^{-2}\phi^{\prime}\phi^{\prime\prime}\right)h^{\prime}+{k^{2}}(1-4\pi\eta\textrm{a}^{-2}\phi^{\prime 2})h=0,

(43)

where for simplicity we have omitted the index
A
.

IV.2.1
Tensor modes in the post-inflationary stage

Neglecting the
\eta
-terms in Eq. (
43
) and substituting
\mathcal{H}=1/2\tau
, we obtain the following equation for tensor modes in the post-inflationary stage:

h^{\prime\prime}+\frac{1}{\tau}h^{\prime}+{k^{2}}h=0.

(44)

Its general solution is

h(\tau,{k})={C^{(t)}_{1,k}}J_{0}(k\tau)+{C^{(t)}_{2,k}}Y_{0}(k\tau),

(45)

where
J_{0}(k\tau)
and
Y_{0}(k\tau)
are the Bessel functions, and
C^{(t)}_{1,k}
and
C^{(t)}_{2,k}
are constants of integration.

For
long-wavelength tensor modes
, when
k\tau\ll 1
, the solution (
45
) reduces to

h(\tau,{k})\approx C^{(t)}_{1,k}+\frac{2}{\pi}C^{(t)}_{2,k}\left(\ln\frac{k\tau}{2}+\gamma\right),

(46)

where
\gamma
is Euler’s constant, and for
short-wavelength tensor modes
, when
k\tau\gg 1
, one has

h(\tau,{k})\propto\frac{1}{(k\tau)^{1/2}}\cos(k\tau+\alpha^{(t)}_{k}),

(47)

where
\alpha^{(t)}_{k}
is an arbitrary phase.

IV.2.2
Tensor modes in the quasi-de Sitter (inflationary) stage

Taking into account that
4\pi\eta\textrm{a}^{-2}\phi^{\prime 2}\gg 1
and substituting into Eq. (
43
) the background expressions for
\textrm{a}(\tau)
,
\mathcal{H}(\tau)
,
\phi(\tau)
given by Eq. (
19
), we obtain the following equation describing the tensor perturbations in the quasi-de Sitter (inflationary) stage:

h^{\prime\prime}+\frac{4}{\tau}h^{\prime}-k^{2}h=0.

(48)

Its general solution reads

h(\tau,k)=\frac{D^{(t)}_{1,k}}{\xi^{3}}(\xi-1)e^{\xi}+\frac{D^{(t)}_{2,k}}{\xi^{3}}(\xi+1)e^{-\xi},

(49)

where
\xi=k\tau
. The constants of integration
D^{(t)}_{1,k}
and
D^{(t)}_{2,k}
are chosen so that initial tensor modes are small,
h_{i,k}\equiv h(\tau_{i},k)\ll 1
, where
\tau_{i}=-1/\textrm{a}_{i}H_{\eta}
.

During the inflationary stage
\tau
is increasing from
\tau_{i}
to 0. In the limit
\tau\to 0
, or, equivalently,
\xi\to 0
, the tensor modes (
49
) behave as

h(\tau,k)\approx\frac{1}{3}(D^{(t)}_{1,k}+D^{(t)}_{2,k})-\frac{1}{\xi^{3}}(D^{(t)}_{1,k}-D^{(t)}_{2,k}).

(50)

Therefore, generally the tensor modes are increasing in the limit
\tau\to 0
and become as large as
h\sim(D^{(t)}_{1,k}-D^{(t)}_{2,k})\xi_{e}^{-3}
, where
\xi_{e}=k\tau_{e}
, and
\tau_{e}
is a time when the inflationary stage ends and the post-inflationary one begins.

IV.3
Vector modes

Let us represent a vector mode as
Q_{i}(\tau,k)=Q(\tau,k)S_{i}
, where
Q(\tau,k)
is a scalar function, and
S_{i}
is a constant vector such that
\Delta S_{i}=k^{2}S_{i}
. Then, the equation for vector modes
Q(\tau,k)
takes the following form:

(1+4\pi\eta\textrm{a}^{-2}\phi^{\prime 2})Q^{\prime}+2(\mathcal{H}+4\pi\eta\textrm{a}^{-2}\phi^{\prime}\phi^{\prime\prime})Q=0.

(51)

IV.3.1
Vector modes in the post-inflationary stage

Neglecting the
\eta
-terms in Eq. (
51
) and substituting
\mathcal{H}=1/2\tau
, we obtain the following equation for vector modes in the post-inflationary stage:

Q^{\prime}+\frac{1}{\tau}Q=0.

(52)

From here we get

Q=\frac{C^{(v)}_{k}}{k\tau}\propto\frac{1}{\textrm{a}^{2}},

(53)

where
C^{(v)}_{k}
is a constant of integration. Therefore, as was expected, the vector modes decay as
\textrm{a}^{-2}
in the post-inflationary stage.

Note that in the framework of GR this fact serves as an argument to ignore completely vector perturbations. Actually, the primordial vector perturbations may have significant amplitudes at present only if they were originally very large. However, in GR there is no reason to expect such large primordial vector perturbations. The situation is changed crucially if one take into account the primary quasi-de Sitter (inflationary) stage, which is realized in the theory of gravity with non-minimal derivative coupling.

IV.3.2
Vector modes in the quasi-de Sitter (inflationary) stage

Taking into account that
4\pi\eta\textrm{a}^{-2}\phi^{\prime 2}\gg 1
and substituting into Eq. (
43
) the background expressions for
\textrm{a}(\tau)
,
\mathcal{H}(\tau)
,
\phi(\tau)
given by Eq. (
19
), we obtain the following equation describing the vector perturbations in the quasi-de Sitter (inflationary) stage:

Q^{\prime}+\frac{4}{\tau}Q=0.

(54)

Its solution is

Q(\tau,k)=\frac{D^{(v)}_{k}}{(k\tau)^{4}},

(55)

where
D^{(v)}_{k}
is a constant of integration, which is chosen so that initial vector modes are small,
Q(\tau_{i},k)\ll 1
, where
\tau_{i}=-1/\textrm{a}_{i}H_{\eta}
. Taking into account that
\textrm{a}(\tau)=-1/H_{\eta}\tau
, one has
Q(\tau,k)\propto\textrm{a}^{4}
. That is the vector modes are increasing as
\textrm{a}^{4}
at the inflationary stage, in distinct with the post-inflationary one, where the modes decaying as
\textrm{a}^{-2}
.
During the inflationary stage
\tau
is increasing from
\tau_{i}
to 0. As
\tau\to 0
, the vector modes are increasing and become as large as
Q(\tau_{e},k)=D^{(v)}_{k}(k\tau_{e})^{-4}
, where
\tau_{e}
is a time when the inflationary stage ends and the post-inflationary one begins. Using the relation
D^{(v)}_{k}=(k\tau_{i})Q(\tau_{i},k)
, we obtain the following estimate:

Q(\tau_{e},k)=\left(\frac{\tau_{i}}{\tau_{e}}\right)^{4}Q(\tau_{i},k).

(56)

Therefore, during the inflationary stage all vector modes, regardless of
k
, are amplified by a factor of
\left({\tau_{i}}/{\tau_{e}}\right)^{4}
. The time
\tau_{e}
, when the inflationary stage ends, depends on parameters of the model and can be obtained numerically.

V
Conclusion

In the paper we have explored perturbations in the isotropic and homogeneous cosmological model with the spatially flat Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker metric in the theory of gravity with non-minimal derivative coupling.
For this aim we have derived a complete set of equations which describe an evolution of scalar, vector and tensor modes of perturbations. All modes was analyzed analytically in two asymptotic stages of the Universe evolution: (i) quasi-de Sitter (inflationary), and (ii) post-inflationary.
Note that the quasi-de Sitter (inflationary) stage is realized on early times, when the non-minimal derivative coupling interaction is dominating. While, the post-inflationary stage is realized on late times, when the influence of non-minimal derivative coupling on the Universe evolution completely disappears, and this naturally leads to the transition to the standard cosmological evolution.

For scalar perturbation modes in the post-inflationary stage we have reproduced the result which is expectable and well-known for cosmology with an ordinary massless scalar field. At the same time, a behavior of all modes (scalar, vector, tensor) in the quasi-de Sitter (inflationary) stage, which is driven by the non-minimal derivative coupling interaction, is completely novel and distinct from that in the standard ‘potential’ inflation.
We found that all modes, including vector ones, are amplified. The degree of amplification is approximately determined by the factor
(\tau_{i}/\tau_{e})^{n}
, where
n=3
for tensor,
n=4
for vector, and
n=5
for scalar modes, where
\tau_{i}
is a time of beginning of the inflationary stage, and
\tau_{e}
is a time when the inflationary stage ends and the post-inflationary one begins. Let us recall that
\tau
is the conformal time,
\tau_{i}=-1/\textrm{a}_{i}H_{\eta}
, and during the inflationary stage
\tau
is increasing from
\tau_{i}
to
\tau_{e}\approx 0
. The time
\tau_{e}
, when the inflationary stage ends, depends on parameters of the model and is obtained numerically.

It is interesting and important to notice the following fact. It is naturally to suppose that all modes of perturbations–scalar, vector, tensor–initially appear at moment of time
\tau_{i}
with approximately equal amplitudes. However, to the end of the inflationary stage, at the time
\tau_{e}
, modes are amplified differently. To characterize this difference, one can introduce two parameters,
r_{k}=h(\tau_{e},k)/\Psi(\tau_{e},k)
and
q_{k}=Q(\tau_{e},k)/\Psi(\tau_{e},k)
. The first is nothing but the tensor-to-scalar ratio – a critical cosmological parameter measuring the amplitude of primordial gravitational waves (tensor perturbations) relative to density fluctuations (scalar perturbations) generated during inflation. While the second is the vector-to-scalar ratio, which is usually does not take into account. The parameter
r_{k}
can be estimated as
r_{k}=(\tau_{e}/\tau_{i})^{2}
. Numerical results give
\tau_{i}\approx-2.79112
and
\tau_{e}\approx-0.25525
, hence
r_{k}\approx 0.0084
. Note that the current limit from combined Planck with BICEP/Keck 2015 data
Tristram:2020
yields an upper limit of
r<0.044
at 95%. Thus, our estimate satisfies the observable constraints. The theoretical estimate for the vector-to-scalar ratio is
q_{k}\approx 0.091
. Here it is worth to recall that we estimate the value of
q_{k}
at the end of the quasi-de Sitter (inflationary) stage and at the beginning of the post-inflationary stage. To characterize a further evolution of the vector-to-tensor ratio parameter
q_{k}
, one needs to take into account not only the non-minimally coupled scalar field, but also an ordinary matter filling the Universe. Anyway, in the post-inflationary stage the vector modes decay as
\tau^{-1}
(see Eq. (
53
)), while the long-wavelength scalar modes with
k\tau\ll 1
are constant (see Eq. (
32
)). Hence, the ratio
q_{\{k\tau\ll 1\}}\sim\tau^{-1}\sim\textrm{a}^{-2}
decays during the post-inflationary stage. On the other hand, the short-wavelength scalar modes with
k\tau\gg 1
behave as
\tau^{-3/2}
(see Eq. (
33
)), and so
q_{\{k\tau\gg 1\}}\sim\tau^{1/2}\sim\textrm{a}(\tau)
is increasing proportionally to the rate of the Universe expansion.
