A general Mayer-Vietoris sequence in algebraic K K -theory
Yakun Zhang

Abstract




Abstract.

This paper investigates the Mayer-Vietoris sequence for the Milnor square. While such sequences often involve elusive intermediate terms, we provide an explicit characterization of the key group
X
in a new, more general variant of the sequence. By identifying
X
as a categorical pullback, we provide a full, constructive proof of the modified Mayer-Vietoris sequence. Furthermore, we show that
X
fits into a structural exact sequence involving the relative
K
-groups
K_{*}(A,B,I)
. Finally, we provide a homotopy-theoretic description of
X
as the homotopy group of a suitable fiber, clarifying its structure, kernel , and image.

algebraic
K
-theory, Mayer-Vietoris sequence, pullback, pushout.

1
Introduction

Consider a Milnor square of associative rings

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A/I}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B/I}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bar{f}}

(1.1)

where
I
is a two-sided ideal of
A
that is mapped isomorphically onto a two-sided ideal of
B
under the ring homomorphism
f
, and the vertical maps are the canonical projections. This pullback square induces a commutative diagram of algebraic
K
-groups:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{i+1}(A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{i+1}(A/I)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{i}(A,I)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{i}(A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{i}(A/I)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{i+1}(B)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{i+1}(B/I)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{i}(B,I)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{i}(B)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{i}(B/I)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha_{i}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varepsilon_{i}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta_{i}}

Here,
\varepsilon_{i}:K_{i}(A,I)\rightarrow K_{i}(B,I)
denotes the
i
-th homomorphism of relative
K
-groups,
\mathrm{ker}(\varepsilon_{i})
is called the
i
-th excision kernel. Since
\varepsilon_{i}
is not always an isomorphism, a Mayer-Vietoris-type long exact sequence cannot be derived directly from this diagram. To address this, Weibel
[
1
, Proposition 5.1]
introduced a modified Mayer-Vietoris sequence for all
i\geq 0
:

\cdots\to K_{i+1}(A/I)\oplus K_{i+1}(B)\rightarrow\mathrm{sub}\text{-}K_{i+1}(B/I)\rightarrow\mathrm{quo}\text{-}K_{i}(A)\rightarrow K_{i}(A/I)\oplus K_{i}(B)\to\cdots

(1.2)

where
\mathrm{sub}\text{-}K_{i+1}(B/I)
denotes the subgroup of
K_{i+1}(B/I)
consisting of elements mapping to
0
under
K_{i+1}(B/I)\rightarrow K_{i-1}(A,B,I)
, and
\mathrm{quo}\text{-}K_{i}(A)
is the quotient of
K_{i}(A)
by the image of
K_{i}(A,B,I)
. However, he did not provide a complete and rigorous proof, indicating the argument only via commutative diagrams and diagram chasing.

In this paper, we re-examine these two groups from a categorical perspective. Specifically,
\mathrm{quo}\text{-}K_{i}(A)=K_{i}(A)/\alpha_{i}\bigl(\ker(\varepsilon_{i})\bigr)
can be characterized as the pushout:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{i}(A,I)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{i}(A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{i}(A,I)/\mathrm{ker}(\varepsilon_{i})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{quo}\text{-}K_{i}(A)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha_{i}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{2}}

(1.3)

Correspondingly,
\mathrm{sub}\text{-}K_{i+1}(B/I)=\{x\in K_{i+1}(B/I)\mid\beta_{i}(x)\in\mathrm{Im}\varepsilon_{i}\}
is the pullback:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{sub}\text{-}K_{i+1}(B/I)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{i}(A,I)/\mathrm{ker}(\varepsilon_{i})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{i+1}(B/I)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{i}(B,I)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta_{i}}

(1.4)

The main contributions of this paper are as follows. First, we re-examine Weibel’s result and provide a full, constructive proof of his variant of the Mayer-Vietoris sequence. Building upon this foundation, we further refine the construction to obtain a new, more general variant of the sequence. Finally, we employ homotopy-theoretic methods to provide a precise characterization of the mysterious third term in our new sequence. By identifying it with the homotopy group of a suitable homotopy fiber, we derive a new exact sequence that thoroughly clarifies its structure, kernel, and image.

2
Generalized Mayer-Vietoris sequence

Throughout this section, we maintain the notation established in the introduction regarding the Milnor square (
1.1
). Specifically, the groups
\mathrm{quo}\text{-}K_{i}(A)
and
\mathrm{sub}\text{-}K_{i+1}(B/I)
are understood as the pushout (
1.3
) and pullback (
1.4
) constructions, respectively.

To prove our main results, we present two key propositions that underlie the following constructions. Their proofs rely only on standard techniques in homological algebra involving exact sequences, pullbacks, and pushouts. Since the arguments are routine and straightforward, we omit the detailed proofs and leave them to the reader. These propositions will be used to provide a rigorous diagram-theoretic proof for Weibel’s Mayer-Vietoris sequence (
1.2
). Building upon this foundation, we present our main result: a more general variant of the Mayer-Vietoris sequence that clarifies the internal structure of these terms.

In the following, a sequence of
R
-modules

and
C

Theorem 2.3
.

The Mayer-Vietoris sequence associated with the Milnor square (
1.1
) admits a more general variant of the form

\cdots\rightarrow K_{i+1}(A/I)\oplus K_{i+1}(B)\rightarrow X_{i}\rightarrow K_{i}(A)\rightarrow K_{i}(A/I)\oplus K_{i}(B)\rightarrow\cdots,

(2.1)

where
X_{i}
is uniquely characterized as the pullback of
\bar{\partial}_{i}:\text{sub-}K_{i+1}(B/I)\rightarrow\text{quo-}K_{i}(A)
and
\pi_{i}:K_{i}(A)\rightarrow\text{quo-}K_{i}(A)
. Furthermore,
X_{i}
fits into an additional exact sequence

\cdots\rightarrow K_{i}(A,B,I)\rightarrow X_{i}\xrightarrow{\varphi_{i}}K_{i+1}(B/I)\rightarrow K_{i-1}(A,B,I)\rightarrow\cdots,

(2.2)

with
\ker\,\varphi_{i}=\alpha_{i}\bigl(\ker(\varepsilon_{i})\bigr)
and
\text{im}\,\varphi_{i}=\text{sub-}K_{i+1}(B/I)
.

Proof
.

To establish the theorem, we proceed in three key steps: first, we rigorously verify the exactness of Weibel’s variant (
1.2
); second, we construct the generalized Mayer-Vietoris sequence (
2.1
) using the pullback characterization of
X_{i}
; and finally, we analyze the internal structure of
X_{i}
by relating it to the homotopy fiber of a suitable map.

Step 1: Exactness of Weibel’s Variant (
1.2
)

We begin by proving the exactness of Weibel’s variant (
1.2
), which serves as a foundational step for our subsequent arguments. As noted in the introduction,
\text{quo-}K_{i}(A)
is understood as a pushout; combining this characterization with Proposition

, we obtain a commutative diagram with exact rows, as shown below:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{i+1}(A/I)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{i}(A,I)/\ker(\varepsilon_{i})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{quo-}K_{i}(A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{i}(A/I)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha_{i}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{2}}

Similarly, leveraging the pullback understanding of

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{i+1}(B)}

Step 2: Construction of the Generalized Mayer-Vietoris Sequence (
2.1
)

Next, we define
X_{i}
as the pullback of the homomorphisms
\bar{\partial}_{i}:\text{sub-}K_{i+1}(B/I)\rightarrow\text{quo-}K_{i}(A)
and
\pi_{i}:K_{i}(A)\rightarrow\text{quo-}K_{i}(A)
, which uniquely characterizes

as stated in the theorem. By Proposition

(a),

fits into the top row of the following commutative diagram:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{i+1}(A/I)\oplus K_{i+1}(B)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{i}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{i}(A)}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{i+1}(A/I)\oplus K_{i+1}(B)}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K(A,B,I)}
