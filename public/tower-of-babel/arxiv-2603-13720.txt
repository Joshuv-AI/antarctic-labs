1
Introduction

The classical Legendre formula describes the
p
-adic valuation of the factorial function
n!
. For a prime
p
one has

v_{p}(n!)=\sum_{k\geq 0}\left\lfloor\frac{n}{p^{k}}\right\rfloor.

The Legendre formula plays a central role in multiplicative number theory and has inspired various generalizations in which the denominators
p^{k}
are replaced by other arithmetic sequences attached to primes—such constructions give rise to generalized factorial functions whose growth properties reflect the arithmetic structure of the underlying sequence. In particular, factorials defined by Legendre-type formulas appear naturally in the study of generalized factorial functions on Dedekind domains. From previous work
[
1
]
, the author studied the asymptotic behaviors for a class of such factorial constructions by analyzing the structure of Chebyshev-like functions. The purpose of the present paper is to develop a Dirichlet-series framework that explains these asymptotics in a more conceptual analytic setting.

Let
K
be a number field with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, and let
\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}
denote the absolute norm of a nonzero prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}
. Given a function

f:\{\text{prime ideals of }\mathcal{O}_{K}\setminus S\}\to\mathbf{Z}_{\geq 1},

we define a generalized factorial
n!_{K,f}
by prescribing the valuations

v_{\mathfrak{p}}(n!_{K,f})=\sum_{k\geq 0}\left\lfloor\frac{n}{f(\mathfrak{p})\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{k}}\right\rfloor.

This construction extends the classical Legendre formula and includes several previously studied factorial-type functions as special cases. The growth of
n!_{K,f}
is governed by the increment

B_{K,f,S}(n)=\log\!\left(\frac{n!_{K,f}}{(n-1)!_{K,f}}\right),

whose summatory function determines the asymptotic behavior of the factorial.

A key observation of the present paper is that
B_{K,f,S}(n)
admits a natural Dirichlet series representation. Indeed, one finds that

D_{K,f,S}(s)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{B_{K,f,S}(n)}{n^{s}}=\zeta(s)\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\notin S}\frac{\log\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}}{f(\mathfrak{p})^{s}(1-(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p})^{-s})}.

This representation reveals a close connection with the logarithmic derivative of the Dedekind zeta function

-\frac{\zeta_{K,S}^{\prime}(s)}{\zeta_{K,S}(s)}=\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\notin S}\frac{\log\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}}{(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p})^{s}(1-(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p})^{-s})}.

Under mild growth assumptions on
f(\mathfrak{p})
, the Dirichlet series
D_{K,f,S}(s)
decomposes as

D_{K,f,S}(s)=\zeta(s)\left(c^{-s}\!\left(-\frac{\zeta_{K,S}^{\prime}(s)}{\zeta_{K,S}(s)}\right)+J_{K,f,S}(s)\right),

where
J_{K,f,S}(s)
is holomorphic in a half-plane containing
s=1
. Consequently
D_{K,f,S}(s)
possesses a double pole at
s=1
, and this analytic structure governs the asymptotic behavior of the summatory function

S_{K,f,S}(x)=\sum_{n\leq x}B_{K,f,S}(n).

We state the main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
K
be a number field and
S
a finite set of prime ideals of
K
. Suppose
f
is a function on the prime ideals of
K\setminus S
satisfying

f(\mathfrak{p})=c\,\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}+O\!\bigl((\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p})^{1-\delta}\bigr)

for some constants
c>0
and
\delta>0
. Define the generalized factorial by

v_{\mathfrak{p}}(n!_{K,f})=\sum_{k\geq 0}\left\lfloor\frac{n}{f(\mathfrak{p})\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{k}}\right\rfloor\qquad(\mathfrak{p}\notin S),

and let

B_{K,f,S}(n)=\log\!\left(\frac{n!_{K,f}}{(n-1)!_{K,f}}\right).

Then the summatory function

S_{K,f,S}(x)=\sum_{n\leq x}B_{K,f,S}(n)

satisfies

S_{K,f,S}(x)=\frac{1}{c}x\log x+C_{K,f,S}x+O\!\bigl(xe^{-c_{1}\sqrt{\log x}}\bigr)

for some constants
C_{K,f,S}\in\mathbb{R}
and
c_{1}>0
, up to a possible additional term
C_{K}^{\ast}x^{\beta_{K}}
if the Dedekind zeta function
\zeta_{K}(s)
has an exceptional real zero
\beta_{K}\in(0,1)
.

The constants
C_{K,f,S}
depend on
K
,
f
, and the finite set
S
; removing finitely many primes affects only the constant term in the asymptotic and does not alter the main term. Nonetheless, the key observation is that the increment admits a natural Dirichlet series representation, i.e.,

B_{K,f,S}(n)=\log\!\left(\frac{n!_{K,f}}{(n-1)!_{K,f}}\right)\implies D_{K,f,S}(s)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{B_{K,f,S}(n)}{n^{s}}=\zeta(s)H_{K,f,S}(s),

where
H_{K,f,S}(s)
can be decomposed into a multiple of the logarithmic derivative of the Dedekind zeta function together with a holomorphic correction term; this decomposition shows that
D_{K,f,S}(s)
possesses a double pole at
s=1
. Applying Perron’s formula and shifting contours then yields the stated asymptotic for the summatory function.

2
Notation and Preliminaries

Throughout the paper
K
denotes a number field of degree
n=[K:\mathbf{Q}]
, and
\mathcal{O}_{K}
denotes its ring of integers. If
\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}
is a nonzero prime ideal, we write

\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}:=|\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{p}|

for its absolute norm. Let
S
be a finite set of prime ideals of
K
. Unless otherwise specified, sums over prime ideals will be taken over primes
\mathfrak{p}\notin S
.

2.1
Rings of
S
-integers

The ring of
S
-integers of
K
is defined by

\mathcal{O}_{K,S}=\{x\in K:v_{\mathfrak{p}}(x)\geq 0\text{ for all }\mathfrak{p}\notin S\}=\mathcal{O}_{K}[S^{-1}],

or, equivalently, the localization of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
obtained by inverting the primes in
S
. The rings
\mathcal{O}_{K,S}
are Dedekind domains, and every Dedekind domain finitely generated as a
\mathbf{Z}
-algebra arises in this way.

2.2
Dedekind zeta functions

The Dedekind zeta function of
K
is defined for
\Re(s)>1
by the Euler product

\zeta_{K}(s)=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}(1-\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{-s})^{-1}.

The logarithmic derivative satisfies

-\frac{\zeta_{K}^{\prime}}{\zeta_{K}}(s)=\sum_{\mathfrak{p}}\frac{\log\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}}{(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p})^{s}-1}.

If
S
is a finite set of prime ideals, the associated
S
-zeta function is

\zeta_{K,S}(s)=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\notin S}(1-\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{-s})^{-1}=\zeta_{K}(s)\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in S}(1-\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{-s}),

since removing finitely many Euler factors affects only lower-order terms in the asymptotic formulas that arise later.

2.3
Analytic properties

The Dedekind zeta function admits a meromorphic continuation to the complex plane with a simple pole at
s=1
. Moreover,

-\frac{\zeta_{K}^{\prime}}{\zeta_{K}}(s)=\frac{1}{s-1}+O(1)\qquad(s\to 1).

The function
\zeta_{K}(s)
satisfies a zero-free region of the form

\Re(s)\geq 1-\frac{c}{\log(D_{K}(|t|+3)^{n})}

except possibly for a single real zero
\beta_{K}\in(0,1)
, where
D_{K}
denotes the absolute discriminant of
K
.

2.4
Perron’s formula

Let

F(s)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{a(n)}{n^{s}}

be a Dirichlet series that converges absolutely for
\Re(s)>1
. Let

A(x)=\sum_{n\leq x}a(n).

For
b>1
and
T\geq 2
, inspired by
[
5
]
, the core proof relies on the truncated Perron’s formula

A(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{b-iT}^{b+iT}F(s)\frac{x^{s}}{s}\,ds+O\!\left(\frac{x^{b}}{T}\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{|a(n)|}{n^{b}}\right).

2.5
Notation

Throughout the paper we use the standard Landau symbols
O(\cdot)
and
\ll
. The implied constants may depend on the number field
K
, the function
f
, and the set
S
, but are independent of the variable
x
.

3
Generalized Legendre Factorials

Let
K
be a number field with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, and let
S
be a finite set of prime ideals of
K
. Throughout this section sums over prime ideals are taken over
\mathfrak{p}\notin S
.

Let
f
be a function defined on the prime ideals of
K\setminus S
with values in the positive integers.

Definition 3.1
.

For
n\geq 1
and for each prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}\notin S
, define

v_{\mathfrak{p}}(n!_{K,f})=\sum_{k\geq 0}\left\lfloor\frac{n}{f(\mathfrak{p})\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{k}}\right\rfloor.

Since the quantities
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(n!_{K,f})
vanish for all sufficiently large
\mathfrak{p}
, this determines uniquely an ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{K,S}
, which we denote by
n!_{K,f}
.

Definition 3.2
.

Define the increment

B_{K,f,S}(n)=\log\!\left(\frac{n!_{K,f}}{(n-1)!_{K,f}}\right).

The asymptotic behavior of the factorial is governed by the summatory function

S_{K,f,S}(x)=\sum_{n\leq x}B_{K,f,S}(n).

Proposition 3.3
.

For every
n\geq 1
,

B_{K,f,S}(n)=\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\notin S}\log\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}\sum_{k\geq 0}\mathbf{1}_{\,f(\mathfrak{p})\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{k}\mid n}.

Proof.

From the definition,

v_{\mathfrak{p}}(n!_{K,f})=\sum_{k\geq 0}\left\lfloor\frac{n}{f(\mathfrak{p})\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{k}}\right\rfloor.

Hence

v_{\mathfrak{p}}\!\left(\frac{n!_{K,f}}{(n-1)!_{K,f}}\right)=\sum_{k\geq 0}\left(\left\lfloor\frac{n}{f(\mathfrak{p})\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{k}}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{f(\mathfrak{p})\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{k}}\right\rfloor\right).

For a fixed integer
m
, one has

\left\lfloor\frac{n}{m}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{m}\right\rfloor=\begin{cases}1&m\mid n,\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Applying this identity with
m=f(\mathfrak{p})\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{k}
yields

v_{\mathfrak{p}}\!\left(\frac{n!_{K,f}}{(n-1)!_{K,f}}\right)=\sum_{k\geq 0}\mathbf{1}_{\,f(\mathfrak{p})\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{k}\mid n}.

Taking logarithms gives the result.
∎

4
The Associated Dirichlet Series

In this section, we derive a Dirichlet series representation for the increment function
B_{K,f,S}(n)
—this representation will allow us to study the asymptotic behavior of the summatory function

S_{K,f,S}(x)=\sum_{n\leq x}B_{K,f,S}(n)

using analytic methods.

Definition 4.1
.

Define the Dirichlet series

D_{K,f,S}(s)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{B_{K,f,S}(n)}{n^{s}}.

Proposition 4.2
.

For
\Re(s)>1
,

D_{K,f,S}(s)=\zeta(s)\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\notin S}\frac{\log\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}}{f(\mathfrak{p})^{s}(1-(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p})^{-s})}.

Proof.

Substituting the expression for
B_{K,f,S}(n)
into the Dirichlet series gives

D_{K,f,S}(s)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n^{s}}\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\notin S}\log\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}\sum_{k\geq 0}\mathbf{1}_{\,f(\mathfrak{p})\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{k}\mid n}.

Since the series converges absolutely for
\Re(s)>1
, we may interchange the order of summation; this yields

D_{K,f,S}(s)=\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\notin S}\log\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}\sum_{k\geq 0}\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{\,f(\mathfrak{p})\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{k}\mid n}}{n^{s}}.

If
m=f(\mathfrak{p})\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{k}
, then

\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{m\mid n}}{n^{s}}=\sum_{r\geq 1}\frac{1}{(mr)^{s}}=\frac{\zeta(s)}{m^{s}}.

Applying this identity gives

D_{K,f,S}(s)=\zeta(s)\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\notin S}\log\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{1}{(f(\mathfrak{p})\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{k})^{s}}.

The inner sum is geometric, so

\sum_{k\geq 0}(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p})^{-ks}=\frac{1}{1-(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p})^{-s}}.

Substituting this completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

The Dirichlet series
D_{K,f,S}(s)
factors naturally as

D_{K,f,S}(s)=\zeta(s)\,H_{K,f,S}(s),

where

H_{K,f,S}(s)=\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\notin S}\frac{\log\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}}{f(\mathfrak{p})^{s}(1-(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p})^{-s})}.

5
Comparison with the Logarithmic Derivative of
\zeta_{K}(s)

The growth condition on
f(\mathfrak{p})
suggests that the function

H_{K,f,S}(s)=\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\notin S}\frac{\log\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}}{f(\mathfrak{p})^{s}(1-(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p})^{-s})}

should be compared with

c^{-s}\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\notin S}\frac{\log\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}}{(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p})^{s}(1-(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p})^{-s})}=c^{-s}\left(-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}_{K,S}}{\zeta_{K,S}}(s)\right).

Lemma 5.1
.

Assume

f(\mathfrak{p})=c\,\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}+O\!\bigl((\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p})^{1-\delta}\bigr)\qquad(c>0,\ \delta>0).

Then for every prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}\notin S
,

\frac{1}{f(\mathfrak{p})^{s}(1-(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p})^{-s})}=\frac{c^{-s}}{(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p})^{s}(1-(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p})^{-s})}+R_{\mathfrak{p}}(s),

where, for every
\sigma_{0}>1-\delta
,

R_{\mathfrak{p}}(s)\ll\frac{1}{(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p})^{\sigma_{0}+\delta}}

uniformly on compact subsets of
\Re(s)\geq\sigma_{0}
.

Proof.

Write

f(\mathfrak{p})=c\,\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}(1+\varepsilon_{\mathfrak{p}}),\qquad\varepsilon_{\mathfrak{p}}=O\!\bigl((\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p})^{-\delta}\bigr).

Then

\frac{1}{f(\mathfrak{p})^{s}}=\frac{c^{-s}}{(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p})^{s}}(1+\varepsilon_{\mathfrak{p}})^{-s},

so

\frac{1}{f(\mathfrak{p})^{s}(1-(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p})^{-s})}-\frac{c^{-s}}{(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p})^{s}(1-(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p})^{-s})}=\frac{c^{-s}}{(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p})^{s}(1-(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p})^{-s})}\bigl((1+\varepsilon_{\mathfrak{p}})^{-s}-1\bigr).

On compact subsets of
\Re(s)\geq\sigma_{0}>1-\delta
,

(1+\varepsilon_{\mathfrak{p}})^{-s}-1=O(\varepsilon_{\mathfrak{p}})=O\!\bigl((\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p})^{-\delta}\bigr),

and

|1-(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p})^{-s}|^{-1}\ll 1.

Therefore

R_{\mathfrak{p}}(s)\ll\frac{1}{(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p})^{\sigma_{0}+\delta}}.

∎

Proposition 5.2
.

Define

J_{K,f,S}(s):=H_{K,f,S}(s)-c^{-s}\!\left(-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}_{K,S}}{\zeta_{K,S}}(s)\right).

Then
J_{K,f,S}(s)
is holomorphic on the half-plane

\Re(s)>1-\delta.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.1
,

J_{K,f,S}(s)=\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\notin S}\log\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}\left(\frac{1}{f(\mathfrak{p})^{s}(1-(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p})^{-s})}-\frac{c^{-s}}{(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p})^{s}(1-(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p})^{-s})}\right),

and the
\mathfrak{p}
-th summand satisfies

\ll\frac{\log\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}}{(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p})^{\sigma_{0}+\delta}}

uniformly on compact subsets of
\Re(s)\geq\sigma_{0}
, where
\sigma_{0}>1-\delta
. Since
\sigma_{0}+\delta>1
, the series converges absolutely and locally uniformly on
\Re(s)>1-\delta
. Hence
J_{K,f,S}
is holomorphic there.
∎

Corollary 5.3
.

For
\Re(s)>1
,

D_{K,f,S}(s)=\zeta(s)\left(c^{-s}\!\left(-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}_{K,S}}{\zeta_{K,S}}(s)\right)+J_{K,f,S}(s)\right).

6
Laurent Expansion of the Dirichlet Series

In this section we determine the behavior of the Dirichlet series
D_{K,f,S}(s)
near
s=1
. This will reveal the pole structure responsible for the asymptotic growth of the summatory function.

Lemma 6.1
.

As
s\to 1
,

-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}_{K,S}}{\zeta_{K,S}}(s)=\frac{1}{s-1}+O(1).

Proof.

The function
\zeta_{K,S}(s)
has a simple pole at
s=1
. Writing

\zeta_{K,S}(s)=\frac{A}{s-1}+B+O(s-1),

we obtain

-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}_{K,S}}{\zeta_{K,S}}(s)=\frac{1}{s-1}+O(1).\qed

Proposition 6.2
.

The Dirichlet series
D_{K,f,S}(s)
has a double pole at
s=1
. More precisely,

D_{K,f,S}(s)=\frac{1/c}{(s-1)^{2}}+\frac{A_{K,f,S}}{s-1}+O(1)\qquad(s\to 1),

for some constant
A_{K,f,S}
.

Proof.

Using Lemma
6.1
we write

-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}_{K,S}}{\zeta_{K,S}}(s)=\frac{1}{s-1}+a_{K,S}+O(s-1)

for some constant
a_{K,S}
.

Also

c^{-s}=c^{-1}\bigl(1-(s-1)\log c+O((s-1)^{2})\bigr)

and

J_{K,f,S}(s)=J_{K,f,S}(1)+O(s-1).

Thus

H_{K,f,S}(s)=\frac{1}{c(s-1)}+B_{K,f,S}+O(s-1)

for some constant
B_{K,f,S}
.

Since

\zeta(s)=\frac{1}{s-1}+\gamma+O(s-1),

multiplying gives

D_{K,f,S}(s)=\frac{1/c}{(s-1)^{2}}+\frac{A_{K,f,S}}{s-1}+O(1).\qed

Corollary 6.3
.

The residue of

D_{K,f,S}(s)\frac{x^{s}}{s}

at
s=1
is

\frac{1}{c}x\log x+C_{K,f,S}x,

where

C_{K,f,S}:=A_{K,f,S}-\frac{1}{c}.

Proof.

Write

\frac{x^{s}}{s}=x\bigl(1+(s-1)\log x+O((s-1)^{2})\bigr)\bigl(1-(s-1)+O((s-1)^{2})\bigr).

Multiplying this with the Laurent expansion from Proposition
6.2
, the coefficient of
(s-1)^{-1}
is

\frac{1}{c}x\log x+\left(A_{K,f,S}-\frac{1}{c}\right)x.

This proves the claim.
∎

7
Perron’s Formula and the Proof of the Main Theorem

In view of Corollary
6.3
, the main term in the asymptotic expansion of

S_{K,f,S}(x)=\sum_{n\leq x}B_{K,f,S}(n)

is determined by the residue of

D_{K,f,S}(s)\frac{x^{s}}{s}

at
s=1
. We now recover this term and estimate the remainder by shifting the contour in Perron’s formula.

Proposition 7.1

(Perron’s formula)
.

For
b>1
and
T\geq 2
,

S_{K,f,S}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{b-iT}^{b+iT}D_{K,f,S}(s)\frac{x^{s}}{s}\,ds+O\!\left(\frac{x^{b}}{T}D_{K,f,S}(b)\right).

In particular, with

b=1+\frac{1}{\log x},

one has

S_{K,f,S}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{b-iT}^{b+iT}D_{K,f,S}(s)\frac{x^{s}}{s}\,ds+O\!\left(\frac{x(\log x)^{2}}{T}\right).

Proof.

Apply truncated Perron’s formula from Section 2. Since Proposition
6.2
implies
D_{K,f,S}(b)\ll(b-1)^{-2}
, the second estimate follows for
b=1+1/\log x
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By Proposition
7.1
,

S_{K,f,S}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{b-iT}^{b+iT}D_{K,f,S}(s)\frac{x^{s}}{s}\,ds+O\!\left(\frac{x(\log x)^{2}}{T}\right),

where
b=1+1/\log x
.

We shift the contour to

\Re(s)=1-\eta(T),\qquad\eta(T)=\frac{a}{\log(D_{K}(T+3)^{n})},

using the zero-free region for
\zeta_{K}(s)
. The poles crossed are the double pole at
s=1
and possibly a simple pole at an exceptional zero
\beta_{K}
of
\zeta_{K}(s)
.

The residue at
s=1
is given by Corollary
6.3
:

\operatorname{Res}_{s=1}\left(D_{K,f,S}(s)\frac{x^{s}}{s}\right)=\frac{1}{c}x\log x+C_{K,f,S}x.

If
\zeta_{K}(s)
has an exceptional real zero
\beta_{K}
, then

-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}_{K,S}}{\zeta_{K,S}}(s)

has a simple pole there, and thus
D_{K,f,S}(s)x^{s}/s
contributes an additional term
C_{K}^{\ast}x^{\beta_{K}}
for some nonzero constant
C_{K}^{\ast}
.

It remains to bound the shifted contour. On the contour,

D_{K,f,S}(s)=\zeta(s)\left(c^{-s}\!\left(-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}_{K,S}}{\zeta_{K,S}}(s)\right)+J_{K,f,S}(s)\right).

Since
J_{K,f,S}(s)
is holomorphic on
\Re(s)>1-\delta
, it is bounded on the compact vertical strips under consideration. Standard zero-free-region bounds imply

D_{K,f,S}(s)\ll(\log T)^{A}

for some
A>0
on the shifted contour.

On the vertical segment
\Re(s)=1-\eta(T)
,

|x^{s}|=x^{1-\eta(T)}=x\exp\!\bigl(-\eta(T)\log x\bigr),

so

\int_{1-\eta(T)-iT}^{1-\eta(T)+iT}D_{K,f,S}(s)\frac{x^{s}}{s}\,ds\ll x^{1-\eta(T)}(\log T)^{A+1}.

On the horizontal segments,

|x^{s}|\leq x^{b}\ll x,\qquad|s|^{-1}\ll T^{-1},

hence their total contribution is

O\!\left(\frac{x(\log T)^{A}}{T}\right).

Choose

T=\exp(\sqrt{\log x}).

Then

\log T=\sqrt{\log x},\qquad\eta(T)\asymp\frac{1}{\sqrt{\log x}},

since
K
is fixed. Thus

x\exp\!\bigl(-\eta(T)\log x\bigr)=xe^{-c_{1}\sqrt{\log x}}

for some
c_{1}>0
, and also

T^{-1}=e^{-\sqrt{\log x}}.

Therefore every error term is

O\!\bigl(xe^{-c_{1}\sqrt{\log x}}\bigr).

Combining all contributions yields

S_{K,f,S}(x)=\frac{1}{c}x\log x+C_{K,f,S}x+\mathbf{1}_{\{\beta_{K}\}}\,C_{K}^{\ast}x^{\beta_{K}}+O\!\bigl(xe^{-c_{1}\sqrt{\log x}}\bigr),

which proves the theorem.
∎

.

Let

such that
q_{i}
, the number of classes

modulo
\mathfrak{M}^{i}
, factors as
q_{i}=f(\mathfrak{p})\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{p}^{i})
; then there exists constants
a_{D,S},C_{D,S}
such that

\log n!_{D,S}=a_{D,S}n\log n+C_{D,S}n+O\!\bigl(ne^{-c_{1}\sqrt{\log n}}\bigr),

up to a possible additional term
C_{D,S}^{\ast}x^{\beta_{D,S}}
coming from an exceptional real zero
\beta_{K}\in(0,1)
of the Dedekind zeta function
\zeta_{K}
.

8
Examples and Remarks

Remark 8.1
.

If
S=\varnothing
, then the preceding argument applies with
\zeta_{K,S}=\zeta_{K}
. Since removing finitely many primes alters the Dirichlet series by a finite sum, the general
S
-integer case differs only in the lower-order constants.

Remark 8.2
.

In the special case
K=\mathbf{Q}
,
S=\varnothing
, and
f(p)=p-1
, one recovers the Legendre-type factorial studied in
[
1
]
. The present Dirichlet-series approach provides an alternative analytic proof of the asymptotic behavior obtained there and yields a sharper remainder term.

Remark 8.3
.

For
K=\mathbf{Q}
and
f(p)=1
, the valuation rule in Definition 3.1 recovers the classical Legendre formula for the ordinary factorial function.
