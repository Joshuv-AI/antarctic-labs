Analogies between Transformer Layers and Power Method
Chenglong Li, Claudio Altafini

Abstract
In the paper we show that there is an analogy between the operations occurring in a layer of a transformer (projections and layer normalizations, disregarding the feedforward neural network) and a step in the power method. Coherently with this analogy, we show that passing through a layer the tokens tend to be tilted towards the principal eigenvector of a matrix which is the product of the output and value weight matrices of that layer. In the special case of a transformer with shared weights (i.e., in which all layers have identical weights) then the alignment with this principal eigenvector is particularly evident empirically, and can also be shown analytically. The analogy also suggests a method to steer the output of the transformer towards an arbitrary desired direction in token space.



1
Introduction

The power method is a simple and efficient numerical method to compute the principal eigenvector of a matrix, say
F
. It works by iteratively multiplying a vector
\bm{x}(t)
with
F
and then normalizing the resulting new vector:
\bm{x}(t+1)=\frac{F\bm{x}(t)}{\|F\bm{x}(t)\|}
, with
t
a step counter (i.e., time). If
F
has a strictly dominant real eigenvalue, say
\lambda_{1}
, then the process initialized in a random vector generically converges to the associated eigenvector, say
\bm{v}_{1}
, i.e.,
\bm{x}(t)\to\bm{v}_{1}
as the number of steps
t
grows.

In this paper we show that the operations occurring in a transformer have analogies with the power method, provided we think of a transformer as the unfolding in time of a discrete-time dynamical system in which time is the layer index.
Such interpretation is by now well-known
[
9
,
27
,
20
]
and has been exploited in several papers
[
2
,
12
,
11
,
13
,
35
]
.
Most of the literature viewing a transformer as a dynamical system focuses on the self-attention mechanism only, viewed as a continuous-time time-invariant system, often limited to query, key and value matrices having a special form, and even more often dealing with a single head.
The asymptotic behavior of such dynamical system is investigated for instance in
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
13
]
, where it is shown that the generic behavior of a transformer (in the infinite depth limit) is to converge to a consensus point, in which all tokens become equal.
This phenomenon is predicted by the mathematical models and it is also known experimentally under the names of oversmoothing, rank collapse, or token-uniformity
[
7
,
8
,
23
,
22
,
28
,
30
,
38
]
.

If consensus (i.e., token oversmoothing in depth) has been confirmed by several empirical and theoretical studies, much less is known regarding what is the vector to which all tokens converge to, and what interpretation does this vector have in practical transformers.
In was shown in
[
3
]
that for the continuous-time single-head self-attention model, all eigenvectors of the value matrix are equilibria of the system, and that most frequently convergence occurs towards the principal eigenvector of such matrix.

In this paper these properties are investigated in discrete-time and for multihead systems. We obtain complete analytical characterizations of the convergence to the principal eigenvector for special cases (full attention, symmetric value matrices) and numerical results for more general cases (causal attention, non-symmetric weight matrices).
More importantly, we show that for off-the-shelf transformers like GPT2
[
25
]
, GPTNeo
[
10
]
, BERT
[
6
]
and ALBERT (“A Lite BERT”, a variant of BERT with shared weights across all its layers
[
17
]
), the theoretical results have predictive power and the aforementioned analogy with the power method makes sense, provided we use the
L_{2}
norm and disregard the feedforward neural network (FFN) downstream the attention block in each layer.

In particular, a transformer like ALBERT, in which the query, key, value and output weight matrices are identical in all layers, corresponds to a time-invariant discrete-time dynamical system like the one we study analytically, and, in fact, we confirm empirically that (if we use the
L_{2}
norm and disregard the FFN) the consensus value is the one predicted by the model: all tokens converge to the principal eigenvector
\bm{v}_{1}
associated to a “layer matrix”
F
corresponding to the product of value and output weight matrices.
For such “universal transformer”, each layer can be seen as a “perturbed” projection of the tokens on the eigenspaces of a matrix like
F
, followed by a layer normalization, where the perturbation is given by the attention matrix.
When the tokens converge to consensus, then a full attention matrix becomes the uniform matrix, and the aforementioned perturbation vanishes as the tokens are mapped through the successive identical layers of the transformer.
The projection action along
\bm{v}_{1}
becomes more and more evident as the number of layers increases.
For such class of transformers, the analogy with the power method is particularly evident.

When the weight matrices differ from layer to layer, as in most commonly used transformers, then the dynamical system becomes time-varying (or layer-varying) and the projection effect is less evident, but the basic principle remains. Each layer can still be seen as one step of the power method, but since the associated matrix
F
changes from layer to layer, i.e.,
F=F_{t}
with
t
the layer index, perfect alignment along the corresponding dominant eigenvector
\bm{v}_{1,t}
is not achieved, because also
\bm{v}_{1,t}
changes from layer to layer.
Nevertheless what can be shown is that if we look at statistics over many tokens (in order to factor out the “perturbation” due to the attention coefficients) and disregard the FFN, then with the
L_{2}
normalization at each layer the projection of the tokens along the current
\bm{v}_{1,t}
typically increases as the tokens pass through the
t
-th layer, i.e., in probability the correlation between tokens and
\bm{v}_{1,t}
before the
t
-th layer is typically lower than the same correlation after the layer.
At each layer, the matrix
F_{t}
for which this occurs is a product between the output weight matrix and the concatenation of the value weight matrices of the various heads.
This increase in alignment is precisely the spirit of the power method: the dominant direction is amplified with respect to all other directions.
We show that this is occurring in nearly all layers of real transformers like GPT2, GPTNeo and BERT, even though the alignment with this dominant direction remains typically weak, because the layer matrices of these transformers have very small spectral gaps between principal and second eigenvalue.

The insight we acquire by the analogy with the power method suggests however a simple method for obtaining a very strong alignment with a desired eigenspace, and thereby for steering the tokens towards an arbitrary direction in token space. Given a transformer with layer-varying weights, it is enough to modify the last layer by adding a properly chosen rank-1 perturbation to
F_{t}
. If this perturbation is sufficiently large, it creates a spectral gap in the modified layer matrix, and the tokens align themselves with the new principal eigenvector induced by this rank-1 perturbation. This eigenvector can be chosen by the designer, meaning that the tokens can be steered essentially in any arbitrary direction in the space of token vector representations.

Steering the output of a large language model toward a desired token alignment can be done at various levels: by intervening on the prompts
[
31
,
21
,
29
]
, at the embedding level (e.g. “soft-prompting”
[
18
]
), at the activation level
[
5
,
32
]
or on the weights. The latter approach, which is closest in spirit to our work, can be attained e.g. by fine-tuning, weight editing, or iterative refinement via self-feedback
[
15
,
16
,
37
]
. None of these methods, however, specifies how to mechanistically modify the layers of a transformer in order to achieve a desired alignment, as we do here. Our methodology suggests an alternative principled way to steer the output of a large language model.

2
Model formulation

Following e.g.
[
1
,
13
]
, we aim at representing a transformer as a discrete-time dynamical system with time given by the layer index
t=1,\ldots,T
, where
T
is the number of layers.
Using a column vector representation for the tokens,
\bm{x}_{i}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
, the update law for each token on a layer of a multihead transformer, including the skip connection and layer normalization, and excluding the FFN can be compactly written as:

\bm{x}_{i}(t+1)=\text{LayerNorm}\left(\bm{x}_{i}(t)+F_{O,t}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\bar{F}_{V,t}^{(h)}\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{t,ij}^{(h)}(\bm{x}(t))\bm{x}_{j}(t)\right),\quad\begin{array}[]{l}i=1,\ldots,n,\\
t=1,\ldots,T,\end{array}

(1)

where
A_{t}^{(h)}(\bm{x}(t))
is the attention matrix of each head
h=1,\ldots,H
, (a function of all tokens
\bm{x}(t)
and of the query and key weight matrices) and the matrices
F_{O,t}
and
\bar{F}_{V,t}^{(h)}
are obtained from the learned output weight matrices and value weight matrices of each head.
In particular, if
W_{O,t}
and
W_{V,t}^{(h)}
are the learned output and value weight matrices in the standard transformer formulation
[
34
]
, then

F_{O,t}=(W_{O,t})^{T},\qquad\bar{F}_{V,t}^{(1)}=\begin{bmatrix}(W_{V,t}^{(1)})^{T}\\
0\\
\vdots\\
0\end{bmatrix},\ldots,\bar{F}_{V,t}^{(H)}=\begin{bmatrix}0\\
0\\
\vdots\\
(W_{V,t}^{(H)})^{T}\end{bmatrix},

where the transpose originates from the column-token convention we use. See a detailed formulation in Section
A
of the Supplementary Materials.

If we disregard attention (which varies from token to token) the matrix that lumps together the contribution of the
H
heads occurring in the transformer layer is the following “layer matrix”

F_{t}=F_{O,t}F_{V,t}\quad\text{with}\quad F_{V,t}=\sum_{h=1}^{H}\bar{F}_{V,t}^{(h)}.

Let
\lambda_{1,t},\ldots,\lambda_{d,t}
be the eigenvalues of
F_{t}
, ordered as
{\rm Re}(\lambda_{1,t})\geq\ldots\geq{\rm Re}(\lambda_{d,t})
, and let
\bm{v}_{1,t},\ldots,\bm{v}_{d,t}
be the associated eigenvectors, normalized s.t.
\|\bm{v}_{j,t}\|=1
.
Then
\bm{v}_{1,t}
is the principal eigenvector of
F_{t}
(i.e., the right eigenvector associated to
\lambda_{1,t}
).

The dynamical system (
1
) is time-varying, i.e.,
F_{t}
changes from layer to layer.
When we disregard the FFN part as we do in (
1
), it is known from the literature that the model (
1
) asymptotically (i.e., when
T\to\infty
) tends to a consensus equilibrium point, in which all tokens are equal
[
1
,
3
,
13
]
.

2.1
Shared weight transformers: Alignment with principal eigenvector

If in a transformer all weight matrices are identical across layers, i.e., in (
1
)
\forall\,t_{1},t_{2}\in\{1,\ldots,T\}

\bar{F}_{V,t_{1}}^{(h)}=\bar{F}_{V,t_{2}}^{(h)},\quad h\in\{1,\ldots,H\},\quad\text{and}\;\;F_{O,t_{1}}=F_{O,t_{2}},

(2)

then we can drop the index
t
in all the matrices in (
2
) and the dynamical system (
1
) becomes autonomous (i.e., time-invariant).

Consider
F=F_{O}F_{V}=F_{O}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\bar{F}_{V}^{(h)}
and denote
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d}
its eigenvalues, ordered as
{\rm Re}(\lambda_{1})\geq\ldots\geq{\rm Re}(\lambda_{d})
, with
\bm{v}_{1},\ldots,\bm{v}_{d}
the associated eigenvectors.
Several cases are possible, depending on the principal eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}
:

Case 1:

\lambda_{1}
is real and strictly dominant:
\lambda_{1}>{\rm Re}(\lambda_{i})
,
i=2,\ldots,d
;

Case 2:

\lambda_{1}
is complex and strictly dominant:
{\rm Re}(\lambda_{1})>{\rm Re}(\lambda_{i})
,
i=3,\ldots,d
;

Case 3:

\lambda_{1}
(real or complex) is not strictly dominant.

2.1.1
Case 1: real strictly dominant principal eigenvalue

For the case of
\text{LayerNorm}(\cdot)
which is the
L_{2}
norm, the system (
1
) can be reformulated as

\bm{x}_{i}(t+1)=\frac{\bm{x}_{i}(t)+\eta F_{O}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\bar{F}_{V}^{(h)}\sum_{j}A_{ij}^{(h)}(\bm{x}(t))\bm{x}_{j}(t)}{\|\bm{x}_{i}(t)+\eta F_{O}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\bar{F}_{V}^{(h)}\sum_{j}A_{ij}^{(h)}(\bm{x}(t))\bm{x}_{j}(t)\|},\qquad\begin{array}[]{l}i=1,\ldots,n,\\
t=1,\ldots,T\end{array}

(3)

where we have added an extra parameter
\eta
(“step size”), which balances the importance of the self-attention part against the skip connection
[
23
]
.
Under the special case of
F
which is symmetric, the asymptotic behavior of the system (
3
) is described in the following theorem.

Theorem 1

Consider the system (
3
).
Assume the attention matrices
A^{(h)}
,
h=1,\ldots,H
, are full and the matrix
F=F_{O}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\bar{F}_{V}^{(h)}
is symmetric with
\lambda_{1}>\lambda_{i}
,
i=2,\ldots,d
,
\lambda_{1}>0
. Assume further that
\eta<\frac{2}{|\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{d}|}
whenever
-\lambda_{d}>\lambda_{1}
. Then the consensus point
\bm{x}_{i}=\bm{v}_{1}

\forall\,i=1,\ldots,n
(or
\bm{x}_{i}=-\bm{v}_{1}

\forall\,i=1,\ldots,n
) is a locally asymptotically stable equilibrium point for the system (
3
) when
T\to\infty
.

The proof of this theorem is in Section
D.4.1
in the Supplementary Materials. It is a consequence of Theorem
3
(also in the Supplementary Materials).

What the theorem says is that when sufficiently many identical layers are available (i.e., sufficiently many time steps in the dynamical system (
3
)), then all tokens align themselves with the principal eigenvalue of
F
,
\bm{v}_{1}
.
This is similar to the behavior occurring in the power method.
The reason for this form of convergence is that when the token
\bm{x}_{i}
approaches a consensus point, each (full) attention matrix approaches a uniform matrix,
A_{ij}^{(h)}(\bm{x})\to\frac{1}{n}
, and can be taken out of the summation in (
3
)
[
23
]
. What is left is a special case of a dynamical system which in Section
D
of the Supplementary Materials we call the discrete-time multigent Oja flow
[
24
]
, whose continuous-time single-agent version is well-known for discovering the principal eigenvector of a matrix
[
36
]
, just like the power method does in discrete-time. The matrix in question is our
F
.

Roughly speaking, the behavior of (
3
) can be described as follows. If we split a token
\bm{x}_{i}(t)
into components associated to the eigenvectors of
F
(the spectral decomposition can be done rigorously only if
F
has real eigenvalues, but let us not focus on this detail here), then at each step of (
3
) (disregarding the residual connection and the attention coefficients), the component along
\bm{v}_{j}
acquires a scalar amplification/attenuation factor equal to the corresponding
\lambda_{j}
. Hence if
\lambda_{1}>\lambda_{j}
,
j=2,\ldots,n
, the component along
\bm{v}_{1}
gets amplified the most in comparison with the other components.
Since this projection is followed by a normalization, the resulting normalized vector
\bm{x}_{i}(t+1)
becomes more aligned with
\bm{v}_{1}
than
\bm{x}_{i}(t)
, because the projection dominates with respect to the remaining eigenvectors of
F
.
The projection + normalization mechanism is what occurs in a power method.

In the single head case discussed in the Supplementary Materials, other consensus-like equilibria, denoted bipartite consensus points, could in principle be locally asymptotically stable, corresponding to the remaining eigenvectors of the matrix
F
,
\bm{v}_{2},\ldots,\bm{v}_{d}
, see Lemma
8
in the Supplementary Materials. The name bipartite consensus is due to the splitting of the
n
tokens into two groups, the first group converging to
\bm{v}_{k}
, the other to the antipodal point
-\bm{v}_{k}
, with
k=2,\ldots,d

[
3
]
. Also more exotic types of multi-partite clustered equilibria are theoretically possible for the single-head model, see Lemma
8
.
In the simulations we carried out for the multi-head model (
3
) we could not observe any of these extra locally asymptotically stable equilibria (see Section
D.5
in the Supplementary Materials), nor were they visible in the experiments with the ALBERT transformer described below.

While we could prove convergence to
\bm{v}_{1}
only locally, in the full attention case and for a symmetric
F
(and for
\eta
small enough), convergence seems to occur under more general conditions (for causal attention, for any
F
with a strictly dominant eigenvalue, for any
\eta
, and for nearly any initial token vector).
We show below that numerically this is the case for the ALBERT transformer.

2.1.2
Case 2: complex conjugate principal eigenvector

When the principal eigenvalue is
\lambda_{1,2}=\alpha\pm i\beta
, and
{\rm Re}(\lambda_{1,2})>{\rm Re}(\lambda_{i})
,
i=3,\ldots,d
, then the power method fails, because the vector
\bm{x}
keeps rotating in the 2D subspace generated by the complex conjugate eigenvector pair
\bm{v}_{1}
and
\bm{v}_{2}
. The same behavior occurs in (
3
) when
T\to\infty
: the
\bm{x}_{i}
keep rotating in the 2D subspace formed by
{\rm Re}(\bm{v}_{1})
and
{\rm Im}(\bm{v}_{1})
.
Denoting
P=[{\rm Re}(\bm{v}_{1})\;{\rm Im}(\bm{v}_{1})]
, the projection onto
{\rm span}(P)
is
\bm{x}_{i,{\rm proj}}=PP^{\dagger}\bm{x}_{i}
.
The quantity that we need to monitor is then the correlation between
\bm{x}_{i}
and its projection
\bm{x}_{i,{\rm proj}}
.
This situation actually happens for some of the variants of ALBERT analyzed below.

2.1.3
Case 3: no strictly dominant eigenvalue

This case, due to
\lambda_{1}
having multiplicity
>1
, is not generic, hence it almost never occurs in real learned weight matrices. However, what can happen (and indeed we encounter in some variants of the ALBERT transformer) is
\lambda_{2}\simeq\lambda_{1}
. The consequence is that convergence of the iterate (
3
) may occur very slowly for some initial conditions
\bm{x}_{i}(0)
, as the two eigenspaces
{\rm span}(\bm{v}_{1})
and
{\rm span}(\bm{v}_{2})
are difficult to separate (a large
T
is required).
The quantity to monitor in this case is the correlation between
\bm{x}_{i}
and its projection
\bm{x}_{i,{\rm proj}}=PP^{\dagger}\bm{x}_{i}
with
P=[\bm{v}_{1}\;\bm{v}_{2}]
.

2.2
Transformers with layer-varying weights: Alignment with the principal eigenvector at the current step

In nearly all transformers, the (query, key, value and output) weight matrices change from layer to layer.
In these cases, we need to consider the time-varying dynamical system (
1
) for which the “layer matrix”
F_{t}
changes from layer to layer.
Since also the associated principal eigenvalue and eigenvector pair
\lambda_{1,t}
and
\bm{v}_{1,t}
change from layer to layer, we cannot expect an incrementally growing alignment of the tokens with
\bm{v}_{1,t}
as
t
grows.
Nevertheless, we can check if at a step
t
the tokens still tend to align themselves with the direction of
\bm{v}_{1,t}
when they pass through the
t
-th layer of the transformer.
Because of the attention coefficients, we cannot expect this to happen to all tokens at all layers, but for each layer
t
we can compute the average correlation increment

\psi(\bm{v}_{1,t})=\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\mathrm{corr}(\bm{x}_{i}{(t+1)},\bm{v}_{1,t})\right|-\left|\mathrm{corr}(\bm{x}_{i}{(t)},\bm{v}_{1,t})\right|\right)

(4)

and the probability of increment

\rho(\bm{v}_{1,t})=\mathbb{P}\!\left(\left|\mathrm{corr}(\bm{x}_{i}{(t+1)},\bm{v}_{1,t})\right|>\left|\mathrm{corr}(\bm{x}_{i}{(t)},\bm{v}_{1,t})\right|\right)

(5)

over a sufficiently large number of token vectors.
Values
\rho(\bm{v}_{1,t})>0.5
and
\psi(\bm{v}_{1,t})>0
imply that the tokens tend to become more aligned with
\bm{v}_{1,t}
when passing through layer
t
, indicating that the layer behaves analogously to a single step of the power method applied to
F_{t}
.

When the principal eigenvalue is complex as in Case 2 (and possibly in Case 3), then instead of (
4
) and (
5
) for each token
\bm{x}_{i}
we must compute

\displaystyle\psi(\bm{x}_{i,{\rm proj}})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\mathrm{corr}(\bm{x}_{i}{(t+1)},\bm{x}_{i,{\rm proj}}(t+1))\right|-\left|\mathrm{corr}(\bm{x}_{i}{(t)},\bm{x}_{i,{\rm proj}}(t))\right|\right)

\displaystyle\rho(\bm{x}_{i,{\rm proj}})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\!\left(\left|\mathrm{corr}(\bm{x}_{i}{(t+1)},\bm{x}_{i,{\rm proj}}(t+1))\right|>\left|\mathrm{corr}(\bm{x}_{i}{(t)},\bm{x}_{i,{\rm proj}}(t))\right|\right)

Hence we denote

\psi_{t}=\begin{cases}\psi(\bm{v}_{1,t})&\text{if $\bm{v}_{1,t}$ real}\\
\psi(\bm{x}_{i,{\rm proj}})&\text{if $\bm{v}_{1,t}$ complex}\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad\rho_{t}=\begin{cases}\rho(\bm{v}_{1,t})&\text{if $\bm{v}_{1,t}$ real}\\
\rho(\bm{x}_{i,{\rm proj}})&\text{if $\bm{v}_{1,t}$ complex.}\end{cases}

(6)

We also denote

\gamma_{t}=\begin{cases}\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\mathrm{corr}(\bm{x}_{i}{(t+1)},\bm{v}_{1,t})\right|\right)&\text{ if $\bm{v}_{1,t}$ real}\\
\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\mathrm{corr}(\bm{x}_{i}{(t+1)},\bm{x}_{i,{\rm proj}}(t+1))\right|\right)&\text{if $\bm{v}_{1,t}$ complex}\end{cases}

(7)

the average correlation between the tokens and the principal eigenvector at the output of each layer.

2.3
Imposing an alignment in a transformer with layer-varying weights

For transformers with layer-varying weights, the alignment effect with the principal eigenvector we obtain (i.e.,
\gamma_{t}
) is often rather limited.
Furthermore also the total alignment with the principal eigenvector of the overall layer matrix product
\Pi_{t=1}^{T}F_{t}
is minimal, if not insignificant (see experiments below).

It is however possible to steer the tokens towards a desired alignment by modifying only the last layer
t=T
of the transformer.
Recall that the layer matrix
F_{T}
is given by the product
F_{T}=F_{O,T}F_{V,T}
with
F_{V,T}=\sum_{h=1}^{H}\bar{F}_{V,T}^{(h)}
the concatenation of the value matrices of the
H
heads.
Assume that
F_{V,T}
is invertible (generically this is true; if not, pseudoinversion or least-squares regression can be used instead). Then replacing the output matrix
F_{O,T}
with
F_{O,T,{\rm mod}}=F_{O,T}+\sigma\bm{w}_{r}\bm{w}_{\ell}^{T}F_{V,T}^{-1}
with
\sigma\gg|\lambda_{1,T}|
(
\lambda_{1,T}
is the principal eigenvalue of the layer matrix
F_{T}
), the new layer matrix is
F_{T,{\rm mod}}=F_{T}+\sigma\bm{w}_{r}\bm{w}_{\ell}^{T}
where the rank-1 addition can be designed at will.
If the spectral gap between
\sigma
and
\lambda_{1,T}
is sufficiently large, then the rank-1 term
\sigma\bm{w}_{r}\bm{w}_{\ell}^{T}
determines the new principal eigenpair of
F_{T,{\rm mod}}
: the principal eigenvalue is (approximately)
\sigma
and the corresponding principal eigenvector (approximately)
\bm{w}_{r}
.
When in
F_{T,{\rm mod}}
the spectral gap between
\lambda_{1,T,{\rm mod}}\approx\sigma
and
\lambda_{2,T,{\rm mod}}
is of at least an order of magnitude, this novel principal direction dominates the projection and leads to a very high correlation between
\bm{v}_{1,T,{\rm mod}}(\approx\bm{w}_{r})
and the tokens.
Since
\bm{w}_{r}
can be chosen freely, the rank-1 modification provides a way to steer the tokens towards a desired direction.

Notice that if
\bm{w}_{r}
and
\bm{w}_{\ell}
correspond to right and left eigenvector of an existing eigenvalue of
F_{T}
, say
\lambda_{j}
, then the spectrum of
F_{T,mod}
is unchanged except for
\lambda_{T,j}
which becomes
\lambda_{T,j}+\sigma
.

Experiments

In this section, we perform experiments with both layer-invariant (ALBERT) and layer-varying (BERT, GPT2, and GPTNeo) real transformers. See Supplementary Materials
B
for details.

Application to ALBERT

If we disregard the FFN part of a layer and use

3.2
Application to transformers with layer-varying weights

When we apply our considerations to transformers like BERT, GPT2 and GPTNeo in which the weight matrices change from layer to layer, then only the level of alignment with the current
\bm{v}_{1,t}
can be checked, and it is not expected to increase over the layers, as
\bm{v}_{1,t-1}
and
\bm{v}_{1,t}
are typically significantly different in direction.
Denote
\psi_{t}^{\rm mean}=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\psi(\bm{v}_{k})/d
the mean of the increment (
4
) over all eigenvectors of
F_{t}
(with obvious corrections for the case of complex eigenvalues, in the spirit of (
6
)). Similarly, denote
\rho_{t}^{\rm mean}=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\rho(\bm{v}_{k})/d
the average probability of increment over all eigendirections, and
\gamma_{t}^{\rm mean}=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\gamma(\bm{v}_{k})/d
the mean of the correlation over all eigenvectors of
F_{t}
.
In Fig.

in the Supplementary Materials). As can be seen in the middle panels,
\psi_{t}
is almost always positive and larger than

For layer-varying transformers, one can wonder if a principal direction of alignment emerges as the tokens pass through the layers.
Since in our framework what decided the behavior of a layer is the layer matrix

as

shows no clear trend in the majority of models when compared with the average correlation

.

3.3
Steering the tokens towards a desired direction

If we apply the rank-1 perturbation described in Section
2.3
to the final layer of a layer-varying transformer, then it is easy to achieve a high correlation
\gamma_{T}=\left|\mathrm{corr}(\bm{x}_{i}{(T)},\bm{v}_{1,T,{\rm mod}})\right|
at the end of a transformer. This, combined with the consensus argument, implies that all tokens get similarly projected onto the subspace
{\rm span}(\bm{v}_{1,T,{\rm mod}})\approx{\rm span}(\bm{w}_{r})
which can be chosen by the designer. Such a correlation is shown in Fig.
3
for BERT-base, GPT2-medium and GPTNeo-2.7B (and in Fig.
10
in the Supplementary Materials for the other 6 transformer models).
In these examples, the spectral gap
\lambda_{1,T,{\rm mod}}-{\rm Re}(\lambda_{2,T,{\rm mod}})
is on the order of
10-10^{3}
, depending on the model, and allows us to achieve a near-perfect alignment with
\bm{v}_{1,T,{\rm mod}}
.

4
Discussion

The main contribution of this paper is to show that in a transformer the behavior of each layer (in which we ignore the effect of the FFN) has some resemblance with a step of the power method algorithm, notably in the tendency of token vectors to tilt towards the principal eigenvector of what we call the layer matrix
F_{t}
.
This behavior is clearly visible in a “universal” transformer with all identical weight matrices across all layers, because in this case the principal eigenvector stays constant across layers and the steps in the power method accumulate, hence convergence is macroscopically observable.
In a transformer with layer-varying weight matrices, instead, the principal eigenvector keeps changing from layer to layer, hence no accumulation of power method steps can occur.
Nevertheless, each layer still represents a projection step as confirmed by the experimental data.
What matters in the amplitude of this projection is the spectral gap between the first and second eigenvalue of the layer matrix, and this is sometimes visible in the data: for instance one of the layers achieving the largest
\gamma_{t}
is in GPTNeo-2.7B, at
t=4
, see Fig.
2
right panel, which happens to correspond to one of the few cases in which the spectral gap in
F_{t}
is sizable (
\lambda_{1,4}-\lambda_{2,4}>5
, see Fig.
7
in the Supplementary Materials).

The other main contribution of the paper builds on this observation.
Since the spectral gap in
F_{t}
matters in the projection, if we impose a sufficiently large spectral gap in the final layer of a transformer then we can get a strong projection along the corresponding eigenvector.
We show above that this is achievable in a constructive way: a user-specified rank-1 modification in the output matrix of the final layer allows to choose the direction of the projection in an arbitrary way. If only a mild steering towards a desired direction is needed, this can be accomplished by choosing a smaller
\sigma
.

In a transformer, token oversmoothing in depth (i.e., consensus) is an undesired effect.
Consensus can be slowed down by the skip connection (for us, by modulating
\eta

[
7
,
23
]
) but in a model like (
3
) it cannot be eliminated, because it is an asymptotic property (infinite depth).
Apart from using skip connections, methods to tame oversmoothing include modifying the attention matrix
[
8
]
, for instance scaling the softmax operation inside the attention block with a scalar parameter (inverse temperature)
[
13
,
14
]
or removing the principal (uniform) mode
[
26
]
.
The idea of
[
26
]
of suppressing the principal mode in the attention matrix relies on the same principle as our token steering method: exploit the presence of a significant spectral gap between the principal eigenvalue and the rest of the spectrum.
The aim and its implementation are however completely different: we aim at creating a spectral gap in the value-output projection map of the last layer of the transformer
F_{t}
, while
[
26
]
aims at removing the uniform behavior inside the attention matrix.

In a model like (
1
) or (
3
), the
L_{2}
layer normalization operation performed at every layer effectively renders the manifold in which each token is evolving a sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, see Section
D
in the Supplementary Materials.
The intersection of a 1-dimensional eigenspace
{\rm span}(\bm{v}_{1})
with
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
consists of two points
\bm{v}_{1}
(normalized to
\|\bm{v}_{1}\|=1
) and
-\bm{v}_{1}
.
As can be seen e.g. in Fig.
1
(b), the alignment of the tokens can be towards
\bm{v}_{1}
or towards
-\bm{v}_{1}
, and the absolute value in the correlations of formulas (
4
)-(
7
) is meant to account for both cases.
As mentioned in Section
2.1
, in the time-invariant case these two consensus points appear to be the only attractors of the dynamics of (
3
), in spite of the presence of other equilibria, mentioned in Lemma
8
in the Supplementary Materials.

5
Limitations

In the model (
1
) (or (
3
)), the FFN inside each layer is ignored.
Furthermore, the input of the model is a vector of tokens, which we choose randomly.
However, in a real transformer, the model receives as input a prompt, which passes through an embedding layer, upstream of the layers considered here.
If we repeat the analysis while including both embedding and FFN networks and using the original (affine)
{\rm LayerNorm}(\cdot)
normalization, then we obtain the results of Fig.
11
for ALBERT and Fig.
12
for BERT, GPT2 and GPTNeo.
All
\gamma_{t}
,
\psi_{t}
and
\rho_{t}
are degraded, in both layer-invariant and layer-varying transformers.
In particular, while the consensus among the tokens is preserved
[
1
]
, the direction of alignment is modified. This is obviously expected, and it is due to the FFNs.
The FFN is a nonlinear static map
\phi_{t}\,:\,\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
which “deforms” the token space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
at each layer.
Since
\phi_{t}
is known (its weights are learned quantities for us), the deformation can however be precompensated: the FFN
\phi_{t}
is typically a 2-layer fully connected map with an activation function (GELU or SiLU for our transformers) in the hidden layer, which has dimension larger than
d
, meaning that we can in general compute a (possibly non-unique) numerical approximation of its inverse. Therefore, if we aim to steer the tokens towards a desired direction
\bm{w}_{\rm des}
in the image of
\phi
as in Section
3.3
, we can alter the principal eigenvector of the last layer opportunely through rank-1 maps, similar to what was already considered, but imposing
\bm{w}_{r}=\phi_{T}^{-1}(\bm{w}_{\rm des})
.

Appendix A
Relation to the Standard Transformer Formulation

The main paper adopts a column-token convention and works with a composite matrix (“layer matrix”)
F_{t}=F_{O,t}F_{V,t}
that differs in notation from the standard per-head description of transformers. In this section, we make the correspondence between the two conventions explicit.

We adopt the column-token convention: a sequence of
n
tokens at layer
t
is represented as
\bm{X}_{t}=[\bm{x}_{1}(t),\ldots,\bm{x}_{n}(t)]\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}
, where each
\bm{x}_{i}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a column vector. Under this convention all matrices are the transposes of the learned parameter matrices commonly used in the ML literature
[
34
,
19
,
4
,
33
]
. We denote them by the letter
F
to distinguish them from the standard parameter matrices
W
. Explicitly,

F_{Q,t}=(W_{Q,t})^{\top},\quad F_{K,t}=(W_{K,t})^{\top},\quad F_{V,t}=(W_{V,t})^{\top},\quad F_{O,t}=(W_{O,t})^{\top}.

A layer with
H
heads and head dimension
d_{k}=d/H
is parameterized by the per-head query, key, and value projections
F_{Q,t}^{(h)},F_{K,t}^{(h)},F_{V,t}^{(h)}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{k}\times d}
and the output projection
F_{O,t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, which correspond to the ML convention parameter matrices via
F_{Q,t}^{(h)}=(W_{Q,t}^{(h)})^{\top},F_{K,t}^{(h)}=(W_{K,t}^{(h)})^{\top},F_{V,t}^{(h)}=(W_{V,t}^{(h)})^{\top}
and
F_{O,t}=(W_{O,t})^{\top}
. With the attention matrix

A_{t}^{(h)}=\text{softmax}\left(\frac{(F_{Q,t}^{(h)}\bm{X}_{t})^{\top}(F_{K,t}^{(h)}\bm{X}_{t})}{\sqrt{d_{k}}}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n},

the resulting multi-head attention output is

\text{MHA}_{t}(\bm{X_{t}})=F_{O,t}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\bar{F}_{V,t}^{(h)}\bm{X}_{t}(A_{t}^{(h)})^{\top}.

The main paper analyzes the spectral properties of a single composite matrix
F_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, obtained by stacking the per-head value projections into a single operator: let
\bar{F}_{V,t}^{(h)}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
be the zero-padded embedding of
F_{V,t}^{(h)}
into the rows corresponding to head
h
. Then

F_{t}=F_{O,t}F_{V,t}\quad\text{with}\quad F_{V,t}=\sum_{h=1}^{H}\bar{F}_{V,t}^{(h)}.

This is precisely the matrix
F_{t}
defined in the main paper, whose spectral properties (eigenvalues, eigenvectors, etc.) are the object of the analysis in Section
2
of the main paper.

Appendix B
Experimental Details for the Transformer Dynamics

In the experiments considered in this paper, we do not aim to reproduce exactly in full detail the computational blocks of ALBERT, BERT, GPT2, and GPTNeo. Rather, our goal is to construct a controlled experimental setting containing most of the features of real transformer and yet that is coherent with the discrete-time dynamical system (
3
). In this model, the update law for each token in a multi-head transformer layer includes the attention block, the skip connection, and a normalization step, while disregarding the FFN. More precisely, at layer
t
, each token is first updated by adding to its current state the multi-head attention output, obtained through the value projections and the output projection, lumped into the “layer matrix”
F_{t}
mentioned in the paper. The resulting vectors are then normalized. In our experiments, the original LayerNorm operation is replaced by
L_{2}
normalization, so that the layer update directly reflects the projection + normalization mechanism underlying the analogy with the power method. The step size is always unitary:
\eta=1
.

More specifically, for ALBERT, BERT, GPT2, and GPTNeo, we disregard the embedding layer and use more than
10^{4}
randomly initialized token vectors in the corresponding model dimension
d
as initial conditions. The initial tokens are drawn from a normal distribution. This avoids the effects of the embedding layer, and allows us to focus on the evolution of the hidden states through the layers. In all four model families, we also disregard the FFN sublayer and its residual branch. This yields the desired experiment setting, in which the dynamics retain the (projection + normalization) components most directly related to the layer matrix
F_{t}=F_{O,t}F_{V,t}
and to the power method analogy.

For ALBERT and BERT, the attention sublayer is followed by a skip connection and LayerNorm. In our experiments, we keep the attention update and its skip connection, and replace this LayerNorm with
L_{2}
normalization. The FFN sublayer, together with its residual branch, is omitted.

For GPT2 and GPTNeo, the original transformer blocks adopt a pre-norm architecture. In the experimental setting considered here, we retain the attention sublayer and its skip connection, and disregard the FFN sublayer together with its residual branch. The normalization located before the FFN sublayer, namely the normalization applied to the state after the attention residual update, is used as the normalization step of the dynamics. This normalization is then replaced by an
L_{2}
normalization. After this simplification, each simulated GPT2/GPTNeo layer first updates the token vectors through the attention sublayer and the skip connection, and then normalizes the updated states.

In summary, although ALBERT, BERT, GPT2, and GPTNeo differ in their original architectural details, our experiments reduce all of them to the same basic layer update: an attention based value and output update with a skip connection, followed by
L_{2}
normalization. This makes the experiments comparable across model families and isolates the geometric effect associated with the “layer matrix”
F_{t}
.

For completeness, in Fig.
11
and Fig.
12
we show the quantities considered in the paper,
\gamma_{t}
,
\psi_{t}
and
\rho_{t}
, also for the complete transformers in their original architecture, including embedding, FFN sublayers, and true (affine) LayerNorm.

Appendix D
A rigorous analysis of the single-head self-attention dynamics

In this section we consider the equivalent of (
3
) but for a single-head. Under certain specific conditions (symmetric value matrix, characterized by a strictly dominant eigenvalue) we can obtain a fairly complete analytical description of its asymptotic behavior, namely classify its equilibria, and infer their local stability. In particular, we show that the landscape of equilibria resembles that of the corresponding continuous-time system investigated in e.g.
[
3
]
. In the discrete-time case discussed here, the analysis is significantly more complicated even though it is based on the same tools (Jacobian linearization). Such analysis is the main contribution of this section.
In order to carry it out, it is useful to analyze first a simpler model which we call the multiagent discrete-time Oja system
[
36
]
, and which corresponds to the single-head self-attention dynamics but without the attention matrix.

D.1
Model formulation

Consider
n
tokens represented as unit length column vectors
\bm{x}_{i}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
.
Denote
{F_{Q}},{F_{K}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
the query and key matrices (for simplicity and without loss of generality we hereafter assume
m=d
) and
{F_{V}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
the value matrix.
Because of our representation of tokens as column vectors, these matrices correspond to the transposes of the weight matrices normally considered in the ML literature
[
34
,
19
,
4
,
33
]
, see Section
2.1
of the paper or Section
A
of these Supplementary Materials for the details.
Since we are dealing with a single head, an explicit output matrix (
F_{O}
in (
1
) in the main paper) is not required, and is therefore omitted.

The following discrete-time difference equation encodes a single-head self-attention dynamics, including a skip connection and an
L_{2}
layer normalization,

\bm{x}_{i}^{+}=\frac{\bm{x}_{i}+\eta{F_{V}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{ij}(\bm{x})\bm{x}_{j}}{\|\bm{x}_{i}+\eta{F_{V}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{ij}(\bm{x})\bm{x}_{j}\|},

(8)

where
\eta>0
is a step-size,
A_{ij}(\bm{x})=\frac{e^{\langle{F_{Q}}\bm{x}_{i},{F_{K}}\bm{x}_{j}\rangle}}{\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}e^{\langle{F_{Q}}\bm{x}_{i},{F_{K}}\bm{x}_{\ell}\rangle}}
is the attention that the token
\bm{x}_{i}
gives to the token
\bm{x}_{j}
, computed through a softmax function (
\bm{x}
is the stack of
\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{n}
vectors) and where the attention matrix
A(\bm{x})
is assumed full, i.e., each token attends to every other token.
In (
8
) we are implicitly interpreting the tokens as layer-dependent state variables:
\bm{x}_{i}=\bm{x}_{i}(t)
and
\bm{x}_{i}^{+}=\bm{x}_{i}(t+1)
, for some layer index
t
.
We let
\|\cdot\|
denote the Euclidean vector norm and the operator norm for matrices.
For the
i
-th token, define the tangent projector on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
at
\bm{x}_{i}
by
\Pi_{\bm{x}_{i}}:=I-\bm{x}_{i}\bm{x}_{i}^{\top}
, which satisfies
\Pi_{\bm{x}_{i}}\bm{x}_{i}=0
and
\Pi_{\bm{x}_{i}}^{\top}=\Pi_{\bm{x}_{i}}
.

We are interested in investigating the asymptotic behavior of (
8
), i.e., in computing the equilibria of (
8
) and studying their stability properties.
For that (as already mentioned), it is convenient to investigate first a simpler system, which, for analogy with the continuous-time case, we call the multiagent DT Oja system
[
3
]
.
This system has the same structure as (
8
) but lacks the attention matrix.

D.2
Multiagent discrete-time Oja system

When we consider uniform attention
[
23
]
, i.e., we take
A(\bf{x})=\frac{1}{n}\mathds{1}\mathds{1}^{T}
, then instead of (
8
) we obtain the simpler system

\bm{x}_{i}^{+}=\frac{\bm{x}_{i}+\frac{\eta}{n}{F_{V}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\bm{x}_{j}}{\|\bm{x}_{i}+\frac{\eta}{n}{F_{V}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\bm{x}_{j}\|},

(9)

which is easier to study.
Let
\bm{y}={F_{V}}\bm{m}(\bm{x})=\frac{1}{n}{F_{V}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\bm{x}_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be the total influence of all agents on agent
i
(which is the same for all agents).
Splitting (
9
) into parts before/after the normalization we can also write:

\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}=\bm{x}_{i}+\eta\bm{y}\quad\text{and}\quad\bm{x}_{i}^{+}=\frac{\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}}{\|\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}\|}.

(10)

We make the following assumption which holds throughout the rest of this section.

Assumption 1

The value matrix
{F_{V}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
is nonsingular and symmetric, of eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}>\lambda_{2}\geq\ldots\geq\lambda_{d}
with
\lambda_{1}>0
simple and positive.

Let
\bm{v}_{1},\ldots,\bm{v}_{d}
be the associated eigenvectors, normalized s.t.
\|\bm{v}_{k}\|=1
.

D.2.1
Equilibria

Let us first give our definition of consensus and bipartite consensus equilibria, which we request to be collinear with the eigenvectors of
{F_{V}}
(the reason will become clear later on when we study the full model (
8
)).
The points
\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{n}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
are said to be in a
consensus equilibrium
if
\bm{x}_{i}=\bm{x}_{j}=\bm{v}_{k}

\forall\,i,j=1,\ldots,n
(or
\bm{x}_{i}=\bm{x}_{j}=-\bm{v}_{k}

\forall\,i,j=1,\ldots,n
) and for some
k=1,\ldots,d
.
They are said to be in a
bipartite consensus equilibrium
if
\bm{x}_{i}=\bm{v}_{k}
for
i\in\mathcal{V}_{1}\subset\mathcal{V}
and
\bm{x}_{i}=-\bm{v}_{k}
for
i\in\mathcal{V}\setminus\mathcal{V}_{1}
, for some
k=1,\ldots,d
.

Lemma 1

Let
\bm{x}=[(\bm{x}_{1})^{T}\,\ldots(\bm{x}_{1})^{T}]^{T}
be an equilibrium point of the system (
9
).
Then
\bm{x}
belongs to one of the following classes:

1.

consensus:
\bm{x}_{i}=\bm{v}_{k}

\forall\,i
, and
k=1,\ldots,d
;

2.

bipartite consensus:
\bm{x}_{i}=\pm\bm{v}_{k}

\forall\,i
and
k=1,\ldots,d
;

3.

polygonal equilibria:
\{{\bm{x}_{i}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\;\text{s.t.}\;{F_{V}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\bm{x}_{j}=0\}.

Proof.

An equilibrium point
\bm{x}_{i}
satisfies
\bm{x}_{i}^{+}=\bm{x}_{i}
for all
i
, hence, from (
10
),
\bm{x}_{i}=\frac{\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}}{\|\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}\|}
, which implies that
\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}
and
\bm{x}_{i}
must be collinear, i.e.,
\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}=\varphi\bm{x}_{i}
for some
\varphi\in\mathbb{R}
.
If
\bm{y}=\frac{1}{n}{F_{V}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\bm{x}_{j}
, then from (
10
),
\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}=\bm{x}_{i}+\eta\bm{y}
holds iff also
\bm{y}
and
\bm{x}_{i}
are collinear or
\bm{y}=0
.
This condition can be written equivalently using projections as

\Pi_{\bm{x}_{i}}\bm{y}=(I-\bm{x}_{i}(\bm{x}_{i})^{T})\bm{y}=0.

There are 3 cases in which this can happen:

1.

consensus:
\bm{y}=\varphi_{i}\bm{x}_{i}
, for some scalar
\varphi_{i}>0
;

2.

bipartite consensus:
\bm{y}=-\varphi_{i}\bm{x}_{i}
, for some scalar
\varphi_{i}>0
;

3.

polygonal equilibria:
\bm{y}=0
.

In fact, in the first two cases,
\Pi_{\bm{x}_{i}}\bm{x}_{i}=\pm\varphi_{i}(\bm{x}_{i}-\bm{x}_{i}(\bm{x}_{i})^{T}\bm{x}_{i})=0
, since
(\bm{x}_{i})^{T}\bm{x}_{i}=1
. The third case follows trivially.
To show that consensus must be an eigenvector of
{F_{V}}
, observe that at this equilibrium point we have
\bm{y}={F_{V}}\bm{x}_{i}
, since
\bm{x}_{i}=\bm{x}_{j}
.
Combining with
\bm{y}=\varphi_{i}\bm{x}_{i}
, we have
{F_{V}}\bm{x}_{i}=\varphi_{i}\bm{x}_{i}
, i.e.,
\bm{x}_{i}
is an eigenvector of
{F_{V}}
and
\varphi_{i}
one of its eigenvalues. The argument for bipartite consensus is identical.

Remark 1

For a given
\bm{v}_{k}
there are
2^{n}
possible consensus or bipartite consensus equilibria.
These are always paired by a global symmetry w.r.t. the origin, i.e., if
\bm{x}_{i}=\bm{v}_{k}

\forall\,i
is a consensus equilibrium point, its antipodal point
\bm{x}_{i}=-\bm{v}_{k}

\forall\,i
is also a consensus equilibrium point, and similarly for the bipartite consensus equilibria.
This is implicitly assumed from now on, but not explicitly written, to avoid overloading the formulation of the results.

D.2.2
Stability

The stability properties of the multiagent Oja system (
9
) are summarized in the following theorem.

Theorem 2

For the system (
9
), under Assumption
1
, if

\eta<\min\left(\frac{2}{|\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{d}|},\,\frac{1}{2\max\{\lambda_{1},|\lambda_{d}|\}}\right)
,
the principal consensus equilibrium
\bm{x}_{i}=\bm{v}_{1}

\forall\,i
is asymptotically stable, while all other consensus equilibria, all bipartite consensus equilibria (including those aligned with
\bm{v}_{1}
), and all polygonal equilibria are unstable.
The trajectories of all tokens
\bm{x}_{i}(t)
converge to
\bm{v}_{1}
(or to
-\bm{v}_{1}
) for almost all initial conditions
\bm{x}_{i}(0)
.

The theorem is proven through a series of lemmas.
Let
\delta_{ih}
be a delta function (
\delta_{ih}=1
if
i=h
and
\delta_{ih}=0
otherwise).
Let the right-hand side of (
9
) be
f_{i}(\bm{x})=\frac{\bm{x}_{i}+\frac{\eta}{n}{F_{V}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\bm{x}_{j}}{\|\bm{x}_{i}+\frac{\eta}{n}{F_{V}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\bm{x}_{j}\|}
, and denote
f(\bm{x})=\begin{bmatrix}f_{1}(\bm{x})^{T}&\ldots&f_{n}(\bm{x})^{T}\end{bmatrix}^{T}
the
(nd)
-dimensional vector field associated to the stacked state vector
\bm{x}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{x}_{1}^{T}&\ldots&\bm{x}_{n}^{T}\end{bmatrix}^{T}
.
The Jacobian
J(\bm{x})=\frac{\partial f(\bm{x})}{\partial\bm{x}}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\partial f_{i}(\bm{x})}{\partial\bm{x}_{h}}\end{bmatrix}
is composed of a diagonal part and a part that repeats itself on all
(i,h)
blocks.

Lemma 2

The Jacobian
J(\bm{x})
of the system (
9
) has the following blocks:

\begin{split}\frac{\partial f_{i}(\bm{x})}{\partial\bm{x}_{h}}=&\frac{\frac{\eta}{n}{F_{V}}}{\|\bm{x}_{i}+\frac{\eta}{n}{F_{V}}\sum_{j}\bm{x}_{j}\|}-\frac{(\bm{x}_{i}+\frac{\eta}{n}{F_{V}}\sum_{j}\bm{x}_{j})\frac{\eta}{n}\left(\bm{x}_{i}^{T}{F_{V}}+\frac{\eta}{n}\left(\sum_{j}\bm{x}_{j}^{T}{F_{V}}^{T}(I-\bm{x}_{i}\bm{x}_{i}^{T}){F_{V}}\right)\right)}{\|\bm{x}_{i}+\frac{\eta}{n}{F_{V}}\sum_{j}\bm{x}_{j}\|^{2}}\\
&+\left(\frac{I}{\|\bm{x}_{i}+\frac{\eta}{n}{F_{V}}\sum_{j}\bm{x}_{j}\|}-\frac{(\bm{x}_{i}+\frac{\eta}{n}{F_{V}}\sum_{j}\bm{x}_{j})\frac{\eta}{n}\left(\sum_{j}\bm{x}_{j}^{T}{F_{V}}^{T}(I-\frac{\eta}{n}\bm{x}_{i}\sum_{\ell}\bm{x}_{\ell}^{T}{F_{V}})\right)}{\|\bm{x}_{i}+\frac{\eta}{n}{F_{V}}\sum_{j}\bm{x}_{j}\|^{2}}\right)\delta_{ih}\end{split}

(11)

Proof.

Let us first express the series expansion of the normed term:

\begin{split}\|\bm{x}_{i}+\frac{\eta}{n}{F_{V}}\sum_{j}\bm{x}_{j}\|=&\left((\bm{x}_{i}+\eta\bm{y})^{T}(\bm{x}_{i}+\eta\bm{y})\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\left(1+2\eta\bm{x}_{i}^{T}\bm{y}+\eta^{2}\|\bm{y}\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\approx&1+\frac{1}{2}\left(2\eta\bm{x}_{i}^{T}\bm{y}+\eta^{2}\|\bm{y}\|^{2}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\eta^{2}(\bm{x}_{i}^{T}\bm{y})^{2}+O(\eta^{3})\\
=&1+\frac{\eta}{n}\bm{x}_{i}^{T}{F_{V}}\sum_{j}\bm{x}_{j}+\frac{\eta^{2}}{2n^{2}}\left(\sum_{j}\bm{x}_{j}^{T}{F_{V}}^{T}{F_{V}}\sum_{j}\bm{x}_{j}-(\bm{x}_{i}^{T}{F_{V}}\sum_{j}\bm{x}_{j})^{2}\right)+O(\eta^{3})\end{split}

where we have used the binomial series expansion (
(1+\xi)^{\frac{1}{2}}\approx 1+\frac{\xi}{2}-\frac{\xi^{2}}{8}+O(\xi^{3})
), disregarding terms of order higher than 2 in
\eta
.
Differentiating this term

\begin{split}\frac{\partial\|\bm{x}_{i}+\frac{\eta}{n}{F_{V}}\sum_{j}\bm{x}_{j}\|}{\partial\bm{x}_{h}}=&\frac{\eta}{n}\bm{x}_{i}^{T}{F_{V}}+\frac{\eta^{2}}{n^{2}}\sum_{j}\bm{x}_{j}^{T}{F_{V}}^{T}(I-\bm{x}_{i}\bm{x}_{i}^{T}){F_{V}}\\
&+\left(\frac{\eta}{n}\sum_{j}\bm{x}_{j}^{T}{F_{V}}^{T}-\frac{\eta^{2}}{n^{2}}\sum_{j}\bm{x}_{j}^{T}{F_{V}}^{T}\bm{x}_{i}\sum_{\ell}\bm{x}_{\ell}^{T}{F_{V}}\right)\delta_{ih}\end{split}

Inserting this expression into

\begin{split}\frac{\partial f_{i}(\bm{x})}{\partial\bm{x}_{h}}=&\frac{(I\delta_{ih}+\frac{\eta}{n}{F_{V}})\|\bm{x}_{i}+\frac{\eta}{n}{F_{V}}\sum_{j}\bm{x}_{j}\|-(\bm{x}_{i}+\frac{\eta}{n}{F_{V}}\sum_{j}\bm{x}_{j})\frac{\partial\|\bm{x}_{i}+\frac{\eta}{n}{F_{V}}\sum_{j}\bm{x}_{j}\|}{\partial\bm{x}_{h}}}{\|\bm{x}_{i}+\frac{\eta}{n}{F_{V}}\sum_{j}\bm{x}_{j}\|^{2}}\end{split}

the result follows.

Lemma 3

For the system (
9
), under Assumption
1
, at a consensus equilibrium
\bm{x}_{i}=\bm{v}_{k}

\forall\,i
the Jacobian
J(\bm{x})
has the following 3 classes of eigenvalues:

1.

if
1+\eta\lambda_{k}>0
,
\frac{1-\eta\lambda_{k}+\eta^{2}\lambda_{k}^{2}}{1+\eta\lambda_{k}}
of multiplicity
n
, while if
1+\eta\lambda_{k}<0
,
\frac{-1-3\eta\lambda_{k}+\eta^{2}\lambda_{k}^{2}}{1+\eta\lambda_{k}}
of multiplicity
1
and
\frac{-1-\eta\lambda_{k}+\eta^{2}\lambda_{k}^{2}}{1+\eta\lambda_{k}}
of multiplicity
n-1
.

2.

\frac{1+\eta\lambda_{h}}{|1+\eta\lambda_{k}|}
of multiplicity 1,
h=1,\ldots,k-1,k+1,\ldots,d
.

3.

\frac{1}{|1+\eta\lambda_{k}|}
of multiplicity
nd-d-n+1
.

Proof.

When computing the Jacobian (
11
) at the consensus
\bm{x}_{i}=\bm{v}_{k}

\forall\,i
, we get

\begin{split}&J(\bm{v}_{k})=\left.\frac{\partial f(\bm{x})}{\partial\bm{x}}\right|_{\bm{x}_{i}=\bm{v}_{k}}\\
&=\mathds{1}\mathds{1}^{T}\otimes\left(\frac{{\rm sgn}(1+\eta\lambda_{k})\frac{\eta}{n}{F_{V}}-\frac{\eta}{n}\lambda_{k}\bm{v}_{k}\bm{v}_{k}^{T}}{1+\eta\lambda_{k}}\right)+I\otimes\left(\frac{{\rm sgn}(1+\eta\lambda_{k})I-\eta\lambda_{k}(1-\eta\lambda_{k})\bm{v}_{k}\bm{v}_{k}^{T}}{1+\eta\lambda_{k}}\right).\end{split}

(12)

where we have used
\bm{v}_{k}^{T}(I-\bm{v}_{k}\bm{v}_{k}^{T})=0
.
The first term represents a factor present in all entries of
J(\bm{v}_{k})
, while the second one is present only on the diagonal.
For
J(\bm{v}_{k})
there are 3 classes of eigenvectors:

1.

The eigenvectors of the first class depend on the sign of
1+\eta\lambda_{k}
. If
1+\eta\lambda_{k}>0
, then choose
\bm{p}=[0\ldots 0\;\bm{v}_{k}\;0\ldots 0]
and compute the two components of
J(\bm{v}_{k})
:

\begin{cases}&\frac{{\rm sgn}(1+\eta\lambda_{k})\frac{\eta}{n}{F_{V}}-\frac{\eta}{n}\lambda_{k}\bm{v}_{k}\bm{v}_{k}^{T}}{1+\eta\lambda_{k}}\bm{v}_{k}=\frac{\frac{\eta}{n}(1-1)}{1+\eta\lambda_{k}}\bm{v}_{k}=0\\
&\frac{{\rm sgn}(1+\eta\lambda_{k})I-\eta\lambda_{k}(1-\eta\lambda_{k})\bm{v}_{k}\bm{v}_{k}^{T}}{1+\eta\lambda_{k}}\bm{v}_{k}=\frac{1-\eta\lambda_{k}(1-\eta\lambda_{k})}{1+\eta\lambda_{k}}\bm{v}_{k}\end{cases}

meaning that

J(\bm{v}_{k})\bm{p}=\frac{1-\eta\lambda_{k}+\eta^{2}\lambda_{k}^{2}}{1+\eta\lambda_{k}}\bm{p}

i.e.,
\frac{1-\eta\lambda_{k}+\eta^{2}\lambda_{k}^{2}}{1+\eta\lambda_{k}}
is an eigenvalue of
J(\bm{v}_{k})
of multiplicity
n
(as there are
n
possible slots for
\bm{v}_{k}
in
\bm{p}
).
If instead
1+\eta\lambda_{k}<0
, then choose the following
n
eigenvectors

\bm{p}=\begin{cases}\bm{p}_{1}=[\bm{v}_{k}\;\bm{v}_{k}\ldots\bm{v}_{k}]\\
\bm{p}_{\ell}=[\phi_{1}^{\ell}\bm{v}_{k}\;\phi_{2}^{\ell}\bm{v}_{k}\ldots\phi_{n}^{\ell}\bm{v}_{k}]\;\text{s.t.}\;\sum_{i}\phi_{i}^{\ell}=0,\;\ell=2,\ldots,n\end{cases}

In correspondence of
\bm{p}_{1}
, one gets

\begin{cases}&\frac{{\rm sgn}(1+\eta\lambda_{k})\frac{\eta}{n}{F_{V}}-\frac{\eta}{n}\lambda_{k}\bm{v}_{k}\bm{v}_{k}^{T}}{1+\eta\lambda_{k}}\bm{v}_{k}=\frac{\eta}{n}\frac{-2\lambda_{k}}{1+\eta\lambda_{k}}\bm{v}_{k}\\
&\frac{{\rm sgn}(1+\eta\lambda_{k})I-\eta\lambda_{k}(1-\eta\lambda_{k})\bm{v}_{k}\bm{v}_{k}^{T}}{1+\eta\lambda_{k}}\bm{v}_{k}=\frac{-1-\eta\lambda_{k}+\eta^{2}\lambda_{k}^{2}}{1+\eta\lambda_{k}}\bm{v}_{k}\end{cases}

hence

J(\bm{v}_{k})\bm{p}_{1}=\eta\frac{-2\lambda_{k}}{1+\eta\lambda_{k}}\bm{p}_{1}+\frac{-1-\eta\lambda_{k}+\eta^{2}\lambda_{k}^{2}}{1+\eta\lambda_{k}}\bm{p}_{1}=\frac{-1-3\eta\lambda_{k}+\eta^{2}\lambda_{k}^{2}}{1+\eta\lambda_{k}}\bm{p}_{1}

while in correspondence of
\bm{p}_{\ell}
one gets

J(\bm{v}_{k})\bm{p}_{\ell}=\begin{bmatrix}\sum_{i}\phi_{i}^{\ell}\left(\frac{\eta}{n}\frac{-2\lambda_{k}}{1+\eta\lambda_{k}}\right)\bm{v}_{k}+\frac{-1-\eta\lambda_{k}+\eta^{2}\lambda_{k}^{2}}{1+\eta\lambda_{k}}\phi_{1}\bm{v}_{k}\\
\vdots\\
\sum_{i}\phi_{i}^{\ell}\left(\frac{\eta}{n}\frac{-2\lambda_{k}}{1+\eta\lambda_{k}}\right)\bm{v}_{k}+\frac{-1-\eta\lambda_{k}+\eta^{2}\lambda_{k}^{2}}{1+\eta\lambda_{k}}\phi_{n}\bm{v}_{k}\end{bmatrix}=\frac{-1-\eta\lambda_{k}+\eta^{2}\lambda_{k}^{2}}{1+\eta\lambda_{k}}\bm{p}_{\ell}

i.e.,
\frac{-1-\eta\lambda_{k}+\eta^{2}\lambda_{k}^{2}}{1+\eta\lambda_{k}}
is an eigenvalue of multiplicity
n-1
.

2.

For the second class of eigenvectors choosing
\bm{q}_{h}=[\bm{v}_{h}\,\ldots\,\bm{v}_{h}]
for
h\neq k
, leads to

\begin{cases}&\frac{{\rm sgn}(1+\eta\lambda_{k})\frac{\eta}{n}{F_{V}}-\frac{\eta}{n}\lambda_{k}\bm{v}_{k}\bm{v}_{k}^{T}}{1+\eta\lambda_{k}}\bm{v}_{h}=\frac{\eta}{n}\frac{\lambda_{h}}{|1+\eta\lambda_{k}|}\bm{v}_{h}\\
&\frac{{\rm sgn}(1+\eta\lambda_{k})I-\eta\lambda_{k}(1-\eta\lambda_{k})\bm{v}_{k}\bm{v}_{k}^{T}}{1+\eta\lambda_{k}}\bm{v}_{h}=\frac{1}{|1+\eta\lambda_{k}|}\bm{v}_{h}\end{cases}

and hence to

J(\bm{v}_{k})\bm{q}_{h}=\frac{1+\eta\lambda_{h}}{|1+\eta\lambda_{k}|}\bm{q}_{h}

i.e.,
\frac{1+\eta\lambda_{h}}{|1+\eta\lambda_{k}|}
is an eigenvalue of
J(\bm{v}_{k})
. There are
d-1
such eigenvalues.

3.

The remaining
nd-d-n+1
eigenvectors are assembled by considering vectors
\bm{r}=\begin{bmatrix}(\bm{z}^{1})^{T}&(\bm{z}^{2})^{T}&\ldots&(\bm{z}^{n})^{T}\end{bmatrix}^{T}
s.t.
\bm{v}_{k}^{T}\bm{z}^{i}=0
for all
i=1,\ldots,n
and all
h=1,\ldots,k-1,k+1,\ldots,d
and
\bm{q}_{h}^{T}\bm{r}=0
. Since the number of such constraints is
d-1+n
, there exist
nd-n-d+1
such vectors
\bm{r}
, and they can always be chosen so that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bm{z}^{i}=0
.
Computing,

\begin{cases}&\frac{{\rm sgn}(1+\eta\lambda_{k})\frac{\eta}{n}{F_{V}}-\frac{\eta}{n}\lambda_{k}\bm{v}_{k}\bm{v}_{k}^{T}}{1+\eta\lambda_{k}}\bm{z}^{i}=\frac{\eta}{n}\frac{{F_{V}}}{|1+\eta\lambda_{k}|}\bm{z}^{i}\\
&\frac{{\rm sgn}(1+\eta\lambda_{k})I-\eta\lambda_{k}(1-\eta\lambda_{k})\bm{v}_{k}\bm{v}_{k}^{T}}{1+\eta\lambda_{k}}\bm{z}^{i}=\frac{1}{|1+\eta\lambda_{k}|}\bm{z}^{i}\end{cases}

hence

J(\bm{v}_{k})\bm{r}=\frac{1}{|1+\eta\lambda_{k}|}\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\eta}{n}{F_{V}}\sum_{i}\bm{z}_{i}+\bm{z}_{1}\\
\vdots\\
\frac{\eta}{n}{F_{V}}\sum_{i}\bm{z}_{i}+\bm{z}_{n}\end{bmatrix}=\frac{1}{|1+\eta\lambda_{k}|}\bm{r}

Remark 2

The discrete-time system (
8
) can be seen as a map from the manifold
(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})^{n}
to itself.
Hence its tangent space is
T_{\bm{x}}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})^{n}
, of dimension
n(d-1)
, while the Jacobian
J(\bm{x})
computed above is in dimension
nd
.
Such Jacobian is what is sometimes called the “extrinsic Jacobian” living in the ambient manifold
\mathbb{R}^{nd\times nd}
rather than in
T_{\bm{x}}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})^{n}
.
What decides stability is however the “intrinsic Jacobian” living on
T_{\bm{x}}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})^{n}
. For Lemma
3
, if we look at the eigenvectors, we can easily deduce that the first class of eigenvectors (of cardinality
n
) does not belong to
T_{\bm{x}}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})^{n}
, while the other two classes are always in
T_{\bm{x}}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})^{n}
, hence it is only these last two classes that decide the stability character of the equilibria.

Lemma 4

For the system (
9
), under Assumption
1
, and, if
-\lambda_{d}>\lambda_{1}
, for
\eta<\frac{2}{|\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{d}|}
, then the principal consensus equilibrium point,
\bm{x}_{i}=\bm{v}_{1}

\forall\,i
, is asymptotically stable.
For
k=2,\ldots,d
, the consensus equilibria
\bm{x}_{i}=\bm{v}_{k}

\forall\,i
are all unstable.

Proof.

Consider the principal consensus equilibrium point
\bm{v}_{1}
and the eigenvalues of
J(\bm{v}_{1})
computed in Lemma
3
.
Notice from Remark
2
that only the second and third class of eigenvalues matter for the stability.
Under Assumption
1
,
1+\eta\lambda_{1}>1
, hence the 3rd class of eigenvalues is inside the unit disk. When
|\lambda_{d}|<\lambda_{1}
, then also the second class is inside the disk for all
h=2,\ldots,d
, regardless of the sign of
\lambda_{h}
, while when
-\lambda_{d}>\lambda_{1}
we need to have
\frac{1+\eta\lambda_{d}}{1+\eta\lambda_{1}}>-1
, i.e.,
\eta<\frac{-2}{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{d}}=\frac{2}{|\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{d}|}
.

When
k>1
(consensus at non-principal eigenvectors), then one of the eigenvalues of
J(\bm{v}_{k})
is
\frac{1+\eta\lambda_{1}}{|1+\eta\lambda_{k}|}
which is always
>1
. This is obvious when
\lambda_{k}\geq 0
, as
\lambda_{k}<\lambda_{1}
. What needs to be checked is the case of
\lambda_{k}
negative with
-\lambda_{k}\gg\lambda_{1}
.

For this case we consider the two situations

1.

If
1+\eta\lambda_{k}>0
, then we have
\frac{1+\eta\lambda_{1}}{1+\eta\lambda_{k}}>1
because this is equivalent to
1+\eta\lambda_{1}>1+\eta\lambda_{k}
.

2.

If
1+\eta\lambda_{k}<0
, then we must show that it is
-\frac{1+\eta\lambda_{1}}{1+\eta\lambda_{k}}>1
, i.e., that
1+\eta\lambda_{1}>-1-\eta\lambda_{k}
, or
\eta(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{k})>-2
. As
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{k}<0
, the expression holds if it holds for the largest possible value of
\eta
.
Taking
\eta=\frac{2}{|\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{d}|}
, we obtain the following inequality
-\frac{2(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{k})}{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{d}}>-2
, or, equivalently,
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{k}>\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{d}
which is always true.

In both situations
J(\bm{v}_{k})
has an eigenvalue outside the unit disk.

At a bipartite consensus point
\bm{x}_{i}=\pm\bm{v}_{k}
, split the
n
tokens into two sets
\mathcal{V}_{1}
and
\mathcal{V}_{2}
,
\mathcal{V}_{1}\cup\mathcal{V}_{2}=\{1,\ldots,n\}
, according to whether
\bm{x}_{i}=\bm{v}_{k}
or
\bm{x}_{i}=-\bm{v}_{k}
at equilibrium. Assume that
n_{1}=|\mathcal{V}_{1}|
tokens are equal to
\bm{v}_{k}
and
n_{2}=|\mathcal{V}_{2}|
equal to
-\bm{v}_{k}
, with
n_{1}+n_{2}=n
.
Let
\nu=\frac{n_{1}-n_{2}}{n}
, where we consider w.l.o.g.
n_{1}\geq n_{2}
(hence
\nu\geq 0
).

Lemma 5

For the system (
9
), under Assumption
1
, the bipartite consensus equilibria,
\bm{x}_{i}=\pm\bm{v}_{k}

\forall\,i
are all unstable
\forall\,k=1,\ldots,d
and
\forall\,\eta>0
.

Proof.

At a bipartite consensus equilibrium there exists at least onne index
i\in\mathcal{V}_{1}
. Consider the perturbation
\bm{x}_{i}=\bm{v}_{k}+\bm{w}_{i}
with
\bm{v}_{k}^{T}\bm{w}_{i}=0

\forall\,i
. For this perturbation, the unnormalized next iterate is

\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}=\bm{v}_{k}+\bm{w}_{i}+\frac{\eta}{n}{F_{V}}\sum_{j}(\bm{v}_{k}+\bm{w}_{j})=(1+\eta\nu\lambda_{k})\bm{v}_{k}+\bm{w}_{i}+\frac{\eta}{n}{F_{V}}\sum_{j}\bm{w}_{j}

and its norm

\|\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}\|=\left(\alpha^{2}\left(1+\frac{\|\bm{u}_{i}\|^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

where
\alpha:=1+\eta\nu\lambda_{k}
,
\bm{u}_{i}:=\bm{w}_{i}+\frac{\eta}{n}{F_{V}}\sum_{j}\bm{w}_{j}
, and we have used
\bm{v}_{k}^{T}\bm{v}_{k}=1
and
\bm{v}_{k}^{T}\bm{w}_{i}=0
.
Hence, using the binomial series expansion (
(1+\xi)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\approx 1-\frac{\xi}{2}+h.o.t.
),

\begin{split}\bm{x}_{i}^{+}&=\frac{\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}}{\|\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}\|}=\frac{\alpha\bm{v}_{k}+\bm{u}_{i}}{\alpha\left(1+\frac{\|\bm{u}_{i}\|^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\approx\bm{v}_{k}+\frac{\bm{u}_{i}}{\alpha}+h.o.t.\\
&=\bm{v}_{k}+\frac{\bm{w}_{i}+\frac{\eta}{n}{F_{V}}\sum_{j}\bm{w}_{j}}{1+\eta\nu\lambda_{k}}+h.o.t.\approx\bm{v}_{k}+(1-\eta\nu\lambda_{k})(\bm{w}_{i}+\frac{\eta}{n}{F_{V}}\sum_{j}\bm{w}_{j})\end{split}

where we have used the expansion
\frac{1}{1+\eta\nu\lambda_{k}}\approx 1-\eta\nu\lambda_{k}
.
The linearized equation is then

\bm{w}_{i}^{+}=(1-\eta\nu\lambda_{k})\bm{w}_{i}+\frac{\eta}{n}{F_{V}}\sum_{j}\bm{w}_{j}.

Expanding all
\bm{w}_{i}
in the orthonormal basis
\bm{v}_{1},\ldots,\bm{v}_{d}
, and denoting
\bm{w}_{i}=\sum_{\ell}\xi^{i}_{\ell}\bm{v}_{\ell}
,

\bm{w}_{i}^{+}=\sum_{\ell}(\xi^{i}_{\ell})^{+}\bm{v}_{\ell}=(1-\eta\nu\lambda_{k})\sum_{\ell}\xi^{i}_{\ell}\bm{v}_{\ell}+\frac{\eta}{n}\sum_{j,\ell}\lambda_{\ell}\xi^{j}_{\ell}\bm{v}_{\ell}.

Projecting along
\bm{v}_{1}
,

(\xi^{i}_{1})^{+}=(1-\eta\nu\lambda_{k})\xi^{i}_{1}+\frac{\eta}{n}\lambda_{1}\sum_{j}\xi^{j}_{1}.

If
\bm{\xi}_{1}:=[\xi_{1}^{1}\ldots\xi_{1}^{n}]^{T}
, then these
n
difference equations can be written in vector form as

\bm{\xi}_{1}^{+}=(1-\eta\nu\lambda_{k})\bm{\xi}_{1}+\frac{\eta}{n}\lambda_{1}\mathds{1}\mathds{1}^{T}\bm{\xi}_{1}.

We need to compute the eigenvalues of the
n\times n
matrix
M(k,1)=(1-\eta\nu\lambda_{k})I+\frac{\eta}{n}\lambda_{1}\mathds{1}\mathds{1}^{T}
.
One such eigenvalues is associated to the consensus vector
\mathds{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

M(k,1)\mathds{1}=(1-\eta\nu\lambda_{k})\mathds{1}+\eta\lambda_{1}\mathds{1}=(1-\eta\nu\lambda_{k}+\eta\lambda_{1})\mathds{1}.

If
k\neq 1
then either
\lambda_{k}>0
and
\lambda_{k}<\lambda_{1}
, or
\lambda_{k}<0
. Since
\nu<1
, in both cases
1-\eta\nu\lambda_{k}+\eta\lambda_{1}>1
and the bipartite equilibrium point is unstable.
If
k=1
, then
1+\eta(1-\nu)\lambda_{1}>1
, i.e., also in this case we have found an eigenvalue outside the unit disk, and hence the equilibrium point is unstable.

Lemma 6

For the system (
9
), under Assumption
1
, all polygonal equilibria are unstable.

Proof.

At a polygonal equilibrium point
\bm{x}=\bm{s}=[\bm{s}_{1}\,\dots\,\bm{s}_{n}]
, consider the perturbation
\bm{x}_{i}=\bm{s}_{i}+\bm{w}_{i}
with
\bm{s}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}_{i}=0

\forall\,i
. For this perturbation, the unnormalized next iterate is

\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}=\bm{s}_{i}+\bm{w}_{i}+\frac{\eta}{n}{F_{V}}\sum_{j}(\bm{s}_{j}+\bm{w}_{j})=\bm{s}_{i}+\frac{\eta}{n}\underbrace{{F_{V}}\sum_{j}\bm{s}_{j}}_{=0}+\bm{w}_{i}+\frac{\eta}{n}{F_{V}}\sum_{j}\bm{w}_{j}

and its norm

\|\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}\|=\left(1+\|\bm{u}_{i}\|^{2}+2\frac{\eta}{n}\bm{s}_{i}^{T}{F_{V}}\sum_{j}\bm{w}_{j}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

where
\bm{u}_{i}:=\bm{w}_{i}+\frac{\eta}{n}{F_{V}}\sum_{j}\bm{w}_{j}
, and we have used
\bm{s}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}_{i}=0
.
Hence, using the binomial series expansion (
(1+\xi)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\approx 1-\frac{\xi}{2}+h.o.t.
),

\begin{split}\bm{x}_{i}^{+}&=\frac{\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}}{\|\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}\|}=\frac{\bm{s}_{i}+\bm{u}_{i}}{\left(1+\|\bm{u}_{i}\|^{2}+2\frac{\eta}{n}\bm{s}_{i}^{T}{F_{V}}\sum_{j}\bm{w}_{j}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\approx(\bm{s}_{i}+\bm{u}_{i})\left(1-\frac{\|\bm{u}_{i}\|^{2}}{2}-\frac{\eta}{n}\bm{s}_{i}^{T}{F_{V}}\sum_{j}\bm{w}_{j}\right)+h.o.t.\\
&=\bm{s}_{i}+\bm{u}_{i}-\frac{\eta}{n}\bm{s}_{i}^{T}{F_{V}}\sum_{j}\bm{w}_{j}\bm{s}_{i}+h.o.t.=\bm{s}_{i}+\bm{w}_{i}+\frac{\eta}{n}(I-\bm{s}_{i}\bm{s}_{i}^{T}){F_{V}}\sum_{j}\bm{w}_{j}+h.o.t.\end{split}

The linearized equation is then

\bm{w}_{i}^{+}=\bm{w}_{i}+\frac{\eta}{n}(I-\bm{s}_{i}\bm{s}_{i}^{T}){F_{V}}\sum_{j}\bm{w}_{j}.

Expanding all
\bm{w}_{i}
and
\bm{s}_{i}
in the orthonormal basis
\bm{v}_{1},\ldots,\bm{v}_{d}
, and denoting
\bm{w}_{i}=\sum_{\ell}\xi^{i}_{\ell}\bm{v}_{\ell}
and
\bm{s}_{i}=\sum_{\ell}\zeta^{i}_{\ell}\bm{v}_{\ell}
,

\begin{split}\bm{w}_{i}^{+}=&\sum_{\ell}(\xi^{i}_{\ell})^{+}\bm{v}_{\ell}=\sum_{\ell}\xi^{i}_{\ell}\bm{v}_{\ell}+\frac{\eta}{n}\left(I-\sum_{h}\zeta^{i}_{h}\bm{v}_{h}\sum_{\ell}\zeta^{i}_{\ell}\bm{v}_{\ell}^{T}\right)\sum_{j,\ell}\lambda_{\ell}\xi^{j}_{\ell}\bm{v}_{\ell}\\
=&\sum_{\ell}\xi^{i}_{\ell}\bm{v}_{\ell}+\frac{\eta}{n}\left(I-\sum_{h}\zeta^{i}_{h}\bm{v}_{h}\sum_{\ell}\zeta^{i}_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}\sum_{j}\xi^{j}_{\ell}\right).\end{split}

Recall that
{F_{V}}
has always at least one positive eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}>0
. We can always choose
\bm{w}
s.t. each
\bm{w}_{j}
has a nonzero component in the direction of the associated eigenvector
\bm{v}_{1}
:
\xi^{j}_{1}\neq 0
.
Projecting along
\bm{v}_{1}

(\xi^{i}_{1})^{+}=\xi^{i}_{1}+\frac{\eta}{n}\left(1-{\zeta_{1}^{i}}\sum_{\ell}\zeta^{i}_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}\sum_{j}\xi^{j}_{\ell}\right).

If the perturbation is such that
\bm{w}_{i}
has only a component along
\bm{v}_{1}
,
\xi_{1}^{i}\neq 0
,
\xi_{j}^{i}=0

\forall\,j\neq i
, then

(\xi^{i}_{1})^{+}=\xi^{i}_{1}+\frac{\eta}{n}(1-{\zeta_{1}^{i}}^{2})\lambda_{1}\sum_{j}\xi^{j}_{1}.

If
\bm{\xi}_{1}:=\begin{bmatrix}\xi_{1}^{1}&\ldots&\xi_{1}^{n}\end{bmatrix}^{T}
and
\bm{\zeta}_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}\zeta^{1}_{1}&\ldots&\zeta^{n}_{1}\end{bmatrix}^{T}
, then in vector form

\bm{\xi}_{1}^{+}=\bm{\xi}_{1}+\frac{\eta}{n}\lambda_{1}\left(I-Z_{1}^{2}\right)\mathds{1}\mathds{1}^{T}\bm{\xi}_{1}=\left(I+\frac{\eta}{n}\lambda_{1}\left(I-Z_{1}^{2}\right)\mathds{1}\mathds{1}^{T}\right)\bm{\xi}_{1}.

where
Z_{1}={\rm diag}(\bm{\zeta}_{1})
.
Since
\bm{s}
is not an eigenvector of
{F_{V}}
, it is
|\zeta^{i}_{1}|<1
. From
\lambda_{1}>0
, it is then
\rho(I+\eta\lambda_{1}(I-Z_{1}^{2})\mathds{1}\mathds{1}^{T})>1
, meaning that when
\bm{\xi}_{1}=\epsilon\mathds{1}
for some scalar coefficient
\epsilon
the system diverges, hence the polygonal equilibrium point

is unstable.

Remark 3

For the multiagent Oja system (

Lemma 7

The function
W(\bm{x})=-\frac{1}{2n^{2}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\bm{x}_{j}^{T}{F_{V}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\bm{x}_{j}\right)
is a Lyapunov function for (
9
). When
\eta\leq\frac{1}{2\|{F_{V}}\|}
it guarantees that (
9
) converges to one of the equilibria determined in Lemma
1
.

Proof.

Since the state space is compact, all functions arising in the expansion of the Lyapunov function
W(\bm{x})
(and hence also of its increment) are uniformly bounded. For the second order truncation of the increment
W(\bm{x}^{+})-W(\bm{x})
, it is possible to compute an explicit bound on
\eta
for which the increment is guaranteed to be nonincreasing.
From (
10
), using the binomial series expansion
(1+\xi)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\approx 1-\frac{\xi}{2}+\frac{3}{8}\xi^{2}+O(\xi^{3})

\begin{split}\bm{x}_{i}^{+}&=\frac{\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}}{\|\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}\|}=(\bm{x}_{i}+\eta\bm{y})\left(1-\eta\bm{x}_{i}^{T}\bm{y}+\frac{\eta^{2}}{2}\left(3(\bm{x}_{i}^{T}\bm{y})^{2}-\|\bm{y}\|^{2}\right)+O(\eta^{3})\right)\\
&=\bm{x}_{i}+\eta g_{i}+\eta^{2}\left(\frac{3}{2}r_{i}^{2}\bm{x}_{i}-\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{y}\|^{2}r_{i}-r_{i}\bm{y}\right)+O(\eta^{3})\end{split}

where we have indicated
r_{i}:=\bm{x}_{i}^{T}\bm{y}
and
g_{i}=g(\bm{x}_{i}):=(I-\bm{x}_{i}\bm{x}_{i}^{T})\bm{y}
.
The increment of
W
up to second order in
\eta
is then

\begin{split}W(\bm{x}^{+})&-W(\bm{x})=-\frac{1}{2n^{2}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\bm{x}_{j}^{+})^{T}{F_{V}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\bm{x}_{j}^{+}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\bm{x}_{j}^{T}{F_{V}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\bm{x}_{j}\right)\\
=&-\frac{\eta}{n}\sum_{j}g_{j}^{T}\bm{y}-\frac{\eta^{2}}{2n^{2}}\left(2n\bm{y}^{T}\sum_{j}\left(\frac{3}{2}r_{i}^{2}\bm{x}_{i}-\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{y}\|^{2}\bm{x}_{i}-r_{i}\bm{y}\right)+\sum_{j}g_{j}^{T}{F_{V}}\sum_{j}g_{j}\right)+O(\eta^{3})\\
=&-\frac{\eta}{n}\sum_{j}\|g_{j}\|^{2}-\frac{\eta^{2}}{n}\left(\sum_{j}\left(\frac{3}{2}r_{j}^{2}\bm{y}^{T}\bm{x}_{j}-\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{y}\|^{2}\bm{y}^{T}\bm{x}_{j}-r_{j}\bm{y}^{T}\bm{y}\right)+\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{j}g_{j}^{T}{F_{V}}\sum_{j}g_{j}\right)+O(\eta^{3})\\
&=-\frac{\eta}{n}\sum_{j}\|g_{j}\|^{2}-\frac{\eta^{2}}{n}\left(\frac{3}{2}\sum_{j}r_{i}(r_{i}^{2}-\|\bm{y}\|^{2})+\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{j}g_{j}^{T}{F_{V}}\sum_{j}g_{j}\right)+O(\eta^{3})\\
&=-\frac{\eta}{n}\sum_{j}\|g_{j}\|^{2}+\frac{\eta^{2}}{n}\left(\frac{3}{2}\sum_{j}r_{i}\|g_{j}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{j}g_{j}^{T}{F_{V}}\sum_{j}g_{j}\right)+O(\eta^{3})\\
\end{split}

where we have used idempotency of the projection
(I-\bm{x}_{i}\bm{x}_{i}^{T})
to get

\begin{split}g_{i}^{T}\bm{y}&=\bm{y}^{T}(I-\bm{x}_{i}\bm{x}_{i}^{T})\bm{y}=\bm{y}^{T}(I-\bm{x}_{i}\bm{x}_{i}^{T})(I-\bm{x}_{i}\bm{x}_{i}^{T})\bm{y}=\|g_{i}\|^{2}\\
\|\bm{y}\|^{2}-r_{i}^{2}&=\bm{y}^{T}\bm{y}-\bm{y}^{T}\bm{x}_{i}\bm{x}_{i}^{T}\bm{y}=\bm{y}^{T}(I-\bm{x}_{i}\bm{x}_{i}^{T})\bm{y}=\bm{y}^{T}(I-\bm{x}_{i}\bm{x}_{i}^{T})(I-\bm{x}_{i}\bm{x}_{i}^{T})\bm{y}=g_{i}^{T}g_{i}=\|g_{i}\|^{2}.\end{split}

Since
\|\sum_{j}g_{j}\|^{2}\leq n\sum_{j}\|g_{j}\|^{2}
, it is

-n\|{F_{V}}\|\sum_{j}\|g_{j}\|^{2}\leq\sum_{j}g_{j}^{T}{F_{V}}\sum_{j}g_{j}\leq n\|{F_{V}}\|\sum_{j}\|g_{j}\|^{2}.

Furthermore

-\|{F_{V}}\|\leq-\|\bm{y}\|\leq r_{i}\leq\|\bm{y}\|\leq\|{F_{V}}\|.

These lead to the inequality

W(\bm{x}^{+})-W(\bm{x})\leq-\frac{\eta}{n}\sum_{j}\|g_{j}\|^{2}+\frac{\eta^{2}}{n}\left(\frac{3}{2}\|{F_{V}}\|\sum_{j}\|g_{j}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|{F_{V}}\|\sum_{j}\|g_{j}\|^{2}\right)

or

W(\bm{x}^{+})-W(\bm{x})\leq\frac{\eta}{n}\left(-1+2\eta\|{F_{V}}\|\right)\sum_{j}\|g_{j}\|^{2}.

Hence when
\eta\leq\frac{1}{2\|{F_{V}}\|}
we have
W(\bm{x}^{+})-W(\bm{x})\leq 0
.
Since the sequence
W(\bm{x}(k))
(where
k
is the time index) is nonincreasing and bounded below, it must converge. Moreover, summability of the increments implies
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|g(\bm{x}_{i}(k))\|^{2}<\infty
, hence
\|g(\bm{x}_{i}(k))\|\to 0
for each
i
.
At an equilibrium point
\bm{x}
it must then be
g(\bm{x}_{i})=(I-\bm{x}_{i}\bm{x}_{i})\bm{y}=0
for all
i
, i.e.,
\bm{y}
must be collinear with
\bm{x}_{i}
. Recalling Lemma
1
, this implies that
\bm{x}
must be a consensus or a bipartite consensus equilibrium point.

D.3
Single-head self-attention dynamics

Let us now consider the self-attention system (
8
), which includes the attention matrix
A(\bm{x})
. For this model, an “individual” weighted average with state dependent weights given by the attention coefficients,
\bm{m}_{i}=\bm{m}_{i}(\bm{x})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{ij}(\bm{x})\bm{x}_{j}
replaces the simple average used in the multiagent Oja model.
Consequently, the total action of all agents on agent
i
varies from agent to agent:
\bm{y}_{i}=\bm{y}_{i}(\bm{x})={F_{V}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{ij}(\bm{x})\bm{\bm{x}}_{j}
.

D.3.1
Equilibria

In addition to the three classes of equilibria already obtained for the multiagent Oja flow, in the self-attention dynamics we have an extra class, due to the fact that
\bm{y}_{i}
now differs from agent to agent.

Lemma 8

The system (
8
) has the following classes of equilibria

1.

consensus:
\bm{x}_{i}=\bm{v}_{k}

\forall\,i
, and
k=1,\ldots,d
;

2.

bipartite consensus:
\bm{x}_{i}=\pm\bm{v}_{k}

\forall\,i
and
k=1,\ldots,d
;

3.

polygonal equilibria:
\{\bm{x}_{i}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\;\text{s.t.}\;{F_{V}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{ij}(\bm{x})\bm{x}_{j}=0\}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
;

4.

clustering equilibria:
\{\bm{x}_{i}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\;\text{s.t.}\;\varphi_{i}\bm{x}_{i}={F_{V}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{ij}(\bm{x})\bm{x}_{j}\}
for some scalars
\varphi_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
,
k=1,\ldots,d
.

Proof.

Once we replace
\bm{y}
with
\bm{y}_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{ij}(\bm{x}){F_{V}}\bm{\bm{x}}_{j}
, the proof of the first three cases is identical to that of Lemma
1
.
The clustering equilibria correspond to
\bm{y}_{i}\in\ker(I-\bm{x}_{i}\bm{x}_{i}^{T})
, or, equivalently,
\bm{y}_{i}
aligned with
\bm{x}_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
, but not necessarily aligned with an eigenvector
\bm{v}_{k}
, i.e.,
\bm{y}_{i}=\varphi_{i}\bm{x}_{i}
for some scalar
\varphi_{i}
, but possibly
\bm{y}_{i}\nparallel\bm{v}_{k}

\forall\,k
.

Remark 5

Clustering equilibria own their name to the fact that typically multiple agents are found at the same value. In particular we say that
\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{n}
are at an
m
-clustering equilibrium point if
\bm{x}_{i}\in\{\bm{w}_{1},\ldots,\bm{w}_{m}\}
with
1\leq m\leq n
, i.e., if
\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{n}
cluster at the
m
vectors
\bm{w}_{1},\ldots,\bm{w}_{m}
. Notice that some
\bm{w}_{i}
can be eigenvectors of
{F_{V}}
.

Remark 6

A clustering equilibrium can be a consensus, i.e,
\bm{x}_{i}=\bm{x}_{j}
for all
i,j
, but with
\bm{x}_{i}
which is not aligned with any eigenvector of
{F_{V}}
, i.e.,
\bm{x}_{i}\nparallel\bm{v}_{k}

\forall\,k
. We refer to these as 1-clustering, while the “consensus” characterization is reserved for the case
\bm{x}_{i}\parallel\bm{v}_{k}
for some
k
. A 2-clustering instead typically is s.t.
\bm{x}_{i}\in\{\bm{w}_{1},\bm{w}_{2}\}

\forall\,i
, with
\bm{w}_{1}\neq-\bm{w}_{2}
.

Proposition 1

The system (
8
) has a clustering equilibrium
\bm{x}\in(\mathbf{S}^{d-1})^{n}
iff
\exists
scalars
\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{n}
s.t. the matrix
I_{n}\otimes I_{d}-(\Theta\otimes I_{d})(A(\bm{x})\otimes I_{d})(I_{n}\otimes{F_{V}})
is singular, where
\Theta={\rm diag}(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{n})
.

Proof.

In vector form, the clustering equilibrium condition
\bm{x}_{i}=\theta_{i}{F_{V}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{ij}(\bm{x})\bm{\bm{x}}_{j}
becomes
\bm{x}=(\Theta\otimes I_{d})(A(\bm{x})\otimes I_{d})(I_{n}\otimes{F_{V}})\bm{x}
, where
\bm{x}^{T}=[\bm{x}_{1}^{T}\,\ldots\,\bm{x}_{n}^{T}]
.
Rewriting it as
(I_{n}\otimes I_{d}-(\Theta\otimes I_{d})(A(\bm{x})\otimes I_{d})(I_{n}\otimes{F_{V}}))\bm{x}=0
, the algebraic equation has nontrivial solutions if and only if the matrix
I_{n}\otimes I_{d}-(\Theta\otimes I_{d})(A(\bm{x})\otimes I_{d})(I_{n}\otimes{F_{V}})
is singular.

Remark 7

From the proof of Proposition
1
, it is
\bm{x}=(\Theta\otimes I_{d})(A(\bm{x})\otimes I_{d})(I_{n}\otimes{F_{V}})\bm{x}
, where
\bm{x}^{T}=[\bm{x}_{1}^{T}\,\ldots\,\bm{x}_{n}^{T}]
. It follows that if
\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{n}
form an
m
-clustering equilibrium point, then also the antipodal point
-\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,-\bm{x}_{n}
is an
m
-clustering equilibrium point because
A(\bm{x})=A(-\bm{x})
. If in a clustering equilibrium only some (but not all)
\bm{x}_{i}
flip sign, then the new state is typically no longer an equilibrium point.

Lemma 13

When
\eta<\min\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}},\,\frac{1}{|\lambda_{d}|}\right)
then
1+\eta\lambda_{k}\gamma_{i}^{k}>0
,
i=1,2
.

Proof.

Notice first that, since
|\gamma_{i}^{k}|\leq 1
,
|\lambda_{k}|\geq|\lambda_{k}\gamma_{i}^{k}|
, hence
\min\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}},\,\frac{1}{|\lambda_{d}|}\right)\leq\frac{1}{|\lambda_{k}|}\leq\frac{1}{|\lambda_{k}\gamma_{i}^{k}|}
.
Consequently,
\eta<\min\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}},\,\frac{1}{|\lambda_{d}|}\right)
implies
\eta|\lambda_{k}\gamma_{i}^{k}|\leq 1
, or
1-\eta|\lambda_{k}\gamma_{i}^{k}|>0
, which in turn implies
1+\eta\lambda_{k}\gamma_{i}^{k}\geq 0
,
i=1,2
.

This limitation on
\eta
implies
\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{2}=1
, which leads to a considerable simplification in the Jacobian terms of (
18
), and therefore in the calculation of its eigenvalues.

Lemma 14

\pm\bm{v}_{k}

\frac{1-(1-\eta\lambda_{k}\gamma_{1}^{k})\eta\lambda_{k}\gamma_{1}^{k}}{1+\eta\lambda_{k}\gamma_{1}^{k}}\

2.

,
b_{j}^{k}

14

3.

\frac{1}{1+\eta\lambda_{k}\gamma_{1}^{k}}
and
\frac{1}{1+\eta\lambda_{k}\gamma_{2}^{k}}

nd-n-2d+2

Proof.

For

1.

If

\bm{p}^{\ell}=[\,\underbrace{0\,\ldots\,0}_{\ell-1}\,\bm{v}_{k}^{T}\,\underbrace{0\,\ldots\,0}_{n-\ell}\,]^{T}

n

\begin{cases}J_{o}^{11}\bm{v}_{k}&=\frac{\eta\frac{\alpha_{1}^{k}}{\beta_{1}^{k}}\left({F_{V}}-\lambda_{k}\bm{v}_{k}\bm{v}_{k}^{T}\right)}{1+\eta\lambda_{k}\gamma_{1}^{k}}\bm{v}_{k}=\frac{\eta\frac{\alpha_{1}^{k}}{\beta_{1}^{k}}\left(\lambda_{k}\bm{v}_{k}-\lambda_{k}\bm{v}_{k}\right)}{1+\eta\lambda_{k}\gamma_{1}^{k}}=0\\

2.

Second class: Consider

Remark 8

As for the multiagent Oja system, in Lemma

Lemma 15

The polygonal equilibria are all unstable for (
8
).

Proof.

The idea of the proof is similar to that used in Lemma
6
for the multiagent Oja flow.
A polygonal equilibrium point
\bm{x}=\bm{s}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{s}_{1}&\ldots&\bm{s}_{n}\end{bmatrix}\in(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})^{n}
is s.t.
{F_{V}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{ij}(\bm{s})\bm{s}_{j}=0
. Let us compute the linearization of (
8

\bm{s}
, obtained perturbing
\bm{s}
with a perturbation
\bm{w}=[\bm{w}_{1}\ldots\bm{w}_{n}]
with
\bm{s}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}_{i}=0

\begin{split}\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}=&\bm{s}_{i}+\bm{w}_{i}+\frac{\eta}{n}{F_{V}}\sum_{j}A_{ij}(\bm{s}+\bm{w})(\bm{s}_{j}+\bm{w}_{j})\\
=&\bm{s}_{i}+\eta\underbrace{{F_{V}}\sum_{j}A(\bm{s})\bm{s}_{j}}_{=0}+\bm{w}_{i}+\eta{F_{V}}\sum_{j}A(\bm{s})\bm{w}_{j}+\eta{F_{V}}\sum_{j}\sum_{h}\frac{\partial A_{ij}(\bm{s})}{\partial\bm{w}_{h}}\bm{w}_{h}\bm{s}_{j}+h.o.t.\end{split}

where the vectors
\frac{\partial A_{ij}(\bm{s})}{\partial\bm{w}_{h}}
are computed in (
16

Denote
\psi^{ij}(\bm{w}):=\sum_{h}\frac{\partial A_{ij}(\bm{s})}{\partial\bm{w}_{h}}\bm{w}_{h}
and observe that
\psi^{ij}(\bm{w})
is a sum of bilinear forms
\bm{s}_{\ell}B_{k}\bm{w}_{h}

(see (

Since

is independent of
\bm{w}_{h}

Denoting
\bm{u}_{i}:=\bm{w}_{i}+\eta{F_{V}}\sum_{j}\left(\psi_{ij}(\bm{w})\bm{s}_{j}+A_{ij}(\bm{s})\bm{w}_{j}\right)

\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}

\|\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}\|=\left(1+\|\bm{u}_{i}\|^{2}+2\eta\bm{s}_{i}^{T}{F_{V}}\sum_{j}\left(\psi_{ij}(\bm{w})\bm{s}_{j}+A_{ij}(\bm{s})\bm{w}_{j}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

where we have used
\bm{s}_{i}^{T}\bm{w}_{i}=0
.
Observe that
\|\bm{u}_{i}\|^{2}
is composed entirely of terms of order 2 or higher in
\bm{w}_{i}
, hence, using the binomial series expansion (
(1+\xi)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\approx 1-\frac{\xi}{2}+h.o.t.

\begin{split}\bm{x}_{i}^{+}&=\frac{\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}}{\|\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}\|}\approx(\bm{s}_{i}+\bm{u}_{i})\left(1-\frac{\|\bm{u}_{i}\|^{2}}{2}-\eta\bm{s}_{i}^{T}{F_{V}}\sum_{j}\left(\psi_{ij}(\bm{w})\bm{s}_{j}+A_{ij}(\bm{s})\bm{w}_{j}\right)\right)+h.o.t.\\
&\approx\bm{s}_{i}-\eta\bm{s}_{i}^{T}{F_{V}}\sum_{j}\left(\psi_{ij}(\bm{w})\bm{s}_{j}+A_{ij}(\bm{s})\bm{w}_{j}\right)\bm{s}_{i}+\bm{u}_{i}+h.o.t.\\
&=\bm{s}_{i}-\eta\bm{s}_{i}\bm{s}_{i}^{T}{F_{V}}\sum_{j}\left(\psi_{ij}(\bm{w})\bm{s}_{j}+A_{ij}(\bm{s})\bm{w}_{j}\right)+\bm{w}_{i}+\eta{F_{V}}\sum_{j}\left(\psi_{ij}(\bm{w})\bm{s}_{j}+A_{ij}(\bm{s})\bm{w}_{j}\right)+h.o.t.\\
&=\bm{s}_{i}+\bm{w}_{i}+\eta(I-\bm{s}_{i}\bm{s}_{i}^{T}){F_{V}}\sum_{j}\left(\psi_{ij}(\bm{w})\bm{s}_{j}+A_{ij}(\bm{s})\bm{w}_{j}\right).\end{split}

The linearized equation is then

\bm{w}_{i}^{+}=\bm{w}_{i}+\eta(I-\bm{s}_{i}\bm{s}_{i}^{T}){F_{V}}\sum_{j}\left(\psi_{ij}(\bm{w})\bm{s}_{j}+A_{ij}(\bm{s})\bm{w}_{j}\right).

Expanding all
\bm{w}_{i}
and
\bm{s}_{i}
in the basis of eigenvectors
\bm{v}_{1},\ldots,\bm{v}_{d}
, and denoting
\bm{w}_{i}=\sum_{\ell}\xi^{i}_{\ell}\bm{v}_{\ell}
and
\bm{s}_{i}=\sum_{\ell}\zeta^{i}_{\ell}\bm{v}_{\ell}

\bm{w}_{i}^{+}=\sum_{\ell}(\xi^{i}_{\ell})^{+}\bm{v}_{\ell}=\sum_{\ell}\xi^{i}_{\ell}\bm{v}_{\ell}+\eta\Big(I-\sum_{k}\zeta^{i}_{k}\bm{v}_{k}\sum_{\ell}\zeta^{i}_{\ell}\bm{v}_{\ell}^{T}\Big)\sum_{j,\ell}\lambda_{\ell}(\psi_{ij}(\bm{w})\zeta^{j}_{\ell}+A_{ij}(\bm{s})\xi^{j}_{\ell})\bm{v}_{\ell}.

Assuming that the perturbation
\bm{w}
is aligned with
\bm{v}_{1}

\xi^{i}_{1}\neq 0
and
\xi^{i}_{k}=0
for

Denoting
\bm{\xi}_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}\xi^{1}_{1}&\ldots&\xi^{n}_{1}\end{bmatrix}^{T}

(20)

D.4
Extensions

D.4.1
Multi-head case

The multi-head case corresponds to the time-invariant dynamical system (
3
) of the paper, repeated here for convenience:

\bm{x}_{i}(t+1)=\frac{\bm{x}_{i}(t)+\eta F_{O}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\bar{F}_{V}^{(h)}\sum_{j}A_{ij}^{(h)}(\bm{x}(t))\bm{x}_{j}(t)}{\|\bm{x}_{i}(t)+\eta F_{O}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\bar{F}_{V}^{(h)}\sum_{j}A_{ij}^{(h)}(\bm{x}(t))\bm{x}_{j}(t)\|},\qquad\begin{array}[]{l}i=1,\ldots,n,\\
t=1,\ldots,T\end{array}

(21)

Recall that in this case what matters is the “layer matrix”
F=F_{O}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\bar{F}_{V}^{(h)}
.
For the system (
21
), in the main paper we stated Theorem
1
, which we can now prove as an easy consequence of Theorem
3
.

Proof of Theorem
1
(in the paper).
Under the assumption of the theorem, the system (
21
) has the consensus points at the eigenvectors of
F
as equilibria, as can be verified by an argument similar to Lemma
8
. In particular, when
\bm{x}_{i}
approaches a consensus point, the attention matrices of (
21
) still approach a uniform matrix:
A^{(h)}(\bm{x})\to\frac{1}{n}
,
h=1,\ldots,H
. Therefore we can use the same argument used in Theorem
3
: at consensus, the system (
21
) has the Jacobian
J(\bm{v}_{k})
of the multiagent Oja system (
9
), whose eigenvalues were computed in Lemma
3
. Consensus aligned with the principal eigenvector
\bm{v}_{1}
is locally asymptotically stable, while all other consensus points at the other eigenvectors of
F
are unstable.

While the picture for consensus is easily obtainable from that of the single-head case, what happens to the bipartite consensus points is less clear.
We currently have no (analytical nor numerical) evidence of such points being stable equilibria for (
21
).

D.4.2
Longer step size

To check stability of the consensus equilibria a condition on the step size is needed only when
\lambda_{d}<0
, and
|\lambda_{d}|>\lambda_{1}
, i.e., when the spectral radius of
F
(or
{F_{V}}
for the single-head case) is
|\lambda_{d}|
, not
\lambda_{1}
.
Extra conditions on
\eta
are required only for checking the stability of the bipartite consensus points.
These extra conditions are only sufficient, and meant to simplify the expression of the eigenvalues of the Jacobian
J(\pm\bm{v}_{k})
, see Lemma
13
.
As mentioned above, locally asymptotically stable bipartite consensus equilibria appear to be rare, even in the single-head case.

D.4.3
Asymmetric value matrix

As mentioned in Section
2.1
of the paper, what really matters to capture the asymptotic behavior of the self-attention dynamics (and to establish the analogy with the power method) is that the dominant eigenvalue of
F
,
\lambda_{1}
, is real, while
\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{d}
can be any.
This together with the strict dominance of
\lambda_{1}
is the essence of the Perron-Frobenius theorem extended beyond nonnegative matrices.
In fact, when the dominance is strict (and the spectral radius is equal to
\lambda_{1}
) not much changes in the behavior of the single-head system (
8
) and neither of its multi-head version (
3
) by considering an
F
which is not symmetric.
A numerical analysis of the asymmetric
F
case is carried out in the main paper when dealing with the ALBERT transformer.
