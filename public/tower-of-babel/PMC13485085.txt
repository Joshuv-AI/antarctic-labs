Clinical profile and perioperative risk factors for post-cataract surgery nonarteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy in an Indian population: An emulated target trial
Authors: Madhumita Gopal, Rohan Arora, Nirmala Devy
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Purpose:Cataract surgery is associated with a four times increased risk of nonarteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy (NAION). This paper presents the first report of patients from Indian ethnicity with post-cataract surgery NAION (psNAION) and aims to evaluate perioperative risk factors for prediction of psNAION.Methods:A retrospective evaluation of patients who developed psNAION was done and clinical characteristics noted. An emulated target trial to evaluate the role of perioperative blood pressure and blood sugar, and postoperative intraocular pressure was conducted with controls matched for age, sex, systemic and ocular comorbidities with known risk of NAION, grade of cataract, type of surgery, type of anesthesia, and intraoperative complications.Results:Nineteen patients (8 males, 11 females) aged 66.6 ± 7.7 years developed psNAION 80.8 ± 50.6 days after cataract surgery. Five (21.1%) patients had no known systemic risk factors for NAION. Three patients presented <7 days and 16 presented >6 weeks from surgery. None of the evaluated variables (perioperative blood pressure and blood sugar, and postoperative intraocular pressure) correlated significantly with increased risk of psNAION.Conclusion:Onset of psNAION is much later in Indian patients. All patients had classical features of NAION, and clinical evaluation was sufficient to establish diagnosis. One-fifth of patients had no known coexisting risk factors. Perioperative control of blood pressure or blood sugar, and postoperative intraocular pressure were not associated with increased risk.

Nonarteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy (NAION) is among the leading causes of visual impairment secondary to optic nerve disease in adults above 50 years of age.[1] Pathophysiology of NAION is not clear and has been hypothesized to be secondary to insufficiency or infarcts in the para-optic branches of short posterior ciliary arteries.[2] Events thereafter are not well understood, but a final common pathway causes optic nerve edema in a crowded disc,[3] resulting in apoptotic ganglion cell death.[4]

NAION affects men and women nearly equally.[5] Sleep apnea[6] and vasculopathy, secondary to dyslipidemia, diabetes mellitus, or systemic hypertension,[5] are identified as risk factors and can cause venous insufficiency, nocturnal hypoperfusion, or dysfunction of vascular autoregulation of the optic nerve. Optic nerve head drusen[5] and small optic nerve discs or cups[7] are postulated to exacerbate mechanical obstruction to axoplasmic flow with secondary compression and ischemia. Phosphodiesterase-5 (PDE-5) inhibitors and smoking have been associated with NAION, but recent evidence points against significant independent risk.[89]

Post-cataract surgery NAION (psNAION) is a recognized entity. Cataract surgery is associated with a four times greater risk of NAION, and older age at the time of surgery has been shown to be the only significant risk factor for psNAION.[10] Most cases occur within 6 months of surgery, with bimodal distribution within 72 hours and 6 weeks post cataract surgery.[10] psNAION within 3 days of surgery is believed to be due to high intraocular pressure (IOP), regional anesthesia, and intraoperative hypotension,[11] and early psNAION is thought to be secondary to postoperative inflammation causing vasculogenic optic disc edema.[12]

So far, no cases have been reported of patients from Indian ethnicity, and there are limited data about the role of perioperative known risk factors for prevention or prediction of psNAION. In this paper, we aim to describe the clinical profile of Indian patients with psNAION and its incidence. We aim to assess the predictive value of potential modifiable perioperative risk factors by means of an emulated target trial to evaluate if modifiable risk factor control could potentially be beneficial.

Methods

Design and settings

A retrospective evaluation of electronic medical records of patients treated at a tertiary eye hospital in southern India between April 2022 and April 2023 was done after approval from the Institutional Ethics Committee. All investigations and treatments were in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, and the patients’ privacy was protected in compliance with HIPAA guidelines.

Participants

Cases

All patients who developed NAION within 6 months of cataract surgery by phacoemulsification or manual small incision cataract surgery (M-SICS) were considered as psNAION for the purpose of this study and were considered for inclusion. Patients with pre-existing optic nerve disease, glaucoma except lens-induced glaucoma, amblyopia, and corneal opacities precluding posterior segment evaluation were excluded from the study. Diagnosis of NAION was established by a neuro-ophthalmologist based on clinical evaluation and typical clinical features such as sudden loss of vision, presence of a relative afferent pupillary defect, altitudinal visual field defects, and optic disc edema. Doubtful cases were evaluated by magnetic resonance imaging.

Controls

For the purpose of performing an emulated target trial, simple random sampling in a ratio of 1 case: 3 controls was done. Patients who had undergone cataract surgery by phacoemulsification or M-SICS over the same time frame but had not developed NAION within 1 year of surgery and had followed up at least 1 year after surgery were considered for inclusion and subject to the same exclusion criteria as cases. All controls underwent uneventful cataract surgery. Each case was matched with three controls with identical age, sex, systemic and ocular comorbidities with known risk of NAION, grade of cataract (mature or immature), type of surgery (M-SICS or Phacoemulsification), and type of anesthesia (Sub-tenon’s anesthesia or topical) to mitigate confounding.

Procedure

All patients had a preoperative assessment by A-Scan biometry, measurement of blood pressure (BP), and random blood sugar (RBS). All patients underwent cataract surgery with either M-SICS or phacoemulsification with intraocular lens implantation. All patients who underwent M-SICS were operated under regional Sub-Tenon’s anesthesia (STA), and patients who underwent phacoemulsification were operated under topical anesthesia, STA, or peribulbar anesthesia. Three milliliters of Lignocaine 2% was injected infero-nasally into the posterior sub-tenon space using a curved bent cannula for all patients who underwent surgery under STA. Proparacaine 0.5% was used for topical anesthesia. Postoperatively, all were given topical Dexamethasone 0.1% 6 times per day, tapered 1 drop per week, topical Ofloxacin 0.3% thrice daily for 15 days, and topical Nepafenac 0.1% thrice daily for 1 month. All patients were followed up on postoperative days 1 and 21. The presentation of patients with psNAION, and the first visit at least 1 year after surgery for controls were considered as the end point for the study. Visual acuity and IOP were measured at all visits along with detailed anterior and posterior segment evaluation. Color vision and central fields were evaluated for patients with psNAION. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) was performed for patients in whom a definitive diagnosis could not be established based on clinical evaluation. Preoperative (systolic BP, diastolic BP, and RBS) and postoperative (postoperative IOP and inflammation) risk factors were compared between cases and controls.

Variables and measurements

Visual acuity was measured using Snellen’s chart at 3 m and converted to logMAR equivalents. Blood pressure was measured in millimeters of mercury (mmHg) using digital manometers (SunTech Medical Inc., North Carolina, USA) and blood sugar in milligrams per deciliter (mg/dL) using handheld glucometers (i-SENS Inc., Seoul, South Korea). IOP was measured in mmHg using Goldmann’s Applanation Tonometry (Haag-Streit, Köniz, Switzerland). Color vision was evaluated using Ishihara’ pseudoisochromatic plates, and central visual fields were checked using Humphrey Field Analyser (HFA) 3 (ZEISS, Baden-Württemberg, Germany), Bjerrum’s tangent screen at 1 m, or confrontation depending on the patient’s visual acuity and co-operation.

Statistical analyses

All data were encoded in Google Sheets (Google Inc., California, USA), and statistical analysis was performed using Google Sheets and SPSS (StataCorp, Texas, USA). Two-tailed, heteroscedastic, unpaired t-test was applied to analyze quantitative variables. P-values < 0.05 were considered as statistically significant.

Results

Clinical profile

Nineteen patients (8 males, 11 females) aged 66.6 ± 7.7 years presented with psNAION [Table 1]. Three patients developed psNAION within the first week of surgery. All others presented more than 6 weeks after surgery. Four patients had a history of spontaneous NAION in the contralateral eye. All patients except one had a demonstrable relative pupillary afferent defect (Bell’s grade 1–3) on Levantin’s swinging flashlight test, and all patients who had visual acuity permissible to test color vision had dyschromatopsia in the affected eye. At presentation, eight patients had visual fields documented, among whom four had an inferior altitudinal defect, two had an inferonasal defect, one had an inferotemporal defect, and one had a superior altitudinal defect in the affected eye. No patients had bilateral psNAION.

Demographics and patient profile at presentation with psNAION with associated risk factors

On fundoscopic examination, four patients had diabetic retinopathy (two nonproliferative, two proliferative) and one patient had a retinochoroidal coloboma. The optic nerve head at presentation showed either diffuse edema (14 eyes, 73.6%) or pallid edema (5 eyes, 26.4%). The contralateral disc was small in 9 patients (47.4%) and normal in 10 patients (52.6%).

Systemically, two patients were asthmatic, two had renal dysfunction, and one patient each had atrial fibrillation, hypothyroidism, and history of myocardial infarction. All systemic parameters were under control prior to surgery for all patients. History of obstructive sleep apnea and PDE-5 inhibitors could not be assessed due to the retrospective nature of the study.

Intraoperatively, two patients had a posterior capsule rent and two patients had intraoperative pupillary miosis requiring use of iris hooks. One patient was operated for lens-induced glaucoma. Apart from development of psNAION, the postoperative period was uneventful for all patients and none of them had an anterior chamber reaction on follow-up 21 days postoperatively.

MRI was done for eight patients. Seven patients showed cerebral small vessel ischemic changes, among whom one had an infarct in the pons. No patients showed any abnormality of the optic nerve. One patient had a normal MRI of the brain and optic nerves.

There were no significant differences in presentation on subgroup analyses between male and female patients.

Risk factor evaluation

Nineteen patients were compared with 57 controls matched for age, sex, systemic and ocular comorbidities with known risk of NAION, grade of cataract, type of surgery, and type of anesthesia. Differences between preoperative systolic BP, diastolic BP, random blood sugar, and postoperative day 21 IOP [Table 2] were evaluated as risk factors for development of psNAION. No factors were found to be significant for prediction of psNAION.

Incidence

Over the study period, the incidence of psNAION was 0.028% for patients who underwent phacoemulsification (7 in 25,353) and 0.047% for patients who underwent M-SICS (12 in 25,705) at our center.

Discussion

Cataract surgery is associated with a fourfold increase in chance of NAION,[10] and the first case of psNAION was reported as early as 1946.[13] By meta-analysis, older age at surgery is the only factor associated with a significant increase in risk, while diabetes mellitus, hypertension, dyslipidemia, obstructive sleep apnea, smoking, PDE-5 inhibitor intake, or ischemic events were not associated with increased chances of psNAION.[10] While some studies suggest the presence of spontaneous NAION in one eye is a significant risk factor for contralateral psNAION,[14] others have not found any significant correlation.[15] As such, psNAION is a poorly understood entity.

All patients were diagnosed clinically with classical features of NAION. Fourteen (78.9%) patients had at least one known systemic risk factor for NAION (diabetes mellitus, systemic hypertension, or dyslipidemia) which could potentially be contributory. Only 3 (15.8%) of our patients presented within 6 weeks of surgery, in contrast to 39%[10] reported in the literature. High postoperative IOP (>28 mmHg) within the first 3 days has been postulated as the mechanism for early psNAION,[16] and all our patients having normal postoperative IOP could possibly explain the lack of early psNAION within our sample. Postoperative inflammation has been postulated as the mechanism for delayed psNAION;[12] however, significant inflammation was not noted in any of our patients on postoperative evaluation.

Risk factor evaluation was aimed at identifying predictive and potentially preventive values of preoperative variables measured for cataract surgery after controlling for as many confounders as possible. Blood pressure, blood sugar levels, and IOP were evaluated as modifiable risk factors which could potentially predict chances of psNAION and possibly be modulated to reduce risk. However, none of the evaluated variables correlated significantly with increased risk of psNAION.

M-SICS had a 67% more chance of psNAION compared to phacoemulsification. This could possibly be due to a larger corneal wound, more anterior chamber fluctuations, and IOP variations causing an ischemic insult to the optic nerve head. Overall, both phacoemulsification and M-SICS had a very low incidence of psNAION in our sample.

This study is limited by its retrospective design, making it susceptible to selection bias, incomplete capture of perioperative hemodynamic and metabolic fluctuations, unmeasured confounding, and inability to establish causal relationships. The limited sample size further restricts statistical power to detect modest associations and restricts generalizability of the findings. Measurement of blood sugar with handheld glucometers and blood pressure with digital manometers alone is not ideal and was done at only a single point of time, which may not reflect long-term control status. Long-term glycemic control, diabetic retinopathy, surgical time, and intraoperative IOP fluctuations were not analyzed. Systemic and ocular risk factor evaluations between cases and controls could not be compared after the occurrence of psNAION as these parameters were not available in the similar timeline following cataract surgery in controls due to the retrospective nature of the study. Exhaustive matching of controls precludes comparison of matched features such as systemic comorbidities, intraoperative complications, and type of anesthesia, between cases and controls. Visual field documentation could not be performed by HFA for all patients. Follow-up and final visual outcome for patients have not been evaluated. No evaluation was done for possible interventions to see the effect of modifiable risk factor modulation.

Conclusion

Despite these limitations, the study provides the first reported clinical data on psNAION in an Indian population and suggests that the condition remains rare and attempts to evaluate potential preoperative risk factors. Our subset of patients had a longer gap between presentation and cataract surgery compared to earlier reports. Five patients among 19 with psNAION had no known risk factors for NAION. Preoperative BP, preoperative RBS, and postoperative IOP were not associated with increased chances of psNAION in our study. However, associations or lack thereof needs to be interpreted with due caution in view of single measurement of BP and RBS and limited number of cases being evaluated. The incidence of psNAION was low, regardless of the technique of cataract surgery. Review of literature suggests reasonable external validity of results. Future prospective, randomized studies are needed for more robust evaluation of risk factors, pathophysiology, and management strategies.
