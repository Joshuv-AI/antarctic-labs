A Measure-Theoretic Finite-Sample Theory for Adaptive-Data Fitted Q-Iteration
Manuel Haußmann, Mustafa Mert Çelikok, Melih Kandemir

Abstract
While reinforcement learning (RL) promises to revolutionize the control of complex nonlinear robotic systems, a profound gap persists between the heuristic success of model-free off-policy deep RL and the underlying theory, which remains largely confined to tabular or linearizable settings. We identify the cause of this gap as an emergent isolation of three traditions: (i) measure-theoretic MDP foundations on general spaces limit their analysis to exact dynamic programming and ignore all error sources of a learning process; (ii) deterministic error propagation analysis addresses the approximation error via concentrability coefficients without a finite-sample analysis of the estimation error; and (iii) PAC generalization bounds characterize the estimation errors of simplified topologies. We bridge these traditions with a unified theoretical framework for fitted Q-iteration (FQI) on general measurable Borel spaces. Our main result provides a finite-sample, adaptive-data performance bound by chaining measure-theoretic probability with Bellman-operator contraction in Banach spaces. We prove that sequential Rademacher complexity controls Bellman-regression generalization under policy-dependent data collection. We further extend this analysis to provide the first cumulative, pathwise online regret guarantee for FQI in continuous spaces. These results lay the necessary foundations for the formal analysis of many modern deep RL algorithms.

Specifically, the classical traditions that establish the Borel state space and Bellman contraction properties lack the statistical complexity measures necessary for finite-sample guarantees. Conversely, modern online analyses provide sharp finite-sample bounds, but they almost universally revert to discrete topologies or assume the distribution-mismatch mechanism without proving the necessary measure-theoretic infrastructure. Consequently, no existing regret bound for online RL with function approximation is formally justified on the continuous state-action spaces where the field achieves its greatest successes in applications.

We bridge these isolated traditions with a self-contained theoretical framework for Fitted Q-Iteration (FQI) on general Borel spaces. We anchor our analysis in FQI because it serves as the fundamental algorithmic template for modern off-policy deep RL, where iterative Bellman updates are approximated through supervised regression. Our three-stage derivation (covering sequential generalization, per-iteration residuals, and final policy suboptimality) cleanly separates sequential complexity, martingale concentration, and Bellman approximation error. A residual-certificate proposition identifies the controls that upgrade these to a cumulative online regret theorem. The Measure-Theoretic Reinforcement Learning Framework

Preliminaries.

To apply tabular reinforcement learning to general spaces, we establish the necessary measure-theoretic infrastructure, including the joint measurability of transition kernels and the existence of measurable optimal selectors
(
Bertsekas and Shreve 1978
)
. By defining measurable spaces
(S,\Sigma_{S})
and
(A,\Sigma_{A})
for states and actions, we ensure optimal values and greedy policies are well defined. Let
\Delta(X,\Sigma_{X})
denote the space of probability measures on a measurable space
(X,\Sigma_{X})
. For any
B\in\Sigma_{X}
, define the evaluation mapping
{e_{B}:\Delta(X,\Sigma_{X})\to[0,1]}
by
e_{B}(\mu)=\mu(B)
for
\mu\in\Delta(X,\Sigma_{X})
. Equip
\Delta(X,\Sigma_{X})
with the evaluation
\sigma
-algebra
{\Sigma_{\Delta}\triangleq\sigma(\{e_{B}^{-1}(E):B\in\Sigma_{X},E\in\mathrm{Borel}([0,1])\})}
. Define a
Markov kernel
from
(S,\Sigma_{\!S})
to
(A,\Sigma_{\!A})
as a
(\Sigma_{\!S},\Sigma_{\Delta})
-measurable mapping
\kappa:S\to\Delta(A,\Sigma_{\!A})
, and write
\mathcal{K}(S\to A)
for the space of such kernels. For any measurable space
(X,\Sigma_{X})
, denote by
\mathcal{B}(X,\Sigma_{X})
the Banach space of bounded
\Sigma_{X}
-measurable real-valued functions on
X
, equipped with the supremum norm
\|f\|_{\infty}\triangleq\sup_{x\in X}|f(x)|
.

Definition 1

(MDP)
.

An infinite-horizon discounted
Markov Decision Process (MDP)
is a tuple
(S,\Sigma_{\!S},A,\Sigma_{\!A},P,r,\gamma)
, where
P\in\mathcal{K}(S\times A\to S)
is the transition kernel,
r\in\mathcal{B}(S\times A,\Sigma_{\!S}\otimes\Sigma_{\!A})
is a bounded measurable reward function with
\|r\|_{\infty}\leq R_{\max}
,
\gamma\in[0,1)
is the discount factor, and the set of all stationary Markov policies is denoted by
\Pi\triangleq\mathcal{K}(S\to A)
.

To analyze iterative RL convergence, we view action values not as look-up tables but as elements of a structured function space. Let
{\mathcal{Q}\triangleq\mathcal{B}(S\times A,\Sigma_{\!S}\otimes\Sigma_{\!A})}
denote the Banach space of bounded measurable action-value functions. We work in this space because Banach completeness ensures iterative updates have well-defined limits. In this general setting, Bellman operators must be redefined as functional mappings on
\mathcal{Q}
so recursive updates preserve topological and measurable structure.

Definition 2

(Bellman operators)
.

The
Bellman expectation operator

T^{\pi}:\mathcal{Q}\to\mathcal{Q}
and the
Bellman optimality operator

T^{*}:\mathcal{Q}\to\mathcal{Q}
are defined for any
Q\in\mathcal{Q}
by:

\displaystyle(T^{\pi}Q)(s,a)

\displaystyle\triangleq r(s,a)+\gamma\int_{S}\int_{A}Q(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})\pi(da^{\prime}|s^{\prime})P(ds^{\prime}|s,a),

(1)

\displaystyle(T^{*}Q)(s,a)

\displaystyle\triangleq r(s,a)+\gamma\int_{S}\sup_{a^{\prime}\in A}Q(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})P(ds^{\prime}|s,a).

(2)

For a fixed policy
\pi
, we define the policy-conditioned transition operator
P^{\pi}:\mathcal{Q}\to\mathcal{Q}
by
(P^{\pi}Q)(s,a)\triangleq\int_{S}\int_{A}Q(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})\,\pi(da^{\prime}|s^{\prime})\,P(ds^{\prime}|s,a)
. Since
P
and
\pi
are probability kernels,
P^{\pi}
is a nonexpansive linear operator, i.e.,
\|P^{\pi}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
. Under this notation, the expectation operator satisfies
(T^{\pi}Q)(s,a)=r(s,a)+\gamma(P^{\pi}Q)(s,a)
.

Assumption 1

(Action-space regularity)
.

S
is a Borel space and
(A,\Sigma_{\!A})
is a compact metrizable topological space
equipped with its Borel
\sigma
-algebra. Moreover:

(i)

is upper semicontinuous (USC; see
Definition

4
) on
S\times A
;

(ii)

the transition kernel

is weakly continuous on

for every bounded continuous
{h:S\to\mathds{R}}
, the map

(s,a)\mapsto\int_{S}h(s^{\prime})\,P(ds^{\prime}|s,a)
is continuous on
S\times A
;

(iii)

the class
\mathcal{Q}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{Q}
on which the operators act
consists of bounded measurable
Q
that are jointly USC on
S\times A
.

Under condition (iii) and the compactness of
A
,
Berge’s maximum theorem yields that
{s^{\prime}\mapsto\sup_{a^{\prime}}Q(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})}
is USC on
S
(in particular Borel-measurabe), and conditions (i)–(ii) guarantee that joint USC is preserved under iteration of
T^{*}
(
Lemma

2
). Two further hurdles complete the infrastructure:
the existence of measurable
\varepsilon
-optimal policies (selectors), guaranteed

2.1
Exact Dynamic Programming

Building on the framework above, we now characterise reinforcement learning in the limit of infinite representational capacity. The foundation is the contraction property of the Bellman operators on the Banach space
(\mathcal{Q},\|\cdot\|_{\infty})
, which yields existence and uniqueness of fixed points for the measure-theoretically defined operators and ensures the mathematical consistency of
Q^{*}
and
Q^{\pi}
.

Theorem 1

(Bellman contraction
(
Bertsekas and Shreve 1978
, e.g.,)
)
.

For every measurable policy
\pi
, the Bellman expectation operator
T^{\pi}
is a self-map on
\mathcal{Q}
and a
\gamma
-contraction on
(\mathcal{Q},\|\cdot\|_{\infty})
. Under
1
, the Bellman optimality operator
T^{*}
is a self-map on
\mathcal{Q}_{0}
and a
\gamma
-contraction on
(\mathcal{Q}_{0},\|\cdot\|_{\infty})
: for all
Q,\bar{Q}
in the respective domains,

\|T^{\pi}Q-T^{\pi}\bar{Q}\|_{\infty}\leq\gamma\|Q-\bar{Q}\|_{\infty},\qquad\|T^{*}Q-T^{*}\bar{Q}\|_{\infty}\leq\gamma\|Q-\bar{Q}\|_{\infty}.

(3)

As
\mathcal{Q}
is a Banach space and
\mathcal{Q}_{0}
is closed in it under uniform convergence, the Banach fixed-point theorem yields unique fixed points
Q^{\pi}\in\mathcal{Q}
and
Q^{*}\in\mathcal{Q}_{0}
satisfying
T^{\pi}Q^{\pi}=Q^{\pi}
and
T^{*}Q^{*}=Q^{*}
.

These fixed points admit a direct trajectory-measure characterisation on general Borel spaces: for any measurable policy

{(S_{0},A_{0},S_{1},A_{1},\ldots)\in(S\times A)^{\mathds{N}}}

{Q^{*}=\sup_{\pi\in\Pi}Q^{\pi}}
admits a measurable greedy selector via the Jankov–von Neumann theorem. We write
{V^{\pi}(s)\triangleq\int_{A}Q^{\pi}(s,a)\,\pi(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}a|s)}
and
{V^{*}(s)\triangleq\sup_{a\in A}Q^{*}(s,a)}
for the value functions. The standard policy-iteration analysis on this Banach space, combining the monotonicity of

and

with greedy identity
{T^{\pi_{k+1}}Q^{\pi_{k}}=T^{*}Q^{\pi_{k}}}

{\|Q^{*}-Q^{\pi_{k}}\|_{\infty}\to 0}

Appendix

C

Off-policy Fitted Q-Iteration (FQI)

Transitioning from exact dynamic programming to practical machine learning requires bridging operator theory with statistical learning theory. We restrict action-values to a parameterized function class
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{Q}
, transforming Bellman updates into a data-driven approximation task. Since tabular action-value representation is impossible here, the standard approach is
Fitted Q-Iteration (FQI)
, summarized in

starting from an initial

via
{\Pi_{\mathcal{F},\mu_{k}}H\triangleq\argmin_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\|f-H\|_{2,\mu_{k}}}

\{\mu_{k}\}\subset\Delta(S\times A)

Algorithm

uses the greedy operator
\sup_{a^{\prime}\in A}Q_{k}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})

\mathcal{F}
is convex and closed in

is a

\Pi_{\mathcal{F},\mu}\circ T^{*}
may fail to be a contraction.
This norm mismatch between the

{\|\varepsilon_{k}\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon}

Distribution mismatch and concentrability.
Bridging the gap between the training distribution
\mu

\rho

3
Adaptive-Data FQI via Sequential Rademacher Complexity

In modern off-policy deep RL
(
Haarnoja et al. 2018
;
Fujimoto et al. 2018
;
Chen et al. 2021
)
, the agent populates a replay buffer while interacting with the environment, inducing a history-dependent, non-i.i.d sampling process. At every round
k
, a behavior policy derived from the current iterate
\widehat{Q}_{k}
collects a batch of transitions whose conditional law depends on the entire interaction history. Such adaptive, policy-dependent data collection violates the i.i.d. hypothesis underlying
Theorems

9
and
2
. We extend the analysis to this adaptive regime by combining the residual-propagation machinery of
Theorem

7
with sequential Rademacher complexity, which controls empirical-process deviations along arbitrary predictable trees
(
Rakhlin et al. 2015
)
. We follow a three-stage logical sequence: first, a sequential generalization theorem for adaptive Bellman regression (
Theorem

2
); second, a per-iteration FQI update-residual bound (
Theorem

3
); and third, a final greedy-policy performance guarantee under adaptive concentrability (
Theorem

4
). A residual-certificate result (
Proposition

1
) addresses control of the diagonal Bellman residual
{\widehat{Q}_{t}-T^{*}\widehat{Q}_{t}}
required to upgrade this final-policy guarantee to a cumulative pathwise online regret theorem.
Appendix

E
discusses the necessary coverage and algorithmic ingredients (including state-action concentrability over
\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}
, diagonal residual minimization, iterate stability, and optimism) and explains why these requirements are kept out of the main result. We emphasise that the analysis is strictly off-policy: the data-generating distribution need not match the occupancy measure of the learned or optimal policy, and the mismatch handled via concentrability coefficients rather than on-policy sampling assumptions.

3.1
Adaptive transition data and residual classes

To analyze FQI under adaptive data collection, we characterize the interaction between the function class
\mathcal{F}
and the non-i.i.d. sampling process. We work on the transition space

\bar{\mu}_{k}\triangleq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mu_{k,i},\qquad\|h\|_{2,\bar{\mu}_{k}}^{2}\triangleq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathds{E}\left[h(S_{k,i},A_{k,i})^{2}\mid\mathcal{H}_{k},Z_{k,1},\ldots,Z_{k,i-1}\right].

(5)

This formulation is agnostic to the data-collection mechanism: sampling may depend arbitrarily on past data and the current iterate.
The samplewise Bellman labels are
Y_{k,i}\triangleq R_{k,i}+\gamma\sup_{a^{\prime}\in A}\widehat{Q}_{k}(S_{k,i}^{\prime},a^{\prime})
, and the empirical squared Bellman loss is

Assumption 4

(Approximate Bellman ERM)
.

At every round

\widehat{L}_{k}(\widehat{Q}_{k+1};\widehat{Q}_{k})\;\leq\;\inf_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\widehat{L}_{k}(f;\widehat{Q}_{k})+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{opt},k}^{2}
for some optimization tolerance

itself appears in

and

5

4

The empirical-process step is phrased on transition-level function classes built from
\mathcal{F}
. For
{f,g\in\mathcal{F}}
, define the
sample Bellman residual

\widetilde{g}_{f,g}(z)\triangleq f(s,a)-r-\gamma\sup_{a^{\prime}\in A}g(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})
for

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathcal{F}}\triangleq\{z\mapsto\widetilde{g}_{f,g}(z)^{2}:f,g\in\mathcal{F}\}

{Z=(S,A,R,S^{\prime})}
is generated from the MDP conditional on

, and every

We control the empirical process via

, which generalizes classical Rademacher complexity from i.i.d. samples to the

of

and matches the martingale structure of adaptive Bellman residuals. The trade-off versus eluder-based alternatives
(
Russo and Van Roy 2013
;

;

)
follows

4
.

Definition 3

(
Rakhlin et al. 2015
)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a class of measurable functions

. A

of depth

with
{x_{t}:\{-1,+1\}^{t-1}\to Z}

of

R^{\mathrm{seq}}_{n}(\mathcal{A})\triangleq\sup_{\mathbf{x}}\,\mathds{E}_{\varepsilon}\biggl[\sup_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\,\sum_{t=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{t}\,a\bigl(x_{t}(\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{t-1})\bigr)\biggr],

(6)

where

When the tree is constant,

3.2
Sequential generalization for adaptive Bellman regression

To quantify statistical error under adaptive sampling, we establish a uniform concentration bound for the Bellman losses using the sequential Rademacher framework. The empirical and conditional-mean squared Bellman losses at round
k
are defined, respectively, as

\displaystyle\widehat{L}_{k}(f;g)\triangleq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\widetilde{g}_{f,g}(Z_{k,i})^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad L_{k}(f;g)\triangleq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathds{E}\left[\widetilde{g}_{f,g}(Z_{k,i})^{2}\mid\mathcal{H}_{k},Z_{k,1},\ldots,Z_{k,i-1}\right].

The next result provides a finite-sample bound on the uniform deviation between these empirical and population quantities using the sequential Rademacher complexity of the Bellman loss class.

Theorem 2

(Sequential generalization for adaptive Bellman regression)
.

Under
1
,
2
and
3
, for every
{\delta\in(0,1)}
and every round
k
, conditional on the history
\mathcal{H}_{k}
, with probability at least
1-\delta
,

\sup_{f,g\in\mathcal{F}}\,\bigl|L_{k}(f;g)-\widehat{L}_{k}(f;g)\bigr|\;\leq\;\alpha_{k}(\delta)\;\triangleq\;\frac{2}{n}\,R^{\mathrm{seq}}_{n}(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathcal{F}})+B_{\mathrm{res}}^{2}\sqrt{\frac{2\log(2/\delta)}{n}}.

(7)

By the sequential contraction principle,
R^{\mathrm{seq}}_{n}(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathcal{F}})\leq 2B_{\mathrm{res}}\,R^{\mathrm{seq}}_{n}(\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{F}})
, so
\alpha_{k}(\delta)
may be replaced by

\alpha_{k}^{\prime}(\delta)\triangleq\frac{4B_{\mathrm{res}}}{n}\,R^{\mathrm{seq}}_{n}(\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{F}})+B_{\mathrm{res}}^{2}\sqrt{\frac{2\log(2/\delta)}{n}}.

(8)

The conditioning on
\mathcal{H}_{k}
is essential: once the past is fixed,
\widehat{Q}_{k}
enters
\widetilde{g}_{f,\widehat{Q}_{k}}
as a deterministic function, and the martingale-difference structure of
{\widetilde{g}_{f,g}(Z_{k,i})^{2}-\mathds{E}\left[\widetilde{g}_{f,g}(Z_{k,i})^{2}\mid\cdot\right]}
is preserved. The bound (
7
) controls empirical-process deviations for the adaptive Bellman regression class; by itself, it does not yield a regret guarantee for the greedy policy sequence.

3.3
Adaptive-data FQI residual control

Having established uniform concentration for the adaptive empirical process, we now translate these statistical guarantees into a bound on the one-step Bellman residuals realized by the FQI iterates. For an iterate
\widehat{Q}_{k}\in\mathcal{F}
, denote by
h_{k}\triangleq T^{*}\widehat{Q}_{k}
the population Bellman target and define the round-
k
population residual as
\varepsilon_{k}\triangleq\|\widehat{Q}_{k+1}-T^{*}\widehat{Q}_{k}\|_{2,\bar{\mu}_{k}}
and the inherent Bellman approximation error as
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{app},k}\triangleq\inf_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\,\|f-T^{*}\widehat{Q}_{k}\|_{2,\bar{\mu}_{k}}
.
The following theorem decomposes the realized Bellman residual
\varepsilon_{k}
into its structural, statistical, and optimization-driven components.

Theorem 3

(Adaptive-data FQI residual bound)
.

Suppose
1
,
2
,
3
and
4
hold and that the iterates
{\widehat{Q}_{0},\ldots,\widehat{Q}_{K}\in\mathcal{F}}
. Then, for every
\delta\in(0,1)
, with probability at least
1-\delta

k\in\{0,\ldots,K-1\}

\varepsilon_{k}\;\leq\;\varepsilon_{\mathrm{app},k}+\sqrt{2\,\alpha_{k}^{\prime}(\delta/K)}+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{opt},k},

(9)

where
\alpha_{k}^{\prime}
is the sequential Rademacher rate of (
8
) (one may equivalently use
\alpha_{k}
from (
7
)).

The bound controls the FQI
update
residual
{\widehat{Q}_{k+1}-T^{*}\widehat{Q}_{k}}
, not the diagonal residual
{\widehat{Q}_{k}-T^{*}\widehat{Q}_{k}}
that governs online regret; we return to this distinction in
Section

3.5
. When the data are i.i.d. from a fixed
\mu
,
\bar{\mu}_{k}=\mu
and
R^{\mathrm{seq}}_{n}
specialises to the standard Rademacher complexity, so (
9
) reduces to the unsquared-
L^{2}
slow rate underlying

2

by
R^{\mathrm{seq}}_{n}

is approximately Bellman-complete in the adaptive sense, the approximation term simplifies.

Corollary 1

(Bellman-complete adaptive FQI)
.

In addition to the assumptions of
Theorem

3
, suppose there exists
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Bell}}\geq 0
such that,
\mathds{P}
-almost surely,

\inf_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\,\|f-T^{*}g\|_{2,\bar{\mu}_{k}}\;\leq\;\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Bell}}\quad\text{for every }g\in\mathcal{F}\text{ and every }k\in\{0,\ldots,K-1\}.

Because

is itself random (
\mathcal{H}_{k}

{\varepsilon_{k}\leq\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Bell}}+\sqrt{2\alpha_{k}^{\prime}(\delta/K)}+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{opt},k}}
holds for all

with probability at least
1-\delta
. Exact Bellman completeness

corresponds to

.

The Bellman-completeness hypothesis must be stated separately:

1

. This distinction is critical because, while the measurability of the target is guaranteed by the MDP structure, its representability within the hypothesis class

is a separate structural requirement.
Corollary

3.4
Main result: Adaptive-data FQI performance bound

The per-round residuals in (
9
) are measured with respect to the adaptive design measures
\bar{\mu}_{k}
, whereas policy performance is evaluated under the target distribution
\rho
. To bridge this distribution shift and propagate errors through the FQI iterations, the training distributions must provide sufficient coverage of the states visited by the final greedy policy. We therefore introduce an adaptive extension of the standard concentrability coefficient in (
4
).

Assumption 5

(Adaptive concentrability)
.

For each
k\in\{0,\ldots,K-1\}
, let
\mathcal{N}_{k}
denote the (finite) collection of state–action measures of the form
\rho B
, where
B
ranges over the products of Markov kernels drawn from
\{P^{\pi^{*}},P^{\widehat{\pi}_{K}}\}\cup\{P^{\pi^{g}_{\ell}}\}_{\ell}
that arise as multipliers of
|\varepsilon_{k}|
when the V-loss expansion of
Theorem

7
is combined with
Lemma

4
for the final greedy policy
\widehat{\pi}_{K}
. There exists

such that,
\mathds{P}
-almost surely, every
\nu_{k}\in\mathcal{N}_{k}
satisfies
\nu_{k}\ll\bar{\mu}_{k}
and
\bigl\|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu_{k}/\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\bar{\mu}_{k}\bigr\|_{\infty}\leq C_{\mathrm{ad}}
.

When
\bar{\mu}_{k}
collapses to a single fixed
\mu
(the i.i.d. case),

reduces to the all-policy concentrability coefficient
C
of (
4
).
5
requires coverage of the residual-evaluation measures of the
final
greedy policy
\widehat{\pi}_{K}
by the predictable design measures
\bar{\mu}_{k}
; the stronger condition required to upgrade this to a cumulative, pathwise online regret statement is discussed in
Appendix

E
. This assumption enables the error components derived in

3
to yield a final performance guarantee.

Theorem 4

(Adaptive-data FQI performance bound)
.

Under
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
and
5
, for every
\delta\in(0,1)
the greedy policy
\widehat{\pi}_{K}
extracted from

satisfies, with probability at least

\|V^{*}-V^{\widehat{\pi}_{K}}\|_{1,\rho}\;\leq\;\frac{4\sqrt{C_{\mathrm{ad}}}}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\,(1-\gamma^{K})\,\max_{0\leq k<K}\,\bigl(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{app},k}+\sqrt{2\,\alpha_{k}^{\prime}(\delta/K)}+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{opt},k}\bigr)\;+\;\frac{8B\gamma^{K}}{1-\gamma}.

(10)

Here

denotes the value of the policy obtained by acting greedily with respect to
\widehat{Q}_{K}
at every state and executing it in the environment from the initial distribution
\rho
.

When the data are i.i.d. from a fixed
\mu
,
\bar{\mu}_{k}=\mu
,

, and
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{app},k}\leq\varepsilon_{\mathrm{approx}}
of (
31
). On constant trees the sequential Rademacher complexity coincides with the classical Rademacher complexity,

, so the rate

takes the same

scaling as

of
Corollary

2

. The two bounds are not numerically identical, however:
Corollary

2
symmetrises over the function class
\mathcal{F}
, exploiting that the regression target

at round

is independent of the round-
k
batch under the fresh-batch protocol;

4
symmetrises over the strictly larger Bellman residual class
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{F}}
indexed by pairs
(f,g)\in\mathcal{F}\times\mathcal{F}
, the same complexity adopted by the single-batch variant of

3

The

3.5
From residual certificates to online regret

Theorem

4
bounds the expected suboptimality of the
final
greedy policy
\widehat{\pi}_{K}
under the evaluation distribution
\rho
. By contrast, a
cumulative online regret
statement controls the pathwise total suboptimality of the policy sequence
\{\widehat{\pi}_{t}\}
executed at the visited states
s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}
, governed by the diagonal Bellman residual
{\widehat{Q}_{t}-T^{*}\widehat{Q}_{t}}
. The two notions are linked only through this diagonal residual.

Proposition 1

(Online regret from diagonal Bellman residuals)
.

Let
{\widehat{Q}_{t}\in\mathcal{Q}_{0}}
be adapted and bounded, and let
\widehat{\pi}_{t}
be a measurable greedy policy for
\widehat{Q}_{t}
. Then, for every sequence
s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}\in S
,

\sum_{t=1}^{n}\bigl(V^{*}(s_{t})-V^{\widehat{\pi}_{t}}(s_{t})\bigr)\;\leq\;\frac{2}{1-\gamma}\,\sum_{t=1}^{n}\|\widehat{Q}_{t}-T^{*}\widehat{Q}_{t}\|_{\infty}.

(11)

In particular, if with probability at least
1-\delta
one has
{\sum_{t=1}^{n}\|\widehat{Q}_{t}-T^{*}\widehat{Q}_{t}\|_{\infty}\leq\mathcal{E}_{n}}
, then with the same probability
{\sum_{t=1}^{n}(V^{*}(s_{t})-V^{\widehat{\pi}_{t}}(s_{t}))\leq 2\mathcal{E}_{n}/(1-\gamma)}
.

The proposition is intentionally phrased as conditional on the existence of a diagonal residual certificate.
Theorem

3
controls the FQI
update
residual
{\widehat{Q}_{k+1}-T^{*}\widehat{Q}_{k}}
, whereas online regret is governed by the diagonal fixed-point residual
{\widehat{Q}_{t}-T^{*}\widehat{Q}_{t}}
. Converting the former into the latter requires either iterate stability (control of
{\sum_{t}\|\widehat{Q}_{t}-\widehat{Q}_{t-1}\|_{\infty}}
) or an exploration mechanism that keeps the residual small at the visited states.
Appendix

E
discusses these routes in detail.

4
Open Questions

While our framework establishes a rigorous metric-aware foundation for adaptive-data FQI on continuous spaces, it leaves three critical directions for future investigation. First, the main result in
Theorem

4
bounds the suboptimality of the final greedy policy
\widehat{\pi}_{K}
under adaptive concentrability.
Appendix

E
extends this analyis to cumulative online regret governing the entire policy sequence
\{\widehat{\pi}_{t}\}
, requiring additional control of the diagonal residual
{\widehat{Q}_{t}-T^{*}\widehat{Q}_{t}}
via either iterate stability, direct diagonal-residual minimisation, or optimism-based exploration. Extending the analysis to directed exploration strategies such as Upper Confidence Bound (UCB)
(
Azar et al. 2017
;
Tiapkin et al. 2022b
)
or Thompson Sampling (TS)
(
Osband et al. 2013
;
Agrawal and Jia 2017
;
Tiapkin et al. 2022a
)
would clarify how complexity-weighted uncertainty estimates drive sample efficiency in sparse-reward environments. Second, our analysis assumes approximate ERM with a single optimisation tolerance. Integrating cutting-edge stochastic optimisation theory would bridge theoretical global optima and the empirical landscapes of neural networks, providing a holistic characterisation of the total error budget in deep reinforcement learning. Third, the adaptive concentrability hypothesis requires that the predictable design measures cover the residual-evaluation measures of the final policy. Relaxing this requirement and quantifying the trade-off in
coverage-deficient
settings, specifically those where neither passive coverage nor explicit exploration is available, remains an open problem.

Definition 4

be a topological space. A function

P

(s,a)\mapsto\int_{S}\int_{A}Q(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})\,\pi(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}a^{\prime}|s^{\prime})\,P(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}|s,a)\in\mathcal{Q}
;
applied to
P
alone, together with part (i), it gives

(s,a)\mapsto\int_{S}\sup_{a^{\prime}\in A}Q(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})\,P(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}|s,a)\in\mathcal{Q}
. Adding the
bounded measurable reward
r
preserves measurability in both cases, so

T^{\pi}Q\in\mathcal{Q}
(for
Q\in\mathcal{Q}
) and
T^{*}Q\in\mathcal{Q}
(for
Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{0}
). The
uniform bound

\|T^{\pi}Q\|_{\infty},\ \|T^{*}Q\|_{\infty}\leq R_{\max}+\gamma\|Q\|_{\infty}

follows from the triangle inequality,
\|r\|_{\infty}\leq R_{\max}
, and the fact that both
kernel integrals are bounded in absolute value by
\|Q\|_{\infty}
.
∎

Lemma 2

(Joint USC propagation)
.

Under
1
, if
Q\in\mathcal{Q}
is bounded measurable and jointly
USC on
S\times A
, then
T^{*}Q
is bounded measurable and jointly USC on

S\times A
. In particular,
\mathcal{Q}_{0}
is closed under
T^{*}
, and the value-
iteration Picard iterates remain in
\mathcal{Q}_{0}
.

Proof.

By compactness of
A
and joint USC of
Q
, Berge’s maximum theorem gives that

{s^{\prime}\mapsto\sup_{a^{\prime}\in A}Q(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})}
is bounded USC on
S
. By joint weak
continuity of
P
(
1
(ii)) and the Portmanteau theorem
applied to bounded USC integrands,
(s,a)\mapsto\int_{S}\sup_{a^{\prime}}Q(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})\,P(ds^{\prime}|s,a)
is jointly USC on
S\times A
. Adding
r
, jointly USC by

1
(i), preserves joint USC. Hence
T^{*}Q
is jointly USC.
∎

C.1
Bellman contraction

Proof of
Theorem

1
.

Lemma

1
shows that
T^{\pi}
maps
\mathcal{Q}
into
\mathcal{Q}
, and
Lemma

2
shows that
T^{*}
maps
\mathcal{Q}_{0}
into
\mathcal{Q}_{0}
under
1
; hence both operators are self-maps on the spaces stated in the theorem.

Bellman expectation operator.

Let
Q,\bar{Q}\in\mathcal{Q}
and fix
(s,a)\in S\times A
. The rewards cancel, so

\displaystyle|(T^{\pi}Q)(s,a)-(T^{\pi}\bar{Q})(s,a)|

\displaystyle=\gamma\left|\int_{S}\int_{A}\bigl(Q(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})-\bar{Q}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})\bigr)\,\pi(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}a^{\prime}|s^{\prime})\,P(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}|s,a)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\gamma\int_{S}\int_{A}|Q(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})-\bar{Q}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})|\,\pi(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}a^{\prime}|s^{\prime})\,P(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}|s,a)

\displaystyle\leq\gamma\|Q-\bar{Q}\|_{\infty}\underbrace{\int_{S}\pi(A|s^{\prime})\,P(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}|s,a)}_{=\,1}.

Taking the supremum over
(s,a)
gives

\|T^{\pi}Q-T^{\pi}\bar{Q}\|_{\infty}\leq\gamma\|Q-\bar{Q}\|_{\infty}
.

Bellman optimality operator.

Let
Q,\bar{Q}\in\mathcal{Q}_{0}
. Using that for bounded
f,g

{|\sup_{a}f(a)-\sup_{a}g(a)|\leq\sup_{a}|f(a)-g(a)|}
, we have

\displaystyle|(T^{*}Q)(s,a)-(T^{*}\bar{Q})(s,a)|

\displaystyle=\gamma\left|\int_{S}\Bigl[\sup_{a^{\prime}}Q(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})-\sup_{a^{\prime}}\bar{Q}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})\Bigr]\,P(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}|s,a)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\gamma\int_{S}\sup_{a^{\prime}}|Q(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})-\bar{Q}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})|\,P(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}|s,a)

\displaystyle\leq\gamma\|Q-\bar{Q}\|_{\infty}.

Taking the supremum over
(s,a)
gives

\|T^{*}Q-T^{*}\bar{Q}\|_{\infty}\leq\gamma\|Q-\bar{Q}\|_{\infty}
.

Fixed points.

(\mathcal{Q},\|\cdot\|_{\infty})
is a Banach space;
\mathcal{Q}_{0}
is a closed subspace under
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
, since the uniform limit of bounded measurable jointly-USC functions is again bounded, measurable, and jointly USC. Hence
(\mathcal{Q}_{0},\|\cdot\|_{\infty})
is a complete metric space. Combining self-mapness with
\gamma
-contraction, the Banach fixed-point theorem applied to
T^{\pi}
on
\mathcal{Q}
and to
T^{*}
on
\mathcal{Q}_{0}
yields unique fixed points
Q^{\pi}\in\mathcal{Q}
and
Q^{*}\in\mathcal{Q}_{0}
with
T^{\pi}Q^{\pi}=Q^{\pi}
and
T^{*}Q^{*}=Q^{*}
. The result is classical on general Borel spaces; see
Bertsekas and Shreve 1978
and
Hernández-Lerma and Lasserre 1996
.
∎

Lemma 3

(Bellman monotonicity)
.

Let
Q,\bar{Q}\in\mathcal{Q}
with
Q\leq\bar{Q}
pointwise on
S\times A
. Then
T^{\pi}Q\leq T^{\pi}\bar{Q}
for every measurable policy
\pi
, and, if additionally
Q,\bar{Q}\in\mathcal{Q}_{0}
, then
T^{*}Q\leq T^{*}\bar{Q}
.

Proof.

For
T^{\pi}
, the integrand
Q(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})-\bar{Q}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})\leq 0
pointwise, and integration against the non-negative product kernel
\pi(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}a^{\prime}|s^{\prime})\,P(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}|s,a)
preserves the inequality, giving
(T^{\pi}Q)(s,a)\leq(T^{\pi}\bar{Q})(s,a)
. For
T^{*}
on
\mathcal{Q}_{0}
, monotonicity of the supremum yields
\sup_{a^{\prime}}Q(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})\leq\sup_{a^{\prime}}\bar{Q}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})
pointwise in
s^{\prime}
; the kernel
P(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}|s,a)
is non-negative, so integration preserves the inequality, giving
(T^{*}Q)(s,a)\leq(T^{*}\bar{Q})(s,a)
.
∎

C.2
Policy iteration convergence and dominance

Theorem 6

(Policy iteration convergence and dominance)
.

Let
\pi_{0}
be a measurable policy and set
Q_{0}\triangleq Q^{\pi_{0}}
. Define the value-iteration sequence by
Q_{k+1}\triangleq T^{*}Q_{k}
, and the exact policy-iteration sequence by selecting, given
\pi_{k}
, a measurable policy
\pi_{k+1}
with

T^{\pi_{k+1}}Q^{\pi_{k}}=T^{*}Q^{\pi_{k}},

(12)

and letting
Q^{\pi_{k+1}}
denote the fixed point of
T^{\pi_{k+1}}
from
Theorem

1
; the existence of such a measurable greedy
\pi_{k+1}
is guaranteed by
Theorem

5
under
1
. Then, for all
k\in\mathds{N}
,
(i)
Q^{\pi_{k}}\leq Q^{\pi_{k+1}}
pointwise (monotonic improvement);
(ii)
{Q_{k}\leq Q^{\pi_{k}}\leq Q^{*}}
pointwise (dominance); and
(iii)
{\|Q^{*}-Q^{\pi_{k}}\|_{\infty}\to 0}
as
k\to\infty
(uniform convergence).

Proof of
Theorem

6
.

Throughout, all inequalities between elements of
\mathcal{Q}
are pointwise on
S\times A
. The proof combines monotonicity (
Lemma

3
),
\gamma
-contraction (
Theorem

1
), and the greedy identity (
12
); measurability of each
\pi_{k+1}
is supplied by
Theorem

5
under
1
.

Step 1: Monotonic improvement,
Q^{\pi_{k}}\leq Q^{\pi_{k+1}}
.

By the greedy identity (
12
) and the fixed-point equation
T^{\pi_{k}}Q^{\pi_{k}}=Q^{\pi_{k}}
,

T^{\pi_{k+1}}Q^{\pi_{k}}\;=\;T^{*}Q^{\pi_{k}}\;\geq\;T^{\pi_{k}}Q^{\pi_{k}}\;=\;Q^{\pi_{k}}.

Iterating
T^{\pi_{k+1}}
and using
Lemma

3
,

Q^{\pi_{k}}\;\leq\;T^{\pi_{k+1}}Q^{\pi_{k}}\;\leq\;(T^{\pi_{k+1}})^{2}Q^{\pi_{k}}\;\leq\;\cdots\;\leq\;(T^{\pi_{k+1}})^{m}Q^{\pi_{k}}.

By
\gamma
-contraction of
T^{\pi_{k+1}}
,
(T^{\pi_{k+1}})^{m}Q^{\pi_{k}}\to Q^{\pi_{k+1}}
in
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
, hence pointwise; passing to the limit in the chain above gives
Q^{\pi_{k}}\leq Q^{\pi_{k+1}}
.

Step 2:
Q_{k}\leq Q^{\pi_{k}}
by induction on
k
.

The base case is the initialization
Q_{0}=Q^{\pi_{0}}
. Assume
Q_{k}\leq Q^{\pi_{k}}
. Then

Q_{k+1}\;=\;T^{*}Q_{k}\;\leq\;T^{*}Q^{\pi_{k}}\;=\;T^{\pi_{k+1}}Q^{\pi_{k}}\;\leq\;Q^{\pi_{k+1}},

using, in order: the value-iteration recurrence; monotonicity of
T^{*}
(
Lemma

3
); the greedy identity (
12
); and Step 1 in the form
T^{\pi_{k+1}}Q^{\pi_{k}}\leq Q^{\pi_{k+1}}
.

Step 3:
Q^{\pi_{k}}\leq Q^{*}
.

For any measurable policy
\pi
, the inequality
\sup_{a^{\prime}}Q^{*}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})\geq\int_{A}Q^{*}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})\,\pi(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}a^{\prime}|s^{\prime})
holds pointwise in
s^{\prime}
, so

T^{\pi}Q^{*}\;\leq\;T^{*}Q^{*}\;=\;Q^{*},

where the equality is the Bellman fixed-point identity from
Theorem

1
. By
Lemma

3
, iterating
T^{\pi}
preserves the inequality
T^{\pi}Q^{*}\leq Q^{*}
, so
(T^{\pi})^{m}Q^{*}\leq Q^{*}
for every
m\geq 1
. By
\gamma
-contraction of
T^{\pi}
,
(T^{\pi})^{m}Q^{*}\to Q^{\pi}
in
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
; taking the pointwise limit gives
Q^{\pi}\leq Q^{*}
. Specializing to
\pi=\pi_{k}
yields
Q^{\pi_{k}}\leq Q^{*}
. Combined with Step 2 this proves the dominance chain
Q_{k}\leq Q^{\pi_{k}}\leq Q^{*}
.

Step 4: Uniform convergence by squeezing.

By
\gamma
-contraction of
T^{*}
on
\mathcal{Q}_{0}
(
Theorem

1
) and
Q^{*}=T^{*}Q^{*}
,

\|Q_{k+1}-Q^{*}\|_{\infty}\;=\;\|T^{*}Q_{k}-T^{*}Q^{*}\|_{\infty}\;\leq\;\gamma\|Q_{k}-Q^{*}\|_{\infty},

so
\|Q_{k}-Q^{*}\|_{\infty}\leq\gamma^{k}\|Q_{0}-Q^{*}\|_{\infty}\to 0
. The dominance chain
Q_{k}\leq Q^{\pi_{k}}\leq Q^{*}
rearranges to
0\leq Q^{*}-Q^{\pi_{k}}\leq Q^{*}-Q_{k}
pointwise, and taking suprema yields

\|Q^{*}-Q^{\pi_{k}}\|_{\infty}\;\leq\;\|Q^{*}-Q_{k}\|_{\infty}\;\to\;0,

which proves (iii).
∎

C.3
Error propagation

Lemma 4

(Error propagation via greedy policy kernels)
.

Let
\{Q_{k}\}_{k\in\mathds{N}}\subset\mathcal{Q}
be a sequence of bounded measurable
action-value functions with per-step Bellman residuals

\varepsilon_{k}\triangleq Q_{k+1}-T^{*}Q_{k}
. Assume that
Q^{*}
admits a measurable
greedy selector
\pi^{*}
and that, for each
k\geq 0
,
Q_{k}
admits a
measurable greedy selector
\pi^{g}_{k}
; under
1
, this
holds in particular whenever
Q_{k}\in\mathcal{Q}_{0}
, in which case both selectors
are supplied by
Theorem

5
. Then for every
k\geq 1
, the pointwise estimate

|Q_{k}-Q^{*}|\leq\gamma^{k}\,A_{k}\,|Q_{0}-Q^{*}|+\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\gamma^{k-1-i}\,A_{k,i}\,|\varepsilon_{i}|

(13)

holds on
S\times A
, where

A_{k}\triangleq(P^{\pi^{*}})^{k}+P^{\pi^{g}_{k-1}}\cdots P^{\pi^{g}_{0}}
and

A_{k,i}\triangleq(P^{\pi^{*}})^{k-1-i}+P^{\pi^{g}_{k-1}}\cdots P^{\pi^{g}_{i+1}}

are sums of products of linear Markov kernels, with the empty product at

i=k-1
interpreted as the identity operator. In particular,

\|Q_{k}-Q^{*}\|_{\infty}\leq 2\gamma^{k}\,\|Q_{0}-Q^{*}\|_{\infty}+2\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\gamma^{k-1-i}\,\|\varepsilon_{i}\|_{\infty}.

(14)

Proof of
Lemma

4
.

All inequalities below are pointwise on
S\times A
. The argument has
four steps: a one-step sandwich on
Q_{k+1}-Q^{*}
(Step 1); induction
producing signed pointwise bounds (Step 2); combination into the
absolute-value estimate (
13
) (Step 3); and sup-norm
specialisation (Step 4).

Step 1 (one-step sandwich).

For any
Q\in\mathcal{Q}
and any measurable policy
\pi
, the definition of

T^{*}
yields
T^{*}Q\geq r+\gamma P^{\pi}Q
, with equality whenever

\pi
is a greedy selector for
Q
. Applying this with
\pi=\pi^{*}

(equality at
Q^{*}
, inequality at
Q_{k}
) and with
\pi=\pi^{g}_{k}

(equality at
Q_{k}
, inequality at
Q^{*}
), and adding
\varepsilon_{k}
to both
sides of the resulting bracket on
T^{*}Q_{k}-T^{*}Q^{*}
, gives the signed
one-step recursions

\gamma\,P^{\pi^{*}}(Q_{k}-Q^{*})+\varepsilon_{k}\;\leq\;Q_{k+1}-Q^{*}\;\leq\;\gamma\,P^{\pi^{g}_{k}}(Q_{k}-Q^{*})+\varepsilon_{k}.

(15)

Step 2 (signed bounds by induction).

We show that for every
k\geq 1
,

\displaystyle Q_{k}-Q^{*}

\displaystyle\geq-\gamma^{k}(P^{\pi^{*}})^{k}(Q^{*}-Q_{0})+\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\gamma^{k-1-i}\,(P^{\pi^{*}})^{k-1-i}\,\varepsilon_{i},

(16)

\displaystyle Q_{k}-Q^{*}

\displaystyle\leq-\gamma^{k}\,P^{\pi^{g}_{k-1}}\!\cdots P^{\pi^{g}_{0}}\,(Q^{*}-Q_{0})+\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\gamma^{k-1-i}\,P^{\pi^{g}_{k-1}}\!\cdots P^{\pi^{g}_{i+1}}\,\varepsilon_{i},

(17)

with the empty product at
i=k-1
in (
17
) read as the
identity. The base case
k=1
is (
15
) at
k=0
. For
the inductive step, applying the positive linear operator
\gamma P^{\pi^{*}}

to (
16
) at index
k
and substituting into the lower
branch of (
15
) promotes every
(P^{\pi^{*}})^{j}
to

(P^{\pi^{*}})^{j+1}
and adds a new
\varepsilon_{k}
term, yielding
(
16
) at index
k+1
. The argument for
(
17
) is analogous, with
\gamma P^{\pi^{g}_{k}}
applied to
the inductive hypothesis: each iteration prepends a fresh greedy kernel,
producing a non-stationary product
P^{\pi^{g}_{k}}\cdots P^{\pi^{g}_{i+1}}

of length
k-i
multiplying
\varepsilon_{i}
, rather than a power.

Step 3 (absolute-value estimate).

Let
L_{k}
and
U_{k}
denote the right-hand sides of (
16
)
and (
17
), so
L_{k}\leq Q_{k}-Q^{*}\leq U_{k}
and
therefore
|Q_{k}-Q^{*}|\leq\max(|L_{k}|,|U_{k}|)\leq|L_{k}|+|U_{k}|
.
Because
P^{\pi}
integrates against a probability kernel,

|P^{\pi}g|\leq P^{\pi}|g|
pointwise for every bounded measurable
g
,
and this property passes to arbitrary products
P^{\pi_{1}}\cdots P^{\pi_{m}}
.
Pushing absolute values through each kernel product in
L_{k}
and
U_{k}

and collecting yields (
13
) with
A_{k}
and
A_{k,i}

as stated.

Step 4 (sup-norm specialisation).

Each Markov kernel satisfies
\|P^{\pi}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
, hence so does
every product of such kernels; therefore
\|A_{k}\|_{\infty},\|A_{k,i}\|_{\infty}\leq 2
. Taking
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
in
(
13
) and applying these bounds termwise yields
(
14
).
∎

Lemma 5

(Error propagation via the max-transition operator)
.

Let
\{Q_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be as in
Lemma

4
, with per-step
residuals
\varepsilon_{k}\triangleq Q_{k+1}-T^{*}Q_{k}
. Define the nonlinear
max-transition operator, for nonnegative bounded measurable
g
on
S\times A

with
s^{\prime}\mapsto\sup_{a^{\prime}}g(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})
measurable,

(\mathbf{P}g)(s,a)\triangleq\int_{S}\sup_{a^{\prime}\in A}g(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})\,P(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}|s,a).

(18)

Under
1
, the quantities
|Q_{k}-Q^{*}|
and
|\varepsilon_{i}|
lie
in the domain of
\mathbf{P}
for every
k,i
(see
Remark

2
). Then for
every
k\geq 1
,

|Q_{k}-Q^{*}|\leq\gamma^{k}\,\mathbf{P}^{k}\,|Q_{0}-Q^{*}|+\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\gamma^{k-1-i}\,\mathbf{P}^{k-1-i}\,|\varepsilon_{i}|

(19)

pointwise on
S\times A
, and

\|Q_{k}-Q^{*}\|_{\infty}\leq\gamma^{k}\,\|Q_{0}-Q^{*}\|_{\infty}+\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\gamma^{k-1-i}\,\|\varepsilon_{i}\|_{\infty}.

(20)

Proof of
Lemma

5
.

All inequalities are pointwise on
S\times A
; write
u_{k}\triangleq|Q_{k}-Q^{*}|

and
e_{i}\triangleq|\varepsilon_{i}|
.

One-step recursion.

For every
s^{\prime}\in S
, the elementary sup-difference inequality

{|\sup_{a^{\prime}}Q(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})-\sup_{a^{\prime}}Q^{\prime}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})|\leq\sup_{a^{\prime}}|Q-Q^{\prime}|(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})}

holds. Since rewards cancel in
{T^{*}Q-T^{*}Q^{\prime}}
, Jensen’s inequality against

gives

{|T^{*}Q-T^{*}Q^{\prime}|\leq\gamma\,\mathbf{P}\,|Q-Q^{\prime}|}

\mathbf{P}
is monotone and sublinear on its

) by applying
\mathbf{P}

termwise to the inductive hypothesis yields (
19
)
by induction on
k\geq 1
, with base case the
k=0
instance of
(

).

A probability kernel integrates bounded functions to at most their

for every nonnegative

).
∎

Remark 2

(Measurability in the domain of
\mathbf{P}
)
.

Under

, each

and

is bounded measurable and

(the latter by

). The function

C.4
FQI under distribution mismatch

Theorem 7

(FQI under distribution mismatch)
.

Let
\{Q_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}\subset\mathcal{Q}_{0}
satisfy
Q_{k+1}=T^{*}Q_{k}+\varepsilon_{k}

with
\|\varepsilon_{i}\|_{2,\mu}\leq\varepsilon
for
i=0,\ldots,K-1
, let
\pi_{K}

be a measurable greedy policy for
Q_{K}
, and assume the concentrability
coefficient
C
of (
4
) is finite. Then

\|V^{*}-V^{\pi_{K}}\|_{1,\rho}\;\leq\;\frac{4\sqrt{C}}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\,(1-\gamma^{K})\,\varepsilon\;+\;\frac{4\gamma^{K}\sqrt{C}}{1-\gamma}\,\|Q_{0}-Q^{*}\|_{2,\mu}.

(22)

In particular,
\limsup_{K\to\infty}\|V^{*}-V^{\pi_{K}}\|_{1,\rho}\leq 4\sqrt{C}\,\varepsilon/(1-\gamma)^{2}
. If
\|Q_{0}-Q^{*}\|_{\infty}<\infty
, the
initialisation term may be replaced by

4\gamma^{K}\|Q_{0}-Q^{*}\|_{\infty}/(1-\gamma)
without an additional
\sqrt{C}

factor.

This bound generalises the seminal performance-loss results of
Bertsekas and Tsitsiklis 1996
and the conservative policy iteration analysis of
Kakade and Langford 2002
. While the classical results typically rely on the supremum norm (effectively assuming
C=1
by requiring uniform approximation over the entire state space),
Theorem

7
relaxes this to a weighted
L^{2}(\mu)
setting, replacing the global error requirement with one on regions where the sampling distribution
\mu
has sufficient mass; the quadratic
1/(1-\gamma)^{2}
penalty is exposed as a fundamental consequence of distribution mismatch when using off-policy data without explicit density ratios or pessimism.

Proof of
Theorem

7
.

Under
1
,
Q_{K},Q^{*}\in\mathcal{Q}_{0}
admit measurable
greedy selectors
\pi_{K},\pi^{*}
via
Theorem

5
. Set

d_{K}\triangleq|Q_{K}-Q^{*}|
; all inequalities between elements of
\mathcal{Q}

are pointwise on
S\times A
. The argument has four steps: a
Q
-loss
inequality with resolvent expansion (Step 1); a
V
-loss decomposition
(Step 2); substitution of
Lemma

4
(Step 3); and a
Cauchy–Schwarz change of measure controlled by
(
4
) (Step 4).

Step 1 (
Q
-loss inequality).

Using
T^{\pi^{*}}Q^{*}=T^{*}Q^{*}=Q^{*}
,

T^{\pi_{K}}Q^{\pi_{K}}=Q^{\pi_{K}}
,
T^{*}Q_{K}=T^{\pi_{K}}Q_{K}
(greediness
of
\pi_{K}
for
Q_{K}
), and
T^{*}Q_{K}\geq T^{\pi^{*}}Q_{K}
, telescope and
rearrange:

(I-\gamma P^{\pi_{K}})(Q^{*}-Q^{\pi_{K}})\;\leq\;\gamma(P^{\pi^{*}}-P^{\pi_{K}})(Q^{*}-Q_{K})\;\leq\;\gamma(P^{\pi^{*}}+P^{\pi_{K}})\,d_{K}.

The resolvent
(I-\gamma P^{\pi_{K}})^{-1}=\sum_{h\geq 0}(\gamma P^{\pi_{K}})^{h}

is a positive linear operator with
L^{\infty}
-operator norm

\leq(1-\gamma)^{-1}
, giving the
Q
-analogue of

Munos 2007
:

0\;\leq\;Q^{*}-Q^{\pi_{K}}\;\leq\;\gamma\,(I-\gamma P^{\pi_{K}})^{-1}(P^{\pi^{*}}+P^{\pi_{K}})\,d_{K}.

(23)

Step 2 (
V
-loss decomposition).

For every
s\in S
, insert the pivot
Q^{*}(s,\pi_{K}(s))
into

V^{*}(s)-V^{\pi_{K}}(s)=Q^{*}(s,\pi^{*}(s))-Q^{\pi_{K}}(s,\pi_{K}(s))
and
expand the first bracket via
Q_{K}
:

\displaystyle V^{*}(s)-V^{\pi_{K}}(s)

\displaystyle=[Q^{*}-Q_{K}](s,\pi^{*}(s))+\underbrace{[Q_{K}(s,\pi^{*}(s))-Q_{K}(s,\pi_{K}(s))]}_{\leq\,0}

\displaystyle\quad+[Q_{K}-Q^{*}](s,\pi_{K}(s))+[Q^{*}-Q^{\pi_{K}}](s,\pi_{K}(s)),

with the underbraced term nonpositive by greediness of
\pi_{K}
for
Q_{K}
.
Writing
\eta^{*}\triangleq\rho\otimes\pi^{*}
,

\eta_{K}\triangleq\rho\otimes\pi_{K}
and integrating against
\rho
,

\|V^{*}-V^{\pi_{K}}\|_{1,\rho}\;\leq\;\eta^{*}d_{K}+\eta_{K}d_{K}+\eta_{K}(Q^{*}-Q^{\pi_{K}}).

(24)

Bounding the third term by (
23
) and expanding the
resolvent,

\|V^{*}-V^{\pi_{K}}\|_{1,\rho}\;\leq\;\eta^{*}d_{K}+\eta_{K}d_{K}+\gamma\sum_{h\geq 0}\gamma^{h}\,\eta_{K}(P^{\pi_{K}})^{h}(P^{\pi^{*}}+P^{\pi_{K}})\,d_{K}.

(25)

The total mass of the positive linear operators acting on
d_{K}
is

2+2\gamma\sum_{h\geq 0}\gamma^{h}=2/(1-\gamma)
: the two direct

\eta
-terms contribute
2
(with no
\gamma
), and the resolvent
contributes
2\gamma/(1-\gamma)
.

Step 3 (Substituting
Lemma

4
).

Applying
Lemma

4
to the
\varepsilon_{k}
-perturbed FQI
recursion yields

d_{K}\;\leq\;\gamma^{K}A_{K}\,|Q_{0}-Q^{*}|\;+\;\sum_{i=0}^{K-1}\gamma^{K-1-i}\,A_{K,i}\,|\varepsilon_{i}|,

(26)

with
A_{K}
and
A_{K,i}
each a sum of two products of Markov kernels, so

A_{K}\mathbf{1}\leq 2
and
A_{K,i}\mathbf{1}\leq 2
pointwise.
Substituting (
26
) into
(
25
), every contributing term to

\|V^{*}-V^{\pi_{K}}\|_{1,\rho}
is a nonnegative linear combination of
expressions
\rho B_{t}|f|
, where
B_{t}
is a product of
t
Markov
kernels drawn from
\{P^{\pi^{*}},P^{\pi_{K}}\}\cup\{P^{\pi^{g}_{\ell}}\}_{\ell}

and
f\in\{Q_{0}-Q^{*},\varepsilon_{0},\ldots,\varepsilon_{K-1}\}
.

Step 4 (Change of measure via uniform marginal
C
).

Each product
\rho B_{t}
equals the step-
t
state–action marginal

P^{\bar{\pi}}_{t}
of the non-stationary Markov policy
\bar{\pi}\in\bar{\Pi}

whose stage-
\ell
kernel is the
(\ell+1)
th factor of
B_{t}
, initialised
at
s_{0}\sim\rho
. By (
4
),

\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(\rho B_{t})/\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu\leq C
pointwise. Combining the pointwise bound
with the fact that
\rho B_{t}
is a probability measure on

\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}
gives the second-moment estimate

\int\biggl(\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(\rho B_{t})}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu}\biggr)^{\!2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu\;\leq\;C\int\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(\rho B_{t})}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu\;=\;C\,(\rho B_{t})(\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A})\;=\;C.

Cauchy–Schwarz then yields

\rho B_{t}|f|\;=\;\int|f|\,\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(\rho B_{t})}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu\;\leq\;\sqrt{C}\,\|f\|_{2,\mu}.

(27)

This is the
p=2
instance of the all-policy concentrability argument of

Munos and Szepesvári 2008
; replacing the discounted-occupancy
convex combination of
Munos and Szepesvári 2008
with the per-marginal bound of
(
4
) sidesteps the need to identify the aggregate
of all
\rho B_{t}
as a single discounted occupancy.

Assembly.

Applying (
27
) termwise after substituting
(
26
) into (
25
), and using
that the V-loss carries total operator mass
2/(1-\gamma)
while

A_{K},A_{K,i}
each contribute mass
2
,

\displaystyle\|V^{*}-V^{\pi_{K}}\|_{1,\rho}

\displaystyle\;\leq\;\tfrac{2}{1-\gamma}\Bigl[2\gamma^{K}\sqrt{C}\,\|Q_{0}-Q^{*}\|_{2,\mu}+2\sqrt{C}\sum_{i=0}^{K-1}\gamma^{K-1-i}\,\|\varepsilon_{i}\|_{2,\mu}\Bigr]

\displaystyle\;\leq\;\frac{4\gamma^{K}\sqrt{C}}{1-\gamma}\,\|Q_{0}-Q^{*}\|_{2,\mu}\;+\;\frac{4\sqrt{C}}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\,(1-\gamma^{K})\,\varepsilon,

using
\sum_{i=0}^{K-1}\gamma^{K-1-i}=(1-\gamma^{K})/(1-\gamma)
and

\|\varepsilon_{i}\|_{2,\mu}\leq\varepsilon
. Taking
\limsup_{K\to\infty}

eliminates the initialisation term and leaves

4\sqrt{C}\,\varepsilon/(1-\gamma)^{2}
. If instead

\|Q_{0}-Q^{*}\|_{\infty}<\infty
, the initialisation term may be bounded
directly via the
L^{\infty}
-non-expansion of Markov kernel products and

A_{K}\mathbf{1}\leq 2
:

\tfrac{2}{1-\gamma}\cdot 2\gamma^{K}\,\|Q_{0}-Q^{*}\|_{\infty}=4\gamma^{K}\,\|Q_{0}-Q^{*}\|_{\infty}/(1-\gamma)
, without invoking
C
.
∎

C.5
Total error decomposition

We work with the classical fresh-batch FQI protocol of
Algorithm

1
: at every iteration the agent draws an independent i.i.d. batch from a fixed distribution
\mu
, and the empirical iterate is the empirical risk minimiser of the squared Bellman loss.

Assumption 6

(Online FQI protocol and
L^{2}
-convexity)
.

At every iteration
k\in\{0,\ldots,K-1\}
, FQI receives a fresh dataset

{D_{n}^{(k)}=\{(s_{i}^{(k)},a_{i}^{(k)},r_{i}^{(k)},s_{i}^{\prime(k)})\}_{i=1}^{n}}
of

n
iid transitions with
{(s_{i}^{(k)},a_{i}^{(k)})\sim\mu}
and

{s_{i}^{\prime(k)}\sim P(\cdot\mid s_{i}^{(k)},a_{i}^{(k)})}
, independent of

{D_{n}^{(0)},\ldots,D_{n}^{(k-1)}}
. Given
\widehat{Q}_{k}\in\mathcal{F}
, the

samplewise Bellman labels
are

Y_{k,i}\triangleq r_{i}^{(k)}+\gamma\sup_{a^{\prime}\in A}\widehat{Q}_{k}\bigl(s_{i}^{\prime(k)},a^{\prime}\bigr),\qquad i=1,\ldots,n,

(28)

and the empirical FQI iterate is

\widehat{Q}_{k+1}\triangleq\widehat{\Pi}_{\mathcal{F},D_{n}^{(k)}}\bigl(Y^{(k)}\bigr)\;\in\;\arg\min_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\,\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bigl(f(s_{i}^{(k)},a_{i}^{(k)})-Y_{k,i}\bigr)^{2},

(29)

where
Y^{(k)}=(Y_{k,1},\ldots,Y_{k,n})
and
\widehat{\Pi}_{\mathcal{F},D_{n}^{(k)}}(y)

denotes a fixed measurable choice of empirical risk minimiser for any label
vector
y\in\mathds{R}^{n}
. When the argument is a function
g:S\times A\to\mathds{R}
,
we write
\widehat{\Pi}_{\mathcal{F},D_{n}^{(k)}}(g)
as shorthand for

\widehat{\Pi}_{\mathcal{F},D_{n}^{(k)}}\bigl((g(s_{i}^{(k)},a_{i}^{(k)}))_{i=1}^{n}\bigr)
.
In addition,
\mathcal{F}
is closed and convex in
L^{2}(\mu)
.

Theorem 8

(Total error decomposition)
.

Under
1
and
6
, fix an iteration

{k\in\{0,\ldots,K-1\}}
and assume
\widehat{Q}_{k}\in\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{Q}_{0}
.
Let
h_{k}\triangleq T^{*}\widehat{Q}_{k}
,
h_{k,i}\triangleq h_{k}\bigl(s_{i}^{(k)},a_{i}^{(k)}\bigr)
,
and let
\Pi_{\mathcal{F},\mu}h_{k}
denote a fixed measurable choice of population

L^{2}(\mu)
-projection of
h_{k}
onto
\mathcal{F}
. With
\widehat{\Pi}_{\mathcal{F},D_{n}^{(k)}}

and the labels
Y_{k,i}
as in
6
, the Bellman residual of

\widehat{Q}_{k+1}
decomposes as

\displaystyle\widehat{Q}_{k+1}-T^{*}\widehat{Q}_{k}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\widehat{\Pi}_{\mathcal{F},D_{n}^{(k)}}\bigl(Y^{(k)}\bigr)-\widehat{\Pi}_{\mathcal{F},D_{n}^{(k)}}(h_{k})}_{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{est},P,k}\ \text{(transition-target noise)}}+\underbrace{\widehat{\Pi}_{\mathcal{F},D_{n}^{(k)}}(h_{k})-\Pi_{\mathcal{F},\mu}h_{k}}_{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{est},\mu,k}\ \text{(sampling/design error)}}

\displaystyle\quad+\underbrace{\Pi_{\mathcal{F},\mu}h_{k}-h_{k}}_{\mathclap{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{approx},k}\ \text{(inherent Bellman approximation error)}}}.

The first two terms represent
estimation errors
(statistical) that vanish as
n\to\infty
, while the third is the
approximation error
(structural) inherent to the expressivity of
\mathcal{F}
. While this decomposition assumes the empirical minimiser
\widehat{Q}_{k+1}
is reached, the use of actor-critic algorithms like SAC
[
Haarnoja et al. 2018
]
or TD3
[
Fujimoto et al. 2018
]
introduces an additional optimisation error due to the non-convexity of neural architectures; this adds a supplementary additive term, but the underlying measure-theoretic and structural stability results remain intact.

Proof of
Theorem

8
.

Well-definedness.

Since
\widehat{Q}_{k}\in\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{Q}_{0}
,

Lemma

1
(iii) gives
h_{k}=T^{*}\widehat{Q}_{k}\in\mathcal{Q}
,
so
h_{k}
is a bounded measurable function on
S\times A
and the population
projection
\Pi_{\mathcal{F},\mu}h_{k}
is well-defined. Likewise,

Lemma

1
(i) ensures

s^{\prime}\mapsto\sup_{a^{\prime}\in A}\widehat{Q}_{k}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})
is measurable, so the labels

Y_{k,i}
of (
28
) are well-defined random variables; the
empirical risk minimiser
\widehat{\Pi}_{\mathcal{F},D_{n}^{(k)}}\bigl(Y^{(k)}\bigr)

of (
29
) exists by closedness and convexity of
\mathcal{F}
in

L^{2}(\mu)
(
6
), and the same holds for

\widehat{\Pi}_{\mathcal{F},D_{n}^{(k)}}(h_{k})
via the function-shorthand of

6
.

Telescoping identity.

By (
29
),

\widehat{Q}_{k+1}=\widehat{\Pi}_{\mathcal{F},D_{n}^{(k)}}\bigl(Y^{(k)}\bigr)
.
Subtracting
h_{k}
on both sides and inserting the intermediate quantities

\widehat{\Pi}_{\mathcal{F},D_{n}^{(k)}}(h_{k})
and
\Pi_{\mathcal{F},\mu}h_{k}
gives

\displaystyle\widehat{Q}_{k+1}-T^{*}\widehat{Q}_{k}

\displaystyle=\widehat{\Pi}_{\mathcal{F},D_{n}^{(k)}}\bigl(Y^{(k)}\bigr)-h_{k}

\displaystyle=\bigl[\widehat{\Pi}_{\mathcal{F},D_{n}^{(k)}}\bigl(Y^{(k)}\bigr)-\widehat{\Pi}_{\mathcal{F},D_{n}^{(k)}}(h_{k})\bigr]

\displaystyle\quad+\bigl[\widehat{\Pi}_{\mathcal{F},D_{n}^{(k)}}(h_{k})-\Pi_{\mathcal{F},\mu}h_{k}\bigr]

\displaystyle\quad+\bigl[\Pi_{\mathcal{F},\mu}h_{k}-h_{k}\bigr]

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{est},P,k}+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{est},\mu,k}+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{approx},k}.

The identity is purely algebraic: it adds and subtracts the well-defined
elements
\widehat{\Pi}_{\mathcal{F},D_{n}^{(k)}}(h_{k})
and
\Pi_{\mathcal{F},\mu}h_{k}

of
\mathcal{Q}
and uses no linearity of either projection. Its content lies
in the distinct asymptotic role of the three terms it isolates:

\varepsilon_{\mathrm{est},P,k}=\widehat{\Pi}_{\mathcal{F},D_{n}^{(k)}}\bigl(Y^{(k)}\bigr)-\widehat{\Pi}_{\mathcal{F},D_{n}^{(k)}}(h_{k})

projects a noise vector with conditional mean zero (cf. the labelling
identity (
35
)) and vanishes as
n\to\infty
;

\varepsilon_{\mathrm{est},\mu,k}
is the uniform-convergence error between
empirical and population
L^{2}
-projections of the same deterministic
target
h_{k}
, and likewise vanishes as
n\to\infty
; and

\varepsilon_{\mathrm{approx},k}
is the structural
L^{2}(\mu)
-distance from

h_{k}
to
\mathcal{F}
, independent of
n
.
Theorem

9
controls the
first two terms simultaneously via uniform convergence and absorbs the
third into
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{approx}}
.
∎

C.6
Finite-time unified FQI bound

We now convert the deterministic residual control of
Theorem

7
into a finite-sample FQI guarantee. The theorem is stated in terms of a generic high-probability estimation bound for the supervised Bellman regression step, isolating the RL-specific part of the analysis: once the regression error is controlled under
\mu
, residual propagation and concentrability yield a policy-performance bound under the evaluation distribution
\rho
.

Theorem 9

(Finite-time unified FQI bound)
.

Under
1
and
6
, let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{Q}_{0}

satisfy
\|f\|_{\infty}\leq B
for every
f\in\mathcal{F}
, with

{B\geq R_{\max}/(1-\gamma)}
so that
\|Q^{*}\|_{\infty}\leq B
, and let

{\widehat{Q}_{0}\in\mathcal{F}}
be the FQI initialisation. Assume the all-policy
concentrability coefficient
C
of (
4
) is finite, and
write
{h_{k}\triangleq T^{*}\widehat{Q}_{k}}
for
k\in\{0,\ldots,K-1\}
.
Suppose the regression procedure satisfies the following conditional
high-probability estimation guarantee: there exists a nonnegative function

\varepsilon_{\mathrm{stat}}(n,\delta^{\prime})
such that, for every
\delta^{\prime}\in(0,1)

and every
k\in\{0,\ldots,K-1\}
, conditionally on the past

{\mathcal{G}_{k}\triangleq\sigma(D_{n}^{(0)},\ldots,D_{n}^{(k-1)})}
, with probability
at least
1-\delta^{\prime}
,

\|\widehat{Q}_{k+1}-h_{k}\|_{2,\mu}\;\leq\;\inf_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\|f-h_{k}\|_{2,\mu}+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{stat}}(n,\delta^{\prime}).

(30)

Define the worst-case inherent Bellman error

\varepsilon_{\mathrm{approx}}\triangleq\sup_{0\leq k<K}\,\inf_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\|f-T^{*}\widehat{Q}_{k}\|_{2,\mu}.

(31)

Then, for every
\delta\in(0,1)
, with probability at least
1-\delta

over the
K
independent batches, the greedy policy
\widehat{\pi}_{K}
extracted
from
\widehat{Q}_{K}
satisfies

\|V^{*}-V^{\widehat{\pi}_{K}}\|_{1,\rho}\;\leq\;\underbrace{\frac{4\sqrt{C}}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\,(1-\gamma^{K})\,\bigl(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{approx}}+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{stat}}(n,\delta/K)\bigr)}_{\text{steady-state error floor}}\;+\;\underbrace{\frac{8B\gamma^{K}}{1-\gamma}}_{\text{initialisation bias}}.

(32)

Proof of
Theorem

9
.

The argument has three steps: define a high-probability event for each
round (Step 1); union-bound to control all
K
Bellman residuals
simultaneously (Step 2); and apply
Theorem

7
with the
sup-norm initialisation variant to convert residual control into a
policy-performance bound (Step 3).

Under
1
,
Theorem

5
supplies the measurable
greedy selector
\widehat{\pi}_{K}
of
\widehat{Q}_{K}
implicit in

V^{\widehat{\pi}_{K}}
; measurability of

s^{\prime}\mapsto\sup_{a^{\prime}\in A}\widehat{Q}_{k}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})
for each
k
is
guaranteed by
Lemma

1
(i), so the labels

Y_{k,i}
of (
28
) are well-defined random variables.

Step 1 (Per-round event).

Fix
k\in\{0,\ldots,K-1\}
and let

\mathcal{E}_{k}\triangleq\Bigl\{\|\widehat{Q}_{k+1}-h_{k}\|_{2,\mu}\leq\inf_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\|f-h_{k}\|_{2,\mu}+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{stat}}(n,\delta/K)\Bigr\}.

The conditional estimation guarantee (
30
) applied with

\delta^{\prime}=\delta/K
yields

{\Pr(\mathcal{E}_{k}^{c}\mid\mathcal{G}_{k})\leq\delta/K}
almost surely; the tower
property gives
{\Pr(\mathcal{E}_{k}^{c})\leq\delta/K}
.

Step 2 (Union bound).

By the union bound, the joint event

{\mathcal{E}\triangleq\bigcap_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathcal{E}_{k}}
satisfies

\Pr(\mathcal{E})\geq 1-\delta
. Set

\bar{\varepsilon}\triangleq\varepsilon_{\mathrm{approx}}+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{stat}}(n,\delta/K)

and
{\varepsilon_{k}\triangleq\widehat{Q}_{k+1}-h_{k}}
. On
\mathcal{E}
, for every

k\in\{0,\ldots,K-1\}
,

\|\varepsilon_{k}\|_{2,\mu}\;\leq\;\inf_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\|f-h_{k}\|_{2,\mu}+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{stat}}(n,\delta/K)\;\leq\;\bar{\varepsilon},

(33)

where the second inequality uses

\inf_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\|f-h_{k}\|_{2,\mu}\leq\varepsilon_{\mathrm{approx}}

from the definition (
31
).

Step 3 (Performance transfer via
Theorem

7
).

The iterates
{\{\widehat{Q}_{k}\}_{k=0}^{K}\subset\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{Q}_{0}}

satisfy
\widehat{Q}_{k+1}=T^{*}\widehat{Q}_{k}+\varepsilon_{k}
by definition of

\varepsilon_{k}
, with
\|\varepsilon_{k}\|_{2,\mu}\leq\bar{\varepsilon}
on
\mathcal{E}
for

k=0,\ldots,K-1
. Applying
Theorem

7
with the
sup-norm initialisation variant gives

\|V^{*}-V^{\widehat{\pi}_{K}}\|_{1,\rho}\;\leq\;\frac{4\sqrt{C}\,(1-\gamma^{K})}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\,\bar{\varepsilon}+\frac{4\gamma^{K}}{1-\gamma}\,\|\widehat{Q}_{0}-Q^{*}\|_{\infty}.

Since
\widehat{Q}_{0}\in\mathcal{F}
has
\|\widehat{Q}_{0}\|_{\infty}\leq B
and

{\|Q^{*}\|_{\infty}\leq R_{\max}/(1-\gamma)\leq B}
, the triangle inequality
gives
{\|\widehat{Q}_{0}-Q^{*}\|_{\infty}\leq 2B}
, hence the initialisation
term is bounded by
8B\gamma^{K}/(1-\gamma)
. Substituting the definition
of
\bar{\varepsilon}
yields (
32
).
∎

Corollary 2

(Global Rademacher instantiation)
.

Under the assumptions of
Theorem

9
, suppose at each round

\widehat{Q}_{k+1}
is the empirical risk minimiser

\widehat{\Pi}_{\mathcal{F},D_{n}^{(k)}}\bigl(Y^{(k)}\bigr)
of (
29
).
Let
R_{n}(\mathcal{F})
denote the expected empirical Rademacher complexity of

\mathcal{F}
under
\mu
, and define

\varepsilon_{\mathrm{est}}^{\mathrm{slow}}(n,\delta^{\prime})\;\triangleq\;\biggl(16B\cdot R_{n}(\mathcal{F})+8B^{2}\sqrt{\tfrac{2\log(2/\delta^{\prime})}{n}}\biggr)^{\!1/2}.

(34)

Then, for every
\delta\in(0,1)
, with probability at least
1-\delta

over the
K
independent batches,

\|V^{*}-V^{\widehat{\pi}_{K}}\|_{1,\rho}\;\leq\;\frac{4\sqrt{C}}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\,(1-\gamma^{K})\,\bigl(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{approx}}+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{est}}^{\mathrm{slow}}(n,\delta/K)\bigr)\;+\;\frac{8B\gamma^{K}}{1-\gamma}.

The global Rademacher corollary is intentionally assumption-light. It uses uniform convergence for the squared loss and therefore yields the slow rate in the unsquared
L^{2}(\mu)
Bellman residual. For standard function classes (linear with
d
parameters, bounded neural networks, RKHS with rapid eigenvalue decay),
R_{n}(\mathcal{F})=O(1/\sqrt{n})
, yielding an overall rate of
O(n^{-1/4})
to the approximation error floor
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{approx}}
, with the concentrability coefficient
C
amplifying errors when
\mu
poorly covers states visited by the optimal policy. Sharper rates can be obtained by replacing the global Rademacher step with localised or Bernstein-type regression guarantees; the FQI residual-to-performance analysis remains unchanged.

Proof of
Corollary

2
.

We verify the conditional regression guarantee (
30
)
of
Theorem

9
with

\varepsilon_{\mathrm{stat}}=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{est}}^{\mathrm{slow}}
; the
claimed bound then follows from
Theorem

9
applied with this
choice and
\delta^{\prime}=\delta/K
.

Fix
k\in\{0,\ldots,K-1\}
and condition on

{\mathcal{G}_{k}=\sigma(D_{n}^{(0)},\ldots,D_{n}^{(k-1)})}
. Under

6
,
\widehat{Q}_{k}
is
\mathcal{G}_{k}
-measurable and

D_{n}^{(k)}
is independent of
\mathcal{G}_{k}
with

{(s_{i},a_{i})\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}\mu}
and

{s_{i}^{\prime}\sim P(\cdot\mid s_{i},a_{i})}
(the superscript
(k)
on samples
is suppressed for readability). Consequently

h_{k}=T^{*}\widehat{Q}_{k}
is
\mathcal{G}_{k}
-deterministic, and the labels

{Y_{i}\triangleq Y_{k,i}}
of (
28
) satisfy

\mathds{E}\left[Y_{i}\mid\mathcal{G}_{k},s_{i},a_{i}\right]=h_{k}(s_{i},a_{i}),\qquad|Y_{i}|\leq R_{\max}+\gamma B\leq B,

(35)

using
{B\geq R_{\max}/(1-\gamma)}
.

Define the conditional risks

L(f)\triangleq\mathds{E}_{(f(s,a)-Y)^{2}}\left[\mathcal{G}_{k}\right],\qquad L_{n}(f)\triangleq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(f(s_{i},a_{i})-Y_{i})^{2},\qquad f\in\mathcal{F}.

The conditional-mean identity (
35
) expands the
population risk as

{L(f)=\|f-h_{k}\|_{2,\mu}^{2}+\mathds{E}_{(Y-h_{k}(s,a))^{2}}\left[\mathcal{G}_{k}\right]}
, so
that for every
f,g\in\mathcal{F}

L(f)-L(g)=\|f-h_{k}\|_{2,\mu}^{2}-\|g-h_{k}\|_{2,\mu}^{2}.

(36)

Each term
(f(s_{i},a_{i})-Y_{i})^{2}
is bounded by
4B^{2}
(since

|f|,|Y_{i}|\leq B
) and is
4B
-Lipschitz in
f\in\mathcal{F}
on the range

[-B,B]
. By Rademacher symmetrisation, the Ledoux–Talagrand
contraction principle (with constant
4B
), and bounded-difference
(McDiarmid’s) concentration, for every
\delta^{\prime}\in(0,1)
, with

\mathcal{G}_{k}
-conditional probability at least
1-\delta^{\prime}
,

\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}|L(f)-L_{n}(f)|\;\leq\;8B\cdot R_{n}(\mathcal{F})+4B^{2}\sqrt{\frac{2\log(2/\delta^{\prime})}{n}}\;\triangleq\;\Delta,

(37)

where the
\log(2/\delta^{\prime})
absorbs the two-sided correction. Since

\widehat{Q}_{k+1}
is the empirical risk minimiser, for every

f\in\mathcal{F}
,

\displaystyle L(\widehat{Q}_{k+1})-L(f)

\displaystyle=\bigl[L(\widehat{Q}_{k+1})-L_{n}(\widehat{Q}_{k+1})\bigr]+\bigl[L_{n}(\widehat{Q}_{k+1})-L_{n}(f)\bigr]+\bigl[L_{n}(f)-L(f)\bigr]

\displaystyle\leq\Delta+0+\Delta=2\Delta,

using
L_{n}(\widehat{Q}_{k+1})\leq L_{n}(f)
for the middle term and
(
37
) twice for the outer terms. Combined with
(
36
),

\|\widehat{Q}_{k+1}-h_{k}\|_{2,\mu}^{2}\;\leq\;\|f-h_{k}\|_{2,\mu}^{2}+2\Delta,\qquad f\in\mathcal{F}.

Taking the infimum over
f\in\mathcal{F}
on the right and applying

\sqrt{u^{2}+v}\leq u+\sqrt{v}
for
u,v\geq 0
,

\|\widehat{Q}_{k+1}-h_{k}\|_{2,\mu}\;\leq\;\inf_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\|f-h_{k}\|_{2,\mu}+\sqrt{2\Delta}.

By (
34
),

{\sqrt{2\Delta}=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{est}}^{\mathrm{slow}}(n,\delta^{\prime})}
,
which establishes the conditional guarantee (
30
)
with
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{stat}}=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{est}}^{\mathrm{slow}}
.
∎

Remark 3

(Single-batch variant)
.

Under
6
the
K
batches are drawn independently; under
a single-batch protocol that reuses one dataset across iterations, the

\mathcal{G}_{k}
-conditioning argument used in the proof of

Corollary

2
is invalid because
\widehat{Q}_{k}

becomes a measurable functional of the same data against which the regression
at iteration
k+1
is run. The remedy is to replace
R_{n}(\mathcal{F})
in
(
34
) by the Bellman-target Rademacher complexity

\widetilde{R}_{n}(\mathcal{F})\triangleq\mathds{E}_{\sigma}\left[\sup_{f,g\in\mathcal{F}}\,\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}\bigl(f(s_{i},a_{i})-r_{i}-\gamma\sup_{a^{\prime}}g(s_{i}^{\prime},a^{\prime})\bigr)^{2}\right],

which is uniform over all targets
T^{*}g
with
g\in\mathcal{F}
and therefore
survives data-dependent target selection. This is the route taken by the
adaptive-data analysis of
Theorem

C.8
Sobolev eluder dimension and global sequential complexity

Proposition 2

(Sobolev eluder dimension and global sequential complexity)
.

Let
S=[0,1]^{d}
with
d\geq 2
,
A=\{0,1\}
, and define

\mathcal{F}^{\dagger}\;=\;\bigl\{\,f(s,a)=a\,g(s)\;:\;g\in W^{\alpha,2}([0,1]^{d}),\ \|g\|_{W^{\alpha,2}}\leq 1\,\bigr\},

where
\alpha>d/2
. Then there exist constants
c,c_{1},c_{2}>0
, depending only on
d
and
\alpha
, such that for all sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
,

\dim_{E}(\mathcal{F}^{\dagger},\varepsilon)\;\geq\;c\,\varepsilon^{-d/(\alpha-d/2)}.

In particular,
\dim_{E}(\mathcal{F}^{\dagger},\varepsilon)=\Omega(\varepsilon^{-d/\alpha})
. On the other hand, the global sequential Rademacher complexity satisfies

c_{1}\sqrt{n}\;\leq\;R^{\mathrm{seq}}_{n}(\mathcal{F}^{\dagger})\;\leq\;c_{2}\sqrt{n}.

Consequently, the global sequential-Rademacher term in
Theorem

4
yields the usual slow residual scaling, up to logarithmic factors, rather than a Sobolev-optimal fast rate.

Proof of
Proposition

2
.

We prove the eluder lower bound and the two-sided sequential Rademacher bound in turn.

Eluder dimension lower bound.

Let
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a fixed nonzero bump, supported in the cube
[-1/2,1/2]^{d}
and normalised so that
\varphi(0)=1
. For a scale
h\in(0,1)
, choose centres
s_{1},\ldots,s_{m}\in[0,1]^{d}
such that the cubes of side
h
around each
s_{j}
are pairwise disjoint and contained in
[0,1]^{d}
. A standard packing of
[0,1]^{d}
by
h
-cubes gives
m\asymp h^{-d}
.

For each
j
, define the bump

g_{j}(s)\;\triangleq\;A_{h}\,\varphi\!\left(\frac{s-s_{j}}{h}\right),

with amplitude
A_{h}>0
to be chosen. The Sobolev scaling identity for dilations gives

\|g_{j}\|_{W^{\alpha,2}}\;\leq\;C\,A_{h}\,h^{d/2-\alpha},

for a constant
C
depending only on
\varphi
and
\alpha
. Setting
A_{h}\triangleq C^{-1}\,h^{\alpha-d/2}
ensures
\|g_{j}\|_{W^{\alpha,2}}\leq 1
, so
g_{j}
lies in the Sobolev unit ball, and at the centre we have
g_{j}(s_{j})=A_{h}=C^{-1}\,h^{\alpha-d/2}
. Pick
h
so that
A_{h}\geq\varepsilon
, equivalently
h\leq(C\,\varepsilon)^{1/(\alpha-d/2)}
. Then

m\;\asymp\;h^{-d}\;\asymp\;\varepsilon^{-d/(\alpha-d/2)}.

Set
f_{j}(s,a)\triangleq a\,g_{j}(s)\in\mathcal{F}^{\dagger}
and
f_{0}\equiv 0\in\mathcal{F}^{\dagger}
. Order the centres
s_{1},\ldots,s_{m}
arbitrarily. Because the supports of
g_{1},\ldots,g_{m}
are pairwise disjoint, for each
j

f_{j}(s_{i},1)\;=\;f_{0}(s_{i},1)\;=\;0\qquad\text{for all }i<j,

while at the new point
(s_{j},1)

\bigl|f_{j}(s_{j},1)-f_{0}(s_{j},1)\bigr|\;=\;|g_{j}(s_{j})|\;=\;A_{h}\;\geq\;\varepsilon.

By the eluder
\varepsilon
-independence definition, each
(s_{j},1)
is
\varepsilon
-independent of its predecessors, witnessed by the pair
(f_{j},f_{0})\in\mathcal{F}^{\dagger}\times\mathcal{F}^{\dagger}
. Hence

\dim_{E}(\mathcal{F}^{\dagger},\varepsilon)\;\geq\;m\;\geq\;c\,\varepsilon^{-d/(\alpha-d/2)},

for a constant
c
depending only on
d
and
\alpha
. Since
\alpha-d/2<\alpha
, the same bound implies the weaker
\dim_{E}(\mathcal{F}^{\dagger},\varepsilon)=\Omega(\varepsilon^{-d/\alpha})
.

Sequential Rademacher upper bound.

Because
\alpha>d/2
, the Sobolev embedding
W^{\alpha,2}([0,1]^{d})\hookrightarrow C([0,1]^{d})
is continuous, so point evaluation
g\mapsto g(s)
is a bounded linear functional on the Hilbert space
W^{\alpha,2}([0,1]^{d})
. Let
K_{s}\in W^{\alpha,2}([0,1]^{d})
denote its Riesz representer, and set

\kappa\;\triangleq\;\sup_{s\in[0,1]^{d}}\|K_{s}\|_{W^{\alpha,2}}\;<\;\infty.

Fix any predictable tree
(s_{t},a_{t})_{t=1}^{n}
with
s_{t}\in[0,1]^{d}
and
a_{t}\in\{0,1\}
, and let
(\sigma_{t})_{t=1}^{n}
be Rademacher random variables. By Riesz representation,

\sup_{\|g\|_{W^{\alpha,2}}\leq 1}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\sigma_{t}\,a_{t}\,g(s_{t})\;=\;\biggl\|\sum_{t=1}^{n}\sigma_{t}\,a_{t}\,K_{s_{t}}\biggr\|_{W^{\alpha,2}}.

By Jensen’s inequality and orthogonality of the Rademacher signs,

\mathbb{E}_{\sigma}\biggl\|\sum_{t=1}^{n}\sigma_{t}\,a_{t}\,K_{s_{t}}\biggr\|_{W^{\alpha,2}}\;\leq\;\biggl(\mathbb{E}_{\sigma}\biggl\|\sum_{t=1}^{n}\sigma_{t}\,a_{t}\,K_{s_{t}}\biggr\|_{W^{\alpha,2}}^{2}\biggr)^{1/2}\;=\;\biggl(\sum_{t=1}^{n}a_{t}^{2}\,\|K_{s_{t}}\|_{W^{\alpha,2}}^{2}\biggr)^{1/2}\;\leq\;\kappa\,\sqrt{n}.

Taking the supremum over predictable trees yields
R^{\mathrm{seq}}_{n}(\mathcal{F}^{\dagger})\leq\kappa\sqrt{n}
, i.e. the upper bound with
c_{2}\triangleq\kappa
.

Sequential Rademacher lower bound.

Take a constant predictable tree with
s_{t}=s_{0}
and
a_{t}=1
for all
t
, where
s_{0}\in(0,1)^{d}
is fixed. The Sobolev unit ball contains a nonzero constant function: there exists
c_{0}>0
such that the constants
g_{+}\equiv c_{0}
and
g_{-}\equiv-c_{0}
both lie in
\{\|g\|_{W^{\alpha,2}}\leq 1\}
. Therefore
f_{\pm}(s,a)\triangleq a\,g_{\pm}(s)
both lie in
\mathcal{F}^{\dagger}
, and

\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}^{\dagger}}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\sigma_{t}\,f(s_{0},1)\;\geq\;c_{0}\,\biggl|\sum_{t=1}^{n}\sigma_{t}\biggr|.

Taking expectations and using Khintchine’s inequality
\mathbb{E}|\sum_{t=1}^{n}\sigma_{t}|\geq c^{\prime}\,\sqrt{n}
for an absolute constant
c^{\prime}>0
gives
R^{\mathrm{seq}}_{n}(\mathcal{F}^{\dagger})\geq c_{0}\,c^{\prime}\,\sqrt{n}
, i.e. the lower bound with
c_{1}\triangleq c_{0}\,c^{\prime}
.

Combining the upper and lower bounds proves
R^{\mathrm{seq}}_{n}(\mathcal{F}^{\dagger})=\Theta(\sqrt{n})
, and the proposition follows.
∎

Appendix D
Examples: What Global Sequential Complexity Captures

Proposition 3

(Global sequential complexities for common classes)
.

The following bounds hold.

1.

Linear classes.
Let
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{lin}}=\{\langle\theta,\phi(x)\rangle:\|\theta\|_{2}\leq W\}
and suppose
\|\phi(x)\|_{2}\leq R_{\phi}
for all
x
. Then

R^{\mathrm{seq}}_{n}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{lin}})\;\leq\;WR_{\phi}\,\sqrt{n}.

2.

RKHS balls.
Let
\mathcal{H}
be an RKHS with kernel
K
satisfying
\sup_{x}K(x,x)\leq\kappa^{2}
, and let
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{RKHS}}=\{f\in\mathcal{H}:\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq W\}
. Then

R^{\mathrm{seq}}_{n}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{RKHS}})\;\leq\;W\kappa\,\sqrt{n}.

If there exist
x_{0}
and
f_{0}\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{RKHS}}
with
f_{0}(x_{0})\neq 0
and
-f_{0}\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{RKHS}}
, then

R^{\mathrm{seq}}_{n}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{RKHS}})\;\geq\;c\,\sqrt{n}

for some constant
c>0
.

3.

Norm-controlled neural networks.
For depth-
L
networks with bounded inputs,
1
-Lipschitz activations satisfying
\sigma(0)=0
, and layer norm bounds
M_{1},\ldots,M_{L}
as above,

R^{\mathrm{seq}}_{n}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{NN}})\;\lesssim\;\biggl(\prod_{\ell=1}^{L}M_{\ell}\biggr)\,\sqrt{n\log(2d)}.

Under the present global-complexity theorem, all three function classes yield slow-rate residual scaling: linear classes give a clean norm-based
n^{-1/4}
rate, RKHS balls match it (eigenvalue decay does not improve the global bound used here), and norm-controlled neural networks contribute only width-independent factors when norm constraints do not grow with width.
Fast nonparametric rates would require a localized or offset refinement; see the discussion following
Theorem

4
and
Proposition

2
.

Proof of
Proposition

3
.

We treat the three classes in turn.

Step 1 (linear classes).

Fix an arbitrary predictable tree
x_{t}(\varepsilon_{1:t-1})
and write
\phi_{t}\triangleq\phi(x_{t}(\varepsilon_{1:t-1}))
. By Cauchy–Schwarz,

\sup_{\|\theta\|_{2}\leq W}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{t}\,\langle\theta,\phi_{t}\rangle\;=\;W\,\biggl\|\sum_{t=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{t}\,\phi_{t}\biggr\|_{2}.

By Jensen’s inequality and orthogonality of the Rademacher signs,

\mathds{E}_{\varepsilon}\biggl\|\sum_{t=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{t}\,\phi_{t}\biggr\|_{2}\;\leq\;\biggl(\mathds{E}_{\varepsilon}\biggl\|\sum_{t=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{t}\,\phi_{t}\biggr\|_{2}^{2}\biggr)^{1/2}\;=\;\biggl(\sum_{t=1}^{n}\|\phi_{t}\|_{2}^{2}\biggr)^{1/2}\;\leq\;R_{\phi}\,\sqrt{n}.

Taking the supremum over predictable trees yields
R^{\mathrm{seq}}_{n}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{lin}})\leq WR_{\phi}\sqrt{n}
.

Step 2 (RKHS upper bound).

Let
K_{x}\in\mathcal{H}
denote the representer of evaluation at
x
, so that
f(x)=\langle f,K_{x}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
for every
f\in\mathcal{H}
, and
\|K_{x}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}=K(x,x)\leq\kappa^{2}
. For any predictable tree
x_{t}(\varepsilon_{1:t-1})
,

\sup_{\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq W}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{t}\,f(x_{t})\;=\;W\,\biggl\|\sum_{t=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{t}\,K_{x_{t}}\biggr\|_{\mathcal{H}}.

Hence, by Jensen and orthogonality,

\mathds{E}_{\varepsilon}\sup_{\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq W}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{t}\,f(x_{t})\;\leq\;W\,\biggl(\mathds{E}_{\varepsilon}\biggl\|\sum_{t=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{t}\,K_{x_{t}}\biggr\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\biggr)^{1/2}\;=\;W\,\biggl(\sum_{t=1}^{n}\|K_{x_{t}}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\biggr)^{1/2}\;\leq\;W\,\kappa\,\sqrt{n}.

Step 3 (RKHS lower bound).

Take the constant tree
x_{t}\equiv x_{0}
. Since both
f_{0}
and
-f_{0}
belong to
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{RKHS}}
,

\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{RKHS}}}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{t}\,f(x_{0})\;\geq\;|f_{0}(x_{0})|\,\biggl|\sum_{t=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{t}\biggr|.

Taking expectations and using Khintchine’s inequality
\mathds{E}_{\varepsilon}|\sum_{t=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{t}|\geq c^{\prime}\sqrt{n}
for an absolute constant
c^{\prime}>0
gives
R^{\mathrm{seq}}_{n}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{RKHS}})\geq c\,\sqrt{n}
with
c\triangleq c^{\prime}\,|f_{0}(x_{0})|
.

Step 4 (norm-controlled neural networks).

The bound follows by applying the sequential contraction inequality
[
Rakhlin et al. 2015
]
layer by layer under the stated
1
-Lipschitz and norm assumptions, starting from the sequential Rademacher complexity of the bounded linear input class. The contraction at each layer multiplies the complexity by the corresponding layer norm
M_{\ell}
, and the input-class complexity contributes the factor
\sqrt{n\log(2d)}
from a maximal inequality over the
d
input coordinates. Combining the
L

R^{\mathrm{seq}}_{n}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{NN}})\;\lesssim\;\biggl(\prod_{\ell=1}^{L}M_{\ell}\biggr)\,\sqrt{n\log(2d)},

where the implicit constant absorbs the standard normalisations in the contraction inequality and the assumption
\sigma(0)=0
.
∎

Practical significance.

Beyond their theoretical role, these three instantiations articulate concrete design rules for FQI on continuous spaces. The linear bound
R^{\mathrm{seq}}_{n}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{lin}})\leq WR_{\phi}\sqrt{n}
scales sublinearly with the feature dimension but linearly with the parameter norm
W
, providing a finite-sample justification for the recent line of work that pretrains low-dimensional state representations and then fits a near-linear value head, including unsupervised representation learning for control
[
Srinivas et al. 2020
,
Stooke et al. 2021
,
Schwarzer et al. 2021
]
and the provably efficient feature-learning analyses of
Yang et al. 2020
. The RKHS bound positions classical kernel- and GP-based RL
[
Ormoneit and Sen 2002
,
Engel et al. 2005
,
Domingues et al. 2021
,
Vakili and Olkhovskaya 2023
]
as a direct corollary of our framework: the sequential Rademacher complexity is controlled by
\sup_{x}K(x,x)
, so smoother kernels and tighter eigenvalue control translate immediately into smaller residuals, even though the global rate does not exploit the spectrum (
Proposition

2
). Finally, the neural-network bound depends only on the product of layer norms
\prod_{\ell=1}^{L}M_{\ell}
, with no explicit dependence on width, recovering the size-independent capacity control of
Bartlett et al. 2017
,
Golowich et al. 2018
and providing the theoretical backbone for the spectral-normalization principle of
Miyato et al. 2018
, whose adaptation to deep
Q
-learning
[
Gogianu et al. 2021
,
Bjorck et al. 2021
]
stabilizes otherwise-divergent value targets precisely by constraining the layer-norm product that drives our bound. Read together, the three cases show that sequential Rademacher complexity is the right common denominator for comparing linear, kernelized, and deep FQI on a continuous Borel state space: the parametric, spectral, and Lipschitz quantities that practitioners already regularize are exactly the quantities that control the residual rate in our analysis.

via sequential Rademacher complexity and adaptive concentrability
5
, but does not provide guarantees on cumulative (online) regret. The gap is structural, because the analysis operates with a single reference distribution
\bar{\mu}_{k}
per round and does not control the mismatch between the learner’s trajectory distribution and comparator policies over time. We describe two routes to bridge this gap: (i) stronger uniform coverage assumptions over policy-induced occupancies, and (ii) algorithmic stability or exploration mechanisms that control distribution drift.

E.1
Why the current analysis does not imply online regret

Sequential Rademacher complexity controls estimation under the observed sampling process, but does not control distribution shift across time. Applied to classes such as

\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{F}}=\bigl\{(s,a,r,s^{\prime})\mapsto f(s,a)-r-\gamma\sup_{a^{\prime}\in A}g(s^{\prime},a^{\prime}):f,g\in\mathcal{F}\bigr\},

it is the correct statistical tool for adaptive Bellman regression (
Theorem

2
) and, with
5
, suffices for the final-policy bound
Theorem

4
. It does not by itself ensure that the state-action regions relevant for the
intermediate
greedy policies
\widehat{\pi}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{\pi}_{n-1}
are covered by the data.

A second structural obstruction is that FQI controls update residuals

e_{k}\;\triangleq\;\widehat{Q}_{k+1}-T^{*}\widehat{Q}_{k},

whereas a pathwise regret certificate for the running greedy policy
\widehat{\pi}_{k}
depends on the diagonal fixed-point residual
\widehat{Q}_{k}-T^{*}\widehat{Q}_{k}
(
Proposition

1
). The two are linked only through a stability term:

\|\widehat{Q}_{k}-T^{*}\widehat{Q}_{k}\|_{\infty}\;\leq\;\|\widehat{Q}_{k}-T^{*}\widehat{Q}_{k-1}\|_{\infty}+\gamma\,\|\widehat{Q}_{k}-\widehat{Q}_{k-1}\|_{\infty}.

In short, sequential Rademacher complexity controls estimation error under a fixed data distribution, but online regret requires controlling how the data distribution evolves with the learner’s policy. This additional layer is not addressed by the current analysis.

E.2
Upgrade path I: strong coverage assumptions

One way to obtain online-style guarantees is to strengthen the coverage assumption so that it holds uniformly over policies and time steps. Define the greedy policy class

\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}\;\triangleq\;\bigl\{\pi_{f}:f\in\mathcal{F},\ \pi_{f}(s)\in\argmax_{a\in A}f(s,a)\bigr\},

and let
\mu_{j}=\bar{\mu}_{j}
denote the predictable design measure at update
j
. For an evaluation round
k>j
and policy
\pi\in\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}
, let
\nu_{k,j}^{\pi}
denote the state-action measure under which
|e_{j}|
is integrated when the resolvent expansion of
Lemma

4
is used to bound
\mathds{E}_{\rho_{k}}[V^{*}-V^{\widehat{\pi}_{k}}]
, with the
(s_{j},a_{j})
marginal obtained by drawing
a_{j}\sim\pi(\cdot\mid s_{j})
.

A coverage condition sufficient for a cumulative expected performance theorem requires a finite
C_{\mathrm{ad}}\geq 1
such that, almost surely, for all
0\leq j<k\leq n
and all
\pi\in\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}
,

\nu_{k,j}^{\pi}\ll\mu_{j},\qquad\biggl\|\frac{d\nu_{k,j}^{\pi}}{d\mu_{j}}\biggr\|_{\infty}\;\leq\;C_{\mathrm{ad}}.

(41)

This is a sufficient but strong assumption, and is not required anywhere in our main results. It imposes uniform absolute continuity across all pairs
(k,j)
and all policies in
\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}
, doing the work that active exploration, optimism, or pessimism would otherwise need to do.

Under (
41
), applying Cauchy–Schwarz in
L^{2}(\mu_{j})
transfers the residual bound to the evaluation measure

\|e_{j}\|_{L^{1}(\nu_{k,j}^{\pi})}\;\leq\;\sqrt{C_{\mathrm{ad}}}\,\|e_{j}\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{j})}.

Combined with
Theorem

3
, which gives
\|e_{j}\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{j})}\leq b_{j}
with
b_{j}\triangleq\varepsilon_{\mathrm{app},j}+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{stat},j}+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{opt},j}
, and applying the ADP propagation bound (
Theorem

7
) to each
\widehat{\pi}_{k}
, geometric resummation over
k
yields the cumulative bound

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathds{E}_{\rho_{k}}\!\bigl[V^{*}-V^{\widehat{\pi}_{k}}\bigr]\;\lesssim\;\frac{1}{(1-\gamma)^{3}}\biggl[\|Q^{*}-\widehat{Q}_{0}\|_{\infty}+\sqrt{C_{\mathrm{ad}}}\,\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}b_{j}\biggr].

Under such uniform coverage, one can convert per-round guarantees into cumulative performance bounds. The strength of the assumption, however, limits its applicability. It delivers an
L^{2}(\mu_{j})\to L^{1}(\nu_{k,j}^{\pi})
transfer but not a sup-norm transfer. Hence, it yields occupancy-weighted cumulative regret rather than pathwise regret. Neither does it show that vanilla FQI
solves
exploration.

E.3
Upgrade path II: stability and exploration

An alternative route is to control the evolution of the data distribution through algorithmic design. Pathwise regret for greedy FQI requires either direct diagonal-residual control, a stability argument controlling
\sum_{k}\|\widehat{Q}_{k}-\widehat{Q}_{k-1}\|_{\infty}
, or an explicit exploration mechanism such as optimism or posterior sampling. Unlike the coverage-based route, this approach does not rely on strong uniform assumptions, but instead requires additional structure on the learning algorithm.

A related complication is replay-buffer reuse. Sequential Rademacher symmetrisation applies cleanly when
\widehat{Q}_{k}
is predictable relative to the regression sample, which is the structure underlying
3
and
Theorem

2
. Training
\widehat{Q}_{k}
on the same samples used at iteration
k+1
breaks this martingale-difference structure, and recovery requires sample splitting, algorithmic stability, or a multiplier-process analysis (cf.
Remark

3
). Developing such guarantees in our setting would require extending the analysis beyond uniform convergence to incorporate trajectory-level dependencies.

Overall, the absence of online regret guarantees in our results is not an artifact of loose analysis, but reflects a genuine gap between statistical estimation and sequential decision-making. Bridging this gap requires either stronger coverage assumptions or additional algorithmic control of distribution shift.
