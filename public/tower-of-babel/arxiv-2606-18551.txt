Scalar-Tracking SAV Schemes with Pullback Corrections for Gradient Flows
Shiheng Zhang, Jie Shen

Abstract
The scalar auxiliary variable (SAV) method is a widely used framework for constructing linear, unconditionally energy-stable time discretizations of gradient flows. In a first-order SAV step, eliminating the auxiliary variable shows that the state equation is a semi-implicit update augmented by a rank-one positive semidefinite correction generated by the previous nonlinear force. The multiple-SAV (MSAV) method produces the corresponding correction componentwise and therefore yields a positive semidefinite correction whose rank is bounded by the number of energy components. This algebraic viewpoint separates two mechanisms that are usually coupled in MSAV: the number of scalar variables used to track the nonlinear energy and the rank of the correction applied to the state equation. We introduce a pullback-corrected SAV (PB-SAV) family that keeps a single scalar auxiliary variable for the total nonlinear energy but replaces the SAV rank-one correction by the pullback correction induced by an admissible component decomposition. The correction remains positive semidefinite, has rank at most the number of components, and may be changed from one time step to the next without changing the scalar energy tracker. We prove modified-energy dissipation laws for fixed and step-dependent decompositions, derive a refinement identity in which the gain from refining a decomposition is an explicit weighted variance, and give a Sherman–Morrison–Woodbury implementation for the resulting low-rank perturbation of the standard semi-implicit solve. We also show, in finite dimensions, that the pullback correction is the Gauss–Newton matrix of a least-squares representation of the nonlinear energy. Numerical experiments for finite-dimensional gradient flows, Allen–Cahn dynamics, and nonlocal Cahn–Hilliard models illustrate both regimes in which PB-SAV mainly changes the first-order error constant and regimes in which it substantially improves trajectory accuracy.

Introduction

Gradient flows are a basic mathematical model for dissipative evolution. They appear in phase-field models for interfacial motion, nonlocal and local materials models, fluid and complex-fluid systems, image processing, and optimization. In all of these settings the solution is driven by the variational derivative of an energy, and the decrease of that energy is not merely an a posteriori diagnostic: it is a structural property of the continuous problem. A time discretization that loses this structure can generate artificial energy growth, distort metastable dynamics, or require a step-size restriction that is not intrinsic to the underlying gradient flow. This is why the construction of schemes with a discrete energy law has become a central issue in the numerical analysis of gradient-flow problems.

We consider gradient flows in the form

\partial_{t}\phi=\mathcal{G}\frac{\delta E}{\delta\phi},

(1)

where
E[\phi]
is the free energy and
\mathcal{G}
is a self-adjoint nonpositive operator. The continuous energy identity is

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}E[\phi(t)]=\left(\frac{\delta E}{\delta\phi},\mathcal{G}\frac{\delta E}{\delta\phi}\right)\leq 0.

For computation one commonly writes

E[\phi]=\frac{1}{2}(\phi,\mathcal{L}\phi)+E_{1}[\phi],

(2)

where the self-adjoint positive semidefinite operator
\mathcal{L}
is treated implicitly and the nonlinear part
E_{1}
is treated explicitly or semi-explicitly. The challenge is to combine three properties that often pull in different directions: unconditional energy stability, good trajectory accuracy, and a linear solve whose dominant part is independent of the time step number.

Many approaches have been developed to preserve energy dissipation at the discrete level; we refer to
[
5
,
17
]
for representative reviews. The scalar auxiliary variable (SAV) method
[
16
,
17
]
is especially attractive because it converts a broad class of nonlinear gradient flows into linearly implicit schemes that dissipate a modified energy. The method introduces

q(t):=\sqrt{E_{1}[\phi(t)]+C},

with
C
chosen so that
E_{1}[\phi]+C>0
, and evolves the pair
(\phi,q)
in a way that is equivalent to the original gradient flow when
q(0)=\sqrt{E_{1}[\phi(0)]+C}
. First-order and higher-order SAV schemes are linear, unconditionally stable with respect to a modified energy, and can often be implemented using constant-coefficient solvers. Convergence and error estimates for SAV schemes were developed in
[
18
]
. The multiple scalar auxiliary variable (MSAV) method
[
3
]
extends this idea by introducing one scalar variable for each term in a decomposition
E_{1}=\sum_{\alpha}E_{1,\alpha}
, which is useful when an energy contains components of different scales or physical origins.

The SAV framework has also led to a large literature of variants and extensions. These include auxiliary-variable variants
[
8
,
9
]
, exponential SAV and SAV-exponential-integrator schemes
[
14
,
12
]
, arbitrarily high-order constructions
[
7
]
, Lagrange multiplier and generalized SAV formulations
[
1
,
2
]
, and general linear energy-stable frameworks
[
19
]
. Another line of work, including relaxed and energy-optimized SAV methods
[
11
,
21
,
10
,
15
,
20
]
, addresses the consistency between the numerical auxiliary variable
q^{n}
and its defining value
\sqrt{E_{1}[\phi^{n}]+C}
. The present paper addresses a different question: how much of the componentwise correction produced by MSAV can be obtained while retaining only one scalar auxiliary variable?

The motivation for the present work is easiest to see after the auxiliary variables have been eliminated. In the first-order SAV scheme (
11
)–(
12
), the state variable
\phi^{n+1}
does not simply satisfy the usual semi-implicit update; it is supplemented by a positive semidefinite correction in the single direction of the total nonlinear force. More precisely, substitution of the scalar update gives a linear equation for
\phi^{n+1}
containing

\mathcal{B}_{1}^{n}\psi=\frac{1}{2(Q^{n})^{2}}\,(U^{n},\psi)\,U^{n},\qquad U^{n}:=\frac{\delta E_{1}}{\delta\phi}[\phi^{n}],\qquad Q^{n}:=\sqrt{E_{1}[\phi^{n}]+C}.

This correction is rank one: all information in the nonlinear force is compressed into the single vector
U^{n}
. Such compression is natural when the nonlinear energy behaves as one coherent term, but it can be too restrictive when
E_{1}
contains several terms with different scales, different spatial locations, or different physical meanings.

The MSAV approach keeps more of this componentwise information. If

E_{1}[\phi]+C=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r}\bigl(E_{1,\alpha}[\phi]+C_{\alpha}\bigr),\qquad E_{1,\alpha}[\phi]+C_{\alpha}>0,

then eliminating the
r
component auxiliary variables in the first-order MSAV scheme (
16
)–(
17
) produces the correction

\mathcal{B}_{r}^{n}\psi=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r}\frac{1}{2(Q_{\alpha}^{n})^{2}}\,(U_{\alpha}^{n},\psi)\,U_{\alpha}^{n},\qquad U_{\alpha}^{n}:=\frac{\delta E_{1,\alpha}}{\delta\phi}[\phi^{n}],\qquad Q_{\alpha}^{n}:=\sqrt{E_{1,\alpha}[\phi^{n}]+C_{\alpha}}.

The range of
\mathcal{B}_{r}^{n}
lies in the span of the component forces
U_{1}^{n},\ldots,U_{r}^{n}
, so the correction can represent several independent force directions instead of only their sum. This observation separates two effects that are coupled in MSAV: the use of several scalar energy trackers and the use of a higher-rank correction in the state equation. The central idea of this paper is that the second effect can be obtained without the first.

We therefore introduce a pullback-corrected SAV (PB-SAV) scheme. PB-SAV retains the single scalar variable
q
associated with the total nonlinear energy
E_{1}[\phi]+C
, so the modified energy and scalar bookkeeping remain those of standard SAV. The only change is in the eliminated state equation: the rank-one SAV correction
\mathcal{B}_{1}^{n}
is replaced by the component correction
\mathcal{B}_{r}^{n}
. Consequently, PB-SAV preserves the main practical advantages of SAV—one scalar auxiliary variable, a scalar modified-energy law, and a low-rank perturbation of the usual semi-implicit solve—while importing the componentwise metric information that is responsible for the improved behavior of MSAV.

The term
pullback
describes this metric viewpoint. Let

R(\phi):=\bigl(Q_{1}(\phi),\ldots,Q_{r}(\phi)\bigr),\qquad Q(\phi)=\|R(\phi)\|_{2}.

Then

\mathcal{B}_{r}^{n}=2\,DR(\phi^{n})^{T}DR(\phi^{n}),\qquad\mathcal{B}_{1}^{n}=2\,DQ(\phi^{n})^{T}DQ(\phi^{n}).

Thus standard SAV first collapses the component map
R
to the scalar norm
Q
and then forms a rank-one pullback correction. PB-SAV instead pulls back the Euclidean metric of the full component space before this collapse. The one-component decomposition recovers standard SAV, whereas finer decompositions add positive semidefinite directions, can be changed adaptively from step to step, and can improve trajectory accuracy without introducing componentwise auxiliary variables.

The main contributions are as follows. First, we construct the first-order PB-SAV family and prove a modified-energy dissipation law with the same scalar modified energy as SAV. Second, we show that the pullback correction always dominates the rank-one SAV correction in the positive semidefinite order; the gap is an explicit weighted variance of normalized component directional derivatives. This identity also gives a monotonicity result under refinement of a component partition. Third, we allow the decomposition to change from step to step and prove that the modified-energy law is unchanged, because the auxiliary variable tracks only the total nonlinear energy. Fourth, we derive a Sherman–Morrison–Woodbury solver form for the low-rank perturbation of the usual semi-implicit operator. Finally, we give a finite-dimensional optimization viewpoint in which the PB-SAV correction is the Gauss–Newton matrix of a least-squares representation of the nonlinear energy, and we use numerical examples to identify when the higher-rank correction is mainly a change in error constant and when it provides a substantial trajectory improvement.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
reviews the SAV and MSAV reformulations and fixes notation. Section
3
derives the pullback correction operators, introduces PB-SAV, proves the stability and refinement results, and describes the low-rank solver and step-dependent decompositions. Section
4
gives the finite-dimensional optimization interpretation and the energy-admissible relaxation of the auxiliary variable. Section
5
presents numerical experiments for finite-dimensional problems, Allen–Cahn dynamics, and nonlocal Cahn–Hilliard models. Section
6
concludes. We restrict the analysis in this paper to first-order time discretizations.

2
Gradient flows and SAV preliminaries

2.1
Gradient flow and energy splitting

We first recall the standard gradient flow notation. Let
\phi(t)
satisfy

\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}=\mathcal{G}\mu,\qquad\mu=\frac{\delta E}{\delta\phi},

(3)

where
(\cdot,\cdot)
denotes the standard
L^{2}
inner product on the spatial domain and
\mathcal{G}
is a self-adjoint, negative semidefinite linear operator. Then the energy dissipates according to

\frac{d}{dt}E[\phi(t)]=\left(\frac{\delta E}{\delta\phi},\mathcal{G}\frac{\delta E}{\delta\phi}\right)\leq 0.

(4)

We use the splitting

E[\phi]=\tfrac{1}{2}(\phi,\mathcal{L}\phi)+E_{1}[\phi],

(5)

where
\mathcal{L}
is self-adjoint and positive semidefinite. We write

U[\phi]:=\frac{\delta E_{1}}{\delta\phi}

(6)

for the nonlinear force, so that
\delta E/\delta\phi=\mathcal{L}\phi+U[\phi]
. Throughout,
E_{1}
is assumed to be bounded from below, and
C
is chosen so that
E_{1}[\phi]+C>0
on the admissible set. Let

Q(\phi):=\sqrt{E_{1}[\phi]+C}.

The shift
C
affects the auxiliary variable but not the variational derivative
\delta E_{1}/\delta\phi
.

2.2
SAV reformulation and first-order scheme

The SAV reformulation augments the state by one scalar auxiliary variable
q(t)
and writes the gradient flow as the coupled system

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{G}\left(\mathcal{L}\phi+\frac{q}{Q(\phi)}U[\phi]\right),

(7)

\displaystyle\frac{dq}{dt}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2Q(\phi)}\left(U[\phi],\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}\right).

(8)

The reformulation is equivalent to the original gradient flow when the auxiliary variable is initialized consistently. Indeed, if
q(0)=Q(\phi(0))
, then

q(t)^{2}-E_{1}[\phi(t)]-C

is constant in time. Hence
q(t)=Q(\phi(t))
for all
t
, and
(
7
) is equivalent to the original gradient flow
(
3
).

For the splitting (
5
), the natural SAV modified energy is

\widetilde{E}[\phi,q]=\tfrac{1}{2}(\phi,\mathcal{L}\phi)+q^{2}.

(9)

Taking the inner product of (
7
) with

\mathcal{L}\phi+(q/Q(\phi))U[\phi]
and using (
8
) gives

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}(\phi,\mathcal{L}\phi)+q^{2}\right)=\left(\mathcal{L}\phi+\frac{q}{Q(\phi)}U[\phi],\mathcal{G}\left(\mathcal{L}\phi+\frac{q}{Q(\phi)}U[\phi]\right)\right)\leq 0.

(10)

Let
Q^{n}:=Q(\phi^{n})
and
U^{n}:=U[\phi^{n}]
. The first-order SAV time discretization treats the linear term
\mathcal{L}\phi
implicitly and freezes the nonlinear force at the previous time level:

\displaystyle\frac{\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n}}{\Delta t}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{G}\left(\mathcal{L}\phi^{n+1}+\frac{q^{n+1}}{Q^{n}}U^{n}\right),

(11)

\displaystyle\frac{q^{n+1}-q^{n}}{\Delta t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2Q^{n}}\left(U^{n},\frac{\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n}}{\Delta t}\right).

(12)

Proposition 1
.

The scheme (
11
)–(
12
) satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Delta t}\left[\widetilde{E}[\phi^{n+1},q^{n+1}]-\widetilde{E}[\phi^{n},q^{n}]\right]+\frac{1}{\Delta t}\left[(q^{n+1}-q^{n})^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n},\mathcal{L}(\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n})\bigr)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad=\left(\mathcal{L}\phi^{n+1}+\frac{q^{n+1}}{Q^{n}}U^{n},\;\mathcal{G}\left(\mathcal{L}\phi^{n+1}+\frac{q^{n+1}}{Q^{n}}U^{n}\right)\right)\leq 0.

(13)

Proof.

Set

\delta\phi^{n+1}:=\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n}

and

\mu^{n+1}:=\mathcal{L}\phi^{n+1}+(q^{n+1}/Q^{n})U^{n}
.
Taking the inner product of (
11
) with
\mu^{n+1}
gives

\frac{1}{\Delta t}(\delta\phi^{n+1},\mu^{n+1})=(\mu^{n+1},\mathcal{G}\mu^{n+1}).

The left-hand side contains

(\mathcal{L}\phi^{n+1},\delta\phi^{n+1})=\tfrac{1}{2}(\phi^{n+1},\mathcal{L}\phi^{n+1})-\tfrac{1}{2}(\phi^{n},\mathcal{L}\phi^{n})+\tfrac{1}{2}(\delta\phi^{n+1},\mathcal{L}\delta\phi^{n+1}),

where self-adjointness of
\mathcal{L}
is used. From the scalar update
(
12
),

\frac{q^{n+1}}{Q^{n}}(U^{n},\delta\phi^{n+1})=2q^{n+1}(q^{n+1}-q^{n})=(q^{n+1})^{2}-(q^{n})^{2}+(q^{n+1}-q^{n})^{2}.

Substitution gives (
13
). The final inequality follows from the assumed nonpositivity of
\mathcal{G}
.

The only implicit state term is the linear term
\mathcal{L}\phi^{n+1}
. Hence (
11
)–(
12
) is linear in
(\phi^{n+1},q^{n+1})
. Eliminating
q^{n+1}
gives a linear system for
\phi^{n+1}
whose principal operator is the time-independent semi-implicit operator
I-\Delta t\,\mathcal{G}\mathcal{L}
.

2.3
MSAV with a component decomposition

Suppose that
E_{1}
admits a decomposition

E_{1}[\phi]+C=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r}\left(E_{1,\alpha}[\phi]+C_{\alpha}\right),\qquad E_{1,\alpha}[\phi]+C_{\alpha}>0.

(14)

A decomposition satisfying the positivity condition in (
14
) will be called
admissible
. The shifts
C_{\alpha}
make the square roots below well defined. They do not change the component forces
U_{\alpha}=\delta E_{1,\alpha}/\delta\phi
, but they do appear in the correction through the weights
(Q_{\alpha})^{2}
. Define

Q_{\alpha}(\phi):=\sqrt{E_{1,\alpha}[\phi]+C_{\alpha}},\qquad U_{\alpha}[\phi]:=\frac{\delta E_{1,\alpha}}{\delta\phi},\qquad\alpha=1,\dots,r.

(15)

MSAV assigns one auxiliary variable to each component and uses

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{G}\left(\mathcal{L}\phi+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r}\frac{q_{\alpha}}{Q_{\alpha}(\phi)}U_{\alpha}[\phi]\right),

(16)

\displaystyle\frac{dq_{\alpha}}{dt}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2Q_{\alpha}(\phi)}\left(U_{\alpha}[\phi],\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}\right),\qquad\alpha=1,\dots,r.

(17)

With consistent initial data
q_{\alpha}(0)=Q_{\alpha}(\phi(0))
for all
\alpha
, the MSAV system is equivalent to the original gradient flow.

The corresponding first-order MSAV scheme treats
\mathcal{L}\phi
implicitly and freezes each component force explicitly:

\displaystyle\frac{\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n}}{\Delta t}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{G}\left(\mathcal{L}\phi^{n+1}+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r}\frac{q_{\alpha}^{n+1}}{Q_{\alpha}^{n}}U_{\alpha}^{n}\right),

(18)

\displaystyle\frac{q_{\alpha}^{n+1}-q_{\alpha}^{n}}{\Delta t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2Q_{\alpha}^{n}}\left(U_{\alpha}^{n},\frac{\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n}}{\Delta t}\right),\qquad\alpha=1,\dots,r.

(19)

Let
\mathbf{q}=(q_{1},\dots,q_{r})
. The scheme satisfies the corresponding
modified-energy law with

\widetilde{E}[\phi,\mathbf{q}]=\tfrac{1}{2}(\phi,\mathcal{L}\phi)+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r}q_{\alpha}^{2}.

The proof is the same as the proof of Proposition
1
, applied component by component.

3
The pullback-corrected SAV scheme

The distinction between SAV and MSAV has two parts: MSAV uses more scalar auxiliary variables, and it also changes the correction that appears after those variables are eliminated. We now isolate the second effect, which is the basis for PB-SAV.

3.1
Pullback correction operators from SAV and MSAV

For SAV, substitute the scalar update (
12
) into the state equation (
11
). The eliminated state equation is

\frac{\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n}}{\Delta t}=\mathcal{G}\left(\mathcal{L}\phi^{n+1}+\frac{q^{n}}{Q^{n}}U^{n}+\mathcal{B}^{n}_{1}(\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n})\right),

(20)

where

\mathcal{B}^{n}_{1}\psi:=\frac{1}{2(Q^{n})^{2}}\bigl(U^{n},\psi\bigr)U^{n}.

(21)

Then

(\psi,\mathcal{B}^{n}_{1}\psi)=\frac{(U^{n},\psi)^{2}}{2(Q^{n})^{2}}\geq 0,\qquad\operatorname{range}(\mathcal{B}^{n}_{1})\subseteq\operatorname{span}\{U^{n}\}.

Suppose
E_{1}
admits the admissible component decomposition (
14
) with

Q_{\alpha}^{n}:=\sqrt{E_{1,\alpha}[\phi^{n}]+C_{\alpha}},\qquad U_{\alpha}^{n}:=\frac{\delta E_{1,\alpha}}{\delta\phi}[\phi^{n}].

For MSAV, the same elimination applied to the component updates (
19
) and the state equation (
18
) gives

\frac{\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n}}{\Delta t}=\mathcal{G}\left(\mathcal{L}\phi^{n+1}+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r}\frac{q_{\alpha}^{n}}{Q_{\alpha}^{n}}U_{\alpha}^{n}+\mathcal{B}^{n}_{r}(\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n})\right),

(22)

where

\mathcal{B}^{n}_{r}\psi:=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r}\frac{1}{2(Q_{\alpha}^{n})^{2}}\bigl(U_{\alpha}^{n},\psi\bigr)U_{\alpha}^{n},

(23)

and

(\psi,\mathcal{B}^{n}_{r}\psi)=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r}\frac{(U_{\alpha}^{n},\psi)^{2}}{2(Q_{\alpha}^{n})^{2}}\geq 0,\qquad\operatorname{range}(\mathcal{B}^{n}_{r})\subseteq\operatorname{span}\{U_{1}^{n},\dots,U_{r}^{n}\}.

Remark 2
(Pullback form of the correction).

The terminology comes from the component square-root map

R(\phi):=\bigl(Q_{1}(\phi),\dots,Q_{r}(\phi)\bigr)
,
for which the admissible decomposition gives

Q(\phi)^{2}=E_{1}[\phi]+C=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r}Q_{\alpha}(\phi)^{2}=\|R(\phi)\|_{2}^{2}
.
Since
DQ_{\alpha}(\phi^{n})\psi=(U_{\alpha}^{n},\psi)/(2Q_{\alpha}^{n})
where
D
denotes the functional derivative for brevity, the correction
(
23
) is

\mathcal{B}_{r}^{n}=2\,DR(\phi^{n})^{T}DR(\phi^{n}),

the Euclidean
metric on the component variables pulled back to the state variable. Similarly, the
rank-one correction (
21
) is

\mathcal{B}_{1}^{n}=2\,DQ(\phi^{n})^{T}DQ(\phi^{n})
. In finite dimensions,

\mathcal{B}_{r}^{n}
is the Gauss–Newton matrix associated with the least-squares
energy
\|R(\phi)\|_{2}^{2}
.

Lemma 3
.

Assume (
14
). Let

Q^{n}:=\sqrt{E_{1}[\phi^{n}]+C},\qquad U^{n}:=\frac{\delta E_{1}}{\delta\phi}[\phi^{n}]=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r}U_{\alpha}^{n}.

For any direction
\psi
, define

y_{\alpha}:=\frac{(U_{\alpha}^{n},\psi)}{(Q_{\alpha}^{n})^{2}},\qquad\bar{y}:=\frac{\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r}(Q_{\alpha}^{n})^{2}y_{\alpha}}{\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r}(Q_{\alpha}^{n})^{2}}.

Then

\left(\psi,(\mathcal{B}_{r}^{n}-\mathcal{B}_{1}^{n})\psi\right)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r}(Q_{\alpha}^{n})^{2}\left(y_{\alpha}-\bar{y}\right)^{2}\geq 0.

(24)

In particular,

\mathcal{B}_{r}^{n}\succeq\mathcal{B}_{1}^{n},

where
A\succeq B
means that
A-B
is positive semidefinite. Moreover, the
two corrections agree in the direction
\psi
if and only if

y_{\alpha}=\bar{y}
for every
\alpha
.

Proof.

By the definition of
\mathcal{B}_{r}^{n}
,

(\psi,\mathcal{B}_{r}^{n}\psi)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r}\frac{(U_{\alpha}^{n},\psi)^{2}}{(Q_{\alpha}^{n})^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r}(Q_{\alpha}^{n})^{2}y_{\alpha}^{2}.

Since
U^{n}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r}U_{\alpha}^{n}
and

(Q^{n})^{2}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r}(Q_{\alpha}^{n})^{2}
,

(\psi,\mathcal{B}_{1}^{n}\psi)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\left(\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r}(U_{\alpha}^{n},\psi)\right)^{2}}{\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r}(Q_{\alpha}^{n})^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r}(Q_{\alpha}^{n})^{2}\right)\bar{y}^{2}.

Therefore

\left(\psi,(\mathcal{B}_{r}^{n}-\mathcal{B}_{1}^{n})\psi\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left[\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r}(Q_{\alpha}^{n})^{2}y_{\alpha}^{2}-\left(\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r}(Q_{\alpha}^{n})^{2}\right)\bar{y}^{2}\right].

The expression in brackets is the weighted variance identity

\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r}(Q_{\alpha}^{n})^{2}y_{\alpha}^{2}-\left(\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r}(Q_{\alpha}^{n})^{2}\right)\bar{y}^{2}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r}(Q_{\alpha}^{n})^{2}(y_{\alpha}-\bar{y})^{2},

which proves (
24
). The nonnegativity gives

\mathcal{B}_{r}^{n}\succeq\mathcal{B}_{1}^{n}
. Equality in the direction
\psi

holds exactly when all terms in the weighted variance vanish, i.e., when

y_{\alpha}=\bar{y}
for every
\alpha
.

The same variance identity gives a useful monotonicity statement under refinement of the component partition.

Proposition 4
.

Let (
14
) be a decomposition into
r

components. Let
\mathcal{P}_{\rm c}
and
\mathcal{P}_{\rm f}
be two
partitions of
\{1,\dots,r\}
, with
\mathcal{P}_{\rm f}
refining

\mathcal{P}_{\rm c}
. For a partition
\mathcal{P}
, define

\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{P}}^{n}\psi:=\sum_{G\in\mathcal{P}}\frac{1}{2w_{G}}\bigl(U_{G}^{n},\psi\bigr)U_{G}^{n},\qquad U_{G}^{n}:=\sum_{\alpha\in G}U_{\alpha}^{n},\qquad w_{G}:=\sum_{\alpha\in G}(Q_{\alpha}^{n})^{2}.

Then

\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{P}_{\rm f}}^{n}\succeq\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{P}_{\rm c}}^{n}.

More precisely, for every direction
\psi
,

\bigl(\psi,(\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{P}_{\rm f}}^{n}-\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{P}_{\rm c}}^{n})\psi\bigr)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{G\in\mathcal{P}_{\rm c}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}H\in\mathcal{P}_{\rm f}\\
H\subseteq G\end{subarray}}w_{H}\,(y_{H}-\bar{y}_{G})^{2},

(25)

where

y_{H}:=\frac{(U_{H}^{n},\psi)}{w_{H}},\qquad\bar{y}_{G}:=\frac{(U_{G}^{n},\psi)}{w_{G}}.

Proof.

Fix
\psi
and a block
G\in\mathcal{P}_{\rm c}
. The refined blocks

H\in\mathcal{P}_{\rm f}
with
H\subseteq G
form a partition of
G
, so

U_{G}^{n}=\sum_{H\subseteq G}U_{H}^{n},\qquad w_{G}=\sum_{H\subseteq G}w_{H}.

Applying Lemma
3
to this block gives

\sum_{H\subseteq G}\frac{(U_{H}^{n},\psi)^{2}}{w_{H}}-\frac{(U_{G}^{n},\psi)^{2}}{w_{G}}=\sum_{H\subseteq G}w_{H}\,(y_{H}-\bar{y}_{G})^{2}.

Summing over
G\in\mathcal{P}_{\rm c}
and multiplying by
1/2
gives
(
25
). The right-hand side is nonnegative, hence

\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{P}_{\rm f}}^{n}\succeq\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{P}_{\rm c}}^{n}
.

3.2
The PB-SAV scheme and stability

We now define the PB-SAV update. The scalar variable remains the single SAV tracker for the total energy, but the rank-one correction in (
20
) is replaced by the pullback correction
\mathcal{B}_{r}^{n}
. The scheme is

\displaystyle\frac{\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n}}{\Delta t}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{G}\left(\mathcal{L}\phi^{n+1}+\frac{q^{n}}{Q^{n}}U^{n}+\mathcal{B}_{r}^{n}(\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n})\right),

(26)

\displaystyle\frac{q^{n+1}-q^{n}}{\Delta t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2Q^{n}}\left(U^{n},\frac{\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n}}{\Delta t}\right).

(27)

Theorem 5
.

Assume that the component decomposition
(
14
) is admissible. Then the scheme
(
26
)–(
27
) satisfies a modified-energy
dissipation law. More precisely, let

\widetilde{E}[\phi,q]=\tfrac{1}{2}(\phi,\mathcal{L}\phi)+q^{2},\qquad\mathcal{S}_{r}^{n}:=\mathcal{B}_{r}^{n}-\mathcal{B}_{1}^{n}.

Then
\mathcal{S}_{r}^{n}\succeq 0
, and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Delta t}\left[\widetilde{E}[\phi^{n+1},q^{n+1}]-\widetilde{E}[\phi^{n},q^{n}]\right]+\frac{(q^{n+1}-q^{n})^{2}}{\Delta t}+\frac{1}{2\Delta t}\bigl(\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n},\mathcal{L}(\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n})\bigr)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{\Delta t}\left(\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n},\mathcal{S}_{r}^{n}(\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n})\right)=\left(\Xi^{n+1},\mathcal{G}\Xi^{n+1}\right)\leq 0,

(28)

where

\Xi^{n+1}:=\mathcal{L}\phi^{n+1}+\frac{q^{n}}{Q^{n}}U^{n}+\mathcal{B}_{r}^{n}(\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n}).

In particular,
\widetilde{E}[\phi^{n+1},q^{n+1}]\leq\widetilde{E}[\phi^{n},q^{n}]
.

Proof.

Set
\delta\phi^{n+1}=\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n}
. Since
\mathcal{B}_{r}^{n}=\mathcal{B}_{1}^{n}+\mathcal{S}_{r}^{n}
and
(
27
) implies

\mathcal{B}_{1}^{n}\delta\phi^{n+1}=\frac{q^{n+1}-q^{n}}{Q^{n}}U^{n},

we may rewrite

\Xi^{n+1}=\mathcal{L}\phi^{n+1}+\frac{q^{n+1}}{Q^{n}}U^{n}+\mathcal{S}_{r}^{n}\delta\phi^{n+1}.

Taking the inner product of (
26
) with
\Xi^{n+1}
gives

\frac{1}{\Delta t}\left(\delta\phi^{n+1},\Xi^{n+1}\right)=\left(\Xi^{n+1},\mathcal{G}\Xi^{n+1}\right).

We now identify the terms on the left-hand side with the modified-energy difference and the nonnegative numerical dissipation terms. The quadratic part satisfies

\left(\mathcal{L}\phi^{n+1},\delta\phi^{n+1}\right)=\tfrac{1}{2}(\phi^{n+1},\mathcal{L}\phi^{n+1})-\tfrac{1}{2}(\phi^{n},\mathcal{L}\phi^{n})+\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\delta\phi^{n+1},\mathcal{L}\delta\phi^{n+1}\bigr),

with the last term nonnegative. The auxiliary-variable update gives

\frac{q^{n+1}}{Q^{n}}\left(U^{n},\delta\phi^{n+1}\right)=2q^{n+1}(q^{n+1}-q^{n})=(q^{n+1})^{2}-(q^{n})^{2}+(q^{n+1}-q^{n})^{2}.

The last term is

\left(\delta\phi^{n+1},\mathcal{S}_{r}^{n}\delta\phi^{n+1}\right).

Combining these identities gives (
28
). The
right-hand side is nonpositive because
\mathcal{G}
is nonpositive, and

\mathcal{S}_{r}^{n}\succeq 0
by Lemma
3
.

Remark 6
.

Along a smooth exact trajectory, the correction is multiplied by
\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n}=O(\Delta t)
. Therefore replacing
\mathcal{B}_{1}^{n}
by
\mathcal{B}_{r}^{n}
does not change the formal first-order consistency of the underlying SAV discretization.

3.3
Solver structure

We next record the solver structure for the PB-SAV step. The purpose is to show that the higher-rank correction can be incorporated as a low-rank update of the same semi-implicit operator used by SAV. Since
\mathcal{L}
is self-adjoint and positive semidefinite, rearranging (
26
) gives

\bigl(I-\Delta t\,\mathcal{G}\mathcal{L}-\Delta t\,\mathcal{G}\mathcal{B}_{r}^{n}\bigr)\phi^{n+1}=\phi^{n}+\Delta t\,\mathcal{G}\frac{q^{n}}{Q^{n}}U^{n}-\Delta t\,\mathcal{G}\mathcal{B}_{r}^{n}\phi^{n}.

(29)

The pullback correction
\mathcal{B}_{r}^{n}
defined by (
23
) factors as
\mathcal{B}_{r}^{n}=V_{n}V_{n}^{T}
for the
N\times r
matrix

V_{n}:=\Bigl[\tfrac{U_{1}^{n}}{\sqrt{2}\,Q_{1}^{n}},\ldots,\tfrac{U_{r}^{n}}{\sqrt{2}\,Q_{r}^{n}}\Bigr],

where
N
is the dimension of the spatial discretization. The system (
29
) is therefore a rank-
r

perturbation of the usual semi-implicit operator

K:=I-\Delta t\,\mathcal{G}\mathcal{L}.

In the standard self-adjoint discretizations used for gradient flows,
K
is invertible
(and often symmetric positive definite after the usual sign convention or mass-matrix
symmetrization). The formula below only requires the invertibility of
K
and of the
small
r\times r
matrix that appears in the low-rank update. Writing the right-hand side as

g_{n}:=\phi^{n}+\Delta t\,\mathcal{G}\frac{q^{n}}{Q^{n}}U^{n}-\Delta t\,\mathcal{G}V_{n}V_{n}^{T}\phi^{n},

the Sherman–Morrison–Woodbury formula
[
6
]
gives

\phi^{n+1}=K^{-1}g_{n}+K^{-1}(\Delta t\,\mathcal{G}V_{n})\bigl[I_{r}-V_{n}^{T}K^{-1}(\Delta t\,\mathcal{G}V_{n})\bigr]^{-1}V_{n}^{T}K^{-1}g_{n}.

(30)

In practice, each time step requires
r+1
applications of
K^{-1}
: one to
g_{n}
and one to each column of
\Delta t\,\mathcal{G}V_{n}
. Since
K=I-\Delta t\,\mathcal{G}\mathcal{L}
is independent of
n
, a factorization or fast solver can be reused throughout the computation. On periodic domains, for example, choices such as
\mathcal{L}=-\Delta
make
K^{-1}
diagonal in the Fourier basis.

3.4
Step-dependent component decompositions

A further advantage of separating scalar tracking from correction rank is that the component decomposition used in the correction need not be fixed. Since the scalar auxiliary variable tracks only the total quantity
Q(\phi)^{2}=E_{1}[\phi]+C
, the components may be recombined or repartitioned from one step to the next. At step
n
, let

E_{1}[\phi]+C=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r_{n}}\left(E_{1,\alpha}^{n}[\phi]+C_{\alpha}^{n}\right),\qquad E_{1,\alpha}^{n}[\phi]+C_{\alpha}^{n}>0,

(31)

and define

\mathcal{B}_{r_{n}}^{n}\psi:=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r_{n}}\frac{1}{2(Q_{\alpha}^{n})^{2}}\left(U_{\alpha}^{n},\psi\right)U_{\alpha}^{n},

(32)

with

Q_{\alpha}^{n}:=\sqrt{E_{1,\alpha}^{n}[\phi^{n}]+C_{\alpha}^{n}},\qquad U_{\alpha}^{n}:=\frac{\delta E_{1,\alpha}^{n}}{\delta\phi}[\phi^{n}].

Replacing
\mathcal{B}_{r}^{n}
in (
26
) by

\mathcal{B}_{r_{n}}^{n}
and keeping the scalar update
(
27
) gives

\displaystyle\frac{\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n}}{\Delta t}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{G}\left(\mathcal{L}\phi^{n+1}+\frac{q^{n}}{Q^{n}}U^{n}+\mathcal{B}_{r_{n}}^{n}(\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n})\right),

(33)

\displaystyle\frac{q^{n+1}-q^{n}}{\Delta t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2Q^{n}}\left(U^{n},\frac{\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n}}{\Delta t}\right).

(34)

Theorem 7
.

Assume that the decomposition
(
31
) is admissible for every step
n
. Let

\mathcal{S}_{r_{n}}^{n}:=\mathcal{B}_{r_{n}}^{n}-\mathcal{B}_{1}^{n}.

Then
\mathcal{S}_{r_{n}}^{n}\succeq 0
, and the
step-dependent scheme (
33
)–(
34
)
satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Delta t}\left[\widetilde{E}[\phi^{n+1},q^{n+1}]-\widetilde{E}[\phi^{n},q^{n}]\right]+\frac{(q^{n+1}-q^{n})^{2}}{\Delta t}+\frac{1}{2\Delta t}\bigl(\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n},\mathcal{L}(\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n})\bigr)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{\Delta t}\left(\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n},\mathcal{S}_{r_{n}}^{n}(\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n})\right)=\left(\Xi_{\mathrm{sd}}^{n+1},\mathcal{G}\Xi_{\mathrm{sd}}^{n+1}\right)\leq 0,

(35)

where

\Xi_{\mathrm{sd}}^{n+1}:=\mathcal{L}\phi^{n+1}+\frac{q^{n}}{Q^{n}}U^{n}+\mathcal{B}_{r_{n}}^{n}(\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n}).

In particular,
\widetilde{E}[\phi^{n+1},q^{n+1}]\leq\widetilde{E}[\phi^{n},q^{n}]
for every admissible step-dependent decomposition
sequence.

Proof.

Fix a time level
n
and set

\delta\phi^{n+1}=\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n}
. The step-
n
admissible decomposition implies

\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r_{n}}U_{\alpha}^{n}=U^{n},\qquad\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r_{n}}(Q_{\alpha}^{n})^{2}=(Q^{n})^{2}.

Therefore Lemma
3
, applied to this particular decomposition, gives

\mathcal{S}_{r_{n}}^{n}=\mathcal{B}_{r_{n}}^{n}-\mathcal{B}_{1}^{n}\succeq 0
. Moreover, the scalar update (
34
) gives

\mathcal{B}_{1}^{n}\delta\phi^{n+1}=\frac{q^{n+1}-q^{n}}{Q^{n}}U^{n}.

Hence

\Xi_{\mathrm{sd}}^{n+1}=\mathcal{L}\phi^{n+1}+\frac{q^{n+1}}{Q^{n}}U^{n}+\mathcal{S}_{r_{n}}^{n}\delta\phi^{n+1}.

Taking the inner product of (
33
) with

\Xi_{\mathrm{sd}}^{n+1}
and using the two identities

(\mathcal{L}\phi^{n+1},\delta\phi^{n+1})=\tfrac{1}{2}(\phi^{n+1},\mathcal{L}\phi^{n+1})-\tfrac{1}{2}(\phi^{n},\mathcal{L}\phi^{n})+\tfrac{1}{2}(\delta\phi^{n+1},\mathcal{L}\delta\phi^{n+1})

and

\frac{q^{n+1}}{Q^{n}}(U^{n},\delta\phi^{n+1})=(q^{n+1})^{2}-(q^{n})^{2}+(q^{n+1}-q^{n})^{2}

gives (
35
). The right-hand side is nonpositive because

\mathcal{G}
is nonpositive, and the additional term involving

\mathcal{S}_{r_{n}}^{n}
is nonnegative by the first part of the proof.

Remark 8
.

The step-dependent construction uses a feature specific to PB-SAV: the auxiliary
variable tracks only the total quantity
Q(\phi)^{2}=E_{1}[\phi]+C
. The component
decomposition at step
n
enters only through the pullback correction

\mathcal{B}_{r_{n}}^{n}
. Hence the stability proof requires only the stepwise
identities

\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r_{n}}U_{\alpha}^{n}=U^{n},\qquad\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r_{n}}(Q_{\alpha}^{n})^{2}=(Q^{n})^{2},

and does not require component auxiliary variables to be matched across time
steps. This differs from MSAV, where the modified energy contains the
componentwise variables
q_{\alpha}
.

4
Finite-dimensional optimization viewpoint

We now give a finite-dimensional interpretation of the correction. This viewpoint clarifies the relation between PB-SAV and Gauss–Newton metrics and motivates the energy-admissible
q^{2}
-relaxation used in the optimization tests.

Consider the unconstrained problem

\min_{\phi\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}f(\phi),\qquad f(\phi)=E_{1}(\phi).

In Sections
4.1
and
4.2
we take
\mathcal{L}=0
and
\mathcal{G}=-I_{m}
, and write
Q(\phi)=\sqrt{f(\phi)+C}
; the relaxation of Section
4.3
is stated for general
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{L}
.

4.1
SAV and PB-SAV as gradient steps

For SAV, substituting the scalar update into the state equation gives

\phi^{n+1}=\phi^{n}-\alpha_{n}P_{1}^{n}\nabla f(\phi^{n}),\qquad\alpha_{n}:=\Delta t\,\frac{q^{n}}{Q(\phi^{n})},\qquad P_{1}^{n}:=\bigl(I+\Delta t\,\mathcal{B}_{1}^{n}\bigr)^{-1},

(36)

where
\mathcal{B}_{1}^{n}
is rank one,

\mathcal{B}_{1}^{n}=\frac{\nabla f(\phi^{n})\nabla f(\phi^{n})^{T}}{2Q(\phi^{n})^{2}}.

(37)

Since
\mathcal{B}_{1}^{n}\succeq 0
, the matrix
I+\Delta t\,\mathcal{B}_{1}^{n}
is positive definite, so (
36
) is a well-defined gradient step in
\phi
; the inverse matrix
P_{1}^{n}
acts as the corresponding preconditioner in this finite-dimensional reading.

For decomposition
f(\phi)=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r}f_{\alpha}(\phi)
, define

Q_{\alpha}(\phi)^{2}=f_{\alpha}(\phi)+C_{\alpha},\qquad R(\phi):=(Q_{1}(\phi),\ldots,Q_{r}(\phi)).

Then
f(\phi)+C=\|R(\phi)\|_{2}^{2}
with
C=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r}C_{\alpha}
, and in finite dimensions

\mathcal{B}_{r}^{n}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r}\frac{\nabla f_{\alpha}(\phi^{n})\nabla f_{\alpha}(\phi^{n})^{T}}{2Q_{\alpha}(\phi^{n})^{2}}=2\,DR(\phi^{n})^{T}DR(\phi^{n}).

Thus PB-SAV uses the Gauss–Newton part of the least-squares energy

\|R(\phi)\|_{2}^{2}
, while standard SAV uses the corresponding scalar pullback
for
Q(\phi)=\|R(\phi)\|_{2}
.

4.2
Model problem: convex quadratic

The effect of the pullback correction is easiest to see for a diagonal convex quadratic,

f(\phi)=\frac{1}{2}\phi^{T}A\phi=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}\phi_{i}^{2},\qquad a_{i}>0.

(38)

With the coordinate decomposition
f_{i}(\phi)=\tfrac{1}{2}a_{i}\phi_{i}^{2}
and
C_{i}>0
, each component has
\nabla f_{i}(\phi)=a_{i}\phi_{i}\,e_{i}
(only its
i
-th coordinate is nonzero) and
Q_{i}(\phi)^{2}=f_{i}(\phi)+C_{i}=\tfrac{1}{2}a_{i}\phi_{i}^{2}+C_{i}
. The contribution from component
i
to
\mathcal{B}_{r}^{n}
is the rank-one matrix

\frac{\nabla f_{i}(\phi^{n})\nabla f_{i}(\phi^{n})^{T}}{2Q_{i}(\phi^{n})^{2}}=\frac{a_{i}^{2}(\phi_{i}^{n})^{2}}{a_{i}(\phi_{i}^{n})^{2}+2C_{i}}\,e_{i}e_{i}^{T},

so summing over
i=1,\dots,m
gives a diagonal correction:

\mathcal{B}_{m}^{n}=\operatorname{diag}(b_{1}^{n},\dots,b_{m}^{n}),\qquad b_{i}^{n}:=\frac{a_{i}^{2}(\phi_{i}^{n})^{2}}{a_{i}(\phi_{i}^{n})^{2}+2C_{i}}\in[0,a_{i}].

(39)

When
C_{i}
is small relative to
f_{i}(\phi^{n})
,
b_{i}^{n}\approx a_{i}
, so
\mathcal{B}_{m}^{n}
approximates the Hessian
A
. In that regime

P_{m}^{n}=\bigl(I+\Delta t\,\mathcal{B}_{m}^{n}\bigr)^{-1}\approx\bigl(I+\Delta t\,A\bigr)^{-1},

matching the implicit Euler step for the quadratic model. By contrast, SAV uses only

\mathcal{B}_{1}^{n}=\frac{(A\phi^{n})(A\phi^{n})^{T}}{(\phi^{n})^{T}A\phi^{n}+2C},

(40)

which is rank one. Grouped decompositions lie between these two cases:
\mathcal{B}_{r}^{n}
is built from grouped component gradients rather than from the total gradient alone.

4.3
Energy-admissible relaxation of the auxiliary variable

SAV-type schemes have also been used as optimization methods
[
13
,
20
]
. In that setting the time step is often chosen much larger than it would be in an accuracy-oriented time integration. The modified energy may still decrease, but the numerical auxiliary variable
q^{n}
can drift away from its defining value
Q(\phi^{n})
. If the ratio
q^{n}/Q(\phi^{n})
becomes very small, the scalar multiplier in the state update suppresses the explicit nonlinear force and the iteration may stagnate before reaching a minimizer. PB-SAV has the same scalar-tracking mechanism, so this auxiliary-variable consistency issue can arise even when the pullback correction supplies a useful local metric.

We therefore use an energy-admissible relaxation of the auxiliary variable. Let the unrelaxed update (
26
)–(
27
) produce
(\phi^{n+1},\widetilde{q}^{n+1})
:

\displaystyle\frac{\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n}}{\Delta t}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{G}\left(\mathcal{L}\phi^{n+1}+\frac{q^{n}}{Q^{n}}U^{n}+\mathcal{B}_{r}^{n}(\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n})\right),

(41)

\displaystyle\frac{\widetilde{q}^{n+1}-q^{n}}{\Delta t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2Q^{n}}\left(U^{n},\frac{\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n}}{\Delta t}\right).

(42)

The unrelaxed update satisfies
\widetilde{E}[\phi^{n+1},\widetilde{q}^{n+1}]\leq\widetilde{E}[\phi^{n},q^{n}]
, and we define the dissipation margin

D_{n}:=\widetilde{E}[\phi^{n},q^{n}]-\widetilde{E}[\phi^{n+1},\widetilde{q}^{n+1}]\geq 0.

Given an admissible relaxation amount
\delta_{n}\in[0,D_{n}]
, the relaxation sets

q^{n+1}:=\sqrt{\min\bigl(Q(\phi^{n+1})^{2},\,(\widetilde{q}^{n+1})^{2}+\delta_{n}\bigr)}.

(43)

The relaxed value is chosen so that the modified-energy dissipation law is preserved.

Proposition 9
.

Under the assumptions above, the relaxed pair satisfies

\widetilde{E}[\phi^{n+1},q^{n+1}]\leq\widetilde{E}[\phi^{n},q^{n}]-(D_{n}-\delta_{n})\leq\widetilde{E}[\phi^{n},q^{n}].

(44)

Proof.

\phi^{n+1}
is unchanged, so

\widetilde{E}[\phi^{n+1},q^{n+1}]-\widetilde{E}[\phi^{n+1},\widetilde{q}^{n+1}]=(q^{n+1})^{2}-(\widetilde{q}^{n+1})^{2}\leq\delta_{n}

by (
43
), hence

\widetilde{E}[\phi^{n+1},q^{n+1}]\leq\widetilde{E}[\phi^{n},q^{n}]-(D_{n}-\delta_{n})
.

The squared defect from
Q(\phi^{n+1})
is

Q(\phi^{n+1})^{2}-(q^{n+1})^{2}=\bigl[Q(\phi^{n+1})^{2}-(\widetilde{q}^{n+1})^{2}-\delta_{n}\bigr]_{+}.

Every choice
\delta_{n}\in[0,D_{n}]
satisfies Proposition
9
. The unrelaxed energy law of Theorem
5
, applied to
(\phi^{n+1},\widetilde{q}^{n+1})
, decomposes
D_{n}
into nonnegative contributions, including

S_{n}:=\bigl(\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n},\,\mathcal{S}_{r}^{n}(\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n})\bigr);

in particular
S_{n}\in[0,D_{n}]
. Three concrete choices are

\delta_{n}=\rho D_{n},\qquad\delta_{n}=\rho S_{n},\qquad\delta_{n}=S_{n}+\rho(D_{n}-S_{n}),

(45)

with
\rho\in[0,1]
. In the finite-dimensional tests below we use the maximal choice
\delta_{n}=D_{n}
.

Numerical experiments

We conclude with numerical experiments illustrating the mechanisms discussed above. PDE reference solutions are computed by ETDRK4
[
4
]
with sufficiently small reference time steps, and the spatial discretization is Fourier spectral on the periodic domain
\Omega=[0,2\pi]^{2}
. Unless stated otherwise, each component energy is given a strictly positive shift
C_{\alpha}=10^{-12}

C=\frac{3|\Omega|}{4\varepsilon^{2}},

and we use the two-component bounded-below decomposition

E_{1,1}[\phi]=\int_{\Omega}\frac{(\phi^{2}-2)^{2}}{4\varepsilon^{2}}\,dx,\qquad E_{1,2}[\phi]=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\phi^{2}}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\,dx.

(48)

Their variational derivatives satisfy

\frac{\delta E_{1,1}}{\delta\phi}+\frac{\delta E_{1,2}}{\delta\phi}=\frac{\phi^{3}-\phi}{\varepsilon^{2}},

and

E_{1,1}[\phi]+E_{1,2}[\phi]=E_{1}[\phi]+C.

Thus the single SAV auxiliary variable is defined from
E_{1}+C
, while rank-
2
PB-SAV and MSAV use the two components
E_{1,1}
and
E_{1,2}
, whose sum is
E_{1}+C
. The decomposition changes neither the Allen–Cahn force nor the reference PDE.

5.2.2
A Fourier-band residual split

The preceding density split does not improve the trajectory error for the smooth initial condition. We therefore use a different admissible split of the same Allen–Cahn energy, designed to expose a finite-step accuracy gain.

Write the double-well residual
\omega:=(\phi^{2}-1)/2
, so that
E_{1}[\phi]=\varepsilon^{-2}\int_{\Omega}\omega^{2}\,dx
. Let
\{\Pi_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha=1}^{r}

\varepsilon^{-2}\int_{\Omega}(\Pi_{\alpha}\omega)^{2}\,dx

A finite-rank nonlocal model problem

5.4
Linear nonlocal Cahn–Hilliard equation with exact spectral reference

Adaptive rank

The exact-reference setting also allows us to test step-dependent compression of a fine decomposition. We start from a fine partition of
E_{1}
into eight initial-energy bands
\{E_{1,\alpha}^{\rm fine}\}_{\alpha=1}^{8}
. At each step, we identify the active set

\mathcal{A}_{n}:=\bigl\{\alpha:E_{1,\alpha}^{\rm fine}[\phi^{n}]/E_{1}[\phi^{n}]\geq\theta\bigr\},\qquad\theta=0.05,

and form the step-
n
decomposition by keeping each
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}_{n}
as a separate component and merging the remaining bands into a single inactive component, so the active rank is
r_{n}=|\mathcal{A}_{n}|+1
(or
r_{n}=8
if every band is active). This step-dependent partition satisfies the admissibility
assumptions of Theorem
7
, and merging inactive bands is the coarsening direction of Proposition
4
; the energy-fraction indicator is a coarse proxy for the weight
w_{\alpha}=(Q_{\alpha}^{n})^{2}
in the weighted-variance identity (
24
), since low-weight components contribute little when the normalized directional slopes
y_{\alpha}
remain moderate; this is
the case in the present spectral example.

Figure

2.0

2.2

T=1

the active rank starts at

3
near

2

6
Conclusion

We have introduced a pullback-corrected SAV (PB-SAV) family for gradient flows. The method retains the single auxiliary variable of SAV but uses the pullback correction generated by an admissible decomposition of the nonlinear energy. In this way the number of scalar energy trackers is separated from the rank of the correction applied to the state equation.

The analysis shows that this separation is compatible with the usual SAV energy mechanism. PB-SAV satisfies a modified-energy dissipation law with the same scalar modified energy as SAV; the result also holds for step-dependent decompositions. The correction produced by a refined decomposition dominates the coarser correction in the positive semidefinite order, and the gap is an explicit weighted variance. This identity explains what refinement changes at the level of the linearized state equation, although the resulting trajectory accuracy still depends on the active component geometry. The Sherman–Morrison–Woodbury formula gives an efficient low-rank implementation, and the finite-dimensional viewpoint identifies the correction with the Gauss–Newton matrix of a least-squares representation of the nonlinear energy.

The numerical experiments are consistent with this interpretation. In some smooth regimes PB-SAV has the same first-order behavior as SAV and mainly changes the error constant. In finite-dimensional problems with low-rank or grouped curvature, and in nonlocal examples where the active component structure is well represented by the chosen decomposition, the higher-rank correction substantially improves the trajectory accuracy.

Future work will address higher-order PB-SAV schemes, systematic adaptive-rank selection, and sharper error estimates that relate the weighted-variance gap to the observed trajectory error.
