Polish topologies on endomorphism monoids of linear orders Thanks: The first named author was supported by the Austrian Science Fund FWF (Grant ESP 399). Thanks: The second named author was supported by the Heilbronn Institute for Mathematical Research.
S. Bardyla, L. Elliott

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate Polish semigroup topologies on the endomorphism monoids End ⁡ ( ℕ , ≤ ) \operatorname{End}(\mathbb{N},\leq) and End ⁡ ( ℤ , ≤ ) \operatorname{End}(\mathbb{Z},\leq) . We introduce a new structural condition, property 𝕏 ​ 𝕏 \mathbb{XX} , which yields automatic continuity of Borel measurable homomorphisms between certain topological semigroups. This provides a new method for analyzing Polish semigroup topologies on monoids with small groups of units. We show that for all monoids considered, the semigroup Zariski topology coincides with the pointwise topology and is therefore the coarsest Hausdorff semigroup topology. We prove that the submonoid End ∞ ⁡ ( ℕ , ≤ ) \operatorname{End}^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},\leq) of End ⁡ ( ℕ , ≤ ) \operatorname{End}(\mathbb{N},\leq) consisting of all endomorphisms with infinite image admits a unique Polish semigroup topology, namely the pointwise topology. On the other hand, despite possessing a finest Polish semigroup topology, the monoids End ⁡ ( ℕ , ≤ ) \operatorname{End}(\mathbb{N},\leq) and End ⁡ ( ℤ , ≤ ) \operatorname{End}(\mathbb{Z},\leq) , admit infinitely many distinct Polish semigroup topologies. Also, we show that the monoid End ⁡ ( ℕ , < ) \operatorname{End}(\mathbb{N},<) admits exactly 2 ℵ 0 2^{\aleph_{0}} Polish semigroup topologies and no maximal second-countable semigroup topology.

Introduction and main results

In this paper, we compose functions from left to right, i.e.,
(x)fg=((x)f)g
, and we let
\mathbb{N}
denote the set of all non-negative integers.
Recall that the
full transformation monoid

X^{X}
on a set
X
consists of all functions from
X
to
X
equipped with the operation of composition of functions.
Let
S
be a subsemigroup of the full transformation monoid
X^{X}
. Then the
pointwise topology on
S
is generated by the subbasis consisting of the sets

U_{x,y}:=\{f\in S:(x)f=y\},

where
x,y\in X
.

It follows from
[
2
,
11
]
that the pointwise topology is the unique Polish group topology on the symmetric group
\operatorname{Sym}(\mathbb{N})
, i.e., it is the unique topology which turns
\operatorname{Sym}(\mathbb{N})
into a separable completely metrizable topological group. Later Kechris and Rosendal
[
16
]
generalized this result and showed that the pointwise topology is the unique separable Hausdorff group topology on
\operatorname{Sym}(\mathbb{N})
. Similar results were discovered for other natural semigroups. For instance, it was shown in
[
9
]
that the pointwise topology is the unique Polish semigroup topology on the full transformation monoid
\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
, i.e., it is the unique topology which turns
\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
into a Polish topological semigroup. Also, the monoids of partial transformations of
\mathbb{N}
, and continuous self-maps of the Hilbert cube
[0,1]^{\mathbb{N}}
or the Cantor space
2^{\mathbb{N}}
possess a unique Polish semigroup topology
[
9
]
.

Let
\rho
be a binary relation on a set
X
. Recall that the
endomorphism monoid

\operatorname{End}(X,\rho)
is the submonoid of
X^{X}
such that for each
f\in\operatorname{End}(X,\rho)
and
(a,b)\in\rho
we have
((a)f,(b)f)\in\rho
. The algebraic and topological structures of endomorphism monoids are widely studied in the literature, see
[
4
,
5
,

,

,
10
,
12
,
18
,
19
,
21
,
22
]
. It was shown in
[
10
]
that the pointwise topology is the unique Polish semigroup topology on endomorphism monoids of the random graph, random digraph, and random partial order.
In
[
22
]
it was shown that the pointwise topology is the unique Polish semigroup topology on
\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{Q},\leq)
. Also, in
[
19
]
it was shown that the pointwise topology is a minimal Hausdorff semigroup topology on the elementary embedding monoids of various structures.

In this paper, we continue this theme by investigating Polish semigroup topologies on the endomorphism monoids
\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{N},<)
,
\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{N},\leq)
, and
\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{Z},\leq)
.
It is noteworthy that the aforementioned results on the uniqueness of Polish semigroup topologies on endomorphism monoids were proven using different versions of the so-called property
\mathbb{X}
(introduced in
[
9
]
) applied to the group of units of the corresponding monoids.
In particular, it was crucial that the corresponding relational structures have large automorphism groups.
Since
\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{N},<)
and
\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{N},\leq)
have trivial groups of units and the group of units of
\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{Z},\leq)
is isomorphic to the additive group of integers, the techniques which depend on a large group of units are not applicable to these monoids.
To remedy this, we introduce a new property defined below.

Definition 1.1
.

We say that a semigroup
S
equipped with a topology has property
\mathbb{XX}
with respect to a subset
A\subseteq S
if

(1)

A
is dense
G_{\delta}
in
S
;

(2)

for all
(a,s)\in A\times S
there exist
b_{0},\ldots,b_{n}\in S^{1}
(here
S^{1}=S
, if
S
is a monoid, and
S^{1}
is equal to
S
with an adjoined unit, otherwise) such that the map
t_{a,s}:S\rightarrow S
defined by
(x)t_{a,s}=b_{0}xb_{1}\cdots xb_{n}
has the following properties:

(2.1)

t_{a,s}
is continuous;

(2.2)

t_{a,s}{\restriction}_{A}:A\rightarrow S
is an open map (here
A
has the subspace topology inherited from
S
);

(2.3)

(a)t_{a,s}=s
.

\mathbb{XX}
allows us to prove the automatic continuity of Borel measurable homomorphisms between certain topological semigroups. In particular, the following technical result (which appears later as

\mathbb{XX}
with respect to some

In this case every Borel measurable homomorphism from

In most of the aforementioned cases the unique Polish semigroup topology on a given semigroup coincides with the semigroup Zariski topology, which has purely algebraic nature and is defined below.

Definition 1.3

Maximal Polish semigroup topologies and property
\mathbb{XX}

In this section, we develop techniques of finding maximal Polish topologies on a given semigroup.
The following useful lemma follows from Lemma 13.2 and Lemma 13.3 of
[
15
]
.

Lemma 2.1

(Folklore)
.

If
\mathcal{T}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{1}\subseteq\ldots
is a sequence of Polish topologies on a set
X
, then the least topology containing
\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{T}_{i}
is Polish as well.
In particular, if
\mathcal{T}
is a Polish topology on a set
X
and
(K_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of sets which are closed with respect to
\mathcal{T}
, then the least topology containing
\mathcal{T}
and all of the sets
K_{i}
is Polish.

Definition 2.2
.

If
\mathcal{T}
is a topology on a set
X
, then we denote the set of Borel subsets of
X
with respect to
\mathcal{T}
by
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{T})
.

A function
f:X\rightarrow X
is called
Borel measurable
if
(U)f^{-1}
is Borel, for each open subset
U
of
X
.
The following proposition follows from Corollary 15.2 in
[
15
]
.

Proposition 2.3
.

If
X
and
Y
are Polish spaces and
f:X\to Y
is a Borel measurable
bijection, then
f^{-1}
is Borel measurable also. In particular, if
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}
are Polish topologies on the same set such that
\tau_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\tau_{2})
, then
\mathcal{B}(\tau_{1})=\mathcal{B}(\tau_{2})
.

Definition 2.4
.

If
A\subseteq X
, then we denote the subspace topology that
\mathcal{T}
induces on
A
by
\mathcal{T}|_{A}
.

A subset
A
of a space
X
is called
meager
if
A
is contained in the union of countably many nowhere dense sets. A subset
A\subseteq X
is called
comeager
, if the set
X\setminus A
is meager. The following proposition is well-known but we include its short proof.

Proposition 2.5

(Folklore)
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{T}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{2}
are topologies on a set
X
where
\mathcal{T}_{2}
is second-countable.

(1)

If
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{T}_{2})\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{T}_{1})
, then there is a subset
C
of
X
which is comeager with respect to
\mathcal{T}_{1}
such that
\mathcal{T}_{2}|_{C}\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{1}|_{C}
.

(2)

If
\mathcal{T}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{2}
are Polish, then there is a subset
C
of
X
which is comeager with respect to
\mathcal{T}_{1}
such that
\mathcal{T}_{1}|_{C}=\mathcal{T}_{2}|_{C}
.

Proof.

(1):
Let
\left\{U_{i}\;\colon\;\begin{tabular}[]{@{}l@{}}$i\in\mathbb{N}$\end{tabular}\right\}
be a countable basis for
\mathcal{T}_{2}
. For each
i\in\mathbb{N}
, we have that
U_{i}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{T}_{2})\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{T}_{1})
. Thus for all
i
, the set
U_{i}
is Borel in
\mathcal{T}_{1}
. Proposition 8.22 of
[
15
]
implies that for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
there are a meager set
M_{i}
and open set
U_{i}^{\prime}
(both with respect to
\mathcal{T}_{1}
) such that
U_{i}\backslash M_{i}=U_{i}^{\prime}\backslash M_{i}
.
Let
C=X\backslash(\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}M_{i})
. By construction, the set
C
is comeager with respect to
\mathcal{T}_{1}
, and all of the sets
U_{i}\cap C
belong to
\mathcal{T}_{1}|_{C}
.
As these sets are a basis for
\mathcal{T}_{2}|_{C}
, the result follows.

(2):

By
Proposition

2.3
, it follows that
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{T}_{2})=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{T}_{1})
.
Thus by
(1)
, there is a subset
C
which is comeager with respect to
\mathcal{T}_{1}
with
\mathcal{T}_{2}{\restriction}_{C}\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{1}{\restriction}_{C}
. As
\mathcal{T}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{2}
, the result follows.
∎

Definition 2.6
.

We say that a topology
\mathcal{T}
on a set
X
is
regular
if every neighborhood of each point in
X
contains a closed neighborhood of that point.

Note that we don’t assume regular spaces to be Hausdorff. In fact,
Lemma

2.7
(2) is false if we replace all instances of the word “regular” with the words “regular Hausdorff”.
However we are primarily concerned with Polish spaces and we discuss regular spaces in order to arrive at
Theorem

2.14
, which is applicable to metrizable spaces.

Lemma 2.7
.

Suppose that
X,Y
are sets,
f:X\to Y
is a function and
\mathcal{T}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{2}
are regular topologies on
Y
. The following topologies are regular:

(1)

The topology on
Y
generated by
\mathcal{T}_{1}
and
\mathcal{T}_{2}
.

(2)

The topology
(\mathcal{T}_{1})f^{-1}:=\left\{(U)f^{-1}\;\colon\;\begin{tabular}[]{@{}l@{}}$U\in\mathcal{T}_{1}$\end{tabular}\right\}
on
X
.

Proof.

(1):
Let
y\in Y
. The set

\left\{N_{1}\cap N_{2}\;\colon\;\begin{tabular}[]{@{}l@{}}$N_{1}$ is a $\mathcal{T}_{1}$ neighborhood of $y$,
$N_{2}$ is a $\mathcal{T}_{2}$ neighborhood of $y$\end{tabular}\right\}

is a neighbourhood basis for
y
in the topology generated by
\mathcal{T}_{1}
and
\mathcal{T}_{2}
.
Let
N_{1}\cap N_{2}
be such a neighbourhood, we need only show that it contains a closed neighborhood of
y
. As
\mathcal{T}_{1}
and
\mathcal{T}_{2}
are regular, there are closed neighborhoods
N_{1}^{\prime}
and
N_{2}^{\prime}
of
y
with respect to
\mathcal{T}_{1}
and
\mathcal{T}_{2}
respectively such that
N_{1}^{\prime}\subseteq N_{1}
and
N_{2}^{\prime}\subseteq N_{2}
. Thus the neighborhood
N_{1}^{\prime}\cap N_{2}^{\prime}
is closed in the topology generated by
\mathcal{T}_{1}
and
\mathcal{T}_{2}
. As
N_{1}^{\prime}\cap N_{2}^{\prime}\subseteq N_{1}\cap N_{2}
the result follows.

(2):
Let
x\in X
and suppose
x\in V\in(\mathcal{T}_{1})f^{-1}
. It follows that there is
U\in\mathcal{T}_{1}
with
V=(U)f^{-1}
and hence
(x)f\in U
. As
\mathcal{T}_{1}
is regular, there is a closed neighborhood
U^{\prime}
of
(x)f
with
U^{\prime}\subseteq U
. Thus
(Y\setminus U^{\prime})f^{-1}
is an open set, witnessing that
(U^{\prime})f^{-1}\subseteq V
is a closed neighborhood of

as required.
∎

The following technical lemma is useful for finding the points of continuity of the identity function on a set
X
, when
X
is endowed with multiple topologies.

Lemma 2.8
.

Suppose that
X
is a set and
\mathcal{T}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{2}
are topologies on
X
with
\mathcal{T}_{2}
regular. Suppose further that
D\subseteq X
is dense with respect to the topology generated by
\mathcal{T}_{1}
and
\mathcal{T}_{2}
, and the identity map
\operatorname{id}:(D,\mathcal{T}_{1}|_{D})\to(D,\mathcal{T}_{2}|_{D})
is continuous.
In this case the identity map
\operatorname{id}:(X,\mathcal{T}_{1})\to(X,\mathcal{T}_{2})
is continuous at each point in
D
.

Proof.

Let
d\in D
and
U
be a neighborhood of
d
with respect to
\mathcal{T}_{2}
, we only need to show that
U
is a neighborhood of
d
with respect to
\mathcal{T}_{1}
.
As
\mathcal{T}_{2}
is regular, we may assume without loss of generality that
U
is closed with respect to
\mathcal{T}_{2}
.

The set
U\cap D
is a closed neighborhood of
d
in the topological space
(D,\mathcal{T}_{2}|_{D})
. Thus
U\cap D
is a closed neighborhood of
d
in the topological space
(D,\mathcal{T}_{1}|_{D})
. Let
V\in\mathcal{T}_{1}
be such that
d\in V
and
V\cap D\subseteq U
. It suffices to show that
V\subseteq U
.
In other words, we only need to show that
V\backslash U=\varnothing
. As
U
is closed in
\mathcal{T}_{2}
and
V
is open in
\mathcal{T}_{1}
, the set
V\backslash U
is open in the topology generated by
\mathcal{T}_{1}
and
\mathcal{T}_{2}
. Thus
D\cap(V\backslash U)
is dense in
V\backslash U
. But
D\cap V\subseteq U
, so
D\cap(V\backslash U)=\varnothing
is dense in
V\backslash U
. Thus
V\backslash U=\varnothing
as required.
∎

A map
f:X\rightarrow Y
is called
open
if
(U)f
is open for each open subset
U
of
X
. To keep the paper self-contained, we include a short proof of the following folklore fact.

Lemma 2.9

(Folklore)
.

The preimage of a meager set under an open continuous map is meager.

Proof.

Let
X,Y
be topological spaces and
f:X\rightarrow Y
be an open continuous map. Fix any meager set
B\subseteq Y
. Then there exist nowhere dense subsets
A_{n}
,
n\in\omega
such that
B=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}A_{n}
. Since
(B)f^{-1}=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}(A_{n})f^{-1}
, it suffices to show that
(A_{n})f^{-1}
is nowhere dense in
X
for every
n\in\omega
.
Assuming the contrary, there exist
n\in\omega
and a nonempty open set
U
such that
U\subseteq\overline{(A_{n})f^{-1}}
. Observe that

(U)f\subseteq(\overline{(A_{n})f^{-1}})f\subseteq\overline{(A_{n})f^{-1}f}\subseteq\overline{A_{n}},

where the first inclusion holds by the choice of
U
, and the second inclusion follows from the continuity of
f
.
Thus
(U)f
is a non-empty open subset of
\overline{A_{n}}
, which contradicts the choice of
A_{n}
. Hence
(B)f^{-1}
is meager.
∎

(1)

(2)

(1):
Let
C

\mathcal{T}_{1}

I
is a prime ideal, the set
\{\varnothing,I,\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{N},<)\backslash I,\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{N},<)\}
is a semigroup topology on
\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{N},<)
. Thus for all
f\in\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{N},<)
, the topology
\tau(f)
generated by

\tau^{*}(\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{N},<)\backslash C_{f})\qquad\hbox{ and }\qquad\{\varnothing,I,\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{N},<)\backslash I,\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{N},<)\}

is a second-countable semigroup topology.
The topological space
(\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{N},<),\tau(f))
is metrizable as well, as it is the topological sum of two metrizable spaces
I
and
\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{N},<)\backslash I
, where each of those carries the subspace topology inherited from
\tau^{*}(\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{N},<)\backslash C_{f})
. Since neither the set
I\backslash C_{f}
nor the set
\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{N},<)\backslash(I\cup C_{f})
contains isolated points, the only isolated points of
(\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{N},<),\tau(f))
are those of
C_{f}
. Thus these topologies are all distinct.

We claim that none of these topologies are Polish.
Suppose for a contradiction that
\tau(f)
is Polish. As the set
C_{f}
is clopen, it follows that the subspace
\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{N},<)\backslash C_{f}
is Polish also.
Similarly, both of the clopen subspaces
I\backslash C_{f}
and
\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{N},<)\backslash(I\cup C_{f})
are Polish.
However, these spaces carry the subspace topology from
\tau^{*}(\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{N},<)\backslash C_{f})
.
Therefore, both these spaces are Polish subspaces of
(\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{N},<),\tau^{*}(\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{N},<)\backslash C_{f}))
. Thus both
I\setminus C_{f}
and
\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{N},<)\backslash(I\cup C_{f})
are
G_{\delta}
with respect to
\tau^{*}(\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{N},<)\backslash C_{f})
.
It follows that the Polish space
\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{N},<)\backslash C_{f}
can be partitioned into two dense
G_{\delta}
subsets, this contradicts the Baire Category Theorem.
∎

4.
\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},\leq)

The goal of this section is to prove each item of
Theorem

1.7
. In doing so it will be convenient to state some of the results in sufficient generality to also apply them in the proof of
Theorem

1.8
.

Recall that
\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},\leq)
is the submonoid of
\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{N},\leq)
consisting of all maps with infinite image. For an element
x
of semigroup
S
by
l_{x}
we denote the left shift by
x
in
S
, i.e.
l_{x}:S\rightarrow S
,
y\mapsto xy
. Similarly,
r_{x}:S\rightarrow S
,
y\mapsto yx
is the right shift by
x
in
S
. A topology on a semigroup
S
is called
shift-continuous
if all left and all right shifts in
S
are continuous.

Theorem 4.1
.

The semigroup Zariski topology on
\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},\leq)
is the pointwise topology.

Proof.

Since the pointwise topology on
\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},\leq)
is a Hausdorff semigroup topology, we get that the semigroup Zariski topology on
\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},\leq)
is contained in the pointwise topology. To show the converse inclusion, it suffices to check that for any positive integer
n
and
h\in\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},\leq)
the set

U_{n}^{h}:=\left\{k\in\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},\leq)\;\colon\;\begin{tabular}[]{@{}l@{}}$k{\restriction}_{n}=h{\restriction}_{n}$\end{tabular}\right\}

is a (not necessarily open) neighborhood of
h
with respect to the semigroup Zariski topology.

We first show that
U_{n+2}^{1_{\mathbb{N}}}
is open.
For all
i\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\iota_{i}\in\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{N},\leq)
be the unique injection with image
\mathbb{N}\backslash\{i\}
. In particular, the maps
\iota_{i},\iota_{i+1}
agree on all values except
(i)\iota_{i}=i+1
and
(i)\iota_{i+1}=i
.

For all
i\in\mathbb{N}
we define

K_{i}:=\left\{f\in\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},\leq)\;\colon\;\begin{tabular}[]{@{}l@{}}$(i)f=(i+1)f$\end{tabular}\right\}=\left\{f\in\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},\leq)\;\colon\;\begin{tabular}[]{@{}l@{}}$\iota_{i}f=\iota_{i+1}f$\end{tabular}\right\}.

Similarly, for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
define

I_{i}:=\left\{f\in\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},\leq)\;\colon\;\begin{tabular}[]{@{}l@{}}$i\not\in\operatorname{im}(f)$\end{tabular}\right\}=\left\{f\in\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},\leq)\;\colon\;\begin{tabular}[]{@{}l@{}}$f\iota_{i}=f\iota_{i+1}$\end{tabular}\right\}.

Observe that for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
the sets
K_{i}
and
I_{i}
are closed in the semigroup Zariski topology on
\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},\leq)
.
It is easy to check that for all
m\in\mathbb{N}

U_{m}^{1_{\mathbb{N}}}=\left(\bigcap_{i<m-1}\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},\leq)\backslash K_{i}\right)\cap\left(\bigcap_{i<m}\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},\leq)\backslash I_{i}\right).

It follows that for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
the set
U_{m}^{1_{\mathbb{N}}}

is open in the semigroup Zariski topology on
\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},\leq)
.

Fix the increasing enumeration
\operatorname{im}(h)=\{x_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
. Define the map
f_{\min}\in\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},\leq)
by

(n)f_{\min}=\min((x_{n})h^{-1})
. Let
h^{\operatorname{im}}
be the function that sends
n
to
x_{n}
and note that
h^{\operatorname{im}}=f_{\min}h
.
Let

(n)g=\begin{cases}0,&\hbox{ if }0\leq n\leq(0)h;\\
k,&\hbox{ if }(k-1)h<n\leq(k)h.\end{cases}

Since
\mathfrak{Z}
is shift-continuous and
h^{\operatorname{im}}g=1_{\mathbb{N}}
, we get that

(U_{n+2}^{1})r_{g}^{-1}

is open in the semigroup Zariski topology and contains
h^{\operatorname{im}}
, where
r_{g}
is the right shift by
g
in the monoid
\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},\leq)
. Moreover, the set

T:=(U_{n+2}^{1_{\mathbb{N}}})r_{g}^{-1}\cap\bigcap_{i<{n+2}}(\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},\leq)\backslash I_{x_{i}})

is open in
(\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},\leq),\mathfrak{Z})
and contains
h^{\operatorname{im}}
.
We show that
T\subseteq U_{n+1}^{h^{\operatorname{im}}}
, which would imply that
U_{n+1}^{h^{\operatorname{im}}}
is a neighborhood of
h^{\operatorname{im}}
in
(\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},\leq),\mathfrak{Z})
.
Suppose that
\phi\in T
, i.e.,
\phi g\in U_{n+2}^{1_{\mathbb{N}}}
and the image of
\phi
contains the first
n+1
points in the image of
h
. Then for
i<n+1
,
(i)\phi
must belong to
(i)g^{-1}
. This implies that for
i<n+1
,
(i)\phi
is the only value in the image of
\phi
which belongs to
(i)g^{-1}
. In particular
(i)\phi=x_{i}
for all
i<n+1
, as
x_{i}\in(i)g^{-1}
for all
i<n+1
by the definition of
g
and by the choice of
x_{i}
. Hence
\phi\in U_{n+1}^{h^{\operatorname{im}}}
, as required.

We finally move to
U_{h}^{n}
. Define
f_{\max}\in\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},\leq)
by
(n)f_{\max}=\max((x_{n})h^{-1})
. Let

W:=(U_{n+1}^{h^{\operatorname{im}}})l_{f_{\min}}^{-1}\cap(U_{n+1}^{h^{\operatorname{im}}})l_{f_{\max}}^{-1}.

Since
f_{\max}h=f_{\min}h=h^{\operatorname{im}}
and
U_{n+1}^{h^{\operatorname{im}}}
is a neighborhood of
h^{\operatorname{im}}
, we get that
W
is a neighborhood of
h
in
(\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},\leq),\mathfrak{Z})
. It remains to show that
W\subseteq U_{n}^{h}
.

Suppose that
\phi\in W
, i.e. both
f_{\min}\phi
and
f_{\max}\phi
belong to
U_{n+1}^{h^{\operatorname{im}}}
. First we show that if
i<(n)f_{\max}
, then
(i)h=(i+1)h
implies that
(i)\phi=(i+1)\phi
.
Observe that for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\displaystyle(i)h=(i+1)h\iff\exists m\hbox{ such that }(i,i+1\in(x_{m})h^{-1})\iff

\displaystyle\iff\exists m\hbox{ such that }((m)f_{\min}\leq i<(m)f_{\max}).

Hence if
i<(n)f_{\max}
and
(i)h=(i+1)h
, then there is
m\in\mathbb{N}
with
(m)f_{\min}\leq i<(m)f_{\max}
, and

(i)\phi\leq(i+1)\phi\leq(m)f_{\max}\phi=(m)h^{\operatorname{im}}=(m)f_{\min}\phi\leq(i)\phi,

where the equalities hold since
(m)f_{\min}\leq i<(n)f_{\max}
implies that
m<n
, and from above
f_{\min}\phi,f_{\max}\phi\in U_{n+1}^{h^{\operatorname{im}}}
. So
(i)\phi=(i+1)\phi
.
Hence
(i)h=(i+1)h
implies that
(i)\phi=(i+1)\phi
for all
i<(n)f_{\max}
.

We show by induction that
(i)\phi=(i)h
for all
i<n
.
Since
(0)f_{\min}=0
, we get that

(0)\phi=(0)f_{\min}\phi=(0)h^{\operatorname{im}}=x_{0}=(0)h.

Suppose that
\phi{\restriction}_{i}=h{\restriction}_{i}
for some
0<i<n
. We show that
(i)\phi=(i)h
.
There are two cases to consider:

(1)

(i)h=(i-1)h
;

(2)

(i)h>(i-1)h
.

(1) Since
i-1<n\leq(n)f_{\max}
, the arguments above imply that
(i)\phi=(i-1)\phi
. By the inductive assumption,
(i-1)h=(i-1)\phi
. Thus
(i)\phi=(i)h
.

(2) In this case
i=\min((i)hh^{-1}
). So there is
x\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
such that
(x)f_{\min}=i
. Since
f_{\min}
is an order preserving injection, we have
x\leq i<n
. Then, taking into account that
\phi\in W
, we obtain the following:

(i)h=(x)f_{\min}h=(x)h^{\operatorname{im}}=(x)f_{\min}\phi=(i)\phi.

Hence
W\subseteq U_{n}^{h}
, witnessing that
U_{n}^{h}
is a neighborhood of
h
with respect to the semigroup Zariski topology, as required.
∎

Definition 4.3
.

We define
\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z},\leq)
to be the submonoid of
\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{Z},\leq)
consisting of those maps whose image neither contains a minimum element nor a maximum element.

We will need to show that both
\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},\leq)
and
\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z},\leq)
have property
\mathbb{XX}
with respect to themselves. The following lemma is the first step in this direction.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
X
be a subset of
(\mathbb{Z},\leq)
.
When giving
\operatorname{End}(X,\leq)
the pointwise topology, the following assertions hold:

(1)

for each surjective endomorphism
f
, the right shift
r_{f}
is open;

(2)

for each injective endomorphism
g
, the left shift
l_{g}
is open.

Proof.

If
X
is finite, then there is nothing to show as
\operatorname{End}(X,\leq)
is discrete, and thus each self-map of
\operatorname{End}(X,\leq)
is open and closed.

Assume that
X
is infinite.
For all
a,b\in X
with
a\leq b
and
f\in\operatorname{End}(X,\leq)
, we define
f_{a,b}
to be the restriction of
f
to the set
\left\{x\in X\;\colon\;\begin{tabular}[]{@{}l@{}}$a\leq x\leq b$\end{tabular}\right\}
.
We then define

U^{f}_{a,b}:=\left\{h\in\operatorname{End}(X,\leq)\;\colon\;\begin{tabular}[]{@{}l@{}}$f_{a,b}\subseteq h$\end{tabular}\right\}.

These sets form a basis for the topology on
\operatorname{End}(X,\leq)
.

(1) Let
f\in\operatorname{End}(X,\leq)
be surjective,
g\in\operatorname{End}(X,\leq)
and
a,b\in X
with
a\leq b
.
In order to show that the right shift
r_{f}
is open, it suffices to check that
U_{a,b}^{g}f=U_{a,b}^{gf}
. The inclusion
U_{a,b}^{g}f\subseteq U_{a,b}^{gf}
is obvious.
To show the converse inclusion fix any
w\in U_{a,b}^{gf}
.
Note that for all
k\in X
, the set
I_{k}:=((k)w)f^{-1}
is a nonempty convex subset of
X
. Moreover the sets
I_{k}
are pairwise disjoint, and
i_{j}<i_{k}
whenever
i_{j}\in I_{j}
,
i_{k}\in I_{k}
and
j<k
. Since
(k)w=(k)gf
providing
a\leq k\leq b
, we have that
(k)g\in I_{k}
for all
a\leq k\leq b
.
For each
k\in X
, the set
\operatorname{im}(g_{a,b})\cap I_{k}
is finite (possibly empty). So we can choose for each
k\in X
elements
c^{k}_{\max},c^{k}_{\min}\in I_{k}
such that
c^{k}_{\max}
is not lower than any element of
\operatorname{im}(g_{a,b})\cap I_{k}
,
(c^{k}_{\min}
is no larger than any element of
\operatorname{im}(g_{a,b})\cap I_{k}
, and
c^{k}_{\max}\geq c^{k}_{\min}
. Note that if
\operatorname{im}(g_{a,b})\cap I_{k}=\emptyset
, then
c^{k}_{\max}\geq c^{k}_{\min}
is the only condition
c^{k}_{\max}
and
c^{k}_{\min}
are obliged to satisfy.
We define
h\in U_{a,b}^{g}
as follows:

(k)h:=\begin{cases}c^{k}_{\min}&k<a\\
c^{k}_{\max}&k>b\\
(k)g_{a,b}&a\leq k\leq b.\end{cases}

As
(k)h\in I_{k}
for all
k\in X
, it follows that
hf=w
.
In particular
w=hf\in U^{g}_{a,b}f
as required.

(2) Let
g,f\in\operatorname{End}(X,\leq)
be such that
g
is an injection. We only need to show that the image of each neighborhood of
f
under
l_{g}
is a neighborhood of
gf
.
Let
a,b\in X
be such that

(1)

a\leq b
,

(2)

either
a\in\operatorname{im}(g)
or
a=\min(X)
,

(3)

either
b\in\operatorname{im}(g)
or
b=\max(X)
.

We show that
gU^{f}_{a,b}
is a neighborhood of
gf
. This is sufficient as the family

\{U_{a,b}^{f}:a,b\hbox{ satisfy conditions }(1)\hbox{--}(3)\}

forms a neighbourhood base at
f
.
Let
a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\in X
be such that whenever
a\leq(k)g\leq b
, we have
a^{\prime}\leq k\leq b^{\prime}
(these exist as
g
is injective and there are only finitely many such
(k)g
).
We show that
U^{gf}_{a^{\prime},b^{\prime}}\subseteq gU^{f}_{a,b}
.
Let
w\in U^{gf}_{a^{\prime},b^{\prime}}
and consider the order preserving map
g^{-1}w:\operatorname{im}(g)\to X
.
If
h\in X^{X}
, then
gh=w\iff h\supseteq g^{-1}w
.
To conclude that
w\in gU^{f}_{a,b}
, we need to show that
g^{-1}w\cup f_{a,b}
has an extension in
\operatorname{End}(X)
.
It is easy to verify that every order preserving map between subsets of
X
has an extension to an element of
\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{Z},\leq)
(in particular to an element of
\operatorname{End}(X,\leq)
).
Thus we only need to show that
p:=g^{-1}w\cup f_{a,b}
is an order preserving partial function.

We first show that
p
is a partial function. By the choice of
a^{\prime},b^{\prime}
, if
i\in\operatorname{im}(g)
and
a\leq i\leq b
then
a^{\prime}\leq(i)g^{-1}\leq b^{\prime}
.
Thus
((i)g^{-1})w=((i)g^{-1})gf=(i)f
witnesses that
g^{-1}w
and
f_{a,b}
agree
on
\operatorname{dom}(g^{-1}w)\cap\{x\in X:a\leq x\leq b\}=\operatorname{im}(g)\cap\{x\in X:a\leq x\leq b\}
.
Hence
p
is a partial function.

It remains only to show that
p
is order preserving. Let
k,k^{+}\in\operatorname{dom}(p)
be arbitrary such that
k^{+}
is the successor of
k
in the poset
\operatorname{dom}(p)
. It suffices to show that
(k)p\leq(k^{+})p
. There are 3 cases to consider:

(1)

k<a
. As
k^{+}\leq a
and
k^{+}
is not minimal in
X
, it follows from the choice of
a
(see condition (2)) that
a\in\operatorname{im}(g)
.
As
k,k^{+}\in\operatorname{dom}(p)
, it follows that
k,k^{+}\in\operatorname{dom}(g^{-1}w)=\operatorname{im}(g)
.

(2)

a\leq k<b
. In this case
k,k^{+}\in\operatorname{dom}(f_{a,b})
.

(3)

b\leq k
. As
k\geq b
and
k
is not maximal in
X
, it follows from the choice of
b
(see condition (3)) that
b\in\operatorname{im}(g)
.
As
k,k^{+}\in\operatorname{dom}(p)
, it follows that
k,k^{+}\in\operatorname{dom}(g^{-1}w)=\operatorname{im}(g)
.

We showed above that in each case the points
k,k^{+}
belong to the domain of a common order preserving map contained in
p
, which implies that
(k)p\leq(k^{+})p
.
∎

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
X\in\{\mathbb{N},\mathbb{Z}\}
. When giving
\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(X,\leq)
the pointwise topology, we have that

(1)

for each surjective endomorphism
f
, the right shift
r_{f}:\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(X,\leq)\rightarrow\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(X,\leq)
is open;

(2)

for each injective endomorphism
g
, the left shift
l_{g}:\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(X,\leq)\rightarrow\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(X,\leq)
is open.

Proof.

Note that
\operatorname{End}(X,\leq)\setminus\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(X,\leq)
is a two-sided ideal and
f,g\in\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(X,\leq)
.
By Lemma
4.4
, the maps
r_{f},l_{g}:\operatorname{End}(X,\leq)\to\operatorname{End}(X,\leq)
are open. Let
U\subseteq\operatorname{End}(X,\leq)
be open. It is straightforward to check that

(U)r_{f}\cap\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(X,\leq)=(U\cap\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(X,\leq))r_{f}.

Thus
(U\cap\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(X,\leq))r_{f}
is open in
\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(X,\leq)
implying that
r_{f}
is an open map from
\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(X,\leq)
to itself. Similarly
l_{g}
is also an open mapping.
∎

The following algebraic fact will help us to establish that the monoids
\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},\leq)
and
\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z},\leq)
endowed with the pointwise topology have property
\mathbb{XX}
with respect to themselves.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
X\in\{\mathbb{N},\mathbb{Z}\}
. For any
f,g\in\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(X,\leq)
, there exist an injection
\phi\in\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(X,\leq)
and a surjection
\psi\in\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(X,\leq)
such that
\phi g\psi=f
.

Proof.

Let
\phi\in\operatorname{End}(X,\leq)
be an arbitrary endomorphism such that

(1)

(0)\phi g\geq(0)f
,

(2)

(n+1)\phi g-(n)\phi g>(n+1)f-(n)f
for all
n\in X
,

The function
\phi
can be constructed inductively exploiting the fact that
g\in\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(X,\leq)
.
Note that in particular
\phi
and
\phi g
are injective and order preserving.

For all
n\in X
, let

\psi_{n}:\left\{x\in X\;\colon\;\begin{tabular}[]{@{}l@{}}$(n)\phi g\leq x\leq(n+1)\phi g$\end{tabular}\right\}\rightarrow\left\{x\in X\;\colon\;\begin{tabular}[]{@{}l@{}}$(n)f\leq x\leq(n+1)f$\end{tabular}\right\}

be an order-preserving surjection.
Note that we must have

((n+1)\phi g)\psi_{n}=(n+1)f=((n+1)\phi g)\psi_{n+1}

for all
n\in X
.
In particular,
\psi_{n}
and
\psi_{n+1}
agree on the unique point in both of their domains.
Thus

\psi:=\bigcup_{n\in X}\psi_{n}

is an order-preserving partial function.
The domain of
\phi
is the set of elements of
X
which are between points in the image of
\phi g
, and the image of
\phi
is the set of points between points in the image of
f
.
If
X=\mathbb{Z}
, then
\psi
is a surjective fully defined function, and if
X=\mathbb{N}
then, since
(0)\phi g\geq(0)f
, we can extend
\psi
to a surjective fully defined function.
Moreover, by construction we have
\phi g\psi=f
as required.
∎

The following fact follows from Corollary
4.5
and Lemma
4.6
.

Proposition 4.7
.

The monoids
\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},\leq)
and
\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z},\leq)
endowed with the pointwise topology have property
\mathbb{XX}
with respect to themselves.

We can now prove the second and final item of
Theorem

1.7
.

Proposition 4.8
.

The pointwise topology is the unique Polish semigroup topology on
\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},\leq)
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a Polish semigroup topology on
\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},\leq)
. As
\mathcal{T}
is a Hausdorff semigroup topology on
\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},\leq)
,
[
9
, Proposition 2.1]
implies that
\mathcal{T}
contains the semigroup Zariski topology on
\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},\leq)
. By
Theorem

4.1
, it follows that
\mathcal{T}
is contains the pointwise topology. Since
\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{N},\leq)
equipped with the pointwise topology is Polish and
\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{N},\leq)\backslash\operatorname{End}^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},\leq)

Lemma 5.6
.

The topologies

are all Polish.

Proof.

Let

We show

to conclude the new topology is Polish.
At each step we make closed sets open (and hence clopen) and each of these sets belongs to

The sequence will conclude with a topology containing all the sets defining

so the result follows.

(1)

The pointwise topology (generated by the sets

) is Polish.

(2)

Next add the singletons
\left\{\{f\}\;\colon\;\begin{tabular}[]{@{}l@{}}$f$ has finite image size at least $n$\end{tabular}\right\}
.

(3)

Next add the complement of the open set
\left\{f\in\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{Z},\leq)\;\colon\;\begin{tabular}[]{@{}l@{}}$f$ has finite image size at least $n$\end{tabular}\right\}

C^{+}\cup C^{-}=\left\{f\in\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{Z},\leq)\;\colon\;\begin{tabular}[]{@{}l@{}}$|\operatorname{im}(f)|\geq n$ and ($|\operatorname{im}(f)|=\aleph_{0}$ or $|\operatorname{im}(f)|<n$)\end{tabular}\right\}.

is open, as it is open in the pointwise topology. Also note that
\{f\in\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{Z},\leq):|\operatorname{im}(f)|=\aleph_{0}\ \hbox{ or }\operatorname{im}(f)|<n\}
is open, as it equals the complement of the set
\left\{f\in\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{Z},\leq)\;\colon\;\begin{tabular}[]{@{}l@{}}$f$ has finite image size at least $n$\end{tabular}\right\}

(4)

(5)

,

for all

(6)

Lemma 5.7

For all

Let

5.5

Next consider the discrete topological semigroup

The map
