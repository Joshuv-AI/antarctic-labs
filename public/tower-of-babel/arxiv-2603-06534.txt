Asymmetric Stream Allocation and Linear Decodability in MIMO Coded Caching
Mohammad NaseriTehrani, MohammadJavad Salehi, and Antti Tölli

Abstract
Coded caching (CC) can transform cache memory at network devices into an active communication resource. Prior studies have shown that CC can significantly enhance the achievable Degrees of Freedom (DoF) in multi-input multi-output (MIMO) systems. To fully exploit MIMO-CC gains across all SNR regimes and enable practical linear receivers, flexible scheduling is required. Existing DoF analysis, scheduling, and linear receiver design, however, largely assume symmetric stream allocations across users. This paper extends the authors’ recent work on DoF and linear decodability analysis for MIMO-CC systems by deriving a simple criterion, based on per-user stream allocation, that guarantees linear decodability for both symmetric and non-symmetric bit-level CC schemes. Building on this, we propose a heuristic MIMO-CC delivery and scheduling framework that enables asymmetric stream allocation while adhering to linear decodability, thereby expanding the feasibility region of achievable DoF compared to symmetric-constrained designs.



I
Introduction

The growing demand for multimedia content from applications like mobile immersive viewing and extended reality is driving a continuous increase in mobile data traffic. This surge has led to the development of innovative techniques, such as coded caching (CC)
[
1
,
2
]
, that offers a performance boost proportional to the cumulative cache size of all network users. CC leverages onboard memory as a communication resource, particularly appealing for cacheable multimedia content. The performance gain of CC arises from multicasting well-constructed codewords to user groups of size
t+1
, where the CC gain
t
corresponds to the cumulative cache size of all users, normalized by the size of the content library.

Initially designed for single-antenna
[
1
]
, CC was later applied to multiple-input single-output (MISO) systems. Studies have shown that spatial multiplexing and CC gains are additive in MISO systems
[
3
]
. By using beamforming to serve multiple groups with multicast messages and suppress intra-group interference, MISO-CC can accommodate
t+L
users in parallel, thus achieving a degree-of-freedom (DoF) of
t+L
[
4
,
3
,
5
]
. To this end, multi-group multicast beamforms were developed for finite signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) regimes, balancing design complexity with finite-SNR performance
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
.

While MISO-CC is well-explored, the application of CC in multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) systems has gained attention only recently. The optimal DoF and bounds for MIMO-CC schemes were studied in
[
9
]
and
[
10
]
, using complex interference alignment techniques.
In
[
11
]
, it was demonstrated that the shared-cache model
[
12
]
can be extended to MIMO-CC setups, achieving an enhanced DoF of
Gt+L
with minimal subpacketization overhead but under strict divisibility constraints, where
G
is the number of receive antennas at each user.
More recently, in
[
13
,
14
,
15
]
,
we further refined the DoF bound and demonstrated that by flexibly adjusting parallel streams decoded by each user and the number of target users per transmission, denoted by
\beta
and
\Omega
, respectively, an enhanced DoF beyond that of
[
11
]
can be achieved.
Building on this result, in
[
15
]
we further extended the framework by introducing a new multicast scheduling scheme that improved practicality by ensuring linear decodability and bit-domain interference cancellation
[
2
]
, while also enhancing finite-SNR performance by allowing the parameter
\beta
to be flexibly selected from a set of feasible values denoted by
{\mathcal{B}}_{\Omega}
.
However, the structure of the predefined set
{\mathcal{B}}_{\Omega}
restricted the number of admissible stream allocations
\beta
for a given
\Omega
, since symmetry across the allocated streams had to be preserved.

This paper addresses the design gap identified in
[
15
]
by proposing a generalized MIMO-CC framework that extends both linear decodability analysis and delivery scheme design to enable a broader range of bit-level multicasting strategies.
First, we establish simple criteria that ensure linear decodability for arbitrary MIMO-CC schemes, allowing both symmetric and non-symmetric per-user stream allocations. We then propose a heuristic scheduling to allocate an asymmetric number of streams per user in each transmission, while adhering to linear decodability criteria.
Extensive simulations validate the enhancements enabled by the proposed solution, from both the DoF and finite-SNR rate perspectives.

System Model

We consider a MIMO setup where a single base station (BS) with

The file library

consists of

The coded caching gain is defined as

[

,
1
]

|{\mathcal{P}}|=t
. The subfile
W_{{\mathcal{P}}}
is stored in the cache memory of every user

.

At the beginning of the delivery phase, each user
k
reveals its requested file

to the BS. The BS constructs and transmits (e.g., in consecutive time slots) a set of

I
transmission vectors
{\mathbf{x}}(i)
, each delivering parts of the requested data to a subset
{\mathcal{K}}(i)
of target users, where

are defined by the delivery algorithm. Depending on the algorithm, we may also need to further split each packet

into a number of smaller subpackets

. The subpacket index

is used to distinguish different subpackets and to avoid the duplicate transmission of the same data, and is removed in ensuing notations for the sake of simplicity. In time slot

, the transmission vector

is constructed as

\begin{array}[]{l}{\mathbf{x}}(i)=\sum_{{\mathcal{T}}\in{\mathsf{S}}(i)}{\mathbf{w}}_{{\mathcal{T}}}X_{{\mathcal{T}}}\end{array}

(1)

where

represents the codeword generated for the user subset

denotes the bit-wise XOR operation),

is the set of codeword indices in time slot
i
,
and
{\mathbf{w}}_{{\mathcal{T}}}
is the multicast beamforming vector (transmit precoder) for

.
After the transmission of

, user

Let
\Theta
denote the final

T(i)=\nicefrac{{1}}{{\Theta R(i)}}

(file/second).

The reference symmetric scheduling
.
This paper extends the results of
[
15
]
on the linear decodability of MIMO-CC schemes and alters the delivery algorithm therein to cover asymmetric number of streams per user. Let
{\mathcal{K}}
be a subset of user with
|{\mathcal{K}}|=\Omega
, and use
{\mathsf{S}}^{{\mathcal{K}}}
to show the set of all the
t+1
-subsets of
{\mathcal{K}}
. Clearly, each user
k\in{\mathcal{K}}
appears in
\beta_{\mathrm{MC}}=\binom{\Omega-1}{t}
sets in
{\mathsf{S}}^{{\mathcal{K}}}
. One can build a transmission vector using all the codeword indices in
{\mathsf{S}}^{{\mathcal{K}}}
, i.e.,
{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathrm{MS}}=\sum_{{\mathcal{T}}\in{\mathsf{S}}^{{\mathcal{K}}}}X_{{\mathcal{T}}}{\mathbf{w}}_{{\mathcal{T}}}
, and multicast it to every user
k\in{\mathcal{K}}
. However, this may pose two challenges;
if
G<\beta_{\mathrm{MC}}
, computationally demanding successive interference cancellation (SIC) structure is needed for decoding, and if
G\gg\beta_{\mathrm{MC}}
, the solution is inefficient as users decode far fewer streams than possible.
To address these issues, the novel scheduling in
[
15
]

allows for flexibly selecting the number of parallel decoded streams per user, denoted by
\beta
, from a predefined feasible set

\begin{array}[]{l}{\mathcal{B}}_{\Omega}=\big\{\eta\hat{\beta}\mid\frac{\delta\hat{S}}{\eta}\in\mathbb{N},\eta\leq\min\big(\frac{L\hat{S}}{1+(\Omega-t-1)\hat{S}\hat{\beta}},\frac{G}{\hat{\beta}}\big)\big\},\end{array}

(2)

while ensuring linear decodability.
In (
2
),
\eta
and
\delta
are two general integers, and

\begin{array}[]{l}\hat{\beta}=\frac{t+1}{\mathrm{gcd}(t+1,\Omega)},\quad\hat{B}=\frac{\Omega}{\mathrm{gcd}(t+1,\Omega)},\quad\hat{S}=\frac{\binom{\Omega}{t+1}}{\hat{B}}.\end{array}

(3)

The underlying idea for this scheduling is to first partition
{\mathsf{S}}^{{\mathcal{K}}}
into
\hat{S}
base scheduling sets
\hat{{\mathsf{S}}}(\hat{i})
,
\hat{i}\in[\hat{S}]
, such that each user
k\in{\mathcal{K}}
appears in exactly
\hat{\beta}
sets in each
\hat{{\mathsf{S}}}(\hat{i})
(the existence of such partitioning is proven through hypergraph theory
[
15
]
). The base scheduling sets are then repeated
\delta
times (by increasing the subpacketization by the same factor of
\delta
), and re-grouped into
S=\nicefrac{{\delta\hat{S}}}{{\eta}}
scheduling sets
{\mathsf{S}}(i)
,
i\in[S]
, such that each user index
k\in{\mathcal{K}}
appears in exactly
\beta=\eta\hat{\beta}
sets in each
{\mathsf{S}}(i)
. The codewords corresponding to a
{\mathsf{S}}(i)
are sent in a dedicated interval (c.f
[
15
]
for more details and linear decodability proof).

While this scheduling provides a flexible, linear MIMO-CC solution,
the constraints imposed by
{\mathcal{B}}_{\Omega}
in (
2
) could enforce
gaps larger than one between feasible values of
\beta
. For example, when
L=11
,
G=8
, and
t=2
, the feasible set reduces to
\beta\in{\mathcal{B}}_{\Omega=4}=\{3,6\}
, thereby excluding
\beta\in\{4,5,7,8\}
as other possible feasible points. This discreteness significantly limits the feasible region, implying that the (locally) optimum symmetric rate obtained over
\beta\in{\mathcal{B}}_{\Omega}
may be far from the globally optimal solution over the full space of feasible schemes. To address this limitation, we extend the feasible set by relaxing
the symmetry constraint on the number of streams decoded per user.

and let

Theorem 1
.

With the transmission model in (
1
), linear decoding of
\beta_{k}(i)
streams is possible at each user

\theta_{\mathcal{T}}(i)
and

\begin{array}[]{l}{C_{1}:}\sum_{k^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{K}}(i)\backslash{\mathcal{T}}}\beta_{k^{\prime}}(i)+\theta_{{\mathcal{T}}}(i)\leq L,\>\;\forall{\mathcal{T}}\in{\mathsf{S}}(i),\\
{C_{2}:}\beta_{k}(i)\leq G,\>\>\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\qquad\qquad\forall k\in{\mathcal{K}}(i).\end{array}

(4)

Proof.

By definition, the beamformer
{\mathbf{w}}_{{\mathcal{T}}}

must null out the inter-stream interference caused by
X_{{\mathcal{T}}}
to every stream decoded by each user
k^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{K}}(i)\backslash{\mathcal{T}}
(as interference at users in
{\mathcal{T}}
is removed using cached content).
Let
{\mathbf{U}}_{k}(i)\in\mathbb{C}^{G\times\beta_{k}(i)}
denote the receive beamforming matrix used by user
k
to decode its
\beta_{k}(i)
parallel streams in interval
i
. Define the equivalent interference channel of
X_{{\mathcal{T}}}
as

\begin{array}[]{l}\bar{{\mathbf{H}}}_{{\mathcal{T}}}(i)=[{\mathbf{H}}_{k^{\prime}}(i)^{\mbox{\scriptsize H}}{\mathbf{U}}_{k^{\prime}}(i)]^{\mbox{\scriptsize H}},\quad\forall k^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{K}}(i)\backslash{\mathcal{T}},\end{array}

(5)

where
[\;\cdot\;]
denotes horizontal concatenation. To eliminate inter-stream interference,
{\mathbf{w}}_{{\mathcal{T}}}
must lie in the nullspace of
\bar{{\mathbf{H}}}_{{\mathcal{T}}}(i)
, i.e.,

{\mathbf{w}}_{{\mathcal{T}}}\in\mathrm{Null}(\bar{{\mathbf{H}}}_{{\mathcal{T}}}(i))
.

Now, to ensure that each user

can linearly decode all its
\beta_{k}(i)\leq G
parallel streams, it is essential that the respective transmit beamformers are linearly independent. This condition can be satisfied for parallel streams

associated with different multicast indices
{\mathcal{T}}
, since their corresponding beamformers are drawn from distinct null spaces.
However, for substreams sharing the same multicast stream index
{\mathcal{T}}
, linear independence is limited by the dimensions of
\mathrm{Null}(\bar{{\mathbf{H}}}_{{\mathcal{T}}}(i))

5

\bar{{\mathbf{H}}}_{{\mathcal{T}}}(i)

\Omega-t-1

, yielding
\bar{{\mathbf{H}}}_{{\mathcal{T}}}(i)\in\mathbb{C}^{\sum_{k^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{K}}(i)\backslash{\mathcal{T}}}\beta_{k^{\prime}}(i)\times L}.

Applying rank-nullity theorem
[
16
]
, we obtain

\mathrm{nullity}(\bar{{\mathbf{H}}}_{{\mathcal{T}}}(i))=L-\mathrm{rank}(\bar{{\mathbf{H}}}_{{\mathcal{T}}}(i))=L-\sum\nolimits_{k^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{K}}(i)\backslash{\mathcal{T}}}\beta_{k^{\prime}}(i).

Thus, the number of substreams sharing the same multicast index must satisfy

\begin{array}[]{l}\theta_{{\mathcal{T}}}(i)\leq L-\sum_{k^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{K}}(i)\backslash{\mathcal{T}}}\beta_{k}(i).\end{array}

(6)

This establishes
C_{1}
, while
C_{2}
follows from the Rx-antenna constraint
\beta_{k}(i)\leq G
, which together complete the proof.
∎

VI
Conclusion

This work extends the feasibility region of per-user stream allocation and linear decoding criteria in MIMO-CC networks to support both symmetric and asymmetric scenarios. The proposed scheduling expands the feasible set of achievable DoF values compared to baseline schemes, while adhering to linear decodability constraints.
