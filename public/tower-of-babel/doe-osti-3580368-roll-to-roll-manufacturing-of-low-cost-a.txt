 Executive Summary:

Arkema, in collaboration with Cabot Corporation, Saft, and Oak Ridge National Laboratory, has
developed waterborne (WB) cathode slurries compatible with advanced roll-to-roll (R2R)
manufacturing processes. This work addresses the urgent need to transition away from N-Methyl-
2-pyrrolidone (NMP) and adopt more sustainable, environmentally friendly battery production
methods. To achieve this, the team successfully synthesized a novel waterborne polyvinylidene
fluoride (WB-PVDF) for use in cathode use, novel waterborne styrene-acrylic-rubber (WB-SAR)
for use in anode, and engineered WB formulations that not only preserve but, in some cases,
enhance the electrochemical performance of cells produced via WB processes.

Moreover, the team developed an innovative, low-cost Composite In-Situ Separator (CIS)
composed of Al₂O₃ nanoparticles and WB binder(s). This CIS offers a promising alternative to
conventional polyolefin separators, which are a significant cost component and also pose supply
chain challenges in lithium-ion battery (LIB) manufacturing. Additional cost reductions and
process efficiencies were achieved through a one-step casting process that co-deposits CIS and
electrode slurries, followed by drying and calendaring to produce Integrated Electrode Separators
(IESs) for both cathode and anode sides.

The resulting LIB cells exhibit enhanced safety, higher energy density, substantially reduced
manufacturing costs, and a lower environmental footprint. The R2R-WB process relies on surface-
modified inorganic materials, conductive carbon, and WB-PVDF, eliminating the need for NMP
and enabling a more sustainable production pathway.

The project has demonstrated the viability of this approach across multiple chemistries—including
LCO, LFP, and NMC—without compromising performance compared to state-of-the-art solution-
cast electrodes. To do so, the team adopted a systematic approach, as detailed below according to
the Statement of Project Objectives.

Summary of Accomplished Tasks According to SOPO

Conductive Carbon
The team identified P-98® from Cabot and Super-P® from Imerys as suitable conductive carbons
for preparing waterborne (WB) slurries for both cathode and anode applications. These
commercially available carbons disperse readily in water under standard mixing conditions,
enabling the preparation of high-yield stress slurries suitable for roll-to-roll casting. Notably, the
excellent dispersion of carbon in aqueous slurries resulted in higher conductivity within WB-
electrodes compared to those made via solution-cast processes. This enhanced conductivity offers
a significant advantage in capacity retention over long cycle life, particularly under high C-rate
conditions, which are critical for electric vehicle (EV) applications.

Aluminum Oxide
Cabot successfully modified the surface of fumed alumina, resulting in a new fumed alumina
dispersion (FAD) with anionic surface charges instead of cationic. This modification improved
compatibility with both WB-PVDF and WB-acrylic binders. Additionally, a commercially
available nano-alumina dispersion was identified that performs well in the formulation. Both
alumina dispersions remained stable (no settling or gelation) after 2 hours. High-quality composite
in-situ separators were successfully cast on both anode and cathode substrates.
 The 1st patent application titled “Usage of Anionic Fumed Aluminum Oxides for
 Preparation of Aqueous Separator Slurry” — was filed describing the advantages of
 using anionically modified alumina in aqueous slurries.

WB-PVDF Synthesis
A novel WB-PVDF binder, named ARK2002, was successfully synthesized at lab scale and
subsequently scaled up to pilot plant production ahead of schedule. ARK2002 contains 27% solids
and demonstrates shear stability >30 minutes at 1000 rpm. The successful pilot-scale synthesis
confirms the feasibility of rapid commercial-scale production, supporting the goals of “USA
energy independence and technological superiority” strategy.

 The 2nd patent application titled “Shear Stable Aqueous Fluoropolymer Dispersion”—
 was filed describing the novel WB-PVDF binder.

Pure PVDF films made from ARK2002 were solid, flexible, and crack-free. To our knowledge,
this is the first time high-quality WB-electrodes of LFP, LCO, and NMC with adhesion >10 N/m,
as well as solid PVDF films, have been fabricated using a zero-VOC WB-PVDF binder without
additives or heated calendaring.

WB-Styrene-Acrylic (SAR) Binder Synthesis
The team designed and synthesized a unique WB-Styrene-Acrylic (SAR) binder tailored for anode
applications. The binder, named Incellion EL 1061, was successfully scaled up to a larger reactor.
It contains 40% solids and exhibits high shear stability >60 minutes at 1000 rpm.

NMC and LFP Testing and Modifications
Originally focused on WB-NMC622, the project scope was expanded to include WB-LFP, in
response to federal funding for LFP production sites in the U.S.

Cabot reported strong performance of fumed alumina-coated NMC622 after 2-hour water
exposure, followed by drying and testing. The coated material retained >90% of its rate capability
and capacity. However, the WB-slurry pH remained highly basic, posing a risk of aluminum foil
corrosion. A complementary approach involving cold water or weak acid washing, followed by
drying of NMC, successfully reduced slurry pH. Electrodes made with washed NMC showed
first-cycle efficiency and capacity comparable to solution-cast electrodes. Both FA-coated and
washed NMC electrodes demonstrated excellent rate capability and cycle life.

Various grades of commercially available LFP were exposed to water, with no significant pH
increase observed (pH remained <9), confirming LFP’s stability in WB formulations. WB-LFP
electrodes exhibited high first-cycle efficiency and long cycle life, comparable to solution-cast
counterparts.

WB-Anode Formulation Using SAR Binder
A set of WB-Styrene-Acrylic binders with unique functionalities was synthesized and scaled to
pilot production. These binders were used to prepare WB-anode slurries that were easy to cast and
suitable for high-energy anodes. The slurry contained >50% solids and ~96% graphite, with peel
strength ~100 N/m and volume resistivity <10 Ω·cm using only 4% binder. Electrode
specifications matched those of solution-cast electrodes. A coin cell assembled with WB-anode
and solution-cast NMC622 passed 1000 cycles without major degradation.

ALE assembled 18650 cylindrical cells (2 Ah capacity) using anodes with 97% graphite and 2%
WB-Styrene-Acrylic binder. Side-by-side testing against top-tier Japanese binders (from Zeon and
JSR) showed superior cycle-life performance for the project-developed binder, with ~90%
capacity retention after 1000 cycles.

WB-NMC Formulation Using WB-PVDF Binder
The formulation and process for WB high-energy NMC cathodes have been established. The
slurry is easy to cast and produces mirror-finish wet electrodes comparable to solution-cast ones.
The slurry contains >40% solids and ~94% NMC, with peel strength ~200 N/m and volume
resistivity <1 kΩ·cm using 3% binder.

Electrodes were successfully fabricated using NMC622 and NMC811 from different suppliers—
Umicore, Targray, and Ronbay. High-nickel slurries initially exhibited pH >12, risking aluminum
corrosion and H₂ generation. Using the washing protocol, the slurry pH was moderated.
Additionally, 1% acid addition reduced pH to 7–9, enabling high-quality electrode fabrication.

Washed NMC622 (Umicore) and NMC811 (Ronbay) combined with the novel WB-PVDF binder
yielded high-quality WB-NMC cathodes with >90% first-cycle efficiency and stable long cycle
life, comparable to solution-cast electrodes.

WB-LFP Formulation Using WB-PVDF Binder
The formulation and process for fabricating WB high-energy LFP cathodes have been successfully
demonstrated at the lab scale. The WB-LFP slurry presents viscosity challenges due to the high
surface area of LFP active materials. Despite this, slurries containing >40% solids and ~92% LFP
loading were prepared using 6% binder, resulting in peel strength of ~150 N/m and volume
resistivity <1 kΩ·cm.

The slurry was easy to cast and produced high-quality wet electrodes with a mirror-like finish,
comparable to those made via solution-cast processes. WB-LFP electrodes with ~20 mg/cm²
loading were fabricated and demonstrated >90% first-cycle efficiency and stable long cycle life,
confirming the viability of WB processing for LFP cathodes.

Pilot-Scale Production of Waterborne PVDF (WB-PVDF)
The novel waterborne PVDF binder, designated ARK2002, was successfully scaled up to an 80-
gallon reactor at the PB2 pilot facility. This milestone establishes a robust foundation for further
optimization in BP3. ARK2002 contains 27% solids and exhibits exceptional shear stability,
maintaining performance for over 30 minutes at 1000 rpm. This achievement significantly
strengthens confidence in the near-term feasibility of commercial-scale WB-PVDF production in
North America—in line with of “USA energy independence and technological superiority”
strategy.
Beyond binder applications, ARK2002 enabled the fabrication of a novel PVDF film with high
tensile strength, flexibility, and a crack-free morphology. To our knowledge, this is the first
successful production of high-quality PVDF films directly from WB-PVDF latex, without
additives or heated calendering. The process is zero-VOC, making it highly attractive for coatings,
impregnations, and freestanding film applications.

Scale-Up of Waterborne Styrene-Acrylic (SAR) Binder for Anodes

Expansion to LFP and Broader Active Material Compatibility
Building on the success with NMC, the scope was expanded to include lithium iron phosphate

Project Goals/Objectives:

The objective of this proposal is to develop and demonstrate, at pilot-scale, a novel low-cost all
water-based (WB) lithium-ion battery (LIB) fabrication process without compromising battery
performance. In addition, we will replace the current polyolefin Free Standing Separator (FSS)
with a novel Composite In-situ Separator (CIS) because it will facilitate a lower thickness
compared to 15-20µm and a higher porosity compared to 40-50% for a conventional FSS.

The thin, highly porous CIS will allow an increase in cell energy density from 600 Wh/L to 650
Wh/L, and an increase in power density from 1744 W/L to 1860 W/L. In addition, the LIB that is
made with CIS is expected to exhibit improved safety aspects because CIS will have a minimal
shrinkage at elevated temperatures.

The proposed all-WB manufacturing will reduce the cost of LIBs by $15.5-$21/kWh compared to
conventional solvent-based manufacturing processes which needs an FSS.

In addition, the proposed fabrication should increase battery cycle life compared to state-of-the-
art high-energy cells, will be retrofittable onto conventional manufacturing lines, and will be
compatible with lower cost LIB cathode materials as they become available.

Other specific objectives:
 • Based on the Argonne National Lab (ANL) 2019 battery manufacturing cost model, the
 cost of producing cells will be reduced to $97.5/(usable kWh) with a stretch goal of
 $92/(usable kWh). The manufacturing cost calculation does not cover cost reduction
 caused by eliminating waste and handling of hazardous materials.
 • Increase of useable specific energy to >250 Wh/kg at C/3.
 • A projected calendar life of 15 years based on accelerated testing and a demonstrated cycle
 life of 1000 cycles with <20% capacity fade.
 • Cost saving from simplification in stacking separators and electrodes, and reduced scrap
 rate from existing 4% down to 1% or lower.
 • Quantification of improvements in safety and performance by having 0% CIS shrinkage at
 150°C for 1 hr. Abuse tests will used to demonstrate that flammability due to separator
 failure is eliminated in the cells with a CIS.
 • Validate high voltage stability of CIS at 4.5V vs. freestanding separator (FSS).
 • Build 20 x 2 Ah cells using a high-energy design that will meet the described electric
 vehicle (EV) cost and performance targets.

1. Background:

A shear-stable water-based (WB) PVDF emulsion was recently developed that enables two
important LIB manufacturing innovations: 1) casting of electrodes which contain PVDF binders
from water-based slurries, eliminating the need for NMP to be used as a solvent for cathode casting
and the requirement for expensive solvent recovery systems and 2) the ability to make Composite
In-situ Separator (CIS) onto the electrode, which eliminates the need for an expensive polyolefin
free-standing-separator (FSS). In addition, a new protective coating for NMC cathode materials is
under development that not only prevents deterioration of high Ni active materials in an aqueous
formulation but also allows a higher voltage operation to deliver higher energy density. Therefore,
the use of WB technology will: a) create substantial cost savings by eliminating the use of NMP
for cathode fabrication and an FSS for the separator; b) increase power and energy density through
the use of a significantly thinner, more porous in-situ separator; c) improve manufacturing safety
through the elimination of NMP; and d) improve in-use safety by reducing separator shrinkage at
elevated temperatures. It is noteworthy that the CIS will not only simultaneously improve battery
energy and power densities but also will improve LIB safety by not shrinking at elevated
temperature and not contributing to thermal runaway because is made from non-shrinkable and
non-combustible materials.

Critical challenges for the success of the project include:
 • Synthesizing WB-PVDF binders with the proper functionality and surface energy
 • Modifying carbon and Al2O3 surfaces to become compatible with WB-PVDF binder, so
 that well-dispersed and stable slurries can be made in water
 • Modifying the surface of high-nickel active materials to make them water-resistant,
 compatible with WB-PVDF binder, and stable in the waterborne slurries
 • Producing high precision, 4-6 micron (dry) in-situ separator on each electrode, having high
 porosity, hence providing a high ion transport to improve power density
 • Demonstrating CIS can withstand high voltage cycles (i.e., 4.45 V) without deterioration
 • Casting thicker cathode electrodes with WB slurries to increase energy density
 • Scaling up and validating lower cost WB manufacturing processes on the existing
 manufacturing equipment

 Progress and Status:
Task 1.1.a (subset of 1.1) milestone is complete. Screening of commercially available conductive
carbons was successfully completed. We identified the best two commercial conductive carbons
that are readily dispersible in water and have affinity for WB-PVDF; namely EP-98® from Cabot,
Super-P® from Imerys. Both apparently have positive interaction with WB-PVDF binder and
resulted in a high conductivity within the WB-cathode. During the WB-anode fabrication (task
2.2.a) a series of commercial conductive carbons also were tested, and Super-P performed the best
in the WB-high-energy anodes.
Task 1.1.b (subtask of 1.1) milestone is complete. This subtask requires modification of fumed
alumina surface to be anionically charged and stable. A new fumed alumina dispersion (FAD) was
received from Cabot. They reported that the viscosity of the 1:1 mixture of FAD-04 and waterborne
PVDF binder (ARK2002) has been stable. The results show no obvious change in the viscosity
over 7 weeks, indicating high compatibility between FAD-04 and ARK2002 and high stability of
the dispersion in water.
Zeta potential measurements confirm the anionic surface charged of the modified FAD-04, as
shown in Figure 1. FAD-04 exhibits a Zeta potential of -60mV at pH 9.5. Unmodified FA has a
small magnitude of negative charges (-20mV) at pH 9.5, which would result in an unstable
dispersion. Considering the pH studies for the NMC and LCO cathodes so far, pH value of 9.5 is
in the suitable range for the CIS layer on the cathode, which is close to the pH value of WB-
cathode slurry.

 One important property of CIS samples to consider is their flexibility. In R2R processes, the
electrodes undergo various curvatures. In cylindrical cells, the initial windup can be as sharp as

assembly process. To evaluate the flexibility of CIS, strips of different anode+CIS combinations

Improved Water-Based (WB) Carbon Dispersion

Historically, it was assumed that grinding carbon at solids concentrations above 10% was
impractical due to excessive viscosity, which hindered processability. However, our team
successfully challenged this limitation by developing a novel high-solids WB carbon dispersion
containing 16.7% solids and a reduced concentration of polyacrylic acid (PAA) as the dispersant.

Through careful formulation and process optimization, we were able to closely match the viscosity
profile of this high-solids dispersion to that of conventional low-solids systems, ensuring
compatibility with standard casting processes. This achievement is illustrated in the viscosity
comparison plot shown in Figure 45, which demonstrates the comparable rheological behavior of
the new formulation.

This advancement not only enables more efficient electrode manufacturing by reducing drying

Key Improvements: The team continued testing and refining the dual-layer coating process using
the R2R coater. A major breakthrough was achieved by filtering the CIS slurry prior to coating.
This step eliminated striping defects and significantly reduced particle-related imperfections,
although some particulate issues persisted (see Figures 48 and 49). The CIS slurry was filtered
externally through a 150 µm mesh using a pressure pot at ~10 PSI, and also filtered in-line with a
mesh of the same size. The anode slurry underwent similar external filtration but lacked in-line
filtering at the time.

Additionally, a thicker 20 µm copper foil -double the thickness of the previously used 10 µm foil
-was introduced, resolving issues with foil buckling and wrinkling during drying.

A test involving a double-sided, single-layer anode coating was also conducted to evaluate
potential challenges. No major issues were observed, aside from the need for precise alignment
between the two coated sides.

Addressing Surface Defects: Despite improvements, surface defects caused by particulate
remained a concern. The root cause is still under investigation, but one hypothesis is that unfiltered
dry particles may be introduced during anode slurry preparation. To mitigate this, both slurries
will be filtered in-line using finer meshes once the second filter is installed. Other potential
solutions were tested including: -adjusting slot die configurations, -modifying slurry viscosities; -
introducing extra surfactants. Neither approach led to a meaningful reduction in surface defects
or a noticeable improvement in overall coating quality.

CMC Coating Trials: A series of trials using carboxymethyl cellulose (CMC) were conducted to
determine whether the defects were due to layer-layer intermixing or intrinsic instability in the
casting die. Both single-layer CMC and dual-layer anode/CMC coatings were tested.
Unfortunately, both exhibited similar defects to those seen in previous trials, including the CMC-
only coating. This indicated that either slurry quality (particles, bubbles, …) or substrate
contamination cause those defects not the dual-slot casting die.

Optimized trial: A final trial was conducted using all previously identified improvements such
as: - an in-line filter (150 mesh) was added to the anode slurry line at the die inlet, -the CIS in-line
filter was also set to 150 mesh at the die inlet, -CIS slurry solids content was reduced from 49% to
46% to reduce viscosity, hence reduce intermixing of anode and CIS layer at die lip.

The initial attempt failed due to rapid clogging of the anode filter. The mesh was then adjusted to
100 for the anode, while maintaining 150 for CIS. This configuration produced a dual-layer coating
of acceptable quality. However, since multiple variables were changed simultaneously, it remains
unclear which specific change(s) led to the improvement. Further testing will be needed to isolate
the contributing factors. While the overall coating quality has improved significantly, some minor
defects and texture inconsistencies remain.

Cathode + CIS Trial at Pilot Scale Casting Line
During the casting of cathode an issue was noted relatively early on, where the cathode texture
would be very inconsistent and large lines/chevrons would become apparent on the film, especially
after calendaring. The cause of this was eventually determined to be due to destabilization of the
slurry during the degassing procedure on the Thinky, which essentially centrifuges the slurry to
force bubbles out of the slurry. This had been done at 2200 RPM, and was evidently too much for
the slurry. Removal of the Thinky degassing step has completely eliminated this defect.

After the elimination of this defect, the single layer cathode was determined to be of good quality
and so the next step was to add the CIS layer. Initial coatings yielded some issues, notably where
the CIS layer would clog at the die exit. As such, the body shim for the CIS die was increased from
5 mil to 7 mil and ultimately 8 mil. The shim for the cathode die was also increased to 7 mil.

Additionally, it was found that the line needed to be ran at a minimum of 15 cm/min to avoid
material accumulation at the die exit. It was believed that this was due to some of the fluid flowing
back onto the die exit due to the thickness of the coated wet layer and relatively low viscosity of
the slurries.
