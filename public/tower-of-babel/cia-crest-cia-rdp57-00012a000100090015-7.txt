Sixth Annual Arthur S. Flemming Awards Program for the outstanding young men in
the Federal Service for 1953 . '

PURPOSE OF THE ARTHUR S. FLEMMING AWARDS PROGRAM

The search for the outstanding young men in the Federal Service has been made each
year by the Junior Chamber of Commerce for the past five years. The purpose of this
Program is threefold: (1) to grant recognition to young men in the Federal Service
who have performed outstanding and unusi al work, ( 2 ) to publicize the accomplishments
of these people and thereby attract other outstanding young men to the Federal Service,
(3) to promote confidence among all peoples in our form of Government and the
opportunities and responsibilities that it presents.

The United States Civil Service Comn ission and the Federal Personnel Council have
enthusiastically supported and assisted the Junior Chamber of Commerce in the further-
ance of this Program.

ELIGIBILITY OF NOMINEES

Any civilian, male officer or employee of the Executive Branch of the United States
Government who will not reach his fortieih birthday before January 1, 1954 is eligible
for nomination in the Arthur S. Flemming Awards Program. In addition, the Program
will cover employees of the Government of the District of Columbia, the Library of
Congress, the General Accounting Office, the Government Printing Office, and the Office
of the Architect of the Capitol. Nominaticns will be received from officers or employees
in both the departmental and field services throughout the world.

BASIS OF JUDGMENT

Anyone who meets the above stated criter. a may be nominated when it can be shown
that the nominee has performed unusual or outstanding work during the Federal Govern-
ment Fiscal Year 1953, in accordance with the following standards which will be
considered by the panel of judges in makirg selections:

a. A specific accomplishment for waich the nominee is primarily responsible,
resulting in a material improvement in service, a substantial financial savings to the
Government or significant social or technilogical progress.

b. Outstanding executive, scientific oi technical ability or performance which has
been displayed by the nominee during the year, but which is not necessarily connected
with or related to a specific accomplishment or project.

Under the fust standard, consideration should be given to significant accomplish-
ments by employees during the current year and persons should be nominated who are
directly responsible for these significant accomplishments. Under this standard, one or
more nominees may be submitted if such nominees were equally responsible for the
project or accomplishment.

Under the second standard, individuals should be nominated who might not neces-
sarily have been responsible for important projects of major significance. This standard
rather applies to individuals who have demonstrated outstanding executive, scientific or
technical ability or performance. In either case, there must be submitted a specific
justification, citing examples and details in support of the nominations, ’this justification
should be limited to facts in so far as is porsible rather than opinions or conclusions on
the part of the one making the presentation. Justification should be limited to three

DEPARTMENT OR AGENCY ACTION

Any separate department or agency of the Executive Branch may submit as many

nominations as they desire for this Program. For the purpose of this Program, each of

the Military Departments of the Department of Defense and the Office of the Secretary
of Defense are to be considered as separate agencies.

Announcements of this Program will be sent to the heads of departments and
agencies of the Federal Government. Implementation of this announcement to depart-
mental and field activities in each department or agency should be the responsibility of
each respective department or agency. ' '

All nominations must be received by the Junior Chamber of Commerce not later

than September 18, 1953.

All nominations must be submitted by and have the approval of the head of the
department or agency concerned.

GENERAL INFORMATION

Separate awards will be made to the outstanding young man in the scientific or technical
fields and the outstanding young man in the administrative or executive fields. Each of
these winners will receive an award consisting of a plaque and a gold distinguished
service award key. In addition, three Distinguished Service Awards will be presented to
other nominees for outstanding accomplishments in each of these fields.

The awards winners will be chosen by a panel of distinguished judges.

The awards will be presented at the Arthur S. Flemming Awards Dinner to be held
in Washington in late October, 1953.
