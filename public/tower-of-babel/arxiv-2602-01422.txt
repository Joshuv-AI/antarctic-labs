PCIe400 generic readout board qualification test
G. Vouters

Abstract
The PCIe400 is a generic board for high-throughput data acquisition system s in high energy physics experiments. Its purpose is to interface up to 48 bidirectional links, supporting custom protocols at 1 to 26 Gbit / s \mathrm{Gbit}\text{/}\mathrm{s} , to modern commercial back-end links providing 400 Gbit / s \mathrm{Gbit}\text{/}\mathrm{s} bandwidth. It also targets clock distribution with phase determinism below 10 ps peak-to-peak. It has been designed for LHCb LS3 enhancement upgrade with experimental features to prepare LHCb Upgrade II, foreseeing an aggregated throughput of 200 Tbit / s \mathrm{Tbit}\text{/}\mathrm{s} . However, its versatility allows it to be used in several experimental environments. The board embeds Altera’s flagship Agilex 7 M-series FPGA with a PCIe Gen 5 interface and an experimental QSFP112 serial interface. We present the results of qualification tests performed on prototype boards and the challenges encountered to meet specifications. Section 1 describes board-level validation, including power-up behavior and peripheral access. Section 2 focuses on high-bandwidth interface qualification through BER measurements. Finally, Section 3 investigates phase determinism in Agilex transceivers, a key requirement for precise clock distribution.

1.1
Powering the board

The board features 23 power rails, with strict requirements on voltage accuracy and ripple noise within
\pm 0.5\%
. Using a custom-made coaxial probe coupled with a DC-blocking capacitor
[
4
]

\mathrm{W}

During PCIe enumeration, the board must power-up within 100
\mathrm{ms}

\mathrm{ms}

The power-up time initially exceeded specifications, while the FPGA started in 7.5
\mathrm{ms}

\mathrm{ms}

\mathrm{ms}

2.2
4x100 Gbit/s interface

The Agilex F-Tile FHT transceiver supports 116
\mathrm{Gbit}\text{/}\mathrm{s}
PAM4 per lane and includes a 400GbE hard IP enabling the integration of a high-bandwidth network interface on-board.
The main challenge lies in the hardware link between the FPGA and the QSFP112 connector, separated by approximately 50
\mathrm{mm}

[
10
]

4

:

1\cdot 10^{-15}

3
Phase determinism

According to the datasheet, Agilex FGT transceivers exhibit a non-deterministic lane-to-lane output skew of up to
\pm~2
unit interval (UI) + 300
\mathrm{ps}

[
11
]
. This translates to jumps of 500
\mathrm{ps}
at 10
\mathrm{Gbit}\text{/}\mathrm{s}
and prevents a straightforward solution to distribute phase-deterministic clocks to the front-end.
Tests were intentionally performed on an Agilex DK-SI-AGI027FA development kit in order to isolate the intrinsic phase behavior of the transceiver from board-level effects.
[
12
]

3.1
Principle and set-up

The design implements four channels running at 10.24
\mathrm{Gbit}\text{/}\mathrm{s}
and a 32b fixed pattern. The phase is measured internally using a 1
\mathrm{ps}
resolution DDMTD
[
13
]
as described in Figure
5
.

Figure 5
:
Test setup implemented on the development kit to study transceiver phase behavior. Four serial channels operate at 10.24 Gbit/s, with phase differences measured internally using DDMTD.

The tx_clkout signal, corresponding to clock generated by the transceiver logic, can not be used to measure the phase of the output serial stream due to non-deterministic internal alignment.
Instead, the transceiver operates in serial-loopback and the
recovered clock
(rx_clkout) measures the
image of the transmitted signal
. Figure
6

illustrates the receiver determinism test: after intentionally shifting the transmitted data pattern by one bit and repeating the receiver synchronization, the phase of the recovered clock is displaced by exactly one unit interval, as expected.

Figure 6
:
Measured phase shift of the recovered clock
rx\_clkout
following a one-bit shift of the transmitted data stream and repeated receiver synchronization. The observed unit-interval phase steps confirm deterministic receiver behavior after synchronization.

3.2
Tx reset stability

The Figure
7
(left) shows that successive transmitter resets produce quantized phase shift of
\pm 3\,\mathrm{UI}\allowbreak~(\approx 300~\mathrm{ps})
. With increased statistics,
a bimodal distribution appears
around each bit position, separated by
\approx 20~$\mathrm{ps}$
. A test with 500 Rx synchronizations and no Tx reset
reveals
that this double-peak pattern persists, as in Figure
7
(right).
It indicates that the phase uncertainty results from the receiver path and likely from the Clock Data Recovery circuit locking on two stable positions. Thus, it does not impact the transmitter serial stream but only the phase monitoring system. This can be mitigated by the selecting the most probable position.

Figure 7
:
(left)
Quantized phase shifts of the recovered clock observed after successive transmitter resets, showing jumps limited to a few unit intervals.
(right)
Phase distribution obtained from 500 receiver synchronizations without transmitter reset, revealing two stable locking positions of the receiver clock recovery.

Finally, we assess the inter-reset stability of the transmitter serial stream on four channels. The
bimodal
distribution is mitigated by defining a
\pm 10~$\mathrm{ps}$
filter window around the most probable phase position. The result, in Figure
8
, shows a stability of 6 to 8
\mathrm{ps}
peak-to-peak over four channels after 500 resets during 12 hours
demonstrating sub-10 ps stability across channels.

Figure 8
:
Phase stability of the recovered clock measured over four serial channels after 500 transceiver resets. The distributions show a peak-to-peak phase variation of 6–8 ps.

4
Summary

In the process of qualifying the generic readout board PCIe400, we developed a versatile software framework that enables efficient testing of complex boards from debugging to production.
Extensive
simulation work during design allowed
the implementation of errorless
4x100
\mathrm{Gbit}\text{/}\mathrm{s}
PAM4 links and PCIe interface. A phase
-
determinism proof of concept on four channels show
s
that Agilex FGT transceivers are compatible with a 10
\mathrm{ps}
peak-to-peak phase determinism precision.

Additional tests are underway to qualify all PCIe400 features. In particular, further studies on clock distribution with phase determinism are required to evaluate stability
across
gateware compilations, temperature variations and at a system level.
