On the Complexity of Computing Outputs of a Metric Turing Machine
Yaroslav Ivanashev

Abstract
The classes 𝖬𝗂𝖽𝖯 \mathsf{MidP} , 𝖬𝖾𝖽𝖯 \mathsf{MedP} , and 𝖬𝖾𝖽𝖯 ¯ \mathsf{\overline{MedP}} contain functions that compute the median solution for certain types of problems. In this paper, for these classes we introduce analogous classes of functions that compute the k k -th solution, where k k is an order function that depends on the input. We prove that the classes 𝖬𝗂𝖽𝖯 \mathsf{MidP} , 𝖬𝖾𝖽𝖯 \mathsf{MedP} , and 𝖬𝖾𝖽𝖯 ¯ \mathsf{\overline{MedP}} are polynomial-time 1-Turing inter-reducible with the corresponding classes, where the order function is from 𝖥𝖯 \mathsf{FP} or 𝖥𝖯 # ​ 𝖯 \mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\#P}} . For 𝖬𝖾𝖽𝖯 \mathsf{MedP} we also prove that it coincides with the corresponding classes, where the order function is from 𝖥𝖯 \mathsf{FP} or # ​ 𝖯 \mathsf{\#P} . For several inclusions between function classes we give equivalent inclusions between language classes. In particular, we establish inclusion relations between 𝖬𝖺𝗑𝖯 \mathsf{MaxP} and median classes 𝖬𝗂𝖽𝖯 \mathsf{MidP} , 𝖬𝖾𝖽𝖯 \mathsf{MedP} , and 𝖬𝖾𝖽𝖯 ¯ \mathsf{\overline{MedP}} . We also prove that 𝖭𝖯𝖲𝖵 𝗍 ⊆ 𝖬𝖺𝗑𝖯 ⊆ 𝖥𝖯 𝖭𝖯 \mathsf{NPSV_{t}}\subseteq\mathsf{MaxP}\subseteq\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{NP}} and both inclusions are proper if and only if 𝖭𝖯 ≠ 𝖼𝗈𝖭𝖯 \mathsf{NP}\neq\mathsf{coNP} .



1
Introduction

Krentel introduced the class
\mathsf{OptP}

[
6
]
that consists of functions that are equal to the maximum or minimum output of a nondeterministic polynomial-time bounded Turing Machine (NPTM) that outputs an integer on every computation path (a NPTM with this property is called metric). He also proved that
\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{OptP}[1]}=\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{NP}}
and studied complete problems for
\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{NP}}
under polynomial-time 1-Turing reductions. Toda defined a class
\mathsf{MidP}
that consists of functions that are equal to the median output of a metric NPTM
[
9
]
, proved that
\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{MidP}[1]}=\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\#P}}
, and studied
complete problems for
\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\#P}}
under polynomial-time 1-Turing reductions.
Vollmer and Wagner defined two related median classes:
\mathsf{MedP}
is defined as
\mathsf{MidP}
except that the multiplicities of outputs are taken into account, and
\mathsf{\overline{MedP}}
consists of functions that are equal to the median witness of an NP problem
[
13
]
. They also showed that these classes are polynomial-time 1-Turing inter-reducible:
\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{MidP}[1]}=\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{MedP}[1]}=\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\overline{MedP}}[1]}=\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\#P}}
.
In
Section

3
, we give a simplified proof of this result.
Vollmer in
[
12
]
studied complete problems under parsimonious reductions for the classes
\mathsf{MidP}
,
\mathsf{MedP}
, and
\mathsf{\overline{MedP}}
.

In this paper, for the median classes
\mathsf{MidP}
,
\mathsf{MedP}
, and
\mathsf{\overline{MedP}}
we define their analogs
\mathsf{KthOutput}(C)
,
\mathsf{KthOutput_{seq}}(C)
, and
\mathsf{KthWitness}(C)
, which consist of functions that are equal to the
k
-th output or witness (instead of median output or witness) for a function
k\in C
. Toda used a class
\mathsf{KthP}

[
9
]
, which is similar to
\mathsf{KthOutput}(\mathsf{FP})
but contains partial functions that take two arguments. We use classes of total functions of one argument in order to compare them with
\mathsf{MidP}
,
\mathsf{MedP}
, and
\mathsf{\overline{MedP}}
. In fact,
\mathsf{KthP}
would be a subset of
\mathsf{KthOutput}(\mathsf{FP})
if it contained only total functions, so
\mathsf{KthOutput}(\mathsf{FP})
can be considered as a generalization of
\mathsf{KthP}
that admits different order functions
k\in\mathsf{FP}
. For example, the problem KthSAT from
[
9
]
belongs to the classes
\mathsf{KthOutput}(\mathsf{FP})
and
\mathsf{KthWitness}(\mathsf{FP})
(the assignments of formulas should be encoded as integers).
In
Section

4
, for the classes
\mathsf{\overline{MedP}}
,
\mathsf{MidP}
, and
\mathsf{MedP}
we show that they are polynomial-time 1-Turing inter-reducible with their corresponding classes, where the function
k
is either in
\mathsf{FP}
or
\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\#P}}
:
\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\#P}}=\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\overline{MedP}}[1]}=\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{KthWitness}(\mathsf{FP})[1]}=\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{KthWitness}(\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\#P}})[1]}=\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{MidP}[1]}=\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{KthOutput}(\mathsf{FP})[1]}=\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{KthOutput}(\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\#P}})[1]}=\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{MedP}[1]}=\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{KthOutput_{seq}}(\mathsf{FP})[1]}\\
=\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{KthOutput_{seq}}(\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\#P}})[1]}
. In
Section

5
, for the class
\mathsf{MedP}
we prove that it coincides with the corresponding classes
\mathsf{KthOutput_{seq}}(\mathsf{FP})
and
\mathsf{KthOutput_{seq}}(\mathsf{\#P})
.

In
Section

6
, for several inclusions between function classes we give equivalent inclusion between language classes. In
[
13
]
, Vollmer and Wagner proved that
\mathsf{\overline{MedP}}\subseteq\mathsf{MidP}\cap\mathsf{MedP}
and the other inclusions between these classes have implausible implications. For some of these implications we show that they hold in the opposite direction. We prove that
\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{coNP}}\subseteq\mathsf{MaxP}\subseteq\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{NP}}
and both inclusions are proper if and only if
\mathsf{NP}\neq\mathsf{coNP}
. We also prove that
\mathsf{MaxP}
contains the classes
\mathsf{MidP}
,
\mathsf{MedP}
, and
\mathsf{\overline{MedP}}
if and only if
\mathsf{PP}=\mathsf{NP}
, and
\mathsf{\overline{MedP}}
contains the classes
\mathsf{SpanP}
and
\mathsf{MaxP}
if and only if
\mathsf{NP}\subseteq\mathsf{SPP}
.

In this paper, we use a standard definition of a nondeterministic polynomial-time bounded Turing machine (NPTM) and binary alphabet
\Sigma=\{0,1\}

\mathsf{NP}
,
\mathsf{PP}
,
\mathsf{SPP}

[

]

\mathsf{SpanP}

[

Definition 2.1
(
[
6
]
)

A NPTM is called metric if on every branch it outputs a binary number from
\mathbb{Z}
and accepts.

In the definition above, the outputs of a NPTM can be negative numbers (in some reasonable encoding).

Krentel defined the class
\mathsf{OptP}

[
6
]
as a union of the classes
\mathsf{MinP}
and
\mathsf{MaxP}
that we define below.

Definition 2.2

[
6
]

\mathsf{MaxP}=\{max_{M}\ |\ M
is a metric NPTM
\}
, where
max_{M}(x)
is the maximum value in a set of all outputs of
M
on input
x\in\Sigma^{*}
. More precisely, if
\{w_{1},\dots,w_{k}\}
is the set of all outputs of
M
on input
x
and if
w_{1}<\ldots<w_{k}
, then
max_{M}(x)=w_{k}
.

The class
\mathsf{MinP}
is defined analogously. In this paper, we will use only the class
\mathsf{MaxP}
. All results that we prove for this class also hold for the class
\mathsf{MinP}
.

Below we give definitions of median classes that we will consider in this paper.

Definition 2.3

1.

[
9
]

\mathsf{MidP}=\{mid_{M}\ |\ M
is a metric NPTM
\}
, where
mid_{M}(x)
is the median value in a set of all outputs of
M
on input
x\in\Sigma^{*}
. More precisely, if
\{w_{1},\dots,w_{k}\}
is the set of all outputs of
M
on input
x
and if
w_{1}<\ldots<w_{k}
, then
mid_{M}(x)=w_{\lfloor(k+1)/2\rfloor}
.

2.

[
13
]

\mathsf{MedP}=\{med_{M}\ |\ M
is a metric NPTM
\}
, where
med_{M}(x)
is the median value in a sequence of all outputs of
M
on input
x\in\Sigma^{*}
. More precisely, if
w_{1}\leqslant\ldots\leqslant w_{k}
is the sequence of all outputs (with multiplicities) of
M
on input
x
, then
med_{M}(x)=w_{\lfloor(k+1)/2\rfloor}
.

3.

[
13
]
The class
\mathsf{\overline{MedP}}
consists of all functions
f
for which there exist a language
A\in\mathsf{P}
and functions
l,r\in\mathsf{FP}
(
\Sigma^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
) such that for every
x\in\Sigma^{*}
:

\displaystyle f(x)=\begin{cases}y_{\lfloor(k+1)/2\rfloor},&\text{if the set $W$ is not empty,}\\
l(x)-1,&\text{$otherwise$,}\end{cases}

where
W=\{y\in\mathbb{Z}\ |\ l(x)\leqslant y\leqslant r(x)\wedge(x,y)\in A\}=\{y_{1},\dots,y_{k}\}
and
y_{1}<\ldots<y_{k}
.

In
[
13
]
, Vollmer and Wagner proved that
\mathsf{\overline{MedP}}
is a subset of
\mathsf{MidP}
and
\mathsf{MedP}
.

Theorem

6.3
shows that the other inclusions between these classes are not likely to hold.

Proposition 2.4
(

]
)

\mathsf{\overline{MedP}}\subseteq\mathsf{MidP}\cap\mathsf{MedP}
.

For the median classes above we define their analogs, where the functions compute outputs or witnesses of some specific order.

Definition 2.5

Let
C
be a class of functions
\Sigma^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
.

1.

The class
\mathsf{KthOutput}(C)

where

is the set of all outputs of

on input

, and

.

2.

The class

\displaystyle f(x)=\begin{cases}w_{g(x)},&\text{if $M$ has at least $g(x)$ paths on input $x$,}\\

w_{1}\leqslant\ldots\leqslant w_{k}

, and

The class

for which there exist a language

, functions

(
\Sigma^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
), and a function

3
Characterization of
\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\#P}}

In
[
9
]
, Toda gave a characterization of the class
\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\#P}}
using
\mathsf{MidP}
functions:
\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\#P}}=\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{MidP}[1]}
. Vollmer and Wagner used the same technique to show that the class
\mathsf{\overline{MedP}}
can be used in this characterization
[
13
]
. We give a simplified version of this proof below.

Theorem 3.1
(
[
13
]
)

\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\#P}}=\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\overline{MedP}}[1]}
.

Proof

1.

\mathsf{\overline{MedP}}\subseteq\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\#P}}
: the middle witness can be found by binary search on the set of witnesses.

2.

\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\#P}}\subseteq\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\overline{MedP}}[1]}
: It suffices to prove
\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{PP}}\subseteq\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\overline{MedP}}[1]}
, because
\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\#P}}=\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{PP}}
. Let
M
be a polynomial-time bounded Turing machine with an oracle
L\in\mathsf{PP}
, and
N
be a NPTM that witnesses
L
and satisfies the conditions of
Lemma

3.2
. The machine
M
can be modified so that on every input of length
n
it has only queries of length
q(n)
and has exactly
p(n)
queries, where
p
and
q
are polynomials. In this proof, we will use binary strings of fixed polynomial length to represent integer witnesses from the definition of
\mathsf{\overline{MedP}}
. This set of witnesses for every
x\in\Sigma^{*}
contains strings of the form
a_{1}q_{1}w_{1}\ldots a_{p(|x|)}q_{p(|x|)}w_{p(|x|)}
, where for every
i
,
a_{i}
is an oracle answer to the
i
-th query
q_{i}
of
M
on input
x
and
w_{i}
is a path of
N
on input
q_{i}
. For every
i
the
i
-th query
q_{i}
is determined by the previous answers
a_{1},\ldots,a_{i-1}
, which can be different from the correct answers. All paths
w_{i}
should be encoded so that they had the same polynomial length. Moreover, this set contains only strings of this form, where for every
i
,
a_{i}=1
if and only if
w_{i}
is an accepting path. The middle witness from this set contains the correct answer
a_{1}
, because the machine
N
has more accepting paths than rejecting paths on the input
q_{1}
if and only if the correct answer is 1. The second answer
a_{2}
in the middle witness is also correct, because the second query
q_{2}
is determined correctly in the middle witness, and every prefix
a_{1}q_{1}w_{1}
can be extended to the same number of prefixes
a_{1}q_{1}w_{1}a_{2}q_{2}w_{2}
. By the same argument, the middle witness contains all correct oracle answers.
∎

Lemma 3.2

For every language
L\in\mathsf{PP}
there exist a NPTM
M
and a function
f\in\mathsf{FP}
such that for every
x\in\Sigma^{*}
:

1.

x\in L\Leftrightarrow

M
has more accepting paths than rejecting paths on input
x
.

2.

The number of paths of
M
on input
x
is odd and depends only on the length of
x
.

3.

f(x)
equals the number of paths of
M
on input
x
.

Proof

Let
L\in\mathsf{PP}
. By definition of
\mathsf{PP}
, there exists a NPTM that satisfies the first condition, and it is well known that this machine can be modified so that on every input all paths have the same number of nondeterministic steps. After that one rejecting path should be added to make the number of paths odd. ∎

In
[
9
]
, Toda proved that
\mathsf{MidP}\subseteq\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\#P}}
, which follows from the main theorem of
[
10
]
. This gives the following corollary:

Corollary 3.3
(
[
9
,
13
]
)

\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\#P}}=\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\overline{MedP}}[1]}=\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{MidP}[1]}=\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{MedP}[1]}
.

4
Computing the K
{}^{\textnormal{{th}}}
Witness and the K
{}^{\textnormal{{th}}}
Value in a Set of Outputs

In this section, we prove that the classes
\mathsf{\overline{MedP}}
,
\mathsf{MidP}
, and
\mathsf{MedP}
are polynomial-time 1-Turing inter-reducible with the corresponding classes, where the order function is either in
\mathsf{FP}
or
\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\#P}}

Lemma 4.1

\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\overline{MedP}}[1]}=\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{KthWitness}(\mathsf{FP})[1]}=\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{KthWitness}(\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\#P}})[1]}
.

Proof

It suffices to prove that
\mathsf{KthWitness}(\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\#P}})\subseteq\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\#P}}
and
\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\#P}}\subseteq

\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{KthWitness}(\mathsf{FP})[1]}
, because
\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\#P}}=\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\overline{MedP}}[1]}
by
Theorem

3.1
.

1.

\mathsf{KthWitness}(\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\#P}})\subseteq\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\#P}}
: as in the first part of
Theorem

3.1
, the
g(x)
-th witness for a function
g\in\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\#P}}
can be found by binary search. To compute the value of
g(x)
another
\mathsf{\#P}
oracle is needed, but one
\mathsf{\#P}
oracle can be used to compute two
\mathsf{\#P}
functions
[
4
]
.

2.

\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\#P}}\subseteq\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{KthWitness}(\mathsf{FP})[1]}
: the proof follows from the second part of
Theorem

3.1
, because the number of witnesses in this proof is polynomial-time computable.
∎

The proof of
\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{MidP}[1]}=\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{KthOutput}(\mathsf{FP})[1]}
is given in
[
9
]
in a different notation.

Lemma 4.2

\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{MidP}[1]}=\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{KthOutput}(\mathsf{FP})[1]}=\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{KthOutput}(\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\#P}})[1]}
.

Proof

It suffices to prove that
\mathsf{KthOutput}(\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\#P}})\subseteq\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\#P}}
and
\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\#P}}\subseteq

\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{KthOutput}(\mathsf{FP})[1]}
, because
\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\#P}}=\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{MidP}[1]}
by
Corollary

3.3
.

1.

\mathsf{KthOutput}(\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\#P}})\subseteq\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\#P}}
: the proof is analogous to the first part of
Lemma

4.1
, except that the binary search on the set of outputs requires queries to
\mathsf{SpanP}
. It follows from the main theorem of
[
10
]
that
\mathsf{SpanP}\subseteq\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\#P}}
.

2.

\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\#P}}\subseteq\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{KthOutput}(\mathsf{FP})[1]}
: the proof follows from the second part of
Theorem

3.1
, because there exists a NPTM that outputs all witnesses from this proof on distinct paths without generating other paths, and the number of witnesses is polynomial-time computable.
∎

For the class
\mathsf{MedP}
analogous lemma can be proved similarly. The next theorem summarizes results of the last two sections.

Theorem 4.3

\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\#P}}=\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\overline{MedP}}[1]}=\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{KthWitness}(\mathsf{FP})[1]}=\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{KthWitness}(\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\#P}})[1]}=\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{MidP}[1]}=\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{KthOutput}(\mathsf{FP})[1]}=\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{KthOutput}(\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\#P}})[1]}=\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{MedP}[1]}=\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{KthOutput_{seq}}(\mathsf{FP})[1]}=\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{KthOutput_{seq}}(\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\#P}})[1]}

5
Computing the K
{}^{\textnormal{{th}}}
Value in a Sequence of Outputs

For the class
\mathsf{MedP}
, we give a stronger result than
Theorem

4.3
. In the next theorem, we prove that the classes
\mathsf{MedP}
,
\mathsf{KthOutput_{seq}}(\mathsf{FP})
, and
\mathsf{KthOutput_{seq}}(\mathsf{\#P})
are equal.

Definition 5.1

The class
\mathsf{MaxMCP}
consists of all functions
f
for which there exist a language
A\in\mathsf{PP}
and functions
l,r\in\mathsf{FP}
(
\Sigma^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
) such that:

1.

For every
x\in\Sigma^{*}
:

\displaystyle f(x)=\begin{cases}y_{k},&\text{if the set $W$ is not empty,}\\
l(x)-1,&\text{$otherwise$,}\end{cases}

where
W=\{y\in\mathbb{Z}\ |\ l(x)\leqslant y\leqslant r(x)\wedge(x,y)\in A\}=\{y_{1},\dots,y_{k}\}
and
y_{1}<\ldots<y_{k}
.

2.

For every
x\in\Sigma^{*}
, if
(x,y)\in A
and
l(x)\leqslant y\leqslant r(x)
, then
(x,y-1)\in A
.

A proof of
\mathsf{MedP}=\mathsf{MaxMCP}
is sketched in an unpublished manuscript
[
14
, Lemma 4.10]
.

Theorem 5.2

\mathsf{MedP}=\mathsf{MaxMCP}=\mathsf{KthOutput_{seq}}(\mathsf{FP})=\mathsf{KthOutput_{seq}}(\mathsf{\#P})
.

Proof

1.

\mathsf{KthOutput_{seq}}(\mathsf{\#P})\subseteq\mathsf{MedP}
: Let
f\in\mathsf{KthOutput_{seq}}(\mathsf{\#P})
and
g\in\mathsf{\#P}
from the definition of
\mathsf{KthOutput_{seq}}(\mathsf{\#P})
. To construct the required function from
\mathsf{MedP}
, for every
x\in\Sigma^{*}
the sequence of outputs
w_{1}\leqslant\ldots\leqslant w_{k}
will be extended so that the
g(x)
-th output in the initial sequence was the middle output in the extended sequence. To the sequence
w_{1}\leqslant\ldots\leqslant w_{k}

k
smaller numbers should be added to the left and the number
2^{p(|x|)}
with multiplicity
2g(x)
should be added to the right, where
p
is a fixed polynomial from the definition of
\mathsf{KthOutput_{seq}}
.

2.

\mathsf{MedP}\subseteq\mathsf{MaxMCP}
: Let
h\in\mathsf{MedP}
, then
h(x)=med_{M}(x)
for a metric NPTM
M
. There exist functions
l,r\in\mathsf{FP}
such that for every
x\in\Sigma^{*}
all outputs of
M
on input
x
are in the interval
[l(x),r(x)]
. For every
x\in\Sigma^{*}
, let
S(x)
be a set
\{(s,t)\ |\ s
is a path of
M
on input
x
that outputs
t\}
. Let
f
and
g
be functions such that for every
x\in\Sigma^{*}
and
y\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
l(x)\leqslant y\leqslant r(x)
,
f(x,y)
equals the number of elements
(s,t)\in S(x)
such that
t<y
, and
g(x,y)
equals the number of elements
(s,t)\in S(x)
such that
t\geqslant y
. The functions
f
and
g
are in
\mathsf{\#P}
, thus the language
A=\{(x,y)\ |\ f(x,y)<g(x,y)\}
is in
\mathsf{PP}
. The median value in the sequence of outputs of
M
on input
x
equals the maximal element in the set
\{y\in\mathbb{Z}\ |\ l(x)\leqslant y\leqslant r(x)\wedge(x,y)\in A\}
, and this set has the second property from the definition of
\mathsf{MaxMCP}
.

3.

\mathsf{MaxMCP}\subseteq\mathsf{MedP}
: Let
f\in\mathsf{MaxMCP}
, and
A\in\mathsf{PP}
,
l,r\in\mathsf{FP}
be defined as in the definition of
\mathsf{MaxMCP}
. Let
N
be a NPTM that witnesses
A
and satisfies the conditions of
Lemma

3.2
.
N
also should be modified so that for every
x\in\Sigma^{*}
and
y\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
l(x)\leqslant y\leqslant r(x)
it has
2^{p(|x|)}+1
paths on input
(x,y)
for a polynomial
p
. To define the required function from
\mathsf{MedP}
, we will describe a NPTM
M
that on every input
x\in\Sigma^{*}
makes a binary search on the set
\{y\in\mathbb{Z}\ |\ l(x)\leqslant y\leqslant r(x)\}
.
On input
x\in\Sigma^{*}
,
M
first simulates the machine
N
on input
(x,\lfloor(l(x)+r(x))/2\rfloor)
. If
N
accepts,
M
simulates
N
on input

(x,\lfloor(l(x)+3r(x))/4\rfloor)
. If
N
rejects,
M
simulates
N
on input
(x,\lfloor(3l(x)+r(x))/4\rfloor)
.
M
continues binary search until it has the same number of simulations on every path, and outputs
y
if the last simulation was on input
(x,y)
. If
N
rejects the input
(x,l(x))
,
M
outputs
l(x)-1
. At every simulation of
N
, more than a half of paths of
N
terminate with a correct decision of
(x,y)\in A
, and accepting paths always lead to larger outputs than rejecting paths. Therefore, the path with the middle output has correct decisions of
(x,y)\in A
, and outputs the value of
f(x)
.

4.

\mathsf{MaxMCP}\subseteq\mathsf{KthOutput_{seq}}(\mathsf{FP})
: follows from the proof of
\mathsf{MaxMCP}\subseteq\mathsf{MedP}
, because the number of paths of
M
is polynomial-time computable. ∎

6
Inclusions Between Function Classes

In this section, we give characterizations for several inclusions between function classes in terms of inclusions between language classes.

Below we give definitions of operators
\mathsf{U}
and
\mathsf{S}
that map classes of functions to classes of languages.

Definition 6.1
(
[
13
]
)

Let
F
be a class of functions
\Sigma^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
.

1.

The class
\mathsf{U}\cdot F
consists of all languages
L
such that there exists a function
f\in F
such that for every
x\in\Sigma^{*}
:

\displaystyle x\in L\Leftrightarrow f(x)=1,

\displaystyle x\notin L\Leftrightarrow f(x)=0.

2.

The class
\mathsf{S}\cdot F
consists of all languages
L
such that there exist functions
f\in F
and
g\in\mathsf{FP}
such that for every
x\in\Sigma^{*}
:

\displaystyle x\in L\Leftrightarrow f(x)>g(x).

\mathsf{U}\cdot\mathsf{\overline{MedP}}=\mathsf{SPP}
