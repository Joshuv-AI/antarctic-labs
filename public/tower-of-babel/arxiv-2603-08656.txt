Structure-preserving model reduction on manifolds of port-Hamiltonian systems
Silke Glas, Hongliang Mu

Abstract
This paper considers structure-preserving model order reduction ( MOR ) techniques for port-Hamiltonian ( pH ) systems, which are typically derived from energy-based modelling. To keep favorable properties of pH systems such as stability and passivity in a reduced order model ( ROM ), we use structure-preserving methods in the reduction process. There exists an extensive literature on structure-preserving MOR methods of pH systems, however, to the best of our knowledge, there does not exist an intrusive structure-preserving MOR method for nonlinear pH systems on the base of general nonlinear approximation maps. To close this gap, we propose a MOR method for pH systems based on the idea of the generalized manifold Galerkin ( GMG ) reduction. The resulting MOR method can be applied to both linear and nonlinear pH systems resulting in ROM s, which are again of pH form. For the numerical examples, we employ a linear and a nonlinear mass-spring-damper system and the results show that the proposed MOR methods have lower relative reduction error compared to existing methods.



Keywords
structure-preserving model reduction, port-Hamiltonian systems, generalized manifold Galerkin reduction, quadratically embedded manifolds

Introduction

Port-Hamiltonian (
pH
) systems are often derived via energy-based modelling, which is e.g., used for multi-physics systems, e.g., electrical circuits
[
9
]
, mechanical systems
[
8
]
, and fluid dynamics
[
5
]
. By using this type of modelling, a (big) overall system can be decomposed into subsystems, which are interconnected through their ports. An important property of these
pH
systems is that they satisfy an energy/power balance equation, i.e.,
the change in the Hamiltonian with respect to time equals the supplied energy minus the dissipated energy, which can be used to derive properties like stability and passivity.
However, for a given high-dimensional
pH
system, its simulation can be computationally expensive.
To overcome this bottleneck, model (order) reduction (
MOR
) techniques, which approximate the full-order model (
FOM
) by a reduced-order model (
ROM
), have been proposed. Some well-known
MOR
methods are, e.g., the proper orthogonal decomposition (
POD
)
[
29
]
, the iterative rational Krylov algorithm (
IRKA
)
[
2
]
, and the balanced truncation
[
19
]
.
Unfortunately, the aforementioned
MOR
methods do not guarantee preservation of the
pH
structure in the
ROM
.
However, in the last years multiple structure-preserving
pH
methods have been developed. In the following, we provide an overview of such methods and subsequently introduce our new method.

We first review MOR methods for linear pH systems. Moment matching-based
MOR
methods were introduced in
[
13
,
31
]
.
Based on this framework,
pH
-
IRKA
was proposed in
[
12
]
.
The authors of
[
26
]
developed a Riemannian optimal
MOR
approach based on a trust region method.
Furthermore, non-intrusive structure-preserving
MOR
methods, including
pH
-dynamic mode decomposition (
pH

DMD
)
[
20
]
,
pH
identification network (
pHIN
)
[
25
]
, data-driven
pH
-operator inference (

-
OpInf
)
[
10
]
, and structured optimization-based model order reduction (SOBMOR)
[
28
]
, have been developed recently.
For
pH
systems with nonlinear Hamiltonians, multiple
MOR
approaches have been introduced, where we provide an overview in the following.
In
[
14
]
, a moment matching-based MOR method for nonlinear pH systems was developed. A structure-preserving balanced truncation
MOR
method based on generalized Gramians was proposed in
[
16
]
.
Moreover, a
POD
-based structure-preserving
MOR
technique was developed by considering a

\nabla_{{\bm{x}}}\mathcal{H}({\bm{x}})

[
6
]
. For
pH
systems with nonlinear Hamiltonian, it is important to note that the evaluation of the

When the

[

21

To overcome this limitation, nonlinear

For

In this work, we propose a novel structure-preserving

[
3
]
, and we analyze the existence of the

In Section

2.2
Generalized manifold Galerkin

To ensure, that underlying structure in an initial value problem is preserved in the
ROM
, the paper
[
3
]
introduces a differential geometric framework. In this section, we recall the essential construction of the so-called
generalized manifold Galerkin
(
GMG
) reduction, introduced in the latter paper for, e.g., Hamiltonian and Lagrangian systems, since we will need this in the remainder of the paper to introduce our structure-preserving
MOR
method of
pH
systems. To stay within the notation of the paper, we denote the reduction map on a fixed chart and work with the representative matrices and vectors. For a formulation of the
GMG
reduction on manifolds, we refer the reader to
[
3
, Section 5]
.

For our full order model (
FOM
), we consider an initial value problem of the form

\displaystyle\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}t}{\bm{x}}(t)=\bm{f}({\bm{x}}(t))\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N},t\in\mathcal{I},\qquad{\bm{x}}(t_{0})={\bm{x}}_{0}\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N},

with
\bm{f}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Our goal is to derive a
ROM
of form

\displaystyle\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}t}\check{\bm{x}}(t)=\check{\bm{f}}(\check{\bm{x}}(t))\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{r},t\in\mathcal{I},\qquad\check{\bm{x}}(t_{0})=\check{\bm{x}}_{0}\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{r},

(5)

where
\check{\bm{f}}:\mathbb{R}^{r}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{r}
, and
r\ll N
. To this end, we define an embedding and point reduction map by

\displaystyle\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{r}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N},\qquad\rho:\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{r}

satisfying a point projection property, i.e.,
\rho\circ\varphi=\mathbb{I}_{r}
for all
\check{\bm{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{r}
, which leads to
\varphi\circ\rho
being a point projection.
To have a projection not only on the manifold itself, but additionally on the tangent bundle, we use the functions

\displaystyle\bm{D}_{\check{\bm{x}}}\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{r}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N},\qquad\mathcal{R}_{\text{red},{\bm{x}}}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{r},

where
\bm{D}_{\check{\bm{x}}}\varphi
denotes the Jacobian of
\varphi
satisfying
\mathcal{R}_{\text{red},\varphi(\check{\bm{x}})}\circ\bm{D}_{\check{\bm{x}}}\varphi=\mathbb{I}_{r}
for all
\check{\bm{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{r}
, as tangent embedding and tangent reduction map, respectively. With these specification, we can define the quantities in (
5
) as the following

\displaystyle\check{\bm{f}}(\check{\bm{x}}):=\mathcal{R}_{\text{red},\varphi(\check{\bm{x}})}\left(\bm{f}(\varphi(\check{\bm{x}}))\right)\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{r}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\check{\bm{x}}_{0}:=\rho({\bm{x}}_{0}).

With this setting, we are able to introduce the
GMG
reduction, which is a special kind of tangent reduction map that allows to preserves structure. We introduce the quantity encoding structure
2
2

2

I.e., a matrix of component functions of
(0,2)-
tensor field, see again
[
3
, Section 5]
.
to be
\bm{G}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
non-degenerate, which e.g., could be the canonical Poisson matrix in the reduction of Hamiltonian systems. Then, we define the
GMG
reduction
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\text{red},\varphi(\check{\bm{x}})}\left(\bm{f}(\varphi(\check{\bm{x}}))\right)=\left(\left(\bm{D}_{\check{\bm{x}}}\varphi(\check{\bm{x}})\right)^{\top}\bm{G}\bm{D}_{\check{\bm{x}}}\varphi(\check{\bm{x}})\right)^{-1}\left(\bm{D}_{\check{\bm{x}}}\varphi(\check{\bm{x}})\right)^{\top}\bm{G}\bm{f}(\varphi(\check{\bm{x}})).

We further denote the
GMG
reduction map in this paper by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\bm{G}}\left(\bm{D}_{\check{\bm{x}}}\varphi(\check{\bm{x}})\right)=\left(\left(\bm{D}_{\check{\bm{x}}}\varphi(\check{\bm{x}})\right)^{\top}\bm{G}\bm{D}_{\check{\bm{x}}}\varphi(\check{\bm{x}})\right)^{-1}\left(\bm{D}_{\check{\bm{x}}}\varphi(\check{\bm{x}})\right)^{\top}\bm{G}.

(6)

Here, we implicitly assume that
\left(\bm{D}_{\check{\bm{x}}}\varphi(\check{\bm{x}})\right)^{\top}\bm{G}\bm{D}_{\check{\bm{x}}}\varphi(\check{\bm{x}})
is non-degenerate and for given
M
, we denote the set of all matrices satisfying this assumption by

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{\bm{G}}\coloneqq\left\{\bm{V}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times r}|{\rm det}(\bm{V}^{\top}\bm{G}\bm{V})\neq 0,\ 0<r\leq N\right\}.

(7)

Although the
GMG
reduction provides a natural way of preserving structure in a
ROM
, it is not clear how to preserve the additional input-output structure of
pH
systems. We address this issue in Section
3

2.3
Discrete empirical interpolation method for
pH
systems

We consider a nonlinear
pH
system
\Sigma(\bm{J},\bm{R},\mathcal{H},\bm{B},\bm{u},{\bm{y}},{\bm{x}}_{0})
where
\bm{J},\bm{R},\bm{B}
are constant and
\mathcal{H}
is a nonlinear function.
Even if a linear projection-based

The

(

[
7

pH

To ensure that the

[
6
]

We consider a nonlinear Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}({\bm{x}})

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}({\bm{x}})=\frac{1}{2}{\bm{x}}^{\top}\bm{Q}{\bm{x}}+p({\bm{x}}),\quad p\colon\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R},\quad\bm{Q}{\succeq}0,\quad\bm{Q}=\bm{Q}^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N},

where
\bm{Q}
is the second order derivative of

at an equilibrium point, i.e.

\displaystyle\bm{Q}=\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{{\bm{x}}}^{2}\mathcal{H}({\bm{x}}_{e}),\quad\nabla_{{\bm{x}}}\mathcal{H}({\bm{x}}_{e})=\mathbf{0}.

Further, we denote
q\colon\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}
by

q({\bm{x}})=\nabla_{{\bm{x}}}p({\bm{x}})
.
Then, a
DEIM
basis
\bm{U}=[\bm{u}_{1},\ldots,\bm{u}_{d}]\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}
is built based on the
POD
basis of
[q({\bm{x}}(t_{0})),\ldots,q({\bm{x}}(t_{n_{t}}))]\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times(n_{t}+1)}
.
After that, an index set
\{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{d}\}
where
\xi_{i}\in\left\{0,\ldots,N\right\}
is computed by using Algorithm

\bm{E}=[\bm{e}_{\xi_{1}},\ldots,\bm{e}_{\xi_{d}}]\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}
is defined, where

\mathbb{I}_{N}
, i.e., the identity matrix if size
N
. Subsequently, with the

\bm{U}
and the interpolation matrix

, a

\displaystyle q({\bm{x}})\approx\hat{q}({\bm{x}})=\mathbb{P}q(\mathbb{P}^{\top}{\bm{x}}),\quad{\rm where}\quad\mathbb{P}=\bm{U}(\bm{E}\bm{U})^{-1}\bm{E}^{\top}.

(8)

3
Generalized manifold Galerkin for
pH
systems

We now detail how the structure-preserving
MOR
on manifolds of
pH
systems is performed based on the
GMG
reduction.
To this end, we assume that we are given a
pH
system
\Sigma(\bm{J},\bm{R},\mathcal{H},\bm{B},\bm{u},{\bm{y}},{\bm{x}}_{0})
(
2.1
). Then, our goal is to find an approximate solution

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{x}}(t)=\varphi(\check{\bm{x}}(t))\approx{\bm{x}}(t),

(9)

where
\check{\bm{x}}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{r}
is the reduced-order state,
\tilde{\bm{x}}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
is the reconstructed solution and
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}^{r}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
m<r\ll N
is a (possibly nonlinear) approximation map.

To derive a
ROM
we first define the time-continuous residual of the
FOM
(
2.1
) with respect to the reconstructed solution
\tilde{\bm{x}}(t)
by

\displaystyle{\bm{r}}(t)\coloneqq

\displaystyle\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}t}\tilde{\bm{x}}(t)-\left(\bm{J}-\bm{R}\right)\nabla_{{\bm{x}}}\mathcal{H}(\tilde{\bm{x}}(t))-\bm{B}\bm{u}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\bm{D}_{\check{\bm{x}}}\varphi(\check{\bm{x}})\right)\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}t}\check{\bm{x}}(t)-\left(\bm{J}-\bm{R}\right)\nabla_{{\bm{x}}}\mathcal{H}(\varphi(\check{\bm{x}}(t)))-\bm{B}\bm{u}(t).

Then, we require that by using the
GMG
reduction (
6
) the projected residual vanishes, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}(\check{\bm{x}}(t))^{\top}{\bm{r}}(t)\overset{!}{=}\mathbf{0},

where we denote by
\mathcal{W}\colon\mathbb{R}^{r}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N\times r}
the

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}\left(\check{\bm{x}}\right)=\left(\mathcal{F}_{\left(\bm{J}-\bm{R}\right)^{-1}}\left(\bm{D}_{\check{\bm{x}}}\varphi(\check{\bm{x}})\right)\right)^{\top}.

(10)

By reformulating the latter equation, we arrive at the following

\displaystyle\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}t}\check{\bm{x}}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{W}(\check{\bm{x}}(t))^{\top}\left(\bm{J}-\bm{R}\right)\nabla_{{\bm{x}}}\mathcal{H}(\varphi(\check{\bm{x}}(t)))+\mathcal{W}(\check{\bm{x}}(t))^{\top}\bm{B}\bm{u}(t),

(11a)

\displaystyle\check{\bm{y}}(t)

\displaystyle=\bm{B}^{\top}\nabla_{{\bm{x}}}\mathcal{H}(\varphi(\check{\bm{x}}(t))),\quad\check{\bm{x}}(0)=\check{\bm{x}}_{0},

(11b)

where
\check{\bm{y}}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and the reduced initial condition is given by
\check{\bm{x}}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{r}
. The following theorem discusses the conditions under which the

(

system.

.

Proof.

We have
\mathcal{V}\colon\check{\bm{x}}\mapsto\bm{D}_{\check{\bm{x}}}\varphi(\check{\bm{x}})
and

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}\colon\check{\bm{x}}\mapsto\left(\mathcal{F}_{\left(\bm{J}-\bm{R}\right)^{-1}}\left(\mathcal{V}(\check{\bm{x}})\right)\right)^{\top},

such that
\mathcal{W}(\check{\bm{x}})^{\top}\mathcal{V}(\check{\bm{x}})=\mathbb{I}_{r}

\displaystyle\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}t}\check{\bm{x}}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{W}^{\top}\left(\bm{J}-\bm{R}\right)\nabla_{{\bm{x}}}\mathcal{H}(\varphi(\check{\bm{x}}(t)))+\mathcal{W}^{\top}\bm{B}\bm{u}(t)

\displaystyle=\left(\mathcal{V}^{\top}\left(\bm{J}-\bm{R}\right)^{-1}\mathcal{V}\right)^{-1}\mathcal{V}^{\top}\nabla_{{\bm{x}}}\mathcal{H}(\varphi(\check{\bm{x}}(t)))+\mathcal{W}^{\top}\bm{B}\bm{u}(t)

\displaystyle=\left(\mathcal{V}^{\top}\left(\bm{J}-\bm{R}\right)^{-1}\mathcal{V}\right)^{-1}\nabla_{\check{\bm{x}}}\check{\mathcal{H}}(\check{\bm{x}}(t))+\mathcal{W}^{\top}\bm{B}\bm{u}(t)

\displaystyle=\left(\mathcal{V}^{\top}\left(\bm{J}-\bm{R}\right)^{-1}\mathcal{V}\right)^{-1}\left(\mathcal{V}^{\top}\left(\bm{J}-\bm{R}\right)^{-\top}\mathcal{V}\right)\left(\mathcal{V}^{\top}\left(\bm{J}-\bm{R}\right)^{-T}\mathcal{V}\right)^{-1}\nabla_{\check{\bm{x}}}\check{\mathcal{H}}(\check{\bm{x}}(t))+\mathcal{W}^{\top}\bm{B}\bm{u}(t)

\displaystyle=\left(\mathcal{V}^{\top}\left(\bm{J}-\bm{R}\right)^{-1}\mathcal{V}\right)^{-1}\left(\mathcal{V}^{\top}\left(\bm{J}-\bm{R}\right)^{-\top}\mathcal{V}\right)\left(\mathcal{V}^{\top}\left(\bm{J}-\bm{R}\right)^{-1}\mathcal{V}\right)^{-T}\nabla_{\check{\bm{x}}}\check{\mathcal{H}}(\check{\bm{x}}(t))+\mathcal{W}^{\top}\bm{B}\bm{u}(t)

\displaystyle=\left(\mathcal{V}^{\top}\left(\bm{J}-\bm{R}\right)^{-1}\mathcal{V}\right)^{-1}\mathcal{V}^{\top}\left(\bm{J}-\bm{R}\right)^{-1}\left(\bm{J}-\bm{R}\right)\left(\bm{J}-\bm{R}\right)^{-\top}\mathcal{V}\left(\mathcal{V}^{\top}\left(\bm{J}-\bm{R}\right)^{-1}\mathcal{V}\right)^{-T}\nabla_{\check{\bm{x}}}\check{\mathcal{H}}(\check{\bm{x}}(t))+\mathcal{W}^{\top}\bm{B}\bm{u}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{W}^{\top}\left(\bm{J}-\bm{R}\right)\mathcal{W}\nabla_{\check{\bm{x}}}\check{\mathcal{H}}(\check{\bm{x}}(t))+\mathcal{W}^{\top}\bm{B}\bm{u}(t),

where we define the reduced quantities naturally by

\displaystyle\check{\mathcal{J}}(\check{\bm{x}})\coloneqq\mathcal{W}(\check{\bm{x}})^{\top}\bm{J}\mathcal{W}(\check{\bm{x}}),\ \check{\mathcal{R}}(\check{\bm{x}})\coloneqq\mathcal{W}(\check{\bm{x}})^{\top}\bm{R}\mathcal{W}(\check{\bm{x}})\quad\text{and}\ \check{\mathcal{H}}\colon\check{\bm{x}}\mapsto\mathcal{H}(\varphi(\check{\bm{x}})).

Note that
\check{\mathcal{J}}(\check{\bm{x}})=-\check{\mathcal{J}}(\check{\bm{x}})^{\top}
,
\check{\mathcal{R}}(\check{\bm{x}})=\check{\mathcal{R}}(\check{\bm{x}})^{\top}\succeq 0
and the reduced Hamiltonian
\check{\mathcal{H}}
is continuously differentiable. However, for calling (
11
) a
pH
system, we still need to ensure that the output equation (
11b
) matches.
Since
{\rm span}(\bm{B})\subseteq{\rm span}(\mathcal{V}(\check{\bm{x}}))
, there exists a matrix
\bm{M}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times m}
such that
\bm{B}=\mathcal{V}(\check{\bm{x}})\bm{M}
. Further, as
\mathcal{V}(\check{\bm{x}})^{\top}\mathcal{W}(\check{\bm{x}})=\mathbb{I}_{r}
, we get

\displaystyle\bm{B}^{\top}=\bm{M}^{\top}\mathcal{V}(\check{\bm{x}})^{\top}=\bm{M}^{\top}\mathcal{V}(\check{\bm{x}})^{\top}\mathcal{W}(\check{\bm{x}})\mathcal{V}(\check{\bm{x}})^{\top}=\bm{B}^{\top}\mathcal{W}(\check{\bm{x}})\mathcal{V}(\check{\bm{x}})^{\top}.

(12)

Thus, we can rewrite (
11b
) by

\displaystyle\bm{B}^{\top}\nabla_{{\bm{x}}}\mathcal{H}(\varphi(\check{\bm{x}}))=\bm{B}^{\top}\mathcal{W}(\check{\bm{x}})\mathcal{V}(\check{\bm{x}})^{\top}\nabla_{{\bm{x}}}\mathcal{H}(\varphi(\check{\bm{x}}))=\check{\mathcal{B}}({\bm{x}})^{\top}\nabla_{\check{\bm{x}}}\check{\mathcal{H}}(\varphi(\check{\bm{x}})),

where we denote
\check{\mathcal{B}}(\check{\bm{x}})\coloneqq\mathcal{W}(\check{\bm{x}})^{\top}\bm{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times m}
and find that (
11
) is an
r
-dimensional
pH
system which can be described by
\Sigma(\check{\mathcal{J}}(\check{\bm{x}}),\check{\mathcal{R}}(\check{\bm{x}}),\check{\mathcal{H}},\check{\mathcal{B}}(\check{\bm{x}}),\bm{u},\check{\bm{y}},\check{\bm{x}}_{0})
.
∎

4
State approximation and corresponding
ROM
s

In this section, we discuss how to construct the approximation map
\varphi
(
9
) such that the requirements from Theorem
3.1
are fulfilled naturally by construction and discuss specific realizations. To this end, we start with the following form of
\varphi

\displaystyle\varphi(\check{\bm{x}})=\bm{B}\check{\bm{x}}_{1}+\overline{\bm{V}}\eta(\check{\bm{x}}_{2}),\quad\check{\bm{x}}_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{m},\quad\check{\bm{x}}_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{r-m},\quad\check{\bm{x}}=\begin{bmatrix}\check{\bm{x}}_{1}\\
\check{\bm{x}}_{2}\end{bmatrix},

(13)

with matrix
\overline{\bm{V}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times(r_{n}+r-m)}
satisfying
\bm{B}^{\top}\overline{\bm{V}}=\bm{0}_{m\times(r_{n}+r-m)}
, and
\eta\in\bm{C}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{r-m},\mathbb{R}^{r_{n}+r-m})
with the dimension
0\leq r_{n}\ll N
being identified for each concrete realization of
\eta
. While
\eta
could be any general nonlinear map, such as e.g., a neural network, we consider a linear and quadratic approximation map in this paper, and detail their choice and the resulting
ROM
s in Section
4.2
. We start by showing that the assumptions Theorem
3.1
are satisfied for the general representation of
\varphi
in (
13
).

4.1
Constructing
pH
systems with state approximation

Theorem
3.1
shows that the
GMG
reduction map preserves the
pH
structure if the approximation map satisfies that (i)
{\rm span}(\bm{B})\subseteq{\rm span}(\bm{D}_{\check{\bm{x}}}\varphi(\check{\bm{x}}))
and (ii)
\bm{D}_{\check{\bm{x}}}\varphi(\check{\bm{x}})\in\mathbf{S}_{\left(\bm{J}-\bm{R}\right)^{-1}}
. We note, that due to the special structure of the approximation map (
13
), we arrive at the Jacobian
\bm{D}_{\check{\bm{x}}}\varphi(\check{\bm{x}})=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{B},&\overline{\bm{V}}\bm{D}_{\check{\bm{x}}_{2}}\eta(\check{\bm{x}}_{2})\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times r}
,
such that condition (i), i.e.,
{\rm span}(\bm{B})\subseteq{\rm span}(\bm{D}_{\check{\bm{x}}}\varphi(\check{\bm{x}}))
is automatically satisfied. Moreover, with this construction, we arrive at
\check{\mathcal{B}}
being a constant function given by
\check{\mathcal{B}}\colon\check{\bm{x}}\mapsto\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{I}_{m}\\
\mathbf{0}_{r-m,m}\end{bmatrix}
, which follows from
\bm{B}^{\top}=\mathcal{V}(\check{\bm{x}})\mathcal{W}(\check{\bm{x}})^{\top}\bm{B}
, see (
12
) and
{\rm span}(\bm{B})\subseteq{\rm span}(\bm{D}_{\check{\bm{x}}}\varphi(\check{\bm{x}}))
.

4.2
State-approximations and resulting
ROM
s

To determine the unknown matrices/function of the approximation map
\varphi
, we use a data-driven approach. To this end, we introduce an equidistant time discretization of the time interval
(t_{0},t_{\text{end}}]
with
\Delta t:=(t_{\text{end}}-t_{0})/n_{t}
,
n_{t}\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, for
t_{i}:=t_{0}+i\Delta t
,
0\leq i\leq n_{t}
, we seek approximations
{\bm{x}}^{i}\approx{\bm{x}}(t_{i})
,
{\bm{y}}^{i}\approx{\bm{y}}(t_{i})
.
We query the
pH
system (
2.1
) to obtain a so-called
snapshot matrix

\displaystyle\bm{X}:=\begin{bmatrix}{\bm{x}}^{0},&\ldots,&{\bm{x}}^{n_{t}}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times(n_{t}+1)}.

(14)

In the following, we state particular cases of approximation map (
9
) such that it results (i) into a linear (Section
4.2.1
) and (ii) into a quadratic map (Section
4.2.2
).

4.2.1
Linear state approximation and
GMG
-
POD
-
ROM

We here choose
\eta
to be the identity map on
\mathbb{R}^{r-m}
, i.e.,
\eta:\mathbb{R}^{r-m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{r-m}
.
Then, the formulation in (
13
) results in a linear approximation map

\displaystyle\varphi_{\text{lin}}(\check{\bm{x}})=\bm{V}\check{\bm{x}}=\bm{B}\check{\bm{x}}_{1}+\overline{\bm{V}}\check{\bm{x}}_{2},

(15)

with
\bm{V}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{B},&\overline{\bm{V}}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times r}
, where we assume, that
\bm{V}
has full column rank. This is a weak assumption as we can ensure full column rank of
\overline{\bm{V}}
by construction and in case that
\bm{B}
is not of full column rank, the
pH
system can be rewritten in a form with less ports. Further, as
\bm{B}
is given by the problem formulation, we only need to find a suitable
\overline{\bm{V}}
such that the conditions of Theorem
3.1
are fulfilled. This is done via the following optimization where
{(\cdot)}^{\dagger}
represents the Moore-Penrose pseudo inverse

\displaystyle\overline{\bm{V}}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}_{\bm{A}\in\Xi_{\overline{\bm{V}}}}\sum_{i=0}^{n_{t}}\left\|{\bm{x}}^{i}-\begin{bmatrix}\bm{B},\bm{A}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\bm{B},\bm{A}\end{bmatrix}^{\dagger}{\bm{x}}^{i}\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}_{\bm{A}\in\Xi_{\overline{\bm{V}}}}\left\|\bm{X}-\begin{bmatrix}\bm{B},\bm{A}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\bm{B},\bm{A}\end{bmatrix}^{\dagger}\bm{X}\right\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}_{\bm{A}\in\Xi_{\overline{\bm{V}}}}\left\|\bm{X}-\bm{B}\bm{B}^{\dagger}\bm{X}-\bm{A}\bm{A}^{\dagger}\bm{X}\right\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}_{\bm{A}\in\Xi_{\overline{\bm{V}}}}\left\|\bm{X}-\bm{B}\bm{B}^{\dagger}\bm{X}-\bm{A}\bm{A}^{\top}\bm{X}+\bm{A}\bm{A}^{\top}\bm{B}\bm{B}^{\dagger}\bm{X}\right\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}_{\bm{A}\in\Xi_{\overline{\bm{V}}}}\left\|(\mathbb{I}_{N}-\bm{A}\bm{A}^{\top})(\bm{X}-\bm{B}\bm{B}^{\dagger}\bm{X})\right\|_{F}^{2},

where

\Xi_{\overline{\bm{V}}}=\left\{\bm{A}\left|\right.\bm{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times(r-m)},\ \bm{B}^{\top}\bm{A}=\bm{0}_{m\times(r-m)},\ \bm{A}^{\top}\bm{A}=\mathbb{I}_{r-m}\right\}.

This means, that we can determine
\overline{\bm{V}}
by the
POD
over the adjusted snapshot matrix
\bm{X}-\bm{B}\bm{B}^{\dagger}\bm{X}
.
For the point reduction map, which is used to map the initial condition, we choose

\displaystyle\rho_{\text{lin}}({\bm{x}}):=\bm{V}^{\dagger}{\bm{x}}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{B}^{\dagger},&\overline{\bm{V}}^{\top}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}{\bm{x}},

(16)

which has been used already in the derivations made above, such that the point projection property, i.e.,
\rho_{\text{lin}}\circ\varphi_{\text{lin}}=\text{id}_{\mathbb{R}^{r}}
is fulfilled. The resulting
ROM
from the choice of
\varphi_{\text{lin}}
,
\rho_{\text{lin}}
and the corresponding
GMG
reduction is called
GMG
-
POD
-
ROM
. We describe the procedure in pseudo-code in Algorithm
2
. Additionally, as we investigate nonlinear
pH
systems in the numerical experiments in this paper, we include the

\displaystyle\overline{\bm{V}}_{1}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}_{\bm{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times(r-m)},\bm{A}^{\top}\bm{A}=\mathbb{I}_{r-m}}\left\|(\mathbb{I}_{N}-\bm{A}\bm{A}^{\top})(\bm{X}-\bm{B}\bm{B}^{\dagger}\bm{X})\right\|_{F}^{2},\qquad\text{ and }

\bm{M}

6
Conclusion and outlook

We propose a new structure-preserving
MOR
framework for
pH
systems with general nonlinear approximation maps based on the
GMG
projection. We provide two concrete realizations with a linear and a quadratic embedding map for linear and nonlinear
pH
systems.
Numerical examples of a linear and a nonlinear mass-spring-damper system are investigated, and the
GMG
-
POD
-
ROM
and the
GMG
-
QM
-
ROM
provide a lower relative error compared to known methods in the literature in terms of state and output.
With respect to future work, we aim to consider different classes of
pH
systems, e.g., a
pH
system, where the resistive term is a nonlinear function of
\nabla_{{\bm{x}}}\mathcal{H}({\bm{x}}(t))
or employing structure-preserving neural networks to speed up the evaluation of a state-dependent systems matrices in a reduced order
pH
model.
