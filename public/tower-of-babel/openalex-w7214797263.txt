Abstract
Waldiomycin is a natural product originally isolated from actinomycete extracts. It inhibits histidine kinases (HKs), which
are essential components of bacterial two-component signal transduction systems, by targeting the conserved H-box region
and suppressing autophosphorylation. Here, we identiﬁed novel HK inhibitors using amino acid mapping (AAM)-based
virtual screening. We compared the AAM descriptor of the naphthoquinone moiety of waldiomycin, which directly interacts
with the H-box region, with those of approximately 12 million commercially available compounds and identiﬁed 714
candidates with similar descriptor values. From these candidates, we selected 9-hydroxy-4H-pyrido[1,2-a]pyrimidin-4-one
as a promising and structurally distinct scaffold and synthesized 15 derivatives. The inhibitory activities of these derivatives
were evaluated against the HKs WalK, VicK, and EnvZ. Three 4H-pyrido[1,2-a]pyrimidin-4-one derivatives (FF-054, FF055, and FF-060) exhibited potent inhibitory activity, with IC50 values ranging from 4.39 to 259 µM. Notably, the IC50
values of these compounds increased three- to six-fold against the EnvZ S242A mutant, in which the conserved H-box
residue is substituted. Furthermore, nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) analysis using the EnvZ DHp domain (residues
223–289, encompassing the H-box region) conﬁrmed direct interaction with this domain. Taken together, these results
demonstrate that pyrido[1,2-a]pyrimidin-4-one derivatives FF-054, FF-055, and FF-060 are novel HK inhibitors that target
the H-box region. This study represents the ﬁrst successful application of AAM-based screening to identify HK inhibitors
targeting this highly conserved region.

The amino acid sequence conserved in the HK H-box
(Fig. 1d) is speciﬁc to HKs—it is not found in serine/
threonine or tyrosine kinases in humans [3]. Genes involved
in the multidrug resistance and virulence of pathogenic
bacteria are regulated by two-component systems consisting
of HKs and response regulators (Fig. 1c) [4]. Therefore,
H-box inhibitors acting on such HKs are key targets as nextgeneration antimicrobial agents capable of modulating the
drug resistance and pathogenicity of pathogens such as
ESKAPE organisms [5, 6]. With this goal in mind, Ishikawa
et al. [7] synthesized novel H-box inhibitors targeting HKs
derived from the NQ moiety of the angucyclic ring in
waldiomycin.
The HK inhibitors synthesized to date are classiﬁed as:
trityles, benzoxazines, salicylanilides, triazoles, 6-oxa-isosteres, terpene derivatives, benzimidazoles, benzoxazoles,
thiazolidiones, thiophenes, adenines, and other heterocycles
[8]. None of these HK inhibitors have yet reached clinical

application, despite the highly diverse structures and wide
range of properties exhibited [8]. It remains essential to
generate novel HK inhibitor compounds suitable for clinical
trials.
Tsumura et al. [9, 10] proposed a novel method—amino
acid mapping (AAM)—that allows information on the
ligand-binding site(s) for target proteins to be directly
derived from a compound, bypassing the elucidation of the
structure of the complex formed by the compound and the
target protein (Fig. 2). The AAM descriptor is the spatial
distribution of single amino acid residues surrounding the
compound, calculated independently of the explicit protein
structure (Fig. 2b; see Materials and Methods for computational details). Structure-independent virtual screening
based on the AAM descriptor derived from a known ligand
enables the efﬁcient identiﬁcation of compounds with distinct scaffolds that are likely to exhibit similar biological
activities. Herein, we applied the AAM descriptor of the
NQ moiety of waldiomycin to screen a library of 12 million
compounds, leading to the identiﬁcation of HK inhibitors
with novel, non-NQ scaffolds.

Similarity of the AAM descriptors
In this study, the AAM similarity between two speciﬁc
conformations was deﬁned as the cosine similarity between
their AAM descriptors. For comparisons between compounds, AAM similarity was deﬁned as the maximum
similarity obtained through the optimization procedure
described below. AAM similarities were calculated for two
cases: (i) between the NQ reference compound and the
11,973,825 compounds from the LC (Fig. 3), and (ii)
among a total of 10 compounds (NQ and nine H-box
inhibitors: FF-054, FF-055, FF-060, and 6a–f), for which
all pairwise combinations were used to construct a 10 × 10
AAM similarity matrix. First, 100 low-precision conformations of NQ were generated using the open-source
Open Babel software [16]. These conformations were then
used as initial structures for geometry optimization with the
Gaussian16 [17] quantum chemistry program—employing
density functional theory with the B3LYP functional
[18, 19], 6-31 G* basis set [20], and the polarizable continuum solvation model [21] with water as the solvent—to
obtain 100 high-precision, locally stable conformations. The
most stable conformation among the 100 was then selected.
For the LC compounds, the geometries used in this study
were not generated de novo during the present screening
study. Rather, they had been prepared in advance, prior to
this study, by large-scale DFT-based conformational

calculations, and these precomputed structures were used as
input geometries for AAM-based virtual screening.
For each LC compound, the precomputed geometry was
used as the initial conformation, and the centroid position,
molecular orientation, and intramolecular dihedral angles
were adjusted within an energy window of +10 kcal/mol to
maximize the AAM similarity to the most stable NQ conformation. The ﬁnal AAM similarity between NQ and each
LC compound was deﬁned as the maximum similarity
obtained through this procedure (Figs. 3 and S1).
To reduce the computational cost of AAM similarity
evaluation for the 11,973,825 LC compounds, a stepwise
accelerated ﬁltering procedure was applied. First, AAM
similarity was calculated using only the descriptor for Phe,
and compounds showing negative similarity values were
excluded. Second, for the remaining compounds, AAM
similarity was calculated using the descriptors for His fully
protonated +1 and Cys-side chain S⁻, representing charged
residue types, and compounds showing negative similarity
values were excluded. Finally, full AAM similarity calculations using all 27 amino acid residue descriptors were
performed only for compounds that passed both ﬁltering
steps. In this stepwise procedure, the ﬁnal conformation
obtained in the Phe-based calculation was used as the initial
conformation for the second-step calculation, and the ﬁnal
conformation obtained in the second-step calculation was
then used as the initial conformation for the full 27-residue
calculation, thereby improving the efﬁciency of conformational optimization.
A threshold of 0.75 was used for hit selection in the LC
screening. This cutoff was determined empirically based on
previous AAM-based screening campaigns against several
target protein systems, in which compounds showing
activities comparable to those of reference ligands were
identiﬁed with an approximate success rate of 10% among
compounds exceeding this AAM similarity value. On this
empirical basis, 0.75 was adopted as the hit-selection
threshold in the present study. The conformations of the
nine H-box inhibitors (FF-054, FF-055, FF-060, and 6a–f)
were determined using the same procedure as for the LC
compounds.
The conformations of NQ and these nine H-box inhibitors were then used to compute a 10 × 10 AAM similarity
matrix, in which each element represented the AAM similarity between a pair of compounds. Because AAM similarity is inherently asymmetric and depends on the order of
comparison, the resulting matrix was symmetrized by adding it to its transpose and dividing each element by two.

screening using an in-house Python script implemented
with RDKit (Open-source cheminformatics, https://www.

a condensed ring system together with adjacent atoms
directly connected to that ring system through double or
higher-order bonds. Ring information was obtained from
each compound using the GetSymmSSSR function, and
rings sharing atoms were merged to deﬁne ring systems.
Non-ring atoms connected to ring-system atoms through
multiple bonds were then added, and the resulting atom set
was extracted as a fragment, sanitized, and converted to a
canonical SMILES string. Duplicate occurrences of the
same substructure within a molecule were counted only
once, and frequencies were tabulated across all compounds.
Exact molecular weights were calculated using
CalcExactMolWt.

Multidimensional scaling analysis of AAM
similarities
We converted the symmetrized 10 × 10 AAM similarity
matrix into a distance matrix to visualize the relationships
between H-box inhibitors. The distance between compounds i and j was deﬁned as dij = 1 − Sij, where Sij is the
AAM similarity between compounds i and j. We then
performed classical multidimensional scaling (MDS) [22]
using the scikit-learn module in Python [23] to create a twodimensional map of the pairwise distances between the
compounds from the distance matrix.

Inhibition assay of HK autophosphorylation
The inhibition assay of HK autophosphorylation was carried out as previously described [7]. Autophosphorylations
of WalK(207-611) and EnvZ(223-450) were performed at 1
and 2 µM, respectively, in a reaction buffer (50 mM TrisHCl (pH 8.5), 100 mM KCl, 5 mM MgCl2, 100 mM
NH4Cl, 5% dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO), and 40 µM ATP
for WalK(207-611); 400 µM ATP for EnvZ(223-450). For
VicK(31-450), the reaction was conducted at 0.5 µM in a
buffer comprising 50 mM Tris-HCl (pH 7.5), 50 mM KCl,
10 mM MgCl2, 5% DMSO, and 20 µM ATP. For all cases,
the total reaction volume was 40 µL. The autophosphorylation reactions were performed at 25 °C for
30 min (WalK(207-611), EnvZ(223-450)) or 10 min
(VicK(31-450)) before being terminated by the addition of
40 µL 2×SDS-PAGE sample buffer. The reaction mixture
was then subjected to SDS-PAGE (10% acrylamide) and
Western blotting using a polyvinylidene diﬂuoride membrane (Bio-Rad). Anti-N3-phosphohistidine antibody (clone
SD56-2; EDM Millipore Corp.) was used as the primary
antibody, goat anti-rabbit IgG-HRP (Abcam) as the secondary antibody, and 2% bovine serum albumin (SigmaAldrich) solution as the blocking agent. Signals originating
from phosphorylated HKs were detected using a horseradish peroxidase (HRP) substrate (Immobilon Western
Chemiluminescent HRP substrate, Millipore) and MultiImager II MultiBox (BioTools). The signal intensities were
quantiﬁed using the CS Analyzer 4 (ATTO) image analysis
software, and 50% inhibitory concentration (IC50) values
were determined using Prism 7 (GraphPad).

Afﬁnity selection/mass spectrometry
The afﬁnity selection/mass spectrometry (AS/MS) method
analyzes the competitive binding of small compounds to
proteins against a reference ligand. Experiments followed a
previously established protocol [7], wherein 1 µM of

VicK(31-450) (Table S1) was combined and incubated with
5 µM of waldiomycin and FF-060 (0, 10, 30, 100 µM) in a
reaction buffer (25 mM Tris-HCl (pH = 8.0), 50 mM KCl,
50 mM NH4Cl, 5 mM MgCl2, 1 mM dithiothreitol, and
0.1% CHAPS(3-((3-cholamidopropyl) dimethylammonio)-1propanesulfonate). The mixture was subjected to sizeexclusion via centrifugation to eliminate free compounds.
The eluted protein was then denatured by adding an equal
volume of methanol/0.1% formic acid. The compounds that
were subsequently released from the protein were detected
using a liquid chromatography–mass spectroscopy (LC–MS)
system.

Results and discussion
Chemical library screening by AAM
714 compounds with sufﬁciently high AAM similarity values
between NQ and the LC compounds (≥0.75) were identiﬁed
(Fig. 4 and Table S2). Rigid substructures, deﬁned as condensed ring systems together with adjacent atoms directly

extracted from the 714 compound structures, yielding 227
distinct scaffolds along with their occurrence frequencies

considered as potential core scaffolds in order to exclude
overly small frameworks. This threshold was chosen as an
approximate size criterion corresponding to a two-ring system, i.e., one ring fewer than the three-ring naphthoquinonecontaining reference compound (NQ), and is roughly intermediate between the molecular weights of biphenyl and
naphthalene. The 11 most frequently occurring structures

HK inhibitory activity of the synthesized compounds
The 15 synthesized candidate compounds (Fig. 6) were ﬁrst
evaluated at 100 μg/mL for their inhibition of WalK(207611) and VicK(31-450) autophosphorylation. Compounds
showing >30% inhibition (i.e., <70% residual activity
compared to the DMSO control) were subjected to IC50
determination. IC50 values against WalK(207-611) ranged
from 105–305 µM and those against VicK(31-450) from
4.39–1817 µM (Table 1). No compounds had stronger
inhibitory activity against WalK(207-611) than waldiomycin (13.1 µM), although FF-008, 054, 060, and 081 showed
relatively strong inhibition. However, the IC50 values
against VicK for FF-055 (6.92 µM) and FF-060 (4.39 µM)
were lower than that of waldiomycin (25.8 µM) (Fig. S2
and Table 1). The amino acid sequences constituting the
H-box regions of WalK and VicK are highly similar
(Fig. 1d). However, the structural differences between the
dimeric forms of WalK and VicK have not yet been fully
elucidated. The conformation of the H-box regions is
affected by the prior and subsequent regions and the CA
domain. In addition, according to the binding model shown
in Fig. 10, FF-060 and FF-055 are predicted to bind to a
site that is close to but distinct from the waldiomycinbinding site and inhibit the histidine kinase activity of the
CA domain. Our results provide no evidence that FF-060
and FF-055 bind to the binding site through the identical
interactions between WalK and VicK. Therefore, it is

We previously showed that mutations within the H-box
region rendered the site-directed mutant proteins of
EnvZ(223-450) mutants highly resistant to waldiomycin
[2]. These showed autophosphorylation activities comparable with that of the wild-type protein in the absence of the
inhibitors and had enough stability under the measurement
conditions [2]. We compared the inhibitory potential of the
FF compounds against EnvZ(223-450) and its S242A and
T247A mutants (Table 2). FF-054 inhibited EnvZ(223-450)
and EnvZ(223-450)T247A at the same level, but not
EnvZ(223-450)S242A, which became modestly resistant.
FF-055 and FF-060 also showed similar levels of inhibition
against EnvZ(223-450) and EnvZ(223-450)T247A, but
weaker inhibition against EnvZ(223-450)S242A. These
results suggest that S242 (which is adjacent to the site of
autophosphorylation H243 (Fig. 1b,d)) is related to the
binding of FF-055 and FF-060, while T247 (which is
within the H-box and has a side chain protruding in a
similar direction to S242) is not. Our results suggest that
FF-054, FF-055, and FF-060 may bind to the S242 side of
H243, in contrast to our model of the binding of waldiomycin to EnvZ(223-289) where the NQ moiety interacts
with the T247 side of H243 [2].

Conclusion
AAM-based screening combined with structural simpliﬁcation of waldiomycin identiﬁed 4H-pyrido[1,2-a]pyrimidin-4one derivatives as novel histidine kinase inhibitors that affect
the H-box region. Our approach provides a practical strategy
for identifying novel inhibitor scaffolds based on amino acid
interaction patterns, without requiring detailed structural
information of the target protein. Furthermore, visualization
of the positional relationships between nine synthesized
H-box inhibitors, 4H-pyrido[1,2-a]pyrimidin-4-ones, in the
present study and previously reported naphthoquinones
(Fig. 11), based on their AAM descriptors, provided
mechanistic insights that correlated with differences in their
modes of action. Collectively, our ﬁndings suggest that
AAM/MDS provides a framework for investigating compounds with diverse modes of action and for generating
