. 1.
Introduction

The Yangian
Y_{m|n}
associated with the Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{m|n}
over the complex
field is a deformation of the universal enveloping algebra
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{m|n}[t])
in the class of Hopf algebras.
The original definition was given by Nazarov
[
Na1
]
in terms of the
RTT presentation
.
A
Drinfeld-type presentation
corresponding to a standard
Borel subalgebra was obtained by Gow
[
Gow
]
,
extending the results of Drinfeld
[
D
]
and
Brundan and Kleshchev
[
BK1
]
on the Yangian
Y_{n}
.
The finite dimensional irreducible representations of
Y_{m|n}
were classified
by Zhang
[
Z1
,
Z2
]

in a way similar to the representations of the Yangians associated with the simple Lie algebras, as given by Tarasov
[
Ta
]
and Drinfeld
[
D
]
(see also
[
Mol1
]
).
Recently,
the finite dimensional irreducible
representations of the orthosymplectic super Yangians were further investigated
in a series of papers
[
Mol2
,
Mol3
,
Mol4
,
MR
]
.

In
[
BBG
]
,
Brown, Brundan and Goodwin introduced the
shifted super Yangians

Y_{1|1}(\sigma)
for
\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1}
.
This parallels the definition of
shifted Yangians
from
[
BK2
]
.
In particular,
they established the connection between the
principal
W
-superalgebra

W_{m|n}
and the
truncated shifted super Yangian

Y_{1|1,\ell}(\sigma)
.
This allowed them to
make an extensive study of the representation theory of
W_{m|n}
,
see also
[
BK3
, Chapter 7]
for the classification of the finite dimensional irreducible representations of the shifted Yangians.
Meanwhile,
Peng
[
Peng
]
obtained a super analogue of the main result of
[
BK2
]
for type
A
Lie superalgebras in full generality.

Let
\mathbbm{k}
be an algebraically closed field of positive characteristic
p>0
.
Brundan and Topley
[
BT18
]
developed the theory of the shifted Yangian
Y_{n}(\sigma)
over
\mathbbm{k}
.
They defined the Yangian
Y_{n}
over
\mathbbm{k}
by the usual RTT presentation,
and extended the
Drinfeld-type presentation
(
[
D
,
BK1
]
) from characteristic zero to characteristic
p
.
In
[
CH1
]
,
we use Nazarov’s approach in the definition of the modular super Yangian and
gave the modular analogue of the Drinfeld-type presentation of the super Yangian
Y_{m|n}
.

In the current article,
we initiate a study of representations of the shifted super Yangian
Y_{1|1}(\sigma)
over
\mathbbm{k}
.
As a first step towards developing the modular representation theory of super Yangians,
we first investigate the finite-dimensional irreducible representations of the restricted Yangian
Y_{1|1}^{[p]}
in detail.
Over complex field,
Zhang
[
Z1
]
developed the representation theory of the super Yangian
Y_{1|1}
.
However, when working over
\mathbbm{k}
, his approach cannot
work in characteristic
p
.
We classify the finite dimensional irreducible
Y_{1|1}^{[p]}
-modules by adapting the methods from
[
CHT
]
to the super setting.
Motivated by the results of
[
BBG
]
,
we study the representations of the
restricted truncated shifted super Yangian

Y_{1|1,\ell}^{[p]}(\sigma)
further (see subsection
4.1
).
As mentioned in Remark
4.7
,
we expect that this result will be of importance in the classification of certain irreducible represetations of the modular Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{m|n}
.

The article is organized as follows.
In Section
2
,
we recall some preliminaries about the modular super Yangian
Y_{1|1}
and its
p
-center.
In particular, Theorem
2.6
shows that the Hopf structure descends to
Y_{1|1}^{[p]}
.
There is an appendix at the end of the paper,
which provides a detailed proof of Proposition
2.4
.
In Section
3
, we define the baby Verma modules for
Y_{1|1}^{[p]}
and prove that every finite dimensional irreducible representation is isomorphic to
the simple head of some baby Verma module (Theorem
3.4
).
We also give the necessary and sufficient condition for an irreducible representation to be finite dimensional in terms of modular Drinfeld polynomials (Theorem
3.8
).
Section
4
is devoted to further study about the irreducible representations of
Y_{1|1,\ell}^{[p]}(\sigma)
(Theorem
4.6
).

Throughout this paper,
\mathbbm{k}
denotes an algebraically closed field of characteristic
\Char(\mathbbm{k})=:p>2
.

2.
Restricted Yangians

2.1.
Modular Yangian
Y_{1|1}

The super Yangian
Y_{m|n}
associated to the general linear Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{m|n}
over the complex
field was defined by Nazarov
[
Na1
]
in terms of the
RTT presentation
.
The
Drinfeld-type presentation
was found by Gow in
[
Gow
]
.
In this paper, we only need the special case of
m=n=1
.
For its definition, we fix a
standard parity

|1|=0
and
|2|=1
.
Following
[
Na1
]
(see also
[
CH1
]
),
the super Yangian associated to the general linear Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1}
, denoted by
\Y:=Y_{1|1}
,
is the associated superalgebra over
\mathbbm{k}
with the RTT generators
\{t_{i,j}^{(r)};1\leq i,j\leq 2,r>0\}
subject the following relations:

\displaystyle[t_{i,j}^{(r)},t_{k,l}^{(s)}]=(-1)^{\left|i\right|\left|j\right|+\left|i\right|\left|k\right|+\left|j\right|\left|k\right|}\sum_{t=0}^{{\rm min}\{r,s\}-1}(t_{k,j}^{(t)}t_{i,l}^{(r+s-1-t)}-t_{k,j}^{(r+s-1-t)}t_{i,l}^{(t)}),

(2.1)

where the parity
t_{i,j}^{(r)}
is defined by
|i|+|j|~({\rm mod}~2)
,
and the bracket is understood as the supercommutator.
By convention, we set
t_{i,j}^{(0)}:=\delta_{i,j}
.

The element
t_{i,j}^{(r)}
is called an
even
(
odd
, respectively) element
if its parity is
0
(
1
, respectively). We define the formal power series

\displaystyle t_{i,j}(u):=\sum_{r\geq 0}t_{i,j}^{(r)}u^{-r}\in\Y[[u^{-1}]].

Then these power series for all
1\leq i,j\leq 2
can be collected together into a single matrix

\displaystyle T(u):=\begin{pmatrix}t_{1,1}(u)&t_{1,2}(u)\\
t_{2,1}(u)&t_{2,2}(u)\end{pmatrix}.

It is easily seen that, in terms of the generating series, the initial defining relation (
2.1
) may be rewritten as follows:

\displaystyle(u-v)[t_{i,j}(u),t_{k,l}(v)]=(-1)^{\left|i\right|\left|j\right|+\left|i\right|\left|k\right|+\left|j\right|\left|k\right|}(t_{k,j}(u)t_{i,l}(v)-t_{k,j}(v)t_{i,l}(u)).

(2.2)

We need another set of generators for
\Y
called
Drinfeld generators
.
To define these, we consider the Gauss factorization

T(u)=F(u)D(u)E(u).

This defines power series
d_{i}(u),e(u),f(u)\in\Y[[u^{-1}]]
such that

\displaystyle D(u)=\begin{pmatrix}d_{1}(u)&0\\
0&d_{2}(u)\end{pmatrix},E(u)=\begin{pmatrix}1&e(u)\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},F(u)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
f(u)&1\end{pmatrix}

Then we have that

\displaystyle t_{1,1}(u)=d_{1}(u),\quad t_{2,2}(u)=f(u)d_{1}(u)e(u)+d_{2}(u),

(2.3)

\displaystyle t_{1,2}(u)=d_{1}(u)e(u),\quad t_{2,1}(u)=f(u)d_{1}(u).

(2.4)

The Drinfeld generators are the elements
d_{i}^{(r)},e^{(r)}
and

f^{(r)}
of
\Y
defined from the expansions
d_{i}(u)=\sum_{r\geq 0}d_{i}^{(r)}u^{-r},e(u)=\sum_{r>0}e^{(r)}u^{-r}
and
f(u)=\sum_{r>0}f^{(r)}u^{-r}
.
Also define
d_{i}^{\prime(r)}
from the identity
d_{i}^{\prime}(u)=\sum_{r\geq 0}d_{i}^{\prime(r)}u^{-r}:=d_{i}(u)^{-1}
.

Under the assumption the characteristic
p>2
,
the Drinfeld presentation of the modular Yangian
\Y
is exactly the same as the one obtained by Gow over the complex field (see
[
CH1
, Theorem 3.8]
and
[
Gow
, Theorem 3]
).
The interested reader is referred to
[
CH3
]
for the definition of the super Yangians in characteristic
2
.

Theorem 2.1
.

The super Yangian
\Y
is generated by the elements
\{d_{i}^{(r)},d_{i}^{\prime(r)};1\leq i\leq 2,r>0\}
and
\{e^{(r)},f^{(r)};r>0\}
subject only to the following relations:

\displaystyle d_{i}^{(0)}=1,\quad\sum\limits_{t=0}^{r}d_{i}^{(t)}d_{i}^{\prime(r-t)}=\delta_{r,0};

(2.5)

\displaystyle[d_{i}^{(r)},d_{j}^{(s)}]=0;

(2.6)

\displaystyle[d_{i}^{(r)},e^{(s)}]=\sum\limits_{t=0}^{r-1}d_{i}^{(t)}e^{(r+s-1-t)},\quad[d_{i}^{(r)},f^{(s)}]=-\sum\limits_{t=0}^{r-1}f^{(r+s-1-t)}d_{i}^{(t)};

(2.7)

\displaystyle[e^{(r)},f^{(s)}]=\sum\limits_{t=0}^{r+s-1}d_{1}^{\prime(t)}d_{2}^{(r+s-1-t)};

(2.8)

\displaystyle[e^{(r)},e^{(s)}]=[f^{(r)},f^{(s)}]=0.

(2.9)

Here is the
PBW theorem
for
\Y
, see
[
CH1
, Theorem 3.11]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Ordered supermonomials in the elements

\displaystyle\{d_{i}^{(r)};~1\leq i\leq 2,r>0\}\cup\{e^{(r)},f^{(r)};~r>0\}

(2.10)

taken in any fixed ordering form a basis for
\Y
.

2.2.
Restricted super Yangian
Y_{1|1}^{[p]}

We proceed to recall the description of the
p
-central elements of
\Y
given in
[
CH1
]
.
For
i=1,2
, we define

b_{i}(u):=\sum\limits_{r\geq 0}b_{i}^{(r)}u^{-r}=\begin{cases}d_{1}(u)d_{1}(u-1)\cdots d_{1}(u-p+1)&\quad\text{if}~i=1,\\
d_{2}(u)^{-1}d_{2}(u-1)^{-1}\cdots d_{2}(u-p+1)^{-1}&\quad\text{if}~i=2.\end{cases}

(2.11)

By
[
CH1
, Theorem 4.8]

the elements in

\displaystyle\{b_{i}^{(rp)};~1\leq i\leq 2,r>0\}

(2.12)

are algebraically independent, and lie in the center
Z(\Y)
of
\Y
.
The subalgebra they generated is called
p
-center
of
\Y
and is denoted by
Z_{p}(\Y)
.
According to
[
CH1
, Corollary 4.10]
, the super Yangian
\Y
is free as a module over
Z_{p}(\Y)
with basis
given by the ordered supermonomials in the generators in (
2.10
) in which no exponent is
p
or more for
d_{i}^{(r)}
,
we refer to such monomials as
p
-restricted supermonomials.

We let
Z_{p}(\Y)_{+}
be the maximal ideal of
Z_{p}(\Y)
generated by the elements given in (
2.12
).
The
restricted super Yangian
is defined by

\Y^{[p]}:=\Y/\Y Z_{p}(\Y)_{+}.

Clearly, the images in
\Y^{[p]}
of the
p
-restricted supermonomials
in the Drinfeld generators of
\Y
form a basis of
\Y^{[p]}
.
When working with
\Y^{[p]}
we often
abuse notation by using the same symbols
d_{i}^{(r)},e^{(r)},f^{(r)},t_{i,j}^{(r)}
to refer to the elements of
\Y

and their images in
\Y^{[p]}
.

Remark 2.3
.

In fact, the elements
\{b_{i}^{(r)};~i=1,2,r>0\}
also belong to the
p
-center
Z_{p}(\Y)
(see
[
CH1
, Lemma 4.5, Theorem 4.6]
).
Hence
b_{i}(u)
is equal to
1
in
\Y^{[p]}[[u^{-1}]]
.

2.3.
Hopf algebra structure

It is well known
[
Na1
, p. 125]
that
\Y
is a Hopf superalgebra.
Its comultiplication
\Delta
and antipode
S
are given by

\displaystyle\Delta(t_{i,j}(u))=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{2}t_{i,k}(u)\otimes t_{k,j}(u),\quad S(t_{i,j}(u))=t_{i,j}^{\prime}(u),

(2.13)

where
t_{i,j}^{\prime}(u)
is the
(i,j)
-entry of the matrix
T(u)^{-1}
. The counit send
t_{i,j}(u)\mapsto\delta_{ij}
.
Now, we denote by
I_{p}:=\Y Z_{p}(\Y)_{+}
the ideal defined in Subsection
2.2
.
In this section, we will prove that
I_{p}
is a Hopf ideal of
\Y
.
Then the restricted super Yangian
\Y^{[p]}
inherits from
\Y
the Hopf algebra
structure.

The following Proposition may be viewed as the super version of
[
CHT
, Corollary 2.6]
. We defer the proof to the Appendix
A
.

Proposition 2.4
.

For
i=1,2
,
we have

\displaystyle\Delta(b_{i}(u))=b_{i}(u)\otimes b_{i}(u).

In particular,
\Delta(I_{p})\subseteq I_{p}\otimes\Y+\Y\otimes I_{p}
.

Now we need to determine
S(b_{i}(u))
.
For that, we introduce one more family of elements, we let

b_{i}^{\prime}(u)=\sum\limits_{r\geq 0}b_{i}^{\prime(r)}u^{(-r)}:=\begin{cases}t^{\prime}_{1,1}(u)t^{\prime}_{1,1}(u-1)\cdots t^{\prime}_{1,1}(u-p+1)&\quad\text{if}~i=1,\\
t_{2,2}(u)t_{2,2}(u-1)\cdots t_{2,2}(u-p+1)&\quad\text{if}~i=2.\end{cases}

Proposition 2.5
.

We have
S(I_{p})\subseteq I_{p}
.

Proof.

Since
S
is an anti-antomorphism of
\Y

[
Na1
, p. 126]
, (
2.3
) and (
2.6
) imply that

S(b_{1}(u))=S(t_{1,1}(u)t_{1,1}(u-1)\cdots t_{1,1}(u-p+1))=b_{1}^{\prime}(u).

Morwover, the Gauss decomposition yields
d_{2}(u)^{-1}=t_{2,2}^{\prime}(u)
.
By the same token, we have

S(b_{2}^{\prime}(u))=S(t_{2,2}(u)t_{2,2}(u-1)\cdots t_{2,2}(u-p+1))=b_{2}(u).

Using the same proof of
[
CH1
, Lemma 5.6 and Theorem 5.7]
, one can show that
the
p
-center
Z_{p}(\Y)
of
\Y
can be generated by
\{b_{1}^{(r)},b_{2}^{\prime(r)};~r>0\}

or
\{b_{1}^{\prime(r)},b_{2}^{(r)};~r>0\}
. The assertion follows.
∎

The main result of this subsection reads:

Theorem 2.6
.

The ideal
I_{p}
is a hopf ideal of
\Y
.
In particular,
\Y^{[p]}
inherits from
\Y
the Hopf algebra
structure.

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of Proposition
2.4
and Proposition
2.5
.
∎

2.4.
Evaluation homomorphism

Let
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1})
be the universal enveloping algebra of the Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1}
.
There exists a surjective homomorphism

\ev:\Y\rightarrow U(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1})

called the
evaluation homomorphism
(see for example
[
Gow
, (7)]
),
defined by

\displaystyle\ev(t_{i,j}(u)):=\delta_{i,j}+(-1)^{|i|}e_{i,j}u^{-1},

(2.14)

where
e_{i,j}\in\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1}
is the elementary matrix.
Since we are in characteristic
p
,
the Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1}
admits a natural structure of
restricted Lie superalgebra
.
That is, the even subalgebra
(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1})_{\bar{0}}
is a restricted Lie algebra with
p
-map
(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1})_{\bar{0}}\rightarrow(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1})_{\bar{0}}
sending
x\mapsto x^{[p]}
, which is defined on a basis of
(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1})_{\bar{0}}
by the rule
e_{i,i}^{[p]}=e_{i,i}
.
General theory (cf.
[
WZ
, Section 2]
) implies that
the element
e_{i,i}^{p}-e_{i,i}\in U(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1})
is central.
Let
J_{p}
be the ideal of
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1})
generated by the even central elements
\{e_{i,i}^{p}-e_{i,i};~i=1,2\}
.
The quotient algebra
U^{[p]}(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1}):=U(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1})/J_{p}
is called the
restricted enveloping algebra
of
\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1}
.

Lemma 2.7
.

The evaluation homomorphism
\ev
factors to a surjective algebra homomorphism

\ev^{[p]}:{\rm Y}^{[p]}\rightarrow U^{[p]}(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1}).

Proof.

It suffices to show that the images of the coefficients of
b_{1}(u)
and
b_{2}^{\prime}(u)
under
\ev
are contained in
J_{p}
.
Since
d_{1}(u)=t_{1,1}(u)
(
2.3
),
we have

\displaystyle\ev(b_{1}(u))

\displaystyle=\ev((t_{1,1}(u)t_{1,1}(u-1)\cdots t_{1,1}(u-p+1))

\displaystyle=(1+e_{1,1}u^{-1})(1+e_{1,1}(u-1)^{-1})\cdots(1+e_{1,1}(u-p+1)^{-1})

\displaystyle=\frac{u+e_{1,1}}{u}\frac{u-1+e_{1,1}}{u-1}\cdots\frac{u-p+1+e_{1,1}}{u-p+1}.

Write

\ev(b_{1}(u))=1+g^{(1)}u^{-1}+g^{(2)}u^{-2}+\cdots.

Here the coefficient
g^{(r)}
is a polynomial of
e_{1,1}
.
For each
r>0
, the above expression implies that
g^{(r)}(i)=0
for all
i\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
This yields
(e_{1,1}^{p}-e_{1,1})\mid g^{(r)}
. As a result,
g^{(r)}\in J_{p}
.
One argues similarly for
b_{2}^{\prime}(u)
.
∎

Using the evaluation homomorphism
\ev
,
we see that any representation of
\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1}
can be regarded as a representation of
\Y
by pullback, and any irreducible representation of
\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1}
remains irreducible over
\Y
.
By the same token, Lemma
2.7

allows us to equip any
U^{[p]}(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1})
-module with a structure of
\Y^{[p]}
-module.

3.
Representations of
{\rm Y}^{[p]}

In this section, we study the representations of the restricted super Yangian
{\rm Y}^{[p]}
.
To describe the finite dimensional irreducible modules of
{\rm Y}^{[p]}
,
we need to construct analogues of highest weight
representations in characteristic zero (see
[
Z1
, Section III]
).

3.1.
Baby Verma modules

We define

{\bf I}_{\mathbb{N}}:=\{(i_{1},i_{2},\dots);~i_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}~\text{and only finitely many are non-zero}\}.

Given
I=(i_{1},i_{2},\cdots)\in{\bf I}_{\mathbb{N}}
, we let

f^{I}:=\prod\limits_{r>0}(f^{(r)})^{i_{r}}

and similarly we define elements
d_{1}^{I},d_{2}^{I}
and
e^{I}
.
Consider two subsets of
{\bf I}_{\mathbb{N}}

{\bf I}_{0}:=\{(i_{1},i_{2},\dots)\in{\bf I}_{\mathbb{N}};~0\leq i_{k}<p\}\quad{\bf I}_{1}:=\{(i_{1},i_{2},\dots)\in{\bf I}_{\mathbb{N}};~0\leq i_{k}<2\},

so that

\displaystyle\{f^{I_{1}}d_{1}^{I_{2}}d_{2}^{I_{3}}e^{I_{4}};~I_{2},I_{3}\in{\bf I}_{0},I_{1},I_{4}\in{\bf I}_{1}\}

(3.1)

is the PBW basis of
{\rm Y}^{[p]}
(see Section
2.2
).

Definition
.

A formal power series
f(u)\in\mathbbm{k}[[u^{-1}]]
is called
restricted
, provided

f(u)f(u-1)\cdots f(u-p+1)=1,

which implies that
f(u)\in 1+u^{-1}\mathbbm{k}[[u^{-1}]]
.
Given two formal series

\lambda_{i}(u)=1+\lambda_{i}^{(1)}u^{-1}+\lambda_{i}^{(2)}u^{-1}+\cdots,\quad i=1,2,

we say that the tuple
\lambda(u):=(\lambda_{1}(u),\lambda_{2}(u))
is restricted if both
\lambda_{1}(u)
and
\lambda_{2}(u)
are restricted.

Let
\lambda(u)=(\lambda_{1}(u),\lambda_{2}(u))
be a restricted tuple.
We define the
baby Verma module

Z^{[p]}(\lambda(u))
corresponding to
\lambda(u)
as the quotient of
{\rm Y}^{[p]}
by the left ideal generated by the elements
e^{(r)}
with
r>0
, and by
d_{i}^{(r)}-\lambda_{i}^{(r)}
with
i=1,2
and
r>0
.
Denote by
1_{\lambda(u)}
the image of the element
1\in{\rm Y}^{[p]}
in the quotient.
Clearly,
Z^{[p]}(\lambda(u))
is a cyclic module and
Z^{[p]}(\lambda(u))={\rm Y}^{[p]}.1_{\lambda(u)}
.
We put
\Y^{[p],-}:=\Span_{\mathbbm{k}}\{f^{I};~I\in{\bf I}_{1}\}
and
(\Y^{[p],-})_{+}:=\Span_{\mathbbm{k}}\{f^{I};~I\in{\bf I}_{1},I\neq(0,0,\dots)\}
.
Owing to (
3.1
), there is an isomorphism

Z^{[p]}(\lambda(u))\cong\Y^{[p],-}\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}}\mathbbm{k}1_{\lambda(u)}

of vector spaces.

Alternatively, the baby Verma modules can be described in terms of the RTT presentation of
\Y
(cf.
[
Mol1
, Proposition 3.2.2]
).

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\lambda(u)=(\lambda_{1}(u),\lambda_{2}(u))
be restricted.
The baby Verma module
Z^{[p]}(\lambda(u))
equals to the quotient of
{\rm Y}^{[p]}
by the left ideal
J
which is generated by the elements
t_{1,2}^{(r)}
with
r>0
and by
by
t_{i,i}^{(r)}-\lambda_{i}^{(r)}
with
i=1,2
and
r>0
.

Proof.

The proof, which uses (
2.3
) and (
2.4
), is similar to the proof of
[
CHT
, Proposition 3.3]
, and will be skipped here.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Z^{[p]}(\lambda(u))
has a unique maximal submodule.

Proof.

We shall prove that any proper submodule
M
of
Z^{[p]}(\lambda(u))
is contained in the vector space
(\Y^{[p],-})_{+}.1_{\lambda(u)}
.
Suppose that
M\nsubseteq(\Y^{[p],-})_{+}.1_{\lambda(u)}
, then there is a non-zero element
y=(1-x).1_{\lambda(u)}\in M
and
x\in(\Y^{[p],-})_{+}
.
Now (
2.9
) implies that
x
is nilpotent.
Assume that
x^{n}=0
for some positive integer
n
.
We have for
x^{\prime}:=1+x+\cdots+x^{n-1}
,

Let

Lemma 3.3
.

If

is a finite dimensional representation of

,
then the space

is non-zero.

Proof.

Suppose that

.
Since

(
2.9
), there exists a non-zero vector
v_{1}\in L
such that
e^{(1)}.v_{1}=0
.
By assumption, we can find a positive integer

such that

for all
1\leq r\leq n_{1}
and
e^{(n_{1}+1)}.v_{1}\neq 0
.
Setting
v_{2}:=e^{(n_{1}+1)}.v_{1}
, we have
e^{(n_{1}+1)}.v_{2}=(e^{(n_{1}+1)})^{2}.v_{1}=0
.
For
1\leq i\leq n_{1}
, again the relation (
2.9
) yields

e^{(i)}.v_{2}=e^{(i)}e^{(n_{1}+1)}.v_{1}=-e^{(n_{1}+1)}e^{(i)}.v_{1}=0.

This shows that
e^{(r)}.v_{2}=0
for all

,
so that we can find a positive integer

such that

for all

and
e^{(n_{2}+1)}.v_{2}\neq 0
.

Repeat the above argument, we obtain two sequences
v_{1},v_{2},\dots
and

which satisfy (i)
v_{i}\neq 0
; (ii)
e^{(r)}.v_{i}=0
for all

; (iii)
e^{(n_{i}+1)}v_{i}\neq 0
for every positive integer

. Let

be an arbitrary positive integer.
Assume that

. It follows from (ii) that

, and property (iii) gives

.
By the same token, we obtain all

.
Therefore

are linearly independent.
As

is finite dimensional, we arrive at a contradiction.
∎

Proposition 3.5

Theorem 3.8
.

The irreducible representation
L^{[p]}(\lambda(u))=L^{[p]}(\lambda_{1}(u),\lambda_{2}(u))
is finite dimensional if and only if there exists monic polynomials
P_{1}(u)
and
P_{2}(u)
in

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{1}(u)}{\lambda_{2}(u)}=\frac{P_{1}(u)}{P_{2}(u)},

(3.9)

where the polynomials
P_{1}(u)
and
P_{2}(u)
are of the same degree and have no common roots.

Proof.

Suppose that the representation
L^{[p]}(\lambda_{1}(u),\lambda_{2}(u))
is finite dimensional. Then by Lemma
3.6
we can find a formal series
f(u)
such that

\displaystyle f(u)\lambda_{1}(u)=(1+\lambda_{1}^{(1)}u^{-1})\cdots(1+\lambda_{1}^{(k)}u^{-1}),

\displaystyle f(u)\lambda_{2}(u)=(1+\lambda_{2}^{(1)}u^{-1})\cdots(1+\lambda_{2}^{(k)}u^{-1}),

for some
k\geq 0
and some scalars
\lambda_{i}^{(r)}
.
It follows that

\frac{\lambda_{1}(u)}{\lambda_{2}(u)}=\frac{f(u)\lambda_{1}(u)}{f(u)\lambda_{2}(u)}=\frac{(1+\lambda_{1}^{(1)}u^{-1})\cdots(1+\lambda_{1}^{(k)}u^{-1})}{(1+\lambda_{2}^{(1)}u^{-1})\cdots(1+\lambda_{2}^{(k)}u^{-1})}.

Without loss of generality, we may
assume further that
\lambda_{1}^{(i)}\neq\lambda_{2}^{(j)}
for any
1\leq i,j\leq k
.
Then the polynomials

P_{1}(u):=(u+\lambda_{1}^{(1)})\cdots(u+\lambda_{1}^{(k)})\quad\text{and}\quad P_{2}(u):=(u+\lambda_{2}^{(1)})\cdots(u+\lambda_{2}^{(k)})

satisfy (
3.9
).

Conversely, suppose (
3.9
) holds for polynomials

P_{1}(u)=(u+\mu_{1}^{(1)})\cdots(u+\mu_{1}^{(s)})\quad\text{and}\quad P_{2}(u)=(u-\mu_{2}^{(1)})\cdots(u-\mu_{2}^{(s)}).

Set

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(u):=(1+\mu_{1}^{(1)}u^{-1})\cdots(1+\mu_{1}^{(s)}u^{-1}),

\displaystyle\mu_{2}(u):=(1-\mu_{2}^{(1)}u^{-1})\cdots(1-\mu_{2}^{(s)}u^{-1}).

By construction we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mu_{1}(u)}{\mu_{2}(u)}=\frac{P_{1}(u)}{P_{2}(u)}.

(3.10)

For each
1\leq r\leq s
, we consider the irreducibe
\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1}
-module

which is generated by

and the module structure is given by

\displaystyle e_{i,i}.\xi_{r}=\mu_{i}^{(r)}\xi_{r},i=1,2,\quad e_{1,2}.\xi_{r}=0.

(3.11)

Since
\mu_{1}^{(r)}+\mu_{2}^{(r)}\neq 0
by assumption,
the module

is two-dimensional with basis
\{\xi_{r},e_{2,1}.\xi_{r}\}
.
We can regard them as
\Y
-modules via (
2.14
) consider the tensor product module

L:=L(\mu_{1}^{(1)},\mu_{2}^{(1)})\otimes\cdots\otimes L(\mu_{1}^{(s)},\mu_{2}^{(s)}).

Obviously, this module is finite dimensional.
We put
\xi:=\xi_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\xi_{s}
.
Using the comultiplication (
2.13
) in conjunction
with (
3.11
) one can show by direct computation that

t_{i,i}(u).\xi=\mu_{i}(u)\xi,i=1,2,\quad t_{1,2}(u).\xi=0.

Let

be the cyclic span of

.
Twisting the action of
\Y
on

by the automorphism (
3.8
) with

,
we obtain a
\Y
-module which will be denoted by

.
Clearly,

is also generated by

.
By abuse of notation, we still write

.
Thanks to (

) and (

), we have

t_{1,1}(u).\xi=\frac{\mu_{1}(u)}{\mu_{2}(u)}\xi=\frac{\lambda_{1}(u)}{\lambda_{2}(u)}\xi,\quad t_{2,2}(u).\xi=\xi,\quad t_{1,2}(u).\xi=0.

Notice that

is restricted.
This implies that

,
and there results a surjective homomorphism

As a result,

\displaystyle d_{1}(u)d_{1}(u-1)\cdots d_{1}(u-p+1).v_{+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{k}\prod\limits_{j=0}^{p-1}(u+a_{i}-j)}{(\prod\limits_{j=0}^{p-1}(u-j))^{k}}v_{+}.

We write

Lemma A.2
.

For any positive integer

Proposition A.3
.

(=Proposition
2.4
)
We have

\displaystyle\Delta(b_{i}(u))=b_{i}(u)\otimes b_{i}(u).

In particular,
\Delta(I_{p})\subseteq I_{p}\otimes\Y+\Y\otimes I_{p}
.

Proof.

Recall from (
2.3
) that

t_{1,1}(u)=d_{1}(u)
,
so that
b_{1}(u)=t_{1,1}(u)\cdots t_{1,1}(u-p+1)
.
Taking in Lemma
A.2

n:=p
gives

\Delta(b_{1}(u))=b_{1}(u)\otimes b_{1}(u).

We now consider the case
i=2
.
In view of
[
Gow
, Proposition 1]
,
the map

\zeta:\Y\rightarrow\Y;~t_{i,j}(u)\mapsto t^{\prime}_{3-i,3-j}(u)

gives an algebra isomorphism.
It is easy to verify that
\zeta(t^{\prime}_{i,j}(u))=t_{3-i,3-j}(u)
.
Moreover,
let
P:\Y\otimes\Y\rightarrow\Y\otimes\Y
be the map given by

P(x\otimes y)=y\otimes x(-1)^{|x||y|}

for all homogeneous elements
x,y\in\Y
.
Then we have
\Delta=(\zeta\otimes\zeta)\circ P\circ\Delta\circ\zeta
(cf.
[
Gow
, Proposition 2]
).
As
t^{\prime}_{2,2}(u)=d_{2}(u)^{-1}
(cf.
[
Gow
, (14)]
),
we obtain

\displaystyle\Delta(b_{2}(u))=

\displaystyle\Delta(t^{\prime}_{2,2}(u)\cdots t^{\prime}_{2,2}(u-p+1))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\zeta\otimes\zeta)\circ P\circ\Delta\circ\zeta(t^{\prime}_{2,2}(u)\cdots t^{\prime}_{2,2}(u-p+1))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\zeta\otimes\zeta)\circ P\circ\Delta(b_{1}(u)).

Remember that we already proved
\Delta(b_{1}(u))=b_{1}(u)\otimes b_{1}(u)
.
Observing that
\zeta(b_{1}(u))=b_{2}(u)
,
we thus obtain
\Delta(b_{2}(u))=b_{2}(u)\otimes b_{2}(u)
,
as desired.

∎
