ANN-driven analysis of Cattaneo–Christov heat transfer and entropy generation in Maxwell hybrid nanofluid flow over a vertical cone

Bhuvana M, Durgaprasad P

Abstract

IntroductionThis research investigates Cattaneo-Christov non-Fourier heat transfer and entropy generation in Maxwell hybrid nanofluid flow over a non-Darcy porous conical surface using numerical, sensitivity, and Artificial neural networks (ANN) approaches.MethodsA coupled momentum-energy model that includes fluid relaxation, magnetic field effects, nanoparticle loading, porous resistance, and thermal relaxation is transformed using similarity variables and solved by a boundary-value numerical method. Sobol' global sensitivity analysis is employed to quantify the direct and interaction effects of the governing parameters on skin friction, Nusselt number, and entropy generation.ResultsThe analysis identifies the porous-medium parameter as the dominant contributor to skin friction and heat transfer, while its contribution to entropy generation remains substantial at both buoyancy conditions. The total-order indices further reveal increased parameter interactions in entropy generation at higher buoyancy. The artificial neural network, trained using the numerically generated dataset, accurately predicts skin friction, Nusselt number, and entropy generation, with test R2 values of 0.99479, 0.99642, and 0.99033, respectively, along with low prediction errors.DiscussionThe combined numerical-Sobol' sensitivity-ANN framework enables rapid evaluation of nonlinear viscoelastic heat-transfer systems. The results underscore the utility of Maxwell hybrid nanofluids in thermally non-Fourier porous media from an engineering perspective and provide a computational framework for evaluating their thermal performance.

- The thermal behavior of Maxwell viscoelastic fluids, especially blood-based hybrid nanofluids, remains insufficiently understood when exposed to magnetic fields, thermal radiation, and porous-medium resistance in conical geometries. Existing research treats Non-Fourier heat transport, entropy generation, hybrid nanoparticles, and MHD porous flow mostly in isolation, leaving a gap in understanding their combined effects on thermodynamic performance.

To address this gap, the present work:

(iv) develops an ANN surrogate model to validate numerical results and enable fast prediction of key engineering quantities.

At a fixed M, the velocity is higher for Gr = 1.5 because the larger buoyancy force assists the upward motion of the heated fluid along the vertical cone. Therefore, the velocity distribution is governed by the competition between magnetic resistance and buoyancy-driven flow. Sobol' sensitivity analysis

To statistically evaluate the influence of the governing parameters on the skin-friction coefficient, Nusselt number, and entropy generation at the two considered buoyancy conditions, Sobol' sensitivity analysis () was employed. The first-order index represents the direct contribution of an individual parameter to the output variance, whereas the total-order index accounts for both its direct influence and its interactions with the remaining parameters. The analysis was performed independently at Gr = 0.5 and Gr = 1.5. For the skin-friction coefficient and Nusselt number, the base Sobol sample size was N = 250, whereas N = 220 was used for entropy generation. Independent sampling matrices were generated with a fixed random seed of 7, and each sampled parameter combination was evaluated independently to ensure that the results were unaffected by the order of evaluation. The first- and total-order indices were evaluated using the Jansen estimators. ANN-based surrogate prediction

An artificial neural network (ANN) was employed as a surrogate model to predict the principal engineering responses obtained from the numerical solution of the Maxwell hybrid nanofluid flow over a cone. The ANN was developed to establish the nonlinear relationship between the governing flow and thermal parameters and the corresponding values of the skin-friction coefficient, Nusselt number, and entropy generation. The numerical solutions generated from the boundary-value model were used as reference targets for ANN training and performance assessment. Therefore, the ANN serves as a computational predictor of the numerical responses rather than an independent validation of the physical model. The overall workflow of the ANN-based prediction framework is presented in , and the architecture of the developed ANN model is illustrated in .

ANN configuration and training procedure

The numerical dataset generated from the boundary-value solutions was used to construct the ANN models for predicting the skin-friction coefficient Rex1/2Cfx, Nusselt number Rex-1/2Nux, and entropy generation Sgen, 0. The selected governing parameters were supplied to the input layer, while each response quantity was predicted through the output layer. The data were normalized before training to reduce differences in the scales of the input variables and to support stable network learning. The ANN architecture was selected through trial evaluations of the hidden-layer configuration to obtain accurate predictions without unnecessary network complexity. The mean squared error (MSE) was employed as the loss measure, and the network weights were updated using the Adam optimization algorithm. During training, the validation data were used to monitor the predictive behavior of the network and identify the epoch associated with the minimum validation error. The independent test data were subsequently used to evaluate the ability of the trained ANN to predict previously unseen numerical samples.

ANN dataset generation and parameter sampling

The ANN dataset was generated from numerical solutions of the transformed boundary-value problem using independent uniform random sampling of the governing parameters. The sampling ranges were 0 ≤ M ≤ 2, 0.01 ≤ K ≤ 1, 0 ≤ F* ≤ 1, 0 ≤ Λ ≤ 0.05, 0 ≤ Gr ≤ 2, 0 ≤ λ ≤ 1, 0 ≤ R ≤ 2, 0 ≤ β ≤ 0.05, 0 ≤ ξ ≤ 1, 10−4 ≤ Br ≤ 5 × 10−3, 1 ≤ α1 ≤ 2, 0 ≤ φ1 ≤ 0.02, and 0 ≤ φ2 ≤ 0.02. A fixed random seed was used to ensure reproducible parameter sampling. Each sampled combination was solved through a continuation-based boundary-value procedure, with an initial computational domain of 0 ≤ η ≤ 8 and adaptive domain extension when required. Parameter combinations that failed to converge or satisfy the far-field tolerance of 2 × 10−3 were discarded, and the sampling process was continued until 1,500 valid numerical solutions were retained. The corresponding Rex12Cf, Re-(12) Nux, and Sgen, 0 values were extracted from the converged solutions and used as the target responses for ANN training, validation, and testing.

ANN methodology and configuration

The numerical dataset contained 1,500 samples and was divided into training, validation, and testing subsets using a nominal ratio of 70:15:15. Owing to integer sample allocation, the resulting subsets contained 1,049 training samples, 226 validation samples, and 225 testing samples, corresponding to 69.9%, 15.1%, and 15.0%, respectively. The same sample indices were retained for all three ANN models to ensure a consistent basis for comparison.

Three independent single-output ANN models were developed to estimate the skin-friction response, heat-transfer response, and wall entropy-generation response. Each network contained 13 input neurons, two hidden layers with 64 neurons in each layer, and one output neuron. Rectified Linear Unit (ReLU) activation was employed in the hidden layers, whereas a linear activation was used at the output layer. The three predicted responses were Rex1/2Cfx, Rex-1/2Nux, and Sgen,0.

Before training, the input variables were standardized using the mean and standard deviation calculated only from the training subset. The corresponding target variable of each ANN was standardized in the same manner using its training data. This procedure prevents information from the validation and testing subsets from entering the scaling stage. Mean squared error (MSE) was used as the loss function, and the network weights were updated using the Adam optimizer.

Training was initiated with a learning rate of 0.001 and a batch size of 32, with a maximum limit of 500 epochs. A ReduceLROnPlateau learning-rate scheduler was used with a reduction factor of 0.5 and a patience of 30 epochs. Early stopping with a patience of 80 epochs was applied according to the validation loss, and the network state corresponding to the minimum validation loss was restored for the final predictions. A random seed of 42 was maintained to support reproducibility.

The best validation epochs were 265, 177, and 141 for Rex1/2Cfx,Rex-1/2Nux, and Sgen,0, respectively. Training terminated at epochs 345, 257, and 221 after the early-stopping criterion was reached. The corresponding minimum validation MSE values during training were 0.00310818, 0.00449019, and 0.02355748, respectively. These training-loss values refer to the standardized target space; the final prediction metrics reported below were calculated after transforming the ANN outputs back to their original physical scale.

The comparison was performed using the coefficient of determination (R2), root mean squared error (RMSE), and mean absolute error (MAE) as shown in .

Conclusion

In this work, the entropy generation by the convection of a Maxwell hybrid nanofluid (blood/Au–Cu) over an impermeable vertical cone embedded in a non-Darcy porous medium with the Cattaneo–Christov heat flux model is investigated. The numerical investigation is complemented by Sobol' global sensitivity analysis and ANN prediction to identify the influential parameters and assess the predictive capability of the developed model.

The most significant findings are summarized as follows:

- The magnetic field raised the temperature in the boundary layer while reducing fluid velocity due to the resistive Lorentz force.
- The porous-medium resistance strongly influences momentum and thermal transport by restricting fluid motion and modifying the associated heat-transfer behavior.
- Velocity and thermal slip alter the near-wall transport behavior and consequently affect the surface friction and heat-transfer characteristics.
- Entropy generation increases with the temperature-ratio parameter and Brinkman number, indicating greater contributions from thermal and viscous irreversibilities.
- The variation in the Bejan number reflects the relative contributions of heat-transfer and fluid-friction irreversibilities to the total entropy generation.
- The addition of Au and Cu nanoparticles improves the thermal transport characteristics of the base fluid; however, the associated variation in entropy generation should also be considered when selecting the nanoparticle concentration.
- Sobol' global sensitivity analysis identifies the porosity parameter K as the dominant factor governing the skin-friction coefficient and Nusselt number. For entropy generation, K and the temperature-ratio parameter make the major contributions within the investigated parameter ranges, while the remaining parameters exhibit comparatively smaller sensitivity contributions.
- The dominant influence of K provides a useful practical guideline for thermal-system design. Adjustment of the permeability and pore structure of the porous medium can be used to regulate the balance between flow resistance and heat-transfer performance. Hence, porous-medium characteristics should be given priority when selecting or optimizing operating conditions.
- The ANN surrogate models accurately predict the skin-friction coefficient and Nusselt number, with test R2 values of 0.99479 and 0.99642, respectively, demonstrating reliable predictive performance on unseen data.
- The ANN model for entropy generation achieves a test R2 value of 0.99033, demonstrating reliable prediction of thermodynamic irreversibility within the investigated parameter ranges.

Overall, the combined numerical, sensitivity, and ANN analyses identify the principal parameters governing momentum transport, heat transfer, and irreversibility. In particular, the sensitivity results provide a practical basis for selecting porous-medium characteristics to achieve an appropriate balance between heat-transfer enhancement and flow resistance.

Physical applications

The present study demonstrates the potential of combining Maxwell hybrid nanofluids with non-Fourier heat transfer in porous conical systems to regulate thermal transport and irreversibility characteristics. Using ANN surrogates provides a rapid and easy means of obtaining predictions of skin friction, Nusselt number and entropy generation without re-running the numerical computations. These capabilities are particularly applicable to nano-enhanced thermal management systems and compact heat exchangers involving viscoelastic fluids.

Prospects for the future and extensions of research

Although the ANN provides accurate predictions, its performance is limited by the numerical dataset used for training. Future research may consider physics-informed neural networks, larger parametric datasets, and unsteady, three-dimensional, and temperature-dependent models to extend the applicability of the predictive framework to more complex thermal systems.
