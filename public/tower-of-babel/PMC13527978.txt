FBXW7 induces apoptosis of nucleus pulposus cells to mediate intervertebral disc degeneration by regulating JNK expression
Authors: Yi Gao, Yuning Zhu, Yuting Gong, Rui Chen, Quan Zhou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo investigate the association between FBXW7 and JNK expression and their roles in regulating nucleus pulposus cell (NPCs) apoptosis in intervertebral disc degeneration (IDD).MethodsFirstly, the differentially expressed genes between normal and IDD groups were screened by bioinformatics technology. Subsequently, clinical samples were collected to verify differential gene expression by qPCR and Western blot. Secondly, the effect of FBXW7 on apoptosis of NPCs was verified in vitro. Finally, the therapeutic effect of FBXW7 knockdown on degenerative intervertebral discs was verified by in vivo experiments.ResultsBioinformatics analysis showed that FBXW7 expression was significantly increased in highly degenerative nucleus pulposus (NP) tissues. Meanwhile, qPCR and Western Blot confirmed that FBXW7 and JNK were upregulated in clinically degenerative NP tissues. On the one hand, transfection of FBXW7 siRNA and JNK siRNA significantly inhibited the apoptosis of NPCs. On the other hand, FBXW7 overexpression upregulated JNK expression, while transfection of JNK siRNA reversed the FBXW7-mediated apoptosis-promoting effect. In vivo experiments further confirmed that FBXW7 shRNA could alleviate the degree of IDD.ConclusionsHigh expression of FBXW7 promotes NPCs apoptosis and accelerates IDD progression by upregulating JNK expression. This study first demonstrates that FBXW7 expression is positively correlated with JNK expression in IDD tissues.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s13018-026-07028-3.Keywords: Intervertebral disc degeneration, FBXW7, JNK, Apoptosis of cells, Nucleus pulposus cells

Introduction

IDD is one of the most common degenerative diseases in orthopedics. About 80% of adults in the world will experience IDD-related low back pain in their lifetime, of which 10–15% need surgical intervention due to worsening symptoms. It seriously affects the quality of life of patients and increases the medical burden of society [1]. As a load-bearing buffer structure between the vertebral bodies, the intervertebral disc is mainly composed of nucleus pulposus (NP) and annulus fibrosus (AF) tissues (together with cartilaginous endplates) and is characterized by a rich extracellular matrix (ECM) network that maintains hydration and mechanical function [2, 3]. Recent studies indicate that, besides apoptosis, other regulated cell-death modes such as ferroptosis and cellular senescence are also involved in IDD and are closely related to inflammatory and metabolic stress [4].

At present, the clinical treatment for IDD is mainly conservative treatment (such as drugs and physiotherapy) and surgical treatment (such as discectomy), but the former can only relieve symptoms, and the latter can not reverse the degenerative process. Moreover, conservative management often fails, and current surgical approaches (e.g., discectomy/arthrodesis) remain the most predictably effective options, highlighting the unmet need for disease-modifying biological strategies [3, 5].Therefore, in-depth understanding of the core pathological mechanism of IDD is the key to the development of targeted therapy strategies. It has been proved that excessive apoptosis of NPCs is the core pathological link of IDD. Apoptosis of NPCs can directly reduce the synthesis and increase the degradation of extracellular matrix (aggrecan and collagen Ⅱ), destroy the stability of the structure of intervertebral disc, and ultimately promote the progression of IDD [6]. In this context, inflammatory cytokines (e.g., IL-1β, TNF-α, IL-6) and oxidative stress in degenerative discs can synergistically disturb ECM homeostasis and trigger multiple regulated cell-death pathways in NPCs [7].

FBXW7 is a key member of the F-box family. As the core component of E3 ubiquitin ligase, its gene is located in the q31.3 region of human chromosome 4. The core function of FBXW7 is to specifically recognize the phosphorylation site of the target protein through the WD40 repeat domain, mediate the ubiquitination of the target protein and its degradation by the proteasome, thereby regulating key intracellular signaling pathways [8–10]. Previous studies have confirmed that FBXW7 can inhibit tumor proliferation and metastasis by degrading oncoproteins such as c-Myc and Notch1 in the field of cancer [11, 12]. In degenerative diseases such as Alzheimer’s disease and Parkinson’s disease, FBXW7 can participate in cell division, differentiation and apoptosis by regulating Wnt/β-catenin, JNK, RhoA and other signaling pathways [13–16]. Hoeck et al. found that FBXW7 can mediate neural progenitor cell apoptosis by regulating JNK signaling pathway [17]. However, whether the regulatory relationship of FBXW7 on JNK pathway exists in NPCs has not been reported.

As a key branch of the MAPK family, JNK (c-Jun N-terminal kinase) is the core regulator of cellular stress response [18]. Mechanical stress, oxidative stress and inflammatory factors can activate JNK signaling pathway, promote apoptosis and accelerate extracellular matrix degradation of NPCs [19]. Notably, intervertebral disc cells exhibit complex mechanobiological responses to loading, and mechanical loading is considered an important contributor to disc degeneration, supporting the relevance of stress-responsive signaling (such as JNK) in IDD progression [20]. Liu et al. confirmed by in vitro cell experiments and animal models that inhibition of JNK activation could significantly reduce the apoptosis rate of NPCs and delay the progression of IDD [21], suggesting that JNK is a potential target for IDD treatment.

Although the phenomenon of FBXW7 regulating JNK pathway has been confirmed in neural progenitor cells [17], and the proapoptotic role of JNK pathway in IDD is clear [21], the following key scientific questions remain unresolved: whether the expression changes of FBXW7 in human nucleus pulposus tissues are related to the degree of degeneration; Whether FBXW7 affects NPCs apoptosis by regulating JNK expression; Whether FBXW7 silencing can attenuate IDD progression by reducing JNK expression.

In view of the above gaps, this study systematically explored the role and molecular mechanism of FBXW7-JNK axis in IDD by integrating bioinformatics analysis, clinical sample verification, cell experiments and animal models, aiming to provide new theoretical basis and potential targets for the targeted therapy of IDD. Integrative transcriptomic analyses have proven valuable in identifying pivotal dysregulated genes associated with disc degeneration severity and related inflammatory and stress responses, supporting our bioinformatics-first strategy [22].

Materials and methods

Bioinformatics analysis

The GSE70362, GSE150408, and GSE124272 datasets were obtained from the GEO database. Batch effects were corrected using the ComBat method from the sva package. Adjusted p‑values were calculated using the Benjamini–Hochberg method [23, 24]. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified using the thresholds |log2FC| > 0.5 and adjusted p‑value < 0.05. Volcano plots were generated using the ggplot2 package, and heatmaps were created with the pheatmap package to validate the differential expression of FBXW7. Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analysis of the DEGs were performed using the clusterProfiler package [25].

Clinical sample collection

From September 2022 to September 2024, 20 tissues samples of nucleus pulposus (10 males and 10 females) from The Affiliated Huai’an Hospital of Xuzhou Medical University were included. According to Pfirrmann classification, they were divided into low-grade degeneration group (Ⅰ-Ⅲ grade, 10 cases) and high-grade degeneration group (Ⅳ-Ⅴ grade, 10 cases) (Table 1). Nucleus pulposus samples were obtained from patients with IDD or thoracolumbar fracture without severe spinal disease. Fracture samples were included to provide non‑degenerated or mildly degenerated controls, and patients with ankylosing spondylitis, lumbar spondylolisthesis, spinal stenosis, spinal tumors, and other patients were excluded [26]. All samples were obtained by spine surgeons during the operation after the informed consent of the patients and their families was signed. This study was approved by the ethics committee of the hospital (approval number: HEYLL202291), in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Isolation and culture of NPCs

The nucleus pulposus tissues obtained during the operation was transferred to the laboratory within 30 min, washed repeatedly with PBS to remove blood and connective tissues, cut into 1–2 mm³ pieces, and added to DMEM medium (KGI Bio, Nanjing, China) containing 0.25% collagenase II (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). The digestion was carried out at 37 ℃ for 5 h. The digested cell suspension was centrifuged at 1200 rpm for 5 min, the precipitate was resuspended in DMEM medium, then added with complete medium (DMEM containing 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, Abcam) and double antibody), and cultured in an incubator with 5% CO2 at 37 ℃. The medium was changed every 48 h, and the cells were passaged when the confluence degree reached 80–90%. The cells were cultured to the third passage for subsequent experiments.

Cellular immunofluorescence

First, the NPCs were washed with PBS, then fixed with 4% formaldehyde, permeabilized with 0.5% Triton X-100, and blocked with 5% BSA. Next, an antibody was added and cultured at 4 °C overnight, and then FITC-labeled fluorescent secondary antibody was added at room temperature in the dark. DAPI (Beyotime, Guangzhou, China) was used for cell nucleus staining. Finally, images were captured using a confocal laser scanning microscope (Leica STELLARIS 5 from Germany).

Transfection

The cells were cultured until the cell confluence reached 60–90% for transfection, and the cell medium was replaced with serum-free DMEM medium. The target siRNA was mixed in proportion with RNA TransMate transfection reagent (Sangon, Shanghai, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions and incubated for 10 min at room temperature to form complexes. Subsequently, the complex was added to the cell culture dish and the dish was gently shaken to ensure uniform distribution. Six hours after transfection, the medium was replaced with complete medium (DMEM containing 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, Abcam), double antibody), and cells were collected 48 h later for subsequent experiments. FBXW7 overexpression lentivirus (Sangon, Shanghai, China) was directly added to the culture medium in proportion according to the manufacturer’s instructions without transfection reagent, and the other steps were the same as above.

NPCs apoptosis model

According to Wang et al., 100µmol/L TBHP was used to induce apoptosis [27]. NPCs were seeded in 6-cm culture dish and cultured to 80% confluence. NPCs were divided into 4 groups: ① Control group: NPCs were cultured with complete medium only; ② TBHP induction group: TBHP containing complete medium was added, incubated for 6 h and then cultured for 24 h; ③ si-FBXW7 + TBHP group: transfection of FBXW7 siRNA for 48 h followed by TBHP induction; ④ si-JNK+TBHP group: transfection of JNK siRNA for 48 h followed by TBHP induction. Cells were collected at the end of the treatment for subsequent assays.

Western blot

Proteins were extracted with RIPA lysate (Beyotime, Shanghai, China), and protein concentrations were measured using the BCA Protein kit (Beyotime, Shanghai, China). Extracted proteins were separated by gel electrophoresis, then electrotransferred to PVDF membranes (Millipore, USA) and incubated with 5% non fat milk powder solution (Beyotime, Shanghai, China) for 1 h at room temperature to block proteins, followed by overnight incubation of primary antibodies at 4℃. Next, membranes were incubated with secondary antibodies for 1 h. Finally, the bands were obtained after reaction using an ultra-hypersensitive ECL chemiluminescence reagent (Beyotime, Shanghai, China). Quantitative and statistical analyses were performed using Image J software.

qPCR

Total RNA was extracted from cells or tissues using Trizol(Takara, Japan) method, and RNA concentration and purity (OD260/OD280(Ratio, R)) were determined using a nucleic acid protein detector. One µg of total RNA was collected and reverse transcribed in a 20µL system (1 h at 42℃ and 5 min at 80℃) using the HiScript II Q RT SuperMix for qPCR kit (Vazyme, Nanjing, China). A 20 µL reaction system was constructed using the ChamQ SYBR qPCR Master Mix kit (Vazyme, Nanjing, China). The reaction conditions were as follows: predenaturation at 95℃ for 5 min, 40 cycles (95℃ for 5s, 60℃ for 30s). GAPDH was used as the reference gene for relative quantification. The primer sequences are shown in Table 2 below. qPCR data were calculated by the 2−ΔΔCt method for relative expression.

CCK8

NPCs were seeded in 96-well plates at a density of 10,000/well, and then incubated for 6 h at 37℃ with 5% CO2. After transfection of siRNA or lentivirus according to experimental design, 48 h later, complete medium containing 100µmol/L TBHP was added and incubated for 6 h, and then replaced with fresh complete medium for further culture. At 24, 48, and 72 h after TBHP treatment, the old medium was aspirated and discarded, and 100µL of serum-free DMEM medium containing 10% CCK-8 reagent (Tao Zhu Biological, Shanghai, China) was added to each well and incubated for 2 h at 37℃. The absorbance (OD value) was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader.

Flow cytometry

The apoptosis of NPCs was detected by Annexin V-FITC/PI double staining, and the operation was performed according to the instructions of Annexin V-FITC/PI double staining apoptosis detection kit (KGI Biology, Nanjing, China). For adherent cells, the cells were collected after digestion with trypsin cell digest (without EDTA) (Beyotime, Shanghai, China). Cells were washed twice with PBS and centrifuged twice (1000 rpm, 5 min). Subsequently, the cells were resuspended in Binding Buffer to precipitate, Annexin V-FITC and PI staining solution was added, and the cells were incubated in the dark for 10 min at room temperature. Finally, the stained cells were examined using a Beckman flow cytometer (Beckman, USA).

Rat IDD model

The rat caudal intervertebral disc degeneration model was established by percutaneous puncture [28, 29]. A total of 40 male 3-month-old SD rats (500 ± 50 g) were used. Four consecutive caudal intervertebral discs (Co7/8, Co8/9, Co9/10, Co10/11) from each rat were randomly assigned to four experimental groups: control, IDD model, IDD+FBXW7 shRNA (rescue), and sham operation. This design resulted in 40 disc samples per group (1 disc × 40 rats per group), which provided sufficient tissues for subsequent Western blot and qPCR analyses. After a 6 h fast before surgery, gas anesthesia was administered with isoflurane (3% induction, 2% maintenance) [30]. Control group received no treatment. In the IDD group, after the rats were anesthetized, a 22-G needle was inserted into the rat caudal intervertebral space, through the entire intervertebral space, rotated 360°after insertion, stayed for 30s and then pulled out. In the rescue group, AAV9-FBXW7-shRNA (2 µL, 2.67*1013 VG/mL) was injected into the disc with a microinjector immediately after puncture. In the sham-operation group, only the peripheral skin and muscles of the intervertebral disc were punctured, and the intervertebral disc was not destroyed. Postoperative rats were housed in single cages and observed daily. The animals were fed for 6 weeks for subsequent examinations and experiments. AAV9-FBXW7-shRNA used in the experiments was prepared by Nanjing Zebrafish Biology Company.

X-rays and MRI

Six weeks after surgery, the rats were anesthetized with isoflurane (3% induction, 2% maintenance) and underwent imaging examinations [30]. Fixed in the prone position on a medical diagnostic X-ray tube assembly (Star Link Industry, Shenzhen, China), the caudal vertebra was scanned, and images were acquired through a PACS system. Caudal T2-weighted sagittal images were acquired on a Biospec 7T/20 USR small animal high-field MRI imaging system (Bruker, Germany). MRI images were evaluated according to the Pfirrmann grading criteria (Table 3) in a double-blind manner by two associate chief spine surgeons.

HE staining

After rats were sacrificed, caudal vertebra samples containing the target intervertebral space were isolated and fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde (Biosharp Biology, Hefei, China) for 12 h. Decalcification was performed with 10% EDTA followed by dehydration and paraffin embedding. Samples were cut into 4 μm thick sections and stained with hematoxylin and eosin. Images were captured using a PrimoStar microscope (Zeiss, Germany) to observe differences in discs from different groups.

Statistical analysis

All quantitative data are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD) and were processed through GraphPad Prism 8.4. Comparative analyses between two experimental groups employed Student’s t-test, while multigroup comparisons utilized one-way ANOVA with appropriate post-hoc tests. Experimental procedures were replicated independently in triplicate to ensure reproducibility.

Results

FBXW7 and JNK are upregulated in IDD tissues and correlated with MAPK/Apoptosis pathway activation

Bioinformatics analysis using integrated GEO datasets (GSE70362, GSE150408, and GSE124272) identified differentially expressed genes between normal and IDD nucleus pulposus tissues. FBXW7 expression was significantly higher in the IDD group than in the normal group (Fig. 1A, B and F). KEGG pathway enrichment analysis showed that differentially expressed genes were concentrated in the MAPK signaling pathway and apoptosis-related pathways. GSEA further confirmed significant enrichment of apoptosis and MAPK signaling gene sets in IDD samples (Fig. 1C and E). Type II collagen (COL2) and aggrecan (ACAN) are widely recognized as positive markers of nucleus pulposus cells [31]. Analysis of these markers in cells extracted from clinical specimens confirmed the successful isolation of nucleus pulposus cells (Fig. 1G). Subsequent validation demonstrated that both mRNA and total protein levels of FBXW7 and JNK were significantly elevated in the high-grade degeneration group relative to the low-grade degeneration group (Fig. 1H and J).

si-FBXW7 and si-JNK reversed TBHP-induced apoptosis of NPCs

Flow cytometry showed that the apoptosis rate of NPCs in TBHP group was significantly higher than that in control group. After transfection with si-FBXW7/si-JNK, the apoptosis rate was significantly lower than that in the TBHP group (Fig. 2A and B). CCK-8 test showed that the cell proliferation rate of TBHP group was significantly lower than that of control group at 48 h and 72 h. Intervention with si-FBXW7 and si-JNK effectively reversed the inhibitory effect of TBHP-induced proliferation (Fig. 2C). WB results showed that compared with the control group, the expression of FBXW7, JNK, Caspase-3 and Bax proteins in the TBHP group was up-regulated, and the expression of Bcl-2 was down-regulated. The expression of the above proteins in the si-FBXW7 + TBHP group and si-JNK+TBHP group were significantly reversed compared with the TBHP group. qPCR showed that the mRNA expression of FBXW7 and JNK in the TBHP group was higher than that in the control group, and the mRNA expression of FBXW7 and JNK was significantly down-regulated after si-FBXW7 transfection (Fig. 2D–I).

FBXW7 promotes NPCs apoptosis by upregulating JNK expression

Functional rescue experiments showed that FBXW7 overexpression could significantly up-regulate the expression of JNK, Bax and Caspase-3, down-regulate the expression of Bcl-2, and significantly increase the apoptosis rate of nucleus pulposus cells. These changes were reversed by co-transfection of JNK siRNA (Fig. 3A–D). qPCR confirmed that si-JNK effectively downregulated the high expression of JNK mRNA mediated by FBXW7 overexpression (Fig. 3E), indicating that FBXW7 can promote NPCs apoptosis by upregulating JNK expression.

E qPCR was used to detect the relative expression of JNK mRNA in the three groups (*P < 0.05, **P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001, ****P < 0.0001)

Animal experiments

HE staining revealed that discs in the IDD group exhibited disrupted structure, the annulus fibrosus was disordered, and the boundary between nucleus pulposus and annulus fibrosus disappeared. In the IDD+FBXW7 shRNA group (rescue group), the nucleus pulposus morphology was improved compared with the IDD group, and the disarrangement of the annulus fibrosus was reduced (Fig. 4A). X-ray demonstrated that intervertebral space stenosis and osteophyte formation were significantly more severe in the IDD group than in the control group (Fig. 4B and C), and the intervertebral disc height index (DHI%) was significantly reduced. The reduction in intervertebral space height was attenuated in the rescue group, and the DHI% was significantly higher than that in the IDD group (Fig. 4D). On T2-weighted MRI, the signal intensity was significantly lower in the IDD group than in the control group and was higher in the rescue group than in the IDD group, with a lower Pfirrmann grade (Fig. 4B and C, and 4E). WB and qPCR showed that the mRNA and protein expressions of FBXW7 and JNK in the NP tissues of the IDD group were significantly higher than those of the control group. The expression of FBXW7 and JNK in the rescue group was significantly lower than that in the IDD group (Fig. 4F–H).

Discussion

IDD is an important factor causing chronic low back pain and spinal dysfunction, and its pathological process is closely related to excessive apoptosis, inflammatory response and extracellular matrix degradation of NPCs [32, 33]. At present, the clinical treatment is mainly based on symptom relief or surgical decompression, and there is a lack of effective means to reverse the degenerative process. Therefore, it is of great clinical significance to excavate the core regulatory mechanism of IDD and find potential targeted therapeutic targets. As a key regulatory axis of cellular stress response, JNK signaling pathway has been confirmed to participate in the progression of IDD by mediating the apoptosis of NPCs and accelerating the degradation of extracellular matrix [34–36], but its upstream regulatory molecules and specific mechanisms have not been fully clarified. Through multi-level verification of clinical samples, cell experiments and animal models, this study demonstrates for the first time that FBXW7 is upregulated in degenerative NP tissues and can regulate JNK expression, thereby accelerating the progression of IDD, providing a new theoretical basis for the targeted intervention of IDD.

FBXW7 is an important recognition component of E3 ubiquitin ligase complex, which is involved in many biological processes such as cell cycle regulation, cell proliferation and apoptosis [37–39]. Existing studies have shown that FBXW7 is involved in the progression of various pathological processes such as cancer, neurodegenerative diseases, cardiovascular diseases, and metabolic diseases [40–43]. Welcker et al. believe that FBXW7 can modify target proteins by ubiquitination and promote their degradation through the proteasome, thus regulating cell differentiation, proliferation and apoptosis [44]. The study of Mao in breast cancer showed that FBXW7 inhibited the proliferation and metastasis of tumor cells by degrading c-Myc and Notch1 [45]. In this study, through bioinformatics analysis and clinical sample detection, it was found that the expression of FBXW7 in NP tissues of IDD patients was significantly higher than that in normal tissues, and the mRNA and protein levels of FBXW7 in the highly degenerative group were significantly higher than those in the low-grade degenerative group, suggesting that FBXW7 may be involved in the pathological progression of IDD. In vitro experiments, FBXW7 expression was significantly up-regulated after TBHP induced NPCs apoptosis, while silencing FBXW7 could significantly reduce the apoptosis rate and reverse the inhibitory effect of TBHP on cell proliferation, indicating that FBXW7 had the function of promoting NPCs apoptosis. Animal experiments further confirmed that local injection of FBXW7 shRNA could significantly reduce the expression of FBXW7 in rat NP tissues, alleviate the damage of intervertebral disc structure, improve MRI T2-weighted signal, and delay the progression of IDD.

As an important member of the MAPK family, JNK can be activated by various factors such as mechanical stress and oxidative stress, and accelerate the apoptosis and extracellular matrix degradation of NPCs by regulating the expression of apoptosis-related proteins and the release of proteases (MMPs, ADAMTs) [46–49]. This study found that the expression of JNK was significantly increased in highly degenerative NP tissues and TBHP-induced apoptotic cells. Silencing JNK effectively inhibited the apoptosis of NPCs, increased the expression of Bcl-2, and decreased the expression of Bax and Caspase-3, which was consistent with the results of Park et al. It also alleviated apoptosis and extracellular matrix destruction of NPCs. In addition, JNK signaling pathway can also accelerate the degradation of extracellular matrix by regulating the expression of MMPs, ADAMTs and other proteases [50, 51], which further confirms its role in promoting degeneration in IDD.

In this study, functional rescue experiments confirmed that FBXW7 overexpression significantly upregulated JNK expression and promoted apoptosis of NPCs, while silencing JNK reversed this effect, indicating that FBXW7 may mediate apoptosis of NPCs by regulating JNK signaling pathway. Qiu et al. studied the effect of FBXW7 on oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) and found that the overexpression of FBXW7 could up-regulate the expression of pro-apoptotic protein Bax and down-regulate the expression of anti-apoptotic protein Bcl-2, thereby promoting cell apoptosis [52]. Our results are consistent with those of Qiu et al. in oral squamous cell carcinoma. However, unlike us, Nateri et al. found that knockdown of FBXW7 induced apoptosis in neurons, and FBXW7 antagonized apoptosis induced by JNK signaling pathway [53]. Qiu and Nateri’s study illustrates the complexity of the relationship between FBXW7 and apoptosis. In the study of osteoarthritis, Zhang et al. showed that FBXW7 inhibited JNK activation by degrading MKK7, while in the present study, FBXW7 was positively correlated with JNK [54], suggesting that FBXW7 showed significant heterogeneity in its effects on cell apoptosis and signaling pathways in different cell types and disease models. This cell type-specific regulatory difference may be due to the non-canonical mechanism of FBXW7 in nucleus pulposus cells: FBXW7 may act as a scaffold protein to regulate the assembly of JNK signaling pathway complex, or indirectly regulate JNK expression through upstream kinases such as MKK4/7. Subsequent studies will identify the specific substrates of FBXW7 in nucleus pulposus cells by co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP) combined with mass spectrometry technology, and further clarify the direct molecular mechanism of FBXW7 regulating JNK signaling pathway.

From a translational perspective, biological interventions that reduce NPCs apoptosis—such as leukocyte-poor PRP—have shown superior reparative effects compared with pro-inflammatory approaches, supporting the rationale for targeting upstream apoptosis regulators like FBXW7 [55]. This study has certain limitations. First, the specific molecular targets of FBXW7 regulating JNK signaling pathway were not identified. The clinical sample size was small (20 cases), and it is necessary to expand the sample size combined with clinical prognostic data to verify the potential of FBXW7/JNK as a diagnostic and prognostic biomarker for IDD. Addressing these limitations may also benefit from combining molecular targeting of stress-apoptosis pathways with biomaterial-based annulus fibrosus repair strategies, which together could provide a more comprehensive approach to disc regeneration [56].

Conclusion

Our study is the first to demonstrate the key role of FBXW7-JNK axis in IDD, that is, FBXW7 may promote NPCs apoptosis and accelerate IDD progression by upregulating JNK expression, while silencing FBXW7 alleviates IDD by reducing JNK expression. This finding not only enriches the molecular regulatory network of IDD, but also provides potential targets for the development of new targeted therapy strategies, which has important basic research value and clinical transformation prospects.
