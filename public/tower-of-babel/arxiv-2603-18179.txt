1.
Introduction

For us an
r
-colouring of a set
X
is a cover
\mathcal{C}
of
X
of size
r
. For a
k\times d
matrix
M
with integer entries we define
\mathfrak{R}_{M}:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
to be the map taking
r\in\mathbb{N}
to the smallest natural
N
such that in any
r
-colouring
\mathcal{C}
of
[N]:=\{1,\dots,N\}
there is some
A\in\mathcal{C}
and
x\in A^{d}
such that
Mx=0
(and
\mathfrak{R}_{M}(r):=\infty
if no such
N
exists).

Following
[
Rad33
, p425]
we say the matrix
M
is partition regular if
\mathfrak{R}_{M}(r)<\infty
for all
r\in\mathbb{N}
. Rado’s Theorem
[
Rad33
, Satz IV, p445]
characterises when
M
is partition regular. In the case where
M
is a single row, say
M=a=(a_{1},\dots,a_{d})
, it reduces
[
Rad33
, p446]
to saying that
a
is partition regular if and only if there is some
I\subset[d]
with
a_{j}\neq 0
for some
j\in I
, and
\sum_{i\in I}{a_{i}}=0
.

In this paper our interest is in the growth of
\mathfrak{R}_{a}(r)
, as a function of
r
, when
a\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is partition regular. In
[
CS17
, Theorem 1.5]
Cwalina and Schoen show that for any partition regular
a
there is
C_{a}\leq 4
such that

\mathfrak{R}_{a}(r)\leq\exp(r^{C_{a}+o_{a;r\rightarrow\infty}(1)}).

(1.1)

There are various refinements in
[
CS17
]
and
[
Koś25
]
for particular classes of
a
which we shall discuss later, but in fact it has been known since work of Schur
[
Sch16
, p114, Hilfssatz]
that
C_{(1,-1,-1)}\leq 1
. On the other hand, Fox has shown (recorded in
[
FK06
, Theorem 5]
) that provided
a
is not invariant, we cannot have
C_{a}<1
. (
a
is said to be invariant if
\sum_{i=1}^{d}{a_{i}}=0
, and in this case
\mathfrak{R}_{a}(r)=1
for all
r
.) The main purpose of this note is to prove that we may take
C_{a}\leq 2
for all partition regular
a
:

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
a\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is partition regular. Then

\mathfrak{R}_{a}(r)=\exp(O_{a}(r^{2}\log^{O(1)}2r))

In
[
CS17
, Theorem 1.7]
Cwalina and Schoen show that one may take
C_{a}\leq 2
for a wide class of
a
, and
[
CS17
, Theorem 1.6]
shows one may take
C_{a}\leq 3
for all
a
involving at least four variables. On the other hand the recent breakthrough work of Kelley and Meka
[
KM23
]
provides a way of lifting counts for equations with three variables into counts for equations with four variables and so it is natural to expect this to give an improvement to Cwalina and Schoen’s results.

In §
2
we shall illustrate our argument for a toy version of the problem. This is essentially just the sparsity-expansion dichotomy of Chapman and Prendiville described in
[
CP20
, Lemma 2.3]
combined with the quantitative refinements of Kelley and Meka in
[
KM23
, Lemma 1.5]
. No difficulties arise in combining them.

In §
3
, we use the Bohr set machinery of Bourgain
[
Bou99
]
to translate the above argument into a proof of Theorem
1.1
. Bloom and Sisask
[
BS23
]
have already extended Kelley and Meka’s work to Bohr sets, and we encounter no new difficulties here. The fact that Theorem
1.1
gives
C_{a}\leq 2
rather than
C_{a}\leq 1
looks like a typical artefact of this machinery: the basic argument is iterative and takes
r^{1+o(1)}
steps and at each step we have to pay the price of an additional dilation.

1.2.
When can we do better?

The aforementioned fact that
C_{(1,-1,-1)}=1
can be proved by first showing that
\mathfrak{R}_{(1,-1,-1)}(r)+2\leq R(3,\dots,3)
, where the right hand side is the smallest natural
n
such that any
r
-colouring of the complete graph on
n
vertices contains a monochromatic triangle. A bound on this Ramsey number such as
[
GG55
, Corollary 3]
can then be used to complete the argument.

The first part of this argument has a generalisation in
[
AH72
, (3.4), p181]
which shows that
\mathfrak{R}_{M}(r)+2\leq R(k,\dots,k)
where
M
is the
\binom{k-1}{2}\times\binom{k}{2}
integer-valued matrix encoding
1
1

1

Meaning, if the rows of
M
are indexed by pairs
(i,j)
with
1\leq i<j\leq k-1
and the columns are indexed by pairs
(i,j)
with
1\leq i<j\leq k
, then the
(i,j)
th row of
M
has a
1
in columns
(i,j)
and
(j,j+1)
, a
-1
in column
(i,j+1)
, and
0
s elsewhere.
the system of equations

x_{i,j}+x_{j,j+1}=x_{i,j+1}\text{ whenever }1\leq i<j\leq k-1.

(1.2)

The same
[
GG55
, Corollary 3]
implies that
R(k,\dots,k)\leq\exp(r^{1+o_{k}(1)})
, and since
\mathfrak{R}_{a}(r)\leq\mathfrak{R}_{M}(r)
for any
a
in the
\mathbb{Q}
-row span of
M
we conclude that
C_{a}=1
for any
a
in the
\mathbb{Q}
-row span of

. In particular, if the

s satisfy (

) then

x_{1,2}+x_{2,3}+\cdots+x_{k-1,k}=x_{1,k},

so
C_{(1,\dots,1,-1)}=1
where
1
occurs

times in the row. Similarly,

x_{1,k-1}+\cdots+x_{k-2,k-1}+(k-1)x_{k-1,k}=x_{1,k}+\cdots+x_{k-2,k},

so
C_{(1,\dots,1,k-1,-1,\dots,-1)}=1
where

and

both occur

[

, Theorems 6 & 7]
.

One can make more vectors

with

from existing ones by appending a vector whose entries sum to

, so that from

we deduce

where

occurs
k
times and
-1
occurs
k+1
times. This is the monotonicity of

-sum-free sets discussed on
[

, p604]
. Combining this with our earlier observations, for

we have

if the

1.1
comes down to showing

.

2.
The toy

Toy problems over finite fields have been standard expository devices since the paper of Green
[
Gre05
]
(updated in the sequels
[
Wol15
]
&
[
Pel24
]
). For our toy we replace
\{1,\dots,N\}
by the vector space
G:=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
, where
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is a finite field with
q
elements. We fix
G
in this way for the remainder of this section only. We shall show the following:

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose that
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
and
\mathcal{C}
is an
r
-colouring of
G
. Then there is a colour class
A\in\mathcal{C}
and
\exp(-O_{q}(r\log^{9}2r))|G|^{2}
triples
(x,y,z)\in A^{3}
such that
ax-ay=bz
.

This toy is supposed to illustrate the argument we will eventually use for the special case of Theorem
1.1
where
a=(a,-a,-b)
with
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}
, which will itself imply the more general case.

We need Proposition
2.2
below, and to record this we need some notation. For
V\leq G
we write
\mu_{V}
for the uniform probability measure on
V
extended to be a probability measure on
G
,
i.e.
\mu_{V}(A)=|A\cap V|/|V|
for all
A\subset G
. We also write
\ast
for convolution on
G
so that for functions
f
and
g
on
G
, and a measure
\mu
on
G
(defined on all subsets of
G
) we have

f\ast g(x):=\int{f(x-y)g(y)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{G}(y)},\text{ and }f\ast\mu(x):=\int{f(x-y)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)},

(2.1)

and

\langle f,g\rangle_{L_{2}(\mu)}:=\int{f(x)\overline{g(x)}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)}.

(2.2)

Proposition 2.2
.

Suppose that
V\leq G
,
A,D\subset V
are non-empty with
\alpha:=\mu_{V}(A)
and
\delta:=\mu_{V}(D)
, and
\epsilon\in(0,1]
is a parameter. Then either

|\langle 1_{-A}\ast 1_{A},1_{D}\rangle_{L_{2}(\mu_{G})}-\alpha^{2}\delta\mu_{G}(V)^{2}|\leq\epsilon\alpha^{2}\delta\mu_{G}(V)^{2};

or else there is
V^{\prime}\leq V
with
\dim V/V^{\prime}=O_{\epsilon}((\log^{4}2\alpha^{-1})(\log^{4}2\delta^{-1}))
such that
\|1_{A}\ast\mu_{V^{\prime}}\|_{\infty}\geq(1+\Omega(\epsilon))\alpha
.

This is a minor variant of
[
BS23
, Proposition 13, p32]
with
1_{A}\ast 1_{A}
replaced by
1_{-A}\ast 1_{A}
and rescaled to be stated for subgroups of a larger ambient group which is helpful for our application.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Write
\mathcal{C}:=\{A_{1},\dots,A_{r}\}
. We proceed iteratively to construct
G=:V_{0}\geq V_{1}\geq\dots
. Writing
\cdot
for the scalar multiplication of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
on
G
, set

S_{i,j}:=\|1_{a\cdot A_{j}}\ast\mu_{V_{i}}\|_{\infty}\text{ for all }j\in[r].

Since
V_{i}\leq V_{k}
whenever
i\geq k
we have
\mu_{V_{i}}\ast\mu_{V_{k}}=\mu_{V_{k}}
and hence

S_{i,j}\geq S_{k,j}\text{ whenever }i\geq k,\text{ and }S_{i,j}\leq 1\text{ for all }i.

(2.3)

At step
i
of the iteration we shall show that either

(1)

there is some
j\in[r]
such that

\langle 1_{a\cdot A_{j}}\ast 1_{-a\cdot A_{j}},1_{b\cdot A_{j}}\rangle_{L_{2}(\mu_{G})}\geq\frac{1}{2r^{3}}\mu_{G}(V_{i})^{2};\text{ or }

(2)

there is
V_{i+1}\leq V_{i}
and
j\in[r]
such that

(a)

\dim V_{i}/V_{i+1}=O(\log^{8}2r)
;

(b)

S_{i+1,j}\geq(1+\Omega(1))S_{i,j}
; and

(c)

S_{i,j}\geq 1/r
.

We stop the iteration the first time we are in case (
1
). Suppose that we are in case (
2
) for a particular
j
at steps
i_{1}<i_{2}<\dots<i_{k}
of the iteration. Then by (
2.3
) and (
2b
) & (
2c
) we have

\displaystyle 1\geq S_{i_{k}+1,j}\geq(1+\Omega(1))S_{i_{k},j}

\displaystyle\geq(1+\Omega(1))S_{i_{k-1}+1,j}

\displaystyle\geq\qquad\dots\qquad\geq(1+\Omega(1))^{k}S_{i_{1},j}\geq(1+\Omega(1))^{k}\cdot(1/r).

It follows that
k=O(\log 2r)
. Since there are
r
possible values for
j
we conclude that we must have terminated the iteration at step
i_{0}=r\cdot O(\log 2r)
, in which case

\dim G/V_{i_{0}}=\dim V_{0}/V_{1}+\cdots+\dim V_{i_{0}-1}/V_{i_{0}}=i_{0}\cdot O(\log^{8}2r)=O(r\log^{9}2r)

by (
2a
).

Since
\mu_{G}(V_{i_{0}})=q^{-\dim G/V_{i_{0}}}
and
|G|^{2}\langle 1_{a\cdot A_{j}}\ast 1_{-a\cdot A_{j}},1_{b\cdot A_{j}}\rangle_{L_{2}(\mu_{G})}
is exactly the number of solutions to
ax-ay=bz
for
x,y,z\in A_{j}
we have the conclusion of Theorem
2.1
from (
1
) as claimed.

It remains to show that at each stage of the iteration we are either in case (
1
) or (
2
). Suppose we are at step
i
of the iteration. Since
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
we have
b\cdot V_{i}=V_{i}
and
\mathcal{C}
is a cover of
G
, and there is some
j=j(i)\in[r]
such that
\mu_{V_{i}}(b\cdot A_{j})\geq\frac{1}{r}
. Since
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
we have
(ab^{-1})V_{i}=V_{i}
, and

S_{i,j}=\|1_{a\cdot A_{j}}\ast\mu_{V_{i}}\|_{\infty}\geq\mu_{V_{i}}(a\cdot A_{j})=\mu_{V_{i}}(b\cdot A_{j}).

In particular
S_{i,j}\geq 1/r
. Let
x_{i}\in G
be such that

\mu_{V_{i}}(x_{i}-a\cdot A_{j})=1_{a\cdot A_{j}}\ast\mu_{V_{i}}(x_{i})=\|1_{a\cdot A_{j}}\ast\mu_{V_{i}}\|_{\infty}.

Apply Proposition
2.2
to
V_{i}\leq G
with
\epsilon:=\frac{1}{2}
, and
A:=(x_{i}-a\cdot A_{j})\cap V_{i}
and
D:=(b\cdot A_{j})\cap V_{i}
to get that either

\displaystyle\langle 1_{a\cdot A_{j}}\ast 1_{-a\cdot A_{j}},1_{b\cdot A_{j}}\rangle_{L_{2}(\mu_{G})}

\displaystyle=\langle 1_{-(x_{i}-a\cdot A_{j})}\ast 1_{x_{i}-a\cdot A_{j}},1_{b\cdot A_{j}}\rangle_{L_{2}(\mu_{G})}

\displaystyle\geq\langle 1_{-A}\ast 1_{A},1_{D}\rangle_{L_{2}(\mu_{G})}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\mu_{V_{i}}(A)^{2}\mu_{V_{i}}(D)\mu_{G}(V_{i})^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2r^{3}}\mu_{G}(V_{i})^{2};

or else there is
V_{i+1}\leq V_{i}
with
\dim V_{i}/V_{i+1}=O(\log^{8}2r)
and
S_{i+1,j}\geq(1+\Omega(1))S_{i,j}
. In the first case we are in (
1
); in the second we are in (
2
). The result is proved.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

We shall show the following result.

Theorem 3.1
.

There is a function
f:\mathbb{Z}^{*}\times\mathbb{Z}^{*}\times\mathbb{N}^{*}\rightarrow(0,1]
such that if
\mathcal{C}
is an
r
-colouring of
\{-N,\dots,N\}
, then there is a colour class
A\in\mathcal{C}
and at least
f(a,b,r)N^{2}
triples
(x,y,z)\in A^{3}
such that
ax-ay=bz
. Moreover,
f(a,b,r)=\exp(-O_{a,b}(r^{2}\log^{O(1)}2r))
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
assuming Theorem
3.1
.

Let
f
be the function in Theorem
3.1
. By Rado’s Single Equation Theorem
[
LR14
, Theorem 9.5, p255]
there is
I\subset[d]
such that
a_{j}\neq 0
for some
j\in I
and
\sum_{i\in I}{a_{i}}=0
. Let
b:=-\sum_{i\not\in I}{a_{i}}
, which we may assume is nonzero since otherwise
a
is invariant and
\mathfrak{R}_{a}(r)=1
for all
r
, and finally let
N
be such that
f(a_{j},b,2r+1)N^{2}>1
, so that
N=\exp(O_{a}(r^{2}\log^{O(1)}2r))
.

Given an
r
-colouring
\mathcal{C}
of
\{1,\dots,N\}
,
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}:=\{A,-A:A\in\mathcal{C}\}\cup\{\{0\}\}
is a
(2r+1)
-colouring of
\{-N,\dots,N\}
. By Theorem
3.1
there is a colour class
A^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
with
f(a_{j},b,2r+1)N^{2}
triples
(y,z,w)\in A^{3}
such that
a_{j}y-a_{j}z=bw
. Since
f(a_{j},b,2r+1)N^{2}>1
we see that
A^{\prime}\neq\{0\}
, and hence
A^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}
or
-A^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}
. In the former case let
x\in[N]^{d}
be defined by
x_{j}=y
;
x_{k}=z
for all
k\in I\setminus\{j\}
; and
x_{k}=w
for all
k\not\in I
. Then
x\in(A^{\prime})^{d}
and

a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{d}x_{d}=a_{j}y+\left(\sum_{i\in I\setminus\{j\}}{a_{i}}\right)z+\left(\sum_{i\not\in I}{a_{i}}\right)w=a_{j}y-a_{j}z-bw=0.

The case for
-A^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}
is similar, we just replace
y
,
z
and
w
by
-y
,
-z
and
-w
. In particular, in either case we have some
A\in\mathcal{C}
and
x\in A^{d}
such that
a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{d}x_{d}=0
and hence
\mathfrak{R}_{a}(r)\leq N
. The result is proved.
∎

3.2.
Notation

First we set some notation extending the notation from §
2
. We shall work in a finite Abelian group
G
, and we only consider measures on
G
defined on all subsets of
G
. For
\emptyset\neq A\subset G
we write
\mu_{A}
for the uniform probability measure supported on
A
. If
\mu
is a measure and
f\in L_{\infty}(\mu_{G})
then

denotes the measure assigning mass
\int{f1_{E}\,\mathrm{d}\mu}
to the set
E\subset G
.

For functions
f
and
g
and a measure
\mu
on
G
we define convolution and the inner product as in (
2.1
) and (
2.2
). If
\nu
is a further measure on
G
then we define
\nu\ast\mu
to be the measure given by

\nu\ast\mu(E):=\int{1_{E}(x+y)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)}\text{ for all }E\subset G.

For
\mu
a measure on
G
we write
\langle f,\mu\rangle:=\int{f(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)}
, and
\widetilde{\mu}(E):=\overline{\mu(-E)}
for all
E\subset G
.

We write
\widehat{G}
for the dual group of

, that is the set of homomorphisms

, and define the Fourier and Fourier-Stieltjes transform of a function

and a measure
\mu
respectively by

\widehat{f}(\gamma):=\int{f(x)\overline{\gamma(x)}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{G}(x)}\text{ and }\widehat{\mu}(\gamma):=\int{\overline{\gamma(x)}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)}.

The labels
C_{1},C_{2},\dots\geq 1
and

denote absolute constants which remain the same in all instances. The

s are different to the

s defined in §
1
.

For
\Gamma
a set of characters on
G
and
\delta\in(0,1]
, the Bohr set with frequency set
\Gamma
and width

is

B(\Gamma,\delta):=\{x\in G:|\gamma(x)-1|\leq\delta\text{ for all }\gamma\in\Gamma\}.

There are two standard ways to define Bohr sets. The other way is
[
TV06
, §4.4, p187]
, and for most purposes they are equivalent via
[
TV06
, (4.25), p187]
.

3.3.
The main iteration lemma

We shall prove Theorem
3.1
by copying the proof of Theorem
2.1
. We replace subspaces with Bohr sets, and codimension with the size of the frequency set, and pay an additional cost in the width parameter.

It can be helpful to think of Bohr sets as somewhat like convex bodies. If
B_{0}
is a convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
B_{1}
is that same body dilated by a factor
\delta
, then
\mu_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(B_{1}+B_{0})=(1+O(d\delta))\mu_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(B_{0})\approx\mu_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(B_{0})
(here
\mu_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
is Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
). In Lemma
A.2
we shall see that many Bohr sets share this property.

In general for any sets
B_{0},B_{1}\subset G
having
\mu_{G}(B_{1}+B_{0})\approx\mu_{G}(B_{0})
we have
x+B_{0}\approx B_{0}
for all
x\in B_{1}-B_{1}
. We think of this as saying that
B_{1}-B_{1}
acts approximately on
B_{0}
, and if this approximation is good enough then we can transfer arguments that work for genuine actions to this approximate setting.

To copy the proof of Theorem
2.1
we need a generalisation of Proposition
2.2
. This is Lemma
3.4
below, but it is complicated enough that we cannot make do with just one pair
B_{0}
and
B_{1}
with
\mu_{G}(B_{1}+B_{0})\approx\mu_{G}(B_{0})
, and instead will need a whole chain of sets
B_{0},B_{1},\dots
.

We can recover Proposition
2.2
in the case
\epsilon=1/2
– the case we actually used – from Lemma
3.4
by setting
G=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
, and
B_{0}=B_{1}=B_{2}=B_{3}=B_{4}=B_{5}=V\leq G
and
k
,
m
, and
l
minimal satisfying the hypotheses. This follows because a Bohr set contains the annihilator of its frequency set which, if
G=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
, is a subspace of codimension at most the size of that frequency set – in this case at most
m
.

Appendix A
Bohr sets

Bohr sets have well-controlled sizes; the following is a version of
[
TV06
, Lemma 4.19, p188]
(see also
[
Bou99
, §2]
) proved with a standard covering argument.

Lemma A.1
.

Suppose that
\Gamma
is a set of
d
characters. Then

\mu_{G}(B(\Gamma,2\delta))\leq\exp(\hypertarget{const:grow}{\mbox{}}C_{6}d)\mu_{G}(B(\Gamma,\delta))\text{ and }\mu_{G}(B(\Gamma,\delta))\geq(\delta/2)^{O(d)}.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma=\{\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{d}\}
and define the map
\phi:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{d};x\mapsto(\gamma_{i}(x))_{i=1}^{d}
. Write
D_{\delta}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z-1|\leq\delta\}
and
Q_{\delta}:=D_{\delta}^{d}
so that
\mu(Q_{\delta})=(\pi\delta^{2})^{d}
where
\mu
is Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
. Let
T\subset Q_{2\delta}
be maximal such that for
s,t\in T
distinct,
(t+Q_{\delta/4})\cap(s+Q_{\delta/4})=\emptyset
– in words
T\subset Q_{2\delta}
is maximal
Q_{\delta/4}
-separated.

Then
\mu(Q_{\delta/4})|T|\leq\mu(Q_{2\delta+\delta/2})
and
Q_{2\delta}\subset T+Q_{\delta/2}
by the triangle inequality. It follows that
|T|\leq 100^{d}
, and

\mu_{G}(B(\Gamma,2\delta))=\mu_{G}(\phi^{-1}(Q_{2\delta}))\leq\mu_{G}(\phi^{-1}(T+Q_{\delta/2}))\leq 100^{d}\sup_{t\in T}{\mu_{G}(\phi^{-1}(t+Q_{\delta/2}))}.

If
x,y\in\phi^{-1}(t+Q_{\delta/2})
then
\phi(x-y)\in Q_{\delta}
and so
\mu_{G}(\phi^{-1}(t+Q_{\delta/2}))\leq\mu_{G}(B(\Gamma,\delta))
. The result is proved, and the size bound follows by iterated application of the growth bound since
\mu_{G}(B(\Gamma,2))=1
.
∎

This growth lets us identify pairs of Bohr sets that produce approximate actions as discussed in §
3.3
:

Lemma A.2
.

Suppose
\delta\in(0,1]
and
\Gamma
is a set of
d
characters, and
l\in\mathbb{N}
,
\eta\in(0,1]
. Then there is
\delta_{*}\in[\delta/2,\delta]
and
\delta^{\prime}\in\Omega(\delta\eta/ld)
such that

\mu_{G}(B(\Gamma,\delta_{*})+lB(\Gamma,\delta^{\prime}))\leq(1+\eta)\mu_{G}(B(\Gamma,\delta_{*}))\text{ and }lB(\Gamma,\delta^{\prime})\subset B(\Gamma,\delta_{*}).

Proof.

Let
k=O(\eta^{-1})
be a natural number such that
\exp(\hyperlink{const:grow}{C_{\ref{const:grow}}}/k)\leq 1+\eta
, and let
\delta^{\prime}=\delta/2lkd
and
\delta_{i}=\delta/2+il\delta^{\prime}
for
0\leq i\leq kd-1
. Then

\prod_{i=0}^{kd-1}{\frac{\mu_{G}(B(\Gamma,\delta_{i}+l\delta^{\prime}))}{\mu_{G}(B(\Gamma,\delta_{i}))}}\leq\frac{\mu_{G}(B(\Gamma,\delta))}{\mu_{G}(B(\Gamma,\delta/2))}\leq\exp(\hyperlink{const:grow}{C_{\ref{const:grow}}}d).

By averaging there is some
\delta_{*}=\delta_{i}
such that

\frac{\mu_{G}(B(\Gamma,\delta_{*}+l\delta^{\prime}))}{\mu_{G}(B(\Gamma,\delta_{*}))}\leq 1+\eta.

The result follows by the triangle inequality.
∎

There is a stronger result in
[

The following lemma is useful for relating large values of the Fourier transform to Bohr sets, as we will need in Appendix
D
:

Lemma A.3
.

Suppose that
\eta,\kappa\in(0,1]
,
\mu_{G}(B_{1}+{B_{0}})\leq(1+\eta)\mu_{G}({B_{0}})
and
|\widehat{\mu_{{B_{0}}}}(\gamma)|\geq\kappa
. Then
|1-\gamma(x)|\leq 2\eta\kappa^{-1}
for all
x\in B_{1}-B_{1}
.

Proof.

For
x\in B_{1}-B_{1}
there are
s,t\in B_{1}
such that
x=s-t
, so
|\gamma(s)-\gamma(t)|=|1-\gamma(x)|
. Now

|\gamma(s)-\gamma(t)||\widehat{\mu_{B_{0}}}(\gamma)|=|\widehat{\mu_{t+{B_{0}}}}(\gamma)-\widehat{\mu_{s+{B_{0}}}}(\gamma)|\leq\frac{\mu_{G}((t+{B_{0}})\triangle(s+{B_{0}}))}{\mu_{G}({B_{0}})}\leq 2\eta.

Dividing by

∎

By a related argument Bohr sets support a technical device for replacing density by a sort of ‘hereditary density’ which is better behaved:

Lemma A.4
.

Suppose that
\alpha,\eta\in(0,1]
,
\mu_{G}(B_{0}+B_{1})\leq(1+\eta)\mu_{G}(B_{0})
, and
\|1_{A}\ast\mu_{B_{0}}\|_{\infty}\geq\alpha
. Then
\min{\{\|1_{A}\ast\mu\|_{\infty}:\mu\text{ is a probability measure supported in }B_{1}-B_{1}\}}\geq\alpha-2\eta
.

Proof.

If
x
is in the support of
\mu
then there is
z,w\in B_{1}
such that
x=z-w
and

\|\mu_{B_{0}+x}-\mu_{B_{0}}\|=\|\mu_{B_{0}+z}-\mu_{B_{0}+w}\|\leq\frac{\mu_{G}((B_{0}+z)\triangle(B_{0}+w))}{\mu_{G}(B_{0})}\leq 2\eta.

By the triangle inequality we conclude that
\|\mu_{B_{0}}\ast\mu-\mu_{B_{0}}\|\leq 2\eta
and hence

\|1_{A}\ast\mu\|_{\infty}\geq\|1_{A}\ast\mu_{B_{0}}\ast\mu\|_{\infty}\geq\|1_{A}\ast\mu_{B_{0}}\|_{\infty}-2\eta.

The result is proved.
∎

Appendix B
A local version of spectral positivity

We need the following version of the spectral positivity arguments of Kelley and Meka
[
KM23
, §5.2]
.

Lemma B.1
.

Suppose that
\eta,\alpha,\epsilon,\delta\in(0,1]
,
k
is a natural number,
\mu_{G}(B_{1}+{B_{0}})\leq(1+\eta)\mu_{G}({B_{0}})
,
\mu_{B_{0}}(A)=\alpha
,
\mu
is a positive-definite probability measure supported on
B_{1}-B_{1}
,
\mu(D)=\delta
, and

|\langle 1_{A\cap{B_{0}}}\ast 1_{-(A\cap{B_{0}})},1_{D}\rangle_{L_{2}(\mu)}-\delta\alpha^{2}\mu_{G}({B_{0}})|\geq\epsilon\delta\alpha^{2}\mu_{G}({B_{0}}).

Then

\|1_{A\cap{B_{0}}}\ast 1_{-(A\cap{B_{0}})}\|_{L_{2k}(\mu)}\geq((1+\epsilon)(\delta/2)^{1/2k}-\hypertarget{const:specpos}{\mbox{}}C_{7}\eta\alpha^{-1})\alpha^{2}\mu_{G}({B_{0}}).

Proof.

It is enough to show this when
A
is a subset of
{B_{0}}
, so we suppose this is the case. Write
f:=(1_{A}-\alpha 1_{B_{0}})\ast(1_{-A}-\alpha 1_{-{B_{0}}})
, so that we have the pointwise estimate

f(x)=1_{A}\ast 1_{-A}(x)-\alpha^{2}\mu_{G}({B_{0}})+O(\eta\alpha\mu_{G}({B_{0}}))\text{ for all }x\in B_{1}-B_{1}.

(B.1)

Hence

|\langle f,1_{D}\rangle_{L_{2}(\mu)}|\geq\epsilon\delta\alpha^{2}\mu_{G}({B_{0}})-O(\eta\alpha\delta\mu_{G}({B_{0}}))\geq\varepsilon\delta\alpha^{2}\mu_{G}({B_{0}})

where
\varepsilon=\epsilon-O(\eta\alpha^{-1})
. For
j\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
apply Hölder’s inequality – this is
[
BS23
, Step 1, pp23]
– to get that

\|f\|_{L_{2j}(\mu)}^{2j}\delta^{2j-1}=\|f\|_{L_{2j}(\mu)}^{2j}\|1_{D}\|_{L_{\frac{2j}{2j-1}}(\mu)}^{2j}\geq|\langle f,1_{D}\rangle_{L_{2}(\mu)}|^{2j}\geq\varepsilon^{2j}\delta^{2j}\alpha^{4j}\mu_{G}({B_{0}})^{2j},

which rearranges to
\int{f^{2j}\,\mathrm{d}\mu}\geq\varepsilon^{2j}\delta\alpha^{4j}\mu_{G}({B_{0}})^{2j}
. On the other hand, by design
\widehat{f}\geq 0
, and by hypothesis
\widehat{\mu}\geq 0
, so
\int{f^{2j+1}\,\mathrm{d}\mu}\geq 0
. By the Binomial formula these then give

\displaystyle\int{(f+\alpha^{2}\mu_{G}({B_{0}}))^{2k}\,\mathrm{d}\mu}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{2k}{\binom{2k}{i}\int{f^{i}\,\mathrm{d}\mu}\alpha^{4k-2i}\mu_{G}({B_{0}})^{2k-i}}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j=0}^{k}{\binom{2k}{2j}\varepsilon^{2j}\delta\alpha^{4j}\mu_{G}({B_{0}})^{2j}\alpha^{4k-4j}\mu_{G}({B_{0}})^{2k-2j}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1+\varepsilon)^{2k}+(1-\varepsilon)^{2k}}{2}\delta\alpha^{4k}\mu_{G}({B_{0}})^{2k}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\geq(1+\varepsilon)^{2k}\cdot\frac{\delta}{2}\cdot\alpha^{4k}\mu_{G}({B_{0}})^{2k}.

Now from (
B.1
) we have
\|f+\alpha^{2}\mu_{G}({B_{0}})\|_{L_{2k}(\mu)}\leq\|1_{A}\ast 1_{-A}\|_{L_{2k}(\mu)}+O(\eta\alpha\mu_{G}({B_{0}}))
and the result follows.
∎

Appendix C
A local version of sifting

We need the following version of the sifting lemma of Kelley and Meka
[
KM23
, §4.1]
. This is proved in the same way as the ‘combinatorial lemma’ of Gowers
[

, Lemma 11]
with ‘five’ there replaced by

Lemma C.1
.

Suppose that
\alpha,\epsilon,\kappa\in(0,1]
,

is a natural,
\mu_{G}(B_{2}+B_{1}+{B_{0}})\leq\left(1+\frac{\epsilon\alpha^{2}}{4}\right)\mu_{G}({B_{0}})
,

, and

\|1_{A\cap{B_{0}}}\ast 1_{-(A\cap{B_{0}})}\|_{L_{2k}(\widetilde{\mu_{B_{1}}}\ast\widetilde{\mu_{B_{2}}}\ast\mu_{B_{2}}\ast\mu_{B_{1}})}\geq(1+\epsilon)\alpha^{2}\mu_{G}({B_{0}}).

Then there are sets
T
and
S
, and elements

and

with

\mu_{B_{2}+z}(T),\mu_{-B_{1}-w}(S)\geq\alpha^{4k}\text{ and }\langle 1_{D},\mu_{T}\ast\mu_{S}\rangle\geq 1-\kappa

D=\left\{u\in-B_{1}-B_{2}+B_{2}+B_{1}:1_{A\cap{B_{0}}}\ast 1_{-(A\cap{B_{0}})}(u)>\left(1+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right)\alpha^{2}\mu_{G}({B_{0}})\right\}.

Proof.

Let
\hyperlink{const:rdc}{C_{\ref{const:rdc}}}>1
be absolute such that
(1+\frac{1}{2}\epsilon)^{2k}(1+\frac{3}{4}\epsilon)^{-2k}\leq\frac{1}{2}\kappa
for all
\epsilon,\kappa\in(0,1]
whenever

.

It is enough to show this when

and so we assume this is the case. We may also translate
B_{2}+B_{1}
such that it contains
0_{G}
. Let
x_{1},\dots,x_{2k}
be chosen independently and uniformly at random from
{B_{0}}
. Now
\mathop{\mathbb{P}}(x,y\in x_{i}-A)=\mu_{B_{0}}((x+A)\cap(y+A))
and so for

,

\displaystyle\frac{1_{A}\ast 1_{-A}(-x+y)}{\mu_{G}({B_{0}})}\geq\mathop{\mathbb{P}}(x,y\in x_{i}-A)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1_{A}\ast 1_{-A}(-x+y)}{\mu_{G}({B_{0}})}-\frac{\mu_{G}(B_{2}+B_{1}+{B_{0}})-\mu_{G}({B_{0}})}{\mu_{G}({B_{0}})}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1_{A}\ast 1_{-A}(-x+y)}{\mu_{G}({B_{0}})}-\frac{1}{4}\epsilon\alpha^{2}.

Setting
A^{\prime}:=(x_{1}-A)\cap\cdots\cap(x_{2k}-A)
, by linearity of expectation we have

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}{\int{\mu_{B_{2}+z}(A^{\prime})\mu_{w+B_{1}}(A^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{B_{1}}(z)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{B_{2}}(w)}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}{\mu_{B_{2}}\ast\mu_{B_{1}}(A^{\prime})\mu_{B_{2}}\ast\mu_{B_{1}}(A^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\int{\prod_{i=1}^{2k}{\mathop{\mathbb{P}}(x,y\in A-x_{i})}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{B_{2}}\ast\mu_{B_{1}}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{B_{2}}\ast\mu_{B_{1}}(y)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\geq\left(\frac{\|1_{A}\ast 1_{-A}\|_{L_{2k}(\widetilde{\mu_{B_{1}}}\ast\widetilde{\mu_{B_{2}}}\ast\mu_{B_{2}}\ast\mu_{B_{1}})}}{\mu_{G}({B_{0}})}-\frac{1}{4}\epsilon\alpha^{2}\right)^{2k}\geq\left(1+\frac{3}{4}\epsilon\right)^{2k}\alpha^{4k}.

(C.1)

On the other hand, for
x,y\in B_{2}+B_{1}
with
-x+y\not\in D
we have

\mathop{\mathbb{P}}((x,y)\in(A^{\prime})^{2})\leq\left(1+\frac{1}{2}\epsilon\right)^{2k}\alpha^{4k}.

Hence

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{1}{2}\epsilon\right)^{2k}\alpha^{4k}

\displaystyle\qquad\geq\mathop{\mathbb{E}}{\int{1_{D^{c}}(-x+y)1_{A^{\prime}}(x)1_{A^{\prime}}(y)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{B_{2}}\ast\mu_{B_{1}}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{B_{2}}\ast\mu_{B_{1}}(y)}}

\displaystyle\qquad=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}{\int{\int{1_{D^{c}}(-x+y)1_{A^{\prime}}(x)1_{A^{\prime}}(y)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{B_{2}+z}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{w+B_{1}}(y)}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{B_{1}}(z)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{B_{2}}(w)}}

\displaystyle\qquad=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}{\int{\langle 1_{D^{c}},\mu_{A^{\prime}\cap(B_{2}+z)}\ast\mu_{-(A^{\prime}\cap(w+B_{1}))}\rangle\mu_{B_{2}+z}(A^{\prime})\mu_{w+B_{1}}(A^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{B_{1}}(z)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{B_{2}}(w)}}.

(C.2)

Consider the inequality resulting from (
C.1
) minus
\kappa^{-1}
times (
C.2
). Since
(1+\frac{1}{2}\epsilon)^{2k}(1+\frac{3}{4}\epsilon)^{-2k}\leq\frac{1}{2}\kappa
, by averaging there is a choice of
x_{1},\dots,x_{2k}
,
z
and
w
such that

\mu_{B_{2}+z}(A^{\prime})\mu_{w+B_{1}}(A^{\prime})(1-\kappa^{-1}\langle 1_{D^{c}},\mu_{A^{\prime}\cap(B_{2}+z)}\ast\mu_{-(A^{\prime}\cap(w+B_{1}))}\rangle)\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\frac{3}{4}\epsilon\right)^{2k}\alpha^{4k}\geq\alpha^{4k}.

Set
T:=A^{\prime}\cap(B_{2}+z)
and
S:=-(A^{\prime}\cap(w+B_{1}))
, and the result is proved.
∎

Appendix D
A local version of Chang’s theorem

The result
[
Cha02
, Lemma 3.1]
was popularised by Green
[
Gre04
]
. We shall need the following local version.

Lemma D.1
.

Suppose
\epsilon,\alpha,\eta,\delta\in(0,1]
,
k\geq\hypertarget{const:llc}{\mbox{}}C_{9}\epsilon^{-2}\log 2\alpha^{-1}
is a natural number,
\mu_{G}({B_{0}}+kB_{1})\leq 2\mu_{G}({B_{0}})
,
\mu_{G}(B_{2}+B_{1})\leq(1+\eta)\mu_{G}(B_{1})
, and
\mu_{B_{0}}(A)\geq\alpha
. Then there is a Bohr set
B_{3}
with frequency set of size at most
k
and width
\delta
such that

|1-\gamma(x)|\leq\hypertarget{const:12}{\mbox{}}C_{10}\eta+k\delta\textrm{ for all }x\in(B_{2}-B_{2})\cap B_{3}

(D.1)

whenever
|(1_{A}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{B_{0}})^{\wedge}(\gamma)|\geq\epsilon\mu_{B_{0}}(A)
.

To prove this we make some definitions: given a set of characters
\Lambda
and a function
\omega:\Lambda\rightarrow\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|\leq 1\}
we define the Riesz product

p_{\omega}:=\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}{(1+\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits\omega(\lambda)\lambda)}.

For a probability measure
\mu
the set
\Lambda
is said to be
K
-dissociated w.r.t.
\mu
if

\int{p_{\omega}\,\mathrm{d}\mu}\leq\exp(K)\textrm{ for all }\omega:\Lambda\rightarrow\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|\leq 1\}.

We make use of this through the next proposition which is substantially the proof of
[
GR07
, Proposition 3.4]
and the duality argument of
[
Gre04
, §5]
.

Lemma D.2
.

Suppose that
\alpha,\epsilon\in(0,1]
,
\mu
a probability measure on
G
, and
\mu(A)\geq\alpha
. If
\Lambda
is
1
-dissociated w.r.t.
\mu
and a subset of

\{\gamma\in\widehat{G}:|(1_{A}\,\mathrm{d}\mu)^{\wedge}(\gamma)|\geq\epsilon\mu(A)\},

then
|\Lambda|\leq\hypertarget{const:lcb}{\mbox{}}C_{11}\epsilon^{-2}\log 2\alpha^{-1}
.

Proof.

For
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
define the linear operator

T:\ell_{2}(\Lambda)\rightarrow L_{2k}(\mu);g\mapsto\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}{g(\lambda)\lambda}.

Our first aim is to estimate the norm of
T
. For
g\in\ell_{2}(\Lambda)
we have

\int{\exp\left(\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}{g(\lambda)\lambda}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\mu}=\int{\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}{\exp(\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits(g(\lambda)\lambda))}\,\mathrm{d}\mu}.

Since
\exp(ty)\leq\cosh t+y\sinh t
whenever
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
-1\leq y\leq 1
, we have

\int{\exp\left(\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}{g(\lambda)\lambda}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\mu}\leq\int{\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda:g(\lambda)\neq 0}{\left(\cosh|g(\lambda)|+\frac{\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits(g(\lambda)\lambda)}{|g(\lambda)|}\sinh|g(\lambda)|\right)}\,\mathrm{d}\mu}.

Let
\omega:\Lambda\rightarrow\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|\leq 1\}
be such that
\omega(\lambda)|g(\lambda)|\cosh|g(\lambda)|=g(\lambda)\sinh|g(\lambda)|
, so

\int{\exp\left(\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}{g(\lambda)\lambda}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\mu}\leq\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}{\cosh|g(\lambda)|}\int{p_{\omega}\,\mathrm{d}\mu}\leq\exp(1+\|g\|_{\ell_{2}(\Lambda)}^{2}/2),

since
\cosh x\leq\exp(x^{2}/2)
and using that
\Lambda
is
1
-dissociated w.r.t.
\mu
. We conclude that

\frac{1}{(2k)!}\left\|\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}{g(\lambda)\lambda}\right\|_{L_{2k}(\mu)}^{2k}\leq\exp(1+\|g\|_{\ell_{2}(\Lambda)}^{2}/2)\text{ for all }g\in\ell_{2}(\Lambda).

Now suppose that
g\in\ell_{2}(\Lambda)
has
\|g\|_{\ell_{2}(\Lambda)}=2\sqrt{k}
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{\|Tg\|_{L_{2k}(\mu)}}{\|g\|_{\ell_{2}(\Lambda)}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\sqrt{k}}(\|\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits Tg\|_{L_{2k}(\mu)}+\|\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits Tig\|_{L_{2k}(\mu)})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\cdot(2k)!^{1/2k}\exp(K/2k+4k/4k)=O(\sqrt{k}).

In particular, it follows that
\|T\|=O(\sqrt{k})
.

The adjoint of
T
is the map
T^{*}:L_{\frac{2k}{2k-1}}(\mu)\rightarrow\ell_{2}(\Lambda);f\mapsto(f\,\mathrm{d}\mu)^{\wedge}|_{\Lambda}
, and
\|T^{*}\|=\|T\|=O(\sqrt{k})
. With this we have

|\Lambda|\epsilon^{2}\mu(A)^{2}\leq\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}{|(1_{A}\,\mathrm{d}\mu)^{\wedge}(\lambda)|^{2}}\leq\|T^{*}1_{A}\|_{\ell_{2}(\Lambda)}^{2}=O(k\|1_{A}\|_{L_{\frac{2k}{2k-1}}(\mu)}^{2})=O(k\mu(A)^{2}\alpha^{-1/2k}).

Optimising by taking
k=O(\log 2\alpha^{-1})
and rearranging gives the result.
∎

Proof of Lemma
D.1
.

Let
\hyperlink{const:llc}{C_{\ref{const:llc}}}>1
be absolute such that

\hyperlink{const:llc}{C_{\ref{const:llc}}}\epsilon^{-2}\log 2\alpha^{-1}\geq 4\hyperlink{const:lcb}{C_{\ref{const:lcb}}}\epsilon^{-2}\log 4\alpha^{-1}\text{ for all }\epsilon,\alpha\in(0,1].

(D.2)

Let

\mu:=\mu_{{B_{0}}+kB_{1}}\ast\overbrace{\mu_{-B_{1}}\ast\cdots\ast\mu_{-B_{1}}}^{k\text{ times}}.

By design and hypothesis

\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{B_{0}}\geq 1_{B_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu=\frac{\mu_{G}({B_{0}})}{\mu_{G}({B_{0}}+kB_{1})}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{B_{0}}\geq\frac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{B_{0}},

hence writing
A^{\prime}:=A\cap{B_{0}}
we have
\alpha\geq\mu(A^{\prime})\geq\frac{1}{2}\alpha
and

\Delta:=\left\{\gamma:|(1_{A}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{B_{0}})^{\wedge}(\gamma)|\geq\epsilon\alpha\right\}\subset\left\{\gamma:|(1_{A^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu)^{\wedge}(\gamma)|\geq\frac{1}{2}\epsilon\mu(A^{\prime})\right\}.

\Lambda_{0}:=\emptyset
is a subset of
\Delta
which is
0
-dissociated w.r.t.
\mu
. Suppose we have defined
\Lambda_{0},\dots,\Lambda_{j}
such that
\Lambda_{i}
is a subset of
\Delta
that is
\frac{i}{k+1}
-dissociated w.r.t.
\mu
for all
i\leq j
. If there is some
\gamma\in\Delta\setminus\Lambda_{j}
such that
\Lambda_{j}\cup\{\gamma\}
is
\frac{j+1}{k+1}
-dissociated then let
\Lambda_{j+1}:=\Lambda_{j}\cup\{\gamma\}
; otherwise, terminate the iteration.

Suppose that
\Lambda_{j}
is defined for all
j\leq k+1
. Then
\Lambda_{k+1}
is
1

\mu
. Apply Lemma
D.2
with the Lemma’s

equal to
\Lambda_{k+1}
; the Lemma’s

A^{\prime}
; the Lemma’s
\alpha

\frac{1}{2}\alpha
; and the Lemma’s

. We get that
k+1=|\Lambda_{k+1}|\leq 4\hyperlink{const:lcb}{C_{\ref{const:lcb}}}\epsilon^{-2}\log 4\alpha^{-1}
which contradicts the hypothesis on

in view of (

In particular, therefore, the iteration terminates for some

be the Bohr set with frequency set

and width

.

Suppose that

) certainly holds. Hence we may assume that

Appendix E
A local version of the Croot-Sisask almost periodicity lemma

We need the following local version of the Croot-Sisask almost periodicity lemma from
[
CS10
]
.

Lemma E.1
.

Suppose that
p,L,K\in[2,\infty)
and
\epsilon\in(0,1]
,
\mu_{G}(T+{B_{0}}-{B_{0}})\leq L\mu_{G}(T)
,
\mu_{G}(S+{B_{0}})\leq K\mu_{G}(S)
, and
f\in L_{\infty}(\mu_{T+{B_{0}}-{B_{0}}-S})
. Then there is
t\in{B_{0}}
and
X\subset{B_{0}}-t
with
\mu_{{B_{0}}-t}(X)\geq K^{-\hypertarget{const:csl}{\mbox{}}C_{12}\epsilon^{-2}L^{2/p}p}
and

\|\rho_{x}(f\ast\mu_{S})-f\ast\mu_{S}\|_{L_{p}(\mu_{T})}\leq\epsilon\sup_{s^{\prime}\in S}{\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mu_{T+{B_{0}}-{B_{0}}-s^{\prime}})}}\text{ for all }x\in X,

where
\rho_{x}(f)(y)=f(y+x)
.

To prove this we need the Marcinkiewicz–Zygmund inequality with the following ‘
p
-dependence’:

Theorem E.2

(Marcinkiewicz–Zygmund inequality,
[
RL01
, Theorem 2, p231]
)
.

Suppose that
p\in[2,\infty)
,
Z_{1},\dots,Z_{k}
are independent random variables with
\mathop{\mathbb{E}}{Z_{i}}=0
and
\mathop{\mathbb{E}}{|Z_{i}|^{p}}<\infty
. Then

\mathop{\mathbb{E}}{\left|\sum_{i=1}^{k}{Z_{i}}\right|^{p}}\leq(\hypertarget{const:mzi}{\mbox{}}C_{13}p)^{p/2}k^{p/2-1}\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\mathop{\mathbb{E}}{|Z_{i}|^{p}}}

Proof of Lemma
E.1
.

Let
k:=\lceil 64\hyperlink{const:mzi}{C_{\ref{const:mzi}}}\epsilon^{-2}L^{2/p}p\rceil
and
\hyperlink{const:csl}{C_{\ref{const:csl}}}>1
be absolute such that the inequality
\hyperlink{const:csl}{C_{\ref{const:csl}}}\epsilon^{-2}L^{2}p\geq k+1
holds. Let
s_{1},\dots,s_{k}
be independent uniformly distributed
S
-valued random variables, and for each
y\in T+{B_{0}}-{B_{0}}
define the random variable
Z_{i}(y):=f(y-s_{i})-f\ast\mu_{S}(y)
. These are independent and have mean
0
, so it follows by Theorem
E.2
that

\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s\in S^{k}}{\left|\sum_{i=1}^{k}{Z_{i}(y)}\right|^{p}}\leq(\hyperlink{const:mzi}{C_{\ref{const:mzi}}}p)^{p/2}k^{p/2-1}\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s\in S^{k}}{|Z_{i}(y)|^{p}}}\text{ for all }y\in T+{B_{0}}-{B_{0}}.

Integrating against
\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{T+{B_{0}}-{B_{0}}}(y)
and dividing by
k^{p}
we get

\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s\in S^{k}}{\int{\left|\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}{Z_{i}(y)}\right|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{T+{B_{0}}-{B_{0}}}(y)}}\leq(\hyperlink{const:mzi}{C_{\ref{const:mzi}}}pk^{-1})^{p/2}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s\in S^{k}}{\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\int{|Z_{i}(y)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{T+{B_{0}}-{B_{0}}}(y)}}}.

Now
Z_{i}(y)=f(y-s_{i})-f\ast\mu_{S}(y)
and so the right hand term can be estimated using the triangle inequality and nesting of norms:

\left(\int{|f(y-s_{i})-f\ast\mu_{S}(y)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{T+{B_{0}}-{B_{0}}}(y)}\right)^{1/p}\leq 2\sup_{s^{\prime}\in S}{\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mu_{T+{B_{0}}-{B_{0}}-s^{\prime}})}}.

Hence, recalling the notation
\rho_{-s_{i}}(f)(y)=f(y-s_{i})
and the definition of
k
, we have

\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s\in S^{k}}{\left\|\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\rho_{-s_{i}}(f)}-f\ast\mu_{S}\right\|_{L_{p}(\mu_{T+{B_{0}}-{B_{0}}})}^{p}}\leq\frac{1}{L}\left(\frac{\epsilon}{4}\right)^{p}\sup_{s^{\prime}\in S}{\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mu_{T+{B_{0}}-{B_{0}}-s^{\prime}})}^{p}}.

Write

\mathcal{L}:=\left\{s\in S^{k}:\left\|\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\rho_{-s_{i}}(f)}-f\ast\mu_{S}\right\|_{L_{p}(\mu_{T+{B_{0}}-{B_{0}}})}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2L^{1/p}}\sup_{s^{\prime}\in S}{\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mu_{T+{B_{0}}-{B_{0}}-s^{\prime}})}}\right\}.

By averaging
\mathop{\mathbb{P}}(\mathcal{L}^{c})\leq 2^{-p}
and hence
\mathop{\mathbb{P}}(\mathcal{L})\geq 1-2^{-p}\geq 1/2
. For
\Delta:=\{(x,\dots,x):x\in{B_{0}}\}
we have
\mathcal{L}+\Delta\subset(S+{B_{0}})^{k}
, so
\mu_{G^{k}}(\mathcal{L}+\Delta)\leq 2K^{k}\mu_{G^{k}}(\mathcal{L})
. By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality

\displaystyle\langle 1_{\Delta}\ast 1_{-\Delta},1_{-\mathcal{L}}\ast 1_{\mathcal{L}}\rangle_{L_{2}(\mu_{G^{k}})}

\displaystyle=\|1_{\mathcal{L}}\ast 1_{\Delta}\|_{L_{2}(\mu_{G^{k}})}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2K^{k}}\mu_{G^{k}}(\Delta)^{2}\mu_{G^{k}}(\mathcal{L}).

Hence there is
t\in{B_{0}}
and
0_{G}\in X\subset{B_{0}}-t
with
\mu_{G}(X)\geq\mu_{G}({B_{0}})/2K^{k}\geq K^{-(k+1)}\mu_{G}({B_{0}})
such that for all
x\in X
then there is
s\in\mathcal{L}
such that
s-(x,\dots,x)\in\mathcal{L}
, and in particular

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\rho_{-(s_{i}-x)}(f)}-\rho_{x}(f\ast\mu_{S})\right\|_{L_{p}(\mu_{T})}=\left\|\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\rho_{-s_{i}}(f)}-f\ast\mu_{S}\right\|_{L_{p}(\mu_{T+x})}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\left(\frac{\mu_{G}(T+{B_{0}}-{B_{0}})}{\mu_{G}(T)}\right)^{1/p}\frac{\epsilon}{2L^{1/p}}\sup_{s^{\prime}\in S}{\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mu_{T+{B_{0}}-{B_{0}}-s^{\prime}})}}.

Finally, apply the triangle inequality to add the two inequalities when
x=0_{G}
and when
x\in X
is arbitrary.
∎
