A categorical Torelli theorem for quartic del Pezzo surfaces
Alexey Elagin

Abstract
. We solve categorical Torelli problem for quartic del Pezzo surfaces. That is, we prove that a del Pezzo surface of degree 4 4 can be canonically reconstructed from its Kuznetsov component, which is the orthogonal subcategory to the structure sheaf in the derived category of the surface. Our methods work in equivariant setting and over arbitrary perfect fields. Using recent theory of atomic semi-orthogonal decompositions [ 11 ] , we conclude that two minimal quartic del Pezzo surfaces are birational if and only if they are isomorphic. We also verify that the Kuznetsov component of a minimal quartic del Pezzo surface is semi-orthogonally indecomposable, confirming a conjecture by Auel and Bernardara [ 2 ] .



1.
Introduction

The bounded derived category of coherent sheaves
\Db(X)
on an algebraic variety
X
is a fundamental invariant of
X
, whose systematic study as such dates back to the 1980s.
The category
\Db(X)
encodes a substantial amount of geometric information about
X
. In particular, by
[
5
]
, a smooth projective variety
X
can be reconstructed from
\Db(X)
provided that the canonical divisor
K_{X}
is either ample or anti-ample. Nevertheless, in general, non-isomorphic varieties may have equivalent derived categories; see, for example,
[
26
]
.

In this note we address the problem of reconstructing a variety
X
not from the entire derived category
\Db(X)
, but from a certain admissible subcategory
{\mathbf{A}}_{X}\subset\Db(X)
, commonly referred to in the literature as the
residual component
or the
Kuznetsov component
. Although there is no uniform definition of this component, it is known to exist for many Fano varieties and is believed to be the “essential part” of the derived category.

The following question is known as the
categorical Torelli problem
:

Given two varieties
X
and
Y
such that the categories
{\mathbf{A}}_{X}
and
{\mathbf{A}}_{Y}
are equivalent, is it true that
X
and
Y
are isomorphic?

Significant progress on this problem has been achieved in recent years. In particular, an affirmative answer has been obtained for many Fano hypersurfaces
[
23
,
24
,
29
]
, for certain Fano threefolds
[
15
,
14
]
, and for Enriques surfaces
[
21
,
22
]
. We refer to
[
28
]
for a survey of these and some other related results.

In this note we solve the categorical Torelli problem for del Pezzo surfaces of degree
4
. Note that for del Pezzo surfaces of degree
\geqslant 5
there is no common understanding of what the Kuznetsov component is; moreover, these surfaces have no moduli, so the categorical Torelli problem in this case is not particularly meaningful. Del Pezzo surfaces of degree
3
are cubic hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
, and, over algebraically closed fields, the categorical Torelli problem admits an affirmative solution by the general results mentioned above, see
[
24
, Cor. 1.6]
. We will address the categorical Torelli problem for del Pezzo surfaces of degrees
2
and
1
in a subsequent work.

By definition, the Kuznetsov component of a quartic del Pezzo surface
X
is the right orthogonal subcategory
{\mathbf{A}}_{X}={\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{\perp}
to the structure sheaf in the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves on
X
.

Assume first that the base field is algebraically closed. In this case, there is a description of
{\mathbf{A}}_{X}
due to Bondal–Orlov and Kuznetsov
[
4
,
17
]
: the category
{\mathbf{A}}_{X}
is equivalent to the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves on a weighted projective line
\mathbb{P}_{X}
canonically associated with
X
. This weighted projective line is given by the pencil of quadrics in
\mathbb{P}^{4}
containing the anticanonical image of
X
, it carries five weighted points of weight
2
, corresponding to the degenerate quadrics in the pencil.

The facts that the category
{\mathbf{A}}_{X}\cong\Db(\coh\mathbb{P}_{X})
determines the weighted projective line
\mathbb{P}_{X}
up to isomorphism, and that
\mathbb{P}_{X}
in turn determines
X
up to isomorphism, are likely known to experts and/or can be obtained by standard technique. What appears to be new in our approach is that the reconstruction of
X
from
{\mathbf{A}}_{X}
can be carried out in a
canonical
manner: to any equivalence of components
{\mathbf{A}}_{X}\xrightarrow{\sim}{\mathbf{A}}_{Y}
we can naturally associate an isomorphism of surfaces
X\xrightarrow{\sim}Y
.

To make precise what we mean by “naturally associate” such an isomorphism, we invoke the notion of a
heavily separable
functor
[
1
]
. A functor
\Phi\colon{\mathbf{C}}\to{\mathbf{D}}
is called heavily separable (Definition
2.20
) if, for all objects
X,Y\in{\mathbf{C}}
, there exist maps

\Hom(\Phi(X),\Phi(Y))\longrightarrow\Hom(X,Y)

that are left inverses to the maps

\Hom(X,Y)\longrightarrow\Hom(\Phi(X),\Phi(Y))

induced by
\Phi
, and that are compatible with composition.

Our first main result is the following theorem (which remains of independent interest even in the case where the group
G
is trivial). We emphasize that we do not impose any
\Bbbk
-linearity assumptions on the group actions, isomorphisms, or equivalences under consideration.

Theorem A

(Theorem
3.2
)
.

Let
\Bbbk
be an algebraically closed field of
\har\neq 2
and
G
be a group. Then the natural functor from the category of
G
-del Pezzo surfaces of degree
4
over
\Bbbk
and
G
-isomorphisms to the category of triangulated
\Bbbk
-linear categories with a
G
-action and
G
-equivalences, sending a surface
X
to its Kuznetsov component
{\mathbf{A}}_{X}
, is heavily separable. In particular, if for two such
G
-surfaces
X,Y
the categories
{\mathbf{A}}_{X},{\mathbf{A}}_{Y}
are
G
-equivalent, then
X,Y
are
G
-isomorphic.

In contrast to most results on the categorical Torelli problem, we do not restrict to algebraically closed fields.
Using Theorem
A
and Galois descent, we deduce that the reconstruction of
X
from
{\mathbf{A}}_{X}
is possible for del Pezzo surfaces of degree
4
over an arbitrary perfect field.

Theorem B

(Theorem
3.3
)
.

Let
\Bbbk
be a perfect field of
\har\neq 2
. Let
X,Y
be two del Pezzo surfaces of degree
4
over
\Bbbk
. If there is a
\Bbbk
-linear equivalence
{\mathbf{A}}_{X}\xrightarrow{\sim}{\mathbf{A}}_{Y}
of Fourier–Mukai type, then the surfaces
X,Y
are isomorphic over
\Bbbk
.

In particular, our results apply to the case when
X
is a
minimal
del Pezzo surface over a perfect field
\Bbbk
: that is, when
\Pic X
has rank
1
. The subcategory
{\mathbf{A}}_{X}
in this case was studied in
[
2
]
as the
Griffiths–Kuznetsov component
of
X
. In a recent work
[
11
]
, the subcategory
{\mathbf{A}}_{X}
was called the
atom
of
X
and was shown to be a birational invariant of minimal del Pezzo surfaces of degree
4
. Moreover, it was shown that any surface birational to
X
has a copy of
{\mathbf{A}}_{X}
as an admissible subcategory in its derived category. Combining this with Theorem
B
, we get the following observation:

Theorem C

(Theorem
3.6
, see also
[
11
, Th. 5.5]
)
.

Let
\Bbbk
be a perfect field of
\har\neq 2
, and let
X,Y
be minimal del Pezzo surfaces of degree
4
over
\Bbbk
.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

the surfaces
X
and
Y
are isomorphic over
\Bbbk
;

(b)

the surfaces
X
and
Y
are birational over
\Bbbk
;

(c)

the categories
{\mathbf{A}}_{X}
and
{\mathbf{A}}_{Y}
are
\Bbbk
-linearly equivalent via an equivalence of Fourier–Mukai type.

This result confirms the conjecture
[
11
, Conj. 5.29]
, which states that two geometrically rational surfaces are birational if and only if they have the same non-trivial atoms.
Recently, the equivalence (a)
\Longleftrightarrow
(b) has been obtained independently by classical methods of birational geometry in
[
30
]
.

Finally, as a result of independent interest, we prove the following.

Theorem D

(Theorems
4.1
,
4.4
)
.

Let
\Bbbk
be a perfect field of
\har\neq 2
, and let
X
be a minimal del Pezzo surface of degree
4
over
\Bbbk
. Then the category
{\mathbf{A}}_{X}
is semi-orthogonally indecomposable.
Moreover, let
f\colon X\to C
be a relatively minimal conic bundle over a smooth curve. If
C
is rational, suppose that
K_{X}^{2}\leqslant 4
. Then the category
{\mathbf{A}}_{X/C}:=\ker f_{*}\subset\Db(X)
is semi-orthogonally indecomposable.

This result confirms the conjecture
[
2
, Conj. 5.6]
, which claims indecomposability of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{\perp}\subset\Db(X)
for any minimal del Pezzo surface
X
of degree
\leqslant 4
. It also justifies the name “atom” (which is Greek for indecomposable) used for the categories
{\mathbf{A}}_{X}
,
{\mathbf{A}}_{X/C}
in
[
11
]
.

Let us now discuss the proofs. For a del Pezzo surface
X
of degree
4
, we use the equivalence
{\mathbf{A}}_{X}\cong\Db(\coh\mathbb{P}_{X})
and employ results of
[
19
]
describing the autoequivalence groups of the categories
\coh\mathbb{P}_{X}
and
\Db(\coh\mathbb{P}_{X})
. These results imply that any equivalence
\Db(\coh\mathbb{P}_{X})\xrightarrow{\sim}\Db(\coh\mathbb{P}_{Y})
, up to some shift, sends
\coh\mathbb{P}_{X}
to
\coh\mathbb{P}_{Y}
, and any equivalence
\coh\mathbb{P}_{X}\to\coh\mathbb{P}_{Y}
induces an isomorphism
\mathbb{P}_{X}\to\mathbb{P}_{Y}
. It is not hard to see then (Lemma
2.8
) that there is an isomorphism
X\xrightarrow{\sim}Y
.

To prove Theorem
A
, we compare the automorphism group of
X
with the autoequivalence group of
{\mathbf{A}}_{X}
. The key statement (Lemma
2.18
) is that the natural group homomorphism
\Aut(X)\to\Aut({\mathbf{A}}_{X})
is split, i.e., has a left inverse. To prove this statement, we observe that these two groups act compatibly on the weighted projective line
\mathbb{P}_{X}
, on the set
H_{X}\subset\Pic X
of ten divisor classes of conic bundles on
X
, and on the set of five degenerate quadrics containing
X
. For
\Aut(X)
this is obvious, while for
\Aut({\mathbf{A}}_{X})
such actions are due to the facts that
{\mathbf{A}}_{X}\cong\Db(\coh\mathbb{P}_{X})
and that
\mathbb{P}_{X}
,
H_{X}
, and
Q_{X}
are intrinsic to the abelian category
\coh\mathbb{P}_{X}
. From these actions we obtain group homomorphisms

\alpha\colon\Aut(X)\to\Aut^{\ev}(H_{X},Q_{X})\times_{\Aut(Q_{X})}\Aut(\mathbb{P}_{X})

and

\beta\colon\Aut({\mathbf{A}}_{X})\to\Aut(H_{X},Q_{X})\times_{\Aut(Q_{X})}\Aut(\mathbb{P}_{X}),

where
\Aut(H_{X},Q_{X})
denotes the group of permutations of
H_{X}
compatible with the natural map
H_{X}\to Q_{X}
, and

\Aut^{\ev}(H_{X},Q_{X})
denotes the subgroup of even permutations. To construct the desired splitting, we observe that
\alpha
is an isomorphism and the embedding of groups
\Aut^{\ev}(H_{X},Q_{X})\to\Aut(H_{X},Q_{X})
is a split homomorphism.

Given that the homomorphism
\Aut(X)\to\Aut({\mathbf{A}}_{X})
splits, the proof of Theorem
A
is purely by abstract category theory, see Lemma
2.21
. Theorem
B
follows from Theorem
A
by Galois descent, and Theorem
C
is a combination of Theorem
B
and results from
[
11
]
.

Theorem
D
is based on results from our paper
[
12
]
, where triangulated subcategories on weighted projective curves are studied. The main result that we use is that most admissible subcategories on a weighted projective curve are generated by uniquely determined
\Hom
-orthogonal exceptional collections of sheaves.

The structure of the paper is as follows. In Section
2
we collect the necessary facts about quartic del Pezzo surfaces, their automorphisms, categories of coherent sheaves on weighted projective lines, and their autoequivalences. Section
2
culminates in proving that the natural homomorphism
\Aut(X)\to\Aut({\mathbf{A}}_{X})
splits. We also discuss heavily separable functors in this section. After proper preparation, we prove Theorems
A
,
B
,
C
in Section
3
. Section
4
is relatively self-contained and devoted to the proof of Theorem
D
.

1.2.
Conventions

We work over a perfect field
\Bbbk
of characteristic
\neq 2
which is not necessarily algebraically closed. Algebraic varieties are supposed to be smooth projective and over
\Bbbk
, but morphisms are not supposed to be morphisms over
\Bbbk
by default. Similarly, functors between
\Bbbk
-linear categories are always additive but not necessarily
\Bbbk
-linear. Functors between triangulated categories are always supposed to be exact. When we consider group actions/
G
-varieties, we make no implicit assumptions, in particular, groups can be infinite or act non-faithfully.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Coxeter groups of type
B_{5}
and
D_{5}

Let
H
be a finite set of
10
elements,
Q
be a finite set of
5
elements, and
\pi\colon H\to Q
be a
2
-to-
1
map.
Denote by
\Aut(H)\cong S_{10}
and
\Aut(Q)\cong S_{5}
the groups of permutations of
H
and
Q
, respectively, denote by
\Aut(H,Q)
the subgroup
\{(f,g)\mid\pi f=g\pi\}\subset\Aut(H)\times\Aut(Q)
of compatible permutations of
H
and
Q
, and by
\Aut(H/Q)\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{5}
the subgroup
\{f\mid\pi f=\pi\}\subset\Aut(H)
of permutations of
H
over
Q
. There is an exact sequence of groups

(2.1)

\{e\}\to\Aut(H/Q)\to\Aut(H,Q)\to\Aut(Q)\to\{e\},

given by the maps
(f)\mapsto(f,e)
and
(f,g)\mapsto g
.
Let
\Aut^{\ev}(H,Q)
be the subgroup
\{(f,g)\mid\text{$f$ is even})\}\subset\Aut(H,Q)
and
\Aut^{\ev}(H/Q)\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{4}
be the subgroup of even permutations in
\Aut(H/Q)
.
These groups form an exact sequence, which is a subsequence of (
2.1
)

(2.2)

\{e\}\to\Aut^{\ev}(H/Q)\to\Aut^{\ev}(H,Q)\to\Aut(Q)\to\{e\}.

Note that the groups
\Aut(H,Q)
and
\Aut^{\ev}(H,Q)
are isomorphic to Coxeter groups of types
B_{5}
and
D_{5}
, respectively.

The following easy lemma will be important for us.

Lemma 2.1
.

The inclusion
\Aut^{\ev}(H,Q)\to\Aut(H,Q)
splits as a group homomorphism over
\Aut(Q)
.

Proof.

Let
c\in\Aut(H/Q)
be the product of five transpositions in the fibres of
\pi
, it is an odd central element of order
2
. Then
\Aut(H,Q)
is the direct product of its subgroups
\Aut^{\ev}(H,Q)\times\langle c\rangle
, and the projection
\Aut(H,Q)\to\Aut^{\ev}(H,Q)
gives the desired splitting.
∎

2.2.
Weighted projective lines and their autoequivalences

We refer to
[
13
]
,
[
20
]
, and
[
19
]
for the definitions and necessary results about weighted projective lines, their categories of coherent sheaves, and the autoequivalences of these categories.
In this subsection we assume that the base field
\Bbbk
is algebraically closed.

A weighted projective line
\mathbb{P}=(P,w)
over
\Bbbk
consists of a projective line
P\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\Bbbk}
over
\Bbbk
together with a weight function

w\colon P(\Bbbk)\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 1}
such that
w(p)=1
for all but finitely many points
p\in P(\Bbbk)
. Points
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}
where
w>1
are called
weighted points
and the integers
w_{i}=w(p_{i})
,
i=1,\ldots,n
are called the
weights
at these points. The collection of weights
(w_{1},\ldots,w_{n})
is called the
type
of
\mathbb{P}
. An
isomorphism of weighted projective lines

(P,w)\xrightarrow{\sim}(P^{\prime},w^{\prime})
is an isomorphism
f\colon P\xrightarrow{\sim}P^{\prime}
of varieties that preserves the weights, that is, such that
w=w^{\prime}f
. We denote by
\Aut(\mathbb{P})
the automorphism group of
\mathbb{P}
and by
\Aut_{\Bbbk}(\mathbb{P})
the subgroup consisting of
\Bbbk
-linear automorphisms.

Remark 2.2
.

Note that
\Aut_{\Bbbk}(\mathbb{P})
is a subgroup of
\Aut_{\Bbbk}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\Bbbk})\cong\PGL_{2}(\Bbbk)
. Moreover, if the number
n
of weighted points of
\mathbb{P}
is at least three, then
\Aut_{\Bbbk}(\mathbb{P})
is a subgroup of
\Aut(\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}\})
, hence is finite. Indeed, a
\Bbbk
-linear automorphism of
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\Bbbk}
is determined by the image of any three points.

The category of coherent sheaves on
\mathbb{P}=(P,w)
can be described following Geigle and Lenzing, see
[
13
]
.
Let
V={\mathrm{H}}^{0}(P,{\mathcal{O}}(1))
, choose non-zero linear forms
u_{i}\in V
such that
u_{i}(p_{i})=0
. Denote

S_{\mathbb{P}}:=(S^{\bullet}(V)\otimes\Bbbk[U_{1},\ldots,U_{n}])/(U_{1}^{w_{1}}-u_{1},\ldots,U_{n}^{w_{n}}-u_{n}),

where
S^{\bullet}(V)
denotes the symmetric algebra.
Let
\mathbb{L}=\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{P}}
be the abelian group generated by elements
\bar{c},\bar{x}_{1},\ldots,\bar{x}_{n}
and relations
\bar{c}=w_{i}\cdot\bar{x}_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
. Then
S_{\mathbb{P}}
is an
\mathbb{L}
-graded commutative algebra with the grading given by
\deg(U_{i})=\bar{x}_{i}
,
\deg(v)=\bar{c}
for
v\in V
.
Define
\coh\mathbb{P}
as the Serre quotient

\coh\mathbb{P}=\frac{\mathrm{mod}^{\mathbb{L}}{-}S_{\mathbb{P}}}{\mathrm{mod}_{0}^{\mathbb{L}}{-}S_{\mathbb{P}}},

where
\mathrm{mod}^{\mathbb{L}}{-}S_{\mathbb{P}}
is the category of finitely generated
\mathbb{L}
-graded
S_{\mathbb{P}}
-modules, and
\mathrm{mod}_{0}^{\mathbb{L}}{-}S_{\mathbb{P}}\subset\mathrm{mod}^{\mathbb{L}}{-}S_{\mathbb{P}}
is the subcategory of finite-dimensional modules. Objects of
\coh\mathbb{P}
are called
coherent sheaves
on
\mathbb{P}
. For a sheaf
F
and an element
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{L}
, denote by
F({\overline{x}})
the sheaf obtained from
F
by twisting with
{\overline{x}}
: that is, by shifting the grading by
{\overline{x}}
.
The category
\coh\mathbb{P}
is a
\Bbbk
-linear abelian hereditary
\Ext
-finite category. It is
equipped with a translation functor
\tau\colon\coh\mathbb{P}\to\coh\mathbb{P}
given by twisting with the canonical element

\bar{\omega}:=-2{\overline{c}}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(w_{i}-1){\overline{x}}_{i}.

The shift
\tau[1]
of the translation functor serves as a Serre functor on the derived category
\Db(\coh\mathbb{P})
, so
\tau
is intrinsic to the category
\coh\mathbb{P}
.

Let
\coh_{0}\mathbb{P}\subset\coh\mathbb{P}
be the full subcategory of torsion sheaves, that is, of objects of finite length. There is a decomposition

\coh_{0}\mathbb{P}=\coprod_{p\in P(\Bbbk)}\coh_{p}\mathbb{P},

where
\coh_{p}\mathbb{P}
is the full subcategory of sheaves supported at the closed point
p\in P
. Each category
\coh_{p}\mathbb{P}
has
w(p)
simple objects and they form one orbit under
\tau
. Therefore, the set of closed points of
P
is identified with the set of
\tau
-orbits of simple objects in
\coh\mathbb{P}
, and the multiplicities are recovered as the lengths of the orbits. Moreover, if a simple sheaf
F
is supported at a weighted point
p_{i}
then all simple sheaves supported at
p_{i}
are
F(k{\overline{x}}_{i})
,
k=0,\ldots,w_{i}-1
, and
F\cong F({\overline{c}})\cong F({\overline{x}}_{j})
for
i\neq j
. We remark that
\mathbb{P}
is canonically determined by
\coh\mathbb{P}
in the following sense.

Lemma 2.3
.

For any weighted projective lines
\mathbb{P},\mathbb{P}^{\prime}
over
\Bbbk
,
any equivalence
\phi\colon\coh\mathbb{P}\xrightarrow{\sim}\coh\mathbb{P}^{\prime}
defines canonically an isomorphism
f=\phi_{\mathbb{P}}\colon\mathbb{P}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb{P}^{\prime}
, which is compatible with
\phi
via the action on simple sheaves: for any point
p\in P(\Bbbk)
one has
\phi(\coh_{p}\mathbb{P})=\coh_{f(p)}\mathbb{P}^{\prime}
as subcategories in
\coh\mathbb{P}^{\prime}
. Moreover, if
\phi
is linear over
\Bbbk
then
f
also is.

Proof.

See the proof of
[
19
, Prop. 3.1, Th. 3.4]
. They assume that
\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}^{\prime}
and
\phi
is
\Bbbk
-linear but the proof works in a more general setting as well.
∎

We will be interested in autoequivalences of the abelian category
\coh\mathbb{P}
. Denote by
\Aut(\coh\mathbb{P})
the group of isomorphism classes of autoequivalences of
\coh\mathbb{P}
, and by
\Aut_{\Bbbk}(\coh\mathbb{P})
the subgroup formed by
\Bbbk
-linear autoequivalences. First, there are geometric equivalences: any isomorphism
f\colon\mathbb{P}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb{P}^{\prime}
of weighted projective lines induces an equivalence
\coh\mathbb{P}\xrightarrow{\sim}\coh\mathbb{P}^{\prime}
given by the direct image functor. There are also geometrically trivial autoequivalences: for any
{\overline{x}}\in\mathbb{L}
, the shift of grading by
{\overline{x}}
defines an autoequivalence of
\coh\mathbb{P}
, which does not change the support of sheaves. These two types of autoequivalences generate the whole group
\Aut(\coh\mathbb{P})
:

Lemma 2.4
.

There are natural split exact sequences of groups

\displaystyle 0\to\mathbb{L}\to\Aut(\coh\mathbb{P})\to\Aut(\mathbb{P})\to\{e\},

\displaystyle 0\to\mathbb{L}\to\Aut_{\Bbbk}(\coh\mathbb{P})\to\Aut_{\Bbbk}(\mathbb{P})\to\{e\}.

Proof.

This is
[
19
, Th. 3.4]
for
\Bbbk
-linear autoequivalences/automorphisms, but their proof works in the general case as well.
∎

The degree homomorphism
\deg\colon\mathbb{L}\to\mathbb{Q}
is given by
\deg({\overline{c}})=1,\deg({\overline{x}}_{i})=\frac{1}{w_{i}}
. The kernel of
\deg
is the torsion subgroup
\mathbb{L}^{\tors}\subset\mathbb{L}
.

Properties of the category
\coh\mathbb{P}
depend on whether the degree
\deg(\bar{\omega})
of the canonical element is negative, zero, or positive. A weighted projective line is called
domestic/tubular/wild
in these three cases respectively.

Since the category
\coh\mathbb{P}
is hereditary, its derived category
\Db(\coh\mathbb{P})
has a very simple structure: any object is isomorphic to the direct sum of its cohomology sheaves. In particular, any indecomposable object is a shift of an indecomposable sheaf. The group
\Aut(\Db(\coh\mathbb{P}))
of isomorphism classes of autoequivalences of
\Db(\coh\mathbb{P})
is described in
[
19
]
; we will only need this description in the wild case.

Lemma 2.5
.

For a weighted projective line
\mathbb{P}
not of tubular type there is a direct product decomposition

\Aut(\Db(\coh\mathbb{P}))\cong\Aut(\coh\mathbb{P})\times\mathbb{Z},

where
\mathbb{Z}
acts by cohomological shifts. Moreover, if
\mathbb{P},\mathbb{P}^{\prime}
are weighted projective lines not of tubular type and
\phi\colon\Db(\coh\mathbb{P})\xrightarrow{\sim}\Db(\coh\mathbb{P}^{\prime})
is an equivalence, then there is some
n\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\phi[n]
restricts to an equivalence
\coh\mathbb{P}\xrightarrow{\sim}\coh\mathbb{P}^{\prime}
. Similar statements hold for
\Bbbk
-linear autoequivalences.

Proof.

See
[
19
, Prop. 4.1, Prop. 4.2]
for the first statement. The second is proved in the same way as
[
19
, Prop. 4.1]
.
∎

From now on we restrict ourselves to weighted projective lines of type
(2,2,2,2,2)
. The degree of
\bar{\omega}

2.3.
Quartic del Pezzo surfaces and their automorphisms

We refer to
[
25
]
and
[
9

for the basic definitions and results about del Pezzo surfaces.
In this subsection we assume that the base field
\Bbbk
is algebraically closed and of characteristic
\neq 2

Let
X
be a del Pezzo surface over
\Bbbk
of degree
4
, that is, a smooth irreducible projective surface over
\Bbbk
with
-K_{X}
ample and
(-K_{X})^{2}=4
. Such a surface is isomorphic to the blow-up of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
at five general points. Any such isomorphism defines a standard basis
H,E_{1},\ldots,E_{5}
in the Picard group
\Pic(X)
, where
H
is the pull-back of a line and
E_{1},\ldots,E_{5}
are exceptional divisors. The intersection form in this basis is given by
H^{2}=1,E_{i}^{2}=-1,H\cdot E_{i}=E_{i}\cdot E_{j}=0
for
i\neq j
, and the canonical class is
K_{X}=-3H+E_{1}+\ldots+E_{5}
. Let
H_{X}\subset\Pic(X)
be the set of
0
-classes:

H_{X}=\{h\in\Pic(X)\mid h^{2}=0,h\cdot K_{X}=-2\}.

Any
h\in H_{X}
is represented by a smooth rational curve and the linear system
|h|
defines a conic bundle
X\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
. There are
10

H_{X}
; in the standard basis they are

H-E_{1},\ldots,H-E_{5},2H-E_{2}-E_{3}-E_{4}-E_{5},\ldots,2H-E_{1}-E_{2}-E_{3}-E_{4}.

Divisors in
H_{X}
are naturally divided into pairs: in any such pair
h,h^{\prime}
one has
h+h^{\prime}=-K_{X}
.

The anticanonical system
|-K_{X}|
defines an embedding
\phi\colon X\to\mathbb{P}^{4}=\mathbb{P}({\mathrm{H}}^{0}(X,\omega_{X}^{-1}))
, whose image is the intersection of a pencil of quadrics. More precisely, the family of quadrics containing the image of
\phi
is one-dimensional; we will denote this family
\mathbb{P}({\mathrm{H}}^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{4},I_{\phi(X)}(2)))\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
by
P_{X}
. A general quadric in
P_{X}
is non-degenerate; there are five degenerate quadrics in
P_{X}
, all of them of corank
1
. We will denote the subset of degenerate quadrics in
P_{X}
by
Q_{X}
. We define the weighted projective line
\mathbb{P}_{X}
as
P_{X}
with weights
2
at the five points of
Q_{X}
. The association
X\mapsto\mathbb{P}_{X}
is canonical in the sense that it extends to isomorphisms between surfaces: any isomorphism
f\colon X\xrightarrow{\sim}Y
of surfaces induces an isomorphism of projective lines
P_{X}\xrightarrow{\sim}P_{Y}
that respects the weighted points, that is, induces an isomorphism
\mathbb{P}_{X}\to\mathbb{P}_{Y}
. The isomorphism class of
X
is uniquely determined by the weighted projective line
\mathbb{P}_{X}
. More precisely, one has the following:

Let
X,Y

There is a natural bijection between pairs of divisors in

2.4.
Orthogonal to the structure sheaf on quartic del Pezzo surfaces

As before, we assume that the base field
\Bbbk
is algebraically closed and of characteristic
\neq 2
.
Let
X
be a del Pezzo surface of degree
4
over
\Bbbk
.

Definition 2.11

Define the subcategory
{\mathbf{A}}_{X}\subset\Db(X)
as the right orthogonal subcategory to the structure sheaf:

{\mathbf{A}}_{X}:={\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{\perp}=\{F\in\Db(X)\mid\Hom({\mathcal{O}}_{X},F[i])=0\quad\text{for all $i\in\mathbb{Z}$}\}.

there is a

We will need a more refined statement, which is proved in

Let

be the sheaf of even parts of Clifford algebras on the projective line

of quadrics containing

There is a
\Bbbk

We explain below that

({\mathbf{A}}_{X},((\rho_{g})_{{\mathbf{A}}}))
and
f
to
f_{\mathbf{A}}

are heavily separable. In particular, if, for
G
-del Pezzo surfaces
X
and
Y
of degree
4
, the categories
{\mathbf{A}}_{X}
and
{\mathbf{A}}_{Y}
are
G
-equivariantly equivalent (resp.
\Bbbk
-linearly
G
-equivariantly equivalent), then
X
and
Y
are
G
-equivariantly isomorphic (resp.
G
-equivariantly isomorphic over
\Bbbk
).

Proof.

Everything follows from Theorem
3.1
.
We will prove that the first functor is heavily separable; for the second one, the arguments are the same. Let
X,Y
be
G
-del Pezzo surfaces of degree
4
. Let
u\colon{\mathbf{A}}_{X}\xrightarrow{\sim}{\mathbf{A}}_{Y}
be a
G
-equivariant equivalence. Let
f=\Psi_{X,Y}(u)
be the isomorphism
X\xrightarrow{\sim}Y
provided by Theorem
3.1
. We have to prove that
f
is
G
-equivariant, that is, for all
g\in G
, we have

\rho_{g}^{Y}f=f\rho_{g}^{X}
. Indeed, we have
(\rho_{g}^{Y})_{\mathbf{A}}\circ u\cong u\circ(\rho_{g}^{X})_{\mathbf{A}}
since
u
is
G
-equivariant. Applying
\Psi_{X,Y}
from Theorem
3.1
, we get the desired equality:

\Psi_{X,Y}((\rho_{g}^{Y})_{\mathbf{A}}\circ u)=\Psi_{Y,Y}((\rho_{g}^{Y})_{\mathbf{A}})\Psi_{X,Y}(u)=\Psi_{Y,Y}(\Phi_{Y,Y}(\rho_{g}^{Y}))f=\rho_{g}^{Y}f,

and similarly,
\Psi_{X,Y}(u\circ(\rho_{g}^{X})_{\mathbf{A}})=f\rho_{g}^{X}
. Therefore, the maps
\Psi_{X,Y}
from Theorem
3.1
take
G
-equivariant equivalences to
G
-equivariant isomorphisms, and satisfy the properties from Definition
2.20
again by Theorem
3.1
.
∎

3.2.
Arbitrary perfect field case

Now we assume that
\Bbbk
is any perfect field and
\bar{\Bbbk}
is its algebraic closure. The field extension
\Bbbk\subset\bar{\Bbbk}
is a (possibly infinite) Galois extension; let
G
be its Galois group. We suppose that
\har\Bbbk\neq 2
.
For a del Pezzo surface
X
over
\Bbbk
, we denote by
{\overline{X}}
the scalar extension
X\times_{\Bbbk}\Spec\bar{\Bbbk}
, which is a del Pezzo surface over
\bar{\Bbbk}
. As for the algebraically closed case, the orthogonal subcategory
{\mathbf{A}}_{X}={\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{\perp}\subset\Db(X)
is defined, and, as before, we prove that
X
is uniquely determined by this subcategory.

Let us say that an equivalence
{\mathbf{A}}_{X}\xrightarrow{\sim}{\mathbf{A}}_{Y}
is
of Fourier–Mukai type
if
the composition

\Db(X)\to{\mathbf{A}}_{X}\xrightarrow{\Phi}{\mathbf{A}}_{Y}\to\Db(Y)

is given by a Fourier–Mukai kernel
{\mathcal{E}}\in\Db(X\times Y)
(where the first and the last arrows are the projection and the inclusion functors, respectively). In the statement below, we restrict ourselves to equivalences of Fourier–Mukai type.

Theorem 3.3
.

If
X,Y
are del Pezzo surfaces of degree
4
over a perfect field
\Bbbk
with
\har\Bbbk\neq 2
, and there is a
\Bbbk
-linear equivalence
{\mathbf{A}}_{X}\xrightarrow{\sim}{\mathbf{A}}_{Y}
of Fourier–Mukai type, then
X
is
\Bbbk
-linearly isomorphic to
Y
.

Remark 3.4
.

We believe that an analogue of Theorem
3.1
holds for any perfect field: the natural functor
(\dP 4,\Iso_{\Bbbk})\to(\TrCat,\Iso_{\Bbbk})
is heavily separable; that is, for any
\Bbbk
-linear equivalence
{\mathbf{A}}_{X}\xrightarrow{\sim}{\mathbf{A}}_{Y}
there is a well-defined isomorphism
X\xrightarrow{\sim}Y
over
\Bbbk
. Its proof consists of three steps: (1) given an equivalence
u\colon{\mathbf{A}}_{X}\xrightarrow{\sim}{\mathbf{A}}_{Y}
, construct a
G
-equivariant equivalence
{\mathbf{A}}_{{\overline{X}}}\xrightarrow{\sim}{\mathbf{A}}_{{\overline{Y}}}
, (2)
construct a
G
-equivariant isomorphism
{\overline{X}}\to{\overline{Y}}
, (3) obtain an isomorphism
X\xrightarrow{\sim}Y
. Step (2) is by Theorem
3.2
, while step (3) is standard Galois descent.
However, doing step (1)
would require knowing that
u
is given by a unique Fourier–Mukai kernel or lifts properly to a dg-enhancement. We prefer not to overload the paper with the relevant details and to prove a weaker statement as in Theorem
3.3
.

Proof of Theorem
3.3
.

We deduce the statement by passing to the algebraic closure and using Theorem
3.2
. Note that
{\overline{X}}
and
{\overline{Y}}
carry natural
G
-actions and may be viewed as
G
-surfaces. We refer to
[
11
, Section 3.5]
for the Galois descent for subcategories in derived categories of coherent sheaves.

First, we claim that the orthogonal subcategories
{\mathbf{A}}_{{\overline{X}}}
and
{\mathbf{A}}_{{\overline{Y}}}
for
{\overline{X}}
and
{\overline{Y}}
are
\bar{\Bbbk}
-linearly
G
-equivariantly equivalent. To explain this, assume that a
\Bbbk
-linear equivalence
{\mathbf{A}}_{X}\to{\mathbf{A}}_{Y}
is of Fourier–Mukai type and is given by a kernel
{\mathcal{E}}
. Consider the scalar extension
\bar{{\mathcal{E}}}\in\Db({\overline{X}}\times{\overline{Y}})
of
{\mathcal{E}}
. By
[
18
, Section 6]
, the kernel
\bar{{\mathcal{E}}}
produces an equivalence
{\mathbf{A}}_{{\overline{X}}}\xrightarrow{\sim}{\mathbf{A}}_{{\overline{Y}}}
,
which is clearly
\bar{\Bbbk}
-linear and
G
-equivariant.
We use now Theorem
3.2
to obtain a
\bar{\Bbbk}
-linear
G
-equivariant isomorphism
f\colon{\overline{X}}\xrightarrow{\sim}{\overline{Y}}
. By Galois descent,
f
produces a
\Bbbk
-linear isomorphism
X\xrightarrow{\sim}Y
.
∎

Using

For an abelian group

A

-del Pezzo surface

.

Let

, and let

over

. Suppose a group

acts on

(not necessarily

-linearly) so that

are

Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

surfaces

and

are
G
-isomorphic;

(b)

surfaces

and

are

-birational;

(c)

categories
{\mathbf{A}}_{X}
and
{\mathbf{A}}_{Y}
are
G
-equivariantly equivalent.

Proof.

Implication
(a)

\implies

(b)
is trivial. Implication
(b)

\implies

(c)
follows from
[
11
, Prop. 5.1]
and Definition
[
11
, Def. 4.21]
of atoms for a

-minimal del Pezzo surface of degree

. Implication
(c)

\implies

is by Theorem
3.2
.
∎

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\Bbbk
be a perfect field of characteristic
\neq 2

.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

.

. Note that

4.
Indecomposability of the orthogonal subcategory

In the final section we establish that the orthogonal component on
minimal
del Pezzo surfaces of degree
4
is semi-orthogonally indecomposable; an analogous statement holds for minimal conic bundles of degree
\leqslant 4
.

Recall that a
G
-del Pezzo surface
X
is called
G
-minimal
if
\rank(\Pic(X)^{G})=1
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\Bbbk
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
\neq 2
, and let
X
be a del Pezzo surface of degree
4
over
\Bbbk
. Suppose a group
G
acts on
X
(not necessarily
\Bbbk
-linearly) in such a way that
X
is
G
-minimal. Then the orthogonal subcategory
{\mathbf{A}}_{X}
admits no semi-orthogonal decompositions into
G
-invariant subcategories.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
\Bbbk
be a perfect field of characteristic
\neq 2
, and let
X
be a minimal del Pezzo surface of degree
4
over
\Bbbk
. Then the orthogonal subcategory
{\mathbf{A}}_{X}
admits no semi-orthogonal decompositions.

Proof of Corollary
4.2
.

Consider the scalar extension
{\overline{X}}=X\times_{\Bbbk}\Spec\bar{\Bbbk}
, endowed with the natural action of the Galois group
G=\Gal(\bar{\Bbbk}/\Bbbk)
. The surface
{\overline{X}}
is
G
-minimal, hence Theorem
4.1
is applicable to
{\overline{X}}
.

The claim then follows from the behaviour of semi-orthogonal decompositions under scalar extension: any semi-orthogonal decomposition of
{\mathbf{A}}_{X}
induces, by scalar extension, a
G
-invariant semi-orthogonal decomposition of
{\mathbf{A}}_{{\overline{X}}}
; see, for instance,
[
11
, Section 3.5]
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Since
X
is rational, there exists a
G
-equivariant isomorphism (see, for instance,
[
16
, Lemma 4.2]
)

K_{0}(X)\xrightarrow{(\rank,c_{1},\chi)}\mathbb{Z}\oplus\Pic(X)\oplus\mathbb{Z},

where the group
G
acts trivially on both copies of
\mathbb{Z}
. Consequently, we obtain

\rank((K_{0}(X)^{G}))=3.

In the semi-orthogonal decomposition

\Db(X)=\langle{\mathbf{A}}_{X},\langle{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\rangle\rangle

the structure sheaf
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
is
G
-invariant and exceptional. Hence, passing to
G
-invariants on
K
-theory, we deduce

\rank((K_{0}({\mathbf{A}}_{X})^{G}))=2.

Recall that there is an equivalence of triangulated categories

{\mathbf{A}}_{X}\cong\Db(\coh\mathbb{P}),

where
\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}_{X}
is the weighted projective line of type
(2,2,2,2,2)
associated with
X
. The group
G
acts on the abelian category
\coh\mathbb{P}
in such a way that the above equivalence is
G
-equivariant. Therefore,

\rank((K_{0}(\coh\mathbb{P})^{G}))=\rank((K_{0}({\mathbf{A}}_{X})^{G}))=2.

Thus, the proof is reduced to a statement concerning weighted projective lines; see Proposition
4.3
below.
∎

In the following proposition, we work in a more general setting by considering not only weighted projective lines, but also weighted projective curves of arbitrary genus, subject to only mild constraints on the weights. This generalization is motivated by subsequent applications; see Theorem
4.4
. Let
\mathbb{P}
denote a weighted projective curve (see, for instance,
[
12
]
).

We write
K_{0}(\mathbb{P})=K_{0}(\coh\mathbb{P})
for the Grothendieck group of the abelian category
\coh\mathbb{P}
, and denote by
K_{0}(\mathbb{P})_{\numerical}
the numerical Grothendieck group, defined as the quotient of
K_{0}(\mathbb{P})_{\mathbb{Q}}
by the kernel of the Euler form. Observe that if
\mathbb{P}
is rational, then the Euler form is non-degenerate, and consequently
K_{0}(\mathbb{P})_{\numerical}=K_{0}(\mathbb{P})_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Assume that the base field
\Bbbk
is algebraically closed.
Let
\mathbb{P}
be a weighted projective curve over
\Bbbk
and, in the case where the underlying curve is rational, suppose in addition that
\mathbb{P}
is not of domestic type. Furthermore, let a group
G
act on the abelian category
\coh\mathbb{P}
of coherent sheaves on
\mathbb{P}
in such a way that
\rank(K_{0}(\mathbb{P})_{\numerical}^{G})=2
.
Under these assumptions, the bounded derived category
\Db(\coh\mathbb{P})
does not admit semi-orthogonal decompositions into
G
-invariant subcategories.

Proof.

Let
F^{1}K_{0}(\mathbb{P})_{\numerical}\subset K_{0}(\mathbb{P})_{\numerical}
be the subspace generated by classes of torsion sheaves; then there is a
G
-invariant exact sequence
0\to F^{1}K_{0}(\mathbb{P})_{\numerical}\to K_{0}(\mathbb{P})_{\numerical}\xrightarrow{\rank}\mathbb{Q}\to 0
. It follows that

(4.1)

\rank(F^{1}K_{0}(\mathbb{P})_{\numerical}^{G})=1.

The proof of the Proposition is based on the analysis of triangulated subcategories in
\Db(\coh\mathbb{P})
from
[
12
]
. Recall the necessary results. For a thick triangulated subcategory
{\mathbf{A}}\subset\Db(\coh\mathbb{P})
let
{\mathcal{A}}={\mathbf{A}}\cap\coh\mathbb{P}
; this is an exact abelian subcategory of
\coh\mathbb{P}
and the natural functor
\Db({\mathcal{A}})\to\Db(\coh\mathbb{P})
is fully faithful with image
{\mathbf{A}}
, see, for instance,
[
12
, Prop. 3.7]
.
Such a triangulated subcategory
{\mathbf{A}}
is called
quiver-like
if the abelian category
{\mathcal{A}}
is equivalent to the category
\mathrm{mod}_{0}{-}\Bbbk\Gamma
of nilpotent finite-dimensional representations of some quiver
\Gamma
. In this case the simple objects of
{\mathcal{A}}
correspond to simple modules concentrated at vertices of
\Gamma
. If
{\mathbf{A}}
is left or right admissible then
\Gamma
has to be finite and without oriented cycles (
[
12
, Prop. 4.6]
); in particular, the simple objects of
{\mathcal{A}}
form a full exceptional collection in
{\mathbf{A}}
.

Assume that a semi-orthogonal decomposition
\Db(\coh\mathbb{P})=\langle{\mathbf{A}},{\mathbf{B}}\rangle
into non-trivial subcategories is given. Then one of the following holds by
[
12
, Th. 8.2, Def. 7.1]
:

(a)

both
{\mathbf{A}}
and
{\mathbf{B}}
are quiver-like;

(b)

either
{\mathbf{A}}
or
{\mathbf{B}}
is quiver-like and contains only torsion sheaves.

Assume case
(a)
. Let
E_{1},\ldots,E_{n}
and
F_{1},\ldots,F_{m}
be full exceptional collections in
{\mathbf{A}},{\mathbf{B}}
formed by simple objects in
{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}
respectively. Then
\Db(\mathbb{P})
has a full exceptional collection, therefore

K_{0}(\mathbb{P})
is finitely generated, and
\mathbb{P}
must be rational, so we have
K_{0}(\mathbb{P})_{\numerical}=K_{0}(\mathbb{P})_{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Since
G
preserves the abelian category
{\mathcal{A}}
,
G
must permute its simple objects
E_{i}
, and similarly for
F_{j}
. It is easy to see that the collections
E_{1},\ldots,E_{n}
and
F_{1},\ldots,F_{m}
split into exceptional orthogonal blocks (for instance, see
[
3
, Lemma 2.12]
), each being a
G
-orbit of sheaves. For any such
G
-orbit

\{E_{i}\}_{i\in I}
the class
\oplus_{i\in I}[E_{i}]\in K_{0}(\mathbb{P})
is
G
-invariant, and such classes are linearly independent in
K_{0}(\mathbb{P})
because the classes
[E_{1}],\ldots,[E_{n}],[F_{1}],\ldots,[F_{m}]
of objects of a full exceptional collection form a basis in
K_{0}(\mathbb{P})
. By the assumption
\rank(K_{0}(\mathbb{P})^{G})=2
we get that there are just two such orbits, that is,
G
acts transitively on the orthogonal blocks
E_{1},\ldots,E_{n}
and
F_{1},\ldots,F_{m}
. By Lemma
4.6
below, the graded vector space
\Hom^{\bullet}(\oplus_{i}E_{i},\oplus_{j}F_{j})
is concentrated in one degree
d=0
or
1
. Then the collection
E_{1},\ldots,E_{n},F_{1}[d],\ldots,F_{m}[d]
is a full strong exceptional collection in
\Db(\coh\mathbb{P})
, consisting of two blocks. It provides (by
[
7
]
) an equivalence
\Db(\coh\mathbb{P})\cong\Db(\mathrm{mod{-}}A)
, where
A=\End((\oplus_{i}E_{i})\oplus(\oplus_{j}F_{j}[d]))
is the path algebra of a bipartite quiver, hence
A
is hereditary. This gives a contradiction because
\mathbb{P}
is a weighted projective line not of domestic type, see, for instance,
[
20
, Section 10.1]
.

Assume now case
(b)
; suppose without loss of generality that
{\mathbf{A}}
is quiver-like and contains only torsion sheaves. Let
E_{1},\ldots,E_{n}
be a full exceptional collection in
{\mathbf{A}}
formed by simple objects in
{\mathcal{A}}
. As above, we observe that this collection splits into orthogonal blocks which are
G
-orbits. Let
\{E_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be such a block. Consider the classes
e=\oplus_{i\in I}[E_{i}]
and
f=[{\mathcal{O}}_{x}]
in
K_{0}(\mathbb{P})_{\numerical}
, where
x\in\mathbb{P}
is any non-weighted point. These classes are
G
-invariant; also, they are not proportional. Indeed, for the Euler form one has
\chi(e,e)=\sum_{i\in I}\chi(E_{i},E_{i})=|I|>0
since
\{E_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is an orthogonal exceptional block, but
\chi(f,f)=0
. Note that the
E_{i}
’s are torsion sheaves, so both
e,f\in F^{1}K_{0}(\mathbb{P})_{\numerical}^{G}
, and we get a contradiction with (
4.1
).

Therefore both cases
(a)
and
(b)
are impossible and
\Db(\mathbb{P})
has no semi-orthogonal decompositions into
G
-invariant subcategories.
∎

Using Proposition
4.3
we can also show indecomposability of the “big atom” of a minimal two-dimensional conic bundle. Let
\Bbbk
be algebraically closed. Assume

is a conic bundle over a smooth projective curve

, and assume a group

acts on

and

compatibly. There is a

where

is the full subcategory of objects sent to zero by the derived push-forward functor

and

is the image of the fully faithful derived pull-back functor

.
It is said that

is

-minimal
if

; suppose this is the case. Assume that

is irrational or

is rational and

. Then in
[

, Defs. 2.11, 4.21, 5.11, Prop. 2.12]
the category

is called the

of

. Now we justify this name by showing that

is indeed indecomposable.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\Bbbk
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
\neq 2
, let
f\colon X\to C
be a conic bundle over a smooth curve. Suppose that
C
is irrational or

is rational and

. Assume a group

acts on

and

compatibly (not necessarily

-linearly) so that

is relatively

-minimal. Then the big atom
{\mathbf{A}}_{X/C}
has no semi-orthogonal decompositions into

-invariant subcategories.

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
\Bbbk
be a perfect field of characteristic
\neq 2
, let
f\colon X\to C
be a relatively minimal conic bundle over a smooth curve. Suppose that
C
is geometrically irrational or
C
is geometrically rational and

. Then the big atom
{\mathbf{A}}_{X/C}
has no semi-orthogonal decompositions.

Proof of Corollary
4.5
.

See the proof of Corollary
4.2
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.4
.

As in the proof of Theorem
4.1
we get a constraint on the rank of the
G
-invariant part of the Grothendieck group of
{\mathbf{A}}_{X/C}
. To accommodate irrational base curves, we need to consider the
numeric
version of the relevant invariants.
Let
K_{0}({\mathbf{T}})_{\numerical}=(K_{0}({\mathbf{T}})_{\mathbb{Q}})/\ker\chi
be the numeric Grothendieck group of a triangulated category
{\mathbf{T}}
(with respect to the Euler form
\chi
). For
0\leqslant k\leqslant 2
, let
N^{k}(X)_{\numerical}=(A^{k}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}})/_{\equiv}
be the
k
-th numeric Chow group of
X
. Denote by
\Pic(X)_{\numerical}=(\Pic(X)_{\mathbb{Q}})/\equiv
the numeric Picard group of
X
(with respect to the intersection form). Then the Chern character gives an isomorphism (for instance, by
[
10
, Lemma 2.1]
)

\mathrm{Ch}\colon K_{0}(X)_{\numerical}\xrightarrow{\sim}N^{0}(X)_{\numerical}\oplus N^{1}(X)_{\numerical}\oplus N^{2}(X)_{\numerical}\cong\mathbb{Q}\oplus\Pic(X)_{\numerical}\oplus\mathbb{Q}.

This map is
G
-equivariant and the action on both summands
\mathbb{Q}
is trivial. Therefore we have
\rank(K_{0}(X)_{\numerical}^{G})=\rank(\Pic(X)_{\numerical}^{G})+2
. For the projective curve
C
we have:

K_{0}(C)_{\numerical}\cong\mathbb{Q}^{2}
(rank and degree form a dual basis in this space) and
\Pic(C)_{\numerical}:=\Pic(C)_{\mathbb{Q}}/\Pic_{0}(C)_{\mathbb{Q}}\cong\mathbb{Q}
(with degree forming a dual basis). The group
G
acts trivially on these spaces, so we get
\rank(K_{0}(C)_{\numerical}^{G})=2
,
\rank(\Pic(C)_{\numerical}^{G})=1
.
Note that
\Pic_{0}(X)=\Pic_{0}(C)
, so there is an exact sequence

0\to\Pic(C)_{\numerical}^{G}\to\Pic(X)_{\numerical}^{G}\to\Pic(X/C)_{\mathbb{Q}}^{G}\to 0,

from which we get
\rank(\Pic(X)_{\numerical}^{G})=1+1=2
. Hence
\rank(K_{0}(X)_{\numerical}^{G})=2+2=4
.
The semi-orthogonal decomposition (
4.2
) yields an isomorphism

K_{0}(X)_{\numerical}^{G}\cong K_{0}({\mathbf{A}}_{X/C})_{\numerical}^{G}\oplus K_{0}(C)_{\numerical}^{G}
,
and we conclude that
\rank(K_{0}({\mathbf{A}}_{X/C})_{\numerical}^{G})=2
.

Let
C_{1}\subset C
be the finite set of closed points parametrising degenerate conics (i.e. reducible fibres of
f
). Then
X
is the blow-up of
n=|C_{1}|
points in fibres of a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle over
C
. If
C
is rational, it follows from the assumption
K_{X}^{2}\leqslant 4
that
n\geqslant 4
.

Consider the weighted projective curve
\mathbb{P}
obtained from
C
by putting weights
2
at points of
C_{1}
. Then by
[
17
, Th. 4.2, Cor. 3.16]
there is an equivalence

{\mathbf{A}}_{X/C}\cong\Db(\coh\mathbb{P}).

Moreover, by construction of
loc. cit.
the
G
-action on
{\mathbf{A}}_{X/C}
comes from a
G
-action on
\coh\mathbb{P}
.
We have
\rank(K_{0}(\mathbb{P})_{\numerical}^{G})=\rank(K_{0}({\mathbf{A}}_{X/C})_{\numerical}^{G})=2
.

We can apply Proposition
4.3
to finish the proof. Note that our assumptions exclude the case of a domestic weighted projective line: if
C
is rational then the number
n
of weighted points is
\geqslant 4
.
∎

Finally we prove the lemma used in the proof of Proposition
4.3
.
Variants of this lemma are standard; we recall the proof in the form suitable for our situation.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
be a weighted projective line and
(E_{1},\ldots,E_{n},F_{1},\ldots,F_{m})
be an exceptional collection of coherent sheaves on
\mathbb{P}
, where
(E_{1},\ldots,E_{n})
and
(F_{1},\ldots,F_{m})
are orthogonal blocks. Assume a group of autoequivalences of
\coh\mathbb{P}
is given such that
\{E_{1},\ldots,E_{n}\}
and
\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{m}\}
are orbits. Then either
\Hom(E_{i},F_{j})=0
for all
i,j
or
\Ext^{1}(E_{i},F_{j})=0
for all
i,j
.

Proof.

Assume that
\Ext^{1}(E_{i},F_{j})\neq 0
for some
i,j
; without loss of generality we may suppose that
i=j=1
. Consider the left mutation
{\tilde{F}}_{1}
of
F_{1}
through
(E_{1},\ldots,E_{n})
(see
[
6
]
), defined by the exact triangle

\oplus_{i}(\Hom^{\bullet}(E_{i},F_{1})\otimes E_{i})\to F_{1}\to{\tilde{F}}_{1}\to.

Since the object
{\tilde{F}}_{1}
is indecomposable, it has non-zero cohomology exactly in one degree. The long exact sequence of cohomology sheaves associated with the above triangle is

0\to{\mathrm{H}}^{-1}({\tilde{F}}_{1})\to\oplus(\Hom(E_{i},F_{1})\otimes E_{i})\to F_{1}\to{\mathrm{H}}^{0}({\tilde{F}}_{1})\to\oplus(\Ext^{1}(E_{i},F_{1})\otimes E_{i})\to 0.

Since
\Ext^{1}(E_{1},F_{1})\neq 0
it follows that
{\mathrm{H}}^{0}({\tilde{F}}_{1})\neq 0
and hence
{\mathrm{H}}^{-1}({\tilde{F}}_{1})=0
. Therefore the evaluation map
\oplus(\Hom(E_{i},F_{1})\otimes E_{i})\to F_{1}
is injective and hence any non-zero map
E_{i}\to F_{1}
is also injective.

From the group symmetry it follows that any non-zero map
E_{i}\to F_{j}
is injective for all
i,j
. Similarly one shows, using a right mutation, that any non-zero map
E_{i}\to F_{j}
is surjective for all
i,j
. Hence any non-zero map
E_{i}\to F_{j}
must be an isomorphism and therefore cannot exist.
∎
