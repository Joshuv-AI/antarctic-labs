Benefits of doula care for labor duration and maternal health during vaginal delivery
Authors: Yun Lin, Lijie Lu, Shunping Hu, Lizhen Hu, Yuhong Pan, Jing Wang, Chaoqun Tan, Tingsong Weng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundDoulas are trained professionals that provide comprehensive emotional, physical, and informational support to women during the perinatal period. The aim of this study was to evaluate the utility and benefits of doula care on the labor duration and maternal health during vaginal delivery.MethodsThis retrospective cohort study involved 2095 women with vaginal deliveries. In the control group, women received essential perinatal and intrapartum cares. In the doula group, the women received additional doula cares during labor, including music, labor ball, birth dance, positioning cushion, massage and breathing exercises. The primary outcomes of interest were labor duration and perineal status. Secondary outcome measures included delivery mode, the occurrence of prenatal fever, the characteristic of amniotic fluid and the amount of postpartum bleeding within 24 h.ResultsCompared to the control group, women who received doula cares experienced significantly shorter labor durations across all stages (first-stage, second-stage, and total) (all p < 0.001). Additionally, the doula group had a lower incidence of episiotomy and second-degree lacerations (21.98% vs. 33.69%, p < 0.001; 7.42% vs. 8.98%, p = 0.002). Furthermore, forceps delivery was more prevalent in the control group (10%) than in the doula group (7%) (p = 0.033), while postpartum bleeding was significantly reduced in the doula care group compared to the control group (227.38 ± 3.21 vs. 247.15 ± 3.69, p < 0.001). However, there were no significant differences in the characteristics of amniotic fluid or the occurrence of prenatal fever between the two groups.ConclusionDoula care during vaginal delivery could shorten labor durations, reduce the need for episiotomy and the incidence of severe perineal lacerations, and decrease the incidence of forceps delivery and postpartum bleeding. These findings supported the promotion of doula care to enhance natural deliveries and ensure maternal and child safety.

Introduction

Fertility is declining in all countries and territories. During the period from 1950 to 2021, global total fertility rate more than halved, from 4.84 (95% CI 4.63–5.06) to 2.23 (95% CI 2.09–2.38) [1]. China had the largest population in the world for many decades [2]. However, its total fertility rate also has been continuously declining since 1990s, reaching 1.3 by 2020 [3]. In 2023, just 9.02 million babies were born in China, half the number born in 2016 [4]. Multiple factors contribute to the decline in fertility rates. Therein, women’s fear of labor and birth is an important reason. The prevalence of “fear of childbirth” for pregnant women was reported as 5–42% in the early studies [5, 6]. A serious fear of childbirth affected 6–10% of expectant mothers, which resulted in several complications, including fetal distress, prolonged labor, dystocia, and hypoxia, as well as postnatal depression and posttraumatic stress disorder [7]. A previous negative or traumatic childbirth experience may further exacerbate women’s fear of childbirth [8–10]. It is a common problem affecting women’s health and wellbeing [11]. Thus, it is vital to identify and develop effective interventions for alleviating women’s fear of childbirth.

Women’s feelings of uncertainty about the birth process seem to be a basis for their fear of giving birth [12, 13]. A doula refers to a trained professional who provides comprehensive emotional, physical, and informational support to mother during the perinatal period [14]. They offer continuous support throughout the labor and delivery process, advocating for the mother’s preferences and providing comfort measures such as massage, positioning suggestions, and relaxation techniques [15]. The use of doulas was introduced in the late 1960s and established as a profession in 1992 by Doulas of North America (DONA) [14]. Numerous studies have demonstrated that the utilization of doulas is an effective strategy for reducing the likelihood of experiencing postpartum depression or anxiety, as well as lowering the rate of caesarean births [16–19]. Unfortunately, limited studies have examined the impact of doula care on maternal health during vaginal delivery. In this study, we analyzed the labor processes of over 2000 women with vaginal deliveries, including perineal statuses, labor durations, delivery modes, the occurrence of prenatal fever, and the amount of postpartum bleeding. This study aimed at evaluating the utility and efficacy of doula care on the maternal health during vaginal delivery.

Materials and methods

Patients

This was a retrospective cohort study. From January 1, 2023, and December 31, 2023, a total of 2095 women with vaginal deliveries in Guangzhou Women and Children’s Medical Center were included in this study. The inclusion criteria were (1) age 18–40 years old, (2) term pregnancy, (3) singleton pregnancy, (4) underwent vaginal delivery. The exclusion criteria were (1) those who had natural deliveries converted to cesarean deliveries, (2) those with vaginal birth after cesarean, (3) those with potential postpartum hemorrhage, (4) those with abnormal fetal positioning (5) those were in induced labor due to a stillbirth, (6) those suffering from psychiatric disorders or cognitive illnesses, (7) those who had no normal language communication and expression abilities. The key baseline characteristics (including age, educational level, monthly income, parity) between the excluded women and the enrolled vaginal delivery cohort. Statistical analysis confirmed no significant differences in these core variables between the two groups (all p > 0.05).

This study was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee of Guangzhou Women and Children’s Medical Center. Written informed consent was obtained from each woman.

Procedures

When the women entered the stage of labor, the detailed information regarding the specific services and fee of doula cares was provided to the women. Written informed consents were obtained from women prior to providing doula cares. Depending on their receipt of doula care, all registered women were categorized into two groups. The decision to receive doula care or not was made entirely based on the patients’ own voluntary choice during their prenatal consultations or upon admission to the labor ward.

In the control group, those women received essential perinatal and intrapartum cares providing by both physicians and midwives. This included regular prenatal check-ups, monitoring the progress of labor, and continuous electronic fetal heart monitoring. It also encompassed postpartum care for the mother and newborn immediately after birth.

In the doula group, the women received additional doula cares when the uterine orifice opened for 2–3 cm. All doulas underwent rigorous and specialized training to ensure they possessed the necessary skills and knowledge to provide comprehensive support. A trained doula would utilize various tools and techniques during labor, including music, labor ball, birth dance, positioning cushion, massage and breathing exercises. During the entire process of labor and delivery, the doulas would play relax music and guide the women through slow and deep breathing. Special positioning cushions or pillows were used to support specific body parts during labor. Meanwhile, the doulas also provided gentle massage to these specific body parts, such as the back or feet. During labor and delivery, the doulas expertly assisted the women in adopting a comfortable free position, by utilizing a large and soft labor ball. Doulas would guide the women through a series of slow and intentional dance movements tailored to her comfort level and the stage of labor. During the active and second stage of labor, doulas would use lubricant and gentle pressure to massage the perineal tissues for women. In addition, doulas offered continuous emotional support and provided information on pregnancy, childbirth, breastfeeding, and newborn care to the women. They answered questions and addressed concerns that arise during labor and delivery.

Measures

All data analyzed were retrospectively extracted from the hospital’s electronic medical records. Demographics questions were asked upon entering the predelivery room to gather information about the women current age, BMI, highest level of education, monthly income, last menstrual period, due date, and parity, complications during pregnancy.

The primary outcomes of interest were labor duration and perineal status. The total duration of labor encompasses all three stages. The first stage of labor refers to regular contractions until full cervical dilation. The second stage of labor commences at 10 cm cervical dilation and ends on delivery of the neonate. Perineal lacerations occurred spontaneously or iatrogenically, as with an episiotomy. Perineal lacerations are classified into four basic categories [20, 21]. First-degree laceration is superficial injury to the vaginal mucosa that may involve the perineal skin. Second-degree laceration is deeper, involving the vaginal mucosa and perineal body. Third-degree laceration extends to the anal sphincter, and fourth-degree laceration involves the rectal mucosa.

The secondary outcome measures included delivery mode, the occurrence of prenatal fever, the characteristic of amniotic fluid and the amount of postpartum bleeding within 24 h. The modes of vaginal delivery include normal vaginal delivery and assisted vaginal delivery, with the latter exclusively involving forceps in this study. An axillary temperature more than 37.5 °C during labor is considered as prenatal fever. For the characteristic of amniotic fluid, the normal amniotic fluid refers to a clear or slightly yellowish fluid without any color, odor, or abnormal substances. Meconium-stained amniotic fluid (MSAF) refers to the presence of meconium in the amniotic fluid and is classified into grades 1 to 3 according to its thickness (grade 1: lightly stained amniotic fluid, green or yellow; grade 2: green- or yellow-stained amniotic fluid with some particulate matter; and grade 3: dense meconium with “pea-soup” consistency) [22].

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed by SPSS software version 25.0 (IBM Corporation; United States). The general characteristics of women in two groups were analyzed by Student’s t test or Pearson’s χ2 test. The variations in the duration of labor and the amount of postpartum bleeding between doula group and control group were determined by Mann-Whitney U test. The Pearson’s χ2 test was used to compare the delivery mode, episiotomy and the occurrence of prenatal fever between two groups. The Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel chi-squared test analyzed the degree of perineal laceration and the characteristic of amniotic fluid. A threshold of p less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Duration of labor

The durations of labor for two groups were shown in Table 2; Fig. 1. The first stage of labor was significantly shorter in the doula group compared to the control group (518.99 ± 9.27 vs. 604.30 ± 9.32 min, p < 0.001). Additionally, the second stage of labor for the doula group was also significantly shorter than that of control group (28.08 ± 0.85 min vs. 33.01 ± 0.85 min, p < 0.001). Overall, the total duration of labor was shorter in the doula group (551.18 ± 9.59 min for doula group, 641.55 ± 9.56 min for control group, p < 0.001).

Perineal status

In this study, 21.98% of women receiving doula cares underwent episiotomy, compared to 33.69% in the control group (p < 0.001). For those who experienced perineal lacerations, 63.28% in the doula group suffered from first-degree lacerations, and 7.42% had second-degree lacerations. In the control group, 55.73% had first-degree lacerations, and 8.98% had second-degree lacerations. Therefore, the incidence of second-degree lacerations was higher in the control group (p = 0.002) (Table 3). No patients in either group experienced third- or fourth-degree perineal lacerations.

Secondary outcomes

In the control group, 10% of women underwent forceps delivery (n = 107), a rate significantly higher than the 7% in the doula group (n = 87) (p = 0.033). The amount of postpartum bleeding for those who received doula care was significantly less than that in the control group (227.38 ± 3.21 vs. 247.15 ± 3.69 ml, p < 0.001). Whereas, there was no difference in the characteristics of amniotic fluid between two groups (p = 0.338). Both the doula group and the control group had approximately 15% of women experiencing prenatal fever, with no statistically significant difference between two groups (p = 0.618) (Table 3).

Discussion

The present study indicated that women who received doula cares during labor and delivery had shorter labor durations compared to those who received standard perinatal and intrapartum care, encompassing the first-stage, second-stage, and total labor durations. Additionally, our study demonstrated that doula care helped reducing the need for episiotomy and the incidence of severe perineal lacerations. Furthermore, the doula care effectively decreased the incidence of forceps delivery and postpartum bleeding. On this basis, we investigated the benefits of doula care on the maternal health during vaginal delivery.

Prolonged labor or dystocia, which refers to arrest disorders during active phase or second stage of labor, has been identified as a major factor contributing to the increasing rates of primary cesarean deliveries [23, 24]. In addition, longer durations of active and second stage of labor have been associated with higher rates of maternal and neonatal morbidity [25, 26]. For infants, prolonged durations of labor lead to a series of complications, such as choking, neurophysiological issues, and even death [27]. For women, prolonged durations of labor can increase the risk of uterine inertia, postpartum hemorrhage, psychological distress, and fatigue [28]. In this study, we found that doula care could shorten the duration of labor. During labor and delivery, the doulas expertly instructed pregnant women to use labor balls and engage in birth dances. A large and soft labor ball enables the mother to perform gentle pelvic rocking and stretching movements, which in turn facilitates baby’s descent [29]. Through widening the pelvic outlet, upright positioning by using labor ball is thought to decrease the length of labor and promote spontaneous vaginal delivery. A recent systematic review and meta-analysis also demonstrated that the use of the labor ball compared with no labor ball is associated with a significantly decrease in the first stage of labor [30]. Birth dance is a series of slow and intentional dance movements. The upright and swaying movements involved in birth dances can assist in the alignment of the baby and the pelvis, encourage the baby to descend into the pelvis and navigate through the birth canal, potentially aiding in the efficient progression of labor [31].

Perineal lacerations following vaginal childbirth are prevalent, impacting approximately 9 out of 10 women [32]. Such lacerations can result in both immediate and enduring complications, particularly in cases of severe lacerations [33]. Perineal lacerations are frequently linked to heightened susceptibility to pelvic floor damage, urinary and fecal incontinence, discomfort, and sexual dysfunction, with symptoms that may endure for many years postpartum [34]. Accordingly, prioritizing advancements in clinical care to mitigate this risk and lessen the occurrence of perineal lacerations is paramount. This study indicated doula cares could reduce the incidence of episiotomy and severe perineal lacerations. The doulas commenced perineal massage for women in the second stage of labor in this study. Perineal massage may increase the flexibility and elasticity of pelvic muscles and allow stretching of the perineum during birth. These may help to reduce the risk of perineal lacerations and the need for episiotomy during childbirth [35]. In a systemic review of 17 randomized controlled trials encompassing 3248 women, Li Y et al. observed that perineal massage administered during the second stage of labor increased the occurrence of intact perineum, reduced the rate of second- and third-degree perineal lacerations, and decreased the incidence of episiotomy. Furthermore, perineal massage during the first stage of labor was found to be effective in shortening the duration of the first and second stages of labor [36]. Additionally, guiding the women to use labor balls by doulas may also help to facilitate the dilation of the pelvic floor muscles, thereby reducing the occurrence of perineal lacerations and the need for episiotomy.

Compared to other studies, the strength of our study lay in the inclusion of a significantly large number of women, approximately 2000 in total. Furthermore, this study was the first to explore the benefit of doula care for the perineal status during vaginal delivery. Nevertheless, there are a few limitations that need to be considered. First, our study was not a double-blinded randomized controlled trial, which could lead to diagnostic suspicion bias and reporting bias. Second, bias may have been introduced due to the fee of doula care, as women who could not afford or were unwilling to pay the fee may have been more likely to choose standard care. Third, the exclusion of women who underwent caesarean sections may limit the generalizability of our findings and introduce potential selection bias. Fourth, the use of different doula techniques across individuals may also lead to bias. Fifth, although we adjusted for parity, the slight difference between groups (53.75% vs. 51.15%) may still have introduced bias. Finally, a multicenter study with a larger sample size is necessary to further evaluate the advantages of doula care, and follow-up is essential to assess longer-term outcomes.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study demonstrated that the implementation of doula cares during vaginal delivery could shorten labor durations and reduce the incidence of episiotomy and severe perineal lacerations. Meanwhile, doula care effectively decreased the incidence of forceps delivery and postpartum bleeding. These findings provide evidence to support the necessity of promoting doula care during labor and delivery, which will enhance the rate of natural deliveries and ensure maternal and child safety.
