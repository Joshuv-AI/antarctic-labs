The Frobenius problem for a class of quotients of numerical semigroups
Feihu Liu

Abstract
Given a numerical semigroup S S and a positive integer p p , the quotient S p = { x ∈ ℕ ∣ p ​ x ∈ S } \frac{S}{p}=\{x\in\mathbb{N}\mid px\in S\} also forms a numerical semigroup. In this paper, we first characterize the Apéry set for a class of quotients of numerical semigroups. Under certain conditions, we then derive half-closed form formulas for their Frobenius number and genus. Furthermore, for specific values of part parameters, we obtain explicit formulas for the Frobenius number of certain quotients of numerical semigroups.

Introduction

Let
\mathbb{N}
denote the set of all non-negative integers.
A subset
S
of
\mathbb{N}
is called a
submonoid
if it contains
0
and is closed under addition in
\mathbb{N}
.
When
\mathbb{N}\setminus S
is finite, we call
S
a
numerical semigroup
.
Given a sequence
A=(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n})
of positive integers, we denote by
\langle A\rangle
the submonoid of
\mathbb{N}
generated by
A
, which consists of all finite linear combinations of elements of
A
with non-negative integer coefficients:

\langle A\rangle=\left\{x_{1}a_{1}+x_{2}a_{2}+\cdots+x_{n}a_{n}\mid x_{i}\in\mathbb{N}\quad\text{for}\quad 1\leq i\leq n\right\}.

In
[
19
, Lemma 2.1]
, it is shown that
\langle A\rangle
forms a numerical semigroup if and only if
\gcd(A)=1
.
When this condition holds, we refer to
A
as a
system of generators
for the numerical semigroup
\langle A\rangle
.
Throughout this paper, we assume
\gcd(A)=1
.

This has led many researchers to investigate the following fundamental invariants:
1) The
Frobenius number
, denoted by
F(\langle A\rangle)
, is the largest integer not contained in
\langle A\rangle
.
2) The
genus
(or
Sylvester number
), denoted by
g(\langle A\rangle)
, counts the number of positive integers not belonging to
\langle A\rangle
. In other words,

F(\langle A\rangle)=\max\{\mathbb{N}\setminus\langle A\rangle\}\quad\text{and}\quad g(\langle A\rangle)=\#\{\mathbb{N}\setminus\langle A\rangle\}.

Given a sequence
A
of positive integers, computing
F(\langle A\rangle)
and
g(\langle A\rangle)
is often referred to as the
Frobenius Problem
or the
Coin Exchange Problem
, see
[
14
]
.
The determination of
F(\langle A\rangle)
was shown by Ramírez Alfonsín
[
15
]
to be NP-hard under Turing reduction.

Sylvester
[
25
]
established the following formulas

\displaystyle F(\langle a_{1},a_{2}\rangle)=a_{1}a_{2}-a_{1}-a_{2}\quad\text{and}\quad g(\langle a_{1},a_{2}\rangle)=\frac{1}{2}(a_{1}-1)(a_{2}-1).

For the case when
n\geq 3
, Curtis
[
5
]
demonstrated that no closed-form expression of a certain type exists for
F(A)
. Nevertheless, numerous special cases have been investigated; we refer to
[
14
, Section 2; Section 3]
and
[
12
]
for detailed results.

Let
p\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Here
\mathbb{Z}^{+}
denotes the set of all positive integers.
We define the
quotient of
\langle A\rangle
by
p
, denoted by
\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}
, to be the set consisting of all non-negative integers
x
such that
px\in\langle A\rangle
; that is,

\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}=\{x\in\mathbb{N}\mid px\in\langle A\rangle\}.

It is easy to verify (see
[
19
, Section 5]
) that
\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}
is a numerical semigroup, that
\langle A\rangle\subseteq\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}
, and that
\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}=\mathbb{N}
if and only if
p\in\langle A\rangle
. The generators of the numerical semigroup
\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}
are discussed in
[
10
]
.

When
A=(a_{1},a_{2})
, a fundamental problem is to determine explicit formulas for the Frobenius number
F\left(\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}\right)
and the genus
g\left(\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}\right)
.
This case remains an open problem, as noted in
[
7
]
. Recent progress has been made in
[
4
,
24
]
.
When
A
is an almost arithmetic progression and
p
is a positive divisor of
a_{1}
, the Frobenius number
F\left(\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}\right)
and the genus
g\left(\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}\right)
is studied in
[
2
,
11
]
.

In this paper, we consider the following class of quotients of numerical semigroups.

Definition 1.1
.

[

]

Let
a,k\geq 2
,
d\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}
, and
\gcd(a,d)=1
.
Define the sequences
B=(b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{k})
and
H=(h_{1},h_{2},\ldots,h_{k})
, where

\displaystyle b_{1}=1,\ b_{i+1}=s_{i}b_{i}+1\quad\text{and}\quad s_{i}\geq s_{i-1}\geq 1\quad\text{for}\quad 1\leq i\leq k-1,

(1)

\displaystyle h_{i}=ub_{i}+1,u\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\quad\text{for}\quad 1\leq i\leq k.

(2)

A family of numerical semigroups generated by
A
is called a
GCNS
numerical semigroup
if

A=(a,Ha+dB)=(a,h_{1}a+db_{1},h_{2}a+db_{2},\ldots,h_{k}a+db_{k}),

with the additional requirement that

for

when

is a negative integer.
In the special case where

for all

and

, that is,

\langle A\rangle
is referred to as a

.

By the condition

, it follows that
\gcd(a,h_{1}a+db_{1})=\gcd(a,db_{1})=1.

Moreover, we have
\gcd(A)=1
. Hence,
\langle A\rangle
is indeed a numerical semigroup.
In

]
, the Frobenius problem for

This family of semigroups generalizes several important special cases, including Mersenne numerical semigroups

]
, repunit numerical semigroups

]

. For details, see
[

]
for a comprehensive overview.

Let
\langle A\rangle
be a

The main objective of this paper is to investigate the Frobenius problem for the quotient of

by

.
Throughout, we assume that

.
This assumption excludes the trivial case

Our first main result characterizes the Apéry set of the numerical semigroup

This Apéry set is closely related to the minimization problem defined by:

\displaystyle O_{B}^{H}(M)=\min\left\{uM+\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\bigm|\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}x_{i}=M,\ x_{i}\in\mathbb{N},1\leq i\leq k\right\}.

A detailed analysis of

, based on the greedy algorithm, can be found in

.

Inspired by the investigation of the Frobenius numbers for the quotients of numerical semigroups generated by almost arithmetic progressions in

, we study the Frobenius number and the genus of

.
The second main result of this paper provides half-closed formulas for

and

.
Given the values of

for all

, some closed-form expressions for

are derived.
When

, these formulas specialize to those for

and

of the classical

established in
[

]
.

All results in this paper were verified using the

package in GAP
[

]
.

The paper is organized as follows.
Section

In Section

2
Apéry Set

We begin by recalling the important invariant known as the
Apéry set
of a numerical semigroup.
For convenience, we set
A:=(a,B)=(a,b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{k})
to facilitate the presentation of some conclusions.

Let
w\in\langle A\rangle\setminus\{0\}
.
The
Apéry set
of
w
in
\langle A\rangle
is defined as
Ape(A,w)=\{s\in\langle A\rangle\mid s-w\notin\langle A\rangle\}
. As shown in
[
19
]
, this set can be expressed as

Ape(A,w)=\{N_{0},N_{1},N_{2},\ldots,N_{w-1}\},

where
N_{r}:=\min\{a_{0}\mid a_{0}\equiv r\mod w,\ a_{0}\in\langle A\rangle\}
for
0\leq r\leq w-1
. It is customary to take
w=a
.

The following results are due to Brauer and Shockley
[
3
]
and Selmer
[
20
]
, respectively.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
3
]
,
[
20
]
)
.

Let
A=(a,b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{k})
, and let
Ape(A,a)=\{N_{0},N_{1},N_{2},\ldots,N_{a-1}\}
be the Apéry set of
a
in
\langle A\rangle
.
Then the Frobenius number and genus of
\langle A\rangle
are given by

\displaystyle F(\langle A\rangle)=\max_{r\in\{0,1,\ldots,a-1\}}N_{r}-a\quad\text{and}\quad g(\langle A\rangle)=\frac{1}{a}\sum_{r=1}^{a-1}N_{r}-\frac{a-1}{2}.

When
w=a
and
\gcd(a,d)=1
, the definition of
N_{r}
implies

\displaystyle\{N_{0},N_{1},N_{2},\ldots,N_{a-1}\}=\{N_{d\cdot 0},N_{d\cdot 1},N_{d\cdot 2},\ldots,N_{d\cdot(a-1)}\}.

In
[
13
]
, the computation of the Frobenius number and genus for
GCNS
numerical semigroups
is closely related to the minimization problem defined by:

O_{B}^{H}(M):=\min\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}h_{i}x_{i}\mid\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}x_{i}=M,\ x_{i}\in\mathbb{N},1\leq i\leq k\right\}.

This reduces to the
O_{B}(M)
studied in
[
12
]
when
H=(1,1,\dots,1)
.

Since the quotient of
\langle A\rangle
by
p
is a numerical semigroup and
\frac{a}{p}\in\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}
, we let
N_{r,p}
denote the element of the Apéry set of
\frac{a}{p}
in
\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}
, where
0\leq r\leq\frac{a}{p}-1
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
A=(a,h_{1}a+db_{1},\ldots,h_{k}a+db_{k})
, where
a,k,h\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
,
a\geq 2
,
d\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
, and
\gcd(A)=1
. If
d<0
, assume
h_{i}a+db_{i}>1
for
1\leq i\leq k
.
Let
p
be a positive divisor of
a
(i.e.,
p\mid a
with
p\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
). Then the Apéry set of
\frac{a}{p}
in the numerical semigroup
\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}
consists of the elements

\displaystyle N_{dr,p}=\min\left\{O_{B}^{H}(ma+rp)\cdot\frac{a}{p}+\left(\frac{ma}{p}+r\right)d\mid m\in\mathbb{N}\right\}\quad\text{for all}\quad 0\leq r\leq\frac{a}{p}-1.

Proof.

By the definition of the Apéry set, for any
0\leq r\leq\frac{a}{p}-1
, we have

\displaystyle N_{dr,p}

\displaystyle=\min\left\{a_{0}\mid a_{0}\equiv dr\mod\frac{a}{p};\ a_{0}\in\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}\right\}=\min\left\{a_{0}\mid a_{0}\equiv dr\mod\frac{a}{p};\ pa_{0}\in\langle A\rangle\right\}

Since
pa_{0}\in\langle A\rangle
, we can write

pa_{0}=ax_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}(h_{i}a+db_{i})x_{i}.

By minimality of
N_{dr,p}
, we must have
x_{0}=0
, and thus

\displaystyle N_{dr,p}

\displaystyle=\min\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{(h_{i}a+db_{i})}{p}x_{i}\mid\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{(h_{i}a+db_{i})}{p}x_{i}\equiv dr\mod\frac{a}{p},\ x_{i}\in\mathbb{N},1\leq i\leq k\right\}

\displaystyle=\min\left\{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}h_{i}x_{i}\right)\frac{a}{p}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{x_{i}b_{i}d}{p}\mid\sum_{i=1}^{k}(h_{i}a+db_{i})x_{i}\equiv drp\mod a,\ x_{i}\in\mathbb{N},1\leq i\leq k\right\}

\displaystyle=\min\left\{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}h_{i}x_{i}\right)\frac{a}{p}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{x_{i}b_{i}d}{p}\mid\sum_{i=1}^{k}db_{i}x_{i}\equiv drp\mod a,\ x_{i}\in\mathbb{N},1\leq i\leq k\right\}

Since
\gcd(A)=1
, it follows that
\gcd(a,d)=1
, and we deduce

\displaystyle N_{dr,p}

\displaystyle=\min\left\{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}h_{i}x_{i}\right)\frac{a}{p}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{x_{i}b_{i}d}{p}\mid\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}x_{i}\equiv rp\mod a,\ x_{i}\in\mathbb{N},1\leq i\leq k\right\}

\displaystyle=\min\left\{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}h_{i}x_{i}\right)\frac{a}{p}+\left(\frac{ma}{p}+r\right)d\mid\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}x_{i}=ma+rp,\ m,x_{i}\in\mathbb{N},1\leq i\leq k\right\}.

For fixed
m
(and hence fixed
M=ma+rp
), the expression
\sum_{i=1}^{k}h_{i}x_{i}
is minimized by
O_{B}^{H}(ma+rp)
. This completes the proof.
∎

By Lemma
2.2
, we can define an intermediate function with respect to
m\in\mathbb{N}
as follows:

N_{dr,p}(m):=O_{B}^{H}(ma+rp)\cdot\frac{a}{p}+\left(\frac{ma}{p}+r\right)d.

In our setting, it is not hard to show that
N_{dr,p}(m)
is increasing with respect to
m
, which implies
N_{dr,p}=N_{dr,p}(0)
. This property facilitates further obtaining the formulas for the Frobenius number
F\left(\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}\right)
and the genus
g\left(\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}\right)
.

Before proceeding, we introduce the following definition.
Given a positive integer sequence
B=(b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{k})
with
1=b_{1}<b_{2}<\cdots<b_{k}
and
M\in\mathbb{N}
, define

\displaystyle opt_{B}(M)=O_{B}(M)=\min\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\mid\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}x_{i}=M,\quad M,x_{i}\in\mathbb{N},1\leq i\leq k\right\}.

This problem is known as the
change-making problem

[
1
]
.

Proposition 2.3
.

[
13
, Lemma 2.6 and Proposition 2.7]

Let
B=\left(b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{k}\right)
, where
b_{1}=1
,
b_{i+1}=s_{i}b_{i}+1
, and
s_{i}\geq s_{i-1}
for
1\leq i\leq k-1
.
Then for any
M\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, the value
opt_{B}(M)=x_{1}+\dots+x_{k}
is uniquely determined by the following properties.

(1)

x_{k}=\left\lfloor\frac{M}{b_{k}}\right\rfloor
.

(2)

x_{i}\in\{0,1,\ldots,s_{i}\}
for every
1\leq i\leq k-1
.

(3)

If
x_{i}=s_{i}
for some
2\leq i\leq k-1
, then
x_{1}=\cdots=x_{i-1}=0
.

If a solution
X=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{k})
of the equation
\sum_{i}x_{i}b_{i}=M
satisfies conditions (1), (2), and (3) above, then
X=X(M)
is called the
greedy presentation
of
M
. The motivation for this terminology is explained in
[
13
]
.
Define
R(M)=\{X(m):0\leq m\leq M\}
, and introduce a colexicographic order on
R(M)
as follows:

\displaystyle(x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{k}^{\prime})\preceq(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{k})\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle x_{i}^{\prime}=x_{i}\ \ \text{for all}\ \ i>0;\ \ \text{or}

\displaystyle x_{j}^{\prime}<x_{j}\ \ \text{for a certain}\ \ j>0\ \ \text{and}\ \ x_{i}^{\prime}=x_{i}\ \ \text{for all}\ \ i>j.

Clearly, the order relation
\preceq
is a total order on
R(M)
.

We now consider the
O_{B}^{H}(M)
, where the sequences
B
and
H
satisfy conditions (
1
) and (
2
).
A direct computation yields

\displaystyle O_{B}^{H}(M)=\min\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}(ub_{i}+1)x_{i}\mid\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}x_{i}=M,\ x_{i}\in\mathbb{N},1\leq i\leq k\right\}=uM+opt_{B}(M).

Let
\langle A\rangle
be a
GCNS
numerical semigroup. When
p
is a positive divisor of
a
, we characterize the Apéry set of
\frac{a}{p}
in the numerical semigroup
\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\langle A\rangle
be a
GCNS
numerical semigroup. Let
p
be a positive divisor of
a
.
If
ua+d+k-2\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}s_{i}
, then
N_{dr,p}(m)
is increasing in
m\in\mathbb{N}
for all
0\leq r\leq\frac{a}{p}-1
. Specifically, if
X(rp)=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{k})
is the greedy presentation of
rp
, then the Apéry set of
\frac{a}{p}
in the numerical semigroup
\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}
consists of the elements

N_{dr,p}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{ax_{i}}{p}+r(ua+d)=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}(ub_{i}+1)x_{i}\right)\frac{a}{p}+rd\quad\text{for all}\quad 0\leq r\leq\frac{a}{p}-1.

(3)

Proof.

Recall that if
X(ma+rp)=(y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{k})
, then

N_{dr,p}(m)=\left(u(ma+rp)+\sum_{i=1}^{k}y_{i}\right)\frac{a}{p}+\left(\frac{ma}{p}+r\right)d.

Let
X((m+1)a+rp)=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})
. Note that
z_{k}\geq y_{k}
, and

\displaystyle N_{dr,p}(m+1)-N_{dr,p}(m)

\displaystyle=\frac{ua^{2}+ad}{p}+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}z_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}y_{i}\right)\frac{a}{p}

\displaystyle\geq\left(ua+d+\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}z_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}y_{i}\right)\frac{a}{p}

\displaystyle(\textrm{by\ Proposition\ \ref{greedproper}})\quad

\displaystyle\geq\left(ua+d-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}s_{i}+k-2\right)\frac{a}{p}\geq 0.

Thus
N_{dr,p}(m)
is increasing so that
N_{dr,p}=N_{dr,p}(0)
. This completes the proof.
∎

3
Frobenius Number and Genus

In Equation (
3
) with
X(rp)=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{k})
, we refer to
w(rp)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(ub_{i}+1)x_{i}
as the
weight
of
rp
. The following result holds.

Lemma 3.1
.

[
13
, Lemma 2.11]

Let
M\in\mathbb{N}
. Suppose the sequences
B
and
H
satisfy conditions (
1
) and (
2
), respectively, with
s_{i}\leq u+1
for all
1\leq i\leq k-1
. Let
X(r_{1}p)=(x_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{k}^{\prime})\in R(M)
and
X(r_{2}p)=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})\in R(M)
. If
(x_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{k}^{\prime})\preceq(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
, then
w(r_{1}p)\leq w(r_{2}p)
. Moreover, if
r_{1}\neq r_{2}
and
s_{i}<u+1
, then
w(r_{1}p)<w(r_{2}p)
.

We now present the main result of this section.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\langle A\rangle
be a
GCNS
numerical semigroup. Let
p
be a positive divisor of
a
and
p\neq a
.
Suppose
X(a-p)=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{k})
is the greedy presentation of
a-p
, and note that
x_{k}=\left\lfloor\frac{a-p}{b_{k}}\right\rfloor
. If
ua+d+k-2\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}s_{i}
,
a+pd\geq 0
, and
s_{i}<u+1
for all
1\leq i\leq k-1
, then

\displaystyle F\left(\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\cdot\frac{a}{p}+\left(\frac{a}{p}-1\right)(ua+d)-\frac{a}{p}.

In particular, if
d
is positive, it suffices to assume
ua+d+k-2\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}s_{i}
and
s_{i}\leq u+1
for
1\leq i\leq k-1
. The above formula remains valid.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.4
, Lemma
3.1
, and Equation (
3
), under the condition
s_{i}<u+1
, we have

N_{d(r+1),p}-N_{dr,p}=(w((r+1)p)-w(rp))\frac{a}{p}+d\geq\frac{a}{p}+d\geq 0.

Therefore, we obtain

\max_{0\leq r\leq\frac{a}{p}-1}N_{dr,p}=N_{d(\frac{a}{p}-1),p}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\cdot\frac{a}{p}+\left(\frac{a}{p}-1\right)(ua+d).

Applying Lemma
2.1
yields the formula for
F\left(\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}\right)
. When
d>0
, the weaker condition
s_{i}\leq u+1
suffices by Lemma
3.1
. This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\langle A\rangle
be a
GCNS
numerical semigroup. Let
p
be a positive divisor of
a
and
p\neq a
.
If
ua+d+k-2\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}s_{i}
, then

\displaystyle g\left(\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}\right)=\sum_{r=1}^{\frac{a}{p}-1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\right)_{rp}+\frac{(ua+d-1)}{2}\left(\frac{a}{p}-1\right),

where
\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\right)_{rp}
denotes the sum of elements in the greedy presentation of
rp
. and
x_{k}=\left\lfloor\frac{rp}{b_{k}}\right\rfloor
.

Proof.

The result follows directly from Equation (
3
) and Lemma
2.1
.
∎

4
Applications

For a
GCNS
numerical semigroup, given the value of
s_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq k-1
, a closed-form formula for
F\left(\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}\right)
is obtained.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
A=(a,(u+1)a+d,((s+1)u+1)a+(s+1)d)
, where
d\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
,
\gcd(a,d)=1
,
a\geq 2
, and
u+1\geq s\geq 1
.
Let
p
be a positive divisor of
a
and
p\neq a
. Then the Frobenius number is given by

\displaystyle F\left(\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}\right)=\left(a-p-s\left\lfloor\frac{a-p}{s+1}\right\rfloor\right)\frac{a}{p}+\left(\frac{a}{p}-1\right)(ua+d)-\frac{a}{p},

and the genus by

\displaystyle g\left(\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}\right)=\left(\frac{a}{p}-1\right)\cdot\frac{ua+a+d-1}{2}-s\sum_{r=1}^{\frac{a}{p}-1}\left\lfloor\frac{rp}{s+1}\right\rfloor.

Proof.

Let
X((\frac{a}{p}-1)p)=(x_{1},x_{2})
be the greedy presentation of
(\frac{a}{p}-1)p
, i.e.,
a-p=(s+1)x_{2}+x_{1}
. Then

x_{1}+x_{2}=\left\lfloor\frac{a-p}{s+1}\right\rfloor+a-p-(s+1)\left\lfloor\frac{a-p}{s+1}\right\rfloor=a-p-s\left\lfloor\frac{a-p}{s+1}\right\rfloor.

The formula for the Frobenius number follows from Theorem
3.2
, and the genus formula from Theorem
3.3
.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
A=(a,(u+1)a+d,(3u+1)a+3d,(10u+1)a+10d)
, where
d\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
,
\gcd(a,d)=1
,
u\geq 2
, and
a\geq 2
.
Let
p
be a positive divisor of
a
and
p\neq a
. Then the Frobenius number is given by

F\left(\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}\right)=\frac{a}{p}\left(\left\lfloor\frac{a-p}{10}\right\rfloor+ua+d+\varphi_{1}((a-p)\mod 10)\right)-(ua+d),

where
(\varphi_{1}(i))_{0\leq i\leq 9}=(-1,0,1,0,1,2,1,2,3,2)
.

Proof.

The result follows directly from Theorem
3.2
with
s_{1}=2,s_{2}=3
by routine computation.
∎

The result follows directly from Theorem
3.2
with

∎

For given values of
s_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq k-1
, similar formulas can be derived.
From Theorems
3.2
and
3.3
, we directly obtain the following results.

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
\langle A\rangle
be a
CNS
numerical semigroup with
d\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Let
p
be a positive divisor of
a
and
p\neq a
.
Suppose
X(a-p)=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{k})
is the greedy presentation of
a-p
.
If
a\geq k-1-\frac{d-1}{b-1}
, then

\displaystyle F\left(\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\cdot\frac{a}{p}+\left(\frac{a}{p}-1\right)((b-1)a+d)-\frac{a}{p}.

Proof.

Since
d>0
, the conclusion follows by applying Theorem
3.2
with
s_{1}=s_{2}=\cdots=s_{k-1}=b
and
u=b-1
.
∎

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
\langle A\rangle
be a
CNS
numerical semigroup with
d\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Let
p
be a positive divisor of
a
and
p\neq a
.
If
a\geq k-1-\frac{d-1}{b-1}
, then

\displaystyle g\left(\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}\right)=\sum_{r=1}^{\frac{a}{p}-1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\right)_{rp}+\frac{((b-1)a+d-1)}{2}\left(\frac{a}{p}-1\right),

where
\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\right)_{rp}
denotes the sum of elements in the greedy presentation of

.

Proof.

The result follows by applying Theorem
3.3
to the case where
s_{1}=s_{2}=\cdots=s_{k-1}=b
and
u=b-1
.
∎
