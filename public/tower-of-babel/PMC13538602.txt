The integrated developmental stress response (ISR-DASR) shift reveals a functional transition from cellular to stem cell organismal adaptation
Authors: Ximena Ruden, Campbell Coddington, Lynessa Asplund, Awoniyi Awonuga, Douglas Ruden, Steven Korzeniewski, Lijun Zhang, Elizabeth Puscheck, Daniel Rappolee
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Stress responses are essential for cellular survival and organismal, developmental progression. In somatic cells and embryonic stem cells (ESC), there exists the sequential transcriptional programs of the integrated stress response (ISR) and senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP), which enable stepwise adaptation to adverse conditions. We recently identified a distinct developmentally associated stress response (DASR) in mouse ESC that hypothetically emerges only after successful completion of cellular adaptation (ISR), reflecting a shift toward organismal-level adaptation. In explanation, a cellular response is a cell autonomous cell survival response, and it enables a stem cell response leading to organismal development and survival. Here, we test whether placental trophoblast stem cells (TSC), also undergo this ISR-DASR transition. Using transcriptomic profiling of mouse TSC exposed to strong, hyperosmotic stress (at 400mM sorbitol), we find that early responses (0.5–2 h) are dominated by biosynthetic and metabolic gene functions characteristic of cellular adaptation, whereas later responses (6–24 h) are enriched for developmental and differentiation pathways, consistent with DASR activation. These findings suggest that the ISR-DASR transition is a general feature of the response of stressed, developmental stem cells. They also highlight potential biomarkers for improving in vitro fertilization, understanding miscarriage, and advancing regenerative medicine.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-55295-1.

Introduction

The recent discovery of a developmentally associated stress response (DASR)1 opens a broad new view on how essential developmental stem cells respond to stress. The DASR has several aspects. It is a shared pan-stress transcriptomic response, which is developmental, not somatic, generally permissive, not instructive1–6. It has two parts, one is shared by qualitatively weak stressors, which cause efficient override and loss of initial stemness and emulates this aspect of normal differentiation (ND), which efficiently zeros out stemness and moves to normal differentiation balance. The other part is shared by strong stressors which override stemness less efficiently, leaving an extended window of reversibility compared with ND and weak stressors. Strong stressors also proportionally increase 1st lineage (for muTSC this is TGC) and suppress later lineages. Strength vs. weakness is defined by increased DEG fold change (FC) and increased significant DEG number above that of ND for strong and similar to ND for weak. Elements of instructive nature of the weak response is override of stemness and of the strong response is more imbalanced differentiation, but a more focused pan-stress shared by weak and strong stressors is more purely permissive. The universal nature of the DASR, discovered in ESC stress testing, is indicated by a powerful nonrandom concordance of directional change of weak pan-stress transcriptomic markers when the predictivity of these markers only required a hypothetical, a priori shared significance.

This suggests the DASR may provide mechanisms and markers to assess the level and efficacy of stress resolution in many developmental stem cells. This may lead to improving applications such as IVF/ART, developmental toxicology, and regenerative medicine. The discovery of the DASR supports the hypothesis that diverse weak stressors share a large significant set of differentially expressed genes (DEG) in the stress-induced override of stemness, and these may diagnose stress and predict outcomes. Weak stressors also share a smaller amount of DEG with strong stressors.

The serendipitous finding was that the significant DEG supporting the hypothesis were also 100% concordant in directional change, a massive non-random stress homeostasis suggesting powerful integrating evolutionarily derived mechanisms in the essential stem cells in early embryos. These integrated mechanisms should work through organizing transcription factors that regulate the pan-stress response. But it was shown during the discovery of the DASR in mouse ESC, that primate ESC and mouse TSC don’t share the pvalue significant DEG defining the mouse ESC pan-stress transcriptomic marker set1,7,8. But the massively non- randomness suggest that the mechanisms that mediate the muESC pan-stress response would also induce conceptually similar responses in the other essential stem cell types, even if the markers were different.

Another reported universal response pan-stress transcriptome, is the integrated stress response (ISR)9–11, occurring immediately after stress initiation and needed to respond stress with an immediate homeostatic anabolic-to-catabolic shift that enables energy redistribution and mechanisms to adapt cells to stress. Hypothetically, this adaptation frees up enough energy during ISR resolution to enable the DASR. Thus, the ISR functions should peak soon after stress initiation and once ISR resolves stress adaptation on the cellular level, it subsides and enables the DASR to increase and mediate sufficient differentiated function by overriding stemness to maintain the developmental trajectory at the organismal level.

A study of stressed, cultured TSC was done previously and the override of TSC stemness forced imbalanced 1st lineage differentiation by the end of 24 h of high, 400mM hyperosmotic sorbitol stress7. This is similar to high hyperosmotic (300mM sorbitol) stress-induced 1st differentiated lineage6 during ESC stemness override and DASR induction1,6. Much of the work with stress forced TSC and ESC differentiation to imbalanced 1st lineage has been done with a hyperosmotic sorbitol stress carefully calibrated to be at the high range of stem cell survival after an initial apoptosis peak in the first 12-24 h12,13. This calibration to produce “biologically equivalent” stress doses insures that transcriptomic responses in different stem cell types address similar biological stress problems. It should be noted that other strong stimuli also cause lineage imbalance in both TSC and ESC; these include hypoxia at 0.5% oxygen below the implantation site normoxia of 2% oxygen3,14,15, and retinoic acid5,6,16.

Here we re-query the DEG outcomes for a time-dependent changes in the putative TSC ISR-DASR shift to support the universal nature of the shift and to discover markers of time-dependent stages of the mouse TSC transcriptomic response7, TSC should undergo an ISR-DASR shift that is predicted by the pan-stress DASR discovered in mouse ESC.

Transcription factors (TF) that are thought to regulate the stages of the ISR-DASR shift were identified at four time points covering the periods previously identified to mediate the immediate cellular response to stress that regulate biosynthetic, anabolic and catabolic pathways through protein kinases like AMPK (AMP-activated protein kinase) and SAPK (Stress-activated protein kinases) which are active and necessary in the 0.5–2 h time period4,17. This is the period of the ISR peak functions. The functions of these protein kinases and switching to organismal responses such as proliferation, development, and differentiation during the 6–24 h highlights the DASR. Overall, these marker sets, once this predictive method is validated, might offer new opportunities to develop biomarkers for miscarriage and identify as yet uncategorized environmental stressors that are capable of disrupting early developmental processes for pluripotent stem cells that give rise to all adult cells.

Based on the recent discovery of the DASR1 one hypothesis states that ISR is early after stress and resolves sufficient cellular stress challenges9 in all cells and a stem cell stress response mediated by biosynthesis regulators AMPK (all rate limiting enzymes in intermediary carbon metabolism18,19 and SAPK (stress-diminished DNA replication rates in S phase)4,20,21. Previously characterized pan-stress protein kinases like AMPK and SAPK are likely part of an ISR in developmental stem cells that enable time-dependent, sequential ISR and DASR adaptive TF and secreted factor (SF) function1 defined by GO groups named by their keywords (Fig. 1C). Here we test for the stepwise nature of the ISR and DASR in cultured TSC under strong stress.

The recently discovered DASR1 placed this pan-stress developmental response as third in a series of four pan-stress responses (Fig. 1B). Emphasized here are the initial adaptive response after stress initiation (stage 2, ISR) and the DASR enabled by the transient successfully resolved ISR (stage 3, DASR). Unresolved ISR leads to apoptosis within the ISR or to a senescence associated secretory phenotype (SASP)23,24. Hypothetically an unresolved DASR would also lead to the SASP.

The successive ISR and DASR stages suggest a reciprocal early ISR that resolves initial stress and its dual metabolic requirements of continuing pre-stress cellular functions and mount an adaptive stress response (Fig. 1C). This is best determined by identifying the time-dependent use of TF and SF DEG, and the GO groups these DEG enable and using keywords using Slim (https://geneontology.org/docs/go-slim-and-subset-guide/) to identify groups of GO groups with shared functions [used previously in plant function studies25,26. A flow chart (Fig. 1D) of analytical methods used to quantitate and analyze the ISR-DASR shift are described in the following sessions.

Stress enzymes like AMPK lower activity of rate-limiting enzymes of anabolic pathways and increase activity of rate-limiting enzymes in catabolic pathways to rebalance energy production18,19. We hypothesized that in a study of the whole transcriptome there would be a lower number of DEG early after stress and these would emphasize downregulation, followed by an increased total DEG and fraction of upregulated after stress adaptation. This hypothesis was supported (Fig. 2A). In previous studies of ESC dose-dependence of strong stress, and of diverse strong and weak stresses, analysis of transcriptomic DEG showed increasing FC ranges as stress increased1,6, and this was also shown here for TSC as stress exposure of a constant magnitude, increased with time (Fig. 2B, Supplemental Fig. 1). A key finding (Supplemental Fig. 1) is that the stress resolved sufficiently by 2 h to enable 1.5fold higher maximal upregulated than downregulated DEG compared with 0.5 h. By 6 h the FC range was 2.4fold higher than 0.5 h, and by 24 h 2.7fold higher maximum upregulated and downregulated DEG compared with the cutoff of 2fold higher DEG minimum at 0.5 h. Thus by 6–24 h, TSC have recovered sufficiently from initial hyperosmotic stress to enable high upregulation, indicating sufficient energy for new transcription of highly upregulated DEG The highly upregulated genes at 6 and 24 h, they should be important in enabling the homeostatic cell survival response and the DASR at 24 h. Note that the Normal differentiation (ND) or Stress forced ESC differentiation after 72 of adaptation induces ratios of pvalue significant up- and down-regulated genes within a ~ 40–60% range (Supplemental Fig. 2). The ~ 40–60% range was the outcome of strong and weak stressors at NOAEL (No Observable Adverse Effects Levels, for growth) and IC75 doses (test toxicant dose with 75% decrease in growth)1,27,28, by 3 doses of the hyperosmotic sorbitol strong stress, retinoic acid (RA), and stressless ND (by LIF removal)1,29. By this comparison, the first 2 TSC timepoints are abnormally highly downregulated at ~ 70%, but 6 and 24 h timepoints are in a normal range of ~ 40–60% (Fig. 2A, Supplemental Fig. 2).

The rapid, dynamic TSC response to stress is shown by the shift in small number of DEG to higher number and tendency to downregulate to tendency to upregulate during the ISR-DASR shift (Supplemental Fig. 3). However, there is a largely shared set of DEG from 0.5 to 6 h timepoints in contrast to the new changes in DEG at 24 h; this is discussed below.

To study functional shifts representing ISR-DASR progression, we used two classes of molecules that we hypothesized were necessary to organize transcriptional regulation and intercellular integration during the pan-stress DASR1. Both TF and SF increases approximately 3-fold, and approximately from 1 to 3% of all DEG, between the ISR during 0.5–2 h and the DASR between 6 and 24 h (Fig. 3, Supplemental Fig. 4). These TF and SF DEG were used to query GO groups’ genes.

Prior to interpreting these results, we tested TF and SF-defined GO groups for the number of three GO group categories, Biological Processes (BP), Molecular Function (MF), and Cellular Component (CC) to rationalize the use of BP to understand the nature of the ISR-DASR shift in terms of function (Fig. 1C) in Fig. 4. Due too few BP groups at 0.5–2 h, we studied only TF.

The dominant and most numerous GO group category for both TF and SF and at every time point, except SF at 0.5 h and 2 h, was BP (Fig. 4A). Inspection of Slim database for keywords also suggested that DASR and ISR functions would be found in the BP category.

Discussion

Many lessons are suggested in the significant, functional predictiveness of the ISR-DASR shift

We show here by three methods of transcriptomic inquiry, that the ISR-DASR shift in mouse TSC should be similar to the same shift in mouse ESC where the DASR was recently discovered1. The ISR-DASR shift establishes a mechanistic framework to develop biomarkers for miscarriage and environmental toxicant screening. The transcriptomic analysis or consonant and corroborate the previous studies of stress kinases proteins regulating the ISR to DASR shift and developmental lineage proteins of the DASR as shown in Supplemental Fig. 15.

The time course ended at 24 h and used a single strong stressor and lacked an ND control, but a follow-up study at 72 h showed that many strong stressors override TSC stemness and induce TGC levels higher than TSC cultured under NS or ND conditions (Fig. 8). It should be noted that for both strong stressors, hyperosmotic sorbitol and hyperoxia at 0.5% O2, TGC levels are higher than ND, but that between 4 and 7 days of extended stress hyperoxia14,83 and hyperosmotic stress28 ND surpasses them in TGC levels. This suggests that TSC have enough stored cellular energy to sustain DNA synthesis, required for endoreduplication and giant nuclei for the first 2–4, and that after 4 days strong stress depletes TSC energy reserves whether the TSC is at low oxygen or not.

A kinetic transition from low-DEG number / highly downregulated early response fraction → high-DEG / upregulated late response strongly supports ISR resolution by ~ 2 h and onset of developmental adaptation (DASR) after 6 h and by 24 h and provides markers for whether a stressed stem cell - or embryo - resolves initial stress.

The work integrates kinetic stressed TSC microarray data to detailed data spanning 20 years of hyperosmotic-stress research, linking embryo12,84–87, ESC1,2,5,6,77,88, and TSC7,13,16,35,36,89,90 models to unify stress-adaptation framework for strong stresses. The discovery that TSC undergo the same reciprocal ISR-DASR kinetic shift previously predicted in the report of the DASR discovery in ESC, suggests that developmental stem-cell stress programs share a DASR-like response across lineages, but this requires stringent testing of ESC and other developmental stem cells during early development.

TF-defined GO groups provide the strongest and most consistent functional signal of ISR-DASR kinetics, exceeding secreted-factor GO groups and specific functional GO groups. For the stress kinetic responses of TSC the TF-defined functional categories segregate: ISR = biosynthetic/metabolic suppression; DASR = morphogenesis, proliferation, differentiation, development.

TF DEG rise ~ 3-fold from ISR-DASR, representing a major transcriptional switch to unique lineage-regulating factors and unique TF arising only at 24 h and correlating with DASR acceleration. This analysis validates BP GO groups as the functional category most informative in tracking stress-adaptation kinetics and the resolution of ISR in developmental stem cells.

Stress-granule (SG) dynamics from external datasets validate11 the manuscript’s ISR timing as the SG peaks at 2 h and decline by 8–24 h and provides markers for whether a stem cell resolves initial stress or not. SG dynamics should provide another means to determine resolution of the ISR. At the 400mM dose used to defined the kinetic response of TSC to strong stress, cardiomyocytes have disassembled 80% of their ribosomes by 30 min, suggesting translation, as well as transcription, are sharply downregulated. In stressed TSC the SG assembly GO group GO:0034063 DEG are enriched 4fold at a 30 min peak enrichment and SG disassembly GO group GO:1,990,112 DEG enriched 5fold at 6 h peak, with return to lower equal 2fold enrichments at 24 h (data not shown). Thus, TSC SG assembly followed by disassembly follow a similar kinetics as shown for controls (supplemental Fig. 6)11,82. Thus, reassembly of ribosomes following SG disassembly mirrors the changes for upregulation of both translation and transcription by 24 h of strong stress adaptation.

In contrast to the simplest support of the hypothesis of ISR-DASR shift, the related UPR, ISR and PERK GO groups are increasingly and significant enriched, as well as DASR related EmbPlac and BlastDev GO groups all increase significantly through 24 h. The ISR groups do not subside in enrichment of DEG with respect to the GO groups or to the increasing size of stressed TSC DEG marker sets. But the significance of EmbPlac and Blast Dev accelerate in significance in time or maintain 1–3 orders of magnitude higher significance than PERK and ISR GO groups during the shift from ISR to DASR. Supporting predictions from peri-implantation TSC lineage priority for proliferation and differentiation compared with ESC, PlacEmb GO group enrichment during stressed TSC response is much higher than DevEmb GO group or the ISR related UPR, ISR and PERK GO Groups.

Biological and medical significance of ISR-DASR switch, and hypothetical universal nature of the DASR

Specifically, the biological significance of ISR-DASR shift and the markers it provide are important directly in medical practice and in experimental studies to improve medical practice. For choice of in vitro fertilization (IVF) embryos to reimplant, visual micrographic assays are important, but good morphology embryos can miscarry, and poor morphology embryos can be reimplanted to produce healthy babies91.

Hypothetically, for embryos where a TSC lineage (or ESC lineage) is under stress - as in poor morphology IVF embryos, transcriptomic markers of failure to resolve ISR or its functional downstream GO groups (Fig. 4) and move to DASR, would indicate poorer chance of successful implantation and healthy birth. Transcriptomic markers associated with the earlier form of miscarriage – implantation failure91, would be an excellent source to test for the ISR-DASR shift defects and for ESC pan-stress, weak vs. strong stress, and dose-dependent stress markers and effects1,6.

Stress marker and protein kinase analysis should improve regenerative medicines and IVF

Markers for weak and strong ESC stress1, dose-dependent strong ESC stress6, and time-dependent strong TSC stress DEG markers here, can all potentially inform stress lineage imbalances with the aim to apply this stress risk analysis to predict outcomes.

Similarly, for reproductive medicine, strong and most probably weak stress encumbered during reprogramming of differentiated cells to produce pluripotent stem cells, or any reprogramming to produce multipotent stem cells without pluripotent intermediates92,93, is likely to reduce full lineage function and incur epigenetic defects that limit the efficacy of the stem cells94,95. Blocking a stress protein kinase SAPK with its inhibitor SP600125, produced a fuller more appropriate set of naïve pluripotency ESC markers than five other PKI (protein kinase inhibitors) alone during pluripotency optimization optimize in a high throughput screen96. SAPK is a commonly upregulated in stress PK in many examples of blastocyst, TSC and ESC stress responses4. Inhibiting SAPK in highest stress, historically older media97, is detrimental to embryo development, but inhibiting SAPK in modern lower stress media improves embryo development98. Thus, detailed knowledge of stress protein kinase function should improve aspects of regenerative medicine and IVF.

CEPT (Chroman 1, Emricasan, Polyamines, trans-ISRIB) is a stress inhibitor cocktail containing an ISR Inhibitor (trans-ISRIB) developed to improve naïve pluripotency maintenance in a high throughput screen99, and this has already been used to improve studies using blastoids to study gastrulation100. Thus, stress inhibition improves naïve pluripotency and embryoid development in vitro.

Time- and dose-dependent markers should classify stress during reprogramming and maintenance for whether cells have experience weak or strong stress and resolved it. Hypothetically, resolving a weak stress may be sufficient for efficacious regenerative medicine, but strong stress or failure to resolve ISR (or DASR) predicts outright homeostatic failure in reproductive medicine.

Venn diagram analysis of the entire time-dependent TSC stress transcriptome shows that it is dynamic with many genes shared during ISR than diversifying during the DASR between timepoints compared with unique genes at each timepoint (Supplemental Fig. 3). However, for the DASR the EmbPlac and BlastDev GO groups marker genes are conserved within early timepoints and become unique only at the 24 h timepoint (Supplemental Tables 1,2).

Although initial conclusions for these data suggested an emphasis on TGC differentiation, this re-analysis shows that transcriptomic preparation through 24 h to override TSC stemness is not limited to 1st lineage TGC. It also includes later lineages of related spongiotrophoblasts and later lineages such syncytiotrophoblast, chorion development, and labyrinthine placental GO groups (studies related to Fig. 6).

But later published studies emphasize that there is a 2 day peak ploidy peak of strong stressor forced 1st lineage in (hyperosmotic sorbitol28, hypoxic stress3) which is significantly higher than TSC ND by FGF4 removal. But by 4 day, ND = strong stress for 1st lineage ploidy and ND has a higher ploidy> stress forced TSC differentiation after 6-7days. In addition, recent ongoing studies show that by 3 days of hyperosmotic stress or retinoic acid control 50–75% of all TSC are TGC16, and manuscript in preparation]. This suggests the other lineages suggested by transcriptomic markers are still minor subpopulations compared with strong imbalance to 1st lineage TGC induced by strong stressors.

Finally, by day 4, hyperosmotic stress induced > 50% of stressed TSC to TGC and stemness loss is irreversible14. This is equivalent to an absolute prediction of miscarriage. It is conceptually similar to the functional difference of spent media from poor and good morphology (aka, GM, and PM) embryos. Cultured GM embryo spent media induce decidualization and implantation prep whereas PM induce ER stress in endometrial cells in vitro and in vivo101,102. This also suggests PM embryos share a possible stress secretome with irreversibly differentiated TSC, although the SASP does not appear as early as 24 h in the studies here.

Limitations of this study

Mouse ESC and TSC studies will have greater and more precise predictive value when the HTS for biologically equivalent stress doses for adverse effects are done in human ESC and TSC. At that time, the biologically equivalent risk doses for adverse effects levels for stem cell growth are then used to test for the transcriptomic programs used to address the equivalent biological adverse outcome. Equivalent biological doses insure that although varied stressors vary widely in type and potency, the induced transcriptomic response addresses a similar homeostatic problem in the growth deficit on perceived stress exposure.

Specifically, the analyses here were done using older, in some ways less precise microarray hybridization, and curation and nomenclature reduces test marker sets by about 10% in size compared with recent RNAseq-mediated analytic outcomes. The inductive generalizations for an ISR-DASR shift in other cell types should be tested for support of the hypothesis, in those other stem cell types such as hematopoietic stem cells (HSC) and mesenchymal stem cells (HSC). The re-queried, stressed TSC dataset also lacked an ND control, which after NS baseline comparisons is especially important with weak stresses which override ESC stemness to produce a lineage balance between 1st and 2nd lineages which emulates ND. In the current study, we tried using several other TSC ND studies outputted as transcriptomic progressions as proxy for the missing ND here103–105. But some element of design, usually due to a focus on later stages of TSC ND later after implantation, made them unusable to study lineage changes during peri-implantation TSC differentiation. A pan-stress muTSC transcriptome in response to diverse stressors and including and ND control baseline is under study (unpublished data), but the important transition is to human TSC and ESC models.

Limited strong stressors were tested here and a survey of weak vs. strong stressors in the TSC-TGC HTS is nearing completion (data not shown). A strength here is that all toxicologically assigned strong stressors categorized in an ESC HTS for cytotoxicity and cardiotoxicity76,106, induced TSC to significant increased TGC subpopulations sizes as high as 60% (Fig. 8). A limitation of these studies is that only two of five of the strong toxicants had TGC effects at relevant levels of exposures to humans or rodents. But it is likely that multiple stress exposures can be relevant and that an internal stress hormone cortisol, and external pollutant bisphenyl A (BPA), are subthreshold for rodent embryo loss, but when co-exposed pregnant rats lose high numbers of implanted embryos107,108.

Clearly, embryos are more resistant to stress than cultured stem cells12,13, which are a reductionist model and also lack maternal and other embryonic cell type crosstalk. But whole mouse embryos at 400mM sorbitol did show stressed-induced changes in implantation in a way that is consistent with increased 1st lineage differentiation87, although invasiveness, not 1st lineage differentiation markers, was tested directly in this study. In addition, human and mouse embryoids may be used in HTS if the low efficacy is improve from the poor 10%/day of embryoids compared to time-matched embryos, to the recently reported 80% efficacy100,109–111. Interestingly, in one of these embryoid models100, efficiency of development was greatly improved with and anti-stress cocktail of inhibitors (CEPT; chroman 1, emricasan, polyamines, and trans-ISRIB- Integrated Stress Response Inhibitor)99. Thus, cross-referencing time and dose, and functional group dependent stress markers here, to markers in experimentally stressed embryoids, or embryos or from which TSC and ESC were derived, would support the efficacy of the marker studies here.

Methods

Materials

muTSC were a kind gift from Dr Janet Rossant113. TSC reagents for maintenance culture, stress and transcriptomic assay3,14,16 by whole mouse genome microarray were described previously7.

Methods

TSC culture

Mouse TSC were a kind gift of Dr Rossant113 and were cultured as described previously7. All TSC were optimized at passage for exponential growth during the stimulus period, which began 18 h after passage at 5% confluence.

Stressed TSC culture referenced to ESC, embryo, stress

Mouse TSC were cultured as described previously in early work establishing hyperosmotic sorbitol as a single variable model examining stemness vs. differentiation in stress prior to the hybridization-based microarray12,13, for the microarray7, and continued after the microarray with additional strong stressors and stimuli (hypoxia at 0.5% O2 and retinoic acid)3,14,16,86. Hyperosmotic sorbitol has been used as a single variable positive control stress for over twenty years of study of blastocysts and their cultured TSC and ESC proxy models1,2,5,6,77. This enables indexing and referencing across the embryo model and the stem cells derived from the embryo.

DEG analysis of normal stemness transition under stress to a differentiated state with associated mRNA programs

To quantify programs of DEG markers from initial TSC stemness to ISR that complemented the previously established imbalanced 1st lineage differentiation caused by strong stress, we used the same reports of these in vitro and in vivo from microarray data as we have previously done6,28. For associated programs we queried time-dependent TSC stress-induced DEG with lists of mouse TF30 and SF114 as done previously in studies of the ESC Pan-stress transcriptome1. Stress transcriptomic data significantly impacted pathways, biological processes, and molecular interactions.
