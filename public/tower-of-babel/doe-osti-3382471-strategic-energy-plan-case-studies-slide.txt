 About Igiugig

 • Igiugig is a remote community
 of 70 people in southwestern
 Alaska.
 • It is located where Lake
 Iliamna is swallowed by the
 Kvichak River.
 • The Kvichak River is vital to
 Igiugig for drinking water and
 for salmon.
 • For more than 8,000 years, the
 Igyararmiut have lived here
 sustainably.
Igiugig Village is located in southwestern Alaska. Photos from Igiugig Village Council
 • The community has relied on
 salmon as a primary staple to
 their subsistence lifestyle for
 generations.

                                                                                                    National Laboratory of the Rockies | 28

 Igiugig’s Energy Key energy and resilience opportunities:
 • Igiugig is a consensus-based community with strong
 Transition and consistent leadership that has made significant
 progress prior to starting this planning initiative.
 • Igiugig has the potential for near-100% renewable
 generation.
 • Workforce development and training on renewable
 energy operations and maintenance in remote
 locations is key to deployment success.

 Key energy and resilience challenges:
 • Heavy dependence on diesel fuel
 • High and volatile fuel prices
 • High construction and maintenance prices for
 renewable energy projects
 • High cost of building roads and transmission results
 in challenges in creating regional plans
 • Loss of autonomy/resiliency with an increase to the
 technical complexity of the power system
 • Status of the RivGen and next steps with unproven
 technology.

 Project Impacts
• Since Igiugig’s CEP was adopted in 2022, the community has made significant progress toward
  achieving their goal of reducing diesel fuel used for electricity by 50% by 2030.
• Leveraging the modeling and heat recovery system schematics created through the Energy Technology
  Innovation Partnership Project, Igiugig was awarded:
 ◦ A 2023 Renewable Energy Fund Grant to install a 200-kW solar PV array and electric boiler at
 the power plant
 ◦ A 2024 U.S. Department of Agriculture High Energy Cost Grant to install a 100-kW Northwind
 Wind Turbine with additional battery energy storage system and electric boilers at the school
 and airport hangar
• The awarded projects are expected to reduce diesel fuel consumption for electricity by over 60% and
  reduce the cost of electricity by $0.26 per kilowatt-hour (kWh).
• Igiugig and NLR continue to collaborate through the 2023 Office of Electricity call on remote microgrids,
  awarded in October 2024. As part of this effort, Igiugig will receive hardware and ongoing technical
  assistance to further optimize renewable energy and storage in its microgrid.

This presentation was funded by the U.S. Department of Energy’s Energy Technology Innovation
Partnership Project (ETIPP). ETIPP is a community-led technical support program for coastal, remote, and
island communities to access unique solutions and increase energy resilience. By uniting federal agencies,
national laboratories, regional organizations, and community stakeholders, ETIPP provides tailored
technical support to help communities achieve affordable reliable solutions to their energy system
challenges. This collaborative model leverages the combined expertise and resources of its partners to
deliver comprehensive, practical solutions that align with local needs.
