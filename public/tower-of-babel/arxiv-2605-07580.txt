On ratios of theta functions
Senping Luo, Juncheng Wei

Abstract
. Motivated by the average partition function of c free bosons ( ( Afhkami-Jeddi et al. [ 3 ] ) ) and the average of the genus 1 partition function over the Narain moduli space ( ( Maloney-Witten [ 42 ] ) ) , we investigate ratios of theta functions. In this paper, we completely classify the minimizers (or maximizers) for ratios of theta and Epstein zeta functions. We find that the hexagonal lattice plays a pivotal role there. These results have direct applications in conformal and Liouville field theory via partition functions. Additionally, they yield the minima of differences of theta and Epstein zeta functions, which have implications for the mathematics of crystallization and interacting particle theory ( [ 7 , 10 ] ).



1.
Introduction and main results

Let
z\in\mathbb{H}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\operatorname{Im}(z)>0\}
. The Epstein zeta and theta functions associated with the lattice
\Lambda
are defined as

\displaystyle\zeta(s,\Lambda):=\sum_{\mathbb{P}\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}}\frac{1}{|\mathbb{P}|^{2s}},\;\;\theta(\alpha,\Lambda):=\sum_{\mathbb{P}\in\Lambda}e^{-\pi\alpha|\mathbb{P}|^{2}}.

By the parametrization
\Lambda=\sqrt{\frac{1}{\operatorname{Im}(z)}}\Big({\mathbb{Z}}\oplus z{\mathbb{Z}}\Big)
, one has

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\zeta(s,z):=\zeta(s,\Lambda)=\sum_{(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\operatorname{Im}(z)^{s}}{|mz+n|^{2s}},\;

(1.1)

\displaystyle\theta(\alpha,z):=\theta(\alpha,\Lambda)=\sum_{(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}e^{-\pi\alpha\cdot\frac{|mz+n|^{2}}{\operatorname{Im}(z)}}.

Define the subgroup of the modular group as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}:\hbox{the group generated by}\;\;\tau\mapsto-\frac{1}{\tau},\;\;\tau\mapsto\tau+1,\;\;\tau\mapsto-\overline{\tau}.

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{G}}:=\{z\in\mathbb{H}:|z|>1,\;0<\operatorname{Re}(z)<\frac{1}{2}\}.

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1}:=\{z\in\mathbb{H}:\operatorname{Re}(z)=0,\;\operatorname{Im}(z)\geq 1\},

Proposition 2.1
.

Assume that
\beta>\alpha\geq 1

\displaystyle\argmax_{z\in\Gamma_{2}}\frac{\theta(\beta,z)}{\theta(\alpha,z)}=e^{i\frac{\pi}{3}},\;\argmin_{z\in\Gamma_{2}}\frac{\theta(\beta,z)}{\theta(\alpha,z)}=i.

Proposition

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume that
\beta>\alpha\geq 1
. Then

\displaystyle\partial_{\theta}\frac{\theta(\beta,e^{i\theta})}{\theta(\alpha,e^{i\theta})}\leq 0,\;\;\hbox{for}\;\;\theta\in[\pi/3,\pi/2].

Proof.

Fix
\beta>\alpha\geq 1
. By Proposition
2.2
,

\displaystyle\partial_{x}\frac{\theta(\beta,e^{i\theta})}{\theta(\alpha,e^{i\theta})}\geq 0,\;\;\hbox{for}\;\;\theta\in[\pi/3,\pi/2].

(2.3)

Since
\frac{\theta(\beta,z)}{\theta(\alpha,z)}
is
\mathcal{G}
-invariant, then

\frac{\theta(\beta,re^{i\theta})}{\theta(\alpha,re^{i\theta})}=\frac{\theta(\beta,1/re^{i\theta})}{\theta(\alpha,1/re^{i\theta})}

After taking derivative with respect to

\displaystyle\partial_{x}\frac{\theta(\beta,e^{i\theta})}{\theta(\alpha,e^{i\theta})}\cos\theta+\partial_{y}\frac{\theta(\beta,e^{i\theta})}{\theta(\alpha,e^{i\theta})}\sin\theta=0.

(2.4)

Thus, (
2.3

\displaystyle\partial_{y}\frac{\theta(\beta,e^{i\theta})}{\theta(\alpha,e^{i\theta})}\leq 0,\;\;\hbox{for}\;\;\theta\in[\pi/3,\pi/2].

(2.5)

On the other hand, by taking derivative with respect to
\theta

\displaystyle\partial_{\theta}\frac{\theta(\beta,e^{i\theta})}{\theta(\alpha,e^{i\theta})}=\partial_{x}\frac{\theta(\beta,e^{i\theta})}{\theta(\alpha,e^{i\theta})}(-\sin\theta)+\partial_{y}\frac{\theta(\beta,e^{i\theta})}{\theta(\alpha,e^{i\theta})}\cos\theta.

(2.6)

In view of (

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\mathcal{W}(z)<0,\;\;z=(x,y)\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]\times[b,\infty)\;\;\hbox{for some}\;\;b<\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2},

and

\displaystyle\frac{a}{\frac{1}{4}+a^{2}}\geq b.

(3.3)

Then

\displaystyle\min_{z\in\mathbb{H}}\mathcal{W}(z)=\min_{z\in\overline{\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{G}}}}\mathcal{W}(z)\;\;\hbox{is attained at}\;\;e^{i\frac{\pi}{3}}(\hbox{hexagonal point}).

Here
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{G}}
is the fundamental domain corresponding to modular group
\hbox{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, explicitly,
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{G}}=\{z\in\mathbb{H}:|z|>1,\;0<x<\frac{1}{2}\}.

Proof.

By the first part of (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle\min_{z\in\overline{\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{G}}}}\mathcal{W}(z)=\min_{z\in\overline{\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{G}}}\cap\{y\leq a\}}\mathcal{W}(z).

We then assume
\min_{z\in\overline{\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{G}}}\cap\{y\leq a\}}\mathcal{W}(z)
is attained at
z_{1}:=(x_{1},y_{1})
.
Then
y_{1}\leq a
. Since
b<a
, by the second part of (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle x_{1}=\frac{1}{2}.

(3.4)

Taking
\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
-1&1\\
\end{array}\right)\in\hbox{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
,
we define

z_{2}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
-1&1\\
\end{array}\right)z_{1}
, it follows that

\displaystyle z_{2}=\frac{1}{1-z_{1}}=\frac{\frac{1}{2}}{\frac{1}{4}+y_{1}^{2}}+i\frac{y_{1}}{\frac{1}{4}+y_{1}^{2}}

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}(z_{2})=\mathcal{W}(z_{1}).

This implies that
z_{2}
still attains the minimum of
\min_{z\in\overline{\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{G}}}}\mathcal{W}(z)
.
Now we need an elementary inequality, namely,

\displaystyle\frac{u_{2}}{\frac{1}{4}+u_{2}^{2}}\geq\frac{u_{1}}{\frac{1}{4}+u_{1}^{2}}\;\;\hbox{if}\;\;\frac{1}{2}\leq u_{2}\leq u_{1}.

From this inequality, one has
\operatorname{Im}(z_{2})\geq b
. In fact,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Im}(z_{2})=\frac{y_{1}}{\frac{1}{4}+y_{1}^{2}}\geq\frac{a}{\frac{1}{4}+a^{2}}\;\;\hbox{if}\;\;\frac{1}{2}\leq y_{1}\leq a.

By (
3.3
), we have
z_{2}\in\overline{\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{G}}}\cap\{y\geq b\}
. Still by the second part of (
3.2
) and
z_{2}
is the minimum point, there must be
\operatorname{Re}(z_{2})=\frac{1}{2}
, i.e.,
\frac{\frac{1}{2}}{\frac{1}{4}+y_{1}^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}
. It yields that
y_{1}=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}
. This and (
3.4
) yield the result. These complete the proof.

We first recall some basic estimates. By skilfully using the Euler-Maclaurin summation formula, Rankin deduced that in his paper implicitly

Lemma 3.4

(A summation formula, Rankin 1953
[
57
]
)
.

Assume that
z\in\mathbb{H}
and
s>1
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{|mz+n|^{2s}}=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(s-\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(s)}y^{1-2s}\frac{1}{m^{2s-1}}+\sigma\cdot\frac{s}{2}\frac{(2s+1)^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}{(2s+2)^{s+1}}y^{-(2s+1)}\frac{1}{m^{2s+1}},\;\;\sigma\in[-1,1].

Here
z=x+iy
is a complex number in the upper half plane.

It looks that there is no exact and explicit summation formula for
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{|mz+n|^{2s}}
, hence Lemma
3.4
is the best available one to use. In Lemma
3.4
, one can view that

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{|mz+n|^{2s}}=\hbox{approximate part}+\hbox{error part}.

And at least for large
y
, we get

\displaystyle\hbox{approximate part}:=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(s-\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(s)}y^{1-2s}\frac{1}{m^{2s-1}},

\displaystyle\hbox{error part}:=\sigma\cdot\frac{s}{2}\frac{(2s+1)^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}{(2s+2)^{s+1}}y^{-(2s+1)}\frac{1}{m^{2s+1}}.

By Lemma
3.4
, one has

Lemma 3.5
.

Assume that
z\in\mathbb{H}
and
s>1
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{|mz+n|^{2s}}=\xi(2s-1)\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(s-\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(s)}y^{1-2s}+\sigma\cdot\xi(2s+1)\frac{s}{2}\frac{(2s+1)^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}{(2s+2)^{s+1}}y^{-(2s+1)},\;\;\sigma\in[-1,1].

Finally, one obtains the approximate and error part of Zeta functions
\zeta(s,z)
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Assume that
z\in\mathbb{H}
and
s>1
. Then

\displaystyle\zeta(s,z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{y^{s}}{|mz+n|^{2s}}

\displaystyle=2\xi(2s)y^{s}+2\xi(2s-1)\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(s-\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(s)}y^{1-s}+\sigma\cdot\xi(2s+1)s\frac{(2s+1)^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}{(2s+2)^{s+1}}y^{-(s+1)},\;\;\sigma\in[-1,1].

Proof.

Recall that

\displaystyle\zeta(s,z)=\sum_{(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{y^{s}}{|mz+n|^{2s}}.

(3.5)

We split the summation in terms of
m
into
m=0
,
m>0
,
m<0
.
Note that when
m=0
, the double summation in (
3.5
) becomes

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{y^{s}}{|n|^{2s}}=2y^{s}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2s}}=2\xi(2s)y^{s}.

Then

\displaystyle\zeta(s,z)=\sum_{(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{y^{s}}{|mz+n|^{2s}}=

\displaystyle 2\xi(2s)y^{s}+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{y^{s}}{|mz+n|^{2s}}+\sum_{m=-\infty}^{-1}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{y^{s}}{|mz+n|^{2s}}

(3.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\xi(2s)y^{s}+2\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{y^{s}}{|mz+n|^{2s}}.

Therefore, by Lemma

,

\displaystyle\zeta(s,z)=2\xi(2s)y^{s}+2\xi(2s-1)\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(s-\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(s)}y^{1-s}+\sigma\cdot\xi(2s+1)s\frac{(2s+1)^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}{(2s+2)^{s+1}}y^{-(s+1)},\;\;\sigma\in[-1,1].

\frac{\zeta(s,z)}{\zeta(s,e^{i\frac{\pi}{3}})}\geq\frac{a_{0}(s,y)-2a_{1}(s,y)}{a_{0}(s,\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2})}.
Or equivalently,

\displaystyle\frac{\zeta(s,z)}{\zeta(s,e^{i\frac{\pi}{3}})}\geq\mathcal{B}_{a}(s,y),

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{a}(s,y):=\frac{2\xi(2s)y^{s}+2\xi(2s-1)\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(s-\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(s)}y^{1-s}-\frac{8\pi^{s}\sqrt{y}}{\Gamma(s)}K_{s-\frac{1}{2}}(2\pi y)}{2\xi(2s)(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2})^{s}+2\xi(2s-1)\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(s-\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(s)}(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2})^{1-s}}.

Proof.

We first use Rankin’s Lemma (or Montegomery’s Lemma
[
43
,
57
]
), i.e.,
\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\zeta(s,z)<0
for

\{\operatorname{Im}(z)\geq\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\}\cap z\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{G}}
and
s>1
. Then
\zeta(s,z)\geq\zeta(s,\frac{1}{2}+iy)
.
We then use Chowla-Selberg formula given by Lemma
4.14
.
Indeed,

\displaystyle\zeta(s,\frac{1}{2}+iy)=a_{0}(s,y)+2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}a_{n}(s,y).

(4.27)

See
a_{0},a_{n}
in Lemma
4.14
. Using Lemma
4.15
, one has

\displaystyle\frac{a_{n+1}(y)}{a_{n}(y)}

\displaystyle=(1+\frac{1}{n})^{s-\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\sigma_{1-2s}(n+1)}{\sigma_{1-2s}(n)}\frac{K_{s-\frac{1}{2}}(2\pi(n+1)y)}{K_{s-\frac{1}{2}}(2\pi ny)}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\frac{1}{n})^{s-1}\frac{\sigma_{1-2s}(n+1)}{\sigma_{1-2s}(n)}e^{-2\pi y}.

In the range
s\in(1,4]
and
y\geq\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}
, then
\frac{a_{n+1}(y)}{a_{n}(y)}<1
for all
n\geq 1
.
Hence
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}a_{n}(s,\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2})
is an alternating series. Then
\zeta(s,z)\geq a_{0}(s,y)-2a_{1}(s,y)
.
For
\zeta(s,e^{i\frac{\pi}{3}})
, still using (
4.27
) and the series is alternating, then
\zeta(s,e^{i\frac{\pi}{3}})\leq a_{0}(s,y)
.
These yield the result.

∎

The lower bound function
\mathcal{B}_{a}(s,y)
is monotone on
s
and
y
directions. It follows that

Lemma 4.24
.

It holds that
\min_{s\in[1,4],y\geq 2}\mathcal{B}_{a}(s,y)=\mathcal{B}_{a}(1,2)
. Here

\mathcal{B}_{a}(1,2)=1.133290376\cdots
.

Now we are ready to prove the case
s\in(1,4]
of Lemma
3.1
. Namely,

Lemma 4.25
.

For
s\in(1,4]
,
y\geq 2,\alpha\geq 3s
and
k\leq 2s
, it holds that

\displaystyle\frac{\zeta(s,z)}{\zeta(s,e^{i\frac{\pi}{3}})}\cdot\Big(\frac{\theta(\alpha,e^{i\frac{\pi}{3}})}{\theta(\alpha,z)}\Big)^{k}>1.

Proof.

By Lemmas
4.22
,
4.23
and
4.24
,

\displaystyle\frac{\zeta(s,z)}{\zeta(s,e^{i\frac{\pi}{3}})}\cdot\Big(\frac{\theta(\alpha,e^{i\frac{\pi}{3}})}{\theta(\alpha,z)}\Big)^{k}\geq

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{a}(s,y)\Big(\frac{\theta(\alpha,e^{i\frac{\pi}{3}})}{\theta(\alpha,z)}\Big)^{2s}\geq\mathcal{B}_{a}(s,y)\Big(\frac{1}{\theta(3s,iy)}\Big)^{2s}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{a}(1,2)\Big(\frac{1}{\theta(3,2i)}\Big)^{2}=1.093639371\cdots>1.

∎

For large
s
, the Chowla-Selberg formula given by Lemma
4.14
does not work well due to the property of
K_{s}(y)
. Instead, we use
summation formula by Lemma
3.6
. A direct consequence of Lemma
3.6
gives that

Lemma 4.26
.

For
s\geq 4
and
y\geq 2
, it holds that

\displaystyle\frac{\zeta(s,z)}{\zeta(s,e^{i\frac{\pi}{3}})}\geq\mathcal{B}_{b}(s,y).

Here

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{b}(s,y):=\frac{2\xi(2s)y^{s}+2\xi(2s-1)\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(s-\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(s)}y^{1-s}-\xi(2s+1)s\frac{(2s+1)^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}{(2s+2)^{s+1}}y^{-(s+1)}}{2\xi(2s)(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2})^{s}+2\xi(2s-1)\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(s-\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(s)}(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2})^{1-s}+\xi(2s+1)s\frac{(2s+1)^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}{(2s+2)^{s+1}}(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2})^{-(s+1)}}.

Now we are ready to prove the case
s>4
of Lemma
3.1
. Namely,

Lemma 4.27
.

For
s>4
,
y\geq 2,\alpha\geq 3s
and
k\leq 2s
, it holds that

\displaystyle\frac{\zeta(s,z)}{\zeta(s,e^{i\frac{\pi}{3}})}\cdot\Big(\frac{\theta(\alpha,e^{i\frac{\pi}{3}})}{\theta(\alpha,z)}\Big)^{k}>1.

Proof.

For
s>4
and
y\geq 2
, one trivially has
\mathcal{B}_{b}(s,y)>\frac{y^{s}}{s(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2})^{s+1}}
.
By Lemmas
4.22
and
4.26
,

\displaystyle\frac{\zeta(s,z)}{\zeta(s,e^{i\frac{\pi}{3}})}\cdot\Big(\frac{\theta(\alpha,e^{i\frac{\pi}{3}})}{\theta(\alpha,z)}\Big)^{k}\geq\mathcal{B}_{b}(s,y)\Big(\frac{1}{\theta(3s,iy)}\Big)^{2s}\geq\frac{y^{s}}{s(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2})^{s+1}}\Big(\frac{1}{\theta(12,2i)}\Big)^{8}>\sqrt{3}.

∎

By Lemmas
4.25
and
4.27
, we complete the proof of Lemma
3.1
.
