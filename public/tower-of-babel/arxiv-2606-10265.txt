Coupling-split clusters in a swarmalator model with uniform coupling disorder
K. P. O’Keeffe

Abstract
We study the one-dimensional swarmalator model in which the phase coupling K i ′ K_{i}^{\prime} is drawn from a uniform distribution. Our main result is a static coupling-split cluster, in which the population partitions across the threshold K ′ = 0 K^{\prime}=0 that separates positively coupled ( K i ′ > 0 K_{i}^{\prime}>0 ) from negatively coupled ( K i ′ < 0 K_{i}^{\prime}<0 ) swarmalators, with smaller order parameter s = μ / γ s=\mu/\gamma set by the positive-coupling excess. The familiar async, phase-wave, and sync states persist, but each stability boundary feels a different part of the distribution: async the mean same-coordinate response, sync the most negatively coupled particle, and the phase wave the full density through a logarithmic characteristic equation. At a cusp where its Hopf and real-eigenvalue branches meet, the phase-wave dispersion has a double zero – the spectral signature of a Bogdanov–Takens point – and simulations nearby show a small-amplitude breathing limit cycle. For supports containing strongly negatively coupled particles the order parameters instead oscillate persistently.

Coupling heterogeneity is a natural next ingredient to study. In ordinary oscillator theory, allowing positive and negative phase couplings gives the conformist–contrarian problem, where even mean-field Kuramoto variants can produce antiphase locking, traveling waves, and clustered dynamics

;

\displaystyle h(K^{\prime})=\begin{cases}\dfrac{1}{2\gamma},&\mu-\gamma\leq K^{\prime}\leq\mu+\gamma,\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

(1)

which is simple enough to allow exact results while capturing the key feature of a continuous spread of coupling strengths.

The main new result is a static state we call the coupling-split cluster, which appears when the support straddles the threshold
K^{\prime}=0

K_{i}^{\prime}<0

II
Model

We consider
N
swarmalators on a ring, each with position
x_{i}\in S^{1}
and phase
\theta_{i}\in S^{1}
. The equations of motion are

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{i}

\displaystyle=\nu_{i}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j}J^{\prime}\sin(x_{j}-x_{i})\cos(\theta_{j}-\theta_{i}),

(2)

\displaystyle\dot{\theta}_{i}

\displaystyle=\omega_{i}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j}K_{i}^{\prime}\sin(\theta_{j}-\theta_{i})\cos(x_{j}-x_{i}),

(3)

where
J^{\prime}=1
is a fixed homogeneous spatial coupling and
K_{i}^{\prime}
is a heterogeneous phase coupling drawn from (
1
), with
K_{i}^{\prime}\in[\mu-\gamma,\mu+\gamma]
. We set
\nu_{i}=\omega_{i}=0
. The limit
\gamma=0
recovers the identical-coupling 1D swarmalator model with
J^{\prime}=1,\ K^{\prime}=\mu

O’Keeffe et al. 2022
;
Yoon et al. 2022
.

In sum and difference coordinates

\displaystyle\xi_{i}=x_{i}+\theta_{i},\qquad\eta_{i}=x_{i}-\theta_{i},

(4)

the trigonometric identity
\sin a\cos b=\tfrac{1}{2}[\sin(a+b)+\sin(a-b)]
recasts the dynamics in terms of two ‘response coefficients’

\displaystyle a_{i}=\frac{1+K_{i}^{\prime}}{2},\qquad b_{i}=\frac{1-K_{i}^{\prime}}{2},\qquad a_{i}+b_{i}=1,

(5)

where
a_{i}
is the same-coordinate response and
b_{i}
the cross-channel response. With the order parameters

\displaystyle re^{i\phi}=\left\langle e^{i\xi}\right\rangle,\qquad se^{i\psi}=\left\langle e^{i\eta}\right\rangle,

(6)

(
\langle\cdot\rangle=N^{-1}\sum_{j}
), the equations of motion become

\displaystyle\dot{\xi}_{i}

\displaystyle=a_{i}\,r\sin(\phi-\xi_{i})+b_{i}\,s\sin(\psi-\eta_{i}),

(7)

\displaystyle\dot{\eta}_{i}

\displaystyle=b_{i}\,r\sin(\phi-\xi_{i})+a_{i}\,s\sin(\psi-\eta_{i}).

(8)

A key structural feature of this model is that not only the same-coordinate response
a_{i}
but also the cross-channel response
b_{i}
is heterogeneous; the constraint
a_{i}+b_{i}=1
encodes the homogeneous spatial coupling
J^{\prime}=1
. This distinguishes the model from conformist–contrarian Kuramoto, where heterogeneity enters only the same-coordinate self-coupling
Hong and Strogatz 2011
.

In the continuum limit the state is a density
\rho(\xi,\eta,K^{\prime},t)
and the order parameters are

\displaystyle re^{i\phi}

\displaystyle=\iiint e^{i\xi}\rho(\xi,\eta,K^{\prime},t)h(K^{\prime})\,d\xi\,d\eta\,dK^{\prime},

(9)

\displaystyle se^{i\psi}

\displaystyle=\iiint e^{i\eta}\rho(\xi,\eta,K^{\prime},t)h(K^{\prime})\,d\xi\,d\eta\,dK^{\prime}.

(10)

We use these to classify the system’s macrostates.

IV.1
Async

We linearize the density
\rho
around the uniform state
\rho_{0}=(2\pi)^{-2}
, using the first-Fourier-mode projection of Strogatz and Mirollo
Strogatz and Mirollo 1991
as adapted to the swarmalator transport equation
O’Keeffe et al. 2022
;
O’Keeffe 2025b
. The full spectrum consists of a
K^{\prime}
-continuum on the imaginary axis plus a single collective order-parameter eigenvalue. The first-Fourier-mode projection of the linearized transport equation gives the collective eigenvalue
\lambda=\langle a\rangle/2=(1+\mu)/4
, so async is stable when

\displaystyle\boxed{\mu<-1.}

(11)

The width
\gamma
does not enter the order-parameter eigenvalue because the
b_{i}\,s\sin(\psi-\eta_{i})
term in

is
\xi

\rho_{0}
, so it contributes nothing to the

-mode of
\partial_{\xi}(\rho_{0}\dot{\xi})
; only the mean of
a
survives. The threshold
\mu=-1
is the point at which the mean same-coordinate response
\langle a\rangle=(1+\mu)/2

IV.2
Sync

The synchronized state has
\xi_{i}=\xi_{0}
,
\eta_{i}=\eta_{0}
, hence
r=s=1
. Setting
\xi_{0}=\eta_{0}=0
and linearizing gives

\displaystyle\dot{\xi}_{i}

\displaystyle=a_{i}(\bar{\xi}-\xi_{i})+b_{i}(\bar{\eta}-\eta_{i}),

(12)

\displaystyle\dot{\eta}_{i}

\displaystyle=b_{i}(\bar{\xi}-\xi_{i})+a_{i}(\bar{\eta}-\eta_{i}).

(13)

The per-particle Jacobian has eigenvalues
-(a_{i}+b_{i})=-1
(symmetric mode) and
-(a_{i}-b_{i})=-K_{i}^{\prime}
(antisymmetric mode). The symmetric mode is always damped; the antisymmetric mode is damped only when
K_{i}^{\prime}>0
, and grows for
K_{i}^{\prime}<0
. Thus sync is stable only if every particle is conformist,
K_{i}^{\prime}>0
for all
i
. For the uniform distribution this gives

\displaystyle\boxed{\mu>\gamma.}

(14)

The sync threshold is set by the most contrarian particle,
K^{\prime}_{\min}=\mu-\gamma
.

\displaystyle\rho_{0}(\xi,\eta,K^{\prime})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\delta(\eta),

\xi
is uniform and
\eta

The only non-neutral collective instability occurs in the first Fourier sector. Let

\displaystyle\dot{U}(K^{\prime},t)

\displaystyle=\frac{a}{2}\bar{U}(t)-i\,b\,V(K^{\prime},t),

(17)

\displaystyle\dot{V}(K^{\prime},t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{i\,b}{2}\bar{U}(t)-a\,V(K^{\prime},t).

(18)

The same-coordinate response is
a
and the cross-channel response is

U,V,\bar{U}\propto e^{\lambda t}
, the second equation gives

\displaystyle 2\lambda=\langle a\rangle-\int\frac{b^{2}(K^{\prime})\,h(K^{\prime})}{\lambda+a(K^{\prime})}\,dK^{\prime}.

The phase-wave boundary is controlled by the same-coordinate-weighted Cauchy transform of the coupling density, with both

\displaystyle\int_{a_{-}}^{a_{+}}\!\frac{(1-u)^{2}}{\lambda+u}du

\displaystyle=\tfrac{(1+\mu)\gamma}{2}-(2+\lambda)\gamma

A Bogdanov–Takens point and the breathing coupling-split cluster

The endpoint where the Hopf and real-eigenvalue boundaries meet carries the spectral signature of a Bogdanov–Takens (BT) point in the phase-wave
order-parameter dispersion

, the double-zero conditions
D(0)=\partial_{\lambda}D(0)=0

\displaystyle(\mu_{\rm BT},\gamma_{\rm BT})

\displaystyle\approx(0.21836,\,0.82728),

\displaystyle\partial_{\lambda}^{2}D(0)

\displaystyle\approx 11.96\neq 0,

(30)

so the marginal root has algebraic multiplicity exactly two (verified to a residual of
10^{-13}
); the locked-clump continuous spectrum lies at
\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda\in[-(1{+}\mu{+}\gamma)/2,-(1{+}\mu{-}\gamma)/2]\approx[-1.02,-0.20]
, bounded away from the axis. Consistently, the Hopf frequency vanishes at the cusp: tracking the rightmost root of
D
along the Hopf boundary gives
\omega^{2}\simeq 1.99\,(\gamma_{\rm BT}-\gamma)
, i.e.
\omega\to 0
as
\sqrt{\gamma_{\rm BT}-\gamma}
[Fig.
5
(b)], the square-root law expected at a BT point
Bogdanov 1975
;
Takens 1974
;
Guckenheimer and Holmes 1983
;
Kuznetsov 2004
. This double zero is a property of the explicit dispersion
D
; we make no claim about a normal form for the full continuum transport problem, whose linearization also carries a neutral continuous spectrum on the imaginary axis.

Near the cusp simulations from random initial conditions show a small-amplitude limit cycle, a
breathing coupling-split cluster
. At
(\mu,\gamma)=(0.05,0.80)
one split coordinate stays locked (
s\approx 1
) while the smaller order parameter

pulses periodically with period
T\approx 13.0
and a clean single-frequency spectrum [Fig.

\omega_{r}\approx 0.48
is twice the linear eigenmode frequency
\omega\approx 0.24

r=|\langle e^{i\xi}\rangle|
is a modulus. The configuration is a coupling-split cluster whose
\xi
-clumps breathe rather than hold the static value
r=|2P(K^{\prime}>0)-1|
; it is shown in panel 8 of Fig.

IV.4
Coupling-Split Clusters

Existence.
The static coupling-split clusters are the new states exposed by the continuous phase-coupling distribution. Define the instantaneous force components

\displaystyle F_{\xi i}=r\sin(\phi-\xi_{i}),\qquad F_{\eta i}=s\sin(\psi-\eta_{i}).

(31)

At equilibrium,

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}a_{i}&b_{i}\\
b_{i}&a_{i}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}F_{\xi i}\\
F_{\eta i}\end{pmatrix}=0.

(32)

The determinant of this matrix is
a_{i}^{2}-b_{i}^{2}=K_{i}^{\prime}
, so for all particles with
K_{i}^{\prime}\neq 0
the matrix is invertible and

\displaystyle F_{\xi i}=F_{\eta i}=0.

(33)

Thus each particle must sit either with or opposite each mean field:

\displaystyle\xi_{i}\in\{\phi,\phi+\pi\},\qquad\eta_{i}\in\{\psi,\psi+\pi\}.

(34)

This immediately gives a general coupling-split construction for any normalized density
h(K^{\prime})
whose support straddles
K^{\prime}=0
(i.e., contains both conformist and contrarian particles). Let

\displaystyle p_{+}=\int_{0}^{\infty}h(K^{\prime})\,dK^{\prime},\qquad p_{-}=\int_{-\infty}^{0}h(K^{\prime})\,dK^{\prime}.

(35)

If
p_{+}>p_{-}
, there is a
\xi
-dominant branch in which the conformist (
K^{\prime}>0
) particles align in
\eta
and the contrarian (
K^{\prime}<0
) particles anti-align in
\eta
:

\displaystyle r=1,\qquad s=p_{+}-p_{-}=2p_{+}-1.

(36)

The coordinate-swapped branch has
r=2p_{+}-1
and
s=1
. The split-coordinate order parameter is not controlled by a moment of
h(K^{\prime})
; it is controlled by the conformist excess – the mass imbalance across the sign threshold
K^{\prime}=0
.

Assume the support straddles
K^{\prime}=0
and the most contrarian particle still has
a>0
:

\displaystyle\mu-\gamma<0<\mu+\gamma,\qquad\mu-\gamma>-1.

(37)

For the
\xi
-dominant coupling-split cluster, all particles align in
\xi
, while their
\eta
coordinate is selected by the sign of
K_{i}^{\prime}
:

\displaystyle\xi_{i}

\displaystyle=\phi,

(38)

\displaystyle\eta_{i}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\psi,&K_{i}^{\prime}>0,\\
\psi+\pi,&K_{i}^{\prime}<0.\end{cases}

(39)

This gives

\displaystyle r=1,\qquad s=P(K^{\prime}>0)-P(K^{\prime}<0).

(40)

For the uniform distribution,

\displaystyle P(K^{\prime}>0)=\frac{\mu+\gamma}{2\gamma},\qquad P(K^{\prime}<0)=\frac{\gamma-\mu}{2\gamma}.

(41)

Self-consistency of the mean-field phase in (
39
) requires the aligned (conformist) clump to have larger mass, so
\mu>0
. In that case

\displaystyle\boxed{r=1,\qquad s=\frac{\mu}{\gamma}.}

(42)

The coordinate-swapped branch has

\displaystyle\boxed{r=\frac{\mu}{\gamma},\qquad s=1.}

(43)

In the original position–phase variables,

\displaystyle x_{i}=\frac{\xi_{i}+\eta_{i}}{2},\qquad\theta_{i}=\frac{\xi_{i}-\eta_{i}}{2}.

(44)

Thus the
\xi
-dominant coupling-split cluster is a two-clump state in which positively and negatively coupled particles share the same
\xi=x+\theta
coordinate but occupy opposite
\eta=x-\theta
clumps. The coupling-split cluster exists as a static solution throughout the region

\displaystyle\boxed{0<\mu<\gamma<\mu+1.}

(45)

The three inequalities encode: positive-coupling majority (
\mu>0
), support straddles
K^{\prime}=0
(
\gamma>\mu
), and no strongly negatively coupled particle (
\gamma<\mu+1
). The coordinate-swapped branch has the same condition.

Transverse stability.
Linearizing the single-particle dynamics (
7
)–(
8
) about each clump position at frozen mean fields
(r,\phi,s,\psi)
gives the Jacobian

\displaystyle J_{i}=-\begin{pmatrix}a_{i}r\sigma_{\xi}&b_{i}s\sigma_{\eta}\\[2.0pt]
b_{i}r\sigma_{\xi}&a_{i}s\sigma_{\eta}\end{pmatrix},

(46)

where
\sigma_{\xi},\sigma_{\eta}=\pm 1
record whether the particle aligns (
+
) or anti-aligns (
-
) with each mean field. Its determinant and trace are

\displaystyle\det J_{i}=\sigma_{\xi}\sigma_{\eta}\,rs\,K_{i}^{\prime},\qquad\mathrm{tr}\,J_{i}=-a_{i}\,(r\sigma_{\xi}+s\sigma_{\eta}).

(47)

For the assignment in (
39
) – conformists aligned in both coordinates and contrarians anti-aligned in
\eta
– one has
\sigma_{\xi}\sigma_{\eta}=\mathrm{sign}(K_{i}^{\prime})
, so
\det J_{i}=rs\,|K_{i}^{\prime}|>0
, while
\mathrm{tr}\,J_{i}=-a_{i}(r+s)<0
for conformists and
-a_{i}(r-s)<0
for contrarians (using
r=1
,
0<s<1
). Both eigenvalues thus have negative real part for every particle precisely when
a_{i}=(1+K_{i}^{\prime})/2>0
for all
i
, i.e. when
\gamma<\mu+1
– the same bound as existence. This is a single-particle (frozen-mean-field) result; the collective stability of the branch is left open and supported only numerically below.

The equal-weight binary split state is the endpoint
s=0
of this picture. For a binary distribution with one mass of contrarians and one mass of conformists in equal weight, the construction gives no majority clump and hence zero partial order in the split coordinate. For the continuous uniform distribution with
\mu>0
, the conformist excess across
K^{\prime}=0
makes the partial order nonzero and turns the coupling-split cluster into an observable partially ordered state in simulations.

IV.5
Strongly-Contrarian Unsteady Dynamics

The model supports two qualitatively distinct forms of nonstatic behavior. The first is the small-amplitude periodic orbit of §
IV.3
:
r
oscillates with period
T\approx 13
in the limit-cycle wedge born at the BT cusp
(\mu_{\rm BT},\gamma_{\rm BT})\approx(0.218,0.827)
. The second, described below, is qualitatively different: it has a broadband power spectrum (Fig.
8
, lower panel), large amplitude, and a wide wedge of occupancy in
(\mu,\gamma)
.

Simulations in the strongly-contrarian regime reveal this second unsteady dynamics. It is produced by the phase-coupling distribution itself, without delay or external forcing. Empirically it occupies the wedge

\displaystyle\boxed{\mu>-1,\qquad\gamma>\mu+1,}

(48)

where the support
[\mu-\gamma,\mu+\gamma]
contains at least one strongly contrarian particle (
K_{i}^{\prime}<-1
) while the mean same-coordinate response stays positive,
\langle a\rangle=(1+\mu)/2>0
(i.e.
\mu>-1
, so that async is unstable). Note the mean coupling
\langle K_{i}^{\prime}\rangle=\mu
may itself be negative here. In this region none of the four static branches analyzed above is admissible: async is linearly unstable because
\mu>-1
; the phase wave is not admissible because the positive-support condition
\mu-\gamma>-1
fails; sync is unreachable because
\mu<\gamma
; and the coupling-split construction of Eq. (
45
) is unavailable because its
\gamma<\mu+1
bound is violated. We do not claim that no static branch exists in the wedge. The pointwise equilibrium condition
F_{\xi i}=F_{\eta i}=0
still admits a combinatorial family of four-corner sign assignments, and the transverse Jacobian determinant computed in §
IV.4
is
\sigma_{\xi}\sigma_{\eta}\,rs\,K_{i}^{\prime}
at each particle, so additional sub-threshold static branches involving particles with
K_{i}^{\prime}<-1
could in principle exist. A complete enumeration of those branches is beyond the scope of this paper; what we establish here is that among the analyzed branches none is attracting in the wedge, and simulations from random initial conditions throughout the wedge converge to unsteady dynamics rather than to any of them.

(\mu,\gamma)
scan (Fig.

800

N=400

T=4000
) gives
\lambda_{\max}\approx 0.11>0
, distinguishing this dynamics from the single-frequency breathing cycle of §
IV.3
, for which the same estimate is consistent with zero. The leading exponent is unaffected by the two neutral rotational modes.

The empirical boundaries of this region are themselves transitions of the analyzed static branches. The lower boundary
\mu=-1
is the async destabilization, on which the leading eigenvalue
\lambda=(1+\mu)/4
crosses zero through a real eigenvalue rather than a Hopf. Thus the unsteady dynamics is not produced by a Hopf of async; it is observed after the analyzed static attractors are absent above
\mu=-1
in the strongly-contrarian wedge. The boundary
\gamma=\mu+1
is the positive-support line of the phase wave (equivalently, the upper edge of the coupling-split region); crossing it from the wedge into
\gamma<\mu+1
restores at least one of the analyzed static attractors (phase wave or coupling-split cluster, depending on
\mu
) and the oscillations disappear. A finer characterization of the spectrum and amplitude of this dynamics, and a systematic enumeration of strongly-contrarian static branches, are left for future work.

IV.6
Robustness to Compact-Support Densities

The coupling-split construction does not rely on the uniform density. Let
g(K^{\prime})
be any compactly supported density with support straddling
K^{\prime}=0
and contained within
(-1,\infty)
. Then the same pointwise equilibrium condition gives

\displaystyle r=1,\qquad s=q_{g}=\left|2P_{g}(K^{\prime}>0)-1\right|,

(49)

provided the larger subpopulation is used as the aligned clump. The uniform distribution is the special case
q_{g}=\mu/\gamma
.

As a first non-uniform example, take the symmetric triangular density centered at
\mu
with half-width
\gamma
. For
\delta=\mu/\gamma\in(0,1)
and
\mu>0
, Eq. (
49
) gives

\displaystyle q_{\triangle}=2\delta-\delta^{2}.

(50)

Figure
9
shows that finite-
N
simulations initialized near the coupling-split branch collapse onto Eq. (
49
) for uniform and triangular couplings (the two symmetric compactly-supported families with closed-form
q_{g}
). Asymmetric compactly-supported families are expected to give the same collapse, since Eq. (
49
) depends only on the conformist mass and not on the shape of
h(K^{\prime})
; we have not exhaustively tested asymmetric densities here because samples placing mass close to
K^{\prime}=0
produce particles whose transverse relaxation timescale
\propto 1/|K_{i}^{\prime}|
diverges, which spoils the cluster construction on the simulation timescale rather than the formula itself.

This robustness test concerns the coupling-split branch, not the full phase diagram. The async state depends on
\langle K^{\prime}\rangle
, sync depends on the lower edge of the support, and the phase-wave boundary depends on the full transform
\int g(K^{\prime})(2\lambda+1+K^{\prime})^{-1}\,dK^{\prime}
. The compact-support restriction is important: if the support extends arbitrarily close to
K^{\prime}=0
or down to
K^{\prime}=-1
, the spectral gap associated with the split construction can collapse.

V
Discussion

We studied the 1D swarmalator model with phase coupling
K_{i}^{\prime}
drawn from a uniform distribution. The main result is simple: each familiar state survives, but feels a different part of the coupling spread – async the mean response
\langle a\rangle=(1+\mu)/2
, sync the most contrarian particle
\mu-\gamma
, and the phase wave the full density through a logarithmic characteristic equation [Eq. (
22
)]. Straddling the sign threshold
K^{\prime}=0
adds a new static state, the coupling-split cluster, in which positively and negatively coupled particles occupy antipodal clumps; its order parameter is set by the conformist excess alone, reducing for the uniform law to
s=\mu/\gamma
.

What is interesting is that this cluster is invisible in the binary conformist–contrarian problem, where the equal-weight split gives
s=0
and the state is marginal. The continuous distribution does more than smooth the binary phase diagram: its nonzero conformist excess turns the split into an observable partially ordered family. Two further features sit at the edges of the static picture – the phase-wave dispersion has a Bogdanov–Takens cusp with a nearby small-amplitude breathing cycle, and strongly contrarian supports (
\gamma>\mu+1
) drive persistent, irregular oscillations of the order parameters.

Future work could pin down what we left numerical: a continuum stability spectrum for the coupling-split branch, whose modes accumulate near
K^{\prime}=0
; the attractors of the strongly-contrarian wedge and any static branches hidden there; and a continuum normal form for the BT cusp. Adding more realism – broader coupling densities, distributed natural frequencies, delay, or two spatial dimensions
O’Keeffe et al. 2024
– may also be fruitful.
