Correlation of the monocyte to high-density lipoprotein cholesterol ratio and neutrophil to high-density lipoprotein cholesterol ratio with the severity of acute ischemic stroke
Authors: Tahreem Sajjad, Iftikhar Haider, Alishba jaffery, Muhammad Ubaid
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundStroke as the major cause of mortality and morbidity worldwide, requires early assessment of severity and prognosis for effective treatment. There is an emerging role of inflammatory markers in stroke severity. Thus, we aimed to determine the correlation of neutrophil to HDL-cholesterol ratio (NHR) and monocyte to HDL-cholesterol ratio (MHR) with the severity of acute ischemic stroke (AIS).MethodologyA single Centre cross-sectional study involving adult patients with AIS. Patients were grouped into mild and moderate-severe stroke by National Institute of Health Severity Scale (NIHSS). Demographic, clinical and laboratory data of participants were collected.Spearman correlation analysis, univariate and multiple logistic regression analysis were utilized to explore the correlation between MHR with AIS severity. A receiver operating characteristic curve was created to determine the discriminatory ability of MHR in assessing stroke severity.ResultsMale gender, diabetes, hypertension, monocyte count, triglyceride, total cholesterol, CRP, uric acid and MHR were found statistically significant between the mild and moderate-severe stroke groups. Spearman correlation analysis produced a weak but statistically significant positive correlation between MHR and NIHSS score with a correlation coefficient of 0.201 (P = 0.026). Binary logistic regression analysis demonstrated that MHR had statistically insignificant positive association with higher NIHSS scores (OR = 2.275, 95% CI: 0.924–5.6, p = 0.074). Multiple logistic regression analysis revealed that MHR was not independently associated with stroke severity scale (adjusted OR = 1.527, 95% CI: 0.559–4.173, p = 0.409). The area under the curve (AUC) for MHR association with AIS was 0.588 (95% CI: 0.485–0.691, p = 0.101) with specificity of 87.5% and sensitivity of 40.5%.ConclusionMHR could serve as a new, readily accessible and inexpensive indicator of the severity of AIS.

Introduction

Stroke remains a cause of high mortality and morbidity worldwide, contributing to around 5.5 million deaths annually despite major advancements in treatment and prevention methods. [1] Worldwide stroke ranks as the second chief cause of death and the third chief cause of death and disability together. [1] The health-related financial impact on stroke survivors is expected to rise at an exceptional pace due to the age-related nature of the condition as more than 50% of the affected individuals being over 65 years. [2]

It is essential to accurately and promptly recognize patients with unfavourable prognosis so that treatment and rehabilitation can be attuned according to the specific needs of each individual. Pakistan being a 5th populous country of world have shown annual occurrence of stroke in Pakistan with 250 per 100,000 individuals with prevalence of 1.2% having male to female ratio was 1.02:1 respectively. [3] In 2019, ischemic stroke accounted for 62.4% of all stroke cases worldwide, making it the most prevalent type [4] and similar trend of ischemic stroke was reported in Pakistan. [3]

Ischemic stroke shows significant differences in its causes, mechanisms and clinical manifestations. Although there have been major improvements in the management of acute ischemic stroke (AIS) and in diagnostic imaging, there is still much progress needed in terms of prognosis and rehabilitation after a stroke. [5], [6] Alongside using different clinical assessment instruments and neurophysiological methods, identifying and creating new biomarkers especially blood biomarkers (immediate accessibility and affordability nearly all healthcare settings) is crucial, particularly in the early stages of ischemic stroke. [7]

Thrombo-inflammation which refers to the interplay between thrombosis and inflammation is a fundamental pathogenic factor underlying ischemic stroke. [8] Inflammation has a vital role in the commencement, advancement, degradation, and disruption of atherosclerotic plaques which can result in thrombo-embolic incidents. Inflammation plays an essential role and is involved in the stages of AIS. Neutrophils are the initial immune cells derived from the blood that infiltrate ischemic areas and release proinflammatory cytokines, reactive oxygen species and proteases, succeeded by monocytes leading to atherosclerosis and thrombosis. [9] Recent studies have shown defensive role of high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL—C) which is attributed to its capability to diminish atherogenesis through anti-inflammatory role, anti-oxidative effect on low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL), anti-thrombotic properties (extract cholesterol from cells) and endothelial repair in ischemic stroke. [10] Considering above rationale various studies have reported the significant correlation of neutrophil to HDL-cholesterol ratio (NHR) in cardiovascular diseases. [11], [12]

In the ongoing process of thromboembolism associated with stroke, monocytes and lymphocytes play a role after ischemia, especially during the initial phase, where monocytes migrate to the damaged region, compromising the blood-brain barrier by releasing inflammatory agents. Lymphocytes enter the ischemic tissue and play a role in the inflammatory response by regulating the inflammatory cytokines cascade. [13], [14] Considering neutrophil and monocyte with their role in acute ischemic inflammatory lieu and relation with HDL—C, NHR and monocyte to C-HDL (MHR) ratio were proposed as biomarkers of AIS.

Earlier studies have shown NHR sensitivity and specificity of 56.3%, 87.0% - 65.1%, 72.5% respectively for diagnosis and clinical stratification for severity. [11], [15] Similarly, Liu et al., 2020 in a retrospective study have shown sensitivity and specificity of MHR in AIS of 66.01% and 77.25% respectively. [16] Liu et al., 2020 in their prospective data have shown sensitivity and specificity of MHR in AIS of 66.5% and 62.3% respectively. [13]

Pakistan has a high incidence of 350,000 with an annual fatality of 141,000 made stroke as a primary cause of mortality and disability. Rising trends of AIS among young individuals compare to affluent nations with 13.2% in hospital mortality and only 36% reported recovery in Pakistan made situation worse. [17] NHR and MHR are recognized as being simple and inexpensive markers that have already demonstrated potential in estimating stroke severity, outcomes, and mortality in global research. Considering the substantial burden and a shortage of severity assessment tools, assessing NHR and MHR in AIS patients may facilitate early risk categorization, inform treatment strategies, and strengthen policies aimed at enhancing stroke management in settings with constrained resources. Thus, the study is designed to examine the relation of NHR and MHR with severity of AIS and discuss their potential as low-cost indicators of severity in AIS patients.

Methodology

This was a cross-sectional study conducted at Dr. Ruth KM Pfau Civil Hospital in Karachi. The duration of this study was 6 months after institutional review board (IRB) approval. Online Open EPI sample size software was used for calculating sample size by proportion of study by Anjum Farooq et al. that reported prevalence of stroke in Pakistan as 1.2%. [18] By taking confidence level as 99.99% and margin of error as 4%, sample size was calculated n = 113 which approximated to 122. Non-probability consecutive sampling technique was used. All patients ≥18 years of age, diagnosed as AIS and presented within 7 days of symptoms onset were included. Patients were grouped on the basis of severity of AIS by National Institute of Health Severity Scale (NIHSS) with NIHSS ≤4 (mild) and > 4 (moderate-severe). [19] NIHSS score was assessed by on duty resident familiar with routine NIHSS assessment at the time of patient first encounter after admission. Furthermore, patients who had a history of prior strokes, conditions affecting NHR like infections, blood disorders, tumors, significant kidney or liver damage and pregnancy were excluded from the study. IRB approval (IRB-3411/DUHS/Approval/2024/121) was sought before the commencement of the study.

Statistical analysis

The Shapiro-Wilk test was used to assess the normality of data. Descriptive statistics were used for baseline characteristics of the participants. Continuous variables with a normal distribution were noted as mean ± standard deviation, while continuous variables with a non-normal distribution were noted as median (quartiles [Q25, Q75]). Mann-Whitney U test and independent sample t-test were used for comparison between groups of continuous variables. Frequencies and percentages were reported for categorical variables and the chi-square test was employed for group comparisons. Spearman's correlation was used to determine the relationship of MHR with NIHSS score. To evaluate discriminatory ability of MHR in determining the stroke severity, a receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was designed. The association between MHR and NHR with AIS was investigated using univariate and multiple logistic regression analysis. Gender, comorbidities, total cholesterol and CRP were included as confounders in multivariate logistic regression analysis. For statistical significance, a P-value of less than 0.05 was taken into consideration.

Univariate and multiple logistic regression analysis

Binary logistic regression analysis was done to assess the relationship between MHR and the NIHSS score. The results demonstrated that MHR had positive association with higher NIHSS scores although it did not reach statistical significance (OR = 2.275, 95% CI: 0.924–5.6, p = 0.074) [Table 3]. MHR was also tested for multiple logistic regression analysis and produced that MHR was not independently associated with stroke severity scale (adjusted OR = 1.527, 95% CI: 0.559–4.173, p = 0.409) after adjustment for confounders (gender, comorbidities, total cholesterol and CRP) [Table 3]. The correlation matrix assessed multicollinearity, showing no substantial correlations (all r < 0.50), suggesting that multicollinearity likely did not affect model estimates. Model adequacy was evaluated using the Omnibus Test of Model Coefficients, pseudo-R2 metrics (Cox & Snell and Nagelkerke), and the classification accuracy. Discussion

Majority of the patients (60.7%) had moderate to severe stroke in this study. The moderate to severe stroke category was predominantly present among male gender. Earlier studies have also shown the similar male predominance among patients having stroke. [21], [22] The gender-related differences in stroke are attributed to variations in sex steroid hormones, mainly estrogen along with genetic and anatomical elements could also play a role in the differences observed between sexes in stroke epidemiology, pathophysiology, and clinical results. [23] In the current study, diabetes and hypertension among the co-morbid conditions were found in patients with moderate to severe AIS. Earlier studies have also shown diabetes and hypertension as the primary risk factors for stroke development and death. [24], [25] There were statistically significant differences between the biochemical parameters like total cholesterol, uric acid, monocyte count, CRP and triglycerides among two groups of AIS in this study. Batool et al. in a large cross-sectional study have shown higher triglycerides levels in AIS when compared with hemorrhagic stroke. [26] Alemayehu E, et al. have also shown higher levels of total cholesterol and C-LDL in AIS when compared with hemorrhagic and healthy control groups. [27] Tong X, et al. have also shown similar results to this study where hyperuricemia was found a predictor of AIS. [28] Tariq MA, et al. have also shown hyperuricemia as a worst prognostic factor in AIS similar to this study. [29] In our study MHR was found to be statistically significant among the groups of AIS [Fig. 1]. Similar study from India have shown association of MHR with severity of AIS, where raised MHR was found in moderate to severe AIS when compared with mild cases. [30] Alagöz, A. et al. proved in their study correlation of MHR with severity of AIS both clinically and radiologically. [31] Omar, T., et al. have shown MHR was an independent predictor of carotid artery disease which is in favour of this study. [32] Similar to this study, Lin H, et al. have even shown correlation of MHR with intracranial large vessels atherosclerosis in AIS patients. Spearman correlation analysis was used to determine the MHR correlation with NIHSS scale. Statistically significant weak positive correlation was found between MHR and NIHSS score. Although the correlation is weak but it suggests the strong relationship between systemic inflammation and stroke severity. Binary logistic regression analysis was done to assess the relationship between MHR and the NIHSS score demonstrated that higher MHR values were associated with greater stroke severity although it did not reach statistical significance. MHR was also tested for multiple logistic regression analysis after adjustment for confounders (gender, comorbidities, total cholesterol and CRP) and revealed that MHR was not independently associated with stroke severity.

Furthermore, ROC curve analysis of MHR in association with severity scale in this study showed an AUC around 0.588 that indicates weak discrimination and p-value was non-significant with specificity and sensitivity of 87.5% and 40.5%, respectively [Fig. 2], indicating that MHR may be a hazard factor and has limited discriminatory performance. Liu H, et al. have shown a sensitivity of 66.01% and a specificity of 77.25% of MHR in predicting AIS severity. [13] Wang, HY have also shown a linear relationship between MHR levels and the likelihood of ischemic stroke within a Chinese population. [34] Current study has shown insignificant association (OR = 1.009, 95% CI: 0.996–1.022, P = 0.171) of NHR with AIS.Fig. 2ROC curve analysis showing sensitivity and specificity of MHR for AIS severity. The study has found statistically significant association of monocytes count with stroke severity but the HDL-C has not showed significant association. So, the observed association between MHR and AIS severity appears primarily driven by monocyte count. In the present study, we did not observe a significant correlation between the NHR and ischemic stroke severity, which contrasts with previously published studies that reported NHR as an independent predictor of stroke outcomes. [11], [35], [36] This discrepancy may be explained by differences in sample size, and patient characteristics. The statistical power to identify subtle relationships may have been restricted by the small size of our sample. Moreover, population-specific factors such as genetic background, lifestyle, and comorbidities (e.g., diabetes, hypertension, dyslipidemia) may have attenuated the relationship between NHR and stroke severity in our sample. It is also possible that variations in timing of blood sample collection, stroke subtypes included (early hyper acute: 0 to 6 h, late hyper acute: 6 to 24 h, acute: 24 h to 1 week), and analytical methods contributed to the observed differences. Importantly, our findings emphasize that NHR may not universally serve as a reliable marker across all populations and clinical settings.

While our results do not diminish the potential role of NHR suggested by prior studies, they highlight the need for larger, multicenter investigations and meta-analyses to better establish the consistency and generalizability of NHR as a potential biomarker in ischemic stroke.

The study's strengths comprise its clinical relevance with practical implications for acute stroke care, a focus on routinely available laboratory markers that may be scalable in lower-resource environments and the inclusion of both MHR and NHR allows comparative interpretation. Employing multivariable analysis reflects an attempt to adjust for confounding. Regional data from Pakistan adds geographic diversity to the literature.

The study's limitations are notable, primarily due to its single-center cross-sectional design, which restricts the ability to draw causal inferences and limits the generalizability of the findings. Additionally, the modest sample size raises concern about potential model instability, while the dichotomization of outcome may compromise statistical power. Additionally, inflammation is a continuous process, and in this research NHR and MHR were only assessed within 24 h of admission and the subgroups on the basis of sampling timing were not made. Thus, future detailed prospective studies that monitor the progression of these inflammatory markers over the course of the disease would be valuable.

Conclusion

Higher MHR was modestly associated with greater baseline ischemic stroke severity whereas NHR was not. Given the limited discriminatory performance and cross-sectional design large prospective multicenter studies are needed before clinical implementation.
