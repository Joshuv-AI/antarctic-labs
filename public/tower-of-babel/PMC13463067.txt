P-YOLOv10: an e-bike safety detection and fine-grained license plate region recognition method with multi-scale attention integration
Authors: Shaohui Zhong, Xiaofei Liu, Caihua Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn response to the increasingly serious traffic safety issues and regulatory challenges of electric bicycles (e-bikes), this study proposes an advanced multi-task detection model called P-YOLOv10. The model aims to achieve end-to-end unified recognition of riding safety factors and fine-grained regional attributes of license plates. To address inaccurate small-object detection and difficulty in distinguishing fine-grained features in complex real-world scenes, P-YOLOv10 introduces systematic optimizations based on the latest YOLOv10 architecture. First, it integrates the Selective Channel-Spatial Attention (SCSA) module to enhance the network’s ability to capture key local features. Second, it adopts the minimum point distance intersection over union (MPDIoU) loss function to improve bounding box regression accuracy, especially for small objects such as license plates. Finally, it uses the Gaussian error linear unit (GELU) activation function to improve nonlinear representation and training stability. This study trains and evaluates the model on a self-built dataset with 2,237 images. The dataset covers diverse scenes in Guangzhou and Foshan and includes new fine-grained regional annotations. The experimental results show that P-YOLOv10 achieves excellent performance. Its overall mean average precision (mAP) reaches 96.5%, which is 1% higher than the baseline YOLOv10. It also achieves high accuracy on the newly added license plate region recognition task. The results of this study confirm the effectiveness of the integrated optimization strategy. They provide a more accurate and more comprehensive technical solution for intelligent traffic regulation systems.Subject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

With the rapid spread of e-bikes in urban transportation systems, their traffic safety management has become an important research and regulatory issue. As a convenient, affordable, and environmentally friendly mode of short-distance travel, e-bikes are increasingly used in China and other Asian regions, as well as in European countries such as the Netherlands. They have partly replaced traditional bicycles and motor vehicles in daily travel1,2. At the same time, risk factors during e-bike use, such as not wearing helmets, carrying passengers illegally, and failing to install license plates as required, have been identified in many studies as common illegal or dangerous behaviors. These behaviors are closely associated with an increased risk of injuries3,4. Computer vision-based automated traffic monitoring technologies have developed rapidly in recent years. They can identify traffic participant behaviors in real time in unattended environments, including vehicle attribute recognition, helmet-wearing detection, and license plate information analysis5,6. In particular, license plate information is not only used to identify vehicle identity. Its regional attribution is also crucial for traffic flow monitoring, cross-regional traffic management, and vehicle tracing. Related studies have used automatic number plate recognition (ANPR) data to analyze vehicle travel patterns, origin-destination relationships, and cross-regional mobility6,7. In this context, building a unified vision system that can perform both e-bike riding safety detection and license plate region recognition in real road environments has important practical value and research significance.

Although deep learning-based visual recognition techniques have made significant progress in the traffic domain, achieving high-accuracy and multi-task recognition of e-bikes and their related behaviors in real road environments still faces many challenges. First, e-bikes often appear as fast-moving and small-sized objects in road surveillance images. The license plate region is even smaller and contains very limited pixels. It is easily affected by lighting changes, occlusion, noise, and motion blur. These factors greatly reduce the stability of detection and recognition8. Previous studies have shown that small-object detection in traffic scenes has much lower accuracy and robustness than medium and large object detection. It is also more sensitive to the design of feature representation networks9. Second, license plate region recognition is a fine-grained visual classification problem. The differences between classes are usually very small. In contrast, variations within the same class, such as viewpoint, lighting, and blur, are significant. This makes it difficult for models to capture discriminative local features10. Third, under a multi-task learning framework, jointly optimizing detection tasks, such as vehicle, license plate, helmet, and passenger behavior recognition, and fine-grained classification tasks often causes task conflict. Different tasks compete for shared feature representations. This leads to unstable training and degrades final performance11. Therefore, designing a unified model that can address these challenges in a coordinated way is key to advancing this field.

In recent years, significant progress has been made in single-task studies such as traffic object detection, license plate recognition, and helmet detection. However, existing methods still struggle to meet the practical needs of urban governance for comprehensive multi-factor recognition. For example, some studies have achieved automatic detection of traffic violations, such as helmet non-use and license plate recognition, in complex traffic scenes. However, these methods mainly focus on a single object or a single task. They do not model or recognize deeper semantic attributes, such as regional attribution12,13. Zhu et al. improved road vehicle and traffic violation detection using a modified YOLO architecture. However, their method did not address fine-grained semantic recognition of license plate regions14. At the same time, existing lightweight helmet detection networks can achieve real-time recognition. However, most methods are still limited to single-class or single-behavior recognition. They lack systematic multi-task integration capability13,15. From a model structure perspective, current mainstream detectors widely use IoU-based loss functions, such as GIoU, DIoU, and CIoU, for bounding box regression. Although these loss functions improve localization accuracy compared with traditional IoU, they still have limitations in fine-grained target localization and convergence efficiency16. In addition, the standard ReLU activation function performs poorly in fine-grained feature learning and capturing subtle shape differences. Because it directly truncates negative values, it may limit the model’s ability to represent continuous and subtle semantic variations17,18. In summary, current studies still show clear limitations in both model capability and task coverage. There is a lack of unified multi-task models that can adapt to real road scenarios. There is also a lack of dedicated optimization mechanisms for fine-grained semantic features such as license plate regions. These research gaps highlight the importance and necessity of developing new multi-task models.

To address the above challenges, this paper proposes an improved multi-task detection framework called P-YOLOv10. The framework aims to achieve high-accuracy detection of e-bike riding safety factors and fine-grained recognition of license plate regions. The main contributions of this paper are as follows:

Related work

Evolution of the YOLO series object detection algorithms

The YOLO series defines object detection as a single regression problem. This idea laid the foundation for real-time object detection19. Its development reflects a continuous pursuit of balance between speed and accuracy. YOLOv3 introduced multi-scale prediction to handle objects of different sizes. Later versions, such as YOLOv5, became widely used baselines by integrating advanced training strategies and network components20,21. In recent years, the evolution of this series has accelerated significantly. YOLOv8 refreshed performance standards with its anchor-free design21,22. The newer YOLOv10 achieves a paradigm-level breakthrough. Its innovative consistent dual assignment strategy removes the need for non-maximum suppression (NMS) during inference. As a result, it reaches a new state-of-the-art balance between latency and accuracy21,23. This NMS-free property simplifies the deployment process and improves efficiency. Given the advanced architecture and performance advantages of YOLOv10, this paper selects it as the base model for further optimization. The goal is to address the specific challenges of e-bike safety detection.

Applications of visual attention mechanisms

Attention mechanisms simulate the human visual system. They allow neural networks to focus dynamically on important information in input features. This improves model performance under limited computational resources24. In computer vision, channel attention mechanisms, such as Squeeze-and-Excitation Network (SENet), reweight feature responses by modeling inter-channel dependencies25. Spatial attention mechanisms focus on important spatial locations in feature maps. To combine the advantages of both, later studies proposed hybrid attention modules, such as Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM). These modules integrate channel and spatial attention in a sequential or parallel manner26. However, simple combinations may not enable optimal cooperation between the two types of attention. The SCSA module used in this paper is a more efficient integration solution27. It processes spatial and channel attention branches in parallel. It selectively aggregates their outputs. This allows the model to capture local features and contextual information that are critical for fine-grained recognition. In this study, SCSA is integrated into the model. The goal is to enhance the network’s ability to recognize small objects, such as license plates, and their internal textures, such as regional characters.

Recent advances in bounding box regression loss functions

The accuracy of bounding box regression directly affects the final performance of object detection. The traditional IoU loss suffers from gradient vanishing when bounding boxes do not overlap. To address this issue, improved methods such as GIoU, DIoU, and CIoU have been proposed. They optimize the convergence process by introducing geometric factors, such as penalty terms, center distance, and aspect ratio28,29. However, these methods still mainly rely on global geometric properties of bounding boxes. Their ability to precisely localize small objects and objects with complex poses remains limited. The MPDIoU loss function used in this study provides a new approach30. It computes geometric distances based on pixel point sets. This allows it to describe the shape and positional relationships of bounding boxes in more detail. It does not rely only on simple rectangular overlap. This point-set-based distance metric is more robust to object rotation and scale variation. It is especially suitable for precise localization of small objects, such as license plates, in this study. It helps the model learn more robust localization capability.

Current research on e-bike visual detection

At present, visual detection research on e-bikes has made progress in several sub-tasks. In helmet-wearing detection, Wei et al. proposed a real-time detection model based on a lightweight YOLOv5 architecture. By optimizing the network structure, the model achieves efficient inference on edge computing devices. However, its detection accuracy decreases under severe occlusion and low-light conditions12. For the license plate recognition task, Ganesamoorthi et al. adopted a two-stage approach. First, Faster R-CNN is used to locate license plates. Then, Fully Convolutional Network (FCN) and Convolutional Recurrent Neural Network (CRNN) networks are combined for character recognition. The method achieves an average accuracy of 90.5% at about 25 frames per second on an NVIDIA RTX 3080 GPU. However, it shows limited robustness to motion blur and large plate tilt angles31. Some studies have begun to explore multi-task learning. For example, Hegde et al. integrated helmet-wearing detection and illegal passenger carrying detection into a unified YOLOv8 framework. They verified the potential of multi-task learning in improving model efficiency32. However, most of these works are limited to a few common safety behavior detection tasks. They generally lack fine-grained recognition of license plate content, especially regional attribution. In summary, although single-task or limited multi-task detection methods have achieved some success, a high-performance end-to-end unified framework is still lacking. Such a framework should be able to perform both macroscopic safety behavior detection, such as helmet wearing and passenger carrying, and microscopic identity recognition, such as license plate region identification. This remains an important research gap in the field.

Yolov10 network structure

YOLO is a single-stage detector that treats object detection as dense prediction: spatial localization and category classification are produced in one forward pass33. Unlike two-stage methods, it omits region proposals and resampling, predicting class probabilities and bounding boxes directly on multi-scale feature maps. This yields lower end-to-end latency and higher throughput, making YOLO a common choice in real-time video analytics, in-vehicle perception, and edge deployment where the speed–accuracy trade-off is critical.

A typical YOLO architecture is organized into three main components: a backbone network, a feature-fusion neck, and a detection head. The backbone is responsible for extracting hierarchical features, while the neck aggregates information in both top-down and bottom-up manners to enhance small-object and multi-scale representation; the head performs parallel box regression and classification at each scale. Contemporary variants use decoupled branches to reduce task interference and stabilize training. The family has also shifted from anchor-based to anchor-free designs, combining one-to-many and one-to-one assignment, IoU-aware regression losses, and augmentations such as random scaling, MixUp, and Mosaic to address sample imbalance and redundancy. Inference typically applies NMS or related variants to remove duplicates.

Engineering practice emphasizes efficient end-to-end execution and portable deployment. Lightweight operators, low branching, and cache-friendly access reduce computation, while scalable width/depth settings target hardware from servers to edge devices. Challenges remain for tiny objects, occlusion, and classes with high visual similarity, motivating work on stronger fusion, more robust assignment, and better train–test consistency. Building on these trends, YOLOv10 refines architecture and learning strategies to cut post-processing and latency without sacrificing accuracy, thereby enabling effective application in dense real-time scenarios, such as detecting traffic participants (e.g., electric bicycles).

YOLOv10 aims to achieve real-time, end-to-end object detection by further reducing inference latency while maintaining high accuracy. It follows the standard input stage–backbone–feature fusion–detection head architecture and introduces refinements in consistency-based label assignment, lightweight network design, and a reduced reliance on traditional NMS. These changes result in a more balanced compromise between recognition performance and computational efficiency.

Since SCSA is inserted into both the Backbone and the Neck, it not only enhances single-layer feature representation but also improves the quality of multi-scale feature fusion. In the Neck, the SMSA branch emphasizes important spatial regions, while the PCSA branch strengthens task-relevant feature channels. These operations refine feature responses before cross-scale aggregation. Shallow features preserve object boundaries, textures, and spatial details, whereas deep features provide semantic context. After selective spatial and channel reweighting, the fused multi-scale features can better suppress background interference and highlight target responses at different scales. This mechanism is particularly useful for detecting license plates, helmets, and passenger regions in complex traffic scenes, where the targets are often small, distant, partially occluded, or affected by weak illumination.

Experiments and result analysis

Experiment preparation and setup

Dataset collection

This study constructs a multi-task dataset for e-bike riding safety detection and license plate region recognition. Data were collected in Guangzhou and Foshan, Guangdong Province. Both cities have a high density of e-bike usage. However, they differ in traffic patterns and management policies. This provides rich scene diversity for the dataset. It also helps improve the generalization ability of the model. High-quality camera equipment was used during data collection. The data cover multiple scenarios, such as main roads and residential roads. They also include different lighting periods. This ensures the quality and representativeness of the image data. Comparative experiment

This work focuses on the joint detection of electric bikes (Ebike), license plates (Plate), regions (fsplace), helmets (Helmet), and passenger carrying status (Manned) in urban traffic scenes. To comprehensively evaluate the proposed P-YOLOv10, we construct a custom dataset that covers both daytime and nighttime conditions, as well as diverse viewing angles and illuminations, and conduct comparative experiments on data from Guangzhou and FoShan. Under the same training and testing protocol, P-YOLOv10 is compared with a series of representative single-stage detectors (YOLOv3, YOLOv8, YOLOv9, YOLOv10, YOLOv11) and the Transformer-based Detection Transformer (DETR) model.

The YOLO family serves as baselines for real-time detection, as they are widely adopted in intelligent traffic systems due to their favorable speed-accuracy trade-off. DETR is included as a representative Transformer-based detector to provide a paradigm-level comparison: its global self-attention mechanism is theoretically better suited for capturing long-range dependencies and handling complex backgrounds without relying on handcrafted region proposals or NMS. By contrasting with DETR, we aim to highlight the practical advantages of our optimized convolutional architecture (P-YOLOv10) in terms of detection accuracy, recall, and real-time inference, especially under the challenging illumination and occlusion conditions of urban traffic scenarios.

According to the experimental results on the Guangzhou dataset (Table 5), the proposed P-YOLOv10 achieves consistent and significant improvements over the vanilla YOLOv10 baseline with identical computational complexity. The performance gains can be attributed to the designed enhancements: the SCSA attention module strengthens the extraction of spatially and channel-wise informative features, which effectively suppresses background clutter and enhances the representation of small targets such as helmets and license plates. The improved multi-scale feature fusion further bridges semantic gaps between different levels, enabling more robust detection under varying illumination and occlusion. Meanwhile, the optimized MPDIoU loss refines bounding box regression, leading to more accurate localization especially for occluded and small objects. These mechanisms collectively explain the notable improvements in detection accuracy and recall, and validate that the proposed designs effectively address the limitations of vanilla YOLOv10 in complex traffic scenarios without introducing extra computation.

As shown in Table 6, the ablation results clarify both the overall effectiveness of P-YOLOv10 and the interaction among its components. Compared with the YOLOv10n baseline A0, the complete model A1 improves recall from 92.5% to 93.8% and increases mAP@0.5:0.95 from 78.3% to 79.6%, while maintaining the same mAP@0.5 of 95.5%. In contrast, the isolated configurations A2–A4 do not outperform the baseline, indicating that replacing only the regression loss, attention module, or activation function cannot guarantee stable improvement. The clear gap between A1 and the single-component settings suggests that MPDIoU, SCSA, and GELU function cooperatively rather than independently. Specifically, SCSA strengthens task-relevant feature representation, GELU supports smoother nonlinear feature propagation, and MPDIoU refines bounding-box regression. Their joint configuration achieves a better balance between representation learning and localization accuracy. Moreover, A0 and A1 share the same computational cost of 8.2 GFLOPs and the same parameter count of 2.71M, showing that the performance gains are obtained without increasing model size or theoretical computation. Therefore, the ablation study confirms that the effectiveness of P-YOLOv10 mainly comes from the coordinated integration of its components rather than from any single isolated modification.

In summary, across two cities and five categories under daytime and nighttime conditions, P-YOLOv10 consistently outperforms the original YOLOv10 baseline and achieves accuracy comparable to stronger but more complex detectors, while maintaining a lightweight computational profile. Compared with DETR, P-YOLOv10 offers substantial gains in both detection accuracy and recall, and compared with YOLOv3 it dramatically reduces computational cost by over an order of magnitude. These results demonstrate that the proposed P-YOLOv10 provides a better trade-off between accuracy, robustness, and efficiency, making it a more suitable choice for real-world applications involving electric bike, helmet, license plate, region, and passenger detection in intelligent traffic monitoring systems.

These experimental results jointly validate the effectiveness of the proposed P-YOLOv10 framework in complex real-world traffic scenarios. Quantitatively, the training comparison in Table 4 shows that P-YOLOv10 achieves higher mAP@0.5, precision, and recall than YOLOv10 after sufficient training. The cross-city comparisons in Table 5 and Table 7 further demonstrate that the proposed framework maintains stable performance on both Guangzhou and Foshan datasets, with clear improvements in fine-grained and safety-critical categories such as license plates, regional attributes, and helmets. In addition, the ablation results in Table 6 show that the performance gains are achieved without increasing GFLOPs or parameters, indicating that the proposed improvements are lightweight and suitable for practical deployment. Qualitatively, Figs. 7 and 8 show that P-YOLOv10 can jointly detect e-bikes, license plates, helmet status, and passenger carrying under daytime, nighttime, low-light, crowded, and partially occluded scenes. Therefore, both quantitative and visual results confirm the robustness and practical applicability of P-YOLOv10 in complex urban traffic environments.

Conclusions

In this paper, we presented P-YOLOv10, an enhanced YOLOv10-based detector tailored for the joint detection of electric bikes, license plates, regions, helmets, and passenger-carrying status in complex urban traffic scenes. Building upon the original YOLOv10 architecture, P-YOLOv10 introduces three key modifications: a MPDIoU loss to strengthen bounding-box regression and better align predicted boxes with ground truth, a GELU activation function to improve non-linear feature representation and gradient flow, and an SCSA attention module to adaptively emphasize informative spatial–channel features. These components work in a complementary manner, allowing the model to focus more accurately on small, partially occluded targets and objects under challenging illumination, such as day–night transitions and varying light incidence angles.

Extensive experiments on datasets collected from different cities demonstrate that, under the same computational budget as YOLOv10, P-YOLOv10 consistently delivers higher detection accuracy and more stable performance across all five categories. Compared with other representative detectors, including classical YOLO variants and a Transformer-based baseline, the proposed model achieves a more favorable balance between robustness and efficiency, particularly in scenarios with dense traffic, cluttered backgrounds, and heterogeneous lighting conditions. These results indicate that P-YOLOv10 is well suited for deployment in real-world intelligent transportation systems, where reliable detection of electric bikes, helmets, license plates, regions, and passenger-carrying behavior is essential for traffic supervision and safety management. Future work will further explore model compression and cross-domain adaptation to extend the applicability of P-YOLOv10 to a wider range of edge devices and deployment environments.
