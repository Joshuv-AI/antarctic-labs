The Hitchin morphism for K K -trivial varieties
Aryaman Patel and Dario Weissmann

Abstract
. We study the Hitchin morphism for higher dimensional varieties and show that, for a certain class of varieties which we call r r -small , the set-theoretic image of the Hitchin morphism from the Dolbeault moduli space coincides with the spectral base. In other words, a stronger version of the conjecture of Chen and Ngô holds for this class of varieties, which includes K K -trivial varieties. As part of the proof, we slightly modify the construction of spectral covers to obtain normal spectral covers.



1.
Introduction

We work over an algebraically closed field
\mathop{{k}}\nolimits
of characteristic zero.
For a smooth proper scheme
X
over
\mathop{{k}}\nolimits
,
the Hitchin morphism is a map from the moduli stack
{M}^{r}_{X}

of Higgs bundles of rank
r
to the
Hitchin base

{A}^{r}_{X}:=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}H^{0}(X,\mathrm{Sym}^{i}\Omega^{1}_{X})
,
which sends a Higgs bundle
(\mathcal{E},\theta)
to the characteristic
polynomial of the Higgs field
\theta
.
We denote it by
h^{r}_{X}:{M}^{r}_{X}\to{A}^{r}_{X}
.

When
X
is a curve, the Hitchin morphism is known to be surjective. Indeed, in this case
\Omega^{1}_{X}
is a line bundle, so the integrability condition
\theta\wedge\theta=0
is automatically satisfied by any vector bundle with a
\Omega^{1}_{X}
-twisted endomorphism. However, for higher dimensional
X
, the Hitchin morphism is in general far from being surjective. A natural question then is:
can we describe the image of
h^{r}_{X}
when
\mathrm{dim}X>1
?

Recent progress has been made in this direction by Chen and Ngô in
[
2
]
, where they show that
h^{r}_{X}
factors through a closed subscheme
{B}^{r}_{X}
of
{A}^{r}_{X}
, called the
spectral base
, which is in general a non-linear subspace of smaller dimension. The morphism
{M}^{r}_{X}\to{B}^{r}_{X}
is called the
spectral data morphism
and is conjectured to be surjective.

Conjecture 1.1

(
[
2
, Conjecture 5.2]
)
.

For every point
s\in{B}^{r}_{X}
, the fiber
(h^{r}_{X})^{-1}(s)
is non-empty.

Although the conjecture has been formulated more generally for
G
-Higgs bundles where
G
is a reductive group, we only treat the case
G=GL_{r}
in this article. Some recent progress has been made towards proving Conjecture
1.1
. For example, Chen and Ngô proved it for ruled and elliptic surfaces in
[
2
]
, and Song-Sun proved it more generally for all surfaces in
[
11
]
.
The case of hyperelliptic varieties was treated by the authors
in
[
9
]
.
In
[
6
]
He and Liu proved a stronger version of the conjecture for rank
2
Higgs bundles,
namely that the Hitchin morphism from the moduli stack of polystable rank
2
Higgs bundles surjects onto the spectral base
{B}^{2}_{X}
.
In
[
7
]
He, Liu, and Mok show that
{B}^{r}_{X}=\{0\}
for all
r
when
X
is a smooth projective quotient of a bounded symmetric domain. In particular, Conjecture
1.1
holds for such varieties.

The motivating question for the present article is:
for which projective varieties
X
is the Hitchin morphism
from the moduli space
M^{P,G-ss}_{X}
of Gieseker semistable
Higgs bundles with fixed Hilbert polynomial
P

surjective onto the spectral base?
A natural choice for
P
is the Hilbert polynomial
P_{0}

of the trivial bundle of rank
r
,
in which case
M^{P_{0},G-ss}_{X}=:M^{r}_{X,\mathrm{Dol}}

is the
Dolbeault moduli space
.
The following result partially answers this question.

Theorem 1.2

(
Theorem

4.11
)
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety whose canonical divisor

K_{X}
is numerically trivial.
Then for all
r\in\mathbb{N}
the restricted Hitchin morphism

h^{r}_{X,\mathrm{Dol}}:M^{r}_{X,\mathrm{Dol}}\to{B}^{r}_{X}

is surjective.

The surjectivity of
h^{r}_{X,\mathrm{Dol}}
actually holds
for a larger class of varieties that satisfy a geometric condition
on the vanishing loci of symmetric differentials.
Theorem
1.2
is thus a special case
of the following more general statement.

Theorem 1.3

(
Proposition

4.8
)
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective scheme and
let
r\in\mathbb{N}
be fixed.
Suppose that
\mathrm{Sym}^{k(k-1)}\Omega^{1}_{X}

does not have a non-zero global section whose vanishing
locus has codimension
1
in
X
, for all
1\leq k\leq r
.
Then the restricted Hitchin morphism

h^{k}_{X,\mathrm{Dol}}:M^{k}_{X,\mathrm{Dol}}\to{B}^{k}_{X}

is surjective for all
1\leq k\leq r
.

In fact, a stronger statement holds – every fiber of

h^{k}_{X,\mathrm{Dol}}
contains a Higgs bundle
whose underlying vector bundle is semistable and
has vanishing Chern classes
(see Proposition
4.8
and Remark
4.9
).
The proof relies essentially on a modification
of the spectral cover construction and purity of branch locus,
which is recalled in the next section.
Although many varieties satisfy the hypothesis of Theorem
1.3
,
the condition that all non-zero global sections of

\mathrm{Sym}^{r(r-1)}\Omega^{1}_{X}
have vanishing locus
of codimension
\geq 2
in
X
is hard to check.
This motivates the notion of
r
-small varieties
.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety and
let
r\in\mathbb{N}
be fixed.
Then
X
is said to be
r
-small

if
\Omega^{1}_{X}
is slope-semistable and

\mathrm{Sym}^{k(k-1)}\Omega^{1}_{X}
satisfies
the slope inequality
\mu(\mathrm{Sym}^{k(k-1)}\Omega^{1}_{X})<1

for all
1\leq k\leq r
.

Remark 1.5
.

Note that in characteristic
0
, a vector bundle
\mathcal{V}
being
semistable implies that the bundle
\mathrm{Sym}^{j}\mathcal{V}

is semistable for all
j\geq 0
.

A standard argument shows that any
r
-small variety
satisfies the hypothesis of Theorem
1.3
.
It follows that the Hitchin morphism

h^{r}_{X,\mathrm{Dol}}:M^{r}_{X,\mathrm{Dol}}\to{B}^{r}_{X}

is surjective for any
r
-small variety
X
.
Since
\Omega^{1}_{X}
is known to be slope-semistable of slope
0

for any
projective variety
X
with numerically trivial canonical divisor
K_{X}
(e.g. by
[
4
, Theorem A]
),
it is immediate that any such variety is
r
-small.

In general, the restricted Hitchin morphism
h^{r}_{X,\mathrm{Dol}}

from the Dolbeault moduli space is not surjective
(see
[
6
, Example 3.4 (5)]
).
It would then be interesting to determine
necessary and sufficient conditions
that
X
must satisfy so that
h^{r}_{X,\mathrm{Dol}}
is surjective.
We believe that this question can be answered
once we have more information on the
geometric and topological properties of the spectral base.

In
[
11
]
, Song-Sun prove that the spectral base is invariant under smooth birational modifications. We use this along with the standard argument of resolving the singularities
of a torsion-free sheaf to reformulate

1.1
as follows.

Conjecture 1.6

(equivalent to Conjecture
1.1
)
.

The set-theoretic image
\mathrm{im}(h^{r}_{X})
of the Hitchin morphism is invariant under smooth birational modifications.

Notation

We work over an algebraically closed base field
\mathop{{k}}\nolimits
of characteristic
0
.
By
variety
we mean an integral separated
\mathop{{k}}\nolimits
-scheme of finite type.
Given an affine group scheme
G
over
\mathop{{k}}\nolimits
acting on an affine scheme

A
, we denote the quotient stack by
[A/G]
which over a scheme
S

parametrizes
G
-torsors over
S

together with a
G
-invariant morphism to
A
.
We denote by
\mathrm{B}G
the quotient stack
[\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}\nolimits(\mathop{{k}}\nolimits)/G]
,
where we equip the point with the trivial action.

For a projective variety
X
we implicitly fix a polarization

\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)
and define the notion
of Gieseker (semi)stability, slope-(semi)stability, Hilbert polynomial,
and degree with respect to
\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)
.

2.
Spectral covers

In this section, we recall the definitions of the Hitchin base,
the spectral base,
and the construction of spectral covers from
[
2
, Section 6]
.
We also define
a stratification of the spectral base in terms of multiplication
of characteristic polynomials, similar to the stratification
on the Chow scheme defined in
[
11
]
,
and analogous to the stratification of the Hitchin base defined in
[
9
]
.

The spectral covers arising from
the open locus of irreducible spectral data are close to being integral,
see
Lemma

2.4
for the precise formulation.
Further, we recall the discriminant and show that
we can factor every spectral datum into a product of irreducible
spectral data with non-zero discriminant, see
Lemma

2.5
.

2.1.
The spectral base

In this subsection, we recall the definitions of the Hitchin base,
the spectral base, and the construction of spectral covers from

[
2
, Section 6]
.
We refer the reader to loc. cit. for a complete and rigorous treatment.

Let
X
be a smooth variety of dimension
d
,
and let
r\in\mathbb{N}
be fixed.
Define
A^{r}
as the affine space

\mathbb{A}^{d}\times\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(\mathbb{A}^{d})\times\dots\times\mathrm{Sym}^{r}(\mathbb{A}^{d})

and
B^{r}
as the Chow scheme
\mathrm{Chow}^{r}(\mathbb{A}^{d}):=(\mathbb{A}^{d})^{r}\sslash S_{r}
,
which classifies
0
-dimensional cycles of length
r
in
\mathbb{A}^{d}
.
Here,
S_{r}
denotes the symmetric group in
r
elements
and acts via permutation.

A closed point
\omega\in B^{r}

can be represented as an unordered tuple of
r
closed points

\omega=[\omega_{1},...,\omega_{r}]
,
where
\omega_{i}\in\mathbb{A}^{d}
for
1\leq i\leq r
.
We denote the
i
-th elementary symmetric polynomial
in
\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{r}

by
\sigma_{i}(\omega)=\sigma_{i}(\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{r})\in\mathrm{Sym}^{i}(\mathbb{A}^{d})
.
For example,
\sigma_{1}(\omega)=\omega_{1}+\dots+\omega_{r}\in\mathbb{A}^{d}
,

\sigma_{2}(\omega)=\sum_{i<j}\omega_{i}\cdot\omega_{j}\in\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(\mathbb{A}^{d})
,
and
\sigma_{r}(\omega)=\omega_{1}\cdot\omega_{2}\cdots\omega_{r}\in\mathrm{Sym}^{r}(\mathbb{A}^{d})
.
By
[
2
, Theorem 4.1]
the morphism

(1)

\displaystyle B^{r}

\displaystyle\to A^{r}

\displaystyle[\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{r}]

\displaystyle\mapsto(\sigma_{1}(\omega),\dots,\sigma_{r}(\omega))

is a closed immersion.

The natural
\mathop{\mathrm{Gl}}\nolimits_{d}
-action on
\mathbb{A}^{d}

induces compatible
\mathop{\mathrm{Gl}}\nolimits_{d}
-actions on
A^{r}
and
B^{r}
.
Consider the quotient stacks
{A}^{r}:=[A^{r}/\mathop{\mathrm{Gl}}\nolimits_{d}]

and
{B}^{r}:=[B/\mathop{\mathrm{Gl}}\nolimits_{d}]
.
Then we define the Hitchin base

{A}^{r}_{X}:={H}om(X,{A}^{r})\times_{{H}om(X,\mathrm{B}\mathop{\mathrm{Gl}}\nolimits_{d})}\mathrm{Spec}(\mathop{{k}}\nolimits)

and the spectral base

{B}^{r}_{X}:={H}om(X,{B}^{r})\times_{{H}om(X,\mathrm{B}\mathop{\mathrm{Gl}}\nolimits_{d})}\mathrm{Spec}(\mathop{{k}}\nolimits)
,
where the cotangent bundle
\Omega^{1}_{X}
corresponds to the morphism
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathop{{k}}\nolimits)\to{H}om(X,\mathrm{B}\mathop{\mathrm{Gl}}\nolimits_{d})
.

We identify the Hitchin base with the affine space

\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}H^{0}(X,\mathrm{Sym}^{i}\Omega^{1}_{X})
and the spectral base with the space of sections

X\to\mathrm{Chow}^{r}(\Omega^{1}_{X}/X)
of the natural map

\mathrm{Chow}^{r}(\Omega^{1}_{X}/X)\to X
, where

\mathrm{Chow}^{r}(\Omega^{1}_{X}/X):=(\Omega^{1}_{X}\times_{X}\dots\times_{X}\Omega^{1}_{X})\sslash S_{r}

is the relative Chow scheme that classifies

0
-dimensional cycles of length
r

in the fibers of the natural morphism
\Omega^{1}_{X}\to X
.
From the closed immersion (
1
),
we obtain a closed immersion

{B}^{r}_{X}\hookrightarrow{A}^{r}_{X}
.

Next, we recall the construction of the spectral cover
corresponding to a point
s\in{B}^{r}_{X}
in the
\mathop{\mathrm{Gl}}\nolimits_{r}
case,
as outlined in
[
2
, Section 6]
.
Consider the Cayley morphism

\displaystyle\chi:B^{r}\times\mathbb{A}^{d}\to\mathrm{Sym}^{r}(\mathbb{A}^{d})

given by

\chi([\omega_{1},...,\omega_{r}],y)=(y-\omega_{1})\cdots(y-\omega_{r})=y^{r}-\sigma_{1}(\omega)y^{r-1}+\sigma_{2}(\omega)y^{r-2}+\dots+(-1)^{r}\sigma_{r}(\omega).

The Cayley scheme is then defined as the fiber over

0\in\mathrm{Sym}^{r}(\mathbb{A}^{d})
of the Cayley morphism, and is denoted by

B^{r,\bullet}:=\mathrm{Cayley}^{r}(\mathbb{A}^{d}):=\chi^{-1}(0)\subset B^{r}\times\mathbb{A}^{d}.

There is a natural projection map

\mathop{\mathrm{pr}}\nolimits:B^{r,\bullet}\to B^{r}

called the
universal spectral cover
of

B^{r}
.

By
[
2
, Proposition 6.1, (1) and (2)]
the morphism
\mathop{\mathrm{pr}}\nolimits
is finite, surjective, and étale exactly
over the open subset
B^{r,\circ}
of
B^{r}

consisting of those elements
[\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{r}]

without multiplicity, i.e.,
\omega_{i}\neq\omega_{j}
for
i\neq j
.
Since
\mathop{\mathrm{pr}}\nolimits
is
\mathop{\mathrm{Gl}}\nolimits_{d}
-equivariant, we obtain a morphism
[B^{r,\bullet}/\mathop{\mathrm{Gl}}\nolimits_{d}]\to[B^{r}/\mathop{\mathrm{Gl}}\nolimits_{d}]
, which is étale exactly
over the open substack
[B^{r,\circ}/\mathop{\mathrm{Gl}}\nolimits_{d}]\subset[B^{r}/\mathop{\mathrm{Gl}}\nolimits_{d}]
.

Recall that for each
\mathop{{k}}\nolimits
-point
s\in{B}^{r}_{X}
,
we have a morphism
s:X\to[B^{r}/\mathop{\mathrm{Gl}}\nolimits_{d}]
.
The
spectral cover

X_{s}
corresponding to
s

is defined as the following fiber product

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{s}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[B^{r,\bullet}/\mathop{\mathrm{Gl}}\nolimits_{d}]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[B^{r}/\mathop{\mathrm{Gl}}\nolimits_{d}]}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{s}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s}

The fiber of
\pi_{s}
over any point

x\in X
is the set of roots of the polynomial

P(s):=y^{r}-s_{1}y^{r-1}+s_{2}y^{r-2}+\dots+(-1)^{r}s_{r}
at
x
.
The spectral cover factors as
\pi_{s}:X_{s}\to\Omega^{1}_{X}\to X
, where

\Omega^{1}_{X}\to X
is the natural projection and
we view
\Omega^{1}_{X}
as the scheme corresponding
to the
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-algebra

\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Sym}^{i}(\Omega^{1}_{X})^{\lor}
.

Note that
X_{s}\to X
is finite
since
[B^{r,\bullet}/\mathop{\mathrm{Gl}}\nolimits_{d}]\to[B^{r}/\mathop{\mathrm{Gl}}\nolimits_{d}]
is finite.
The coherent sheaf
\pi_{s*}\mathcal{O}_{X_{s}}
on
X

is naturally equipped with a
Higgs field whose characteristic polynomial is
P(s)
.

However,
\pi_{s}
is not necessarily flat because

X_{s}
is not necessarily Cohen-Macaulay, so
\pi_{s*}\mathcal{O}_{X_{s}}
may not be locally free.
More precisely, the morphism
\pi_{s}
is étale over the open subset
X^{\circ}
of
X

that is mapped into
[B^{r,\circ}/\mathop{\mathrm{Gl}}\nolimits_{d}]
by
s
.
It is unclear whether
\pi_{s}
is flat over the locus
X\setminus X^{\circ}
.

Although the spectral base can be described quite explicitly
as a closed subscheme of the Hitchin base,
little is known about it’s geometry and topology.
For example, it is not known whether
{B}^{r}_{X}
is irreducible.

We know that
{B}^{r}_{X}
admits a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action,
given by scaling. As a consequence, we observe that it is connected.

Lemma 2.1
.

The spectral base is connected.

Proof.

Choose any point
s\in{B}^{r}_{X}(\mathop{{k}}\nolimits)
.
We view the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-orbit of
s
as a morphism

s:\mathbb{G}_{m}\to{B}^{r}_{X}
,
t\mapsto t\cdot s
.
Since
{B}^{r}_{X}\subset{A}^{r}_{X}
is closed,
the limit of this orbit as
t\to 0
exists,
and is precisely the origin
0\in{B}^{r}_{X}
.

Thus, the closure of every
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-orbit contains
0
,
and we conclude that
{B}^{r}_{X}
is connected.
∎

2.2.
Irreducible spectral data

In this subsection we define a stratification of the spectral base
and recall the discriminant. We also make some preliminary observations on irreducible spectral data.

Each
\mathop{{k}}\nolimits
-point
s\in{B}^{r}_{X}
is an
r
-tuple
(s_{1},\dots,s_{r})
,
with
s_{i}\in H^{0}(X,\mathrm{Sym}^{i}\Omega^{1}_{X})
,
which we may view as a homogeneous polynomial
P(s)

of degree
r
in a formal variable
y

\displaystyle P(s):=y^{r}-s_{1}y^{r-1}+\dots+(-1)^{r}s_{r}.

We can define morphisms of Chow schemes

\displaystyle B^{p}\times B^{q}

\displaystyle\to B^{r}

\displaystyle([\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{p}],[\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{q}])

\displaystyle\mapsto[\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{p},\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{q}]

for all
p,q\in\mathbb{N}
such that
p+q=r
.
Note that this morphism is
\mathop{\mathrm{Gl}}\nolimits_{d}
-invariant.
This yields a morphism

\displaystyle m_{p,q}:{B}^{p}_{X}\times{B}^{q}_{X}\to{B}^{r}_{X},

which sends an
S
-point on the left-hand side, corresponding
to
\mathop{\mathrm{Gl}}\nolimits_{d}
-invariant morphisms
f:\Omega_{X}^{1}\times S\to B^{p}

and
g:\Omega^{1}_{X}\times S\to B^{q}
, where we view
\Omega^{1}_{X}

as a
\mathop{\mathrm{Gl}}\nolimits_{d}
-torsor over
X
, to

f\times g:\Omega_{X}^{1}\times S\to B^{p}\times B^{q}
.
On the level of
\mathop{{k}}\nolimits
-points,
m_{p,q}
is
given by multiplication of characteristic polynomials. Note that we can analogously define a multiplication map
{A}^{p}_{X}\times{A}^{q}_{X}\to{A}^{r}_{X}

on the Hitchin base,
which we use in the proof of Lemma
2.2
.

We define
(p,q)\subset{B}^{r}_{X}

as the subset consisting of those
s
such that
P(s)

decomposes as a product a degree
p
polynomial in
{B}^{p}_{X}

and a degree
q
polynomial in
{B}^{q}_{X}
.
We define
(r)\subset{B}^{r}_{X}
as the subset consisting
of irreducible polynomials, i.e., those that do not decompose.
Thus, the spectral base can be written as the union

(2)

\displaystyle{B}^{r}_{X}=\bigcup_{p+q=r;\;p\leq q}(p,q)\cup(r).

It is straightforward to check that the set-theoretic
image (i.e. the image of the
\mathop{{k}}\nolimits
-points) of
m_{(p,q)}
is precisely the subset
(p,q)
.
Using the same method as the proof of
[
9
, Lemma 5.6]
,
we show that the multiplication maps are proper.

Lemma 2.2
.

The morphisms
m_{(p,q)}
are proper for all

p,q\in\mathbb{N}
such that
p+q=r
.

Proof.

Any
\mathop{{k}}\nolimits
-point
s=(s_{1},\dots,s_{p})
of the Hitchin base

{A}^{p}_{X}
can also be expressed as a homogeneous polynomial

y^{p}-s_{1}y^{p-1}+\dots+(-1)^{p}s_{p}
and we can define multiplication maps

\oplus_{(p,q)}:{A}^{p}_{X}\times{A}^{q}_{X}\to{A}^{r}_{X}
.
Since
{B}^{p}_{X}
is a closed subscheme of
{A}^{p}_{X}
,
we have closed immersions

\iota_{(p,q)}:{B}^{p}_{X}\times{B}^{q}_{X}\to{A}^{p}_{X}\times{A}^{q}_{X}
,
and it is easy to check that
m_{(p,q)}=\oplus_{(p,q)}\circ\iota_{(p,q)}
.

We show that the map
\oplus_{(p,q)}
is proper,
from which it immediately follows that
m_{(p,q)}
is proper.

Let
{P}^{r}_{X}
denote the projective completion of
{A}^{r}_{X}

formed by adding an additional variable in degree zero.
More precisely,
{P}^{r}_{X}:=(\bigoplus_{i=0}^{r}H^{0}(X,\mathrm{Sym}^{i}\Omega^{1}_{X})\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\})\sslash\mathbb{G}_{m}
,
where
H^{0}(X,\mathrm{Sym}^{0}\Omega^{1}_{X})=H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
,

\mathbf{0}:=(0,\dots,0)\in\bigoplus_{i=0}^{r}H^{0}(X,\mathrm{Sym}^{i}\Omega^{1}_{X})

is the origin, and the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action is given by scaling, i.e.,

\lambda\cdot(s_{0},\dots,s_{r})=(\lambda s_{0},\dots,\lambda s_{r})
,

\lambda\in\mathbb{G}_{m}
.
Thus, a point
s\in{P}_{X}^{r}
can be represented by

[s_{0}:\dots:s_{r}]
in homogeneous coordinates
and
{A}_{X}^{r}
can be identified with the chart
\{s_{0}\neq 0\}
.

We define a map

\overline{\oplus}_{(p,q)}:{P}^{p}_{X}\times{P}^{q}_{X}\to{P}^{r}_{X}

extending
\oplus_{(p,q)}
, given by

([s_{0}:s_{1}:\dots:s_{p}],[t_{0}:t_{1}:\dots:t_{q}])\mapsto[v_{0}:v_{1}:\dots:v_{r}],

where each
v_{i}
is
(-1)^{i}
times the degree
r-i
coefficient of the polynomial

(s_{0}y^{p}-s_{1}y^{p-1}+\dots+(-1)^{p}s_{p})\cdot(t_{0}y^{q}-t_{1}y^{q-1}+\dots+(-1)^{q}t_{q}),

for
0\leq i\leq p+q=r
.

Note that
\overline{\oplus}_{(p,q)}
is indeed well defined,
because the product of two such polynomials is identically zero
if and only if one of them is identically zero.
We recover
\oplus_{(p,q)}
by restricting
\overline{\oplus}_{(p,q)}

to the chart
\{s_{0}\neq 0\}\times\{t_{0}\neq 0\}
.

We have the following Cartesian diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{A}^{p}_{X}\times{A}^{q}_{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{P}^{p}_{X}\times{P}^{q}_{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{A}^{r}_{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{P}^{r}_{X}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\oplus}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{\oplus}}

from which it follows that
\oplus_{(p,q)}:{A}^{p}_{X}\times{A}^{q}_{X}\to{A}^{r}_{X}
is proper.
∎

In particular, the subsets
(p,q)
are closed,
while
(r)
is open in
{B}^{r}_{X}
.

Definition 2.3

(Irreducible spectral datum)
.

We call a
\mathop{{k}}\nolimits
-point of
(r)
an

irreducible spectral datum
.

Let
0\neq s\in{B}^{r}_{X}
be a
\mathop{{k}}\nolimits
-point.
Recall that the roots of the associated characteristic polynomial

P(s)
at any point
x\in X
are precisely the elements

\{\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{r}\}
of the
fiber
\pi_{s}^{-1}(x)\subset X_{s}
of the spectral cover

\pi_{s}:X_{s}\to X
, counted with multiplicity.

We observe that the irreducibility of
P(s)
is closely related to the irreducibility of the spectral cover
X_{s}
.
The following argument is closely related to and
inspired by
[
7
, Section 2]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
s\in{B}^{r}_{X}
be a closed point.
If
s\in(r)

has exactly one irreducible
component dominating

is in addition generically integral.
Furthermore, the morphism

Proof.

As we are in characteristic

Consider the irreducible components

,
and note that

is a dense open subset of
X
.

Let

be a decomposition
into irreducible components,

Consider the coherent Higgs sheaf

Restricted to

\mathcal{F}_{U,k}

is the direct image of
\mathcal{O}_{U_{s,k}}
along

.
We obtain a factorization of

over

,
where
P_{U,k}
denotes the characteristic polynomial
of
\mathcal{F}_{U,k}
.

Let
X_{s,k}
denote the scheme-theoretic closure of

in
X_{s}
, and consider the natural morphisms
\pi_{s,k}:X_{s,k}\to X
.
Via pushforward we obtain coherent Higgs sheaves

\mathcal{F}_{k}:=\pi_{s,k*}\mathcal{O}_{X_{s,k}}
on
X
which
extend the Higgs sheaves
\mathcal{F}_{U,k}
, that is,

\mathcal{F}_{k}|_{U}=\mathcal{F}_{U,k}
for
1\leq k\leq n
.
Let
t_{k}
be the image via the Hitchin morphism of

\mathcal{F}_{k}
and note that

,

\mathcal{F}_{k}

\Omega^{1}_{X}
.
Associating to each point

Consider the characteristic polynomial

Note that we have

It follows that

There is a commutative diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}_{X_{s}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}_{X_{s}}\otimes\pi_{s}^{*}\Omega^{1}_{X_{s}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{*}\iota^{*}\mathcal{O}_{X_{s}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{*}\iota^{*}(\mathcal{O}_{X_{s}}\otimes\pi_{s}^{*}\Omega^{1}_{X_{s}})}

, where

\pi_{s}^{*}\Omega^{1}_{X}
.
Pushing forward by
\pi_{s}
,

For any
\mathop{{k}}\nolimits
-point
\omega=[\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{r}]\in B^{r}
, we can define its

as the expression
D(\omega):=\prod_{i<j}(\omega_{i}-\omega_{j})^{2}\in\mathrm{Sym}^{r(r-1)}\mathbb{A}^{d}
. Note that this is the same as the discriminant of the polynomial
\prod_{i=1}^{r}(y-\omega_{i})
, where

is a formal variable, hence the name. By
[
2

, the morphism

is étale precisely over the open subset

The assignment

defines a
\mathop{\mathrm{Gl}}\nolimits_{d}
-invariant morphism

{B}^{r}=[B^{r}/\mathop{\mathrm{Gl}}\nolimits_{d}]\to[\mathrm{Sym}^{r(r-1)}{\mathbb{A}^{d}}/\mathop{\mathrm{Gl}}\nolimits_{d}]=:{D}^{r}.

This further induces a morphism of Hom-stacks

D:{H}om(X,{B}^{r})\times_{{H}om(X,\mathrm{B}\mathop{\mathrm{Gl}}\nolimits_{d})}\mathrm{Spec}(\mathop{{k}}\nolimits)\to{H}om(X,{D}^{r})\times_{{H}om(X,\mathrm{B}\mathop{\mathrm{Gl}}\nolimits_{d})}\mathrm{Spec}(\mathop{{k}}\nolimits),

where the morphism
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathop{{k}}\nolimits)\to{H}om(X,\mathrm{B}\mathop{\mathrm{Gl}}\nolimits_{d})

\Omega^{1}_{X}
. The target can be identified with the affine space

, so we have a morphism

that is given on
\mathop{{k}}\nolimits
-points by sending

to the discriminant

of the polynomial
P(s)
. We call

the

.

Note that

is a homogeneous expression in the

. For example, when

. By definition,

vanishes at a point

if and only if

has roots with multiplicity
>1
at

i.e., if and only if

for some

We observe that for any nonzero

,
the polynomial

can be decomposed
as a product of irreducible polynomials,
such that each irreducible factor has nonzero discriminant.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
0\neq s\in{B}^{r}_{X}
be a
\mathop{{k}}\nolimits
-point.
Then
s
can be written as a product
s=\prod_{k=1}^{n}t_{k}^{m_{k}}

of irreducible factors

t_{k}\in{B}^{l_{k}}_{X}(\mathop{{k}}\nolimits)
,
m_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
,
each of which has nonzero discriminant.

Proof.

Let
s=\prod_{k=1}^{n}t_{k}^{m_{k}}

be a decomposition of
s
into irreducible factors.
Then
t_{k}\in(l_{k})\subset{B}^{l_{k}}_{X}

for some
l_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
and
m_{k}\in\mathbb{N}

denotes the multiplicity of
t_{k}
in
s
for
1\leq k\leq n
.

We know from Lemma
2.4

that the spectral cover
\pi_{t_{k}}:X_{t_{k}}\to X

corresponding to

is generically étale of degree

.
Thus, there is a dense open subset

over which
\pi_{t_{k}}
is étale.

Recall that the fibers of
\pi_{t_{k}}
consist of roots of

,
which are all pairwise distinct over

.
This means that

is nonzero over

and is thus a nonzero section of

\mathrm{Sym}^{l_{k}(l_{k}-1)}\Omega^{1}_{X}
for

.
∎

3.
Normalized spectral covers

In this section we modify the construction of
a spectral cover of an irreducible spectral datum
to obtain a normal spectral cover.
We also observe that the Hitchin morphism
from the stack of reflexive Higgs sheaves
factors via the spectral base and that this morphism is onto.

Let
X
be a smooth proper variety
and let
r\in\mathbb{N}
be fixed.
Let
s\in{B}^{r}_{X}
be a closed point.
In view of Remark
2.6
, we assume that
s\in(r)
.
Henceforth, we slightly abuse notation
by using
s
and

interchangeably.

Let
\widetilde{X}_{s}
denote the reduced subscheme underlying the unique irreducible component of

that is mapped onto

\pi_{s}

\widetilde{X}_{s}
is isomorphic to
X_{s}
over the dense open subset

where
\pi_{s}

by Lemma
2.4
.

Recall from the last paragraph of Section
2
that, in general,
\widetilde{X}_{s}
may not be normal or Cohen-Macaulay.
Let
\nu:X^{\prime}_{s}\to\widetilde{X}_{s}

We call

the

corresponding to

.

We have the following commutative triangle

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{\prime}_{s}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{X}_{s}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{\prime}_{s}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\nu}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{s}}

denote the loci where the morphisms
\pi_{s}

and

are branched, respectively, and note that

It follows from the construction of the spectral cover

is ramified precisely over those points

.

With notation as above, we have

.

Let

denote the loci in

Since

is empty.

We want to determine sufficient conditions such that,
for a general
\mathop{{k}}\nolimits
-point

3.1.

Let

The natural morphism

Y^{\prime}

to find a birational morphism
\pi:Y\to X

with smooth proper
Y
such that
the strict transform
\pi^{[\ast]}\mathcal{F}

of
\pi^{\ast}\mathcal{F}
is locally free.

We also can pullback the Higgs field

\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{F}\otimes\Omega^{1}_{X}
to
Y
.
Taking strict transform
and composing with the natural morphism

\pi^{\ast}\Omega^{1}_{X}\to\Omega^{1}_{Y}
yields
a Higgs field on the strict transform

\pi^{[\ast]}\mathcal{F}
on
Y
.

Via restricting to the open locus where
\pi
is an
isomorphism we conclude that the characteristic
polynomial of
\pi^{[\ast]}\mathcal{F}

coincides with
\pi^{\ast}s
.
∎

In other words, Conjecture
1.1
is equivalent to the conjecture that the set-theoretic image of the Hitchin morphism is a birational invariant.

4.
A stronger version of the conjecture

In this section we prove our main theorem:
the Hitchin morphism from the Dolbeault moduli space surjects onto the spectral base for
K
-trivial varieties, i.e., a stronger version of the Chen-Ngô conjecture holds in this case. Our approach is to study the vanishing locus of the symmetric
differential arising from the discriminant.

Let
X
be a smooth proper variety.
Let
r\in\mathbb{N}
be fixed and let
s\in{B}^{r}_{X}

be a
\mathop{{k}}\nolimits
-point.
By Lemma
3.1
,
we have the inclusion
Z^{\prime}\subset Z
of branch loci of

\pi^{\prime}_{s}:X^{\prime}_{x}\to X
and
\pi_{s}:X_{s}\to X
(normalized) spectral cover
constructed in
Section

3
,
and
Z
is given by the vanishing locus of the discriminant
D(s)
.
If
\mathrm{codim}_{X}Z=1
,
then in general the (normalized) spectral cover may not be flat.
However, if
\mathrm{codim}_{X}Z\geq 2
,
then it follows that
Z^{\prime}=\emptyset

and
\pi_{s}^{\prime}:X^{\prime}_{s}\to X
is étale by purity of branch locus.
We want to determine sufficient conditions for which the latter case occurs.

Consider the vector space
H^{0}(X,\mathrm{Sym}^{k}\Omega^{1}_{X})

for each
k\geq 1
, and define the subsets
W^{i}
as follows.

\displaystyle W^{i}:=\{\omega\in H^{0}(X,\mathrm{Sym}^{k}\Omega^{1}_{X})\;|\;\mathrm{codim}_{X}V(\omega)\leq i\},

where
V(\omega)\subset X
denotes the vanishing locus of
\omega
.

The
W^{i}
are closed algebraic subsets of

H^{0}(X,\mathrm{Sym}^{k}\Omega^{1}_{X})
,
but they may not be linear unless
i=1
and
k=1

(
[
3
, Theorem 1.11]
).
By definition, we have the inclusion
W^{i}\subset W^{i+1}
for all
i
.
This defines a stratification of

H^{0}(X,\mathrm{Sym}^{k}\Omega^{1}_{X})
given by

\displaystyle W^{1}\subset W^{2}\subset\dots\subset W^{d}\subset H^{0}(X,\mathrm{Sym}^{k}\Omega^{1}_{X}).

Consider a spectral datum
s\in{B}^{r}_{X}(\mathop{{k}}\nolimits)

such that its image

D(s)
via the discriminant map lies in
H^{0}(X,\mathrm{Sym}^{r(r-1)}\Omega^{1}_{X})\setminus W^{1}
.
Then, by the purity of branch locus,
the normalized spectral cover
X^{\prime}_{s}\to X

is étale and
X^{\prime}_{s}
is smooth.

For a general point
s\in{B}^{r}_{X}
,
it seems a difficult but interesting problem
to determine conditions such that

D(s)\in H^{0}(X,\mathrm{Sym}^{r(r-1)}\Omega^{1}_{X})\setminus W^{1}
.
This is related to the vanishing loci of symmetric differentials,
which (to our knowledge) have not been widely studied.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth proper variety such that
{B}^{r}_{X}

is irreducible.
Suppose there is a point
s\in{B}^{r}_{X}

such that
D(s)\in H^{0}(X,\mathrm{Sym}^{r(r-1)}\Omega^{1}_{X})\setminus W^{1}
.
Then the preimage

D^{-1}(\mathrm{im}(D)\setminus W^{1})\subset{B}^{r}_{X}

is a dense open subset.

Proof.

The hypothesis implies that the image of
D

is not contained entirely in
W^{1}
,
therefore
\mathrm{im}(D)\cap W^{1}
is a closed subset of the image.
The complement of its preimage is
a non-empty open, thus dense in
{B}^{r}_{X}
by irreducibility.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

We can weaken the irreducibility condition in Lemma
4.1

to the condition that each irreducible component of

{B}^{r}_{X}
contains a point
s
such that

D(s)\in H^{0}(X,\mathrm{Sym}^{r(r-1)}\Omega^{1}_{X})\setminus W^{1}

to arrive at the same conclusion.

A natural follow-up question is:

Question 4.3
.

(When) is
{B}^{r}_{X}
irreducible?

There are several examples of varieties
X
such that the spectral base

{B}^{r}_{X}
is irreducible for all
r\in\mathbb{N}
.
Any variety that has a ”sufficiently negative” cotangent bundle has

{A}^{r}_{X}=\{0\}
and thus
{B}^{r}_{X}=\{0\}
.
Interestingly, quotients of bounded symmetric domains
of sufficiently large dimension have
{B}^{r}_{X}=\{0\}
for all
r
,
but the dimension of
{A}^{r}_{X}
is large for
r

sufficiently large (see
[
7
, Theorem 1.3]
).

It follows from
[
2
, Proposition 8.1]

that ruled surfaces and non-isotrivial elliptic surfaces
with reduced fibers satisfy
{B}^{r}_{X}={A}^{r}_{X}

for all
r\in\mathbb{N}
,
and thus have irreducible spectral base.

The spectral base for an Abelian variety
X
is given by

{B}^{r}_{X}\cong\mathrm{Chow}^{r}(H^{0}(X,\Omega^{1}_{X}))

(see
[
2
, Example 5.1]
), and is thus irreducible. It follows from
[
9
, Theorem 1.4]
that hyperelliptic varieties also have irreducible spectral base.

A nice consequence of the normalized spectral cover associated to
s

being étale is that the vector bundle
\pi^{\prime}_{s*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}_{s}}

corresponds to a representation of the étale fundamental group

\pi_{1}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(X)
.
More precisely, the following is true.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
f:Y\to X
be a finite étale cover
between normal proper varieties.
Then
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}
is an étale trivializable
vector bundle on
X
, that is,
there exists a finite étale cover
X^{\prime}\to X

such that the pullback of
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}
is isomorphic to
the trivial bundle on
X^{\prime}
.

In particular, if
X
and
Y
are projective, then
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}
is a polystable vector bundle on
X
and all its Chern classes vanish.

Proof.

Let
g:Z\to Y\to X
be the Galois closure of
f
.

Applying Chow’s lemma and normalization allow
us to find a normal projective
variety
\tilde{X}
together with a birational morphism

\pi:\tilde{X}\to X
.
Set
\tilde{Y}:=\tilde{X}\times_{X}Y
and

\tilde{Z}:=\tilde{X}\times_{X}Z
.
Then
\tilde{g}:\tilde{Z}\to\tilde{Y}\xrightarrow{\tilde{f}}\tilde{X}
are
étale covers of normal projective varieties
and
\tilde{Z}\to\tilde{X}
is Galois.
Further, the morphisms
\pi_{Z}:\tilde{Z}\to Z
and

\pi_{Y}:\tilde{Y}\to Y
are birational.
Then we have
\pi_{Z,\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\tilde{Z}}=\mathcal{O}_{Z}

and by the projection formula it suffices to show
that
\pi^{\ast}_{Z}g^{\ast}f_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Y}

is the trivial bundle.

Since
f
is affine, we have

\pi^{\ast}_{Z}g^{\ast}f_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Y}=\tilde{g}^{\ast}\pi^{\ast}f_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Y}=\tilde{g}^{\ast}\tilde{f}_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\tilde{Y}},

see

[
12
,
Lemma 02KG
]
.
Thus, we are reduced to showing the lemma in the case where
X

and
Y
are normal projective varieties.
We assume this from now on.

Note that
g
is an étale Galois cover satisfying

Z\times_{X}Z\cong\bigsqcup_{\sigma\in\mathrm{Gal}(Z/X)}Z
.
Then we conclude via affine base change that

g^{\ast}g_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Z}\cong\bigoplus_{\sigma\in\mathrm{Gal}{Z/X}}\mathcal{O}_{Z}
.
By adjunction we have
f_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Y}\subseteq g_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Z}

and conclude by flatness of
g
that

g^{\ast}f_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Y}\subseteq g^{\ast}g_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Z}
.
Thus, by the previous discussion
we find that
g^{\ast}f_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Y}
is a subsheaf of the trivial bundle.

Fix a polarization
\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)
of
X

and let
\mathcal{O}_{Y}(1)
and
\mathcal{O}_{Z}(1)

the polarizations obtained via
pullback to
Y
and
Z
respectively.
We consider slope-(semi)stability with respect to these polarizations.

Recall that slope-semistability is preserved under pushforward and
pullback along an étale cover, by
[
8
, Lemma 3.2.1]
.
Recall that the degree of a bundle does not change under
pushforward along an étale cover,
and the degree under pullback is multiplied by the degree of the cover.
Thus,
g^{\ast}f_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Y}

slope-semistable of slope
0
and
contained in the trivial bundle by the above discussion.
We conclude that
g^{\ast}f_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Y}
is trivial.

Since we are in characteristic
0
this
implies that
f_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Y}
is polystable by

[
8
, Lemma 3.2.3]
. Note that pullback along
g
induces an
injection between cohomology groups of
X
and
Z
.
The naturality property of Chern classes then implies
that all Chern classes of
f_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Y}
vanish.
∎

Recall that
M^{P,G-ss}_{X}
denotes the good moduli space of Gieseker
semistable Higgs bundles of rank
r
on
X
with Hilbert polynomial
P
,
and
P_{0}
denotes the Hilbert polynomial of the trivial bundle of rank
r
.
The
Dolbeault moduli space
is defined as the moduli space

M^{r}_{X,\mathrm{Dol}}:=M^{G-ss,P_{0}}_{X}
.
The
\mathop{{k}}\nolimits
-points of
M^{r}_{X,\mathrm{Dol}}

correspond to Gieseker polystable rank
r

Higgs bundles with vanishing Chern classes.

We return to the situation where
s\in{B}^{r}_{X}(\mathop{{k}}\nolimits)

is a spectral datum such that its discriminant
D(s)
lies in

H^{0}(X,\mathrm{Sym}^{r(r-1)}\Omega^{1}_{X})\setminus W^{1}
.

Recall from Lemma
3.2

that
\pi^{\prime}_{s*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}_{s}}

is equipped with a Higgs field
\theta^{\prime}

whose associated spectral datum is
s
.
It follows from Proposition
4.4

that the Higgs bundle
(\pi^{\prime}_{s*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}_{s}},\theta^{\prime})

corresponds to a point in the Dolbeault moduli space
M^{r}_{X,\mathrm{Dol}}
.

Thus, one might wonder when a stronger version
of Chen-Ngô’s conjecture holds.

Question 4.5
.

When is the restricted Hitchin morphism

h^{r}_{X,\mathrm{Dol}}:M^{r}_{X,\mathrm{Dol}}\to{B}^{r}_{X}
surjective?

Remark 4.6
.

Note that for an irreducible spectral datum,
that is, a
\mathop{{k}}\nolimits
-point
s
of
(r)\subset{B}^{r}_{X}
,
any torsion-free Higgs sheaf with spectral datum
s

is in fact slope-stable:
it does not even have a non-trivial saturated Higgs-subsheaf
since the characteristic polynomial is multiplicative
in short exact sequences.

However, direct sums of slope-stable sheaves need
not be slope-polystable since the slope may vary
and the spectral cover construction does not lend itself
to controlling the slope (as far as we can see).

In general,
h^{r}_{X,\mathrm{Dol}}
is not surjective;
see
[
6
, Example 3.4 (5)]
for a counterexample.
However, using the properness of
h^{r}_{X,\mathrm{Dol}}

and assuming that
{B}^{r}_{X}
is irreducible,
we make the following observation,
which partially answers the above question.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective scheme and let
r\in\mathbb{N}

be fixed.
Suppose that
{B}^{r}_{X}
is irreducible,
and that there is a point
s\in{B}^{r}_{X}
such that

D(s)
lies in
H^{0}(X,\mathrm{Sym}^{r(r-1)}\Omega^{1}_{X})\setminus W^{1}
.
Then the map
h^{r}_{X,\mathrm{Dol}}:M^{r}_{X,\mathrm{Dol}}\to{B}^{r}_{X}

is surjective.

Proof.

It follows from the hypothesis and from Lemma
4.1

that the subset
U
of spectral data
s
such that
D(s)

lands outside
W^{1}
is a dense open subset of
{B}^{r}_{X}
.

For every point
s\in U
, the normalized spectral cover

\pi^{\prime}_{s}:X^{\prime}_{s}\to X
is étale,
and thus the associated Higgs bundle

(\pi^{\prime}_{s*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}_{s}},\theta^{\prime})

is étale trivializable by Proposition
4.4

and corresponds to a point in
M^{r}_{X,\mathrm{Dol}}
.
In other words,
U
is contained in
\mathrm{im}(h^{r}_{X,\mathrm{Dol}})
.

By
[
10
, Theorem 6.11]
,
we know that
h^{r}_{X,\mathrm{Dol}}
is proper.
In particular,
\mathrm{im}(h^{r}_{X,\mathrm{Dol}})

is closed in
{B}^{r}_{X}
.
Since
U\subset\mathrm{im}(h^{r}_{X\mathrm{Dol}})
and

U
is dense in
{B}^{r}_{X}
,
we conclude that
\mathrm{im}(h^{r}_{X,\mathrm{Dol}})

coincides with
{B}^{r}_{X}
set-theoretically.
∎

Similarly as for Lemma
4.1
,
one can replace the irreducibility of
{B}^{r}_{X}

by the condition that every irreducible component of
{B}^{r}_{X}

contains a point
s
such that
D(s)
is not contained in
W^{1}
,
to arrive at the same conclusion.

Although Proposition
4.7
provides a sufficient condition for the
restricted Hitchin morphism
M^{r}_{X,\mathrm{Dol}}\to{B}^{r}_{X}

to be surjective, the irreducibility of

{B}^{r}_{X}
is not a reasonable condition to check.
We can replace this
with the condition that the subsets
W^{1}
be zero and still achieve the
surjectivity of
M^{r}_{X,\mathrm{Dol}}\to{B}^{r}_{X}
.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
X
be a smooth proper scheme and let
r\in\mathbb{N}
be fixed.
Suppose that
W^{1}=\{0\}\subset H^{0}(X,\mathrm{Sym}^{k(k-1)}\Omega^{1}_{X})

for all
1\leq k\leq r
.
Then for every
s\in{B}^{k}_{X}
the fiber
(h^{k}_{X})^{-1}(s)

contains an étale trivializable Higgs bundle for all
1\leq k\leq r
.

In particular, if
X
is also projective,
the morphism
h^{k}_{X}:M^{k}_{X,\mathrm{Dol}}\to{B}^{k}_{X}

is surjective for all
1\leq k\leq r
.

Proof.

For
s=0\in{B}^{k}_{X}
it is clear that the trivial bundle
of rank
k
is contained in the fiber
(h^{k}_{X})^{-1}(0)
.
Suppose
0\neq s\in{B}^{k}_{X}
is a
\mathop{{k}}\nolimits
-point
such that
D(s)\neq 0
.
Then the normalized spectral cover
\pi^{\prime}_{s}:X^{\prime}_{s}\to X

is étale and
(\pi^{\prime}_{s*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}_{s}},\theta^{\prime})

is an étale trivializable Higgs bundle for all
1\leq k\leq r

by Proposition
4.4
.
If
X
is projective, this corresponds to a point
in
M^{k}_{X,\mathrm{Dol}}
for all
1\leq k\leq r
.

Now, let
0\neq s\in{B}^{k}_{X}
be a point such that
D(s)=0
.
Then by Lemma
2.5
the characteristic polynomial
associated with
s
splits as a product
s=\prod_{j}t_{j}
,
where
t_{j}\in(m_{j})\subset{B}^{m_{j}}_{X}

for some
m_{j}
such that
\sum_{j}m_{j}=k
and non-vanishing
discriminant

D(t_{j})\in H^{0}(X,\mathrm{Sym}^{m_{j}(m_{j}-1)}\Omega^{1}_{X})\setminus\{0\}
.

By hypothesis, the normalized spectral cover

\pi^{\prime}_{t_{j}}:X^{\prime}_{t_{j}}\to X
is étale and the Higgs bundle

(\pi^{\prime}_{t_{j}*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}_{t_{j}}},\theta^{\prime}_{j})
is étale
trivializable for each
j
, again by Proposition
4.4
.
Then the direct sum
\bigoplus_{j}(\pi^{\prime}_{t_{j}*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}_{t_{j}}},\theta^{\prime}_{j})

is an étale trivializable Higgs bundle of rank
k
,
and thus corresponds to a point in
M^{k}_{X,\mathrm{Dol}}
if
X

is projective for all
1\leq k\leq r
.
∎

Remark 4.9
.

From the proof of Proposition
4.8
,
we see that in the case where
X
is projective,
we have a stronger statement.
That is, for each point
s\in{B}^{r}_{X}
,
the fiber
(h^{r}_{X,\mathrm{Dol}})^{-1}(s)
contains a
Higgs bundle whose underlying vector bundle is étale trivializable.

The hypothesis in Proposition
4.8

is still somewhat intractable.
However, they are satisfied by
r
-small varieties.

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
X
be a
r
-small variety,
as in Definition
1.4
and let
r\in\mathbb{N}
be fixed.
Then
h^{k}_{X,\mathrm{Dol}}:M^{k}_{X,\mathrm{Dol}}\to{B}^{k}_{X}

is surjective for all
1\leq k\leq r
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that any
r
-small variety satisfies
the hypothesis of Proposition
4.8
.
By definition,
\Omega^{1}_{X}
is slope-semistable,
which implies that
\mathrm{Sym}^{j}\Omega^{1}_{X}

is slope-semistable for all
j\geq 1
since
we are working in characteristic
0
.

We claim that

W^{1}=\{0\}\subset H^{0}(X,\mathrm{Sym}^{k(k-1)}\Omega^{1}_{X})

for all
1\leq k\leq r
.
Indeed, suppose that there was a non-zero section

\sigma\in H^{0}(X,\mathrm{Sym}^{k(k-1)}\Omega^{1}_{X})

such that
\sigma
vanishes along a divisor
E
in
X
.
Consider the injective morphism

\mathcal{O}_{X}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Sym}^{k(k-1)}\Omega^{1}_{X}

induced by
\sigma
.
Denote the quotient by
Q:=\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}\nolimits^{k(k-1)}\Omega^{1}_{X}/\mathcal{O}_{X}
.
Let
\mathcal{L}
be the saturation of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
in

\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}\nolimits^{k(k-1)}\Omega^{1}_{X}
.
Then the torsion subsheaf
T
of
Q
fits
into a short exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{L}\to T\to 0
.
Since
\sigma
vanishes along
E
by assumption,
we have that
T
has support

\mathop{\mathrm{Supp}}\nolimits(T)=E
and we conclude that
\mathcal{L}

has strictly positive degree, that is,
\deg(\mathcal{L})\geq 1
.
This contradicts the slope-semistability of
\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}\nolimits^{k(k-1)}\Omega^{1}_{X}

which is assumed to be slope-semistable of slope
<1
.

We conclude that
W^{1}=\{0\}
as claimed and that

h^{k}_{X,\mathrm{Dol}}
is surjective for all
1\leq k\leq r

by Proposition
4.8
.
∎

It is known that the cotangent bundle of a smooth projective variety with
numerically trivial canonical class is slope-semistable in characteristic zero (see, e.g.
[
4
, Theorem A]
).
Thus, every
K_{X}
-numerically trivial variety is

r
-small, and we arrive at the following special case.

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective scheme with
K_{X}
-numerically trivial.
Then
h^{r}_{X,\mathrm{Dol}}:M^{r}_{X,\mathrm{Dol}}\to{B}^{r}_{X}

is surjective for all
r\in\mathbb{N}
.

Next, we make the following observation about the structure
of the spectral base for varieties which satisfy
the hypothesis in Proposition
4.8
.

Proposition 4.12
.

Let
r\in\mathbb{N}
be fixed
and let
X
be as in Proposition
4.8
.
Then there are finitely many étale-trivializable vector bundles
\mathcal{V}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{V}_{m}
of rank
r
on
X
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that for each point
s\in{B}^{r}_{X}
, the fiber
(h^{r}_{X})^{-1}(s)
contains some
(\mathcal{V}_{i},\theta_{i})\in M^{r}_{X,\mathrm{Dol}}
.

Proof.

By induction on
r
, it suffices to prove the assertion for each point in the stratum
(r)\subset{B}^{r}_{X}
.

By assumption, the discriminant of each point
s\in(r)
has a vanishing locus of codimension
\geq 2
. The corresponding normalized spectral cover
\pi^{\prime}_{s}:X^{\prime}_{s}\to X
is thus étale of degree
r
and by Proposition
4.4
, the vector bundle
\pi^{\prime}_{s*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}_{s}}
underlying the Higgs bundle
(\pi^{\prime}_{s*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}_{s}},\theta^{\prime})
is étale trivializable.

Since there are finitely many étale covers of
X
of degree
r

up to isomorphism, there are finitely many such étale trivializable vector bundles

\pi^{\prime}_{s*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}_{s}}
of rank
r
up to isomorphism underlying the Higgs bundles
(\pi^{\prime}_{s*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}_{s}},\theta^{\prime})
, and we are done.
∎

Remark 4.13
.

With notation as in Proposition
4.12
, let
V_{i}
denote the vector space of all Higgs fields on the vector bundle
\mathcal{V}_{i}
, for
1\leq i\leq m
. The Hitchin morphism induces maps
V_{i}\to{B}^{r}_{X}
,
\theta\mapsto h^{r}_{X}(\mathcal{V}_{i},\theta)
for each
i
. Proposition
4.12
then implies that we can express the spectral base as a finite union
{B}^{r}_{X}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}h^{r}_{X}(V_{i})
.

However, it is unclear whether this description would
imply irreducibility of the spectral base in this case. Rather, it may provide us with an example of the spectral base of a
K
-trivial variety where the spectral base is not irreducible.
