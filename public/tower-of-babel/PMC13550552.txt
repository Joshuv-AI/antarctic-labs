Effect of chemical treatment on the mechanical and thermal performance of flax fiber reinforced aluminium 6082 laminate: a machine learning-enhanced investigation
Authors: M Vinod, B S Nithyananda, Shrishail B Sollapur, Satish Gajbhiv, R N Chikkangoudar, Priya Dongare Jadhav, Shekhar Milind Mane, Rasika Gajendra Patil, Abhijit Bhowmik, Nagaraj Ashok
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis work examined the role of alkaline and epoxy-based surface chemical treatments on the mechanical and thermal properties of flax fiber reinforced Aluminum Alloy 6082 hybrid Fiber Metal Laminates (FMLs), integrated with machine learning (ML) predictive frameworks. Flax fiber mats underwent surface modification via a 1% NaOH alkaline soak followed by an epoxy sizing treatment to promote stronger bonding with the aluminum matrix. Concurrently, aluminum sheets were treated with an NaOH–Na₂CO₃ alkaline bath to enhance metal-polymer interfacial compatibility. Composite laminates were manufactured through hand layup combined with compression molding at 30 bar and 70 °C curing for four hours. Tensile characterization followed ASTM D638 protocols, thermal conductivity measurements employed a guarded hot plate (GHP) system per ASTM C177, and corrosion evaluation relied on gravimetric immersion in 3.5 wt.% NaCl over 30 days. Predictive models using Artificial Neural Networks (ANN), Support Vector Regression (SVR), and Random Forest (RF) were developed on a structured 180-observation experimental dataset. Steady-state thermal conductivity values of 0.1039 W/m·K and 0.065 W/m·K were recorded for untreated and chemically treated FFAL laminates, respectively. Surface treatment yielded a 31% gain in tensile strength (168.5 MPa to 220.7 MPa) and a 34% rise in tensile modulus (12.3 GPa to 16.34 GPa), while thermal conductivity declined by 37.4%. Among all models, the Random Forest algorithm demonstrated superior predictive capability with R2 = 0.972 for tensile strength and R2 = 0.969 for thermal conductivity; MAPE values remained below 3.5%. All experimental trials were performed in triplicate with mean values reported alongside standard deviation. These findings substantiate that combining chemical surface modification with data-driven ML optimization constitutes a reliable design methodology for high-performance, sustainable natural fiber metal composites.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-58852-w.Keywords: Fiber metal laminates, Flax fiber, Alkaline treatment, Machine learning, Random forest, Tensile properties, Thermal conductivity, Predictive modelingSubject terms: Engineering, Materials science

Introduction

Growing demands for lightweight yet structurally robust engineering materials have catalyzed significant research activity in natural fiber composites and hybrid laminate configurations. Contemporary industrial sectors, spanning aerospace, automotive, marine, rail, and civil construction, require materials with reduced structural mass while preserving load-bearing integrity and long-term reliability under demanding service conditions1–3. Although glass and carbon fiber reinforced synthetic composites deliver superior mechanical output, concerns over high fabrication costs, environmental persistence, and energy-intensive processing have redirected efforts toward ecologically benign material alternatives4–6. Naturally derived plant fibers have consequently emerged as viable reinforcement candidates by virtue of their low mass density, biological degradability, renewable origin, acceptable tensile performance, minimal surface abrasion, and reduced ecological footprint7–10. Cellulosic plant fibers including flax, jute, sisal, hemp, kenaf, agave, and vetiver have proven suitable for semi-structural and lightweight load-bearing configurations where a favorable strength-to-weight balance and environmental responsibility are concurrently necessary11–15. Among naturally occurring plant-based reinforcements, flax fiber occupies a particularly notable position owing to its relatively elevated tensile stiffness, low mass per unit volume, and effective vibration attenuation properties16–18. Flax-reinforced polymer matrix composites have consequently found application in automotive interior components, sports equipment, marine hull panels, and crash energy absorption structures14,19,20. Notwithstanding these favorable attributes, naturally derived fiber composites exhibit characteristic limitations in comparison with their synthetic counterparts. Hydrophilic affinity, poor matrix compatibility, fiber dimensional variability, and constrained thermal durability collectively compromise long-term structural reliability21–23. These shortcomings restrict their suitability for structurally demanding applications, and hybridization strategies have thus gained recognition as a practical approach to augment natural fiber composite performance while preserving their environmental advantages24.

Fiber metal laminates constitute a category of advanced hybrid structural materials formed by interspersing metallic sheet layers with fiber-reinforced polymer plies. These constructions leverage the ductility, damage tolerance, and fatigue resistance of metals alongside the low-density and high-specific-stiffness characteristics of composite layers25. Legacy FML configurations such as GLARE and ARALL have established an admirable track record in aerospace structural components through their exceptional crack retardation, corrosion endurance, and damage containment capabilities26. Contemporary research has extended this concept toward sustainable FML variants that substitute conventional synthetic reinforcements with natural fibers, reducing both manufacturing carbon footprint and material costs27–29. Combining naturally derived fibers with low-density aluminum alloys has therefore emerged as a productive avenue for fabricating structurally functional and environmentally responsible laminates. Aluminum alloy 6082 represents a particularly well-suited metallic layer candidate for natural fiber-based FML development owing to its balanced combination of strength, corrosion resistance, weldability, and specific stiffness30. While the alloy possesses desirable intrinsic mechanical and thermal attributes for lightweight structural contexts, realizing the full potential of flax fiber reinforced Al-6082 laminates hinges critically on the quality of adhesive bonding at both fiber-matrix and metal-matrix contact planes. The quality of interfacial bonding directly governs load redistribution across constituent layers and profoundly affects tensile response, thermal transport, fatigue behavior, and corrosion durability31–33. Insufficient interfacial attachment frequently triggers premature delamination initiation and progressive crack extension, degrading the overall structural integrity of the laminate system. Lignocellulosic natural fibers inherently possess a hydrophilic surface character arising from the presence of cellulose, hemicellulose, lignin, wax, and pectin constituents, which promote moisture uptake and diminish compatibility with hydrophobic polymer matrices34. Incompatible interfacial chemistry between fiber and matrix phases results in inadequate stress transfer and poor adhesion quality. To address this fundamental challenge, various surface engineering strategies including alkaline treatment, silane functionalization, acetylation, and plasma modification have been investigated to improve fiber-matrix compatibility35. Sodium hydroxide-based alkaline treatment has risen to particular prominence among these alternatives on account of its operational simplicity, low material cost, and documented effectiveness. Alkali processing removes surface contaminants such as lignin residues, hemicellulosic fractions, oily deposits, and waxy coatings, simultaneously increasing fiber surface roughness and exposing reactive hydroxyl functionalities36. These surface alterations collectively promote fiber wettability, enhance mechanical anchoring between fiber and matrix, and improve overall interfacial bonding quality.

Prior studies have consistently documented meaningful improvements in mechanical strength, thermal stability, and dimensional integrity following chemical modification of natural reinforcements including flax, sisal, hemp, and jute fibers37–39. Hybridization coupled with appropriate surface treatment protocols further amplifies these gains through synergistic property enhancement in stiffness, moisture uptake resistance, and durability40. Complementary to fiber surface engineering, modification of metallic surfaces carries equal significance for optimizing interfacial quality in FML systems. Bonding strength between aluminum sheet faces and polymer matrix layers is strongly dependent on surface topography, surface energy state, and chemical affinity characteristics41. Surface preparation methods encompassing mechanical abrasion, chemical etching, anodic oxidation, and coupling agent application have accordingly been implemented to enhance metal-polymer adhesion by increasing contact area through surface micro-roughening, thereby suppressing the likelihood of interfacial delamination under applied mechanical loading. While considerable progress has been made in natural fiber FML development, a comprehensive understanding of how simultaneous fiber and metallic surface treatment collectively influence multiple functional properties remains an open research question. Most preceding studies focused on isolated mechanical characterization outcomes such as tensile or impact properties, with relatively fewer investigations systematically linking surface modifications to thermal conductivity behavior and corrosion performance within hybrid laminate architectures. Beyond this, experimental design optimization of composite systems conventionally entails resource-intensive parametric screening involving large numbers of fabrication variables, inflating material usage, processing duration, and total development expenditure. Machine learning-based computational approaches have emerged as effective tools for addressing this challenge within composite materials research by enabling predictive modeling and process optimization. Computational algorithms including ANN, SVR, and RF have successfully mapped nonlinear structure–property relationships across a wide range of composite material systems. Published studies demonstrate that ML-based models can reliably forecast tensile strength, thermal transport coefficients, wear rates, surface finish quality, and tribological behavior while substantially curtailing experimental workload and design cycle duration. ANN architectures are particularly well suited for capturing complex multi-parameter nonlinear interactions, while RF ensembles offer robust generalization alongside feature contribution analysis capabilities. SVR models deliver strong predictive reliability particularly for moderately sized experimental datasets by virtue of their structural risk minimization approach.

The application domain of ML in composite materials engineering has broadened considerably to encompass machining optimization, additive manufacturing process control, thermal characterization, tribology prediction, laser processing, and hybrid laminate design. Data-driven modeling approaches have also been deployed to examine the influence of chemical processing parameters, fiber stacking configurations, filler content, and environmental exposure on composite performance outcomes. Multi-objective optimization frameworks combining ML with evolutionary algorithms such as Genetic Algorithms and NSGA-II have further enabled simultaneous optimization of competing engineering responses including structural strength, thermal conductivity, wear resistance, and process efficiency, facilitating Pareto-optimal solution identification for material selection and process design. Despite these research advances, an integrated experimental characterization and ML-guided optimization framework specifically addressing chemically treated flax fiber reinforced Al-6082 laminates across multiple functional properties has not been established in the existing literature. Current studies predominantly investigate either experimental mechanical behavior or isolated predictive modeling in isolation, without constructing a unified analytical framework that connects surface modification protocols, microstructural evolution, and multi-property performance outcomes. To address this research gap, the present investigation reports a combined experimental and computational study of chemically treated FFAL laminates, systematically evaluating tensile strength, thermal conductivity, and corrosion behavior under varying treatment conditions while simultaneously employing ANN, SVR, and RF algorithms for multi-property prediction. Feature importance evaluation and Genetic Algorithm-guided multi-objective optimization were additionally implemented to identify dominant process drivers and generate balanced performance trade-off solutions. Mechanistic correlations between surface microstructural changes and experimentally observed property improvements are established throughout, contributing a foundational framework for designing next-generation sustainable high-performance natural fiber metal composite structures.

Chemical treatments

Alkaline surface treatment of aluminium 6082

An alkaline pre-treatment protocol was applied to Al-6082 sheet surfaces to augment metal-polymer interfacial adhesion prior to laminate assembly. This process removes native surface oxide films and adsorbed contaminants while creating a micro-roughened surface texture that promotes mechanical anchoring with the epoxy matrix. The treatment bath was formulated by dissolving 30 g/L NaOH and 30 g/L Na₂CO₃ in deionized water and heating the solution to 60 °C. Sheets were submerged for one minute, subsequently rinsed thoroughly with deionized water, and permitted to air-dry under ambient laboratory conditions. This alkaline etching procedure generates reactive hydroxyl-enriched surface sites that facilitate covalent interfacial bonding with the epoxy system, as schematically illustrated in Fig. 1.

Surface preparation process of Al-6082 sheets: alkaline solution preparation, brief immersion at 60 °C, and subsequent air drying.

Chemical modification of flax fibers

The flax fiber modification protocol comprised two sequential stages. In the initial stage, unidirectional fiber mats were submerged in a 1% NaOH aqueous solution at ambient temperature for 20 min. Following immersion, fibers were washed with cold water and subsequently neutralized through brief exposure to mildly acidified rinse water containing 20 drops of 0.1 M HCl per liter, with a final cold water wash to complete the neutralization cycle. In the second stage, fibers were thermally dried at 80 °C in a circulating-air oven for eight hours, after which they were immersed in an epoxy-based sizing solution comprising LY556 resin and hardener dissolved in acetone at 3% concentration for two hours at ambient temperature. This sizing treatment layer enhances fiber surface wettability and promotes more uniform epoxy resin penetration during laminate manufacture. The complete surface modification sequence is illustrated in Fig. 2.

Flax fiber surface treatment process: NaOH soaking and neutralization (a), followed by epoxy sizing and oven drying at 80 °C for 8 h (b).

Laminate fabrication

FFAL composite laminates were produced via a hand layup technique succeeded by compression consolidation. Each laminate comprised two unidirectional flax fiber plies encased between two Al-6082 metallic plates in an [Al/FF/FF/Al] stacking arrangement. LY556 epoxy resin and the accompanying hardener were blended at a 10:1 weight proportion and uniformly applied across each interfacial layer. The stacked assembly was then consolidated under 30 bar compression, cured at 70 °C over four hours, and subsequently cooled to ambient temperature under controlled conditions. The fabrication sequence and laminate layer arrangement are presented in Figs. 3 and 4, respectively.

Fabrication of FFAL laminates via hand layup followed by compression molding under 30 bar pressure and curing at 70 °C for 4 h.

Experimental characterization

Tensile testing

Uniaxial tensile characterization was performed following ASTM D638 to quantify ultimate tensile strength (UTS) and Young’s modulus of the FFAL laminates. ASTM D638 was adopted because the laminate system is fundamentally polymer-matrix dominant, incorporating flax fiber reinforcement and epoxy matrix plies with thin integrated aluminum face sheets. Numerous prior investigations on natural fiber-reinforced hybrid laminates and comparable FML configurations have likewise employed this standard for polymer-matrix-dominant composite tensile evaluation4,19,20. Test specimens with gauge dimensions of 33 mm (length) × 6 mm (width) × 3 mm (thickness) were prepared using precision water jet cutting, as shown in Fig. 5. Testing was executed under displacement-controlled mode at a crosshead velocity of 20 mm/min using hardened steel grips mounted on a 5000 N capacity load frame. All test conditions were repeated three times and mean values are reported with corresponding standard deviation. Post-fracture failure analysis was conducted by visual examination and optical microscopy.

Tensile test specimen dimensions as per ASTM D638.

Thermal conductivity measurement

Steady-state thermal conductivity was determined using a guarded hot plate (GHP) apparatus operated in compliance with ASTM C177. Square test specimens measuring 250 × 250 mm with 3 mm thickness were prepared for evaluation, as shown in Fig. 6. The GHP instrument maintains a controlled steady-state temperature differential across specimen thickness, enabling thermal conductivity calculation via Fourier’s conduction relation: k = Q·d / (A·ΔT), where Q denotes heat flux in watts, d is specimen thickness in meters, A is the cross-sectional heat flow area in m2, and ΔT is the imposed temperature gradient in Kelvin. Thermal equilibrium was typically established within approximately 45 min. It is essential to emphasize that the experimentally recorded steady-state thermal conductivity values (0.1039 W/m·K for untreated and 0.065 W/m·K for treated FFAL laminates) are specific to the fabricated specimen configuration tested at baseline process conditions. The ML-predicted conductivity ranges presented in subsequent figures were generated over a broader parametric design space and therefore cannot be directly compared against these specific experimental measurements.

Untreated (a) and treated (b) specimens of 250 mm × 250 mm prepared for thermal conductivity test.

Corrosion resistance evaluation

Environmental durability was quantified via a gravimetric immersion method wherein specimens were continuously submerged in a 3.5 wt.% sodium chloride solution for a 30-day exposure period maintained at 25 ± 2 °C. Mass measurements were recorded at five-day intervals using a high-precision analytical balance with 0.0001 g resolution. Initial and final specimen mass data for both treated and untreated samples are documented in Fig. 7. The gravimetric approach was selected as a well-established and reproducible method for preliminary environmental durability assessment of hybrid laminate materials. It is recognized that this technique carries inherent sensitivity constraints and cannot resolve localized electrochemical corrosion mechanisms. Supplementary electrochemical characterization methods including electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) and potentiodynamic polarization will be incorporated in follow-up research for comprehensive corrosion behavior assessment.

Initial and final weight of untreated (a, b) and treated (c, d) specimens for corrosion evaluation.

Machine learning methodology

Experimental dataset construction

The ML training dataset was assembled from structured factorial experimental trials by systematically varying four primary processing variables: NaOH solution concentration (0.5%, 1.0%, 1.5%, 2.0%), fiber immersion duration (10, 15, 20, 25, 30 min), thermal drying temperature (60 °C, 70 °C, 80 °C, 90 °C), and compaction pressure (20, 25, 30, 35 bar). The complete factorial design matrix generated 180 distinct experimental conditions. It is important to clarify that this expanded ML training dataset was constructed from the complete factorial design space to achieve robust predictive and sensitivity modeling capability, whereas the detailed mechanical and thermal property characterization reported herein was focused on the optimized baseline experimental condition (1% NaOH, 20 min treatment). The full parameter space constitutes the model training foundation rather than the baseline characterization condition alone. Each parametric combination was evaluated in triplicate, with mean values utilized for model training. Statistical outliers were identified and removed using the interquartile range (IQR) criterion. Input features were standardized via z-score normalization, and the full dataset was partitioned into training (80%), validation (10%), and test (10%) subsets employing stratified random sampling.

Feature engineering

Engineered interaction features were derived including the multiplicative product of NaOH concentration and immersion duration (treatment intensity index), dimensionless fiber-to-matrix volumetric ratios, and second-order polynomial expansion features. Following interaction-term construction and polynomial expansion, the expanded feature space was subjected to Principal Component Analysis (PCA) dimensionality reduction retaining 95% of cumulative explained variance. This reduction process yielded six dominant principal components from the expanded feature pool for the final compact feature representation used in all model training procedures, providing a balance between predictive fidelity, computational tractability, and result reproducibility.

Machine learning architectures

ANN: A feedforward multilayer perceptron architecture employing two fully connected hidden layers containing 128 and 64 neurons respectively, with ReLU activation functions, linear output activation, Adam optimization (learning rate 0.001), 500 training epochs, batch size of 32, and Dropout regularization (dropout rate 0.2).

SVR: A support vector regressor utilizing an RBF kernel with hyperparameters optimized through a fivefold cross-validated grid search spanning C (0.1–100), ε (0.01–1), and γ (0.001–1).

Random Forest: An ensemble of 200 decision trees with maximum depth 15, bootstrap aggregation, Gini impurity as the node-splitting criterion, minimum split samples of 5, and minimum leaf node size of 2.

Model evaluation

Predictive model performance was quantified using the coefficient of determination (R2), mean absolute error (MAE), root mean square error (RMSE), and mean absolute percentage error (MAPE). Ten-fold cross-validation was applied across the full dataset to verify generalization capacity. The training-to-testing R2 differential remained at or below 0.017 across all models, confirming satisfactory generalization without overfitting and validating the adequacy of the 180-sample factorial dataset for training both ANN and RF model architectures with the engineered feature representation.

Results and discussion

Tensile characterization results

Figure 8 displays tensile fracture morphologies and quantitative tensile property data for both untreated and chemically modified FFAL specimens. The untreated specimen fracture surface in Fig. 8a exhibits a comparatively clean failure plane accompanied by clear interfacial debonding at the fracture zone, consistent with weaker adhesion at the fiber-aluminum-epoxy tri-phase interfaces. The treated specimen in Fig. 8b, by contrast, maintains considerably better structural cohesion following fracture, with the failure zone demonstrating enhanced structural continuity and a more tortuous crack trajectory, indicative of improved inter-laminar load transfer efficiency resulting from surface chemical modification.

Untreated (a) and treated (b) specimens after tensile testing, showing improved fracture resistance in treated laminates. (c)Tensile test results.

Quantitative tensile data presented in Fig. 8c further substantiate the performance benefits of chemical treatment. The untreated FFAL laminate recorded a mean tensile strength of 168.5 ± 3.2 MPa alongside a tensile modulus of 12.3 ± 0.4 GPa. Post-treatment values rose to 220.7 ± 4.1 MPa and 16.34 ± 0.5 GPa, representing 30.97% and 32.83% improvements in UTS and elastic modulus, respectively. All reported values represent means ± standard deviation from three independent test specimens. This significant property enhancement confirms that the dual surface treatment effectively elevated both the stiffness and load-carrying capacity of the FFAL system. Mechanistic interpretation of the tensile improvement centers on several coupled phenomena. NaOH treatment of the flax fiber mats dissolved surface contaminants including lignin residues, hemicellulosic polymers, pectin, and wax fractions, thereby exposing additional fiber hydroxyl groups and increasing surface micro-roughness. This promoted stronger mechanical interlocking with the epoxy matrix and enhanced chemical bonding efficiency at the fiber-matrix interface. The subsequent epoxy sizing coat contributed further to fiber-matrix compatibility by improving resin penetration uniformity. Concurrently, chemical etching of the Al-6082 sheet surfaces eliminated the native passivation oxide layer and produced a chemically active, topographically roughened metal surface, raising adhesive bonding affinity between the aluminum faces and epoxy matrix and diminishing susceptibility to interfacial delamination under tensile load application.

The SEM micrographs and EDS spectral data in Fig. 9a–b characterize the structural differences in fracture surface morphology between untreated and treated FFAL specimens. The untreated sample (Fig. 9a) displays numerous fiber pull-out cavities, internal voids, matrix cracking patterns, and irregular interfacial discontinuities, collectively reflecting inadequate adhesion and poor mechanical stress transmission. EDS analysis confirms the dominance of carbon and aluminum spectral signals with minimal oxygen-containing bonding phases. The treated specimen fracture surface (Fig. 9b) presents a markedly denser and more uniform morphology with substantially reduced debonding area and improved matrix coverage around individual fibers, confirming that surface treatment enhanced both surface roughness and chemical reactivity to strengthen fiber-matrix and metal-matrix adhesive contacts.

Thermal conductivity results

Untreated FFAL laminate

The untreated FFAL specimen exhibited a characteristic transient heating phase followed by a plateau at steady-state thermal equilibrium under the applied thermal gradient conditions. Thermal equilibrium was attained after approximately 45 min, yielding a stable measured thermal conductivity of k = 0.1039 W/m·K as documented in Fig. 13. This experimentally derived value was specifically obtained via the guarded hot plate methodology operating under steady-state conditions applied to the fabricated laminate at the baseline treatment specification of 1% NaOH and 20-min immersion duration. The low conductivity magnitude reflects the thermal insulation contribution of the flax fiber plies embedded within the laminate configuration.

Thermal conductivity data for untreated FFAL: time–temperature profile (left) and steady-state conductivity value (right). Experimentally measured k = 0.1039 W/m·K.

Chemically treated FFAL laminate

Surface chemical treatment produced a 37.4% decrease in steady-state thermal conductivity relative to the untreated baseline, with the treated FFAL laminate recording an experimentally measured value of k = 0.065 W/m·K (Fig. 14). The alkaline surface modification process dissolves fiber-bound impurity phases, generating a porous fiber surface microstructure containing micro-scale air pockets that inhibit phonon-mediated heat transport pathways. The additional fiber-matrix interfacial contacts introduced through surface modification further promote phonon scattering, progressively reducing the effective thermal conductivity of the composite. The experimentally measured conductivity values (0.1039 W/m·K untreated; 0.065 W/m·K treated) represent specific steady-state GHP measurements at the baseline characterization conditions. ML model predictions presented in subsequent figures span a wider parametric range (approximately 0.18–1.6 W/m·K) corresponding to the extended experimental design space encompassing elevated NaOH concentrations and prolonged treatment durations beyond the baseline conditions. These two value sets represent different regions of the parametric space and should not be directly compared.

Thermal conductivity data for treated FFAL: time–temperature profile (a) and steady-state conductivity value (b). Experimentally measured k = 0.065 W/m·K.

Both sample groups exhibited monotonically increasing mass over the immersion period, reflecting progressive accumulation of corrosion-derived reaction products on specimen surfaces. The untreated specimens accumulated 0.20 g of mass gain versus 0.17 g for the treated group, translating to an approximately 11.5% reduction in corrosion rate attributable to chemical surface modification. This improvement is ascribed to three complementary protective mechanisms: superior sealing of fiber-matrix interfacial voids by the epoxy sizing layer, development of a more protective passive oxide layer on the alkaline-conditioned Al-6082 surface, and restricted moisture diffusion pathways through the consolidated laminate cross-section. The moderate magnitude of this improvement (~ 11.5%) is acknowledged, and the gravimetric method’s inherent limitations in capturing localized electrochemical corrosion events are recognized. Subsequent investigations will integrate EIS and potentiodynamic polarization measurements to yield a more detailed mechanistic assessment of interfacial corrosion behavior. Nevertheless, the demonstrated improvement confirms that chemical treatment delivers multifunctional performance benefits encompassing mechanical, thermal, and environmental durability dimensions of FFAL laminate behavior.

Multi-property optimization via machine learning

A Genetic Algorithm (GA) optimizer coupled with the trained RF models was deployed to identify the optimal combination of processing parameters for simultaneous mechanical-thermal performance optimization. The GA attained convergence after 150 generations at the following optimal parameter combination: NaOH concentration 1.2%, treatment duration 22 min, drying temperature 82 °C, and compaction pressure 32 bar. The optimizer convergence trajectory is shown in Fig. 17. These GA-identified parameter values represent ML-guided predictive optimization outputs derived from experimentally validated model architectures (RF: R2 = 0.972 for tensile; R2 = 0.969 for thermal conductivity). The proximity of these optimal values to the experimentally characterized baseline condition (1% NaOH, 20 min duration) supports their practical implementability. Dedicated experimental validation of this optimized parameter combination constitutes an important priority for future research to confirm the predicted multi-property performance gains.

Genetic algorithm convergence plot over 150 generations. Fitness value rises sharply from ~ 0.35 to ~ 0.95, indicating effective convergence toward the optimal solution.

Conclusion

This investigation systematically characterized the effect of a dual chemical surface modification protocol on the multifunctional performance of flax fiber reinforced Aluminum 6082 fiber metal laminates, integrating machine learning-assisted predictive modeling throughout. The principal conclusions and engineering implications may be summarized as follows:

Tensile Properties: Dual surface chemical treatment elevated tensile strength by 30.97% (168.5 to 220.7 MPa) and tensile stiffness modulus by 32.83% (12.3 to 16.34 GPa), driven by enhanced interfacial adhesion quality through surface roughening, contaminant removal, and epoxy sizing. These performance gains substantiate the viability of concurrent fiber and metal surface modification as a design strategy for structural natural fiber metal laminate applications.

Thermal Insulation: Steady-state thermal conductivity decreased by 37.4% from 0.1039 W/m·K in untreated specimens to 0.065 W/m·K following treatment at baseline conditions, confirming substantially improved thermal insulation performance beneficial for thermal management applications.

Corrosion Resistance: Chemical treatment moderated the 30-day corrosion mass gain by approximately 11.5% in 3.5 wt.% NaCl immersion conditions. Optimal Process Parameters: GA-guided ML optimization identified 1.2% NaOH concentration, 22 min treatment duration, 82 °C drying temperature, and 32 bar compression pressure as the optimal parameter set for balanced multi-property performance. Experimental confirmation of these ML-guided optimal conditions constitutes a planned future research activity. Collectively, these findings establish that integrating chemical surface engineering with ML-guided process optimization constitutes a powerful and efficient design framework for sustainable, high-performance natural fiber metal laminates. The methodology carries substantial implications for lightweight structural, thermal management, and multifunctional engineering applications requiring simultaneous optimization of structural and thermal material properties.
