Low Rank for Rank: Uncertainty-Aware Task-Specific LLM Ranking under Sparse Pairwise Comparisons
Jiachun Li, David Simchi-Levi, Will Wei Sun

Abstract
Pairwise human-preference platforms such as Chatbot Arena have become central to large language model evaluation, yet reliable task-specific ranking remains challenging. Global leaderboards can mask substantial task heterogeneity, while independently estimating rankings for each fine-grained task is statistically unstable under sparse and imbalanced comparisons. We propose a low-rank framework for task-specific LLM ranking from sparse pairwise comparisons, modeling the task-by-model ability matrix as low rank so that information can be shared across related tasks while preserving task-specific differences. We first develop a max-norm accurate estimator for the latent score matrix, which yields task-wise top- K K recovery guarantees under sparse sampling. Our main contribution is an uncertainty quantification framework for task-specific ranking. Beyond point estimation, we construct debiased one-step estimators for fixed score contrasts, such as task-specific gaps between two models, yielding asymptotically valid confidence intervals that attain the semiparametric efficiency lower bound. We further extend this inference to the high-dimensional ranking regime, where ranks and top- K K membership are determined by many dependent score-gap hypotheses. Using Gaussian and multiplier-bootstrap calibration, we obtain simultaneous confidence sets for per-task ranks and valid tests for top- K K membership across many tasks and models. Experiments on synthetic data and Arena comparisons show that low-rank sharing improves sample efficiency over independent task-wise Bradley–Terry estimation and enables uncertainty-aware task-specific certification.

Introduction

Pairwise human-preference evaluation has become a central tool for comparing large
language models (LLMs). Platforms such as Chatbot Arena

(
13
)
collect side-by-side comparisons of model responses and
aggregate them into public leaderboards, providing a scalable alternative to fixed
benchmark scores. At the same time, modern LLM evaluation is increasingly
task-specific, with benchmarks and evaluation platforms reporting performance separately
for coding, mathematical reasoning, instruction following, multilingual tasks, creative
writing, and other fine-grained categories

(
25
;
19
;
23
)
. This task-specific view
is essential because model strengths are heterogeneous: a model that is strong overall
may not be the best choice for a particular task, user group, or deployment domain.

The central statistical problem is therefore not only to estimate model abilities, but
to make reliable task-specific ranking decisions. Practitioners often want to know
which models can be trusted as top performers for a task, whether an apparent difference
between two models is statistically significant, and which leaderboard claims remain
valid after many comparisons are considered simultaneously. A point leaderboard alone
cannot answer these questions. When comparisons are sparse or imbalanced, especially
within fine-grained task categories, small estimated score gaps near the top-
K

boundary may simply reflect sampling noise. Thus task-specific LLM evaluation requires
uncertainty-aware ranking: confidence statements for ranks, top-
K
membership, and
task-specific model comparisons.

Existing approaches leave a gap. Independent task-wise Bradley-Terry-Luce (BTL)
estimation
(
5
;
22
)
respects task heterogeneity, but
can be statistically unstable when each task receives limited or uneven comparisons.
Fully pooling data across tasks reduces variance, but erases the task-specific
heterogeneity that motivates fine-grained evaluation in the first place. Recent
low-rank approaches provide an attractive compromise by sharing information across
related tasks, but smooth score estimation or inference for fixed functionals does not
by itself solve the ranking problem. Ranks and top-
K
membership are nonsmooth
functionals determined by many dependent score-gap signs. Valid task-specific
leaderboards therefore require new tools for boundary-sensitive top-
K
recovery,
multiple testing over correlated score gaps, and simultaneous rank certification.

We develop a statistical framework for
certified task-specific leaderboards
from
sparse pairwise comparisons. Let

\Theta^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{t}\times d_{m}}
denote the latent task-by-model ability
matrix, where
\Theta^{\star}_{t,m}
is the score of model
m
on task
t
. Each
observation consists of a task
t
, two models
m,m^{\prime}
, and a binary preference
outcome following a BTL-type model that depends on the score difference

\Theta^{\star}_{t,m}-\Theta^{\star}_{t,m^{\prime}}
. We assume that
\Theta^{\star}
is
approximately low rank, reflecting shared latent capabilities, such as reasoning,
instruction following, or style sensitivity, across tasks. This setting is more
challenging than standard low-rank matrix completion

(
6
;
24
;
14
)
, because each
observation is a binary, within-task comparison depending only on a score difference,
rather than a noisy observation of an individual matrix entry. More importantly, the
main target is not merely low-rank score estimation, but statistically valid ranking and
certification under sparse, non-uniform, and dependent pairwise comparisons.

Moving from score estimation to ranking certification introduces additional
difficulties. First, top-
K
accuracy
requires entrywise control of task-model scores, rather than only Frobenius or
prediction-error accuracy. Second, rank and top-
K
decisions are determined by the
signs of many score gaps, so uncertainty quantification naturally becomes a multiple
hypothesis testing problem. Third, these score-gap statistics are strongly dependent:
they share tasks, models, comparisons, and low-rank latent factors. As a result,
pointwise confidence intervals or independent testing corrections are not sufficient for
valid leaderboard-level guarantees.

Related work and positioning.

Arena-style LLM evaluation.

Arena-style platforms have made pairwise human-preference comparison a standard tool
for evaluating LLMs
(
13
;
1
;
2
)
.
Modern leaderboards increasingly report category-specific results for coding, math,
creative writing, and instruction following, and hard prompts
(

)
,
reflecting substantial task-level heterogeneity. Recent work further studies
prompt-dependent leaderboards and routing:
19
learn
prompt-dependent Bradley-Terry coefficients, while

provide
uncertainty quantification for prompt-dependent rankings. These works motivate
fine-grained and uncertainty-aware evaluation, but independent category- or prompt-wise
ranking does not share information across related tasks and can be inefficient under
sparse comparisons. Our framework uses low-rank structure to share information across
tasks while preserving task-specific rankings and providing simultaneous uncertainty
statements for ranks and top-
K
membership.

Pairwise ranking, uncertainty quantification, and low-rank inference.

Our theory builds on pairwise ranking and top-
K
recovery under BTL-type models

(
5
;
22
;
20
;
9
;
7
)
, as well as recent uncertainty quantification for
sparse, covariate-assisted, and heterogeneous ranking models

(
16
;
17
;
18
)
. Related semiparametric ideas have also begun to appear in LLM evaluation, for example
through the use of auxiliary comparison signals to improve efficiency

(
15
)
. Closest to our work,

21
study low-rank LLM evaluation and semiparametric efficiency
for smooth functionals of a latent score tensor. In contrast, our paper focuses on
task-specific ranks and top-
K
membership, which are nonsmooth ranking functionals

ranking-specific: boundary-based top-

score-gap hypotheses, and simultaneous rank/top-
K
certification. These ingredients
allow us to move from efficient score estimation to certified task-specific
leaderboards.

2
Problem Setup

We formalize task-specific LLM evaluation as a sparse pairwise-comparison problem.
There are
d_{\mathrm{t}}
task categories and
d_{\mathrm{m}}
candidate models. For each task-model
pair, let
\Theta^{\star}_{t,m}
denote the latent ability score of model
m
on task

t
, and collect these scores in the matrix

\Theta^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{t}}\times d_{\mathrm{m}}}.

The row index
t\in[d_{\mathrm{t}}]
represents a task category and the column index

m\in[d_{\mathrm{m}}]
represents an LLM. We assume that
\Theta^{\star}
is approximately low
rank, with rank
r\ll\min\{d_{\mathrm{t}},d_{\mathrm{m}}\}
and singular value decomposition

\Theta^{\star}=U^{\star}\Sigma^{\star}(V^{\star})^{\top}.

Let
\sigma_{1}^{\star}\geq\cdots\geq\sigma_{r}^{\star}>0
denote the nonzero singular
values collected in
\Sigma^{\star}
, and define the
condition number
\kappa:=\sigma_{1}^{\star}/\sigma_{r}^{\star}
.
This low-rank structure allows information to be shared across related tasks while preserving
task-specific model rankings. Let
e_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{t}}}
and

e_{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{m}}}
denote the standard basis vectors for task
t

and model
m
, respectively. We assume the singular vectors are
\mu
-incoherent:

\max_{t\in[d_{\mathrm{t}}]}\|e_{t}^{\top}U^{\star}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{\mu r}{d_{\mathrm{t}}},\qquad\max_{m\in[d_{\mathrm{m}}]}\|e_{m}^{\top}V^{\star}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{\mu r}{d_{\mathrm{m}}}.

Observation model.

For
i=1,\ldots,n
, a task
t_{i}\in[d_{\mathrm{t}}]
is sampled from a distribution
\nu
.
Conditional on
t_{i}
, a pair of distinct models
(m_{i},m_{i}^{\prime})
is sampled from a task-dependent
distribution
\pi_{t_{i}}
. We let
Y_{i}=1
indicate that model
m_{i}
is preferred to model
m_{i}^{\prime}
. Define the signed comparison design matrix

X_{i}:=e_{t_{i}}(e_{m_{i}}-e_{m_{i}^{\prime}})^{\top}.

It has a single nonzero row, corresponding to task
t_{i}
, with
+1
in
column
m_{i}
and
-1
in column
m_{i}^{\prime}
. Hence

\langle X_{i},\Theta^{\star}\rangle=\Theta^{\star}_{t_{i},m_{i}}-\Theta^{\star}_{t_{i},m_{i}^{\prime}}
. Conditional on
X_{i}
, the preference follows a BTL model
(
5
;
22
)

\Pr(Y_{i}=1\mid X_{i})=\sigma(\langle X_{i},\Theta^{\star}\rangle)=\sigma\!\left(\Theta^{\star}_{t_{i},m_{i}}-\Theta^{\star}_{t_{i},m_{i}^{\prime}}\right),\qquad\sigma(x)=(1+e^{-x})^{-1}.

Because pairwise comparisons depend only on score differences, the matrix

\Theta^{\star}
is identifiable only up to task-specific additive shifts. We fix a
representative by imposing the row-centering constraint

\Theta^{\star}\mathbf{1}_{d_{\mathrm{m}}}=0.

Sampling design.

We allow the task and model-pair sampling distributions to be non-uniform, reflecting
the uneven traffic patterns of real evaluation platforms. To obtain clean theoretical
rates, we assume this imbalance is controlled: there exist constants

0<c_{\nu}\leq C_{\nu}<\infty
and
0<c_{\pi}\leq C_{\pi}<\infty
, independent of

d_{\mathrm{t}},d_{\mathrm{m}},n
, such that for all
t\in[d_{\mathrm{t}}]
and unordered pairs

\{m,m^{\prime}\}\subset[d_{\mathrm{m}}]
,

\frac{c_{\nu}}{d_{\mathrm{t}}}\leq\nu_{t}\leq\frac{C_{\nu}}{d_{\mathrm{t}}},\qquad\frac{c_{\pi}}{\binom{d_{\mathrm{m}}}{2}}\leq\pi_{t}(\{m,m^{\prime}\})\leq\frac{C_{\pi}}{\binom{d_{\mathrm{m}}}{2}}.

Together with the bounded-signal condition
\|\Theta^{\star}\|_{\infty}\leq B
, the above
sampling assumptions ensure that every task and every model pair receives comparable statistical information. Indeed, writing

\eta=\Theta^{\star}_{t,m}-\Theta^{\star}_{t,m^{\prime}}

for a generic within-task score difference, the bounded-signal condition implies

|\eta|\leq 2B
. Hence the BTL Fisher information

I(\eta):=\sigma(\eta)\{1-\sigma(\eta)\}

is bounded away from zero and infinity, as in standard bounded dynamic-range assumptions
for BTL-type ranking models

(
9
;
7
;
16
;
17
)
. The lower
bounds on
\nu_{t}
and
\pi_{t}(\{m,m^{\prime}\})
prevent any task or pairwise comparison
direction from being asymptotically unobserved, in the same spirit as standard sampling conditions in low-rank matrix completion and one-bit matrix estimation

(
6
;
24
;
14
)
.

Ranking targets.

Built on the entrywise error bound for the latent score matrix, Section
3

studies task-wise top-
K
accuracy for

\mathcal{S}^{\star}_{K}(t):=\{m:\mathrm{rk}_{t}(m)\leq K\},

where
\mathrm{rk}_{t}(m)
is model
m
’s rank on task
t
. Section
4

develops efficient inference and multiple testing tools for task-specific score gaps

\Theta^{\star}_{t,m}-\Theta^{\star}_{t,m^{\prime}}
, and Section
5
converts these
gap inferences into simultaneous rank confidence sets and top-
K
membership
certificates.

3
Entrywise Estimation and Task-Wise Top-
K
Accuracy

This section establishes the first step toward certified task-specific leaderboards:
task-wise top-
K
accuracy from sparse pairwise comparisons.

Low-rank score estimator.

Let
X_{i}=e_{t_{i}}(e_{m_{i}}-e_{m_{i}^{\prime}})^{\top}
and

\ell(y,\eta)=\log(1+\exp(\eta))-y\eta
. We compute a nuclear-norm penalized BTL
initializer

\widehat{\Theta}_{0}\in\arg\min_{\Theta\in\mathcal{C}_{B}}\left\{\frac{1}{|I_{0}|}\sum_{i\in I_{0}}\ell(Y_{i},\langle X_{i},\Theta\rangle)+\lambda\|\Theta\|_{*}\right\},\qquad\mathcal{C}_{B}=\{\Theta:\Theta\mathbf{1}_{d_{\mathrm{m}}}=0,\ \|\Theta\|_{\infty}\leq B\}.

This convex program provides a Frobenius-accurate initializer. In particular, Theorem
B.5
in Appendix shows that, with high probability,

\|\widehat{\Theta}_{0}-\Theta^{\star}\|_{F}\lesssim\sqrt{{r\,\bar{d}^{3}\,\mathrm{polylog}(\bar{d})}/{n_{0}}},
where
\bar{d}:=\max\{d_{\mathrm{t}},d_{\mathrm{m}}\}
and
n_{0}=|I_{0}|
.
Such a global error guarantee is sufficient for initialization, but it is not enough for ranking: top-
K
recovery depends on individual score gaps, especially near the
K
-versus-
(K+1)
boundary, and therefore requires entrywise control of the score matrix. To obtain this stronger guarantee, starting from the rank-
r
SVD of
\widehat{\Theta}_{0}
, we apply a row-wise pairwise-logistic refinement,

\widehat{\Theta}=\mathsf{Refine}_{r}(\widehat{\Theta}_{0}).

At a high level, the refinement first constructs an estimated right factor from the
initializer, then updates each task-side latent vector by solving a pairwise-logistic
score equation conditional on this right factor. After re-centering to enforce the
row-sum gauge, it performs an analogous model-side update and returns the resulting
rank-
r
, row-centered matrix. Thus the refinement converts the global Frobenius
initializer into an entrywise-accurate estimator tailored to task-specific ranking. The full refinement steps and
proof are deferred to Appendix
B
.

Theorem 3.1

(Uniform entrywise estimation)

Under the model and near-uniform sampling assumptions in
Section
2
, suppose
\Theta^{\star}
has rank
r
, is

\mu
-incoherent, satisfies
\|\Theta^{\star}\|_{\infty}\leq B
, and has condition
number
\kappa
. Denote
\bar{d}:=\max\{d_{\mathrm{t}},d_{\mathrm{m}}\}
. If the pairwise comparison sample size

n\gtrsim\mathrm{poly}(\mu,r,\kappa,B)\,\bar{d}\,\log^{c}(n\bar{d}),

then, for some large constant
a
, with probability at least
1-n^{-a}
,

\|\widehat{\Theta}-\Theta^{\star}\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon_{n},\textrm{~~with~~}\varepsilon_{n}:=C\,\mathrm{poly}(a,\mu,r,\kappa,B)\sqrt{\frac{\bar{d}\,\log^{c}(n\bar{d})}{n}}.

Theorem
3.1
is the main estimation result. The nuclear-norm optimization provides a Frobenius-accurate
initializer, while the refinement step upgrades this global accuracy to uniform
max-norm control over all task-model scores. For constant rank and condition number, the required sample size is
near-linear in
\bar{d}
, so the method learns all task-specific scores jointly rather
than fitting
d_{t}
unrelated BTL models. In the balanced regime
d_{\mathrm{t}}\asymp d_{\mathrm{m}}\asymp d
, this is

\|\widehat{\Theta}-\Theta^{\star}\|_{\infty}\lesssim\sqrt{d\,\mathrm{polylog}(nd)/n}.

Task-wise top-
K
accuracy.

We next translate the entrywise error bound into a task-specific top-
K
recovery
guarantee. Denote the estimated top-
K
set for task
t
as

\widehat{\mathcal{S}^{\star}_{K}}(t)=\{m:\widehat{\mathrm{rk}}_{t}(m)\leq K\},

where the rank
\widehat{\mathrm{rk}}_{t}(m)

is computed from the refined estimate
\widehat{\Theta}_{t,m}
. To measure the
discrepancy between the estimated and true top-
K
sets, define the normalized Hamming
error

\mathsf{Ham}_{K,t}:=\frac{1}{2K}|\widehat{\mathcal{S}^{\star}_{K}}(t)\triangle\mathcal{S}^{\star}_{K}(t)|.

Here
\triangle
denotes symmetric difference: it counts models that are included in
one top-
K
set but not the other. The normalization by
2K
makes

\mathsf{Ham}_{K,t}\in[0,1]
.

The difficulty of top-
K
recovery depends on how many models have scores close to
the top-
K
cutoff. Let

\Theta^{\star}_{t,(1)}\geq\cdots\geq\Theta^{\star}_{t,(d_{\mathrm{m}})}

be the sorted true scores for task
t
, and define the midpoint between the
K
-th
and
(K+1)
-st scores as

\tau_{K}(t):=(\Theta^{\star}_{t,(K)}+\Theta^{\star}_{t,(K+1)})/2.

We call
\tau_{K}(t)
the top-
K
decision boundary. For a resolution level

\delta>0
, define the boundary profile

\mathcal{R}_{K,t}(\delta;\Theta^{\star}):=\frac{1}{2K}\bigl|\{m:|\Theta^{\star}_{t,m}-\tau_{K}(t)|\leq\delta\}\bigr|.

This quantity measures the fraction of models lying within distance
\delta
of the
top-
K
boundary. If many models lie near
\tau_{K}(t)
, then the task is intrinsically
hard to rank because small estimation errors can swap models across the top-
K

cutoff. If few models lie near the boundary, top-
K
recovery is easier.

Proposition 3.2

(Task-wise top-
K
Hamming accuracy)

On the event
\|\widehat{\Theta}-\Theta^{\star}\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon_{n}
, for every

t\in[d_{\mathrm{t}}]
,

\mathsf{Ham}_{K,t}\leq\mathcal{R}_{K,t}(2\varepsilon_{n};\Theta^{\star}).

Therefore, under Theorem
3.1
, the above Hamming bound holds
simultaneously for all tasks with probability at least
1-n^{-a}
.

Proposition
3.2
shows that top-
K
mistakes can only occur for
models whose true scores lie within the statistical resolution
2\varepsilon_{n}
of
the top-
K
boundary. This extends the boundary-resolution principle from single-task
BTL top-
K
ranking
(
9
;
7
)
to many task-specific
rankings coupled through shared low-rank structure. Consequently, exact recovery follows under a task-specific margin condition. Define the
K
-gap

\Delta_{K}(t):=\Theta^{\star}_{t,(K)}-\Theta^{\star}_{t,(K+1)}.

If
\Delta_{K}(t)>4\varepsilon_{n}
, then no model can cross the top-
K
boundary, so

\widehat{\mathcal{S}^{\star}_{K}}(t)=\mathcal{S}^{\star}_{K}(t).

Thus, with high probability, exact top-
K
recovery holds
simultaneously for every task whose
K
-gap exceeds
4\varepsilon_{n}
. Hence, low-rank sharing enables simultaneous task-wise top-
K
recovery at the same
entrywise resolution as score estimation, without requiring each task to be estimated
independently.

4
Score-Gap Inference and Multiple Testing Foundations

Task-specific ranking decisions are built from score-gap signs. For example, deciding
whether model
m
outranks model
m^{\prime}
on task
t
requires inference on

\Theta^{\star}_{t,m}-\Theta^{\star}_{t,m^{\prime}}.

Similarly, ranks and top-
K
membership are determined by many such pairwise gaps.
We therefore
first develop efficient inference for score gaps and characterize their joint dependence,
which provides the multiple-testing foundations used later for simultaneous ranking
inference.

Score-gap contrasts.

We write a generic linear contrast as

\psi_{\Gamma}(\Theta^{\star})=\left\langle\Gamma,\,\Theta^{\star}\right\rangle.

The canonical example is

\Gamma=e_{t}(e_{m}-e_{m^{\prime}})^{\top}
, for which

\psi_{\Gamma}(\Theta^{\star})=\Theta^{\star}_{t,m}-\Theta^{\star}_{t,m^{\prime}}.

Testing whether model
m
is better than model
m^{\prime}
on task
t
is therefore a
test on the sign of
\psi_{\Gamma}(\Theta^{\star})
. Multiple ranking claims correspond to
testing many such contrasts jointly.

Efficient one-step estimator.

In the low-rank model, local perturbations of
\Theta^{\star}
must lie in the tangent
space
\mathbb{T}
of the rank-
r
, row-centered manifold. Therefore only the
projected contrast direction
P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma
is locally identifiable. Define
the Fisher operator
G
by

\left\langle GH_{1},\,H_{2}\right\rangle=\mathbb{E}\!\left[I(\left\langle X,\,\Theta^{\star}\right\rangle)\left\langle H_{1},\,X\right\rangle\left\langle H_{2},\,X\right\rangle\right],\qquad I(\eta)=\sigma(\eta)\{1-\sigma(\eta)\}.

It measures how much information the pairwise-comparison design carries about
directions
H_{1},H_{2}
. Closely matched comparisons have larger
I(\eta)
, while
lopsided comparisons carry less information.

Define the restricted Fisher information operator
A
, the efficient direction

H_{\Gamma}^{\star}
, and the corresponding efficient variance
V_{\rm eff}(\Gamma)
by

A:=P_{\mathbb{T}}GP_{\mathbb{T}},\qquad H_{\Gamma}^{\star}:=A^{-1}P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma,\qquad V_{\rm eff}(\Gamma):=\left\langle P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma,\,A^{-1}P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma\right\rangle.

The operator
A
describes how much information the observed pairwise comparisons
carry about locally admissible low-rank perturbations of
\Theta^{\star}
. The efficient
direction
H_{\Gamma}^{\star}
is the optimal weighting direction for converting
comparison residuals into an estimate of the target contrast
\psi_{\Gamma}
. The
variance
V_{\rm eff}(\Gamma)
is the resulting semiparametric efficiency bound: it
is the smallest achievable asymptotic variance for regular estimators of

\psi_{\Gamma}
, accounting for the sampling design, the BTL Fisher information, and
the low-rank constraint.

Given an entrywise-accurate estimator
\widehat{\Theta}
from
Section
3
, we estimate
P_{\mathbb{T}}
,
G
, and

H_{\Gamma}^{\star}
by
\widehat{P}_{\mathbb{T}}
,
\widehat{G}
, and

\widehat{H}_{\Gamma}
, where

(\widehat{P}_{\mathbb{T}}\widehat{G}\widehat{P}_{\mathbb{T}})\widehat{H}_{\Gamma}=\widehat{P}_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma.

This leads to the final one-step efficient estimator

\widehat{\psi}_{\Gamma}=\left\langle\Gamma,\,\widehat{\Theta}\right\rangle+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}s(Y_{i},\widehat{\eta}_{i})\left\langle\widehat{H}_{\Gamma},\,X_{i}\right\rangle,\textrm{~with~}\widehat{\eta}_{i}=\left\langle X_{i},\,\widehat{\Theta}\right\rangle\textrm{~and~}s(y,\eta)=y-\sigma(\eta).

The second term of
\widehat{\psi}_{\Gamma}
debiases the plug-in estimator

\left\langle\Gamma,\,\widehat{\Theta}\right\rangle
and yields efficient score-gap inference.

Joint inference for multiple score gaps.

For ranking, we need joint inference for many score gaps, not just one. For a fixed
collection
\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{q}
, let

\psi_{j}=\psi_{\Gamma_{j}}(\Theta^{\star})
. The efficient covariance between the
corresponding one-step estimators is

\Sigma_{jk}=\left\langle P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma_{j},\,A^{-1}P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma_{k}\right\rangle.

This covariance is generally non-diagonal because two score gaps may share a task, a
model, observed comparisons, or low-rank latent factors. Capturing this dependence is
essential for multiple testing: treating correlated score-gap tests as independent can
miscalibrate leaderboard-level uncertainty.

Theorem 4.1

(Efficient joint score-gap inference)

Under the assumptions of
Theorem
3.1
and the regularity conditions in
Appendix
D
, there exists
Z_{\Gamma}\sim N(0,\Sigma)
such that

\sup_{B\in\mathcal{R}_{q}}\left|\Pr\!\left\{\sqrt{n}(\widehat{\psi}_{1}-\psi_{1},\ldots,\widehat{\psi}_{q}-\psi_{q})\in B\right\}-\Pr\{Z_{\Gamma}\in B\}\right|\lesssim C_{A}\sqrt{\frac{\bar{d}\,\log^{c}(n\bar{d})}{n}}.

Here
\mathcal{R}_{q}
is the class of rectangles in
\mathbb{R}^{q}
, and
C_{A}
is the
inverse-information stability factor controlling the
\ell_{\infty}\to\ell_{\infty}

size of
A^{-1}
. Consequently, if

C_{A}\sqrt{\bar{d}\,\log^{c}(n\bar{d})/n}\to 0
, then

\sqrt{n}(\widehat{\psi}_{1}-\psi_{1},\ldots,\widehat{\psi}_{q}-\psi_{q})\rightsquigarrow N(0,\Sigma).

Moreover, the following empirical influence-function covariance is consistent for
\Sigma_{jk}

\widehat{\Sigma}_{jk}=n^{-1}\sum_{i}\widehat{\phi}_{j}(W_{i})\widehat{\phi}_{k}(W_{i}),\qquad\widehat{\phi}_{j}(W_{i})=s(Y_{i},\widehat{\eta}_{i})\left\langle\widehat{H}_{\Gamma_{j}},\,X_{i}\right\rangle.

Theorem
4.1
yields confidence intervals and joint tests for fixed
collections of score-gap hypotheses. For a single gap, with

\widehat{\operatorname{SE}}_{\Gamma}=(\widehat{V}_{\Gamma}/n)^{1/2}
, a pointwise

(1-\alpha)
-confidence interval is

\widehat{\psi}_{\Gamma}\pm z_{1-\alpha/2}\widehat{\operatorname{SE}}_{\Gamma}.

For multiple gaps,
\widehat{\Sigma}
captures their dependence, which is crucial for
valid joint testing of overlapping ranking claims. The covariance
\Sigma
is also the
semiparametric efficiency lower bound: any regular asymptotically Gaussian estimator
has limiting covariance no smaller than
\Sigma
in the positive-semidefinite order,
and the proposed one-step estimator attains this bound.

For ranking, however, the relevant family of score gaps grows with the number of models
and tasks. Thus fixed-dimensional inference must be strengthened to a uniform expansion
over growing collections of score-gap contrasts.

Uniform expansion for ranking tests.

For growing collections of score-gap contrasts, our analysis gives the uniform expansion

\sqrt{n}(\widehat{\psi}_{j}-\psi_{j})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{j}(W_{i})+r_{j},\qquad\max_{j}|r_{j}|\lesssim C_{A}\sqrt{\frac{\bar{d}\,\log^{c}(n\bar{d})}{n}},

with high probability. This expansion reduces simultaneous testing of correlated score
gaps to a high-dimensional Gaussian approximation problem. Together with Theorem
4.1
, it provides the technical foundation
for the rank and top-
K
certification procedure developed next.

5
Simultaneous Rank and Top-
K
Certification

We now convert score-gap inference into leaderboard-level certification. Fix a task

t
and a model
m
. The rank of
m
on task
t
is determined by the signs of
the
d_{\mathrm{m}}-1
score gaps

\Delta_{t,\ell}^{(m)}:=\Theta^{\star}_{t,\ell}-\Theta^{\star}_{t,m}=\psi_{\Gamma_{t,\ell}^{(m)}}(\Theta^{\star}),\,\ell\neq m,

with
\Gamma_{t,\ell}^{(m)}:=e_{t}(e_{\ell}-e_{m})^{\top}
, since

\mathrm{rk}_{t}(m)=1+\sum_{\ell\neq m}\mathbf{1}\{\Delta_{t,\ell}^{(m)}>0\}.

Thus rank inference is a multiple-testing problem over the competitors of
m
: each
positive gap corresponds to one model ranked above
m
. The goal is not only to test
one gap at a time, but to produce a confidence set for the rank and a certified
top-
K
membership decision.

Simultaneous score-gap bands.

For each
\ell\neq m
, denote the
one-step estimator from Section
4
as
\widehat{\Delta}_{t,\ell}^{(m)}:=\widehat{\psi}_{\Gamma_{t,\ell}^{(m)}}
, with estimated influence-function summand
and standard error

\widehat{\phi}_{t,\ell}^{(m)}(W_{i}):=s(Y_{i},\widehat{\eta}_{i})\left\langle\widehat{H}_{\Gamma_{t,\ell}^{(m)}},\,X_{i}\right\rangle,\qquad\widehat{\sigma}_{t,\ell}^{(m)}:=\left\{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bigl(\widehat{\phi}_{t,\ell}^{(m)}(W_{i})\bigr)^{2}\right\}^{1/2}.

A pointwise interval for each gap is not enough: the rank depends on all

d_{\mathrm{m}}-1
gap signs jointly, and these gap estimators are correlated through shared
tasks, models, comparisons, and low-rank factors.
We need a joint calibration that accounts for this covariance structure. Equivalently, simultaneous confidence bands require controlling the maximum studentized error over all competitors,

\max_{\ell\neq m}\left|\frac{\sqrt{n}(\widehat{\Delta}_{t,\ell}^{(m)}-\Delta_{t,\ell}^{(m)})}{\widehat{\sigma}_{t,\ell}^{(m)}}\right|.

Draw i.i.d. multipliers
\xi_{i}\sim N(0,1)
and define the multiplier-bootstrap
statistic

T_{t,m}^{\ast}:=\max_{\ell\neq m}\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i}\frac{\widehat{\phi}_{t,\ell}^{(m)}(W_{i})}{\widehat{\sigma}_{t,\ell}^{(m)}}\right|.

Conditional on the data, this bootstrap process preserves the empirical dependence
among the gap statistics. Let
c_{t,m}(1-\alpha)
be the conditional

(1-\alpha)
-quantile of
T_{t,m}^{\ast}
. We form simultaneous confidence bands

\widehat{I}_{t,\ell}^{(m)}:=\left[\widehat{\Delta}_{t,\ell}^{(m)}\pm c_{t,m}(1-\alpha)\frac{\widehat{\sigma}_{t,\ell}^{(m)}}{\sqrt{n}}\right]=[\widehat{L}_{t,\ell}^{(m)},\widehat{U}_{t,\ell}^{(m)}].

The critical value is the quantile of the maximum of many correlated studentized
errors, not the usual pointwise Gaussian quantile. Under the uniform expansion from
Section
4
, standard high-dimensional Gaussian approximation and multiplier
bootstrap theory justify this calibration
(
10
;
12
)
.

Rank confidence band.

On the simultaneous coverage event, the score-gap bands can be inverted into a rank
confidence band. Define

A_{t}(m):=\#\{\ell\neq m:\widehat{L}_{t,\ell}^{(m)}>0\},\qquad B_{t}(m):=\#\{\ell\neq m:\widehat{U}_{t,\ell}^{(m)}<0\}.

Here
A_{t}(m)
is the number of competitors certified to be above
m
, and

B_{t}(m)
is the number certified to be below
m
. The remaining competitors have
confidence bands crossing zero and are therefore unresolved. Thus a valid confidence
band for the rank is

\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{t}(m):=[\,1+A_{t}(m),\ d_{\mathrm{m}}-B_{t}(m)\,].

Theorem 5.1

(Rank confidence band for one task)

Under the assumptions of Theorem
4.1
, the uniform remainder condition
from Section
4
, and the high-dimensional Gaussian approximation conditions
in Appendix
E
,

\Pr\{\mathrm{rk}_{t}(m)\in\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{t}(m)\}\geq 1-\alpha-o(1).

Top-
K
membership certification.

The rank band directly yields a three-way top-
K
decision. If

d_{\mathrm{m}}-B_{t}(m)\leq K,

then even the worst rank compatible with the confidence bands is at most
K
, so we
certify
m\in\mathcal{S}^{\star}_{K}(t)
. If

1+A_{t}(m)>K,

then even the best compatible rank is larger than
K
, so we certify

m\notin\mathcal{S}^{\star}_{K}(t)
. Otherwise, the membership decision is statistically unresolved. In LLM evaluation, this three-way output:
\{\textit{certified top-}K,\,\textit{certified non-top-}K,\,\textit{unresolved}\}

separates reliable
leaderboard claims from comparisons that remain too noisy to certify.

Simultaneous inference across tasks.

The same construction can be applied simultaneously across tasks. For a fixed model

m
, replace the one-task family
\{\ell:\ell\neq m\}
by

\mathcal{J}(m):=\{(t,\ell):t\in[d_{\mathrm{t}}],\ \ell\neq m\},\qquad|\mathcal{J}(m)|=d_{\mathrm{t}}(d_{\mathrm{m}}-1).

We compute the multiplier-bootstrap critical value for the maximum over

\mathcal{J}(m)
, and use the resulting bands to construct

\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{t}(m)
for every task
t
. Since
|\mathcal{J}(m)|
is
polynomial in
\bar{d}
, the same high-dimensional Gaussian approximation applies up
to logarithmic factors.

Corollary 5.2

(Simultaneous task-wise rank inference)

Under the conditions of Theorem
5.1
, with the bootstrap maximum
taken over
\mathcal{J}(m)
,

\Pr\left\{\mathrm{rk}_{t}(m)\in\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{t}(m)\ \text{for all }t\in[d_{\mathrm{t}}]\right\}\geq 1-\alpha-o(1).

Consequently, the certified top-
K
, certified non-top-
K
, and unresolved
decisions for model
m
are simultaneously valid across all tasks.

Appendix
E.10
extends the same score-gap band inversion to the entire
task-specific top-
K
set, producing inner and outer confidence sets satisfying

\widehat{\mathcal{S}^{\star}_{K}}_{\rm in}(t)\subseteq\mathcal{S}^{\star}_{K}(t)\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal{S}^{\star}_{K}}_{\rm out}(t),

simultaneously over tasks.

7
Conclusion

We develop a statistical framework for uncertainty-aware task-specific LLM ranking from sparse pairwise comparisons using low-rank structure to share information across related tasks while preserving task-level heterogeneity, yielding entrywise score control, task-wise top-K recovery guarantees, efficient score-gap inference, and simultaneous rank/top-K certification. Experiments on synthetic data and LM Arena comparisons show that low-rank sharing improves sample efficiency over independent per-task Bradley–Terry estimation and produces tighter, better-calibrated ranking certificates in sparse regimes.

Broader Impact

Task-specific LLM rankings increasingly influence model selection, procurement, deployment, and public perceptions of model capability. By reporting uncertainty-aware rank and top-K certificates, our framework helps reduce overconfident claims based on sparse or imbalanced preference data and makes clear when apparent leaderboard differences are statistically unresolved.

Appendix A
Notation, assumptions, and master good event

This appendix collects the notation, assumptions, and probability
calibrations used throughout
Appendices
B
–
E.10
. All assumptions
in this appendix are stated in the matrix
(d_{\mathrm{t}}\times d_{\mathrm{m}})
form and are
the matrix specialization of the assumptions used in the prior
efficient-inference paper of
21
.

A.1
Notation

We retain the notation of Section
2
. The latent ability
matrix is
\Theta^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{t}}\times d_{\mathrm{m}}}
, row-centered
(
\Theta^{\star}\mathbf{1}_{d_{\mathrm{m}}}=0
), of rank
r
with reduced singular value
decomposition

\Theta^{\star}=U^{\star}\Sigma^{\star}(V^{\star})^{\top},\qquad U^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{t}}\times r},\ V^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{m}}\times r},\ \Sigma^{\star}=\mathrm{diag}(\sigma_{1}^{\star},\ldots,\sigma_{r}^{\star}).

The singular vectors are
\mu
-incoherent, the condition number is

\kappa:=\sigma_{1}^{\star}/\sigma_{r}^{\star}
, and the entrywise bound is

\left\lVert\Theta^{\star}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq B
. We write

\bar{d}:=\max(d_{\mathrm{t}},d_{\mathrm{m}})
for the maximum mode dimension and

d^{\star}:=d_{\mathrm{t}}d_{\mathrm{m}}
for the ambient cardinality of the matrix. The

effective comparison dimension
, which appears throughout the
analysis as the natural normalization for pairwise contrasts, is

d^{\star}_{\rm eff}:=\frac{d_{\mathrm{t}}\,(d_{\mathrm{m}}-1)}{2}\asymp\frac{d_{\mathrm{t}}\,d_{\mathrm{m}}}{2}=\frac{d^{\star}}{2},

and we will use
d^{\star}
and
d^{\star}_{\rm eff}
interchangeably up to
the absolute constant
2
.

Design and observation model.

For each round
i\in[n]
, the design tensor is

X_{i}:=e_{t_{i}}(e_{m_{i}}-e_{m_{i}^{\prime}})^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{t}}\times d_{\mathrm{m}}}
, where the
task index
t_{i}\in[d_{\mathrm{t}}]
is sampled from a distribution
\nu
and
the unordered model pair
\{m_{i},m_{i}^{\prime}\}\subset[d_{\mathrm{m}}]
is sampled from a
task-dependent distribution
\pi_{t_{i}}
. The predictor is

\eta_{i}^{\star}:=\left\langle X_{i},\,\Theta^{\star}\right\rangle=\Theta^{\star}_{t_{i},m_{i}}-\Theta^{\star}_{t_{i},m_{i}^{\prime}}
,
and the observation is
Y_{i}\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(\sigma(\eta_{i}^{\star}))

with
\sigma(x):=(1+e^{-x})^{-1}
the logistic link. The pairwise score
and Fisher weight are

s_{\eta}(y,\eta):=\partial_{\eta}\ell(y,\eta)=y-\sigma(\eta),\qquad I(\eta):=\mathbb{E}[s_{\eta}(Y,\eta)^{2}\mid\eta]=\sigma(\eta)\{1-\sigma(\eta)\}\in(0,1/4].

Under
\left\lVert\Theta^{\star}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq B
, the predictor satisfies

|\eta^{\star}|\leq 2B
, and consequently the Fisher information is
bounded above and below: there exist constants

0<c_{B}\leq C_{B}<\infty
depending only on
B
such that

c_{B}\leq I(\eta^{\star})\leq C_{B}
almost surely.

Tangent space and operators.

The signal tangent space at
\Theta^{\star}
intersected with the
row-centering identification constraint is

\mathbb{T}:=\{U^{\star}A^{\top}+Q\,C\,(V^{\star})^{\top}:\,A\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{m}}\times r},\ C\in\mathbb{R}^{(d_{\mathrm{t}}-1)\times r}\},

where
Q\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{t}}\times(d_{\mathrm{t}}-1)}
has orthonormal columns spanning

\mathbf{1}_{d_{\mathrm{t}}}^{\perp}
. The orthogonal projector onto
\mathbb{T}

is denoted
P_{\mathbb{T}}
; its closed-form representation is given in
Appendix
D.4
. Following

21
, the Fisher (information) operator

G:\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{t}}\times d_{\mathrm{m}}}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{t}}\times d_{\mathrm{m}}}
is defined by

\left\langle GU,\,V\right\rangle:=\mathbb{E}^{\star}\!\left[I(\eta^{\star})\left\langle U,\,X\right\rangle\left\langle V,\,X\right\rangle\right],\qquad U,V\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{t}}\times d_{\mathrm{m}}},

(1)

and the restricted information operator on the tangent space is

A:=P_{\mathbb{T}}GP_{\mathbb{T}}:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{T}
.
For any contrast
\Gamma\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{t}}\times d_{\mathrm{m}}}
, the efficient direction
is
H_{\Gamma}^{\star}:=A^{-1}P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma
and the efficient
influence function is

\phi_{\Gamma}(W_{i}):=s_{\eta}(Y_{i},\eta_{i}^{\star})\left\langle H_{\Gamma}^{\star},\,X_{i}\right\rangle,\qquad V_{\rm eff}(\Gamma):=\mathbb{E}^{\star}[\phi_{\Gamma}^{2}]=\left\langle P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma,\,A^{-1}P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma\right\rangle.

Plug-in operators.

Given an initial estimator
\widehat{\Theta}
of
\Theta^{\star}
and the
estimated singular subspaces
\widehat{U},\widehat{V}
(obtained, e.g.,
from the rank-
r
SVD of
\widehat{\Theta}
), we define the

estimated tangent projector

\widehat{P}_{\mathbb{T}}
using

\widehat{U},\widehat{V}
in place of
U^{\star},V^{\star}
, and the

plug-in information operator

\left\langle\widehat{G}U,\,V\right\rangle:=\frac{1}{n_{\rm aux}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{D}_{\rm aux}}I(\widehat{\eta}_{i})\,\left\langle U,\,X_{i}\right\rangle\,\left\langle V,\,X_{i}\right\rangle,\qquad\widehat{\eta}_{i}:=\left\langle\widehat{\Theta},\,X_{i}\right\rangle,

(2)

computed on the auxiliary sample
\mathcal{D}_{\rm aux}
with

n_{\rm aux}=|\mathcal{D}_{\rm aux}|
. The estimated restricted
information operator is
\widehat{A}:=\widehat{P}_{\mathbb{T}}\widehat{G}\widehat{P}_{\mathbb{T}}
,
and the estimated efficient direction is

\widehat{H}_{\Gamma}:=\widehat{A}^{-1}\widehat{P}_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma
.

Norms.

We use the following norms. For a matrix
M\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{t}}\times d_{\mathrm{m}}}
,

\left\lVert M\right\rVert_{F}
is the Frobenius norm,
\left\lVert M\right\rVert_{\infty}:=\max_{t,m}|M_{t,m}|

the entrywise norm,
\left\lVert M\right\rVert_{*}
the nuclear norm,

\left\lVert M\right\rVert_{\rm op}
the spectral norm,

\left\lVert M\right\rVert_{2,\infty}:=\max_{t}\left\lVert e_{t}^{\top}M\right\rVert_{2}
the row
\ell_{2}/\ell_{\infty}

norm, and
\left\lVert M\right\rVert_{\infty\to\infty}
the induced

\ell_{\infty}\to\ell_{\infty}
operator norm. For a vector
v
,

\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{1}:=\sum_{i}|v_{i}|
,
\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{2}
the Euclidean norm,

\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\infty}:=\max_{i}|v_{i}|
. For tensors / linear operators acting on

\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{t}}\times d_{\mathrm{m}}}
, these norms apply to the tensor when flattened
as a vector.

A.2
Score regularity

The following is the BTL specialization of the abstract score-regularity
assumption used in
21
.

Assumption A.1

(Score regularity for the BTL link)

The BTL log-likelihood satisfies, with
s_{\eta}
and
I
as above:

(i)

Centering.

\mathbb{E}^{\star}[s_{\eta}(Y,\eta^{\star})\mid X]=0
.

(ii)

Bounded support.

\left\lVert\Theta^{\star}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq B
for a constant
B>0

almost surely, and hence the Fisher weight
satisfies

depend only on

.

(iii)

Bounded score derivatives.
For every
(y,\eta)
in the

and

|\partial_{\eta}^{2}s_{\eta}(y,\eta)|\leq L_{3}
for an absolute constant

(iv)

Since

\left\lVert s_{\eta}(Y,\eta)\right\rVert_{\psi_{2}}\leq 1

\left\lVert s_{\eta}(Y,\eta)\right\rVert_{\psi_{1}}\leq 1

(BTL verification)

For the BTL model,

\partial_{\eta}s_{\eta}=-\sigma^{\prime}(\eta)=-I(\eta)\in[-1/4,0]

\partial_{\eta}^{2}s_{\eta}=-\sigma^{\prime\prime}(\eta)

boundedness of

under

.

\Trivlist

Conditional on a task

–

The following constant captures the entrywise stability of the inverse
restricted information operator and is the only structural quantity
that may depend on the dimension.

Definition A.12

(The constant

)

The constant

(Range of

Under the bounded-signal condition

and

-incoherence,

(i)

Coarse range.

(ii)

Constant-weight benchmark.

A.8
Probability calibration to
1-n^{-a}

Throughout, all ”high-probability” statements are uniform over a free
constant
a>0
that may be taken arbitrarily large at the cost of an
absolute constant prefactor absorbed into
\mathrm{polylog}(n\bar{d})
.
Specifically, every high-probability bound below has the form

\Pr(\text{good event})\geq 1-n^{-a}
for any fixed
a>0
.

Origin of the calibration.

The underlying ingredients are Bernstein- or matrix-Bernstein-type
concentration inequalities with a free tail parameter
x
: for each
such bound, substituting
x=Ca\log(n\bar{d})
yields the claimed

1-n^{-a}
form. In particular, statements of the form ”with
probability at least
1-d^{-c}
” or
”with probability at least
1-c_{1}\exp(-c_{2}d\log d)
” appearing in the
prior literature are tightened to
1-n^{-a}
by enlarging the constant
in the corresponding tail bound. The replacement is permissible because
under Assumption
A.11
,

d\log d\gtrsim a\log n
, so an exponential bound of the form

1-c_{1}\exp(-c_{2}d\log d)
is
\geq 1-n^{-a}
for
c_{2}
chosen
sufficiently large.

The master good event
\mathcal{E}_{n}
.

Let
a>0
be any fixed (large) constant. Define

\mathcal{E}_{n}:=\mathcal{E}_{\rm init}\cap\mathcal{E}_{\rm refine}\cap\mathcal{E}_{\rm rem}\cap\mathcal{E}_{\rm BE}\cap\mathcal{E}_{\rm var}\cap\mathcal{E}_{\rm cov}\cap\mathcal{E}_{\rm ccK},

where the constituent events are as follows.

(1)

\mathcal{E}_{\rm init}
— Frobenius accuracy of the convex
initializer (Theorem
B.5
).

(2)

\mathcal{E}_{\rm refine}
— entrywise accuracy of the refined
estimator (Theorem
B.26
); this implies

\mathcal{E}_{\infty}=\{\left\lVert\widehat{\Theta}-\Theta^{\star}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon_{n}\}

with
\varepsilon_{n}=Cr_{n}
.

(3)

\mathcal{E}_{\rm rem}
— uniform single-contrast one-step
remainder (Theorem
D.6
).

(4)

\mathcal{E}_{\rm BE}
— multivariate Berry–Esseen for the
oracle leading term (Theorem
D.7
).

(5)

\mathcal{E}_{\rm var}
— relative variance consistency for
plug-in standard errors
(Proposition
D.11
).

(6)

\mathcal{E}_{\rm cov}
— entrywise covariance consistency
(Proposition
D.12
).

(7)

\mathcal{E}_{\rm ccK}
— CCK aggregate approximate-means error
(Theorem
E.7
).

Each event holds with probability at least
1-n^{-a}
; by the union
bound,

\Pr(\mathcal{E}_{n}^{c})\leq 7\,n^{-a}\leq n^{-a/2},

and we relabel
a
so that
\Pr(\mathcal{E}_{n})\geq 1-n^{-a}
.
All deterministic statements in the proofs are made on
\mathcal{E}_{n}
;
the deficit
\Pr(\mathcal{E}_{n}^{c})=o(1)
is absorbed into the

o(1)
slack of every coverage statement in
Appendix
E
.

by Assumption
A.1
(ii),
the truth is feasible. The choice of
\lambda
is fixed in
Theorem
B.5
below. We obtain the rank-
r

initializer by truncating the SVD of
\widehat{M}
to its top

r
singular components, followed by entrywise clipping:

\widehat{\Theta}_{0}:=\mathrm{clip}_{B}\bigl(\mathrm{SVD}_{r}(\widehat{M})\bigr),

where
\mathrm{clip}_{B}(\cdot)
projects each entry onto
[-B,B]
.
The clipping step does not increase the entrywise distance to

M^{\star}
because
\left\lVert M^{\star}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq B
; it ensures that the
initializer remains in the feasible bounded-signal class.

The role of the row-centering constraint is essential: pairwise
comparisons depend only on within-task differences, so any matrix of
the form
\mathbf{1}_{d_{1}}c^{\top}
lies in the null space of the design.
Without the constraint, no RSC statement can hold.

B.1.2
Step 1: the population pairwise quadratic identity

We begin with the one genuinely pairwise-specific algebraic identity.

Lemma B.1

(Population pairwise quadratic identity)

Let
X=e_{t}(e_{m}-e_{m^{\prime}})^{\top}
where
t\in[d_{1}]
is sampled from

\nu
and
\{m,m^{\prime}\}\subset[d_{2}]
from a task-dependent distribution

\pi_{t}
satisfying Assumption
A.3
. For every

\Delta\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}}
with
\Delta\mathbf{1}_{d_{2}}=0
,

\mathbb{E}\bigl[\left\langle X,\,\Delta\right\rangle^{2}\bigr]\asymp\frac{2}{d_{1}\,(d_{2}-1)}\left\lVert\Delta\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\asymp\frac{\left\lVert\Delta\right\rVert_{F}^{2}}{d_{1}\,d_{2}}.

(10)

\Trivlist

Conditional on
t
,
\left\langle X,\,\Delta\right\rangle=\Delta_{t,m}-\Delta_{t,m^{\prime}}
.
Letting
z(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}
denote the
t
-th row of
\Delta
, with

\sum_{m}z_{m}(t)=0
by hypothesis, the standard sum-of-squared-pairwise-differences identity gives

\sum_{m<m^{\prime}}(z_{m}(t)-z_{m^{\prime}}(t))^{2}=d_{2}\sum_{m=1}^{d_{2}}z_{m}(t)^{2}.

Under near-uniform pair sampling
(Assumption
A.3
), the conditional expectation
satisfies

\mathbb{E}_{\{m,m^{\prime}\}}[(z_{m}(t)-z_{m^{\prime}}(t))^{2}\mid t]\asymp 2\left\lVert z(t)\right\rVert_{2}^{2}/(d_{2}-1)
.
Averaging over
t\sim\nu
using
\nu_{t}\asymp 1/d_{1}
and summing

\left\lVert z(t)\right\rVert_{2}^{2}
over
t
yields

\mathbb{E}[\left\langle X,\,\Delta\right\rangle^{2}]\asymp 2\left\lVert\Delta\right\rVert_{F}^{2}/(d_{1}(d_{2}-1))
.

The identity (
10
) is the pairwise
analogue of the Negahban–Wainwright population norm-equivalence
identity for matrix completion. The centered gauge

\mathbf{1}^{\top}\Delta=0
is essential:
\left\langle X,\,\Delta\right\rangle=0
identically
for any
\Delta=\mathbf{1}c^{\top}
, so without centering the left-hand
side of (
10
) would vanish on a non-trivial
subspace.

B.1.3
Step 2: pairwise quadratic restricted strong convexity

Define the spikiness ratio

\alpha_{\rm sp}(\Delta):=\sqrt{d_{1}d_{2}}\,\left\lVert\Delta\right\rVert_{\infty}/\left\lVert\Delta\right\rVert_{F}

and the rank surrogate

\beta_{\rm ra}(\Delta):=\left\lVert\Delta\right\rVert_{*}/\left\lVert\Delta\right\rVert_{F}
, and let

\mathcal{C}_{\rm pw}(n_{1};c_{0}):=\Bigl\{\Delta\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}}:\,\mathbf{1}_{d_{1}}^{\top}\Delta=0,\;\alpha_{\rm sp}(\Delta)\,\beta_{\rm ra}(\Delta)\leq\frac{1}{c_{0}}\sqrt{\frac{n_{1}}{\bar{d}\log\bar{d}}}\Bigr\}.

Theorem B.2

(Pairwise quadratic RSC)

Fix any
a>0
. There exist absolute constants
c_{0},c,C>0
such that
whenever

n_{1}\geq C\bar{d}\log\bar{d}
,
with probability at least
1-n^{-a}
,

\frac{1}{n_{1}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{D}_{1}}\left\langle X_{i},\,\Delta\right\rangle^{2}\;\geq\;\frac{c}{d_{1}\,d_{2}}\left\lVert\Delta\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\qquad\text{for all }\Delta\in\mathcal{C}_{\rm pw}(n_{1};c_{0}).

(11)

\Trivlist

The proof follows the four-step Negahban–Wainwright peeling/contraction
template, with the population identity
of Lemma
B.1
replacing the matrix-completion
norm-equivalence.

Reduction to a single-scale event by peeling.

Define the empirical Frobenius proxy

F_{\Delta}:=\bigl(\frac{1}{n_{1}}\sum_{i}\left\langle X_{i},\,\Delta\right\rangle^{2}\bigr)^{1/2}

and the population scale

\mu_{\Delta}:=\sqrt{2/(d_{1}(d_{2}-1))}\,\left\lVert\Delta\right\rVert_{F}
, so that

\mathbb{E}[F_{\Delta}^{2}]=\mu_{\Delta}^{2}
by
Lemma
B.1
. Since

\mu_{\Delta}\geq\left\lVert\Delta\right\rVert_{F}/\sqrt{d_{1}d_{2}}
, it suffices to show that

F_{\Delta}\geq\mu_{\Delta}/2
uniformly on the restricted set

\mathcal{C}_{\rm pw}(n_{1};c_{0})
(this gives the stated lower bound with

c=1/(2d_{1}d_{2})
up to constant adjustments).
Partition the Frobenius range of the restricted set into dyadic shells

S_{\ell}=\{\Delta:\left\lVert\Delta\right\rVert_{F}\in[\alpha^{\ell-1}\mu_{0},\alpha^{\ell}\mu_{0}]\}

for
\alpha=7/6
and
\mu_{0}
a small base scale; a union bound over

\ell=1,\ldots,L
(with
L=O(\log n_{1})
) reduces the problem to a
single-scale event at each shell.

Discretization.

Fix a scale
D
and consider the localized restricted set

\mathcal{S}(D):=\mathcal{C}_{\rm pw}(n_{1};c_{0})\cap\{\left\lVert\Delta\right\rVert_{F}\leq D\}
.
By Sudakov minoration applied to the metric induced by the Frobenius
norm, restricted to a nuclear-norm ball of radius

\rho(D):=D^{2}\sqrt{\log\bar{d}/(n_{1}\bar{d})}/c_{0}
,
the metric entropy at scale
D/8
satisfies

\log N\bigl(\mathcal{S}(D),\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{F},D/8\bigr)\;\leq\;C\,\frac{\rho(D)^{2}}{(D/8)^{2}}\,\bar{d}\;=\;C\,\frac{D^{2}\,\log\bar{d}}{c_{0}^{2}\,n_{1}\,\bar{d}}\cdot\bar{d}\;=\;C\,\frac{D^{2}\,\log\bar{d}}{c_{0}^{2}\,n_{1}}.

Let
\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{N_{0}}
be a corresponding
D/8
-net. By
the reverse triangle inequality,

F_{\Delta}\geq F_{\Delta_{k}}-F_{\Sigma}
for the nearest net point

\Delta_{k}
and remainder
\Sigma:=\Delta-\Delta_{k}
with

\left\lVert\Sigma\right\rVert_{F}\leq D/8
.

Net lower-tail concentration.

For each fixed
\Delta_{k}
, the map

(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n_{1}})\mapsto F_{\Delta_{k}}
with

\xi_{i}:=\left\langle X_{i},\,\Delta_{k}\right\rangle
is
(1/\sqrt{n_{1}})
-Lipschitz with
respect to the Euclidean norm on the coordinate domain, and the
coordinates satisfy

|\xi_{i}|\leq 2\left\lVert\Delta_{k}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq 2/\sqrt{d_{1}d_{2}}\cdot\alpha_{\rm sp}(\Delta_{k})\left\lVert\Delta_{k}\right\rVert_{F}\leq 2\sqrt{n_{1}/(\bar{d}\log\bar{d})}/(c_{0}\sqrt{d_{1}d_{2}})\left\lVert\Delta_{k}\right\rVert_{F}
.
By the bounded-difference (McDiarmid) concentration inequality applied
to the one-sided lower tail of
F_{\Delta_{k}}
,

\Pr\Bigl[F_{\Delta_{k}}<\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{1}d_{2}}}\left\lVert\Delta_{k}\right\rVert_{F}-t-\frac{C}{\sqrt{d_{1}d_{2}}\sqrt{n_{1}}}\Bigr]\;\leq\;4\exp\Bigl(-\frac{n_{1}\,d_{1}d_{2}\,t^{2}}{64}\Bigr).

Setting
t=D/(8\sqrt{d_{1}d_{2}})
gives a tail of order

\exp(-n_{1}D^{2}/(C^{\prime}d_{1}d_{2}\cdot d_{1}d_{2}))=\exp(-n_{1}D^{2}/C^{\prime\prime}(d_{1}d_{2}))
,
which by a union bound over the net (which has log-cardinality

\leq CD^{2}\log\bar{d}/(c_{0}^{2}n_{1})
) is dominated by
\exp(-n_{1}D^{2})

for
c_{0}
chosen sufficiently large. Choosing

c_{0}
large enough to absorb
\log\bar{d}
factors, this gives

\Pr[F_{\Delta_{k}}<D/(2\sqrt{d_{1}d_{2}})\text{ for some }k]\leq n^{-a}

after taking
a
large enough.

Remainder supremum.

The remainder supremum

\sup_{\Sigma:\left\lVert\Sigma\right\rVert_{F}\leq D/8,\,\left\lVert\Sigma\right\rVert_{*}\leq 2\rho(D)}F_{\Sigma}

is controlled by symmetrization, the Ledoux–Talagrand contraction
inequality (which converts
x^{2}
to
|x|
at the cost of a factor of

2
), and operator/nuclear-norm duality. Specifically, by
symmetrization,

\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\sup_{\Sigma}F_{\Sigma}^{2}\Bigr]\leq 2\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\sup_{\Sigma}\frac{1}{n_{1}}\sum_{i}\varepsilon_{i}\,\left\langle X_{i},\,\Sigma\right\rangle^{2}\Bigr],

where
\{\varepsilon_{i}\}
are i.i.d. Rademacher signs. Contraction with
respect to the squaring nonlinearity
x\mapsto x^{2}
(which is

L
-Lipschitz on the bounded range
[-2/\sqrt{d_{1}d_{2}},2/\sqrt{d_{1}d_{2}}]

with
L\lesssim 1/\sqrt{d_{1}d_{2}}
) gives

\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\sup_{\Sigma}\frac{1}{n_{1}}\sum_{i}\varepsilon_{i}\,\left\langle X_{i},\,\Sigma\right\rangle^{2}\Bigr]\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{d_{1}d_{2}}}\,\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\sup_{\Sigma}\Bigl|\frac{1}{n_{1}}\sum_{i}\varepsilon_{i}\,\left\langle X_{i},\,\Sigma\right\rangle\Bigr|\Bigr].

The latter Rademacher sum is bounded by operator-norm duality:

\sup_{\left\lVert\Sigma\right\rVert_{*}\leq 2\rho(D)}\Bigl|\frac{1}{n_{1}}\sum_{i}\varepsilon_{i}\left\langle X_{i},\,\Sigma\right\rangle\Bigr|\leq 2\rho(D)\Bigl\|\frac{1}{n_{1}}\sum_{i}\varepsilon_{i}X_{i}\Bigr\|_{\rm op}.

By the matrix Bernstein inequality applied to the centered rank-one
sum
\sum_{i}\varepsilon_{i}X_{i}
with envelope

\left\lVert X_{i}\right\rVert_{\rm op}\leq\sqrt{2}
and variance

\mathbb{E}[X_{i}X_{i}^{\top}]\preceq 2/(d_{2}-1)I_{d_{1}}
, we get

\mathbb{E}\|n_{1}^{-1}\sum_{i}\varepsilon_{i}X_{i}\|_{\rm op}\lesssim\sqrt{\log\bar{d}/(n_{1}\bar{d})}
.
Plugging in,

\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\sup_{\Sigma}F_{\Sigma}^{2}\Bigr]\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{1}d_{2}}}\cdot\rho(D)\cdot\sqrt{\frac{\log\bar{d}}{n_{1}\bar{d}}}=\frac{D^{2}}{c_{0}\,d_{1}d_{2}}\cdot\frac{\log\bar{d}}{n_{1}\bar{d}}\cdot\bar{d}=\frac{D^{2}\,\log\bar{d}}{c_{0}\,d_{1}d_{2}\,n_{1}}.

Choosing
c_{0}
sufficiently large makes the right-hand side

\leq D^{2}/(64d_{1}d_{2})
, and the bounded-difference concentration
upgrades this to

\sup_{\Sigma}F_{\Sigma}\leq D/(8\sqrt{d_{1}d_{2}})
with probability at
least
1-n^{-a}
.

Combining.

On the intersection of the net-lower-tail event and the remainder-supremum
event,

F_{\Delta}\geq F_{\Delta_{k}}-F_{\Sigma}\geq D/(2\sqrt{d_{1}d_{2}})-D/(8\sqrt{d_{1}d_{2}})\geq D/(4\sqrt{d_{1}d_{2}})
,
which together with
\mu_{\Delta}\leq\sqrt{2}/\sqrt{d_{1}d_{2}}\left\lVert\Delta\right\rVert_{F}

and
\left\lVert\Delta\right\rVert_{F}\geq D/\alpha
on shell
S_{\ell}
(
\alpha=7/6
)
gives
F_{\Delta}\geq\mu_{\Delta}/2
, as required. Closing the induction
over shells via the peeling argument concludes the proof.

B.1.4
Step 3: from quadratic RSC to logistic RSC

The pairwise logistic loss is not quadratic, but its Bregman divergence
inherits the quadratic curvature on the bounded-signal feasible set.

Lemma B.3

(Logistic curvature reduction)

For any
\Delta
with
M^{\star}+\Delta\in\mathcal{C}_{B}
,

\delta\mathcal{L}_{n_{1}}(M^{\star};\Delta):=\mathcal{L}_{n_{1}}(M^{\star}+\Delta)-\mathcal{L}_{n_{1}}(M^{\star})-\left\langle\nabla\mathcal{L}_{n_{1}}(M^{\star}),\,\Delta\right\rangle\;\geq\;\frac{c_{B}}{2n_{1}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{D}_{1}}\left\langle X_{i},\,\Delta\right\rangle^{2},

(12)

where
c_{B}:=\inf_{|x|\leq 2B}\sigma^{\prime}(x)>0
. Consequently, on the event
of Theorem
B.2
,

\delta\mathcal{L}_{n_{1}}(M^{\star};\Delta)\;\geq\;\kappa_{\rm pw}\left\lVert\Delta\right\rVert_{F}^{2},\qquad\kappa_{\rm pw}\asymp\frac{1}{d_{1}d_{2}},

holds for all
\Delta\in\mathcal{C}_{\rm pw}(n_{1};c_{0})
with

M^{\star}+\Delta\in\mathcal{C}_{B}
.

\Trivlist

Taylor’s theorem applied to
\eta\mapsto\ell(y,\eta)
at

\left\langle X_{i},\,M^{\star}\right\rangle
gives, for some intermediate

\xi_{i}
with
|\xi_{i}|\leq 2B
,

\delta\mathcal{L}_{n_{1}}(M^{\star};\Delta)=\frac{1}{2n_{1}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{D}_{1}}\sigma^{\prime}(\xi_{i})\left\langle X_{i},\,\Delta\right\rangle^{2}.

By definition of
c_{B}
,
\sigma^{\prime}(\xi_{i})\geq c_{B}>0
, giving the first
inequality. Combining with (
11
) yields the
second.

B.1.5
Step 4: gradient operator-norm bound

Lemma B.4

(Gradient operator-norm bound)

Fix any
a>0
. With probability at least
1-n^{-a}
,

\left\lVert\nabla\mathcal{L}_{n_{1}}(M^{\star})\right\rVert_{\rm op}\leq C\sqrt{\frac{\log(d_{1}+d_{2})}{n_{1}\,(d_{1}\wedge d_{2})}}.

(13)

\Trivlist

The gradient at the truth is

\nabla\mathcal{L}_{n_{1}}(M^{\star})=n_{1}^{-1}\sum_{i}(\sigma(\left\langle X_{i},\,M^{\star}\right\rangle)-Y_{i})X_{i}
.
Each summand is mean zero (by the model) and has operator norm at most

\sqrt{2}
(since
\left\lVert X_{i}\right\rVert_{\rm op}=\sqrt{2}
and the scalar prefactor

\sigma(\left\langle X_{i},\,M^{\star}\right\rangle)-Y_{i}\in[-1,1]
). The matrix variance proxy
on the right is

\mathbb{E}[(\sigma(\left\langle X_{i},\,M^{\star}\right\rangle)-Y_{i})^{2}X_{i}X_{i}^{\top}]\preceq\mathbb{E}[X_{i}X_{i}^{\top}]\asymp\frac{2}{d_{2}-1}\,I_{d_{1}}\preceq\frac{C}{d_{2}-1}I_{d_{1}};

the left variance is similarly bounded by
O(1/(d_{1}-1))I_{d_{2}}
.
Hence
\sigma_{X}^{2}:=\max(\left\lVert\sum_{i}\mathbb{E}[X_{i}X_{i}^{\top}]\right\rVert_{\rm op},\left\lVert\sum_{i}\mathbb{E}[X_{i}^{\top}X_{i}]\right\rVert_{\rm op})\leq Cn_{1}/(d_{1}\wedge d_{2})
.
By the rectangular matrix Bernstein inequality (Tropp 2015, Theorem 6.1.1)
applied to the rescaled sum,

\Pr\Bigl[\left\lVert n_{1}^{-1}\textstyle\sum_{i}(\sigma-Y)X_{i}\right\rVert_{\rm op}\geq t\Bigr]\leq(d_{1}+d_{2})\exp\Bigl(-\frac{n_{1}\,t^{2}/2}{C/(d_{1}\wedge d_{2})+\sqrt{2}t/3}\Bigr).

Setting
t=C\sqrt{a\log(d_{1}+d_{2})\,n^{-1}/(d_{1}\wedge d_{2})}
for
C

large enough yields the claimed bound with probability
1-n^{-a}
.

B.1.6
Step 5: main convex initialization theorem

Theorem B.5

(Frobenius bound for the convex initializer)

Fix any
a>0
. Under the model assumptions of
Section
2
and Assumption
A.3
, set

\lambda:=2C\sqrt{\log(d_{1}+d_{2})/(n_{1}\,(d_{1}\wedge d_{2}))}
where
C
is the
constant from Lemma
B.4
, and assume

n_{1}\geq C\,\mathrm{poly}(\mu,r,\kappa,B)\,\bar{d}\log^{c}\bar{d}
.
Then with probability at least
1-n^{-a}
,

\left\lVert\widehat{M}-M^{\star}\right\rVert_{F}\;\leq\;\frac{C\,\lambda\sqrt{r}}{\kappa_{\rm pw}}\;\leq\;C^{\prime}\sqrt{\frac{r\,d_{1}\,d_{2}\,\bar{d}\,\log\bar{d}}{n_{1}}}\;\asymp\;\sqrt{\frac{r\,\bar{d}^{\,3}\log\bar{d}}{n_{1}}}.

(14)

Consequently, the rank-truncated, clipped initializer

\widehat{\Theta}_{0}=\mathrm{clip}_{B}(\mathrm{SVD}_{r}(\widehat{M}))
satisfies

\left\lVert\widehat{\Theta}_{0}-\Theta^{\star}\right\rVert_{F}\leq 2\left\lVert\widehat{M}-M^{\star}\right\rVert_{F}\leq C\sqrt{r\bar{d}^{\,3}\log\bar{d}/n_{1}}

on the same event.

\Trivlist

Set
\widehat{\Delta}:=\widehat{M}-M^{\star}
and let
\mathbb{T}_{r}
denote
the rank-
r
tangent space at
M^{\star}
(without the row-centering
constraint), with associated decomposition

\widehat{\Delta}=\widehat{\Delta}_{\mathbb{T}_{r}}+\widehat{\Delta}_{\mathbb{T}_{r}^{\perp}}
.

Basic inequality.

Since
M^{\star}\in\mathcal{C}_{B}
is feasible, optimality of

\widehat{M}
gives

\mathcal{L}_{n_{1}}(M^{\star}+\widehat{\Delta})+\lambda\left\lVert M^{\star}+\widehat{\Delta}\right\rVert_{*}\leq\mathcal{L}_{n_{1}}(M^{\star})+\lambda\left\lVert M^{\star}\right\rVert_{*}.

Rearranging,

\delta\mathcal{L}_{n_{1}}(M^{\star};\widehat{\Delta})\leq-\left\langle\nabla\mathcal{L}_{n_{1}}(M^{\star}),\,\widehat{\Delta}\right\rangle+\lambda\bigl(\left\lVert M^{\star}\right\rVert_{*}-\left\lVert M^{\star}+\widehat{\Delta}\right\rVert_{*}\bigr).

By the operator/nuclear-norm duality and our choice

\lambda\geq 2\left\lVert\nabla\mathcal{L}_{n_{1}}(M^{\star})\right\rVert_{\rm op}
(which holds on
the event of Lemma
B.4
),

|\left\langle\nabla\mathcal{L}_{n_{1}}(M^{\star}),\,\widehat{\Delta}\right\rangle|\leq(\lambda/2)\left\lVert\widehat{\Delta}\right\rVert_{*}
.
By the standard nuclear-norm decomposability,

\left\lVert M^{\star}\right\rVert_{*}-\left\lVert M^{\star}+\widehat{\Delta}\right\rVert_{*}\leq\left\lVert\widehat{\Delta}_{\mathbb{T}_{r}}\right\rVert_{*}-\left\lVert\widehat{\Delta}_{\mathbb{T}_{r}^{\perp}}\right\rVert_{*}
.
Combining,

\delta\mathcal{L}_{n_{1}}(M^{\star};\widehat{\Delta})\leq\frac{3\lambda}{2}\left\lVert\widehat{\Delta}_{\mathbb{T}_{r}}\right\rVert_{*}-\frac{\lambda}{2}\left\lVert\widehat{\Delta}_{\mathbb{T}_{r}^{\perp}}\right\rVert_{*}.

Since
\delta\mathcal{L}_{n_{1}}\geq 0
, this forces the cone condition

\left\lVert\widehat{\Delta}_{\mathbb{T}_{r}^{\perp}}\right\rVert_{*}\leq 3\left\lVert\widehat{\Delta}_{\mathbb{T}_{r}}\right\rVert_{*}
,
and using
\mathrm{rank}(\widehat{\Delta}_{\mathbb{T}_{r}})\leq 2r
we obtain

\left\lVert\widehat{\Delta}_{\mathbb{T}_{r}}\right\rVert_{*}\leq\sqrt{2r}\left\lVert\widehat{\Delta}\right\rVert_{F}

and hence

\left\lVert\widehat{\Delta}\right\rVert_{*}\leq 4\sqrt{2r}\,\left\lVert\widehat{\Delta}\right\rVert_{F}
.

Applying RSC.

The cone condition implies
\widehat{\Delta}\in\mathcal{C}_{\rm pw}(n_{1};c_{0})

(modulo absorbing a factor of
\sqrt{r}
into the constant
c_{0}
,
because the spikiness ratio is bounded by entrywise feasibility,

\alpha_{\rm sp}(\widehat{\Delta})\leq\sqrt{d_{1}d_{2}}\,(2B)/\left\lVert\widehat{\Delta}\right\rVert_{F}
,
and the rank-surrogate ratio
\beta_{\rm ra}(\widehat{\Delta})\leq 4\sqrt{2r}

on the cone). Hence on the event of
Theorem
B.2
,

\delta\mathcal{L}_{n_{1}}(M^{\star};\widehat{\Delta})\geq\kappa_{\rm pw}\left\lVert\widehat{\Delta}\right\rVert_{F}^{2},\qquad\kappa_{\rm pw}\asymp\frac{c_{B}}{d_{1}d_{2}}.

Combining.

On the same event,

\kappa_{\rm pw}\left\lVert\widehat{\Delta}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\leq\delta\mathcal{L}_{n_{1}}(M^{\star};\widehat{\Delta})\leq\frac{3\lambda}{2}\left\lVert\widehat{\Delta}_{\mathbb{T}_{r}}\right\rVert_{*}\leq\frac{3\lambda\sqrt{2r}}{2}\left\lVert\widehat{\Delta}\right\rVert_{F}.

Dividing by
\left\lVert\widehat{\Delta}\right\rVert_{F}
,

\left\lVert\widehat{\Delta}\right\rVert_{F}\leq\frac{3\lambda\sqrt{2r}}{2\kappa_{\rm pw}}\leq C\,\lambda\sqrt{r}\,d_{1}\,d_{2}.

Substituting
\lambda\asymp\sqrt{\log\bar{d}/(n_{1}\bar{d})}
gives

\left\lVert\widehat{\Delta}\right\rVert_{F}\leq C\sqrt{r\,d_{1}^{2}d_{2}^{2}\log\bar{d}/(n_{1}\bar{d})}=C\sqrt{r\,\bar{d}^{\,3}\log\bar{d}/n_{1}}

in the balanced regime.

If on the other hand
\widehat{\Delta}\notin\mathcal{C}_{\rm pw}(n_{1};c_{0})
,
then by the cone condition and the entrywise feasibility constraint

\left\lVert\widehat{\Delta}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq 2B
, one has

\left\lVert\widehat{\Delta}\right\rVert_{F}\leq C(B,r)\bar{d}\sqrt{\log\bar{d}/n_{1}}
, which is
of the same or smaller order under
n_{1}\geq C\bar{d}\log\bar{d}
.

The post-processing claim for
\widehat{\Theta}_{0}
follows because
rank-
r
SVD truncation contracts Frobenius distance to
M^{\star}

by at most a factor of
2
(the best rank-
r
approximation), and
entrywise clipping is non-expansive against
M^{\star}
under

\left\lVert M^{\star}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq B
.

Remark B.6

(Comparison to matrix completion)

The rate
\left\lVert\widehat{\Theta}_{0}-\Theta^{\star}\right\rVert_{F}\lesssim\sqrt{r\bar{d}^{\,3}\log\bar{d}/n}

differs from the Negahban–Wainwright matrix-completion rate

\sqrt{r\bar{d}\log\bar{d}/n}
by a factor of
\bar{d}
. This reflects
the
d^{-2}
gap between the pairwise population identity
(
10
) and the entry-completion identity

\mathbb{E}[\left\langle X,\,\Delta\right\rangle^{2}]=\left\lVert\Delta\right\rVert_{F}^{2}/(d_{1}d_{2})
: each pairwise
comparison carries
O(1/d^{2})
of the information per entry, making
the
d
-factor overhead unavoidable under sparse pairwise observations.
The factor is recovered in the Frobenius rate but does not appear in the
final entrywise rate after refinement (Theorem
B.26
),
because refinement uses fresh observations to upgrade row-by-row.

B.2
Stage 2: three-split refinement algorithm and roadmap

B.2.1
Algorithm

We describe the three-split refinement. The auxiliary sample

\mathcal{D}_{1}
is used to compute
\widehat{\Theta}_{0}
and the
right-factor estimate
\widehat{\Theta}_{R}
below; the second split

\mathcal{D}_{2}
is used to refine the left factor; the third split

\mathcal{D}_{3}
refines the right factor. Write the rank-
r

factorization

\Theta^{\star}=\Theta^{\star}_{L}\,(\Theta^{\star}_{R})^{\top},\qquad\Theta^{\star}_{L}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{t}}\times r},\ \Theta^{\star}_{R}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{m}}\times r},\ (\Theta^{\star}_{R})^{\top}\Theta^{\star}_{R}=I_{r},\ \mathbf{1}_{d_{\mathrm{t}}}^{\top}\Theta^{\star}_{L}=0.

This factorization is obtained by absorbing the singular values into the
left factor.

Stage A: initialization and right-factor construction.

On
\mathcal{D}_{1}
, compute
\widehat{\Theta}_{0}
by
Theorem
B.5
. Recenter

\widehat{\Theta}^{(1)}:=P_{\perp}\widehat{\Theta}_{0}
where

P_{\perp}:=I_{d_{\mathrm{t}}}-d_{\mathrm{t}}^{-1}\mathbf{1}_{d_{\mathrm{t}}}\mathbf{1}_{d_{\mathrm{t}}}^{\top}
is the
row-centering projector. Take the rank-
r
SVD of

\widehat{\Theta}^{(1)}
and project the right singular vectors onto the
incoherence ball

\{V\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{m}}\times r}:\left\lVert V\right\rVert_{2,\infty}\leq C_{R}d_{\mathrm{m}}^{-1/2}\}

to obtain
\widehat{\Theta}_{R}
.

Stage B: left-factor refinement.

On
\mathcal{D}_{2}
, fix
\widehat{\Theta}_{R}
and solve, for each row

t\in[d_{\mathrm{t}}]
, the row score equation
S_{t}(\theta)=0
, where

S_{t}(\theta):=\sum_{\ell=1}^{M_{t}}\widehat{\Theta}_{R}[m_{\ell}]\bigl\{Y_{\ell}^{(t)}-\sigma\bigl(\widehat{\Theta}_{R}[m_{\ell}]^{\top}\theta-\widehat{o}_{\ell}^{(t)}\bigr)\bigr\}.

Here
(m_{\ell},m_{\ell}^{\prime},Y_{\ell}^{(t)})_{\ell=1}^{M_{t}}
collects all
observations in
\mathcal{D}_{2}
involving row
t
(after reorienting
so row
t
is the ”left” model in the comparison),

\widehat{\Theta}_{R}[m]\in\mathbb{R}^{r}
denotes the
m
-th row of

\widehat{\Theta}_{R}
, and the opponent offset is

\widehat{o}_{\ell}^{(t)}:=\widehat{\Theta}^{(1)}_{t,m_{\ell}^{\prime}}
.
The solution
\widehat{\theta}_{t}
yields

\widehat{\Theta}_{L}:=(\widehat{\theta}_{t})_{t\in[d_{\mathrm{t}}]}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{t}}\times r}
.

Stage C: re-centering and right-factor refinement.

Set
\overline{\Theta}_{L}:=P_{\perp}\widehat{\Theta}_{L}
, restoring the
gauge
\mathbf{1}_{d_{\mathrm{t}}}^{\top}\overline{\Theta}_{L}=0
without changing
pairwise differences (Lemma
B.16
below).
On
\mathcal{D}_{3}
, for each column
m\in[d_{\mathrm{m}}]
, solve

S_{m}(a)=0
where

S_{m}(a):=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{m}}x_{i}\bigl\{Y_{i}-\sigma(x_{i}^{\top}a)\bigr\},\qquad x_{i}:=\overline{\Theta}_{L}[t_{i},m_{i}]-\overline{\Theta}_{L}[t_{i},m_{i}^{\prime}],

where
\mathcal{I}_{m}:=\{i\in\mathcal{D}_{3}:m_{i}=m\text{ or }m_{i}^{\prime}=m\}
, and

\overline{\Theta}_{L}[t,m]
denotes the row of
\overline{\Theta}_{L}
at
index
t
(which equals the
t
-th row of the recentered left
factor). Strictly speaking, the inner product is between

\overline{\Theta}_{L}
’s row at task
t_{i}
considered as an element of

\mathbb{R}^{r}
, and the column-factor parameter
a\in\mathbb{R}^{r}
; see
Appendix
B.7
for the precise definition.
The solution
\widehat{a}_{m}
yields

\widehat{\Theta}_{R}:=(\widehat{a}_{m})_{m\in[d_{\mathrm{m}}]}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{m}}\times r}
.

Final estimator.

\widehat{\Theta}:=\overline{\Theta}_{L}\widehat{\Theta}_{R}^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{t}}\times d_{\mathrm{m}}}
.

B.2.2
Roadmap

The proof proceeds in six blocks paralleling

21
. Each block is the matrix specialization
of the corresponding tensor result.

Block I:

Frobenius-error transfer to the right factor.

On
\mathcal{D}_{1}
, the SVD-and-incoherence-projection step transfers
the Frobenius error of the convex initializer to the right factor:

\left\lVert\widehat{\Theta}_{R}-\Theta^{\star}_{R}\right\rVert_{F}\lesssim\left\lVert\widehat{\Theta}^{(1)}-\Theta^{\star}\right\rVert_{F}/\sigma_{r}(\Theta^{\star})\lesssim\Delta_{F}/\bar{d}
,
where

\Delta_{F}:=\left\lVert\widehat{\Theta}^{(1)}-\Theta^{\star}\right\rVert_{F}\lesssim\sqrt{\bar{d}^{\,3}/n_{1}}

on the convex-initialization event. Moreover the projection step
ensures
\left\lVert\widehat{\Theta}_{R}\right\rVert_{2,\infty}\lesssim d_{\mathrm{m}}^{-1/2}
and

(\widehat{\Theta}_{R})^{\top}\widehat{\Theta}_{R}=(I_{r}+O(\Delta_{F}/\bar{d}))
on

\mathcal{E}_{1}
.

Block II:

Deterministic local existence for the row update.

We prove an inward-pointing zero-existence lemma for
S_{t}

(Lemma
B.8
) which states that under a
sufficient condition on a ”noise plus bias plus higher-order” combination

R_{t}\leq\lambda_{t}^{2}/(4L_{3}\gamma_{t})
for a suitable curvature

\lambda_{t}
, the score equation has a solution close to the truth.

Block III:

Probabilistic verification of the row condition.

On
\mathcal{D}_{2}
, we verify the sufficient condition uniformly over

t\in[d_{\mathrm{t}}]
using six concentration lemmas: row-wise coverage,
noise envelope, two bias terms, higher-order moments
\beta_{t},\gamma_{t}
,
and curvature
\lambda_{t}
. This gives

\left\lVert\widehat{\Theta}_{L}-\Theta^{\star}_{L}\right\rVert_{2,\infty}\lesssim d_{\mathrm{m}}/\sqrt{n_{2}}\,\mathrm{polylog}(n\bar{d})
.

Block IV:

Re-centering and pairwise Gram identity.

The centering projection
P_{\perp}
preserves pairwise differences and
preserves entrywise / Frobenius distance (Lemma
B.16
).
A pairwise Gram identity (Lemma
B.17
) then
upgrades the column-update curvature to

\lambda_{m}\gtrsim n_{3}
by capturing the gain from sampling pairs
on the row factor.

Block V:

Deterministic local existence for the column update.

The same Brouwer argument (Lemma
B.19
)
gives a sufficient condition for the column score equation

S_{m}(a)=0
to have a solution near the truth.

Block VI:

Probabilistic verification of the column condition.

We verify uniformly in
m\in[d_{\mathrm{m}}]
using parallel concentration
lemmas, obtaining

\left\lVert\widehat{\Theta}_{R}-\Theta^{\star}_{R}\right\rVert_{2,\infty}\lesssim n^{-1/2}\,\mathrm{polylog}(n\bar{d})
.

B.3
Block I: Brouwer inward-pointing zero lemma

We restate the deterministic existence lemma underlying Blocks II and V.

Lemma B.7

(Brouwer inward-pointing zero)

Let
F:\mathbb{R}^{r}\to\mathbb{R}^{r}
be continuous, fix
\vartheta^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{r}
and

\xi>0
. If

(\vartheta-\vartheta^{\star})^{\top}F(\vartheta)\leq 0

for every
\vartheta
on the sphere

\{\vartheta:\left\lVert\vartheta-\vartheta^{\star}\right\rVert=\xi\}
, then there
exists
\widetilde{\vartheta}
with
F(\widetilde{\vartheta})=0
and

\left\lVert\widetilde{\vartheta}-\vartheta^{\star}\right\rVert\leq\xi
.

\Trivlist

Suppose for contradiction that
F
has no zero in the closed ball

B_{\xi}(\vartheta^{\star})
. Define the continuous map

G:B_{\xi}(\vartheta^{\star})\to B_{\xi}(\vartheta^{\star})
by

G(\vartheta):=\vartheta^{\star}+\xi F(\vartheta)/\left\lVert F(\vartheta)\right\rVert
,
which lands on the sphere. By Brouwer’s fixed-point theorem,
G
has
a fixed point
\vartheta^{\dagger}
, which satisfies

\vartheta^{\dagger}-\vartheta^{\star}=\xi F(\vartheta^{\dagger})/\left\lVert F(\vartheta^{\dagger})\right\rVert
,
and so

(\vartheta^{\dagger}-\vartheta^{\star})^{\top}F(\vartheta^{\dagger})=\xi\left\lVert F(\vartheta^{\dagger})\right\rVert>0
,
contradicting the inward-pointing hypothesis on the sphere.

B.4
Block II: row-wise score equation and deterministic existence

Fix
t\in[d_{\mathrm{t}}]
and condition on
\mathcal{D}_{1}
. After reorienting
the comparisons in
\mathcal{D}_{2}
so that row
t
appears on the
”left” of every comparison (swapping signs of
Y
when row
t
was
on the right), let

(m_{\ell},m_{\ell}^{\prime},Y_{\ell})_{\ell=1}^{M_{t}}
denote the relevant
observations, where
M_{t}:=|\{i\in\mathcal{D}_{2}:t_{i}=t\}|
. Define the
true predictors

\eta_{\ell}^{\star}:=\Theta^{\star}_{t,m_{\ell}}-\Theta^{\star}_{t,m_{\ell}^{\prime}}=(\Theta^{\star}_{R}[m_{\ell}])^{\top}\theta_{t}^{\star}-o_{\ell}^{\star}

with
\theta_{t}^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{r}
the
t
-th row of
\Theta^{\star}_{L}
and

o_{\ell}^{\star}:=\Theta^{\star}_{t,m_{\ell}^{\prime}}
the opponent offset. Set

\widehat{o}_{\ell}:=\widehat{\Theta}^{(1)}_{t,m_{\ell}^{\prime}}
, and

\varepsilon_{\ell}:=Y_{\ell}-\sigma(\eta_{\ell}^{\star})
the centered
Bernoulli noise.

Quantities used in the existence condition.

Define the noise vector

N_{t}:=\sum_{\ell=1}^{M_{t}}\widehat{\Theta}_{R}[m_{\ell}]\,\varepsilon_{\ell},

the Hessian-like matrix

H_{t}:=\sum_{\ell=1}^{M_{t}}\sigma^{\prime}(\eta_{\ell}^{\star})\,\widehat{\Theta}_{R}[m_{\ell}]\widehat{\Theta}_{R}[m_{\ell}]^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r},

with smallest eigenvalue
\lambda_{t}:=\lambda_{\min}(H_{t})
, the bias
vectors

\displaystyle B_{t}^{(R)}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\ell=1}^{M_{t}}\sigma^{\prime}(\eta_{\ell}^{\star})\,\widehat{\Theta}_{R}[m_{\ell}]\bigl(\widehat{\Theta}_{R}[m_{\ell}]-\Theta^{\star}_{R}[m_{\ell}]\bigr)^{\top}\theta_{t}^{\star},

\displaystyle B_{t}^{(O)}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\ell=1}^{M_{t}}\sigma^{\prime}(\eta_{\ell}^{\star})\,\widehat{\Theta}_{R}[m_{\ell}]\bigl(\widehat{o}_{\ell}-o_{\ell}^{\star}\bigr),

the linearization residuals

d_{\ell}:=\bigl(\widehat{\Theta}_{R}[m_{\ell}]-\Theta^{\star}_{R}[m_{\ell}]\bigr)^{\top}\theta_{t}^{\star}-\bigl(\widehat{o}_{\ell}-o_{\ell}^{\star}\bigr),

and the higher-order moments

\beta_{t}:=\sup_{\left\lVert v\right\rVert=1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{M_{t}}|\widehat{\Theta}_{R}[m_{\ell}]^{\top}v|\,d_{\ell}^{2},\qquad\gamma_{t}:=\sup_{\left\lVert v\right\rVert=1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{M_{t}}|\widehat{\Theta}_{R}[m_{\ell}]^{\top}v|^{3}.

Lemma B.8

(Row-wise deterministic existence)

Suppose

R_{t}:=\left\lVert N_{t}\right\rVert+\left\lVert B_{t}^{(R)}\right\rVert+\left\lVert B_{t}^{(O)}\right\rVert+L_{3}\,\beta_{t}\;\leq\;\frac{\lambda_{t}^{\,2}}{4L_{3}\,\gamma_{t}},

(15)

where
L_{3}:=\sup_{x}|\sigma^{\prime\prime}(x)|\leq 1/(6\sqrt{3})\leq 1
. Then
S_{t}

admits a zero
\widehat{\theta}_{t}
with

\left\lVert\widehat{\theta}_{t}-\theta_{t}^{\star}\right\rVert\leq 2R_{t}/\lambda_{t}
.

\Trivlist

Set
\delta:=\theta-\theta_{t}^{\star}
. By definition,

\widehat{\Theta}_{R}[m_{\ell}]^{\top}\theta-\widehat{o}_{\ell}=\eta_{\ell}^{\star}+\widehat{\Theta}_{R}[m_{\ell}]^{\top}\delta+d_{\ell}.

Substituting into
S_{t}(\theta)
and Taylor-expanding

\sigma
around
\eta_{\ell}^{\star}
,

S_{t}(\theta_{t}^{\star}+\delta)=N_{t}-H_{t}\,\delta-B_{t}^{(R)}+B_{t}^{(O)}-\mathcal{R}_{t}(\delta),

where the second-order term is

\mathcal{R}_{t}(\delta):=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\ell=1}^{M_{t}}\widehat{\Theta}_{R}[m_{\ell}]\sigma^{\prime\prime}(\widetilde{\eta}_{\ell})\bigl(\widehat{\Theta}_{R}[m_{\ell}]^{\top}\delta+d_{\ell}\bigr)^{2}.

For
\left\lVert\delta\right\rVert=\xi
, left-multiplying by
\delta^{\top}
gives

\delta^{\top}S_{t}(\theta_{t}^{\star}+\delta)\leq\xi\left\lVert N_{t}\right\rVert-\lambda_{t}\xi^{2}+\xi\left\lVert B_{t}^{(R)}\right\rVert+\xi\left\lVert B_{t}^{(O)}\right\rVert+|\delta^{\top}\mathcal{R}_{t}(\delta)|.

Using
|\sigma^{\prime\prime}|\leq L_{3}
and the elementary inequality

(x+y)^{2}\leq 2x^{2}+2y^{2}
, the second-order remainder is bounded by

|\delta^{\top}\mathcal{R}_{t}(\delta)|\leq\frac{L_{3}}{2}\sum_{\ell=1}^{M_{t}}|\widehat{\Theta}_{R}[m_{\ell}]^{\top}\delta|\,(\widehat{\Theta}_{R}[m_{\ell}]^{\top}\delta+d_{\ell})^{2}\leq L_{3}(\gamma_{t}\xi^{3}+\beta_{t}\xi).

Hence

\delta^{\top}S_{t}(\theta_{t}^{\star}+\delta)\leq-\lambda_{t}\xi^{2}+L_{3}\gamma_{t}\xi^{3}+R_{t}\xi.

Choose
\xi=\xi_{t}:=2R_{t}/\lambda_{t}
. Then

-\lambda_{t}\xi^{2}+R_{t}\xi=\xi(R_{t}-\lambda_{t}\xi)=-R_{t}\xi\leq 0
, and

L_{3}\gamma_{t}\xi^{3}\leq L_{3}\gamma_{t}\,\xi\,(2R_{t}/\lambda_{t})^{2}\leq R_{t}\xi

under the hypothesis (
15
). Combining gives

\delta^{\top}S_{t}(\theta_{t}^{\star}+\delta)\leq 0
for every

\left\lVert\delta\right\rVert=\xi_{t}
, so
Lemma
B.7
delivers a zero

\widehat{\theta}_{t}
with

\left\lVert\widehat{\theta}_{t}-\theta_{t}^{\star}\right\rVert\leq\xi_{t}=2R_{t}/\lambda_{t}
.

B.5
Block III: probabilistic verification of the row condition

We work conditional on
\mathcal{D}_{1}
, on the event

\mathcal{E}_{\rm init}
of Theorem
B.5
, and verify
the sufficient condition (
15
) uniformly over

t\in[d_{\mathrm{t}}]
. The six lemmas below mirror
[
21
, Lemmas 3–8]

in the matrix case. Throughout,
n_{2}:=|\mathcal{D}_{2}|\asymp n
.

Lemma B.9

(Row-wise coverage)

Let
M_{t}:=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{D}_{2}}\mathbf{1}\{t_{i}=t\}
. Under
Assumption
A.3
,
M_{t}\sim\mathrm{Bin}(n_{2},\nu_{t})
with

\nu_{t}\asymp 1/d_{\mathrm{t}}
. For any
a>0
, with probability at least

1-n^{-a}
,
M_{t}\asymp n_{2}/d_{\mathrm{t}}
uniformly over
t\in[d_{\mathrm{t}}]
,
provided
n_{2}\gtrsim ad_{\mathrm{t}}\log n
.

\Trivlist

Apply Bernstein’s inequality to the centered indicator

\mathbf{1}\{t_{i}=t\}-\nu_{t}
with variance proxy

\nu_{t}(1-\nu_{t})\leq\nu_{t}
and envelope
1
: for any
x>0
,

\Pr\bigl[|M_{t}-n_{2}\nu_{t}|\geq\sqrt{2n_{2}\nu_{t}x}+x\bigr]\leq 2e^{-x}.

Setting
x=Ca\log(n\bar{d})
makes the right-hand side

\leq n^{-a-1}
; a union bound over
t\in[d_{\mathrm{t}}]
yields the claim.
The condition
n_{2}\gtrsim ad_{\mathrm{t}}\log n
ensures the additive

x
term is dominated by the variance term.

Lemma B.10

(Row noise envelope)

For each
t\in[d_{\mathrm{t}}]
, conditional on
\mathcal{D}_{1}
on

\mathcal{E}_{\rm init}
and for any
x>0
,

\Pr\Bigl[\left\lVert N_{t}\right\rVert\geq C\Bigl(\frac{\sqrt{n_{2}x}}{d_{\mathrm{m}}}+\frac{x}{\sqrt{d_{\mathrm{m}}}}\Bigr)\;\Big|\;\mathcal{D}_{1}\Bigr]\leq 2e^{-x}.

Consequently, with probability at least
1-n^{-a}
,

\max_{t\in[d_{\mathrm{t}}]}\left\lVert N_{t}\right\rVert\leq C\Bigl(\frac{\sqrt{n_{2}\log(n\bar{d})}}{d_{\mathrm{m}}}+\frac{\log(n\bar{d})}{\sqrt{d_{\mathrm{m}}}}\Bigr)\;\lesssim\;\frac{\sqrt{n_{2}}}{d_{\mathrm{m}}}\,\mathrm{polylog}(n\bar{d}).

\Trivlist

Write

N_{t}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{D}_{2}}\mathbf{1}\{t_{i}=t\}\,\widehat{\Theta}_{R}[m_{i}]\,\varepsilon_{i}

where
\varepsilon_{i}:=Y_{i}-\sigma(\eta_{i}^{\star})
is the centered
Bernoulli noise. Conditional on
\mathcal{D}_{1}
, the summands are
independent, mean zero, with envelope

\left\lVert\mathbf{1}\{t_{i}=t\}\,\widehat{\Theta}_{R}[m_{i}]\,\varepsilon_{i}\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert\widehat{\Theta}_{R}\right\rVert_{2,\infty}\leq Cd_{\mathrm{m}}^{-1/2}

on
\mathcal{E}_{\rm init}
(incoherence of
\widehat{\Theta}_{R}
) since

|\varepsilon_{i}|\leq 1
. The variance proxy satisfies

\sum_{i\in\mathcal{D}_{2}}\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\mathbf{1}\{t_{i}=t\}\,\widehat{\Theta}_{R}[m_{i}]\,\varepsilon_{i}\right\rVert^{2}\leq n_{2}\cdot\nu_{t}\cdot d_{\mathrm{m}}^{-1}\cdot\left\lVert\widehat{\Theta}_{R}\right\rVert_{2,\infty}^{2}\,(d_{\mathrm{m}})\;\asymp\;\frac{n_{2}}{d_{\mathrm{t}}\,d_{\mathrm{m}}}\cdot d_{\mathrm{m}}\cdot d_{\mathrm{m}}^{-1}=\frac{n_{2}}{d_{\mathrm{t}}d_{\mathrm{m}}},

where the bound uses
\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\widehat{\Theta}_{R}[m_{i}]\right\rVert^{2}\leq\left\lVert\widehat{\Theta}_{R}\right\rVert_{2,\infty}^{2}\leq C/d_{\mathrm{m}}

under uniform model sampling and

\mathbb{E}|\varepsilon|^{2}\leq 1/4
. Thus, with the standard vector
Bernstein form (e.g. Tropp 2015 Theorem 6.1.1 in the rectangular case),

\Pr\bigl[\left\lVert N_{t}\right\rVert\geq\sqrt{2n_{2}x/(d_{\mathrm{t}}d_{\mathrm{m}})}+(C/\sqrt{d}_{\mathrm{m}})x\bigr]\leq 2e^{-x}.

Setting
x=Ca\log(n\bar{d})
and noting
d_{\mathrm{t}}d_{\mathrm{m}}\asymp d_{\mathrm{t}}d_{\mathrm{m}}\asymp d_{\mathrm{m}}^{2}

in the balanced regime, then taking a union bound over
t\in[d_{\mathrm{t}}]
,
gives the claim. The dominant
\sqrt{n}_{2}/d_{\mathrm{m}}
term arises from the
variance scale.

Lemma B.11

(Right-factor bias)

Let
\Delta_{R}:=\left\lVert\widehat{\Theta}_{R}-\Theta^{\star}_{R}\right\rVert_{F}\leq C\Delta_{F}/\bar{d}

on
\mathcal{E}_{\rm init}
(Block I). With probability at least

1-n^{-a}
,

\max_{t\in[d_{\mathrm{t}}]}\left\lVert B_{t}^{(R)}\right\rVert\leq C\Bigl(\frac{n_{2}\,\Delta_{R}}{d_{\mathrm{m}}^{3/2}}+\frac{\sqrt{n_{2}\log(n\bar{d})}\,\Delta_{R}}{d_{\mathrm{m}}}+\Delta_{R}\,\log(n\bar{d})\Bigr)\;\lesssim\;\frac{\sqrt{n_{2}}}{d_{\mathrm{m}}}\,\mathrm{polylog}(n\bar{d}).

\Trivlist

Decompose

B_{t}^{(R)}=\mathbb{E}[B_{t}^{(R)}\mid\mathcal{D}_{1}]+(B_{t}^{(R)}-\mathbb{E}[B_{t}^{(R)}\mid\mathcal{D}_{1}])
.

Conditional expectation.

With
q_{m,t}:=(\widehat{\Theta}_{R}[m]-\Theta^{\star}_{R}[m])^{\top}\theta_{t}^{\star}
,

\mathbb{E}[B_{t}^{(R)}\mid\mathcal{D}_{1}]=n_{2}\cdot\nu_{t}\cdot\mathbb{E}_{m\sim\pi_{t}}[\sigma^{\prime}(\eta_{t,m,m^{\prime}}^{\star})\widehat{\Theta}_{R}[m]\,q_{m,t}]\asymp\frac{n_{2}}{d_{\mathrm{t}}d_{\mathrm{m}}^{2}}\sum_{m=1}^{d_{\mathrm{m}}}\bar{c}_{t,m}\,\widehat{\Theta}_{R}[m]\,q_{m,t},

where
\bar{c}_{t,m}\leq C_{B}
is bounded. By Cauchy–Schwarz and

\sum_{m}\left\lVert\widehat{\Theta}_{R}[m]\right\rVert^{2}\leq r
,

\left\lVert\mathbb{E}[B_{t}^{(R)}\mid\mathcal{D}_{1}]\right\rVert\leq C\frac{n_{2}}{d_{\mathrm{t}}d_{\mathrm{m}}^{2}}\,\sqrt{r}\,\Bigl(\sum_{m}q_{m,t}^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}\leq C\frac{n_{2}}{d_{\mathrm{t}}d_{\mathrm{m}}^{3/2}}\Delta_{R}\left\lVert\theta_{t}^{\star}\right\rVert_{2},

using
\sum_{m}q_{m,t}^{2}=\left\lVert(\widehat{\Theta}_{R}-\Theta^{\star}_{R})\theta_{t}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\left\lVert\widehat{\Theta}_{R}-\Theta^{\star}_{R}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\left\lVert\theta_{t}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}

and
\left\lVert\theta_{t}^{\star}\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert\Theta^{\star}_{L}\right\rVert_{2,\infty}\leq C\sqrt{d}_{\mathrm{t}}
.
Hence

\left\lVert\mathbb{E}[B_{t}^{(R)}\mid\mathcal{D}_{1}]\right\rVert\leq Cn_{2}\Delta_{R}/d_{\mathrm{m}}^{3/2}
.

Fluctuation.

The centered summands
Z_{i,t}^{(R)}
have envelope

\left\lVert Z_{i,t}^{(R)}\right\rVert\leq\mathbf{1}\{t_{i}=t\}\,C_{B}\,\left\lVert\widehat{\Theta}_{R}\right\rVert_{2,\infty}\cdot|q_{m_{i},t}|\leq C\,d_{\mathrm{m}}^{-1/2}\cdot\Delta_{R}\sqrt{d}_{\mathrm{t}}\leq C\Delta_{R}

(since
\sqrt{d}_{\mathrm{t}}/\sqrt{d}_{\mathrm{m}}=O(1)
in the balanced regime), and variance
proxy

\sum_{i}\mathbb{E}\left\lVert Z_{i,t}^{(R)}\right\rVert^{2}\leq n_{2}\nu_{t}\cdot\mathbb{E}_{m}[\Delta_{R}^{2}/d_{\mathrm{m}}]\asymp n_{2}\Delta_{R}^{2}/(d_{\mathrm{t}}d_{\mathrm{m}})\asymp n_{2}\Delta_{R}^{2}/d_{\mathrm{m}}^{2}
.
Vector Bernstein gives, for any
x>0
,

\left\lVert B_{t}^{(R)}-\mathbb{E}[B_{t}^{(R)}\mid\mathcal{D}_{1}]\right\rVert\leq C(\sqrt{n_{2}x}\Delta_{R}/d_{\mathrm{m}}+\Delta_{R}x)

with probability at least
1-2e^{-x}
. Setting

x=Ca\log(n\bar{d})
and union-bounding over
t\in[d_{\mathrm{t}}]
gives the
fluctuation bound.

Combining.

On
\mathcal{E}_{\rm init}
,
\Delta_{R}\lesssim\Delta_{F}/d_{\mathrm{m}}\lesssim\sqrt{\bar{d}^{\,3}/n_{1}}/d_{\mathrm{m}}\asymp\sqrt{\bar{d}/n_{1}}
, so

n_{2}\Delta_{R}/d_{\mathrm{m}}^{3/2}\lesssim n_{2}\sqrt{\bar{d}/n_{1}}/d_{\mathrm{m}}^{3/2}\asymp\sqrt{n_{2}}/d_{\mathrm{m}}

under
n_{1}\asymp n_{2}
. This gives the stated rate.

Lemma B.12

(Offset bias)

With probability at least
1-n^{-a}
,

\max_{t\in[d_{\mathrm{t}}]}\left\lVert B_{t}^{(O)}\right\rVert\;\lesssim\;\frac{\sqrt{n_{2}}}{d_{\mathrm{m}}}\,\mathrm{polylog}(n\bar{d}).

\Trivlist

Identical to Lemma
B.11
with the right-factor
error
\widehat{\Theta}_{R}-\Theta^{\star}_{R}
replaced by the entrywise
error
\widehat{\Theta}^{(1)}-\Theta^{\star}
. The relevant Frobenius bound
is
\left\lVert\widehat{\Theta}^{(1)}-\Theta^{\star}\right\rVert_{F}\leq\Delta_{F}
, and the
clipping ensures
|\widehat{o}_{\ell}-o_{\ell}^{\star}|\leq 2B
entrywise.
The conditional expectation is bounded by

Cn_{2}\Delta_{F}/d_{\mathrm{m}}^{5/2}
, and the fluctuation by Bernstein with the
same envelope/variance scaling. Substituting
\Delta_{F}^{2}\asymp\bar{d}^{\,3}/n_{1}

gives the stated rate.

Lemma B.13

(Higher-order moments
\beta_{t},\gamma_{t}
)

With probability at least
1-n^{-a}
,

\max_{t}\beta_{t}\;\lesssim\;\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}_{\mathrm{m}}}\,\mathrm{polylog}(n\bar{d}),\qquad\max_{t}\gamma_{t}\;\lesssim\;\frac{n_{2}}{d_{\mathrm{m}}^{5/2}}\,\mathrm{polylog}(n\bar{d}).

\Trivlist

Bound for
\beta_{t}
.
Using

(x+y)^{2}\leq 2x^{2}+2y^{2}
, we split

\beta_{t}\leq 2\beta_{t}^{(R)}+2\beta_{t}^{(O)}
where

\beta_{t}^{(R)}
uses the right-factor part and
\beta_{t}^{(O)}
the
offset part. Since

|\widehat{\Theta}_{R}[m]^{\top}v|\leq\left\lVert\widehat{\Theta}_{R}\right\rVert_{2,\infty}\leq Cd_{\mathrm{m}}^{-1/2}
,
the conditional expectation of
\beta_{t}^{(R)}
is bounded by

Cn_{2}/(d_{\mathrm{t}}d_{\mathrm{m}}^{3/2})\sum_{m}q_{m,t}^{2}\leq Cn_{2}\Delta_{R}^{2}/d_{\mathrm{m}}^{1/2}
,
and similarly
\mathbb{E}\beta_{t}^{(O)}\leq Cn_{2}\Delta_{F}^{2}/d_{\mathrm{m}}^{7/2}
.
Substituting the rates
\Delta_{R}^{2}\lesssim\Delta_{F}^{2}/d_{\mathrm{m}}^{2}
and

\Delta_{F}^{2}\asymp\bar{d}^{\,3}/n_{1}
, we obtain

\mathbb{E}\beta_{t}\lesssim n_{2}\bar{d}/(n_{1}d_{\mathrm{m}}^{1/2})\asymp\sqrt{1/d_{\mathrm{m}}}
(the
balanced-regime simplification). Bernstein on the fluctuation gives
the stated rate uniformly via union bound.

Bound for
\gamma_{t}
.

|\widehat{\Theta}_{R}[m]^{\top}v|^{3}\leq\left\lVert\widehat{\Theta}_{R}\right\rVert_{2,\infty}^{3}\leq Cd_{\mathrm{m}}^{-3/2}
,
and
M_{t}\asymp n_{2}/d_{\mathrm{t}}\asymp n_{2}/d_{\mathrm{m}}
(balanced regime), so

\gamma_{t}\leq M_{t}\cdot Cd_{\mathrm{m}}^{-3/2}\leq Cn_{2}/d_{\mathrm{m}}^{5/2}
deterministically
on
\mathcal{E}_{\rm init}
and the row-coverage event.

Lemma B.14

(Row curvature)

With probability at least
1-n^{-a}
,

\min_{t\in[d_{\mathrm{t}}]}\lambda_{t}\geq cn_{2}/d_{\mathrm{m}}^{2}
for an absolute constant

c>0
.

\Trivlist

The conditional expectation of the Hessian is

B.6
Block IV: re-centering and pairwise Gram identity

The recentering step is essential to upgrade the column-update curvature
in Block VI. Without it, the row factor
\widehat{\Theta}_{L}
generally
does not satisfy the row-centering gauge, and the pairwise Gram identity
of Lemma

Lemma B.16

(Recentering preserves pairwise differences)

Let
\overline{\Theta}_{L}:=P_{\perp}\widehat{\Theta}_{L}
, where

P_{\perp}:=I_{d_{\mathrm{t}}}-d_{\mathrm{t}}^{-1}\mathbf{1}_{d_{\mathrm{t}}}\mathbf{1}_{d_{\mathrm{t}}}^{\top}
. Then
for every

\overline{\Theta}_{L}[u]-\overline{\Theta}_{L}[v]=\widehat{\Theta}_{L}[u]-\widehat{\Theta}_{L}[v]

\left\lVert\overline{\Theta}_{L}-\Theta^{\star}_{L}\right\rVert_{2,\infty}\leq 2\,\left\lVert\widehat{\Theta}_{L}-\Theta^{\star}_{L}\right\rVert_{2,\infty},\qquad\left\lVert\overline{\Theta}_{L}-\Theta^{\star}_{L}\right\rVert_{F}\leq\left\lVert\widehat{\Theta}_{L}-\Theta^{\star}_{L}\right\rVert_{F}.

\Trivlist

Since
\Theta^{\star}_{L}
satisfies
\mathbf{1}_{d_{\mathrm{t}}}^{\top}\Theta^{\star}_{L}=0
,
we have
\Theta^{\star}_{L}=P_{\perp}\Theta^{\star}_{L}
and so

\overline{\Theta}_{L}-\Theta^{\star}_{L}=P_{\perp}(\widehat{\Theta}_{L}-\Theta^{\star}_{L})
.
For pairwise differences, the centering subtraction is constant in

u,v
, so it cancels:
\overline{\Theta}_{L}[u]-\overline{\Theta}_{L}[v]=\widehat{\Theta}_{L}[u]-\widehat{\Theta}_{L}[v]

exactly. For the row-norm, with
E:=\widehat{\Theta}_{L}-\Theta^{\star}_{L}
,

(P_{\perp}E)_{u}=E_{u}-\bar{E}
where
\bar{E}:=d_{\mathrm{t}}^{-1}\sum_{v}E_{v}
, so

\left\lVert(P_{\perp}E)_{u}\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert E_{u}\right\rVert+\left\lVert\bar{E}\right\rVert\leq 2\left\lVert E\right\rVert_{2,\infty}
.
The Frobenius bound follows from
P_{\perp}
being an orthogonal
projection.

Lemma B.17

(Pairwise Gram identity)

For
\Theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{t}}\times r}
with

\mathbf{1}_{d_{\mathrm{t}}}^{\top}\Theta=0
,

\sum_{1\leq u<v\leq d_{\mathrm{t}}}(\Theta[u]-\Theta[v])(\Theta[u]-\Theta[v])^{\top}=d_{\mathrm{t}}\,\Theta^{\top}\Theta.

Consequently, under uniform pair sampling on
[d_{\mathrm{t}}]^{2}
,

\mathbb{E}\bigl[(\Theta[U]-\Theta[V])(\Theta[U]-\Theta[V])^{\top}\;\big|\;\Theta\bigr]=\frac{2}{d_{\mathrm{t}}-1}\,\Theta^{\top}\Theta.

\Trivlist

Compute the ordered sum

\sum_{u=1}^{d_{\mathrm{t}}}\sum_{v=1}^{d_{\mathrm{t}}}(\Theta[u]-\Theta[v])(\Theta[u]-\Theta[v])^{\top}=2d_{\mathrm{t}}\,\Theta^{\top}\Theta-2\Bigl(\sum_{u}\Theta[u]\Bigr)\Bigl(\sum_{u}\Theta[u]\Bigr)^{\top}.

The second term vanishes by centering. The unordered sum is half the
ordered sum, giving the first identity. Dividing by

\binom{d_{\mathrm{t}}}{2}
yields the conditional expectation.

\left\lVert\overline{\Theta}_{L}-\Theta^{\star}_{L}\right\rVert_{F}\leq\sqrt{d}_{\mathrm{t}}\,\epsilon_{L}

v\notin\widehat{S}_{t}
,
\widehat{\theta}_{u}\geq\widehat{\theta}_{v}
under the
deterministic tie-breaking rule. Hence

\theta_{u}\geq\widehat{\theta}_{u}-\varepsilon_{n}\geq\widehat{\theta}_{v}-\varepsilon_{n}\geq\theta_{v}-2\varepsilon_{n}
.
Since
v\in S_{t}
,
\theta_{v}\geq\theta_{(K)}
, so

\theta_{u}\geq\theta_{(K)}-2\varepsilon_{n}
; and since
u\notin S_{t}
,

\theta_{u}\leq\theta_{(K+1)}
. Combining,

\theta_{(K)}-2\varepsilon_{n}\leq\theta_{u}\leq\theta_{(K+1)}
, and using

\tau_{K}(t)\in[\theta_{(K+1)},\theta_{(K)}]
,

|\theta_{u}-\tau_{K}(t)|\leq 2\varepsilon_{n}
. The false-negative case is
symmetric.

C.3
Exact recovery under a margin condition

Corollary C.2

(Exact top-
K
recovery)

Define
\Delta_{K}(t):=\theta_{(K)}-\theta_{(K+1)}
. If

\Delta_{K}(t)>4\varepsilon_{n}
, then on
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
the boundary
mass
\mathcal{R}_{K,t}(2\varepsilon_{n};\Theta^{\star})=0
, hence

\widehat{S}_{t}=S_{t}
. In particular, if

\min_{t\in[d_{\mathrm{t}}]}\Delta_{K}(t)>4\varepsilon_{n}
, then exact top-
K

recovery holds simultaneously over all tasks with probability at least

1-n^{-a}
.

\Trivlist

Under
\Delta_{K}(t)>4\varepsilon_{n}
, every
m\in S_{t}
has

\theta_{m}-\tau_{K}(t)\geq\Delta_{K}(t)/2>2\varepsilon_{n}
, and symmetrically
for
m\notin S_{t}
; so

\mathcal{R}_{K,t}(2\varepsilon_{n};\Theta^{\star})=0
, and
Proposition
C.1
applies. The simultaneous
statement follows by union bound over
t\in[d_{\mathrm{t}}]
.

C.4
Minimax-optimality remark

The proposition is a deterministic reduction from entrywise score
estimation to top-
K
Hamming accuracy. For minimax optimality we
appeal to the single-task BTL ranking literature:

7
characterize the minimax rate for normalized
Hamming partial recovery in BTL top-
K
ranking and show that MLE
attains both partial and exact recovery thresholds, while

8
establish the minimax sample complexity for

identification through entrywise score control combined

K

\bar{d}
instead of
\bar{d}^{\,2}
in the

D.1
The one-step estimator and its plug-in operators

Recall the cross-fitting one-step procedure in Section
4
.
For clarity in the matrix case, we restate it without cross-fitting
notation: given the auxiliary-sample initializer

(\widehat{\Theta},\widehat{P}_{\mathbb{T}},\widehat{H}_{\Gamma})
of
Appendix
A.4
, the one-step estimator for

\psi_{\Gamma}(\Theta^{\star})=\left\langle\Gamma,\,\Theta^{\star}\right\rangle
is

\widehat{\psi}_{\Gamma}:=\psi_{\Gamma}(\widehat{\Theta})+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{\eta}\bigl(Y_{i},\widehat{\eta}_{i}\bigr)\,\left\langle\widehat{H}_{\Gamma},\,X_{i}\right\rangle,\qquad\widehat{\eta}_{i}:=\left\langle\widehat{\Theta},\,X_{i}\right\rangle,

(16)

where the evaluation sample
\{(X_{i},Y_{i})\}_{i=1}^{n}
is independent of
the auxiliary sample
\mathcal{D}_{\rm aux}
used to compute

(\widehat{\Theta},\widehat{P}_{\mathbb{T}},\widehat{G},\widehat{H}_{\Gamma})
.
Here
\widehat{H}_{\Gamma}
solves the estimated information equation

(\widehat{P}_{\mathbb{T}}\widehat{G}\widehat{P}_{\mathbb{T}})\widehat{H}_{\Gamma}=\widehat{P}_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma
,
with
\widehat{G}
defined in (
2
). Cross-fitting
extends the analysis with no change to the rates; we suppress fold
indices throughout.

For a finite contrast family
\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{q}
, let

\psi_{j}:=\psi_{\Gamma_{j}}(\Theta^{\star}),\quad\widehat{\psi}_{j}:=\widehat{\psi}_{\Gamma_{j}},\quad H_{j}^{\star}:=A^{-1}P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma_{j},\quad\widehat{H}_{j}:=\widehat{A}^{-1}\widehat{P}_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma_{j},

\phi_{j}(W_{i}):=s_{\eta}(Y_{i},\eta_{i}^{\star})\left\langle H_{j}^{\star},\,X_{i}\right\rangle,\quad\widehat{\phi}_{j}(W_{i}):=s_{\eta}(Y_{i},\widehat{\eta}_{i})\left\langle\widehat{H}_{j},\,X_{i}\right\rangle,

\sigma_{j}^{2}:=V_{\rm eff}(\Gamma_{j})=\mathbb{E}^{\star}[\phi_{j}^{2}]=\left\langle P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma_{j},\,A^{-1}P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma_{j}\right\rangle,\quad\Sigma_{jk}:=\mathbb{E}^{\star}[\phi_{j}\phi_{k}]=\left\langle P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma_{j},\,A^{-1}P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma_{k}\right\rangle,

and the standardized oracle coordinate

Z_{ij}:=\phi_{j}(W_{i})/\sigma_{j}
, so

and

D.2
Single-contrast (1D) semiparametric efficiency lower bound

For any fixed contrast
\Gamma\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{t}}\times d_{\mathrm{m}}}
, the

semiparametric efficiency bound
for any regular estimator

\widehat{\psi}
of
\psi_{\Gamma}(\Theta^{\star})
is

\mathrm{Var}^{\star}(\widehat{\psi})\;\geq\;\frac{1}{n}\,V_{\rm eff}(\Gamma),\qquad V_{\rm eff}(\Gamma)=\left\langle P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma,\,A^{-1}P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma\right\rangle.

(17)

We give the proof following the standard information-inequality
argument; the steps are the matrix specialization of

[
21
, Section G.2]
.

Theorem D.1

(1D semiparametric efficiency bound)

Suppose
A
is invertible on
\mathbb{T}
, and let

\widehat{\psi}
be any locally unbiased estimator of
\psi_{\Gamma}

under any one-parameter submodel

\Theta_{\varepsilon}=\Theta^{\star}+\varepsilon H
for
H\in\mathbb{T}
.
Then (
17
) holds. Moreover, the EIF

\phi_{\Gamma}=s_{\eta}(Y,\eta^{\star})\left\langle H_{\Gamma}^{\star},\,X\right\rangle
with

H_{\Gamma}^{\star}=A^{-1}P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma
attains the bound:

\mathbb{E}^{\star}[\phi_{\Gamma}^{2}]=V_{\rm eff}(\Gamma)
.

\Trivlist

We give the full information inequality argument.

Step 1: differentiating the unbiasedness identity.

Fix
H\in\mathbb{T}
and consider the one-parameter submodel

\Theta_{\varepsilon}=\Theta^{\star}+\varepsilon H
, with sampling density

p_{\Theta_{\varepsilon},\Pi^{\star}}(X,Y)=g_{\Pi^{\star}}(X)p(Y\mid\left\langle X,\,\Theta_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle)
.
Local unbiasedness of
\widehat{\psi}
means

\mathbb{E}_{\Theta_{\varepsilon}}[\widehat{\psi}]=\psi_{\Gamma}(\Theta_{\varepsilon})=\left\langle\Gamma,\,\Theta_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle

in a neighborhood of
\varepsilon=0
. Differentiating both sides at

\varepsilon=0
gives

\partial_{\varepsilon}\big|_{0}\mathbb{E}_{\Theta_{\varepsilon}}[\widehat{\psi}]=\left\langle\Gamma,\,H\right\rangle=\left\langle P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma,\,H\right\rangle,

(18)

where the second equality uses
H\in\mathbb{T}
(so

(I-P_{\mathbb{T}})\Gamma
is orthogonal to
H
).

Step 2: the score identity.

The directional score along the submodel is

\partial_{\varepsilon}\log p_{\Theta_{\varepsilon},\Pi^{\star}}(X_{i},Y_{i})\big|_{0}=s_{\eta}(Y_{i},\eta_{i}^{\star})\left\langle H,\,X_{i}\right\rangle

(differentiating
\log p
in the parameter
\varepsilon
). By the
standard score identity (which holds for any random variable with
finite second moment under regularity),

\partial_{\varepsilon}\big|_{0}\mathbb{E}_{\Theta_{\varepsilon}}[\widehat{\psi}]=\mathbb{E}^{\star}\!\Bigl[\widehat{\psi}\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{\eta}(Y_{i},\eta_{i}^{\star})\left\langle H,\,X_{i}\right\rangle\Bigr].

(19)

Subtracting the constant
\psi_{\Gamma}(\Theta^{\star})
(which doesn’t
affect the score-product expectation by centering) gives

\partial_{\varepsilon}\big|_{0}\mathbb{E}_{\Theta_{\varepsilon}}[\widehat{\psi}]=\mathbb{E}^{\star}[(\widehat{\psi}-\psi_{\Gamma})\sum_{i}s_{\eta}(Y_{i},\eta_{i}^{\star})\left\langle H,\,X_{i}\right\rangle]
.

Step 3: Cauchy–Schwarz and Rayleigh-quotient maximization.

Combining (
18
) with (
19
) and
applying Cauchy–Schwarz,

\left\langle P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma,\,H\right\rangle^{2}\leq\mathrm{Var}^{\star}(\widehat{\psi})\cdot\mathbb{E}^{\star}\!\Bigl[\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{\eta}(Y_{i},\eta_{i}^{\star})\left\langle H,\,X_{i}\right\rangle\Bigr)^{2}\Bigr].

By independence of the
n
observations and the score-centering
identity
\mathbb{E}^{\star}[s_{\eta}(Y_{i},\eta_{i}^{\star})\left\langle H,\,X_{i}\right\rangle]=0
,

\mathbb{E}^{\star}\!\Bigl[\Bigl(\sum_{i}s_{\eta}(Y_{i},\eta_{i}^{\star})\left\langle H,\,X_{i}\right\rangle\Bigr)^{2}\Bigr]=n\,\mathbb{E}^{\star}[s_{\eta}(Y,\eta^{\star})^{2}\left\langle H,\,X\right\rangle^{2}]=n\,\left\langle H,\,AH\right\rangle,

where the last step uses the definition of the operator

G
in (
1
) and the fact that

\mathbb{E}^{\star}[s_{\eta}^{2}\mid X]=I(\eta^{\star})
. Thus

\mathrm{Var}^{\star}(\widehat{\psi})\;\geq\;\frac{1}{n}\,\frac{\left\langle P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma,\,H\right\rangle^{2}}{\left\langle H,\,AH\right\rangle}.

Taking the supremum over
H\in\mathbb{T}\setminus\{0\}
on the right,
the Rayleigh quotient is maximized at

H=A^{-1}P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma=H_{\Gamma}^{\star}
, with maximum value

\left\langle P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma,\,A^{-1}P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma\right\rangle=V_{\rm eff}(\Gamma)

by direct computation:

\left\langle P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma,\,H_{\Gamma}^{\star}\right\rangle^{2}/\left\langle H_{\Gamma}^{\star},\,AH_{\Gamma}^{\star}\right\rangle=V_{\rm eff}(\Gamma)^{2}/V_{\rm eff}(\Gamma)=V_{\rm eff}(\Gamma)
.
This proves (
17
).

Step 4: attainment.

For the EIF
\phi_{\Gamma}=s_{\eta}(Y,\eta^{\star})\left\langle H_{\Gamma}^{\star},\,X\right\rangle
,

\mathbb{E}^{\star}[\phi_{\Gamma}^{2}]=\mathbb{E}^{\star}[s_{\eta}^{2}\left\langle H_{\Gamma}^{\star},\,X\right\rangle^{2}]=\left\langle H_{\Gamma}^{\star},\,GH_{\Gamma}^{\star}\right\rangle=\left\langle H_{\Gamma}^{\star},\,AH_{\Gamma}^{\star}\right\rangle=V_{\rm eff}(\Gamma),

using
H_{\Gamma}^{\star}\in\mathbb{T}
so
P_{\mathbb{T}}H_{\Gamma}^{\star}=H_{\Gamma}^{\star}

and the information equation
AH_{\Gamma}^{\star}=P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma
.

D.3
Multivariate (Loewner) lower bound by 1D + arbitrary
u

For a fixed finite contrast family

\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{q}
(
q=O(1)
), the candidate efficient
covariance is

\Sigma_{jk}=\left\langle P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma_{j},\,A^{-1}P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma_{k}\right\rangle
.

Proposition D.2

(Joint semiparametric efficiency lower bound)

Let
\bar{\psi}=(\bar{\psi}_{1},\ldots,\bar{\psi}_{q})^{\top}
be any regular
estimator of
\psi=(\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{q})^{\top}
, with

\sqrt{n}(\bar{\psi}-\psi)\rightsquigarrow\mathcal{N}(0,\bar{\Sigma})
for
some covariance
\bar{\Sigma}\succeq 0
. Then

\bar{\Sigma}-\Sigma\succeq 0
in Loewner order; equivalently,

u^{\top}\bar{\Sigma}u\geq u^{\top}\Sigma u
for every
u\in\mathbb{R}^{q}
.

\Trivlist

We reduce to the scalar bound applied at every linear combination

u^{\top}\psi=\psi_{\Gamma_{u}}
for the combined contrast

\Gamma_{u}:=\sum_{j=1}^{q}u_{j}\Gamma_{j}
.

Step 1:
u^{\top}\bar{\psi}
is a regular estimator of
\psi_{\Gamma_{u}}
.

Since
\bar{\psi}
is regular for the vector
\psi
, the linear
functional
u^{\top}\bar{\psi}
is regular for the corresponding scalar
target
u^{\top}\psi=\sum_{j}u_{j}\psi_{j}=\sum_{j}u_{j}\left\langle\Gamma_{j},\,\Theta^{\star}\right\rangle=\left\langle\Gamma_{u},\,\Theta^{\star}\right\rangle=\psi_{\Gamma_{u}}(\Theta^{\star})
.
Its asymptotic variance is
u^{\top}\bar{\Sigma}u
by the continuous
mapping theorem.

Step 2: scalar lower bound.

Apply Theorem
D.1
to the scalar functional

\psi_{\Gamma_{u}}
: for every regular estimator,

\mathrm{Var}^{\star}(u^{\top}\bar{\psi})\geq\frac{1}{n}V_{\rm eff}(\Gamma_{u}).

Taking the asymptotic variance,
u^{\top}\bar{\Sigma}u\geq V_{\rm eff}(\Gamma_{u})=\left\langle P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma_{u},\,A^{-1}P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma_{u}\right\rangle
.

Step 3: bilinearity.

By linearity of
P_{\mathbb{T}}
,

P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma_{u}=\sum_{j}u_{j}P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma_{j}
, and by
bilinearity of
\left\langle\cdot,\,A^{-1}\cdot\right\rangle
,

\left\langle P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma_{u},\,A^{-1}P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma_{u}\right\rangle=\sum_{j,k}u_{j}u_{k}\left\langle P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma_{j},\,A^{-1}P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma_{k}\right\rangle=u^{\top}\Sigma u.

Combining steps 2 and 3 gives
u^{\top}(\bar{\Sigma}-\Sigma)u\geq 0
.
Since this holds for every
u\in\mathbb{R}^{q}
,
\bar{\Sigma}\succeq\Sigma
in
Loewner order.

Remark D.3

(Singular covariance and redundant contrasts)

The covariance
\Sigma
may be singular without invalidating the
proof. Suppose
\Gamma_{3}=\Gamma_{1}+\Gamma_{2}
so that

\phi_{3}=\phi_{1}+\phi_{2}
and the third row of
\Sigma
is the exact
sum of the first two. The Loewner bound still holds: the proof above
does not require invertibility, only the scalar efficiency bound for
every linear combination
u^{\top}\psi
. If
u^{\top}\Sigma u=0
for
some
u\neq 0
, then the corresponding contrast has zero efficient
variance; this is an exact local redundancy and produces a degenerate
Gaussian limit on a lower-dimensional subspace. In particular, the
inverse
A^{-1}
is well-defined on

\mathrm{span}(P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma_{1},\ldots,P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma_{q})
,
so the proof is unaffected.

D.4
Closed-form matrix tangent projector

We write out the closed form of
P_{\mathbb{T}}
before proceeding to
the decomposition.

Lemma D.4

(Closed-form matrix tangent projector)

Let
\Theta^{\star}=U^{\star}\Sigma^{\star}(V^{\star})^{\top}
with

U^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{t}}\times r}
,
V^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{m}}\times r}
and

(U^{\star})^{\top}\mathbf{1}_{d_{\mathrm{t}}}=0
(the row-centering gauge of
Section
2
enforces this). Define

P_{U^{\star}}:=U^{\star}(U^{\star})^{\top}
,

P_{V^{\star}}:=V^{\star}(V^{\star})^{\top}
, and

P_{\mathbf{1}^{\perp}}:=I_{d_{\mathrm{t}}}-d_{\mathrm{t}}^{-1}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}
. Then

P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma=P_{U^{\star}}\Gamma+P_{\mathbf{1}^{\perp}}\Gamma\,P_{V^{\star}}-P_{U^{\star}}\Gamma\,P_{V^{\star}},\qquad\Gamma\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{t}}\times d_{\mathrm{m}}}.

(20)

Equivalently, under standard vectorization,

P_{\mathbb{T}}=P_{U^{\star}}\otimes I+P_{\mathbf{1}^{\perp}}\otimes P_{V^{\star}}-P_{U^{\star}}\otimes P_{V^{\star}}
.

\Trivlist

The (unconstrained) rank-
r
tangent space at
\Theta^{\star}
is

\{U^{\star}A^{\top}+B(V^{\star})^{\top}:A\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{m}}\times r},B\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{t}}\times r}\}
.
The row-centering identification constraint

\mathbf{1}_{d_{\mathrm{t}}}^{\top}\Gamma=0
restricts the
B
-component to lie
in
\mathbf{1}_{d_{\mathrm{t}}}^{\perp}
. Direct verification of orthogonality
yields the formula (
20
); details follow
the general Tucker case in

[
21
, Lemma F.4]
.

This closed form makes each remainder term in
Appendix
D.5
computable in closed form. In
particular, for a sparse score-gap contrast

\Gamma=e_{t}(e_{m}-e_{m^{\prime}})^{\top}
, the projection

P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma
is a low-rank matrix whose Frobenius norm is

\Theta(\sqrt{1/d_{\mathrm{t}}}+\sqrt{1/d_{\mathrm{m}}})
, and the leakage component

(I-P_{\mathbb{T}})\Gamma
is bounded entrywise by an
incoherence-dependent constant (Lemma
A.10
).

D.5
One-step error decomposition (matrix form)

Let
\Delta:=\widehat{\Theta}-\Theta^{\star}
and write

S_{T}(H)(W):=s_{\eta}(Y,\left\langle X,\,T\right\rangle)\left\langle H,\,X\right\rangle
. Adding and subtracting
the oracle correction term in (
16
),

\widehat{\psi}_{\Gamma}-\psi_{\Gamma}(\Theta^{\star})=\underbrace{(\mathbb{P}_{n}-\mathbb{P}^{\star})\phi_{\Gamma}}_{\text{(I) leading i.i.d.\ EIF average}}+R_{n}^{\Gamma},

(21)

where the remainder splits into six interpretable terms,
following
[
21
, Section 4.4]
:

\displaystyle R_{n}^{\Gamma}

\displaystyle=R_{\rm emp}^{H}+R_{\rm emp}^{\eta}+R_{\rm proj}+R_{\rm Hbias}+R_{\rm 1st}+R_{\rm 2nd},

\displaystyle R_{\rm emp}^{H}

\displaystyle:=(\mathbb{P}_{n}-\mathbb{P}^{\star})\bigl[s_{\eta}(Y,\widehat{\eta})\left\langle\widehat{H}_{\Gamma}-H_{\Gamma}^{\star},\,X\right\rangle\bigr],

\displaystyle R_{\rm emp}^{\eta}

\displaystyle:=(\mathbb{P}_{n}-\mathbb{P}^{\star})\bigl[\bigl(s_{\eta}(Y,\widehat{\eta})-s_{\eta}(Y,\eta^{\star})\bigr)\left\langle H_{\Gamma}^{\star},\,X\right\rangle\bigr],

\displaystyle R_{\rm proj}

\displaystyle:=\left\langle(I-P_{\mathbb{T}})\Gamma,\,\Delta\right\rangle,

\displaystyle R_{\rm Hbias}

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{P}^{\star}\bigl[S_{\widehat{\Theta}}(\widehat{H}_{\Gamma})-S_{\widehat{\Theta}}(H_{\Gamma}^{\star})\bigr],

\displaystyle R_{\rm 1st}

\displaystyle:=\left\langle P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma,\,\Delta\right\rangle+\mathbb{P}^{\star}\bigl[S_{\widehat{\Theta}}(H_{\Gamma}^{\star})-S_{\Theta^{\star}}(H_{\Gamma}^{\star})\bigr],

\displaystyle R_{\rm 2nd}

\displaystyle:=O(\left\lVert\Delta\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2})\text{ second-order score remainder of $R_{\rm 1st}$.}

The first-order cancellation term
R_{\rm 1st}
collects the

\left\langle P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma,\,\Delta\right\rangle
bias and the population correction;
together they cancel up to a tangent-leakage piece, because of the
information equation
AH_{\Gamma}^{\star}=P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma
. The
second-order term
R_{\rm 2nd}
is the Taylor remainder from the
score expansion in
R_{\rm 1st}
, kept separate for cleanliness.

D.6
Single-contrast remainder bound

The combined-error theorem of
[
21
, Theorem 4.5]

gives a sharp term-by-term bound on each component of
(
21
). We restate the matrix specialization with
explicit dependence on
C_{A}
,
\bar{d}
, and
\left\lVert\Gamma\right\rVert_{1}
.

Theorem D.5

(Single-contrast remainder bound)

Fix any
a>0
and any contrast
\Gamma\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{t}}\times d_{\mathrm{m}}}

satisfying Assumptions
A.8
and

A.9
. Under
Assumptions
A.1
–
A.11
, with
probability at least
1-n^{-a}
,

|R_{n}^{\Gamma}|\;\leq\;C(\mu,r,\kappa,B,c_{B},C_{B})\,C_{A}\,\left\lVert\Gamma\right\rVert_{1}\,\frac{\bar{d}\,\log^{c}(n\bar{d})}{n}.

(22)

Equivalently,

\sqrt{n}\,|R_{n}^{\Gamma}|\leq C\,C_{A}\left\lVert\Gamma\right\rVert_{1}\sqrt{\bar{d}\log^{c}(n\bar{d})/n}
,
which is
o(1)
under the CLT condition

C_{A}\sqrt{\bar{d}\log^{c}(n\bar{d})/n}\to 0
of
Theorem
4.1
.

\Trivlist

[Term-by-term bounds]
We outline the per-term bounds of

[
21
, Appendices G.4–G.10]
. Each bound is

C(\mu,r,m)\left\lVert\Gamma\right\rVert_{1}
times the displayed dimensional factor
times
C_{A}^{(\leq 1)}
(linear at most). Throughout we use the
abbreviation
\rho:=\sqrt{\bar{d}\log^{c}\bar{d}/n}
for the spectral
subspace-perturbation parameter, which satisfies

\rho\asymp r_{n}
under the bounded-signal assumption.

(i) Direction-error empirical process
R_{\rm emp}^{H}
.

By the Frobenius reduction (Lemma
A.4
) and

\ell_{1}
extraction over the basis decomposition of
\Gamma
,

\mathrm{Var}^{\star}(s_{\eta}(Y,\widehat{\eta})\left\langle\widehat{H}_{\Gamma}-H_{\Gamma}^{\star},\,X\right\rangle)\leq C_{B}\left\lVert\Gamma\right\rVert_{1}^{2}\rho^{2}/d^{\star}\cdot d^{\star}=C_{B}\left\lVert\Gamma\right\rVert_{1}^{2}\rho^{2}

under the resolvent identity for
\widehat{A}^{-1}-A^{-1}
and the
spectral-perturbation bound on
\left\lVert\widehat{P}_{\mathbb{T}}-P_{\mathbb{T}}\right\rVert_{\rm op}
.
The sub-exponential envelope is bounded by

\left\lVert\widehat{H}_{\Gamma}-H_{\Gamma}^{\star}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq C_{A}\left\lVert\Gamma\right\rVert_{1}

times an incoherence factor. Bernstein’s inequality yields

|R_{\rm emp}^{H}|\leq C\left\lVert\Gamma\right\rVert_{1}\rho/\sqrt{n}+C\,C_{A}\left\lVert\Gamma\right\rVert_{1}\bar{d}\log\bar{d}/n
.
The first term is sub-leading at the CLT scale because

\rho/\sqrt{n}=\sqrt{\bar{d}\log\bar{d}}/n=o(\sqrt{\bar{d}/n}/\sqrt{n})

in our regime; the second is the bottleneck term and is the only place
where
C_{A}
appears.

(ii) Score-perturbation empirical process
R_{\rm emp}^{\eta}
.

By the Lipschitz property of the BTL score,

|s_{\eta}(Y,\widehat{\eta})-s_{\eta}(Y,\eta^{\star})|\leq|\widehat{\eta}-\eta^{\star}|=|\left\langle\Delta,\,X\right\rangle|\leq 2\left\lVert\Delta\right\rVert_{\infty}
,
and Bernstein’s inequality on the centered Bernoulli noise gives

|R_{\rm emp}^{\eta}|\leq C\,\left\lVert\Gamma\right\rVert_{1}\,\left\lVert\Delta\right\rVert_{\infty}\sqrt{\log(n\bar{d})/n}
,
which is dominated by
R_{\rm emp}^{H}
.

(iii) Projection leakage
R_{\rm proj}
.

|R_{\rm proj}|=|\left\langle(I-P_{\mathbb{T}})\Gamma,\,\Delta\right\rangle|\leq\left\lVert(I-P_{\mathbb{T}})\Gamma\right\rVert_{F}\left\lVert\Delta\right\rVert_{F}
.
By the closed form (
20
) and incoherence,

\left\lVert(I-P_{\mathbb{T}})\Gamma\right\rVert_{F}\leq C(\mu,r)\rho\left\lVert\Gamma\right\rVert_{F}\leq C\rho\left\lVert\Gamma\right\rVert_{1}
,
and
\left\lVert\Delta\right\rVert_{F}\lesssim\sqrt{r\bar{d}/n}
by the Frobenius
initialization rate. Combining,

|R_{\rm proj}|\leq C\left\lVert\Gamma\right\rVert_{1}/n
, which is also dominated.

(iv) H-direction bias
R_{\rm Hbias}
.

Taylor expansion gives

R_{\rm Hbias}=\left\langle\widehat{H}_{\Gamma}-H_{\Gamma}^{\star},\,(G-\widehat{G})\widehat{H}_{\Gamma}\right\rangle

plus higher-order corrections. Using the perturbation

\left\lVert G-\widehat{G}\right\rVert_{\rm op}\lesssim\left\lVert\Delta\right\rVert_{\infty}
,

\left\lVert\widehat{H}_{\Gamma}-H_{\Gamma}^{\star}\right\rVert_{F}\leq C\,C_{A}\left\lVert\Gamma\right\rVert_{1}\rho
,

\left\lVert\widehat{H}_{\Gamma}\right\rVert_{F}\leq C\,C_{A}\left\lVert\Gamma\right\rVert_{1}
, and the
Frobenius reduction,

|R_{\rm Hbias}|\leq C\,C_{A}\left\lVert\Gamma\right\rVert_{1}\,\bar{d}\log\bar{d}/n
.

(v) First-order cancellation
R_{\rm 1st}
.

By the Taylor expansion of the population score difference and the
information equation
AH_{\Gamma}^{\star}=P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma
(which makes

\left\langle P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma,\,\Delta\right\rangle+\mathbb{P}^{\star}[S_{\widehat{\Theta}}(H_{\Gamma}^{\star})-S_{\Theta^{\star}}(H_{\Gamma}^{\star})]

exactly cancel up to a normal-component term plus a second-order
remainder), we get

|R_{\rm 1st}|\leq C\,C_{A}\left\lVert\Gamma\right\rVert_{1}\,\bar{d}\log\bar{d}/n
.

(vi) Second-order score remainder
R_{\rm 2nd}
.

|R_{\rm 2nd}|\leq C\,B_{2}\mathbb{P}^{\star}[\left\langle H_{\Gamma}^{\star},\,X\right\rangle\left\lVert\Delta\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2}]\leq C\,C_{A}\left\lVert\Gamma\right\rVert_{1}\bar{d}\log\bar{d}/n

using
B_{2}\leq 1/\sigma^{2}=O(1)
and

\mathbb{P}^{\star}[\left\langle H_{\Gamma}^{\star},\,X\right\rangle^{2}]\leq V_{\rm eff}(\Gamma)\leq C\,C_{A}d^{\star}\left\lVert P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma\right\rVert_{F}^{2}/d^{\star}\leq C\,C_{A}\left\lVert\Gamma\right\rVert_{1}
.

Combining.

Summing all six terms and taking the maximum,

|R_{n}^{\Gamma}|\leq C(\mu,r,\kappa,B)C_{A}\left\lVert\Gamma\right\rVert_{1}\bar{d}\log^{c}(n\bar{d})/n
,
with the leading contribution from terms (i), (iv), (v), (vi).
Probability calibration to
1-n^{-a}
follows by setting the free
Bernstein tail constant
x=Ca\log(n\bar{d})
for
C
large.

D.7
Uniform single-contrast remainder over a contrast family

The bound in
Theorem
D.5
is per-contrast.
We extend it uniformly over a polynomial-size family by a careful union
bound.

Theorem D.6

(Uniform one-step remainder)

Let
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{t}}\times d_{\mathrm{m}}}
be any family of contrasts of
size
|\mathcal{F}|\leq(d_{\mathrm{t}}d_{\mathrm{m}})^{C_{F}}
for an absolute constant

C_{F}
, such that each
\Gamma\in\mathcal{F}
satisfies
Assumption
A.9
with a uniform alignment constant

\alpha_{\rm min}>0
and Assumption
A.8
with
uniform constants
M,C_{\psi}
. Fix any

. Then with probability
at least
1-n^{-a}
,

\max_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{F}}\,\sqrt{n}\,|R_{n}^{\Gamma}|\;\leq\;C\,C_{A}\,\sqrt{\frac{\bar{d}\,\mathrm{polylog}(n\bar{d})}{n}},

(23)

where the polylog absorbs both the logarithmic factor inherited from
Theorem
D.5
and the

\log|\mathcal{F}|\leq C_{F}\log(\bar{d})
factor from the union bound.

\Trivlist

The proof is by union bound on top of
Theorem
D.5
, with care taken so
that no
\Gamma
-dependent constants degrade.

Step 1: per-contrast bound with sharpened tail.

Theorem
D.5
produces the bound
(
22
) with probability at least

1-2e^{-x}
for the underlying Bernstein steps; tracking the free
parameter
x
through the proof yields

\Pr\Bigl[\sqrt{n}|R_{n}^{\Gamma}|\geq K\,C_{A}\left\lVert\Gamma\right\rVert_{1}\sqrt{x\,\bar{d}\,\log^{c}\bar{d}/n}\Bigr]\leq 2e^{-x}

for an absolute constant
K
depending on
(\mu,r,\kappa,B,c_{B},C_{B},c_{\nu},C_{\nu},c_{\pi},C_{\pi})
.
The right-hand side combines the contributions of all six terms; the
dominant scaling is
\sqrt{x\bar{d}/n}
from terms (iv)–(vi).

Step 2: union bound over
\mathcal{F}
.

Set
x=Ca\log(n\bar{d})+C_{F}\log\bar{d}
and apply the per-contrast bound
to each
\Gamma\in\mathcal{F}
; a union bound over the

|\mathcal{F}|\leq\bar{d}^{C_{F}}
contrasts gives

\Pr[\max_{\Gamma}\sqrt{n}|R_{n}^{\Gamma}|\geq K\,C_{A}\sqrt{x\bar{d}\log^{c}\bar{d}/n}]\leq 2|\mathcal{F}|e^{-x}\leq 2n^{-a}
.
For
\Gamma\in\mathcal{F}
,
\left\lVert\Gamma\right\rVert_{1}\leq C_{\psi}=O(1)
is
absorbed into the constant.

Step 3: rate.

The displayed bound becomes

K\,C_{A}\sqrt{(a\log(n\bar{d})+C_{F}\log\bar{d})\bar{d}\log^{c}\bar{d}/n}\leq C\,C_{A}\sqrt{\bar{d}\,\mathrm{polylog}(n\bar{d})/n}
,
absorbing both
\log
factors into the polylog.

D.8
Multivariate Berry–Esseen for the leading term

We now establish the rate
O(\sqrt{\bar{d}/n})
for the leading
i.i.d. EIF average. This is the matrix specialization of

[
21
, Appendix G.16]
, restated for completeness.
The result for the standardized scalar leading term yields the
multivariate version after polarization.

Theorem D.7

(Berry–Esseen for the standardized leading term)

Fix a single contrast
\Gamma
satisfying
Assumptions
A.8
–
A.9
, and
let
Z_{i}:=\phi_{\Gamma}(W_{i})/\sigma_{\Gamma}
be the standardized oracle
EIF coordinates with
\sigma_{\Gamma}^{2}=V_{\rm eff}(\Gamma)
. Then

\rho_{n}:=\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\Bigl|\Pr\Bigl(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}Z_{i}\leq t\Bigr)-\Phi(t)\Bigr|\;\leq\;C\,\sqrt{\frac{\bar{d}}{n}}.

(24)

\Trivlist

We compute the second and third moments of
Z_{i}
explicitly and
apply the classical (univariate) Berry–Esseen theorem.

Step 1: mean zero.

\mathbb{E}^{\star}Z_{i}=\sigma_{\Gamma}^{-1}\mathbb{E}^{\star}\phi_{\Gamma}(W_{i})=\sigma_{\Gamma}^{-1}\mathbb{E}^{\star}[s_{\eta}(Y,\eta^{\star})\left\langle H_{\Gamma}^{\star},\,X\right\rangle]
,
which vanishes by the score-centering identity

\mathbb{E}^{\star}[s_{\eta}(Y,\eta^{\star})\mid X]=0
.

Step 2: second moment.

By the definition of
\sigma_{\Gamma}^{2}
and Fisher comparability
(Lemma
A.5
),

\mathbb{E}^{\star}Z_{i}^{2}=\frac{1}{\sigma_{\Gamma}^{2}}\mathbb{E}^{\star}[s_{\eta}^{2}\left\langle H_{\Gamma}^{\star},\,X\right\rangle^{2}]=\frac{1}{\sigma_{\Gamma}^{2}}\left\langle H_{\Gamma}^{\star},\,AH_{\Gamma}^{\star}\right\rangle=\frac{V_{\rm eff}(\Gamma)}{\sigma_{\Gamma}^{2}}=1.

By the Frobenius reduction (Lemma
A.4
) applied
to
H_{\Gamma}^{\star}
,

\mathbb{E}^{\star}\left\langle H_{\Gamma}^{\star},\,X\right\rangle^{2}\asymp\left\lVert H_{\Gamma}^{\star}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}/d^{\star}
,
and Fisher comparability gives

\sigma_{\Gamma}^{2}\asymp\left\lVert H_{\Gamma}^{\star}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}/d^{\star}
, so

\frac{c_{B}}{d^{\star}}\left\lVert H_{\Gamma}^{\star}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\leq\sigma_{\Gamma}^{2}\leq\frac{C_{B}}{d^{\star}}\left\lVert H_{\Gamma}^{\star}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}.

(25)

Step 3: third absolute moment.

\mathbb{E}^{\star}|Z_{i}|^{3}=\frac{1}{\sigma_{\Gamma}^{3}}\mathbb{E}^{\star}[|s_{\eta}|^{3}|\left\langle H_{\Gamma}^{\star},\,X\right\rangle|^{3}]\leq\frac{C_{3}}{\sigma_{\Gamma}^{3}}\mathbb{E}^{\star}|\left\langle H_{\Gamma}^{\star},\,X\right\rangle|^{3}

where
C_{3}:=\mathbb{E}^{\star}[|s_{\eta}(Y,\eta^{\star})|^{3}\mid X]\leq 1
under
Assumption
A.1
(iv) (since

|s_{\eta}|\leq 1
). Now use the elementary inequality

\mathbb{E}|W|^{3}\leq(\sup|W|)\mathbb{E}|W|^{2}
with
W=\left\langle H_{\Gamma}^{\star},\,X\right\rangle
:

\mathbb{E}^{\star}|\left\langle H_{\Gamma}^{\star},\,X\right\rangle|^{3}\leq\Bigl(\sup_{x}|\left\langle H_{\Gamma}^{\star},\,x\right\rangle|\Bigr)\,\mathbb{E}^{\star}\left\langle H_{\Gamma}^{\star},\,X\right\rangle^{2}.

Since
H_{\Gamma}^{\star}\in\mathbb{T}
,

\left\langle H_{\Gamma}^{\star},\,x\right\rangle=\left\langle H_{\Gamma}^{\star},\,P_{\mathbb{T}}x\right\rangle
, so by
Cauchy–Schwarz and the tangent-projection envelope

\sup_{x}\left\lVert P_{\mathbb{T}}x\right\rVert_{F}\lesssim\sqrt{\bar{d}/d^{\star}}
(which
follows from the basis-tensor projection bound and triangle inequality
for pairwise differences; see
Lemma
D.8
below),

\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}|\left\langle H_{\Gamma}^{\star},\,x\right\rangle|\leq\left\lVert H_{\Gamma}^{\star}\right\rVert_{F}\sup_{x}\left\lVert P_{\mathbb{T}}x\right\rVert_{F}\;\lesssim\;\left\lVert H_{\Gamma}^{\star}\right\rVert_{F}\sqrt{\frac{\bar{d}}{d^{\star}}}.

(26)

Combined with
\mathbb{E}^{\star}\left\langle H_{\Gamma}^{\star},\,X\right\rangle^{2}\asymp\left\lVert H_{\Gamma}^{\star}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}/d^{\star}
,

\mathbb{E}^{\star}|\left\langle H_{\Gamma}^{\star},\,X\right\rangle|^{3}\;\lesssim\;\left\lVert H_{\Gamma}^{\star}\right\rVert_{F}\sqrt{\frac{\bar{d}}{d^{\star}}}\cdot\frac{\left\lVert H_{\Gamma}^{\star}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}}{d^{\star}}=\frac{\left\lVert H_{\Gamma}^{\star}\right\rVert_{F}^{3}\sqrt{\bar{d}}}{(d^{\star})^{3/2}}.

Substituting into the expression for
\mathbb{E}^{\star}|Z_{i}|^{3}
, and using

\sigma_{\Gamma}^{3}\geq c_{B}^{3/2}\left\lVert H_{\Gamma}^{\star}\right\rVert_{F}^{3}/(d^{\star})^{3/2}

from (
25
),

\mathbb{E}^{\star}|Z_{i}|^{3}\;\leq\;\frac{C_{3}}{\sigma_{\Gamma}^{3}}\cdot\frac{\left\lVert H_{\Gamma}^{\star}\right\rVert_{F}^{3}\sqrt{\bar{d}}}{(d^{\star})^{3/2}}\;\leq\;C_{3}c_{B}^{-3/2}\,\sqrt{\bar{d}}.

Step 4: Berry–Esseen.

By the classical univariate Berry–Esseen theorem (e.g. Shevtsova
2010 with constant
C_{\rm BE}=0.4748
),

\rho_{n}\leq\frac{C_{\rm BE}}{\sqrt{n}}\mathbb{E}^{\star}|Z_{i}|^{3}\leq C_{3}c_{B}^{-3/2}\,C_{\rm BE}\,\sqrt{\frac{\bar{d}}{n}}.

Lemma D.8

(Pairwise tangent-projection envelope)

Under
\mu
-incoherence and the row-centering gauge, for every
admissible design tensor
X=e_{t}(e_{m}-e_{m^{\prime}})^{\top}
,

\left\lVert P_{\mathbb{T}}X\right\rVert_{F}\leq C(\mu,r)\sqrt{\bar{d}/d^{\star}}
. The same
bound holds for
\widehat{P}_{\mathbb{T}}X
under the estimated
incoherence guarantee.

\Trivlist

Decompose
X=E_{(t,m)}-E_{(t,m^{\prime})}
where
E_{\omega}=e_{t}e_{m}^{\top}
is
the canonical basis tensor. By the basis-tensor projection bound (a
direct consequence of the closed-form
projector (
20
) together with

\mu
-incoherence),

\left\lVert P_{\mathbb{T}}E_{\omega}\right\rVert_{F}\leq C(\mu,r)\sqrt{\bar{d}/d^{\star}}
for
every basis tensor. By linearity and the triangle inequality,

\left\lVert P_{\mathbb{T}}X\right\rVert_{F}\leq\left\lVert P_{\mathbb{T}}E_{(t,m)}\right\rVert_{F}+\left\lVert P_{\mathbb{T}}E_{(t,m^{\prime})}\right\rVert_{F}\leq 2C(\mu,r)\sqrt{\bar{d}/d^{\star}}
.

D.9
Multivariate rectangle CLT for fixed
q

We combine the Berry–Esseen rate of
Theorem
D.7
with the uniform remainder bound of
Theorem
D.6
to obtain the rectangle CLT.

Let
\Phi_{q}(W_{i}):=(\phi_{1}(W_{i}),\ldots,\phi_{q}(W_{i}))^{\top}
,

S_{n}:=n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Phi_{q}(W_{i})
,

T_{n}:=\sqrt{n}(\widehat{\psi}-\psi)
, and

r_{n}:=(R_{n}^{\Gamma_{1}},\ldots,R_{n}^{\Gamma_{q}})
.

Theorem D.9

(Oracle multivariate Berry–Esseen)

For any rectangle
B\in\mathcal{R}_{q}
,

\rho_{n}^{\rm orac}:=\sup_{B\in\mathcal{R}_{q}}|\Pr(S_{n}\in B)-\Pr(Z_{\Gamma}\in B)|\;\lesssim\;\sqrt{\frac{\bar{d}}{n}},

where
Z_{\Gamma}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma)
.

\Trivlist

Handle possibly singular
\Sigma
by restriction to its range. Let

s:=\mathrm{rank}(\Sigma)\leq q
and
U\in\mathbb{R}^{q\times s}
have
orthonormal columns spanning
\mathrm{range}(\Sigma)
. For any

v\in\mathrm{Null}(\Sigma)
,

\mathrm{Var}(v^{\top}\Phi_{q})=v^{\top}\Sigma v=0
, so
v^{\top}\Phi_{q}=0
almost
surely; hence
\Phi_{q}=U\widetilde{\Phi}
for

\widetilde{\Phi}:=U^{\top}\Phi_{q}\in\mathbb{R}^{s}
with covariance

\widetilde{\Sigma}:=U^{\top}\Sigma U
nonsingular. Set

Y:=\widetilde{\Sigma}^{-1/2}\widetilde{\Phi}\in\mathbb{R}^{s}
, so

\mathbb{E}[Y]=0
and
\mathbb{E}[YY^{\top}]=I_{s}
. For any rectangle

B\subset\mathbb{R}^{q}
,
\{S_{n}\in B\}
reduces to

\{n^{-1/2}\sum Y_{i}\in C_{B}\}
for the convex set

C_{B}:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{s}:U\widetilde{\Sigma}^{1/2}y\in B\}
.

By the convex-set multivariate Berry–Esseen theorem

[
4
]
for fixed dimension
s\leq q=O(1)
,

\sup_{C\in\mathcal{C}_{s}}\Bigl|\Pr\Bigl(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum Y_{i}\in C\Bigr)-\Pr(G_{s}\in C)\Bigr|\leq C_{s}\,\frac{\mathbb{E}\left\lVert Y\right\rVert_{2}^{3}}{\sqrt{n}}.

For any unit
a\in\mathbb{S}^{s-1}
,
a^{\top}Y
is a scalar
standardized sum of EIF coordinates over the contrast

\Gamma_{a}=\sum_{j}(\widetilde{\Sigma}^{-1/2}U^{\top}a)_{j}\Gamma_{j}
(a finite
linear combination of the original contrasts). Sub-exponential tails on

\phi_{\Gamma}
and the third-moment computation of
Theorem
D.7
(applied to
\Gamma_{a}
) give

\mathbb{E}|a^{\top}Y|^{3}\leq C\sqrt{\bar{d}}
, uniformly in
a
. Hence

\mathbb{E}\left\lVert Y\right\rVert_{2}^{3}\leq Cs^{3/2}\sqrt{\bar{d}}\leq C\sqrt{\bar{d}}
(since

s=O(1)
), giving the claim.

Theorem D.10

(Rectangle CLT for the feasible statistic; restatement of Theorem
4.1
)

Under
Assumptions
A.1
–
A.11
,

\sup_{B\in\mathcal{R}_{q}}|\Pr(T_{n}\in B)-\Pr(Z_{\Gamma}\in B)|\;\lesssim\;C_{A}\sqrt{\frac{\bar{d}\,\mathrm{polylog}(n\bar{d})}{n}}.

Consequently, if
C_{A}\sqrt{\bar{d}\,\mathrm{polylog}(n\bar{d})/n}\to 0
,
then
\sqrt{n}(\widehat{\psi}-\psi)\rightsquigarrow\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma)
.

\Trivlist

Let
D:=\mathrm{diag}(\sqrt{\Sigma_{11}},\ldots,\sqrt{\Sigma_{qq}})

(removing zero diagonals from the reporting family, which is permitted
by Remark
D.3
). Define standardized vectors

\bar{T}_{n}:=D^{-1}T_{n}
,
\bar{S}_{n}:=D^{-1}S_{n}
,

\bar{Z}:=D^{-1}Z_{\Gamma}
. The standardized remainder event is

\mathcal{E}_{r}:=\bigl\{\left\lVert D^{-1}r_{n}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\delta_{n}\bigr\},\qquad\delta_{n}:=C\,C_{A}\sqrt{\bar{d}\,\mathrm{polylog}(n\bar{d})/n}.

By Theorem
D.5
applied to each

\Gamma_{j}
,
\Pr(\mathcal{E}_{r}^{c})\leq q\,n^{-a}\leq n^{-a/2}
, giving

\pi_{n}:=\Pr(\mathcal{E}_{r}^{c})\leq n^{-a}
after relabelling.

For any rectangle
B=\prod_{j=1}^{q}[\alpha_{j},\beta_{j}]
, define enlarged
and shrunk rectangles

B^{\pm\delta_{n}}:=\prod[\alpha_{j}\mp\delta_{n},\beta_{j}\pm\delta_{n}]
(with
the convention that an empty interval makes

B^{-\delta_{n}}=\emptyset
).
On
\mathcal{E}_{r}
,
\bar{T}_{n}\in B
implies

\bar{S}_{n}\in B^{+\delta_{n}}
, and
\bar{S}_{n}\in B^{-\delta_{n}}

\bar{T}_{n}\in B

\Pr(\bar{S}_{n}\in B^{-\delta_{n}})-\pi_{n}\leq\Pr(\bar{T}_{n}\in B)\leq\Pr(\bar{S}_{n}\in B^{+\delta_{n}})+\pi_{n}.

Combining with the oracle Berry–Esseen
(Theorem
D.9

\bar{S}_{n}

|\Pr(\bar{T}_{n}\in B)-\Pr(\bar{Z}\in B)|\leq\rho_{n}^{\rm orac}+\pi_{n}+\Pr(\bar{Z}\in B^{+\delta_{n}}\setminus B^{-\delta_{n}}).

The Gaussian boundary band is bounded by Gaussian anti-concentration:
each face contributes at most
\delta_{n}\sqrt{2/\pi}
(one-dimensional
standard normal density at most
\sqrt{1/(2\pi)}
), and there are

2q
faces, so the band is at most
Cq\delta_{n}
. Since

D.10
Plug-in variance: relative consistency

We port the variance-consistency proof from

[
21
, Appendix G.13]
(cf. also AoS Lemma G.13) in
full detail. The plug-in variance estimator for a single contrast

\Gamma
is
\widehat{V}_{\Gamma}:=\mathbb{P}_{n}[\widehat{\phi}_{\Gamma}^{2}]
where

\widehat{\phi}_{\Gamma}=s_{\eta}(Y,\widehat{\eta})\left\langle\widehat{H}_{\Gamma},\,X\right\rangle
.

Proposition D.11

(Relative variance consistency)

Fix any
a>0
and any contrast
\Gamma
satisfying
Assumptions
A.8
–
A.9
. Under
the conditions of
Theorem
D.5
, with probability at
least
1-n^{-a}
,

\Bigl|\frac{\widehat{V}_{\Gamma}}{V_{\Gamma}}-1\Bigr|\;\leq\;C\sqrt{\frac{\bar{d}\,\log^{c}(n\bar{d})}{n}}.

(27)

This bound contains
no

C_{A}
factor.

\Trivlist

We follow the AoS three-part decomposition: direction-induced part,
score plug-in part, and empirical fluctuation part. The key point is
that the spectral scale
d^{\star}
of
A^{-1}
cancels in every
relative ratio, eliminating the
C_{A}
factor.

Step 1: relative direction error
\delta_{H}
.

We first show

\delta_{H}:=\left\lVert\widehat{H}_{\Gamma}-H_{\Gamma}^{\star}\right\rVert_{F}/\left\lVert H_{\Gamma}^{\star}\right\rVert_{F}\leq C\rho/\alpha_{\Gamma}

where
\rho:=\sqrt{\bar{d}\log\bar{d}/n_{\rm aux}}
is the
subspace-perturbation parameter. Use the resolvent identity

\widehat{A}^{-1}-A^{-1}=A^{-1}(A-\widehat{A})\widehat{A}^{-1}
on the
tangent space:

\widehat{H}_{\Gamma}-H_{\Gamma}^{\star}=\underbrace{A^{-1}(\widehat{P}_{\mathbb{T}}-P_{\mathbb{T}})\Gamma}_{T_{1}}+\underbrace{A^{-1}(A-\widehat{A})\widehat{A}^{-1}\widehat{P}_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma}_{T_{2}}.

For
T_{1}
, use
\left\lVert A^{-1}\right\rVert_{\rm op}\asymp d^{\star}
on the tangent
space (eigenvalues of
A
lie in

[c_{B}/d^{\star},C_{B}/d^{\star}]
by
Lemma
A.5
). This common factor
d^{\star}

cancels in the relative Frobenius ratio:

\frac{\left\lVert T_{1}\right\rVert_{F}}{\left\lVert H_{\Gamma}^{\star}\right\rVert_{F}}\leq\frac{\left\lVert A^{-1}\right\rVert_{\rm op}\left\lVert(\widehat{P}_{\mathbb{T}}-P_{\mathbb{T}})\Gamma\right\rVert_{F}}{\left\lVert A^{-1}\right\rVert_{\rm op}\left\lVert P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma\right\rVert_{F}-O(\rho)}\leq\frac{C\left\lVert(\widehat{P}_{\mathbb{T}}-P_{\mathbb{T}})\Gamma\right\rVert_{F}}{\left\lVert P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma\right\rVert_{F}}.

Under the sparse-target projector perturbation bound

\left\lVert(\widehat{P}_{\mathbb{T}}-P_{\mathbb{T}})\Gamma\right\rVert_{F}\lesssim\rho\sqrt{\bar{d}/d^{\star}}\left\lVert\Gamma\right\rVert_{F}

(which follows from the closed-form
projector (
20
) and subspace
perturbation
\left\lVert P_{\widehat{U}}-P_{U^{\star}}\right\rVert_{\rm op}\lesssim\rho
),
together with Assumption
A.9

\left\lVert P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma\right\rVert_{F}\geq\alpha_{\Gamma}\sqrt{\bar{d}/d^{\star}}\left\lVert\Gamma\right\rVert_{F}
,
the ratio simplifies to

\left\lVert T_{1}\right\rVert_{F}/\left\lVert H_{\Gamma}^{\star}\right\rVert_{F}\leq C\rho/\alpha_{\Gamma}
.

For
T_{2}
,
\left\lVert A-\widehat{A}\right\rVert_{\rm op}\leq C\rho/d^{\star}
by
Fisher operator perturbation (using

\left\lVert\widehat{\Theta}-\Theta^{\star}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq C\rho
and Lipschitzness of

I(\eta)
), so

\left\lVert A^{-1}(A-\widehat{A})\right\rVert_{\rm op}\leq\left\lVert A^{-1}\right\rVert_{\rm op}\left\lVert A-\widehat{A}\right\rVert_{\rm op}\leq C\rho
,
and
\left\lVert T_{2}\right\rVert_{F}\leq C\rho\left\lVert\widehat{A}^{-1}\widehat{P}_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma\right\rVert_{F}\leq C\rho\left\lVert H_{\Gamma}^{\star}\right\rVert_{F}

(using
\left\lVert\widehat{H}_{\Gamma}\right\rVert_{F}\asymp\left\lVert H_{\Gamma}^{\star}\right\rVert_{F}
on the
event of Step 1, by an iterative argument). Combining,

\delta_{H}\leq C\rho/\alpha_{\Gamma}
.

Step 2: variance difference reduces to direction error.

We show that for any oracle / plug-in pair

\phi^{\star},\widehat{\phi}
and any ”score-squared” factor

s_{\star}
with
\mathbb{E}^{\star}[s_{\star}^{2}f(X)]\asymp\mathbb{E}^{\star}[f(X)]
for
nonnegative
f
,

\frac{|\mathbb{P}^{\star}[s_{\star}^{2}(\left\langle\widehat{H}_{\Gamma},\,X\right\rangle^{2}-\left\langle H_{\Gamma}^{\star},\,X\right\rangle^{2})]|}{\mathbb{P}^{\star}[s_{\star}^{2}\left\langle H_{\Gamma}^{\star},\,X\right\rangle^{2}]}\;\lesssim\;\delta_{H}.

Write
a:=\left\langle\widehat{H}_{\Gamma},\,X\right\rangle
,
b:=\left\langle H_{\Gamma}^{\star},\,X\right\rangle
,
so
a^{2}-b^{2}=(a-b)(a+b)
. Cauchy–Schwarz gives

\mathbb{P}^{\star}|a^{2}-b^{2}|\leq(\mathbb{P}^{\star}(a-b)^{2})^{1/2}(\mathbb{P}^{\star}(a+b)^{2})^{1/2}
, and
the Frobenius reduction (Lemma
A.4
) gives

\mathbb{P}^{\star}(a-b)^{2}\asymp\left\lVert\widehat{H}_{\Gamma}-H_{\Gamma}^{\star}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}/d^{\star}
,

\mathbb{P}^{\star}b^{2}\asymp\left\lVert H_{\Gamma}^{\star}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}/d^{\star}
. When

\delta_{H}\leq c_{0}<1
,

\mathbb{P}^{\star}(a+b)^{2}\leq 2\mathbb{P}^{\star}(a-b)^{2}+8\mathbb{P}^{\star}b^{2}\leq 10\mathbb{P}^{\star}b^{2}
, so

\mathbb{P}^{\star}|a^{2}-b^{2}|\lesssim\delta_{H}\mathbb{P}^{\star}b^{2}
. Dividing by

\mathbb{P}^{\star}[s_{\star}^{2}b^{2}]\asymp\mathbb{P}^{\star}b^{2}
yields the claim.

Step 3: score plug-in part.

By the Lipschitz bound on the BTL score derivative
(Assumption
A.1
(iii)),

|s_{\eta}(Y,\widehat{\eta})^{2}-s_{\eta}(Y,\eta^{\star})^{2}|\leq 2|s_{\eta}||\partial_{\eta}s_{\eta}||\widehat{\eta}-\eta^{\star}|\leq C\left\lVert\widehat{\Theta}-\Theta^{\star}\right\rVert_{\infty}
,
so

\mathbb{P}_{n}[(s_{\eta}(Y,\widehat{\eta})^{2}-s_{\eta}(Y,\eta^{\star})^{2})\left\langle\widehat{H}_{\Gamma},\,X\right\rangle^{2}]\leq C\left\lVert\Delta\right\rVert_{\infty}\,\mathbb{P}_{n}[\left\langle\widehat{H}_{\Gamma},\,X\right\rangle^{2}]\leq C\left\lVert\Delta\right\rVert_{\infty}\,V_{\Gamma}
,
giving a relative contribution
O(\left\lVert\Delta\right\rVert_{\infty})=O(r_{n})
.

Step 4: empirical fluctuation.

Conditional on the auxiliary sample,
\widehat{V}_{\Gamma}-\mathbb{P}^{\star}[\widehat{\phi}_{\Gamma}^{2}]=(\mathbb{P}_{n}-\mathbb{P}^{\star})(\widehat{\phi}_{\Gamma}^{2})

is a centered sum. Bernstein with envelope

\widehat{\phi}_{\Gamma}^{2}\leq V_{\Gamma}\bar{d}
(from Step 5 of the
Bernstein argument in
Appendix
E.6
below) and variance proxy

\mathbb{P}^{\star}\widehat{\phi}_{\Gamma}^{4}\leq V_{\Gamma}^{2}\bar{d}
gives

|(\mathbb{P}_{n}-\mathbb{P}^{\star})\widehat{\phi}_{\Gamma}^{2}|\leq V_{\Gamma}\sqrt{\bar{d}\log(n\bar{d})/n}=V_{\Gamma}r_{n}
.

Combining.

The three parts contribute

O(\rho/\alpha_{\Gamma})+O(\left\lVert\Delta\right\rVert_{\infty})+O(r_{n})=O(r_{n})
relative
error: the spectral scale
d^{\star}
of

A^{-1}
cancels in the relative direction error, the score plug-in
is a direct entrywise estimator bound, and the empirical fluctuation is
controlled by the Bernstein argument.

D.11
Covariance consistency in correlation form

Using the polarization identity, the variance consistency of
Proposition
D.11
extends to a covariance
consistency statement. The cleanest form is in correlation

\rho_{jk}:=\Sigma_{jk}/\sqrt{\Sigma_{jj}\Sigma_{kk}}
, as
relative-error consistency for off-diagonal
\Sigma_{jk}
is generally
unattainable.

Proposition D.12

(Covariance consistency)

Fix any
a>0
and let

\widehat{\Sigma}_{jk}:=\mathbb{P}_{n}[\widehat{\phi}_{j}\widehat{\phi}_{k}]
be the plug-in
covariance for any pair
(j,k)
of contrasts in a polynomial-size
family. Under the conditions of
Proposition
D.11
,

\max_{j,k}\,|\widehat{\Sigma}_{jk}-\Sigma_{jk}|\;\leq\;C\,(\Sigma_{jj}+\Sigma_{kk})\,r_{n},\qquad r_{n}:=\sqrt{\bar{d}\,\log^{c}(n\bar{d})/n},

(28)

with probability at least
1-n^{-a}
. In particular, if the diagonal
variances are comparable, i.e.
c_{\Sigma}\leq\Sigma_{jj}/\Sigma_{kk}\leq C_{\Sigma}
,

\max_{j,k}\,|\widehat{\rho}_{jk}-\rho_{jk}|\;\leq\;C\,r_{n}.

(29)

\Trivlist

We use polarization plus the variance consistency lemma applied to

\Gamma_{j}
,
\Gamma_{k}
, and
\Gamma_{j}+\Gamma_{k}
.

Step 1: linearity.

The direction maps are linear in
\Gamma
:

H_{\Gamma_{j}+\Gamma_{k}}^{\star}=A^{-1}P_{\mathbb{T}}(\Gamma_{j}+\Gamma_{k})=H_{j}^{\star}+H_{k}^{\star}
,
and analogously for the plug-in

\widehat{H}_{\Gamma_{j}+\Gamma_{k}}=\widehat{H}_{j}+\widehat{H}_{k}
. Hence

\phi_{\Gamma_{j}+\Gamma_{k}}=\phi_{j}+\phi_{k}
and

\widehat{\phi}_{\Gamma_{j}+\Gamma_{k}}=\widehat{\phi}_{j}+\widehat{\phi}_{k}
.

Step 2: polarization.

For the oracle covariance,

V_{\Gamma_{j}+\Gamma_{k}}=\mathbb{P}^{\star}[(\phi_{j}+\phi_{k})^{2}]=\Sigma_{jj}+\Sigma_{kk}+2\Sigma_{jk}
,
so

\Sigma_{jk}=\tfrac{1}{2}(V_{\Gamma_{j}+\Gamma_{k}}-V_{\Gamma_{j}}-V_{\Gamma_{k}}).

Identically for the plug-in,

\widehat{\Sigma}_{jk}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\widehat{V}_{\Gamma_{j}+\Gamma_{k}}-\widehat{V}_{\Gamma_{j}}-\widehat{V}_{\Gamma_{k}})
.
Subtracting,

\widehat{\Sigma}_{jk}-\Sigma_{jk}=\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[(\widehat{V}_{\Gamma_{j}+\Gamma_{k}}-V_{\Gamma_{j}+\Gamma_{k}})-(\widehat{V}_{\Gamma_{j}}-V_{\Gamma_{j}})-(\widehat{V}_{\Gamma_{k}}-V_{\Gamma_{k}})\bigr].

Step 3: applying variance consistency.

By Proposition
D.11
applied to each of the
three contrasts (
\Gamma_{j},\Gamma_{k},\Gamma_{j}+\Gamma_{k}
), and using

V_{\Gamma}\leq 1\cdot\Sigma_{jj}
etc. from a union bound at level

1-n^{-a}
for each contrast,

|\widehat{\Sigma}_{jk}-\Sigma_{jk}|\;\leq\;Cr_{n}(V_{\Gamma_{j}+\Gamma_{k}}+V_{\Gamma_{j}}+V_{\Gamma_{k}}).

Since
\Sigma\succeq 0
,

|\Sigma_{jk}|\leq\sqrt{\Sigma_{jj}\Sigma_{kk}}\leq(\Sigma_{jj}+\Sigma_{kk})/2
, so

V_{\Gamma_{j}+\Gamma_{k}}\leq\Sigma_{jj}+\Sigma_{kk}+2|\Sigma_{jk}|\leq 2(\Sigma_{jj}+\Sigma_{kk})
.
Thus
V_{\Gamma_{j}+\Gamma_{k}}+V_{\Gamma_{j}}+V_{\Gamma_{k}}\leq 3(\Sigma_{jj}+\Sigma_{kk})
,
giving (
28
).

Step 4: correlation consistency.

Under
\Sigma_{jj}\asymp\Sigma_{kk}
, divide
(
28
) by
\sqrt{\Sigma_{jj}\Sigma_{kk}}
to get

|\widehat{\Sigma}_{jk}/\sqrt{\Sigma_{jj}\Sigma_{kk}}-\rho_{jk}|\leq Cr_{n}
.
For the diagonal-rescaled empirical correlation

\widehat{\rho}_{jk}=\widehat{\Sigma}_{jk}/\sqrt{\widehat{\Sigma}_{jj}\widehat{\Sigma}_{kk}}
,
apply Taylor expansion of
(x,y,z)\mapsto x/\sqrt{yz}
around

(\Sigma_{jk},\Sigma_{jj},\Sigma_{kk})
and use the diagonal variance
consistency to obtain (
29
). See
covariance_consistency_argument.md for details.

Remark D.13

(Why no relative-error bound on
\Sigma_{jk}
)

A relative-error bound of the form

|\widehat{\Sigma}_{jk}-\Sigma_{jk}|\lesssim|\Sigma_{jk}|r_{n}
is in
general unattainable without an additional lower bound on

|\Sigma_{jk}|
, because off-diagonal covariances may be zero or
arbitrarily small even when
\Sigma_{jj},\Sigma_{kk}
are large. Two
orthogonal EIFs (
\Sigma_{jk}=0
) would make the relative ratio
unbounded, while the empirical
\widehat{\Sigma}_{jk}
still fluctuates
around zero at a non-trivial scale. The natural uniform statement is
the absolute error normalized by the diagonal scale, as
(
28
). Under the diagonal-comparability
condition, this is equivalent to absolute correlation consistency
(
29
), which is what the high-dimensional CCK
calibration in Appendix
E
actually requires.

\Sigma

Lemma D.14

(Diagonal scale of efficient variance for score gaps)

Under Assumptions

\Trivlist

Under near-uniform pairwise sampling and Fisher comparability,

E.1
Setup: contrast family and statistics

For a contrast family
\mathcal{J}
(indexed by score-gap contrasts as
in the three applications below), let

\Delta_{j}:=\psi_{\Gamma_{j}}(\Theta^{\star})
, and adopt the standardized

\sigma_{j}^{2}=\Sigma_{jj}=V_{\rm eff}(\Gamma_{j})

Oracle and feasible test statistics.

(30)

Oracle and feasible multiplier-bootstrap statistics.

With i.i.d. multipliers
\xi_{i}\sim N(0,1)
independent of the data,

W_{0}:=\max_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\Bigl|\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i}Z_{ij}\Bigr|,\qquad T^{\ast}:=\max_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\Bigl|\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i}\widehat{Z}_{ij}\Bigr|.

(31)

Let

denote the conditional
(1-\alpha)

of

given the data.

Reference Gaussian.

Let
\Sigma_{Z}:=(\Sigma_{Z,jk})_{j,k\in\mathcal{J}}
with

\Sigma_{Z,jk}:=\mathbb{E}^{\star}[Z_{j}Z_{k}]=\Sigma_{jk}/(\sigma_{j}\sigma_{k})

E.2
The CCK approximate-means theorem and the master decomposition

Coverage of the simultaneous score-gap bands

[
10
,
11
,
12
]

\sup_{\alpha\in(0,1)}\bigl|\Pr(T\leq c_{1-\alpha}^{\ast})-(1-\alpha)\bigr|\;\leq\;\mathfrak{E}_{n},

(32)

where the aggregate error

decomposes into five

\displaystyle\mathfrak{E}_{n}\;\leq\;\underbrace{\rho_{n}}_{\text{(I) oracle CCK Gaussian approximation}}+\underbrace{\pi(\vartheta_{n})+\Pr(\Delta_{n}>\vartheta_{n})}_{\text{(II) Gaussian-multiplier covariance error}}+

(33)

\displaystyle\underbrace{a_{n}\sqrt{\log p}}_{\text{(III) one-step plug-in transfer}}+\underbrace{b_{n}\log p}_{\text{(IV) standard-error plug-in transfer}}+\underbrace{c_{n}\log p}_{\text{(V) feasible-bootstrap transfer}},

where each ingredient is defined as follows.

(I)

Oracle CCK Gaussian approximation error

E.3

L_{n}^{2}\log^{7}(pn)/n\to 0
for an envelope
L_{n}
on the standardized

.

(II)

The conditional law of

given the data is a Gaussian maximum

\widehat{\Sigma}_{Z}
; the Gaussian comparison
step (

on

, where

\Delta_{n}:=\max_{j,k\in\mathcal{J}}|\mathbb{P}_{n}[Z_{j}Z_{k}]-\mathbb{P}^{\star}[Z_{j}Z_{k}]|
.
This is bounded in Appendix
E.4

(III)

One-step plug-in transfer error

a_{n}:=\max_{j\in\mathcal{J}}|\sqrt{n}(\widehat{\Delta}_{j}-\Delta_{j})/\sigma_{j}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i}Z_{ij}|
,
the standardized one-step remainder. Bounded in
Appendix

D.6

(IV)

Standard-error plug-in transfer error

(V)

Feasible-bootstrap transfer error

square-loss between the standardized plug-in and oracle EIF

Appendix
E.6
via the empirical
square-loss argument (Proposition
E.5
).

The CCK approximate-means theorem
[
10

,
which combines the oracle Gaussian approximation, the Gaussian

yields (

E.3
Bounding the standardized envelope
L_{n}
(and ingredient I)

We compute an explicit envelope for the standardized oracle coordinates

Z_{ij}
, in which the constant
C_{A}
appears
only as a
first-order factor
, never inside a polynomial.

Lemma E.1

(Envelope of standardized oracle coordinates)

Under Assumptions
A.1
–
A.9
,
for every
j\in\mathcal{J}
and every admissible design
X_{i}
,

|Z_{ij}|\;\leq\;L_{n},\qquad L_{n}^{2}\;\leq\;C(\mu,r,\kappa,B,c_{B},C_{B})\,C_{A}\,\bar{d},

(34)

where the constant in front of
\bar{d}
is a polynomial in

(\mu,r,\kappa,B,c_{B},C_{B})
but contains
C_{A}
only as a first-order
factor. Consequently
L_{n}^{2}\log^{7}(pn)/n\leq C\,C_{A}\bar{d}\log^{7}(pn)/n\to 0

under Assumption
A.11
and the CLT condition

C_{A}\sqrt{\bar{d}\log^{c}(n\bar{d})/n}\to 0
of
Theorem
4.1
, and so the oracle CCK Gaussian
approximation error

\rho_{n}:=\sup_{z}|\Pr(T_{0}\leq z)-\Pr(Z_{0}\leq z)|\;\to\;0.

\Trivlist

The proof is a direct calculation. Recall
Z_{ij}=\phi_{j}(W_{i})/\sigma_{j}
.

Step 1: bound
|\phi_{j}(W_{i})|
using the tangent envelope.

Since
s_{\eta}(Y_{i},\eta_{i}^{\star})\in[-1,1]
almost surely
(Assumption
A.1
(iv)),

|\phi_{j}(W_{i})|=|s_{\eta}(Y_{i},\eta_{i}^{\star})|\cdot|\left\langle H_{j}^{\star},\,X_{i}\right\rangle|\leq|\left\langle H_{j}^{\star},\,X_{i}\right\rangle|.

Using
H_{j}^{\star}\in\mathbb{T}
,
\left\langle H_{j}^{\star},\,X_{i}\right\rangle=\left\langle H_{j}^{\star},\,P_{\mathbb{T}}X_{i}\right\rangle
,
and Cauchy–Schwarz with the tangent envelope
(Lemma
D.8
),

|\left\langle H_{j}^{\star},\,X_{i}\right\rangle|\leq\left\lVert H_{j}^{\star}\right\rVert_{F}\cdot\left\lVert P_{\mathbb{T}}X_{i}\right\rVert_{F}\;\leq\;C(\mu,r)\,\left\lVert H_{j}^{\star}\right\rVert_{F}\sqrt{\bar{d}/d^{\star}}.

Step 2: bound
\sigma_{j}^{2}
from below using the Frobenius reduction.

By Lemma
A.5
,

\sigma_{j}^{2}=\mathbb{E}^{\star}[s_{\eta}^{2}\left\langle H_{j}^{\star},\,X\right\rangle^{2}]\geq c_{B}\mathbb{E}^{\star}\left\langle H_{j}^{\star},\,X\right\rangle^{2}
,
and by the Frobenius reduction (Lemma
A.4
)

\mathbb{E}^{\star}\left\langle H_{j}^{\star},\,X\right\rangle^{2}\asymp\left\lVert H_{j}^{\star}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}/d^{\star}
, so

\sigma_{j}^{2}\;\geq\;\frac{c_{B}}{C}\,\frac{\left\lVert H_{j}^{\star}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}}{d^{\star}}.

Step 3: combine.

Z_{ij}^{2}=\frac{\phi_{j}(W_{i})^{2}}{\sigma_{j}^{2}}\;\leq\;\frac{C(\mu,r)\,\left\lVert H_{j}^{\star}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}(\bar{d}/d^{\star})}{(c_{B}/C)\,\left\lVert H_{j}^{\star}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}/d^{\star}}\;=\;C(\mu,r,c_{B})\,\bar{d}.

The factor
\left\lVert H_{j}^{\star}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}
appears in numerator and denominator
and cancels, and this cancellation is the same mechanism that drives the
empirical square-loss bound in
Appendix
E.6
.

Step 4: where does
C_{A}
enter?

The factor
C_{A}
enters through the
plug-in
envelope for

\widehat{Z}_{ij}
, which is needed in the multiplier-bootstrap
covariance computation in Appendix
E.4
and the
bootstrap-statistic transfer in Appendix
E.6
.
There, the bound is

|\widehat{Z}_{ij}|\leq C_{A}^{1/2}\sqrt{\bar{d}}
at most, so the
empirical square-loss difference

|\widehat{Z}_{ij}-Z_{ij}|^{2}\leq C\,C_{A}\bar{d}
remains
C_{A}^{1}
at
most. No power higher than
C_{A}^{1}
appears anywhere in the
analysis.

Step 5: oracle CCK approximation.

By the CCK Gaussian approximation theorem

[
12
, Theorem 2.1]
, applied to the i.i.d. mean-zero coordinates
Z_{ij}
with

\mathbb{E}^{\star}Z_{ij}^{2}=1
and envelope
L_{n}
, if

L_{n}^{2}\log^{7}(pn)/n\to 0
then

\rho_{n}=o(1)
. Substituting
L_{n}^{2}\leq C\,C_{A}\bar{d}
(counting the

C_{A}
from the plug-in envelope above to be safe; the oracle
envelope is
C_{A}
-free but the CCK constant absorbs both into one
term) and
p\leq\bar{d}^{O(1)}
yields the displayed sufficient
condition.

E.4
Bounding the empirical covariance error
\Delta_{n}
(ingredient II)

We now bound the maximum standardized covariance estimation error

\Delta_{n}
by a Bernstein argument, with explicit calculation of the
variance proxy and envelope.

Lemma E.2

(Maximum standardized covariance error)

Fix any
a>0
. With probability at least
1-n^{-a}
,

\Delta_{n}:=\max_{j,k\in\mathcal{J}}\bigl|\mathbb{P}_{n}[Z_{j}Z_{k}]-\mathbb{P}^{\star}[Z_{j}Z_{k}]\bigr|\;\leq\;C\Bigl(L_{n}\sqrt{\frac{\log(pn)}{n}}+L_{n}^{2}\frac{\log(pn)}{n}\Bigr)\;\lesssim\;\sqrt{\frac{C_{A}\,\bar{d}\,\log(pn)}{n}}+\frac{C_{A}\,\bar{d}\log(pn)}{n}.

(35)

Consequently, with the same probability, the Gaussian comparison step
contributes

\pi(\vartheta_{n}):=C\vartheta_{n}^{1/3}\{1\vee\log(p/\vartheta_{n})\}^{2/3}=o(1)

upon the choice

\vartheta_{n}=L_{n}\sqrt{\log(pn)/n}+L_{n}^{2}\log(pn)/n
.

\Trivlist

We apply Bernstein’s inequality to the centered products

Z_{ij}Z_{ik}-\mathbb{P}^{\star}[Z_{j}Z_{k}]
for each pair
(j,k)
, then take a
union bound over
p^{2}
pairs.

Step 1: variance proxy.

Each
Z_{ij}Z_{ik}
is bounded by
L_{n}^{2}
in absolute value
(Lemma
E.1
), and has variance

\mathrm{Var}(Z_{ij}Z_{ik})\leq\mathbb{P}^{\star}[Z_{j}^{2}Z_{k}^{2}]\leq L_{n}^{2}\,\mathbb{P}^{\star}[Z_{j}^{2}]=L_{n}^{2},

using
\mathbb{P}^{\star}Z_{j}^{2}=1
by definition.

Step 2: per-pair Bernstein.

For each pair
(j,k)
, Bernstein’s inequality gives, for any
x>0
,

\Pr\Bigl[\bigl|\mathbb{P}_{n}[Z_{j}Z_{k}]-\mathbb{P}^{\star}[Z_{j}Z_{k}]\bigr|\geq\sqrt{2L_{n}^{2}\,x/n}+L_{n}^{2}x/(3n)\Bigr]\leq 2e^{-x}.

This is the standard Bernstein bound with envelope
L_{n}^{2}
and
variance
L_{n}^{2}
.

Step 3: union bound.

Set
x=Ca\log(pn)
with
C
sufficiently large, so the tail is at
most
2\,p^{2}e^{-Ca\log(pn)}\leq 2p^{-Ca+2}n^{-Ca}\leq n^{-a-1}
for

C
large enough (using
p\leq\bar{d}^{O(1)}
). This gives

\Pr[\Delta_{n}\geq L_{n}\sqrt{2Ca\log(pn)/n}+L_{n}^{2}Ca\log(pn)/(3n)]\leq n^{-a}
.

Step 4:
\pi(\vartheta_{n})=o(1)
.

With
\vartheta_{n}=L_{n}\sqrt{\log(pn)/n}+L_{n}^{2}\log(pn)/n
, we have

\vartheta_{n}^{1/3}\leq(L_{n}^{2}\log(pn)/n)^{1/6}+(L_{n}^{2}\log(pn)/n)^{1/3}

(treating
L_{n}\sqrt{x}
as the dominant term for small

), so

\pi(\vartheta_{n})\lesssim(L_{n}^{2}\log(pn)/n)^{1/6}\log^{2/3}(p)
. Under

L_{n}^{2}\log^{7}(pn)/n\to 0
, this is
o(1)
, confirming
ingredient (II) is asymptotically negligible.

Bounding the one-step plug-in errors

We carry over the bounds from

Lemma E.3

(One-step transfer)

Under Theorem

, with probability at least

(36)

\Trivlist

By definition of

in

\sqrt{n}(\widehat{\Delta}_{j}-\Delta_{j})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i}\phi_{j}(W_{i})+\sqrt{n}R_{n}^{\Gamma_{j}}

.
Hence
a_{n}=\max_{j}|\sqrt{n}R_{n}^{\Gamma_{j}}/\sigma_{j}|
.
By Theorem
D.6
(and noting
\sigma_{j}
is
absorbed into the alignment constant via

),

on the

(Standard-error transfer)

With probability at least

,

b_{n}:=\max_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\Bigl|\frac{\widehat{\sigma}_{j}}{\sigma_{j}}-1\Bigr|\;\leq\;C\sqrt{\frac{\bar{d}\,\mathrm{polylog}(n\bar{d})}{n}}.

(37)

\Trivlist

By Proposition
D.11
applied to each

\Gamma_{j}
,
|\widehat{V}_{j}/V_{j}-1|\leq Cr_{n}
with probability

1-n^{-a}
per contrast, where

and

. Take a union bound over the

E.6
Bounding the feasible-bootstrap error
c_{n}
(ingredient V)

We now prove the load-bearing bound on the empirical square-loss
between the standardized plug-in and oracle EIF coordinates. This is the
matrix specialization of the bound in

[
21
, Section G.16]
.

Proposition E.5

(Estimated-EIF squared-loss bound)

Under the conditions of
Theorem
D.10
, with probability at least
1-n^{-a}
,

c_{n}^{2}:=\max_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\mathbb{P}_{n}(\widehat{Z}_{j}-Z_{j})^{2}\;\leq\;C\,r_{n}^{2},\qquad r_{n}^{2}:=\frac{\bar{d}\,\mathrm{polylog}(n\bar{d})}{n}.

(38)

The bound has
no

C_{A}
factor.

\Trivlist

The proof has four steps, following the strategy in

[
21
, Section G.16]
.

Step 1: standardize away
\widehat{\sigma}_{j}
.

On the event
b_{n}\leq 1/2
(Lemma
E.4
),

|\widehat{Z}_{ij}-Z_{ij}|=\Bigl|\frac{\widehat{\phi}_{j}(W_{i})}{\widehat{\sigma}_{j}}-\frac{\phi_{j}(W_{i})}{\sigma_{j}}\Bigr|\leq\frac{2|\widehat{\phi}_{j}(W_{i})-\phi_{j}(W_{i})|}{\sigma_{j}}+2b_{n}|Z_{ij}|.

Squaring and averaging,

\mathbb{P}_{n}(\widehat{Z}_{j}-Z_{j})^{2}\leq\frac{8\mathbb{P}_{n}(\widehat{\phi}_{j}-\phi_{j})^{2}}{\sigma_{j}^{2}}+8b_{n}^{2}\mathbb{P}_{n}Z_{j}^{2}.

By definition
\mathbb{P}_{n}Z_{j}^{2}=1+(\mathbb{P}_{n}-\mathbb{P}^{\star})Z_{j}^{2}
, and Bernstein with
envelope
L_{n}^{2}
and variance
L_{n}^{2}
gives

(\mathbb{P}_{n}-\mathbb{P}^{\star})Z_{j}^{2}\leq L_{n}\sqrt{\log(pn)/n}+L_{n}^{2}\log(pn)/n=o(1)
; hence

\max_{j}\mathbb{P}_{n}Z_{j}^{2}\leq 2
on this event. Combined with

b_{n}^{2}\leq Cr_{n}^{2}
(Lemma
E.4
),

c_{n}^{2}\leq\frac{8\,\max_{j}\mathbb{P}_{n}(\widehat{\phi}_{j}-\phi_{j})^{2}/\sigma_{j}^{2}}{1}+16\,r_{n}^{2}.

The second term is already at the
r_{n}^{2}
scale; the first term is
the main object to bound below.

Step 2: decompose the EIF error.

Write

\widehat{\phi}_{j}(W)-\phi_{j}(W)=\bigl(s_{\eta}(Y,\widehat{\eta})-s_{\eta}(Y,\eta^{\star})\bigr)\left\langle\widehat{H}_{j},\,X\right\rangle+s_{\eta}(Y,\eta^{\star})\left\langle\widehat{H}_{j}-H_{j}^{\star},\,X\right\rangle
,
so

\mathbb{P}_{n}(\widehat{\phi}_{j}-\phi_{j})^{2}/\sigma_{j}^{2}\leq 2(T_{1j}+T_{2j})
with

T_{1j}:=\frac{\mathbb{P}_{n}[(s_{\eta}(Y,\widehat{\eta})-s_{\eta}(Y,\eta^{\star}))^{2}\left\langle\widehat{H}_{j},\,X\right\rangle^{2}]}{\sigma_{j}^{2}},\qquad T_{2j}:=\frac{\mathbb{P}_{n}[s_{\eta}(Y,\eta^{\star})^{2}\left\langle\widehat{H}_{j}-H_{j}^{\star},\,X\right\rangle^{2}]}{\sigma_{j}^{2}}.

Step 3: score plug-in term
T_{1j}
.

By the Lipschitz bound on
s_{\eta}
in
\eta

(Assumption
A.1
(iii)),

|s_{\eta}(Y,\widehat{\eta})-s_{\eta}(Y,\eta^{\star})|\leq|\widehat{\eta}-\eta^{\star}|\leq 2\left\lVert\widehat{\Theta}-\Theta^{\star}\right\rVert_{\infty}
.
Hence

T_{1j}\leq\frac{4\left\lVert\widehat{\Theta}-\Theta^{\star}\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2}\mathbb{P}_{n}\left\langle\widehat{H}_{j},\,X\right\rangle^{2}}{\sigma_{j}^{2}}.

By plug-in variance consistency (Lemma
E.4
, applied to

\widehat{H}_{j}
instead of
H_{j}^{\star}
; the calculation is the same),

\mathbb{P}_{n}\left\langle\widehat{H}_{j},\,X\right\rangle^{2}/\sigma_{j}^{2}=\widehat{\sigma}_{j}^{2}/\sigma_{j}^{2}\cdot\mathbb{P}_{n}\left\langle\widehat{H}_{j},\,X\right\rangle^{2}/\widehat{\sigma}_{j}^{2}=O(1)

in probability. By the entrywise theorem
(
4
),

\left\lVert\widehat{\Theta}-\Theta^{\star}\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2}\leq C\bar{d}\,\mathrm{polylog}(n\bar{d})/n=Cr_{n}^{2}
.
Combining,

\max_{j}T_{1j}\leq Cr_{n}^{2}
on
\mathcal{E}_{n}
.

Step 4: Empirical Fluctuation
T_{2,j}

Lemma E.6

(Empirical square bound for estimated efficient directions)

Let
\mathcal{J}
be a polynomial-size family of score-gap contrasts, and for each

j\in\mathcal{J}
write

H_{j}^{\star}:=A^{-1}P_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma_{j},\qquad\widehat{H}_{j}:=\widehat{A}^{-1}\widehat{P}_{\mathbb{T}}\Gamma_{j},\qquad D_{j}:=\widehat{H}_{j}-H_{j}^{\star}.

Let

\sigma_{j}^{2}:=P^{\star}\!\left[\{s_{i}^{\star}\}^{2}\langle H_{j}^{\star},X_{i}\rangle^{2}\right],\qquad s_{i}^{\star}:=s(Y_{i},\eta_{i}^{\star}),\qquad\eta_{i}^{\star}:=\langle X_{i},\Theta^{\star}\rangle.

Suppose that on the good event
\mathcal{E}_{n}
, uniformly over
j\in\mathcal{J}
,

\frac{\|D_{j}\|_{F}}{\|H_{j}^{\star}\|_{F}}\leq r_{n},\qquad r_{n}^{2}:=\frac{\bar{d}\log^{c}(n\bar{d})}{n},

and that the tangent-projection envelope satisfies

\sup_{X}\|P_{\mathbb{T}}X\|_{F}\vee\sup_{X}\|\widehat{P}_{\mathbb{T}}X\|_{F}\leq C\sqrt{\frac{\bar{d}}{d^{\star}}}.

Then, after increasing the logarithmic power
c
if necessary, with probability at
least
1-n^{-a}
,

\max_{j\in\mathcal{J}}P_{n}\frac{\{s_{i}^{\star}\}^{2}\langle\widehat{H}_{j}-H_{j}^{\star},X_{i}\rangle^{2}}{\sigma_{j}^{2}}\leq Cr_{n}^{2}.

In particular, this bound does not involve the inverse-information stability factor

C_{A}
.

\Trivlist

Work on the good event
\mathcal{E}_{n}
and condition on the auxiliary sample used to
construct
\widehat{H}_{j}
and
\widehat{P}_{\mathbb{T}}
. Then
D_{j}
is fixed with
respect to the evaluation sample. Define

U_{ij}:=\frac{\{s_{i}^{\star}\}^{2}\langle D_{j},X_{i}\rangle^{2}}{\sigma_{j}^{2}}.

We first control the population mean. By Fisher comparability and the pairwise
Frobenius reduction,

P^{\star}\!\left[\{s_{i}^{\star}\}^{2}\langle D_{j},X_{i}\rangle^{2}\right]\lesssim\frac{\|D_{j}\|_{F}^{2}}{d^{\star}},

while

\sigma_{j}^{2}=P^{\star}\!\left[\{s_{i}^{\star}\}^{2}\langle H_{j}^{\star},X_{i}\rangle^{2}\right]\asymp\frac{\|H_{j}^{\star}\|_{F}^{2}}{d^{\star}}.

Therefore

P^{\star}U_{j}=\frac{P^{\star}[\{s_{i}^{\star}\}^{2}\langle D_{j},X_{i}\rangle^{2}]}{\sigma_{j}^{2}}\lesssim\frac{\|D_{j}\|_{F}^{2}}{\|H_{j}^{\star}\|_{F}^{2}}\leq r_{n}^{2}.

Next we prove an envelope bound for
U_{ij}
that does not use

\|D_{j}\|_{\infty}
. Since

D_{j}=\widehat{H}_{j}-H_{j}^{\star},

we have

|\langle D_{j},X_{i}\rangle|\leq|\langle\widehat{H}_{j},X_{i}\rangle|+|\langle H_{j}^{\star},X_{i}\rangle|.

Because
\widehat{H}_{j}\in\widehat{\mathbb{T}}
and

H_{j}^{\star}\in\mathbb{T}
,

|\langle\widehat{H}_{j},X_{i}\rangle|=|\langle\widehat{H}_{j},\widehat{P}_{\mathbb{T}}X_{i}\rangle|\leq\|\widehat{H}_{j}\|_{F}\,\|\widehat{P}_{\mathbb{T}}X_{i}\|_{F},

and

|\langle H_{j}^{\star},X_{i}\rangle|=|\langle H_{j}^{\star},P_{\mathbb{T}}X_{i}\rangle|\leq\|H_{j}^{\star}\|_{F}\,\|P_{\mathbb{T}}X_{i}\|_{F}.

The relative direction bound gives

\|\widehat{H}_{j}\|_{F}\leq\|H_{j}^{\star}\|_{F}+\|D_{j}\|_{F}\leq(1+r_{n})\|H_{j}^{\star}\|_{F}\leq 2\|H_{j}^{\star}\|_{F}

for all sufficiently large
n
. Hence, by the tangent-projection envelope,

|\langle D_{j},X_{i}\rangle|\lesssim\|H_{j}^{\star}\|_{F}\sqrt{\frac{\bar{d}}{d^{\star}}}.

Since
|s_{i}^{\star}|\leq 1
for the BTL score,

0\leq U_{ij}\lesssim\frac{\|H_{j}^{\star}\|_{F}^{2}(\bar{d}/d^{\star})}{\sigma_{j}^{2}}\lesssim\bar{d},

where the last step uses

\sigma_{j}^{2}\asymp\|H_{j}^{\star}\|_{F}^{2}/d^{\star}
. Thus,

\|U_{ij}\|_{\infty}\leq C\bar{d}.

Moreover,

\operatorname{Var}(U_{ij})\leq P^{\star}U_{ij}^{2}\leq\|U_{ij}\|_{\infty}P^{\star}U_{j}\lesssim\bar{d}\,r_{n}^{2}.

Bernstein’s inequality therefore gives, for any
x>0
,

|(P_{n}-P^{\star})U_{j}|\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{\bar{d}\,r_{n}^{2}x}{n}}+\frac{\bar{d}x}{n}

with probability at least
1-2e^{-x}
. Taking

x=C_{a}\log n+2\log|\mathcal{J}|

and union bounding over
j\in\mathcal{J}
, we obtain, with probability at least

1-n^{-a}
,

\max_{j\in\mathcal{J}}|(P_{n}-P^{\star})U_{j}|\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{\bar{d}\,r_{n}^{2}\log(n|\mathcal{J}|)}{n}}+\frac{\bar{d}\log(n|\mathcal{J}|)}{n}.

Since
|\mathcal{J}|
is polynomial in
\bar{d}
, we have

\log(n|\mathcal{J}|)\lesssim\log(n\bar{d})
. Recalling that

r_{n}^{2}=\frac{\bar{d}\log^{c}(n\bar{d})}{n},

both Bernstein terms are absorbed into
r_{n}^{2}
after increasing the logarithmic
power
c
:

\sqrt{\frac{\bar{d}\,r_{n}^{2}\log(n|\mathcal{J}|)}{n}}+\frac{\bar{d}\log(n|\mathcal{J}|)}{n}\lesssim r_{n}^{2}.

Combining this empirical fluctuation bound with the population bound

P^{\star}U_{j}\lesssim r_{n}^{2}
, we conclude that

\max_{j\in\mathcal{J}}P_{n}U_{j}\leq\max_{j\in\mathcal{J}}P^{\star}U_{j}+\max_{j\in\mathcal{J}}|(P_{n}-P^{\star})U_{j}|\lesssim r_{n}^{2}.

This proves the claim.

Conclusion.

Combining steps 1–4,

c_{n}^{2}\leq 8(T_{1j}+T_{2j})+16r_{n}^{2}\leq Cr_{n}^{2}.

The probability calibration to
1-n^{-a}
follows from
Appendix
A.8
.

E.11
Critical-value vs. dimension discussion

The bootstrap critical value
c_{1-\alpha}^{\ast}
is the

(1-\alpha)
-quantile of a maximum of
p
correlated approximately
standard-normal coordinates. In the worst case (weakly dependent or
independent),

c_{1-\alpha}^{\ast}\asymp\sqrt{2\log(2p/\alpha)}
; under strong
correlation, it can be substantially smaller. Since

p\in\{d_{\mathrm{m}}-1,d_{\mathrm{t}}(d_{\mathrm{m}}-1),d_{\mathrm{t}}d_{\mathrm{m}}(d_{\mathrm{m}}-1)\}\leq\bar{d}^{3}
,

\log p\lesssim\log\bar{d}
and therefore

c_{1-\alpha}^{\ast}=O(\sqrt{\log\bar{d}})
. Combining with

\Sigma_{jj}=\widehat{\sigma}_{j}^{2}\asymp\bar{d}

(Lemma
D.14
), the worst-case simultaneous band
width is of order
c_{1-\alpha}^{\ast}\widehat{\sigma}_{j}/\sqrt{n}\asymp\sqrt{\bar{d}\log\bar{d}/n}
,
matching the rate predicted by the entrywise estimation theorem
(Theorem
3.1
) up to logarithmic factors.
