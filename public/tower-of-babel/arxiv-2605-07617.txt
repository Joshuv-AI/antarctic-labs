1.
Introduction

The classification of algebraic varieties up to isomorphism is a central and often hard problem in algebraic geometry, even for families of varieties that are very special in nature. A classical illustration is provided by the family of Danielewski surfaces
\{D_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, where

D_{n}=V(x_{1}^{n}x_{2}+x_{3}^{2}+1)\subset\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{3}.

A natural question is:

Question
.

Let
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
. Does
D_{m}\cong D_{n}
imply
m=n
?

The answer to this question is not immediately obvious, and yet this question has an affirmative answer. The surfaces
D_{n}
are pairwise non-isomorphic and can be distinguished by their homology groups at infinity
[
13
]
. Such examples highlight that even in seemingly simple families of affine hypersurfaces that are parametrized by
\mathbb{N}
, it may well be difficult to distinguish which members are part of the same isomorphism class. A classification of the isomorphism classes of Danielewski surfaces was later given in
[
22
]
. While the above question was very natural in view of the importance of these Danielewski surfaces as counterexamples to the Generalized Zariski Cancellation Problem, a similar question can be asked for various interesting families of affine varieties.

In this article, we investigate analogous questions for a family of affine hypersurfaces parameterized by
(\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2})^{n}
. For
{\bf a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in(\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2})^{n}
, consider the polynomial

f_{{\bf a}}=x_{1}^{a_{1}}+\cdots+x_{n}^{a_{n}}\in\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}],

and the surjective morphism of
\mathbb{C}
-varieties

\varphi_{{\bf a}}:\mathbb{A}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}

induced by the
\mathbb{C}
-algebra monomorphism
\mathbb{C}[f_{\bf a}]\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}].

We view
\varphi_{{\bf a}}
as an affine fibration. A basic but crucial observation is that the restriction of
\varphi_{{\bf a}}
over
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0\}
is isotrivial: for any
t\in\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0\}
, the fiber
\varphi_{{\bf a}}^{-1}(t)
is isomorphic to
\varphi_{{\bf a}}^{-1}(-1)
. This follows from the weighted homogeneity of
f_{{\bf a}}
, which allows one to rescale coordinates and identify all nonzero fibers via
\mathbb{C}
-isomorphisms. Thus the affine fibration
\varphi_{{\bf a}}
is governed by two distinguished fibres:

•

the special fiber
V(f_{{\bf a}})=\varphi_{{\bf a}}^{-1}(0)
, which is a singular hypersurface with an isolated singularity at the origin—famously known as a
Pham-Brieskorn hypersurface
;

•

the general fiber
V(f_{{\bf a}}+1)=\varphi_{{\bf a}}^{-1}(-1)
, which is a smooth hypersurface.

This leads naturally to the problem of understanding to what extent the isomorphism type of these fibers determines
{\bf a}
.

Given
{\bf a},{\bf b}\in(\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2})^{n}
, we write
{\bf a}\sim{\bf b}
if
{\bf b}
is obtained from
{\bf a}
by a permutation of its entries. Consider the following two questions (the
n=3
case of Question (2) was asked by R. V. Gurjar):

Question
.

Let
{\bf a},{\bf b}\in(\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1})^{n}
, and consider the associated affine fibrations
\varphi_{{\bf a}},\varphi_{{\bf b}}:\mathbb{A}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}
defined above.

(1)

Suppose
V(f_{{\bf a}})\cong V(f_{{\bf b}})
(the special fibers are isomorphic). Is
{\bf a}\sim{\bf b}
?

(2)

Suppose
V(f_{{\bf a}}+1)\cong V(f_{{\bf b}}+1)
(the general fibers are isomorphic). Is
{\bf a}\sim{\bf b}
?

Question (1) is natural in view of the fact that Pham-Brieskorn hypersurfaces are widely studied from various perspectives. (A simple internet search will yield hundreds of academic articles on the varieties and manifolds defined by these equations.) Question (1) asks whether the isomorphism class of a Pham-Brieskorn hypersurface determines its exponents, while Question (2) asks whether the same is true for smooth affine hypersurface
V(f_{{\bf a}}+1)
. Although all fibers of
\varphi_{{\bf a}}
over
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0\}
for a fixed
{\bf a}
are isomorphic, it is not immediately clear whether their common isomorphism type determines
{\bf a}
.

Note that the seemingly subtle distinction between Questions (1) and (2) plays an essential role in our analysis. While Question (1) has an affirmative answer for all
n
(see Proposition
2.1
), Question (2) presents more of a difficulty. The main issue is that the hypersurfaces
V(f_{{\bf a}}+1)
and
V(f_{{\bf b}}+1)
are smooth, and hence lack the singular structure that provides the accessible invariants used to resolve Question (1).

Question (2) is trivial in the
n=1
case, and was settled for
n=2
(i.e., for curves) in
[
15
]
. The main result of this article is an affirmative answer to Question (2) in the
n=3
case.

Main Theorem
.

Let
{\bf a},{\bf b}\in(\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2})^{3}
and let
f_{{\bf a}},f_{{\bf b}}\in\mathbb{C}[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]
. If
V(f_{{\bf a}}+1)\cong V(f_{{\bf b}}+1)
then
{\bf a}\sim{\bf b}
.

We emphasize that, for fixed tuples
{\bf a},{\bf b}\in(\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2})^{k}
, it is often possible to find an invariant that distinguishes the hypersurfaces
V(f_{{\bf a}}+1)
and
V(f_{{\bf b}}+1)
. The difficulty lies in proving that such a distinguishing invariant must exist
a priori
, without any assumptions on the specific chosen tuples
{\bf a}
and
{\bf b}
. The main result of this article shows that this is indeed the case for the 2-dimensional hypersurfaces of form
V(f_{\bf a}+1)
.

Let us give the main ideas behind our proof of the Main Theorem. We let
{\bf a},{\bf b}\in(\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2})^{3}
and define
X_{\bf a}=V(f_{\bf a}+1)=V(x_{1}^{a_{1}}+x_{2}^{a_{2}}+x_{3}^{a_{3}}+1)\subset\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{3}
and
X_{\bf b}=V(f_{\bf b}+1)=V(x_{1}^{b_{1}}+x_{2}^{b_{2}}+x_{3}^{b_{3}}+1)\subset\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{3}
. Our goal is to show that if
X_{\bf a}\cong X_{\bf b}
then
{\bf a}\sim{\bf b}
.

The first step is to partition
(\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2})^{3}
into four subsets
S_{0},S_{+,+},S_{+,0},S_{+,-}
with the property that if
X_{\bf a}\cong X_{\bf b}
, then both
{\bf a}
and
{\bf b}
belong in the same subset. We call subsets with this property
parameter-closed subsets
(see Section
5
). The fact that these four subsets are parameter-closed is established in Lemma
6.8
. The proof of that lemma mainly depends on two things:

(1)

that
X_{\bf a}
can always be embedded as an affine open subset of a well-formed quasismooth weighted projective surface, which we denote by
\overline{X_{\bf a}}
(see Section
3
);

(2)

the birational geometry of projective surfaces with rational singularities (see Propositions
2.7
,
2.8
).

The second step is to show that if
{\bf a}
and
{\bf b}
are in any of the four subsets above, then
X_{\bf b}\cong X_{\bf a}
implies
{\bf b}\sim{\bf a}
. When
{\bf a},{\bf b}\in S_{+,+}
we prove that any isomorphism between
X_{\bf a}
and
X_{\bf b}
extends to an isomorphism between
\overline{X_{\bf a}}
and
\overline{X_{\bf b}}
. This allows us to use a recent result of Esser
[
12
, Theorem 2.1]
to conclude that
{\bf a}\sim{\bf b}
. It turns out that
S_{+,0}
is a finite set. If
{\bf a},{\bf b}\in S_{+,0}
and
X_{\bf a}\cong X_{\bf b}
, the fact that
{\bf a}\sim{\bf b}
can be deduced by computing two invariants (the rank of the divisor class group and the genus of the boundary curve) for each surface
X_{\bf a}
such that
{\bf a}\in S_{+,0}
. If
{\bf a},{\bf b}\in S_{+,-}
and
X_{\bf a}\cong X_{\bf b}
, the equivalence
{\bf a}\sim{\bf b}
is deduced by combining a recent result of Daigle on locally nilpotent derivations
[
9
, Proposition 10.5]
together with the same two invariants used in the
S_{+,0}
case. Finally, if
{\bf a},{\bf b}\in S_{0}
, the proof combines:

•

a characterization of when the surfaces
V(f_{\bf a})\subseteq\mathbb{A}^{3}
are rational (see Proposition
6.10
),

•

an analysis of the singular points of the weighted projective surface
\overline{X_{\bf a}}
(see Section
4
),

•

an analysis of weighted graphs that appear as dual graphs of SNC boundaries (see
2.2
to
2.5
).

The article is organized as follows. We give the necessary preliminaries in Section
2
. Section
3
establishes the notation, Section
4
classifies the singularities of certain weighted projective hypersurfaces, Section
5
defines and gives basic properties of parameter-closed subsets and Section
6
proves the Main Theorem using the previously established results.

Preliminaries

The following proposition gives an affirmative answer to Question (1), valid in any dimension. This proof is due to the second author and M. Upmanyu. While this result may be known, we have not found a published version in the literature, so we include their proof for the convenience of the reader.

2.1 Proposition
.

Let
f_{\bf a}=x_{1}^{a_{1}}+\dots+x_{n}^{a_{n}}
and let
f_{\bf b}=x_{1}^{b_{1}}+\dots+x_{n}^{b_{n}}
where
f_{\bf a},f_{\bf b}\in\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
. If
V(f_{\bf a})\cong V(f_{\bf b})
, then
{\bf a}\sim{\bf b}
.

Proof.

Let
M_{\bf a}
and
T_{\bf a}
respectively denote the Milnor and Tjurina algebras of the singularity of
V(f_{\bf a})
. Since
f_{\bf a}\in\langle\{x_{i}^{a_{i}-1}\}_{i=1}^{n}\rangle
,
M_{\bf a}=T_{\bf a}
. We obtain

\frac{\mathbb{C}\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}}{\langle x_{1}^{a_{1}-1},\ldots,x_{n}^{a_{n}-1}\rangle}=M_{\bf a}\cong T_{\bf a}\cong T_{\bf b}\cong M_{\bf b}=\frac{\mathbb{C}\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}\}}{\langle y_{1}^{b_{1}-1},\ldots,y_{n}^{b_{n}-1}\rangle}

where the middle isomorphism is by
[
21
, Theorem (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii)]
, . Since
(M_{\bf a},\mathfrak{m}_{\bf a})
and
(M_{\bf b},\mathfrak{m}_{\bf b})
are local rings, any isomorphism must map
\mathfrak{m}_{\bf a}
to
\mathfrak{m}_{\bf b}
. Consequently, the Hilbert polynomials of
M_{\bf a}
and
M_{\bf b}
are equal. Comparing them gives

\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}(1+t+t^{2}+\cdots+t^{a_{i}-2})=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}(1+t+t^{2}+\cdots+t^{b_{i}-2}).

Factoring these two polynomials in
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}[t]
into products of linear terms, we deduce that
\underset{1\leq i\leq n}{\max}\{a_{i}-2\}=\underset{1\leq i\leq n}{\max}\{b_{i}-2\}
. Assume without loss of generality that this maximum is achieved at
a_{n}-2=b_{n}-2
. Then
a_{n}=b_{n}
and we can divide both sides by
1+t+\cdots+t^{a_{n}-2}
. Repeating this process gives
{\bf a}\sim{\bf b}
.

∎

Weighted graphs and their equivalence classes.

2.2
.

Throughout this article, a
weighted graph

\mathcal{G}
is a finite connected undirected graph such that

•

no edge connects a vertex to itself

•

there is at most one edge between each pair of vertices

•

each vertex is decorated with an integer
n\in\mathbb{Z}
call a
weight
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a weighted graph.

(1)

Let
v
be a vertex of
\mathcal{G}
.
The
blowing-up of
\mathcal{G}
at
v
is the weighted graph obtained from
\mathcal{G}
by adding a vertex
x
of weight
(-1)
,
adding the edge
\{v,x\}
, and decreasing the weight of
v
by
1
.

(2)

Let
\epsilon=\{u,v\}
be an edge of
\mathcal{G}
.
The
blowing-up of
\mathcal{G}
at
\epsilon
is the weighted graph obtained from
\mathcal{G}
by deleting the edge
\epsilon
,
adding a vertex
x
of weight
(-1)
and two edges
\{u,x\}
and
\{v,x\}
,
and decreasing the weights of
u
and
v
by
1
.

In (a) and (b), we refer to
x
as
the vertex created by the blowing-up.

The
degree
of a vertex
x
is the number of edges incident to
x
. A vertex
x
of a weighted graph
\mathcal{H}
is
superfluous
if
its weight is
(-1)
,
its degree is
1
or
2
,
and no two neighbors of
x
are neighbors of each other.
It is not hard to see that a vertex
x
of
\mathcal{H}
is superfluous if and only if
\mathcal{H}
is the result of blowing-up some
weighted graph
\mathcal{G}
at some vertex or edge in such a way that
x
is the vertex created by that blowing-up.
The graph
\mathcal{G}
is then uniquely determined by the pair
(\mathcal{H},x)
, and is called the
blowing-down of
\mathcal{H}
at
x
.
A weighted graph is
minimal
if it does not contain any superfluous vertex, or equivalently, if it cannot be blown down.
Weighted graphs
\mathcal{G}_{1},\mathcal{G}_{2}
are
equivalent
(and we write
\mathcal{G}_{1}\sim\mathcal{G}_{2}
) if
\mathcal{G}_{1}
and
\mathcal{G}_{2}
can be obtained from one another via a sequence of blowing up and blowing down operations. Moreover,
\sim
defines an equivalence relation on the set of weighted graphs. (Note that the assumption that our weighted graphs are connected allows us to avoid considering the “free blowing up” operation which needs to be considered when studying equivalence classes of weighted graphs that are not necessarily connected. See
[
8
, p.65]
for example.)

2.5 Lemma
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a star-shaped weighted graph whose central vertex
v_{0}
has degree at least 3. If every vertex of
\mathcal{G}
other than
v_{0}
has weight at most
-2
, then
\mathcal{G}
is the unique minimal graph in its equivalence class.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{G}’
be any minimal element of the equivalence class of
\mathcal{G}
. Then there exists a sequence of weighted graphs

(3)

\mathcal{G}_{0},\mathcal{G}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{G}_{n}

such that
\mathcal{G}_{0}=\mathcal{G},\mathcal{G}_{n}=\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
and for each
i=0,\dots,n-1
,
\mathcal{G}_{i+1}

It is well known that cyclic quotient singularities are rational and that the exceptional locus in a resolution of a rational surface singularity is a tree of rational curves

denote the minimal resolution of

. Then
\pi^{-1}(p)
is a chain of rational curves

that satisfy

,
E_{1}
intersects
\widetilde{V(y)}
transversely and
E_{k}
intersects
\widetilde{V(x)}

Let

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{0}\!\left(Y,\operatorname{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}(mrK_{Y})\right)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{0}\!\left(X,\operatorname{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(mrK_{X})\right)}

Let

5.
Parameter-Closed Subsets

5.1 Definition
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
S\subseteq T\subseteq(\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2})^{k}
. Let
\textbf{a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{k}),\textbf{b}=(b_{1},\dots,b_{k})\in T
. We say that
S
is parameter-closed in
T
if the following condition holds:

(14)

if
{\bf a}\in S
and
X_{\bf b}\cong X_{\bf a}
then
{\bf b}\in S
.

In the special case where
T=(\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2})^{k}
, we will simply say
S
is parameter-closed
.

5.2
.

Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
and consider the following family of subsets of
(\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2})^{k}

(15)

\mathscr{C}=\{\text{parameter-closed subsets of $(\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2})^{k}$}\}.

Observe that
\varnothing,(\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2})^{k}\in\mathscr{C}
. We show that
\mathscr{C}
is closed both under arbitrary unions, and arbitrary intersections. Indeed, let
\{C_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a collection of parameter-closed sets. Suppose
{\bf a}\in\cup_{i\in I}C_{i}
and
X_{\bf b}\cong X_{\bf a}
. Then
{\bf a}\in C_{j}
for some
j\in I
and since
C_{j}
is parameter closed,
{\bf b}\in C_{j}\subseteq\cup_{i\in I}C_{i}
. So
\cup_{i\in I}C_{i}
is parameter-closed and hence that
\mathscr{C}
is closed under arbitrary unions. Similarly, suppose
X_{\bf b}\cong X_{\bf a}
where
{\bf a}\in\cap_{i\in I}C_{i}
. Since each
C_{i}
is parameter-closed,
{\bf b}\in C_{i}
for all
i\in I
, so
{\bf b}\in\cap_{i\in I}C_{i}
. This shows that
\mathscr{C}
is closed under arbitrary intersections. Thus,
\mathscr{C}
defines an Alexandrov topology on
(\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2})^{k}
. Note also that if
C\subseteq D
and
C
is parameter-closed in
D
, then
D\setminus C
is parameter-closed in
D
. It is also easy to see that if
S
is parameter-closed in
T
and
T
is parameter-closed in
U
, then
S
is parameter-closed in
U
.

5.3 Example
.

Recall that a
{\rm\bf k}
-variety
X
is

if the only
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-action on

is the trivial one. Define

It is easy to see that

is parameter-closed in

. Furthermore, we claim

For

is isomorphic to a Danielewski surface and hence is not rigid. The inclusion

follows by combining

6.15 Remark
.

For each
m\geq 1
, the surface
D_{m}=\operatorname{{\rm Spec}}(\mathbb{C}[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]/\langle x_{1}^{m}x_{2}+x_{3}^{2}+1\rangle
is non-rigid. By Example
5.3
,
D_{m}
is isomorphic to
X_{{\bf a}}
for some
{\bf a}\in(\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2})^{3}
if and only if
{\bf a}\in S_{\text{\rm Dan}}
, in which case we must have
m=1
. Furthermore, by Danielewski’s famous result,
X_{2,2,2}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}\cong D_{m}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}
for every
m\geq 1
. This shows that
X_{\bf a}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}\cong X^{\prime}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}
does not imply that
X^{\prime}\cong X_{\bf a}
for some
{\bf a}\in(\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2})^{3}
. If however, we impose the additional restriction that
X^{\prime}=X_{\bf b}
for some
{\bf b}\in(\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2})^{3}
, then these surfaces are cancellative. We obtain:

6.16 Corollary
.

Let
{\bf a},{\bf b}\in(\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2})^{3}
. If
X_{\bf a}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}\cong X_{\bf b}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}
, then
X_{\bf a}\cong X_{\bf b}
and
{\bf a}\sim{\bf b}
.

Proof.

Assume
X_{\bf a}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}\cong X_{\bf b}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}
. Then
X_{\bf a}
is non-rigid if and only if
X_{\bf b}
is non-rigid (by
[
14
, Theorem 2.24]
, for example). If
X_{\bf a}
is not rigid then
X_{\bf b}
is not rigid, and so
{\bf a},{\bf b}\in S_{\text{\rm Dan}}
. If particular we may assume
{\bf a}=(2,2,a_{3})
and
{\bf b}=(2,2,b_{3})
by Example
5.3
. By Table
1
, we have
a_{3}-1=\operatorname{\rm rank}(\operatorname{{\rm Cl}}(X_{\bf a}))=\operatorname{\rm rank}(\operatorname{{\rm Cl}}(X_{\bf a}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}))=\operatorname{\rm rank}(\operatorname{{\rm Cl}}(X_{\bf b}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}))=\operatorname{\rm rank}(\operatorname{{\rm Cl}}(X_{\bf b}))=b_{3}-1
and so
{\bf a}\sim{\bf b}
.

If
X_{\bf a}
is rigid, then so is
X_{\bf b}
and by
[
3
, Lemma 2]
,
X_{\bf a}=\operatorname{{\rm ML}}(X_{\bf a}\times\mathbb{A}^{1})\cong\operatorname{{\rm ML}}(X_{\bf b}\times\mathbb{A}^{1})=X_{\bf b}
. So
X_{\bf a}\cong X_{\bf b}
and by Theorem
6.14
,
{\bf a}\sim{\bf b}
.
∎
