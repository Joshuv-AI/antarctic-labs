ORGANIZATION

Legislative and Liaison Division

RESPONSIBILITY. The Chief, Legislative and Liaison Division, War Department
Special Staff will be responsible for the initiation and supervision of all
War Department relations with the Congress and the maintenance of liaison
between the War Department and certain Federal agencies. The Legislative
and Liaison Division will —

1. Supervise the preparation and processing of legislation in which the War
Department has interest.

2. Maintain liaison with the Congress on legislative matters, investigations
of War Department activities, and War Department matters of interest to
individual Members of Congress.

3. Maintain liaison between the War Department and certain Federal agencies
on legislative matters of mutual interest, including the Federal Works
Agency and the Civil Aeronautics Authority, and with respect to certain

war projects.

U. Clear and coordinate all War Department correspondence and reports to the
Congress prepared for the signature of the Secretary of War or the Chief Stafl
as well as Congressional correspondence involving War Department policy but
not signed by the above.

ORGANIZATION. The subdivisions of the Legislative and Liaison Division and
their functions are:

1. Legislative Branch.

a. Keeps abreast of legislation pending before the Congress to determine
that which is of interest to the War Department.

b. Analyzes proposed War Department legislation in terms of pending
legislation, existing law. War Department policies, and the President's program,
and coordinates it within and outside of the War Department.

c. Handles the processing from the War Department, through the Bureau of
the Budget and to the Congress, of all legislation approved by the Secretary
of War.

d. Prepares or supervises t tie preparation of reports on legislation re-
quested by the Bureau of the Budget or by the Congress.

e. Digests the Congressional Record for distribution to certain interested
War Department agencies.

f. Presents testimony before the Congress or Congressional committees in
connection with legislation of interest to the War Department and arranges
for the appearance of War Department witnesses.

2, Congressional Investigations Branch.

a. Supervises all liaison between the War Department and the Congress in
connection with Congressional investigations of War Department activities.

b. Supervises preparation and presentation of evidence requested of
the War Department by the Congress.

c* Assists Congressional committees in connection with investigations
of War Department activities.

d. Analyzes information received which suggests possible criticism of
War Department activities and takes action when necessary.

3. Liaison Branch.

a. Maintains liaison with the Congress, its individual members, or its
committees (except the Appropriations Committees) in connection vdth legisla-
tion and reports prepared under the supervision of the Legislative Branch.

b. Assists Members of Congress in matters referred to the War Department
for action (including the preparation of correspondence and the gathering of
information from all echelons of the Army, when necessary).

c. Controls the processing of all War Department correspondence and
reports for the Congress and prepared for the signature of the Secretary of
War or the Chief of Staff as well as Congressional correspondence involving
War Department policy but not signed by the above.

d. Coordinates with the Legislative Branch, the appearance of War
Department witnesses before the Congress.

U. Federal Agencies Branch.

a. Maintains liaison with certain Federal agencies concerning war
projects (except defense housing and access roads projects) submitted for
the recommendation of the War Department as follows:

(1) War Public 'forks Division, Federal forks Agency, with reference
to projects proposed under the Lanham Act.

(2) Civil Aeronautics Authority, Commerce Department, with reference
to the development of the airport construction program in
connection with the duties of the Secretary of War as a member
of the Airport Approval Board, established by Public Law 812,

76th Congress.
