The strength of Ramsey’s theorem for α \alpha -large sets
Lorenzo Carlucci, Andrea Volpi, Konrad Zdanowski

Abstract
. We calibrate the reverse mathematical strength of a family of extensions of Ramsey’s theorem to finite colorings of certain subsets of the natural numbers of unbounded finite dimension. Specifically, we analyze the principles 𝖱𝖳 ! α k \mathsf{RT}^{!\alpha}_{k} asserting that every k k -coloring of the exactly α \alpha -large subsets of an infinite X ⊆ ℕ X\subseteq\mathbb{N} admits an infinite homogeneous set, where α \alpha -largeness is defined via systems of fundamental sequences in the style of Ketonen and Solovay. For each countable ordinal α < Γ 0 \alpha<\Gamma_{0} and each k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 , we prove over 𝖱𝖢𝖠 0 \mathsf{RCA}_{0} that the hierarchy of theorems 𝖱𝖳 ! α k \mathsf{RT}^{!\alpha}_{k} corresponds exactly to the hierarchy of systems axiomatized by closure under transfinite Turing jumps, yielding a fine-grained classification between 𝖠𝖢𝖠 0 \mathsf{ACA}_{0} and 𝖠𝖳𝖱 0 \mathsf{ATR}_{0} . Our results extend previous work on the case α = ω \alpha=\omega and provide a uniform correspondence between countable indecomposable ordinals below Γ 0 \Gamma_{0} and natural Ramsey-like theorems.

1.
Introduction

The study of the logical strength of Ramsey-like theorems has long been central in reverse mathematics.
In this paper we calibrate the axiomatic strength of a family of far-reaching generalizations of the classical infinite Ramsey’s theorem to colorings of sets of unbounded finite dimension.
In particular we analyze Ramsey-like theorems for colorings of so called exactly
\alpha
-large or
\alpha
-size subsets of the natural numbers.

A motivation to generalize largeness notions for finite sets of natural numbers comes from the celebrated result of Paris and Harrington.
They proved in
[
PH77
]
that a certain statement in finite Ramsey theory, expressible in Peano arithmetic, is not provable in this system.
This is often regarded as the first “natural” true statement independent from Peano arithmetic.
The concept of a relatively large (or
\omega
-large) finite subset of the natural numbers is the basic ingredient of the Paris-Harrington principle.
In this context, a finite set
s
of natural numbers is called relatively large (or
\omega
-large) if
|s|\geq\min s+1
.
With this terminology, the Paris-Harrington principle is just the standard finite Ramsey’s theorem with the extra condition that the monochromatic set is relatively large.
Thus, the largeness of the homogeneous set is given in terms of the elements of the set and not as a fixed prescribed cardinality.
It is possible to generalize this idea to obtain largeness notions associated to ordinals.
Largeness notions of this kind, based on systems of fundamental sequences, were introduced in
[
KS81
]
.
They have since been used by many other authors, especially in the study of finite Ramsey-like statements
[
BK99
,
BK02
,
BK06
,
KPW07
,
KZ09
]
.
Recently, a far-reaching extension of the Paris-Harrington principle has been introduced and analyzed in
[
MMV26
]
.

Relatively large sets also naturally arise in Ramsey theory for purely combinatorial reasons.
The natural generalization of Ramsey’s theorem to finite colorings of
all finite sets
fails, as witnessed by coloring according to the parity of the size of the set.
The following weakening is also false: for every finite coloring
c
of the finite subsets of the natural numbers there exists an infinite set
H
of natural numbers such that for infinitely many numbers
n
,
c
is constant on the
n
-size subsets of
H
.
Interestingly, a counterexample is given by the coloring that assigns one color to all relatively large sets and the opposite color to all other sets.

While
\omega
-large sets provide a counterexample to the natural extension of Ramsey’s theorem to colorings of all finite sets, finite sets
s
such that
|s|=\min s+1
, called
exactly

\omega
-large sets or
\omega
-size
sets, are the key to define extensions of Ramsey’s theorem for colorings of families of finite sets containing elements
of unbounded size
.
Weakening the requirement of homogeneity from all finite sets to all exactly
\omega
-large sets results in a true principle, which we denote by
\mathsf{RT}^{!\omega}
following
[
CZ14
]
.
This principle is the base case of a generalization of Ramsey’s theorem to colorings of exactly
\alpha
-large sets for ordinals
\alpha>\omega
, due to Pudlák and Rödl
[
PR82
]
and by Farmaki and Negrepontis
[
FN08
]
, which we denote by
\mathsf{RT}^{!\alpha}
.

\mathsf{RT}^{!\omega}
is known to be computationally and proof-theoretically stronger than the usual Ramsey’s theorem for each fixed finite dimension
\mathsf{RT}^{n}_{k}
and even stronger than Ramsey’s theorem for all finite dimensions
\forall n\mathsf{RT}^{n}_{k}
(see
[
Clo84
]
).
From the point of view of computability theory, the theorem corresponds the
\omega
-th Turing jump; in reverse mathematics terms, it is equivalent to
\mathsf{ACA}_{0}^{+}
over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
, where
\mathsf{ACA}_{0}^{+}
extends
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
by the axiom of closure under the
\omega
-th Turing jump (see
[
CZ14
]
).

Our main goal is to extend
[
CZ14
]
to all countable ordinals
\alpha<\Gamma_{0}
and to prove the following Main Theorem.

Theorem 1.1

(Main theorem)
.

For each
\alpha<\Gamma_{0}
, for all
k\geq 2
,

\mathsf{RCA}_{0}\vdash\mathsf{RT}^{!\alpha}_{k}\leftrightarrow\Pi^{0}_{\operatorname{lead}(\alpha)}\mathsf{\text{-}CA}_{0}.

Here
\operatorname{lead}(\alpha)
denotes the first term in the Cantor normal form of
\alpha
.
The system
\Pi^{0}_{\beta}\mathsf{\text{-}CA}_{0}
is axiomatized by closure under the
\beta
-th Turing jump.
These systems form a hierarchy intermediate between
\mathsf{ACA}_{0}
and
\mathsf{ATR}_{0}
.
The rest of the notation will be introduced in the next sections.
The Main Theorem shows that for each indecomposable ordinal
\omega^{\alpha}<\Gamma_{0}
, the system
\Pi^{0}_{\omega^{\alpha}}\mathsf{\text{-}CA}_{0}
is equivalent to countably many Ramsey-like statements.

Ramsey’s theorem for
\alpha
-size sets
\mathsf{RT}^{!\alpha}
can be seen as particular cases of Nash-Williams’ generalization of Ramsey’s theorem to families of finite subsets of the natural numbers satisfying some specific properties and called
barriers

[
Tod10
]
.
Nash-Williams’ Ramsey’s theorem for barriers is, in turn, a consequence of the clopen Ramsey’s theorem (see
[
Sim09
]
).
In this context, the family of exactly
\omega
-large sets is known as the Schreier barrier
[
Tod10
]
.
More generally,
\alpha
-size sets are barriers under mild assumptions (see
[
MMV26
]
for details).

Clote
[
Clo84
,
Clo86
]
gave a fine-grained analysis of the effective content of Nash-Williams’ Ramsey’s theorem for barriers as a function of the order-type of the barrier under lexicographic ordering.
From the viewpoint of computability theory, Clote’s results are comparable with ours.
On the other hand, our reverse mathematics results are new and are not trivial consequences of Clote’s results.
The main differences between Clote’s work and the present work are the following.
First, Clote proves the computability-theoretic lower bounds with respect to a notion of “canonical barrier” that is similar to, but does not coincide with, the notion of
\alpha
-size set.
More importantly, Clote’s lower bound proofs are not formalizable in the setting of subsystems of second-order arithmetic to the extent that they are based on a generalized notion of limit taken along a (canonical) barrier.
Indeed, the lower bounds need to be proved in a very different way in order to obtain reverse mathematics results.
Note that, in general, the following is the case.
Let
P
be a
\Pi^{1}_{2}
-principle such that there exists a computable instance of
P
such that all of its solutions compute the
\alpha
-th jump
\emptyset^{(\alpha)}
.
This does
not
imply, in general, that
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+P
proves
\forall X\exists Y(Y=X^{(\alpha)})
, even if the proof relativizes.

We now describe the structure of the paper.
In Section
2
we introduce the basic notions needed throughout the paper.
In particular we introduce a system of fundamental sequences; we fix a definition of transfinite Turing jumps; we introduce the crucial largeness notions based on ordinals; we define the axiomatic systems
\Pi^{0}_{\alpha}\mathsf{\text{-}CA}_{0}
; we formulate the Ramsey-like principles of interest.

In Section
3
we deal with the first half of the Main Theorem
1.1
, i.e. we show that
\mathsf{RT}^{!\omega^{\alpha}}
implies
\Pi^{0}_{\omega^{\alpha}}\mathsf{\text{-}CA}_{0}
.
We give two proofs of this result, based on quite different ideas.
In the first proof we define computable colorings of
\alpha
-size sets so as to ensure that all of their homogeneous sets code the appropriate transfinite Turing jump.
In the second proof we show that that
\mathsf{RT}^{!\omega^{\alpha}}_{4}
proves a well-ordering preservation principle known to be equivalent to
\Pi^{0}_{\omega^{\alpha}}\mathsf{\text{-}CA}_{0}
.
This proof combines the strategy of
[
CMZ24
]
with recent results of
[
MMV26
]
.

In Section
4
we conclude the proof of the Main Theorem
1.1
by showing that for each
\alpha
, the
(\alpha+1)
-th Turing jump of an instance of
\mathsf{RT}^{!\alpha}_{k}
computes a solution.

2.
Preliminaries

We work with the well-known theories of second-order arithmetic
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
,
\mathsf{ACA}_{0}
,
\mathsf{ATR}_{0}
and with the less studied intermediate systems
\Pi^{0}_{\alpha}\mathsf{\text{-}CA}_{0}
for
\alpha<\Gamma_{0}
that we define below.
For background on reverse mathematics we refer to
[
Sim09
,
DM22
]
.
For background on fundamental sequences and functions in the Hardy hierarchy we refer to
[
Poh09
,
FW98
]
.
We also use some results from
[
MM11
]
, which brings together some aspects of reverse mathematics, notations for well-orderings, and functions in the Hardy hierarchy.

We start by fixing some notation.
As usual, we denote ordinals by lowercase Greek letters.
We use uppercase letters
X,Y,Z
to denote subsets of
{\mathbb{N}}
which may be finite or infinite.
We use lowercase letters
s,t,u
to denote finite subsets of
{\mathbb{N}}
.
We use
{\mathbb{N}}^{+}
to denote the set of positive integers.

If
X\subseteq{\mathbb{N}}
we denote by
[X]^{<\omega}
the set of finite subsets of
X
and, for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, by
[X]^{n}
the set of subsets of
X
with exactly
n
elements.

We identify a subset
X
of
{\mathbb{N}}
with the strictly increasing sequence (finite or infinite) which enumerates it and denote by
X(i)
or
X_{i}
the element in position
i
in the sequence.
If
s
is such a finite sequence then
|s|
denotes its length (coinciding with the cardinality of the set) and we write
s=\langle s_{0},\ldots,s_{|s|-1}\rangle
.
We ambiguously use the same notation to refer to the numerical code of the sequence with respect to a standard pairing function.

Given
s,t\subseteq{\mathbb{N}}
, by
s\sqsubseteq t
we mean that
s
, as a sequence, is an initial segment (or prefix) of
t
.
This is stronger than
s\subseteq t
, which denotes set-theoretic inclusion as usual.
The irreflexive versions of the previous relations are denoted by
\sqsubset
and
\subset
.

We let
s^{*}=s\setminus\{\max s\}
and
X^{-}=X\setminus\{\min X\}
.
By
s<X
we mean that each element of
s
is strictly smaller than each element of
X
or equivalently (when
s
and
X
are nonempty)
\max s<\min X
.
We write
s{}^{\smallfrown}X
for the concatenation of the sequences
s
and
X
.
Notice that in our setting
s{}^{\smallfrown}X
does make sense only if
s<X
and, set-theoretically, coincides with
s\cup X
.

2.1.
Ordinals and fundamental sequences

The
Feferman–Schütte ordinal

\Gamma_{0}
is the first fixed-point of the binary Veblen function, namely the least ordinal
\alpha
such that
\alpha=\varphi_{\alpha}(0)
.
It is the proof-theoretic ordinal of the system
\mathsf{ATR}_{0}
(see
[
Sim09
]
and references therein).

Ordinals below
\Gamma_{0}
can be represented in a standard way using the binary Veblen function.
We refer to
[
Poh09
]
for a detailed treatment of such notations.
This gives a
recursive system of ordinal notation
for ordinals below
\Gamma_{0}
and from now on we tacitly assume to work with ordinals in such an ordinal notation system.
A specific notation system will be defined later.
We use
\leq
to refer to the ordering relation and we write
\alpha+1
to denote the successor of
\alpha
in the ordinal notation system.

We express the fact that
\alpha
is a (notation for a) well-ordering as follows.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\alpha
be a (notation for a) linear ordering
(X,\leq_{X})
.
We say that
\alpha
is a
well-ordering
if the following holds

\forall f\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to\alpha\,(\forall i<j(f(j)\leq_{X}f(i))\rightarrow\exists n\forall i\geq n(f(i)=f(n))).

We denote the above formula by
{\textrm{WO}}(\alpha)
.

If
{\textrm{WO}}(\alpha)
is provable in a theory
T
then
\alpha
is a well-ordering in (all models of)
T
and we say that
\alpha

is a well-ordering in

T
.

Recall that a
fundamental sequence
for an ordinal
\alpha
is a non-decreasing sequence converging to
\alpha
.
If we have a fundamental sequence for each ordinal below or equal to
\Gamma_{0}
, we speak of a
system of fundamental sequences on

\Gamma_{0}
.
We use the
system of fundamental sequences
on
\Gamma_{0}
defined in
[
MMV26
]
and the associated notion of normal form.

For ordinals
\alpha
and
\beta
we write
\alpha\mathrel{\geq\!\!\!\geq}\beta
to mean that the last term in the normal form of
\alpha
is greater than or equal to the first term in the normal form of
\beta
.

Definition 2.2
.

We stipulate that the fundamental sequence of
0
is
0[n]=0
for all
n
.
For a positive
\alpha<\Gamma_{0}
, we distinguish cases.

(1)

If
\alpha=\alpha_{0}+\omega^{\alpha_{1}}
with
\alpha_{0}\mathrel{\geq\!\!\!\geq}\omega^{\alpha_{1}}
then
\alpha[n]=\alpha_{0}+(\omega^{\alpha_{1}}[n])
.

(2)

If
0<\alpha<\omega^{\alpha}
then
\omega^{\alpha}[n]=\omega^{\alpha[n]}\cdot n
.

(3)

If
0<\delta<\varphi_{\delta}(0)
then
\varphi_{\delta}(0)[n]=\varphi_{\delta[n]}^{n+1}(0)
.

(4)

If
0<\alpha<\varphi_{\delta}(\alpha)
and
0<\delta
then
\varphi_{\delta}(\alpha)[n]=\varphi_{\delta[n]}^{n+1}(\varphi_{\delta}(\alpha[n])+1)
.

(5)

\Gamma_{0}[n]=\varphi_{\varphi_{\ddots\varphi_{0}(0)\iddots}(0)}(0)
, where
\varphi
is iterated
n+1
times.

A crucial property satisfied by this system of fundamental sequences is
nestedness
, which is a weakening of the Bachmann property.
A system of fundamental sequences is
nested
if it is never the case for
\gamma<\beta
and
n>1
that
\gamma>\beta[n]>\gamma[n]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

[
MMV26
, Theorem 2.16]

The system of fundamental sequences of Definition
2.2
is nested.

For
s\in[{\mathbb{N}}]^{<\omega}
and
\alpha<\Gamma_{0}

we denote by
\alpha[s]

the ordinal
\alpha[s_{0}]\ldots[s_{|s|-1}]
.

2.2.
Coding Turing jumps

We use standard notation from computability theory.
For a (finite or infinite) sequence
s
of natural numbers,
\{e\}^{s}(n)\downarrow=m
(or
\{e\}^{s}(n)=m
) denotes the fact that the computation of the oracle Turing machine with code
e
on input
n
, run with oracle
s
, terminates and outputs
m
.
If this computation does not halt we write
\{e\}^{s}(n)\uparrow
, otherwise we write
\{e\}^{s}(n)\downarrow
.
We write
\{e\}^{s}_{t}(n)\downarrow
(or
\{e\}^{s}(n)\downarrow\leq t
) if the computation halts in less than
t
steps.
We write
\{e\}^{s}(n)\downarrow_{m}
to denote that the computation has code below
m
, relative to a fixed coding scheme for computations.

We code Turing jumps as follows, by transfinite induction.
We tacitly use the fact that the ordinal
\alpha
is coded by a natural number.

Definition 2.4
.

We denote by
{\textrm{TJ}}(X,0)
the
0
-th jump of
X
, which is the set of pairs
{\{{(0,x)\colon x\in X}\}}
.
The
(\alpha+1)
-th jump of
X
,
{\textrm{TJ}}(X,\alpha+1)
, is the set

{\textrm{TJ}}(X,\alpha)\cup{\{{(\alpha+1,e)\colon{\{{e}\}}^{{\textrm{TJ}}(X,\alpha)}(0)\downarrow}\}}.

For a limit
\lambda
, we set
{\textrm{TJ}}(X,\lambda)
as
\bigcup_{\alpha<\lambda}{\textrm{TJ}}(X,\alpha)
.

Such a coding allows to compute easily
{\textrm{TJ}}(X,\beta)
from
{\textrm{TJ}}(X,\alpha)
, for
\beta<\alpha
.
Moreover, it is easy to see that there is a first-order formula that decides whether a given set
Y
is an
\alpha
-th Turing jump of
X
.
Let
Y\restriction\gamma
be a formula which restricts
Y
to elements
x
of the form
(\delta,z)
, for
\delta\leq\gamma
, for example
x=(\delta,z)\land\delta\leq\gamma\land x\in Y
.
Now, we can write the formula stating that
Y={\textrm{TJ}}(X,\alpha)
as follows

\forall x\,((0,x)\in Y\leftrightarrow x\in X)\land\forall\delta\leq\alpha\forall x\,((\delta+1,x)\in Y\leftrightarrow{\{{x}\}}^{Y\restriction\delta}(0){\downarrow}),

where we assume that
{\{{x}\}}^{Y\restriction\delta}(0){\downarrow}
is properly expressed.
Given a recursive ordinal notation system, the above formula is
\Pi^{0}_{2}
. In what follows, we will use the fact that
Y={\textrm{TJ}}(X,\alpha)
is definable in first-order arithmetic.

2.3.
Largeness notions based on ordinals

We next define the crucial notion of largeness based on ordinals.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
s\in[{\mathbb{N}}]^{<\omega}
and
\alpha<\Gamma_{0}
.
We say that
s
is
\alpha
-large
if
\alpha[s]=0
,
\alpha
-small
if
\alpha[s]>0
and
\alpha
-size
(or exactly
\alpha
-large) if
s
is
\alpha
-large but
s^{*}
is
\alpha
-small.

It is clear that for each
\alpha
every infinite set has an initial segment which is
\alpha
-large. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, a set
s\subseteq{\mathbb{N}}
is
n
-size precisely when its cardinality is
n
, so that
[X]^{!n}
coincides with
[X]^{n}
.

It was proved in
[
MMV26
]
that for each
s,t\in[{\mathbb{N}}]^{<\omega}
such that
s\subseteq t
and each
\alpha<\Gamma_{0}
, if
s
is
\alpha
-large, then
t
is
\alpha
-large, while if
s\subsetneq t
and
t
is
\alpha
-size, then
s
is
\alpha
-small. Nestedness is crucial to ensure these properties.

For a set
X\subseteq{\mathbb{N}}
, we write
[X]^{!\alpha}
to denote the set of subsets of
X
which are
\alpha
-size and by
[X]^{!\geq\alpha}
the set of subsets of
X
which are
\alpha
-large.
As any
\alpha
-size set is finite,
[X]^{!\alpha}
and
[X]^{!\geq\alpha}
are coded as second-order objects, as in our base theory
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
we have the totality of the exponential function.

We also introduce a sum operation
\uplus
between largeness notions.

Definition 2.6
.

We say that
s\in[{\mathbb{N}}]^{<\omega}
is
(\alpha\uplus\beta)
-large if
s
can be partitioned in two parts
s_{\beta}<s_{\alpha}
such that
s=s_{\beta}{}^{\smallfrown}s_{\alpha}
,
s_{\beta}
is
\beta
-size and
s_{\alpha}
is
\alpha
-large.
We may also say that
s
is
(\alpha\uplus\beta)
-size if in addition
s_{\alpha}
is
\alpha
-size.

Given ordinals
\beta<\alpha
, an
\alpha
-large set need not be
\beta
-large.
To address this issue, Ketonen and Solovay introduced in
[
KS81
]
the relation
\Rightarrow_{n}
and the norm function, and showed that these have well-behaved structural properties for ordinals below
\epsilon_{0}
.
As a consequence, any
\alpha
-large set whose minimum exceeds a bound depending only on
\beta
(called the norm of
\beta
) is guaranteed to be
\beta
-large.
We adapt these ideas to our system of fundamental sequences.

Definition 2.7
.

Given
\alpha,\beta<\Gamma_{0}
and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
we write
\alpha\Rightarrow_{n}\beta
to mean that
\beta=\alpha[n]\cdots[n]
for some number (possibly
0
) of
n
’s.

To each ordinal
\alpha
we associate the natural number

|\alpha|=\min\{n>1:\Gamma_{0}\Rightarrow_{n}\alpha\},

that we call the
norm
of
\alpha
.

Notice that we can always assume without loss of generality that
|\alpha|<|\alpha+1|
for each
\alpha<\Gamma_{0}
.

The following is
[
MMV26
, Proposition 2.12]
.
The proof needs nestedness of the system of fundamental sequences.

Proposition 2.8
.

For all
\beta,\delta<\Gamma_{0}
, if
\beta<\delta
then
\beta\leq\delta[|\beta|]
.
Moreover, if
s\in[{\mathbb{N}}]^{<\omega}
,
\delta[s]\leq\beta
and
\min s\geq|\beta|
then for some
t\sqsubseteq s
we have
\delta[t]=\beta
.
Consequently, if
s
is
\delta
-large and
\min s\geq|\beta|
then
s
is
\beta
-large.

We comment about the proofs of Theorem
2.3
and Proposition
2.8
in Remark
3.3
at the beginning of Section
3
, after introducing all the technical machinery we need.

Note that the minimum in the definition of the norm (Definition
2.7
) may not be recursive. However we can get a recursive norm by looking for the first
n
that we notice gets the job done (which may not be the minimum in the standard ordering). Then the properties of the norm stated in Proposition
2.8
are still satisfied by this notion of norm. Thus we make no distinction between the two in what follows.

2.4.
Systems between
\mathsf{ACA}_{0}
and
\mathsf{ATR}_{0}

A classification of subsystems of second-order arithmetic that lie strictly between
\mathsf{ACA}_{0}
and
\mathsf{ATR}_{0}
is obtained in
[
MM11
]
.
These systems can be characterized by an axiom stating the existence of the
\alpha
-th Turing jump of any given set for
\alpha<\Gamma_{0}
and are denoted by
\Pi^{0}_{\alpha}\mathsf{\text{-}CA}_{0}
.
If
\alpha<\omega
we simply obtain
\mathsf{ACA}_{0}
while if
\alpha=\omega
we get
\mathsf{ACA}_{0}^{+}
.
With the formalization of the Turing jump of Subsection
2.2
we define
\Pi^{0}_{\alpha}\mathsf{\text{-}CA}_{0}
as the theory

\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\,{\textrm{WO}}(\alpha)+\,\forall X\exists Y(Y={\textrm{TJ}}(X,\alpha)).

It is easy to see that in the context of reverse mathematics, only jumps corresponding to indecomposable ordinals are meaningful.
Let
\operatorname{lead}(\alpha)
be the leading term in the Cantor normal form of
\alpha
.

Lemma 2.9
.

For each
\alpha<\Gamma_{0}
,
\Pi^{0}_{\alpha}\mathsf{\text{-}CA}_{0}
and
\Pi^{0}_{\operatorname{lead}(\alpha)}\mathsf{\text{-}CA}_{0}
are equivalent over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
.

Proof.

The left-to-right direction is trivial since
\alpha\geq\operatorname{lead}(\alpha)
and models of
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
are closed under Turing reducibility.

For the right-to-left direction let
n
be a standard natural number such that
\operatorname{lead}(\alpha)\cdot n\geq\alpha
.
Then
\Pi^{0}_{\operatorname{lead}(\alpha)}\mathsf{\text{-}CA}_{0}
proves the existence of the
(\operatorname{lead}(\alpha)\cdot n)
-th Turing jump and hence also of the
\alpha
-th Turing jump.
∎

In view of Lemma
2.9
we only study the systems corresponding to
\omega^{\alpha}
-th Turing jumps for
\alpha<\Gamma_{0}
.
These subsystems of second-order arithmetic lie strictly between
\mathsf{ACA}_{0}
and
\mathsf{ATR}_{0}
and if
\alpha>\beta
then
\Pi^{0}_{\omega^{\alpha}}\mathsf{\text{-}CA}_{0}\vdash\Pi^{0}_{\omega^{\beta}}\mathsf{\text{-}CA}_{0}
.
It is easy to see that the hierarchy is strict.

2.5.
Ramsey theory

For each
n,k\in{\mathbb{N}}
the classical infinite Ramsey’s theorem
\mathsf{RT}^{n}_{k}
states that for each coloring
c
of the
n
-size subsets of an infinite set in

A purely computability-theoretic analysis of

We refer to an instance of a Ramsey-like theorem to indicate a coloring of the appropriate type and to a solution of a Ramsey-like theorem to indicate a homogeneous set.
Moreover some computable instance of

codes the jump
in the sense that all of its homogeneous sets compute the first Turing jump.

The above results translate to the framework of reverse mathematics as follows: for each

,

while for

Ramsey’s theorem for pairs

[

]
and

[

]

We next introduce the generalizations of Ramsey’s theorem to colorings of

Definition 2.10
.

Let
\alpha
a (notation for a) countable ordinal and let

\mathsf{RT}^{!\alpha}_{k}
is the following statement: for each infinite

and each coloring

The set

In the previous definition, largeness is understood in the sense of Subsection

, relative to the system of fundamental sequences on

introduced in Definition

As usual in reverse mathematics it is routine to show that for each

and each

3.
Lower bound

In this section we establish a lower bound on the logical strength of the principles
\mathsf{RT}^{!\omega^{\alpha}}_{k}
. We give two different proofs. The first one proceeds by designing colorings of the
\alpha
-size subsets of
{\mathbb{N}}
with hard homogeneous sets. The second one hinges on the recent
[

]
and ideas from
[

]
and proceeds by showing an implication from Ramsey’s theorem for
\alpha
-size sets to a well-ordering preservation principle of appropriate logical strength.

We start with some preliminary lemmas that we need for some technical steps in the rest of the section.

Lemma 3.1
.

For all
0<\alpha<\Gamma_{0}
, for all

\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{RT}_{k}^{!\omega^{\alpha}}\vdash{\textrm{WO}}(\omega^{\alpha})

Proof.

First we claim that for each

, for each

,
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{RT}_{k}^{!\omega^{\alpha}}\vdash\mathsf{RT}_{k}^{!\omega^{\beta}}
.
Let
c\colon[X]^{!\omega^{\beta}}\to k
be a coloring and let
n
be the norm of
\omega^{\beta}
(recall Definition
2.7
).
By Proposition
2.8
every
\omega^{\alpha}
-large set

is also
\omega^{\beta}
-large.
Let

be the coloring defined as

is the unique
\omega^{\beta}
-size initial segment of

Let

be an infinite homogeneous set for

We show that

We know by inductive hypothesis that for each

By

∎

We assume we fixed a recursive ordinal notation for

(1)

we say that

is in the interval
(x,y)
if

and
\max s<y
.

Lemma 3.12

(
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+{\textrm{WO}}(\Lambda)
)
.

Let
X\subseteq{\mathbb{N}}
be infinite.
Let
\alpha\in\Lambda
and let
S(\alpha,X)={\{{a_{0}<a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdots}\}}
.

(1)

For each
\beta\leq_{o}\alpha
, for each

the following holds: if
|\beta|\leq a_{i}
, then there exists a set
s
in
(a_{i},a_{i+1})
of consecutive elements of

such that
\alpha[s]=\beta
.

(2)

For each
\gamma\leq_{o}\beta\leq_{o}\alpha
, for each
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
the following holds: if
|\beta|,|\gamma|\leq a_{i}
, then there exists a set
s
in
(a_{i},a_{i+1})
of consecutive elements of
X
such that
\beta[s]=\gamma
.

Proof.

We prove both points using the properties of fundamental sequences.
The first point follows easily from Proposition
2.8
and the fact that
X
contains an
\alpha
-size set in
(a_{i},a_{i+1})
by definition of
S(\alpha,X)
.
As for the second point we take the sequence
t=\langle t_{0},\dots,t_{k-1}\rangle
witnessing the first point for
\gamma
and
\alpha
and, by Proposition
2.8
we find its subsequence
\langle t_{0},\dots,t_{j-1}\rangle
such that
\alpha[t_{0}]\cdots[t_{j-1}]=\beta
.
Then, the sequence
\langle t_{j},\dots,t_{k-1}\rangle

∎

We introduce some auxiliary functions that are used in the next proof.
Let us fix a total computable function
f\colon{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}}
such that for all
e\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
\beta\in\Lambda
,
f(e,\beta)
is the code of a Turing machine constructed from
e
and
\beta
that acts as
e
except treating oracle calls as follows: for any oracle call of the form
\langle\gamma,z\rangle
the machine
f(e,\beta)
checks whether
\gamma\leq_{o}\beta
.
If
\gamma\leq_{o}\beta
, then it simply passes the question to the oracle, if not it assumes that the oracle answer is “not”.
Thus,
f(e,\beta)
with oracle
X
acts like
e
with the oracle
{\{{\langle\gamma,z\rangle\in X\colon\gamma\leq_{o}\beta}\}}
.

The correspondence between the two machines
e
and
f_{\beta}(e)
can be given by computable translations
of their computations
, which we now describe.

The function
f_{\beta\rightarrow\alpha}
takes a (code of a) computation
C
of a machine
e
with oracle
X
and computes
C^{\prime}
, a (code of a) computation of
f_{\beta}(e)
with oracle
X
.
Concretely, it replaces each oracle query
\langle\gamma,z\rangle
with a subroutine which checks whether
\gamma\leq_{o}\beta
.
If
\gamma\leq_{o}\beta
, then
f_{\beta}(e)
passes the question to
X
, otherwise,
f_{\beta}(e)
assumes the answer is “not”.
It may happen that
\langle\gamma,z\rangle\in X
and it is not the case that
\gamma\leq_{o}\beta
.
In this case the computation coded by
f_{\beta\rightarrow\alpha}(C)
stops with no output as there would be inconsistency in behaviors of
e
and
f_{\beta}(e)
.

The function
f_{\alpha\rightarrow\beta}
computes from a (code of a) computation
C^{\prime}
of
f_{\beta}(e)
with oracle
X
a corresponding a (code of a) computation of
e
with oracle
\{\langle\gamma,z\rangle\in X\colon\gamma\leq_{o}\beta\}
.
It replaces the subroutine checking whether
\gamma\leq_{o}\beta
by a direct oracle call.

We need the following properties of
S(\alpha,X)
.
Note that we do not quantify over
X
in the inductive thesis.
Thus, we treat
X
as a second order parameter.
To simplify the notation in the rest of the section, let
n_{\alpha,\beta}
be the maximum between
|\beta|
,
|\alpha|
, the code of
f_{\beta}
, the code of
f_{\alpha\rightarrow\beta}
and the code of
f_{\beta\rightarrow\alpha}
.

Lemma 3.13

(
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+{\textrm{WO}}(\Lambda)
)
.

Let
\beta\leq_{o}\alpha\in\Lambda
, let
H\subseteq{\mathbb{N}}
be an infinite,
c_{\alpha+_{o}3}
-homogeneous set.
Then,
S(\alpha,H)^{>n_{\alpha,\beta}}
is
c_{\beta+_{o}3}
-homogeneous.

Proof.

Let
\beta\leq_{o}\alpha\in\Lambda
and

satisfy the assumptions of the lemma.
Let
\langle b_{0},b_{1},b_{2}\rangle{}^{\smallfrown}t
be a
(\beta+_{o}3)
-size sequence in
S(\alpha,H)^{>n_{\alpha,\beta}}
.
Let

be such that

{\{{e}\}}^{Y_{\beta}^{t}}(x){\downarrow}_{b_{2}},\mbox{ where }Y_{\beta}^{t}={\{{y\leq\max(t)\colon M_{\beta}(y,t)\mbox{ accepts}}\}}.

We need to show that
{\{{e}\}}^{Y_{\beta}^{t}}(x){\downarrow}_{b_{1}}
.

Let
a^{1}_{0},a^{2}_{0},a^{3}_{0}\in X
be such that

b_{0}<a^{1}_{0}<a^{2}_{0}<b_{1}.

The code of
f_{\beta}
is below
a^{2}_{0}
.
Now, let
a_{2}\in H
and let
s
be a sequence in
H
such that

b_{2}<a_{2}<\min s<\max s<\min t

and

\beta=\alpha[s].

The existence of
a_{2}
and

is guaranteed by Lemma
3.12
.
Finally, let

Y_{\alpha}^{s{}^{\smallfrown}t}={\{{y\leq\max t\colon M_{\alpha}(y,s{}^{\smallfrown}t)\mbox{ accepts}}\}}.

By Lemma
3.7
we have the following equality

(1)

Y_{\beta}^{t}={\{{\langle\gamma,z\rangle\in Y_{\alpha}^{s{}^{\smallfrown}t}\colon\gamma\leq_{o}\beta}\}}.

Thus,
e
with oracle
Y_{\beta}^{t}
acts as
f_{\beta}(e)
with oracle
Y_{\alpha}^{s{}^{\smallfrown}t}
, i.e.,

\forall x\in{\mathbb{N}}\,(\{e\}^{Y_{\beta}^{t}}(x)\simeq\{f_{\beta}(e)\}^{Y_{\alpha}^{s{}^{\smallfrown}t}}(x)).

The correspondence between the two machines coded by
e
and
f_{\beta}(e)
can be given by recursive translations,
f_{\beta\rightarrow\alpha}
and
f_{\alpha\rightarrow\beta}
, of their computations.

Recall that the computable function
f_{\beta\rightarrow\alpha}
takes a (code of a) computation of a machine
e
with an oracle
X
and outputs a (code of a) computation of
f_{\beta}(e)
with oracle
X
.
The main property of

is the following.

(2)

\begin{split}\lx@add@cssclass{ltx_eqn_lefteqn}$\displaystyle\mbox{If $C$ is a computation of $e$ with oracle $Y_{\beta}^{t}$}$\mbox{}\hfil&\\
&\mbox{\ \ \ \ \ \ then $f_{\beta\rightarrow\alpha}(C)$ is a computation of $f_{\beta}(e)$ with oracle $Y_{\alpha}^{s{}^{\smallfrown}t}$}.\end{split}

Property (

) follows easily from equality (
1
) which ensures that there is no inconsistency between these two computations.

It is crucial that the transformation
f_{\beta\rightarrow\alpha}
does not use the oracle since this allows us to apply Lemma
3.10
.
More precisely, by our choice of
n_{\alpha,\beta}
we know that the code of
f_{\beta\rightarrow\alpha}
is
<a^{1}_{0}
.

Now, let
C
be the computation of
e
on
x
with oracle
Y_{\beta}^{t}
.
Recall that we are reasoning under the assumption
\{e\}^{Y_{\beta}^{t}}(x){\downarrow}_{b_{2}}
, so that
C<b_{2}
.
Then,
C^{\prime}=f_{\beta\rightarrow\alpha}(C)
is the computation of
f_{\beta}(e)
on
x
with oracle
Y_{\alpha}^{s{}^{\smallfrown}t}
.
By Lemma
3.10
and the above observations on
f_{\beta\rightarrow\alpha}
, we have
C^{\prime}<a_{2}
.
Since the sequence
\langle a^{2}_{0},a^{3}_{0},a_{2}\rangle{}^{\smallfrown}s{}^{\smallfrown}t
is
(\alpha+_{o}3)
-size and in
H
, it has to be the case that

c_{\alpha+_{o}3}(\langle a^{2}_{0},a^{3}_{0},a_{2}\rangle{}^{\smallfrown}s{}^{\smallfrown}t)=1,

by Lemma
3.9
.
Since
f_{\beta}(e),x<a^{2}_{0}
this implies, in particular, that

\{f_{\beta}(e)\}^{Y_{\alpha}^{s{}^{\smallfrown}t}}(x){\downarrow}_{a_{2}}\Rightarrow\{f_{\beta}(e)\}^{Y_{\alpha}^{s{}^{\smallfrown}t}}(x){\downarrow}_{a^{3}_{0}}.

Therefore
C^{\prime}<a^{3}_{0}
.

It should be obvious that the other operation,
f_{\alpha\rightarrow\beta}
, which computes from a computation
C^{\prime}
of
f_{\beta}(e)
with oracle
X
a corresponding computation of
e
with oracle
{\{{(\gamma,z)\in X\colon\gamma\leq_{o}\beta}\}}
satisfies the following property:

(3)

\begin{split}\lx@add@cssclass{ltx_eqn_lefteqn}$\displaystyle\mbox{If $C^{\prime}$ is a computation of $f_{\beta}(e)$ with
oracle $Y_{\alpha}^{s{}^{\smallfrown}t}$}$\mbox{}\hfil&\\
&\mbox{\ \ \ \ \ \ then $f_{\alpha\rightarrow\beta}(C^{\prime})$ is a computation of $e$ with oracle $Y_{\beta}^{t}$.}\end{split}

Again by assumption we have that a code of
f_{\beta\to\alpha}
is below
n_{\alpha,\beta}
.
Then, since
C^{\prime}<a^{3}_{0}<b_{1}
, Lemma
3.10
ensures that

C=f_{\alpha\rightarrow\beta}(C^{\prime})<b_{1}
which shows that
{\{{e}\}}^{Y_{\beta}^{t}}(x){\downarrow}_{b_{1}}
and ends the proof.
∎

Now we want to prove that given an infinite
c_{\alpha+_{o}3}
-homogeneous set

, an ordinal
\beta\leq_{o}\alpha
and two
\beta
-size sequences

from

it does not matter which sequence we input to the machine

(Lemma

).
We need some preliminary lemmas.

Lemma 3.14

(
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+{\textrm{WO}}(\Lambda)
)
.

Let
\alpha\in\Lambda
and let
H\subseteq{\mathbb{N}}
be an infinite
c_{\alpha+_{o}3}
-homogeneous set, let
\beta\leq_{o}\alpha
, let
s
be a
\beta
-size sequence in
S^{2}(\alpha,H)^{>n_{\alpha,\beta}}
and let
s_{0},s_{1}
be in
S^{2}(\alpha,H)^{>n_{\alpha,\beta}}
such that
s_{0}<s_{1}<\min s
.
Then,

\forall e,x<s_{0}\left({\{{e}\}}^{Y_{\beta}^{s}}(x){\downarrow}_{\min s}\Longrightarrow{\{{e}\}}^{Y_{\beta}^{s}}(x){\downarrow}_{s_{1}}\right),

where
Y_{\beta}^{s}={\{{y<\max s\colon M_{\beta}(y,s)\mbox{ accepts}}\}}
.

Proof.

Let
s=\langle s_{2},\dots,s_{k}\rangle
for some

s_{2}<s_{3}<\dots<s_{k}
in
S^{2}(\alpha,H)^{>n_{\alpha,\beta}}
such that
s_{1}<s_{2}
.
Let
e,x<s_{0}
be such that

{\{{e}\}}^{Y_{\beta}^{s}}(x){\downarrow}_{s_{2}}.

We claim that
{\{{e}\}}^{Y_{\beta}^{s}}(x){\downarrow}_{s_{1}}
.
We consider two cases according to whether
\beta
is a successor or a limit ordinal.

Case 1:

\beta=\delta+1
.
Choose
s^{1}_{2}\in S(\alpha,H)
such that
s_{2}<s^{1}_{2}<s_{3}
.
This is possible by the properties of
S(\alpha,H)
.
The sequence
\langle s^{1}_{2}\rangle{}^{\smallfrown}s^{-}=\langle s^{1}_{2},s_{3},\dots,s_{k}\rangle
is
(\delta+1)
-size since
\beta=\delta+1
and
s
is
\beta
-size by hypothesis. By definition we have

\langle\gamma,z\rangle\in Y^{s}_{\delta+1}\Longleftrightarrow\langle\gamma,z\rangle<\max s\mbox{ and }M_{\delta+1}(\langle\gamma,z\rangle,s)\mbox{ accepts}.

Moreover, for each
\gamma
and

such that
\gamma\leq_{o}\beta
and
\langle\gamma,z\rangle<s_{2}=\min s
, by the definitions of
Y^{s}_{\delta+1}
and of
M_{\delta+1}
, we have that

\displaystyle\langle\gamma,z\rangle\in Y_{\delta+1}^{s}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow M_{\delta+1}(\langle\gamma,z\rangle,s)\mbox{ accepts}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow M_{\delta+1}(\langle\gamma,z\rangle,\langle s^{1}_{2}\rangle{}^{\smallfrown}s^{-})\mbox{ accepts.}

To show this we observe that for ordinals
\gamma<_{o}\beta
and for
\langle\gamma,z\rangle<s_{2}
we have

\langle\gamma,z\rangle\in Y_{\delta+1}^{s}\Longleftrightarrow M_{\delta}(\langle\gamma,z\rangle,s^{-})\mbox{ accepts}

by definition of
M_{\delta+1}
. Moreover,
\langle\delta+1,z\rangle\in Y_{\delta+1}^{s}
if and only if
{\{{z}\}}^{Y_{\delta}^{s^{-}}}(0){\downarrow}_{s_{3}}
, where, as usual, we set

{Y_{\delta}^{s^{-}}}={\{{\langle\gamma,e\rangle<\max(s^{-})\colon M_{\delta}(\langle\gamma,e\rangle,s^{-})\mbox{ accepts.}}\}}

Then note that the right hand side of the above equality does not depend on
s_{2}
or
s^{1}_{2}
.
It follows that if the pair
\langle e,x\rangle<s_{0}
is such that

{\{{e}\}}^{Y_{\delta}^{s^{-}}}(x){\downarrow}_{s_{2}}\mbox{ and not }{\{{e}\}}^{Y_{\delta}^{s^{-}}}(x){\downarrow}_{s_{1}}.

then
\langle e,x\rangle
witnesses that the
(\beta+_{o}3)
-size sequence
\langle s_{0},s_{1},s_{2},s^{1}_{2},s_{3},\dots,s_{k}\rangle
in
S(\alpha,H)
is colored
0
by
c_{\beta+_{o}3}
.
This is a contradiction since
n_{\alpha,\beta}<s_{0}
,
S(\alpha,H)^{>n_{\alpha,\beta}}
is an infinite homogeneous set for
c_{\beta+_{o}3}
by Lemma
3.13
and any such set above
n_{\alpha,\beta}
has to be colored
1
by Lemma
3.9
.

Case 2:

\beta
is a limit ordinal.
Recall that the set
Y^{s}_{\beta}
involved in the statement in this case is

Y^{s}_{\beta}=\{y<\max s\colon M_{\beta[s_{2}]}(y,s^{-})\mbox{ accepts}\}

by definition of
M_{\beta}
.

We choose
s^{1}_{2}\in S(\alpha,H)
and a (possibly empty) sequence
\tilde{s}
in
S(\alpha,H)
such that
s_{2}<s^{1}_{2}<\min\tilde{s}
and
\max\tilde{s}<s_{3}=\min(s^{-})
and

\beta[s^{1}_{2}][\tilde{s}]=\beta[s_{2}].

The last property ensures that the sequence
\langle s^{1}_{2}\rangle{}^{\smallfrown}\tilde{s}{}^{\smallfrown}s^{-}
is
\beta
-size.
Now, let

\widetilde{Y}={Y_{\beta}^{\langle s^{1}_{2}\rangle{}^{\smallfrown}\tilde{s}{}^{\smallfrown}s^{-}}}={\{{y<\max s\colon M_{\beta}(y,\langle s^{1}_{2}\rangle{}^{\smallfrown}\tilde{s}{}^{\smallfrown}s^{-})\mbox{ accepts}}\}}.

We need to show that below
s_{2}
the sets
\widetilde{Y}
and
Y_{\beta}^{s}
coincide.
If we succeed then we get that
{\{{e}\}}^{\widetilde{Y}}(x){\downarrow}_{s_{2}}
from the hypothesis that
\{e\}^{Y^{s}_{\beta}}(x){\downarrow}_{\min s}
.
Recall that by Lemma
3.13

S(\alpha,H)^{>n_{\alpha,\beta}}
is
c_{\beta+_{o}3}
-homogeneous and by Lemma
3.9
it is colored
1
.
It follows then that
{\{{e}\}}^{\widetilde{Y}}(x){\downarrow}_{s_{1}}
since otherwise the color of the
(\beta+_{o}3)
-size sequence
\langle s_{0},s_{1},s_{2},s^{1}_{2}\rangle{}^{\smallfrown}\tilde{s}{}^{\smallfrown}s^{-}
would be
0
under
c_{\beta+_{o}3}
.

To complete the proof let us take a pair
\langle\gamma,z\rangle<s_{2}
with
\gamma\leq_{o}\beta
.
Since
|\gamma|<s_{2}
by Remark
3.5
about the choice of the pairing function, it follows that if
\gamma<_{o}\beta
then
\gamma\leq_{o}\beta[s_{2}]
.
Since
\beta[s^{1}_{2}][\tilde{s}]=\beta[s_{2}]
, and
\langle s^{1}_{2}\rangle{}^{\smallfrown}\tilde{s}{}^{\smallfrown}s^{-}
is
\beta
-size, then there exists

such that

\gamma=\beta[s^{1}_{2}][\tilde{s}][s_{3}]\cdots[s_{j}]=\beta[s_{2}][s_{3}]\cdots[s_{j}].

Then

\begin{split}\langle\gamma,z\rangle\in\widetilde{Y}&\Longleftrightarrow M_{\beta}(\langle\gamma,z\rangle,\langle s^{1}_{2}\rangle{}^{\smallfrown}\tilde{s}{}^{\smallfrown}s^{-})\mbox{ accepts}\\
&\Longleftrightarrow M_{\gamma}(\langle\gamma,z\rangle,\langle s_{j+1},\dots,s_{k}\rangle)\mbox{ accepts}\\
&\Longleftrightarrow M_{\beta}(\langle\gamma,z\rangle,\langle s_{2}\dots,s_{k}\rangle)\mbox{ accepts}\\

The first and last of the above equivalences are by definition, the second and the third are by Lemma
3.7
.
This ends the proof.
∎

The next lemma guarantees a crucial indiscernibility property of
c_{\alpha+_{o}3}
-homogeneous sets.

Lemma 3.15

(
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+{\textrm{WO}}(\Lambda)
)
.

Let
H\subseteq{\mathbb{N}}
be an infinite
c_{\alpha+_{o}3}
-homogeneous set.

and for each choice of
\beta

\langle\gamma,e\rangle<\min s

Let us assume that we have
\beta\leq_{o}\alpha

and

such that

and

from

satisfying the hypotheses of the Lemma and such that they falsify the thesis.
First, let us observe that for all
\gamma\leq_{o}\beta
such that
|\gamma|<\min s
, there are
k^{\prime}\leq k
and
n^{\prime}\leq n
such that

\gamma=\beta[a_{0}]\cdots[a_{k^{\prime}-1}]=\beta[b_{0}]\cdots[b_{n^{\prime}-1}].

Indeed, if
|\gamma|<\min s<\min t
(by Remark
3.5
about the choice of the pairing function) and
\gamma\leq_{o}\beta
then, by Proposition
2.8
,
\gamma
has to occur as
\beta[a_{0}]\cdots[a_{k^{\prime}-1}]
and as
\beta[b_{0}]\cdots[b_{n^{\prime}-1}]

and
n^{\prime}\leq n
.

So, let
\gamma\leq_{o}\beta
and
k^{\prime}\leq k
and
n^{\prime}\leq n

\gamma=\beta[a_{0}]\cdots[a_{k^{\prime}-1}]=\beta[b_{0}]\cdots[b_{n^{\prime}-1}].

k^{\prime}

\gamma
and to the sequences

and

and

We also set
k_{\beta}=n_{\beta}=0

\beta[\emptyset]=\beta
.

Let us observe that for all
\gamma\leq_{o}\beta
such that
k_{\gamma}
and
n_{\gamma}
are defined and for all
\langle\delta,e\rangle<a_{k_{\gamma}}
, again by Lemma

we have the following equivalences.

M_{\beta}(\langle\delta,e\rangle,s)\mbox{ accepts}\Longleftrightarrow M_{\gamma}(\langle\delta,e\rangle,\langle a_{k_{\gamma}},\dots,a_{k}\rangle)\mbox{ accepts}

M_{\beta}(\langle\delta,e\rangle,t)\mbox{ accepts}\Longleftrightarrow M_{\gamma}(\langle\delta,e\rangle,\langle b_{n_{\gamma}},\dots,b_{n}\rangle)\mbox{ accepts}.

For all
\delta\leq_{o}\gamma

and

\gamma=\beta[a_{0}]\cdots[a_{k^{\prime}-1}]
and
|\delta|\leq a_{k^{\prime}}

\delta\leq_{o}\gamma

k^{\prime\prime}

k^{\prime}\leq k^{\prime\prime}\leq k

\delta=\beta[a_{0}]\cdots[a_{k^{\prime\prime}-1}]
.

|\delta|\leq a_{k^{\prime}}

2.8
.

By the above observations we thus rule out the case in which

\langle\delta,e\rangle

does not occur as
\beta[a_{0}]\cdots[a_{k^{\prime}-1}]

\gamma

\gamma\leq_{o}\beta

\displaystyle Y^{\gamma}_{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\{{\langle\delta,e\rangle<a_{k_{\gamma}}\colon M_{\gamma}(\langle\delta,e\rangle,\langle a_{k_{\gamma}},\dots,a_{k}\rangle)\mbox{ accepts}}\}},

\displaystyle Y^{\gamma}_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\{{\langle\delta,e\rangle<b_{n_{\gamma}}\colon M_{\gamma}(\langle\delta,e\rangle,\langle b_{n_{\gamma}},\dots,b_{n}\rangle)\mbox{ accepts}}\}}.

\Delta^{0}_{1}

\gamma\leq_{o}\beta

\Delta^{0}_{1}

Let

.
By the construction of the machine

where

and
Y^{\gamma_{1}}_{t}
are defined as above.

\delta=\gamma_{0}
.

, as

is well defined. Note that
\delta=\gamma_{0}

\langle\gamma_{0},e\rangle<a_{k_{0}}
.

We distinguish two cases and derive a contradiction in both of them.

Case 1:

We argue that each query of type

So, we proved that

Case 2:

{\{{e}\}}^{Y^{\gamma_{1}}_{s}}(0){\downarrow}_{a_{k_{0}+1}}

Then, we argue as in the previous case that

3.2.
Proof through well-ordering principles

The second lower bound proof is based on a characterization of the systems
\Pi^{0}_{\omega^{\alpha}}\mathsf{\text{-}CA}_{0}
in terms of well-ordering preservation principles, due to Marcone and Montalbàn
[
MM11
]
.
They showed that for each
\alpha
the system
\Pi^{0}_{\omega^{\alpha}}\mathsf{\text{-}CA}_{0}
is equivalent to the statement “for each linear order
\mathcal{X}
if
\mathcal{X}
is a well-ordering then
\varphi_{\alpha}(\mathcal{X})
is a well-ordering”, where
\varphi_{\alpha}(\mathcal{X})
is an operator, depending on
\alpha
, that maps linear orders to linear orders.
We call the previous statement the
well-ordering principle for

\varphi_{\alpha}(\mathcal{X})
and denote its standard formalization in second-order arithmetic by
\mathsf{WOP}(\mathcal{X}\to\varphi_{\alpha}(\mathcal{X}))
.
We now describe the operator
\varphi_{\alpha}(\mathcal{X})
.
The general definition can be found in
[
MM11
]
.
Here we focus on the specific case we need.
For the rest of the section, let
\mathcal{X}
be a linear ordering and let
\leq_{\mathcal{X}}
be its order relation.

Definition 3.24
.

We define
\varphi_{\alpha}(\mathcal{X})
to be the set of formal terms defined as follows:

•

0
and
\varphi_{\alpha}(x)
for
x\in\mathcal{X}
belong to
\varphi_{\alpha}(\mathcal{X})
and are called constants,

•

if
\mathtt{t}_{1},\mathtt{t}_{2}\in\varphi_{\alpha}(\mathcal{X})
then
\mathtt{t}_{1}+\mathtt{t}_{2}\in\varphi_{\alpha}(\mathcal{X})
,

•

if
\mathtt{t}\in\varphi_{\alpha}(\mathcal{X})
and
\delta<\alpha
then
\varphi_{\delta}(\mathtt{t})\in\varphi_{\alpha}(\mathcal{X})
.

We denote terms by letters like
\mathtt{s}
,
\mathtt{t}
and
\mathtt{u}
.

The normal form of a term and the order relation
\leq_{\varphi_{\alpha}(\mathcal{X})}
on
\varphi_{\alpha}(\mathcal{X})
are defined simultaneously.
We say that a term
\mathtt{t}=\mathtt{t}_{0}+\ldots+\mathtt{t}_{n}
is in

if either
\mathtt{t}=0
or
\mathtt{t}_{0}\geq\ldots\geq\mathtt{t}_{n}
and each
t_{i}
is either a constant or of the form
\varphi_{\delta}(\mathtt{s}_{i})
for
\delta<\alpha
and
\mathtt{s}_{i}
a term in normal form with
\mathtt{s}_{i}\neq\varphi_{\delta^{\prime}}(\mathtt{s}_{i}^{\prime})
for
\delta^{\prime}>\delta
.
To write a term in normal form we apply the following rules:

•

+
is associative and
0
is the neutral element,

•

if
\varphi_{\delta^{\prime}}(\mathtt{s})<_{\varphi_{\alpha}(\mathcal{X})}\varphi_{\delta}(\mathtt{u})
then
\varphi_{\delta^{\prime}}(\mathtt{s})+\varphi_{\delta}(\mathtt{u})=\varphi_{\delta}(\mathtt{u})
,

•

if
\delta^{\prime}>\delta
then
\varphi_{\delta}(\varphi_{\delta^{\prime}}(\mathtt{s}))=\varphi_{\delta^{\prime}}(\mathtt{s})
,

•

if
\delta<\alpha
then
\varphi_{\delta}(\varphi_{\alpha}(\mathtt{s}))=\varphi_{\alpha}(\mathtt{s})
.

Given
\mathtt{t}=\mathtt{t}_{0}+\ldots+\mathtt{t}_{n}
and
\mathtt{s}=\mathtt{s}_{0}+\ldots+\mathtt{s}_{m}
written in normal form, we say that
\mathtt{t}\leq\mathtt{s}
if and only if one of the following applies:

•

\mathtt{t}=0
,

•

\mathtt{t}=\varphi_{\alpha}(x)
and for some
y\geq_{\mathcal{X}}x
,
\varphi_{\alpha}(y)
occurs in
\mathtt{s}
,

•

\mathtt{t}=\varphi_{\delta}(\mathtt{t}^{\prime})
,
\mathtt{s}_{0}=\varphi_{\delta^{\prime}}(\mathtt{s}^{\prime})
and

\begin{cases}\delta<\delta^{\prime}\text{ and }\mathtt{t}^{\prime}\leq_{\varphi_{\alpha}(\mathcal{X})}\mathtt{s}_{0}\text{ or }\\
\delta=\delta^{\prime}\text{ and }\mathtt{t}^{\prime}\leq_{\varphi_{\alpha}(\mathcal{X})}\mathtt{s}^{\prime}\text{ or }\\
\delta>\delta^{\prime}\text{ and }\mathtt{t}\leq_{\varphi_{\alpha}(\mathcal{X})}\mathtt{s}^{\prime},\end{cases}

•

n>0
and
\mathtt{t}_{0}<_{\varphi_{\alpha}(\mathcal{X})}\mathtt{s}_{0}
,

•

n>0
,
\mathtt{t}_{0}=\mathtt{s}_{0}
,
m>0
and
\mathtt{t}_{1}+\ldots+\mathtt{t}_{n}\leq_{\varphi_{\alpha}(\mathcal{X})}\mathtt{s}_{1}+\ldots\mathtt{s}_{m}
.

For
\mathtt{s},\mathtt{t}\in\varphi_{\alpha}(\mathcal{X})
we may write
\mathtt{t}<\mathtt{s}
for
\mathtt{t}<_{\varphi_{\alpha}(\mathcal{X})}\mathtt{s}
for ease of readability.

The following result is crucial for our lower bound proof of
\mathsf{RT}^{!\omega^{\alpha}}

Theorem 3.25
.

[

The base case

for some

.
Moreover

are computable from

.
Since

\mathsf{RT}^{1}_{k}
which admits computable solutions, we get that

computes an infinite homogeneous set

for

.
It is immediate to verify that

is homogeneous for

∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\alpha<\Gamma_{0}
,

be an instance of
\mathsf{RT}^{!\alpha}_{k}

Proof.

The proofs are by simultaneous induction.
For both statements we consider separately the cases
\alpha
decomposable and
\alpha
indecomposable.

Notice that for

was proved in

]
, while for

it was proved in
[

]
.

but as we noticed at the end of Subsection

, the statements for the same

.
For
\mathsf{RT}^{!1\uplus\omega}_{k}
it was proved in Lemma
4.1
above.
Therefore for the rest of the proof we fix

and suppose that both statements are true for every

.

Case 1:
Suppose that
\alpha
is indecomposable and let

.
We perform the construction of a strictly increasing sequence

of elements of

of infinite subsets of

of Lemma

.

(1)

;

(2)

;

(3)

is computable from

;

for each

.

.

and

.

and consider the

\displaystyle[H_{i-1}\setminus\{h_{i}\}]^{!\alpha[h_{i}]}\to k

\displaystyle t\mapsto c(\langle h_{i}\rangle{}^{\smallfrown}t)

Then since

for

.
By the construction we know that

is computable in

.
Therefore the sequences up to

satisfy the requirements.

Let

and let

be defined as

for some

: by definition of
H_{i}
the coloring

is well defined and does not depend on the choice of

.

for

,

uniformly computes the sequences
(h_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
and
(H_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
.
Since

is an instance of
\mathsf{RT}^{1}_{k}
which admits computable solutions, we get that
{\textrm{TJ}}(c\oplus X,\alpha)
(and so
{\textrm{TJ}}(c\oplus X,\alpha+1)
) computes an infinite homogeneous set
H
for

.
It is immediate to verify that

is homogeneous for
c
too.

Case 2:
Suppose that

is indecomposable and let

.

of elements of

with

), while property 4 is:

for each

.

The steps of the construction are the same, with the difference that the

computable coloring we consider is

\displaystyle f_{h_{i}}\colon

\displaystyle[H_{i-1}\setminus\{h_{i}\}]^{!1\uplus\alpha[h_{i}]}\to k

\displaystyle t\mapsto d(\langle h_{i}\rangle{}^{\smallfrown}t)

Case 3:
Suppose that
\alpha
is decomposable, let
\alpha^{\prime}<\alpha
be such that
\alpha=\operatorname{lead}(\alpha)+\alpha^{\prime}
and let

.
Since
\alpha
is decomposable then
\alpha^{\prime}>0
and by Definition
2.2
an
\alpha
-size set consists of an
\alpha^{\prime}
-size set followed by a
\operatorname{lead}(\alpha)
-size set.
The strategy is similar to the previous cases, but it is slightly more delicate and requires additional care, so we provide the full details.
Fix a recursive enumeration
\{s_{i}:i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
of all the elements of
[X]^{!1\uplus\alpha^{\prime}}
such that for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, if
i
is the least with

then each
(1\uplus\alpha^{\prime})
-size set of numbers strictly smaller than
n
is enumerated before
s_{i}
.
We aim to define a sequence
(h_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of
(1\uplus\alpha^{\prime})
-size subsets of
X
and a sequence
(H_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of infinite subsets of
X
with the following properties:

(1)

comes before
h_{i+1}
in the fixed enumeration
\{s_{i}:i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
;

(2)

H_{i+1}\subset H_{i}
;

(3)

h_{i}<\min(H_{i})
;

(4)

h_{i}\subset\bigcup_{j<i}h_{j}\cup H_{i-1}
;

(5)

is computable from
{\textrm{TJ}}(c\oplus X,\beta)
for some
\beta<\operatorname{lead}(\alpha)
;

(6)

for each
s,t\in[H_{i}]^{!\operatorname{lead}(\alpha)[\max h_{i}]}

.

We make an observation about property 4.
At each stage

, if

we say that

is an

.
Being eligible means that

is a suitable candidate to be chosen as

at stage
i
.
Notice that if

then for each

,

will be eligible at stage
n
.

Initialize the construction by leaving
h_{-1}
undefined and setting
H_{-1}=X
.
Suppose we have defined the sequences up to
h_{i-1}
and
H_{i-1}
.
Let
h_{i}
be the least eligible set at stage

in the enumeration
\{s_{i}:i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
which is different from
h_{j}
for each

.
Notice that

was eligible also at stage

for

and so it must occur after each
h_{j}
for

in the enumeration

, as otherwise it would have been chosen at a previous stage.
Consider the

computable coloring

\displaystyle f_{h_{i}}\colon

\displaystyle[H_{i-1}\setminus\{0,\ldots,\max h_{i}\}]^{!\operatorname{lead}(\alpha)[\max h_{i}]}\to k

\displaystyle t\mapsto c(h_{i}{}^{\smallfrown}t)

Then by inductive hypothesis
{\textrm{TJ}}(f_{h_{i}}\oplus H_{i-1},\operatorname{lead}(\alpha)[\max h_{i}]+1)
computes an infinite homogeneous set
H_{i}
for
f_{h_{i}}
.
By the construction we know that

is computable in
{\textrm{TJ}}(c\oplus X,\beta)
and since
\operatorname{lead}(\alpha)
is indecomposable we also know that
\beta<\operatorname{lead}(\alpha)
.
Therefore the sequences up to
i
satisfy the requirements.

Let
Z=\bigcup_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}h_{i}
.
We claim that for each

such that

.
Let
u
be a
(1\uplus\alpha^{\prime})
-size subset of

and say

in the fixed enumeration.
Then for some

.
Since

-size sets, it will be chosen as one of the

Let

Notice that since

for

is homogeneous for

too.

Corollary 4.3

(
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
)
.

For each
\alpha<\Gamma_{0}
and each
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
\Pi^{0}_{\operatorname{lead}(\alpha)}\mathsf{\text{-}CA}_{0}\vdash\mathsf{RT}^{!\alpha}_{k}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.2
we know that for each instance
c\colon[X]^{!\alpha}\to k
of
\mathsf{RT}^{!\alpha}_{k}
,
{\textrm{TJ}}(c\oplus X,\alpha+1)
computes an infinite homogeneous set.
Hence
\Pi^{0}_{\alpha+1}\mathsf{\text{-}CA}_{0}\vdash\mathsf{RT}^{!\alpha}_{k}
.
By Lemma
2.9

\Pi^{0}_{\alpha+1}\mathsf{\text{-}CA}_{0}\leftrightarrow\Pi^{0}_{\operatorname{lead}(\alpha)}\mathsf{\text{-}CA}_{0}
.
∎

5.
Conclusions and perspectives

We have characterized the proof-theoretic strength of the family
\mathsf{RT}^{!\alpha}_{k}
of Ramsey-like theorems for
\alpha
-size sets for
\alpha<\Gamma_{0}
.
In particular we showed that, over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
, each principle
\mathsf{RT}^{!\alpha}_{k}
is equivalent to the system
\Pi^{0}_{\operatorname{lead}(\alpha)}\mathsf{\text{-}CA}_{0}
, providing a precise calibration of these combinatorial statements in reverse mathematics.
This extends the analysis of
\mathsf{RT}^{!\omega}_{2}
by Carlucci and Zdanowski
[
CZ14
]
to all countable ordinals
\alpha<\Gamma_{0}
, establishing a clear hierarchy of logical strength corresponding to transfinite Turing jumps.

Our work highlights the interplay between largeness notions and the computational content of combinatorial principles.
In particular, the
\alpha
-size sets capture the combinatorial strength needed to ensure homogeneous sets of high computational complexity since they allow us to code the
\alpha
-th Turing jump of a set.
Our reverse mathematics perspective complements previous computability-theoretic analyses of similar Ramsey-like theorems
[
Clo84
]
, providing formal equivalences within subsystems of second-order arithmetic.

From the perspective of proof strategies, our lower bound proof through reduction of a well-ordering principle extends the approach from
[
CZ12
,
CMZ24
]
.
It would be interesting to investigate into further extensions of such an approach to stronger Ramsey-like theorems and axiomatic systems.

The recent
[
CGLP25
]
analyzes the reverse mathematics and the computability-theoretic strength of the generalization to
\omega
-size sets of three weak Ramsey-like principles (the Free set, Thin set and Rainbow Ramsey theorems).
Some results on the generalization of these principles to Nash-Williams barriers, including the
\alpha
-size sets are in
[
CG26
]
. A natural question is to inquire into the reverse mathematics of extensions of these principles to
\alpha
-size sets for
\alpha>\omega
.
