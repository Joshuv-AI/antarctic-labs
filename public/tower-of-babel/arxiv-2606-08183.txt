Abstract
. We establish a Remez inequality for functions in quasianalytic Denjoy–Carleman classes 𝒞 M \mathcal{C}_{M} on a large family of fat compact sets K ⊆ ℝ n K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n} with tame geometry, including all fat compact sets definable in the o-minimal expansion of ℝ \mathbb{R} by restricted 𝒞 M \mathcal{C}_{M} functions. The inequality generalizes the classical Remez inequality for polynomials and its quasianalytic versions on convex bodies, replacing the polynomial degree by the Bang degree, an integer associated with the weight M M and the size of the function. The constants depend explicitly on the Bang degree and the geometry of K K . We derive a range of quantitative consequences: estimates for the volume of sublevel sets, comparison of L p L^{p} -norms, effective inequalities of Łojasiewicz, Harnack, and Markov type with explicit constants, and decay estimates for oscillatory integrals.

Contents

1.
Introduction

The Remez inequality is a fundamental tool in approximation theory.
The classical result of Remez
[
Rem36
]
states that for a Lebesgue measurable subset
E
of the unit interval
[0,1]
with
Lebesgue measure
|E|>0

we have

\|p\|_{[0,1]}\leq T_{d}\Big(\frac{2-|E|}{|E|}\Big)\,\|p\|_{E},

for all polynomials
p\in\mathbb{R}[x]
of degree at most
d
,
where
T_{d}(x)=\cos(d\arccos x)
is the
d
-th Chebyshev polynomial.

In higher dimensions, there is the generalization due to Yu. Brudnyi and Ganzburg
[
BG73
]

for convex bodies
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
: for Lebesgue measurable subsets
E\subseteq K
with
|E|>0

we have the sharp inequality

(1.1)

\|p\|_{K}\leq T_{d}\Big(\frac{|K|^{1/n}+(|K|-|E|)^{1/n}}{|K|^{1/n}-(|K|-|E|)^{1/n}}\Big)\,\|p\|_{E},

for all polynomials
p\in\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
of degree at most
d
.

Beyond convex sets, (
1.1
) has been generalized by Pierzchała
[
Pie15
]

to a large family of compact sets
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
admitting cusps,
including all compact fat subanalytic sets: there exist
\nu>0
and
m\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
, depending only on
K
,
such that

(1.2)

\|p\|_{K}\leq T_{md}\Big(\frac{|K|^{\nu}+(|K|-|E|)^{\nu}}{|K|^{\nu}-(|K|-|E|)^{\nu}}\Big)\,\|p\|_{E},

for all Lebesgue measurable
E\subseteq K
with
|E|>0
and all
p\in\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
of degree at most
d
.
(Recall that a compact set
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called
fat
if
K=\overline{\operatorname{int}(K)}
.)

The Remez inequality has been extended to various classes of functions
(e.g. analytic
[
Bru99b
]
, plurisubharmonic
[
Bru99a
]
, and holomorphic
[
Bru08
]
),
generally, in the univariate or convex multivariate case. Still in the convex setting,

[
Yom11
]
showed that, in the Remez inequality for polynomials, the Lebesgue measure can be replaced by a certain geometric invariant
which may be nonzero for discrete or even finite sets
E
; see also
[
BY15
]
.

In this paper, we focus on quasianalytic Denjoy–Carleman classes
\mathcal{C}_{M}
, that is,
classes of smooth functions whose sequence of derivatives is bounded by a given positive sequence
M=(M_{j})_{j\geq 0}

with suitable properties.

Nazarov, Sodin, and Volberg
[
NSV04
]
proved a univariate Remez inequality for functions
f

of class
\mathcal{C}_{M}
, where the so-called
Bang degree
replaces the polynomial degree.
The Bang degree is a nonnegative integer which can be computed from the sequence
M

and which depends on the size of
f
as a parameter. This number was introduced by Bang
[
Ban53
]

and named Bang degree by
[
NSV04
]
.
A multivariate Remez inequality for functions
f
of class
\mathcal{C}_{M}
on convex bodies
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}

was obtained in
[
Rai23
]
, where also effective bounds on the zero sets were proved.

The main goal of this paper is to establish a Remez inequality for functions
f
of class
\mathcal{C}_{M}
on
a large family of compact sets
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
with “tame geometry”.
In addition, we develop several analytic and geometric consequences of this Remez inequality.
The family of admissible sets
K
(the same as the one used by
[
Pie15
]
)
includes all fat compact sets that are definable in
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{C}_{M}}
, the o-minimal
expansion of the real ordered field by all restricted functions of class
\mathcal{C}_{M}
(assuming that
M
satisfies some
standard regularity conditions). In particular, all fat compact subanalytic sets are admissible.
The constants that appear in the Remez inequality depend only on the Bang degree associated with
M
and
on the geometry of
K
and can be computed explicitly.
Similarly, all the consequences,
including sublevel set estimates, reverse Hölder inequalities,
inequalities of Łojasiewicz, Harnack, and Markov type, and decay estimates for oscillatory integrals,
have a quantitative character.
The Bang degree plays the role of an effective complexity parameter, analogous to the polynomial degree
in semialgebraic geometry.

In the following, we present our main results in more detail.

1.1.
Quasianalytic Remez inequalities on tame sets

Let
\mu^{|\infty}=(\mu_{j})_{j\geq 0}
be a positive infinite sequence satisfying
0<\mu_{1}\leq\mu_{2}\leq\cdots
.
Let us also assume here in the introduction (for simplicity of exposition) that
\mu_{j}\geq j
for
j\geq 1
.
Given
M_{0}>0
, we then set

M_{j}:=M_{0}\prod_{i=1}^{j}\mu_{i},\quad j\geq 1,

and get a sequence
M=(M_{j})_{j\geq 0}
, which conversely determines the pair
(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0})

in a unique way.

For open
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the
Denjoy–Carleman class

\mathcal{C}_{M}(U)
consists of all
f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(U)

such that for each compact
K\subseteq U
there exist constants
C,\rho>0
such that

(1.3)

|\partial^{\alpha}f(x)|\leq C\rho^{|\alpha|}M_{|\alpha|},\quad\text{ for all }x\in K,\,\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}.

For instance,
\mathcal{C}_{M}(U)
coincides with space of real analytic functions on
U
, if
\mu_{j}=j
.
By the Denjoy–Carleman theorem, the class
\mathcal{C}_{M}
is
quasianalytic
, i.e.,
the Taylor series homomorphism
\mathcal{C}_{M}(U)\to\mathbb{R}\llbracket x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}\rrbracket
at any point of
U
is injective,
provided that
U
is connected,
if and only if

(1.4)

\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{1}{\mu_{j}}=\infty.

See
[
Rai22
]
and
[
Thi08
]
for background on Denjoy–Carleman classes.
Throughout the introduction, we make the quasianalyticity assumption (
1.4
);
this can be relaxed somewhat as shall be seen later.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be nonempty and compact.
It will be convenient to work with the following variant of condition (
1.3
):
a function
f:K\to\mathbb{R}

is called
(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0})
-smooth
if
f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(K)
and

(1.5)

\|f\|_{j,K}:=\sup_{x\in K}\sum_{|\alpha|=j}\frac{j!}{\alpha!}|\partial^{\alpha}f(x)|\leq M_{j},\quad j\geq 0.

(This is no restriction relative to (
1.3
) because the constants
C,\rho
can be absorbed by
M
,
by redefining
(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0})
.)

Given
\mu^{|\infty}
and
b>0
, we define the
Bang degree

(1.6)

\mathbf{D}_{\mu^{|\infty}}(b):=\sup\Big\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\sum_{j=j_{0}(b)+1}^{n}\frac{1}{\mu_{j}}<e\Big\},

where
j_{0}(b):=\lceil\log(b^{-1})\rceil_{\mathbb{N}}
is the smallest nonnegative integer
j
with
j\geq\log(b^{-1})
.
Thanks to (
1.4
),
\mathbf{D}_{\mu^{|\infty}}(b)
is a nonnegative integer.

We will define a collection
\mathscr{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
of fat compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
admissible for the
Remez inequality (see
Section

4.4
).
Informally, the sets in
\mathscr{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
admit a uniformly controlled polynomial parametrization.
For the purposes of this introduction, it suffices to note that

\mathscr{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
includes all compact UPC-sets,
in particular, convex bodies, fat compact sets with Hölder boundary, and all fat compact sets that are
subanalytic or definable in
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{C}_{M}}
, where
M=(M_{j})_{j\geq 0}
is any quasianalytic weight
sequence fulfilling some standard regularity conditions (see
Example

3.4
).

With each
K\in\mathscr{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
we will associate constants

(1.7)

a_{K}>0\quad\text{ and }\quad 0<\nu_{K}\leq 1,

which depend only on the geometry of
K
(see
Section

4.5
).
For instance, if
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a convex body
then
a_{K}=\operatorname{diam}(K)
and
\nu_{K}=1/n
.

The following quasianalytic Remez inequality is our main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mu^{|\infty}=(\mu_{j})_{j\geq 1}
and
M_{0}>0
be as above.
Let
L\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body
and
K\in\mathscr{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
K\subseteq L
.
Let
b_{0}\in(0,M_{0})
.
Then for each
(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0})
-smooth function
f:L\to\mathbb{R}
with
\|f\|_{K}\geq b_{0}
and
each Lebesgue measurable set
E\subseteq K
with
|E|>0
we have

\|f\|_{K}\leq\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}\,\Big(\frac{|K|^{\nu_{K}}}{|K|^{\nu_{K}}-(|K|-|E|)^{\nu_{K}}}\Big)^{\mathbf{N}}\,\|f\|_{E},

where

\mathbf{N}=2\,\mathbf{D}_{a_{K}\,\mu^{|\infty}}(\tfrac{b_{0}}{M_{0}})

and
\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}
is a constant depending only on
\mathbf{N}
and
\mu^{|\infty}
.

Theorem

1.1
can be interpreted as a
“quantitative propagation-of-smallness principle” for
(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0})
-smooth functions
on general tame compact sets beyond the convex setting.

The constant
\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}
is explicitly given in
Definition

2.13
. For standard examples of
\mu^{|\infty}
it
is exponential in
\mathbb{N}
.

The main defining property of the sets
K
in
\mathscr{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is that they can be covered by (possibly infinite)
families of images of compact convex or star-shaped subsets of the unit cube, with positive measure uniformly bounded below,
under polynomial maps
\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
of uniformly bounded degree and uniformly controlled Jacobian.
While
\nu_{K}
can be computed (with some effort) from these data, the constant
a_{K}
is explicitly given as a
function of the diameter
\operatorname{diam}(K)
and the maximal degree
d
of the polynomial maps; see (
4.6
).
It follows that
a_{K}
grows linearly in
\operatorname{diam}(K)
and asymptotically quadratically in
d
.

The proof of
Theorem

1.1
is based on a fundamental technical result of
[
Pie15
]

on sets in
\mathscr{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, combined with the univariate Remez inequality for
(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0})
-smooth functions
as well as its multivariate version on convex bodies.
In fact, a more general version of
Theorem

1.1
is stated in
Theorem

4.13
which will be proved in
Sections

5
and
6
.
In particular,
Theorem

4.13
provides a Remez inequality for vector-valued
maps
f=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}):L\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

Remark 1.2
.

Contrary to the Remez inequality for polynomials ((
1.1
) and (
1.2
)),
the vector-valued Remez inequality of
Theorem

4.13
does not simply follow from its single-valued version
applied component-wise. The reason is that we may have
\|f\|_{K}\geq b_{0}>0
while
\|f_{i}\|_{K}
is much smaller than
b_{0}
for some
i
.
A component-wise treatment would cause
\mathbf{N}
to be much larger than actually needed.

Remark 1.3
.

The requirement that
\|f\|_{K}
is bounded below by a positive number
b_{0}
, on which the degree
\mathbf{N}
depends, is vital,
as we will see in
Example

1.4
. Setting
b_{0}=\|f\|_{K}
we obtain a Remez inequality,
where the Bang degree

(1.8)

\mathbf{N}_{f,K}=2\,\mathbf{D}_{a_{K}\,\mu^{|\infty}}(\tfrac{\|f\|_{K}}{M_{0}})

explicitly depends on
f|_{K}
,
similarly to Remez’s inequality for polynomials.

The Bang degree is however not a generalization of the polynomial degree and the
analogy between the two is limited. For instance,
the Bang degree
\mathbf{N}_{f,K}
of a
(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0})
-smooth function
f:L\to\mathbb{R}

tends to infinity
as we shrink
K
to a zero of
f
, since then
\|f\|_{K}\to 0
. (The accompaning decreasing effect on
a_{K}
fails
to prevent this; see
Propositions

A.1
,
A.3
and
A.4
.)

Example 1.4
.

Consider the sequence of polynomials
p_{k}(x):=\frac{x^{k}}{k!}
, for
k\geq 1
, on
\mathbb{R}
.
Each
f_{k}:=p_{k}|_{[0,1]}
is
(\mu^{|\infty},1)
-smooth for
\mu_{j}\equiv 1
, or
\mu_{j}=j
(if one insists on
the condition
\mu_{j}\geq j
), or other appropriate choices of
\mu^{|\infty}
.
On the other hand,

(1.9)

\|f_{k}\|_{[0,1]}=\frac{1}{k!}\quad\text{ and }\quad\|f_{k}\|_{[0,\frac{1}{2}]}=\frac{1}{2^{k}k!}.

Thus a Remez inequality for
f_{k}
with a constant that is independent of the size of the functions
f_{k}
, i.e.,
independent of
k
, cannot hold.

The set of functions for which
Theorem

1.1
applies (with
specified
\mu^{\infty}
,
M_{0}
, and
b_{0}
)
can be infinite dimensional.

Remark 1.5
.

Setting
K=B(x,r)
and
b_{0}=\|f\|_{B(x,r)}
in
Theorem

1.1
we get a bound for the

doubling exponent
of
f
on the ball
B(x,r)
:

\displaystyle\log\frac{\|f\|_{B(x,r)}}{\|f\|_{B(x,\tfrac{r}{2})}}\leq(n\log 2+\log n)\,\mathbf{N}_{f}(x,r)+\log\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}_{f}(x,r)},

where

\mathbf{N}_{f}(x,r)=2\,\mathbf{D}_{2r\,\mu^{|\infty}}(\tfrac{\|f\|_{B(x,r)}}{M_{0}}).

1.2.
Finite determinacy

So far we only considered
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-functions with derivatives of all orders controlled by
the sequence
M=(M_{j})
and we assumed that the quasianalyticity assumption (
1.4
) is
satisfied.

Actually, the result in
Theorem

1.1
and many of its consequences
depend only on the derivatives up to some finite order
and remain true even for functions that are only differentiable up to said finite order.
Also the quasianalyticity requirement can often be relaxed.

We will make this precise below, in particular, we will specify the finite order and use
customized terminology; see
Section

2
.

Next we discuss various analytic and geometric consequences of
Theorem

1.1
and
retain its setting for simplicity.

1.3.
Volume of sublevel sets and applications

In the setup of
Theorem

1.1
, we obtain the following corollaries; see
Section

7
,
where more general versions are proved.

Corollary 1.6

(Volume of sublevel sets)
.

The sublevel set
K_{t}:=\{x\in K:|f(x)|\leq t\}
of
f|_{K}
satisfies

(1.10)

|K_{t}|\leq\frac{|K|}{\nu_{K}}\,\Big(\frac{\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}\,t}{\|f\|_{K}}\Big)^{1/\mathbf{N}},\quad t>0.

This simple consequence is very useful. For instance, it implies the following
inequality of Nikolskii type which reverses Hölder’s inequality.

Corollary 1.7

(Comparison of
L^{p}
-norms)
.

For all
0<q<p\leq\infty
,

(1.11)

\|f\|_{L^{p}(K)}\leq\Big(\frac{\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}}{\nu_{K}^{\mathbf{N}}}\Big)^{1-\frac{q}{p}}\Big(\frac{q\mathbf{N}+1}{|K|}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{q}(K)}.

We conclude integrability of
|f|^{-\alpha}
in the range
0<\alpha<1/\mathbf{N}
.

Corollary 1.8

(Integrability of
|f|^{-\alpha}
)
.

The integral
\int_{K}|f(x)|^{-\alpha}\,dx
converges, provided that
0<\alpha<1/\mathbf{N}
.

We get a bound for the mean oscillation of
\log|f|
over Lebesgue measurable sets.

Corollary 1.9

(Mean oscillation of
\log|f|
)
.

For each Lebesgue measurable set
E\subseteq K
with
|E|>0
, the mean oscillation of
\log|f|
over
E

satisfies

(1.12)

\operatorname{mo}_{E}(\log|f|)\leq 2\log\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}+2\mathbf{N}\,\Big(1+\log\Big(\frac{|K|}{\nu_{K}\,|E|}\Big)\Big).

This bound is not sufficient for concluding that
\log|f|
is BMO, in contrast to polynomials or analytic functions;
see
[
Bru99b
]
.
What is missing is control near the zero set of
f
, since the Bang degree depends strongly on the size of the function;
see
Remark

1.3
and
Remark

7.10
.

1.4.
Inequalities of Łojasiewicz type

Functions definable in polynomially bounded o-minimal structures satisfy inequalities of Łojasiewicz type
(cf.
[
vdDM96
, 4.14]
),
but the constants are typically unspecified.
In
Section

8
, we establish several inequalities of Łojasiewicz type with
explicit

constants, depending on the Bang degree and the geometry of
K
.

Following
[
Pie22
, Definition 1.16]
, we say that
K\in\mathscr{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is

(c_{K},\theta_{K})
-thick
if there exist constants
c_{K},\theta_{K}>0
such that

(1.13)

|K\cap B(x,r)|\geq c_{K}\,r^{\theta_{K}},

for all
x\in K
and
0<r\leq 1
.
Every compact fat nonempty set
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
that is definable in a polynomially bounded o-minimal structure
is
(c_{K},\theta_{K})
-thick for some
c_{K},\theta_{K}>0
, as a consequence of the mentioned Łojasiewicz inequalities
(see
[
Pie22
, Theorem 1.18]
).
Each convex body
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}

is
(c_{K},\theta_{K})
-thick with
\theta_{K}=n
.

Corollary 1.10
.

In the setup of
Theorem

1.1
,
assume additionally that
K
is
(c_{K},\theta_{K})
-thick.
For any function
g:K\to[-1,1]
,
we have

(1.14)

\|f\|_{B(x,\frac{|g(x)|}{2})\cap K}\geq\frac{\|f\|_{K}}{\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}}\,\Big(\frac{c_{K}\,\nu_{K}}{2^{\theta_{K}}\,|K|}\Big)^{\mathbf{N}}\,|g(x)|^{\theta_{K}\mathbf{N}},

for all
x\in K
.

Note that we do not require
f|_{K}^{-1}(0)\subseteq g^{-1}(0)
. See
Theorem

8.3
for a more general version.

In particular, this applies to the distance to the zero set of
f
,

(1.15)

g(x):=\min\{\operatorname{dist}(x,f|_{K}^{-1}(0)),1\}.

Taking
g\equiv r
for
r\in(0,1)
, we get a bound on the order of vanishing.

Corollary 1.11
.

The order of vanishing of
f
at each
x\in K
is bounded by
\theta_{K}\mathbf{N}
.

We deduce several inequalities for the gradient with completely explicit constants.
For instance, the following corollary is a special case of
Theorem

8.9
.

Corollary 1.12
.

Assume that
(\operatorname{grad}f|_{K})^{-1}(0)\subseteq f|_{K}^{-1}(0)
and
M_{0}\geq 1
.
Then,

(1.16)

|\operatorname{grad}f(x)|\geq\frac{1}{2\sqrt{M_{2}}}\Big(\frac{\|f\|_{K}}{2\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\theta_{K}\mathbf{N}}}\,\Big(\frac{c_{K}\,\nu_{K}}{|K|}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\theta_{K}}}\,|f(x)|^{\frac{3}{2}-\frac{1}{\theta_{K}\mathbf{N}}}

in a neighborhood of
f|_{K}^{-1}(0)
in
K
.

It is well-known (see
Lemma

8.8
) that there exist
c>0
and
\alpha\in[0,1)
such that

|\operatorname{grad}f(x)|\geq c\,|f(x)|^{\alpha},

but
c
and
\alpha
are not explicit.
We have the following variant with an explicit exponent
<1
; see
Corollary

8.10
.

Corollary 1.13
.

Assume that
(\operatorname{grad}f|_{K})^{-1}(0)\subseteq f|_{K}^{-1}(0)
and
M_{0}\geq 1
.
Then,

(1.17)

\|\operatorname{grad}f\|_{B_{x}}\geq\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{2\|f\|_{K}}{3\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\theta_{K}\mathbf{N}}}\,\Big(\frac{c_{K}\,\nu_{K}}{|K|}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\theta_{K}}}\,|f(x)|^{1-\frac{1}{\theta_{K}\mathbf{N}}}

for
x
in a neighborhood of
f|_{K}^{-1}(0)
in
K
, where
B_{x}
is a neighborhood of
x
in
L
that shrinks
to
x
if
x
tends to
f|_{K}^{-1}(0)
.

See
Section

8
for further inequalities for the gradient.

1.5.
Inequalities of Harnack type

If
f:L\to\mathbb{R}
is nonvanishing on
K
and
\min_{x\in K}|f(x)|\geq m_{0}>0
, then

Theorem

1.1
easily implies

(1.18)

\max_{x\in K}|f(x)|\leq 2\,\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}\,\Big(\frac{|K|^{\nu_{K}}}{|K|^{\nu_{K}}-(|K|-|E_{m_{0}}|)^{\nu_{K}}}\Big)^{\mathbf{N}}\,\min_{x\in K}|f(x)|,

where
E_{m_{0}}=\{x\in K:|f(x)|\leq\min_{x\in K}|f(x)|+m_{0}\}
. But it is difficult to determine
|E_{m_{0}}|
.

In
Section

9
, we combine this inequality for
f
with the one for
1/f
in order to obtain the following
inequality of Harnack type with completely explicit constants.
This requires an additional regularity assumption on
\mu^{|\infty}
which allows to conclude that

1/f:L\to\mathbb{R}
is
(\beta_{0}\,\mu^{|\infty},\frac{2}{m_{0}})
-smooth, provided that
f:L\to\mathbb{R}
is
(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0})
-smooth and
\min_{x\in L}|f(x)|\geq m_{0}>0
,
where

(1.19)

\beta_{0}=\beta_{0}(\mu^{|\infty},n,M_{0},m_{0})>1.

Corollary 1.14
.

Then, for all
K\in\mathscr{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
K\subseteq L
,

\max_{x\in K}|f(x)|\leq\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}^{2}\Big(\frac{2^{\nu_{K}}}{2^{\nu_{K}}-1}\Big)^{2\mathbf{N}}\min_{x\in K}|f(x)|,

where

\mathbf{N}=2\,\mathbf{D}_{a_{K}\beta_{0}\,\mu^{|\infty}}(\tfrac{m_{0}}{2M_{0}}).

See
Theorem

9.5
, where
\beta_{0}
is made fully explicit.

1.6.
Inequalities of Markov type

The Bang degree gives an upper bound for the total number of zeros of an
(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0})
-smooth function
on an interval,
as proved in
[
Rai23
]
; see
Proposition

10.1
.
Using this fact and the reverse Hölder inequality in

Corollary

1.7
, we obtain inequalities of Markov type in
Section

10
.

Here is an exemplary result for univariate functions.
Since the zero bound is applied to the first derivative
f^{\prime}
, we work with the shifted weight
\mu^{|\infty}_{+1}=(\mu_{j+1})_{j\geq 1}
.

Corollary 1.15
.

Let
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be a compact interval.
Let
b_{1}\in(0,M_{1})
.
Every
(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0})
-smooth function
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\|f^{\prime}\|_{I}\geq b_{1}

satisfies

(1.20)

\|f^{\prime}\|_{I}\leq\frac{2\,\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}}{|I|}(\mathbf{N}+1)^{2}\,\|f\|_{I},

where

\mathbf{N}=2\,\mathbf{D}_{|I|\,\mu^{|\infty}_{+1}}(\tfrac{b_{1}}{M_{1}}).

This has consequence for sampling the supremum norm; see
Section

10.3
.

In
Section

10
, we also provide multivariate versions of
Corollary

1.15
on
K\in\mathscr{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

1.7.
Oscillatory integrals

In
Section

11
, we combine
the estimate for the volume of sublevel sets in
Corollary

1.6
and the bound for the number of zeros
with the van der Corput lemma (which we recall in
Lemma

11.1
)
in order to obtain decay estimates for oscillatory integrals whose phase functions are

(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0})
-smooth.

Corollary 1.16
.

Let
I^{n}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a compact cube.
Let
b_{2}\in(0,M_{2})
.
Let
f:I^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a
(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0})
-smooth function.
Assume, for some
1\leq i_{0}\leq n
, we have
\|\partial_{i_{0}}^{2}f\|_{\ell}\geq b_{2}
for each line segment
\ell
in
I^{n}
with direction
e_{i_{0}}
.
Let
g:I^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
belong to
\mathcal{C}^{1}(I^{n})
. Then,

(1.21)

\Big|\int_{I^{n}}e^{i\lambda f(x)}g(x)\,dx\Big|\leq B\,\lambda^{-\frac{1}{\mathbf{N}+2}},\quad\text{ if }\lambda\geq\Big(\frac{2}{b_{2}}\Big)^{1+\frac{2}{\mathbf{N}}},

where

(1.22)

\displaystyle\mathbf{N}=2\,\mathbf{D}_{\sqrt{n}\,|I|\,\mu^{|\infty}_{+2}}(\tfrac{\frac{b_{2}}{2}}{M_{2}+\frac{b_{2}}{2}}),

(1.23)

\displaystyle B=\frac{\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}^{1/\mathbf{N}}\,n\,|I|^{n}\,\|g\|_{I^{n}}}{\|\partial_{i_{0}}^{2}f\|_{I^{n}}^{1/\mathbf{N}}}+2C\,(\mathbf{N}+1)\,\big(|I|^{n-1}\,\|g\|_{I^{n}}+\|\partial_{i_{0}}g\|_{L^{1}(I^{n})}\big),

and
C>0
is an absolute constant.

If
K\in\mathscr{K}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is definable in
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{C}_{M}}
, we can use uniform finiteness in o-minimal structures
and obtain the following variant for integrals over
K
.

Corollary 1.17
.

In the setting of
Corollary

1.16
,
assume additionally that
K\in\mathscr{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is contained in the interior of
I^{n}
and definable
in
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{C}_{M}}
. If
\|\partial_{i_{0}}^{2}f\|_{K}\geq b_{2}^{\prime}>0
, then

(1.24)

\Big|\int_{K}e^{i\lambda f(x)}g(x)\,dx\Big|\leq B\,\lambda^{-\frac{1}{\mathbf{N}+2}},\quad\text{ for }\lambda>0,

where

(1.25)

\displaystyle\mathbf{N}=2\,\mathbf{D}_{\sqrt{n}\,|I|\,\mu^{|\infty}_{+2}}(\tfrac{\frac{b_{2}}{2}}{M_{2}+\frac{b_{2}}{2}}),

(1.26)

\displaystyle B=\frac{\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}^{1/\mathbf{N}}\,|K|\,\|g\|_{K}}{\nu_{K}\,\|\partial_{i_{0}}^{2}f\|_{K}^{1/\mathbf{N}}}+2C\,N_{f,K}\,\big(|I|^{n-1}\,\|g\|_{I^{n}}+\|\partial_{i_{0}}g\|_{L^{1}(I^{n})}\big),

C>0
is an absolute constant, and
N_{f,K}\in\mathbb{N}
is an integer depending only on
f
and
K
.

1.8.
Metric entropy of sublevel sets

Let
\epsilon>0
and
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
relatively compact.
The
covering number

N_{\epsilon}(K)
of
K
is the minimal number of closed balls of radius
\epsilon
covering
K

and
H_{\epsilon}(K):=\log_{2}N_{\epsilon}(K)
is the
\epsilon
-entropy
of
K
.

Corollary 1.18
.

In the setup of
Theorem

1.1
, assume additionally that
K\in\mathscr{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is definable in
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{C}_{M}}
and
L=B(0,r)
.
Then the covering number of the sublevel set
K_{t}
of
f|_{K}
satisfies

(1.27)

N_{\epsilon}(K_{t})\leq C(n)\frac{|K|}{\nu_{K}}\,\Big(\frac{\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}\,t}{\|f\|_{K}}\Big)^{1/\mathbf{N}}\frac{1}{\epsilon^{n}}+C_{K_{t}}\Big(1+\Big(\frac{r}{\epsilon}\Big)^{n-1}\Big),\quad t>0,

for a constant
C_{K_{t}}>0
.

Proof.

This follows from
Corollary

1.6
and
[
YC04
, Proposition 5.6]
.
∎

The constant
C_{K_{t}}
is of the form

(1.28)

C_{K_{t}}=C(n)\max_{1\leq j\leq n-1}\omega_{j}\cdot B_{n-j}(K_{t}),

where
B_{n-j}(K_{t})
bounds the number of connected components of
P\cap K_{t}
, where
P
is any affine
(n-j)
-plane in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
The bounds
B_{n-j}(K_{t})
exist because
K_{t}
is definable in
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{C}_{M}}
.
It would be interesting to find explicit bounds via the Bang degree depending on the Denjoy–Carleman complexity of
K_{t}
.

Organization of the paper

In

In

In

(

5
and
6
.

1.10.
Notation

We use the Euclidean norm
|x|=(\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}^{2})^{1/2}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Sometimes we also use the
1
-norm
|x|_{1}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}|x_{j}|
.
Recall that
|x|\leq|x|_{1}\leq\sqrt{n}\,|x|
.

For differentiable functions
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\supseteq K\to\mathbb{R}
we typically use (semi)norms based on the
1
-norm:

(1.29)

\|f\|_{j,K}:=\sup_{x\in K}\sum_{|\alpha|=j}\frac{j!}{\alpha!}|\partial^{\alpha}f(x)|,

in particular,

(1.30)

\|\operatorname{grad}f\|_{K}:=\sup_{x\in K}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\partial_{i}f(x)|=\|f\|_{1,K}.

The Lebesgue measure in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is denoted by
\mathcal{L}^{n}
and often we also write
|E|=\mathcal{L}^{n}(E)
if the
ambient dimension
n
is clear. Let
\omega_{n}
denote the volume of the unit ball
B(0,1)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
The standard basis vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
are denoted by
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}
.

2.
Weights, quasianalytic functions, and the Bang degree

This section introduces the central objects of the paper and sets the terminology used throughout.

2.1.
Admissible weights and
(\mu^{|N},M_{0})
-smooth functions

Definition 2.1

(Admissible weights)
.

An
admissible weight
is an infinite increasing sequence
\mu^{|\infty}=(\mu_{j})_{j\geq 1}
of elements in
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, i.e.,

0<\mu_{1}\leq\mu_{2}\leq\cdots
.

Definition 2.2

(Quasianalytic weights)
.

An admissible weight
\mu^{|\infty}=(\mu_{j})_{j\geq 1}
is called
quasianalytic
if

(2.1)

\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{1}{\mu_{j}}=\infty.

Given an admissible weight
\mu^{|\infty}=(\mu_{j})_{j\geq 1}

and any positive real number
M_{0}>0
,
we define

(2.2)

M_{j}:=M_{0}\prod_{i=1}^{j}\mu_{i},\quad j\geq 1.

Then the sequence
M=(M_{j})_{j\geq 0}
satisfies the logarithmic convexity property

(2.3)

M_{j}^{2}\leq M_{j-1}M_{j+1},\quad j\geq 1.

Conversely,
M=(M_{j})_{j\geq 0}
uniquely determines the pair
(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0})
(setting
\mu_{j}=\frac{M_{j}}{M_{j-1}}
).

Definition 2.3

(
(\mu^{|N},M_{0})
-smooth functions and maps)
.

Let
\mu^{|\infty}=(\mu_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be an admissible weight and
M_{0}>0
.
Let
N\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}\cup\{\infty\}
.
A function
f:K\to\mathbb{R}
, defined on a nonempty compact set
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
is called
(\mu^{|N},M_{0})
-smooth
if
f
is of class
\mathcal{C}^{N}
(in some open neighborhood of
K
)
and

(2.4)

\|f\|_{j,K}\leq M_{j},\quad 0\leq j<N+1,

where

\|f\|_{j,K}:=\sup_{x\in K}\sum_{|\alpha|=j}\frac{j!}{\alpha!}|\partial^{\alpha}f(x)|

and
\|f\|_{K}=\|f\|_{0,K}=\sup_{x\in K}|f(x)|
denotes the sup-norm.

Similarly, a map
f=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}):K\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is
called
(\mu^{|N},M_{0})
-smooth
if
f
is of class
\mathcal{C}^{N}

and

(2.5)

\|f\|_{j,K}\leq M_{j},\quad 0\leq j<N+1,

where

\|f\|_{j,K}:=\sup_{x\in K}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{|\alpha|=j}\frac{j!}{\alpha!}|\partial^{\alpha}f_{i}(x)|.

We set
\|f\|_{K}:=\|f\|_{0,K}=\sup_{x\in K}\sum_{i=1}^{m}|f_{i}(x)|
.

Remark 2.4
.

In the single-valued case,

\|f\|_{j,K}
is the supremum over
x\in K
of the
1
-norm of the vector with entries
\partial^{\alpha}f(x)
,
where
|\alpha|=j
and the entry
\partial^{\alpha}f(x)
appears
\frac{j!}{\alpha!}
times.
This is convenient because the directional derivatives satisfy

\displaystyle|d_{v}^{j}f(x)|=\big|\partial_{t}^{j}|_{t=0}f(x+tv)\big|=\Big|\sum_{|\alpha|=j}\frac{j!}{\alpha!}\partial^{\alpha}f(x)v^{\alpha}\Big|\leq|v|^{j}\sum_{|\alpha|=j}\frac{j!}{\alpha!}|\partial^{\alpha}f(x)|.

The vector-valued case behaves similarly.

2.2.
The Bang degree

Let
\mu^{|\infty}=(\mu_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be an admissible weight.
We consider the function
\Sigma_{\mu^{|\infty}}:\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}\times\mathbb{N}\to[0,\infty)

defined by

\Sigma_{\mu^{|\infty}}(m,n):=\sum_{j=m}^{n}\frac{1}{\mu_{j}}.

If
n<m
then
\Sigma_{\mu^{|\infty}}(m,n)=0
(being an empty sum).
The function
(m,n)\mapsto\Sigma_{\mu^{|\infty}}(m,n)
is increasing in
n

and decreasing in
m
.

For
b>0
, let
j_{0}(b)
be the smallest integer
j\in\mathbb{N}
with
j\geq\log(b^{-1})
, i.e.,

j_{0}(b):=\lceil\log(b^{-1})\rceil_{\mathbb{N}}.

Definition 2.5

(Bang degree)
.

Let
\mu^{|\infty}=(\mu_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be an admissible weight and
b>0
.
We define the
Bang degree

\mathbf{D}_{\mu^{|\infty}}(b)\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}

by setting

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}_{\mu^{|\infty}}(b):=\sup\big\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\Sigma_{\mu^{|\infty}}(j_{0}(b)+1,n)<e\big\}.

Lemma 2.6
.

We have:

(1)

\mathbf{D}_{\mu^{|\infty}}(b)\geq j_{0}(b)
and
\mathbf{D}_{\mu^{|\infty}}(b)=j_{0}(b)
if and only if
\mu_{j_{0}(b)+1}\leq 1/e
.

(2)

\mathbf{D}_{\mu^{|\infty}}(b)=\infty
if and only if
\Sigma_{\mu^{|\infty}}(j_{0}(b)+1,n)<e
for all
n
.

(3)

The map
(a,b)\mapsto\mathbf{D}_{a\,\mu^{|\infty}}(b)
is increasing in
a>0
and decreasing in
b>0
.

(4)

If
\mu^{|\infty}
is quasianalytic, then
\mathbf{D}_{\mu^{|\infty}}(b)
is finite.

Proof.

All properties follow easily from the definition.
∎

Remark 2.7
.

Our notion of Bang degree differs slightly from the notion proposed in
[
NSV04
]
,
where it is attached to
a given
(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0})
-smooth function
f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}

and equals
\mathbf{D}_{\mu^{|\infty}}(\|f\|_{[0,1]}/M_{0})
(in our notation).
The unspecified argument
b
allows for more flexibility.

2.3.
Shifted weights

Occasionally, we will deal with derivatives of
(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0})
-smooth functions. This naturally requires
a “shift” of the weight
\mu^{|\infty}
.

Given an admissible weight
\mu^{|\infty}=(\mu_{j})_{j\geq 1}
, also the shifted sequence

(2.6)

\mu^{|\infty}_{+i}=(\mu_{j+i})_{j\geq 1},

where
i\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
,
is an admissible weight.
Evidently,
\mu^{|\infty}
is quasianalytic if and only if
\mu^{|\infty}_{+i}
is quasianalytic.

The Bang degree for
\mu^{|\infty}_{+i}
can always be reduced to the Bang degree for
\mu^{|\infty}
.

Lemma 2.8
.

We have
\mathbf{D}_{\mu^{|\infty}_{+i}}(b)=\mathbf{D}_{\mu^{|\infty}}(e^{-i}b)-i
.

Proof.

This follows from
\Sigma_{\mu^{|\infty}_{+1}}(j_{0}(b)+1,n)=\Sigma_{\mu^{|\infty}}(j_{0}(b)+2,n+1)
.
∎

2.4.
Further definitions and conventions

Let us adjust the definition of the Bang degree to its use in the Remez inequality.
We will not automatically assume that
\mu^{|\infty}
is quasianalytic.
Instead we work with an admissibility condition that guarantees that the Bang degree is finite.

Definition 2.9

(Associated degree)
.

Let
\mu^{|\infty}=(\mu_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be an admissible weight and
M_{0},a,b>0
.
We say that
(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0},a,b)
is
admissible
, if

(2.7)

\sum_{j=j_{0}+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\mu_{j}}>a\,e,\quad\text{ where }j_{0}=\lceil\log(\tfrac{M_{0}}{b})\rceil_{\mathbb{N}}.

In that case, we define the integer

(2.8)

\mathbf{N}=\mathbf{N}(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0},a,b):=2\,\mathbf{D}_{a\,\mu^{|\infty}}(\tfrac{b}{M_{0}})

and say that
\mathbf{N}
is the
degree associated with
(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0},a,b)
.

Remark 2.10
.

(1) By definition, for
r,s>0
,
(r\,\mu^{|\infty},s\,M_{0},a,b)
is admissible if and only
if
(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0},r\,a,\frac{b}{s})
is admissible,
and

\mathbf{N}(r\,\mu^{|\infty},s\,M_{0},a,b)=\mathbf{N}(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0},r\,a,\tfrac{b}{s}).

(2) Assuming admissibility, the integer
\mathbf{N}(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0},a,b)
is increasing in
a
and decreasing in
b
.

(3) If
\mu^{|\infty}
is quasianalytic, then (
2.7
) is satisfied for all
M_{0},a,b>0
.

Remark 2.11
.

In our results, the integer
\mathbf{N}
will be useless if it is zero.
We have
\mathbf{N}(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0},a,b)=0
if and only if
ae\mu_{1}\leq 1
and
M_{0}\leq b
(by
Lemma

2.6
(1)).
Therefore, we will generally assume that
M_{0}>b
so that
\mathbf{N}(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0},a,b)\geq 2
.

In
[
Rai23
]
, we occasionally used
M_{0}=b=\|f\|_{K}
, for a
(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0})
-smooth function
f
,
and took extra care for the case
\mathbf{N}(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0},a,b)=0
.
These occasions can simply be adjusted to the convention
M_{0}>b
by setting
b=\|f\|_{K}
and
M_{0}=2\,\|f\|_{K}
.
This possibly increases
\mathbf{N}
a little, while
f
is still
(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0})
-smooth.

Definition 2.12

(The function
\gamma_{\tilde{\mu}}
)
.

Let
\mu^{|\infty}=(\mu_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be an admissible weight.
Let
\tilde{\mu}:[1,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
be an increasing continuous and (piecewise)
\mathcal{C}^{1}

function such that

\mu_{j}=\tilde{\mu}(j),\quad j\geq 1.

For instance, we may take
\tilde{\mu}
piecewise affine and work consistently with the left derivative
at points, where
\tilde{\mu}
is not differentiable.

Then we define (following
[
NSV04
]
)

\gamma_{\tilde{\mu}}(n):=\sup_{1\leq s\leq n}\frac{s\tilde{\mu}^{\prime}(s)}{\tilde{\mu}(s)},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}.

Note that
\gamma_{\tilde{\mu}}
depends on the choice of
\tilde{\mu}
which is not unique.

Definition 2.13

(The constant
\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}
)
.

Let
\mu^{|\infty}=(\mu_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be an admissible weight and
M_{0},a,b>0
with
M_{0}>b
.
Assume that
(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0},a,b)
is admissible and let
\mathbf{N}=\mathbf{N}(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0},a,b)
.
Suppose that
\tilde{\mu}
has been chosen.
We define

(2.9)

\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}:=(4e^{4+\gamma_{\tilde{\mu}}(\mathbf{N})})^{\mathbf{N}}.

If
\sup_{1\leq s<\infty}\frac{s\tilde{\mu}^{\prime}(s)}{\tilde{\mu}(s)}
is finite (see e.g.
Propositions

A.2
and
A.5
),
then
\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}
can be replaced
by the potentially larger constant
C^{\mathbf{N}}
for some suitable
C>0
.

Remark 2.14
.

Our notation differs somewhat from the notation used in
[
Rai23
]
. For instance,
the integer
\mathbf{N}
defined in (
2.8
) is twice the integer denoted by the same symbol in
[
Rai23
]
.

3.
O-minimal structures

We recall the definition of an
o-minimal structure
over the real ordered field and some background;
cf.
[
vdDM96
]
and
[
vdD98
]
.

Definition 3.1

(O-minimal structure over the real ordered field)
.

A
structure
over the real ordered field
(\mathbb{R},+,\cdot,<)
is a sequence
\mathscr{S}=(\mathscr{S}_{n})_{n\geq 1}

of collections of subsets
\mathscr{S}_{n}
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, such that for all
n,n^{\prime}\geq 1
:

(1)

\mathscr{S}_{n}
is a Boolean algebra with respect to the usual set-theoretic operations;

(2)

\mathscr{S}_{n}
contains all semialgebraic subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
;

(3)

if
X\in\mathscr{S}_{n}
and
X^{\prime}\in\mathscr{S}_{n^{\prime}}
, then
X\times X^{\prime}\in\mathscr{S}_{n+n^{\prime}}
;

(4)

if
n\geq n^{\prime}
and
X\in\mathscr{S}_{n}
, then
\pi(X)\in\mathscr{S}_{n^{\prime}}
, where
\pi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n^{\prime}}

is the projection on the first
n^{\prime}
coordinates.

A subset
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to be
definable
in the structure
\mathscr{S}
if
X\in\mathscr{S}_{n}
.
A map
f:X\to\mathbb{R}^{n^{\prime}}
is called
definable
in
\mathscr{S}
if its graph is definable.
A structure
\mathscr{S}
is called
o-minimal
if

(5)

every set in
\mathscr{S}_{1}
is a finite union of intervals and points.

A structure
\mathscr{S}
is called
polynomially bounded
if for every function
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
that is definable in
\mathscr{S}

there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
f(t)=O(t^{N})
as
t\to\infty
.
An o-minimal structure
\mathscr{S}
either is polynomially bounded or the exponential function
\exp:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is definable in
\mathscr{S}

(see
[
Mil94
]
).

Example 3.2

(Semialgebraic sets)
.

The family of all semialgebraic sets forms a polynomially bounded o-minimal structure.

Example 3.3

(Globally subanalytic sets)
.

The family of globally subanalytic sets forms a polynomially bounded o-minimal structure denoted
\mathbb{R}_{\operatorname{an}}
.
It is the smallest structure over the real ordered field containing all restricted analytic functions

f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
, i.e.,
f|_{[-1,1]^{n}}
is analytic and
f=0
outside
[-1,1]^{n}
.

Example 3.4

(The structure
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{C}_{M}}
)
.

Let
\mu^{|\infty}=(\mu_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be an admissible weight and
M_{0}>0
.
Let
M=(M_{j})_{j\geq 0}
be defined by (
2.2
).
Consider
the associated Denjoy–Carleman class
\mathcal{C}_{M}
(see (
1.3
)).

Let us make the following additional assumptions on the admissible weight
\mu^{|\infty}
.

(1)

The sequence

a_{j}:=\Big(\frac{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{j}}{j!}\Big)^{1/j},\quad j\geq 1,

satisfies
\liminf_{j\to\infty}a_{j}>0
.

(2)

The sequence
a_{j}
is almost increasing, i.e., there exists a constant
H\geq 1
such that

a_{j}\leq Ha_{k}
whenever
j\leq k
.

(3)

There is a constant
C\geq 1
such that
\mu_{j+1}\leq C^{j}
, equivalently,

M_{j+1}\leq C^{j}M_{j}
, for
j\geq 1
.

(4)

\mu^{|\infty}
is quasianalytic.

Under the assumptions (1)–(4), the class
\mathcal{C}_{M}
is quasianalytic, containes all real analytic functions,
and is stable under composition, differentiation, and inverse and implicit functions (cf.
[
Rai22
]
).

Under these assumptions, the expansion
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{C}_{M}}

of the real ordered field by all restricted
\mathcal{C}_{M}
-functions
is a polynomially bounded o-minimal structure, by
[
RSW03
]
.
A function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is called a
restricted
\mathcal{C}_{M}
-function
if
there exists
\tilde{f}\in\mathcal{C}_{M}(U)
for an open neighborhood
U
of
[-1,1]^{n}

such that
f(x)=\tilde{f}(x)
if
x\in[-1,1]^{n}
and
f(x)=0
if
x\not\in[-1,1]^{n}
.

Note that (2) is satisfied with
H=1
if the sequence
\mu_{j}/j
is increasing (which means that
M_{j}/j!
is logarithmically convex).
Under this stronger condition, one can drop the assumption (3) (which guarantees that
\mathcal{C}_{M}
is stable under differentiation):
the expansion
\mathbb{R}_{\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{M}}

of the real ordered field by all restricted
\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{M}
-functions
is a polynomially bounded o-minimal structure, where

\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{M}:=\bigcup_{k\geq 0}\mathcal{C}_{M_{+k}}\quad\text{ and }\quad M_{+k}:=(M_{j+k})_{j\geq 0}.

4.
Quasianalytic Remez inequalities

After reviewing quasianalytic Remez inequalities on intervals and convex bodies,
we discuss a large collection of sets in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to which the quasianalytic Remez inequality can be extended;
see
Theorem

4.13
.

4.1.
Remez inequality for univariate functions

The following theorem is a Remez inequality for univariate vector-valued functions.
It generalizes
[
NSV04
, Theorem B]
for single-valued functions; see also
[
Rai23
, Theorem 5.4]
.
It is proved in
Appendix

B
for the reader’s convenience.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mu^{|\infty}=(\mu_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be an admissible weight and
M_{0}>b_{0}>0
.
Assume that
(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0},1,b_{0})
is admissible and let
\mathbf{N}=\mathbf{N}(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0},1,b_{0})
.
Let
f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
be
(\mu^{|\mathbf{N}},M_{0})
-smooth such that
\|f\|_{[0,1]}\geq b_{0}
.
Then for each interval
I\subseteq[0,1]
and each Lebesgue measurable set
E\subseteq I
with
|E|>0
we have

(4.1)

\|f\|_{I}\leq\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}\,\Big(\frac{m\,|I|}{|E|}\Big)^{\mathbf{N}}\|f\|_{E}.

4.2.
Remez inequality on convex bodies

Theorem

4.1
, for
m=1
, was extended to convex bodies in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
in
[
Rai23
, Theorem 5.4]
.
Here we state a vector-valued version.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\mu^{|\infty}=(\mu_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be an admissible weight and
M_{0}>b_{0}>0
.
Let
K\subseteq L\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be convex bodies.
Assume that
(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0},\operatorname{diam}(K),b_{0})
is admissible
and let
\mathbf{N}=\mathbf{N}(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0},\operatorname{diam}(K),b_{0})
.
Let
f:L\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
be
(\mu^{|\mathbf{N}},M_{0})
-smooth
such that
\|f\|_{K}\geq b_{0}
.
If
E\subseteq K
is a Lebesgue measurable subset with
|E|>0
, then

(4.2)

\|f\|_{K}\leq\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}\Big(\frac{m\,|K|^{1/n}}{|K|^{1/n}-(|K|-|E|)^{1/n}}\Big)^{\mathbf{N}}\|f\|_{E}.

Even for
m=1
,
the statement of
Theorem

4.2
is slightly stronger than the one of
[
Rai23
, Theorem 5.4]
.
In fact,
in the latter, the potentially bigger integer
\mathbf{N}(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0},\operatorname{diam}(L),b_{0})
is
used rather than
\mathbf{N}=\mathbf{N}(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0},\operatorname{diam}(K),b_{0})
.
The stronger vector-valued version is obtained by the same proof (based on the technique of
[
BG73
]
).
Alternatively, we shall see that
Theorem

4.2
follows from
Corollary

4.4
below.

4.3.
Star-shaped sets

The following lemma, due to
[
Pie15
, Lemma 4.1]
,
will play an important role in the proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a compact set that is star-shaped with respect to
x_{0}\in K
and satisfies
|K|>0
.
Let
E\subseteq K
be Lebesgue measurable.
Then there exists an affine curve
\gamma:[0,1]\to K
with
\gamma(0)=x_{0}
and such that
\gamma^{-1}(E)
contains a
Lebesgue measurable subset
F
satisfying

(4.3)

\mathcal{L}^{1}(F)\geq 1-\Big(1-\frac{|E|}{|K|}\Big)^{1/n}.

We give a corollary which implies
Theorem

4.2
: for the convex body
K
, it suffices to choose
x_{0}\in K
such that
|f(x_{0})|_{1}=\|f\|_{K}
.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
\mu^{|\infty}=(\mu_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be an admissible weight and
M_{0}>b_{0}>0
.
Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a compact set that is star-shaped with respect to
x_{0}\in K
and such that
|K|>0
.
Assume that
(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0},\operatorname{diam}(K),b_{0})
is admissible
and let
\mathbf{N}=\mathbf{N}(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0},\operatorname{diam}(K),b_{0})
.
Let
f:K\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
be
(\mu^{|\mathbf{N}},M_{0})
-smooth
such that
|f(x_{0})|_{1}\geq b_{0}
.
If
E\subseteq K
is a Lebesgue measurable subset with
|E|>0
, then

(4.4)

|f(x_{0})|_{1}\leq\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}\Big(\frac{m\,|K|^{1/n}}{|K|^{1/n}-(|K|-|E|)^{1/n}}\Big)^{\mathbf{N}}\|f\|_{E}.

Proof.

Let
\gamma:[0,1]\to K
,
\gamma(t)=x_{0}+t(x_{1}-x_{0})
with
x_{1}=\gamma(1)
, be the affine curve from
Lemma

4.3
.
We apply
Theorem

4.1
to
g:=f\circ\gamma
which is
(\operatorname{diam}(K)\,\mu^{|\mathbf{N}},M_{0})
-smooth
and to the set
F
from
Lemma

4.3
,

(4.5)

\displaystyle|f(x_{0})|_{1}=|g(0)|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}\Big(\frac{m}{\mathcal{L}^{1}(F)}\Big)^{\mathbf{N}}\,\|g\|_{F}.

This implies the assertion, by (
4.3
).
∎

4.4.
Admissible sets

We introduce a collection of sets in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
admissible for the Remez inequality.
The following definitions are based on
[
Pie15
, Definition 9.1]
.

Definition 4.5

(Admissible collection)
.

Suppose that a family
(K_{w},a_{w},\Upsilon_{w})_{w\in W}
is given, where
K_{w}\subseteq[0,1]^{n}
is compact,
a_{w}\in K_{w}
,

\Upsilon_{w}:[0,\infty)^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a map, and
W
is an arbitrary set.
This family is called an
admissible collection
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
if the following holds.
There exist constants
\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\delta_{3}>0
,
d,q\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
, and polynomial maps
R_{w}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
of degree at most
d
such that,
for all
w\in W
:

(1)

K_{w}
is star-shaped with respect to
a_{w}
and
|K_{w}|\geq\delta_{1}
;

(2)

\Upsilon_{w}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=R_{w}(x_{1}^{1/q},\ldots,x_{n}^{1/q})
;

(3)

\delta_{2}\leq\sum_{\kappa}|c_{w,\kappa}|\leq\delta_{3}
, where
c_{w,\kappa}
are the coefficients of the polynomial
\operatorname{Jac}(R_{w})
.

(4)

a_{w,i}=0
whenever
\Upsilon_{w}
is not a polynomial map with respect to the variable
x_{i}
, where
a_{w}=(a_{w,1},\ldots,a_{w,n})
.

We also say that
(d,q,\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\delta_{2})
is the
characteristic
of
(K_{w},a_{w},\Upsilon_{w})_{w\in W}
.

Definition 4.6

(Admissible set)
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a nonempty compact set.
We say that
K
is an
admissible set
if
there exists an admissible collection
(K_{w},a_{w},\Upsilon_{w})_{w\in K}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

(1)

\Upsilon_{w}(a_{w})=w
for each
w\in K
;

(2)

K=\bigcup_{w\in K}\Upsilon_{w}(K_{w})
.

We also say that
K
has the
characteristic

(d,q,\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\delta_{2})
if
(K_{w},a_{w},\Upsilon_{w})_{w\in K}
has this characteristic.

Let us denote by
\mathscr{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
the family of all admissible sets in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

In particular, every compact set
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the
special parameterization property

as defined in
[
Pie15
, Definition 1.3]

is admissible.

Example 4.7
.

Each convex body
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an admissible set with characteristic
(1,1,\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\delta_{3})
.
Indeed, we may use the admissible collection
(K_{w},a_{w},\Upsilon_{w})_{w\in K}
, where

a_{w}:=(\frac{1}{2},\ldots,\frac{1}{2})
,
T_{w}(x):=a_{w}+\frac{1}{2\operatorname{diam}(K)}(x-w)
,

K_{w}:=T_{w}(K)
, and
\Upsilon_{w}:=T_{w}^{-1}
.

Definition 4.8

(UPC set)
.

A subset
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
UPC
if there exists a map
P:\overline{K}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
constants
A,B>0
and
d\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
such that, for each
x\in\overline{K}
,

•

P(x,\cdot)
is a polynomial curve of degree at most
d
;

•

P(x,0)=x
;

•

\operatorname{dist}(P(x,t),\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus K)\geq A\,t^{B}
, for
t\in[0,1]
.

Proposition 4.9

(
[
Pie15
, Lemma 3.8]
)
.

Each compact UPC set is admissible with characteristic
(\max\{d,\lceil B\rceil\},1,\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\delta_{3})
,
where
d\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
and
B>0
are the constants from
Definition

4.8
.

Proof.

The characteristic follows easily from the proof of Lemma 3.8 in
[
Pie15
]
.
∎

Example 4.10
.

Each compact fat subanalytic set is UPC and hence admissible, by
[
PP86
]
.
More generally, each compact fat set that is definable in
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{C}_{M}}
is UPC and hence admissible,
where
\mathcal{C}_{M}
is a suitable quasianalytic Denjoy–Carleman class; see
Example

3.4
.
This is due to
[
Pie05
]
.

Example 4.11
.

The closure of a bounded Lipschitz domain is UPC and thus admissible with characteristic

(1,1,\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\delta_{3})
.
More generally, the closure of a bounded Hölder domain with Hölder exponent
\alpha\in(0,1]

is UPC and thus admissible with characteristic

(\lceil\alpha^{-1}\rceil,1,\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\delta_{3})
. Cf.
[
Rai24
, Section 1.3]
.

4.5.
Remez inequality on admissible sets

Definition 4.12

(The constant
a_{K}
)
.

Let
K\in\mathscr{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Assume that
K
has the characteristic
(d,q,\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\delta_{3})
.
Then we associate the positive number

(4.6)

a_{K}:=\begin{cases}4\,\frac{(d+1)^{d+1}}{(d-1)^{d-1}}\cdot\max\{\operatorname{diam}(K),1\},&\text{ if }d\geq 2,\\
\operatorname{diam}(K),&\text{ if }d=1.\end{cases}

We are ready to state the general Remez inequality.

Theorem 4.13
.

Let
L\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body and
K\in\mathscr{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
K\subseteq L
.
There exists
\nu_{K}\in(0,1]
, depending only on
K
, such that the following holds.
Let
\mu^{|\infty}=(\mu_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be an admissible weight satisfying
\mu_{j}\geq j
for
j\geq 1
.
Let
M_{0}>b_{0}>0
.
Let
m\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
.
Assume that
(\mu^{|\infty},m\,M_{0},a_{K},b_{0})
is admissible and let

\mathbf{N}=\mathbf{N}(\mu^{|\infty},m\,M_{0},a_{K},b_{0}).

Then, for each
(\mu^{|\mathbf{N}},M_{0})
-smooth map
f:L\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
with
\|f\|_{K}\geq b_{0}
and
each Lebesgue measurable set
E\subseteq K
with
|E|>0
, we have

\|f\|_{K}\leq\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}\,\Big(\frac{m\,|K|^{\nu_{K}}}{|K|^{\nu_{K}}-(|K|-|E|)^{\nu_{K}}}\Big)^{\mathbf{N}}\,\|f\|_{E}.

Clearly,
Theorem

1.1
is a direct consequence of
Theorem

4.13
.
The proof of
Theorem

4.13
comprises
Section

5
and
Section

6
.

Remark 4.14
.

It is possible to explicitly compute a number
\nu_{K}
for which
Theorem

4.13
holds;
this requires the full characteristic
(d,q,\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\delta_{3})
of
K
as well as other
specifics of
K
such as the ambient dimension
n
and
|K|
.

Remark 4.15
.

Theorem

4.13
extends
Theorem

4.2
; in fact, if
K
is a convex body we may
take
d=1
,
a_{K}=\operatorname{diam}(K)
,
\nu_{K}=1/n
, and
\mathbf{N}=\mathbf{N}(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0},a_{K},b_{0})
(even if
m>1
).

Remark 4.16
.

Noting that

\frac{(d+1)^{d+1}}{(d-1)^{d-1}}=\Big(\frac{d+1}{d-1}\Big)^{d-1}(d+1)^{2}=\Big(1+\frac{2}{d-1}\Big)^{d-1}(d+1)^{2}

we see that the growth of
a_{K}
(as defined in (
4.6
)) is quadratic in
d
,

a_{K}\sim 4e^{2}(d+1)^{2}\cdot\max\{\operatorname{diam}(K),1\}\quad\text{ as }d\to\infty.

Remark 4.17
.

The proof will show that

Theorem

4.13
remains valid in the following more general setup:
let
a>0
,
\mathbf{N}=\mathbf{N}(\mu^{|\infty},m\,M_{0},a,b_{0})
, and assume that
the maps
f:L\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
with
\|f\|_{K}\geq b_{0}
satisfy

•

f|_{\ell}
is
(a\,\mu^{|\mathbf{N}},M_{0})
-smooth for each line segment
\ell\subseteq L
;

•

f\circ p
is
(a\,\mu^{|\mathbf{N}},M_{0})
-smooth for each polynomial curve
p:[0,1]\to K
of degree at most
d
.

5.
Bounds along polynomial curves

In this section, we establish bounds for the composition of a
(\mu^{|N},M_{0})
-smooth function
with a polynomial curve of degree at most
d
.

We will use the following corollary of the Bernstein–Walsh lemma; see e.g.
[
RS02
, Corollary 12.1.6]
.

Lemma 5.1

(Bernstein’s lemma)
.

Let
p
be a polynomial of degree at most
d
.
For
r>1
, let
E_{r}\subseteq\mathbb{C}
be the ellipse with foci
~\pm 1
and semiaxes
\frac{1}{2}(r+r^{-1})

and
\frac{1}{2}(r-r^{-1})
.
Then

(5.1)

|p(z)|\leq r^{d}\,\|p\|_{[-1,1]},\quad z\in E_{r}.

The next proposition is the goal of this section.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\mu^{|\infty}
be an admissible weight satisfying

\mu_{j}\geq j
, for
j\geq 1
,
and
M_{0}>0
.
Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a compact nonempty set containing the origin.
Let
f:K\to\mathbb{R}
be a
(\mu^{|N},M_{0})
-smooth function and
p:[-1,1]\to K
a polynomial curve of degree at most
d
.
Then
f\circ p:[-1,1]\to\mathbb{R}
is
(a\,\mu^{|N},M_{0})
-smooth,
where

(5.2)

a:=\begin{cases}2\,\frac{(d+1)^{d+1}}{(d-1)^{d-1}}\cdot\max\{\operatorname{diam}(K),1\},&\text{ if }d\geq 2,\\
\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{diam}(K),&\text{ if }d=1.\end{cases}

Proof.

If
d=1
then we may assume that
p(t)=\frac{1}{2}(x_{0}+x_{1})+\frac{t}{2}(x_{1}-x_{0})
, where
x_{0},x_{1}\in K

and the assertion follows easily.

Let us assume
d\geq 2
.
Write
p=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})
.
Since
0\in K
, we have

(5.3)

\|p_{i}\|_{[-1,1]}\leq\max_{t\in[-1,1]}|p(t)|\leq\operatorname{diam}(K),\quad 1\leq i\leq n.

Obviously,
\|f\circ p\|_{[-1,1]}\leq M_{0}
.
The derivative
(f\circ p)^{(k)}(t)
, for
1\leq k\leq N
and
t\in[-1,1]
, coincides with the
k
-th derivative of

(5.4)

z\mapsto\sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}(\partial^{\alpha}f)(p(t))\frac{(p(z)-p(t))^{\alpha}}{\alpha!}

at
z=t
. For fixed
t\in[-1,1]
, this is a polynomial in
z
and we may assume that
z\in\mathbb{C}
.
By (
5.1
) and (
5.3
),
we have

\displaystyle|(p(z)-p(t))^{\alpha}|

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{n}|p_{i}(z)-p_{i}(t)|^{\alpha_{i}}

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{i=1}^{n}((r^{d}+1)\|p_{i}\|_{[-1,1]})^{\alpha_{i}}\leq(r^{d}+1)^{|\alpha|}\operatorname{diam}(K)^{|\alpha|}.

Thus,
the modulus of the polynomial (
5.4
) can be estimated on
E_{r}
by

\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}\frac{1}{\alpha!}|\partial^{\alpha}f(p(t))|(r^{d}+1)^{|\alpha|}\operatorname{diam}(K)^{|\alpha|}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\sum_{|\alpha|=j}\frac{1}{\alpha!}|\partial^{\alpha}f(p(t))|(r^{d}+1)^{j}\operatorname{diam}(K)^{j}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{\|f\|_{j,K}}{j!}(r^{d}+1)^{j}\operatorname{diam}(K)^{j}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{M_{j}}{j!}((r^{d}+1)\operatorname{diam}(K))^{j}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{M_{k}}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}((r^{d}+1)\operatorname{diam}(K))^{j},

where we used
\mu_{j}\geq j
in the last step.

For each
t\in[-1,1]
the ball
B(t,\delta)
with radius

(5.5)

\delta=\frac{1}{2}(r+r^{-1})-1=\frac{(r-1)^{2}}{2r}

is contained in
E_{r}
.
Thus, by Cauchy’s estimates, we find

(5.6)

|(f\circ p)^{(k)}(t)|\leq\frac{M_{k}}{\delta^{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{k}((r^{d}+1)\operatorname{diam}(K))^{j},

for
1\leq k\leq N
and
t\in[-1,1]
.

Let us estimate

(5.7)

\frac{1}{\delta^{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{k}((r^{d}+1)\operatorname{diam}(K))^{j}=\frac{2^{k}r^{k}}{(r-1)^{2k}}\sum_{j=0}^{k}((r^{d}+1)\operatorname{diam}(K))^{j}.

We distinguish two cases:

(1)
\operatorname{diam}(K)<1
: Then (
5.7
) is bounded by

\displaystyle\frac{2^{k}r^{k}}{(r-1)^{2k}}\sum_{j=0}^{k}(2r^{d})^{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{k}r^{k}}{(r-1)^{2k}}\frac{(2r^{d})^{k+1}-1}{2r^{d}-1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{k}r^{k}}{(r-1)^{2k}}\frac{(2r^{d})^{k+1}}{2r^{d}-1}\leq\Big(\frac{8r^{d+1}}{(r-1)^{2}}\Big)^{k},

since
\frac{2r^{d}}{2r^{d}-1}\leq 2
.
As
d\geq 2
,
the minimum of
\frac{r^{d+1}}{(r-1)^{2}}
for
r>1
is attained at
r_{0}=\frac{d+1}{d-1}
and amounts to

\frac{1}{4}\frac{(d+1)^{d+1}}{(d-1)^{d-1}}
. Thus (
5.7
) is bounded by

(5.8)

\Big(\frac{2(d+1)^{d+1}}{(d-1)^{d-1}}\Big)^{k}.

(2)
\operatorname{diam}(K)\geq 1
: In that case,
r^{d}\operatorname{diam}(K)\geq 1
and hence (
5.7
) is bounded by

\displaystyle\frac{2^{k}r^{k}}{(r-1)^{2k}}\sum_{j=0}^{k}(2r^{d}\operatorname{diam}(K))^{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{k}r^{k}}{(r-1)^{2k}}\frac{(2r^{d}\operatorname{diam}(K))^{k+1}-1}{2r^{d}\operatorname{diam}(K)-1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{k}r^{k}}{(r-1)^{2k}}\frac{(2r^{d}\operatorname{diam}(K))^{k+1}}{2r^{d}\operatorname{diam}(K)-1}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\frac{8r^{d+1}\operatorname{diam}(K)}{(r-1)^{2}}\Big)^{k},

since
\frac{2r^{d}\operatorname{diam}(K)}{2r^{d}\operatorname{diam}(K)-1}\leq 2
.
As in case (1), we conclude that (
5.7
) is bounded by

(5.9)

\Big(\frac{2(d+1)^{d+1}}{(d-1)^{d-1}}\cdot\operatorname{diam}(K)\Big)^{k}.

In view of (
5.6
), we conclude that, for
1\leq k\leq N
and
t\in[-1,1]
,

(5.10)

|(f\circ p)^{(k)}(t)|\leq a^{k}M_{k},

where
a
is given by (
5.2
).
This completes the proof.
∎

6.
Proof of the Remez inequality on admissible sets

This section is dedicated to the proof of
Theorem

4.13
.

6.1.
Preparatory results

We will make crucial use of the following result on admissible sets, due to
[
Pie15
]
.

Proposition 6.1

(
[
Pie15
, Corollary 9.11]
)
.

Let
K\in\mathscr{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be an admissible set with admissible collection
(K_{w},a_{w},\Upsilon_{w})_{w\in K}
.
There exist constants
\sigma,\eta>0
, and
\varrho\in[0,1)

such that for each Lebesgue measurable
E\subseteq K
and each
w\in K
,

(6.1)

\frac{|\Upsilon_{w}^{-1}(E)\cap K_{w}|}{|K_{w}|}\geq\frac{1}{\eta(1-\lambda)^{1/\sigma}+1}\quad\text{ if }\lambda:=\frac{|E|}{|K|}>\varrho.

The following lemma is a slight modification of
[
Pie15
, Lemma 8.2]
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
a,b,c>0
. Consider the functions
\varphi:(-\infty,1]\to(0,1]
and
\psi:(0,1]\to[1,\infty)
given by

(6.2)

\varphi(t)=\frac{1}{a(1-t)^{b}+1}\quad\text{ and }\quad\psi(t)=\frac{1}{1-(1-t)^{c}}.

There exists
d=d(a,b,c)>0
such that

(6.3)

(\psi\circ\varphi)(t)\leq\frac{1}{1-(1-t)^{d}},\quad t\in(0,1].

Proof.

We claim that there exists
r=r(a)\in(0,1]
such that

(6.4)

as\leq(1+as)s^{r},\quad\text{ for all }s\geq 0.

Therefore, setting
s=(1-t)^{b}
,

\varphi(t)\geq 1-(1-t)^{br},\quad t\in(-\infty,1],

and,
consequently, as
\psi
is decreasing,

(\psi\circ\varphi)(t)\leq\psi\big(1-(1-t)^{br}\big)=\frac{1}{1-(1-t)^{bcr}}

for
t\in(0,1]
.

It remains to prove (
6.4
). If
a\in(0,1]
, it suffices to set
r:=1
.
Let us assume that
a>1
. The inequality is always true for
s=0
and
s\geq 1
. So let
s\in(0,1)
.
By substituting
u=s^{r}
, (
6.4
) is equivalent to

u^{\frac{1-r}{r}}(1-u)\leq\frac{1}{a}.

The maximum of the left-hand side for
u\in(0,1)
as attained at
u=1-r
. Hence, for
a>1
, letting

r
be the unique solution of

r(1-r)^{\frac{1-r}{r}}=\frac{1}{a}

in the interval
(0,1)
,
implies (
6.4
).
(Such
r
exists because the left-hand side defines a strictly increasing surjective function
(0,1)\to(0,1)
.)
∎

6.2.
A preliminary Remez inequality

The following Remez inequality holds if the ratio
|E|/|K|
is not too small.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
K\in\mathscr{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Then there exist
\nu\in(0,1]
and
\varrho\in[0,1)
such that the following holds.
Let
\mu^{|\infty}=(\mu_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be an admissible weight satisfying
\mu_{j}\geq j
for
j\geq 1
.
Let
M_{0}>b_{0}>0
.
Let
m\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
.
Assume that
(\mu^{|\infty},m\,M_{0},a_{K},b_{0})
is admissible and let
\mathbf{N}=\mathbf{N}(\mu^{|\infty},m\,M_{0},a_{K},b_{0})
.
Let
f:K\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
be
(\mu^{|\mathbf{N}},M_{0})
-smooth such that
\|f\|_{K}\geq b_{0}
.
Then for each Lebesgue measurable set
E\subseteq K

we have

\|f\|_{K}\leq\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}\,\Big(\frac{m}{1-(1-\lambda)^{\nu}}\Big)^{\mathbf{N}}\,\|f\|_{E}\quad\text{ if }\lambda:=\frac{|E|}{|K|}>\varrho.

Proof.

We may assume that
K
contains the origin; indeed, the statement of
Theorem

6.3
is invariant under translations.
Let
(K_{w},a_{w},\Upsilon_{w})_{w\in K}
be an admissible collection for
K
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
characteristic
(d,q,\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\delta_{3})
.
Let
\sigma,\eta>0
and
\varrho\in[0,1)
be the constants provided by
Proposition

6.1
.
Assume that
E\subseteq K
is Lebesgue measurable and
\lambda:=\frac{|E|}{|K|}>\varrho
.

Fix
w\in K
such that
|f(w)|_{1}=\|f\|_{K}
.
The preimage
\Upsilon_{w}^{-1}(E)\subseteq[0,\infty)^{n}
is Lebesgue measurable
(indeed,
E
is the union of a
F_{\sigma}
-set and a set
of measure zero and the preimage under
\Upsilon_{w}
of the latter is again of measure zero).

By
Lemma

4.3
, there exists an affine curve
\gamma_{w}:[0,1]\to K_{w}
such that
\gamma_{w}(0)=a_{w}

and
\gamma_{w}^{-1}(\Upsilon_{w}^{-1}(E)\cap K_{w})
contains a Lebesgue measurable subset
F_{0}
satisfying

(6.5)

\mathcal{L}^{1}(F_{0})\geq 1-\Big(1-\frac{|\Upsilon_{w}^{-1}(E)\cap K_{w}|}{|K_{w}|}\Big)^{1/n}.

By
Proposition

6.1
, we conclude that

(6.6)

\mathcal{L}^{1}(F_{0})\geq 1-\Big(1-\frac{1}{\eta(1-\lambda)^{1/\sigma}+1}\Big)^{1/n}.

Consider the function
G_{q}:[0,1]\to[0,1]
,
G_{q}(t)=t^{q}
, and
F_{1}:=G_{q}^{-1}(F_{0})
. Then
(by
[
LL01
, Theorem 1.13]
and
[
Pie15
, Lemma 7.1]
)

\mathcal{L}^{1}(F_{0})\leq 1-(1-\mathcal{L}^{1}(F_{1}))^{q}

and, hence, using (
6.6
),

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{1}(F_{1})\geq 1-(1-\mathcal{L}^{1}(F_{0}))^{1/q}\geq 1-\Big(1-\frac{1}{\eta(1-\lambda)^{1/\sigma}+1}\Big)^{1/(nq)}.

Thus, by
Lemma

6.2
, there exists
\nu>0
such that

(6.7)

\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}^{1}(F_{1})}\leq\frac{1}{1-(1-\lambda)^{\nu}}.

Replacing
\nu
by
\min\{\nu,1\}
, we may assume that
\nu\in(0,1]
.

The composite
p:=\Upsilon_{w}\circ\gamma_{w}\circ G_{q}:[0,1]\to K
is a polynomial curve of degree at most
d
,
by
Definition

4.5
(4)
(cf.
[
Pie15
, Lemma 9.6]
).

We claim that
f\circ p:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is
(a_{K}\,\mu|^{\mathbf{N}},m\,M_{0})
-smooth.
Let
\ell:[-1,1]\to[0,1]
be given by
\ell(t)=\frac{1}{2}(t+1)
.
Then
p\circ\ell:[-1,1]\to K
is a polynomial curve of degree at most
d
.
Since
\|f\|_{K}=|f(w)|_{1}=|f(p(0))|_{1}
,
we have

(6.8)

b_{0}\leq\|f\circ p\|_{[0,1]}=\|f\|_{K}\leq M_{0}.

By
Proposition

5.2
,
for
1\leq k\leq\mathbf{N}
,

\displaystyle\|(f\circ p)^{(k)}\|_{[0,1]}

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in[0,1]}\sum_{i=1}^{m}|(f_{i}\circ p)^{(k)}(x)|=\sup_{x\in[0,1]}\sum_{i=1}^{m}|(f_{i}\circ p\circ\ell\circ\ell^{-1})^{(k)}(x)|

\displaystyle=2^{k}\sup_{x\in[-1,1]}\sum_{i=1}^{m}|(f_{i}\circ p\circ\ell)^{(k)}(x)|\leq m\,M_{0}\,a_{K}^{k}\,\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\cdots\mu_{k},

where
a_{K}
is defined in (
4.6
).
This implies the claim.

In view of
Remark

2.10
(1),
we may apply
Theorem

4.1
to
f\circ p:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
F_{1}\subseteq[0,1]
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{K}=\|f\circ p\|_{[0,1]}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}\,\Big(\frac{m}{\mathcal{L}^{1}(F_{1})}\Big)^{\mathbf{N}}\,\|f\circ p\|_{F_{1}}.

By (
6.7
) and
f(p(F_{1}))=f(\Upsilon_{w}(\gamma_{w}(F_{0})))\subseteq f(\Upsilon_{w}(\Upsilon_{w}^{-1}(E)\cap K_{w}))\subseteq f(E)
,
the proof is complete.
∎

6.3.
Proof of
Theorem

4.13

Let
L\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body and

K\in\mathscr{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

such that
K\subseteq L
.
Let
\mu^{|\infty}=(\mu_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be an admissible weight satisfying
\mu_{j}\geq j
for
j\geq 1
.
Let
M_{0}>b_{0}>0
. Let
m\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
.
Assume that
(\mu^{|\infty},m\,M_{0},a_{K},b_{0})
is admissible and let

\mathbf{N}=\mathbf{N}(\mu^{|\infty},m\,M_{0},a_{K},b_{0})
.
Let
f:L\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
be
(\mu^{|\mathbf{N}},M_{0})
-smooth such that
\|f\|_{K}\geq b_{0}
.
Let
E\subseteq K
be Lebesgue measurable with
|E|>0
and set
\lambda:=\frac{|E|}{|K|}
.

We may assume without loss of generality that
L
is the convex hull of
K
and hence
\operatorname{diam}(L)=\operatorname{diam}(K)
.
Indeed the convex hull
\operatorname{co}(K)
of
K
is contained in
L
and the restriction
f|_{\operatorname{co}(K)}

is
(\mu^{|\mathbf{N}},M_{0})
-smooth.

Let
\nu\in(0,1]
and
\varrho\in[0,1)
be the constants associated with
K
in
Theorem

6.3
.
Assume that
\varrho>0
(otherwise we are done).
If
\lambda\in(\varrho,1]
, then the statement follows from
Theorem

6.3
.
Suppose that
\lambda\in(0,\varrho]
.

Set
\mu:=\frac{|K|}{|L|}\in(0,1]
.
There is a unique
\epsilon_{0}\in(0,1]
such that
1-\mu\varrho=(1-\varrho)^{\epsilon_{0}}
.
Thus
u(t):=(1-t)^{\epsilon_{0}}-1+\mu t
vanishes at
t=0
and
t=\varrho
and is concave in
[0,\varrho]

since
u^{\prime\prime}(t)=\epsilon_{0}(\epsilon_{0}-1)(1-t)^{\epsilon_{0}-2}\leq 0
.
It follows that

(6.9)

(1-\mu t)\leq(1-t)^{\epsilon_{0}}\quad\text{ for }t\in[0,\varrho].

Since
\operatorname{diam}(L)=\operatorname{diam}(K)\leq a_{K}
(see (
4.6
)),
(\mu^{|\infty},m\,M_{0},\operatorname{diam}(L),b)
is admissible and

\mathbf{N}^{\prime}=\mathbf{N}(\mu^{|\infty},m\,M_{0},\operatorname{diam}(L),b)\leq\mathbf{N}(\mu^{|\infty},m\,M_{0},a_{K},b)=\mathbf{N}

as well as
\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}^{\prime}}\leq\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}
(see
Remark

2.10
(2)).
Thus, by
Theorem

4.2
and (

),

\displaystyle\|f\|_{K}\leq\|f\|_{L}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}^{\prime}}\,\Big(\frac{m}{1-(1-\mu\lambda)^{1/n}}\Big)^{\mathbf{N}^{\prime}}\,\|f\|_{E}\leq\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}\,\Big(\frac{m}{1-(1-\lambda)^{\epsilon_{0}/n}}\Big)^{\mathbf{N}}\,\|f\|_{E}.

It follows that
Theorem

4.13
holds with
\nu_{K}:=\min\{\nu,\epsilon_{0}/n\}
,
which depends only on
K
, because we assumed that
L=\operatorname{co}(K)
.

7.2.
Sublevel sets

Let
K_{t}:=\{x\in K:|f(x)|\leq t\}
denote the sublevel set of

Theorem 7.2
.

We have

(7.1)

|K_{t}|\leq\frac{|K|}{\nu_{K}}\,\Big(\frac{\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}\,t}{\|f\|_{K}}\Big)^{1/\mathbf{N}},\quad t>0.

Proof.

7.3.
Comparison of
L^{p}
-norms

Recall that the
distribution function

d_{f}
of
a Borel measurable function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\supseteq X\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by

d_{f}(t):=|\{x\in X:|f(x)|>t\}|

and the
decreasing rearrangement

f^{*}
of
f
by

f^{*}(y):=\inf\{t>0:d_{f}(t)\leq y\}.

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
E\subseteq K
be a Lebesgue measurable set with
|E|>0
.
Then

(7.2)

(f|_{E})^{*}(|E|\,\lambda)\geq\frac{\|f\|_{K}}{\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}}\Big(\frac{\nu_{K}\,|E|\,(1-\lambda)}{|K|}\Big)^{\mathbf{N}},\quad\lambda\in(0,1).

Proof.

For
E_{t}:=\{x\in E:|f(x)|\leq t\}
,
we have

d_{f|_{E}}(t)=|E|\,\lambda_{t},\quad\text{ where }\lambda_{t}:=1-\frac{|E_{t}|}{|E|}.

Let
s_{\lambda}
denote the right-hand side of (
7.2
).
If
t\in(0,s_{\lambda})
then, by
Theorem

7.2
,

\displaystyle|E|(1-\lambda_{t})=|E_{t}|\leq\frac{|K|}{\nu_{K}}\,\Big(\frac{\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}\,t}{\|f\|_{K}}\Big)^{1/\mathbf{N}}<\frac{|K|}{\nu_{K}}\,\Big(\frac{\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}\,s_{\lambda}}{\|f\|_{K}}\Big)^{1/\mathbf{N}}=|E|(1-\lambda),

implying that
d_{f|_{E}}(t)=|E|\,\lambda_{t}>|E|\,\lambda
, for
t\in(0,s_{\lambda})
.
Thus,
(f|_{E})^{*}(|E|\,\lambda)\geq s_{\lambda}
and (
7.2
) is proved.
∎

Let us write

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p}(E)}^{\sharp}

\displaystyle:=\Big(\frac{1}{|E|}\int_{E}|f(x)|^{p}\,dx\Big)^{1/p},\quad 0<p<\infty,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(E)}^{\sharp}

\displaystyle:=\esssup_{E}|f|,

for the normalized
L^{p}
-norms (or quasinorms if
0<p<1
) of
f
on a Lebesgue measurable set
E
with
0<|E|<\infty
.
Then, as a consequence of Hölder’s inequality,

\|f\|_{L^{q}(E)}^{\sharp}\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}(E)}^{\sharp},\quad\text{ if }0<q\leq p\leq\infty.

In the setup of
Section

7.1
, also opposite inequalities hold.

Theorem 7.4
.

Let
E\subseteq K
be a Lebesgue measurable subset with
|E|>0
.
Then, for all
0<q<p\leq\infty
,

(7.3)

\|f\|_{L^{p}(K)}^{\sharp}\leq\Big(\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}\Big(\frac{|K|}{\nu_{K}\,|E|}\Big)^{\mathbf{N}}\Big)^{1-\frac{q}{p}}(q\,\mathbf{N}+1)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}(\|f\|_{L^{q}(K)}^{\sharp})^{\frac{q}{p}}(\|f\|_{L^{q}(E)}^{\sharp})^{1-\frac{q}{p}}.

In particular,

(7.4)

\|f\|_{L^{p}(K)}^{\sharp}\leq\Big(\frac{\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}}{\nu_{K}^{\mathbf{N}}}\Big)^{1-\frac{q}{p}}(q\,\mathbf{N}+1)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{q}(K)}^{\sharp}

and

(7.5)

\|f\|_{L^{p}(K)}^{\sharp}\leq\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}\Big(\frac{|K|}{\nu_{K}\,|E|}\Big)^{\mathbf{N}}\,(q\,\mathbf{N}+1)^{\frac{1}{q}}\,\|f\|_{L^{q}(E)}^{\sharp}.

Proof.

Both (
7.4
) and (
7.5
) are simple consequences of (
7.3
):
it suffices to specialize to
E=K

and to use
\|f\|_{L^{q}(K)}^{\sharp}\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}(K)}^{\sharp}
, respectively.

Let us prove (
7.3
).
By
Lemma

7.3
,

(7.6)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|E|}\int_{E}|f(x)|^{q}\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|E|}\int_{0}^{|E|}\big((f|_{E})^{*}(y)\big)^{q}\,dy=\int_{0}^{1}\big((f|_{E})^{*}(|E|\,\lambda)\big)^{q}\,d\lambda

(7.7)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\|f\|_{K}^{q}}{\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}^{q}}\Big(\frac{\nu_{K}\,|E|}{|K|}\Big)^{q\mathbf{N}}\int_{0}^{1}(1-\lambda)^{q\mathbf{N}}\,d\lambda

(7.8)

\displaystyle=\frac{\|f\|_{K}^{q}}{\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}^{q}}\Big(\frac{\nu_{K}\,|E|}{|K|}\Big)^{q\mathbf{N}}\frac{1}{q\mathbf{N}+1}

and thus

(7.9)

\|f\|_{K}\leq\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}\Big(\frac{|K|}{\nu_{K}\,|E|}\Big)^{\mathbf{N}}\,(q\,\mathbf{N}+1)^{\frac{1}{q}}\,\|f\|_{L^{q}(E)}^{\sharp}

which is (
7.3
) for
p=\infty
.

If
0<q<p<\infty
, then (
7.9
) implies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|K|}\int_{K}|f(x)|^{p}\,dx\leq\|f\|_{K}^{p-q}\frac{1}{|K|}\int_{K}|f(x)|^{q}\,dx=\|f\|_{K}^{p-q}\,(\|f\|_{L^{q}(K)}^{\sharp})^{q}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}\Big(\frac{|K|}{\nu_{K}\,|E|}\Big)^{\mathbf{N}}(q\,\mathbf{N}+1)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big)^{p-q}\,(\|f\|_{L^{q}(K)}^{\sharp})^{q}(\|f\|_{L^{q}(E)}^{\sharp})^{p-q}

from which (
7.3
) follows easily.
∎

7.4.
Integrability of
|f|^{-\alpha}

Theorem 7.5
.

The integral

\int_{K}|f(x)|^{-\alpha}\,dx

converges, provided that
0<\alpha<1/\mathbf{N}
.

Proof.

By
[
Rai23
, Theorem 4.6]
, the zero set
Z_{f}
of
f:L\to\mathbb{R}
has Lebesgue measure zero.
Thus
K\ni x\mapsto|f(x)|^{-\alpha}
is defined almost everywhere and measurable.
A computation similar to (
7.8
) (using
Lemma

7.3
for
E=K
) shows

\displaystyle\int_{K}|f(x)|^{-\alpha}\,dx

\displaystyle=|K|\int_{0}^{1}\big((f|_{K})^{*}(|K|\,\lambda)\big)^{-\alpha}\,d\lambda

\displaystyle\leq|K|\,\Big(\frac{\|f\|_{K}\,\nu_{K}^{\mathbf{N}}}{\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}}\Big)^{-\alpha}\int_{0}^{1}(1-\lambda)^{-\alpha\mathbf{N}}\,d\lambda.

The integral on the right-hand side converges precisely if

.
∎

7.5.
On the mean oscillation of
\log|f|

Recall that the
mean oscillation
of a locally integrable function
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\supseteq X\to\mathbb{R}

over a Lebesgue measurable set
E\subseteq X
with
0<|E|<\infty
is given by

\operatorname{mo}_{E}(g):=\frac{1}{|E|}\int_{E}|g(x)-g_{E}|\,dx,

where
g_{E}
is the average of
g
over
E
,

g_{E}:=\frac{1}{|E|}\int_{E}g(x)\,dx.

In the Setup
7.1
, we have the following bound for the mean oscillation of
\log|f|
.

Corollary 7.6
.

For each Lebesgue measurable set
E\subseteq K
with
|E|>0
,

(7.10)

\operatorname{mo}_{E}(\log|f|)\leq 2\log\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}+2\mathbf{N}\,\Big(1+\log\Big(\frac{|K|}{\nu_{K}\,|E|}\Big)\Big).

Proof.

Observe that

(7.11)

\operatorname{mo}_{E}(\log|f|)\leq\frac{2}{|E|}\int_{E}\Big|\log\frac{|f(x)|}{\|f\|_{K}}\Big|\,dx.

Indeed,

\displaystyle\Big|\log|f|-\frac{1}{|E|}\int_{E}\log|f|\,dx\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\Big|\log|f|-\log\|f\|_{K}\Big|+\Big|\log\|f\|_{K}-\frac{1}{|E|}\int_{E}\log|f|\,dx\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\log\frac{|f|}{\|f\|_{K}}\Big|+\Big|\frac{1}{|E|}\int_{E}\log\|f\|_{K}-\log|f|\,dx\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\Big|\log\frac{|f|}{\|f\|_{K}}\Big|+\frac{1}{|E|}\int_{E}\Big|\log\frac{|f|}{\|f\|_{K}}\Big|\,dx.

By
Lemma

7.3
,

(7.12)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|E|}\int_{E}\Big|\log\frac{|f(x)|}{\|f\|_{K}}\Big|\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|E|}\int_{0}^{|E|}\Big|\log\frac{(f|_{E})^{*}(y)}{\|f\|_{K}}\Big|\,dy=\int_{0}^{1}\Big|\log\frac{(f|_{E})^{*}(|E|\,\lambda)}{\|f\|_{K}}\Big|\,d\lambda

(7.13)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}-\log\Big(\frac{1}{\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}}\Big)-\mathbf{N}\,\log\Big(\frac{\nu_{K}\,|E|\,(1-\lambda)}{|K|}\Big)\,d\lambda

(7.14)

\displaystyle=\log\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}+\mathbf{N}\,\Big(1+\log\Big(\frac{|K|}{\nu_{K}\,|E|}\Big)\Big)

and (
7.10
) follows.
∎

Corollary

7.6
provides an explicit bound on how fast
\operatorname{mo}_{E}(\log|f|)
can grow
as
|E|
tends to zero. In the following corollary, we derive a different bound, where
the dependence is integrated in the integer
\mathbf{N}
. We only consider complex bodies and balls therein, for simplicity.

Corollary 7.7
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body.
Let
\mu^{|\infty}=(\mu_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be an admissible quasianalytic weight.
Let

be a

-smooth function which is not identically zero and where

is such that

for each ball

,

(7.15)

\operatorname{mo}_{B}(\log|f|)\leq 2\log\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}_{B}}+2(1+\log n)\,\mathbf{N}_{B},

where
\mathbf{N}_{B}:=\mathbf{N}(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0},\operatorname{diam}(K),\|f\|_{B})

Proof.

Since
M_{0}>\|f\|_{K}
, we have
\mathbf{N}_{B}\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}
, by

2.11
,
and thus the constant
\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}_{B}}>0

(applied to

(f|_{B})^{*}(|B|\,\lambda)\geq\frac{\|f\|_{B}}{\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}_{B}}}\Big(\frac{1-\lambda}{n}\Big)^{\mathbf{N}_{B}}.

Then, the computation (
7.14
)
implies the assertion.
∎

Remark 7.8
.

We refer to

A.1
,

A.3
, and

We cannot infer that
\log|f|
is BMO, but we have the following.

Corollary 7.9
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body.
Let
\mu^{|\infty}=(\mu_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be an admissible quasianalytic weight.
Let
M_{0}>b_{0}>0
.
Let
f:K\to\mathbb{R}
be a
(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0})
-smooth function such that
M_{0}>\|f\|_{K}
. Then,
for each ball
B\subseteq K
satisfying
\|f\|_{B}\geq b_{0}
,

(7.16)

\operatorname{mo}_{B}(\log|f|)\leq 2\log\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}+2(1+\log n)\,\mathbf{N},

where
\mathbf{N}:=\mathbf{N}(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0},\operatorname{diam}(K),b_{0})
.
In particular, for each
b_{0}>0
,
the restriction of
\log|f|
to the set
\{x\in K:|f(x)|\geq b_{0}\}
is BMO.

Proof.

Since
\mathbf{N}(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0},\operatorname{diam}(K),\|f\|_{B})\leq\mathbf{N}(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0},\operatorname{diam}(K),b_{0})
,
this is a consequence of
Corollary

7.7
.
∎

Remark 7.10
.

A locally integrable function
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is BMO if and only if
g=C_{1}\,\log\omega

for some constant
C_{1}
and some nonnegative
\omega:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
that satisfies a reverse Hölder inequality
in the sense that there exist
p=p(\omega)>1
and
C_{2}=C_{2}(\omega)>0
such that

\|\omega\|^{\sharp}_{L^{p}(B)}\leq C_{2}\,\|\omega\|^{\sharp}_{L^{1}(B)},

for all balls
B
. See
[
Ste93
, Chapter V]
. In our reverse Hölder inequality (
7.4
) for
K=B
,
the constant depends on
B
(through
\mathbf{N}
).

8.
Inequalities of Łojasiewicz type

The goal of this section is to establish several inequalities of Łojasiewicz type with explicit constants, which
depend on
\mathbf{N}
and the geometry of the underlying admissible set
K
.

8.1.
Thickness of a set

Let us recall the definition of thickness,
following
[
Pie22
, Definition 1.16]
.

Definition 8.1

(Thick sets)
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a Lebesgue measurable nonempty set.
We say that
K
is
(c_{K},\theta_{K})
-thick
if there exist constants

c_{K},\theta_{K}>0
such that

(8.1)

|K\cap B(x,r)|\geq c_{K}\,r^{\theta_{K}}

for all
x\in K
and
0<r\leq 1
.

Every compact fat nonempty
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
that is definable in a polynomially bounded o-minimal
expansion of the real field is
(c_{K},\theta_{K})
-thick
for some
c_{K},\theta_{K}>0
. This follows
easily from the Łojasiewicz inequality that is available for polynomially bounded o-minimal structures;
see e.g. the proof of
[
Pie22
, Theorem 1.18]
.

Remark 8.2
.

Each convex body
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
(c_{K},\theta_{K})
-thick with

(8.2)

\theta_{K}=n\quad\text{ and }\quad c_{k}=\omega_{n}\Big(\frac{\rho}{H}\Big)^{n},

where
H:=\max\{\operatorname{diam}(K),1\}
and
\rho>0
is the radius of the largest ball contained in
K
.
Indeed, there exists a ball
B(x_{0},\rho)\subseteq K
.
For given
x\in K
and
0<r\leq 1
, consider the ball
B:=\{x+\frac{r}{H}(y-x):y\in B(x_{0},\rho)\}
.
Then
B\subseteq K
, by convexity, and
B\subseteq B(x,r)
since

\tfrac{r}{H}|y-x|\leq\tfrac{r}{H}\operatorname{diam}(K)\leq r
. It follows that

|K\cap B(x,r)|\geq|B|=\omega_{n}(\tfrac{\rho}{H})^{n}\,r^{n}.

8.2.
Inequalities of Łojasiewicz type with explicit constants

The following theorem is formulated for vector-valued maps, since among other things we will apply it to the gradient.

Theorem 8.3
.

Let
L\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body and

K\in\mathscr{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

with
K\subseteq L
.
Assume that
K
is
(c_{K},\theta_{K})
-thick.
Let
\mu^{|\infty}=(\mu_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be an admissible weight satisfying
\mu_{j}\geq j
for
j\geq 1
.
Let
M_{0}>b_{0}>0
.
Let
m\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
.
Assume that
(\mu^{|\infty},m\,M_{0},a_{K},b_{0})
is admissible and let

\mathbf{N}=\mathbf{N}(\mu^{|\infty},m\,M_{0},a_{K},b_{0}).

Let
f:L\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a
(\mu^{|\mathbf{N}},M_{0})
-smooth map
satisfying
\|f\|_{K}\geq b_{0}
.
For any function
g:K\to[-1,1]
,
we have

(8.3)

\|f\|_{B(x,\frac{|g(x)|}{2})\cap K}\geq\frac{\|f\|_{K}}{\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}}\,\Big(\frac{c_{K}\,\nu_{K}}{m\,2^{\theta_{K}}\,|K|}\Big)^{\mathbf{N}}\,|g(x)|^{\theta_{K}\mathbf{N}},

for all
x\in K
.

Note that we do not require that
f|_{K}^{-1}(0)\subseteq g^{-1}(0)
.

Proof.

If
x\in g^{-1}(0)
, then (
8.3
) holds trivially; in that case, the left-hand side of (
8.3
)
equals
|f(x)|_{1}
.
Fix
x\in K\setminus g^{-1}(0)
and set

B:=B(x,\frac{r}{2})\cap K
for
r:=|g(x)|
. Note that
r\in(0,1]
.

We formulated
Theorem

7.2
for single-valued function, but it remains true in the vector-valued setting
with the same proof. Thus, for
t>0
,

(8.4)

|K_{t}|\leq\frac{m\,|K|}{\nu_{K}}\,\Big(\frac{\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}\,t}{\|f\|_{K}}\Big)^{1/\mathbf{N}},

where
K_{t}:=\{x\in K:|f(x)|_{1}\leq t\}
.

Let
T
be the set of all
t>0
such that

(8.5)

\frac{m\,|K|}{\nu_{K}}\,\Big(\frac{\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}\,t}{\|f\|_{K}}\Big)^{1/\mathbf{N}}<c_{K}\Big(\frac{r}{2}\Big)^{\theta_{K}}.

That means
t\in T
if and only if

(8.6)

0<t<\frac{\|f\|_{K}}{\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}}\,\Big(\frac{c_{K}\,\nu_{K}}{m\,2^{\theta_{K}}\,|K|}\Big)^{\mathbf{N}}\,r^{\theta_{K}\mathbf{N}}.

Since
|B|\geq c_{K}(\frac{r}{2})^{\theta_{K}}
because
K
is
(c_{K},\theta_{K})
-thick,
we have
B\not\subseteq K_{t}
and hence
\|f\|_{B}>t

for all
t\in T
, by (
8.4
) and (
8.5
).
In view of (
8.6
), this implies (
8.3
).
∎

Let us apply this to the distance function.

Corollary 8.4
.

In the setup of
Theorem

8.3
with
m=1
, we have, for all
x\in K
,

(8.7)

\|f\|_{B(x,\frac{r(x)}{2})\cap K}\geq\frac{\|f\|_{K}}{\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}}\,\Big(\frac{c_{K}\,\nu_{K}}{2^{\theta_{K}}\,|K|}\Big)^{\mathbf{N}}\,r(x)^{\theta_{K}\mathbf{N}},

where

(8.8)

r(x):=\min\{\operatorname{dist}(x,f|_{K}^{-1}(0)),1\},\quad x\in K,

or, alternatively,

(8.9)

r(x):=\frac{\operatorname{dist}(x,f|_{K}^{-1}(0))}{\max\{\operatorname{diam}(K),1\}},\quad x\in K.s

8.3.
Order of vanishing

The order of vanishing of a continuous function
f:K\to\mathbb{R}
at
x\in K
is defined by

(8.10)

\operatorname{ord}_{x}f=\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{\log\|f\|_{B(x,r)\cap K}}{\log r}.

If
f
is smooth and
K
is open, then
\operatorname{ord}_{x}f
is the order of the smallest nonzero partial derivative of
f
at
x
.

Corollary 8.5
.

In the setup of
Theorem

8.3
with
m=1
,
we have
\operatorname{ord}_{x}f\leq\theta_{K}\mathbf{N}
for all
x\in K
.

Proof.

Applying
Theorem

8.3
to
g\equiv r
for
r\in(0,1)
, gives

\|f\|_{B(x,\frac{r}{2})\cap K}\geq\frac{\|f\|_{K}}{\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}}\,\Big(\frac{c_{K}\,\nu_{K}}{|K|}\Big)^{\mathbf{N}}\,\Big(\frac{r}{2}\Big)^{\theta_{K}\mathbf{N}},

for all
x\in K
.
This implies the assertion.
∎

8.4.
Inequalities for the gradient

We will deduce several inequalities for the gradient.

8.4.1.
First gradient inequality

The first is a direct application of
Theorem

8.3
for
\operatorname{grad}f
and
f
in place of
f
and
g
.
It requires a “shift” of the weight
\mu^{|\infty}
, see (
2.6
).

Corollary 8.6
.

Let
L\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body and

K\in\mathscr{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

such that
K\subseteq L
.
Assume that
K
is
(c_{K},\theta_{K})
-thick.
Let
\mu^{|\infty}=(\mu_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be an admissible weight satisfying
\mu_{j}\geq j
for
j\geq 1
.
Let
M_{1}=M_{0}\mu_{1}>b_{1}>0
.
Assume that
(\mu^{|\infty}_{+1},n\,M_{1},a_{K},b_{1})
is admissible and let

\mathbf{N}=\mathbf{N}(\mu^{|\infty}_{+1},n\,M_{1},a_{K},b_{1}).

Let
f:L\to\mathbb{R}
be a
(\mu^{|\mathbf{N}+1},M_{0})
-smooth function satisfying
\|f\|_{K}\leq 1
and

\|\operatorname{grad}f\|_{K}\geq b_{1}
.
Then, for all
x\in K
,

(8.11)

\|\operatorname{grad}f\|_{B(x,\frac{|f(x)|}{2})\cap K}\geq\frac{\|\operatorname{grad}f\|_{K}}{\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}}\,\Big(\frac{c_{K}\,\nu_{K}}{n\,2^{\theta_{K}}\,|K|}\Big)^{\mathbf{N}}\,|f(x)|^{\theta_{K}\mathbf{N}}.

We do not require
(\operatorname{grad}f|_{K})^{-1}(0)\subseteq f|_{K}^{-1}(0)
.

Proof.

It is easy to see that
\operatorname{grad}f:L\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
(\mu^{|\infty}_{+1},M_{1})
-smooth.
Thus the statement follows from
Theorem

8.3
.
∎

Remark 8.7
.

Alternatively, using that
\|\operatorname{grad}f\|_{K}\geq b_{1}
implies
\|\partial_{j_{0}}f\|_{K}\geq b_{1}/n
for some
j_{0}

and
Theorem

8.3
for
\partial_{j_{0}}f
implies the following version of (
8.11
):

(8.12)

\|\operatorname{grad}f\|_{B(x,\frac{|f(x)|}{2})\cap K}\geq\frac{b_{1}}{\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}\,n}\,\Big(\frac{c_{K}\,\nu_{K}}{2^{\theta_{K}}\,|K|}\Big)^{\mathbf{N}}\,|f(x)|^{\theta_{K}\mathbf{N}}.

with
\mathbf{N}=\mathbf{N}(\mu^{|\infty}_{+1},n\,M_{1},a_{K},b_{1})
.

8.4.2.
Second gradient inequality

The constants in (
8.11
) are completely explicit, but the exponent
\theta_{K}\mathbf{N}
of
|f(x)|

is typically large. In the analytic or quasianalytic setting (see
Lemma

8.8
),
one has an (unspecified) exponent
\alpha\in(0,1)
.
In the following, we discuss gradient inequalities with explicit constants and
small
exponents of
|f(x)|
.

Lemma 8.8

(
[
BM04
, Theorem 6.3]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{M}
be a quasianalytic Denjoy–Carleman class satisfying the properties listed in
Example

3.4
.
Let
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open and
f\in\mathcal{C}_{M}(U)
.
Let
K\subseteq U
be compact and assume that
(\operatorname{grad}f|_{K})^{-1}(0)\subseteq f|_{K}^{-1}(0)
.
Then there exist
c>0
and
\alpha\in[0,1)
such that

|\operatorname{grad}f(x)|\geq c\,|f(x)|^{\alpha}

in a neighborhood of
K
.

Theorem 8.9
.

Let
L\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body and
K\in\mathscr{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
K\subseteq\operatorname{int}(L)
.
Assume that
K
is
(c_{K},\theta_{K})
-thick.
Let
\mu^{|\infty}=(\mu_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be a quasianalytic admissible weight satisfying
\mu_{j}\geq j
for
j\geq 1

and all the properties listed in
Example

3.4
.
Let
M_{0}\geq 1
and
M_{0}>b_{0}>0

and

\mathbf{N}=\mathbf{N}(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0},a_{K},b_{0}).

Let
f:L\to\mathbb{R}
be a
(\mu^{|\mathbf{N}},M_{0})
-smooth function
satisfying
\|f\|_{K}\geq b_{0}
.
Assume that
(\operatorname{grad}f|_{K})^{-1}(0)\subseteq f|_{K}^{-1}(0)
.
Then,

(8.13)

|\operatorname{grad}f(x)|\geq\frac{1}{2\sqrt{M_{2}}}\Big(\frac{\|f\|_{K}}{2\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\theta_{K}\mathbf{N}}}\,\Big(\frac{c_{K}\,\nu_{K}}{|K|}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\theta_{K}}}\,|f(x)|^{\frac{3}{2}-\frac{1}{\theta_{K}\mathbf{N}}}

in a neighborhood of
f|_{K}^{-1}(0)
in
K
.

Proof.

By
Lemma

8.8
, there exist
c>0
and
\alpha\in[0,1)
such that, for
x\in K
,

\frac{|f(x)|}{|\operatorname{grad}f(x)|}=\frac{|f(x)|^{\alpha}|f(x)|^{1-\alpha}}{|\operatorname{grad}f(x)|}\leq\frac{|f(x)|^{1-\alpha}}{c}.

Thus there is a neighborhood
U
in
K
of
f|_{K}^{-1}(0)
such that

(8.14)

|f(x)|\leq 1\quad\text{ and }\quad\frac{|f(x)|}{|\operatorname{grad}f(x)|}\leq 1\quad\text{ for }x\in U.

In particular, the function
g:K\to\mathbb{R}
which is
0
on
(\operatorname{grad}f|_{K})^{-1}(0)
and

(8.15)

g(x):=\frac{|f(x)|^{3/2}}{\sqrt{M_{2}}\,|\operatorname{grad}f(x)|}\quad\text{ for }x\not\in(\operatorname{grad}f|_{K})^{-1}(0),

is well-defined on
K
and satisfies
0\leq g\leq 1
on
U
.

Let
x\in U
and
y\in B(x,\frac{g(x)}{2})\cap L
. Then

\displaystyle|f(y)-f(x)|

\displaystyle\leq|\langle\operatorname{grad}f(x),y-x\rangle|+\frac{M_{2}}{2}|x-y|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\sqrt{M_{2}}}|f(x)|^{3/2}+\frac{1}{8}|f(x)|

and consequently (noting that
M_{2}\geq 1
),

(8.16)

|f(y)|\leq 2\,|f(x)|.

By
Theorem

8.3
applied to the choice (
8.15
) of
g
and (
8.16
),
for
x\in U
,

\displaystyle 2\,|f(x)|\geq\|f\|_{B(x,\frac{g(x)}{2})\cap K}\geq\frac{\|f\|_{K}}{\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}}\,\Big(\frac{c_{K}\,\nu_{K}}{2^{\theta_{K}}\,|K|}\Big)^{\mathbf{N}}\,\Big(\frac{|f(x)|^{3/2}}{\sqrt{M_{2}}\,|\operatorname{grad}f(x)|}\Big)^{\theta_{K}\mathbf{N}},

implying

(8.17)

|\operatorname{grad}f(x)|^{\theta_{K}\mathbf{N}}\geq\frac{\|f\|_{K}}{2\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}\,M_{2}^{\theta_{K}\mathbf{N}/2}}\,\Big(\frac{c_{K}\,\nu_{K}}{2^{\theta_{K}}\,|K|}\Big)^{\mathbf{N}}\,|f(x)|^{\frac{3}{2}\theta_{K}\mathbf{N}-1}.

This gives (
8.13
).
∎

Corollary 8.10
.

In the setup of
Theorem

8.9
,

(8.18)

\|\operatorname{grad}f\|_{B_{x}}\geq\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{2\|f\|_{K}}{3\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\theta_{K}\mathbf{N}}}\,\Big(\frac{c_{K}\,\nu_{K}}{|K|}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\theta_{K}}}\,|f(x)|^{1-\frac{1}{\theta_{K}\mathbf{N}}}

for
x
in a neighborhood of
f|_{K}^{-1}(0)
in
K
, where
B_{x}
is a neighborhood of
x

in
L
that shrinks to
x
if
x
tends to
f|_{K}^{-1}(0)
.

Proof.

We follow the proof of
Theorem

8.9

and replace (
8.15
) by

(8.19)

g(x):=\frac{|f(x)|}{|\operatorname{grad}f(x)|}.

Then the function
g:K\to\mathbb{R}
is well-defined and satisfies
0\leq g\leq 1
on
U
.
Moreover,
g(x)\to 0
as
x
tends to
f|_{K}^{-1}(0)
.

Set
B_{x}:=B(x,\frac{g(x)}{2})\cap L
.
For
x\in U
and
y\in B_{x}^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle|f(y)-f(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{z\in B_{x}}|\operatorname{grad}f(z)|\frac{|f(x)|}{2\,|\operatorname{grad}f(x)|}\leq\frac{\|\operatorname{grad}f\|_{B_{x}}}{2\,|\operatorname{grad}f(x)|}\,|f(x)|,

(using
|\operatorname{grad}f(z)|\leq|\operatorname{grad}f(z)|_{1}
)
and thus

(8.20)

|f(y)|\leq\Big(1+\frac{\|\operatorname{grad}f\|_{B_{x}}}{2\,|\operatorname{grad}f(x)|}\Big)\,|f(x)|\leq\frac{3}{2}\,\|\operatorname{grad}f\|_{B_{x}}\,\frac{|f(x)|}{|\operatorname{grad}f(x)|}.

Then
Theorem

8.3
applied to this choice of
g
gives

(8.21)

\frac{3}{2}\,\|\operatorname{grad}f\|_{B_{x}}\,\frac{|f(x)|}{|\operatorname{grad}f(x)|}\geq\frac{\|f\|_{K}}{\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}}\,\Big(\frac{c_{K}\,\nu_{K}}{2^{\theta_{K}}\,|K|}\Big)^{\mathbf{N}}\,\Big(\frac{|f(x)|}{|\operatorname{grad}f(x)|}\Big)^{\theta_{K}\mathbf{N}},

and hence

(8.22)

\|\operatorname{grad}f\|_{B_{x}}^{\theta_{K}\mathbf{N}}\geq\frac{2\|f\|_{K}}{3\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}}\,\Big(\frac{c_{K}\,\nu_{K}}{2^{\theta_{K}}\,|K|}\Big)^{\mathbf{N}}\,|f(x)|^{\theta_{K}\mathbf{N}-1}.

which implies the statement.
∎

8.4.3.
Third gradient inequality

We close this section with another inequality of Łojasiewicz type based on the coarea formula, which we recall
in the following lemma in the form we will use (cf.
[
Fed69
]
).

Lemma 8.11
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be Lipschitz.
Then, for all Borel measurable functions
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty]
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(x)\,|\operatorname{grad}f(x)|\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{f^{-1}(y)}g\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\,dy.

Theorem 8.12
.

Let
L\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body and
K\in\mathscr{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
K\subseteq L
.
Let
\mu^{|\infty}=(\mu_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be an admissible weight satisfying
\mu_{j}\geq j
for
j\geq 1
.
Let
M_{0}>b_{0}>0
.
Assume that
(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0},a_{K},b_{0})
is admissible and let

\mathbf{N}=\mathbf{N}(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0},a_{K},b_{0}).

Let
f:L\to\mathbb{R}
be a
(\mu^{|\mathbf{N}},M_{0})
-smooth function satisfying
\|f\|_{K}\geq b_{0}
.
Assume that there exist
\alpha\geq 0
and
l,t_{0}>0
such that

(8.23)

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\{|f|=y\}\cap K)\geq l\,y^{\alpha}\quad\text{ for almost every }y\in[0,t_{0}].

Then, for
K_{t}:=\{x\in K:|f(x)|\leq t\}
,

(8.24)

\|\operatorname{grad}f\|_{K_{t}}\geq\frac{l\,\nu_{K}}{(\alpha+1)\,|K|}\Big(\frac{\|f\|_{K}}{\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}}\Big)^{1/\mathbf{N}}\,\|f\|_{K_{t}}^{\alpha+1-\frac{1}{\mathbf{N}}},\quad 0\leq t\leq t_{0}.

Proof.

First we observe that
|\operatorname{grad}|f(x)||=|\operatorname{grad}f(x)|
if
f(x)\neq 0
. The zero set of
f
has Lebesgue measure zero,
by
[
Rai23
, Theorem 4.6]
.
Set
K_{t}^{\prime}:=\{x\in K:0<|f(x)|\leq t\}
.
By
Lemma

8.11
,

\displaystyle|K_{t}^{\prime}|=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\{|f|=y\}\cap K}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{K_{t}^{\prime}}}{|\operatorname{grad}f|}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\,dy.

We conclude that, for
0\leq t\leq t_{0}
,

\displaystyle|K_{t}^{\prime}|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\|\operatorname{grad}f\|_{K_{t}^{\prime}}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\{|f|=y\}\cap K}\mathbf{1}_{K_{t}^{\prime}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\,dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\|\operatorname{grad}f\|_{K_{t}^{\prime}}}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\{|f|=y\}\cap K)\,dy\geq\frac{l\,t^{\alpha+1}}{(\alpha+1)\,\|\operatorname{grad}f\|_{K_{t}^{\prime}}}.

Combined with
Theorem

7.2
, this implies the assertion.
∎

9.
Inequalities of Harnack type

In this section, we prove two inequalities of Harnack type for
(\mu^{|N},M_{0})
-smooth functions.
The first is a simple consequence of the Remez inequality, but the involved constant is somewhat implicit
and not easy to compute.
The second inequality has a completely explicit constant but requires more work.

9.1.
First inequality of Harnack type

Proposition 9.1
.

Let
L\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body and

K\in\mathscr{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

such that
K\subseteq L
.
Let
\mu^{|\infty}=(\mu_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be an admissible weight satisfying
\mu_{j}\geq j
for
j\geq 1
.
Let
M_{0}>b_{0}>0
.
Assume that
(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0},a_{K},b_{0})
is admissible and let

\mathbf{N}=\mathbf{N}(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0},a_{K},b_{0}).

Let
f:L\to\mathbb{R}
be
(\mu^{|\mathbf{N}},M_{0})
-smooth with
\|f\|_{K}\geq b_{0}

and
\min_{x\in K}|f(x)|\geq m_{0}>0
.
Then

(9.1)

\max_{x\in K}|f(x)|\leq 2\,\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}\,\Big(\frac{|K|^{\nu_{K}}}{|K|^{\nu_{K}}-(|K|-|E_{m_{0}}|)^{\nu_{K}}}\Big)^{\mathbf{N}}\,\min_{x\in K}|f(x)|,

where
E_{m_{0}}:=\{x\in K:|f(x)|\leq\min_{x\in K}|f(x)|+m_{0}\}
.

Proof.

The set
E_{m_{0}}
is closed and
|E_{m_{0}}|>0
, by the continuity of
f
.
The statement follows from
Theorem

4.13
and
the fact that
\|f\|_{E_{m_{0}}}\leq 2\,\min_{x\in K}|f(x)|
.
∎

9.2.
Bounds for
1/f

Let
\mu^{|\infty}=(\mu_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be an admissible weight and
M_{0}>0
.
Let us assume that the sequence

(9.2)

a_{j}:=\Big(\frac{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{j}}{j!}\Big)^{1/j},\quad j\geq 1,

is almost increasing
(cf.
Example

3.4
), i.e.,
there exists a constant
H\geq 1
such that

(9.3)

a_{j}\leq H\,a_{k}\quad\text{ whenever }j\leq k.

Lemma 9.2
.

Let
\mu^{|\infty}=(\mu_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be an admissible weight satisfying (
9.3
) with constant
H\geq 1
and let
M_{0}\geq m_{0}>0
.
Let
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be a compact interval and
f:I\to\mathbb{R}

a
(\mu^{|N},M_{0})
-smooth function such that
\min_{x\in I}|f(x)|\geq m_{0}
.
Then the function
1/f:I\to\mathbb{R}
is well-defined
and
\big(H(1+\frac{2M_{0}}{m_{0}})\,\mu^{|N},\frac{2}{m_{0}}\big)
-smooth.

Proof.

We follow the proof of
[
Rud62
, Theorem 2.1]
. Recall that
N\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}\cup\{\infty\}
.
For
1\leq n<N+1
, define
r_{n}
by

(9.4)

H\,a_{n}r_{n}=\frac{m_{0}}{m_{0}+2M_{0}}.

Fix
x_{0}\in I
and consider the polynomial
P(z):=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{f^{(j)}(x_{0})}{j!}z^{j}
.
Using (
9.3
), we find that, if
|z|\leq r_{n}
,

\displaystyle|P(z)|

\displaystyle\geq|f(x_{0})|-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{|f^{(j)}(x_{0})|}{j!}|z|^{j}

\displaystyle\geq m_{0}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{M_{j}}{j!}r_{n}^{j}=m_{0}-M_{0}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(a_{j}r_{n})^{j}\geq m_{0}-M_{0}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(Ha_{n}r_{n})^{j}

\displaystyle=m_{0}-M_{0}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Big(\frac{m_{0}}{m_{0}+2M_{0}}\Big)^{j}>m_{0}-\frac{m_{0}M_{0}}{m_{0}+2M_{0}}\frac{m_{0}+2M_{0}}{2M_{0}}=\frac{m_{0}}{2}.

Since
f^{(j)}(x_{0})=P^{(j)}(0)
, for
0\leq j\leq n
, we have
(1/f)^{(n)}(x_{0})=(1/P)^{(n)}(0)

and hence, by (
9.2
) and (
9.4
),

(9.5)

\displaystyle|(1/f)^{(n)}(x_{0})|

\displaystyle=\Big|\frac{n!}{2\pi i}\int_{|z|=r_{n}}\frac{dz}{z^{n+1}P(z)}\Big|

(9.6)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{m_{0}}\frac{n!}{r_{n}^{n}}=\frac{2}{m_{0}}\Big(H\Big(1+\frac{2M_{0}}{m_{0}}\Big)\Big)^{n}\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{n}.

This implies the assertion.
∎

Lemma 9.3
.

Let
T:(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{j}\to\mathbb{R}
,

T(v_{1},\ldots,v_{j})=\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{j}=1}^{n}T_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{j}}v_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots v_{j}^{i_{j}}

be a
j
-linear symmetric map, where
v_{k}=(v_{k}^{1},\ldots,v_{k}^{n})
.
Then

\|T\|_{2}=\Big(\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{j}=1}^{n}T_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{j}}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\leq n^{\frac{j-1}{2}}\sup_{|v|\leq 1}|T(v,\ldots,v)|.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
j
. For
j=1
, we have equality.
Assume
j\geq 2
. By induction hypothesis, for the symmetric
(j-1)
-linear maps
T_{i}:=T(e_{i},\cdots)
,

\|T_{i}\|_{2}\leq n^{\frac{j-2}{2}}\sup_{|v|\leq 1}|T_{i}(v,\ldots,v)|.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|T\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{i_{1}=1}^{n}\|T_{i_{1}}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\sum_{i_{1}=1}^{n}n^{j-2}\sup_{|v|\leq 1}|T_{i_{1}}(v,\ldots,v)|^{2}\leq n^{j-1}\sup_{|v|\leq 1}|T(v,\ldots,v)|^{2},

where the last inequality is a consequence of

\sup_{|v_{1}|\leq 1,\ldots,|v_{j}|\leq 1}|T(v_{1},\ldots,v_{j})|=\sup_{|v|\leq 1}|T(v,\ldots,v)|,

see
[
BS71
, Proposition 1]
.
∎

Lemma 9.4
.

Let
\mu^{|\infty}=(\mu_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be an admissible weight satisfying (
9.3
) with constant
H\geq 1
and let
M_{0}\geq m_{0}>0
.
Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a nonempty fat compact set and
f:K\to\mathbb{R}
a
(\mu^{|N},M_{0})
-smooth function such that

\min_{x\in K}|f(x)|\geq m_{0}
.
Then the function
1/f:K\to\mathbb{R}
is well-defined and
\big(nH(1+\frac{2M_{0}}{m_{0}})\,\mu^{|N},\frac{2}{m_{0}}\big)
-smooth.

Proof.

Fix
x\in\operatorname{int}(K)
and
v\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. Let
g(t):=f(x+tv)
for
t\in I
,
where
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
is a compact interval such that
x\in x+Iv\subseteq K
.
Then
g:I\to\mathbb{R}
is
(\mu^{|N},M_{0})
-smooth. By
Lemma

9.2
,

(9.7)

|(1/g)^{(j)}(0)|=|d_{v}^{j}(1/f)(x)|\leq\frac{2}{m_{0}}H^{j}\Big(1+\frac{2M_{0}}{m_{0}}\Big)^{j}\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{j}.

Since
\sum_{|\alpha|=j}\frac{j!}{\alpha!}=n^{j}
, we have

\sum_{|\alpha|=j}\frac{j!}{\alpha!}|\partial^{\alpha}(1/f)(x)|\leq n^{j/2}\,\Big(\sum_{|\alpha|=j}\frac{j!}{\alpha!}|\partial^{\alpha}(1/f)(x)|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

and, by
Lemma

9.3
, the right-hand side is bounded by

n^{j/2}\cdot n^{(j-1)/2}\sup_{|v|\leq 1}\Big|\sum_{|\alpha|=j}\frac{j!}{\alpha!}\partial^{\alpha}(1/f)(x)v^{\alpha}\Big|=\frac{n^{j}}{\sqrt{n}}\sup_{|v|\leq 1}|d_{v}^{j}(1/f)(x)|.

It follows that, for
j\geq 1
,

\sum_{|\alpha|=j}\frac{j!}{\alpha!}|\partial^{\alpha}(1/f)(x)|\leq\frac{2}{\sqrt{n}\,m_{0}}(nH)^{j}\Big(1+\frac{2M_{0}}{m_{0}}\Big)^{j}\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{j},

for all
x\in\operatorname{int}(K)
. By continuity and since
K
is fat, the estimate holds for all
x\in K
.
Clearly,
\|1/f\|_{K}\leq 1/m_{0}
.
∎

9.3.
Second inequality of Harnack type

Theorem 9.5
.

Let
L\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body and

K\in\mathscr{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

such that
K\subseteq L
.
Let
\mu^{|\infty}=(\mu_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be an admissible weight satisfying
\mu_{j}\geq j
for
j\geq 1

as well as (
9.3
) with constant
H\geq 1
.
Let
M_{0}\geq m_{0}>0
.
Set

(9.8)

\beta_{0}:=nH(1+\tfrac{2M_{0}}{m_{0}}).

Assume that
(\mu^{|\infty},2M_{0},a_{K}\beta_{0},m_{0})
is admissible and let

\mathbf{N}=\mathbf{N}(\mu^{|\infty},2M_{0},a_{K}\beta_{0},m_{0}).

If
f:L\to\mathbb{R}
is
(\mu^{|\mathbf{N}},M_{0})
-smooth and satisfies

\min_{x\in L}|f(x)|\geq m_{0}
, then

(9.9)

\max_{x\in K}|f(x)|\leq\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}^{2}\,\Big(\frac{2^{\nu_{K}}}{2^{\nu_{K}}-1}\Big)^{2\mathbf{N}}\,\min_{x\in K}|f(x)|.

Proof.

Let us set
m_{f}:=\min_{x\in K}|f(x)|
and
M_{f}:=\max_{x\in K}|f(x)|
.
Clearly,
m_{0}\leq m_{f}\leq M_{f}\leq M_{0}
.
The set
E_{-}:=\{x\in K:|f(x)|\leq\sqrt{M_{f}m_{f}}\}
has positive measure.
By
Theorem

4.13
,

(9.10)

M_{f}\leq\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}^{\prime}}\,\Big(\frac{|K|^{\nu_{K}}}{|K|^{\nu_{K}}-(|K|-|E_{-}|)^{\nu_{K}}}\Big)^{\mathbf{N}^{\prime}}\,\sqrt{M_{f}m_{f}},

where
\mathbf{N}^{\prime}:=\mathbf{N}(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0},a_{K},m_{0})
.
Since
\mathbf{N}^{\prime}\leq\mathbf{N}
(see
Remark

2.10
), we conclude

(9.11)

M_{f}\leq\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}^{2}\,\Big(\frac{|K|^{\nu_{K}}}{|K|^{\nu_{K}}-(|K|-|E_{-}|)^{\nu_{K}}}\Big)^{2\mathbf{N}}\,m_{f}.

By
Lemma

9.4
, the function
1/f:L\to\mathbb{R}
is
\big(\beta_{0}\,\mu^{|\mathbf{N}},\frac{2}{m_{0}}\big)
-smooth
and satisfies
\min_{x\in L}|1/f(x)|\geq 1/M_{0}
. Note that
m_{1/f}:=\min_{x\in K}|1/f(x)|=1/M_{f}
and

M_{1/f}:=\max_{x\in K}|1/f(x)|=1/m_{f}
.
Observe that

\mathbf{N}(\mu^{|\infty},\tfrac{2}{m_{0}},a_{K}\beta_{0},\tfrac{1}{M_{0}})=\mathbf{N}(\mu^{|\infty},2M_{0},a_{K}\beta_{0},m_{0}).

Thus,
Theorem

4.13
applied to
1/f
, gives

(9.12)

M_{1/f}\leq\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}\,\Big(\frac{|K|^{\nu_{K}}}{|K|^{\nu_{K}}-(|K|-|E_{+}|)^{\nu_{K}}}\Big)^{\mathbf{N}}\,\sqrt{M_{1/f}m_{1/f}},

where
E_{+}:=\{x\in K:|1/f(x)|<\sqrt{M_{1/f}m_{1/f}}\}
, which implies

(9.13)

M_{f}\leq\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}^{2}\,\Big(\frac{|K|^{\nu_{K}}}{|K|^{\nu_{K}}-(|K|-|E_{+}|)^{\nu_{K}}}\Big)^{2\mathbf{N}}\,m_{f}.

Now
E_{+}=\{x\in K:\sqrt{M_{f}m_{f}}<|f(x)|\}
so that
E_{-}
and
E_{+}
form a partition of
K
.
Therefore,
|E_{-}|\geq|K|/2
or
|E_{+}|\geq|K|/2
. Consequently,

(9.14)

M_{f}\leq\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}^{2}\,\Big(\frac{2^{\nu_{K}}}{2^{\nu_{K}}-1}\Big)^{2\mathbf{N}}\,m_{f}.

The proof is complete.
∎

Inequalities of Markov type

Here we discuss a few inequalities of Markov type which are consequences
of an upper bound for the number of zeros in terms of
\mathbf{N}
which we recall in
Proposition

10.1

and the comparison of the
L^{1}
- and
L^{\infty}
-norm, see
Theorem

7.4
.

10.1.
Number of zeros

Proposition 10.1

(
[
Rai23
, Corollary 3.6 and Remark 3.7]
)
.

Let
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be a compact interval.
Let
\mu^{|\infty}=(\mu_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be an admissible weight and
M_{0}>b_{0}>0
.
Assume that
(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0},|I|,b_{0})
is admissible and let

\mathbf{N}=\mathbf{N}(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0},|I|,b_{0}).

Let
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
be a
(\mu^{|\mathbf{N}},M_{0})
-smooth function satisfying
\|f\|_{I}\geq b_{0}
.
Then the total number of zeros of
f
in
I
(counted with multiplicities) is finite and
bounded by
\mathbf{N}
.

10.2.
Inequalities of Markov type for univariate functions

The following lemma is a variant of
[
Pie22
, Lemma 8.1]
.

Lemma 10.2
.

Let
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be a compact interval.
Let
\mu^{|\infty}=(\mu_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be an admissible weight and
M_{1}=M_{0}\mu_{1}>b_{1}>0
.
Assume that
(\mu^{|\infty}_{+1},M_{1},|I|,b_{1})
is admissible and let

\mathbf{N}=\mathbf{N}(\mu^{|\infty}_{+1},M_{1},|I|,b_{1}).

Let
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
be a
(\mu^{|\mathbf{N}+1},M_{0})
-smooth function satisfying
\|f^{\prime}\|_{I}\geq b_{1}
.
Then, for each nonempty Lebesgue measurable subset
E\subseteq I
,

(10.1)

\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{1}(E)}\leq 2(\mathbf{N}+1)\,\|f\|_{E}.

Proof.

By
Proposition

10.1

f^{\prime}
has at most
\mathbf{N}
zeros in
I
(counted with multiplicities).
Thus there is sequence
t_{1}<\cdots<t_{n}
, with
n\leq\mathbf{N}
, of points in
I
such that the sign of
f^{\prime}

is constant on each interval
I_{j}:=(t_{j},t_{j+1})
, for
j=1,\ldots,n-1
,
I_{0}:=I\cap(-\infty,t_{1})
and

I_{n}:=I\cap(t_{n},\infty)
.
Then

\displaystyle\int_{E}|f^{\prime}(x)|\,dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\int_{E\cap I_{j}}|f^{\prime}(x)|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=0}^{n}|f(\sup E\cap I_{j})-f(\inf E\cap I_{j})|\leq 2(n+1)\,\|f\|_{E}

which implies the assertion.
∎

Combining
Lemma

10.2
with
Theorem

7.4
, we obtain the following
inequality of Markov type.

Proposition 10.3
.

In the setting of
Lemma

10.2
, for each Lebesgue measurable subset
E\subseteq I
with
|E|>0
,

(10.2)

\|f^{\prime}\|_{I}\leq 2\,\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}\,\frac{|I|^{\mathbf{N}}}{|E|^{\mathbf{N}+1}}(\mathbf{N}+1)^{2}\,\|f\|_{E}.

Proof.

Combine
Lemma

10.2
with (
7.5
) for
f^{\prime}
.
The condition
\mu_{j}\geq j
for
j\geq 1
is not needed; see
Remark

7.1
.
∎

Corollary 10.4
.

Let
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be a compact interval.
Let
\mu^{|\infty}=(\mu_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be a quasianalytic admissible weight and
M_{0}>0
.
Let
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
be a
(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0})
-smooth function.
Then, for all

,

(10.3)

\|f^{(j)}\|_{I}\leq\Big(\frac{2}{|I|}\Big)^{j}\,\prod_{i=1}^{j}\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}_{i}}(\mathbf{N}_{i}+1)^{2}\,\|f\|_{I},

where
\mathbf{N}_{i}=\mathbf{N}(\mu^{|\infty}_{+i},M_{i},|I|,\frac{\|f^{(i)}\|_{I}}{2})

Iterate

10.3
. In the definition of

.
∎

10.3.
Sampling the supremum norm

We need an easy consequence of
Proposition

10.3
.

Corollary 10.5
.

Let
\mu^{|\infty}=(\mu_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be a quasianalytic admissible weight,
M_{0}>0
, and
\mu_{1}>1
.
Let
f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
be a nonzero
(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0})
-smooth function.
Then

(10.4)

\|f^{\prime}\|_{[0,1]}\leq 2\,\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}\,(\mathbf{N}+1)^{2}\,\|f\|_{[0,1]},

where
\mathbf{N}=\mathbf{N}(\mu^{|\infty}_{+1},M_{1},1,\|f\|_{[0,1]})
.

Proof.

The assertion is trivially true if
\|f^{\prime}\|_{[0,1]}\leq\|f\|_{[0,1]}
.
If
\|f^{\prime}\|_{[0,1]}>\|f\|_{[0,1]}
, then the statement follows from
Proposition

10.3
.
The assumption
\mu_{1}>1
guarantees that
\|f\|_{[0,1]}<M_{1}
so that
\mathbf{N}\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}
.
∎

Recall that an
\epsilon
-net
for
[0,1]
is a subset
X\subseteq[0,1]
such that for each
y\in[0,1]
there
exists
x\in X
with
|x-y|\leq\epsilon
.

Corollary 10.6
.

In the setting of
Corollary

10.5
,
let
X
be any
\epsilon
-net for
[0,1]
, where

(10.5)

\epsilon:=\frac{1}{4\,\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}\,(\mathbf{N}+1)^{2}}.

Then

(10.6)

\|f\|_{X}\leq\|f\|_{[0,1]}\leq 2\,\|f\|_{X}.

Proof.

Let
y\in[0,1]
. There exists
x\in X
such that
|x-y|\leq\epsilon
. By
Corollary

10.5
,

\displaystyle|f(y)|\leq|f(x)|+|x-y|\,\|f^{\prime}\|_{[0,1]}\leq\|f\|_{X}+2\epsilon\,\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}\,(\mathbf{N}+1)^{2}\,\|f\|_{[0,1]}.

With the choice (
10.5
) for

, the statement follows easily.
∎

So given a nonzero
(\mu^{|\infty},M_{0})
-smooth function

,
we may compute

at a random point

.

, then

implies that, if

).

.

∎

The next lemma is a variant of
[
Pie22
, Lemma 8.1]
.
If
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a nonempty compact set, let

under the projection in direction

.

.

∎

Let us combine

10.8
with
Theorem

7.4
.

Corollary 10.9
.

Let

be a compact cube.

11.
Oscillatory integrals

The sublevel set estimates of
Theorem

7.2
and the bound for the numbers of zeros of

can be combined with the van der Corput lemma to provide estimates for oscillatory integrals

-smooth.
A similar approach was pursued by
[

]

11.1.
Van der Corput lemma

Lemma 11.1

(
[
CCW99
, Proposition 2.2]
,
[
Ste93
]
)
.

There exists an absolute constant
C>0
such that for any compact interval

,
any

, and any integrable function

satisfying

in the sense of distributions,

(11.1)

\Big|\int_{I}e^{i\lambda f(x)}\,dx\Big|\leq C\,k\,\lambda^{-1/k},\quad\lambda>0.

11.2.
Univariate phase and amplitude

Proposition 11.2
.

Let
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be a compact interval.
Let
\mu^{|\infty}=(\mu_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be an admissible weight and
M_{2}=M_{0}\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\geq b_{2}>0
.
Assume that
(\mu^{|\infty}_{+2},M_{2}+\frac{b_{2}}{2},|I|,\frac{b_{2}}{2})
is admissible and let

\mathbf{N}=\mathbf{N}(\mu^{|\infty}_{+2},M_{2}+\tfrac{b_{2}}{2},|I|,\tfrac{b_{2}}{2}).

Let
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
be a
(\mu^{|\mathbf{N}+2},M_{0})
-smooth function satisfying
\|f^{\prime\prime}\|_{I}\geq b_{2}
.
Let

belong to
\mathcal{C}^{1}(I)
. Then,

(11.2)

\Big|\int_{I}e^{i\lambda f(x)}g(x)\,dx\Big|\leq B\,\lambda^{-\frac{1}{\mathbf{N}+2}},\quad\text{ if }\lambda\geq\Big(\frac{2}{b_{2}}\Big)^{1+\frac{2}{\mathbf{N}}},

where

(11.3)

B=\frac{\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}^{1/\mathbf{N}}\,|I|\,\|g\|_{I}}{\|f^{\prime\prime}\|_{I}^{1/\mathbf{N}}}+2C\,(\mathbf{N}+1)\big(\|g\|_{I}+\|g^{\prime}\|_{L^{1}(I)}\big)

and
C>0
is an absolute constant.

Proof.

Consider the sublevel set
I_{t}:=\{x\in I:|f^{\prime\prime}(x)|\leq t\}
of
f^{\prime\prime}
. By
Theorem

7.2
and
Remark

7.1
, we have

(11.4)

|I_{t}|\leq|I|\Big(\frac{\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}\,t}{\|f^{\prime\prime}\|_{I}}\Big)^{1/\mathbf{N}},\quad t>0.

Consequently,

(11.5)

\Big|\int_{I_{t}}e^{i\lambda f(x)}g(x)\,dx\Big|\leq\|g\|_{I}\,|I|\,\Big(\frac{\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}\,t}{\|f^{\prime\prime}\|_{I}}\Big)^{1/\mathbf{N}},\quad t>0.

For
0<t\leq\frac{b_{2}}{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\|f^{\prime\prime}\pm t\|_{I}

\displaystyle\leq\|f^{\prime\prime}\|_{I}+t\leq M_{2}+\tfrac{b_{2}}{2}

and

\displaystyle\|f^{\prime\prime}\pm t\|_{I}

\displaystyle\geq\|f^{\prime\prime}\|_{I}-t\geq b_{2}-\tfrac{b_{2}}{2}=\tfrac{b_{2}}{2}

so that, by
Proposition

10.1
,
|f^{\prime\prime}|-t
has at most
\mathbf{N}
zeros in
I
. We conclude that, for

,
the set

consists of at most
\mathbf{N}+1

(a_{i},b_{i})
(the intervals containing the endpoints of
I
are half-open).
By partial integration,

\displaystyle\int_{a_{i}}^{b_{i}}e^{i\lambda f(x)}g(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{a_{i}}^{b_{i}}\partial_{x}\Big(\int_{a_{i}}^{x}e^{i\lambda f(u)}\,du\Big)g(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=\Big(\int_{a_{i}}^{b_{i}}e^{i\lambda f(u)}\,du\Big)g(b_{i})-\int_{a_{i}}^{b_{i}}\Big(\int_{a_{i}}^{x}e^{i\lambda f(u)}\,du\Big)g^{\prime}(x)\,dx.

By

11.1
, for
\lambda>0
and

Thus,

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{a_{i}}^{b_{i}}e^{i\lambda f(x)}g(x)\,dx\Big|\leq 2C\,(\lambda t)^{-1/2}\big(\|g\|_{I}+\|g^{\prime}\|_{L^{1}(I)}\big).

It follows that, for
0<t\leq\frac{b_{2}}{2}
and
\lambda>0
,

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{I}e^{i\lambda f(x)}g(x)\,dx\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\|g\|_{I}\,|I|\,\Big(\frac{\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{N}}\,t}{\|f^{\prime\prime}\|_{I}}\Big)^{1/\mathbf{N}}+2C\,(\mathbf{N}+1)\,(\lambda t)^{-1/2}\big(\|g\|_{I}+\|g^{\prime}\|_{L^{1}(I)}\big)

and setting
t=\lambda^{-\frac{\mathbf{N}}{\mathbf{N}+2}}
, we conclude (
11.2
).
∎

f=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}):[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is
(\mu^{|\mathbf{N}},M_{0})
smooth if
f\in\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{N}}([0,1])
and

(B.2)

\|f\|_{j,[0,1]}=\sup_{x\in[0,1]}\sum_{i=1}^{m}|f_{i}^{(j)}(x)|\leq M_{j},\quad 0\leq j\leq\mathbf{N}.

We also use
\|f\|_{[0,1]}=\|f\|_{0,[0,1]}=\sup_{x\in[0,1]}\sum_{i=1}^{m}|f_{i}(x)|
.

In the following, we write
m_{j}:=M_{j}/j!
, for
j\geq 1
, and assume
M_{0}=m_{0}=1
.

Proof.

We may assume that
M_{0}=1
, by replacing
f
by
f/M_{0}
and
b_{0}
by
b_{0}/M_{0}
.
Choose points
x_{1}<x_{2}<\cdots<x_{\mathbf{N}}
in
E
such that
\min_{j}(x_{j+1}-x_{j})\geq\frac{|E|}{\mathbf{N}-1}
.
Put
Q(x):=(x-x_{1})(x-x_{2})\cdots(x-x_{\mathbf{N}})
. Then, by the Lagrange interpolation formula, for
x\in I
,

f_{i}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{\mathbf{N}}\frac{Q(x)f_{i}(x_{j})}{Q^{\prime}(x_{j})(x-x_{j})}+\frac{Q(x)f_{i}^{(\mathbf{N})}(\xi)}{\mathbf{N}!},\quad\xi=\xi_{i,x}\in I.

It follows that

\|f\|_{I}\leq\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{\mathbf{N}}\frac{1}{|Q^{\prime}(x_{j})|}\Big)\|f\|_{E}|I|^{\mathbf{N}-1}+m_{\mathbf{N}}|I|^{\mathbf{N}}.

Now

\displaystyle|Q^{\prime}(x_{j})|

\displaystyle=(x_{j}-x_{j-1})\cdots(x_{j}-x_{1})(x_{j+1}-x_{j})\cdots(x_{\mathbf{N}}-x_{j})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(j-1)!(\mathbf{N}-j)!}{(\mathbf{N}-1)^{\mathbf{N}-1}}|E|^{\mathbf{N}-1}>\frac{(j-1)!(\mathbf{N}-j)!}{(\mathbf{N}-1)!}\Big(\frac{|E|}{e}\Big)^{\mathbf{N}-1}

so that

\sum_{j=1}^{\mathbf{N}}\frac{1}{|Q^{\prime}(x_{j})|}<\Big(\frac{2e}{|E|}\Big)^{\mathbf{N}-1}.

Thus

(B.3)

\|f\|_{I}\leq\Big(\frac{2e|I|}{|E|}\Big)^{\mathbf{N}-1}\|f\|_{E}+m_{\mathbf{N}}|I|^{\mathbf{N}}.

We claim that

(B.4)

m_{\mathbf{N}}|I|^{\mathbf{N}}\leq e^{-\mathbf{N}(\log m+3+\gamma_{\tilde{\mu}}(\mathbf{N}))}\implies m_{\mathbf{N}}|I|^{\mathbf{N}}\leq\frac{2}{3}\|f\|_{I}.

If (
B.4
) is satisfied, then we conclude

(B.5)

\|f\|_{I}\leq\Big(\frac{2e|I|}{|E|}\Big)^{\mathbf{N}}\|f\|_{E}.

Let us suppose that (
B.4
) is not satisfied.
Choose a subinterval
I_{1}\subseteq I
such that (
B.4
) is satisfied for
I_{1}
, as well as

(B.6)

|E\cap I_{1}|\geq|E|\frac{|I_{1}|}{|I|},

and

m_{\mathbf{N}}|I_{1}|^{\mathbf{N}}\geq 2^{-\mathbf{N}}e^{-\mathbf{N}(\log m+3+\gamma_{\tilde{\mu}}(\mathbf{N}))}.

Then, using (
B.4
) and (
B.5
) for
I_{1}
,

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\leq\Big(2e^{\log m+3+\gamma_{\tilde{\mu}}(\mathbf{N})}\Big)^{\mathbf{N}}m_{\mathbf{N}}|I_{1}|^{\mathbf{N}}\leq\frac{2}{3}\Big(2e^{\log m+3+\gamma_{\tilde{\mu}}(\mathbf{N})}\Big)^{\mathbf{N}}\|f\|_{I_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{3}\Big(2e^{\log m+3+\gamma_{\tilde{\mu}}(\mathbf{N})}\Big)^{\mathbf{N}}\Big(\frac{2e|I_{1}|}{|E\cap I_{1}|}\Big)^{\mathbf{N}}\|f\|_{E\cap I_{1}}\leq\Big(4e^{\log m+4+\gamma_{\tilde{\mu}}(\mathbf{N})}\frac{|I|}{|E|}\Big)^{\mathbf{N}}\|f\|_{E}.

This implies (
B.1
) because
\|f\|_{I}\leq\|f\|_{[0,1]}\leq M_{0}=1
.
∎

The proof of the claim (
B.4
) comprises the Lemmas
B.2
—
B.7
.

Lemma B.2

(
[
Rai23
, Lemma 3.1]
)
.

The function

\beta(x):=\sup_{0\leq j\leq\mathbf{N}}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{m}|f_{i}^{(j)}(x)|}{e^{j}M_{j}},\quad x\in[0,1],

is continuous. More precisely,
for all
1\leq j\leq\mathbf{N}
and all distinct
x,y\in[0,1]
,

(B.7)

\beta(x)<\max\{\beta(y),e^{-j}\}e^{e|x-y|\mu_{j}}.

Proof.

[
Rai23
, Lemma 3.1]
is formulated and proved in the case
m=1
, but the argument works for general
m
.
∎

Lemma B.3
.

\min_{x\in[0,1]}\beta(x)>e^{-\mathbf{D}-1}
.

Proof.

Suppose

\min_{x\in[0,1]}\beta(x)\leq e^{-\mathbf{D}-1}
and let
x_{0}\in[0,1]
be such that
\beta(x_{0})=e^{-\mathbf{D}-1}
.
Since
\|f\|_{[0,1]}\geq b_{0}
, we have
\max_{x\in[0,1]}\beta(x)\geq\frac{b_{0}}{M_{0}}\geq e^{-j_{0}}
,
where
j_{0}:=\lceil\log\frac{M_{0}}{b_{0}}\rceil_{\mathbb{N}}
.
Since
\beta
is continuous, there is a monotonic sequence
x_{j}\in[0,1]
such that
\beta(x_{j})=e^{-j}

for all
j_{0}\leq j\leq\mathbf{D}+1
.
By (
B.7
), we have

(B.8)

|x_{j}-x_{j-1}|>\frac{1}{e\,\mu_{j}}\quad\text{ for }j_{0}+1\leq j\leq\mathbf{D}+1,

so that, summing over
j
, we find

(B.9)

e>\sum_{j=j_{0}+1}^{\mathbf{D}+1}\frac{1}{\mu_{j}},

contradicting the definition of
\mathbf{D}
.
∎

Lemma B.4
.

For
0\leq k\leq\mathbf{D}
,

\Big(\frac{1}{\mathbf{N}-k}-\frac{1}{\mathbf{N}}\Big)\log m_{\mathbf{N}}-\frac{1}{\mathbf{N}-k}\log m_{k}<\gamma_{\tilde{\mu}}(\mathbf{N})-1.

Proof.

The left-hand side equals

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{1}{\mathbf{N}-k}-\frac{1}{\mathbf{N}}\Big)\sum_{j=1}^{\mathbf{N}}\log(\tfrac{\mu_{j}}{j})-\frac{1}{\mathbf{N}-k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\log(\tfrac{\mu_{j}}{j})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathbf{N}-k}\sum_{j=k+1}^{\mathbf{N}}\big(\log(\tfrac{\mu_{j}}{j})-\log\mu_{1}\big)-\frac{1}{\mathbf{N}}\sum_{j=1}^{\mathbf{N}}\big(\log(\tfrac{\mu_{j}}{j})-\log\mu_{1}\big)

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{\mathbf{N}}\frac{\hat{\mu}^{\prime}(t)}{\hat{\mu}(t)}S(t)\,dt,

where
\hat{\mu}(t):=\tilde{\mu}(t)/t
and

S(t):=\frac{1}{\mathbf{N}-k}\sum_{j=k+1}^{\mathbf{N}}\mathbf{1}_{[1,j]}(t)-\frac{1}{\mathbf{N}}\sum_{j=1}^{\mathbf{N}}\mathbf{1}_{[1,j]}(t).

Since
0\leq S(t)<t/\mathbf{N}
, we
find

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\mathbf{N}}\frac{\hat{\mu}^{\prime}(t)}{\hat{\mu}(t)}S(t)\,dt\leq\sup_{1\leq t\leq\mathbf{N}}\frac{t\hat{\mu}^{\prime}(t)}{\hat{\mu}(t)}=\sup_{1\leq t\leq\mathbf{N}}\frac{t\tilde{\mu}^{\prime}(t)}{\tilde{\mu}(t)}-1=\gamma_{\tilde{\mu}}(\mathbf{N})-1.

∎

Lemma B.5
.

Let
I
be a subinterval of
[0,1]
.
If

(B.10)

m_{\mathbf{N}}|I|^{\mathbf{N}}\leq e^{-\mathbf{N}(\log m+3+\gamma_{\tilde{\mu}}(\mathbf{N}))}

then, for
0\leq k\leq\mathbf{D}
,

(B.11)

m_{\mathbf{N}}|I|^{\mathbf{N}}\leq\frac{e^{-2\mathbf{N}}}{m}m_{k}|I|^{k}.

Proof.

By (
B.10
),

(B.12)

\frac{m_{\mathbf{N}}|I|^{\mathbf{N}}}{m_{k}|I|^{k}}\leq e^{-(\mathbf{N}-k)(\log m+3+\gamma_{\tilde{\mu}}(\mathbf{N}))}\frac{m_{\mathbf{N}}^{k/\mathbf{N}}}{m_{k}}.

By
Lemma

B.4
,

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{m_{\mathbf{N}}^{k/\mathbf{N}}}{m_{k}}\Big)^{1/(\mathbf{N}-k)}=\frac{m_{\mathbf{N}}^{\frac{1}{\mathbf{N}-k}-\frac{1}{\mathbf{N}}}}{m_{k}^{\frac{1}{\mathbf{N}-k}}}<e^{\gamma_{\tilde{\mu}}(\mathbf{N})-1}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\frac{m_{\mathbf{N}}|I|^{\mathbf{N}}}{m_{k}|I|^{k}}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-(\mathbf{N}-k)(\log m+3+\gamma_{\tilde{\mu}}(\mathbf{N}))+(\mathbf{N}-k)(\gamma_{\tilde{\mu}}(\mathbf{N})-1)}

\displaystyle=e^{-(\mathbf{N}-k)(\log m+4)}\leq e^{-2\mathbf{N}-\log m}=\frac{e^{-2\mathbf{N}}}{m},

if
k\leq\mathbf{D}=\mathbf{N}/2
.
∎

Lemma B.6
.

For some
0\leq k\leq\mathbf{D}
,

(B.13)

m_{k}\Big(\frac{|I|}{2}\Big)^{k}\leq me^{(2+\frac{1}{e})\mathbf{D}}\Big(\|f\|_{I}+m_{\mathbf{N}}\Big(\frac{|I|}{2}\Big)^{\mathbf{N}}\Big).

Proof.

Let
c
be the center of
I
. Then, for
x\in I
,

f_{i}(x)=T^{\mathbf{N}-1}_{c}f_{i}(x)+\frac{f_{i}^{(\mathbf{N})}(\xi)}{\mathbf{N}!}(x-c)^{\mathbf{N}},

where
T^{\mathbf{N}-1}_{c}f_{i}(x)
is the Taylor polynomial of degree
\mathbf{N}-1
of
f_{i}
at
c
and
\xi_{i,x}\in I
.
By
[
Man52
, 6.4.III]
,

\displaystyle|f_{i}^{(k)}(c)|=|(T^{\mathbf{N}-1}_{c}f_{i})^{(k)}(c)|

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\frac{2}{|I|}\Big)^{k}(\mathbf{N}-1)^{k}\,\|T^{\mathbf{N}-1}_{c}f_{i}\|_{I}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\frac{2}{|I|}\Big)^{k}(\mathbf{N}-1)^{k}\Big(\|f_{i}\|_{I}+m_{\mathbf{N}}\Big(\frac{|I|}{2}\Big)^{\mathbf{N}}\Big).

Thus

\displaystyle\frac{|f_{i}^{(k)}(c)|}{e^{k}M_{k}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{m_{k}}\Big(\frac{2}{|I|}\Big)^{k}\Big(\frac{\mathbf{N}-1}{e}\Big)^{k}\frac{1}{k!}\Big(\|f_{i}\|_{I}+m_{\mathbf{N}}\Big(\frac{|I|}{2}\Big)^{\mathbf{N}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{m_{k}}\Big(\frac{2}{|I|}\Big)^{k}e^{\frac{\mathbf{N}-1}{e}}\Big(\|f_{i}\|_{I}+m_{\mathbf{N}}\Big(\frac{|I|}{2}\Big)^{\mathbf{N}}\Big).

By
Lemma

B.3
,

e^{-\mathbf{D}-1}<\beta(c)
.
Therefore, there is some
0\leq k\leq\mathbf{D}
such that

e^{-\mathbf{D}-1}<\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{|f_{i}^{(k)}(c)|}{e^{k}M_{k}}.

This implies the assertion.
∎

Lemma B.7
.

If (
B.10
), then
m_{\mathbf{N}}|I|^{\mathbf{N}}<\frac{2}{3}\|f\|_{I}
.

Proof.

By
Lemmas

B.5
and
B.6
, there exists
0\leq k\leq\mathbf{D}
such that

\displaystyle m_{\mathbf{N}}|I|^{\mathbf{N}}\leq\frac{e^{-2\mathbf{N}}}{m}m_{k}|I|^{k}

\displaystyle\leq e^{k-2\mathbf{N}}e^{(2+\frac{1}{e})\mathbf{D}}\Big(\|f\|_{I}+m_{\mathbf{N}}\Big(\frac{|I|}{2}\Big)^{\mathbf{N}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-(1-\frac{1}{e})\mathbf{D}}\Big(\|f\|_{I}+\frac{1}{4}m_{\mathbf{N}}|I|^{\mathbf{N}}\Big).

This implies the assertion,
since
\frac{3}{2}<e^{1-\frac{1}{e}}-\frac{1}{4}
.
∎
