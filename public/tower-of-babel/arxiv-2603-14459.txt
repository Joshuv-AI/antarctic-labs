Reduced One-Fluid GENERIC Closure from Relativistic Moment Kinetics
Madison J. Newell, Salman A. Nejad

Abstract
In this work we derive a reduced one-fluid plasma model from the relativistic Vlasov–Boltzmann–Maxwell system using a moment hierarchy reduction combined with strong-guide-field anisotropic ordering. The unresolved higher-moment sector of the hierarchy is projected onto its dominant slow thermodynamic mode, producing a scalar regulator variable that represents a coarse-grained combination of charge imbalance, pressure anisotropy, and irreversible kinetic production channels. The resulting reduced state vector admits a GENERIC (General Equation for Non-Equilibrium Reversible–Irreversible Coupling) representation in which the reversible sector reproduces reduced electromagnetic field-line dynamics while the irreversible sector governs slow thermodynamic relaxation. Linearization yields a pair of electromagnetic eigenmodes together with an additional real thermodynamic eigenvalue. The fast modes recover the standard gyrotropic cold-plasma response, including familiar limits such as whistler dispersion, while the slow mode drives gradual drift of the effective electromagnetic spectrum and provides a reduced mechanism for variability in relativistic magnetized plasmas with slowly evolving macroscopic equilibrium. The previous 3-field model captured only the thermodynamic slow-mode sector, whereas the fully closed extended GENERIC model also contains the explicit reversible nonneutral charge degree of freedom, whose frozen-thermodynamic limit recovers the nonneutral whistler-Alfvén equations as a strict subset. The model is formulated within the GENERIC framework, ensuring consistency with first-principles nonequilibrium thermodynamics.



I
Introduction

Electromagnetic eigenmodes play a central role in plasma dynamics, governing wave propagation, transport, and stability in systems ranging from laboratory devices to astrophysical magnetospheres. In conventional plasma theory these modes are derived by linearizing the electromagnetic response of a fluid or kinetic system about a fixed equilibrium state. The resulting dielectric tensor and dispersion relations provide accurate descriptions of wave propagation when the thermodynamic state of the plasma remains approximately stationary on the timescale of the waves
29
.

However, many high-energy and weakly collisional plasmas violate the frozen-equilibrium assumption underlying standard wave theory. Relativistic pair plasmas in pulsar magnetospheres, runaway-electron populations in fusion devices, and ultra-intense laser–plasma systems all provide examples in which kinetic physics, relativistic inertia, and slowly evolving dissipation channels play an essential role
25
;
6
;
14
. In such regimes the background plasma cannot be treated as a strictly static medium. Instead, electromagnetic oscillations evolve within a thermodynamic environment that may drift because of pair creation, radiation reaction, anisotropy relaxation, turbulent transport, and other irreversible kinetic processes.

A natural first-principles language for such systems is relativistic kinetic theory. Continuum and reduced-geometry relativistic solvers have been developed for special cases, including relativistic neutral Boltzmann approaches and reduced-geometry relativistic Vlasov–Maxwell formulations
1
;
33
;
34
. At the same time, the dominant numerical paradigm for relativistic collisionless plasma modeling remains the particle-in-cell (PIC) method, with major astrophysical and laboratory implementations spanning several generations of codes and applications
13
;
12
;
7
;
24
. PIC methods are extraordinarily successful, but their Monte Carlo representation introduces particle noise that decreases only slowly with particles per cell
2
. This makes it natural to ask whether reduced continuum closures can capture kinetically informed relativistic plasma response while retaining a cleaner thermodynamic interpretation.

Continuum Vlasov–Maxwell methods have already achieved major success in the nonrelativistic setting, especially in discontinuous Galerkin (DG), finite-element, spectral, and hybrid-kinetic formulations
19
;
27
. A particularly important thread concerns conservative discretization and Hamiltonian structure: energy-conserving weak-form Vlasov schemes, conservative DG algorithms, and Hamiltonian DG formulations have clarified how geometric structure, basis choice, and weak-form design control robustness and conservation properties
8
;
19
;
21
;
11
;
16
. These developments strongly suggest that reduced plasma descriptions should inherit as much of the underlying kinetic and thermodynamic structure as possible.

A complementary issue is physical interpretation. In strongly magnetized relativistic plasmas, variability need not arise solely from changes in the macroscopic equilibrium itself. Pulsar pair discharges, pair-cascade cycles, and radio-emission phenomenology all point to situations in which the plasma remains near a quasi-steady global state while the kinetic production and relaxation channels evolve in time
32
;
31
;
30
;
26
;
5
;
9
. This suggests that part of the observable variability may be encoded not in the instantaneous cold dielectric response, but in the slow evolution of unresolved thermodynamic degrees of freedom.

A natural framework for describing systems with coupled reversible and irreversible dynamics is the General Equation for Non-Equilibrium Reversible–Irreversible Coupling (GENERIC)
15
;
22
;
23
. GENERIC expresses the evolution of a state vector
x
as

\dot{x}=L(x)\nabla E(x)+M(x)\nabla S(x),

(1)

where the antisymmetric operator
L
generates reversible Hamiltonian evolution and the symmetric positive semidefinite operator
M
generates irreversible entropy-producing relaxation. The degeneracy conditions

L\nabla S=0,\qquad M\nabla E=0,

(2)

guarantee conservation of energy and nonnegative entropy production. Although GENERIC has been widely used in nonequilibrium thermodynamics and soft-matter physics, its implications for electromagnetic eigenmode structure in relativistic plasmas remain largely unexplored.

In this work we derive a reduced one-fluid GENERIC closure for magnetized relativistic plasma dynamics starting from the relativistic Vlasov–Boltzmann–Maxwell system. The derivation proceeds through three steps. First, we perform a relativistic moment reduction of the kinetic equations to obtain the one-fluid density, velocity, and pressure hierarchy. Second, we impose a strong-guide-field anisotropic ordering appropriate for magnetized plasmas, introducing the reduced field variables

\mathbf{u}_{\perp}=\hat{z}\times\nabla_{\perp}\phi,\qquad\mathbf{b}_{\perp}=\hat{z}\times\nabla_{\perp}A_{\parallel},

(3)

which describe the perpendicular fluid velocity and magnetic perturbations. Finally, the unresolved higher-moment sector is projected onto a single dominant slow thermodynamic mode,

\alpha,

(4)

which obeys a leading-order relaxation law

\tau_{\alpha}\partial_{t}\alpha+\alpha=\lambda_{\alpha}\nabla_{\perp}^{2}\phi+\cdots.

(5)

The resulting reduced state vector

X=(\phi,A_{\parallel},\alpha)

(6)

admits a GENERIC representation in which reversible field-line dynamics are coupled to slow irreversible thermodynamic relaxation.

Linearization yields a pair of electromagnetic eigenmodes together with an additional real thermodynamic eigenvalue. The fast modes recover the standard gyrotropic cold-plasma response, including familiar limits such as whistler dispersion, while the slow mode produces gradual drift of the effective electromagnetic spectrum. In that sense, the paper proposes a thermodynamically consistent reduced closure in which part of the apparent variability of a relativistic plasma can be traced to slow motion within the unresolved kinetic sector rather than to rapid restructuring of the bulk equilibrium itself.

II
Derivation of the Reduced One-Fluid GENERIC Closure from Relativistic Moment Kinetics

In this section we derive the reduced one-fluid GENERIC closure from a relativistic kinetic parent model under strong-guide-field anisotropic ordering. The purpose of the derivation is to identify the reduced field variables
(\phi,A_{\parallel})
and the thermodynamic regulator variable
\alpha
as quantities inherited from the relativistic moment hierarchy rather than introduced phenomenologically. The resulting closure is therefore not an arbitrary control ansatz, but the leading projected slow-mode truncation of unresolved higher moments in a magnetized relativistic plasma.

II.1
Parent kinetic system

We begin from the relativistic Vlasov–Boltzmann–Maxwell system for two species
s\in\{+,-\}
,

\partial_{t}f_{s}+\mathbf{v}_{s}\cdot\nabla f_{s}+q_{s}\left(\mathbf{E}+\frac{\mathbf{v}_{s}}{c}\times\mathbf{B}\right)\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{p}}f_{s}=C_{s}[f_{+},f_{-}]+R_{s}[f_{+},f_{-}],

(7)

where
f_{s}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{p},t)
is the phase-space distribution,
q_{\pm}=\pm e
, and

\mathbf{v}_{s}=\frac{\mathbf{p}}{\gamma_{s}m},\qquad\gamma_{s}=\sqrt{1+\frac{|\mathbf{p}|^{2}}{m^{2}c^{2}}}.

(8)

The operators
C_{s}
and
R_{s}
represent, respectively, collisional and irreversible non-collisional effects such as radiative relaxation, coarse-grained kinetic dissipation, or other entropy-producing channels. Equations of this general relativistic kinetic type underlie both continuum Vlasov–Maxwell approaches and modern PIC descriptions of relativistic plasmas
19
;
33
;
34
;
13
;
12
.

The electromagnetic fields satisfy Maxwell’s equations

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathbf{B}

\displaystyle=-c\nabla\times\mathbf{E},

(9)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathbf{E}

\displaystyle=c\nabla\times\mathbf{B}-4\pi\mathbf{J},

(10)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\mathbf{E}

\displaystyle=4\pi\rho_{c},

(11)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B}

\displaystyle=0,

(12)

with charge and current densities

\rho_{c}=\sum_{s}q_{s}\int f_{s}\,d^{3}p,\qquad\mathbf{J}=\sum_{s}q_{s}\int\mathbf{v}_{s}f_{s}\,d^{3}p.

(13)

Equation (
7
) is the first-principles starting point for the reduced closure derived below.

II.2
Relativistic moments and one-fluid variables

Define the number density, bulk velocity, and pressure tensor moments

\displaystyle n_{s}

\displaystyle=\int f_{s}\,d^{3}p,

(14)

\displaystyle n_{s}\mathbf{u}_{s}

\displaystyle=\int\mathbf{v}_{s}f_{s}\,d^{3}p,

(15)

\displaystyle P_{s}^{ij}

\displaystyle=\int\left(p^{i}-h_{s}mu_{s}^{i}\right)\left(v^{j}-u_{s}^{j}\right)f_{s}\,d^{3}p,

(16)

where
h_{s}=h_{s}(\Theta_{s})
is the specific enthalpy factor and

\Theta_{s}\equiv\frac{k_{B}T_{s}}{mc^{2}}

(17)

is the dimensionless temperature.

The continuity equations are

\partial_{t}n_{s}+\nabla\cdot(n_{s}\mathbf{u}_{s})=0,

(18)

and the corresponding momentum equations may be written schematically as

\partial_{t}\!\left(h_{s}n_{s}m\mathbf{u}_{s}\right)+\nabla\cdot\!\left(h_{s}n_{s}m\,\mathbf{u}_{s}\mathbf{u}_{s}+P_{s}\right)=q_{s}n_{s}\left(\mathbf{E}+\frac{\mathbf{u}_{s}}{c}\times\mathbf{B}\right)+\mathcal{D}_{s},

(19)

where
\mathcal{D}_{s}
denotes the moment-level irreversible contribution induced by
C_{s}
and
R_{s}
.

To pass to a one-fluid description, define

\displaystyle n

\displaystyle\equiv n_{+}+n_{-},

(20)

\displaystyle\delta n

\displaystyle\equiv n_{+}-n_{-},

(21)

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{h_{+}n_{+}\mathbf{u}_{+}+h_{-}n_{-}\mathbf{u}_{-}}{h_{+}n_{+}+h_{-}n_{-}},

(22)

\displaystyle\mathbf{w}

\displaystyle\equiv\mathbf{u}_{+}-\mathbf{u}_{-},

(23)

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle\equiv P_{+}+P_{-},

(24)

\displaystyle\Delta P

\displaystyle\equiv P_{+}-P_{-}.

(25)

Then

\rho_{c}=e\,\delta n,\qquad\mathbf{J}=e\left(n_{+}\mathbf{u}_{+}-n_{-}\mathbf{u}_{-}\right).

(26)

The effective one-fluid inertia inherits its temperature dependence through the enthalpy factor. Accordingly, the thermodynamic inertial scale entering the reduced theory is

d_{h}^{2}(\Theta)=\frac{h(\Theta)c^{2}}{\omega_{p}^{2}},

(27)

where
h(\Theta)
is the appropriate coarse-grained enthalpy function of the one-fluid state.

II.3
Strong-guide-field anisotropic ordering

We consider a strongly magnetized configuration with guide field

\mathbf{B}=B_{0}\hat{z}+\mathbf{b}_{\perp},\qquad|\mathbf{b}_{\perp}|\ll B_{0},

(28)

and impose the anisotropic reduced ordering

k_{\parallel}\ll k_{\perp},\qquad\partial_{z}\sim\epsilon\nabla_{\perp},\qquad\epsilon\ll 1.

(29)

Introduce the standard reduced potentials

\mathbf{u}_{\perp}=\hat{z}\times\nabla_{\perp}\phi,\qquad\mathbf{b}_{\perp}=\hat{z}\times\nabla_{\perp}A,\qquad A\equiv A_{\parallel},

(30)

and define the nonlinear parallel derivative

\nabla_{\parallel}=\partial_{z}+\frac{1}{B_{0}}(\hat{z}\times\nabla_{\perp}A)\cdot\nabla_{\perp}.

(31)

Under the ordering (
29
), the reduced electromagnetic dynamics are carried by the field variables
(\phi,A_{\parallel})
, while the higher-moment sector enters through projected thermodynamic corrections.

II.4
Reduced field equations from the moment hierarchy

Projecting the one-fluid momentum equation perpendicular to the guide field and combining the result with Ampère’s law and Faraday’s law yields a reduced vorticity–induction structure of the form

\partial_{t}Q_{\phi}+v_{A}(\Theta)\,\nabla_{\parallel}A_{\parallel}=\mathcal{N}_{\phi}+\mathcal{D}_{\phi},

(32)

\partial_{t}Q_{A}+v_{A}(\Theta)\,\nabla_{\parallel}\left[\phi-\rho_{h}^{2}(\Theta)\nabla_{\perp}^{2}\phi-\Xi\right]=\mathcal{N}_{A}+\mathcal{D}_{A}.

(33)

Here
\mathcal{N}_{\phi}
and
\mathcal{N}_{A}
denote the nonlinear reduced advection terms and
\mathcal{D}_{\phi}
,
\mathcal{D}_{A}
denote explicit dissipative terms inherited from the irreversible kinetic sector. At leading order, the generalized reduced variables take the form

Q_{\phi}=\left(1-d_{n}^{2}(\Theta)\nabla_{\perp}^{2}\right)\phi-\Xi_{\phi},\qquad Q_{A}=\left(1-d_{h}^{2}(\Theta)\nabla_{\perp}^{2}\right)A_{\parallel}.

(34)

The quantities
\Xi_{\phi}
and
\Xi
are the unresolved thermodynamic contributions generated by the higher-moment sector. Their explicit representation is obtained by projecting the unresolved moment hierarchy onto its dominant slow mode.

II.5
Definition of the thermodynamic regulator variable

We now define the internal regulator variable
\alpha
as a projected combination of unresolved higher moments. Let
\mathbf{m}_{\mathrm{high}}
denote the vector of higher moments required to close the reduced field equations, including for example charge imbalance, pressure anisotropy, heat-flux asymmetry, and irreversible production channels. We define

\alpha\equiv a_{1}\frac{\delta n}{n_{0}}+a_{2}\frac{\Delta P_{\parallel}-\Delta P_{\perp}}{P_{0}}+a_{3}\frac{\Delta q_{\parallel}}{q_{0}}+a_{4}\frac{\Pi_{\mathrm{irr}}}{\Pi_{0}},

(35)

where
\Delta q_{\parallel}
is the species-difference parallel heat flux,
\Pi_{\mathrm{irr}}
is an effective irreversible production variable, and
a_{i}
are projection coefficients specifying the dominant slow thermodynamic direction in the unresolved moment sector.

Equation (
35
) defines the projected slow thermodynamic coordinate used in the reduced closure.

II.6
Projected higher-moment dynamics

Let the unresolved moment vector satisfy the schematic evolution law

\partial_{t}\mathbf{m}_{\mathrm{high}}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{high}}\mathbf{m}_{\mathrm{high}}+\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{high}}[\phi,A_{\parallel}]+\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{irr}},

(36)

where
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{high}}
is the linearized higher-moment operator,
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{high}}
denotes forcing by the resolved field sector, and
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{irr}}
contains projected irreversible relaxation.

Projecting (
36
) onto the dominant slow eigenvector
e_{\alpha}
gives

\alpha=e_{\alpha}^{\top}\mathbf{m}_{\mathrm{high}},\qquad\partial_{t}\alpha=e_{\alpha}^{\top}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{high}}\mathbf{m}_{\mathrm{high}}+e_{\alpha}^{\top}\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{high}}[\phi,A_{\parallel}]+e_{\alpha}^{\top}\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{irr}}.

(37)

We assume that the higher-moment sector possesses a spectral gap separating one dominant slow thermodynamic mode from faster unresolved modes. Under this assumption, the projected linear evolution is approximated at leading order by

e_{\alpha}^{\top}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{high}}\mathbf{m}_{\mathrm{high}}\approx-\frac{1}{\tau_{\alpha}(\Theta)}\,\alpha.

(38)

Under the anisotropic ordering, the leading resolved forcing reduces to curvature or vorticity forcing, so that

e_{\alpha}^{\top}\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{high}}[\phi,A_{\parallel}]\approx\lambda_{\alpha}(\Theta)\,\nabla_{\perp}^{2}\phi.

(39)

Hence the projected higher-moment evolution becomes

\tau_{\alpha}(\Theta)\,\partial_{t}\alpha+\alpha=\lambda_{\alpha}(\Theta)\,\nabla_{\perp}^{2}\phi+D_{\alpha}.

(40)

Equation (
40
) is the leading projected slow-mode closure for the unresolved relativistic moment hierarchy.

II.7
Projection of unresolved thermodynamic corrections

Because the unresolved terms
\Xi_{\phi}
and
\Xi
in
Eqs. (
32
)–(
34
)
arise from the same higher-moment sector, the same slow-mode projection yields

\Xi_{\phi}=\Gamma_{\phi}(\Theta)\,\alpha,\qquad\Xi=\Gamma_{A}(\Theta)\,\alpha.

(41)

To leading order in the reduced closure we set

\Gamma_{\phi}(\Theta)=\Gamma_{A}(\Theta)\equiv\Gamma(\Theta),

(42)

which gives the closed reduced one-fluid system

\partial_{t}\left[\left(1-d_{n}^{2}(\Theta)\nabla_{\perp}^{2}\right)\phi-\Gamma(\Theta)\alpha\right]+v_{A}(\Theta)\,\nabla_{\parallel}A_{\parallel}=\mathcal{N}_{\phi}+\mathcal{D}_{\phi},

(43)

\partial_{t}\left[\left(1-d_{h}^{2}(\Theta)\nabla_{\perp}^{2}\right)A_{\parallel}\right]+v_{A}(\Theta)\,\nabla_{\parallel}\left[\phi-\rho_{h}^{2}(\Theta)\nabla_{\perp}^{2}\phi-\Gamma(\Theta)\alpha\right]=\mathcal{N}_{A}+\mathcal{D}_{A},

(44)

\tau_{\alpha}(\Theta)\,\partial_{t}\alpha+\alpha=\lambda_{\alpha}(\Theta)\,\nabla_{\perp}^{2}\phi+D_{\alpha}.

(45)

Equations (
43
)–(
45
) constitute the desired reduced one-fluid closure derived from relativistic moment kinetics under projected slow-mode closure. The philosophy is similar in spirit to reduced kinetic closures in Landau-fluid and extended-MHD descriptions, where slowly relaxing higher moments encode kinetic information beyond ideal-fluid theory
17
;
28
. What differs here is that the slow unresolved sector is organized explicitly as a projected thermodynamic mode embedded in a reversible–irreversible GENERIC split.

II.8
Linearization and recovered dispersion relation

We linearize about a homogeneous guide-field state with constant coefficients and set

\phi,\,A_{\parallel},\,\alpha\propto e^{i(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x}-\omega t)},\qquad k_{\perp}^{2}\equiv q.

(46)

Then Eqs. (
43
)–(
45
) reduce to

-i\omega\left[(1+d_{n}^{2}q)\phi-\Gamma\alpha\right]+iv_{A}k_{\parallel}A_{\parallel}=0,

(47)

-i\omega(1+d_{h}^{2}q)A_{\parallel}+iv_{A}k_{\parallel}\left[(1+\rho_{h}^{2}q)\phi-\Gamma\alpha\right]=0,

(48)

(1-i\omega\tau_{\alpha})\alpha=-\lambda_{\alpha}q\,\phi.

(49)

From Eq. (
49
),

\alpha=-\frac{\lambda_{\alpha}q}{1-i\omega\tau_{\alpha}}\,\phi.

(50)

Defining

\mathcal{C}(\omega,\Theta)=\frac{\Gamma(\Theta)\lambda_{\alpha}(\Theta)q}{1-i\omega\tau_{\alpha}(\Theta)},

(51)

we obtain the linear dispersion relation

\omega^{2}\left(1+d_{h}^{2}q\right)\left(1+d_{n}^{2}q+\mathcal{C}\right)=v_{A}^{2}k_{\parallel}^{2}\left(1+\rho_{h}^{2}q+\mathcal{C}\right).

(52)

Thus the same reduced master dispersion relation obtained previously follows here from a first-principles moment reduction plus projected slow-mode closure. The electromagnetic part of this relation has the same structural form as reduced kinetic plasma models derived under strong-guide-field ordering
28
;
3
, while the additional term
\mathcal{C}
represents the thermodynamic correction generated by the projected unresolved moment sector. In the limit that the thermodynamic branch is frozen, the reduced electromagnetic response reverts to the usual gyrotropic field-line dynamics; when the thermodynamic branch evolves on comparable slow timescales, it induces a drift of the effective linear spectrum.

II.9
GENERIC representation of the reduced closure

We now show that the reduced system admits a GENERIC representation. Define the reduced state vector

X=(\phi,A_{\parallel},\alpha),

(53)

the reduced energy functional

E[X]=\frac{1}{2}\int d^{3}x\left[|\nabla_{\perp}A_{\parallel}|^{2}+d_{h}^{2}(\Theta)|\nabla_{\perp}^{2}A_{\parallel}|^{2}+\mu_{\phi}(\Theta)|\nabla_{\perp}\phi|^{2}+\mu_{\alpha}(\Theta)\alpha^{2}\right],

(54)

and the reduced entropy functional

S[X]=\int d^{3}x\,s(\alpha,\Theta),\qquad\frac{\partial^{2}s}{\partial\alpha^{2}}>0.

(55)

The reversible part of the reduced dynamics is inherited from the Hamiltonian field-line coupling of the parent kinetic system and may be written as

\partial_{t}X\big|_{\mathrm{rev}}=L(X)\frac{\delta E}{\delta X},

(56)

where
L
is antisymmetric and generates the reversible terms in
Eqs. (
43
) and (
44
).
The irreversible projected higher-moment relaxation is written as

\partial_{t}X\big|_{\mathrm{irr}}=M(X)\frac{\delta S}{\delta X},

(57)

where
M
is symmetric positive semidefinite and reproduces the relaxation term in
Eq. (
45
) together with the explicit dissipative contributions
\mathcal{D}_{\phi}
,
\mathcal{D}_{A}
, and
D_{\alpha}
.

Accordingly, the reduced closure takes GENERIC form

\partial_{t}X=L(X)\frac{\delta E}{\delta X}+M(X)\frac{\delta S}{\delta X},

(58)

with the degeneracy relations

L(X)\frac{\delta S}{\delta X}=0,\qquad M(X)\frac{\delta E}{\delta X}=0,

(59)

inherited from the reversible–irreversible split of the projected moment dynamics.

II.10
Assumptions underlying the reduced closure

The derivation above relies on the following assumptions.

Assumption 1:
The plasma is strongly magnetized and satisfies the reduced ordering

k_{\parallel}\ll k_{\perp}
, so that the dynamics are well described by the reduced variables
(\phi,A_{\parallel})
.

Assumption 2:
The relativistic moment hierarchy is truncated at finite order, with the unresolved higher moments represented by a reduced vector
\mathbf{m}_{\mathrm{high}}
.

Assumption 3:
The unresolved higher-moment sector possesses a spectral gap, allowing projection onto a single dominant slow thermodynamic mode
\alpha
.

Assumption 4: The projected slow mode obeys a relaxation law with characteristic time
\tau_{\alpha}(\Theta)
, so that memory effects beyond leading order are neglected.

Assumption 5:
The unresolved thermodynamic corrections entering the reduced field equations are linear in the dominant projected mode at leading order, yielding
\Xi_{\phi},\Xi\propto\alpha
.

Under these assumptions, the reduced one-fluid closure is derived from the relativistic moment hierarchy rather than postulated independently.

II.12
Example constitutive closure across relativistic heat regimes

To demonstrate that the reduced system closes with explicit constitutive relations, we introduce a simple family of temperature-dependent coefficients valid from cold to ultrarelativistic regimes.

The relativistic enthalpy factor is approximated by

h(\Theta)=1+4\Theta,

(60)

which captures the correct asymptotic limits

h\rightarrow 1\quad(\Theta\ll 1),\qquad h\rightarrow 4\Theta\quad(\Theta\gg 1).

(61)

The thermodynamic relaxation time of the projected mode is taken to scale with temperature as

\tau_{\alpha}(\Theta)=\tau_{0}(1+\Theta)^{p},

(62)

where
\tau_{0}
is a characteristic microscopic relaxation time and
p
determines the thermodynamic scaling of the unresolved sector.

The projected field forcing coefficient is modeled as

\lambda_{\alpha}(\Theta)=\lambda_{0}(1+\Theta)^{r},

(63)

while the thermodynamic feedback coefficient entering the reduced field equations is

\Gamma(\Theta)=\Gamma_{0}(1+\Theta)^{s}.

(64)

These relations provide a minimal closure family allowing the reduced theory to interpolate continuously between cold, relativistic, and ultrarelativistic plasma regimes while preserving the same reduced field structure.

II.13
Explicit frozen GENERIC realization

To make the GENERIC structure explicit, consider linearization about a homogeneous state with constant coefficients. Define the perturbation vector

\delta X=\begin{pmatrix}\phi\\
A_{\parallel}\\
\alpha\end{pmatrix}.

(65)

The quadratic free-energy functional is

F=\frac{1}{2}\int\delta X^{\top}K_{0}\delta X\,d^{3}x,

(66)

with symmetric metric

K_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}1+d_{n}^{2}\nabla_{\perp}^{2}&0&-\Gamma\\
0&1+d_{h}^{2}\nabla_{\perp}^{2}&0\\
-\Gamma&0&\mu_{\alpha}\end{pmatrix}.

(67)

The reversible operator takes the antisymmetric form

L_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}0&v_{A}\nabla_{\parallel}&0\\
-v_{A}\nabla_{\parallel}&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix},

(68)

which generates electromagnetic field-line coupling.

The irreversible operator governing thermodynamic relaxation is

M_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&\tau_{\alpha}^{-1}\end{pmatrix}.

(69)

The linearized dynamics therefore take the GENERIC form

\delta\dot{X}=(L_{0}-M_{0})K_{0}\delta X,

(70)

making explicit that reversible electromagnetic evolution and irreversible thermodynamic relaxation enter through separate operators.

The eigenvalue spectrum therefore contains a pair of electromagnetic modes together with a slow thermodynamic mode,

\omega_{\alpha}\approx-\frac{i}{\tau_{\alpha}}.

(71)

Because the dispersion relation depends on
\alpha
, this branch produces gradual drift of the effective electromagnetic spectrum.

II.14
Thermodynamic consistency of the reduced closure

An important property of the GENERIC formulation is that it enforces simultaneous compatibility with macroscopic energy conservation and microscopic entropy production. We verify that the reduced closure constructed above satisfies these structural constraints.

Let the reduced state vector be

X=(\phi,A_{\parallel},\alpha),

(72)

with dynamics written in GENERIC form

\partial_{t}X=L(X)\frac{\delta E}{\delta X}+M(X)\frac{\delta S}{\delta X},

(73)

where
L^{\top}=-L
is antisymmetric and
M^{\top}=M
is symmetric positive semidefinite.

The degeneracy relations

L(X)\frac{\delta S}{\delta X}=0,\qquad M(X)\frac{\delta E}{\delta X}=0,

(74)

guarantee that reversible dynamics do not produce entropy and that irreversible relaxation does not violate energy conservation.

Energy conservation.

Taking the time derivative of the reduced energy functional

E[X]
gives

\frac{dE}{dt}=\int\frac{\delta E}{\delta X}\cdot\partial_{t}X\,d^{3}x.

(75)

Substituting Eq. (
73
) yields

\frac{dE}{dt}=\int\frac{\delta E}{\delta X}\cdot L\frac{\delta E}{\delta X}\,d^{3}x+\int\frac{\delta E}{\delta X}\cdot M\frac{\delta S}{\delta X}\,d^{3}x.

(76)

Because
L
is antisymmetric, the first term vanishes identically,

\frac{\delta E}{\delta X}\cdot L\frac{\delta E}{\delta X}=0.

(77)

The second term vanishes by the degeneracy condition

M\delta E=0
, giving

\frac{dE}{dt}=0.

(78)

Thus the reduced closure conserves energy exactly in the absence of external forcing.

Entropy production.

The entropy evolution is

\frac{dS}{dt}=\int\frac{\delta S}{\delta X}\cdot\partial_{t}X\,d^{3}x.

(79)

Using Eq. (
73
) gives

\frac{dS}{dt}=\int\frac{\delta S}{\delta X}\cdot L\frac{\delta E}{\delta X}\,d^{3}x+\int\frac{\delta S}{\delta X}\cdot M\frac{\delta S}{\delta X}\,d^{3}x.

(80)

The first term vanishes because
L\delta S=0
. The second term is nonnegative because
M
is positive semidefinite:

\frac{dS}{dt}=\int\left(\frac{\delta S}{\delta X}\right)^{\top}M\left(\frac{\delta S}{\delta X}\right)\,d^{3}x\geq 0.

(81)

III
Relation to slow-mode plasma closures

The projected variable
\alpha
plays the role of a slow thermodynamic coordinate generated by the unresolved relativistic moment hierarchy. Its relaxation law,

\tau_{\alpha}\partial_{t}\alpha+\alpha=\lambda_{\alpha}\nabla_{\perp}^{2}\phi,

(83)

has the same general structure as slow-moment closures used in extended MHD and Landau-fluid theory, but here the slow mode is embedded in a GENERIC framework and inherits a direct kinetic interpretation through spectral projection.

In extended MHD, analogous relaxation equations arise for pressure anisotropy or heat flux, while Landau-fluid closures introduce additional slowly relaxing moments to represent kinetic corrections. The present construction differs in that the unresolved sector is reduced to a single projected thermodynamic coordinate whose evolution is constrained by the reversible–irreversible GENERIC split.

Accordingly, the reduced model may be viewed as a thermodynamically consistent one-fluid closure in which electromagnetic waves propagate through a slowly evolving kinetic environment.

III.1
Relativistic parameter matching and physical limits

The reduced dispersion relation (
52

terms of effective inertial and thermodynamic coefficients

,
\rho_{h}(\Theta)

\Gamma(\Theta)

To clarify the physical meaning of these coefficients, we relate them to
standard relativistic plasma parameters through the enthalpy-controlled
effective inertia. For a relativistic pair plasma the inertia of the
fluid elements is governed by the enthalpy per particle

h(\Theta)=\frac{w}{nmc^{2}},

(84)

where

is the enthalpy density and

\Theta=\frac{k_{B}T}{mc^{2}}

(85)

is the dimensionless temperature.

The effective inertia entering the reduced equations is therefore

(86)

This replacement modifies the characteristic plasma scales in a way that
is consistent with relativistic kinetic theory. In particular the plasma
frequency and cyclotron frequency become

\omega_{p,\mathrm{eff}}^{2}(\Theta)=\frac{n_{0}e^{2}}{\epsilon_{0}m_{\mathrm{eff}}(\Theta)},\qquad\Omega_{\mathrm{eff}}(\Theta)=\frac{eB_{0}}{m_{\mathrm{eff}}(\Theta)}.

(87)

Using these definitions, the inertial scale appearing in the reduced

d_{h}^{2}(\Theta)=\frac{h(\Theta)c^{2}}{\omega_{p}^{2}},

(88)

may be written as

d_{h}(\Theta)=\frac{c}{\omega_{p,\mathrm{eff}}(\Theta)},

(89)

showing that the reduced electromagnetic operator retains the same
structure as the conventional plasma dielectric response while the
effective inertia varies with temperature.

Cold limit.

When
\Theta\ll 1

h(\Theta)\rightarrow 1,

(90)

so that

m_{\mathrm{eff}}\rightarrow m,

(91)

and the reduced dispersion relation (
52

reduces to the standard cold-plasma form.

Relativistic thermal regime.

For moderately relativistic temperatures
\Theta\sim 1

factor increases the effective inertia,

m_{\mathrm{eff}}\approx m(1+4\Theta),

(92)

which modifies both the plasma frequency and the cyclotron frequency
through the reduced inertial mass.

Ultrarelativistic limit.

In the ultrarelativistic regime
\Theta\gg 1

(93)

increased inertia while preserving the same reduced field structure.

These parameter relations show that the reduced model preserves the standard electromagnetic plasma-wave structure while incorporating relativistic kinetic effects through temperature-dependent effective inertia. The thermodynamic sector enters through

\mathcal{C}(\omega,\Theta)=\frac{\Gamma(\Theta)\lambda_{\alpha}(\Theta)q}{1-i\omega\tau_{\alpha}(\Theta)},

(94)

which provides a slow correction to the instantaneous electromagnetic response. The framework therefore interpolates smoothly across cold, relativistic, and ultrarelativistic regimes while adding a derived slow thermodynamic branch.

IV
Applications

The reduced closure developed above provides a framework for describing electromagnetic wave propagation in relativistic magnetized plasmas whose unresolved thermodynamic sector evolves on timescales longer than the wave period. In pair-plasma and pulsar applications, this allows one to separate fast electromagnetic response from slow irreversible kinetic evolution while preserving thermodynamic consistency through the GENERIC structure.

This viewpoint is particularly natural in environments where pair creation, discharge cycles, and radiative or kinetic relaxation are all dynamically important. Polar-cap pair discharges and pair-cascade models already show that pulsar plasmas can evolve through repeating screening and unscreening episodes rather than through a single fixed equilibrium state
32
;
31
;
30
. More recent work has connected such discharge dynamics and pair-loaded plasma variability to coherent radio-emission phenomenology
26
;
5
;
9
. Within that broader context, the present reduced closure supplies a minimal thermodynamic mechanism by which an approximately steady macroscopic magnetosphere can still exhibit a drifting local electromagnetic response.

The model also suggests a useful conceptual bridge to noise-free continuum kinetic calculations. One of the advantages emphasized in modern continuum relativistic Vlasov–Maxwell work is the ability to access local phase-space structure without the Monte Carlo noise intrinsic to PIC methods
2
;
19
. Such clean phase-space access is especially valuable for diagnostics based on field–particle correlations and pressure-tensor-mediated energy transfer
20
;
10
. Although the present paper develops a reduced analytical closure rather than a continuum DG algorithm, it is motivated by the same physical idea: unresolved kinetic physics should be represented in a way that preserves thermodynamic meaning rather than being absorbed into a purely phenomenological damping coefficient.

A second application is to strongly magnetized relativistic plasmas where aligned coordinates or reduced velocity-space descriptions become advantageous
18
. In such settings, the present one-fluid GENERIC closure can be interpreted as an asymptotic thermodynamic reduction of a more detailed kinetic description. The slow variable
\alpha
then summarizes the dominant unresolved kinetic direction most relevant to the reduced electromagnetic response.

Finally, the framework is compatible with recent solver-enabled astrophysical applications in which weak electromagnetic signals emerge from plasma instabilities in magnetar or pulsar-like environments
35
. In the present theory language, such signals would correspond not only to ordinary electromagnetic eigenmodes but also to their slow modulation by the evolving projected thermodynamic sector. This makes the reduced closure a candidate intermediate model between fully kinetic simulation and purely frozen-equilibrium wave theory.

\tau_{\alpha}\rightarrow 0
, the slow variable is slaved to the field,

\alpha=\lambda_{\alpha}(\Theta)\,\nabla_{\perp}^{2}\phi+O(\epsilon^{2}),

(A35)

and the three-field system reduces to the conventional two-field reduced
electromagnetic closure with modified effective coefficients.

Cold limit.

When
\Theta\ll 1
, the relativistic enthalpy factor tends to unity and the
effective inertial scales reduce to their standard cold-plasma values,
recovering the conventional reduced cold electromagnetic structure.

A.10
Schematic derivation of the unresolved operator from the linearized relativistic moment equations

In this subsection we show schematically how the unresolved operator

A_{\mathrm{high}}
arises from the linearized relativistic moment
hierarchy. The purpose is not to reproduce the full tensor algebra of the
parent kinetic system, but to demonstrate that each entry of

A_{\mathrm{high}}
corresponds to a definite moment-balance contribution
generated by the specified kinetic operator.

Starting point.

We begin from the relativistic kinetic equation

p^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}f_{s}+q_{s}F^{\mu\nu}p_{\nu}\frac{\partial f_{s}}{\partial p^{\mu}}=-\frac{u_{\alpha}p^{\alpha}}{\tau_{c}c^{2}}\left(f_{s}-f_{s}^{(J)}\right)+\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{i}}\left[D^{ij}(p_{j}-hmu_{j})f_{s}\right].

(A36)

Linearization is performed about a homogeneous Maxwell–Jüttner
background with guide field
\mathbf{B}_{0}=B_{0}\hat{z}
, vanishing equilibrium
flow, and pair-symmetric equilibrium densities and temperatures.

Using the unresolved vector defined in Sec.
A.2
, the
linearized moment balances take the following reduced form.

Linearized density-difference balance.

Taking the species-difference of the continuity equations and retaining the
leading reduced-order couplings gives

\partial_{t}\delta\hat{n}=-\nu_{n}\,\delta\hat{n}+\eta_{n}\,\Delta\hat{\Pi}+b_{n}\nabla_{\perp}^{2}\phi+O(\epsilon^{2}).

(A37)

The first term represents direct relaxation of charge imbalance induced by
the Anderson–Witting operator. The coupling proportional to
\eta_{n}

arises because pressure anisotropy modifies the compressive response of the
species-difference sector under the reduced ordering. The forcing term

b_{n}\nabla_{\perp}^{2}\phi
is the curvature or reduced-vorticity forcing
generated by the resolved perpendicular flow.

Linearized pressure-anisotropy balance.

Projecting the linearized pressure-tensor equation onto the scalar
combination
\Delta P_{\parallel}-\Delta P_{\perp}
gives

\partial_{t}\Delta\hat{\Pi}=-\nu_{\Pi}\,\Delta\hat{\Pi}+\eta_{\Pi}\,\Delta\hat{q}+b_{\Pi}\nabla_{\perp}^{2}\phi+O(\epsilon^{2}).

(A38)

Here
\nu_{\Pi}
receives contributions from both the isotropizing
Anderson–Witting relaxation and the perpendicular anisotropic drag

\nu_{\perp}
. The coefficient
\eta_{\Pi}
arises because the heat-flux
equation enters the pressure evolution at the next rank of the moment
hierarchy. The forcing coefficient
b_{\Pi}
comes from the projection of
the reduced field curvature drive into the pressure-anisotropy channel.

Linearized heat-flux-asymmetry balance.

Projecting the rank-three moment equation onto the species-difference
parallel heat-flux component yields

\partial_{t}\Delta\hat{q}=-\nu_{q}\,\Delta\hat{q}+b_{q}\nabla_{\perp}^{2}\phi+O(\epsilon^{2}).

(A39)

The damping rate
\nu_{q}
arises from the relaxation of the rank-three
moment under the Anderson–Witting operator together with the parallel drag
rate
\nu_{\parallel}
. At the order retained here there is no direct
back-coupling from
\hat{\Pi}_{\mathrm{irr}}
into
\Delta\hat{q}
, which is
why the corresponding upper-right entry of
A_{\mathrm{high}}
vanishes.
Likewise, to leading reduced order the resolved forcing enters through the
same curvature source
\nabla_{\perp}^{2}\phi
.

Linearized irreversible production balance.

Finally, let
\Pi_{\mathrm{irr}}
denote the lowest retained scalar moment
representing entropy-producing unresolved production. Its linearized
balance has the form

\partial_{t}\hat{\Pi}_{\mathrm{irr}}=\chi_{n}\,\delta\hat{n}+\chi_{\Pi}\,\Delta\hat{\Pi}+\chi_{q}\,\Delta\hat{q}-\nu_{I}\,\hat{\Pi}_{\mathrm{irr}}+O(\epsilon^{2}).

(A40)

The coefficients
\chi_{n},\chi_{\Pi},\chi_{q}
represent feeding of the slow
irreversible production channel by the faster unresolved density,
anisotropy, and heat-flux sectors. The rate
\nu_{I}
is the intrinsic
relaxation rate of the irreversible production moment. By construction,
this is the slowest rate in the unresolved block.

Matrix form.

Collecting
Eqs. (
A37
)–(
A40
) yields

\partial_{t}\mathbf{m}_{\mathrm{high}}=A_{\mathrm{high}}\mathbf{m}_{\mathrm{high}}+B_{\mathrm{high}}\nabla_{\perp}^{2}\phi+O(\epsilon^{2}),

(A41)

with

A_{\mathrm{high}}=\begin{pmatrix}-\nu_{n}&\eta_{n}&0&0\\
0&-\nu_{\Pi}&\eta_{\Pi}&0\\
0&0&-\nu_{q}&0\\
\chi_{n}&\chi_{\Pi}&\chi_{q}&-\nu_{I}\end{pmatrix},\qquad B_{\mathrm{high}}=\begin{pmatrix}b_{n}\\
b_{\Pi}\\
b_{q}\\
0\end{pmatrix}.

(A42)

The triangular structure of
A_{\mathrm{high}}
reflects the hierarchical
ordering of the relativistic moment system: density-difference perturbations
couple to pressure anisotropy, which couples to heat flux, while the
irreversible production channel receives contributions from all faster
unresolved moments. The absence of certain off-diagonal terms follows from
the reduced ordering and the truncation of the hierarchy at one rank above
the heat-flux sector.

This construction shows that each entry of the unresolved operator

A_{\mathrm{high}}
corresponds to a definite contribution from the
linearized relativistic moment equations generated by the specified kinetic
parent model. Consequently the slow thermodynamic coordinate
\alpha

identified in Eq. (
A18
) is not introduced
phenomenologically but arises as the slow eigenmode of the unresolved
moment dynamics.

Appendix B: Derivation of the Reduced Closure Coefficients from the Relativistic Kinetic Parent Model

In this Appendix we show that the closure relations used in the reduced system
(Eqs. (43)–(45)) follow directly from the relativistic kinetic parent model
introduced in Appendix A when the unresolved moment hierarchy is reduced using
a slow–manifold projection of the linearized collision operator. This removes
the remaining phenomenological assumptions and shows that the reduced closure
is the leading-order kinetic reduction consistent with the specified parent
theory.

1. Linearized kinetic operator and moment hierarchy

Consider the relativistic kinetic equation

p^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}f_{s}+q_{s}F^{\mu\nu}p_{\nu}\frac{\partial f_{s}}{\partial p^{\mu}}=\mathcal{C}_{s}[f],

(A43)

with collision operator

\mathcal{C}_{s}[f]=-\frac{u_{\alpha}p^{\alpha}}{\tau_{c}c^{2}}\left(f_{s}-f^{(J)}_{s}\right)+\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{i}}\left[D_{ij}(p_{j}-hmu_{j})f_{s}\right].

(A44)

Linearization about a homogeneous Maxwell–Jüttner equilibrium

f_{s}=f_{s0}+\delta f_{s}
yields

\partial_{t}\delta f_{s}=\mathcal{L}_{s}\delta f_{s}+\mathcal{F}_{s}[\phi,A_{\parallel}],

(A45)

where
\mathcal{L}_{s}
is the linearized kinetic operator and

\mathcal{F}_{s}
represents forcing from the resolved electromagnetic fields.

Define the moment vector

M=\{N^{\mu},\,T^{\mu\nu},\,Q^{\mu\nu\lambda},\,R^{\mu\nu\lambda\sigma},\dots\}.

(A46)

Taking moments of the linearized kinetic equation gives the hierarchy

\partial_{t}M=\mathcal{A}M+\mathcal{B}[\phi,A_{\parallel}],

(A47)

where the operator
\mathcal{A}
arises from the linearized collision operator
and
\mathcal{B}
from electromagnetic forcing.

2. Separation of resolved and unresolved sectors

Under the strong-guide-field ordering

\frac{k_{\parallel}}{k_{\perp}}\sim\epsilon,\qquad\frac{\omega}{\Omega_{c}}\sim\epsilon,

(A48)

the hierarchy separates into

M=\begin{pmatrix}M_{\rm res}\\
M_{\rm high}\end{pmatrix},

(A49)

where

M_{\rm res}=(\phi,A_{\parallel}),

(A50)

and the unresolved sector is

M_{\rm high}=(\delta n,\,\Delta\Pi,\,\Delta q,\,\Pi_{\rm irr}).

(A51)

Projection of the moment hierarchy onto this decomposition yields

\displaystyle\partial_{t}M_{\rm res}

\displaystyle=A_{rr}M_{\rm res}+A_{rh}M_{\rm high},

(A52)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}M_{\rm high}

\displaystyle=A_{hh}M_{\rm high}+B_{h}\nabla_{\perp}^{2}\phi.

(A53)

The matrix
A_{hh}
is the unresolved moment operator obtained from the
linearized relativistic collision operator.

3. Spectral decomposition of the unresolved sector

Let the eigenvalue decomposition of
A_{hh}
be

A_{hh}r_{i}=-\nu_{i}r_{i}.

(A54)

Because the Anderson–Witting relaxation and drag operators strongly damp
higher moments, the eigenvalues satisfy

\nu_{1}\ll\nu_{2},\nu_{3},\nu_{4}.

(A55)

Thus the unresolved sector possesses a single isolated slow eigenmode.
Let
l_{\alpha}
and
r_{\alpha}
denote the corresponding left and right
eigenvectors,

\displaystyle l_{\alpha}^{\top}A_{hh}

\displaystyle=-\nu_{I}l_{\alpha}^{\top},

(A56)

\displaystyle A_{hh}r_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=-\nu_{I}r_{\alpha},

(A57)

with normalization
l_{\alpha}^{\top}r_{\alpha}=1
.

The slow thermodynamic coordinate is therefore uniquely defined as

\alpha=l_{\alpha}^{\top}M_{\rm high}.

(A58)

All faster unresolved components decay on timescales
\nu_{i}^{-1}
and may
be eliminated using standard center-manifold reduction.

4. Slow-manifold reduction

Express the unresolved vector as

M_{\rm high}=\alpha r_{\alpha}+M_{\rm fast}.

(A59)

The fast components satisfy

\partial_{t}M_{\rm fast}=-\Lambda_{\rm fast}M_{\rm fast}+O(\epsilon),

(A60)

where
\Lambda_{\rm fast}
contains the large relaxation rates

\nu_{2},\nu_{3},\nu_{4}
.

Thus on timescales
t\gg\nu_{2,3,4}^{-1}

M_{\rm fast}\approx 0,

(A61)

and the unresolved sector lies on the slow manifold

M_{\rm high}\approx\alpha r_{\alpha}.

(A62)

5. Derived relaxation equation

Projecting the unresolved subsystem onto the slow eigenvector gives

\partial_{t}\alpha=l_{\alpha}^{\top}A_{hh}M_{\rm high}+l_{\alpha}^{\top}B_{h}\nabla_{\perp}^{2}\phi.

(A63)

Using the eigenvalue relation and slow-manifold representation yields

\partial_{t}\alpha=-\nu_{I}\alpha+l_{\alpha}^{\top}B_{h}\nabla_{\perp}^{2}\phi.

(A64)

Thus

\tau_{\alpha}=\nu_{I}^{-1},\qquad\lambda_{\alpha}=l_{\alpha}^{\top}B_{h},

(A65)

giving the relaxation law

\tau_{\alpha}\partial_{t}\alpha+\alpha=\lambda_{\alpha}\nabla_{\perp}^{2}\phi.

(A66)

6. Closure of unresolved field corrections

The unresolved contributions entering the reduced field equations are
linear functionals of the moment vector,

\Xi_{\phi}=c_{\phi}^{\top}M_{\rm high},\qquad\Xi_{A}=c_{A}^{\top}M_{\rm high}.

(A67)

Substituting the slow-manifold representation yields

\Xi_{\phi}=\Gamma_{\phi}\alpha,\qquad\Xi_{A}=\Gamma_{A}\alpha,

(A68)

where

\Gamma_{\phi}=c_{\phi}^{\top}r_{\alpha},\qquad\Gamma_{A}=c_{A}^{\top}r_{\alpha}.

(A69)

These coefficients are therefore uniquely determined by the same
slow eigenvector defining
\alpha
.

7. Resulting closed reduced system

The reduced plasma dynamics therefore close on the state vector

X=(\phi,A_{\parallel},\alpha)

(A70)

with evolution equations

\displaystyle\partial_{t}Q_{\phi}+v_{A}\nabla_{\parallel}A_{\parallel}

\displaystyle=N_{\phi}-\Gamma_{\phi}\alpha,

(A71)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}Q_{A}+v_{A}\nabla_{\parallel}\left(\phi-\rho_{h}^{2}\nabla_{\perp}^{2}\phi-\Gamma_{A}\alpha\right)

\displaystyle=N_{A},

(A72)

\displaystyle\tau_{\alpha}\partial_{t}\alpha+\alpha

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\alpha}\nabla_{\perp}^{2}\phi.

(A73)

All closure coefficients are therefore derived from the spectral
data of the unresolved kinetic operator and the projection of the
moment hierarchy.

Consequently the reduced one-fluid closure is the unique
leading-order slow-manifold reduction of the specified relativistic
kinetic parent model under the strong-guide-field ordering.
