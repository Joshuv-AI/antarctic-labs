 Testing machine-learning physics models of ICF/IFE implosion performance
 G. Sutcliffe and M. Jones
                                        LDRD: 26-FS-008

Project Overview
Three-dimensional asymmetries in inertial confinement fusion implosions can reduce yield and
gain, but they are difficult and computationally expensive to model. This project expanded on a
machine-learning framework that combines fast, reduced-physics rocket-piston simulations
with experimental measurements, including neutron images and scalar diagnostics. The
framework reconstructs implosion conditions, estimates unmeasured quantities, fills gaps in
incomplete diagnostic data, and represents uncertainty in its predictions.
The project demonstrated that the framework can incorporate sparse experimental data and
varying diagnostic coverage. By systematically removing individual neutron-imaging views and
energy channels, the analysis identified which measurements most effectively constrain
particular implosion asymmetries and performance parameters. These results provide a
practical method for comparing candidate diagnostic suites and determining the marginal value
of individual measurements. The capability can support diagnostic planning for NIF EYC and
future NextGen inertial confinement fusion and inertial fusion energy facilities, where
optimized diagnostic coverage will be essential for understanding performance while managing
cost and complexity.

Mission Impact
This project supports the Cognitive Simulation Institutional Initiative goal to “accelerate the
integration of AI, high-performance computing, and empirical data for a range of scientific
applications” and the High Energy Density Science Core Competency by marrying our best
understanding of ICF/IFE-related physics and diagnostic expertise with AI data analysis
techniques to extract the most information from our simulations and data. This work aligns with
the IFE Institutional Initiative by taking a step toward understanding diagnostic requirements for
IFE plants.
