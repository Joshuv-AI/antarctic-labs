The unbreakable quasi-graphic matroids
Sayantani Bhattacharya, John David Clifton, Zach Walsh

Abstract
A matroid M M is unbreakable if it is connected and M / F M/F is connected for every flat F F of M M . Oxley and Pfeil characterized the unbreakable graphic matroids, and Fife, Mayhew, Oxley, and Semple characterized the graphs underlying 3 3 -connected unbreakable frame matroids. We extend the latter result by giving a complete characterization of the 3 3 -connected unbreakable quasi-graphic matroids. As a special case we obtain a characterization of the 3 3 -connected lifted-graphic matroids.



1
Introduction

A matroid
M
is
unbreakable
if it is connected, and
M/F
is connected for every flat
F
of
M
.
These matroids were first defined by Oxley and Pfeil
[
12
]
, and have since accrued a number of interesting equivalent characterizations.
For example, unbreakable matroids can be characterized by forbidden parallel minors
[
12
, Theorem 1.1]
, forbidding skew circuits in the dual matroid
[
12
, Theorem 1.1]
, or a symmetric circuit exchange axiom in the dual matroid
[
5
, Theorem 1.1]
, and unbreakable binary matroids can be characterized by a circuit-difference property
[
6
, Lemma 1.2]
.
Moreover, unbreakability may be a useful property for inductive arguments involving matroid minors as it generalizes the notion of
roundness
, which played a crucial role in
[
10
]
and
[
11
]
, for example.

In light of this list of properties of unbreakable matroids, it may be useful to know whether or not a given matroid is unbreakable.
The first result in this direction is due to Oxley and Pfeil
[
12
]
, who characterized the unbreakable regular matroids.
The following corollary gives a characterization of the unbreakable graphic matroids (writing
\si(G)
for the simplification of a graph
G
).

Theorem 1.1

(
[
12
, Theorem 1.2]
)
.

If
G
is a graph with no isolated vertices, then the graphic matroid
M(G)
is unbreakable if and only if
G
is loopless and
\si(G)
is a cycle or a complete graph.

Theorem
1.1
was extended by Fife, Mayhew, Oxley, and Semple
[
7
]
to the class of
3
-connected
frame matroids
, which is an important class of matroids that contains all graphic matroids, signed-graphic matroids, and bicircular matroids.
A frame matroid can be defined from a
biased graph
, which is a pair
(G,\mathcal{B})
where
G
is a graph and
\mathcal{B}
is a collection of cycles of
G
(called
balanced
cycles) that does not contain exactly two cycles from any theta subgraph of
G
.
The following is the main result of
[
7
]
; the lower bound on the number of vertices is sharp.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
7
, Theorem 1.2]
)
.

Let
M
be a
3
-connected unbreakable frame matroid on a graph
G
with
|V(G)|>6
and no isolated vertices.
Then
\si(G)
can be obtained from a complete graph by deleting the edges of a path of length at most two.

We will extend Theorem
1.2
to the class of
quasi-graphic matroids
, which is a broad class of matroids derived from graphs that properly contains the class of frame matroids.
A connected quasi-graphic matroid can be defined from a tuple
(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})
where
(G,\mathcal{B})
is a biased graph and
(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})
is a partition of the cycles of
G
so that every cycle in
\mathcal{L}
shares a vertex with every cycle in
\mathcal{F}
.
The triple
(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})
is a
proper tripartition
of the cycles of
G
.
When
\mathcal{L}=\varnothing
the quasi-graphic matroid of
(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})
is simply the frame matroid of
(G,\mathcal{B})
, and when
\mathcal{F}=\varnothing
the quasi-graphic matroid of
(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})
is the
lifted-graphic
matroid of
(G,\mathcal{B})
, which is another fundamental matroid that can be defined from a biased graph.
We will give more details about these matroids in Section
2
.
The following is our main result, and it implies Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
M
be a
3
-connected unbreakable quasi-graphic matroid on a graph
G
so that
|V(G)|>6
and each component has at least two vertices.
Then either
\si(G)
is a cycle and
M
is lifted-graphic, or
\si(G)
can be obtained from a complete graph by deleting the edges of a path of length at most two.

The condition that
|V(G)|>6
is necessary as there are several examples of a
3
-connected unbreakable frame matroid on a graph

so that
|V(G)|=6
and
\si(G)
is not a cycle or the complement of a path with at most two edges, as shown in Figure

.
Since adding an isolated vertex or a set of loops in
\mathcal{L}
at an isolated vertex does not change the quasi-graphic matroid, the condition that each component has at least two vertices is also necessary. Restricting to lifted-graphic matroids, we obtain the following new result as a corollary.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
M
be a
3
-connected unbreakable lifted-graphic matroid on a graph
G
so that
|V(G)|>6
and each component has at least two vertices.
Then
\si(G)
is a cycle, or
\si(G)
can be obtained from a complete graph by deleting the edges of a path of length at most two.

Fife, Mayhew, Oxley, and Semple also proved a partial converse to Theorem
1.2
by showing that if

can be obtained from a complete graph by deleting the edges of a path of length at most two, then there exists a
3
-connected unbreakable frame matroid

on
G

[
7
, Theorem 1.5]
.
We significantly generalize this result by giving a complete characterization of the
3
-connected unbreakable quasi-graphic matroids on a graph
G
with
|V(G)|>6
and
\si(G)
a cycle or the complement of a path with length at most two.
We separately consider the cases when
\si(G)
is a cycle and when

is nearly complete, and in each case we also characterize which quasi-graphic matroids on

are

-connected.
The following result shows that when

is a cycle and

is both lifted-graphic and
3
-connected,

is always unbreakable.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
M
be a lifted-graphic matroid on a graph
G
so that
\si(G)
is a cycle and
|V(G)|>6
.
Then

is
3
-connected if and only if

is simple and at most one edge of

is
3
-connected, then

is unbreakable.

When

is the complement of a path with at most two edges,
we show that

is unbreakable unless

has a highly structured

, which is a set

of edges so that

has an unbalanced cycle but

The exact statement (Theorem

) is somewhat technical, so for now we will only give an informal statement.
However, it is straightforward to determine when

is
3
-connected.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
M
be a quasi-graphic matroid on a graph

so that
\si(G)
is the complement of a path with length at most two and

.
Then

is

-connected if and only if

Theorems

,

together give a complete characterization of the
3
-connected unbreakable quasi-graphic matroids on graphs with more than six vertices.
This leads to the following question: what about unbreakable quasi-graphic matroids that are not
3
-connected?
If
M
is obtained from a
3
-connected unbreakable matroid by making a parallel copy of an element, then
M
is not
3
-connected but is unbreakable, so it suffices to consider simple matroids.
We give a complete answer to the above question in Section
7
by considering two cases separately.
We say that
\mathcal{L}
(respectively
\mathcal{F}
) is
degenerate
if it contains no vertex-disjoint cycles, and is otherwise
non-degenerate.
If
\mathcal{L}
(respectively
\mathcal{F}
) is degenerate, then
M(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})
is the frame matroid (respectively lifted-graphic matroid) of
(G,\mathcal{B})
.
We will show that, for simple quasi-graphic matroids

, the structure imposed by having non-degenerate collections
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{F}
is incompatible with the structure imposed by being unbreakable when
M
is not
3
-connected.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
M=M(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})
be a simple connected quasi-graphic matroid such that neither
\mathcal{L}
nor
\mathcal{F}
is degenerate. If
M
is not 3-connected, then
M
is not unbreakable.

In light of the above results, to obtain a complete characterization of the unbreakable quasi-graphic matroids it suffices to consider frame matroids or lifted-graphic matroids that are not
3
-connected. To this end, we utilize additional results of
[
12
]
, along with Theorem
1.3
, to articulate a 2-sum decomposition of the connected but not 3-connected unbreakable frame and lifted-graphic matroids.
Here a
free element
of a matroid is an element that is in a circuit and is only in spanning circuits.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
M
be a simple frame or lifted-graphic matroid that is connected but not 3-connected. Then
M
is unbreakable if and only if
M
is obtained via 2-sums along free elements with a sequence of either unbreakable frame matroids or unbreakable lifted-graphic matroids such that:

(i)

If
M
is a frame matroid, the rank of each summand is at most 6.

(ii)

If
M
is lifted-graphic, each summand with rank greater than 6 is on a graph which simplifies to a cycle.

(iii)

If any summand is also graphic, it has a graph which is a cycle on exactly 3 vertices.

2
Background

In this section we will give some background information on biased graphs, frame matroids, lifted-graphic matroids, and quasi-graphic matroids.

2.1
Biased graphs and their matroids

A
theta graph
is a graph consisting of two vertices with three internally vertex-disjoint paths between them.
A
biased graph
is a pair
(G,\mathcal{B})
where
G
is a graph and
\mathcal{B}
is a set of cycles of
G
that satisfies the
theta property
: no theta subgraph contains exactly two cycles in
\mathcal{B}
.
Biased graphs were originally introduced by Zaslavsky
[
14
]
as combinatorial objects that store information about certain group-labelings of the edges of a graph.
The cycles in
\mathcal{B}
are
balanced
and the cycles not in
\mathcal{B}
are
unbalanced
.
More generally, a set
X
of edges of
G
is
balanced
if every cycle of
G[X]
is balanced.

Every biased graph
(G,\mathcal{B})
has two associated matroids on
E(G)
, both originally defined by Zaslavsky
[
15
]
, in which the circuits are particular types of biased subgraphs.
A
tight handcuff
is a graph consisting of two edge-disjoint cycles with exactly one common vertex, and a
loose handcuff
is a graph consisting of two vertex-disjoint cycles with a minimal path connecting them.
Equivalently, they are subdivisions of the graphs shown in Figure
1
.
The
frame matroid
of
(G,\mathcal{B})
, denoted
F(G,\mathcal{B})
, is the matroid on
E(G)
whose circuits are the balanced cycles, tight handcuffs with both cycles unbalanced, theta graphs with all cycles unbalanced, and loose handcuffs with both cycles unbalanced.
Note that if all cycles are balanced, then
F(G,\mathcal{B})
is the graphic matroid of
G
.
Important special types of frame matroids include signed-graphic matroids, Dowling geometries, and bicircular matroids.

The
lift matroid
of
(G,\mathcal{B})
, denoted
L(G,\mathcal{B})
, is the matroid on
E(G)
whose circuits are the balanced cycles, tight handcuffs with both cycles unbalanced, theta graphs with all cycles unbalanced, and unions of two vertex-disjoint unbalanced cycles.
If all cycles are balanced,
L(G,\mathcal{B})
is the graphic matroid of
G
.
A matroid is
lifted-graphic
if it is isomorphic to the lift matroid of a biased graph.
Lifted-graphic matroids are very close to being graphic: a matroid
M
is lifted-graphic if and only if there is a matroid
N
with an element
e
so that
N\!\setminus\!e=M
, and
N/e
is graphic.
Important special cases of lifted-graphic matroids include even-cycle matroids and spikes.

2.2
Quasi-graphic matroids

While the classes of frame matroids and lifted-graphic matroids are fundamental, they have the following significant drawbacks: both classes have an infinite list of excluded minors
[
3
]
, and neither class has a polynomial-time recognition algorithm
[
4
]
.
In an attempt to bypass these undesirable properties, Geelen, Gerards, and Whittle
[
9
]
defined a common generalization of frame matroids and lifted-graphic matroids.
A matroid
M
is
quasi-graphic
if it has a
framework
: that is, a graph
G
with
(i)\ E(G)=E(M)
, such that
(ii)
the rank of the edge set of each component of
G
is at most the size of its vertex set,
(iii)
for each vertex
v\in V(G)
the closure in
M
of
E(G-v)
does not contains an edge with endpoints
v,w
with
w\neq v
, and
(iv)
no circuit of
M
induces a subgraph in
G
of more than two components.

The class of quasi-graphic matroids is closed under direct sums (by taking disjoint unions of frameworks) and minors
[
9
, Lemma 4.1]
, and contains the class of frame matroids
[
9
, Theorem 1.3]
and the class of lifted-graphic matroids
[
9
, Theorem 1.2]
.
Moreover, it is conjectured that the list of excluded minors for the class of quasi-graphic matroids is finite
[
3
, Conjecture 1.5]
, and that there is a polynomial-time algorithm for recognition of
3
-connected quasi-graphic matroids
[
9
, Conjecture 1.7]
.

Quasi-biased graphs.

It is straightforward to show that if
M
is a quasi-graphic matroid with framework
G
then the set
\mathcal{B}
of cycles of
G
that are circuits of
M
satisfies the theta property
[
9
, Lemma 3.2]
, so every quasi-graphic matroid has a unique associated underlying biased graph.
While this biased graph does not uniquely determine
M
, Bowler, Funk, and Slilaty
[
2
]
show if
G
or
M
is connected then
M
can be recovered with slightly more information about the unbalanced cycles.
If
G
is a graph and
(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})
is a partition of its cycles, then
(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})
is a
proper tripartition
if the cycles in
\mathcal{B}
satisfy the theta property and every cycle in
\mathcal{L}
meets every cycle in
\mathcal{F}
.
An unbalanced cycle is
frame-type
if it is in
\mathcal{F}
and
lift-type
if it is in
\mathcal{L}
.
Bowler, Funk, and Silaty
[
1
, Theorem 2.3]
show that there is a matroid
M(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})
with ground set
E(G)
and the following circuits:

•

cycles in
\mathcal{B}
,

•

theta subgraphs with no cycles in
\mathcal{B}
,

•

tight handcuffs with neither cycle in
\mathcal{B}
,

•

unions of two vertex-disjoint cycles in
\mathcal{L}
, and

•

loose handcuffs with both cycles in
\mathcal{F}
.

They prove that the class of matroids that arise in this way is very closely related to the class of quasi-graphic matroids.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
2
, Theorem 2]
)
.

Let
M
be a matroid and let
(G,\mathcal{B})
be a biased graph with
E(G)=E(M)
.
Consider the following statements.

(1)

M
is quasi-graphic with framework
G
and
\mathcal{B}
is the set of cycles of
G
that are circuits of
M
.

(2)

There is a proper tripartition
(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})
of the cycles of
G
such that
M=M(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})
.

Then
(2)
implies
(1)
, and if
M
is connected or
G
is connected, then
(1)
implies
(2)
.

It is straightforward to see that the condition in Theorem
2.1
that
M
or
G
is connected cannot be removed: the direct sum of a frame matroid and a lifted-graphic matroid is quasi-graphic, but does not always arise via a proper tripartition.

While our main results concern quasi-graphic matroids, it will be more convenient to work with matroids that arise from proper tripartitions.
To this affect, we make the following definition.

Definition 2.2
.

A
quasi-biased graph
is a tuple
(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})
where
G
is a graph and
(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})
is a proper tripartition of the cycles of
G
.

Since we will only be concerned with connected matroids in this paper, we will not lose any generality by considering the quasi-graphic matroids that arise from quasi-biased graphs.
We say that
(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})
is
connected
if
G
is connected.
One benefit of working with quasi-biased graphs instead of frameworks is that it is very clear how matroids from quasi-biased graphs relate to frame matroids and lifted-graphic matroids.
Given a quasi-biased graph
(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})
, the collection
\mathcal{L}
(respectively
\mathcal{F}
) is
degenerate
if no two cycles of
\mathcal{L}
(respectively
\mathcal{F}
) are vertex-disjoint, and is otherwise
non-degenerate
. Observe that if
\mathcal{L}
is degenerate, then
M(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})
is equal to
F(G,\mathcal{B})
, the frame matroid of
(G,\mathcal{B})
.
Similarly if
\mathcal{F}
is degenerate, then
M(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})
is equal to
L(G,\mathcal{B})
, the lifted-graphic matroid of
(G,\mathcal{B})
.
In particular, note that if either of
\mathcal{L}
or
\mathcal{F}
contains a loop, then necessarily the other set is degenerate as all cycles in
\mathcal{L}
or
\mathcal{F}
must meet this loop.

We comment that if
M
is a connected quasi-graphic matroid with rank at least two, then there is a quasi-biased graph
(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})
so that
M=M(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})
and each component of
G
has at least two vertices. To see this, note that a component with only one vertex consists of either an isolated vertex or a vertex with incident loops. Clearly deleting isolated vertices does not change the underlying quasi-graphic matroid. Since
M
is connected it has no balanced loops, and it is straightforward to see that if a vertex is incident with any lift-type loops, these loops may be freely placed on any vertex of
G
without changing the circuits of the matroid. If any component consists of a vertex incident with a frame-type loop, this contradicts that
M
is connected since
M
has rank at least two.

Rank and minors.

We will need to know the independent sets and rank function of matroids associated with quasi-biased graphs.
Bowler, Funk, and Slilaty stated the rank function of such a matroid
[
1
, Lemma 2.4]
.

Proposition 2.3

(
[
1
, Lemma 2.4]
)
.

Let
(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})
be a quasi-biased graph and let
X
be a subset of

. Then the rank of

is given by:

\displaystyle r(X)=\begin{cases}\lvert V(G[X])\rvert-b(X)&\textit{if $X$ contains a cycle in $\mathcal{F}$}\\
\\
\lvert V(G[X])\rvert-c(X)+l(X)&\textit{otherwise}\end{cases}

where
b(X)
is the number of balanced components of
G[X]
,
c(X)
is the number of components of
G[X]
, and

is
1
if

contains an unbalanced cycle and
0
otherwise.

In particular, note that if

is connected and has an unbalanced cycle, then the rank of

is equal to

.
From the rank function we can recover the independent sets.

Proposition 2.4

(
[
1
, Theorem 5.1]
)
.

Let
(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})

(i)

(ii)

G[X]

.

(iii)

Each component of
G[X]
has at most one cycle, each of which is in
\mathcal{F}
.

\displaystyle=\{C:C\in\mathcal{B}\ \text{or}\ C\cup e\in\mathcal{B}\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=\{C:C\in\mathcal{L}\ \text{or}\ C\cup e\in\mathcal{L}\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=\{C:C\in\mathcal{F}\ \text{or}\ C\cup e\in\mathcal{F}\}.

Again, it is straightforward to show that
M(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})/e=M((G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})/e)

[
1
, Theorem 4.5]
.

We are left to deal with the contraction of a loop
e
of
G
.
If
e\in\mathcal{B}
then
e
is also a loop of
M(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})
, so we can define
(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})/e
to be
(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})\!\setminus\!e
.
If
e\notin\mathcal{B}
, then
M(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})
is a frame matroid (if
\{e\}\in\mathcal{F}
) or a lifted-graphic matroid (if
\{e\}\in\mathcal{L}
) and we consider these cases separately, as in
[
8
]
.
If
\{e\}\in\mathcal{L}
, we define
(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})/e=(G/e,\mathcal{C}(G/e),\varnothing,\varnothing)
where
\mathcal{C}(G/e)
is the set of all cycles of
G/e
.
In other words, to contract a loop in
\mathcal{L}
, delete the loop and declare all cycles to be balanced.
As described in
[
8
, page 9]
,
M(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})/e=M((G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})/e)
in this case.
If
\{e\}\in\mathcal{F}
, define
(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})/e=(G^{\prime},\mathcal{B}^{\prime},\varnothing,\mathcal{C}(G^{\prime})-\mathcal{B}^{\prime})
where
G^{\prime}
is obtained from
G
by replacing each non-loop incident with
v
by a loop at its other end, and
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
consists of all cycles in
\mathcal{B}
disjoint from
v
and all loops of
G
incident with
v
.
To use language from
[
8
]
,
G^{\prime}
is obtained by taking each non-loop incident with

and “rolling it up” to a loop at its opposite endpoint.
As described in
[
8
, page 9]
,
M(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})/e=M((G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})/e)
in this case.

It is straightforward to show that contractions from
(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})
commute and that contractions and deletions commute, so for disjoint sets
X
and
Y
of edges of

we will write
(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})\!\setminus\!X/Y
for the quasi-biased graph obtained from
(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})
by deleting the edges in
X
and contracting the edges in
Y
in any order.
Note that if
Y
contains no cycles in
\mathcal{F}
, then the graph of
(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})\!\setminus\!X/Y
is

; we will freely use this fact.

We will need two straightforward properties of contraction for quasi-biased graphs.
The first helps us understand how unbalanced cycles behave with respect to contraction of balanced sets.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})
be a quasi-biased graph, let
C
be an unbalanced cycle, and let
F
be a flat of
M(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})
so that
C-F\neq\varnothing
and
F
contains no unbalanced cycles.
Then
C-F
contains an unbalanced cycle of
(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})/F
.

Proof.

It suffices to consider the case when
F
consists of a single edge
f
which is not an unbalanced loop. If
f
is an edge of
C
, then
C-f
is an unbalanced cycle of
(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})/f
. If
f
is incident with two vertices
\{u,v\}
of
C
, then in
G/f
, the edge set
C-f
is a disjoint union of two cycles
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
. At least one such cycle, say
C_{1}
must be unbalanced. To see this, suppose otherwise. Then each of the cycles
C_{i}\cup f
for
i=1,2
is balanced. By the theta property, this forces
C
to be balanced, a contradiction. Lastly consider the case when
f
is incident with at most one vertex of
C
. If
f
is a non-loop edge or a balanced loop, then
C
is an unbalanced cycle of
(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})/f
.

∎

We will also need to understand what happens when we contract a flat that contains a frame-type cycle.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})
be a quasi-biased graph so that
G
is connected, and let
M=M(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})
.
If
F
is a flat of
M
so that
r(M/F)\geq 1
and
F
contains a cycle in
\mathcal{F}
, then
(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})/F
has a frame-type loop.

Proof.

Let
T
be an edge-maximal forest of
G[F]
and let
(G^{\prime},\mathcal{B}^{\prime},\mathcal{L}^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})=(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})/\cl(T)
.
Note that
\cl(T)
is balanced and so
G^{\prime}=G/\cl(T)
.
Since
G
is connected this implies that
G^{\prime}
is connected.
Also, each element in
F-\cl(T)
is a loop of
G^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
.
Let
V^{\prime}
be the set of vertices of
G^{\prime}
with an incident loop in
F
.
Then the graph of
(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})/F
is obtained from
G^{\prime}
as follows: for each non-loop edge

with exactly one end in
V^{\prime}
, replace

with a frame-type loop at the end of

that is not in

.
Since
G^{\prime}
is connected, there is at least one non-loop edge with exactly one end in

, so
(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})/F
has at least one frame-type loop.
∎

Preliminaries

In this section we will state some preliminary lemmas for quasi-graphic matroids and unbreakable matroids.
We will follow
[
7
, Section 2]
very closely, with each lemma either taken verbatim or generalized from frame matroids to quasi-graphic matroids.

The first was proved by Oxley and Pfeil
[
12
]
and holds for general unbreakable matroids.

Lemma 3.1

(
[
7
, Lemma 2.1]
)
.

If
M
is an unbreakable matroid and
F
is a flat of
M
, then
M/F
is unbreakable.

We can use the rank function to certify that every induced subgraph has a natural corresponding flat. The following lemma generalizes
[
7
, Lemma 2.3]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})
be a quasi-biased graph,
L
its set of loops in
\mathcal{B}
, and
L^{\prime}
its set of loops in
\mathcal{L}
. Let
U\subseteq V(G).
If
E(G[U])
contains no cycles in
\mathcal{L}
, then
E(G[U])\cup L
is a flat of
M(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})
. Otherwise
E(G[U])\cup L\cup L^{\prime}
is a flat of
M(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{C}
denote the collection of circuits of
M
. We recall the following characterization of closure for a subset
X\subseteq E(M)
and any matroid
M
:

\displaystyle\cl(X)=X\cup\{x:\exists\ C\in\mathcal{C}\text{ so that }x\in C\subseteq X\cup x\}.

First suppose
E(G[U])
contains no cycles in
\mathcal{L}
. Then, for any edge
e
, if
e\in C\subseteq(E(G[U])\cup e)
for some circuit
C
, then
C
is either a balanced cycle, a theta graph with all cycles unbalanced, or a handcuff with both cycles unbalanced. For each of these cases either
e\in E(G[U])
or
e
is a balanced loop.

Now suppose
E(G[U])
contains a cycle in
\mathcal{L}
. Again, for any
e\in C\subseteq(E(G[U])\cup e)
, we have that either
e\in E(G[U])
,
e
is a balanced loop, or
e
is a loop in
\mathcal{L}
.
∎

We will also need the following lemma, which relies on Theorem 1.1.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})
be a quasi-biased graph and
M=M(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})
. If
C
is an unbalanced cycle, then
M/C
is either frame or lifted-graphic, depending on whether
C
belongs to
\mathcal{F}
or
\mathcal{L}
, respectively. Moreover, if
M
is unbreakable and

, then
\si(G/\cl(C))
is a complete graph or a cycle up to deleting isolated vertices.

Proof.

Let
e\in C
. Contracting the edges of
C-e
, we have an unbalanced loop resulting from

. Thus in
(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})/(C-e)
, one of
\mathcal{F}
or
\mathcal{L}
is degenerate, and

is either a frame matroid or a lifted-graphic matroid. Now suppose in addition that
C\in\mathcal{L}
. Then
(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})/C
has no unbalanced cycles, and its graph is

. Thus

is graphic and unbreakable.
So
\si(G/\cl(C))
is isomorphic to the cycle matroid of a complete graph or a cycle by Theorem
1.1
.
∎

A utility of this result is that we can occasionally default to the results of
[
7
]
even when properties outside of biased graphs are not sufficient, so long as our contraction consists of flats containing cycles in

.

The following three lemmas from

Lemma 3.4

(
[

, Lemma 2.4]
)
.

Let

.

Let

with

.

Let

The next lemma will help us identify when a quasi-graphic matroid is not unbreakable. It generalizes
[
7
, Lemma 2.7]
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
M=M(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})
be a quasi-graphic matroid where
G
is connected.
Let
C
be an unbalanced cycle and let
T
be a tree in
G
such that
V(C)\subseteq V(T)
and
G-V(T)
is disconnected.
Then
M
is not unbreakable.

Proof.

We may assume that
G
has no balanced loops. Suppose that
C
is an unbalanced cycle and
T
is a tree in
G
such that
V(C)\subseteq V(T)
. Let
L^{\prime}
denote the set of loops in
\mathcal{L}
. Note that
G/T
contains an unbalanced loop
e
that is incident with the compound vertex obtained by identifying the vertices of
T
. Observe that this vertex is a cutvertex of
G/T
. As well, by Lemma
3.2
either
E(G[V(T)])
or
E(G[V(T)])\cup L^{\prime}
is a flat of
M
.

If
C
belonged to
\mathcal{L}
, then after contracting the loop
e
in
G/T
, every cycle of the biased graph
G/(T\cup e)
is balanced, and the resulting matroid is graphic. Since it contains a cutvertex, the cycle matroid of this graph is not connected by
[

, Proposition 4.1.7]
. Since
E(G[V(T)]\cup L^{\prime}
is a flat of

by Lemma

, we conclude that

is not unbreakable.

If
C
belonged to
\mathcal{F}
, then

is a frame matroid. In this case, contracting the unbalanced loop

of

yields a biased graph

associated with the frame matroid

. This biased graph has more than one component having an edge. We deduce that

is disconnected. Observe that if

, then
\mathcal{F}
is degenerate. In this case, we may assume

and the result follows by the previous case. Thus we may assume

4
Beginning the proof of the main theorem

In this section we will prove Theorem
1.3
in the case that the underlying graph
G
is not
3
-connected.
Throughout this section we will assume that
M=M(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})
is a
3
-connected unbreakable quasi-graphic matroid so that each component of
G
has at least two vertices.

We will further assume that
M
is not graphic, which implies that
|E(G)|\geq 4
and not all cycles of
G
are balanced.

The next four lemmas all hold by the
3
-connectivity of
M
.
We will use the facts that
3
-connected matroids with at least four elements contain no loops, coloops, parallel pairs, or series pairs.

Lemma 4.1
.

G
is connected.

Proof.

Let
E=E(G)
.
First, we note a helpful result from
[
1
, Corollary 4.7]
, which states that for a connected quasi-graphic matroid
M=M(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})
, either
G
is connected or
M
is lifted-graphic. Suppose
G
is disconnected. By
[
1
, Corollary 4.7]
, we may assume
M
is lifted graphic.
Recall that every component of
G
has at least two vertices, by assumption.

If any component of
G
is balanced, then the edges of this component do not belong to any circuits with edges of other components, contradicting that
M
is connected. Thus we may assume that every component of
G
contains an unbalanced cycle.
Let
A
be a component of
G
.
We see that,

\displaystyle r(A)+r(E-E(A))

\displaystyle\leq\lvert V(A)\rvert+\lvert V(E-E(A))\rvert-(c(G)-1)+l(E-E(A))

\displaystyle\leq\lvert V(G)\rvert-(c(G)-1)+l(E-E(A))

\displaystyle\leq r(M)+1,

which contradicts that
M
is 3-connected.
∎

Since
G
is connected and not all cycles of
G
are balanced we see from Proposition
2.3
that
r(M)=|V(G)|
.

Lemma 4.2

(
[
7
, Lemma 3.1]
)
.

All
1
- and
2
-cycles in
G
are unbalanced.

Proof.

If
e
is a balanced loop then
M
is disconnected. Thus any
1
-cycle in
G
must be unbalanced. Next let
X
be a balanced
2
-cycle in
G
. Let
Y=E(G)-X
. Then,

\displaystyle r(X)+r(Y)-r(X\cup Y)

\displaystyle\leq\lvert V(X)\rvert-1+\lvert V(Y)\rvert-\lvert V(G)\rvert\leq 1.

This is a contradiction to
M
being
3
-connected. Thus, any
2
-cycle in
G
is unbalanced.
∎

The following result holds verbatim from
[
7
, Lemma 3.2]
. Its proof is included for the convenience of verifying the ranks involved.

Lemma 4.3

(
[
7
, Lemma 3.2]
)
.

G
has no vertex that meets fewer than three edges.

Proof.

Let
u\in V(G)
. Suppose
\lvert E_{u}\rvert\leq 2
. Then
r(E(G)-E_{u})\leq\lvert V(G-u)\rvert=\lvert V(G)\rvert-1
, so
E_{u}
contains a cocircuit of

. This contradicts the fact that
M
is 3-connected having at least four elements.
∎

Lemma 4.4

(
[
7
, Lemma 3.3]
)
.

G
is
2
-connected.

Proof.

Suppose that
G
has a cutvertex
v
. Let
A_{1}
be a component of
G-v
. Let
A
be the graph induced by the vertex set
V(A_{1})\cup v
, and let
B
be the graph induced by the edge set

. Then we know that
r(E(A))\leq|V(A)|
and
r(E(B))\leq|V(B)|
. Recall that, because

is connected and

is not graphic, we know that
r(M)=\lvert V(G)\rvert
.
Furthermore we can see that
|V(G)|=|V(A)|+|V(B)|-1
. Thus,

\displaystyle r(E(A))+r(E(B))-r(M)

\displaystyle\leq|V(A)|+|V(B)|-|V(G)|=1.

By Lemma

we see that each of

has size at least two, thus the rank calculation above contradicts that

is 3-connected.
∎

The following lemma differs significantly from the frame matroid case
[

, Lemma 3.4]
.

Lemma 4.5
.

If
\si(G)
is a cycle, then either
\lvert V(G)\rvert\leq 6
or
\mathcal{F}
is degenerate.

Proof.

Suppose that
\si(G)
is a cycle and that
\lvert V(G)\rvert\geq 7
. By Lemma
4.3
and Lemma

, each vertex of
G
meets an unbalanced cycle of length at most 2. If any loops of
G
belongs to
\mathcal{F}
then
\mathcal{L}
is degenerate, and

. By
[
7
, Lemma 3.4]
,
|V(G)|\leq 6
. Thus we may assume that any loops of

belong to
\mathcal{L}
. When
\mathcal{L}
contains a loop,
\mathcal{F}
is degenerate and the conclusion holds. Now suppose

has no loops. For a vertex

choose an incident unbalanced 2-cycle

. Note that, since

, for each vertex

there is a vertex

so that

We can now prove the first important case of our main result, generalizing

, Theorem 3.5]

Theorem 4.6

Let

with corresponding partition

of

We now consider the case when

\mathcal{F}

.

Since

∎

We have now characterized all unbreakable quasi-graphic matroids on a graph

is the complement of a path with at most two edges.
It is natural to wonder which of these quasi-graphic matroids are

Our next result answers this question.

Proposition 6.4
.

Let

\si(G)

Then

(G,\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})

Proof.

It is straightforward to see that if

M

.
Let

, so

.

Conversely, suppose that

is not

-connected. If all cycles are balanced, then
\si(G)=G
and

3
-connected and it follows from

is
3
-connected. So not all cycles are balanced, so

.
Then
E(M)
has a partition
(X,Y)

|X|,|Y|\geq i+1
for some

. We begin by showing the following.

6.4.1
.

G[X]
or
G[Y]
contains a spanning tree of
G
.

Proof.

Suppose

does not contain a spanning tree of

. We will show that with

contains a spanning tree of

, or

contains a forest with at least

|V(G)|-3
edges. Since

does not contain a spanning tree, it is disconnected and we will consider cases according to the number of components in

. First, assume that

has

having

G[Y]
contains

for some positive integers

. Observe that

is connected and does not have any

. Since
\si(G)
is obtained by deleting a path of length at most 2 from a complete graph,

contains a spanning tree. Next we assume that
G[X]
has
3
components. Then
G[Y]
contains

for integers

so that

.
Note that

has no cut-edge. So

has a
(|V(G)|-2
)-edge forest, and

has a
(|V(G)|-3)
-edge forest. Finally assume that

has
2

contains

for integers

contains a forest with

In all cases, by possibly switching

i\geq r(X)+r(Y)-r(M)\geq r(M)-2+r(M)-3-r(M)\geq r(M)-5

∎

So we may assume that

contains a spanning tree of

Otherwise

Theorem

6.2
Cycles

In this section we will characterize the unbreakable

We first characterize

Proposition 6.5
.

Let

such that
\si(G)
is a cycle and

. Then

is
3
-connected if and only if

does not have balanced loops or
2
-cycles,

has at most one unbalanced loop, and at most one edge of

is not in an unbalanced
2

Proof.

Suppose

is 3-connected. Then

has no balanced loops or 2-cycles. If

has at least two unbalanced loops, these loops are a parallel pair of

, a contradiction. Suppose by contradiction that

has a pair of edges
\{e,f\}
, neither of which belongs to an unbalanced 2-cycle. Letting

and

, we note that
\lvert Y\rvert\geq 2
since
\lvert V(G)\rvert\geq 4
. Moreover,
r(X)=2
and, since

is unbalanced,

by Proposition
2.3
because
G[Y]
has more components than

, contradicting that

Conversely, suppose that

has no balanced loops or
2
-cycles,
G
has at most one unbalanced loop, and at most one edge of
\si(G)
is not in an unbalanced
2
-cycle of

otherwise. Suppose that

has a partition
(X,Y)

\lvert X\rvert,\lvert Y\rvert\geq i+1

. First assume that

\lvert V(G)\rvert=\lvert V(G[X])\rvert+\lvert V(G[Y])\rvert-\lvert V(G[X])\cap V(G[Y])\rvert

\lvert V(G[X])\cap V(G[Y])\rvert\geq c(X)+c(Y)

\displaystyle r(X)+r(Y)

\displaystyle=\lvert V(G[X])\rvert-c(X)+l(X)+\rvert V(G[Y])\rvert-c(Y)+l(Y)

\displaystyle=\lvert V(G)\rvert+\lvert V(G[X])\cap V(G[Y])\rvert-(c(X)+c(Y))+2

\displaystyle\geq r(M)+2,

a contradiction.

Now suppose

. We claim that

contains a spanning tree of

. If

, we see that

. This contradicts that

, then

Then

∎

We next show that if

Proposition 6.6
.

If

-connected and

By Theorem

7

In this section we will prove Theorem
1.7

This section will utilize the notion of a

from

which explicitly verifies that the class of quasi-graphic matroids is closed under 2-sums with graphic matroids. Let
G_{1}
and

on basepoint

With the link-sum construction in mind, we have the following theorem of Bowler, Funk, and Slilaty

Theorem 7.1

(
[
1
, Theorem 4.8]
)

Let

The key is the following lemma. We say a 2-sum (or link-sum)

Lemma 7.2

Proof.

To prove this, we will follow the proof as
[

By
[

7.2.1

There is a balanced component of

Proof.

Suppose that the claim is false.
We use
r_{G}
to denote the rank function of

(

). We define

to be
(r(A)-r_{G}(A))+(r(B)-r_{G}(B))

tells us that

1=r(A)+r(B)-r(E(G))=(r_{G}(A)+r_{G}(B)-r_{G}(E(G)))-1+d\geq d,

since

in at least
