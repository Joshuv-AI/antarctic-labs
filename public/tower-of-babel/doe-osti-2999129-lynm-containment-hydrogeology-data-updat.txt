ABSTRACT
This report presents new two-phase flow parameters measured in samples of vitric tuff units located
stratigraphically above the PE1 A chamber, which intersect or are near the top of the B and DL
chambers. These two-phase flow data show the permeability, porosity, and mean pore size of these
units to be higher than the stratigraphic units below them. These data indicate the rocks support fast
gas transport (high porosity and permeability) across these units, depending on their water content.

1. TWO-PHASE FLOW PARAMETERS FROM P-TUNNEL CORE
Estimation of two-phase fluid flow properties is important to understand and predict both water
and gas flow through the vadose zone for agricultural, hydrogeological, and engineering applications
such as vapor-phase contaminant transport and/or containment of noble gases in the subsurface.
We report two-phase flow data on volcanic tuff core plug samples from Aqueduct Mesa and we
discuss possible implications for two-phase flow modeling.
This report is a Fiscal Year 2025 (FY25) update on the Hydrogeology task in Low Yield Nuclear
Monitoring (LYNM) Containment at Sandia National Laboratories (SNL). This document presents
recent measurements on the two-phase flow properties of tuff from Aqueduct Mesa.
In January 2025, samples of core were obtained from the Mercury Core Library, upon consultation
with geologists from Nevada National Security Site (NNSS). These samples were focused on the
vitric nonwelded tuff (VNT) units found stratigraphically above the units that were previously tested
(other VNT and upper zeolitic nonwelded tuff – UZNT). These samples were obtained to conduct
imbibition and drainage tests in units that are relevant to gas transport in the units found in P-
Tunnel.
To prepare the samples for imbibition and small centrifuge drainage tests, at least one sample end
each was cut perpendicular to the length of the core, as needed for testing. Scrap pieces that were
cut off as part of the preparation were sent to CoreSpec for mercury intrusion capillary pressure
(MICP) testing. The samples sent were sub-cored to be approximately 2.54-cm long cylinders 2.54
cm in diameter for MICP analysis. The sample names tested as part of this analysis consist of a
shortened version of the borehole name, the distance (in feet, including tenths) along that borehole,
and the stratigraphic subunit name, all separated by hyphens.

1.1. Mercury Intrusion Capillary Pressure (MICP) bulk data
These data are compared to data obtained previously for P-Tunnel core using the same method.
Table 1 lists the samples and gives their porosity, bulk density, and air permeability. The new
samples obtained in FY25 are given in bold at the end of the table. The table is organized in inverse
stratigraphically (the sample at the bottom of the table is stratigraphically on top, while the sample
on the top is stratigraphically lowest).
Sample porosity vs. air permeability are plotted in Figure 1. Each stratigraphic subunit is indicated
with a different symbol (see legend). Dashed ellipses indicate physically adjacent samples from a
previous round of testing. The differences between these samples give some indication of the
variability of the samples over the scale of a few cm.
Figure 2 plots the same data in Figure 1, but against the stratigraphic order they are shown in Table
1. The increasing trend with stratigraphic unit is clearer here. There are a couple units with
significant internal variability (i.e., VNT-4), and there are high-permeability samples from
stratigraphically lower subunits (i.e., VNT-6), but the overall trend holds that permeability and
porosity increase as elevation increases in the rocks presented in this data set at P-Tunnel.

2. CONSIDERATION OF MICP DATA FOR FUTURE PREDICTIONS
The data from FY25 presented in this report is from stratigraphically higher subunits than were
previously tested at P-Tunnel (previous tests were not conducted on samples above VNT-1).
Generally, stratigraphically lower rocks from P-Tunnel are more zeolitized and lower permeability,
especially the UZNT unit, which lies below VNT-10. The pore size distributions of samples from
UZNT, and VNT-10 through VNT-1 have more bimodal pore size distributions than the samples
observed in the upper stratigraphic units (which intersect or are near to the B and DL chambers).

Figure 17 shows the MICP data, with water saturation scaled by porosity (i.e., volumetric water
content). The previously tested samples from lower stratigraphic subunits (dashed) have a larger
proportion of small-diameter pores (i.e., are to the right at high capillary pressure), and a lower total

 16

porosity (i.e., curves end near the bottom middle of the diagram), than the FY25 tested samples.
Samples from the higher stratigraphic subunits tend to be higher total porosity, and a smaller
fraction of the total porosity tends to be small pores (small pores correspond to high capillary
pressures).

Both factors together (higher porosity and less-bimodal pore size distribution) lead to faster gas
transport through these layers in a single-phase system. The effects that water in the formation has
on gas transport would depend on the in situ saturation of the formation. If the saturation is not
near full liquid saturation, there should be enough connected gas pathway through the samples to
allow gas transport.

These observations of the porosity, permeability, and pore-size distribution should be useful for
refining pre-test predictions that involve the rocks stratigraphically above the B and DL chambers in
P-Tunnel. Two-phase numerical flow models require parameterization of the capillary pressure
curve, and the van Genuchten model fit here is a popular model to use.
