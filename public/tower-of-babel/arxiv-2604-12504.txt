Saeed shaabanian

Abstract
. Cover time, in the context of dynamical systems, quantifies the rate at which orbits cover the system. We prove that for countable full shifts with a Gibbs measure, equipped with a natural metric, the rate of covering of orbits of points behaves according to the minimum measure of balls. Moreover, this rate exhibits sensitivity to changes in the metric.

1.
Introduction

Assume that
(Y,d)
is a compact metric space and let
X\subseteq Y
. Consider
(f,\mu)
where
f:X\to X
and
\mu
is an ergodic probability measure.

For such a dynamical system, assume that
X=\overline{\{f^{n}x\}}_{n}
for
\mu
a.e.
x\in X
. We refer to this property as
covering
. The question that arises is, for the point
x
, when does covering to some scale
\delta
occur for the first time?
This question can be posed as follows.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\delta>0
. We say
A
is
\delta
-dense
in
B
if for all
b\in B
, there exists
a\in A
such that
d(a,b)\leq\delta.

Cover time quantifies the rate at which orbits become dense in
X
:

Definition 1.2
.

Let
\delta>0
. The
\delta
-covering time
is the function
\tau_{\delta}:X\to\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
such that

(1.1)

\tau_{\delta}(x):=\inf\{n\geq 1:\{x,f(x),\ldots,f^{n}(x)\}\;\text{is}\;\delta\text{-dense in}\;X\}.

A natural question here is, what is the asymptotic behaviour of the expected cover time in
(f,\mu)
i.e.
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(\tau_{\delta})
as
\delta\to 0
?

Cover time has been well studied in probability, such as in random-walk dynamics on graphs (
[
Al1
]
,
[
L
]
and
[
DLP
]
), Markov chains (
[
LP
]
), and stochastic geometry (
[
FN
]
,
[
J
]
,
[
P
]
and
[
Al2
]
). Recently, results have also been obtained in the chaos game using the cover time in
[
JM
,
BJK
]
. This concept was first studied by Jurga and Todd in the dynamical systems context
[
JT
]
.
They proved for a large class of interval maps that
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(\tau_{\delta})
is roughly like
\delta^{-\dim_{M}\mu}
as
\delta\to 0
, where

(1.2)

\dim_{M}\mu:=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\frac{\log M_{\mu}(\delta)}{\log\delta},

exists and is called the
Minkowski dimension
of
\mu
where

M_{\mu}(\delta):=\min_{x\in\text{supp}(\mu)}\mu(B(x,\delta)).

The Minkowski dimension was first introduced in
[
FFK
]
and by
[
FFK
, Proposition 4.2]
it is equal to the
L^{-\infty}
dimension.

The sharp result in
[
JT
]
is obtained for Gibbs Markov interval maps when the map has at least two full branches. In this case,
[
JT
, Theorem 2.1]
shows that
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(\tau_{\delta})
is approximately like
\delta^{-\dim_{H}\Lambda}\left(\log\frac{1}{\delta}\right)
, where
\dim_{H}\Lambda
is the Hausdorff dimension of the repeller.
To obtain these results, they used the symbolic dynamics associated with interval maps. This observation motivated us to pursue this question for a symbolic system equipped with an appropriate metric.

Assume that
(\Sigma,\sigma)
is a countable full shift which admits a Gibbs measure. Since the usual metric here is not totally bounded and would yield
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(\tau_{\delta})=\infty
for
\delta<1
, we use a metric with polynomial or exponential behaviour (see (
3.1
) and (
3.2
)). For such systems, we obtain the bounds for
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(\tau_{\delta})
. To this end, we first construct a minimal finite disjoint open cover of the space (see Proposition
4.1
), whose number of elements affects the limits of
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(\tau_{\delta})
, which helps us to obtain an upper bound for
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(\tau_{\delta})
.

One of the main tools here is the
\psi
-mixing property, which holds due to the fact that the measure is a Gibbs measure. This property can also play a key role through one of the standard recurrence rates in dynamical systems, namely the first hitting time.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
\delta>0
and
U\subset\Sigma
. The
first hitting time
of
\underline{x}\in\Sigma
to
U
is
\tau_{U}:\Sigma\to\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
such that,

\tau_{U}(\underline{x})=\inf\left\{n\geq 1:\sigma^{n}(\underline{x})\in U\right\}.

Many results have been obtained regarding the behaviour of the hitting time. For background on hitting time statistics in dynamical systems, see
[
L+
, Chapter 5]
, and for extensive information on hitting time in symbolic dynamical systems, see
[
C
]
and
[
GS
]
.

As a step in the proof of our main results, we also show that for
U
a union of cylinders of the same depth, then
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(\tau_{U})
is of order
1/\mu(U)
, see Theorem
4.4
. The key issue here is that we require uniform constants, like in the case of intervals in
[
JT
, Theorem 4.1]
. We note that Kac Lemma says that
\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{U}}(\tau_{U})=1/\mu(U)
, and our proof involves estimating
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(\tau_{U})
in terms of
\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{U}}(\tau_{U})
.

There is a close connection between hitting time and cover time. We use the mixing property of the system to exploit this relation, and then the problem of determining the covering behaviour of the system by orbits reduces to studying the hitting time behaviour on a finite open cover of the countable full shift. Then make use of some lemmas that were proved in
[
JT
]
and that remain valid in our setting. These lemmas provide bounds for
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(\tau_{\delta})
in terms of
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(\tau_{U})
for suitable
U
. Then, using the estimated bounds obtained for
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(\tau_{U})
and properties of the finite open cover, we derive the desired bounds for
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(\tau_{\delta})
.

Finally, we prove that for countable full shifts endowed with the Gibbs property, equipped with a suitable totally bounded metric, the rate of
\delta
-covering of orbits is variable and its range changes with respect to each metric. As we consider two metrics with different behaviours for the system in question: one exhibiting polynomial behaviour and the other exponential behaviour, it is shown that the expected time for the orbit of a point to cover the system equipped with the exponential metric is smaller than that for the same system equipped with polynomial behaviour. Note that, in this setting, obtaining a sharp lower bound, as done in
[
JT
, Theorem 2.1]
(see Theorem
2.6
), is out of reach, since the conditions for having a sharp lower bound in that theorem do not hold for a system with a Gibbs measure and the metrics under consideration (see Remark
3.11
).

2.
Cover time for interval maps

Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be a bounded interval and
\mathcal{Z}=\{Z_{i}\}_{i\in\mathcal{I}}
be at most a countable collection of subintervals of
I
with disjoint interiors. Suppose that
f:\bigcup_{n\in\mathcal{I}}Z_{n}\to I
is continuous and strictly monotone on each
Z_{n}
. Set

\Lambda:=\left\{x\in I:f^{k}(x)\in\bigcup_{n\in\mathcal{I}}Z_{n}\;\text{for all}\;k\geq 0\right\}.

Define a nonatomic Borel probability measure
m
such that
m(D)=0
where
D=I\setminus\bigcup_{i}(Z_{i})
, which is conformal with respect to a potential
\phi:\Lambda\to\mathbb{R}
(i.e.
\frac{dm}{d(m\circ f)}=e^{\phi}
).

One of the most important features of
f
is its symbolic coding, which will play a key role for us later on. Let
\mathcal{Z}^{n}
be the set
n
-cylinders, i.e. this is the set of maximal intervals
Z
of
I
such that
f^{k}(x)\subset Z_{i_{k}}
for some
Z_{i_{k}}\in\mathcal{Z}
for
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Let
\Sigma^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{I}^{\mathbb{N}}
be the subshift given by the set of sequences
\textbf{i}=(i_{0},i_{1},\ldots)\in\mathcal{I}^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
for which there exists an
x\in\Lambda
such that
f^{n}(x)\in Z_{i_{n}}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Define the projection
\Pi:\Sigma^{\prime}\to I
such that,

\Pi(i_{0},i_{1},\ldots)=\lim_{n\to\infty}f^{-1}_{i_{0}}\circ\cdots\circ f^{-1}_{i_{n-1}}(I)=x,

where
f_{i}=f|_{Z_{i}}
. Then
f\circ\Pi=\Pi\circ\sigma
where
\sigma:\Sigma^{\prime}\to\Sigma^{\prime}
is the left shift map.
Denote
\tilde{\mu}
to be the measure on
\Sigma^{\prime}
such that
\mu=\Pi_{*}\tilde{\mu}
,
\Sigma^{\prime}_{n}
the set of all words of length
n
in
\Sigma^{\prime}
and
\Sigma^{\prime*}
the set of all finite words in
\Sigma^{\prime}
. Also for
w\in\Sigma^{\prime*}
we write
[w]=\{\textbf{i}\in\Sigma^{\prime}:\textbf{i}|n=w\}
where
\textbf{i}|n=(i_{0},\ldots,i_{n-1})
if
\textbf{i}=(i_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}.

The following two assumptions for
\tilde{\mu}
are used as conditions of the main theorems in
[
JT
]
. First
\tilde{\mu}
is quasi-Bernoulli i.e. there exists
C_{*}>1
such that for all finite words
\textbf{i},\textbf{j}\in\Sigma^{\prime*},C_{*}^{-1}\tilde{\mu}([\textbf{i}])\tilde{\mu}([\textbf{j}])\leq\tilde{\mu}([\textbf{i}\textbf{j}])\leq C_{*}\tilde{\mu}([\textbf{i}])\tilde{\mu}([\textbf{j}])
, and the second assumption is
\tilde{\mu}
is
\psi
-mixing, i.e.

(2.1)

\left|\frac{\tilde{\mu}(\cup[x_{0},\ldots,x_{i-1},y_{0},\ldots,y_{j-1},z_{0},\ldots,z_{l-1}]}{\tilde{\mu}([x_{0},\ldots,x_{i-1}])\tilde{\mu}([z_{0},\ldots,z_{l-1}])}-1\right|\leq\psi(j),

where the union is taken over all words
y_{0},\ldots,y_{j-1}
of length
j
such that

(x_{0},\ldots,x_{i-1},y_{0},\ldots,y_{j-1},z_{0},\ldots,z_{l-1})\in\Sigma^{\prime}_{i+j+l},

and
\psi(j)\to 0
as
j\to\infty.

We introduce a class of systems that satisfy the conditions of the theorems in
[
JT
]
(see Remark
2.5
).

Definition 2.1
.

Given a Lipschitz potential
\phi:\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}
,
\mu
(or
\tilde{\mu}
) is
Gibbs
if there exist constants
K,P
such that for all
i_{0},\ldots,i_{n-1}\in\Sigma^{\prime}_{n}
and
\textbf{i}\in[i_{0},\ldots,i_{n-1}],

K^{-1}\leq\frac{\tilde{\mu}([i_{0},\ldots,i_{n-1}])}{e^{S_{n}\phi(\Pi(\textbf{i}))+nP}}\leq K,

where
S_{n}\phi(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\phi\circ f^{i}(x).

Definition 2.2
.

f:\Lambda\to\Lambda
is
Markov
, if for each
Z\in\mathcal{Z},f(Z)
is a union of elements of
\mathcal{Z}
. Also, we say that
f
satisfies the
big images and pre-images property (BIP)
if there exists a finite set
\{Z_{j}\}_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\subset\mathcal{Z}
such that,

\forall Z\in\mathcal{Z},\exists j,k\in\mathcal{J}:Z\subseteq f(Z_{j}),Z_{k}\subseteq f(Z).

Definition 2.3
.

We say that
(f,\mu)
is
Gibbs-Markov
with BIP if

is Markov and satisfies BIP and
\mu
is Gibbs.

In the following a condition of the existence of a pairwise disjoint finite open cover is used in
[
JT
]
. We mention it here since to obtain the result under discussion in this note we use an analogous cover.

Condition (U).
We say that the system
(f,\mu)
satisfies
(U)
if there exists
\delta_{0}>0
such that for all
0<\delta\leq\delta_{0}
we can find a finite collection
\mathcal{U}_{\delta}
of cylinders such that

(U)(a) there exists

such that for all elements
U

\mathcal{U}(\delta_{0}):=\bigcup_{0<\delta\leq\delta_{0}}\bigcup\left\{U:U\in\mathcal{U}_{\delta}\right\}
, there exists

such that

,

(U)(b) for all
0<\delta\leq\delta_{0}

\mathcal{U}_{\delta}
are pairwise disjoint,

(U)(c) for all
0<\delta\leq\delta_{0}
,

is covered by

,

(U)(d) for all

Now, the main theorem in

.

Remark 2.5
.

(1)

Three conditions relate to the regularity properties of

. Two conditions are that
\tilde{\mu}
satisfies Definitions

and

, and the other conditions, e.g. control over quantities such as ratio of derivatives of iterates of

(2)

The following property of the measure helps to obtain a sharp result. The measure

3.
Setting and main results

Now, we turn to the dynamical system that will be our study’s focus.

We define the symbolic space by

\Sigma:=\left\{\underline{x}=(x_{0},x_{1},\ldots)\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}:a_{x_{n}x_{n}+1}=1,\ \forall n\in{\mathbb{N}_{0}}\right\},

for
A=(a_{ij})
, an
\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}
matrix of
0
s and
1
s, where
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. The shift map
\sigma:\Sigma\to\Sigma
is defined by
\sigma(\underline{x})=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots)
, and the system
(\Sigma,\sigma)
is called a
one-sided countable Markov shift
(CMS). If all the entries of the matrix are one, then
\Sigma
includes all sequences over the
\mathbb{N}
, and it is called a
one-sided countable full shift
(CFS).

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the subset

Z_{n}=[y_{0},\ldots,y_{n-1}]:=\left\{\underline{x}\in\Sigma:y_{0}=x_{0},\ldots,y_{n-1}=x_{n-1}\right\}\subset\Sigma,

is called an
n
-cylinder
. Denote the collection of all
n
-cylinders by
\mathcal{Z}^{n}
, then
\Sigma
has a topology generated by the cylinder sets
i.e.
,
\left\{Z_{n}:Z_{n}\in\mathcal{Z}^{n},\forall n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
.

We denote the set of all words in
\Sigma
with length
n
by
\Sigma_{n}
and
\Sigma^{*}:=\cup_{k\geq 0}\Sigma_{k}
denotes the set of all finite words in
\Sigma
.

Note that depending on
A
,
\Sigma
may be locally compact
[
K
, Observation 7.2.3]
, but the system
(\Sigma,\sigma)
is never compact. However, for the cover time definition to make sense, the system must be a subspace of a compact space. In, for example
[
Z
]
and
[
IT
, Section 4.1]
, by defining a metric associated with a totally bounded metric, a completed space can be constructed for CMS that is compact. As this metric is employed throughout this paper, we begin by introducing it.

Recall that a metric space
(X,\rho)
is totally bounded if for all
\varepsilon>0,
there exists a finite set
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}:x_{i}\in X,\forall\ 1\leq i\leq k\}
such that
X\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}B_{\varepsilon}(x_{i})
.

Remark 3.1
.

Define
\rho:\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{i,j\geq n}\rho(i,j)=0,

then
(\mathbb{N},\rho)
is a totally bounded metric space. Therefore, one can consider totally bounded metrics with different asymptotic behaviours. An example of a polynomial behaviour of a totally bounded metric on
\mathbb{N}
is
\rho_{1}(x,y)=\left|\frac{1}{x}-\frac{1}{y}\right|,
which was used in
[
Z
]
. Also, the metric
\rho_{2}(x,y)=\left|\frac{1}{e^{\alpha x}}-\frac{1}{e^{\alpha y}}\right|,
for some
\alpha>0
exhibits exponential behaviour. The discrete metric is not totally bounded.

Now, let
\theta\in(0,1)
, and
\rho:\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}\to[0,1]
be a totally bounded metric, then

d(\underline{x},\underline{y})=\sum_{n\geq 0}\theta^{n}\rho(x_{n},y_{n}),

is a metric on
\Sigma
with the following properties (see e.g.
[
IT
, Section 4.1]
):

•

(\overline{\Sigma}_{\rho},\overline{d})
, the completion of
\Sigma
with respect to totally bounded metric
\rho
, is compact,

•

d
generates the cylinder topology and
\sigma
is a uniformly continuous on
\Sigma
where it extends to
\overline{\Sigma}
as a continuous map.

One of the key features of the metric
d
is that it is totally bounded. How this result is obtained is shown in Proposition
4.1
.

We next explain our dynamics and measures.

Definition 3.2
.

Given
\phi:\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}
be the potential on
\Sigma
, the
n
-th variation of
\phi
is,

var_{n}(\phi):=\sup\left\{|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|:x_{0}=y_{0},\ldots,x_{n-1}=y_{n-1}\right\}.

We say
\phi
has

if
\sum_{n\geq 2}var_{n}(\phi)<\infty
.

Similarly, Definition
2.2
for countable Markov shifts can be rewritten as follows.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
A
be the transition matrix.
A
has the
big images property (BIP)
if

\exists\ b_{1},\ldots,b_{N}\in\mathbb{N}:\forall t\in\mathbb{N},\quad\exists\ i,j\quad\text{such that}\quad a_{b_{i}t}a_{tb_{j}}=1.

Note that CFS naturally has this condition.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
\phi
be the potential on a countable full shift. The
Gurevich pressure
of
\phi
is,

P_{G}(\phi)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\sum_{\sigma^{n}\underline{x}=\underline{x}}e^{S_{n}\phi(\underline{x})}.

Note that it can be shown that Gurevich pressure exists
[
S1
]
. We can rewrite Definition
2.1
for countable Markov shifts.

Definition 3.5
.

A
Gibbs measure
for
\phi
on a countable Markov shift is an invariant probability measure

such that for some constants
K>1
and
P
and every

,

K^{-1}\leq\frac{m(Z_{n})}{e^{S_{n}\phi(\underline{x})-nP}}\leq K,\quad\forall\underline{x}\in Z_{n}.

Note that by
[

, Proposition 2.2]
,

equals the pressure
P_{G}(\phi)
. For convenience in calculations, we usually normalise the potential by
\phi-P_{G}(\phi)
, which ensures that

.

From this point onwards, we assume
(\Sigma,\sigma)
represents the countable full shift space and
\phi
be the potential such that
\phi\big|_{[n]}\leq-\kappa\log(n+1)

has Gurevich pressure and by
[

, Theorem 1]

has a Gibbs measure. It is well known that any Gibbs measure is a

-mixing measure as well (see

We consider the following metrics and, endowing the CFS with each of them, examine the asymptotic behaviour of

,

(3.1)

d_{1}(\underline{x},\underline{y}):=\sum_{n\geq 0}\theta^{n}\left|\frac{1}{x_{n}}-\frac{1}{y_{n}}\right|,

where

,

and

Theorem 3.7

\phi:\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}
be a potential such that
\phi\big|_{[n]}\leq-\kappa\log(n+1)
for some
\kappa>1
, which admits the Gibbs measure
\mu
. Then there exist
0<c<C<\infty
and
\varepsilon>0
such that for all
\delta>0
, we have

\frac{c}{M_{\mu}(\delta/\varepsilon)}\leq\mathbb{E}(\tau_{\delta})\leq\frac{C}{M_{\mu}(\varepsilon\delta)}\left(\log\frac{1}{\delta}\right)\left(\log\left(\log\frac{1}{\delta}\right)\right).

Remark 3.8
.

(1)

In these results, the upper bounds depend on the metric. This is due to the use of a finite open cover on
(\Sigma,\sigma)
to derive these upper bounds, since the cardinality of the cover directly affects the bounds. In fact, the cover is chosen with properties that allow us to reduce the problem of estimating the expected cover time to that of estimating the expected hitting time of the elements of the cover. Then, these properties also allow us to derive an upper bound for this expected hitting time in terms of the number of sets in the cover. In each metric setting, the cardinality of the finite open cover is compared in exact correspondence with the bounds discussed above.

(2)

In view of the obtained results and those established in
[
JT
]
, we conclude that the upper bound of the cover time is sensitive to changes in the metric. Moreover, the bounds obtained for the metric
d_{1}
are larger than those corresponding to the metric
d_{2}
, and both exceed the bounds for the interval maps (i.e. results in
[
JT
]
). That is, one expects that the orbits in an interval map cover the system rapidly in comparison to the orbits in a CFS with metrics
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
. Furthermore, the orbits in a CFS equipped with a metric with exponential behaviour cover the system faster than when it is equipped with a polynomial metric, but still slower than the interval maps.

Lemma 3.9
.

Assume that
(\Sigma,\sigma)
is the countable full shift equipped with the metric
d_{1}
as in (
3.1
). Let
\phi:\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}
be a potential such that
\phi\big|_{[n]}\to-\infty
as
n\to\infty
, which admits the Gibbs measure
\mu
. Then,
\dim_{M}\mu=\infty
. Consequently, Ahlfors regularity does not hold for
\mu
.

Proof.

We first evaluate the diameter of the cylinders with respect to
d_{1}
. The greatest distance between elements of
Z_{k}=[u_{0},\ldots,u_{k-1}]\in\mathcal{Z}^{k}
is approached for
\underline{x}
and
\underline{y}
when they take the following forms:

\underline{x}=(u_{0},\ldots,u_{k-1},\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptstyle k\text{-th component}}}{{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\small{\downarrow}}}{{M}}}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!,M,\ldots),

\underline{y}=(u_{0},\ldots,u_{k-1},1,1,\ldots),

where
M
is a large number. Then we have,

d_{1}(\underline{x},\underline{y})=\theta^{k}\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}\theta^{n}\left|\frac{1}{M}-1\right|\right),

and as
M\to\infty
,
d_{1}(\underline{x},\underline{y})=\frac{\theta^{k}}{1-\theta}
. Hence,

(3.3)

\text{diam}(Z_{k})=\frac{\theta^{k}}{1-\theta}.

Now, let
\delta>0
. If
\text{diam}(Z_{k})=\frac{\theta^{k}}{1-\theta}=\delta
, then a cylinder with the length of
k=\left\lceil\frac{\log(\delta(1-\theta))}{\log\theta}\right\rceil
has diameter
\delta
. This yields for all
\underline{x}\in\Sigma
,
\mu\left(B_{\delta}(\underline{x})\right)=\mu\left([x_{0},\ldots,x_{k-1}]\right)
, where
k=\left\lceil\frac{\log(\delta(1-\theta))}{\log\theta}\right\rceil
. To show
\dim_{M}\mu=\infty
, we first approximate the quantity
M_{\mu}(\delta)=\min_{\underline{x}}\mu\left(B_{\delta}(\underline{x})\right)
using the Gibbs property, and then use this approximation to compute
\dim_{M}\mu
. By the Gibbs property, there exists some
K>1
such that

\mu\left(B_{\delta}(\underline{x})\right)=\mu(\left[x_{0},\ldots,x_{k-1}]\right)\leq Ke^{S_{k}\phi(\underline{x})}=K\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}e^{\phi(\sigma^{i}(\underline{x}))}\leq K\left(e^{\sup_{{x\in[x_{i}],0\leq i\leq k-1}}(\phi(x))}\right)^{k},

it follows that

\displaystyle\min_{\underline{x}\in\Sigma}\left(\mu\left(B_{\delta}(\underline{x})\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq K\inf_{\underline{x}\in\Sigma}\left(e^{\sup_{{x\in[x_{i}],0\leq i\leq k-1}}(\phi(x))}\right)^{k}\leq K\left(\inf_{\underline{x}\in\Sigma}e^{\sup_{{x\in[x_{i}],0\leq i\leq k-1}}(\phi(x))}\right)^{k}

\displaystyle=K\left(e^{\inf_{\underline{x}\in\Sigma}\left(\sup_{{x\in[x_{i}],0\leq i\leq k-1}}(\phi(x))\right)}\right)^{k}=K\left(e^{\limsup_{x\in[n],n\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\phi(x)\right)}\right)^{k}.

Therefore,

\log\left(\min_{\underline{x}\in\Sigma}\left(\mu\left(B_{\delta}(\underline{x})\right)\right)\right)\leq\log\left(K\left(e^{\limsup_{x\in[n],n\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\phi(x)\right)}\right)^{k}\right),

after dividing by
\log(\delta)
and taking
\lim_{\delta\to 0}
on both sides, we obtain

\displaystyle\dim_{M}\mu

\displaystyle=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\frac{\log M_{\mu}(\delta)}{\log\delta}\geq\lim_{\delta\to 0}\frac{\log\left(K\left(e^{\limsup_{x\in[n],n\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\phi(x)\right)}\right)^{k}\right)}{\log\delta}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\frac{\log K}{\log\delta}+\lim_{\delta\to 0}\frac{\frac{\log(\delta(1-\theta))}{\log\theta}\left(\log e^{\limsup_{x\in[n],n\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\phi(x)\right)}\right)}{\log\delta}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\frac{\frac{\log\delta}{\log\theta}\left({\limsup_{x\in[n],n\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\phi(x)\right)}\right)}{\log\delta}+\lim_{\delta\to 0}\frac{\frac{\log(1-\theta)}{\log\theta}\left({\limsup_{x\in[n],n\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\phi(x)\right)}\right)}{\log\delta}

\displaystyle=\frac{{\limsup_{x\in[n],n\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\phi(x)\right)}}{\log\theta}.

Since
\phi
on
[n]
tends to
-\infty
as
n\to\infty
, we obtain
\dim_{M}\mu=\infty
.

Since there is no chance to get
\min_{\underline{x}\in\Sigma}\left(\mu\left(B_{\delta}(\underline{x})\right)\right)\leq\delta^{c}
for some constant
c>0
, the Ahlfors regularity condition also fails to hold.
∎

with respect to metric

Remark 3.11

4.
Cover time under a polynomial metric

In this section, we prove the result related to the metric
d_{1}
, that is, Theorem
3.6
. First, we present one proposition and one theorem that play a key role in the proof of our result. The first establishes a finite open cover satisfying condition
(U)
for the full shift with respect to metrics
d_{1}
, the second introduces a connection between cover time and hitting time.

We divide this section into two subsections. First, we present the propositions mentioned above and in the second subsection, we prove Theorem
3.6
.

4.1.
Preliminary Propositions for
d_{1}

We start with a proposition which gives a pairwise disjoint cover of the union of cylinders on
\Sigma
with respect to the metric
d_{1}
, which satisfies the condition
(U)
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\delta>0
and assume that
(\Sigma,\sigma)
is a countable full shift with the metric
d_{1}
as in (
3.1
). Then, there exists a pairwise disjoint finite open cover
\mathcal{U}_{\delta}
for
(\Sigma,d_{1})
with
\#(\mathcal{U}_{\delta})=\left\lceil\left(2\sqrt{\frac{1}{\delta}}\left(1+o\left(\sqrt{\delta}\right)\right)\right)^{\frac{\log(\delta(1-\theta))}{\log\theta}}\right\rceil
such that

(1)

for all
U\in\mathcal{U}_{\delta}
, we have
U=\bigcup_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathrm{U^{*}}}[\mathbf{i}]
such that
|\mathbf{i}|=\left\lceil\frac{\log(\delta(1-\theta))}{\log\theta}\right\rceil
, where
\mathrm{U^{*}}\subset\Sigma^{*}
is a set of finite words of length
|\mathbf{i}|
with respect to
U
,

(2)

there exists
1<T<\infty
such that for all
\underline{x}\in\Sigma
, there exists
U\in\mathcal{U}_{\delta}
such that
B_{\delta}(\underline{x})\subset U\subset B_{T\delta}(\underline{x})
.

Proof.

We divide the proof into two steps. In the first step, we are looking for a finite collection of cylinders that efficiently cover
\Sigma
. In the second step, we then verify properties (1) and (2) for this collection.

Step 1
. To obtain the desired cover, the idea is to take advantage of the total boundedness of
\rho_{1}
, since this allows us to find a finite cover of
\mathbb{N}
with respect to
\rho_{1}
. For this reason, let
\delta>0
, and start by letting
\mathcal{V}_{\delta}
be the empty set, to which elements are then added step by step. Let
N
be the minimal natural number such that
\frac{1}{N}\leq\delta
. In fact,
N
provides a convenient approximation of the diameter of the neighbourhoods, which simplifies the computations. Moreover, for

(4.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\rho_{1}}(N):=\{N,N+1,\ldots\},

if
n\in\mathcal{N}_{\rho_{1}}(N)
, then
{\rho_{1}}(N,n)=\frac{1}{N}-\frac{1}{n}<\delta
. We add
\mathcal{N}_{\rho_{1}}(N)
to
\mathcal{V}_{\delta}
.

Now, we need to find neighbourhoods that cover all
n<N
for the desired finite open cover. We divide the argument into two parts. Some of the smallest natural numbers have the property that their
\frac{1}{N}
-neighbourhood contains no other natural numbers. We first identify them and then discuss the remaining elements.

We can obtain the largest number
n<N
such that
\mathcal{N}_{\rho_{1}}(n)
is a singleton using the following inequality

\frac{1}{n-1}-\frac{1}{n}<\frac{1}{N},

after simplification, we have

n^{2}-n-N>0
, which admits the following solutions

n>\frac{1+\sqrt{1+4N}}{2}\geq\frac{1+\sqrt{1+\frac{4}{\delta}}}{2},

therefore,
N_{s}:=\left\lfloor\frac{1+\sqrt{1+\frac{4}{\delta}}}{2}\right\rfloor
is the largest
n<N
with a singleton neighbourhood. We add all
\mathcal{N}_{\rho_{1}}(n)
for
n<N_{s}
to
\mathcal{V}_{\delta}
.

To obtain an optimal finite open cover for
\mathbb{N}
, we are looking for
\delta
-neighbourhoods for all
n
, such that
N_{s}<n<N
. To this end, let
\mathcal{N}_{\rho_{1}}(N-1)
be the last neighbourhood before
\mathcal{N}_{\rho_{1}}(N)
whose elements satisfy:

{\rho_{1}}(N-1-i,N-1)=\frac{1}{N-1-i}-\frac{1}{N-1}\leq\frac{1}{N},

for some
i
. As a result, we have
Ni\leq N^{2}-2N+1+i(1-N).
Consequently,
i\leq\left\lceil\frac{N^{2}-2N+1}{2N-1}\right\rceil
that is
i\leq\left\lceil\frac{N}{2}\left(1+o\left(\frac{1}{N}\right)\right)\right\rceil
. Thus, we obtain the following neighbourhood for large
N
:

(4.2)

\mathcal{N}_{\rho_{1}}(N-1)=\left\{\left\lceil\frac{N}{2}\right\rceil,\ldots,N-1\right\},

which is the largest finite
\delta
-neighbourhood. Next, the neighbourhood before
\mathcal{N}_{\rho_{1}}\left(N-1\right)
is
\mathcal{N}_{\rho_{1}}\left(\left\lceil\frac{N}{2}\right\rceil-1\right)
in which the elements satisfy

{\rho_{1}}\left(\frac{N}{2}-1-i,\frac{N}{2}-1\right)=\frac{1}{\frac{N}{2}-1-i}-\frac{1}{\frac{N}{2}-1}\leq\frac{1}{N},

we obtain
i\leq\left\lceil\frac{N^{2}-4N-4}{6N-4}\right\rceil
, and in fact we have
i\leq\left\lceil\frac{N}{6}\left(1+o\left(\frac{1}{N}\right)\right)\right\rceil
. Hence, for large
N

\mathcal{N}_{\rho_{1}}\left(\left\lceil\frac{N}{2}\right\rceil-1\right)=\left\{\left\lceil\frac{N}{3}\right\rceil,\ldots,\left\lceil\frac{N}{2}\right\rceil-1\right\}.

Similarly, for large
N
, these neighbourhoods can be represented in the following form:

(4.3)

\mathcal{N}_{\rho_{1}}\left(\left\lceil\frac{N}{j}\right\rceil-1\right)=\left\{\left\lceil\frac{N}{j+1}\right\rceil,\ldots,\left\lceil\frac{N}{j}\right\rceil-1\right\},

with
\#\mathcal{N}_{\rho_{1}}\left(\left\lceil\frac{N}{j}\right\rceil-1\right)=\left\lceil\frac{N}{j(j+1)}\left(1+o\left(\frac{1}{N}\right)\right)\right\rceil
. To determine
j
, representing the number of such neighbourhoods based on
\delta
, we apply the following condition:

\left\lceil\frac{N}{j+1}\right\rceil-1=N_{s}\left(=\left\lfloor\frac{1+\sqrt{1+\frac{4}{\delta}}}{2}\right\rfloor\right),

because
\left\lceil\frac{N}{j+1}\right\rceil
is the smallest natural number among the multi-element neighbourhoods, so
\left\lceil\frac{N}{j+1}\right\rceil-1
should be equal to
N_{s}
which is the largest natural number that has a singleton neighbourhood.
It follows that
j=\left\lceil\frac{2N}{1+\sqrt{1+4N}+2}-1\right\rceil
, where we can rewrite it as

j=\left\lceil\sqrt{N}\left(1+o\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\right)\right)\right\rceil.

Now we also add all such neighbourhoods in form of
\mathcal{N}_{\rho_{1}}\left(\left\lceil\frac{N}{i}\right\rceil-1\right)
for
1\leq i\leq\left\lceil\sqrt{N}\right\rceil
to
\mathcal{V}_{\delta}
. Hence,
\mathcal{V}_{\delta}
is the optimal finite open cover for
\mathbb{N}
with respect to
{\rho_{1}}
.

To find the number of open neighbourhoods in
\mathcal{V}_{\delta}
, for large
N
we have

\displaystyle K_{\delta}:=1+j+N_{s}

\displaystyle=1+\left\lceil\sqrt{N}+\frac{1+\sqrt{1+4N}}{2}\left(1+o\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\right)\right)\right\rceil

\displaystyle=1+\left\lceil\frac{2\sqrt{N}+\sqrt{1+4N}+1}{2}\left(1+o\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\right)\right)\right\rceil

\displaystyle=1+\left\lceil 2\sqrt{N}\left(1+o\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\right)\right)\right\rceil=\left\lceil 2\sqrt{\frac{1}{\delta}}\left(1+o\left(\sqrt{\delta}\right)\right)\right\rceil.

So, for
\delta>0
, there exists the finite set

W_{\delta}:=\left\{1,\ldots,\left\lceil\sqrt{N}\right\rceil-1,\left\lceil\sqrt{N}\right\rceil,\ldots,N-1,N\right\},

with
K_{\delta}
elements, such that
\mathbb{N}\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{K_{\delta}}\mathcal{N}_{\rho_{1}}(v_{i})
, where
v_{i}\in W_{\delta}
. We set
V_{i}:=\mathcal{N}_{\rho_{1}}(v_{i})
for
1\leq i\leq K_{\delta}
, then

(4.4)

\mathcal{V}_{\delta}=\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{K_{\delta}}

is the open cover of
\mathbb{N}
, with respect to which
{\rho_{1}}
is totally bounded.

We can now construct, using the collection
\mathcal{V}_{\delta}=\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{K_{\delta}}
, a finite collection of cylinders for
\Sigma
. Let
k\geq 1
and suppose
1\leq i_{0},\ldots,i_{k-1}\leq K_{\delta}
, then we consider the following sets.

(4.5)

(i_{0},\ldots,i_{k-1})_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}:=\left\{(x_{0},\ldots,x_{k-1},\ldots):x_{0}\in V_{i_{0}},\ldots,x_{k-1}\in V_{i_{k-1}}\right\}.

In fact, these sets are a union of cylinders, i.e.,
(i_{0},\ldots,i_{k-1})_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}=\bigcup_{\textbf{i}\in\mathrm{U^{*}}}[\textbf{i}]
, where

\mathrm{U^{*}}=\left\{(x_{0},\ldots,x_{k-1}):x_{0}\in V_{i_{0}},\ldots,x_{k-1}\in V_{i_{k-1}}\right\}.

We are interested in finding
\text{diam}\left((i_{0},\ldots,i_{k-1})_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}\right)
. To this end, note that the diameter is determined by elements of different cylinders, since the metric between two points within a single
k
-cylinder from this collection is zero in the first
k
components, whereas this metric between two members from distinct cylinders is nonzero. These two elements are of the following form:

\underline{x}=(l_{i_{0}},\ldots,l_{i_{k-1}},\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptstyle k\text{-th component}}}{{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\small{\downarrow}}}{{1}}}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!,1,\ldots),

\underline{y}=(s_{i_{0}},\ldots,s_{i_{k-1}},M,M,\ldots),

where
M
is a large number and for all
i_{0}\leq i\leq i_{k-1}
if
V_{i}\neq\mathcal{N}_{\rho_{1}}(N)
,
l_{i},s_{i}\in V_{i}
are such that
\text{diam}(V_{i})={\rho_{1}}(l_{i},s_{i})
, and if
V_{i}=\mathcal{N}_{\rho_{1}}(N)
,
s_{i}=N
and
l_{i}=l
such that
l\to\infty
. Then,

d_{1}(\underline{x},\underline{y})=\sum_{n=0}^{k-1}\theta^{n}\text{diam}(V_{i})+\sum_{n\geq k}\theta^{n}\left|1-\frac{1}{M}\right|=\delta\sum_{n=0}^{k-1}\theta^{n}+\sum_{n\geq k}\theta^{n}\left|1-\frac{1}{M}\right|,

where, as
M\to\infty
, we have:

d_{1}(\underline{x},\underline{y})=\delta\sum_{n=0}^{k-1}\theta^{n}+\sum_{n\geq k}\theta^{n}=\delta\left(\frac{1-\theta^{k}}{1-\theta}\right)+\frac{\theta^{k}}{1-\theta}.

Hence, the result is

(4.6)

\text{diam}\left((i_{0},\ldots,i_{k-1})_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}\right)=\delta\left(\frac{1-\theta^{k}}{1-\theta}\right)+\frac{\theta^{k}}{1-\theta}.

We then define
\mathcal{U}_{\delta}:=\left\{(i_{0},\ldots,i_{k-1})_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}:1\leq i_{l}\leq K_{\delta},\ \forall\ 0\leq l\leq k-1\right\}
, which is a finite collection with
\#(\mathcal{U}_{\delta})=(K_{\delta})^{k}
that covers
\Sigma
.

Step 2.
We now prove that the cover obtained has properties (1) and (2). In the first step for each
k\geq 1
, we obtain a finite collection
\mathcal{U}_{\delta}=\left\{(i_{0},\ldots,i_{k-1})_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}:1\leq i_{l}\leq K_{\delta},\ \forall\ 0\leq l\leq k-1\right\}
. For this
\mathcal{U}_{\delta}
to satisfy properties (1) and (2), we assume that
k=\left\lceil\frac{\log(\delta(1-\theta))}{\log(\theta)}\right\rceil
. Note that
\mathcal{U}_{\delta}
is a pairwise disjoint cover for
\Sigma
.

We can rewrite
(i_{0},\ldots,i_{k-1})_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}
as an element of
\mathcal{U}_{\delta}
in the form
(i_{0},\ldots,i_{k-1})_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}=\bigcup_{\textbf{i}\in\mathrm{U^{*}}}[\textbf{i}]
where
\mathrm{U^{*}}=\left\{(x_{0},\ldots,x_{k-1}):x_{0}\in V_{i_{0}},\ldots,x_{k-1}\in V_{i_{k-1}}\right\}\subset\Sigma^{*}
is a countable set such that
|\textbf{i}|=\left\lceil\frac{\log(\delta(1-\theta))}{\log(\theta)}\right\rceil
, which implies property (1).

To obtain property (2), let
\underline{x}\in\Sigma
. Since
\mathcal{U}_{\delta}
covers
\Sigma
, then there exists
(i_{0},\ldots,i_{k-1})_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}
such that
\underline{x}\in(i_{0},\ldots,i_{k-1})_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}
. Since
k=\left\lceil\frac{\log(\delta(1-\theta))}{\log(\theta)}\right\rceil
, by what we calculated in (
3.3
), we have
\text{diam}(B_{\delta}(\underline{x}))\geq\frac{\theta^{k}}{1-\theta}
. Now, according to (
4.6
), we have

\displaystyle\text{diam}\left((i_{0},\ldots,i_{k-1})_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}\right)

\displaystyle=\delta\left(\frac{1-\theta^{\left\lceil\frac{\log(\delta(1-\theta))}{\log(\theta)}\right\rceil}}{1-\theta}\right)+\frac{\theta^{\left\lceil\frac{\log(\delta(1-\theta))}{\log(\theta)}\right\rceil}}{1-\theta}\leq\delta\left(\frac{1-\theta^{\left\lceil\frac{\log(\delta(1-\theta))}{\log(\theta)}\right\rceil}}{1-\theta}\right)+\delta

\displaystyle=\delta\left(\frac{1-\theta^{\left\lceil\frac{\log(\delta(1-\theta))}{\log(\theta)}\right\rceil}}{1-\theta}+1\right).

Since
1\leq\frac{1-\theta^{k}}{1-\theta}<\frac{1}{1-\theta}
for all
k>0
, we obtain

\text{diam}(B_{\delta}(\underline{x}))=\delta\leq\delta\left(\frac{1-\theta^{\left\lceil\frac{\log(\delta(1-\theta))}{\log(\theta)}\right\rceil}}{1-\theta}\right)<\text{diam}\left((i_{0},\ldots,i_{k-1})_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}\right),

and consequently
B_{\delta}(\underline{x})\subset(i_{0},\ldots,i_{k-1})_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}.

Setting
1<T:=\frac{1}{1-\theta}+1<\infty
, we have

\text{diam}(B_{T\delta}(\underline{x}))=\delta\left(\frac{1}{1-\theta}+1\right)>\delta\left(\frac{1-\theta^{\left\lceil\frac{\log(\delta(1-\theta))}{\log(\theta)}\right\rceil}}{1-\theta}+1\right)\geq\text{diam}\left((i_{0},\ldots,i_{k-1})_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}\right),

we conclude that

(i_{0},\ldots,i_{k-1})_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}\subset B_{T\delta}(\underline{x}).

Hence, for
k=\left\lceil\frac{\log(\delta(1-\theta))}{\log(\theta)}\right\rceil
, we found
T=\frac{1}{1-\theta}+1
, such that for all
\underline{x}\in\Sigma
, there exists
(i_{0},\ldots,i_{k-1})_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}\in\mathcal{U}_{\delta}
such that
\underline{x}\in(i_{0},\ldots,i_{k-1})_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}\in\mathcal{U}_{\delta}
and we have
B_{\delta}(\underline{x})\subset(i_{0},\ldots,i_{k-1})_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}\subset B_{T\delta}(\underline{x})
.

Notice that, because
k=\left\lceil\frac{\log(\delta(1-\theta))}{\log(\theta)}\right\rceil
and
K_{\delta}=\left\lceil 2\sqrt{\frac{1}{\delta}}\left(1+o\left(\sqrt{\delta}\right)\right)\right\rceil
, the total number of elements in
\mathcal{U}_{\delta}
is consequently given by

\#(\mathcal{U}_{\delta})=(K_{\delta})^{k}=\left\lceil\left(2\sqrt{\frac{1}{\delta}}\left(1+o\left(\sqrt{\delta}\right)\right)\right)^{\frac{\log(\delta(1-\theta))}{\log(\theta)}}\right\rceil.

Here, it should be noted that
\mathcal{U}_{\delta}
is minimal. In fact, for any collection of cylinders of length less than
k
in
\Sigma
, this collection can not be obtained. So, the desired conclusion of the proposition is obtained, and the proof is complete.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

Note that since
\mathcal{U}_{\delta}
is a partition, property (2) for
\mathcal{U}_{\delta}
is equivalent to (U)(a) in condition
(U)
. In addition, property (1) is the same as (U)(d) in condition
(U)
. (U)(b) and (U)(c) in condition
(U)
are also the natural properties for
\mathcal{U}_{\delta}
.

Note that at the first step of proving Proposition

\rho_{1}
for
\mathbb{N}
to show that
d_{1}
is totally bounded for
\Sigma
.

There exists a connection between the expected hitting time (
1.3
) and the expected cover time, which plays an important role in the proof of our results. To this end, we first present a remark that we use in the proof of the following theorem.

Remark 4.3
.

The collection
\mathcal{U}_{\delta}
inherits the
\psi
-mixing property. In fact, for each
(i_{0},\ldots,i_{k-1})_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}
and
(z_{0},\ldots,z_{k-1})_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}
in
\mathcal{U}_{\delta}
we can consider

\frac{{\mu}\left(\cup\left\{[x_{i_{0}},\ldots,x_{i_{k-1}},y_{0},\ldots,y_{j-1},x_{z_{0}},\ldots,x_{z_{k-1}}]:x_{i_{l}}\in V_{i_{l}},x_{z_{l}}\in V_{z_{l}},\ 0\leq l\leq k-1\right\}\right)}{{\mu}((i_{0},\ldots,i_{k-1})_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}){\mu}((z_{0},\ldots,z_{k-1})_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}})},

where the union is taken over
\Sigma_{j}
, all words
y_{0},\ldots,y_{j-1}
of length

, and since
(i_{0},\ldots,i_{k-1})_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}
and

are disjoint, this can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{i_{l}}\in V_{i_{l}}\\
0\leq l\leq k-1\end{subarray}}\ \ \sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{j_{l}}\in V_{j_{l}}\\
0\leq l\leq k-1\end{subarray}}}{\mu}(\cup_{y_{0},\ldots,y_{j-1}\in\Sigma_{j}}[x_{i_{0}},\ldots,x_{i_{k-1}},y_{0},\ldots,y_{j-1},x_{z_{0}},\ldots,x_{z_{k-1}}])}{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{i_{l}}\in V_{i_{l}}\\
0\leq l\leq k-1\end{subarray}}\ {\mu}([x_{i_{0}},\ldots,x_{i_{k-1}}])\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{j_{l}}\in V_{j_{l}}\\
0\leq l\leq k-1\end{subarray}}}{\mu}([x_{z_{0}},\ldots,x_{z_{k-1}}])}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{i_{l}}\in V_{i_{l}}\\
0\leq l\leq k-1\end{subarray}}\ \ \sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{j_{l}}\in V_{j_{l}}\\
0\leq l\leq k-1\end{subarray}}}{\mu}(\cup_{y_{0},\ldots,y_{j-1}\in\Sigma_{j}}[x_{i_{0}},\ldots,x_{i_{k-1}},y_{0},\ldots,y_{j-1},x_{z_{0}},\ldots,x_{z_{k-1}}])}{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{i_{l}}\in V_{i_{l}}\\
0\leq l\leq k-1\end{subarray}}\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{j_{l}}\in V_{j_{l}}\\
0\leq l\leq k-1\end{subarray}}}\ {\mu}([x_{i_{0}},\ldots,x_{i_{k-1}}]){\mu}([x_{z_{0}},\ldots,x_{z_{k-1}}])}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{i_{l}}\in V_{i_{l}}\\
0\leq l\leq k-1\end{subarray}}\ \ \sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{j_{l}}\in V_{j_{l}}\\
0\leq l\leq k-1\end{subarray}}}{\mu}([x_{i_{0}},\ldots,x_{i_{k-1}}]){\mu}([x_{z_{0}},\ldots,x_{z_{k-1}}])\left(\psi(j)+1\right)}{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{i_{l}}\in V_{i_{l}}\\
0\leq l\leq k-1\end{subarray}}\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{j_{l}}\in V_{j_{l}}\\
0\leq l\leq k-1\end{subarray}}}\ {\mu}([x_{i_{0}},\ldots,x_{i_{k-1}}]){\mu}([x_{z_{0}},\ldots,x_{z_{k-1}}])}=\psi(j)+1,

where in the last inequality we applied the upper bound of
\psi
-mixing property for cylinders. Similarly, we can find the lower bound, so we have the
\psi
-mixing property for elements in
\mathcal{U}_{\delta}
.

Notation
.

We introduce some notation that helps us for the sake of brevity in the proof of the next theorem. Assume that
U=(i_{0},\ldots,i_{k-1})_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}},W=(j_{0},\ldots,j_{k-1})_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}\in\mathcal{U}_{\delta}
and
n,m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
n\geq m
. If we are interested in points in
U
whose their
m
-th to
n
-th iterates under the shift map have no intersection with the set
W
, we denote the set of all such points by

\mathcal{F}_{m-1}^{n-1}(U:W):=\left\{\underline{x}\in U:W\cap\bigcup_{i=m-1}^{n-1}[x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{i+k}]=\varnothing\right\},

so, in the case we seek the same property for points in
\Sigma
, we use
\mathcal{F}_{m-1}^{n-1}(\Sigma:W)
, and the set of all points in
U
, where their all
n
initial iterates of the shift map do not hit
W
is
\mathcal{F}_{0}^{n-1}(U:W)
.
Moreover, we use
{\mathcal{F}_{m-1}^{n-1}(U:W)}^{c}
for the complement of
\mathcal{F}_{m-1}^{n-1}(U:W)
, i.e.

{\mathcal{F}_{m-1}^{n-1}(U:W)}^{c}:=\left\{\underline{x}\in U:W\cap\bigcup_{i=m-1}^{n-1}[x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{i+k}]\neq\varnothing\right\}.

The following theorem not only contributes to the proof of the main result but is also of independent interest.
In
[
JT
, Theorem 4.1]
, bounds for
\mathbb{E}(\tau_{U})
were obtained, where
U
is a subinterval i.e., for some
0<c<C<\infty
,

\frac{c}{\mu(U)}\leq\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left(\tau_{U}\right)\leq\frac{C}{\mu(U)}.

We now present, in the following theorem, the corresponding result for
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left(\tau_{U}\right)
depending on metric
d_{1}
, where
U\in\mathcal{U}_{\delta}
. The key point is that the bounds for this result involve uniform constants as in the interval case.

Theorem 4.4
.

Assume that
(\Sigma,\sigma)
is the countable full shift equipped with the metric
d_{1}
as in (
3.1
). Let
\phi:\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}
be a potential such that
\phi\big|_{[n]}\leq-\kappa\log(n+1)
for some
\kappa>1
. Suppose that
U=(i_{0},\ldots,i_{k-1})_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}
is an element of finite open cover
\mathcal{U}_{\delta}
as defined in Proposition
4.1
. Then there exists
0<c<1<C<\infty
such that

(4.7)

\frac{c}{\mu(U)}\leq\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left(\tau_{U}(\underline{x})\right)\leq\frac{C}{\mu(U)}.

Proof.

The proof of this theorem relies on the use of Kac’s lemma
[
Ka
, Lemma 2
′
]
. This lemma states that for every measurable set
A
, the mean of
\mu_{A}\left(\left\{\underline{x}\in A:\tau_{A}(\underline{x})\geq n\right\}\right)
in an ergodic system is equal to
\frac{1}{\mu(A)}
. Therefore, the approach taken in this proof is to establish an inequality between
\mu_{U}\left(\left\{\underline{x}\in U:\tau_{U}(\underline{x})\geq n\right\}\right)
and
\mu\left(\left\{\underline{x}\in U:\tau_{U}(\underline{x})\geq n\right\}\right)
, which was obtained earlier in
[
JT
, Proposition 3.1]
for cylinders. Then, by summing both sides, we obtain their respective mean. As a result, one side of the inequality corresponds to
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(\tau_{U}(\underline{x}))
, while the other side is expressed in terms of
\frac{1}{\mu(U)}
, which serves as a bound. We first present some preliminaries used in both bounds, then establish the upper bound, and finally derive the lower bound on (
4.7
).

Recall that
k=\left\lceil\frac{\log(\delta(1-\theta))}{\log(\theta)}\right\rceil
for some
\delta>0
and
0<\theta<1
. Fix
m\in\mathbb{N}
and assume that
n\geq k+m
. We have the following expression

\displaystyle\sigma^{-1}\left(\left\{\underline{x}\in U:\tau_{U}(\underline{x})\geq n\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\qquad\quad\qquad\qquad=\sigma^{-1}\left(\left\{\underline{x}\in U:\underline{u}\notin\bigcup_{i=0}^{n-1}[x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{i+k}],\ \forall\underline{u}\in U\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\qquad\quad\qquad\qquad=\left\{\underline{x}\in U:\underline{u}\notin\bigcup_{i=0}^{n-2}[x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{i+k}],\ \forall\underline{u}\in U\right\}=\mathcal{F}_{0}^{n-2}(U:U).

So, since the system is measure preserving then,

(4.8)

\mu_{U}\left(\left\{\underline{x}\in U:\tau_{U}(\underline{x})\geq n\right\}\right)=\mu_{U}\left(\mathcal{F}_{0}^{n-2}(U:U)\right).

To use the
\psi
-mixing property, given
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we can rewrite
\mu_{U}\left(\mathcal{F}_{0}^{n-2}(U:U)\right)
as

(4.9)

\mu_{U}\left(\mathcal{F}_{k+m}^{n-2}(U:U)\right)-\mu_{U}\left({\mathcal{F}_{0}^{k+m-1}(U:U)}^{c}\cap\mathcal{F}_{k+m}^{n-2}(U:U)\right).

We can consider
\mathcal{F}_{k+m}^{n-2}(U:U)
as follows

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{k+m}^{n-2}(U:U)

\displaystyle\ \ =\bigcup\left\{[u_{0},\ldots,u_{k-1},x_{k},\ldots,x_{k+m},x_{k+m+1},\ldots,x_{n+k-2}]:\underline{u}\notin\bigcup_{i=k+m}^{n-2}[x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{i+k}],\forall\underline{u}\in U\right\},

where the union is taken over all words
(x_{k},\ldots,x_{k+m})\in\Sigma_{m}
of length
m
such that

(u_{0},\ldots,u_{k-1},x_{k},\ldots,x_{k+m},x_{k+m+1},\ldots,x_{n+k-2})\in\Sigma_{n+k-2}.

The elements in
{\mathcal{F}_{k+m}^{n-2}(U:U)}
have a nice property: on each cylinder

[u_{0},\ldots,u_{k-1},x_{k},\ldots,x_{k+m},x_{k+m+1},\ldots,x_{n+k-2}],

a part
(x_{k},\ldots,x_{k+m})
which can be considered as a gap (with length
m
) between two subsets,
U
at the beginning and at the end of the cylinder, all
(x_{k+m+1},\ldots,x_{n+k-2})\in\Sigma_{n-m-3}
where
\underline{u}\notin\bigcup_{i=k+m}^{n-2}[x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{i+k}],\ \forall\ u_{0}\in V_{i_{0}},\ldots,u_{k-1}\in V_{i_{k-1}}
, which has equal measure to
\mathcal{F}_{0}^{n-k-m-2}(\Sigma:U)
. Hence, we can apply the property of
\psi
-mixing (
2.1
) on
{\mathcal{F}_{k+m}^{n-2}(U:U)}
by Remark
4.3
,

(4.10)

1-\psi(m)\leq\frac{\mu\left({\mathcal{F}_{k+m}^{n-2}(U:U)}\right)}{\mu(U)\mu\left(\mathcal{F}_{0}^{n-k-m-2}(\Sigma:U)\right)}\leq\psi(m)+1.

Proof of upper bound on (
4.7
).
We divide the proof into two steps. First, we establish a lower bound for (
4.8
), and in the second part, we obtain an upper bound for (
4.7
).

First step.
To obtain a lower bound on (
4.8
), using (
4.9
) we derive a lower bound for
\mu_{U}\left(\mathcal{F}_{0}^{n-2}(U:U)\right)
, which in turn provides a lower bound for
\mu_{U}\left(\left\{\underline{x}\in U:\tau_{U}(\underline{x})\right\}\right)
by (
4.8
). To find a lower bound for
\mu_{U}\left(\mathcal{F}_{0}^{n-2}(U:U)\right)
, we first find a lower bound for
\mu_{U}\left(\mathcal{F}_{k+m}^{n-2}(U:U)\right)
in terms of
k
and
m
and then an upper bound for
\mu_{U}\left({\mathcal{F}_{0}^{k+m-1}(U:U)^{c}}\cap\mathcal{F}_{k+m}^{n-2}(U:U)\right)
. We can find a lower bound for
\mu_{U}\left(\mathcal{F}_{k+m}^{n-2}(U:U)\right)
by (
4.10
) as below,

\displaystyle\mu_{U}\left(\mathcal{F}_{k+m}^{n-2}(U:U)\right)=\frac{1}{\mu(U)}\mu\left(\mathcal{F}_{k+m}^{n-2}(U:U)\right)\geq\frac{1}{\mu(U)}\mu(U)\mu\left(\mathcal{F}_{0}^{n-k-m-2}(\Sigma:U)\right)(1-\psi(m))

\displaystyle=\mu\left(\left\{\underline{x}\in\Sigma:\tau_{U}(\underline{x})\geq n-m-k\right\}\right)(1-\psi(m)).

Now, to find an upper bound for
\mu_{U}\left({\mathcal{F}_{0}^{k+m-1}(U:U)}^{c}\cap\mathcal{F}_{k+m}^{n-2}(U:U)\right)
, we are dealing with three almost independent conditions (due to the
\psi
-mixing property) on
U
. In addition to requiring the elements of the set to belong to
U
, there are two additional conditions.

To find an upper bound for
\mu_{U}\left({\mathcal{F}_{0}^{k+m-1}(U:U)^{c}}\right)
, we rewrite this

\displaystyle\mu_{U}\left({\mathcal{F}_{0}^{k+m-1}(U:U)^{c}}\right)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ =\mu_{U}\left(\left\{\underline{x}\in U:\ \exists\ u_{0}\in V_{i_{0}},\ldots,u_{k-1}\in V_{i_{k-1}},\ [u_{0},\ldots,u_{k-1}]\in\left\{[x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{i+k}]\right\}_{i=0}^{k+m-1}\right\}\right).

As
U=\bigcup_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathrm{U^{*}}}[\mathbf{i}]
by property (1) in Proposition
4.1
for
\mathcal{U}_{\delta}
, where this union is pairwise disjoint, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{U}\left(\left\{\underline{x}\in U:\ \exists\ u_{0}\in V_{i_{0}},\ldots,u_{k-1}\in V_{i_{k-1}},\ [u_{0},\ldots,u_{k-1}]\in\left\{[x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{i+k}]\right\}_{i=0}^{k+m-1}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ =\frac{\mu\left(\left\{\underline{x}\in U:\ \exists\ u_{0}\in V_{i_{0}},\ldots,u_{k-1}\in V_{i_{k-1}},\ [u_{0},\ldots,u_{k-1}]\in\left\{[x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{i+k}]\right\}_{i=0}^{k+m-1}\right\}\right)}{\mu(U)}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ =\frac{\mu\left(\bigcup_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathrm{U^{*}}}\left\{\underline{x}\in[\textbf{i}]:\ [\textbf{i}]\in\left\{[x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{i+k}]\right\}_{i=0}^{k+m-1}\right\}\right)}{\sum_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathrm{U^{*}}}\mu([\textbf{i}])}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \leq\frac{\sum_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathrm{U^{*}}}\mu\left(\left\{\underline{x}\in[\textbf{i}]:\ [\textbf{i}]\in\left\{[x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{i+k}]\right\}_{i=0}^{k+m-1}\right\}\right)}{\sum_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathrm{U^{*}}}\mu([\textbf{i}])}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ =\frac{\sum_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathrm{U^{*}}}\mu_{[\textbf{i}]}\left(\left\{\underline{x}\in[\textbf{i}]:\ [\textbf{i}]\in\left\{[x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{i+k}]\right\}_{i=0}^{k+m-1}\right\}\right)\mu([\textbf{i}])}{\sum_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathrm{U^{*}}}\mu([\textbf{i}])}\leq\tilde{\theta},

where in the last inequality we use a result in
[
JT
, p16]
, where they proved for each
i
there exists a uniform
\tilde{\theta}<1
such that

\mu_{[\textbf{i}]}\left(\left\{\underline{x}\in[\textbf{i}]:[\textbf{i}]\in\left\{[x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{i+k}]\right\}_{i=0}^{k+m-1}\right\}\right)\leq\tilde{\theta}.
Hence, we conclude that
\mu_{U}\left({\mathcal{F}_{0}^{k+m-1}(U:U)^{c}}\right)\leq\tilde{\theta}
.

To find an upper bound for
\mu_{U}(\mathcal{F}_{k+m}^{n-2}(U:U))
, we use the upper bound in (
4.10
), hence:

\displaystyle\mu_{U}\left({\mathcal{F}_{0}^{k+m-1}(U:U)^{c}}\cap\mathcal{F}_{k+m}^{n-2}(U:U)\right)=\mu_{U}\left({\mathcal{F}_{0}^{k+m-1}(U:U)^{c}}\right)\mu_{U}\left(\mathcal{F}_{k+m}^{n-2}(U:U)\right)(1+\psi(m))

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\ \leq\left(\tilde{\theta}\cdot\frac{1}{\mu(U)}\cdot\mu(U)\mu\left(\left\{\underline{x}\in\Sigma:\tau_{U}(\underline{x})\geq n-m-k\right\}\right)\left(\psi(m)+1\right)\right)(1+\psi(m))

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\ =\left(\tilde{\theta}\mu\left(\left\{\underline{x}\in\Sigma:\tau_{U}(\underline{x})\geq n-m-k\right\}\right)\left(\psi(m)+1\right)^{2}\right),

where in the first equality, we use Remark
4.3
with the error term
(1+\psi(m))
. Hence, we can find the upper bound for
\mathbb{E}(\tau_{U}(\underline{x}))
as follows, by (
4.9
) and the above estimates,

\displaystyle\mu_{U}\left(\left\{\underline{x}\in U:\tau_{U}(\underline{x})\geq n\right\}\right)\geq\bigg(\mu\left\{\underline{x}\in\Sigma:\tau_{U}(\underline{x})\geq n-m-k\right\}(1-\psi(m))

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\ \ \ \ \ -\tilde{\theta}\left(\psi(m)+1\right)^{2}\mu\left(\left\{\underline{x}\in\Sigma:\tau_{U}(\underline{x})\geq n-m-k\right\}\right)\bigg)

\displaystyle\ \ \qquad\quad\qquad\qquad\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ =\left((1-\psi(m))-\tilde{\theta}\left(\psi(m)+1\right)^{2}\right)\mu\left(\left\{\underline{x}\in\Sigma:\tau_{U}(\underline{x})\geq n-m-k\right\}\right).

As
n\geq k+m
, we always have
\mu\left(\left\{\underline{x}\in\Sigma:\tau_{U}(\underline{x})\geq n-m-k\right\}\right)\geq\mu\left(\left\{\underline{x}\in\Sigma:\tau_{U}(\underline{x})\geq n\right\}\right)
, then,

\mu_{U}\left(\left\{\underline{x}\in U:\tau_{U}(\underline{x})\geq n\right\}\right)\geq\left((1-\psi(m))-\tilde{\theta}\left(\psi(m)+1\right)^{2}\right)\mu\left(\left\{\underline{x}\in\Sigma:\tau_{U}(\underline{x})\geq n\right\}\right).

We can fix some large enough
m
such that
\psi(m)
is small enough, then

(4.11)

\mu_{U}\left(\left\{\underline{x}\in U:\tau_{U}(\underline{x})\geq n\right\}\right)\geq({1-\tilde{\theta}})\mu\left(\left\{\underline{x}\in\Sigma:\tau_{U}(\underline{x})\geq n\right\}\right).

Second step.
We now use the inequality (
4.11
) obtained in the first step to derive an upper bound for (
4.7
). By summing over
n
in (
4.11
), for large
n
,

\frac{\sum_{n}\mu_{U}\left(\left\{\underline{x}\in U:\tau_{U}(\underline{x})\geq n\right\}\right)}{1-\tilde{\theta}}\geq\sum_{n}\mu\left(\left\{\underline{x}\in\Sigma:\tau_{U}(\underline{x})\geq n\right\}\right)=\mathbb{E}(\tau_{U}(\underline{x})).

Now, by Kac’s lemma we have
\sum_{n}\mu_{U}\left(\left\{\underline{x}\in U:\tau_{U}(\underline{x})\geq n\right\}\right)=\frac{1}{\mu(U)}
. Thus, we obtain

\frac{1}{(1-\tilde{\theta})}\cdot\frac{1}{\mu(U)}\geq\mathbb{E}(\tau_{U}(\underline{x})).

Since
\tilde{\theta}<1
, setting
C:=\frac{1}{1-\tilde{\theta}}>1
,

\frac{C}{\mu(U)}\geq\mathbb{E}(\tau_{U}(\underline{x})).

Proof of lower bound on (
4.7
)
. To find a lower bound for
\mathbb{E}(\tau_{U}(\underline{x}))
, we use (
4.9
) and the lower bound of (
4.10
) to obtain

\displaystyle\mu_{U}\left(\left\{\underline{x}\in U:\tau_{U}(\underline{x})\geq n\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\mu_{U}\left(\mathcal{F}_{k+m}^{n-2}(U:U)\right)-\mu_{U}\left({\mathcal{F}_{0}^{k+m-1}(U:U)^{c}}\cap\mathcal{F}_{k+m}^{n-2}(U:U)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{U}\left(\mathcal{F}_{k+m}^{n-2}(U:U)\right)=\frac{1}{\mu(U)}\mu\left(\mathcal{F}_{k+m}^{n-2}(U:U)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\mu(U)}\mu\left(\left\{\underline{x}\in\Sigma:\tau_{U}(\underline{x})\geq n-m-k\right\}\right)\mu(U)\left(\psi(m)+1\right)

(4.12)

\displaystyle=\mu\left(\left\{\underline{x}\in\Sigma:\tau_{U}(\underline{x})\geq n-m-k\right\}\right)\left(\psi(m)+1\right).

Now, based on (
[
Sh
, Theorem 3.7]
), which states that in any symbolic dynamical system with
\psi
-mixing measure, we have the estimate
\mu\left(\left\{\underline{x}\in\Sigma:\tau_{U}(\underline{x})\geq n\right\}\right)=e^{-\mu(U)(n)}(1+o(1))
, for large
n
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\mu\left(\left\{\underline{x}\in\Sigma:\tau_{U}(\underline{x})\geq n-m-k\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=e^{-\mu(U)(n-m-k)}(1+o(1))=e^{-\mu(U)n}e^{\mu(U)(m+k)}(1+o(1))

\displaystyle=\mu\left(\left\{\underline{x}\in\Sigma:\tau_{U}(\underline{x})\geq n\right\}\right)e^{\mu(U)(m+k)}(1+o(1)).

Using this approximation, we can rewrite
(
4.1
)
as follows.

\frac{\mu_{U}\left(\left\{\underline{x}\in U:\tau_{U}(\underline{x})\geq n\right\}\right)}{\left(\psi(m)+1\right)}\leq\mu\left(\left\{\underline{x}\in\Sigma:\tau_{U}(\underline{x})\geq n\right\}\right)e^{\mu(U)(m+k)}(1+o(1)).

Taking the summation over
n
and using Kac’s lemma, we conclude,

(4.13)

\frac{1}{\mu(U)\left(\psi(m)+1\right)}\leq e^{\mu(U)(m+k)}(\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(\tau_{U}(\underline{x}))+C^{\prime}),

where
\sum_{n}\mu\left(\left\{\underline{x}\in\Sigma:\tau_{U}(\underline{x})\geq n\right\}\right)o(1)=C^{\prime}
. It remains to bound
\mu(U)(m+k)
. We first estimate
\mu(U)
. By Definition
3.5
of the Gibbs measure there exists
K
such that for all
u_{0}\in V_{i_{0}},\ldots,u_{k-1}\in V_{i_{k-1}}
we have
\mu([u_{0},\ldots,u_{k-1}])\leq Ke^{S_{k}\phi(\underline{x})}
for all
\underline{x}\in[u_{0},\ldots,u_{k-1}]
. As we assume that
\phi\big|_{[n]}\leq-\kappa\log(n+1)<0
for some
\kappa>1
, then for all
\underline{x}\in[u_{0},\ldots,u_{k-1}]
,

\mu([u_{0},\ldots,u_{k-1}])\leq Ke^{S_{k}{\phi}(\underline{x})}\leq K\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}e^{\log(u_{j}+1)^{-\kappa}}=K\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}(u_{j}+1)^{-\kappa}.

Now, to calculate
\mu(U)
, there are three general cases for
U\in\mathcal{U}_{\delta}
based on the number of elements in
V_{i_{j}}
. Since one of the
V_{i_{j}}
is infinite, i.e.
\mathcal{N}_{\rho_{1}}(N)
which is defined in (
4.1
), first case can be considered as all
V_{i_{j}}
for
U
are finite, the second case is that all
V_{i_{j}}
are
\mathcal{N}_{\rho_{1}}(N)
and the last case is where
k^{\prime}
of
V_{i_{j}}
are finite for
1<k^{\prime}<k
but the others are
\mathcal{N}_{\rho_{1}}(N)
. In the following, we derive an upper bound for
\mu(U)
for each of these cases.

For the first case, we obtain

\displaystyle\mu(U)

\displaystyle=\mu\left(\bigcup_{u_{j}\in V_{i_{j}},\ 0\leq j\leq k-1}[u_{0},\ldots,u_{k-1}]\right)=\sum_{u_{j}\in V_{i_{j}},\ 0\leq j\leq k-1}\mu([u_{0},\ldots,u_{k-1}])

(4.14)

\displaystyle\leq K\sum_{u_{j}\in V_{i_{j}},\ 0\leq j\leq k-1}\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}(u_{j}+1)^{-\kappa}\leq K\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\sum_{u_{j}\in V_{i_{j}}}(u_{j}+1)^{-\kappa}\leq K\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\#V_{i_{j}}(\min_{n\in V_{i_{j}}}(n)+1)^{-\kappa}.

In this case, as
V_{i_{j}}
is not
\mathcal{N}_{\rho_{1}}(N)
, we have two general behaviours for the term
\#V_{i_{j}}(\min_{n\in V_{i_{j}}}(n)+1)^{-\kappa}
. Either

is a singleton, which implies that

\#V_{i_{j}}(\min_{n\in V_{i_{j}}}(n)+1)^{-\kappa}=(\min_{n\in V_{i_{j}}}(n)+1)^{-\kappa}\leq 2^{-\kappa},

or

, in which case, according to (

has the form of

for some

, such that

and
\min_{n\in V_{i_{j}}}=\left\lceil\frac{N}{n_{j}+1}\right\rceil
. Then,

\displaystyle\#V_{i_{j}}(\min_{n\in V_{i_{j}}}(n)+1)^{-\kappa}

\displaystyle=\frac{N}{n_{j}^{2}}\left(1+o\left(\frac{1}{N}\right)\right)\left(\frac{N}{n_{j}+1}+1\right)^{-\kappa}

\displaystyle=\frac{N}{n_{j}^{2}}\left(1+o\left(\frac{1}{N}\right)\right)\left(\frac{N+n_{j}+1}{n_{j}+1}\right)^{-\kappa}

\displaystyle\leq N\left(1+o\left(\frac{1}{N}\right)\right)\left(\frac{N+\sqrt{N}+1}{2}\right)^{-\kappa}

\displaystyle\leq N\left(1+o\left(\frac{1}{N}\right)\right)\left(N\right)^{-\kappa}=\frac{\left(1+o\left(\frac{1}{N}\right)\right)}{\left(N\right)^{\kappa-1}}<1,

where the last inequality is obtained since
\kappa>1
. Hence, it follows that

0<\#V_{i_{j}}(\min_{n\in V_{i_{j}}}(n)+1)^{-\kappa}<1,

for all
0\leq j\leq k-1
. By considering
\eta_{1}(k):=\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\#V_{i_{j}}(\min_{n\in V_{i_{j}}}(n)+1)^{-\kappa}
, consequently, for the first case we have
\mu(U)\leq K\eta_{1}(k)
, where
\eta_{1}(k)
decays exponentially to 0 as
k\to\infty
.

Now, suppose the second case, where for all
0\leq j\leq k-1
,
V_{i_{j}}=\mathcal{N}_{\rho_{1}}(N)
. Using the same method as for (
4.1
), we have

\displaystyle\mu(U)

\displaystyle\leq K\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\sum_{u_{j}\in V_{i_{j}}}(u_{j}+1)^{-\kappa}=K\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\sum_{u_{j}=N}^{\infty}(u_{j}+1)^{-\kappa}=K\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\sum_{u_{j}=N+1}^{\infty}(u_{j})^{-\kappa}.

Now, since we have

(4.15)

\sum_{u_{j}=N+1}^{\infty}(u_{j})^{-\kappa}\leq\int_{N}^{\infty}x^{-\kappa}dx=\left(\frac{N^{-\kappa+1}}{\kappa-1}\right)<1.

Hence, also in this case we write
\eta_{2}(k):=\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\sum_{u_{j}=N+1}^{\infty}(u_{j})^{-\kappa}
and
\mu(U)\leq K\eta_{2}(k)
, which decays exponentially to 0 as
k\to\infty
.

For the last case of
U
, if we consider the number of finite
V_{i_{j}}
is
k^{\prime}
for some
1<k^{\prime}<k
, so there are
k-k^{\prime}
infinite
V_{i_{j}}
such that all are
\mathcal{N}_{\rho_{1}}(N)
. In this case we use the same method in (
4.1
) to obtain the following.

\displaystyle\mu(U)

\displaystyle\leq K\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\sum_{u_{j}\in V_{i_{j}}}(u_{j}+1)^{-\kappa}=K\prod_{j=0}^{k^{\prime}-1}\sum_{u_{j}\in V_{i_{j}}}(u_{j}+1)^{-\kappa}\prod_{j=k^{\prime}}^{k-1}\sum_{u_{j}\in V_{i_{j}}}(u_{j}+1)^{-\kappa}

\displaystyle\leq K\prod_{j=0}^{k^{\prime}-1}\#V_{i_{j}}(\min_{n\in V_{i_{j}}}(n)+1)^{-\kappa}\prod_{j=k^{\prime}}^{k-1}\sum_{u_{j}=N+1}^{\infty}(u_{j})^{-\kappa}=K\eta_{1}(k^{\prime}-1)\eta_{2}(k-k^{\prime}).

Since
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
decay exponentially to 0, and taking
\eta_{3}(k):=\eta_{1}(k).\eta_{2}(k)
, in the third case also we obtain
\mu(U)\leq K\eta_{2}(k)
, where
\eta_{2}
decays exponentially to 0.

This leads us to the conclusion that for all

,
\mu(U)=\mathcal{O}\left(\eta(k)\right)
, such that
\eta(k)
has exponential behaviour converging to 0. Thus, we have
e^{\mu(U)(m+k)}\leq e^{\eta(k)(m+k)}
.

Hence, using this inequality in (

),

\frac{{}^{1}}{\mu(U)\left(\psi(m)+1\right)}\leq\left(e^{\eta(k)(m+k)}\right)\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(\tau_{U}(\underline{x}))+C^{\prime}.

We can fix some large enough

\frac{c}{\mu(U)}\leq\frac{1}{\mu(U)}-C^{\prime}\leq\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(\tau_{U}(\underline{x})),

for some

∎

Remark 4.5
.

A statement analogous to Theorem
4.4
can be obtained for any cylinder in a countable Markov shift with a Gibbs measure.

As we use the method used in
[
JT
, Section 5]
to prove our results, we state a lemma that was established in the proof of Theorem
2.4
. Before stating the lemma, we consider the following definitions.

Definition 4.6
.

Let
\mathcal{U}
be a finite set of subsets in
\Sigma
. We say
\mathcal{U}
is a
subpartition
if all elements in this set are pairwise disjoint and there exists a finite or countable set of finite words
\mathrm{U}^{*}\subset\Sigma^{*}
such that
\mathcal{U}=\bigcup_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathrm{U}^{*}}[\textbf{i}]
.

Note that a subpartition is not necessarily a partition. Recall that for
\textbf{i}\in\Sigma
, we have
\tau_{\mathcal{U}}(\textbf{i})=\inf\left\{n\geq 1:\{\textbf{i},\ldots,\sigma^{n}(\textbf{i})\}\ \text{hits each}\ U\in\mathcal{U}\right\}
.

We now state the following lemma. To see the proof of this lemma, see
[
JT
, Proof of Theorem 2.2]
.

Lemma 4.7
.

Assume that
\mathcal{U}=\left\{\mathrm{U}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{U}_{N}\right\}
is a finite subpartition on
\left(\Sigma,\sigma\right)
equipped with a Gibbs measure
\mu
. Let
\mathcal{U}=\bigcup_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathrm{U}^{*}}[\mathbf{i}]
such that
|\mathbf{i}|=L
. Then, there exist
C>1
such that

\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(\tau_{\mathcal{U}})\leq C\left(L+\max_{1\leq m\leq N}\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(\tau_{\mathrm{U}_{m}})\right)\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{k}\right).

We use this lemma in establishing the upper bounds in our results.

4.2.
Proof of Theorem
3.6

In this section we present the proof of Theorem
3.6
.

Proof of Theorem
3.6
.

We first prove the upper bound and then proceed to prove the lower bound. The idea of the proof for both bounds is to make use of property (2) for
\mathcal{U}_{\delta}
. Indeed, having a cover time for
\Sigma
leads to having a hitting time for
\mathcal{U}_{\delta}
, and vice versa. This is the key point that, with the help of the propositions in Section
4
, completes the proof.

Proof of upper bound
. Let
\delta>0
. By Proposition
4.1
, there exists pairwise disjoint open cover
\mathcal{U}_{\delta}=\left\{(i_{0},\ldots,i_{k-1})_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}:1\leq i_{l}\leq K_{\delta},\ \forall\ 0\leq l\leq k-1\right\}
satisfying properties (1) and (2) for
(\Sigma,\sigma)
, where
K_{\delta}=\left\lceil 2\sqrt{\frac{1}{\delta}}\left(1+o\left(\sqrt{\delta}\right)\right)\right\rceil
. In particular, there exist
1<T<\infty
such that for
\underline{x}\in\Sigma
, there is a set
(i_{0},\ldots,i_{k-1})_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}
with
B_{\delta}(\underline{x})\subset(i_{0},\ldots,i_{k-1})_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}\subset B_{T\delta}(\underline{x})
. If for all
\underline{x}\in\Sigma
,
\{\underline{x},\ldots,\sigma^{n}(\underline{x})\}
hits each element of
\mathcal{U}_{\delta/2T}
, then
\{\underline{x},\ldots,\sigma^{n}(\underline{x})\}
is
\delta
-dense in
\Sigma
. Moreover, as
(i_{0},\ldots,i_{k-1})_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}\subset B_{2\delta}(\underline{x})
for all
(i_{0},\ldots,i_{k-1})_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}\in\mathcal{U}_{\delta/2T}
, we have
\tau_{\delta}\leq\tau_{\mathcal{U}_{\delta/2T}}
. Hence,

\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(\tau_{\delta})\leq\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(\tau_{\mathcal{U}_{\delta/2T}}).

For convenience in the computations, we fix
\mathcal{U}_{\delta/2T}=\mathcal{U}_{\delta}
. Since
\mathcal{U}_{\delta}=\bigcup_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathrm{U}^{*}}[\mathbf{i}]
is a subpartition for
\Sigma
such that
|\textbf{i}|=\left\lceil\frac{\log(\delta(1-\theta))}{\log(\theta)}\right\rceil
, where
\mathrm{U}^{*}=\left\{(x_{0},\ldots,x_{k-1}):\ 0\leq l\leq k-1,x_{l}\in V_{j},\ 1\leq j\leq K_{\delta}\right\}\subset\Sigma^{*}
is a countable set, therefore, by Lemma
4.7
there exists constant

such that

\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(\tau_{\mathcal{U_{\delta}}})\leq C\left(\left\lceil\frac{\log(\delta(1-\theta))}{\log(\theta)}\right\rceil+\max_{1\leq i_{0},\ldots,i_{k-1}\leq K_{\delta}}\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left(\tau_{(i_{0},\ldots,i_{k-1})_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}}\right)\right)\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\#(\mathcal{U}_{\delta})}\frac{1}{k}\right).

Now, since
\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\#(\mathcal{U}_{\delta})}\frac{1}{k}\right)\leq C^{\prime}\log\#(\mathcal{U}_{\delta})
for some
C^{\prime}>1
, and in view of Theorem
4.4
, where we showed that
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left(\tau_{(i_{0},\ldots,i_{k-1})_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}}(\underline{x})\right)\leq\frac{C}{\mu\left((i_{0},\ldots,i_{k-1})_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}\right)}
for some

, we conclude

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(\tau_{\mathcal{U_{\delta}}})

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\lceil\frac{\log(\delta(1-\theta))}{\log(\theta)}\right\rceil+\max_{1\leq i_{0},\ldots,i_{k-1}\leq K_{\delta}}\frac{1}{\mu\left((i_{0},\ldots,i_{k-1})_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}\right)}\right)\left(\log\#(\mathcal{U}_{\delta})\right)

(4.16)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\lceil\frac{\log(\delta(1-\theta))}{\log(\theta)}\right\rceil+\frac{1}{\min_{\underline{x}\in\Sigma}\mu(B_{\delta/2T}(\underline{x}))}\right)\left(\log\#(\mathcal{U}_{\delta})\right)

(4.17)

\displaystyle=C\left(\left\lceil\frac{\log(\delta(1-\theta))}{\log(\theta)}\right\rceil+\frac{1}{M_{\mu}(\delta/2T)}\right)\left(\log\#(\mathcal{U}_{\delta})\right).

Recall that
\#(\mathcal{U}_{\delta})=\left\lceil\left(2\sqrt{\frac{1}{\delta}}\left(1+o\left(\sqrt{\delta}\right)\right)\right)^{\frac{\log(\delta(1-\theta))}{\log(\theta)}}\right\rceil
. Now, we have

\log\#(\mathcal{U}_{\delta})=C\left(\log\frac{1}{\delta}\right)^{2}+o(1).
Hence, we can replace
\log\#(\mathcal{U}_{\delta})
by
\left(\log\frac{1}{\delta}\right)^{2}
in (

\frac{\log(\delta(1-\theta))}{\log(\theta)}\left(\log\frac{1}{\delta}\right)^{2}=\mathcal{O}\left(\left(\log\frac{1}{\delta}\right)^{3}\right)

\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(\tau_{\mathcal{U_{\delta}}})\leq\left(\frac{C}{M_{\mu}(\delta/2T)}\right)\left(\log\frac{1}{\delta}\right)^{2}.

Proof of lower bound
. We now find a lower bound for

is
\delta

Now, by the definition,

, for all
(i_{0},\ldots,i_{k-1})_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}\in\mathcal{U}_{2\delta}

4.4
, where for some

and for each

we have

\frac{c}{\mu((i_{0},\ldots,i_{k-1})_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}})}\leq\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(\tau_{(i_{0},\ldots,i_{k-1})_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}}).

Since there is some

Therefore, the proof of Theorem

Remark 5.1

We note the behaviour of the two maps that will be used in the proof of the following proposition. The map

In addition, the mapping

Now, analogously to Proposition

(5.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\rho_{2}}(N):=\{N,N+1,\ldots\},

\mathcal{N}_{\rho_{2}}\left(N-C_{\alpha,1}-1\right)
in which the elements satisfy

{\rho_{2}}\left(N-2-\frac{1}{\alpha}\log\left(e^{-\alpha}+1\right)-i,N-2-\frac{1}{\alpha}\log\left(e^{-\alpha}+1\right)\right)\leq\frac{1}{e^{\alpha N}},

which simplifies to

(5.3)

\displaystyle\frac{e^{\alpha\left(N-2-\frac{1}{\alpha}\log\left(e^{-\alpha}+1\right)\right)}-e^{\alpha\left(N-2-\frac{1}{\alpha}\log\left(e^{-\alpha}+1\right)-i\right)}}{e^{\alpha\left(N-2-\frac{1}{\alpha}\log\left(e^{-\alpha}+1\right)-i\right)}e^{\alpha\left(N-2-\frac{1}{\alpha}\log\left(e^{-\alpha}+1\right)\right)}}=\frac{1-e^{-\alpha i}}{e^{\alpha\left(N-2-\frac{1}{\alpha}\log\left(e^{-\alpha}+1\right)-i\right)}}\leq\frac{1}{e^{\alpha N}}.

In order to find
i
, that gives the cardinality of
\mathcal{N}_{\rho_{2}}\left(N-C_{\alpha,1}-1\right)
, we have the following condition by (
5.3
),

\displaystyle\frac{1-e^{-\alpha i}}{e^{\alpha(-2-\frac{1}{\alpha}\log\left(e^{-\alpha}+1\right)-i)}}\leq 1,

which implies

1-e^{-\alpha i}\leq e^{-2\alpha-\log\left(e^{-\alpha}+1\right)}e^{-i\alpha},

from which it follows that

. Hence,

i\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}\log\left(e^{-2\alpha-\log\left(e^{-\alpha}+1\right)}\right)
, and we set
i=\left\lfloor\frac{1}{\alpha}\log\left(e^{-2\alpha-\log\left(e^{-\alpha}+1\right)}\right)\right\rfloor
. So, the smallest number in
\mathcal{N}_{\rho_{2}}\left(N-C_{\alpha,1}-1\right)
is

\displaystyle N-2-\left\lfloor\frac{1}{\alpha}\log\left(e^{-\alpha}+1\right)\right\rfloor-i

\displaystyle=N-2-\left\lfloor\frac{1}{\alpha}\log\left(e^{-\alpha}+1\right)\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\frac{1}{\alpha}\log\left(e^{-2\alpha-\log\left(e^{-\alpha}+1\right)}\right)\right\rfloor

Hence,

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\rho_{2}}\left(N-C_{\alpha,1}-1\right)=\left\{N-C_{\alpha,2},\ldots,N-C_{\alpha,1}-1\right\},

where
C_{\alpha,2}:=2+\left\lfloor\frac{1}{\alpha}\log\left(e^{-\alpha}+1\right)\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor\frac{1}{\alpha}\log\left(e^{-2\alpha-\log\left(e^{-\alpha}+1\right)}\right)\right\rfloor
. Similarly, by continuing this process, the first elements of these neighbourhoods can be represented in the form of

is a constant in terms of
\alpha
in the

j^{\prime}
, the number of these neighbourhoods, depends on
\alpha

\mathcal{V}_{\delta}^{\alpha}

with respect to
{\rho_{2}}
.

\displaystyle K_{\delta}^{\alpha}=1+j^{\prime}+N_{s}^{\alpha}=1+C_{\alpha}+\left\lfloor N+\frac{1}{\alpha}\log(1-e^{-\alpha})+1\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\frac{1}{\alpha}\log\left(\frac{1}{\delta}\right)\right\rfloor+\mathcal{O}_{\alpha}(1).

elements.

So, for

K_{\delta}^{\alpha}

, as a finite collection of open neighbourhoods with

.

Theorem 5.3
.

Assume that
(\Sigma,\sigma)

Proof.

The proof is analogous to the proof given in Theorem
4.4
. In fact, since
\mathcal{U}_{\delta}^{\alpha}
satisfies the
\psi
-mixing property (as explained in Remark
4.3
), the upper bound follows. For the lower bound, it suffices to show that
\mu(U)=\mathcal{O}(\eta(k))
where
U
is an element in
\mathcal{U}_{\delta}^{\alpha}
and
\eta(k)
is an exponentially decreasing to zero as

. To this end, let
U=(i_{0},\ldots,i_{k-1})_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}}^{\alpha}\in\mathcal{U}_{\delta}^{\alpha}
. We use the following inequality, which was obtained in (

\mu(U)\leq K\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\#V_{i_{j}}^{\alpha}(\min_{n\in V_{i_{j}}^{\alpha}}(n)+1)^{-\kappa}.
Now, since in the proof of Proposition
5.2
the quantity of the sets

vary with respect to
\gamma

are finite (singletons). Second, all the

are infinite. Finally, the third case is a combination of the previous two, namely, some are finite, and others are infinite.

Hence,

\mu(U)\leq K\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\#V_{i_{j}}^{\alpha}(\min_{n\in V_{i_{j}}^{\alpha}}(n)+1)^{-\kappa}\leq K(2^{-\kappa})^{k},

which tends to zero exponentially as

.
For the case all the

are infinite i.e. for all

, where

For the third case, if we consider the number of finite

is

\mathcal{N}_{\rho_{2}}(N)

\displaystyle\mu(U)

\displaystyle\leq K\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\sum_{u_{j}\in V_{i_{j}}^{\alpha}}(u_{j}+1)^{-\kappa}\leq K\prod_{j=0}^{k^{\prime}-1}\#V_{i_{j}}^{\alpha}(\min_{n\in V_{i_{j}}^{\alpha}}(n)+1)^{-\kappa}\prod_{j=k^{\prime}}^{k-1}\sum_{u_{j}=N+1}(u_{j})^{-\kappa}

. So, in the case

Now, if we consider the second case, where

The remainder of the proof proceeds as in Theorem

Proof of Theorem

k=\left\lceil\frac{\log(e^{\alpha}\delta(1-\theta))}{\log(\theta)}\right\rceil=D\log\left(\frac{1}{\delta}\right)+\mathcal{O}(1)
for some constant
D>0
, we then obtain

\min_{(i_{0},\ldots,i_{k-1})_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}^{\alpha}}\in\mathcal{U}_{\delta}^{\alpha}}\mu((i_{0},\ldots,i_{k-1})_{\mathcal{V}_{\delta}^{\alpha}})=D2^{-\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}\log\frac{1}{\delta}\right)\log\frac{1}{\delta}}(1+o(1)).

Note that for each
r>0
, we have

\min_{(i_{0},\ldots,i_{k-1})_{\mathcal{V}_{r\delta}^{\alpha}}\in\mathcal{U}_{r\delta}^{\alpha}}\mu((i_{0},\ldots,i_{k-1})_{\mathcal{V}_{r\delta}^{\alpha}})=D2^{-\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}\log\frac{1}{r\delta}\right)\log\frac{1}{\delta}}(1+o(1)).

Now, considering
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{2T}
for
U\in\mathcal{U}_{\frac{\varepsilon\delta}{T}}^{\alpha}
we have
\min_{U\in\mathcal{U}_{\frac{\varepsilon\delta}{T}}^{\alpha}}\mu(U)=D2^{-\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}\log\frac{T}{\varepsilon\delta}\right)\log\frac{1}{\delta}}(1+o(1))
and then

D2^{-\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}\log\frac{T}{\varepsilon\delta}\right)\log\frac{1}{\delta}}(1+o(1))\leq M_{\mu}\left(\varepsilon\delta\right).

Similarly for
\mathcal{U}_{\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon}}^{\alpha}
, we have
\min_{U\in\mathcal{U}_{\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon}}^{\alpha}}\mu(U)=D2^{-\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}\log\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta}\right)\log\frac{1}{\delta}}(1+o(1))
, and then

M_{\mu}\left(\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon}\right)\leq D2^{-\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}\log\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta}\right)\log\frac{1}{\delta}}(1+o(1)).

Hence, by Theorem
3.7
,

(6.4)

c2^{\frac{1}{\alpha}\log\left(\frac{T}{\varepsilon\delta}\right)\log\frac{1}{\delta}}\leq\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(\tau_{\delta}(\underline{x}))\leq C2^{\frac{1}{\alpha}\log\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta}\right)\log\frac{1}{\delta}}\left(\log\frac{1}{\delta}\right)\left(\log\log\frac{1}{\delta}\right),

for some
0<c<C<\infty
.
