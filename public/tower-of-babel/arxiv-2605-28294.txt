Simultaneous approximation properties of a certain genuine hybrid type operator Thanks: ∗ Corresponding author Keywords: Positive Linear operators, rate of convergence. 2010 AMS Subject classification:41A36, 41A25


Abstract
. We obtain estimates in simultaneous approximation for a summation-integral type genuine hybrid operator. The convergence of derivatives of operator to the corresponding derivatives of the functions is proved and estimates for rate of approximation are obtained.

Introduction

The classical Sz
\acute{a}

[

V_{n}(f,x)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}f\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)\frac{e^{-nx}(nx)^{k}}{k!}.

Through the years extensive study has been done on approximation properties of these operators. Several modifications of the Sz
\acute{a}
sz-Mirakian Operators have also been introduced. One such modification was established by Prasad et al.
[
13
]
where he considered Baskakov basis function as the weight function and subsequently defined it as

G_{n}(f;x)=(n-1)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-nx}(nx)^{k}}{k!}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}f(t)\binom{n+k-1}{k}\frac{t^{k}}{(1+t)^{n+k}}\,dt.

Păltǎnea
[
12
]
studied a two parameter integral operator with modified Szász–Mirakyan basis in summation.
Gupta et al.
[
7
]
further generalized operators
G_{n}(f;x)
by taking a general form of two basis functions which are modified Szász and Baskakov function and referred to them as genuine hybrid operator. For function
f:[0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and parameters
\rho>0,

c\in(0,1)
the operators in
[
7
]
are given by

(1)

B^{\rho}_{\alpha}(f;x,c)=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}p_{\alpha,k}(x,c)\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\theta^{\rho}_{\alpha,k}(t)f(t)dt+p_{\alpha,0}(x,c)f(0),

where

p_{\alpha,k}(x,c)=\frac{(-x)^{k}}{k!}\phi^{(k)}_{\alpha,c}(x),\theta^{\rho}_{\alpha,k}(t)=\frac{\alpha\rho}{\Gamma(k\rho)}e^{-\alpha\rho t}(\alpha\rho t)^{k\rho-1}.

The authors of
[
7
]
established direct approximation theorems in terms of modulus of smoothness and proved point-wise convergence for derivatives of the operator
B^{\rho}_{\alpha}(f;x,c).

The simultaneous approximation properties i.e. the study of convergence behaviour of derivatives of a linear positive operator for a function with given smoothness is an interesting part of approximation theory.
Recently, Tang et al.
[
18
]
explored positive and inverse results for weighted simultaneous approximation of linear combinations of Baskakov operator using jacobi weight function. It is worth mentioning Mohammed et al.
[
4

Lately, Abel
[
2

Mohammed et.al.
[
11

s>\frac{-1}{2}.

The aim of this paper is to study a special case of the operator (
1
) which is defined as

(2)

L_{n,c}f(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{n,k}(x)\int_{0}^{\infty}\theta_{n,k}(t)f(t)\,dt

p_{n,k}(x)=\frac{(n/c)_{k}(cx)^{k}}{k!(cx+1)^{\frac{n}{c}+k}},

\theta_{n,k}(t)=\frac{ne^{-nx}(nx)^{k}}{k!}
and

(n/c)_{k}=\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}\left(\frac{n}{c}+i\right).

The direct results in simultaneous approximation are obtained in local setting. For global results, we use the properties of Steklov means to obtain error estimates in terms of modulus of smoothness. The Steklov averaging means are defined as follows.

f_{h,s}(t)=h^{-s}\int\limits_{-h/2}^{h/2}\!\!...\!\!\int\limits_{-h/2}^{h/2}\left(f(t)+(-1)^{s-1}\Delta^{s}_{\sum_{i=1}^{s}t_{i}}f(t)\right)\prod_{i=1}^{s}dt_{i},t\in[a_{1},b_{1}].

.

For the function

(a)

has derivatives up to order

over

[a_{1},b_{1}]
;

(b)

\big\|f^{(r)}_{h,s}\big\|_{[a_{1},b_{1}]}\leqslant Ch^{-r}\omega_{s}(f,h,[a,b]),r=1,2,...,s;

(c)

\|f-f_{h,s}\|_{[a_{1},b_{1}]}\leqslant C\omega_{s}(f,h,[a,b])
;

(d)

\|f_{h,s}\|_{[a_{1},b_{1}]}\leqslant C\|f\|_{[a,b]};

(e)

\big\|f^{(s)}_{h,s}\big\|_{[a_{1},b_{1}]}\leqslant Ch^{-s}\|f\|_{[a,b]},

where

Lemma 3
.

Let
\mu_{n,i}(x)=L_{n,c}((t-x)^{i},x).
Then, there holds the recurrence

(3)

\mu_{n,m+1}(x)=\frac{1}{n}\Big(x(1+cx)\left[\mu^{\prime}_{n,m}(x)+m\mu_{n,m-1}(x)\right]+(m+1)\mu_{n,m}(x)+mx\mu_{n,m-1}(x)\Big)

Proof.

We have
x(1+cx)\frac{d}{dx}p_{n,k}(x)=(k-nx)p_{n,k}
and
\frac{d}{dt}\theta_{n,k}(x)=(\frac{k}{t}-n)\theta_{n,k}(t).

Making use of the identity
k-nx=(\frac{k}{t}-n)(t-x)+(\frac{k}{t}-n)x+n(t-x)
in the identity

\mu^{\prime}_{n,m}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(p^{\prime}_{n,k}(x)(t-x)^{m}-m(t-x)^{m-1}p_{n,k}(x)\right)\theta_{n,k}(t)f(t)\,dt

we get

\displaystyle x(1+cx)\left[\mu^{\prime}_{n,m}(x)+m\mu_{n,m-1}(x)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{n,k}(x)\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\left(\frac{k}{t}-n\right)\left[(t-x)^{m+1}+x(t-x)^{m}\right]+n(t-x)^{m+1}\right)\theta_{n,k}(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{n,k}(x)\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[(t-x)^{m+1}+x(t-x)^{m}\right]\theta^{\prime}_{n,k}(t)\,dt+n\mu_{n,m+1}(x).

Integration by parts implies

\displaystyle x(1+cx)\left[\mu^{\prime}_{n,m}(x)+m\mu_{n,m-1}(x)\right]

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{n,k}(x)\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(m+1)(t-x)^{m}+mx(t-x)^{m+1}\right)\theta_{n,k}(t)\,dt+n\mu_{n,m+1}(x)

\displaystyle=-(m+1)\mu_{n,m}(x)-mx\mu_{n,m-1}(x)+n\mu_{n,m+1}(x).

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4
.

Let
M_{n,r}(x)=L_{n,c}(t^{r},x).
Then,

(4)

M_{n,r}(x)=n^{-r}\sum_{j=0}^{r}\binom{r}{j}(1+cx)^{j}(n/c)_{j}(-1)^{r-j}\prod_{k=1}^{r-j}(n/c-k).

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle L_{n,c}(e^{\theta t},x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{n,k}(x)\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{n}{k!}(nt)^{k}e^{-(n-\theta)t}\,dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{n,k}(x)\left(\frac{n}{n-\theta}\right)^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{n(n-\theta)^{n/c-1}}{(n-\theta(1+cx))^{n/c}}\right).

Now, (
4
) follows by the linearity of the operator
L_{n,c}(f,x).

By Leibniz rule we get

\displaystyle M_{n,r}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial^{r}}{\partial\theta^{r}}\left(\frac{n(n-\theta)^{n/c-1}}{(n-\theta(1+cx))^{n/c}}\right)\Bigg|_{\theta=0}

\displaystyle=n\sum_{j=0}^{r}\binom{r}{j}\frac{\partial^{j}}{\partial\theta^{j}}(n-\theta(1+cx))^{-n/c}\frac{\partial^{r-j}}{\partial\theta^{r-j}}(n-\theta)^{n/c-1}\Bigg|_{\theta=0}

\displaystyle=n\sum_{j=0}^{r}\binom{r}{j}(1+cx)^{j}(n/c)_{j}n^{-r-1}(-1)^{r-j}\prod_{k=1}^{r-j}(n/c-k)\Bigg|_{\theta=0}

\displaystyle=n^{-r}\sum_{j=0}^{r}\binom{r}{j}(1+cx)^{j}(n/c)_{j}(-1)^{r-j}\prod_{k=1}^{r-j}(n/c-k).

∎

Lemma 7
.

Let
f\in C^{r}[0,\infty).
Then,

(6)

\frac{d^{r}}{dw^{r}}L_{n,c}(f,w)\Big|_{w=x}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{n+r,k}(x)\int_{0}^{\infty}\theta_{n,k+r}(t)f^{(r)}(t)\,dt

Proof.

Since,

\frac{d}{dx}p_{n,k}(x)=n\left(p_{n+1,k-1}(x)-p_{n+1,k}(x)\right)

and

\frac{d}{dt}\theta_{n,k}(t)=n\left(\theta_{n,k-1}(t)-\theta_{n,k}(t)\right),

for

we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dw}L_{n,c}(f,w)\Big|_{w=x}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}n\left(p_{n+1,k-1}(x)-p_{n+1,k}(x)\right)\int_{0}^{\infty}\theta_{n,k+r}(t)f^{(r)}(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}np_{n+1,k}(x)\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\theta_{n,k+1}(t)-\theta_{n,k}(t)\right)f(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{n+1,k}(x)\int_{0}^{\infty}\theta^{\prime}_{n,k+1}(t)f(t)\,dt.

Integration by parts implies

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dw}L_{n,c}(f,w)\Big|_{w=x}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{n+1,k}(x)\int_{0}^{\infty}\theta_{n,k+1}(t)f^{\prime}(t)\,dt.

Now, by straightforward calculations and principal of mathematical induction, the proof follows.
∎

3.
Main Results

Theorem 1
.

Let
f\in C^{s}[0,\infty)
and
x\in[0,\infty)
then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}(c,s)}\frac{d^{s}}{dw^{s}}L_{n,c}(f;w)\Big|_{w=x}=f^{(s)}(x).

Proof.

Writing

f(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{s}\frac{f^{(k)}(x)}{k!}(t-x)^{k}+(t-x)^{s}\epsilon(t,x)

and operating
L_{n,c}(f;\cdot)
we get

\displaystyle L_{n,c}(f;w)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{s}\frac{f^{(k)}(x)}{k!}L_{n,c}((t-x)^{k},w)+L_{n,c}((t-x)^{s}\epsilon(t,x),w).

Since
L_{n,c}((t-x)^{k},w)
are polynomials in
w
of degree at most
k,

\displaystyle\frac{d^{s}}{dw^{s}}L_{n,c}(f;w)\Big|_{w=x}

\displaystyle=\frac{f^{(s)}(x)}{s!}\frac{d^{s}}{dw^{s}}L_{n,c}((t-x)^{s},w)\Big|_{w=x}+\frac{d^{s}}{dw^{s}}L_{n,c}((t-x)^{s}\epsilon(t,x),w)\Big|_{w=x}.

The coefficient of
w^{r}
in
L_{n,c}(t^{r},w)
is equal to

n\frac{\partial^{r}}{\partial\theta^{r}}(n-\theta(1+cw))^{-n/c}(n-\theta)^{n/c-1}\Bigg|_{\theta=0}=n^{-r}(n/c)_{r}(1+cw)^{r}.

Thus

\displaystyle\frac{d^{s}}{dw^{s}}L_{n,c}((t-x)^{s},w)\Big|_{w=x}

\displaystyle=\frac{d^{s}}{dw^{s}}\left(\sum_{r=0}^{s}(-x)^{s-r}\binom{s}{r}L_{n,c}(t^{r},w)\right)\Big|_{w=x}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=0}^{s}(-x)^{s-r}\binom{s}{r}n^{-r}(n/c)_{r}\frac{d^{s}}{dw^{s}}(1+cw)^{r}\Big|_{w=x}

(8)

\displaystyle=s!\lambda_{n}(c,s).

As the operator
L_{n,c}
are bounded and linear, it follows by standard arguments that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{d^{s}}{dw^{s}}L_{n,c}((t-x)^{s}\epsilon(t,x),w)\Big|_{w=x}=0.

The first term is therefore

\frac{f^{(s)}(x)}{s!}\frac{d^{s}}{dw^{s}}L_{n,c}((t-w)^{s},w)\Big|_{w=x}=f^{(s)}(x)\lambda_{n}(c,s).

It is plain that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}(c,s)}\left(\frac{f^{(s)}(x)}{s!}\frac{d^{s}}{dw^{s}}L_{n,c}((t-w)^{s},w)\Big|_{w=x}\right)=f^{(s)}(x).

∎

Remark 1
.

If,
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}c^{s}n^{-s}(n/c)_{s}=1,
then we get the limit

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{d^{s}}{dw^{s}}L_{n,c}(f;w)\Big|_{w=x}=f^{(s)}(x).

Theorem 2
.

Let
f\in C^{s+2}[0,\infty)
and
x\in[0,\infty)
then

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}n\Bigg(\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}(c,s)}\frac{d^{s}}{dw^{s}}L_{n,c}(f,w)\Big|_{w=x}-f^{(s)}(x)\Bigg)

\displaystyle=(cx+s+1)f^{(s+1)}(x)+\frac{x(2+cx)}{2}f^{(s+2)}(x).

Proof.

We write

f(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{s+2}\frac{f^{(k)}(x)}{k!}(t-x)^{k}+(t-x)^{s}\epsilon(t,x)

and operate by
L_{n,c}(f;\cdot)
both side to get

\displaystyle L_{n,c}(f;w)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{s+2}\frac{f^{(k)}(x)}{k!}L_{n,c}((t-x)^{k},w)+L_{n,c}((t-x)^{s}\epsilon(t,x),w)

Since,
L_{n,c}((t-x)^{k},w)
are polynomials of degree at most
k,
we have

\displaystyle\frac{d^{s}}{dw^{s}}L_{n,c}(f;w)\Big|_{w=x}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=s}^{s+2}\frac{f^{(k)}(x)}{k!}\frac{d^{s}}{dw^{s}}L_{n,c}((t-x)^{k},w)\Big|_{w=x}+\frac{d^{s}}{dw^{s}}L_{n,c}((t-x)^{s}\epsilon(t,x),w)\Big|_{w=x}

\displaystyle=E_{1}+E_{2}.

It is sufficient to calculate coefficients of
w^{k}
for
k=s,s+1
and
k=s+2
only. By (
3
), we have that coefficient of
w^{s}
in

L_{n,c}((t-x)^{s},w)
is
\lambda_{n}(c,s).
So that
L_{n,c}((t-x)^{s},w)=\lambda_{n}(c,s)w^{s}+O(w^{s-1}).
Therefore,

(9)

\frac{d^{s}}{dw^{s}}L_{n,c}((t-x)^{s},w)\Big|_{w=x}=s!\lambda_{n}(c,s).

Next, we calculate
\frac{d^{s}}{dw^{s}}L_{n,c}((t-x)^{s+1},w)\Big|_{w=x}
as follows. We have

\displaystyle L_{n,c}((t-x)^{s+1},w)

\displaystyle=L_{n,c}(t^{s+1},w)-(s+1)xL_{n,c}(t^{s},w)+O(w^{s-1}),

\displaystyle=-(s+1)x\left(\lambda_{n}(c,s)w^{s}\right)+M_{n,s+1}(w)+O(w^{s-1}).

Using (
4
),

\displaystyle\frac{d^{s}}{dw^{s}}M_{n,s+1}(w)

\displaystyle=\frac{d^{s}}{dw^{s}}\Bigg[n\Bigg\{(s+1)\frac{\partial^{s}}{\partial\theta^{s}}(n-\theta(1+cw))^{-n/c}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}(n-\theta)^{n/c-1}\Bigg|_{\theta=0}

\displaystyle+(n-\theta)^{n/c-1}\frac{\partial^{s+1}}{\partial\theta^{s+1}}(n-\theta(1+cw))^{-n/c}\Bigg|_{\theta=0}\Bigg\}+O(w^{s-1})\Bigg]

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda_{n}(c,s)}{n}(s+1)!\left((n+cs)(w+1/c)-n/c+1\right).

Therefore, we have

(10)

\displaystyle\frac{f^{(s+1)}(x)}{(s+1)!}\frac{d^{s}}{dw^{s}}L_{n,c}((t-x)^{s+1},w)\Big|_{w=x}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{n}(c,s)(1+s(1+cx))\frac{f^{(s+1)}(x)}{n}.

Finally, we calculate
\frac{d^{s}}{dw^{s}}L_{n,c}((t-x)^{s+2},w)\Big|_{w=x}.
In the identity

\frac{d^{s}}{dw^{s}}L_{n,c}((t-x)^{s+2},w)=\sum_{j=0}^{s+2}\binom{s+2}{j}(-x)^{s+2-j}\frac{d^{s}}{dw^{s}}L_{n,c}(t^{j},w)

we need to compute three terms
E_{j}^{\prime}s
corresponding to

respectively.
Now, following the steps of (
9
), we get

L_{n,c}(t^{s},w)=\binom{s+2}{2}x^{2}\lambda_{n}(c,s)w^{s}+O(w^{s-1})

so that

(11)

E_{s}=\binom{s+2}{2}x^{2}\lambda_{n}(c,s)s!,

\displaystyle E_{s+1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{f^{(s+2)}(x)}{(s+2)!}(s+2)x\frac{d^{s}}{dw^{s}}L_{n,c}((t-x)^{s+1},w)\Big|_{w=x}

(12)

\displaystyle=-\frac{f^{(s+2)}(x)}{(s+2)!}(s+2)x\lambda_{n}(c,s)(s+1)!\left(\frac{(n+cs)(w+1/c)-n/c+1}{n}\right).

And for

we observe

\displaystyle E_{s+2}

\displaystyle=\frac{f^{(s+2)}(x)}{(s+2)!}\frac{d^{s}}{dw^{s}}L_{n,c}((t-x)^{s+2},w)\Big|_{w=x}

\displaystyle=\frac{f^{(s+2)}(x)}{(s+2)!}n\frac{d^{s}}{dw^{s}}\Bigg[\frac{\partial^{s+2}}{\partial\theta^{s+2}}(n-\theta(1+cw))^{-n/c}(n-\theta)^{n/c-1}

\displaystyle+(s+2)\frac{\partial^{s+1}}{\partial\theta^{s+1}}(n-\theta(1+cw))^{-n/c}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}(n-\theta)^{n/c-1}

\displaystyle+\frac{(s+1)(s+1)}{2}\frac{\partial^{s}}{\partial\theta^{s}}(n-\theta(1+cw))^{-n/c}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\theta^{2}}(n-\theta)^{n/c-1}\Bigg]_{w=x}

\displaystyle=\frac{f^{(s+2)}(x)}{(s+2)!}\frac{\lambda_{n}(c,s)}{n^{s+2}}\frac{d^{s}}{dw^{s}}\Bigg[(n/c+s+1)(n/c+s)(1+cw)^{s+2}

\displaystyle-(s+2)(n/c+s)(1+cw)^{s+1}(n/c-1)

\displaystyle+\frac{(s+2)(s+1)}{2}(n/c-1)(n/c-2)(1+cw)^{s}\Bigg]_{w=x}

\displaystyle=\frac{f^{(s+2)}(x)\lambda_{n}(c,s)}{n^{2}}\Bigg[(n/c+s+1)(n/c+s)\frac{(1+cx)^{2}}{2}

(13)

\displaystyle-(n/c+s)(n/c-1)(1+cx)+\frac{(n/c-1)(n/c-2)}{2}\Bigg].

Combining (
11
)-(
3
), it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{d^{s}}{dw^{s}}L_{n,c}((t-x)^{s+2},w)\Big|_{w=x}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{n}(c,s)f^{(s)}(x)+\frac{\lambda_{n}(c,s)}{n}f^{(s+1)}(x)\left(1+s(1+cx)\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{f^{(s+2)}(x)}{2n^{2}}\left(2+s(1+cx)(2+cx)+s^{2}(1+cx)^{2}+nx(2+cx)\right).

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}n\Bigg(\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}(c,s)}\frac{d^{s}}{dw^{s}}L_{n,c}(f,w)\Big|_{w=x}-f^{(s)}(x)\Bigg)=(cx+s+1)f^{(s+1)}(x)+x\left(1+\frac{cx}{2}\right)f^{(s+2)}(x).

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 2
.

For
s=0,
we obtain the following result

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}n\Bigg(\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}(c,0)}L_{n,c}(f,x)-f(x)\Bigg)=(cx+1)f^{\prime}(x)+x\left(1+\frac{cx}{2}\right)f^{\prime\prime}(x).

Further, if
c\uparrow 1,
then the operator
L_{n,c}(f,x)
reduces to the Baskakov-Szász type operator. And we get

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}n\left(L_{n,c}(f,x)-f^{\prime}(x)\right)=(x+1)f^{(s+1)}(x)+\frac{x(x+2)}{2}f^{\prime\prime}(x).

Next theorem provide an upper bound in simultaneous approximation in global setting.

Theorem 3
.

Let
[a_{1},b_{1}]\subset(a,b)\subset(0,\infty),
and
f^{(r)}
exists and is continuous on
[a,b]
then for sufficiently large
n,

\big\|L_{n,c}^{(r)}\left(f,\cdot\right)-f^{(r)}\big\|_{[a_{1},b_{1}]}\leqslant C\left(n^{-1}\|f\|+\omega_{2}\big(f^{(r)};n^{-1/2};[a_{1},b_{1}]\big)\right),

where
C
is independent of
f
and
n.

Proof.

We have that

\displaystyle\big\|L_{n,c}^{(r)}\left(f,\right)-f^{(r)}\big\|_{[a_{1},b_{1}]}

\displaystyle\leqslant\big\|L_{n,c}^{(r)}\left(f-f_{h,2},\right)\big\|_{[a_{1},b_{1}]}+\big\|L_{n,c}^{(r)}\left(f_{h,2},\right)-f_{h,2}^{(r)}\big\|_{[a_{1},b_{1}]}

\displaystyle+\big\|f^{(r)}-f^{(r)}_{h,2}\big\|_{[a_{1},b_{1}]}=A_{1}+A_{2}+A_{3},\ \textrm{say.}

Since,
\left(f_{h,2}\right)^{(r)}=\left(f^{(r)}\right)_{h,2},
by property of the Steklov mean it follows that

A_{3}\leqslant C\,\omega_{2}\left(f^{(r)},h,[a_{1},b_{1}]\right).

Next, applying Theorem
2
and the Riesz-Thorin interpolation theorem, we have that

\displaystyle A_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\,n^{-1}\sum_{m=r}^{r+2}\Big\|f^{(m)}_{h,2}\Big\|_{[a_{1},b_{1}]}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\,n^{-1}\left(\Big\|f_{h,2}\Big\|_{[a_{1},b_{1}]}+\Big\|f^{(r+2)}_{h,2}\Big\|_{[a_{1},b_{1}]}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant C\,n^{-1}\left(\Big\|f_{h,2}\Big\|_{[a_{1},b_{1}]}+\Big\|\left(f^{(r)}\right)^{\prime\prime}_{h,2}\Big\|_{[a_{1},b_{1}]}\right).

Hence, by properties of Steklov mean we have

A_{2}\leqslant C\,n^{-1}\left(\|f\|+h^{-2}\omega_{2}\big(f^{(r)},h,[a_{1},b_{1}]\big)\right).

In order to find estimation for
A_{3},
let us write
f-f_{h,2}=\Psi.
By our hypothesis, we can write

\displaystyle\Psi(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{r}\frac{\Psi^{(m)}(x)}{m!}(t-x)^{m}+\frac{\Psi^{(r)}(\xi)-\Psi^{(r)}(x)}{r!}(t-x)^{r}\chi(t)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle h(t,x)\left(1-\chi(t)\right),

where
\xi
lies between
t
and
x
, and
\chi
is the
characteristic function of the interval
(a,b).
For

t\in(a,b)
and
x\in[a_{1},b_{1}],
we have

\displaystyle\Psi(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{r}\frac{\Psi^{(m)}(x)}{m!}(t-x)^{m}+\frac{\Psi^{(r)}(\xi)-\Psi^{(r)}(x)}{r!}(t-x)^{r},

If
t\in[0,\infty)\setminus(a,b),x\in[a_{1},b_{1}]
then,
we define

\displaystyle h(t,x)=\Psi(t)-\sum_{m=0}^{r}\frac{\Psi^{(m)}(x)}{m!}(t-x)^{m}.

Now,

\displaystyle\frac{d^{r}}{dw^{r}}L_{n,c}\big(\Psi(t),w\big)\Big|_{w=x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{r}\frac{\Psi^{(m)}(x)}{m!}\frac{d^{r}}{dw^{r}}L_{n,c}\big((t-x)^{m},w\big)\Big|_{w=x}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{d^{r}}{dw^{r}}\frac{L_{n,c}\big(\big(\Psi^{(r)}(\xi)-\Psi^{(r)}(x)\big)(t-x)^{r}\chi(t),w\big)\Big|_{w=x}}{r!}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{d^{r}}{dw^{r}}L_{n,c}\big(h(t,x)\left(1-\chi(t)\right),w\big)\Big|_{w=x}:=B_{1}+B_{2}+B_{3},\ \ \textrm{say.}

By using the identity (
9
), we write that

\displaystyle B_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{r}\frac{\Psi^{(m)}(x)}{m!}\frac{d^{r}}{dw^{r}}L_{n,c}\big((t-x)^{m},x\big)\Big|_{w=x}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{r}\frac{\Psi^{(m)}(x)}{m!}\sum_{l=0}^{m}{m\choose l}(-x)^{m-l}\frac{d^{r}}{dw^{r}}L_{n,c}\big(t^{l},x\big)\Big|_{w=x}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{n}(c,r)\Psi^{(r)}(x).

Hence,

|B_{1}|\leqslant C\,\lambda_{n}(c,r)\|f^{(r)}-f^{(r)}_{h,2}\|_{[a_{1},b_{1}]}.

Next, using Lemma
5
and Schwarz’s inequality for integration and then for summation we get

\displaystyle|B_{2}|

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{2}{r!}\|f^{(r)}-f^{(r)}_{h,2}\|_{[a_{1},b_{1}]}\frac{d^{r}}{dw^{r}}L_{n,c}\big(\chi(t)|t-x|^{r},w\big)\Big|_{w=x}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{2}{r!}\|f^{(r)}-f^{(r)}_{h,2}\|_{[a_{1},b_{1}]}\sum_{2i+j\leqslant r\atop i,j\geqslant 0}n^{i}\frac{|q_{i,j,r}(x)|}{x^{r}(1+x)^{r}}n\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{n,k}(x)|k-nx|^{j}\times

\displaystyle\times\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\theta_{n,k}(t)\chi(t)|t-x|^{r}\,dt

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{2}{r!}\|f^{(r)}-f^{(r)}_{h,2}\|_{[a_{1},b_{1}]}\sum_{2i+j\leqslant r\atop i,j\geqslant 0}n^{i+1}\frac{|q_{i,j,r}(x)|}{x^{r}(1+x)^{r}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{n,k}(x)|k-nx|^{j}\times

\displaystyle\times\Bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\theta_{n,k}(t)\,dt\Bigg)^{\!\!1/2}\Bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\theta_{n,k}(t)(t-x)^{2r}\,dt\Bigg)^{\!\!1/2}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\,\|f^{(r)}-f^{(r)}_{h,2}\|_{[a_{1},b_{1}]}\sum_{2i+j\leqslant r\atop i,j\geqslant 0}n^{i}\Bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{n,k}(x)(k-nx)^{2j}\Bigg)^{\!\!1/2}\times

\displaystyle\times\Bigg(n\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{n,k}(x)\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\theta_{n,k}(t)(t-x)^{2r}\Bigg)^{\!\!1/2}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\,\|f^{(r)}-f^{(r)}_{h,2}\|_{[a_{1},b_{1}]}\sum_{2i+j\leqslant r\atop i,j\geqslant 0}n^{i}O\big(n^{j/2}\big)O\big(n^{-r/2}\big)

\displaystyle\leqslant C\,\|f^{(r)}-f^{(r)}_{h,2}\|_{[a_{1},b_{1}]}.

Since
t\in(0,\infty)\setminus(a,b),
we can choose a
\delta>0
in
such a way that
|t-x|\geqslant\delta
for all
x\in[a_{1},b_{1}].

Thus, by Lemma
5
, we obtain

\displaystyle|B_{3}|

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{2i+j\leqslant r\atop i,j\geqslant 0}n^{i}\frac{|q_{i,j,r}(x)|}{x^{r}(1+x)^{r}}n\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{n,k}(x)|k-nx|^{j}\int\limits_{|t-x|\geqslant\delta}\theta_{n,k}(t)|h(t,x)|\,dt

For
|t-x|\geqslant\delta,
we can find a constant
C>0
such that
|h(t,x)|\leqslant C|t-x|^{s}.
Finally using Schwarz’s inequality for integration and then for integration, and Lemma
6
, it easily follows that
B_{3}=O\big(n^{-s}\big)
for any
s>0.

Combining the estimates
B_{1}-B_{3},
we obtain in view of (c) of Steklov mean,

\displaystyle A_{3}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C\,\|f^{(r)}-f^{(r)}_{h,2k+2}\|_{[a_{1},b_{1}]}+O\big(n^{-s}\big)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C\,\omega_{2k+2}\big(f^{(r)},h,[a_{1},b_{1}]\big)+O\big(n^{-s}\big).

Taking
h=n^{-1/2}
the proof is completed.∎

Our last result proves local rate of approximation.

Theorem 4
.

Let
f^{(r)}\in C[0,\infty).
Then

\left|\frac{d^{r}}{dw^{r}}L_{n,c}(f;w)\Big|_{w=x}-f^{(r)}(x)\right|\leq 2\,\omega\left(f^{(r)},\frac{q_{n}(x,r)}{n}\right),

where
q_{n}(x,r)=\left(nx(cx+2)+r(x(cx+4)+3)+r^{2}(x+1)^{2}+2\right)^{1/2}.

Proof.

We have

\frac{d^{r}}{dw^{r}}L_{n,c}(f;w)\Big|_{w=x}=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}P_{n+r,k}(x)\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\theta_{n,k+r}(t)f^{(r)}(t)\,dt

Since
\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}P_{n+r,k}(x)=1
and
\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\theta_{n,k+r}(t)dt=1.

Therefore

\displaystyle\left|\frac{d^{r}}{dw^{r}}L_{n,c}(f;w)\Big|_{w=x}-f^{(r)}(x)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}P_{n+r,k}(x)\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\theta_{n,k+r}(t)\left(f^{(r)}(t)dt-f^{(r)}(x)\right)\right|

\displaystyle=L_{n,c,r}(|f^{(r)}(t)-f^{(r)}(x)|;x).

In view of the inequality

|f^{(r)}(t)-f^{(r)}(x)|\leq\left(1+\frac{|t-x|}{\delta}\right)\omega\left(f^{(r)};\delta\right),

we have

\left|\frac{d^{r}}{dw^{r}}L_{n,c}(f;w)\Big|_{w=x}-f^{(r)}(x)\right|\leq\omega\left(f^{(r)};\delta\right)\left(L_{n,c,r}(1;x)+\frac{L_{n,c,r}(|t-x|;x)}{\delta}\right).

Using Schwarz’s inequality, we obtain

L_{n,c,r}(|t-x|;x)\leq(L_{n,c,r}((t-x)^{2});x)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

∎

By direct calculations, we have

L_{n,c,r}(t-x)^{2});x)=\frac{nx(cx+2)+r(x(cx+4)+3)+r^{2}(x+1)^{2}+2}{n^{2}}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\left|\frac{d^{r}}{dw^{r}}L_{n,c}(f;w)\Big|_{w=x}-f^{(r)}(x)\right|\leq\omega\left(f^{(r)};\delta\right)\left(1+\frac{(L_{n,c,r}((t-x)^{2});x)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\delta}\right).

The theorem now follows by
choosing

\delta=\left(\frac{nx(cx+2)+r(x(cx+4)+3)+r^{2}(x+1)^{2}+2}{n^{2}}\right)^{1/2}.
