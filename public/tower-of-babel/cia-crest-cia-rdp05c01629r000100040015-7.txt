SUBJECT : Attached Letter from the ABA

STAT

Harry:

1. As requested, attached are some comments on the letter from Fred
Hitz of the ABA to Stan Sporkin.

2. i

Its a first observation, I find it interesting that

memorandum transmitting the ABA letter for comment takes it for

granted that a response will be supplied. I think one might argue both
sides of this question:

a. On the positive:

states that the ABA has been

a strong supporter of U.S. intelligence efforts, and by providing an
informed response on the subject of intelligence oversight and
accountability, we can favorably influence any recommendations the
committee makes or the positions it takes. If views of the ABA have
in the past actually helped the Intelligence Community, or CIA
specifically, then obviously there is merit in cooperating. I would,

of course, be leery of any attempt to “favorably influence" the ABA

position because the attempt to do so could boomerang if it became

public. At the same time, I am no judge as to whether this kind of

effort to obtain positive support from the ABA is proper.

b. On the negative side, one could argue that to respond to the
ABA questionnaire could create a dangerous precedent— if we have not
already responded to this type of questionnaire. Would we also
respond, for example, to questionnaires submitted by the American
Psychiatric Association, the American Medical Association, the
American Management Association, or any of the other professional
associations in engineering, accounting, etc.? We might find it
difficult to avoid cooperating with those other associations if in
fact we cooperate with the ABA.

3. I find puzzling that a response to the ABA will represent Stan's
"personal" views. I find it difficult to understand how the General
Counsel of the CIA could respond to the ABA on a variety of important gut
issues on CIA and the Intelligence Community without such a response
being treated as an official CIA position. The let ter from the AB A was
addressed to Stan as the Agency's General Counsel. has STAT

asked all of the deputies and the Director, IC Staff for views and
suggestions to incorporate in a response. All of these would be
official, yet the response under Stan's signature would be his views
only. Hardly believable.

4. On a more general note, the subject matters identified in the
questionnaire are pretty key matters for us to be discussing openly.

They deal with Congressional oversight, the relations between the DCI and
the Administration, covert action at a time when the Agency's role has
been challenged, domestic operations, and possible institutional changes
which could involve reorganization of oversight and levels of
responsibility within the executive branch. Certainly, unclassified
comments can be prepared on all of these matters, but any such response
would have to be carefully worded to avoid being misunderstood or
otherwise criticized. If a response is to be submitted, we might
consider taking the ABA up on their offer of anonymity.

5. I really am in no position to judge or advise on the substance of
the response. I hope, though, that the few comments made herein will
allow you to think a little more clearly about your input to the
memorandum. I
