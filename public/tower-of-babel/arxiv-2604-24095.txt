Improving Reachability in Vector Addition Systems through Pumpability
Weijun Chen, Yuxi Fu, Yangluo Zheng

Abstract
Vector addition systems (VAS) constitute an important model of computation and concurrency that is equally expressive as the Petri net model. Recently, a lot of research has been conducted on vector addition systems with states (VASS), which are VASes equipped with a finite state control. Results on VASS naturally carry over to VAS, but no straightforward improvement is available. In this paper, we investigate the reachability problem in VAS in fixed dimensions. Based on a pumpability analysis of VAS that refines Rackoff’s extraction for VASS, we obtain an F d − 2 \textsf{F}_{d-2} upper bound for the d d -dimensional VAS reachability problem, improving the F d \textsf{F}_{d} upper bound inherited from the d d -dimensional VASS reachability problem. Low-dimensional VASes are also considered. In particular, we establish a PSPACE upper bound for reachability in 4-dimensional VAS and an ELEMENTARY upper bound for 5-dimensional VAS, while the same upper bounds were known only for 2-VASS and 3-VASS, respectively. The result for 4-VAS particularly hinges on a simplified projection technique developed for geometrically 2-dimensional VASSes, whose reachability problem is shown to be equivalent to 2-VASS.

Introduction

Vector addition systems (VAS), being equivalent to Petri nets, are a well-established model of concurrency. A VAS is simply defined by a finite set of integer vectors called actions. Configurations, which are non-negative integer vectors, may move along these actions. The reachability problem, asking whether there is a run from one configuration to another, is the central algorithmic problem in the study of VAS and Petri nets that has found various applications
[
22
]
. Decidability of the reachability problem in VAS has been known for over 40 years
[
15
,
9
,
11
]
. Its complexity was settled to be Ackermann-complete only recently
[
12
,
14
,
5
]
. Zooming in to the
d
-dimensional VAS reachability problem with
d
fixed, a significant gap still remains between the complexity lower bound
\textsf{F}_{O(d/2)}

[
3
]
and upper bound
\textsf{F}_{d}

[
12
]
, where
\textsf{F}_{d}
is the
d
-th level of the fast-growing complexity hierarchy
[
18
]
.

In the literature, the enhanced model “vector addition system with states (VASS)” turns out to be more popular. VASS augments VAS with a finite state control, so that each state has its own set of actions. Indeed, the Ackermann-completeness result mentioned above was proved directly for VASS. Considering the expressive power, Hopcroft and Pansiot
[
8
]
showed that the finite state control can be simulated by a VAS with three additional dimensions. In general, this implies problems in VASS are inter-reducible with those in VAS if the dimension is not a fixed parameter. In fixed dimensions, one might naturally expect better complexity bounds for VAS than for VASS. However, such results are not known for most problems. For the
d
-dimensional VAS reachability problem, the best upper bound we have is
\textsf{F}_{d}
inherited from
d
-dimensional VASS
[
6
]
. Gaps between VAS and VASS are clearer in low dimensions. For example, it was shown in
[
8
]
that reachability relations in both 2-dimensional VASS and 5-dimensional VAS are effectively semilinear. Inspired by this semilinearity,
PSPACE
-completeness is established for reachability in 2-VASS
[
1
]
. However, for 5-VAS, currently we do not even have an elementary upper bound. This paper is devoted to narrowing the gap between VAS and VASS.

Contribution.

In this paper, we mainly focus on the reachability problem in fixed-dimensional VAS. Our first result shows that in the same fixed dimension
d
, the complexity of reachability in VAS could be lower than that in VASS.

Theorem 1
.

Reachability in
(d+2)
-VAS is in
\mathsf{F}_{d}
for every
d\geq 3
.

This result is based on a sharper analysis of pumpability in VAS. A
d
-dimensional configuration
\bm{s}
is said to be pumpable if
\bm{s}
can reach some
\bm{s}^{\prime}\geq\bm{s}+\bm{1}
. Intuitively, when both the source and the target configurations are pumpable, one can bound the length of the shortest runs between them by a polynomial. On the other hand, when pumpability fails, a known technique called Rackoff’s extraction
[
17
]
exhibits one bounded coordinate on any run. The dimension can then be reduced by one. In
section 3
, we improve this technique and show that for a specific type of VAS, the failure of pumpability yields two bounded coordinates. This allows us to reduce the
(d+2)
-VAS reachability problem to “almost”
d
-VASS reachability: the reduced VASS might have dimension more than
d
, but the
geometric dimension
of each of its strongly connected components is at most
d
. Geometric dimension, defined as the dimension of the vector space spanned by effects of simple cycles, is a metric on VASS gaining importance in recent research
[
6
,
4
,
21
,
7
]
. A closer inspection of
[
6
]
shows that the
\textsf{F}_{d}
upper bound for
d
-VASS indeed holds for VASSes produced by our reduction where each strongly connected component has geometric dimension at most
d
.

Our second result focuses on low-dimensional VAS, especially 4-VAS and 5-VAS.

Theorem 2
.

Reachability in 5-VAS is in
ELEMENTARY
. Reachability in 4-VAS is in
PSPACE
under binary encoding and is
NL
-complete under unary encoding.

To the best of our knowledge, this provides the first elementary upper bound for VAS of dimension greater than
3
(we remark that a
PSPACE
upper bound for
3
-VAS could be derived from
[
4
, Lemma 2]
). For low-dimensional VAS, we need a fine-grained characterization of shortest runs. We show that the shortest runs in
d
-dimensional VAS are captured either by
(d-2)
-dimensional VASS, or by a VASS consisting of two sub-VASSes whose geometric dimensions are at most
d-3
. For 4- and 5-VAS, this allows us to use as building blocks the existing results on 2-VASS
[
1
]
, 3-VASS
[
4
]
, and geometrically 2-dimensional VASS
[
6
,
21
]
. We remark that a projection technique converting geometrically 2-dimensional VASS to 2-VASS is also provided in this paper, simplifying that of
[
21
]
.
The technique produces tight complexity bounds for reachability in geometrically 2-dimensional VASS under unary encoding, which is not answered in
[
21
]
. To be specific, we show that this problem is
NL
-complete when the actual dimension is fixed, and is
NP
-complete otherwise.

Organization.

In
section 2
, we fix notation and introduce important concepts in VAS. Section
3
exhibits our main technical contribution—a weaker pumpability condition based on fine-tuning Rackoff’s extraction. In
section 4
we apply the pumpability condition to show
\textsf{F}_{d-2}
upper bound for
d
-dimensional VAS where
d>4
. In
section 5
we show a fine-grained analysis of VAS runs using similar techniques. This will be useful in
section 6
, where we derive better bounds for 4- and 5-VAS. Finally,
section 7
proposes possible future directions.

Preliminaries

Let
\mathbb{N},\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Q}
denote the sets of natural numbers, integers, and rational numbers, respectively.
We use, for example,
\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}
for the set of rationals restricted to non-negative values. Let
m\leq n
be two integers, we write
[m,n]
for the set
\{m,m+1,\ldots,n\}
. The interval
[1,n]
is abbreviated as
[n]
.
Vectors are written in boldface
\bm{u},\bm{v},\bm{x},\bm{y}
, etc. For a vector
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d}
, we write
\bm{x}(i)
for its
i
-th component, where
i\in[d]
. We compare vectors component-wise, so that
\bm{x}\leq\bm{y}
means
\bm{x}(i)\leq\bm{y}(i)
for all
i\in[d]
. We use the max norm for vectors, denoted by
\left\|{\bm{x}}\right\|:=\max_{i\in[d]}|\bm{x}(i)|
. For a finite set
X\subseteq\mathbb{Q}^{d}
we write
\left\|{X}\right\|:=\max_{\bm{x}\in X}\left\|{\bm{x}}\right\|
. The
inner product
of two vectors
\bm{x},\bm{y}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d}
is written as
\langle{\bm{x},\bm{y}}\rangle:=\sum_{i\in[d]}\bm{x}(i)\cdot\bm{y}(i)
. When the dimension
d
is clear from the context, we write
\bm{e}_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d}
for the unit vector with a
1
in its
i
-th coordinate. We write
\bm{1}:=(1,1,\ldots,1)
and
\bm{0}:=(0,0,\ldots,0)
. For a finite set
X\subseteq\mathbb{Q}^{d}
of vectors where
X=\{\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{n}\}
, the
cone
generated by
X
is
\mathop{\operatorname{\textup{cone}}}\nolimits(X):=\{\lambda_{1}\bm{x}_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{n}\bm{x}_{n}\mid\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}\}
.

VASS, VAS, and Sequential VAS

A
d
-dimensional
vector addition system with states
(
d
-VASS) is a pair
V=(Q,T)
where
Q
is a finite set of
states
and
T\subseteq Q\times\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times Q
is a finite set of
transitions
. By a
configuration
of
V
we mean a pair
(p,\bm{x})
, often denoted by
p(\bm{x})
, of a state
p\in Q
and a non-negative vector
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
. A transition
t=(p,\bm{a},q)
induces one-step runs
p(\bm{x})\xrightarrow{t}q(\bm{y})
where
\bm{x},\bm{y}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
satisfy
\bm{x}+\bm{a}=\bm{y}
. Paths are words over transitions
\pi=(p_{1},\bm{a}_{1},q_{1})\ldots(p_{k},\bm{a}_{k},q_{k})=:t_{1}\ldots t_{k}\in T^{*}
such that
q_{i}=p_{i+1}
for
i\in[k-1]
. If further
q_{k}=p_{1}
, we say
\pi
is a
cycle
in
V
. A cycle consisting of a single transition is called a
self-loop
. The effect of such a path
\pi
is
\Delta(\pi):=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\bm{a}_{k}
. The length of
\pi
is denoted by
|\pi|:=k
. Runs induced by paths are concatenations of the one-step runs induced by the transitions, e.g.,

\pi:p_{1}(\bm{x}_{1})\xrightarrow{t_{1}}q_{1}(\bm{y_{1}})\xrightarrow{t_{2}}\cdots\xrightarrow{t_{k}}q_{k}(\bm{y}_{k}).

(1)

We emphasize that
\bm{x}_{1},\bm{y}_{1},\ldots,\bm{y}_{k}\geq\bm{0}
must be non-negative.
We write
p(\bm{x})\xrightarrow{\pi}q(\bm{y})
if there is a run induced by
\pi
from
p(\bm{x})
to
q(\bm{y})
. We also write
p(\bm{x})\xrightarrow{*}q(\bm{y})
, quantifying the path existentially. Two paths
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
can be concatenated into
\pi_{1}\pi_{2}
if the target state of
\pi_{1}
matches the source state of
\pi_{2}
. If
\pi
is a cycle, we write
\pi^{m}
for the
m
-fold concatenation of
\pi
. Sometimes we also consider
\mathbb{Z}
-configurations
p(\bm{z})
, where the vector
\bm{z}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
can take negative values. We define
\mathbb{Z}
-runs similarly over
\mathbb{Z}
-configurations, denoted by
p(\bm{z})\xrightarrow{\pi}_{\mathbb{Z}}q(\bm{w})
.
Consider a run
\pi=t_{1}\ldots t_{k}
from state
p
to
q
.
We define the
drop
of
\pi
to be the vector
\mathop{\operatorname{\textup{{drop}}}}\nolimits(\pi)\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
where for each
i\in[d]
,
\mathop{\operatorname{\textup{{drop}}}}\nolimits(\pi)(i)
is the maximum decrease of the
i
-th component along this path, i.e.,

\mathop{\operatorname{\textup{{drop}}}}\nolimits(\pi)(i):=-\min(0,\min_{j\in[k]}\Delta(t_{1}\dots t_{j})(i)).

(2)

It is straightforward that for any
\bm{z}\geq\mathop{\operatorname{\textup{{drop}}}}\nolimits(\pi)
,
p(\bm{z})\xrightarrow{\pi}q(\bm{w})
holds for some
\bm{w}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
.

The (unary-encoded)
size
of
V
is defined by
\mathop{\operatorname{\textup{{size}}}}\nolimits(V):=|Q|+d\cdot|T|\cdot(\left\|{T}\right\|+1)
, where
\left\|{T}\right\|:=\max_{(p,\bm{a},q)\in T}\left\|{\bm{a}}\right\|
.
The
reverse
of
V
is given by
V^{\text{rev}}=(Q,T^{\text{rev}})
where
T^{\text{rev}}=\{(q,-\bm{a},p)\mid(p,\bm{a},q)\in T\}
.
The norm of a (
\mathbb{Z}
-)configuration
p(\bm{x})
is defined as
\left\|{p(\bm{x})}\right\|:=\|\bm{x}\|
.

Vector addition systems
(VAS) are the main focus of this paper. A
d
-VAS is a
d
-VASS
V=(Q,T)
where
Q
contains a single state only. Transitions in
T
are determined by its effect, and we can view
T
as a subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. The unique state is often omitted. So a configuration is regarded as a vector in
\mathbb{N}^{d}
.

In the analysis of VAS runs, we may identify some transitions that can be fired only a bounded number of times. In order to distinguish these transitions from others, we introduce the model of
sequential VAS
. Let
V
be a
d
-VAS whose transition set is
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, and let
\bm{a}_{1},\ldots,\bm{a}_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be vectors. The sequential VAS
S=V[\bm{a}_{1},\ldots,\bm{a}_{k}]
is defined to be the VASS
(Q,T)
where
Q:=\{q_{0},q_{1},\ldots,q_{k}\}
and

T:=\{(q_{j},\bm{a},q_{j})\mid j\in[0,k],\bm{a}\in A\}\cup\{(q_{j-1},\bm{a}_{j},q_{j})\mid j\in[k]\}.

(3)

We call
q_{0}
the
source state
and
q_{k}
the
target state
in
S
. Vectors
\bm{a}_{1},\ldots,\bm{a}_{k}
are called the
bridges
in
S
.
By a source/target configuration, we mean a configuration whose state is the source/target state in
S
.
Intuitively,
V[\bm{a}_{1},\ldots,\bm{a}_{k}]
can be viewed as the VAS
V
enhanced with
k
special transitions which can be fired exactly once in the order they are specified.

Geometric Dimension and Reachability Problems

Let
V
be a
d
-VASS. Its
cycle space

\mathop{\operatorname{\textup{{cyc}}}}\nolimits(V)\subseteq\mathbb{Q}^{d}
is the vector space spanned by the effects of simple cycles in
V
, i.e.,
\mathop{\operatorname{\textup{{cyc}}}}\nolimits(V):=\mathop{\operatorname{\textup{span}}}\nolimits\{\Delta(\theta)\mid\theta\text{ is a simple cycle in }V\}
. Geometric dimension was introduced recently as an important parameter of VASSes. Here, we consider two types of geometric dimension:
The
cycle-dimension

\dim_{\textup{cyc}}(V):=\dim(\mathop{\operatorname{\textup{{cyc}}}}\nolimits(V))

is the dimension of the whole cycle space. This was known as
geometric dimension
in
[
6
]
and
[
21
]
.
The
component-dimension

\dim_{\textup{com}}(V):=\max_{\text{SCC }V^{\prime}\text{ in }V}\dim(\mathop{\operatorname{\textup{{cyc}}}}\nolimits(V^{\prime}))

is the maximum value among the dimensions of the cycle spaces of strongly connected components in
V
. This coincides with the dimension introduced in
[
7
]
.
By convention, we say that a VASS
V
is geometrically
d
-dimensional if
\dim_{\textup{cyc}}(V)\leq d
. Notice that for a VAS
V
, both
\dim_{\textup{cyc}}(V)
and
\dim_{\textup{com}}(V)
are equal to the dimension of the vector space spanned by the effects of transitions of
V
, as every transition is a self-loop. We refer the readers to
[
2
]
for further research about these two parameters.

The reachability problem in VASSes can be formulated as follows:

Input:

A VASS
V=(Q,T)
with configurations
p(\bm{x}),q(\bm{y})
.

Question:

Does it hold
p(\bm{x})\xrightarrow{*}q(\bm{y})
?

Complexity bounds for reachability problems are usually obtained from length bounds on runs witnessing the reachability. Let
V
be a VASS and
s,t
be two configurations of
V
. We also call the triple
(V,s,t)
a VASS, with
s
and
t
understood as the source and target configurations. We write
\mathop{\operatorname{\textup{{len}}}}\nolimits(V,s,t)
for the set of lengths of runs from
s
to
t
, and
\mathop{\operatorname{\textup{{size}}}}\nolimits(V,s,t)
for
\mathop{\operatorname{\textup{{size}}}}\nolimits(V)+d\cdot(\left\|{s}\right\|+\left\|{t}\right\|+1)
. The following bound can be obtained from the famous KLMST decomposition algorithm.

Theorem 3

(
[
6
, Lemma 6.2]
)
.

Let
(V,s,t)
be a VASS of component-dimension
m\geq 3
. Then
\min\mathop{\operatorname{\textup{{len}}}}\nolimits(V,s,t)\leq F_{m}(g(\mathop{\operatorname{\textup{{size}}}}\nolimits(V,s,t)))
if
\mathop{\operatorname{\textup{{len}}}}\nolimits(V,s,t)\neq\emptyset
, where
g
is some elementary function independent of
m
, and
(V,s,t)
.

The function
F_{m}
is the
m
-th fast-growing function, which will be introduced below. We remark that the statement of this theorem in
[
6
]
is for fixed dimension
m
rather than fixed component-dimension
m
. The version we use here can be easily obtained from the ranking function defined in
[
6
]
.

Fast-Growing Complexity Hierarchy

The fast-growing functions
(F_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
are defined as follows:

F_{0}(x):=x+1,\quad F_{k+1}(x):=F_{k}^{x+1}(x)
,
where
F_{k}^{x+1}
means the
(x+1)
-fold iteration of
F_{k}
. For example,
F_{2}(x)=2^{x+1}(x+1)-x
is exponential in
x
, and
F_{3}(x)
grows even faster than a tower of exponents. We say a function is
elementary
if it is bounded by
F_{2}^{c}
for some constant
c
. Based on these functions we may define a hierarchy
(\mathscr{F}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of computable functions:
\mathscr{F}_{k}:=\bigcup_{c\in\mathbb{N}}\textsf{FDTIME}(F_{k}^{c}(n))
.
The fast-growing complexity hierarchy is defined as follows:

\textsf{F}_{k}:=\bigcup_{p\in\mathscr{F}_{k-1}}\textsf{DTIME}(F_{k}(p(n))).

For more details of this complexity hierarchy—which we will not use for this paper—we refer the readers to
[
18
]
.

Wideness, Pumpability and Fixed Coordinates

We have introduced the cycle space of a VASS, which is the vector space spanned by the effects of simple cycles. However, it is the
cone
generated by the effects of simple cycles that better describes the behavior of the VASS. Let
V
be a VASS and
E=\{\bm{e}_{1},\ldots,\bm{e}_{k}\}
be the set of effects of simple cycles in
V
. We define the cone of
V
as
\mathop{\operatorname{\textup{cone}}}\nolimits(V):=\mathop{\operatorname{\textup{cone}}}\nolimits(E)
.
And
V
is said to be
wide
if
\mathop{\operatorname{\textup{cone}}}\nolimits(V)=\mathop{\operatorname{\textup{{cyc}}}}\nolimits(V)
, so any combination of cycle effects can be expressed using non-negative coefficients. We remark that the notion of wideness was proposed in
[
4
]
in a slightly different manner.

Pumpability is another important notion that we will investigate. Let
p(\bm{x})
be a configuration in
V
. We say
p(\bm{x})
is
forward pumpable
(or simply
pumpable
) in
V
if there is a run
p(\bm{x})\xrightarrow{*}p(\bm{x}^{\prime})
with
\bm{x}^{\prime}\geq\bm{x}+\bm{1}
. We say
p(\bm{x})
is
backward pumpable
in
V
if it is forward pumpable in
V^{\text{rev}}
, or equivalently, if there is a run
p(\bm{x}^{\prime})\xrightarrow{*}p(\bm{x})
in
V
with
\bm{x}^{\prime}\geq\bm{x}+\bm{1}
.

Let
S=V[\bm{a}_{1},\dots,\bm{a}_{k}]
be a sequential
d
-VAS. We say that a coordinate
i\in[d]
is
fixed
in
S
if
\bm{u}(i)=0
holds for every transition
\bm{u}
in
V
. Fixed coordinates are annoying from time to time. We may use the following easy lemma to get rid of them.

Lemma 4
.

Let
(S,s,t)
be a sequential
d
-VAS. Then there exists a sequential
d^{\prime}
-VAS
(S^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime})
containing no fixed coordinates such that
d^{\prime}\leq d
,
\mathop{\operatorname{\textup{{size}}}}\nolimits(S^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime})\leq\mathop{\operatorname{\textup{{size}}}}\nolimits(S,s,t)
, and
\mathop{\operatorname{\textup{{len}}}}\nolimits(S,s,t)=\mathop{\operatorname{\textup{{len}}}}\nolimits(S^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime})
.

Unless otherwise specified, we may assume without loss of generality that the sequential
d
-VAS in the subsequent discussion contains no fixed coordinates, i.e., there exists at least one transition
\bm{u}_{i}
with
\bm{u}_{i}(i)\neq 0
in
V
for each
i\in[d]
.

3
Pumpability from
d-1
Unbounded Counters

Rackoff’s extraction

[
12
, Lemma A.1]
(see also
[
17
]
,
[
4
, Lemma 5]
, and
[
10
]
) is an important technique to extract pumpability or coverability from an “unbounded run”. In the scenario of
d
-VASS runs, this technique states, intuitively, that if there is a run where each of the
d
coordinates has ever been raised above some sufficiently large value, then one can construct a run from the same source whose target has large values in all
d
coordinates. A consequence of this fact is that, within one strongly connected component of VASS, at least one coordinate on a run from some unpumpable configuration must remain bounded. Dimension reduction can then be conducted for reachability instances starting from an unpumpable configuration.

In this section, we improve Rackoff’s extraction for wide sequential
d
-VASes. We show that coverability or pumpability is guaranteed if
all but one
coordinates can be raised above some large value in a run. This will give us at least two bounded coordinates in the unpumpable case. Let us now state the result formally. Let
\pi
be a run in some
d
-VASS
V
and
B\in\mathbb{N}
, and we introduce the notation
\operatorname{\textsc{ub}}(\pi,B)
for the set of indices
i\in[d]
such that
\pi
contains a configuration
q_{i}(\bm{x}_{i})
with
\bm{x}_{i}(i)\geq B
. That is,
\operatorname{\textsc{ub}}(\pi,B)
are those coordinates that ever reached a value above
B
along
\pi
. Our improved Rackoff’s extraction is stated as follows.

Theorem 5
.

There is a polynomial
P
such that, given a wide sequential
d
-VAS
S=V[\bm{a}_{1},\ldots,\bm{a}_{k}]
with source configuration
q_{0}(\bm{x}_{0})
, if there is a run
\pi
from
q_{0}(\bm{x}_{0})
such that
|\operatorname{\textsc{ub}}(\pi,U)|\geq d-1
where
U:=P(\left\|{\bm{x}_{0}}\right\|,\mathop{\operatorname{\textup{{size}}}}\nolimits(S))^{(d+1)!}
, then there is a run
\rho
with
|\rho|\leq U
such that
q_{0}(\bm{x}_{0})\xrightarrow{\rho}q(\bm{x})
for some configuration
q(\bm{x})
pumpable in
S
. Moreover,
\rho
can be obtained from
\pi
by removing some self-loops.

We remark that the “moreover” part of this theorem guarantees that the target of
\pi
is reachable from the pumpable configuration
q(\bm{x})
by a
\mathbb{Z}
-run consisting of transitions in
V

First, we show that the pumpability of

Lemma 6
.

Let

(4)

Proof.

Let the states of

\displaystyle q_{1}(\bm{y}_{1})\xrightarrow{\bm{a}_{2}}\cdots\xrightarrow{\bm{a}_{\ell}}q_{\ell}(\bm{x}_{\ell})\xrightarrow{\pi_{\ell}}

\displaystyle q_{\ell}(\bm{y}_{\ell})=q(\bm{x})

.

For every

, there is a polynomial

(d+2)
-VAS

Reachability in

-VAS is in

The pattern of statement in

Let

•

A VASS

of VASSes
(V^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime})

Hence,

Theorem 11

4.1
Non-Wide VAS to Wide Sequential VAS

We will actually prove
Theorem 8
for wide sequential VASes. Here, we show how to convert a non-wide VAS to a family of wide sequential VASes.

Lemma 12
.

For every
d\in\mathbb{N}
, there is a polynomial
W_{d}
such that, every
d
-VAS
(V,\bm{s},\bm{t})
is length-captured by a family
\mathcal{S}
of wide sequential
d
-VASes
(S,s^{\prime},t^{\prime})
with
W_{d}
-amplification. Moreover, if
V
is non-wide, then
\dim_{\textup{cyc}}(S)\leq\dim_{\textup{cyc}}(V)-1
.

The proof idea is similar to
[
4
, Sect. 4, Case 2]
. If
V
is non-wide, then there exists a vector
\bm{n}
such that every transition belongs to the half-space determined by
\bm{n}
. In particular, the inner product of
\bm{n}
with the configuration cannot be decreased by any transition. Hence, every transition whose effect has a non-zero inner product with
\bm{n}
can only be fired for a bounded number of times determined by
\langle{\bm{t}-\bm{s},\bm{n}}\rangle
. We then enumerate every combination of these transitions and expand
V
as a sequential VAS correspondingly. What is crucial here is that we need to find a good
\bm{n}
such that the VAS with transitions orthogonal to
\bm{n}
is wide. For this purpose, we introduce the following lemma.

Lemma 13
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be a finite set of vectors such that
\mathop{\operatorname{\textup{cone}}}\nolimits(X)\neq\mathop{\operatorname{\textup{span}}}\nolimits(X)
. There exists a non-zero vector
\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
such that

1.

\langle{\bm{n},\bm{x}}\rangle\geq 0
for all
\bm{x}\in X
;

2.

let
X_{0}:=\{\bm{x}\in X\mid\langle{\bm{n},\bm{x}}\rangle=0\}
; then
\mathop{\operatorname{\textup{cone}}}\nolimits(X_{0})=\mathop{\operatorname{\textup{span}}}\nolimits(X_{0})
;

3.

\left\|{\bm{n}}\right\|\leq(d+1)\cdot(r\left\|{X}\right\|)^{r}
, where
r:=\dim(\mathop{\operatorname{\textup{span}}}\nolimits(X))
.

With the help of
13
, we are able to identify the set of bounded transitions. A VAS can then be turned into a wide sequential VAS by making these transitions as bridges.

Proof of
12
.

Let
V
be a
d
-VAS with transitions

.
If

is wide, then we are already done. Otherwise,

with
\left\|{\bm{n}}\right\|\leq D:=(d+1)\cdot(d\left\|{T}\right\|)^{d}

\displaystyle T_{1}:=\{\bm{t}\in T\mid\langle{\bm{n},\bm{t}}\rangle>0\},\quad T_{0}:=\{\bm{t}\in T\mid\langle{\bm{n},\bm{t}}\rangle=0\}.

(9)

Any transition in

increases the inner product with

while no transition can decrease this inner product. Fix configurations
\bm{s},\bm{t}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
. Consider a run

. The number of occurences of transitions from

on

is bounded by

\displaystyle K:=\langle{\bm{n},\bm{t}}\rangle-\langle{\bm{n},\bm{s}}\rangle\leq 2D\cdot\mathop{\operatorname{\textup{{size}}}}\nolimits(V,\bm{s},\bm{t}).

We take

to be the family of sequential

-VAS

is the VAS consists of transitions in

captures every run from
\bm{s}
to
\bm{t}
. Every run in some

also induces a run in

contains only transitions from

. Moreover,

.
Notice that
\mathop{\operatorname{\textup{{cyc}}}}\nolimits(S)=\mathop{\operatorname{\textup{span}}}\nolimits(T_{0})\subsetneq\mathop{\operatorname{\textup{span}}}\nolimits(T)=\mathop{\operatorname{\textup{{cyc}}}}\nolimits(V)

Considering the size amplification, we have

We remark that after the decomposition presented in the proof of

-VASes, possibly with several fixed coordinates. However, by applying

4.2
Boundedness of Runs

Now we focus on runs in wide sequential VAS. We shall identify two cases depending on whether
Theorem 5
can be applied to extract forward and backward pumpable configurations.

Let
\pi
be a run in some
d
-VASS
V
and
B\in\mathbb{N}
. Recall that
\operatorname{\textsc{ub}}(\pi,B)
is the set of indices
i\in[d]
such that
\pi
contains a configuration
q_{i}(\bm{x}_{i})
with
\bm{x}_{i}(i)\geq B
. We say
\pi
is
k
-bounded
by
B
if
|\operatorname{\textsc{ub}}(\pi_{1},B)|\leq d-k
. In the literature of VASS,

-boundedness is often used to perform dimension reduction. For wide sequential VAS,

allows us to shift the focus to
2
-boundedness. For technical reasons, we shall make a slight generalization here. We say that
\pi
is
almost (

if there exists a partition

such that both
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
are

B
. On the other hand,
\pi
is
almost unbounded by

if there exists a partition
\pi=\pi_{1}\pi_{2}
such that
|\operatorname{\textsc{ub}}(\pi_{1},B)|,|\operatorname{\textsc{ub}}(\pi_{2},B)|\geq d-1
.
Intuitively, almost bounded runs allow us to perform dimension reduction, while almost unbounded runs permit us to apply
Theorem 5
to extract forward and backward pumpable configurations.
Although these two conditions do not appear to be complementary, a dichotomy between them still persists.

Lemma 14
.

Suppose that
(V,s,t)
is a
d
-VASS with
s\xrightarrow{\pi}t
in
V
and
B\in\mathbb{N}
. Then either
\pi
is almost unbounded by
B
, or
\pi
is almost bounded by

In the following, we handle almost unbounded runs and almost bounded runs in wide sequential VAS separately.

4.2.1
Almost Unbounded Runs

In this part, we address almost unbounded runs in wide sequential VASes. We show that in this case, reachability is relatively easy to verify in the sense that a polynomial upper bound can be established on the length of shortest witnesses for such runs.

Lemma 15
.

For every
d\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a polynomial

such that, given a wide sequential

-VAS
(S,s,t)
with

in

The proof idea is summarized as follows. If a run

is almost unbounded by a sufficiently large value, then

can be decomposed into two segments

such that, in both segments, at least

\mathbb{Z}

The proof consists of two steps: (i) replacing an arbitrary

Lemma 16
.

Let

We remark that the wideness is essential in the proof of

For each

, there is a polynomial

and
(d+2)
-VASS

The proofs of both lemmas are quite standard. We defer them to the appendix for space limitations.
So far, we have analyzed both cases of (almost) boundedness. We now return to the proof of

.

Let

Let

Notes on Geometrically
d
-Dimensional VASes

So far, we have established
Theorem 8
. A natural extension is to ask what the corresponding result would be if we fix the geometric dimension
g
of the original VAS rather than its (standard) dimension
d
. In fact, by taking a closer look at the above proofs, we can easily derive the following conclusion.

Lemma 19
.

For every
g\in\mathbb{N}
, there is a polynomial
P_{g}
such that, every
d
-VAS
(V,\bm{s},\bm{t})
with
\dim_{\textup{cyc}}(V)=g+1
is length-bounded by a
d
-VASS
(V^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime})
with
f
-amplification, where
\dim_{\textup{com}}(V^{\prime})\leq g
and
f(x)=P_{g}(x)^{(d+1)!}
.

Proof Sketch.

The analysis is analogous to that in
Theorem 8
. For the size bound of the new VASS, the
(d+1)!
term in the exponent originates from Rackoff’s extraction (
7
). For the component-dimension of the new VASS, it suffices to show that the dimension reduction in
17
decreases the cycle-dimension of the sequential VAS
S
by at least one. Note that after encoding any
i\in[d]
into the state, every cycle
\theta
in the resulting VASS, denoted by

, satisfies
\Delta(\theta)(i)=0
. In contrast, the original sequential VAS

contains no fixed coordinates, i.e., there exists a self-loop
u
with

. Consequently, we have
\mathop{\operatorname{\textup{{cyc}}}}\nolimits(V^{\prime})\subsetneq\mathop{\operatorname{\textup{{cyc}}}}\nolimits(S)
, which implies that
\dim_{\textup{cyc}}(V^{\prime})\leq\dim_{\textup{cyc}}(S)-1\leq g
.
∎

The existence of a
2
-bounded run allows us to remove two coordinates of the VAS, which, however, might only decrease the geometric dimension by one. As an example, consider a
3
-VAS
(V,\bm{s},\bm{t})
for which
\bm{u}(1)+\bm{u}(2)=0
holds for every transition vector
\bm{u}
in
V
. It is straightforward that these two coordinates are always bounded by
\mathop{\operatorname{\textup{{size}}}}\nolimits(V,\bm{s},\bm{t})^{O(1)}
in every run in
V
. However, removing these two coordinates can only reduce the component-dimension by one, as they are collinear.

Finally, we can combine
Theorem 3
and

to derive the following corollary.

Corollary 20
.

Reachability in geometrically
(g+1)
-dimensional VAS is in
\mathsf{F}_{g}
for
g\geq 3
.

5
Fine-Grained Characterization of VAS Runs

Theorem 8
gives a nice characterization of shortest runs in
d
-dimensional VAS using VAS of component-dimension
d-2
. This suffices to prove the
\mathsf{F}_{d-2}
upper-bound for fixed dimension
d\geq 5
. However, some good properties of this characterization are hidden beneath the technical proofs. In this section, we give a finer characterization of runs in
d
-VAS, relating them to
(d-2)
-VASS or VASS of component-dimension
d-3
, which is formulated as follows.

Proposition 21
.

For each
d\in\mathbb{N}
where
d\geq 2
, there is a polynomial
P_{d}
such that
every
d
-VAS
(V,\bm{s},\bm{t})
is length-bounded by a VASS
(V^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime})
with
P_{d}
-amplification. Moreover,

•

either
V^{\prime}
is a
(d-2)
-VASS with
\dim_{\textup{cyc}}(V^{\prime})\leq\dim_{\textup{cyc}}(V)-1
,

•

or
V^{\prime}
is comprised of two
d
-VASSes
V_{1},V_{2}
connected by a single transition, where

\dim_{\textup{cyc}}(V_{1}),\dim_{\textup{cyc}}(V_{2})\leq\max\{0,\dim_{\textup{cyc}}(V)-3\}.

(15)

In particular, it holds that
\dim_{\textup{com}}(V^{\prime})\leq\max\{0,\dim_{\textup{cyc}}(V)-3\}
.

This proposition is particularly favorable for low-dimensional VAS compared to
Theorem 8
. For example,
Theorem 8
bounds the length of shortest runs in 5-VAS by VASS of component-dimension
3
, for which we know nothing better than
Theorem 3
. On the other hand,
21
allows us to rely on sharper results for 3-VASS
[
4
]
and VASS of component-dimension 2
[
6
]
.

Before proving
21
, we introduce the notion of
collinear coordinates
in VASes. Let
V=(Q,T)
be a
d
-VAS and
i,j\in[d]
. We say
i
and
j
are
collinear (in
V
)
if there exists
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}
such that for every transition
\bm{u}\in T
, we have
\bm{u}(i)=\alpha\cdot\bm{u}(j)
. Furthermore,
i
and
j
are
positively collinear
if
\alpha>0
and
negatively collinear
if
\alpha<0
. We remark that by
4
we may assume all coordinates are not fixed, so we cannot have
\alpha=0
in the case where
i
and
j
are collinear. For a sequential VAS
S=V[\bm{a}_{1},\ldots,\bm{a}_{k}]
, collinearity is defined with respect to
V
. We observe that negatively collinear coordinates are always bounded, and can be removed by encoding into states. Formally, we have the following lemma, whose proof is provided in the appendix.

Lemma 22
.

There is a polynomial
P
such that every sequential
d
-VAS
(S,s,t)
, which contains a pair of negatively collinear coordinates, is length-bounded by a
(d-2)
-VASS
(V^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime})
with
P
-amplification, where
\dim_{\textup{cyc}}(V^{\prime})\leq\dim_{\textup{cyc}}(S)-1
.

The core idea of proving
21
is to improve the pumping condition
6
. It states that in wide sequential VASes, pumpability always holds in a target configuration where at most one coordinate is bounded. This condition can be weakened to the point that all bounded coordinates are pairwise positively collinear.

Lemma 23
.

Let
B
be the polynomial given by
6
,
S=V[\bm{a}_{1},\ldots,\bm{a}_{k}]
be a wide sequential
d
-VAS (containing no fixed coordinate) with source configuration
q_{0}(\bm{x}_{0})
. If there is a run
q_{0}(\bm{x}_{0})\xrightarrow{*}q(\bm{x})
for some state

such that the coordinates in the set
\{i\in[d]\mid\bm{x}(i)<B(\left\|{\bm{x}_{0}}\right\|,\mathop{\operatorname{\textup{{size}}}}\nolimits(S))\}
are pairwise positively collinear, then
q(\bm{x})
is pumpable in

.

Proof Sketch.

Let
K:=\{i\in[d]\mid\bm{x}(i)<B(\left\|{\bm{x}_{0}}\right\|,\mathop{\operatorname{\textup{{size}}}}\nolimits(S))\}
. The proof is almost the same as that of
6
with one difference: we need to find a transition
\bm{t}
in
V
such that
\bm{t}(i)>0
for all

in order to pump up coordinates in
K
. Since
V
is wide, there must be a transition
\bm{t}
with
\bm{t}(i)>0
for some

. As every pair of coordinates in

is positively collinear, the effect of
\bm{t}
on these coordinates must be positive simultaneously.
∎

In the following, we prove

depending on whether the VAS is wide or not.

5.1
Wide VAS

First, we deal with wide VASes. Recall that
Theorem 8
was established for wide sequential VASes. There, we introduced the notions of almost boundedness and almost unboundedness to handle non-trivial bridges between VAS components. Since a wide VAS consists of only a single state, it suffices to distinguish runs based on whether they are
2
-bounded. This simplification yields the result that the shortest run length in a wide

-VASS, rather than a VASS of component-dimension

Lemma 24
.

For each
d\in\mathbb{N}
where

, there is a polynomial

such that every wide

-VAS

-VASS
(V^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime})
with

-amplification, where

Proof Sketch.

Let

be the polynomial in

,
M:=\mathop{\operatorname{\textup{{size}}}}\nolimits(V,\bm{s},\bm{t})

∎

5.2

In the case where a given

-VAS

. Built upon

Lemma 25
.

For each

, there is a polynomial

such that

-VAS

-VASSes

The proof resembles that of
Theorem 8
with one major difference: we need to make sure that the bounded coordinates in a bounded run are not collinear so that eliminating them can reduce the component-dimension by at least
2
. To begin with, we introduce two new notions. Let

, and

if

, where

contains at least one pair of non-collinear coordinates for each

. We have the following dichotomy lemma.

Lemma 26
.

Let
(V,s,t)
be a VASS with

and

. If
V
contains no negatively collinear pairs, then

is either quasi-unbounded by

.

With the help of
23
, we can extract pumpability from quasi-unbounded runs. Hence, the following polynomial length bound on quasi-unbounded runs can be obtained. Its proof is just a replay of that of
15
.

Lemma 27
.

For every
d\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a polynomial
U_{d}
such that, given a wide sequential
d
-VAS
(S,s,t)
with
s\xrightarrow{\pi}t
in

.

As for the quasi-bounded case, one can repeat the dimension reduction technique in

, which captures the two parts

separately by encoding into states the two bounded coordinates. Quasi-boundedness ensures the existence of two bounded coordinates that are not collinear. Therefore, the cycle-dimension drops by

after the encoding.

Now we can establish the finer characterization in

.

Proof of
21
.

Consider a
d
-VAS
(V,\bm{s},\bm{t})
with

We may abbreviate the two cases in the lemma as the “first characterization” (

is comprised of two

-VASSes

The case where

6

In this section, we apply the fine-grained characterization of VAS runs (

) to prove better complexity bounds for 4- and 5-VAS:

Theorem 28
.

Reachability in 5-VAS is in

. Reachability in 4-VAS is in

under binary encoding and is

-complete under unary encoding.

The

Lemma 29
.

In a 5-VAS
(V,\bm{s},\bm{t})
, if
\bm{s}\xrightarrow{*}\bm{t}
, then
\bm{s}\xrightarrow{\rho}\bm{t}
for some

.

Proof of
Theorem 28
, for 5-VAS.

Enumerating all paths of triply-exponential length (in terms of unary-encoded size) can be done in
3
-
EXPSPACE
(in terms of binary-encoded size). Therefore, we obtain the
ELEMENTARY
upper bound for 5-VAS.
∎

4
-VAS, we can also apply

to capture the shortest runs by either a

On the other hand, we are not able to apply the linear-path schemes characterization to the sequence of geometrically 1-dimensional VASSes, as it yields merely a doubly-exponential bound in terms of binary-encoded size. Hence, for both the case of a 2-VAS and the case of a geometrically 2-VASS, we are able to bound the length of their shortest runs using known results for 2-VASS

]
. We then establish the length bound for 4-VAS in the following lemma.

For any

By

∎

For any

7
Future Work

Several intriguing questions remain at the boundary of our current understanding. A fundamental open problem is whether the length of runs in
d
-VAS can be effectively captured by those in
(d-3)
-VASS. Regarding VAS in fixed dimensions, numerous challenges remain unresolved. For binary 2-VAS and 3-VASS, a complexity gap exists as the problems currently reside between an
NP
-hard lower bound and a
PSPACE
upper bound. Furthermore, the lower bound for the unary reachability problem in 2-VAS, and more generally, in geometrically 2-dimensional systems where
d
is not fixed, remains elusive; specifically, it is yet to be determined whether these problems are
NL
-hard. The case of 5-VAS presents another significant challenge. While the reachability sets of 5-VAS are known to be effectively semilinear, the current 3-
EXPSPACE
upper bound, derived from the length-boundedness of 3-VASS, appears to be loose. We suggest that a more refined analysis of the geometric constraints in 5-dimensional systems might yield tighter complexity bounds, making the reachability problem in 5-VAS a compelling subject for further exploration.

See

Proof.

Consider a reachability instance
(V,s,t)
. Under binary encoding, the length bound in
49
becomes exponential in the input size. But polynomial space is enough to enumerate every run of exponential length. This confirms the
PSPACE
membership. The
PSPACE

[
1
]
.

Under unary encoding, we may decide reachability by searching for a run of polynomial length, which can be done in
NP

d

since in the searching we may only keep record of the number of steps and the current configuration whose size is bounded by

The

-hardness follows from the trivial reduction from the graph reachability problem. For

-hardness, we reduce from the

problem, viewing the
d
-coordinates as a
d

[
13

.

Consider a
Subset-Sum
instance
\langle S=\{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\},s\rangle
, where
S\subseteq\mathbb{N}
and the goal is to find a subset of
S
whose sum is
s
. Let
d
be the minimum integer such that
2^{d}>a_{1}+a_{2}+\dots+a_{n}
, so that the sum of any possible subset can be represented as a
d
-bit binary number. For a vector
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
we define
\mathop{\operatorname{\textup{{num}}}}\nolimits(\bm{v})=\sum_{i=1}^{d}2^{i-1}\bm{v}(i)
. For each number
a<2^{d}
, we define
\mathop{\operatorname{\textup{{bin}}}}\nolimits(a)
to be the unique vector in
\mathbb{N}^{d}
such that
\mathop{\operatorname{\textup{{num}}}}\nolimits(\mathop{\operatorname{\textup{{bin}}}}\nolimits(a))=a
.

We will construct a
d
-VASS

with no cycles so that the

instance has a solution if and only if there is a run in

from
\bm{0}
to
\mathop{\operatorname{\textup{{bin}}}}\nolimits(s)
. First we add to

the states

. For each

we add the following transitions:

Intuitively, at state

we can non-deterministically choose

by adding its binary representation. Now before moving from

to

If

Note that
