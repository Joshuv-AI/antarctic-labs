Elliptic curves with rank one and nontrivial 2 2 -part of Tate Shafarevich groups over the ℤ 2 {\mathbb{Z}}_{2} -extension of ℚ {\mathbb{Q}}

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

Let
p
be a prime.
One motivation of this paper is the following question:

Question 1.1
.

Is there an elliptic curve
E
defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}
, and
are there infinitely many number fields
{\mathfrak{F}}_{n}/{\mathbb{Q}}
with
[{\mathfrak{F}}_{n}:{\mathbb{Q}}]\to\infty
,
such that
E({\mathfrak{F}}_{n})
has rank
r_{n}=0
(resp.
r_{n}=1
) for all
n
,
and the
p
-primary part
\hbox{\cyr X}(E/{\mathfrak{F}}_{n})(p)
of
\hbox{\cyr X}(E/{\mathfrak{F}}_{n})
is nontrivial (or trivial)?

For the nontrivial case, in
[
16
]
, Kurihara showed that there exists
E/{\mathbb{Q}}
and a prime
p
that is a good supersingular odd prime for
E
,
such that in the case
r_{n}=0
, the answer to the above question is affirmative when
\mathfrak{F}_{n}
is the
n^{th}
cyclotomic
p
-extension of
\mathbb{Q}
.
Later, in
[
17
]
, Kurihara and Otsuki extended this result to the case
r_{n}=1
and
p=2
,
assuming the finiteness of Tate-Shafarevich group for
E/{\mathfrak{F}}_{1}
, and they showed that the answer remains true under these conditions.

Also in the nontrivial case, when
r_{n}=0
and
p=2
,
[
5
, Theorem 8.1]
shows that if
E
is the Gross curve defined over a certain number field,
the answer is also affirmative. For the trivial case, when
r_{n}=1
and
p=2
,

[
18
, Proposition 5.2]
(see also
[
4
, Theorems 1.2 and 1.3]
) shows that there exists an elliptic curve
E/{\mathbb{Q}}
such that there exist number fields
{\mathfrak{F}}_{n}/{\mathbb{Q}}
with degree tending to
infinity, where
E({\mathfrak{F}}_{n})
has rank one, but
\hbox{\cyr X}(E/{\mathfrak{F}}_{n})(2)=0
for all
n
.

The first goal of this paper is to answer the above question for
r_{n}=1
and
p=2
, without assuming the Birch-Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture or the finiteness of Tate-Shafarevich groups.

On the other hand, the study of twists of elliptic curves over
{\mathbb{Q}}
has a long history. For example, the study of quadratic twists of the elliptic curve

{\mathcal{E}}:y^{2}=x^{3}-x

is related to the congruence number problem. Specifically, for a squarefree integer
d
, we define the quadratic twist
{\mathcal{E}}^{(d)}
of
{\mathcal{E}}
by the
equation

{\mathcal{E}}^{(d)}:dy^{2}=x^{3}-x.

Similarly, one can define the quadratic twist
E^{(d)}
for any elliptic curve
E/{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Another motivation for this paper is the following question:

Question 1.2
.

Is there an elliptic curve
E/{\mathbb{Q}}
, and are there infinitely many
d
’s
such that
E^{(d)}({\mathbb{Q}})
has rank
0
(resp. rank
1
), and
\hbox{\cyr X}(E^{(d)}/{\mathbb{Q}})(p)\neq 0
(or
=0
)?

For the nontrivial case, in
[
23
]
, for the elliptic curve
{\mathcal{E}}/{\mathbb{Q}}
, Razar showed there are infinitely many primes
\ell
such that
{\mathcal{E}}^{(\ell)}({\mathbb{Q}})
has rank zero and

\hbox{\cyr X}({\mathcal{E}}^{(\ell)}/{\mathbb{Q}})(2)\neq 0
. In
[
12
]
, the second author and his collaborator provided
evidence for the existence of infinitely many cubic twists of the curve
X_{0}(27)
with rank one and nontrivial
2
-part of the Tate-Shafarevich groups.

For the trivial case, Tian showed in
[
26
]
that there are infinitely many
d
, with arbitrarily finitely many prime factors,
such that
{\mathcal{E}}^{(d)}({\mathbb{Q}})
has rank
one, but all have
\hbox{\cyr X}({\mathcal{E}}^{(d)}/{\mathbb{Q}})(2)=0
. If further
E
is an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
with complex multiplication, then for almost all prime
p
, by
[
14
]
there are infinitely many
d
such that
\hbox{\cyr X}(E^{(d)}/{\mathbb{Q}})(p)=0
.

Building on our first goal, the main aim of this paper is to find a family of quadratic twists
E^{(d)}
of an elliptic curve
E
over
{\mathbb{Q}}
, along with
the number fields
{\mathfrak{F}}_{n}
as described in the first question,
such that
{\rm rank}(E^{(d)}({\mathfrak{F}}_{n}))=1
and
\hbox{\cyr X}(E^{(d)}/{\mathfrak{F}}_{n})(2)\neq 0
. Furthermore, we
demonstrate the rank part of the Birch-Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture for all these elliptic curves
E^{(d)}/{\mathfrak{F}}_{n}
as
n\to+\infty
. Our result generalizes previous ones from a fixed curve or a fixed number field
\mathbb{Q}
to an infinite family of number fields whose degree tends to infinity, and to a quadratic twist family of
E
over these fields.

1.2.
Main Results

Let
E
be the elliptic curve defined by

E:y^{2}+y=x^{3}+2.

The curve
E
has conductor
243
, and its Mordell-Weil group
E({\mathbb{Q}})
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{Z}}/3{\mathbb{Z}}
. Additionally, the algebraic value
of the
L
-function of
E
at
s=1
is given by

\frac{L(E,1)}{\Omega_{E}}=\frac{1}{3},

where
\Omega_{E}
is the real period of
E
. Let
f=\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}a_{n}q^{n}
be the normalized weight
2
newform corresponding to
E
,
where
q=e^{2\pi iz}
. Note that
a_{2}=0
, and
E
has good supersingular reduction at
2
.
For a squarefree integer
d\equiv 1\ \mathrm{mod}\ 4
, we denote by
E^{(d)}
the quadratic twist of
E
under the extension
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{d})/{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Let
{\mathbb{Q}}_{\infty}
be the cyclotomic
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}
. For each integer
n\geq 0
, we denote by
{\mathbb{Q}}_{n}
the unique intermediate field
in
{\mathbb{Q}}_{\infty}
such that
[{\mathbb{Q}}_{n}:{\mathbb{Q}}]=2^{n}
. Note that
{\mathbb{Q}}={\mathbb{Q}}_{0}
.

For each
n\geq 0
, the
2
-power Selmer group
{\mathrm{Sel}}(E^{(d)}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{n})
fits into the middle of the following exact sequence:

0\to E^{(d)}({\mathbb{Q}}_{n})\otimes({\mathbb{Q}}_{2}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})\to{\mathrm{Sel}}(E^{(d)}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{n})\to\hbox{\cyr X}(E^{(d)}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{n})(2)\to 0.

We denote by
{\mathrm{Sel}}(E^{(d)}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{n})^{\vee}
the Pontryagin dual of
{\mathrm{Sel}}(E^{(d)}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{n})
. The (
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-)corank of
{\mathrm{Sel}}(E^{(d)}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{n})
is defined to be
the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-rank of its Pontryagin dual. Let
L(E^{(d)}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{n},s)
denote the
L
-series of
E^{(d)}
over
{\mathbb{Q}}_{n}
. From Deuring’s theorem, this is
a holomorphic function on the whole complex plane.

For any squarefree integer
{\mathfrak{t}}
,
we denote by
h({\mathfrak{t}})
the class number of the quadratic field
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{{\mathfrak{t}}})
.
For an odd prime
\ell
, we denote by
\left(\frac{\cdot}{\ell}\right)
the Legendre symbol.
For a finite abelian group
{\mathfrak{A}}
, we define the
2
-rank of
{\mathfrak{A}}
to be the
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
-dimension of
{\mathfrak{A}}/2{\mathfrak{A}}
. The main theorem of our paper is as follows.

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose that
p
and
q
are distinct primes satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

p\equiv q\equiv 7\ \mathrm{mod}\ 12
;

(2)

a_{p}
and
a_{q}
are odd;

(3)

\left(\frac{-2p}{q}\right)=1

(4)

3\nmid h(-q)h(-6pq)
.

Let
m=pq
. Then the following hold:

(i)

{\rm rank}(E^{(m)}({\mathbb{Q}}))=0={\mathrm{ord}}_{s=1}L(E^{(m)}/{\mathbb{Q}},s)
.

(ii)

For each integer
n\geq 1
, we have

{\mathrm{ord}}_{s=1}L(E^{(m)}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{n},s)=1={\rm rank}(E^{(m)}({\mathbb{Q}}_{n})).

Furthermore, let

\hbox{\cyr X}(E^{(m)}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{\infty})=\lim_{n}\hbox{\cyr X}(E^{(m)}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{n}).

Then both the order and the
2
-rank of
\hbox{\cyr X}(E^{(m)}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{\infty})(2)
are infinite.

We remark that there are infinitely many primes
p
and
q
satisfying conditions (1) and (2) by the Chebotarev density theorem.
There are many examples of primes
p
and
q
satisfying the conditions (1)–(4) in Theorem
1.3
. For instance, one can take
p=31
and
q=7
.
According to the Cohen-Lenstra heuristic, there should be infinitely many such pairs of primes. For further examples of primes
p
and
q
satisfying the conditions of Theorem
1.3
, we refer the reader to the numerical data in Appendix C.

1.3.
Idea for the Proofs

The key ingredients for proving Theorems
1.3
are as follows;

—

The Mazur-Tate elements introduced in
[
21
]
, their vertical distribution properties, integral properties of the modular symbols (
[
19
]
) and especially the congruence relations between every cyclotomic specialization under quadratic twists;

—

Iwasawa theory for elliptic curves with complex multiplication for odd good ordinary primes, as developed by Coates, Wiles and Rubin (
[
3
]
,
[
25
]
);

—

The congruence properties of the Heegner points (
[
15
]
), derived from the
p
-adic Waldspurger formula developed by Bertolini, Darmon and Prasanna
[
1
]
.

Indeed, using the first ingredient, we can show that

L(E^{(m)},\eta,1)\neq 0

for all finite order characters
\eta\neq\psi_{1}
of
{\rm Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}_{\infty}/{\mathbb{Q}})
. Here
L(E^{(m)},\eta,s)
denotes the
L
-series of
E^{(m)}/{\mathbb{Q}}
twisted by
\eta
, and
\psi_{1}
is the nontrivial character of
{\rm Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}_{1}/{\mathbb{Q}})
.
Using the third ingredient, we can show that the Heegner point associated to
E^{(-3q)}
and
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{-2p})
is non-torsion. Combining this with the Gross-Zagier theorem and Kolyvagin theorem, we obtain

{\rm ord}_{s=1}L(E^{(m)},\psi_{1},s)=1.

Thus, the statement on the analytic rank in Theorem
1.3
follows. The algebraic rank statement follows from an equivariant Coates-Wiles theorem for
E^{(m)}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{n}(\sqrt{-3})
, which involves the second ingredient. Theorem
1.3
then follows from the standard fact that the
2
-power Selmer group of
E^{(m)}
over
{\mathbb{Q}}_{\infty}

is free of rank one over the Iwasawa algebra (see
[
6
]
), together with the observation that a finitely generated
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-module must be of torsion over the Iwasawa algebra.

For each finite-order character
\rho
of
{\rm Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}_{\infty}/{\mathbb{Q}})
,
determining the
2
-adic properties of the Mazur-Tate elements at
\rho
presents a challenge,
as the original method due to Kurihara
[
16
]
(later modified by Pollack
[
22
]
) is not directly applied in our setting.
Indeed, while the method can determine the
2
-adic properties of Mazur-Tate elements associated with
E
at
\rho
, it fails for
the Mazur-Tate elements associated with
E^{(m)}
.
To overcome this difficulty, we define a modular element
\xi_{E}(\chi)
associated
with the pair
(E,\chi)
, where
\chi
is the nontrivial character of
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{m})/\mathbb{Q}
. The specializations of
\xi_{E}(\chi)
at
\rho
are equal to the specializations of the Mazur-Tate elements associated
with
E^{(m)}
up to
2
-adic units.
By employing the integral properties of modular symbols (see
[
19
]
), we establish that the specializations of
\xi_{E}(\chi)

and the Mazur-Tate element associated with
E
are related modulo
2
, and their differences are a product of
2
-adic units coming from Euler factors.
This key observation allows us to resolve the problem, except in the case of the primitive characters of
{\rm Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}_{3}/{\mathbb{Q}})
.
To handle this exceptional case, we consider one such character
\rho_{3}
.
Using the Hecke action on modular symbols, we derive several higher congruence
relations (i.e. modulo
4
) between the coefficients in the
specialization of
\xi_{E}(\chi)
at
\rho_{3}
.
By combining these higher congruence relations with the
2
-adic property of
\rho_{3}(\xi_{E}(\chi))
, which is obtained from the modulo
2
congruence, we can establish the required
2
-adic property for
\rho_{3}(\xi_{E}(\chi))
. Equivalently, we get the
2
-adic properties for
the Mazur-Tate elements associated with
E^{(m)}
at
\rho_{3}
.

We remark that the method described above has several applications to other arithmetic problems. In
[
8
]
, we determine the Iwasawa invariants for the higher even
K
-groups of the rings of integers in the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-extension of real quadratic number fields. In
[
9
]
, the first author determines the variations of the analytic Iwasawa invariants of elliptic curves over the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}
under quadratic twists.

1.4.
Layout of the Paper

In the final part of the introduction, we outline the structure of the paper. In Section
2
, we introduce some notation and provide the necessary preliminaries. In Section
3
, we discuss the Mazur-Tate elements and their relationship with the special values of
L
-functions associated with elliptic curves. Section
4
is dedicated to determining the
2
-adic properties of Mazur-Tate elements for
E^{(m)}
at all finite-order characters of
{\rm Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}_{\infty}/{\mathbb{Q}})
.
Section
5
utilizes the congruence properties of Heegner points (as established by Kriz and Li
[
15
]
) to demonstrate the non-torsionness of these points, and proves Theorem
1.3
. In Appendix A , we provide a detailed analysis of the
2
-adic properties of the Mazur-Tate elements for
E^{(m)}
at the primitive characters of
{\rm Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}_{3}/{\mathbb{Q}})
. In Appendix B, we give a sketch proof of an equivariant Coates-Wiles theorem. Finally, in Appendix C, we present numerical data for primes
p
and
q
satisfying the conditions of Theorem
1.3
.

2.
Notation and Preliminaries

2.1.
General Setting

Let
E
denote the elliptic curve given by

E:\quad y^{2}+y=x^{3}+2

which is defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}
and is a sextic twist of
X_{0}(27)
. The elliptic curve
E
has complex multiplication by
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{-3})
, conductor
N=N_{E}=243
, discriminant
\Delta_{E}=-2187
, and real period

\Omega_{E}=3.6747....

Furthermore, the Mordell-Weil group of
E/{\mathbb{Q}}
, denoted by
E({\mathbb{Q}})
, is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{Z}}/3{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Let

f=\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}a_{n}q^{n},\qquad q=\exp(2\pi iz),

be the weight
2
newform associated to
E
. The
L
-function of
E
is denoted by
L(E,s)
, and we have

L(E,s)=L(f,s).

It is known that

\frac{L(E,1)}{\Omega_{E}}=\frac{1}{3}.

Let
\epsilon_{E}
denote the root number of
L(E,s)
. For any Dirichlet character
\eta
, we denote by
L(E,\eta,s)
the
L
-function of
E
twisted by
\eta
.

For an abelian group
{\mathfrak{A}}
and a positive integer
n
, we denote by
{\mathfrak{A}}_{n}
the kernel of the multiplication by
n
map on
{\mathfrak{A}}
. We set

{\mathfrak{A}}(n)=\bigcup_{m\geq 1}{\mathfrak{A}}_{n^{m}}.

For a squarefree integer
d\equiv 1\ \mathrm{mod}\ 4
, we denote by
E^{(d)}
the elliptic curve obtained by twisting
E
under the quadratic extension
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{d})/{\mathbb{Q}}
. For any number field
F
, we define the
2
-power Selmer group via the following exact sequence:

0\to{\mathrm{Sel}}(E^{(d)}/F)\to H^{1}(F,E^{(d)}(2))\to\prod_{v}\frac{H^{1}(F_{v},E^{(d)}(2))}{E^{(d)}(F_{v})\otimes({\mathbb{Q}}_{2}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})},

where the product is taken over all places of
F
. Denote by
\hbox{\cyr X}(E^{(d)}/F)(2)
the
2
-primary part of the Tate-Shafarevich group of
E^{(d)}/F
. We have an exact sequence:

0\to E^{(d)}(F)\otimes({\mathbb{Q}}_{2}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})\to{\mathrm{Sel}}(E^{(d)}/F)\to\hbox{\cyr X}(E^{(d)}/F)(2)\to 0.

For any finite prime
{\mathfrak{l}}
of
F
, we denote by
c_{\mathfrak{l}}(E^{(d)}/F)
the Tamagawa number of
E^{(d)}/F
at
{\mathfrak{l}}
.

2.2.
Cyclotomic Extensions

For each non-negative integer
n
, let
\zeta_{n}
denote a primitive
n
-th root of unity. Let
{\mathbb{Q}}_{n}
denote the maximal totally real subfield in
{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{2^{n+2}})
. Denote by
\mathscr{G}_{n}={\rm Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{2^{n}})/{\mathbb{Q}})
and
G_{n}={\rm Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}_{n}/{\mathbb{Q}})
. It is straightforward to observe that
G_{n}\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}/2^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}
.

The natural projection
\mathscr{G}_{n}\to\mathscr{G}_{n-1}
induces a map

\pi_{n}:{\mathbb{Q}}[\mathscr{G}_{n}]\to{\mathbb{Q}}[\mathscr{G}_{n-1}].

Define

\nu_{n-1}:{\mathbb{Q}}[\mathscr{G}_{n-1}]\to{\mathbb{Q}}[\mathscr{G}_{n}]

by sending any
\sigma\in\mathscr{G}_{n-1}
to

\sum_{\pi_{n}(\sigma^{\prime})=\sigma}\sigma^{\prime},

where the sum is over all
\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathscr{G}_{n}
that map to
\sigma
under the projection
\pi_{n}
.
Note that we also have canonical projection maps

\mathscr{G}_{n+2}\to G_{n}.

Similarly, we can define the maps

\pi_{n}:{\mathbb{Q}}[G_{n}]\to{\mathbb{Q}}[G_{n-1}]

and

\nu_{n-1}:{\mathbb{Q}}[G_{n-1}]\to{\mathbb{Q}}[G_{n}].

Now define
{\mathbb{Q}}_{\infty}=\bigcup_{n\geq 0}{\mathbb{Q}}_{n}
, which is called the cyclotomic
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}
. Let

\Gamma={\rm Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}_{\infty}/{\mathbb{Q}})\quad\textrm{and}\quad\Gamma_{n}={\rm Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}_{\infty}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{n}).

We define the group algebras
\Lambda_{n}={\mathbb{Z}}_{2}[G_{n}]
associated with
G_{n}
and
\Lambda={\mathbb{Z}}_{2}[[\Gamma]]
, which is the Iwasawa algebra of
\Gamma
.

For a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-module
{\mathfrak{M}}
, we denote by
{\mathfrak{M}}^{\vee}={\rm Hom}({\mathfrak{M}},{\mathbb{Q}}_{2}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
the Pontryagin dual of
{\mathfrak{M}}
. Let
{\mathfrak{M}}_{\rm tor}
denote the torsion submodule of
{\mathfrak{M}}
.

In
{\mathbb{Q}}_{2}(\zeta_{2^{n}})
, we define
v
to be the normalized additive valuation at the unique prime above
2
, satisfying
v(2)=1
.

2.3.
An Application of Chebotarev Density Theorem

Let
p
and
q
be two distinct primes satisfying the following conditions:

—

p\equiv q\equiv 7\ \mathrm{mod}\ 12
,

—

a_{p}
and
a_{q}
are odd.

Here,
a_{p}
and
a_{q}
are the Fourier coefficients of
f
.
We denote by
m=pq
and
\chi
the Dirichlet character associated with the quadratic extension
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{m})/{\mathbb{Q}}
.

We define the set
{\mathfrak{S}}
as follows:

{\mathfrak{S}}=\{p:\,p\textrm{ is a prime, }p\equiv 7\ \mathrm{mod}\ 12,\quad a_{p}\not\equiv 0\ \mathrm{mod}\ 2\}.

Proposition 2.1
.

The set
{\mathfrak{S}}
has infinite cardinality.

Proof.

Since
E
has good reduction at
p
, let
\widetilde{E}
denote the reduction of
E
modulo
p
. We observe that

a_{p}=p+1-\#(\widetilde{E}({\mathbb{F}}_{p})).

Therefore, the condition
2\nmid a_{p}
is equivalent to
\widetilde{E}({\mathbb{F}}_{p})_{2}=0
. A direct computation shows that

L={\mathbb{Q}}(E_{2})={\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[3]{18},\sqrt{-3}).

For
p\equiv 1\ \mathrm{mod}\ 3
, since
p
splits in
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{-3})
, it follows that
2\nmid a_{p}
if and only if

is inert in
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[3]{18})/{\mathbb{Q}}
. Consider the following field extensions

3.
Mazur-Tate Elements and Special Value Formulas of
L
-Functions

In this section, we will introduce modular symbols and Mazur-Tate modular elements for a general elliptic curve
A
defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}
. We will then study the distribution properties of these modular elements and their relationship with special values of the
L
-functions of
A
. In the final part of this section, we will focus on the case when
A=E
or a certain quadratic twist of
E
, and present results that allow us to determine the algebraic part of
L(A,1)
.

3.1.
Modular Symbols

For a positive integer
N
, we denote by
S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
the space of cusp forms of weight
2
with respect to
\Gamma_{0}(N)
. Let
\phi=\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}(\phi)q^{n}
be a normalized newform in
S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
, where we assume that
a_{n}(\phi)\in{\mathbb{Q}}
for all
n\geq 1
. It is well-known that
\phi
corresponds to an isogeny class of elliptic curves defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}
, and we denote by
A
the unique optimal elliptic curve in this
{\mathbb{Q}}
-isogeny class. We also write
L(A,s)
for the complex
L
-function associated with
A
. The
L
-function
L(A,s)
coincides with
L(\phi,s)
, and there exists a non-constant morphism defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}
,

\iota:X_{0}(N)\to A,

which does not factor through any other curves in the isogeny class of
A
. Let
\omega
denote the Néron differential on a global Weierstrass minimal model of
A
. Then there exists a nonzero integer
\nu_{A}
such that

(3.1)

\iota^{*}\omega=\nu_{A}\cdot\phi(z)dz.

The number
\nu_{A}
is called the Manin constant, and it is known that
\nu_{A}
is odd when
N
is odd
[
2
]
.

Let
{\mathbb{H}}
be the upper half plane, and let
{\mathbb{H}}^{*}={\mathbb{H}}\cup{\mathbb{P}}^{1}({\mathbb{Q}})
. Let
g
be an element of
\Gamma_{0}(N)
. Let
\alpha
and
\beta
be two points in
{\mathbb{H}}^{*}
such that
\beta=g\alpha
. Then, any path from
\alpha
to
\beta
in
{\mathbb{H}}^{*}
is a closed path on

X_{0}(N)
, whose homology class depends only on
\alpha
and
\beta
. Thus, it determines an integral homology class in
H_{1}(X_{0}(N),{\mathbb{Z}})
, which we denote by the modular symbol
\{\alpha,\beta\}
. For further details on the basic properties of these modular symbols, one may refer to
[
19
]
,
[
7
]
and
[
27
]
.

From now on, we assume that the conductor
N
of
A
is odd and that the discriminant
\Delta_{A}
of
A
is negative.
For a modular symbol
\{\alpha,\beta\}\in H_{1}(X_{0}(N),{\mathbb{Z}})
, we define the pairing

\langle\{\alpha,\beta\},\phi\rangle=2\pi i\int^{\beta}_{\alpha}\phi(z)dz.

The period lattice
\Lambda_{\phi}
of the modular form
\phi
is defined as the set of all such pairings
\langle\{\alpha,\beta\},\phi\rangle
for all modular symbols

\{\alpha,\beta\}\in H_{1}(X_{0}(N),{\mathbb{Z}})
. Since
\Delta_{A}<0
, it is known (see
[
21
]
) that there exist two nonzero real numbers
\Omega^{\pm}_{\phi}
such that

(3.2)

\Lambda_{\phi}={\mathbb{Z}}\cdot\Omega^{+}_{\phi}+{\mathbb{Z}}\cdot\frac{\Omega^{+}_{\phi}+i\Omega^{-}_{\phi}}{2}.

Let
\Lambda_{A}
denote the period lattice of the Néron differential
\omega
for
A
. From (
3.1
), we have

\Lambda_{A}=\nu_{A}\Lambda_{\phi}.

We define
\Omega^{+}_{A}
(resp.
i\Omega^{-}_{A}
) as the minimal positive real (resp. purely imaginary) period of
A
. Thus, we have

\Omega^{+}_{A}=\nu_{A}\Omega^{+}_{\phi}\quad\textrm{ and }\quad\Omega^{-}_{A}=\nu_{A}\Omega^{-}_{\phi}.

Since we assume that
A
has odd conductor,
\nu_{A}
is odd. For the purpose of
2
-adic valuation, we may assume that

\nu_{A}=1
. In fact, when
A=E
, where
E
is optimal in its isogeny class, it is always the case that
\nu_{E}=1
. For simplicity, we denote
\Omega_{A}=\Omega^{+}_{A}
.

Let
\frac{k}{t}
be a rational number such that
(k,t)=1
and
(t,N)=1
. From
[
19
, Proposition 2.2 and Theorem 3.9]
, we have

\{0,\frac{k}{t}\}\in H_{1}(X_{0}(N),{\mathbb{Z}}).

We then define

{\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{k}{t}\right)={\mathrm{Re}}\left(\langle\{0,\frac{k}{t}\},\phi\rangle/\Omega_{A}\right).

From equation (
3.2
), we have
{\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{k}{t}\right)\in\frac{1}{2}{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

(1)

We have
{\mathcal{S}}(0)=0
. For any rational number
\beta=\frac{k}{t}
satisfying
(k,t)=1
and
(t,N)=1
, we have
{\mathcal{S}}(\beta)={\mathcal{S}}(-\beta)
, and
{\mathcal{S}}
is periodic with period
1
, i.e.,
{\mathcal{S}}(\beta+1)={\mathcal{S}}(\beta)
.

(2)

Let
\ell
be a prime such that
(\ell,N)=1
. Let
\beta
be as in (1), then we have

(3.3)

\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}/\ell{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{\beta+x}{\ell}\right)=a_{\ell}(\phi)\cdot{\mathcal{S}}(\beta)-{\mathcal{S}}(\ell\beta)+\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}/\ell{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{x}{\ell}\right).

Proof.

(1) follows directly from the definition of the modular symbols. For example, the equation
{\mathcal{S}}(\beta)={\mathcal{S}}(-\beta)
follows from the fact that the complex conjugation of
\langle\{0,\beta\},\phi\rangle
is equal to
\langle\{0,-\beta\},\phi\rangle
(see
[
7
]
). Let
{\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}
be the Hecke operator associated with
\Gamma_{0}(N)
. From the Hecke action on modular symbols (see
[
7
]
), we have

{\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}\{\alpha,\gamma\}=\{\ell\alpha,\ell\gamma\}+\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}/\ell{\mathbb{Z}}}\left\{\frac{\alpha+x}{\ell},\frac{\gamma+x}{\ell}\right\}.

Since the pairing is compatible under the Hecke action, i.e.

\langle{\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}\{\alpha,\gamma\},\phi\rangle=\langle\{\alpha,\gamma\},{\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}\phi\rangle,

and
{\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}\phi=a_{\ell}(\phi)\phi
, (2) follows by taking
\alpha=0
and
\gamma=\beta
.
∎

3.2.
Modular Elements

For a positive integer
n
, we recall that
\mathscr{G}_{n}={\rm Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{2^{n}})/{\mathbb{Q}})
, which is isomorphic to
({\mathbb{Z}}/2^{n}{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}
. For each
k\in({\mathbb{Z}}/2^{n}{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}
, we denote by
\sigma_{k}
the element in
\mathscr{G}_{n}
corresponding to the automorphism of
{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{2^{n}})
that sends
\zeta_{2^{n}}
to
\zeta^{k}_{2^{n}}
.

The modular element associated with
A
for
\mathscr{G}_{n}
is defined as follows:

\xi_{2^{n}}=\sum_{k\in({\mathbb{Z}}/2^{n}{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}}{\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{k}{2^{n}}\right)\sigma_{k}\in{\mathbb{Q}}[\mathscr{G}_{n}],

and the trace element
\tau_{2^{n}}
in
{\mathbb{Q}}[\mathscr{G}_{n}]
is given by

\tau_{2^{n}}=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathscr{G}_{n}}\sigma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\phi
be the weight
2
newform associated with
A
,
and assume that
a_{2}(\phi)=0
.
For any integer
n\geq 3
, we have the following distribution relation

\pi_{n}(\xi_{2^{n}})=-\nu_{n-2}(\xi_{2^{n-2}})+{\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)\tau_{2^{n-1}}.

Proof.

By the definition of
\xi_{2^{n}}
, we have

\pi_{n}(\xi_{2^{n}})=\pi_{n}\left(\sum_{1\leq k\leq 2^{n-1},2\nmid k}\left({\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{k}{2^{n}}\right)\sigma_{k}+{\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{k+2^{n-1}}{2^{n}}\right)\sigma_{k+2^{n-1}}\right)\right).

Using the fact that

\pi_{n}(\sigma_{k+2^{n-1}})=\pi_{n}(\sigma_{k}),

we obtain

\pi_{n}(\xi_{2^{n}})=\sum_{1\leq k\leq 2^{n-1},2\nmid k}\left({\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{k}{2^{n}}\right)+{\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{k+2^{n-1}}{2^{n}}\right)\right)\sigma_{k}.

Since the conductor
N
of
A
is odd and
a_{2}(\phi)=0
, we take
\ell=2
in (2) of Proposition
3.1
, which gives

{\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{k}{2^{n}}\right)+{\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{k+2^{n-1}}{2^{n}}\right)=-{\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{k}{2^{n-2}}\right)+{\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right).

Combining this with the relation

\nu_{n-2}(\sigma_{k})=\sigma_{k}+\sigma_{k+2^{n-2}},

the lemma follows.
∎

We define the Mazur-Tate element
\xi_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{n}}
as the image of
\xi_{2^{n+2}}
under the natural restriction map

{\mathbb{Q}}[\mathscr{G}_{n+2}]\to{\mathbb{Q}}[G_{n}].

We also denote by

\tau_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{n}}=\sum_{\sigma\in G_{n}}\sigma

the trace element of
G_{n}
. From Lemma
3.2
and the fact that
\nu_{A}
is odd, we can easily prove the following lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

(1)

For any integer
n\geq 2
, we have

\pi_{n}(\xi_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{n}})=-\nu_{n-2}(\xi_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{n-2}})+2{\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)\tau_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{n-1}}.

(2)

The modular element
\xi_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{n}}
belongs to
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}[G_{n}]
.

3.3.
Relation with
L
-functions

Let
M
be an integer such that
(M,N)=1
. Let
\eta
be a primitive Dirichlet character modulo
M
. Define the Gauss sum of
\eta
by

g(\eta)=\sum^{M}_{x=1}\eta(x)\exp\left(\frac{2\pi ix}{M}\right).

We record the special value formulas in the following theorem (see
[
19
, Theorem 4.2]
).

Theorem 3.4
.

(1)

Let
\sigma(M)
denote the sum of divisors of
M
, i.e.,
\sigma(M)=\sum_{l\mid M}l
. Then we have

\left(a_{M}(\phi)-\sigma(M)\right)L(A,1)=\sum_{l\mid M}\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}/l{\mathbb{Z}}}\langle\{0,\frac{x}{l}\},\phi\rangle.

(2)

Assume that
\eta
is a nontrivial primitive even Dirichlet character modulo
M
. Then we have

g(\eta)\frac{L(A,\overline{\eta},1)}{\Omega_{A}}=\sum_{x\in({\mathbb{Z}}/M{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}}\eta(x)\cdot{\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{x}{M}\right).

Inspiring by the second part of Theorem
3.4
, for any primitive Dirichlet character
\psi
with conductor dividing
M
, we define

(3.4)

\mathscr{P}_{\psi,M}({\mathcal{S}})=\sum_{x\in({\mathbb{Z}}/M{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}}\psi(x){\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{x}{M}\right).

When
\psi
has conductor exactly
M
, we simply denote
\mathscr{P}_{\psi,M}({\mathcal{S}})
by
\mathscr{P}_{\psi}({\mathcal{S}})
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
M
be a squarefree positive integer such that
(M,N)=1
.

(1)

Let
\mathds{1}
be the trivial character, then

\mathscr{P}_{\mathds{1},M}({\mathcal{S}})=\left(\prod_{p\mid M}(a_{p}(\phi)-2)-\varphi(M)\right)\cdot\frac{L(A,1)}{\Omega_{A}},

where the product is taken over all prime divisors
p
of
M
, and
\varphi(M)
is the Euler function.

(2)

For any prime
\ell\mid M
, let
\psi
be a nontrivial primitive Dirichlet character with conductor dividing
M/\ell
. Then

\mathscr{P}_{\psi,M}({\mathcal{S}})=\left(a_{\ell}(\phi)-\psi(\ell)-\overline{\psi}(\ell)\right)\mathscr{P}_{\psi,M/\ell}({\mathcal{S}}).

We remark that for
\psi
a quadratic character, an analogue of (2) can be found in
[
28
, (2.5)]
and the references cited there.

Proof.

(1) Observe the identity

\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}/M{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{k}{M}\right)=\sum_{d\mid M}\mathscr{P}_{\mathds{1},d}({\mathcal{S}}).

Viewing the left hand side as a function of
M
, and applying the Möbius inversion formula, we obtain

(3.5)

\mathscr{P}_{\mathds{1},M}({\mathcal{S}})=\sum_{d\mid M}\mu(M/d)\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}/d{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{k}{d}\right).

Note that
(a_{d}(\phi)-\sigma(d))\frac{L(A,1)}{\Omega_{A}}
is real. By (1) of Theorem
3.4
, we have

\sum_{l\mid d}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}/l{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{k}{l}\right)=(a_{d}(\phi)-\sigma(d))\frac{L(A,1)}{\Omega_{A}}.

Therefore, by applying the Möbius inversion formula, we have

\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}/d{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{k}{d}\right)=\sum_{l\mid d}\mu(d/l)\left(a_{l}(\phi)-\sigma(l)\right)\frac{L(A,1)}{\Omega_{A}}.

Since
M
is squarefree,
d
is also squarefree.
Using the identities

\sum_{l\mid d}\mu(d/l)a_{l}(\phi)=\prod_{p\mid d}(a_{p}(\phi)-1),

where, in the product,
p
runs over all prime divisors of
d
, and

\sum_{l\mid d}\mu(d/l)\sigma(l)=d,

the equation (
3.5
) becomes

\mathscr{P}_{\mathds{1},M}({\mathcal{S}})=\sum_{d\mid M}\mu(M/d)\cdot\left(\prod_{p\mid d}(a_{p}(\phi)-1)-d\right)\frac{L(A,1)}{\Omega_{A}}.

Noting that
\sum_{d\mid M}\mu(M/d)d=\varphi(M)
, and

\sum_{d\mid M}\mu(M/d)\prod_{p\mid d}(a_{p}(\phi)-1)=\prod_{p\mid M}(a_{p}(\phi)-2),

we can conclude that part (1) follows.

(2) Denote
M^{\prime}=M/\ell
. Since
(\ell,M^{\prime})=1
, any element in
({\mathbb{Z}}/M{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}
can be written uniquely as

xM^{\prime}+y\ell
, where
x\in({\mathbb{Z}}/\ell{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}
and
y\in({\mathbb{Z}}/M^{\prime}{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}
.
A direct computation shows that

(3.6)

\mathscr{P}_{\psi,M}({\mathcal{S}})=\sum_{y\in({\mathbb{Z}}/M^{\prime}{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}}\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}/\ell{\mathbb{Z}}}\psi(y){\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{y+M^{\prime}x}{M}\right)-\sum_{y\in({\mathbb{Z}}/M^{\prime}{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}}\psi(\ell y){\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{\ell y}{M}\right).

Taking
\beta=\frac{y}{M^{\prime}}
in (
3.3
) and noting that
\psi
is a nontrivial character, from (
3.6
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathscr{P}_{\psi,M}({\mathcal{S}})=

\displaystyle a_{\ell}(\phi)\sum_{\ell\in({\mathbb{Z}}/M^{\prime}{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}}\psi(y){\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{y}{M^{\prime}}\right)-\sum_{y\in({\mathbb{Z}}/M^{\prime}{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}}\psi(y){\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{y\ell}{M^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in({\mathbb{Z}}/M^{\prime}{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}}\psi(\ell y){\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{y}{M^{\prime}}\right).

Since
\ell
is prime to
M^{\prime}
, the second sum is equal to
\overline{\psi}(\ell)\mathscr{P}_{\psi,M^{\prime}}({\mathcal{S}})
. Thus, part (2) follows.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\ell
be a prime and
M=\ell^{n}
. Assume that
(M,N)=1
. Then, we have

\mathscr{P}_{\mathds{1},M}({\mathcal{S}})=\left((a_{M}-2a_{M/\ell}+a_{M/\ell^{2}})-\varphi(M)\right)\cdot\frac{L(A,1)}{\Omega_{A}}.

Here, we have used the convention
a_{l}=0
if
l\notin{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Proof.

Consider the following identity:

\sum_{d\mid M}\mu(M/d)a_{d}=a_{M}-a_{M/\ell},

where the sum is taken over all positive divisors
d
of
M
. This follows from
the identity

a_{M}=\sum_{\ell^{i}\mid M}(a_{\ell^{i}}-a_{\ell^{i-1}})

and Möbius inversion formula. Next,
by applying the same method, we obtain

\sum_{d\mid M}\mu(M/d)a_{d/\ell}=a_{M/\ell}-a_{M/\ell^{2}}.

Thus, the lemma follows from a similar argument as in (1) of Lemma
3.5
.
∎

3.4.
L
-Values for Quadratic Twists of
E

In the last subsection, we take
A
to be the elliptic curve

E:y^{2}+y=x^{3}+2.

Note that the conductor
N=243
,
a_{2}=0
and
\Delta_{E}<0
, our assumption is automatically satisfied. We simply write
a_{n}
for
a_{n}(\phi)
in the follows.

Lemma 3.7
.

(1)

{\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)=-1
and
{\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)={\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)=-\frac{1}{2}
.

(2)

{\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{x}{8}\right)=0
for all odd
x
.

Proof.

The first assertion of part (1) follows directly from Lemmas
3.5
and
3.6
. We now prove the second assertion.
Since
{\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)={\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)
, by applying Lemma
3.6
, we obtain

2{\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)=(a_{4}-2a_{2}-1)\cdot\frac{L(E,1)}{\Omega_{E}}.

Recall that
L(E,1)/\Omega_{E}=1/3
, and observe that
a_{4}=-2
and
a_{2}=0
, we have

{\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)=-1/2
.

For part (2), note that

{\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{1}{8}\right)={\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{7}{8}\right)\quad\textrm{and}\quad{\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{3}{8}\right)={\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{5}{8}\right).

Since
a_{8}=0
, and applying Lemma
3.6
, we have
{\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{1}{8}\right)=-{\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{3}{8}\right)
. Next, we consider the elliptic curve

E^{(2)}
, which is the quadratic twist of
E
by the extension
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{2})/{\mathbb{Q}}
. Let
\eta_{8}
be the Dirichlet character associated to
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{2})/{\mathbb{Q}}
. Since
E^{(2)}
has root number
-1
, applying (2) in Theorem
3.4
to
E
and
\eta_{8}
, we obtain

\sum^{8}_{x=1}\eta_{8}(x){\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{x}{8}\right)=0,

which is equivalent to
{\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{1}{8}\right)={\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{3}{8}\right)
. Therefore,
{\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{x}{8}\right)=0
for all odd
x
.
∎

Take
p,q\in{\mathfrak{S}}
. As always, we denote by
m=pq
and
\chi
the Dirichlet character associated with the extension
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{m})/{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Proposition 3.8
.

We have

(1)

\frac{L(E^{(m)},1)}{\Omega_{E^{(m)}}}
is an odd integer.

(2)

L(E^{(2m)},1)=0
.

Proof.

Part (2) follows from the fact that
E^{(2m)}
has root number
-1
. We now prove part (1). Since
\chi
is even and
{\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{x}{m}\right)={\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{m-x}{m}\right)
, applying (2) in Theorem
3.4
, we obtain

(3.7)

\frac{L(E^{(m)},1)}{\Omega_{E^{(m)}}}=\sum^{\frac{m-1}{2}}_{x=1}\chi(k)\cdot 2{\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{x}{m}\right).

Note that the Manin constant
\nu_{E}=1
and
2{\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{x}{m}\right)\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, we know that
L(E^{(m)},1)/\Omega_{E^{(m)}}
is an integer. Since
\chi(x)=\pm 1
if
(x,m)=1
, we take both sides of (
3.7
) modulo
2
, yielding

\frac{L(E^{(m)},1)}{\Omega_{E^{(m)}}}\equiv\sum_{1\leq x\leq\frac{m-1}{2},\,(x,m)=1}2{\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{x}{m}\right)\ \mathrm{mod}\ 2.

By applying the relation
{\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{x}{m}\right)={\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{m-x}{m}\right)
again, we obtain

\frac{L(E^{(m)},1)}{\Omega_{E^{(m)}}}\equiv\mathscr{P}_{\mathds{1},m}({\mathcal{S}})\ \mathrm{mod}\ 2.

Finally, applying (1) from Lemma
3.5
, we have

\frac{L(E^{(m)},1)}{\Omega_{E^{(m)}}}\equiv\frac{1}{3}\left((a_{p}-2)(a_{q}-2)-(p-1)(q-1)\right)\ \mathrm{mod}\ 2.

Since
a_{p}
and
a_{q}
are odd, we conclude that
\frac{L(E^{(m)},1)}{\Omega_{E^{(m)}}}
is an odd integer.
∎

4.
2
-Adic Properties of Mazur-Tate Elements under Specializations

4.1.
\mu
and
\lambda
Invariants

Let
{\mathbb{Q}}_{\infty}/{\mathbb{Q}}
be the cyclotomic
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}
, and let
{\mathbb{Q}}_{n}
denote the unique intermediate field in
{\mathbb{Q}}_{\infty}
such that
[{\mathbb{Q}}_{n}:{\mathbb{Q}}]=2^{n}
. Define
G_{n}={\rm Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}_{n}/{\mathbb{Q}})
, and let
\Lambda_{n}={\mathbb{Z}}_{2}[G_{n}]
denote the group algebra associated with
G_{n}
.
In this subsection, we assume that
n
is a positive integer. We will review Pollack’s reinterpretation of Kurihara’s method for determining the
2
-adic valuations of the specializations of modular elements on finite-order characters of
{\rm Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}_{\infty}/{\mathbb{Q}})
. A key concept in Pollack’s approach is the use of Iwasawa invariants for the group ring
\Lambda_{n}={\mathbb{Z}}_{2}[G_{n}]
. While Pollack’s work primarily focused on odd primes (cf.
[
22
]
), we will show that the same method can be extended to the case of the prime
2
. For the convenience of the reader, we will carefully review the results and provide a detailed proof specifically for the case of the prime
2
.

Let
I_{n}
denote the augmentation ideal of
\Lambda_{n}
. We also define
\widetilde{\Lambda}_{n}={\mathbb{F}}_{2}[G_{n}]
and let
\widetilde{I}_{n}
be the augmentation ideal of
\widetilde{\Lambda}_{n}
.

Definition 4.1
.

For a nonzero element
g\in\Lambda_{n}
, we define the following:

(1)

The
\mu
-invariant of
g
is the unique integer
\mu(g)
such that

g\in 2^{\mu(g)}\Lambda_{n}
but
g\notin 2^{\mu(g)+1}\Lambda_{n}
.

(2)

The
\lambda
-invariant of
g
is the unique integer
\lambda(g)
such that the reduction of
2^{-\mu(g)}g
modulo
2
lies in
\widetilde{I}^{\lambda(g)}_{n}
but does not lie in
\widetilde{I}^{\lambda(g)+1}_{n}
.

The
\mu
and
\lambda
invariants are not additive in general. However, they satisfy the following weaker properties, whose proofs we omit.

Lemma 4.2
.

For nonzero
f,g\in\Lambda_{n}
, we have

(1)

\mu(fg)\geq\mu(f)+\mu(g)

(2)

If
\mu(fg)=0
, then
\lambda(fg)=\lambda(f)+\lambda(g)
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\psi_{n}
be a character of
G_{n}
of order
2^{n}
. If
f\in\Lambda_{n}
satisfies
\lambda(f)<2^{n-1}
, then

v(\psi_{n}(f))=\mu(f)+\frac{\lambda(f)}{2^{n-1}},

where
v
denotes the normalized additive
2
-adic valuation on
{\mathbb{Q}}_{2}(\zeta_{2^{n}})
such that
v(2)=1
.

Proof.

Let
\gamma
be a generator of
G_{n}
.
We can express
f\in\Lambda_{n}
as

f=2^{\mu(f)}\left((\gamma-1)^{\lambda(f)}u+2f_{0}\right),

where
u\in\Lambda^{\times}_{n}
and
f_{0}\in\Lambda_{n}
. Since
\psi_{n}
has order
2^{n}
, we know that
\psi_{n}(\gamma)
is a primitive
2^{n}
-th root of unity. Thus

v(\psi_{n}(\gamma)-1)=\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}.

Observe that, when
\lambda(f)<2^{n-1}
, we have

\lambda(f)\cdot v(\psi_{n}(\gamma)-1)<v(2\psi_{n}(f_{0})).

Therefore, the lemma follows.
∎

Define

{\mathfrak{d}}_{n}=\sum_{x\in G_{n},x^{2}=1}x\in\Lambda_{n}.

Note that the sum is taken over elements in the kernel of the projection
\pi_{n}:G_{n}\to G_{n-1}
. It is clear that
\mu({\mathfrak{d}}_{n})=0
.
As in the proof of Lemma
4.3
, we let
\gamma
be a generator of
G_{n}
. Then

{\mathfrak{d}}_{n}=1+\gamma^{2^{n-1}}\equiv(\gamma-1)^{2^{n-1}}\ \mathrm{mod}\ 2,

therefore, we obtain that
\lambda({\mathfrak{d}}_{n})=2^{n-1}
.

Lemma 4.4
.

For nonzero
f\in\Lambda_{n-1}
and
g\in\Lambda_{n}
, we have

(1)

\pi_{n}\circ\nu_{n-1}(f)=2f
.

(2)

\nu_{n-1}\circ\pi_{n}(g)={\mathfrak{d}}_{n}g
.

Proof.

For part (1), let
f=\sum_{x\in G_{n-1}}c_{x}x
. We observe that
\nu_{n-1}(x)=x_{0}{\mathfrak{d}}_{n}
, where
x_{0}
is any lift of
x
in
G_{n}
, and
\pi_{n}(x_{0}y)=x

for any
y\in{\rm ker}(\pi_{n})
. Therefore, we have

\pi_{n}\circ\nu_{n-1}(f)=\sum_{x\in G_{n-1}}c_{x}\pi_{n}(x_{0}{\mathfrak{d}}_{n})=2f.

Part (2) follows in a similar manner, and we omit the proof for brevity.
∎

Proposition 4.5
.

For nonzero elements
g\in\Lambda_{n}
and
f\in\Lambda_{n-1}
, we have

(1)

\mu(\pi_{n}(g))\geq\mu(g)
. In particular,
\mu(\pi_{n}(g))=0
implies
\mu(g)=0
.

(2)

If
\mu(\pi_{n}(g))=\mu(g)
, then
\lambda(\pi_{n}(g))=\lambda(g)
.

(3)

\mu(\nu_{n-1}(f))=\mu(f)
.

(4)

\lambda(\nu_{n-1}(f))=2^{n-1}+\lambda(f)
.

Proof.

Part (1) follows directly from the definition of
\mu
-invariants. Parts (2) and (3) follow by the same way as in
[
22
, Proposition 4.9]
. We now prove part (4). By considering
2^{-\mu(f)}f
instead of
f
, we may assume that
\mu(f)=0
. From part (3), we know that
\mu(\nu_{n-1}(f))=0
. Let
f_{0}
be any element in
\Lambda_{n}
such that
\pi_{n}(f_{0})=f
. From part (2) of Lemma
4.4
, we obtain

\nu_{n-1}(f)={\mathfrak{d}}_{n}f_{0}.

By part (2) of Lemma
4.2
, we have
\lambda(\nu_{n-1}(f))=\lambda({\mathfrak{d}}_{n})+\lambda(f_{0})
. Since
\mu(\pi_{n}(f_{0}))=\mu(f)=0
by part (1), it follows that
\mu(f_{0})=0
. From part (2), we obtain
\lambda(f)=\lambda(f_{0})
. Thus, part (4) follows from the fact that
\lambda({\mathfrak{d}}_{n})=2^{n-1}
.
∎

over

We determine the rank of

0\to{\mathrm{Sel}}_{\mathfrak{L}}(A/{\mathfrak{K}})\to H^{1}({\mathfrak{K}},A({\mathfrak{L}}))\to\prod_{v}H^{1}({\mathfrak{K}}_{v},A)({\mathfrak{L}}),

where the product runs over all places of
{\mathfrak{K}}
.
Observe that
{\mathrm{Sel}}_{\mathfrak{L}}(A/{\mathcal{K}})
is a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}[{\rm Gal}({\mathcal{K}}/K)]
-module.
Theorem
7.1
is a consequence of the following proposition.

Proposition 7.2
.

If
L(\overline{\psi_{A}\eta},1)\neq 0
, then
{\mathrm{Sel}}_{\mathfrak{L}}(A/{\mathcal{K}})^{\eta}
is finite.

For a finite extension
{\mathfrak{K}}/K
, we define another Selmer group
{\mathrm{Sel}}^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{L}}(A/{\mathfrak{K}})
by the exact sequence

0\to{\mathrm{Sel}}^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{L}}(A/{\mathfrak{K}})\to H^{1}({\mathfrak{K}},A({\mathfrak{L}}))\to\prod_{v\nmid{\mathfrak{L}}}H^{1}({\mathfrak{K}}_{v},A)({\mathfrak{L}}),

where the product runs over all primes of
{\mathfrak{K}}
not lying above
{\mathfrak{L}}
. Let
{\mathcal{F}}={\mathcal{K}}(A_{\mathfrak{L}})
and
\Delta={\rm Gal}({\mathcal{F}}/{\mathcal{K}})
. For a group
{\mathfrak{G}}
and a
{\mathfrak{G}}
-module
{\mathfrak{M}}
, we denote by
{\mathfrak{M}}^{\mathfrak{G}}
the submodule of
{\mathfrak{G}}
-invariant elements and
{\mathfrak{M}}_{\mathfrak{G}}
for the maximal quotient of
{\mathfrak{M}}
on which
{\mathfrak{G}}
acts trivially.

Lemma 7.3
.

The following statements hold:

(1)

The index of
{\mathrm{Sel}}_{\mathfrak{L}}(A/{\mathcal{K}})
in
{\mathrm{Sel}}^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{L}}(A/{\mathcal{K}})
is finite.

(2)

{\mathrm{Sel}}^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{L}}(A/{\mathcal{K}})\simeq({\mathrm{Sel}}^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{L}}(A/{\mathcal{F}}))^{\Delta}
.

(3)

Let
{\mathcal{F}}_{n}={\mathcal{K}}(A_{{\mathfrak{L}}^{n+1}})
for each integer
n\geq 0
, and set
{\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}={\mathcal{K}}(A({\mathfrak{L}}))
. Define

{\mathrm{Sel}}^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{L}}(A/{\mathcal{F}}_{\infty})=\lim_{n}{\mathrm{Sel}}^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{L}}(A/{\mathcal{F}}_{n}),

where the direct limit is taken with respect to the restriction maps. Let
\Gamma={\rm Gal}({\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}/{\mathcal{F}})
. Then

{\mathrm{Sel}}^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{L}}(A/{\mathcal{F}})\simeq{\mathrm{Sel}}^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{L}}(A/{\mathcal{F}}_{\infty})^{\Gamma}.

All claims in Lemma
7.3
are standard; see
[
3
]
(or
[
13
, Theorem 5.1]
and references therein) for detailed proofs. Let
{\mathcal{G}}={\rm Gal}({\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}/{\mathcal{K}})
and
G={\rm Gal}({\mathcal{K}}/K)
. Note that
\Gamma={\rm Gal}({\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}/{\mathcal{F}})\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}
. Since
\ell
is coprime to
[{\mathcal{F}}:K]
and unramified in
{\mathcal{K}}/K
, we have

{\rm Gal}({\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}/K)\simeq G\times{\mathcal{G}}.

Similarly,
{\mathcal{G}}\simeq\Gamma\times\Delta
. The following proposition will complete the proof of Proposition
7.2

Proposition 7.4
.

For each character
\eta
of
G
, if
L(\overline{\psi_{A}\eta},1)\neq 0
, then
({\mathrm{Sel}}^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{L}}(A/{\mathcal{F}}_{\infty})^{\mathcal{G}})^{\eta}
is finite.

Proof.

Let
X_{A}({\mathfrak{L}})
denote the Pontryagin dual of
{\mathrm{Sel}}^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{L}}(A/{\mathcal{F}}_{\infty})
. For a character
\eta
of
G
, let
X_{A}({\mathfrak{L}})^{\eta}
be its
\eta
-part. Let
\Lambda({\mathcal{G}})
denote the Iwasawa algebra of
{\mathcal{G}}
.
By a theorem of Coates (see
[
3
]
),
X_{A}({\mathfrak{L}})^{\eta}
is a finitely generated torsion
\Lambda({\mathcal{G}})
-module. Let
f_{A,\eta}
be a generator of the characteristic ideal of
X_{A}({\mathfrak{L}})^{\eta}
. Let
\mathscr{I}
be the completion of the ring of integers in the maximal unramified extension of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}
, and let
\Lambda_{\mathscr{I}}({\mathcal{G}})=\Lambda({\mathcal{G}})\widehat{\otimes}\mathscr{I}
. Denote by
\kappa
the character of
{\mathcal{G}}
giving its action on
A({\mathfrak{L}})
.
By the elliptic analogue of Iwasawa’s theorem for cyclotomic fields (this is due to Coates-Wiles, see
[
24
, Theorem 3.6]
or
[
11
, Theorem 4.12]
) together with Rubin’s proof of the Iwasawa main conjecture
[
25
]
,
the ideal
f_{A,\eta}\Lambda_{\mathscr{I}}({\mathcal{G}})
admits a generator
g_{{\mathfrak{L}},\eta}
satisfying

\kappa(g_{{\mathfrak{L}},\eta})=\left(1-\frac{\psi_{A}\eta({\mathfrak{L}})}{\ell}\right)\cdot\frac{L(\overline{\psi_{A}\eta},1)}{\Omega}
,
where
\Omega
is an
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
-generator of the period lattice of a minimal model of
A
over
K
. The hypothesis
L(\overline{\psi_{A}\eta},1)\neq 0
thus implies
\kappa(f_{A,\eta})\neq 0
. A standard result in Iwasawa theory (see
[
13
, Lemma 5.2]
) then shows that
(X_{A}({\mathfrak{L}})^{\eta})_{\mathcal{G}}
is finite. By duality, this is equivalent to the finiteness of
({\mathrm{Sel}}^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{L}}(A/{\mathcal{F}}_{\infty})^{\mathcal{G}})^{\eta}
.
