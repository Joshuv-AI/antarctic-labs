Integro-derivation Dzhumadildaev algebras: from the algebra of polynomials


Abstract
This paper introduces and investigates some properties of algebras constructed from the algebra of polynomials via derivation and integration operators using a process presented by Dzhumadildaev in an earlier work. In particular, we discover new classes of infinite-dimensional simple conservative algebras and describe the derivations of these algebras for ranks 1 1 and 2 2 .

derivation, Rota-Baxter operator, conservative algebra, algebra of polynomials.

Introduction

The idea of obtaining new objects from old ones by using derivative operations has long been known in algebra
[
2
]
. In its most general form, the idea was realized by Malcev
[
21
]
. Let
\rm M_{n}
be the algebra of matrices of order
n
over a field. Assume that some finite collection
\Lambda=\big(a_{ij},b_{ij},c_{ij}\big)
of matrices in
\rm M_{n}
is given. Denote by
\rm M^{(\Lambda)}_{n}
an algebra defined on a space of matrices in
\rm M_{n}
with respect to new multiplication

x\cdot_{\Lambda}y=\sum\limits_{i,j}a_{ij}xb_{ij}yc_{ij}.

It was proved that every
n
-dimensional algebra is isomorphic to a subalgebra of
\rm M^{(\Lambda)}_{n}

[
21
]
. Other interesting ways to derive the initial multiplication are isotopes, homotopes, and mutations
[
3
,
25
,
22
]
. The concept of an isotope was introduced by Albert
[
2
]
. Let algebras
\rm A
and
\rm A_{0}
have a common linear space on which right multiplication operators
R_{x}
and
R^{(0)}_{x}
are defined (for
\rm A
and
\rm A_{0}
, resp.). We say that
\rm A
and
\rm A_{0}
are isotopic if there exist invertible linear operators
\phi,\psi,\xi
such that
R^{(0)}_{x}=\phi R_{x\psi}\xi.
We call
\rm A_{0}
an isotope of
\rm A
. Another similar construction appears in the study of Novikov algebras and their generalizations. Namely, let
\rm A
be an associative commutative algebra with a derivation
d,
then a new multiplication
x\ast y=xd(y)
gives a structure of a Novikov algebra
[
11
]
. The present construction was generalized to the case of noncommutative Novikov algebras
[
24
]
and
\delta
-Novikov algebras
[
16
]
. On the other hand, an associative commutative algebra with a Rota-Baxter operator
R
gives a structure of a Zinbiel algebra under the multiplication
x*y=xR(y)

[
11
]
. Recently, in a paper of Dzhumadildaev
[
11
]
, an idea of mixing the two above-presented constructions was introduced. The present paper is dedicated to the study of non-associative algebras, which we call
integro-derivation Dzhumadildaev
algebras
\big(
see, Definition
2.1
\big),
obtained analogously by the construction of Dzhumadildaev given in
[
11
]
.

The algebra of restricted polynomials (also known as null-filiform associative algebra) is still a subject of interest
[
10
,
1
,
19
,
15
]
. The present paper is dedicated to the study of some algebras obtained from the
n
-dimensional algebra of restricted polynomials (and their unital versions) under a specific multiplication given by derivation-integration operators. Namely, we give the definition of algebras under our consideration in Proposition
2.1
and define the exact table of multiplications of algebras under our consideration in Definition
2.7
. Proposition
2.10
gives a characterization of nontrivial algebras under our consideration, and the next subsection
2.2
characterizes simple, perfect, and nilpotent algebras. Subsection
2.3
provides some new examples of simple conservative algebras. The second part of the paper is dedicated to the study of derivations of integro-derivation Dzhumadildaev algebras of ranks
1
and
2.
The description of algebras of derivations and their generalizations of associative and non-associative algebras is a classical, but still current problem
[
20
,
13
,
8
,
6
,
5
,
4
]
.

Notations
We do not provide some well-known definitions
\big(
such as definitions of Lie algebras, Leibniz algebras, nilpotent algebras, solvable algebras, etc.
\big)
, and refer the readers to consult previously published papers. For a set of vectors
S,
we denote by
\big\langle S\big\rangle
the vector space generated by
S.
For an algebra
{\rm A}
and an element

we denote the right multiplication on

In general, we are working in the complex field, but some results are applicable to other fields as well. We also always assume algebras under our consideration are nontrivial, i.e., they have nonzero multiplications.

l:={\rm lev}\big({\rm IDD}(K^{k}_{\infty},m_{1},m_{2})\big),
then

{\rm A}\diamond{\rm A}

=

\big\langle e_{k+(k-l)},\ldots,e_{n},\ldots\big\rangle

\subset

{\rm A};

{\rm A}\diamond\big({\rm A}\diamond{\rm A}\big)+\big({\rm A}\diamond{\rm A}\big)\diamond{\rm A}

=

\big\langle e_{k+2(k-l)},\ldots,e_{n},\ldots\big\rangle

\subset

{\rm A}\diamond{\rm A}.

Applying mathematical induction, we find that

{\rm A}^{\diamond t}:=\sum\limits_{i+j=t}{\rm A}^{\diamond i}\diamond{\rm A}^{\diamond j}=\big\langle e_{k+(t-1)(k-l)},\ldots,e_{n},\ldots\big\rangle.

Due to
l<k,
we have that
{\rm A}^{\diamond t_{1}}\subset{\rm A}^{\diamond t_{2}},
if
t_{1}>t_{2}.
It is easy to see that
\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}{\rm A}^{\diamond n}=\big\{0\big\},
i.e.,
{\rm A}
is pro-nilpotent.

2.3
Conservative
{\rm IDD}

In 1972, Kantor introduced conservative algebras as a generalization of Jordan algebras
\big(
also, see surveys about the study of conservative algebras and superalgebras
[
23
,
17
]
\big).

Definition 2.25

(see
[
14
]
)
.

A vector space
{\rm V}
with a multiplication
\cdot
is called a conservative algebra if there is a new multiplication
*:{\rm V}\times{\rm V}\rightarrow{\rm V}
such that

\big[L_{b}^{\cdot},[L_{a}^{\cdot},\cdot]\big]\ =\ -\big[L_{a*b}^{\cdot},\cdot\big],
where
\big[L_{c}^{\cdot},{\rm F}\big](x,y)\ =\ c\cdot{\rm F}(x,y)-{\rm F}(c\cdot x,y)-{\rm F}(x,c\cdot y).

In other words, the following identity holds for all
a,b,x,y\in{\rm V}
:

b\big(a(xy)-(ax)y-x(ay)\big)-a\big((bx)y\big)+\big(a(bx)\big)y+(bx)(ay)-\\
-a\big(x(by)\big)+(ax)(by)+x\big(a(by)\big)\ =\ -(a*b)(xy)+\big((a*b)x\big)y+x\big((a*b)y\big).

(1)

Proposition 2.26
.

{\rm IDD}(K^{k}_{\infty},m,0),
where
m\in\mathbb{Z},
is a left commutative algebra.

Proof 2.27
.

It follows from Proposition
2.7
.

Definition 2.28

(see
[
18
]
)
.

An algebra
{\rm A}
is called a generalized associative algebra if there exists one multiplication
F
defined on the same vector space, such that
a(bx)\ =\ F(a,b)x.

Theorem 2.29
.

For any
m\in\mathbb{Z},

{\rm IDD}(K^{k}_{\infty},m,0)
is conservative with an additional multiplication given by

e_{i}*e_{j}=\frac{(i+j-2m)!}{(i+j-m)!}\frac{i!}{(i-m)!}\frac{j!}{(j-m)!}e_{i+j-m}.

Proof 2.30
.

By a direct computation, we obtain that
{\rm IDD}(K^{k}_{\infty},m,0)
is a generalized associative algebra with additional multiplication
e_{i}*e_{j}
given in our statement. By Proposition
2.26
,
{\rm IDD}(K^{k}_{\infty},m,0)
is left commutative. Thanks to
[
18
]
, each left-commutative generalized associative algebra is conservative, hence
{\rm IDD}(K^{k}_{\infty},m,0)
is conservative.

Remark 2.31

Cantarini and Kac classified all complex linearly compact commutative and anticommutative conservative simple superalgebras

give a wide class of examples of non-(anti)commutative conservative simple algebras. It is known that
{\rm IDD}(K^{0}_{\infty},1,0)
is a Novikov algebra, and it seems that it firstly appeared in

Theorem 3.1

and

Firstly, we have

0

=

D(e_{0}\diamond e_{0})\ =\ D(e_{0})\diamond e_{0}+e_{0}\diamond D(e_{0})

=

\left(\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n}\alpha_{i}e_{i}\right)\diamond e_{0}+e_{0}\diamond\left(\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n}\alpha_{i}e_{i}\right)\ =\ \sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}i\alpha_{i}e_{i-1};

that immediately gives
\alpha_{k}=0
for
1\leq k\leq n
and
D(e_{0})=\alpha_{0}e_{0}.
Secondly,

D(e_{0})

=

D(e_{1}\diamond e_{0})\ =\ D(e_{1})\diamond e_{0}+e_{1}\diamond D(e_{0})

=

\left(\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n}\beta_{i}e_{i}\right)\diamond e_{0}+e_{1}\diamond\left(\alpha_{0}e_{0}\right)\ =\ \sum\limits_{i=0}^{n}i\beta_{i}e_{i-1}+\alpha_{0}e_{0}

=

\left(\alpha_{0}+\beta_{1}\right)e_{0}+\sum\limits_{i=2}^{n}i\beta_{i}e_{i-1};

that immediately gives
\beta_{k}=0
for
1\leq k\leq n,
i.e.,
D(e_{1})=\beta_{0}e_{0}.

Thirdly,

2D(e_{1})

=

D(e_{2}\diamond e_{0})\ =\ D(e_{2})\diamond e_{0}+e_{2}\diamond D(e_{0})

=

\left(\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n}\gamma_{i}e_{i}\right)\diamond e_{0}+e_{2}\diamond\left(\alpha_{0}e_{0}\right)

=

\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n}i\gamma_{i}e_{i-1}+2\alpha_{0}e_{1}\ =\ \gamma_{1}e_{0}+2(\alpha_{0}+\gamma_{2})e_{1}+\sum\limits_{i=3}^{n}i\gamma_{i}e_{i-1}.

that immediately gives
\gamma_{1}=2\beta_{0},\ \gamma_{2}=-\alpha_{0},\ \gamma_{k}=0,

3\leq k\leq n,

D(e_{2})\ =\ \gamma_{0}e_{0}+2\beta_{0}e_{1}-\alpha_{0}e_{2}.

Next,

D(e_{2})\ =\ \frac{1}{2}D(e_{2}\diamond e_{1})\ =\ \frac{1}{2}\left(\left(\gamma_{0}e_{0}+2\beta_{0}e_{1}-\alpha_{0}e_{2}\right)\diamond e_{1}+e_{2}\diamond\left(\beta_{0}e_{0}\right)\right)\ =\ \par 2\beta_{0}e_{1}-\alpha_{0}e_{2};

that immediately gives
\gamma_{0}=0,

D(e_{0})=\alpha_{0}e_{0},\ D(e_{1})=\beta_{0}e_{0},\ D(e_{2})=2\beta_{0}e_{1}-\alpha_{0}e_{2}.

It is easy to see that
\varphi(e_{i})=(i-1)e_{i}
and
\phi(e_{i})=ie_{i-1}
are derivations of
{\rm IDD}(K^{0}_{n},1,0)

\varphi(e_{i}\diamond e_{j})

=

i(i+j-2)e_{i+j-1}\ =\ i(i-1)e_{i+j-1}+i(j-1)e_{i+j-1}

=

(i-1)e_{i}\diamond e_{j}+(j-1)e_{i}\diamond e_{j}\ =\ \varphi(e_{i})\diamond e_{j}+e_{i}\diamond\varphi(e_{j})

and

\phi(e_{i}\diamond e_{j})

=

i(i+j-1)e_{i+j-2}\ =\ i(i-1)e_{i+j-2}+ije_{i+j-2}

Then replacing
D
by
D+\alpha_{0}\varphi-\beta_{0}\phi
we can suppose that
D(e_{1})=D(e_{2})=0
.

Furthermore, using the induction method, we prove that
D(e_{k})=0
for
k\geq 2.
To do this, let us consider

It follows that each derivation of
{\rm IDD}(K^{0}_{n},1,0)
is a linear combination of
\varphi
and
\phi
, that completes the proof of the statement.
