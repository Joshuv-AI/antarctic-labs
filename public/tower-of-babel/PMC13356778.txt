Transcriptional–secretory uncoupling in RDEB skin creates a non-permissive microenvironment for sensory nerve regeneration
Authors: Paula Diaz, Carolina Flores-Muñoz, Margarita Calvo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractRecessive dystrophic epidermolysis bullosa (RDEB) is a severe skin fragility disorder caused by mutations in COL7A1 gene, leading to chronic injury, inflammation, and debilitating sensory symptoms, including pain and itch. While structural defects in the dermo-epidermal junction are well characterized, the mechanisms underlying impaired sensory reinnervation and neuropathic manifestations remain poorly understood. Here, we investigated whether defective reinnervation in RDEB is driven by intrinsic neuronal deficits or by alterations in the cutaneous microenvironment. Using a prospective cohort of RDEB patients with small fiber neuropathy (SFN), combined with high-resolution digital PCR and multiplex cytokine profiling, we analyzed the transcriptional and secretory responses of wounded skin and primary keratinocytes. RDEB tissue exhibited a markedly blunted transcriptional response to injury, with failure to induce key inflammatory, proteolytic, and axonal guidance genes, despite a sustained pro-inflammatory secretome characterized by elevated IL-6, TNF-α, IL-1β, CCL2, and MMP9. Functionally, RDEB blister fluid induced growth cone collapse and impaired neurite outgrowth in sensory neurons. These findings reveal a dissociation between transcriptional activation and extracellular signalling, resulting in a non-permissive niche for nerve regeneration. We propose that this altered microenvironment simultaneously impairs reinnervation and promotes maladaptive nociceptive signalling, providing a mechanistic link between chronic denervation and neuropathic pain in RDEB.Keywords: RDEB1, Keratinocytes2, Small fiber neuropathy3, Peripheral nervous system4

Introduction

Recessive Dystrophic Epidermolysis Bullosa (RDEB) is a devastating socio-genetic skin fragility disorder caused by biallelic mutations in the COL7A1 gene, which encodes type VII collagen (C7). The absence or dysfunction of C7 leads to the failure of anchoring fibrils at the dermo-epidermal junction (DEJ), resulting in chronic blistering, exuberant scarring, a high risk of squamous cell carcinoma and fibrosis (Dang and Murrell, 2008). While the mechanical instability of RDEB skin is well-characterized, the neurological complications specifically chronic pain and itch remain profoundly debilitating and poorly understood in terms of its mechanisms.

Recent clinical evidence indicates that patients with RDEB frequently develop Small Fiber Neuropathy (SFN), characterized by a significant reduction in intraepidermal nerve fiber density (IENFD) (von Bischhoffshausen et al., 2017). Under normal physiological conditions, the skin microenvironment acts as a dynamic signalling hub where keratinocytes and denervated epidermis secrete neurotrophic factors and cytokines to guide the reinnervation of sensory axons following injury (Terenghi, 1999; Talagas et al., 2020a, Talagas et al., 2020b). However, in RDEB, the persistent state of tissue trauma and chronic inflammation may create a non-responsive or inhibitory microenvironment that prevents successful sensory regeneration or an intrinsic inability of the skin to modulate its genes after damage.

The interaction between epidermal keratinocytes and sensory neurons is critical for cutaneous homeostasis. Upon injury, healthy keratinocytes release a specific secretome involving mediators such as NGF, CCL2, and various interleukins that orchestrate both wound healing and axonal sprout guidance (Z. Liu et al., 2021; El-Serafi et al., 2022; Piipponen et al., 2020). In RDEB, the proteomic and transcriptomic landscape of the skin is chronically altered by TGF-β signalling and matrix metalloproteinase (MMP) dysregulation (Akasaka et al., 2021; Nyström et al., 2025). Recent studies show that impaired neurotrophic support from RDEB keratinocytes contributes to defective epidermal reinnervation (Diaz et al., 2026), specifically demonstrating that these cells fail to upregulate and secrete essential survival and elongation factors, such as NGF and GDNF, following skin injury. Building upon this mechanism, it remains unclear whether the failure of reinnervation in RDEB is strictly limited to this neurotrophic deficiency, or if it reflects a broader failure of the epidermal niche to provide the necessary molecular cues—including inflammatory and spatial guidance signals—to trigger repair.

In this study, we investigated whether impaired reinnervation in RDEB reflects intrinsic defects in the nerve or alterations in the epidermal niche that may fail to provide appropriate molecular cues for repair. We explored the possibility that RDEB skin exhibits a limited or dysregulated transcriptional response to injury, potentially affecting the ability of keratinocytes to dynamically modulate gene expression. Using a prospective cohort of RDEB patients with confirmed SFN, and high-sensitivity approaches including dPCR and multiplex cytokine assays, we examined the transcriptional and secretory responses of RDEB keratinocytes in primary culture following standardized minor injury. Our findings aim to provide insight into how an altered or insufficiently responsive microenvironment may contribute to impaired sensory nerve regeneration and chronic pain in mechanobullous diseases.

Materials and methods

Participants

This prospective cohort study assessed epidermal reinnervation after minor skin injury in patients with RDEB and SFN versus age-and sex-matched healthy controls. Inclusion criteria for RDEB participants included age > 18 years, genetic confirmation of mutation on the COL7A1 gene, and SFN diagnosis (defined as alterations in thermal thresholds on quantitative sensory testing and reduced intraepidermal nerve fiber density on skin biopsy); secondary neuropathies were excluded. Six RDEB patients (recruited via DebRA-Chile) and six controls were enrolled. At baseline, a standardized injury consisting of four superficial incisions within a 1.5 cm area was performed on the distal leg. After ten days, 3 mm punch biopsies were collected from the injured and contralateral uninjured sites for RNA processing. The protocol was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee (No. 210622019), and all subjects provided informed consent. This design was intended to explore differences in the cutaneous response to injury between groups.

Human skin biopsy collection and RNA extraction

Skin biopsy samples were immediately stored at −80 °C until processing. Total RNA was extracted from the tissue using the RNeasy Fibrous Tissue Mini Kit (QIAGEN), according to the manufacturer's protocol. RNA concentration was measured via spectrophotometry. Subsequently, cDNA was synthesized from 500 ng of total RNA using the Omniscript RT Kit (QIAGEN).

Digital PCR

Absolute quantification of cDNA from skin biopsies (healthy and RDEB volunteers) was performed using the QIAcuity EG PCR Kit on a QIAcuity One 5-plex Digital PCR System (QIAGEN). Reaction mixtures (12 μL) containing 1 μL of cDNA were loaded into QIAcuity Nanoplate 8.5 k 24-well plates (QIAGEN). The cycling conditions involved reverse transcription at 50 °C for 40 min, followed by activation at 95 °C for 2 min. Amplification consisted of 40 two-step cycles: denaturation at 95 °C for 10 s and annealing/extension at 55–60 °C for 30 s, concluding with a 5 min hold at 40 °C. Data acquisition was performed via the green channel (500 ms exposure, gain 3) and analyzed using the QIAcuity Software Suite (QIAGEN). Housekeeping gene normalization was not applied because digital PCR (dPCR) provides absolute target quantification (copies/μL). In accordance with the Digital MIQE Guidelines (Huggett et al., 2013), normalization to reference genes is not required in dPCR when upstream template loading is robustly standardized. To account for input variability, standardization was strictly maintained upstream by using equivalent amounts of total RNA for all reverse transcription reactions (500 ng), and identical volumes of the resulting cDNA were processed under identical dPCR experimental conditions. Therefore, comparisons between groups were performed directly using absolute transcript copy numbers. Positive/negative partition discrimination was determined through automated thresholding, with final results expressed as copies/μL of loaded RNA. Primer sequences are detailed below.

Isolation and culturing of epidermal keratinocyte

Human keratinocytes were isolated from 3-mm skin biopsies collected from the lower legs of three healthy and three patients with RDEB donors. All participants provided written informed consent. Protocols were approved by the Ethics Committee of the Pontificia Universidad Católica de Chile (No. 210622019) and conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Keratinocytes were isolated immediately following previously described methods (Diaz et al., 2026). Briefly, skin samples were minced with a sterile scalpel and incubated in 0.25% trypsin (Gibco) at 37 °C for 20 min. The resulting pellet was resuspended in a collagenase solution (1×) prepared in culture media (DMEM: Ham's F12 [3:1]) supplemented with 10% FBS, 10 ng/mL EGF, 10 − 10 M cholera toxin, 0.4 μg/mL hydrocortisone, 5 μg/mL transferrin, 5 μg/mL insulin, 13 ng/mL liothyronine, and antibiotics (1× Fungizone, Pen/Strep, and Gentamicin, Gibco). After overnight incubation at 37 °C in a humidified incubator under 5% CO2 atmosphere and 99% humidity, the suspension was centrifuged at 300 ×g for 5 min. In subsequent days, fibroblasts were selectively removed to promote enriched keratinocyte growth.

Conditioned medium from human epidermal keratinocyte culture

The keratinocytes were seeded in 6-well plates at a density of 4 × 105 cells/well. At confluence, the monolayer was scratched using a 200 μL pipette tip and washed with medium to remove detached cells. The post-scratch conditioned medium was collected, clarified at 500 ×g for 5 min, and either used immediately or stored at −80 °C. Next, the media were centrifuged at 400 ×g for 10 min to remove whole cells. The resulting conditioned media were filtered through a 0.22-μm membrane, concentrated 10-fold (v/v) using 3-kDa molecular weight cut-off (MWCO) centrifugal filter (Millipore), and stored at −80 °C for qPCR and multiplex cytokine assays.

Multiplex cytokine assays

Cytokines, chemokines, and inflammatory mediators (CCL2/JE/MCP-1, IL-6, IL-10, MMP9, IL-1β, IL-13, MMP2 and TNF-α) were evaluated from two sets: 1. The contents of active blisters on RDEB patients compared to fresh frozen plasma from healthy volunteers, 2. The conditioned media of cultured primary keratinocytes obtained from healthy and RDEB. Detection assays were conducted using a multiplex immunoassay platform with magnetic beads coated with labeled antibodies, employing the Human Magnetic Luminex Assays (Premixed Multiplex, LXSAHM-10, R&D Systems), following the manufacturer's instructions. All samples were analyzed in duplicate and blinded to treatment assignments. Plate was read using a Luminex 200 xMAP (Luminex Corporation). The limit of detection for cytokines measured ranged from 1.0 to 104.440 pg/mL, according to the manufacturer's instructions.

Collection of plasma and RDEB blister fluid

Ten healthy volunteers and ten patients with RDEB were recruited following written informed consent and approval by the local ethics committee. Peripheral blood was collected from healthy donors into plasma separation tubes, was used as a control biological fluid because it contains circulating proteins, growth factors, cytokines, and extracellular matrix components that are also present in wound exudates and blister fluid, while lacking the chronic inflammatory and proteolytic signature characteristic of RDEB lesions (Esposito et al., 2016; Lehr et al., 2022), processed according to the manufacturer's instructions, and plasma aliquots were stored at −80 °C until use. Blister fluid was collected from intact RDEB skin blisters by sterile needle aspiration and transferred into microcentrifuge tubes. Samples were centrifuged, when necessary, to remove cellular debris and stored at −80 °C until analysis. All samples were thawed immediately before experimental use.

Neurite outgrowth in DRG and exposure to RDEB blister fluid

Dorsal root ganglia (DRG) explants were prepared from E16.5 rat embryos as previously described (López-Leal et al., 2018). DRGs were dissected from the spinal cord, stripped of attached roots and nerves, and maintained as whole explants. DRGs from 10 to 15 embryos were pooled per experiment, and each pregnant dam was considered one biological replicate. Explants were cultured on poly-l-lysine/collagen-coated 24-well plates in Neurobasal medium supplemented with B27, GlutaMAX, antibiotic-antimycotic solution, aphidicolin, 5-fluoro-2′-deoxyuridine, and 50 ng/mL NGF (Gibco, ThermoFisher Scientific). At 5 days in vitro (DIV5), cultures were exposed to healthy donor fresh frozen plasma (FFP) or RDEB blister fluid at 1% or 2.5% (v/v). After 72 h (DIV8), explants were fixed and stained with acetylated tubulin (Tub-Ac) and DAPI. Fluorescence images were acquired, and an axonal growth area was quantified using ImageJ (version 1.54p) and normalized to the ganglion soma area. Experiments were performed using samples from 10 independent plasma or blister fluid donors, with three independent biological replicates and 3–6 technical replicates per condition.

Statistical analysis

All analyses were performed using the GraphPad Prism 10 software; data distribution was assessed using normality tests. Normally distributed data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD), whereas non-normally distributed data are presented as median and interquartile range (IQR). Parametric or non-parametric tests were applied accordingly. Comparisons between two groups were performed using unpaired t-tests or Mann–Whitney U tests, as appropriate. A two-tailed p-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Transcriptional dysregulation in RDEB skin after skin wounding

During physiological wound healing, skin cells produce a range of cytokines and inflammatory mediators that contribute to tissue repair and sensory nerve reinnervation. To investigate their role in small fiber neuropathy associated with RDEB, we performed RT-dPCR to characterize the expression profile of these mediators. At recruitment, four superficial linear incisions (each 1.5 cm in length) were made on the distal leg, arranged in a crossed pattern with both parallel and perpendicular orientations. After ten days, 3 mm punch biopsies were collected from the injured and contralateral uninjured sites for RNA processing. To characterize the molecular response to mechanical injury, we utilized digital PCR (dPCR) to quantify the absolute mRNA expression (copies/μL) of key inflammatory, proteolytic, and guidance molecules in intact (uninjured skin, green column) and wounded (post-injured skin, purple column) skin samples from healthy controls and RDEB patients.

The transcriptional activation of key cytokines following wounding was significantly blunted in RDEB skin compared to the robust response observed in healthy controls. In healthy skin, wounding induced a significant upregulation of IL-6 mRNA and TNFα mRNA (Fig. 1). However, this inductive response was absent in RDEB skin, where mRNA levels remained statistically unchanged between intact and wounded states. While healthy skin showed a significant increase in IL-10 mRNA expression upon wounding (Fig. 1), RDEB skin failed to mount a similar regulatory response. Consistent with the expected resolution of the inflammatory phase, IL-1β expression was not significantly increased 10 days after injury in healthy skin, nor was it upregulated in RDEB skin. The induction of matrix metalloproteinases, essential for keratinocyte migration and extracellular matrix (ECM) remodelling, was markedly suppressed in RDEB tissue. Healthy skin exhibited a marked transcriptional increase in MMP9 mRNA following wounding (Fig. 1). In striking contrast, RDEB skin showed no significant induction of MMP9 transcripts for post-injury. In accordance with the temporal dynamics of matrix remodelling, wounded RDEB skin expressed significantly lower levels of MMP2 mRNA compared to wounded healthy tissue, suggesting an impaired activation of proteolytic pathways required for efficient repair. This pattern is consistent with the known temporal regulation of gelatinases during wound healing, whereby MMP2 peaks early and declines, while MMP9 expression is sustained into later phases of repair. This further indicates an overall failure of the proteolytic machinery required for efficient repair. Consistent with their role in matrix deposition and remodelling, both TGFB1 and TGFB3 remained elevated at 10 days after injury in healthy skin, whereas no upregulation was observed in RDEB skin.

Among axon guidance molecules, SEMA3A and SEMA5A were upregulated following wounding n healthy skin, whereas SEMA3E was not significantly altered. However, RDEB skin failed to upregulate these axonal guidance molecules after injury. Both NEO1 and DCC were upregulated 10 days after injury in healthy skin, consistent with ongoing tissue remodelling, whereas no induction was observed in RDEB skin. These results show that RDEB skin is characterized by a transcriptional failure in response to wounding. Unlike healthy skin, which mounts a coordinated induction of pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, TNF), remodelling proteases (MMPs), and tissue remodelling and axonal guidance factors (TGFB1, TGFB3, SEMA3A, SEMA5A, NEO1, and DCC), RDEB skin shows a comparatively blunted or suppressed transcriptional response. Notably, this occurs in the absence of sustained IL-1β expression at this late timepoint, consistent with resolution of the inflammatory phase in healthy tissue. These observations led us to hypothesize that such a diminished response may contribute to the chronic denervation observed in RDEB skin.

Pro-inflammatory and proteolytic signature in RDEB blister fluid

To assess the localized inflammatory milieu in RDEB, we analyzed the protein composition of blister fluid from RDEB patients and compared it with plasma from healthy subjects. Plasma from healthy donors was used as a reference for baseline circulating protein levels, enabling us to evaluate the extent to which inflammatory and proteolytic mediators are locally enriched within RDEB lesions. Importantly, these protein measurements reflect a distinct biological compartment and may not directly mirror transcriptional changes observed in skin tissue, as they integrate contributions from multiple cell types, extracellular release, and temporal dynamics of protein accumulation. Levels of TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β, and IL-13 were significantly increased in RDEB blister fluid compared to what is found in plasma from healthy donors (Fig. 2). We identified a differential pattern in gelatinase expression. MMP9 levels were significantly higher in RDEB blister fluid (Fig. 2), whereas MMP2 levels were significantly lower compared to controls (Fig. 2), suggesting a proteolytic imbalance. The chemokine CCL2 (MCP-1) was also markedly elevated in RDEB samples (Fig. 2), consistent with enhanced signalling for the recruitment of inflammatory cells. Overall, these findings indicate that the RDEB microenvironment is characterized by increased levels of pro-inflammatory mediators and altered MMP expression.

Secretory profile of RDEB keratinocytes in vitro

To determine the specific cellular contribution to this inflammatory milieu, we analyzed conditioned media from primary keratinocyte cultures (RDEB and healthy controls) during a 72-h in vitro scratch assay. Our results suggest that RDEB contributes to the production of the inflammatory mediators observed in vivo. RDEB keratinocytes consistently secreted higher levels of TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-1β compared with healthy controls at all time points (0, 24, 48, and 72-h) (Fig. 3). Notably, IL-6 secretion in RDEB cultures was approximately two-fold higher than in controls at 72 h. Consistent with the blister fluid data, CCL2 and MMP9 were significantly upregulated in RDEB keratinocyte-conditioned media (KCM) throughout the assay, with MMP9 showing a marked increase immediately after the scratch (0 h) and remaining elevated (Fig. 3). Interestingly, while MMP2 was barely detectable at baseline, its secretion increased over time in both groups (Fig. 3). However, healthy keratinocytes produced significantly more MMP2 than RDEB cells between 24 and 72 h, mirroring relative reduction observed in the blister fluid (Fig. 3). In contrast to the pro-inflammatory markers, IL-13 levels remained stable and showed no significant differences between healthy and RDEB keratinocytes, suggesting that the Th2-related pathway may not be primarily driven by keratinocytes in this model.

Functional impact of the RDEB microenvironment on sensory nerve growth

To determine whether the pro-inflammatory and proteolytic milieu observed in RDEB skin may contribute to impaired epidermal reinnervation, we evaluated the functional impact of RDEB blister fluid on sensory nerve outgrowth ex vivo. Embryonic rat DRG explants were cultured for 5 days to allow robust neurite extension, followed by a 72-h exposure to either RDEB blister fluid or fresh frozen plasma (FFP) from healthy donors (Fig. 4). Under basal conditions or when exposed to healthy FFP, sensory neurons maintained a typical cytoarchitecture, characterized by extended neurites and active, actin-rich growth cones (Fig. 4A). In contrast, exposure to RDEB blister fluid was associated with morphological alterations in a concentration-dependent manner. Neurons treated with the RDEB secretome exhibited growth cone collapse, characterized by a reduction in growth cone area and disorganization of the actin cytoskeleton (Fig. 4A).

Discussion

In this study, we provide convergent molecular, cellular, and functional evidence that the cutaneous microenvironment in recessive dystrophic epidermolysis bullosa (RDEB) is profoundly altered in a manner that is not permissive for sensory nerve regeneration. Importantly, these alterations are not only structural but also directly relevant to peripheral sensory dysfunction and pain processing. Rather than reflecting a simple inflammatory state, our findings support a model of a dysregulated and partially uncoupled response to injury, characterized by a dissociation between inflammation, extracellular matrix (ECM) remodelling, and axonal guidance signalling. This uncoupling likely contributes to the persistent denervation observed in RDEB skin. Consistent with this, RDEB skin exhibits an attenuated transcriptional activation following injury alongside a sustained pro-inflammatory extracellular milieu, suggesting that cellular outputs do not fully reflect the transcriptional dynamics observed in vivo. In a sensory context, such mismatch may promote both structural failure of reinnervation and maladaptive nociceptive signalling.

A first key observation is that the RDEB microenvironment is strongly pro-inflammatory, yet this inflammatory signature appears maladaptive. Both RDEB blister fluid and keratinocyte-conditioned media demonstrated consistently elevated levels of IL-6, TNF-α, and IL-1β, along with increased CCL2, indicating a sustained state of immune activation and recruitment of inflammatory cells. Importantly, our in vitro data suggest that keratinocytes themselves are a major contributor to this aberrant cytokine milieu, as RDEB keratinocytes displayed persistently elevated secretion of these mediators following mechanical injury. These findings are in line with the concept of keratinocytes as active immunomodulatory cells in chronic skin disease (Klimitz et al., 2025) but further indicate that in RDEB this response may become chronic and dysregulated. Functionally, exposure to RDEB blister fluid induced growth cone collapse and cytoskeletal disorganization in sensory neurons, supporting the idea that this inflammatory niche is not only sustained but also directly detrimental to axonal stability and extension (Zipp et al., 2023; Kolter et al., 2025). Inflammatory mediators are known to promote local oxidative stress (Morris et al., 2022), disrupt calcium homeostasis (Michalak and Michalak, 2025), and alter cellular energy metabolism (Willemen et al., 2023), ultimately leading to activation of axonal caspases and degeneration (Cusack et al., 2013). Consistent with this, acute and chronic blister fluid in epidermolysis bullosa has been shown to contain high levels of chemokines (CXCL8, CXCL1/2/3, CCL2, CCL4 and CCL5) and cytokines (IL-6, IL-1β, TNF-α) (Alexeev et al., 2017), which may directly affect axons or indirectly promote neurotoxicity through immune cell recruitment and oxidative stress. Notably, similar increases in inflammatory cytokines have also been reported in other small fiber neuropathies, suggesting that this pro-inflammatory milieu is not unique to RDEB (Uçeyler et al., 2010). In addition to impairing regeneration, sustained exposure to these cytokines is a well-recognized driver of peripheral sensitization, lowering nociceptor activation thresholds and promoting aberrant pain signalling. Rather than promoting repair, this environment may therefore exert neurotoxic or growth-inhibitory effects on regenerating fibres.

In contrast to this heightened inflammatory tone of the blister and the keratinocyte conditioned media, we observed a marked failure to activate key regenerative and axonal guidance pathways in full-thickness RDEB skin following injury. In healthy skin, injury triggered a temporally appropriate response marked by transient induction of pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, TNF), resolution of inflammatory cytokine signalling (including IL-1β), activation of proteolytic pathways (MMP9), and sustained engagement of remodelling and axonal guidance signals, including TGFB1, TGFB3, semaphorins, and their receptors (NEO1 and DCC). This pattern is consistent with a transition from inflammation to tissue remodelling and reinnervation. (Zheng et al., 2022). However, in full-thickness RDEB skin we detected a generalized downregulation of axon guidance molecules. Given the established roles of these molecules in directing axonal growth, target recognition, and tissue patterning (Dickson, 2002), their downregulation suggests that regenerating fibres in RDEB skin may lack the necessary positional and directional cues. In addition, we have previously reported in RDEB keratinocytes a lack of induction of neurotrophic factors that support axonal growth (Diaz et al., 2026). Therefore, beyond exposure to a hostile inflammatory environment, sensory axons in RDEB may also face a deficit of instructive signals required for reinnervation, resulting in ineffective or misdirected growth. Such miswiring or incomplete target reinnervation has been closely linked to the development of neuropathic pain states in peripheral nerve injury models. It should be noted that these transcriptional analyses were performed in whole skin biopsies and therefore may not capture cell-type-specific contributions; however, the consistent patterns observed across complementary protein and in vitro datasets support the robustness of this dysregulated response, a finding that will require further resolution using cell-type-resolved approaches such as single-cell profiling.

A third major component of the altered RDEB microenvironment is a profound imbalance in proteolytic activity and ECM remodelling. Efficient axonal regeneration requires tightly regulated protease activity to allow growth cones to navigate through the basement membrane and interstitial matrix (Santiago-Medina et al., 2015; Andries et al., 2017). Consistent with previous literature, we observed increased expression of both MMP9 and MMP2 following wounding (Koppel et al., 2014; Kirgiz et al., 2026; Page-McCaw et al., 2007). In contrast, RDEB skin failed to induce MMP9 transcription following injury and exhibited reduced levels of MMP2 in wounded tissue. Notably, this transcriptional profile contrasts with the protein landscape observed in blister fluid and keratinocyte-conditioned media, where MMP9 levels were elevated and MMP2 was relatively reduced. This apparent divergence likely reflects differences in the temporal and cellular context captured by each approach (Legendre et al., 2026). While transcriptional analyses primarily assess the inducible response of resident skin cells following injury (captured here at day 10), protein measurements in blister fluid represent the cumulative output of multiple cell types, including keratinocytes, infiltrating immune cells, and damaged tissue. In addition, sustained inflammation may promote protein accumulation through increased secretion, stability, or extracellular retention despite a limited capacity for dynamic gene induction. Together with our in vitro findings showing persistent MMP9 secretion by RDEB keratinocytes, these data support a decoupling between transcriptional regulation and downstream secretory activity, resulting in a spatially and temporally disorganized proteolytic environment. Increased extracellular MMP9 in the absence of a coordinated transcriptional program may contribute to excessive or mislocalized matrix degradation, while reduced MMP2 may impair structured remodelling processes (Chen et al., 2023). Together, these alterations point to an ECM environment that is structurally unstable yet functionally restrictive, potentially limiting effective growth cone penetration and tissue repair. Such defective matrix remodelling may further impair trophic support and contribute indirectly to sensory fiber dysfunction and altered mechanosensory signalling.

In addition to transcriptional regulation, MMP9 expression is also tightly controlled at the level of mRNA translation, including through MNK–eIF4E–dependent signalling pathways (Gkogkas et al., 2014). Notably, this pathway has been implicated in inflammatory pain modulation (Kawasaki et al., 2008), raising the possibility that enhanced MMP9 protein levels in RDEB may arise, at least in part, from post-transcriptional mechanisms. MMP9 has also been reported to participate in the processing and maturation of multiple cytokines (Yabluchanskiy et al., 2013), suggesting that its upregulation could amplify local inflammatory signalling. Together, these observations raise the hypothesis that MNK-dependent translational control of MMP9 may represent a mechanistically relevant and potentially druggable pathway in RDEB, although this remains to be directly tested.

Importantly, these three axes—chronic inflammation, impaired guidance signalling, and disordered ECM remodelling—are not independent but likely interact to define a non-permissive neuro-regenerative niche. Chronic exposure to inflammatory cytokines may further suppress regenerative gene programs (Stewart et al., 2025; Zigmond, 2012), while inadequate ECM remodelling may prevent the proper presentation and distribution of guidance cues (Chen et al., 2023). Conversely, the absence of appropriate guidance signals may render regenerating axons more vulnerable to inflammatory damage (T. Liu et al., 2024). Our functional data, demonstrating growth cone collapse upon exposure to RDEB blister fluid, provide direct support for the physiological relevance of this integrated model. Collectively, these interacting mechanisms provide a framework linking impaired regeneration with ongoing nociceptive dysfunction.

These findings have potential implications for understanding the pathophysiology of sensory dysfunction and pain in RDEB. Chronic denervation and failed reinnervation may contribute to altered sensory processing, including both hypoesthesia and neuropathic pain (Mauermann and staff, 2026). Rather than being driven solely by inflammation, these alterations may arise from a combination of axonal loss, impaired regenerative capacity, and maladaptive signalling within the skin microenvironment. This coexistence of sensory loss and pathological pain is a hallmark of small fiber neuropathies and supports a model in which structural degeneration and aberrant regeneration occur in parallel (Oaklander and Nolano, 2019). From a therapeutic perspective, this suggests that targeting a single pathway, such as inflammation alone, may be insufficient. Instead, strategies aimed at restoring a permissive regenerative environment, including modulation of ECM remodelling and reactivation of axonal guidance pathways, may be required.

Some limitations should be acknowledged. The sample size is necessarily limited given the rarity of RDEB, and the use of ex vivo and in vitro models may not fully capture the complexity of the in vivo environment. In addition, while our data demonstrate associations between the RDEB secretome and impaired neurite outgrowth, we cannot attribute these effects to specific mediators. Future studies using targeted inhibition or supplementation approaches will be necessary to establish causal relationships. Moreover, longitudinal analyses will be required to determine how these molecular alterations evolve over the course of disease and wound healing.

In conclusion, our study identifies a coordinated disruption of inflammatory, proteolytic, and axonal guidance pathways in RDEB skin. Together, these alterations define a microenvironment that is not only chronically inflamed but fundamentally non-permissive for sensory nerve regeneration. This integrated model provides a mechanistic framework to understand chronic denervation in RDEB and may inform the development of therapeutic strategies aimed at restoring cutaneous nerve integrity.
