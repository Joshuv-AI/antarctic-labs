The role of ESRRA and JUNB in oxyphil cells: insights from spatial transcriptomics and single-cell transcriptomics in uremic secondary hyperparathyroidism
Authors: Zeyu Zhang, Xiangyuan Qiu, Botao Sun, Xinying Li, Fada Xia
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractOxyphil cells are a primary cellular component of the parathyroid gland. Due to the limitations of techniques for separating oxyphil cells from chief cells, the characteristics and functions of oxyphil cells in uremic secondary hyperparathyroidism (SHPT) remain largely unclear. Therefore, we integrated spatial transcriptomics and single-cell transcriptomics to investigate the characteristics of oxyphil cells and the association between oxyphil cells and calcitriol resistance in SHPT. 6 uremic SHPT samples and 3 normal parathyroid samples were used for single-cell transcriptomics, while the most suitable SHPT sample was used for spatial transcriptomics. By integrating spatial transcriptomics with single-cell transcriptomics data (93073 cells), we identified a subset of 7653 cells with high confidence as oxyphil cells. Both transcriptomic data indicated a higher mitochondrial transcript proportion in oxyphil cells, and further investigation revealed that the transcription factor estrogen related receptor alpha (ESRRA), participated in mitochondrial biogenesis in these cells. Meanwhile, lower VDR expression and calcitriol resistance were observed in oxyphil cells. Further investigation demonstrated that the transcription factor JUNB promoted the transcription of VDR gene by binding to the promoter area of VDR gene, thus ameliorating calcitriol resistance of oxyphil cells in SHPT. These findings offer reliable evidence and new insights into the characteristics of oxyphil cells and their potential role in SHPT, and provide JUNB as a potential target for calcitriol resistance in SHPT.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s13062-026-00876-3.Keywords: Oxyphil cells, Chief cells, Mitochondrial biogenesis, Calcitriol, Secondary hyperparathyroidism

Background

As a common complication of chronic kidney disease (CKD), secondary hyperparathyroidism (SHPT) is characterized by parathyroid hyperplasia and excessive synthesis and secretion of parathyroid hormone (PTH), leading to bone disorders, cardiovascular morbidity, and diminished quality of life of CKD patients [1]. Although parathyroidectomy demonstrates definitive efficacy in treating SHPT, calcimimetics or vitamin D receptor agonists (VDRAs) serve as an important alternative therapeutic agents when parathyroidectomy is unfeasible or unsuccessful [2]. However, certain patients exhibit resistance to these treatments, which may be associated with the low expression of calcium-sensing receptor (CASR) and vitamin D receptor (VDR), while an increased drug dosage may cause severe side effects [3, 4]. Therefore, further elucidating the mechanism of drug resistance and dysregulated receptors may improve the effectiveness of medical treatment and help the management of SHPT patients.

In parathyroid gland, PTH secreting cells primarily consist of chief cells and oxyphil cells [5]. Similar to chief cells, oxyphil cells also express key parathyroid markers, including VDR, CASR, PTH, and GCM2 [6]. Oxyphil cells are distinguished by eosinophilic cytoplasm and elevated mitochondrial content. Although the association between the defects of cytochrome-c-oxidase and the increased mitochondria number in oxyphil cells has been investigated [7], the specific mechanisms underlying the increased mitochondria in oxyphil cells remain largely unknown.

Studies have demonstrated a significant increase of oxyphil cells in SHPT, suggesting that oxyphil cells may play a potential role in the progression of SHPT [8, 9]. The expressions of CASR and VDR in parathyroid cells are crucial factors affecting the effectiveness of therapeutic agents. Nevertheless, the characteristics of oxyphil cells remain a subject of debate. Some studies identified the downregulation of CASR and VDR in oxyphil cells [5, 10]. However, other studies have revealed higher level or no difference in CASR and VDR expression between chief cells and oxyphil cells [9, 11]. Therefore, there is an urgent need to further investigate the characteristics of oxyphil cells and the association between oxyphil cells and drug resistance in SHPT.

These conflicting results are mainly attributed to the limitations of experimental techniques in separating oxyphil cells and chief cells. Even the single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) may have certain limitations because there is no specific cell marker to separate oxyphil cells from chief cells. Therefore, in this study, we employed spatial transcriptomics and scRNA-seq to explore the characteristics of oxyphil cells in SHPT, considering hematoxylin and eosin (HE) staining is the gold standard to identify oxyphil cells. The association between oxyphil cells and calcitriol resistance was also investigated. This study may contribute to further clarifying the characteristics of oxyphil cells, revealing their functions, and uncovering underlying mechanisms in SHPT.

Methods

Additional details for all methods are in the Supplementary Methods.

Patient enrollment and tissue collection

Thirty parathyroid tissues from CKD patients with secondary hyperparathyroidism (SHPT) and three accidentally resected normal parathyroid tissues from patients with benign tumor at Xiangya Hospital Central South University (September 2023 - August 2025) were collected for scRNA-seq, spatial RNA-seq, primary cell culture, and etc. Ethical approval was granted by the Ethics Committee of Xiangya Hospital Central South University (No. 2023030424) in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants. For details, see the Supplementary Methods.

Single cell RNA-sequencing and spatial RNA-sequencing

scRNA-seq and spatial RNA-seq of resected parathyroid tissues were performed by OE Biotech Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). For details, see the Supplementary Methods.

Primary cell culture in vitro

Three surgically resected parathyroid tissues were minced into fragment, digested at 37℃, and further dissociated. For details, see the Supplementary Methods.

Statistical analyses

Bioinformatic analyses and associated statistical analyses were conducted using R (version 4.3.1), and statistical analyses for experiments were conducted using GraphPad Prism (version 9.3.1). All quantitative data are presented as the mean±standard deviation (SD). Differences between two groups were assessed using an independent, two-tailed Student’s t-test. Comparisons among multiple groups were performed by one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), followed by Tukey test for correction in GraphPad Prism (version 9.3.1). Statistical significance was defined as *P < 0.05, **P < 0.01, and ***P < 0.001.

Results

Oxyphil cells were identified by spatial RNA-seq and scRNA-seq

6 SHPT samples and 3 normal parathyroid samples were used for scRNA-seq, while the most optimal SHPT sample was used for additional spatial RNA-seq. HE staining clearly distinguished the chief cells and oxyphil cells, which was fully consistent with the clustering analysis results in the spatial RNA-seq (Fig. 1A). Oxyphil cells exhibited a higher mitochondrial transcript proportion, which was consistent with the characteristics of oxyphil cells. PTH (log2 fold change = -0.259) and CASR (log2 fold change = -0.334) were slightly downregulated in oxyphil cells, while VDR (log2 fold change = -2.208) was markedly downregulated in oxyphil cells (Fig. 1B). Meanwhile, 93,073 cells passed the quality controls (percent.mt < 30%, according to the results of spatial RNA-seq) in scRNA-seq. 7 cell types were identified by the clustering analysis, and the parathyroid cell cluster was further divided into the normal parathyroid cell cluster and the SHPT cell cluster (Fig. 1C and Supplement Fig. 1). After excluding normal parathyroid cells, 44,636 SHPT cells were further clustered into 19 subclusters (Fig. 1D). To accurately identify oxyphil cells in scRNA-seq, we further integrated the data from scRNA-seq and spatial RNA-seq by Robust Cell Type Decomposition (RCTD). According to the mitochondrial transcript proportion and the results of RCTD, clusters 1, 12, 14, and 17 were identified as oxyphil cells, and clusters 5, 11, 18 were identified as chief cells. The expression of parathyroid markers (PTH, CASR, VDR) were consistent with results from spatial RNA-seq (Fig. 1E-G).

In vitro verification of oxyphil cell characteristics

To validate the characteristics of oxyphil cells, we examined mitochondrial markers (TOMM20 and VDAC1) and parathyroid markers (CASR and VDR) in SHPT tissues by immunofluorescence (IF). The results demonstrated that TOMM20 and VDAC1 were upregulated in oxyphil cells, while VDR was downregulated in oxyphil cells (Fig. 2A and Supplement Fig. 1). We further applied flow cytometry to sort oxyphil cells and chief cells, which was validated at both morphological and molecular levels. HE staining and transmission electron microscopy (TEM) revealed the morphology of cells (Fig. 2B-C). The higher mitochondria content in oxyphil cells was also confirmed by the detection of mtDNA and MitoTrack assay (Fig. 2D-E), ensuring the reliability of the sorting strategy. Western blot results confirmed the upregulated expression level of TOMM20 and VDAC1, and the downregulated expression level of VDR in oxyphil cells (Fig. 2F).

Estrogen related receptor alpha (ESRRA) was upregulated in oxyphil cells and promoted mitochondrial biogenesis

To investigate the potential transcription factors affecting the mitochondrial biogenesis in oxyphil cells, single-cell regulatory network inference and clustering (SCENIC) analyses were performed in both spatial RNA-seq and scRNA-seq (Fig. 3A-B). By integrating these results, 7 transcription factors were screened, including ESRRA, HOXB4, IRF7, IRX3, MAFK, RARG, and STAT1 (Fig. 3C). All these transcription factors were upregulated in oxyphil cells (Supplement Fig. 2). Among them, the target genes of ESRRA were enriched in mitochondria-related in Gene Ontology (GO) terms and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathways (Fig. 3D and Supplement Fig. 3). Therefore, we subsequently investigated the association between ESRRA and mitochondrial biogenesis in oxyphil cells. The expression level of ESRRA was upregulated in oxyphil cells (Fig. 3E), and overexpression of ESRRA increased the expression of TOMM20 and VDAC1 (Fig. 3F). The overexpression of ESRRA also significantly increased the mtDNA copy number in chief cells (Fig. 3G). Furthermore, MitoTrack assay and TEM both showed the significant increase of mitochondria after the overexpression of ESRRA in chief cells (Fig. 3H-I). The expression of multiple mitochondria-related genes also significantly increased after overexpressing ESRRA (Fig. 3J). These results suggested that ESRRA promoted mitochondrial biogenesis, and might contribute to the increased mitochondrial content observed in oxyphil cells.

Oxyphil cells were associated with calcitriol resistance

Based on our prior findings, oxyphil cells showed a low expression of VDR, suggesting the association between oxyphil cells and calcitriol resistance. Consequently, 100 nM calcitriol significantly reduced the supernatant PTH level in chief cells but had a significantly weaker effect on oxyphil cells (Fig. 4A-B), which was related to downregulated PTH mRNA level (Fig. 4C). Meanwhile, both the Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) assay and EdU assay showed that calcitriol reduced cell proliferation more strongly in chief cells than in oxyphil cells (Fig. 4D-E). These results indicated that oxyphil cells may be associated with calcitriol resistance.

JUNB ameliorated calcitriol resistance in oxyphil cells by regulating VDR

We subsequently investigated the associated transcription factors regulating VDR level. SCENIC analyses showed that 13 regulons included VDR as a target gene (Fig. 5A). Among the transcription factors corresponding to these regulons, three of them showed a downregulated expression level in oxyphil cells in both spatial RNA-seq data and scRNA-seq data (Fig. 5B). The overexpression of JUNB produced the largest increase on VDR mRNA level (Fig. 5C-D). Therefore, further experiments were focused on JUNB.

JUNB was downregulated in oxyphil cells according to RNA-seq data, which was also verified at protein level (Fig. 5E-F). The overexpression of JUNB could upregulate the protein level of VDR (Fig. 5G). Furthermore, there was a significant positive correlation between the expression of JUNB and VDR (Fig. 5H), which was further verified by IF staining of clinical tissues (Fig. 5I). The binding sites of JUNB on the VDR promoter were predicted by the JASPAR database. Three binding sites with high confidence were tested by chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP) assay, and site 1 and site 2 were verified (Fig. 5J). Meanwhile, the dual luciferase assay showed only the mutation of site 1 inhibited the promoting effect of JUNB on the VDR promoter (Fig. 5K). In addition, no association was observed between JUNB and CASR expression (Supplement Fig. 4).

The overexpression of JUNB could significantly enhanced the inhibitory effect of calcitriol on oxyphil cells, including supernatant PTH level and cell proliferation, which could be reversed by the knockdown of VDR (Fig. 6A-D). These results indicated that JUNB ameliorated calcitriol resistance in oxyphil cells by regulating VDR (Fig. 7).

Discussion

In the present study, spatial RNA-seq and scRNA-seq were employed to elucidate the characteristics of oxyphil cells in SHPT. Oxyphil cells exhibited a lower expression level of VDR than chief cells, which was associated with calcitriol resistance. Further investigations showed ESRRA was upregulated in oxyphil cells and promoted mitochondrial biogenesis, while JUNB regulated the transcription of VDR to participate in calcitriol resistance of oxyphil cells by binding to the promoter region of VDR. These findings provide reliable evidence and new insights into the characteristics of oxyphil cells and the potential function of oxyphil cells in SHPT.

It is widely recognized that oxyphil cells are distinguished by eosinophilic cytoplasm, which is caused by a high mitochondrial content. Numerous studies have confirmed the higher number of mitochondria in oxyphil cells [12, 13]. Nevertheless, the specific mechanism underlying the enhanced mitochondrial biogenesis in oxyphil cells has not been well elucidated. Our data identified ESRRA as a driver of this mitochondrial-rich phenotype of oxyphil cells in SHPT, implicating a role of ESRRA in this metabolic adaptation that may support pathological parathyroid function. This finding might also provide a potential marker to identify oxyphil cells, and offer novel insight into the process of chief-oxyphil cell differentiation in SHPT. ESRRA is an orphan nuclear receptor, which has been reported to play an important role in cellular metabolism, energy homeostasis, and mitochondrial biogenesis [14]. Specifically, the inhibition of ESRRA may hinder the mitochondrial biogenesis in cardiac tissue and skeletal muscle [15]. ESRRA has also been reported to drive mitochondrial biogenesis in esophageal cancer [16]. Therefore, the association between ESRRA and the mitochondrial biogenesis in SHPT is consistent with the previous research on ESRRA.

Meanwhile, the function-related characteristics of oxyphil cells are still under controversy. Oxyphil cells was once reported to express lower PTH level than chief cells [8, 17], while subsequently studies showed a higher PTH level in oxyphil cells than chief cells [10, 11, 18]. There were also some studies showing no difference in PTH level between oxyphil cells and chief cells [19, 20]. Similar controversies also exist in the expression level of CASR and VDR. These controversies may be mainly caused by the limitation of technology in the past decades. Therefore, our spatial RNA-seq may provide important and reliable information about the function-related characteristics of oxyphil cells, revealing slightly downregulated PTH and CASR levels, and a markedly downregulated VDR level.

VDRA is one of the long-established treatments for SHPT patients to correct vitamin D deficiency, reduce PTH synthesis and secretion, and suppress parathyroid cell growth. However, the clinical use of VDRA has been limited because of the increased risk of hypercalcemia, hyperphosphatemia, and associated cardiovascular and adynamic bone diseases [21]. Considering that the efficacy of VDRA is linked to VDR expression and signaling pathway, elucidating VDR regulation and identifying VDRA-sensitive patients may help overcome the current limitations in VDRA administration by enabling optimized dosing, minimized side effects, and a precise patient selection. Our study revealed that JUNB regulated VDR transcription to mediate VDRA resistance in SHPT. This finding provided JUNB as a potential therapeutic target for SHPT patients with VDRA resistance.

Given no consensus has been reached about the characteristics of oxyphil cells in past decades, the role of oxyphil cells in drug resistance remains largely unclear. A case report introduced that a CKD-SHPT patient with the proportion of oxyphil cells as high as 40%, is not responsive to cinacalcet therapy [22]. As for VDRA, a study demonstrated that the duration and dosage of calcitriol treatment were independent predictors of oxyphil cell content, and vitamin D binding protein was downregulated in oxyphil cells [8], suggesting an association between oxyphil cells and VDRA resistance in SHPT. Our study also revealed the association between oxyphil cells and calcitriol resistance. It should be mentioned that the functional weight of this mechanism is related to the abundance of oxyphil cells within the parathyroid gland. SHPT patients with a low proportion of oxyphil cells may respond more favorably to VDRA therapy. Conversely, patients with a high proportion of oxyphil cells may need additional therapies to overcome VDRA resistance. However, the present evidence is still preliminary, and further clinical studies are necessary to confirm this association and determine a potential cutoff value of oxyphil cell proportion to predict VDRA resistance in SHPT.

Although the significance of CASR is well-recognized in SHPT [21], few studies have directly demonstrated the potential role of CASR on VDR expression or VDRA resistance. Two studies reported that calcimimetics upregulated VDR mRNA expression, [23–24] which may implicate a potential role of CASR on VDR expression. However, the specific mechanisms remain largely unknown, and further clinical evidence is lacking. The present study revealed a discrepancy in the downregulation of CASR and VDR expression levels, and no significant association between JUNB and CASR, thus failed to demonstrate the associations between CASR and VDR. Future studies should comprehensively investigate the impact of CASR on VDR expression or VDRA resistance.

Conclusion

In summary, our study revealed the characteristics of oxyphil cells in SHPT by integrating spatial RNA-seq and scRNA-seq data. ESRRA was associated with mitochondrial biogenesis, while reduced JUNB expression contributed to calcitriol resistance by impairing transcriptional activation of VDR gene in oxyphil cells. These findings offered new insights into the characteristics and functions of oxyphil cells in SHPT.
