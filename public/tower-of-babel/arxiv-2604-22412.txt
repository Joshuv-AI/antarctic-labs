Abstract
. We introduce the notion of uniform exactness, or uniform amenability at infinity, for discrete groups and prove it for a wide class of groups containing free groups and their limit groups. This shows a novel strong convergence phenomenon that any convergent sequence of such groups in the space of marked groups converges strongly in the operator algebraic sense. In particular, convergence of the spectral radius formula is uniform over probability measures on such groups whose supports have a fixed cardinality.

1.
Introduction

Amenability is one of the most important notions in analytic group theory.
However, many groups, in particular those groups that contain non-cyclic free groups,
are not amenable.
It is a remarkable fact that many non-amenable groups still enjoy
the weaker yet very useful property,

exactness
or a.k.a.
amenability at infinity

(
[
A-D
,
KW
]
).
A group
\Gamma
is exact if the
\Gamma
-action
on the Stone–Čech compactification
\beta\Gamma
, or equivalently
on the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
\ell_{\infty}\Gamma
of bounded functions on
\Gamma
,
is amenable.
Here the action
\Gamma\curvearrowright\ell_{\infty}\Gamma
is given by

(sf)(x)\coloneq f(s^{-1}x)
for
s,x\in\Gamma
and
f\in\ell_{\infty}\Gamma
.
For example, the class of exact groups contains
hyperbolic groups (
[
Ad
]
)
and linear groups (
[
GHW
]
),
but not all groups are exact (
[
AD
,
Gr
,
Os
]
).
Both amenability and exactness are inherited by subgroups.
See
[
AR
,
BO
]
for a comprehensive treatment on
this topic.

A group
\Gamma
is said to be
uniformly amenable

(
[
Boż
,
Ke
,
KS
,
Wy
]
) if
its ultrapower
\Gamma^{\mathcal{U}}
is amenable.
Here and throughout the paper, we fix
a free ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}
on
\mathbb{N}
.
All of the arguments do not depend on the choice of a free ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}
.
The ultrapower
\Gamma^{\mathcal{U}}
is defined to be
\prod_{\mathbb{N}}\Gamma/{\sim}
,
where
(s_{n})_{n}\sim(t_{n})_{n}
if
\{n:s_{n}=t_{n}\}\in\mathcal{U}
.
The element in the ultrapower that arises from
a sequence
(x_{n})_{n}
etc will be written by
[x_{n}]_{n}
.
For example, solvable groups are uniformly amenable
since being solvable of derived length less than a fixed number is
maintained by ultraproduct. On the other hand,
the amenable group
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
of finite permutations
on
\mathbb{N}
is not since it contains a non-cyclic free group
in the ultrapower.
It is tempting to introduce the notion of uniform exactness
similarly by exactness of the ultrapower,
but this appears too weak for meaningful applications.
Instead, we define a group
\Gamma
to be
uniformly exact

(or
uniformly amenable at infinity
) if
the
\Gamma^{\mathcal{U}}
-action on
(\ell_{\infty}\Gamma)^{\mathcal{U}}

is amenable.
This notion will have applications
to a strong convergence phenomenon
and a uniform estimate of the spectral radius formula.
Recall that for a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
A
, its ultrapower is defined to be

A^{\mathcal{U}}\coloneq\{(a_{n})_{n}\in\ell_{\infty}(\mathbb{N},A)\}/\{(a_{n})_{n}:\Lim_{\mathcal{U}}\|a_{n}\|=0\}.

Here
\Lim_{\mathcal{U}}
is the character on
\ell_{\infty}\mathbb{N}
associated
with the free ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}
.
We view
(\ell_{\infty}\Gamma)^{\mathcal{U}}

as the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-subalgebra of “internal” functions
in
\ell_{\infty}(\Gamma^{\mathcal{U}})

via the
\Gamma^{\mathcal{U}}
-equivariant isometric embedding given by

[f_{n}]_{n}([x_{n}]_{n})\coloneq\Lim_{\mathcal{U}}f_{n}(x_{n})
.
Since the condition for amenability gets more restrictive
as the relevant
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra gets smaller,
uniform exactness of
\Gamma
implies exactness of its
ultrapower
\Gamma^{\mathcal{U}}
.
It is known that
\Gamma^{\mathcal{U}}
is exact
for torsion-free hyperbolic groups (
[
KM2
,
Se2
]
)
and linear groups (
[
GHW
]
).
Besides uniformly amenable groups, these
are the main source (and the only source as far as
the author is aware) of groups that have exact ultrapowers.

Conjecture
.

Torsion-free hyperbolic groups and linear groups are uniformly exact.

We solve this conjecture for the case of free groups
\mathbf{F}
,
by viewing the ultrapower
\mathbf{F}^{\mathcal{U}}
as a
\mathbb{Z}^{\mathcal{U}}
-tree
(see
[
Ch
]
) and studying the “definable” functions
on
\mathbf{F}^{\mathcal{U}}
through the tree compactification.

Theorem A
.

Free groups are uniformly exact.

Let
\lambda
denote the left regular representation
of
\Gamma
on
\ell_{2}\Gamma
.
The
reduced group
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra

\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{r}}\Gamma
of
\Gamma
is the norm closure of

\lambda(\mathbb{C}\Gamma)
in the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
\mathbb{B}(\ell_{2}\Gamma)

of all bounded linear operators on
\ell_{2}\Gamma
.
It is well-known that exactness has
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebraic
characterization (
[
KW
]
, Theorem 5.1.7 in
[
BO
]
).
Namely, a group
\Gamma
is exact if and only if

\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{r}}\Gamma
is
exact
as a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra.
Note that the ultrapower
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra

(\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{r}}\Gamma)^{\mathcal{U}}
is exact if and only if
it is
subhomogeneous
if and only if

\Gamma
is virtually abelian.
This shows a stark contrast between
exactness of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{r}}(\Gamma^{\mathcal{U}})
and that of
(\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{r}}\Gamma)^{\mathcal{U}}
.

Theorem B
.

A group
\Gamma
is uniformly exact if and only if
the canonical embedding

A^{\mathcal{U}}\otimes\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{r}}(\Gamma^{\mathcal{U}})\hookrightarrow(A\otimes\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{r}}\Gamma)^{\mathcal{U}},\quad[a_{n}]_{n}\otimes\lambda([t_{n}]_{n})\mapsto[a_{n}\otimes\lambda(t_{n})]_{n}

is continuous and isometric for every
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
A
.
Here the tensor product
\otimes
is
the minimal (a.k.a. spatial) tensor product of
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras.

A group
\Gamma
is said to be

mixed-identity-free
or MIF (
[
HO
]
)
if there is a nontrivial element in
\Gamma^{\mathcal{U}}

that is free from
\Gamma
, or equivalently
if there is an embedding

\Gamma\ast\mathbb{Z}\hookrightarrow\Gamma^{\mathcal{U}}

that extends the diagonal embedding of
\Gamma
.
It has been proved in
[
AGKP
,
Oz
,
Ro
]

that
some
of these
embeddings for some of the MIF groups
are extended to the

\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra embeddings

\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{r}}(\Gamma\ast\mathbb{Z})\hookrightarrow(\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{r}}\Gamma)^{\mathcal{U}}
,
proving that such
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{r}}\Gamma
are selfless.
Theorem
B
states that

all
of these embeddings are extended,
provided that
\Gamma
is uniformly exact.
In particular, a uniformly exact group is

completely
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-selfless

(
[
AGKP
,
Oz
,
Ro
]
)
if it is MIF.

Recall that a
marked group
of rank
d

is a group
\Gamma
together with
an epimorphism
\varphi

from the rank
d
free group
\mathbf{F}_{d}

(with the distinguished generating set) onto
\Gamma
.
A marked group (or more precisely, its
marked isomorphism class)
is in the one-to-one correspondence to
a normal subgroup
\ker\varphi
in
\mathbf{F}_{d}
.
The space
\mathcal{M}_{d}
of marked groups of rank
d

is a compact topological space as a closed subset of
2^{\mathbf{F}_{d}}
.
The space
\mathcal{M}
of marked groups is the disjoint
union
\bigsqcup_{d}\mathcal{M}_{d}
.
A
limit group
(
[
KM1
,
Se1
]
)
is a group that appears as the limit in
\mathcal{M}

of marked groups that are isomorphic to free groups.
We say that a subgroup-closed class
\mathcal{C}
of groups
is
compact
if the image of
\mathcal{C}

in
\mathcal{M}
(under all possible markings) is closed.
The class of limit groups is compact by definition.
Every uniformly exact group has a function
k\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
as
a
modulus of uniform exactness
(see Section
9
).
The class
\mathcal{UE}
of uniformly exact groups
is the union of uniformly exact groups
\mathcal{UE}(k)
having

k
as a modulus of uniform exactness.

Corollary C
.

The class
\mathcal{UE}=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}}\mathcal{UE}(k)
satisfies the following.

(1)

The class
\mathcal{UE}
contains free groups and more generally
torsion-free hyperbolic groups having tame geometry at infinity
(see Section
8
).

(2)

For every
k\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
,
the class
\mathcal{UE}(k)
is closed under
taking subgroups, directed unions,
and quotients by normal amenable subgroups.

(3)

The class
\mathcal{UE}(k)
is compact for every
k\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
.
In particular, limit groups are uniformly exact with the same
modulus of uniform exactness as free groups.

(4)

An amenable group is uniformly exact if and only if
it is uniformly amenable.
Moreover, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
, the class of amenable
groups in
\mathcal{UE}(k)
is compact.
In particular,
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
is not uniformly exact.

(5)

The class
\mathcal{UE}
is closed under extensions, free products, and finite-index supergroups.
More precisely, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
, there is
k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}

such that extensions and free products
of groups from
\mathcal{UE}(k)
are contained in
\mathcal{UE}(k^{\prime})
.
For example,
one has
\Gamma*_{\Delta}(\Delta\times\mathbb{Z})\in\mathcal{UE}

for every
\Gamma\in\mathcal{UE}
and
\Delta\leq\Gamma
.

Exactness is closed under free products over
arbitrary amalgamation and under HNN extensions
(
[
Dy
]
, see also Theorem 5.2.7 in
[
BO
]
).
The same for uniform exactness is much unclear.

The following proposition strengthens the result of
[
LM
]

that every limit group is a strong limit of
some

sequence of free groups.
See
[
Ma
,
vH
]
for recent surveys on
strong convergence phenomena.

Proposition D
.

Fix a modulus
k\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
of uniform exactness.
Then for every
\Gamma\in\mathcal{UE}(k)
,
f\in\mathbb{C}\Gamma
, and
\varepsilon>0

with
F\coloneq\supp f
and

E\coloneq(F\cup\{1\}\cup F^{-1})^{k(|F|+\lfloor 2/\varepsilon\rfloor)}
,
one has

\|\lambda(f)\|_{\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{r}}\Gamma}\leq(1+\varepsilon)\|P_{\ell_{2}E}\lambda(f)|_{\ell_{2}E}\|_{\mathbb{B}(\ell_{2}E)}.

In particular, every convergent sequence

(\varphi_{n}\colon\mathbf{F}_{d}\to\Gamma_{n})_{n}
in
\mathcal{M}_{d}

with
\Gamma_{n}\in\mathcal{UE}(k)
converges to
the limit
\varphi_{\infty}\colon\mathbf{F}_{d}\to\Gamma_{\infty}

strongly in the sense that

\forall f\in\mathbb{C}\mathbf{F}_{d}\quad\lim_{n}\|\lambda(\varphi_{n}(f))\|_{\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{r}}\Gamma_{n}}=\|\lambda(\varphi_{\infty}(f))\|_{\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{r}}\Gamma_{\infty}}.

Also, convergence of the spectral radius formula

\|\lambda(f)\|_{\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{r}}\Gamma}=\lim_{n}\sqrt[2n]{(f^{*}*f)^{*n}(1)}.

is uniform over
\Gamma\in\mathcal{UE}(k)
and over
f\in\mathbb{C}\Gamma

with uniform bounds on
|\supp f|
and
\|f\|_{1}
.

2.
Main theorem for actions on
\Lambda
-trees

The following is the main technical result of this paper.
The terminologies used in the statement will be explained eventually.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a countable group and
X
be a
\Lambda
-tree
on which
\Gamma
acts isometrically without inversions.
Assume that the action
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
satisfies
the following conditions.

(1)

Every isometric embedding of an open segment

(a,b)\coloneq\{c\in\Lambda:a<c<b\}

into
X
extends to an isometric embedding of
\Lambda
.

(2)

The set of amenable subgroups of
\Gamma
is countable.

(3)

For every
x,y\in X
,
x\neq y
,
the subgroup

\{t\in\Gamma:\dist(tx,x)+\dist(ty,y)\ll\dist(x,y)\}

is amenable.

(4)

The action
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
has finite
\Lambda
-rank

\rank_{\Lambda}(\Gamma\curvearrowright X)
.

Let
K
be a compact
\Gamma
-space.
Assume that
K
is amenable
as a compact
\Gamma^{x}
-space for the stabilizer subgroup

\Gamma^{x}\coloneq\{t\in\Gamma:tx=x\}
at every
x\in X

and that the tree compactification
\hat{X}
of
X
is
a
\Gamma
-equivariant quotient of
K
.
Then the compact
\Gamma
-space
K
is amenable.

3.
Amenability of
\Gamma
-spaces and equivalence relations

Let
\Gamma
be a group and denote by

\Prob\Gamma\subset\ell_{1}\Gamma

the convex subset of probability measures on
\Gamma
.
The space
\Prob\Gamma
is
equipped with the norm topology
(which coincides with the pointwise convergence topology) and with
the
\Gamma
-action by left translations.
Recall that a group
\Gamma
is
amenable
if there is
a net
\xi_{i}\in\Prob\Gamma
,
called an
approximate invariant mean
, that
satisfies

\lim_{i}\|\xi_{i}-s\xi_{i}\|_{1}=0
for every
s\in\Gamma
.
We review the notion of amenability that is generalized
for
\Gamma
-actions (
[
A-D
,
AR
]
)
and equivalence relations (
[
JKL
]
) through
existence of appropriate approximate invariant means.

A
compact
\Gamma
-space

K

is a compact topological space
K

equipped with an action of
\Gamma
by homeomorphisms,
and a
Borel
\Gamma
-space

Z
is a
standard Borel space equipped
with an action of a countable group

\Gamma
by Borel isomorphisms.
Be aware that we put a countability assumption
on Borel
\Gamma
-spaces, but not on compact
\Gamma
-spaces.

Definition 2
.

A compact
\Gamma
-space
K
is
(topologically) amenable

if there is a net
of continuous maps
\eta_{i}\colon K\to\Prob\Gamma
that satisfies

\forall s\in\Gamma\quad\lim_{i}\sup_{x\in K}\|\eta_{i}^{sx}-s\eta_{i}^{x}\|_{1}=0.

By continuity of
\eta_{i}
, we may assume that

E_{i}\coloneq\bigcup_{x}\supp\eta_{i}^{x}
is a finite subset of
\Gamma

(see Lemma 4.3.8 in
[
BO
]
).
The net
(\eta_{i})_{i}
is called an
approximate invariant mean
.
Instead of saying that
K
is amenable,
we also say that the action
\Gamma\curvearrowright K

is
topologically amenable

or the
\Gamma
-action on the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
C(K)
is amenable.

Let
\Prob Z
denote the set of probability measures on
Z
.
A Borel
\Gamma
-space
Z
(with
\Gamma
being countable)
is
amenable
if there is a net of
Borel maps
\eta_{i}\colon Z\to\Prob\Gamma

that satisfies

\forall s\in\Gamma\ \forall m\in\Prob Z\quad\lim_{i}\int_{Z}\|\eta_{i}^{sx}-s\eta_{i}^{x}\|_{1}\,dm(x)=0,

or equivalently, if for every finite
subset
F\Subset\Gamma
,
m\in\Prob Z
, and
\varepsilon>0
,
there is a Borel map
\eta\colon Z\to\Prob\Gamma
that satisfies

\sum_{s\in F}\int_{Z}\|\eta^{sx}-s\eta^{x}\|_{1}\,dm(x)<\varepsilon.

Here
\eta\colon Z\to\Prob\Gamma
is Borel if and only if

x\mapsto\eta^{x}(t)
is Borel for every
t\in\Gamma
.

We make some observations.
For a compact
\Gamma
-space
K
and its
quotient
\Gamma
-space
L

(or equivalently
C(L)\subset C(K)
),
amenability of
L
is succeeded by
K
.
A compact/Borel
\Gamma
-space is amenable if
it is the case for the restriction to
finitely generated subgroups.
A countable union of amenable Borel
\Gamma
-spaces
is amenable.
The following is very useful.

Theorem 3

(
[
A-D
,
AR
]
, see also 5.2.1 in
[
BO
]
)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a countable group
and
K
be a second countable compact
\Gamma
-space.
Then
K
is topologically amenable if and only if it is amenable
as a Borel
\Gamma
-space.

Let
Z
be a standard Borel space.
A
countable Borel equivalence relation

\mathcal{R}
on
Z

is an equivalence relation
\mathcal{R}\subset Z\times Z
such that
each equivalence class is at most countable and that
\mathcal{R}

is a Borel subset of
Z\times Z
.
For
x\in Z
, we write
[x]_{\mathcal{R}}

for the equivalence class that contains
x
.
We denote by
[\mathcal{R}]
the
full group
consisting
of Borel bijections
\varphi
on
Z
that
satisfies
\varphi(x)\in[x]_{\mathcal{R}}
for all
x
.
For a function
\nu
on
\mathcal{R}
,
we denote by
\nu^{x}
the function on
[x]_{\mathcal{R}}

given by
\nu^{x}(y)\coloneq\nu(x,y)
.
We need a notion of amenability for countable Borel
equivalence relations that is stronger than
the standard one (
[
JKL
]
).
Although Fréchet-amenability (
[
JKL
]
) meets
our purpose, we introduce an even stronger one.

Definition 4
.

A countable Borel equivalence relation
\mathcal{R}
on
Z

is
\mathcal{M}
-amenable
if there is a net of
non-negative Borel functions
\nu_{i}
on
\mathcal{R}

such that
\nu_{i}^{x}\in\Prob{[x]_{\mathcal{R}}}

for all
x
and

\forall\varphi\in[\mathcal{R}]\,\forall m\in\Prob Z\quad\int_{Z}\lim_{i}\|\nu_{i}^{\varphi(x)}-\nu_{i}^{x}\|_{1}\,dm(x)=0.

For a Borel
\Gamma
-space
Z
(with
\Gamma
being countable),
we denote the corresponding

orbit equivalence relation
by

\mathcal{R}_{\Gamma\curvearrowright Z}\coloneq\{(x,t^{-1}x):x\in Z,\ t\in\Gamma\}\subset Z\times Z.

By the Feldman–Moore theorem,
every countable Borel equivalence relation arises in this way.
If the Borel action
\Gamma\curvearrowright Z
is free, then the bijective
correspondence

Z\times\Gamma\ni(x,t)\leftrightarrow(x,t^{-1}x)\in\mathcal{R}_{\Gamma\curvearrowright Z}

gives equivalence between amenability of
\Gamma\curvearrowright Z
and
that of
\mathcal{R}_{\Gamma\curvearrowright Z}
.
We note that
\mathcal{R}_{\Gamma\curvearrowright Z}
is
\mathcal{M}
-amenable
whenever
\Gamma
is amenable.
We will collect basic permanence properties of amenability.

Lemma 5
.

Let
\Gamma
be a countable group and

Z
be a Borel
\Gamma
-space.
Suppose
\Delta\leq\Gamma
is a subgroup and
Y\subset Z
is
a
\Delta
-invariant Borel subset that satisfy

gY\cap Y=\emptyset
for
g\in\Gamma\setminus\Delta
and

\Gamma Y=Z
.
If
Y
is amenable as a Borel
\Delta
-space, then
Z
is amenable.

Proof.

We view
\bar{\Gamma}\coloneq\Gamma/\Delta

as a space on which
\Gamma
acts;

\Gamma\times\bar{\Gamma}\ni(s,p)\mapsto sp\in\bar{\Gamma}
.
Fix a lift
\varsigma\colon\bar{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
and let

\alpha\colon\Gamma\times\bar{\Gamma}\to\Delta
be
the corresponding cocycle given by

\alpha(s,p)\coloneq\varsigma(sp)^{-1}s\varsigma(p)
.
We may assume
Z=\bar{\Gamma}\times Y
, via
the Borel bijection

\bar{\Gamma}\times Y\ni(p,y)\mapsto\varsigma(p)y\in Z
,
where

\Gamma
acts on
\bar{\Gamma}\times Y
by
s(p,y)=(sp,\alpha(s,p)y)
.
Given an approximate invariant mean
\eta_{i}\colon Y\to\Prob\Delta
,
we define
\zeta_{i}\colon\bar{\Gamma}\times Y\to\Prob\Gamma

by
\zeta_{i}^{(p,y)}\coloneq\varsigma(p)\eta_{i}^{y}
.
Then, for every
s\in\Gamma
,

\|\zeta_{i}^{s(p,y)}-s\zeta_{i}^{(p,y)}\|_{1}=\|\varsigma(sp)\eta_{i}^{\alpha(s,p)y}-s\varsigma(p)\eta_{i}^{y}\|_{1}=\|\eta_{i}^{\alpha(s,p)y}-\alpha(s,p)\eta_{i}^{y}\|_{1}.

Hence, for every
m=(m_{p})_{p\in\bar{\Gamma}}\in\Prob(\bar{\Gamma}\times Y)
, one has

\int\|\zeta_{i}^{s(p,y)}-s\zeta_{i}^{(p,y)}\|_{1}\,dm(p,y)=\sum_{p}\int\|\eta_{i}^{\alpha(s,p)y}-\alpha(s,p)\eta_{i}^{y}\|_{1}\,dm_{p}(y)\to 0,

since each integral in the RHS is bounded by
2\|m_{p}\|
and
converges to
0
.
∎

Lemma 6
.

Let
\Gamma
be a countable group and

Z
be a Borel
\Gamma
-space.
Suppose that the countable Borel equivalence
relation
\mathcal{R}_{\Gamma\curvearrowright Z}
is
\mathcal{M}
-amenable
and that
\{\Gamma^{x}:x\in Z\}
consists of
a countable family of amenable subgroups.
Then the Borel
\Gamma
-space
Z
is amenable.

Proof.

We may assume by a countable decomposition
that all
\Gamma^{x}
are conjugate to a single subgroup
\Delta\leq\Gamma
.
The Borel subset
Y\coloneq\{x\in Z:\Gamma^{x}=\Delta\}

is invariant under the normalizer subgroup
N_{\Gamma}(\Delta)

of
\Delta
in
\Gamma
.
Thanks to Lemma
5
,
it suffices to show that
Y
is amenable as
a Borel
N_{\Gamma}(\Delta)
-space.
Thus, we may assume that
Y=Z
and
N_{\Gamma}(\Delta)=\Gamma
,
i.e.,
\Delta=\ker(\Gamma\curvearrowright Z)

and the action
\bar{\Gamma}\coloneq\Gamma/\Delta\curvearrowright Z
is free.
It follows that
Z
is amenable as a Borel
\bar{\Gamma}
-space.

As in Proof of Lemma
5
,
fix a lift
\varsigma\colon\bar{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
and the cocycle

\alpha\colon\Gamma\times\bar{\Gamma}\to\Delta
.
Thus the bijection

\theta\colon\bar{\Gamma}\times\Delta\to\Gamma
, given by

(p,t)\mapsto\varsigma(p)t
,
satisfies
s\theta(p,t)=\theta(sp,\alpha(s,p)t)
.
For
\eta\colon Z\to\Prob\bar{\Gamma}

and
\xi\in\Prob\Delta
, we define

\zeta\colon Z\to\Prob\Gamma
by

\zeta^{x}=(\eta^{x}\otimes\xi)\circ\theta^{-1}
.
Then

\displaystyle\|\zeta^{sx}-s\zeta^{x}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\|\zeta^{sx}-(s\eta^{x}\otimes\xi)\circ\theta^{-1}\|_{1}+\|(s\eta^{x}\otimes\xi)\circ\theta^{-1}-s\zeta^{x}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\|\eta^{sx}-s\eta^{x}\|_{1}+\sum_{p}\eta^{x}(s^{-1}p)\|\xi-\alpha(s,s^{-1}p)\xi\|_{1}.

Let a finite subset
F\Subset\Gamma
,
m\in\Prob Z
, and
\varepsilon>0
be given.
There is
\eta
that satisfies

\sum_{s\in F}\int_{Z}\|\eta^{sx}-s\eta^{x}\|_{1}\,dm(x)<\varepsilon/2.

Since
\sum_{s,p}\int_{Z}\eta^{x}(s^{-1}p)\,dm(x)=|F|
,
there is
\xi
that satisfies

\sum_{s\in F}\int_{Z}\sum_{p}\eta^{x}(s^{-1}p)\|\xi-\alpha(s,s^{-1}p)\xi\|_{1}\,dm(x)<\varepsilon/2.

It follows that for these
\eta
and
\xi
,
the Borel map
\zeta\colon Z\to\Prob\Gamma
satisfies

\sum_{s\in F}\int_{Z}\|\zeta^{sx}-s\zeta^{x}\|_{1}\,dm(x)<\varepsilon.

∎

4.
Real trees and the tree compactification

An
\mathbb{R}
-tree
(or a
real tree
)
T

is a geodesic metric space that is
0
-hyperbolic.
See
[
Bes
,
Ch
,
Ev
]
for details.
The
Gromov product
with respect
to a point
u\in T
is defined for
(x,y)\in T\times T
by

\mathopen{\langle}x,y\mathclose{\rangle}_{u}\coloneq\frac{1}{2}(\dist(x,u)+\dist(y,u)-\dist(x,y)).

An
arc
(resp.
tripod
) is a bounded complete
subtree with exactly two (resp. three) endpoints.
For every
x,y\in T
,
x\neq y
, there is a unique arc
[x,y]

connecting
x
and
y
.
For every
x,y,u
, the center
w
of tripod spanned by
\{x,y,u\}

satisfies
\dist(w,u)=\mathopen{\langle}x,y\mathclose{\rangle}_{u}
.
A point in
T
is called a
branching point
if it is a center of tripod.
A
ray
is the image in
T

of an isometric embedding
\alpha

of the interval
[0,d)\subset\mathbb{R}
,
where
d\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\cup\{\infty\}
,
such that
\lim_{t\to d}\alpha(t)
does not exist in
T
.
The
\mathbb{R}
-tree
T
is complete if and only if there are no rays of finite length.
The
boundary

\partial T
is the space
of the equivalence classes
of rays, where two rays are
equivalent

if their intersection is a ray.
We write
\bar{T}\coloneq T\cup\partial T
.
The Gromov product extends on
\bar{T}\times\bar{T}
.
For
u\in T
and
x\in\partial T
, we write
[u,x)

for the unique ray that represents
x
and
has
u
as the endpoint, and
[u,x]\coloneq[u,x)\cup\{x\}
.
We also write
[x,y)\coloneq[x,y]\setminus\{y\}
and

(x,y)\coloneq[x,y]\setminus\{x,y\}
, which is called an
open arc
.

We will introduce the tree compactification
\hat{T}
of an
\mathbb{R}
-tree,
following the construction for a simplicial tree (see
[
Bow
,
MS
]

and Section 5.2 in
[
BO
]
).
It will be shown that
\hat{T}
coincides with
\bar{T}
as a set of points.
For every
u,v\in T
, we define the function
h_{u,v}

on
T
by
h_{u,v}(x)\coloneq\dist(u,v)^{-1}\mathopen{\langle}x,v\mathclose{\rangle}_{u}
(or identically

0
if
u=v
).
We consider the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-subalgebra
\mathcal{C}(T)

of
\ell_{\infty}T
generated by
\{h_{u,v}:u,v\in T\}
and
define the
tree compactification

\hat{T}
of
T
to be
the Gelfand spectrum of the unital commutative

\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{C}(T)
.
The point evaluation at
T
gives rise
to a dense embedding of
T
into
\hat{T}
.
Since every
h_{u,v}
is continuous
w.r.t. the original metric topology on
T

and has limit along every ray
\alpha
, one has the following.

Lemma 11
.

The dense embedding of
T
into
\hat{T}
is continuous.
Moreover, it extends to an embedding of
\bar{T}
into
\hat{T}
.

The embedding of
T
into
\hat{T}
need not be homeomorphic as
any net of points
x_{i}
that exist in different directions relative to

x
converges to
x
.
For
x\in T
, we observe that
x_{i}\to x
in
\hat{T}

if and only if
\mathopen{\langle}x_{i},u\mathclose{\rangle}_{x}\to 0
for every
u\in T
, since

|h_{u,v}(x_{i})-h_{u,v}(x)|\leq\mathopen{\langle}x_{i},u\mathclose{\rangle}_{x}+\mathopen{\langle}x_{i},v\mathclose{\rangle}_{x}.

Theorem 12
.

\hat{T}=\bar{T}
.

Proof.

We follow the proof of Proposition 5.2.5 in
[
BO
]
.
It suffices to show that every net
(x_{i})_{i\in I}

in
T
has a limit point (in the topology of
\hat{T}
) in
\bar{T}
.
Take a cofinal ultrafilter
\mathcal{V}
on
I
.
Pick a base point
o
and consider the subset

\alpha\coloneq\{z\in T:\{i\in I:z\in[o,x_{i}]\}\in\mathcal{V}\}

It is not hard to see that
\alpha
is connected
and does not contain a tripod.
Hence
\alpha
is either
[o,x)
for
x\in\bar{T}

or
[o,x]
for
x\in T
which is possibly degenerate
when
x=o
.
We claim that in either case
\Lim_{\mathcal{V}}x_{i}=x
in
\hat{T}
.
We assume that
x\in T

as the case for
x\in\partial T
is easier.
Thus it suffices to show that

\Lim_{\mathcal{V}}\mathopen{\langle}x_{i},u\mathclose{\rangle}_{x}=0
for every
u\in T
.
Suppose that this is not the case and
there is
u\in T
such that
\Lim_{\mathcal{V}}\mathopen{\langle}x_{i},u\mathclose{\rangle}_{x}>0
.
Since
\alpha
does not extend beyond
x
, this implies

\mathopen{\langle}o,u\mathclose{\rangle}_{x}>0
.
Choose
z\in\alpha
such that

\dist(z,x)<\mathopen{\langle}o,y\mathclose{\rangle}_{x}\wedge\Lim_{\mathcal{V}}\mathopen{\langle}x_{i},u\mathclose{\rangle}_{x}
.
Then
z\not\in[o,x_{i}]
for
\mathcal{V}
-many
i
, a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 13
.

Let
T
be an
\mathbb{R}
-tree and
S\subset T
be
a closed
\mathbb{R}
-subtree.
Then the projection
p\colon T\to S
(see 2.1.9 in
[
Ch
]
)
extends to the quotient map
\hat{p}\colon\hat{T}\to\hat{S}
.
In other words,

p_{*}\colon\mathcal{C}(S)\ni h\mapsto h\circ p\in\mathcal{C}(T)

is an isometric embedding that
sends
h_{u,v}
,
u,v\in S
, to
h_{u,v}
.

Proof.

For every
u,v\in S
and
x\in T
, one has
h_{u,v}(x)=h_{u,v}(p(x))
.
∎

Remark 14
.

Let
T
be an
\mathbb{R}
-tree.

(1)

The topology of
\hat{T}=T\cup\partial T

is generated by the
half spaces

H_{u}(v)\coloneq\{x\in\bar{T}:\mathopen{\langle}x,v\mathclose{\rangle}_{u}>0\}=\{x\in\bar{T}:u\notin[v,x]\}

with
u,v\in T
.

(2)

Every isometric isomorphism on
T
extends to
a homeomorphism on
\hat{T}
.

(3)

Suppose that
T
is separable (in the original metric)
and
\{y_{k}\}
is a countable dense subset of
T
.
Then the tree compactification
\hat{T}
is second countable, since

\mathcal{C}(T)
is generated by the countable subset
\{h_{y_{k},y_{l}}:k\neq l\}
.
Moreover,
T
has at most countable branching points,
since it is enough to consider the tripods spanned by triples from
\{y_{k}\}
.
For every
z\in T
, the function

x\mapsto\dist(x,z)=\sup_{k}\mathopen{\langle}x,y_{k}\mathclose{\rangle}_{z}
is Borel on
T\subset\hat{T}
.
Thus the Borel structures on
T
induced by
\hat{T}

and by the original metric coincide,
provided that
T\subset\hat{T}
is Borel.

Lemma 15
.

Let
T
be a non-trivial
\mathbb{R}
-tree on which
a group
\Gamma
acts isometrically.
Then for every
x\in\hat{T}
that is not a branching point,
the stabilizer subgroup
\Gamma^{x}
at
x
is an abelian extension
of a directed union of arc stabilizers.
In particular, it is amenable if all arc stabilizers are amenable.

Proof.

Let
x\in\partial T
be an endpoint of a ray
\alpha=[o,x)

then
s\mapsto\lim_{y\in\alpha}\dist(sy,o)-\dist(y,o)
defines
a homomorphism from
\Gamma^{x}
into
\mathbb{R}
whose kernel is
the union of the stabilizers of arcs
[y,x)
with
y\in\alpha
.
Similarly, if
x
is an endpoint in
T
(i.e., there
is no open arc
(y,z)
that contains
x
), then

\Gamma^{x}
is the union of the stabilizers of arcs
[y,x)
,
where
y\in[o,x)
.
Now suppose that
x\in T
is not a branching point
nor an endpoint and let
\Delta\leq\Gamma^{x}
be the union
of the stabilizers of open arcs containing
x
.
Since every
s\in\Gamma^{x}
either fixes an open arc
containing
x
or flips around
x
,
the subgroup
\Delta
has index at most two in
\Gamma^{x}
.
∎

5.
\Lambda
-trees and the tree compactification

Let
\Lambda
be an ordered abelian (additive) group.
Every subgroup inherits the order.
A subgroup
M\leq\Lambda
is
convex
if
for every
b\in\Lambda
, that
|b|<a
for some
a\in M_{+}
implies
b\in M
.
We denote by
M^{\circ}
the union of proper convex subgroups of
M
.
For every
a\in\Lambda_{+}
,
the convex subgroup
\mathopen{\langle}a\mathclose{\rangle}_{\conv}
generated by
a
is given by

\mathopen{\langle}a\mathclose{\rangle}_{\conv}\coloneq\{b\in\Lambda:\exists n\mbox{ such that }|b|<na\}

and its largest proper convex subgroup
\mathopen{\langle}a\mathclose{\rangle}_{\conv}^{\circ}
is given by

\mathopen{\langle}a\mathclose{\rangle}_{\conv}^{\circ}\coloneq\{b\in\Lambda:\forall n\quad n|b|<a\}=\ker\varepsilon^{a}.

Here
\varepsilon^{a}\colon\mathopen{\langle}a\mathclose{\rangle}_{\conv}\to\mathbb{R}
is the unique ordered homomorphism
that sends
a
to
1
(Theorem 1.1.2 in
[
Ch
]
).
For
a,b\in\Lambda_{+}
,
we write
b\approx a
,
b\lessapprox a
, and
b\ll a

respectively if

\mathopen{\langle}b\mathclose{\rangle}_{\conv}=\mathopen{\langle}a\mathclose{\rangle}_{\conv}
,
b\in\mathopen{\langle}a\mathclose{\rangle}_{\conv}
,
and
b\in\mathopen{\langle}a\mathclose{\rangle}_{\conv}^{\circ}
.
Note that if
b\approx a
,
then
\varepsilon^{b}=\varepsilon^{a}(b)^{-1}\varepsilon^{a}
.

A
\Lambda
-metric space is a space
X
equipped with
a
\Lambda
-metric
\dist
.
The Gromov product
\mathopen{\langle}x,y\mathclose{\rangle}_{u}\in\frac{1}{2}\Lambda
is defined verbatim.
A
\Lambda
-metric space
X
is a
\Lambda
-tree

if it is geodesic,
0
-hyperbolic, and
\mathopen{\langle}x,y\mathclose{\rangle}_{u}\in\Lambda
.
See
[
Ch
]
for a comprehensive treatment
of
\Lambda
-trees (in particular, 2.1.6 and 2.4.3 for
the above definition of a
\Lambda
-tree).
When
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}
, a
\Lambda
-tree is nothing but a simplicial tree.
When
\Lambda=\mathbb{R}
, the notions of
\Lambda
-tree and
\mathbb{R}
-tree coincide.
For a
\Lambda
-tree
X
and
u,v\in X
, we define
the function
h_{u,v}
on
X
by
h_{u,v}(x)=\varepsilon^{\dist(u,v)}(\mathopen{\langle}x,v\mathclose{\rangle}_{u})
.
We denote by
\mathcal{C}(X)\subset\ell_{\infty}X

the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-subalgebra generated by
\{h_{u,v}:u,v\in X\}
.
We define the
tree compactification

\hat{X}
to be
the Gelfand spectrum of the unital commutative
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{C}(X)
.
This definition is compatible with the one for
a
\mathbb{Z}
-tree (see Section 5.2 in
[
BO
]
)
and for an
\mathbb{R}
-tree.
Every isometry on
X
extends to a homeomorphism on
\hat{X}
.

For a
\Lambda
-tree
X
,
x\in X
, and a convex subgroup
M\leq\Lambda
,
we define the
full
M
-subtree
centered at
x
by

X(x,M)\coloneq\{y\in X:\dist(y,x)\in M\}.

For an ordered homomorphism
\varepsilon\colon\Lambda\to\mathbb{R}
,
we consider the associated
\mathbb{R}
-valued pseudo-metric

\dist^{\varepsilon}\coloneq\varepsilon\circ\dist
and denote by

\varepsilon_{*}
the quotient map from
X
onto
the
\varepsilon(\Lambda)
-tree
\varepsilon_{*}(X)\coloneq X/{\sim}
,
where
x\sim y
if
\dist^{\varepsilon}(x,y)=0
.
This process is known as
base change
.

Remark 16
.

Suppose that
T
is a
\Lambda
-tree with
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}
.
There are two cases: either
\Lambda
is discrete or
\Lambda
is dense.
The first case is when

is a simplicial tree.
The second case is handled by Theorem 2.4.7 in
[

]
:
T

is completed to an
\mathbb{R}
-tree

and

, or equivalently,
\hat{T}^{\prime}=\hat{T}
.
Note that every branching point of

has its center in

).

Lemma 17
.

Let
X
be a
\Lambda
-tree.
For every
x\in X
and every convex subgroup
M\leq\Lambda
,
the canonical embedding

\mathcal{C}(X(x,M))\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}(X),\ h_{u,v}\mapsto h_{u,v}

is isometric.
For every
x\in X
and

, the canonical embedding

\mathcal{C}(\varepsilon^{a}_{*}(X(x,\mathopen{\langle}a\mathclose{\rangle}_{\conv})))\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}(X(x,\mathopen{\langle}a\mathclose{\rangle}_{\conv})),\ h_{\varepsilon^{a}_{*}(u),\varepsilon^{a}_{*}(v)}\mapsto h_{u,v}

Let finitely many

Then the subtree

.
Hence for every

)

.
It follows that the “identity” map

into

.

For any
u,v\in X(x,\mathopen{\langle}a\mathclose{\rangle}_{\conv})
with

6.
Amenability of real trees

Theorem 19
.

Let
\Lambda
be either
\mathbb{Z}
or
\mathbb{R}
and
let
\Gamma\curvearrowright T
be an isometric action of
a countable group
\Gamma
on a separable
\Lambda
-tree.
Let
B\subset T
denote the countable set of branching points
and
\hat{T}
denote the tree compactification of
T
.
Assume that
T
satisfies conditions
(\ref{cond:LaTreeNL})
,

(\ref{cond:LaTreeCA})
, and
(\ref{cond:LaTreeAS})

in Theorem
1
.
Then the Borel
\Gamma
-space
\hat{T}\setminus B
is amenable.

Before entering the proof, we recall the notion of
the
length measure

\mu
on an
\mathbb{R}
-tree
T
.
See Section 4.3.5 in
[
Ev
]
.
In case
T
is separable,

\mu
is the unique
\sigma
-finite atomless
Borel measure whose restriction to an arc
coincides with the associated Lebesgue measure.
Every isometry on
T
preserves the length measure.

Proof.

By condition (
3
), all arc stabilizers
for
\Gamma\curvearrowright T
are amenable and hence the stabilizer
subgroup
\Gamma^{x}
at every
x\in\hat{T}\setminus B

is amenable by Lemma
15
.
We prove amenability of
T\setminus B
and
\partial T
separately.

We prove amenability of
\Gamma\curvearrowright T\setminus B
.
Our proof is inspired by
[
GLP
]
.
Thanks to Lemma
6
,
it suffices to show that the orbit equivalence
relation
\mathcal{R}_{\Gamma\curvearrowright T}
is
\mathcal{M}
-amenable.
The case of simplicial tree is trivial as
T
is countable.
We assume that
T
is an
\mathbb{R}
-tree and take
a countable dense subset
\{y_{k}\}
of
T
.
By condition (
1
), one has

T=\bigcup_{k,l}[y_{k},y_{l}]
.
By Lemma
10

It only remains to show Corollary

The permanence property for
free products follows from that of extensions.
Indeed, for a pair

,
the group

The group
\Gamma^{*}
contains the free product of

\Gamma

\Delta
.
See
[

One way to prove the permanence property for extensions

\Gamma
is uniformly exact if and only if
for every

,
the
reduced twisted crossed product
satisfies

This fact can be proved in the same way as

For a normal subgroup
\Delta

Instead, we prove here the permanence property

Proof.

Let

be a group with a normal subgroup

Let a finite unital symmetric subset

for

there is

\bigcup_{p\in\bar{\Gamma}}\supp\eta^{p}\subset\bar{F}^{k}\ \mbox{ and }\ \max_{s\in\bar{F}}\sup_{p\in\bar{\Gamma}}\|\eta^{sp}-s\eta^{p}\|_{1}\leq\varepsilon.

\displaystyle\zeta^{s^{-1}x}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p\in\bar{\Gamma}}\eta^{\bar{s}^{-1}\bar{x}}(p)\varsigma(p)\xi^{\alpha(s,p)^{-1}\varsigma(\bar{s}p)^{-1}x}
