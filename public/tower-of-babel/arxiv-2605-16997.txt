Leray–Hopf Type Weak Solutions for the Three-Dimensional Beris–Edwards System with Stable Landau–de Gennes Potential
Yao Zhang, Han Ni Soe, Zhipeng Xu

Abstract
We prove existence of a weak solution to the three-dimensional Beris–Edwards system in the whole space under the stable bulk assumption c > 0 c>0 . The solution satisfies the natural bounds Q ∈ L t ∞ ​ H x 1 ∩ L t 2 ​ H x 2 Q\in L^{\infty}_{t}H^{1}_{x}\cap L^{2}_{t}H^{2}_{x} and u ∈ L t ∞ ​ L x 2 ∩ L t 2 ​ H x 1 u\in L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}\cap L^{2}_{t}H^{1}_{x} , the distributional form of the equations, and the expanded Leray–Hopf type energy inequality used in weak–strong uniqueness arguments. The proof does not pass directly to the limit in that expanded inequality, where the non-corotational terms contain products of the form | Q n | 4 ​ Q n : ∇ u n |Q^{n}|^{4}Q^{n}:\nabla u^{n} . It first obtains the physical free-energy inequality through a hyperviscous approximation and a localized tail estimate, and then derives the expanded inequality from a low-order chain rule for the bulk part of the energy. The last section records the elementary uniaxial reduction which explains why the present argument is restricted to stable bulk potentials.

We consider the incompressible Beris–Edwards system in
\mathbb{R}^{3}

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}Q+u\cdot\nabla Q-S(\nabla u,Q)=\Gamma H,\\
&\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u=\mu\Delta u-\nabla p+\operatorname{div}(\tau+\sigma),\\
&\operatorname{div}u=0,\\
&(Q,u)|_{t=0}=(Q_{0},u_{0}).\end{aligned}\right.

(1)

Here

is symmetric and traceless,

\displaystyle S(\nabla u,Q)

\displaystyle:=\xi D\left(Q+\frac{1}{3}I_{3}\right)+\xi\left(Q+\frac{1}{3}I_{3}\right)D-2\xi\left(Q+\frac{1}{3}I_{3}\right)\operatorname{tr}(Q\nabla u)

\displaystyle\qquad+\Omega Q-Q\Omega,

(2)

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle:=L\Delta Q-aQ+b\left(Q^{2}-\frac{\operatorname{tr}(Q^{2})}{3}I_{3}\right)-cQ\operatorname{tr}(Q^{2}),

(3)

\displaystyle\tau

\displaystyle:=-\xi\left(Q+\frac{1}{3}I_{3}\right)H-\xi H\left(Q+\frac{1}{3}I_{3}\right)+2\xi\left(Q+\frac{1}{3}I_{3}\right)\operatorname{tr}(QH)-L\nabla Q\odot\nabla Q,

(4)

\displaystyle\sigma

\displaystyle:=QH-HQ.

(5)

2
Algebraic identities

The Beris–Edwards stress is chosen so that the stretching term in the
Q
-equation and the elastic stress in the momentum equation cancel in the energy balance
[
4
,
21
]
. We record the algebraic identity in the form used throughout the proof. The calculation is local in
x
and does not use boundary conditions.

Lemma 2.1

(Basic cancellation)
.

Let
Q,H\in\mathcal{S}^{3}_{0}
and let
\nabla u=D+\Omega
with
D^{T}=D
and
\Omega^{T}=-\Omega
. Define
S,\tau,\sigma
by (
2
), (
4
), and (
5
). Then

(\tau+\sigma):\nabla u+H:S(\nabla u,Q)+L(\nabla Q\odot\nabla Q):\nabla u=0.

(12)

Proof.

The term
-L\nabla Q\odot\nabla Q
in
\tau
gives

\left(-L\nabla Q\odot\nabla Q\right):\nabla u=-L(\nabla Q\odot\nabla Q):\nabla u,

which cancels the last term in (
12
). We only need to check the remaining part.

The skew stress satisfies

\sigma:\nabla u=(QH-HQ):(D+\Omega)=(QH-HQ):\Omega,

because
QH-HQ
is skew-symmetric and hence orthogonal to
D
. By cyclicity of the trace,

(QH-HQ):\Omega=\operatorname{tr}((QH-HQ)\Omega)=\operatorname{tr}(H(\Omega Q-Q\Omega)).

This cancels precisely the contribution
H:(\Omega Q-Q\Omega)
in
H:S
.

For the symmetric part, set
G=Q+I_{3}/3
. The
\xi
-dependent part of
S
is

S_{\xi}=\xi DG+\xi GD-2\xi G\operatorname{tr}(Q\nabla u).

The
\xi
-dependent part of
\tau
is

\tau_{\xi}=-\xi GH-\xi HG+2\xi G\operatorname{tr}(QH).

Since
D
is symmetric and
\operatorname{tr}(Q\nabla u)=\operatorname{tr}(QD)
because
Q
is symmetric and
\Omega
is skew,

\displaystyle\tau_{\xi}:\nabla u

\displaystyle=\tau_{\xi}:D

\displaystyle=-\xi(GH+HG):D+2\xi(G:D)\operatorname{tr}(QH),

whereas

\displaystyle H:S_{\xi}

\displaystyle=\xi H:(DG+GD)-2\xi(H:G)\operatorname{tr}(QD)

\displaystyle=\xi(GH+HG):D-2\xi\operatorname{tr}(GH)\operatorname{tr}(QD).

Because
G:D=Q:D+\operatorname{tr}D/3=Q:D
and
H:G=H:Q+\operatorname{tr}H/3=H:Q
, since
\operatorname{tr}D=\operatorname{div}u=0
and
\operatorname{tr}H=0
, the two expressions cancel. This proves (
12
).
∎

3
The hyperviscous approximation

Let
Q_{0}^{\varepsilon}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3};\mathcal{S}^{3}_{0})
and
u_{0}^{\varepsilon}\in C_{c,\sigma}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
be such that

Q_{0}^{\varepsilon}\to Q_{0}\quad\text{in }H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\qquad u_{0}^{\varepsilon}\to u_{0}\quad\text{in }L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}).

(13)

For each
\varepsilon>0
consider

\partial_{t}Q^{\varepsilon}+u^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla Q^{\varepsilon}-S(\nabla u^{\varepsilon},Q^{\varepsilon})=\Gamma H^{\varepsilon},

(14)

\partial_{t}u^{\varepsilon}+u^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla u^{\varepsilon}=\mu\Delta u^{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon\Delta^{2}u^{\varepsilon}-\nabla p^{\varepsilon}+\operatorname{div}(\tau^{\varepsilon}+\sigma^{\varepsilon}),\qquad\operatorname{div}u^{\varepsilon}=0,

(15)

with
H^{\varepsilon},\tau^{\varepsilon},\sigma^{\varepsilon}
defined from
(Q^{\varepsilon},u^{\varepsilon})
by (
3
), (
4
), (

Proposition 3.1

(Smooth approximate solutions)
.

For each
\varepsilon>0
and
T>0
, system (
14
)–(
15
) has a smooth solution on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
with initial data
(Q_{0}^{\varepsilon},u_{0}^{\varepsilon})
. Moreover, the solution preserves the constraints
Q^{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{S}^{3}_{0}
and
\operatorname{div}u^{\varepsilon}=0
.

Proof.

One may first solve the system on large tori
\mathbb{T}_{R}^{3}
by a Fourier Galerkin approximation, keeping the full expressions
S,\tau,\sigma
unaltered. The equation for
u^{\varepsilon}
contains the fourth-order dissipative term
-\varepsilon\Delta^{2}u^{\varepsilon}
, while the
Q^{\varepsilon}
-equation is a second-order parabolic system with polynomial nonlinearities. Local smooth solutions follow from standard finite-dimensional ODE theory and parabolic bootstrapping. The global energy estimates in Proposition
3.2
below rule out finite-time blow-up of the smooth Galerkin approximations. Passing the Galerkin dimension to infinity gives a smooth solution on
\mathbb{T}_{R}^{3}
; passing
R\to\infty
by local parabolic compactness gives a smooth solution on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
on every finite time interval. The symmetry and tracelessness of
Q^{\varepsilon}
are preserved because the right-hand side of (
14
) is symmetric and traceless whenever
Q^{\varepsilon}
is. The divergence-free condition is enforced by the pressure or, equivalently, by applying the Leray projection to the velocity equation.
∎

Proposition 3.2

(Global physical energy identity)
.

For every
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\|u^{\varepsilon}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\mathcal{F}(Q^{\varepsilon}(t))+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\mu\left\|\nabla u^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon\left\|\Delta u^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\Gamma\left\|H^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{2}\left\|u_{0}^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\mathcal{F}(Q_{0}^{\varepsilon}).

(16)

Proof.

Multiply (
15
) by
u^{\varepsilon}
and integrate. The transport and pressure terms vanish by
\operatorname{div}u^{\varepsilon}=0
, while

\int u^{\varepsilon}\cdot\mu\Delta u^{\varepsilon}=-\mu\left\|\nabla u^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\qquad\int u^{\varepsilon}\cdot(-\varepsilon\Delta^{2}u^{\varepsilon})=-\varepsilon\left\|\Delta u^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

The stress term gives

\int u^{\varepsilon}\cdot\operatorname{div}(\tau^{\varepsilon}+\sigma^{\varepsilon})=-(\tau^{\varepsilon}+\sigma^{\varepsilon}):\nabla u^{\varepsilon}.

Next, since
H=-\delta\mathcal{F}/\delta Q
in the traceless class,

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{F}(Q^{\varepsilon})=-\int H^{\varepsilon}:\partial_{t}Q^{\varepsilon}.

Using (
14
),

-\int H^{\varepsilon}:\partial_{t}Q^{\varepsilon}=\int H^{\varepsilon}:u^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla Q^{\varepsilon}-\int H^{\varepsilon}:S(\nabla u^{\varepsilon},Q^{\varepsilon})-\Gamma\left\|H^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

The transport contribution satisfies

\int H^{\varepsilon}:u^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla Q^{\varepsilon}=-L(\nabla Q^{\varepsilon}\odot\nabla Q^{\varepsilon}):\nabla u^{\varepsilon},

where all lower-order bulk terms are transported by the divergence-free velocity and hence integrate to zero. Adding the two identities and using Lemma
2.1
yields (
16
).
∎

Lemma 3.3

(Uniform natural bounds)
.

For every
T>0
there is
C_{T}
, independent of
\varepsilon
, such that

\displaystyle\left\|u^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})}+\left\|u^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1})}\leq C_{T},

(17)

\displaystyle\sqrt{\varepsilon}\,\left\|\Delta u^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2})}\leq C_{T},

(18)

\displaystyle\left\|Q^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1})}+\left\|Q^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{2})}\leq C_{T},

(19)

\displaystyle\left\|H^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2})}\leq C_{T}.

(20)

Consequently,

Q^{\varepsilon}\text{ is bounded in }L^{10}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{3}),\qquad\nabla Q^{\varepsilon}\text{ is bounded in }L^{4}(0,T;L^{3}).

(21)

Proof.

The bounds for
u^{\varepsilon}
,
\sqrt{\varepsilon}\Delta u^{\varepsilon}
,
H^{\varepsilon}
, and
\nabla Q^{\varepsilon}
follow from (
16
), the lower bound (
9
), and the initial convergence (
13
), modulo a bound for
\left\|Q^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}
. To obtain this bound, multiply (
14
) by
Q^{\varepsilon}
and integrate. The transport term vanishes and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\|Q^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\Gamma L\left\|\nabla Q^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\Gamma c\left\|Q^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{4}}^{4}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|Q^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\int|\nabla u^{\varepsilon}|\bigl(|Q^{\varepsilon}|+|Q^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+|Q^{\varepsilon}|^{3}\bigr)\,dx.

Using Young’s inequality, Sobolev embedding and the Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality
[
11
,
20
]
, together with the already controlled quantities
\nabla u^{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}_{t}L^{2}_{x}
and
\nabla Q^{\varepsilon}\in L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}
, we obtain

\frac{d}{dt}\left\|Q^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C_{T}\left(1+\left\|\nabla u^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\left(1+\left\|Q^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)

a.e. in
t
. Gronwall gives
Q^{\varepsilon}\in L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}
uniformly.

Finally,

L\Delta Q^{\varepsilon}=H^{\varepsilon}+aQ^{\varepsilon}-\mathcal{B}(Q^{\varepsilon}).

Since
Q^{\varepsilon}\in L^{\infty}_{t}H^{1}_{x}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}_{t}L^{6}_{x}
, we have
\mathcal{B}(Q^{\varepsilon})\in L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}\subset L^{2}_{t}L^{2}_{x}
on finite time intervals. This yields

. The interpolation estimates in (

\left\|Q^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{10}_{x}}\lesssim\left\|\Delta Q^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{1/5}\left\|Q^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{6}_{x}}^{4/5},

and

\left\|\nabla Q^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{3}_{x}}\lesssim\left\|\nabla Q^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{1/2}\left\|\nabla Q^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{6}_{x}}^{1/2}.

∎

4
Localized physical energy and tail tightness

Let
\eta_{R}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
satisfy

0\leq\eta_{R}\leq 1,\quad\eta_{R}=0\text{ on }B_{R},\quad\eta_{R}=1\text{ on }\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B_{2R},\quad|\nabla\eta_{R}|\leq C/R,\quad|\Delta\eta_{R}|\leq C/R^{2}.

(22)

Let
A_{R}=\{x:R<|x|<2R\}
.

4.1
Local energy balance

Set

f(Q,\nabla Q)=\frac{L}{2}|\nabla Q|^{2}+\frac{a}{2}|Q|^{2}-\frac{b}{3}\operatorname{tr}(Q^{3})+\frac{c}{4}|Q|^{4},\qquad e^{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{2}|u^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+f(Q^{\varepsilon},\nabla Q^{\varepsilon}).

Lemma 4.1

(Localized physical energy identity)
.

For every
\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int\eta e^{\varepsilon}\,dx+\int\eta\left(\mu|\nabla u^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\Gamma|H^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\varepsilon|\Delta u^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad=-\int\mathcal{J}^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\eta\,dx+\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}_{\rm hyp}[\eta],

(23)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=-u^{\varepsilon}e^{\varepsilon}-p^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon}+\mu(\nabla u^{\varepsilon})u^{\varepsilon}+(\tau^{\varepsilon}+\sigma^{\varepsilon})u^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\qquad+L\nabla Q^{\varepsilon}:\left(S(\nabla u^{\varepsilon},Q^{\varepsilon})+\Gamma H^{\varepsilon}\right),

(24)

and

\displaystyle|\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}_{\rm hyp}[\eta]|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int\eta|\Delta u^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dx+C\varepsilon\int_{\operatorname{supp}\nabla\eta}\left(|\nabla\eta|^{2}|\nabla u^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+|\Delta\eta|^{2}|u^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right)\,dx.

(25)

Proof.

A direct calculation gives

\partial_{t}f+\operatorname{div}(u^{\varepsilon}f)=-H^{\varepsilon}:(\partial_{t}Q^{\varepsilon}+u^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla Q^{\varepsilon})+L\operatorname{div}\left(\nabla Q^{\varepsilon}:(\partial_{t}Q^{\varepsilon}+u^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla Q^{\varepsilon})\right)-L(\nabla Q^{\varepsilon}\odot\nabla Q^{\varepsilon}):\nabla u^{\varepsilon}.

Using
\partial_{t}Q^{\varepsilon}+u^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla Q^{\varepsilon}=S+\Gamma H^{\varepsilon}
gives

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f+\operatorname{div}(u^{\varepsilon}f)+\Gamma|H^{\varepsilon}|^{2}

\displaystyle=-H^{\varepsilon}:S(\nabla u^{\varepsilon},Q^{\varepsilon})-L(\nabla Q^{\varepsilon}\odot\nabla Q^{\varepsilon}):\nabla u^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\quad+L\operatorname{div}\left(\nabla Q^{\varepsilon}:(S(\nabla u^{\varepsilon},Q^{\varepsilon})+\Gamma H^{\varepsilon})\right).

The velocity equation yields

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\frac{|u^{\varepsilon}|^{2}}{2}+\operatorname{div}\left(u^{\varepsilon}\frac{|u^{\varepsilon}|^{2}}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=-\operatorname{div}(p^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon})+\mu\operatorname{div}((\nabla u^{\varepsilon})u^{\varepsilon})-\mu|\nabla u^{\varepsilon}|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\operatorname{div}((\tau^{\varepsilon}+\sigma^{\varepsilon})u^{\varepsilon})-(\tau^{\varepsilon}+\sigma^{\varepsilon}):\nabla u^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\quad-\varepsilon u^{\varepsilon}\cdot\Delta^{2}u^{\varepsilon}.

Adding these identities and using Lemma
2.1
gives the local balance except for the hyperviscous term. For the latter,

\int\eta u^{\varepsilon}\cdot\Delta^{2}u^{\varepsilon}\,dx=\int\Delta(\eta u^{\varepsilon})\cdot\Delta u^{\varepsilon}\,dx=\int\eta|\Delta u^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dx+\text{commutators},

and the commutators are bounded by (
25
) by Cauchy’s inequality. This proves (
23
).
∎

(Localized

For every

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int\eta_{R}|Q^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dx+\Gamma L\int\eta_{R}|\nabla Q^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dx+\Gamma c\int\eta_{R}|Q^{\varepsilon}|^{4}\,dx

4.3
Tail tightness

Choose
M\geq 1
so large that

\frac{1}{2}|u|^{2}+\frac{L}{2}|\nabla Q|^{2}+\frac{a}{2}|Q|^{2}-\frac{b}{3}\operatorname{tr}(Q^{3})+\frac{c}{4}|Q|^{4}+\frac{M}{2}|Q|^{2}\geq c_{0}\left(|u|^{2}+|\nabla Q|^{2}+|Q|^{2}+|Q|^{4}\right).

(29)

Define the modified tail energy

Y_{R}^{\varepsilon}(t):=\int\eta_{R}\left(e^{\varepsilon}+\frac{M}{2}|Q^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right)\,dx.

(30)

Lemma 4.3

(Boundary fluxes vanish uniformly)
.

For every
T>0
,

\lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{T}\left|\int\mathcal{J}^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\eta_{R}\,dx\right|\,dt=0,

(31)

\lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{T}|\mathcal{R}_{R}^{\varepsilon}(t)|\,dt=0,

(32)

and the hyperviscous commutator in (
25
) is uniformly
o_{R}(1)
after absorbing
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int\eta_{R}|\Delta u^{\varepsilon}|^{2}
into the left-hand side.

Proof.

We first record the integrability of the flux. From Lemma
3.3
,

u^{\varepsilon}\in L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}\cap L^{2}_{t}L^{6}_{x},\quad Q^{\varepsilon}\in L^{\infty}_{t}H^{1}_{x}\cap L^{2}_{t}H^{2}_{x},\quad H^{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}_{t,x},

which imply

Q^{\varepsilon}\in L^{10}_{t,x}\cap L^{4}_{t}L^{12}_{x},\qquad\nabla Q^{\varepsilon}\in L^{4}_{t}L^{3}_{x}\cap L^{2}_{t}L^{6}_{x}.

Then the terms
u^{\varepsilon}e^{\varepsilon}
are in
L^{1}_{t,x}
:

|u|^{3}\in L^{1},\quad|u||\nabla Q|^{2}\in L^{1},\quad|u||Q|^{4}\in L^{1}.

The last one follows from
u\in L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}
and
Q\in L^{4}_{t}L^{8}_{x}
.

The stress flux is also integrable. The tensor
\tau+\sigma
is bounded pointwise by

C\left(|H|+|Q||H|+|Q|^{2}|H|+|\nabla Q|^{2}\right).

Multiplying by
u
, the terms are controlled respectively by

H\in L^{2}L^{2},u\in L^{2}L^{2};

Q\in L^{\infty}L^{6},H\in L^{2}L^{2},u\in L^{2}L^{3};

Q^{2}\in L^{\infty}L^{3},H\in L^{2}L^{2},u\in L^{2}L^{6};

and

\nabla Q\in L^{4}L^{3},\nabla Q\in L^{4}L^{3},u\in L^{2}L^{3}.

Here and below
L^{p}L^{q}
abbreviates
L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
.

For the last part of
\mathcal{J}^{\varepsilon}
,

\nabla Q:(S+\Gamma H),

the term
\nabla Q:H
is in
L^{1}
. The remaining terms are bounded by

|\nabla Q|\,|\nabla u|(1+|Q|+|Q|^{2}),

which are integrable by using respectively

\nabla Q\in L^{\infty}L^{2},\nabla u\in L^{2}L^{2};

\nabla Q\in L^{2}L^{6},\nabla u\in L^{2}L^{2},Q\in L^{\infty}L^{3};

and

\nabla Q\in L^{2}L^{6},\nabla u\in L^{2}L^{2},Q^{2}\in L^{\infty}L^{3}.

The pressure is recovered from

-\Delta p^{\varepsilon}=\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\left(u_{i}^{\varepsilon}u_{j}^{\varepsilon}-(\tau^{\varepsilon}+\sigma^{\varepsilon})_{ij}\right).

Calderon–Zygmund estimates
[
5
]
and the bounds just listed give

p^{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}L^{2}+L^{2}L^{3/2}+L^{2}L^{6/5}+L^{2}L^{3/2}.

Pairing with

u^{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}L^{2}\cap L^{2}L^{3}\cap L^{2}L^{6}

shows
p^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon}\in L^{1}_{t,x}
. Hence

\left\|\mathcal{J}^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{1}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C_{T}

independently of
\varepsilon
, and therefore

\int_{0}^{T}\left|\int\mathcal{J}^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\eta_{R}\right|\,dt\leq\frac{C}{R}\left\|\mathcal{J}^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{1}_{t,x}}\leq\frac{C_{T}}{R}\to 0.

For (
32
), use (
27
) and the global bounds:

\frac{1}{R}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{A_{R}}|u||Q|^{2}\leq\frac{C_{T}}{R},\qquad\frac{1}{R}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{A_{R}}|Q||\nabla Q|\leq\frac{C_{T}}{R}.

Finally, (
25
) gives

C\varepsilon\int_{0}^{T}\int_{A_{R}}\left(R^{-2}|\nabla u^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+R^{-4}|u^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right)\leq C_{T}\varepsilon(R^{-2}+R^{-4})=o_{R}(1),

while the term
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int\eta_{R}|\Delta u^{\varepsilon}|^{2}
is absorbed.
∎

Proposition 4.4

(Uniform tail tightness)
.

For every
T>0
,

\lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{\varepsilon}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}Y_{R}^{\varepsilon}(t)=0,

(33)

and

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{|x|>R}\left(|\nabla u^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+|H^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+|\nabla Q^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+|Q^{\varepsilon}|^{4}\right)\,dx\,dt=0,

(34)

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{|x|>R}|\Delta Q^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dx\,dt=0.

(35)

Proof.

Combining Lemma
4.1
, Lemma
4.2
, the estimate (
28
), and choosing
\delta
sufficiently small, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}Y_{R}^{\varepsilon}(t)

\displaystyle+c_{0}\int\eta_{R}\left(|\nabla u^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+|H^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+|\nabla Q^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+|Q^{\varepsilon}|^{4}\right)\,dx+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int\eta_{R}|\Delta u^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}Y_{R/2}^{\varepsilon}(t)+\omega_{R}^{\varepsilon}(t),

(36)

where

\lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{T}|\omega_{R}^{\varepsilon}(t)|\,dt=0

by Lemma
4.3
.
Integrating in time,

Y_{R}^{\varepsilon}(t)\leq Y_{R}^{\varepsilon}(0)+C_{T}\int_{0}^{t}Y_{R/2}^{\varepsilon}(s)\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\omega_{R}^{\varepsilon}(s)\,ds.

Because
Q_{0}^{\varepsilon}\to Q_{0}
in
H^{1}
and
u_{0}^{\varepsilon}\to u_{0}
in
L^{2}
, the initial data are uniformly tight:

\lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{\varepsilon}Y_{R}^{\varepsilon}(0)=0.

Let

Z_{R}(t)=\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}Y_{R}^{\varepsilon}(s).

Then

Z_{R}(t)\leq Z_{R}(0)+C_{T}\int_{0}^{t}Z_{R/2}(s)\,ds+o_{R}(1).

Letting
R\to\infty
and using monotonicity in
R
gives

Z(t)\leq C_{T}\int_{0}^{t}Z(s)\,ds,\qquad Z(t):=\lim_{R\to\infty}Z_{R}(t).

Gronwall implies
Z(t)=0
, proving (
33
). The dissipative tail estimate (
34
) follows by integrating (
36
) and using (
33
).

Finally,

L\Delta Q^{\varepsilon}=H^{\varepsilon}+aQ^{\varepsilon}-\mathcal{B}(Q^{\varepsilon}),

so

|\Delta Q^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\leq C\left(|H^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+|Q^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+|Q^{\varepsilon}|^{4}+|Q^{\varepsilon}|^{6}\right).

The first three tail terms are controlled by (
33
) and (
34
). For the last one, a tail Sobolev estimate gives

\int_{|x|>R}|Q^{\varepsilon}|^{6}\,dx\leq C\left(\int_{|x|>R/2}(|\nabla Q^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+|Q^{\varepsilon}|^{2})\,dx\right)^{3},

which tends to zero uniformly after integration in time. This proves (
35
).
∎

5
Compactness and the physical free-energy inequality

Proposition 5.1

(Compactness)
.

There exists a subsequence, still denoted by
\varepsilon
, and a pair
(Q,u)
such that

\displaystyle Q^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\rightharpoonup^{*}Q\quad\text{in }L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}),\qquad Q^{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup Q\quad\text{in }L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}),

\displaystyle u^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\rightharpoonup^{*}u\quad\text{in }L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}),\qquad u^{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup u\quad\text{in }L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}),

\displaystyle Q^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\to Q\quad\text{strongly in }L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),

\displaystyle u^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\to u\quad\text{strongly in }L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(Q^{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{B}(Q)\quad\text{strongly in }L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}),

\displaystyle H^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\rightharpoonup H:=L\Delta Q-aQ+\mathcal{B}(Q)\quad\text{in }L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}).

Moreover,
(Q,u)
satisfies the weak formulation of (
1
).

Proof.

The weak convergences follow from Lemma
3.3
. The equations imply that
\partial_{t}Q^{\varepsilon}
and
\partial_{t}u^{\varepsilon}
are bounded in negative Sobolev spaces locally in space; hence the compactness lemma of Aubin, Lions and Simon gives local strong compactness
[
2
,
19
,
24
]
:

Q^{\varepsilon}\to Q\quad\text{in }L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(B_{R})),\qquad u^{\varepsilon}\to u\quad\text{in }L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(B_{R}))

for every
R>0
. Proposition
4.4
upgrades the first convergence to the whole space,

Q^{\varepsilon}\to Q\quad\text{in }L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})).

Interpolating with the uniform
L^{\infty}_{t}H^{1}_{x}
bound gives

Q^{\varepsilon}\to Q\quad\text{in }L^{p}(0,T;L^{6}),\qquad 1\leq p<\infty.

This implies
\mathcal{B}(Q^{\varepsilon})\to\mathcal{B}(Q)
strongly in
L^{2}_{t}L^{2}_{x}
. Since
\Delta Q^{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup\Delta Q
in
L^{2}_{t}L^{2}_{x}
,
H^{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup H
follows.

The weak formulation is obtained by passing to the limit in the approximate equations. The hyperviscous term vanishes because

\left\|\varepsilon\Delta^{2}u^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{-2})}\leq\varepsilon\left\|\Delta u^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2})}\leq\sqrt{\varepsilon}C_{T}\to 0.

All other nonlinear terms pass to the limit by the local strong convergence of
u^{\varepsilon},Q^{\varepsilon},\nabla Q^{\varepsilon}
and the weak convergence of
H^{\varepsilon},\nabla u^{\varepsilon}
.
∎

Proposition 5.2

(Physical free-energy inequality)
.

The limit
(Q,u)
satisfies (
11
) for every
t\in[0,T]
.

Proof.

From (
16
), dropping the nonnegative hyperviscous dissipation,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\|u^{\varepsilon}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\mathcal{F}(Q^{\varepsilon}(t))+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\mu\left\|\nabla u^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\Gamma\left\|H^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|u_{0}^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\mathcal{F}(Q_{0}^{\varepsilon}).

The right-hand side converges to
\frac{1}{2}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\mathcal{F}(Q_{0})
. The dissipative terms are weakly lower semicontinuous. The strong convergence
Q^{\varepsilon}\to Q
in
L^{2}_{t}H^{1}_{x}
yields, after extracting a further subsequence if necessary,

Q^{\varepsilon}(t)\to Q(t)\quad\text{strongly in }H^{1}

for a.e.
t
. Therefore
\mathcal{F}(Q^{\varepsilon}(t))\to\mathcal{F}(Q(t))
for a.e.
t
, while
u^{\varepsilon}(t)\rightharpoonup u(t)
in
L^{2}
for a.e.
t
. Passing to the lower limit gives (
11
) for a.e.
t
. The standard weak-continuity representative of Leray–Hopf solutions extends the inequality to all
t\in[0,T]
.
∎

6
The low-order chain rule and recovery of the Leray–Hopf inequality

Define

\mathcal{G}(Q):=\frac{1}{2}\left\|Q\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(\frac{a}{2}|Q|^{2}-\frac{b}{3}\operatorname{tr}(Q^{3})+\frac{c}{4}|Q|^{4}\right)\,dx.

(37)

Then

D\mathcal{G}(Q)=(1-a)Q+\mathcal{B}(Q)=:A(Q).

(38)

Lemma 6.1

(Low-order chain rule)
.

For the weak solution obtained above,

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(Q(t))-\mathcal{G}(Q_{0})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\left(A(Q),S(\nabla u,Q)\right)\,ds+\Gamma\int_{0}^{t}\left(A(Q),H\right)\,ds

(39)

for every
t\in[0,T]
.

Proof.

The regularity

Q\in L^{\infty}_{t}H^{1}_{x}\cap L^{2}_{t}H^{2}_{x}

implies
Q\in L^{10}_{t,x}
, hence
|Q|^{4}Q\in L^{2}_{t,x}
and
A(Q)\in L^{2}_{t,x}
. Since
H\in L^{2}_{t,x}
, the term
(A(Q),H)
is integrable. Moreover,

|A(Q):S(\nabla u,Q)|\leq C|\nabla u|(|Q|+|Q|^{2}+|Q|^{3}+|Q|^{4}+|Q|^{5}),

which is integrable because
\nabla u\in L^{2}_{t,x}
and
Q\in L^{10}_{t,x}
.

The argument follows the standard time-mollification device used in energy identities for weak solutions
[
23
,
12
]
. Let
\rho_{\delta}
be a time mollifier and set
Q^{\delta}=\rho_{\delta}*_{t}Q
. After multiplying the time-mollified
Q
-equation by
A(Q^{\delta})
and integrating on
(0,t)\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we may pass
\delta\to 0
by the strong convergence

A(Q^{\delta})\to A(Q)\quad\text{in }L^{2}_{\operatorname{loc}}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{3})

and by the integrability estimates just stated. The transport term vanishes: indeed, if

g(Q)=\frac{1-a}{2}|Q|^{2}+\frac{b}{3}\operatorname{tr}(Q^{3})-\frac{c}{4}|Q|^{4},

then
A(Q)=Dg(Q)
and

A(Q):u\cdot\nabla Q=u\cdot\nabla g(Q).

Using spatial cutoffs
\chi_{R}
and
\operatorname{div}u=0
,

\int_{0}^{t}\int\chi_{R}u\cdot\nabla g(Q)=-\int_{0}^{t}\int g(Q)u\cdot\nabla\chi_{R}\to 0

as
R\to\infty
, since
g(Q)u\in L^{1}_{t,x}
. We obtain (
39
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

By Proposition
5.1
we have a weak solution satisfying the natural Leray–Hopf regularity and the distributional equations. By Proposition
5.2
, it satisfies the physical free-energy inequality (
11
). Adding the chain-rule identity (
39
) to (
11
) yields the desired expanded inequality.

Indeed,

\mathcal{F}(Q)+\mathcal{G}(Q)=\frac{1}{2}\left\|Q\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{L}{2}\left\|\nabla Q\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Furthermore,

H-A(Q)=L\Delta Q-Q,

so

\displaystyle\left\|H\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-(A(Q),H)

\displaystyle=(H,L\Delta Q-Q)

\displaystyle=L^{2}\left\|\Delta Q\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+(a+1)L\left\|\nabla Q\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+a\left\|Q\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+L(\mathcal{B}(Q),\Delta Q)-(\mathcal{B}(Q),Q).

This accounts for the dissipative terms and the term

2\Gamma\int_{0}^{t}(\mathcal{B}(Q),Q-L\Delta Q)\,ds

on the right-hand side of (
10
).

It remains to expand
(A(Q),S(\nabla u,Q))
. Since
A(Q)
is a polynomial in
Q
, it commutes with
Q
and is symmetric. Hence

A(Q):(\Omega Q-Q\Omega)=0.

Using (
2
) and
\operatorname{tr}(Q\Omega)=0
,

\displaystyle(A(Q),S(\nabla u,Q))

\displaystyle=2\xi\left(A(Q)\left(Q+\frac{1}{3}I_{3}\right):\nabla u\right)-2\xi\left(A(Q):\left(Q+\frac{1}{3}I_{3}\right)\right)\operatorname{tr}(Q\nabla u).

Substituting
A(Q)=(1-a)Q+\mathcal{B}(Q)
gives exactly the two
\xi
-dependent lines on the right-hand side of (
10
) after multiplying the whole inequality by
2
. Thus (
10
) holds for every
t\in[0,T]
. Since
T>0
was arbitrary, the construction is global.
∎

7
Comments on nonstable bulk parameters

The proof above uses the sign of the quartic term in the bulk potential. The following elementary reduction explains the restriction and gives a simple obstruction to a direct large-data extension of the same argument. It is included only to clarify the role of the assumption
c>0
; it is not used in the existence proof.

Let

A_{0}=\operatorname{diag}(2,-1,-1)\in\mathcal{S}^{3}_{0}.

Then

A_{0}^{2}-\frac{\operatorname{tr}(A_{0}^{2})}{3}I_{3}=A_{0},\qquad\operatorname{tr}(A_{0}^{2})=6.

In the corotational case
\xi=0
, take
u=0
and
Q(t,x)=q(t,x)A_{0}
. The
Q
-equation reduces to

\partial_{t}q=\Gamma\left(L\Delta q-aq+bq^{2}-6cq^{3}\right).

(40)

When
c<0
, the cubic term is focusing,
-6cq^{3}=6|c|q^{3}
. For nonnegative compactly supported data of sufficiently large size, Kaplan’s eigenfunction method on a large ball yields finite-time blow-up of a positive moment of
q

[
17
]
. When
c=0
and
b\neq 0
, the quadratic term
bq^{2}
is focusing after choosing the sign of
q
. This reduction shows why the stable case is separated in the theorem. The case
c=0=b
has no polynomial focusing term in the bulk force and can be treated as a separate linear-bulk case; it is not included here in order to keep the proof tied to the coercive potential used in the tail estimate.
