Clinical utility of preoperative lymphocyte–albumin–neutrophil ratio for prognostication in patients with oral cancer undergoing surgery
Authors: Hung-Hua Chen, Yu-Tsai Lin, Hui-Ching Chuang, Chih-Yen Chien, Chao-Hui Yang, Fu-Min Fang, Hui Lu, Ming-Hsien Tsai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground:Systemic inflammation and nutritional status are key determinants of prognosis in oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC). The neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio and serum albumin in peripheral blood are established markers reflecting host immune response and nutritional condition, respectively. The lymphocyte–albumin–neutrophil ratio (LANR), a composite index integrating these parameters, has emerged as a potential prognostic biomarker in several cancers; however, its role in OSCC remains unclear. This study aimed to evaluate the prognostic value of preoperative LANR in OSCC.Methods:We retrospectively analyzed OSCC patients who underwent radical surgery between 2007 and 2017, randomly dividing them into training and validation cohorts by a 2:1 ratio. LANR was calculated by multiplying the absolute lymphocyte count by the serum albumin level and dividing by the absolute neutrophil count. The primary outcomes were 5-year overall survival (OS) and disease-free survival (DFS). Survival-associated factors were identified through univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses, serving as the foundation for constructing a predictive nomogram model.Results:This study included a total of 660 patients, with 440 assigned to the training cohort and 220 to the validation cohort. A LANR cutoff value of 18.7 effectively stratified patients into distinct prognostic groups, demonstrating significant differences in survival outcomes. A low LANR independently predicted worse OS (hazard ratio [HR]: 1.54, 95% CI: 1.04-2.28; p = 0.03) and DFS (HR: 1.54, 95% CI: 1.08-2.19; p = 0.017). The LANR-based nomogram demonstrated strong predictive performance, with concordance indices of 0.75 and 0.71 in the training and validation cohorts, respectively.Conclusion:Preoperative LANR serves as an independent prognostic marker in patients with OSCC. Incorporating LANR into risk models may enhance patient stratification and guide treatment strategies.Keywords: Inflammation, LANR, NLR, Nutrition, Oral cancer, Serum albumin

1. INTRODUCTION

Oral cancer affected approximately 400 000 people in 2020, with 178 000 deaths, ranking 16th globally in incidence and mortality, according to the International Agency for Research on Cancer.1 Oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) accounts for over 90% of oral malignancies, with a higher prevalence in males, particularly in South Asia. In Taiwan, OSCC was the third most common cancer in men and the fifth overall in 2021.2 In clinical practice, the tumor–node–metastasis (TNM) staging system is the most commonly used and convenient tool for prognostic assessment in OSCC, although its accuracy is limited due to disease heterogeneity that includes factors such as underlying conditions and nutritional status. As a result, emerging biomarkers have gained increasing importance in biomedical research. Recent studies on the interplay between immune-inflammatory status and cancer pathogenesis have highlighted the significant tumor-promoting role of immune cells, particularly within the innate immune system. The neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) is one of the most well-known immune-inflammation markers, defined as the absolute neutrophil count (ANC) divided by the absolute lymphocyte count (ALC) in peripheral blood. It has been proposed as a pretreatment biomarker and identified as an independent prognostic factor for survival in various malignancies,3–6 including OSCC.7

Nutritional status is a crucial factor in OSCC prognosis. Serum albumin, a key nutritional marker, has been associated with survival outcomes in various cancers.8 Systemic inflammation can lower serum albumin levels, leading to poor performance status, weight loss, and worse prognosis.9 Our previous studies have also demonstrated a correlation between lower serum albumin levels and increased postoperative complications in head and neck cancer patients.10,11

The lymphocyte–albumin–neutrophil ratio (LANR) is a novel biomarker integrating serum albumin and NLR. It has been identified as an independent prognostic factor in colorectal and nasopharyngeal cancers,12,13 although its prognostic value in OSCC remains unclear. Accordingly, this study aimed to assess the impact of preoperative LANR on OSCC survival outcomes and develop a predictive nomogram to validate its prognostic utility.

2. METHODS

2.1. Study population

This retrospective study included patients with histologically confirmed first primary OSCC who underwent primary curative surgery at Kaohsiung Chang Gung Memorial Hospital, Taiwan, between January 2007 and April 2017. The TNM stage was reclassified according to the 8th edition of the American Joint Committee on Cancer staging system. Patients receiving upfront surgery with or without adjuvant therapy were eligible for this study, as all such patients had complete clinical and pathological information available for review. Patients were excluded if they had (a) a previous history of malignancy; (b) distant metastatic disease at diagnosis; (c) received any preceding treatment, such as neoadjuvant chemotherapy, radiotherapy, or concurrent chemoradiotherapy before surgery; (d) clinical evidence of acute infection within 4 weeks before blood sampling; or (e) incomplete preoperative laboratory data, including differential white blood cell count in peripheral blood and serum albumin value. The indication for postoperative adjuvant therapy, including radiotherapy and chemoradiotherapy, was determined by a multidisciplinary tumor board in accordance with the American National Comprehensive Cancer Network guideline.

2.2. Study design and data collection

Patients were randomly divided in a 2:1 ratio into training and validation cohorts, with the randomization being performed using simple random sampling in R statistical software. Each patient was assigned a unique index, and 66.7% of cases were randomly selected for the training cohort using the sample() function, with the remaining 33.3% allocated to the validation cohort. A fixed random seed (set.seed) was applied to ensure reproducibility. Predictive models were developed using patient characteristics and survival data from the training cohort, and their performance was subsequently validated in the validation cohort.

Clinical and pathological variables were collected, including sex, age, TNM stage, primary cancer location, depth of invasion (DOI), surgical margin status, perineural invasion (PNI), lymphovascular invasion (LVI), extranodal extension (ENE), and treatment modalities. Preoperative laboratory data, obtained within 1 week before surgery, were used to calculate the LANR using the following formula: LANR = ALC (×109/L) × serum albumin (g/L)/ANC (×109/L).

The optimal cutoff value for LANR was determined using X-tile software (version 3.6.1; Yale University, New Haven, CT),14 stratifying patients into high- and low-LANR groups. Outcomes of interest included 5-year overall survival (OS) and disease-free survival (DFS). OS was defined as the time from radical surgery to death from any cause or last follow-up, while DFS was defined as the time from radical surgery to disease recurrence, death, or last follow-up.

2.3. Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using R Studio and SPSS 25.0 software (SPSS/IBM, Inc., Chicago, IL). Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were used to evaluate the association between clinical variables and survival outcomes, with the hazard ratio (HR) and corresponding 95% CI reported. A two-tailed test, with a p value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

A predictive nomogram for 5-year OS was developed based on independent prognostic factors identified through multivariate analysis in the training cohort, using the “rms” package in R (Version 5.1–0; Vanderbilt University, Nashville, TN). Model performance was evaluated using the concordance index (C index), and calibration plots were generated to assess the agreement between predicted and observed survival probabilities. Internal validation was performed via bootstrap resampling. This study was approved by the Medical Ethics and Human Clinical Trial Committees of Chang Gung Memorial Hospital for research involving human participants (Ethical Application Reference Number: 202500190B0) and was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. The requirement for informed consent was waived by the committee.

3.1. Prognostic significance of LANR in OSCC

The median follow-up for the entire cohort was 69.05 months, where treatment failure occurred in 100 (22.7%) patients in the training cohort and 39 (17.7%) patients in the validation cohort. The 5-year OS rates were 77.5% in the entire cohort, 78.4% in the training cohort, and 75.7% in the validation cohort. The corresponding 5-year DFS rates were 73.3% in the entire cohort, 74.2% in the training cohort, and 71.5% in the validation cohort. Kaplan–Meier survival curves demonstrated that patients in the low-LANR group had significantly poorer survival outcomes compared to those in the high-LANR group in both training and validation cohorts. In the training cohort, the 5-year OS rate was 82.9% in the high-LANR group and 72.7% in the low-LANR group (p = 0.001); similarly, in the validation cohort, the 5-year OS rate was 80.6% for high-LANR vs 69.8% for low-LANR (p = 0.044) (Fig. 2A and B). With respect to DFS, patients in the high-LANR group had significantly better outcomes. In the training cohort, the 5-year DFS rate was 79.2% for the high-LANR group compared to 68.1% for the low-LANR group (p = 0.004). In the validation cohort, the 5-year DFS rate was 76.4% vs 65.8% in the high- and low-LANR groups, respectively (p = 0.045) (Fig. 2C and D).

Kaplan–Meier survival curves according to different groups of LANR. A, Training cohort in OS. B, Validation cohort in OS. C, Training cohort in DFS. D, Validation cohort in DFS. DFS = disease-free survival; LANR = lymphocyte–albumin–neutrophil ratio; OS = overall survival.

To assess whether the prognostic effect of LANR varied by tumor location, subgroup analyses were performed comparing tongue and non-tongue cancers. In tongue cancer patients, higher LANR was associated with improved 5-year OS and DFS in the entire and training cohorts, while in the validation cohort, the trends were similar but did not reach statistical significance, likely due to the smaller sample size. Similarly, in non-tongue cancer patients, high LANR consistently predicted better OS and DFS across cohorts. These findings indicate that the prognostic effect of LANR was independent of tumor site. Detailed survival analyses are presented in Supplementary Table S1, https://links.lww.com/JCMA/A397.

Stage-stratified analyses were conducted using the entire cohort to evaluate whether the prognostic impact of LANR differed between early-stage (stages I/II) and advanced-stage (stages III/IV) OSCC. Among early-stage patients (N = 308), no significant differences in OS or DFS were observed between the low- and high-LANR groups (5-year OS: 91.3% vs 91.3%, p = 0.88; 5-year DFS: 85.9% vs 88.4%, p = 0.708). In advanced-stage patients (N = 352), a trend toward improved OS was observed in the high-LANR group compared with the low-LANR group (69.2% vs 62.7%; p = 0.058), whereas DFS differences were not statistically significant (63.8% vs 58.7%; p = 0.107). No statistically significant interaction between LANR and tumor stage was identified, supporting the interpretation that LANR primarily reflects host-related inflammatory and nutritional status rather than tumor burden alone.

Furthermore, to address concerns regarding cutoff dependency, LANR was analyzed as a continuous variable in the entire cohort. Higher preoperative LANR values remained significantly associated with improved OS (HR: 0.98; 95% CI: 0.97-1.00; p = 0.017) and DFS (HR: 0.98; 95% CI: 0.97-1.00; p = 0.027), supporting the robustness of LANR as a prognostic marker independent of dichotomization.

3.2. Independent prognosticators for OS

In the training cohort, univariate Cox regression analysis identified several variables that met the predetermined significance threshold (p < 0.05), including pathological stage, histological grade, DOI, presence of PNI, LVI, ENE, surgical margin, need for adjuvant therapy, and preoperative LANR.

In the training cohort, univariate Cox regression analysis identified several variables that met the predetermined significance threshold (p < 0.05), including pathological TNM stage, DOI, histological grade, presence of PNI, LVI, ENE, surgical margin status, requirement for adjuvant therapy, and preoperative LANR. Subsequent multivariate Cox regression analysis was conducted to assess the independent associations between survival outcomes and the significant variables identified in the univariate analysis. Low preoperative LANR remained a significant independent negative prognostic factor for OS (HR: 1.54; 95% CI: 1.04-2.28, p = 0.03). The advanced pathological stage was also significantly correlated with worse OS (HR: 2.57; 95% CI: 1.35-4.93, p = 0.004). Compared with well-differentiated squamous cell carcinoma (WDSCC), both moderately-differentiated squamous cell carcinoma (MDSCC) and poorly-differentiated squamous cell carcinoma (PDSCC) tumors were associated with poorer OS (MDSCC: HR: 1.57, 95% CI: 1.05-2.36, p = 0.029; PDSCC: HR: 5.03, 95% CI: 1.86-13.59, p = 0.001). Close surgical margins (1-4 mm) were also linked to decreased OS compared to adequate margins (HR: 4.90, 95% CI: 1.10-21.82, p = 0.037), and the presence of ENE was another independent adverse prognostic factor (HR: 1.85, 95% CI: 1.12-3.08, p = 0.017) (Table 3).

3.3. Independent prognosticators for DFS

In the training cohort, variables met the predetermined significance threshold (p < 0.05) in the univariate Cox regression analysis, including pathological TNM stage, tumor location, DOI, histological grade, presence of PNI, LVI, ENE, surgical margin, need for adjuvant therapy, and preoperative LANR. Multivariate Cox regression analysis was subsequently performed to determine independent prognostic factors for DFS. Low preoperative LANR remained a significant independent predictor of inferior DFS (HR: 1.54, 95% CI: 1.08-2.19; p = 0.017). Advanced pathological stage was also associated with poorer DFS (HR: 1.98; 95% CI: 1.13-3.47; p = 0.017). Furthermore, compared to WDSCC, both MDSCC and PDSCC tumors were independently linked to unfavorable DFS (MDSCC: HR: 1.65, 95% CI: 1.14-2.38, p = 0.008; PDSCC: HR: 4.85, 95% CI: 1.95-12.08, p = 0.001). Close surgical margins (1-4 mm) were significantly associated with decreased DFS compared to adequate margins (HR: 5.73, 95% CI: 1.28-25.60, p = 0.022). Additionally, the presence of ENE was identified as an independent adverse prognostic factor for DFS (HR: 1.70, 95% CI: 1.06-2.72, p = 0.028) (Table 4).

3.4. Development of the prognostic model for OS based on LANR

To improve the accuracy of OS prediction in patients with OSCC, a novel prognostic nomogram was constructed by integrating the significant independent prognostic factors identified through multivariate Cox regression analysis in the training cohort. As illustrated in Fig. 3A, surgical margin status was identified as the most impactful predictor of OS, followed sequentially by histologic grade, pathological stage, ENE, and preoperative LANR. Each variable in the nomogram was assigned a weighted point value based on its prognostic contribution, and a cumulative score for individual patients was derived by summing these values. The 5-year OS probability was then estimated by mapping the total score onto the survival probability scale embedded within the nomogram. Model calibration was assessed using calibration plots for both the training (Fig. 3B) and validation (Fig. 3C) cohorts, which demonstrated good alignment between the predicted and observed OS rates, supporting the model’s reliability. The nomogram demonstrated robust predictive performance, yielding a C index of 0.75 (95% CI: 0.71-0.80) in the training cohort and 0.71 (95% CI: 0.65-0.77) in the validation cohort. These values surpassed those of the conventional TNM staging system, which yielded C indexes of 0.71 (95% CI: 0.67-0.75) and 0.69 (95% CI: 0.63-0.75) in the training and validation cohorts, respectively, highlighting the potential clinical utility of the LANR-based model in risk stratification and individualized management of patients with OSCC.

4. DISCUSSION

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to explore the clinical significance of the preoperative immune-inflammatory-nutritional index, LANR, in OSCC. Our findings demonstrate that preoperative LANR is an independent prognostic factor, with lower LANR significantly associated with poorer 5-year OS and DFS rates.

LANR integrates serum albumin concentration, ANC, and ALC in peripheral blood, reflecting nutritional, inflammatory, and immune status. Malnutrition is a common complication in cancer progression, particularly in head and neck cancers.15 Serum albumin, primarily synthesized by the liver, plays a crucial role in regulating plasma oncotic pressure, tissue fluid distribution, vascular permeability, acid–base balance, and immune modulation. It also reduces oxidative stress, prevents endothelial apoptosis, inhibits coagulation, and transports endogenous and exogenous compounds.16 Given its essential biological functions, serum albumin is widely recognized as a key nutritional marker, with lower levels linked to tumor progression.17 Numerous studies have also identified serum albumin and its associated biomarkers as an independent prognostic factor in various cancers.18–23 Lymphocytes play a pivotal role in antitumor immunity, particularly within the tumor microenvironment. Higher levels of CD8+ T cells are often associated with improved immune therapy responsiveness and better prognosis.24,25 Conversely, systemic inflammation, a hallmark of cancer, is largely mediated by neutrophils, which release bioactive molecules such as growth factors, proangiogenic factors, and extracellular matrix-modifying enzymes. Neutrophils also produce reactive oxygen species, which promote genetic mutations and drive cancer progression26 wherein elevated neutrophil levels have been linked to worse survival outcomes in several cancers, including OSCC.27 Given these associations, patients with a low LANR might have a poorer prognosis in OSCC.

In cancer research, LANR has shown significant prognostic potential across multiple tumor types, correlating with survival outcomes and disease progression. Studies in colorectal cancer, pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, and nasopharyngeal carcinoma have identified LANR cutoff values to effectively stratify patients into high- and low-risk groups for disease progression and mortality.12,13,28 The optimal LANR cutoff varies by cancer type, generally ranging from 14 to 18, with lower values associated with poorer prognosis. For example, in colorectal cancer, a LANR threshold of 16.81 independently predicts survival,13 while in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, a threshold of 18.145 distinguishes patients at higher risk of adverse outcomes.28 Similarly, in nasopharyngeal carcinoma, the optimal cutoff is 14.3.12 In our study, the optimal preoperative LANR cutoff was determined to be 18.7. The variability in cutoff values highlights the need for cancer-specific investigations to refine prognostic accuracy and guide clinical decision-making based on LANR stratification.

Additionally, multivariate analysis in our study confirmed that the presence of ENE, advanced stage, inadequate surgical margin, and a more aggressive histologic grade independently predicted worse OS and DFS outcomes. Similar findings have been reported in previous studies, suggesting that treatment intensification for patients with these high-risk factors might improve survival outcomes.29,30

Finally, our study has several limitations. First, as a single-center retrospective study, the risk of bias is unavoidable and may influence the findings; second, the study’s results were not validated using an independent dataset. External validation of the results by using an independent patient cohort could strengthen the derived evidence, revealing that LANR is a good prognostic factor in 5-year OS and DFS rates in OSCC patients. Despite these limitations, this study introduces a novel, readily accessible prognostic index with strong predictive performance, providing valuable guidance for clinicians and patients with OSCC.

In conclusion, our findings indicate that preoperative LANR is an independent prognostic factor for survival outcomes in patients with OSCC. The nomogram, integrating preoperative LANR and diverse clinicopathological features, offers a practical tool for clinicians to tailor individualized estimations of mortality risk in OSCC patients.
