 ABSTRACT

This report provides an update on the status of the composite code rule development for the American
Society of Mechanical Engineers (ASME) Boiler Pressure Vessel (BPV) code Section III Division 5
Subsection HA Subpart B (HAB) and Subsection HH Subpart B (HHB) during FY 2025 (from October
2024 to September 2025). In the context of this document, reference to “the code” is specific to these
subsections unless otherwise specified.

A composites task group under the ASME Nonmetallic Design and Materials Working Group (WG-
NDM), with the support of external experts, was established and is now working toward addressing
previously identified areas.

Support for this research was provided by the US Department of Energy Office of Nuclear Energy
Advanced Reactor Technologies program for the completion of activity milestone M3TG-25OR0501054.

 1. INTRODUCTION

The use and benefits of ceramic matrix composites (CMCs) for the core components of high-temperature
reactors (HTRs) were introduced in the early 2000s as these materials were explored for fusion devices
and some HTR technologies. From 2011 until 2018, the code was developed to include rules for CMCs in
HHB. It was first published in the 2019 edition of the code.

Since then, the code matured through industry review and participation, and various gaps arose that are
now being addressed through committee and task group activity. In addition, the US Nuclear Regulatory
Commission (NRC) also started the revision on their initial review of Sec. III-5 in 2023 and issued a
report in 2024.

A lacking component of the CMC code, as per ASME BPVC Sec. III-5, was the supporting technical
basis to demonstrate the rules. A report was prepared, describing how the modeling techniques are
applied according to the code rules. The major change was how to derive design allowables based on the
maximum failure mode in all orthotropic directions for all stress states rather than in only one direction.

2.1 TECHNICAL BASIS AND BENCHMARK STUDY

A draft white paper was prepared to present a benchmark case study which provided an overview of the
design analysis and structural assessment process for a composite in-core component. The process
followed the approach presented in HHB. The white paper includes content about related aspects of
composite component design, including development of a material specification, calculated representative
material properties, and calculated estimated allowable stresses.

This general process is the same as the one used to analyze components fabricated from isotropic
materials, such as metals, but with the added complexity of property directional dependence. The
directional dependence necessitates additional material testing, a more general material definition for
finite element analysis (orthotropic vs. isotropic), and a more detailed assessment of stress response that
considers the magnitudes of each individual stress component and the strength in that individual direction.

Relative to graphite or metals, composites pose challenges due to a current lack of standardization, a more
extensive required material testing regimen, and a somewhat more complicated analysis and structural
assessment procedure. Even with these challenges, and a relatively high level of effort likely being
required from reactor original equipment manufacturers in the near future, the procedure outlined in HHB
provides a viable path for designers.

The white paper will be incorporated as part of the ASME Nuclear Technical Book collection as an
exemplar of how Articles 2000 and 3000, as well as mandatory appendices, are to be interpreted when
using the code.

2.2 NRC ENDORSEMENT

On December 2024, the NRC completed their review of Sec. III-5 and Section XI Division 2 of the 2023
edition of the code. The regulatory guide RG 1.87 Rev 3 was issued which endorsed the code with
exceptions and limitations. Several comments were made on HHB. Some of the comments mentioned
have been addressed in later editions, and some remain a work in progress. More details are provided in
Table 2.

The designer is tasked with much of the technical development of the design requirements. Therefore, the
NRC placed a condition that any design decision or determination that is assigned to the designer in HHB
shall be subject to review by the NRC.

2.3 NUCLEAR ENERGY AGENCY WORKSHOP

On June 3–5, 2025, the NRC hosted a workshop presented by the Nuclear Energy Agency Working
Group on New Technology. The workshop objective was to present the regulatory frameworks and
technical approaches for qualification and through-life performances of materials in advanced reactors.
Qualification comprises efforts to inform the design of structures, systems, and components, and through-
life performance comprises efforts to ensure that operation occurs within the design envelope once
structures, systems, and components go into service.

 2

The working group focused on approaches with high technical maturation that have been or are planned
to support regulatory engagement. It did not exclude traditional light-water reactor approaches with
principles that can be applied to advanced non-water-cooled reactors within the scope.

This workshop connected regulators with key stakeholders, including subject matter experts from technical
support organizations and vendors. It had a dedicated track on the discussion of composite materials, as
mentioned in HHB. The discussion addressed the types of materials and how to specify and qualify those
as well as how to design when working with a composite. During the workshop meeting, two presentations
highlighted code-related topics for ceramic composites. One part focused on design modeling and the other
on materials properties.

2.4 GENERATION IV INTERNATIONAL FORUM VERY HIGH TEMPERATURE
 REACTOR PROJECT MANAGEMENT BOARD

Significant effort has been made to the ASME BPVC code rules in composites within the Generation IV
International Forum very high temperature reactor materials collaboration. A new section, HHB
Composites, has been added to the BPVC in the past 5 years, and this effort is anticipated to continue to
grow as experimental data become available. These data will be used to validate and develop improved
code rules for the use of ceramic and carbon composites in nuclear core applications.

This work is performed in support of the Generation IV International Forum very high temperature
reactor project management board agreement, in which the US Department of Energy committed to
contribute to consensus design codes, such as ASME, which are needed for nonmetallic structures, and
consensus test methods, such as ASTM International (ASTM) or European Norm, which are needed for
property determinations. Materials collected will be captured in the Gen IV Materials Handbook.

For this fiscal year, one meeting was held in March in Vienna, Austria.

2.5 CMC TASK GROUP EFFORTS

The code, defined in HHB, needs additional basis to support and demonstrate the rules, and the
community has questions about and interest in this topic. Therefore, a specific task group within the
ASME WG-NDM was established to work on the design basis and to support and demonstrate the rules
as well as address code changes as they come up. The composites task group had three focus areas this
past year: (1) address the critical code changes in the 2025 edition, (2) provide an exemplar technical
basis or benchmark study of how these rules will be implemented (discussed in Section 2.1), and (3)
address mission-relevant activities as they relate to HAB and HHB on CMCs, as defined by the gap
analysis and the NRC review.

The list of record actions is shown in Table 2. Several members volunteered for different tasks, showing
good team effort. Four critical code changes were successfully adopted in the 2025 edition of the code:
R23-2453 on HHB design by analysis and design by test, R23-2453 on the rules on maximum loading
mode stress, R23-1572 on change per lot testing requirements for as-manufactured ceramic composite
materials, and R22-2267 on the Weibull notation changes to align with Sec. III-5 Subsection HH Subpart
A (HHA).

The composite task group supporting ASME WG-NDM met several times of the past year. This task
group petitioned ASME to become an official registered task group. ASME provided a positive response
in this regard. This effort will be pursued in the next year.

A qualification methodology for the use of ceramic composites is provided in HHB. The methodology is
supported by ASTM guides, which provide a pathway to accomplish this effort. Part of the qualification
strategy is for the designer to collect material property data on environmental conditions representative of
its design envelope. These data include irradiation effects.

Other ongoing and relevant work includes an experimental study that was developed to partially address
the mechanical and physical property data required for design. A variety of different materials using
different fabrication techniques are considered as part of this campaign. The test plan suggests performing
a screening or partial irradiation study to assist the designer during the material selection process. The
designer can then perform a more comprehensive qualification study if the material performance is
promising.

Graphite and composites both fall under Class SN components, which means they follow similar design
and qualification processes. As such, recent code changes in HHA may influence HHB, and any change
in HAB will affect graphite and composite core components.

The code does not explicitly require the designer to apply NQA-1 when completing the material data
sheets. It imposes the NQA-1 level standards on the designer’s organization. However, ASME NQA-1
quality assurance is invoked when ASTM D7219, which is the specified standard to complete the material
data sheet for graphite, is applied. Similarly, NQA-1 is invoked when ASTM C1783 and C1793 are used
for quality assurance requirements, A recent effort by the ASTM community modified the standard to
remove this particular requirement.

 3. CONCLUSION

HHB was reviewed by the NRC for endorsement. Critical errors and/or flaws were revised and corrected
in the 2025 edition of the code. The most critical findings were related to the structural assessment
procedure, the generation of material properties, and the material specification. Several actions by the
composites task group volunteers have been taken to revise the code for further improvement.

A white paper or technical basis has been prepared to benchmark the code with preliminary component
design. This effort demonstrates what component design would look like according to the code.

Industry interaction is an important aspect for code development; throughout the year, several meetings
were chaired and/or attended to perform outreach by emphasizing the topic and to create synergies where
possible.

Limited information on environmental material performance exists, and a preliminary experimental study
to support code qualification on CMC materials is being performed.
