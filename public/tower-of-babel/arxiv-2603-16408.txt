Critical Self-Similar Markov Trees
Nicolas Curien, Xingjian Hu, Dongjian Qian

Abstract
Recently introduced and studied in [ 7 ] , a self-similar Markov tree (ssMt) is a random decorated tree that vastly generalises the fragmentation tree. We study here the critical case that was left aside in [ 7 ] . Borrowing techniques from branching random walk, in particular the recent result of Aïdékon–Hu–Shi [ 3 ] , we can complete the picture by constructing critical ssMt, computing their fractal dimension and studying their associated harmonic and length measures using spinal decomposition.

Introduction

Self-similar Markov trees have recently been introduced in
[
7
]
. They can be seen as generalisations of the fragmentation trees of Haas & Miermont
[
22
]
, or as the genealogical trees underlying the Lamperti transformation of branching Lévy processes
[
8

. They encompass and unify various models including Aldous’ famous Brownian Continuum Random tree
[
4
]
and its stable generalisations
[
21

as well as more exotic trees such as the Brownian Cactus
[
19
]
sitting inside the Brownian sphere
[
25
,
6
]
, or the scaling limits of peeling or parking trees
[
5
,
17
]
. They are conjectured to describe scaling limits of multi-type Bienaymé–Galton–Watson trees, exactly as Lamperti’s self-similar Markov processes describe the scaling limits of positive Markov chains on the integers. Their central role in the theory of random tree sparked a recent interest, see e.g.
[
9
,
18
]
for their intrinsic studies or
[
1
,
25
,
6
,
20
]
for their connections with various models of random planar geometry.

Formally, a self-similar Markov tree (ssMt) is a family of laws
(\mathbb{Q}_{x}:x>0)
of decorated continuous random
\mathbb{R}
-trees
\mathtt{T}=(T,d_{T},\rho,g)
where
\rho
is the root of the tree and
g:T\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is a real decoration which is upper semi-continuous (usc) on
T
and positive on its skeleton. Under the law
\mathbb{Q}_{x}
, the random decorated tree
\mathtt{T}
starts from the initial decoration
g(\rho)=x
and enjoys the following two eponymic properties:

1.

Markov property.
For each
h>0
, conditioned on the subtree truncated at height
h
, the decorated subtrees above
h
are independent of each other and have law
\mathbb{Q}_{y}
if the decoration at its root is
y
.

2.

Self-similarity.
There exists
\alpha>0
, the
self-similar index
, such that for each
x>0
, the tree
(T,d_{T},\rho,g)
under
\mathbb{Q}_{x}
has the same law as
(T,x^{\alpha}d_{T},\rho,x\cdot g)
under
\mathbb{Q}_{1}
.

Following
[
7
, Section 2]
, a self-similar Markov tree can be described by its characteristic quadruplet
(\sigma^{2},\mathrm{a},\boldsymbol{\Lambda};\alpha)
, which through a Lamperti transformation encapsulates the law of their underlying branching Lévy processes. In particular, the generalised Lévy measure
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}
is a (possibly infinite) measure on the space

\mathcal{S}=\{\mathbf{u}=(u_{0},u_{1},...):u_{0}\in\mathbb{R}\mbox{ and }u_{1}\geq u_{2}\geq...\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}\}

which describes the splitting rules: Informally, the ssMt is the genealogical tree of a system of individuals evolving independently of each other, and where an individual of decoration
x>0
sees its decoration instantaneously moved to
x\cdot\mathrm{e}^{y_{0}}
while giving rise to a family of new individuals with decorations
x\cdot\mathrm{e}^{y_{1}},x\cdot\mathrm{e}^{y_{2}},...
(which are interpreted as the birth of new individuals)

\displaystyle x\to(x\cdot\mathrm{e}^{y_{0}},(x\cdot\mathrm{e}^{y_{1}},x\cdot\mathrm{e}^{y_{2}},\cdots))\quad\mbox{ at a rate }\quad x^{-\alpha}\cdot\boldsymbol{\Lambda}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}),

(1.1)

where
\alpha>0
is the self-similarity parameter. The projection
\Lambda_{0}
of
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}
on its first coordinate is required to be a Lévy measure, and its projection
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{1}
onto the second coordinate satisfies a mild integrability assumption (see (
2.1
)). The coefficient
\mathrm{a}\in\mathbb{R}
encodes the drift term while
\sigma^{2}
controls the Brownian part of the evolution of the decoration along branches. See Section
2.1
or
[
7
]
for details. When the starting decoration
x>0
is fixed, the law
\mathbb{Q}_{x}
is the distribution of the above genealogical tree started with an individual of decoration
x
.

A crucial quantity to consider is the
cumulant function
defined by

\begin{split}\kappa(\gamma)&=\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}\gamma^{2}+\mathrm{a}\gamma+\int_{\mathcal{S}}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{u})\left(\mathrm{e}^{\gamma u_{0}}-1-\gamma u_{0}\mathbf{1}_{\{|u_{0}|\leq 1\}}+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{\gamma u_{i}}\right),\\
&=\psi(\gamma)+\int_{\mathcal{S}}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{u})\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{\gamma u_{i}}\bigg)\end{split}

(1.2)

where
\psi(\gamma)
is the Laplace exponent of the Lévy process with characteristics
(\sigma^{2},\mathrm{a},\Lambda_{0})
. This function
\kappa
also appears as the Biggins transform of the underlying branching Lévy process. In particular, if
\kappa
takes strictly negative values then the types of individuals is decaying over generations in expectation, and the genealogical tree of the above system of particles is compact and provides a ssMt. While if
\kappa
only takes positive values then the tree explodes locally, see
[
10
]
. The case when
\kappa
touches
0
while remaining non-negative was left aside in
[
7
]
and is the context of this paper:

Measures and spine decomposition.

Any decorated tree
\mathtt{T}=(T,g)
carries a natural Lebesgue measure
\lambda_{T}
on its skeleton, and the length measures are obtained by using the decoration
g
as density. Formally, for any
\gamma\geq 0
, we define the
\gamma
-length measure
on
T
as

\mathrm{d}\lambda^{\gamma}:=g^{\gamma-\alpha}\cdot\mathrm{d}\lambda_{T}.

In the subcritical case, those measures are defined as soon as
\gamma>\omega_{-}
and Proposition 2.12 of
[
7
]
even ensures that
\lambda^{\gamma}
has finite expected mass when
\kappa(\gamma)<0
. In the critical case, we prove in
Section

3
that
\lambda^{\gamma}
is still a finite measure when
\gamma>\omega_{-}
a.s. but with
infinite expected length
. Let us now turn to the equivalent of the harmonic measure of
[
7
, Chapter 2.3.3]
. Recall from Assumption
1.1
that
\kappa(\omega_{-})=0
. In this case, the process

W_{n}=\sum_{|u|=n}(\chi(u))^{\omega_{-}},

where
\chi(u)
are the initial decorations of the individuals appearing in the genealogical tree (indexed by the Ulam’s tree, see the construction of ssMt in
Section

2.1
) is a martingale. In the context of branching random walks, such a martingale is called an additive martingale. In the critical case, although positive, this martingale has a trivial limit (see
[
28
, Theorem 3.3]
), so the construction of the harmonic measure from
[
7
]
needs to be adapted. The standard way to remedy this problem is to consider the
derivative martingale
defined by

D_{n}=-\sum_{|u|=n}(\chi(u))^{\omega_{-}}\log(\chi(u)).

(1.3)

Standard results in the field of branching random walks (see
[
28
, Section 5]
) ensures convergence of the derivative martingale towards a non-trivial positive limit
{D}_{\infty}
. This enables us to endow the ssMt
\mathtt{T}
with a non-trivial measure
\mu
of mass
{D}_{\infty}
which plays the role of the harmonic measure in the subcritical case (we keep the same name in our context). Although not immediate from the definition, we will show that the harmonic measure
\mu
is intrinsic, i.e. measurable with respect to the decorated tree only (as opposed to its genealogical representation). In fact
\mu
can be obtained as a limit of the (intrinsic) length measures
\lambda^{\gamma}
when
\gamma\downarrow\omega_{-}

\lim_{\gamma\downarrow\omega_{-}}\frac{\kappa^{\prime\prime}(\omega_{-})}{2}(\gamma-\omega_{-})\lambda^{\gamma}=\mu,

at least along a subsequence as it was the case in the subcritical case
[
7
, Proposition 2.15]
(see
4.4
for details). This convergence represents the most technical part of this work and requires delicate truncation estimates and fluctuation identities for Lévy processes.

As in
[
7
, Chapter 4 ]
those random measures are used to perform
spinal decomposition
of the underlying ssMt. The spinal decomposition originates in the setting of branching random walks and generalises to many different genealogical models, such as branching Lévy processes and self-similar Markov trees. When doing so, we want to deal with the law of the decorated tree
{\tt T}
together with a marked point
r
sampled from the harmonic (or length) measure. The line segment between the root
\rho
and the marked point
r
is called the spine. The spinal decomposition theorem describes the law of the spine and the law of the subtrees dangling to the spine. In the critical case, the harmonic or length measures have infinite expected total mass, so we cannot directly bias the law of the tree by sampling a point according to those measures. However, this can be performed after a convenient cut-off on the decoration, see
4.1
in
Section

4.1
. In particular, in contrast to
[
7
]
, the Lévy processes controlling the evolution of the decoration along the spine are now conditioned to stay below a barrier. The spinal decomposition is profound and has many applications.

The rest of the paper is organised as follows. In section 2, after a quick recap of the construction in
[
7
]
we apply the results of
[
3
]
to construct critical self-similar Markov trees. We then import many results from the subcritical case using a "continuity argument" since critical ssMt can, after a slight perturbation, be transformed into a subcritical ssMt. This is used in particular to obtain a lower bound on their Hausdorff dimension, the spinal decomposition and characterisation of bifurcators. In section 3, we discuss the length and harmonic measures. A key idea imported from branching random walks is to consider the tree conditioned to stay below a barrier. In section 4, we present the spinal decomposition theorem with respect to the truncated harmonic measure. Section 5, perhaps the most technical part of this paper, is devoted to analysing the relations between harmonic and lengths measures (
4.4
).

We first recall the definition of

(\sigma^{2},\mathrm{a},\boldsymbol{\Lambda};\alpha)

Topology of the space of (measured) decorated trees
.
We close this section with a brief note on the topology on the space of decorated compact real tree, see
[
7
, Section 1.4]
. We say
\mathtt{T}:=(T,d_{T},\rho,g)
and
\mathtt{T}^{\prime}:=(T^{\prime},d_{T}^{\prime},\rho^{\prime},g^{\prime})
are isomorphic if there exists a bijective isometry
\phi:(T,d_{T})\to(T^{\prime},d_{T}^{\prime})
such that
\phi(\rho)=\rho^{\prime}
and
g^{\prime}=g\circ\phi^{-1}
. Denote by
\mathbb{T}
(resp.
\mathbb{T}^{\bullet}
or
\mathbb{T}_{m}
) the space of equivalence (up to isomorphisms) classes of decorated compact real trees (resp. with an additional point, or a finite Borel measure). We always abuse
\mathtt{T}=(T,d_{T},\rho,g)
or
\mathtt{T}^{\bullet}=(T,d_{T},\rho,g,r)
to represent an equivalence class in the sequel (where

is an additional point), since the quantities and properties we will consider are invariant under isomorphisms. These spaces are endowed with
d_{\mathbb{T}},d_{\mathbb{T}^{\bullet}},d_{\mathbb{T}_{m}}
, which are adaptations of the Gromov-Hausdorff-pointed/Prokhorov distances that also take the decorations into account as hypographs. They are all Polish spaces. See
[
7
, Section 1.4]
for details.

Critical self-similar Markov trees

For a generic choice of quadruplet
(\sigma^{2},\mathrm{a},\boldsymbol{\Lambda};\alpha)

(f_{u},\eta_{u})_{u\in\mathbb{U}}

2.3
Approximation by subcritical ssMt

In this section, we introduce several approaches to couple a critical ssMt with an approximating sequence of subcritical ssMt. These couplings will be used to lift properties from subcritical ssMt to critical ssMt (lower bound on Hausdorff dimension, spinal decomposition…).

A simple way to couple self-similar Markov trees is to couple their decoration-reproduction processes for each decorated branch. Fix a (critical) characteristic quadruplet
(\sigma^{2},\mathrm{a},\boldsymbol{\Lambda};\alpha)
and fix
\varepsilon>0
. Recall from
Section

2.1
the construction of the decoration–reproduction process
(X,\eta)
from a Lévy process
\xi
and a point process
\mathbf{N}_{1}
whose atoms are of the form
(s,\mathbf{y})
. We provide three different processes
(X^{i,\varepsilon},\eta^{i,\varepsilon})
(

coupled with

with law
P
.

Adding negative drift

(X^{1,\varepsilon},\eta^{1,\varepsilon})
is obtained by adding a drift
-\varepsilon
to the underlying Lévy process
\xi

\xi^{1,\varepsilon}_{t}=\xi_{t}-\varepsilon t
and denote by

the Lamperti transform of

\eta^{1,\varepsilon}
by (

unchanged and
\xi

.

Adding killing

(X^{2,\varepsilon},\eta^{2,\varepsilon})

\varepsilon
to

an independent exponential time with parameter
\varepsilon

of

equals

where

is the Lamperti time transform of

(X^{2,\varepsilon},\eta^{2,\varepsilon})=(X\cdot\mathds{1}_{[0,\epsilon(z^{2,\varepsilon})]},\eta\cdot\mathds{1}_{[0,\epsilon(z^{2,\varepsilon})]\times\mathbb{R}^{*}})
. We always
couple the exponential times
\mathrm{e}(\varepsilon)
for different

such that they are decreasing in
\varepsilon
(i.e.
\mathrm{e}(\varepsilon)\uparrow\infty
as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
) a.s..

Lowering the reproduction

(X^{3,\varepsilon},\eta^{3,\varepsilon})
is obtained by shifting downwards the atoms

of

by

. We set

and

\eta^{3,\varepsilon}=\sum\mathds{1}_{\{\epsilon(s)\leq z\}}\delta_{(\epsilon(s),\exp(\xi(s-)+y_{\ell}-\varepsilon))}.

Denote by

Proposition 2.5

(Subcritical approximations)
.

Suppose that
(\sigma^{2},\mathrm{a},\boldsymbol{\Lambda};\alpha)
is a critical characteristic quadruplet satisfying
1.1
or
2.2
. For any
\varepsilon>0
, the decorated trees
\mathtt{T}^{i,\varepsilon}
(
i\in\{1,2,3\}
) are subcritical ssMt with characteristics, respectively, given by
(\sigma^{2},\mathrm{a}-\varepsilon,\boldsymbol{\Lambda};\alpha)
,
(\sigma^{2},\mathrm{a},\boldsymbol{\Lambda}+\varepsilon\delta_{(-\infty,(-\infty,\ldots))};\alpha)
and
(\sigma^{2},\mathrm{a},\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{\varepsilon};\alpha)
with

\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{\varepsilon}(\mathrm{d}y,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{y})=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}(\mathrm{d}y,(\mathrm{d}(y_{i}+\varepsilon))_{i\geq 1}).

In all three cases we have
{\tt T}^{i,\varepsilon}\to\tt T
a.s. for the Gromov–Hausdorff–decorated distance and furthermore
p^{i,\varepsilon}:{\tt T}^{i,\varepsilon}\mapsto\tt T

1

Proof.

The claim that
\mathtt{T}^{i,\varepsilon}
are subcritical ssMt with the above quadruplets is clear from the construction. Notice that in the second case (adding killing), the subtree
\mathtt{T}^{3,\varepsilon}
is obtained by pruning
\mathtt{T}
at countably many points at most. In the third case, lowering the reproduction amounts to scale the branches
\mathtt{T}
by
\mathrm{e}^{-\varepsilon n}
where
n
is the generation of the individual. In those two cases, clearly the projection is
1
-Lipschitz. In case
1
, the Lamperti time transform over any interval
(s,t)
satisfies

\epsilon^{1,\varepsilon}(t)-\epsilon^{1,\varepsilon}(s)<\epsilon(t)-\epsilon(s).

Iterating the argument, we get that the canonical projection is also
1-
Lipschitz. We finally prove the convergence of the approximating subcritical trees. We first see that the first branch converges as
\varepsilon\to 0
since the process converges a.s.. We make it more precisely in the second case. If
f_{\varnothing}(z_{\varnothing}-)>0
, we have
(f^{2,\varepsilon}_{\varnothing},\eta^{2,\varepsilon}_{\varnothing})=(f_{\varnothing},\eta_{\varnothing})
when
\varepsilon
is small enough. Otherwise, when
f_{\varnothing}(z_{\varnothing}-)=0
, as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
, the Skorohod distance between
f_{\varnothing}
and
f_{\varnothing}^{2,\varepsilon}
goes to
0
a.s.. A moment’s thought shows that for any
N\geq 1
, as
\varepsilon\to 0
, the subtree
\mathtt{T}^{i,\varepsilon}(N)\subset\mathtt{T}^{i,\varepsilon}
spanned by the individuals

converges in the Gromov–Hausdorff–decorated sense
d_{\mathbb{T}}
(no measure) towards the analogous subtree in
\mathtt{T}(N)\subset\mathtt{T}
. However, by the monotonicity properties of the coupling we have
d_{\mathbb{T}}(\mathtt{T}^{i,\varepsilon},\mathtt{T}^{i,\varepsilon}(N))\leq d_{\mathbb{T}}(\mathtt{T},\mathtt{T}(N))

by the compactly glueability of

. This enables us to exchange the limits

and

and get the statement.
∎

Remark 2.6

(Intrinsic approximation)
.

Contrary to the third case which requires the underlying family of decoration-reproduction, adding a killing or a drift is an operation which is geometrically intrinsic, that is, (the isometry class of) the random decorated tree
\mathtt{T}^{i,\varepsilon}
is a measurable function of
\mathtt{T}\in\mathbb{T}
for
i=1
and
i=2
(together with additional independent randomness). Let us give some explanation and leave the technical details to the reader: For
x\in T
, we view the function
g:[\hskip-1.00006pt[\rho,x]\hskip-1.00006pt]\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
as a function
g:[0,d(\rho,x)]\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
. We can then take the inverse Lamperti transform to get the underlying Lévy process, add a drift
-\varepsilon
or a killing rate
\varepsilon
, and finally take the Lamperti transform back to obtain the corresponding branch in
\mathtt{T}^{i,\varepsilon}
. For different
x,y\in{\tt T}
, such chain of transformations agrees on
[\hskip-1.00006pt[\rho,x\wedge y]\hskip-1.00006pt]
where
x\wedge y
is the last common point of the geodesics. The point is that, by the memoryless property of the exponential variables, these constructions can be performed for all
x\in\mathtt{T}
simultaneously and coherently, which ensures the well-posedness of the construction.

On subcritical self-similar Markov trees, two different kinds of measures were constructed in

Branching Lévy processes.
Branching Lévy processes were formally introduced by Bertoin & Mallein in

More precisely, let

is the Lamperti transform of

x\geq 0
, define the renewal functions

\displaystyle R(b)=\hat{E}\bigg[\int^{\infty}_{0}\mathds{1}_{\{\hat{\tau}_{b}>t\}}\mathrm{d}L_{t}\bigg],\quad R^{-}(b)=\hat{E}\bigg[\int^{\infty}_{0}\mathds{1}_{\{\hat{\tau}^{-}_{-b}>t\}}\mathrm{d}L^{-}_{t}\bigg].

(3.5)

These functions are increasing and since
\hat{\xi}
does not drift to
\infty
, by
[
15
, Lemma 1]
the function
R
is harmonic for
\hat{\xi}
killed above
b
, that is for
b>0
and
t\geq 0

\displaystyle R(b)=\hat{E}[R(b-\hat{\xi}_{t})\mathds{1}_{\{\hat{\tau}_{b}>t\}}].

(3.6)

We use the convention that
R(b)=R^{-}(b)=0
for
b<0
. The strong Markov property shows that
R
and
R^{-}
are sub-additive, which implies that they grow linearly with constants
c_{0},c_{0}^{-}>0
(which depend on the normalisation of the local times) i.e.
R(b)\sim c_{0}b
and
R^{-}(b)\sim c_{0}^{-}b
as
b\to\infty
. More precisely, the renewal theorem implies that for
h>0
we have

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{R([x,x+h])}{h}=c_{0},\quad\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{R^{-}([x,x+h])}{h}=c^{-}_{0},

(3.7)

where we let
R([x,x+h])=R(x+h)-R(x)
and
R^{-}([x,x+h])=R^{-}(x+h)-R^{-}(x)
.

We define the ladder height processes
H(t)=\hat{\xi}_{L^{-1}(t)}
and
H^{-1}(t)=\hat{\xi}_{(L^{-})^{-1}(t)}
. Introduce the Laplace exponent of the ascending ladder process
(L^{-1},H)
, formally given by

\displaystyle\exp(-\kappa^{+}(a,b))=\hat{E}_{1}[\exp(-aL^{-1}(1)-bH(1))].

(3.8)

In the same way, the Laplace exponent of the descending ladder process
((L^{-})^{-1},H^{-})
is given by

\displaystyle\exp(-\kappa^{-}(a,b))=\hat{E}_{1}[\exp(-a(L^{-})^{-1}(1)-bH^{-}(1))].

(3.9)

By
[
11
, Section 5, Equation (3)(4)]
, there exists a constant
K>0
(which depend on the normalisation of the local times), such that for
\lambda>0
we have

\kappa^{+}(\lambda,0)\kappa^{-}(\lambda,0)=K\lambda

(3.10)

and

K\psi_{\omega_{-}}(\lambda)=\kappa^{+}(0,-i\lambda)\kappa^{-}(0,i\lambda).

By the renewal theorem, we have
c_{0}=\hat{\mathbb{E}}[H(1)]^{-1}
and
c_{0}^{-}=\hat{\mathbb{E}}[H^{-}(1)]^{-1}
. The characteristic functions satisfy
\kappa^{+}(0,-i\lambda)\sim-i\lambda\hat{\mathbb{E}}[H(1)]
and
\kappa^{-}(0,i\lambda)\sim i\lambda\hat{\mathbb{E}}[H^{-}(1)]
when
\lambda
is small. Together with
\psi_{\omega_{-}}(\lambda)=\kappa(\omega_{-}+\lambda)\sim\frac{\kappa^{\prime\prime}(\omega_{-})\lambda^{2}}{2}
, we obtain a relation between
c_{0}
,
c_{0}^{-}
and
K
:

Kc_{0}c_{0}^{-}=\frac{2}{\kappa^{\prime\prime}(\omega_{-})}.

(3.11)

We also introduce a variant of the renewal function
R
. For
\varepsilon>0
, we define
\mathscr{V}^{\varepsilon}:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
by

\mathscr{V}^{\varepsilon}(y):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\hat{E}_{1}[\exp(-\varepsilon L^{-1}(t))\cdot\mathds{1}_{\{H(t)\leq y\}}]\mathrm{d}t.

(3.12)

We see that
\mathscr{V}^{\varepsilon}(y)\uparrow R(y)
as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
.

For future use, we introduce a bit of the analogous results for the random walk
{\tt S}
. Similarly, we define the weak descend ladder process
({\tt T}_{k},{\tt H}_{k})
and the strict ascending ladder process
({\tt T}^{+}_{k},{\tt H}^{+}_{k})
of the random walk
-{\hat{\tt S}}
. We also define the discrete renewal functions
{\tt R}
and
{\tt R}^{+}
by

{\tt R}(b)={\tt E}\bigg[\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\mathds{1}_{\{{\tt H}_{k}<b\}}\bigg],\quad{\tt R}^{+}(b)={\tt E}\bigg[\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\mathds{1}_{\{{\tt H}^{+}_{k}<b\}}\bigg].

(3.13)

By convention,
{\tt R}(b)={\tt R}^{-}(b)=0
for
b<0
. The renewal theorem states that there exist constants
{\tt c}_{0},{\tt c}_{0}^{-}>0
such that

\lim_{b\to\infty}\frac{{\tt R}(b)}{b}={\tt c}_{0},\quad\lim_{b\to\infty}\frac{{\tt R}^{-}(b)}{b}={\tt c}^{-}_{0}.

(3.14)

3.3
Length measure of the tree below a barrier

As we have seen in
Section

3
, the arguments of
[
7
, Section 2.3]
based on first moment calculation break down in the critical case. This comes from the unusual event that the decoration on the tree reaches a high level. The solution is to impose a barrier constraint on the decoration on the tree. This method is standard for the branching random walks in the boundary case. See for example
[
16
,
3
]
.

Specifically, if
\mathtt{T}
is a decorated tree starting from
g(\rho)=x
then for
c\geq x
, we let
\mathtt{T}^{c}=(T^{c},d_{T^{c}},\rho,g)
be the decorated subtree obtained by pruning the tree at the first time the decoration exceeds the level
c
, namely

T^{c}=\{x\in T:g([\hskip-1.00006pt[\rho,x]\hskip-1.00006pt])\leq c\}

where
[\hskip-1.00006pt[\rho,x]\hskip-1.00006pt]
is the geodesic from
\rho
to
x
. Since
(||f_{u}||)_{u\in T}
is a null family, the tree
T^{c}
is obtained by pruning
T
at finitely many points. In particular
T^{c}
is closed a.s.. We show that
\mathtt{T}^{c}=\mathtt{T}
with high probability as a foundation to the trick.

Lemma 3.3
.

Under
1.1
, we have

\mathbb{Q}_{1}(\mathtt{T}^{c}=\mathtt{T})\geq 1-c^{-\omega_{-}}.

(3.15)

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{L}^{c}
be the set of pairs
(u,t_{u})
in the branching Lévy process
(\Xi(t,u),u\in\mathcal{N}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, where
t_{u}<\infty
denotes the first time at which the process along the lineage hits
(\log c,\infty)
. Then
\mathcal{L}^{c}
is a stopping line. By the many-to-one formula (
3.4
), we have

\mathbb{P}(\#\mathcal{L}^{c}\geq 1)\leq\mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{(u,t_{u})\in\mathcal{L}^{c}}1\Big]\leq c^{-\omega_{-}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{(u,t_{u})\in\mathcal{L}^{c}}\mathrm{e}^{\omega_{-}\Xi(t_{u},u)}\Big]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{Lem.}\ref{lem: brl many-to-one}}}{{=}}c^{-\omega_{-}}\hat{P}(\hat{\tau}_{\log c}<\infty)\leq c^{-\omega_{-}}.

∎

Denote the expected total mass of the
\gamma
-length measure
\lambda^{\gamma}
on
\mathtt{T}^{c}
by
R_{\gamma}(c)
, that is,

R_{\gamma}(c):=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{T^{c}}g(v)^{\gamma-\alpha}\mathrm{d}\lambda_{T}(v)\bigg].

(3.16)

Recall the constant
K
introduced in (
3.10
). We prove that
R_{\gamma}(c)
is finite for
\gamma>\omega_{-}
:

Proposition 3.4

(
\gamma
-Length measure of the tree below a barrier)
.

Under
1.1
, for
c>1
and
\gamma>\omega_{-}
, we have

R_{\gamma}(c)=K\bigg(\int_{[0,\log c]}\mathrm{e}^{(\gamma-\omega_{-})x}R(\mathrm{d}x)\bigg)\bigg(\int_{[0,\infty)}\mathrm{e}^{-(\gamma-\omega_{-})x}R^{-}(\mathrm{d}x)\bigg)<\infty.

(3.17)

We deduce in particular that the measure
\lambda^{\gamma}
is finite on
T^{c}
,
\mathbb{P}_{1}
-a.s for any
\gamma>\omega_{-}
. Since
\mathbb{Q}_{1}({\tt T}^{c}={\tt T})\to 1
as
c\to\infty
by
3.3
we deduce that
\lambda^{\gamma}
is a finite measure
\mathbb{P}_{1}
-a.s for any
\gamma>\omega_{-}
.

Proof.

Set
b=\log c
and
\tau_{b}(u)=\inf\{t:\Xi(t,u)>b\}
. Recall that
\hat{\tau}_{b}=\inf\{t:\hat{\xi}_{t}>b\}
. We have

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{T^{c}}g(v)^{\gamma-\alpha}\mathrm{d}\lambda_{T}(v)\bigg]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{Lamperti}}}{{=}}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int^{\infty}_{0}\bigg(\sum_{u\in{\cal N}_{t}}\mathrm{e}^{\gamma\Xi(t,u)}\cdot\mathds{1}_{\{\tau_{b}(u)>t\}}\bigg)\mathrm{d}t\bigg]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{Lem.}\ref{lem: brl many-to-one}}}{{=}}\hat{E}\bigg[\int^{\infty}_{0}\mathrm{e}^{(\gamma-\omega_{-})\hat{\xi}_{t}}\cdot\mathds{1}_{\{\hat{\tau}_{b}>t\}}\mathrm{d}t\bigg].

By
[
11
, Lemma 20, Chapter VI]
, for the constant
K>0
introduced in (
3.10
),

\hat{E}\bigg[\int^{\infty}_{0}\mathrm{e}^{(\gamma-\omega_{-})\hat{\xi}_{t}}\cdot\mathds{1}_{\{\hat{\tau}_{b}>t\}}\bigg]=K\bigg(\int_{[0,b]}\mathrm{e}^{(\gamma-\omega_{-})x}R(\mathrm{d}x)\bigg)\bigg(\int_{[0,\infty)}\mathrm{e}^{-(\gamma-\omega_{-})x}R^{-}(\mathrm{d}x)\bigg).

The limit (
3.7
) implies that the integrals on the right hand side are finite.
∎

3.4
The harmonic measure

We now define the harmonic measure. Under
1.1
, since
\kappa(\omega_{-})=0
, the function
h(x)=x^{\omega_{-}}
is a harmonic function (related to the additive martingale in the literature of branching random walks) i.e.

x^{\omega_{-}}=\mathbb{E}_{x}\bigg[\sum_{|u|=1}(\chi(u))^{\omega_{-}}\bigg],\quad\mbox{for all }x>0.

(3.18)

This was used in
[
7
, Section 2.3.2]
to prove that the family of spreading mass

\widetilde{m}_{u}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{|v|=n}(\chi(uv))^{\omega_{-}}

(3.19)

defines a random measure, called the harmonic measure on
\mathtt{T}
. However, in the critical case, it is known, see
[
28
, Theorem 3.2]
, that the total mass of this measure is
\widetilde{m}_{\varnothing}=0
. In order to define a non-degenerate harmonic measure, instead we should use here the derivative martingale

D_{n}=-\sum_{|u|=n}(\chi(u))^{\omega_{-}}\log(\chi(u)),

(3.20)

which also converges
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. to some random variable
D_{\infty}
by
[
28
, Theorem 5.2]
. To ensure positivity of the latter, we need to impose an analogue of
[
7
, Assumption 2.13]
:

Assumption 3.5

(Critical Cramér condition)
.

There exists
1<p_{0}\leq 2
and
0<\Delta_{0}<\gamma_{1}-\omega_{-}
such that for
\gamma\in(\omega_{-}-\Delta_{0},\omega_{-}+\Delta_{0})
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}\mathbf{\Lambda}_{1}(\mathrm{d}y)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{y_{i}\gamma}\right)^{p_{0}}<\infty.

(3.21)

We also assume
\psi(\omega_{-}+\Delta_{0})<0
and
\psi(\omega_{-}-\Delta_{0})<0
when
\Delta_{0}
is small enough.

Lemma 3.6

(Definition of harmonic measure in the critical case)
.

Under Assumptions
1.1
and
3.5
, under
\mathbb{P}_{x}
for
x>0
, the family of spreading mass

m_{u}=-\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{|v|=n}(\chi(uv))^{\omega_{-}}\log(\chi(uv))\geq 0,

(3.22)

defines a non-trivial measure
\mu
on the leaves of
\mathtt{T}
called the
harmonic measure
.

Proof.

Under
3.5
, for
1<p\leq p_{0}
and
\gamma\in(\omega_{-}-\Delta_{0},\omega_{-}+\Delta_{0})
, by
[
23
]
, we have
\mathbb{E}_{1}[(\sum^{\infty}_{u=1}\chi(i)^{\gamma})^{p}]<\infty.

By the convexity of
\psi
, there exist
1\leq p<2
and
C>0
, for
\gamma\in(\omega_{-}-\Delta_{0},\omega_{-}+\Delta_{0})
,

\psi(p\gamma)<0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum^{\infty}_{u=1}\chi(i)^{\gamma}\right)^{p}\right]\leq C<\infty.

(3.23)

By
[
16
]
, we see that under
1.1
and
3.5
,
D_{\infty}
is positive
\mathbb{P}_{x}
-a.s..

By
[
14
, Lemma 6.1]
, we have a.s.
m_{u}=\sum_{i\geq 1}m_{ui}
. Therefore, by
[
7
, Section 1.3]
, the family of spreading mass
(m_{u})_{u\in\mathbb{U}}
gives rise to a harmonic measure
\mu
supported on
\partial T
.

∎

As for the length measures, in general we have
\mathbb{E}[\mu(T)]=\mathbb{E}[D_{\infty}]=\infty
. We will see that when restricted to the truncated tree
\mathtt{T}_{c}
the harmonic measure also has finite expectation. To this end, we introduce the truncated derivative martingale
(D^{c}_{n})_{n}
, whose limit equals
D_{\infty}
restricted on
T^{c}
up to a multiplicative constant. Recall the renewal function
R
defined in
Section

3.2
. For
u\in\mathbb{U}
, let
\rho_{u}
be the root of the decorated branches
(f_{u},\eta_{u})
. For
c>x
, we set

D^{c}_{n}=\sum_{|u|=n}(\chi(u))^{\omega_{-}}\cdot R(\log c-\log\chi(u))\cdot\mathds{1}_{\{g([\hskip-0.81949pt[\rho,\rho_{u}]\hskip-0.81949pt])\leq c\}}.

(3.24)

Proposition 3.7

(Harmonic measure of the tree below a barrier)
.

For each
c>x
, we have
\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[D^{c}_{1}\right]=x^{\omega_{-}}R(\log c-\log x)
and thus
(D^{c}_{n})_{n}
is a non-negative martingale. As a consequence, the family of spreading mass

m_{u}^{(c)}=\mathds{1}_{\{X([\hskip-0.81949pt[\rho,\rho_{u}]\hskip-0.81949pt])\leq c\}}\cdot\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{|v|=n}(\chi(uv))^{\omega_{-}}R(\log c-\log\chi(uv))\cdot\mathds{1}_{\{X([\hskip-0.81949pt[\rho_{u},\rho_{uv}]\hskip-0.81949pt])\leq c\}}

(3.25)

defines a non-trivial measure
\mu^{c}
on the leaves of
\mathtt{T}
which is exactly
c_{0}\mu
restricted to
T^{c}
.

Proof.

Set
b=\log c
. We first prove
\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[D^{c}_{1}\right]=x^{\omega_{-}}R(\log c-\log x)
. Recall the branching Lévy process
(\Xi(t,u),u\in\mathcal{N}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
constructed in
Section

3.1
and the hitting times
\tau_{b}(u)=\inf\{t:\Xi(t,u)>b\}
for
u\in\mathbb{U}
. For
t>0
,

\mathbb{E}[\sum_{u\in\mathcal{N}_{t}}\mathrm{e}^{\omega_{-}\Xi(t,u))}R(b-\Xi(t,u))\cdot\mathds{1}_{\{\tau_{b}(u)>t\}}]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{Lem.}\ref{lem: brl many-to-one}}}{{=}}x^{\omega_{-}}\hat{E}[R(b-\hat{\xi}_{t})\cdot\mathds{1}_{\{\hat{\tau}_{b}>t\}}]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq: renewal martingale}}}{{=}}R(b).

(3.26)

For
i\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
we write
\mathcal{N}_{t}(i)
for the set of
u\in\mathcal{N}_{t}
such that
i
is an ancestor of
u
(possibly
i=u
) in the Ulam tree. We apply the display above to each
i
if its birth time
b(i)\leq t
. Condition on
(\xi_{\varnothing},N_{\varnothing})|_{[0,t]}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{u\in\mathcal{N}_{t}(i)}\mathrm{e}^{\omega_{-}\Xi(t,u))}R(b-\Xi(t,u))\cdot\mathds{1}_{\{\tau_{b}(u)>t\}}\Big|(\xi_{\varnothing},N_{\varnothing})|_{[0,t]}\right]

\displaystyle\,=(\chi(i))^{\omega_{-}}R(b-\log(\chi(i)))\cdot\mathds{1}_{\{\tau_{b}(\varnothing)>b(i)\}}\cdot\mathds{1}_{\{b(i)\leq t\}}.

Summing in
i
, we get

\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{u\in\mathcal{N}_{t}}\mathrm{e}^{\omega_{-}\Xi(t,u))}R(b-\Xi(t,u))\cdot 1_{\{\tau_{b}(u)>t\}}\Big|(\xi_{\varnothing},N_{\varnothing})|_{[0,t]}\right]\\
=\mathrm{e}^{\omega_{-}\xi_{\varnothing}(t)}R(b-\xi_{\varnothing}(t))\cdot\mathds{1}_{\{\tau_{b}(\varnothing)>t\}}+\sum_{t_{i}<t}(\chi(i))^{\omega_{-}}R(b-\log(\chi(i)))\cdot\mathds{1}_{\{\tau_{b}(\varnothing)>b(i)\}}\cdot\mathds{1}_{\{b(i)\leq t\}}.

Taking expectation on both sides, the left hand side equals
x^{\omega_{-}}R(b-\log x)
by (
3.26
). For the right hand side, we let
t\to\infty
and show that the expectation of the summation increases to
\mathbb{E}[D_{1}^{c}]
. It suffices to show the expectation on the first term goes to
0
. Recall in
1.1
, there exists
\gamma_{1}>\omega_{-}
, such that
\psi(\gamma_{1})<0
. By (
3.7
), there exists
0<\delta<\gamma_{1}-\omega_{-}
such that
R(x)\leq C\mathrm{e}^{\delta x}
. Thus

E_{x}[\mathrm{e}^{\omega_{-}\xi_{\varnothing}(t)}R(b-\xi_{\varnothing}(t))\cdot\mathds{1}_{\{\tau_{b}(\varnothing)>t\}}]\leq CE_{x}[\mathrm{e}^{(\omega_{-}+\delta)\xi_{t}}]=C\mathrm{e}^{t\psi(\omega_{-}+\delta)}\xrightarrow{t\to\infty}0

since
\psi
is convex and
\psi(\gamma)<0
for
\gamma\in(\omega_{-},\gamma_{1})
.

It follows from the branching property and the above calculations that
(D^{c}_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is a non-negative martingale under
\mathbb{P}_{x}
and thus converges a.s. Let us prove that the convergence is also in
L^{1}(\mathbb{P}_{x})
. Using the asymptotics (
3.7
), (
3.14
) and the fact
{\tt R}(0)>0
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that
R(x)\leq C{\tt R}(x)
. Therefore,
D^{c}_{n}
is controlled by
C\sum_{|u|=n}(\chi(u))^{\omega_{-}}{\tt R}(\log c-\log(\chi(u)))\mathds{1}_{\{\chi(u_{i})\leq c,i\leq|u|\}}
, which is uniformly integrable by
[
28
, Lemma 5.5]
. Thus
D^{c}_{n}
is uniformly integrable and thus converges in
L^{1}(\mathbb{P}_{x})
.

The mass
m^{(c)}_{u}
in (
3.25
) is now well-defined. By Fatou’s lemma,
m^{(c)}_{u}\geq\sum_{i\geq 1}m^{(c)}_{ui}
. Taking expectation on both sides, we see the inequality is in fact an equality
\mathbb{P}_{x}
-a.s. since they have the same expectation. By
[
7
, Section 1.3]
again, the family of spreading mass
(m^{(c)}_{u})_{u\in\mathbb{U}}
induces a measure
\mu^{c}
with
\mathbb{E}_{x}[\mu^{c}(T)]=x^{\omega_{-}}R(\log c-\log x)<\infty
supported on
\partial T^{c}
. We call this measure
\mu^{c}
the truncated harmonic measure.

We next show the measure
\mu^{c}
and
c_{0}\mu
restricted to
T^{c}
are equal by a standard argument in branching random walks. By
[
28
, Lemma 3.1]
,
M_{n}:=-\sup_{|u|=n}\log\chi(u)\to\infty
. For a fixed
c>0
, by (
3.7
),
\mathbb{P}_{1}
-a.s., there exists
C>0
, for
y\geq 0
,
|R(y+\log c)-R(y)|\leq C|\log c|
. As
n\to\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{|u|=n}(\chi(u))^{\omega_{-}}\big|R(\log c-\log\chi(u))-R(-\log\chi(u))\big|\cdot\mathds{1}_{\{X([\hskip-0.81949pt[\rho,\rho_{u}]\hskip-0.81949pt])\leq c\}}\leq C\,|\log c|\,W_{n}\longrightarrow 0

and

\displaystyle\sum_{|u|=n}(\chi(u))^{\omega_{-}}\big|R(-\log\chi(u))+c_{0}\log\chi(u)\big|\cdot\mathds{1}_{\{X([\hskip-0.81949pt[\rho,\rho_{u}]\hskip-0.81949pt])\leq c\}}\leq\sup_{x\geq M_{n}}\Big|\frac{R(x)}{x}-c_{0}\Big|\,D_{n}\longrightarrow 0.

We see that the total variation between the families of spreading mass (
3.22
) and (
3.25
) converges to
0
when restricted to
T^{c}
, whence
\mu^{c}
is the same as
c_{0}\mu
restricted to
T^{c}
.

∎

3.5
The Hausdorff dimension of the leaves

In this section, we compute the Hausdorff dimension of
\mathtt{T}
as stated in
1.2
. By self-similarity, it suffices to work under
\mathbb{Q}_{1}
. The approximation argument in
Section

2.3
applies directly to the lower bound of Hausdorff dimension:

Proof of lower bound in
1.2
. .

Recall the construction of
{\tt T}^{i,\varepsilon}
in
2.5
. For ease of notation we fix
i=2
(the proof goes through for
i=1
or
i=3
when
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}
is non-trivial) and omit the superscript
i
. We calculate that
\kappa^{\varepsilon}=\kappa-\epsilon
by the explicit characteristic quadruplet in
2.5
. When
\varepsilon>0
is small enough, there exists
\omega^{\varepsilon}_{-}
such that
\kappa^{\varepsilon}(\omega^{\varepsilon}_{-})=0
and
\omega_{-}^{\varepsilon}\uparrow\omega_{-}
as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
. As a stronger assumption where we assume
L^{p}
boundedness over an interval,
3.5
implies
[
7
, Assumption 2.13]
(
L^{p}
boundedness at a point). Applying
[
7
, Proposition 6.14]
, the Hausdorff dimension of
\partial{\tt T}^{\varepsilon}
is
\omega_{-}^{\varepsilon}/\alpha
,
\mathbb{Q}_{1}
-a.s.. Since
p^{\varepsilon}:{\tt T}\to{\tt T}^{\varepsilon}
is
1
-Lipschitz, for
d\in(0,\infty)
, the
d
-dimensional Hausdorff measure of
\partial T
is no less than that of
\partial T^{\varepsilon}
. Thus
\dim_{H}(\partial T)\geq\omega_{-}^{\varepsilon}/\alpha
. The lower bound is established since
\omega_{-}^{\varepsilon}\uparrow\omega_{-}
as

.
∎

In the subcritical case, the upper bound on the Hausdorff dimension is a consequence of
\mathrm{Height}(T)^{\gamma/\alpha}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{P})
(see
[
7
, Lemma 2.6]
). We prove below the analogous estimate for the truncated tree in the critical case. The proof is inspired by the arguments of Aïdékon, Hu, and Shi
[
3
]
. The main idea is to estimate the local time of types staying inside intervals.

Lemma 3.8
.

Under
1.1
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that for any
c>0
and any
\gamma\in(\omega_{-},\gamma_{1})
,

\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathrm{Height}(T^{c})^{\gamma/\alpha}\Big]\leq C\cdot c^{(\gamma-\omega_{-})}.

(3.27)

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{U}^{c}=\{u\in\mathbb{U}:g([\hskip-1.00006pt[\rho,\rho_{u}]\hskip-1.00006pt])\leq c\}
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
c=\mathrm{e}^{b}
where
b
is an integer. By definition of
T^{c}
,

\mathrm{Height}(T^{c})=\sup_{v\in T^{c}}d(\rho,v)\leq\sup_{u\in\mathbb{U}^{c}}\sum^{|u|}_{i=0}z_{u_{i}}.

For an integer
k\geq-b
and
u\in\mathbb{U}
, define the local time of
(\chi(u_{i}))_{i\leq|u|}
spent in the interval
(e^{-k-1},e^{k})
by

N^{k}_{u}=\sum^{|u|}_{i=0}\mathds{1}_{\{\mathrm{e}^{-k-1}<\chi(u_{i})\leq\mathrm{e}^{-k}\}}.

For
n\geq 1
, define the stopping lines

\mathcal{L}^{k}_{n}=\{u\in\mathbb{U}:N^{k}_{u}=n,N^{k}_{u-}=n-1;\chi(u_{i})\leq c,\forall 1\leq i\leq|u|\}.

(3.28)

For every
u\in\mathbb{U}^{c}
, for each
0\leq i\leq|u|
there exist unique
k
and
n
such that
u_{i}\in\mathcal{L}^{k}_{n}

\displaystyle\hat{E}^{c}_{1,\varepsilon}[F(\hat{\xi}|_{[0,t]})]

\displaystyle=(Z_{\varepsilon}(\log c))^{-1}\hat{E}[F(\hat{\xi}|_{[0,t]})\cdot\mathds{1}_{\{\hat{\xi}_{s}\leq\log c,0\leq s\leq\zeta\wedge\mathrm{e}(\varepsilon)\}}]

\displaystyle=(Z_{\varepsilon}(\log c))^{-1}\hat{E}[F(\hat{\xi}|_{[0,t]})\cdot\mathds{1}_{\{\hat{\xi}_{s}\leq\log c,0\leq s\leq\zeta\wedge\mathrm{e}(\varepsilon)\}}\mathds{1}_{\{\mathrm{e}(\varepsilon)>t\}}]

\displaystyle\quad+(Z_{\varepsilon}(\log c))^{-1}\hat{E}[F(\hat{\xi}|_{[0,t]})\cdot\mathds{1}_{\{\hat{\xi}_{s}\leq\log c,0\leq s\leq\zeta\wedge\mathrm{e}(\varepsilon)\}}\mathds{1}_{\{\mathrm{e}(\varepsilon)\leq t\}}].

The second term on the right hand side converges to
0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
since
F
is bounded,
P(\mathrm{e}(\varepsilon)\leq t)=1-\exp(-\varepsilon t)\sim t\varepsilon
and
\varepsilon/\kappa^{+}(\varepsilon,0)=K^{-1}\kappa^{-}(\varepsilon,0)\to 0
. Conditioned on
\mathrm{e}(\varepsilon)>t
,
\mathrm{e}^{\prime}(\varepsilon):=\mathrm{e}(\varepsilon)-t
is exponentially distributed with parameter
1/\varepsilon
. By Markov property at time
t
, the first term equals

\begin{split}&\hat{E}\bigg[F(\hat{\xi}|_{[0,t]})\cdot\mathds{1}_{\{\hat{\xi}_{s}\leq\log c,0\leq s\leq t\wedge\zeta\}}\mathds{1}_{\{\mathrm{e}(\varepsilon)>t\}}\frac{\hat{P}^{c}_{y,\varepsilon}(\hat{\xi}_{s}\leq\log c,0\leq s\leq\zeta\wedge\mathrm{e}^{\prime}(\varepsilon))|_{y=\exp(\hat{\xi}_{t})}}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\log c)}\bigg]\\
&\,=\hat{E}\bigg[F(\hat{\xi}|_{[0,t]})\cdot\mathds{1}_{\{\hat{\xi}_{s}\leq\log c,0\leq s\leq t\wedge\zeta\}}\mathds{1}_{\{\mathrm{e}(\varepsilon)>t\}}\frac{Z_{\varepsilon}(\log c-\hat{\xi}_{t})}{Z_{\varepsilon}(\log c)}\bigg].\end{split}

(4.10)

Then (
4.9
) implies that the expectation on the right hand side of (
4.10
) converges to

\hat{E}\bigg[F(\hat{\xi}|_{[0,t]})\cdot\mathds{1}_{\{\hat{\xi}_{s}\leq\log c,0\leq s\leq t\wedge\zeta\}}\frac{R(\log c-\hat{\xi}_{t})}{R(\log c)}\bigg]=\hat{E}^{c}[F(\hat{\xi}|_{[0,t]})]

as
\varepsilon\to 0
by dominated convergence. Since
t
is arbitrary, we conclude that the law of the spine satisfies
\hat{P}^{c}_{1,\varepsilon}\to\hat{P}^{c}
.

The law for each dangling tree
{\tt T}_{i}
converges a.s. to a ssMt with law
\mathbb{P}_{y_{i}}
by
2.5
. To see the independence of the dangling trees, we remark that the type of the root
y_{i}
does not depends on
\varepsilon
once its birth time
t_{i}
is smaller than
\mathrm{e}(\varepsilon)
. Thus the independence still holds as
\varepsilon\to 0
. This concludes that
\hat{\mathbb{Q}}^{c}_{(\varepsilon)}\to\hat{\mathbb{Q}}^{c}
.

Finally, to see that the gluing operation produces compact decorated trees, notice that the decorated trees under
\hat{\mathbb{Q}}^{c}_{(\varepsilon)}
are subtrees of
{\tt T}^{\bullet}
under
\tilde{\mathbb{Q}}^{c}
by the coupling. Therefore,
\hat{\mathbb{Q}}^{c}_{(\varepsilon)}\to\hat{\mathbb{Q}}^{c}
could be viewed as convergence of subtrees inside
{\tt T}^{\bullet}
under
\tilde{\mathbb{Q}}^{c}
. Since
{\tt T}^{\bullet}
is compact under
\tilde{\mathbb{Q}}^{c}
, the same holds for
\hat{\mathbb{Q}}^{c}
.
∎

4.1.3
Bifurcators

We next discuss the bifurcators in the critical case. Bifurcation is the phenomenon that two different characteristic quadruplets may determine the same law of a ssMt: two such quadruplets are called bifurcators of each other. We recall some background from
[
7
, Chapter 5]
: Let
\mathrm{ord}:\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{S}_{1}
be the map sending
(y_{0},(y_{i})_{i\geq 1})
to the sequence
(y_{i})_{i\geq 0}
arranged in decreasing order.

Definition 4.2
.

We say that the two quadruplets
(\sigma^{2},\mathrm{a},\boldsymbol{\Lambda};\alpha)
and
(\sigma^{2}_{\Yleft},\mathrm{a}_{\Yleft},\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\Yleft};\alpha_{\Yleft})
are bifurcators of each other, denoted by
(\sigma^{2},\mathrm{a},\boldsymbol{\Lambda};\alpha)\approx(\sigma^{2}_{\Yleft},\mathrm{a}_{\Yleft},\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\Yleft};\alpha_{\Yleft})
, if and only if

\sigma^{2}=\sigma^{2}_{\Yleft},\quad\alpha=\alpha_{\Yleft},\quad\boldsymbol{\Lambda}\circ\mathrm{ord}^{-1}=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\Yleft}\circ\mathrm{ord}^{-1},

(4.11)

and

\mathrm{a}-\mathrm{a}_{\Yleft}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\bigg(\int_{\varepsilon<|y|\leq 1}y\cdot\boldsymbol{\Lambda}(\mathrm{d}y,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{y})-\int_{\varepsilon<|y|\leq 1}y\cdot\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\Yleft}(\mathrm{d}y,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{y})\bigg).

(4.12)

Now we fix two quadruplets
(\sigma^{2},\mathrm{a},\boldsymbol{\Lambda};\alpha)
and
(\sigma^{2}_{\Yleft},\mathrm{a}_{\Yleft},\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\Yleft};\alpha_{\Yleft})
. With these two quadruplets we define, respectively,
P_{x}
and
P^{\Yleft}_{x}
as the laws of decoration-reproduction processes for an individual with type
x>0
,
\mathbb{P}_{x}
and
\mathbb{P}^{\Yleft}_{x}
as the laws of the families of decoration-reproduction processes, and finally
\mathbb{Q}_{x}
and
\mathbb{Q}^{\Yleft}_{x}
as the laws of self-similar Markov trees with roots of type
x
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Assume that
(\sigma^{2},\mathrm{a},\boldsymbol{\Lambda};\alpha)
and
(\sigma^{2}_{\Yleft},\mathrm{a}_{\Yleft},\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\Yleft};\alpha_{\Yleft})
satisfy
1.1
. Then
\mathbb{Q}_{x}=\mathbb{Q}^{\Yleft}_{x}
for all
x>0
, if and only if
(\sigma^{2},\mathrm{a},\boldsymbol{\Lambda};\alpha)
and
(\sigma^{2}_{\Yleft},\mathrm{a}_{\Yleft},\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\Yleft};\alpha_{\Yleft})
are bifurcators of each other.

To prove the theorem, one could use the spinal decomposition theorem
4.1
following the same arguments as in
[
7
, Section 5.3]
. We instead apply the approximation arguments in
Section

2.3
to avoid repetition.

Proof.

Let
{\tt T}^{\varepsilon}
be the subcritical trees coupled with
{\tt T}
as in
Section

2.3
by adding a killing rate of
\varepsilon
, and similarly
{\tt T}^{\varepsilon}_{\Yleft}
for
{\tt T}_{\Yleft}
. Suppose that
(\sigma^{2},\mathrm{a},\boldsymbol{\Lambda};\alpha)
and
(\sigma^{2}_{\Yleft},\mathrm{a}_{\Yleft},\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\Yleft};\alpha_{\Yleft})
are bifurcators of each other, then for each
\varepsilon>0
, the pair
(\sigma^{2},\mathrm{a}-\varepsilon,\boldsymbol{\Lambda};\alpha)
and
(\sigma^{2}_{\Yleft},\mathrm{a}_{\Yleft}-\varepsilon,\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\Yleft};\alpha_{\Yleft})
are bifurcators of each other. By
[
7
, Theorem 5.13]
, the trees
{\tt T}^{\varepsilon}
and
{\tt T}^{\varepsilon}_{\Yleft}
have the same law. By
2.5
we have
{\tt T}^{\varepsilon}\to{\tt T}
and
{\tt T}^{\varepsilon}_{\Yleft}\to{\tt T}_{\Yleft}
almost surely, we conclude that
\mathbb{Q}_{x}=\mathbb{Q}^{\Yleft}_{x}
.

Conversely, we assume that
\mathbb{Q}_{x}=\mathbb{Q}^{\Yleft}_{x}
. By self-similarity, we have
\alpha=\alpha_{\Yleft}
. From
2.6
, adding killing to a ssMt is intrinsic hence
{\tt T}^{\varepsilon}
and
{\tt T}^{\varepsilon}_{\Yleft}
have the same law. By
[
7
, Theorem 5.13]
again, we see that
(\sigma^{2},\mathrm{a}-\varepsilon,\boldsymbol{\Lambda};\alpha)
and
(\sigma^{2}_{\Yleft},\mathrm{a}_{\Yleft}-\varepsilon,\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\Yleft};\alpha_{\Yleft})
are bifurcators of each other. So are
(\sigma^{2},\mathrm{a},\boldsymbol{\Lambda};\alpha)
and
(\sigma^{2}_{\Yleft},\mathrm{a}_{\Yleft},\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\Yleft};\alpha_{\Yleft})
. ∎

Under

in the sense of weak convergence of finite measures on

Remark 4.5

Here we restrict ourselves to convergence along a sequence as in

7
, Proposition 2.15]
though we also believe it also holds for
\gamma\downarrow\omega_{-}
.

We denote by
\lambda^{\gamma}_{c}
the measure
\lambda^{\gamma}
restricted to
T_{c}
. We see

a.s. for some

and

along a sequence for

, and then apply the standard diagonal argument. With (

),

is a direct consequence of the following lemma by adapting the proof of

7
, Proposition 2.15]
:

Lemma 4.6
.

We have

\lim_{\gamma\downarrow\omega_{-}}\frac{(\gamma-\omega_{-})}{Kc_{0}^{-}}\lambda^{\gamma}_{c}(T^{c})=\mu^{c}(T^{c}),\quad\mbox{in }L^{1}(\mathbb{P}_{1}).

From now on we focus on the proof of
4.6
. The idea of the proof resembles that in the subcritical case. We first introduce a measure
\tilde{\lambda}^{\gamma}_{c}
obtained by transferring the mass of each decorated branch under
\mathrm{d}\lambda^{\gamma}_{c}
to its root. The difference between
\tilde{\lambda}^{\gamma}_{c}

{\lambda}^{\gamma}_{c}

7

. The next step is the main technical part, which is to compare

. We now formalise these ideas.

Consider the contribution from a decorated branch with type

to the total mass of

. The expectation of

over this decorated branch is given by

E_{x}\bigg[\int^{z}_{0}X(s)^{\gamma-\alpha}\mathds{1}_{\{\tau^{c}>t\}}\mathrm{d}t\bigg]=x^{s}E\bigg[\int^{\zeta}_{0}\mathrm{e}^{\gamma\xi_{s}}\mathds{1}_{\{\tau^{\log(c/x)}>s\}}\mathrm{d}s\bigg].

For ease of notation, we set

r_{\gamma}(c):=\mathbb{E}[\int^{\zeta}_{0}\mathrm{e}^{\gamma\xi_{s}}\mathds{1}_{\{\tau^{\log c}>s\}}\mathrm{d}s]

We could rewrite (
3.17
) as

R_{\gamma}(c)=\mathbb{E}[\lambda^{\gamma}_{c}(T^{c})]=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{u\in\mathbb{U}}(\chi(u))^{\gamma}r_{\gamma}\Big((c/\chi(u))\Big)\cdot\mathds{1}_{\{g([\hskip-0.81949pt[\rho,\rho_{u}]\hskip-0.81949pt])\leq c\}}\bigg].

(4.13)

A rough bound on

r_{\gamma}(c)\leq\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int^{\zeta}_{0}\mathrm{e}^{\gamma\xi_{s}}\mathrm{d}s\bigg]=-\frac{1}{\psi(\gamma)}

(4.14)

We define the measure
\tilde{\lambda}^{\gamma}_{c}

\tilde{\lambda}^{\gamma}_{c}=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{U}}\delta_{\rho_{u}}(\chi(u))^{\gamma}r_{\gamma}\Big(\frac{c}{\chi(u)}\Big)\cdot\mathds{1}_{\{g([\hskip-0.81949pt[\rho,\rho_{u}]\hskip-0.81949pt])\leq c\}}.

(4.15)

The next lemma shows that the difference between the total mass of
\tilde{\lambda}^{\gamma}_{c}
and
{\lambda}^{\gamma}_{c}
remains bounded. The proof of this lemma is close to (ii) in the proof of
[
7
, Lemma 2.16]
. We use the strategy in
3.8
to sum over intervals and apply the estimates on the local time for the types.

Lemma 4.7
.

There exists a constant
C>0
, such that for
\gamma\in(\omega_{-},\omega_{-}+\Delta_{0})

\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\tilde{\lambda}^{\gamma}_{c}(T^{c})-\lambda^{\gamma}_{c}(T^{c})\Big|\Big]\leq C.

(4.16)

Proof of
4.7
.

Write for
u\in\mathbb{U}
,

A^{c}_{\gamma}(u)=\chi(u)^{-\gamma}\int^{z_{u}}_{0}f_{u}(t)^{\gamma-\alpha}\cdot\mathds{1}_{\{\tau^{\log(c/\chi(u))}>t\}}\mathrm{d}t.

We rewrite the difference of measure by (recall the definition of

in (
3.28

\displaystyle\lambda^{\gamma}_{c}(T^{c})-\tilde{\lambda}^{\gamma}_{c}(T^{c})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{u\in\mathbb{U}}\mathds{1}_{\{g([\hskip-0.81949pt[\rho,\rho_{u}]\hskip-0.81949pt])\leq c\}}\cdot\chi(u)^{\gamma}\bigg(A^{c}_{\gamma}(u)-r_{\gamma}\Big(\frac{c}{\chi(u)}\Big)\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=-b}^{\infty}\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\sum_{u\in\mathcal{L}^{k}_{n}}\mathds{1}_{\{g([\hskip-0.81949pt[\rho,\rho_{u}]\hskip-0.81949pt])\leq c\}}\cdot\chi(u)^{\gamma}\bigg(A^{c}_{\gamma}(u)-r_{\gamma}\Big(\frac{c}{\chi(u)}\Big)\bigg).

Choose
q\in(1,2)
to be defined later. By triangle inequality and the Jensen’s inequality, we bound the expectation in (

) by

\mathbb{E}\Big[|\tilde{\lambda}^{\gamma}_{c}(T^{c})-\lambda^{\gamma}_{c}(T^{c})|\Big]\leq\sum^{\infty}_{k=-b}\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\bigg|\sum_{u\in\mathcal{L}^{k}_{n}}\mathds{1}_{\{g([\hskip-0.81949pt[\rho,\rho_{u}]\hskip-0.81949pt])\leq c\}}\cdot\chi(u)^{\gamma}\bigg(A^{c}_{\gamma}(u)-r_{\gamma}\Big(\frac{c}{\chi(u)}\Big)\bigg)\bigg|^{q}\Bigg]^{1/q}.

c(q)\sum^{\infty}_{k=-b}\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{u\in\mathcal{L}^{k}_{n}}\mathds{1}_{\{g([\hskip-0.81949pt[\rho,\rho_{u}]\hskip-0.81949pt])\leq c\}}\cdot(\chi(u))^{\gamma q}\bigg]^{1/q}\cdot E_{1}\bigg[\bigg|\int^{\zeta}_{0}\exp(\gamma\xi(t))\cdot\mathds{1}_{\{\tau^{\log(c/\chi(u))}>t\}}\mathrm{d}t\bigg|^{q}\bigg]^{1/q},

\sup_{\gamma\in[\omega_{-},\omega_{-}+\Delta_{0}]}E_{1}\bigg[\bigg|\int^{\zeta}_{0}\mathrm{e}^{\gamma\xi(t)}\mathrm{d}t\bigg|^{q}\bigg]\\
\leq E_{1}\bigg[\bigg|\int^{\zeta}_{0}\mathrm{e}^{\omega_{-}\xi(t)}\mathrm{d}t\bigg|^{q}\bigg]+E_{1}\bigg[\bigg|\int^{\zeta}_{0}\mathrm{e}^{(\omega_{-}+\Delta_{0})\xi(t)}\mathrm{d}t\bigg|^{q}\bigg].

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[|\tilde{\lambda}^{\gamma}_{c}(T^{c})-\lambda^{\gamma}_{c}(T^{c})|\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Cc(q)\sum^{\infty}_{k=-b}\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{u\in\mathcal{L}^{k}_{n}}\mathds{1}_{\{g([\hskip-0.81949pt[\rho,\rho_{u}]\hskip-0.81949pt])\leq c\}}\cdot(\chi(u))^{\gamma q}\bigg]^{1/q}

.

Fix

\mathbb{E}[\tilde{\lambda}^{\gamma}_{c}(T^{c})|\mathcal{F}_{n}]=\sum_{|u|=n}(\chi(u))^{\gamma}R_{\gamma}(c/\chi(u))\mathds{1}_{\{g([\hskip-0.81949pt[\rho,\rho_{u}]\hskip-0.81949pt])\leq c\}}+\sum_{|u|<n}(\chi(u))^{\gamma}r_{\gamma}(c/\chi(u))\mathds{1}_{\{g([\hskip-0.81949pt[\rho,\rho_{u}]\hskip-0.81949pt])\leq c\}}.

(Properties of

The following statements hold.

(4.22)

2.

For

, there exists a constant

, such that for all

and

(4.23)

3.

C>0
, such that for all

and

such that for

Proof.

We first prove (

). As

, we have

\psi(\gamma)\to\psi(\omega_{-})
and

\int_{[0,\log c]}\mathrm{e}^{(\gamma-\omega_{-})x}R(\mathrm{d}x)\to R([0,\log c])=R(\log c)

\displaystyle(\gamma-\omega_{-})\int_{[0,\infty)}\mathrm{e}^{-(\gamma-\omega_{-})x}R^{+}(\mathrm{d}x)

\displaystyle=(\gamma-\omega_{-})^{2}\int^{\infty}_{0}\mathrm{e}^{-(\gamma-\omega_{-})x}R^{+}(x)\mathrm{d}x

Here we change variables by setting

. Since

converges to

and is bounded by a linear function in

.

To prove (

) and (

), notice that the term

is bounded. It remains to control the integral

. From (

), there exist constants

and

such that for

. For each

, we have

\displaystyle K(n):=\int_{[M+a(n-1),M+an]}\mathrm{e}^{(\gamma-\omega_{-})x}R(\mathrm{d}x)

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{(\gamma-\omega_{-})(M+an)}c_{+}a

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{(\gamma-\omega_{-})a}c_{+}\int^{M+an}_{M+a(n-1)}\mathrm{e}^{(\gamma-\omega_{-})x}\mathrm{d}x

and

When

R_{\gamma}(0)\leq R_{\gamma}(c_{i})\leq R_{\gamma}(2M)
. When
\log c>2M
, we have the upper bound

R_{\gamma}(c)\leq R_{\gamma}(2M)+c_{+}\sum_{n=1}^{[(\log c-M)/a]+1}K(n)\leq R_{\gamma}(M)+\mathrm{e}^{(\gamma-\omega_{-})a}c_{+}\int^{\log c+a}_{M}\mathrm{e}^{(\gamma-\omega_{-})x}\mathrm{d}x

(4.26)

and the lower bound

R_{\gamma}(c)\geq R_{\gamma}(M)+c_{-}\sum_{n=1}^{[(\log c-M)/a]}K(n)\geq R_{\gamma}(M)+\mathrm{e}^{-(\gamma-\omega_{-})a}c_{-}\int^{\log c-a}_{M}\mathrm{e}^{(\gamma-\omega_{-})x}\mathrm{d}x.

(4.27)

By calculating the integral
\int^{\log c-a}_{M}\mathrm{e}^{(\gamma-\omega_{-})x}\mathrm{d}x
, (
4.23
) and (
4.24
) are direct consequences of (
4.26
).

We finally prove (
4.25
). By (
3.17
), we have

\frac{R_{\gamma}(c_{1})}{R_{\gamma}(c_{2})}=\frac{\int_{[0,\log c_{1}]}\mathrm{e}^{(\gamma-\omega_{-})x}R(\mathrm{d}x)}{\int_{[0,\log c_{2}]}\mathrm{e}^{(\gamma-\omega_{-})x}R(\mathrm{d}x)}.

We argue by distinguishing cases depending on the values of
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
. When
\log c_{1},\log c_{2}\leq 2M
, the fraction is bounded by constants. When
\log c_{1}>2M\geq\log c_{2}
(
\log c_{2}>2M\geq\log c_{1}
),
R_{\gamma}(c_{2})
(
R_{\gamma}(c_{1})
) is bounded from below (above), and the upper (lower) bound in (
4.26
) ((
4.27
)) gives the desired bound. When
\log c_{1},\log c_{2}>2M
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\frac{R_{\gamma}(c_{1})}{R_{\gamma}(c_{2})}\leq\frac{R_{\gamma}(M)+\mathrm{e}^{(\gamma-\omega_{-})a}c_{+}\int^{\log c_{1}+a}_{M}\mathrm{e}^{(\gamma-\omega_{-})x}\mathrm{d}x}{R_{\gamma}(M)+\mathrm{e}^{-(\gamma-\omega_{-})a}c_{-}\int^{\log c_{2}-a}_{M}\mathrm{e}^{(\gamma-\omega_{-})x}\mathrm{d}x}\leq C\Big(\frac{c_{1}}{c_{2}}\Big)^{\gamma-\omega_{-}}\left(1+\log_{+}(c_{1}/c_{2})\right).

We conclude by combining all four cases.
∎

Corollary 4.10
.

For the random walk
({\hat{\tt S}}_{n})_{n\geq 0}

(2): For

, there exists a constant

such that for all

and

(\gamma-\omega_{-})\cdot{\tt E}\left[\sum^{{\tt T}^{+}_{b}-1}_{n=0}\mathrm{e}^{-(\gamma-\omega_{-}){\hat{\tt S}}_{n}}\right]\leq C\mathrm{e}^{(\gamma-\omega_{-})b}.

(4.28)

4.2.2
Proof of
4.8
: good and bad branches

To prove
4.8
, we use a similar idea to that in
[
2
, Section 5]
by introducing a classification of decorated branches into good and bad branches. The criterion is chosen such that the bad branches is small in
L^{1}(\mathbb{P})
, while the good branches is bounded in
L^{2}(\mathbb{P})
. We consider the following condition analogous to
[
2
, Definition 5.4]
. The heuristic is to control the increment of the expected length above each generation along the lineage of the vertex. It could be viewed as
a decaying condition compared to the estimates of
R_{\gamma}(c)
in
4.9
.

Definition 4.11
.

Set
\theta=\frac{\omega_{-}}{4}
. Let
(c,A)
be a pair of parameters with
c>1
and
A>1
. We say that a decorated branch with label
u\in\mathbb{U}^{c}
is good if for
0\leq k\leq|u|-1
,

\sum^{\infty}_{i=1}\bigg(\frac{\chi(u_{k}i)}{\chi(u_{k})}\bigg)^{\omega_{-}}\Big(1+\ln_{+}\Big(\frac{\chi(u_{k})}{\chi(u_{k}i)}\Big)\Big)\leq A\bigg(\frac{c}{\chi(u_{k})}\bigg)^{\theta}.

(4.29)

We say that a decorated branch is bad if it is not good. Denote by
\mathbb{B}^{c}
and
\mathbb{G}^{c}
the set of labels of bad and good regions, and by
T^{c}_{G}
and
T^{c}_{B}
for the subset of
T^{c}
consisting of good and bad decorated branches, respectively.

With the classification, we could state our estimates on the good and bad branches.

Lemma 4.12
.

There exists a constant
C>0
, such that for
(c,A)
and
\gamma\in(\omega_{-},\omega_{-}+\Delta_{0})
, we have

\mathbb{E}[(\gamma-\omega_{-})\tilde{\lambda}^{\gamma}_{c}(T^{c}_{B})]\leq CA^{-\eta}c^{(\gamma-\omega_{-})}.

(4.30)

Lemma 4.13
.

There exists a constant
C>0
, such that for
(c,A)
and
\gamma\in(\omega_{-},\omega_{-}+\Delta_{0})
, we have

\mathbb{E}[(\gamma-\omega_{-})^{2}\tilde{\lambda}^{\gamma}_{c}(T^{c}_{G})^{2}]\leq CA^{2}c^{(2\gamma-\omega_{-})}.

(4.31)

Let us see how these two lemmas imply
4.8
.

Proof of
4.8
.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
. By
4.12
, we choose
A>0
such that uniformly for
\gamma\in(\omega_{-},\omega_{-}+\Delta_{0})
,

(\gamma-\omega_{-})\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\tilde{\lambda}^{\gamma}_{c}(T^{c}_{B})-\mathbb{E}\big[\tilde{\lambda}^{\gamma}_{c}(T^{c}_{B})\big|\mathcal{F}_{n}\big]\Big|\Big]\leq 2\mathbb{E}[(\gamma-\omega_{-})\tilde{\lambda}^{\gamma}_{c}(T^{c}_{B})]\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{3}.

It suffices to show that when
n
is large enough, for
\gamma\in(\omega_{-},\omega_{-}+\Delta_{0})
,

(\gamma-\omega_{-})\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\tilde{\lambda}^{\gamma}_{c}(T^{c}_{G}(n))-\mathbb{E}\big[\tilde{\lambda}^{\gamma}_{c}(T^{c}_{G}(n))\big|\mathcal{F}_{n}\big]\Big|\mathds{1}_{\big\{(\gamma-\omega_{-})\big|\tilde{\lambda}^{\gamma}_{c}(T^{c}_{G})-\mathbb{E}[\tilde{\lambda}^{\gamma}_{c}(T^{c}_{G})|\mathcal{F}_{n}]\big|>\frac{\varepsilon}{3}\big\}}\Big]\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{3}.

(4.32)

The left-hand side is smaller than

\frac{3}{\varepsilon}(\gamma-\omega_{-})^{2}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\tilde{\lambda}^{\gamma}_{c}(T^{c}_{G}(n))-\mathbb{E}\big[\tilde{\lambda}^{\gamma}_{c}(T^{c}_{G}(n))\big|\mathcal{F}_{n}\big]\Big|^{2}\Big]\leq\frac{3}{\varepsilon}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{|u|=n}\mathds{1}_{\{u\in\mathbb{G}^{c}\}}(\gamma-\omega_{-})^{2}(\tilde{\lambda}^{\gamma}_{c}(T^{c}_{G}(u)))^{2}\bigg].

Here,
T^{c}_{G}(u)
is the subtree consisting of good branches rooted at
u
. By Markov property and the scaling property, the law of
\tilde{\lambda}^{\gamma}_{c}(T^{c}_{G}(u))
condition on
\mathcal{F}_{n}
has the same law as
(\chi(u))^{\gamma}\tilde{\lambda}^{\gamma}_{c^{\prime}}(T^{c^{\prime}}_{G})
where
c^{\prime}=c/\chi(u)
and the good branches are defined according to the parameters
(c^{\prime},A)
. We apply
4.13
to bound the display above by

\displaystyle\frac{3CA^{2}}{\varepsilon}\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\sum_{|u|=n}\mathds{1}_{\{u\in\mathbb{G}^{c}\}}(\chi(u))^{2\gamma}\Big(\frac{c}{\chi(u)}\Big)^{2\gamma-\omega_{-}}\Bigg]\leq\frac{3CA^{2}c^{2\gamma-\omega_{-}}}{\varepsilon}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{|u|=n}\mathds{1}_{\{\chi(u_{k})\leq c,k\leq n\}}(\chi(u))^{\omega_{-}}\right].

Applying the many-to-one formula (
3.4
), we have

\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\sum_{|u|=n}\mathds{1}_{\{\chi(u_{k})\leq c,k\leq n\}}(\chi(u))^{\omega_{-}}\Bigg]={\tt P}({\hat{\tt S}}_{k}\geq-\log c,1\leq k\leq n)\leq\frac{C{\tt R}(\log c)}{\sqrt{n}}.

The last inequality is from
[
28
, Equation (A.7)]
, where the constant
C>0
only depends on the random walk. Now (
4.32
) holds uniformly for
\gamma\in(\omega_{-},\omega_{-}+\Delta_{0})
when
n
is large.

∎

Now we return to the proofs of

Proof of
4.12

Throughout the proof we allow the universal constant

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg(\sum^{\infty}_{i=1}g(\chi(i))\bigg)^{1+q}\bigg]\leq C\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg(\sum^{\infty}_{i=1}(\chi(i)^{\omega_{-}}+\chi(i)^{\omega_{-}\delta})\bigg)^{1+q}\bigg]<\infty.

(4.33)

Let
\mathbb{B}^{c}_{\leftarrow}
be the set of labels
v\in\mathbb{U}
such that

\mathbb{E}[\tilde{\lambda}^{\gamma}_{c}(T^{c}_{B})]=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\sum_{|v|=n}\mathds{1}_{\{v\in\mathbb{G}^{c}\}}\mathds{1}_{\big\{\sum^{\infty}_{i=1}g(\frac{\chi(vi)}{\chi(v)})>A(\frac{c}{\chi(v)})^{\theta}\big\}}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{U}^{*}}(\chi(vu))^{\gamma}r_{\gamma}\Big(\frac{c}{\chi(vu)}\Big)\mathds{1}_{\{vu\in\mathbb{U}^{c}\}}\Bigg].

(4.34)

Let

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x}\bigg[\mathds{1}_{B_{x}}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{U}^{*}}(\chi(u))^{\gamma}r_{\gamma}\Big(\frac{c}{\chi(u)}\Big)\mathds{1}_{\{u\in\mathbb{U}^{c}\}}\bigg]

\displaystyle=x^{\gamma}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\mathds{1}_{B_{x}}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{U}^{*}}(\chi(u))^{\gamma}r_{\gamma}\Big(\frac{c(x)}{\chi(u)}\Big)\mathds{1}_{\{u\in\mathbb{U}^{c(x)}\}}\bigg]

Taking conditional expectation with respect to

Using the bounds of the fraction in (

Replacing the event

Therefore,

\mathbb{E}[(\gamma-\omega_{-})\tilde{\lambda}^{\gamma}_{c}(T^{c}_{B})]\leq CA^{-q}c^{\gamma-\omega_{-}}c^{-\theta q}{\tt E}\bigg[\sum^{{\tt T}_{\log c}^{+}-1}_{n=0}\mathrm{e}^{-\theta q{\hat{\tt S}}_{n}}\bigg]\leq CA^{-q}c^{\gamma-\omega_{-}},
