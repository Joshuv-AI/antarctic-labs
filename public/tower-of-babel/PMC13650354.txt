Efficacy and safety of trastuzumab emtansine in patients with advanced human epidermal growth factor receptor 2-positive extramammary Paget’s disease: an investigator-initiated, open-label, single-arm, multicentre, prospective, phase II clinical trial (TEMENOS-2)☆
Authors: S. Arakawa, I. Hirai, Y. Nakamura, H. Uchi, S. Ohe, T. Isei, T. Takenouchi, S. Mori, T. Takai, S. Yoshikawa, T. Fujimura, Y. Yamamoto, T. Ozaki, R. Takemura, T. Funakoshi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundExtramammary Paget’s disease (EMPD) is a rare cutaneous adenocarcinoma with no established standard systemic therapy owing to limited clinical evidence. Approximately 40% of metastatic EMPD cases overexpress human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2), suggesting a potential therapeutic target. However, prospective evidence for HER2-directed antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) in advanced EMPD is lacking. This study aimed to assess the efficacy and safety of trastuzumab emtansine (T-DM1) in patients with HER2-positive advanced EMPD.Patients and methodsThis investigator-initiated, open-label, single-arm, multicentre, prospective phase II trial enrolled Japanese patients with HER2-positive metastatic EMPD who had not received prior HER2-targeted therapy. Patients received intravenous T-DM1 at 3.6 mg/kg every 3 weeks for up to 2 years. The primary endpoint was objective response rate (ORR) assessed by independent central review. Secondary endpoints included overall survival (OS), progression-free survival (PFS), and safety.ResultsFourteen patients were enrolled and received study treatment between December 2021 and July 2024. The median age was 65 (range: 42-80) years. Thirteen patients had an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status of 0, and 12 had distant metastases at baseline. The ORR assessed by central review was 92.9% [95% confidence interval (CI) 66.1%-99.8%], with best responses of complete response (n = 2), partial response (n = 11), and progressive disease (n = 1). Median OS was not reached, whereas median PFS was 16.95 months (95% CI 5.75 months–not applicable). Grade 3 adverse events occurred in 6 (42.9%) patients. No grade ≥4 adverse events were observed, and one patient discontinued treatment due to grade 1 interstitial lung disease.ConclusionsThis study provides the first prospective clinical evidence supporting the efficacy and safety of a HER2-directed ADC in HER2-positive advanced EMPD. T-DM1 demonstrated robust antitumour activity with a tolerable safety profile in this predominantly older adult population.

Introduction

Extramammary Paget’s disease (EMPD) is a rare cutaneous adenocarcinoma that typically presents in apocrine gland–rich areas of older adults, involving the genital, perianal, and axillary skin.1 In the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results registries, the age-adjusted incidence of EMPD was 0.23 per 100 000 person-years, with the highest rate observed in the Asian/Pacific Islander ethnic group at 0.44 per 100 000 person-years.2 Although most tumour cells proliferate in situ within the epidermis and exhibit indolent behaviour,3 they acquire high metastatic potential once they become invasive,4 leading to a poor prognosis. The 5-year survival rate of patients with distant metastatic EMPD has been reported to be as low as 7%.5 Owing to its rarity, robust clinical evidence for effective systemic therapies in advanced EMPD remains limited,6 highlighting an unmet medical need.

Overexpression or gene amplification of human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) is a well-recognised driver of tumour malignancy and poor clinical outcomes in several cancers.6 Approximately 40% of patients with metastatic EMPD display HER2 positivity, which appears to correlate with poorer survival outcomes.6,7 These observations suggest that HER2 serves as a key oncogenic driver and a promising therapeutic target in this disease. Supporting this notion, several case reports have documented tumour regression and symptomatic improvement with trastuzumab monotherapy in HER2-overexpressing EMPD.8, 9, 10, 11, 12

Antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs), which combine the target specificity of monoclonal antibodies with the cytotoxic potency of chemotherapeutic agents, have transformed treatment approaches across multiple tumour types.6 Preclinical mouse models and retrospective analyses have suggested that HER2-targeted ADCs are also effective in EMPD.6,13 However, prospective clinical evidence is lacking. Given the biological rationale and the unmet clinical need, evaluating the therapeutic potential of HER2-targeted ADCs in EMPD represents a logical next step.

Therefore, this phase II study aimed to assess the efficacy and safety of trastuzumab emtansine (T-DM1), an ADC consisting of trastuzumab linked to the antimicrotubular agent emtansine,14 in patients with HER2-positive advanced EMPD.

Materials and Methods

Study design and patients

TEMENOS-2 is an investigator-initiated, open-label, single-arm, multicentre, prospective, phase II trial (Japan Registry of Clinical Trials identifier: jRCT2031210436) conducted to evaluate the efficacy and safety of T-DM1 in patients with HER2-positive metastatic EMPD. The trial was conducted at nine sites across Japan, and patients were recruited between September 2021 and September 2024.

Eligible patients were aged 20-80 years with histopathologically diagnosed unresectable or metastatic EMPD. HER2-positive tumour tissue from primary, recurrent, or metastatic lesions was required, and patients must not have received prior HER2-targeted therapy. HER2 positivity was defined as immunohistochemistry (IHC) 3+ confirmed by central pathological review or HER2 gene amplification by in situ hybridisation (ISH), based on the HER2 testing algorithm for breast cancer in the American Society of Clinical Oncology/College of American Pathologists guideline.15 Other key eligibility criteria included an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0 or 1,16 at least one measurable metastatic lesion other than the primary lesion as defined by RECIST version 1.1, a period of more than 3 weeks since the last antitumour treatment (if any), a left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) ≥50%, and adequate haematologic and organ function.

Patients with active cardiac dysfunction or brain metastases, interstitial lung disease, hypersensitivity to the study drug, or other uncontrolled medical conditions were excluded. Supplementary Table S1 (available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108282) presents the detailed inclusion and exclusion criteria.

Procedure

T-DM1 (Kadcyla®) was administered intravenously in an outpatient setting at a dose of 3.6 mg/kg every 3 weeks. Dose reductions to a minimum of 2.4 mg/kg were permitted based on toxicity.17 Treatment continued for up to 2 years or until confirmed disease progression, unacceptable adverse events (AEs), pregnancy, patient refusal, physician decision, or a delay in T-DM1 administration beyond day 43 from the scheduled dosing date of each cycle.

Tumour response was evaluated every 6 weeks for the first 6 months and every 9 weeks thereafter until disease progression was confirmed, using computed tomography or magnetic resonance imaging according to RECIST version 1.1. Imaging assessments were performed every 3 months during the post-treatment follow-up phase.

Safety assessments included monitoring for infusion-related reactions during and after each T-DM1 administration. LVEF was assessed regularly to evaluate potential trastuzumab-associated cardiac toxicity. AEs were assessed and graded throughout the treatment period according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE), version 5.0. Clinical examinations, blood counts, and biochemistry tests were performed on day 1 of each cycle.

Patients were followed up for safety until 4 weeks after treatment discontinuation, 15 days after study withdrawal, or initiation of another antitumour treatment, whichever occurred first.

Outcomes

The primary endpoint was the objective response rate (ORR) assessed by independent central review, defined as the proportion of patients achieving a best overall response of complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) according to RECIST version 1.1.

Secondary endpoints included efficacy and safety outcomes. Efficacy endpoints comprised ORR assessed by investigators at each medical institution, overall survival (OS), progression-free survival (PFS), rate of change in tumour size, duration of response, time to response, total treatment period, and number of treatment cycles. Safety endpoints included the type, frequency, and severity of AEs, evaluated according to CTCAE, version 5.0. OS was defined as the time from treatment initiation to death from any cause. PFS was defined as the time from treatment initiation to the first documentation of disease progression or death from any cause, whichever occurred first.

Tumour biomarker analysis

Serum levels of the following five tumour markers were measured: carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) using chemiluminescent immunoassay (CLIA) with the ARCHITECT® CEA assay (Abbott, Abbott Park, IL, USA; cut-off 5.0 ng/mL); cytokeratin 19 fragment 21-1 (CYFRA 21-1) using electrochemiluminescence immunoassay with the Elecsys® CYFRA 21-1 assay (Roche, Basal, Switzerland; cut-off 3.5 ng/mL); carbohydrate antigen 125 (CA125) using CLIA with the ARCHITECT® CA125 II assay (Abbott; cut-off 35.0 U/mL); carbohydrate antigen 19-9 (CA19-9) using CLIA with the ARCHITECT® CA19-9 XR assay (Abbott; cut-off 37.0 U/mL); and cancer antigen 15-3 (CA15-3) using the ARCHITECT® CA15-3 assay (Abbott; cut-off 31.3 U/mL). All tumour marker levels were assessed at the same time points as imaging assessments.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using two-sided tests with a significance level of 0.05. Efficacy analyses were conducted using the full analysis set (FAS), which included all patients who received at least one dose of the study drug. ORR was the primary endpoint prespecified in the protocol and statistical analysis plan. The expected ORR of 35% was based on reported response rates of T-DM1 in HER2-positive metastatic breast cancer: 43.6% in the EMILIA study18 and 38.4% in the domestic phase II JO22997 trial.19 Given the lack of standard therapy in the present patient population and limited data on previously untreated patients, a uniform expected ORR of 35% was considered clinically reasonable. The threshold ORR of 5% was set based on historical placebo response rates in various cancers (∼2.7%)20 and to account for uncertainty.

A minimum sample size of 12 patients was required to achieve 80% power with a two-sided significance level of 0.05. To allow for potential dropouts, the target sample size was set at 14 patients. The null hypothesis was rejected if the lower bound of the Clopper–Pearson 95% confidence interval (CI) for ORR exceeded 5%.

For OS and PFS, survival functions were estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method. Median survival times and corresponding 95% CIs were estimated using the Brookmeyer–Crowley method. Safety analyses were conducted using the safety analysis set (SAF), which comprised all patients who received at least one dose of the study drug.

All AEs were coded using MedDRA/J version 27.1, and incidence rates were tabulated with exact two-sided 95% CIs calculated from the binomial distribution. Statistical analyses were performed using SAS version 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC), and figures were generated using R version 4.5.1 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

The analysis data cut-off was defined as the time point when all patients who had not discontinued or withdrawn from the study had completed the 6-month efficacy assessment following the first administration of the study drug. Patients who were alive or progression free at the data cut-off date were censored.

Results

Study population and clinical characteristics

A total of 37 patients provided consent and underwent study screening between December 2021 and July 2024. To illustrate the HER2 assessment, representative immunohistochemical staining patterns across the full range of scores (0–3+) are shown in Supplementary Figure S1 (available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108282). Twenty-two patients had negative HER2 test results, and one patient had interstitial lung disease, resulting in 14 enrolled patients. All 14 patients received the planned treatment and were included in both the FAS and SAF.

No patients experienced an infusion reaction to T-DM1 or showed a decrease in LVEF below 45% at any point during the treatment course. One patient required a dose reduction due to anorexia, and another required a dose reduction due to fatigue. Only one (7.1%) patient discontinued study treatment because of an AE, specifically grade 1 interstitial lung disease.

Tumour biomarker analysis

The positivity rates of tumour markers were as follows: CEA, 92.9% (13/14); CYFRA 21-1, 85.7% (12/14); CA125, 35.7% (5/14); CA19-9, 28.6% (4/14); and CA15-3, 28.6% (4/14) (Supplementary Table S2, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108282). Excluding patients with progressive disease, levels of all tumour markers decreased from baseline at certain time points during treatment.

Discussion

To our knowledge, this is the first prospective study demonstrating the efficacy and safety of a HER2-directed ADC in the treatment of HER2-positive advanced EMPD. T-DM1 showed a notably high ORR of 92.9% (95% CI 66.1%-99.8%). As the lower limit of the 95% CI exceeded the prespecified threshold of 5%, the study met its primary efficacy criterion. A discrepancy was observed between blinded independent central review and investigator-assessed ORR, which may be partly attributable to differences in the evaluation of a pulmonary finding in one patient, although the exact reason remains unclear. The favourable outcomes observed for ORR, OS, PFS, and tumour shrinkage confirm the strong antitumour activity of T-DM1.

HER2-targeted therapy in EMPD has previously been supported mainly by isolated case reports of trastuzumab monotherapy.8, 9, 10, 11, 12 Three case reports of T-DM1 have also shown favourable responses in HER2-overexpressing EMPD, including one case in which failure of trastuzumab monotherapy was initially observed.22, 23, 24 A retrospective analysis of patients with metastatic EMPD treated with disitamab vedotin at a tertiary cancer centre in China between January 2017 and April 2024 reported an objective response in 8 of 11 patients, with a median OS and progression-free interval of 21.9 and 5.5 months, respectively.13 The DESTINY-PanTumor02 trial is one of the prospective tumour-agnostic studies evaluating HER2-targeted ADCs. In this trial, three patients with advanced EMPD received trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd), among whom two achieved a clinical response.25 Findings from the present prospective study are consistent with the growing body of clinical evidence supporting the effectiveness of HER2-targeted therapies in HER2-positive EMPD and further reinforce HER2 as a clinically relevant therapeutic target.

Regarding prospective clinical studies specifically focused on EMPD, the EMPD-HER2DOC study—the only trial reported to date for advanced EMPD—evaluated HER2-based therapy with trastuzumab combined with docetaxel.26 Although the ORR was high at 76.9%, the independent effect of HER2 inhibition alone remained unclear in this combination regimen. In contrast, the present study provides prospective evidence of the clinical efficacy of pure HER2-targeted therapy, highlighting its unique significance.

With respect to tumour markers, elevations in either CEA or CYFRA 21-1 levels were observed in all patients, and changes in these markers appeared to correlate with treatment response. These findings are consistent with a previous report demonstrating that combination assays of CEA and CYFRA 21-1 are useful for monitoring treatment response in patients with metastatic EMPD, particularly among those with elevation of either marker.27

Safety considerations are especially important in EMPD, as most patients are older adults and may be more vulnerable to treatment-related toxicities. In the EMPD-HER2DOC study, haematological toxicity occurred more frequently than expected, likely due to advanced age, and 10 of 13 patients required docetaxel dose reduction because of neutropenia, with 7 ultimately discontinuing docetaxel.26 In the present study, the tolerability of T-DM1 was comparable to that observed in the pivotal EMILIA study of HER2-positive metastatic breast cancer, despite our cohort being ∼10 years older (median age: 65 versus 53 years).18 Although differences in sample size and study design should be considered, favourable tolerability in our cohort was evidenced by the absence of grade 4-5 AEs, a low incidence of bleeding events, mild cardiac and hepatic toxicities, and high treatment continuity. Preservation of tolerability in this older population represents an important clinical finding.

Regarding the selection of the HER2-targeted ADC in this study, T-DM1 was chosen primarily due to its availability at the time of study planning, as T-DXd was not accessible under the same conditions. T-DM1 and T-DXd share the same monoclonal antibody; however, T-DM1 differs in that it employs a microtubule inhibitor rather than a topoisomerase I inhibitor, uses a stable rather than cleavable linker, has a lower drug-to-antibody ratio (3.5 versus 8), and lacks a bystander effect.28 These differences likely account for the distinct efficacy and toxicity profiles of the two agents. T-DXd has recently demonstrated superior efficacy compared with T-DM1 in breast cancer and has replaced T-DM1 in many treatment pathways.29 However, in advanced HER2-positive EMPD, the high efficacy observed in the present study indicates that T-DM1 remains a viable therapeutic option, and replacement by T-DXd appears unwarranted. This is further supported by data from metastatic breast cancer, in which T-DXd has been associated with a higher incidence of drug-related AEs than T-DM1, suggesting that T-DM1 may be a safer option in certain clinical contexts.29

Another critical issue is the unmet therapeutic need in patients with HER2-low EMPD, for whom evidence remains extremely limited. Among HER2-negative tumours, those with IHC 1+ or IHC 2+/ISH− are classified as HER2-low. This subgroup has gained attention because patients with this level of expression can still respond to HER2-targeted therapies in breast cancer, with T-DXd already in clinical use.30,31 In a retrospective analysis of 954 patients across 16 studies, including both metastatic and non-metastatic EMPD cases, HER2 positivity was observed in 25.6% of patients, while HER2-low expression accounted for 38.5%.32 This substantial proportion of HER2-low EMPD supports the need for future studies to investigate the efficacy of HER2-targeted ADCs in this subgroup.

Moreover, accumulating evidence suggests that somatic HER2 gene mutations represent a distinct, biologically relevant subgroup that may confer sensitivity to HER2-targeted therapies, even in the absence of HER2 gene amplification.33 The ERBB2 S310F mutation is one such example, with reports identifying it in ∼26.5%-40% of EMPD cases.34,35 This gene mutation is a common mutation in the extracellular domain of HER2, enhancing C-terminal tail phosphorylation and activating oncogenic HER2 downstream pathways independently of dimerisation.33,34 Clinically, the therapeutic relevance is supported by case reports in HER2-nonamplified breast cancer, demonstrating objective responses to trastuzumab-based regimens in patients harbouring the ERBB2 S310F mutation.36,37 This suggests that, in addition to the well-established HER2-low subgroup, there may be a further subgroup within HER2-negative EMPD, as determined by conventional IHC/ISH criteria, that could potentially benefit from HER2-targeted ADCs.

The results of the TEMONOS-2 trial should be interpreted cautiously due to some limitations. The small sample size, single-arm design, and inclusion of only Japanese patients may limit generalisability. In addition, as HER2 status in this study was defined solely by IHC and ISH, the findings cannot be extrapolated to patients identified as HER2-positive by next-generation sequencing. Further studies involving larger and more diverse populations are warranted to better define the clinical benefit of T-DM1.

Conclusions

The TEMENOS-2 trial provides the first prospective evidence supporting the efficacy and safety of a HER2-directed ADC in the treatment of HER2-positive advanced EMPD. Despite limitations inherent to the rarity of this disease, the favourable balance between antitumour activity and tolerability suggests that T-DM1 represents a promising therapeutic option for this predominantly older adult population with potentially aggressive disease. Systemic treatment options for HER2-low EMPD remain an unmet need and warrant further investigation.
