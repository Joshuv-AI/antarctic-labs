Final Technical Report
 Understanding and Predicting Pedestal Scenarios on NSTX-U
 David R. Hatch
 Institute for Fusion Studies, University of Texas at Austin

1 Executive Summary

This project investigated gyrokinetic instabilities in the NSTX pedestal, identified the major trans-
port mechanisms (some of them novel) in the NSTX pedestal, and developed and validated a
predictive modeling capability for pedestal transport in spherical tokamaks, with particular em-
phasis on NSTX discharges. The work combined first-principles gyrokinetic simulations, reduced
transport models, integrated transport calculations with ASTRA, and exploratory machine learn-
ing tools. The central outcome is a practical modeling capability for pedestal temperature profiles
based on reduced models informed and constrained by gyrokinetic physics [1, 2].
 A major scientific result of the project is the demonstration that NSTX pedestal temperature
profiles can be reproduced using a physically interpretable combination of electron-temperature-
gradient (ETG) turbulence, ion-scale KBM transport, and neoclassical transport [2]. This was
demonstrated for two very distinct NSTX discharges—one ELM-free lithiated discharge with a
broad pedestal, and another narrow pedestal ELMing discharge. In parallel, the project established
that electromagnetic effects associated with parallel magnetic fluctuations can fundamentally alter
ETG stability and transport in the pedestal-top region [1]. Together, these results provide both
new physics insight and a viable route toward predictive pedestal modeling in NSTX-U and future
spherical tokamaks.
 The project also explored data-driven approaches for surrogate modeling and regime identifi-
cation. These analyses indicate that some turbulence regimes, especially ETG and MTM, can be
identified cleanly in high-dimensional parameter space, while ion-scale regimes such as ITG, TEM,
and KBM overlap substantially. This activity is the focus of Joseph Schmidt’s dissertation research,
which is entering its final stages. Related publications will be finalized in the coming year prior to
Joseph Schmidt’s graduation.
 An early goal was to investigate and understand magnetic fluctuations in NSTX. However, this
task was adopted by other groups in collaboration with us. One result of this is the paper [3].
After discussions with the program manager we shifted to focus more on other turbulent transport
mechanisms [1] as well as predictive pedestal profile modeling [2]

2 Scientific Motivation

Accurate prediction of pedestal structure remains one of the central challenges in magnetic fusion
research. The pedestal sets the boundary conditions for the core plasma and is the main factor
determining global confinement and fusion performance. Pedestal structure is determined by a
coupled interplay between microturbulence, neoclassical transport, and macroscopic stability con-
straints, and these mechanisms can vary substantially across radius and/or from one discharge
regime to another.
 Gyrokinetic simulations provide a first-principles description of turbulent transport, but their
computational cost makes them impractical for routine predictive modeling, large parameter scans,
or optimization. The scientific motivation of this project was therefore to bridge the gap between
high-fidelity gyrokinetic physics and fast predictive capability by constructing reduced models that
preserve the essential transport mechanisms active in NSTX pedestals. A second motivation was
to test whether data-driven methods could help organize the high-dimensional parameter space
associated with pedestal turbulence and thereby inform expectations moving toward future devices
like NSTX-U or fusion pilot plants.

3 Modeling Framework

The modeling framework developed in this project combines gyrokinetic simulations with reduced
transport models embedded in the ASTRA integrated transport solver [2]. In the present imple-
mentation, ASTRA evolves the temperature profiles in time while holding density fixed, which
allows systematic assessment of the transport mechanisms required to reproduce the experimental
pedestal structure. The same framework is in principle capable of modeling particle transport and
density profile evolution. This is more challenging due to large uncertainties in the edge recycling
particle source and is left for future work. For thermal transport, the electron channel includes
ETG and neoclassical contributions, while the ion channel is primarily neoclassical. In addition,
ion-scale turbulent transport from KBM is required to reproduce the experimental profiles and is
active in both the electron and ion channels.
 For the electron-scale contribution, an algebraic reduced model for ETG transport was employed
and benchmarked against nonlinear gyrokinetic simulations. Under NSTX pedestal conditions, the
model tends to underpredict the electron heat flux by about a factor of two. However, because
pedestal transport is strongly stiff with respect to the temperature gradient, this discrepancy leads
to only modest differences in the predicted temperature profiles. This robustness is itself an im-
portant result: the pedestal self-adjusts toward near-marginal conditions, so profile prediction can
remain accurate even when the underlying reduced flux model is only approximately correct.
 To represent ion-scale transport, a quasi-linear surrogate based on linear gyrokinetic calcula-
tions was constructed and coupled to ASTRA [2]. The resulting model introduces only one free
parameter, calibrated to one discharge and then applied to others without further retuning. This
provides a stringent test of predictive capability. The combined framework successfully reproduces

4 Physics Results

4.1 Electromagnetic ETG Turbulence

One of the most important physics findings is the role of electromagnetic effects in ETG turbulence
at the top of the pedestal. Simulations that retain parallel magnetic fluctuations (δBk ) reveal a
strongly unstable ETG branch that is absent in simulations that neglect parallel magnetic fluctua-
tions [1]. As shown in Fig. 1, including δBk increases ETG-driven transport by orders of magnitude
in the pedestal-top region. This result is notable because, in many local gyrokinetic simulations,
parallel magnetic fluctuations have only a modest effect; here they qualitatively change the trans-
port outcome.

Figure 1: ETG transport is enhanced by orders of magnitude when parallel magnetic fluctuations
(δBk ) are included in the gyrokinetic simulations, revealing a novel electromagnetic branch of ETG
turbulence that is absent in purely electrostatic models.

 The radial location where this enhanced ETG transport is strongest coincides with the region
where the experimental pressure profile lies furthest below the KBM stability threshold inferred
from gyrokinetic analysis. As illustrated in Fig. 2, this correspondence suggests that the newly
identified electromagnetic ETG mechanism is the primary transport process limiting the pressure
gradient in this region, precisely where KBM does not appear to be the dominant constraint [1, 3].

Figure 2: The electromagnetic ETG transport is large precisely in the radial region where there
is a significant discrepancy between the experimental pressure profile and the KBM stability limit

 The comparison of narrow-pedestal and wide-pedestal NSTX discharges further demonstrates
the regime dependence of pedestal transport. In the narrow-pedestal case, strong electromagnetic
ETG turbulence is found near the pedestal top, whereas in the wide-pedestal case ETG transport
is weaker at the pedestal top [1]. This contrast highlights the need for transport models that can
adapt to changing scenarios rather than assuming a fixed ordering of dominant instabilities.

4.2 Predictive Modeling of Pedestal Profiles

The project results show that NSTX pedestal transport is governed by multiple interacting mech-
anisms rather than a single universal constraint. Neoclassical transport is dominant in the ion
channel across much of the pedestal, while ETG turbulence can make a large contribution to elec-
tron heat transport, especially in regions with weak density gradients and large electron temper-
ature gradients. Ion-scale modes, represented in the reduced framework through KBM/MHD-like
transport, are also necessary to reproduce the measured profiles [2].
 The effective diffusivities inferred from the reduced models coupled to ASTRA are shown in
Fig. 3. Ion neoclassical transport is substantial across the full pedestal in both discharges. ETG
contributes significantly near the separatrix and, for the narrow-pedestal case, also near the pedestal
top. KBM/ion-scale transport is required in both discharges, with varying relative importance in
the ion and electron channels. The full pedestal structure is reproduced only when these mechanisms

 Quantitative validation of the reduced-model framework is provided in Fig. 5, which compares
experimental electron and ion temperature profiles with the ASTRA predictions [2]. The agreement
is strong across the full pedestal region for both discharges and is achieved with only a single free
parameter. This is a significant outcome for spherical tokamak pedestal modeling: it demonstrates
that time-dependent, transport-based profile prediction can be carried out with a compact set of
reduced physics models while maintaining close agreement with experiment. To the best of our
knowledge this is the first time this has been achieved. More extensive validation throughout a
broader NSTX(-U) parameter space would be a logical next step given sufficient resources. Particle
transport and density profile evolution can, in principle, be modeled using the same framework.
Due to large uncertainties in the edge particle source, we leave this for future work.

5 Machine Learning and Data-Driven Analysis

In addition to the physics-based modeling effort, the project explored machine learning tools for
surrogate modeling and turbulence-regime identification. Neural-network models trained on large
gyrokinetic databases showed strong performance for tasks such as KBM stability prediction and
promising capability for growth-rate estimation. These results support the broader goal of embed-
ding fast, data-driven surrogates into integrated modeling, optimization, and control workflows.
 A complementary unsupervised analysis used dimensionality reduction and clustering on high-
dimensional fingerprints derived from pedestal parameters. ETG and MTM modes form relatively
pure and well-separated clusters, indicating that they occupy distinct regions of parameter space
that can be recognized reliably from the available features. By contrast, ion-scale modes such as
ITG, TEM, and KBM represent a continuum in parameter space as opposed to clearly separated
instability classes.

6 Conclusions

This project discovered novel new transport mechanisms in the NSTX pedestal and established
a physics-based framework for predictive pedestal transport modeling in spherical tokamaks. The
combined results show that NSTX pedestal structure can be understood through the joint action of
ETG turbulence, KBM/MHD-like ion-scale transport, and neoclassical transport [2]. The work also
demonstrated that these mechanisms can be embedded successfully in a reduced-model framework
coupled to ASTRA, enabling time-dependent reproduction of experimental temperature profiles
with minimal free calibration. The project also discovered novel electromagnetic effects, which can
be decisive for electron-scale turbulence in the NSTX pedestal top [2].
