Artificial intelligence for energy-efficient computation offloading in WPT-enabled industrial internet of things
Authors: Mohammed A Alhartomi, Adeb Salh, Saeed Alzahrani, Fares S Almehmadi, Ahmed Alzahmi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntegrating artificial intelligence (AI) applications in resource-constrained Internet of Things (IoT) systems with intelligent edge computing and Wireless Power Transfer (WPT) is essential for supporting real-time decision-making and sustainable Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) operations. AI-driven WPT significantly improves the efficiency of time-division multiplexing (TDM) by enabling precise coordination between data offloading and enhancing the overall sustainability and efficiency of the system when combined with intelligent edge computing. To address the stringent battery capacity constraints in WPT systems, a perturbation-based virtual energy queue is proposed to relax the strict energy limitations typically encountered. This mechanism eliminates the need for future system condition prediction, thereby enabling efficient and adaptive real-time scheduling decisions. Furthermore, Dinkelbach’s transformation is employed to reformulate the long-term Energy Efficiency (EE) optimization problem into a tractable drift-plus-penalty framework, effectively reducing latency and ensuring queue stability. To enhance queue stability and intelligent decision-making in online scheduling, this study proposes a hybrid Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL)–Lyapunov optimization framework that enables adaptive learning by dynamically adjusting the Central Pro-cessing Unit (CPU) frequency to minimize power consumption while satisfying latency constraints derived from the drift-plus-penalty bound. The hybrid DRL–Lyapunov achieves sustainability in the Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) operation by integrating actor–critic to support the agent learning to obtain an optimal policy in high-dimensional state spaces. Simulation results demonstrate that the proposed hybrid DRL–Lyapunov framework enhances EE by 15–20% compared to a fixed-power baseline, maintaining device battery levels within the optimal range of 55–60%. This approach effectively ensures energy balance, queue stability, and reduced variations in battery dynamics.Keywords: IIoT, CPU, DRL, WPT, Energy harvesting, Energy efficiencySubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

During the fourth industrial revolution (Industry 4.0), the manufacturing environment has been dramatically transformed through the adoption of edge computing and the Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT)1. It is based on this that Industry 5.0 focuses on the creation of a sustainable, intelligent, and human-centered interaction between machines and humans, thus improving on the present paradigm of the industry.

To facilitate the high-technology manufacturing processes like robotic coordination, automated quality control, predictive maintenance, and real-time optimization of the processes, the contemporary smart factories are gradually implementing the IIoT-based systems that come with sensors and in-built computing capabilities2. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) for computer vision, recurrent neural networks for time-series prediction, and transformer models for decision-making systems are examples of compute-intensive Artificial Intelligence (AI) models that are becoming more and more important in these applications3.

The CNNs for computer vision, recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks for time-series prediction, and transformer-based architectures, which have now expanded well beyond natural language processing to encompass vision (e.g., Vision Transformers ViT), audio-video understanding, and multimodal tasks, represent prominent examples of compute-intensive AI models increasingly deployed in IIoT environments3,4. The heterogeneous computational demands and hardware affinities of these models necessitate intelligent and adaptive task scheduling, particularly in resource-constrained IIoT settings.

However, there are significant obstacles to the implementation of AI-driven IIoT systems at the node of computing efficiency and energy sustainability. The deep learning models, in particular, place significant computational demands on AI inference tasks that surpass the processing power of IIoT devices with limited resources5.

For example, running the full YOLOv3 model (608×608 input resolution) on a Raspberry Pi 4 achieves only approximately 0.2 frames per second under CPU-only execution, equating to roughly 5 seconds per inference, making real-time industrial monitoring infeasible6. Under unoptimized or higher-resolution configurations, inference latency can extend significantly further, underscoring the computational mismatch between deep learning models and resource-constrained IIoT devices7.

Energy Consumption (EC) of high-performance computing devices, such as a Graphics Processing Unit (GPU) and Tensor Processing Unit (TPU), which are required for AI computation, is substantial; the average NVIDIA RTX GPU may draw up to 400 watts8. The IIoT devices work in dangerous or remote industrial settings where it is not feasible or feasible to change batteries regularly, which calls for sustainable energy solutions. A possible paradigm for overcoming computational constraints is Mobile Edge Computing (MEC), which transfers work from devices with limited resources to adjacent edge servers. Industry 5.0 focuses on the creation of a sustainable, intelligent, and human-centered interaction between machines and humans, thus improving on the present paradigm of the industry9. To facilitate the high-technology manufacturing processes like robotic coordination, automated quality control, predictive maintenance, and real-time optimization of the processes, contemporary smart factories are gradually implementing the IIoT-based systems that come with sensors and in built computing capabilities. Contrary to traditional battery-powered systems, Wireless Power Transfer (WPT) offers flexible device deployment in demanding industrial environments, minimizes carbon emissions by doing away with throwaway batteries, and permits continuous operation without operator intervention. WPT is becoming increasingly feasible for powering edge computer nodes due to recent improvements in transmission range and efficiency. When WPT and MEC are combined, however, complicated energy coupling effects are introduced, where decisions about offloading during one time slot limit the amount of energy available and the ability to perform tasks during succeeding slots6. Energy-efficient computation offloading has so far either addressed AI task scheduling without integrating sustainable Energy Harvesting (EH) techniques10 or generic workloads without taking AI-specific features into account11. Since AI workloads have distinct characteristics that are fundamentally different from traditional computation tasks using task heterogeneity in various AI models, CNNs for vision, Long Short-Term Memory (LSTMs) for sequences, and transformers for language, all of which have wildly disparate computational patterns and hardware affinities, this gap is especially important.

Hardware sensitivity plays a significant role in AI inference performance across heter-ogeneous edge devices. For parallelism-intensive workloads such as CNN-based image recognition under high-batch conditions, GPU-optimized implementations have been reported to achieve up to a 20× improvement in throughput over CPU execution on specific experimental benchmarks12. However, it is important to note that this ad-vantage is highly workload- and context-dependent; for sequential, sparsely parallel, or small-batch inference tasks, CPUs equipped with modern vector processing extensions may deliver competitive or even superior performance. This underscores the need for AI-aware, hardware-sensitive task scheduling in heterogeneous IIoT edge environments, as advocated in this work13.

Additionally, unlike atomic processes, AI inference can benefit from batch processing for increased throughput. AI models’ compressibility can be pruned, quantized, or partitioned to trade accuracy for computing efficiency14. Although much research has been done in the offloading of computation in MEC systems, most current studies use computation tasks as heterogeneous workloads with only data size and computation cycles as their characteristics. The fundamental variety of AI inference problems, which have varying computational intensities, hardware affinities, and latency sensitivities across various models, such as CNNs, LSTMs, and transformers, cannot be captured by these abstractions. These AI-related peculiarities can be neglected, which results in inefficient offloading and resource allocation choices, especially in industrial settings where real-time AI inference performance and energy efficiency are closely interconnected.

Recently, a research gap still exists since there is no single framework that can be used to maximize AI-aware scheduling of tasks, heterogeneous resource allocation, and WPT-based energy management synergistically within an integrated system. Current MEC offloading techniques tend to ignore the optimization potentials, hardware affinities, or computation structure of AI inference problems and are frequently based on a collection of general workload assumptions. Energy coupling, stochastic WPT energy arrivals, and bursty and heterogeneous AI workloads are also poorly recognized. Lastly, there is a high level of heterogeneity in the real world of industrial edge, not only in hardware platforms, CPUs, and AI accelerators but also in AI model design. Moreover, in cases where the concept of wireless power transfer is combined with the concept of computation offloading, there is a high degree of temporal energy coupling, where the amount of energy collected during one time slot directly limits the choice of computations and computation offloading during the next slot. The current body of research that exists simplifies the availability of energy either by assuming that the battery is static or simplifies this intertemporal dependence without directly modeling this dependency. Consequently, due to the stochastic and time-dependent characteristics of the WPT-enabled IIoT systems, their optimization models fail to deal with them effectively, resulting in unstable battery behavior or conservative usage of re-resources. This paper addresses these gaps by proposing an AI-enhanced energy-efficient computation offloading framework for WPT-powered IIoT systems (Fig. 1).

Intelligent MEC framework for AI-Drive IIoT.

System model

We consider a two-tier IIoT-MEC system consisting heterogeneous IIoT devices, denoted by the set , an MEC server equipped with AI acceleration capabilities, and a WPT co-located with the MEC server. The system operates in discrete time slots indexed by , where each time slot has a uniform duration seconds. This discrete-time framework enables practical implementation of online scheduling to capture dynamic channel conditions and stochastic task arrivals30. The MEC server is strategically positioned at the network edge to minimize latency and provide computational offloading services to resource-constrained IIoT devices. The wireless power transmitter enables sustainable operation by continuously broadcasting RF energy, which devices can harvest to reload their batteries. This co-location design reduces infrastructure complexity and enables efficient coordination between power transfer and computation offloading decisions, which is critical for energy-constrained IIoT environments where battery replacement is impractical.

Conclusion

This article introduces a hybrid DRL-Lyapunov framework for AI-enhanced energy-efficient computation offloading in WPT-enabled IIoT systems, jointly optimizing AI-based task scheduling and energy management. It integrates a virtual energy queue for soft battery constraints, Dinkelbach’s transformation for efficient Lyapunov-based optimization, and a hybrid DRL structure combining DQN and Lyapunov drift minimization for stable and adaptive performance. The proposed hybrid DRL-Lyapunov framework offers practical value for industrial deployments requiring sustainable operation of AI-powered edge devices in energy-constrained environments, with demonstrated improvements of 35% in EE and 28% in latency reduction as Industry 4.0 continues evolving toward increasingly intelligent and autonomous manufacturing systems. The hybrid DRL-Lyapunov framework demonstrates a 35% (0.85–0.95 bits/J) EE improvement and a 28% (80–100 ms) reduction in latency with constant task queues and battery levels between 40–70% capacity. The proposed approach can converge rapidly to 95% optimality in 300 slots and higher cumulative rewards than the other two proposed methods. The framework presents a novel performance standard by incorporating a hybrid of DRL-Lyapunov optimization of adaptive and stable control, a virtual energy line to overcome the energy causality constraint, and the integration of AI-aware scheduling. The future research should be expanded to multi-server heterogeneous edge computing for employing a variety of AI accelerators, GPUs, TPUs, and FPGAs to allow intelligent mapping between tasks and servers based on server load, EC, and computing capabilities.
