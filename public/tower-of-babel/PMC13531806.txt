Association between the TP63 and CIR1 polymorphisms and non-small cell lung cancer in Chinese population
Authors: Yingying Shen, Peiqing Zheng, Jiaxu Zheng, Jianzhong Gu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectivesLung cancer is a leading cause of cancer-related death globally, with non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) being the predominant form. Genetic factors significantly influence individual susceptibility. This study investigated the association of TP63 (rs7631358) and CIR1 (rs13009079) polymorphisms with NSCLC risk in a Chinese population.MethodsA case-control study was conducted with 500 NSCLC patients and 500 matched controls. Genotyping was performed using the SEQUENOM MassARRAY platform. Associations were evaluated using chi-square tests and logistic regression to calculate odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs).ResultsIn the pre-specified primary analysis with FDR correction, both TP63 rs7631358 (A/G + A/A vs. G/G: adjusted OR = 1.409, FDR P = 0.030) and CIR1 rs13009079 (T/T vs. C/T + C/C: adjusted OR = 1.390, FDR P = 0.030) were significantly associated with an increased risk of lung adenocarcinoma after adjustment for smoking status, gender, and age. Exploratory subgroup analyses suggested that the TP63 association was more pronounced among ever-smokers, although the gene–smoking interaction did not reach statistical significance.ConclusionsTP63 rs7631358 and CIR1 rs13009079 polymorphisms are significantly associated with the susceptibility to lung adenocarcinoma in the Chinese Han population after correction for multiple testing. These findings support a role for the TP63-mediated DNA damage response pathway and the CIR1-Notch signaling pathway in ADC pathogenesis. Replication in larger, independent cohorts is warranted to confirm these associations and to further evaluate their potential for clinical risk stratification.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s13019-026-04478-y.

Introduction

In most countries, primary lung cancer is a leading cause of death from cancer [1]. In China, lung cancer is also a major health problem and has been reported with high mortality rate for both men and women [2]. Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) is the most common histological subtype, accounting for approximately 85% of all lung cancer cases [3]. Geographic differences, ethnic background, tobacco, air pollution, radiation, occupational exposures, dietary, physical activity, and reproductive history have been identified as independent or contributing risk factors for lung cancer [4, 5]. However, not all people who were exposed to those risk factors develop lung cancer. It is biologically conceivable that host genetic susceptibility is a very important factor in the development of lung cancer, which contributes to the variation in individual cancer risk.

Currently, the association between single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) of several genes (e.g., LRP6, CHRNA9, COMT, and PLCH1) and NSCLC has been confirmed in the Han Chinese population [6–8]. Recently, a genome-wide association study in Korea reported that TP63 rs7631358G > A and CIR1 rs13009079T > C were significantly associated with lung cancer in the same direction as the discovery set [9]. Although Korea and China share some similarities, their living environments and dietary habits differ. In this study, we investigated the relationship between TP63 rs7631358 and CIR1 rs13009079 SNPs and NSCLC risk in the Han Chinese population, and also evaluated the impact of these SNPs on NSCLC susceptibility and clinicopathological characteristics. These results may also provide clues for preoperative screening of high-risk patients in thoracic surgery.

Materials and methods

Study population

This was a population-based case-control study including 500 patients with NSCLC and 500 age-matched unrelated healthy controls. All participants were Han Chinese individuals living in Zhejiang Province, China, and were recruited from Zhejiang Cancer Hospital between March 2011 and April 2012. Patients were eligible for enrollment if they had a histologically or cytologically confirmed diagnosis of primary NSCLC, had complete clinical and pathological records, and provided written informed consent. Patients were excluded if they had secondary or metastatic lung cancer, a previous history of other malignant tumors, severe dysfunction of the heart, liver, kidney, or hematologic system, or incomplete clinical and pathological data. Control subjects were individually matched to patients by age, were free of any malignant diseases and chronic respiratory diseases including chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, pulmonary fibrosis, and tuberculosis, and had no family history of inherited tumor syndromes. All controls provided written informed consent and had no history of cancer or severe systemic diseases. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Zhejiang Cancer Hospital, and all procedures were conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

SNPs selection and genotyping

TP63 rs7631358 and CIR1 rs13009079 were selected based on a previous study [9]. Genomic DNA was extracted from whole blood samples using the AxyPrep Blood Genomic DNA Miniprep Kit (Axygen Biosciences, Union City, CA). Genotyping was performed using the SEQUENOM MassARRAY matrix‑assisted laser desorption ionization‑time of flight mass spectrometry platform (Sequenom, San Diego, CA). PCR primers and single‑base extension primers were designed with Assay Designer software version 3.0 (Sequenom) and synthesized by Sangon Biotech (Shanghai, China).

The primer sequences were as follows:

rs7631358:

First primer: 5′‑ACGTTGGATGGGAGCCGACTGAGAGATTAA‑3′; Second primer: 5′‑ACGTTGGATGACACATGCACATGTACTTAC‑3′; Extension primer: 5′‑CATTTGAAATAATTTGTGCACTTCA‑3′.

rs13009079:

First primer: 5′‑ACGTTGGATGTCCTTAGCTTTCCCTACGAC‑3′; Second primer: 5′‑ACGTTGGATGAGCCACGTCCACTCAAAAAC‑3′; Extension primer: 5′‑GGAGGATTGCAGCTTGATGGCCC‑3′.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 13.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL). Deviation from Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium for each SNP was assessed by the χ² test, with P < 0.001 considered statistically significant. Differences in allele and genotype frequencies between cases and controls were evaluated using the χ² test. Associations between SNPs and NSCLC risk were estimated by odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) derived from multivariable unconditional logistic regression models, adjusted for smoking status (ever vs. never), gender, and age (Supplementary Table S1). Gender-stratified analyses were adjusted for smoking status and age. A two‑sided P value < 0.05 was regarded as statistically significant.

In addition, we predefined the analytical strategy for the two target SNPs according to previous research. Both SNPs examined in this study were selected a priori based on prior genome-wide association study findings [9], which identified TP63 rs7631358 and CIR1 rs13009079 as susceptibility loci primarily associated with lung adenocarcinoma. Accordingly, the primary analysis was pre-defined as the association of each SNP with adenocarcinoma (ADC) risk in the overall study population, using the genetic models consistent with prior reports (dominant model [A/G + A/A vs. G/G] for TP63 rs7631358; T/T vs. C/T + C/C for CIR1 rs13009079). The Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR) method was applied to correct for multiple testing in the two primary comparisons. All other analyses—including overall NSCLC and squamous cell carcinoma (SCC), gender-stratified subgroups, alternative genetic models, and gene–smoking interaction analyses—were designated as exploratory and are reported with nominal P values. Associations with nominal P < 0.05 in exploratory analyses are described as “suggestive.”

Results

Study population characteristics

A total of 500 patients (350 males and 150 females) and 500 healthy controls (259 males, 240 females, with gender information missing for one control subject) of Han Chinese origin were included. Among the NSCLC patients, 331 were diagnosed with ADC and 169 with SCC. Regarding smoking history, 280 male and 21 female patients were current or former smokers, compared to 189 male and 14 female controls. The gender distribution differed significantly between the case and control groups (P < 0.001; case group: 70.0% male; control group: 51.9% male); therefore, gender was included as a covariate in the logistic regression models, and gender-stratified analyses were also performed (Tables 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6). Association of allele frequencies with NSCLC risk

The associations between the allele frequencies of TP63 rs7631358 and CIR1 rs13009079, and the clinicopathological parameters as well as gender of NSCLC patients, are analyzed and presented in Tables 1, 2 and 3. The allele frequency of TP63 rs7631358 differed significantly between NSCLC patients and controls (OR = 1.237; 95%CI = 1.036–1.477; P = 0.0186), and also between ADC patients and controls (OR = 1.277; 95%CI = 1.047–1.556; P = 0.0156). These results indicate that the A allele of TP63 rs7631358 is associated with an increased risk of NSCLC and ADC development.

For CIR1 rs13009079, the allele frequency was significantly different between ADC patients and controls (OR = 1.349; 95%CI = 1.044–1.741; P = 0.0216), and also between male ADC patients and male controls (OR = 1.424; 95%CI = 1.004–2.020; P = 0.0466). Association of genotype frequencies with NSCLC risk

The associations between the genotype frequencies of TP63 rs7631358 and CIR1 rs13009079, and the clinicopathological parameters as well as gender of NSCLC patients, are analyzed and presented in Tables 4, 5 and 6. Compared with subjects carrying the G/G genotype, the frequency of the A/G + A/A genotypes in TP63 rs7631358 was statistically significantly higher in NSCLC patients (OR = 1.351; 95%CI = 1.033–1.766; P = 0.0278) and ADC patients (OR = 1.415; 95%CI = 1.044–1.917; P = 0.0249). Additionally, subjects carrying at least one A allele at TP63 rs7631358 showed a trend toward developing advanced NSCLC and ADC.

Logistic regression analysis revealed that the T/T homozygous genotype of CIR1 rs13009079 was associated with higher ORs for ADC (OR = 1.4; 95%CI = 1.040–1.884; P = 0.0261) and male ADC (OR = 1.516; 95%CI = 1.007–2.283; P = 0.0458). Furthermore, subjects carrying the homozygous T/T genotype at CIR1 rs13009079 also showed a trend toward developing advanced disease.

Primary analysis. After further adjustment for smoking status, the associations of the TP63 rs7631358 A/G + A/A genotypes with ADC risk (adjusted OR = 1.409, 95%CI = 1.039–1.912, P = 0.027, FDR-corrected P = 0.030). Similarly, the CIR1 rs13009079 T/T genotype was significantly associated with ADC risk compared with C/T + C/C (adjusted OR = 1.390, 95%CI = 1.032–1.871, nominal P = 0.030, FDR-corrected P = 0.030).

Exploratory analyses. In the overall NSCLC population, TP63 rs7631358 showed a suggestive association under the dominant model (adjusted OR = 1.310, 95%CI = 0.996–1.723, nominal P = 0.054), while CIR1 rs13009079 did not reach nominal significance (adjusted OR = 1.224, 95%CI = 0.938–1.598, nominal P = 0.137). No significant associations were observed for either SNP with SCC risk. In gender-stratified analyses, the CIR1 rs13009079 T/T genotype showed a nominally significant association with ADC risk in males (adjusted OR = 1.512, 95%CI = 1.003–2.280, nominal P = 0.048), but this did not survive correction for multiple testing. Full results for all genetic models, outcomes, and subgroups are presented in Supplementary Table S1.

Discussion

In the current study, we investigated the association between SNPs (TP63 rs7631358 and CIR1 rs13009079) and the risk of developing NSCLC. We found that the A allele of TP63 rs7631358 was significantly associated with increased risk of NSCLC and ADC, and subjects carrying A/G + A/A genotypes showed a trend toward to develop advanced NSCLC and ADC. Meanwhile, the T allele of CIR1 rs13009079 was associated with elevated ADC risk, especially in males, and the T/T homozygote conferred a higher risk of ADC. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to report the combined effects of TP63 rs7631358 and CIR1 rs13009079 polymorphisms on NSCLC susceptibility in the Han Chinese population. These findings suggest that these two genetic variants may serve as candidate susceptibility markers for NSCLC.

TP63, a member of the tumor suppressor TP53 gene family, plays a critical role in cell-cycle regulation and is essential for the normal development and differentiation of stratified epithelial tissues [10, 11]. The gene is predominantly expressed as two major isoforms: the transactivation isoform and the N-terminal-truncated isoform, and its biological role is more complex than that of a classic tumor suppressor [12]. TAp63 isoforms exhibit high transcriptional activity and are crucial for maintaining adult epidermal stem cells [13], as well as for cell proliferation, differentiation, and survival upon NF-κB activation [14]. Alterations in TP63 expression have been linked to the development and progression of various cancers, including lung cancer [15]. One TP63 isoform exerts oncogenic effects via dominant-negative inhibition of p53, and genomic gains of TP63 serve as potential biomarkers for pre-invasive lesions and early lung cancer [16]. TP63 maps to the 3q28 locus, a well-recognized lung adenocarcinoma susceptibility hotspot in East Asian populations identified by multiple genome-wide association studies [17]. As a central mediator of the DNA damage response, TP63 activates TP53 target genes under genotoxic stress to induce cell cycle arrest and apoptosis. The rs7631358 G > A intronic polymorphism may alter TP63 expression or mRNA splicing, thus impairing cellular DNA damage repair. Defective DNA repair is a key driver of lung adenocarcinoma, which explains the higher cancer risk associated with the A allele in our study. Cigarette smoke is a major source of exogenous genotoxic damage, which accounts for the stronger association of this variant among ever-smokers (Table 7). Carriers of this dysfunctional TP63 variant fail to mount an adequate defense against tobacco-induced genotoxic stress.

Recent studies have extensively investigated the association between specific TP63 SNPs, including rs10937405, rs17429138, rs9816619, rs4396880, and rs4488809, and lung cancer risk [18, 19]. A large-scale genome-wide association study in East Asian populations further confirmed that TP63 polymorphisms are closely related to the risk of lung adenocarcinoma [17], which is consistent with our findings. Moreover, TP63 has been shown to regulate the transcriptional activity of downstream target genes involved in cell proliferation and apoptosis, and its aberrant expression is closely associated with the occurrence and development of NSCLC [20–22]. Consistent with this evidence, our results support the role of TP63 as a susceptibility gene in NSCLC pathogenesis. Specifically, we found that the A allele of TP63 rs7631358 was associated with an elevated risk of NSCLC (OR = 1.237; 95%CI = 1.036–1.477; P = 0.0186) and ADC (OR = 1.277; 95%CI = 1.047–1.556; P = 0.0156). Compared with subjects carrying the G/G genotype, those with the A/G or A/A genotypes of TP63 rs7631358 showed a significantly higher frequency in both NSCLC (OR = 1.351; 95%CI = 1.033–1.766; P = 0.0278) and ADC (OR = 1.415; 95%CI = 1.044–1.917; P = 0.0249), indicating that this polymorphism contributes primarily to ADC risk.

Emerging evidence indicates that TP63 polymorphisms can interact with smoking to modify NSCLC risk [23], implicating gene-environment interactions in the underlying mechanism. These polymorphisms cannot directly guide surgical approach selection or predict surgical complications, but can serve as an auxiliary molecular reference for preoperative susceptibility assessment in patients preparing for lung cancer surgery. Importantly, these associations were robust to adjustment for smoking status, indicating that the observed genetic effects were not confounded by smoking behavior. Nevertheless, although the TP63 rs7631358 association appeared more pronounced among ever-smokers, the formal interaction tests were not statistically significant, suggesting that current data are insufficient to confirm a multiplicative gene-smoking interaction. Larger studies with detailed smoking quantity and duration data are warranted to clarify potential gene-environment interactions.

CIR1 interacts with C promoter binding factor 1 (CBF1) to function as a transcriptional corepressor [24]. As a member of the CBF-1, Su(H), Lag-1 family [25], CBF1 regulates gene transcription and acts as a key mediator of the Notch signaling pathway [26]. This pathway governs cell differentiation, proliferation and apoptosis [27], and its aberrant activation drives the proliferation, invasion and metastasis of NSCLC, and correlates with advanced tumor stage, cancer stem cell properties and poor clinical outcomes [28–33]. Mechanistically, CIR1 is an essential component of the RBPJ-associated corepressor complex containing histone deacetylases [24, 26]. When Notch ligands are absent, this complex maintains the silencing of Notch target genes; upon pathway activation, the corepressor is displaced by a coactivator complex, thereby inducing the expression of downstream HES and HEY family genes. Existing meta-analyses have verified that high Notch1 expression is linked to lymph node metastasis, advanced TNM stage and poor overall survival in NSCLC patients [29, 33]. The rs13009079 T > C polymorphism may alter CIR1 expression or protein function, breaking the dynamic balance of Notch signaling. Persistent abnormal Notch activity can strengthen tumor cell survival and resistance to apoptosis. Collectively, these mechanistic interpretations support our finding that the CIR1 T/T genotype is associated with elevated susceptibility to lung adenocarcinoma, indicating CIR1 as a promising susceptibility gene for NSCLC.

In the present study, CIR1 rs13009079 was associated with an increased risk of ADC, particularly in male subjects. Specifically, the T allele of CIR1 rs13009079 was linked to an increased risk for both ADC (OR = 1.349; 95%CI = 1.044–1.741; P = 0.0216) and male ADC (OR = 1.516; 95%CI = 1.007–2.283; P = 0.0458). Individuals with the T/T homozygous genotype exhibited a 1.4-fold increased risk of ADC and a 1.516-fold increased risk of male ADC, compared to those carrying the C/C or C/T genotypes. Our findings are not consistent with those reported by Kang HG et al. [9]. This discrepancy may be due to distinct genetic backgrounds and differing living environments between the Chinese population and other studied populations [5, 34]. It is well established that the distribution of genetic polymorphisms varies significantly across ethnic groups, and the same polymorphism may differentially influence disease risk among populations [4, 35, 36]. Additionally, variations in environmental exposure levels between studies could also contribute to inconsistent results [37]. Given the gender-specific correlation between CIR1 rs13009079 and lung adenocarcinoma risk, thoracic surgeons may pay extra attention to this genetic locus during preoperative evaluation of male patients with suspected lung adenocarcinoma, so as to realize more detailed preoperative risk assessment.

From a clinical perspective, the TP63 rs7631358 and CIR1 rs13009079 polymorphisms may have potential value as genetic biomarkers for risk stratification in NSCLC. The identification of high-risk genotypes could facilitate the early detection of NSCLC in high-risk populations, particularly among smokers carrying the TP63 A allele. Since the Notch signaling pathway is a validated therapeutic target for NSCLC, gamma-secretase inhibitors and related Notch inhibitors are currently undergoing clinical trials [29, 30, 33]. Genotyping of CIR1 is therefore expected to guide the selection of candidates suitable for Notch-targeted therapy. Accumulated clinical data also reveal that NSCLC patients carrying the TP63 GA or AA genotypes have poorer overall survival than GG carriers, especially among never-smoking females [19], indicating that this polymorphism also possesses prognostic implications.

An important methodological consideration is the issue of multiple comparisons. In this study, a total of 52 statistical tests were performed across two SNPs, three genetic models, three histological outcomes, and three population subgroups. To balance the risk of type I error against the loss of statistical power, we adopted a pre-specified primary/exploratory analytical framework. Our two primary hypotheses — the associations of TP63 rs7631358 (dominant model) and CIR1 rs13009079 (T/T genotype) with ADC risk — were defined a priori based on the original GWAS findings by Kang et al. [9], and both survived FDR correction (P_FDR = 0.030). In contrast, none of the exploratory analyses retained significance after FDR correction across all 52 tests (minimum P_FDR = 0.339; Supplementary Table S1). We therefore emphasize that the exploratory findings, including gender- and histology-specific associations, should be interpreted with caution and require independent validation. Nevertheless, the consistent direction of effect across multiple genetic models and the biological plausibility of the implicated pathways lend support to the primary findings.

There are some limitations in this study that should be acknowledged. First, this is a single-center case-control study with a relatively limited sample size, which may lead to potential selection bias. Notably, the gender distribution differed significantly between the case and control groups (case group: 70.0% male; control group: 51.9% male). To address this imbalance, we adjusted for gender in the logistic regression analyses and performed gender-stratified analyses; however, residual confounding cannot be entirely excluded. Second, only two SNPs were analyzed. We performed stratified analyses and interaction tests for gene-smoking relationships, but interactions with other environmental factors including air pollution and diet were not explored in the present study. Third, the present study is a hospital-based case-control study designed to evaluate genetic susceptibility; survival follow-up data were not collected and therefore a prognostic analysis could not be performed. Future prospective cohort studies incorporating survival outcomes are needed to validate the prognostic significance of these polymorphisms and to explore their utility in clinical decision-making. Fourth, the functional mechanism of TP63 rs7631358 and CIR1 rs13009079 polymorphisms was not verified by molecular experiments. Besides, this study only analyzed the correlation between gene polymorphisms and NSCLC risk, and did not explore their relationships with surgical complications and postoperative recovery. Further validation based on thoracic surgery cohort data is still required for clinical application. Fifth, population stratification may exist even though all subjects were Han Chinese from Zhejiang Province.

In future research, a multi-center, large-sample prospective cohort study should be conducted to verify our results. Meanwhile, more SNPs in TP63, CIR1, and other related genes should be included to analyze gene-gene and gene-environment interactions. Furthermore, cell and animal experiments are needed to explore the specific molecular mechanism of these polymorphisms in the occurrence and development of NSCLC.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study confirmed that TP63 rs7631358 and CIR1 rs13009079 polymorphisms are associated with increased risk of NSCLC, especially lung adenocarcinoma in Chinese Han population. These findings provide new genetic evidence for NSCLC susceptibility, and have certain reference value for preoperative screening and risk assessment in thoracic surgery.
