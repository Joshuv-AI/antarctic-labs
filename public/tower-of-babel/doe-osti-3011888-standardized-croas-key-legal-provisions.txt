Key Legal Provisions

 Methodology (Registry and Protocol) and MRV

• The contract templates reviewed provide opportunity for the
  contracting parties to define the applicable standard, carbon registry,
  and protocol to govern the transaction.
• Approaches vary whether the buyer must approve the methodology
  (defined by a term such as “Applicable Standard” or “Carbon
  Standard”).
• General seller requirements to, for example, “do all things reasonably
  necessary” for validation in accordance with the standard.
• While pathway and location dependent, opportunity exists for more
  robust measurement, reporting, and verification (MRV) definition.
                                                                       NREL | 4

  Volume and Shortfalls

• Contracting for a certain amount of CDR credits on an annual
  basis at a fixed price.
• Variation: Seller option to identify and change the
  project(s) from which CDR credits can be generated.
• Shortfall: Proration based on total CDR credits generated
  relative to amount contracted for; Seller to pay buyer
  replacement cost of comparable credits; failure to deliver a
  specified minimum quantity can be an event of default.

                                                                 NREL | 5

  Reversal and Leakage

• One template requires seller to maintain a reversal security in
  the form of a “recourse pool” for the duration of the durability
  period. The amount of credits in the recourse pool is
  determined by the applicable carbon standard.
• A template provides for a verification contractor to monitor the
  project during the durability period for reversals and leakage,
  with different remedies available to buyer whether such
  reversal or leakage is avoidable or unavoidable.

                                                                NREL | 6

 Project Description and Operation

• Clear definitions of what the project is (e.g., a description of
  the technology), a project timeline, and what constitutes
  milestones such as “commencement date” and “commercial
  operation date.”
• If not yet complete, the seller provides periodic progress
  updates.
• One template included a seller covenant to operate the project
  in accordance with Prudent Industry Practices.
• Seller obligation to develop project documents in accordance
  with the standard/protocol requirements.
                                                                 NREL | 7

  Delivery & CDR Credit Title

• Include a clear identification of when title transfer occurs.
• Delivery defined to occur, for example, upon transfer to registry
  account of buyer or retirement to order of buyer.
• Restrictions on “double counting” CDR credits.

                                                                 NREL | 8

 Default

• Events of Default appear to be typical and similar across
  templates: e.g., failure to pay, failure to achieve commercial
  operation, and failure to perform a material obligation.
• Approaches differ with respect to delivery shortfalls. For
  example, one template only considers delivery shortfall to be
  an event of default only in the case of fraud, gross negligence,
  or willful misconduct. Another includes a shortfall carveout,
  i.e., shortfall is not considered a breach of a material obligation.

                                                                    NREL | 9

 Termination

• Termination right upon breach of material provision, failure to
  pay, bankruptcy, events of default, and certain defined no-fault
  termination events (e.g., force majeure event, defined in some
  instances to include change of law).
• One template provides a buyer right to terminate as a remedy
  for MFN, ROFO, and failure to deliver breach, and upon change
  of control to a restricted person (as defined).
• Mechanism for determining termination payments owed.

                                                                NREL | 10

 Right of First Offer

• Some CROA templates provide a buyer a right of first offer or
  right of first refusal, while others do not.
• For those templates that include a ROFO, items such as (i) the
  floor for a sale to a third party upon refusal, and (ii) the scope
  of what constitutes a new carbon removal credit subject to the
  ROFO, can vary.

                                                                   NREL | 11

  Representations and Warranties

• Typical for transactions, e.g., each party validly existing and
  duly incorporated, parties have obtained the requisite
  authorizations, entering into the agreement will not cause a
  breach of organizational documents, no litigation etc, licenses
  obtained.
• Variations include whether any reps & certs are limited by a
  knowledge qualifier, MFN statement (if applicable), specific
  reps & certs regarding registry accounts.

                                                                    NREL | 12

 Limitation of Liability and Indemnification

• Consistent waiver of incidental, special, consequential
  damages.
• Limitations of liabilities set at either a set amount (e.g.,
  purchase price x 1 year CDR credits), or a sliding scale based on
  when the breach occurs.
• One template includes a seller indemnification of buyer for
  claims relating to ownership, financing or operation of the
  project, negligence or willful misconduct, environmental
  impacts, or personal injury or property damage related to the
  project.
                                                                 NREL | 13

 Dispute Resolution

• Approaches vary whether the template provides for alternative
  dispute resolution, good faith negotiation, mediation, or
  arbitration.

                                                             NREL | 14

 Assignment

• Assignment typically requires consent of the non-assigning
  party.
• Assignment to an affiliate does not require consent.
• One template included a buyer right terminate upon seller
  assignment.

                                                               NREL | 15

 Insurance

• Two templates included a placeholder where to identify
  insurances required to be maintained, without providing any
  examples. There is an opportunity to specify the types of
  insurance the parties should consider.

                                                                NREL | 16

 Taxes

• Taxes are the responsibility of the party having a legal
  obligation to pay such taxes.
• Two templates include a reimbursement mechanism if a party
  pays the other party’s tax obligations.
• Opportunity exists to develop tax credit concepts.
