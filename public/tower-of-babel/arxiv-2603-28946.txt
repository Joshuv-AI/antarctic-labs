From Categorized Neural Architectures to Subexponential Proof Theory
Carlos Ramírez Ovalle ORCID:0000-0002-4254-5610

Abstract
. We study a resource-sensitive fragment of the problem of extracting a logical discipline from a class of neural architectures by passing through categorization. The starting point is not a pre-existing logic but a category of zone-labelled parametrised blocks together with a disciplined record of which forms of copying, discarding, and zone coercion are architecturally licensed. From this categorized architecture we read off a subexponential signature and then define a tensorial sequent calculus whose structural rules are indexed by the extracted zones. The paper proves three kinds of results. First, the resulting architectural category is symmetric monoidal. Second, the extracted proof system admits cut elimination. Third, derivations are sound with respect to the licensed categorical diagrams generated by the architectural discipline. The outcome is a theorem-bearing core of the architecture-to-category-to-logic programme: subexponential structure is not postulated in advance but read from categorical data encoding differentiated memory and context behaviour.

1.
Introduction

The use of category theory in machine learning has progressed well beyond analogy. Compositional accounts of supervised learning and backpropagation show that parametrised learning systems admit categorical semantics in which composition and update can be studied functorially
[
5
]
. More recently, categorical deep learning has been proposed as a broad algebraic language for architectural composition, reparametrisation, and weight sharing
[
6
]
. On the other hand, categorical logic studies how structural features of categories give rise to doctrines, internal languages, and proof systems
[
8
,
9
]
. These two lines suggest a natural question: can one begin from a suitably organized family of neural architectures, pass to a categorical description of their resource behaviour, and then extract an appropriate logic from that categorical structure?

The present article develops a first theorem-bearing answer in a resource-sensitive setting. The emphasis is important. Our aim is not to begin with a fully formed logic and then search for a semantics. Instead, we start with a zone-labelled architectural formalism. Some forms of information are reusable, some discardable, some linear, and some coercible from one zone to another. These permissions are encoded categorically. Only after the categorical organization is fixed do we read off a subexponential signature and define the corresponding proof theory.

This order of construction can be displayed schematically as

\text{architecture family}\longrightarrow\text{categorized architecture}\longrightarrow\text{subexponential signature}\longrightarrow\text{proof theory}.

The final step is then to show that the extracted proof system is sound in the original categorized architecture. In this sense the category plays two roles: it is first an abstraction of architectural structure and only afterwards a semantics validating the logic extracted from that structure.

For broader background on categorical semantics of linear logic, including the passage
from linear structure to categorical models and the treatment of exponentials, see

[
13
,
2
,
7
,
10
]
.

The resulting logic is not intended to describe an entire transformer or a complete contemporary learning stack. That would require a richer treatment of nonlinearities, internal homs, and likely a linear–nonlinear adjunction in the sense of Benton
[
4
]
. The goal here is more focused. We isolate the part of the logical discipline already forced by differentiated structural behaviour. This leads naturally to a tensorial fragment with zone-indexed weakening and contraction, that is, to a subexponential proof theory in which structural permissions depend on zones
[
11
,
15
]
. The proof-theoretic stance is therefore deliberately modest but mathematically crisp.

The main novelty of the paper is not the mere use of subexponentials. Subexponential proof theory and semantics are by now well established, and recent work has pushed their categorical analysis significantly further
[
12
]
. What is new here is the direction of travel: the subexponential data are not fixed beforehand as logical syntax, but extracted from a categorized resource discipline motivated by architectures with differentiated memory and context. The key theorem is therefore an extraction-and-soundness result rather than a purely semantic one.

The contributions of the article are the following.

•

We define a category
\mathbf{Arch}_{Z}
of zone-labelled parametrised architectural blocks and prove that it is symmetric monoidal.

•

We introduce the notion of a
coherent zone discipline
on
\mathbf{Arch}_{Z}
, consisting of licensed zone coercions, discards, and diagonals.

•

From such a disciplined category we extract a subexponential signature
\Sigma_{\mathsf{Lic}}
and an associated tensorial sequent calculus
\mathsf{TZ}_{\Sigma_{\mathsf{Lic}}}
.

•

We prove cut elimination for
\mathsf{TZ}_{\Sigma_{\mathsf{Lic}}}
.

•

We prove soundness of
\mathsf{TZ}_{\Sigma_{\mathsf{Lic}}}
with respect to the class of licensed diagrams generated by the categorized architecture.

The article should therefore be read as a foundational first step in the programme

\text{neural architecture}\to\text{category}\to\text{logic},

with particular attention to resource-sensitive behaviour. The missing connective is linear implication. We explain below why its absence does not undercut the present contribution: the current theorems already identify the structural fragment of the logic that is forced by the architecture, and that fragment is the one most directly tied to differentiated resource usage.

At a high level, the main theorem-bearing content of the paper can be summarized as follows:
from a coherently disciplined category of zone-labelled architectural blocks one can
canonically reconstruct a subexponential signature, define the associated tensorial
proof theory, and interpret every derivation back into the licensed categorical diagrams.

Accordingly, the present article should not be read as a logic of all neural architectures,
nor as a full logic of transformers, but as a theorem-bearing identification of the structural
resource fragment already forced by categorized architectural permissions.

The individual results of Sections 2–4 may thus be read as the categorical, syntactic,
and semantic components of a single extraction theorem.

Section 4 closes with explicit examples, including a two-block neural architecture showing
concretely how differentiated input roles induce the extracted substructural discipline.

2.
Categorized architectures

2.1.
Typed resource contexts and the architectural category

We now introduce the categorical object that mediates between architecture and logic.
The purpose of this subsection is to extract, from a resource-sensitive architectural description,
a category whose objects are zone-labelled typed contexts and whose morphisms are parametrised computational blocks.
This construction is the first step in the chain

\text{architecture}\;\longrightarrow\;\text{categorisation}\;\longrightarrow\;\text{subexponential discipline}\;\longrightarrow\;\text{proof theory}.

At this stage, only the compositional structure of contexts and blocks is relevant.
Accordingly, we work over a category with finite products and make no further assumptions.

Let
\mathbf{Ctx}
be a category with finite products, and let
Z
be a fixed set of zone labels.

{defi}

[Typed resource context]
A
typed resource context
is a finite list

\Gamma=((z_{1},A_{1}),\dots,(z_{n},A_{n})),

where each
z_{i}\in Z
and each
A_{i}\in\Ob(\mathbf{Ctx})
. Its
carrier
is the object

[\![\Gamma]\!]:=A_{1}\times\cdots\times A_{n},

with the convention that the carrier of the empty list is the terminal object
1
of
\mathbf{Ctx}
.
The empty context is denoted by
\emptyset
.

If

\Gamma=((z_{1},A_{1}),\dots,(z_{n},A_{n}))\quad\text{and}\quad\Delta=((w_{1},B_{1}),\dots,(w_{m},B_{m})),

their
tensor concatenation
is the context

\Gamma\otimes\Delta:=((z_{1},A_{1}),\dots,(z_{n},A_{n}),(w_{1},B_{1}),\dots,(w_{m},B_{m})).

The notation
\Gamma\otimes\Delta
is justified by the fact that concatenation of contexts is implemented on carriers by the product operation in
\mathbf{Ctx}
.

Lemma 1
.

For all typed resource contexts
\Gamma
and
\Delta
, there is a canonical isomorphism

\mu_{\Gamma,\Delta}\colon[\![\Gamma\otimes\Delta]\!]\xrightarrow{\cong}[\![\Gamma]\!]\times[\![\Delta]\!].

Proof 2.1
.

Write

\Gamma=((z_{1},A_{1}),\dots,(z_{n},A_{n})),\qquad\Delta=((w_{1},B_{1}),\dots,(w_{m},B_{m})).

By definition,

[\![\Gamma\otimes\Delta]\!]=A_{1}\times\cdots\times A_{n}\times B_{1}\times\cdots\times B_{m},

whereas

[\![\Gamma]\!]\times[\![\Delta]\!]=(A_{1}\times\cdots\times A_{n})\times(B_{1}\times\cdots\times B_{m}).

The universal property of finite products yields a unique morphism

\mu_{\Gamma,\Delta}\colon[\![\Gamma\otimes\Delta]\!]\to[\![\Gamma]\!]\times[\![\Delta]\!]

whose first component is the tuple of projections onto
A_{1},\dots,A_{n}
and whose second component is the tuple of projections onto
B_{1},\dots,B_{m}
.

Similarly, there is a unique morphism

\nu_{\Gamma,\Delta}\colon[\![\Gamma]\!]\times[\![\Delta]\!]\to[\![\Gamma\otimes\Delta]\!]

whose components are the evident projections onto the factors

A_{1},\dots,A_{n},B_{1},\dots,B_{m}.

The composites
\nu_{\Gamma,\Delta}\circ\mu_{\Gamma,\Delta}
and
\mu_{\Gamma,\Delta}\circ\nu_{\Gamma,\Delta}
are identities because they have the same projections as the corresponding identity morphisms. Hence
\mu_{\Gamma,\Delta}
is an isomorphism, with inverse
\nu_{\Gamma,\Delta}
.

For
z\in Z
and
A\in\Ob(\mathbf{Ctx})
, we write

\langle z,A\rangle

for the one-variable context
((z,A))
.

{defi}

[Parametrised resource block]
Let
\Gamma
and
\Delta
be typed resource contexts. A
parametrised resource block

(P,\varphi)\colon\Gamma\to\Delta

consists of an object
P\in\Ob(\mathbf{Ctx})
together with a morphism

\varphi\colon P\times[\![\Gamma]\!]\longrightarrow[\![\Delta]\!]

in
\mathbf{Ctx}
.

The parameter object is part of the presentation of a block, but not part of its essential behaviour.
Two parametrised presentations should therefore be identified whenever they differ only by an isomorphic reparametrisation.

{defi}

[Isomorphism of parametrised blocks]
Let
(P,\varphi)
and
(P^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})
be parametrised resource blocks
\Gamma\to\Delta
.
An
isomorphism of parametrised blocks

u\colon(P,\varphi)\xrightarrow{\cong}(P^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})

is an isomorphism
u\colon P\to P^{\prime}
in
\mathbf{Ctx}
such that

\varphi^{\prime}\circ(u\times\id_{[\![\Gamma]\!]})=\varphi.

We write

(P,\varphi)\sim(P^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})

when such an isomorphism exists.

Lemma 2
.

For fixed contexts
\Gamma
and
\Delta
, the relation
\sim
on parametrised resource blocks
\Gamma\to\Delta
is an equivalence relation.

Proof 2.2
.

Reflexivity is immediate, since

\varphi\circ(\id_{P}\times\id_{[\![\Gamma]\!]})=\varphi.

For symmetry, assume that

u\colon(P,\varphi)\xrightarrow{\cong}(P^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})

is an isomorphism of parametrised blocks. Then

\varphi^{\prime}\circ(u\times\id_{[\![\Gamma]\!]})=\varphi.

Composing on the right with
u^{-1}\times\id_{[\![\Gamma]\!]}
gives

\varphi\circ(u^{-1}\times\id_{[\![\Gamma]\!]})=\varphi^{\prime},

so

u^{-1}\colon(P^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})\xrightarrow{\cong}(P,\varphi)

is again an isomorphism of parametrised blocks.

For transitivity, suppose

u\colon(P,\varphi)\xrightarrow{\cong}(P^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime}),\qquad v\colon(P^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})\xrightarrow{\cong}(P^{\prime\prime},\varphi^{\prime\prime}).

Then

\varphi^{\prime}\circ(u\times\id_{[\![\Gamma]\!]})=\varphi,\qquad\varphi^{\prime\prime}\circ(v\times\id_{[\![\Gamma]\!]})=\varphi^{\prime}.

Substituting the second equation into the first yields

\varphi^{\prime\prime}\circ(v\times\id_{[\![\Gamma]\!]})\circ(u\times\id_{[\![\Gamma]\!]})=\varphi.

Since the product is functorial,

(v\times\id_{[\![\Gamma]\!]})\circ(u\times\id_{[\![\Gamma]\!]})=(v\circ u)\times\id_{[\![\Gamma]\!]},

hence

\varphi^{\prime\prime}\circ((v\circ u)\times\id_{[\![\Gamma]\!]})=\varphi.

Therefore

v\circ u\colon(P,\varphi)\xrightarrow{\cong}(P^{\prime\prime},\varphi^{\prime\prime}),

and
\sim
is transitive.

{defi}

[The architectural category
\mathbf{Arch}_{Z}
]
The objects of
\mathbf{Arch}_{Z}
are typed resource contexts.

A morphism

[\![P,\varphi]\!]\colon\Gamma\to\Delta

is a
\sim
-equivalence class of parametrised resource blocks
(P,\varphi)\colon\Gamma\to\Delta
.

Given morphisms

[\![P,\varphi]\!]\colon\Gamma\to\Delta,\qquad[\![Q,\psi]\!]\colon\Delta\to\Theta,

their composite is defined by

[\![Q,\psi]\!]\circ[\![P,\varphi]\!]:=[\![Q\times P,\,\psi\circ(\id_{Q}\times\varphi)\circ\alpha^{-1}_{Q,P,\Gamma}]\!],

where

\alpha_{Q,P,\Gamma}\colon Q\times(P\times[\![\Gamma]\!])\xrightarrow{\cong}(Q\times P)\times[\![\Gamma]\!]

is the canonical associativity isomorphism of the finite product.

The identity on
\Gamma
is the class of

(1,\pi_{2})\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma,

where
\pi_{2}\colon 1\times[\![\Gamma]\!]\to[\![\Gamma]\!]
is the second projection.

This composition is the evident sequential execution of parametrised blocks.
The first block consumes the input context, and its output is fed to the second block; the composite parameter object simply remembers both parameter choices.

Proposition 3
.

The data of
subsection 2.1
define a category
\mathbf{Arch}_{Z}
.

Proof 2.3
.

We first prove that composition is well defined on equivalence classes.

Let

(P,\varphi)\sim(P^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime}),\qquad(Q,\psi)\sim(Q^{\prime},\psi^{\prime}).

Choose isomorphisms

u\colon P\xrightarrow{\cong}P^{\prime},\qquad v\colon Q\xrightarrow{\cong}Q^{\prime}

such that

\varphi^{\prime}\circ(u\times\id_{[\![\Gamma]\!]})=\varphi,\qquad\psi^{\prime}\circ(v\times\id_{[\![\Delta]\!]})=\psi.

We must show that the blocks

(Q\times P,\,\psi\circ(\id_{Q}\times\varphi)\circ\alpha^{-1}_{Q,P,\Gamma})

and

(Q^{\prime}\times P^{\prime},\,\psi^{\prime}\circ(\id_{Q^{\prime}}\times\varphi^{\prime})\circ\alpha^{-1}_{Q^{\prime},P^{\prime},\Gamma})

are equivalent. The candidate isomorphism is

v\times u\colon Q\times P\xrightarrow{\cong}Q^{\prime}\times P^{\prime}.

Using functoriality of products and naturality of
\alpha^{-1}
, we compute

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime}\circ(\id_{Q^{\prime}}\times\varphi^{\prime})\circ\alpha^{-1}_{Q^{\prime},P^{\prime},\Gamma}\circ((v\times u)\times\id_{[\![\Gamma]\!]})

\displaystyle\qquad=\psi^{\prime}\circ(\id_{Q^{\prime}}\times\varphi^{\prime})\circ(v\times(u\times\id_{[\![\Gamma]\!]}))\circ\alpha^{-1}_{Q,P,\Gamma}

\displaystyle\qquad=\psi^{\prime}\circ(v\times\id_{[\![\Delta]\!]})\circ(\id_{Q}\times\varphi)\circ\alpha^{-1}_{Q,P,\Gamma}

\displaystyle\qquad=\psi\circ(\id_{Q}\times\varphi)\circ\alpha^{-1}_{Q,P,\Gamma}.

Hence composition is well defined.

We next verify associativity. Let

[\![P,\varphi]\!]\colon\Gamma\to\Delta,\qquad[\![Q,\psi]\!]\colon\Delta\to\Theta,\qquad[\![R,\chi]\!]\colon\Theta\to\Lambda.

The two triple composites are represented, respectively, by

((R\times Q)\times P,\;\chi\circ(\id_{R}\times\psi)\circ\alpha^{-1}_{R,Q,\Theta}\circ(\id_{R\times Q}\times\varphi)\circ\alpha^{-1}_{R\times Q,P,\Gamma})

and

(R\times(Q\times P),\;\chi\circ(\id_{R}\times(\psi\circ(\id_{Q}\times\varphi)\circ\alpha^{-1}_{Q,P,\Gamma}))\circ\alpha^{-1}_{R,Q\times P,\Gamma}).

These representatives are equivalent via the canonical associativity isomorphism

\alpha_{R,Q,P}\colon R\times(Q\times P)\xrightarrow{\cong}(R\times Q)\times P.

Indeed, both composites describe the same iterated map obtained by reassociating

R\times Q\times P\times[\![\Gamma]\!]

so as to apply first
\varphi
, then
\psi
, then
\chi
. Since canonical maps between finite products are uniquely determined by their projections, the two resulting morphisms coincide.

Finally, we check the identity laws. Let

[\![P,\varphi]\!]\colon\Gamma\to\Delta.

The composite with the identity on the left is represented by

(1\times P,\;\pi_{2}\circ(\id_{1}\times\varphi)\circ\alpha^{-1}_{1,P,\Gamma}),

which is equivalent to
(P,\varphi)
via the canonical isomorphism

\lambda_{P}\colon 1\times P\xrightarrow{\cong}P.

Similarly, the composite with the identity on the right is represented by

(P\times 1,\;\varphi\circ(\id_{P}\times\pi_{2})\circ\alpha^{-1}_{P,1,\Gamma}),

which is equivalent to
(P,\varphi)
via the canonical isomorphism

\rho_{P}\colon P\times 1\xrightarrow{\cong}P.

Therefore the stated identities are indeed units for composition, and
\mathbf{Arch}_{Z}
is a category.

We next place blocks side by side. On contexts this is just concatenation; on morphisms it is parallel composition, obtained by keeping both parameter objects and letting each block see only its own part of the input carrier.

{defi}

[Tensor product on
\mathbf{Arch}_{Z}
]
On objects, the tensor product is context concatenation:

\Gamma\otimes\Delta.

On morphisms, let

[\![P,\varphi]\!]\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma^{\prime},\qquad[\![Q,\psi]\!]\colon\Delta\to\Delta^{\prime}.

Define

[\![P,\varphi]\!]\otimes[\![Q,\psi]\!]:=[\![P\times Q,\varphi\otimes\psi]\!],

where

\varphi\otimes\psi\colon(P\times Q)\times[\![\Gamma\otimes\Delta]\!]\longrightarrow[\![\Gamma^{\prime}\otimes\Delta^{\prime}]\!]

is the composite

(P\times Q)\times[\![\Gamma\otimes\Delta]\!]\xrightarrow{\id\times\mu_{\Gamma,\Delta}}(P\times Q)\times([\![\Gamma]\!]\times[\![\Delta]\!])\xrightarrow{\sigma}(P\times[\![\Gamma]\!])\times(Q\times[\![\Delta]\!])\xrightarrow{\varphi\times\psi}[\![\Gamma^{\prime}]\!]\times[\![\Delta^{\prime}]\!]\xrightarrow{\mu^{-1}_{\Gamma^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime}}}[\![\Gamma^{\prime}\otimes\Delta^{\prime}]\!].

Here
\sigma
denotes the canonical reassociation and symmetry isomorphism of finite products.
The monoidal unit is the empty context
\emptyset
.

Lemma 4
.

The tensor product of
subsection 2.1
is well defined on equivalence classes of parametrised resource blocks.

Proof 2.4
.

Assume

(P,\varphi)\sim(P^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime}),\qquad(Q,\psi)\sim(Q^{\prime},\psi^{\prime}).

Choose isomorphisms

u\colon P\xrightarrow{\cong}P^{\prime},\qquad v\colon Q\xrightarrow{\cong}Q^{\prime}

such that

\varphi^{\prime}\circ(u\times\id_{[\![\Gamma]\!]})=\varphi,\qquad\psi^{\prime}\circ(v\times\id_{[\![\Delta]\!]})=\psi.

We show that

(P\times Q,\varphi\otimes\psi)\sim(P^{\prime}\times Q^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime}\otimes\psi^{\prime}).

The relevant isomorphism is
u\times v\colon P\times Q\to P^{\prime}\times Q^{\prime}
.

Expanding the definition of
\varphi\otimes\psi
and
\varphi^{\prime}\otimes\psi^{\prime}
, and using the naturality of the canonical product isomorphisms involved, it suffices to verify commutativity of

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(P\times[\![\Gamma]\!])\times(Q\times[\![\Delta]\!])}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces[\![\Gamma^{\prime}]\!]\times[\![\Delta^{\prime}]\!]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(P^{\prime}\times[\![\Gamma]\!])\times(Q^{\prime}\times[\![\Delta]\!])}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces[\![\Gamma^{\prime}]\!]\times[\![\Delta^{\prime}]\!].}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi\times\psi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(u\times\id)\times(v\times\id)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi^{\prime}\times\psi^{\prime}}

But this square commutes because

\varphi^{\prime}\circ(u\times\id_{[\![\Gamma]\!]})=\varphi\qquad\text{and}\qquad\psi^{\prime}\circ(v\times\id_{[\![\Delta]\!]})=\psi.

Hence

(\varphi^{\prime}\otimes\psi^{\prime})\circ((u\times v)\times\id)=\varphi\otimes\psi,

as required.

Proposition 5
.

The category
\mathbf{Arch}_{Z}
, equipped with the tensor product of
subsection 2.1
and unit object
\emptyset
, is a symmetric monoidal category.

Proof 2.5
.

The associator, left and right unitors, and symmetry of
\mathbf{Arch}_{Z}
are induced by the corresponding canonical isomorphisms of finite products in
\mathbf{Ctx}
, transported along the carrier isomorphisms of
1
. More precisely, each of these structural morphisms is represented by a parametrised block with parameter object
1
and underlying map the corresponding canonical product isomorphism.

It remains to verify the coherence axioms. Since all structural morphisms arise from canonical reassociations, symmetries, and unit isomorphisms of finite products, the pentagon, triangle, and hexagon identities in
\mathbf{Arch}_{Z}
reduce to the usual coherence identities for monoidal categories. These hold by Mac Lane’s coherence theorem; see
[
9
, Chapter VII]
. Therefore
\mathbf{Arch}_{Z}
is a symmetric monoidal category.

2.2.
Coherent zone disciplines and extracted subexponential data

The category
\mathbf{Arch}_{Z}
encodes typed contexts and parametrised blocks, together with their sequential and parallel composition.
This, however, is still not enough to recover a logical discipline.
Indeed, because the ambient category
\mathbf{Ctx}
has finite products, terminal and diagonal maps are always available at the level of carriers.
If all such maps were admitted indiscriminately as structural, then the intended resource sensitivity would disappear.
What must therefore be specified is not the mere existence of structural maps in
\mathbf{Ctx}
, but rather which among them are to be regarded as
architecturally licensed
.

The role of a coherent zone discipline is precisely to supply this information.
It enriches the architectural category with three kinds of data:
a preorder of zone refinement, coercions between refined zones, and designated structural families expressing where discard and duplication are allowed.
This is the point at which the passage

\text{architecture}\;\longrightarrow\;\text{categorisation}\;\longrightarrow\;\text{subexponential structure}

becomes explicit.

We begin with a piece of notation that allows us to speak precisely about naturality in the carrier object.

{defi}

[The one-zone embedding]
For each zone
z\in Z
, define a mapping

J_{z}\colon\mathbf{Ctx}\longrightarrow\mathbf{Arch}_{Z}

on objects by

J_{z}(A):=\langle z,A\rangle,

and on morphisms by

J_{z}(f):=[\![1,f\circ\pi_{2}]\!]\colon\langle z,A\rangle\longrightarrow\langle z,B\rangle

for every morphism
f\colon A\to B
in
\mathbf{Ctx}
, where

\pi_{2}\colon 1\times A\to A

is the second projection.

Lemma 6
.

For each
z\in Z
, the assignment
J_{z}
of
subsection 2.2
defines a functor

J_{z}\colon\mathbf{Ctx}\to\mathbf{Arch}_{Z}.

Proof 2.6
.

Let
A\in\Ob(\mathbf{Ctx})
. Then

J_{z}(\id_{A})=[\![1,\id_{A}\circ\pi_{2}]\!].

Since

\id_{A}\circ\pi_{2}=\pi_{2}\colon 1\times A\to A,

this is exactly the identity morphism on
\langle z,A\rangle
in
\mathbf{Arch}_{Z}
, by
subsection 2.1
. Hence

J_{z}(\id_{A})=\id_{J_{z}(A)}.

Now let

f\colon A\to B,\qquad g\colon B\to C

be morphisms in
\mathbf{Ctx}
. By definition,

J_{z}(f)=[\![1,f\circ\pi_{2}]\!],\qquad J_{z}(g)=[\![1,g\circ\pi_{2}]\!].

Their composite in
\mathbf{Arch}_{Z}
is represented by

(1\times 1,\;(g\circ\pi_{2})\circ(\id_{1}\times(f\circ\pi_{2}))\circ\alpha^{-1}),

where

\alpha\colon 1\times(1\times A)\xrightarrow{\cong}(1\times 1)\times A

is the canonical associativity isomorphism.
Using the canonical identifications

1\times 1\cong 1\qquad\text{and}\qquad 1\times A\cong A,

the displayed morphism is canonically equal to

g\circ f\circ\pi_{2}\colon 1\times A\to C.

Therefore

J_{z}(g)\circ J_{z}(f)=[\![1,(g\circ f)\circ\pi_{2}]\!]=J_{z}(g\circ f).

Thus
J_{z}
preserves identities and composition, and is therefore a functor.

We may now formulate the structural data that refine the bare category
\mathbf{Arch}_{Z}
.

in

and

, the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces J_{z}(A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces J_{z^{\prime}}(A)}

(a)

in

(b)

Remark 7
.

The coherence conditions say that structural behaviour is stable under refinement of zones.
If a zone
z
admits discard or duplication and
z\preceq z^{\prime}
, then the corresponding structural operation at
z^{\prime}
is compatible with the coercion from
z
to
z^{\prime}
.
This is the categorical precursor of the upward closure conditions in a subexponential signature.

The preceding remark suggests that the structural information carried by a coherent discipline should be read not merely from the names of the designated subsets
\mathcal{W}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
and
\mathcal{C}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
, but from the actual family of structural morphisms licensed by the architecture. We therefore reconstruct the weakening and contraction zones directly from
subsection 2.2
.

{defi}
[Licensed family]
Let
\mathsf{Lic}
be a coherent zone discipline on
\mathbf{Arch}_{Z}
. The licensed family
\Str(\mathsf{Lic})
is the collection of all structural morphisms permitted by
\mathsf{Lic}
.

{defi}

[Structural classes reconstructed from the licensed family]
Let
\mathsf{Lic}
be a coherent zone discipline on
\mathbf{Arch}_{Z}
. Define

\mathcal{W}^{\disc}_{\mathsf{Lic}}:=\left\{z\in Z\;\middle|\;\forall A\in\Ob(\mathbf{Ctx}),\ \Str(\mathsf{Lic})\text{ contains a licensed discard }\omega_{z,A}\colon J_{z}(A)\to\emptyset\right\},

and

\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{diag}}_{\mathsf{Lic}}:=\left\{z\in Z\;\middle|\;\forall A\in\Ob(\mathbf{Ctx}),\ \Str(\mathsf{Lic})\text{ contains a licensed diagonal }\delta_{z,A}\colon J_{z}(A)\to J_{z}(A)\otimes J_{z}(A)\right\}.

{defi}

[Extracted subexponential signature]
Let
(\mathbf{Arch}_{Z},\mathsf{Lic})
be a coherently disciplined architectural category. Its
extracted subexponential signature
is the quadruple

\Sigma_{\mathsf{Lic}}:=(Z,\preceq,\mathcal{W}^{\disc}_{\mathsf{Lic}},\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{diag}}_{\mathsf{Lic}}).

The next theorem shows that the reconstructed classes coincide exactly with the originally designated weakening and contraction zones. In this sense, the extracted signature is not an arbitrary repackaging of the discipline, but a faithful reading of the structural permissions carried by the licensed family.

Theorem 8

(Faithful reconstruction of the structural signature)
.

Let
(\mathbf{Arch}_{Z},\mathsf{Lic})
be a coherently disciplined architectural category. Then

\mathcal{W}^{\disc}_{\mathsf{Lic}}=\mathcal{W}_{\mathsf{Lic}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{diag}}_{\mathsf{Lic}}=\mathcal{C}_{\mathsf{Lic}}.

Consequently,

\Sigma_{\mathsf{Lic}}=(Z,\preceq,\mathcal{W}_{\mathsf{Lic}},\mathcal{C}_{\mathsf{Lic}}),

and in particular
\Sigma_{\mathsf{Lic}}
is a subexponential signature.

Proof 2.7
.

We first prove that

\mathcal{W}^{\disc}_{\mathsf{Lic}}=\mathcal{W}_{\mathsf{Lic}}.

Assume that

z\in\mathcal{W}^{\disc}_{\mathsf{Lic}}.

By
subsection 2.2
, this means that for every object
A\in\Ob(\mathbf{Ctx})
, the licensed structural family
\Str(\mathsf{Lic})
contains a morphism

\omega_{z,A}\colon J_{z}(A)\to\emptyset.

By
subsection 2.2
, the family
\Str(\mathsf{Lic})
is the union

\Str(\mathsf{Lic})=\left\{\omega_{u,B}\;\middle|\;u\in\mathcal{W}_{\mathsf{Lic}},\ B\in\Ob(\mathbf{Ctx})\right\}\;\cup\;\left\{\delta_{u,B}\;\middle|\;u\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathsf{Lic}},\ B\in\Ob(\mathbf{Ctx})\right\}.

Since the morphism

\omega_{z,A}\colon J_{z}(A)\to\emptyset

has codomain
\emptyset
, it cannot be one of the diagonals

\delta_{u,B}\colon J_{u}(B)\to J_{u}(B)\otimes J_{u}(B),

because those morphisms have codomain of the form

J_{u}(B)\otimes J_{u}(B),

which is not equal to
\emptyset
. Therefore

\omega_{z,A}\in\left\{\omega_{u,B}\;\middle|\;u\in\mathcal{W}_{\mathsf{Lic}},\ B\in\Ob(\mathbf{Ctx})\right\}.

Hence there exist
u\in\mathcal{W}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
and
B\in\Ob(\mathbf{Ctx})
such that

\omega_{z,A}=\omega_{u,B}

as morphisms in
\mathbf{Arch}_{Z}
.

Equality of morphisms in a category implies equality of their domains and codomains. Thus

J_{z}(A)=J_{u}(B)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\emptyset=\emptyset.

By
subsection 2.2
, one has

J_{z}(A)=\langle z,A\rangle,\qquad J_{u}(B)=\langle u,B\rangle.

Therefore

\langle z,A\rangle=\langle u,B\rangle.

Since one-variable contexts are equal if and only if both their zone labels and their carrier objects are equal, it follows that

z=u\qquad\text{and}\qquad A=B.

In particular,

z=u\in\mathcal{W}_{\mathsf{Lic}}.

This proves that

\mathcal{W}^{\disc}_{\mathsf{Lic}}\subseteq\mathcal{W}_{\mathsf{Lic}}.

Conversely, assume that

z\in\mathcal{W}_{\mathsf{Lic}}.

We must prove that

z\in\mathcal{W}^{\disc}_{\mathsf{Lic}}.

Let
A\in\Ob(\mathbf{Ctx})
be arbitrary. By
subsection 2.2
, since
z\in\mathcal{W}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
, the data of the discipline include a licensed discard

\omega_{z,A}\colon J_{z}(A)\to\emptyset.

By
subsection 2.2
, every such licensed discard belongs to
\Str(\mathsf{Lic})
. Since
A
was arbitrary, we conclude that for every
A\in\Ob(\mathbf{Ctx})
, the family
\Str(\mathsf{Lic})
contains a licensed discard

\omega_{z,A}\colon J_{z}(A)\to\emptyset.

Therefore, by
subsection 2.2
,

z\in\mathcal{W}^{\disc}_{\mathsf{Lic}}.

Hence

\mathcal{W}_{\mathsf{Lic}}\subseteq\mathcal{W}^{\disc}_{\mathsf{Lic}}.

We have proved both inclusions, so

\mathcal{W}^{\disc}_{\mathsf{Lic}}=\mathcal{W}_{\mathsf{Lic}}.

We now prove that

\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{diag}}_{\mathsf{Lic}}=\mathcal{C}_{\mathsf{Lic}}.

Assume that

z\in\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{diag}}_{\mathsf{Lic}}.

By
subsection 2.2
, this means that for every object
A\in\Ob(\mathbf{Ctx})
, the family
\Str(\mathsf{Lic})
contains a licensed diagonal

\delta_{z,A}\colon J_{z}(A)\to J_{z}(A)\otimes J_{z}(A).

Again by
subsection 2.2
,

\Str(\mathsf{Lic})=\left\{\omega_{u,B}\;\middle|\;u\in\mathcal{W}_{\mathsf{Lic}},\ B\in\Ob(\mathbf{Ctx})\right\}\;\cup\;\left\{\delta_{u,B}\;\middle|\;u\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathsf{Lic}},\ B\in\Ob(\mathbf{Ctx})\right\}.

Since the morphism

\delta_{z,A}\colon J_{z}(A)\to J_{z}(A)\otimes J_{z}(A)

has codomain of the form

J_{z}(A)\otimes J_{z}(A),

it cannot be one of the discards

\omega_{u,B}\colon J_{u}(B)\to\emptyset,

because those morphisms have codomain
\emptyset
. Therefore

\delta_{z,A}\in\left\{\delta_{u,B}\;\middle|\;u\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathsf{Lic}},\ B\in\Ob(\mathbf{Ctx})\right\}.

Hence there exist
u\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
and
B\in\Ob(\mathbf{Ctx})
such that

\delta_{z,A}=\delta_{u,B}

as morphisms in
\mathbf{Arch}_{Z}
.

Equality of morphisms implies equality of domains and codomains. Therefore

J_{z}(A)=J_{u}(B)\qquad\text{and}\qquad J_{z}(A)\otimes J_{z}(A)=J_{u}(B)\otimes J_{u}(B).

From the equality of domains we already obtain

J_{z}(A)=J_{u}(B).

Using again
subsection 2.2
,

J_{z}(A)=\langle z,A\rangle,\qquad J_{u}(B)=\langle u,B\rangle,

hence

\langle z,A\rangle=\langle u,B\rangle.

Therefore

z=u\qquad\text{and}\qquad A=B.

In particular,

z=u\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathsf{Lic}}.

This proves that

\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{diag}}_{\mathsf{Lic}}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\mathsf{Lic}}.

Conversely, assume that

z\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathsf{Lic}}.

We must prove that

z\in\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{diag}}_{\mathsf{Lic}}.

Let
A\in\Ob(\mathbf{Ctx})
be arbitrary. By
subsection 2.2
, since
z\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
, the data of the discipline include a licensed diagonal

\delta_{z,A}\colon J_{z}(A)\to J_{z}(A)\otimes J_{z}(A).

By
subsection 2.2
, every such licensed diagonal belongs to
\Str(\mathsf{Lic})
. Since
A
was arbitrary, it follows that for every
A\in\Ob(\mathbf{Ctx})
, the family
\Str(\mathsf{Lic})
contains a licensed diagonal

\delta_{z,A}\colon J_{z}(A)\to J_{z}(A)\otimes J_{z}(A).

Hence, by
subsection 2.2
,

z\in\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{diag}}_{\mathsf{Lic}}.

Therefore

\mathcal{C}_{\mathsf{Lic}}\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{diag}}_{\mathsf{Lic}}.

We have proved both inclusions, so

\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{diag}}_{\mathsf{Lic}}=\mathcal{C}_{\mathsf{Lic}}.

It remains to justify the final claim. Since
\preceq
is a preorder on
Z
by
subsection 2.2
, and since

\mathcal{W}^{\disc}_{\mathsf{Lic}}=\mathcal{W}_{\mathsf{Lic}},\qquad\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{diag}}_{\mathsf{Lic}}=\mathcal{C}_{\mathsf{Lic}},

the sets
\mathcal{W}^{\disc}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{diag}}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
are upward closed because
\mathcal{W}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
and
\mathcal{C}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
are upward closed by
subsection 2.2
. Therefore the quadruple

\Sigma_{\mathsf{Lic}}=(Z,\preceq,\mathcal{W}^{\disc}_{\mathsf{Lic}},\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{diag}}_{\mathsf{Lic}})

is a subexponential signature. Using the equalities already established, this is the same as

\Sigma_{\mathsf{Lic}}=(Z,\preceq,\mathcal{W}_{\mathsf{Lic}},\mathcal{C}_{\mathsf{Lic}}),

as claimed.

Thus the signature is not imposed on the architecture from outside: it is reconstructed from
the structurally licensed families already present in the disciplined category.

Corollary 9
.

Let
(\mathbf{Arch}_{Z},\mathsf{Lic})
be a coherently disciplined architectural category, and let

\Sigma_{\mathsf{Lic}}=(Z,\preceq,\mathcal{W}^{\disc}_{\mathsf{Lic}},\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{diag}}_{\mathsf{Lic}})

be the extracted signature of
subsection 2.2
. Then the weakening and contraction zones of

\Sigma_{\mathsf{Lic}}

are exactly the zones in which the discipline
\mathsf{Lic}
designates discard and diagonal families.

Proof 2.8
.

By
subsection 2.2
, the weakening and contraction zones of
\Sigma_{\mathsf{Lic}}
are

\mathcal{W}^{\disc}_{\mathsf{Lic}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{diag}}_{\mathsf{Lic}},

respectively. By
8
,

\mathcal{W}^{\disc}_{\mathsf{Lic}}=\mathcal{W}_{\mathsf{Lic}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{diag}}_{\mathsf{Lic}}=\mathcal{C}_{\mathsf{Lic}}.

By definition, the sets
\mathcal{W}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
and
\mathcal{C}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
are exactly the zones in which the discipline

\mathsf{Lic}
supplies licensed discard and diagonal families. Therefore the extracted signature retains
exactly the structural zone information carried by the discipline.

The previous results complete the passage from the architectural category to a subexponential signature.
What remains is to show how this extracted signature governs the proof theory.
That step belongs to the next section, where the tensorial calculus
\mathsf{TZ}_{\Sigma}
is defined and the soundness of its structural rules is established with respect to the disciplined architecture.

3.
The extracted proof theory

The previous section showed how a coherent zone discipline on the architectural category determines a subexponential signature

\Sigma_{\mathsf{Lic}}=(Z,\preceq,\mathcal{W}^{\disc}_{\mathsf{Lic}},\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{diag}}_{\mathsf{Lic}}).

By
8
, one may equivalently write

\Sigma_{\mathsf{Lic}}=(Z,\preceq,\mathcal{W}_{\mathsf{Lic}},\mathcal{C}_{\mathsf{Lic}}).

We now pass from this extracted signature to a sequent calculus.
This is the syntactic step in the chain

\text{architecture}\;\longrightarrow\;\text{categorisation}\;\longrightarrow\;\text{subexponential signature}\;\longrightarrow\;\text{proof theory}.

The guiding principle is that the proof system should remember exactly the structural permissions extracted from the architecture and nothing more.

The fragment considered here is intentionally modest.
It contains atoms, the tensor unit, tensor product, and the zone-dependent structural rules of weakening and contraction.
This is the part of the logic directly forced by the architectural data developed so far.
In particular, no implication is introduced at this stage, since its correct treatment would require additional categorical structure not yet available in the present framework.The tensorial system considered here should be read as a deliberately restricted,
zone-indexed fragment of intuitionistic linear proof theory; for a classical term-calculus
reference point on intuitionistic linear logic, see
[
3
]
.

{defi}

[Subexponential signature]
A
subexponential signature
is a quadruple

\Sigma=(Z,\preceq,\mathcal{W},\mathcal{C}),

where
Z
is a set of zones,
\preceq
is a preorder on
Z
, and
\mathcal{W},\mathcal{C}\subseteq Z
are upward-closed subsets.
The elements of
\mathcal{W}
are the zones in which weakening is allowed, and the elements of
\mathcal{C}
are the zones in which contraction is allowed.

Although the signature of interest for this article is the extracted signature
\Sigma_{\mathsf{Lic}}
of
subsection 2.2
, the proof system is most conveniently stated for an arbitrary signature.

{defi}

[Tensorial formulas and zone contexts]
Fix a set
\mathsf{At}
of atomic formulas.
The set of
tensorial formulas
is inductively generated by

A::=p\mid I\mid A\otimes B\qquad(p\in\mathsf{At}).

A
zone context
is a finite multiset of expressions

z\!:\!A,

where
z\in Z
and
A
is a tensorial formula.
If
\Gamma
and
\Delta
are zone contexts, their concatenation is written
\Gamma,\Delta
.
Contexts are taken modulo permutation.

The preorder
\preceq
is retained because it is part of the subexponential data extracted from the architecture and will be relevant for richer fragments.
In the tensorial system defined below, however, only the sets
\mathcal{W}
and
\mathcal{C}
occur explicitly.

{defi}

[The extracted zone calculus
\mathsf{TZ}_{\Sigma}
]
The calculus
\mathsf{TZ}_{\Sigma}
has judgments of the form

\Gamma\vdash A,

where
\Gamma
is a zone context and
A
is a tensorial formula.
Its inference rules are the following:

\frac{}{z\!:\!A\vdash A}\;\mathrm{ax}\qquad\frac{}{\emptyset\vdash I}\;I\mathrm{R}\qquad\frac{\Gamma\vdash A\qquad\Delta\vdash B}{\Gamma,\Delta\vdash A\otimes B}\;\otimes\mathrm{R}

\frac{\Gamma,z\!:\!A,z\!:\!B\vdash C}{\Gamma,z\!:\!A\otimes B\vdash C}\;\otimes\mathrm{L}\qquad\frac{\Gamma\vdash C}{\Gamma,z\!:\!I\vdash C}\;I\mathrm{L}

\frac{\Gamma\vdash A\qquad\Delta,z\!:\!A\vdash B}{\Gamma,\Delta\vdash B}\;\mathrm{cut}

For each zone
z\in\mathcal{W}
, there is a weakening rule

\frac{\Gamma\vdash B}{\Gamma,z\!:\!A\vdash B}\;\mathrm{W}_{z},

and for each zone
z\in\mathcal{C}
, there is a contraction rule

\frac{\Gamma,z\!:\!A,z\!:\!A\vdash B}{\Gamma,z\!:\!A\vdash B}\;\mathrm{C}_{z}.

Remark 10
.

The choice of fragment is deliberate.
It isolates the part of the logic that is directly forced by the structural architectural behaviour encoded in the signature: tensorial aggregation, the tensor unit, and zone-dependent weakening and contraction.
A satisfactory extension to linear implication would require additional semantic structure, such as an internal hom compatible with the resource discipline or a linear–nonlinear adjunction; see
[
4
,
12
]
.
The present article does not assume such structure.

The next proposition makes precise that the extracted proof theory depends only on the extracted signature, and not on further accidental features of the disciplined architecture.

Theorem 11

(Invariance of the extracted logic under structural equivalence)
.

Let
\mathsf{Lic}
and
\mathsf{Lic}^{\prime}
be coherent zone disciplines on the same architectural category
\mathbf{Arch}_{Z}
.
Assume that they induce the same preorder on
Z
and have the same licensed structural family:

\preceq_{\mathsf{Lic}}=\preceq_{\mathsf{Lic}^{\prime}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Str(\mathsf{Lic})=\Str(\mathsf{Lic}^{\prime}).

Then

\mathcal{W}^{\disc}_{\mathsf{Lic}}=\mathcal{W}^{\disc}_{\mathsf{Lic}^{\prime}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{diag}}_{\mathsf{Lic}}=\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{diag}}_{\mathsf{Lic}^{\prime}}.

Consequently,

\Sigma_{\mathsf{Lic}}=\Sigma_{\mathsf{Lic}^{\prime}},

and therefore

\mathsf{TZ}_{\Sigma_{\mathsf{Lic}}}=\mathsf{TZ}_{\Sigma_{\mathsf{Lic}^{\prime}}}.

Proof 3.1
.

We first prove that

\mathcal{W}^{\disc}_{\mathsf{Lic}}=\mathcal{W}^{\disc}_{\mathsf{Lic}^{\prime}}.

Assume that

z\in\mathcal{W}^{\disc}_{\mathsf{Lic}}.

By
subsection 2.2
, this means that for every object
A\in\Ob(\mathbf{Ctx})
, the family

\Str(\mathsf{Lic})

contains a licensed discard

\omega_{z,A}\colon J_{z}(A)\to\emptyset.

Since

\Str(\mathsf{Lic})=\Str(\mathsf{Lic}^{\prime}),

the same morphism belongs to
\Str(\mathsf{Lic}^{\prime})
. As this holds for every
A\in\Ob(\mathbf{Ctx})
, the definition of
\mathcal{W}^{\disc}_{\mathsf{Lic}^{\prime}}
yields

z\in\mathcal{W}^{\disc}_{\mathsf{Lic}^{\prime}}.

Hence

\mathcal{W}^{\disc}_{\mathsf{Lic}}\subseteq\mathcal{W}^{\disc}_{\mathsf{Lic}^{\prime}}.

Conversely, assume that

z\in\mathcal{W}^{\disc}_{\mathsf{Lic}^{\prime}}.

Then for every
A\in\Ob(\mathbf{Ctx})
, the family

\Str(\mathsf{Lic}^{\prime})

contains a licensed discard

\omega_{z,A}\colon J_{z}(A)\to\emptyset.

Again using

\Str(\mathsf{Lic}^{\prime})=\Str(\mathsf{Lic}),

we conclude that for every
A\in\Ob(\mathbf{Ctx})
, the family
\Str(\mathsf{Lic})
contains such a licensed discard. Therefore

z\in\mathcal{W}^{\disc}_{\mathsf{Lic}}.

Thus

\mathcal{W}^{\disc}_{\mathsf{Lic}^{\prime}}\subseteq\mathcal{W}^{\disc}_{\mathsf{Lic}}.

We have proved both inclusions, so

\mathcal{W}^{\disc}_{\mathsf{Lic}}=\mathcal{W}^{\disc}_{\mathsf{Lic}^{\prime}}.

We next prove that

\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{diag}}_{\mathsf{Lic}}=\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{diag}}_{\mathsf{Lic}^{\prime}}.

Assume that

z\in\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{diag}}_{\mathsf{Lic}}.

By
subsection 2.2
, this means that for every object
A\in\Ob(\mathbf{Ctx})
, the family

\Str(\mathsf{Lic})

contains a licensed diagonal

\delta_{z,A}\colon J_{z}(A)\to J_{z}(A)\otimes J_{z}(A).

Since

\Str(\mathsf{Lic})=\Str(\mathsf{Lic}^{\prime}),

the same morphism belongs to
\Str(\mathsf{Lic}^{\prime})
. As this holds for every
A\in\Ob(\mathbf{Ctx})
, we obtain

z\in\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{diag}}_{\mathsf{Lic}^{\prime}}.

Hence

\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{diag}}_{\mathsf{Lic}}\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{diag}}_{\mathsf{Lic}^{\prime}}.

Conversely, assume that

z\in\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{diag}}_{\mathsf{Lic}^{\prime}}.

Then for every
A\in\Ob(\mathbf{Ctx})
, the family

\Str(\mathsf{Lic}^{\prime})

contains a licensed diagonal

\delta_{z,A}\colon J_{z}(A)\to J_{z}(A)\otimes J_{z}(A).

Using again

\Str(\mathsf{Lic}^{\prime})=\Str(\mathsf{Lic}),

we conclude that for every
A\in\Ob(\mathbf{Ctx})
, the family
\Str(\mathsf{Lic})
contains such a licensed diagonal. Therefore

z\in\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{diag}}_{\mathsf{Lic}}.

Thus

\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{diag}}_{\mathsf{Lic}^{\prime}}\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{diag}}_{\mathsf{Lic}}.

We have proved both inclusions, and therefore

\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{diag}}_{\mathsf{Lic}}=\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{diag}}_{\mathsf{Lic}^{\prime}}.

By
subsection 2.2
, one has

\Sigma_{\mathsf{Lic}}=(Z,\preceq_{\mathsf{Lic}},\mathcal{W}^{\disc}_{\mathsf{Lic}},\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{diag}}_{\mathsf{Lic}})

and

\Sigma_{\mathsf{Lic}^{\prime}}=(Z,\preceq_{\mathsf{Lic}^{\prime}},\mathcal{W}^{\disc}_{\mathsf{Lic}^{\prime}},\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{diag}}_{\mathsf{Lic}^{\prime}}).

Since the preorders agree by assumption and the reconstructed classes have just been shown to agree, it follows that

\Sigma_{\mathsf{Lic}}=\Sigma_{\mathsf{Lic}^{\prime}}.

It remains to prove that

\mathsf{TZ}_{\Sigma_{\mathsf{Lic}}}=\mathsf{TZ}_{\Sigma_{\mathsf{Lic}^{\prime}}}.

By
section 3
, the rule schemes of
\mathsf{TZ}_{\Sigma}
are determined by the following data:

(1)

the zone set
Z
, which determines the labels that may occur in zone contexts;

(2)

the weakening class
\mathcal{W}
, which determines exactly for which zones the rules
\mathrm{W}_{z}
are present;

(3)

the contraction class
\mathcal{C}
, which determines exactly for which zones the rules
\mathrm{C}_{z}
are present.

The remaining rules

\mathrm{ax},\quad I\mathrm{R},\quad\otimes\mathrm{R},\quad\otimes\mathrm{L},\quad I\mathrm{L},\quad\mathrm{cut}

are independent of any further data. Since

\Sigma_{\mathsf{Lic}}=\Sigma_{\mathsf{Lic}^{\prime}},

the two calculi have the same zone set and the same weakening and contraction classes. Therefore they have exactly the same inference rules, and hence

\mathsf{TZ}_{\Sigma_{\mathsf{Lic}}}=\mathsf{TZ}_{\Sigma_{\mathsf{Lic}^{\prime}}}.

This completes the proof.

The preceding theorem formalises a central point of the paper: the extracted proof theory is invariant under replacement of a coherent discipline by another one carrying the same structural family and the same zone preorder. Thus the calculus depends only on the reconstructed structural data, and not on further accidental features of the chosen disciplined presentation.

We now turn to the main proof-theoretic property of the system, namely admissibility of cut.
Since
\mathsf{TZ}_{\Sigma}
is a tensorial fragment of intuitionistic linear logic with zone-dependent structural rules, it is natural to derive cut elimination from the known cut-elimination theorems for subexponential systems.
To do this rigorously, one must still verify that the cut-free derivation produced in the ambient system remains inside the tensorial fragment.

The technical bridge between the ambient subexponential metatheory and the extracted
tensorial calculus is the following closure lemma, which ensures that cut elimination in the
ambient system descends to the extracted fragment itself.

Lemma 12

(Closure of the tensorial fragment under cut-free derivations)
.

Let
\Pi
be a cut-free derivation in a standard single-conclusion sequent calculus for intuitionistic linear logic with subexponentials, with signature
\Sigma=(Z,\preceq,\mathcal{W},\mathcal{C})
.
Assume that the end-sequent of
\Pi
has the form

\Gamma\vdash A,

where every formula occurring in
\Gamma
and
A
belongs to the tensorial grammar of
section 3
.
Then every formula occurring anywhere in
\Pi
is tensorial, and every inference of
\Pi
is an instance of one of the rules of
\mathsf{TZ}_{\Sigma}
.

Proof 3.2
.

We argue by induction on the height of the cut-free derivation
\Pi
.

If
\Pi
has height
0
, then
\Pi
consists of a single axiom.
In a standard single-conclusion sequent calculus, the only cut-free derivation of height
0
with a nonempty succedent is an identity axiom.
Its conclusion is therefore of the form

z\!:\!B\vdash B

for some tensorial formula
B
.
This is an instance of the rule
\mathrm{ax}
of
\mathsf{TZ}_{\Sigma}
, and every formula occurring in the derivation is tensorial.

Assume now that
\Pi
has positive height, and let its last inference be
R
.
We examine the possible forms of
R
.

Case 1:
R
is an identity axiom.

This is the same as the height-
0
case and is already covered.

Case 2:
R
is the right rule for the tensor unit.

Then the conclusion of
R
is

\emptyset\vdash I.

This is an instance of
I\mathrm{R}
, and there are no premises.
Hence the derivation lies in
\mathsf{TZ}_{\Sigma}
.

Case 3:
R
is the right rule for tensor.

Then the conclusion of
R
is

\Gamma,\Delta\vdash A\otimes B

for some tensorial formulas
A
and
B
, and the premises are

\Gamma\vdash A\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Delta\vdash B.

Since
\Pi
is cut-free, both premises are derivable by cut-free subderivations of strictly smaller height.
By the induction hypothesis, every formula in those subderivations is tensorial and every inference in them belongs to
\mathsf{TZ}_{\Sigma}
.
The last rule
R
is precisely
\otimes\mathrm{R}
.
Therefore the whole derivation belongs to
\mathsf{TZ}_{\Sigma}
.

Case 4:
R
is the left rule for tensor.

Then the conclusion of
R
is

\Gamma,z\!:\!A\otimes B\vdash C,

and its premise is

\Gamma,z\!:\!A,z\!:\!B\vdash C.

Since
A\otimes B
is tensorial, both
A
and
B
are tensorial.
The premise is therefore a sequent all of whose formulas are tensorial.
By the induction hypothesis, the subderivation of the premise belongs to
\mathsf{TZ}_{\Sigma}
.
The last rule
R
is exactly
\otimes\mathrm{L}
.
Hence the whole derivation belongs to
\mathsf{TZ}_{\Sigma}
.

Case 5:
R
is the left rule for the tensor unit.

Then the conclusion is

\Gamma,z\!:\!I\vdash C,

and the premise is

\Gamma\vdash C.

Again the premise contains only tensorial formulas, so by the induction hypothesis its subderivation lies in
\mathsf{TZ}_{\Sigma}
.
The last rule is precisely
I\mathrm{L}
.
Therefore the entire derivation lies in
\mathsf{TZ}_{\Sigma}
.

Case 6:
R
is a weakening rule.

In a standard subexponential sequent calculus, such a rule may be applied only in zones that admit weakening.
Its conclusion has the form

\Gamma,z\!:\!A\vdash B,

and its premise has the form

\Gamma\vdash B.

Since the conclusion contains only tensorial formulas, the premise does as well.
By the induction hypothesis, the subderivation of the premise belongs to
\mathsf{TZ}_{\Sigma}
.
Since
z\in\mathcal{W}
, the rule
R
is exactly an instance of
\mathrm{W}_{z}
.
Thus the entire derivation lies in
\mathsf{TZ}_{\Sigma}
.

Case 7:
R
is a contraction rule.

Its conclusion has the form

\Gamma,z\!:\!A\vdash B,

and its premise has the form

\Gamma,z\!:\!A,z\!:\!A\vdash B.

Again all formulas occurring are tensorial, so by the induction hypothesis the subderivation of the premise belongs to
\mathsf{TZ}_{\Sigma}
.
Since
z\in\mathcal{C}
, the rule
R
is exactly an instance of
\mathrm{C}_{z}
.
Hence the whole derivation lies in
\mathsf{TZ}_{\Sigma}
.

Case 8:
R
is exchange.

Since contexts in
\mathsf{TZ}_{\Sigma}
are taken modulo permutation, exchange is implicit in our presentation.
Thus an exchange step does not take the derivation outside the fragment.
Its premises contain exactly the same formulas as its conclusion, merely in different order, so the induction hypothesis applies directly.

Case 9:
R
is any other rule of the ambient subexponential calculus.

We show that this is impossible.

Any right rule for a connective other than
I
or
\otimes
would have a conclusion whose succedent is headed by that connective.
Since the succedent formula of the conclusion of
R
is tensorial, no such rule can occur.

Any left rule for a connective or modality other than
I
or
\otimes
would have a conclusion containing, in its antecedent, a principal formula headed by that connective or modality.
Since every antecedent formula in the conclusion is tensorial, no such rule can occur either.

Any modal rule introducing or manipulating explicit subexponential formulas is likewise impossible, because no formula in the conclusion carries such a connective.

Finally, the derivation is cut-free by assumption, so
R
cannot be a cut.

Thus no other case can occur.

Having exhausted all possibilities for the last rule
R
, we conclude that every formula in
\Pi
is tensorial and every inference of
\Pi
belongs to
\mathsf{TZ}_{\Sigma}
. This completes the induction.

We may now state the main proof-theoretic theorem for the extracted calculus.

Theorem 13

(Cut elimination)
.

For every subexponential signature
\Sigma
, the cut rule is admissible in the calculus
\mathsf{TZ}_{\Sigma}
.
Equivalently, every derivable sequent of
\mathsf{TZ}_{\Sigma}
has a cut-free derivation in
\mathsf{TZ}_{\Sigma}
.

Proof 3.3
.

Let

\Pi

be a derivation in
\mathsf{TZ}_{\Sigma}
of some sequent

\Gamma\vdash A.

By construction, every rule of
\mathsf{TZ}_{\Sigma}
is an instance of a rule of the standard single-conclusion sequent calculi for intuitionistic linear logic with subexponentials studied in the literature; see, for example,
[
11
,
15
]
.
Therefore
\Pi
may be viewed, without any modification, as a derivation in such an ambient subexponential calculus.

By the cut-elimination theorem for that ambient calculus, there exists a cut-free derivation

\Pi^{\prime}

of the same end-sequent

\Gamma\vdash A.

Since the end-sequent contains only tensorial formulas,
12
applies to
\Pi^{\prime}
.
It follows that every formula occurring in
\Pi^{\prime}
is tensorial and every inference of
\Pi^{\prime}
belongs to the rule set of
\mathsf{TZ}_{\Sigma}
.

Hence
\Pi^{\prime}
is in fact a cut-free derivation in
\mathsf{TZ}_{\Sigma}
itself.
Therefore the cut rule is admissible in
\mathsf{TZ}_{\Sigma}
, and every derivable sequent has a cut-free derivation in the same calculus.

Although the general cut-elimination theorem is imported from the known subexponential literature, it is useful to record the principal reduction specific to the tensorial fragment, since it is the only genuinely connective reduction used in the system.

Remark 14

(Principal cut on tensor)
.

Suppose one has derivations

\frac{\Pi_{1}\colon\Gamma\vdash A\qquad\Pi_{2}\colon\Delta\vdash B}{\Gamma,\Delta\vdash A\otimes B}\;\otimes\mathrm{R}

and

\frac{\Pi_{3}\colon\Theta,z\!:\!A,z\!:\!B\vdash C}{\Theta,z\!:\!A\otimes B\vdash C}\;\otimes\mathrm{L}.

A cut on the principal formula
A\otimes B
reduces to two successive cuts:

\Pi_{2}\colon\Delta\vdash B

\Pi_{1}\colon\Gamma\vdash A

\Pi_{3}\colon\Theta,z\!:\!A,z\!:\!B\vdash C

cut

\Gamma,\Theta,z\!:\!B\vdash C

cut

\Gamma,\Delta,\Theta\vdash C

up to permutation of the multiset context.
The two new cuts are on the strictly smaller formulas
A
and
B
.
This is the tensorial core of the cut-reduction argument carried out in the general systems cited above.

Remark 15
.

The proof-theoretic stance adopted here is compatible with the broader literature on focusing and subexponentials; see
[
1
,
11
,
15
]
.
We do not develop a focused presentation in the present article.
For the purposes of this work,
13
is the essential proof-theoretic property needed to support the extracted tensorial system.

4.
Soundness in the categorized architecture

Let
(\mathbf{Arch}_{Z},\mathsf{Lic})
be a coherently disciplined architectural category, and let

\Sigma_{\mathsf{Lic}}=(Z,\preceq,\mathcal{W}^{\disc}_{\mathsf{Lic}},\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{diag}}_{\mathsf{Lic}})

be its extracted subexponential signature. By
8
, one may equivalently write

\Sigma_{\mathsf{Lic}}=(Z,\preceq,\mathcal{W}_{\mathsf{Lic}},\mathcal{C}_{\mathsf{Lic}}).

We now return from syntax to architecture and show that every derivation in the extracted calculus is interpreted by a morphism built from the architectural data and the designated structural families.

The interpretation of sequents requires a harmless extension of the label set.
Indeed, while antecedent formulas are interpreted in the zones prescribed by the context, the succedent must be represented uniformly as an output object.
To achieve this, we adjoin a fresh output label.

{defi}

[Output extension of the zone set]
Fix a symbol
\mathsf{o}\notin Z
, and let

\widehat{Z}:=Z\sqcup\{\mathsf{o}\}.

All categorical constructions of Section
2
may be carried out with
\widehat{Z}
in place of
Z
.
We write

\mathbf{Arch}_{\widehat{Z}}

for the resulting architectural category.
By construction, every object and morphism of
\mathbf{Arch}_{Z}
may be regarded canonically as an object or morphism of
\mathbf{Arch}_{\widehat{Z}}
.

Thus the disciplined architecture embeds canonically into a larger category in which a dedicated output zone is available.
Only this output zone is new; no additional structural permissions are attached to it.

We now interpret formulas and contexts.

{defi}

[Formula and context interpretation]
Fix an assignment

\rho\colon\mathsf{At}\to\Ob(\mathbf{Ctx}).

Extend it recursively to tensorial formulas by

[\![p]\!]:=\rho(p),\qquad[\![I]\!]:=1,\qquad[\![A\otimes B]\!]:=[\![A]\!]\times[\![B]\!].

For each zone
z\in\widehat{Z}
, let

J_{z}\colon\mathbf{Ctx}\to\mathbf{Arch}_{\widehat{Z}}

be the one-zone embedding of
subsection 2.2
.

If

\Gamma=(z_{1}\!:\!A_{1},\dots,z_{n}\!:\!A_{n})

is an ordered representative of a zone context, define

[\![\Gamma]\!]:=J_{z_{1}}([\![A_{1}]\!])\otimes\cdots\otimes J_{z_{n}}([\![A_{n}]\!]).

For the empty context, set

[\![\emptyset]\!]:=\emptyset.

Finally, define the
output object
of a formula
A
by

\Out(A):=J_{\mathsf{o}}([\![A]\!]).

Remark 16
.

Since zone contexts are taken modulo permutation, the object
[\![\Gamma]\!]
depends a priori on the chosen ordered representative.
However, any two representatives are related by a canonical composite of associators and symmetries in the symmetric monoidal category
\mathbf{Arch}_{\widehat{Z}}
.
Because these structural isomorphisms will belong to the diagrammatic class defined below, the existence of an interpretation morphism is independent of the chosen representative.

To interpret the logical rules we require, besides the structural maps already supplied by the discipline, a small family of canonical interface morphisms connecting zone-labelled inputs to the output zone.

{defi}

[Canonical semantic interface blocks]
For every zone
z\in Z
and every tensorial formula
A
, define the following morphisms in
\mathbf{Arch}_{\widehat{Z}}
.

(1)

The
readout block

r_{z,A}\colon J_{z}([\![A]\!])\longrightarrow\Out(A)

is the parametrised block induced by the identity morphism

\id_{[\![A]\!]}\colon[\![A]\!]\to[\![A]\!].

(2)

The
input block

\iota_{z,A}\colon\Out(A)\longrightarrow J_{z}([\![A]\!])

is the parametrised block induced by the same identity morphism

\id_{[\![A]\!]}\colon[\![A]\!]\to[\![A]\!].

(3)

The
tensor output block

\tau_{A,B}\colon\Out(A)\otimes\Out(B)\longrightarrow\Out(A\otimes B)

is the parametrised block induced by the identity on

[\![A]\!]\times[\![B]\!]=[\![A\otimes B]\!].

(4)

The
tensor decomposition block

\mu_{z,A,B}\colon J_{z}([\![A\otimes B]\!])\longrightarrow J_{z}([\![A]\!])\otimes J_{z}([\![B]\!])

is the parametrised block induced by the identity on

[\![A\otimes B]\!]=[\![A]\!]\times[\![B]\!].

(5)

The
unit output block

\eta\colon\emptyset\longrightarrow\Out(I)

is the parametrised block induced by the identity on the terminal object

1=[\![I]\!].

(6)

For each
z\in Z
, the
unit discard block

\nu_{z}\colon J_{z}([\![I]\!])\longrightarrow\emptyset

is the parametrised block induced by the unique morphism

1\to 1.

Remark 17
.

The blocks of
section 4
are not structural permissions.
They play a different role: they connect the zone-indexed antecedent semantics with the distinguished output zone, and they unpack the tensor and unit at the semantic level.
The only genuinely resource-sensitive maps remain the licensed discards and diagonals supplied by the discipline.

We may now define the class of diagrams through which all derivations will be interpreted.

{defi}

[Licensed diagram class]
Let
\mathrm{Diag}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
be the smallest class of morphisms in
\mathbf{Arch}_{\widehat{Z}}
satisfying the following conditions.

(1)

It contains all identities, associators, unitors, and symmetries of the symmetric monoidal structure.

(2)

It contains all interface blocks of
section 4
.

(3)

It contains all coercions

\chi_{z,z^{\prime},X}\colon J_{z}(X)\to J_{z^{\prime}}(X)

supplied by the discipline
\mathsf{Lic}
, for all
z\preceq z^{\prime}
in
Z
and all
X\in\Ob(\mathbf{Ctx})
.

(4)

It contains all licensed discards

\omega_{z,X}\colon J_{z}(X)\to\emptyset

supplied by
\mathsf{Lic}
, for all
z\in\mathcal{W}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
and all
X\in\Ob(\mathbf{Ctx})
.

(5)

It contains all licensed diagonals

\delta_{z,X}\colon J_{z}(X)\to J_{z}(X)\otimes J_{z}(X)

supplied by
\mathsf{Lic}
, for all
z\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
and all
X\in\Ob(\mathbf{Ctx})
.

(6)

It is closed under composition.

(7)

It is closed under tensor product.

Lemma 18
.

The class
\mathrm{Diag}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
is closed under precomposition and postcomposition with canonical associators, unitors, and symmetries in
\mathbf{Arch}_{\widehat{Z}}
.

Proof 4.1
.

By
section 4
(1), every associator, unitor, and symmetry belongs to
\mathrm{Diag}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
.
By
section 4
(6), the class is closed under composition.
Hence, if

f\in\mathrm{Diag}_{\mathsf{Lic}}

and

\sigma,\tau\in\mathrm{Diag}_{\mathsf{Lic}}

are canonical structural isomorphisms such that the composites are defined, then

\sigma\circ f,\qquad f\circ\tau,\qquad\sigma\circ f\circ\tau

all belong to
\mathrm{Diag}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
.

We are now in position to prove the soundness theorem.

Theorem 19

(Soundness of the extracted calculus)
.

For every derivation

\pi\colon\Gamma\vdash A

in the extracted calculus

\mathsf{TZ}_{\Sigma_{\mathsf{Lic}}},

there exists a morphism

[\![\pi]\!]\colon[\![\Gamma]\!]\longrightarrow\Out(A)

belonging to
\mathrm{Diag}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
.

Moreover, if the last rule of
\pi
is an instance of
\mathrm{W}_{z}
, then the construction of
[\![\pi]\!]
uses the designated discard family

\omega_{z,-};

if the last rule of
\pi
is an instance of
\mathrm{C}_{z}
, then the construction of
[\![\pi]\!]
uses the designated diagonal family

\delta_{z,-}.

Proof 4.2
.

We proceed by induction on the derivation
\pi
.

Case 1:
\pi
ends with
\mathrm{ax}
.

Then

\pi\;=\;\frac{}{z\!:\!A\vdash A}\;\mathrm{ax}.

By definition,

[\![z\!:\!A]\!]=J_{z}([\![A]\!]),\qquad\Out(A)=J_{\mathsf{o}}([\![A]\!]).

Define

[\![\pi]\!]:=r_{z,A}\colon J_{z}([\![A]\!])\to\Out(A).

By
section 4
(2), the readout block belongs to
\mathrm{Diag}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
.

Case 2:
\pi
ends with
I\mathrm{R}
.

Then

\pi\;=\;\frac{}{\emptyset\vdash I}\;I\mathrm{R}.

Here

[\![\emptyset]\!]=\emptyset\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Out(I)=J_{\mathsf{o}}(1).

Define

[\![\pi]\!]:=\eta\colon\emptyset\to\Out(I).

By
section 4
(2), one has

\eta\in\mathrm{Diag}_{\mathsf{Lic}}.

Case 3:
\pi
ends with
\otimes\mathrm{R}
.

Then

\pi\;=\;\frac{\pi_{1}\colon\Gamma\vdash A\qquad\pi_{2}\colon\Delta\vdash B}{\Gamma,\Delta\vdash A\otimes B}\;\otimes\mathrm{R}.

By the induction hypothesis, there exist morphisms

[\![\pi_{1}]\!]\colon[\![\Gamma]\!]\to\Out(A),\qquad[\![\pi_{2}]\!]\colon[\![\Delta]\!]\to\Out(B)

in
\mathrm{Diag}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
.
By closure under tensor,

[\![\pi_{1}]\!]\otimes[\![\pi_{2}]\!]\colon[\![\Gamma]\!]\otimes[\![\Delta]\!]\to\Out(A)\otimes\Out(B)

lies in
\mathrm{Diag}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
.
By
section 4
(2), the tensor output block

\tau_{A,B}\colon\Out(A)\otimes\Out(B)\to\Out(A\otimes B)

belongs to
\mathrm{Diag}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
.
Define

[\![\pi]\!]:=\tau_{A,B}\circ([\![\pi_{1}]\!]\otimes[\![\pi_{2}]\!]).

Then

[\![\pi]\!]\colon[\![\Gamma]\!]\otimes[\![\Delta]\!]\to\Out(A\otimes B)

lies in
\mathrm{Diag}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
.
Since
[\![\Gamma,\Delta]\!]
is, by definition, represented by
[\![\Gamma]\!]\otimes[\![\Delta]\!]
up to canonical associativity and symmetry isomorphism,
18
shows that
[\![\pi]\!]
may be regarded as a morphism

[\![\Gamma,\Delta]\!]\to\Out(A\otimes B)

in
\mathrm{Diag}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
.

Case 4:
\pi
ends with
\otimes\mathrm{L}
.

Then

\pi\;=\;\frac{\pi_{0}\colon\Gamma,z\!:\!A,z\!:\!B\vdash C}{\Gamma,z\!:\!A\otimes B\vdash C}\;\otimes\mathrm{L}.

By the induction hypothesis, there exists a morphism

[\![\pi_{0}]\!]\colon[\![\Gamma,z\!:\!A,z\!:\!B]\!]\to\Out(C)

in
\mathrm{Diag}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
.

By definition of context semantics,

[\![\Gamma,z\!:\!A\otimes B]\!]

and

[\![\Gamma]\!]\otimes J_{z}([\![A\otimes B]\!])

differ only by canonical associativity and symmetry isomorphisms. Likewise,

[\![\Gamma,z\!:\!A,z\!:\!B]\!]

is canonically isomorphic to

[\![\Gamma]\!]\otimes J_{z}([\![A]\!])\otimes J_{z}([\![B]\!]).

By
section 4
(2), the block

\mu_{z,A,B}\colon J_{z}([\![A\otimes B]\!])\to J_{z}([\![A]\!])\otimes J_{z}([\![B]\!])

belongs to
\mathrm{Diag}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
.
Hence

\id_{[\![\Gamma]\!]}\otimes\mu_{z,A,B}\colon[\![\Gamma]\!]\otimes J_{z}([\![A\otimes B]\!])\to[\![\Gamma]\!]\otimes J_{z}([\![A]\!])\otimes J_{z}([\![B]\!])

belongs to
\mathrm{Diag}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
by closure under tensor.
Composing with the relevant structural isomorphisms and then with
[\![\pi_{0}]\!]
, we obtain a morphism

[\![\pi]\!]\colon[\![\Gamma,z\!:\!A\otimes B]\!]\to\Out(C)

in
\mathrm{Diag}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
.
More explicitly,
[\![\pi]\!]
is the composite

[\![\Gamma,z\!:\!A\otimes B]\!]\xrightarrow{\sigma_{1}}[\![\Gamma]\!]\otimes J_{z}([\![A\otimes B]\!])\xrightarrow{\id\otimes\mu_{z,A,B}}[\![\Gamma]\!]\otimes J_{z}([\![A]\!])\otimes J_{z}([\![B]\!])\xrightarrow{\sigma_{2}}[\![\Gamma,z\!:\!A,z\!:\!B]\!]\xrightarrow{[\![\pi_{0}]\!]}\Out(C),

where
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
are canonical structural isomorphisms.

Case 5:
\pi
ends with
I\mathrm{L}
.

Then

\pi\;=\;\frac{\pi_{0}\colon\Gamma\vdash C}{\Gamma,z\!:\!I\vdash C}\;I\mathrm{L}.

By the induction hypothesis,

[\![\pi_{0}]\!]\colon[\![\Gamma]\!]\to\Out(C)

lies in
\mathrm{Diag}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
.
Since

[\![z\!:\!I]\!]=J_{z}(1),

the block

\nu_{z}\colon J_{z}(1)\to\emptyset

belongs to
\mathrm{Diag}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
by
section 4
(2).
Using closure under tensor, we obtain

\id_{[\![\Gamma]\!]}\otimes\nu_{z}\colon[\![\Gamma]\!]\otimes J_{z}(1)\to[\![\Gamma]\!]\otimes\emptyset.

Composing with the right unitor

\rho_{[\![\Gamma]\!]}\colon[\![\Gamma]\!]\otimes\emptyset\to[\![\Gamma]\!]

and then with
[\![\pi_{0}]\!]
yields a morphism

[\![\Gamma]\!]\otimes J_{z}(1)\to\Out(C)

in
\mathrm{Diag}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
.
As in the tensor-left case, after inserting the canonical structural isomorphism between

[\![\Gamma,z\!:\!I]\!]\quad\text{and}\quad[\![\Gamma]\!]\otimes J_{z}(1),

we obtain the required interpretation

[\![\pi]\!]\colon[\![\Gamma,z\!:\!I]\!]\to\Out(C).

Case 6:
\pi
ends with
\mathrm{W}_{z}
.

Then

\pi\;=\;\frac{\pi_{0}\colon\Gamma\vdash B}{\Gamma,z\!:\!A\vdash B}\;\mathrm{W}_{z},

with

z\in\mathcal{W}_{\mathsf{Lic}}

because the rule
\mathrm{W}_{z}
is present in
\mathsf{TZ}_{\Sigma_{\mathsf{Lic}}}
exactly for such zones.
By the induction hypothesis,

[\![\pi_{0}]\!]\colon[\![\Gamma]\!]\to\Out(B)

lies in
\mathrm{Diag}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
.
Since
z\in\mathcal{W}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
, the discipline provides the licensed discard

\omega_{z,[\![A]\!]}\colon J_{z}([\![A]\!])\to\emptyset,

and this morphism belongs to
\mathrm{Diag}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
by
section 4
(4).
By closure under tensor,

\id_{[\![\Gamma]\!]}\otimes\omega_{z,[\![A]\!]}\colon[\![\Gamma]\!]\otimes J_{z}([\![A]\!])\to[\![\Gamma]\!]\otimes\emptyset

belongs to
\mathrm{Diag}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
.
Composing with the right unitor

\rho_{[\![\Gamma]\!]}\colon[\![\Gamma]\!]\otimes\emptyset\to[\![\Gamma]\!]

and then with
[\![\pi_{0}]\!]
yields a morphism

[\![\Gamma]\!]\otimes J_{z}([\![A]\!])\to\Out(B)

in
\mathrm{Diag}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
.
Up to the canonical structural isomorphism between

[\![\Gamma,z\!:\!A]\!]\quad\text{and}\quad[\![\Gamma]\!]\otimes J_{z}([\![A]\!]),

this gives the required interpretation of the conclusion.
By construction, the interpretation uses the designated family

\omega_{z,-}.

Case 7:
\pi
ends with
\mathrm{C}_{z}
.

Then

\pi\;=\;\frac{\pi_{0}\colon\Gamma,z\!:\!A,z\!:\!A\vdash B}{\Gamma,z\!:\!A\vdash B}\;\mathrm{C}_{z},

with

z\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathsf{Lic}}.

By the induction hypothesis,

[\![\pi_{0}]\!]\colon[\![\Gamma,z\!:\!A,z\!:\!A]\!]\to\Out(B)

lies in
\mathrm{Diag}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
.
Since
z\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
, the discipline provides the licensed diagonal

\delta_{z,[\![A]\!]}\colon J_{z}([\![A]\!])\to J_{z}([\![A]\!])\otimes J_{z}([\![A]\!]),

and this morphism belongs to
\mathrm{Diag}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
by
section 4
(5).
Therefore

\id_{[\![\Gamma]\!]}\otimes\delta_{z,[\![A]\!]}\colon[\![\Gamma]\!]\otimes J_{z}([\![A]\!])\to[\![\Gamma]\!]\otimes J_{z}([\![A]\!])\otimes J_{z}([\![A]\!])

belongs to
\mathrm{Diag}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
.
Composing, as needed, with canonical associativity and symmetry isomorphisms to identify the codomain with

[\![\Gamma,z\!:\!A,z\!:\!A]\!],

and then with
[\![\pi_{0}]\!]
, yields a morphism

[\![\Gamma,z\!:\!A]\!]\to\Out(B)

in
\mathrm{Diag}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
.
By construction, this interpretation uses the designated family

\delta_{z,-}.

Case 8:
\pi
ends with
cut
.

Then

\pi\;=\;\frac{\pi_{1}\colon\Gamma\vdash A\qquad\pi_{2}\colon\Delta,z\!:\!A\vdash B}{\Gamma,\Delta\vdash B}\;\mathrm{cut}.

By the induction hypothesis, there exist morphisms

[\![\pi_{1}]\!]\colon[\![\Gamma]\!]\to\Out(A),\qquad[\![\pi_{2}]\!]\colon[\![\Delta,z\!:\!A]\!]\to\Out(B)

in
\mathrm{Diag}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
.
Choose the ordered representative of the second premise so that

[\![\Delta,z\!:\!A]\!]

is canonically isomorphic to

[\![\Delta]\!]\otimes J_{z}([\![A]\!]).

The input block

\iota_{z,A}\colon\Out(A)\to J_{z}([\![A]\!])

belongs to
\mathrm{Diag}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
by
section 4
(2).
Hence

[\![\pi_{1}]\!]\otimes\id_{[\![\Delta]\!]}\colon[\![\Gamma]\!]\otimes[\![\Delta]\!]\to\Out(A)\otimes[\![\Delta]\!]

belongs to
\mathrm{Diag}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
, and so does its composite with the symmetry

\beta\colon\Out(A)\otimes[\![\Delta]\!]\to[\![\Delta]\!]\otimes\Out(A).

By closure under tensor,

\id_{[\![\Delta]\!]}\otimes\iota_{z,A}\colon[\![\Delta]\!]\otimes\Out(A)\to[\![\Delta]\!]\otimes J_{z}([\![A]\!])

also lies in
\mathrm{Diag}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
.
Composing these morphisms, and then using the canonical isomorphism

[\![\Delta]\!]\otimes J_{z}([\![A]\!])\xrightarrow{\cong}[\![\Delta,z\!:\!A]\!],

we obtain a morphism

h\colon[\![\Gamma]\!]\otimes[\![\Delta]\!]\to[\![\Delta,z\!:\!A]\!]

in
\mathrm{Diag}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
.
Finally, define

[\![\pi]\!]:=[\![\pi_{2}]\!]\circ h.

Then

[\![\pi]\!]\colon[\![\Gamma]\!]\otimes[\![\Delta]\!]\to\Out(B)

lies in
\mathrm{Diag}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
.
Up to the canonical structural isomorphism between

[\![\Gamma,\Delta]\!]\quad\text{and}\quad[\![\Gamma]\!]\otimes[\![\Delta]\!],

this yields the required interpretation

[\![\Gamma,\Delta]\!]\to\Out(B).

These are all the rules of
\mathsf{TZ}_{\Sigma_{\mathsf{Lic}}}
.
In every case, the interpretation lies in
\mathrm{Diag}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
.
This completes the induction.

The theorem shows that every derivation is interpreted by a diagram assembled from the architectural data, the coherence isomorphisms of the monoidal structure, and the designated structural families.
In particular, the structural rules do not receive arbitrary semantic witnesses: they are interpreted by the very discards and diagonals licensed by the architecture.

Corollary 20
.

The extracted proof theory is sound exactly for the structural behaviour explicitly licensed by the coherently disciplined architecture.

Proof 4.3
.

By
19
, every derivation in
\mathsf{TZ}_{\Sigma_{\mathsf{Lic}}}
is interpreted by a morphism in
\mathrm{Diag}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
, and the rules
\mathrm{W}_{z}
and
\mathrm{C}_{z}
are interpreted using the designated families

\omega_{z,-}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\delta_{z,-},

respectively.

Conversely, by

\mathsf{Lic}
supplies licensed discard and diagonal families.
Therefore the structural rules present in the extracted proof theory are precisely those corresponding to structurally licensed architectural behaviour.

Hence the soundness theorem identifies the proof theory with exactly the structural behaviour explicitly licensed by the categorized architecture.

{exa}

We spell out the extraction process on a minimal three-zone discipline.

Let

Z=\{p,r,\ell\},

and let the preorder on
Z
be discrete, so that

z\preceq z^{\prime}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad z=z^{\prime}.

Thus there are no nontrivial zone coercions: for each
z\in Z
and each
A\in\Ob(\mathbf{Ctx})
, the only coercion is

\chi_{z,z,A}=\id_{J_{z}(A)}.

We interpret the three zones as follows:

•

p
is a
persistent
zone;

•

r
is a
relevant
zone;

•

\ell
is a
linear
zone.

The discipline is specified by

\mathcal{W}_{\mathsf{Lic}}=\{p\},\qquad\mathcal{C}_{\mathsf{Lic}}=\{p,r\}.

Accordingly, for every object
A\in\Ob(\mathbf{Ctx})
, the licensed structural family contains:

•

a discard

\omega_{p,A}\colon J_{p}(A)\to\emptyset;

•

diagonals

\delta_{p,A}\colon J_{p}(A)\to J_{p}(A)\otimes J_{p}(A),\qquad\delta_{r,A}\colon J_{r}(A)\to J_{r}(A)\otimes J_{r}(A);

•

no discard in zone
r
;

•

neither discard nor diagonal in zone
\ell
.

Since the preorder is discrete, upward closure imposes no further conditions. The reconstructed structural classes therefore satisfy

\mathcal{W}^{\disc}_{\mathsf{Lic}}=\{p\},\qquad\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{diag}}_{\mathsf{Lic}}=\{p,r\}.

Hence the extracted signature is

\Sigma_{\mathsf{Lic}}=(\{p,r,\ell\},=,\{p\},\{p,r\}).

The corresponding extracted calculus has:

•

a weakening rule only in zone
p
,

\frac{\Gamma\vdash B}{\Gamma,p\!:\!A\vdash B}\;\mathrm{W}_{p};

•

contraction rules in zones
p
and
r
,

\frac{\Gamma,p\!:\!A,p\!:\!A\vdash B}{\Gamma,p\!:\!A\vdash B}\;\mathrm{C}_{p},\qquad\frac{\Gamma,r\!:\!A,r\!:\!A\vdash B}{\Gamma,r\!:\!A\vdash B}\;\mathrm{C}_{r};

•

no weakening rule in zone
r
;

•

neither weakening nor contraction in zone
\ell
.

Thus the proof theory mirrors the intended architectural behaviour:
persistent information may be copied and discarded, relevant information may be copied but not silently erased, and linear information must be used without free copying or erasure.

For instance, the sequent

p\!:\!X\vdash I

is derivable, since one may first derive

\emptyset\vdash I

by
I\mathrm{R}
and then apply
\mathrm{W}_{p}
. By contrast, the calculus contains no rule
\mathrm{W}_{r}
, so the same derivation pattern is unavailable in zone
r
.

Likewise, from the axiom

r\!:\!X\vdash X

one obtains

r\!:\!X,r\!:\!X\vdash X

by applying
\mathrm{W}_{r}
is
not
possible, since
r\notin\mathcal{W}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
. However, if a derivation already contains two copies of
r\!:\!X
, then
\mathrm{C}_{r}
permits them to be contracted to one. In this way the relevant zone supports duplication control without unrestricted erasure.

This example makes explicit the mechanism developed in the paper: one starts from a categorized structural discipline, reconstructs from it the weakening and contraction classes, and thereby obtains a proof system whose structural rules are exactly those licensed by the architecture.

The previous example isolates the structural mechanism abstractly. We now instantiate the same mechanism on a minimal neural architecture.

{exa}

[A two-block neural architecture and its extracted logic]
We now give a concrete example showing the passage

\text{neural architecture}\;\longrightarrow\;\text{categorisation}\;\longrightarrow\;\text{subexponential signature}\;\longrightarrow\;\text{proof theory}.

Let
\mathbf{Ctx}=\mathbf{FinVect}_{\mathbb{R}}
, and let

Z=\{p,r,\ell\},

where
p
is a persistent zone,
r
is a relevant zone, and
\ell
is a linear zone.
Assume that the preorder on
Z
is discrete.

Choose finite-dimensional real vector spaces

M=\mathbb{R}^{m},\qquad R=\mathbb{R}^{r},\qquad X=\mathbb{R}^{d},\qquad H=\mathbb{R}^{h},\qquad Y=\mathbb{R}^{k}.

We interpret:

•

M
as persistent memory;

•

R
as retrieved contextual information;

•

X
as the current observation;

•

H
as a hidden representation;

•

Y
as the output space.

Consider first a neural block

f\colon M\times R\times X\longrightarrow H,\qquad f(m,r,x)=\sigma(W_{M}m+W_{R}r+W_{X}x+b),

where

W_{M}\colon M\to H,\qquad W_{R}\colon R\to H,\qquad W_{X}\colon X\to H

are linear maps,
b\in H
, and
\sigma\colon H\to H
is a componentwise activation.

This determines a parametrised resource block with trivial parameter object

(1,f)\colon\langle p,M\rangle\otimes\langle r,R\rangle\otimes\langle\ell,X\rangle\longrightarrow\langle\ell,H\rangle,

hence a morphism

[\![1,f]\!]\colon J_{p}(M)\otimes J_{r}(R)\otimes J_{\ell}(X)\longrightarrow J_{\ell}(H)

in the architectural category.

Next, consider a second neural block

g\colon H\times M\longrightarrow Y,\qquad g(h,m)=\tau(V_{H}h+V_{M}m+c),

where

V_{H}\colon H\to Y,\qquad V_{M}\colon M\to Y

are linear maps,
c\in Y
, and
\tau\colon Y\to Y
is another componentwise activation.

This yields a second morphism

[\![1,g]\!]\colon J_{\ell}(H)\otimes J_{p}(M)\longrightarrow\Out(Y).

The resulting architecture uses the persistent memory input twice: once in the first block and once again in the second. To model this categorically, we impose a coherent zone discipline by declaring

\mathcal{W}_{\mathsf{Lic}}=\{p\},\qquad\mathcal{C}_{\mathsf{Lic}}=\{p,r\}.

Thus:

•

the persistent zone
p
admits both discard and contraction;

•

the relevant zone
r
admits contraction but not discard;

•

the linear zone
\ell
admits neither discard nor contraction.

The licensed structural family therefore contains

\omega_{p,A}\colon J_{p}(A)\to\emptyset

for every
A\in\Ob(\mathbf{Ctx})
, and

\delta_{p,A}\colon J_{p}(A)\to J_{p}(A)\otimes J_{p}(A),\qquad\delta_{r,A}\colon J_{r}(A)\to J_{r}(A)\otimes J_{r}(A)

for every
A\in\Ob(\mathbf{Ctx})
, but contains neither

\omega_{r,A},\ \omega_{\ell,A},\ \delta_{\ell,A}.

The extracted subexponential signature is therefore

\Sigma_{\mathsf{Lic}}=(\{p,r,\ell\},=,\{p\},\{p,r\}).

The associated proof system has:

•

weakening only in zone
p
;

•

contraction in zones
p
and
r
;

•

no weakening in zones
r
and
\ell
;

•

no contraction in zone
\ell
.

The logical reading is immediate:

•

persistent memory behaves as reusable and discardable context;

•

retrieved context behaves as reusable but non-discardable context;

•

the current observation behaves linearly.

The categorical composition of the two neural blocks uses the licensed diagonal

\delta_{p,M}\colon J_{p}(M)\to J_{p}(M)\otimes J_{p}(M)

to duplicate the persistent input. Up to canonical associativity and symmetry isomorphisms, one obtains the composite

J_{p}(M)\otimes J_{r}(R)\otimes J_{\ell}(X)\xrightarrow{\ \delta_{p,M}\otimes\id\otimes\id\ }J_{p}(M)\otimes J_{p}(M)\otimes J_{r}(R)\otimes J_{\ell}(X)

followed by

[\![1,f]\!]\otimes\id_{J_{p}(M)}

and then by

[\![1,g]\!].

This yields a morphism

J_{p}(M)\otimes J_{r}(R)\otimes J_{\ell}(X)\longrightarrow\Out(Y)

in the licensed diagram class
\mathrm{Diag}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
.

The example also illustrates the soundness theorem at the level of individual rules.
The rule

\frac{\Gamma\vdash B}{\Gamma,p\!:\!A\vdash B}\;\mathrm{W}_{p}

is interpreted in
\mathrm{Diag}_{\mathsf{Lic}}
by tensoring the interpretation of the premise with the designated discard

\omega_{p,[\![A]\!]}\colon J_{p}([\![A]\!])\to\emptyset,

followed by the canonical right unitor

\rho_{[\![\Gamma]\!]}\colon[\![\Gamma]\!]\otimes\emptyset\to[\![\Gamma]\!].

Thus the soundness interpretation of
\mathrm{W}_{p}
is the composite

[\![\Gamma]\!]\otimes J_{p}([\![A]\!])\xrightarrow{\ \id_{[\![\Gamma]\!]}\otimes\omega_{p,[\![A]\!]}\ }[\![\Gamma]\!]\otimes\emptyset\xrightarrow{\ \rho_{[\![\Gamma]\!]}\ }[\![\Gamma]\!]\xrightarrow{\ [\![\pi_{0}]\!]\ }\Out(B),

where
[\![\pi_{0}]\!]\colon[\![\Gamma]\!]\to\Out(B)
interprets the premise derivation.

Likewise, the rule

\frac{\Gamma,p\!:\!A,p\!:\!A\vdash B}{\Gamma,p\!:\!A\vdash B}\;\mathrm{C}_{p}

is interpreted by tensoring with the designated diagonal

\delta_{p,[\![A]\!]}\colon J_{p}([\![A]\!])\to J_{p}([\![A]\!])\otimes J_{p}([\![A]\!]),

so that its semantic action is given by

[\![\Gamma]\!]\otimes J_{p}([\![A]\!])\xrightarrow{\ \id_{[\![\Gamma]\!]}\otimes\delta_{p,[\![A]\!]}\ }[\![\Gamma]\!]\otimes J_{p}([\![A]\!])\otimes J_{p}([\![A]\!])\longrightarrow\Out(B),

where the second arrow is the interpretation of the premise, up to the canonical associativity and symmetry isomorphisms used to identify the codomain with

[\![\Gamma,p\!:\!A,p\!:\!A]\!].

Similarly, the rule

\frac{\Gamma,r\!:\!A,r\!:\!A\vdash B}{\Gamma,r\!:\!A\vdash B}\;\mathrm{C}_{r}

is interpreted using the designated diagonal

\delta_{r,[\![A]\!]}\colon J_{r}([\![A]\!])\to J_{r}([\![A]\!])\otimes J_{r}([\![A]\!]).

By contrast, there is no rule

\mathrm{W}_{r}

in the extracted calculus, and this absence is reflected semantically by the absence of a designated discard

\omega_{r,[\![A]\!]}\colon J_{r}([\![A]\!])\to\emptyset

from the licensed family. Likewise, there is no rule

\mathrm{C}_{\ell},

because the discipline supplies no diagonal

\delta_{\ell,[\![A]\!]}\colon J_{\ell}([\![A]\!])\to J_{\ell}([\![A]\!])\otimes J_{\ell}([\![A]\!]).

Hence the proof theory mirrors the structural behaviour of the architecture exactly: the logic is not imposed externally on the network, but is read from the way the categorized architecture permits or forbids reuse and erasure of its different classes of inputs.

This example may be read as a minimal witness that the passage

\text{neural architecture}\to\text{categorisation}\to\text{logic}

is non-vacuous already at the level of small compositional networks.

6.
Conclusion

The article has established a first rigorous fragment of the architecture-to-category-to-logic programme. The main message is that one can begin from a categorized family of resource-sensitive architectural blocks, read off a subexponential signature from the licensed structural behaviour, and obtain a sound proof theory for the resulting tensorial fragment. The direction of explanation matters: the logic is extracted from the categorized architecture rather than fixed in advance.

Three extensions are immediate.

(1)

Enrich the categorized architecture with the additional structure needed to interpret linear implication, most likely through a satisfactory internal hom or a linear–nonlinear adjunction
[
4
,
12
]
.

(2)

Replace the tensorial fragment by a fuller subexponential proof system using the extracted preorder more actively.

(3)

Study concrete architecture families whose memory, retrieval, or tool-use policies induce nontrivial zone disciplines.

(4)

The two-block neural example shows that the extraction programme is already non-vacuous
at the level of small compositional architectures: the logic is read from architectural
resource policies rather than imposed externally.

Even in its present form, however, the paper already yields nontrivial theorems: cut elimination for the extracted calculus, structural adequacy for the licensed permissions, and soundness in the original categorized architecture. Those results make the proposal mathematically substantive enough to stand as a first paper in its own right.
