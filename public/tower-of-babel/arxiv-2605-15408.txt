Second-order moment equivalence of twisted Gaussian Schell model beams and orbital angular momentum eigenmodes
T. Ferreira, G. Santos, S. Ayala, Lucas Hutter, E. S. Gómez, G. Lima, G. Cañas, S. P. Walborn

Abstract
We show that the covariance matrix of any cylindrically symmetric coherent orbital angular momentum (OAM) eigenmode with quantum number ℓ \ell takes a universal form depending only on ⟨ r 2 ⟩ \langle r^{2}\rangle , ⟨ k r 2 ⟩ \langle k_{r}^{2}\rangle , and ℓ \ell , independently of the radial profile, and that this form is identical to the covariance matrix of a twisted Gaussian Schell-model (TGSM) beam. More specifically, both matrices share the same pattern of zero and nonzero entries, with the off-diagonal blocks proportional to ℓ \ell and the TGSM twist parameter u u , respectively. This result holds for an arbitrary radial profile and provides direct term-by-term identification of parameters between the two sets of beams. We work out the correspondence in detail for three important families: Laguerre–Gaussian (LG), Bessel–Gaussian, and perfect vortex beams (PVBs), and derive the conditions under which each coherent OAM mode maps onto a physically realizable TGSM beam. Because the covariance matrix governs second-moment evolution under arbitrary ABCD (symplectic) transformations, any two beams sharing the same covariance matrix are second-order indistinguishable at every propagation plane. In particular, the matched TGSM and coherent OAM beams share identical beam-width evolution, far-field divergence, and M 2 M^{2} beam-quality factor. In particular, the well-developed TGSM propagation toolbox applies directly to the second-order moment evolution of the three coherent families. We further show that within each beam family the covariance matrix uniquely determines the beam parameters, with exact uniqueness established for LG modes. Additional results include cross-family second-moment equivalence conditions and a proof that PVB modes form a complete orthonormal basis in the limit w → 0 w\to 0 .

II
Twisted Gaussian Schell Beams

The TGSM describes gaussian beams with cross spectral density

\begin{split}\Gamma(\mathbf{r}_{1},\mathbf{r}_{2})\propto\exp\!\Bigg[&-\frac{(\mathbf{r}_{1}+\mathbf{r}_{2})^{2}}{4\sigma^{2}}-\frac{(\mathbf{r}_{1}-\mathbf{r}_{2})^{2}}{2\delta^{2}}\\
&+iku\,(\mathbf{r}_{1}\times\mathbf{r}_{2})_{z}\Bigg]\;.\end{split}

(2)

Here
\sigma
is the beam waist,
\delta
is the transverse coherence length, and the real parameter
u
, known as the “twist phase”, controls the amount of
correlation-induced rotation.

In a transverse plane located at longitudinal position
z
, we can define the vector of cartesian coordinates of the transverse position (
x,y
) and wave vector (
k_{x},k_{y}
) as
\boldsymbol{\xi}=(x,k_{x},y,k_{y})
. In this coordinate system, the TGSM describes a gaussian beam with a covariance matrix given by
Simon and Mukunda 1993
;
Simon and Mukunda 1998

T=\begin{pmatrix}\sigma^{2}&-\frac{k\sigma^{2}}{R_{c}}&0&ku\sigma^{2}\\
-\frac{k\sigma^{2}}{R_{c}}&\tau^{2}&-ku\sigma^{2}&0\\
0&-ku\sigma^{2}&\sigma^{2}&-\frac{k\sigma^{2}}{R_{c}}\\
ku\sigma^{2}&0&-\frac{k\sigma^{2}}{R_{c}}&\tau^{2}\\
\end{pmatrix},

(3)

where
\tau^{2}=\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}+\frac{1}{4\sigma^{2}}+k^{2}\left(\frac{\sigma^{2}}{R_{c}^{2}}+u^{2}\sigma^{2}\right)
is the variance of the wave vector distribution, with
R_{c}
representing the radius of curvature. For the CM above to represent a bonafide TGSM beam, the twist phase must satisfy
Simon and Mukunda 1998

\lvert u\rvert\leq\frac{1}{k\delta^{2}}.

(4)

By calculating
\langle L_{z}\rangle=\langle xk_{y}\rangle-\langle yk_{x}\rangle
, using units in which
\hbar=1
so that
p_{j}=k_{j}
, we see that TGSM beams carry an average orbital angular momentum (OAM) of
2ku\sigma^{2}
per photon.

The propagation of the beams’ second moments through ABCD systems can be calculated from their covariance matrices in a single transverse plane. For simplicity, we take this plane to be
z=0
, where the radius of curvature
R_{c}
tends to infinity, causing the terms proportional to
\frac{1}{R_{c}}
to vanish.

III
Covariance matrix of Cylindrically Symmetric Eigenmodes

Let us now consider a paraxial optical beam with cylindrical symmetry, such that it be written in the form (

\frac{\ell}{2}&0&0&\frac{\langle k_{r}^{2}\rangle}{2}\end{pmatrix}.

(5)

All calculations are shown in Appendix
VI
. Here

\langle r^{2}\rangle={2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{3}|\mathcal{U}(r)|^{2}dr

(6)

and

\langle k_{r}^{2}\rangle={2\pi}\ \int_{0}^{\infty}\left[r\left(\frac{d\mathcal{U}}{dr}\right)^{2}+\frac{\ell^{2}}{r}\mathcal{U}^{2}\right]dr\;,

(7)

where in general the beam divergence depends on
\ell

Vallone et al. 2016

3

Comparing the elements of these two covariance matrices, we see that we can identify the beam parameters of the TGSM with those of the OAM eigenmodes. We can associate the beam widths as

\sigma^{2}=\langle r^{2}\rangle/2,

(8)

\tau^{2}=\langle k_{r}^{2}\rangle/2,

(9)

the twist phase with the OAM number

u=\frac{\ell}{2k\sigma^{2}}=\frac{\ell}{k\langle r^{2}\rangle},

(10)

and the inverse square of the coherence length as

\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}=\tau^{2}-\frac{(1+\ell^{2})}{4\sigma^{2}}=\frac{\langle k_{r}^{2}\rangle}{2}-\frac{(1+\ell^{2})}{2\langle r^{2}\rangle}.

(11)

III.1
Second-order equivalence classes

Equation (
5
) establishes a universal covariance form: regardless of the
specific radial profile
\mathcal{U}(r)
, the entire second-order structure of any
cylindrically symmetric OAM eigenmode is captured by just three quantities,

\langle r^{2}\rangle
,
\langle k_{r}^{2}\rangle
, and
\ell

second-order equivalence relation: two beams

and

are

second-order equivalent

\psi_{1}\sim\psi_{2}
, if and only if

C_{1}=C_{2}
. Under symplectic (ABCD) propagation, the covariance matrix transforms
as
C\mapsto SCS^{T}
, so the equivalence relation is preserved at every propagation
plane: if
\psi_{1}\sim\psi_{2}
at
z=0
, then
\psi_{1}\sim\psi_{2}
for all
z
.
Consequently, all second-order observables, that is the beam width, far-field divergence,
M^{2}

factor, and OAM content, are identical for any two members of the same equivalence
class, independently of the detailed radial structure. The specific beam families
considered below (LG, PVB, BG) represent distinct equivalence classes, each
parametrized by their respective values of
(\langle r^{2}\rangle,\langle k_{r}^{2}\rangle,\ell)
. The TGSM beam matched to each class is identified by the
correspondence of Eqs. (
10
)–(
11

III.2
Laguerre-Gaussian Beams

An important set of optical beams with OAM and of the form (
1
) are the
the LG modes. They are labeled by their radial and azimuthal indices
p

\ell

\mathcal{U}_{p,\ell}(r)=C_{p,\ell}\frac{2^{\frac{\ell}{2}}r^{|\ell|}}{w_{0}^{|\ell|+1}}L_{p}^{|\ell|}\left(\frac{2r^{2}}{w_{0}^{2}}\right)e^{-r^{2}/w_{0}^{2}},

|\ell|

In appendix
VII
, we show that the CM of the LG beam is given by Eq. (
5

\langle r^{2}\rangle=\frac{w_{0}^{2}}{2}\left(2p+|\ell|+1\right)\text{, and}

(13)

and

\langle k_{r}^{2}\rangle=\frac{2}{w_{0}^{2}}\left(2p+|\ell|+1\right)\;.

(14)

Substituting these results in eqs. (
10
) and (

u=\frac{2\ell}{kw_{0}^{2}(N+1)}\text{, and}

\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}=\frac{4p^{2}+4p(|\ell|+1)+2|\ell|}{w_{0}^{2}(2p+|\ell|+1)}=\frac{N(N+2)-\ell^{2}}{w_{0}^{2}(N+1)}\;.

(16)

It now important to determine under which conditions these parameters satisfy the requirement given in Eq. (
4

16

III.3
Perfect Vortex Beams

PVBs are optical fields whose ring radius remains almost

III.4
Bessel-Gauss Beams

BG beams are optical fields formed by modulating an ideal (non-diffracting) Bessel beam with a Gaussian envelope, yielding a physically realizable beam that maintains a narrow central core and exhibits extended propagation invariance. The combination of their quasi-non-diffracting propagation, self-healing behavior and well-defined orbital angular momentum makes these beams attractive for free-space optical communication systems

They are of the form (

The three families represent three qualitatively different regimes of the

III.6
Parameter Determination from the Covariance Matrix Within a Mode Family

Although a single covariance matrix may represent both a TGSM beam and a coherent OAM beam, as shown above, it uniquely determines the TGSM beam through the parameters
\sigma
,
u
, and
\delta

Simon and Mukunda 1993
. Thus, two distinct TGSM beams cannot share the same CM. The LG, PVB and BG beams are non-gaussian functions, in general, and thus it is to be expected that they cannot be determined by the elements of the CM alone. However, somewhat surprisingly, we note here that the CM is indeed sufficient in some cases. First, for all beams of the form (
1
), the parameter
\ell
follows directly from the anti-diagonal elements of Eq. (
5
), so that only the remaining parameters need to be assessed:
w_{0}
and
p
for LG modes,
w
and
R
for PVBs, and
w_{b}
and
k_{b}
for BG beams.

For an LG beam, the radial parameter

can be determined from the product
\langle x^{2}\rangle\langle k_{x}^{2}\rangle
, as shown in Eq. (
18
). Since
\ell
is already fixed and

, this relation specifies

) or Eq. (
14

For both PVB and BG beams, the diagonal elements of the covariance matrix lead to a nonlinear system relating the beam parameters, which may admit multiple solutions. In the regime of interest, however, the dominant terms yield relations in which each parameter is approximately determined by a single second moment. For PVBs, Eqs. (
21
) and (
22
) give
\langle r^{2}\rangle\approx R^{2}
and
\langle k_{r}^{2}\rangle\approx\frac{1}{w^{2}}
. Similarly, for BG beams, Eqs. (

) and (
29

\langle r^{2}\rangle\approx\frac{w_{b}^{2}}{4}
and
\langle k_{r}^{2}\rangle\approx k_{b}^{2}
. Thus, within this approximation, the covariance matrix determines the beam parameters to leading order for both families. A rigorous proof of exact uniqueness from the full, unapproximated moment equations is beyond the scope of this work. The leading-order inversions above are sufficient for practical parameter estimation in the regimes considered.

IV
Beam propagation and
M^{2}
factor equivalence

A direct consequence of the CM equivalence is that the full TGSM propagation toolbox applies to any matched coherent OAM beam. Once the parameter identifications of Sec.
III
are made, all TGSM results for beam-width evolution, Rayleigh range, and far-field divergence under arbitrary ABCD systems carry over without further calculation. For a beam propagating in free space, the covariance matrix
evolves as
C(z)=S(z)\,C(0)\,S(z)^{T}
, where
S(z)
is the symplectic transfer matrix
for free-space propagation a distance
z

Deng et al. 2004
.
Because two beams with the same
C(0)
are mapped by the same
S(z)
, they have the same
C(z)
at every plane. In particular, they share
the same beam-width evolution, the same Rayleigh range, and the same far-field divergence angle.

At the reference plane
z=0
, chosen such that the position–momentum correlation vanishes, a compact measure of this propagation equivalence is the beam-quality parameter

M^{2}=\frac{\pi\sigma_{w}(0)\theta_{\infty}}{\lambda}\,,

(39)

where
\sigma_{w}(z)=\sqrt{2\langle r^{2}\rangle(z)}
is the second-moment beam radius and
\theta_{\infty}=\sqrt{2\langle k_{r}^{2}\rangle(0)/k^{2}}
is the far-field divergence half-angle, with both definitions holding for cylindrically symmetric beams. Substituting these expressions into Eq. (
39
) and using
k=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}
, we obtain

M^{2}=\sqrt{\langle r^{2}\rangle(0)\,\langle k_{r}^{2}\rangle(0)}\,,

(40)

which is invariant under symplectic transformations.
This definition follows the second-moment convention of Refs.
Simon and Mukunda 1993
;
Simon and Mukunda 1998
, where
M^{2}=1
for a fundamental Gaussian beam.

V
Conclusion

In this work we established a correspondence between the covariance matrices of twisted Gaussian–Schell model beams and those of coherent OAM-carrying beams, including Laguerre–Gaussian, Bessel–Gaussian, and perfect vortex beams, showing that these matrices share the same structural form. As a consequence, the covariance matrix alone may not uniquely identify the family to which an OAM beam belongs. More broadly, Eq. (
5
) defines a second-order equivalence relation
on cylindrically symmetric OAM beams: two such beams are second-order equivalent if
and only if they share the same values of
\langle r^{2}\rangle
,
\langle k_{r}^{2}\rangle
, and
\ell
, and this equivalence is preserved under all symplectic
propagation. The coherent beam families studied here and the matched TGSM beams
belong to the same equivalence classes in this sense. In particular, within the regimes considered here, a single covariance matrix can represent Bessel–Gaussian and perfect vortex beams, as well as high-order Laguerre–Gaussian modes. We also examined the conditions under which the same covariance matrix can describe both a TGSM beam and a coherent OAM beam. Because two beams sharing a covariance matrix are mapped identically by any symplectic (ABCD) transformation, they exhibit the same beam-width evolution, Rayleigh range, far-field divergence, and
M^{2}
beam-quality factor. This expands the experimental flexibility in situations where only the beam’s orbital angular momentum and spreading are relevant, and the detailed radial structure of the field is not important. Conversely, the correspondence provides a prescription for designing a partially coherent TGSM beam that mimics the propagation and OAM content of any specified coherent OAM eigenstate, without reproducing its full field structure.
We further examined the invertibility of the covariance matrix within each beam family. Surprisingly, for LG modes, the beam parameters are uniquely and exactly determined by the second-order moments. For PVB and BG beams, the CM determines the beam parameters to leading order in the relevant approximation regime, though a proof of exact uniqueness from the full moment equations remains an open question.
We also showed in the appendix that the PVB modes form a complete orthonormal basis in the narrow-ring
limit (
w\to 0
). The practical condition
for the PVB expansion to faithfully represent a given field is
w\ll\lambda_{\min}
,
where
\lambda_{\min}
is the shortest transverse length scale of the field,
with the OAM spectrum being reproduced exactly for any
w
. In addition to making a fundamental connection between partially coherent TGSM beams and coherent OAM eigenmodes, we expect our results to be useful in structured-light applications.

The second derivatives are

xk_{x}

Using the derivative (

\begin{split}\langle xk_{x}\rangle=&-i\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{2}\,\mathcal{U}(r)\,\Bigg[\int_{0}^{2\pi}\bigg(\cos^{2}\phi\frac{d\,\mathcal{U}(r)}{dr}\\

For physically admissible beams,
\mathcal{U}(r)
does not diverge for

and decays sufficiently fast as

VI.7
Fourier Transform

The two-dimensional Fourier transform of a function
f(x,y)
in cartesian coordinates is given by:

F(k_{x},k_{y})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\int f(x,y)e^{-i(k_{x}x+k_{y}y)}\,dx\,dy.

(67)

In polar coordinates, with:

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=r\cos\phi,\quad y=r\sin\phi,

\displaystyle k_{x}

\displaystyle=k_{r}\cos\theta,\quad k_{y}=k_{r}\sin\theta,

(68)

the Fourier transform of a function of the form given in Eq. (
41
) becomes:

F(k_{r},\theta)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathcal{U}(r)e^{i\ell\phi}e^{-ik_{r}r\cos(\phi-\theta)}r\,d\phi\,dr.

(69)

Using the Jacobi–Anger expansion:

e^{-ik_{r}r\cos(\phi-\theta)}=\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}(-i)^{m}J_{m}(k_{r}r)e^{im(\phi-\theta)},

(70)

where
J_{m}(k_{r}r)
is the Bessel function of the first kind of order
m
, we can write the angular integral as:

I=\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{i\ell\phi}\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}(-i)^{m}J_{m}(k_{r}r)e^{im(\phi-\theta)}d\phi.

(71)

Interchanging summation and integration, we have

I=\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}(-i)^{m}J_{m}(k_{r}r)\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{i(\ell+m)\phi}e^{-im\theta}d\phi.

(72)

Using the orthogonality condition:

\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{i(\ell+m)\phi}d\phi=2\pi\delta_{\ell,-m},

(73)

this simplifies to:

I=2\pi(-i)^{-\ell}J_{-\ell}(k_{r}r)e^{i\ell\theta}.

(74)

Noting
J_{-n}(x)=(-1)^{n}J_{n}(x)
for
n\in\mathds{Z}
, we obtain:

I=2\pi(-i)^{\ell}J_{\ell}(k_{r}r)e^{i\ell\theta}.

(75)

Substituting this result back into the Fourier transform gives

F(k_{r},\theta)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{U}(r)\cdot(-i)^{\ell}J_{\ell}(k_{r}r)e^{i\ell\theta}r\,dr\;.

(76)

Defining the Hankel transform of order
\ell
:

\mathcal{H}_{\ell}(k_{r})=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{U}(r)J_{\ell}(k_{r}r)\,r\,dr\;,

(77)

we arrive at the final result:

F(k_{r},\theta)=(-i)^{\ell}e^{i\ell\theta}U_{\ell}(k_{r}).

(78)

This shows that the Fourier transform preserves the azimuthal phase structure
e^{i\ell\theta}
and the radial dependence follows a Hankel transform.

From the normalization condition of

[Eq. (

, which results in

\frac{1}{A^{2}}\approx 2\pi\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{\sqrt{2}}Rw\;.

(104)

VIII.2
Expectation Value of
x^{2}

Substituting the approximate PVB expression from Eq.(
92
) into Eq.(
59
), we find

\langle x^{2}\rangle=\pi A^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{3}e^{-\frac{2(r-R)^{2}}{w^{2}}}\,dr\;.

(105)

Using the change of variable
v=r-R
:

\langle x^{2}\rangle=\pi A^{2}\int_{-R}^{\infty}\left(v^{3}+3Rv^{2}+3R^{2}v+R^{3}\right)e^{-\frac{2v^{2}}{w^{2}}}dv\;.

(106)

Using results (
94
)-(
101
), we obtain:

\begin{split}\langle x^{2}\rangle=\,\pi A^{2}\Biggl[&\frac{\sqrt{\pi}Rw}{8\sqrt{2}}\left(1+\text{erf}\left(\frac{\sqrt{2}R}{w}\right)\right)\left(3w^{2}+4R^{2}\right)\\
&+\frac{w^{2}}{8}e^{-\frac{2R^{2}}{w^{2}}}\left(2R^{2}+w^{2}\right)\Biggr]\;.\end{split}

(107)

In the regime
\frac{R}{w}\gg 1
, we may approximate this result as

\langle x^{2}\rangle\approx\pi A^{2}\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\,Rw}{4\sqrt{2}}(4R^{2}+3w^{2})\;.

(108)

Finally, substituting
A^{2}\approx\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,\pi\,Rw}
[Eq. (
104
)], we arrive at

\langle x^{2}\rangle\approx\frac{1}{2}\left(R^{2}+\frac{3w^{2}}{4}\right)\;.

(109)

VIII.3
Expectation Value of
r

Using the approximate PVB form given in Eq. (
92
), the expectation value of
r
can be written as

\langle r\rangle=2\pi A^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{2}e^{-\frac{2(r-R)^{2}}{w^{2}}}dr\;.

(110)

Making the substitution
v=r-R
, we obtain

\langle r\rangle=2\pi A^{2}\int_{-R}^{\infty}\left(v^{2}+2Rv+R^{2}\right)e^{-\frac{2v^{2}}{w^{2}}}dv\;.

(111)

Using Eqs. (
94
)-(
99
), we find

\begin{split}\langle r\rangle=2\pi A^{2}\Biggl[&\frac{\sqrt{\pi}Rw}{2\sqrt{2}}\left(1+\text{erf}\left(\frac{\sqrt{2}R}{w}\right)\right)\left(R+\frac{w^{2}}{4R}\right)\\
&+\frac{w^{2}}{4}e^{-\frac{2R^{2}}{w^{2}}}R\Biggr]\;.\end{split}

(112)

Since
\frac{R}{w}\gg 1
, this expression can be approximated as

\langle r\rangle\approx 2\pi A^{2}\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\,Rw}{\sqrt{2}}\left(R+\frac{w^{2}}{4R}\right)\;.

(113)

Finally, inserting the previously determined normalization constant from Eq. (
104
), we obtain:

\langle r\rangle\approx R+\frac{w^{2}}{4R}\;.

(114)

VIII.4
Expectation value of
k_{x}^{2}

Inserting the approximate PVB expression from Eq. (
92
) into Eq. (
64
) and using

\frac{d\,\mathcal{U}(r)}{dr}=-\frac{2A}{w^{2}}(r-R)e^{-\frac{(r-R)^{2}}{w^{2}}}\;,

(115)

we obtain

\langle k_{x}^{2}\rangle=\pi A^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{4r}{w^{4}}(r-R)^{2}e^{-\frac{2(r-R)^{2}}{w^{2}}}+\frac{\ell^{2}}{r}e^{-\frac{2(r-R)^{2}}{w^{2}}}\right)dr\;.

(116)

A comparison of this integral with those given in Eqs. (
105
) and (
110
), together with

\left\langle\frac{1}{r^{2}}\right\rangle=2\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{r}|\mathcal{U}(r)|^{2}\,dr\;,

(117)

allows us to express
\langle k_{x}^{2}\rangle
in terms of other expectation values:

\langle k_{x}^{2}\rangle=\frac{4}{w^{4}}\left(\langle x^{2}\rangle-R\langle r\rangle+\frac{R^{2}}{2}\right)+\frac{\ell^{2}}{2}\left\langle\frac{1}{r^{2}}\right\rangle\;.

(118)

Using Eqs. (
109
) and (
114
),this becomes:

\langle k_{x}^{2}\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{w^{2}}+\ell^{2}\left\langle\frac{1}{r^{2}}\right\rangle\right)\;.

(119)

Introducing the parameter
\delta=\frac{R-r}{R}
,we write

\frac{1}{r^{2}}=\frac{1}{R^{2}}\frac{1}{(1-\delta)^{2}}\;.

(120)

The Maclaurin expansion of
\frac{1}{(1-\delta)^{2}}
reads

\frac{1}{(1-\delta)^{2}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(n+1)\delta^{n}=1+2\delta+3\delta^{2}+...\;.

(121)

Because
R\gg w
, the dominant contributions to the integral arise from values of
r
close to
R
, implying
\delta\ll 1
. For this reason, we can approximate
\frac{1}{r^{2}}\approx\frac{1}{R^{2}}
, and the expectation value becomes

\langle k_{x}^{2}\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{w^{2}}+\frac{\ell^{2}}{R^{2}}\right)\;.

(122)

IX
Appendix: On Perfect Vortex Beams as a mode basis

The PVBs are distinct from other mode families (such as the
LGs
) in that they are described by the discrete OAM winding number
\ell
, and the continuous parameter
R
relating to the radial ring radius. Here we derive the conditions in which the PVBs can be considered as a mode basis.

For convenience, we remember from (
19
) that the PVB modes are defined as

\Phi_{\ell,R}(r,\phi)=C_{0}\,e^{i\ell\phi}\,e^{-\frac{r^{2}+R^{2}}{w^{2}}}\,I_{\ell}\!\left(\frac{2Rr}{w^{2}}\right),

(123)

The modes
\Phi_{\ell,R}
defined in Eq. (
123
) with prefactor
C_{0}
are
not
individually normalized. Their norm, obtained by applying Eq. (
128
) with
\beta=\gamma=2R/w^{2}
and
\alpha=2/w^{2}
, is

\|\Phi_{\ell,R}\|^{2}=\frac{1}{w^{2}}\,e^{-R^{2}/w^{2}}\,I_{\ell}\!\left(\frac{R^{2}}{w^{2}}\right),

(124)

which depends on both
R
and
\ell
. The value of
C_{0}
is therefore
not
the per-mode normalization constant
A
of Appendix
VIII
; instead it is fixed entirely by the resolution-of-identity condition (see below), yielding
C_{0}=\sqrt{2/\pi}/w^{2}
.

The use of a uniform,
\ell
-independent prefactor
C_{0}
is a structural necessity of the completeness calculation, not merely a choice of convention. To establish completeness one must evaluate the sum
\sum_{\ell}e^{i\ell(\phi-\phi^{\prime})}I_{\ell}(rr^{\prime}/w^{2})
in closed form using the Bessel generating function
\sum_{\ell}e^{i\ell\varphi}I_{\ell}(z)=e^{z\cos\varphi}
. This identity requires the coefficient multiplying each
I_{\ell}
to be the same for all
\ell
. The exact per-mode normalization constant

N_{\ell}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi w^{2}e^{-R^{2}/w^{2}}I_{\ell}(R^{2}/w^{2})}}

(125)

carries
\ell
-dependence through the factor
I_{\ell}(R^{2}/w^{2})
, and its use in place of
C_{0}
would render the sum over
\ell
intractable. The constant
A
of Appendix
VIII
is the large-
R/w
approximation to
N_{\ell}
; in that regime
I_{\ell}(R^{2}/w^{2})\approx e^{R^{2}/w^{2}}/\sqrt{2\pi R^{2}/w^{2}}
is
\ell
-independent at leading order, and one recovers
A^{2}\approx 1/(\sqrt{2\pi}\,\pi\,Rw)
.

Orthogonality and overlaps

The inner product of two PVB modes is

\langle\Phi_{\ell,R}\,|\,\Phi_{\ell^{\prime},R^{\prime}}\rangle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\!\!d\phi\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!r\,dr\;\Phi_{\ell,R}^{*}(r,\phi)\,\Phi_{\ell^{\prime},R^{\prime}}(r,\phi).

(126)

Substituting Eq. (
123
) and separating angular and radial parts, the angular integral gives
2\pi\delta_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}
, so modes with different
azimuthal index are exactly orthogonal. The radial integral is then

|C_{0}|^{2}e^{-\frac{R^{2}+R^{\prime 2}}{w^{2}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}r\,e^{-\frac{2r^{2}}{w^{2}}}I_{\ell}\!\left(\frac{2Rr}{w^{2}}\right)I_{\ell}\!\left(\frac{2R^{\prime}r}{w^{2}}\right)dr,

(127)

which can be evaluated using the formula

Gradshteyn and Ryzhik 2007

\int_{0}^{\infty}r\,e^{-\alpha r^{2}}\,I_{\ell}(\beta r)\,I_{\ell}(\gamma r)\,dr=\frac{1}{2\alpha}\,e^{\frac{\beta^{2}+\gamma^{2}}{4\alpha}}\,I_{\ell}\!\left(\frac{\beta\gamma}{2\alpha}\right),\qquad

(128)

with
\mathrm{Re}(\alpha)>0,\;\ell>-1
.
The constraint
\ell>-1
can here be resolved using the reflection identity
I_{-n}(x)=I_{n}(x)
, which holds for all integer

n
, such that the integrand for order
-|\ell|
is identical to that for order

+|\ell|
. We can define
\alpha=2/w^{2}
,
\beta=2R/w^{2}
,
\gamma=2R^{\prime}/w^{2}
, giving

(\beta^{2}+\gamma^{2})/4\alpha=(R^{2}+R^{\prime 2})/2w^{2}
and

\beta\gamma/2\alpha=RR^{\prime}/w^{2}
.
The radial integral therefore equals

(w^{2}/4)\,\exp[(R^{2}+R^{\prime 2})/2w^{2}]\,I_{\ell}(RR^{\prime}/w^{2})
.
Inserting this result and fixing
C_{0}=\sqrt{2/\pi}/w^{2}
(see below), so that

\pi|C_{0}|^{2}w^{2}/2=1/w^{2}
, we obtain the overlap

\langle\Phi_{\ell,R}\,|\,\Phi_{\ell,R^{\prime}}\rangle=\frac{1}{w^{2}}\,e^{-\frac{R^{2}+R^{\prime 2}}{2w^{2}}}\,I_{\ell}\!\left(\frac{RR^{\prime}}{w^{2}}\right).

(129)

This is a smooth, positive function of
R
and
R^{\prime}
for all
R,R^{\prime}\geq 0
:
modes of the same
\ell
but different
R
are
not
orthogonal in general. However, it does not necessarily imply that the PVB modes do not form a basis, as is the case for the coherent states in quantum optics
Perelomov 1986
.
It does mean, however, that if the PVB family turns out to span the space, it does so
with redundancy rather than as a minimal basis. We investigate this question
in the next subsection.

Two further remarks on Eq. (
129
) are worth noting.
First, it has the same functional form as the PVB radial
profile (
123
) itself. That is, with
r\to R
,
R\to R^{\prime}
, and

w^{2}\to w^{2}/2
, the overlap function is itself a PVB amplitude evaluated at

r=R
.
Second, in the limit
w\to 0
, we have

I_{\ell}(x)\sim e^{x}/\sqrt{2\pi x}
for
x=RR^{\prime}/w^{2}\to\infty
, which gives

\langle\Phi_{\ell,R}\,|\,\Phi_{\ell,R^{\prime}}\rangle\xrightarrow{\;w\,\to\,0\;}\frac{\delta(R-R^{\prime})}{R},

(130)

where we used

(1/w\sqrt{2\pi})\exp[-(R-R^{\prime})^{2}/2w^{2}]\to\delta(R-R^{\prime})

and
\sqrt{RR^{\prime}}\to R
at
R^{\prime}=R
.
With the natural measure
R\,dR
on the radial space, this gives

\int_{0}^{\infty}\langle\mathcal{U}_{\ell,R}|\mathcal{U}_{\ell,R^{\prime}}\rangle R^{\prime}\,dR^{\prime}\to 1
as
w\to 0
: the modes become orthonormal with respect to the
measure
R\,dR
only in this limit.

Finally, we note that the overlap (
129
) depends on the envelope parameter
w

in a physically transparent way. In the limit
w\to 0
the family approaches an
orthonormal set, while for large
w
the PVB radial ring
is broad, the overlaps are strong, and the family is far from orthogonal.
This
w
-dependence is qualitatively different from the quantum-optical coherent states
Perelomov 1986
, for which the
overlap
|\langle\alpha|\beta\rangle|^{2}=e^{-|\alpha-\beta|^{2}}
is independent
of any tunable parameter and never vanishes: the non-orthogonality of coherent
states is irreducible, whereas for PVBs it is controlled by a physical parameter
— the ring width — and can be made arbitrarily small.

Completeness

We now evaluate

K=\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}R\,dR\;\Phi_{\ell,R}(r,\phi)\,\Phi_{\ell,R}^{*}(r^{\prime},\phi^{\prime}),

(131)

which should give

K=\delta^{(2)}(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}),

(132)

if the PVBs form a complete basis.
Writing out the left-hand side and separating the
R
-dependence:

\displaystyle K=

\displaystyle|C_{0}|^{2}\,e^{-\frac{r^{2}+r^{\prime 2}}{w^{2}}}\sum_{\ell}e^{i\ell(\phi-\phi^{\prime})}\times

(133)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}R\,e^{-\frac{2R^{2}}{w^{2}}}I_{\ell}\!\left(\frac{2Rr}{w^{2}}\right)I_{\ell}\!\left(\frac{2Rr^{\prime}}{w^{2}}\right)dR.

The
R
-integral has exactly the same form as the
r
-integral in the
overlap calculation, with
r\leftrightarrow R
,
R\leftrightarrow r
, and

R^{\prime}\leftrightarrow r^{\prime}
. Applying Eq. (
128
) with

\alpha=2/w^{2}
,
\beta=2r/w^{2}
,
\gamma=2r^{\prime}/w^{2}
gives

\frac{w^{2}}{4}\,e^{\frac{r^{2}+r^{\prime 2}}{2w^{2}}}\,I_{\ell}\!\left(\frac{rr^{\prime}}{w^{2}}\right).

(134)

Substituting this into Eq. (
133
), we have

K=\frac{|C_{0}|^{2}w^{2}}{4}\,e^{-\frac{r^{2}+r^{\prime 2}}{2w^{2}}}\sum_{\ell}e^{i\ell(\phi-\phi^{\prime})}\,I_{\ell}\!\left(\frac{rr^{\prime}}{w^{2}}\right).

(135)

The remaining sum over
\ell
can be evaluated using the modified Bessel generating
function
\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{i\ell\varphi}\,I_{\ell}(z)=e^{z\cos\varphi}

Gradshteyn and Ryzhik 2007
.
With
z=rr^{\prime}/w^{2}
and
\varphi=\phi-\phi^{\prime}
, we have

\sum_{\ell}e^{i\ell(\phi-\phi^{\prime})}\,I_{\ell}\!\left(\frac{rr^{\prime}}{w^{2}}\right)=\exp\!\left(\frac{rr^{\prime}\cos(\phi-\phi^{\prime})}{w^{2}}\right).

(136)

Inserting Eq. (
136
) into Eq. (
135
) and
using
r^{2}+r^{\prime 2}-2rr^{\prime}\cos(\phi-\phi^{\prime})=|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|^{2}
, we arrive at

K=\frac{|C_{0}|^{2}w^{2}}{4}\,e^{-\frac{|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|^{2}}{2w^{2}}}=G_{w}(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}),

(137)

where the last equality fixes the normalisation constant via

|C_{0}|^{2}w^{2}/4=1/(2\pi w^{2})
, i.e.
C_{0}=\sqrt{2/\pi}/w^{2}
, and

G_{w}(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\equiv\frac{1}{2\pi w^{2}}\,e^{-\frac{|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|^{2}}{2w^{2}}}

(138)

is a normalised 2D Gaussian kernel.
The sequence
\{G_{w}\}_{w>0}
satisfies the standard criteria for a Dirac delta sequence
Arfken et al. 2013
.

Therefore
G_{w}\to\delta^{(2)}(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime})
distributionally as
w\to 0
, which implies that we can establish Eq. (
132
) only in that limit. This is physically natural: sharper ring profiles (
w\to 0
)
resolve finer radial structure, and in this limit the PVB family saturates the full

L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
space.

Practical validity of the PVB expansion

In practice, when
w
is finite, the completeness of the PVB “basis”
is not exact but approximate. Let us briefly discuss this fact and determine in what conditions a coherent field can be expanded in terms of PVBs. The PVB
expansion of any field
\psi(\mathbf{r})
is:

\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}R\,dR\;c_{\ell,R}\;\Phi_{\ell,R}(\mathbf{r})=\int G_{w}(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\,\psi(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\,d^{2}r^{\prime},

(139)

where
c_{\ell,R}=\langle\Phi_{\ell,R}|\psi\rangle
and
G_{w}
is defined in Eq. (
138
). This is not
\psi
itself but its
convolution with the Gaussian kernel
G_{w}
, which acts as a spatial filter.
In Fourier space, convolution with
G_{w}
multiplies each spatial-frequency
component by the transfer function
\widetilde{G}_{w}(\mathbf{k})=e^{-w^{2}k^{2}/2}
,
which suppresses transverse wavenumbers
k\gtrsim 1/w
. The PVB expansion
is therefore a faithful representation of
\psi
provided
\psi
contains no
significant structure on transverse scales smaller than
w
, i.e. provided

w\ll\lambda_{\min}(\psi),

(140)

where
\lambda_{\min}(\psi)
denotes the shortest transverse length scale present
in
\psi
.

We note from (
139
) that the OAM content of
\psi
is always faithfully represented.
This follows from the fact that the kernel
G_{w}(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime})
is isotropic. In Fourier space, the isotropic filter
e^{-w^{2}k^{2}/2}
does
not mix azimuthal harmonics. Therefore the OAM spectrum of
\psi
is reproduced exactly
by the PVB expansion for any
w>0
: the angular part of the resolution of identity is
exact (it follows from Fourier completeness on the circle, not from the
w\to 0

limit), and only the radial profile is affected by the finite-
w
smoothing.

Let us illustrate the discussion above by considering the PVB expansion of an LG mode.
Consider
\psi=\operatorname{LG}_{p,\ell_{0}}
with beam waist
w_{\psi}
. Its OAM index

\ell_{0}
is preserved exactly as noted above. Its radial profile is governed by the
associated Laguerre polynomial
L_{p}^{|\ell_{0}|}(2r^{2}/w_{\psi}^{2})
, which has
p
radial
nodes. For large
p
, the nodes are approximately separated by
\Delta r\sim w_{\psi}/\sqrt{2p}
, following the asymptotic spacing of Laguerre polynomial zeros, so the finest radial scale is
\lambda_{\min}=w_{\psi}/\sqrt{2p}
.
The convolution

(G_{w}*\operatorname{LG}_{p,\ell_{0}})
smooths over these nodes and the
reconstruction degrades once
w\gtrsim w_{\psi}/\sqrt{2p}
. The condition for a
faithful expansion is therefore

w\;\ll\;\frac{w_{\psi}}{\sqrt{2p}}\,,

(141)

which reduces to
w\ll w_{\psi}
for the pure-vortex case
p=0
, in which no radial
nodes are present and only the overall beam scale must be resolved.
Equation (
141
) shows that higher radial order demands
proportionally sharper PVB rings. In the opposite limit
w\gg w_{\psi}/\sqrt{2p}
the
reconstructed field retains the correct OAM but its radial structure is washed out,
leaving only the envelope
e^{-r^{2}/w_{\psi}^{2}}
modulated by
e^{i\ell_{0}\theta}
.

Analytic expansion coefficients for
p=0
LG modes

For the pure-vortex case
p=0
, the expansion coefficients
c_{\ell,R}=\langle\Phi_{\ell,R}|\psi\rangle
with
\psi=\operatorname{LG}_{0,\ell_{0}}
can be evaluated in closed form. Restricting to
\ell=\ell_{0}
(the only non-vanishing azimuthal sector, by the angular orthogonality established above), we insert the LG mode

\mathcal{U}_{0,\ell_{0}}(r)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi\,|\ell_{0}|!}}\,\frac{1}{w_{\psi}}\left(\frac{\sqrt{2}\,r}{w_{\psi}}\right)^{|\ell_{0}|}e^{-r^{2}/w_{\psi}^{2}}

(142)

into the overlap integral and perform the radial integration using Eq. 6.631-1 of Ref.
Gradshteyn and Ryzhik 2007
,

\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\nu+1}e^{-\alpha x^{2}}I_{\nu}(\beta x)\,dx=\frac{\beta^{\nu}}{(2\alpha)^{\nu+1}}\exp\!\left(\frac{\beta^{2}}{4\alpha}\right),

(143)

valid for
\mathrm{Re}(\alpha)>0
,
\mathrm{Re}(\nu)>-1
. Setting
\nu=|\ell_{0}|
,
\alpha=(w_{\psi}^{-2}+w^{-2})
, and
\beta=2R/w^{2}
, and collecting all prefactors, one obtains

c_{\ell_{0},R}=N_{\ell_{0}}\,\frac{2^{|\ell_{0}|/2}}{\sqrt{\pi\,|\ell_{0}|!}}\,\frac{R^{|\ell_{0}|}\,w_{\psi}^{|\ell_{0}|+1}\,w^{2}}{\left(w_{\psi}^{2}+w^{2}\right)^{|\ell_{0}|+1}}\exp\!\left(-\frac{R^{2}}{w_{\psi}^{2}+w^{2}}\right),

(144)

where the per-mode normalization constant
N_{\ell_{0}}
is given by Eq. (
125
). In the regime
R\gg w
the factor
N_{\ell_{0}}\approx(\sqrt{2\pi}\,R\,w)^{1/2}/w
(from the asymptotic form of
I_{\ell_{0}}
) becomes
\ell_{0}
-independent, and Eq. (
144
) simplifies accordingly.

A few features of Eq. (
144
) are worth noting. First, the coefficient is maximized at
R^{\star}=\sqrt{(|\ell_{0}|+1)(w_{\psi}^{2}+w^{2})/2}
, showing that the PVB ring radius that captures most of the LG mode’s radial weight grows with OAM order as
R^{\star}\propto\sqrt{|\ell_{0}|}
, consistent with the known scaling of the LG peak radius. Second, the Gaussian factor
\exp(-R^{2}/(w_{\psi}^{2}+w^{2}))
provides exponential suppression for rings much larger than
\sqrt{w_{\psi}^{2}+w^{2}}
, confirming that only a finite band of ring radii contributes significantly to the expansion. Third, Eq. (
144
) applies only to the
p=0
case; for
p\geq 1
, the associated Laguerre polynomial introduces additional radial structure and the integral does not reduce to a single closed-form expression of this type.

Substituting the expression for the BG mode given in (

) into Eq. (

) gives

\mathcal{H}_{\ell}(k_{r})=B\int_{0}^{\infty}J_{\ell}(k_{b}r)\,J_{\ell}(k_{r}r)\,e^{-r^{2}/w_{b}^{2}}\,r\,dr\;.

(145)

, we use the relation
J_{-m}(x)=(-1)^{m}J_{m}(x)

\mathcal{H}_{\ell}(k_{r})=B\int_{0}^{\infty}J_{|\ell|}(rk_{b})\,J_{|\ell|}(k_{r}r)\,e^{-r^{2}/w_{b}^{2}}\,r\,dr\;.

(146)

\mathcal{H}_{\ell}(k_{r})=B\frac{w_{b}^{2}}{2}e^{-\frac{\left(k_{r}^{2}+k_{b}^{2}\right)w_{b}^{2}}{4}}I_{|\ell|}\left(\frac{k_{r}k_{b}w_{b}^{2}}{2}\right)\;.

), we find that the Fourier transform of a BG beam is

F_{\ell}(k_{r},\theta)=(-i)^{\ell}B\,e^{i\ell\theta}\frac{w_{b}^{2}}{2}e^{-\frac{\left(k_{r}^{2}+k_{b}^{2}\right)w_{b}^{2}}{4}}I_{\ell}\left(\frac{k_{r}k_{b}w_{b}^{2}}{2}\right)\;.

(148)

Comparing this result with the PVB expression in Eq. (

. Therefore, there is a direct correspondence between their second moments. The results for BG beams can be obtained from those of the PVBs by applying the substitution
x\leftrightarrow k_{x}
with the appropriate change of variables. The regime
R\gg w
and
|\ell|\ll\frac{R}{w}
, used in the PVB calculations, corresponds here to
w_{b}\gg\frac{1}{k_{b}}
and

.

) and making the substitutions
