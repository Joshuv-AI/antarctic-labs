METTL16-mediated upregulation of LINC00665 inhibits the apoptosis of breast cancer via regulating miR-28-5p/GLG1 axis
Authors: Ya Zhou, Jie Ren, Xiaojuan Cai, Shiyu Lu, Hanbing Gu, Weizhe Xu, Xun Zhu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundLong non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) are intensively involved in the progression of breast cancer. This study aimed to investigate the potential of LINC00665 in breast cancer.MethodsRNA expression was determined by quantitative reverse transcriptase polymerase chain reaction. The binding sites between miR-28-5p and LINC00665/golgi glycoprotein 1 (GLG1) was predicted by Starbase3. The bindings sites were confirmed by luciferase assay. N6-methyladenosine (m6A) modification of LINC00665 was determined by methylated RNA immunoprecipitation assay. Cell viability was determined by Cell Counting Kit-8 assay. Cell migration was detected by wound healing assay. Cell invasion was detected by transwell assay. Cell apoptosis was detected using flow cytometry.ResultsWe found that methyltransferase-like 16 (METTL16)-mediated m6A modification drove the upregulation of LINC00665 in breast cancer. However, LINC00665 knockdown inhibited the proliferation, migration and invasion of breast cancer cells, as well as promoted cell apoptosis. Furthermore, LINC00665 sponged miR-28-5p to upregulate GLG1. GLG1 overexpression alleviated the effects of LINC00665 knockdown and mediated malignant behaviors of breast cancer cells.ConclusionsTaken together, LINC00665 promotes the progression of breast cancer via regulating miR-28-5p/GLG1 axis.

Introduction

Breast cancer ranks as the leading cause of cancer-related mortality among women worldwide (1). Despite significant advances in early detection and treatment, the prognosis for breast cancer patients with distant metastasis remains poor (2-4). This clinical challenge underscores the urgent need to uncover novel molecular mechanisms underlying breast cancer pathogenesis, particularly those governing apoptosis resistance, a hallmark of tumor progression and therapy evasion (5,6).

Methyltransferase-like 16 (METTL16), a recently characterized RNA methyltransferase, has emerged as a critical regulator of m6A modification (7). Unlike the well-studied METTL3/METTL14 complex, METTL16 is unique in its ability to methylate both message RNA (mRNA) and noncoding RNAs, including long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) (8,9). Accumulating evidence suggests that METTL16 is upregulated in multiple cancers, including breast cancer. For example, METTL16 promotes tumor growth and metastasis by stabilizing oncogenic transcripts such as glutathione peroxidase 4 and F-box protein 5 (10,11). However, the mechanisms by which METTL16-mediated m6A modification influences lncRNA function and apoptosis in breast cancer remain poorly understood.

lncRNAs are a class of noncoding RNAs at the length >200 nucleotides (12). Among these, LINC00665 has recently gained attention as a potent oncogenic lncRNA. LINC00665 overexpression is associated with larger tumor size, advanced tumor-node-metastasis stage, and poor overall survival (13). LINC00665 promotes breast cancer progression (14-16). Importantly, silencing of LINC00665 has been shown to inhibit proliferation, migration, and invasion. However, the upstream regulatory mechanisms controlling LINC00665 expression and stability—particularly via m6A modification—remain unexplored.

Recent studies have illuminated that m6A methylation can enhance the stability of oncogenic lncRNAs, such as prostate cancer-associated transcript 6, LINC00115, and metastasis associated lung adenocarcinoma transcript 1, thereby promoting breast cancer progression (17-19). These context-dependent effects suggest that the functional outcome of m6A modification is determined by the specific reader proteins involved and the cellular environment.

This study aimed to investigate the role of LINC00665 in breast cancer. Our findings reveal a novel METTL16/LINC00665/miR-28-5p/golgi glycoprotein 1 (GLG1) axis in breast cancer, providing a mechanistic link between RNA epigenetic modification and lncRNA-mediated aggressiveness and apoptosis resistance. Targeting this axis may offer a promising therapeutic strategy for breast cancer. We present this article in accordance with the MDAR reporting checklist (available at https://tcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tcr-2025-1-2774/rc).

Methods

Cell culture

Human mammary epithelial cells (MCF-10) and breast cancer cell lines (MDA-MB-231, MDA-MB-468, BT-549, Hs578T, and SUM-159) were provided by ATCC, USA. Cells were maintained in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle medium/F-12 (Gibco) containing 10 % fetal bovine serum (FBS; Gibco), 100 U/mL penicillin, and 100 µg/mL streptomycin at 37 ℃ in 5 % CO2.

Plasmid construction and site-directed mutagenesis

Full-length METTL16 (NM_024086) and LINC00665 (NR_110260.1) were PCR-amplified from MDA-MB-231 cDNA and cloned into pcDNA3.1(+) with HindIII/XhoI sites. For m6A-mutant LINC00665, the RRACH motif (R = G/A, H = A/C/U) at positions 417, 521, and 633 was mutated to GGACT → GGCCT using overlapping PCR. All constructs were verified by Sanger sequencing.

CRISPR/Cas9 stable knockdown

For stable knockdown of LINC00665, two independent shRNAs (sh1: 5'-GCAGAUAGUUGGACCUUAATT-3'; sh2: 5'-CCUGGAAACUUCAGAGAAATT-3') were cloned into pLKO.1-puro. Cells were transfected with LINC00665 shRNAs.

Locked-nucleic-acid (LNA) and miRNA mimics/inhibitors

miR-28-5p mimic (5'-AAGGAGCUCACAGUCUAUUGAGA-3'), inhibitor (5'-UCUCAAUAGACUGUGAGCUCCUU-3'), and negative-control RNA (NC) were synthesized with 2'-O-Me modifications. LNA-enhanced miR-28-5p probe for in-situ hybridization (ISH) was 5'-FAM-labeled (Exiqon). Transfection was performed using Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen, USA) at 50 nM final concentration for 48 h unless otherwise stated.

Methylated RNA immunoprecipitation (MeRIP)

Total RNA (100 µg) was fragmented (150 nt) and immunoprecipitated with 5 µg anti-m6A antibody (202-003; Synaptic Systems, USA) or control IgG. RNA was extracted with TRIzol and analyzed by quantitative reverse transcriptase polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR).

RNA extraction, reverse transcription, and qRT-PCR

Total RNA was extracted with TRIzol (Invitrogen). Cytoplasmic/nuclear fractions were separated using the PARIS kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). For mRNA/lncRNA, 1 µg RNA was reverse-transcribed with random hexamers using PrimeScript RT Master Mix (Takara, Japan). For miRNA, 500 ng RNA was poly-adenylated and reverse-transcribed using the miScript II RT Kit (Qiagen, USA). qPCR was performed on a QuantStudio 6 Flex (Applied Biosystems, USA) using SYBR Green (Takara) or TaqMan probes (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Relative expression was calculated by 2−ΔΔCt normalized to β-actin (mRNA/lncRNA) or U6 snRNA (miRNA). Melting-curve analysis and agarose-gel electrophoresis confirmed single products. Efficiency (90–105%) was validated by standard curves (R2≥0.99).

RNA stability assay

Cells were treated with 5 µg/mL actinomycin D (ActD) (SBR00013; Sigma-Aldrich, Germany) and harvested at 0, 2, 4, 6, 8, and 12 h. Total RNA was extracted and LINC00665 levels quantified by qRT-PCR. Half-life (t1/2) was calculated using one-phase exponential decay.

Luciferase reporter assay

The wild-type (WT) LINC00665/GLG1 fragment containing three putative miR-28-5p binding sites was cloned into psiCHECK-2 (Promega, USA) downstream of Renilla luciferase (Rluc). Seed-region mutations were introduced using the Q5 kit. For m6A-loss reporters, A → G mutations were introduced at the m6A sites within each binding site. Cells (2×104 per well, 96-well plate) were co-transfected with 100 ng reporter and 50 nM miR-28-5p mimic or NC. After 24 h, dual-luciferase activity was measured (Promega, USA).

Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) assay

After transfection, cells were seeded into a 96-well plate and cultured for 48 h. Then cells were added with CCK-8 solutions. Finally, the optic values were analyzed using a microplate reader (BD Biosciences, USA) at the wavelength of 450 nm.

Wound-healing migration assay

Cells (5×105 per well) were seeded in 6-well plates and grown to 100% confluence. A sterile 200-µL pipette tip created a vertical scratch. After PBS washing to remove debris, cells were cultured in serum-free medium (0.5% FBS) to minimize proliferation. Images were captured at 0 and 24 h using an Olympus IX73 microscope (×100) (Olympus, Japan). Wound area was quantified with ImageJ (MRI Wound-Healing Tool).

Transwell invasion assays

For invasion, 8-µm pore polycarbonate inserts (Corning, USA) were pre-coated with 60 µL Matrigel (1:8 in serum-free medium, BD Biosciences) and solidified (37 ℃, 4 h); 600 µL complete medium (10% FBS) served as chemoattractant. After 36 h, non-invading cells were removed with cotton swabs. Invaded cells on the lower surface were fixed, stained, and photographed. Cell counts were averaged and normalized to control.

Apoptosis assay

Cells were harvested and resuspended in 100 µL binding buffer. After incubated with 5 µL annexin V-FITC and PI in shade, apoptosis rates were analyzed using a flow cytometry was conducted on a BD FACSCanto II (10,000 events) (BD Biosciences).

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using GraphPad Prism 9.5.1 and presented as mean ± standard deviation. Comparisons between two groups were performed using two-tailed unpaired Student’s t-test. Multiple groups were analyzed by one-way or two-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey’s post-hoc test. P<0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

METTL16 drove m6A modification of LINC00665 in breast cancer

m6A modification is the most abundant form of RNA modification in eukaryotic cells (20). Figure 1A showed the m6A sites of LINC00665. We found that there were 4 sites at very high confidence. To confirm the m6A modification of LINC00665, MeRIP was performed. We found that the enrichment of LINC00665 was significantly increased in anti-m6A group (Figure 1B). Online database GEPIA showed that METTL16 and LINC00665 expression in breast cancer patients was in positive correlation (Figure 1C). To confirm this, MCF-7 cells were transfected with METTL16 shRNAs and overexpression plasmids. As shown in Figure 1D, LINC00665 expression was significantly increased in breast cancer cells, especially in MCF-7 cells. LINC00665 expression was significantly reduced by METTL16 shRNAs, especially sh2# (Figure 1E). METTL16 overexpression significantly upregulated LINC00665 expression (Figure 1F). Furthermore, METTL16 overexpression significantly increased the enrichment of LINC00665 (Figure 1G), whereas METTL16 knockdown exerted opposite effect. METTL16 knockdown significantly inhibited the RNA stability of LINC00665 compared with shNC group (Figure 1H).

(C) The correlation between METTL16 and LINC00665 was analyzed using GEPIA. (D-F) LINC00665 in breast cancer cells were analyzed using qRT-PCR. (G) m6A modification of LINC00665 in MCF-7 cells transfected with METTL16 overexpression plasmids or shRNA was determined using MeRIP assay. (H) LINC00665 RNA stability in MCF-7 cells was determined using ActD assay. **, P<0.01; ***, P<0.001 vs. anti-IgG group, MCF10A group, shNC group, vector group; ##, P<0.01 vs. vector group; &&, P<0.01 vs. shNC group. MeRIP, methylated RNA immunoprecipitation; qRT-PCR, quantitative reverse transcriptase polymerase chain reaction.

LINC00665 knockdown inhibited the migration and invasion of breast cancer

To verify the role of LINC00665 in breast cancer, MCF-7 cells were transfected with LINC00665 shRNAs. Sh1 showed more remarkable efficiency (Figure 2A), which was used in further study. LINC00665 knockdown significantly inhibited the cell viability of MCF-7 cells (Figure 2B). LINC00665 knockdown significantly reduced the migration of MCF-7 cells (Figure 2C,2D). Furthermore, LINC00665 knockdown significantly reduced the number of invaded cells (Figure 2E,2F).

(C,D) Cell migration was determined using wound healing assay. (E,F) Cell invasion was determined using transwell assay. **, P<0.01; ***, P<0.001 vs. shNC group. CCK-8, Cell Counting Kit-8; qRT-PCR, quantitative reverse transcriptase polymerase chain reaction.

LINC00665 knockdown promoted the apoptosis of breast cancer

As shown in Figure 3A,3B, LINC00665 knockdown significantly increased the apoptosis rates of MCF-7 cells (Figure 3A,3B). Furthermore, LINC00665 knockdown significantly decreased the mRNA expression of apoptosis inhibitor Bcl-2 (Figure 3C), whereas upregulating BCL2 associated X (BAX) and Caspase3 (Figure 3D,3E).

LINC00665 knockdown promoted the apoptosis of breast cancer. (A,B) The apoptosis rates of MCF-7 cells were analyzed using flow cytometry. (C-E) The mRNA of apoptosis markers in MCF-7 cells was analyzed using qRT-PCR. ***, P<0.001 vs. shNC group. qRT-PCR, quantitative reverse transcriptase polymerase chain reaction.

LINC00665 targeted miR-28-5p

lncRNA, without protein-binding ability, is involved in regulating biological processes via targeting miRNAs. Starbase3.0 was used to predict the potential target miRNAs. We found that LINC00665 could bind to miR-28-5p (Figure 4A). To confirm this, the binding sites were mutated. We found that miR-28-5p mimic significantly reduced luciferase activities (Figure 4B), whereas miR-28-5p inhibitor increasing luciferase activities (Figure 4C). miR-28-5p expression was significantly reduced in breast cancer patients and cells (Figure 4D,4E). Furthermore, miR-28-5p expression was significantly downregulated by LINC00665 overexpression (Figure 4F), whereas upregulated by LINC00665 knockdown (Figure 4G). These results suggest that LINC00665 can directly target miR-28-5p.

miR-28-5p targeted GLG1

Figure 5A showed the binding sites between miR-28-5p and GLG1 (Figure 5A). Co-transfection with GLG1 3'UTR WT and miR-28-5p mimic significantly reduced luciferase activities (Figure 5B). However, co-transfection with GLG1 3'UTR WT and miR-28-5p inhibitor significantly increased the luciferase activities (Figure 5C). Breast cancer patients with low levels of GLG1 showed better overall survival rates compared with those with high levels of GLG1 (Figure 5D). GLG1 mRNA expression was significantly upregulated in breast cancer cells (Figure 5E). GLG1 mRNA expression was significantly downregulated by miR-28-5p mimic (Figure 5F). Furthermore, GLG1 mRNA expression was significantly upregulated by LINC00665 overexpression (Figure 5G), which was reversed by miR-28-5p mimic. These results suggest that LINC00665 upregulates GLG1 through sponging miR-28-5p.

GLG1 overexpression promoted the migration and invasion of breast cancer

Rescue assays were conducted to further verify the role of LINC00665-GLG1 axis in breast cancer. GLG1 mRNA expression was significantly upregulated by its overexpression plasmids (Figure 6A), suggesting that cells were successfully transfected. GLG1 overexpression significantly increased cell viability of MCF-7 cells compared with sh1 + vector group (Figure 6B). GLG1 overexpression significantly promoted the migration of MCF-7 cells compared with sh1 + vector group (Figure 6C,6D). Additionally, GLG1 overexpression significantly increased the number of invaded cells compared with sh1 + vector group (Figure 6E,6F).

GLG1 overexpression inhibited the apoptosis of breast cancer

As shown in Figure 7A,7B, GLG1 overexpression significantly reduced the apoptosis rates of MCF-7 cells compared with sh1 + vector group (Figure 7A,7B). Moreover, GLG1 overexpression significantly upregulated the mRNA expression of Bcl-2 compared with sh1 + vector group (Figure 7C), whereas downregulating BAX and Caspase3 (Figure 7D,7E).

Discussion

Non-coding RNAs play crucial roles in tumorigenesis (21). In this study, LINC00665 functioned as an oncogene in breast cancer. METTL16-mediated m6A modification and upregulation of LINC00665. Interestingly, LINC00665 knockdown inhibited the migration and invasion of breast cancer, as well as mediating tumor cell apoptosis. Furthermore, LINC00665 sponged miR-28-5p to upregulate GLG1, high expression of which was associated with poor prognosis of breast cancer patients. GLG1 overexpression contributed to the aggressiveness of breast cancer and inhibited tumor cell apoptosis. Therefore, METTL16/LINC00665/miR-28-5p/GLG1 axis may be a potential a potential target for breast cancer. This is the first study to investigate the m6A modification of LINC00665 in breast cancer.

LINC00665 functions as an oncogene in multi-type cancers. For example, LINC00665 interacts with RNA binding protein YBX1 to promote the proliferation and metastasis of gastric cancer (22). LINC00665 overexpression promotes the stemness in ovarian cancer (23). LINC00665 mediates immune escape and inhibits the radiosensitivity of non-small cell lung cancer (24). Furthermore, LINC00665 overexpression in breast cancer patients predicts poor outcome (25). In this study, METTL16-mediated m6A modification drove the upregulation of LINC00665 in breast cancer. However, inhibition of LINC00665 effectively inhibits the proliferation and invasion of breast cancer.16 Here, we demonstrated that targeting LINC00665 by its specific shRNA inhibited the proliferation and invasion of breast cancer cells, as well as promoted tumor cell apoptosis. These findings suggest that targeting LINC00665 may be of vital importance to develop personalized medicine and holds a great promise for the treatment of breast cancer (26,27).

Presious studies have demonstrated that LINC00665 binds miRNAs to promote the carcinogenesis. For example, LINC00665 mediates the proliferation, migration, and invasion of breast cancer via regulating miR-379-5p/lin-28 RNA binding posttranscriptional regulator B axis (14). LINC00665 inhibits the apoptosis of breast cancer cells via downregulating miR-551b-5p (15). Indeed, dysregulated miRNAs are associated with poor prognosis of breast cancer (28). Here, we found that LINC00665 could bind to miR-28-5p, which was downregulated in breast cancer. miR-28-5p functions as an anti-tumor gene in breast cancer. For example, miR-28-5p overexpression suppresses the proliferation of breast cancer (29). Yue et al.’s study demonstrates that miR-28-5p functions as a metastasis suppressor in breast cancer (30). In our study, miR-28-5p was downregulated in breast cancer. LINC00665 sponged miR-28-5p to drive the metastasis of breast cancer.

LINC00665 functions as a ceRNA to promote the progression of breast cancer. For example, LINC00665/miR-410-3p/ER membrane protein complex subunit 2 axis mediates tumor immune cell infiltration and predicts poor prognosis of breast cancer patients (31). LINC00665 mediates the aggressiveness and inhibits the apoptosis of breast cancer cells via regulating miR-3619-5p/catenin beta 1 axis (16). In this study, LINC00665 sponged miR-28-5p to upregulate GLG1. Recent studies have demonstrated that GLG1-mediated cell adhesion and vesicle distribution contribute to the metastasis of gastric cancer and colorectal cancer (32,33). Here, we demonstrated that GLG1 promoted the proliferation, migration, and invasion of breast cancer, as well as inhibiting tumor cell apoptosis.

Conclusions

In conclusion, METTL16-mediated upregulation of LINC00665 promotes aggressiveness and inhibits the apoptosis of breast cancer via regulating miR-28-5p/GLG1 axis. Therefore, targeting METTL16/LINC00665/miR-28-5p/GLG1 axis may be a promising strategy for breast cancer.
