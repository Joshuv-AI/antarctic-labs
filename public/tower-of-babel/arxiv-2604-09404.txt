Classification of irreducible real modules of real Lie superalgebras
Siddhartha Sahi, Hadi Salmasian, Vera Serganova

Abstract
. We classify irreducible finite-dimensional modules of a collection of real Lie superalgebras that includes the simple ones, their classical variants, complex Lie superalgebras after restriction of scalars, and all real Lie algebras. Our strategy is to reduce this classification to determining the orbits of the parity and conjugation functors on irreducible modules of the complexifications of the aforementioned algebras. Then we provide explicit results for the computation of these orbits. For Lie superalgebras of basic type or of type 𝐐 ⁡ ( n ) \mathbf{Q}(n) , our classification applies to any highest-weight parametrization of irreducible complex modules with respect to an arbitrary Borel subalgebra. As a consequence, in the special case of real simple Lie algebras we obtain a new perspective on the classification of real simple modules and establish a conceptual connection with Kostant’s cascade of strongly orthogonal roots.

1.
Introduction

While the study of finite dimensional modules of
complex simple Lie (super)algebras has been a mainstream research direction in algebra, much less attention has been paid to such modules over real numbers. Still, the problem of characterizing modules of real simple Lie algebras has a long history, going back to the works of É. Cartan, Iwahori, and Karpelevich
[
2
,
6
,
8
]
. The monograph by Onishchik
[
10
]
offers a simplified exposition of the classification from the viewpoint of
[
8
]
. We refer the reader to the introduction of
[
10
]
for a meticulous examination of the history of this problem. For the analogous problem over more general fields (but in the framework of representations of reductive groups), we refer the reader to
[
13
]
.

In the super setting, the only work on classification of representations over general fields that we are aware of is
[
5
]
. The qualitative result of Hayashi’s paper (see
[
5
, Thm 1.2]
) is in the setting of representations of affine supergroup schemes, and its more quantitative result (see
[
5
, Thm 1.7]
) is
stated for quasi-reductive algebraic supergroups. Despite working in a general setting, Hayashi’s paper has three
drawbacks. First, the characterization given in
[
5
, Thm 1.7]
is only applicable when irreducible modules
are parametrized by their highest weights with respect to
special
choices of a Borel in the quasi-reductive supergroup
\mathbb{G}
. When the base field is
\mathbb{R}
, the condition on the Borel
\mathbb{B}
is that

\tau(\mathbb{B})
and
\mathbb{B}
are conjugate under the Weyl group of the even part, where
\tau
denotes the anti-holomorphic involution of
\mathbb{G}
that determines its real form. However, the majority of Borels
\mathbb{B}
do not satisfy this property. For example, for the unitary supergroups
U(p,q|r,s)
, the Borel subalgebra has to be of a special palindromic form given in
[
5
, Example 5.25]
; see also Remark
6.11
below.
Second, the results of
[
5
]
only apply to integrable modules of real simple Lie superalgebras, i.e., those modules that can be realized as representations of the corresponding algebraic supergroup. However, finite dimensional modules of
\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)
are generically
not
integrable to the supergroup
\mathrm{SL}(m|n)
. Third, Hayashi’s paper does not address the case of Lie superalgebras of Cartan type.

In the present paper, we give a concrete characterization of irreducible finite dimensional modules of
a collection of real Lie superalgebras
that includes real simple Lie superalgebras and their classical variants (see Definition
1.2
for a precise description).
Furthermore,
our characterization is explicit for any parametrization of highest weights by an
arbitrary
Borel subalgebra. As such, our paper addresses the three drawbacks of
[
5
]
.

1.1.
Main results

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a complex Lie superalgebra, and let
\tau:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
be an antilinear involution of
\mathfrak{g}
corresponding to a real form
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}
of
\mathfrak{g}
(throughout this paper, all Lie superalgebras are finite dimensional). Let
\mathscr{R}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}
denote the category of finite dimensional

\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}
-modules (over real vector spaces). Then
\mathscr{R}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}
is an
\mathbb{R}
-linear abelian category, equipped with an
\mathbb{R}
-linear involutive endofunctor
\Pi
, namely the parity change. Using
\Pi
, we can define enriched endomorphism superalgebras

\underline{\mathrm{End}}_{\mathscr{R}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}}(V):=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathscr{R}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}}(V,V)\oplus\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathscr{R}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}}(V,\Pi V),

for any object
V\in\mathrm{Obj}(\mathscr{R}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau})
; see Remark
3.1
for the connection to superized endomorphism algebras.
If
V
is irreducible, then
\underline{\mathrm{End}}_{\mathscr{R}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}}(V)
is a division superalgebra over
\mathbb{R}
. Up to isomorphism, there are 10 such division superalgebras (sometimes physicists call this fact the
10-fold way
).
By an abstract construction from category theory, in Section
2
we associate to
\mathscr{R}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}
a
\mathbb{C}
-linear abelian category whose objects are those of
\mathscr{R}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}
that possess a complex structure.
Not surprisingly, the resulting category is equivalent to the category
\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{g}}
of finite dimensional
\mathfrak{g}
-modules (over complex vector spaces). The importance of
\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{g}}
is that it is automatically equipped with a complex conjugation functor
\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}
.

Let

\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{X})
denote the set of isomorphism classes of simple objects of a given small category
\mathscr{X}
. We write
[M]
for the isomorphism class of any
M\in\mathrm{Obj}(\mathscr{X})
. Given
[W]\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{g}})
, by restriction of scalars we can consider
W
as a
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}
-module. This restriction either remains irreducible, or is a direct sum of two isomorphic irreducible
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}
-modules. Denoting an irreducible
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}
-summand of
W
by
V
, we obtain a map

\Phi:\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{g}})\to\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{R}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}),

given by
\Phi([W])=[V]
; see (
1
).
In
Theorem
2.1
, we show that
\Phi
induces a bijection from
\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}
-orbits in
\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{g}})
onto
\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{R}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau})
.
Furthermore, in Theorem
2.5
we show that we can decide between
W=V
and

if we can compute the

of
W
, defined as

\mathsf{ET}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}(W):=\underline{\mathrm{End}}_{\mathscr{R}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}}(V)
.
Theorems
2.1
and
2.5
reduce the classification of irreducible real representations of
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}
to the computation of the endotypes
\mathsf{ET}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}(W)
; see
Remark
4.2
. We solve the problem of computing

\mathsf{ET}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}(W)

for
(\mathfrak{g},\tau)
corresponding to real Lie superalgebras in the collection

described in Definition

. This is accomplished in two steps: first, in Sections
4
and

We now define the collections of Lie superalgebras mentioned in the previous paragraph.

Definition 1.1
.

We say a Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}
is of

if it is isomorphic to one of the following Lie superalgebras:

(i)

A simple Lie algebra.

(ii)

One of the Lie superalgebras
\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)
,
\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
,
\mathfrak{psl}(m|n)
, or
\mathfrak{osp}(m|2n)
for

.

(iii)

An exceptional Lie superalgebra
G(2|1)
,
F(3|1)
, or
D(2|1,\alpha)
.

We say
\mathfrak{g}
is of
type
\mathbf{Q}(n)
if it is isomorphic to one of
\mathfrak{q}(n)
,
\mathfrak{pq}(n)
,
\mathfrak{sq}(n)
, or
\mathfrak{psq}(n)
for
n\geq 1
. We say
\mathfrak{g}
is of
type
\mathbf{P}(n)
if it is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{pe}(n)
or
\mathfrak{spe}(n)
for
n\geq 1
. Finally, we say
\mathfrak{g}
is of

if it is isomorphic to one of the Lie superalgebras
\mathbf{W}(n)
,
\mathbf{H}(n)
,
\mathbf{S}(n)
or
\tilde{\mathbf{S}}(2n)
for
n\geq 1
.

Given a complex Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}
, we denote the intersection of kernels of irreducible finite dimensional
\mathfrak{g}
-modules by
\mathfrak{f}_{\mathfrak{g}}
. For a real Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}
, we define
\mathfrak{f}_{\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}}
analogously. We now give the definition of the collection
\mathcal{M}
of Lie superalgebras whose irreducible real modules are classified in this paper.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be the smallest collection of finite dimensional real Lie superalgebras that is closed under taking direct sums and satisfies the following properties.

(i)

\mathcal{M}
contains real forms of the four types of Lie superalgebras of Definition
1.1
, every real Lie algebra,
and every complex Lie superalgebra (considered as a real Lie superalgebra via restriction of scalars).

(ii)

If
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}
is a real Lie superalgebra
such that either
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}/\mathfrak{f}_{\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}}\in\mathcal{M}
, or

\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}
has a hyperbolic type triangular decomposition

(see Subsection
4.2
) such that
\mathfrak{l}^{\mathbb{R}}\in\mathcal{M}
, then
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}\in\mathcal{M}
.

When
\mathfrak{g}
is either of basic type or of type
\mathbf{Q}(n)
, our method for the computation of
\mathsf{ET}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}(W)
from the highest weight of
W

(which is carried out in Theorems

and
6.12
) is quite different from the one used in
[
5
]
: rather than using tools from Galois cohomology in the spirit of
[
13
]
, we use the Harish-Chandra projection of products of root vectors of a sequence of isotropic roots associated to the highest weight.

For Lie superalgebras of basic type or of type
\mathbf{Q}(n)
,
in Section
7
we establish a slightly different method for computing

that relies on the evaluation of the highest weight
\lambda
of

on the sum of coroots associated to Kostant’s cascade of strongly orthogonal roots in a Levi subalgebra of

; see Theorem

.
Even in the special case of real reductive Lie algebras, this viewpoint leads to a classification of irreducible modules that (in our opinion) is substantially simpler than the one given in
[

]
; see Theorem
7.12
.
In
[

, the characterization of irreducible modules of real simple Lie algebras reduces to the calculation of an invariant of complex modules known as the Karpelevich index. However, the arguments to arrive at this result are rather intricate. Furthermore, the conceptual connection with Kostant’s cascade has, to our best knowledge, remained unnoticed in
[

2.
Categorical generalities

The goal of this section is to lay out some abstract categorical foundations that will be used later in the setting of module categories. Let
\mathscr{R}
be an essentially small
\mathbb{R}
-linear abelian category with the property that
\mathrm{End}_{\mathscr{R}}(V)
is a finite dimensional real vector space for every simple object
V\in\mathrm{Obj}(\mathscr{R})
.
Consider the category
\mathscr{C}
defined as follows:

(i)

Objects of
\mathscr{C}
are pairs
(V,\iota)
where
\iota\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathscr{R}}(V,V)
and
\iota^{2}=-1
.

(ii)

For objects
W_{1}:=(V_{1},\iota_{1})
and
W_{2}:=(V_{2},\iota_{2})
of
\mathscr{C}
we set

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}}(W_{1},W_{2}):=\{\phi\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathscr{R}}(V_{1},V_{2})\,:\,\phi\iota_{1}=\iota_{2}\phi\}.

We will need the following three functors that relate the categories
\mathscr{R}
and
\mathscr{C}
.

(i)

\mathcal{B}:\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{C}
, where
\mathcal{B}(V,\iota):=(V,-\iota)
and
\mathcal{B}(\alpha):=\alpha
for every
\mathscr{C}
-morphism
\alpha
.

(ii)

\mathcal{E}:\mathscr{R}\to\mathscr{C}
, defined by

\mathcal{E}(V):=(V\oplus V,-\epsilon_{1}\pi_{2}+\epsilon_{2}\pi_{1}),

where the morphisms
\pi_{i}=\pi_{V,i}:V\oplus V\to V

and

\epsilon_{i}=\epsilon_{V,i}:V\to V\oplus V
for
i=1,2
are
the canonical projections and embeddings, and

\mathcal{E}(\phi):=\epsilon^{\prime}_{1}\phi\pi_{1}+\epsilon^{\prime}_{2}\phi\pi_{2}\quad\text{ for }\phi\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathscr{R}}(V,V^{\prime}),

where
\epsilon_{i}^{\prime}:=\epsilon_{V^{\prime},i}
for
i=1,2
.

(iii)

\mathcal{F}:\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{R}
, where
\mathcal{F}
maps
(V,\iota)
to
V
and
\mathcal{F}(\alpha):=\alpha
for every
\mathscr{C}
-morphism
\alpha
.

We denote the identity functors on
\mathscr{R}
and
\mathscr{C}
by
\mathcal{I}_{\mathscr{R}}
and
\mathcal{I}_{\mathscr{C}}
, respectively.

The category
\mathscr{C}
is a
\mathbb{C}
-linear abelian category,
with zero object
\mathbf{0}_{\mathscr{C}}:=\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{0}_{\mathscr{R}})

and the biproduct of
W_{1}:=(V_{1},\iota_{1})
and

(V_{1}\oplus V_{2},\iota_{1}\oplus\iota_{2})
.
This can be verified directly

Freyd-Mitchell embedding theorem:

as a category of

-modules for a ring

, then
\mathscr{C}
is a category of

-modules where

.

The functor
\mathcal{B}
induces an involution
\Theta_{\mathcal{B}}
on
\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C})
because
\mathcal{B}^{2}=\mathcal{I}_{\mathscr{C}}
(see Remark
A.3
). We denote the set of
\Theta_{\mathcal{B}}
-orbits by
\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C})/\mathcal{B}
.
We now define three subsets of
\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C})
:

•

\mathrm{Irr}_{+}(\mathscr{C}):=\left\{[W]\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C})\,:\,\mathcal{F}(W)\cong V\oplus V\text{ for some }V\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{R})\right\}
,

•

\mathrm{Irr}_{\circ}(\mathscr{C}):=\left\{[W]\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C})\,:\,\mathcal{F}(W)\text{ is simple and }W\not\cong\mathcal{B}(W)\right\}
,

•

\mathrm{Irr}_{-}(\mathscr{C}):=\left\{[W]\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C})\,:\,\mathcal{F}(W)\text{ is simple and }W\cong\mathcal{B}(W)\right\}
.

By Schur’s Lemma in
\mathscr{R}
, we have
\mathrm{End}_{\mathscr{R}}(V)\in\{\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C},\mathbb{H}\}
whenever
[V]\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{R})
. We define three subsets of
\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{R})

as follows:

•

\mathrm{Irr}_{+}(\mathscr{R}):=\left\{[V]\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{R})\,:\,\mathrm{End}_{\mathscr{R}}(V)\cong\mathbb{R}\right\}
.

•

\mathrm{Irr}_{\circ}(\mathscr{R}):=\left\{[V]\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{R})\,:\,\mathrm{End}_{\mathscr{R}}(V)\cong\mathbb{C}\right\}
.

•

\mathrm{Irr}_{-}(\mathscr{R}):=\left\{[V]\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{R})\,:\,\mathrm{End}_{\mathscr{R}}(V)\cong\mathbb{H}\right\}
.

By Lemma

, for any
[W]\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C})
either

is simple or
\mathcal{F}(W)\cong V\oplus V
for a simple

where

uniquely determines

.
Thus, we have a map

(1)

\Phi:\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C})\to\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{R})\quad,\quad\Phi([W]):=\begin{cases}[\mathcal{F}(W)]&\text{ if }\mathcal{F}(W)\text{ is simple},\\
[V]&\text{ if }\mathcal{F}(W)\cong V\oplus V.\end{cases}

We denote the set of
\Theta_{\mathcal{B}}
-orbits of
\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C})

\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C})/\mathcal{B}

Then

2.2.
The Brauer–Wall monoid

Isomorphism classes of finite dimensional central division superalgebras over a field
\mathbb{F}
form a group
\mathbf{BW}_{\mathbb{F}}
with the binary operation uniquely determined by the property that
[D]\cdot[D^{\prime}]=[D^{\prime\prime}]
if and only if
D\otimes_{\mathbb{F}}D^{\prime}
is Morita equivalent to
D^{\prime\prime}
.
The disjoint union

\mathbf{BW}:=\mathbf{BW}_{\mathbb{R}}\cup\mathbf{BW}_{\mathbb{C}}
naturally inherits a monoid structure that extends the group structures of
\mathbf{BW}_{\mathbb{R}}
and
\mathbf{BW}_{\mathbb{C}}
: the product of
[D]\in\mathbf{BW}_{\mathbb{R}}
and
[D^{\prime}]\in\mathbf{BW}_{\mathbb{C}}
is defined to be
[D\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}D^{\prime})]
. This monoid is called the
Brauer–Wall monoid
of the field
\mathbb{R}
(see
[
14
]
for a theory over general fields). It has 10 elements: 2 division superalgebras with supercenter
\mathbb{C}
that form a group isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, and 8 division superalgebras with supercenter
\mathbb{R}
that form a group isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{8}
.
In what follows, we denote the former 2 division superalgebras by
k_{\mathbb{C}}
for
0\leq k\leq 1
and the latter 8 by
k_{\mathbb{R}}
for
0\leq k\leq 7
. In this notation,
k_{\mathbb{F}}
is Morita equivalent to the Clifford
\mathbb{F}
-algebra on
k

3.
Endotypes for tensor products of modules

In this section we investigate endotypes of irreducible modules of tensor products of associative superalgebras. We begin by introducing some notation that will be used in the rest of the paper.

Throughout the paper, we denote the parity of a vector
v
in a superspace by
|v|
.
For a (Lie or associative)
\mathbb{F}
-superalgebra
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}}\oplus\mathcal{A}_{\overline{\mathbf{1}}}

\mathsf{Rep}(\mathcal{A})
(respectively,
\mathsf{Rep}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathcal{A})
) to denote the category of
\mathcal{A}
-modules on
\mathbb{F}
-vector superspaces (respectively, finite dimensional
\mathbb{F}
-vector superspaces). The morphisms in these categories are assumed to respect the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading.

Remark 3.1
.

For
W,W^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Obj}(\mathsf{Rep}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathcal{A}))
we define the super endomorphism spaces

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}^{s}(W,W^{\prime}):=\{T:W\to W^{\prime}\,:\,T\text{ is linear and }Ta=(-1)^{|T|\cdot|a|}aT\text{ for }a\in\mathcal{A}\},

where, as usual,
|T|,|a|
, etc. denote parity.
As in (
3
),
one can also define

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}^{s}(W,W^{\prime}):=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathsf{Rep}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathcal{A})}(W,W^{\prime})\oplus\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathsf{Rep}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathcal{A})}(W,\Pi{{}^{\sigma}W^{\prime}}),

where
{}^{\sigma}W^{\prime}
is the twist of
W^{\prime}
by the automorphism
\sigma
of
\mathcal{A}
given by
\sigma(a):=(-1)^{|a|}a
. There is an isomorphism
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}^{s}(W,W^{\prime})\to\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathsf{Rep}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathcal{A})}}(W,W^{\prime})
given by
T\mapsto T^{\diamond}
where

T^{\diamond}w:=(-1)^{|T|\cdot|w|}Tw
for
w\in W
.
Setting
\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{A}}^{s}(W):=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}^{s}(W,W)
, the latter map induces an isomorphism of superalgebras

(4)

\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{A}}^{s}(W)\cong((\underline{\mathrm{End}}_{\mathsf{Rep}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathcal{A})}(W))^{\mathrm{op}})^{\mathrm{sop}},

where
(X^{\mathrm{op}})^{\mathrm{sop}}
denotes the super opposite of the opposite of a superalgebra
X
.

Let

\mathcal{A}
be a (Lie or associative)
\mathbb{C}
-superalgebra and let
\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{R}}
be a real form of
A
, obtained as fixed points of an antilinear involution
\tau:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}
.
Henceforth we set

\mathscr{R}:=\mathscr{R}_{\mathcal{A},\tau}:=\mathsf{Rep}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{R}}).

The category
\mathscr{C}
associated to
\mathscr{R}
as in Section
2
is
\mathscr{C}_{\mathcal{A}}:=\mathsf{Rep}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathcal{A})
.
The yoga of functors
in Section
2
applied to these choices of categories
\mathscr{R}
and
\mathscr{C}
results in functors

\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{A},\tau},\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{A},\tau},\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{A},\tau}
. When the choice of
\mathcal{A}
is clear, we simplify our notation to

\mathcal{B}_{\tau},\mathcal{E}_{\tau},\mathcal{F}_{\tau}
. For simplicity we define

(5)

\mathsf{ET}_{\tau}(W):=\mathsf{ET}_{\mathcal{A},\tau}(W):=\mathsf{ET}_{\mathscr{R}_{\mathcal{A},\tau}}(W)\quad\text{ for }W\in\mathrm{Obj}(\mathscr{C}_{\mathcal{A}}).

Remark 3.2
.

Given
[W]\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C}_{A})
,
if
\overline{\mathcal{A}}
denotes the image of
\mathcal{A}
in
\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{A}}^{s}(W)
then by the classification of primitive
\mathbb{C}
-superalgebras we have
\overline{\mathcal{A}}=\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{D}}^{s}(W)
where
\mathbb{D}:=\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{A}}(W)\cong\mathbb{C}
or
\mathrm{Q}(1)
. (In particular, it happens that
\underline{\mathrm{End}}_{\mathscr{C}_{\mathcal{A}}}(W)\cong\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{A}}^{s}(W)
.)
We equip
W
with a right
\mathbb{D}
-module structure with the action
w\cdot d:=(-1)^{|d|\cdot|w|}d\cdot w
for
d\in\mathbb{D}
,
w\in W
.

In the rest of this section we assume that
A
and
B
are associative
\mathbb{C}
-superalgebras. Given

[W_{A}]\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C}_{A})
and
[W_{B}]\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C}_{B})
, we set

\mathbb{D}_{A}:=\mathrm{End}_{A}^{s}(W_{A})
and
\mathbb{D}_{B}:=\mathrm{End}_{B}^{s}(W_{B})
. From the general rules about external tensor products of irreducible modules of types
\mathtt{M}
and
\mathtt{Q}
(for example see
[
3
, Sec. 3.1.3]
), it follows that
[W_{A}\otimes_{\mathbb{D}}W_{B}]\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C}_{A\otimes B})
, where
\mathbb{D}:=\mathbb{D}_{W_{A},W_{B}}
is defined as follows:
\mathbb{D}=\mathbb{C}
if
either
\mathbb{D}_{A}\cong\mathbb{C}
or
\mathbb{D}_{B}\cong\mathbb{C}
, and
\mathbb{D}=\mathrm{Q}(1)
otherwise.
In what follows, tensor products over unspecified rings are always construed to be over
\mathbb{C}
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
[W]\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C}_{A\otimes B})
.
Then
there exist
[W_{A}]\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C}_{A})
and
[W_{B}]\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C}_{B})
such that
W\cong W_{A}\otimes_{\mathbb{D}}W_{B}
, where
\mathbb{D}=\mathbb{D}_{W_{A},W_{B}}
.

Proof.

For any irreducible
B
-submodule
W_{B}\subseteq W
, the canonical
A\otimes B
-module map

\mathrm{Hom}^{s}_{B}(W_{B},W)\otimes W_{B}\to W\ ,\ T\otimes w\mapsto Tw

is a surjection. Thus, as a
B
-module
W
is a direct sum of copies of
W_{B}
and
\Pi W_{B}
.
Similarly, if
W_{A}\subseteq\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{B}(W_{B},W)
is an irreducible
A
-submodule, then the restriction map
W_{A}\otimes W_{B}\to W
is a surjection, so that
W
as
A
-module is a direct sum of copies of
W_{A}
and
\Pi W_{A}
.

If
\mathbb{D}_{A}\cong\mathbb{C}
, then
\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{D}_{A}}^{s}(W_{A})\cong\mathrm{Mat}_{m|n}(\mathbb{C})
where
(m|n)
is the graded dimension of
W_{A}
. Similarly, if
\mathbb{D}_{A}\cong\mathrm{Q}(1)
, then
\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{D}_{A}}^{s}(W_{A})\cong\mathrm{Q}(n)
where
n=\dim(W_{A})_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}}=\dim(W_{A})_{\overline{\mathbf{1}}}
. From
Remark
3.2
and
well-known descriptions of tensor products of superalgebras
\mathrm{Mat}_{m|n}(\mathbb{C})
and
\mathrm{Q}(n)
it follows that if either
\mathbb{D}_{A}\cong\mathbb{C}
or
\mathbb{D}_{B}\cong\mathbb{C}
, then
W_{A}\otimes W_{B}
is irreducible and hence isomorphic to
W
, whereas if
\mathbb{D}_{A}\cong\mathbb{D}_{B}\cong\mathrm{Q}(1)
, then

W_{A}\otimes W_{B}\cong W\oplus\Pi W
. Since the map
W_{A}\otimes W_{B}\to W
factors through the proper quotient
W_{A}\otimes_{\mathbb{D}}W_{B}
, we have
W_{A}\otimes_{\mathbb{D}}W_{B}\cong W
.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
W
,
W_{A}
,
W_{B}
, and
\mathbb{D}
be as in Proposition
3.3
and let
A^{\mathbb{R}}
and
B^{\mathbb{R}}
be real forms of
A
and
B
, corresponding to involutions
\tau_{A}:A\to A
and
\tau_{B}:B\to B
, respectively.
Set
C:=A\otimes B
with real form
C^{\mathbb{R}}:=A^{\mathbb{R}}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}B^{\mathbb{R}}
corresponding to
\tau_{C}:=\tau_{A}\otimes\tau_{B}
.
Then
\mathsf{ET}_{{\tau_{C}}}(W)=\mathsf{ET}_{{\tau_{A}}}(W_{A})\bullet\mathsf{ET}_{{\tau_{B}}}(W_{B})
, where
\bullet
denotes the product of
\mathbf{BW}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{D}_{A},\mathbb{D}_{B},\mathbb{D}_{C}
be division superalgebras corresponding to endotypes of
W_{A},W_{B},W_{C}
, respectively. Our goal is to prove that

[\mathbb{D}]=[\mathbb{D}_{A}]\bullet[\mathbb{D}_{B}]
.
The
C^{\mathbb{R}}
-module
\mathcal{F}_{\tau_{A}}(W_{A})\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{F}_{\tau_{B}}(W_{B})
is semisimple.
Furthermore,
\mathcal{F}_{\tau_{C}}(W)
is a quotient of
\mathcal{F}_{\tau_{A}}(W_{A})\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{F}_{\tau_{B}}(W_{B})
.

For any superalgebra
X
, we denote
(X^{\mathrm{op}})^{\mathrm{sop}}
by
X^{\diamond}
.
By Remark
2.8
and (
4
) we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{End}^{s}_{C^{\mathbb{R}}}(\mathcal{F}_{\tau_{A}}(W_{A})\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{F}_{\tau_{B}}(W_{B}))

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{End}^{s}_{A^{\mathbb{R}}}(\mathcal{F}_{\tau_{A}}(W_{A}))\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{End}^{s}_{B^{\mathbb{R}}}(\mathcal{F}_{\tau_{B}}(W_{B}))

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{Mat}_{d_{1}}(\mathbb{D}_{A}^{\diamond})\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{Mat}_{d_{2}}(\mathbb{D}_{B}^{\diamond})\cong\mathrm{Mat}_{d_{1}d_{2}}(\mathbb{D}_{A}^{\diamond}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{D}_{B}^{\diamond}),

where
d_{1},d_{2}\in\{1,2\}
. Furthermore,
\mathbb{D}_{A}^{\diamond}\otimes\mathbb{D}_{B}^{\diamond}\cong(\mathbb{D}_{A}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{D}_{B})^{\diamond}
. It suffices to verify that
\mathbb{D}_{A}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{D}_{B}
is a direct product of central simple algebras that are Brauer equivalent to
[\mathbb{D}_{A}]\bullet[\mathbb{D}_{B}]
. If both
\mathbb{D}_{A}
and
\mathbb{D}_{B}
are
\mathbb{R}
-central then
\mathbb{D}_{A}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{D}_{B}
is also an
\mathbb{R}
-central simple superalgebra and we have
[\mathbb{D}_{A}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{D}_{B}]=[\mathbb{D}_{A}]\bullet[\mathbb{D}_{B}]
. If exactly one of
\mathbb{D}_{A}
and
\mathbb{D}_{B}
(say
\mathbb{D}_{A}
) is a
\mathbb{C}
-central superalgebra, then

[\mathbb{D}_{A}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{D}_{B}]=[\mathbb{D}_{A}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{D}_{B})]=[\mathbb{D}_{A}]\bullet[\mathbb{D}_{B}].

Finally, if both of
\mathbb{D}_{A}
and
\mathbb{D}_{B}
are
\mathbb{C}
-central, then from
\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}\cong\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}
we obtain

\mathbb{D}_{A}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{D}_{B}\cong\mathbb{D}_{A}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C})\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{D}_{B}\cong(\mathbb{D}_{A}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{D}_{B})\times(\mathbb{D}_{A}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{D}_{B}),

hence
[\mathbb{D}_{A}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{D}_{B}]=[\mathbb{D}_{A}]\bullet[\mathbb{D}_{B}]
.

∎

4.
Irreducible modules of
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}
, endotypes, and reductions

In the rest of this paper we utilize the general framework of Section
2
in the context of categories of modules for real and complex Lie superalgebras.
Given a finite dimensional complex Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}
, we use
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}
to denote a real form of
\mathfrak{g}
obtained as fixed points of an antilinear involution
\tau:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
.
We set

\mathscr{R}:=\mathsf{Rep}(\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}})
. The corresponding category
\mathscr{C}
is

\mathsf{Rep}(\mathfrak{g})
.
As in Section
2
, we have
functors
\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}
,
\mathcal{E}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}
,
\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}
relating
\mathscr{R}
and
\mathscr{C}
, that descend to functors relating the categories
\mathscr{R}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}:=\mathsf{Rep}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}})
and
\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{g}}:=\mathsf{Rep}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathfrak{g})
. Recall from (
5
) that for
[W]\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{g}})
we set

\mathsf{ET}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}(W):=\mathsf{ET}_{\mathscr{R}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}}(W).

Remark 4.1
.

Given an object
W
of
\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{g}}
, it will be helpful to think of
\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}W
in the following concrete way. As a vector (super)space,
\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}W
is just
W
equipped with the conjugate complex structure. The action of any
x\in\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}W

is the same as the action of
\tau(x)
on
W
.
Any homogeneous
\phi\in\underline{\mathrm{End}}_{\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{g}}}(W,\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}W)
can be thought of as an (either even or odd) antilinear map
\phi:W\to W
such that
\phi(x\cdot w)=\tau(x)\cdot\phi(w)
for
x\in\mathfrak{g}
and
w\in W
.

Remark 4.2
.

Concrete realizations of irreducible real modules of
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}
can be obtained from the complete description of endotypes
\mathsf{ET}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}(W)
for
[W]\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{g}})
. Indeed by Lemmas
A.4
and
A.1
(i) it follows that for any row in Table
1
, if we have
\mathcal{E}(V)=W\oplus\mathcal{B}W
, then
\mathcal{F}(W)
is irreducible and
\Phi(W)=[\mathcal{F}(W)]
. In other words, if
\mathsf{ET}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}(W)\not\in\{0_{\mathbb{R}},1_{\mathbb{R}},7_{\mathbb{R}}\}
, then
W
itself is an irreducible real
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}
-module.
If

\mathsf{ET}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}(W)\in\{0_{\mathbb{R}},1_{\mathbb{R}},7_{\mathbb{R}}\}
,
then
\mathcal{F}(W)=V\oplus V
for a simple
V
and we have
[\Phi(W)]=[V]
. In this case
(\mathcal{B},+)\in\mathfrak{S}_{W}
, i.e., there exists an even antilinear map
T:W\to W
such that
Tx=xT
for
x\in\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}
. Having such a
T
, we can obtain a direct sum decomposition
W=V\oplus V
by considering the two subspaces
\{w\pm Tw\,:\,w\in W\}
. When
W
is a highest weight module, an explicit
T
can also be computed by first defining it on the highest weight space of
W
and then extending it to all of
W
.

In the rest of this section we show that computing endotypes for modules of
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}\in\mathcal{M}
can be reduced to the case of modules of the Lie superalgebras in Definition
1.1
.

4.1.
The direct sum reduction

Suppose that
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{b}
and
\tau=\tau_{\mathfrak{a}}\oplus\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}
where
\tau_{\mathfrak{a}}:\mathfrak{a}\to\mathfrak{a}
and
\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}:\mathfrak{b}\to\mathfrak{b}
are antilinear involutions associated to real forms
\mathfrak{a}^{\mathbb{R}}
and
\mathfrak{b}^{\mathbb{R}}
.
The results of Section
3
have the following immediate consequence.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
W\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{g}})
. Then the following statements hold.

(i)

There exist
[W_{\mathfrak{a}}]\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{a}})
and
[W_{\mathfrak{b}}]\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{b}})
such that
W\cong W_{\mathfrak{a}}\otimes_{\mathbb{D}}W_{\mathfrak{b}}
, where
\mathbb{D}
is defined from
\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{a}}:=\mathrm{End}^{s}_{\mathfrak{a}}(W_{\mathfrak{a}})
and

\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{b}}:=\mathrm{End}^{s}_{\mathfrak{b}}(W_{\mathfrak{b}})
by

(6)

\mathbb{D}:=\begin{cases}\mathbb{C}&\text{if either $\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{a}}\cong\mathbb{C}$ or $\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{b}}\cong\mathbb{C}$},\\
\mathrm{Q}(1)&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(ii)

\mathsf{ET}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}(W)=\mathsf{ET}_{\mathfrak{a},\tau_{\mathfrak{a}}}(W_{\mathfrak{a}})\bullet\mathsf{ET}_{\mathfrak{b},\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}}(W_{\mathfrak{b}})
, where
\bullet
denotes the product of
\mathbf{BW}
.

Since abelian Lie algebras occur frequently as direct summands, we record the following simple fact.

Remark 4.4
.

If
\mathfrak{g}
is an abelian Lie algebra, for
[W]\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{g}})
we have
\mathsf{ET}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}(W)\in\{0_{\mathbb{R}},0_{\mathbb{C}}\}
. Furthermore,

\mathsf{ET}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}(W)=0_{\mathbb{R}}

if and only if elements of
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}
act on
W
by real scalars. To prove this, first note that every irreducible
\mathfrak{g}
-module
W
is one-dimensional and corresponds to a linear functional
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. The existence of an antilinear map
\phi:W\to W
as in Remark
4.1
is equivalent to
\mu(\tau(x))=\overline{\mu(x)}
for
x\in\mathfrak{g}
, or in other words to
\mu(\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}})\subseteq\mathbb{R}
. Furthermore, antilinear maps on a one-dimensional vector space are of the form
z\mapsto\alpha\bar{z}
, hence
\phi^{2}(z)=|\alpha|^{2}z
. This implies that
(\mathcal{B},-)\not\in\mathfrak{S}_{W}
.

4.2.
The triangular reduction

Recall that a triangular decomposition of
\mathfrak{g}
is a vector space decomposition

(7)

\mathfrak{g}=\overline{\mathfrak{u}}\oplus\mathfrak{l}\oplus\mathfrak{u}

such that
\mathfrak{u},\overline{\mathfrak{u}},\mathfrak{l}
are subalgebras satisfying
[\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{u}]\subseteq\mathfrak{u}
and
[\mathfrak{l},\overline{\mathfrak{u}}]\subseteq\overline{\mathfrak{u}}
. We say this triangular decomposition is
\tau
-stable
when
\tau(\mathfrak{u})=\mathfrak{u}
,
\tau(\overline{\mathfrak{u}})=\overline{\mathfrak{u}}
, and
\tau(\mathfrak{l})=\mathfrak{l}
.

For any
\mathfrak{g}
-module
W
, the subspace
W^{\mathfrak{u}}:=\left\{w\in W\,:\,x\cdot w=0\text{ for all }x\in\mathfrak{u}\right\}
is an
\mathfrak{l}
-module. Set
\mathfrak{q}:=\mathfrak{l}\oplus\mathfrak{u}
and let
\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\mathfrak{g}}:\mathsf{Rep}(\mathfrak{l})\to\mathsf{Rep}(\mathfrak{g})
denote the induction functor given by the assignment
W\mapsto\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{q})}W
. Given
[W]\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{g}})
, if
\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\mathfrak{g}}(W^{\mathfrak{u}})
has a unique maximal proper
\mathfrak{g}
-submodule
M
, then the kernel of the canonical surjection

\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\mathfrak{g}}(W^{\mathfrak{u}})\to W
must be
M
, so that
(\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\mathfrak{g}}(W^{\mathfrak{u}}))/M\cong W
.
For an
\mathfrak{l}
-module
W_{\mathfrak{l}}
,
we denote the unique irreducible quotient of

\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\mathfrak{g}}(W_{\mathfrak{l}})
, if it exists,
by

\underline{\mathrm{Ind}}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\mathfrak{g}}(W)
.

Theorem 4.5
.

Assume that
\mathfrak{g}
has a
\tau
-stable triangular decomposition as in (
7
). Furthermore, assume that
for every
W\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{g}})
, the
\mathfrak{l}
-module
W^{\mathfrak{u}}
is irreducible and the induced module
\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\mathfrak{g}}W^{\mathfrak{u}}
has a unique maximal proper
\mathfrak{g}
-submodule.
Then
for every
[W]\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{g}})
we have
\mathsf{ET}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}(W)=\mathsf{ET}_{\mathfrak{l},\tau}(W^{\mathfrak{u}})
.

Proof.

According to Theorem
2.5
, we need to verify that the symmetry data of
W
and
W^{\mathfrak{u}}
are the same.
From
\tau(\mathfrak{u})=\mathfrak{u}
it follows that
(\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}W)^{\mathfrak{u}}=\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{l},\tau}(W^{\tau(\mathfrak{u})})=\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{l},\tau}\left(W^{\mathfrak{u}}\right)
. Since
W^{\mathfrak{u}}
generates
W
as a
\mathfrak{g}
-module, we have injective linear maps

(8)

\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}}(W,W)\to\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{l},\tau}}(W^{\mathfrak{u}},W^{\mathfrak{u}})

and

(9)

\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}}(W,\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}W)\to\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{l},\tau}}(W^{\mathfrak{u}},\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{l},\tau}(W^{\mathfrak{u}})).

To complete the proof, it suffices to prove that (
8
) and (
9
) are surjections. We only give the argument for (
9
), which is slightly less trivial.
By Remark
4.1
, any homogeneous
\phi\in\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{l},\tau}}(W^{\mathfrak{u}},\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}W^{\mathfrak{u}})
corresponds to an (even or odd) antilinear map

T:W^{\mathfrak{u}}\to W^{\mathfrak{u}}
such that
Tx=\tau(x)T
for
x\in\mathfrak{l}
. From
T
we obtain an antilinear map

U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{q})}W^{\mathfrak{u}}\to U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{q})}W^{\mathfrak{u}}\ ,\ D\otimes w\mapsto\tau(D)\otimes Tw,

corresponding to a
\mathfrak{g}
-module homomorphism

U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{q})}W^{\mathfrak{u}}\to\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}(U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{q})}W^{\mathfrak{u}})
.
Passing to the quotients on both sides by the unique maximal proper invariant subspaces, we obtain a
\mathfrak{g}
-module homomorphism
{\tilde{T}}:W\to\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}(W)
that restricts to
T
, hence it corresponds to
\phi
under (
9
).
∎

In the rest of this subsection, we describe two kinds of triangular decompositions that satisfy the assumption of Theorem
4.5
. See Definitions
4.6
and
4.9
.

One way to obtain a
\tau
-stable triangular decomposition is to choose
x_{\circ}\in\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}}
such that
\mathrm{ad}_{x_{\circ}}:\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}
is diagonalizable with real eigenvalues (i.e., a
hyperbolic
element of
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}
) and decompose
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}
as

(10)

\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}=\overline{\mathfrak{u}}^{\mathbb{R}}\oplus\mathfrak{l}^{\mathbb{R}}\oplus{\mathfrak{u}}^{\mathbb{R}},

where
\mathfrak{u}^{\mathbb{R}}:=\oplus_{c>0}\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R},c}
,

\bar{\mathfrak{u}}^{\mathbb{R}}:=\oplus_{c<0}\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R},c}
,
and
\mathfrak{l}^{\mathbb{R}}:=\left\{y\in\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}\,:\,\mathrm{ad}_{x_{\circ}}(y)=0\right\}
.
Here
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R},c}
denotes the
c
-eigenspace of
\mathrm{ad}_{x_{\circ}}
for
c\in\mathbb{R}
. Passing to complexifications, we obtain a
\tau
-stable triangular decomposition.

Definition 4.6
.

A triangular decomposition (
7
) is said to be of
hyperbolic type
if it is obtained as above from a hyperbolic
x_{\circ}\in\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}}
.

Remark 4.7
.

Let
W
be a
\mathfrak{g}
-module that is generated by an irreducible finite dimensional
\mathfrak{l}
-module
W^{\prime}\subseteq W
. Then by Schur’s Lemma,

x_{\circ}
acts on
W^{\prime}
by a scalar
c\in\mathbb{C}
, and since
\mathfrak{g}
is a direct sum of
\mathrm{ad}_{x_{\circ}}
-eigenspaces it follows that
W
is a direct sum of
x_{\circ}
-eigenspaces as well.

Lemma 4.8
.

Assume that either
\overline{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{\mathbf{1}}}
or the triangular decomposition (
7
) is obtained by a hyperbolic element
x_{\circ}
.
Then
for every

[W]\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathsf{Rep}(\mathfrak{l}))
the induced module

\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\mathfrak{g}}(W)
has a unique maximal proper
\mathfrak{g}
-submodule.

Proof.

A proper submodule
M
of
\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\mathfrak{g}}(W)=\mathfrak{U}(\overline{\mathfrak{u}})\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}W
satisfies
M\cap(1\otimes W)=\{0\}
. Our strategy is to show that there is a proper subspace of
\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\mathfrak{g}}(W)
that contains every proper submodule; hence the sum of proper submodules remains proper.

First assume that
\overline{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{\mathbf{1}}}
. We follow an argument originally due to Shapovalov. It suffices to prove that every proper submodule of
\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\mathfrak{g}}(W)
is contained in
\mathfrak{U}(\overline{\mathfrak{u}})_{+}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}W
, where
\mathfrak{U}(\overline{\mathfrak{u}})_{+}
is the augmentation ideal of
\mathfrak{U}(\overline{\mathfrak{u}})
. To this end, we prove that any element of
\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\mathfrak{g}}(W)
outside
\mathfrak{U}(\overline{\mathfrak{u}})_{+}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}W
generates
\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\mathfrak{g}}(W)
. Take such an element

w\in\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\mathfrak{g}}(W)
.
From
\overline{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{\mathbf{1}}}
it follows that
\mathfrak{U}(\overline{\mathfrak{u}})
is isomorphic to the exterior algebra on
\overline{\mathfrak{u}}
, hence
\mathfrak{U}(\overline{\mathfrak{u}})
has a natural
\mathbb{Z}
-grading.
By irreducibility of
W
we can express
w
as
w=\sum_{k=0}^{N}w_{k}
where
w_{0}\neq 0
and
w_{k}\in\mathfrak{U}(\overline{\mathfrak{u}})^{(k)}\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{l})w_{0}

for
k\geq 1
. Thus

w=(1+u_{1}+\cdots+u_{N})w_{0},

where
u_{i}\in\mathfrak{U}(\overline{\mathfrak{u}})^{(k)}\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{l})
. Now
u:=u_{1}+\cdots+u_{N}
is nilpotent in
\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{l}\oplus\overline{\mathfrak{u}})
, hence
1+u
is invertible. Thus, the
\mathfrak{g}
-submodule generated by
w
contains
w_{0}
, and the latter generates
\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\mathfrak{g}}(W)
.

Next assume that the triangular decomposition is obtained by a hyperbolic
x_{\circ}
. From Remark
4.7
It follows immediately that
\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\mathfrak{g}}(W)
is a direct sum of eigenspaces for
x_{\circ}
. By Schur’s Lemma,
x_{\circ}
acts on the irreducible
\mathfrak{l}
-module
1\otimes W\subseteq\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\mathfrak{g}}(W)
by a scalar
c\in\mathbb{C}
.
The action of
\overline{\mathfrak{u}}
moves each eigenspace
\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\mathfrak{g}}(W)_{c^{\prime}}

of
\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\mathfrak{g}}(W)

with eigenvalue
c^{\prime}
into another one with eigenvalue
c^{\prime\prime}
where
\Re(c^{\prime\prime})<\Re(c^{\prime})
. Thus, we have
1\otimes W=\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\mathfrak{g}}(W)_{c}
. Consequently,
every proper submodule
M\subseteq\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\mathfrak{g}}(W)
must be a subspace of the sum of
x_{\circ}
-eigenspaces other than
c
, i.e.,

\textstyle M\subseteq\bigoplus_{c^{\prime}\neq c}\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\mathfrak{g}}(W)_{c^{\prime}}
.
The right hand side is a proper subspace of
\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\mathfrak{g}}(W)
.
∎

Definition 4.9
.

The triangular decomposition (
7
) is called of
graded type
if the following hold:

(i)

\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{g}^{(i)}
is
\mathbb{Z}
-graded, i.e., each
\mathfrak{g}^{(i)}
is
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded and

[\mathfrak{g}^{(i)},\mathfrak{g}^{(j)}]\subseteq\mathfrak{g}^{(i+j)}
.

(ii)

\mathfrak{l}=\mathfrak{g}^{(0)}
,
\mathfrak{u}=\bigoplus_{i\geq 1}\mathfrak{g}^{(i)}
and

\overline{\mathfrak{u}}=\bigoplus_{i\leq-1}\mathfrak{g}^{(i)}
.

(iii)

For some Borel subalgebra
\mathfrak{b}^{(0)}\subseteq(\mathfrak{g}^{(0)})_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}}
, if we set
\mathfrak{b}:=\mathfrak{b}^{(0)}\oplus\mathfrak{u}
then

[\mathfrak{b}_{\overline{\mathbf{1}}},\mathfrak{b}_{\overline{\mathbf{1}}}]\subseteq[\mathfrak{b}_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}},\mathfrak{b}_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}}]
and

\left[\mathfrak{b}^{(0)},(\mathfrak{g}^{(1)})_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}}\right]=(\mathfrak{g}^{(1)})_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}}
.

(iv)

\mathfrak{g}^{(1)}
generates
\mathfrak{u}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{(-1)}
generates
\overline{\mathfrak{u}}
.

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
\mathrm{Res}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}:\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{g}}\to\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{l}}
be the functor
W\mapsto W^{\mathfrak{u}}
.

(i)

Assume that the triangular decomposition (
7
) is of graded type. Then
\mathrm{Res}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}
induces a bijection
\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{g}})\to\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{l}})
.

(ii)

Assume that the triangular decomposition (
7
) is of hyperbolic type.
Then
\mathrm{Res}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}
induces an injection
\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{g}})\to\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{l}})
.

Proof.

(i) This is one of the steps in Kac’s classification of irreducible modules of Cartan-type and periplectic Lie superalgebras; see
[
7
, Prop 5.2.5]
. We remark that Kac’s arguments hold under the slightly weaker assumptions stated in Definition
4.9
.

(ii)
This is standard, but for the reader’s convenience we sketch the argument.
Assuming that
W^{\mathfrak{u}}
is irreducible, injectivity of
\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{g}})\to\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{l}})
follows from Lemma
4.8
and
the isomorphism
W\cong\underline{\mathrm{Ind}}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}(W^{\mathfrak{u}})
.
Next we prove that
W^{\mathfrak{u}}
is an irreducible
\mathfrak{l}
-module.
Suppose that there exists a proper irreducible
\mathfrak{l}
-submodule
W^{\prime}\subseteq W^{\mathfrak{u}}
. By Schur’s Lemma,
x_{\circ}
acts on
W^{\prime}
by a scalar
c\in\mathbb{C}
. By Remark
4.7
,
W
(and hence
W^{\mathfrak{u}}
) is a direct sum of
x_{\circ}
-eigenspaces.
For any
c^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C}
, let
W_{c^{\prime}}
be the
c^{\prime}
-eigenspace of
x_{\circ}
on
W
.
Every
W^{\prime\prime}:=W_{c^{\prime}}\cap W^{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq W^{\mathfrak{u}}
is an
\mathfrak{l}
-module and by the PBW Theorem if
W^{\prime\prime}\neq 0
then we have

W=\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{g})W^{\prime\prime}=\mathfrak{U}(\overline{\mathfrak{u}})W^{\prime\prime}
. It follows that the set of
x_{\circ}
-eigenvalues on
W
is a subset of
\left\{c^{\prime}-t\,:t\in\mathbb{R}^{\geq 0}\right\}
that contains
c^{\prime}
. From this, it follows that
x_{\circ}
acts on all of
W^{\mathfrak{u}}
by the same scalar
c
. But then
\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{g})W^{\prime}=\mathfrak{U}(\overline{\mathfrak{u}})W^{\prime}\subseteq W^{\prime}\oplus\bigoplus_{t>0}W_{c-t}
,
and the right hand side is a proper subspace of
W
. This contradicts irreducibility of
W
.
∎

.

Assume that the
\tau
-stable triangular decomposition (
7

\overline{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{\mathbf{1}}}
.
Then
for
[W]\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{g}})
, we have
\mathsf{ET}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}(W)=\mathsf{ET}_{\mathfrak{l},\tau}(W^{\mathfrak{u}})

Proof.

By Proposition
4.10
and Lemma
4.8
, the hypotheses of

4.5
are satisfied.
∎

4.3.
The reductive reduction

Recall that
\mathfrak{f}_{\mathfrak{g}}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
denotes the common kernel of the irreducible finite dimensional
\mathfrak{g}
-modules, and set
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}:=\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{f}_{\mathfrak{g}}
. We denote the image of
[W]\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{g}})
under the canonical
map

\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{g}})\to\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}})

by
[W]
. We have
\tau(\mathfrak{f}_{\mathfrak{g}})=\mathfrak{f}_{\mathfrak{g}}
and thus
\tau
induces an antilinear involution
\tau^{\prime}:\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
. The following assertion is trivial.

Proposition 4.12
.

For
[W]\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{g}})
we have
\mathsf{ET}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}(W)=\mathsf{ET}_{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}}(W)
.

In the rest of this subsection we assume that
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}}
. Let
\mathfrak{r}
denote the solvable radical of
\mathfrak{g}
, i.e., the sum of solvable ideals of
\mathfrak{g}
. Then
\mathfrak{r}
is
\tau
-stable and
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{r}
is semisimple. Furthermore,
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{r}]
is a
\tau
-stable ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Lemma 4.13
.

\mathfrak{g}/[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{r}]
is reductive.

Proof.

By Levi’s theorem, we have a semidirect sum decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{s}\ltimes\mathfrak{r}
where
\mathfrak{s}
is semisimple. Thus
\mathfrak{g}/[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{r}]\cong\mathfrak{s}\ltimes(\mathfrak{r}/[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{r}])
. Clearly
\mathfrak{r}/[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{r}]
is an abelian central ideal of
\mathfrak{g}/[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{r}]
. Hence
\mathfrak{g}/[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{r}]
is reductive.
∎

Lemma 4.14
.

Let
W
be an irreducible
\mathfrak{g}
-module. Then
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{r}]
acts on
W
trivially.

Proof.

By Lie’s theorem, every irreducible
\mathfrak{r}
-module is one-dimensional. Thus, there exists
\lambda\in\mathfrak{r}^{*}
such that the subspace
W_{\lambda}:=\{w\in W\,:\,x\cdot w=\lambda(x)w\text{ for all }x\in\mathfrak{r}\}

is nonzero. For
x\in\mathfrak{r}
, we have
\mathrm{tr}(x|_{W_{\lambda}})=\lambda(x)\dim W_{\lambda}
. By a well-known lemma from the proof of Lie’s theorem (for example see
[
4
, Lemma 9.13]
),
W_{\lambda}
is
\mathfrak{g}
-invariant. But then the elements of
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{r}]
are sums of commutators of linear maps on
W_{\lambda}
, hence their traces (as linear maps on
W_{\lambda}
) vanish. This proves that
\lambda(x)\dim W_{\lambda}=0
for
x\in[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{r}]
, hence
\lambda(x)=0
. In other words,
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{r}]
acts by zero on
W_{\lambda}
. Next set
W^{\prime}:=\{w\in W\,:\,x\cdot w=0\text{ for all }x\in[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{r}]\}
. Then
W_{\lambda}\subseteq W^{\prime}
, and
W^{\prime}
is a
\mathfrak{g}
-submodule (since
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{r}]
is an ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
), hence
W^{\prime}=W
.
∎

Since
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{r}]
is
\tau
-stable,
\tau
induces an antilinear involution on
\mathfrak{g}/[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{r}]
, which we will also denote by
\tau
. The following statement is an immediate consequence of Lemma
4.14
.

Theorem 4.15
.

Set
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{red}}:=\mathfrak{g}/[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{r}]
. For
[W]\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{g}})
we have
\mathsf{ET}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}(W)=\mathsf{ET}_{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{red}},\tau}(W)
.

5.
Endotypes for realified complex Lie superalgebras

The goal of this section is to prove Theorem
5.7
, which classifies endotypes of real superalgebras that have a complex structure. Given a complex Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}
,
its complex conjugate
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}
is the Lie superalgebra with the same superbracket but equipped with the conjugate structure as a complex vector space. If
W
is a
\mathfrak{g}
-module, then
\overline{W}
is a
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}
-module, and vice versa.
We have a
\mathbb{C}
-linear isomorphism

\mathfrak{g}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}\to\mathfrak{g}\oplus\overline{\mathfrak{g}}\ ,\ x\otimes z\mapsto(zx,z\bar{x}),

where to avoid confusion we denote elements of
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}
by
\bar{x}
for
x\in\mathfrak{g}
.
The Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}
is the real form of
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\overline{\mathfrak{g}}
corresponding to
\tau(x,\bar{y})=(y,\bar{x})
.

Remark 5.1
.

Recall that for a
\mathfrak{g}
-module
W
, we use
{}^{\sigma}W
to denote the twist of
W
by the automorphism
x\mapsto(-1)^{|x|}x
of
\mathfrak{g}
. Then
{}^{\sigma}W\cong W
as
\mathfrak{g}
-modules via the map
w\mapsto(-1)^{|w|}w
.

Remark 5.2
.

Let
W^{\prime}
and
W^{\prime\prime}
be modules for Lie superalgebras
\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{b}
, and let
\mathbb{D}
be defined as in (
6
). Then we have
\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{b}
-module isomorphisms

W^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathbb{D}}W^{\prime\prime}\cong\Pi W^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathbb{D}}\Pi W^{\prime\prime}
given by
v\otimes w\mapsto(-1)^{|w|}v\otimes w
, and
\Pi(W^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathbb{D}}W^{\prime\prime})\cong W^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathbb{D}}\Pi W^{\prime\prime}
given by the identity map.

In the rest of this section we assume that
[W]\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus\overline{\mathfrak{g}}})
. By Proposition
3.3
we have
W=W^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathbb{D}}W^{\prime\prime}
where
W^{\prime}
and
W^{\prime\prime}
are irreducible modules of
\mathfrak{g}
and
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}
, respectively.

Lemma 5.3
.

\Pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{W}
if and only if
\Pi
belongs to exactly one of
\mathfrak{S}_{W^{\prime}}
and
\mathfrak{S}_{W^{\prime\prime}}
.

Proof.

Note that
\Pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{W}
if and only if

\mathrm{End}_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus{\overline{\mathfrak{g}}}}(W^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathbb{D}}W^{\prime\prime})\cong\mathrm{Q}(1)
.
The assertion follows from general facts about tensor products of modules of types
\mathtt{M}
and
\mathtt{Q}

[
3
, Sec. 3.1.3]
.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Assume that
W\cong\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus\overline{\mathfrak{g}},\tau}W
, and the isomorphism corresponds to the even antilinear map
T:W^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathbb{D}}W^{\prime\prime}\to W^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathbb{D}}W^{\prime\prime}
. Fix a nonzero
w_{0}\in W^{\prime\prime}_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}}
. Then there exist
v_{0}\in W^{\prime}_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}}
,
v_{1}\in W^{\prime}_{\overline{\mathbf{1}}}
, an even antilinear map
T_{0}:W^{\prime}\to W^{\prime\prime}
, and an odd antilinear map
T_{1}:W^{\prime}\to W^{\prime\prime}
satisfying the following:

(i)

T(v\otimes w_{0})=v_{0}\otimes_{\mathbb{D}}T_{0}v+v_{1}\otimes_{\mathbb{D}}T_{1}v
for
v\in W^{\prime}
.

(ii)

T_{0}x=\bar{x}T_{0}
and
T_{1}x=(-1)^{|x|}\bar{x}T_{1}
for
x\in\mathfrak{g}
.

If
\mathbb{D}\cong\mathrm{Q}(1)
, then we can assume that
T_{1}=0
.

Proof.

Fix a homogeneous
\mathbb{D}
-basis
v_{1},\ldots,v_{p},v_{\bar{1}},\ldots,v_{\bar{q}}
of
W^{\prime}
, where the first
p
are in
W^{\prime}_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}}
and the last
q
are in
W^{\prime}_{\overline{\mathbf{1}}}
(and if
\mathbb{D}=\mathrm{Q}(1)
then we assume
q=0
). Then we have

T(v\otimes_{\mathbb{D}}w_{0})=\sum_{i=1}^{p}v_{i}\otimes_{\mathbb{D}}T_{i}v+\sum_{i=1}^{p}v_{\bar{i}}\otimes_{\mathbb{D}}T_{\bar{i}}v\quad\text{for }v\in W^{\prime},

where the
T_{i}:W^{\prime}\to{W^{\prime\prime}}
are even antilinear maps that satisfy
T_{i}x=\bar{x}T_{i}
for
x\in\mathfrak{g}
, whereas the
T_{\bar{i}}:W^{\prime}\to{W^{\prime\prime}}
are odd antilinear maps that satisfy
T_{\bar{i}}x=(-1)^{|x|}\bar{x}T_{\bar{i}}
.

By Schur’s Lemma, any two
T_{i}
are related by a scalar in
\mathbb{D}_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}}
.
This allows us to combine the summands
v_{i}\otimes_{\mathbb{D}}T_{i}v
into one term
v_{0}\otimes_{\mathbb{D}}T_{0}v
.
The same argument applies to the summands
v_{\bar{i}}\otimes_{\mathbb{D}}T_{\bar{i}}v
.
∎

Proposition 5.5
.

We have
(\mathcal{B},+)\in\mathfrak{S}_{W}
if and only if either
W^{\prime}\cong\overline{W^{\prime\prime}}
or
W^{\prime}\cong\Pi\overline{W^{\prime\prime}}
as
\mathfrak{g}
-modules. Furthermore,
(\mathcal{B},-)\not\in\mathfrak{S}_{W}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{S}_{W}\cap\{(\mathcal{B},+),(\mathcal{B},-)\}\neq\varnothing
.
By Remark
5.2
we can assume that
W^{\prime\prime}_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}}
is nonzero.
Now in Lemma
5.4
,
if
T_{0}\neq 0
then
\overline{W^{\prime\prime}}\cong W^{\prime}
, and if
T_{1}\neq 0
then
{{\overline{W^{\prime\prime}}}}\cong\Pi W^{\prime}
(for the latter, we use the isomorphism
W^{\prime\prime}\cong{{}^{\sigma}W^{\prime\prime}}
; see Remark
5.1
).

Conversely, if
\overline{W^{\prime\prime}}\cong W^{\prime}
then define

T:W^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathbb{D}}\overline{W^{\prime}}\to W^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathbb{D}}\overline{W^{\prime}}\ ,\ v\otimes_{\mathbb{D}}w\mapsto(-1)^{|v|\cdot|w|}w\otimes_{\mathbb{D}}v,

and if
\overline{W^{\prime\prime}}\cong\Pi W^{\prime}
then define

T:W^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathbb{D}}\Pi\overline{W^{\prime}}\to W^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathbb{D}}\Pi\overline{W^{\prime}}\ ,\ v\otimes_{\mathbb{D}}w\mapsto(-1)^{|v|\cdot|w|+|v|}w\otimes_{\mathbb{D}}v.

In both cases,
T
is even, antilinear, and satisfies

for
(x,\bar{y})\in\mathfrak{g}\oplus\overline{\mathfrak{g}}

is the identity map. This proves that in both cases we have
(\mathcal{B},+)\in\mathfrak{S}_{W}
, and thus
(\mathcal{B},-)\not\in\mathfrak{S}_{W}
(this follows from Table

).
∎

Proposition 5.6
.

\{(\Pi\mathcal{B},+),(\Pi\mathcal{B},-)\}\cap\mathfrak{S}_{W}=\varnothing
.

Proof.

Suppose that
W\cong\Pi\mathcal{B}W
and the isomorphism corresponds to the odd, antilinear map
T:W^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathbb{D}}W^{\prime\prime}\to W^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathbb{D}}W^{\prime\prime}
.
By Remark
5.2
, we can assume that
W^{\prime\prime}_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}}
is nonzero. As in Lemma
5.4
, for a nonzero

we can write
T
as

(11)

T(v\otimes_{\mathbb{D}}w_{0})=v_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}}\otimes_{\mathbb{D}}T_{1}v+v_{\overline{\mathbf{1}}}\otimes_{\mathbb{D}}T_{0}v,

where
T_{0}:W^{\prime}\to\overline{W^{\prime\prime}}
is an even linear map that satisfies
T_{0}x=(-1)^{|x|}xT_{0}
for
x\in\mathfrak{g}
, while
T_{1}:W^{\prime}\to\overline{W^{\prime\prime}}
is an odd linear map that satisfies
T_{1}x=xT_{1}
for
x\in\mathfrak{g}
.
(If
\mathbb{D}=\mathrm{Q}(1)

Assume that

. Then
W^{\prime}\cong{{}^{\sigma}\overline{W^{\prime\prime}}}\cong\overline{W^{\prime\prime}}
and by Proposition

we have
(\mathcal{B},+)\in\mathfrak{S}_{W}
.
Since

is odd, this also proves that
W^{\prime}_{\overline{\mathbf{1}}}
and
W^{\prime\prime}_{\overline{\mathbf{1}}}
are nonzero. But now choosing a nonzero

(12)

T(v_{1}\otimes_{\mathbb{D}}w)=S_{0}w\otimes_{\mathbb{D}}w^{\prime\prime}_{0}+S_{1}w\otimes_{\mathbb{D}}w^{\prime\prime}_{1},

where

6.1.
Endotypes for Clifford-type Lie superalgebras

In this subsection we assume that
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{h}_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{\overline{\mathbf{1}}}
is a Lie superalgebra such that
[\mathfrak{h}_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}},\mathfrak{h}]=0
. Let
\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{R}}
be a real form of
\mathfrak{h}
corresponding to an antilinear involution
\tau:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{h}
. Given an irreducible
\mathfrak{h}
-module
(\rho,W)
, by Schur’s Lemma there exists
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}}^{*}
such that
\rho(h)v=\lambda(h)1_{W}
for every
h\in\mathfrak{h}_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}}
.
We define a symmetric bilinear form

B_{\lambda}:\mathfrak{h}_{\overline{\mathbf{1}}}\times\mathfrak{h}_{\overline{\mathbf{1}}}\to\mathbb{C}\ ,\ B_{\lambda}(x,y):=\lambda([x,y]).

Set
R_{\lambda}:=\mathrm{Rad}(B_{\lambda})
. Then
W
is an irreducible module for
U(\mathfrak{h})/I_{\lambda}
, where
I_{\lambda}
is the ideal of
U(\mathfrak{h})
generated by
R_{\lambda}
and the elements
h-\lambda(h)1
for
h\in\mathfrak{h}_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}}
. Note that
U(\mathfrak{h})/I_{\lambda}
is isomorphic to the complex Clifford superalgebra
\mathrm{Cl}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
of rank
r:=\dim(\mathfrak{h}_{\overline{\mathbf{1}}}/R_{\lambda})
.

The action of
\mathfrak{h}_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}}
on both
\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{h},\tau}W
and
\Pi\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{h},\tau}W
is by the
\mathbb{C}
-linear functional
\overline{\lambda\circ\tau}
. Thus, if
\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{h}}}(W,\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{h},\tau}W)\neq 0
then
we must have
\lambda=\overline{\lambda\circ\tau}
.
Indeed it follows from Proposition
6.1
below that the converse also holds. Assume that
\lambda=\overline{\lambda\circ\tau}
. Then

B_{\lambda}(\tau(x),\tau(y))=\lambda\circ\tau([x,y])=\overline{\lambda([x,y])}=\overline{B_{\lambda}(x,y)}.

In particular, the restriction of
B_{\lambda}
to
\mathfrak{h}_{\overline{\mathbf{1}}}^{\mathbb{R}}\times\mathfrak{h}_{\overline{\mathbf{1}}}^{\mathbb{R}}
is real-valued, with radical equal to

R_{\lambda}\cap\mathfrak{h}_{\overline{\mathbf{1}}}^{\mathbb{R}}
.
Thus,
B_{\lambda}
induces a non-degenerate real symmetric bilinear form

(13)

B_{\lambda,\tau}:\mathfrak{h}_{{\overline{\mathbf{1}}},\lambda}\times\mathfrak{h}_{{\overline{\mathbf{1}}},\lambda}\to\mathbb{R}\quad,\quad\mathfrak{h}_{{\overline{\mathbf{1}}},\lambda}:=\mathfrak{h}_{\overline{\mathbf{1}}}^{\mathbb{R}}/R_{\lambda}\cap\mathfrak{h}_{\overline{\mathbf{1}}}^{\mathbb{R}}.

Suppose that the signature of
B_{\lambda,\tau}
is
(p,q)
, i.e., the quadratic form
{B}_{\lambda,\tau}(x,x)
is equivalent to
\sum_{k=1}^{p}x_{k}^{2}-\sum_{k=1}^{q}x_{p+k}^{2}
. Then

\tau
induces an antilinear involution on
\mathrm{Cl}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
, whose fixed subalgebra is isomorphic to the real Clifford algebra
\mathrm{Cl}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
[W]\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{h}})
and assume that
W
has central character
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}}^{*}
.

(i)

If
\lambda\neq\overline{\lambda\circ\tau}
, then
\mathsf{ET}_{\mathfrak{h},\tau}(W)=r_{\mathbb{C}}
for
r:=\mathrm{rank}(B_{\lambda})\mod 2
.

(ii)

If
\lambda=\overline{\lambda\circ\tau}
, then
\mathsf{ET}_{{\mathfrak{h},\tau}}(W)=t_{\mathbb{R}}
for

t:=q-p\mod 8
, where
(p,q)
is the signature of
B_{\lambda,\tau}
.

Proof.

Set
s:={\lfloor\frac{r-1}{2}\rfloor}
. As superalgebras we have

(14)

\mathrm{Cl}_{r}(\mathbb{C})\cong\mathrm{Cl}_{1}(\mathbb{C})^{\otimes r}\cong\mathrm{Q}(1)^{\otimes r}\cong\begin{cases}\mathrm{Mat}_{2^{s}|2^{s}}(\mathbb{C})&r\text{ even},\\
\mathrm{Q}(2^{s})&r\text{ odd}.\end{cases}

It follows that
W\cong\Pi W
if and only if
r
is odd. This proves (i).

In the rest of the proof we assume that
W:=\mathbb{C}^{1|1}
is the unique irreducible
\mathrm{Cl}_{1}(\mathbb{C})
-module. Note that

\mathrm{Cl}_{1}(\mathbb{C})\cong\mathrm{Q}(1)
and

\mathrm{Cl}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})\cong\mathrm{Cl}_{1,0}(\mathbb{R})^{\otimes p}\otimes\mathrm{Cl}_{0,1}(\mathbb{R})^{\otimes q}
. The real forms
\mathrm{Cl}_{1,0}(\mathbb{R})
and
\mathrm{Cl}_{0,1}(\mathbb{R})
of
\mathrm{Q}(1)
correspond to the antilinear involutions

\tau_{\pm}:\mathrm{Q}(1)\to\mathrm{Q}(1)\ ,\ a+b\iota\mapsto\bar{a}\pm\bar{b}\iota.

In both cases we have
\Pi,(\mathcal{B},+)\in\mathfrak{S}_{W}
, with the corresponding isomorphisms
\phi_{\pm}:W\to\mathcal{B}W
given by
z\oplus w\mapsto\bar{z}\oplus\pm\bar{w}
. Now let
\pi:W\to\Pi W
be the parity switch and set
\phi:=\pi\circ\phi_{\pm}
. We denote the action map by
\rho:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{gl}(W)
. Then
\phi:W\to\Pi\mathcal{B}W
is an antilinear map that satisfies
\phi\rho(\tau(x))=\rho(x)\phi
for
x\in\mathrm{Q}(1)
, and we need to compute
\mathbf{c}(\phi)
. We have
\pi\circ\phi_{\pm}\circ\pi=\pm\phi_{\pm}
, hence
\phi^{2}=\pm\phi_{\pm}\circ\phi_{\pm}=\pm 1_{W}
, where the sign
\pm
agrees with the subscript of
\tau_{\pm}
. This proves that

(15)

\mathsf{ET}_{\mathrm{Q}(1),\tau}(W)=\begin{cases}7_{\mathbb{R}}&\text{ if }\tau=\tau_{+},\\
1_{\mathbb{R}}&\text{ if }\tau=\tau_{-}.\end{cases}

The assertion of the proposition follows from (
15
) and Proposition
3.4
.
∎

6.2.
Some general Lie theory notation

Given a Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
, if
\mathfrak{g}
has a root space decomposition
with respect to
\mathfrak{h}_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}}
then we denote the corresponding root system of
\mathfrak{g}
by
\Phi_{\mathfrak{g}}
.
We suppress
\mathfrak{h}
in this notation as it will always be clear from the context. The root space of
\mathfrak{g}
associated to
\alpha\in\Phi_{\mathfrak{g}}
will be denoted
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
.

If
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}}
is reductive, then
G_{\bullet}
denotes the connected and simply connected complex semisimple algebraic group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{\bullet}:=[\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}},\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}}]
, and

H_{\bullet}
denotes the Cartan subgroup of
G_{\bullet}
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\bullet}
. The restriction of

\tau
to
\mathfrak{g}_{\bullet}
can be integrated to an anti-holomorphic
involution of
G_{\bullet}
, which we will still denote by
\tau
.
Every finite dimensional
\mathfrak{g}_{\bullet}
-module
(\rho,W)
can be integrated to a representation of
G_{\bullet}
, which we still denote by
\rho
. Every
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}_{\bullet}^{*}
that occurs as a weight in
(\rho,W)
is then integrated to a multiplicative character
\lambda:H_{\bullet}\to\mathbb{C}^{*}
. The integrated adjoint representation of
\mathfrak{g}_{\bullet}
on
\mathfrak{g}
is denoted
\mathrm{Ad}:G_{\bullet}\to\mathrm{GL}(\mathfrak{g})
.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{g}
is either of basic type or of type
\mathbf{Q}(n)
. Any positive system
\Phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{+}
of
\Phi_{\mathfrak{g}}
corresponds to a Borel subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\mathfrak{b}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
.
We denote the fundamental system of
\Phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{+}
by
\Pi_{\mathfrak{b}}
.
For
w\in N_{G_{\bullet}}(H_{\bullet})
, we denote the positive system of the Borel
\mathrm{Ad}_{w}\mathfrak{b}
by
w\cdot\Pi_{\mathfrak{b}}
.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{g}
is of basic type (so that
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{h}_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}}
). If
\alpha\in\Pi_{\mathfrak{b}}
is isotropic, we use

r_{\mathfrak{g},\alpha}\cdot\Pi_{\mathfrak{b}}
to denote the fundamental system obtained from
\Pi_{\mathfrak{b}}
by the odd reflection associated to
\alpha
.
Thus,
r_{\mathfrak{g},\alpha}\cdot\Pi_{\mathfrak{b}}
corresponds to the positive system
(\Phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{+}\cup\{-\alpha\})\backslash\{\alpha\}
.

Remark 6.2
.

We continue with
\mathfrak{g}
of basic type. Let
\mathfrak{b}_{\alpha}
denote the Borel subalgebra corresponding to
r_{\mathfrak{g},\alpha}\Pi_{\mathfrak{b}}
, where
\alpha
is an isotropic root.
If
\mathfrak{g}
is not isomorphic to
\mathfrak{sl}(2|2)
or
\mathfrak{psl}(2|2)
, then the subalgebra
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha}\oplus[\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha},\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha}]
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{sl}(1|1)
. This implies that for an irreducible
\mathfrak{g}
-module
W
with
\mathfrak{b}
-highest weight
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
, the
\mathfrak{b}_{\alpha}
-highest weight of
W
is

\lambda_{\alpha}:=\begin{cases}\lambda&\text{ if }\lambda([\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha},\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha}])=0,\\
\lambda-\alpha&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

However, for
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}(2|2)
or
\mathfrak{psl}(2|2)
, the subalgebra

\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha}\oplus[\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha},\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha}]
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{sl}(1|1)\oplus\mathfrak{sl}(1|1)
or
(\mathfrak{sl}(1|1)\oplus\mathfrak{sl}(1|1))/\mathbb{C}
.
In these cases, the computation of the
\mathfrak{b}_{\alpha}
-highest weight of
W
is explained in Proposition
B.2
.

In this subsection
\mathfrak{g}

Remark 6.3

The Lie superalgebras

\mathfrak{g}

Lemma 6.4

Let

and assume that the

-highest weight of

By Theorems

Step 1:

-highest weights
that lie in the same

In the following lemma (as well as the other assertions of this subsection), when
\mathfrak{g}

\mathbf{Q}(n)

k=0

Lemma 6.5
.

There exist
w\in N_{G_{\bullet}}(H_{\bullet})
and isotropic roots
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k}\in\Phi_{\mathfrak{g}}
such that
w^{-1}\tau(w^{-1})\in H_{\bullet}
and

(16)

\Pi_{\tau(\mathfrak{b})}=w\cdot(r_{\mathfrak{g},\alpha_{k}}\cdot(\cdots(r_{\mathfrak{g},\alpha_{1}}\cdot\Pi_{\mathfrak{b}}))).

Proof.

\tau(\mathfrak{b}_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}})
and
\mathfrak{b}_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}}
are Borel subalgebras of
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}}
, therefore they are conjugate by an element of the Weyl group
N_{G_{\bullet}}(H_{\bullet})/H_{\bullet}
. This proves that

(17)

\mathrm{Ad}_{w^{-1}}(\tau(\mathfrak{b}_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}}))=\mathfrak{b}_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}}\quad\text{for some }w\in N_{G_{\bullet}}(H_{\bullet}).

The two Borel subalgebras
\mathrm{Ad}_{w^{-1}}(\tau(\mathfrak{b}))
and
\mathfrak{b}
of
\mathfrak{g}
have identical even parts, therefore they are related by a sequence of odd reflections. This completes the proof of (
16
).

Next we prove
w^{-1}\tau(w^{-1})\in H_{\bullet}
. Taking
\tau
of both sides of (
17
)
yields

\mathrm{Ad}_{\tau(w^{-1})}(\mathfrak{b}_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}})=\tau(\mathfrak{b}_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}})

hence

\mathrm{Ad}_{w^{-1}\tau(w^{-1})}(\mathfrak{b}_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}})=\mathrm{Ad}_{w^{-1}}(\mathrm{Ad}_{\tau(w^{-1})}\mathfrak{b}_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}})=\mathfrak{b}_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}}.

We have
\tau(H_{\bullet})=H_{\bullet}
, hence
w^{-1}\tau(w^{-1})\in N_{G_{\bullet}}(H_{\bullet})
. Since the Weyl group acts simply transitively on positive systems, we must have
w^{-1}\tau(w^{-1})\in H_{\bullet}
.
∎

\mathfrak{g}

\mathrm{HC}_{\mathfrak{b}}:\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{g})\to\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{h})

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}}
, then
c_{\lambda}
is obtained by the evaluation of
\lambda
at a semisimple element of
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
. One interesting outcome of Theorem
7.11
is that it establishes a connection between computing endotypes and Kostant’s cascade of strongly orthogonal roots.

We retain the notation of
Section
6
.
We denote the centre of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{m}
by
\mathrm{Z}(\mathfrak{m})
, and the centralizer of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{s}
in the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{m}
by
\mathrm{Z}_{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathfrak{s})
.

7.1.
Cartan decomposition and compact Cartans

We begin with some general facts about semisimple Lie algebras with a compact Cartan subalgebra.
In this subsection
\mathfrak{g}
will be a semisimple Lie algebra.
To the real form
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}
of
\mathfrak{g}
corresponding to the antilinear involution
\tau:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
, one can associate a
Cartan involution
, i.e., a
\mathbb{C}
-linear involution
\theta:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
with the following properties:

(i)

\theta\tau=\tau\theta
. This implies that
\theta(\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}})=\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}
.

(ii)

The antilinear involution
\theta\tau
corresponds to the compact real form of
\mathfrak{g}
.

(iii)

The symmetric bilinear form

\kappa_{\theta}:\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}\times\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by
\kappa_{\theta}(x,y):=-\kappa(x,\theta(y))
is positive definite. Here
\kappa(\cdot,\cdot)
denotes the Killing form of
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}
.

Let

\mathfrak{k}^{\mathbb{R}}
and
\mathfrak{p}^{\mathbb{R}}
be the
+1
and
-1
eigenspaces of
\theta
, respectively.
The decomposition

(24)

\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}=\mathfrak{k}^{\mathbb{R}}\oplus\mathfrak{p}^{\mathbb{R}}

is called the Cartan decomposition of
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}
. Complexifying (
24
), we obtain a decomposition

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}.

Let
\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{R}}
be a
\theta
-stable Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}
, i.e.,
\theta(\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{R}})=\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{R}}
. Then
\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{R}}=\mathfrak{t}^{\mathbb{R}}\oplus\mathfrak{a}^{\mathbb{R}}
where
\mathfrak{t}^{\mathbb{R}}:=\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{R}}\cap\mathfrak{k}^{\mathbb{R}}
and
\mathfrak{a}^{\mathbb{R}}:=\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{R}}\cap\mathfrak{p}^{\mathbb{R}}
.

Definition 7.1
.

We say
\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{R}}
is a
compact Cartan subalgebra
if
\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{R}}=\mathfrak{t}^{\mathbb{R}}
.

Recall that
\Phi_{\mathfrak{g}}
denotes the root system of
\mathfrak{g}
(with respect to an a priori chosen Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
).

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a complex semisimple Lie algebra and let
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}
,
\tau
,
\theta
,
\mathfrak{k}^{\mathbb{R}}
, and
\mathfrak{p}^{\mathbb{R}}
be as above. Let
\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{R}}
be a compact
\theta
-stable Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}
, with complexification
\mathfrak{h}
. Then

\tau(\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha})=\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha}

for every
\alpha\in\Phi_{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Proof.

For any
h\in\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{R}}
, the map
\mathrm{ad}_{h}:\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}
is skew-symmetric because

\kappa_{\theta}(\mathrm{ad}_{h}x,y)=-\kappa(\mathrm{ad}_{h}x,\theta(y))=\kappa(x,\mathrm{ad}_{h}\theta(y))=\kappa(x,\theta(\mathrm{ad}_{h}y))=-\kappa_{\theta}(x,\mathrm{ad}_{h}y).

It follows that the eigenvalues of
\mathrm{ad}_{h}
are imaginary.
Now for
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
and
h\in\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{R}}
we have

\mathrm{ad}_{h}\tau(x)=[h,\tau(x)]=\tau([h,x])=\tau(\alpha(h)x)=\overline{\alpha(h)}\tau(x)=-\alpha(h)\tau(x).

Since
\alpha
is
\mathbb{C}
-linear, it follows that
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha}
.
∎

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a complex reductive Lie algebra with a real form
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}
. Let
\theta
be a Cartan involution of
[\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}},\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}]
. Let
\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{R}}
be a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}
, with complexification
\mathfrak{h}
, such that
\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{R}}\cap[\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}},\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}]
is a compact
\theta
-stable Cartan subalgebra of
[\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}},\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}]
.
Then for every Borel subalgebra
\mathfrak{b}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
that satisfies
\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\mathfrak{b}
we have
\tau(\mathfrak{b})=\mathfrak{b}^{\mathrm{op}}
.

Proof.

This follows from Lemma
7.2
, noting that Borel subalgebras of
\mathfrak{g}
are of the form
\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}\oplus\mathrm{Z}(\mathfrak{g})
for a Borel subalgebra
\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}
of
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
.
∎

Lemma 7.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a semisimple Lie algebra with a real form
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}
, and let
\theta
be a Cartan involution of
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}
. Let
\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{R}}=\mathfrak{t}^{\mathbb{R}}\oplus\mathfrak{a}^{\mathbb{R}}
be a
\theta
-stable Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
, and set
\mathfrak{l}^{\mathbb{R}}:=\mathrm{Z}_{\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}}(\mathfrak{a}^{\mathbb{R}})
. Then
\theta|_{[\mathfrak{l}^{\mathbb{R}},\mathfrak{l}^{\mathbb{R}}]}
is a Cartan involution of
[\mathfrak{l}^{\mathbb{R}},\mathfrak{l}^{\mathbb{R}}]
and
\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{R}}\cap[\mathfrak{l}^{\mathbb{R}},\mathfrak{l}^{\mathbb{R}}]
is a compact
\theta
-stable Cartan subalgebra of
[\mathfrak{l}^{\mathbb{R}},\mathfrak{l}^{\mathbb{R}}]
.

Proof.

As before, let
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{a}
denote the complexification of
\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{R}}=\mathfrak{t}^{\mathbb{R}}\oplus\mathfrak{a}^{\mathbb{R}}
and let
\Phi_{\mathfrak{g}}
denote the
\mathfrak{h}
-root system of
\mathfrak{g}
. The complexification
\mathfrak{l}
of
\mathfrak{l}^{\mathbb{R}}
is a Levi subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
, with Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
, and with root system

\Phi_{\mathfrak{l}}:=\{\alpha\in\Phi_{\mathfrak{g}}\,:\,\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbb{R}}\subseteq\ker(\alpha)\}
. Henceforth we assume
\Phi_{\mathfrak{l}}\neq\varnothing
, as otherwise there is nothing to prove.

Let
\kappa
and
\kappa^{\prime}
denote the Killing forms of
\mathfrak{g}
and
[\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{l}]
, respectively. The restriction of
\kappa
to
[\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{l}]
is a non-degenerate invariant form, hence
\kappa^{\prime}=c\kappa
for a nonzero scalar
c
. Note that
c>0
because for a coroot vector
h_{\alpha}
with
\alpha\in\Phi_{\mathfrak{l}}
we have

\kappa(h_{\alpha},h_{\alpha})=\sum_{\beta\in\Phi_{\mathfrak{g}}}\beta(h_{\alpha})^{2}>0
, and for a similar reason
\kappa^{\prime}(h_{\alpha},h_{\alpha})>0
. Furthermore,
\theta
restricts to an involution of
[\mathfrak{l}^{\mathbb{R}},\mathfrak{l}^{\mathbb{R}}]
, and
\kappa_{\theta}
is positive definite on
[\mathfrak{l}^{\mathbb{R}},\mathfrak{l}^{\mathbb{R}}]
. These prove that
\theta
induces a Cartan involution of
[\mathfrak{l}^{\mathbb{R}},\mathfrak{l}^{\mathbb{R}}]
.

We have
\mathfrak{l}=[\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{l}]\oplus\mathrm{Z}(\mathfrak{l})
where

\mathrm{Z}(\mathfrak{l})=\bigcap_{\alpha\in\Phi_{\mathfrak{l}}}\ker(\alpha)
, and
\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}:=\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi_{\mathfrak{l}}}[\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha},\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha}]
is a Cartan subalgebra of
[\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{l}]
(see for example
[
9
, Cor 5.94]
).
Clearly
\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathfrak{h}
, hence
\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{h}\cap[\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{l}]
.
This in particular implies that
\tau(\mathfrak{h}^{\prime})=\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}
.
Consequently, the
\tau
-fixed real subalgebra of
\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}
is a Cartan subalgebra
(\mathfrak{h}^{\prime})^{\mathbb{R}}
of
[\mathfrak{l}^{\mathbb{R}},\mathfrak{l}^{\mathbb{R}}]
.
Since
\theta(\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha})=\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in\Phi_{\mathfrak{l}}
,
it follows that
\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}
is
\theta
-stable. From
\theta\tau=\tau\theta
it follows that
(\mathfrak{h}^{\prime})^{\mathbb{R}}
is
\theta
-stable as well.
We have
\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}\cap\mathfrak{a}\subseteq[\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{l}]\cap\mathfrak{a}=\{0\}
,
hence
\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathfrak{t}
. Taking
\tau
-fixed points we obtain
(\mathfrak{h}^{\prime})^{\mathbb{R}}\subseteq\mathfrak{t}^{\mathbb{R}}\subseteq\mathfrak{k}^{\mathbb{R}}
.
Thus,
(\mathfrak{h}^{\prime})^{\mathbb{R}}=\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{R}}\cap[\mathfrak{l}^{\mathbb{R}},\mathfrak{l}^{\mathbb{R}}]
is a compact
\theta
-stable Cartan subalgebra of
[\mathfrak{l}^{\mathbb{R}},\mathfrak{l}^{\mathbb{R}}]
.

∎

it follows that

Lemma 7.5
.

Every
\alpha\in\Phi_{\mathfrak{g}}
is real-valued on
i\mathfrak{t}^{\mathbb{R}}\oplus\mathfrak{a}^{\mathbb{R}}
.

Proof.

When
\alpha\in\Phi_{\mathfrak{g}_{\bullet}}
, the argument is similar to the proof of Lemma
7.2
: again the map
\mathrm{ad}_{h}:\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}

and skew symmetric for

(see also

-weights of finite dimensional

-modules. By considering

as a
\mathfrak{g}_{\bullet}
-module we obtain the assertion for all

.
∎

We now construct the
\tau
-compatible Borel subalgebras of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Definition 7.6
.

Let
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{h}_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{\overline{\mathbf{1}}}
be the Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
corresponding to
\mathfrak{h}_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}}
defined in (
25
).
Let
\mathfrak{l}
be the complexification of
\mathfrak{l}^{\mathbb{R}}
, defined in (
26
).
We choose any positive system
\Phi_{\mathfrak{l}}^{+}
for
\Phi_{\mathfrak{l}}
.
Then we choose
h_{\circ}\in\mathfrak{a}^{\mathbb{R}}
such that for every
\alpha\in\Phi_{\mathfrak{g}}\backslash\Phi_{\mathfrak{l}}
we have
\alpha(h_{\circ})\neq 0
, and
we extend
\Phi_{\mathfrak{l}}^{+}
to a positive system
\Phi^{+}_{\mathfrak{g}}
of
\Phi_{\mathfrak{g}}
by setting

\Phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{+}:=\Phi_{\mathfrak{l}}^{+}\cup\Phi^{+}_{\mathfrak{u}},\quad\text{ where}\quad\Phi^{+}_{\mathfrak{u}}:=\{\alpha\in\Phi_{\mathfrak{g}}\,:\,\alpha(h_{\circ})>0\}.

Let
\mathfrak{b}_{\mathfrak{l}}
and
\mathfrak{b}
be the Borel subalgebras of
\mathfrak{l}
and
\mathfrak{g}
that contain
\mathfrak{h}
and correspond to
\Phi_{\mathfrak{l}}^{+}
and
\Phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{+}
, so that

(28)

\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{b}_{\mathfrak{l}}\oplus\mathfrak{u}\quad\text{ for }\mathfrak{u}:=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Phi_{\mathfrak{u}}^{+}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}.

We call
\mathfrak{b}
the
\tau
-compatible
Borel subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
associated to
\Phi^{+}_{\mathfrak{l}}
and
h_{\circ}
.
We denote the fundamental systems of
\mathfrak{b}_{\mathfrak{l}}
and
\mathfrak{b}
by
\Pi_{\mathfrak{b}_{\mathfrak{l}}}
and
\Pi_{\mathfrak{b}}
. Note that

\Pi_{\mathfrak{b}_{\mathfrak{l}}}\subseteq\Phi_{\mathfrak{l}}^{+}\subseteq\Phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{+}
.

Lemma 7.7
.

We have
\Pi_{\mathfrak{b}_{\mathfrak{l}}}=\Pi_{\mathfrak{b}}\cap\Phi_{\mathfrak{l}}
.

Proof.

By definition, the elements of
\Pi_{\mathfrak{b}_{\mathfrak{l}}}
are the roots in
\Phi_{\mathfrak{l}}^{+}
that cannot be expressed as a sum of two roots in
\Phi_{\mathfrak{l}}^{+}
. Let
\alpha\in\Pi_{\mathfrak{b}_{\mathfrak{l}}}
. If
\alpha\not\in\Pi_{\mathfrak{b}}
, then
\alpha=\beta+\gamma
for some
\beta,\gamma\in\Phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{+}
. At least one of
\beta,\gamma
must be in
\Phi_{\mathfrak{u}}^{+}
. Thus,

0=\alpha(h_{\circ})=\beta(h_{\circ})+\gamma(h_{\circ})>0
,
which is a contradiction. This proves
\Pi_{\mathfrak{b}_{\mathfrak{l}}}\subseteq\Pi_{\mathfrak{b}}\cap\Phi_{\mathfrak{l}}
. The reverse inclusion is trivial.
∎

Let
G_{\bullet}
and
H_{\bullet}
be as in Subsection
6.2
, and
let
L_{\bullet}
be the connected algebraic subgroup of
G_{\bullet}
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{l}_{\bullet}
.

Remark 7.8
.

Let
L_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}}\subseteq G_{\bullet}
be the connected algebraic subgroup of
G_{\bullet}
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{l}_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}}
. Then
[L_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}},L_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}}]
is connected and has Lie algebra
\mathfrak{l}_{\bullet}
, hence

[L_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}},L_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}}]=L_{\bullet}
.
As
L_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}}
is reductive, we have
L_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}}=\mathrm{Z}(L_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}})^{\circ}L_{\bullet}
where
\mathrm{Z}(L_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}})^{\circ}
is the connected centre of
L_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}}
. Now
H_{\bullet}
is a maximal torus of
L_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}}
, hence
\mathrm{Z}(L_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}})^{\circ}\subseteq H_{\bullet}
.
Consequently,
H_{\bullet}=\mathrm{Z}(L_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}})^{\circ}(H_{\bullet}\cap L_{\bullet})
. The Lie algebra of
H_{\bullet}\cap L_{\bullet}
is
\mathfrak{h}_{\bullet}\cap\mathfrak{l}_{\bullet}
, which is a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{l}_{\bullet}
. Thus,
(H_{\bullet}\cap L_{\bullet})^{\circ}
is a maximal torus of
L_{\bullet}
. But then
(H_{\bullet}\cap L_{\bullet})^{\circ}=H_{\bullet}\cap L_{\bullet}
, because a maximal torus in a connected reductive group is self-centralizing
(see
[
1
, Corollary 11.12]
).
Furthermore,

N_{L_{\bullet}}(H_{\bullet}\cap L_{\bullet})=N_{L_{\bullet}}(H_{\bullet})
.

As in Subsection
6.2
,
for a fundamental system
\Pi_{\mathfrak{b}_{\mathfrak{l}}}
of the
\mathfrak{h}
-root system
\Phi_{\mathfrak{l}}
of

corresponding to a Borel subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\mathfrak{b}_{\mathfrak{l}}\subseteq\mathfrak{l}
, the actions of odd reflections (associated to
\alpha
) and elements

of

on

are denoted by

and

, respectively. We denote the positive systems associated to

and

and

Lemma 7.9
.

Let
\alpha\in\Pi_{\mathfrak{b}_{\mathfrak{l}}}
be isotropic. Then

\Phi_{\mathfrak{g},\alpha}^{+}=\Phi_{\mathfrak{l},\alpha}^{+}\cup\Phi_{\mathfrak{u}}^{+}

Recall that

The first assertion is a consequence of this, the analogous description of

.
For the second assertion, note that

Proposition 7.10
.

Let
w\in N_{L_{\bullet}}(H_{\bullet}\cap L_{\bullet})
and

for

, so that

Then the following assertions hold.

(i)

The element

corresponds to the longest element of the Weyl group

N_{L_{\bullet}}(H_{\bullet}\cap L_{\bullet})/H_{\bullet}\cap L_{\bullet}

of the root system
\Phi_{\mathfrak{l}_{\bullet}}
.

(ii)

We have
w\in N_{G_{\bullet}}(H_{\bullet})
and

\Pi_{\tau(\mathfrak{b})}=w\cdot(r_{\mathfrak{g},\alpha_{k}}\cdot(\cdots(r_{\mathfrak{g},\alpha_{1}}\cdot\Pi_{\mathfrak{b}}))).

Proof.

(i)
Recall from (

) that
\mathrm{Ad}_{w}((\mathfrak{b}_{\mathfrak{l}})_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}})=\tau((\mathfrak{b}_{\mathfrak{l}})_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}})
. From Lemma

it follows that
\tau((\mathfrak{b}_{\mathfrak{l}})_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}})

.

(ii)

7.8

\tau(\mathfrak{b})=\tau(\mathfrak{b}_{\mathfrak{l}})\oplus\mathfrak{u}.

implies that

of (
30
) is obtained from
\Phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{+}
by the following procedure: we remove

and include

).

7.3.
Kostant’s cascade and a Lie algebraic formula for

In this subsection we continue with the notation of

7.2
. Thus,
\mathfrak{h}_{\bullet}=\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{a}
is a

-stable Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}_{\bullet}

\tau(\mathfrak{h})=\mathfrak{h}

\mathfrak{h}_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}}=\mathfrak{h}_{\bullet}\oplus\mathrm{Z}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}})

\tau

We recall the construction of Kostant’s cascade of strongly orthogonal roots in the case of the root system

.
Next set

\Phi^{(k),\perp}:=\{\alpha\in\Phi^{(k)}\,:\,(\alpha,\beta_{k})=0\}\quad\text{for }1\leq k\leq N_{1}.

Add the highest roots
\beta_{N_{1}+1},\ldots,\beta_{N_{2}}
of the irreducible components of the
\Phi^{(k),\perp}
to the sequence of the
\beta
’s in some order, and repeat this process until no more roots are left. The resulting sequence of roots
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{N}
is a maximal set of strongly orthogonal roots in
\Phi_{\mathfrak{l}}
, that is,
\beta_{i}\pm\beta_{j}\not\in\Phi_{\mathfrak{l}_{\bullet}}
for
1\leq i,j\leq N
.

For the following theorem, recall that
c_{\lambda}
is the real scalar defined in (
20
).

Theorem 7.11
.

Let
h_{\beta_{1}},\ldots,h_{\beta_{N}}\in\mathfrak{h}_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}}
denote the coroots of
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{N}
, so that
\beta_{i}(h_{\beta_{i}})=2
for
1\leq i\leq N
. Then for every
[W]\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{g}})
with
\mathfrak{b}
-highest weight
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}_{\overline{\mathbf{0}}}^{*}
we have

c_{\lambda}=(-1)^{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lambda(h_{\beta_{i}})}\lambda(\mathrm{HC}_{\mathfrak{b}}(D_{\lambda})).

Proof.

We have
\theta(\mathfrak{l}_{\bullet})=\mathfrak{l}_{\bullet}
, and the Cartan decomposition of

\mathfrak{l}_{\bullet}^{\mathbb{R}}:=\mathfrak{l}_{\bullet}\cap\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}

is

\mathfrak{l}_{\bullet}^{\mathbb{R}}=(\mathfrak{l}_{\bullet}^{\mathbb{R}}\cap\mathfrak{k}^{\mathbb{R}})\oplus(\mathfrak{l}_{\bullet}^{\mathbb{R}}\oplus\mathfrak{p}^{\mathbb{R}}).

By Lemma
7.4
,
\mathfrak{h}_{\bullet}^{\mathbb{R}}\cap\mathfrak{l}_{\bullet}^{\mathbb{R}}
is a
compact
\theta
-stable Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{l}_{\bullet}^{\mathbb{R}}
. Let
\alpha\in\Phi_{\mathfrak{l}_{\bullet}}
and set

\mathbf{e}:=e_{\alpha}\quad,\quad\mathbf{f}:=-\tau(e_{\alpha})\quad,\quad\mathbf{h}:=-[e_{\alpha},\tau(e_{\alpha})],

where the
e_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{l}_{\alpha}
are choices of root vectors.
By Lemma
7.2
we have
\tau(e_{\alpha})\in\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha}
, and since
\mathfrak{a}^{\mathbb{R}}\subseteq\ker(\alpha)
we have
\theta(\alpha)=\alpha
. Furthermore,

\mathbf{h}\in i(\mathfrak{h}_{\bullet}^{\mathbb{R}}\cap\mathfrak{l}_{\bullet}^{\mathbb{R}})

because
\tau(\mathbf{h})=-\mathbf{h}
.
Lemma
7.5
implies that
\alpha(\mathbf{h})\in\mathbb{R}
. Hence, by suitably scaling
e_{\alpha}
and substituting
\alpha
by
-\alpha
if necessary, we can assume that
\mathbf{e},\mathbf{f},\mathbf{h}
is a standard
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple. In matrix notation, the real form of the spanned
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
subalgebra that is induced by
\tau
corresponds to the antilinear involution
X\mapsto-X^{*}
, and we have

\mathbf{e}=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}\quad,\quad\mathbf{f}=\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
1&0\end{bmatrix}\quad,\quad\mathbf{h}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{bmatrix}.

The algebraic subgroup of
G_{\bullet}
corresponding to this
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}_{\bullet}
is isomorphic to either
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
or its quotient by
\{\pm I\}
,
and the antiholomorphic involution of
G_{\bullet}
restricts to the map
X\mapsto(X^{*})^{-1}
.
The simple reflection corresponding to
\alpha
corresponds to

(32)

w_{\alpha}=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{bmatrix}\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C}),

and we have
w_{\alpha}\tau(w_{\alpha})=w_{\alpha}^{2}=-I
, where
I\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
is the identity matrix. it follows that
\lambda(w_{\alpha}\tau(w_{\alpha}))=(-1)^{\lambda(\mathbf{h})}
.

It is a well known fact (originally due to Kostant) that the longest element of the Weyl group of a semisimple Lie algebra is equal to the product of reflections associated to the roots in the Kostant cascade. These reflections commute because of strong orthogonality. This fact together with the above
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
calculation implies the assertion of the corollary.
∎

7.4.
Revisiting the classification of modules of real reductive Lie algebras

Combining Theorems
6.10
and
7.11
, we obtain the classification of irreducible modules of real reductive Lie algebras, which we state below independently as it might be of independent interest.

Given a reductive Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, we have a decomposition

\mathfrak{g}=\mathrm{Z}(\mathfrak{g})\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
, where
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}=[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
.
For every
[W]\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{g}})
, the elements of
\mathrm{Z}(\mathfrak{g})
act by scalars (because of Schur’s Lemma), hence
W
is an irreducible
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
-module.

For a real form
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}
of
\mathfrak{g}
that is the fixed subalgebra of the antilinear involution
\tau
, we have
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}=(\mathrm{Z}(\mathfrak{g})\cap\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}})\oplus(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}\cap\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}})
.
Let
\theta
be a Cartan involution of the real semisimple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}\cap\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}
, corresponding to the decomposition

\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}\cap\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}=\mathfrak{k}^{\mathbb{R}}\oplus\mathfrak{p}^{\mathbb{R}}.

Let
\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{R}}=\mathfrak{t}^{\mathbb{R}}\oplus\mathfrak{a}^{\mathbb{R}}
be a
\theta
-stable Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}\cap\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}
, with complexification
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{a}
. Set
\mathfrak{l}:=\mathrm{Z}_{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{a})
.
As before,

\Phi_{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}}
and
\Phi_{\mathfrak{l}}
denote the root systems of
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
and
\mathfrak{l}
with respect to
\mathfrak{h}
.

Choose a Borel subalgebra
\mathfrak{b}_{\mathfrak{l}}
of
\mathfrak{l}
that contains
\mathfrak{h}
, and
an element
h_{\circ}\in\mathfrak{a}^{\mathbb{R}}
such that
\alpha(h_{\circ})\neq 0
for every
\alpha\in\Phi_{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}}\backslash\Phi_{\mathfrak{l}}
. Let
\mathfrak{b}
be the Borel subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
obtained from
\mathfrak{b}_{\mathfrak{l}}
and
h_{\circ}
as follows:
\mathfrak{b}_{\mathfrak{l}}\subseteq\mathfrak{b}
and for every
\alpha\in\Phi_{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}}\backslash\Phi_{\mathfrak{l}}
we have

\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}_{\alpha}\subseteq\mathfrak{b}
if and only if
\alpha(h_{\circ})>0
.
Let
w_{\circ}
denote the longest element of the Weyl group of
\mathfrak{h}\cap[\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{l}]
, considered as an element of the Weyl group of
\mathfrak{h}
in a canonical way (see Remark
7.8
). Finally, let
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{N}
be Kostant’s cascade of strongly orthogonal roots for the positive system of
\mathfrak{b}_{\mathfrak{l}}
, and let
h_{\beta_{i}}\in\mathfrak{h}
denote the coroot corresponding to
\beta_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq N
.

Theorem 7.12
.

Let
[W]\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{g}})
and let
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
be the
\mathfrak{b}
-highest weight of
W
as a
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
-module.

(i)

If
\mathrm{Z}(\mathfrak{g})\cap\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}
acts on
W
by real scalars,
\mathrm{Ad}^{*}_{w_{\circ}}(\lambda)=\overline{\lambda\circ\tau}
, and
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lambda(h_{\beta_{i}})
is even, then
\mathsf{ET}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}(W)=0_{\mathbb{R}}
, i.e., it is isomorphic to
\mathbb{R}
.

(ii)

If
\mathrm{Z}(\mathfrak{g})\cap\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}
acts on
W
by real scalars,
\mathrm{Ad}^{*}_{w_{\circ}}(\lambda)=\overline{\lambda\circ\tau}
, and
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lambda(h_{\beta_{i}})
is odd, then
\mathsf{ET}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}(W)=4_{\mathbb{R}}
, i.e., it is isomorphic to
\mathbb{H}
.

(iii)

Otherwise,
\mathsf{ET}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}(W)=0_{\mathbb{C}}
, i.e., it is isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

From Theorem
4.3
(ii) it follows that the exterior tensor product of two irreducible representations has endotype
0_{\mathbb{C}}
if and only if at least one of them has endotype
0_{\mathbb{C}}
. Combining this observation with Theorem
6.10
, Remark
4.4
, Proposition
7.10
, and Theorem
7.11
, we obtain the assertions of the theorem. Note that in the notation of (
20
) we have
r=0
, so that
\lambda_{\mathcal{B}}=\mathrm{Ad}_{w_{\circ}}(\lambda)
and
D_{\lambda}=1
.
∎

8.
Computing
\mathsf{ET}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}(W)
when
\mathfrak{g}
is of Cartan type or of type
\mathbf{P}(n)

In this section
\mathfrak{g}
will be either
of type
\mathbf{P}(n)
or
a Cartan type Lie superalgebra.
Similar to Remark
6.3
, we exclude
\mathfrak{pe}(1)
and
\mathfrak{spe}(1)
which are of Clifford type.
Note that up to isomorphism, each of the Lie superalgebras
\mathbf{W}(n)
,
\mathbf{S}(n)
, and
\tilde{\mathbf{S}}(n)
has a unique real form. (We remind the reader that
\tilde{\mathbf{S}}(n)
exists only for
n
even).

Our strategy is to use Corollary
4.11
for a
\tau
-stable triangular decomposition of
\mathfrak{g}
that is either of hyperbolic type, or of graded type (see Definitions
4.6
and
4.9
).
First we consider the case where
\mathfrak{g}
is not isomorphic to

\mathbf{S}(n)
or

\tilde{\mathbf{S}}(n)
.
Then
\mathfrak{g}
has a
\mathbb{Z}
-grading

\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{k\geq-1}\mathfrak{g}_{k}

that is consistent with the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading of
\mathfrak{g}
. Furthermore, for every real form of
\mathfrak{g}
, we can assume that the corresponding
\tau:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
preserves the
\mathbb{Z}
-grading.
We have

\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}=\overline{\mathfrak{u}}^{\mathbb{R}}\oplus\mathfrak{l}^{\mathbb{R}}\oplus{\mathfrak{u}}^{\mathbb{R}},

where

\mathfrak{u}^{\mathbb{R}}:=\bigoplus_{k\geq 1}(\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}})_{k}\quad,\quad\mathfrak{l}^{\mathbb{R}}:=(\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}})_{0}\quad,\quad\overline{\mathfrak{u}}^{\mathbb{R}}:=(\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}})_{-1}.

Passing to complexifications, we obtain a direct sum decomposition

\mathfrak{g}=\overline{\mathfrak{u}}\oplus\mathfrak{l}\oplus\mathfrak{u}
. This is Kac’s triangular decomposition, and it is of graded type.

Remark 8.1
.

When
\mathfrak{g}\neq\tilde{\mathbf{S}}(n)
, Kac gave a classification of irreducible
\mathfrak{g}
-modules by proving that the functor
\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{g}}\to\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{l}}
defined by
W\mapsto W^{\mathfrak{u}}
induces a bijection
\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{g}})\to\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{l}})
; see
[
7
, Prop 5.2.5]
. Note that
\mathfrak{l}
is a reductive Lie algebra. Together with Corollary
4.11
, this provides a complete answer to the computation of endotypes for irreducible
\mathfrak{g}
-modules.
For
\mathfrak{g}=\tilde{\mathbf{S}}(n)
, we do not have a bijection
\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{g}})\to\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{l}})
, but we still have an injection for a hyperbolic triangular decomposition that will be described below; see Proposition
4.10
(ii).

Next, suppose that
\mathfrak{g}
is a Lie superalgebra of type

\mathbf{S}(n)
or

\tilde{\mathbf{S}}(n)
. The Lie superalgebra
\tilde{\mathbf{S}}(n)
is not
\mathbb{Z}
-graded, but it has a subalgebra isomorphic to

\underline{\mathfrak{g}}:=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}\mathbf{S}(n)_{k}.

Furthermore, in both cases
\mathfrak{g}
has a unique real form, the corresponding
\tau
preserves the graded components of
\underline{\mathfrak{g}}
, and

(\underline{\mathfrak{g}}^{\mathbb{R}})_{0}\cong\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{R})
. Let
\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{R}}
be a Cartan subalgebra of
(\underline{\mathfrak{g}}^{\mathbb{R}})_{0}
and let
\mathfrak{h}
be the complexification of
\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{R}}
. Then we have an
\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{R}}
-root decomposition of
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}
with roots

\varepsilon_{i_{1}}+\cdots+\varepsilon_{i_{k}}\quad\text{and}\quad\varepsilon_{i_{1}}+\cdots+\varepsilon_{i_{k}}-\varepsilon_{j},

where
\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq n
with
0\leq k\leq n-1
, and
j\not\in\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}
.
Here as usual the
\varepsilon_{i}
are standard characters of the diagonal Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{R})
, so that

\varepsilon_{1}+\cdots+\varepsilon_{n}=0.

Following the notation of Section
6.2
, let
\Phi_{\mathfrak{g}}
denote the set of
\mathfrak{h}
-roots of
\mathfrak{g}
.
We choose
x_{\circ}\in\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{R}}
such that
\alpha(x_{\circ})\neq 0
for every
\alpha\in\Phi_{\mathfrak{g}}
, and we consider the
corresponding
\tau
-stable triangular decomposition of hyperbolic type, as in Subsection
4.2
. Then we have

\overline{\mathfrak{u}}=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\Phi_{\mathfrak{g}}\\
\alpha(x_{\circ})<0\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\quad,\quad{\mathfrak{l}}=\mathfrak{h}\quad,\quad{\mathfrak{u}}=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\Phi_{\mathfrak{g}}\\
\alpha(x_{\circ})>0\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}.

Recall that in all cases
\mathfrak{l}
is a reductive Lie algebra.
We fix a Borel subalgebra
\mathfrak{b}_{\mathfrak{l}}\subseteq\mathfrak{l}
.
In Theorem
8.2
below, by the highest weight of an irreducible
\mathfrak{g}
-module
W
we mean the
\mathfrak{b}_{\mathfrak{l}}
-highest weight of
W^{\mathfrak{u}}
.

Theorem 8.2
.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{g}
is of one of the types
\mathbf{P}(n)
,
\mathbf{W}(n)
,
\mathbf{H}(n)
,
\mathbf{S}(n)
, or
\tilde{\mathbf{S}}(n)
. Let
[W]\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{g}})
. Then the following statements hold.

(i)

If
\mathfrak{g}=\mathbf{S}(n)
or
\tilde{\mathbf{S}}(n)
, then for the unique real form of
\mathfrak{g}
we always have
\mathsf{ET}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}(W)=0_{\mathbb{R}}
.

(ii)

If
\mathfrak{g}=\mathbf{W}(n)
, then for the unique real form of
\mathfrak{g}
we have

\mathsf{ET}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}(W)=\begin{cases}0_{\mathbb{R}}&\text{if }\lambda\big|_{\mathrm{Z}\left((\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}})_{0}\right)}\subseteq\mathbb{R},\\
0_{\mathbb{C}}&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

where
\lambda
is the highest weight of
W
with respect to some choice of a Borel
\mathfrak{b}_{\mathfrak{l}}\subseteq\mathfrak{l}
.

(iii)

If
\mathfrak{g}
is of type
\mathbf{P}(n)
or
\mathbf{H}(n)
, then
\mathsf{ET}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}(W)
is either
0_{\mathbb{C}}
,
0_{\mathbb{R}}
, or
4_{\mathbb{R}}
, and determined from the highest weight
\lambda
of
W
according to Corollary
4.11
and Theorem
6.10
.

Proof.

All of the assertions are immediate consequences of Corollary
4.11
and Theorem
6.10
. For (i), note that
\mathfrak{l}^{\mathbb{R}}
is the Cartan subalgebra of a Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}
that is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{R})
and
\lambda
is the highest weight of an irreducible
\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{C})
-module. Thus,
\lambda
is integral, hence real valued on
\mathfrak{l}^{\mathbb{R}}
. For (ii), note that
(\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}})_{0}\cong\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{R})=\mathbb{R}\oplus\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{R})
and the endotype of every
irreducible
\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{R})
module is
0_{\mathbb{R}}
. Finally, for (iii) recall that irreducible modules of a reductive Lie algebra can only have endotypes
0_{\mathbb{C}}
,
0_{\mathbb{R}}
, and
4_{\mathbb{R}}
.
∎

Examples of explicit computations of endotypes

This section is devoted to concrete examples that demonstrate how our results

Example 9.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(\mathbb{C})

\tau(X):=-X^{*}
, so that
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{R}}=\mathfrak{su}(n)

\theta

\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}
denote the standard upper triangular Borel subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}

\tau(\mathfrak{b})=\mathfrak{b}^{\mathrm{op}}
, so that in Theorem
6.10
we have
r=0
and
w
is the longest element of the Weyl group (represented by an antidiagonal matrix). Furthermore,
\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{R}}
is a compact Cartan subalgebra, and the Kostant cascade is
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}
where
\beta_{i}=\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{n+1-i}
for
1\leq i\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor

\mathfrak{b}
by

h_{i}:=h_{\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{i+1}}
.
The
\mathfrak{b}
-highest weight
\lambda
of an irreducible
\mathfrak{g}
-module
W
corresponds to a sequence of non-negative integers
(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n-1})
where
\lambda_{i}:=\langle\lambda,h_{i}\rangle

\bar{\lambda}_{i}=\lambda_{i}
for all
i
, the condition
\lambda_{\mathcal{B}}=\overline{\lambda\circ\tau}
is equivalent to

(33)

\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{n+1-i}\quad\text{for every }1\leq i\leq n.

Let
E_{i,j}
denote the
n\times n
matrices with a 1 in the
(i,j)
position and 0’s elsewhere. Then

h_{i}=E_{i,i}-E_{i+1,i+1}
and
h_{\beta_{i}}=E_{i,i}-E_{n+1-i,n+1-i}
. It follows that

\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}h_{\beta_{i}}=\begin{cases}\sum_{i=1}^{m}ih_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}(m-i)h_{m+i}&\text{ if }n=2m,\\[11.38109pt]

By a direct calculation, if tollows that if (
33
) holds, then

\textstyle\lambda\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}h_{\beta_{i}}\right)=\begin{cases}m\lambda_{m}+\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}2i\lambda_{i}&\text{ if }n=2m,\\
\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}2i\lambda_{i}&\text{ if }n=2m+1.\end{cases}

From Theorem
7.11
and Theorem
6.10
it follows that:

(i)

If
\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{n+1-i}
for at least one
1\leq i\leq n
, then
\mathsf{ET}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}(W)=0_{\mathbb{C}}
.

(ii)

If
\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{n+1-i}
for every
1\leq i\leq n
and
n=2m+1
, then
\mathsf{ET}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}(W)=0_{\mathbb{R}}
.

(iii)

If
\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{n+1-i}=0
for every
1\leq i\leq n
and
n=2m
, then

\mathsf{ET}_{\mathfrak{g},\tau}(W)=\begin{cases}0_{\mathbb{R}}&\text{ if }(-1)^{m\lambda_{m}}=1,\\
4_{\mathbb{R}}&\text{ if }(-1)^{m\lambda_{m}}=-1.\end{cases},

Example 9.2
.

let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)
and let
\mathfrak{b}
denote the standard upper triangular Borel subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}

\mathfrak{g}
corresponds to
\tau(X)=\bar{X}

\tau(\mathfrak{b})=\mathfrak{b}

for every

Next we consider the compact form
\mathfrak{u}(1,0|1,0)

e_{1},e_{2}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha_{1}}
,
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha_{1}}
,
[e_{i},f_{j}]=0
for
i\neq j
, and
h_{i}=[e_{i},f_{i}]
. It is straightforward to verify that the choices of
e_{i},f_{i},h_{i}
are unique up to scaling.

Let
W
be an irreducible
\mathfrak{g}
-module with
\mathfrak{b}^{1}
-highest weight
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
, corresponding to the highest weight vector
w_{\lambda}
. In what follows, we set
\alpha_{1}:=\varepsilon_{1}-\delta_{1}
.

Lemma B.1
.

With
W
and
w_{\lambda}
as above, the have the following assertions.

(i)

Let
e_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
where
\alpha\in\Phi^{2,+}\backslash\{-\alpha_{1}\}
.
Then
e_{\alpha}w_{\lambda}=0
.

(ii)

Let
e_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
, where
\alpha\in\Phi^{2,+}\backslash\{-\alpha_{1}\}
. Then

e_{\alpha}f_{1}w_{\lambda}=e_{\alpha}f_{2}w_{\lambda}=0
.

(iii)

Let

e_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
, where
\alpha\in\Phi^{2,+}\backslash\{-\alpha_{1}\}
. Then

e_{\alpha}f_{1}f_{2}w_{\lambda}=0
.

Proof.

(i) follows from
\Phi^{2,+}\backslash\{-\alpha_{1}\}=\Phi^{1,+}\backslash\{\alpha_{1}\}
.
For (ii), either
\alpha-\alpha_{1}
is not a root or
\alpha-\alpha_{1}\in\Phi^{1,+}
. For (iii), either
\alpha-2\alpha_{1}
is not a root or
\alpha-2\alpha_{1}\in\Phi^{1,+}
.
∎

Let
\omega_{\lambda,\alpha_{1}}:\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha_{1}}\times\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha_{1}}\to\mathbb{C}
denote the bilinear form defined by
\omega_{\lambda,\alpha_{1}}(x,y):=\lambda([x,y])
. Then
\mathrm{rank}(\omega_{\lambda,\alpha_{1}})=\#\{i:\lambda(h_{i})\neq 0\}
.

Proposition B.2
.

The
\mathfrak{b}^{2}
-highest weight of
W
is obtained as follows.

(i)

If
\mathrm{rank}(\omega_{\lambda,\alpha_{1}})=0
, then the
\mathfrak{b}^{2}
-highest weight of
W
is
\lambda
and the corresponding highest weight vector is
w_{\lambda}
.

(ii)

If
\mathrm{rank}(\omega_{\lambda,\alpha_{1}})=1
, then
the
\mathfrak{b}^{2}
-highest weight of
W
is
\lambda-\alpha_{1}
and the corresponding highest weight vector is
e_{-\alpha_{1}}w_{\lambda}
for any
e_{-\alpha_{1}}\in\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha_{1}}
such that
\lambda([e_{-\alpha_{1}},\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha_{1}}])\neq\{0\}
.

(iii)

If
\mathrm{rank}(\omega_{\lambda,\alpha_{1}})=2
, then
the
\mathfrak{b}^{2}
-highest weight of
W
is
\lambda-2\alpha_{1}
and the corresponding highest weight vector is
f_{1}f_{2}w_{\lambda}
.

Proof.

(i) By Lemma
B.1
(i) it suffices to show that
f_{1}w_{\lambda}=f_{2}w_{\lambda}=0
. We have

e_{i}f_{j}w_{\lambda}=-f_{j}e_{i}w_{\lambda}+\lambda([e_{i},f_{j}])w_{\lambda}=0
for
1\leq i,j\leq 2
. From this and
Lemma
B.1
(ii) it follows
that if
f_{j}w_{\lambda}\neq 0
for some
j\in\{1,2\}
, then

f_{j}w_{\lambda}
is a
\mathfrak{b}^{1}
-highest weight of
W
. But this contradicts the uniqueness (up to a scalar) of
w_{\lambda}
.

(ii) Without loss of generality we can assume that
\lambda(h_{1})=0
and
\lambda(h_{2})=1
. By the argument of (i), we must have
f_{1}w_{\lambda}=0
. Since

e_{2}f_{2}w_{\lambda}=-f_{2}e_{2}w_{\lambda}+\lambda(h_{2})w_{\lambda}=w_{\lambda},

we must have
f_{2}w_{\lambda}\neq 0
. By Lemma
B.1
(ii) and the relations
f_{1}f_{2}w_{\lambda}=-f_{2}f_{1}w_{\lambda}=0
and

f_{2}(f_{2}w_{\lambda})=(f_{2})^{2}w_{\lambda}=0
it follows that
f_{2}w_{\lambda}
(or
(f_{2}+cf_{1})w_{\lambda}
for any
c\in\mathbb{C}
) is a
\mathfrak{b}^{2}
-highest weight vector of
W
.

(iii) Moving the
e_{i}
past the
f_{j}
, we obtain
e_{2}e_{1}f_{1}f_{2}w_{\lambda}=\lambda(h_{1})\lambda(h_{2})w_{\lambda}\neq 0
, hence in particular
f_{1}f_{2}w_{\lambda}\neq 0
.
Lemma
B.1
(iii) together with the relations

f_{1}(f_{1}f_{2}w_{\lambda})=(f_{1}^{2})f_{2}w_{\lambda}=0

and

f_{2}(f_{1}f_{2}w_{\lambda})=-f_{1}(f_{2}^{2})w_{\lambda}=0,

imply that
f_{1}f_{2}w_{\lambda}
is a
\mathfrak{b}^{2}
-highest weight vector of
W
.
∎

The analysis of the case of
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{psl}(2|2)

is the same.
Irreducible modules of
\mathfrak{psl}(2|2)
are the same as irreducible
\mathfrak{sl}(2|2)
-modules satisfying
\lambda(I)=0
where
I
spans the center of
\mathfrak{sl}(2|2)
. The latter constraint and uniqueness up to scaling of
h_{1},h_{2}
imply that
\lambda(h_{1})=c\lambda(h_{2})
for some
c\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
. In particular,
the assumption of Proposition
B.2
(ii) cannot happen. Consequently, the
\mathfrak{b}_{\alpha}
-highest weight of
W
is determined by parts (i) and (iii) of Proposition
B.2
.
