 7 Abstract
 8 We present a new method for solving diffusion equation across slide surfaces.
 9 In general, it can be applied in the case of non-matching meshes with geometric
10 interface inconsistencies. This method allows to use existing packages for solving
11 algebraic systems with SPD matrices. Using numerical examples, we demonstrate
12 the performance of the new method.

13 1 Introduction
14 The motivation for this paper is the need to solve partial differential equations in multi-
15 physics simulations, for example, the heat transfer across slide surfaces (contacts) in multi-
16 material Lagrangian hydrocodes [3, 2, 1]. Deformations of materials in the Lagrangian
17 hydrodynamic simulations may lead to scenarios where one material starts to slide along the
18 other. Algorithms for treating slide surfaces take into account that two or more deformable
19 or rigid bodies can come into contact but cannot interpenetrate. They can, however, slide
20 tangentially along the contact surface. Slide surface is not known in advance and is a part
21 of the solution. The algorithms for treating slide surfaces require to break an originally
22 conformal mesh into several submeshes along the sliding interface. These submeshes may
23 have gaps and overlaps between them and are usually classified as non-matching meshes
24 with geometric inconsistencies. The important feature of this type of problems is that the
25 exact interface (the slide surface) is unknown, and we do not know in advance where the

27 where some of mesh faces are known to represent the contact.
28 In general, in each region we may have to solve different physics problems, which
29 are coupled by some physics-motivated continuity conditions. For second-order linear or
30 nonlinear PDEs, these conditions typically enforce continuity of potential and its flux, e.g.
31 the temperature and the heat flux in the heat conduction problems.
32 In [5] we have developed a new method for solving a heat conduction problem across
33 slide surfaces. It has several unique features such as the extended stencil for the flux
34 approximation near a slide surface. Changing the flux stencil is an intrusive technology
35 which prohibits the use of standard packages for solving subdomain physics, e.g. packages
36 for solving a diffusion equation in a subdomain. From a practical point of view, this makes
37 it difficult to use this new method in the established production-quality hydrodynamic
38 codes.
39 The goal of this paper is to present and numerically analyze another method which
40 allows the use of existing packages to solve partial differential equations in each material
41 region. It adds only one additional block responsible for the enforcement of weak continuity
42 of relevant physical quantities to the global algebraic system.
43 The general review of algorithms for solving partial differential equations on non-
44 matching meshes with geometric interface inconsistencies is given in [5], so we will not
45 repeat it here.
46 We will demonstrate the main idea of the proposed method on the diffusion equation
47 and show the method’s feasibility by comparing it with the method developed in [5].
48 The main ideas are as follows. In each domain we use the standard mimetic finite
49 difference (MFD) discretization with the Dirichlet boundary conditions on interfaces. The
50 MFD method gives equations for all the internal face temperatures (and optionally internal
51 cell temperatures) in each subdomain. To obtain equations for interface face temperatures
52 coming from the neighboring subdomains, we enforce weak continuity of temperature and
53 heat flux by introducing a quadratic functional. This functional involves projections of
54 interface unknowns (temperatures and fluxes) between two subdomains attached to the
55 common interface. The algebraic equations are obtained by differentiating this functional
56 with respect to the interface temperature unknowns.
 For example, for two material regions separated by one slide surface the global system
 of equations has the following block structure. The first block, A11 , corresponds to the
 diffusion problem in the first region. Similarly, the second block, A22 , corresponds to the
 diffusion problem in the second region. The third block, A33 , corresponds to the interface
 temperatures. The off-diagonal blocks corresponds to the communication between the
 regions and the interface. The structure of global matrix is then:

147 The blocks A11 and A22 correspond to the MFD discretization of the elliptic equations with
148 the Dirichlet boundary conditions (external and internal boundaries) in the corresponding
149 regions. Recall that all the interface unknowns are included in the vector U3 .

154 The off-diagonal blocks A31 and A32 are sparse. They only act on unknowns belonging
155 to the layers of cells adjacent to the interfaces.

157 In this approach, we are only allowed to use the existing packages for solving subdomain
158 problems, which leads to the iterative solution strategy. Let us assume that we have some
159 initial guesses for subdomains, Us1 and Us2 , and for the interface, Us3 . At the first stage,
160 we independently solve equations for the first and the second subdomains, using interface
161 unknowns as the Dirichlet boundary conditions:

                                1   = F1 − A13 Us3 ,                     A22 Us+1
                                                                              2   = F2 − A23 Us3 .
162 Note that this step uses the standard method for imposing boundary conditions. This
163 gives us the updated values of internal unknowns for each subdomain. Next, we update
164 the interface unknowns:
                                         A33 Us+1            s+1
                                              33 = F3 − A31 U1   − A32 Us+1
 2 .

165 Here, Fi includes the source data from the original equation and the contributions from
166 the Dirichlet boundary condition on the external boundary. This can be interpreted as a
167 block Gauss-Seidel iterative method.

168 4.2 Global solution strategy
169 In this approach, we are allowed to use other iterative methods and can now solve the global
170 system in any way we want. Since the global system is not SPD, we need a GMRES-type
171 method with a proper preconditioner.
172 The main theoretical question is how to prove that the global system has a solution.
173 Currently, for all the numerical examples that we have tried, the global system has a
174 solution and the block Gauss-Seidel method converges. Numerous examples of numerical
175 experiments are presented in the next section.

255 6 Conclusion and Future Work
256 We have developed a new method for solving heat flux conduction across slide surfaces.
257 More generally, this method can be used for solving elliptic equations on non-matching
258 meshes with geometric interface inconsistencies.
259 The method allows us to use the existing packages for solving subdomain problems.
260 The interface problem has an SPD matrix and the existing packages can also be use to
261 invert it.

 15

 Figure 4: The non-matching meshes with geometric inconsistencies (left panel) for the two
 shells, and the computed solution (right panel).

262 Using multiple numerical examples, we demonstrated that the proposed method is
263 accurate and comparable to the method [5].
264 The future work is to prove that the global system of linear equations always has a
265 solution. So far, we only have the numerical evidence to support that, and it was true in
266 all the examples we had tried. Also, for small meshes, we analyzed the problem’s solvability
267 by computing eigenvalues of the global matrix. In all the cases, that system had no zero
268 eigenvalues, implying the uniqueness of the solution.
