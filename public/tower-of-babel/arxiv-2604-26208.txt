A note on the moduli of stable sheaves on elliptic ruled surfaces.
Kōta Yoshioka

Abstract




0.
Introduction

Let
\pi:X\to C
be a
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
-bundle over a smooth projective curve
C

of genus
g
. Let
C_{0}
be a minimal section of
\pi

with
(C_{0}^{2})=-e
and
f
a fiber of
\pi
.
Then
K_{X}\equiv-2C_{0}-(2-2g+e)f
.
If
((K_{X}+f)\cdot H)<0
. then
\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(E,E(-f))=0
for all
\mu
-semi-stable
sheaves. Thus
E
is a prioritary sheaf in the sense of Walter.
Then Walter
[
7
]

proved the irreducibility by using deformation theory
of prioritary sheaves.

In this note, we shall slightly generalize the irreducibility of the moduli spaces.
Thus we shall prove the irreducibility of the moduli spaces under the assumption

(K_{X}\cdot H)<0
.
We note that
(K_{X}\cdot H)<0
for any ample divisor
H
, if
g=0,1
.
Indeed
-K_{X}
is numerically equivalent to an effective divisor.
If
g=0
, then
-K_{X}-f
is also effective, and hence the irreducibility is a consequence of
[
7
]
.
On the other hand if
g=1
,
then
-K_{X}-f
is numerically equivalent to an effective divisor
if and only if
e\geq 1
. So we are interested in the case where
e=0,-1
.
We also study the existence condition of the
\mu
-semistable sheaves
in these cases.

For a coherent sheaf on
X
,
\operatorname{rk}E
denotes the rank of
E
and we set

\tau(E):=(\operatorname{rk}E,c_{1}(E),\chi(E))
.
Let
{\mathcal{M}}_{H}({\bf e})^{\mu ss}
be the moduli stack of
\mu
-semi-stable sheaves
E
with

\tau(E)={\bf e}
.
Then we have the following result.

Theorem 0.1

(Proposition
2.7
,
2.9
)
.

Let
X
be an elliptic ruled surface and
H(x):=C_{0}+xf
be an ample divisor on
X
.

Then for

{\bf e}=(r,\xi,\chi)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}\times\operatorname{NS}(X)\times{\mathbb{Z}}
,
with
0\leq(\xi\cdot f)<r
, we have the following.

(1)

{\mathcal{M}}_{H(x)}(r,\xi,\chi)^{\mu ss}
is irreducible.

(2)

{\mathcal{M}}_{H(x)}(r,\xi,\chi)^{\mu ss}\neq\emptyset
if and only if

r^{2}\Delta({\bf e})\geq r_{1}(r-r_{1})(x-\frac{e}{2})
, where
r_{1}=(\xi\cdot f)
.
For the definition of
\Delta({\bf e})
, see (
1.2
).

(3)

Assume that
r>1
.
Let
E\in{\mathcal{M}}_{H(x)}(r,\xi,\chi)^{\mu ss}
be a general member. Then

E
is a
\mu
-stable locally free sheaf such that

E_{|\pi^{-1}(t)}
is a rigid vector bundle for a general
t\in C
.

Let
X
be an elliptic ruled surface and
assume that
-K_{X}
is nef, that is,
e=0,-1
.
Then
(K_{X}^{2})=0
and there is an adjacent chamber for
{\bf e}=(r,\xi,\chi)
.
We denote the moduli stack by
{\mathcal{M}}_{-K_{X}}({\bf e})^{\mu ss}
.

Corollary 0.2
.

Let
X
be an elliptic ruled surface and
assume that
-K_{X}
is nef, that is,
e=0,-1
. Then

{\mathcal{M}}_{-K_{X}}(r,\xi,\chi)^{\mu ss}\neq\emptyset
if and only if
\Delta({\bf e})>0

or
r\mid(\xi\cdot f)
and
\Delta({\bf e})=0
.

1.
Basic results on moduli spaces

1.1.
Notation.

In this note, instead of using equivariant cohomology of suitable quot-schemes as in
[
9
]
,
we use moduli stacks of coherent sheaves.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}({\bf e})
be the stack of coherent sheaves
E
with
\tau(E)={\bf e}
.
Let
{\mathcal{M}}_{H}({\bf e})^{\mu ss}
be the open substack of
{\mathcal{M}}({\bf e})
consisting of

\mu
-semi-stable sheaves
and
{\mathcal{M}}_{H}({\bf e})^{\mu s}
the open substack consisting of

\mu
-stable sheaves.
Let
{\mathcal{M}}_{H}({\bf e})^{ss}
(resp.
{\mathcal{M}}_{H}({\bf e})^{s}
)
be the substack of
{\mathcal{M}}_{H}({\bf e})^{\mu ss}
consisting of
semi-stable sheaves (resp. stable sheaves).
Let
{\mathcal{M}}_{H}({\bf e})^{\mu s}_{0}
be the open substack of

{\mathcal{M}}_{H}({\bf e})^{\mu s}
consisting of
\mu
-stable locally free sheaves.

Let
\overline{M}_{H}({\bf e})
be the moduli space of
S
-equivalence classes of semi-stable sheaves

E
with
\tau(E)={\bf e}
and
M_{H}({\bf e})
the open subscheme consisting of stable sheaves.
Let
M_{H}({\bf e})_{0}^{\mu s}
be the moduli space of
\mu
-stable locally free sheaves.

For a coherent sheaf
E
with
\operatorname{rk}E=r\neq 0
,
we set

(1.1)

\begin{split}\mu(E):=&\frac{c_{1}(E)}{r},\\
\Delta(E):=&\frac{2rc_{2}(E)-(r-1)(c_{1}(E)^{2})}{2r^{2}}\\
=&\frac{-2r\chi(E)+2r^{2}\chi({\mathcal{O}}_{X})-r(c_{1}(E)\cdot K_{X})+(c_{1}(E)^{2})}{2r^{2}}.\end{split}

For
{\bf e}=(r,\xi,\chi)
, we also set

(1.2)

\mu({\bf e}):=\frac{\xi}{r},\;\Delta({\bf e}):=\frac{-2r\chi+2r^{2}\chi({\mathcal{O}}_{X})-r(\xi\cdot K_{X})+(\xi^{2})}{2r^{2}}.

For coherent sheaves
E,F
with
\operatorname{rk}E,\operatorname{rk}F>0
,
Riemann-Roch theorem implies

(1.3)

\chi(E,F)=\operatorname{rk}E\operatorname{rk}F\left(\frac{((\mu(F)-\mu(E))^{2})-((\mu(F)-\mu(E))\cdot K_{X})}{2}+\chi({\mathcal{O}}_{X})-\Delta(E)-\Delta(F)\right).

1.2.
Stack of filtrations

For
{\bf e}_{i}:=(r_{i},\xi_{i},\chi_{i})

(1\leq i\leq s)
,
let
{\mathcal{F}}_{H}({\bf e}_{1},{\bf e}_{2},...,{\bf e}_{s})
be the stack of filtrations

(1.4)

0\subset F_{1}\subset F_{2}\subset\cdots\subset F_{s}=E

such that
E_{i}:=F_{i}/F_{i-1}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{H}({\bf e}_{i})^{\mu ss}

(1\leq i\leq s)

and

\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(E_{j},E_{i})=0
(
i<j
).

Proposition 1.1
.

\dim{\mathcal{F}}_{H}({\bf e}_{1},{\bf e}_{2},...,{\bf e}_{s})=2r^{2}\Delta({\bf e})-r^{2}\chi({\mathcal{O}}_{X})+\sum_{i<j}\chi({\bf e}_{i},{\bf e}_{j})
.

Proof.

By the proof of
[
2
, Lem. 5.2]
and Proposition
1.5
, we see that

(1.5)

\begin{split}\dim{\mathcal{F}}_{H}({\bf e}_{1},{\bf e}_{2},...,{\bf e}_{s})=&\sum_{i}\dim{\mathcal{M}}_{H}({\bf e}_{i})^{\mu ss}-\sum_{i>j}\chi({\bf e}_{i},{\bf e}_{j})\\
=&-\chi({\bf e},{\bf e})+\sum_{i<j}\chi({\bf e}_{i},{\bf e}_{j}).\end{split}

∎

Remark 1.2
.

(i)

Assume that

\frac{(\xi_{1}\cdot H)}{r_{1}}>\frac{(\xi_{2}\cdot H)}{r_{2}}>\cdots>\frac{(\xi_{s}\cdot H)}{r_{s}}.

Then (
1.4
) is the Harder-Narasimhan filtration of
E
.

In this case,
{\mathcal{F}}_{H}({\bf e}_{1},{\bf e}_{2},...,{\bf e}_{s})\to{\mathcal{M}}({\bf e})

is an immersion.

(ii)

Assume that

\frac{(\xi_{1}\cdot H)}{r_{1}}=\frac{(\xi_{2}\cdot H)}{r_{2}}=\cdots=\frac{(\xi_{s}\cdot H)}{r_{s}}.

Then (
1.4
) is a Jordan-Hölder filtration of
E
.
In this case, the image of
{\mathcal{F}}_{H}({\bf e}_{1},{\bf e}_{2},...,{\bf e}_{s})\to{\mathcal{M}}({\bf e})

parameterizes properly
\mu
-semi-stable sheaves having Jordan-Hölder filtrations
(
1.4
).

1.3.
Walls and chambers

Let
\operatorname{Amp}(X)
be the ample cone of
X
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}({\bf e})

be the set of subsheaves
F\subset E

which satisfy

(1)

\tau(E)={\bf e}
,

(2)

0<\operatorname{rk}F<\operatorname{rk}E
,

(3)

\Delta(F),\Delta(E/F)\geq 0
and

(4)

\mu(E)-\mu(F)\neq 0
and
there is an element
H\in\operatorname{Amp}(X)
with
((\mu(E)-\mu(F))\cdot H)=0
.

Definition 1.3
.

For an element
0\subset F\subset E
of
{\mathcal{F}}({\bf e})
, we define a wall
W^{F}
by

W^{F}:=\{H\in\operatorname{Amp}(X)|((\mu(E)-\mu(F))\cdot H)=0\}.

\cup_{F}W^{F}
is locally finite.
We shall call chamber a connected component of
\operatorname{Amp}(X)\setminus\cup_{F}W^{F}
.

Lemma 1.4
.

Assume that
(K_{X}\cdot H)<0
.
Let
E
and
F
be
\mu
-semi-stable sheaves of positive rank
satisfying

\frac{(c_{1}(E)\cdot H)}{\operatorname{rk}E}=\frac{(c_{1}(F)\cdot H)}{\operatorname{rk}F}
.
Then
\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(E,F)=0
.

Proof.

Since
(-K_{X}\cdot H)>0
, we have

\frac{(c_{1}(E(K_{X}))\cdot H)}{\operatorname{rk}E}<\frac{(c_{1}(F)\cdot H)}{\operatorname{rk}F}
.
Hence

\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(E,F)=\operatorname{Hom}(F,E(K_{X}))^{\vee}=0.

∎

Proposition 1.5
.

(1)

Let
{\mathcal{M}}
be an irreducible component of
{\mathcal{M}}_{H}({\bf e})^{\mu ss}
.
Then

\dim{\mathcal{M}}\geq 2r^{2}\Delta({\bf e})-r^{2}\chi({\mathcal{O}}_{X}).

(2)

Assume that
(K_{X}\cdot H)<0
. Then
{\mathcal{M}}_{H}({\bf e})^{\mu ss}
is smooth of

\dim{\mathcal{M}}_{H}({\bf e})^{\mu ss}=2r^{2}\Delta({\bf e})-r^{2}\chi({\mathcal{O}}_{X}).

Proof.

By the deformation theory of coherent sheaves,

\dim{\mathcal{M}}\geq\dim\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(E,E)-\dim\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(E,E)-\dim\operatorname{Hom}(E,E)=-\chi(E,E)

for
E\in{\mathcal{M}}
.
Hence we get the first claim.
By
(K_{X}\cdot H)<0
,
\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(E,E)=0
for all
E\in{\mathcal{M}}_{H}({\bf e})^{\mu ss}
.
Hence
{\mathcal{M}}_{H}({\bf e})^{\mu ss}
is smooth.
∎

Theorem 1.6

(Beauville
[
1
]
, Markman
[
3
]
)
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective surface and
H
an ample divisor on
X

such that
(K_{X}\cdot H)<0
.
If
\gcd(r,\xi,\chi)=1
, then

M_{H}(r,\xi,\chi)
is a smooth, projective and irreducible.

Proof.

By
(K_{X}\cdot H)<0
,

\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(E,F)=\operatorname{Hom}(F,E(K_{X}))^{\vee}=0
for all

E,F\in M_{H}(r,\xi,\chi)
.
Hence
[
3
, Thm. 8]
holds.
Then the proof of
[
3
, Cor. 10]
implies the claim.
∎

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective surface and
H
an ample divisor on
X

such that
(K_{X}\cdot H)<0
.
Then
M_{H}(r,\xi,\chi)_{0}^{\mu s}
is irreducible.

Proof.

For the proof,
we shall apply Theorem
1.6

to the moduli space on a blow-up of
X
.
By using a result of Nakashima
[
4
, Thm. 1]
on the relations of moduli spaces on

X
and the blow-up, we shall prove the claim.

Let
p:\widetilde{X}\to X
be the blow-up of
X
at a point
x
and
C_{1}
the exceptional divisor.
For
E\in M_{H}(r,\xi,\chi)_{0}^{\mu s}
,
let
E^{\prime}
be a locally free sheaf on
\widetilde{X}
fitting in an exact sequence

(1.6)

0\to p^{*}(E)\to E^{\prime}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{C_{1}}(-1)\to 0.

Then
E^{\prime}
is a
\mu
-stable locally free sheaf with respect to
H_{n}:=np^{*}(H)-C_{1}
for sufficiently large

n

[
4
, Prop. 2.5]
.
We note that
c_{1}(E^{\prime})=\pi^{*}(\xi)+C_{1}
and
\chi(E^{\prime})=\chi(E)=\chi
.
Since
c_{1}(E^{\prime})
is primitive and
(H_{n}\cdot K_{\widetilde{X}})=n(H\cdot K_{X})+1<0
for
n\gg 0
,
Theorem
1.6
implies
M_{H_{n}}(r,\pi^{*}(\xi)+C_{1},\chi)
is irreducible.
Since
[
4
, Thm 2.6]
holds without assuming the universal family,
we get the irreducibility of
M_{H}(r,\xi,\chi)_{0}^{\mu s}
.
∎

Remark 1.8
.

If there is not a universal family, then the Grassmanian bundle in
[
4
, Thm 2.6]

is étale locally trivial.

2.
A study of chambers

2.1.
Several dimension estimates

Let
C_{0}+xf
be an ample
{\mathbb{Q}}
-divisor on
X
.
Then
x>\frac{e}{2}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume that
x>\frac{e}{2}
,

(D\cdot(C_{0}+xf))=0
and
(D\cdot f)>0
.
Then

(D\cdot K_{X})\geq 0
.

Proof.

We set
D=aC_{0}+bf
.
By
(D\cdot(C_{0}+xf))=0
and
(D\cdot f)\geq 0
, we get

0=-ae+b+xa
and
a\geq 0
.
Hence
b=-a(x-e)
.

(2.1)

\begin{split}(D\cdot K_{X})=&((aC_{0}+bf)\cdot(-2C_{0}-(2-2g+e)f))\\
=&2ae-2b-(2-2g+e)a\\
=&a(2x-e+2g-2)\geq 0.\end{split}

∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume that
(K_{X}\cdot H(x))<0
.

(2.2)

\dim({\mathcal{M}}_{H(x)}({\bf e})^{\mu ss}\setminus{\mathcal{M}}_{H(x-\epsilon)}({\bf e})^{\mu ss})<2r^{2}\Delta({\bf e})+r^{2}(g-1).

Proof.

For
E\in{\mathcal{M}}_{H(x)}({\bf e})^{\mu ss}\setminus{\mathcal{M}}_{H(x-\epsilon)}({\bf e})^{\mu ss}
,
let
0\subset F_{1}\subset F_{2}\subset\cdots\subset F_{s}=E
be the Harder-Narasimhan filtration
of
E
with respect to

H(x-\epsilon)
.
We set
E_{i}:=F_{i}/F_{i-1}
.
Then

\frac{(c_{1}(E_{i})\cdot(H(x-\epsilon)))}{\operatorname{rk}E_{i}}>\frac{(c_{1}(E_{j})\cdot(H(x-\epsilon)))}{\operatorname{rk}E_{j}},\;\frac{(c_{1}(E_{i})\cdot H(x))}{\operatorname{rk}E_{i}}=\frac{(c_{1}(E_{j})\cdot H(x))}{\operatorname{rk}E_{j}},

for
i<j
.
Then

\frac{(c_{1}(E_{i})\cdot f)}{\operatorname{rk}E_{i}}<\frac{(c_{1}(E_{j})\cdot f)}{\operatorname{rk}E_{j}}

for
i<j
.

We set
{\bf e}_{i}:=\tau(E_{i})
.

\mu_{i}:=\mu({\bf e}_{i})
,
\Delta_{i}:=\Delta({\bf e}_{i})
.
Then
((\mu_{j}-\mu_{i})\cdot f)>0
.
By Lemma
2.1
, we get
((\mu_{j}-\mu_{i})\cdot K_{X})\geq 0
.
Hence
-\chi({\bf e}_{i},{\bf e}_{j})>0
for
i<j
. By Proposition
1.1
, we get

(2.3)

\dim{\mathcal{F}}_{H(x-\epsilon)}({\bf e}_{1},{\bf e}_{2},...,{\bf e}_{s})<2r^{2}\Delta({\bf e})+r^{2}(g-1).

∎

Proposition 2.3
.

Assume that
(K_{X}\cdot H(x))<0
.
Let
N(x)
be the set of irreducible components
{\mathcal{M}}
of
{\mathcal{M}}({\bf e})

such that
{\mathcal{M}}\cap{\mathcal{M}}_{H(x)}({\bf e})^{\mu ss}\neq\emptyset
.
Then
N(x)\subset N(y)
for
x\geq y
.

Proof.

For an ample divisor
H(x)
, we take a sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
.
Since
{\mathcal{M}}_{H(x\pm\epsilon)}({\bf e})^{\mu ss}
are open substacks of
{\mathcal{M}}_{H(x)}({\bf e})^{\mu ss}
,

N(x\pm\epsilon)\subset N(x)
.
By Proposition
1.5
and Lemma
2.2
,
we also have
N(x)=N(x-\epsilon)
.
Therefore the claim holds.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
E
be a
\mu
-semi-stable sheaf of rank
r
on
X
with respect to an ample divisor
such that
\Delta(E)=0
.
Then
E
is
\mu
-semi-stable for any ample divisor and

r\mid(c_{1}(E)\cdot f)
.

Proof.

Assume that
E
is
\mu
-semi-stable with respect to an ample divisor
H
and

E
is not
\mu
-semi-stable with respect to an ample divisor
H^{\prime}
.
Then there is a
\mu
-semi-stable subsheaf
E_{1}
of
E
satisfying

(i)

E_{2}:=E/E_{1}
is
\mu
-semi-stable,

(ii)

((\mu(E_{1})-\mu(E_{2}))\cdot H)\leq 0
and
((\mu(E_{1})-\mu(E_{2}))\cdot H^{\prime})>0
.

By Hodge index theorem, we get
((\mu(E_{1})-\mu(E_{2}))^{2})<0
.
By
[
9
, Lem. 2.1]
,

\Delta(E)=\frac{\operatorname{rk}(E_{1})}{\operatorname{rk}(E)}\Delta(E_{1})+\frac{\operatorname{rk}(E_{2})}{\operatorname{rk}(E)}\Delta(E_{2})-\frac{\operatorname{rk}(E_{1})\operatorname{rk}(E_{2})}{2\operatorname{rk}(E)^{2}}((\mu(E_{1})-\mu(E_{2}))^{2}).

Since
\Delta(E_{1})\geq 0
and
\Delta(E_{2})\geq 0
,
we have
((\mu(E_{1})-\mu(E_{2}))^{2})\geq 0
. Therefore
E
is
\mu
-semi-stable for all
ample divisors.
For an ample divisor
C_{0}+nf

(n\gg 0)
,
there is no
\mu
-semi-stable sheaf
E
if
r\nmid(c_{1}(E)\cdot f)
.
Therefore the claim holds.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

We assume that
(K_{X}\cdot H)<0
.

(i)

Assume that
r\nmid(\xi\cdot f)
. Then

\dim({\mathcal{M}}_{H}({\bf e})^{\mu ss}\setminus{\mathcal{M}}_{H}({\bf e})^{\mu s})<\dim{\mathcal{M}}_{H}({\bf e})^{\mu s}

for a general
H
.

(ii)

Assume that
r\mid(\xi\cdot f)
and
\Delta>0
.
Then

\dim({\mathcal{M}}_{H}({\bf e})^{\mu ss}\setminus{\mathcal{M}}_{H}({\bf e})^{\mu s})<\dim{\mathcal{M}}_{H}({\bf e})^{\mu s}

for a general
H
.

Proof.

For a
\mu
-semi-stable sheaf
E
,
let

F:0\subset F_{1}\subset F_{2}\subset\cdots\subset F_{s}=E

be a Jordan-Hölder filtration of a
\mu
-semi-stable sheaf
E

with respect to
\mu
-stability.
We set
E_{i}=F_{i}/F_{i-1}
.
Then

-\chi(E_{i},E_{j})=\operatorname{rk}(E_{i})\operatorname{rk}(E_{j})(g-1+\Delta(E_{i})+\Delta(E_{j})).

For the first case, Lemma
2.4
implies

\Delta(E_{i})>0
for all
i
.
Hence
-\sum_{i<j}\chi(E_{i},E_{j})>0
.

For the second case, by

\operatorname{rk}E\Delta(E)=\sum_{i}\operatorname{rk}E_{i}\Delta(E_{i})

(
[
9
, Lem. 2.1]
),

\Delta(E_{i})>0
for an integer
i
.
Hence
-\sum_{i<j}\chi(E_{i},E_{j})>0
.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

We assume that
(K_{X}\cdot H)<0
. Then

\dim({\mathcal{M}}_{H}({\bf e})^{\mu s}\setminus{\mathcal{M}}_{H}({\bf e})_{0}^{\mu s})=\dim{\mathcal{M}}_{H}({\bf e})^{\mu s}-(r-1)
.

Proposition 2.7
.

We assume that
(K_{X}\cdot H)<0
.
If
r\nmid(\xi\cdot f)
or
\Delta>0
,
then
{\mathcal{M}}_{H}({\bf e})^{\mu s}_{0}
is an open dense substack of
{\mathcal{M}}_{H}({\bf e})^{\mu ss}
.
In particular
{\mathcal{M}}_{H}({\bf e})^{\mu ss}
is irreducible.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.5
and Lemma
2.6
, we get the first claim.
Then the irreducibility follows from Corollary
1.7
.
∎

Remark 2.8
.

If
\Delta=0
, then
there is no wall (Lemma
2.4
). Hence all
E\in{\mathcal{M}}_{H}({\bf e})^{\mu ss}
are pull-back of
vector bundles on

, which shows the irreducibility of

.

2.2.
Non-emptiness for
g=1
.

Proposition 2.9
.

Assume that
g=1
.
For
{\bf e}=(r,\xi,\chi)
with
0<(\xi\cdot f)<r
,
there exists a
\mu
-semi-stable sheaf
E
of
\tau(E)={\bf e}
with respect to
H(x)

if and only if
x\leq\frac{e}{2}+\frac{r^{2}}{r_{1}r_{2}}\Delta({\bf e})
,
where
r_{1}:=(\xi\cdot f)
and
r_{2}:=r-r_{1}
.

Proof.

If
e>0=2g-2
, then the claim is proved in
[
9
, Prop. 4.1]
.
So we assume that
e=0,-1
.

We set
\xi=r_{1}C_{0}+df
.
Let
E
be a vector bundle of rank
r

defined by the following exact sequence

(2.4)

0\to F_{1}(C_{0})\to E\to F_{2}\to 0,

where
F_{1}
(resp.
F_{2}
) is the pull-back of a semi-stable vector bundle of rank
r_{1}
(resp.
r_{2}
) on
C

with degree
d_{1}=r_{1}e-d+\chi-(r+r_{1})(1-g)
(resp.
d-d_{1}
).
Then

(2.5)

\begin{split}\chi(E)=&\chi(F_{1}(C_{0}))+\chi(F_{2})\\
=&d+d_{1}+(r+r_{1})(1-g)-r_{1}e=\chi.\end{split}

Hence we get

\tau(E)=(r,r_{1}C_{0}+df,\chi)={\bf e}
.
We set

x_{0}:=\frac{e}{2}+\frac{r^{2}}{r_{1}r_{2}}\Delta(E)=e+\frac{1}{r_{1}r_{2}}(r_{1}d-rd_{1}).

Then

((\mu(F_{1}(C_{0}))-\mu(F_{2}))\cdot H(x))=0
if and only if

x=x_{0}
.
Hence
E\in{\mathcal{M}}_{H(x_{0})}({\bf e})^{\mu ss}
.
Thus
{\mathcal{M}}_{H(x_{0})}({\bf e})^{\mu ss}
is not empty.
By Proposition
2.3
,

{\mathcal{M}}_{H(x)}({\bf e})^{\mu ss}
is also nonempty for
x<x_{0}
.

Conversely assume that there is a
\mu
-semi-stable sheaf
E
with respect to
H(y)
