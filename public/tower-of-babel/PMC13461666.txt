Quantitative Pharmacology Justifying Ribociclib Dose in Early Breast Cancer
Authors: Yan Ji, Craig Wang, Francois Pierre Combes, Yu-Yun Ho, Peter A Fasching, Michael Untch, Juan Pablo Zarate, John Crown
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and ObjectiveRibociclib was recently approved for the treatment of hormone receptor-positive/human epidermal growth factor receptor 2-negative early breast cancer (EBC) at the starting dose of 400 mg orally once daily (3 weeks on/1 week off), following previous approval in hormone receptor-positive/human epidermal growth factor receptor 2-negative advanced breast cancer at the starting dose of 600 mg orally once daily (3 weeks on/1 week off). This study aims to characterize ribociclib pharmacokinetics and its relationship with QT interval corrected according to Fridericia’s formula (QTcF) in patients with EBC and justify the dose in EBC.MethodsData from several studies, including the pivotal phase III trial in patients with EBC and phase I–III trials in patients with advanced breast cancer and advanced cancer were analyzed. Ribociclib pharmacokinetics was characterized by non-compartmental or population pharmacokinetic analyses. The exposure–QTcF relationship was assessed by linear mixed-effects modeling. Neutropenia and QTcF data were compared by dose and population.ResultsAt the 400-mg dose, ribociclib steady-state clearance was ~ 20% higher in patients with EBC than patients with advanced cancer, suggesting differences in pharmacokinetics between populations. Steady-state mean area under the plasma concentration–time curve and maximum (peak) plasma drug concentration were ~ 50% lower and steady-state clearance was ~ 50% higher in patients with EBC receiving 400 mg compared with patients with advanced cancer and advanced breast cancer receiving 600 mg, attributed to differences in both dose and population. The pharmacokinetic–QTcF analysis showed that the patient population was a significant covariate in QTcF response. The model-estimated mean change from baseline in QTcF at steady-state maximum (peak) plasma drug concentration was substantially lower in patients with EBC receiving 400 mg (10.0 ms) versus in patients with advanced breast cancer receiving 600 mg (20.7–23.7 ms), likely due to the dose and pharmacokinetic exposure effect and the population effect. Patients with EBC receiving 400 mg experienced lower rates and severity of both neutropenia and QTcF prolongation than patients with advanced breast cancer receiving 600 mg.ConclusionsThis integrative analysis characterized the ribociclib pharmacokinetic and exposure–QTcF relationship, revealed the population effect on both pharmacokinetic and QTcF response, and justified the 400-mg dose in EBC. This work illustrates the utility and impact of quantitative pharmacology in justifying different doses across different patient populations for oncology therapies.Clinical Trial RegistrationNATALEE (NCT03701334, 2018-09-21); AMALEE (NCT03822468, 2019-01-28); MONALEESA-2 (NCT01958021, 2013-10-04); MONALEESA-3 (NCT02422615, 2015-04-01); MONALEESA-7 (NCT02278120, 2014-10-22); X1101 (NCT01898845, 2013-07-01); X2101 (NCT01237236, 2010-11-05); X2107 (NCT01872260, 2013-05-30).Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s40262-026-01643-3.

Key Points

A pharmacokinetic analysis demonstrated lower ribociclib exposure for the 400-mg dose in early breast cancer (EBC) versus 600 mg in advanced breast cancer; these differences in exposure can be attributed to lower dose, higher clearance with 400 mg due to pharmacokinetic non-linearity, and a population effect potentially due to undetectable disease burden with EBC.A pharmacokinetic–QT interval corrected according to Fridericia’s formula (QTcF) analysis showed that patient population (EBC vs advanced breast cancer) was a significant covariate in QTcF response; the lower rates of QTcF prolongation observed with the ribociclib 400-mg dose in EBC (NATALEE) versus 600 mg in advanced breast cancer are likely due to the lower dose, lower pharmacokinetic exposure in EBC, and a population effect in QTcF response.This integrative analysis justified the ribociclib 400-mg dose in patients with EBC and illustrates the utility and impact of quantitative pharmacology in justifying different doses across different patient populations for oncology therapies.

Introduction

The addition of cyclin-dependent kinases 4 and 6 (CDK4/6) inhibitors to endocrine therapy has improved outcomes in patients with hormone receptor-positive (HR+)/human epidermal growth factor receptor 2-negative (HER2−) advanced breast cancer (ABC); the recommended treatment options for HR+/HER2− ABC include a first-line CDK4/6 inhibitor combined with endocrine therapy [1]. Ribociclib, an orally bioavailable and highly selective CDK4/6 inhibitor, has been approved for the first-line and second-line treatment of pre-/peri-menopausal and post-menopausal women with HR+/HER2− ABC at the starting dose of 600 mg once daily (3 weeks on/1 week off) [2, 3]. Dose reductions to 400 mg or 200 mg are permitted, and clinical evidence has shown that these dose reductions do not compromise treatment efficacy [4]. As the only CDK4/6 inhibitor to demonstrate statistically significant overall survival benefit in all of its phase III trials (MONALEESA-2, MONALEESA-3, and MONALEESA-7) in HR+/HER2− ABC [5–7], ribociclib plus a non-steroidal aromatase inhibitor (NSAI) has been given the highest recommendations for HR+/HER2− ABC across international guidelines (National Comprehensive Cancer Network®: category 1; European Society for Medical Oncology-Magnitude of Clinical Benefit Scale: “4” for each MONALEESA trial, with “green” color) [1, 8–10].

The favorable efficacy and tolerable safety profile of ribociclib observed in the MONALEESA trials in ABC prompted evaluation in early breast cancer (EBC) in the adjuvant setting. In the pivotal phase III NATALEE trial, conducted in patients with stage II/III HR+/HER2− EBC at high risk of recurrence, ribociclib plus NSAI demonstrated a statistically significant and clinically meaningful improvement in invasive disease-free survival (iDFS), corresponding to a 25% relative risk reduction compared with NSAI alone (hazard ratio: 0.749; 95% confidence interval [CI] 0.628–0.892) [11–13]. At the preplanned 5-year analysis, this benefit was maintained and deepened, with a 28% relative risk reduction (hazard ratio: 0.716; 95% CI 0.618–0.829) [14]. Based on the results of the NATALEE trial, ribociclib recently received regulatory approval in the USA and European Union for the adjuvant treatment of patients with high-risk stage II/III HR+/ HER2− EBC [15, 16]. Unlike in the ABC setting, the approved and recommended starting dose of ribociclib in EBC is 400 mg once daily (3 weeks on/1 week off). Dose reduction to 200 mg is permitted to manage adverse events (AEs), and analyses have demonstrated that dose reduction to manage AEs does not compromise the efficacy benefit of ribociclib [17].

The starting dose of 400 mg in patients with EBC in the NATALEE trial was selected based on pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic (PK/PD) modeling informed by previous data from patients with ABC, together with clinical considerations for long-term tolerability, adherence, and efficacy in the adjuvant setting. In the absence of detectable tumor burden, a lower starting dose was expected to maintain sufficient target inhibition while improving tolerability and supporting sustained treatment adherence. Regarding tolerability, ribociclib has a well-characterized safety profile, with dose-dependent AEs that are predictable, manageable, and largely asymptomatic [4]. In the MONALEESA-2, MONALEESA-3, and MONALEESA-7 studies, the most common AE was neutropenia (all-grade, 74%; grade 3/4, 60.0%), while all-grade QT prolongation occurred in 6.5% of patients (grade 3/4, 1.2%). In patients with ABC, the exposure–response relationships for neutropenia and QT interval corrected according to Fridericia’s formula (QTcF) prolongation (concentration-dependent AEs of special interest [AESIs] with ribociclib) have been characterized, and these analyses supported dose justification and the risk-management recommendations for patients with ABC in the ribociclib label [17, 18]. However, the population PK (popPK) and exposure–safety relationships have not yet been reported in the EBC patient population, which is substantially larger than the ABC patient population.

This study aimed to characterize the PK profile of ribociclib in patients with EBC, evaluate the exposure–response relationship for QTcF, and compare the PK and safety findings with data from ABC and advanced cancer (AC) populations. The analysis revealed population effects on both ribociclib PK and PD response (QTcF prolongation), which explains the notably lower QTcF prolongation observed in patients with EBC compared with those with ABC. These quantitative pharmacology assessments justify the ribociclib 400-mg dose in patients with EBC.

Methods

Pharmacokinetic (PK) Assessments

Pharmacokinetic data from the NATALEE (NCT03701334), AMALEE (NCT03822468), X2101 (NCT01237236), and A2106 trials were analyzed and compared by dose and population. The study designs and key characteristics are summarized in Table S1 of the Electronic Supplementary Material (ESM). Briefly, NATALEE studied ribociclib 400 mg in patients with HR+/HER2− EBC [11], AMALEE studied 400-mg versus 600-mg doses in patients with HR+/HER2− ABC [19], X2101 was a first-in-human, phase I dose-escalation study in patients with advanced solid tumors and lymphomas and evaluated ribociclib at 600 mg [20], and A2106 investigated single and multiple doses of ribociclib and evaluated multiple dosing of 400 mg in healthy volunteers (HVs) [21]. Sparse PK blood samples were collected from a subset of patients in NATALEE, sparse samples (and extensive samples, in a subset of patients) were collected in AMALEE, and extensive PK samples were collected in Studies X2101 and A2106. Ribociclib plasma concentrations were measured using a validated liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry assay with a lower limit of quantification of 1.00 ng/mL [22].

Observed steady-state concentrations of ribociclib were compared across the NATALEE, AMALEE, X2101, and A2106 trials at the same dose levels. Steady-state PK parameters that were analyzed included the maximum (peak) plasma drug concentration (Cmax), time to reach the maximum (peak) plasma concentration following drug administration (tmax), area under the plasma concentration–time curve from time zero to 24 h (AUC24), trough plasma concentration (Ctrough), and apparent total body clearance of drug from plasma after oral administration (CL/F). Pharmacokinetic parameters were determined using a non-compartmental analysis (NCA) for AMALEE, X2101, A2106, and NATALEE (Ctrough), or predicted by a popPK model for NATALEE (Cmax, tmax, AUC, Ctrough, CL/F). The parameters were summarized as geometric means (percentage geometric coefficient of variation, geo-CV%) or medians (min, max), as appropriate. For comparison across populations based on the observed data (PK concentrations, or parameters derived from the NCA), the data at the same dose level were compared and data of patients with dose reduction were not included in the analysis.

Population PK Modeling

A previous popPK model based on patients with ABC or AC was updated to incorporate data from patients with EBC [18]. The previous popPK model was a two-compartment model with delayed zero-order oral absorption and clearance from the central compartment. It included dose and body weight as covariates on clearance, intercompartment clearance, and peripheral volume. The dose effect was added to account for the non-linearity of ribociclib pharmacokinetics. For this study, the popPK model was updated to describe the lower exposure observed in patients with EBC in NATALEE in comparison with those with ABC or AC as reported previously [23]. The updated model retained the same structural model as the previous popPK model but was refitted using data from NATALEE only. For the final updated popPK model in patients with EBC, clearance was re-estimated to capture the population difference. In addition, interindividual variability was included in Tk0, the parameter describing the duration of the zero-order absorption process. The residual error model was also adjusted to reflect the new study data. Final population parameter estimates in patients with EBC are presented in Table S2 of the ESM. This updated model was used to estimate PK parameters in patients with EBC. The analysis was performed using Monolix Suite Version 2021R2. Data preparation, graphical exploration, and other plots were performed using R 4.1.0.

Exposure–Response Analysis for QTcF

The PK-QTcF model previously established in patients with ABC and AC was updated with the addition of data from the NATALEE and AMALEE studies [17]. The updated PK-QTcF analysis was conducted using pooled data from ribociclib in combination with endocrine therapy arms from the NATALEE, AMALEE, MONALEESA-2 (NCT01958021), MONALEESA-3 (NCT02422615), MONALEESA-7 (NCT02278120), X1101 (NCT01898845), X2101, and X2107 (NCT01872260) studies (Table S1 of the ESM). All data from patients on the 3-weeks-on/1-week-off ribociclib regimen in these studies and on the continuous ribociclib dosing regimen in Study X2101 were included, with the exception of the ribociclib plus tamoxifen arm in MONALEESA-7, which was excluded from the pool because ribociclib is not indicated in combination with tamoxifen. These pooled data formed the PK-electrocardiogram set that was used for the PK-QTcF analysis.

The relationship between ribociclib plasma concentration and change from baseline in QTcF (ΔQTcF) was assessed with a linear mixed-effects model. The ΔQTcF, which has a matched ribociclib concentration, was used as the response variable in this PK-QTcF analysis. The covariates evaluated include patient population (EBC vs ABC and AC), baseline QTcF, age group (< 40, ≥ 40 to < 65, ≥ 65 years), sex (male vs female), race (Asian vs non-Asian), and combination partner (NSAI [letrozole or anastrozole] vs fulvestrant vs no combination). The model with the lowest Akaike information criterion was selected, and non-significant (p-value > 0.01) covariates were removed.

Model-based estimates of ΔQTcF were derived using the popPK model-predicted geometric mean steady-state Cmax in patients with EBC from NATALEE and the observed geometric mean steady-state Cmax in the ABC populations in AMALEE and MONALEESA-2, MONALEESA-3, and MONALEESA-7.

Analysis of Neutropenia and QTcF Data

Neutropenia and QTcF prolongation are concentration-dependent AESIs with ribociclib, and data on these AESIs from NATALEE were compared with results from ABC studies AMALEE (600 mg) and MONALEESA-2, MONALEESA-3, and MONALEESA-7 (600 mg). The incidence of all-grade and grade ≥ 3 neutropenia was summarized across studies. Observed ΔQTcF values were obtained from 2 and/or 4 h post-dose on cycle 1 day 15. Model-based ΔQTcF estimates at steady-state Cmax were derived from the updated PK-QTcF model using either observed or popPK model-predicted steady-state Cmax values. Notable QTcF values were summarized as the proportion of patients with values exceeding predefined QTcF thresholds (> 450 ms, > 480 ms, or > 500 ms, or ΔQTcF > 30 ms or > 60 ms) at any post-baseline assessment.

Results

PK Assessments

Because there is no drug–drug interaction between ribociclib and either NSAIs or fulvestrant [23], ribociclib concentrations and PK parameters were compared across monotherapy and combination regimens with an NSAI or fulvestrant.

Comparison of Ribociclib Pharmacokinetics by Population

Ribociclib PK data were compared by population at the same dose level (400 or 600 mg). At the 400-mg dose level, the mean PK concentrations in patients with EBC (NATALEE study) were lower than those observed in patients with ABC (AMALEE study) but were comparable to those observed in HVs (Study A2106) (Fig. 1). The model-estimated mean steady-state CL/F in patients with EBC was approximately 22% higher than that in patients with ABC at the 400-mg dose (38.4 and 31.5 L/h, respectively) but similar to the observed value in HVs (37.7 L/h) at the same dose level (Table 1). Similarly, at the 600-mg dose, the model-estimated mean steady-state CL/F was approximately 22% higher in patients with EBC than in patients with ABC (32.7 and 26.8 L/h, respectively) (Table 1). These data showed that ribociclib pharmacokinetics is different by population, with higher clearance and lower exposure in patients with EBC and in HVs than in patients with ABC.

Comparison of Ribociclib Pharmacokinetics by Dose

Ribociclib PK data were compared by dose within the same patient population (ABC/AC or EBC). In patients with ABC in the AMALEE trial [19], the observed geometric mean CL/F was approximately 16% higher at 400 mg than at 600 mg (24.4 vs 21.0 L/h) (Table 1). The previous popPK model based on data from patients with ABC and AC showed consistent findings, with the estimated mean steady-state CL/F approximately 18% higher at 400 mg than at 600 mg (31.5 vs 26.8 L/h) [18]. The updated popPK model predicted a similar trend within the EBC population, with the estimated mean CL/F approximately 17% higher at 400 mg than the predicted value at 600 mg (38.4 vs 32.7 L/h) (Table 1). These data showed that ribociclib exhibited non-linear pharmacokinetics, with higher clearance at the 400-mg dose compared with the 600-mg dose.

Comparison of Ribociclib Pharmacokinetics by Population and Dose

Ribociclib PK data were compared between patients with EBC receiving 400 mg and patients with ABC/AC receiving 600 mg. Compared with the steady-state exposure of 600 mg in patients with AC (Study X2101), popPK model-estimated ribociclib steady-state exposure in patients with EBC at the 400-mg dose was approximately 48% and 56% lower for Cmax and AUC, respectively (Table 1). In patients with EBC treated with 400 mg, the mean popPK model-estimated CL/F (38.4 L/h) was approximately 50% higher than that observed in patients with AC treated with 600 mg (25.5 L/h; Study X2101) and approximately 43% higher than that estimated by the popPK model in patients with ABC/AC at the 600-mg dose (26.8 L/h) (Table 1).

Exposure–Response Analysis for QTcF Prolongation

In the pooled data used for the PK-QTcF analyses, 1297 of 1372 patients (94.5%) were female and the remaining were male (75 patients [5.5%]). The overall median age was 58.0 years, with 8.2%, 60.0%, and 31.8% of patients being aged < 40 years, 40 to < 65 years, and ≥ 65 years, respectively. Patients were predominantly non-Asian (87.0%), with the majority of patients (62.4%) having an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group Performance Status of 0 (Table S3 of the ESM). The distribution of baseline QTcF in patients was comparable across the eight studies pooled, with a mean (standard deviation) baseline QTcF of 413.38 (17.600) ms in the PK-electrocardiogram set (Table S4 of the ESM).

The updated model characterized the PK–QTcF relationship in patients with EBC and patients with ABC/AC (Fig. 2). The final model (Table 2) showed that baseline QTcF, combination partner (NSAI vs fulvestrant vs no combination), and population (EBC vs ABC/AC) were significantly associated with ΔQTcF among the covariates that were considered, while age (<40, ≥ 40 to < 65, ≥ 65 years), sex, and race (Asian vs non-Asian) were not statistically significant and were therefore removed during covariate selection steps. The fitted model parameters showed that at the same ribociclib plasma concentration, patients with EBC exhibited a lower mean ΔQTcF compared with patients with ABC, with an estimated difference of 5.37 ms (90% CI 3.26–7.49) (Fig. 2; Table 2). Using the updated PK–QTcF model, in the EBC population receiving ribociclib 400 mg with an NSAI as a combination partner, the estimated mean ΔQTcF was 10.0 ms (90% CI 8.02–11.91) (Table 3) based on the geometric mean of steady-state Cmax predicted by the updated popPK model.

Comparison of Neutropenia and QTcF Data by Population and Dose

The neutropenia and QTcF prolongation data were compared between patients with EBC receiving 400 mg and patients with ABC receiving 600 mg. The incidence of neutropenia for both all-grade and grade 3/4 events was lower in patients with EBC receiving ribociclib 400 mg in NATALEE than in patients with ABC receiving 600 mg in AMALEE and the MONALEESA trials (Table 4). In patients with EBC receiving ribociclib 400 mg in NATALEE, the model-estimated mean ΔQTcF at steady-state Cmax was 10.7–13.7 ms lower than that observed in patients with ABC receiving 600 mg in AMALEE and the MONALEESA trials (10.0 vs 20.7–23.7 ms, respectively) (Table 5). Consistently, at the 400-mg dose in NATALEE, the observed mean ΔQTcF at cycle 1 day 15 (2 and 4 h post-dose) was lower than that in patients with ABC treated with 600 mg at equivalent time points (Table 5). The percentage of patients with notable QTcF values (QTcF > 450 ms, > 480 ms, or > 500 ms; ΔQTcF values > 30 ms or > 60 ms) was also lower among patients with EBC receiving 400 mg compared with patients with ABC receiving 600 mg (Table 6).

Discussion

In this work, we characterize the PK profile and exposure–safety relationship of ribociclib in patients with EBC, which covers a substantially greater population than ABC, the previously approved indication. As anticipated, compared with patients with ABC treated with ribociclib 600 mg, patients with EBC treated with ribociclib 400 mg showed lower ribociclib exposure and experienced less QTcF prolongation. However, the exposure difference between patients in NATALEE and patients in ABC or AC studies was greater than what would be expected based solely on a 200-mg dose difference and the previously characterized ribociclib pharmacokinetics in patients with ABC or AC. Similarly, the reduction in QTcF prolongation in patients with EBC was greater than anticipated from the established exposure–response relationship in the ABC and AC population. Leveraging data from patients with ABC and HVs, our comprehensive PK and exposure–response analyses revealed that the lower exposure and substantially lower QTcF prolongation observed in patients with EBC could be explained by multiple factors beyond the lower dose level alone.

First, a population effect was observed in ribociclib pharmacokinetics, with approximately 22% higher CL/F of ribociclib at the 400-mg dose in patients with EBC compared with those with ABC. As ribociclib is a cytochrome P450 3A4 isozyme substrate, this difference in clearance may be related to the downregulation of cytochrome P450 3A4 expression in patients with AC, which can lead to reduced clearance and higher exposure in ABC, a population with higher tumor burden than patients with EBC [24]. Second, ribociclib exhibits non-linear pharmacokinetics, with clearance decreasing as dose increases within the same population (EBC or ABC/AC). This trend was evident in AMALEE, in which the mean observed CL/F was approximately 16% higher at 400 mg than at 600 mg in patients with ABC (24.4 vs 21.0 L/h). In addition, the popPK-estimated steady-state CL/F was 18% higher for 400 mg than for 600 mg in the same population (31.5 vs 26.8 L/h). In patients with EBC, the popPK model-estimated steady-state CL/F was 17% higher at 400 mg than that estimated at 600 mg (38.4 vs 32.7 L/h). Consistently, previous analyses have also reported that ribociclib exposure increases were slightly greater than dose proportional [23]. Taken together, the lower PK exposure in patients with EBC at the dose of 400 mg than that in patients with ABC at the dose of 600 mg can be attributed to lower dose, higher clearance due to PK non-linearity, and population effect due to undetectable disease burden in patients with EBC.

To elucidate the exposure–response relationship for QTcF in patients with EBC, the prior ribociclib PK-QTcF model developed using data from patients with ABC or AC was updated to include PK and QTcF data from patients with EBC in the pivotal NATALEE trial, supplemented with data from AMALEE, which was a randomized phase II trial comparing ribociclib 400 mg and 600 mg in patients with ABC [19]. The updated PK-QTcF model revealed that patients with EBC had lower ΔQTcF (− 5.37 ms) than those in the ABC population at the same ribociclib concentration, demonstrating that patient population is a significant covariate for QTcF response. Using this model, the estimated mean ΔQTcF at steady-state Cmax was substantially lower for 400 mg in the EBC population than that at 600 mg in the ABC population (10.0 vs 20.7–23.7 ms, respectively). These findings were consistent with observed ΔQTcF values: at steady state on cycle 1 day 15 (2 h post-dose), the mean observed ΔQTcF was 9.4 ms in patients with EBC receiving 400 mg compared with 19.5–20.8 ms in patients with ABC receiving 600 mg. The proportion of patients with notable electrocardiogram values was also consistently lower among patients with EBC receiving 400 mg compared with those with ABC receiving 600 mg.

Integrating data from the PK and exposure–response analyses, the substantially lower QTcF prolongation in patients in NATALEE compared with patients with ABC receiving 600 mg is likely due to several factors. First, patients with EBC in NATALEE had lower PK exposure than that of patients with ABC, driven by the lower dose, faster clearance due to PK non-linearity, and population-related differences in clearance as described above. Additionally, the PK-QTcF analysis identified patient population (EBC vs ABC/AC) as a significant covariate for QTcF prolongation response, suggesting that patients with EBC may be less sensitive to the PD response of QTcF prolongation than those with ABC. The mechanism of the population effect in drug-mediated QTcF response is unclear. A possible explanation could be the healthier cardiac condition in patients with EBC, as it has been reported that differences in cardiac disease and electrolyte imbalances can contribute to differences in ΔQTcF between populations (e.g., patients with cancer vs a healthy population) [25]. To summarize, the substantially lower QTcF prolongation observed in patients in NATALEE at 400 mg than in patients with ABC at 600 mg is likely due to the difference in dose, the effect of non-linearity on pharmacokinetics, as well as the effect of patient population on both PK exposure and QTcF response.

For neutropenia, another concentration-dependent AESI, the incidence of all-grade and grade 3/4 events at 400 mg in patients with EBC was also lower than that observed at 600 mg in the ABC trials. Collectively, these findings confirm the improved tolerability profile of ribociclib 400 mg in EBC compared with 600 mg in ABC, with lower rates and severity of both neutropenia and QTcF prolongation.

Because of the sparse PK sampling in NATALEE, steady-state PK exposure of patients with EBC (Cmax and AUC) was predicted by the updated popPK model rather than being derived by an NCA as for the other studies, representing a limitation of this analysis. Another limitation is that exposure–response analysis for efficacy could not be conducted because of the limited number of iDFS events observed in the PK cohort of NATALEE. However, ribociclib 400 mg demonstrated clinically meaningful and statistically significant improvements in iDFS and consistent benefits across secondary endpoints, including recurrence-free survival and distant disease-free survival [11]. Ribociclib benefits persisted beyond treatment discontinuation, with sustained iDFS improvements observed at the prespecified 5-year follow-up (median follow-up, 55.4 months) [14]. Together, the clinical outcomes of NATALEE and the PK and exposure–safety analyses presented here confirm the selection of the 400-mg dose of ribociclib in patients with EBC.

In the ABC setting, the approved ribociclib starting dose is 600 mg once daily (3 weeks on/1 week off), supported by prior PK/PD analyses from the three pivotal phase III MONALEESA trials [17] and by the observed lack of non-inferior efficacy of the 400-mg dose compared with 600 mg in the phase II AMALEE study [19]. The current analysis further supports the 600-mg starting dose in ABC, indicating that patient population (EBC vs ABC) may influence both PK and PD responses; therefore, dose selection should be evaluated within each disease setting.

Conclusions

Integrating data from the phase III NATALEE trial in EBC and the phase II and III trials in ABC, this analysis characterized ribociclib PK and exposure–safety relationships in EBC, identified a population effect on both PK and PD response (QTcF prolongation) as key contributors to the substantially improved cardiac safety in patients with EBC, and justified the lower 400-mg dose in patients with EBC than the 600-mg dose in patients with ABC. The findings from this integrative analysis illustrate the value and impact of quantitative clinical pharmacology in justifying different doses across patient populations for oncology therapeutics.
