Causal Edge Rees Algebras for Spatiotemporal Graphs
Marcílio Ferreira dos Santos, Cleiton de Lima Ricardo

Abstract
Understanding the evolution of connectivity in spatiotemporal systems requires mathematical frameworks capable of encoding not only instantaneous interactions but also their cumulative causal structure. In this work, we introduce the Causal Edge Rees Algebra (CERA), a new algebraic construction associated with causal spatiotemporal graphs. Given a temporal filtration induced by causal constraints, we associate a sequence of edge ideals whose Rees algebra encodes the full history of connectivity evolution in a single graded object. This construction establishes a bridge between dynamic graph topology and commutative algebra. In particular, we show that successive quotients of the filtration capture the emergence of new structural connections, allowing the identification of critical edges responsible for the fusion of previously disconnected components. This leads to the definition of temporal bridge modules and to a bridge detection theorem, which relates the dimension of these modules to the reduction in the number of connected components over time. Unlike existing algebraic approaches in topological data analysis, which are primarily based on geometric filtrations, the proposed framework is driven by intrinsic causal constraints. As a result, the CERA captures not only topological features but also their temporal organization and mechanisms of coalescence. The theory provides a new algebraic perspective on causal network dynamics, connecting edge ideals, Rees algebras, and temporal graphs. Beyond its theoretical significance, the framework opens new directions for the analysis of spatiotemporal systems, including epidemic networks, transport systems, and information propagation processes.

Introduction

Mathematical modeling of networked dynamical systems has assumed a central role across several domains, including epidemiology, information spreading, transportation networks, biological systems, and the analysis of complex spatiotemporal processes
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
. In many of these contexts, the observed phenomena are not adequately described by static graphs, as the connections between system elements emerge progressively over time, subject to temporal, spatial, and causal constraints. This motivates the need for mathematical structures capable of representing not only the instantaneous connectivity of a network, but also the accumulated historical evolution of its causal architecture.

Temporal and dynamic graphs have been extensively studied in the literature as natural extensions of classical graph theory, incorporating time into the relationships between vertices
[
2
,
3
]
. However, most existing approaches focus on combinatorial, probabilistic, or topological descriptions of temporal evolution, while the development of an algebraic theory associated with cumulative causality remains largely unexplored. In particular, there is currently no canonical framework that naturally associates a graded algebra to the temporal filtration induced by spatiotemporal causal graphs.

In parallel, recent advances in Topological Data Analysis (TDA) have sought to enrich traditional topological descriptions through tools from commutative algebra. In particular,
[
4
]
introduce the concept of
persistent ideals
, constructing algebraic invariants associated with simplicial filtrations that refine the information captured by persistent homology. These approaches demonstrate that structures such as edge ideals and Stanley–Reisner ideals can provide richer representations of topological evolution. However, such constructions remain essentially tied to geometric filtrations (e.g., Vietoris–Rips type), where dynamics are driven by scale parameters rather than intrinsic causal constraints of the system.

Recent developments have highlighted the emergence of commutative algebra as a powerful framework for the analysis of data-driven complex systems. In particular, the paradigm of
persistent commutative algebra
has been proposed as a multiscale approach to extract algebraic signatures from evolving datasets, extending ideas from topological data analysis to richer algebraic structures
[
5
]
. These approaches emphasize the role of ideals, graded invariants, and algebraic relations as fundamental descriptors of structural patterns across different scales.

Nevertheless, existing methods remain predominantly tied to geometric or scale-based filtrations, where evolution is induced by resolution levels rather than the intrinsic dynamics of the system. In contrast, the framework proposed in this work is fundamentally causal: the filtration emerges from temporal constraints that govern the system itself. This distinction naturally leads to a construction via Rees algebras, capable of encoding not only the presence of structures, but also their causal organization and accumulation over time.

In this work, we propose a new algebraic structure for this problem: the
Causal Edge Rees Algebra (CERA)
, constructed from an increasing filtration of edge ideals associated with the temporal evolution of a causal graph. The notion of causal graph adopted here is based on the framework introduced in
[
6
]
, where spatiotemporal causal graphs were proposed to model temporal constraints in urban epidemic networks.

Given a causal spatiotemporal graph

G=(V,E,\tau),

in which each edge respects a temporal ordering induced by the function
\tau
, we consider the increasing family of accumulated subgraphs

E_{1}\subseteq E_{2}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq E_{k},

and associate to each temporal level an edge ideal
I_{n}
, in the spirit of the classical theory of edge ideals
[
7
,
8
,
9
]
. The CERA is then defined as the graded algebra

\mathrm{CERA}(G)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}I_{n}T^{n},

which encodes, within a single algebraic object, the entire cumulative causal evolution of the system’s connectivity.

Unlike approaches based on persistent ideals, where information is analyzed layer by layer along a filtration, CERA provides a global structure that integrates the full temporal dynamics into a single algebraic object. This difference allows one to capture not only the presence of structures, but also their causal organization and the mechanisms of coalescence between components over time.

The relevance of this construction lies in its ability to translate dynamical graph properties into algebraic and geometric invariants. In particular, we show that the successive quotients

I_{n}/I_{n-1}

precisely capture the emergence of new structural connections, enabling the identification of critical edges responsible for merging previously disconnected components. Based on this observation, we introduce the concept of the
temporal bridge module
, which algebraically describes causal coalescence events in the graph, establishing a novel connection between dynamic graph theory, commutative algebra, and the topology of connectivity.

From a conceptual standpoint, CERA embeds temporal causality into the context of Rees algebras, which are traditionally studied in commutative algebra and algebraic geometry as tools to analyze blow-ups, asymptotic growth of ideals, and associated graded structures
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
. By reinterpreting these constructions in the setting of causal graphs, we open a new line of research in applied combinatorial algebra, closely related to the interactions between monomial ideals, graphs, and simplicial complexes studied in
[
13
,
14
]
.

In addition to establishing the theoretical foundations of this new structure, we present initial results on properties of CERA, including filtration criteria, finite generation, and a bridge detection theorem, which directly relates the dimension of bridge modules to the reduction in the number of connected components throughout the temporal evolution of the graph. These results suggest that CERA constitutes not only a natural generalization of the classical theory of edge ideals
[
7
,
9
]
, but also a new mathematical tool for the study of complex causal networks.

This paper is organized as follows: in Section 2 we introduce the formal definition of spatiotemporal causal graphs and the associated temporal filtration; in Section 3 we define CERA and study its basic algebraic properties; in Section 4 we introduce the temporal bridge module and prove the central bridge detection theorem; in Section 5 we present illustrative examples in model problems; and finally, in Section 6 we discuss future perspectives, including extensions to noncommutative versions and applications to real epidemic networks.

2
Spatiotemporal Causal Graphs and Temporal Filtrations

In this section, we introduce the fundamental structure underlying the construction of the Causal Edge Rees Algebra (CERA): spatiotemporal causal graphs and the temporal filtration induced by their cumulative evolution. Temporal networks and dynamic graphs have been extensively studied in network science and complex systems
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
. Here, we consider a formulation driven by temporal causality, inspired by the framework introduced in
[
6
]
.

2.1
Spatiotemporal causal graphs

We consider graphs modeling systems in which relations between events or entities emerge progressively over time under causal constraints.

Definition 1

(Spatiotemporal causal graph)
.

A
spatiotemporal causal graph
is a triple

G=(V,E,\tau),

where:

-

V
is a finite set of vertices;

-

E\subseteq V\times V
is a set of directed edges;

-

\tau:V\to\mathbb{R}
is a temporal function assigning to each vertex a time of occurrence.

The graph satisfies the causal condition:

(u,v)\in E\;\Longrightarrow\;\tau(u)<\tau(v).

This definition is consistent with directed acyclic graph (DAG) structures used to model temporal and causal dependencies
[
15
,
16
]
. The condition implies that all edges respect temporal orientation, hence the graph is acyclic.

Proposition 1
.

Every spatiotemporal causal graph is a directed acyclic graph (DAG).

Proof.

Assume there exists a directed cycle

v_{1}\to v_{2}\to\cdots\to v_{k}\to v_{1}.

Then

\tau(v_{1})<\tau(v_{2})<\cdots<\tau(v_{k})<\tau(v_{1}),

a contradiction.
∎

2.2
Spatiotemporal causal constraints

In many applications, particularly in epidemiological and spatiotemporal propagation systems, the existence of a causal edge depends on both temporal and spatial constraints.

Let
d:V\times V\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be a distance function, and let
\Delta>0
,
\varepsilon>0
.

Definition 2

(Admissible causal edge)
.

An edge
(u,v)
is admissible if

\tau(u)<\tau(v),\qquad\tau(v)-\tau(u)\leq\Delta,\qquad d(u,v)\leq\varepsilon.

Thus,

E=\{(u,v)\in V\times V:\text{ the above conditions hold}\}.

2.3
Cumulative temporal filtration

Let

t_{1}<t_{2}<\cdots<t_{k}

be ordered time instants.

Definition 3

(Temporal filtration)
.

For
n\in\{1,\dots,k\}
, define

E_{n}=\{(u,v)\in E:\tau(v)\leq t_{n}\},

and

G_{n}=(V_{n},E_{n}),

where
V_{n}\subseteq V
consists of vertices incident to edges in
E_{n}
.

Then

E_{1}\subseteq E_{2}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq E_{k},\qquad G_{1}\subseteq G_{2}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq G_{k}.

This chain provides the combinatorial basis for the algebraic filtration defining CERA.

Remark 1
.

Each level of the filtration encodes all causal connections occurring up to the corresponding time.

2.4
Example: causal graph on a lattice

Let

M\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}

be a finite lattice. Assign to each point a time
\tau(v)
, and define edges via admissibility conditions.

In this setting:

-

vertices represent spatially localized events;

-

causality is determined by spatial proximity and temporal ordering;

-

the filtration describes progressive propagation over the lattice.

2.5
Algebraic objective of the filtration

From

G_{1}\subseteq G_{2}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq G_{k},

we construct a sequence of edge ideals

I_{1}\subseteq I_{2}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq I_{k},

whose associated Rees algebra defines CERA.

This transition from combinatorial to algebraic structure follows the classical theory of edge ideals and Rees algebras
[
10
,
7
,
9
]
, allowing the encoding of causal evolution into a graded algebraic framework.

3
The Causal Edge Rees Algebra (CERA)

In this section, we introduce the central algebraic construction of this work: the Causal Edge Rees Algebra (CERA), associated with the temporal filtration of a spatiotemporal causal graph. This structure encodes the cumulative evolution of the graph into a graded object, allowing the study of dynamical connectivity through algebraic invariants. The construction is inspired by both the classical theory of Rees algebras
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
and the theory of edge ideals associated with graphs
[
7
,
8
,
13
,
9
]
.

3.1
Temporal edge ideals

Let

G=(V,E,\tau)

be a spatiotemporal causal graph with temporal filtration

G_{1}\subseteq G_{2}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq G_{k},

induced by the increasing chain

E_{1}\subseteq E_{2}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq E_{k}.

Consider the commutative polynomial ring

R=\Bbbk[x_{v}:\;v\in V],

where
\Bbbk
is a field.

Definition 4

(Edge ideal at time
n
)
.

For each temporal level
n\in\{1,\dots,k\}
, define the edge ideal

I_{n}=\langle x_{u}x_{v}:\;(u,v)\in E_{n}\rangle\subseteq R.

Since the filtration is increasing, we have

I_{1}\subseteq I_{2}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq I_{k}.

We extend this filtration to all
n\geq 0
by setting

I_{0}=(0),\qquad I_{n}:=I_{k}\quad\text{for all }n\geq k.

Thus,
\{I_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is an increasing filtration of monomial ideals which stabilizes at level
k
.

Proposition 2
.

The sequence
\{I_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
forms an increasing filtration of monomial ideals.

Proof.

For
n<k
, the inclusion follows from
E_{n}\subseteq E_{n+1}
. For
n\geq k
, we have
I_{n}=I_{k}
, so the sequence stabilizes. Hence

I_{n}\subseteq I_{n+1}\quad\text{for all }n.

∎

3.2
Definition of CERA

We now define the graded algebra encoding the cumulative causal structure.

Definition 5

(CERA)
.

The
Causal Edge Rees Algebra
associated with the causal graph
G
is defined as

\mathrm{CERA}(G)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}I_{n}T^{n}\subseteq R[T],

where
T
is an indeterminate.

This construction is analogous to the classical Rees algebra associated with a filtration of ideals. The stabilization of the filtration ensures that
\mathrm{CERA}(G)
is a well-defined graded subalgebra of
R[T]
.

Each homogeneous component
I_{n}T^{n}
corresponds to the accumulated causal structure up to level
n
, and for
n\geq k
, these components coincide.

Remark 2
.

The variable
T
records the filtration degree, while the stabilization reflects the finite horizon of the underlying causal process.

3.3
Multiplicative structure

Proposition 3
.

\mathrm{CERA}(G)
is a graded subalgebra of
R[T]
.

Proof.

Let

fT^{a}\in I_{a}T^{a},\qquad gT^{b}\in I_{b}T^{b}.

Then

(fT^{a})(gT^{b})=fgT^{a+b}.

Since the filtration is increasing, we have
I_{a}\subseteq I_{a+b}
, hence
fg\in I_{a+b}
, and therefore

fgT^{a+b}\in I_{a+b}T^{a+b}.

∎

3.4
Associated graded ring

To analyze the incremental structure of the filtration, we introduce the associated graded ring.

Definition 6

(Associated graded ring)
.

Define

\mathrm{gr}_{\mathcal{I}}(R)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 1}I_{n}/I_{n-1},

with
I_{0}=(0)
.

This object captures the successive differences between layers of the filtration.

Remark 3
.

The quotient

I_{n}/I_{n-1}

represents the new generators appearing at level
n
. For
n>k
, these quotients are zero, reflecting the stabilization of the filtration.

3.5
Elementary example

Consider a causal graph with

E_{1}=\{(1,2),(3,4)\},

E_{2}=\{(1,2),(3,4),(2,3)\}.

Then

I_{1}=\langle x_{1}x_{2},\;x_{3}x_{4}\rangle,\qquad I_{2}=\langle x_{1}x_{2},\;x_{3}x_{4},\;x_{2}x_{3}\rangle.

Thus,

I_{2}/I_{1}

is generated by the class of
x_{2}x_{3}
, corresponding to the edge connecting the two components.

3.6
Structural perspective

The algebra
\mathrm{CERA}(G)
provides the algebraic core of the theory:

-

the ideals
I_{n}
encode cumulative causal structure;

-

the Rees algebra encodes global evolution;

-

the associated graded ring encodes incremental structure.

In the next section, we introduce temporal bridge modules, which algebraically detect the merging of connected components.

4
Temporal Bridge Modules and the Bridge Detection Theorem

In this section, we introduce the central concept connecting the algebraic structure of CERA with the evolving topology of causal graphs: temporal bridge modules. These modules algebraically identify the edges responsible for merging previously disconnected components.

4.1
Temporal bridges between components

Consider the increasing filtration

G_{1}\subseteq G_{2}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq G_{k},

and denote by
G_{n}^{*}
the underlying undirected graph.

Let

\beta_{0}(G_{n}^{*})

be the number of connected components of
G_{n}^{*}
.

Definition 7

(Temporal bridge)
.

An edge

e=(u,v)\in E_{n}\setminus E_{n-1}

is called a
temporal bridge
if its addition reduces the number of connected components, i.e.,

\beta_{0}(G_{n}^{*})<\beta_{0}(G_{n-1}^{*}).

Equivalently, a temporal bridge connects two distinct connected components of
G_{n-1}^{*}
.

4.2
Temporal bridge module

The quotient

I_{n}/I_{n-1}

contains all new edges introduced at level
n
. We isolate those responsible for topological merging.

Definition 8

(Temporal bridge module)
.

Define
B_{n}\subseteq I_{n}/I_{n-1}
as the submodule generated by the classes

x_{u}x_{v}+I_{n-1}

such that
(u,v)
is a temporal bridge.

Proposition 4

(Structure of
B_{n}
)
.

Let
\mathcal{B}_{n}
be the set of temporal bridges at level
n
. Then

B_{n}\cong\bigoplus_{e\in\mathcal{B}_{n}}\Bbbk\cdot[m_{e}],

where
m_{e}=x_{u}x_{v}
and
[m_{e}]
denotes its class in
I_{n}/I_{n-1}
.

Proof.

Each temporal bridge corresponds to a distinct monomial
x_{u}x_{v}
. Since distinct monomials are linearly independent in a polynomial ring over a field, their classes remain linearly independent in the quotient
I_{n}/I_{n-1}
. The result follows.
∎

Remark 4
.

Thus,
B_{n}
is a finite-dimensional
\Bbbk
-vector space whose basis is naturally indexed by temporal bridges.

4.3
Bridge detection theorem

We now state the main structural result.

Theorem 1

(Bridge detection theorem)
.

Let
G=(V,E,\tau)
be a spatiotemporal causal graph with temporal filtration

G_{1}\subseteq G_{2}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq G_{k}.

Then, for each
n\geq 1
,

\dim_{\Bbbk}(B_{n})=\beta_{0}(G_{n-1}^{*})-\beta_{0}(G_{n}^{*}).

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{B}_{n}
be the set of temporal bridges at level
n
. Each bridge connects two distinct connected components of
G_{n-1}^{*}
, reducing the number of connected components by one.

Let
r_{n}=|\mathcal{B}_{n}|
. Then

\beta_{0}(G_{n}^{*})=\beta_{0}(G_{n-1}^{*})-r_{n}.

By the previous proposition,

\dim_{\Bbbk}(B_{n})=r_{n}.

Combining the two equalities yields the result.
∎

4.4
Interpretation

The theorem establishes a correspondence between:

-

a topological phenomenon: merging of connected components;

-

an algebraic invariant: the dimension of
B_{n}
.

Thus, the modules
B_{n}
detect structural transitions in the causal connectivity of the graph.

4.5
Example

Consider

E_{1}=\{(1,2),(3,4)\},\qquad E_{2}=\{(1,2),(3,4),(2,3)\}.

Then

\beta_{0}(G_{1}^{*})=2,\qquad\beta_{0}(G_{2}^{*})=1.

The edge
(2,3)
is a temporal bridge, and

B_{2}=\langle[x_{2}x_{3}]\rangle,\qquad\dim_{\Bbbk}(B_{2})=1.

4.6
Bridge polynomial

The sequence
\{B_{n}\}
defines a generating function.

Definition 9

(Bridge polynomial)
.

Define

P_{G}(t)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\dim_{\Bbbk}(B_{n})t^{n}.

4.7
Consequences

Corollary 1
.

If
B_{n}=0
, then no merging of connected components occurs at level
n
.

Corollary 2
.

If
\dim_{\Bbbk}(B_{n})=m
, then exactly
m
independent merging events occur at level
n
.

4.8
Structural role

The modules
B_{n}
play a central role in the theory:

-

they detect topological transitions;

-

they identify critical edges;

-

they encode connectivity changes algebraically.

They provide a direct link between the topology of causal connectivity and the graded algebraic structure of CERA.

4.9
Decomposition of incremental structure

We formalize the classification of edge behavior at each level of the filtration.

Let
E_{n}\setminus E_{n-1}
denote the set of edges introduced at level
n
, and consider the quotient

I_{n}/I_{n-1}.

Each edge
e=(u,v)\in E_{n}\setminus E_{n-1}
gives rise to a monomial
m_{e}=x_{u}x_{v}
, whose class lies in
I_{n}/I_{n-1}
.

Definition 10

(Edge type decomposition)
.

Partition the set
E_{n}\setminus E_{n-1}
into three disjoint subsets:

•

\mathcal{B}_{n}
: temporal bridges (edges connecting distinct components of
G_{n-1}^{*}
);

•

\mathcal{C}_{n}
: cycle edges (edges creating cycles within a connected component);

•

\mathcal{R}_{n}
: remaining edges (edges that do not affect the topological structure).

Definition 11

(Cycle module)
.

Define

C_{n}\subseteq I_{n}/I_{n-1}

as the submodule generated by the classes of monomials corresponding to edges in
\mathcal{C}_{n}
.

Definition 12

(Residual module)
.

Define

R_{n}\subseteq I_{n}/I_{n-1}

as the submodule generated by the classes corresponding to edges in
\mathcal{R}_{n}
.

Proposition 5

(Direct sum decomposition)
.

There is a direct sum decomposition of
\Bbbk
-vector spaces:

I_{n}/I_{n-1}\;\cong\;B_{n}\oplus C_{n}\oplus R_{n}.

Proof.

Each edge
e\in E_{n}\setminus E_{n-1}
corresponds to a distinct monomial
m_{e}=x_{u}x_{v}
. Since distinct monomials are linearly independent over
\Bbbk
, their classes in
I_{n}/I_{n-1}
form a basis indexed by
E_{n}\setminus E_{n-1}
.

The partition

E_{n}\setminus E_{n-1}=\mathcal{B}_{n}\sqcup\mathcal{C}_{n}\sqcup\mathcal{R}_{n}

induces a decomposition of this basis, yielding the direct sum decomposition.
∎

Corollary 3
.

We have

\dim_{\Bbbk}(I_{n}/I_{n-1})=\dim_{\Bbbk}(B_{n})+\dim_{\Bbbk}(C_{n})+\dim_{\Bbbk}(R_{n}).

5.4
Structural classification of new edges

The previous examples suggest a natural classification of edges added at level

Proposition 6
.

Let

•

;

2.

Cycle formation
: edges that create cycles within a connected component, without affecting
\beta_{0}
;

3.

Internal expansion
: edges that lie within a component and do not modify its global topological structure.

This classification separates topological transitions from purely internal structural changes.

5.7
Role of CERA

The algebra
\mathrm{CERA}(G)
encodes the full evolution of connectivity along the filtration, while the associated graded components

I_{n}/I_{n-1}

record all newly introduced edges at each level.

Within this structure, the modules
B_{n}
isolate precisely those elements corresponding to merging events. In particular,

\dim_{\Bbbk}(B_{n})=\beta_{0}(G_{n-1}^{*})-\beta_{0}(G_{n}^{*}),

so that
B_{n}
provides an algebraic measure of the reduction in the number of connected components.

Thus, CERA distinguishes between:

•

edges that alter global connectivity (detected by
B_{n}
);

•

edges that contribute only to internal structure (detected in
I_{n}/I_{n-1}
but not in
B_{n}
).

5.8
Outlook

The examples suggest that CERA provides a natural framework for distinguishing different regimes of structural evolution in filtered graphs. In particular, the decomposition of
I_{n}/I_{n-1}
according to edge behavior indicates the possibility of refining the theory to capture additional topological features, such as cycle formation.

Extensions to more general settings, including stochastic models and large-scale systems, will be investigated in future work.

7
Functoriality of CERA and Temporal Collapse

In this section, we show that the construction of the Causal Edge Rees Algebra (CERA) is naturally functorial. Moreover, we establish the existence of a natural transformation connecting this dynamic structure to the classical theory of edge ideals of static graphs. This relationship formalizes the interpretation of CERA as a temporal refinement of classical algebraic graph structures in the sense of category theory
[
27
,
28
]
.

7.1
Underlying categories

Let
\mathbf{CGraph}_{f}
denote the category whose objects are filtered spatiotemporal causal graphs

G=(V,E,\tau,\{E_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}),

and whose morphisms

\varphi:G\to G^{\prime}

are vertex maps preserving edges and the filtration, i.e.,

(u,v)\in E_{n}\Longrightarrow(\varphi(u),\varphi(v))\in E_{n}^{\prime}\quad\text{for all }n.

Let
\mathbf{GrAlg}_{\Bbbk}
denote the category of graded
\Bbbk
-algebras with graded homomorphisms
[
12
]
.

7.2
Functoriality of the construction

Proposition 12
.

The assignment

G\longmapsto\mathrm{CERA}(G)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}I_{n}T^{n}

defines a covariant functor

\mathcal{F}:\mathbf{CGraph}_{f}\longrightarrow\mathbf{GrAlg}_{\Bbbk}.

Proof.

For each object
G
, define
\mathcal{F}(G)=\mathrm{CERA}(G)
.

Given a morphism
\varphi:G\to G^{\prime}
, define

\mathcal{F}(\varphi)(x_{v})=x_{\varphi(v)},\qquad\mathcal{F}(\varphi)(T)=T,

and extend multiplicatively and linearly.

Since
\varphi
preserves the filtration,

(u,v)\in E_{n}\Longrightarrow(\varphi(u),\varphi(v))\in E_{n}^{\prime},

hence

x_{\varphi(u)}x_{\varphi(v)}\in I_{n}^{\prime},

which implies

\mathcal{F}(\varphi)(I_{n}T^{n})\subseteq I_{n}^{\prime}T^{n}.

Thus
\mathcal{F}(\varphi)
is a graded algebra homomorphism. Functoriality (preservation of identities and composition) follows immediately.
∎

7.3
The forgetful functor

Consider the forgetful functor

U:\mathbf{CGraph}_{f}\longrightarrow\mathbf{Graph},

which associates to each causal graph
G=(V,E,\tau)
its underlying graph

U(G)=(V,E),

obtained by forgetting the temporal structure.

Associated to this, define the functor

\mathcal{G}:\mathbf{CGraph}_{f}\longrightarrow\mathbf{Alg}_{\Bbbk},

given by

\mathcal{G}(G)=I(G)=\langle x_{u}x_{v}:(u,v)\in E\rangle,

the classical edge ideal of the underlying graph.

7.4
Natural transformation: temporal collapse

Proposition 13
.

Evaluation at
T=1
defines a natural transformation

\eta:\mathcal{F}\Rightarrow\mathcal{G},

that is, for each causal graph
G
, the map

\eta_{G}:\mathrm{CERA}(G)\to I(G),\quad\eta_{G}\Bigl(\sum f_{n}T^{n}\Bigr)=\sum f_{n},

is compatible with morphisms in
\mathbf{CGraph}_{f}
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi:G\to G^{\prime}
be a morphism. For
x_{v}
, we have

\eta_{G^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F}(\varphi)(x_{v}))=\eta_{G^{\prime}}(x_{\varphi(v)})=x_{\varphi(v)},

while

\mathcal{G}(\varphi)(\eta_{G}(x_{v}))=\mathcal{G}(\varphi)(x_{v})=x_{\varphi(v)}.

Since both maps are algebra homomorphisms, it follows that

\eta_{G^{\prime}}\circ\mathcal{F}(\varphi)=\mathcal{G}(\varphi)\circ\eta_{G},

and therefore
\eta
is a natural transformation.
∎

7.5
Structural interpretation

The previous result shows that CERA provides a functorial refinement of the classical edge ideal.

-

\mathrm{CERA}(G)
encodes the full causal evolution of the graph;

-

the transformation
\eta
collapses the temporal structure;

-

I(G)
recovers the aggregated connectivity.

In particular,

\mathrm{CERA}(G)\quad\text{is a functorial refinement of}\quad I(G).

Remark 6
.

The transformation
\eta
can be interpreted as a temporal collapse operator: it preserves the underlying combinatorial structure while forgetting the causal organization encoded in the grading.

8
Bigraded Structure: Time and Simplicial Dimension

In this section, we introduce a natural refinement of the CERA construction, combining the temporal evolution of a causal graph with the combinatorial complexity of associated simplicial structures. This leads to a naturally
bigraded
algebra, allowing simultaneous analysis of temporal dynamics and interaction complexity.

8.1
Associated simplicial filtration

Let
G=(V,E,\tau)
be a spatiotemporal causal graph with filtration

G_{1}\subseteq G_{2}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq G_{k},

induced by

E_{1}\subseteq E_{2}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq E_{k}.

Let
G_{n}^{*}
denote the underlying undirected graph of
G_{n}
. To each level we associate a simplicial complex
\Delta_{n}
. A natural choice is the
clique complex
:

\Delta_{n}=\mathrm{Cl}(G_{n}^{*}),

whose simplices correspond to vertex subsets forming cliques in
G_{n}^{*}
.

By construction,

\Delta_{1}\subseteq\Delta_{2}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\Delta_{k},

yielding an increasing filtration of simplicial complexes.

8.2
Stanley–Reisner ideals and Rees construction

For each
n
, let

I_{\Delta_{n}}\subseteq R=\Bbbk[x_{v}:v\in V]

be the Stanley–Reisner ideal associated to
\Delta_{n}
, generated by monomials corresponding to minimal non-faces of
\Delta_{n}
.

Define the
simplicial CERA
by

\mathrm{CERA}_{\mathrm{SR}}(G)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}I_{\Delta_{n}}T^{n}\subseteq R[T].

8.3
Bigraded structure

The algebra
\mathrm{CERA}_{\mathrm{SR}}(G)
carries a natural bigrading:

\mathrm{CERA}_{\mathrm{SR}}(G)=\bigoplus_{n,d\geq 0}\left(I_{\Delta_{n}}\right)_{d}\,T^{n},

where
\left(I_{\Delta_{n}}\right)_{d}
denotes the degree-
d
homogeneous component of
I_{\Delta_{n}}
.

Proposition 14
.

The above decomposition defines a bigraded algebra structure on
\mathrm{CERA}_{\mathrm{SR}}(G)
, i.e., if

f\in\left(I_{\Delta_{n}}\right)_{d}T^{n},\quad g\in\left(I_{\Delta_{m}}\right)_{e}T^{m},

then

fg\in\left(I_{\Delta_{\max(n,m)}}\right)_{d+e}T^{n+m}.

Proof.

Since the filtration is increasing, we have

I_{\Delta_{n}}\subseteq I_{\Delta_{\max(n,m)}},\quad I_{\Delta_{m}}\subseteq I_{\Delta_{\max(n,m)}}.

Thus,
fg\in I_{\Delta_{\max(n,m)}}
.

The polynomial degree is additive, so
\deg(fg)=d+e
, and the temporal degree satisfies
T^{n}T^{m}=T^{n+m}
. The result follows.
∎

8.4
Structural interpretation

The bigrading separates two fundamental aspects of the theory:

•

the temporal degree
n
, encoding causal evolution;

•

the polynomial degree
d
, reflecting combinatorial complexity of simplicial obstructions.

In particular:

•

in the edge-ideal case, all generators have degree
2
, capturing only pairwise interactions;

•

in the Stanley–Reisner setting, generators may have higher degrees, encoding higher-order interactions.

Thus,
\mathrm{CERA}_{\mathrm{SR}}(G)
provides a multiscale description of the evolving structure, combining temporal and combinatorial dimensions.

8.5
Bigraded Hilbert function

The bigraded structure allows the definition of refined invariants.

Definition 13
.

The
bigraded Hilbert function
of
\mathrm{CERA}_{\mathrm{SR}}(G)
is

H(n,d)=\dim_{\Bbbk}\left(I_{\Delta_{n}}\right)_{d},\quad n,d\geq 0.

This function simultaneously measures:

•

the temporal evolution of the system;

•

the combinatorial complexity of missing simplices.

Remark 7
.

The function
H(n,d)
can be interpreted as a dynamic invariant tracking the emergence of higher-order combinatorial structures over time. In particular, it suggests a connection between CERA and multiscale methods such as persistent homology.

9
Discussion and Conclusions

In this work, we introduced the Causal Edge Rees Algebra (CERA), a graded algebraic structure associated with the temporal evolution of causal graphs. The construction assigns to a filtered causal graph a graded algebra that encodes the cumulative evolution of connectivity within a single algebraic object.

The central idea of the theory is to reinterpret temporal causality within the framework of Rees algebras, which are classically used in commutative algebra and algebraic geometry to study blow-ups, asymptotic behavior of ideals, and graded structures
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
. By transporting these tools to the setting of causal graphs, we obtain an algebraic formalism capable of encoding dynamic phenomena such as the emergence of edges and the merging of connected components.

A key structural result is the bridge detection theorem, which establishes a correspondence between the reduction in the number of connected components and the dimension of the temporal bridge module
B_{n}
. This provides an explicit link between a topological invariant and an algebraic structure arising from the filtration.

9.1
Mathematical significance

The construction of CERA naturally connects three levels of description:

1.

Combinatorial:
the filtered structure of the underlying graph;

2.

Algebraic:
the associated filtration of edge ideals;

3.

Geometric:
the projective scheme

\mathrm{Proj}(\mathrm{CERA}(G)).

This suggests that CERA may serve as a bridge between combinatorial commutative algebra and geometric methods associated with graded algebras
[
18
]
.

9.2
Limitations

The theory developed here is preliminary, and several structural questions remain open:

•

characterization of normality;

•

Cohen–Macaulay properties;

•

description of singularities of
\mathrm{Proj}(\mathrm{CERA}(G))
;

•

asymptotic behavior of the ideals
I_{n}
;

•

local structure via localization.

These questions are natural in the broader context of Rees algebras and monomial ideals
[
10
,
21
,
11
]
.

We also note that the present formulation is entirely commutative. A noncommutative version, better suited to directed causal structures, remains to be developed.

9.3
Future directions

Several directions for further investigation arise naturally:

•

noncommutative extensions of CERA;

•

local and homological properties of
\mathrm{CERA}(G)
;

•

extensions to hypergraphs;

•

connections with persistent homology;

•

interpretation in terms of quiver representations.

These directions connect the theory to areas such as representation theory, combinatorial topology, and associative algebras
[
19
,
20
,
17
]
.

From an applied perspective, the framework may be relevant for the analysis of temporal networks, including epidemic spreading, mobility systems, and information propagation
[
1
,

,

.

9.4
Concluding remarks

The results presented here indicate that temporal structures in graphs can be studied within an algebraic framework based on filtered Rees algebras. In this sense, CERA extends the classical theory of edge ideals to a temporal setting, providing a mechanism to encode both connectivity and its evolution.

The present work should be viewed as a first step toward a broader theory of algebraic structures associated with causal networks.
