Executive Summary
In this project, Mojave and SRI have demonstrated field validation and load shifting of liquid desiccant air
conditioning systems with two liquid desiccant dedicated outdoor air system (DOAS) air conditioner units
– one employing traditional thermal desiccant regeneration and the other utilizing Mojave’s novel
electrochemical desiccant regeneration technology based on Redox Assisted electrodialysis. The Redox
Assisted Dehumidification Air Conditioning (RAD-AC) technology is further developed and validated in this
work to demonstrate an Integrated Seasonal Moisture Removal Efficiency (ISMRE) > 5.0 kg/kWh and
shifting of ≥ 10% electric load in ≤ 72 hours. Furthermore, the longevity and durability of Mojave’s liquid
desiccant air conditioning technology is validated in field testing of the thermally regenerated unit, with
the unit demonstrating operation with >90% uptime while experiencing an efficiency degradation of <
10% during a testing campaign of over 5400 hours.

One major outcome of the project was the development of a fully packaged thermally regenerated field
test prototype. This unit operates at a 1000 CFM capacity with a cooling capacity of over 7 tons.
Acceptance testing of the unit demonstrated a measured ISMRE of 3.8 kg/kWh, which is 65% higher than
that of the industry standard. Following acceptance testing, in April of 2023, the unit was installed in the
field at an office building in Tampa, FL. Between April and December of 2023, the unit operated for 5400
hours with an uptime of 95.5%. During this time, the unit experienced a wide range of inlet ambient
temperature and humidity conditions and demonstrated a mean moisture removal efficiency (MRE)
within 3% of predictions based on an analytical model matched to its acceptance testing performance,
indicating minimal degradation in performance over this time. Therefore, the extended operational
period and low rate of degradation demonstrate the robustness of Mojave’s liquid desiccant air
conditioning technology. During the project, Mojave released their first thermally regenerated liquid
desiccant DOAS for commercial purchase. Mojave’s ArctiDry® DOAS line sales started in January 2024.
Since then we have engaged with over 30 Manufacturer’s Representative partners to begin transforming
the marketplace.

Another major outcome of the project was the development of a packaged electrochemically
regenerated DOAS prototype showing high efficiency. The electrochemical unit operates at a 300 CFM
capacity with a cooling capacity of over 2 tons. Two major improvements in system efficiency were
achieved by transitioning the liquid desiccant absorber-regenerator system from a single-stage to a multi-
stage system as well as using a water chilled cooling system in lieu of a vapor compression system to
provide pre-cooling for the airstream. With these changes, the ISMRE of the electrochemically
regenerated liquid desiccant air conditioner reached a maximum of 5.01 kg/kWh. One key benefit of
using electrochemical regeneration is the ability to decouple dehumidification and regeneration of the
liquid desiccant, allowing for shifting of portions of the overall electrical load, resulting in improved grid
stability and reduced cost of electricity by shifting regeneration energy consumption to off-peak periods.
Development of a two-sump load shifting scheme allowed for demonstration of shifting of 11% of the
electric load of the unit over a 12-hour period without any energy penalty, thus maintaining the observed
ISMRE of the steady-state condition without load shifting. A techno-economic analysis supported by

Mojave’s thermally regenerated Arctidry® cost and product specifications indicate that a 2-3 year simple
payback period is possible due to the energy efficiency demonstrated by the electrochemically
regenerated DOAS technology.

Thermally Regenerated Unit
A thermally regenerated liquid desiccant dedicated outdoor air system (DOAS) air conditioning prototype
unit was built for field testing validation and durability demonstration. The unit was designed to operate
at 1000 CFM with a cooling capacity of over 7 tons. The thermal unit utilizes vapor compression based
cooling and liquid desiccant dehumidification in order to cool and dehumidify outdoor air to desired
supply conditions.

Acceptance Testing
The thermal unit was acceptance tested at PARC to evaluate beginning of life performance. Acceptance
testing was conducted over a rigorous 100-hour test involving various weather conditions with the
prototype demonstrating 100% operating uptime. The weather conditions tested encompassed standard
AHRI 920 weather condition test points A, B, C, and D such that an Integrated Seasonal Moisture Removal
Efficiency (ISMRE) could be evaluated as a representative unit efficiency metric. The dry bulb and wet
bulb temperatures as well as the measured moisture removal efficiency (MRE) for each test point are
shown in Table 2.

Electrochemically Regenerated Unit
Testing

 Figure 3. A) Electrochemical regenerator test platform. B) Screen capture of the electrochemical
 regenerator test platform control software.
A demonstration electrochemical regenerator was built and tested as shown in Figure 3. The unit
consisted of four stacks each containing 25 cell pairs of commercial anion and cation exchange

 7

membranes. The membranes employed are frequently used in chlor-alkali, electrodialysis, fuel
cell/electrolyzer, and other electrochemical processes. An additional low cell pair count stack was added
as a finishing step to minimize any residual desiccant concentration in the wastewater stream. Each cell
pair had an active area of 200 cm2.

The demonstration unit was tested over a range of desiccant concentrations and moisture removal rates.
Inlet concentrations to the regenerator were varied from 10 to 20 weight percent (wt%) of lithium
chloride (LiCl). Flow rates in and out of the regenerator were controlled to maintain a moisture removal
rate of 2 to 10 mL/min.

Modeling
The test data from the demonstration regenerator unit were combined with system models and test data
from Mojave’s thermally regenerated units to determine the expected ISMRE of a fully integrated
electrochemically regenerated liquid desiccant unit. System modeling indicated that utilizing multiple
absorber-regenerator stages would significantly reduce power consumption. The stages were configured
such that air flowed through the absorber stages in series, with each stage increasing in desiccant
concentration, thus decreasing the humidity of the airstream. Each absorber stage was then equipped
with its own electrochemical regenerator. This allowed for the air stream to be dehumidified in steps
such that a smaller portion of the total moisture removed would occur at the highest desiccant
concentration, which would require the highest power consumption for regeneration. Analysis showed
that utilization of a three-stage absorber could reduce regenerator power consumption by a factor of up
to four, however, utilization of an increased number of stages would lead to diminishing gains. System
modeling also allowed for optimization of the desiccant concentrations of each stage in order to deliver
desired supply air temperatures and humidities.

Similar to the thermal unit, the proposed electrochemically regenerated liquid desiccant air conditioning
unit utilizes a cooling unit as well as a liquid desiccant dehumidifier. System modeling allowed for further
optimization of the design of the unit by investigating the split of moisture removal by condensation in
the cooling unit and dehumidification in the absorber. This split was adjusted by varying the supply air
temperature as determined by the cooling unit. For example, at cooler supply air temperatures, a larger
portion of the moisture removal occurs by condensation, and thus the pre-cooling system handles a
larger load than the regenerator. Therefore, optimization of the supply temperature accounts for the
differing efficiencies of the pre-cooling system and liquid desiccant system. The cooling unit was modeled
as both a standard vapor compression unit and a water chilled unit. The addition of an evaporatively
cooled condenser (ECC) and/or energy recovery ventilator (ERV) was also studied.

As shown in Figure 4, the analysis indicated that the ISMRE of the unit was expected to be between 4.2
and 5.2 kg/kWh depending on the configuration of the system and whether evaporative compressor
cooling and energy recovery were integrated into the system. Of the options considered, utilization of a
commercial chiller with a high efficiency magnetic bearing rotary/screw compressor provided the most

 8

benefit to the electrochemically regenerated liquid desiccant system. As such, this configuration was
selected for prototype development.

Construction
A chilled water air handling unit was utilized to construct the electrochemical regenerator prototype. The
unit is rated up to 1000 CFM, but was expected to operate at 300 CFM accounting for the additional
pressure drop from the absorber. Sensors and controls were added to the unit to allow the unit to act as
a liquid desiccant DOAS, including temperature and humidity sensors along the air stream and desiccant
concentration sensors in the absorber sumps.

A multistage absorber comprising 3 media bed stages was designed and constructed. As mentioned, each
stage is designed to operate at different desiccant concentrations. The absorber is designed to allow
operation with a small minimum amount of desiccant for quick and responsive testing.

To accompany each absorber stage, three regenerator stages were also built. Each regenerator consists

concentration of desiccant in and out of the regenerator was measured to monitor performance. Power
supply was provided by potentiostat in order to provide fine voltage control.

Acceptance Testing
Acceptance testing of the unit occurred in two phases. First, the performance of the absorber subsystem
was tested over an 8-hour period. The effectiveness of the absorber, a measure of how closely the
absorber approaches the theoretical maximum moisture removal capacity for a given desiccant, is
measured to be about 94%. This indicates the high performance of the multistage absorber and its ability
to maximize the dehumidification capacity of the liquid desiccant.

 Figure 8. Measured effectiveness of the three-stage absorber.

Finally, the completed prototype was tested ensuring that the absorbers and regenerators can effectively
dehumidify the inlet supply air. Over an 8-hour continuous test, the regenerators displayed stable
concentrations, indicating the regenerator was capable of maintaining steady state desiccant conditions
for dehumidification (Figure 9A). This was accomplished by matching the rate of moisture removed from
the desiccant by the electrochemical regenerator system to the rate of moisture removed from the inlet
air stream into the desiccant by the absorber (Figure 9B). The moisture removal rates of the regenerators
were calculated from concentration and mass flow sensors in the inlets and outlets of the regenerator.
Moisture removal rates of the absorbers were determined by the measured effectiveness of the
absorbers and desiccant concentration of the sumps.

Load Shifting
Electrochemical regeneration of liquid desiccants enables load shifting by temporally decoupling the
absorption of water from air by the liquid desiccant and regeneration of the liquid desiccant. However,
the percentage of load shifting that can be accomplished is limited because the cooling unit’s power
cannot be load shifted, as it must be running as long as dehumidification is desired. The cooling unit’s
power consumption dominates the total power consumption, especially at AHRI points A and B where
temperature and humidity are the highest. Therefore, the amount of available load shifting is dependent
on the system configuration and varies between 10 to 20% for the highest system efficiency conditions.

 Figure 14. Power consumption distribution of the electrochemically regenerated prototype unit at a 54.5
 degrees Fahrenheit dew point delivery.

Figure 14 displays the distribution of power consumption by the electrochemically regenerated prototype
at a 54.5 degrees Fahrenheit dew point delivery, where the regenerator consumes approximately 11% of
the overall system power. As previously mentioned, power associated with the air handling and cooling
system, such as the compressor of the chiller system, supply air fan, and desiccant pump, cannot be
shifted. Nonetheless, shifting of the electrical load can be very significant to control electrical grid
infrastructure when air conditioning loads are the highest.

 Figure 15. Diagram illustrating the arrangement of two liquid desiccant tanks designed for enabling load
  shifting functionality in the liquid desiccant air dehumidification system. Tank 1 indicates the absorber
  desiccant sump and Tank 2 indicates the secondary desiccant reservoir. The concentration of Tank 2 is

 absorber, and the electrochemical regenerator. Dashed lines indicate inactive flow between subsystems.

Load shifting is achieved by utilizing a secondary desiccant reservoir for each stage that is separate from
the primary absorber sump. This desiccant sump contains concentrated desiccant that is produced by the
regenerator. As shown in Figure 15, operation can be separated into three modes: integrated operation,
dehumidification, and regeneration. Integrated operation is defined as operating periods when both the
air handling system and regenerator are active and are running in sync and is the operating mode
demonstrated in the previous section. The air handler passes air through the absorber, dehumidifying the
air, and the regenerator removes moisture from the desiccant to maintain steady state conditions.
Concentrated desiccant produced by the regenerator passes through the secondary sump and returns to
the absorber. During dehumidification, the regenerator is turned off but the air handler maintains
operation. Dehumidification in the absorber reduces desiccant concentration, but steady state operation
is maintained by dosing in concentrated desiccant from the secondary reservoir. Finally, regeneration
mode concentrates desiccant from the primary absorber sump and stores it in the secondary desiccant
reservoir in order to provide concentrated desiccant that can be used to maintain desiccating power
during dehumidification mode. The air handling system is turned off during regeneration. The decoupling

 16

of integrated operation into separate dehumidification and regeneration operating modes enables
shifting of the load associated with regeneration.

 (dehumidification), D) Moisture removal rate in the absorber (regeneration), and E) desiccant
 concentration during load shift testing in integrated operation mode (blue), dehumidification mode

Figure 16 displays a representative test of the electrochemically regenerated prototype in the various
load shifting modes, where blue represents integrated operation, purple represents dehumidification,
and green represents regeneration. The electrochemically regenerated prototype easily transitions
between the different operation modes, allowing the unit to readily change operating modes to ease
strain on the grid and take advantage of electricity pricing structures. The duration of load shifting that is
achievable is determined by the size of the secondary desiccant sump used to maintain desiccating power
during dehumidification mode. As such, the possible duration of load shifting is easily increased to 12
hours or beyond by increasing the size of the secondary sump.

TEA

advantages, including high efficiency, possible indoor operation, and load shifting. Undoubtedly, one of

Final Analysis and Summary
In this project, we successfully built two liquid desiccant air conditioning prototype systems to satisfy all
objectives of the project. First, a 1000 CFM thermally regenerated unit was field tested in Tampa, Florida
for over 5400 hours. During this time, the unit demonstrated a runtime of over 95% and demonstrated
less than 5% degradation in benchmarked efficiency, illustrating the robustness and durability of liquid
desiccant air conditioning systems. Overall, this unit demonstrated an ISMRE of 3.8 kg/kWh, which is 65%
higher than the industry standard DOAS unit.

Secondly, an electrochemically regenerated prototype unit was designed, built, and tested. The
electrochemical regenerator was designed from system modeling grounded in experimental results from
a demonstration electrochemical regenerator. In order to achieve high efficiency, a multi-stage concept
was utilized in order to shift regeneration of desiccant to lower concentrations. In this configuration,
multiple desiccant concentrations are utilized to dehumidify air streams in steps, allowing for a large
portion of desiccant regeneration to occur at lower concentrations. Ultimately, this reduces the power
consumption for an electrochemical regenerator, which increases with concentration handled.
Furthermore, the balance of work handled by the liquid desiccant absorber and pre-cooling system was
optimized considering different system configurations. Ultimately it was found that integration of a high-
efficiency commercial chiller cooling system to provide pre-cooling of the air stream prior to
dehumidification by the liquid desiccant would present the most benefit to the electrochemical
regenerator, enabling an ISMRE of above 5 kg/kWh.

A 300 CFM electrochemically regenerated prototype was constructed in this configuration, including
integration of a water chilled cooling unit, three-stage absorber, and three regenerator stages. Testing at
various supply air conditions showed that an ISMRE of 5 kg/kWh can be demonstrated delivery dew
points ranging from 49 to 53 degrees Fahrenheit. The demonstrated ISMRE is 120% higher than that of
the industry standard DOAS. This improved energy efficiency represents state-of-the-art performance
and drastically reduces energy consumption of HVAC systems.

Electrochemical regeneration also allows for decoupling of liquid desiccant dehumidification and
regeneration, allowing for the electrical load associated with desiccant regeneration to be shifted to a
different time period. This allows for decreased strain on the grid during peak demand times and
provides cost-saving incentives for potential customers. Our prototype unit demonstrated load shifting of
11% of the unit’s overall power consumption at a delivery dew point of 54.5 degrees Fahrenheit.

A techno-economic analysis reveals that, as a result of its high operating efficiency, the electrochemically
regenerated liquid desiccant DOAS system has a simple payback period of 2-3 years. The analysis
considered projected costs of an electrochemical regenerator system as well as the cost of air-side
components based on Mojave’s ArctiDry® DOAS.
