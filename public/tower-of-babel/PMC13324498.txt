Bio-based trifunctional integration strategies for aquatic products during frozen storage: Synergistic mechanisms and perspectives
Authors: Yuqi Ma, Zhenshun Li, Ruipeng Chen, Xiaopeng Hu, Xiangna Yin, Ruicong Wang, Dan Ouyang, Akang Dan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractFrozen storage is essential for aquatic logistics, but temperature fluctuations during distribution cause repeated freeze-thaw cycles, leading to ice recrystallization and lipid-protein co-oxidation. This review explains how these cycles damage aquatic products and provides an overview of emerging bio-based cryoprotectants. We propose a proactive trifunctional integration strategy shifting from passive protection to deep molecular functional integration. The core concept is a synergistic feedback loop targeting a coupled deterioration network defined by three kinetic variables: ice growth rate (Vice), protein conformational change rate (Vconf), and oxidative reaction rate (Vox). Cascading intervention was achieved through ice-binding crystal regulation, water replacement driven conformational stabilization, and radical scavenging biochemical interception. Finally, by addressing industrial challenges such as sensory degradation, this review envisioned a shift toward intelligent, low-dosage, high-efficiency composite systems. This work contributes to the theoretical groundwork for a sustainable aquatic cryopreservation framework, aiming to balance environmental responsibility with quality control.Keywords: Aquatic products, Bio-based cryoprotectants, Trifunctional integration strategy, Frozen storage, Antifreeze peptides

Introduction

Freezing storage is one of the most common preservation methods in the industrial sector for maintaining the shelf life of high-value aquatic resources (Mao et al., 2025a). During freezing, water crystallization significantly decreases water activity in the system, effectively inhibiting microbial growth and endogenous enzyme activity, thus preventing spoilage and extending product shelf life. Although freezing technology offers great logistical convenience, it is not a thermodynamically static state (X. Feng et al., 2023). In practice, improper temperature control can lead to frequent freeze-thaw (F-T) cycles, causing water within tissues to enter a supercooled state and form ice nuclei (Jia, Chen, et al., 2022). Temperature fluctuations cause ice crystals to grow along these nuclei, forming large, irregular recrystallized structures. These structures generate mechanical stress, which can damage cell membranes and disrupt the structural integrity of myofibrils (Huo et al., 2024). Additionally, interactions among cell dehydration, solute concentration, and pH shifts can trigger entropy-driven conformational unfolding of proteins, leading to irreversible denaturation and aggregation (Cheng et al., 2024). Simultaneously, the interconnected oxidation of lipids and proteins creates a complex cascade of damage, severely impairing the sensory quality, and nutritional integrity of aquatic products (Ying et al., 2025a). Therefore, incorporating cryoprotectants to synergistically inhibit ice crystal formation, protein denaturation, and lipid oxidation has become a key strategy to ensure consistent frozen quality amid multidimensional damage, ranging from microstructural disruption to molecular biochemical deterioration in aquatic products.

To date, numerous studies have reported the remarkable efficacy of various cryoprotectants in improving the quality of aquatic products and the corresponding cryoprotective mechanisms, providing an important theoretical basis for aquatic product preservation (Fan et al., 2024). The key to this protective mechanism lies in alleviating F-T stress by enhancing the stability of biological macromolecules. Specifically, cryoprotectants not only have the ability of ice recrystallization inhibition (IRI) to reduce physical stress but also anchor the conformation of myofibrillar proteins (MP) through intermolecular interactions (Liu, Sun, & Tian, 2025). This synergistic effect from physical shielding to molecular stability maintains the excellent sensory quality and processing characteristics of aquatic products at the root. Although existing traditional antifreeze systems can alleviate these problems to some extent, the high sugar load sensory deviation, high calorie health risks, and non-clean label characteristics of traditional sugar and phosphate systems make them increasingly difficult to meet the demands of the high-quality market (G. Zhang, Zhu, et al., 2023). At the same time, most existing studies remain limited to empirical evaluations of the single antifreeze efficacy of specific components, lacking in-depth deconstruction of the dynamic coupling relationship between physical mechanical damage, conformational instability, and biochemical oxidative stress. In view of these gaps, this review systematically analyzes the structure-activity mechanisms of cryoprotectants and emphasizes a trifunctional integration strategy as an active molecular defense paradigm. Unlike the substance classification approach for trifunctional cryoprotectants pioneered by Tian, Walayat, Ding and Liu (2022), the present review further extends this framework from a mechanistic perspective by decomposing the freeze induced deterioration cascade into three interrelated kinetic variables: ice growth rate (Vice), protein conformational change rate (Vconf), and oxidative reaction rate (Vox). This advancement builds upon the important foundation established by Tian, Walayat, Ding and Liu (2022) while shifting the focus from static categorization to dynamic mechanistic understanding. Our analysis indicates that these three variables are not independent but are engaged in bidirectional feedback coupling. Ice growth drives protein denaturation, which in turn accelerates lipid oxidation, while the resulting oxidation products reciprocally damage protein conformation. At the same time, we evaluate the application of different cryoprotective strategies in maintaining surimi gel strength, preserving myofibrillar integrity in fish fillets, and inhibiting melanosis in crustaceans. Finally, this review addresses realistic industrial hurdles, including sensory defects and economic costs, while prospectively integrating artificial intelligence (AI) driven virtual screening and circular economy strategies (Al-Sarayreh et al., 2023). The overarching goal is to provide systemic theoretical support and practical guidance for establishing a new global aquatic cryopreservation ecosystem that balances green sustainability with precision quality control.

Mechanisms of F-T cycle-induced quality deterioration in aquatic products

Lipid-protein co-oxidation

During F-T cycles, the quality deterioration of aquatic products is not an isolated superposition of lipid oxidation and protein denaturation, but rather a deeply coupled process. The growth of large ice crystals during F-T cycles directly disrupts the structural integrity of cell membranes, facilitating the release of previously encapsulated endogenous lipases and pro-oxidants into the matrix (Rahman et al., 2025).

The core of this systemic impairment, depicted in the middle section of Fig. 1A, lies in the collapse of the protective solvation shell surrounding MP (Zhang, Bai, et al., 2023). In their native state, proteins are stabilized by a dense layer of hydration water that maintains their folded conformation. However, during freezing and frozen storage, the formation and expansion of ice crystals disrupt this hydration shell, leading to protein denaturation and an increased susceptibility to oxidation (J. Liu et al., 2022). Fig. 1A explicitly shows this molecular transition: the protein shifts from a Natural state to an Unfolded state (Z. Liu et al., 2023a). This unfolding leads to the extensive exposure of internal hydrophobic groups and reactive side chains, rendering the protein highly susceptible to biochemical attack.

Concomitant with the protein unfolding, the liberation of free unsaturated fatty acids through lipid hydrolysis provides the substrate for alkoxy radical generation. These radicals initiates the carbonylation of protein side chains by abstracting hydrogen atoms from the now-exposed residues of the Unfolded protein (G. Liao et al., 2022). The synergistic effect is further exacerbated by active electrophilic agents such as malondialdehyde, which form covalent bonds with the unfolded protein residues via Schiff base reactions or Michael addition (R. Feng et al., 2024). Fig. 1B systematically reveals the multiple trigger pathways of lipid oxidation in aquatic products and their effects on quality (Ying et al., 2025a). As the oxidative process intensifies, the system progresses from fatty acid oxidation to the generation of primary hydroperoxides, which ultimately decompose into small-molecule volatile compounds such as aldehydes, ketones, and alcohols.

Ultimately, this microstructural evolution led to the comprehensive degradation of macroscopic functional properties (Klunklin et al., 2025). The compromised MP network, having lost its structural regularity and hydration stability, fails to construct a dense three-dimensional gel matrix, thereby directly weakening the system's capacity for water entrapment (C. Xu et al., 2024). Consequently, the co-oxidation cross-linking of lipids and proteins, rooted in the initial collapse of the molecular hydrogen-bonding network and the protein solvation layer, is regarded as the critical biochemical pathway underlying the impairment of processing quality in aquatic products.

Ice crystal formation

Cryopreservation serves as a fundamental strategy for preserving aquatic products by reducing water activity to suppress microbial proliferation and retard endogenous enzymatic degradation (Jia, Roy, Pan and Mraz, 2022). Nevertheless, unavoidable temperature fluctuations during processing and distribution frequently induce recurrent F-T cycles that facilitate ice crystal formation and recrystallization. These phenomena trigger oxidative stress within muscle cells and cause substantial damage to myofibrillar and cellular architectures. As the most prominent consequence of the freezing process, ice crystal morphology and spatial distribution are critical determinants of the extent of cryogenic damage. The evolution of these crystals encompasses nucleation and growth stages (Tan, Mei, & Xie, 2021). During the initial phase, the nucleation process is governed by interfacial characteristics and involves a competition between homogeneous and heterogeneous nucleation (Sun et al., 2023). Fig. 1C reveals the biochemical basis of this process from a molecular dynamics perspective: the spatial arrangement of water molecules has transitioned from ordered to disordered. As shown in Fig. 1C, the physical state of water molecules depends on their hydrogen bond connection patterns, which manifests as the dynamic evolution from dimer water to more complex clusters of trimeric, tetramer, and even hexamer water (Zheng et al., 2024). Subsequently, the growth pattern is primarily modulated by the freezing rate. Rapid freezing utilizes a high degree of supercooling to induce the formation of high-density and uniformly distributed intracellular ice crystals, which maximizes the preservation of tissue microstructural integrity (Lu et al., 2022). In contrast, slow freezing promotes the migration of moisture toward extracellular spaces and results in the formation of coarse and irregular ice crystals that exert severe mechanical compression and physical trauma on muscle fibers.

Temperature fluctuations encountered during storage and distribution represent the primary driving force for IRI. Guided by the principles of Ostwald ripening, smaller ice crystals tend to migrate toward and refreeze onto the surfaces of larger crystals (Malinowska et al., 2022). This dynamic evolution not only exacerbates mechanical damage to myofibrils but also triggers a profound solute concentration effect (Q. Jiang et al., 2021). As the solute concentration within the unfrozen phase increases drastically, fluctuations in systemic pH and ionic strength impose extreme chemical stress on the matrix and necessitate the dehydration and conformational destabilization of MP (Tan, Ye, & Xie, 2021). Furthermore, the disruption of physical barriers by ice crystals synergistically facilitates lipid oxidation, textural softening, and color browning to establish a complex network of quality degradation (Z. Xu et al., 2026). Consequently, the effective inhibition of ice crystal nucleation and recrystallization growth during F-T cycles has emerged as a critical imperative for the aquatic industry to achieve precision quality control and ensure consistency in shelf-life performance.

Microstructure-quality correlations

The quality of aquatic products deteriorates during F-T cycles through a systematic progression initiated by physical structural breakdown and water redistribution. This process begins when ice crystal formation ruptures muscle cells and disrupts the sarcoplasmic protein distribution, which leads to significant moisture migration and nutrient loss. Quantitative evidence demonstrates the severity of such damage; for instance, repeated F-T stress reduced the water-holding capacity (WHC) of bonito fillets by approximately 35% after five cycles, which was accompanied by a significant decline in Ca2+-ATPase activity (Yatmaz & Gokoglu, 2024a). In crustacean matrices, the incorporation of carrageenan into minced shrimp (Litopenaeus vannamei) gels successfully suppressed the reduction in WHC and effectively maintained the microstructural integrity of the protein matrix during frozen storage (Xia et al., 2023).

Beyond physical damage, chemical-mediated oxidative processes further reshape the processing functionalities of aquatic products. In tilapia surimi, the addition of konjac glucomannan stabilized the MP network and maintained gel strength above 250 g·cm under salt-reduced conditions, thereby mitigating the loss of edible traits (Shi et al., 2024). The role of oxidative regulation is also prominent in surimi sol, where γ-polyglutamic acid inhibited the elevation of carbonyl content and protected free thiol groups from F-T-induced carbonylation (Yan, Ding, et al., 2024). Furthermore, lipid deterioration plays a critical role in quality loss, as observed in yellowfin tuna (Thunnus albacares), where the correlation between lipid oxidation and quality characteristics highlighted the systemic impact of rancidity during cold storage (Yu et al., 2025). This oxidative degradation is often driven by water dynamics, a mechanism exemplified by the mechanisms of lipid hydrolysis and association colloid formation in Antarctic krill oil (Pei et al., 2025). Collectively, these molecular and structural alterations, characterized by the disordered association of damaged proteins via hydrophobic interactions, account for the transition of aquatic products from succulent textures to porous and discrete matrices.

Classification and mechanisms of cryoprotectants

Traditional cryoprotectants

Disaccharides or oligosaccharides

Disaccharides and oligosaccharides have become the most widely utilized traditional cryoprotectants in aquatic product preservation due to the exceptional polarity and solubility conferred by their polyhydroxy structures (J. Liu et al., 2019). These saccharides can stabilize MP conformation through physical occupancy and regulate water status during freezing through multiple physical mechanisms, including water replacement, glass transition, water capture, and freezing point depression (Klunklin et al., 2025).

The water replacement theory serves as the cornerstone for maintaining the structural stability of MP. Under freezing induced dehydration stress, the hydroxyl groups of sugar molecules form hydrogen bond clusters, generating passive occupancy that replaces the original bound water molecules and directly associated with polar amino acid residues on the protein surface. This mechanism occupies the active sites on the protein surface and effectively blocked the conformational unfolding of peptide chains (Fig. 2A) (T. Zhang et al., 2024).

The glass transition theory explains the regulatory ability of sugars on the evolution of ice crystals from the perspective of kineticism (D. Wu et al., 2023). The introduction of saccharides significantly elevates the glass transition temperature of the unfrozen phase, causing the system to tend toward the formation of a high viscosity amorphous glassy state (D. Wu et al., 2023). This state transition produces a kinetic hindrance effect that greatly restricted the migration rate of water molecules toward the ice nucleus surface (as shown in Fig. 2C), thereby macroscopically manifesting as the inhibition of ice crystal nucleation and recrystallization (Z. Guo et al., 2025).

The water capture hypothesis and freezing point depression effect collectively optimize the WHC of aquatic products. Sugar molecules induce the formation of sterically confined regions around proteins, enhancing the structuring of surrounding water molecules and restricting their mobility, thereby preventing water from migrating to extracellular spaces during temperature fluctuations. This regulation significantly improves WHC during thawing and maximized the retention of flavor compounds (Yu, Zhang, et al., 2025a). Furthermore, the molecular weight, or Mw, and charge characteristics of saccharides constitute the structural basis for their complex behavior with proteins. Compared to high molecular weight polysaccharide systems, low molecular weight oligosaccharides possess higher diffusion coefficients and steric accessibility, making them more likely to penetrate the dense muscle matrix and establish tight hydrogen bonds or electrostatic associations with target protein sites (T. Zhang et al., 2024).

For instance, xylo-oligosaccharides (XOS) can significantly inhibit freezing-induced protein denaturation and aggregation in dynamic F-T environments by interacting directly with proteins and reducing intermolecular hydrophobic interactions to alleviate the quality deterioration caused by low-temperature stress (Walayat et al., 2024). Some studies have compared the cryoprotective effects of XOS and other oligosaccharides on the quality of frozen aquatic products (Qi et al., 2020). The results indicate that various oligosaccharide treatments exhibited certain protective influences on Litopenaeus vannamei stored under temperature fluctuations. Among the evaluated quality indicators, XOS demonstrated favorable effects on maintaining the lightness of shrimp meat and preserving Ca2+-ATPase activity, which effectively mitigated the denaturation of MP. Furthermore, researches identified that the optimal concentration of XOS in aquatic matrices was approximately 3% (Zhan et al., 2023; B. Zhang et al., 2020). Under this concentration, XOS facilitates the formation of a dense protective hydration layer that significantly inhibited the process of ice recrystallization, thereby maximizing the preservation of cellular structural integrity and enhancing the WHC of the shrimp meat after thawing.

Nevertheless, the established application of disaccharides and oligosaccharides remains constrained by significant sensory and nutritional deficiencies. To achieve ideal cryoprotective effects, industrial inclusion levels must typically be maintained at high concentrations between 4% and 8%. This requirement not only imparts excessive sweetness and caloric content to aquatic products while masking the intrinsic flavor characteristics of the matrix but also fails to align with the contemporary consumer demand for clean labels and low-sugar diets. Consequently, investigating how to reduce dosage through the precise modification of saccharide derivatives or the integration of multifunctional strategies based on the aforementioned theories has become a highly promising research direction in this field.

Phosphates

In the industrial sector of aquatic product preservation, phosphate cryoprotectants serve as a cornerstone because of their significant capacity to enhance quality and efficacy. These agents effectively mitigate protein denaturation and drip loss triggered by F-T cycles through the precise regulation of the microenvironmental balance within muscle tissues (Z. Liu et al., 2023a). The most widely utilized phosphates in industrial production include tetrasodium pyrophosphate, sodium tripolyphosphate, and sodium hexametaphosphate. These compounds exhibit distinct protective focus areas in practical applications owing to variations in their degree of polymerization, charge density, and chain length. For example, tetrasodium pyrophosphate demonstrates superior performance in promoting the dissociation of actomyosin, whereas sodium tripolyphosphate is more advantageous for enhancing electrostatic repulsion and improving WHC (Y. Hu et al., 2021; B. Jia et al., 2023). Through their unique chemical configurations, different types of phosphates collectively construct a multi-layered defense system aimed at maintaining the quality homeostasis of aquatic products during long-term frozen storage.

The protective efficacy of phosphates primarily originates from three core mechanisms involving charge remodeling, biochemical mimicry, and metal chelation. Regarding charge remodeling and electrostatic repulsion, the introduction of phosphates significantly increases the net negative charge on the surface of MP and utilizes their buffering capacity to adjust the systemic pH away from the protein isoelectric point, or pI. Research confirmed that the addition of sodium tripolyphosphate to frozen shrimp effectively induces strong electrostatic repulsive forces within the protein network (Q. Du et al., 2022). This forces highly aggregated structures to relax and thereby reconstructs a highly efficient space for water retention. As illustrated in Fig. 2B, the WHC of the group treated with sodium tripolyphosphate remains at a high level even after multiple F-T cycles, which provides direct verification of the contribution of charge regulation to water locking (C. Yin et al., 2026). Furthermore, phosphates possess characteristics that mimic adenosine triphosphate and promote the dissociation of rigor-associated actomyosin into free myosin and actin. Studies by (Lee et al., 2018) and colleagues on grass carp surimi found that this dissociation not only restores protein solubility but also releases critical gelling sites, which is essential for maintaining the elasticity of frozen surimi. Finally, the multidentate ligand structure of phosphates constructs a biochemical defense barrier by strongly chelating transition metal ions such as ferrous iron, Fe2+, to block the catalysis of oxidation reactions. Experimental results from researchers correspond with the oxidation monitoring data in Fig. 2B, indicating that phosphates significantly inhibit the lipid and protein oxidation cascade (Ying et al., 2021). Additionally, the IRI results presented in Fig. 2B reveal that phosphates can significantly restrict ice crystal growth and maintain the density of muscle fibers by modulating thermophysical properties (C. Yin et al., 2026).

While phosphates effectively enhance the WHC of aquatic products and reduce losses during thawing, their application faces significant challenges. Excessive phosphate intake can interfere with calcium absorption in the human body and disrupt the balance of the calcium-to‑phosphorus ratio. Such imbalances may induce osteoporosis and exacerbate cardiovascular or renal diseases, thus posing potential risks to human health. Moreover, phosphates often impart undesirable metallic or soapy off-flavors, which limit the sensory expression of high-end aquatic products to some extent. With the global surge in demand for clean labels and low-salt diets, de-phosphating has become an inevitable trend in industrial development. These limitations have prompted the scientific community to shift focus toward innovative cryoprotective systems that are phosphate-free, low in salt, and more bio-friendly. This shift drives the evolution of aquatic preservation technology toward a greener and more precise next phase.

Emerging cryoprotectants

Polyphenol

Natural polyphenols are secondary metabolites with multidimensional bioactivities. They have become a focal point in aquatic cryopreservation research due to their strong radical-scavenging abilities and distinctive interactions with biological macromolecules (Kamal et al., 2025). In addition to supporting the global clean label trend, natural polyphenols offer distinct academic value for enhancing the F-T stability of aquatic products compared to traditional synthetic antioxidants.

Current research focuses on two areas: terrestrial plant extracts and marine-derived polyphenols. Terrestrial polyphenols, such as tea polyphenols and guava leaf extracts, have high hydroxyl density and established extraction methods; they usually have lower molecular weights, providing superior steric accessibility and faster diffusion. These small molecules quickly penetrate the myofibrillar matrix and couple with protein sites. Marine polyphenols, in contrast, are complex polymers built from phloroglucinol units linked by C − C or C − O − C bonds (Shrestha et al., 2021). This structure provides them with greater stability and activity under extreme sub-zero or hypersaline conditions. Studies show marine polyphenols anchor more robustly to lipid bilayers and proteins. This strong anchoring enables them to inhibit oxidative reactions and improve gel properties in frozen fish, with effects that persist longer than those of terrestrial polyphenols at the same dosage. Polyphenols establish a defense system against the deterioration in quality of aquatic products during F-T cycles through the synergistic effects of biochemical interception and molecular anchoring (Wei et al., 2021a). This cryoprotective efficacy is initially manifested in the blockade of free radical cascades and the physical stabilization of protein conformations: the robust radical-scavenging capacity of polyphenols delays protein degradation and lipid oxidation, thereby inhibiting biochemical quality loss (S. Zhu et al., 2021). Fig. 3A further illustrates that this interaction builds a stable protective film on the protein surface, effectively enhancing conformational stability and reducing the IRI process (X. Chen et al., 2024). Concurrently, the phenolic hydroxyl groups of young apple polyphenols engage in site-specific hydrogen bonding with protein amino groups, creating a protective effect that stabilizes the secondary structure (e.g., maintaining higher α-helix content) and inhibits the oxidative degradation of MP during frozen storage (Y. Zhang, Yu, et al., 2023). Research has demonstrated that systems such as gelatin-tea polyphenol microgels significantly reduce ice crystal dimensions, mitigating mechanical damage to tissue structures (Zhu, Wang, et al., 2025a). Furthermore, the chelation of metallic cofactors by polyphenols obstructs the initiation of adverse biochemical pathways at their source, as the hydroxyl groups sequester metal ions to form stable complexes, effectively inactivating enzymes such as trimethylamine oxide demethylase (Y. Li et al., 2022). This integrated mechanism, incorporating antioxidant interception, structural reinforcement, and enzymatic inhibition, ultimately preserves the morphological integrity and sensory attributes of fish fillets, shrimp, and surimi-based products.

The industrial application of natural polyphenols is primarily constrained by dose-dependency and sensory interference. Excessive concentrations may trigger pro-oxidative activity, leading to aberrant protein aggregation and impaired texture. Studies have demonstrated that tea polyphenols effectively stabilize MP within a lower concentration range. However, when the content reached or exceeded 20 μmol/g protein, they could induce protein oxidation and denaturation by reducing metal ions in the system and initiating the Fenton reaction (X. Li et al., 2020). In practice, this pro-oxidant risk can be effectively mitigated by combining low concentrations of polyphenols with metal chelators such as citric acid, or by employing microencapsulation technologies to achieve controlled release of the active substances (Xue et al., 2025). Additionally, the inherent astringency, bitterness, and pigmentation of polyphenols can adversely affect the sensory profile of aquatic products. Furthermore, the high costs associated with high-purity extraction remain a barrier to broad industrial adoption. Consequently, future research should focus on enhancing targeting specificity and solubility through mild molecular modifications, as well as on developing synergistic formulations that combine polyphenols with Antifreeze proteins (AFPs) or polysaccharides.

AFPs

AFPs have gained increasing attention as promising cryoprotectants for aquatic product preservation because they function via a non-colligative mechanism at micromolar concentrations, in contrast to traditional cryoprotectants that rely on high osmolyte concentrations (typically >8%) to achieve comparable protective effects (Fu et al., 2025).The functional basis of these proteins originates from their high structural heterogeneity, which represented diverse defensive strategies evolved through independent pathways across different species. Fish-derived AFPs, the earliest category to be systematically investigated, encompass Types I through IV and antifreeze glycoproteins. Type I proteins function through alanine-rich α−helical structures, whereas antifreeze glycoproteins utilized repeating tripeptide sequences to construct a dynamic aqueous phase that obstructed water molecule adsorption onto ice surfaces (Y. Liao et al., 2025). Although these proteins establish a stable foundation for early industrial applications, their low natural abundance restricts large-scale commercialization (Tejo et al., 2020). In contrast, insect-derived AFPs exhibited exceptionally high thermal hysteresis activity. Their regular β−helices and highly conserved threonine ladder sequences enabled them to bind simultaneously to the prism and basal planes of ice crystals, offering overwhelming advantages in ice control under scenarios characterized by severe temperature fluctuations (Ye et al., 2020).

The fundamental distinction between AFPs and traditional cryoprotectants such as lies in their targeted intervention at the ice-crystal interface. Unlike saccharides that stabilize proteins by replacing bound water molecules on protein surfaces. AFPs do not directly interact with proteins. Instead, their functional core is the hydrophobic ice-binding surface, which adsorbed irreversibly onto specific crystal planes. According to the Gibbs-Thomson effect, this adsorption forces ice crystals to grow in curved geometries between protein molecules (Gerhäuser & Gaukel, 2021; W. Jiang et al., 2026). The resulting increase in surface curvature renders the incorporation of water molecules into the lattice thermodynamically unfavorable, thereby significantly depressing the freezing point while the melting point remained constant. Molecular dynamics simulations revealed a sophisticated Janus bifunctionality in AFPs, referring to the spatial segregation of their distinct functional surfaces. The ice-binding surface utilizes regularly arranged hydrophobic residues to adsorb irreversibly onto specific crystal planes, thereby creating a thermodynamic barrier to ice growth. Concurrently, the non-binding surface presents a highly disordered hydrophilic network that obstructs the deposition of external water molecules. This structural asymmetry enables AFPs to simultaneously exhibit high thermal hysteresis and potent IRI activity, effectively mitigating the mechanical damage to aquatic MP (S. Guo et al., 2024; K. Liu et al., 2016). As demonstrated in the experimental control in Fig. 3B, AFPs significantly increase the thermodynamic resistance of ice crystal migration by adsorbing at the ice-water interface (S. Zhu et al., 2021). In hyperactive proteins, a clathrate-like structure forms by water molecules around the methyl groups of threonine residues locked the protein firmly onto the advancing ice front, effectively inhibiting the Ostwald ripening process that had historically led to the quality degradation of frozen fish meat (Ollis et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2025).

To overcome the bottlenecks of high extraction costs and molecular complexity associated with natural AFPs, research focus has pivoted toward AFPs with molecular weights below 3000 Da obtained through controlled enzymatic hydrolysis (X. Chen et al., 2021). These short-chain peptides not only retain core thermal hysteresis and recrystallization inhibition functions but also exhibit superior cryoprotective efficacy by penetrating MP matrix and stabilizing its structure at lower concentrations. In practical applications, low concentrations of AFPs significantly reduce thaw loss and maintained the microstructural integrity of MP in species such as largemouth bass and large yellow croaker (Mao et al., 2025b; Ren et al., 2025). This protective logic extends beyond physical ice control to the maintenance of biochemical homeostasis. AFPs construct a dual barrier on the protein surface by displacing damaged water molecules through synergistic hydration. In studies involving frozen shrimp surimi, AFP and stabilizers such as short-chain inulin demonstrated significant synergistic effects when combined with trehalose, effectively delaying protein denaturation and maintaining gel properties even after seven F-T cycles (Yu et al., 2025b).

Despite the exceptional protective performance demonstrated by AFPs and their biomimetic peptides, large-scale industrialization remains hindered by rigorous challenges regarding yield and cost balance. Current research has been directed toward achieving mass manufacturing through genetic engineering or microbial fermentation, while regulatory frameworks concerning the allergenicity assessment of fish-derived proteins and the legal approval of transgenic products have been continuously refined. Furthermore, enhancing the functional stability of these proteins under thermal processing or high-salinity conditions is essential for their industrial utility. Future research should prioritize the development of biomimetic short peptides through site-directed mutagenesis and molecular simplification. Efforts should also be directed at exploring synergistic formulations with polysaccharides or natural deep eutectic solvents (NADESs) to construct a proactive defense matrix based on the triad of ice inhibition, antioxidation, and homeostasis maintenance, ultimately providing green and sustainable cryopreservation solutions for the global food industry.

NADESs

NADESs, synthesized through the self-assembly of primary metabolites, have emerged as highly competitive candidates for the cryopreservation of aquatic products. The core chemical identity of these systems resides in their complex supramolecular networks, which are constructed via extensive intermolecular hydrogen bonding between hydrogen bond acceptors (HBA) and donors (HBD) at specific molar ratios (Tian, Sun and Zhu, 2022a). As confirmed by Fig. 3C, NADESs based on components including betaine, proline, sucrose, or trehalose increase the survival rate of Lactobacillus plantarum to 84.5%–94.6%, significantly outperforming traditional glycerol (R. Hu, Sun, & Tian, 2024). As shown in Fig. 3D, the charge delocalization induced by this arrangement significantly disrupts the lattice regularity of individual precursors, resulting in a system freezing point much lower than its starting components and allowing the maintenance of a stable liquid state in sub-zero environments (Y. Li et al., 2025a). Based on precursor chemistry, NADESs utilized in aquatic preservation are categorized into four groups: organic acid-based systems, polyol-based systems, carbohydrate-based formulations, and amino acid or amide-based systems, each exerting distinct modulatory effects on the hydrogen bonding network of water. Following the holistic R&D paradigm shown in Fig. 3E, the design of modern NADESs has transitioned from mitigating symptoms toward addressing root causes such as protein denaturation and lipid oxidation (Zhu et al., 2021).

It is noteworthy that the cryoprotective mechanisms of saccharides, AFPs, and NADESs are fundamentally distinct. Saccharides directly replace bound water on protein surfaces through hydroxyl hydrogen bonding, a colligative effect requiring high concentrations (4–8%). AFPs non-colligatively adsorb onto ice crystal surfaces via a hydrophobic ice-binding surface, inhibiting ice growth at very low concentrations. In contrast, NADESs neither directly bind to proteins nor specifically adsorb to ice. Their protection arises from the kinetic constraints imposed on water molecules by the supramolecular hydrogen bond network and the concomitant induction of a glassy state. These mechanistic differences explain why NADESs achieve superior cryoprotection at moderate concentrations (1–3%) compared to traditional saccharides.

The cryoprotective efficacy of NADESs is fundamentally derived from their multidimensional regulation of water molecular dynamics. High-density hydrogen-bonding networks within NADESs enable their components to actively compete with water molecules for bonding sites, imposing severe constraints on the Brownian motion of water (R. Hu et al., 2026). Specifically, the HBA and HBD molecules can sterically insert into nascent hexagonal ice lattices, disrupting the long-range tetrahedral coordination required for ice formation, thereby increasing the unfrozen water content within the aquatic matrix from a thermodynamic perspective. At the micro-kinetic level, NADESs facilitate the transition into a non-equilibrium, metastable vitrified state across a broad temperature range. As cooling progresses, the systemic viscosity increases exponentially, transforming free water into a highly viscous, kinetically hindered matrix (X. Du et al., 2023). As demonstrated in Fig. 3F, this molecular stabilization is verified through nuclear magnetic resonance and ice adhesion strength measurements, confirming that NADESs facilitate a transition into a metastable vitrified state (Tian, Sun and Zhu, 2022b). This glass transition effectively anchors the conformation of MP in place and restricts solute diffusion, thereby averting mechanical damage triggered by IRI and suppressing localized solute concentration effects.

Furthermore, moisture content exerts a significant plasticizing effect on NADESs, thereby modulating the molecular mobility of the entire system. Previous research has indicated that moderate moisture levels, typically between 10% and 25%, optimize the integrity of the supramolecular framework (J. Yin et al., 2023). However, dilution to more than 50% water content led to the structural collapse of the original hydrogen-bonding matrix as components became fully solvated, resulting in the loss of collective supramolecular properties (Spaggiari et al., 2025).

In practical applications, the cryoprotective mechanism of NADESs enables them to significantly enhance the quality of frozen aquatic products. For instance, the strengthening of hydrogen bond networks in NADESs at lower temperatures allows these solvents to remain unfrozen even at −80 °C, effectively mitigating the formation and growth of ice nucleation and suppressing ice recrystallization. This hydrogen bond reinforcement also strengthens the molecular interaction with water, thereby stabilizing the protein hydration environment. When applied to frozen shrimp surimi, betaine-based NADESs demonstrated superior cryoprotective performance, which can be attributed to their ability to reinforce hydrogen bonding with water molecules and maintain a stable hydration state around MP during freezing and thawing (Y. Li et al., 2025b). This interfacial stabilization prevents the exposure of internal hydrophobic residues and maintains low surface hydrophobicity even under extreme cryogenic stress. For instance, trehalose-citric acid-based NADESs were found to reduce thaw loss by approximately 37.9% in mirror carp surimi during F-T cycles, a performance that significantly surpassed that of traditional glycerol-based systems (X. Du et al., 2023).

Despite their substantial potential, the industrialization of NADESs faces rigorous challenges concerning chemical stability and rheological bottlenecks. Formulations containing reducing sugars and amino acids, such as the proline-glucose system, are prone to Amadori rearrangements during storage, which can trigger browning and the formation of potentially cytotoxic substances. Additionally, the exponential increase in viscosity at cryogenic temperatures severely restricts mass transfer and uniform penetration, which has prevented consistent protection of deep muscle tissues in large whole fish or thick fillets in earlier trials. Consequently, future research should prioritize the development of novel formulations with enhanced chemical stability and leverage their properties as green solvents to encapsulate bioactive compounds such as polyphenols or AFPs. Such strategies aim to construct proactive, multidimensional defense matrices for the precision preservation of high-quality aquatic products.

Synergistic effects and multifunctional integration strategies

Analysis of F-T deterioration mechanisms has indicated that quality loss in aquatic products is not driven by a single factor, but rather by a systemic impairment involving water migration, protein denaturation, and oxidative stress. Evaluations of conventional and emerging cryoprotectants have shown that single active components often struggle to achieve comprehensive spatial coverage in complex muscle tissues. This functional limitation has necessitated a transition from single-component exploration to the integration of multifunctional strategies. Consequently, this section evaluates the construction of systematic solutions through physical synergy and functional integration, while analyzing the intrinsic connectivity and causal feedback mechanisms between protective variables. Physical synergy: matrix-targeted protection mechanisms

Following the logic of multi-component formulation, physical synergy aims to construct a complementary defense network by integrating substances with heterogeneous physicochemical properties (Teng et al., 2025). The physicochemical complementarity of these systems is initially illustrated by the molecular anchoring of bioactive components. As demonstrated in Fig. 4A, the incorporation of polyphenols into protein matrices, particularly gelatin, effectively inhibits ice recrystallization and stabilizes protein structure in surimi during repeated F-T cycles. (Zhu et al., 2025b). This is achieved through the formation of carbonyl-phenol adducts via hydrogen bonding and electrostatic interactions, which block oxidative cross-linking and preserve the structural integrity of the MP network. Furthermore, Fig. 4B reveals that chitosan oligosaccharides with specific polymerization degrees, including COS 988, 1500, and 3061, promote the formation of a multi-scale water-locking network (J. Gao et al., 2024). In this arrangement, macromolecules establish complex spatial barriers through steric hindrance to minimize mechanical damage from ice crystals, while smaller molecules flexibly fill gaps within the protein network.

The trifunctional integration strategy: a proactive molecular defense paradigm

The physical synergy strategies described above primarily alleviate ice induced mechanical damage by constructing spatial barriers at macroscopic and mesoscopic scales. However, these strategies mainly act on the downstream consequences of quality deterioration rather than directly targeting the core kinetic drivers of the deterioration process. Therefore, it is necessary to elucidate the coupling relationships among Vice, Vconf, and Vox, at the molecular level, in order to achieve active intervention against the deterioration cascade.

It should be noted that (Tian, Walayat, Ding and Liu, 2022) first proposed the concept of trifunctional cryoprotectants, with their primary contribution lying in substance classification, that is, identifying cryoprotectants that simultaneously possess IRI, protein stabilization, and antioxidant activities. In contrast, the present review advances this framework from a mechanistic perspective by decomposing the deterioration cascade into three interrelated kinetic variables and emphasizing the bidirectional feedback coupling among them. The core contribution of this review is not merely to identify which substances possess three functions, but to understand how these three functions mechanistically interact and synergize, thereby achieving cascading intervention through coordinated regulation of the coupling relationships among Vice, Vconf, and Vox.

Vice serves as the fundamental physical driver of systemic deterioration. During frozen storage, the formation and growth of ice crystals disrupt protein structure through two pathways. First, the direct physical piercing and compression of cell membranes and muscle fibers by ice crystals disrupts the secondary bonds that maintain protein conformational stability, leading to molecular unfolding and abnormal exposure of internal hydrophobic groups (Z. Liu et al., 2023b). Second, the solute concentration effect driven by ice crystal growth causes a sharp increase in solute concentration within the unfrozen phase, altering the ionic strength and pH environment around proteins, thereby inducing a significant increase in surface hydrophobicity (Bao et al., 2021; X. Chen, Li, et al., 2022). These changes are the primary factors driving the increase in Vconf. Simultaneously, these conformational changes further expose oxidation sensitive groups, promoting free radical diffusion in the polyunsaturated fatty acid rich matrix of aquatic products, thereby accelerating the Vox.

AFPs and NADESs are two typical classes of ice growth regulators. As described in Section 3.2.2, AFPs adsorb irreversibly onto specific ice crystal planes through their hydrophobic ice-binding surface, producing a thermal hysteresis effect that effectively inhibits ice crystal growth and recrystallization. Studies demonstrated that treatment with AFPs exhibits potent IRI activity in surimi. AFPs effectively prevented protein denaturation by reducing the loss of Ca2+-ATPase activity and retarding the oxidation of sulfhydryl groups. Furthermore, AFPs mitigate the impact of freeze induced stress on water migration, thereby protecting surimi from the loss of immobilized and bound water during frozen storage (X. Chen, Wu, et al., 2022). NADESs, through their supramolecular hydrogen bond networks, enhance their ability to compete with water molecules for binding sites at low temperatures, restricting water molecular motion and thereby inhibiting ice nucleation and recrystallization. (X. Du et al., 2026) demonstrated that after treatment with fructooligosaccharides/trehalose-based NADESs, the deformation rate of 3D printed mirror carp surimi products was only 3.70% following five freeze thaw cycles. This value was significantly lower than that of the control group (25.30%) and was also superior to that of the commercial cryoprotectant group (7.05%). The NADESs suppressed the migration of immobilized water to free water, thereby effectively reducing ice crystal growth and recrystallization induced damage to protein structure. These Vice targeting cryoprotectants, by controlling Vice, not only directly reduce the physical damage of ice crystals to proteins but also indirectly slow down the progression of protein denaturation and lipid oxidation, thereby achieving source interception of the deterioration cascade.

As frozen storage time prolonged, the Vconf evolved into a central control node within the deterioration network and exhibited bidirectional feedback network characteristics. On one hand, after the unfolding of MP, particularly the sensitive fish myosin, oxidation sensitive groups originally buried inside the molecule become exposed to the microenvironment, significantly increasing the susceptibility of the protein backbone to reactive oxygen species attack, thereby elevating Vox (S. Gao et al., 2023; Yan, Du, et al., 2024). Studies demonstrated that protein denaturation occurrs prior to large scale oxidation during frozen storage, with conformational changes providing abundant reaction targets for oxidative reactions (S. Gao et al., 2023). On the other hand, unfolded protein molecules expose more hydrophobic groups. According to the theory of heterogeneous ice nucleation, hydrophobic surfaces could serve as templates for ice crystal nucleation, potentially promoting Vice (P. Wu & Yuan, 2026). Experimental evidence confirm that hydrophobic surfaces significantly lowered the free energy barrier for ice nucleation and induced the formation of an ice like ordered structure in interfacial water molecules.

To target this central node, disaccharides or and Vox, oligosaccharides, phosphates, or functional NADESs could suppress the increase in Vconf through water replacement mechanisms or by forming protective barriers on protein surfaces. Experimental evidence showed that the combination of trehalose and sodium pyrophosphate simultaneously inhibited both the oxidation and structural changes of MP (D. Wu et al., 2024). Once protein conformation was stabilized during freeze thaw cycles, Vox was suppressed. Concurrently, the intact protein structure maintained its original water holding network, reducing the generation of mobilizable free water, thereby counteracting the increase in Vice at the molecular scale and achieving interruption of the deterioration cascade from this central node.

Lipid oxidation products exert reciprocal interactions with proteins, establishing a reinforcing feedback loop from Vox to Vconf During lipid oxidation, peroxide radicals attack the protein backbone through chain reactions. Moreover, reactive carbonyl compounds generated from the oxidation of aquatic polyunsaturated fatty acids, such as malondialdehyde and 4 hydroxynonenal, form covalent lipoxidation adducts via Schiff base formation or Michael addition reactions with nucleophilic residues on protein side chains. This covalent modification induces irreversible hydrogen bond breakage within proteins, thereby creating a positive feedback vicious cycle from Vox to Vconf (Bao et al., 2025).

Polyphenols are typical antioxidants. Their phenolic hydroxyl structures confer strong free radical scavenging and metal ion chelating abilities, enabling them to effectively interrupt the lipid oxidation chain reaction. Studies demonstrated that polyphenol treatment increased the hardness of tilapia fillets from 183 g to over 230 g and extended the shelf life from 77 days to 102 days, while significantly improving protein conformational stability (Wei et al., 2021b). Polyphenols can also form hydrogen bonds with proteins, producing a molecular anchoring effect that further enhances protein structural stability (Günal-Köroğlu et al., 2025). These Vox targeting cryoprotectants, by inhibiting lipid oxidation to intervene in Vox, not only reduced the attack of oxidation products on proteins but also further decreased ice crystal nucleation sites and lowered Vice, thereby achieving multi point inhibition of the deterioration network.

Based on the above analysis, the present review considers that freeze induced deterioration is not a unidirectional linear process but rather a complex network initiated by Vice and reinforced through the mutual coupling of Vconf and Vox. In this network, the progression of any single variable accelerates the deterioration of the other variables through feedback mechanisms. This integrated perspective not only provides a mechanistic explanation for the rapid quality deterioration of aquatic products but also establishes a theoretical foundation for the development of trifunctional integrated cryoprotective strategies.

Challenges and future perspectives

Although novel bio based cryoprotectants have demonstrated excellent efficacy at the laboratory stage, their widespread application still faces multiple challenges. Traditional cryoprotectants such as monosaccharides and phosphates require high addition concentrations, which affects sensory quality and health. Natural active ingredients such as polyphenols and antifreeze peptides exhibit low penetration efficiency and poor stability in muscle tissues, reducing protection uniformity. Furthermore, existing strategies are largely experience driven and lack precise adaptation to heterogeneous aquatic matrices. To overcome these limitations, future strategies must transition from passive defense to precision regulation toward intelligence, low dosage, and high efficiency synergy. In this regard, this article proposes technological innovations in the following three dimensions.

AI is providing a new pathway for cryoprotectant development. This strategy has achieved preliminary success in antifreeze peptide discovery. For example, through molecular simulation and peptidomics, researchers identified novel antifreeze peptides from Evynnis japonica scales and Takifugu obscurus skin (W. Jiang et al., 2025; F. Yang et al., 2022). Machine learning also identified two peptides from shrimp by products with thermal hysteresis activities of 1.37 °C and 1.57 °C (K. Zhu et al., 2022). The integration of machine learning with molecular dynamics simulations holds promise as an effective tool for discovering and optimizing antifreeze peptides, allowing simultaneous evaluation of their ice binding activity and conformational stability, thus providing new opportunities for the coordinated regulation of Vice and Vconf. Beyond peptide discovery, mechanism-driven AI is increasingly being integrated into food cold-chain control and governance frameworks, enabling predictive risk assessment and intelligent decision-making for temperature-sensitive aquatic products (Xiong et al., 2026). However, these peptides require further validation of their long-term protective effects and safety in real frozen storage systems. In addition, AI designed cryoprotectants must undergo rigorous safety evaluation and regulatory approval, such as GRAS or Novel Food Regulation (Cattaneo et al., 2023). Existing regulatory frameworks are designed for traditional ingredients, and AI designed novel molecules with non-natural structures pose challenges for current toxicological methods. Establishing harmonized safety standards is critical for moving AI designed cryoprotectants from computational models to practical applications.

Nano targeted delivery offers a feasible solution for polyphenols with poor stability and low penetration efficiency. Through physical encapsulation or covalent crosslinking, nanocarriers improve the solubility and chemical stability of polyphenols in frozen systems (Kar, 2023). For example, chitosan/pectin nanoparticles loaded with tea polyphenols were used as nucleating agents for slurry ice in large yellow croaker preservation, reducing supercooling degree by 8.3 °C and energy consumption by 24.8% (Qiu et al., 2025). Similarly, nano hydroxyapatite loaded with tea polyphenols was incorporated into a chitosan matrix to prepare a composite film for semi dried golden pompano, decreasing water vapor permeability by 29.78% and oxygen permeability by 35.59%, while inhibiting protein and lipid oxidation (Zhou, Xie, et al., 2025). The core value of this technology lies in precise delivery of antifreeze molecules to the MP interface, maintaining frozen storage protection while reducing effective concentration.

Physical field assistance technologies such as ultrasound and magnetic fields have the potential to amplify the application potential of antifreeze factors, achieving physical and biochemical synergistic enhancement. Ultrasound assisted technology can drive rapid penetration of antifreeze molecules into deep tissues (Pantuhan et al., 2026). Magnetic field intervention can effectively reduce ice crystal size and protect muscle integrity. For example, static magnetic field assisted immersion freezing reduced ice crystal diameter in Atlantic salmon muscle from 205 μm to 69 μm, decreased centrifugal loss to 12.92%, and better maintained myosin Ca2+-ATPase activity and fish hardness (Liu et al., 2025). Under repeated freeze thaw conditions, alternating magnetic field assisted freezing of golden pompano fillets resulted in 9.09% lower lipid oxidation products and 3.45% lower protein carbonyl content (Fang et al., 2026). This approach aims to reduce chemical cryoprotectant use through physical field guidance. However, the initial investment and operational energy consumption of magnetic field assisted freezing equipment are relatively high, and its economic feasibility requires further evaluation.

Conclusion

During frozen storage and cold-chain distribution of aquatic products, frequent F-T cycles trigger IRI, protein conformational unfolding, and lipid oxidation cascades, which lead to severe structural damage and sensory deterioration. These thermomechanical injuries, caused by fluctuating temperatures, directly result in significant drip loss, texture softening, and rancid off-flavors. These issues represent major quality bottlenecks within the aquatic processing industry. To prevent such degradation, the addition of cryoprotectants has become the primary intervention. However, traditional saccharide and phosphate systems, though instrumental in maintaining MP stability through water replacement mechanisms, are increasingly at odds with clean-label and health trends due to high sweetness and phosphate residue risks. Consequently, emerging systems, such as AFPs and NADESs hold promise by influencing ice crystal morphology through non-colligative mechanisms, thus demonstrating superior preservation potential.

Despite these advances, most existing studies remain focused on evaluating individual cryoprotectants or classifying them by function, leaving the mechanistic interactions among different protective actions largely unexplored. To address this gap, the present review advances the trifunctional cryoprotectant framework originally proposed by Tian et al. (2022). Unlike their focus on substance classification, we decompose the freeze induced deterioration cascade into three interrelated kinetic variables: Vice, Vconf, and Vox. Our analysis indicates that these three variables are not independent but rather engaged in a bidirectional feedback network. Ice crystal growth drives protein conformational changes (Vice to Vconf), which expose oxidation sensitive groups and accelerate lipid oxidation (Vconf to Vox). Lipid oxidation products, in turn, reciprocally damage protein conformation (Vox to Vconf), while protein unfolding may also promote ice recrystallization (Vconf to Vice), forming a feedback loop.

Based on this mechanistic understanding, this review proposes a cascading intervention strategy. Cryoprotectants targeting Vice, such as AFPs and NADESs, not only directly inhibit ice growth but also indirectly suppress protein denaturation and lipid oxidation. Cryoprotectants targeting Vconf, such as saccharides, phosphates, and NADESs, stabilize protein conformation, thereby reducing oxidation and counteracting ice growth. Cryoprotectants targeting Vox, such as polyphenols, inhibit lipid oxidation, thereby protecting protein conformation and reducing ice nucleation sites. This integrated perspective provides a theoretical basis for designing low-dose, high-efficiency cryoprotective strategies through coordinated regulation of Vice, Vconf, and Vox.

Going forward, incorporating AI virtual screening, advanced nanodelivery techniques, and processing by-products such as fish scale proteins could further improve quality and stability, with the key goal of closely mimicking the texture and flavor of fresh aquatic products in dynamic cold-chain environments, ultimately driving a green transformation of the high-quality aquatic supply chain.
