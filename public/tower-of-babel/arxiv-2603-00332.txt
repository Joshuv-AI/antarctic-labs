Stabilization of the Marine Riser model by controllers depending on finitely many parameters
V.K. Kalantarov, A.A. Namazov and E. S. Titi

Abstract
. We prove global stabilization of the marine riser models using a feedback controller that depend on finitely many finite-volume elements and finitely many nodal observables. Our approach is based on a feedback control design for dissipative nonlinear partial differential equations, inspired by the methodology introduced in [Evol. Equ. Control Theory, 3 (2014), 579–594]. The proposed control strategy ensures asymptotic stabilization while maintaining computational feasibility, making it suitable for practical applications.

1.
Introduction

We study the problem of stabilization to the zero stationary state of the marine riser equation

mu_{tt}+ku_{xxxx}-\left[{a(x)u_{x}}\right]_{x}+\gamma u_{tx}+bu_{t}\left|{u_{t}}\right|^{p}=0,\ x\in(0,L),t>0,

(1.1)

subject to the homogeneous joint Dirichlet and Neumann boundary conditions

u(0,t)=u_{x}(0,t)=u(L,t)=u_{x}(L,t)=0,\quad t>0,

(1.2)

where
k>0
is the flexural rigidity of the riser,
b>0
is the nonlinear drag force and
m>0
represents the mass
line density,
\gamma
is the Coriolis force parameter,
a(\cdot)\in C^{1}[0,L]

describes the effective tension coefficient and
u|{u_{t}}|^{p},\ p\in[1,\infty)
is the nonlinear damping term.

The nonlinear wave equation (
1.1
) wıth
p=1
arises in the mathematical model of marine risers - long, slender pipes used in offshore drilling to transport oil or gas from the seabed to surface platforms. Such structures are subject to complex interactions with surrounding fluids and external forces, including gravity, ocean currents, and drilling-induced vibrations. Their safe operation critically depends on stability: excessive vibrations or negative effective tension may lead to buckling, compromising the integrity of the riser. The study of the dynamical behavior of equation (
1.1
) is therefore not only of mathematical interest, but also of direct importance in offshore engineering. At the same time, it fits naturally into the broader mathematical framework of feedback stabilization of nonlinear dissipative partial differential equations, where one seeks to design effective control mechanisms to suppress instabilities and ensure long-time stability.

Earlier studies of the marine riser model (see e.g.
[

) under the homogeneous Dirichlet’s boundary conditions

u(0,t)=u_{xx}(0,t)=u(L,t)=u_{xx}(L,t)=0,t>0,

(1.3)

The polynomial decay in time estimate for solutions of this problem is

when the effective tension

function that satisfies the conditions

-a_{0}\leq a(x)\leq a_{1},\ \ \forall x\in[0,L],\quad\hbox{with}\quad a_{0}\geq 0,\ a_{1}>0,

(1.4)

and

d_{0}:=k-a_{0}\frac{L^{2}}{\pi^{2}}>0.

Similar results for the multidimensional marine riser equation are obtained in

However, it is well known that if the effective tension becomes negative, the riser may buckle and stability is lost.

The design of finite-dimensional controllers for PDEs has a long history, going back to works such as

This method has since been applied to a wide class of systems, including nonlinear wave equations, the Boussinesq, the Kuramoto–Sivashinsky equations, the complex Ginzburg–Landau equation, Navier–Stokes–Voigt models, and chevron pattern equations (see

Recently, similar ideas have been applied to the stabilization of the marine riser model with negative effective tension. In particular,

This result demonstrates that even when the uncontrolled system is unstable, it can be stabilized through appropriately designed low-dimensional control laws.

In this paper, we extend the results of
[
3
]
and develop new feedback control strategies for the marine riser equation (
1.1
)–(
1.2
). Our main contributions can be summarized as follows:

•

We establish global asymptotic stabilization of the marine riser equation using feedback controllers depending on finitely many finite-volume elements rather than Fourier modes. These feedbacks are more natural from a practical experimental and computational viewpoint. From an applied perspective, the proposed feedback design is computationally efficient and suitable for real-time implementation, as it requires only coarse measurements of the system state. It, therefore, offers a promising approach to vibration control in flexible offshore structures.

•

We provide explicit conditions on the controller parameters ensuring stabilization and derive uniform energy estimates for the closed-loop system.

•

We analyze the influence of both linear and nonlinear damping mechanisms on the decay rates of the energy, identifying parameter regimes leading to polynomial or exponential in time stabilization rate.

•

Our analysis combines Lyapunov functional techniques, compactness arguments, and the finite-dimensional feedback framework introduced in
[
4
]
, adapted here to a fourth-order wave-type equation with variable coefficients. The results obtained contribute to the ongoing development of control and stabilization theory for nonlinear dissipative nonlinear wave equations, particularly for models involving both tension and bending effects.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we present the functional setting, main assumptions, and preliminary lemmas. Section 3 is devoted to the design of the feedback controllers and the proof of global stabilization results. Here we study the influence of nonlinear damping and provide explicit decay rate estimates. Finally, in Section 4 feedback controllers based on finite-volume elements are utilized to stabilize the zero stationary state of the linear equation

mu_{tt}+ku_{xxxx}-\left[{a(x)u_{x}}\right]_{x}+\gamma u_{tx}+bu_{t}=0,\ \ x\in(0,L),t>0,

3.
Stabilization of the equation with nonlinear damping term

1.2

{u}(0,t)={u}(L,t)=u_{x}(0,t)=u_{x}(L,t)=0,\ t>0,

\mu>0

First, we derive the following energy equality by taking the inner product of

\frac{d}{dt}\left[\frac{m}{2}\|u_{t}(t)\|^{2}+\frac{k}{2}\|u_{xx}(t)\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{L}a(x)u^{2}_{x}(x,t)dx+\frac{\mu h}{2}{\mathcal{B}}_{N}(u(t))\right]\\
=-b\int_{0}^{L}|u_{t}(x,t)|^{p+2}dx.

(3.6)

Here, and in what follows we denote

{\mathcal{B}}_{N}(u(t)):=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}\overline{u}_{k}^{2}(t),\ \ \overline{u}_{k}(t)=\frac{1}{h}\int\limits_{J_{k}}u(x,t)dx.

(3.7)

Next, we take the inner product of (

\|u_{x}\|^{2}\leq\|u\|\|u_{xx}\|,

Consequently, if we assume that

b\delta\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{L}u(x,s)|u_{t}(x,s)|^{p}u_{t}(x,s)dxds\right|\\
\leq b\delta\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{L}|u_{t}(x,s)|^{p+2}dxds\right)^{\frac{p+1}{p+2}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{L}|u(x,s)|^{p+2}dxds\right)^{\frac{1}{p+2}}\\
\leq b\delta\frac{\beta_{L}}{\lambda_{1}}\left(\frac{1}{b}{\mathcal{E}}(0)\right)^{\frac{p+1}{p+2}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|u_{xx}(s)\|^{p+2}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{p+2}}\leq R_{0}t^{\frac{1}{p+2}},

where

R_{1}:=b\delta\frac{\beta_{L}}{\lambda_{1}}\left(\frac{1}{b}{\mathcal{E}}(u(0))\right)^{\frac{p+1}{p+2}}\frac{8}{k}{\mathcal{E}}(u(0)).

Thanks to the last two inequalities we derive from the inequality (
3.30
)
the desired decay estimate

D_{1}E(u(t))\leq E(u(0))t^{-1}+R_{0}t^{-\frac{p}{p+2}}+R_{1}t^{-\frac{p+1}{p+2}}.

In conclusion we have proved the following theorem:

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\delta=\min\left\{\frac{\lambda_{1}\sqrt{k}}{4\sqrt{m}},\ \frac{k\lambda_{1}}{\gamma^{2}}\right\}
. Suppose that
the feedback nudging parameter
\mu
is large enough and
h
is small enough such that conditions (
3.16
), (
3.22
) and (
3.28
) are satsfied, that is,

h\leq\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}\min\left\{\frac{k}{2a_{0}},\frac{k}{\sqrt{2a_{0}}},\frac{D_{0}}{2a_{0}}\right\},\ \ \mu\geq a_{0}^{2}\max\left\{\frac{1}{k},\frac{1}{D_{0}}\right\},

then the zero stationary state of equation (
3.1
) is globally asymptotically stable.
Moreover, the following decay estimate is satisfied

\|u_{t}(t)\|^{2}+\|u_{xx}(t)\|^{2}\leq C_{0}t^{-(p+1)/(p+2)},\ \ \forall t>0,

where
C_{0}
is a positive constant which is independent of
t.

4.
Stabilization of the linear equation .

In this section we are employing feedback controllers based on finite-volume elements to stabilize the zero stationary state of the linear wave equation

mu_{tt}+ku_{xxxx}-\left[{a(x)u_{x}}\right]_{x}+\gamma u_{tx}+bu_{t}=0,

modeling dynamics of the marine riser conveying
fluid (see, e.g.,
[
2
]
,
[
31
]
and references therein).
Just as in the previous section, we assume that the observables are the finite-volume elements
\overline{u}_{k}(t):=\frac{1}{|J_{k}|}\int\limits_{J_{k}}u(x,t)dx,\ (k=1,\cdots,N)
:

mu_{tt}+ku_{xxxx}-\left[{a(x)u_{x}}\right]_{x}+\gamma u_{tx}+bu_{t}=-\mu\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}\overline{u}_{k}(t)\chi_{J_{k}}(x),x\in(0,L),\ t>0,

(4.1)

{u}(0,t)={u}(L,t)=u_{x}(0,t)=u_{x}(L,t)=0,\ t>0,

(4.2)

u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\ \ u_{t}(x,0)=u_{1}(x),\ \ x\in(0,L),

(4.3)

where
k,\gamma
and
\mu
are given positive
parameters.

Taking the inner product of
the equation (
3.1
) with
u_{t}+\varepsilon u
we obtain:

\frac{d}{dt}\Big[\frac{m}{2}\|u_{t}(t)\|^{2}+\frac{k}{2}\|u_{xx}(t)\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{L}a(x)u_{x}^{2}(x,t)dx+\frac{1}{2}\mu h{\mathcal{B}}_{N}(u(t))\\
+\varepsilon m(u(t),u_{t}(t))+\frac{\varepsilon b}{2}\|u(t)\|^{2}\Big]+(b-\varepsilon m)\|u_{t}\|^{2}+\varepsilon k\|u_{xx}(t)\|^{2}\\
+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{L}a(x)u_{x}^{2}(x,t)dx+\mu h\varepsilon{\mathcal{B}}_{N}(u(t))=0,

(4.4)

where
{\mathcal{B}}_{N}(u(t))
is defined in (
3.7
) and
\varepsilon>0
is a parameter to be determined below.

By using condition (
1.4
), the interpolation inequality (
3.10
), the Young’s inequality, the inequality (
2.5
) and the Poincaré inequality we have

-\varepsilon\int_{0}^{L}a(x)u_{x}^{2}(x,t)dx\leq\varepsilon a_{0}\|u_{x}(t)\|^{2}\\
\leq\frac{\varepsilon k}{4}\|u_{xx}(t)\|^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon a_{0}^{2}}{k}\|u(t)\|^{2}\leq\left(\frac{\varepsilon k}{4}+\frac{\varepsilon a_{0}^{2}h^{2}}{k\lambda_{1}}\right)\|u_{xx}(t)\|^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon a_{0}^{2}h}{k}{\mathcal{B}}_{N}(u(t),

(4.5)

Employing the inequality (
4.5
) we obtain from (
4.4
) the inequality

\frac{d}{dt}{\mathcal{W}}(t)+(b-\varepsilon m)\|u_{t}(t)\|^{2}+\left(\frac{3\varepsilon k}{4}-\frac{\varepsilon a_{0}^{2}h^{2}}{k\lambda_{1}}\right)\|u_{xx}(t)\|^{2}\\
+\left(\mu h\varepsilon-\frac{\varepsilon a_{0}^{2}h}{k}\right){\mathcal{B}}_{N}(u(t))\leq 0,

(4.6)

where

{\mathcal{W}}(t):=\frac{m}{2}\|u_{t}(t)\|^{2}+\frac{k}{2}\|u_{xx}(t)\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{L}a(x)u_{x}^{2}(x,t)dx+\frac{1}{2}\mu h{\mathcal{B}}_{N}(u(t))\\
+\varepsilon m(u(t),u_{t}(t))+\frac{\varepsilon b}{2}\|u(t)\|^{2}.

(4.7)

Utilizing the inequalities

\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{L}a(x)u_{x}^{2}(x,t)dx\geq-\frac{a_{0}}{2}\|u_{x}(t)\|^{2}\geq-\frac{a_{0}}{2}\|u(t)\|\|u_{xx}(t)\|\geq-\frac{k}{4}\|u_{xx}(t)\|^{2}-\frac{a_{0}^{2}}{4k}\|u(t)\|^{2},

and

\varepsilon m(u(t),u_{t}(t))\geq-\frac{m}{4}\|u_{t}(t)\|^{2}-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{m}\|u(t)\|^{2},

we we obtain

{\mathcal{W}}(t)\geq\frac{m}{4}\|u_{t}(t)\|^{2}+\frac{k}{4}\|u_{xx}(t)\|^{2}+\left(\frac{\varepsilon b}{2}-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{m}-\frac{a_{0}^{2}}{4k}\right)\|u(t)\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\mu h{\mathcal{B}}_{N}(u(t)).

Suppose that

\varepsilon\leq\frac{mb}{2}.

(4.8)

Then employing inequality (
2.5
) yields

{\mathcal{W}}(t)\geq\frac{m}{4}\|u_{t}(t)\|^{2}+\left(\frac{k}{4}-\frac{a_{0}^{2}h^{2}}{4k\lambda_{1}}\right)\|u_{xx}(t)\|^{2}+\left(\frac{1}{2}\mu h-\frac{a_{0}^{2}h}{4k}\right){\mathcal{B}}_{N}(u(t)).

Hence if

h^{2}\leq\frac{k^{2}\lambda_{1}}{2a_{0}^{2}}\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \mu\geq\frac{a_{0}^{2}}{k},

(4.9)

then

{\mathcal{W}}(t)\geq\frac{m}{4}\|u_{t}(t)\|^{2}+\frac{k}{8}\|u_{xx}(t)\|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\mu h{\mathcal{B}}_{N}(u(t)).

(4.10)

It follows from (
4.6
) that

\frac{d}{dt}{\mathcal{W}}(t)+\frac{b}{2}\|u_{t}(t)\|^{2}+\frac{3\varepsilon k}{8}\|u_{xx}(t)\|^{2}+\frac{a_{0}^{2}h}{\varepsilon k}{\mathcal{B}}_{N}(u(t))\leq 0,

(4.11)

provided

\varepsilon\leq\frac{b}{2m},\ h\leq\varepsilon k\sqrt{\frac{3\lambda_{1}}{8a_{0}}}\ \mbox{and}\ \ \mu\geq\frac{2a_{0}^{2}}{k\varepsilon^{2}}.

Moreover, it is clear that there exists a constant
\nu>0
, small enough, such that

\frac{b}{2}\|u_{t}(t)\|^{2}+\frac{3\varepsilon k}{8}\|{u}_{xx}(t)\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\mu h\varepsilon{\mathcal{B}}_{N}(u(t))\geq\nu{\mathcal{W}}(t).

Hence one has

\frac{d}{dt}{\mathcal{W}}(t)+\nu{\mathcal{W}}(t)\leq 0.

The above inequality together with (
4.10
) imply the exponential
stabilization estimate

\|u_{t}(t)\|^{2}+\|{u}_{xx}(t)\|^{2}\leq D_{0}e^{-\nu t}.

(4.12)

Consequently we have proved the following:

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose that

h\leq\min\left\{\frac{k}{a_{0}}\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2}},k\sqrt{\frac{3\lambda_{1}}{8}}\right\}\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \mu\geq\frac{a_{0}^{2}}{k}\max\left\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{2}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right\},

where
\varepsilon=\min\left\{\frac{mb}{2},\frac{b}{2m}.\right\}

Then all
solutions of problem (
4.1
)-(
4.2
) tend to zero with
an exponential rate, as
t\to\infty
.
