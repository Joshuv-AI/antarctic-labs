Quantum Geometric Quadrupole of Cooper Pairs
Wenqin Chen, Kaijie Yang, Ting Cao, Shi-Zeng Lin, Jiabin Yu, Di Xiao

Abstract
The size of Cooper pairs defines a fundamental length scale of superconductivity, conventionally set by band dispersion and the superconducting gap. This picture breaks down in flat bands, where quenched dispersion makes quantum geometry essential. Here we develop a general framework based on the Cooper pair quadrupole moment, whose trace gives the pair size. The framework holds for both dispersive and flat-band cases, and provides a unified description of the geometric origin of this length scale. In particular, when time-reversal symmetry is broken, Berry curvature enters through the phase structure of the pair wavefunction and gives an essential contribution absent from previous quantum-metric theories. Together, Berry curvature and quantum metric impose a geometric lower bound on the pair size. Applying this framework to rhombohedral graphene, we find that the Berry-curvature-induced contribution can dominate and yields pair sizes comparable to experimentally inferred coherence lengths. These results identify Berry curvature as a central geometric ingredient controlling the microscopic length scale of superconductivity.

In this work, we resolve this question by developing a general theory of the Cooper pair quadrupole moment, whose trace defines the pair size. For intraband pairing, we show that once time-reversal symmetry (TRS) is broken, the Cooper pair size acquires an essential geometric contribution from Berry curvature—absent in previous theories—which enters through the phase structure of the pair wavefunction. Together with the quantum metric, this contribution sets a geometric lower bound on the Cooper pair size.
Applying our theory to rhombohedral graphene with spin-polarized intravalley pairing, we find a substantial Berry-curvature-induced phase contribution, ranging from about 50% to nearly 100% of the total pair size.
The resulting Cooper pair size is comparable in magnitude to the experimentally observed coherence length, suggesting that quantum geometry sets the characteristic microscopic length scale of superconductivity in rhombohedral graphene. Our theory thus identifies a concrete and previously missing consequence of Berry curvature in superconductors, and establishes quantum geometry as a central ingredient controlling the microscopic structure of Cooper pairs.

Quadrupole moment of Cooper pairs.

To motivate the general formalism, we begin with a semiclassical picture of the Cooper pair quadrupole moment, as illustrated in Fig.
1
. When two Bloch electrons form a bound state via an attractive interaction
V
, their charge centers orbit around each other with a transverse velocity, which consists of a Fermi velocity contribution
\mathbf{v}_{F}
from the band dispersion and an anomalous velocity
\mathbf{v}_{A}=\nabla V\times\bm{\Omega}
from the Berry curvature
Xiao et al. 2010

Zhou et al. 2015
;
Srivastava and Imamoğlu 2015

\mathbf{d}

\mathbf{d}\otimes\mathbf{d}

The Cooper pair quadrupole moment (whose trace defines the pair size) is therefore controlled by both the relative motion of the pair and the intrinsic spread of its constituents, set respectively by the Berry curvature, band dispersion, and quantum metric.

, the resulting pair size is of the same order as the coherence length inferred experimentally from the upper critical field, which is on the order of tens of nanometers.
Although our theory computes a microscopic pair size whereas the experiment probes a phenomenological Ginzburg-Landau coherence length, the order-of-magnitude agreement is nevertheless encouraging, especially because the observed superconducting states lie on the BCS side of the BCS-BEC crossover
Strinati et al. 2018
.

We have also applied our theory to the Berry Trashcan model
Bernevig and Kwan 2025
;
Li et al. 2025b
and found similar orders of magnitude and trends, as summarized in the SM
SM 2026
.

Conclusion and Discussion.

In summary, we establish the geometric quadrupole moment as a fundamental quantity linking quantum geometry to the microscopic structure of Cooper pairs.
The pair quadrupole moment directly enters the London equation through a nonlocal Pippard/BCS kernel that accounts for the finite spread of the pair
Bardeen et al. 1957
;
Pippard 1953
, making it accessible via nonlocal electromagnetic response measurements
Suter et al. 2004
;
Suter et al. 2005
;
Kozhevnikov et al. 2013
.
Similar probes in flat-band superconductors could thus provide a direct window into the geometric structure of Cooper pairs predicted here.
Our framework applies to generic two-body bound states in Bloch bands and can be straightforwardly extended to systems such as excitons.

S2
Geometric bound of Cooper pair size

In a Brillouin zone with periodic boundary conditions, the amplitude contribution to the pair size can be integrated by parts, and the boundary terms vanish. The projected squared relative-coordinate operator then takes the form

\displaystyle\hat{r}^{2}=\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\ket{\vb k_P}\Bigl[\mathcal{D}_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\cdot\mathcal{D}_{\mathbf{k}}+\Tr g^{\mu\nu}_{P}(\mathbf{k})\Bigr]\bra{\vb k_P},

(S43)

where
\Tr g^{\mu\nu}_{P}\equiv\delta_{\mu\nu}g_{P}^{\mu\nu}
and

\mathcal{D}_{\mathbf{k}}=\nabla_{\mathbf{k}}-i\mathbf{A}_{P}(\mathbf{k})

is the pair covariant derivative. Defining the Hermitian operator

D_{\mathbf{k}}=i\mathcal{D}_{\mathbf{k}}=i\nabla_{\mathbf{k}}+\mathbf{A}_{P}(\mathbf{k}),

we have

\displaystyle\hat{r}^{2}=\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\ket{\vb k_P}\Bigl[D_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}+\Tr g_{P}^{\mu\nu}(\mathbf{k})\Bigr]\bra{\vb k_P}.

(S44)

This operator is formally analogous to the Hamiltonian of a charged particle moving in a magnetic field in real space. In this analogy, the pair Berry curvature

\Omega_{P}(\mathbf{k})=\nabla_{\mathbf{k}}\!\times\!\mathbf{A}_{P}(\mathbf{k})

acts as an effective magnetic field in momentum space, while the trace of the pair quantum metric,
\Tr g_{P}^{\mu\nu}
, plays the role of a scalar potential. The commutator of the covariant derivative components is

\displaystyle\big[D_{k_{x}},D_{k_{y}}\big]=i\,\Omega_{P}(\mathbf{k}).

(S45)

Introducing holomorphic and antiholomorphic operators,

\displaystyle D=\frac{D_{k_{x}}+iD_{k_{y}}}{2},\qquad\bar{D}=\frac{D_{k_{x}}-iD_{k_{y}}}{2},

(S46)

we obtain

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\,\Omega_{P}(\mathbf{k}),

(S47)

which allows us to complete the square in two equivalent ways:

\displaystyle D_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}=2(D\bar{D}+\bar{D}D)=4\bar{D}D+\Omega_{P}(\mathbf{k})=4D\bar{D}-\Omega_{P}(\mathbf{k}).

(S48)

Now consider a normalized Cooper pair state whose envelope function is supported, or strongly concentrated, in a momentum-space region
\mathcal{R}
.
We assume that the pair Berry curvature does not change sign in this region. For definiteness, take
\Omega_{P}(\mathbf{k})\geq 0
in
\mathcal{R}
.
Using the ordering

D_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}=4\bar{D}D+\Omega_{P}(\mathbf{k})
,
the expectation value of
\hat{r}^{2}
becomes

\displaystyle\langle\hat{r}^{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathcal{R}}\psi_{\mathbf{k}}^{*}\Bigl[4\bar{D}D+\Omega_{P}(\mathbf{k})+\Tr g^{\mu\nu}_{P}(\mathbf{k})\Bigr]\psi_{\mathbf{k}}

(S49)

\displaystyle=4\langle D\Psi|D\Psi\rangle+\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathcal{R}}|\psi_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}\Bigl[\Omega_{P}(\mathbf{k})+\Tr g_{P}(\mathbf{k})\Bigr].

Since the first term is nonnegative, the Cooper pair size obeys

\displaystyle\langle\hat{r}^{2}\rangle\geq\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathcal{R}}|\psi_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}\Bigl[\Omega_{P}(\mathbf{k})+\Tr g_{P}(\mathbf{k})\Bigr]\equiv\xi^{2}_{\rm Bound}.

(S50)

If instead
\Omega_{P}(\mathbf{k})\leq 0
throughout
\mathcal{R}
, one uses the equivalent ordering

D_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}=4D\bar{D}-\Omega_{P}(\mathbf{k})

and obtains the corresponding result with
\Omega_{P}
replaced by
-\Omega_{P}
. Below we keep the convention
\Omega_{P}(\mathbf{k})\geq 0
in
\mathcal{R}
.
Since the envelope is normalized within the region
\mathcal{R}
, the right-hand side is bounded below by the minimum value of the local geometric contribution in that region,

\displaystyle\langle\hat{r}^{2}\rangle\geq\min_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathcal{R}}\Bigl\{\Omega_{P}(\mathbf{k})+\Tr g_{P}(\mathbf{k})\Bigr\}\equiv\xi_{\rm fund}^{2}.

(S51)

This
\xi_{\rm fund}
is a geometry-only lower bound associated with the sign-definite region
\mathcal{R}
. It is weaker than the envelope-weighted bound above, but it is independent of the detailed pairing channel as long as the Cooper pair envelope is supported, or strongly concentrated, in
\mathcal{R}
.

The bound can be further simplified using the trace inequality. The single-particle trace inequality

\Tr g(\mathbf{k})\geq|\Omega(\mathbf{k})|

implies the corresponding inequality for the pair geometric quantities,

\displaystyle\Tr g^{\mu\nu}_{P}(\mathbf{k})=\Tr g^{\mu\nu}(\mathbf{k})+\Tr g^{\mu\nu}(-\mathbf{k})\geq|\Omega(\mathbf{k})|+|\Omega(-\mathbf{k})|\geq|\Omega_{P}(\mathbf{k})|.

(S52)

In the chosen sign convention,
\Omega_{P}(\mathbf{k})\geq 0
in
\mathcal{R}
, this becomes
\Tr g_{P}(\mathbf{k})\geq\Omega_{P}(\mathbf{k})
in
\mathcal{R}
. Applying it to the envelope-weighted bound gives

\displaystyle\langle\hat{r}^{2}\rangle\geq 2\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathcal{R}}|\psi_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}\Omega_{P}(\mathbf{k}).

(S53)

S3
Two-band model

In this section, we present the details of the Cooper pair size calculation for the two-band model of rhombohedral pentalayer graphene. The single-particle Hamiltonian in valley
K
is

h(\mathbf{k})=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\Delta}{2}+\gamma_{-}\!\left(k_{x}^{2}+k_{y}^{2}\right)&\dfrac{v_{F}^{5}}{t_{1}^{4}}\,(k_{x}-\mathrm{i}k_{y})^{5}\\[6.0pt]
\dfrac{v_{F}^{5}}{t_{1}^{4}}\,(k_{x}+\mathrm{i}k_{y})^{5}&-\frac{\Delta}{2}+\gamma_{+}\!\left(k_{x}^{2}+k_{y}^{2}\right)\end{pmatrix},

(S54)

where
v_{F}=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}t_{0}a
,
a=0.246
nm,
t_{0}=3.1\,\text{eV}
,

t_{1}=380\,\text{meV}
,

t_{2}=141\,\text{meV}
,

\gamma_{\pm}\approx\frac{2v_{F}v_{2}}{t_{1}}\pm\frac{\Delta v_{F}^{2}}{4t_{1}^{2}}
, and
v_{2}=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,t_{2}a
.
The corresponding band dispersions are

E_{\pm}(\mathbf{k})=\frac{\gamma_{+}+\gamma_{-}}{2}\,k^{2}\;\pm\;\sqrt{\Bigl(\frac{\Delta}{2}+\frac{\gamma_{-}-\gamma_{+}}{2}\,k^{2}\Bigr)^{2}+\left(\frac{v_{F}^{5}}{t_{1}^{4}}\right)^{2}k^{10}},\qquad k=\sqrt{k_{x}^{2}+k_{y}^{2}}.

(S55)

The conduction-band eigenvector is

\ket{u(\mathbf{k})}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2|\mathbf{d}|\bigl(|\mathbf{d}|+d_{z}\bigr)}}\begin{pmatrix}|\mathbf{d}|+d_{z}\\[6.0pt]
\dfrac{v_{F}^{5}}{t_{1}^{4}}\,(k_{x}+ik_{y})^{5}\end{pmatrix},

(S56)

where

|\mathbf{d}|=\sqrt{d_{z}^{2}+\left(\frac{v_{F}^{5}}{t_{1}^{4}}\,k^{5}\right)^{2}},\quad d_{z}=\frac{\Delta}{2}+\frac{1}{2}(\gamma_{-}-\gamma_{+})k^{2},\quad k=\sqrt{k_{x}^{2}+k_{y}^{2}}.

(S57)

We restrict attention to intravalley, spin-polarized electronic states and consider a local attractive interaction projected onto the conduction-band basis. The projected interaction matrix element is

V_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}=-U\bra{u(\vb{k})}\ket{u(\vb{k}')}\bra{u(-\vb{k})}\ket{u(-\vb{k}')}.

(S58)

Because the pairing occurs within the same spin and valley sector, the interaction must be antisymmetrized
Patri and Franz 2025
;
Guerci et al. 2025
, which gives

\displaystyle V_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{U}{2}[\bra{u(\vb{k})}\ket{u(\vb{k}')}\bra{u(-\vb{k})}\ket{u(-\vb{k}')}-\bra{u(-\vb{k})}\ket{u(\vb{k}')}\bra{u(\vb{k})}\ket{u(-\vb{k}')}]

(S59)

\displaystyle=-U\frac{\Lambda^{P}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})-\Lambda^{P}(-\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})}{2}.

Using the explicit conduction-band spinor, we obtain

\displaystyle\Lambda^{P}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})=\frac{[(d+d_{z})(d^{\prime}+d_{z}^{\prime})+\frac{v_{F}^{10}}{t_{1}^{8}}(k_{x}-ik_{y})^{5}(k_{x}^{\prime}+ik_{y}^{\prime})^{5}]^{2}}{4dd^{\prime}(d+d_{z})(d^{\prime}+d_{z}^{\prime})},

(S60)

\displaystyle\Lambda^{P}(-\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})=\frac{[(d+d_{z})(d^{\prime}+d_{z}^{\prime})-\frac{v_{F}^{10}}{t_{1}^{8}}(k_{x}-ik_{y})^{5}(k_{x}^{\prime}+ik_{y}^{\prime})^{5}]^{2}}{4dd^{\prime}(d+d_{z})(d^{\prime}+d_{z}^{\prime})},

(S61)

\displaystyle\Lambda^{P}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})-\Lambda^{P}(-\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})=\frac{\frac{v_{F}^{10}}{t_{1}^{8}}(k_{x}-ik_{y})^{5}(k_{x}^{\prime}+ik_{y}^{\prime})^{5}}{dd^{\prime}},

(S62)

so that the antisymmetrized band-projected interaction becomes

\displaystyle V_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}=-U\frac{\frac{v_{F}^{10}}{t_{1}^{8}}(k_{x}-ik_{y})^{5}(k_{x}^{\prime}+ik_{y}^{\prime})^{5}}{2dd^{\prime}},

(S63)

where
d=|\mathbf{d}(\mathbf{k})|
,
d^{\prime}=|\mathbf{d}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime})|
,
d_{z}=d_{z}(\mathbf{k})
, and
d_{z}^{\prime}=d_{z}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime})
.

Figure S1:
Comparison between the Cooper pair size obtained from the decomposition and the direct evaluation using Eq. (2) of the main text.

Substituting the interaction and the Cooper pair state
\ket{\Psi}=\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\psi(\mathbf{k})a^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}}a^{\dagger}_{-\mathbf{k}}\ket{GS}
into the two-body Schrödinger equation
(H_{0}+V)\ket{\Psi}=E_{B}\ket{\Psi}
, we obtain the pair eigenvalue equation

\displaystyle\psi(\mathbf{k})=\frac{1}{2S}\sum_{|\mathbf{k}^{\prime}|<k_{C}}V_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\psi(\mathbf{k}^{\prime}).

(S64)

Here
S
is the two-dimensional sample area. In the flat-band limit, we set
\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}-\varepsilon_{F}=0
, so the equation reduces to

\displaystyle E_{B}\psi(\mathbf{k})=\frac{1}{2S}\sum_{|\mathbf{k}^{\prime}|<k_{C}}V_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\psi(\mathbf{k}^{\prime}),

(S65)

\displaystyle E_{B}\psi(\mathbf{k})=-U\frac{v_{F}^{10}}{2t_{1}^{8}}\frac{(k_{x}-ik_{y})^{5}}{d}\frac{1}{2S}\sum_{|\mathbf{k}^{\prime}|<k_{C}}\frac{(k_{x}^{\prime}+ik_{y}^{\prime})^{5}}{d^{\prime}}\psi(\mathbf{k}^{\prime}).

(S66)

Because
V_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}
factorizes into independent functions of
\mathbf{k}
and
\mathbf{k}^{\prime}
, the pair envelope function can be obtained analytically as

\displaystyle\psi(\mathbf{k})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathcal{N}}}\frac{(k_{x}-ik_{y})^{5}}{|\mathbf{d}(\mathbf{k})|},

(S67)

where
\mathcal{N}
is the normalization factor.

To verify the decomposition of the Cooper pair size, we compare it with the direct evaluation of
\sqrt{\langle r^{2}\rangle}
from Eq. (2) of the main text. As shown in Fig. S
S1
, the two results agree for all
k_{C}
, confirming that
\xi_{\rm Pair}=\sqrt{\xi_{\rm Phase}^{2}+\xi_{\rm Amp}^{2}+\xi_{\rm Metric}^{2}}
is exact.

S4
Berry trashcan model

In this section, we present the details of the Cooper pair size calculation for the Berry trashcan effective model for rhombohedral graphene introduced in Refs.
Bernevig and Kwan 2025
;
Li et al. 2025b
. This model captures the low-energy conduction band shown in Fig.
S2
(a). Near valley
K
, the Hamiltonian is
\hat{H}=\hat{h}+\hat{V}
, with kinetic term

\hat{h}=\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}\,\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}}\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k}},\quad\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}=\Theta(k-k_{C})\,v(k-k_{C}),

(S68)

where
k_{C}
is the radius of the flat-band region, determined by the number of layers
Bernevig and Kwan 2025
,
v
sets the slope of the dispersive part of the band, and
\Theta(x)
is the step function.
The projected attractive interaction is

\hat{V}=-\frac{U}{2S}\sum_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\mathbf{q}}e^{\alpha q^{2}}\Lambda_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q}}\Lambda^{*}_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\mathbf{q}}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}-\mathbf{q}}\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k}},

(S69)

where
S
is the system area and the form factor is

\Lambda_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q}}=\exp[-i\beta(\mathbf{q}\times\mathbf{k})-\tfrac{1}{2}|\beta|q^{2}].

(S70)

The corresponding Berry curvature and quantum metric are
\Omega_{\mathbf{k}}=2\beta
and
g^{\mu\nu}_{\mathbf{k}}=|\beta|\,\delta_{\mu\nu}
, respectively, with
\beta=v_{F}^{2}/t_{1}^{2}

Bernevig and Kwan 2025
.

Reference
Li et al. 2025b
studied intravalley spin-polarized pairing in this model and, in the limit
v=\infty
and
\alpha=0
, obtained the analytic pair envelope function

\psi_{m}(\mathbf{k})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathcal{N}}}(k_{x}+ik_{y})^{m}e^{-|\beta|k^{2}},

(S71)

where
m
is an odd angular-momentum channel and
\mathcal{N}
is the normalization factor.
Figure S
S2
(b) shows the vortex structure of
\psi_{m}(\mathbf{k})
, whose phase winds by
m
around the origin.
We adopt the symmetric gauge for the Berry connection,
\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{k})=\beta\,(-k_{y},\,k_{x})
. The corresponding pair Berry connection is

\mathbf{A}_{P}(\mathbf{k})=\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{k})-\mathbf{A}(-\mathbf{k})=2\beta\,(-k_{y},\,k_{x}),

(S72)

and the pair Berry curvature is
\Omega_{P}=2\Omega=4\beta
.
As shown in Fig. S
S2
(b), the pair geometric dipole
\mathbf{d}_{G}(\mathbf{k})=\nabla_{\mathbf{k}}\phi(\mathbf{k})-\mathbf{A}_{P}(\mathbf{k})
forms a circulating texture around the vortex core, with circulation

\oint d\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{d}_{G}(\mathbf{k})=2\pi m-4\pi\beta k_{C}^{2}=2\pi m-\Phi_{P},

(S73)

where
\Phi_{P}=\Omega_{P}\pi k_{C}^{2}=4\pi\beta k_{C}^{2}
is the pair Berry flux enclosed by the flat-band region.

The amplitude contribution is independent of both
k_{C}
and
m

\displaystyle|\psi(\mathbf{k})|=k^{m}e^{-|\beta|k^{2}},\quad\partial_{k}|\psi(\mathbf{k})|=k^{m-1}e^{-|\beta|k^{2}}\bigl(m-2|\beta|k^{2}\bigr),

(S74)

so that

\displaystyle\xi^{2}_{\rm Amp}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\sum_{|\mathbf{k}|<k_{C}}\frac{1}{2}\Bigl[(\nabla_{\mathbf{k}}|\psi(\mathbf{k})|)^{2}-|\psi(\mathbf{k})|\,\nabla_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}|\psi(\mathbf{k})|\Bigr]

k_{C}
and

g^{\mu\nu}_{P}(\mathbf{k})=g^{\mu\nu}(\mathbf{k})+g^{\mu\nu}(-\mathbf{k})=2|\beta|\,\delta_{\mu\nu}.

\mathrm{Tr}\,g_{P}(\mathbf{k})=\delta_{\mu\nu}g^{\mu\nu}_{P}(\mathbf{k})=4|\beta|,

(S77)

and therefore

\displaystyle\xi^{2}_{\rm Metric}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\sum_{|\mathbf{k}|<k_{C}}|\psi(\mathbf{k})|^{2}\,\mathrm{Tr}\,g^{\mu\nu}_{P}(\mathbf{k})=4|\beta|.

(S78)

Thus,
\xi_{\rm Metric}=\sqrt{4|\beta|}

\xi_{\rm Amp}=\sqrt{2|\beta|}

(S79)
