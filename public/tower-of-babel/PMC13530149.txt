Artificial Intelligence based on behavioral recognition and optimization for low carbon fertilization in agriculture
Authors: Yan Hao, Yanmei Yuan, Hui Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Abstract Corresponding Author：Yanmei Yuan and Hui Liu, email: taiskjxy2020yym@163.com;liuhsdau@163.comThis study addresses the limitation of conventional fertilization monitoring methods that fail to capture dynamic nutrient trends for low-carbon precision management by proposing a data-driven approach for fertilization behavior recognition and low-carbon decision optimization based on multi-source agricultural time-series data. Traditional approaches lack temporal continuity and are unable to support real-time behavioral identification or carbon emission-constrained decision-making. The long short-term memory (LSTM) model is selected for its ability to process long-sequence heterogeneous sensor data and accurately recognize sparse fertilization events under environmental noise, while MILP is used to formulate a globally optimal fertilization plan that minimizes carbon emissions subject to crop nitrogen demand and environmental safety constraints. By deploying soil, meteorological, and crop growth sensors to establish an edge-cloud collaborative architecture, this method enables real-time collection and feature extraction of multi-source heterogeneous farmland data. A behavior recognition model combining a bidirectional LSTM network with an attention mechanism is developed to accurately annotate fertilization events and their temporal and spatial parameters. Carbon equivalent is calculated based on nitrogen dynamic balance and lifecycle carbon emission factors. Using carbon emission minimization as the objective function and crop nitrogen requirement and environmental safety as constraints, a mixed integer linear programming (MILP) model is constructed to generate a low-cost, high-yield fertilization plan. Results show that the system’s prediction error is 8.5% on day 30, and carbon emission intensity is reduced to 0.365 kgCO₂-eq/kg fertilizer, supporting the feasibility of this AI-driven decision framework in terms of behavior recognition accuracy and carbon emission control effectiveness under the tested plot conditions.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-57895-3.

Introduction

The continuous increase in fertilizer input in agricultural production has led to a series of environmental and ecological problems, including soil nutrient imbalance, eutrophication of surface runoff, and increased greenhouse gas emissions1,2. Traditional fertilization methods rely on farmers’ experience and judgment, lacking real-time quantitative monitoring of soil fertility, crop nutrient requirements, and environmental conditions. This results in a large gap between fertilizer use and actual crop uptake, low nitrogen use efficiency, and high carbon emission intensity3,4. Low-carbon, precision fertilization has become a key direction for sustainable agricultural development, but its practical implementation faces numerous technical and management difficulties5,6. Most existing fertilization monitoring methods rely on discrete sampling or manual recording, lacking timeliness and continuity, and are unable to reflect dynamic trends in farmland nutrients7,8. Data processing methods for identifying fertilization behavior rely on manually set rules, making them difficult to adapt to the changing patterns of different crop types and complex field environments9,10. Decision-making processes lack coupling with carbon emission assessments, and fertilization plan optimization often focuses solely on crop yield or fertilizer efficiency without constraining carbon emissions. This means that even if precise fertilization is achieved, it may not truly achieve low-carbon goals11,12. In this context, the core research problem is defined as data-driven fertilization behavior recognition and low-carbon decision optimization based on multi-source agricultural time-series data. Such a framework must handle long-sequence heterogeneous inputs, resolve sparse event detection under environmental noise, and embed carbon constraints into actionable plans, enabling precise and sustainable fertilization management13,14. Deep learning methods for agricultural monitoring can be categorized into unsupervised, self-supervised, and semi-supervised approaches. Unsupervised methods identify patterns in unlabeled sensor data but often lack precision in specific event detection due to the absence of ground truth15,16. Self-supervised approaches leverage inherent temporal structures to learn representations without explicit labels, demonstrating robustness to environmental noise but requiring extensive pre-training that may not align with agricultural data characteristics17,18. Semi-supervised techniques combine limited labeled data with abundant unlabeled data, effectively addressing sparse fertilization events under field noise while maintaining computational efficiency for edge deployment19,20. The proposed framework employs a semi-supervised approach with bidirectional LSTM and attention mechanism, specifically designed to handle the challenges of sparse event detection and environmental noise in farmland monitoring scenarios.

To address these issues, this paper proposes an integrated framework that couples multi-source intelligent sensing with a bidirectional long short-term memory network enhanced by an attention mechanism and mixed integer linear programming. The approach enables continuous, real-time identification of fertilization behaviors under field noise and sparse event conditions, establishes a life-cycle carbon emission assessment model grounded in nitrogen dynamic balance, and generates globally optimal low-carbon fertilization plans under crop nutrient and environmental constraints. The core contribution of this work is a behavior-decision coupled framework that integrates a bidirectional long short-term memory network with attention mechanism for fertilization event recognition and a mixed integer linear programming model for carbon-constrained fertilization optimization. The multi-source intelligent sensing and edge-cloud communication subsystems enable data acquisition but do not constitute the primary novelty. A carbon emission assessment based on nitrogen dynamic balance and lifecycle emission factors is established. Methodological novelty resides in the bidirectional LSTM attention mechanism for sparse fertilization event detection and the MILP formulation with carbon emission minimization constrained by dynamic nitrogen balance. Engineering integration couples edge-cloud architecture with incremental learning. Practical contribution is a feedback loop demonstrated through reduced prediction error and carbon emission intensity in field trials.

Related works

Research on multi-source intelligent sensing technology in the field of agricultural environmental monitoring is undergoing a profound transformation from experience-driven to data-driven. Radocaj D21 et al. systematically compared the application efficiency of traditional interpolation methods and modern machine learning algorithms in precision agriculture, combined field soil sampling data with multi-source remote sensing technology, and revealed the advantages of modern methods that integrate machine learning and high-resolution remote sensing data in improving fertilization prediction accuracy and preserving soil spatial heterogeneity. This provides an intelligent solution to the dilemma of high cost and low prediction accuracy of traditional soil sampling, and promotes the paradigm shift of precision fertilization from experience-driven to data-driven. Innovations in data processing methods have directly supported the construction and development of new monitoring systems22,23. Chen X et al.24 integrated unmanned aerial vehicle hyperspectral remote sensing, Geographic Information System (GIS) spatial analysis, and machine learning technology to build a soil nutrient dynamic monitoring and precision fertilization decision-making system, achieving real-time processing and continuous learning optimization of multi-source data. In field trials across multiple ecological zones, they significantly reduced fertilizer use and increased crop yields, effectively breaking through the static limitations of traditional soil nutrient assessment methods and providing intelligent solutions for sustainable agriculture. The in-depth mining and application of monitoring data has led to the emergence of more refined management strategies25,26. Toselli M27 et al. constructed a multi-level nutrient management model based on soil fertility monitoring and precision agriculture framework, integrating agronomic management monitoring and dynamic modeling analysis technology under soil climate conditions. They achieved precise optimization of nutrient distribution at different scales, including regions, farms, fields, and plots, effectively balancing crop growth needs with environmental sustainability, and providing a new paradigm for intelligent fertilization decision-making for agricultural production that balances economic benefits with reduced environmental impact. Existing studies generally ignore the actual impact of farmers’ behavior patterns on technology implementation, resulting in the real challenge of low adoption rate when the system is applied in the field.

The application of deep learning technology in the field of low-carbon agricultural decision-making has formed a multi-level research system from spatial planning to behavior recognition. Li S28 et al. constructed a land use change simulation framework by coupling Convolutional Neural Network (CNN)-LSTM and PLUS, and designed a multi-scenario simulation scheme by combining Markov chain and multi-objective planning model. They achieved accurate prediction and sustainability assessment of land use pattern in the cultivated land reserve resource area of Da’an City, Jilin Province, revealing the core advantages of sustainable development scenarios in the coordinated optimization of the three dimensions of ecosystem service value, agricultural production benefits, and carbon balance, and provided a new decision-making support paradigm for the scientific planning of cultivated land reserve resource areas that took into account both ecological security and food security. The accurate simulation results at the regional scale have laid a spatial decision-making foundation for low-carbon management of the entire agricultural industry chain29,30. Wang X et al.31 constructed a low-carbon supply chain management optimization framework for fresh agricultural products based on Recurrent Neural Network (RNN), integrating multi-source time series information such as historical sales data, meteorological conditions, and consumer behavior to achieve accurate demand forecasting and dynamic optimization of transportation routes. While reducing product loss rates, they effectively balanced supply chain efficiency and carbon emission intensity, providing a new intelligent management path for the agricultural product circulation field that takes into account both environmental sustainability and economic feasibility. Low-carbon optimization practices in the circulation link have further promoted innovation in precise decision-making technology at the source of production32,33. Xiang Z et al.34 quantitatively analyzed the differences in the impact of three types of perceived factors, namely economic risk, social risk, and psychological risk, on farmers’ fertilizer reduction decisions, revealing the dynamic regulatory mechanism of environmental attitudes between risk perception and behavioral choices. This clarifies the vulnerability of farmers’ decisions when facing economic risks and the key role of environmental cognition in risk offsetting. Current research provides theoretical breakthroughs for understanding the behavioral logic of agricultural actors in sustainable transformation, based on a risk-attitude interaction perspective35,36. As a well-known alternative, dynamic Bayesian networks require only a small number of training samples while achieving comparable predictive performance without overfitting for fertilization behavior prediction37. While breakthroughs have been made in technical architecture and decision-making models, the underlying contradiction between technological adaptability and behavioral inertia in farmers’ actual operational scenarios has been generally overlooked. The present framework distinguishes itself by coupling behavior recognition with carbon-constrained optimization under a unified feedback loop, moving beyond isolated application of existing techniques.

Intelligent sensing and behavioral intervention systems

Data collection layer

The proposed decision framework operates on a closed-loop architecture where deep learning and optimization modeling execute core inference and planning functions. Sensor data collection and edge preprocessing support these functions as enabling components, as illustrated in Fig. 1. This architecture handles long-sequence heterogeneous inputs and embeds carbon constraints into actionable plans enabling precise and sustainable fertilization management. Compared to single-sensor solutions, a multi-source heterogeneous sensor combination can comprehensively reflect the dynamic changes in the soil-crop-atmosphere continuum.

Multi-source intelligent farmland sensing and data processing system.

The sensor layout follows the spatial variation law of farmland and adopts a hierarchical gridding strategy.

Edge processing layer

Time synchronization between sensor nodes and edge computing devices is achieved through network time protocol combined with global positioning system timing.

High-frequency noise interference in the sensor signal is addressed through discrete wavelet transform denoising.

Missing data are addressed through an interpolation algorithm based on the spatiotemporal correlation of farmland environmental parameters.

Temporal features, frequency domain features, and vegetation index features are extracted from the preprocessed multi-source data.

After normalization, all features are subjected to dimensionality reduction using Principal Component Analysis (PCA) to eliminate multicollinearity between features and improve the computational efficiency and generalization of subsequent models. PCA performs eigenvalue decomposition on the covariance matrix of the normalized feature matrix. The eigenvalues λ₁≥λ₂≥ … ≥ λd and corresponding eigenvectors constitute the principal components. The cumulative variance explained by the first k components equals the sum of the first k eigenvalues divided by the total sum of all eigenvalues. The number of retained principal components meets a cumulative variance explained threshold of 95%.

Cloud processing layer

Fertilization behavior identification processes long-sequence heterogeneous sensor data under specific constraints. Temporal dependence spans multiple days as fertilization effects persist through subsequent irrigation and rainfall events. Noise magnitude varies with sensor drift, temperature fluctuations, and crop canopy development. Fertilization events occupy less than 5% of the total monitoring duration. The bidirectional LSTM architecture addresses these constraints through its gating mechanism. The forward LSTM layer accumulates pre-fertilization nutrient trends. The backward LSTM layer captures post-fertilization depletion patterns. The attention mechanism computes temporal importance weights, aggregating context only from time steps where parameter deviations exceed natural variability. The model hyperparameters were selected via logarithmic grid search on the validation set. Learning rate ranged 0.0001 to 0.01, batch size 16 to 64, weight decay 1e− 5 to 1e− 3, hidden units 64 to 256, and bidirectional LSTM layers one to three. The configuration achieving the highest validation F1 score used a learning rate of 0.001, batch size 32, weight decay 1e− 4, 128 hidden units, and two bidirectional LSTM layers. Alternative architectures including unidirectional LSTM, bidirectional LSTM without attention, and a Transformer decoder were trained under the identical search space. The BiLSTM‑Attention architecture outperformed these alternatives. The final model contains 287,432 trainable parameters. The average floating‑point operations per time step in forward inference are 1.87 × 10⁷ FLOPS, verified by field measurements on edge computing nodes to complete a single prediction task within 1.2 s, meeting real‑time requirements. Training monitored the loss difference between training and validation sets, automatically adjusting regularization strength when the difference exceeded a preset threshold.

The proposed BiLSTM attention model contains two bidirectional LSTM layers each with 128 hidden units, followed by a temporal attention layer and two task specific output layers. The tanh activation function applies to the LSTM cells and the softmax activation to the output layers. Layer normalization is inserted between the two LSTM layers. The loss function is categorical cross entropy for the main classification task and for the auxiliary fertilization amount prediction task, with a task weight coefficient lambda one of 0.7. Training proceeds for 200 epochs with an early stopping patience of 20 epochs based on validation loss. The performance metrics are accuracy, F1 score, and recall.

The model output layer generates the probability distribution of fertilization behavior categories and the prediction of fertilization amount intervals, which are converted into specific fertilization event parameters through the post-processing module to complete the accurate identification from time series features to fertilization behavior.

It visually demonstrates the inherent logic of how behavior identification results are transformed into low-carbon decision-making through nitrogen dynamic balance, and clarifies the technical implementation path for balancing carbon emission constraints with crop needs at the algorithmic level.

Optimizing low-carbon fertilization plans requires simultaneously meeting crop nutritional needs and environmental safety constraints, and minimizing carbon emissions. This presents a multi-objective constrained optimization problem. The study selects MILP as its optimization method. Compared to the heuristic genetic algorithm, MILP guarantees a globally optimal solution. Compared to nonlinear programming, MILP offers greater computational efficiency and is more suitable for real-time decision-making. Compared to multi-objective optimization methods, MILP, by prioritizing carbon emissions as the primary objective and other requirements as constraints, better aligns with the practical decision-making logic of agricultural management.

The optimization of low-carbon fertilization schemes builds a carbon emission assessment system based on the principle of nitrogen dynamic balance.

LSTM model outputs crop nitrogen demand and soil nitrogen supply values serving as input parameters for the MILP optimization model. The nitrogen dynamic balance equation defines relationships between soil nitrogen supply crop nitrogen uptake and fertilizer application. Nitrogen surplus expression equals the difference between total nitrogen input and crop nitrogen requirement. Total nitrogen input comprises soil mineralization nitrogen and fertilizer nitrogen application. Crop nitrogen requirement derives from LSTM predicted yield and growth stage parameters. This nitrogen surplus value enters the MILP objective function to calculate carbon emission equivalents. MILP constraints utilize LSTM predicted crop nitrogen demand to ensure fertilization plans meet growth needs. Integration ensures real-time sensing data directly influences optimization decisions through nitrogen balance calculation.

The carbon emission equivalent calculation adopts the life cycle assessment method, covering the entire process of fertilizer production, transportation, application, and farmland emissions. Comparative analysis of fertilization performance

To evaluate the performance of AI-based intelligent sensing technology in identifying farmers’ fertilization behavior under different farmland environmental conditions, the study conducted systematic tests in five typical farmland types covering three major crops: wheat, corn, and rice. LSTM and its variants were used to process time series data collected by multiple sensors. The Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (BiLSTM)-Attention model, a fusion of bidirectional LSTM and attention mechanisms, uses forward and backward LSTMs to capture the impact of historical and future states on the current moment, respectively, and dynamically aggregates key information through attention weights. CNN-LSTM combines the spatial feature extraction capability of CNN with the temporal modeling advantage of LSTM; traditional RNN is used as the basic temporal model for comparison; the rule-based method reflects the application of expert knowledge in fertilization behavior identification and farmers’ experience and judgment represent the common practices in current agricultural production. The accuracy metric reflects the model’s overall recognition performance. The F1 score balances precision and recall to assess the model’s overall performance under conditions of positive and negative sample imbalance. Recall specifically focuses on the ability to detect fertilization events, which is crucial for avoiding missed fertilization events. Qualitative analysis of attention distribution

The attention weight distribution revealed the model’s temporal focus. Within the thirty-minute window surrounding a fertilization event, attention weights concentrated on the abrupt slopes of soil ammonium nitrogen and nitrate nitrogen, accounting for 72% of the total time window weight. During non-fertilization periods, attention weights scattered evenly across time steps and feature dimensions without any dominant feature. The backward LSTM assigned higher weights to the descending segment of nitrate nitrogen within ten to fifteen minutes after the fertilization event ended, reflecting its constraint on post-application nutrient dynamics. The concatenation of forward and backward hidden states enabled the model to simultaneously utilize pre-application nutrient accumulation signals and post-application depletion signals, forming a bidirectional verification of fertilization timing and amount.

Discussion

The model’s recognition performance declines significantly in organic soil rice paddies, primarily due to the dramatic fluctuations in soil redox potential caused by flooding-drainage cycles, which rapidly transform nitrogen forms and exhibit nonstationary sensor signals. While the attention mechanism can capture sudden changes, it suffers from temporal drift in determining fertilization event boundaries under continuous disturbances. In sandy soil areas, short periods of heavy rainfall cause nutrient leaching, biasing carbon emission estimates based on nitrogen balance, reflecting the limited ability of existing models to represent nonlinear processes driven by extreme climate change. After three months of continuous high-load operation, edge computing nodes experience cumulative memory leaks, impacting real-time inference stability and indicating that the long-term robustness of the current system in resource-constrained environments needs to be improved. Human-computer interaction data shows that farmers tend to adopt system recommendations in the early stages of growth but rely more on empirical intervention during critical growth periods, suggesting a convergence tension between automated decision-making and traditional agronomic knowledge. Future research may decompose the framework into standalone studies on sensing hardware, optimization theory, and edge deployment. The current work remains focused on the integrated AI modeling and decision-making core.

Conclusions

Agricultural non-point source pollution and carbon emissions are becoming increasingly serious problems. Traditional fertilization management relies on experience-based judgment and lacks the ability to perceive and precisely control dynamic changes in the farmland environment in real time, making it difficult to achieve the synergistic goal of efficient resource utilization and environmental risk control. This study aims to construct a closed-loop management system that integrates multi-source intelligent sensing and artificial intelligence algorithms to achieve high-precision identification and low-carbon control of farmers’ fertilization behavior. The system deploys soil, meteorological, and crop growth sensors to establish an edge-cloud collaborative architecture and acquire multi-dimensional dynamic data of the farmland environment. Discrete wavelet transform is used for signal denoising and feature extraction, and a bidirectional long short-term memory network and attention mechanism model are combined to analyze changes in time-series parameters and accurately identify fertilization events. Based on the principle of nitrogen dynamic balance, a full life-cycle carbon emission assessment system is constructed. With carbon emission minimization as the objective function and crop nitrogen demand and environmental safety as constraints, a mixed integer linear programming model is established to generate optimization schemes. Experimental results show that after 30 days of system operation, the model prediction error decreased to 8.5%, and the low-carbon fertilization strategy controlled carbon emission intensity to 0.365 kgCO₂-eq/kg fertilizer, verifying the feasibility of this method in terms of behavior recognition accuracy and carbon emission reduction effectiveness. This verification is based on a limited data scale from the specific experimental plots. Current research relies on fixed sensor networks, and their applicability in mobile operation scenarios needs to be expanded. Future work should explore the integration of lightweight sensing modules and wireless ad hoc network technology to improve the system’s adaptability to complex agricultural operations.
