Nanofiber-like polar configurations enable ultrahigh energy storage in relaxor ferroelectrics via high-entropy design
Authors: Ning Liu, Aiwen Xie, Liqiang He, Ao Tian, Xiang Wu, Zehao Li, Ruzhong Zuo, Xiaofan Sun, Wenwen Wu, Fang Qi, Xia Fang, Zupei Yang, Zhen Wang, Shaoming Dong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Dielectric capacitors are highly promising for advanced power electronics due to their superior power density. However, their widespread application has been limited by inadequate energy storage performance. In this work, we propose a distinct strategy, centered on creating a nanofiber-like polar configuration, to achieve ultrahigh energy storage performance in relaxor ferroelectrics. This is realized through a high-entropy composition design in NaNbO3-based perovskites, where multiple cations with significant ferroelectric activity and ionic radius differences are introduced at both A- and B-sites. This approach effectively disrupts the long-range ferroelectric and antiferrodistortive orders simultaneously. The resulting nanofiber-like domain structure, which features several nanometers in length but only a few unit cells in width, exhibits a low-hysteresis and nearly linear polarization response to applied electric field (E). Consequently, an exceptional energy storage density (Wrec) of ~19.3 J cm−3, a high efficiency (η) of ~95.2%, and an impressive Wrec/E coefficient of 208 J kV mm−3 are simultaneously achieved in the NaNbO3-(Bi0.5Li0.5)(Ti0.8Zr0.1Sn0.1)O3 multilayer ceramic capacitors. These results underscore the immense potential of the nanofiber-like polar configuration strategy for developing high-performance dielectric energy storage materials.

Introduction

The relentless drive toward miniaturized energy-autonomous systems, coupled with the extreme demands of high-power electronics in applications such as ballistic missile defense, oil well drilling, and next-generation electric vehicles, has placed unprecedented requirements on energy storage components. These applications demand not only ultrahigh power density and rapid charge-discharge capabilities but also exceptional reliability under harsh mechanical and thermal conditions1–5. Dielectric capacitors, owing to their intrinsic fast charging/discharging kinetics and high voltage tolerance, have emerged as critical enablers in such scenarios. Among them, ceramic-based capacitors, particularly multilayer ceramic capacitors (MLCCs), stand out due to their excellent stability, large volumetric capacitance enabled by their multilayer structure, and reliable operation under high voltages6–8. This volumetric advantage allows MLCCs to achieve higher overall capacitance within a given footprint compared to ceramic-based thin film capacitors. The performance of MLCCs is largely governed by the properties of their dielectric ceramic layers. Consequently, considerable research efforts in recent years have been directed toward developing ceramic dielectric materials with superior energy storage characteristics2,6–13.

The energy storage performance of dielectric materials is primarily determined by their polarization-electric field (P-E) response. Based on the nature of P-E behavior, dielectric materials can be categorized into several types, among which antiferroelectrics (AFEs) and relaxor ferroelectrics (FEs) have shown great promise for energy storage applications due to their distinctive P-E characteristics3–5. However, each type faces inherent limitations. AFEs exhibit high maximum polarization (Pmax) driven by field-induced AFE-FE phase transitions, yet the associated large hysteresis from the first-order transition leads to low energy storage efficiency (η). In contrast, relaxor FEs display low hysteresis and thus high η owing to the dynamics of highly active polar nanoregions (PNRs), but their relatively low Pmax often restricts the recoverable energy density (Wrec). Therefore, achieving simultaneous optimization of Wrec and η remains challenging, and most current studies report a compromise between the two. In recent years, various strategies and concepts, such as multi-phase nanodomain coexistence2,6, relaxor AFE14,15, superparaelectric state16, high-entropy design17, supercritical state18, and dipole glass12, have been proposed to address the Wrec-η trade-off, leading to remarkable progress. Nevertheless, attaining an ultrahigh Wrec (>15 J cm−3) along with η exceeding 95% remains a bottleneck3–18. Moreover, many reported high-performance values are achieved under extremely high electric fields, which hinder practical device integration. In this context, achieving efficient energy storage per unit volume under low electric fields is a critical consideration for applications that demand miniaturization and system-level integration19. In parallel, the figure of merit Wrec/E—representing the energy storage capacity per unit applied field—has also been widely adopted in the literature to assess the field efficiency of dielectric materials20,21, as it provides complementary insight into material performance across different field ranges. Addressing these field-dependent performance metrics is therefore essential for translating material-level innovations into viable device platforms, where MLCCs offer a particularly compelling architecture. Their multilayer structure enables high volumetric energy storage without relying solely on extreme operating fields, directly tackling the integration challenges that often limit practical deployment.

Conventional design strategies for relaxor FEs, which involve disrupting long-range ferroelectric order to generate PNRs, inevitably face a trade-off between hysteresis and polarization. Furthermore, the high dynamics of PNRs can induce premature polarization saturation. A recent phase-field simulation study on relaxor FE energy storage materials suggests that the formation of nano-columnar domains can simultaneously yield low hysteresis and high polarization19; however, this prediction has not yet been experimentally verified. In this work, we propose a new strategy to resolve the conflict between Wrec and η by constructing a nanofiber-like polar configuration within a relaxor FE matrix, as shown in Fig. 1. We selected NaNbO3 (NN), known for its coexisting AFE and FE phases21,22, as the base matrix. The incorporation of Bi0.5Li0.5TiO3 (BLT) stabilizes the FE phase, while its small tolerance factor enhances antiferrodistortive behavior, promoting stripe-like domains, and the mixed valence and ionic radii of Bi3+, Li+, and Ti4+ effectively disrupt long-range FE order. Further substitution of Zr4+ and Sn4+ at the B-site increases configurational entropy, refining the domains into a nanofiber-like configuration that differs from conventional PNRs. Results and discussion

To gain theoretical insight into the impact of configurational entropy on domain morphology and polarization behavior, we first performed phase-field simulations on the NaNbO3-based system with increasing entropy levels. As shown in Fig. 1a, pure NaNbO3 exhibits long-range ordered striped antiferroelectric domains, characteristic of its pristine state, which yields a square polarization–electric field (P–E) loop with large hysteresis22. With moderate entropy increase (0.7NN-0.3(Bi0.5Li0.5)TiO3, abbreviated as NN-BLT), the long-range order is partially disrupted, resulting in a more complex and fragmented domain structure accompanied by a slimmer P–E loop. Remarkably, at the highest entropy level (0.7NN-0.3(Bi0.5Li0.5)(Ti0.8Zr0.1Sn0.1)O3, abbreviated as NN-BLTSZ), the system spontaneously reorganizes into anisotropic, nanofiber-like polar domains. This configuration exhibits a nearly hysteresis-free, linear P–E response while maintaining high polarization, suggesting an optimal balance between local disorder and cooperative polarization.

The emergence of such nanofiber-like domains can be understood in terms of the unique energy landscape of a high-entropy state. In this regime, the gradient energy term becomes dominant due to the extreme compositional fluctuations. The polarization vectors along the short axis of the fibers are highly constrained, forming quasi-one-dimensional polarization channels that enable continuous polarization rotation with a low energy barrier, a fundamentally different mechanism from the nucleation-and-growth mechanism in conventional polar nanoregions. However, the atomic-scale structural features underpinning this simulated behavior, including the precise cation arrangements, oxygen octahedral tilting, and the real-space morphology of these domains, require direct experimental validation. In the following sections, we employ multiscale characterization techniques, from X-ray diffraction to atomically resolved scanning transmission electron microscopy (STEM), to experimentally verify the existence of nanofiber-like domains and elucidate their structural origin in our high-entropy NN-BLTSZ system.

The ceramics with compositions of NN, 0.7NN-0.3(Bi0.5Li0.5)TiO3 (NN-BLT), 0.7NN-0.3(Bi0.5Li0.5)(Ti0.8Zr0.1Sn0.1)O3 (NN-BLTSZ) were designed and synthesized via a conventional solid-state reaction route. X-ray diffraction analysis (Fig. S1) confirms the formation of a single-phase perovskite structure without any secondary phases for all compositions. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images (Fig. S2) reveal densely packed microstructures. For the pure NN sample, clearly defined grain boundaries are observed, whereas the NN-BLT sample exhibits relatively blurred grain boundaries, likely due to the incorporation of BLT components that introduce local compositional heterogeneity and disrupt grain boundary contrast. The domain morphology evolution was investigated using transmission electron microscopy (TEM) and piezoresponse force microscopy (PFM), as illustrated in Fig. 2. In NN ceramics, the coexistence of AFE orthorhombic P and FE orthorhombic Q phases is confirmed by striped macroscopic domains, along with 1/4 and 1/2 superlattice spots in selected-area electron diffraction (SAED) patterns (Fig. 2a), consistent with earlier reports20–22. In contrast, NN-BLT exhibits only the FE orthorhombic Q phase, characterized by nanoscale stripe-like domains with 1/2 superlattice spots (Fig. 2b), indicating that BLT incorporation stabilizes the FE phase21. Further substitution of Sn4+ and Zr4+ at the B-site enhances local structural inhomogeneity, leading to additional domain refinement and the emergence of nanofiber-like domain configurations in NN-BLTSZ (Fig. 2c). Moreover, TEM and SAED analyses also confirm that the NN-BLTSZ composition maintains a single-phase perovskite structure without any detectable secondary phases or local phase separation, indicating that the high-entropy design results in a homogeneous solid solution. Domain dynamics were probed via in-situ PFM (Fig. 2d-f). Under a low electric field (10 V), NN shows pronounced amplitude signals corresponding to FE macrodomains, whereas NN-BLT and NN-BLTSZ exhibit a featureless response indicative of polar nanodomains, corroborating the relaxor FE character. Temperature-dependent dielectric spectroscopy (Fig. S3) further supports their relaxor FE behaviors. At higher voltages (50 V), NN-BLT develops micron-scale FE domains with strong piezoelectric activity, while NN-BLTSZ maintains finer domain structures. This suggests a progressive suppression of the electric-field-induced transition from short-range to long-range polar order from NN-BLT to NN-BLTSZ compositions. Correspondingly, the P-E loops become progressively slimmer from NN to NN-BLT to NN-BLTSZ, as shown in Fig. S4. To ensure a fair comparison, NN-BLT was measured up to its maximum applicable field of 35 kV mm−1, under which its η remains below 80% and decreases with increasing field. In contrast, NN-BLTSZ maintains an efficiency above 90% even at fields up to 60 kV mm−1, clearly demonstrating the benefit of the high-entropy design in reducing hysteresis. We plot the calculated configurational entropy (ΔSconf) against the measured Wrec and η across our composition series, as shown in the Table S1. A clear positive correlation is observed, where the compositions with higher ΔSconf consistently trend toward superior Wrec and η. Concurrently, the current density-electric field (J-E) curves exhibit broadened and suppressed current peaks. This fiber-like nature of the nanodomains is as opposed to the commonly observed equiaxed PNRs. Typically, the disruption of long-range FE order favors the formation of spherically shaped PNRs to minimize the system’s energy29,30. The formation of the observed fiber-like domains, which are only several unit cells in width and extend for tens of nanometers, is intimately linked to the patterns of oxygen octahedral tilting. The oxygen octahedral tilts are calculated from the iDPC image and the corresponding tilt angle is shown in the schematic diagram (Fig. 3f). The oxygen octahedral tilt mapping (Fig. 3e) reveals a short-range-ordered tilting pattern with a modulation period nearly twice (showed in the enlarged aera 1 and aera 2) that of the polar modulation, yet without developing long-range correlation, which is also visibly shown in the oxygen octahedral tilt angle as a function of distance along [0-10]c direction (Fig. 3h). This specific structural manifestation is attributed to the low tolerance factor (τ) inherent to the high-entropy composition design. As listed in Table S1, the τ decreases from 0.967 for NN to 0.955 for NN-BLT and further to 0.953 for NN-BLTSZ. The reduced τfavors the preservation and enhancement of oxygen octahedral tilting31,32, while the high-entropy effect subsequently disrupts the coherence of these tilts, preventing long-range ordering26. This relaxor FE system, characterized by its nanofiber-like domains, is fundamentally distinct from conventional relaxors with quasi-spherical PNRs. It concurrently maintains high spontaneous polarization and short-range ferroic order, which is instrumental in achieving the concurrent high polarization and low hysteresis observed in the macroscopic P-E loops. Furthermore, the local high orientational alignment of these fiber-like domains is believed to be responsible for the delayed polarization saturation under increasing electric fields.

The MLCCs fabricated using the NN-BLTSZ composition demonstrate outstanding structural integrity and high functional performance. Cross-sectional SEM imaging (Fig. 4a) reveals a well-defined, homogeneous laminated structure consisting of four densely packed dielectric layers, each with an average thickness of approximately 13 μm, alternated with continuous and coherent internal electrodes. Energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) elemental mapping confirms minimal interdiffusion across the dielectric-electrode interfaces, indicating excellent chemical compatibility and interfacial stability. Furthermore, the dielectric layers exhibit very low porosity and are free from macroscopic defects such as cracks or delamination, with interlayer thickness variation tightly controlled within ±0.7 μm. P-E loop measurements (Fig. 4b) underscore the superior energy storage characteristics of the NN-BLTSZ MLCCs. The materials exhibit slim hysteresis loops with strongly enhanced Pmax of ~52.4 μC cm-2 at 93 kV mm-1 and ultralow remnant polarization (Pr < 0.5 μC cm-2), resulting in hysteresis losses accounting for less than 5% of the total energy input. This behavior can be attributed to the presence of localized nanofiber-like domains, which effectively restrict long-range ferroelectric ordering under high electric fields while facilitating rapid and reversible polarization switching. As a result, the NN-BLTSZ MLCCs achieve a remarkable Wrec of 19.3 J cm-3 and a high η of 95.2% under an applied electric field of 93 kV mm-1 (Fig. 4d). The stability of energy storage performance is essential for ensuring the reliable operation of NN-BLTZS MLCCs41–44. As illustrated in Fig. 5a, P-E hysteresis loops were measured over a frequency range of 1-100 Hz. The MLCCs exhibit remarkable frequency stability, with a Wrec of 7.7 J cm-3 varying within only ±1%, and η maintained above 96.7% with less than 2% fluctuation across the tested frequencies (Fig. 5c). In addition, the P-E loops show minimal temperature dependence over a broad temperature span from 25 oC to 200 oC, highlighting excellent thermal stability. Both hysteresis loss and Pmax are well retained, leading to negligible variations in energy storage parameters (ΔWrec < 3%, Δη < 2%), as summarized in Fig. 5b and e. The fatigue endurance was also evaluated by subjecting the MLCCs to repeated unipolar cycling. As shown in Fig. 5c, the P-E loop remains nearly unchanged even after 103 fatigue cycles. After 104 cycles, a slight decrease in Pmax is observed, resulting in less than 5% reduction in Wrec, which is within an acceptable range for practical applications. This indicates that the nanofiber-like polar configuration maintains its structural integrity under repeated electrical cycling. The charge-discharge performance further underscores the application potential of these capacitors. Under external electric fields ranging from 30 to 50 kV mm-1, the NN-BLTZS MLCCs display well-defined underdamped discharge waveforms (Fig. 5e). The power density (PD) increases with applied electric field, reaching an ultrahigh value of ≈167.5 MW cm-3 at 50 kV mm-1 (inset of Fig. 5e). In the overdamped discharge mode with a fixed load resistance of 150 Ω, stable oscillatory waveforms are observed across the same field range. As shown in Fig. 5f, a discharge energy density (WD) of ≈4.7 J cm-3 is achieved at 50 kV mm-1, with 90% of the stored energy released within an extremely short duration of ≈0.34 μs. This rapid discharge behavior is attributed to the fast polarization-field response42, consistent with the frequency-insensitive P-E loops observed in Fig. 5a. In summary, we have demonstrated an effective strategy to overcome the long-standing trade-off between Wrec and η in dielectrics by constructing a nanofiber-like polar configuration via a rationally designed high-entropy system. Multiscale structural analyses reveal that the high-entropy composition disrupts the long-range and antiferrodistortive orders of the NaNbO3 matrix, spontaneously forming anisotropic nanodomains with lengths of several nanometers and widths of merely a few unit cells, where a morphology fundamentally distinct from conventional polar nanoregions. This unique, constrained geometry enables a nearly anhysteretic and linear polarization response, yielding record-breaking MLCC performance: an ultrahigh Wrec of ~19.3 J cm-3, a superior η of ~95.2%, and an impressive Wrec/E coefficient of 208 J kV·mm-3 in MLCCs, outperforming most reported lead-free dielectrics. Beyond these metrics, our work establishes a new paradigm of entropy-mediated nanofiber-like texture engineering, offering a viable pathway to tailor dielectrics for demanding real-time applications such as extreme-environment power electronics, high-frequency traction inverters, and next-generation grid infrastructure, while potentially extending to other functional material systems.

Methods

Fabrication of bulk ceramics and MLCCs

The perovskite powders (NN, NN-BLT, and NN-BLTSZ) were prepared through a conventional solid-state reaction using high-purity precursors (>99%): Na2CO3, Nb2O5, Li2CO3, Bi2O3, TiO2, ZrO2 and SnO2. After weighing, the raw materials were thoroughly mixed and ball-milled in ethanol with zirconia media for 6 h. The dried mixtures were then calcined at 900°C for 8 h in covered alumina crucibles. To produce bulk ceramics, the calcined powders were pressed into pellets. To minimize sodium volatilization during sintering, a layer of powder with the same composition as the pellet was placed on top of each sample. The pellets were then sintered in air at optimized temperatures: 1350 °C for pure NN, 1100 °C for NN-BLT, and 1150 °C for NN-BLTSZ, all for 2 h in sealed crucibles.

The fabrication process for MLCCs began with dispersing the calcined NN-BLTSZ powder in a solvent containing a dispersant, followed by ball milling for 12 h. Subsequently, binders and plasticizers were introduced, and the mixture was milled for another 12 h. After filtration and de-airing, the slurry was tape-cast into green sheets. Ag/Pd (70:30) electrode paste was screen-printed onto the sheets, which were then stacked, laminated at 50 °C for 5 min, and warm isostatically pressed at 50 MPa and 55 °C to improve green density. Thermal debinding was performed at 600 °C for 2 h with a ramping rate of 0.5 °C/min, followed by sintering at 1210 °C for 2 h to obtain the final MLCCs.

The electric energy felec can be expressed as:5\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${f}_{elec}=-\frac{1}{2}{E}_{i}{P}_{i}-\frac{1}{2}{E}_{i,depol}\overline{{P}_{i}}$$\end{document}felec=−12EiPi−12Ei,depolPi¯where Ei represents the inhomogeneous electric field caused by dipole-dipole interactions, Ei,depol is the average depolarization field. \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\overline{{P}_{i}}$$\end{document}Pi¯ is the average polarization.

The temporal evolution of the polarization field can be calculated by solving the time-dependent Ginzburg-Landau (TDGL) equation:7\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\frac{d{P}_{i}(x,t)}{dt}=-M\frac{\delta F}{\delta {P}_{i}(X,t)},i=1,2,3$$\end{document}dPi(x,t)dt=−MδFδPi(X,t),i=1,2,3where M is the kinetic coefficient related to the domain mobility, t is time and i is the variant of polarization. The simulations were carried out in two dimensions with cell sizes 128Δx×128Δy (Δx and Δy are the number of grid points, which are equal to 2 nm) with periodic boundary conditions.

Structure characterizations

XRD data were acquired using a diffractometer (D/Max-RB, Rigaku, Japan). Grain morphology of the ceramics was examined by field-emission SEM (SU8020, JEOL, Japan). Domain evolution under bias voltage was studied via PFM (Asylum Research MFP-3D Origin + , Oxford Instruments, UK). Raman spectra were recorded on well-polished samples with a Raman spectrometer (LabRam HR Evolution, HORIBA JOBIN YVON, France). A probe-corrected Thermo Scientific™ Spectra 300 S/TEM, equipped with a Super-X Detector, was used for aberration-corrected scanning transmission electron microscope (STEM) imaging. The operating conditions were as follows: accelerating voltage=300 kV, convergence semi-angle=30 mrad. HAADF images were obtained at the atomic scale using a detector semi-angle range of 64−200 mrad. The integrated differential phase contrast (iDPC) images were collected with a segmented four‑quadrant DF4 detector. The atomic column positions at picometer precision fitting were operated by MATLAB code47,48. Additional domain-morphology observations and SAED were conducted on a JEOL JEM-F200 microscope at 200 kV. Specimens for (S)TEM analysis were prepared by mechanical thinning followed by final Ar+ ion-milling using a Gatan PIPS II system.

Electric property measurements

Dielectric properties as functions of temperature and frequency were measured with an LCR meter (E4980A, Agilent Technologies, USA) coupled with a high/low-temperature probing stage (HCT1821, Tongguo Technology, China). P-E hysteresis loops were obtained using a FE test system (TF Analyzer 2000E, aixACCT Systems GmbH, Germany). Charge-discharge characteristics were evaluated on a dedicated energy-storage testing platform (CFD-003, Tongguo Technology, China).
