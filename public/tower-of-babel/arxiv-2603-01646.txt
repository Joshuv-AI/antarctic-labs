Exact controllability of two-dimensional hydroelastic waves
Lizhe Wan, Jiaqi Yang

Abstract
. We prove the exact controllability of two-dimensional hydroelastic waves in the periodic setting. We show that if the initial data and the final data are small, for exterior pressure whose support is any non-empty open set ω \omega , the two-dimensional hydroelastic wave system is exactly controllable in arbitrary short time.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
76B15, 74F10, 76B07

1.
Introduction

We consider two-dimensional irrotational, inviscid and incompressible fluid, with the top surface being in contact with a frictionless thin elastic sheet.
The fluid occupies a time-dependent domain
\Omega_{t}\subseteq\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}
with a free periodic upper boundary
\Gamma_{t}
and a fixed flat lower boundary
\Gamma_{b}=\{(x,y)|y=-h\}
(
\Gamma_{b}
is empty in the infinite depth case).
Denoting the fluid velocity by
(u(t,x,y),v(t,x,y))
, the pressure by
p(t,x,y)
, the equations inside the domain
\Omega_{t}
are given by the following incompressible irrotational Euler equations:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}u_{t}+uu_{x}+vu_{y}=-p_{x},&\\
v_{t}+uv_{x}+vv_{y}=-g-p_{y},&\\
u_{x}+v_{y}=0,\quad u_{y}-v_{x}=0.&\end{array}\right.

On the boundary
\Gamma_{t}
, we assume no surface tension.
Instead, the dynamic boundary condition involves a nonlinear elastic restoring force together with an exterior pressure
P_{ext}
,

p=-\sigma\Delta_{\Gamma_{t}}\kappa-\frac{\sigma}{2}\kappa^{3}+P_{ext}\quad\text{on }\Gamma_{t},

and the kinematic boundary condition is

\partial_{t}+(u,v)\cdot\nabla_{x,y}\text{ is tangent to }\Gamma_{t}.

Here
\kappa
is the mean curvature of the surface
\Gamma_{t}
,
g>0
represents the gravitational acceleration, and
\sigma>0
is the coefficient of flexural rigidity.
In the finite depth case we also impose a boundary condition at the bottom:

v=0\quad\text{on }\Gamma_{b}.

This free boundary problem, commonly referred to as the two-dimensional
hydroelastic wave
system, was first introduced by Toland
[
34
]
in the study of steady periodic waves.
The Cauchy problem of hydroelastic waves was studied by Ambrose and Siegel
[
8
]
, Ambrose and Liu
[
28
]
, as well as Wang and Yang
[
37
,
38
]
.
More recently, a low-regularity well-posedness result was established in
[
35
]
.
In addition, authors
[
36
]
proved the well-posedness of the Muskat problem with an elastic interface. For results on solitary waves in hydroelastic waves, we refer to the works of Groves
et al.

[
1
,
23
]
.

In this paper, we study the internal controllability of two-dimensional hydroelastic waves.
The problem can be written in the Zakharov-Craig-Sulem formulation.
Suppose the fluid domain
\Omega_{t}
at time
t
can be represented by

\Omega_{t}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R};-h<y<\eta(t,x)\},

where
\eta(t,x):\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}_{x}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
parametrizes the free surface
\Gamma_{t}
, and
h
is the depth of the domain (which may also be
\infty
in the case of infinite depth).
Without loss of generality, we assume that
\eta
has zero mean.
We define the velocity potential
\phi
such that
(u,v)=\nabla\phi
, where
\phi
solves the equation

\Delta_{x,y}\phi=0,\quad\partial_{t}\phi+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla_{x,y}\phi|^{2}+gy=p,\quad\partial_{y}\phi|_{y=-h}=0.

By the classical theory of elliptic equations, the problem reduces to the evolution of the velocity potential on the boundary
\Gamma_{t}
.
Let
\psi(t,x)=\phi(t,x,\eta(t,x))
denote the trace of the velocity potential on the free surface.
The system can then be reformulated as:

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\eta_{t}=G(\eta)\psi\,,\\
\psi_{t}+g\eta+\frac{1}{2}\psi_{x}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{(\eta_{x}\psi_{x}-G(\eta)\psi)^{2}}{1+\eta^{2}_{x}}+\sigma\mathbf{E}(\eta)=P_{ext}\,,\end{cases}

where
G(\eta)
is the Dirichlet–Neumann operator defined by

G(\eta)\psi:=\sqrt{1+\eta_{x}^{2}}\phi_{n}|_{y=\eta(t,x)}=(\phi_{y}-\eta_{x}\phi_{x})|_{y=\eta(t,x)}\,,

and the elastic term
\mathbf{E}(\eta)
is given by

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}(\eta):=

\displaystyle\sigma\left\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\eta_{x}^{2}}}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\eta_{x}^{2}}}\left(\frac{\eta_{xx}}{(1+\eta_{x}^{2})^{3/2}}\right)_{x}\right]_{x}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\eta_{xx}}{(1+\eta_{x}^{2})^{3/2}}\right)^{3}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma\left\{\left(\frac{\eta_{xx}}{(1+\eta^{2}_{x})^{\frac{5}{2}}}\right)_{xx}+\frac{5}{2}\left(\frac{\eta_{x}\eta_{xx}^{2}}{(1+\eta^{2}_{x})^{\frac{7}{2}}}\right)_{x}\right\}.

The hydroelastic wave system is a special kind of water wave problem with nonlinear elastic boundary condition.
The control theory for related water wave models is by now well developed, and we briefly recall the relevant results.
Reid & Russell
[
31
]
and Reid
[
32
,
33
]
studied the controllability of linearized water wave equations at the origin.
Later, Alazard, Baldi and Han-Kwan showed in
[
3
]
the controllability of the full gravity-capillary water wave system in two space dimensions using paradifferential calculus and an Ingham type inequality.
Zhu in
[
39
]
extended their result in higher dimensions using a different semiclassical approach.
Alazard also proved the boundary observability of gravity water waves in two and three dimensions using the multiplier method
[
6
]
.
In addition, Alazard obtained the stabilization results for both gravity water waves
[
7
]
and gravity-capillary water waves
[
5
]
.

Before stating the controllability result of the system (
1.1
), we first briefly examine the control theory for the linearized hydroelastic waves around the null solution.
The system is given by

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\partial_{t}\eta=G(0)\psi,&\\
\partial_{t}\psi+g\eta+\sigma\partial_{x}^{4}\eta=P_{ext},&\end{array}\right.

where
G(0)=|D|tanh(h|D|)
(
G(0)=|D|
in the infinite depth case).
When
g=P_{ext}=0
and
h=+\infty
, the linear system (
1.3
) is well-posed in
H^{s+\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{T})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
.
We then define the product Sobolev space

{\mathcal{H}}^{s}:=H^{s+\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{T})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{T}).

The inverse
G(0)^{-1}
is well-defined for periodic functions with zero mean.
Introducing the linear operator

L:=((g+\sigma\partial_{x}^{4})G(0))^{\frac{1}{2}},

then
u=\psi-iLG(0)^{-1}\eta
solves the linear dispersive equation

(1.4)

(\partial_{t}+iL)u=P_{ext}.

From Section
4.1
of Micu and Zuazua
[
29
]
, we have the following
Ingham inequality
for a variant of (
1.4
).

Lemma 1.1

(
[
29
]
)
.

For every
T>0
, there exists a positive constant
C_{1}(T)
such that, for all
(w_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{C})
,
m\geq c
for some constant
c>0
, one has the following Ingham inequality:
Let
\mathfrak{t}(\xi)
be a symbol that is defined in (
4.8
), then

\int_{0}^{T}\Big|\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}w_{n}e^{-i\mathfrak{t}(n)t}\Big|^{2}dt\geq C_{1}(T)\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}|w_{n}|^{2}.

The exact controllability of (
1.4
) can be established by the above Ingham inequality and the
Hilbert Uniqueness Method
(HUM).

Compared to the linearized system around the null solution, the main difficulties in proving the controllability of the full hydroelastic waves are:

(1)

The hydroelastic wave system is a quasi-linear system rather than a single dispersive equation with constant coefficients.
One cannot expect a simple step of symmetrization that turns (
1.1
) into (
1.4
).

(2)

The linearized system (
1.3
) does not have the sub-leading term of order
\frac{3}{2}
and first-order transport term after symmetrization.

(3)

The classical Ingham inequality and harmonic analysis method only work for the linear dispersive equation with constant coefficients.

In this paper, we overcome the above difficulties and prove the exact controllability of the full 2D hydroelastic waves.

1.1.
The main result

Our main result asserts that, the hydroelastic wave system (
1.1
) is exactly controllable in arbitrary short time.
More precisely, we have the following result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
T>0
be an arbitrary (possibly small) time and let
\omega\subset{\mathbb{T}}
be a non-empty open set.
There exist a positive universal constant
s_{*}
, and a small enough positive constant
\delta_{*}
depending on
T
and
\omega
such that if
(\eta_{in},\psi_{in}),(\eta_{end},\psi_{end})\in{\mathcal{H}}^{s_{*}}(\mathbb{T};\mathbb{R})
with

\|(\eta_{in},\psi_{in})\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{s_{*}}}+\|(\eta_{end},\psi_{end})\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{s_{*}}}<\delta_{*}\,,

then there exists an exterior pressure
P_{ext}(t,x)
supported on
[0,T]\times\omega
with the following control properties:

(1)

The hydroelastic wave system (
1.1
) admits a unique solution

(\eta,\psi)\in C([0,T];{\mathcal{H}}^{s_{*}})\cap C^{1}([0,T];{\mathcal{H}}^{s_{*}-\frac{5}{2}})\cap C^{2}([0,T];{\mathcal{H}}^{s_{*}-5}),

and
(\eta,\psi)|_{t=T}=(\eta_{end},\psi_{end})
.

(2)

P_{ext}(t,x)
is in the function space
C([0,T];H^{s_{*}})\cap C^{1}([0,T];H^{s_{*}-\frac{5}{2}})\cap C^{2}([0,T];H^{s_{*}-5})
, and satisfies the control estimates

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{2}\left(\|P_{ext}\|_{C^{k}([0,T];H^{s_{*}-\frac{5}{2}k})}+\|(\eta,\psi)\|_{C^{k}([0,T];{\mathcal{H}}^{s_{*}-\frac{5}{2}k})}\right)\lesssim\|(\eta_{in},\psi_{in})\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{s_{*}}}+\|(\eta_{end},\psi_{end})\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{s_{*}}}.

Although one could in principle adapt the strategy of
[
3
]
to establish null controllability for the system (
1.1
), from which exact controllability would follow, we instead employ a direct Nash-Moser Hörmander theorem approach, developed by Baldi, Haus and Montalto
[
13
]
in the context of Hamiltonian nonlinear Schrödinger (NLS) equations.
As observed in
[
35
]
, the system (
1.1
) can be rewritten as a quasilinear paradifferential equation of order
\frac{5}{2}
.
Compared to gravity–capillary water waves of order
\frac{5}{2}
, the symbolic calculus and symmetrization for hydroelastic waves are substantially more involved and present additional technical challenges.
The Nash–Moser–Hörmander approach avoids a direct reliance on paradifferential formulations and circumvents the need for a full paradifferential symbolic calculus.

The small-data assumption in Theorem
1.2
is inherent to the quasilinear nature of the problem.
We expect that a suitable stabilization result for two-dimensional hydroelastic waves would allow the extension of controllability to large data, following the argument in Section 2.2 of
[
5
]
.

1.2.
Ideas of the proof

We now briefly outline the method for proving the controllability of 2D hydroelastic waves.
We rewrite the system (
1.1
) as

(1.5)

\Phi(\mathbf{u},f):=\begin{pmatrix}P_{1}(\mathbf{u})\\
P_{2}(\mathbf{u})-\chi_{\omega}f\\
\mathbf{u}(0)\\
\mathbf{u}(T)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
0\\
\mathbf{u}_{in}\\
\mathbf{u}_{end}\end{pmatrix},

where
\mathbf{u}=(\eta,\psi)
,
P_{ext}=\chi_{\omega}f
, and

\displaystyle P_{1}(\mathbf{u})=\eta_{t}-G(\eta)\psi\,,

\displaystyle P_{2}(\mathbf{u})=\psi_{t}+g\eta+\frac{1}{2}\psi_{x}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{(\eta_{x}\psi_{x}-G(\eta)\psi)^{2}}{1+\eta^{2}_{x}}+\sigma\mathbf{E}(\eta).

The linearized operator
\Phi^{\prime}(\mathbf{u},f)[\tilde{\mathbf{u}},\tilde{f}]
of
\Phi(\mathbf{u},f)
at
(\mathbf{u},f)
in the direction
(\tilde{\mathbf{u}},\tilde{f})
is

(1.6)

\displaystyle\Phi^{\prime}(\mathbf{u},f)[\tilde{\mathbf{u}},\tilde{f}]=\begin{pmatrix}P_{1}^{\prime}(\mathbf{u})[\tilde{\mathbf{u}}]\\
P_{2}^{\prime}(\mathbf{u})[\tilde{\mathbf{u}}]-\chi_{\omega}\tilde{f}\\
\tilde{\mathbf{u}}(0)\\
\tilde{\mathbf{u}}(T)\end{pmatrix}.

We first obtain the existence of a right inverse of the linearized operator.
More precisely, we prove that, given any
(\mathbf{u},f)
and any
\mathbf{g}:=(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3},g_{4})^{T}
in suitable function spaces, there exist
(\tilde{\mathbf{u}},\tilde{f})
such that

(1.7)

\Phi^{\prime}(\mathbf{u},f)[\tilde{\mathbf{u}},\tilde{f}]=\mathbf{g}.

Moreover, we have to obtain appropriate estimates of
(\tilde{\mathbf{u}},\tilde{f})
in terms of
\mathbf{u},f,\mathbf{g}
.

To obtain the desired existence of the linearized control, we will first need to reduce and symmetrize the operator matrix
\mathbf{P}^{\prime}(\mathbf{u})=(P_{1}^{\prime}(\mathbf{u}),P_{2}^{\prime}(\mathbf{u}))^{T}
in (
2.5
) to differential operators with constant coefficients.
This is carried out in the following steps:

(1)

Conjugating
\mathbf{P}^{\prime}(\mathbf{u})
by the matrix
\mathcal{Z}
defined in (
2.8
), so that by (
2.6
), one has
\mathcal{L}_{0}=\mathcal{Z}^{-1}\mathbf{P}^{\prime}(\mathbf{u})\mathcal{Z}
.

(2)

Conjugating the matrix
\mathcal{L}_{0}
by the transformation
\mathcal{B}
defined in (
3.1
), so that the matrix
\mathcal{L}_{0}
is reduced to
\mathcal{L}_{1}
in (
3.3
).

(3)

Conjugating the matrix
\mathcal{L}_{1}
by the transformation
\mathcal{A}
defined in (
3.6
), so that the matrix
\mathcal{L}_{1}
is reduced to
\mathcal{L}_{2}
in (
3.7
).

(4)

Choosing matrices
P,Q
in (
3.14
), then the matrix
\mathcal{L}_{2}
is reduced to
\mathcal{L}_{3}
in (
3.15
), which has constant coefficients at the leading
\frac{5}{2}
order terms.

(5)

Conjugating the matrix
\mathcal{L}_{3}
by the matrix operator
\mathcal{S}
defined in (
3.18
), so that the matrix
\mathcal{L}_{4}
is symmetrized at the leading
\frac{5}{2}

(6)

Conjugating the matrix
\mathcal{L}_{4}
by the matrix
M
defined in (
3.22
), so that the matrix
\mathcal{L}_{4}
is reduced to an anti-symmetric matrix
\mathcal{L}_{5}
up to order zero.
This also eliminates the sub-leading terms of order
\frac{3}{2}
.

(7)

Performing a translation of space variable
\mathcal{T}
defined in (
4.1
) to eliminate frequency zero component of order
1

The matrix
\mathcal{L}_{5}
is further reduced to
\mathcal{L}_{6}
.

(8)

Conjugating the matrix
\mathcal{L}_{6}
by a unitary matrix
\mathcal{O}
defined in (
4.4
), so that the matrix
\mathcal{L}_{6}
becomes a diagonal matrix
\mathcal{L}_{7}
up to order zero.

(9)

Using Fourier integral operators
A^{(1)}
and
A^{(2)}
in (
4.11
) to eliminate the order
1
and
\frac{1}{2}
terms in
\mathcal{L}_{7}
.
We finally obtain the diagonal matrix
\mathcal{L}_{8}
.
The remainder matrix
\mathcal{R}_{8}
is treated perturbatively.

Using these reductions, it is convenient for us to obtain the well-posedness and the observability of the linearized hydroelastic wave system by means of an Ingham-type inequality.
They can be obtained in a backward way in the above reduction steps.
Furthermore, one can obtain the controllability of the linearized system from the observability, the well-posedness of the Cauchy problem and HUM.
Finally, the controllability of the full nonlinear system can then be obtained from the linearized controllability by applying the
Nash-Moser-Hörmander
Theorem
A.6
.

We remark that using the above reduction procedure, one can also prove the local well-posedness of the two-dimensional hydroelastic waves (
1.1
) in the periodic setting as in the perturbation of Korteweg–de Vries (KdV) equation in
[

.
However, this well-posedness result is at high regularity, and is much weaker compared to previous results in
[
37
,
35
]
.
We do not prove this well-posedness result for the two-dimensional hydroelastic waves here.

The above symmetrization and reduction procedure for the linearized operator adapts the method used by Feola & Procesi
[

]
in the context of KAM theory for quasi-linear NLS equations.
Similar reduction techniques were also developed and used in the periodic setting for problems such as water waves, quasi-linear KdV, Benjamin-Ono, Kirchhoff, and incompressible Euler equations, see for instance
[

,

,

]
.

Concerning the controllability for other quasi-linear dispersive PDEs except water waves, Baldi, Floridia & Haus
[
12
,
14
]

studied the internal controllability of quasi-linear perturbations of the KdV equation.
Baldi, Haus & Montalto
[
13
]
and Iandoli & Niu
[
24
]
proved exact controllability for quasi-linear Hamiltonian Schrödinger equations in dimensions
d=1
and

respectively.

In Section

In the next two sections, we reduce the linearized hydroelastic wave system (

In Section

2.
Linearized hydroelastic waves

Recall that the hydroelastic waves (
1.1
) can be recast as (
1.5
).
In this section, we compute the linearized operator
\Phi^{\prime}(\mathbf{u},f)[\tilde{\mathbf{u}},\tilde{f}]
, and obtain an estimate for the second order derivative
\Phi^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{u},f)[(\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_{1},\tilde{f}_{1}),(\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_{2},\tilde{f}_{2})]

The linearized operator
\Phi^{\prime}(\mathbf{u},f)[\tilde{\mathbf{u}},\tilde{f}]

(\mathbf{u},f)
in the direction
(\tilde{\mathbf{u}},\tilde{f})

).
It suffices to compute

.
We first recall the following result for the

]

Lemma 2.1

(
[
26
,
27
]
)
.

Let

.

Lemma 2.2

Hence, for
\mathbf{u}=(\eta,\psi)

\displaystyle P_{1}^{\prime}(\mathbf{u})[\tilde{\mathbf{u}}]=

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\tilde{\eta}+\partial_{x}(V\tilde{\eta})-|D|(\tilde{\psi}-B\tilde{\eta})+\mathcal{R}_{G}(\eta)\psi,

\displaystyle P_{2}^{\prime}(\mathbf{u})[\tilde{\mathbf{u}}]=

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\tilde{\psi}+V\partial_{x}\tilde{\psi}-BG(\eta)\tilde{\psi}+(g+BV_{x})\tilde{\eta}+BG(\eta)(B\tilde{\eta})

\displaystyle+\sigma\sum_{\alpha=1}^{4}E_{5-\alpha}(\eta)\partial_{x}^{5-\alpha}\tilde{\eta},

where
\mathcal{R}_{G}(\eta)\psi
is a lower order remainder term that satisfies (
2.3
).
Alternatively, one can write these linearized operators in matrix form.

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}^{\prime}(\mathbf{u})[\tilde{\mathbf{u}}]=\mathbf{P}^{\prime}(\mathbf{u})(\tilde{\eta},\tilde{\psi})^{T}:=(P_{1}^{\prime}(\mathbf{u})[\tilde{\mathbf{u}}],P_{2}^{\prime}(\mathbf{u})[\tilde{\mathbf{u}}])^{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{t}+V\partial_{x}+V_{x}+|D|B&-|D|+\mathcal{R}_{G}(\eta)\\
(g+BV_{x})+BG(\eta)B+\sigma\sum_{\alpha=1}^{4}E_{5-\alpha}(\eta)\partial_{x}^{5-\alpha}&\partial_{t}+V\partial_{x}-BG(\eta)\end{pmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{\eta}\\
\tilde{\psi}\end{bmatrix}.

It is well-known that for water waves, instead of working with variables
(\eta,\psi)
, one may work with the
good unknown of Alinhac
, namely
(\eta,\psi-T_{B}\eta)
; see for example
[
26
,
2
]
.
Motivated by this,
\mathbf{P}^{\prime}(\mathbf{u})
has the following conjugation structure:

(2.6)

\mathbf{P}^{\prime}(\mathbf{u})=\mathcal{Z}\mathcal{L}_{0}\mathcal{Z}^{-1},

where the matrix
\mathcal{Z}
and its inverse
\mathcal{Z}^{-1}
are given by

(2.7)

\mathcal{Z}:=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
B&1\end{pmatrix},\quad\mathcal{Z}^{-1}:=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
-B&1\end{pmatrix},

and the matrix
\mathcal{L}_{0}
is given by

(2.8)

\mathcal{L}_{0}:=\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{t}+V\partial_{x}+V_{x}&-|D|+\mathcal{R}_{G}\\
a+\sigma\sum_{\alpha=1}^{4}E_{5-\alpha}(\eta)\partial_{x}^{5-\alpha}&\partial_{t}+V\partial_{x}\end{pmatrix},\quad a=g+B_{t}+VB_{x}.

In the matrix
\mathcal{L}_{0}
,
a,B,V
are periodic functions of
(\eta,\psi)
, and are thus periodic in space.
In addition, we have the bound

(2.9)

\|(\mathcal{Z}-I)\tilde{\mathbf{u}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{s}}+\|(\mathcal{Z}^{-1}-I)\tilde{\mathbf{u}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{s}}=\|B\tilde{\eta}\|_{H^{s+\frac{3}{2}}}\lesssim\|B\|_{{}_{H^{s+\frac{3}{2}}}}\|\tilde{\eta}\|_{H^{s+\frac{3}{2}}}\lesssim\|\psi\|_{{}_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}}}\|\tilde{\eta}\|_{H^{s+\frac{3}{2}}}.

In the next two sections, we will further simplify the matrix operator
\mathcal{L}_{0}
.

3.
Reduction of the linearized operator at leading and sub-leading order

In this section, we use several changes of variables to simplify the matrix operator
\mathcal{L}_{0}
in (
2.8
), so that the top order terms become constant-coefficient and symmetrized.
This is done using a change of space variable, a time reparametrization, a conjugation of matrices
P^{-1}
and
Q
, and then the symmetrization of top order by conjugating
S
.
Then we symmetrize the matrix operator of lower orders.
The sub-leading terms of the matrix operator are eliminated at the same time.

3.1.
Change of space variable

We start with an invertible time-dependent change of the space variable.
Consider a change of variables
y=x+\beta(t,x)\Leftrightarrow x=y+\tilde{\beta}(t,y)
, where
\beta(t,x)
is a periodic function in space with
|\beta_{x}|\leq\frac{1}{2}
.
Denote the transformation
\mathcal{B}
and its inverse
\mathcal{B}^{-1}
by

(3.1)

(\mathcal{B}h)(t,x):=h(t,x+\beta(t,x)),\quad(\mathcal{B}^{-1}h)(t,y):=h(t,y+\tilde{\beta}(t,y)).

We will later choose the function
\beta
in (
3.13
), so that using Moser’s estimate (
A.3
), for each
t\in[0,T]
,

(3.2)

\|\beta(t,x)\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|(1+\eta_{x}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}-1\|_{H^{s-1}}\lesssim\|\eta\|_{H^{s}},\quad s>1.

For a given function
u
, we have the conjugation rule for
\mathcal{B}
:
\mathcal{B}^{-1}u\mathcal{B}=(\mathcal{B}^{-1}u)
.
For the conjugation rule for
\mathcal{B}
on partial derivatives, we compute directly and use the rule
\mathcal{B}^{-1}\partial_{x}^{j+k}\mathcal{B}=(\mathcal{B}^{-1}\partial_{x}^{j}\mathcal{B})(\mathcal{B}^{-1}\partial_{x}^{k}\mathcal{B})
.

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{-1}\partial_{x}\mathcal{B}

\displaystyle=\{\mathcal{B}^{-1}(1+\beta_{x})\}\partial_{y},\quad\mathcal{B}^{-1}\partial_{xx}\mathcal{B}=\{\mathcal{B}^{-1}(1+\beta_{x})\}^{2}\partial_{yy}+(\mathcal{B}^{-1}\beta_{xx})\partial_{y},

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{-1}\partial_{x}^{3}\mathcal{B}

\displaystyle=\{\mathcal{B}^{-1}(1+\beta_{x})\}^{3}\partial_{y}^{3}+3\{\mathcal{B}^{-1}(1+\beta_{x})\}(\mathcal{B}^{-1}\beta_{xx})\partial_{y}^{2}+(\mathcal{B}^{-1}\partial^{3}_{x}\beta)\partial_{y},

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{-1}\partial_{x}^{4}\mathcal{B}

\displaystyle=\{\mathcal{B}^{-1}(1+\beta_{x})\}^{4}\partial_{y}^{4}+6\left\{\mathcal{B}^{-1}(1+\beta_{x})\right\}^{2}(\mathcal{B}^{-1}\beta_{xx})\partial_{y}^{3}+4\left\{\mathcal{B}^{-1}(1+\beta_{x})\right\}(\mathcal{B}^{-1}\partial^{3}_{x}\beta)\partial^{2}_{y}

\displaystyle+3(\mathcal{B}^{-1}\beta_{xx})^{2}\partial^{2}_{y}+(\mathcal{B}^{-1}\partial^{4}_{x}\beta)\partial_{y},

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{-1}\partial_{t}\mathcal{B}

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}+(\mathcal{B}^{-1}\beta_{t})\partial_{y}.

For the conjugation rule for
\mathcal{B}
on
|D_{x}|=\partial_{x}\mathcal{H}
, where
\mathcal{H}
is the Hilbert transform, we write

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{-1}|D_{x}|\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}^{-1}\partial_{x}\mathcal{H}\mathcal{B}=(\mathcal{B}^{-1}\partial_{x}\mathcal{B})(\mathcal{B}^{-1}\mathcal{H}\mathcal{B})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{B}^{-1}(1+\beta_{x})\}\partial_{y}[\mathcal{H}+(\mathcal{B}^{-1}\mathcal{H}\mathcal{B}-\mathcal{H})]=\{\mathcal{B}^{-1}(1+\beta_{x})\}|D_{y}|+\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{B}},

where
\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{B}}:=\{\mathcal{B}^{-1}(1+\beta_{x})\}\partial_{y}(\mathcal{B}^{-1}\mathcal{H}\mathcal{B}-\mathcal{H})
is bounded in time, regularizing in space, since by (
A.6
) and (
3.2
), we have for
s>0
,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{B}}f\|_{C([0,T];H^{s+2})}\lesssim\left(1+\|\mathcal{B}^{-1}\beta_{x}\|_{C([0,T];H^{s+2})}\right)\|\partial_{y}^{3}(\mathcal{B}^{-1}\mathcal{H}\mathcal{B}-\mathcal{H})f\|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left(1+\|\mathcal{B}^{-1}\beta_{x}\|_{C([0,T];H^{s+2})}\right)\bigl(\|\beta\|_{C([0,T];H^{5})}\|f\|_{C([0,T];H^{s})}+\|\beta\|_{C([0,T];H^{s+5})}\|f\|_{C([0,T];L^{2})}\bigr)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\eta\|_{C([0,T];H^{5})}\|f\|_{C([0,T];H^{s})}+\|\eta\|_{C([0,T];H^{s+5})}\|f\|_{C([0,T];L^{2})}.

Hence, when conjugating the matrix operator
\mathcal{L}_{1}
by
\mathcal{B}
, we obtain

(3.3)

\mathcal{L}_{1}:=\mathcal{B}^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{0}\mathcal{B}=\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{t}+a_{1}\partial_{y}+a_{2}\ &\ -a_{3}|D_{y}|+R_{1}\\
\sigma\sum_{j=1}^{4}a_{3+j}\partial_{y}^{5-j}+a_{8}\ &\ \partial_{t}+a_{1}\partial_{y}\end{pmatrix},

where coefficients are

\displaystyle a_{1}

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{B}^{-1}[\beta_{t}+V(1+\beta_{x})],\quad a_{2}:=\mathcal{B}^{-1}(V_{x}),\quad a_{3}:=\mathcal{B}^{-1}(1+\beta_{x}),\quad a_{4}:=\mathcal{B}^{-1}[E_{4}(1+\beta_{x})^{4}],

\displaystyle a_{5}

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{B}\left[6E_{4}(1+\beta_{x})^{2}\beta_{xx}+E_{3}(1+\beta_{x})^{3}\right],

\displaystyle a_{6}

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{B}^{-1}\left[E_{4}\left(4(1+\beta_{x})\partial^{3}_{x}\beta+3\beta^{2}_{xx}\right)+3E_{3}(1+\beta_{x})\beta_{xx}+E_{2}(1+\beta_{x})^{2}\right],

\displaystyle a_{7}

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{B}^{-1}\left[E_{4}\partial_{x}^{4}\beta+E_{3}\partial_{x}^{3}\beta+E_{2}\beta_{xx}+E_{1}(1+\beta_{x})\right],\qquad a_{8}:=\mathcal{B}^{-1}a,

and
R_{1}:=-\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{B}}-\mathcal{B}^{-1}\mathcal{R}_{G}\mathcal{B}
.

To obtain bounds for the transformations
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{B}^{-1}
, we use Lemma
A.2
, for
s\geq 0
,

(3.4)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{B}f\|_{C([0,T];H^{s})}\lesssim\|f\|_{C([0,T];H^{s})}+\|\eta\|_{C([0,T];H^{s+2})}\|f\|_{C([0,T];L^{2})},

(3.5)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{B}^{-1}f\|_{C([0,T];H^{s})}\lesssim\|f\|_{C([0,T];H^{s})}+\|\eta\|_{C([0,T];H^{s+4})}\|f\|_{C([0,T];L^{2})}.

The operator
R_{1}
is also regularizing in space, and for
s\geq s_{0}

\|R_{1}f\|_{C([0,T];H^{s+\frac{3}{2}})}\lesssim\|\eta\|^{\theta}_{C([0,T];H^{s+4})}\|f\|_{C([0,T];H^{s})}.

3.2.
Time reparametrization and reduction at top order

Consider a reparametrization of time in
[0,T]
,
\tau=t+\alpha(t)\Leftrightarrow t=\tau+\tilde{\alpha}(\tau)
, where
\alpha(t)
satisfies that
\alpha(0)=\alpha(T)=0
.
Denote the transformation
\mathcal{A}
and its inverse
\mathcal{A}^{-1}
by

(3.6)

(\mathcal{A}h)(t,y):=h(t+\alpha(t),y),\quad(\mathcal{A}^{-1}h)(\tau,y):=h(\tau+\tilde{\alpha}(\tau),y).

We will later choose the function
\alpha(t)
in (
3.11
) and (
3.12
).
One can check that

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{-1}\partial_{y}^{k}\mathcal{A}=\partial_{y}^{k},\quad\mathcal{A}^{-1}|D_{y}|\mathcal{A}=|D_{y}|,\quad\mathcal{A}^{-1}\partial_{t}\mathcal{A}=\{\mathcal{A}^{-1}(1+\alpha^{\prime})\}\partial_{\tau}.

Thus, we get the conjugation

(3.7)

\mathcal{L}_{2}:=\mathcal{A}^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{1}\mathcal{A}=\begin{pmatrix}a_{9}\partial_{\tau}+a_{10}\partial_{y}+a_{11}\ &\ -a_{12}|D_{y}|+R_{2}\\
\sigma\sum_{j=1}^{4}a_{12+j}\partial_{y}^{5-j}+a_{17}\ &\ a_{9}\partial_{\tau}+a_{10}\partial_{y}\end{pmatrix},

where
R_{2}=\mathcal{A}^{-1}R_{1}\mathcal{A}
, and coefficients
a_{i}=a_{i}(\tau,y)
are

\displaystyle a_{9}:=\mathcal{A}^{-1}(1+\alpha^{\prime}),\quad a_{k}:=\mathcal{A}^{-1}(a_{k-9}),\quad k=10,...,17.

\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{A}^{-1}
preserve the Sobolev norm such that

(3.8)

\|\mathcal{A}h\|_{C([0,T];H^{s})}=\|\mathcal{A}^{-1}h\|_{C([0,T];H^{s})}=\|h\|_{C([0,T];H^{s})},\quad\forall h\in C([0,T];H^{s}).

Given
b_{1},b_{2},b_{3},b_{4}
as functions of
(t,x)
, the system

\begin{pmatrix}b_{1}&b_{2}\\
b_{3}&b_{4}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}f&0\\
0&g\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{1}&\lambda_{2}\\
\lambda_{3}&\lambda_{4}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}p&0\\
0&q\end{pmatrix}

has solutions
f,g,p,q
,

(3.9)

f=\frac{b_{1}}{\lambda_{1}p}\,,\quad g=\frac{b_{3}}{\lambda_{3}p}\,,\quad q=\frac{\lambda_{1}b_{2}p}{b_{1}\lambda_{2}}\,,\quad p=\text{any}\,,

if and only if
b_{i},\lambda_{i}
satisfy

\frac{b_{1}b_{4}}{b_{2}b_{3}}\,=\frac{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{4}}{\lambda_{2}\lambda_{3}}\,.

In particular, to have
\lambda_{i}
constant at the leading order, it is necessary that
b_{1}b_{4}/b_{2}b_{3}
is a constant at the leading order.

In order to reduce the coefficients of the leading terms of the matrix
\mathcal{L}_{2}
to constants, we use the above observation and look for functions
\alpha(t),\beta(t,x)
such that

ma^{2}_{9}=a_{12}a_{13},

for some constant
m\in\mathbb{R}
, which is equivalent to

m(1+\alpha^{\prime})^{2}=a_{3}a_{4}.

Since
a_{3}=\mathcal{B}^{-1}(1+\beta_{x})
,
a_{4}=\mathcal{B}^{-1}[E_{4}(1+\beta_{x})^{4}]
, and
\alpha^{\prime}=\mathcal{B}^{-1}(\alpha^{\prime})
, we then need

m(1+\alpha^{\prime})^{2}=E_{4}(1+\beta_{x})^{5},

that is,

(3.10)

m^{\frac{1}{5}}(1+\alpha^{\prime}(t))^{\frac{2}{5}}E_{4}(t,x)^{-\frac{1}{5}}=1+\beta_{x}.

Integrating this equality in
dx
, and using the fact that
E_{4}(t,x)=(1+\eta_{x}^{2})^{-\frac{5}{2}}
we obtain that

\displaystyle 1+\alpha^{\prime}(t)=m^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}(1+\eta_{x}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}dx\right)^{-\frac{5}{2}}.

Integrating the above equality in
dt
, we have

(3.11)

\displaystyle m=\left\{\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}(1+\eta_{x}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}dx\right)^{-\frac{5}{2}}dt\right\}^{2},

(3.12)

\displaystyle\alpha(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\left[m^{-1/2}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}(1+\eta_{x}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}dx\right)^{-\frac{5}{2}}-1\right]dt.

Set

f(t)=\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}(1+\eta_{x}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}dx\right)^{-\frac{5}{2}},

then from (
3.11
), one can write

1+\alpha^{\prime}(t)=\frac{f(t)}{\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}f(t)dt}.

Since
(1+\|\eta_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}})^{-\frac{5}{2}}\leq f(t)\leq 1
, we have a uniform bound

(1+\|\eta_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}})^{-\frac{5}{2}}\leq 1+\alpha^{\prime}(t)\leq(1+\|\eta_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}})^{\frac{5}{2}}.

Using the equation (
3.10
), we get

(3.13)

\beta(t,x)=\partial_{x}^{-1}\left[m^{\frac{1}{5}}(1+\alpha^{\prime}(t))^{\frac{2}{5}}(1+\eta_{x}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}-1\right],

where
\partial_{x}^{-1}f
is the primitive of
f
in
x
, with zero spatial average.
Alternatively,

\partial_{x}^{-1}\,e^{ikx}=\frac{1}{ik}\,e^{ikx}\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\},\qquad\partial_{x}^{-1}\,1=0.

We follow the elementary observation above, with
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=\lambda_{4}=1
,
\lambda_{3}=m
,
p=1
,
b_{1}=a_{9}
,
b_{2}=a_{12}
,
b_{3}=a_{13}
,
b_{4}=a_{9}
, and set

(3.14)

\displaystyle P:=\begin{pmatrix}a_{9}&0\\
0&a_{13}m^{-1}\end{pmatrix},\quad P^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}a^{-1}_{9}&0\\
0&a^{-1}_{13}m\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle Q:=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&a^{-1}_{13}a_{9}\end{pmatrix},\quad Q^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&a_{13}a_{9}^{-1}\end{pmatrix}.

According to (
3.9
), we then have

(3.15)

\mathcal{L}_{3}:=P^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{2}Q=\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{\tau}+a_{18}\partial_{y}+a_{19}\ &\ -|D_{y}|+a_{20}\mathcal{H}+R_{3}\\
\sigma m\partial^{4}_{y}+m\sigma\sum_{j=2}^{4}a_{19+j}\partial_{y}^{5-j}+ma_{24}\ &\ \partial_{\tau}+a_{18}\partial_{y}+a_{25}\end{pmatrix},

where coefficients are

\displaystyle a_{18}=\frac{a_{10}}{a_{9}},\quad a_{19}=\frac{a_{11}}{a_{9}},\quad a_{20}=-\frac{a_{12}}{a_{9}}\left(\frac{a_{12}}{a_{9}}\right)_{y},

\displaystyle a_{19+j}=\frac{a_{12+j}}{a_{13}},\,j=2,3,4,\quad a_{25}=m\frac{a_{9}}{a_{13}}\left(\frac{a_{9}}{a_{12}}\right)_{\tau}+m\frac{a_{10}}{a_{13}}\left(\frac{a_{9}}{a_{12}}\right)_{y},

and the remainder term
R_{3}
is given by

\displaystyle R_{3}=-\frac{a_{12}}{a_{9}}\partial_{y}\left[\mathcal{H},\frac{a_{9}}{a_{12}}\right]+\frac{1}{a_{9}}R_{2}\frac{a_{9}}{a_{12}}.

Note that the leading order operators in matrix
\mathcal{L}_{3}
, namely
-|D_{y}|
and
\sigma m\partial_{y}^{4}
now have constant coefficients.

Recall that by definition, constants
a_{9}
,
a_{13}
and
a_{13}
are given by

a_{9}=1+\mathcal{A}^{-1}\alpha^{\prime},\quad a_{12}=m^{\frac{1}{5}}\mathcal{A}^{-1}\mathcal{B}^{-1}[(1+\alpha^{\prime}(t))^{\frac{2}{5}}(1+\eta_{x}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}],\quad a_{13}=m^{\frac{4}{5}}\mathcal{A}^{-1}\mathcal{B}^{-1}[(1+\alpha^{\prime}(t))^{\frac{8}{5}}(1+\eta_{x}^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}].

Using Moser’s estimate (
A.3
), we get that for
s>2
,

(3.16)

\|(P^{\pm 1}-\text{diag}\{1,m^{\pm\frac{1}{5}}\})\tilde{u}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{s}}+\|(Q^{\pm 1}-\text{diag}\{1,m^{\mp\frac{1}{5}}\})\tilde{u}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{s}}\lesssim\|\eta\|_{H^{2}}\|\tilde{u}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}+\|\eta\|_{H^{s+2}}\|\tilde{u}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}.

Using the commutator estimate (
A.5
), the operator
R_{3}
satisfies the estimate, for
s\geq s_{0}
,

\|R_{3}f\|_{C([0,T];H^{s+\frac{3}{2}})}\lesssim\|\eta\|^{\theta}_{C([0,T];H^{s+4})}\|f\|_{C([0,T];H^{s})}.

3.3.
Symmetrization of top order

Let
\mathfrak{g}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
be a
C^{\infty}
function such that

(3.17)

\mathfrak{g}(\xi)=\left(\frac{g+\sigma\xi^{4}}{|\xi|}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad\forall|\xi|\geq\frac{2}{3};\quad\mathfrak{g}(\xi)=1,\quad\forall|\xi|\leq\frac{1}{3}.

We define
\Lambda
as the Fourier multiplier with symbol
\mathfrak{g}
, and

(3.18)

S:=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&m^{\frac{1}{2}}\Lambda\end{pmatrix},\quad S^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&m^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Lambda^{-1}\end{pmatrix},

where the
m
is constant in (
3.11
).

Writing the matrix
\mathcal{L}_{3}=\begin{pmatrix}A_{3}&B_{3}\\
C_{3}&D_{3}\end{pmatrix}
, we then have the conjugation

S^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{3}S=\begin{pmatrix}A_{3}&m^{1/2}B_{3}\Lambda\\
m^{-1/2}\Lambda^{-1}C_{3}&\Lambda^{-1}D_{3}\Lambda\end{pmatrix}=:\begin{pmatrix}A_{3}^{+}&B_{3}^{+}\\
C_{3}^{+}&D_{3}^{+}\end{pmatrix}.

We define the operator

(3.19)

T:=\sqrt{m}|D_{y}|^{\frac{1}{2}}(g+\sigma\partial_{y}^{4})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Using the symbolic expansion (
A.7
), we get that
A_{3}^{+}=A_{3}
and

\displaystyle B_{3}^{+}

\displaystyle=-T+\sqrt{m}a_{20}\mathcal{H}|D_{y}|^{-\frac{1}{2}}(g+\sigma\partial_{y}^{4})^{\frac{1}{2}}+\sqrt{m}R_{3}\Lambda,

\displaystyle C_{3}^{+}

\displaystyle=T+\sqrt{m}\sigma a_{21}\Lambda^{-1}\partial_{y}^{3}+\sqrt{m}\sigma(a_{21})_{y}(\Lambda^{-1})_{1}\partial_{y}^{3}+\sqrt{m}a_{22}\Lambda^{-1}\partial_{y}^{2}+g\sqrt{m}\pi_{0}+\mathcal{R}_{3,C}^{+},

\displaystyle D_{3}^{+}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\tau}+a_{18}\partial_{y}+(a_{18})_{y}(\Lambda^{-1})_{1}\Lambda\partial_{y}+a_{25}+\mathcal{R}_{3,D}^{+},

where
(\Lambda^{-1})_{n}:=\frac{1}{i^{n}}
Op
(\partial_{\xi}^{n}(1/\mathfrak{g}))
is the
n
-th expansion of
\Lambda^{-1}
, and
\pi_{0}(h):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}hdy
is the spatial average.
We further expand

\displaystyle(g+\sigma\partial_{y}^{4})^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\sigma}\partial_{y}^{2}+\sqrt{g}\pi_{0}+\mathcal{R}_{\sigma},\quad\Lambda^{-1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sigma}}|D_{y}|^{-\frac{3}{2}}+O(|D_{y}|^{-\frac{11}{2}}),

\displaystyle(\Lambda^{-1})_{1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{3}{2\sqrt{\sigma}}|D_{y}|^{-\frac{5}{2}}\mathcal{H}+O(|D_{y}|^{-\frac{11}{2}}),

where each remainder term of
O(|D_{y}|^{\alpha})
type is a Fourier multiplier whose symbol
g(\xi)
admits the bound
|g(\xi)|\leq C(1+|\xi|)^{\alpha}
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
and some
C>0
depending only on
\sigma
.

Hence, we get using
\partial_{y}=-|D_{y}|\mathcal{H}
,

(3.20)

S^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{3}S=\mathcal{L}_{4}+\mathcal{R}_{4}:=\begin{pmatrix}A_{4}&B_{4}\\
C_{4}&D_{4}\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}0&\mathcal{R}_{4,B}\\
\mathcal{R}_{4,C}&\mathcal{R}_{4,D}\end{pmatrix},

for

\displaystyle A_{4}:=\partial_{\tau}+a_{18}\partial_{y}+a_{19},\quad B_{4}:=-T-\sqrt{m\sigma}a_{20}|D_{y}|^{\frac{3}{2}}\mathcal{H},

\displaystyle C_{4}:=T+\sqrt{m\sigma}a_{21}|D_{y}|^{\frac{3}{2}}\mathcal{H}+a_{26}|D_{y}|^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad D_{4}:=\partial_{\tau}+a_{18}\partial_{y}+a_{27},

and
\mathcal{R}_{4}
is a smoothing matrix that absorbs all remainder terms.
It satisfies the bound

\|\mathcal{R}_{4}\mathbf{f}\|_{C([0,T];H^{s}\times H^{s})}\lesssim\|\eta\|^{\theta}_{C([0,T];H^{s+4})}\|\mathbf{f}\|_{C([0,T];H^{s}\times H^{s})},\quad s\geq s_{0}.

The coefficients
a_{26}
and
a_{27}
are given by

a_{26}=\frac{3}{2}\sqrt{m\sigma}(a_{21})_{y}-\frac{\sqrt{m}}{\sqrt{\sigma}}a_{22},\quad a_{27}=a_{25}+\frac{3}{2}(a_{18})_{y}.

3.4.
Symmetrization of lower orders

Next, we further symmetrize and rewrite the linearized operator, so that the sub-leading terms of order
\frac{3}{2}
are eliminated.
To achieve this goal, we symmetrize the matrix operator
\mathcal{L}_{4}+\mathcal{R}_{4}
up to order zero.
Let us consider the symmetrized matrix operator

(3.21)

\mathcal{L}_{5}:=\begin{pmatrix}A_{5}&-C_{5}\\
C_{5}&A_{5}\end{pmatrix},\qquad\begin{array}[]{l}A_{5}:=\partial_{\tau}+a_{18}\partial_{y}+a_{28},\\
C_{5}:=T+a_{29}\mathcal{H}|D_{y}|^{3/2}+a_{30}|D_{y}|^{1/2},\end{array}

where
a_{28}
,
a_{29}
and
a_{30}
are real-valued spatial-periodic functions of
(\tau,y)
.
We consider a transformation

(3.22)

M=\begin{pmatrix}1&\ell\\
0&v\end{pmatrix},\qquad\begin{array}[]{l}\ell=\ell_{1}|D_{y}|^{-5/2},\\
v=1+v_{1}\mathcal{H}|D_{y}|^{-1}+v_{2}|D_{y}|^{-2},\end{array}

where
v_{1}
,

4.
Reduction of the linearized operator at lower orders

In this section, we further reduce and simplify the linearized operator at order
1
and
\frac{1}{2}
.
First, we use a translation of space variable
z=y+p(\tau)
to remove the space average of the coefficient
a_{18}(\tau,y)
of the transport term in
\mathcal{L}_{5}
.
Then we construct a Fourier integral operator to eliminate the order
1
and
\frac{1}{2}
terms.

4.1.
Translation of space variable

We consider the operator
\mathcal{T}
and its inverse:

(4.1)

\mathcal{T}h(\tau,y):=h(\tau,y+p(\tau)),\quad\mathcal{T}^{-1}h(\tau,z):=h(\tau,z-p(\tau)).

\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{T}^{-1}
preserve the Sobolev norm in the sense that

(4.2)

\|\mathcal{T}h\|_{C([0,T];H^{s})}=\|\mathcal{T}^{-1}h\|_{C([0,T];H^{s})}=\|h\|_{C([0,T];H^{s})},\quad\forall h\in C([0,T];H^{s}).

One can check that
\mathcal{T}^{-1}T(D_{y})\mathcal{T}=T(D_{z})
,
\mathcal{T}^{-1}\partial_{y}\mathcal{T}=\partial_{z}
and
\mathcal{T}^{-1}\partial_{\tau}\mathcal{T}=\partial_{\tau}+p^{\prime}(\tau)\partial_{z}
. Thus, we have

\mathcal{L}_{6}:=\mathcal{T}^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{5}\mathcal{T}=\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{\tau}+a_{31}\partial_{z}+a_{32}\ &\ -T-a_{33}|D_{z}|^{1/2}\\
T+a_{33}|D_{z}|^{1/2}\ &\ \partial_{\tau}+a_{31}\partial_{z}+a_{32}\end{pmatrix},

where

\displaystyle a_{31}(\tau,z)=\mathcal{T}^{-1}a_{18}(\tau,z)+p^{\prime}(\tau),\quad a_{32}(\tau,z)=\mathcal{T}^{-1}a_{28}(\tau,z),\quad a_{33}(\tau,z)=\mathcal{T}^{-1}a_{30}(\tau,z).

Choosing

p(\tau)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{0}^{2\pi}a_{18}(s,y)\,dyds,

then the transport coefficient
a_{31}
has zero space average:

\int_{0}^{2\pi}a_{31}(\tau,z)\,dz=0.

Therefore, we get that

(4.3)

\mathcal{T}^{-1}(\mathcal{L}_{5}+\mathcal{R}_{5})\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{L}_{6}+\mathcal{R}_{6},\quad\mathcal{R}_{6}:=\mathcal{T}^{-1}\mathcal{R}_{5}\mathcal{T}.

We further choose unitary matrices
\mathcal{O}
and
\mathcal{O}^{-1}

(4.4)

\mathcal{O}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
i&-i\end{pmatrix},\quad\mathcal{O}^{-1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1&-i\\
1&i\end{pmatrix}.

Then we have the conjugation

\mathcal{L}_{7}:=\mathcal{O}^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{6}\mathcal{O}=\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{\tau}+iT+a_{31}\partial_{z}+ia_{33}|D_{z}|^{\frac{1}{2}}+a_{32}&0\\
0&\partial_{\tau}-iT+a_{31}\partial_{z}-ia_{33}|D_{z}|^{\frac{1}{2}}+a_{32}\end{pmatrix}.

\mathcal{L}_{7}
is a diagonal matrix.
We also define the remainder matrix
\mathcal{R}_{7}=\mathcal{O}^{-1}\mathcal{R}_{6}\mathcal{O}
.

4.2.
Reduction at lower orders

We now use a Fourier integral operator
A
to eliminate the coefficients
a_{31}
and
a_{33}
.
Given a spatial periodic function

, we define the Fourier integral operator
A^{(1)}
as

(4.5)

h(\tau,z)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}h_{j}(\tau)\,e^{ijz}\quad\mapsto\quad A^{(1)}h(\tau,z)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}h_{j}(\tau)\,p(\tau,z,j)\,e^{i\phi(\tau,z,j)},

where the amplitude
p(\tau,z,j)
is a spatial-periodic symbol of order zero,
the phase function
\phi(\tau,z,j)
is of the form

(4.6)

\phi(\tau,z,j)=jz+|j|^{1/2}\gamma(\tau,z),

and
\gamma(\tau,z)
is a spatial-periodic function, with
|\gamma_{z}(\tau,z)|<1/2
.
We consider the operator

(4.7)

\mathcal{D}_{+}:=\partial_{\tau}+iT,\quad\mathcal{L}_{7}^{(1)}:=\partial_{\tau}+iT+a_{31}\partial_{z}+ia_{33}|D_{z}|^{\frac{1}{2}}+a_{32}.

In the following, we will show the existence of functions
p(\tau,z,j)
and
\gamma(\tau,z)
such that
\mathcal{L}_{7}^{(1)}A^{(1)}-A^{(1)}\mathcal{D}_{+}=O(|D_{z}|^{-\frac{1}{2}})
.
Let
\mathfrak{t}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a
C^{\infty}
function such that

(4.8)

\mathfrak{t}(\xi)=\{m|\xi|(1+\sigma\xi^{4})\}^{1/2},\quad\forall|\xi|\geq 2/3;\qquad\mathfrak{t}(\xi)=0,\quad\forall|\xi|\leq 1/3,

so that Op
(\mathfrak{t})=T
on the periodic functions.
Using Lemma
A.4
, we rewrite the commutator
[T,A^{(1)}]
as

[T,A^{(1)}]=\sum_{n=1}^{4}\frac{1}{n!}\text{Op}(\partial_{z}^{n}a)\circ\text{Op}(i^{-n}\partial_{\xi}^{n}\mathfrak{t})+\mathcal{R}_{A1},\quad\mathcal{R}_{A1}=O(1),

where

is the symbol

a(\tau,z,j)=p(\tau,z,j)\,e^{i|j|^{1/2}\gamma(\tau,z)},

and
\mathcal{R}_{A1}
is the lower-order remainder term of the commutator.
We compute

\displaystyle\text{Op}(i^{-1}\partial_{\xi}\mathfrak{t})=

\displaystyle\frac{5}{2}\sqrt{m\sigma}|D_{z}|^{\frac{3}{2}}\mathcal{H}+O(|D_{z}|^{-\frac{5}{2}}),

\displaystyle\text{Op}(i^{-2}\partial_{\xi}^{2}\mathfrak{t})=

\displaystyle-\frac{15}{4}\sqrt{m\sigma}|D_{z}|^{\frac{1}{2}}+O(|D_{z}|^{-\frac{7}{2}}),

\displaystyle\text{Op}(i^{-3}\partial_{\xi}^{3}\mathfrak{t})=

\displaystyle-\frac{15}{8}\sqrt{m\sigma}|D_{z}|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{H}+O(|D_{z}|^{-\frac{9}{2}}),

\displaystyle\text{Op}(i^{-4}\partial_{\xi}^{4}\mathfrak{t})=

\displaystyle-\frac{15}{16}\sqrt{m\sigma}|D_{z}|^{-\frac{3}{2}}+O(|D_{z}|^{-\frac{11}{2}}),

\displaystyle\partial_{z}a=

\displaystyle\{i|j|^{\frac{1}{2}}p\gamma_{z}+p_{z}\}e^{i|j|^{\frac{1}{2}}\gamma},

\displaystyle\partial^{2}_{z}a=

\displaystyle\{-i|j|p\gamma^{2}_{z}+i|j|^{\frac{1}{2}}(2p_{z}\gamma_{z}+p\gamma_{zz})+p_{zz}\}e^{i|j|^{\frac{1}{2}}\gamma},

where

\displaystyle\|r_{k}(\cdot,\cdot,j)\|_{H^{s}}\leq(1+|j|)^{k-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\|\gamma\|_{H^{s+2}}+\|p-1\|_{H^{s+2}}\right),\quad\forall s\geq 1,\quad k=1,2,\quad j\in\mathbb{Z}.

We also have

\displaystyle\partial_{z}A^{(1)}h=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}h_{j}(ijp+i|j|^{\frac{1}{2}}\gamma_{z}p+p_{z})e^{i\phi(\tau,z,j)}.

From Lemma 12.10 of
[
4

\displaystyle|D_{z}|^{\frac{1}{2}}A^{(1)}h=\sum_{j\neq 0}h_{j}|j|^{\frac{1}{2}}p(\tau,z,j)e^{i\phi(\tau,z,j)}+R_{A2}h,\quad R_{A2}=O(1).

The commutator
[\partial_{\tau},A^{(1)}]=\partial_{\tau}A^{(1)}-A^{(1)}\partial_{\tau}
is

\displaystyle[\partial_{\tau},A^{(1)}]h=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}h_{j}\left(p_{\tau}(\tau,z,j)+i|j|^{\frac{1}{2}}\gamma_{\tau}(\tau,z)p(\tau,z,j)\right).

Using the expansions above, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{7}^{(1)}A^{(1)}-A^{(1)}\mathcal{D}_{+}=E+\mathcal{R}_{A3},\quad\mathcal{R}_{A3}:=i\mathcal{R}_{A1}+ia_{33}\mathcal{R}_{A2},

and

\displaystyle Eh=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}h_{j}(t)c(\tau,z,j)e^{i\phi(\tau,z,j)},

\displaystyle c(\tau,z,j)=i\frac{5}{2}\sqrt{m\sigma}\text{sign}(j)\gamma_{z}p|j|^{2}+\left(-\frac{15}{8}\sqrt{m\sigma}\gamma^{2}_{z}p+\frac{5}{2}\sqrt{m\sigma}\text{sign}(j)p_{z}\right)|j|^{\frac{3}{2}}

\displaystyle+\left(i\frac{15}{48}\sqrt{m\sigma}\text{sign}(j)\gamma_{z}^{3}p-i\frac{15}{4}\sqrt{m\sigma}\gamma_{z}p_{z}-i\frac{15}{8}\sqrt{m\sigma}\gamma_{zz}p+ia_{31}\text{sign}(j)p\right)|j|

\displaystyle+\left(-i\frac{15}{8}\sqrt{m\sigma}p_{zz}+\frac{15}{16}\sqrt{m\sigma}\text{sign}(j)\gamma_{z}(p_{z}\gamma_{z}+p\gamma_{zz})-\frac{5}{2^{7}}p\gamma_{z}^{4}+i\gamma_{\tau}p+ia_{33}p+ia_{31}\gamma_{z}p\right)|j|^{\frac{1}{2}}

where

We now need to choose

For the order

.
Since we cannot have

\gamma(\tau,z)=\gamma_{0}(\tau),

for some function
\gamma_{0}(\tau)

\tau

We further expand symbol

p(\tau,z,j)=\sum_{-2\leq m\leq 0}|j|^{m/2}p^{(m)}(\tau,z,j),\quad p^{(m)}\text{ is bounded in }j.

Using the fact that

\displaystyle c(\tau,z,j)=\left(\frac{5}{2}\sqrt{m\sigma}\text{sign}(j)p_{z}^{(0)}\right)|j|^{\frac{3}{2}}+\left(\frac{5}{2}\sqrt{m\sigma}\text{sign}(j)p_{z}^{(-1)}+ia_{31}\text{sign}(j)p^{(0)}\right)|j|

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left(\frac{5}{2}\sqrt{m\sigma}\text{sign}(j)p_{z}^{(-2)}+ia_{31}\text{sign}(j)p^{(-1)}-i\frac{15}{8}\sqrt{m\sigma}p_{zz}^{(0)}+i\gamma_{\tau}p^{(0)}+ia_{33}p^{(0)}\right)|j|^{\frac{1}{2}}+\tilde{r}(\tau,z,j),

where
\tilde{r}(\tau,z,j)
is the remainder term of order less than or equal to zero.

For the order
\frac{3}{2}
term in
c(\tau,z,j)
, we need to have
\frac{5}{2}\sqrt{m\sigma}\text{sign}(j)p_{z}^{(0)}=0
.
For simplicity, we take

.
For the order
1

, we have

\frac{5}{2}\sqrt{m\sigma}\text{sign}(j)p_{z}^{(-1)}+ia_{31}\text{sign}(j)p^{(0)}=0.

To this end, we take

Note that we have chosen
a_{31}
such that it does not have zero frequency component.
Hence,
\partial_{z}^{-1}a_{31}

For the order
\frac{1}{2}

\frac{5}{2}\sqrt{m\sigma}\text{sign}(j)p_{z}^{(-2)}+\frac{2}{5\sqrt{m\sigma}}a_{31}\text{sign}(j)\partial_{z}^{-1}a_{31}+i(\gamma_{\tau}+a_{33})=0.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{2}{5\sqrt{m\sigma}}a_{31}\text{sign}(j)\partial_{z}^{-1}a_{31}+i(\partial_{\tau}\gamma_{0}+a_{33})\right)dz=0.

\gamma_{0}(\tau)=\frac{i}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{2}{5\sqrt{m\sigma}}a_{31}\text{sign}(j)\partial_{z}^{-1}a_{31}+ia_{33}\right)dzds,

Then, we obtain the expression for

\displaystyle p^{(-2)}=-\frac{2}{5\sqrt{m\sigma}}\partial^{-1}_{z}\left(\frac{2}{5\sqrt{m\sigma}}a_{31}\text{sign}(j)\partial_{z}^{-1}a_{31}+i(\gamma_{\tau}+a_{33})\right).

Hence, we have constructed the operator
A^{(1)}
.
In particular, for each
\tau\in[0,T]
,

(4.9)

\|\gamma(\tau,\cdot)\|_{H^{s}}+\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\|(p-1)(\tau,\cdot,j)\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|(\eta,\psi)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{s}}

Hence, using Lemma

, the operator

is invertible.
Moreover,

(A^{(1)})^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{7}^{(1)}A^{(1)}=\mathcal{D}_{+}+R_{8}^{+},

where the remainder term
R_{8}^{+}
satisfies

(4.10)

\|R_{8}^{+}f\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|(\eta,\psi)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{2}}\|f\|_{H^{s}}+\|(\eta,\psi)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{s}}\|f\|_{H^{2}}.

Similarly, one can also construct an invertible operator
A^{(2)}
such that for

\mathcal{D}_{-}=\partial_{\tau}-iT,\quad\mathcal{L}_{7}^{(2)}=\partial_{\tau}-iT+a_{31}\partial_{z}-ia_{33}|D_{z}|^{\frac{1}{2}}+a_{32},

(A^{(2)})^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{7}^{(2)}A^{(2)}=\mathcal{D}_{-}+R_{8}^{-},

where the remainder term

satisfies the same estimate as (
4.10
).
Setting the matrix operators

(4.11)

\mathcal{U}=\begin{pmatrix}A^{(1)}&0\\

0&(A^{(2)})^{-1}\end{pmatrix},\quad\mathcal{L}_{8}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{D}_{+}&0\\

we then have the conjugation

(4.12)

\mathcal{L}_{8}+\mathcal{R}_{8}=\mathcal{U}^{-1}(\mathcal{L}_{7}+\mathcal{R}_{7})\mathcal{U},\quad\mathcal{R}_{8}:=\mathcal{U}^{-1}\mathcal{R}_{7}\mathcal{U}+(\mathcal{U}^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{7}\mathcal{U}-\mathcal{L}_{8}).

The remainder matrix
\mathcal{R}_{8}
satisfies for
s\geq s_{0}
,

(4.13)

\|\mathcal{R}_{8}\mathbf{f}\|_{C([0,T];H^{s}\times H^{s})}\lesssim\|(\eta,\psi)\|^{\theta}_{C([0,T];{\mathcal{H}}^{s+\frac{5}{2}})}\|\mathbf{f}\|_{C([0,T];H^{s}\times H^{s})}.

5.
Well-posedness of the linear system

In this section, we prove the well-posedness of the linear system

(5.1)

\mathbf{P}^{\prime}(\mathbf{u})[\tilde{\mathbf{u}}]=\tilde{\mathbf{f}},\quad\tilde{\mathbf{u}}|_{t=0}=(\tilde{\eta}_{in},\tilde{\psi}_{in})^{T},

where the
\mathbf{P}^{\prime}(\mathbf{u})[\tilde{\mathbf{u}}]
is defined in (
2.5
).
We assume that

\|(\eta,\psi)\|_{C([0,T];{\mathcal{H}}^{s+\frac{5}{2}})}+\|\tilde{\mathbf{f}}\|_{C([0,T];{\mathcal{H}}^{s})}<\delta,

for some small constant
\delta
.
While it is possible to prove the well-posedness of this linear system directly, we will make use of the result in the reductions of
\mathbf{P}^{\prime}(\mathbf{u})[\tilde{\mathbf{u}}]
in previous two sections.

We start from the linear system

(5.2)

(\mathcal{L}_{8}+\mathcal{R}_{8})\mathbf{h}=\mathbf{f}_{8},\quad\mathbf{h}|_{\tau=0}=\mathbf{h}_{in}.

By (
4.13
), we get a bound for the remainder matrix operator
\mathcal{R}_{8}
.
For
s\geq s_{0}
,

\|\mathcal{R}_{8}\mathbf{f}\|_{C([0,T];H^{s}\times H^{s})}\lesssim\delta^{\theta}\|\mathbf{f}\|_{C([0,T];H^{s}\times H^{s})}.

We prove the following well-posedness result of the system (
5.2
).

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
T>0
, and
s_{1}\geq 0
.
Suppose
\mathbf{h}_{in}\in H^{s_{1}}({\mathbb{T}})\times H^{s_{1}}({\mathbb{T}})
,
\mathbf{f}_{8}\in C([0,T];H^{s_{1}}({\mathbb{T}})\times H^{s_{1}}({\mathbb{T}}))
.
There exists
\varepsilon_{8}>0
depending on
T
such that if

\|\mathcal{R}_{8}\mathbf{f}\|_{C([0,T];H^{s_{1}}\times H^{s_{1}})}\lesssim\varepsilon_{8}\|\mathbf{f}\|_{C([0,T];H^{s_{1}}\times H^{s_{1}})},

there exists a unique solution
\mathbf{h}\in C([0,T];H^{s_{1}}\times H^{s_{1}})
of the Cauchy problem (
5.2
) satisfying the estimate:
For
s\in[0,s_{1}]
,

(5.3)

\|\mathbf{h}\|_{C([0,T];H^{s}\times H^{s})}\lesssim_{s}e^{CT}\left(\|\mathbf{h}_{in}\|_{H^{s}\times H^{s}}+\|\mathbf{f}_{8}\|_{C([0,T];H^{s}\times H^{s})}\right),

where the constant
C
depends on
\varepsilon_{8}
.

Proof.

We decompose
\mathbf{h}=\mathbf{h}_{1}+\mathbf{h}_{2}
, where

(5.4)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\mathcal{L}_{8}\mathbf{h}_{1}=\mathbf{f}_{8}&\\
\mathbf{h}_{1}|_{\tau=0}=\mathbf{h}_{in},&\end{array}\right.\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\mathcal{L}_{8}\mathbf{h}_{2}=-\mathcal{R}_{8}\mathbf{h}_{1}-\mathcal{R}_{8}\mathbf{h}_{2}&\\
\mathbf{h}_{2}|_{\tau=0}=\mathbf{0}.&\end{array}\right.

The first system in (
5.4
) is a linear system that can be solved explicitly.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
T>0
and
\omega\subset{\mathbb{T}}
be an open set.
Let
[\mathbf{P}^{\prime}(\mathbf{u})]^{*}
be the adjoint operator of
\mathbf{P}^{\prime}(\mathbf{u})

There exists
\delta_{0}\in(0,1)
small enough such that, if
\|(\eta,\psi)\|_{C([0,T];{\mathcal{H}}^{s_{0}+\frac{5}{2}})}<\delta_{0}
, then for any real-valued
\mathbf{h}_{in},\mathbf{h}_{end}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{0}({\mathbb{T}}),
and
\mathbf{q}\in C([0,T];{\mathcal{H}}^{0}({\mathbb{T}}))
, there exists a unique real-valued function
\mathbf{f}\in C([0,T];{\mathcal{H}}^{0}({\mathbb{T}}))
that solves
[\mathbf{P}^{\prime}(\mathbf{u})]^{*}\mathbf{f}=\mathbf{0}
such that the only solution
\mathbf{h}\in C([0,T];{\mathcal{H}}^{0}({\mathbb{T}}))
of the Cauchy problem

(7.1)

\mathbf{P}^{\prime}(\mathbf{u})\mathbf{h}=\text{diag}\{0,\chi_{\omega}\}\mathbf{f}+\mathbf{q},\quad\mathbf{h}|_{t=0}=\mathbf{h}_{in}

satisfies
\mathbf{h}|_{t=T}=\mathbf{h}_{end}
.
Furthermore, the function
\mathbf{f}
satisfies the control estimate

\|\mathbf{f}\|_{C([0,T];{\mathcal{H}}^{0})}\lesssim\|\mathbf{h}_{in}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{0}}+\|\mathbf{h}_{end}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{0}}+\|\mathbf{q}\|_{C([0,T];{\mathcal{H}}^{0})}.

We remark that the control function that leads to the linearized control problem is not unique.
The function
\mathbf{f}
that we construct is unique in the sense that it is the unique solution to the first system in (
7.2
) below.

Proof.

We first prove the existence of the real-valued function
\mathbf{f}\in C([0,T];{\mathcal{H}}^{0}({\mathbb{T}}))
.
For any
\mathbf{f}_{1},\mathbf{g}_{1}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{0}({\mathbb{T}})
, we consider real-valued
\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g}\in C([0,T];{\mathcal{H}}^{0}({\mathbb{T}}))
as the unique solutions of the backward adjoint Cauchy problems

(7.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}[\mathbf{P}^{\prime}(\mathbf{u})]^{*}\mathbf{f}=\mathbf{0}&\\
\mathbf{f}|_{t=T}=\mathbf{f}_{1},&\end{array}\right.\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}[\mathbf{P}^{\prime}(\mathbf{u})]^{*}\mathbf{g}=\mathbf{0}&\\
\mathbf{g}|_{t=T}=\mathbf{g}_{1},&\end{array}\right.

and we define the bilinear form

\mathfrak{B}(\mathbf{f}_{1},\mathbf{g}_{1}):=\int_{0}^{T}\langle\text{diag}\{0,\chi_{\omega}\}\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}^{0}_{x}}\,dt,

and the linear form

\mathbf{\Lambda}(\mathbf{g}_{1}):=\langle\mathbf{h}_{end},\mathbf{g}_{1}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}^{0}_{x}}-\langle\mathbf{h}_{in},\mathbf{g}(0,\cdot)\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}^{0}_{x}}-\int_{0}^{T}\langle\mathbf{q}(t,\cdot),\mathbf{g}(t,\cdot)\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}^{0}_{x}}\,dt,

where the scalar product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}^{0}_{x}}
is defined as

\langle(u_{1},u_{2}),(v_{1},v_{2})\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}^{0}_{x}}:=\int_{\mathbb{T}}|D|^{\frac{3}{2}}u_{1}(x)|D|^{\frac{3}{2}}\bar{v}_{1}(x)+u_{2}(x)\bar{v}_{2}(x)\,dx.

Using the energy estimate (
5.17
) for the backward adjoint problem, we get

|\mathfrak{B}(\mathbf{f}_{1},\mathbf{g}_{1})|\lesssim\|\mathbf{f}_{1}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{0}}\|\mathbf{g}_{1}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{0}},\quad|\mathbf{\Lambda}(\mathbf{g}_{1})|\lesssim\left(\|\mathbf{h}_{in}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{0}}+\|\mathbf{h}_{end}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{0}}+\|\mathbf{q}\|_{C([0,T];{\mathcal{H}}^{0})}\right)\|\mathbf{g}_{1}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{0}}.

In addition, the observability inequality (
6.18
) implies that the bilinear form
\mathfrak{B}
is coercive.
Therefore, according to Lax-Milgram theorem, there exists a unique real-valued
\mathbf{f}_{1}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{0}({\mathbb{T}})
such that

(7.3)

\mathfrak{B}(\mathbf{f}_{1},\mathbf{g}_{1})=\mathbf{\Lambda}(\mathbf{g}_{1}),\quad\forall\mathbf{g}_{1}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{0}({\mathbb{T}}).

Moreover,
\mathbf{f}_{1}
satisfies the estimate

\|\mathbf{f}_{1}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{0}}\lesssim\|\mathbf{h}_{in}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{0}}+\|\mathbf{h}_{end}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{0}}+\|\mathbf{q}\|_{C([0,T];{\mathcal{H}}^{0})}.

For the function
\mathbf{f}\in C([0,T];{\mathcal{H}}^{0}({\mathbb{T}}))
that is the solution of the first system in (
7.2
), we let
\mathbf{h}
be the solution of the Cauchy problem (
7.1
), whose existence is given in Lemma
6.7
.
Using the definition,

\displaystyle 0=\mathfrak{B}(\mathbf{f}_{1},\mathbf{g}_{1})-\mathbf{\Lambda}(\mathbf{g}_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\langle\text{diag}\{0,\chi_{\omega}\}\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}^{0}}\,dt-\langle\mathbf{h}_{end},\mathbf{g}_{1}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}^{0}}+\langle\mathbf{h}_{in},\mathbf{g}(0,\cdot)\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}^{0}}+\int_{0}^{T}\langle\mathbf{q}(t,\cdot),\mathbf{g}(t,\cdot)\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}^{0}}\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\langle\mathbf{P}^{\prime}(\mathbf{u})\mathbf{h},\mathbf{g}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}^{0}}\,dt-\langle\mathbf{h}_{end},\mathbf{g}_{1}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}^{0}}+\langle\mathbf{h}_{in},\mathbf{g}(0,\cdot)\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}^{0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\langle\mathbf{h},[\mathbf{P}^{\prime}(\mathbf{u})]^{*}\mathbf{g}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}^{0}}\,dt+\langle\mathbf{h}|_{t=T},\mathbf{g}_{1}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}^{0}}-\langle\mathbf{h}|_{t=0},\mathbf{g}_{1}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}^{0}}-\langle\mathbf{h}_{end},\mathbf{g}_{1}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}^{0}}+\langle\mathbf{h}_{in},\mathbf{g}(0,\cdot)\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}^{0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{h}|_{t=T}-\mathbf{h}_{end},\mathbf{g}_{1}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}^{0}}.

Since this relation holds for any
\mathbf{g}_{1}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{0}
, we conclude that
\mathbf{h}|_{t=T}=\mathbf{h}_{end}

a.e.

This finishes the proof of the existence of such a control.

7.3

\mathbf{f}_{1}
that satisfies (

We must have
\tilde{\mathbf{f}}_{1}=\tilde{\mathbf{f}}
.

\mathbf{f}
is unique because it is the unique solution of the first backward adjoint system in (

If
\mathbf{h}_{in},\mathbf{h}_{end}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{0}({\mathbb{T}}),
and
\mathbf{q}\in C([0,T];{\mathcal{H}}^{0}({\mathbb{T}}))

For a periodic function

\Phi(u)=\Phi(0)+g
.
