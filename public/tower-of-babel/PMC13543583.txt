Triglyceride-glucose index and systemic immune-inflammation index are associated with left ventricular hypertrophy in type 2 diabetes mellitus patients
Authors: Peipei Xu, Kangwen Lu, Jingjing Liu, Xiaoqin Zhao, Renyue Hu, Yi Sun, Jie Yuan, Yunjuan Gu, Chen Qian, Xinlei Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo examine the relationship between triglyceride-glucose (TyG) index, systemic immune-inflammation index (SII) and left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH) in type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) patients. Additionally, to assess the ability of the combination of TyG index and SII to predict LVH.MethodsWe enrolled 600 patients with T2DM and divided them into two groups according to the diagnostic criteria for LVH. Differences in demographic characteristics and clinical indicators between the two groups were compared. Spearman’s or Pearson’s correlation analysis was employed to find the correlation between TyG index, SII and left ventricular mass index (LVMI). Logistic regression was utilized to examine the associations of the TyG index, SII and LVH. The ability of the combination of TyG index and SII to predict LVH in T2DM patients was assessed by plotting a receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve.ResultsIn the LVH group, both the TyG index and SII demonstrated markedly elevated levels compared to the non-LVH group, and these parameters exhibited a positive association with LVMI. Logistic regression revealed that, after adjusting for confounders, both the TyG index (OR = 5.62, 95% CI: 2.882 ~ 10.959, P < 0.001) and SII (OR = 1.014, 95% CI: 1.01 ~ 1.017, P < 0.001) were independent predictors for LVH in T2DM. ROC analysis demonstrated that the combined index provided the strongest predictive capability for the occurrence of LVH (area under the curve: AUC = 0.857, 95% CI: 0.826 ~ 0.888, sensitivity 84.4%, specificity 72.9%).ConclusionBoth TyG index and SII are independent predictors for LVH. The combined index of TyG index and SII shows stronger discriminative ability for identifying LVH in T2DM patients.Clinical trial numberNot applicable.

Introduction

Diabetes mellitus (DM) is a chronic disease caused by insufficient insulin secretion or impaired insulin action [1]. Studies indicate that as of 2021, the number of diagnosed diabetes patients worldwide had reached 529 million, and it is projected that by 2050, this population will increase to over 1.31 billion [2]. In China, over 90% of diabetes cases are classified as type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) [3]. T2DM not only disrupts glucose and lipid metabolism, but also leads to excessive lipid deposition, oxidative stress, and chronic inflammation in cardiomyocytes. These processes are associated with pathological remodeling of the left ventricle and may be linked to the development of left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH) [4]. LVH can weaken the blood supply capacity of the heart and become a reliable predictor of serious cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events such as myocardial ischemia, arrhythmias, heart failure and stroke [5]. As a result, this significantly impacts the patients’ quality of life, and brings heavy care and economic burden to the family and society.

Insulin resistance (IR) refers to a state of reduced responsiveness of the body’s tissues to insulin, resulting in decreased efficiency of glucose uptake and use. IR is a hallmark feature of T2DM and is also crucial in the progression of LVH. Specifically, IR can directly damage vascular endothelial cells and disrupt the balance between vasodilation and vasoconstriction. The imbalance promotes increased vasoconstriction, leading to elevated blood pressure and increased cardiac load, which in turn contributes to the development of LVH [6]. The triglyceride-glucose (TyG) index, a noninvasive indicator used to assess IR, offers advantages including simple calculation, easy accessibility, and cost-effectiveness. Research has demonstrated TyG index is linked to more severe cardiovascular disease and worse clinical outcomes [7].

Chronic inflammatory state is closely associated with LVH. Research has demonstrated that pro-inflammatory cytokines, like TNF-α and IL-6, can directly cause cardiomyocyte hypertrophy and apoptosis. They also regulate extracellular matrix synthesis and degradation, playing a key role in ventricular remodeling [8]. Recently, the systemic immune-inflammation index (SII) has garnered increasing interest in its role as a new inflammatory marker. It provides a broader perspective on the overall inflammatory state within the body by integrating three routine blood parameters—neutrophil count (NEUT), lymphocyte count (LYM) and platelet count (PLT) [9]. The prognostic value of SII has been validated in various cardiovascular settings, including patients admitted to coronary care units [10].

The progression of LVH greatly increases the probability of adverse cardiovascular outcomes in T2DM. While echocardiography serves as the primary modality for clinical diagnosis of LVH [11], its accuracy can be compromised by operator technique and image quality, especially for detecting early subclinical myocardial dysfunction in diabetic patients [12]. Therefore, this report aims to use the readily accessible IR surrogate marker- TyG index and the novel inflammatory biomarker- SII to investigate their association with LVH in T2DM patients. The objective is to assess the discriminative ability of combining the TyG index with SII for LVH in this population.

Materials and methods

Study design and patients

This research enrolled 600 patients with T2DM who were hospitalized in the Endocrinology Department, Affiliated Hospital of Nantong University, from January 2023 to February 2025. The participants were comprised of 377 males (62.83%) and 223 females (37.17%) who underwent echocardiographic examination. Inclusion criteria: (1) age over 18 years; (2) diagnosed with T2DM as per the 2020 American Diabetes Association (ADA) guidelines [13]; (3) complete data. Exclusion criteria: (1) acute complications associated with diabetes (such as diabetic ketoacidosis, hyperosmolar hyperglycemic syndrome); (2) gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) or diabetes associated with pregnancy; (3) severe cardiovascular, cerebrovascular, renal or hepatic diseases; (4) severe hematological disorders; (5) concurrent disorders associated with hypertriglyceridemia (e.g., acute or chronic pancreatitis, obstructive jaundice, hypothyroidism); (6) used lipid-lowering medications within the past three months; (7) severe infectious diseases, stress conditions, immune dysfunction or malignant tumors; (8) psychiatric disorders or intellectual disabilities. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Nantong University Affiliated Hospital, Jiangsu, China. All procedures complied strictly with the Declaration of Helsinki and relevant Chinese clinical research regulations.

Anthropometric indices

General information was collected on the participants, including sex, age, diabetes duration, height, weight, waist circumference (WC), hypertension history, alcohol consumption history and smoking history. WC was measured at the narrowest part of the torso. A calibrated Omron HEM-6000 device was needed to measure systolic blood pressure (SBP) and diastolic blood pressure (DBP) after 15 min of rest.

All participants underwent overnight fasting for over 8 h after hospitalization. Blood specimens were meticulously collected and analysed on the second morning. Fasting blood samples were used to measure white blood cell count (WBC), NEUT, PLT, LYM, hemoglobin (Hb), fasting plasma glucose (FPG), 2-hour postprandial glucose (2hPG), fasting insulin (FINS), 2-hour postprandial insulin (2hINS), glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c), total cholesterol (TC), triglycerides (TG), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), serum creatinine (SCr), serum uric acid (SUA). Complete blood count was measured using the hematology analyzer (BC6800). Blood biochemistry tests were performed on an AU5800 automated biochemical analyzer from Beckman Coulter, USA. FPG and 2hPG levels were measured precisely using the glucose oxidase method on an ADVIA 2400 automatic biochemical analyzer (Siemens). FINS and 2hINS levels were determined by a fully automated electrochemiluminescence immunoassay on a Roche Cobas E411 analyzer. HbA1c was measured using high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) (Bio-Rad 10, USA). The CKD-EPI formula was employed to calculate Estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) [14]. Indicators of insulin resistance included the TyG index, homeostasis model assessment-estimated insulin resistance (HOMA-IR) and TG/HDL-C. Inflammatory indicators included the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR) and SII. The relevant formulas are as follows: TyG index = Ln(TG (mg/dL)* FPG (mg/dL)/2) [15]; HOMA-IR= (FPG (mmol/L)* FINS (mIU/L))/22.5 [16]; SII= NEUT* PLT/ LYM [17].

Echocardiographic measurements

Echocardiographic examinations were performed using Philips iE33 and EPIQ7C Doppler ultrasound systems with a 1–5 MHz transducer. All echocardiographic examinations were performed and interpreted by two experienced echocardiographers who were blinded to patients’ clinical information; Intra- and interobserver variability were assessed in 30 randomly selected images, with intraclass correlation coefficients (ICCs) of 0.96 and 0.94, respectively, indicating good reproducibility. Parameters including left ventricular end-diastolic dimension (LVDd), left ventricular posterior wall thickness (LVPWT) and end-diastolic interventricular septal thickness (IVST), etc. were obtained by averaging measurements over three consecutive cardiac cycles. LVH was defined as LVMI ≥ 115 g/m2 in males or ≥ 95 g/m2 in females [18]. The following formulas were employed: LVM(g) = 0.8*1.04*((IVST+ LVPWT+ LVDd)³- LVDd³) + 0.6; BSA (㎡) =0.0061* height (cm) + 0.0128* weight (kg)-0.1529; LVMI(g/㎡) = LVM/ BSA.

Statistical analysis

IBM SPSS (version 26.0) and R Studio (version 4.5.1) were employed to analyze data in this study. A p-value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant. The normality of data distribution was evaluated with the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. Data conforming to a normal or approximately normal distribution were presented as mean ± SD, data with a non-normal distribution were expressed as median (interquartile range). Between-group comparisons performed using the Student’s t-test and Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical data were presented as counts or percentages (%), and between groups were analyzed using the chi-square test. Spearman’s or Pearson’s correlation analysis and multiple logistic regression analysis were employed to explore the relationships between TyG index, SII and LVH. Potential confounding factors were selected based on univariate regression analysis and multicollinearity assessment using the variance inflation factor (VIF). Subgroup analyses were conducted to test whether the associations were consistent across predefined patient subgroups. To construct the combined index, a binary logistic regression model was performed with LVH as the dependent variable and both TyG index and SII as independent variables. The predicted probability of LVH generated from this model was defined as the combined index, and this predicted probability was used as the test variable for ROC analysis. The ability of the combination of TyG index and SII to predict LVH in T2DM patients was assessed by plotting a ROC curve. Bootstrap internal validation with 1,000 resamples was performed to assess overfitting. Finally, the model was evaluated using clinical decision curve analysis (DCA) and calibration curve.

Results

Baseline characteristics

A total of 600 T2DM patients were included in this study, consisting of 377 males (62.8%) and 223 females(37.2%). The average age of all patients was 58.9 ± 12.12 years, with an average BMI of 24.83 ± 3.43 kg/m². According to the diagnostic criteria for LVH, participants were categorized into two groups: a group with LVH (N = 154) and a group without LVH (N = 446).

Table 1 shows significant intergroup differences in sex, age, diabetes duration and multiple clinical parameters (all P < 0.05). Compared to the NLVH group, the LVH group was characterized by older age, a lower proportion of males, a longer diabetes duration and a higher prevalence of hypertension. With regard to measured values, the LVH group had lower height, weight, LYM, Hb and eGFR but higher SBP, PLT, NEUT, FPG, 2hPG, TG, SCr, LVMI, TyG index, HOMA-IR, TG/HDL-C, SII, NLR and PLR. There were no statistically differences in BMI, WC, DBP, smoking history, alcohol consumption history, WBC, FINS, 2hINS, HbA1c, TC, HDL-C, LDL-C, ALT, AST or SUA between groups.

Correlation analysis of the related factors and LVMI

Spearman’s or Pearson’s correlation analysis revealed that age, diabetes duration, WC, SBP, PLT, NEUT, FPG, TG, SCr, TyG, SII, NLR, and PLR were all positively correlated with LVMI, with SII (r = 0.356, P<0.001) demonstrating a higher correlation coefficient with LVMI than NLR or PLR (r = 0.318, P<0.001; r = 0.282, P<0.001). Conversely, height, LYM, Hb, TC, LDL-C, and eGFR were inversely connected with LVMI (P < 0.05 for all). Weight, BMI, DBP, WBC, 2hPG, FINS, 2hINS, HbA1c, HDL-C, ALT, AST, SUA, HOMA-IR and TG/HDL-C showed no correlation with LVMI (P > 0.05).

Multivariate logistic regression analysis: risk factors for LVH in T2DM patients

To further explore the independent role of the TyG index and SII in LVH, multivariate logistic regression analyses were performed after accounting for collinearity. As shown in Table 2, LVH is independently associated with the TyG index after stepwise adjustment for confounding factors, (OR = 5.620, 95%CI: 2.882 ~ 10.959, P < 0.001). Similarly, SII is also an independent predictor for LVH after stepwise adjustment for confounding factors in Table 3 (OR = 1.014, 95%CI: 1.010 ~ 1.017, P < 0.001).

Subgroup analysis

To further evaluate the risk differences of TyG index, SII for the outcome LVH across different populations, subgroup analyses were conducted among 600 T2DM patients based on the following characteristics (sex, age, diabetes duration, and hypertension). As shown in Fig. 1A, there was no interaction between TyG index and subgroup variables (P for interaction > 0.05), and TyG index was connected with an increased risk of LVH in all subgroups. Similarly, there was no interaction between SII and subgroup variables (P for interaction > 0.05), and elevated SII was linked to higher LVH risk in all subgroups (Fig. 1B).

Discriminative ability for LVH risk

An assessment of the ability of TyG index, SII, and the combined index to predict LVH risk demonstrated that the combined analysis of TyG index and SII(AUC = 0.857, 95%CI: 0.826 ~ 0.888, sensitivity:84.4%, specificity:72.9%, P < 0.001) provided the strongest predictive capability for the occurrence of LVH as shown in Fig. 2. Bootstrap internal validation with 1,000 resamples yielded a corrected AUC of 0.857, identical to the original AUC, indicating no overfitting.

Model validation

Building on prior evidence indicating the superior predictive accuracy of the combined index for LVH, the present model was further evaluated using DCA and calibration curve analysis. DCA indicated that the combined analysis of the TyG index and SII provided significant clinical net benefit in predicting LVH among patients with T2DM (Fig. 3A). The calibration curve demonstrated good model fit (Fig. 3B). The Hosmer-Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test showed a chi-square value of 9.187 (P = 0.3268), suggesting that the predicted probability curve of the combined index was in good agreement with the ideal curve, and the model was well-calibrated.

Discussion

In this study, we investigated the correlation between TyG index, SII, and LVH. Four main findings were identified. Firstly, the TyG index and SII were higher in the LVH group. Secondly, TyG index and SII were positively correlated with LVMI. Thirdly, the TyG index and SII were independent predictors for LVH. Finally, the combined analysis of TyG index and SII provided the strongest discriminative ability for LVH.

The persistent hyperglycemic state in T2DM patients drives the accumulation of advanced glycosylation end products (AGEs), stimulating the production of profibrotic factors such as transforming growth factor-β [19]. This process leads to stiffening of the walls of large cardiac blood vessels, increasing cardiac afterload, and resulting in heart failure or even sudden cardiac death. Therefore, early identification and implementation of timely and effective treatment are essential for intervening in or even reversing LVH, which also contributes to improving long-term prognosis.

Previous studies have established that advanced age, obesity, hypertension, dyslipidemia, renal dysfunction and IR are significant risk factors for LVH [20–22]. In the present study, patients with LVH were older, had a longer diabetes duration, a higher prevalence of hypertension, and exhibited more adverse metabolic profiles, including elevated SBP, TG, SCr, TyG index, HOMA-IR and TG/HDL-C, consistent with prior evidence. Obesity is conventionally classified into two phenotypes based on adipose tissue distribution: generalized obesity and visceral obesity. BMI serves as a surrogate measure of generalized adiposity, whereas WC is a marker of abdominal fat accumulation. Although previous reports indicate higher adiposity indices in LVH populations, our study found BMI and WC were comparable between two groups, with no statistically significant differences. This discrepancy may be due to several factors. First, BMI cannot distinguish lean from fat mass and is limited in older populations where sarcopenia is prevalent [23]. Second, the lower proportion of male participants in the LVH group may have attenuated the difference in WC, given known sex differences in fat distribution [24]. Finally, the use of glucose-lowering medications (e.g., metformin) with potential effects on body weight could further confound this association, although detailed medication data were not available in this study. Given the higher mean age in the LVH group, comorbidities such as sarcopenia and osteopenia may have contributed to lower overall body weight. This could weaken the differences in BMI between groups by masking true differences in fat mass. In addition, the use of medications such as metformin may further narrow the differences in obesity indicators between groups. The cardiac protective effect of strict blood glucose control on T2DM patients has been well documented [25]. Our findings partially support this paradigm, demonstrating significantly higher FPG and 2hPG in the LVH group. HbA1c provides a measure of the average blood glucose level over the preceding three months and is widely used to assess overall glycemic control. However, no significant differences were observed in HbA1c levels among the groups, which might reflect the limitations of this indicator in capturing acute blood glucose fluctuations and its susceptibility to a mixture of behavioral and medication compliance factors. Beyond glucose metabolism, our observation of significantly lower Hb levels in the LVH group provides clinical support for the link between anemia-driven tissue hypoxia and LVH [26].

Previous studies on the association between sex and LVH have yielded inconsistent conclusions. A European prospective cohort study involving 12,329 patients with primary hypertension reported a significantly higher detection rate of LVH in women than in men (43.4% vs. 32.1%) [27]. In contrast, a Korean cohort study demonstrated higher baseline LVH prevalence in men versus women (27.0% vs. 19.3%) [28]. In present study, the baseline prevalence of LVH was significantly higher in women, and subgroup analysis further indicated that women faced a significantly elevated risk of developing LVH. The potential reasons for this sex difference are as follows: First, the diagnostic criteria for LVH differ by sex, with the cut-off value for LVMI typically lower in women. Secondly, the influence of sex hormones. Estrogen primarily binds to its receptors (ERα/ ERβ), directly acting on cardiomyocytes to inhibit myocardial fibroblast proliferation and reduce collagen deposition, thereby suppressing pathological myocardial hypertrophy. Additionally, estrogen confers cardiovascular protection through mechanisms such as mitigating inflammatory responses, increasing prostaglandin levels, and inhibiting the secretion of pro-atherogenic cytokines. The mean age of participants in this study was 58.9 ± 12.12 years, and most of the female participants were postmenopausal. Estrogen levels drop sharply, which diminishes the hormone’s cardioprotective effect and raises the risk of LVH.

In this research, the TyG index and SII were found to be higher in the LVH group. TyG index and SII were both positively associated with LVMI in correlation analysis, indicating that elevated TyG index and/or SII are related to a higher likelihood of ventricular structural remodeling. The synergistic effects of the TyG index and SII on LVH can be explained by several overlapping biological mechanisms. IR, reflected by the TyG index, promotes LVH through multiple pathways, including activation of the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system, sympathetic nervous system overactivity, and impaired myocardial glucose utilization, all of which contribute to pathological cardiac remodeling. Concurrently, systemic inflammation, reflected by the SII, drives LVH via pro-inflammatory cytokines (e.g., IL-6, TNF-α) that stimulate fibroblast proliferation and collagen deposition in the myocardium. Importantly, IR and chronic inflammation do not act in isolation; they reciprocally amplify each other. Hyperinsulinemia secondary to IR can directly enhance inflammatory responses by activating nuclear factor-κB signaling, while inflammatory cytokines can further worsen IR by interfering with insulin signaling pathways at the receptor and post-receptor levels. This bidirectional interaction creates a vicious cycle that may synergistically accelerate left ventricular remodeling. Therefore, combining TyG index and SII captures both dimensions of this pathophysiological interplay, which may explain why their combination provides superior discriminative ability compared with either index alone. Pooling cross-sectional data from 6,354,990 participants in general population, a meta-analysis demonstrated that for every 1-unit increase in TyG index, the risk of stroke increased by 35% and the risk of cardiovascular disease increased by 23% [29]. This meta-analysis provides important empirical support for our conclusions. After adjusting for confounding factors, both TyG index (OR = 5.620, 95%CI: 2.882 ~ 10.959) and SII (OR = 1.014, 95% CI: 1.010–1.017) correlated with LVH in T2DM. Subgroup analyses stratified by characteristics such as age and sex showed no significant interaction effects on the main associations, thereby enhancing the generalizability of findings.

In the present study, the optimal cutoff value for predicting LVH was 7.44 for the TyG index (AUC = 0.685) and 393.32 for the SII (AUC = 0.824), based on ROC curve analysis. A combined model integrating both markers demonstrated superior discriminatory ability, achieving an AUC of 0.857. This improved predictive performance suggests that the combined use of TyG index and SII could offer more accurate risk stratification in clinical practice. Further evaluation using decision curve analysis confirmed that this combined approach provides a significant clinical net benefit for predicting LVH in T2DM patients. Besides, the calibration curve showed that predicted probability closely matched the actual rate, supporting the model’s reliability.

Our study suggests that TyG index combined with SII could better assess the risk of LVH in T2DM patients. When T2DM patients are admitted to the hospital, by calculating the TyG index and SII of the patients, high-risk groups with left ventricular hypertrophy can be effectively identified. For T2DM patients with elevated TyG index and SII, further echocardiography is required, which is helpful for the early detection of left ventricular hypertrophy and timely intervention, and may improve the clinical outcomes of T2DM patients.

This study has limitations. To begin with, this is a cross-sectional research and cannot clearly infer the causal relationship among the TyG index, SII and LVH. Prospective cohort studies are still warranted in the future to validate causal associations. In addition, as a single-center research with a relatively small sample size and selection bias, it has certain limitations in identifying and verifying confounding factors. It is necessary to further expand the sample size or develop a multi-center clinical study. Finally, medication use was not recorded in this study. Antihypertensive therapies (e.g., ACE inhibitors, ARBs, beta-blockers) and antidiabetic agents (e.g., metformin, SGLT2 inhibitors, GLP-1 receptor agonists) may significantly influence LVH, inflammatory status, and insulin resistance. Recent evidence has demonstrated that SGLT2 inhibitors improve subclinical left ventricular systolic function in T2DM patients [30]. The absence of adjustment for these medications represents an important source of potential residual confounding. Future prospective studies should collect detailed medication histories to allow appropriate statistical adjustment. Additionally, although bootstrap validation suggested minimal overfitting, external validation in an independent cohort is still lacking.

Conclusions

This study demonstrates that increased TyG index and SII levels are positively correlated with LVMI and are independent predictors of LVH in T2DM. The combined assessment of TyG index and SII has a better ability to predict LVH in T2DM patients and is well calibrated. In addition, this model has significant application value in clinical decision-making and can effectively guide clinical practice.
