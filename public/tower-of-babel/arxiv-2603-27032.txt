Asymptotic Universal Koszulity in Galois Cohomology
Marina Palaisti

Abstract
. We introduce the notion of asymptotic universal Koszulity for graded-commutative algebras generated in degree 1 1 , capturing the idea that an infinite-dimensional algebra can be approximated by a filtered system of finite-type universally Koszul quadratic subalgebras. We establish basic structural properties of this class, including stability under filtered colimits, direct products, and base change, as well as a local finite-type criterion expressed in terms of finite-dimensional subspaces of the degree-one component. In the context of Galois cohomology, we prove a colimit theorem for pro- p p groups under mild assumptions, showing that cohomology rings arise as filtered colimits of finite quotients. This yields a general criterion under which the cohomology algebra of a profinite group is asymptotically universally Koszul. We further analyze finitely generated quotients via a finite-type capture result, identifying their cohomology with canonical quadratic subalgebras of the ambient algebra. Finally, we formulate conditional local–global and patching principles that isolate the mechanisms by which asymptotic universal Koszulity may arise in arithmetic settings. These results provide a flexible structural framework linking homological algebra, quadratic algebras, and Galois cohomology, and suggest several directions for further investigation.



Keywords:
Galois cohomology; asymptotic universal Koszulity; pro-
p
groups; quadratic algebras; filtered colimits; local–global descent; patching methods

1.
Introduction

Let
p
be a fixed prime and let
K\supset\mu_{p}
be a field. Write

G_{K}^{(p)}:=\mathrm{Gal}(K(p)/K)\quad\text{and}\quad A_{K}^{\bullet}:=H^{\bullet}(G_{K}^{(p)},\mathbb{F}_{p})

for the maximal pro-
p
Galois group of
K
and its mod-
p
cohomology ring. The structure of
A_{K}^{\bullet}
encodes deep arithmetic information about
K
, and in many important cases it exhibits strong homological regularity properties. When
G_{K}^{(p)}
is finitely generated, the algebra
A_{K}^{\bullet}
is often quadratic and satisfies refined linearity conditions such as Koszulity or universal Koszulity. These properties impose rigid constraints on the Galois group and play a central role in recent work on enhanced Koszul phenomena in Galois cohomology
[
11
,
12
,
13
]
. In particular, for several classes of local and global fields and their finite extensions, the cohomology algebra is known to be quadratic and universally Koszul. In contrast, for many natural fields – including infinite extensions, composita, and function fields– the group
G_{K}^{(p)}
is not finitely generated and
H^{1}(G_{K}^{(p)},\mathbb{F}_{p})
is infinite-dimensional. In this setting, the classical finite-type framework for Koszul algebras breaks down, and it is not clear how to extend linearity properties such as universal Koszulity to the infinite-type case.

The goal of this paper is to introduce and study a notion that captures the residual finite-type linearity present in such situations. The guiding principle is that, although global Koszul duality phenomena may fail in infinite type, linearity can still be recovered on finite collections of degree-one classes. We formalize this idea by introducing
asymptotic universal Koszulity
. Roughly speaking, a graded algebra generated in degree
1
is asymptotically universally Koszul if it can be expressed as a filtered colimit of finite-type quadratic algebras that are universally Koszul, and if every finite-dimensional subspace of the degree-one part is captured inside one of these finite-type pieces. This definition provides a local-to-global framework for extending Koszul-type methods beyond the finitely generated setting. Our first main result shows that this notion is stable under filtered colimits of finite-type universally Koszul algebras and admits a concrete finite-dimensional formulation.

We then apply this framework to Galois cohomology. Under suitable compatibility assumptions on finite quotients, we show that the algebra
H^{\bullet}(G_{K}^{(p)},\mathbb{F}_{p})
is asymptotically universally Koszul. This reduces the problem to verifying finite-type Koszul properties on a directed system of finitely generated quotients together with compatibility of cohomology under passage to the limit. Beyond this quotient-based approach, we develop a general
local-global descent principle
. This principle isolates a mechanism by which asymptotic universal Koszulity can be deduced from local data: if every finite set of global cohomology classes can be detected inside a finite local comparison algebra that is finite-type and universally Koszul, in a manner compatible with enlarging the set of classes, then the global cohomology algebra inherits asymptotic universal Koszulity. This formulation is flexible and is designed to interact with existing local-global principles and patching techniques in Galois cohomology.

As an illustration, we formulate a patching framework for function fields of curves over complete discretely valued fields, following the work of Harbater, Hartmann, and Krashen
[
15
,
17
]
. Within this framework, asymptotic universal Koszulity can be reduced to verifying finite-type universally Koszul models for suitable local fields, together with compatibility of the associated local-global maps on finite-dimensional subspaces of
H^{1}
.

The results of this paper provide a first systematic extension of universal Koszulity to infinite-type Galois cohomology. Rather than attempting to impose global homological conditions, the asymptotic approach isolates the finite-dimensional data that control the algebra and organizes it into a coherent filtered structure. This perspective opens the door to new interactions between homological algebra, pro-
p
Galois theory, and local-global principles.

We conclude with several questions concerning the scope of asymptotic universal Koszulity, its possible strengthening to obstruction results for Galois groups, and its behavior in arithmetic settings arising from patching and composita constructions.

2.
Quadratic algebras and universal Koszulity

Definition 2.1
.

A connected graded
k
-algebra
A=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}A_{n}
, generated in degree
1
, is
quadratic
if

A\cong T(V)/\langle R\rangle,

for a (possibly infinite-dimensional) vector space
V=A_{1}
and a subspace
R\subset V\otimes_{k}V
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
A
be a quadratic algebra with
\dim_{k}A_{1}<\infty
. Let

\mathcal{L}(A):=\{\,I\subseteq A\mid I\text{ is a graded left ideal and }I=AI_{1}\,\}

be the set of graded left ideals generated in degree
1
. We say that
A
is
universally Koszul
if for every
I\in\mathcal{L}(A)
and every
x\in A_{1}\setminus I_{1}
, the left colon ideal

I:x\;:=\;\{\,a\in A\mid ax\in I\,\}

belongs to
\mathcal{L}(A)
(equivalently,
I:x
is generated in degree
1
).

Remark 2.3
.

If
A
is graded-commutative (as in mod-
p
cohomology rings), then “graded left ideal” coincides with “graded ideal”, and the colon ideal
I:x
is two-sided.

We restrict attention to quadratic algebras with finite-dimensional degree-one part. In this finite-type setting, universal Koszulity admits several equivalent formulations, including characterizations in terms of colon ideals and linear resolutions of cyclic modules (see, e.g.,
[
11
,
13
]
). In particular, every cyclic module
A/I
, with
I
generated in degree
1
, admits a linear minimal free resolution, a perspective that is often useful in establishing stability properties in concrete situations. These equivalences and homological consequences depend fundamentally on finiteness assumptions and do not extend to infinite-type settings, which necessitates the asymptotic framework developed in the next section.

3.
Asymptotic universal Koszulity

Throughout this section, let
k
be a field.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
A
be a connected graded
k
-algebra generated in degree
1
. We say that
A
is
asymptotically universally Koszul
if there exists a filtered system of quadratic graded subalgebras
\displaystyle(A_{i})_{i\in I}\subset A
such that:

(i)

each
A_{i}
is generated in degree
1
with
\dim_{k}(A_{i})_{1}<\infty
;

(ii)

each
A_{i}
is universally Koszul;

(iii)

the natural map
\displaystyle\varinjlim_{i\in I}A_{i}\to A
is an isomorphism
of graded algebras;

(iv)

every finite-dimensional subspace of
A_{1}
is contained in some

(A_{i})_{1}
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(A_{i})_{i\in I}
be a filtered system of quadratic graded
k
-algebras with injective transition maps such that each
A_{i}
is finite-type and universally Koszul. Then
A:=\displaystyle\varinjlim_{i\in I}A_{i}
is asymptotically universally Koszul.

Proof.

We identify each
A_{i}
with its image in
A
via the injective structure maps.
Let
W\subseteq A_{1}
be finite-dimensional and choose a basis
w_{1},\dots,w_{r}
of
W
. For each
j
, pick
i_{j}\in I
such that
w_{j}\in(A_{i_{j}})_{1}
.
Since
I
is filtered, there exists
k\in I
such that
i_{j}\leq k
for all
j
, hence
(A_{i_{j}})_{1}\subseteq(A_{k})_{1}
for all
j
. Therefore
W\subseteq(A_{k})_{1}
. By construction of the filtered colimit in the category of graded algebras, the canonical map
\varinjlim_{i}A_{i}\to A
is an isomorphism. Thus all
conditions in Definition
3.1
are satisfied.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
A
be a connected graded
k
-algebra generated in degree
1
. Suppose that for every finite-dimensional subspace
W\subset A_{1}
, there exists a quadratic subalgebra
B_{W}\subset A
such that:

(a)

W\subset(B_{W})_{1}
and
\dim_{k}(B_{W})_{1}<\infty
;

(b)

B_{W}
is universally Koszul;

and suppose moreover that the family
(B_{W})
can be chosen to be filtered under inclusion. Then
A
is asymptotically universally Koszul.

Proof.

By assumption, every finite-dimensional subspace of
A_{1}
is contained in some
(B_{W})_{1}
. Since
A
is generated in degree
1
, every homogeneous element of
A
lies in a subalgebra generated by finitely many degree-one elements, hence lies in some
B_{W}
. Therefore

A=\bigcup_{W}B_{W}

as graded algebras. Since the family
(B_{W})
is filtered under inclusion, the natural map
\displaystyle\varinjlim_{W}B_{W}\longrightarrow A
is an isomorphism. The conclusion follows from Definition
3.1
.
∎

Please note that the filteredness assumption in Proposition
3.3
is essential. In general, the existence of finite-type universally Koszul subalgebras containing prescribed finite-dimensional subspaces does not guarantee that these subalgebras can be arranged into a filtered system under inclusion.

4.
Local finite-type structure

We now give an intrinsic formulation of asymptotic universal Koszulity in terms of finite-dimensional subspaces of the degree-one part. Throughout this section, let
k
be a field, and let
A=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}A_{n}
be a connected graded-commutative quadratic
k
-algebra generated in degree
1
.

For any finite-dimensional subspace
W\subseteq A_{1}
, we denote by

B_{W}:=\langle W\rangle_{A}

the graded subalgebra generated by
W
. Since
A
is quadratic,
B_{W}

is again quadratic, with relation space

R_{B_{W}}=R_{A}\cap(W\otimes W).

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
A
be a connected graded-commutative quadratic
k
-algebra generated in degree
1
. Suppose that for every finite-dimensional subspace
W\subseteq A_{1}
, the canonical quadratic subalgebra
B_{W}
is universally Koszul. Then
A
is the directed union of finite-type universally Koszul quadratic subalgebras.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{W}
be the poset of finite-dimensional subspaces of
A_{1}
, ordered by inclusion. This poset is directed: given
W,W^{\prime}
, the sum
W+W^{\prime}
is again finite-dimensional and contains both. For each
W\in\mathcal{W}
, the algebra
B_{W}
is quadratic, generated in degree
1
, and finite-type by construction. By assumption, it is universally Koszul. Moreover, if
W\subseteq W^{\prime}
, then
B_{W}\subseteq B_{W^{\prime}}
, since
B_{W}=\langle W\rangle_{A}\subseteq\langle W^{\prime}\rangle_{A}=B_{W^{\prime}}
. Thus the family
(B_{W})_{W\in\mathcal{W}}
forms a directed system. Finally, since
A
is generated in degree
1
, every homogeneous element of
A
lies in the subalgebra generated by finitely many elements of
A_{1}
, hence in some
B_{W}
. Therefore

A=\bigcup_{W\in\mathcal{W}}B_{W},

and the union is directed. The result follows.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
A
be a connected graded algebra generated in degree
1
. Suppose that
A
is the directed union of finite-type universally Koszul quadratic subalgebras. Then
A
is asymptotically universally Koszul.

Proof.

This is immediate from Definition
3.1
. The given directed system satisfies conditions (i)-(iv) by construction.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

Propositions
4.1
and
4.2
show that a local condition on finite-dimensional subspaces of
A_{1}
implies asymptotic universal Koszulity. In particular, if all canonical quadratic subalgebras
B_{W}
are universally Koszul, then
A
is asymptotically universally Koszul. The converse direction, namely whether asymptotic universal Koszulity forces each
B_{W}
to be universally Koszul, appears to require a descent principle for quadratic subalgebras and is not established here.

5.
Structural theorems

In this section we establish structural results about asymptotically universally Koszul algebras: a subalgebra theorem under a compatibility condition, a direct-product theorem, and a base-change theorem. Throughout,
k
is a field and all graded algebras are connected, generated in degree
1
, and graded-commutative.

We begin with a restricted form of the PBW-type statement sufficient
for controlling quadratic relations.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
C
be a Koszul quadratic
k
-algebra generated in degree
1
,
with relation space
R_{C}\subseteq C_{1}\otimes_{k}C_{1}
. Let

W\subseteq C_{1}
be a
k
-subspace and define

D:=T(W)/\langle R_{C}\cap(W\otimes W)\rangle.

Then the natural map
D_{2}\to C_{2}
is injective.

Proof.

Since
C
is quadratic, the map
C_{1}^{\otimes 2}\to C_{2}
has kernel exactly
R_{C}
. Restricting to
W^{\otimes 2}
, the kernel is therefore
R_{C}\cap(W\otimes W)
, which gives the claim.
∎

In general, universal Koszulity does not descend to arbitrary quadratic subalgebras. The following result isolates a natural situation where it does.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
C
be a finite-type universally Koszul quadratic
k
-algebra, graded-commutative, and suppose that

C_{1}=W\oplus U

as
k
-vector spaces, with the property that the quadratic relations split:

R_{C}=R_{W}\oplus R_{U},\quad R_{W}\subseteq W\otimes W,\;R_{U}\subseteq U\otimes U.

Let
D:=\langle W\rangle\subseteq C
be the graded subalgebra generated by
W
. Then
D
is a finite-type universally Koszul quadratic algebra.

Proof.

Under the splitting assumption, there are no quadratic relations mixing
W
and
U
, so
D
identifies with the quadratic algebra
T(W)/\langle R_{W}\rangle
. Let
J\in\mathcal{L}(D)
and
y\in W\setminus J_{1}
. Viewing
J
inside
C
, we may consider
I:=C\cdot J_{1}\in\mathcal{L}(C)
. Since
C
is universally Koszul,
I:_{C}y=C\cdot M
is generated in degree
1
. Because there are no relations mixing
W
and
U
, multiplication by
y
does not introduce terms outside
W
, and the intersection
(I:_{C}y)\cap D
is exactly
D\cdot(M\cap W)
. Thus

J:_{D}y=(I:_{C}y)\cap D=D\cdot(M\cap W)

which is generated in degree
1
. Hence
D
is universally Koszul.
∎

We now obtain a subalgebra theorem under a natural compatibility
assumption.

Theorem 5.3

(Finite-type compatible subalgebras)
.

Let
A
be an asymptotically universally Koszul algebra with filtered system
(A_{i})_{i\in I}
of finite-type universally Koszul quadratic subalgebras.

Let
B\subseteq A
be a connected graded subalgebra generated in degree
1
with
\dim_{k}B_{1}<\infty
. Suppose that there exists
i\in I
such that:

(i)

B_{1}\subseteq(A_{i})_{1}
,

(ii)

(A_{i})_{1}=B_{1}\oplus U
for some subspace
U
,

(iii)

the quadratic relations of
A_{i}
split as
R_{A_{i}}=R_{B}\oplus R_{U}
.

Then
B
is a finite-type universally Koszul quadratic algebra.

Proof.

By assumption,
B
is a quadratic subalgebra of
A_{i}
satisfying the compatibility conditions of Lemma
5.2
. Since
A_{i}
is finite-type universally Koszul, the lemma implies that
B
is universally Koszul. Finite generation follows from
\dim_{k}B_{1}<\infty
.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

The assumptions of Theorem
5.3
are automatically satisfied when
B_{1}
is a coordinate subspace of
(A_{i})_{1}
, i.e. when
(A_{i})_{1}
admits a basis such that
B_{1}
is spanned by a subset and the relations respect this decomposition. This occurs in many natural constructions, including certain filtered systems arising in Galois cohomology.

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
A
be a connected graded
k
-algebra generated in degree
1
which is asymptotically universally Koszul, and let
B\subseteq A
be a graded subalgebra generated in degree
1
with
\dim_{k}B_{1}<\infty
. Suppose there exists
i
such that:

(i)

B_{1}\subseteq(A_{i})_{1}
,

(ii)

(A_{i})_{1}=B_{1}\oplus U
for some subspace
U
,

(iii)

the quadratic relations split, i.e.
R_{A_{i}}=R_{B}\oplus R_{U}
.

Then
B
is a finite-type universally Koszul quadratic algebra.

Proof.

Follows from Theorem
5.3
.∎

Theorem 5.6

(Direct products of asymptotically universally Koszul algebras)
.

Let
A
and
B
be asymptotically universally Koszul
k
-algebras. Then the graded direct product
A\times B
, defined degree-wise by
(A\times B)_{n}:=A_{n}\oplus B_{n}
, is asymptotically universally Koszul.

Proof.

Let
(A_{i})_{i\in I}
and
(B_{j})_{j\in J}
be the filtered systems witnessing the asymptotic universal Koszulity of
A
and
B
, respectively (cf. Definition
3.1
). Consider the directed set
I\times J
with componentwise partial order, and for each
(i,j)\in I\times J
set

P_{ij}\;:=\;A_{i}\times B_{j},

defined degree-wise as
(P_{ij})_{n}:=(A_{i})_{n}\oplus(B_{j})_{n}
with multiplication
(a,b)(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})=(aa^{\prime},bb^{\prime})
. We verify the four conditions of Definition
3.1
for the system
(P_{ij})_{(i,j)\in I\times J}
.

(i)

(P_{ij})_{1}=(A_{i})_{1}\oplus(B_{j})_{1}
, which has dimension
\dim_{k}(A_{i})_{1}+\dim_{k}(B_{j})_{1}<\infty
.

(ii)

Quadraticity is immediate: the relation space of
A_{i}\times B_{j}
is

R_{A_{i}}\oplus R_{B_{j}}\subseteq(A_{i})_{1}^{\otimes 2}\oplus(B_{j})_{1}^{\otimes 2}=(P_{ij})_{1}^{\otimes 2},

since there are no cross-term relations. To check universal Koszulity, let
K\in\mathcal{L}(P_{ij})
, generated in degree
1
by a subspace
K_{1}\subseteq(P_{ij})_{1}
. Let

\pi_{A}\colon(P_{ij})_{1}\to(A_{i})_{1}\text{ and }\pi_{B}\colon(P_{ij})_{1}\to(B_{j})_{1}

be the coordinate projections, and set
K_{1}^{A}:=\pi_{A}(K_{1})\subseteq(A_{i})_{1}
and
K_{1}^{B}:=\pi_{B}(K_{1})\subseteq(B_{j})_{1}
. Define
I_{A}:=A_{i}\cdot K_{1}^{A}\in\mathcal{L}(A_{i})
and
I_{B}:=B_{j}\cdot K_{1}^{B}\in\mathcal{L}(B_{j})
.

Since
(P_{ij})_{0}=k\cdot e_{A}\oplus k\cdot e_{B}
with orthogonal idempotents
e_{A}=(1_{A_{i}},0)
and
e_{B}=(0,1_{B_{j}})
, any
(a,b)\in K_{n}
satisfies
(a,0)=e_{A}\cdot(a,b)\in K_{n}
and
(0,b)=e_{B}\cdot(a,b)\in K_{n}
for
n\geq 1
. Thus
K_{n}=\pi_{A}(K_{n})\times\pi_{B}(K_{n})
for all
n\geq 1
. Moreover,
K\subseteq I_{A}\times I_{B}
and the projections of
K_{n}
onto
(A_{i})_{n}
and
(B_{j})_{n}
contain
(I_{A})_{n}
and
(I_{B})_{n}
, so
K_{n}=(I_{A})_{n}\times(I_{B})_{n}
.

Let
z=(y_{A},y_{B})\in(P_{ij})_{1}\setminus K_{1}
and consider the colon ideal
K:_{P_{ij}}z
. For
(a,b)\in P_{ij}
,

(a,b)\cdot z\in K\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad ay_{A}\in I_{A}\ \text{and}\ by_{B}\in I_{B},

so

K:_{P_{ij}}z=(I_{A}:_{A_{i}}y_{A})\times(I_{B}:_{B_{j}}y_{B}).

Since
z\notin K_{1}=(I_{A})_{1}\times(I_{B})_{1}
, at least one of
y_{A}\notin(I_{A})_{1}
or
y_{B}\notin(I_{B})_{1}
holds. Universal Koszulity of
A_{i}
and
B_{j}
implies that each colon ideal
I_{A}:_{A_{i}}y_{A}
and
I_{B}:_{B_{j}}y_{B}
is in
\mathcal{L}(A_{i})

or
\mathcal{L}(B_{j})
(or equals the whole algebra). Hence
K:_{P_{ij}}z\in\mathcal{L}(P_{ij})
, so
P_{ij}
is universally Koszul.

(iii)

Filtered colimits commute with finite products, so

\varinjlim_{(i,j)}(A_{i}\times B_{j})\;\cong\;\Bigl(\varinjlim_{i}A_{i}\Bigr)\times\Bigl(\varinjlim_{j}B_{j}\Bigr)\;\cong\;A\times B.

(iv)

Let
V\subseteq(A\times B)_{1}=A_{1}\oplus B_{1}
be finite-dimensional. Its projections
V_{A}\subseteq A_{1}
and
V_{B}\subseteq B_{1}
are finite-dimensional. By condition (iv) for
A
and
B
, there exist
i,j
with
V_{A}\subseteq(A_{i})_{1}
and
V_{B}\subseteq(B_{j})_{1}
. Then
V\subseteq V_{A}\oplus V_{B}\subseteq(P_{ij})_{1}
.

Therefore
A\times B
is asymptotically universally Koszul.
∎

Corollary 5.7
.

Let
A
and
B
be asymptotically universally Koszul
k
-algebras in the sense of Definition
3.1
. Then their graded direct product
A\times B
, viewed as a graded algebra (not necessarily connected), is asymptotically universally Koszul.

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
C
be a finite-type universally Koszul quadratic
k
-algebra, graded-commutative, and let
L/k
be a field extension. Then
C_{L}:=C\otimes_{k}L
is a finite-type universally Koszul quadratic
L
-algebra.

Proof.

That
C_{L}
is quadratic over
L
is clear, since

C_{L}\;\cong\;T_{L}(C_{1}\otimes_{k}L)\big/\langle R_{C}\otimes_{k}L\rangle,

and
(C_{L})_{1}=C_{1}\otimes_{k}L
has
L
-dimension
\dim_{k}C_{1}<\infty
.

We verify the colon-ideal criterion for
C_{L}
over
L
. Let
J\subseteq C_{L}
be a graded left ideal generated in degree
1
over
L
, with generating subspace
J_{1}\subseteq(C_{L})_{1}=C_{1}\otimes_{k}L
.

Step 1: Descending
J_{1}
to
k
.
Choose a
k
-basis
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}
of
C_{1}
. Then
(C_{L})_{1}\cong L^{n}
as an
L
-vector space. There exists a unique
k
-subspace
W\subseteq C_{1}
such that
J_{1}=W\otimes_{k}L
; indeed, any finite-dimensional
L
-subspace of
C_{1}\otimes_{k}L
arises as
W\otimes_{k}L
for a uniquely determined
W
. Set
I_{0}:=C\cdot W\in\mathcal{L}(C)
. Then

J=C_{L}\cdot J_{1}=(C\cdot W)\otimes_{k}L=I_{0}\otimes_{k}L.

Step 2: Form of the colon ideal.

Let
y\in(C_{L})_{1}\setminus J_{1}
. Write

y\;=\;\sum_{s=1}^{r}x_{s}\otimes\lambda_{s},

where
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\}
is a
k
-basis of
C_{1}
and
\lambda_{s}\in L
. Reorder the basis so that
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{d}\}
is a
k
-basis of
W
and
\{x_{d+1},\dots,x_{r}\}
is a
k
-basis of a
k
-complement of
W
in
C_{1}
. Since
y\notin W\otimes_{k}L
, at least one
\lambda_{s}
with
s>d
is nonzero.

For each
s>d
, consider the ideal
I_{0}:_{C}x_{s}
. By universal Koszulity of
C
and the fact that
x_{s}\notin(I_{0})_{1}=W
for
s>d
, we have
I_{0}:_{C}x_{s}\in\mathcal{L}(C)
, i.e. it is generated in degree
1
. We claim that

(5.1)

J:_{C_{L}}y\;=\;\Bigl(\bigcap_{s>d}I_{0}:_{C}x_{s}\Bigr)\otimes_{k}L.

Indeed, let
\xi\in(C_{L})_{n}
with
\xi\cdot y\in J=I_{0}\otimes_{k}L
. Write
\xi=\sum_{t}c_{t}\otimes\mu_{t}
with
c_{t}\in C_{n}
and
\mu_{t}\in L
. Then

\xi\cdot y\;=\;\sum_{t,s}(c_{t}x_{s})\otimes\mu_{t}\lambda_{s}.

The condition
\xi\cdot y\in I_{0}\otimes_{k}L
is equivalent to
\sum_{t}\mu_{t}(c_{t}x_{s})\in I_{0}
for every
s
such that
\lambda_{s}\neq 0
, by
k
-linear independence of the
x_{s}
and the identification
I_{0}\otimes_{k}L\cong I_{0}\otimes_{k}L
. In particular, for each
s>d
with
\lambda_{s}\neq 0
we require
\sum_{t}\mu_{t}(c_{t}x_{s})\in I_{0}
, i.e.
\xi\in(I_{0}:_{C}x_{s})\otimes_{k}L
. Thus

\xi\in J:_{C_{L}}y\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\xi\in\bigcap_{s>d,\ \lambda_{s}\neq 0}(I_{0}:_{C}x_{s})\otimes_{k}L,

which is the same as (
5.1
) when we intersect over all
s>d
.

Step 3: Intersections of degree-one generated ideals.
In a finite-type universally Koszul algebra
C
, the class
\mathcal{L}(C)
is closed under finite intersections, i.e. if
I,I^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}(C)
, then
I\cap I^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}(C)
. Indeed,
I\cap I^{\prime}
is the kernel of the map
C\to C/I\oplus C/I^{\prime}
and both quotients have linear resolutions, so the kernel also has a linear resolution and belongs to
\mathcal{L}(C)
. Iterating this, any finite intersection of ideals in
\mathcal{L}(C)
lies in
\mathcal{L}(C)
.

Applying this to the ideals
I_{0}:_{C}x_{s}
for
s>d
, we conclude that

I^{\prime}\;:=\;\bigcap_{s>d}I_{0}:_{C}x_{s}

belongs to
\mathcal{L}(C)
and is therefore generated in degree
1
. Let
N:=(I^{\prime})_{1}\subseteq C_{1}
; then
I^{\prime}=C\cdot N
.

By (
5.1
) we have

J:_{C_{L}}y=I^{\prime}\otimes_{k}L=(C\cdot N)\otimes_{k}L=C_{L}\cdot(N\otimes_{k}L),

so
J:_{C_{L}}y
is generated in degree
1
as a left ideal of
C_{L}
.

We have shown that for every graded left ideal
J\subseteq C_{L}
generated in degree
1
and every
y\in(C_{L})_{1}\setminus J_{1}
, the colon
J:_{C_{L}}y
is generated in degree
1
. Thus
C_{L}
is universally Koszul over
L
. Since
C_{L}
is quadratic with
\dim_{L}(C_{L})_{1}<\infty
, it is a finite-type universally Koszul quadratic
L
-algebra.
∎

Theorem 5.9

(Base change for asymptotically universally Koszul algebras)
.

Let
A
be an asymptotically universally Koszul
k
-algebra and let
L/k
be a field extension. Then
A_{L}:=A\otimes_{k}L
is an asymptotically universally Koszul graded
L
-algebra.

Proof.

Let
(A_{i})_{i\in I}
be the filtered system witnessing the asymptotic universal Koszulity of
A
(cf. Definition
3.1
). For each
i\in I
set

(A_{i})_{L}\;:=\;A_{i}\otimes_{k}L.

We verify conditions (i)-(iv) of Definition
3.1
for the system
((A_{i})_{L})_{i\in I}
over the ground field
L
.

(i)

((A_{i})_{L})_{1}=(A_{i})_{1}\otimes_{k}L
has
L
-dimension equal to
\dim_{k}(A_{i})_{1}<\infty
. Since
A_{i}
is generated in degree
1
over
k
,
(A_{i})_{L}
is generated in degree
1
over
L
.

(ii)

By Lemma
5.8
,
(A_{i})_{L}
is universally Koszul over
L
.

(iii)

Tensor product commutes with filtered colimits, that is,

\varinjlim_{i\in I}(A_{i}\otimes_{k}L)\cong\Bigl(\varinjlim_{i\in I}A_{i}\Bigr)\otimes_{k}L\cong A\otimes_{k}L=A_{L}.

(iv)

Let
V\subseteq(A_{L})_{1}=A_{1}\otimes_{k}L
be a finite-dimensional
L
-subspace. Choose an

-basis
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{m}\}
of

. Each

can be written as
v_{\ell}=\sum_{s}a_{\ell s}\otimes\lambda_{\ell s}
with
a_{\ell s}\in A_{1}
and
\lambda_{\ell s}\in L
. Let

be the finite set of all

and let

. Then

is finite-dimensional. By condition (iv) for

, there is an index

such that

. Consequently
V\subseteq W\otimes_{k}L\subseteq(A_{i})_{1}\otimes_{k}L=((A_{i})_{L})_{1}
.

Therefore

is asymptotically universally Koszul over

.
∎

Corollary 5.10
.

Let
A
be an asymptotically universally Koszul
k
-algebra and let
L/k
be a field extension. Then the base-changed algebra
A_{L}:=A\otimes_{k}L
, viewed as a graded
L
-algebra, is asymptotically universally Koszul.

We record a brief discussion of situations where the assumptions of Proposition
6.3
are expected to hold, illustrating the scope of asymptotic universal Koszulity in Galois cohomology.

Example 5.11

(Fields with universally Koszul finite quotients)
.

Let
p
be a prime and
K
a field containing
\mu_{p}
. In several classes of arithmetic fields, the maximal pro-
p
Galois group
G_{K}^{(p)}
is known to have mod-
p
cohomology
H^{\bullet}(G_{K}^{(p)})
which is quadratic and exhibits strong Koszul-type properties. For instance:

•

For many local and global fields and their finite extensions, results of Mináč, Palaisti, Pasini, Tân, Quadrelli and others establish that
H^{\bullet}(G_{K}^{(p)})
is quadratic and, in many cases, universally (or enhanced) Koszul; see, for example,
[
11
,
13
]
.

•

Recent composita stability theorems for enhanced Koszul properties in Galois cohomology (e.g.
[
26
]
) construct large classes of fields obtained from such local and global fields by admissible composita and patching, for which similar quadratic and Koszul-type properties persist.

Fix such a field
K
and write
G=G_{K}^{(p)}
. Let
(N_{i})_{i\in I}
be a cofinal directed system of open normal subgroups of
G
with
\bigcap_{i}N_{i}=1
, and set
G_{i}=G/N_{i}
. In the situations described above, each
G_{i}
arises as the maximal pro-
p
Galois group of a finite extension
K_{i}/K
, and the cited results indicate that
H^{\bullet}(G_{i})
is a finite-type quadratic algebra which is, in many cases, universally Koszul.

Moreover, for natural choices of
(N_{i})
(for example those arising from patching and composita constructions in
[
15
,
17
,
26
]
), the inflation maps

\inf^{n}_{G_{i}}\colon H^{n}(G_{i})\longrightarrow H^{n}(G)

are known to be injective for
n=1,2
, and the ring
H^{\bullet}(G)
is generated in degree
1
by
H^{1}(G)
(by Bloch-Kato).

Under these conditions, the assumptions of Proposition
6.3
are expected to hold, and one is led to the conclusion that
H^{\bullet}(G_{K}^{(p)})
is asymptotically universally Koszul.

We emphasize that this conclusion is conditional on the cited quadraticity and Koszul-type results for the finite quotients, as well as on the injectivity of inflation in low degrees, which are verified in specific arithmetic settings but are not proved here.

6.
Galois cohomology

Let
p
be a prime. All cohomology groups are continuous cohomology with
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-coefficients. For a profinite group
G
, we write

H^{\bullet}(G):=H^{\bullet}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p}),

viewed as a graded-commutative
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra via the cup product.

We begin by introducing the notion of asymptotic universal Koszulity in
this setting.

Definition 6.1
.

We say that a field
K
has
asymptotically universally Koszul cohomology
if the graded algebra
H^{\bullet}(G_{K}^{(p)},\mathbb{F}_{p})
is asymptotically universally Koszul.

We first establish a colimit description for cohomology rings of pro-
p
groups under mild assumptions. The key point is that the ring-level colimit follows from the degree-
1
colimit together with generation in degree
1
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
G
be a pro-
p
group and let
(N_{i})_{i\in I}
be a cofinal directed system of closed normal subgroups of
G
with
\displaystyle\bigcap_{i\in I}N_{i}=1
. Set
G_{i}=G/N_{i}
. Assume:

(i)

H^{\bullet}(G)
is generated as an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra by
H^{1}(G)
;

(ii)

for each
i\in I
and each
n\in\{1,2\}
, the inflation map

\inf^{n}_{G_{i}}\colon H^{n}(G_{i})\to H^{n}(G)

is injective.

Then the inflation maps induce an isomorphism of graded
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebras

\varinjlim_{i\in I}H^{\bullet}(G_{i})\;\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\;H^{\bullet}(G).

Proof.

We proceed degree by degree. In degree 1, since
G=\varprojlim_{i}G_{i}
, every continuous homomorphism
G\to\mathbb{F}_{p}
factors through a finite quotient. Thus

H^{1}(G)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{cts}}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})=\varinjlim_{i}\operatorname{Hom}(G_{i},\mathbb{F}_{p})=\varinjlim_{i}H^{1}(G_{i}),

from
[
3
, Proposition 1.5.1]
.

Now, by assumption (i),, every element of
H^{n}(G)
is a cup product

\alpha=\alpha_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\alpha_{n},\qquad\alpha_{j}\in H^{1}(G).

Each
\alpha_{j}
lifts from some
H^{1}(G_{i_{j}})
, and since the system is directed, there exists
i_{0}
with
i_{0}\geq i_{j}
for all
j
. Compatibility of inflation with cup products then shows that
\alpha
lies in the image of
H^{\bullet}(G_{i_{0}})
. Thus the colimit map is surjective.

Moreover, by assumption (ii), the maps

\inf^{n}_{G_{i}}\colon H^{n}(G_{i})\to H^{n}(G)

are injective for
n=1,2
. Since both
H^{\bullet}(G_{i})
and
H^{\bullet}(G)
are graded
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebras generated in degree
1
, it follows from
[
3
, Proposition 3.6.1]
that injectivity in degrees
1
and
2
implies injectivity in all degrees.

Hence, the colimit map is an isomorphism of graded vector spaces. Since inflation maps are compatible algebra homomorphisms, it is an isomorphism of graded
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebras.
∎

We now combine the colimit result with the asymptotic framework.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
G
be a pro-
p
group and let
(N_{i})_{i\in I}
be a cofinal directed system of closed normal subgroups of
G
with
\bigcap_{i}N_{i}=1
, and set
G_{i}=G/N_{i}
. Assume:

(i)

H^{\bullet}(G)
is generated as an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra by
H^{1}(G)
;

(ii)

for each
i
and each
n\in\{1,2\}
, the inflation map

\inf^{n}_{G_{i}}\colon H^{n}(G_{i})\to H^{n}(G)

is injective;

(iii)

for each
i
, the algebra
H^{\bullet}(G_{i})
is universally Koszul.

Then
H^{\bullet}(G)
is asymptotically universally Koszul.

Proof.

By Lemma
6.2
, the inflation maps induce an isomorphism

\varinjlim_{i}H^{\bullet}(G_{i})\;\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\;H^{\bullet}(G).

Each
H^{\bullet}(G_{i})
is a finite-type graded algebra (in the arithmetic situations of interest) and is universally Koszul by assumption (iii). The inflation maps are injective in all degrees, so
H^{\bullet}(G)
is a filtered colimit of finite-type universally Koszul quadratic subalgebras. It follows from Proposition
3.2
that
H^{\bullet}(G)
is asymptotically universally Koszul.
∎

Theorem 6.4

(Finite-type capture and quadratic image)
.

Let
G
be a pro-
p
group such that
H^{\bullet}(G)
is asymptotically universally Koszul via a filtered system
\{A_{K,i}\}_{i\in I}
of finite-type universally Koszul quadratic subalgebras with
\varinjlim_{i}A_{K,i}\cong H^{\bullet}(G)
. Let
\pi\colon G\twoheadrightarrow Q
be a continuous epimorphism, where
Q
is a finitely generated pro-
p
group such that
H^{\bullet}(Q)
is quadratic and generated in degree
1
. Let
\pi^{*}\colon H^{\bullet}(Q)\to H^{\bullet}(G)
denote inflation.

(1)

There exists a finite-type universally Koszul quadratic subalgebra
B\subseteq H^{\bullet}(G)
in the filtered system such that
\pi^{*}(H^{1}(Q))\subseteq B_{1}
.

(2)

If
H^{\bullet}(Q)
is generated in degree
1
, then
\pi^{*}(H^{\bullet}(Q))\subseteq B
.

(3)

Suppose moreover that
\pi^{*}
is injective on
H^{\bullet}(Q)
. Then:

(a)

\pi^{*}(H^{\bullet}(Q))=B_{W}
,
W:=\pi^{*}(H^{1}(Q))
, i.e. the image is the canonical quadratic subalgebra generated by
W
.

(b)

The quadratic relations satisfy
R_{B_{W}}=\pi^{*}(R_{H^{\bullet}(Q)})
.

In particular,
\pi^{*}
induces an isomorphism of quadratic algebras

H^{\bullet}(Q)\;\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\;B_{W}.

Remark 6.5
.

Part (
3
) gives a structural obstruction: if
G\twoheadrightarrow Q
and
\pi^{*}
is injective on
H^{\bullet}(Q)
, then
H^{\bullet}(Q)
embeds as a quadratic subalgebra of
H^{\bullet}(G)
. In particular, if
H^{\bullet}(Q)
fails to satisfy structural properties compatible with asymptotic universal Koszulity, this can obstruct asymptotic universal Koszulity of
H^{\bullet}(G)
. A full obstruction theory would require a descent principle for universal Koszulity along such embeddings, which is not established here.

Proof.

Let
W:=\pi^{*}(H^{1}(Q))\subseteq H^{1}(G)
. Since
Q
is finitely generated,
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}H^{1}(Q)<\infty
, and hence
W
is finite-dimensional.

(1)

Since
H^{\bullet}(G)
is asymptotically universally Koszul, there exists

i\in I
such that

W\subseteq(A_{K,i})_{1}.

Set
B:=A_{K,i}
. Then
B
is a finite-type universally Koszul
quadratic subalgebra of
H^{\bullet}(G)
containing
W
in degree
1
,
as required.

(2)

Assume that
H^{\bullet}(Q)
is generated in degree
1
. Then

H^{\bullet}(Q)=\langle H^{1}(Q)\rangle,

and since
\pi^{*}
is a graded algebra homomorphism,

\pi^{*}(H^{\bullet}(Q))=\langle\pi^{*}(H^{1}(Q))\rangle_{H^{\bullet}(G)}=\langle W\rangle_{H^{\bullet}(G)}=B_{W}.

By construction of
B
, we have
W\subseteq B_{1}
, hence
B_{W}\subseteq B
, and therefore

\pi^{*}(H^{\bullet}(Q))\subseteq B.

(3)

Assume now that
\pi^{*}
is injective on all of
H^{\bullet}(Q)
.

(a)

Since
H^{\bullet}(Q)
is generated in degree
1
, every element of
H^{\bullet}(Q)
is a cup product of elements of
H^{1}(Q)
. Applying
\pi^{*}
yields

\pi^{*}(H^{\bullet}(Q))=\langle\pi^{*}(H^{1}(Q))\rangle_{H^{\bullet}(G)}=B_{W}.

(b)

We compute the quadratic relations of
B_{W}
. By definition,

R_{B_{W}}=R_{H^{\bullet}(G)}\cap(W\otimes W).

Since
H^{\bullet}(Q)
is quadratic,

R_{H^{\bullet}(Q)}=\ker\bigl(H^{1}(Q)^{\otimes 2}\xrightarrow{\cup}H^{2}(Q)\bigr).

Applying
\pi^{*}
to the exact sequence

0\to R_{H^{\bullet}(Q)}\to H^{1}(Q)^{\otimes 2}\xrightarrow{\cup}H^{2}(Q),

and using:

•

injectivity of
\pi^{*}
on
H^{2}(Q)
,

•

compatibility of
\pi^{*}
with cup products,

we obtain an exact sequence

0\to\pi^{*}(R_{H^{\bullet}(Q)})\to W^{\otimes 2}\xrightarrow{\cup}H^{2}(G).

Thus

\pi^{*}(R_{H^{\bullet}(Q)})=\ker\bigl(W^{\otimes 2}\to H^{2}(G)\bigr)=R_{H^{\bullet}(G)}\cap(W\otimes W)=R_{B_{W}}.

From the above, we have that
\pi^{*}(H^{\bullet}(Q))=B_{W}
,
\pi^{*}
is injective by assumption, and the quadratic relations are preserved. Therefore,

\pi^{*}\colon H^{\bullet}(Q)\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}B_{W}

is an isomorphism of quadratic algebras.

∎

Examples

Example 7.1
.

Let
V=\bigcup_{n}V_{n}
be an increasing union of finite-dimensional
k
-vector spaces with
\dim_{k}V_{n}<\infty
, and set

A_{n}=\Lambda(V_{n}).

Each
A_{n}
is a quadratic, Koszul, and universally Koszul exterior algebra. Then

A=\varinjlim_{n}A_{n}

is a graded-commutative algebra generated in degree
1
by the infinite-dimensional space
V
. By Proposition
3.2
, the algebra
A
is asymptotically universally Koszul.

Example 7.2
.

Let
K=\bigcup_{i}K_{i}
be a directed union of fields containing
\mu_{p}
, and suppose that for each
i
the maximal pro-
p
Galois group
G_{K_{i}}^{(p)}
is finitely generated and
H^{\bullet}(G_{K_{i}}^{(p)},\mathbb{F}_{p})
is quadratic and universally Koszul. Assume that the inflation maps are injective in degrees
1
and
2
, and that
H^{\bullet}(G_{K}^{(p)},\mathbb{F}_{p})
is generated in degree
1
(for instance by the Bloch-Kato Theorem). Then, by Lemma
6.2
, the natural map

\varinjlim_{i}H^{\bullet}(G_{K_{i}}^{(p)},\mathbb{F}_{p})\;\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}H^{\bullet}(G_{K}^{(p)},\mathbb{F}_{p})

is an isomorphism of graded algebras. Since each
H^{\bullet}(G_{K_{i}}^{(p)},\mathbb{F}_{p})
is a finite-type universally Koszul quadratic algebra, it follows from Proposition
6.3
that
H^{\bullet}(G_{K}^{(p)},\mathbb{F}_{p})

has asymptotically universally Koszul cohomology.

8.
Conditional local-global descent

We now formulate a conditional local–global mechanism for establishing asymptotic universal Koszulity. The key idea is that if finite-dimensional collections of global degree-one classes can be realized inside finite-type universally Koszul local models in a compatible way, then the global cohomology ring inherits asymptotic universal Koszulity.

Theorem 8.1

(Conditional local-global descent criterion)
.

Let
K\supset\mu_{p}
be a field, and set

A_{K}^{\bullet}:=H^{\bullet}(G_{K}^{(p)},\mathbb{F}_{p}).

Suppose that for every finite-dimensional subspace
W\subset A_{K}^{1}
, there exist:

(i)

a finite set of places or local data
S=S(W)
;

(ii)

a graded algebra homomorphism

\lambda_{S}\colon A_{K}^{\bullet}\longrightarrow A_{S}^{\bullet}:=H^{\bullet}\!\Bigl(\prod_{v\in S}G_{K_{v}}^{(p)},\mathbb{F}_{p}\Bigr);

(iii)

a finite-type universally Koszul quadratic algebra
B_{W}
;

(iv)

an isomorphism

\phi_{W}\colon\langle W\rangle\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }B_{W};

such that:

(a)

\phi_{W}
is induced by
\lambda_{S}
; and

(b)

the family is compatible under inclusion:

W\subset W^{\prime}\;\Rightarrow\;\langle W\rangle\subset\langle W^{\prime}\rangle,\quad B_{W}\subset B_{W^{\prime}}.

Then
A_{K}^{\bullet}
is a filtered union of finite-type universally Koszul quadratic subalgebras, and hence asymptotically universally Koszul.

Proof.

For each
W
, let
C_{W}:=\langle W\rangle
. By assumption,
C_{W}\cong B_{W}
, hence
C_{W}
is finite-type and universally Koszul. Compatibility implies that
(C_{W})
is a filtered system. Since
A_{K}^{\bullet}
is generated in degree
1
, every element lies in some
C_{W}
, and thus
\displaystyle A_{K}^{\bullet}=\bigcup_{W}C_{W}
. Hence
A_{K}^{\bullet}
is a filtered colimit of finite-type universally Koszul quadratic algebras. The result follows from Proposition
3.3
.
∎

Corollary 8.2
.

Let
K\supset\mu_{p}
be a field. Suppose that for every finite-dimensional subspace
W\subseteq H^{1}(G_{K}^{(p)},\mathbb{F}_{p})
there exists a finite set
S
and a map

\lambda_{S}\colon H^{\bullet}(G_{K}^{(p)},\mathbb{F}_{p})\to H^{\bullet}\!\Bigl(\prod_{v\in S}G_{K_{v}}^{(p)},\mathbb{F}_{p}\Bigr),

such that:

(i)

\lambda_{S}
is injective on
\langle W\rangle
;

(ii)

\lambda_{S}(\langle W\rangle)
is finite-type universally Koszul; and

(iii)

compatibility holds under inclusion of subspaces.

Then
H^{\bullet}(G_{K}^{(p)},\mathbb{F}_{p})
is asymptotically universally Koszul.

We now outline how the preceding criterion applies in patching situations. Let
F
be a complete discretely valued field containing
\mu_{p}
, let
X
be a smooth projective curve over
F
, and set
K=F(X)
. Suppose a patching datum
\Gamma
is given with local fields
K_{v}
. Assume there is a natural map

\lambda_{\Gamma}\colon H^{\bullet}(G_{K}^{(p)},\mathbb{F}_{p})\to\prod_{v\in\Gamma}H^{\bullet}(G_{K_{v}}^{(p)},\mathbb{F}_{p}),

whose restriction to any finite-dimensional
\langle W\rangle
factors through a finite subproduct.

Theorem 8.3

(Conditional patching criterion)
.

Assume:

(i)

finite-dimensional subspaces embed via
\lambda_{S}
;

(ii)

their images are finite-type universally Koszul;

(iii)

compatibility under inclusion holds.

Then
H^{\bullet}(G_{K}^{(p)},\mathbb{F}_{p})
is asymptotically universally Koszul.

This theorem should be viewed as a template: it isolates precisely the local-to-global conditions required to deduce asymptotic universal Koszulity via patching.
