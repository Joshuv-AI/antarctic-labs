Mid-Year Roundup: So You are Saying There’s a Chance…
Authors: Brett R. Levine
Published: 2026

With the first half of 2026 behind us, we continue to set records for Arthroplasty Today (AT) in the number of submissions received and reviews conducted. I want to thank everyone who read the last editorial and took it to heart, as we saw a spike in new reviewers last quarter. This was a welcome surprise, and I hope we can maintain that momentum as we welcomed our first group of chief residents into the mentor program (special thanks to Josh Rainey for leading this effort). As we move into the second half of the year, we will hold virtual meetings of the AT editorial board and associate editors. Our goals are to update reviewer and author requirements for articles and to finalize a plan for scored continuing medical education credits through AT. With the 2026 AAHKS Annual Meeting approaching quickly (consider this a shameless plug to attend the meeting), I look forward to sharing detailed updates with the board and all our members.

A few other journal items to look out for in the near future include a review article from the Adult Reconstruction Team at Loyola on the Ergonomics of Arthroplasty. This will be featured in the next edition of the journal and promises to be an eye-opening discussion on how, as surgeons, we need to do a better job taking care of ourselves and maintaining a healthy lifestyle. Additionally, the Advocacy Committee will have some viewpoints on the current state of healthcare reform and future programs that are looming. Let this serve as a teaser for future volumes, and if you, as a reader, have any additional ideas or concepts that you would like to share, please reach out to me directly.

So, now you may be wondering about the title of the editorial. At this point, I am going to take off my orthopaedic hat and make a strong push for my favorite NBA team, the New York Knicks. By the time this editorial is published, I will either be ecstatic having witnessed the first NBA title coming home to NY in my lifetime, or I will be devastated and have to steal a quote from Cubs fans— “There’s always next year.” After what was a lackluster NBA regular season, the playoffs have me invigorated and I am inspired by a gutsy Knicks team that has played the underdog role well. So if you are not going to review for the journal, at least rally behind the Editor-in-chief and root for the Knickerbockers!

Before I completely lose sight of the task at hand, I want to highlight some of the outstanding offerings that we have in Volume 39 of AT. There are four highlighted original research articles, including:•Optimizing Daily Surgical Scheduling Improves Operative Time Consumption: A Retrospective Study—this one may stimulate you to reassess your scheduling processes to see if you can better utilize operative time.•The Economic Impact and Clinical Benefits of Intraosseous Injections in Total Knee Arthroplasty—intraosseous injections have some efficacy for infection prophylaxis; read here for other clinical benefits and an overall economic assessment of the technique.•Incidence and Reasons for Direct Transfers Among Hip and Knee Arthroplasties Performed at Ambulatory Surgical Centers—see why patients have to transfer to the hospital from an ASC. Although this is a rare occurrence, it is important to develop appropriate protocols for when necessary.•Evaluating the Utility of Artificial Intelligence in Conducting Systematic Reviews—see how AI stacks up to orthopaedic surgeons in conducting systematic reviews.

The Did You Know installment for Volume 39 involves the facts regarding the need for physical therapy after a primary total joint arthroplasty. For those not capitalizing on the Principal Care Management codes in clinic, you need to read the office tip by Malholtra et al to gain tips to implement these revenue-generating codes. Additionally, there is a systematic review that covers the very common postoperative problem of sleep disturbance after a total hip or total knee arthroplasty. There are a total of 65 articles slated for this volume covering topics involving Artificial intelligence, robotics, revision surgery, and other popular arthroplasty subjects.

I hope you enjoy this well-rounded volume of AT. I apologize for the NBA digression, but the excitement is real. As we look to the months leading up to the annual meeting, I wish all those who submitted abstracts the best of luck. We once again reached a new record for submissions, and this annual meeting, led by Antonia Chen and program chair Linda Sulieman, is shaping up to be outstanding. With registration now open, please sign up for the meeting, and let us set a record for attendance. I urge you all to participate in AAHKS and AT, and if you have any questions on getting involved, you can reach me directly at brettlevinemd@gmail.com. I am always open to suggestions and encourage you to help us all at AT in providing the highest-quality products for our members and readers. In between NBA finals games take a look at the new journal volume, register for the annual meeting, and please root for the Knicks. While it may be slim, there is a chance that this EIC may finally see a ticker-tape parade for the Knicks in 2026.
