Conformal anomaly in a vector field model with auxiliary scalar field


Abstract
The conformal anomaly has well-known ambiguities related to the possible schemes of regularization and renormalization. In case of dimensional regularization, one of the options is to formulate the theory as conformal in the dimension D ≠ 4 D\neq 4 . For a gauge vector field this can be done in several ways and one of the options is to introduce an auxiliary scalar playing the role of a compensator. The advantage of this approach is that it preserves gauge symmetry and avoids problems with possible violation of unitarity. We explore the consequences of introducing such an auxiliary field for the anomaly and anomaly-induced action. It is shown that the new scalar degree of freedom gains an independent dynamics after taking the 4 ​ D 4D limit. The remnant scalar, also, demonstrates some interesting properties. Keywords: Conformal anomaly, gauge vector field, dimensional regularization, auxiliary scalar field MSC. 81T20, 81T15, 83C47

Introduction

The local conformal symmetry and its quantum violation in the form
of trace anomaly is an object of extensive studies from the epoch of
initial works
[
1
]
and
[
2
,
3
]
. In the
subsequent decades, this area got a lot of importance, in particular
because of the relevant applications in string theory, cosmology and
black hole physics (see, e.g.,
[
4
]
and
[
5
]
).
On the other hand, there are some questions which are difficult to
answer and there is still a possibility to find some new surprising
aspects of the problem. In particular, this concerns the ambiguity related to regularization (see, e.g., the discussion in

[
6
,
4
]
) which concerns a possibility to extend the
theory which is conformal in
4D
to the dimension
D\neq 4
.
It was shown in
[
7
]
that the possibility to make
this extension, in the vacuum (i.e., metric-dependent) sector of the
theory, in different ways produces an ambiguity in the local term
in the anomaly-induced action. The purpose of the present work is
to indicate one more ambiguity related to dimensional regularization,
this time concerning the nonlocal action.

There are two ways to apply dimensional regularization. One can
either use it only in the loop integrals, or try to formulate the
theory in the dimension
D\neq 4
from the very beginning. The last
approach may be more problematic, especially in the case of a gauge
vector field in curved spacetime. The point is that, in
D\neq 4
,
a direct extension of the action does not possess local conformal
invariance. The importance of this problem was noted a long ago,
but the solution which was found in
[
8
]
for
the Abelian gauge field is not very well suited for the quantum
applications because the theory loses gauge invariance. It is
well-known that the simultaneous existence of transverse and
longitudinal modes of a vector field may produce ghost degrees
of freedom in a theory with mass and, probably, in the case of
external gravitational field. More recently, there were found other
ways of extending the vector theory to
D\neq 4
, which guarantee
local conformal symmetry and, at the same time, preserve the gauge
invariance
[
9
]
. It was shown that two out of three
models are closely related. One of these models is very simple and
the conformal symmetry is maintained by introducing a special
auxiliary scalar, called to compensate the effect of changing the
dimension.

In the present work, we explore the conformal anomaly in the
mentioned model with an auxiliary scalar field. One may expect
that, after subtracting divergences and taking the
4D
limit, this
field should completely disappear. We will show that this does
not happen, such that the remnant of this field remains propagating
in the theory with quantum corrections to the vacuum effective
action.

The paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
2
we write down
the action of
[
9
]
in an appropriate way and prepare the
technical background for deriving the one-loop divergences in
Sec.
3
. Sec.
4
reports on the calculation of
anomaly and anomaly-induced effective action. Finally, in
Sec.
5
, we draw our conclusions.

The conformal vector field model and its bilinear form

In
[
9
]
four alternative approaches are formulated to
construct a conformal Abelian vector field action. One of the models
is equivalent to the renowned construction of
[
8
]
,
which violates the gauge symmetry. In other versions, this symmetry
is preserved. In particular, the non-analytic model

\displaystyle S^{\star}_{d}(A,g)\,=\,-\,\,\frac{1}{4}\!\int\!d^{D}x\sqrt{-g}\,\big(F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}\big)^{D/4},

(1)

is similar to what was proposed earlier in
[
7
]
for
the conformal action constructed with the Weyl tensor.
Another model, which is closely related to (
1
),
has the action

\displaystyle S\,=\,-\,\dfrac{1}{4}\int d^{D}x\sqrt{-g}\,\psi\,F^{2}_{\mu\nu}\,,

(2)

where
\psi
is an auxiliary scalar field, which can also be regarded
as a coordinate-dependent coupling constant
[
10
]
. It
is worth noting that an equivalent model was formulated much earlier
in
[
11
]
.

In both cases, the electromagnetic field strength tensor is defined
as
F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}
, with

F^{2}_{\mu\nu}=F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}
.
In addition to the usual gauge invariance, the action (
2
)
is invariant under the local conformal transformation,

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}=\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}e^{2\sigma}\,,\quad A_{\mu}=\bar{A}_{\mu}\,,\quad\psi=\bar{\psi}\,e^{(4-D)\sigma}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\sigma=\sigma(x)\,.

(3)

The transformation rule for
\psi
compensates the dimension

D\neq 4
and the corresponding transformation for
\sqrt{-g}
.
In four dimensions, the auxiliary scalar field does not transform,
leaving the
4D
action in its conventional form. The conformal
invariance of the action (
2
) make it possible to derive the
contributions of the gauge vector field to the anomaly not only in

4D
, but also in other dimensions. In particular, two of the
present authors recently reported on the calculation in
2D
in
Ref.
[
12
]
, with some unexpected output, e.g., full
set of the possible terms in the
2D
anomaly, according to the
classification of
[
2
,
13
]
, which could be
observed only in four dimensions and beyond, before this
work. Here we explore the same problem in
4D
and, as can
be seen below, also report on unexpected results.

Using the action (
2
) to derive the anomaly for a quantum
field
A_{\mu}
requires, at the first place, calculating the one-loop
divergences around
D=4
. In what follows, we solve this problem
using the heat-kernel method
[
14
,
15
]
, which is a standard
instrument for quantum field theory in curved space-time.
The Schwinger-DeWitt technique requires the bilinear form of
the action with respect to quantum fields.
Since the action (
2
) is bilinear in
A_{\mu}
, while
\psi

is treated as a classical background field, one can skip the use of
the background field method and directly consider quantum
A_{\mu}
.

To provide an efficient removing of the degeneracy, the
Faddeev–Popov procedure requires introducing the
gauge-fixing action depending on the auxiliary field
\psi
,

\displaystyle S_{\textrm{gf}}\,=\,-\,\dfrac{1}{2}\int d^{D}x\sqrt{-g}\,\psi\big(\nabla_{\mu}A^{\mu}\big)^{2}\,.

(4)

After some algebra (see
[
12
]
for most of the details),
the bilinear part of the action (
2
), together with the
gauge-fixing term (
4
), takes the form

\displaystyle\left(S+S_{\textrm{gf}}\right)^{(2)}\,=\,\dfrac{1}{2}\int d^{D}x\sqrt{-g}A^{\mu}H_{\mu}{}^{\nu}A_{\nu}\,,

(5)

where the Hessian operator is

\displaystyle\hat{H}=H_{\mu}{}^{\nu}=\delta^{\nu}_{\mu}\cx-R_{\mu}{}^{\nu}+\psi^{-1}\Big[\delta^{\nu}_{\mu}(\nabla^{\lambda}\psi)\nabla_{\lambda}-(\nabla^{\nu}\psi)\nabla_{\mu}+(\nabla_{\mu}\psi)\nabla^{\nu}\Big]\,.

(6)

By comparing this expression with the canonical form of the differential
operator employed in the heat-kernel technique (see, e.g.,
[
16
]

for an introduction),

\displaystyle\hat{H}\,=\,\hat{1}\cx+2\hat{h}^{\lambda}\nabla_{\lambda}+\hat{\Pi}\,,

(7)

we can identify the corresponding components as

\displaystyle\hat{\Pi}=-R_{\mu}{}^{\nu}\,\,\quad\textrm{and}\qquad\hat{h}^{\lambda}=\dfrac{1}{2}\psi^{-1}\left[\delta^{\nu}_{\mu}\nabla^{\lambda}\psi-\delta^{\lambda}_{\mu}\nabla^{\nu}\psi+g^{\nu\lambda}\nabla_{\mu}\psi\right]\,.

(8)

At this point, it becomes clear that a more useful parametrization of
the auxiliary scalar is
\psi=\exp\varphi
, which will be used below.
The transformation rule for the new scalar
\varphi
can be easily
obtained from (
3
).

One-loop divergences in four dimensions

The one-loop divergences of the operator (

be extracted from the expression

\displaystyle\bar{\Gamma}^{(1)}\,=\,\dfrac{i}{2}\,\mbox{Tr}\,\,\mbox{ln}\,\hat{H}\,-\,i\,\mbox{Tr}\,\,\mbox{ln}\,\hat{H}_{\rm ghost}\,.

(9)

Let us note that the ghost operator

Using the dimensional regularization, the relevant one-loop

\displaystyle\bar{\Gamma}^{(1)}_{\textrm{div}}\,=\,-\,\dfrac{\mu^{D-4}}{\epsilon}\int d^{D}x\sqrt{-g}\,\mbox{tr}\,a_{2}\,,

(11)

\displaystyle\hat{a}_{2}\,=\,\dfrac{\hat{1}}{180}\left(R^{2}_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}-R^{2}_{\mu\nu}+\cx R\right)+\dfrac{1}{2}\hat{P}^{2}+\dfrac{1}{6}\big(\cx\hat{P}\big)+\dfrac{1}{12}\hat{S}^{2}_{\mu\nu}\,.

(12)

Here

is the
parameter of dimensional regularization and

is the
renormalization parameter. Direct calculations give

\displaystyle\hat{P}\,=\,\left[P\right]_{\mu}{}^{\nu}\,=\,\hat{\Pi}+\dfrac{\hat{1}}{6}R-\nabla_{\alpha}\hat{h}^{\alpha}-\hat{h}_{\alpha}\hat{h}^{\alpha}\,

\displaystyle\qquad=\,-\,R_{\mu}{}^{\nu}+\dfrac{1}{6}\delta^{\nu}_{\mu}R-\dfrac{1}{2}\delta^{\nu}_{\mu}\cx\varphi+\dfrac{\left(D-2\right)}{4}\left(\nabla_{\mu}\varphi\right)\nabla^{\nu}\varphi\,,

(13)

\displaystyle\hat{S}_{\alpha\beta}\,=\,\left[S_{\alpha\beta}\right]_{\mu}{}^{\nu}\,=\,\mathcal{\hat{R}}_{\alpha\beta}+\nabla_{\beta}\hat{h}_{\alpha}-\nabla_{\alpha}\hat{h}_{\beta}+\hat{h}_{\beta}\hat{h}_{\alpha}-\hat{h}_{\alpha}\hat{h}_{\beta}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad=\,\,-\,R_{\alpha\beta\mu\,\cdot}^{\hskip 8.19447pt\,\,\,\nu}-\dfrac{1}{4}\,\delta^{\nu}_{[\beta}\,\left(\nabla_{\alpha]}\varphi\right)\,\nabla_{\mu}\varphi-\dfrac{1}{2}\,\delta^{\nu}_{[\beta}\,\nabla_{\alpha]}\nabla_{\mu}\varphi+\dfrac{1}{4}\,g_{\mu[\beta}\left(\nabla_{\alpha]}\varphi\right)\nabla^{\nu}\varphi

Trace anomaly and anomaly-induced action

The derivation of the trace anomaly may be performed by the method
suggested by Duff
[
3
]
, with certain simplifications

[
5
]
. This method works in the case of gauge theory and
dimensional regularization because:
i)
the one-loop
divergences are given by the sum of
C
-type and
N
-type conformal
terms
[
19
]
and because;
ii)
the auxiliary scalar field

\varphi=\,\mbox{ln}\,\psi
transforms, according to the rule (
3
), as

\varphi=\bar{\varphi}+\left(4-D\right)\sigma
, such that the conformal Noether
identity does not depend on
\varphi
after taking the
4D
limit. These two
points guarantee that the presence of
\varphi
does not modify the general
scheme for anomaly.

The renormalized one-loop effective action has the form

\displaystyle\Gamma_{R}\,=\,S_{\textrm{class}}\,+\,\bar{\Gamma}{}^{(1)}\,+\,\Delta S\,,

(18)

where
S_{\textrm{class}}
is the classical action,
\bar{\Gamma}{}^{(1)}=\bar{\Gamma}{}^{{}^{(1)}}_{\textrm{div}}+\bar{\Gamma}{}^{{}^{(1)}}_{\textrm{fin}}

is the non-renormalized one-loop quantum correction to the classical
action, and
\Delta S
is the infinite local counterterm introduced
to cancel the UV divergences. Owing to the conformal integrand of
the divergences
[
19
]
, only
\Delta S
may produce the violation
of conformal invariance of
\Gamma_{R}
after removing the dimensional
regularization,
[
3
,
4
]
(see also

[
5
,
16
]
for details), i.e.,

\displaystyle\langle T\rangle\,=\,-\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{-\bar{g}}}\,e^{-4\sigma}\,\frac{\delta\Delta S}{\delta\sigma}\bigg|_{\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}\to g_{\mu\nu},\,\,\bar{\varphi}\to\varphi,\,\,\sigma\to 0}\,.

(19)

Taking into account the conformal transformation rules
(
3
) as well as those for
C_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}
,

E_{4}
and
\cx R
, after taking the
4D
limit, one
arrives at the trace anomaly,

\displaystyle\left\langle T\right\rangle\,=\,-\,\Big\{wC^{2}_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}+bE_{4}+c\cx R+\beta_{1}\varphi\Delta_{4}\varphi+\beta_{2}\left(\nabla\varphi\right)^{4}+\nabla_{\mu}\chi^{\mu}\Big\}\,,

(20)

where

\displaystyle\chi^{\mu}\,=\,\gamma_{1}R^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\nu}\varphi+\gamma_{2}R\varphi^{\mu}+\gamma_{3}\varphi^{\mu}\cx\varphi+\gamma_{4}\varphi\nabla^{\mu}\cx\varphi

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\,\gamma_{5}\varphi R^{\mu\nu}\varphi_{\nu}+\gamma_{6}\varphi R\varphi^{\mu}+\gamma_{7}\left(\nabla\varphi\right)^{2}\varphi^{\mu}+\gamma_{8}\nabla^{\mu}\cx\varphi\,.

(21)

In the last expression
\varphi_{\mu}=\nabla_{\mu}\varphi
,

\varphi^{\mu}=\bar{g}^{\mu\nu}\varphi_{\nu}

\varphi_{\mu\nu}=\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\varphi

\displaystyle w=-\,c=\dfrac{1}{10(4\pi)^{2}}\,,\quad b=-\dfrac{31}{180(4\pi)^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle\beta_{1}=\dfrac{1}{4(4\pi)^{2}}\,,\quad\beta_{2}=\dfrac{1}{16(4\pi)^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}=-\,2\gamma_{2}=-\,2\gamma_{6}=\gamma_{8}=-\dfrac{1}{3(4\pi)^{2}}\,,\quad\gamma_{3}=\dfrac{5}{12(4\pi)^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle\gamma_{4}=\,\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{5}=-\dfrac{1}{4(4\pi)^{2}}\,,\quad\gamma_{7}=\dfrac{5}{12(4\pi)^{2}}\,.

\displaystyle\Gamma_{ind,\,7}\,=\,\frac{1}{2}\int_{x}\mathcal{L}_{71}\qquad\mbox{satisfies}\qquad-\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\,\frac{\delta\Gamma_{ind,\,7}}{\delta\sigma}\,=\,\nabla_{\mu}\big[\varphi^{\mu}(\nabla\varphi)^{2}\big],

(31)

and this resolves the part related to
\gamma_{7}
.

Considering now the integration of the terms that are linear in the
auxiliary scalar
\varphi
, they have coefficients
\gamma_{1}
,

\gamma_{2}
, and
\gamma_{8}
. The candidate terms to be part of the
corresponding induced action needs to have four derivatives,
not to be surface integrals and are supposed to be local. The
possible Lagrangians satisfying these conditions are

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{11}=R\cx\varphi\,,\qquad\mathcal{L}_{12}=R^{\mu\nu}\varphi_{\mu\nu}\,,\qquad\mathcal{L}_{13}=R^{2}\varphi\,,\qquad\mathcal{L}_{14}=R^{2}_{\mu\nu}\varphi.

(32)

Note that
\mathcal{L}_{12}
can be reduced to

\mathcal{L}_{11}
through integrating by parts and using the
third Bianchi identity, hence we do not need to consider this term.
Also, the linear combination of
\mathcal{L}_{13}
,

\mathcal{L}_{14}
and
R^{2}_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\varphi
form the
conformal invariant term. Therefore, the variation of the last
structure is not independent and do not need to be considered.
So, we end up with the three candidate terms only.

The corresponding conformal variations, in the linearized
form, are as follows:

\displaystyle\delta\,\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}_{11}\,=\,2\sqrt{-g}\,\big\{R\varphi^{\mu}\sigma_{\mu}-3(\cx\varphi)(\cx\sigma)\big\}\,,

\displaystyle\delta\,\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}_{13}\,=\,-\,12\sqrt{-g}\,R\varphi\cx\sigma\,,

\displaystyle\delta\,\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}_{14}\,=\,-\,2\sqrt{-g}\,\big\{2\varphi R^{\mu\nu}\sigma_{\mu\nu}+\varphi R\cx\sigma\big\}.

(33)

Consider a local term, which is a linear combination with
unknown coefficients
\alpha_{1k}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{(1)}\,=\,\alpha_{11}\mathcal{L}_{11}+\alpha_{13}\mathcal{L}_{13}+\alpha_{14}\mathcal{L}_{14}\,.

(34)

Using (
33
) and (
34
), after some partial
integrations, we arrive at the equation

\displaystyle-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\,\dfrac{\delta}{\delta\sigma}\int_{x}\mathcal{L}_{(1)}\,=\,\nabla_{\mu}\chi^{\mu}_{\,(1)},

(35)

where

\displaystyle\chi^{\mu}_{\,(1)}\,=\,\big(2\alpha_{11}+12\alpha_{13}+2\alpha_{14}\big)R\varphi^{\mu}+6\alpha_{11}\nabla^{\mu}\cx\varphi

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\,\,4\big(3\alpha_{13}+\alpha_{14}\big)\varphi\nabla^{\mu}R+4\alpha_{14}R^{\mu\nu}\varphi_{\nu}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad=\,-\,\gamma_{1}R^{\mu\nu}\varphi_{\nu}-\gamma_{2}R\varphi^{\mu}-\gamma_{8}R\nabla^{\mu}\cx\varphi\,.

(36)

Satisfying this condition is nontrivial because the number of free
parameters is smaller than the number of equations. One can easily
check that the system is compatible only if

6\gamma_{2}+3\gamma_{1}=2\gamma_{8}
.
This condition is not satisfied for the coefficients (
22
).
This means, there are no local terms producing the given type of
anomaly terms, in this case.

Finally, consider the most complicated terms which are quadratic
in the scalar
\varphi
. In this case, the list of irreducible terms is

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{21}=R(\nabla\varphi)^{2}\,,\qquad\mathcal{L}_{22}=R^{\mu\nu}\varphi_{\mu}\varphi_{\nu}\,,\qquad\mathcal{L}_{23}=R^{2}\varphi^{2}\,,\qquad\mathcal{L}_{24}=R^{2}_{\mu\nu}\varphi^{2}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{25}=(\cx\varphi)^{2}\,,\qquad\mathcal{L}_{26}=R\varphi\cx\varphi\,,\qquad\mathcal{L}_{27}=\varphi R^{\mu\nu}\varphi_{\mu\nu}\,.

(37)

Other possible terms, such as

\varphi\varphi_{\mu}\nabla^{\mu}R
and
R^{2}_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\varphi^{2}
are
reducible by means of partial integration or owing to the conformal
symmetry. The transformation rules for the candidate terms are as
follows:

\displaystyle\delta\,\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}_{21}\,=\,-\,6\sqrt{-g}\,(\nabla\varphi)^{2}\cx\sigma\,,

\displaystyle\delta\,\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}_{22}\,=\,-\,\sqrt{-g}\,\big\{2\varphi^{\mu}\varphi^{\nu}\sigma_{\mu\nu}\,+\,(\nabla\varphi)^{2}\cx\sigma\big\}\,,

\displaystyle\delta\,\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}_{23}\,=\,-\,12\sqrt{-g}\,\varphi^{2}R\cx\sigma\,,

\displaystyle\delta\,\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}_{24}\,=\,-\,2\sqrt{-g}\,\big\{2\varphi^{2}R^{\mu\nu}\sigma_{\mu\nu}\,+\,\varphi^{2}R\cx\sigma\big\}\,,

\displaystyle\delta\,\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}_{25}\,=\,4\sqrt{-g}\,(\cx\varphi)\varphi^{\mu}\sigma_{\mu}\,,

\displaystyle\delta\,\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}_{26}\,=\,2\sqrt{-g}\,\big\{R\varphi\varphi^{\mu}\sigma_{\mu}-3\varphi(\cx\varphi)(\cx\sigma)\big\}\,,

\displaystyle\delta\,\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}_{27}\,=\,\sqrt{-g}\,\varphi\big\{R\varphi^{\mu}\sigma_{\mu}-2R^{\mu\nu}\varphi_{\mu}\sigma_{\nu}-(\cx\varphi)(\cx\sigma)-2\varphi^{\mu\nu}\sigma_{\mu\nu}\big\}\,.

(38)

As in the previous case, we construct a linear combination

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{(2)}\,=\,\sum_{k=1}^{7}\alpha_{2k}\mathcal{L}_{2k}\,.

(39)

Taking the variational derivative

\displaystyle-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\,\frac{\delta}{\delta\sigma}\int_{x}\mathcal{L}_{(2)}\,=\,\nabla_{\mu}\chi^{\mu}_{\,(2)},

(40)

we arrive at the equation

\displaystyle-\,\chi^{\mu}_{\,(2)}\,=\,2\big(6\alpha_{21}+2\alpha_{22}+\alpha_{27}\big)\varphi^{\mu\nu}\varphi_{\nu}+\big(2\alpha_{22}+4\alpha_{25}+6\alpha_{26}+\alpha_{27}\big)\varphi^{\mu}\cx\varphi

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\,\big(24\alpha_{23}+4\alpha_{24}+2\alpha_{26}+\alpha_{27}\big)\varphi\varphi^{\mu}R\,+\,4\big(3\alpha_{23}+\alpha_{24}\big)\varphi^{2}\nabla^{\mu}R

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\,8\alpha_{24}R^{\mu\nu}\varphi_{\nu}\varphi\,+\,3\big(2\alpha_{26}+\alpha_{27}\big)\varphi\nabla^{\mu}\cx\varphi

\displaystyle\qquad=\,-\,\gamma_{3}\varphi^{\mu}\cx\varphi-\gamma_{4}\varphi\nabla^{\mu}\cx\varphi-\gamma_{5}\varphi R^{\mu\nu}\varphi_{\nu}-\gamma_{6}\varphi R\varphi^{\mu}\,.

(41)

It turns out that the system of equations for
\alpha_{2k}
is
degenerate. The existence of a solution depends on the
identity
6\gamma_{6}+3\gamma_{5}=2\gamma_{4}
, which is satisfied for
the coefficients (
22
). On the other hand, there
is an ambiguity since there are only two equations for

\alpha_{21}
,
\alpha_{22}
,
\alpha_{25}
, and only one equation
for
\alpha_{26}
,
\alpha_{27}
. This means, the local solution
for
\mathcal{L}_{(2)}
exists, but the coefficients are
ambiguous.

Conclusions

We have analysed, for the first time, the one-loop divergences
and trace anomaly in the Abelian gauge theory formulated as
conformal out of
4D
dimensions. It is known for a long time
that the vector theory demonstrates certain differences compared
to the scalar and fermion models. At the first place, using the
dimensional regularization in a direct way one violates the
conformal symmetry because for the vector model
4D
is a
very special dimension. According to the existing theorem
[
19
]
,
the manner of how dimensional regularization is used cannot lead
to the nonconformal one-loop divergences. However, the anomaly
may be affected, depending on how the theory is formulated in

D\neq 4
. We have found that, using one of the equivalent ways
of symmetries-preserving extensions
[
9
]
, there are
significant differences with the traditional approaches. The
preservation of both gauge and conformal symmetries requires
either giving up the locality of the theory or introducing an
auxiliary scalar field. We have found that, contrary to the
original expectations, this auxiliary scalar “survives” the limit

D\to 4
in the finite part of the effective action of vacuum. As
a result, the anomaly-induced effective action in this theory has
not two, but three auxiliary scalars. All three scalars are related
to the Paneitz operator and the new one
\varphi
has the most
complicated interaction term, as can be seen in (
25
).

The new scalar has one extra surprising feature. For a long time,
the community working on anomalies had an interest to find a
proof of that any total derivative term in the anomaly can be
generated by local Lagrangians in the anomaly-induced effective
action. Usually the discussions of this issue were concentrated
on
6D
(see, e.g.,
[
26
]
,
[
27
]
, and

[
28
]
).
2
2

2

In
4D
the existence of such
local actions was conformed for all types of the extra background
fields, such as usual scalar or torsion
[
23
,
29
]
.

The situation described in the previous section shows that this
proof probably does not exist since we have an explicit
counterexample of the opposite.

In conclusion, the anomaly in the vector case and dimensional
regularization produces a new surprising ambiguity related to the
way of how the theory is extended to
D\neq 4
. On top of
that, in this case, there is a surprising violation of the common
belief that the total derivative terms in anomaly should be always
produced by local vacuum action.
