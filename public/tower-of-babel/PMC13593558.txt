Atrial anatomy with respect to atrial pacing
Authors: Robert H. Anderson, Richard Sutton, Damian Sanchez Quintana
Published: 2026

Interest is growing at a significant rate with regard to the pathways involved in conduction from the sinus node to the left atrium. The major pathway for such conduction, of course, is the bundle described by Bachmann at the beginning of the twentieth century.1 In a recent report submitted to the journal, Brigido et al2 claim to have discussed the “relevant anatomy” for sites of pacing to activate the left atrium other than directly involving Bachmann's bundle. Therefore, to illustrate their anatomical concepts, they use the images prepared by McAlpine and initially published in his excellent Atlas.2 The authors, however, make a fundamental mistake when suggesting that they have implanted their pacing lead in the atrial septum. Their own images show conclusively that their lead is implanted in the anterior wall of the right atrium, and probably in the atrial vestibule (see their Figure 3B). As is shown by the diagram prepared by McAlpine,3 the impulse delivered at their chosen site would traverse the wall of the antero-medial recess of the right atrial appendage, also initially described by McAlpine,3 and then the “interatrial bundle,” before activating the walls of the left atrium. Neither of these anatomical entities is part of the atrial septum, as can be established by examination of the middle panel of Figure 2 provided by the authors. It is right and proper that investigators continue to explore the optimal means of pacing the left atrium. If such endeavors are to be successful; however, it is essential that the underlying anatomy be properly described and appreciated.4
