Rigidity of the Julia set for Hénon–Sibony maps Thanks: The author’s research is partially supported by the ANR project DynAtrois (ANR-24-CE40-1163).
Gabriel Vigny

Abstract
. Let f f and g g be two Hénon–Sibony maps of ℂ k {\mathbb{C}}^{k} . We show that if they have the same forward Julia set, then they share a common iterate, thereby extending Lamy’s results from dimension 2.



Keywords. Hénon–Sibony maps, Julia sets, Green currents

Mathematics Subject Classification (2020): 37A25, 37A50, 37F80

1.
Introduction

The classification of polynomial automorphisms of
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
of Friedland and Milnor
[
FM89
]
establishes complex generalized Hénon maps as the paradigmatic examples of polynomial automorphisms with positive entropy. In higher dimensions, however, the structure of the group of polynomial automorphisms of
{\mathbb{C}}^{k}
is significantly more complex, and no such central family exists. Sibony
[
Sib99
]
proposed regular polynomial automorphisms as a natural generalization of Hénon maps. He demonstrated that their rich dynamics can be developed (e.g.,
[
Sib99
,
DS09
]
) in a manner analogous to that of Hénon maps, which have been extensively studied over the last thirty years—most notably by Bedford, Lyubich, and Smillie, as well as Fornæss and Sibony (e.g.,
[
BS91
,
BLS93
,
FS92
]
). We shall refer to these as
Hénon–Sibony maps
. Such a map
f
(resp.
f^{-1}
) is a polynomial automorphism of
{\mathbb{C}}^{k}
of degree
d>1
(resp.
\delta>1
) whose extension to
\mathbb{P}^{k}
possesses an indeterminacy set
I_{f}^{+}
(resp.
I_{f}^{-}
) such that
I_{f}^{+}\cap I_{f}^{-}=\varnothing
. This condition implies the existence of an integer
1\leq s\leq k-1
such that
\dim(I_{f}^{+})=k-s-1
,
\dim(I_{f}^{-})=s-1
, and
d^{s}=\delta^{k-s}
.

For such a Hénon–Sibony map
f
, in analogy with the dynamics of polynomials in
{\mathbb{C}}
, one can define the
forward Julia set

K_{f}^{+}
and the
backward Julia set

K_{f}^{-}
by

K_{f}^{+}:=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}^{k}\;;\;\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\|f^{n}(z)\|<\infty\}\quad\text{and}\quad K_{f}^{-}:=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}^{k}\;;\;\limsup_{n\to-\infty}\|f^{n}(z)\|<\infty\},

which are, respectively, the sets of points with bounded forward and backward orbits.
One can then construct the
Green functions

G^{+}_{f}:{\mathbb{C}}^{k}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
and
G^{-}_{f}:{\mathbb{C}}^{k}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
as

G^{+}_{f}(z):=\lim_{n\to\infty}d^{-n}\log^{+}\|f^{n}(z)\|\quad\text{and}\quad G^{-}_{f}(z):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\delta^{-n}\log^{+}\|f^{-n}(z)\|,

where
\log^{+}(x)=\max(\log x,0)
. The Green functions satisfy
K_{f}^{\pm}=\{G_{f}^{\pm}=0\}
and make it possible to apply pluripotential theory to the study of the dynamics of
f
.
Indeed,
G^{+}_{f}
is a plurisubharmonic (psh) function and it satisfies
G^{+}_{f}\circ f=d\,G^{+}_{f}
. Consequently,
T_{f}^{+}:=\mathrm{dd}^{c}G^{+}_{f}
is a well-defined positive closed
(1,1)
-current on
{\mathbb{C}}^{k}
of mass
1
(the mass being computed with respect to the Fubini–Study form on
\mathbb{P}^{k}
). For any
q\leq s
, one can then define the
Green current

T^{+}_{f,q}:=(T_{f}^{+})^{q}
. Similarly, for any
p\leq k-s
, one defines
T^{-}_{f,p}:=(T_{f}^{-})^{p}
.
These currents can then be used to define the
Green measure

\mu_{f}
of
f
by

\mu_{f}:=T^{+}_{f,s}\wedge T^{-}_{f,k-s}.

The measure
\mu_{f}
is an invariant and mixing probability measure, and it is the unique measure of maximal entropy
s\log d
(
[
BS92
,
BLS93
]
in dimension
2
, and
[
Sib99
,
DS09
,
dT10
]
in higher dimensions).

In dimension
1
, Beardon
[
Bea90
,
Bea92
]
proved that polynomials sharing the same Julia set must share an iterate (i.e.,
f^{n}=g^{m}
for some
n,m\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
), up to an affine symmetry of the Julia set. In our setting, two Hénon–Sibony maps that share an iterate automatically have the same Julia sets, the same Green functions, and the same Green measure. This naturally raises the question of whether the converse holds, namely to what extent these dynamically defined objects rigidly determine the map.

In dimension
2
, this question was addressed by Lamy in
[
Lam01
]
, where he shows that if two Hénon maps share the same forward Julia set, then they must share an iterate. As a consequence, Dujardin and Favre
[
DF17
]
proved that the same conclusion already follows from the equality of the Green measure. Since then, this rigidity phenomenon has been revisited and strengthened in several directions, notably by Bera, Pal, and Verma
[
BPV20
,
Ber21
]
. In higher dimension, related questions have also been investigated by Bera and Pal for polynomial shift-like maps
[
BP22
]
.

Both Lamy’s and Bera, Pal, and Verma’s proofs rely on Jung’s theorem
[
Jun42
]
, either directly or through the lens of Bass-Serre theory
[
Ser77
]
and the Friedland–Milnor classification
[
FM89
]
. In the two-dimensional case, Bera, Pal, and Verma also rely on the Böttcher coordinate introduced by Hubbard and Oberste-Vorth
[
HOV94
]
. However, in higher dimensions, a simple classification of automorphisms is unavailable, even for Hénon–Sibony maps; furthermore, the Green function fails to be pluriharmonic on the basin of attraction of
I_{f}^{-}
. This article aims to establish a similar result for Hénon–Sibony maps, providing a novel proof for the classical Hénon case. Our main result is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f
and
g
be two Hénon–Sibony maps of
{\mathbb{C}}^{k}
. If
K_{f}^{+}=K^{+}_{g}
, then
f
and
g
share a common iterate; that is, there exist positive integers
n
and
m
such that
f^{n}=g^{m}
. Furthermore, for a given
f
, there are only finitely many such maps
g
with the same degree as
f
.

In Example
3.4
, we show that two Hénon–Sibony maps may possess the same Green measure yet fail to share an iterate.

Let us now describe the organization of the article and outline the main ideas of the proof. In Section
2
, we recall that for Hénon–Sibony maps, the condition
K^{+}_{f}=K^{+}_{g}
is equivalent to
G_{f}^{+}=G_{g}^{+}
. This classical result allows one to reformulate the initial topological question in a measurable framework. We show that if
G_{f}^{+}=G_{g}^{+}
, then the algebraic degrees
d_{f}
and
d_{g}
satisfy
d_{f}^{n}=d_{g}^{m}
, so that, up to passing to suitable iterates, one may reduce to the case where
f
and
g
have the same degree. In the same vein, we prove that for any
q\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the map
f^{q}\circ g^{-q}
is an affine automorphism preserving
G_{f}^{+}
. This reduces the problem to showing that there are only finitely many affine automorphisms preserving
G_{f}^{+}
. This reduction constitutes the starting point of the approaches developed in
[
Lam01
]
and
[
BPV20
]
.

In Section
3
, we prove Theorem
1.1
under the additional assumption that
G^{\pm}_{f}=G^{\pm}_{g}
. The key idea is that an affine automorphism
\beta
preserving both
G^{+}_{f}
and
G^{-}_{f}
must preserve the Green measure, hence its barycenter, as well as the indeterminacy sets. This forces
\beta
to preserve many analytic sets of the form
f^{n}(L)
and
f^{-n}(L^{\prime})
, where
L
and
L^{\prime}
are analytic sets of codimension
s
and
k-s
, respectively. A deep convergence result of Dinh and Sibony
[
DS16
]
then shows that, for
n
large enough,
f^{n}(L)\cap f^{-n}(L^{\prime})
spans
{\mathbb{C}}^{k}
, which allows us to conclude.

In Section
4
, we introduce the set

N:=\left\{\beta\in\mathrm{Aff}_{k}\;;\;\forall n\in{\mathbb{N}},\;f^{n}\circ\beta\circ f^{-n}\in\mathrm{Aff}_{k}\right\},

where
\mathrm{Aff}_{k}
denotes the group of affine automorphisms of
{\mathbb{C}}^{k}
. Although
N
may be larger than the set of affine automorphisms preserving
G^{+}_{f}
, it has the crucial advantage of being an algebraic subgroup of
\mathrm{Aff}_{k}
. Showing that
N
is finite will yield the desired result. Arguing by contradiction, we assume that
N
has positive dimension and thus produce a fibration invariant by
f
on which
f
will have both topological entropy
<d^{s}
and equal to
d^{s}
. This argument notably relies on another result of Dinh and Sibony
[
DS09
]
concerning the extremality of the Green current
T^{+}_{f,s}
.

Finally, in Section
5
, we show that when
s=1
, the unique positive closed
(1,1)
-current of mass
1
supported on
\{G^{+}_{f}=a\}
is
\mathrm{dd}^{c}\max(G^{+}_{f},a)
. This argument was communicated to us by Dinh
[
Din
]
. In contrast, we point out that this uniqueness may fail to hold when
s>1
. Section
6
is then devoted to the study of an example.

2.
Preliminary facts on Hénon–Sibony maps

For a Hénon–Sibony map
f
, the Julia set
K^{+}_{f}
, the Green function
G_{f}^{+}
and the Green currents
T^{+}_{f}
and
T^{+}_{f,s}
are defined in the introduction.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f
and
g
be two Hénon–Sibony maps of
{\mathbb{C}}^{k}
. Let
s=\dim(I_{f}^{-})+1
. Then we have the equivalence:

(1)

K^{+}_{f}=K^{+}_{g}
;

(2)

G_{f}^{+}=G^{+}_{g}
;

(3)

T^{+}_{f}=T^{+}_{g}
;

(4)

\dim(I^{+}_{f})\geq\dim(I^{+}_{g})
and
T^{+}_{f,s}=T^{+}_{g,s}
.

Furthermore, in all the above cases, we have
I^{+}_{f}=I^{+}_{g}
.

Proof.

Obviously
(1)\Leftarrow\ (2)\Leftrightarrow(3)\Rightarrow(4)
. Recall that, in
\mathbb{P}^{k}
,
\overline{K^{+}_{f}}=K^{+}_{f}\cup I^{+}_{f}

[
Sib99
]
so
I^{+}_{f}
is characterized by
K^{+}_{f}
. We have that
(1)\Rightarrow(4)
is an immediate consequence of
[
DS09
, Theorem 5.5.4]
which states that a positive closed current of bounded mass supported by
K^{+}_{f}
is proportional to
T^{+}_{f,s}
.

Let us show
(4)\Rightarrow(2)
.
Consider a generic linear space
P
of dimension
s
so that
P\cap(I^{+}_{f}\cup I^{+}_{g})=\varnothing
. On
P
,
(dd^{c}G_{f}^{+})^{s}=(dd^{c}G_{g}^{+})^{s}
and both functions
G^{+}_{f}
and
G^{+}_{g}
are in the Lelong class of
P
(e.g.
[
DS14
, Theorem 8.4]
). Uniqueness of the solution of the Monge-Ampère equation in the Lelong class implies that
G^{+}_{f}
and
G^{+}_{g}
differ by a constant on
P
. Moving
P
implies this constant does not depend on
P
and it is equal to 0 evaluating at points in
K^{+}_{f}
and
K^{+}_{g}
. So we have that
(4)\Rightarrow(2)
and
I^{+}_{f}=I^{+}_{g}
by the above.∎

The following lemma shows that a map is linear by using the growth of the Green function. See
[
Lam01
,
BPV20
,
CD24
]
for a similar argument.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
f
and
g
be two Hénon–Sibony maps of
{\mathbb{C}}^{k}
, of degree
d_{f}
and
d_{g}
such that
G^{+}_{f}=G^{+}_{g}
, then there exist integers
m
and
n
such that
d_{f}^{n}=d_{g}^{m}
and for any
q\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
f^{qn}\circ g^{-qm}
is an affine automorphism that preserves
G_{f}^{+}
.

Proof.

Let
f
and
g
be as above. Let
\delta_{f}
and
\delta_{g}
denote the algebraic degree of
f^{-1}
and
g^{-1}
. By Lemma
2.1
, one has
I_{f}^{+}=I_{g}^{+}
, thus
d_{f}^{s}=\delta_{f}^{k-s}
and
d_{g}^{s}=\delta_{g}^{k-s}
.
Since

\{d_{f}^{n}d_{g}^{m},\ n,m\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
is a multiplicative subgroup of
{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
, replacing
f
and
g
by iterates and possibly
f
by
g
, we assume that
d^{s}_{f}\leq d^{s}_{g}<2d^{s}_{f}
. By the above, this implies
\delta^{k-s}_{f}\leq\delta^{k-s}_{g}<2\delta^{k-s}_{f}
. We deduce from that
d_{f}d_{g}^{-1}\leq 1
and
\delta_{f}^{-1}\delta_{g}<2
. Using the invariance

G^{+}_{f}\circ f=d_{f}G^{+}_{f}
,
G^{+}_{f}\circ g^{-1}=d_{g}^{-1}G^{+}_{f}
(
[
Sib99
, Remarque 2.2.15]
) gives

(1)

G^{+}_{f}\circ f\circ g^{-1}=d_{f}d_{g}^{-1}G^{+}_{f}.

Similarly, we have
G^{-}_{f}\circ f\circ g^{-1}=\delta_{f}^{-1}G^{-}_{f}\circ g^{-1}
.
Now, using
G^{-}_{f}(z)\leq\log^{+}\|z\|+O(1)
, which is an immediate consequence of
[
Sib99
, Théorème 1.6.1]
, and using the fact
\log^{+}\|g^{-1}(z)\|\leq\delta_{g}\log^{+}\|z\|+O(1)
since
g^{-1}
is a rational map of degree
\delta_{g}
, imply

(2)

G^{-}_{f}\circ f\circ g^{-1}(z)\leq\delta_{f}^{-1}\delta_{g}\log^{+}\|z\|+O(1).

Now, we have that
G_{f}:=\max(G_{f}^{+},G^{-}_{f})=\log^{+}\|z\|+O(1)
(e.g.
[
DS14
, Theorem 8.4]
). So combining (
1
) and (
2
) with
d_{f}d_{g}^{-1}\leq 1
and
\delta_{f}^{-1}\delta_{g}<2
gives

\log^{+}\|f\circ g^{-1}(z)\|<2\log^{+}\|f\circ g^{-1}(z)\|+O(1).

In particular, the polynomial map
f\circ g^{-1}
has degree
<2
so it has degree
1
. This implies that
f\circ g^{-1}
is an affine automorphism of
{\mathbb{C}}^{k}
and by (
1
), it preserves
G_{f}^{+}
. We can replace
f
by
f^{q}
and
g
by
g^{q}
and the same argument apply so for any
q\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
f^{q}\circ g^{-q}
is an affine automorphism that preserves
G_{f}^{+}
.
∎

3.
Hénon–Sibony maps sharing both filled Julia sets.

In this section, we consider two Hénon–Sibony maps that share both filled Julia sets
K^{+}_{f}
and
K^{-}_{f}
and prove the main theorem under that additional hypothesis.

The following lemma is where we use our main ingredient which is a convergence result of Dinh and Sibony
[
DS16
]

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f
be a Hénon–Sibony maps of
{\mathbb{C}}^{k}
. Then, the set

G:=\{\alpha\lx@nobreakspace\in\mathrm{Aff}({\mathbb{C}}^{k}),\alpha^{*}(T^{\pm}_{f})=T^{\pm}_{f}\}

is a finite group and
\begin{cases}L_{f}:&G\to G\\
&\alpha\mapsto f\circ\alpha\circ f^{-1}\end{cases}
is a well defined group isomorphism.

Proof.

The fact that
G
is a group is immediate. Let us show that

L_{f}:G\to G
is a well-defined group isomorphism. For this, we only need to check that for any

\alpha\in G
, we have
f\circ\alpha\circ f^{-1}\in G
. First,

f\circ\alpha\circ f^{-1}
is a polynomial automorphism of
\mathbb{C}^{k}
that still leaves

T_{f}^{+}
and
T_{f}^{-}
invariant. In particular, by Lemma
2.1
, we have the equalities

G_{f}^{+}=G_{f}^{+}\circ f\circ\alpha\circ f^{-1}
and

G_{f}^{-}=G_{f}^{-}\circ f\circ\alpha\circ f^{-1}
.
Since
\max(G_{f}^{+},G_{f}^{-})=\log^{+}\|z\|+O(1)
, we deduce that

\log^{+}\|f\circ\alpha\circ f^{-1}(z)\|=\log^{+}\|z\|+O(1)
.
This implies that
f\circ\alpha\circ f^{-1}
has algebraic degree
1
, hence belongs to
G
.

It remains to show that
G
is finite. Pick
\alpha\in G
. Then

\alpha^{*}((T_{f}^{+})^{s}\wedge(T_{f}^{-})^{k-s})=(T_{f}^{+})^{s}\wedge(T_{f}^{-})^{k-s}
, so

\alpha^{*}(\mu_{f})=\mu_{f}=\alpha_{*}(\mu_{f})
.
Consider the barycenter

p:=\int z\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{f}(z)\in\mathbb{C}^{k},

where
z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{k})
. Since
\alpha
is affine,

\alpha(p)=\int\alpha(z)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{f}(z)=\int z\,\mathrm{d}\alpha_{*}(\mu_{f})(z)=p.

Up to an affine change of coordinates, we may assume that
p=(0,\dots,0)\in\mathbb{C}^{k}
is the origin, so that
G
is a linear group, i.e.
G\subset\mathrm{GL}(\mathbb{C}^{k})
.
Consider homogeneous coordinates on
\mathbb{P}^{k}
given by

z=[z_{0}:z_{1}:\dots:z_{k}]
, so that
\mathbb{C}^{k}
is identified with
\{z_{0}=1\}
and

H_{\infty}:=\{z_{0}=0\}
is the hyperplane at infinity.
Let
\pi:\mathbb{P}^{k}\dasharrow H_{\infty}
be the meromorphic projection defined by

\pi([z_{0}:z_{1}:\dots:z_{k}])=[z_{1}:\dots:z_{k}]
.
Since
\alpha^{*}((T_{f}^{+})^{s})=(T_{f}^{+})^{s}
and the support of
(T_{f}^{+})^{s}
intersected with
H_{\infty}
is equal to
I_{f}^{+}
, any
\alpha\in G
, viewed as an automorphism of
\mathbb{P}^{k}
, fixes
I_{f}^{+}
(and similarly
I_{f}^{-}
).
As
\alpha
is linear, it also fixes
A^{+}:=\overline{\pi^{-1}(I_{f}^{+})}
(resp.
A^{-}:=\overline{\pi^{-1}(I_{f}^{-})}
) which is an algebraic set of dimension
k-s
(resp. of dimension
s
).

By the above, we deduce that for any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
f^{n}\circ\alpha\lx@nobreakspace\circ f^{-n}(A^{+})=A^{+}
, so
\alpha(f^{-n}(A^{+}))=f^{-n}(A^{+})
. Similarly, we have
\alpha(f^{n}(A^{-}))=f^{n}(A^{-})
. Now, by
[
DS16
, Remark 5.15]
, we know that points in
f^{-n}(A^{+})\cap f^{n}(A^{-})
are equidistributed with respect to
\mu_{f}
as
n\to\infty
. Since
\mu_{f}
does not charge pluripolar sets, hence analytic sets, we deduce that the finite set
f^{-n_{0}}(A^{+})\cap f^{n_{0}}(A^{-})
spans
{\mathbb{C}}^{k}
for a suitably large
n_{0}
. Now, any
\alpha\in G
fixes
f^{-n_{0}}(A^{+})\cap f^{n_{0}}(A^{-})
and there are only finitely many linear maps fixing
f^{-n_{0}}(A^{+})\cap f^{n_{0}}(A^{-})
,
so that
G
is finite. ∎

Remark 3.2
.

While
[
DS16
]
proves a much stronger result that what we need (i.e
\mathrm{span}(f^{-n_{0}}(A^{+})\cap f^{n_{0}}(A^{-}))={\mathbb{C}}^{k}
for some
n_{0}
), we have found no simpler argument. Note that we can show that
f^{-n_{0}}(A^{+})\cap A^{-}\backslash\{(0,\dots,0)\}\neq\varnothing
and
f^{n_{0}}(A^{-})\cap A^{+}\backslash\{(0,\dots,0)\}\neq\varnothing
for some
n_{0}
using arguments similar to
[
DF17
, Proposition 7.1]
. This would be enough to conclude in dimension
2
, where
A^{\pm}
are one-dimensional vector spaces but is insufficient in higher dimensions (
\mathrm{span}(f^{-n_{0}}(A^{+})\cap A^{-})
may have dimension
<k-s
).

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
f
and
g
be two Hénon–Sibony maps of
{\mathbb{C}}^{k}
. If
T^{\pm}_{f}=T^{\pm}_{g}
, then
f
and
g
share an iterate. Furthermore, for a given
f
, there are only finitely many such maps
g
with the same degree as
f
.

Proof.

Let
f
and
g
be as in the lemma. By Lemma
2.2
, up to iterating
f
and
g
, we can assume that
f
and
g
have common degree
d
and for all
n
, we have that
\alpha_{n}:=f^{n}\circ g^{-n}
is an affine automorphism which preserves both
T^{+}_{f}
and
T^{-}_{f}
. By Lemma
3.1
whose notations we use,
\alpha_{n}\in G
for all
n
and
G
is finite. In particular, there exist integers
n\neq m
such that
\alpha_{n}=\alpha_{m}
. This implies
f^{n-m}=g^{n-m}
.

Finally, observe that for all
g
which have the same degree than
f
and such that
T^{\pm}_{f}=T^{\pm}_{g}
, then
f\circ g^{-1}
is in
G
which is finite by Lemma
3.1
.
∎

The following example shows that two Hénon–Sibony maps can have the same Green measure while not sharing an iterate. It also gives that a Hénon–Sibony map can preserve the forward Julia set of another Hénon–Sibony map without sharing an iterate.

Example 3.4
.

Let
\pi_{i}:{\mathbb{C}}^{4}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
for
i=1,2
denote the projections defined by
\pi_{1}(x,y,z,w)=(x,y)
and
\pi_{2}(x,y,z,w)=(z,w)
. Let
f
be a Hénon map of
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
of degree
d
. Then

F(x,y,z,w)=\bigl(f(x,y),\,f(z,w)\bigr)\quad\text{and}\quad G(x,y,z,w)=\bigl(f(x,y),\,f^{-1}(z,w)\bigr)

are both Hénon–Sibony maps of
{\mathbb{C}}^{4}
with
s=2
. The Green currents of order
2
associated with
F
and
G
can be expressed in terms of those of
f
via

T^{+}_{F,2}=\pi_{1}^{*}(T^{+}_{f})\wedge\pi_{2}^{*}(T^{+}_{f}),\quad T^{-}_{F,2}=\pi_{1}^{*}(T^{-}_{f})\wedge\pi_{2}^{*}(T^{-}_{f}),

T^{+}_{G,2}=\pi_{1}^{*}(T^{+}_{f})\wedge\pi_{2}^{*}(T^{-}_{f}),\quad T^{-}_{G,2}=\pi_{1}^{*}(T^{-}_{f})\wedge\pi_{2}^{*}(T^{+}_{f}).

From this, one verifies that

\mu_{F}=\pi_{1}^{*}(\mu_{f})\wedge\pi_{2}^{*}(\mu_{f})=\mu_{G}.

Moreover,
G(K^{+}_{F})=K^{+}_{F}
. Clearly,
F
and
G
commute (
F\circ G=G\circ F
), but they do not share any iterate.

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

By Lemma
2.2
, we can thus assume that
f
and
g
have the same degree (so that
n=m=1
). To simplify the notations, we let
d
be the common degree of
f
and
g
and
\delta
the common degree of
f^{-1}
and
g^{-1}
. Let
\mathrm{Aff}_{k}
denote the variety of all the affine automorphisms of
{\mathbb{C}}^{k}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
N:=\{\beta\in\mathrm{Aff}_{k},\ \forall n\in{\mathbb{N}},\ f^{n}\circ\beta\circ f^{-n}\in\mathrm{Aff}_{k}\}
then
N
is an algebraic subgroup of
\mathrm{Aff}_{k}
, for any
q\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
f^{q}\circ g^{-q}\in N
and
\begin{cases}L_{f}:&N\to N\\
&\beta\mapsto f\circ\beta\circ f^{-1}\end{cases}
is a bijection.

Proof.

By intersection,
N
is an algebraic group. To show that

f^{q}\circ g^{-q}\in N
, observe that, by Lemma
2.2
,

f^{q}\circ g^{-q}
is affine and preserves
G_{f}^{+}
:

G_{f}^{+}\circ f^{n}\circ f^{q}\circ g^{-q}\circ f^{-n}=G_{f}^{+},

and similarly for
G_{f}^{-}
:

\displaystyle G_{f}^{-}\circ f^{n}\circ f^{q}\circ g^{-q}\circ f^{-n}

\displaystyle=\delta^{-n-q}G_{f}^{-}\circ g^{-q}\circ f^{-n}

\displaystyle\leq\delta^{-n-q}\log^{+}\|g^{-q}\circ f^{-n}(z)\|+O(1)

\displaystyle\leq\log^{+}\|z\|+O(1),

since

is a polynomial map of degree

.
As in the proof of Lemma

, we deduce that

so

is an affine automorphism. Finally, the endomorphism

\begin{cases}N\to N\\
\beta\mapsto f\circ\beta\circ f^{-1}\end{cases}

is both injective and a morphism of varieties, hence it is a bijection.
∎

Assume now that

is finite. Then there exist

such that

.

Proposition 4.2
.

The set
N_{1}
is finite.

In what follows, for

(3)

G_{a}:=\max(G_{f}^{+},a)\ \mathrm{and}\ T_{a}:=\mathrm{dd}^{c}G_{a}

so that
G_{a}
is psh and

is a positive closed current of mass

Lemma 4.3
.

Take
\beta\in N
and
a\geq 0
. Then
(\beta^{*}T_{a})\wedge T^{+}_{f,s}=0
.

Proof.

Take
n\in\mathbb{N}
. In
\mathbb{C}^{k}
,

\displaystyle(\beta^{*}T_{a})\wedge T^{+}_{f,s}

\displaystyle=(f^{n})^{*}(f^{n})_{*}\big((\beta^{*}T_{a})\wedge T^{+}_{f,s}\big)

\displaystyle=(f^{n})^{*}\big((f^{n})_{*}(\beta^{*}T_{a})\wedge(f^{n})_{*}T^{+}_{f,s}\big)

\displaystyle=(f^{n})^{*}\big((f^{n})_{*}(\beta^{*}d^{-n}(f^{n})^{*}T_{d^{n}a})\wedge d^{-sn}T^{+}_{f,s}\big)

\displaystyle=d^{-(s+1)n}(f^{n})^{*}\big((f^{n}\circ\beta\circ f^{-n})^{*}T_{d^{n}a}\wedge T^{+}_{f,s}\big),

where we used
T_{a}=d^{-n}(f^{n})^{*}T_{d^{n}a}
and
(f^{n})_{*}T^{+}_{f,s}=d^{-sn}T^{+}_{f,s}
. Since

, the current
(f^{n}\circ\beta\circ f^{-n})^{*}T_{d^{n}a}
has mass

. By Bézout, its wedge product with

has mass at most
1
, and the pullback of a positive closed
(s+1,s+1)
-current of mass
1
by

has mass at most
\delta^{(k-s-1)n}\ll d^{-(s+1)n}
. Hence
(\beta^{*}T_{a})\wedge T^{+}_{f,s}=0
.
∎

Since
(\beta^{*}T_{a})\wedge T^{+}_{f,s}=\mathrm{dd}^{c}(G_{a}\circ\beta\,T^{+}_{f,s})
, the current
G_{a}\circ\beta\,T^{+}_{f,s}
is positive harmonic and supported on

. If

(e.g. when

is

from

]

Lemma 4.4
.

Take
\beta\in N
, then
G^{+}_{f}\circ\beta
is constant on the support of

.

Proof.

Assume it is not the case. In particular, we can take

such that
H_{a}:=\min(G^{+}_{f}\circ\beta,a)=G_{0}\circ\beta+a-G_{a}\circ\beta
is non constant on the support of
T^{+}_{f,s}
. It is a non negative function and, by difference, Lemma
4.3
gives
\mathrm{dd}^{c}H_{a}\wedge T^{+}_{f,s}=0
. To show that
H_{a}\circ\beta T^{+}_{f,s}
is closed, it suffices to show that for any smooth
1
-form
\theta
, compactly supported in
{\mathbb{C}}^{k}
and any Kähler form
\tilde{\omega}
, one has

\langle\mathrm{d}H_{a}\circ\beta\wedge T^{+}_{f,s},\theta\tilde{\omega}^{k-s-1}\rangle=0,

where
\langle,\rangle
denotes the usual duality pairing for currents. By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality:

\displaystyle\ \left|\langle\mathrm{d}H_{a}\circ\beta\wedge T^{+}_{f,s},\theta\tilde{\omega}^{k-s-1}\rangle\right|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\left|\langle\mathrm{d}H_{a}\wedge\mathrm{d}^{c}H_{a}\wedge T^{+}_{f,s},\tilde{\omega}^{k-s-1}\rangle\right|\left|\langle i\theta\wedge\bar{\theta}\wedge T^{+}_{f,s},\tilde{\omega}^{k-s-1}\rangle\right|.

The second term of the right-hand side is bounded and we show that the first term vanishes. For that, observe that

\mathrm{d}H_{a}\wedge\mathrm{d}^{c}H_{a}=1/2\mathrm{dd}^{c}H_{a}^{2}-H_{a}\mathrm{dd}^{c}H_{a}
and since

\mathrm{dd}^{c}H_{a}\wedge T^{+}_{f,s}=0
, we deduce that
\mathrm{d}H_{a}\wedge\mathrm{d}^{c}H_{a}\wedge T^{+}_{f,s}\wedge\tilde{\omega}^{k-s-1}=\mathrm{dd}^{c}H_{a}^{2}\wedge T^{+}_{f,s}\wedge\tilde{\omega}^{k-s-1}
is a non negative measure and

\left|\langle\mathrm{d}H_{a}\wedge\mathrm{d}^{c}H_{a}\wedge T^{+}_{f,s},\tilde{\omega}^{k-s-1}\rangle\right|=\left|\langle\mathrm{dd}^{c}H_{a}^{2}\wedge T^{+}_{f,s},\tilde{\omega}^{k-s-1}\rangle\right|.

It remains to show that the mass of
\mathrm{dd}^{c}H_{a}^{2}\wedge T^{+}_{f,s}\wedge\tilde{\omega}^{k-s-1}
is equal to
0
on
{\mathbb{C}}^{k}
. For that take
R\gg 1
and consider

\phi_{R}=\frac{\log\max(\|z\|,R^{2})-\log\max(\|z\|,R)}{\log R}

so
\phi_{R}
is equal to
1
inside
B(0,R)
and equal to
0
outside
B(0,R^{2})
. By Stokes formula:

\left|\langle\mathrm{dd}^{c}H_{a}^{2}\wedge T^{+}_{f,s}\wedge\tilde{\omega}^{k-s-1},\phi_{R}\rangle\right|=\left|\langle H_{a}^{2}T^{+}_{f,s}\wedge\tilde{\omega}^{k-s-1},\mathrm{dd}^{c}\phi_{R}\rangle\right|.

Now

can be written as

where

are positive closed currents of mass

. By Bézout, since
H_{a}^{2}\leq a^{2}
, we deduce

\left|\langle\mathrm{dd}^{c}H_{a}^{2}\wedge T^{+}_{f,s}\wedge\tilde{\omega}^{k-s-1},\phi_{R}\rangle\right|\leq\frac{2a^{2}}{\log R},

so
\mathrm{dd}^{c}H_{a}^{2}\wedge T^{+}_{f,s}\wedge\tilde{\omega}^{k-s-1}
is zero on
{\mathbb{C}}^{k}
by letting

and (

But now,

states that a positive closed current of bounded mass supported by

is proportional to

. This implies that

is constant on the support of

Lemma 4.6
.

The function
\Phi:N_{1}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{+}

\Phi(\beta)=\log^{+}\|\beta\|_{\mathrm{Aff}}+O(1).

For each

Proof of Proposition
4.2
.

Recall that we assumed
\dim N_{1}>0
. However, Lemmas
4.10
and

, and hence

Corollary 4.12
.

The set
N
is finite.

Proof.

Pick
\beta\in N
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
N
is a group and by the chain rule, we have
f^{n}\circ\beta\circ f^{-n}\circ\beta^{-1}\in N_{1}
. By Proposition
4.2
,
N_{1}
is finite, so there exist
n\neq m
such that
f^{n}\circ\beta\circ f^{-n}\circ\beta^{-1}=f^{m}\circ\beta\circ f^{-m}\circ\beta^{-1}
. We deduce

\beta^{-1}\circ f^{\,n-m}\circ\beta=f^{\,n-m}.

In particular, since
G_{f^{n-m}}^{+}=G_{f}^{+}
and
G^{+}_{\beta^{-1}\circ f^{n-m}\circ\beta}=G_{f^{n-m}}^{+}\circ\beta
, we deduce
G_{f}^{+}\circ\beta=G_{f}^{+}
and similarly
G_{f}^{-}\circ\beta=G_{f}^{-}
. Then Lemma
3.1
implies that there are only finitely many such
\beta
. ∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem
1.1
is now a direct consequence of Lemma
4.1
and Corollary
4.12
.
∎

Remark 4.13
.

The finiteness of
N
can be formulated over an arbitrary base field, not necessarily
{\mathbb{C}}
, for instance in positive characteristic. However, our approach relies heavily on complex-analytic methods. It would be interesting to obtain a purely algebraic proof of this result.

Remark 4.14
.

In the statement of Theorem
1.1
, we assume that
f
and
g
are Hénon–Sibony maps. It would be natural to seek an extension to the case where
f
and
g
become Hénon–Sibony maps only after passing to a suitable birational model. However, it is not clear in general why two maps sharing the same forward Julia set should be simultaneously conjugate to Hénon–Sibony maps in a common birational model (see
[
DF17
]
for related results in dimension
2
).

5.
Rigidity of
\{G_{f}^{+}=a\}

The proof of Theorem
1.1
would be simpler if one knew that
\{G_{f}^{+}=a\}
supports a unique positive closed
(s,s)
-current of mass
1
. Indeed, Lemma
4.4
would then almost directly imply that
G_{f}^{+}\circ\beta=0
on the support of
T^{+}_{f,s}
, so that
\beta
preserves both
G_{f}^{+}
and
G_{f}^{-}
. We will see that this property holds when
s=1
(hence in dimension
2
), but that counterexamples exist in higher dimensions.

When
a=0
, the following result is due to Fornæss and Sibony
[
FS95
]
in the case
k=2
, and to Dinh and Sibony
[
DS09
]
in general (without any assumption on
s
). For
a>0
and
k=2
, Dinh and Sibony
[
DS14
, Proposition 6.11]
proved the result under the additional assumption that
K_{f}^{+}
is not contained in a (possibly singular) real-analytic hypersurface. As mentioned in the introduction, the general proof was communicated to us by Dinh
[
Din
]
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
f
be a Hénon–Sibony map of
{\mathbb{C}}^{k}
of degree
d
with
s=1
. Then, for any
a\geq 0
, the current
T_{a}=\mathrm{dd}^{c}G_{a}
is the unique positive closed current of mass
1
supported on
\{G_{f}^{+}=a\}
.

Proof.

Pick
a>0
. Let
(T_{1,n})
and
(T_{2,n})
be two sequences of positive closed currents of mass
1
supported on
\{G^{+}_{f}=ad^{n}\}
. We want to show that

\lim_{n\to\infty}d^{-n}(f^{n})^{*}(T_{1,n}-T_{2,n})=0.

Since
\dim I^{-}_{f}=a-1=0
,
I^{-}_{f}
is reduced to a point. We pick homogeneous coordinates
[z_{1}:z_{2}:\dots:z_{k+1}]
on
\mathbb{P}^{k}
so that the hyperplane at infinity is given by
z_{k+1}=0
with
I^{-}_{f}=[1:0:\dots:0]
, and we pick the corresponding coordinates
(z_{1},\dots,z_{k})
on
{\mathbb{C}}^{k}
.
Pick
z
such that
G_{f}^{+}(z)=c>a
, then
G_{f}^{+}(f^{n}(z))=d^{n}c
. Let
L
be the complex line joining
f^{n}(z)
and
I^{-}_{f}
. We take
z_{1}
as the coordinate on
L
and we write

f^{n}(z)=(z_{1}^{n},\dots,z_{k}^{n})
so
f^{n}(z)
has coordinate
z_{1}^{n}
on
L
.

From the fact that
G_{f}^{+}(w)=\log^{+}\|w\|+O(1)
in a neighborhood of
I^{-}_{f}
and
G_{f}^{-}(w)\leq\log^{+}\|w\|+O(1)
, one deduces from
G_{f}^{+}(f(w))=dG_{f}^{+}(w)
and
G_{f}^{-}(f(w))=\delta^{-1}G_{f}^{-}(w)
that there exist a neighborhood
V
of
I^{-}_{f}
in
\mathbb{P}^{k}
such that
\log|z_{1}|=\log^{+}\|w\|+O(1)
and
\log^{+}\|w\|+O(1)=G^{+}_{f}>G_{f}^{-}
on
V\cap{\mathbb{C}}^{k}
.

Hence, provided
n
is large enough,
f^{n}(z)\in V
and

G_{f}^{+}(f^{n}(z))=\log|z_{1}^{n}|+O(1)
while for any
z^{\prime}\in V\cap{\mathbb{C}}^{k}
, such that
G_{f}^{+}(z^{\prime})=ad^{n}
, one has
\log|z_{1}^{\prime}|\leq G_{f}^{+}(z^{\prime})+O(1)\lx@nobreakspace=ad^{n}+O(1)
. On the other hand, we have that there exist a constant
C>1
such that for any
z^{\prime}\in L\lx@nobreakspace\backslash V
,

C^{-1}\leq|1-\frac{z_{1}^{\prime}}{z_{1}^{n}}|\leq C
(simply because
z^{n}_{1}
is very large and that outside
V
,
z^{\prime}_{1}
can not be too large). Combining those two facts gives, changing
C
if necessary,

(6)

\forall z^{\prime}\in L\cap\{G^{+}_{f}=ad^{n}\},\ C^{-1}\leq|1-\frac{z_{1}^{\prime}}{z_{1}^{n}}|\leq C.

Since
T_{1,n}-T_{2,n}
is smooth near
I^{-}_{f}
, we can write
T_{1,n}-T_{2,n}=\mathrm{dd}^{c}u_{n}
with
u_{n}
uniquely determined by
u_{n}(I^{-}_{f})=0
. We thus have by Stokes and the fundamental solution of the Laplacian on
L

u_{n}(f^{n}(z))=\langle u_{n},[L]\wedge\mathrm{dd}^{c}\log|z^{\prime}_{1}-z_{1}^{n}|\rangle=\langle(T_{1,n}-T_{2,n})\wedge[L],\log|z^{\prime}_{1}-z_{1}^{n}|\rangle.

Using that
(T_{i,n}\wedge[L]
are probability measures, we deduce:

u_{n}(f^{n}(z))=\left\langle(T_{1,n}-T_{2,n})\wedge[L],\log\left|1-\frac{z^{\prime}_{1}}{z^{n}_{1}}\right|\right\rangle.

By (
6
), we deduce
C^{-1}\leq u_{n}(f^{n}(z))\leq C
so letting
n\to\infty
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}d^{-n}u_{n}(f^{n}(z))=0.

Pick
T_{1,n}=T_{ad^{n}}
and let
L^{\prime}
be a generic line passing trough
I^{-}_{f}
. Then,
d^{-n}(f^{n})^{*}T_{ad^{n}}=T_{a}
and
T_{a}\wedge[L^{\prime}]
is the Green measure
\mu_{a,L^{\prime}}
of the compact set
\{G^{+}_{f}=a\}\cap L^{\prime}
in
L^{\prime}
. In particular, when extracting a converging subsequence
T_{2,n}\to S
, we have
\mu_{n,2}:=T_{2,n}\wedge[L^{\prime}]\to S\wedge[L^{\prime}]=:\mu_{S,L^{\prime}}
(
L^{\prime}
is generic) hence
\mu_{S,L^{\prime}}
is a probability measure supported on
L^{\prime}\cap\{G^{+}_{f}=a\}
. By the above, we have, on
L^{\prime}
,
\mu_{a,L^{\prime}}-\mu_{2,n}=\mathrm{dd}^{c}d^{-n}u_{n}(f^{n}(z))
and we can extract a converging subsequence in
L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
so if we write
\mu_{a,L^{\prime}}-\mu_{S,L^{\prime}}=\mathrm{dd}^{c}v
, we have that
v\equiv 0
in
\{G^{+}_{f}>a\}\cap L^{\prime}
(up to subtracting a constant). By extremality of the Green measure, this implies that
\mu_{a,L^{\prime}}=\mu_{S,L^{\prime}}
so that
v\equiv 0
in
\{G^{+}_{f}>a\}\cap L^{\prime}
. This implies that
T_{a}=T_{S}
thus
\lim_{n\to\infty}d^{-n}(f^{n})^{*}(T_{1,n}-T_{2,n})=0
for any sequences
(T_{1,n})
and
(T_{2,n})
of positive closed currents of mass
1
supported on
\{G^{+}_{f}=ad^{n}\}
.

The proof of the proposition is now classical: pick
S
a positive closed current of mass
1
supported by
\{G^{+}_{f}=a\}
, then
S_{n}:=d^{n}(f^{n})_{*}(S)
is a positive closed current of mass
1
supported on
\{G^{+}_{f}=ad^{n}\}
so by the above
S=d^{-n}(f^{n})^{*}S_{n}\to T_{a}
.
∎

Example 5.2
.

Let
f
and
g
be two Hénon maps of
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
of degree
d
, and set

F(x,y,z,w)=\bigl(f(x,y),\,g(z,w)\bigr).

Then
F
is a Hénon–Sibony map of
{\mathbb{C}}^{4}
, with
\dim I^{+}_{F}=\dim I^{-}_{F}=1
, so
s=2
and

G^{+}_{F}=\max(G^{+}_{f},G^{+}_{g}).

Moreover,

(\mathrm{dd}^{c}\max(G^{+}_{F},a))^{2}=\mathrm{dd}^{c}\max(G^{+}_{f},a)\wedge\mathrm{dd}^{c}\max(G^{+}_{g},a).

For
b<a
, the current

\mathrm{dd}^{c}\max(G^{+}_{f},b)\wedge\mathrm{dd}^{c}\max(G^{+}_{g},a)

is supported on
\{G^{+}_{f}=b\}\times\{G^{+}_{g}=a\}\subset\{G^{+}_{F}=a\}
, but differs from
(\mathrm{dd}^{c}\max(G^{+}_{F},a))^{2}
. Hence
\{G^{+}_{F}=a\}
carries infinitely many distinct positive closed currents of mass
1
.

6.
An example

Consider the Hénon–Sibony map of
{\mathbb{C}}^{3}
given by

F(x,y,z)=(y+x^{2},\,z+y^{2},\,x),

so that

F^{-1}(x,y,z)=\bigl(z,\,x-z^{2},\,y-(x-z^{2})^{2}\bigr).

We have
I^{+}_{F}=\{[0:0:1:0]\}
and
I^{-}_{F}=\{[x:y:0:0]\mid(x,y)\neq(0,0)\}
, hence
I^{+}_{F}\cap I^{-}_{F}=\varnothing
. We aim to determine explicitly

N=\left\{\beta\in\mathrm{Aff}_{3}\;\middle|\;\forall n\in{\mathbb{N}},\;F^{n}\circ\beta\circ F^{-n}\in\mathrm{Aff}_{3}\right\}.

As observed above, any
\beta\in N
fixes both
I^{+}_{F}
and
I^{-}_{F}
, and therefore can be written in the form

\begin{pmatrix}a&b&0\\
c&d&0\\
0&0&e\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x\\
y\\
z\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}x_{0}\\
y_{0}\\
z_{0}\end{pmatrix}.

A direct computation yields

(F\circ\beta\circ F^{-1})(x,y,z)=\begin{pmatrix}cz+d(x-z^{2})+y_{0}+(az+b(x-z^{2})+x_{0})^{2}\\
e\!\left(y-(x-z^{2})^{2}\right)+z_{0}+(cz+d(x-z^{2})+y_{0})^{2}\\
az+b(x-z^{2})+x_{0}\end{pmatrix}.

Since
F\circ\beta\circ F^{-1}\in N
, inspection of the third coordinate gives
b=0
and
a\neq 0
. Hence

(F\circ\beta\circ F^{-1})(x,y,z)=\begin{pmatrix}cz+d(x-z^{2})+y_{0}+(az+x_{0})^{2}\\
e\!\left(y-(x-z^{2})^{2}\right)+z_{0}+(cz+d(x-z^{2})+y_{0})^{2}\\
az+x_{0}\end{pmatrix}.

Examining the first coordinate yields
c=-2ax_{0}
and
d=a^{2}
, while the second coordinate gives
e=d^{2}=a^{4}
. Thus

(F\circ\beta\circ F^{-1})(x,y,z)=\begin{pmatrix}a^{2}x+y_{0}+x_{0}^{2}\\[6.0pt]
a^{4}y-4a^{3}x_{0}xz+4a^{3}x_{0}z^{3}+4a^{2}x_{0}^{2}z^{2}+2a^{2}y_{0}x-2a^{2}y_{0}z^{2}\\
\qquad-4ax_{0}y_{0}z+y_{0}^{2}+z_{0}\\[6.0pt]
az+x_{0}\end{pmatrix}.

Since
a\neq 0
, the presence of the nonlinear term
-4a^{3}x_{0}xz
in the second coordinate forces
x_{0}=0
. It then follows (for instance from the first and third coordinates) that
y_{0}=0
and
z_{0}=0
. Hence

(F\circ\beta\circ F^{-1})(x,y,z)=\begin{pmatrix}a^{2}x\\
a^{4}y\\
az\end{pmatrix}.

Applying the same argument to
F\circ\beta\circ F^{-1}
shows that
a=(a^{2})^{4}
, hence
a^{7}=1
. In conclusion,

N=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&0&0\\
0&a^{2}&0\\
0&0&a^{4}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x\\
y\\
z\end{pmatrix}\;\middle|\;a^{7}=1\right\}.

One can further observe that for
a\neq 1
,
\beta
and
F
do not commute as we have

\beta\circ F\circ\beta^{-1}(x,y,z)=\bigl(a^{-2}(x^{2}+y),\,a^{3}(z+y^{2}),\,a^{-1}x\bigr)\neq F(x,y,z).
