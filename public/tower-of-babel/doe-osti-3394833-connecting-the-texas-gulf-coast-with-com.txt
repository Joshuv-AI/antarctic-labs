Executive Summary/Abstract

Ecosystems that occupy the Terrestrial-Aquatic Interface (TAI) play critical roles and provide
important ecosystem services to humans. These systems are complex, dynamic, support high
levels of biodiversity, and have ecological characteristics that shift across the interface. In 2022,
DOE’s COMPASS project was a pilot study aimed to better understand the functions and roles
of freshwater and saline TAIs. Our intent with this project was to work with the COMPASS team
to fill in the vegetation sampling component, as the plant community was considered to be a key
piece in shaping TAI structure and function. We also planned to engage with the
COMPASS/broader DOE team to work towards taking advantage of collaboration opportunities.

Our team at Texas A&M University Corpus Christi (TAMUCC) interacted with DOE personnel,
facilities, and resources, specifically through the COMPASS project, as its TAI focus overlaps
substantially with many of our past, current, and planned research interests. These interactions
were helpful to our research group for responding to DOE calls for proposals, and specifically to
develop TAI research with an emphasis on plant-microbial interactions, at multiple sites along
the Gulf Coast. Being faculty at TAMUCC, a university with few DOE-funded projects, PI
Battaglia and Co-PI Zhang (and the broader university team) have been committed to building
pathways for our students to careers in environmental science and ecology. Exposure to “big
research” and world class facilities is a critical part of those efforts with our student population.
A major goal of this initiative was to engage and involve students, so that these career avenues
are clear and accessible, and they learn to network professionally.

This project benefitted numerous students in terms of networking and hands-on experience. Our
team also aided COMPASS teams with provision of field assistance, plant identification and
vegetation expertise.

Objectives

The original objectives of the project, as stated in our proposal, are as follows:

1) to develop a relationship with DOE that will benefit future research endeavors at TAMUCC, a
university with no history of DOE funding; 2) to build a mutually beneficial partnership with the
COMPASS team; 3) to develop capabilities for research development, and specifically on TAI
ecosystems; and 4) to expose TAMUCC students to “big science” and DOE national laboratory
resources (personnel and facilities).

The objectives of the project did not change over the course of the work. Although there were
some unavoidable delays in starting the project, all objectives were successfully accomplished.
We met and worked with multiple DOE personnel/teams involved with the COMPASS research.
During our site visits and meetings, we built strong relationships with the DOE researchers.

Project Activities

Engagement with the broader team resulted in a collaborative grant proposal (declined) and a
current partnership between TAMUCC (Battaglia and M.S. student Annika Gorman) and
Argonne National Laboratory researchers. This exciting project focuses on exterior microbiomes
of sea turtles. We continue to engage with our DOE colleagues to find collaboration and funding
opportunities on TAI ecosystems. The direct involvement of 14 students (3 undergraduates and
11 graduate students) in this project continues to be beneficial to their science career
development and trajectories. Some of these students were able to participate in multiple events
(see below)

Our first trip was planned to coincide with a field flooding experiment (TEMPEST III) the week
of June 10, 2024. Battaglia and Zhang brought a group of five students to work with and assist
the team of DOE scientists at the Smithsonian Environmental Research Center near Edgewater,
MD, during the three-day experiment.

The week of November 1, 2024, Battaglia and Zhang brought a group of four students to
Argonne National Laboratory (near Chicago, IL) to engage with the DOE scientists, give
presentations of their research, and brainstorm collaborative efforts to benefit TAMUCC students
in the context of the broader COMPASS project.

The week of March 25, 2025, Battaglia and two TAMUCC graduate students visited the Pacific
Northwest National Laboratory (PNNL) main campus in Richland, WA. Our group toured the
facilities, met DOE scientists, and gave presentations on our research to their team. We
discussed collaborative opportunities. Then, we visited the coastal PNNL campus in Sequim,
WA where we met their team, toured their facilities, and gave our research presentations to their
team.

Battaglia took six TAMUCC graduate students to the COMPASS project’s Lake Erie site the
week of May 18, 2025. The COMPASS team was prepping for their first large-scale freshwater
flooding experiment, which is similar to TEMPEST III, although the Lake Erie site is inland and
will consist of freshwater flooding instead of saline water. We assisted assist their team with pre-
flood vegetation sampling.

Battaglia brought three TAMUCC graduate students to Argonne National Laboratory the week of
October 7, 2025. The intent of this trip was to introduce these students, new to the graduate
program at TAMUCC, to the DOE programs and research. One of these students is working on
sea turtle microbiomes and used the occasion to interact with the molecular lab personnel. She
will be collaborating with these researchers and receiving trainings on molecular techniques with
microbial communities and data analyses. This work with the Argonne team will be very
beneficial for this student’s and ongoing research and will result in multi-authored manuscripts.

There were many delays at the onset of our project, including having insufficient numbers of
students to engage with the team, and the timing of field campaigns conducted by the DOE
COMPASS team. Once we established a large cohort of graduate and undergraduate students,
and the COMPASS team solidified their schedules for tasks, we were able to coordinate and
work with the team during field and lab visits. The timing of these visits required close
alignment with COMPASS teams, field schedules, and availability of DOE scientists.
Scheduling tasks and visits that are meaningful for our students, which was the intent of the
project, was challenging and was not completely under our control. The project was simply not
ready to roll out in 2022 when it was initially awarded. The proposed methodology never
changed, but the schedule shifted. We received a no-cost extension to accommodate the revised
schedule.

Products and Accomplishments

A publication detailing the project’s goals and achievements is being drafted and will be
submitted by end of 2026. Since this project is somewhat unconventional, we are carefully
selecting a target journal that focuses on education and outreach. Future manuscripts (expect
two) from the ongoing sea turtle microbiome collaboration will be submitted to peer-reviewed
scientific journals (e.g., Estuaries and Coasts, Ecology). The TAMUCC Center for Coastal
Studies released an Instagram post for each of the events funded by this award.
