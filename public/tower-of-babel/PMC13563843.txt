Synthesis and application of exopolysaccharide-selenium nanocomposite produced by Pseudoduganella armeniaca ZMN-3
Authors: Xintong Duan, Xiuwei Hou, Ying Zhai, Ming Ying, Meitong Li, Lei Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study investigated the antioxidant and anticancer activities of selenium nanoparticles stabilized by exopolysaccharides from Pseudoduganella armeniaca ZMN-3 (EPS‑SeNPs). The synthesized EPS‑SeNPs had an average size of 145 nm and were thoroughly characterized. In vitro antioxidant assays revealed that at 4 mg/mL, EPS‑SeNPs scavenged 86.49 ± 0.53% of DPPH radicals, 82.96 ± 0.32% of ABTS radicals, and 36.41 ± 0.77% of hydroxyl radicals. The reducing power assay showed that, at the same concentration, EPS‑SeNPs exhibited an absorbance value of 1.28 ± 0.029, which was higher than that of the other groups. The EPS‑SeNPs composite exhibited pronounced time- and concentration-dependent antiproliferative effects against the two selected cancer cell lines, MCF‑7 and SMMC. After 48 h of treatment at the highest concentration (250 µg/mL), the inhibition rates reached 52.88% for SMMC cells and 54.41% for MCF-7 cells. This effect was significantly stronger than that of EPS or SeNPs alone, indicating a marked enhancement. Cytokine analysis further revealed that even at a low concentration (31.25 µg/mL), a measurable effect was observed. The levels of TNF‑α released by SMMC and MCF‑7 cells reached their highest values at the high concentration of 250 µg/mL, namely 344.79 pg/mL and 132.6 pg/mL, respectively. This promoting effect exceeded that of the positive control (LPS group) and was notably stronger than the effects of SeNPs or EPS alone, with the enhancement being particularly prominent in SMMC cells. Collectively, EPS-SeNPs exhibit both antioxidant and in vitro anti-tumor activity, warranting further investigation.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12896-026-01194-2.Keywords: Extracellular polysaccharides, Selenium nanoparticles, Antioxidant, Anticancer activity

Introduction

Selenium (Se) is an essential nutrient in the diet of humans and animals, and also an indispensable trace element [1]. Research has demonstrated that selenium can scavenge intracellular free radicals and, to some extent, inhibit the formation and development of certain cancer cells, thereby engendering salutary outcomes [2, 3]. Selenium is found in two chemical forms: inorganic selenium compounds and organic selenium compounds [4]. It has been demonstrated that the substance provides essential trace element support to the body by regulating the active sites of various redox enzymes [5]. However, it should be noted that high doses of selenium have been shown to produce toxic effects, and the extent of its toxicity is closely related to the valence state of selenium [6]. Though selenite and selenate are both toxic substances, the former exhibits a higher degree of toxicity relative to the latter. This pattern carries over to selenium speciation: inorganic selenium forms generally come with stronger toxic impacts than organic ones [7]. Consequently, the optimisation of existing forms of selenium has emerged as a prominent area of present investigative priorities.

Zero-valent selenium at the nanoscale has been demonstrated to exhibit reduced toxicity and enhanced bioavailability [8, 9]. Selenium nanoparticles (SeNPs), as a novel form of selenium, it possesses antioxidant activity and anti-cancer efficacy [10]. In addition, it has been reported that SeNPs can inhibit tumor growth by triggering apoptotic cell death [11]. During the early 21st century, Zhang et al. introduced an innovative approach by applying nanotechnology to SeNPs, thus accomplishing the first-ever preparation of bioactive red selenium nanoparticles [8]. The atomic forms of elemental selenium are known to exist in two primary types: one is a biologically inert gray-black form, and the other is a bioactive red form [8]. However, unmodified bare SeNPs have significant stability defects and are prone to aggregation into black elemental selenium, leading to a noticeable reduction in their bioactivity and biocompatibility [12, 13], which severely limits their practical application. The present focus of research is shifting towards the identification of suitable bioactive stabilisers with the aim of enhancing the stability of synthesised SeNPs [3, 14]. It has been established that certain natural biomacromolecules (for example, proteins and polysaccharides) have the capacity to function as surface modifiers for the purpose of stabilising SeNPs [15]. Multiple studies have corroborated the hypothesis that polysaccharides, due to their excellent hydrophilicity, intricate branched structures, and high specific surface area, can serve as effective stabilizers in the preparation of SeNPs [16]. Liu et al. demonstrated that Tremella polysaccharide-coated SeNPs significantly improved the stability of the composite system and effectively inhibited the growth and metastasis of canine mammary tumors without causing significant toxicity to major organs [17]. Similarly, Peng et al. successfully synthesized Bletilla striata polysaccharide‑modified SeNPs (BSPs‑SeNPs) with excellent stability across various pH, temperature, and ionic strength conditions, and demonstrated their potent in vitro antioxidant activity (DPPH, ABTS, hydroxyl radical, and superoxide anion scavenging) as well as in vivo hepatoprotective effects against APAP‑induced acute liver injury, as evidenced by reduced serum ALT/AST levels and restored SOD/GSH activities [18]. A study by Cao et al. reported that Mori Fructus polysaccharide‑stabilized SeNPs (MFP‑SeNPs) exhibited strong scavenging activity against DPPH, ABTS, hydroxyl, and superoxide anion radicals, with IC50 values significantly lower than those of MFP or SeNPs alone, indicating a enhanced antioxidant effect [19]. Sargassum fusiforme polysaccharide‑stabilized SeNPs (SFPS‑Tw‑SeNPs) were prepared by Chen, yielding uniform spherical nanoparticles with an average size of approximately 50 nm. These nanoparticles demonstrated excellent storage stability at 4 °C and potent antiproliferative activity against A549 lung cancer cells (IC50 = 6.14 µg/mL), significantly outperforming bare SeNPs or polysaccharides alone. Mechanistic investigations revealed that they inhibit cancer cell migration, invasion, and vasculogenic mimicry through downregulation of the NF‑κB/MMP‑9/2 signaling pathway and suppression of VEGFR2 expression [13]. However, to the best of our knowledge, no study has yet characterized exopolysaccharides (EPS) or EPS-stabilized SeNPs derived from the genus Pseudoduganella, nor investigated their antioxidant and anticancer activities. Therefore, this study aims to synthesize and characterize EPS-SeNPs using EPS produced by Pseudoduganella armeniaca ZMN-3, and evaluate their antioxidant and anticancer activities in vitro.

The main attention in this work is to determine applicability of EPS on the Pseudoduganella armeniaca ZMN-3 strain in the research and development of SeNPs. In particular, the application of this EPS as a stabiliser allowed to prepare successfully a new EPS-SeNPs composite material. Dynamic light scattering (DLS), transmission electron microscopy (TEM), energy-dispersive spectroscopy (EDS), scanning electron microscopy (SEM), x-ray diffraction (XRD), X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS), ultraviolet-visible spectrophotometry (UV-Vis) and fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy (FT-IR) were used to analyze the obtained EPS-SeNPs. As far as the in vitro analysis of the activity was concerned, it was proposed to determine the scavenging ability of EPS-SeNPs against radical and, thus, get a thorough evaluation of the antioxidant potential of the samples. Also, the research was analytically conducted to assess the antiprolliferative activities of EPS-SeNPs against various cancer cells and compared the activity level of EPS-SeNPs with the feedback of EPS and sodium selenite (Na2SeO3).

Materials and methods

Materials

Sodium selenite was supplied by Shanghai Adamas Reagent Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). Ascorbic acid was obtained from Damao Chemical Reagent Factory (Tianjin, China). Limulus amebocyte lysate (LAL) endotoxin detection kit (Beyotime Biotechnology, China). Lipopolysaccharide (LPS) was obtained from Sigma-Aldrich Co. Ltd. (Beijing, China). 3-(4,5-dimethylthiazol-2-yl)-2,5-diphenyltetrazolium bromide (MTT) and Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium were sourced from Merck Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China). Mouse fibroblasts (L929 cells) were purchased from Beijing Dingguo Changsheng Biotechnology Co., Ltd. Epithelioid human hepatocellular carcinoma cells (SMMC-7721) and human breast cancer cells (MCF-7) were purchased from Shanghai Qida Biotechnology. They tested negative for mycoplasma and were authenticated by STR profiling, matching their respective reference profiles with no cross-contamination.

Preparation of EPS

EPS employed in the present work was isolated from ZMN-3 [20]. The entire extraction and purification process can be summarized as follows: First, the ZMN-3 strain was inoculated into liquid culture medium and incubated for 72 h. After cultivation, the culture broth was diluted by adding two volumes of distilled water, and an appropriate amount of potassium hydroxide was used to adjust the pH to alkaline. To remove cell debris, the treated mixture was centrifuged at 4 °C and 10,000 ×g for 15 min, and the supernatant was collected. Three volumes of anhydrous ethanol were put in the supernatant to precipitate anhydrous polysaccharides. The resultant deposit was dried, re-dissolved in distilled water, and the pH was adjusted to neutral (pH 7.0).

Trichloroacetic acid (TCA) was added to the polysaccharide solution to a final concentration of 10% (w/v), and the mixture was then allowed to stand overnight at 4 °C. This suspension was then centrifuged at 10,000 r/min; 20 min and the supernatant thus collected was mixed with three times of anhydrous ethanol. Centrifugation was also done again after incubating at 4 °C within 24 h. Dialysis of the precipitate was carried out. Lastly, the polysaccharide crude EPS was freeze dried.

Subsequently, following the previously established method [21], the crude EPS was dispersed in deionized water and loaded onto a DEAE 52 column. Elution was performed with distilled water and NaCl gradients (0.2, 0.5, and 1.0 M) at 5 mL/min. Fractions were collected, analyzed by the phenol-sulfuric acid method, pooled, dialyzed (MWCO > 30 kDa), and lyophilized. The resulting EPS was then further purified on a Sephadex G-75 column at 0.2 mL/min, followed by dialysis, concentration, and freeze-drying.

The structural characteristics of EPS were reported in our previous study [21]. Briefly, the EPS is a heteropolysaccharide composed of glucose (Glc), rhamnose (Rha), galactose (Gal), galactosamine (GalN), and guluronic acid (GulA). Its molecular weight was determined to be 364,865 Da. The total sugar content was 86.52%, and the protein content was 2.57% as determined by the Bradford method. The yield of EPS was 2,956 mg/L of culture broth. FTIR analysis confirmed the presence of abundant hydroxyl groups, and UV spectroscopy showed no absorbance at 260–280 nm, indicating the absence of nucleic acids and proteins.

Subsequently, endotoxin levels in extracellular polymeric substance (EPS) samples were determined using a chromogenic Limulus amebocyte lysate (LAL) endotoxin detection kit (Beyotime Biotechnology, China) following the manufacturer’s instructions. As shown in Figure S1, a standard curve for endotoxin quantification was established using endotoxin standards ranging from 0.01 to 0.10 EU/mL. The linear regression equation was 𝑦=6.3888𝑥+0.05 with a correlation coefficient 𝑅2 = 0.996, where 𝑦 is the absorbance at 545 nm and 𝑥 is the endotoxin concentration (EU/mL). The calculated absorbance for the 0.01 EU/mL standard was 0.114. The absorbance of the EPS sample was 0.078, which is lower than that of the 0.01 EU/mL standard, indicating that the endotoxin concentration in EPS was below the detection limit (< 0.01 EU/mL). This result collectively demonstrates that the observed biological activities were not confounded by endotoxin contamination.

Preparation of EPS-SeNPs

To prepare EPS-SeNPs, the present paper used a straightforward redox technique, which can be slightly adapted to the existing techniques [22]. To be more precise, we disintegrated 100 milligrams of EPS in 100 milliliters of distilled water thereby creating the desired EPS mixture. Subsequently, 20 mL of the EPS solution (1 mg/mL) was mixed with varying volumes (0.5–6.8 mL) of a 50 mM sodium selenite solution. Next, different volumes (0.5–6.8 mL) of a 200 mM ascorbic acid solution were slowly added dropwise to the mixture until the solution turned red, yielding a set of EPS-SeNPs solutions with Se/EPS mass ratios of 1:10, 1:5, 1:2, 2:3, 1:1, and 4:3, respectively.

As a control, SeNPs were prepared by replacing the EPS solution with 20 mL of water and adding 3.4 mL of a 200 mM ascorbic acid solution to 3.4 mL of a 50 mM sodium selenite solution. After centrifuging the reactants, combine the collected supernatant with anhydrous ethanol in a 1:3 volume ratio. Harvest the precipitate via centrifugation at 8000 rpm for 10 min, then redisperse the resultant EPS-SeNPs deposit in distilled water. Subsequently, the samples were subjected to freeze-drying at a temperature of -50 °C for a duration of 24–48 h, which resulted in the production of powdered EPS-SeNPs. These samples were then utilized in subsequent experimental procedures. Verify successful EPS-SeNPs synthesis by subjecting the blend to ultraviolet-visible spectrophotometry across a 200–600 nm wavelength spectrum.

EPS-SeNPs characterization

The surface morphology of the samples was examined using a scanning electron microscope (Merlin Compact, Carl Zeiss Group) after gold coating. The microstructure of EPS-SeNPs was analyzed by TEM (JEM-1400Flash, Japan). Particle size distribution of the specimens was gauged through dynamic light scattering (DLS), with additional assessment carried out via a nanoparticle size and zeta potential analyzer for products fabricated at varying Se/EPS ratios.

Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (STA6000-TL9000-Clarus SQ8, PerkinElmer) was employed to scan EPS-SeNPs over a wavenumber range of 4000–400 cm⁻¹ to investigate interaction mechanisms. Crystal structure was characterized by X-ray diffraction (Ultima IV, Rigaku Corporation).

Additionally, X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (ESCALAB250Xi, Thermo Scientific) was used for XPS analysis.

Determination of reducing power

Assay for in vitro anti-cancer activity

Cytotoxicity determination of EPS-SeNPs

The cells were cultured in RPMI-1640 medium (Gibco, USA) supplemented with 10% inactivated fetal bovine serum (Kangyuan, China), 100 U/mL penicillin (Solarbio, Beijing), and 100 µg/mL streptomycin (Solarbio, Beijing). The normal cell (L929), human hepatocellular carcinoma cell (SMMC), and human breast cancer cell (MCF-7) were inoculated into 96-well plate (1 × 10⁵ cells per well) at 37 °C and 5% CO₂ for 24 h. Thereafter, 100 µL of EPS, SeNPs, and EPS-SeNPs liquids at different concentrations (31.25, 62.5, 125 and 250 µg/mL) were added to the respective wells in the experimental groups and none of the cells were treated were used a control group. They were kept in the dark in the 24, 48 and 72 h in the cells respectively. The incubation was subsequently followed by the addition of 20 µL of MTT reagent per well and subsequently the plates incubated further by a duration of 4 h. The medium of culture was then abandoned, and 100 µL of DMSO was added to dissolve the formazan crystals. Absorbance was measured with the help of a microplate reader at the wavelength of 570 nm and the cell viability ratio was calculated using the following formula.

Cell migration assay of EPS-SeNPs

The cell antiproliferative activity assay was conducted with reference to previously reported protocols [27]. L929, MCF-7, and SMMC cells were inoculated into 96-well plates (1 × 10⁵ cells per well) and incubated at 37 °C with 5% CO₂ for 24 h. Following 6 h of fetal bovine serum (FBS) starvation treatment, the cell monolayers were scratched with a sterile pipette tip. Subsequently, 250 µg/mL of the specimen liquid was introduced into the wells. Cellular images were captured using an inverted microscope at 0, 24, 48, and 72 h post-incubation.

Effect of EPS-SeNPs on cytokines

IL‑6 and TNF‑α were selected for analysis because they are key cytokines involved in immune regulation and play important roles in the tumor microenvironment. Although they are often associated with inflammation, accumulating evidence indicates that under specific experimental conditions, these cytokines can contribute to anti‑tumor responses. Therefore, measuring their levels may help to reveal whether EPS‑SeNPs induce such context‑dependent anti‑tumor activities. These two cytokines also serve as widely used indicators for evaluating the immunomodulatory effects of nanomaterials. The concentrations of tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) and interleukin-6 (IL-6) were gauged by means of enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits, respectively.

The morphological observation of cell apoptosis

Cells in the logarithmic growth phase were digested with trypsin (Solarbio, Beijing), counted, and seeded into 12-well plates at a density of 50,000 cells/well. The cells were then cultured at 37 °C in a 5% CO2 incubator for 6–8 h to allow complete adhesion. After the adhesion period, the cells were treated with the test agent, and a blank control group was also established. Following a 48-hour incubation, the old culture medium was removed, and Hoechst 33,342 (Invitrogen) was added to a final concentration of 5 µg/mL. After a 20-minute reaction, Propidium Iodide (PI, Dingguo, Beijing) was added to a final concentration of 5 µg/mL, and the incubation was continued for another 20 min. Finally, the stained cells were observed under a fluorescence microscope to assess their staining status and evaluate apoptosis.

Statistical analysis

All experiments were performed in triplicate, and data are presented as mean ± standard deviation. Statistical significance was evaluated by one–way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey’s post–hoc test using SPSS 26.0, after confirming data normality (Kolmogorov–Smirnov’s test) and homogeneity of variances (Levene’s test). Differences were considered statistically significant at p < 0.05.

Results

Synthesis of EPS-SeNPs

Initially, Na₂SeO₃ was incorporated into the EPS aqueous solution and homogenously blended, after which ascorbic acid was added. As the redox reaction proceeded continuously, a large number of Se atoms gradually aggregated to form Se crystal nuclei, which rapidly grew and eventually formed SeNPs stably encapsulated by EPS. Upon completion of the reaction, the colour of the liquid slowly transitioned from yellow to brick red, which corroborated the generation of amorphous selenium particles [28]. Figure S2 shows a comparison of the appearance of aqueous solutions of SeNPs containing EPS and control solutions without EPS at six Se/EPS ratios (1:10, 1:5, 1:2, 2:3, 1:1, and 4:3). In the control group devoid of EPS, SeNPs aggregated into black clusters, this phenomenon was presumably attributed to their high surface energy [29]. With the gradual elevation of the Se/EPS ratio, the color of the solution gradually shifted from pale yellow to brick red, confirming the successful preparation of SeNPs. It is thus evident that EPS plays a crucial role in the formation process and stability enhancement of SeNPs. When the Se/EPS ratio reached 4:3, the EPS-SeNPs solution began to turn cloudy, whereas the most stable state was observed at a ratio of 2:3.

UV spectroscopic characterization of EPS-SeNPs

The UV-vis absorption plot of EPS-SeNPs when the wavelength ranges between 200 and 600 nm are illustrated in Fig. 1. In the conditions of different ratios EPS-SeNPs has characteristic absorption peak of 267 nm that is related to the surface plasmon resonance effect of SeNPs [30]. The location of the absorption peak does not exhibit any important changes among the ratios, which confirms the findings of earlier studies that reveal the formation of EPS-SeNPs was successfully achieved [31]. Interestingly, with a consistent change in the ratio of 1: 10 to 4: 3 (w/ w), a consistent increase in the intensity of the maximum absorption peak of EPS-SeNPs will occur. Upon adding SeO32− to the system, ascorbic acid replaces selenium atoms so that eventually selenium atoms generated will eventually build to create crystal nuclei. These nuclei also develop to SeNPs. As the Se/EPS ratio gradually increased, the absorption peak intensity reached its maximum at a Se/EPS ratio of 2:3; when the ratio was increased to 4:3, the peak intensity decreased. The higher the level of growth of the Se/EPS ratio, the greater the systemic concentration of SeO32−, and the more the SeNPs are produced, and the stronger the resulting increase in absorption signal intensity. Nevertheless, in cases where the Se/EPS ratio remains in the increasing manner (> 2:3), the absorption peak intensity starts to decrease. The presence of excess selenite ions (SeO₃²⁻) in the redox system resulted in the observation of abundant selenium nanoparticles (SeNPs) in the solution [32]. Polysaccharides, the stabilisers used in this experiment, were observed to be not sufficient enough to stabilise and in dispersing the ever-growing nanoparticles extensively. It is known that this phenomenon leads to the creation of the larger size SeNPs and their aggregates. Thus, 2:3 of Se/EPS ratio can be considered the most effective to stabilise and disperse SeNPs when they are in the presence of EPS.

Assessment of particle size and zeta potential in EPS-SeNPs dispersions

It is generally recognized that smaller particle sizes can interact more effectively with microorganisms [33]; however, the resulting antioxidant effects are not solely determined by particle size and may depend on additional factors such as surface chemistry. Dynamic light scattering (DLS) represents a widely applied technique for determining the particle size and size distribution of nanoscale materials dispersed in liquid media. As observed by TEM, EPS-SeNPs exhibit a spherical structure, allowing their particle size to be determined by the equivalent spherical diameter measured via DLS. As shown in Fig. 2(A), the size distribution profiles of EPS-SeNPs undergo noticeable changes with variations in the Se/EPS ratio. The sample prepared at a Se/EPS ratio of 2:3 exhibited a DLS-measured hydrodynamic diameter of approximately 145 nm. When compared to the particle size determined by TEM as shown in Fig. 4, the data of DLS are higher. This difference can be explained by the fact that DLS gains the hydrodynamic diameter of the dispersed particles, and TEM identifies the actual size of the particles under consideration, when the specimens are dried [29].

The potential of Zeta is a valuable tool in the assessment of the actual state and stability of nanoparticles in solution [34]. In cases where the absolute zeta potential value is substantial (generally greater than ± 30 mV), nanoparticles can achieve good dispersion and remain stable through electrostatic repulsion. The measurement of zeta potential is a method of evaluating the dispersion stability of nanoparticles in a diverse range of solvents. Figure 2(B) shows the surface zeta potential of EPS-SeNPs exhibits a variation in response to varying ratios. The sample at a ratio of 2:3 showing the most negative potential value (-44 mV), indicating higher stability of EPS/SeNPs in aqueous media at this ratio.

Polydispersity index (PDI) is a very important parameter used to estimate the homogeneity of particle sizes, and determine the dispersion performance of nanomaterials. Taking PDI value as approaching 0, nanomaterials are generally regarded as a monodisperse system and the lower the PDI value, the better the dispersion of particles in the solution [35]. Figure 2(C) illustrates the PDI measurement at a ratio of 2:3, where EPS-SeNPs exhibited acceptable dispersion. At this ratio, the PDI value was 0.216, indicating moderate dispersion and optimal dispersive ability among the tested conditions.

They are unambiguously presented in SEM and TEM analysis to demonstrate the morphological features and size distribution of the formed EPS-SeNPs. Figure 3 compares the images of SEM of SeNPs prepared with 6 different ratios of Se/EPS and the control SENPs. Figure 3 (C-H) also indicates that as the ratio of Se/EPS increases, gradually the size of the selenium particles is increased. Besides, there is great agglomeration when this ratio is above that of 2:3. Generally, the preparation ratio of 2:3 is the best ratios that showed the best dispersion state. The results of the scanning electron microscopy are similar to ultraviolet analysis results. Based on the experimental results mentioned above, it is suggested that the EPS-SeNPs that are prepared at the ratio of 2:3 should be chosen to be further implemented. In contrast, SeNPs prepared without EPS (Fig. 3B) formed large agglomerates, indicating poor dispersion. Thus, EPS is one of the most powerful individual contributors of the surface modification and stabilization of SeNPs.

Interaction mechanisms of EPS-SeNPs

Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FT-IR) can clearly display characteristic absorption peaks of different functional groups, making it an effective technique for identifying the functional group composition of substances. Figure 6(A) presents the infrared spectra of SeNPs, pristine EPS, and EPS-SeNPs. The spectrum of EPS-SeNPs displays the majority of characteristic absorption peaks belonging to EPS [36], which implies that the nanocomposite retains the functional group architecture of EPS without the formation of new covalent linkages [37]. The broad absorption band at 3298 cm⁻¹ arises from the stretching vibration of O–H bonds, and the distinct absorption peak at 2979 cm⁻¹ is assigned to the stretching vibration of C–H single bonds—a typical diagnostic feature of polysaccharide-based EPS [38]. The stretching vibration of C-O bonds corresponds to the absorption feature observed at 1395 cm⁻¹, typically associated with ether or ester functional groups in polysaccharide structures [39]. The absorption peaks at 1250 cm⁻¹ and 1065 cm⁻¹ reflect the bending vibration of O-H deformation. The absorption at 900 cm⁻¹ originates from the C-H bending vibration of β-anomeric isomers in pyran-type glycosides [40]. Comparison with the EPS spectrum reveals that the absorption peak of C-O-H in EPS-SeNPs shifts from 3298 cm⁻¹ to 3277 cm⁻¹, signifying that hydrogen bonding interactions occur between the hydroxyl groups of EPS and selenium atoms in SeNPs [41]. By driving the formation of stable spherical architectures and suppressing nanoparticle aggregation, such interactions are capable of improving the overall stability of the system [42]. To summarize, the superior stability and dispersive properties of EPS-SeNPs stem predominantly from the strong interfacial interactions between EPS-derived hydroxyl groups and selenium atoms in SeNPs.

FT-IR spectra and XRD patterns of EPS, SeNPs, and EPS-SeNPs. A: FT-IR spectra of EPS, SeNPs and EPS-SeNPs powders, B: XRD spectra of EPS, SeNPs and EPS-SeNPs powders

Figure 6(B) shows the XRD patterns of EPS, SeNPs, and EPS-SeNPs. Two broad peaks are observed in the XRD spectrum of SeNPs across the 2θ intervals of 20–40° and 40–60°, a feature that signifies the amorphous nature of these nanoparticles. The present result aligns well with the observations documented by Chen [43]. The absence of sharp characteristic peaks in the XRD profiles of EPS and EPS-SeNPs verifies that both materials possess non-crystalline properties. When contrasted against the XRD profiles of EPS and SeNPs, both the peak intensity levels of EPS-SeNPs underwent marked changes, indicating the formation of the EPS-SeNPs composite structure. These results demonstrate that the encapsulation of SeNPs by EPS macromolecules promotes the formation of amorphous SeNPs [44].

XPS analysis of EPS-SeNPs

Comparison of the C 1s core-level spectra (Fig. 7B, E) reveals that EPS–SeNPs retain nearly identical binding energy profiles to pure EPS. When the O1s spectra of EPS and EPS-SeNPs are compared (Fig. 7C, F), it can be observed that the binding energy rises from 531.9 eV in EPS to 532.6 eV in EPS-SeNPs. The observed shift can be attributed to strong interfacial interactions and the establishment of C–O/Se bonds between selenium nanoparticles and the EPS matrix [45]. As shown in Fig. 7D, compared to EPS, EPS-SeNPs exhibit distinct Se 3d characteristic peaks at 55.11 eV (Se 3d5/2) and 55.99 eV (Se 3d3/2), in addition to carbon and oxygen signals.

Evaluation of antioxidant capacity

Assessment of DPPH scavenging potential

DPPH in alcohol exhibits a distinct purple hue with prominent absorption peaks at 517 nm. Numerous studies have confirmed that antioxidants can reduce the absorption intensity of DPPH at this specific wavelength, thereby causing a concomitant colour change [46]. Figure 8(A) shows the scavenging effects of EPS, Na₂SeO₃, and EPS-SeNPs at a Se/EPS ratio of 2:3 on DPPH free radicals. Compared to pure EPS, the DPPH scavenging ability of EPS-SeNPs at this ratio is significantly enhanced. The experimental results show that the scavenging ability increases with the concentration of EPS-SeNPs. At 4 mg/mL, the maximum scavenging rate of EPS-SeNPs against DPPH reaches 86.49 ± 0.53%.

Antioxidant activity of EPS-SeNPs. A: Scavenging activity against DPPH free radicals, B: Scavenging activity against ABTS free radicals, C: Scavenging activity against hydroxyl radicals, D: Total reducing capacity

Assessment of ABTS cation radical scavenging efficiency

In Fig. 8(B), the experimental results indicate that the ABTS scavenging rates of all tested samples increase with rising concentration. However, when the concentration reaches 4 mg/mL, the scavenging rate increases significantly to 82.96 ± 0.32%.

Determination of hydroxyl radical scavenging ability

Hydroxyl radicals, as highly reactive free radicals, can cause significant damage to biomacromolecules. As displayed in Fig. 8(C), the outcomes of the scavenging assay revealed that over the 1–4 mg/mL concentration interval, the peak scavenging rate of EPS-SeNPs reached (36.41 ± 0.77)%, which was 22.80% and 6.65% higher than that of sodium selenite and EPS, respectively.

Determination of reducing power

A compound’s reducing ability may be regarded as a marker of its antioxidative properties, with elevated absorbance readings typically signifying more potent reducing capacity. Figure 8(D) presents the results of the reducing capacity measurements for Na₂SeO₃, EPS, and EPS-SeNPs. Experimental data indicated that with increasing concentrations, the absorbance of all three substances exhibits a gradual upward trend. At the point where the EPS-SeNPs concentration reaches 4 mg/mL, the maximum absorbance value is 1.28 ± 0.029.

Assay for anticancer activity in vitro

Evaluation of EPS-SeNPs cytotoxic effects

The effects of different concentrations of EPS, SeNPs, and EPS-SeNPs on the viability of MCF-7, SMMC, and L929 cells were assessed using the MTT assay. The results of the experiments are depicted in Fig. 9. The EPS-SeNPs composite demonstrated pronounced time and concentration-dependent antiproliferative effects against the two selected cancer cell lines, namely MCF-7 and SMMC. After 48 h of treatment at the highest concentration (250 µg/mL), the inhibition rates reached 52.88% for SMMC cells and 54.41% for MCF-7 cells. Its effect was significantly superior to that of EPS or SeNPs alone, demonstrating a notable enhanced effect.

Antiproliferative activity of EPS-SeNPs. A: Cytotoxicity to MCF-7 cells after 24 h, B: Cytotoxicity to SMMC cells after 24 h, C: Cytotoxicity to L929 cells after 24 h, D: Cytotoxicity to MCF-7 cells after 48 h, E: Cytotoxicity to SMMC cells after 48 h, F: Cytotoxicity to L929 cells after 48 h

Of special note is the fact that this composite has an outstanding selective toxicity property, in which given the same treatment, its toxicity is dramatically lower in normal L929 cells as compared to all other cancer cell lines. After 24 h of treatment it was observed that L929 cells had great viabilities even at high concentrations of the compound thus indicating that there is a selective targeting mechanism that provides a great favorable activity of the compound in developing the anticancer drug.

The testing of individual components revealed that during a 24-hour treatment period, SeNPs exhibited weak anticancer activity. This was likely due to their tendency to aggregate into larger particles, which likely hindered effective cellular uptake via endocytosis [47]. Consistent with our observations, Albanese and Chan found that aggregated nanoparticles exhibited reduced cellular uptake compared to their monodisperse counterparts, which was attributed to less efficient receptor-mediated endocytosis [48]. Conversely, EPS exhibited minimal toxicity. The combination of EPS and SeNPs resulted in a dual-faceted effect. Firstly, EPS functioned as a safe carrier, thereby reducing the potential harm of SeNPs to normal tissues. Secondly, the two substances acted in an enhanced manner, thereby enhancing their killing effect on cancer cells. Taken together, these results verify that EPS-SeNPs possess substantial potential as a highly efficient and low-toxicity anticancer nanomaterial. In comparison with the other two cell lines, EPS-SeNPs exhibited a more pronounced antiproliferative effect on SMMC cells.

Cell migration assay of EPS-SeNPs

Wound healing assay being a well-established research method in vitro in the field of cell biology is mostly used in the assessment of the cell migration capability. To evaluate the time fixation of EPS, SeNPs, and EPS-SeNPs on MCF-7, SMMC, and L929 cell massage, the scratch assay results presented in Fig. 10 were used to assess the degree of EPS, SeNPs, and EPS-SeNPs at time points of 0, 24, 48, and 72 h through the cell migration degree of the human cell. The research shows that various treatment forms have varying regulatory actions to the migration capacity of normal and tumour cells. The three treatment groups used in the normal L929 cell line showed a progressive healing of the wound throughout the time in which it was cultivated. After 72 h of cultivation, SeNPs were found to be able to inhibit the migratory capacity of L929 cells and the rate of wound healing of L929 cells was 40.52%. Conversely, EPS treatment showed positive rate of promoting, where the rate of healing was 73.57%. It is noteworthy that the healing rate of the EPS-SeNPs composite treatment was 63.57%.

Wound healing activity of EPS – SeNPs. A: Wound healing assay of MCF-7 after 24, 48, and 72 h of treatment, B: Wound healing assay of SMMC after 24, 48, and 72 h of treatment, C: Wound healing assay of L929 after 24, 48, and 72 h of treatment

In the MCF-7 and SMMC tumor cell lines, the scratches in the control group cells closed almost completely within 72 h, whereas the closure of scratches in cells treated with EPS-SeNPs was significantly inhibited. After 72 h of treatment, the healing rate of MCF-7 cells decreased to 35.08%, and that of SMMC cells decreased to 29.77%, both significantly lower than those of the groups treated with EPS or SeNPs alone. In summary, the EPS-SeNPs composite had a relatively mild impact on the migration of normal L929 cells, while demonstrating stronger inhibitory effects on the migration of MCF-7 and SMMC tumor cells.

Effect of EPS-SeNPs on cytokines

As demonstrated in Fig. 11, exposure to the experimental group resulted in a dose-dependent increase in TNF-α and IL-6 levels in SMMC and MCF-7 cells. With regard to the secretion of TNF-α, EPS-SeNPs were observed to have a stimulatory effect as early as 24 h, which was significantly amplified by 48 h, as illustrated in Figs. 11A(b) and 11B(b). Despite the low amount of concentration used (31.25 µg/mL), a certain effect could be identified. The TNF-α levels released by the SMMC and MCF-7 cells were highest at 344.79 pg/mL and 132.6 pg/mL respectively at the high concentration of 250 µg/mL. This promoting effect was seen to be greater than the positive control of LPS group and was found to be particularly better than the influence of SeNPs or EPS alone with the effect of SMMC cells, being particularly notable.

As demonstrated in Figs. 11A(c) and 11B(c), following a 24-hour treatment period with EPS-SeNPs at 250 µg/mL, the release of IL-6 from SMMC and MCF-7 cells was recorded at 7.951 pg/mL and 11.91 pg/mL, respectively. In comparison, the LPS control groups exhibited releases of 10.1 pg/mL and 11.81 pg/mL. Following a 48-hour treatment period, the release of IL-6 from the two cell types increased further, reaching 16.61 pg/mL (SMMC) and 23.62 pg/mL (MCF-7) (Figs. 11A(d) and 11B(d)). At this stage, the LPS control groups released 16.52 pg/mL and 40.84 pg/mL, respectively. The results indicate that EPS-SeNPs achieved a promoting effect on IL-6 in SMMC cells at 48 h and in MCF-7 cells at 24 h, comparable to the positive control counterpart. In conclusion, our treatment elevated TNF-α and IL-6 levels. Hoechst 33,342/PI dual staining further confirmed the occurrence of apoptosis, as it directly visualized increased cell death following treatment (Figure S3).

Discussion

In this study, EPS-SeNPs were successfully synthesized using exopolysaccharides (EPS) from Pseudoduganella armeniaca ZMN-3 as a stabilizing agent. UV-Vis spectroscopy revealed a characteristic shift in the absorption peak compared to bare SeNPs, confirming the formation of EPS-SeNPs.

Particle size analysis showed that the minimum size (145 nm) was achieved at a Se/EPS ratio of 2:3. Liu et al. [17] used polysaccharides isolated from Tremella fuciformis to disperse SeNPs, obtaining a size of 184.1 nm, which is larger than our EPS-SeNPs. Yu et al. [49] extracted SBPPs (S. brachyanthera pulp polysaccharides) and achieved a minimum particle size of 147.13 nm at 4 mg/mL SBPPs and 4 mM Na2SeO3, close to our 145 nm. At an EPS/SeNPs ratio of 2:3, our EPS-SeNPs reached a maximum negative zeta potential of -44 mV. Zhao et al. [50] employed XG-3 (isolated and purified from Weissella confusa XG-3) to enhance SeNP stability, reporting a zeta potential of -25.2 mV after SGF digestion, which is lower than that of our EPS-SeNPs.

The experimental results show that the SEM imaging effect is the best when the EPS/SeNPs ratio is 2:3, while severe aggregation occurs in SeNPs without EPS addition. Based on the conclusions of the previous research: The dispersion of SeNPs in the study by Chen et al. [13] was based on the use of Sargassum fusiforme polysaccharide (SFPS). In their study, when characterized using SEM, SeNPs exhibited strong aggregation properties and appearance of bulky aggregates of particles. On the other hand, homogenous SeNPs were formed by the introduction of SFPS, and this fact justifies the effectiveness of the synthesis of this type of hybrid nanomaterial. Besides, in Chen et al. [43], chitosan is conjugated with selenium nanoparticles. These experiments proved that SeNPs which were not modified with CS displayed excessive aggregation which led to the appearance of large aggregates of particles. Introduction of CS led to the preparation of uniformly dispersed, and with high level of dispersion, SeCS, which is another confirmation of the production of SeCS. In a manner consistent with the findings of preceding studies, Qi et al. [51] synthesized selenium nanoparticles modified with Ophiocordyceps gracilis polysaccharide (GSP-1a-SeNPs). The EDS analysis results showed that the proportions of C, O, and Se were 40.5%, 32.1%, and 27.4%, respectively, which are similar to the EDS elemental ratios obtained in this study.

In this study, compared with EPS, EPS-SeNPs exhibit distinct Se 3d peaks at 55.11 eV (Se 3d5/2) and 55.99 eV (Se 3d3/2), along with carbon and oxygen signals. This result is similar to the Se 3d peak positions (54.86 eV and 55.58 eV) reported by Shan et al. [45] for plant polysaccharide-based selenium nanoparticles (SBP-Se), with both falling within the characteristic binding energy range of elemental selenium (Se⁰). In their analysis, Štefanková et al. [52] observed in the reaction system, SeO32− is in the system reduced by ascorbic acid to obtain Se atoms, which in turn accumulate to form Se nuclei and subsequently develop to form SeNPs. EPS, therefore, entraps the SeNPs by C–O/Se chemical bonds or a strong hydroxyl moester interaction that ultimately forms the composite structure of the EPS-SeNPs [53].

Scavenging assays show that at 4 mg/mL EPS-SeNPs, the DPPH, ABTS, and hydroxyl radical scavenging rates are 86.49 ± 0.53%, 82.96 ± 0.32%, and 36.41 ± 0.77%, respectively. Although this value is lower than the 98% DPPH free radical clearance rate of ascorbic acid at an equivalent concentration, it represents a 14.19% increase compared to the DPPH scavenging rate (72.3% at 4 mg/mL) of EPS-SeNPs synthesized from EPS derived from mung beans in previous studies [50]. Wang et al. [54] synthesized Se-PEP from Pleurotus eryngii, achieving DPPH and ABTS scavenging rates of 64.7% and 75% at 4 mg/mL, which are significantly lower than those in this study. However, the hydroxyl radical scavenging rate was 40%, which is comparable to that measured in this study.

EPS-SeNPs showed superior concentration- and time-dependent cytotoxicity against MCF-7 and SMMC cells compared to EPS or SeNPs alone, with maximum inhibition rates of 52.88% and 54.41% at 250 µg/mL after 48 h. This finding is consistent with what other research studies have found out. Liu et al. [17] made SeNPs-TP with Tremella polysaccharides. The cell experiments revealed that selenium nanoparticles (SeNPs) and SeNPs-TP all had dose-dependent effects on the CMT-7364 tumour cells whereby they inhibited cell proliferation. At a concentration of 40 µg/mL, the cell viability rates of CMT-7364 tumour cells treated with SeNPs and SeNPs-TP were 49.01 ± 5.92% and 58.40 ± 6.78%, respectively. As reported in the work of Chen et al. [13], the successful synthesis of spherical selenium nanoparticles was achieved. Within the concentration range of 4–250 µg/mL, the proliferation of A549 cells was markedly suppressed by SFPS-Tw-SeNPs, whereas SeNPs and SFPS showed negligible effects on cancer cell activity.

The scratch assay results showed that EPS-SeNPs exhibited a stronger inhibitory effect on the migration of MCF-7 and SMMC tumor cells than on normal L929 cells, with healing rates of 35.08% and 29.77%, respectively, after 72 h, compared to 63.57% for L929 cells. In the study conducted by Liu et al. [17], SeNPs-TP were prepared using Tremella polysaccharides. These nanoparticles were found to inhibit tumour cell migration in a concentration-dependent manner. Following a 24-hour treatment period, the rate of inhibition on CMT-7364 cell wound healing was recorded as 37.7% ± 0.07%. In the study by Chen et al. [13], Sargassum fusiforme polysaccharide was utilised for the dispersion and preparation of spherical SFPS-Tw-SeNPs, which exhibited effective inhibitory properties on the migration of A549 cells, as evidenced by a wound healing rate reduced to 9.7 ± 5.4%. This effect was superior to that of SeNPs alone (19.34 ± 1.3%), while the polysaccharide alone showed no such activity.

As demonstrated in Fig. 11, EPS-SeNPs significantly enhanced TNF-α and IL-6 release in both cancer cell lines, with maximum TNF-α levels reaching 344.79 pg/mL (SMMC) and 132.6 pg/mL (MCF-7) at 250 µg/mL, and IL-6 secretion increasing further at 48 h. Similar findings have been reported in other studies. Lan et al. [55] synthesized FCP-SeNPs using an acidic polysaccharide from Cornus officinalis, which promoted TNF-α production up to approximately 210 pg/mL. Luo et al. [56] prepared Se-HEP-PLGA nanoparticles that induced approximately 1500 pg/mL of TNF-α and 10 pg/mL of IL-6 at 1.95 µg/mL, effectively activating macrophages.

It is important to note, however, that TNF-α and IL-6 are pleiotropic cytokines with complex, context‑dependent roles. While they are generally considered pro‑inflammatory mediators, accumulating evidence has demonstrated that under specific conditions, these cytokines can also exert anti‑tumorigenic effects. For instance, Pencik et al. [57] reported that in a PTEN‑deficient prostate cancer model, IL‑6–STAT3 signaling protects against tumor progression by maintaining the ARF–MDM2–p53 senescence axis. Orange et al. [58] showed that acute exercise‑induced IL‑6 reduces colon cancer cell proliferation by decreasing γ‑H2AX expression, indicating enhanced DNA damage repair. Mise‑Omata et al. [59] demonstrated that in the absence of SOCS3 in T cells, IL‑6 acquires anti‑tumor functionality by promoting effector T cell differentiation. Additionally, Dong et al. [60] reported that 100 ng/mL TNF‑α markedly suppressed A549 and H23 cell proliferation after 24 h.

Nevertheless, both TNF-α and IL-6 are pleiotropic cytokines whose biological effects are highly complex and vary according to the cellular environment and the duration of exposure. These cytokines can influence multiple processes, including cell survival, angiogenesis, and immune regulation. Further investigations are needed to clarify their precise role in the anti-tumor activity of EPS-SeNPs.

Study limitation

This study did not include direct cellular uptake experiments to compare the internalization of EPS-SeNPs versus bare SeNPs. Consequently, although we hypothesize that enhanced endocytosis contributes to the observed bioactivity based on literature support, the mechanism remains speculative. Future quantitative uptake studies are needed to verify this hypothesis. As this study is based solely on in vitro experiments, no conclusions can be drawn regarding the in vivo efficacy, pharmacokinetics, or safety of EPS-SeNPs. Future studies, including animal models and toxicological assessments, are necessary to evaluate the potential of EPS-SeNPs for any practical applications.

Conclusion

In this study, extracellular polysaccharides (EPS) isolated from the novel bacterial strain Pseudoduganella armeniaca ZMN-3 were employed to stabilise SeNPs. Comparative analyses revealed that the as-synthesized EPS-SeNPs composite displayed distinct advantages over bare SeNPs (prepared without EPS incorporation): the nanoparticles exhibited homogeneous dispersion in aqueous solution, a well-defined spherical morphology, and a uniform average particle diameter of approximately 145 nm. Furthermore, with no obvious aggregation or precipitation observed over an extended period, in contrast to the rapid agglomeration of unmodified SeNPs. To comprehensively elucidate the structural and physicochemical properties of the fabricated nanocomposite, a suite of complementary characterization techniques was implemented.

A series of in vitro biological assays were performed to test the possibility of the practical application of the EPS-SeNPs nanocomposite. Depending on the measurements of antioxidant activity, it was revealed that the EPS-SeNPs composite exhibited a strong free radical scavenging ability. It is worth noting that the scavenging activity of the composite was much higher compared to the scavenging activity of EPS and bare SeNPs.

In vitro anti-tumor tests were also conducted as investigations into the inhibitory capacity of EPS-SeNPs against cancer cell development. The cell scratch healing test further evidenced that, EPS-SeNPs had a significant suppressive effect on the migration ability of MCF-7 and SMMC cells. The quantitative analysis demonstrated that the rate of migration inhibition in the composite was significantly greater than using the individual components (EPS and bare SeNPs) separately, which demonstrated the increased anti-migratory activity of the nanocomposite. In addition, this suppressive effect was observed to be dose-dependent: the further the concentration increased, the stronger was the inhibition effect on the motility and the invasions of cancer cells. This observation suggests that the incorporation of EPS enhances the anti-tumor activity of SeNPs.

Parallel to this, cytokine detection analyses were performed in order to examine the molecular pathophysiology behind the anti-tumor effects of EPS-SeNPs. These findings showed that EPS-SeNPs treatment caused the regulation of major pro-inflammatory cytokines, namely, it caused a marked increase in IL-6 and TNF-alpha. These cytokines have been known to play important functions in the regulation of cancer cell apoptosis and proliferation and their up-insurgence could be involved in the effect that is seen in inhibiting cancer cell growth and stimulating programmed cell death.

In conclusion, the incorporation of EPS effectively improves the biocompatibility of selenium nanoparticles (EPS-SeNPs), while also reducing the potential cytotoxicity associated with bare SeNPs. Under the tested in vitro conditions, EPS-SeNPs demonstrate notable antioxidant activity. Furthermore, they exhibit a clear inhibitory effect on the viability of MCF-7 and SMMC cells, indicating that EPS enhances the anti-tumor activity of SeNPs. Together, these findings suggest that EPS-SeNPs possess improved physicochemical properties and biological activities compared to bare SeNPs.
