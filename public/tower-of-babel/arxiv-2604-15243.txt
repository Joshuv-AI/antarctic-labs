Classifying spaces for families of virtually abelian subgroups of surface braid groups
Ramón Flores, Juan González-Meneses, Porfirio L. León Álvarez

Abstract
. Given a group G G and an integer n ≥ 0 n\geq 0 , let ℱ n \mathcal{F}_{n} denote the family of all virtually abelian subgroups of G G of rank at most n n . In this article, we show that for each n ≥ 1 n\geq 1 , the minimal dimension of a model for the classifying space E ℱ n ​ G E_{\mathcal{F}_{n}}G for the pure braid group of a surface of non-negative Euler characteristic with at least one boundary component or one puncture is equal to the virtual cohomological dimension of G G plus n n . We prove an analogous result for the full braid group of the sphere. As an application, we compute the minimal dimension of a model for the classifying space associated to the family of amenable subgroups of pure surface braid groups.

1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a group. A collection
{\mathcal{F}}
of subgroups of
G
is called a
family
if it is non-empty and closed under conjugation and taking subgroups. For a given family
{\mathcal{F}}
of subgroups of
G
, a
G
-CW-complex
X
is a
model for the classifying space

E_{{\mathcal{F}}}G
if all its isotropy groups belong to
{\mathcal{F}}
, and for any
G
-CW-complex
Y
whose isotropy groups are also in
{\mathcal{F}}
, there exists a unique
G
-map
Y\to X
, up to
G
-homotopy. It can be shown that a model for the classifying space
E_{{\mathcal{F}}}G
always exists and is unique up to
G
-homotopy equivalence. The
{\mathcal{F}}
-geometric dimension
of
G
is defined as

\gd_{{\mathcal{F}}}(G)=\min\{n\in\mathbb{N}\mid\text{there exists a model for }E_{{\mathcal{F}}}G\text{ of dimension }n\}.

The
{\mathcal{F}}
-geometric dimension has an algebraic counterpart, the
{\mathcal{F}}
-cohomological dimension

\cd_{{\mathcal{F}}}(G)
, which can be defined using Bredon cohomology; see for example
[
32
]
. The
{\mathcal{F}}
-geometric and
{\mathcal{F}}
-cohomological dimensions are related by the following inequality (see
[
26
, Theorem 0.1]
):

\cd_{{\mathcal{F}}}(G)\leq\gd_{{\mathcal{F}}}(G)\leq\max\{\cd_{{\mathcal{F}}}(G),3\}.

In particular, if
\cd_{{\mathcal{F}}}(G)\geq 3
, then
\cd_{{\mathcal{F}}}(G)=\gd_{{\mathcal{F}}}(G)
. However, this equality does not always hold. For instance, in the case of the family
{\mathcal{F}}_{0}
of finite subgroups, it was shown in
[
4
]
that there exists a right-angled Coxeter group
W
such that
\cd_{{\mathcal{F}}_{0}}(W)=2
and
\gd_{{\mathcal{F}}_{0}}(W)=3
. For other examples see
[
36
]
.

Let
n\geq 0
be an integer. A group is said to be
virtually
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
if it contains a finite-index subgroup isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. We define the family

{\mathcal{F}}_{n}=\{H\leq G\mid H\text{ is virtually }\mathbb{Z}^{r}\text{ for some }0\leq r\leq n\}.

The classifying spaces
E_{{\mathcal{F}}_{0}}G
and
E_{{\mathcal{F}}_{1}}G
are of particular interest because they appear explicitly in the formulation of the Baum-Connes and Farrell-Jones isomorphism conjectures, respectively. For more details, see
[
27
]
.

The classifying spaces
E_{{\mathcal{F}}_{n}}G
have been extensively studied in recent years. In 2018, Nucinkis and collaborators in
[
8
]
proved that for a free abelian group
G
of rank
n
, the inequality
\gd_{{\mathcal{F}}_{k}}(G)\leq n+k
holds; later, in
[
23
]
, was proven that this upper bound is sharp.
In 2021, Tomasz in
[
35
]
studied CAT(0)-groups. He shows that for a group
G
acting on a CAT(0) space of topological dimension
n
, and under the assumption that
G
satisfies condition C, the inequality

\cd_{{\mathcal{F}}_{r}}(G)\leq n+r+1

holds for all
0\leq r\leq n
.

In 2023, the following was proven in
[
17
]
. Denote the mapping class group by
\mcg(S)
of a connected, orientable, compact surface
S
, possibly with a finite number of punctures. If
S
has negative Euler characteristic, then for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the geometric dimension satisfies

\gd_{{\mathcal{F}}_{n}}(\mcg(S))\leq\cd(\mcg(S))+n.

In this article, we show that for the pure braid group
G
of a surface, the minimal dimension of a model for
E_{\mathcal{F}_{n}}G
is equal to
\mathrm{cd}(G)+n
. In what follows, we state this result precisely.

Virtually abelian dimension of finite-type surface braid groups

Let
\Sigma
be a connected surface, possibly of infinite type (i.e., with non-finitely generated fundamental group), possibly non-orientable, and possibly with punctures or boundary.
Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
The
configuration space of
n
points on
\Sigma
, denoted
F_{n}(\Sigma)
, is defined as

F_{n}(\Sigma)=\{(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})\in\Sigma^{n}\mid x_{i}\neq x_{j}\text{ for }i\neq j\}.

Let
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
denote the symmetric group on
n
letters. There is a natural free action of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
on
F_{n}(\Sigma)
by permuting the coordinates.

The
pure braid group
on
\Sigma
with
n
strands, denoted
\pb_{n}(\Sigma)
, is defined as the fundamental group of the configuration space
F_{n}(\Sigma)
. The
braid group
on
\Sigma
with
n
strands, denoted
B_{n}(\Sigma)
, is the fundamental group of the quotient space
F_{n}(\Sigma)/\mathfrak{S}_{n}
. The following short exact sequence relates these two groups:

1\to\pb_{n}(\Sigma)\to B_{n}(\Sigma)\to\mathfrak{S}_{n}\to 1.

Let
\Sigma
be a surface with boundary
\partial\Sigma
, then
B_{n}(\Sigma)
is isomorphic to
B_{n}(\Sigma-\partial\Sigma)
, see for example
[
15
, Remarks 8]
.
It has been established that normally poly-free groups satisfy the Farrell-Jones conjecture
[
5
, Theorem A]
, see also
[
1
]
,
[
2
, Theorem 1.1]
, and
[
19
, Theorem 2.3.7]
. In particular, the pure braid group of a surface with non-empty boundary is known to be normally poly-free
[
1
]
. Moreover, in the case of surfaces of infinite type, the pure braid group is also normally poly-free
[
22
, Theorem 1.5(b)]
, and then these groups also satisfy the Farrell-Jones conjecture.

One of the main results of this paper is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Sigma
be a connected surface of finite type, possibly non-orientable and possibly with punctures or boundary components, with non-positive Euler characteristic and at least one puncture or one boundary component. Then for all integers
r\geq 0

\gd_{{\mathcal{F}}_{r}}(\pb_{n}(\Sigma))=\cd(\pb_{n}(\Sigma))+r.

If, on the other hand,
\Sigma
is closed and has non-positive Euler characteristic, then the following inequalities hold:

\cd(\pb_{n}(\Sigma))+r-1\leq\gd_{{\mathcal{F}}_{r}}(\pb_{n}(\Sigma))\leq\cd(\pb_{n}(\Sigma))+r

for all integers
r\geq 0
.

For the full braid group of the sphere, we obtain an analogous result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Sigma
be the sphere. For all integers
r\geq 0

\gd_{{\mathcal{F}}_{r}}(B_{n}(\Sigma))=\vcd(B_{n}(\Sigma))+r.

Remark 1
.

When
\chi(\Sigma)>0
, the
\mathcal{F}_{n}
-dimension is known only in certain cases. In particular, for the braid group on the disk it was computed in
[
13
]
and
[
23
, Theorem 1.3]
. The computation of the
\mathcal{F}_{n}
-dimension in
Theorem

1.1
is explicit since the cohomological dimension of surface braid groups has already been established in the literature; see, for example,
[
22
, Theorem 1.1]
and the references therein.

To prove
Theorem

1.1
, we obtain the upper bound by applying the Lück–Weiermann construction and the Fadell–Neuwirth fibration to analyze the geometric dimension for proper actions of the Weyl groups associated to abelian subgroups.

For the lower bound, we show that the surface braid group admits an abelian subgroup of rank equal to its cohomological dimension. This fact may be of independent interest.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Sigma
be a connected surface of finite type, possibly non-orientable, with a non-positive Euler characteristic. Then the pure braid group
\pb_{n}(\Sigma)
contains a free abelian subgroup of rank
n
.

Virtually cyclic dimension of infinite-type surface braid groups

When the surface is of infinite type, we can also compute the virtually cyclic geometric dimension.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\Sigma
be a connected surface of infinite type. Then

\gd_{\mathcal{F}_{1}}\bigl(\pb_{n}(\Sigma)\bigr)=n+1.

To establish the theorem above, it was necessary to show that the pure braid group
\pb_{n}(\Sigma)
satisfies condition C. Namely, for every element of infinite order

and every

implies

4.3
reduces the problem to computing the proper geometric dimension of the Weyl groups of virtually cyclic subgroups.

Let

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
G
be a group such that for every virtually free abelian subgroup
H\leq G

there exists a subgroup
H^{\prime}\leq G
commensurable with
H

N_{G}[H]=N_{G}(H^{\prime})

Proof.

Let
L\leq G
and let
H\leq L
be a virtually free abelian subgroup. By
hypothesis, there exists a subgroup
H^{\prime}\leq G
commensurable with
H
such that

N_{G}[H]=N_{G}(H^{\prime})
. It follows that

N_{L}[H]=L\cap N_{G}[H]=L\cap N_{G}(H^{\prime})\subseteq N_{L}(H^{\prime}\cap L).

We claim that
H^{\prime}\cap L
is commensurable with
H

H\cap(H^{\prime}\cap L)=H\cap H^{\prime}
, which is commensurable with
H

has finite index in

[H^{\prime}\cap L:H\cap H^{\prime}]\leq[H^{\prime}:H\cap H^{\prime}]<\infty,

Since

N_{L}[H^{\prime}\cap L]=N_{L}[H]\subseteq N_{L}(H^{\prime}\cap L).

Since the reverse inclusion
N_{L}(H^{\prime}\cap L)\subseteq N_{L}[H^{\prime}\cap L]
always

N_{L}(H^{\prime}\cap L)=N_{L}[H^{\prime}\cap L]
, as required.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let

be a surface with

Theorem 3.8

(Upper bound)
.

Let
\Sigma
be a connected surface of finite type, possibly non-orientable
and possibly with punctures, with
\chi(\Sigma)\leq 0
.
For every integer
r\geq 0
,

\gd_{{\mathcal{F}}_{r}}(\pb_{n}(\Sigma))\leq\cd(\pb_{n}(\Sigma))+r.

Proof.

The proof is by induction on
r
. Let
G=\pb_{n}(\Sigma)
. For
r=0
, it follows from
Lemma

3.7

\gd_{{\mathcal{F}}_{0}}(G)=\gd(G)=\cd(G).

Now, assume that the inequality holds for all
r<k
. We now prove it for
r=k
. Consider the equivalence relation
\sim
on
{\mathcal{F}}_{k}-{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}
defined by commensurability, and let

be a complete set of representatives of the equivalence classes in
({\mathcal{F}}_{k}-{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1})/\sim
. By
Corollary

2.9
, we have

\gd_{{\mathcal{F}}_{k}}(G)\leq\max\left\{\gd_{{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}}(G)+1,\gd_{{\mathcal{F}}_{k}[L]}(N_{G}[L])\mid L\in I\right\}.

Using the inductive hypothesis, we can bound
\gd_{{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}}(G)\leq\cd(G)+(k-1)
, and thus

\gd_{{\mathcal{F}}_{k}}(G)\leq\max\left\{\cd(G)+k,\gd_{{\mathcal{F}}_{k}[L]}(N_{G}[L])\mid L\in I\right\}.

To complete the proof, it suffices to show that
\gd_{{\mathcal{F}}_{k}[L]}(N_{G}[L])\leq\cd(G)+k
for all
L\in I
.

Let
L\in I
. By
Lemma

3.4
, there exists a subgroup
H\leq G
commensurable with
L
such that
N_{G}[L]=N_{G}(H)
. Therefore, we may replace
N_{G}[L]
with
N_{G}(H)
and work with
{\mathcal{F}}_{k}[H]
.

We write the family as

{\mathcal{F}}_{k}[H]=\{K\leq N_{G}(H)\mid K\in{\mathcal{F}}_{k}-{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1},K\sim H\}\cup({\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}\cap N_{G}(H))

as the union of two families,
{\mathcal{F}}_{k}[H]={\mathcal{G}}\cup({\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}\cap N_{G}(H))
, where
{\mathcal{G}}
is generated by

\{K\leq N_{G}(H)\mid K\in{\mathcal{F}}_{k}-{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1},[K]=[H]\}.

By

2.11
, we obtain

\begin{split}\gd_{{\mathcal{F}}_{k}[L]}(N_{G}(H))&\leq\max\left\{\gd_{{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}}(N_{G}(H)),\gd_{{\mathcal{G}}\cap({\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}\cap N_{G}(H))}(N_{G}(H))+1,\gd_{{\mathcal{G}}}(N_{G}(H))\right\}\\
&\leq\max\left\{\cd(G)+k-1,\gd_{{\mathcal{G}}\cap({\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}\cap N_{G}(H))}(N_{G}(H))+1,\gd_{{\mathcal{G}}}(N_{G}(H))\right\},\end{split}

where the second inequality follows from the inductive hypothesis.

We now proceed to prove the following inequalities:

i)

\gd_{{\mathcal{G}}}(N_{G}(H))\leq\cd(G)-k
,

ii)

\gd_{{\mathcal{G}}\cap({\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}\cap N_{G}(H))}(N_{G}(H))\leq\cd(G)+k-1
.

Once these inequalities are established, we conclude that
\gd_{{\mathcal{F}}_{k}[L]}(N_{G}(H))\leq\cd(G)+k
.

To prove item
i)
, note that a model for
E_{{\mathcal{F}}_{0}}(W_{G}(H))
is a model for

via the projection

. Therefore, the claim follows directly from

3.6
.

For item

, we apply

2.12
to the inclusion
{\mathcal{G}}\cap({\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}\cap N_{G}(H))\subset{\mathcal{G}}
, we obtain

\gd_{{\mathcal{G}}\cap({\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}\cap N_{G}(H))}(N_{G}(H))\leq\gd_{{\mathcal{G}}}(N_{G}(H))+d

such that for any
K\in{\mathcal{G}}
, we have
\gd_{{\mathcal{G}}\cap({\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}\cap N_{G}(H))}(K)\leq d
.

Since we have already established that
\gd_{{\mathcal{G}}}(N_{G}(H))\leq\cd(G)-k
, our next task is to show that

can be chosen to be

.

Recall that any
K\in{\mathcal{G}}
is virtually
\mathbb{Z}^{t}
for some

. We now consider two cases: If
K\in{\mathcal{G}}
is virtually
\mathbb{Z}^{t}
for some

, then

4.
Classifying spaces of infinite-type surface braid groups

In this section we compute the virtually cyclic dimension of infinite-type surface braid groups. For this we need some lemmas.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\Sigma
be a connected surface of infinite type. For every infinite virtually abelian subgroup
L
of
\pb_{m}(\Sigma)
, we have

\gdfin(W_{G}(L))\leq\cd(\pb_{m}(\Sigma))-\rank(L).

Proof.

The proof is by induction on
m
. When
m=1
, we have
\pb_{1}(\Sigma)=\pi_{1}(\Sigma)
, it is well known that
\pi_{1}(\Sigma)
is infinite countable non-finitely generated free group. Then
L\cong\mathbb{Z}
and
N_{\pb_{1}(\Sigma)}(L)\cong\mathbb{Z}
, it follows that

\gdfin(W_{G}(L))\leq\cd(\pb_{1}(\Sigma))-\rank(L).

Assume the lemma holds for
m
. We now prove it for
m+1
. By
Theorem

2.2
we have the following short exact sequence

1\to\pi_{1}(\Sigma-Q_{m})\to\pb_{m+1}(\Sigma)\to\pb_{m}(\Sigma)\to 1,

where
Q_{m}
denotes the set of
m
distinct points on
\Sigma
. Now the proof follows along the same lines as
Lemma

3.6
.
∎

Definition 4.2
.

[
30
, Condition 4.1]

We say that a group
G
satisfies
condition C
if for every
g,h\in G
with
|h|=\infty
and
k,l\in\mathbb{Z}
, the equation
gh^{k}g^{-1}=h^{l}
implies that
|k|=|l|
.

Lemma 4.3
.

[
30
, Lemma 4.4]

Let
n
be an integer. Suppose that
G
satisfies condition C. Suppose that
\gdfin(G)\leq n
and for every infinite cyclic subgroup
H
of
G
we have
\gdfin(W_{G}(H))\leq n
. Then
\gdvc(G)\leq n+1
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\Sigma
be a connected surface of infinite type. Then
\pb_{m}(\Sigma)
and
B_{n}(\Sigma)
satisfies condition C.

Proof.

By
[
37
, Lemma 4.14]
it is enough to prove the claim for
\pb_{m}(\Sigma)
.
The proof proceeds by induction on
m
. For
m=1
, we have
\pb_{1}(\Sigma)=\pi_{1}(\Sigma)
, which is a countable free group and hence satisfies condition
C
see for example
[
18
, Lemma 2.3]
.

Assume that the claim holds for all
k\leq m
, and we prove it for
m+1
. Consider the short exact sequence:

1\longrightarrow\pi_{1}(\Sigma-Q_{m})\longrightarrow\pb_{m+1}(\Sigma)\xrightarrow{p}\pb_{m}(\Sigma)\longrightarrow 1.

Let
g,h\in\pb_{m+1}(\Sigma)
with
|h|=\infty
, and suppose there exist
k,l\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

gh^{k}g^{-1}=h^{l}.

We distinguish two cases:

Case 1:

h\notin\ker(p)
. Then

p(g)\,p(h)^{k}\,p(g)^{-1}=p(h)^{l}.

Since
Theorem

2.4
ensures that
\pb_{m}(\Sigma)
is torsion-free, the element
p(h)
has infinite order. Moreover, by the inductive hypothesis,
\pb_{m}(\Sigma)
satisfies Condition
C
, and hence
k=\pm l
.

Case 2:

h\in\ker(p)=\pi_{1}(\Sigma-Q_{m})
. Conjugation by
g
defines a homomorphism

\varphi\colon\pi_{1}(\Sigma-Q_{m})\longrightarrow\pi_{1}(\Sigma-Q_{m}),

and
\varphi(h^{k})=h^{l}
. Note that the cyclic subgroup
\langle h\rangle
is contained in a unique maximal infinite cyclic subgroup
H\leq\pi_{1}(\Sigma-Q_{m})
. Since conjugation preserves maximal cyclic subgroups, we must have
\varphi(H)=H
. As
H\cong\mathbb{Z}
, it follows that
\varphi|_{H}=\pm\mathrm{id}
, and therefore
k=\pm l
.

This concludes the induction step, and hence the proof.
∎

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\Sigma
be a connected surface of infinite type. Then,

\gd_{{\mathcal{F}}_{1}}(\pb_{n}(\Sigma))=n+1.

Proof.

We first prove the upper bound. By
Lemma

4.3
and
Proposition

4.4
, it suffices to verify the following conditions:

(i)

\gd(\pb_{n}(\Sigma))\leq n
;

(ii)

for every infinite cyclic subgroup
H\leq\pb_{n}(\Sigma)
, we have
\underline{\gd}(W(H))\leq n
.

Item (i) was proved in
[
22
, Theorem 4.3]
, and item (ii) follows from
Lemma

4.1
.

We now prove the lower bound. Observe that
\Sigma
contains a subsurface homeomorphic to
\Sigma_{0}^{2}
such that each component of
\Sigma-\Sigma_{0}^{2}
is not homeomorphic to a disk. By
[
22
, Proposition 4.2]
, this implies that
\pb_{n}(\Sigma_{0}^{2})
embeds as a subgroup of
\pb_{n}(\Sigma)
. The desired lower bound then follows from
Lemma

3.9
and
Lemma

3.10
.

∎
