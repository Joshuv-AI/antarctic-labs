VIOLENCE IN EL SALVADOR

THE SUBJECT OF POLITICAL VIOLENCE IN EL SALVADOR IMPACTS DIRECTLY
ON THE CREDIBILITY OF THE GOVERNMENT AND THE ELECTORAL PROCESS.
APART FROM DEATHS DUE TO ACTUAL COMBAT, THE LEVEL OF POLITICAL
VIOLENCE HAS DIMINISHED SOMEWHAT SINCE SEPTEMBER AND IS CON-
SIDERABLY LOWER THAN THE LEVEL OF LAST YEAR AT THIS TIME. THE
GOVERNMENT IS MORE CONSCIOUS OF THE DAMAGE DONE TO ITS IMAGE BY
CONTINUED VIOLENCE AND NEDIA REPORTS OF ATROCITIES, AND HAS
TRIED--ALTHOUGH NOT VERY EBFFECTIVELY--TO CONTROL IT.

~- There are several categories of violence which should be
recognized.

~- There is, for example, the sectarian violence by the extreme
right and left; there ig official government violence;
anarchic violence and banditry by opportunists; and the
normal domestic violence that has givaye plagued El Salvador.

-- Groups sympathetic to or allied with the insurgent left--like
the Catholic Legal Ald Society, the Salvadoran Human Rights |
Commission, and the Mothers of Political Prisoners and
Victims Organization—continue to inflate the death toll
figures to where the world media routinely repeats numbers
ranging from 35,000 to 40,000 deaths since January 1980. It
is often alleged that the junta is responsible for over 80%
of this total.

-- Our best estimate of the total number of deaths due to
violence in El Salvador for the past two years would be
around 22,000.

-- This figure includes all battlefield deaths and non-political
deaths of which there may be perhaps 6,000.

~- Of the 16,000 or go deaths we feel are politically inspired,
about 30% are attribatable to right-wing terrorist and
vigilante groups, about 40% are due to left-wing violence,
and a remaining 30% to official government police and
military operations.

~- These estimates--while necessarily rough--do reflect our
concerted effort to analyze quantitative and qualitative date
