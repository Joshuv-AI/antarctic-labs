Evaluating the impact of stable cellphone access and digital navigation on 6-month health outcomes and service connection in emergency care: protocol for a randomised controlled trial
Authors: Andrea S Wallace, Brenda L Luther, Sara E Simonsen, Sara Jackson, Ernest Grigorian, Bob Wong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionHealthcare systems increasingly screen for social needs, yet referrals often fail to connect patients with services—particularly in emergency department (ED) settings where unmet social needs are common. Our research highlights critical barriers to service connection, including unstable access to cellphones, limited internet and low digital literacy. These challenges disproportionately affect socio-economically disadvantaged groups and are associated with poorer health outcomes and more frequent ED visits. With the recognition of information and communication technology (ICT) access as a ‘super social determinant of health’, we have a timely opportunity to test solutions that address these disparities.Methods and analysisWe will conduct a three-arm randomised controlled trial enrolling 600 ED patients across three ED sites. Participants are eligible if they self-report at least one unmet social need using the validated Screener for Intensifying Community Referrals for Health (SINCERE) screening instrument, identify barriers to ICT access and want to be referred to community services. Arm 1 will receive community service referrals using email or alternate phone numbers (211 service navigator outreach, usual care). Arm 2 will receive stable cellphone access and community service referrals (cellphone with data+211 service navigator outreach). Arm 3 will receive cellphone access, community service referrals and tailored digital navigation support (Cellphone with data+211 service navigator outreach+digital navigation). Primary outcomes will include community service use and health-related outcomes such as depression, anxiety, social support, loneliness, self-reported global health and healthcare utilisation over 6 months. Using a mixed methods design, we will explore contextual and individual factors influencing engagement and outcomes.Ethics and disseminationThis study has been approved by the University of Utah Institutional Review Board (IRB). Introduction

Background and rationale

With increasing recognition of social conditions as major contributors to health outcomes and disparities, health systems are expanding efforts to screen for and address patients’ social needs, such as for food, housing, utilities and transportation.1 Emergency departments (EDs), which are often critical access points for underserved populations, may be the most opportune settings for this work. Across multiple studies, our team has screened approximately 50 000 patients for social needs, finding that up to 50% of ED patients report at least one unmet social need.2 3 To address these needs, we partnered with Utah 211, a statewide information and referral system operated by United Way that connects individuals with community resources addressing needs such as housing, food assistance, transportation, utility support, healthcare access and financial assistance through telephone, text and web-based platforms (see https://211utah.org/).

Despite these efforts, substantial gaps in successful service connection persist. We found that 211 service navigators are able to reach fewer than one-third of patients who request follow-up after reporting social needs.2 This shortfall may be associated with limited or unstable access to information and communication technology (ICT)—the digital tools and infrastructure used to communicate, access information and engage with health and social services—including inconsistent telephone numbers, reliance on email rather than telephone contact and lack of home internet access. As healthcare and community resource systems increasingly rely on digital platforms, these barriers may impede successful connection to needed services. Among ED patients reporting social needs and requesting follow-up, 34% provided either another person’s phone number or only an email address, both of which are associated with lower service connection rates, greater reported severity of needs and increased emergency care utilisation. Further compounding this issue, 24% of patients reported no home internet access.4 Qualitative interviews with 63 patients (English and Spanish speakers) who successfully connected to services through 211 revealed high satisfaction with the service navigators, but also described significant challenges in navigating online forms and completing required steps for service enrolment.5 These findings underscore the role of ICT—particularly stable phone and internet access—as both a gatekeeper and enabler in addressing social needs.

ICT access has been recognised as a ‘super social determinant of health’, with consistent associations between digital exclusion and poorer health outcomes.6 Households with incomes under US$30 000 annually are significantly more likely to rely solely on cellphones for both voice and internet access; these devices are often unstable due to cost-related service interruptions and pose barriers to completing online forms and uploading documents often required in service applications. The 2021 Digital Equity Act presents a historic opportunity to close these digital access gaps, but evidence is urgently needed to guide resource allocation and demonstrate how overcoming ICT barriers can improve health.7

Our study responds to this critical need by testing a scalable, clinically integrated solution that addresses ICT access in real-world clinical settings. Through the provision of stable cellphone access and digital navigation support and comparing both to the standard practice of simply referring patients to community service providers, we aim to understand whether and how addressing ICT barriers increases patients’ connection to community services and improves health outcomes. Although this study is conducted within the US healthcare system, challenges related to digital access, service navigation and health equity are increasingly recognised internationally. As healthcare and social service systems continue to adopt digital platforms, understanding how ICT access influences engagement with services may inform intervention development across diverse healthcare settings and countries.

Objectives

This study seeks to evaluate the effectiveness of an intervention designed to overcome barriers to ICT access—specifically, the provision of stable cellphone access and digital navigation support—in improving health outcomes among ED patients with unmet social needs.

The study has three primary objectives:

Study Aim 1. Refine an intervention facilitating ICT access through community-engaged methods and rapid-cycle testing across three geographically and organisationally diverse ED settings.

Study design

This study, a parallel superiority trial, employs a community-engaged approach to refine the e-SINCERE intervention, a digitally enhanced version of the SINCERE social needs screening and referral program designed to address barriers related to ICT. This approach involves ongoing engagement with a community advisory board (CAB) comprised of community health workers, service navigators and leaders in promoting ICT access in the intermountain western USA. Since November 2024, the CAB has met virtually approximately once per month for 7 months to refine the ICT-focused screening questions and digital navigation support intervention. Following finalisation of the study protocol with the CAB, we will conduct a National Institutes of Health (NIH) Stage 3 real-world efficacy trial to evaluate 6-month outcomes related to community and health service use, as well as health outcomes, among patients with social needs and barriers to ICT access. According to the NIH Stage Model for behavioural intervention development, Stage 3 studies evaluate intervention efficacy under real-world conditions while maintaining sufficient methodological rigour to assess effectiveness and implementation feasibility. This approach is particularly appropriate for testing interventions intended for future large-scale implementation within existing health and community service systems. Participants will be recruited from three ED sites in the intermountain region of the United States and referred for 211 community service connections.

Patients will be screened in person using touchscreen devices. Screening will involve the validated 10-item SINCERE screener8 and ICT-focused questions developed with input from the CAB. Those with barriers to ICT access will be randomised into one of three treatment arms to support their engagement with community service referrals:

211 service navigator outreach using the patient’s own device (eg, phone, email or a contact’s phone)—this represents usual care.

This design introduces two levels of intervention intensification compared with usual care. Data sources will include screening results, referral information from 211 (via Salesforce electronic records and referral system), patient-reported outcome measures (via surveys) and clinical outcomes extracted from electronic health records (EHRs). By integrating ICT supports (eg, phone/data access and digital navigation) into the existing SINCERE screening and referral process, this study will rigorously assess the extent to which ICT barriers affect engagement with community and health services, as well as health outcomes. Qualitative interviews will further explore how ICT barriers interact with environmental and individual factors, influencing both service utilisation and outcomes among patients with unmet social needs. The qualitative data will serve to contextualise and triangulate the quantitative data thereby fully describing how ICT factors influence the experience of engaging with needed social support services. Finally, community partner involvement in the design, conduct and reporting of the trial will ensure the relevance to our intended audiences. Study setting

The trial will be conducted across three EDs located in a single state in the western USA. These sites were purposefully selected to represent a range of geographical and organisational contexts, including urban academic, suburban community and rural hospitals. Each site serves a socio-economically diverse patient population, allowing for a robust evaluation of the intervention across varying community needs and health system structures.

The urban academic ED is a Level I trauma centre situated in a metropolitan area and serves approximately 50 000 patients annually. As part of a regional academic health system, it functions as a key referral hub for both health services and community resources.

The suburban community hospital ED is a 40-bed facility located in a rapidly growing suburban region and is part of a multistate health system. It serves an estimated 16 500 patients each year and provides an important point of contrast to the urban academic site in terms of both organisational infrastructure and the sociodemographic characteristics of the surrounding community.

The rural community hospital ED is a 44-bed facility serving a geographically expansive rural area with approximately 17 000 annual visits. This isolated region is marked by substantial social risk factors, including a high proportion of households that speak a language other than English, limited access to broadband internet and elevated rates of disability, uninsurance, unemployment and poverty.

Including three distinct ED settings will enable the evaluation of: (1) the intervention’s effectiveness under real-world conditions, (2) mechanisms of action at the patient, community and health system levels, and (3) the sustainability of observed effects. Social needs screening and community service referrals as well as digital navigation support will be implemented uniformly across all sites through a centralised 211 call centre using standardised protocols to ensure consistency in intervention delivery.

Eligibility criteria

This study includes ED patients who report social needs, barriers to ICT access, want to be contacted by 211 and reflects the demographic and socio-economic diversity of the participating clinical sites. Eligibility is not restricted by clinical condition, enhancing generalisability. However, patients who are non-verbal, reside in long-term care facilities or whose illness prevents them from comfortably or safely communicating (eg, acute trauma, psychosis) will be excluded, as the intervention is designed to support independent, community-based service engagement and follow-up.

The study’s eligible population reflects the diversity of the ED settings, which serve a range of urban, suburban and rural communities. Preliminary data from related screening efforts show that many participants face substantial ICT-related challenges. For example, in preliminary data from our team’s ongoing studies, approximately one-quarter of participants report no home internet access and over half have household incomes below US$30 000. A significant proportion of patients also report difficulty understanding written medical information, underscoring the importance of tailored support for digital and health literacy.

Intervention

Social needs screening and referral

Patient recruitment will be integrated into existing clinical workflows, with ED patients completing social needs screening facilitated by bilingual study coordinators and touchscreen tablets. SINCERE, the social needs screener, is a validated 10-item tool available in both English and Spanish. Responses to the SINCERE screener are entered directly into a secure, Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA)-compliant Research Electronic Data Capture (REDCap) electronic system.

Patients who report at least one unmet social need will be asked if they are interested in receiving outreach through a centralised community navigation service (ie, 211). Those who agree will then be screened for ICT barriers. Patients without ICT barriers will be referred to 211 using their own contact information in accordance with standard procedures. Those with identified ICT barriers will be eligible for the study. A positive ICT barriers screen, co-developed with the CAB, includes statements regarding not owning nor regularly using a cellphone or having limited access to internet or data plans. Patients who screen positive for ICT barriers will be informed about the opportunity to participate in a research study that offers enhanced support. If the patient declines participation, a referral will be completed using available contact information and the refusal noted in the study record. For patients who agree to participate, written informed consent will be obtained prior to completion of the baseline survey. Participants will receive a US$20 gift card for completing the baseline survey. They will then be randomised using permuted block randomisation stratified by ED site into one of three study arms:

Service navigator outreach: Participants will receive outreach from a 211-community service navigator using contact information provided during screening, such as their own phone, email or that of a third party. No additional phone or data support will be provided.

Service navigator outreach: Participants will receive outreach from a 211-community service navigator using contact information provided during screening, such as their own phone, email or that of a third party. No additional phone or data support will be provided.

Service navigator outreach+cellphone with data: Participants will receive a basic cellphone with prepaid plan covering cellular minutes and data, along with outreach from a community service navigator. No digital navigation support will be provided.

Service navigator outreach+cellphone with data: Participants will receive a basic cellphone with prepaid plan covering cellular minutes and data, along with outreach from a community service navigator. No digital navigation support will be provided.

Service navigator outreach+cellphone with data+digital navigation: Participants will receive a basic cellphone with prepaid plan covering cellular minutes and data, and outreach from a community service navigator trained in digital literacy who will provide real-time support with online forms, service applications and digital health tools. Examples of such tools and training, that will be adapted with the help of the CAB, can be found at https://www.digitalinclusion.org/resource/2025-dn-toolkit/.

Service navigator outreach+cellphone with data+digital navigation: Participants will receive a basic cellphone with prepaid plan covering cellular minutes and data, and outreach from a community service navigator trained in digital literacy who will provide real-time support with online forms, service applications and digital health tools. Examples of such tools and training, that will be adapted with the help of the CAB, can be found at https://www.digitalinclusion.org/resource/2025-dn-toolkit/.

All participants who request assistance will be referred to the centralised navigation service through a secure, automated messaging system that includes a unique identifier, contact preferences, ZIP code and SINCERE social needs screener results. For those enrolled in the study, group assignment and ICT status are included in the referral to tailor outreach appropriately. To support continuity, all participants will receive printed or digital cards with direct contact details for both 211 service navigators and study staff. While enrolment in one arm of the study will prohibit participation in other arms, no effort will be made to prevent patients from seeking assistance or information through other means (eg, via hospital social workers).

Service navigator outreach

Service navigators will attempt to contact all referred patients within 48 hours of their ED visit, using the preferred method of communication identified during screening. Each interaction and follow-up attempt will be recorded in a secure case management system. Based on prior studies, patients typically receive multiple resource referrals during their initial conversation with a service navigator.

The follow-up protocol incorporates standardised behaviour change techniques, including assessment of patient’s readiness and self-efficacy, the development of specific action steps (eg, when and how to pursue referrals) and scheduled follow-up calls by service navigators approximately every 2 weeks for up to 3 months. Adjustments to goals and support strategies will be made based on reported progress and persistent barriers. All navigator interactions will include documentation of patient goals, confidence ratings, barriers encountered and outcomes. Navigators will receive regular supervision and training, and interactions will be reviewed for adherence to the study protocol through regular audits within the Utah 211 system.

Service navigator outreach + cellphone with data

Participants randomised to the service navigator follow-up+enhanced ICT arm will receive a basic cellphone with 6 months of data and cellular service at the time of recruitment. The phone will be preloaded with the 211 digital application (‘App’). The 211 service navigators will contact patients within 48 hours using the study-provided cellphone via voice and/or text.

Service navigator outreach + cellphone with data + digital navigation

Participants randomised to the enhanced ICT+digital navigation arm will receive a basic cellphone with 6 months of data and cellular service at the time of recruitment. The phone will be preloaded with the 211 digital application (‘App’). Within 48 hours, a 211-service navigator trained in digital literacy will contact participants via voice call and/or text using the cellphone provided at enrolment. In addition to standard resource navigation, these digital navigators will offer real-time support, helping participants access and complete online forms for services such as housing assistance, utility support and healthcare coverage. They may also assist with navigating patient portals and other digital health tools. Training for these navigators will be based on materials co-developed with the CAB and adapted from established programmes, including those offered by the National Digital Inclusion Alliance. This approach represents an enhancement over usual care, where navigators typically provide resource links without live, hands-on support.

Outcomes

Framework and evaluation approach

Outcomes will be evaluated using the Reach, Effectiveness, Adoption, Implementation and Maintenance (RE-AIM) framework, which supports the assessment of public health interventions in real-world settings and is particularly relevant for complex, multilevel health service interventions.9–12

Reach will be assessed by determining the proportion of patients with unmet social needs who successfully engage with community-based services following social needs screening and referral. Effectiveness will be measured by examining whether overcoming ICT barriers leads to improvements in engagement with both community and health services, better overall health outcomes and a reduction in known health disparities, as guided by the National Institute on Minority Health and Health Disparities research framework13 and Andersen’s model of healthcare access.14 15 Adoption will focus on the degree to which patients accept and use services once ICT barriers have been addressed. Implementation will be evaluated by examining participant engagement with the ICT components of the intervention, specifically receipt and use of the study-provided cellphone to connect with 211 and participation in digital navigation. Maintenance will be assessed by whether ICT access and service use are sustained during the 6-month follow-up period. Environmental characteristics

Environmental variables will be collected from REDCap-based participant surveys, 211 administrative data and qualitative interviews. Geographical context will be captured using rural versus urban designations based on county classification. Socio-economic disadvantage will be indexed using the Area Deprivation Index, calculated at the census tract and county levels, which incorporates key factors relevant to ICT access such as housing quality, educational attainment and access to telephone services.15 Health system characteristics—including ED site (urban, suburban or rural; academic or community-based), local ICT infrastructure (eg, broadband access, cellular coverage) and availability of community-based services—will also be included in the analysis.

Population and patient characteristics

Participant characteristics will be collected through the EHR and structured surveys and will include demographic characteristics (age, gender, race, ethnicity, primary language and educational attainment), insurance status, health literacy assessed using a validated measure16; social needs as measured by the SINCERE screener,8 and comorbidity burden as measured through the Self-Report Generated Charlson Comorbidity Index.17

ICT access will be assessed by availability of communication devices (eg, telephone, cellphone, smartphone, computer) and data services (eg, cellular minutes, mobile data plans, broadband internet). ICT access questions were developed with input from the CAB and consist of one question added to the original SINCERE screener. Because digital literacy is a rapidly evolving and context-specific construct, several candidate measures will be reviewed and selected in collaboration with the CAB during the refinement phase of the study.18 19

Health behaviours

In this study, engagement with community services is conceptualised as a health behaviour, focusing specifically on participants’ engagement with community resources following referral. These data will be drawn from 211 administrative records and self-reported survey responses. Engagement will be measured by attempts to contact services, successful connection with recommended resources and ongoing service use over time. Healthcare utilisation will be measured as the number of ED and primary care visits during the 6 months before and 6 months after enrolment.

Health outcomes

Health outcomes will be assessed through both EHR and survey data. Patient-reported health outcomes will be assessed using a battery of validated instruments. These include the Patient-Reported Outcomes Measurement Information System (PROMIS) Global Health short forms, which capture general health status as well as specific domains such as depression and anxiety19–23; PROMIS social health measures, including ability to participate in social roles and activities, and informational and instrumental support24–26; and the Medical Outcomes Study Social Support Survey, used alongside PROMIS measures to explore the multifaceted nature of social support in the context of ICT access.27–29 Additional outcomes include loneliness, measured using the University of California Los Angeles (UCLA) Loneliness Scale,30 and trust in the healthcare system, defined as the degree to which individuals believe healthcare organisations and providers act in patients’ best interests and deliver care in a reliable, fair and trustworthy manner and assessed using validated scales.31 Additional instruments available through the PROMIS and PhenX measurement toolkits will be considered for inclusion based on feasibility, relevance to study aims and input from the CAB. Finally, healthcare utilisation variables will be extracted from the EHR and measured as the number of ED and primary care visits during the 6 months before and 6 months after enrolment.

Procedures

Community-engaged ICT intervention co-creation and refinement

This study will follow community-engaged research principles, leveraging prior experience from the investigative team to co-create and refine the ICT intervention. A CAB representing digital equity policymakers, frontline community service providers and advocates—will guide all stages of the study. CAB members will contribute to refining screening questions for ICT access barriers, selecting measures of digital literacy, co-developing intervention components (eg, cellphone and data provision, digital navigation) and adapting navigator training materials. The CAB will also provide feedback on study materials, recruitment strategies and culturally appropriate outreach procedures.

The CAB will meet monthly during the first year of the study to guide intervention development and measurement selection. Meetings will shift to a quarterly schedule during the implementation and analysis phases. After each meeting, participating CAB members will be compensated US$100 for their time and expertise via electronic check.

Participant timeline and study activities

Following screening, recruitment, consent and randomisation, all participants will be referred within 48 hours to a centralised community navigation service. This referral is transmitted via a secure electronic system and includes each participant’s unique identifier, preferred contact method, documented social needs, ICT access status and assigned intervention group. Follow-up support will be provided every 2 weeks over a 12-week period. Participants in the service navigator outreach+cellphone with data+digital navigation arm will receive a basic cellphone or a prepaid card covering minutes and data, along with individualised support from digital navigators trained to assist with accessing online resources and completing digital forms. Those in the service navigator outreach+cellphone with data arm will receive the same cellphone or prepaid data access but without additional digital navigation. Participants receiving service navigator outreach (usual care) will be contacted using their own device or alternative contact information provided during enrolment; they will not receive any ICT-related support.

Navigators will use behaviour change strategies including goal setting, barrier resolution and confidence-building techniques. Standardised communication prompts and templates will be used to ensure consistency.

Patient-reported outcome surveys will be administered at baseline, 12 weeks and 24 weeks, capturing data on social needs resolution, patient trust and health status using PROMIS and other validated instruments. Survey delivery will be coordinated via REDCap, and follow-up surveys will be conducted via text, email or telephone, depending on participant preferences. To minimise differential attrition across intervention groups, follow-up surveys may be completed through multiple participant-preferred methods, including telephone, text messaging, email or other available communication channels. Telephone administration is used solely as a retention strategy and is available to participants in all study arms. Finally, healthcare utilisation variables will be extracted from the EHR 6 months after enrolment. The schedule of study activities and outcome assessments is summarised in table 2.

Sample size

Justification and power analysis

Sample size projections are based on ongoing and prior ED screening initiatives, although some uncertainty remains due to the inclusion of two new community hospital EDs and the novel focus on ICT barriers. Over the planned 3.75-year screening period, approximately 12 000 patients are expected to report at least one unmet social need, with an estimated 4000 of these patients requesting follow-up through referral to a community service navigator.

Among referred patients, it is anticipated that 960–1440 will meet criteria for ICT barriers, as approximately 34% currently report limited contact methods (such as only an email or another person’s phone) and 24% report lacking home internet access. From this subset, 600 patients are expected to consent to participate in the trial and complete follow-up assessments. These participants will be randomised equally across three intervention arms (n=200 per arm), stratified by recruitment site.

A sensitivity analysis using G*Power 3.1 was conducted to assess the adequacy of this sample size. A conservative repeated measures analysis of variance model was used to approximate the planned mixed-effects analyses. Assumptions included a two-tailed alpha of 0.05, a correlation of 0.50 between repeated measures and an attrition rate of 18%. Under these conditions, a sample of 600 yields 82% power to detect a small effect size (Cohen’s d=0.25), which is within the range considered clinically meaningful.

Assignment of interventions: allocation

Sequence generation

Participants will be randomised using a computer-generated permuted block randomisation procedure, stratified by ED site (urban academic, suburban community, rural community), to ensure balanced allocation across intervention arms within each setting.

Allocation concealment mechanism

Randomisation will be implemented using the REDCap randomisation module, which is embedded within the secure electronic data capture system. This module prevents study personnel from accessing the allocation sequence prior to randomisation and ensures that assignment is concealed until after eligibility confirmation and baseline data collection are complete.

Implementation

After providing consent and completing the baseline survey, participants will be randomised by trained study coordinators using the REDCap system. They will then be informed of their group assignment, including the format and timing of outreach from community service navigators. Participants assigned to either ICT intervention arm will receive a basic cellphone with 6 months of prepaid minutes and data and will be informed whether they will receive additional support from a digital navigator. To promote ongoing engagement, all participants—including those in the usual care arm—will be given printed or virtual cards with direct contact information for the service navigation team and study staff.

Data collection and management

Data collection methods

Patient-reported outcomes will be collected at three time points: baseline (during the index ED visit), 3 months and 6 months postrecruitment (see table 2). The baseline survey will be administered in person using touchscreen tablets, with responses recorded in REDCap—a secure, HIPAA-compliant electronic data capture system. Follow-up surveys will be delivered via participants’ preferred communication method (text message, email, telephone or postal mail). Participants will receive a US$20 incentive for each completed survey.

To reduce bias, a study coordinator who is not involved in the intervention and is blinded to treatment assignment will monitor survey completion. When necessary, this coordinator will contact participants to complete surveys by telephone. While not anticipated in this low-risk study, any potential harms will be escalated via this role to a data monitoring committee comprised of external academic reviewers.

Administrative data will be obtained from two sources. First, encounter-level data from the 211 Salesforce system—including contact attempts, referral types and services used—will be exported weekly and imported into REDCap using CSV files matched by a unique patient identifier. Second, clinical data will be extracted from the Epic EHR system. Structured data fields (eg, demographics, diagnoses and healthcare utilisation) will be obtained from the institutional data warehouse, covering the 6 months preceding and following the index ED visit (and study enrolment).

Qualitative data collection and analysis

To address Aim 3, qualitative data will be collected from both CAB interactions and semi-structured patient interviews. CAB meetings will be conducted virtually, audio-recorded with participant consent and professionally transcribed. Meeting discussions will be guided by a structured set of questions and detailed notes will be maintained regarding recommendations, decisions and intervention refinements.

Patient interviews will be conducted approximately 6 months following enrolment using a stratified sampling approach to ensure representation across intervention groups, service engagement outcomes and geographical contexts (urban, suburban and rural).

Interviews will be conducted by trained bilingual study personnel via telephone, audio-recorded, professionally transcribed and translated when conducted in Spanish. Interviews are expected to last approximately 60 min and will explore participants’ experiences with ICT barriers, community service referrals, digital navigation support, health behaviours, healthcare utilisation and perceived health outcomes. Recruitment for interviews will continue until thematic saturation is achieved, defined as the point at which no substantially new themes emerge from ongoing analysis.

Data management

All data will be maintained in REDCap with audit trails and role-based permissions to protect access. Weekly data checks will be performed by a programme manager to ensure accuracy and completeness. Imported salesforce and EHR data will be linked via semiautomated processes and validated for accuracy. Personally identifiable information will be retained only for data linkage and removed during the final analysis phase. Only authorised personnel will access identifiable datasets.

Confidentiality

Participant confidentiality will be strictly maintained throughout the study. All electronic data will be stored on secure, password-protected institutional servers. Personally identifiable information will be accessible only to designated staff and will not be included in analytical datasets. De-identified data will be stored separately from the master linking file. All data handling will comply with institutional and federal regulations, including the HIPAA.

Analytical methods

Primary and secondary outcome analysis

For Aim 1, qualitative data from CAB meetings will be analysed using content analysis of guided questions, emergent insights and decisions. Discussion points, suggestions, decisions and future needs will be collected at each meeting and tabulated for reporting. Multiple team members will independently review the content analysis, and discrepancies will be resolved through team discussion and consensus.

For service utilisation outcomes (eg, ED visits, community service engagement), a generalised linear mixed-effects model will be used. Poisson distribution will be assumed, and overdispersion will be tested. If present, a negative binomial distribution will be applied using the glmer.nb function in the R MASS package.

Post hoc pairwise comparisons will be conducted to identify significant differences between specific groups following detection of overall group effects.

Intention-to-treat analysis

All analyses will be conducted using an intention-to-treat framework, whereby participants are analysed according to their original group assignment regardless of adherence to the intervention protocol.

Handling of missing data

Patterns of missingness will be explored, and appropriate statistical methods will be applied. Mixed-effects models inherently accommodate data missing at random. As a sensitivity analysis, multiple imputation using chained equations will be conducted, and results across imputed datasets will be pooled to assess robustness.

Qualitative analysis

Qualitative data will be analysed using conventional content analysis. Initially, two trained members of the research team will independently review transcripts and develop a preliminary coding framework based on interview content and study aims. The coding framework will then be applied systematically to all transcripts, with additional codes added inductively as needed to capture emerging concepts. An independent reviewer will evaluate coded transcripts to enhance consistency and accuracy. Following coding, codes will be grouped into categories and themes describing participant experiences with ICT access, service engagement and health-related outcomes. NVivo software will be used to support coding, data management and maintenance of an audit trail.

Several strategies will be employed to enhance trustworthiness of the qualitative findings, including verbatim transcription, independent coding, peer debriefing among study investigators, maintenance of analytical memos and coding records, and ongoing review of emerging findings. These procedures will support credibility, dependability, auditability and transferability of study findings. Qualitative findings will be used to contextualise and complement quantitative results, providing a richer understanding of how ICT access influences engagement with community resources and subsequent health outcomes.

Ethics and dissemination

Research ethics approval

This study has been reviewed and approved by the University of Utah Institutional Review Board IRB_00181891 on 10 October 2024. All procedures comply with the Declaration of Helsinki and applicable institutional and federal human subjects’ regulations. Any protocol alterations or deviations will be reported to this body.

Consent

Written or electronic informed consent will be obtained from all participants by trained study staff. The purpose, procedures, risks and benefits of participation will be explained, and participants will be informed of their right to withdraw at any time without consequences to their care.

Confidentiality

Confidentiality will be safeguarded through secure data storage, limited access to identifiable information and the use of de-identified datasets for analysis. Data management practices adhere to institutional and HIPAA standards.

Dissemination policy

Findings will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications, scientific conferences, reports for health system and community partners, and community-facing summaries developed in collaboration with the CAB. Dissemination activities will prioritise accessibility and relevance to patients, community organisations, policymakers and healthcare leaders.

Patient and public involvement

The study has incorporated patient and public involvement throughout its development. A CAB composed of eight members—including individuals with lived experience, service providers and policy advocates—has provided input on study design, digital literacy measures and culturally appropriate engagement strategies. The CAB will remain involved in data interpretation and dissemination, and review of reported harms if applicable. Community-friendly formats will be prioritised to ensure accessibility and relevance.
