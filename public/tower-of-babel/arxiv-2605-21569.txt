When Support Escalates Distress: Regulation and Escalation in LLM Responses to Venting and Advice-Seeking
Vivienne Bihe Chi, Adithya V Ganesan, Ryan L Boyd, Lyle Ungar, Sharath Chandra Guntuku

Abstract
Large language models are increasingly used for mental health support, yet little is known about whether their responses are psychologically safe across different help-seeking styles. We examine a foundational distinction in emotional disclosure, venting vs. advice-seeking, and whether LLMs respond in ways that regulate or amplify distress. Using 178,800 Reddit posts, we first show the two help-seeking styles are linguistically distinguishable at scale. We then introduce a measurement framework grounded in interpersonal emotion regulation theory that captures Regulation and Escalation as empirically independent dimensions. Across persona conditions (default, friend, therapist), GPT-5.3 responses systematically mirror help-seeking style: venting elicits more regulation, but also more escalation. Therapist personas reduce escalation while maintaining regulation, whereas friend personas increase both. A crowdsourced human study finds no user experience penalty for the safer therapist condition, but reveals that lay raters cannot reliably detect escalation without expert knowledge. Responses that feel supportive may simultaneously intensify distress in ways standard safety evaluation cannot see, and empathy metrics alone cannot replace a framework that measures both.



1
Introduction

Mental health support is among the most consequential and fastest-growing use cases for large language models (LLMs).
Available, low-stigma, and increasingly empathic, these systems are already used by more than 1 in 8 U.S. adolescents and young adults for mental health advice
(
21
)
, many of whom receive no professional care otherwise.
Among adults with diagnosed mental health conditions, nearly half report using LLMs for mental health support
(
31
)
, and recent survey suggests that nearly a quarter of U.S. adults use LLMs for mental health overall
(
39
)
.
LLMs have become de facto emotion regulation partners for a large and vulnerable population, and how LLMs respond to emotional distress has direct consequences for mental health outcomes.

Ensuring these interactions are safe requires more than preventing overtly harmful outputs.
A more latent risk is that LLM responses can appear empathetic and supportive while functionally worsening emotional outcomes—amplifying distress, reinforcing maladaptive appraisals, or encouraging rumination.
This failure mode is invisible to refusal-based safety frameworks
(
43
)
,
which evaluate whether a model refuses a harmful request rather than whether it harms through the dynamics of an ordinary supportive exchange.
It is, however, consistent with the sycophantic tendencies of LLMs: optimizing for affective alignment and perceived helpfulness over responses that genuinely serve the user
(
38
;
10
)
.
In factual domains, sycophancy produces misinformation
(
4
)
;
In emotional support contexts, this tendency has a clinical analog. Co-rumination describes a pattern in which validation co-occurs with sustained focus on negative experiences and affect intensification, producing poorer psychological outcomes despite appearing supportive
(
30
)
. Whether LLM responses exhibit this structure is unknown, because existing safety tools are not designed to detect it.

To investigate this risk, we study a foundational distinction in help-seeking discourse: venting versus advice-seeking.
Venting is an open-ended and emotion-focused disclosure strategy
(
40
;
24
)
, while advice-seeking is more regulated and solution-oriented
(
18
)
.
Both involve distress disclosure, but they differ in communicative intent in ways that should shape a well-calibrated response. Because venting is oriented toward validation and emotional acknowledgment, it provides a sensitive test case for whether LLMs prioritize agreement over emotional regulation, and whether that prioritization comes at a cost.

We address this question through a combination of large-scale Reddit data and a theory-grounded measurement framework capturing regulatory and escalatory dynamics in LLM responses. Concretely, we contribute:
(1) A linguistic characterization of venting and advice-seeking behavior, derived from a large-scale, person-matched Reddit corpus of 178,800 posts from 14,040 users, showing that the two help-seeking styles are robustly distinguishable at scale, and that LLMs accommodate the emotional register of input discourse in their responses.
(2) A theory-grounded measurement framework capturing Regulation and Escalation as two validated, largely independent response dimensions, grounded in interpersonal emotion regulation theory.
(3) Empirical findings showing that regulatory and escalatory behaviors co-occur rather than trade off in response to venting, and that persona conditioning shifts this balance in theoretically coherent ways.
(4) A crowdsourced human annotation study demonstrating that automated annotation underestimates escalatory behaviors relative to human observers, and that safer therapist-persona responses carry no user experience penalty.

2
Related Works

2.1
LLMs for Mental Health Support

Automated support for mental health has a history that predates current LLMs. Early work demonstrated that even scripted, rule-based conversational agents could reduce depression and anxiety symptoms under controlled conditions:
11
showed that Woebot, a CBT-based chatbot, produced clinically meaningful reductions over two weeks in a randomized trial. The arrival of large, general-purpose language models has substantially expanded both the reach and the ambiguity of this use case. LLMs are now among the most commonly used mental health resources for adolescents, young adults, and diagnosed adults in the United States
(
21
;
31
;
39
)
, and users routinely assign relational personas to them when seeking support, most commonly framing the model as a close friend or a therapist
(
19
)
.

Evaluating whether these interactions are safe has proven difficult. Most safety research focuses on refusal of overtly harmful requests
(
43
)
, a framework that is well-suited for preventing explicit harms but cannot detect subtler risks. More recent behavioral work offers a starting point:
5
proposed BOLT, a computational framework for assessing LLM behavior across therapeutic approaches, finding that LLMs more closely resemble low-quality therapy than high-quality therapy on several dimensions. Our work complements this by focusing specifically on the interactional dynamics triggered by different help-seeking styles, and by grounding the measurement framework in interpersonal emotion regulation theory rather than therapeutic technique taxonomies.

2.2
Venting and Advice-Seeking in Online Communities

Reddit has become a central data source for computational research on emotional disclosure and mental health.
8
provided an early characterization of mental health discourse across Reddit communities, showing that the platform attracts a distinctive quality of social support, ranging from emotional to prescriptive, particularly in response to self-disclosing posts.
This body of work has established the value of Reddit as a naturalistic site for studying emotional support at scale, but it has largely treated help-seeking as a homogeneous category.

In the psychology literature, venting and advice-seeking are theoretically distinct. Venting is an emotion-focused, open-ended disclosure strategy oriented toward emotional release and social acknowledgment
(
29
;
24
)
, whereas advice-seeking is more regulated, solution-oriented, and future-directed
(
18
)
. These differences in communicative intent have real consequences for how responses should be calibrated:
40
showed that the effect of venting on emotional outcomes depends heavily on the quality of the social response received. Despite the theoretical maturity of this distinction, no prior computational work has examined venting and advice-seeking in direct contrast using a within-user design that controls for individual differences in language and distress expression. Our dataset addresses this gap.

2.3
Accommodation, Sycophancy, and Co-Rumination

Conversational accommodation, the tendency of interlocutors to align with each other’s linguistic register, provides the theoretical basis for expecting LLMs to mirror the emotional tone of their inputs
(
25
;
7
)

In the LLM literature, this mirroring tendency has been studied primarily under the label of sycophancy: a disposition to affirm the user’s expressed views and validate their emotional frame regardless of accuracy
(
36
;
10
;
28
)
. Sycophancy research has primarily examined factual domains, but in emotional support contexts the relevant risk is different.
30
identified co-rumination in peer relationships as a pattern in which validation and focused engagement with negative experiences co-occur with affect intensification and appraisal reinforcement, producing poorer mental health outcomes despite appearing supportive.
42
and
15
formalized the distinction between interpersonal responses that down-regulate distress and those that escalate it. Whether LLM accommodation in emotional contexts resembles co-rumination, producing supportive and escalatory responses simultaneously, is a question prior work has not addressed. Our measurement framework is designed to make this testable.

Prior computational work has primarily operationalized response quality as empathy
(
37
)
, establishing that theoretically grounded, multidimensional annotation is both tractable and meaningfully linked to user outcomes.
Our framework builds on this tradition while identifying a key limitation: measuring only supportive qualities like empathy leaves escalatory response features unmeasured. A response can score high on empathy while simultaneously amplifying distress, reinforcing negative appraisals, or endorsing blame attributions. Capturing this requires dimensions grounded not just in empathy theory but in co-rumination research and interpersonal emotion regulation theory
(
30
;
42
)
.
We use GPT-5.3 as annotator at scale, supported by evidence that LLM annotators approach human reliability on safety-sensitive tasks
(
2
)
—whether this holds across escalatory dimensions specifically is tested in our human study.

,

, or

From the filtered corpus we sampled 1,500 posts per category.

)

Drawing on

6
A Theory-Grounded Framework for Measuring Regulatory and Escalatory Response Behaviors

6.1
Dimension development

To move beyond binary safety metrics toward interactionally meaningful constructs, we developed a six-dimension ordinal annotation framework grounded in two complementary sources.
Theoretically, dimensions were derived from interpersonal emotion regulation research distinguishing regulatory from escalatory interpersonal responses
(
42
)
and co-rumination research
documenting how validation and affect intensification co-occur in peer support
(
30
)
.
Empirically, the choice of dimensions was anchored in the specific linguistic features of the venting register characterized in the Linguistic Registers section:
Appraisal Endorsement
captures the risk of mirroring absolutism and certitude;
Moral Alignment
, the risk of mirroring accusatory and conflict-laden framing;
Emotional Amplification
, the risk of mirroring high-arousal negative affect;
while
Cognitive Reappraisal
(cognitive reframing),
Emotional Validation
(acknowledgment of the user’s affective state), and
Regulatory Containment
(emotional stabilization) capture the stabilizing behaviors that register structurally elicits but a co-ruminative response withholds.
Each dimension was operationalized as a 0-4 ordinal scale with behaviorally anchored rubric descriptors.

6.2
LLM annotation procedure

Given scale (9,000 responses), manual annotation was infeasible.
We used GPT-5.3 as the primary annotator, prompted with detailed per-dimension rubrics as seen in Appendix B.
This approach is supported by a growing body of work demonstrating that LLM annotators achieve reliability comparable to trained human annotators across sensitive NLP tasks, including subjective and safety-sensitive domains closely related to mental health
(
2
;
28
)
.

8
Human Studies

We conducted two complementary human studies. The first used expert
annotators to validate the measurement framework. The second used
crowdsourced workers to examine how lay users evaluate LLM responses
across persona conditions.

8.1
Expert Annotation: Framework Validity

8.1.1
Method

The original rubric requires PhD-level expertise in clinical psychology,
consistent with evidence that frontier LLMs achieve annotation
reliability comparable to domain experts on subjective tasks

(
2
;
28
)
. We developed a simplified rubric that
preserves the six-dimensional structure and 0-4 ordinal scales but
replaces technical terminology with plain-language descriptions and
everyday anchor examples (See Appendix B). Two PhD researchers with expertise in psychology independently annotated 20 LLM responses using this rubric.

8.1.2
Results

We computed quadratic weighted Cohen’s
\kappa
between the mean expert scores and GPT annotations. Agreement between expert means and GPT annotations was substantial for the regulation composite (
\kappa=0.47
) and near-perfect for the escalation composite (

(
17
;
22
)
. This confirms that the six-dimensional framework, as operationalized by the LLM annotator, captures constructs that domain-expert human raters recognize and apply consistently.

8.2
Crowdsourced Lay Evaluation

8.2.1
Participants and design

We recruited 68 annotators via Prolific who were U.S. adults with at least a bachelor’s degree and English as their first language. Annotators evaluated responses to help-seeking Reddit posts using a custom interface that supported side-by-side comparison and optional text highlighting.

Each annotator was assigned one help-seeking message from a pool of 34 Reddit posts (17 venting, 17 advice-seeking) and rated all three corresponding LLM responses (default, friend, therapist). This within-annotator design controlled for individual rating tendencies when comparing personas. Across the study, each of the 102 responses received ratings from two independent annotators. Annotators were not informed that the responses were LLM-generated or that the source messages originated from Reddit.

8.2.2
Measures

9
General Discussion

9.1
Regulation and escalation increase together

Our central finding is that LLM responses do not trade off between
support and risk. They increase both simultaneously, especially in
response to venting. This coupling resembles co-rumination, where
emotional validation co-occurs with sustained focus on negative
experiences and affect intensification
(
30
)
. The
mismatch between perceived empathy and functional emotional outcome is precisely what makes it a latent safety risk: responses that feel supportive may still reinforce distress.

9.2
Persona conditioning steers the balance

Persona conditioning reveals that this alignment can be steered.
The friend persona increases both regulation and escalation, producing more emotionally engaged but also more amplifying responses.
In contrast, the therapist persona reduces escalation while maintaining high levels of regulation.
This is encouraging: it shows the balance can be shifted through simple prompt-level interventions, without architectural changes or fine-tuning.
Given that users, especially adolescents, often frame LLMs as friends when seeking mental health support
(
6
;
16
)
, the friend persona’s amplification effect raises a safety concern: the model’s tendency to prioritize relational warmth and agreement may contribute to co-ruminative dynamics, reinforcing maladaptive appraisals under the guise of empathy.

9.3
Users do not penalize the safer persona

Across 102 responses and 68 crowdsourced evaluators, the therapist
persona received ratings of desirability and helpfulness
indistinguishable from the friend and default conditions. This null
held within both venting and advice-seeking contexts. In venting
posts, where escalation risk is highest, therapist responses
trended toward higher helpfulness than friend responses, though
this did not reach significance. The result suggests that the UX
cost of adding a therapist-style system prompt to emotional support
applications may be low. Whether this generalizes to real-world
deployment, including extended multi-turn interactions and users in
genuine distress, warrants further study. With that caveat, the
present evidence gives product designers little basis for omitting
a therapist-style prompt on user experience grounds alone.

9.4
Escalation is harder to measure than regulation

The human studies reveal an important asymmetry in construct accessibility.
Regulation evaluation overlaps with everyday empathy perception, and domain experts and lay crowdworkers agree with the LLM almost equally well on default responses (
\kappa
= 0.47 vs. 0.55). Evaluating escalation, on the other hand, requires clinical concepts that are not part of lay intuition, and the expert-prolific gap is large (
\kappa
= 0.81 vs. 0.22).
The friend persona further reveals a specific blind spot in LLM annotation: the model over-credits relational warmth as genuine regulatory support.
If the LLM systematically inflates regulation scores for friend responses, the safety gap between friend and therapist personas
may be even larger than what was reported.

9.5
Empathy and sycophancy may be intertwined

Our findings also speak to the relationship between empathy and sycophancy in high-affect contexts
(
28
)
. Rather than treating empathy as uniformly desirable and sycophancy as a distinct failure mode, the co-occurrence of validation and escalation suggests they may be intertwined. While we do not directly measure user-perceived empathy or independently validate sycophancy, this should be interpreted as a hypothesis-generating observation, highlighting the challenge of disentangling supportive validation from escalatory agreement in model design and evaluation.

9.6
Limitations

Our results should be interpreted with several limitations in mind.
First, our annotation relies primarily on GPT-5.3 as annotator, using the same model family that generated the responses. The expert validation study provides independent support for the framework, but the risk of systematic bias remains.
Second, our analysis is limited to first-turn interactions. It is therefore unclear whether the observed regulatory–escalatory coupling would persist, attenuate, or intensify over longer multi-turn exchanges.
Third, our Reddit corpus provides naturalistic discourse, but LLM mental health tool users may differ from Reddit populations in communication style and need. In addition, Reddit populations are disproportionately young, English-speaking, and Western, and subreddit self-selection may exaggerate stylistic distinctions between conversational registers relative to broader help-seeking populations.
Fourth, the persona prompts are stylized experimental manipulations rather than naturalistic user inputs, limiting ecological validity. However, robustness analyses suggest that the observed therapist effect is not prompt-specific.

Finally, we do not measure real-world mental health outcomes. The framework identifies response properties associated with regulatory and escalatory dynamics. Whether these translate into downstream psychological effects requires longitudinal and clinical validation.

9.7
Ethical Considerations

This work examines LLM use in sensitive mental health contexts, and several ethical considerations warrant explicit attention.
Our findings highlight that seemingly empathetic responses may reinforce distress, underscoring that LLMs should not replace professional care.
Persona prompting that frames a model as a friend or therapist may encourage users to attribute expertise and relational commitment to systems that cannot provide sustained or accountable support. Over time, this could discourage help-seeking from qualified professionals, particularly among younger or socially isolated users who are also most likely to use friend-framed interactions and most susceptible to escalatory responses.

Risks are unlikely to be evenly distributed. Adolescents, highly distressed individuals, and those lacking support networks may be disproportionately harmed by over-validating or escalatory responses. Safety evaluation must therefore attend to high-risk subpopulations, not only average behavior.

We also note the dual-use potential of the annotation framework. Detailed rubrics for escalatory behavior could in principle guide optimization toward those same behaviors. Responsible use requires that the framework be applied in its intended evaluative context. Reddit data was collected under public API terms; all analyses are conducted at the aggregate level without identifying individuals. The corpus contains offensive language and descriptions of distress consistent with the naturalistic venting register; no personally identifiable information is retained or reported.

10
Conclusion

LLMs actively shape emotional exchanges in ways that refusal-based safety frameworks cannot detect. When responding to venting, they increase both regulatory and escalatory behaviors simultaneously, mirroring the structure of co-rumination: responses that feel empathetic may still reinforce distress.
Our framework makes this testable by treating Regulation and Escalation as independent dimensions rather than poles of a single scale. Our human studies show that escalation is harder for lay raters to detect than regulation, underscoring the need for expert-informed annotation in safety evaluation.
Persona conditioning shifts this balance meaningfully: therapist-style prompts reduce escalation while maintaining regulation, at no measurable cost to user experience. Given that friend framing is the most common real-world persona for mental health help-seeking, its elevated escalation is a population-level concern. Our framework provides a theory-informed, empirically validated lens for evaluating LLM safety in high-stakes emotional contexts, one that empathy metrics alone cannot replace.

Appendix C
Appendix C: Corpus Construction and Linguistic Analysis Methods

C.1
Corpus Construction

We collected original posts (submissions) and applied two filters: users must have (a) posted in at least one venting and one advice-seeking forum, and (b) maintained a posting rate of no more than one post per day on average, removing single-post users and high-frequency spam accounts.
This yielded 14,040 users with 92,217 posts in venting forums (mean = 6.56, SD = 12.78) and 86,641 posts in advice-seeking forums (mean = 6.17, SD = 9.28); descriptive statistics and corpus-level Cohen’s
d
values are reported in Table
1
.

Each record contains the post timestamp, subreddit, username, a unique post identifier, and the concatenated title and body text.
We derived two analysis variables:
is_venting
, a boolean flag indicating membership in a venting subreddit, and
userid_isventing
, a concatenation of username and
is_venting
that serves as the unit of analysis for within-user feature aggregation.

C.2
Differential Language Analysis

To quantify how language differs between a user’s venting and advice-seeking posts, we apply Differential Language Analysis (DLA;
34
), a framework for measuring associations between linguistic features and an outcome variable at the user level.
For each user, features are aggregated separately over their venting posts and their advice-seeking posts at the user-forum level.
Group differences are then quantified using Cohen’s
d
:

d\;=\;\frac{\,\bar{x}_{\,\text{vent}}\;-\;\bar{x}_{\,\text{advice}}\,}{\sigma_{\text{pooled}}}

(1)

where
\bar{x}_{\text{vent}}
and
\bar{x}_{\text{advice}}
are the mean feature values computed over users’ venting and advice-seeking posts respectively, and
\sigma_{\text{pooled}}
is the pooled standard deviation across both conditions.
Positive
d
indicates higher usage in venting; negative
d
indicates higher usage in advice-seeking.
All
p
-values are corrected for multiple comparisons using the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure.
The within-person structure of the analysis would allow each user to contribute observations to both conditions and negates systematic between-person confounds, such as individual differences in verbosity, topic interest, or base affect, and the estimated effects reflect
intra
-individual shifts in language as a function of intent, i,e., either to vent or seek advice.

Appendix D
Appendix D: Reproducibility, Compensation, and Ethics

Code for the DLA pipeline and annotation procedure is available upon request.
A screenshot of the annotation interface, full participant instructions, and the annotation data will be made available upon request.

Annotators were compensated at a rate of $20/hour; total participant compensation was approximately $500. Prolific participant IDs were de-identified prior to analysis, and no identifying information was retained or reported. The annotation task was determined to be exempt from IRB review because it involved no deception, no collection of sensitive participant data, and no foreseeable risk beyond exposure to potentially distressing content. Participants were informed in advance that posts could contain descriptions of emotional distress and were free to withdraw at any time.
