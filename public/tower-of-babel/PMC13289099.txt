Predictive variables for early detection of sepsis in adults admitted to intensive care units: protocol for a systematic review
Authors: Zahra Keshavarz, Farid Zand, Naeimehossadat Asmarian, Mahkameh Rafiee, Roghayeh Ershad Sarabi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Abstract AbstractIntroductionSepsis is a life-threatening condition in intensive care units (ICUs), where any delay in diagnosis and treatment can lead to organ dysfunction, prolonged hospital stay and increased mortality. Early identification of sepsis prior to its clinical manifestation may enable timely intervention and improve outcomes. This review aims to identify, synthesise and categorise predictor variables assessed in adult ICU patients prior to sepsis diagnosis.Methods/designThis protocol, in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis Protocols (PRISMA-P) guideline, will involve a comprehensive search of PubMed/Medline, Scopus, Embase, Web of Science, IEEE Xplore and Cochrane Library for English-language studies from database inception. The search is planned to be conducted between March 2026 and June 2026. We will include prospective, retrospective, cohort, case–control and cross-sectional studies, and both randomised and non-randomised designs. Additionally, we will consider secondary analysis of electronic health records, clinical registries and routinely acquired clinical data that determine risk factors and predictive variables that will help in the early identification of sepsis using machine learning, artificial intelligence, computational or statistical methods. Two independent reviewers will perform title and abstract screening followed by full-text review and data extraction. The risk of bias will be evaluated using appropriate tools depending on study design: the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale for observational studies, Cochrane Risk of Bias tool for randomised trials and PROBAST for prediction model studies. We will perform a narrative synthesis structured by variable-type, timing and performance metrics (area under the curve, sensitivity, ORs). Subgroup analyses will be conducted based on study design, sepsis definition, type of ICU (medical, surgical, mixed) and geographic area (high-income and low- and middle-income countries).Ethics and disseminationFindings from this systematic review will be disseminated through publication in a peer-reviewed journal and/or presentation at scientific conferences. Introduction

Description of the condition

Sepsis is a life-threatening clinical syndrome, which is evidenced by a dysregulated immune response to infection. As one of the major causes of mortality in intensive care units (ICUs), sepsis is a worldwide leading burden on healthcare systems; as the diagnosis is delayed, it can lead to multisystem organ failure, prolonged hospital stays, and increased healthcare costs.1 Although sepsis is a preventable and treatable condition, it continues to cause millions of deaths per year in ICUs around the world.1 2

Early detection of sepsis is critically important, but remains challenging due to early symptoms usually not being specific and obscured by other acute disorders.3 4 Even with updated protocols and improved guidelines, delays in diagnosis and treatment persist, contributing to worse clinical outcomes such as multiorgan dysfunction, longer hospital stays, increased healthcare costs, and higher mortality.5 6

Description of the intervention

In recent years, predictive analytics, particularly artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning (ML) techniques, have been increasingly employed to facilitate early detection of sepsis. These models leverage routinely collected clinical data including vital signs, laboratory results, as well as comorbidities to predict sepsis onset.7 8 However, studies indicate substantial heterogeneity in the predictive value, consistency and clinical applicability of these variables.7 8 Moreover, while several studies report promising results, there is currently no consensus regarding which early indicators or variables are most effective and can be applied in various ICU settings and patient populations.9

How the intervention might work

Prior systematic reviews have explored various aspects of sepsis prediction. Some have focused broadly on the general topic of risk factors,10 while others have concentrated more narrowly on the performance of predictive analytics solutions,11 or on specific feature engineering approaches and model performance.12 Critically, a review on predictor variables of sepsis at triage was limited to the paediatric age group only.13 While these efforts have advanced specific aspects (eg, model performance or paediatric triage), their predominant reliance on late-stage variables and narrow population scopes limits their applicability to early sepsis prediction in adults ICU population. Collectively, these gaps highlight the necessity for methodologically robust and generalisable approaches based on early measurable indicators in adult-centred ICU settings.

Although a prior systematic review by Hassan et al14 provides useful information on identifying predictor variables related to sepsis, it is restricted to the consideration of predictors in ML-based studies only. In particular, the study mainly focused on predicting infection-related, specifically bacterial-related sepsis, and thus neglected other possible clinical and environmental factors that might influence susceptibility or predisposition to the development of sepsis by an individual.

Why it is important to do this review

More recent systematic reviews published in 2024–2025 predominantly focus on evaluating predictive model performance, early warning scores or the algorithmic accuracy of AI-based sepsis detection systems in ICU settings.7 15 16 While these reviews offer valuable insights into model effectiveness, they largely adopt a model-centric perspective and do not systematically synthesise individual predictor variables across different analytical methodologies.

In contrast, the present review adopts a variable-centric and methodologically agnostic approach, aiming to identify and integrate discrete, measurable predictor variables that demonstrate early predictive value across AI/ML-based models, computational approaches or conventional statistical analyses. Rather than comparing algorithms or performance metrics, the focus is placed on predictor variables themselves, which are essential for clinical interpretability and real-time risk assessment.

Accordingly, this systematic review addresses this gap by examining studies that report risk factors and early predictive variables of sepsis in adults admitted to the ICU, irrespective of the underlying analytical framework. By emphasising early warning indicators at the variable level, the findings aim to provide an integrated overview of predictive variables relevant to early sepsis detection and support the development and validation of clinically applicable, real-time risk stratification tools and ML-based prediction models for adult critically ill populations.

Methods

This systematic review will follow the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis Protocols (PRISMA-P) Protocol guidelines in its design and reporting so that it can establish methodological transparency and scientific rigour. This protocol has been registered with the PROSPERO (registration ID: CRD420251113781, registered on 21 July 2025). Findings from this systematic review will be disseminated through publication in a peer-reviewed journal and/or presentation at scientific conferences. The data used will not include individual patient data, so ethical approval is not required.

Inclusion criteria for study selection

Types of studies

Eligible studies will include both randomised and non-randomised designs. Prospective, retrospective, cohort, case–control and cross-sectional studies, as well as secondary analyses of electronic health records (EHRs), clinical registries or routinely collected clinical data, will all be included. Studies must identify risk factors or predictive variables for early detection of sepsis using ML, AI, computational or statistical methods.

Types of participants

The study population must be adult patients (≥18 years) admitted to ICUs for at least 24 hours; this allows enough time to observe sepsis prior to its appearance.

Types of interventions

In our study, we will consider predictive variables identified using AI/ML methods as well as those derived from traditional statistical methods. This approach is adopted since each framework has unique advantages: traditional methods are highly interpretable and have a strong theoretical foundation, whereas AI/ML methods can identify complex non-linear patterns and detect variables that conventional linear models may fail to identify. Through the narrative synthesis of the results, we aim to present a comprehensive and complementary understanding of the predictive factors under investigation, without positioning the two approaches in a competitive or comparative framework.

Search methods for identification of studies

Electronic searches

All the relevant studies will be identified through a comprehensive and systematic search strategy. The following electronic databases will be searched: PubMed/Medline, Scopus, Embase, Web of Science, IEEE Xplore and the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (CENTRAL). The search strategy will be iteratively constructed and will be a combination of controlled vocabulary terms (eg, MeSH words in PubMed, Emtree words in Embase) combined with free-text words to maximise sensitivity. A comprehensive literature search extending back to database inception will be conducted between March 2026 and June 2026. Data extraction and synthesis will follow, with the final manuscript expected to be completed by August 2026.

The significant phrases will be the following:

Sepsis: ‘sepsis’, ‘Pyemia’

Detection and predictive early factors: ‘prognosis’, ‘prediction’, ‘early detection’, ‘risk factor’, ‘early diagnosis’, ‘predictor’, ‘predictive’

Methodologies: ‘machine learning’, ‘artificial intelligence’, ‘computational model’, ‘statistical model’, ‘algorithm’

Setting and population: ‘intensive care unit’, ‘ICU’, ‘critical care’

Boolean operators will be applied (‘OR’ within concepts, ‘AND’ between concepts) along with database-specific filters to limit results to English-language studies only. All records will be imported into Microsoft Excel for deduplication, and the search strategy will be peer-reviewed by a medical librarian. The draft search strategy for PubMed is provided in online supplemental file 1.

Searching other resources

We will investigate the reference lists of identified publications for additional articles. We will search electronic databases for existing systematic reviews published in the last 5 years that are potentially relevant to this systematic review, for reference mining to identify additional primary studies; however, these reviews will not be included in the data synthesis.

Data collection and analysis

Selection of studies

Once the search is completed, all retrieved records from electronic databases will be imported into reference management software (eg, EndNote, Zotero or Rayyan) for systematic deduplication. Subsequently, the screening process will commence.

Selection of studies will be conducted in two phases by two independent reviewers:

Phase I: Title and abstract screening: The reviewers will independently screen titles and abstracts against predefined eligibility criteria; this will help identify potentially relevant studies and decrease the number of records for full-text review

Phase II: Full text review: Studies that pass the first-stage screening will undergo full-text scrutiny regarding methodological soundness, relevance and data quality. If studies do not meet the conditions for inclusion, the judgments made will be documented clearly along with the reasons for exclusion.

Any discrepancies between reviewers will be resolved by structured discussions to reach consensus. Failing that, a third reviewer will adjudicate. The study selection process is summarised in the PRISMA flow diagram (online supplemental figure 1).

Inclusion criteria require studies to define sepsis according to standard guidelines such as Sepsis-1, Sepsis-2, Sepsis-3, International Classification of Diseases codes or Centers for Disease Control and Prevention definitions, and report variables measured before diagnosis. Only full texts in the English language are eligible for inclusion.

Randomised controlled trials (RCTs) will be evaluated using the Cochrane Risk of Bias tool (RoB 2) across five domains: randomisation process, deviations from intended interventions, missing outcome data, outcome measurement, and selection of reported results. Risk judgments will be categorised as ‘low’, ‘some concerns’ and ‘high’.

Data synthesis

Given the expected heterogeneity in sepsis definitions, timing of measurements, ICU settings and analytical approaches, quantitative meta-analysis is not planned. Instead, a structured narrative synthesis will be conducted, supported by tabular and graphical summaries to facilitate comparison and transparency.

Grading the quality of evidence

The overall quality and certainty of the evidence will be evaluated by considering the risk of bias assessments and the methodological rigour of the included studies. Evidence will be interpreted in light of the quality appraisal performed using the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOS) for observational studies, the Cochrane Risk of Bias tool (RoB 2) for RCTs and the PROBAST tool for prediction model studies. The strength and consistency of the identified predictor variables will be examined across studies, taking into account study design, methodological limitations and the robustness of reported performance metrics.

Discussion

As a protocol for an upcoming systematic review, this work will establish a variable-centric framework to synthesise evidence across these distinct methodological domains: AI/ML-derived features, traditional statistical predictors and established clinical risk factors. While seminal reviews that evaluate clinical warning scores or AI-driven predictive systems for sepsis provide valuable insights into predictive tools, they predominantly focus on aggregate model metrics rather than systematically cataloguing discrete, preonset predictor variables measured prior to clinical diagnosis.7 15 16

This study is not aimed at ranking predictive algorithms, but rather at generating a clinically actionable, methodologically agnostic inventory of discrete, measurable variables (eg, dynamic vital sign trends or specific laboratory derangements) that can be consistently identified across diverse analytical frameworks and integrated into ICU monitoring infrastructures. By focusing on variables rather than models, this synthesis seeks to inform and support the development of electronic early warning systems, enhance clinician surveillance strategies and facilitate the design of data-driven clinical decision support tools by health information technology specialists.

The findings will highlight well-known predictor variables, as well as new indicators, of ML models or statistical approaches, as well as identify main gaps in existing research, including inconsistent timing of measurement, limited external validation and insufficient consideration of socioenvironmental determinants.

Limitations include restriction to English-language publications, and the inability to perform meta-analysis due to methodological heterogeneity. However, the PRISMA-P protocol, a transparent research process, a high-quality assessment and an inclusive search strategy enhance the credibility and reproducibility of this work

This review will add value to the area of precision critical care where timely, patient-specific insights can be used to facilitate earlier clinical intervention with improved patient outcomes. The results will be disseminated through publication in a peer-reviewed journal and presentation at international conferences related to critical care, medical informatics and sepsis research.
