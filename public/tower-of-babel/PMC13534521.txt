Pythagorean fuzzy time series approach based on hybrid high-order artificial neural network for nonlinear time series forecasting
Authors: Ozge Gundogdu, Erol Egrioglu, Eren Bas
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractFuzzy time series forecasting methods rely on the fuzzification of time-series observations through membership functions and the subsequent use of fuzzy-valued variables in the forecasting process. Although Pythagorean fuzzy time series models provide a richer representation of uncertainty than classical and intuitionistic fuzzy approaches, existing methods still face important challenges, including high-dimensional input structures generated by membership and non-membership values, a lack of systematic lag selection mechanisms, and limited capability to model complex nonlinear relationships. To address these limitations, this study employs an artificial neural network with a hybrid architecture combining a modified Pi-Sigma artificial neural network and simple exponential smoothing to capture both the linear and nonlinear dynamics and model the relationship in Pythagorean fuzzy time series. To mitigate the high-dimensional input structure arising from membership and non-membership values, principal component analysis was used for input dimension reduction and orthogonalization. Particle swarm optimization was used to estimate all the parameters in the Pythagorean fuzzy time series method. To evaluate its performance, the proposed forecasting algorithm was assessed against existing approaches in the literature using 10 distinct sub-time series generated via a rolling window approach from the S&P 500 and Dow-Jones 30 stock market composite indices, and the proposed method’s superior performance was demonstrated experimentally. From an artificial intelligence perspective, these contributions jointly define a novel Pythagorean fuzzy time series forecasting framework that advances hybrid neural network modelling and optimization, while from an engineering perspective, they provide a systematic and implementable methodology for constructing accurate forecasting models for complex nonlinear time series, particularly in financial and other engineering-oriented applications.Keywords: Pythagorean fuzzy time series, Modified pi-sigma artificial neural network, Simple exponential smoothing, Principal component analysis, Particle swarm optimizationSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

The fuzzy time series (FTS) modeling approach has been widely used in forecasting problems because it reveals the uncertainty inherent in time series, eliminates the limitations of traditional time series methods, and requires no assumptions. FTS models and modeling approaches employed in forecasting problems, along with the associated limitations of these methods, have been discussed concisely in the literature1–3.

The concept of FTS, utilized in time series forecasting, originated from the adaptation of fuzzy set theory, originally introduced in4, to time series data, as presented in5. In this context, historical time series were transformed into linguistic values, and fuzzy relations were employed to model the resulting FTS. Furthermore, two distinct FTS models, namely, the first-order time-invariant model and the first-order time-variant model, were proposed to address forecasting problems, and the forecasting procedures are detailed in6,7. In time-variant FTS models, the relationships within the FTS framework vary over time, and capturing this variability in forecasting necessitates the use of different model structures. In this way, while such models offer increased flexibility and adaptability, they also entail higher computational complexity. Although time-invariant FTS models are frequently used in the literature, time-variant models are not commonly used. Nevertheless, studies such as8–15 exemplify the development and application of time-variant FTS models and related forecasting methods.

The estimation steps in the FTS modeling approach can be expressed as determining the interval lengths in which the universal set will be divided, the definition of linguistic terms for intervals, fuzzification of time series data, determination of fuzzy relationships and relationship groups between the fuzzified time series data, defuzzification, and forecasting computation. The procedures performed in the remarked steps significantly affected the forecasting performance and accuracy. The FTS model introduced in5 was refined through various enhancements aimed at increasing forecasting accuracy and ease of computation. A new method based on the FTS introduced in5,6 was proposed in16. In this method, fuzzy relationship tables are used instead of operators for the fuzzy set in the process of determining fuzzy relationships. This approach has formed the basis for later studies by simplifying the construction of fuzzy relationships and making the modeling process in FTS forecasting more efficient and understandable. In the first step of the FTS modeling approach, the universal set is defined as a closed interval and is divided according to a fixed interval length in the5–7,16,17 studies. However18, stated that the forecasting accuracy can change depending on the interval length. In FTS modeling, the forecasting performance is aimed to be improved by determining the interval length based on the mean and distribution of the data. The determination of the interval length and number may cause differences in the forecasting results by affecting all fuzzy relationship identification processes. In this context, several studies have focused on determining the optimal interval length and number, as demonstrated in18–27. Optimization-based approaches have also been employed to improve fuzzy partitioning and forecasting performance. Beyond interval optimization, several studies have integrated metaheuristic optimization techniques into FTS frameworks to enhance forecasting accuracy28. proposed a variational weighted fuzzy time series model optimized using particle swarm optimization and reported improved forecasting accuracy across different benchmark datasets29. proposed a forecasting methodology that combines fuzzy particle swarm optimization with reprogrammed large language models to improve prediction performance.

A novel formulation for high-order FTS models was introduced in30, targeting enhanced forecasting performance and improved computational efficiency. Subsequently31, introduced a novel high-order FTS model along with a forecasting algorithm aimed at enhancing the performance of first-order FTS models. Comparative analyses with widely recognized methods in the academic literature have demonstrated that higher-order models yield more accurate results than first-order models, which consider fuzzy relationships only one period ahead in forecasting time series. The high-order FTS model provides complex relationships in real-world data to be revealed more clearly by handling the relationships comprehensively. This feature of high-order FTS has been investigated by32–43 and used for time series forecasting in different fields. Although the high-order FTS approach improves the forecasting accuracy, it also increases the model complexity and computational burden.

In FTS models based on fuzzy set theory introduced by4, only the membership degree of data points is taken into account during the fuzzification process. In this sense, uncertainty cannot be fully reflected in the model44,45. proposed intuitionistic fuzzy sets as an extended form of classical fuzzy set theory to overcome this limitation. According to the intuitionistic fuzzy set approach, there are membership and non-membership degrees to the set for each element in the universal set, and it provides a more enhanced representation of this uncertainty. In addition, the nondeterminacy degree of an element is defined. Intuitionistic fuzzy sets have also been adapted to time series analysis due to their capacity to provide more accurate and comprehensive solutions for decision-making under uncertainty. In46, a novel approach to perform fuzzy inference using intuitionistic fuzzy systems was proposed, and it was indicated that this approach could also be applied to time series forecasting. In47, an intuitionistic fuzzy model and forecasting method with simplified computational procedures were developed by utilizing the intuitionistic fuzzy set approach to identify fuzzy relationships in time series forecasting. Similarly48, proposed an intuitionistic FTS forecasting method that incorporates both intuitionistic fuzzification and defuzzification stages. In this way, the uncertainty inherent in time series data can be modeled more effectively. Furthermore, studies49–56 employed the intuitionistic fuzzy set approach to better capture uncertainty and increase the forecasting precision of time series models. Building upon these developments57, proposed a new algorithm based on intuitionistic FTS, focusing on forecasting nonstationary time series. Subsequently58, presented a multifactor high-order intuitionistic FTS model and employed a fuzzy clustering algorithm to generate unequal intervals in the analysis. Furthermore59, proposed a high-order intuitionistic FTS forecasting model and method in which fuzzy relationships were identified using a Pi–Sigma neural network60. developed a mixed-order intuitionistic fuzzy time series forecasting method integrating a novel artificial neural network architecture with the grey wolf optimization algorithm.

In light of this development, based on the intuitionistic fuzzy sets approach, picture fuzzy sets were presented in61 as an extension of classical fuzzy and intuitionistic fuzzy sets. Thereafter62, proposed a new concept within the context of picture fuzzy sets. This approach allows for the modeling of situations that involve additional response options such as ‘abstention’ and ‘rejection’, in addition to conventional responses such as ‘yes’ and ‘no’. According to this approach, the degrees of positive, negative, and neutral membership and refusal to belong to a cluster are calculated for each element. The definition of uncertainty that is more flexible and extensive is obtained by using the degree of abstention as distinct from the intuitionistic fuzzy sets. From this perspective, picture fuzzy sets provide more information about the data and enhance the overall modeling capabilities. Building on this foundation63,64, proposed hybrid forecasting methods based on picture fuzzy set theory and evaluated their performance in the context of weather nowcasting. In line with the uncertainty definition provided by picture fuzzy sets65, introduced the concept of a picture FTS. In the present study, a high-order picture FTS model and a forecasting algorithm are proposed. In66, a high-order multivariate picture FTS forecasting model and its solution method were proposed, combining picture fuzzy set theory with a Pi–Sigma artificial neural network (ANN) for time series forecasting. Similarly67, presented a hybrid picture FTS forecasting model that integrates the picture fuzzy set approach with a new type of particle swarm optimization. Nevertheless, certain methodological limitations can be identified. Despite the innovative integration, the study does not propose a specific method for determining the hyperparameters of the model, nor does it employ any test data to assess its forecasting performance. Instead, the evaluation is limited to in-sample forecasting, which restricts the generalizability of the findings.

Although recent studies have explored time series forecasting using ambiguous set theory and quantum optimization algorithms68–70, these approaches mainly focus on uncertainty representation and fuzzy partition optimization. In contrast, the present study concentrates on improving the predictive capability of Pythagorean fuzzy time series through dimensionality reduction, hybrid neural modelling, and metaheuristic parameter estimation.

In parallel with these studies aimed at enhancing the modeling capabilities of fuzzy sets71–73, introduced the concept of Pythagorean fuzzy subsets and Pythagorean membership degrees, which are a more comprehensive version of intuitionistic fuzzy set membership degrees. In the Pythagorean fuzzy set, for any element in the universal set, the membership degree (μ), non-membership degree (ν), and hesitation degree (π) must satisfy the condition . This condition ensures that the square sum of the membership and non-membership degrees does not exceed one. It provides a wider search space than intuitionistic fuzzy sets, where the sum is linear (i.e., ), thus allowing for more flexibility in representing uncertainty.

Following this advancement74, introduced n-Pythagorean fuzzy sets to provide a more accurate expression of fuzziness by extending the search space of membership degrees. n-Pythagorean fuzzy sets can be described as a generalized form of intuitionistic fuzzy sets and Pythagorean fuzzy sets. In the n-Pythagorean fuzzy sets, for any element in the universal set, the membership degree (μ), non-membership degree (ν), and hesitation degree (π) must satisfy the condition , where n ≥ 1 is a real number. This generalized condition allows for a more flexible modeling of uncertainty compared to the intuitionistic fuzzy set (n = 1) and Pythagorean fuzzy set (n = 2) cases75. proposed a FTS model and forecasting method grounded in the fundamental properties of n-Pythagorean fuzzy sets. In their approach, the n-Pythagorean fuzzy c-means algorithm was employed for the fuzzification of data points and segmentation of the universal set. Additionally, an improved Markov weighted estimation method was utilized in the defuzzification phase of the FTS to enhance the forecasting performance of the model. More recently76, proposed a Pythagorean fuzzy time series forecasting method based on a wolf-raven optimization algorithm for partition. Their results demonstrated that optimization-based partitioning strategies can significantly improve forecasting accuracy by enhancing the quality of fuzzy partitions.

Fundamentally, the main differences between fuzzy sets arise from the definition of membership degrees and the way uncertainty is formulated. In practice, the selection of the appropriate fuzzy set approach to a given problem is crucial for both estimation accuracy and a precise representation of uncertainty. These conceptual differences directly influence the construction and performance of fuzzy time series forecasting models. Despite significant advancements in uncertainty modeling, a comprehensive review of the current literature reveals that several critical research gaps remain unaddressed in the domain of fuzzy time series forecasting.

Pythagorean fuzzy time series models improve uncertainty representation through membership and non-membership degrees; however, existing approaches still suffer from several methodological limitations. The simultaneous use of membership and non-membership values generates high-dimensional input structures that may increase the model complexity and reduce the forecasting efficiency. Second, existing Pythagorean fuzzy time series studies generally lack systematic and statistically grounded procedures for selecting lagged variables, leading to potential model misspecifications and suboptimal forecasting performance. Third, most existing approaches have limited capability to capture the complex nonlinear relationships inherent in real-world time series, particularly in financial and engineering applications. Consequently, there remains a need for a comprehensive Pythagorean fuzzy time series forecasting method that can simultaneously address dimension reduction, lag selection, and nonlinear modeling within a unified methodology.

The motivation for this study is to present an effective Pythagorean FTS method based on Pythagorean fuzzy sets. This study proposes and evaluates a Pythagorean FTS approach that is based on artificial neural networks for the first time in the literature. By integrating neural network-based learning mechanisms into the Pythagorean fuzzy time series structure, the study aims to enhance the modeling capability for complex nonlinear time series and to extend the applicability of Pythagorean fuzzy set theory to advanced forecasting problems.

Many tools previously proposed in the literature were used in developing the proposed method. These tools are mathematical and statistical tools, which are briefly introduced in this section.

Autocorrelation and partial autocorrelations

The most effective method for investigating the internal relationship of a time series is to examine the autocorrelation and partial autocorrelation coefficients. Autocorrelation coefficients are important tools that reveal the internal relationships within a time series. Partial autocorrelations, on the other hand, are a type of partial correlation coefficient calculated by fixing the effect of the lagged variables in between when the step length of the calculated autocorrelation coefficient is 2 or more. Autocorrelation coefficients represent the relationship between the error term and lagged variables, while partial autocorrelation coefficients can be used to determine the number of autoregressive lags. The number of lags in a linear autoregressive model can be determined by partial autocorrelation coefficients that are not equal to zero. This approach, which is used for linear models, can also be used as a preliminary tool in nonlinear modeling. The autocorrelation coefficients are calculated using Eq. Hypothesis tests can be used to determine whether the autocorrelation and partial autocorrelation coefficients are different from zero for the population. Although many test statistics with a chi-square distribution are defined in the literature, an easier approach is to use the standard errors of the autocorrelation and partial autocorrelation. For a white noise process with all autocorrelations equal to zero, the variance of the partial autocorrelations is 1/n. Therefore, partial autocorrelations outside the limits can be considered to be different from zero.

Principal component analysis

In multivariate statistical analyses, high correlations between independent variables and a large number of independent variables cause the models created to be ineffective and to work with too many parameters, resulting in low statistics in the degrees of freedom used in model testing. In principal component analysis, new variables consisting of linear combinations of independent variables that can represent a large portion of the total variation are obtained by utilizing the eigenvalues of the covariance or correlation matrix. These linear components, which are fewer than the total number of variables, are also uncorrelated, allowing models with fewer parameters to be used as the inputs. Particle swarm optimization

Particle swarm optimization is an artificial intelligence optimization algorithm that mimics the foraging methods of bird flocks. In this algorithm, a set of candidate solutions for the problem is used, and this set employs the intelligent strategies of a bird flock. Just like in a flock of birds, the candidate solutions use each other’s information to determine their new positions in the search space. Each candidate solution has a position and the current speed at which it uses to change this position. When a candidate solution changes its position, it uses the memory created by the steps it takes in the search space and the shared memory of all candidate solutions. While both memories play a critical role in determining the speed of each candidate solution, the weighting of each memory is determined randomly in order to explore different points in the search space and reduce the risk of getting stuck in a local optimum. Simple exponential smoothing

One of the most basic forecasting methods is simple exponential smoothing, where forecasts are a weighted average of the previous true value and the previous forecast. The simple exponential smoothing method works by assigning more weight to the observations closest to the period for which a forecast is to be obtained. The proposed pythagorean fuzzy time series forecasting method study

In this study, a new Pythagorean FTS method is proposed. The proposed method can be considered a new generation forecasting method. The process of obtaining forecasts is based on revealing the nonlinear mathematical function between the forecasts and the inputs determined in relation to the forecasts. Inputs with linear or nonlinear relationships with the forecast may be deterministic functions of time, lags in errors produced by the system, lagged variables in the time series, or nonlinear transformations of all these variables. Determining what inputs will be used in the forecasting method is one of the key factors influencing the forecasting method’s performance. Therefore, one of the issues studied and resolved in this study is the selection of method inputs. In the proposed method, membership and non-membership values, which are nonlinear combinations of lagged variables, have been added to the system using Pythagorean fuzzy clustering. In determining lagged variables, the method of identifying significant partial autocorrelation coefficients was preferred. Thanks to this method, lagged variables with the least linear relationship were included in the model, and these variables were used in nonlinear modeling. Although this approach is not optimal, it can be used as an effective method for reducing the number of inputs. Another important issue is the high correlation between the inputs of the method. The high correlation of commonly used inputs shows that it is possible to work with fewer inputs. In this study, a solution was developed by decoupling the inputs associated with principal component analysis and reducing the dimension. Estimating the nonlinear function between inputs and predictions is the most challenging part of the modeling process. A new hybrid ANN is proposed for this task. Since the proposed ANN is combined with a simple exponential smoothing method, it is a mixture of both complex and simple models in this study. Unlike other FTS in the literature, this approach combines the strengths of classical and contemporary modeling methods in modeling. The optimization problem that arose in the proposed method was solved using particle swarm optimization. In the literature, FTS is defined in different ways. This study is based on the definition of Pythagorean FTS given in75. The Pythagorean FTS forecasting method is implemented using a newly defined forecasting model, which is described below.

Conclusions and discussion

Alternative forecasting methods to statistical forecasting methods are intensively discussed in the academic literature. In fuzzy set-based forecasting methods, a fully systematic approach is required for the selection of model inputs and hyperparameters. A novel prediction algorithm based on Pythagorean fuzzy sets is presented in this study. In the proposed Pythagorean FTS forecasting method, a new hybrid ANN used in modeling fuzzy relationships is presented. This neural network is a feedback and combined ANN that incorporates a simple exponential smoothing method, which is a classic forecasting method. One of the most important problems in fuzzy set-based prediction approaches is the use of large input data provided by membership values in modeling. In the proposed method, instead of using large data such as membership and non-membership values, principal component analysis is used to include smaller and unrelated data in the ANN. In the proposed method, a validity data set-based approach is used for hyperparameter selection, thereby eliminating the influence of hyperparameters in evaluating the method’s performance and ensuring that the method’s performance is presented in a fairer manner. In evaluating the performance of the proposed method, two distinct stock market datasets were employed. The rolling window approach was preferred to obtain different time series from stock market data. When the obtained results are examined, it is clear that the predicted performance of the proposed method is the best in each series compared to the compared methods for the S&P 500 series and has a very good performance for the Dow-Jones 30 series. Although statistically different prediction forms are obtained between the compared methods in the S&P 500 time series applications, it is evident that the performance differences in the Dow-Jones 30 series are statistically insignificant. It is observed that the proposed method produces promising results in both data set applications. It is clear that the input selection based on partial autocorrelations saves the researchers from a significant parameter selection problem and creates an effective input selection method. In future studies, it is planned to transform the proposed method into a robust method that can also work in the presence of outliers. In addition, it seems possible to develop the proposed method for high-order n-Pythagorean FTS (n > 2), which is another type of Pythagorean FTS and is another future research topic.
