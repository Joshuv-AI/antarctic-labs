Abstract
Synthesizing controllers for real-time systems under both timing uncertainty and adversarial environments requires exploring prohibitively large symbolic state spaces. While zone inclusion checking has been applied to Parametric Timed Games, more aggressive abstractions from the Parametric Timed Automata and Timed Games literature—double inclusion, zone merging, hull abstractions, and location-based abstraction—have not yet been lifted to the parametric game setting. We define a general abstraction framework for Parametric Timed Games, instantiate it with each of the aforementioned abstractions, and prove that the framework preserves correctness of parameter synthesis and winning strategies. Experimental results on an established production cell benchmark and a novel adversarial IoT case study show that the abstractions significantly improve scalability, solving instances previously intractable for existing techniques.

Introduction

State-space abstractions have proven effective for scaling the verification of parametric real-time systems, but a general abstraction framework is lacking for the game-theoretic setting where a controller must win against an adversary.
Timed automata (TA)
[
1
]
provide a formal model for real-time systems, extended by
Parametric Timed Automata (PTA)
[
2
]
, which replace concrete timing constants
with unspecified parameters.
PTA support
parameter synthesis
: computing the set of parameter valuations under which a given property holds.
Timed Games (TG)
[
27
,
12
]
complement this by introducing a two-player structure,
distinguishing
controllable
actions (chosen by a controller) from
uncontrollable
ones
(chosen by an adversary), and asking whether a winning strategy exists for the controller.
Parametric Timed Games (PTG)
[
23
,
24
]

combine both settings: they synthesize the set of parameters under which the controller can guarantee winning.
An on-the-fly PTG reachability algorithm was implemented in
[
16
]

and extended to full controller synthesis in
[
17
]
.

PTG algorithms suffer from
state-space explosion
:
the state space is explored symbolically using
parametric zones
, convex constraints over clocks and parameters. Reducing the number of zones while preserving correctness is the central challenge addressed in this paper.
While zone inclusion checking has been applied to PTG
[
16
]
,
more aggressive abstractions remain unexplored:
double inclusion and zone merging
[
4
,
6
]
from the PTA setting
and location-based abstraction
[
22
]
from the TG setting.
Lifting these to PTG is non-trivial:
the PTA techniques must be shown to preserve winning strategies, not just reachability,
while the TG technique must be extended to handle parameters.

In the non-parametric setting, extrapolation techniques such as LU-bounds

are widely used zone abstractions for timed automata, e.g. in

Zone extrapolation was first adapted to the parametric setting in

For PTA, zone merging was introduced in

Preliminaries

A Parametric Timed Game (PTG) is a structure based on timed automata (TA). Like classical automata, it has locations connected by discrete transitions. It also has clocks.
Locations are associated a condition on clock valuations (invariant) that must
be satisfied while staying in the location.
An action in a timed automaton is either to take a discrete transition or to let some
time pass.
Discrete transitions have a guard that must be
satisfied in order to take the transition. In a parametric setting, these conditions
use linear terms over clocks and parameters. Parameters are unspecified but constant during a run. A discrete transition has a subset of clocks which are reset when the transition is taken.

A
clock valuation
is a function
v_{X}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{X}
assigning a non-negative real value to each clock.
A
parameter valuation

v_{P}\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}^{P}
assigns a non-negative rational value to each parameter.
A
valuation of a game
is a pair
v=(v_{X},v_{P})
.
The set of all valuations of the game is denoted

V=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{X}\times\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}^{P}
.
A
linear term
over
P
is a term defined by the following grammar:

plt\;:=\;k\;|\;kp\;|\;plt+plt

where
k\in\mathbb{Q}
and
p\in P
.

Zones
allow for capturing a set of valuations in a game.
The set of
parametric zones

\mathcal{Z}(X,P)
is the set of formulas
defined inductively by the following grammar:

\phi\;:=\;\top\;|\;\phi\land\phi\;|\;x\sim plt\;|\;x-y\sim plt\;|\;plt\sim 0

where
x,y\in X
,
{\sim}\in\{<;\leq;=;\geq;>\}
and
plt
is a linear term over
P
.

In a two-player timed game, discrete transitions are partitioned
between controllable transitions and uncontrollable (environment) transitions.

Definition 1
(PTG)

A
Parametric Timed Game
is a tuple of the form

G=(\mathbb{L},X,P,Act,T_{c},T_{u},\ell_{0},\mathit{Inv})
such that

•

,

,

,

,

.

•

(

) and
uncontrollable
(

) ones, where

is the set of

of the form

where:

\ell

\ell^{\prime}
are source

•

Function

With

2.1
Semantics of Parametric Timed Games

A
state
of a PTG consists of a location and a valuation of clocks and parameters.
Transitions modify clock valuations by letting time pass or resetting clocks.

Let
v=(v_{X},v_{P})
be a valuation of the game
and
\delta\geq 0
a delay.

\forall x\in X:(v_{X}+\delta)(x)=v_{X}(x)+\delta

and

v+\delta=(v_{X}+\delta,v_{P})
.
Let

Y\subseteq X
.

v_{X}[Y:=0]
is the valuation obtained by
resetting the clocks
in
Y
,
i.e.:

\forall x\in Y:v_{X}[Y:=0](x)=0
and

\forall x\in X\setminus Y:v_{X}[Y:=0](x)=v_{X}(x)

, and

v[Y:=0]=(v_{X}[Y:=0],v_{P})
.

The semantics of a Parametric Timed Game is defined
as a timed transition system on states, with timed and discrete transitions, and a winning condition.
A
state
of a PTG is a pair
(\ell,v)
where
\ell

is a location and
v
a valuation of the game satisfying its invariant:

v\models\mathit{Inv}(\ell)
. The
state space
is then

\mathbb{S}=\{(\ell,v)\in\mathbb{L}\times V\;|\;v\models\mathit{Inv}(\ell)\}={\bigcup}_{\ell\in\mathbb{L}}\;\{\ell\}\times\mathit{Inv}(\ell)
.
Let
\delta\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be a time delay. A
timed transition
is
a relation
{\to^{\delta}}\in\mathbb{S}\times\mathbb{S}
s.t.

\forall(\ell,v),(\ell^{\prime},v^{\prime})\in\mathbb{S}:(\ell,v)\to^{\delta}(\ell^{\prime},v^{\prime})
iff

\ell=\ell^{\prime}
and
v^{\prime}=v+\delta
.
Let
t=(\ell,g,a,Y,\ell^{\prime})\in T

be a transition. A
discrete transition
is a relation

{\to^{t}}\in\mathbb{S}\times\mathbb{S}
s.t.

\forall(\ell,v),(\ell^{\prime},v^{\prime})\in\mathbb{S}:(\ell,v)\to^{t}(\ell^{\prime},v^{\prime})
iff

v\models g
and
v^{\prime}=v[Y:=0]
.

States are grouped in symbolic states, similar to valuations grouped in zones.

Definition 2
(symbolic state)

A
symbolic state
of a PTG is a pair
\xi=(\ell,Z)
where
\ell

is a location and
Z
a zone of the game satisfying its invariant:

Z\models\mathit{Inv}(\ell)
.
The symbolic state
(\ell,Z)
represents the subset of states
\{\ell\}\times Z\subseteq\mathbb{S}
.

Definition 3
(temporal zone operators)

For a zone
Z
, its
time-forward closure
and

time-backward closure
are:

\displaystyle Z^{\nearrow}

\displaystyle:=\{\,v+\delta\mid v\in Z,\,\delta\geq 0\,\},

\displaystyle Z^{\swarrow}

\displaystyle:=\{\,v\mid\exists\,\delta\geq 0:v+\delta\in Z\,\}.

Definition 4
(discrete zone operators)

Let
t=(\ell,g,a,Y,\ell^{\prime})\in T
.
For a zone
Z
:

\displaystyle\mathit{Succ}(t,Z)

\displaystyle:=\{\,v[Y:=0]\mid v\in Z,\,v\models g\,\},

\displaystyle\mathit{Pred}(t,Z)

\displaystyle:=\{\,v\mid v\models g,\;v[Y:=0]\in Z\,\}.

Definition 5
(safe temporal predecessor)

For zones
W
and
U
:

\mathit{SafePred}(W,U):=\bigl\{\,v\;\bigm|\;\exists\,\delta\geq 0:\;v+\delta\in W\;\land\;\forall\,\delta^{\prime}\in[0,\delta]:v+\delta^{\prime}\notin U\,\bigr\}.

That is,
v\in\mathit{SafePred}(W,U)
if time can elapse from
v
to reach
W

without passing through
U
.

Remark 1
(zone operators on symbolic states)

The operators above are defined on zones (sets of valuations).
They lift to symbolic states by intersecting with the location invariant
(so that the zone of the result satisfies the invariant, as required by
definition

2
):
for
\xi=(\ell,Z)
and
t=(\ell,g,a,Y,\ell^{\prime})
:

\displaystyle\xi^{\nearrow}

\displaystyle:=(\ell,\;Z^{\nearrow}\cap\mathit{Inv}(\ell)),

\displaystyle\xi^{\swarrow}

\displaystyle:=(\ell,\;Z^{\swarrow}\cap\mathit{Inv}(\ell)),

\displaystyle\mathit{Succ}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle:=(\ell^{\prime},\;\mathit{Succ}(t,Z)\cap\mathit{Inv}(\ell^{\prime})).

\mathit{Pred}
and
\mathit{SafePred}
are used directly on zones throughout.
We will not distinguish a zone from its corresponding set of valuations in our notation.

Let
\vec{0}
be the clock valuation where all clocks have value
0
.
The set of possible initial states of the PTG is

\xi_{0}=\{(\ell_{0},(\vec{0},v_{P}))\;|\;v_{P}\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}^{P}:(\vec{0},v_{P})\models\mathit{Inv}(\ell_{0})\}
.

Algorithms for PTA (and thus PTG) explore and operate on a Parametric Zone Graph (PZG).

Definition 6
(parametric zone graph)

A
Parametric Zone Graph
(PZG) for a PTG

G=(\mathbb{L},X,P,Act,T_{c},T_{u},\ell_{0},\mathit{Inv})

is a tuple
(\Xi,\xi_{I},\Rightarrow^{t}_{c},\Rightarrow^{t}_{u})

where
\Xi\subseteq 2^{\mathbb{S}}
is a set of symbolic states,

\xi_{I}\in\Xi
is the initial symbolic state,
and
{\Rightarrow^{t}_{c}},{\Rightarrow^{t}_{u}}\subseteq\Xi\times\Xi
are transition relations labeled by transitions of
G
.

Definition 7
(canonical PZG)

The
canonical PZG
of a PTG
G
is the PZG
(\Xi,\xi_{I},\Rightarrow^{t}_{c},\Rightarrow^{t}_{u})
where
\xi_{I}=\xi_{0}^{\nearrow}
and the transition relations are defined by:

\xi\Rightarrow^{t}_{c}\xi^{\prime}
iff
\xi^{\prime}=\mathit{Succ}(t,\xi)^{\nearrow}
and
t\in T_{c}
; and

\xi\Rightarrow^{t}_{u}\xi^{\prime}
iff
\xi^{\prime}=\mathit{Succ}(t,\xi)^{\nearrow}
and
t\in T_{u}
;
and
\Xi
is the smallest set containing
\xi_{I}
and closed under these transitions.

The canonical PZG exists and is unique:
\Xi
is well-defined as the least fixed point of forward exploration from
\xi_{I}
, and the transitions are deterministic (each pair
(\xi,t)
yields exactly one successor), so the structure is uniquely determined by
G
.

Definition 8
(zones at a location)

For a location
\ell
(with
X
,
P
, and
\Xi
implicit from the PZG under discussion), the associated zone set is

\mathcal{Z}_{\ell}\;:=\;\{\,Z\in\mathcal{Z}(X,P)\mid(\ell,Z)\in\Xi\,\}.

We study PTGs with a
reachability objective
given by a target set
{R}\subseteq\mathbb{L}
.
The controller is considered winning from a state if there exists a memoryless strategy for the controller such that, regardless of how the environment plays, the game eventually reaches a location in
{R}
.
Solving a PTG then means characterizing the parameter valuations for which the controller is winning from the initial state
\xi_{0}
. A detailed definition of winning strategies can be found in
[
16
]
.

3
State space reduction techniques

3.1
General abstraction framework

We now formally define a general framework for state space abstractions in PTG and prove its correctness in
section

3.3
. The remaining subsections instantiate the framework with concrete abstraction functions, recovering known techniques and introducing new ones.

Definition 9
(abstraction function)

Given a PZG with symbolic state set
\Xi
, an
abstraction function
is a function

\alpha:\mathbb{L}\to 2^{\mathcal{Z}(X,P)}
.
For every location
\ell
, it must over-approximate the original zones:

\forall Z\in\mathcal{Z}_{\ell}\;\exists Z^{\prime}\in\alpha(\ell)\text{ such that }Z\subseteq Z^{\prime}.

Equivalently, every zone in
\mathcal{Z}_{\ell}
must be covered by some zone in
\alpha(\ell)
.

The simplest instance, which serves as a baseline, is the trivial abstraction function.

Example 1

\alpha_{\mathrm{triv}}
is the abstraction function that leaves the zone set unchanged:

\alpha_{\mathrm{triv}}(\ell)=\mathcal{Z}_{\ell}

To define the reduced PZG, we need to know which abstract zone each original zone maps to. This is captured by the representative map induced by
\alpha
.

Definition 10
(representative map)

Given an abstraction function
\alpha
, let
rep_{\alpha}(\ell,Z)
be an arbitrary but fixed zone
Z^{\prime}\in\alpha(\ell)
such that
Z\subseteq Z^{\prime}
(non-empty by coverage).
Define the
representative map
as the function taking symbolic states and returning their representative in the reduced symbolic state space:

\rho_{\alpha}(\ell,Z):=(\ell,rep_{\alpha}(\ell,Z))

Definition 11
(reduced parametric zone graph)

Let
(\Xi,\xi_{I},\Rightarrow_{c}^{t},\Rightarrow_{u}^{t})
be a PZG and
\alpha:\mathbb{L}\to 2^{\mathcal{Z}(X,P)}
an abstraction function with representative map
\rho_{\alpha}
.
The reduced transition relation for controllable transitions is

\Rightarrow_{c,red}^{t}:=\{(\rho_{\alpha}(\xi),\rho_{\alpha}(\xi^{\prime}))\;\mid\;\xi\Rightarrow_{c}^{t}\xi^{\prime}\}

and analogously for uncontrollable transitions:

\Rightarrow_{u,red}^{t}:=\{(\rho_{\alpha}(\xi),\rho_{\alpha}(\xi^{\prime}))\;\mid\;\xi\Rightarrow_{u}^{t}\xi^{\prime}\}

Together with the abstract symbolic states
\{\rho_{\alpha}(\xi)\mid\xi\in\Xi\}
, these define the
reduced parametric zone graph
, itself a PZG for
G
:

\mathit{PZG}^{\alpha}=(\{\rho_{\alpha}(\xi)\mid\xi\in\Xi\},\;\rho_{\alpha}(\xi_{I}),\;\Rightarrow_{c,red}^{t},\;\Rightarrow_{u,red}^{t})

The abstraction changes only which symbolic states are explored and how they are grouped, not the game’s moves:

Lemma 1
(Zone correspondence)

Let
\alpha
be an abstraction function.
For every abstract symbolic state
(\ell,Z^{\prime})\in\mathit{PZG}^{\alpha}
and
every original symbolic state
(\ell,Z)
in the canonical PZG with

\rho_{\alpha}(\ell,Z)=(\ell,Z^{\prime})
:

\mathit{Win}^{*}_{\alpha}[(\ell,Z^{\prime})]\;\cap\;Z\;=\;\mathit{Win}^{*}[(\ell,Z)].

(1)

Proof

We show both inclusions separately, using the correctness theory
of
[
16
]
.
For each location
\ell
, write
W(\ell)
for the set of valuations
from which the controller has a winning strategy at
\ell
.
By
[
16
]
,
the PTG algorithm is sound at every step (Invariant 4: every state
marked winning is genuinely winning) and complete in the limit
(Theorem 3: under fair exploration, every winning state is eventually
discovered).
In particular,
\mathit{Win}^{*}[(\ell,Z)]=W(\ell)\cap Z
for every
symbolic state
(\ell,Z)
in the canonical PZG.

Soundness

(
\mathit{Win}^{*}_{\alpha}[(\ell,Z^{\prime})]\cap Z\subseteq\mathit{Win}^{*}[(\ell,Z)]
).
We show that every valuation added to
\mathit{Win}_{\alpha}[(\ell,Z^{\prime})]
is
genuinely winning at
\ell
, by induction on the order in which
valuations are added.
At target locations (
\ell\in{R}
), the algorithm initializes

\mathit{Win}_{\alpha}[(\ell,Z^{\prime})]\leftarrow Z^{\prime}
; every
v\in Z^{\prime}
at a
target location is winning by definition.
At non-target locations, a valuation
v
is added only through the

\mathit{SafePred}
computation, which requires:
(i) a controllable transition
t
and a delay
\delta\geq 0
such that

v+\delta\models g
, with
(v+\delta)[Y\!:=\!0]
in a set already marked winning;
and (ii) every uncontrollable successor during
[0,\delta]
is also in a
set already marked winning.
Since abstract transitions inherit their guards and resets from genuine
PZG transitions, conditions (i) and (ii) correspond to real game moves.
By the inductive hypothesis, the successor sets contain only genuinely
winning valuations, so
v
is genuinely winning.
Therefore,
\mathit{Win}^{*}_{\alpha}[(\ell,Z^{\prime})]\subseteq W(\ell)
.
Intersecting with
Z\subseteq Z^{\prime}
:

\mathit{Win}^{*}_{\alpha}[(\ell,Z^{\prime})]\cap Z\subseteq W(\ell)\cap Z=\mathit{Win}^{*}[(\ell,Z)]
.

Completeness

(
\mathit{Win}^{*}[(\ell,Z)]\subseteq\mathit{Win}^{*}_{\alpha}[(\ell,Z^{\prime})]\cap Z
).
Let
v\in\mathit{Win}^{*}[(\ell,Z)]=W(\ell)\cap Z
.
Since
Z\subseteq Z^{\prime}
(coverage),
v\in Z^{\prime}
, so
v
is present
in the abstract symbolic state
(\ell,Z^{\prime})
.
We verify the completeness argument
of
[
16
]
(Theorem 3)
for
\mathit{PZG}^{\alpha}
, by induction on the discrete distance

n(\ell,v)
to the target: the minimum, over winning strategies
from
(\ell,v)
, of the maximum number of discrete transitions
along any play consistent with that strategy.

Base case (
n=0
):

v
is at a target location
\ell\in{R}
.
The algorithm sets
\mathit{Win}_{\alpha}[(\ell,Z^{\prime})]\leftarrow Z^{\prime}
upon exploration,
so
v\in Z^{\prime}\subseteq\mathit{Win}_{\alpha}[(\ell,Z^{\prime})]
.

Inductive step (
n>0
):

The winning strategy from
v
waits time
\delta\geq 0
and then takes a
controllable transition
t
to location
\ell^{\prime}
, reaching state

v^{\prime}=(v+\delta)[Y\!:=\!0]
with
n(\ell^{\prime},v^{\prime})<n
.
We show in two parts that
v
is eventually added to

\mathit{Win}_{\alpha}[(\ell,Z^{\prime})]
.

(a) Lifting successors to the abstract graph.

Since
v\in Z\subseteq Z^{\prime}
, every original PZG transition
from
(\ell,Z)
lifts to an abstract transition from
(\ell,Z^{\prime})

whose successor zone contains the original.
For the controllable transition
t
:

(\ell,Z)\Rightarrow_{c}^{t}(\ell^{\prime},Z_{\mathrm{img}})

gives
(\ell,Z^{\prime})\Rightarrow_{c,red}^{t}(\ell^{\prime},Z^{\prime}_{\mathrm{img}})

with
Z^{\prime}_{\mathrm{img}}=\rho_{\alpha}(\ell^{\prime},Z_{\mathrm{img}})\supseteq Z_{\mathrm{img}}
.
Since
v+\delta\in Z
(PZG zones include time successors) and

v+\delta\models g
, we have

v^{\prime}=(v+\delta)[Y\!:=\!0]\in Z_{\mathrm{img}}\subseteq Z^{\prime}_{\mathrm{img}}
(image-preserving).
As
n(\ell^{\prime},v^{\prime})<n
, the inductive hypothesis gives that
v^{\prime}
is eventually
in
\mathit{Win}_{\alpha}[(\ell^{\prime},Z^{\prime}_{\mathrm{img}})]
.
For each uncontrollable transition
t_{u}
enabled from

v+\delta^{\prime}
(
\delta^{\prime}\in[0,\delta]
), the same lifting applies:
the concrete successor
v^{\prime\prime}=(v+\delta^{\prime})[Y_{u}\!:=\!0]
reaches
location
\ell^{\prime}_{u}
and lies in the original successor zone

Z_{\mathrm{img}}^{u}
(image-preserving), which lifts to an abstract successor

Z^{\prime u}_{\mathrm{img}}\supseteq Z_{\mathrm{img}}^{u}
.
Since
v
is genuinely winning,
v^{\prime\prime}
has distance

n(\ell^{\prime}_{u},v^{\prime\prime})\leq n-1
(the uncontrollable move consumes one
discrete transition), so by the inductive hypothesis
v^{\prime\prime}
is eventually
in
\mathit{Win}_{\alpha}
at the corresponding abstract successor.

(b) Backward propagation.

Once all successors are resolved, the algorithm’s update step
(
27
) at
(\ell,Z^{\prime})
computes

\mathit{NewWin}=\mathit{SafePred}(\mathit{Win}_{\alpha}[(\ell,Z^{\prime})]\cup\mathit{WinningMove},\;\mathit{UnCtrl})\cap Z^{\prime}
, where:

•

Controllable:

\mathit{WinningMove}=\!\!\bigcup_{(\ell,Z^{\prime})\Rightarrow_{c,red}^{t_{c}}(\ell^{\prime}_{c},Z^{\prime}_{c})}\!\!\mathit{Pred}(t_{c},\,\mathit{Win}_{\alpha}[(\ell^{\prime}_{c},Z^{\prime}_{c})]).

Since
v^{\prime}\in\mathit{Win}_{\alpha}[(\ell^{\prime},Z^{\prime}_{\mathrm{img}})]
and

v+\delta\models g
with
(v+\delta)[Y\!:=\!0]=v^{\prime}
, we get

v+\delta\in\mathit{Pred}(t,\,\mathit{Win}_{\alpha}[(\ell^{\prime},Z^{\prime}_{\mathrm{img}})])\subseteq\mathit{WinningMove}
.

•

Uncontrollable:

\mathit{UnCtrl}=\!\!\bigcup_{(\ell,Z^{\prime})\Rightarrow_{u,red}^{t_{u}}(\ell^{\prime}_{u},Z^{\prime}_{u})}\!\!\mathit{Pred}(t_{u},\,Z^{\prime}_{u}\setminus\mathit{Win}_{\alpha}[(\ell^{\prime}_{u},Z^{\prime}_{u})]).

Since every uncontrollable successor from the waiting interval

[0,\delta]
is now in
\mathit{Win}_{\alpha}
at its abstract successor,

v+\delta^{\prime}\notin\mathit{UnCtrl}
for all
\delta^{\prime}\in[0,\delta]
.

With both conditions met:
v+\delta\in\mathit{WinningMove}
and

v+\delta^{\prime}\notin\mathit{UnCtrl}
for
\delta^{\prime}\in[0,\delta]
.
By definition of
\mathit{SafePred}
, this gives
v\in\mathit{NewWin}
, so

v
is added to
\mathit{Win}_{\alpha}[(\ell,Z^{\prime})]
.
Under fair exploration, this update happens in finite time.

Combining both directions gives

\mathit{Win}^{*}_{\alpha}[(\ell,Z^{\prime})]\cap Z=\mathit{Win}^{*}[(\ell,Z)]
.
∎

Remark 2
(Consequence)

Lemma

1
says that merging zones loses no precision on the states
that were actually there:
\mathit{Win}^{*}_{\alpha}[(\ell,Z^{\prime})]
restricted to the
original zone
Z\subseteq Z^{\prime}
recovers exactly

\mathit{Win}^{*}[(\ell,Z)]
(see

Theorem 3.1
(Correctness of reduced PZG)

Let
\alpha
be an abstraction function satisfying the coverage requirement.
Under fair exploration, running the PTG algorithm on
\mathit{PZG}^{\alpha}

converges to the same winning parameter set as running it on the canonical PZG.
In particular, if either algorithm terminates, both yield the same result;
if the PZG is infinite, every winning parameter is eventually discovered
by the reduced algorithm.

Proof

Recall
\xi_{0}=(\ell_{0},Z_{\mathrm{init}})

(all clocks zero, parameters unconstrained), and let

(\ell_{0},Z_{0})=\xi_{I}=\xi_{0}^{\nearrow}
be the initial symbolic state
of the canonical PZG.
Let
(\ell_{0},Z^{\prime}_{0})=\rho_{\alpha}(\ell_{0},Z_{0})
be its abstract
counterpart.
By the coverage requirement,
Z_{0}\subseteq Z^{\prime}_{0}
.
By Lemma
1
applied at
(\ell_{0},Z_{0})
:

\mathit{Win}^{*}_{\alpha}[(\ell_{0},Z^{\prime}_{0})]\cap Z_{0}=\mathit{Win}^{*}[(\ell_{0},Z_{0})].

Since
Z_{\mathrm{init}}\subseteq Z_{0}
(the zero-clock zone is contained in
its time-successor closure):

\mathit{Win}^{*}_{\alpha}[(\ell_{0},Z^{\prime}_{0})]\cap Z_{\mathrm{init}}=\mathit{Win}^{*}[(\ell_{0},Z_{0})]\cap Z_{\mathrm{init}}.

Projecting onto parameters:

\bigl(\mathit{Win}^{*}_{\alpha}[(\ell_{0},Z^{\prime}_{0})]\cap Z_{\mathrm{init}}\bigr){\downarrow_{P}}=\bigl(\mathit{Win}^{*}[(\ell_{0},Z_{0})]\cap Z_{\mathrm{init}}\bigr){\downarrow_{P}}.

By the limit-correctness of the PTG algorithm on the canonical
PZG
[
16
]
,
the right-hand side equals the true winning parameter set.
Since the left-hand side equals the right-hand side, the reduced algorithm
converges to the same set.
∎

3.4
Instantiating the framework

The remaining subsections instantiate the framework with concrete abstraction methods.
Each method is specified as a type of
abstraction step
: a local operation that produces a valid abstraction function from an existing one.
This formulation avoids requiring the full zone set
\mathcal{Z}_{\ell}
(which may be infinite) and matches the incremental nature of on-the-fly exploration.

Definition 12
(abstraction step)

Let
\alpha
be an abstraction function. An
abstraction step
at location
\ell
produces a new function
\alpha^{\prime}:\mathbb{L}\to 2^{\mathcal{Z}(X,P)}
satisfying:

1.

\alpha^{\prime}(\ell^{\prime})=\alpha(\ell^{\prime})
for all
\ell^{\prime}\neq\ell

(locality)
,

2.

for every
Z\in\alpha(\ell)
, there exists
Z^{\prime}\in\alpha^{\prime}(\ell)
with
Z\subseteq Z^{\prime}

(coarsening)
.

An
abstraction sequence
is a sequence
(\alpha_{i})_{i\geq 0}
where
\alpha_{0}=\alpha_{\mathrm{triv}}
and each
\alpha_{i+1}
is obtained from
\alpha_{i}
by an abstraction step.

Lemma 2

Every element of an abstraction sequence is a valid abstraction function.

Proof

\alpha_{\mathrm{triv}}
satisfies coverage. Each step preserves coverage: if
Z\in\mathcal{Z}_{\ell}
was covered by
Z^{\prime}\in\alpha(\ell)
, then coarsening gives
Z^{\prime\prime}\in\alpha^{\prime}(\ell)
with
Z\subseteq Z^{\prime}\subseteq Z^{\prime\prime}
.
∎

Remark 3

For finite PZGs, each abstraction sequence stabilizes after finitely many steps;
the closed-form characterizations given alongside each step type below describe these fixed points.
In general, the result of applying steps may depend on the order in which they are applied, but every abstraction function produced by a step sequence is sound by
theorem

3.1
.

3.5
Zone inclusion check

The idea is to prune redundant symbolic states: if two zones
Z\subseteq Z^{\prime}

appear at the same location,
Z
is already covered by
Z^{\prime}
and can be discarded.
We consider two variants depending on whether this check is applied in one or both directions.

Definition 13
(inclusion step)

Let
\alpha
be an abstraction function and
Z\in\alpha(\ell)
.
If there exists
Z^{\prime}\in\alpha(\ell)
with
Z\neq Z^{\prime}
and
Z\subseteq Z^{\prime}
,
then removing
Z
is an
inclusion step
:

\alpha^{\prime}(\ell)=\alpha(\ell)\setminus\{Z\}.

Coarsening is immediate:
Z
itself is covered by
Z^{\prime}
, and all other zones are unchanged.

Remark 4

An inclusion step can be applied in two directions during on-the-fly exploration.

Forward
: when a newly discovered zone
Z
is already subsumed by an existing
Z^{\prime}\in\alpha(\ell)
, discard
Z
immediately.

Backward
: when a newly discovered zone
Z^{\prime}
subsumes an existing
Z\in\alpha(\ell)
, retroactively remove
Z
.
Both are the same formal step; they differ only in which zone is new and which is old.

When
\mathcal{Z}_{\ell}
is finite, a maximal sequence of inclusion steps applied in
both
directions (
double inclusion
) stabilizes to the
antichain
of
\subseteq
-maximal zones:

\alpha_{\subseteq}^{\leftrightarrow}(\ell)=\{Z\in\mathcal{Z}_{\ell}\mid\nexists\,Z^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Z}_{\ell}:Z\subsetneq Z^{\prime}\}.

Applying steps in only one direction may stabilize earlier: forward-only never removes an existing zone, and backward-only never discards a new zone subsumed by an already-present one.

3.6
Zone merging

Zone merging, introduced for parametric timed automata in
[
4
,
6
]
,
reduces the state space by replacing two zones at the same location with their union,
when that union is already convex (so no new states are introduced).

Definition 14
(merge step)

Let
\alpha
be an abstraction function and
Z_{1},Z_{2}\in\alpha(\ell)
where
Z_{m}=Z_{1}\cup Z_{2}
is convex
[
4
]
.
A
merge step
replaces them with
Z_{m}
:

\alpha^{\prime}(\ell)=(\alpha(\ell)\setminus\{Z_{1},Z_{2}\})\cup\{Z_{m}\}.

Coarsening holds:
Z_{1},Z_{2}\subseteq Z_{m}=Z_{1}\cup Z_{2}
, and all other zones are unchanged.

3.7
Location-based

The location-based abstraction is the coarsest instantiation of the framework:
every zone at location
\ell
is replaced by the invariant
\mathit{Inv}(\ell)
, yielding
a single abstract state per location.

Definition 15
(
\alpha_{\mathrm{loc}}
)

\alpha_{\mathrm{loc}}(\ell)=\{\mathit{Inv}(\ell)\}

Coverage holds trivially: every symbolic state
(\ell,Z)
satisfies

Z\subseteq\mathit{Inv}(\ell)
by definition, so the single zone
\mathit{Inv}(\ell)
covers
all zones at
\ell
.
Unlike the other instantiations,
\alpha_{\mathrm{loc}}
does not require the abstraction step
machinery: the characterization above is well-defined for any PZG,
including infinite ones, since it depends only on the invariant of each location,
not on enumerating the reachable zones.
A similar location-based abstraction is used in
[
22
]

for timed ATL.

3.8
Hull abstractions

Unlike zone merging, which only combines zones when their union is already convex,
hull abstractions always collapse zones into a single over-approximating zone, potentially introducing new valuations.
The variants differ in the class of zones they produce, trading precision for computational cost.

Definition 16
(hull operator)

A
hull operator
is a function
\mathcal{H}:2^{\mathcal{Z}(X,P)}\to\mathcal{Z}(X,P)
satisfying
\bigcup_{Z\in S}Z\subseteq\mathcal{H}(S)
for
every finite set of zones
S
.

Definition 17
(hull step)

Let
\alpha
be an abstraction function,
Z\in\alpha(\ell)
a newly discovered zone, and
H\in\alpha(\ell)
an existing zone at
\ell
.
A
hull step
replaces both with the hull of the pair:

\alpha^{\prime}(\ell)=(\alpha(\ell)\setminus\{H,Z\})\cup\{\mathcal{H}(\{H,Z\})\}.

Coarsening holds:
H,Z\subseteq\mathcal{H}(\{H,Z\})
, and all other zones are unchanged.
Successive hull steps at a location accumulate into a running hull: after processing zones
Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{k}
, the abstract zone at
\ell
is
\mathcal{H}(\{\ldots\mathcal{H}(\{Z_{1},Z_{2}\}),\ldots,Z_{k}\})
.
The result is order-independent for associative hull operators (convex hull, octagonal hull, box hull).

Fixed-point characterization (finite PZGs)

When
\mathcal{Z}_{\ell}
is finite, any maximal sequence of hull steps at
\ell
stabilizes to a single zone.
For a hull operator
\mathcal{H}
, the fixed point is:

\alpha_{h}(\ell):=\bigl\{\,\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{Z}_{\ell})\,\bigr\}.

Instances

The three principal instances of
\mathcal{H}
, ordered from finest to coarsest, are:

\alpha_{\mathrm{ch}}
(convex hull)

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{conv}}(S)
is the smallest convex zone containing
\bigcup S
.

\alpha_{\mathrm{oct}}
(octagonal hull)

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{oct}}(S)
is the smallest octagonal zone containing
\bigcup S
.
Octagonal zones extend difference-bound constraints with terms of the form

x_{i}\pm x_{j}\leq c
, capturing more diagonal structures than a bounding box.

\alpha_{\mathrm{box}}
(box hull)

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{box}}(S)
is the smallest axis-aligned bounding zone containing

\bigcup S
, using only individual-variable bounds
x_{i}\leq c
and
x_{i}\geq c
.
It is the cheapest to compute but the coarsest.

These satisfy
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{conv}}(S)\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{oct}}(S)\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{box}}(S)

for any
S
, so
\alpha_{\mathrm{ch}}
is the finest and
\alpha_{\mathrm{box}}
the coarsest hull abstraction.

4.2
Case Study: Adversarial IoT Scheduler

We introduce a case study inspired by an adversarial IoT scenario in which a central scheduler coordinates multiple IoT devices under adversarial delays. We refer to this benchmark as
Malicious Synchronization
in the experiments, reflecting the adversary’s goal of forcing simultaneous transmissions. The setting is easily scalable in the number of devices and naturally induces the synchronized timing patterns that cause symbolic state explosion.

System Overview

The system consists of a network of battery-powered IoT devices that periodically transmit data to a cloud server, a central scheduler that grants transmission requests to prevent network congestion, and an adversary that can delay transmissions by dropping packets (modelled as a time delay). The adversary’s goal is to force enough devices to transmit simultaneously, causing congestion. The adversary observes scheduler state but cannot interfere with scheduler-device communication, and has a limited energy budget bounding the number of delays.

Parametric Timed Game Model

The system is modelled as a network of PTG: one for each IoT device and one for the scheduler. Data transmission is simulated by enforcing a time delay in a
transmission
location of each device. Each device has an uncontrollable action, available when the adversary has remaining energy, that models adversarial delay by injecting additional time before transmission begins.

Three values are parametric: the minimum time between consecutive grants, the maximum adversarial delay per intervention, and the transmission duration. The adversary’s energy budget is a discrete constant that bounds the total number of delay interventions. The goal is to reach a state where all devices have completed their data transmission, while avoiding states in which too many devices transmit simultaneously, causing congestion.
An overview and the
Imitator
models for the two-device instance are given in
appendices

0.B
and
0.A
.

it times out on Production Cell 4 yet solves instance 5 in 453 s.
Empirically, convex hull abstractions can accumulate
increasingly complex polyhedra, at which point the underlying
arithmetic operations stall; whether this occurs appears to depend
on the geometry of the specific instance rather than its size alone.

Box
avoids this by restricting to simpler shapes,
but its rectangular enclosure discards diagonal constraints
that are structurally important in the Production Cell’s geometry,
which may lead to larger zones with more successors, potentially explaining the timeouts on all but the smallest instance.

BoxH
can avoid both pitfalls: on Malicious Synchronization (3/3),
it succeeds where
CH
and
Box
individually time out.

Octagonal hull.

Oct
is the most robust configuration:
it solves every instance solved by any other method
and is the only one to solve Malicious Synchronization (3/4).
It appears to strike the right balance between aggressiveness
and geometric fidelity,
retaining enough diagonal structure to keep zone operations cheap
without over-approximating to the degree that causes the blowup seen with
Box
.

OctH
behaves similarly to
Oct
across most instances,
with no consistent advantage in either direction.

Location-based abstraction.

Loc
can reach a state count comparable to the hull methods
yet be disproportionately slow:
on Malicious Synchronization (3/3) it explores 1,438 states in 475 s,
versus 24.3 s for
Oct
.
The larger zones likely introduce spurious transitions
in the parametric zone graph,
causing repeated propagation through states
that do not contribute to convergence.

On-the-fly merge.

Mrg
is the strongest configuration that introduces
no geometric over-approximation of zones.
It handles all Production Cell instances comfortably
and is competitive on Malicious Synchronization,
making it a reliable fallback when a tighter zone representation
is preferred over raw speed.

Our evaluation is limited to two benchmark families; further case studies are needed to confirm that the relative performance of the abstractions generalises.

5
Conclusion

We presented a general abstraction framework for parametric timed games that reduces
the symbolic state space while preserving correctness of both parameter synthesis
and winning strategies. The framework subsumes the existing zone
inclusion technique for PTG and lifts double inclusion and zone merging from PTA and location-based abstraction from timed games to the PTG setting, while also introducing new hull abstractions inspired by classical numerical abstract domains.
Experiments on a production cell benchmark and a novel adversarial IoT case study show order-of-magnitude reductions in explored states, with octagonal hull emerging as the most robust configuration, solving every tractable instance, including instances previously intractable for existing techniques.

Several directions remain open.
First, combining abstractions, for instance merge steps followed by hull steps, could exploit the strengths of both.
Second, the hybrid configurations showed promise but relied on a fixed constraint-count threshold; a principled heuristic for switching from convex to a coarser hull would make these strategies more broadly applicable.
Finally, evaluating the framework on benchmarks with richer clock and parameter structure, would clarify which abstractions suit which problem characteristics.
