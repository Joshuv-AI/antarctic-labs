Interaction-Split Edge Spectral Flow and Neutral Triplet Boundary Modes in a C = 2 C=2 Hubbard Pump
Yong-Feng Yang, Zhao-Rui Tian, Chen Cheng, Hong-Gang Luo

Abstract
We show that a sliding spatial modulation of the on-site Hubbard interaction realizes a many-body Thouless pump whose boundary spectral flow is reconstructed by correlations. For a period-three Hubbard chain at filling ρ = 2 / 3 \rho=2/3 , density-matrix renormalization group (DMRG) calculations identify a correlated insulator with many-body Chern number C = + 2 C=+2 , corresponding to two units of charge pumped per cycle. Its excitation spectrum separates the charge gap from the lowest neutral spin gap, revealing an interacting bulk response beyond a simple spin-degenerate band pump. Under open boundary conditions, this contrast becomes even more pronounced. A spin-degenerate Hartree/Aubry-André-Harper reference pump exhibits simultaneous edge flow in the two spin channels, whereas the full modulated Hubbard model suppresses the boundary doublon channel and splits the spectral flow into two distinct edge events. Between these events, the boundary charge is neutralized while a neutral, spinful triplet-like excitation is localized at the edge. The global μ − U \mu-U phase diagram reveals filling-dependent topology: the ρ = 2 / 3 \rho=2/3 regime remains in the C = + 2 C=+2 sector, while ρ = 4 / 3 \rho=4/3 regime undergoes a spin-gap-closing transition from a C = − 2 C=-2 pump to a distinct C = + 1 C=+1 topological Mott pump.

In one dimension, correlations are especially consequential: charge and spin degrees of freedom can separate, interaction-driven insulating gaps can emerge, and boundary excitations often defy single-particle expectations

A natural setting in which to probe these effects is the Thouless pump, where an adiabatic cycle transports a quantized charge determined, in interacting systems, by a many-body Chern number

;

, less is known about how strong local correlations reconstruct the open-boundary spectral flow associated with an otherwise integer many-body pump.

Recent progress has sharpened this question from several directions.
In two-component Rice-Mele-Hubbard pumps, interactions can split a pumping process inherited from an underlying band pump
Bertok et al. 2022
.
Quantum-gas experiments have demonstrated the quantization and breakdown of Hubbard-Thouless pumping and shown that interactions can enable pumping in nonsliding lattices

Interaction-controlled pumps have also been studied in spin chains, spinless fermions, and hard-core bosons

These works establish interactions as an active ingredient in topological pumping.
For spinful fermions, however, a distinct possibility arises: a spatially modulated onsite Hubbard interaction can couple charge and spin sectors, thereby reconstructing the boundary response into a neutral, spinful edge sector.

Here we demonstrate this mechanism in a spinful Hubbard chain with a sliding period-three modulation of the on-site interaction.
At filling
\rho=2/3

C=2
, corresponding to two units of charge pumped per cycle.
Because a modulated Hubbard interaction at finite density inevitably generates a Hartree channel, we compare the full modulated Hubbard model with a spin-degenerate Hartree/Aubry-André-Harper (AAH)

Aubry and André 1980

This reference system realizes the same total integer pump through independent spin channels and exhibits simultaneous spin-degenerate edge flow.

The full modulated Hubbard pump realizes the same integer bulk charge transport as the Hartree reference, but with a reconstructed boundary spectral flow. Local correlations suppress the boundary singlet/doublon channel and split the open-chain edge flow into two distinct events. Between these events, the boundary charge response is neutralized, while a triplet-like spin excitation remains localized at the edge. The result is therefore not a fractional charge pump, but an integer

many-body pump with a neutral, spinful boundary sector. The global
\mu
-

\rho=4/3

Model and method.—

At each pump phase
\phi
, we study a spinful Hubbard chain with a sliding modulation of the onsite interaction,

H=-t\sum_{i,\sigma}\left(\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{i,\sigma}\hat{c}_{i+1,\sigma}+\mathrm{H.c.}\right)+\sum_{i}U_{i}(\phi)\hat{n}_{i,\uparrow}\hat{n}_{i,\downarrow},

(1)

where
t=1
sets the energy scale,
\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{i,\sigma}
(
\hat{c}_{i,\sigma}
) is the creation (annihilation) operator for an electron with spin
\sigma\in\{\uparrow,\downarrow\}
at site
i
, and
\hat{n}_{i,\sigma}=\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{i,\sigma}\hat{c}_{i,\sigma}
is the corresponding particle-number operator. The term
U_{i}(\phi)=U\left[1+\cos\left(2\pi i/3+\phi\right)\right]
defines a period-three interaction pattern that slides spatially as the phase
\phi\in[0,2\pi]
is varied. We primarily focus on finite chains of length
L=3m
(where
m
is an integer) at a fractional filling
\rho=N/L=2/3
, restricting the ground state to the balanced spin sector
N_{\uparrow}=N_{\downarrow}=L/3
. However, we also map out the thermodynamic phase diagram to draw comparisons with other commensurate fillings.

Because the interaction modulation occurs at a finite particle density, it intrinsically generates a single-particle Hartree potential
SM
. We therefore compare Eq. (
1
) with the spin-degenerate Hartree/AAH reference model,

H_{\rm H}(\phi)=-t\sum_{i,\sigma}\left(\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{i,\sigma}\hat{c}_{i+1,\sigma}+\mathrm{H.c.}\right)+\frac{\rho}{2}\sum_{i}U_{i}(\phi)\hat{n}_{i},

(2)

where
\hat{n}_{i}=\hat{n}_{i,\uparrow}+\hat{n}_{i,\downarrow}
is the total local density. At filling
\rho=2/3
, this reference system reduces to two independent spin copies of a period-three Harper pump.

Interaction-split boundary spectral flow.—

Figure
1
summarizes the boundary mechanism.
The Hartree/AAH reference in Eq. (
2
) is equivalent to two independent period-three pumps, one for each spin species. Each of these pumps corresponds to a single spin component and is characterized by a Chern number of
C_{\uparrow}=C_{\downarrow}=1

Lang et al. 2012
;
Kraus et al. 2012
;
Kraus and Zilberberg 2012
.
Consequently, its open-boundary spectral flow is strictly spin-degenerate: electron- and hole-like edge excitations from both spin channels traverse the bulk gap simultaneously [Fig.
1
(b)].

The fully modulated Hubbard model realizes the same integer bulk pump, but with a distinct boundary realization.
The on-site repulsion suppresses the local singlet/doublon channel and splits the open-chain spectral flow into two separated edge events [Fig.
1
(c)].
Between these events, the right-boundary charge response is neutralized, while a spin-polarized neutral excitation remains localized at the edge.
Thus, the intermediate edge sector carries the quantum numbers of a neutral, triplet-like boundary excitation.
Our central claim is therefore not fractional charge pumping, but interaction-reconstructed boundary spectral flow within an integer many-body pump.

To strictly isolate the topological edge contributions from the bulk background, we evaluate all local observables as the difference between their expectation values in the
S^{z}_{\text{tot}}=1

Discussion and Conclusion.—

In summary, we have demonstrated that a sliding spatial modulation of the on-site Hubbard interaction provides a robust route to interaction-reconstructed topological boundary transport. For the period-three Hubbard chain at filling
\rho=2/3
, the modulation drives a gapless, noninteracting parent state into a
C=+2
correlated insulating pump phase. Unlike the spin-degenerate Hartree/AAH reference pump, local correlations suppress the boundary doublon channel and explicitly split the otherwise simultaneous edge spectral flow. Between these distinct edge events, the boundary charge is neutralized, while a spinful excitation remains localized near the edge. Protected by the exact global SU(2) spin symmetry of the model, this dynamic spatial decoupling strictly isolates a neutral triplet boundary mode (
S=1
).

Furthermore, the global
\mu-U
phase diagram reveals that this boundary reconstruction is part of a broader, filling-dependent topology. Rather than simply suppressing topological transport via Mott localization, the Hubbard interaction actively selects distinct topological manifolds depending on the carrier density. Specifically, it stabilizes the
C=+2
pump with a triplet edge sector at filling
\rho=2/3
, while driving a topological phase transition into a
C=+1
topological Mott pump hosting fermionic edge modes at
\rho=4/3
.

These results are directly relevant to ongoing efforts to engineer correlated topological dynamics in quantum simulators. The essential ingredients—a one-dimensional lattice, tunable filling, and a spatially patterned onsite interaction—are highly amenable to experiments utilizing ultracold fermionic atoms in optical lattices, where spatially dependent interactions can be engineered using optical Feshbach techniques
Clark et al. 2015
;
Yamazaki et al. 2010
or modulated cavity fields
Landig et al. 2016
;
Vaidya et al. 2018
.
Recent progress in tunable interacting 1D channels in moiré superlattices
Wang et al. 2022
;
Yang et al. 2025
;
Kawakami et al. 2026
, transition metal dichalcogenide arrays
Jolie et al. 2019
;
Deng et al. 2025
, and quasi-1D van der Waals materials
Yao et al. 2023
also points to promising solid-state platforms. In these settings, the defining experimental signatures would be the quantized bulk charge pumping, the splitting of the open-boundary spectral flow, and the emergence of boundary quasiparticles carrying filling-dependent quantum numbers.

Looking ahead, the interaction-split edge spectral flow provides a powerful organizing principle for correlated topological pumps. A natural next step is to characterize the large-
U
,
C=+1
phase at filling
\rho=4/3
in the same depth as the
\rho=2/3
regime. More broadly, extending spatially modulated interactions to systems with longer-range forces (e.g., Rydberg atom arrays) or higher-dimensional geometries may uncover new routes to stabilizing correlated topological boundary modes from otherwise gapless or topologically trivial parent states.

S1. DMRG Methodology and Convergence

We compute the many-body ground state
|\Psi_{0}(\phi)\rangle
and low-lying excitations using the density-matrix renormalization group (DMRG) algorithm within the matrix product state (MPS) framework
Östlund and Rommer 1995
;
Dukelsky et al. 1998
;
Fishman et al. 2022
. Simulations are performed on finite chains up to
L=96

\chi=3000
and perform up to 20 sweeps to ensure energy convergence and a strict truncation error bound below
\epsilon\sim 10^{-8}
. To capture the complete physical picture, we employ periodic boundary conditions (PBC) with finite-size scaling to rigorously establish thermodynamic bulk gaps, alongside open boundary conditions (OBC) to resolve the topological edge modes. To track the dynamic spin-charge separation of the pump over the adiabatic cycle, we extract the local charge density
\langle\hat{n}_{i}\rangle=\langle\hat{n}_{i,\uparrow}+\hat{n}_{i,\downarrow}\rangle
and spin polarization
\langle\hat{S}^{z}_{i}\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\langle\hat{n}_{i,\uparrow}-\hat{n}_{i,\downarrow}\rangle

S2
S2. MANY-BODY CHERN NUMBER

For non-interacting systems, the calculation of the Chern number typically relies on single-particle wave functions in momentum space, where the total Chern number is obtained by summing the Chern numbers of all occupied bands. However, in strongly correlated many-body models, the single-particle band picture breaks down. In such cases, one must impose twisted boundary conditions
Niu et al. 1985
;
Xiao et al. 2010
, where fermions hopping across the system boundary pick up a twisted phase
\theta
:

\hat{c}_{L+1,\sigma}=e^{i\theta}\hat{c}_{1,\sigma}.

(S1)

By utilizing the two-dimensional parameter space spanned by the twisted boundary phase
\theta\in[0,2\pi]
and the adiabatic hopping phase
\phi\in[0,2\pi]
introduced in the main text, the topological invariant is determined solely from the many-body ground-state wave function
|\Psi_{0}(\theta,\phi)\rangle
. The many-body Chern number
C
is defined as the integral of the Berry curvature over this parameter space:

C=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\phi\left(\partial_{\theta}A_{\phi}-\partial_{\phi}A_{\theta}\right),

(S2)

where
A_{\mu}=i\langle\Psi_{0}(\theta,\phi)|\partial_{\mu}|\Psi_{0}(\theta,\phi)\rangle
is the many-body Berry connection.

Figure S1:

C
as a function of the Hubbard interaction strength

L=24
. At filling
\rho=2/3
, the Chern number is robustly quantized to
C=+2
across the interaction range discussed in the main text. Conversely, at the higher filling

C=-2
below a critical interaction threshold
U_{c}
, and shifts to a distinct topological sector with
C=+1

In practical numerical calculations, directly integrating the continuum Berry curvature is susceptible to gauge-fixing issues. We therefore employ an efficient, gauge-invariant discretization method
Fukui et al. 2005
;
Varney et al. 2011
. We divide the parameter space into a discrete grid of
(N_{\theta},N_{\phi})=(12,12)
and define the U(1) link variables between adjacent grid points
\mathbf{q}=(\theta,\phi)
:

\mathcal{U}_{\mu}(\mathbf{q})=\frac{\langle\Psi_{0}(\mathbf{q})|\Psi_{0}(\mathbf{q}+\Delta\hat{\mu})\rangle}{|\langle\Psi_{0}(\mathbf{q})|\Psi_{0}(\mathbf{q}+\Delta\hat{\mu})\rangle|}.

(S3)

By calculating the gauge-invariant Berry flux on each elementary plaquette from these link variables and summing it over the entire discretized parameter space, we obtain the exact integer many-body Chern number
C
.

As shown in Fig.
S1
, we compute the evolution of this many-body invariant as a function of the interaction strength on a lattice of size
L=24

\rho=2/3

C=+2

\rho=4/3

C=-2
below the critical interaction threshold
U_{c}
, and cleanly shifts to
C=+1
above
U_{c}

S3
S3. Mean-Field Hartree Analysis and the Breakdown of the Single-Particle Picture

To rigorously demonstrate that the interaction-reconstructed edge modes and bulk spin-charge separation are strictly non-perturbative many-body phenomena, it is instructive to contrast our full correlated model with its single-particle mean-field limit.

We begin with the full onsite Hubbard interaction,
H_{\text{int}}=\sum_{i}U_{i}(\phi)\hat{n}_{i,\uparrow}\hat{n}_{i,\downarrow}
. In the weak-coupling regime, we can decompose the number operators into their expectation values and quantum fluctuations:
\hat{n}_{i,\sigma}=\langle\hat{n}_{i,\sigma}\rangle+\delta\hat{n}_{i,\sigma}
. Neglecting second-order fluctuations (
\delta\hat{n}_{i,\uparrow}\delta\hat{n}_{i,\downarrow}\approx 0
) yields the standard Hartree mean-field decoupling:

\hat{n}_{i,\uparrow}\hat{n}_{i,\downarrow}\approx\langle\hat{n}_{i,\uparrow}\rangle\hat{n}_{i,\downarrow}+\langle\hat{n}_{i,\downarrow}\rangle\hat{n}_{i,\uparrow}-\langle\hat{n}_{i,\uparrow}\rangle\langle\hat{n}_{i,\downarrow}\rangle.

(S4)

By enforcing the global SU(2) spin symmetry of our unmagnetized ground state alongside the commensurate filling constraint, the average density per spin channel is uniform:
\langle\hat{n}_{i,\uparrow}\rangle=\langle\hat{n}_{i,\downarrow}\rangle=\rho/2
. Substituting this into the interaction term generates the non-interacting Hartree control Hamiltonian:

H_{\text{H}}=-t\sum_{i,\sigma}\left(\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{i,\sigma}\hat{c}_{i+1,\sigma}+\text{H.c.}\right)+\sum_{i}V_{i}^{\text{H}}(\phi)\hat{n}_{i},

(S5)

where
V_{i}^{\text{H}}(\phi)=\frac{\rho}{2}U_{i}(\phi)
acts as an effective, spin-independent sliding scalar potential.

In this mean-field limit, the Hamiltonian fundamentally decouples into two identical, non-interacting spin sectors:
H_{\text{H}}=H_{\uparrow}\oplus H_{\downarrow}
. At
\rho=2/3
, the period-three sliding potential
V_{i}^{\text{H}}(\phi)
opens a single-particle bandgap, isolating a lowest filled subband in both spin channels. Because each independent sector behaves as a topological commensurate Harper model with a Chern number of
C_{\sigma}=1
, the mean-field model successfully captures a macroscopic topological pump of
Q_{\text{pump}}=C_{\uparrow}+C_{\downarrow}=2
charges per cycle.

However, this single-particle framework fails to capture the true topological nature of the correlated system in two critical ways:

1. Spin-Degenerate Edge Transport: Because
H_{\uparrow}
and
H_{\downarrow}
are strictly identical and uncoupled in the Hartree limit, their corresponding topological edge states are perfectly degenerate. In a mean-field pump, the spin-up and spin-down edge modes cross the Fermi level simultaneously. The emergence of split charge transport, the extended topological plateau, and the isolation of the neutral triplet boundary mode (
S=1
) are entirely absent in
H_{\text{H}}
. These features are exclusively driven by the full non-local correlations of the onsite Hubbard interaction dynamically splitting the edge spectral flow.

2. The Absence of Bulk Spin-Charge Separation: In any time-reversal symmetric band insulator governed by
H_{\text{H}}
, the fundamental quasiparticle remains the electron. Consequently, creating a charge excitation or a spin-flip excitation requires promoting an identical electron across the same single-particle bandgap
E_{g}
. A Hartree description strictly dictates that the thermodynamic gaps must be identical:
\Delta_{c}=\Delta_{s}=E_{g}
.

Conversely, our exact DMRG calculations (see Fig. 4 of the main text) reveal a strict inequality in the thermodynamic limit, where
\Delta_{s}<\Delta_{c}
. This explicit divergence of the energy scales proves that the fundamental electronic quasiparticle has been qualitatively destroyed. The lowest neutral excitation is a purely magnetic spin-flip that operates on an energy scale completely decoupled from the charge sector. This definitively rules out any non-interacting mean-field description, proving that the topological pump operates within a genuinely strongly correlated, many-body correlated insulator.

S4
S4. Definition of Energy Gaps and Boundary Observables

To strictly account for finite-size effects and accurately extract the single-particle excitation spectra under open boundary conditions (Fig. 2 of the main text), all single-particle energies are calculated relative to a dynamic mid-gap reference energy. We define this reference as the center of the charge gap:

\mu_{0}(\phi)=\frac{1}{2}\left[E_{0}(N+1,1/2,\phi)-E_{0}(N-1,1/2,\phi)\right],

(S6)

where
E_{0}(N,S^{z},\phi)
denotes the absolute many-body ground state energy for a system with
N
particles and total spin
S^{z}
at the adiabatic pumping phase
\phi
.

The single-particle addition energy, representing the cost to inject an electron, is evaluated by targeting the corresponding
S^{z}=1/2
sector. For the
m
-th excitation, this is given by:

\epsilon^{+}_{m}(\phi)=E_{m}(N+1,1/2,\phi)-E_{0}(N,0,\phi)-\mu_{0}(\phi),

(S7)

where
E_{m}
denotes the energy of the
m
-th state in the target Hilbert space sector.

Similarly, the single-hole removal energy is defined symmetrically with respect to the mid-gap reference:

\epsilon^{-}_{m}(\phi)=\left[E_{0}(N,0,\phi)-E_{m}(N-1,1/2,\phi)\right]-\mu_{0}(\phi).

(S8)

These definitions explicitly remove finite-size charging energies, allowing the difference
\epsilon^{+}_{0}(\phi)-\epsilon^{-}_{0}(\phi)
to accurately reflect the true thermodynamic fermion charge gap.

To construct the global grand-canonical phase diagram (Fig. 4 of the main text), the independent thermodynamic chemical potential
\mu
dictates the stability of the density phases. The upper and lower boundaries of the insulating lobes (
\mu^{+}
and
\mu^{-}
) are extracted from the energy cost to inject or remove particles. To maintain the
S=0
ground-state sector and minimize finite-size spin artifacts, these boundaries are calculated by injecting and removing singlet pairs (
\Delta N=\pm 2
):

\displaystyle\mu^{+}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[E_{0}(N+2,0)-E_{0}(N,0)\right],

(S9)

\displaystyle\mu^{-}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[E_{0}(N,0)-E_{0}(N-2,0)\right].

(S10)

Because the pair-binding energy
E_{b}
is strictly zero in the studied regime, these two-particle bounds are identical to the single-particle addition and removal energies in the thermodynamic limit.

The corresponding bulk charge gap
\Delta_{c}
, spin gap
\Delta_{s}
, and pair-binding energy
E_{b}
evaluated under periodic boundary conditions are given by:

\displaystyle\Delta_{c}

\displaystyle=\mu^{+}-\mu^{-}=E_{0}(N+2,0)+E_{0}(N-2,0)

\displaystyle-2E_{0}(N,0),

(S11)

\displaystyle\Delta_{s}

\displaystyle=E_{0}(N,1)-E_{0}(N,0),

(S12)

\displaystyle E_{b}

\displaystyle=2E_{0}(N+1,1/2)-E_{0}(N+2,0)-E_{0}(N,0).

(S13)

These quantities are computed at multiple system sizes and extrapolated to the thermodynamic limit (
L\to\infty
) using standard polynomial scaling.

Finally, to isolate the interaction-reconstructed boundary spectral flow under open boundary conditions, the spatiotemporal boundary observables are obtained by subtracting the singlet ground-state background from the lowest macroscopic spin-flip excitation (
S^{z}_{\text{tot}}=1
):

\displaystyle\Delta\langle\hat{n}_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\hat{n}_{i}\rangle_{S=1}-\langle\hat{n}_{i}\rangle_{S=0},

(S14)

\displaystyle\Delta\langle\hat{S}^{z}_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\hat{S}^{z}_{i}\rangle_{S=1}-\langle\hat{S}^{z}_{i}\rangle_{S=0}.

(S15)

The integrated right-boundary charge
\Delta Q_{R}
and magnetization
\Delta M_{R}^{z}
are rigorously defined by summing these local deviations over the right half of the lattice (
i>L/2
):

\displaystyle\Delta Q_{R}=\sum_{i>2L/3}\Delta\langle\hat{n}_{i}\rangle,

(S16)

\displaystyle\Delta M_{R}^{z}=\sum_{i>2L/3}\Delta\langle\hat{S}^{z}_{i}\rangle.

(S17)
