Final Technical Report:
 DOE Award No. DE-SC0021156
Investigation of the Suppression of Edge
Localized Modes by Resonant Magnetic
 Perturbations in NSTX/NSTX-U

 Richard Fitzpatrick

              April 20, 2026

 The purpose of the grant was to investigate the suppression of edge localized
modes by resonant magnetic perturbations in both NSTX and NSTX-U. Un-
fortunately, the project quickly exhausted all of the relevant experimental data
from NSTX. It was originally intended to use new data from NSTX-U, when
it restarted. However, the considerable delay in the restart made this impossi-
ble. Hence, with the permission of DOE, the grant was pivoted toward a more
general examination of the MHD stability of tokamak plasmas.
 The research associated with this grant was performed between 2020 and
2025 by the principal investigation, in collaboration with researchers from the
NSTX team, the KSTAR tokamak, and the DIII-D tokamak. Two graduate
students, Tong Wang and Yuchen Lin, were supported by the grant. Tong
Wang published the results of his research in 2025. A paper on the results of
Yuchen Lin’s research will be forthcoming.
 The research described in the eleven papers listed below was either partially,
or fully, funded by the grant. The papers fall into three main groups. First,
there are two papers that are very directly relevant to the grant: these are the
papers on edge localized mode suppression in KSTAR (2021), and the triggering
of neoclassical tearing modes in NSTX (2023). Both of these papers combine
theory with experimental data in order to come up with a better understanding
of ELM suppression and the triggering of NTMs in both regular and spherical
tokamaks. Second, there are two theoretical papers on the breaking of tearing
mode rotation in ITER (2023), and error-field penetration in ITER and SPARC
(2023). Although these papers investigate dangerous MHD activity in regular
tokamaks, their broad conclusions are applicable to spherical tokamaks. The
remaining seven papers perform necessary background theoretical research into
the MHD stability of tokamak plasmas.
 The main findings and/or accomplishments of the research funded by the
grant are:
 • Theoretical conformation that ELM suppression by RMPS in tokamaks is
 associated with the introduction of a magnetic island chain close to the
 top of the pedestal, and that this is only possible in relatively narrow
 windows of q95 .
 • A new calculation of the breaking of tearing mode rotation in ITER which
 shows that the critical island width at which a dangerous locked mode
 appears is not likely to be very much different in ITER, or any next-step
 devices, than that observed in present-day tokamaks.
 • A new calculation of error-field penetration in ITER and SPARC that
 shows that the critical plasma density below which error-field penetration
 occurs in these devices is not likely to be much smaller than in present-day
 devices.
 • An improved resonant resistive layer model for tokamak plasmas.
These findings/accomplishments are all pertinent to the stated mission of the
DOE’s FES program.
