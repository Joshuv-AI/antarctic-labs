Introduction

As one of the central and fast growing topics among extremal combinatorics in the last couple of years, extremal set theory seeks to determine the maximum or minimum size of a family of sets subject to specific constraints. One of the most influential results in this area is the Bollobás’s celebrated Two Families Theorem (or set pair inequality)
[
2
]
proved in 1965, which gives a fundamental inequality for families of set pairs with prescribed intersection constraints.
We begin by introducing the concept of a
Bollobás system.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i}):i\in[m]\}
be a collection of
m
pairs of subsets of
[n]
. Then
\mathcal{P}
is called a Bollobás system if

(i)

for any
\ i\in[m]
,
A_{i}\cap B_{i}=\emptyset
; and

(ii)

for any
\ i\neq j\in[m]
,
A_{i}\cap B_{j}\neq\emptyset
.

Bollobás proved the following landmark result, with a striking feature that
the bound does not depends on the size of the ground set.

Theorem 1.2

(Bollobás
[
2
]
)
.

Suppose that
A_{i},B_{i}\subseteq[n],~i\in[m]
, and
\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i}):i\in[m]\}
is a Bollobás system with
|A_{i}|=a_{i}
and
|B_{i}|=b_{i}
for every
i
. Then

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{\binom{a_{i}+b_{i}}{a_{i}}}\leq 1.

For the uniform case with
|A_{i}|=a
and
|B_{i}|=b
for all
i\in[m]
, Theorem
1.2
implies the sharp bound
m\leq\binom{a+b}{a}
, which was proved by Jaeger and Payan
[
10
]
in 1971, and by Katona
[
12
]
in 1974 independently. Theorem
1.2
has a wide range of applications, such as covering problems for graphs
[
6
,
16
]
, counting cross-intersecting families
[
5
]
, and transversals of hypergraphs
[
20
,
21
,
24
]
.
Theorem
1.2
also has been generalized in a number of different directions in the literature.
A natural variant arises when the intersection condition is required only in one direction in the following manner.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i}):i\in[m]\}
be a collection of
m
pairs of subsets of
[n]
. Then
\mathcal{P}
is called a skew Bollobás system if

(i)

for any
\ i\in[m]
,
A_{i}\cap B_{i}=\emptyset
; and

(ii)

for any
\ i<j\in[m]
,
A_{i}\cap B_{j}\neq\emptyset
.

For the uniform case with
|A_{i}|=a
and
|B_{i}|=b
for all
i\in[m]
, Lovász
[
15
]
, and independently Frankl
[
4
]
, proved that

m\leq\binom{a+b}{a}

holds under the weaker conditions in Definition
1.3
. Alon
[
1
]
further extended this result to the setting of
r
-partitions.

Theorem 1.4

(Alon
[
1
]
)
.

Let
[n]
be the disjoint union of some sets
X_{1},\ldots,X_{r}
. Suppose that
A_{i},B_{i}\subseteq[n],\ i\in[m]
, and
\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i}):i\in[m]\}
is a skew Bollobás system satisfying that

|A_{i}\cap X_{k}|=a_{k},~|B_{i}\cap X_{k}|=b_{k},~\forall~i\in[m],k\in[r].

Then

m\leq\prod_{k=1}^{r}\binom{a_{k}+b_{k}}{a_{k}}.

For the nonuniform case with
a_{k}
(resp.
b_{k}
) being not a constant, however, things become a bit complicate. The natural analogue of Theorem
1.2
fails for skew Bollobás systems without further assumptions. It is exhilarating that, Scott and Wilmer
[
18
]
achieved a breakthrough and showed a monotone version holds under additional monotonicity conditions.
Hegedűs and Frankl
[
8
]
proved that for a skew Bollobás system
\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i}):i\in[m]\}
of subsets of
[n]
with
|A_{i}|=a_{i}
and
|B_{i}|=b_{i}
for every
i
, then

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{\binom{a_{i}+b_{i}}{b_{i}}}\leq n+1.

This bound is tight but depends on
n
. It was recently strengthened by
Yue
[
28
]
, who showed the following stronger inequality independent of
n
:

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{(1+a_{i}+b_{i})\binom{a_{i}+b_{i}}{a_{i}}}\leq 1.

In the same paper, Yue
[
28
]
also extended the Hegedűs-Frankl inequality to the setting of
r
-partitions.

Theorem 1.5

(Yue
[
28
]
)
.

Suppose that
[n]
is the disjoint union of some sets
X_{1},\ldots,X_{r}
with
|X_{k}|=n_{k}
.
For
A_{i},B_{i}\subseteq[n],\ i\in[m]
, let
\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i}):i\in[m]\}
be a skew Bollobás system satisfying that

|A_{i}\cap X_{k}|=a_{i,k},~|B_{i}\cap X_{k}|=b_{i,k},~~\forall~i\in[m],~k\in[r].

Then

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\prod_{k=1}^{r}\frac{1}{\binom{a_{i,k}+b_{i,k}}{a_{i,k}}}\leq\prod_{k=1}^{r}(1+n_{k})\leq\left(1+\frac{n}{r}\right)^{r}.

Motivated by the above results, we in this paper consider their natural analogues in the vector space setting.

Definition 1.6
.

Let
V
be an
n
-dimension real vector space, and
\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i}):i\in[m]\}
be a collection of pairs of subspaces of
V
. Then
\mathcal{P}
is called a skew Bollobás system if

(i)

for any
\ i\in[m]
,
\dim(A_{i}\cap B_{i})=0
; and

(ii)

for any
\ i<j\in[m]
,
\dim(A_{i}\cap B_{j})>0
.

In 2021, Scott and Wilmer
[
18
]
first obtained a weighted skew Bollobás-type inequality in the subspace setting. Using the exterior algebra method, they proved that under the additional monotonicity condition
a_{1}\leq a_{2}\leq\cdots\leq a_{m}
and
b_{1}\geq b_{2}\geq\cdots\geq b_{m}
, where
\dim(A_{i})=a_{i}
and
\dim(B_{i})=b_{i}
, the following inequality holds:

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{\binom{a_{i}+b_{i}}{a_{i}}}\leq 1.

Recently, Wu, Li, Lu and Feng
[
26
]
further generalized this result to a subspace analogue of
r
-partitions. Their generalization also yields a subspace extension of Alon’s theorem
[
1
]
.
Using the weight invariance argument, they also gave a subspace extension of Yue’s inequality,
showing that, for any skew Bollobás system
\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i}):i\in[m]\}
of subspaces of
V
with
\dim(A_{i})=a_{i}
and
\dim(B_{i})=b_{i}
, one has

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{\bigl(a_{i}+b_{i}+1\bigr)\binom{a_{i}+b_{i}}{a_{i}}}\leq 1.

In this paper, we continue this line of investigation by establishing a subspace extension of Yue’s second result (Theorem
1.5
), which concerns skew Bollobás systems with respect to a fixed direct sum decomposition.

Our first result is:

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
V
be an
n
dimensional real vector space with a direct sum decomposition
V=V_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{r}
. Suppose that
\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i}):i\in[m]\}
is a skew Bollobás system of subspaces of
V
satisfying
A_{i}=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{r}(A_{i}\cap V_{k})
and
B_{i}=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{r}(B_{i}\cap V_{k})
for each
i\in[m]
. Set
a_{i,k}=\dim({A_{i}\cap V_{k}})
and
b_{i,k}=\dim({B_{i}\cap V_{k}})
for
k\in[r]
.
Then we have

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left[\prod_{k=1}^{r}\binom{a_{i,k}+b_{i,k}}{a_{i,k}}(1+a_{i,k}+b_{i,k})\right]^{-1}\leq 1.

Theorem
1.7
indeed implies the set version in the following way. Let
\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i}):i\in[m]\}
be a skew Bollobás system of subsets of
[n]
, and let
[n]=X_{1}\cup\cdots\cup X_{r}
be a partition into pairwise disjoint subsets. Consider the standard basis
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and define
V_{k}=\operatorname{span}\{e_{p}:p\in X_{k}\}
for each
k\in[r]
, so that
\mathbb{R}^{n}=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{r}V_{k}
. For each
i\in[m]
, set
A_{i}^{\prime}=\operatorname{span}\{e_{p}:p\in A_{i}\}
and
B_{i}^{\prime}=\operatorname{span}\{e_{p}:p\in B_{i}\}
. Then
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}=\{(A_{i}^{\prime},B_{i}^{\prime}):i\in[m]\}
is a skew Bollobás system of subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For
i\in[m]
and
k\in[r]
, we have
\dim(A_{i}^{\prime}\cap V_{k})=|A_{i}\cap X_{k}|
and
\dim(B_{i}^{\prime}\cap V_{k})=|B_{i}\cap X_{k}|
, and clearly
A_{i}^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{r}(A_{i}^{\prime}\cap V_{k})
and
B_{i}^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{r}(B_{i}^{\prime}\cap V_{k})
. Applying Theorem
1.7
to
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
yields the following corollary, which strengthens Theorem
1.5
.

Corollary 1.8
.

Suppose that
[n]
is the disjoint union of the subsets
X_{1},\ldots,X_{r}
. Let
\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i}):i\in[m]\}
be a skew Bollobás system of subsets of
[n]
with
a_{i,k}=|A_{i}\cap X_{k}|
and
b_{i,k}=|B_{i}\cap X_{k}|
. Then

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left[\prod_{k=1}^{r}\binom{a_{i,k}+b_{i,k}}{a_{i,k}}(1+a_{i,k}+b_{i,k})\right]^{-1}\leq 1.

Apart from the skew setting, there is another natural relaxation of the Bollobás condition, introduced by Tuza
[
22
,
23
]
. Instead of requiring a single pair
(A_{i},B_{i})
with
A_{i}\cap B_{i}=\emptyset
and
A_{i}\cap B_{j}\neq\emptyset
for
i<j
, Tuza considered families of
d
-tuples, and the nonempty intersection condition is required only for some ordered pair of components.

Definition 1.9
.

Let
\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i}^{(1)},\ldots,A_{i}^{(d)}):i\in[m]\}
be
a collection of
d
-tuples of subsets of
[n]
.
Then
\mathcal{P}
is called a weak Bollobás system if the following hold:

(i)

for any
~i\in[m]
, the subsets
A_{i}^{(1)},\ldots,A_{i}^{(d)}
are pairwise disjoint.

(ii)

for any
\ i<j\in[m]
,
\exists\ p<q\in[d]
such that

A_{i}^{(p)}\cap A_{j}^{(q)}\neq\emptyset\ \text{or}\ A_{i}^{(q)}\cap A_{j}^{(p)}\neq\emptyset.

Every skew Bollobás system is weak, but the converse does not hold.

For such systems, using a probabilistic argument based on random partitions of the ground set, Tuza
[
23
]
obtained the following result.

Theorem 1.10

(Tuza
[
23
]
)
.

Let
p_{1},\ldots,p_{d}
be arbitrary positive real numbers such that
p_{1}+\ldots+p_{d}=1
. Suppose that
\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i}^{(1)},\ldots,A_{i}^{(d)}):i\in[m]\}
is a weak Bollobás system of
d
-tuples of subsets of
[n]
. Then

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\prod_{\ell=1}^{d}p_{r}^{|A_{i}^{(\ell)}|}\leq 1.

For the special case
d=2
, we may write
A_{i}=A_{i}^{(1)}
,
B_{i}=A_{i}^{(2)}
, then
\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i}):i\in[m]\}
is a weak Bollobás system. If
|A_{i}|=a
and
|B_{i}|=b
for all
i
, in view of Theorem
1.10
, Tuza
[
22
]
derived the
bound
m\leq\frac{(a+b)^{a+b}}{a^{a}b^{b}}
.
Theorem
1.10
also has many applications in other various combinatorial problems, including bounds for graph coloring
[
13
,
19
]
and hypergraph covering numbers
[
25
]
.

In this paper, we give a new combinatorial proof for Theorem
1.10
.
Motivated by this new proof, we consider its natural extension of the vector space setting. We first introduce the appropriate definitions of
d
-tuples for subspace.

Definition 1.11
.

Let
V
be an
n
dimensional real vector space, and let
\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i}^{(1)},\ldots,A_{i}^{(d)}):i\in[m]\}
be a collection of
d
-tuples of subspaces of
V
. Then

(1)

\mathcal{P}
is called a skew Bollobás system if

(i)

for any
\ i\in[m],\ \dim{(A_{i}^{(1)}+\cdots+A_{i}^{(d)})}=\dim{(A_{i}^{(1)})}+\cdots+\dim{(A_{i}^{(d)})}
; and

(ii)

for
any
\ i<j\in[m],\ \exists\ p<q\in[d]
such that
\dim{(A_{i}^{(p)}\cap A_{j}^{(q)})}>0
.

(2)

\mathcal{P}
is called a weak Bollobás system if

(i)

for any
\ i\in[m],\ \dim{(A_{i}^{(1)}+\cdots+A_{i}^{(d)})}=\dim{(A_{i}^{(1)})}+\cdots+\dim{(A_{i}^{(d)})}
; and

(ii)

for
any
\ i<j\in[m],\ \exists\ p<q\in[d]
such that
\dim{(A_{i}^{(p)}\cap A_{j}^{(q)})}>0
or
\dim{(A_{i}^{(q)}\cap A_{j}^{(p)})}>0
.

The weak condition is the subspace analogue of Tuza’s original definition for sets. However, a direct extension of Theorem
1.10
to weak Bollobás systems of subspaces encounters an obstacle: a uniform bound analogous to Lemma
2.3
(i) is not available for weak systems. This limitation leads us to consider the stronger skew Bollobás
condition.

Our second main result is a subspace analogue of Tuza’s theorem for skew Bollobás system.

Theorem 1.12
.

Let
V
be an
n
dimensional real vector space, and let
p_{1},\ldots,p_{d}
be arbitrary positive real numbers with
p_{1}+\ldots+p_{d}=1
.
Suppose that
\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i}^{(1)},\ldots,A_{i}^{(d)}):i\in[m]\}
is a skew Bollobás system of subspaces of
V
. Let
a_{i}^{(\ell)}=\dim({A_{i}^{(\ell)}})
for
\ell\in[d]
and
i\in[m]
. Then

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\prod_{\ell=1}^{d}p_{\ell}^{a_{i}^{(\ell)}}\leq 1.

1.7
,
1.10
and

This method is originated from
[

]
, and subsequently refined in
[

]
, to deal with Bollobás-type inequalities as well as Tuza-type theorems.
For more related results on Bollobás-type theorems and their variations, we refer the reader to
[
7
,
9
,
11
,
14
,

,

and the references therein.

2.1
Proof of Theorem
1.7

Our proof relies crucially on a recent result established by Wu et al.
[
26
]
, which provides a general bound for skew Bollobás systems of subspaces under direct sum decompositions.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
26
]
)
.

Let
V
be an
n
dimensional real vector space with a direct sum decomposition
V=V_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{r}
. Let
\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i}):i\in[m]\}
be a skew Bollobás system of subspaces of
V
satisfying
A_{i}=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{r}(A_{i}\cap V_{k})
and
B_{i}=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{r}(B_{i}\cap V_{k})
for each
i\in[m]
. Suppose that

\dim(A_{i}\cap V_{k})=a_{k}
and
\dim(B_{i}\cap V_{k})=b_{k}
for every
i\in[m]
and
k\in[r]
.
Then

m\leq\prod_{k=1}^{r}{a_{k}+b_{k}\choose a_{k}}.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
8
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i}):i\in[m]\}
be a skew Bollobás system of subspaces of
V\cong\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then
m\leq 2^{n}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

Assume
n_{k}=\dim(V_{k})
.
Since
V=V_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{r}
, we have
n=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{r}
.
For each
k\in[r]
, we denote

D_{k}=\{(a,b)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}:0\leq a,b\leq n_{k},a+b\leq n_{k}\}.

With the symbols in the assumption, we
define the
type
of
\mathcal{P}
as

\tau(\mathcal{P})=\left(m;(a_{1,1},b_{1,1}),\ldots,(a_{1,r},b_{1,r}),\ldots,(a_{m,1},b_{m,1}),\ldots(a_{m,r},b_{m,r})\right),

and define the
weight
of
\mathcal{P}
as

\omega(\mathcal{P})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left[\prod_{k=1}^{r}\binom{a_{i,k}+b_{i,k}}{a_{i,k}}(1+a_{i,k}+b_{i,k})\right]^{-1}.

By Lemma
2.2
, we have
m\leq 2^{n}
. Since
(a_{i,k},b_{i,k})\in D_{k}
, the number of possible types of
\mathcal{P}
is bounded by

\sum_{m=0}^{2^{n}}(|D_{1}|\cdots|D_{r}|)^{m},

which is finite.

If there exists some
\mathcal{P}
having type
\tau
, then we say that
\tau
is
realizable
, and we define
\omega(\tau)=\omega(\mathcal{P})
.
Let
T
denote the set of all realizable types. Since
T
is finite, the set
\{\omega(\tau):\tau\in T\}
is a finite set of real numbers.
Therefore we may choose a realizable type
\tau^{*}
such that
\omega(\tau^{*})=\text{max}~\omega(\tau)
.
Without loss of generality, we may assume that the skew Bollobás system
\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i}):i\in[m]\}
of the theorem satisfies
\omega(\mathcal{P})=\omega(\tau^{*})
.

Fix a pair
(A_{i},B_{i})\in\mathcal{P}
and
k\in[r]
. If there exists a vector
x\in V_{k}\setminus((A_{i}\cap V_{k})\oplus(B_{i}\cap V_{k}))
, then we consider two new pairs

(A_{i}\oplus\left\langle x\right\rangle,B_{i})

and

(A_{i},B_{i}\oplus\left\langle x\right\rangle).

By the definition of a skew Bollobás system, neither of these pairs belongs to

\mathcal{P}.

Replacing
(A_{i},B_{i})\in\mathcal{P}
with
(A_{i}\oplus\left\langle x\right\rangle,B_{i}),(A_{i},B_{i}\oplus\left\langle x\right\rangle)
, we obtain a new skew Bollobás system
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
.
Let
s_{i,k}=a_{i,k}+b_{i,k}
. A direct computation shows that

\displaystyle\omega(\mathcal{P}^{\prime})-\omega(\mathcal{P})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\prod_{j\neq k}\binom{s_{i,j}}{a_{i,j}}(1+s_{i,j})}\left(\frac{1}{\binom{s_{i,k}+1}{a_{i,k}+1}(s_{i,k}+2)}+\frac{1}{\binom{s_{i,k}+1}{a_{i,k}}(s_{i,k}+2)}-\frac{1}{\binom{s_{i,k}}{a_{i,k}}(1+s_{i,k})}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

Thus,
\omega(\mathcal{P})=\omega(\mathcal{P}^{\prime})
. This weight invariance allows us to modify a system by such operations without changing its weight.

For a pair
(A_{i},B_{i})\in\mathcal{P}
and a subspace
V_{k}
, we denote

d_{i,k}:=n_{k}-\dim\big((A_{i}\cap V_{k})\oplus(B_{i}\cap V_{k})\big)\geq 0.

A pair
(A_{i},B_{i})
is called
full
if
d_{i,k}=0
for all
k
, i.e.,

(A_{i}\cap V_{k})\oplus(B_{i}\cap V_{k})=V_{k}

for all
k
.
For a full pair
(A_{i},B_{i})
, we have
a_{i,k}+b_{i,k}=n_{k}
for all
k
, and
A_{i}\oplus B_{i}=V
.
We define the
potential
of
\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i}):i\in[m]\}
as

\phi(\mathcal{P})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\prod_{k=1}^{r}2^{n_{k}-d_{i,k}}.

If
\mathcal{P}
contains a non-full pair, then there exist
i\in[m]
and
k\in[r]
such that
d_{i,k}\geq 1
.
Choose a vector
x\in V_{k}\setminus((A_{i}\cap V_{k})\oplus(B_{i}\cap V_{k}))
, we can construct
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
as described above. Then we similarly have

\omega(\mathcal{P}^{\prime})=\omega(\mathcal{P}),

and

\displaystyle\phi(\mathcal{P}^{\prime})-\phi(\mathcal{P})

\displaystyle=2\left(\prod_{j\neq k}2^{n_{j}-d_{i,j}}\right)2^{n_{k}-(d_{i,k}-1)}-\left(\prod_{j\neq k}2^{n_{j}-d_{i,j}}\right)2^{n_{k}-d_{i,k}}

\displaystyle=3\prod_{j=1}^{r}2^{n_{j}-d_{i,j}}>0.

Thus, the potential strictly increases under such an operation.
By Lemma
2.2
, we have

\phi(\mathcal{P}^{\prime})\leq 2^{n}\cdot\prod_{k=1}^{r}2^{n_{k}}=4^{n}.

Since
\phi
is strictly increasing under each such operation and bounded above by
4^{n}
, this process must stop in finite steps. Hence, repeated applications of this operation must eventually yield a system consisting entirely of full pairs. We may therefore assume without loss of generality that
\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i}):i\in[m]\}
is such a system. Consequently,

(A_{i}\cap V_{k})\oplus(B_{i}\cap V_{k})=V_{k}

for all
k\in[r]
and
i\in[m]
.

For a fixed
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})
with
0\leq a_{k}\leq n_{k}
for each
k
, we define

I_{\mathbf{a}}=\left\{i\in[m]:(a_{i,1},\ldots,a_{i,r})=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})\right\}.

For any
i\in I_{\mathbf{a}}
and
k\in[r]
, the fullness implies

\displaystyle\dim(A_{i}\cap V_{k})=a_{k},\ \dim(B_{i}\cap V_{k})=n_{k}-a_{k}.

Hence, the subfamily
\{(A_{i},B_{i}):i\in I_{\mathbf{a}}\}
is a skew Bollobás system satisfying the conditions in Theorem
2.1
, so we have

\left|I_{\mathbf{a}}\right|\leq\prod_{k=1}^{r}{a_{k}+n_{k}-a_{k}\choose a_{k}}=\prod_{k=1}^{r}{n_{k}\choose a_{k}}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\omega(\mathcal{P})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\prod_{k=1}^{r}\left[\binom{n_{k}}{a_{i.k}}(1+n_{k})\right]^{-1}

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{k=1}^{r}\frac{1}{1+n_{k}}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\prod_{k=1}^{r}\frac{1}{\binom{n_{k}}{a_{i,k}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{k=1}^{r}\frac{1}{1+n_{k}}\right)\sum_{\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})}\sum_{i\in I_{\mathbf{a}}}\frac{1}{\prod_{k=1}^{r}{n_{k}\choose a_{k}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\prod_{k=1}^{r}\frac{1}{1+n_{k}}\right)\sum_{\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})}1

\displaystyle\leq 1.

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.7
.
∎

2.2
An alternative proof of Theorem
1.10

In this subsection, we present a new combinatorial proof of Theorem
1.10
.

Proof of Theorem
1.10
.

Let
\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i}^{(1)},\ldots,A_{i}^{(d)}):i\in[m]\}
be a weak Bollobás system of
d
-tuples of subsets of
[n]
.
By definition, each
d
-tuple occurs at most once in
\mathcal{P}
, and since there are
(d+1)^{n}
possible
d
-tuples of pairwise disjoint subsets of
[n]
, we have
m\leq(d+1)^{n}
. Now we define

\phi(\mathcal{P})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}|A_{i}^{(\ell)}|.

It follows that
\phi(\mathcal{P})\leq n(d+1)^{n}
. We define

\omega(\mathcal{P})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\prod_{\ell=1}^{d}p_{\ell}^{|A_{i}^{(\ell)}|}.

Consider a tuple
(A_{i}^{(1)},\ldots,A_{i}^{(d)})\in\mathcal{P}
. If there exists
x\in[n]\setminus\Bigl(\bigcup_{j\in[d]}A_{i}^{(j)}\Bigr)
, we can construct
d
new tuples:

\left(A_{i}^{(1)}\cup\{x\},\ldots,A_{i}^{(d)}\right),\ldots,\left(A_{i}^{(1)},\ldots,A_{i}^{(d)}\cup\{x\}\right),

each is not contained in
\mathcal{P}
. Replacing
(A_{i}^{(1)},\ldots,A_{i}^{(d)})
in
\mathcal{P}
with these
d
new tuples yields a new weak Bollobás system
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
.
Then

\phi(\mathcal{P}^{\prime})\leq n(d+1)^{n}.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\omega(\mathcal{P}^{\prime})-\omega(\mathcal{P})

\displaystyle=\prod_{\ell=1}^{d}p_{\ell}^{|A_{i}^{(\ell)}|}\big(p_{1}+\cdots+p_{d}-1\big)=0,

\displaystyle\phi(\mathcal{P}^{\prime})-\phi(\mathcal{P})

\displaystyle=d>0.

By repeated applications of the above replacement operation, we may eventually obtain a weak Bollobás system
\mathcal{P}^{*}=\{(B_{i}^{(1)},\ldots,B_{i}^{(d)}):i\in[m^{*}]\}
such that
\omega(\mathcal{P}^{*})=\omega(\mathcal{P})
, and

B_{i}^{(1)}\cup\cdots\cup B_{i}^{(d)}=[n]~\text{ for all }~i\in[m^{*}].

For a fixed
\mathbf{a}=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}\}
with
a_{1}+\cdots+a_{d}=n
, we define

I_{\mathbf{a}}=\left\{i\in[m^{*}]:(|B_{i}^{(1)}|,\ldots,|B_{i}^{(d)}|)=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{d})\right\}.

Note that, here, for
n=a_{1}+\cdots+a_{d}
, the multinomial coefficient is written as
\binom{n}{a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}}:=\frac{n!}{a_{1}!\cdots a_{d}!}
. Then

|I_{\mathbf{a}}|\leq\binom{n}{a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\omega(\mathcal{P})

\displaystyle=\omega(\mathcal{P}^{*})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{d})}\sum_{i\in I_{\mathbf{a}}}p_{1}^{a_{1}}\ldots p_{d}^{a_{d}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{d})}\binom{n}{a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}}p_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots p_{d}^{a_{d}}

\displaystyle=(p_{1}+\cdots+p_{d})^{n}=1.

This yields the desired result.
∎

2.3
Proof of Theorem
1.12

We begin by recalling a result on skew Bollobás systems, which can be found in
[
3
,
17
,
26
,
29
]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i}^{(1)},\ldots,A_{i}^{(d)}):i\in[m]\}
be a skew Bollobás system of
d
-tuples of subspaces of a vector space
V\cong\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then the following hold:

(i)

If
\dim{(A_{i}^{(\ell)})}=a_{\ell}
for every
i\in[m]
and
\ell\in[d]
, then

m\leq\binom{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{d}}{a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}}.

(ii)

In general,
m\leq d^{n}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.12
.

Let
\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i}^{(1)},\ldots,A_{i}^{(d)}):i\in[m]\}
be a skew Bollobás system of subspaces of
V
with
a_{i}^{(\ell)}=\dim({A_{i}^{(\ell)}})
.
By Lemma
2.3
(ii), we have
m\leq d^{n}
.

Define

\omega(\mathcal{P})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\prod_{\ell=1}^{d}p_{\ell}^{a_{i}^{(\ell)}}.

As in the proof of Theorem
1.7
, we may assume that
\omega(\mathcal{P})
is maximal.

Define

\phi(\mathcal{P})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}a_{i}^{(\ell)}

and note that
\phi(\mathcal{P})\leq nd^{n}
.
We may further assume, by the argument used in the proof of Theorem
1.10
, that

\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i}^{(1)},\ldots,A_{i}^{(d)}):i\in[m]\}
satisfies

A_{i}^{(1)}\oplus\cdots\oplus A_{i}^{(d)}=V~\text{ for all }~i\in[m].

For a fixed
\mathbf{a}=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}\}
with
a_{1}+\cdots+a_{d}=n
, we define

I_{\mathbf{a}}=\left\{i\in[m]:(a_{i}^{(1)},\ldots,a_{i}^{(d)})=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{d})\right\}.

Since the subfamily
\{(A_{i}^{(1)},\ldots,A_{i}^{(d)}):i\in I_{\mathbf{a}}\}
is a skew Bollobás system, applying Lemma
2.3
(i), it yields

\left|I_{\mathbf{a}}\right|\leq\binom{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{d}}{a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}}=\binom{n}{a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\omega(\mathcal{P})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{1}^{a_{i}^{(1)}}\ldots p_{d}^{a_{i}^{(d)}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{d})}\sum_{i\in I_{\mathbf{a}}}p_{1}^{a_{1}}\ldots p_{d}^{a_{d}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{d})}\binom{n}{a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}}p_{1}^{a_{1}}\ldots p_{d}^{a_{d}}

\displaystyle=(p_{1}+\ldots p_{d})^{n}=1.

This completes the proof.
∎

3
Concluding Remarks

A natural question arising from our work concerns the weak Bollobás condition for subspaces. In the set setting, Tuza’s theorem holds for weak Bollobás systems, where the nonempty intersection condition is required only for some ordered pair of components. However, as noted in Section
1
, a direct subspace analogue of the weak condition does not admit a uniform bound analogous to Lemma
2.3
(i), which is essential for the weight invariance argument.
We propose the following open problem regarding the weak Bollobás system of subspaces.

Problem 3.1
.

Let
V
be an
n
dimensional real vector space, and let
p_{1},\ldots,p_{d}
be arbitrary positive real numbers with
p_{1}+\ldots+p_{d}=1
.
Suppose that
\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i}^{(1)},\ldots,A_{i}^{(d)}):i\in[m]\}
is a weak Bollobás system of subspaces of
V
. Let
a_{i}^{(\ell)}=\dim({A_{i}^{(\ell)}})
for
\ell\in[d]
and
i\in[m]
. Then does the inequality

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\prod_{\ell=1}^{d}p_{\ell}^{a_{i}^{(\ell)}}\leq 1

holds? Or whether additional structural assumptions are necessary?
