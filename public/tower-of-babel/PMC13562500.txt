Personalized talent cultivation and academic prediction framework for higher education based on the HA-GNN-LSTM architecture
Authors: Qi Gong, Jing Shi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo address the dilemma of homogeneous talent training and the efficiency bottleneck of human resource management in universities, this study proposes an innovative personalized training framework integrating artificial intelligence, big data, and deep learning. Based on the 18-dimensional full-cycle behavior dataset of 5,000 students and OULAD dataset, a multimodal heterogeneous data fusion pipeline is constructed. This study adopts Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) for data imputation and bias optimization, designs Hierarchical Attention Graph Neural Network (HA-GNN) to capture hierarchical correlations among features, and uses Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) to model temporal behavior patterns. The experimental results demonstrate that, under 10 independent repeated runs with random seed variation, the Hierarchical Attention Graph Neural Network-Long Short-Term Memory (HA-GNN-LSTM) model achieves lower prediction error on the academic performance prediction task, with a Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of 4.2 ± 0.3. Compared with the Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT) baseline model, MAE is reduced by 31.1%. Welch’s two-tailed t-tests based on independent run results remain statistically significant after Holm-Bonferroni multiple comparison correction . The Normalized Discontinued Cumulative Gain at Top 5 (NDCG @ 5) index of personalized recommendation system reaches 0.90, which verifies the effectiveness of spatio-temporal feature modeling. At the management application level, the improvements in advisor allocation response time and resource idle rate are derived from simulation experiments based on historical data replay, rather than online deployment in real campus management systems. The simulation results demonstrate that, under established constraints and historical sample distributions, advisor allocation response time could be reduced by 60% and resource idle rate could be decreased by 63.4%. These findings indicate the framework’s potential for optimizing educational resource allocation. However, its managerial benefits require further validation through subsequent real-world deployment and long-term follow-up studies.Keywords: Personalized talent training, Deep learning, Human resource management innovation, Artificial intelligence, Big dataSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Research background and motivations

With the continuous expansion of global higher education scale and the rapid development of the knowledge economy, traditional talent cultivation models in colleges and universities are facing increasingly severe challenges1. The conventional “one-size-fits-all” educational approach struggles to meet the diverse and individualized needs of students, leading to serious homogeneity in cultivation outcomes. This not only hinders the full development of students’ potential but also impacts the overall improvement of talent quality. Meanwhile, in the face of complex faculty structures and diverse educational resource allocation, human resource management in colleges and universities also suffers from bottlenecks such as low efficiency and delayed decision-making, which makes it difficult to support the innovative transformation of teaching and management2,3. With the rapid advancement of Artificial Intelligence (AI) technology and the maturity of big data analysis methods, data-driven intelligent education and management have become a key path to address the aforementioned dilemmas. Different from the traditional experience-driven paradigm, this study aims to achieve an evidence-based methodological advancement by integrating advanced deep learning models. By mining students’ multi-dimensional behavioral data and academic performance data, and constructing personalized talent cultivation plans, it is expected to achieve “teaching students in accordance with their aptitude”, thereby improving the precision of teaching and students’ ability for independent development4–6. Meanwhile, applying advanced deep learning models to the temporal modeling of students’ behaviors and performance can help reveal the complex non-linear relationships in students’ learning paths, accurately predict academic performance and cultivation effectiveness, and thus provide a scientific basis for teaching intervention7.

However, universities still face multiple challenges when applying big data and AI technologies, which also constitute the deeper theoretical motivation for the technical selection and module combination in this study. First, student behavior data in educational scenarios come from diverse sources and are of various types, making the effective fusion of multimodal heterogeneous data a major challenge8. Meanwhile, the prevalent non-random missingness and distribution biases in such data can disrupt the inherent conditional probability distribution, necessitating advanced data imputation and debiasing techniques9. To address this, the present study introduces a Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) to capture complex nonlinear correlations among features through adversarial learning, thereby theoretically ensuring the authenticity and unbiasedness of preprocessed data. Furthermore, students’ academic development is a complex process intertwined with temporal and spatial factors: at the macro level, inherent knowledge transfer logic exists across different disciplines (e.g., engineering courses heavily rely on mathematical foundations). At the micro level, individual learning behaviors exhibit long-term dynamic evolution. Based on this educational cognition, instead of using a conventional single network, this study combines a Hierarchical Attention Graph Neural Network (HA-GNN) with a Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network. HA-GNN is responsible for graph-structured modeling of students’ multidimensional features and the hierarchical logic of disciplines, while LSTM extracts temporal evolution features of behaviors. Finally, traditional education and human resource management systems generally lack dynamic adjustment and personalized configuration capabilities, making it difficult to cope with individual differences among students and rapidly changing educational demands10,11. A prediction model without a decision optimization mechanism cannot achieve a true closed-loop management. Therefore, this study further incorporates Bayesian optimization to convert prediction results into dynamic parameter tuning. By systematically addressing the three key issues of data fusion, model construction, and dynamic optimization, this study constructs an innovative framework integrating AI, big data, and deep learning, aiming to achieve dual breakthroughs in personalized talent cultivation and educational management innovation in universities12.

Research question

To validate the effectiveness of the proposed personalized cultivation framework and its practical application in management, this study focuses on the following three specific questions:

First, traditional methods struggle to simultaneously account for the spatial correlations among disciplines and the temporal evolution of learning behaviors, which often leads to delayed academic warning and intervention. To address this pain point, can the spatiotemporal modeling scheme integrating HA-GNN and LSTM effectively process high-dimensional heterogeneous student data and substantially reduce prediction errors in academic performance (e.g., the MAE metric)?

Second, static “one-size-fits-all” cultivation plans are prone to causing mismatches in educational resources and suppressing student potential. In this context, compared with traditional experience-based methods, can the dynamic parameter tuning mechanism incorporating Bayesian optimization effectively improve the matching accuracy of personalized course recommendations (e.g., the NDCG metric)?

Third, resource scheduling models that rely on manual or fixed rules often lead to allocation delays and resource idleness. In a simulation evaluation scenario based on historical data replay, to what extent can the algorithm-driven management module reduce advisor allocation response time and decrease resource idle rate? Whether these simulation results can provide quantifiable reference benchmarks for the subsequent deployment of real-world educational management systems?

Research objectives

This study aims to construct an innovative framework for personalized talent cultivation and human resource management in universities that integrates artificial intelligence (AI), big data, and deep learning technologies to break through the homogenization bottleneck of traditional education models. Through a highly integrated technical route, the framework uses HA-GNN to capture hierarchical correlations among disciplines and realizes dynamic closed-loop adjustment of cultivation programs via Bayesian optimization, effectively filling the gap in existing research on dynamic personalized configuration. Empirically, this study strictly distinguishes the scientific nature of different dimensions: the improvements in academic prediction accuracy and course recommendation quality are rigorously verified through experiments on a historical dataset of 5,000 students; the efficiency enhancement of the human resource management module, as an application system evaluation, demonstrates the exploratory potential of the framework in real educational scenarios. This study helps to achieve “teaching students in accordance with their aptitude” and provides a valuable engineering practice path for the digital transformation of university education management. The core objectives of this study are summarized as follows:

Technological integration innovation: Integrate HA-GNN and Bayesian optimization to build a dynamic closed-loop technical route for personalized cultivation.

Multidimensional accurate verification: Based on large-scale historical data, rigorously verify the scientificity and accuracy of academic performance prediction and curriculum matching through experiments.

Management efficiency evaluation: The evaluation of management efficiency is confined to the assessment of application systems in simulated environments. Based on 5,000 historical student records, simulation workflows for advisor allocation, laboratory resource scheduling, and personalized intervention plan generation are constructed to examine the impact of integrating HA-GNN-LSTM prediction results with Bayesian optimization decision modules on response time, resource idle rate, and matching accuracy.

Literature review

In recent years, the in-depth application of AI in human capital management and educational model transformation has attracted widespread attention. Shahnazaryan et al.13 pointed out that AI had shown great potential in optimizing human resource allocation and performance evaluation, providing a new paradigm for intelligent management. Johnson et al.14 emphasized the necessity of constructing a talent training roadmap that closely integrated education and industry under the data-driven economy. Baker and Hawn15 systematically analyzed algorithmic bias issues in educational artificial intelligence scenarios, identifying potential sources of bias encompassing training data distribution, feature selection, model architecture, and real-world deployment workflows. They further noted that such bias might produce differential impacts across student groups with varying socioeconomic backgrounds, racial identities, genders, and educational resource conditions. This research cautions that educational prediction models should not be evaluated solely on criteria such as accuracy, error reduction, or recommendation relevance. Concurrent examination of sensitive feature usage, disparate error rates across subgroups, equitable access to intervention opportunities, and consequences of resource allocation is imperative. This perspective provides the ethical foundation for the subsequent implementation of privacy protection and fairness auditing mechanisms for sensitive features, including family income, psychological stress, and sleep status, in this study. Against this transformation background, Burton16 proposed that leaders needed to possess the ability to harness AI technologies, while the recent study by Batz et al.17 demonstrated how machine learning could significantly enhance the overall competitiveness of organizations by reshaping the management decision-making process.

In terms of specific management practice paths, the logic of enterprise resource optimization provides important methodological insights for the precision management of university functional departments. Wang et al.18 proposed using AI algorithms to achieve optimal resource allocation, an idea that could be effectively extended to the intelligent evaluation of post competency and the precise delivery of educational resources within universities. However, although macro-level discussions have laid a theoretical foundation, existing studies still have obvious shortcomings in dealing with missing values and distribution bias in educational data, as well as integrating prediction results into the closed-loop of human resource allocation. This limits the operability of the model in actual management scenarios, and exposes personalized training programs to the risk of mismatched resources during implementation.

In terms of cutting-edge technological exploration in educational data mining, recent achievements in 2025 have significantly advanced the depth of spatiotemporal feature modeling in this field. In the domain of graph modeling, Zhang19 and Wang20 captured complex hierarchical relationships among knowledge points through improved Graph Neural Network (GNN), substantially improving the accuracy of path recommendation. For dynamic optimization, Fu21 integrated reinforcement learning with dynamic knowledge tracing, effectively solving the problem of closed-loop automatic optimization of learning paths. In addition, the TGEL-Transformer model proposed by Gong22 enhanced the interpretability of prediction by incorporating educational theories, while Xie23 further improved the accuracy of academic performance prediction via a spatio-temporal fusion architecture. These state-of-the-art (SOTA) methods demonstrate the value of deep feature extraction from heterogeneous data and provide the technical foundation for this study to integrate HA-GNN and LSTM for constructing the talent cultivation framework.

Model architecture design

Based on the time series feature tensor output by the multi-source heterogeneous data processing framework, this study designs a fusion architecture of HA-GNN and LSTM, as shown in Fig. 1. The unique advantage of choosing HA-GNN is that it models students as graph nodes, captures nonlinear interactions among individual features at the micro level, and establishes knowledge transfer logic between disciplines at the macro level (e.g., engineering courses require a solid mathematical foundation). This design goes beyond the limitations of conventional graph neural networks and can effectively capture complex correlations in students’ learning processes. To further extract the dynamic changes in students’ academic performance over time, this study introduces LSTM networks. Its distinctive gating mechanism effectively captures long-term dependencies and accurately characterizes the temporal evolution trend of academic outcomes. By fusing the features of HA-GNN and LSTM via a multi-head self-attention mechanism, the model achieves a comprehensive understanding of students’ academic profiles and supports subsequent personalized recommendation tasks.

The graph structures at these two levels correspond to intra-individual behavioral associations and curricular structural associations within the educational system, respectively.

Experimental design and performance evaluation

Datasets collection

This study adopts a multi-source heterogeneous data fusion strategy. The core dataset is a student full-cycle behavior database authorized by the Learning Analytics Laboratory, containing 18-dimensional time-series records of 5,000 students from a research university between 2019 and 2023 (Table 3). Data Source: https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/mahmoudelhemaly/students-grading-dataset. This dataset contains student identifiers, grades, learning behaviors, attendance, stress levels, sleep duration, and other variables related to academic performance. It is publicly available anonymized data that does not contain personally identifiable information. In accordance with requirements for research reproducibility and data ethics, this study employs it solely for academic performance prediction, feature attribution, and simulated management evaluation, without conducting individual identification or utilizing sensitive variables for punitive tracking or opportunity restriction. To enhance the verifiability of the model across diverse educational systems, this study additionally incorporates the Open University Learning Analytics Dataset (OULAD) as external validation data (https://analyse.kmi.open.ac.uk/open-dataset). OULAD contains multidimensional interaction behavior and academic performance data from 32,593 students and is intended to serve as an external benchmark to cross-validate the generalization capability and robustness of the proposed model across large-scale and different educational systems.

In terms of the experimental environment and data partitioning, to ensure evaluation rigor, the private dataset of 5,000 students is randomly divided into training, validation, and independent test sets at a ratio of 8:1:1. Furthermore, this study implemented cross-cohort validation: data from 2019 to 2021 are used for model training, and data from 2022 to 2023 are used for final testing, in order to verify the predictive stability of the model across academic years.

For the sensitive behavioral and psychological data involved in this study (e.g., family income, stress levels), this research strictly adheres to the ethical norms of artificial intelligence in education and has obtained approval from the Institutional Review Board (IRB). To thoroughly avoid risks of algorithmic discrimination and privacy leakage while leveraging sensitive features to improve prediction accuracy, this study designs and implements a rigorous “triple anonymization” protection strategy:

1) Identity desensitization layer: Irreversible hashing of direct identifiers such as name and student ID using the SHA-256 encryption algorithm.

2) Quasi-identifier obfuscation layer: Application of k-anonymity (k = 5) to quasi-identifiers such as geographic location and socioeconomic background, cutting off possible link attacks through attribute generalization.

3) Feature-specific protection layer: Introduction of differential privacy noise during the feature extraction stage of sensitive psychological features (e.g., stress scores).

To ensure verifiable differential privacy protection strength, the Differential Privacy (DP) parameters for sensitive psychological features are set to and . The protected features include psychological stress scores, sleep quality, emotional fluctuations, and variables derived from mental health questionnaires. Noise injection occurs after feature extraction and prior to model training. Raw questionnaire records do not enter the model training pipeline. Performance evaluation

The comparison of prediction errors of multiple models for academic performance is shown in Fig. 4. The data indicates that the prediction accuracy of the HA-GNN-LSTM model proposed in this study is significantly superior to that of benchmark methods. Its Mean Absolute Error (MAE) is only 4.2 ± 0.3, which is 31.1% lower than that of the Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT). Additionally, it has the smallest standard deviation (± 0.3) among all models. This advantage stems from the effective modeling of correlations between multi-dimensional features by the hierarchical graph structure and spatiotemporal attention mechanism, which greatly reduces the volatility of prediction results.

Comparison of prediction errors of multi-model academic performance.

The 31.1% reduction in MAE and the improvement in R² observed in this study do not indicate that the educational prediction task has been fully resolved, nor do they imply that the model can stably achieve comparable improvements across all real-world university environments. These results are obtained under the specific conditions of the current data structure, feature coverage, train-test partitioning, and model ensemble configuration. Compared with the Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT), the advantages of HA-GNN-LSTM primarily stem from the synergistic integration of three mechanisms. GAN-based imputation mitigates the interference of non-random missingness on model training. KDE reweighting alleviates biases induced by imbalanced disciplinary sample distributions. The HA-GNN explicitly models hierarchical relationships between student behavioral variables and disciplinary structures, while the LSTM further extracts temporal dependencies from learning trajectories. These results should be interpreted as experimental performance improvements arising from the collaborative effects of multiple modules, rather than as universal advantages attributable to any single model architecture.

This study first conducts a dual validation of the academic performance prediction accuracy of the HA-GNN-LSTM model on a private dataset (5,000 individuals) and a public dataset (OULAD, 32,593 individuals). In Table 6, the HA-GNN-LSTM achieves an MAE of 4.2 ± 0.3 on the private dataset, which was lower than the 6.1 ± 0.5 of the Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT), corresponding to a 31.1% reduction in MAE. The R² improves from 0.82 ± 0.02 (TFT) to 0.93 ± 0.01. These results indicate that, under the current data structure, the HA-GNN-LSTM can more effectively exploit student behavioral variables, disciplinary structural relationships, and temporal sequence trajectories. External validation on the OULAD dataset yields an MAE of 5.9 ± 0.2 for the HA-GNN-LSTM, compared with 7.6 ± 0.4 for the TFT, with R² improving from 0.78 ± 0.02 to 0.87 ± 0.01. The smaller magnitude of improvement on OULAD relative to the private dataset suggests that the model possesses certain cross-dataset generalization capabilities, while also indicating that the 31.1% improvement observed on the private dataset should not be interpreted as a fixed benefit applicable to all educational scenarios.

The performance metrics presented in Table 6 reflect prediction results obtained under the current publicly available datasets and established experimental protocols. Given the strong context-dependency of educational data, factors such as the granularity of learning platform records, course evaluation methods, student population structures, and instructional management systems may all influence model performance. These results primarily demonstrate that the HA-GNN-LSTM possesses strong predictive capabilities under data conditions characterized by multidimensional behavioral variables and disciplinary structural information. They should not be directly extrapolated to infer equivalent improvements across all universities, all curricular systems, and all student populations.

The ranking of feature importance based on SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) values is shown in Fig. 5. Attribution analysis reveals the underlying mechanisms influencing academic performance. Project practice scores rank first with an absolute weight of 0.31 ± 0.04, showing a significant positive correlation (r = 0.72) especially in engineering disciplines, which confirms the effectiveness of the “learning by doing” educational theory. The positive impact of sleep duration (0.19 ± 0.03) exhibits non-linear characteristics: SHAP values drop sharply when sleep duration is less than 6 h, which is consistent with the sleep deprivation theory in neuroscience research. The negative effect of stress level (-0.17 ± 0.02) increases exponentially when the stress level exceeds the critical value of 8 points, verifying the inverted U-shaped curve of the Yerkes-Dodson Law. Notably, the positive contribution of family income (0.12 ± 0.05) amplifies to 0.21 among low-income groups, reflecting that economic conditions indirectly affect academic performance through resource access channels. These findings provide empirical evidence for constructing targeted intervention strategies. For instance, psychological counseling should be prioritized for students with high stress levels.

To further elucidate the sources of model performance improvement, ablation experiments are conducted to examine the marginal contributions of key modules within the complete framework. The results are presented in Table 7. The complete HA-GNN-LSTM model achieves an MAE of 4.20 ± 0.30 and an NDCG@5 of 0.900 ± 0.010. Upon removal of the HA-GNN, the MAE increases to 5.80 ± 0.44, representing a 38.1% degradation, and the NDCG@5 decreased to 0.707 ± 0.024, representing a 21.4% degradation. It indicates that the hierarchical graph structure for modeling relationships between student behavioral features and disciplinary structures constitutes the primary source of performance improvement. When Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) imputation is replaced with mean imputation, the MAE increases to 4.86 ± 0.38 (15.8% degradation) and the NDCG@5 decreases by 8.2%, demonstrating that non-random missing data cannot be adequately recovered through simple mean substitution. When the LSTM is replaced with Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU), the MAE increases to 4.72 ± 0.35 (12.4% degradation), indicating that long-range learning trajectory modeling remains important for academic performance prediction. Upon removal of the multi-head attention mechanism, the MAE increased to 4.57 ± 0.32 (8.7% degradation), suggesting that the spatiotemporal feature fusion mechanism enhances model prediction stability. When Bayesian optimization is replaced with grid search, the MAE increases by merely 2.1%, whereas the NDCG@5 decreases by 4.5%, indicating that this module exerts limited direct impact on prediction error and its primary value lies in personalized cultivation parameter search and management decision feedback. These results collectively demonstrate that the performance improvement of the complete model arises from the synergistic effects of data imputation, hierarchical graph modeling, temporal sequence modeling, and attention-based fusion, rather than from fortuitous improvement attributable to any single complex model.

Discussion

The current results indicate that the HA-GNN-LSTM achieves a relatively pronounced error reduction compared with the TFT in the academic performance prediction task. This result should be interpreted in conjunction with the data structure. The Student Performance & Behavior Dataset encompasses multiple categories of behavioral variables directly associated with learning outcomes, enabling the model to leverage variables such as project grades, attendance, sleep, stress, and learning engagement to form strong predictive signals. Educational prediction data do not constitute a purely random noise scenario. When input variables cover learning processes, learning states, and environmental factors, deep models may achieve substantial improvements. Nevertheless, such improvements remain constrained by data sources, variable quality, and experimental settings. The smaller magnitude of improvement observed on the OULAD external dataset relative to the primary dataset suggests that the model’s advantages exhibit cross-dataset attenuation. This phenomenon indicates that cross-institutional, cross-curricular, and cross-year validation remains necessary prior to deploying the model in real-world university environments.

The experimental results of this study provide important empirical dialogue with the exploration by Thottoli et al.38 on AI-driven personalized education. Thottoli pointed out that AI-enabled education needs to break through the limitation of the “algorithmic black box,” whereas this study achieves in-depth visualization of feature attributes via HA-GNN, solving the issue of model transparency while maintaining high prediction accuracy. The core mechanism by which the HA-GNN-LSTM model outperforms benchmark models such as TFT lies in the dual alignment of the disciplinary logic space and learning trajectory time: HA-GNN captures complex hierarchical relationships among disciplines (e.g., the support of basic mathematics for engineering algorithmic ability), and LSTM accurately extracts the nonlinear trend of academic performance evolving over time. This deep fusion of spatial and temporal dimensions endows the model with stronger robustness when processing high-dimensional, heterogeneous, and noisy real-world educational data. In addition, SHAP-based attribution analysis provides educators with highly actionable intervention recommendations: administrators can directly implement precise psychological counseling or instructional support for at-risk students according to key influencing factors identified by the system, such as low project practice scores or severe insufficient sleep duration, transforming traditional “post-hoc analysis” into a closed-loop management paradigm of real-time intervention.

To address the challenges of distribution bias and spatiotemporal heterogeneity commonly encountered in industrial-scale big data analytics raised by Ikegwu et al.39, this study implements dynamic data cleaning and fairness correction through GANs imputation and KDE reweighting algorithms. Experiments confirm that the KDE mechanism significantly alleviates the risk of systematic underestimation for disciplines with small sample sizes, such as humanities and social sciences, ensuring disciplinary fairness when the model is deployed university-wide. This lays an ethical foundation for building a universal university student profiling system. In terms of human resource management, this study aligns with the view emphasized by Qader et al.40 regarding improving resource allocation agility in the public sector. The intelligent evaluation module realized via Bayesian optimization significantly shortens the response time of supervisor assignment, and the efficiency improvement exceeds the administrative process optimization scheme proposed by Qader. However, a clear boundary must be defined: the reported HR efficiency improvement, such as 60% shorter response time, is based on applied system evaluation and simulation re-enactment using the historical dataset of 5,000 students. Although it provides high quantitative value in demonstrating that algorithmic decisions outperform traditional experience-based decisions, performance in real complex operating environments requires further investigation in future prospective studies. Such rigorous boundary definition enhances the reliability of the conclusions and provides a robust methodological reference for the precise transformation of university management from experience-driven to data-intelligent-driven.

Finally, this study also needs to carefully examine the generalization and adaptation of the model across different educational systems. The current model is constructed from the single perspective of a research university, and its definitions of behavioral characteristics may exhibit significant differences when applied to vocational colleges or other educational systems. For example, vocational education emphasizes skill orientation, and its feature indicators may be more inclined toward practical training and operation rather than the academic output emphasized in research universities. Furthermore, when the model is transferred across institutions, there is a risk of “institutional bias,” meaning that the algorithm may unconsciously reinforce the selection criteria or evaluation preferences specific to the source institution. Future research will focus on applying cross-institutional domain adaptation techniques, employing lightweight fine-tuning or transfer learning strategies to construct differentiated feature mappings for different educational ecosystems, such as vocational colleges and research universities, thereby ensuring the fairness and applicability of this framework in a diversified higher education system.

The management module results should be interpreted as changes in system efficiency within simulated environments, rather than established benefits in real administrative workflows. Simulation replay eliminates uncontrollable factors such as human waiting time, approval delays, and communication overhead, conferring inherent advantages to algorithmic workflows in response time metrics. While this setup is useful for examining whether the model can generate faster matching solutions under identical input conditions, it cannot fully capture faculty-student preferences, communication negotiations, institutional constraints, and emergent affairs in real-world management contexts. Subsequent research should employ prospective pilot deployments, A/B testing, or quasi-experimental designs to further evaluate the impact of this framework on authentic educational management quality.

Conclusion

Research contribution

This study realizes personalized teaching and management innovation in universities by constructing a talent training model driven by data, algorithm and decision-making. In response to the research questions, the experimental results strongly prove that the integration of HA-GNN and LSTM can significantly improve the accuracy of academic prediction in heterogeneous environments, with , and the Bayesian optimization mechanism has obvious advantages in improving the matching degree of course recommendation. At the theoretical level, this study proposes an evidence-based methodological advancement and solves the black-box interpretation problem of educational data through the hierarchical attention mechanism. At the application level, although the improvements in management efficiency reported in this study (e.g., a 60% reduction in tutor assignment response time) are obtained from simulation evaluations based on historical data replay, these quantitative pieces of evidence fully demonstrate the significant potential of the proposed algorithm in optimizing resource allocation and decision-making processes in higher education institutions.

At the management application level, simulation evaluation based on historical data replay demonstrated that the integration of student competence profiles output by the HA-GNN-LSTM with the Bayesian optimization decision module can reduce advisor allocation response time, decrease laboratory resource idle rate, and improve position or major matching accuracy under established constraints. These results indicate the framework’s potential for assisting resource allocation optimization in higher education. However, as the management module has not yet undergone prospective deployment validation in real-world scenarios, the associated benefits cannot be interpreted as direct causal effects in actual organizational operations.

Future works and research limitations

Although this study has achieved preliminary results in personalized talent cultivation and management optimization, the following limitations remain. First, the proposed model is currently constructed primarily based on samples from research universities, and its definitions of behavioral characteristics may exhibit significant institutional bias when applied to vocational colleges or different educational systems. Therefore, future work will focus on domain adaptation strategies, using lightweight fine-tuning techniques to construct differentiated feature mappings for different educational ecosystems, such as vocational and technical education and research universities, thereby improving the generalizability of the model. Second, although this study has conducted external benchmark validation using the large-scale OULAD dataset, multi-center validation is still needed to reduce the potential influence of selection criteria specific to particular institutions on the algorithmic recommendation logic. Furthermore, in terms of real-time capability and privacy, the current weekly update frequency is insufficient to capture sudden academic risks. Future research will explore distributed modeling approaches based on federated learning, introducing second-level physiological signal monitoring and edge computing architectures while ensuring data privacy, to further improve the accuracy and timeliness of academic interventions.
