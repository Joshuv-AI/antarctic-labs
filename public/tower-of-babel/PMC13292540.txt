Prognostic scoring based on ER, PgR and HER2-low from the CYCLHER study: strengths and unaddressed challenges for global clinical application
Authors: Yueting Tan, Benyuan Cheng, Zhangcheng Feng, Kun He, Xiaobing Xie
Published: 2026

HR+/HER2-negative metastatic breast cancer (mBC) is the most common subtype, and endocrine therapy combined with CDK4/6 inhibitors serves as standard first-line treatment. The prognostic and predictive values of ER, PgR and HER2-low expression remain controversial across real-world cohorts.

The multi-center retrospective CYCLHER study enrolled 597 Italian patients receiving first-line CDK4/6i plus endocrine therapy [1]. With a 41-month median follow-up, the authors confirmed HER2-low as an adverse prognostic factor for real-world progression-free survival (rwPFS), identified optimal ER (≥87%) and PgR (≥60%) cut-offs, and established a three-biomarker prognostic score. This commentary evaluates its strengths, methodological limitations and implications for Asian populations.

The CYCLHER study delivers three key merits. As one of the largest European real-world cohorts with mature long-term survival data, its median rwPFS reached 28.1 months, supplementing evidence from phase III trials [1,2]. Different from the 1% ER positivity threshold defined by ASCO/CAP guidelines, the study adopted data-driven cut-offs to refine risk stratification. Its simple scoring system stratifies patients into four distinct risk groups and relies solely on routine IHC, which is widely applicable in general oncology settings.

Nevertheless, several limitations restrict its global application. First, the cohort only included Caucasian patients. Published Asian studies have demonstrated distinct HR distribution and HER2-low prevalence in Chinese mBC patients, so the identified cut-offs cannot be directly applied to East Asian populations [3]. Second, centralized pathological review was absent, and the study failed to distinguish the HER2-ultralow subgroup defined by the DESTINY-Breast 06 trial, a population sensitive to trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd) [4]. Third, the ER and PgR thresholds were derived from internal analysis only, raising the risk of statistical overfitting without external validation [5]. Fourth, unbalanced use of CDK4/6 inhibitors (66% palbociclib, 5.7% abemaciclib) limits the generalizability of conclusions.

Inconsistent real-world findings further complicate interpretation. European cohorts support the poor prognosis of HER2-low disease, while multiple Asian studies show no significant impact of HER2-low status on CDK4/6i efficacy [6,7]. In addition, post-progression treatments in this study were dominated by traditional chemotherapy, which cannot reflect current regimens including novel SERDs and HER2-targeted ADCs [8].

In summary, CYCLHER provides solid real-world evidence for biomarker-based risk stratification in HR+/HER2-negative mBC. Given ethnic disparities and methodological defects, we propose a three-step research roadmap for Asian cohorts: recalculate population-adapted ER/PgR cut-offs and validate the scoring system; conduct prospective trials with unified pathology and standardized HER2-ultralow classification; combine IHC markers with PAM50 subtyping and ctDNA detection to explore early ADC intervention, referring to the ongoing PONTIAC trial [9].
