Algebraic Hodge generic points are dense
Gregorio Baldi, Gal Binyamini, David Urbanik

Abstract
Let f : X → S f:X\to S be a quasi-projective family of varieties defined over ℚ ¯ ⊂ ℂ \overline{\mathbb{Q}}\subset\mathbb{C} . We show that the points of S ⁡ ( ℚ ¯ ) S(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}) that are Hodge generic for the variation of Hodge structures associated to f f are analytically dense in S ⁡ ( ℂ ) S(\mathbb{C}) . In fact, in the spirit of the Grothendieck period conjecture and under a large monodromy assumption, we prove the density of the points of S ⁡ ( ℚ ¯ ) S(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}) where the periods of the fibre do not satisfy extra relations “up to degree δ \delta ”. As a by-product, we also establish new instances of the Mumford-Tate conjecture, beyond the realm of abelian motives. When the base S S is a curve, we provide quantitative estimates for points satisfying these properties. The main technical contribution is a new result on relations satisfied by solutions of G G -operators, which relies on height estimates due to Bombieri and André.

1.3
Strategy: controlling period relations of bounded degree

Our method for finding Hodge generic points ultimately reduces to the question of how to certifiably produce fibres of algebraic families whose periods do not satisfy exceptional
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
-algebraic relations of degree
\delta>0

is some fixed integer. See indeed our discussion in §

For simplicity, we describe the strategy in the

f:X\to S

K

\mathbb{V}=R^{j}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}
to be the associated polarizable
\mathbb{Z}

-points on

2.1
Hodge conjecture

A fundamental question in algebraic geometry is to understand, given a smooth projective family
f:X\to S
, which fibres
X_{s}
of
f
carry more algebraic cycles than the geometric generic fibre
X_{\overline{\eta}}
of
f

H^{\bullet}(-)

k

H^{\bullet}(X^{k}_{s})
(where
X^{k}
denotes the
k

X_{\overline{\eta}}
; see also the introduction of
[
12
]
.

Several central conjectures in mathematics give precise criteria on when such extra cycles should exist. The first of these is the Hodge conjecture, which works with the Betti cohomology.

Conjecture 2.1
.

(Hodge conjecture)

Let
Y
be a smooth projective algebraic variety over
\mathbb{C}
, let
j\geq 0
be an integer, and suppose that
c\in H^{2j}_{B}(Y,\mathbb{Q}(j))
is a class which lies inside the middle Hodge summand
H^{j,j}\subset H^{2j}_{B}(Y,\mathbb{C})
. Then
c
is in the
\mathbb{Q}
-span of the algebraic cycle classes.

Classes
c
as in

Conjecture 2.3

(Grothendieck Period Conjecture)

When

Conjecture 2.8

2.4
Related work

The most common approach to the existence of Hodge generic points is via the “easy direction” of the Mumford-Tate conjecture: roughly, the statement that Hodge cycles associated to a cohomology group
H^{j}(X_{s},\mathbb{Z})
above a point
s\in S(K)
should constrain the image of corresponding
\ell
-adic Galois representations
\rho_{s}:\textrm{Gal}(\overline{K}/K)\to\operatorname{GL}(H^{j}(X_{s,\overline{K}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}))
. This is automatic under the Hodge conjecture, as well as weaker versions of the Hodge conjecture that assert such cycles have appropriate motivic properties. For instance, Voisin in
[
50
, Thm. 1.11]
explains how the existence of many Hodge generic
S(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
-points can be proven using the conjecture that Hodge cycles are absolute Hodge. In a similar vein, André in
[
5
, Thm 5.2(3)]
gives a similar statement for points in
S(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
admitting extra cycles that are motivated in an appropriate sense, and
[
43
, Thm. 1]
gives such arguments in the explicit case of complete intersections and where the Hodge cycles one wishes to avoid come from line bundles.

The basic principle of all such arguments can be found in
[
43
]
. The idea is to assemble the representations
\rho_{s}
into a family by considering the associated representation
\rho:\pi^{\'{e}t}_{1}(S,s_{0})\to\operatorname{GL}(H^{j}(X_{s_{0},\overline{K}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}))
of the étale fundamental group at some base-point
s_{0}\in S(K)
, and then study the set of
s
for which the restriction
\rho_{s}
maps into a strict subgroup of the image of
\rho
. A result of Serre
[
39
, §10.6]
(cf. the introduction to
[
16
]
and
[
18
, Fact 3.3.1.1]
) can be used to show that such
s
lie in a
thin set
, defined as in
[
39
, §9.1]
. When
S
has many
K
-points, one can show that the complement of such a thin set is analytically dense, hence obtain the corresponding Hodge-theoretic density statement under Hodge or absolute Hodge-like assumptions.

We briefly mention that, in the case where
f
is an abelian family, Masser
[
33
]
proved that the rank of the endomorphism ring does not jump at almost all
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
-points using transcendental methods. This result builds on difficult work by Masser-Wüstholz on endomorphism ring estimates for abelian varieties. We will not have such tools available to us.

In a related direction, the
p
-adic density of the Noether–Lefschetz locus is investigated in
[
34
]
. Using crystalline cohomology, the authors of
[
34
]
strengthen Terasoma’s theorems by proving that the Noether-Lefschetz locus is nowhere
p
-adically dense, under adequate assumptions on
p
. We refer also to the surveys
[
51
]
, and
[
17
]
for results on related topics. In general, the mixed case seems to be less investigated.

The period conjecture,
2.3
, is known for the cohomology of projective spaces and elliptic curves with complex multiplication; the case of projective spaces is the transcendence of
\pi
, and the result for elliptic curves is due to
[
21
]
. For other special cases, we refer also to
[
15
]
.

André has also used the
G
-function method to address certain cases of the period conjecture up to degree
\delta
, however, and this result is perhaps the closest to our

and
6.8
. Indeed, in
[
2
, Ch. IX (page 193)]
André considered families

of abelian schemes with (completely) multiplicative reduction in
s_{0}
, and projective smooth morphisms satisfying a certain strong degeneration condition in
s_{0}
. (See page 176 for the definition of

, as well as Mustafin’s work
[
36
]
). He then proved that

of sufficiently large height and which are sufficiently close to

satisfy the following condition: every polynomial relation of degree
\leq\delta
with coefficients in
K
among the values at
s
of the locally invariant periods comes from specializations at

where the family degenerates. However, our conclusion is weaker and only shows that “99 percent” of the points we produce are Hodge-generic.

As for the Mumford-Tate conjecture, to the best of our knowledge, little is known outside of motives that can be related to abelian varieties, where various partial results are known; see, for instance, the survey
[
35
]
of Moonen.
For abelian varieties

3.1

By a

[

[
47
]

Definition 3.1

A

G(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}[[z]],

generates a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}

writing
d_{n}\geq 1

By a
differential algebra

we mean an algebra
\mathcal{A}
which is a module over

\overline{\mathbb{Q}}[z]

We consider the differential algebra

\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{C}\{\!\{z\}\!\}\big[(z^{a})_{a\in\mathbb{C}},\log(z)\big],

(3.1)

whose elements are expressions

P(z)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}z^{a_{i}}\log(z)^{b_{i}}G_{i}(z),

(3.2)

where

We consider the differential subalgebra

which consists of expressions of the form

P(z)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}z^{a_{i}}\log(z)^{b_{i}}G_{i}(z),

Following

Definition 3.2

Suppose

The

Definition 3.4

A differential operator

5.1

\mathbb{Z}
-Hodge structures and period maps

Let

•

\mathbb{Z}_{S^{\operatorname{an}}}

•

•

is a filtered regular algebraic flat connection on

such that the analytification
(\mathcal{V}^{\operatorname{an}},\nabla^{\operatorname{an}})

\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{C}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{S^{\operatorname{an}}}}\mathcal{O}_{S^{{\operatorname{an}}}}

(the
filtration

3

[
28
]

\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}

We denote the algebraic monodromy group by
\mathbf{M}=\mathbf{M}(\mathbb{V})
, or sometimes
\mathbf{M}_{s_{0}}
and recall here that it is a
\mathbb{Q}
-normal subgroup of the derived subgroup of the generic Mumford-Tate group of

, as established in
[
3
, Thm. 1]
.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{C}
be a field, and let
\mathbb{V}
be a
\mathbb{Z}
VHS on a
K
-algebraic open subvariety

. Fix a point

. Then there exists a
K
-rational curve

passing through

which is Hodge generic for
\mathbb{V}
.

Proof.

Let
g:\mathcal{R}\to T
be the family of all curves in
U
obtained as intersections with

of one-dimensional affine linear subspaces of
\mathbb{A}^{\ell}
passing through
p
. By the Lefschetz hyperplane theorem, there is a non-empty

-algebraic open subset

such that, for each fibre

with

, the map
\pi_{1}(g^{-1}(t),p)\to\pi_{1}(U,p)
is surjective. Let
t\in T^{\circ}(K)
and set
R=g^{-1}(t)
. By construction

has the same algebraic monodromy of

. We claim that this implies that they have the same generic Mumford–Tate group. By a theorem of Chevalley (see
[
24
, Prop. 3.1]
) every algebraic group is the stabilizer of a line in some faithful linear algebraic representation. Recall also that the algebraic monodromy group
\mathbf{M}_{R}
of
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\mathbb{V}\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{R}}
is a subgroup of the Mumford-Tate group
\mathbf{G}_{R}=\mathbf{MT}({\left.\kern-1.2pt\mathbb{V}\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{R}})
of
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\mathbb{V}\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{R}}
(cf.
[
3
, Thm. 1]
and the theorem of the fixed part). Let

be a rational line (in some tensorial representation) fixed by

is invariant under the algebraic monodromy of

. By construction of

is fixed by the algebraic monodromy of

∎

The next lemmas are needed to control the representations needed to define Mumford-Tate subgroups associated to a fixed graded-polarized lattice
(V,W_{\bullet},Q)
.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
(V,W_{\bullet},Q)
be a graded-polarized lattice, and let
\mathcal{S}=\{h^{p,q}\}_{p,q\geq 0}
be a set of Hodge numbers. Then there are finitely many
\mathbb{Q}
-algebraic families of Mumford-Tate subgroups of
V
associated to graded-polarized mixed Hodge structures with Hodge numbers those of
\mathcal{S}
. In particular, letting
g:\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{Y}
be one of these families, each fibre of
g
is an algebraic subgroup of
\operatorname{GL}(V)
preserving
W_{\bullet}
, and all Mumford-Tate subgroups associated to
(V,W_{\bullet},Q,\mathcal{S})
appear as a
\mathbb{Q}
-fibre of some such

.

Proof.

In the case where
W_{\bullet}
is trivial, so the Hodge structures are pure, this follows from the fact that there are finitely many Mumford-Tate subgroups of
\operatorname{GL}(V)
up to complex conjugation, as proven in
[
50
, Thm. 4.14]
(and attributed to P. Deligne); see also
[
4
, Lemma (page 58)]
. Next we consider the short exact sequence (cf.
[
3
, Lemma 2]
)

1\to U\to\operatorname{GL}(V)^{W}\xrightarrow{\psi}\operatorname{GL}(\operatorname{gr}^{W}V)\to 1.

Then
(\operatorname{gr}^{W}V,Q)
is a polarized lattice, so applying the result in the pure case the groups
\psi^{-1}(M)
, with
M
a Mumford-Tate subgroup of
\operatorname{gr}^{W}V
, belong to finitely many algebraic families. Then all maximal reductive subgroups of
\psi^{-1}(M)
are isomorphic to
M
, and can likewise be parameterized algebraically in terms of

. In particular, there is a family

which parameterizes all reductive subgroups of
\operatorname{GL}(V)^{W}
which map isomorphically to a Mumford-Tate subgroup of
\operatorname{gr}^{W}V
.

On the other hand, subgroups of

are all affine linear, hence belong to some family

. Given two families of subgroups of
\operatorname{GL}(V)
(or
\operatorname{GL}(V)^{W}
), extension groups whose factors are the fibres of those families can also be parameterized by an algebraic family. Each Mumford-Tate group associated to
(V,W_{\bullet},Q,\mathcal{S})
is such an extension, so the result follows.
∎

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
(V,W_{\bullet},Q,\mathcal{S})
be as in
5.6
. Then there exists an integer

with the following property: for any Mumford-Tate group

associated to a graded-polarized mixed Hodge structure on

and with Hodge numbers in

, the group

is the stabilizer of a line in a tensor representation

Proof.

By

of algebraic subgroups of

∎

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
\mathbb{V}
be a
\mathbb{Z}

with associated flag variety

. Let

\mathbb{V}=[R^{j}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}]_{\operatorname{prim}}
at some point
s_{0}\in S(\mathbb{C})
, and lets
P
be the graph of the change-of-basis isomorphism. Let
\mathbf{G}_{s_{0}}
be the realization of the generic Mumford-Tate group in
\operatorname{GL}(\mathbb{V}_{s_{0}})
. Then if
t
is a tensor invariant of
\mathbf{G}_{s_{0}}
,
t
extends to a flat section
\widetilde{t}
of a local system in the Tannakian category generated by
\mathbb{V}
, and has a corresponding de Rham realization
\widetilde{t}_{\operatorname{dR}}
. Moreover, as a consequence of our assumption (
2.2
), the section
\widetilde{t}_{\operatorname{dR}}
is defined over
K
. Then the relation on
(s,A)\in S\times\operatorname{GL}_{m}
given by
A\cdot\widetilde{t}_{\operatorname{dR}}(s)=t
is a
K
-algebraic relation on the graph of
\mathbf{P}
. Ranging over all such tensor invariants
t
, one sees that
B_{\mathbf{P}}
lies inside a torsor
B_{\operatorname{MT}}
for the Mumford-Tate group
\mathbf{G}_{s_{0}}
.

On the other hand, by analytic continuation,
B_{\mathbf{P}}
contains a torsor
B_{M}
for the (complex points of the) algebraic monodromy group
\mathbf{M}
. Since
\mathbf{M}=\mathbf{G}_{s_{0}}/\mathbb{G}_{m}
, it suffices to show that the extra relation of the form
\det\mathbf{P}(s)=F(s)
, where
F(s)
is some algebraic function, is not defined over
K
unless
j=0
. Specializing to some
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
-point
s\in S(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
, one observes via the standard computation (cf.
[
24
, §1]
) that
F(s)
is a
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
-multiple of
\pi^{j}
, and therefore not
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
-algebraic.
∎

\mathbb{P}^{1}-\Delta

f(x)(x-t)

.

6.3.2
The Mumford-Tate Conjecture: proof of
2.10

Assume first that
\ell
is a prime (recall our conventions on
\ell
from §
1.1
). Arguing as in the beginning of the proof of
6.4
, we reduce ourselves to the case where
S\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}
, with the inclusion defined over a number field
K
. We will fix a parameter
s=1/t
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, and show that points in
S_{\operatorname{MT},j,\ell}
accumulate near
s=0
.

As explained in
Section

2.4
, Serre’s version of the Hilbert irreducibly theorem shows all points of
\mathbb{P}^{1}(K)
outside of a thin set have
\ell
-adic monodromy “as big as possible”; let us recall what this means.

First, we recall that a thin subset of
\mathbb{P}^{1}(K)
is a subset given as the union of the images of finitely many maps
C(K)\to\mathbb{P}^{1}(K)
, where
C\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
is a ramified covering of degree
>1
. Regarding
\ell
-adic monodromy, recall that we have a split exact sequence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{\textrm{\'{e}t}}_{1}(S_{\overline{K}},\overline{t})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{\textrm{\'{e}t}}_{1}(S,\overline{t})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\textrm{Gal}(\overline{K}/K)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma_{t}}

(6.1)

with the section labeled “
\sigma_{t}
” corresponding to a choice of
K
-point
t\in S(K)
. The family
f:X\to S
over
K
induces a lisse
\ell
-adic sheaf
[R^{j}f_{\'{e}t,*}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}]_{\operatorname{prim}}
, which can be identified with a representation
\rho:\pi^{\textrm{\'{e}t}}_{1}(S,\overline{t})\to\operatorname{GL}(H^{j}_{\'{e}t}(X_{\overline{t}},\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})_{\operatorname{prim}})
. By
[
39
, §10.6]
(cf.
[
18
, Fact 3.3.1.1]
) there exists a thin subset of
S(K)
outside of which
\rho
and
\rho\circ\sigma_{t}
have the same image.

Observe that the intersection with
\mathbb{N}=\{[t:1]\,|\,t\in\mathbb{Z}\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}(K)
and a thin subset
\mathcal{T}
of
\mathbb{P}^{1}(K)
has density zero: indeed the number of integral points of height at most
H
of
\mathcal{T}
is
O(H^{1/2})
(cf.
[
40
, p. 27]
). By
6.6
, the integral values of
t
that are Hodge generic have natural density one in
\mathbb{N}
, so we obtain an infinite subset of
S(K)
, accumulating near
s=0
, where the both the Mumford-Tate group and the Galois action are as large as possible.

Now the generic Mumford-Tate group at
t
has the algebraic monodromy group
\mathbf{M}_{t}\subset\operatorname{GL}(H^{j}(X_{t,\overline{\mathbb{C}}},\mathbb{Q}))
as a subgroup, and the Zariski closure of

\textrm{im}\left[\pi^{\'{e}t}_{1}(S,\overline{t})\to\operatorname{GL}(H^{j}_{\'{e}t}(X_{\overline{t}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})_{\operatorname{prim}})\right]

has as a subgroup the Zariski closure of the image of
\pi^{\'{e}t}_{1}(S_{\overline{K}},\overline{t})
. Under the Artin comparison theorem
H^{j}(X_{t,\mathbb{C}},\mathbb{Q})_{\operatorname{prim}}\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}\xrightarrow{\sim}H^{j}(X_{\overline{t}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})_{\operatorname{prim}}
these two geometric monodromy groups agree by
[
45
, Lem. 3.3]
(cf.
[
10
]
). It then follows from (
2.2
) that the Mumford-Tate group of
H^{j}(X_{t,\mathbb{C}},\mathbb{Q})_{\operatorname{prim}}
and the Zariski closure of the image of
\rho\circ\sigma_{t}
agree under the Artin comparison, up to the homothety factor
\mathbb{G}_{m}
. This homothety factor is non-trivial on either side if and only if
j=0
, so the result follows.

The case where
\ell
is not prime follows similarly by carrying out the argument for each prime factor of
\ell
and observing that the intersection of finitely many density one sets still has density one.

Remark 6.9
.

If one assumes only the validity of the first equality of (
2.2
), i.e., that the monodromy
\mathbf{M}
is equal to the generic Mumford-Tate group up to the diagonal
\mathbb{G}_{m}
factor (which is needed whenever the weight is non-zero), the above argument shows the abundance of points
t\in S(K)
where
\mathbf{MT}(X_{t})_{\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}}
is naturally contained in the Zariski closure of the
\ell
-adic monodromy at
s
. Indeed, one obtains that, up to the action of
\mathbb{G}_{m,\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}}
corresponding to the cyclotomic character,
\mathbf{MT}(X_{t})_{\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}}
is contained in the Zariski closure of the image of
\pi_{1}^{\textrm{\'{e}t}}(S_{\overline{K}})
. This is “half of” the Mumford-Tate conjecture for
X_{t}
; the other half would follow if one knew that the global Hodge tensors which define the generic Mumford-Tate group satisfied the
\ell
-adic absolute Hodge property.

6.3.3
Families of smooth hypersurfaces

In this final section, we prove of the results announced in the introductory section §
1.1
, and in particular discuss applications to the Hodge and Tate conjectures. Let
S=U_{n,d}
be the parameter space of smooth degree
d
hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{P}^{n+1}
. This is naturally defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, for more details on its construction see e.g.
[
11
]
. We let
\mathbb{V}
be the polarized
\mathbb{Z}
VHS associated to the primitive cohomology in degree
n
. We would like to show density of the locus

S_{\operatorname{Motivic},j,\ell,\delta}\;=\;S_{P,j,\delta}\;\cap\;S_{\mathrm{H},j}\;\cap\;S_{\mathrm{T},\ell,j}\;\cap\;S_{\mathrm{MT},\ell,j}.

The idea is that each of loci appearing in the above intersection has density one (after restriction to any rational curve, and counting integer-valued points), and therefore their intersection is again dense.

Proof of
1.1
.

First, recall that the primitive cohomology in degree
n
of the universal family of smooth hypersurfaces on
U_{n,d}(\mathbb{C})
has large monodromy and so satisfies the assumption (
2.2
); see indeed
[
12
, Sec. 9.1]
and references therein. Arguing as in the previous sections, we can reduce ourselves to the case of a Hodge generic family of smooth hypersurfaces over
S=\mathbb{P}^{1}-\Sigma
, defined over a number field
K\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\subset\mathbb{C}
(as usual, we fix a parameter
s=1/t
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
). Consider the loci

\mathbb{N}_{\mathrm{MT}}:=\{t\in\mathbb{N}:[t:1]\in S_{\mathrm{MT},\ell,n}\}\ \ \text{ and }\ \ \mathbb{N}_{P,\delta}:=\{t\in\mathbb{N}:[t:1]\in S_{\mathrm{P},n,\delta}\}.

Since condition (
2.2
) is satisfied, we can apply the density one version of
6.8
(which is argued analogously to the density one result
6.6
) to see that
\mathbb{N}_{P,\delta}
has natural density one. Moreover, as explained in the proof in
Section

6.3.2
,
\mathbb{N}_{\mathrm{MT}}
has also density one. It follows that the subset
\mathbb{N}_{\mathrm{MT}}\cap\mathbb{N}_{P,\delta}
has density one in
\mathbb{N}
, since it is the intersection of two density one subsets.

From the construction in
Section

6.3.2
, the hypersurface
X_{t}
corresponding to a
t\in\mathbb{N}_{\mathrm{MT}}\cap\mathbb{N}_{P,\delta}
is Hodge generic. We are left to show that the (tannakian) Hodge and Tate conjectures hold for
X_{t}
. Since we already know that
X_{t}
satisfies the Mumford-Tate conjecture, it is enough to show that the Hodge conjecture holds. The fact that a Hodge generic hypersurface satisfies the (tannakian) Hodge conjecture is proven in
6.10
below, which follows from considerations on the cohomology of hypersurfaces and Weyl’s theorems on invariants of symplectic and orthogonal groups. We conclude that the intersection (
1.1
) admits points arbitrarily close to
s=0
in
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Since the curve
S
and coordinate
s
was arbitrary, we conclude by dévissage the desired analytic density in
U_{n,d}(\mathbb{C})
.

∎

Proof of
1.2
.

Same proof as above, using that Lefschetz pencils have large monodromy (cf.
[
1
, Exposé XVII]
).
∎

Lemma 6.10
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{n+1}
be a smooth hypersurface of degree
d\geq 1
over
\mathbb{C}
, let

V\;:=\;H^{n}(X,\mathbb{Q})_{\textrm{prim}},\qquad T^{a,b,c}(V)\;:=\;V^{\otimes a}\otimes(V^{\vee})^{\otimes b}\otimes\mathbb{Q}(c),\quad a,b,c\geq 0.

Suppose
X
is Hodge generic in
S=U_{n,d}
. Then
X
satisfies the (tannakian) Hodge conjecture: every Hodge tensor in some
T^{a,b,c}(V)
is algebraic.

This lemma is certainly well-known, but we give it for completeness. We set
Q\colon V\otimes V\to\mathbb{Q}(-n)
for the standard intersection form on
V
(the cup product), let
h=c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(1))\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})
be the hyperplane class.

Proof.

Consider the diagonal
\Delta_{X}\subset X\times X
with fundamental class
[\Delta_{X}]\in H^{2n}(X\times X,\mathbb{Q})
. By the Künneth formula,

[\Delta_{X}]\;=\;\sum_{i=0}^{2n}\pi_{i},\qquad\pi_{i}\;\in\;H^{2n-i}(X,\mathbb{Q})\otimes H^{i}(X,\mathbb{Q}).

It is well-known that every
\pi_{i}
is an algebraic cycle class (with
\mathbb{Q}
-coefficients). Indeed, by the Lefschetz hyperplane theorem, the restriction map
H^{i}(\mathbb{P}^{n+1},\mathbb{Q})\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}H^{i}(X,\mathbb{Q})
is an isomorphism for
i<n
, and by Poincaré duality the analogous statement holds for
i>n
. So
H^{i}(X,\mathbb{Q})=0
for
i
odd and
i\neq n
, hence
\pi_{i}=0
for these
i
. On the other hand
H^{2k}(X,\mathbb{Q})=\mathbb{Q}\cdot h^{k}
for
2k\neq n
. One then observes that
\pi_{2k}=\frac{1}{d}\,h^{n-k}\otimes h^{k}
and
\pi^{\prime}_{n}\;=\;[\Delta_{X}]-\sum\frac{1}{d}\,h^{n-k}\otimes h^{k}
(where the sums ranges from
k=0
to
n
), are algebraic cycle classes; note that
\pi^{\prime}_{n}=\pi_{n}
when
n
is odd and differs by
\frac{1}{d}h^{n/2}\otimes h^{n/2}
when
n
is even. By construction, the cohomology class associated to
\pi^{\prime}_{n}
lies in
V\otimes V^{\vee}
.

Note that, because
\pi_{n}
also gives an algebraic-cycle-induced isomorphism between
V\otimes\mathbb{Q}(n)
and
V^{\vee}
, we may assume that
b=0
and
a=2r
is even. When
n
is odd (resp. even), the intersection form
Q
is alternating (resp. symmetric),
so
V
carries a symplectic (resp. orthogonal) structure, and the generic Mumford-Tate group is
\mathbf{MT}(V)=\mathrm{GSp}(V,Q)
(resp.
\operatorname{GSO}(V,Q)
). By Weyl’s Fundamental Theorems of invariant theory for the symplectic and orthogonal groups
[
26
, Ch. 17,18]
,
[
30
, Thm. 1B]
all Hodge tensors of interest lie in
V^{\otimes 2r}\otimes\mathbb{Q}(nr)
and are generated by taking products of
\pi^{\prime}_{n}
and applying the action of the symmetric group
S_{2r}
. The action of
S_{2r}
in this case corresponds to algebraic automorphisms of
X^{2r}
which permute the factors, so the result follows.
