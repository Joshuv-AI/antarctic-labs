The uncertainty principles of random signals related to the linear canonical transform
Jia-Yin Peng, Bing-Zhao Li

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate uncertainty principles for random signals associated with the linear canonical transform (LCT). First, the LCT of random signals is formulated on the probability space. Based on this representation, the Heisenberg uncertainty principle is established to characterize the relationship between the expectations in the time and frequency domains. Furthermore, the Donoho-Stark uncertainty principle, developed from a measure theoretic perspective, reveals that a random signal cannot be simultaneously concentrated on arbitrarily small sets in both the time and frequency domains. The bounds obtained in these two uncertainty principles explicitly depend on the LCT parameters, indicating that the LCT offers greater flexibility than the Fourier transform (FT). The corresponding results in the fractional Fourier transform and FT domains are also given as special cases.



1
Introduction

Uncertainty principles constitute an important topic in harmonic analysis and were originally rooted in quantum mechanics. The celebrated Heisenberg uncertainty principle (HUP), proposed by the German physicist Heisenberg, states that the position and momentum of an electron cannot be measured simultaneously with arbitrary precision
10
. From the perspective of signal processing, the HUP reveals a fundamental trade-off between the energy distribution of a signal in the time and frequency domains
21
. In mathematical terms, for a signal
f(t)
and its Fourier transform (FT)
\hat{f}(\xi)
, the time duration
\Delta f(t)
and the frequency bandwidth
\Delta\hat{f}(\xi)
satisfy the following inequality
3

\Delta f(t)\Delta\hat{f}(\xi)\geq\frac{1}{2}.

(1)

This principle has inspired extensive subsequent studies on uncertainty principles and serves as a significant tool in time-frequency analysis
23
,
15
,
4
. As a representative development, Donoho and Stark studied time and frequency limitations from the viewpoint of measurable sets
7
. Their result shows that a nonzero signal and its FT cannot both be concentrated on sets with arbitrarily small measures.
More precisely, if a signal
f(t)
is
\varepsilon_{T}
-concentrated on a measurable set
T
and its FT
\hat{f}(\xi)
is
\varepsilon_{W}
-concentrated on another measurable set
W
, with
\varepsilon_{T},\varepsilon_{W}\geq 0
and
\varepsilon_{T}+\varepsilon_{W}<1
, then
2

|T||W|\geq\left(1-\varepsilon_{T}-\varepsilon_{W}\right)^{2}.

(2)

The Donoho-Stark uncertainty principle (DSUP) provides a useful theoretical tool for signal recovery from incomplete, missing and bandlimited observations
6
,
16
,
19
.

The above uncertainty principles and their extensions have led to a substantial body of theoretical results, most of which have been developed for deterministic signals that depend only on the time variable
t

11
,
8
. However, real signals are often accompanied by random effects caused by measurements, environments or perturbations.
Therefore, the study of random signals is of significant practical importance in signal processing
14
,
18
.
In general, a random signal can be represented as
f(t,\eta)
, where the random parameter
\eta
is defined on the probability space.
Since
f(t,\eta)
reduces to a deterministic signal for each fixed
\eta
, random signals can be regarded as a generalization of deterministic signals
17
,
22
.

In recent years, uncertainty principles for random signals have begun to attract increasing attention. Dang et al. investigated HUP and DSUP for random signals
5
. More precisely, they established a HUP for random signals in the FT domain, which can be expressed as

\mathbb{E}_{\eta}\!\left[\sigma_{t}^{2}(\eta)\right]\mathbb{E}_{\eta}\!\left[\sigma_{\xi}^{2}(\eta)\right]\geq\frac{1}{4}+\left[\int_{\Omega}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\left|(t-\mu_{t})\left(\frac{\partial\theta(t,\eta)}{\partial t}-\mu_{\xi}\right)\right|\psi^{2}(t,\eta)\,dt\,dP\right]^{2},

(3)

where

\mathbb{E}_{\eta}\!\left[\sigma_{t}^{2}(\eta)\right]=\int_{\Omega}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}(t-\mu_{t})^{2}|f(t,\eta)|^{2}\,dt\,dP,

(4)

and

\mathbb{E}_{\eta}\!\left[\sigma_{\xi}^{2}(\eta)\right]=\int_{\Omega}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}(\xi-\mu_{\xi})^{2}|\hat{f}(\xi,\eta)|^{2}\,d\xi\,dP.

(5)

Here,
\mu_{t}
and
\mu_{\xi}
denote the mean time and the mean Fourier frequency, respectively, given by

\mu_{t}=\int_{\Omega}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}t|f(t,\eta)|^{2}\,dt\,dP,

(6)

and

\mu_{\xi}=\int_{\Omega}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\xi|\hat{f}(\xi,\eta)|^{2}\,d\xi\,dP.

(7)

In addition, they established a DSUP for random signals in the FT domain. More precisely, let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
be a probability space,
B_{T}\times T
and
B_{W}\times W
are measurable sets in
\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
.
If
f(t,\eta)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\Omega)
is
\varepsilon_{B_{T}\times T}
-concentrated on
B_{T}\times T
and
\hat{f}(\xi,\eta)
is
\varepsilon_{B_{W}\times W}
-concentrated on
B_{W}\times W
, then

|T||W|\,P(B_{T}\cap B_{W})\geq\left(1-\varepsilon_{B_{T}\times T}-\varepsilon_{B_{W}\times W}\right)^{2}.

(8)

Here,
|T|
and
|W|
denote the Lebesgue measures of
T
and
W
, respectively, while
P(B_{T}\cap B_{W})
describes the probability that the corresponding time domain and frequency domain concentration events occur simultaneously. This result shows that the simultaneous concentration of a random signal and its FT is constrained not only by the measures of the time and frequency sets, but also by the probability of the associated random events.

Motivated by the above developments, this paper focuses on uncertainty principles for random signals in the linear canonical transform (LCT)
9
. As a generalization of the FT and the fractional Fourier transform (FrFT), the LCT offers greater flexibility through its parameter selection and serves as a powerful tool for nonstationary signal analysis
1
,
20
,
13
. Therefore, the study of random signals in the LCT domain is of considerable importance for analyzing signals involving both nonstationarity and random variations in practical signal processing
12
.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section
2
introduces the basic definitions required for the subsequent analysis. Section
3
establishes the HUP for random signals in the LCT domain. Section
4
further investigates the DSUP for random signals in the LCT domain. Finally, Section
5
concludes the paper.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we review the mathematical representation of random signals and their Fourier transform (FT), and extend the fractional Fourier transform (FrFT) and the linear canonical transform (LCT) to random signals.

Definition 1
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
be a probability space, where
\Omega
represents the sample set,
\mathcal{F}
is a
\sigma
-algebra and
P
denotes the probability measure. A random signal is represented as
5

f(t,\eta)=\psi(t,\eta)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}\theta(t,\eta)},\ \ t\in\mathbb{R},\ \ \eta\in\Omega,

(9)

where
\psi(t,\eta)
and
\theta(t,\eta)
denote the amplitude and phase of the random signal, respectively, with
t
being the time variable and
\eta
representing the random parameter.

Assume that
f(\cdot,\eta)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
for almost every
\eta\in\Omega
. Then for each fixed
\eta
,
f(\cdot,\eta)
can be regarded as a deterministic signal on
\mathbb{R}
. Its energy is given by

\|f(\cdot,\eta)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(t,\eta)|^{2}\,dt.

(10)

A random signal is said to be square-integrable if its mean energy is finite,

\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\Omega)}^{2}=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(t,\eta)|^{2}\,dt\,dP=\mathbb{E}_{\eta}\left[\|f(\cdot,\eta)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\right]<\infty.

(11)

In what follows, we assume that the random signals under consideration belong to
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\Omega)
and are normalized such that

\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\Omega)}^{2}=1.

(12)

Definition 2
.

The FT of the random signal
f(t,\eta)
is defined by
5

\hat{f}(\xi,\eta)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}f(t,\eta)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}t\xi}\,dt.

(13)

We extend the Definition
2
to the FrFT and the LCT, leading to Definition
3
and
4
.

Definition 3
.

The FrFT of the random signal
f(t,\eta)
is defined by

F_{\alpha}(\rho,\eta)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathrm{j}2\pi\sin\alpha}}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}f(t,\eta)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}\frac{(t^{2}+\rho^{2})\cos\alpha-2t\rho}{2\sin\alpha}}\,dt,\ \ \sin\alpha\neq 0.

(14)

where
\alpha
is the rotation parameter.

Definition 4
.

The LCT of the random signal
f(t,\eta)
is defined by

L_{A}(u,\eta)=\mathcal{L}_{A}[f(t,\eta)](u)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}f(t,\eta)K_{A}(t,u)\,dt,&b\neq 0,\\[8.61108pt]
\displaystyle\sqrt{d}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}\frac{cdu^{2}}{2}}f(du,\eta),&b=0,\end{cases}

(15)

where the parameter matrix
A=\left[\begin{array}[]{@{}cc@{}}a&b\\
c&d\end{array}\right]
,
with
a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{R},ad-bc=1
, and the LCT kernel
K_{A}(t,u)
is given by

K_{A}(t,u)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathrm{j}2\pi b}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}\left(\frac{a}{2b}t^{2}-\frac{1}{b}tu+\frac{d}{2b}u^{2}\right)}.

(16)

It can be seen from the above definition, when
A=\left[\begin{array}[]{@{}cc@{}}\cos\alpha&\sin\alpha\\
-\sin\alpha&\cos\alpha\end{array}\right]

the LCT reduces to the FrFT for random signals and
when

A=\left[\begin{array}[]{@{}cc@{}}0&1\\
-1&0\end{array}\right]

the LCT reduces to the FT for random signals.

3
HUP for random signals in the LCT domain

In this section, we introduce the mean and expectation of random signals in the linear canonical transform (LCT) domain and establish the corresponding Heisenberg uncertainty principle (HUP).

Definition 5
.

The mean of LCT frequency is defined as

\mu_{u}=\int_{\Omega}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}u|L_{A}(u,\eta)|^{2}\,du\,dP,

(17)

and the duration is given by

\sigma_{u}^{2}(\eta)=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}(u-\mu_{u})^{2}|L_{A}(u,\eta)|^{2}\,du.

(18)

Lemma 1
.

Suppose that for almost every
\eta\in\Omega
,

f(t,\eta)
,
tf(t,\eta)
,
\partial_{t}f(t,\eta)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
,
\psi(\cdot,\eta)
,
\theta(\cdot,\eta)
are continuously differentiable, and
\displaystyle\lim_{|t|\to\infty}f(t,\eta)=0
. Then, the following identity holds

\displaystyle\sigma_{u}^{2}(\eta)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\left(at+b\partial_{t}\theta(t,\eta)-\mu_{u}\right)^{2}\psi^{2}(t,\eta)\,dt+b^{2}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\left|\partial_{t}\psi(t,\eta)\right|^{2}\,dt.

(19)

where
a
and
b
are the LCT parameters associated with the matrix

A
.

Proof.

We first compute the mean
\mu_{u}
in the LCT domain,

\displaystyle\mu_{u}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}u|L_{A}(u,\eta)|^{2}\,du\,dP

(20)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\overline{L_{A}(u,\eta)}uL_{A}(u,\eta)\,du\,dP

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\overline{L_{A}(u,\eta)}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}f(t,\eta)uK_{A}(t,u)\,dt\,du\,dP.

Using the differentiation property of the LCT, we have

uK_{A}(t,u)=atK_{A}(t,u)-\frac{b}{\mathrm{j}}\partial_{t}K_{A}(t,u).

(21)

It follows from (
20
) and (
21
) that

\displaystyle\mu_{u}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\overline{L_{A}(u,\eta)}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}f(t,\eta)uK_{A}(t,u)\,dt\,du\,dP

(22)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\overline{L_{A}(u,\eta)}\left(a\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}tf(t,\eta)K_{A}(t,u)\,dt-\frac{b}{\mathrm{j}}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}f(t,\eta)\partial_{t}K_{A}(t,u)\,dt\right)\,du\,dP

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\overline{L_{A}(u,\eta)}\left(a\mathcal{L}_{A}[tf(t,\eta)](u)-\frac{b}{\mathrm{j}}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}f(t,\eta)\,d(K_{A}(t,u))\,dt\right)\,du\,dP

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\overline{L_{A}(u,\eta)}\Biggl(a\,\mathcal{L}_{A}[tf(t,\eta)](u)-\frac{b}{\mathrm{j}}\Bigl(f(t,\eta)K_{A}(t,u)\big|_{-\infty}^{+\infty}

\displaystyle-\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}K_{A}(t,u)\partial_{t}f(t,\eta)\,dt\Bigr)\Biggr)\,du\,dP.

By the assumption that
f(t,\eta)
vanishes at infinity, we obtain

\displaystyle\mu_{u}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\overline{L_{A}(u,\eta)}\left(a\mathcal{L}_{A}[tf(t,\eta)](u)+\frac{b}{\mathrm{j}}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}K_{A}(t,u)\partial_{t}f(t,\eta)\,dt\right)\,du\,dP

(23)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\overline{L_{A}(u,\eta)}\left(a\mathcal{L}_{A}[tf(t,\eta)](u)+\frac{b}{\mathrm{j}}\mathcal{L}_{A}[\partial_{t}f(t,\eta)](u)\right)\,du\,dP.

Using the Parseval theorem in the LCT domain, we get

\displaystyle\mu_{u}

\displaystyle=a\int_{\Omega}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\overline{f(t,\eta)}\,tf(t,\eta)\,dt\,dP+\frac{b}{\mathrm{j}}\int_{\Omega}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\overline{f(t,\eta)}\,\partial_{t}f(t,\eta)\,dt\,dP

(24)

\displaystyle=a\int_{\Omega}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}t|f(t,\eta)|^{2}\,dt\,dP+\frac{b}{\mathrm{j}}\int_{\Omega}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\psi(t,\eta)e^{-j\theta(t,\eta)}\partial_{t}\!\left(\psi(t,\eta)e^{\mathrm{j}\theta(t,\eta)}\right)dt\,dP

\displaystyle=a\int_{\Omega}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}t|f(t,\eta)|^{2}\,dt\,dP+\frac{b}{\mathrm{j}}\int_{\Omega}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\left(\psi(t,\eta)\partial_{t}\psi(t,\eta)+\mathrm{j}\psi^{2}(t,\eta)\partial_{t}\theta(t,\eta)\right)dt\,dP.

We then compute
\sigma_{u}^{2}(\eta)
in the LCT domain. By (
18
) and (
24
) , we have

\displaystyle\sigma_{u}^{2}(\eta)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}(u-\mu_{u})^{2}\left|L_{A}(u,\eta)\right|^{2}\,du

(25)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\left|uL_{A}(u,\eta)-\mu_{u}L_{A}(u,\eta)\right|^{2}du

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\left|a\mathcal{L}_{A}[tf(t,\eta)](u)+\frac{b}{\mathrm{j}}\mathcal{L}_{A}\left[\partial_{t}f(t,\eta)\right](u)-\mu_{u}\mathcal{L}_{A}[f(t,\eta)](u)\right|^{2}du

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\left|\mathcal{L}_{A}\left[\left(at+\frac{b}{\mathrm{j}}\partial t-\mu_{u}\right)f(t,\eta)\right](u)\right|^{2}du.

Using the Parseval theorem, we get

\displaystyle\sigma_{u}^{2}(\eta)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\left|\left(at+\frac{b}{\mathrm{j}}\partial t-\mu_{u}\right)f(t,\eta)\right|^{2}dt

(26)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\left|at\psi(t,\eta)e^{\mathrm{j}\theta(t,\eta)}+\frac{b}{\mathrm{j}}\partial_{t}\left(\psi(t,\eta)e^{\mathrm{j}\theta(t,\eta)}\right)-\mu_{u}\psi(t,\eta)e^{\mathrm{j}\theta(t,\eta)}\right|^{2}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\left|at\psi(t,\eta)e^{\mathrm{j}\theta(t,\eta)}+\frac{b}{\mathrm{j}}\left(\partial_{t}\psi(t,\eta)e^{\mathrm{j}\theta(t,\eta)}+\psi(t,\eta)e^{\mathrm{j}\theta(t,\eta)}\mathrm{j}\partial_{t}\theta(t,\eta)\right)-\mu_{u}\psi(t,\eta)e^{\mathrm{j}\theta(t,\eta)}\right|^{2}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\left|\left(at+b\partial_{t}\theta(t,\eta)-\mu_{u}\right)\psi(t,\eta)+\frac{b}{\mathrm{j}}\partial_{t}\psi(t,\eta)\right|^{2}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\left(at+b\partial_{t}\theta(t,\eta)-\mu_{u}\right)^{2}\psi^{2}(t,\eta)dt+b^{2}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\left|\partial_{t}\psi(t,\eta)\right|^{2}dt.

This completes the proof.
∎

4
DSUP for random signals in the LCT domain

We begin by establishing several lemmas and operator-theoretic results. These preliminary results provide the foundation for deriving the Donoho-Stark uncertainty principle (DSUP) for random signals in the linear canonical transform (LCT) domain. We then use this principle to investigate the recovery of random signals from incomplete or partially missing observations.

A random signal
f(t,\eta)
is said to be time-limited to a measurable set
B_{T}\times T
if

f(t,\eta)=0,\qquad\text{for a.e. }(\eta,t)\notin B_{T}\times T.

(39)

Similarly,
f(t,\eta)
is said to be frequency-limited to a measurable set
B_{U}\times U
in the LCT domain if

L_{A}(u,\eta)=0,\qquad\text{for a.e. }(\eta,u)\notin B_{U}\times U.

(40)

Then the time-limiting operator is defined by

\tau_{B_{T}\times T}f(t,\eta)=\chi_{B_{T}}(\eta)\chi_{T}(t)f(t,\eta),

(41)

where
\chi_{B_{T}}
and
\chi_{T}
denote the characteristic functions of
B_{T}
and
T
, respectively. And the frequency-domain limiting operator in the LCT domain is defined by

\tau_{B_{U}\times U}f(t,\eta)=\chi_{B_{U}}(\eta)\frac{1}{\sqrt{-\mathrm{j}2\pi b}}\int_{U}L_{A}(u,\eta)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}\frac{at^{2}+du^{2}-2tu}{2b}}\,du,

(42)

where
\chi_{B_{U}}
denote the characteristic functions of
B_{U}
. Here,
\chi_{B_{T}}
and
\chi_{B_{U}}
are determined only by the measurable sets and are independent of the signal
f(t,\eta)
.

We shall also use the standard operator norm on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\Omega)
.
For a bounded linear operator

Q:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\Omega)\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\Omega)
, its norm is defined by

\|Q\|=\sup_{f\neq 0}\frac{\|Qf\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\Omega)}}{\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\Omega)}}.

(43)

In addition, if
Q
acting on
f(t,\eta)
can be expressed as

Qf(t,\eta)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}q(\eta,t,x)f(x,\eta)\,dx,

(44)

where the kernel
q(\eta,t,x)
is chosen such that

P\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x,\eta)q(\eta,t,x)|\,dx<\infty\right)=1,\ x\in\mathbb{R}
,
and

Lemma 3
.

The Hilbert-Schmidt norm of
\tau_{B_{U}\times U}\tau_{B_{T}\times T}
satisfies

\left\|\tau_{B_{U}\times U}\tau_{B_{T}\times T}\right\|_{HS}^{2}=\frac{1}{2\pi|b|}|U||T|P(B_{T}\cap B_{U}),\ \ b\neq 0.

(47)

Proof.

For any
f(t,\eta)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\Omega)
, by (
41

42
), we have

\displaystyle\tau_{B_{U}\times U}\tau_{B_{T}\times T}f(t,\eta)

(48)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\chi_{B_{U}}(\eta)\frac{1}{\sqrt{-\mathrm{j}2\pi b}}\int_{U}\mathcal{L}_{A}[\tau_{B_{T}\times T}f(t,\eta)](u)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}\left(\frac{d}{2b}u^{2}-\frac{u}{b}t+\frac{a}{2b}t^{2}\right)}\,du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\chi_{B_{U}}(\eta)\frac{1}{\sqrt{-\mathrm{j}2\pi b}}\int_{U}\chi_{B_{T}}(\eta)\int_{T}f(x,\eta)\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathrm{j}2\pi b}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}\left(\frac{a}{2b}x^{2}-\frac{u}{b}x+\frac{d}{2b}u^{2}\right)}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}\left(\frac{d}{2b}u^{2}-\frac{u}{b}t+\frac{a}{2b}t^{2}\right)}\,dx\,du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi|b|}\chi_{B_{T}}(\eta)\chi_{B_{U}}(\eta)\int_{U}\int_{T}f(x,\eta)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}\left(\frac{a}{2b}(x^{2}-t^{2})-\frac{u}{b}(x-t)\right)}\,dx\,du.

For convenience, we define

h(\eta,t,x)=\frac{1}{2\pi|b|}\chi_{B_{T}}(\eta)\chi_{B_{U}}(\eta)\int_{U}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}\left(\frac{a}{2b}(x^{2}-t^{2})-\frac{u}{b}(x-t)\right)}\,du.

(49)

It follows that

\displaystyle\tau_{B_{U}\times U}\tau_{B_{T}\times T}f(t,\eta)

\displaystyle=\int_{T}f(x,\eta)h(\eta,t,x)\,dx

(50)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi_{T}(x)f(x,\eta)h(\eta,t,x)\,dx.

Then let

q(\eta,t,x)=\chi_{T}(x)h(\eta,t,x),

(51)

so that

\tau_{B_{U}\times U}\tau_{B_{T}\times T}f(t,\eta)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x,\eta)q(\eta,t,x)\,dx.

(52)

Therefore, by (
45
), we get

\displaystyle\left\|\tau_{B_{U}\times U}\tau_{B_{T}\times T}\right\|_{HS}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|q(\eta,t,x)|^{2}\,dx\,dt\,dP

(53)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{T}|h(\eta,t,x)|^{2}\,dx\,dt\,dP.

Moreover, by the Parseval theorem in the LCT domain, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}|h(\eta,t,x)|^{2}\,dt=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\mathcal{L}[h(\eta,t,x)](v)\right|^{2}\,dv

(54)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi|b|}\chi_{B_{T}}(\eta)\chi_{B_{U}}(\eta)\int_{U}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}\left(\frac{a}{2b}(x^{2}-t^{2})-\frac{u}{b}(x-t)\right)}du\,\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathrm{j}2\pi b}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}\left(\frac{a}{2b}t^{2}-\frac{v}{b}t+\frac{d}{2b}v^{2}\right)}dt\right|^{2}dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\frac{1}{2\pi|b|}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathrm{j}2\pi b}}\chi_{B_{T}}(\eta)\chi_{B_{U}}(\eta)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}\left(\frac{a}{2b}x^{2}+\frac{d}{2b}v^{2}\right)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{U}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{-j}\frac{u}{b}x}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}\frac{u-v}{b}t}du\,dt\right|^{2}dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\frac{1}{2\pi|b|}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathrm{j}2\pi b}}\chi_{B_{T}}(\eta)\chi_{B_{U}}(\eta)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}\left(\frac{a}{2b}x^{2}+\frac{d}{2b}v^{2}\right)}\int_{U}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{-j}\frac{u}{b}x}2\pi|b|\,\delta(u-v)du\,\right|^{2}dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{U}\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathrm{j}2\pi b}}\chi_{B_{T}}(\eta)\chi_{B_{U}}(\eta)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}\left(\frac{a}{2b}x^{2}-\frac{u}{b}x+\frac{d}{2b}u^{2}\right)}\right|^{2}dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi|b|}\int_{U}\chi_{B_{T}\cap B_{U}}(\eta)dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi|b|}|U|\chi_{B_{T}\cap B_{U}}(\eta).

Consequently, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\tau_{B_{U}\times U}\tau_{B_{T}\times T}\right\|_{HS}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\int_{T}\frac{1}{2\pi|b|}|U|\chi_{B_{T}\cap B_{U}}(\eta)\,dx\,dP

(55)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi|b|}|U||T|P(B_{T}\cap B_{U}).

This completes the proof.

With the above preparation, we establish the DSUP in the LCT domain.

Theorem 2
.

Assume that
\|f\|_{\Omega}=1
. If
f(t,\eta)
is

\varepsilon_{B_{T}\times T}
-concentrated on
B_{T}\times T
and

L_{A}(u,\eta)
is

\varepsilon_{B_{U}\times U}
-concentrated on
B_{U}\times U
, then the DSUP is as follows

\frac{1}{2\pi|b|}|U||T|P(B_{T}\cap B_{U})\geq\left(1-\varepsilon_{B_{T}\times T}-\varepsilon_{B_{U}\times U}\right)^{2},\ \ b\neq 0.

(60)

Proof.

By (
58

\displaystyle\left\|f-\tau_{B_{U}\times U}\tau_{B_{T}\times T}f\right\|_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|f-\tau_{B_{T}\times T}f\right\|_{\Omega}+\left\|\tau_{B_{T}\times T}f-\tau_{B_{U}\times U}\tau_{B_{T}\times T}f\right\|_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{B_{T}\times T}+\varepsilon_{B_{U}\times U}.

Applying the reverse triangle inequality, we obtain

\|f\|_{\Omega}-\left\|\tau_{B_{U}\times U}\tau_{B_{T}\times T}f\right\|_{\Omega}\leq\left\|f-\tau_{B_{U}\times U}\tau_{B_{T}\times T}f\right\|_{\Omega}.

(62)

Since
\|f\|_{\Omega}=1
, it follows that

\left\|\tau_{B_{U}\times U}\tau_{B_{T}\times T}f\right\|_{\Omega}\geq 1-\varepsilon_{B_{T}\times T}-\varepsilon_{B_{U}\times U}.

\left\|\tau_{B_{U}\times U}\tau_{B_{T}\times T}\right\|^{2}\geq\left(1-\varepsilon_{B_{T}\times T}-\varepsilon_{B_{U}\times U}\right)^{2}.

(64)

Applying (

\frac{1}{2\pi|b|}|U||T|P(B_{T}\cap B_{U})\geq\left(1-\varepsilon_{B_{T}\times T}-\varepsilon_{B_{U}\times U}\right)^{2}.

(65)

This completes the proof.

Definition 7
.

Let
n(t,\eta)
denote a random noise. The observed noisy random signal

is defined by

r(t,\eta)=\begin{cases}f(t,\eta)+n(t,\eta),&(t,\eta)\in(B_{T}\times T)^{c},\\
0,&(t,\eta)\in B_{T}\times T.\end{cases}

(68)

A stable recovery result for random signals in the LCT domain is presented in the following theorem.

Theorem 3
.

Assume that
|U||T|P(B_{T}\cap B_{U})<2\pi|b|,b\neq 0
.
If there exists a linear operator
Q
satisfying
\|f-Qr\|_{\Omega}\leq C\|n\|_{\Omega}
,
where

C\leq\left(1-\sqrt{\frac{|U||T|P(B_{T}\cap B_{U})}{2\pi|b|}}\right)^{-1},

then the random signal
f(t,\eta)
can be recovered uniquely from
r(t,\eta)
.

Proof.

First, we prove uniqueness. Suppose that there exists another random signal
f^{\star}(t,\eta)
that is also recovered from
r(t,\eta)
. Define the difference between the two signals by

l(t,\eta)=f(t,\eta)-f^{\star}(t,\eta).

(69)

Since both
f(t,\eta)
and
f^{\star}(t,\eta)
coincide with the observation
on
(B_{T}\times T)^{c}
, we have

l(t,\eta)=0,\qquad(t,\eta)\in(B_{T}\times T)^{c}.

(70)

Hence,
l(t,\eta)
is supported on
B_{T}\times T
.
Moreover, since both signals are frequency-limited to
B_{U}\times U
in the LCT domain, it follows that

\tau_{B_{U}\times U}l=\tau_{B_{U}\times U}(f-f^{\star})=f-f^{\star}=l.

(71)

Thus,
l
is also frequency-limited to
B_{U}\times U
in the LCT domain.

If
l(t,\eta)
were not identically zero, by the Theorem
2
to
l(t,\eta)
, we have

\frac{1}{2\pi|b|}|U||T|P(B_{T}\cap B_{U})\geq\left(1-\varepsilon_{B_{T}\times T}-\varepsilon_{B_{U}\times U}\right)^{2}.

This contradicts the assumption
|U||T|P(B_{T}\cap B_{U})<2\pi|b|
.
Therefore,

l(t,\eta)=0,\quad(t,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}\times\Omega,

(72)

and hence
f^{\star}(t,\eta)=f(t,\eta)
almost everywhere. This proves uniqueness.

We next construct the recovery operator. By the (
57
), we obtain

\left\|\tau_{B_{T}\times T}\tau_{B_{U}\times U}\right\|=\left\|\tau_{B_{U}\times U}\tau_{B_{T}\times T}\right\|\leq\sqrt{\frac{1}{2\pi|b|}|U||T|P(B_{T}\cap B_{U})}\leq 1.

(73)

Consequently, the operator

is invertible, and we define

Q=\left(I-\tau_{B_{T}\times T}\tau_{B_{U}\times U}\right)^{-1}.

(74)

Since

\left(I-\tau_{B_{T}\times T}\right)f(t,\eta)=\left(I-\tau_{B_{T}\times T}\tau_{B_{U}\times U}\right)f(t,\eta),

(75)

we have

\displaystyle f(t,\eta)-Qr(t,\eta)

(76)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(t,\eta)-Q(I-\tau_{B_{T}\times T})f(t,\eta)-Qn(t,\eta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(t,\eta)-\left(I-\tau_{B_{T}\times T}\tau_{B_{U}\times U}\right)^{-1}\left(I-\tau_{B_{T}\times T}\tau_{B_{U}\times U}\right)f(t,\eta)-Qn(t,\eta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-Qn(t,\eta).

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\|f(t,\eta)-Qr(t,\eta)\|_{\Omega}

(77)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|Qn(t,\eta)\|_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|Q\|\,\|n(t,\eta)\|_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(1-\left\|\tau_{B_{T}\times T}\tau_{B_{U}\times U}\right\|\right)^{-1}\|n(t,\eta)\|_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(1-\left\|\tau_{B_{T}\times T}\tau_{B_{U}\times U}\right\|_{HS}\right)^{-1}\|n(t,\eta)\|_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1-\sqrt{\frac{1}{2\pi|b|}|U||T|P(B_{T}\cap B_{U})}\right)^{-1}\|n(t,\eta)\|_{\Omega}.

∎

The following theorem further establishes the stability of random signal recovery under noisy conditions.

Theorem 4
.

Suppose that
|U||T|P(B_{T}\cap B_{U})<2\pi|b|,b\neq 0
, and let the noise satisfy

\|n(t,\eta)\|_{\Omega}^{2}\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{2\pi|b|}|T||U|P(B_{T}\cap B_{U})\right)\frac{\delta}{4}.

(78)

If the reconstructed random signal
\widetilde{f}(t,\eta)
, obtained from the noisy observation

, satisfies

\left\|r(t,\eta)-\tau_{(B_{T}\times T)^{c}}\widetilde{f}(t,\eta)\right\|_{\Omega}^{2}\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{2\pi|b|}|T||U|P(B_{T}\cap B_{U})\right)\frac{\delta}{4},

(79)

then the reconstruction error satisfies

\left\|f(t,\eta)-\widetilde{f}(t,\eta)\right\|_{\Omega}^{2}\leq\delta.

(80)

Proof.

First, we decompose the reconstruction error as

\displaystyle\left\|f(t,\eta)-\widetilde{f}(t,\eta)\right\|_{\Omega}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\tau_{(B_{T}\times T)^{c}}\left(f(t,\eta)-\widetilde{f}(t,\eta)\right)\right\|_{\Omega}^{2}

(81)

\displaystyle+\left\|\left(I-\tau_{(B_{T}\times T)^{c}}\right)\left(f(t,\eta)-\widetilde{f}(t,\eta)\right)\right\|_{\Omega}^{2}.

For the first term, by (
68
), (
78
) and (
79
), we have

\displaystyle\left\|\tau_{(B_{T}\times T)^{c}}\left(f(t,\eta)-\widetilde{f}(t,\eta)\right)\right\|_{\Omega}^{2}

(82)

\displaystyle=\left\|\tau_{(B_{T}\times T)^{c}}\left(r(t,\eta)-n(t,\eta)\right)-\tau_{(B_{T}\times T)^{c}}\widetilde{f}(t,\eta)\right\|_{\Omega}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\left\|r(t,\eta)-\tau_{(B_{T}\times T)^{c}}\widetilde{f}(t,\eta)\right\|_{\Omega}+\left\|n(t,\eta)\right\|_{\Omega}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{2\pi|b|}|U||T|P(B_{T}\cap B_{U})\right)\delta.

For the second term, since
I-\tau_{(B_{T}\times T)^{c}}=\tau_{B_{T}\times T}
, and by using (
57
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\left(I-\tau_{(B_{T}\times T)^{c}}\right)\left(f(t,\eta)-\widetilde{f}(t,\eta)\right)\right\|_{\Omega}^{2}

(83)

\displaystyle=\left\|\left(I-\tau_{(B_{T}\times T)^{c}}\right)\tau_{B_{U}\times U}\left(f-\widetilde{f}\right)(t,\eta)\right\|_{\Omega}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\tau_{B_{T}\times T}\tau_{B_{U}\times U}\left(f-\widetilde{f}\right)(t,\eta)\right\|_{\Omega}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\tau_{B_{T}\times T}\tau_{B_{U}\times U}\right\|^{2}\left\|f(t,\eta)-\widetilde{f}(t,\eta)\right\|_{\Omega}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\pi|b|}|T||U|P(B_{T}\cap B_{U})\left\|f(t,\eta)-\widetilde{f}(t,\eta)\right\|_{\Omega}^{2}.

Combining (
82
) and (
83
), we have

\displaystyle\left\|f(t,\eta)-\widetilde{f}(t,\eta)\right\|_{\Omega}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{2\pi|b|}|U||T|P(B_{T}\cap B_{U})\right)\delta

(84)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\pi|b|}|T||U|P(B_{T}\cap B_{U})\left\|f(t,\eta)-\widetilde{f}(t,\eta)\right\|_{\Omega}^{2}.

Therefore, we get

\left\|f(t,\eta)-\widetilde{f}(t,\eta)\right\|_{\Omega}^{2}\leq\delta.

(85)

This completes the proof.
∎

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we investigate uncertainty principles for random signals in the linear canonical transform (LCT) domain. By formulating the LCT of random signals on the probability space, we establish the corresponding Heisenberg and Donoho-Stark uncertainty principles. Both principles reveal that the uncertainty bounds depend jointly on the random signals and the parameters of the LCT, thereby providing additional degrees of freedom in controlling the balance between time and frequency localization.
Since the LCT provides a unified generalization of the Fourier transform and the fractional Fourier transform, the corresponding results in these two domains are also presented as special cases. The findings of this work are of both theoretical and practical significance, particularly in characterizing time-frequency localization and the recovery of random signals from incomplete or partially missing observations.
