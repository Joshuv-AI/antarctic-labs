1.
Introduction

The iteration of polynomial maps over finite fields and finite rings is a classical topic at the intersection of number theory, permutation polynomials, and arithmetic dynamics; see, for example, Silverman
[
18
]
and Narkiewicz
[
14
]
for general background. Chebyshev polynomials, defined by
T_{n}(\cos\theta)=\cos n\theta
, form one of the most natural families in this setting. They satisfy the composition law
T_{mn}=T_{m}\circ T_{n}
, the Chebyshev–Fermat congruence
T_{p}(a)\equiv a\pmod{p}
, and the derivative identity
T_{n}^{\prime}=nU_{n-1}
, which gives explicit control over multipliers at fixed points. Via the relation
D_{n}(2x,1)=2T_{n}(x)
, they are equivalent to the Dickson polynomials of the first kind, which play a central role in the theory of permutation polynomials over finite fields
[
3
,
10
,
12
]
. Classical cycle-structure results for Dickson permutation polynomials include Lidl–Mullen
[
9
]
. Standard identities for
T_{n}
and
U_{n}
may be found in Rivlin
[
16
]
and Mason–Handscomb
[
11
]
.

Prior work

The global functional graph of Chebyshev polynomials over finite fields is now well understood. Gassert
[
5
, Theorems 2.3 and 2.4]
described the graph of
T_{\ell}
over finite fields for prime degree
\ell
, including the cycle structure and the number of periodic vertices. Qureshi and Panario
[
15
, Theorem 4]
gave a detailed structural description of the functional graph of
T_{n}
over finite fields for arbitrary
n
, decomposing it into rational, quadratic, and special components; see also
[
15
, Corollaries 1–2 and Theorem 5]
for periodic-point and aggregate graph-count consequences. Hutz and Patel
[
6
, Sec. 3, Thm. 3.6]
obtained formulas for periodic points of Chebyshev polynomials of non-prime degree as part of their study of split polynomial maps over finite fields. Since fixed points are cycles of length one, the prime-field count in Theorem
5.1
is compatible with this graph-theoretic description. The purpose of Theorem
5.1
is more specific: it isolates the length-one case as a four-GCD formula and retains the split/nonsplit branch data needed for prime-power lifting. From a complementary algebraic viewpoint, Rosen, Scherr, Weiss, and Zieve
[
17
]
identified the semigroup of Chebyshev maps on
\mathbb{F}_{p}
with a quotient of a multiplicative semigroup, reflecting the involution
\zeta\leftrightarrow\zeta^{-1}
that underlies our approach.

For higher prime-power levels, Li, Lu, Tan, and Chen
[
8
, Eq. (1) and Props. 5–7]
studied fixed-point extension and cycle-count phenomena for Chebyshev permutation polynomials over
\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}
for
p>3
under the permutation condition
\gcd(n,p^{3}-p)=1
. The results below have a different focus: they give local fixed-point lifting formulas for arbitrary
n
, including non-permutation cases and degenerate residue classes where ordinary Hensel lifting is not unique. Yoshioka
[
22
]
studied period growth for Chebyshev sequences and individual orbits modulo
p^{k}
; by contrast, the quantities
P_{r}^{(k)}
below count all residue classes of exact period
r
. Diarra and Sylla
[
2
]
investigated
p
-adic dynamics of Chebyshev polynomials, especially the case
T_{p}
. More generally, Nara
[
13
, main theorem]
gives multiplier alternatives for the one-step lifting of cycles in polynomial functional graphs over prime-power residue rings. Compared with those general multiplier alternatives, Theorems
10.4
and
11.9
use the Chebyshev source parametrization to identify possible lifted periods explicitly as
\operatorname{cord}_{ep}(n)
and
\operatorname{cord}_{ep^{q}}(n)
, with residue-wise multiplicities above each base point. The prime
p=2
has separate 2-adic behavior and is outside the scope of the present paper; see Fan and Liao
[
4
]
for a description of the dynamical structure of Chebyshev polynomials on
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. Bluher
[
1
]
studied subsets of finite fields stabilized by Dickson and Chebyshev polynomials, deriving factorization formulas with number-theoretic applications. A recent preprint of Tan and Li
[
19
]
develops a period-distribution and stabilization framework for a class of permutation maps over
\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}
using linear-recurrence, minimal-polynomial, and lifting methods. This is adjacent to the present lifting viewpoint, but it remains in a permutation-map setting and does not address the non-permutation Chebyshev fixed-point lifting formulas below.

Thus the existing literature gives detailed functional-graph descriptions over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
and, in important permutation settings, over
\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}
. Here we extract explicit arithmetic formulas for fixed-point counts and exact-period point counts, with attention to cases where
T_{n}
need not be a permutation. Throughout the paper,
P_{r}
and
P_{r}^{(k)}
count points of exact period
r
, not cycles; orbit counts are obtained by dividing by
r
. Although fixed and periodic points over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
are encoded in known global graph descriptions, the formulas below are organized around the two-source parametrization and the local lifting behavior at degenerate residue classes.

Over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, fixed points arise from the factorization

(\zeta^{n-1}-1)(\zeta^{n+1}-1)=0

inside the two source groups
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
and
\mu_{p+1}
. Over
\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}
, fixed-point lifting requires a local analysis of degenerate classes, where ordinary Hensel lifting is no longer unique. Exact-period point counts then follow from the same local input applied to the iterates
T_{n}^{\circ j}=T_{n^{j}}
, together with Möbius inversion. This is the central structure of the paper: fixed-point lifting is the local engine, while exact-period point counts and, under additional hypotheses, ghost-orbit descriptions are the dynamical payoff. Informally, a ghost lift is a lift of a periodic residue whose exact period strictly increases after passing from modulo
p
to a higher prime-power level.

Contribution

The main results are as follows.

(i)

In the finite-field setting, we derive a closed four-GCD formula for
N_{1}=|\operatorname{Fix}(T_{n},\mathbb{F}_{p})|
(Theorem
5.1
) and a branch-wise criterion for degeneracy, yielding an explicit formula for the number
d
of degenerate fixed points (Proposition
7.1
).

(ii)

We prove the unconditional first-lift formula

N_{2}=N_{1}+d(p-1)

for every odd prime
p
, and for
p\geq 5
the all-level formula

N_{k}=N_{1}+d\bigl(p^{\min(k-1,\nu_{p}(n^{2}-1))}-1\bigr),

equivalent to a GCD formula with
p\pm 1
replaced by
(p\pm 1)p^{k-1}
(Theorem
7.3
).

(iii)

We use the Chebyshev quotient order

\operatorname{cord}_{e}(n)=\min\{r\geq 1:n^{r}\equiv\pm 1\pmod{e}\},

equivalently the order of the class of
n
modulo the identification
\pm 1
, to express exact periods over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
and organize their prime-power lifts (Theorem
9.1
).

(iv)

For
p\nmid n
, we prove a sign-sensitive first-lift theorem over
\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z}
: above a periodic residue, either all
p
lifts retain the base period, or exactly one lift retains it and the remaining
p-1
lifts become ghost periodic points of period
\operatorname{cord}_{ep}(n)
(Theorem
10.4
).

(v)

For every odd prime
p
, applying Möbius inversion to the fixed-point counts of the iterates
T_{n^{j}}
gives an exact-period point-count identity over every
\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}
(Corollary
11.1
), including cases with
p\mid n
; for
p\geq 5
, substituting the closed fixed-point formula gives an explicit closed form. The orbitwise tower description requires
p\geq 5
and
p\nmid n
: under these hypotheses, lifted periods are governed by the tower
\operatorname{cord}_{ep^{q}}(n)
(Theorem
11.9
). The exceptional Chebyshev–Fermat case
n=p
is treated separately.

The finite-field results in Theorems

The main new contributions begin after this finite-field repackaging: the branch-wise degeneracy criterion in Proposition

2.
Chebyshev polynomials

The Chebyshev polynomials of the first kind
T_{n}(x)
and second kind
U_{n}(x)
are the integer polynomials determined by

T_{0}=1,\qquad T_{1}=x,\qquad T_{m+1}=2xT_{m}-T_{m-1},

and

U_{0}=1,\qquad U_{1}=2x,\qquad U_{m+1}=2xU_{m}-U_{m-1}.

Thus
T_{n},U_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
for every
n
, and
T_{n}
defines maps on
\mathbb{F}_{p}
and on
\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}
by coefficientwise reduction. Over the complex numbers these polynomials are equivalently characterized by

(1)

T_{n}(\cos\theta)=\cos n\theta,\qquad U_{n}(\cos\theta)=\frac{\sin((n+1)\theta)}{\sin\theta}.

The key properties for our purposes are:

(i)

Derivative:

T_{n}^{\prime}(x)=nU_{n-1}(x)
.

(ii)

Composition:

T_{mn}(x)=T_{m}(T_{n}(x))
.

(iii)

Pythagorean identity:

T_{n}(x)^{2}-1=(x^{2}-1)\,U_{n-1}(x)^{2}
.

(iv)

Special values:

T_{n}(1)=1
,
T_{n}(-1)=(-1)^{n}
,
U_{n-1}(1)=n
,
U_{n-1}(-1)=(-1)^{n-1}n
.

We refer to Rivlin
[
16
]
and Mason–Handscomb
[
11
]
for classical identities and further background. All polynomial identities used below are identities in
\mathbb{Z}[x]
before reduction modulo
p
or
p^{k}
. Since all finite-field and prime-power results in the main body assume
p
is odd, the factor
2
is invertible in every residue ring under consideration; the averaging map
(z+z^{-1})/2
is therefore well defined whenever the source parameter is a unit.

The Dickson-polynomial normalization used here is

D_{n}(2x,1)=2T_{n}(x),

equivalently
D_{n}(y,1)=2T_{n}(y/2)
for all
n
, where
D_{n}
is the
n
th Dickson polynomial of the first kind with parameter
\alpha=1

[
10
]
. For the finite-field and permutation-polynomial viewpoint on Dickson polynomials, see also Wang and Yucas
[
21
]
.

,

denotes the normalized

, on

and on finite extensions of

. For a statement

, the Iverson bracket

is true and

otherwise. The exponent

whenever level and period notation occur together, and

3.
Dynamics over
\mathbb{F}_{p}

For an odd prime
p
, the Chebyshev polynomial
T_{n}
induces a map
\mathbb{F}_{p}\to\mathbb{F}_{p}
. The
\zeta
-parametrization of this dynamics, and the quotient structure coming from
\zeta\leftrightarrow\zeta^{-1}
, are standard in the finite-field literature on Chebyshev maps; see, for example, Gassert
[
5
]
, Qureshi and Panario
[
15
]
, and Rosen, Scherr, Weiss, and Zieve
[
17
]
.

For
a\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
, set

H_{a}(Z)=Z^{2}-2aZ+1.

The discriminant of
H_{a}
is
4(a^{2}-1)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
, so its roots lie in
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
. If
a\neq\pm 1
, these roots are distinct, nonzero, inverse to one another, and neither root is equal to
\pm 1
. Thus either root may be used as a parameter
\zeta
satisfying

a=\frac{\zeta+\zeta^{-1}}{2}.

Lemma 3.1

(The
\zeta
-parametrization)
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
with
a\neq\pm 1
, and let
\zeta\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}^{\times}
be either root of
H_{a}(Z)
. Then
\zeta^{2}\neq 1
,
a=(\zeta+\zeta^{-1})/2
, and

T_{n}(a)=\frac{\zeta^{n}+\zeta^{-n}}{2}.

Moreover, for the Chebyshev polynomial of the second kind,

U_{n-1}(a)=\frac{\zeta^{n}-\zeta^{-n}}{\zeta-\zeta^{-1}}.

Proof.

Set

F_{m}=\frac{\zeta^{m}+\zeta^{-m}}{2},\qquad G_{m}=\frac{\zeta^{m+1}-\zeta^{-(m+1)}}{\zeta-\zeta^{-1}}\qquad(m\geq 0).

Then
F_{0}=1
and
F_{1}=a
. Moreover,

2aF_{m}=\frac{\zeta+\zeta^{-1}}{2}\,(\zeta^{m}+\zeta^{-m})=\frac{\zeta^{m+1}+\zeta^{m-1}+\zeta^{-(m-1)}+\zeta^{-(m+1)}}{2}=F_{m+1}+F_{m-1}.

Thus
F_{m+1}=2aF_{m}-F_{m-1}
, so the sequence
(F_{m})
satisfies the same recurrence and initial values as
(T_{m}(a))
. Hence
F_{m}=T_{m}(a)
for
m\geq 0
.

For the second identity, note that
G_{0}=1
and

G_{1}=\frac{\zeta^{2}-\zeta^{-2}}{\zeta-\zeta^{-1}}=\zeta+\zeta^{-1}=2a.

Also,

(\zeta+\zeta^{-1})(\zeta^{m+1}-\zeta^{-(m+1)})=(\zeta^{m+2}-\zeta^{-m})+(\zeta^{m}-\zeta^{-(m+2)}),

which gives
2aG_{m}=G_{m+1}+G_{m-1}
. Thus
(G_{m})
satisfies the same recurrence and initial values as
(U_{m}(a))
, and therefore
G_{m}=U_{m}(a)
for
m\geq 0
. Taking
m=n-1
yields the stated formula.
∎

For
\zeta\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}^{\times}
, set

F(\zeta)=\frac{\zeta+\zeta^{-1}}{2}.

Proposition 3.2

(Two-source parametrization)
.

The map
F
sends
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}\cup\mu_{p+1}
onto
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, where

\mu_{p+1}=\{\zeta\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}^{\times}:\zeta^{p+1}=1\}.

Moreover, for
\zeta\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}^{\times}
, one has
F(\zeta)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
if and only if

\zeta\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}\quad\text{or}\quad\zeta\in\mu_{p+1}.

The fibers are the orbits of the involution
\zeta\leftrightarrow\zeta^{-1}
, except that
\zeta=1
and
\zeta=-1
are fixed by the involution. For
a\neq\pm 1
, the two preimages
\zeta
and
\zeta^{-1}
lie in the same source group and in no other source group.

Proof.

If
\zeta\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
, then
F(\zeta)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
immediately. If
\zeta\in\mu_{p+1}
, then
\zeta^{p}=\zeta^{-1}
, so

F(\zeta)^{p}=\frac{\zeta^{p}+\zeta^{-p}}{2}=\frac{\zeta^{-1}+\zeta}{2}=F(\zeta),

and again
F(\zeta)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

Conversely, suppose
F(\zeta)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Then

\zeta^{p}+\zeta^{-p}=\zeta+\zeta^{-1}.

The elements
\zeta
and
\zeta^{-1}
are the two roots of

X^{2}-(\zeta+\zeta^{-1})X+1,

and the same equation shows that
\zeta^{p}
is also a root. Hence
\zeta^{p}=\zeta
or
\zeta^{p}=\zeta^{-1}
. In the first case
\zeta\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
, and in the second case
\zeta^{p+1}=1
, so
\zeta\in\mu_{p+1}
. In particular, for every
a\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
, the roots of
H_{a}(Z)
lie in
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}\cup\mu_{p+1}
and map to
a
under
F
; hence
F
is surjective onto
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

The equality
F(\zeta)=F(\xi)
is equivalent to

\zeta+\zeta^{-1}=\xi+\xi^{-1},

or
(\zeta-\xi)(1-\zeta^{-1}\xi^{-1})=0
. Thus
\xi=\zeta
or
\xi=\zeta^{-1}
, and the only fixed points of the involution are
\zeta=\pm 1
. Finally,

\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}\cap\mu_{p+1}=\{\zeta\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}:\zeta^{2}=1\}=\{\pm 1\},

so if
a\neq\pm 1
, its two preimages cannot lie in both source groups. This proves the asserted uniqueness of the source for nonboundary points.
∎

This two-source decomposition is consistent with the Dickson/Chebyshev subgroup viewpoint used in
[
1
,
21
,
15
]
. For a nonboundary point, the source group is intrinsic, although the parameter itself is defined only up to inversion. We will freely refer to the source of its parameter
\zeta
and to the order
e=\operatorname{ord}(\zeta)
; this order is well-defined because the two source parameters are inverse to one another and
\operatorname{ord}(\zeta)=\operatorname{ord}(\zeta^{-1})
. The two fixed-point branches can overlap only at the boundary parameters
\zeta=\pm 1
, which is why the finite-field count below requires a boundary correction.

Lemma 3.3

(Fixed-point factorization)
.

For
a\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
,
a\neq\pm 1
,
T_{n}(a)=a
if and only if
\zeta^{n-1}=1
or
\zeta^{n+1}=1
.

Proof.

T_{n}(a)=a
iff
\zeta^{n}+\zeta^{-n}=\zeta+\zeta^{-1}
. Multiplying by the unit
\zeta^{n}
gives

\zeta^{2n}-\zeta^{n+1}-\zeta^{n-1}+1=(\zeta^{n-1}-1)(\zeta^{n+1}-1)=0.

∎

Remark 3.4
.

At
a=1
:
T_{n}(1)=1
for all
n
, so
a=1
is always a fixed point. At
a=-1
:
T_{n}(-1)=(-1)^{n}
, so
a=-1
is fixed iff
n
is odd.

4.
Hensel’s lemma

We use the following standard form of Hensel’s lemma; see, for example, Wan
[
20
]
, Koblitz
[
7
, Ch. I, Sec. 6]
, or Silverman
[
18
, Chapter 2]
. For fixed points of
T_{n}
we write
g_{n}(x)=T_{n}(x)-x
, so

g_{n}^{\prime}(a_{0})=T_{n}^{\prime}(a_{0})-1=nU_{n-1}(a_{0})-1.

Theorem 4.1

(Hensel’s lemma)
.

Let
g\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
and let
a_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfy

g(a_{0})\equiv 0\pmod{p},\qquad g^{\prime}(a_{0})\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}.

Then for each
q\geq 1
, there exists a unique residue class

a_{q}\in\mathbb{Z}/p^{q}\mathbb{Z}
such that

a_{q}\equiv a_{0}\pmod{p},\qquad g(a_{q})\equiv 0\pmod{p^{q}}.

Equivalently, there is a unique
p
-adic root of
g
reducing to
a_{0}
modulo
p
.

We shall also use the standard complete-DVR form of the same result. If
R
is a complete discrete valuation ring with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
, residue field
\kappa
, and
g\in R[x]
, then any simple root
\bar{a}\in\kappa
of
\bar{g}\in\kappa[x]
lifts uniquely to a root
a\in R
of
g
. Equivalently, for each
q\geq 1
there is a unique class modulo
\mathfrak{m}^{q}
reducing to
\bar{a}
and annihilating
g
modulo
\mathfrak{m}^{q}
. We use this form in particular for
R=\mathcal{O}_{K}
, the ring of integers of the unramified quadratic extension
K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.

For the fixed-point equation
g(x)=T_{n}(x)-x
, the multiplier at a fixed point
a_{0}
is
\lambda=g^{\prime}(a_{0})+1=T_{n}^{\prime}(a_{0})=nU_{n-1}(a_{0})
. Hensel’s lemma applies when
g^{\prime}(a_{0})=\lambda-1\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}
.

Definition 4.2
.

A fixed point
a_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
of
T_{n}
is
nondegenerate
if
nU_{n-1}(a_{0})\not\equiv 1\pmod{p}
, and
degenerate
if
nU_{n-1}(a_{0})\equiv 1\pmod{p}
.

By Hensel’s lemma, each nondegenerate fixed point lifts uniquely to
\mathbb{Z}/p^{q}\mathbb{Z}
for every
q\geq 1
.

5.
Fixed point count over
\mathbb{F}_{p}

The global functional-graph descriptions of Gassert
[
5
, Theorems 2.3 and 2.4]
in prime-degree cases and Qureshi–Panario
[
15
, Theorem 4]
for arbitrary degree already determine the fixed points of
T_{n}
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
as the cycles of length
1
; compare also the finite-field periodic-point formula for Chebyshev polynomials of non-prime degree in Hutz–Patel
[
6
, Sec. 3, Thm. 3.6]
. The next formula can therefore be read as a length-one extraction from known finite-field graph data. We include the proof because it keeps track of the split/nonsplit source branches used later in the degeneracy and lifting analysis; no new global graph classification over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
is claimed here. Its proof uses the two-source decomposition of Proposition
3.2
, which is also natural from the Dickson/Chebyshev subgroup viewpoint; compare
[
1
,
21
]
.

Theorem 5.1
.

For an odd prime
p
and
n\geq 2
,

(2)

N_{1}=|\operatorname{Fix}(T_{n},\mathbb{F}_{p})|=\frac{G_{1}+G_{2}+G_{3}+G_{4}-2\delta}{2},

where
G_{1}=\gcd(n\!-\!1,p\!-\!1)
,
G_{2}=\gcd(n\!+\!1,p\!-\!1)
,
G_{3}=\gcd(n\!-\!1,p\!+\!1)
,
G_{4}=\gcd(n\!+\!1,p\!+\!1)
, and
\delta=\gcd(n-1,2)
. Thus
\delta=1
when
n
is even and
\delta=2
when
n
is odd.

Proof.

Let
S=\{\zeta\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}\cup\mu_{p+1}:\zeta^{n-1}=1\text{ or }\zeta^{n+1}=1\}
. The set
S
is stable under the involution
\zeta\mapsto\zeta^{-1}
, since
\zeta^{n\pm 1}=1
holds if and only if
(\zeta^{-1})^{n\pm 1}=1
. We compute
|S|
by inclusion-exclusion over the two sources
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
and
\mu_{p+1}
, then convert to fixed points
a=(\zeta+\zeta^{-1})/2\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
using the quotient
\zeta\sim\zeta^{-1}
.

Source
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
:

|\{\zeta\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}:\zeta^{n-1}=1\}|=G_{1}
and
|\{\zeta\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}:\zeta^{n+1}=1\}|=G_{2}
. Their overlap consists of the solutions of
\zeta^{\gcd(n-1,n+1)}=1
. Since
\gcd(n-1,n+1)=\gcd(n-1,2)=\delta
, we have
\delta=1
when
n
is even and
\delta=2
when
n
is odd. In either case
\delta
divides
p-1
, so this overlap has exactly
\delta
elements. Thus the overlap consists only of boundary source points
\zeta=\pm 1
that satisfy the relevant parity condition; no nonboundary source element can lie on both branches. The parameter
\delta
therefore measures overlap between the two branch equations within one source group; the overlap between the split and nonsplit source groups is accounted for separately by
B
below. By inclusion-exclusion,

|S\cap\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}|=G_{1}+G_{2}-\delta.

Source
\mu_{p+1}
:
Similarly,
\delta
also divides
p+1
, so the same argument gives

|S\cap\mu_{p+1}|=G_{3}+G_{4}-\delta.

Boundary overlap at
\{\pm 1\}
:
The intersection
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}\cap\mu_{p+1}=\{\pm 1\}
. These boundary source parameters occur in both source groups before the final quotient by
\zeta\sim\zeta^{-1}
. The element
1
always satisfies
1^{n-1}=1
, so
1\in S
. The element
-1
satisfies
(-1)^{n-1}=1
iff
n
is odd, and
(-1)^{n+1}=1
iff
n
is odd; hence
-1\in S
iff
n
is odd. Set

B=|S\cap\{\pm 1\}|=1+[n\text{ odd}].

Total:
By inclusion-exclusion on
S=(S\cap\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times})\cup(S\cap\mu_{p+1})
,

|S|=(G_{1}+G_{2}-\delta)+(G_{3}+G_{4}-\delta)-B.

Passing from source roots
\zeta
to fixed points

a=\frac{\zeta+\zeta^{-1}}{2}

amounts to taking orbits of the involution
\zeta\mapsto\zeta^{-1}
on
S
. The only fixed points of this involution on
S
are the boundary elements
S\cap\{\pm 1\}
, again of cardinality
B
. Hence the number of orbits is

N_{1}=\frac{|S|-B}{2}+B=\frac{|S|+B}{2}.

Equivalently, this is Burnside’s lemma for the involution: the nonboundary source parameters are paired, while the boundary source parameters are counted once. Substituting the formula for
|S|
cancels the boundary term
B
and gives

N_{1}=\frac{G_{1}+G_{2}+G_{3}+G_{4}-2\delta}{2}.

∎

Thus Theorem
5.1
is used here as the length-one specialization of the known finite-field Chebyshev/Dickson graph structure, written in a two-source GCD form for the lifting arguments rather than as a new finite-field graph classification.

Example 5.2
.

p=7
,
n=5
:
G_{1}=\gcd(4,6)=2
,
G_{2}=\gcd(6,6)=6
,
G_{3}=\gcd(4,8)=4
,
G_{4}=\gcd(6,8)=2
,
\delta=2
. So
N_{1}=(2+6+4+2-4)/2=5
. Indeed,
T_{5}(a)\equiv a\pmod{7}
for
a\in\{0,1,3,4,6\}
.

Special cases

Corollary 6.1

(Chebyshev–Fermat)
.

Let
p
be an odd prime. For
n=p
, one has
N_{1}=p
. That is,
T_{p}(a)\equiv a\pmod{p}
for all
a\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

Proof.

G_{1}=p-1
,
G_{2}=\gcd(p+1,p-1)=2
,
G_{3}=\gcd(p-1,p+1)=2
,
G_{4}=p+1
,
\delta=2
. So
N_{1}=(p-1+2+2+p+1-4)/2=p
.
∎

This is the Chebyshev analogue of Fermat’s little theorem. In the Dickson language,
D_{p}(y,1)\equiv y\pmod{p}
for all
y
, a classical consequence of Dickson’s theory; see
[
3
,
10
]
. Compare also Diarra and Sylla
[
2
]
for related
p
-adic dynamical properties of
T_{p}
.

Corollary 6.2
.

Let
p\geq 5
be prime. For
n=2
, one has
N_{1}=2
.

Proof.

G_{1}=1
,
G_{2}=\gcd(3,p-1)
,
G_{3}=1
,
G_{4}=\gcd(3,p+1)
,
\delta=1
. Since exactly one of
p\pm 1
is divisible by
3
:
G_{2}+G_{4}=4
. Hence
N_{1}=(1+4+1-2)/2=2
.
∎

For

, the same formula gives
G_{1}=1
,
G_{2}=1
,
G_{3}=1
,

, and hence

.

Remark 6.3
.

The Dickson permutation criterion says that
T_{n}
permutes
\mathbb{F}_{p}
precisely when
\gcd(n,p^{2}-1)=1
. This criterion goes back to Dickson
[
3
]
; for the present normalization see Lidl–Mullen–Turnwald
[
10
, Theorem 3.2]
. This criterion is independent of the condition
G_{1}=G_{2}=G_{3}=G_{4}=1
that appears in Theorem

. In one direction, the Dickson criterion does

imply

: Example

has
(p,n)=(7,5)
, where

yet

. Conversely, for

one has

\gcd(11,4)=\gcd(13,4)=\gcd(11,6)=\gcd(13,6)=1,

so

and hence

, while

\gcd(12,5^{2}-1)=\gcd(12,24)=12.

Thus neither condition implies the other. The equality
G_{1}=G_{2}=G_{3}=G_{4}=1
is simply a sufficient condition, via Theorem

, for the fixed-point set of

over

to be the singleton

.

.

. For

.

For

,

,

. Since

. Hence

.

For

. As above,

.

Remark 6.5
.

The formula

does

hold in general when

or

. For example,

7.
Degenerate fixed points and higher fixed-point lifting

We now study how fixed points of
T_{n}
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
lift to higher prime-power levels. The general framework is Hensel lifting, but the present setting admits a more explicit branch-wise analysis because the fixed-point equation factors through the two source groups
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
and
\mu_{p+1}
. In the permutation-polynomial literature,
p
-adic lifting arguments of this type go back at least to Wan
[
20
]
; see also Silverman
[
18
, Chapter 2]
for the dynamical viewpoint. For Chebyshev permutation polynomials modulo
p^{k}
, Li, Lu, Tan, and Chen
[
8
]
describe the global functional-graph structure under permutation hypotheses, while Yoshioka
[
22
]
studies period growth of Chebyshev sequences modulo
p^{k}
. The results in this section are local: they include arbitrary degrees
n
, record the contribution of degenerate residue classes, give a first-lift count for every odd prime, and give an all-level closed formula for
p\geq 5
.

Proposition 7.1

(Degeneracy criterion)
.

Let
a_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
be a fixed point of
T_{n}
.

(a)

(
Nonboundary case,
a_{0}\neq\pm 1
.) Write
a_{0}=(\zeta+\zeta^{-1})/2
with
\zeta\neq\pm 1
. Then

T_{n}^{\prime}(a_{0})\equiv\begin{cases}n\pmod{p},&\text{if }\zeta^{n-1}=1,\\
-n\pmod{p},&\text{if }\zeta^{n+1}=1.\end{cases}

In particular,
a_{0}
is degenerate if and only if
p\mid(n-1)
in the first case, or
p\mid(n+1)
in the second.

(b)

(
Boundary case.
)
T_{n}^{\prime}(1)=n^{2}
, and when
-1
is a fixed point of
T_{n}
(equivalently,
n
is odd),
T_{n}^{\prime}(-1)=n^{2}
as well. Hence each boundary fixed point is degenerate if and only if
p\mid(n^{2}-1)
.

Thus in all cases, degenerate fixed points exist if and only if
p\mid(n^{2}-1)
.

For the count, we use the following boundary assignment. If
p\mid(n-1)
, then only the
\zeta^{n-1}=1
branch is counted, and each boundary degenerate fixed point is assigned to this branch. If
p\mid(n+1)
, the same convention is used for the
\zeta^{n+1}=1
branch. The alternatives
p\mid(n-1)
and
p\mid(n+1)
are mutually exclusive for
p\geq 3
, so the assignment is unambiguous. With this convention, every degenerate boundary fixed point is included exactly once, and the number of degenerate residue classes is

(3)

d=\begin{cases}\dfrac{G_{1}+G_{3}}{2}=\dfrac{\gcd(n\!-\!1,p\!-\!1)+\gcd(n\!-\!1,p\!+\!1)}{2},&\text{if }p\mid(n-1),\\[8.53581pt]
\dfrac{G_{2}+G_{4}}{2}=\dfrac{\gcd(n\!+\!1,p\!-\!1)+\gcd(n\!+\!1,p\!+\!1)}{2},&\text{if }p\mid(n+1),\\[8.53581pt]
0,&\text{if }p\nmid(n^{2}-1).\end{cases}

Only the number
d
is used in Theorem
7.3
; the branch assignment is a bookkeeping device for (
3
), not canonical dynamical data attached to a boundary point. No dynamical distinction between the two source branches is intended at
\zeta=\pm 1
; the convention is used only to avoid double-counting in
d
.

Proof.

Nonboundary case.
For
a_{0}\neq\pm 1
, we have
\zeta\neq\pm 1
, so
\zeta-\zeta^{-1}
is a unit in
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
and the identity
U_{n-1}(a_{0})=(\zeta^{n}-\zeta^{-n})/(\zeta-\zeta^{-1})
is valid. If
\zeta^{n-1}=1
then
\zeta^{n}=\zeta
, so
U_{n-1}(a_{0})=1
and
T_{n}^{\prime}(a_{0})=nU_{n-1}(a_{0})=n
. If
\zeta^{n+1}=1
then
\zeta^{n}=\zeta^{-1}
, so
U_{n-1}(a_{0})=-1
and
T_{n}^{\prime}(a_{0})=-n
.

Boundary case.
Using
T_{n}^{\prime}=nU_{n-1}
and the special values
U_{n-1}(1)=n
and
U_{n-1}(-1)=(-1)^{n-1}n
, we obtain
T_{n}^{\prime}(1)=n^{2}
and, when
n
is odd and hence
-1
is fixed,
T_{n}^{\prime}(-1)=n^{2}
. Hence the boundary multipliers are
\equiv 1\pmod{p}
iff
p\mid(n^{2}-1)
.

Count.
With the above assignment, consider first the case
p\mid(n-1)
, so
d
counts degenerate fixed points arising from the
\zeta^{n-1}=1
branch. The set

S_{n-1}=\{\zeta\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}\cup\mu_{p+1}:\zeta^{n-1}=1\}

has
|S_{n-1}\cap\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}|=G_{1}
and
|S_{n-1}\cap\mu_{p+1}|=G_{3}
, with overlap

|S_{n-1}\cap\{\pm 1\}|=\gcd(n-1,2).

By inclusion–exclusion,
|S_{n-1}|=G_{1}+G_{3}-\gcd(n-1,2)
. The involution
\zeta\leftrightarrow\zeta^{-1}
on
S_{n-1}
has
\gcd(n-1,2)
fixed points, namely the boundary parameters
\zeta=\pm 1
satisfying
\zeta^{n-1}=1
. Hence the number of distinct
a
-values from this branch is

\frac{|S_{n-1}|-\gcd(n-1,2)}{2}+\gcd(n-1,2)=\frac{G_{1}+G_{3}}{2}.

The case
p\mid(n+1)
is the same with the branch
\zeta^{n+1}=1
. In that case

S_{n+1}=\{\zeta\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}\cup\mu_{p+1}:\zeta^{n+1}=1\}

has source sizes
G_{2}
and
G_{4}
, and the boundary overlap in each source group has cardinality
\gcd(n+1,2)
. The same involution count gives

\frac{|S_{n+1}|-\gcd(n+1,2)}{2}+\gcd(n+1,2)=\frac{G_{2}+G_{4}}{2}.

The converse (no degenerate fixed points when
p\nmid(n^{2}-1)
) follows because both
n
and
-n
are
\not\equiv 1\pmod{p}
, and
n^{2}\not\equiv 1\pmod{p}
.
∎

Remark 7.2

(Boundary branch convention)
.

At the boundary source parameters
\zeta=\pm 1
, the inverse-pair description degenerates because
\zeta=\zeta^{-1}
. The branch assignment in Proposition
7.1
is therefore a bookkeeping convention for counting distinct residue classes
a\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
, not a canonical choice of source representative. Nonboundary classes are independent of this convention.

Before stating the lifting theorem, we record the dependency structure. The first-lift assertion uses only Taylor expansion at degenerate fixed residues and Hensel uniqueness at nondegenerate residues. The all-level assertion for
p\geq 5
uses the nonboundary branch parametrization in Lemma
7.5
, the boundary valuation estimate in Proposition
7.8
, and the elementary GCD saturation in Lemma
7.10
. No analytic estimate is used in the first-lift formula.

Theorem 7.3
.

Let
p
be an odd prime and let
n\geq 2
.

(First lift.)

For every odd prime
p
, one has

N_{2}=N_{1}+d\cdot(p-1),

where
d
is the number of degenerate fixed points given by Proposition
7.1
.

(All levels for
p\geq 5
.)

Assume in addition that
p\geq 5
. For
k\geq 1
, let

N_{k}=|\operatorname{Fix}(T_{n},\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z})|.

Set

G_{1,k}=\gcd(n\!-\!1,(p\!-\!1)p^{k-1}),\quad G_{2,k}=\gcd(n\!+\!1,(p\!-\!1)p^{k-1}),

G_{3,k}=\gcd(n\!-\!1,(p\!+\!1)p^{k-1}),\quad G_{4,k}=\gcd(n\!+\!1,(p\!+\!1)p^{k-1}),

and let
\delta=\gcd(n-1,2)
. Then, for
p\geq 5
,

N_{k}=\frac{G_{1,k}+G_{2,k}+G_{3,k}+G_{4,k}-2\delta}{2}.

Equivalently, if
s=\nu_{p}(n^{2}-1)
, then, for
p\geq 5
,

N_{k}=N_{1}+d\bigl(p^{\min(k-1,s)}-1\bigr).

The equivalence of the two displayed all-level formulas uses Proposition
7.1
and Lemma
7.10
; in particular, if
p\nmid n^{2}-1
, then
d=0
and the formula reduces to
N_{k}=N_{1}
. When
p\mid n^{2}-1
, exactly one of
n-1
and
n+1
is divisible by
p
(since
p
is odd), so the single valuation
s=\nu_{p}(n^{2}-1)
controls every degenerate branch.
The restriction
p\geq 5
is essential only for these all-level formulas; the first-lift formula above holds for every odd prime. See Remark
7.13
.

The proof of Theorem
7.3
proceeds in two parts. The first part, valid for any odd prime
p
, shows that above each degenerate residue class all
p
lifts are fixed modulo
p^{2}
; combined with the unique nondegenerate Hensel lifts, this gives
N_{2}=N_{1}+d(p-1)
. The second part, which requires
p\geq 5
, extends the local count from
p^{2}

Lemma 7.4

(Nonsplit principal-unit kernel)
.

Let
K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be the unramified quadratic extension, with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, and let
p
be odd. For
k\geq 1
,

\ker\!\Bigl(N:(1+p\mathcal{O}_{K})/(1+p^{k}\mathcal{O}_{K})\to(1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p})/(1+p^{k}\mathbb{Z}_{p})\Bigr)

is cyclic of order
p^{k-1}
. Moreover, every class in this kernel has a representative
\eta\in 1+p\mathcal{O}_{K}
with
N(\eta)=1
.

Proof.

For
k=1
both quotients are trivial, so assume
k\geq 2
.
The
p
-adic logarithm identifies
(1+p\mathcal{O}_{K})/(1+p^{k}\mathcal{O}_{K})
with
p\mathcal{O}_{K}/p^{k}\mathcal{O}_{K}
and is compatible with norm and trace:

\log N(\eta)=\operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(\log\eta).

Thus the logarithm of the norm kernel is the trace-zero subgroup of
p\mathcal{O}_{K}/p^{k}\mathcal{O}_{K}
. Because
K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is unramified,
\mathcal{O}_{K}
is a finite free
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module of rank
2
, and the trace map
\operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}:\mathcal{O}_{K}\to\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is surjective. Here surjectivity is immediate, since
\operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(1)=2
is a unit in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
for odd
p
; hence
\ker(\operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}})
is a direct summand of the free rank-two
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, and is therefore free of rank
1
. After multiplying by
p
and reducing modulo
p^{k}
, the trace-zero subgroup is cyclic of order
p^{k-1}
.

The same log/exp argument gives surjectivity of
N:1+p^{k}\mathcal{O}_{K}\to 1+p^{k}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Indeed, on these deeper principal units the logarithm identifies the norm with the trace:

\log N(u)=\operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(\log u),\qquad\log u\in p^{k}\mathcal{O}_{K}.

Since
\operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(\mathcal{O}_{K})=\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, we have

\operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(p^{k}\mathcal{O}_{K})=p^{k}\mathbb{Z}_{p},

and exponentiating gives the asserted norm-surjectivity. Hence, if
\eta_{0}
represents a kernel class, then
N(\eta_{0})\in 1+p^{k}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, so choose
u\in 1+p^{k}\mathcal{O}_{K}
with
N(u)=N(\eta_{0})^{-1}
. Then
\eta=\eta_{0}u
represents the same class and satisfies
N(\eta)=1
.
∎

Lemma 7.5

(Hensel inverse for the averaging map)
.

Let
a_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\setminus\{\pm 1\}
, and choose

a_{0}=\frac{\zeta_{0}+\zeta_{0}^{-1}}{2}.

In the split case
\zeta_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
, let
\tilde{\zeta}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
be the Teichmüller lift and set

\mathcal{P}_{k}=(1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p})/(1+p^{k}\mathbb{Z}_{p}).

In this case
\tilde{\zeta}\mathcal{P}_{k}
means classes represented by
\tilde{\zeta}\eta
with
\eta\in 1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, taken modulo
1+p^{k}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.
In the nonsplit case
\zeta_{0}\in\mu_{p+1}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
, let
K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be the unramified quadratic extension, let
\mathcal{O}_{K}
be its ring of integers, let
\tilde{\zeta}\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
be the Teichmüller lift, and set

\mathcal{P}_{k}=\ker\!\Bigl(N:(1+p\mathcal{O}_{K})/(1+p^{k}\mathcal{O}_{K})\to(1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p})/(1+p^{k}\mathbb{Z}_{p})\Bigr).

In the nonsplit case, the notation
\tilde{\zeta}\mathcal{P}_{k}
means classes represented by
\tilde{\zeta}\eta
with

\eta\in 1+p\mathcal{O}_{K},\qquad N(\eta)=1,

taken modulo
1+p^{k}\mathcal{O}_{K}
; Lemma
7.4
ensures that such representatives exist.
Then the map

F_{k}([z])=\frac{z+z^{-1}}{2}\pmod{p^{k}\mathbb{Z}_{p}}

is independent of the representative
z
of the class
[z]
. It induces a bijection from the coset quotient

\tilde{\zeta}\mathcal{P}_{k}

onto the residue classes modulo
p^{k}\mathbb{Z}_{p}

\{x\in\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}:x\equiv a_{0}\pmod{p}\}.

Proof.

The group
\mathcal{P}_{k}
is cyclic of order
p^{k-1}
in both cases. In the split case this follows from the logarithm isomorphism

\log:(1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p})/(1+p^{k}\mathbb{Z}_{p})\xrightarrow{\sim}p\mathbb{Z}_{p}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}_{p}.

In the nonsplit case this is Lemma
7.4
; we also use its norm-one representative statement below.

For the remainder of the proof, put
R=\mathbb{Z}_{p}
in the split case and
R=\mathcal{O}_{K}
in the nonsplit case.

We first construct the inverse. Let
x\in\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}
with
x\equiv a_{0}\pmod{p}
, and choose any lift
\hat{x}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Put

H_{\hat{x}}(Z)=Z^{2}-2\hat{x}Z+1.

Modulo
p
, this polynomial has the distinct roots
\zeta_{0}
and
\zeta_{0}^{-1}
. Moreover,

H_{\hat{x}}^{\prime}(\tilde{\zeta})=2(\tilde{\zeta}-\hat{x})\equiv\zeta_{0}-\zeta_{0}^{-1}\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}.

The complete-DVR form of Hensel’s lemma therefore gives a unique root

z(\hat{x})\in\tilde{\zeta}+pR

of
H_{\hat{x}}
, equivalently
F(z(\hat{x}))=\hat{x}
.

This construction depends only on
x\bmod p^{k}
. Indeed, if
\hat{x}_{1}\equiv\hat{x}_{2}\pmod{p^{k}}
, then

H_{\hat{x}_{1}}(Z)\equiv H_{\hat{x}_{2}}(Z)\pmod{p^{k}R[Z]}.

The congruence of the two lifted roots reducing to
\zeta_{0}
modulo
p^{k}R
follows directly from a unit-factor calculation. If
z_{i}\in\tilde{\zeta}+pR
satisfies
z_{i}^{2}-2\hat{x}_{i}z_{i}+1=0
for
i=1,2
, then subtracting the two equations gives

(z_{1}-z_{2})(z_{1}+z_{2}-2\hat{x}_{1})\equiv 0\pmod{p^{k}R}.

The second factor is congruent to
\zeta_{0}-\zeta_{0}^{-1}
modulo
p
, hence is a unit. Thus
z_{1}\equiv z_{2}\pmod{p^{k}R}
.

In the split case,
H_{\hat{x}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[Z]
and the Hensel root reducing to
\zeta_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
lies in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, so

z(\hat{x})\in\tilde{\zeta}(1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}).

In the nonsplit case, let
\varphi
be the nontrivial automorphism of
K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Since
H_{\hat{x}}
has coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
,
\varphi(z(\hat{x}))
is also a root; it reduces to
\zeta_{0}^{-1}
and hence equals
z(\hat{x})^{-1}
. Thus
N(z(\hat{x}))=1
, so

z(\hat{x})\in\tilde{\zeta}\cdot\{\eta\in 1+p\mathcal{O}_{K}:N(\eta)=1\}.

This proves well-definedness in the direction from lifts of
a_{0}
to the chosen branch
\tilde{\zeta}\mathcal{P}_{k}
.

Conversely, let
z=\tilde{\zeta}\eta
with
\eta
representing a class of
\mathcal{P}_{k}
; in the nonsplit case choose
\eta
with
N(\eta)=1
. Then
F(z)\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
: this is immediate in the split case, while in the nonsplit case
\varphi(z)=z^{-1}
and hence
F(z)
is fixed by
\varphi
.

It remains to check that the value depends only on the class modulo
1+p^{k}R
. If
z^{\prime}\equiv z\pmod{p^{k}R}
, then
z^{\prime-1}\equiv z^{-1}\pmod{p^{k}R}
, since both elements are units. Hence

F(z^{\prime})-F(z)\in p^{k}R.

In the nonsplit case the norm-one choice gives
\varphi(z)=z^{-1}
and
\varphi(z^{\prime})=z^{\prime-1}
, so both
F(z)
and
F(z^{\prime})
lie in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
; therefore

F(z^{\prime})-F(z)\in p^{k}R\cap\mathbb{Z}_{p}=p^{k}\mathbb{Z}_{p}.

This also proves independence of the chosen norm-one representative: if
z
and
z^{\prime}
represent the same nonsplit class modulo
1+p^{k}\mathcal{O}_{K}
, then
z^{\prime}/z\in 1+p^{k}\mathcal{O}_{K}
, so the same congruence and the identity
p^{k}\mathcal{O}_{K}\cap\mathbb{Z}_{p}=p^{k}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
show that the averaged value is unchanged modulo
p^{k}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. The same conclusion is immediate in the split case. Thus
F
gives a well-defined map

F_{k}:\tilde{\zeta}\mathcal{P}_{k}\to\{x\in\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}:x\equiv a_{0}\pmod{p}\}.

We now separate the three parts of the bijection. Well-definedness in the direction from the branch to residue classes follows from the congruence calculation above. Surjectivity follows from the Hensel construction at the beginning of the proof: given any lift
\hat{x}
of
a_{0}
, the polynomial
Z^{2}-2\hat{x}Z+1
has a unique root reducing to
\zeta_{0}
, and this root lies in the chosen split or nonsplit branch. For injectivity, suppose
z_{1},z_{2}
lie in the chosen branch and
F(z_{1})\equiv F(z_{2})\pmod{p^{k}}
. Then both
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
are roots modulo
p^{k}R
of

Z^{2}-2F(z_{1})Z+1\equiv 0.

They also both reduce to
\zeta_{0}
. The derivative
2Z-2F(z_{1})
at
Z=\zeta_{0}
reduces to
\zeta_{0}-\zeta_{0}^{-1}
, which is nonzero because
a_{0}\neq\pm 1
. Hensel uniqueness modulo
p^{k}R
therefore gives
z_{1}\equiv z_{2}\pmod{p^{k}R}
. Although
F(z)=F(z^{-1})
globally, the inverse branch lies in a different residue disk: if
z\equiv\zeta_{0}\pmod{p}
, then
z^{-1}\equiv\zeta_{0}^{-1}\pmod{p}
, and
\zeta_{0}\neq\zeta_{0}^{-1}
. Thus the restriction of
F
to the chosen Hensel branch is injective modulo
p^{k}
, and the map
F_{k}
is a bijection.
∎

Lemma 7.6

(Local count above a nonboundary degenerate class)
.

Let
p
be an odd prime, and let
a_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\setminus\{\pm 1\}
be a degenerate fixed point of
T_{n}
. Choose
\zeta_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}^{\times}
such that

a_{0}=\frac{\zeta_{0}+\zeta_{0}^{-1}}{2}.

Let
\sigma\in\{\pm 1\}
be determined by
\zeta_{0}^{n}=\zeta_{0}^{\sigma}
, equivalently
\zeta_{0}^{\,n-\sigma}=1
. Since
a_{0}
is degenerate on this branch, Proposition
7.1
gives
p\mid n-\sigma
; as
p
is odd,
p\nmid n+\sigma
, and hence
\nu_{p}(n^{2}-1)=\nu_{p}(n-\sigma)
. Set

s=\nu_{p}(n^{2}-1)=\nu_{p}(n-\sigma).

Then for every
k\geq 1
, the number of fixed points of
T_{n}
modulo
p^{k}
which reduce to
a_{0}
modulo
p
is exactly

p^{\min(k-1,s)}.

Proof.

The cases
\zeta_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
and
\zeta_{0}\in\mu_{p+1}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
are handled with the notation of Lemma
7.5
. Put
R=\mathbb{Z}_{p}
in the split case and
R=\mathcal{O}_{K}
in the nonsplit case. The Teichmüller lift
\tilde{\zeta}
has the same prime-to-
p
order as
\zeta_{0}
; hence
\zeta_{0}^{\,n-\sigma}=1
implies

\tilde{\zeta}^{\,n-\sigma}=1.

By Lemma
7.5
, fixed lifts above
a_{0}
may be counted on the parameter side as elements
z=\tilde{\zeta}\eta
with
\eta\in\mathcal{P}_{k}
.

Choose a representative
\hat{\eta}
of
\eta
in
1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
in the split case, and in the nonsplit case choose a representative
\hat{\eta}\in 1+p\mathcal{O}_{K}
with
N(\hat{\eta})=1
. Put

\hat{z}=\tilde{\zeta}\hat{\eta},\qquad\hat{x}=F(\hat{z}).

Then
\hat{x}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and represents the corresponding residue class modulo
p^{k}
. Using

T_{n}\!\left(\frac{w+w^{-1}}{2}\right)=\frac{w^{n}+w^{-n}}{2},

we have

T_{n}(\hat{x})-\hat{x}=\frac{(\hat{z}^{\,n-1}-1)(\hat{z}^{\,n+1}-1)}{2\,\hat{z}^{\,n}}.

Since
\hat{z}\equiv\zeta_{0}\pmod{p}
, the factor
\hat{z}^{\,n+\sigma}-1
is a unit, while the other branch is
\hat{z}^{\,n-\sigma}-1
. Indeed, if both branch factors vanished modulo
p
, then
\zeta_{0}^{\,n-\sigma}=\zeta_{0}^{\,n+\sigma}=1
, which would imply
\zeta_{0}^{2}=1
, contradicting
a_{0}\notin\{\pm 1\}
. Hence

T_{n}(\hat{x})\equiv\hat{x}\pmod{p^{k}}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\hat{z}^{\,n-\sigma}\equiv 1\pmod{p^{k}R}.

Using
\tilde{\zeta}^{\,n-\sigma}=1
, this is equivalent to

\hat{\eta}^{\,n-\sigma}\equiv 1\pmod{p^{k}R},

or to
\eta^{\,n-\sigma}=1
in
\mathcal{P}_{k}
.

The cyclic group
\mathcal{P}_{k}
has order
p^{k-1}
, so the kernel of

\eta\mapsto\eta^{\,n-\sigma}

has size

\gcd(n-\sigma,p^{k-1})=p^{\min(k-1,\nu_{p}(n-\sigma))}=p^{\min(k-1,s)}.

This is the desired number of fixed lifts above
a_{0}
.
∎

For
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
with
T_{n}(\varepsilon)=\varepsilon
, the factor theorem gives
x-\varepsilon\mid T_{n}(x)-x
in
\mathbb{Z}[x]
. Define

Q_{\varepsilon}(x)=\frac{T_{n}(x)-x}{x-\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{Z}[x].

The boundary count rests on the following
p
-adic valuation estimate, which is the analytic core of the argument and is stated separately so that it can be cited on its own. Here, as throughout, valuations on finite extensions are normalized by
\nu_{p}(p)=1
; the strict inequality
\nu_{p}(\cdot)>1/(p-1)
is the hypothesis under which the usual
p

Lemma 7.7

(Finite-extension log/exp estimate)
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be a finite extension, and extend
\nu_{p}
to
E
with
\nu_{p}(p)=1
.
All logarithms and exponentials below are taken on the open disk
\nu_{p}(\cdot)>1/(p-1)
, where the
p
-adic logarithm and exponential converge and the linear term controls the valuation.
If
\xi,\mu\in E
satisfy

\nu_{p}(\xi)>\frac{1}{p-1},\qquad\nu_{p}(\mu)>\frac{1}{p-1},

then

\nu_{p}(\log(1+\xi))=\nu_{p}(\xi),\qquad\nu_{p}(\exp(\mu)-1)=\nu_{p}(\mu).

Consequently, if
w\in E
satisfies
\nu_{p}(w-1)>1/(p-1)
and

A_{m}(w)=1+w+\cdots+w^{m-1}\qquad(m\geq 1),

then

\nu_{p}(A_{m}(w))=\nu_{p}(m).

Proof.

The displayed logarithm and exponential estimates are the standard finite-extension form of the
p
-adic log/exp valuation identities on the common convergence disk; see, for example, Koblitz
[
7
, Ch. IV, Sec. 1, Thm. 1, p. 81]
. For completeness, we recall the valuation reason. If
\nu_{p}(\xi)>1/(p-1)
, then for every
r\geq 2
,

\nu_{p}(\xi^{r}/r)-\nu_{p}(\xi)=(r-1)\nu_{p}(\xi)-\nu_{p}(r)>0,

so the linear term of
\log(1+\xi)=\xi-\xi^{2}/2+\cdots
has strictly smaller valuation than all later terms. Hence
\nu_{p}(\log(1+\xi))=\nu_{p}(\xi)
. Similarly, for
r\geq 2
,

\nu_{p}(\mu^{r}/r!)-\nu_{p}(\mu)=(r-1)\nu_{p}(\mu)-\nu_{p}(r!)>0,

because
\nu_{p}(r!)\leq(r-1)/(p-1)
. Thus the linear term also has strictly smaller valuation in

\exp(\mu)-1=\mu+\frac{\mu^{2}}{2!}+\frac{\mu^{3}}{3!}+\cdots

and
\nu_{p}(\exp(\mu)-1)=\nu_{p}(\mu)
whenever
\nu_{p}(\mu)>1/(p-1)
.

For the geometric sum, the case
w=1
gives
A_{m}(1)=m
. If
w\neq 1
, put
\lambda=\log(w)
. Then
\nu_{p}(\lambda)=\nu_{p}(w-1)
and
w=\exp(\lambda)
. Since

\nu_{p}(m\lambda)\geq\nu_{p}(\lambda)>\frac{1}{p-1},

we have

A_{m}(w)=\frac{\exp(m\lambda)-1}{\exp(\lambda)-1},

and therefore

\nu_{p}(A_{m}(w))=\nu_{p}(m\lambda)-\nu_{p}(\lambda)=\nu_{p}(m).

∎

Proposition 7.8

(
p
-adic valuation of
Q_{\varepsilon}
on the residue disk at
\varepsilon
)
.

Assume
p\geq 5
. Let
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
with
T_{n}(\varepsilon)=\varepsilon
, and suppose
\varepsilon
is a degenerate fixed point of
T_{n}
modulo
p
. Set
s=\nu_{p}(n^{2}-1)
. Then

\nu_{p}\bigl(Q_{\varepsilon}(x)\bigr)=s\qquad\text{for every }x\in\varepsilon+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}.

Proof.

Throughout this proof, valuations on finite extensions of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
are normalized by
\nu_{p}(p)=1
. All uses of
\log
and
\exp
occur only for elements whose normalized valuation is strictly greater than
1/(p-1)
.
The finite-extension log/exp valuation estimate used below is precisely Lemma
7.7
, where the standard source is cited explicitly.
Fix
x\in\varepsilon+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, and put
u=\varepsilon x\in 1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Let
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be a finite extension containing a root
w
of

W^{2}-2uW+1=0.

The extension
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
need not be unramified; throughout this proof the valuation
\nu_{p}
is extended to
E
and normalized by
\nu_{p}(p)=1
, so Lemma
7.7
applies in this finite-extension setting. Both roots are units. For either root
w
,

(w-1)^{2}=w^{2}-2w+1=2w(u-1).

Since
u\in 1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and
p
is odd, this gives
\nu_{p}(w-1)\geq 1/2
; in particular both roots reduce to
1
modulo the maximal ideal of
E
. Here the valuation on
E
is normalized by
\nu_{p}(p)=1
, so positive valuation is exactly membership in the maximal ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{E}
. Thus
\nu_{p}(w-1)
may be fractional in the ramified extension
E
, but the normalized value is at least
1/2
, which lies in the open log/exp range precisely for
p\geq 5
. Choose one such root
w
. Then

u=\frac{w+w^{-1}}{2},\qquad x=\varepsilon\frac{w+w^{-1}}{2}.

If
\varepsilon=-1
, then
T_{n}(\varepsilon)=\varepsilon
forces
n
to be odd. By the Chebyshev parity identity
T_{n}(-y)=(-1)^{n}T_{n}(y)
, we have

T_{n}(\varepsilon y)=\varepsilon T_{n}(y)

for all
y
. Thus, in both cases
\varepsilon=1
and
\varepsilon=-1
,

T_{n}(x)=\varepsilon\frac{w^{n}+w^{-n}}{2}.

Therefore

T_{n}(x)-x=\varepsilon\frac{w^{n}+w^{-n}-w-w^{-1}}{2}=\varepsilon\frac{(w^{n-1}-1)(w^{n+1}-1)}{2w^{n}},

and

x-\varepsilon=\varepsilon\frac{w+w^{-1}-2}{2}=\varepsilon\frac{(w-1)^{2}}{2w}.

For
w\neq 1
, dividing gives

Q_{\varepsilon}(x)=w^{1-n}\,\frac{w^{n-1}-1}{w-1}\,\frac{w^{n+1}-1}{w-1}.

Since

\frac{w^{m}-1}{w-1}=A_{m}(w)=1+w+\cdots+w^{m-1}

is a polynomial identity for
w\neq 1
and hence extends regularly to
w=1
, the same identity gives

(4)

Q_{\varepsilon}(x)=w^{1-n}A_{n-1}(w)A_{n+1}(w).

This is an exact product identity in
E
, so the valuation of
Q_{\varepsilon}(x)
is the sum of the valuations of the displayed factors; no cancellation estimate is being used. If the other root
w^{-1}
is used instead, then
A_{m}(w^{-1})=w^{1-m}A_{m}(w)
for
m\geq 1
, so the expression in (
4
) is unchanged. In particular, the valuation conclusion is independent of the chosen square-root branch. Although
w
may lie in a ramified extension and the normalized valuations of the separate factors may be fractional, the product
Q_{\varepsilon}(x)
lies in
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Hence the extended valuation on
E
restricts to the usual
\nu_{p}
on
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, and the valuation computed in
E
descends to the desired valuation over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. The endpoint
w=1
corresponds exactly to
u=1
, hence to
x=\varepsilon
, and (
4
) gives

Q_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon)=(n-1)(n+1)=n^{2}-1.

Equivalently,
Q_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon)=T_{n}^{\prime}(\varepsilon)-1=n^{2}-1
.

It remains to justify that Lemma
7.7
applies when
w\neq 1
. The estimate above gives
\nu_{p}(w-1)\geq 1/2
. When
p\geq 5
, one has
1/2>1/(p-1)
, so Lemma
7.7
applies to both
A_{n-1}(w)
and
A_{n+1}(w)
. If
w=1
, the same valuations follow directly from
A_{m}(1)=m
. The hypothesis
p\geq 5
is used here in an essential way: for
p=3
, the bound
\nu_{p}(w-1)\geq 1/2
does
not
strictly exceed
1/(p-1)=1/2
, and the log/exp estimates fail at the boundary of convergence.

Since
p
is odd and
\varepsilon
is degenerate, exactly one of
n-1
and
n+1
is divisible by
p
, and

s=\nu_{p}(n^{2}-1)=\nu_{p}(n-1)+\nu_{p}(n+1).

The factor
w^{1-n}
is a unit. Hence (
4
) and Lemma
7.7
give

\nu_{p}\bigl(Q_{\varepsilon}(x)\bigr)=\nu_{p}(n-1)+\nu_{p}(n+1)=s.

∎

Lemma 7.9

(Local count above a boundary degenerate class)
.

Assume
p\geq 5
, and let
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
be a degenerate fixed point of
T_{n}
modulo
p
. (The point
\varepsilon=1
is fixed for every
n
, so when degenerate boundary fixed points exist it is one of them; the point
\varepsilon=-1
is fixed only when
n
is odd.) Set
s=\nu_{p}(n^{2}-1)
. Then for every
k\geq 1
, the number of fixed points of
T_{n}
modulo
p^{k}
which reduce to
\varepsilon
modulo
p
is exactly

p^{\min(k-1,s)}.

Proof.

Let
g(x)=T_{n}(x)-x
, so
g(x)=(x-\varepsilon)Q_{\varepsilon}(x)
. We use the convention
\nu_{p}(0)=+\infty
. For
x=\varepsilon
, the congruence
g(x)\equiv 0\pmod{p^{k}}
is immediate. For
x\neq\varepsilon
, Proposition
7.8
gives
\nu_{p}(Q_{\varepsilon}(x))=s
, hence

\nu_{p}\bigl(g(x)\bigr)=\nu_{p}(x-\varepsilon)+s.

Therefore, for
x\in\varepsilon+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, the congruence
g(x)\equiv 0\pmod{p^{k}}
holds iff
\nu_{p}(x-\varepsilon)\geq k-s
. Combined with
x\equiv\varepsilon\pmod{p}
, this is equivalent to
\nu_{p}(x-\varepsilon)\geq\max(1,k-s)
. Modulo
p^{k}
, the number of residue classes
x
with this property is

p^{\,k-\max(1,k-s)}=p^{\min(k-1,s)}.

∎

Lemma 7.10

(GCD saturation)
.

Let
M
be a positive integer with
p\nmid M
, and let
m
be a nonzero integer. Set
s=\nu_{p}(m)
and
G=\gcd(m,M)
. Then, for every
k\geq 1
,

\gcd(m,Mp^{k-1})=G\,p^{\min(k-1,s)}.

Proof.

Write
m=p^{s}m_{0}
with
p\nmid m_{0}
. Since
p\nmid M
, we have
G=\gcd(m_{0},M)
, and hence

\gcd(m,Mp^{k-1})=\gcd(p^{s}m_{0},Mp^{k-1})=\gcd(m_{0},M)\,p^{\min(k-1,s)}.

∎

Proof of Theorem
7.3
.

The proof has two parts. The first part proves the unconditional first assertion (
N_{2}=N_{1}+d(p-1)
for any odd
p
) by showing that, above each degenerate residue class
a_{0}
, all
p
lifts solve
T_{n}(x)\equiv x\pmod{p^{2}}
; combined with the unique nondegenerate Hensel lift, this gives the count. The second part assumes
p\geq 5
and uses Lemmas
7.6
and
7.9
to extend the local count from
p^{2}
to all
p^{k}
, yielding both the closed form
N_{k}=N_{1}+d(p^{\min(k-1,s)}-1)
and its GCD reformulation.

Part 1:
N_{2}=N_{1}+d(p-1)
.

Nondegenerate fixed points lift uniquely by Hensel’s lemma (Theorem
4.1
).

Let
g(x)=T_{n}(x)-x
. For each degenerate fixed point
a_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
, choose an integer representative
A_{0}\equiv a_{0}\pmod{p}
; for boundary classes we take
A_{0}=\varepsilon=\pm 1
. For a degenerate residue class, the residue of
g(A)
modulo
p^{2}
is independent of the integer representative
A\equiv a_{0}\pmod{p}
. Indeed, if
A^{\prime}=A+tp
with
t\in\mathbb{Z}
, then Taylor’s formula gives

g(A^{\prime})=g(A)+tp\,g^{\prime}(A)+p^{2}r

for some
r\in\mathbb{Z}
, and degeneracy gives
g^{\prime}(A)\equiv 0\pmod{p}
. Hence

g(A^{\prime})\equiv g(A)\pmod{p^{2}}.

We show that all
p
lifts
A_{0}+jp
(
j=0,\ldots,p-1
) satisfy
g(A_{0}+jp)\equiv 0\pmod{p^{2}}
. Since
g\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
, the Taylor expansion is coefficientwise integral, and

g(A_{0}+jp)=g(A_{0})+jp\,g^{\prime}(A_{0})+p^{2}r_{j}\equiv g(A_{0})\pmod{p^{2}}

for some
r_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}
, because
p\mid g^{\prime}(A_{0})
. It therefore suffices to prove
p^{2}\mid g(A_{0})
. We treat the boundary and nonboundary degenerate classes separately, consistent with the split used later in Lemmas
7.6
and
7.9
.

Boundary case:
a_{0}=\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
.

Here
A_{0}=\varepsilon
and
T_{n}(\varepsilon)=\varepsilon
holds in
\mathbb{Z}
: at
\varepsilon=1
this is immediate, and at
\varepsilon=-1
(fixed only when
n
is odd) it follows from
T_{n}(-1)=(-1)^{n}=-1
. Thus
g(A_{0})=0
in
\mathbb{Z}
, and in particular
p^{2}\mid g(A_{0})
.

Nonboundary case:
a_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\setminus\{\pm 1\}
.

Let
K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be the unramified quadratic extension with residue field
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
, and let
\mathcal{O}_{K}
be its ring of integers. By Lemma
3.1
, write

a_{0}=\frac{\zeta_{0}+\zeta_{0}^{-1}}{2},\qquad\zeta_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}^{\times}.

Since
a_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
, the element
\zeta_{0}
lies in either
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
or
\mu_{p+1}
. Let
\tilde{\zeta}\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
be the Teichmüller lift of
\zeta_{0}
.

Let
\varphi
denote the arithmetic Frobenius of
K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. If
\zeta_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
, then
\varphi(\tilde{\zeta})=\tilde{\zeta}
. If
\zeta_{0}\in\mu_{p+1}
, then
\zeta_{0}^{p}=\zeta_{0}^{-1}
, so

\varphi(\tilde{\zeta})=\widetilde{\zeta_{0}^{p}}=\widetilde{\zeta_{0}^{-1}}=\tilde{\zeta}^{-1}.

Thus

\tilde{a}:=\frac{\tilde{\zeta}+\tilde{\zeta}^{-1}}{2}

is Frobenius-invariant, hence lies in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, and satisfies
\tilde{a}\equiv a_{0}\pmod{p}
.

Because
a_{0}
is degenerate, Proposition
7.1
gives a unique branch sign
\sigma\in\{\pm 1\}
such that
\zeta_{0}^{n}=\zeta_{0}^{\sigma}
and
p\mid(n-\sigma)
. Since Teichmüller lifts preserve prime-to-
p
order, the same branch relation holds for
\tilde{\zeta}
:

\tilde{\zeta}^{\,n-\sigma}=1.

Using

T_{n}\!\left(\frac{z+z^{-1}}{2}\right)=\frac{z^{n}+z^{-n}}{2},

we obtain
T_{n}(\tilde{a})=\tilde{a}
, so
g(\tilde{a})=0
in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

Write
\tilde{a}=A_{0}+tp
with
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Then

g(A_{0})=g(\tilde{a}-tp)=\underbrace{g(\tilde{a})}_{=\,0}-tp\,g^{\prime}(\tilde{a})+p^{2}r

for some
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.
Since
a_{0}\notin\{\pm 1\}
, we have
\zeta_{0}^{2}\neq 1
, so
\tilde{\zeta}-\tilde{\zeta}^{-1}
is a unit in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. By Lemma
3.1
,

U_{n-1}(\tilde{a})=\frac{\tilde{\zeta}^{n}-\tilde{\zeta}^{-n}}{\tilde{\zeta}-\tilde{\zeta}^{-1}}=\frac{\tilde{\zeta}^{\sigma}-\tilde{\zeta}^{-\sigma}}{\tilde{\zeta}-\tilde{\zeta}^{-1}}=\sigma.

Therefore

g^{\prime}(\tilde{a})=T_{n}^{\prime}(\tilde{a})-1=n\,U_{n-1}(\tilde{a})-1

equals
\sigma(n-\sigma)
, and is thus divisible by
p
. It follows that
\nu_{p}(tp\,g^{\prime}(\tilde{a}))\geq 2
, so
p^{2}\mid g(A_{0})
.

(This nonboundary construction applies in particular when
a_{0}=0
: then
\zeta_{0}+\zeta_{0}^{-1}=0
, so
\zeta_{0}^{2}=-1
and
\zeta_{0}
is a primitive fourth root of unity. The fixed-point condition
T_{n}(0)=0
is equivalent to
n
odd, that is,
n\equiv\pm 1\pmod{4}
. Thus
\zeta_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
if
4\mid p-1
, and
\zeta_{0}\in\mu_{p+1}
otherwise.)

In both the boundary and nonboundary cases, all
p
lifts are roots modulo
p^{2}
, giving a net gain of
p-1
per degenerate fixed point. This proves the first assertion.

Part 2:
N_{k}=N_{1}+d(p^{\min(k-1,s)}-1)
for
p\geq 5
.

Assume now that
p\geq 5
. For each degenerate residue class
a_{0}
, the construction above produces an exact fixed point
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
lifting
a_{0}
: in the boundary case
\alpha=a_{0}
, while in the nonboundary case
\alpha=(\tilde{\zeta}+\tilde{\zeta}^{-1})/2
. Let
s=\nu_{p}(n^{2}-1)
. At such a degenerate fixed point, the derivative has valuation exactly
s
. Indeed, for nonboundary points the preceding computation gives

g^{\prime}(\alpha)=n-1\qquad\text{or}\qquad g^{\prime}(\alpha)=-(n+1),

according to the branch
\zeta^{n-1}=1
or
\zeta^{n+1}=1
. For the boundary points
\alpha=\pm 1
, one has
g^{\prime}(\alpha)=T_{n}^{\prime}(\alpha)-1=n^{2}-1
. Since
p
is odd, the degenerate condition implies that exactly one of
n-1
and
n+1
is divisible by
p
, so in every degenerate case

\nu_{p}(g^{\prime}(\alpha))=\nu_{p}(n^{2}-1)=s.

We now count fixed lifts above each degenerate residue class.

If
a_{0}\notin\{\pm 1\}
, Lemma
7.6
shows that the number of fixed points modulo
p^{k}
reducing to
a_{0}
modulo
p
is exactly
p^{\min(k-1,s)}
.

If
a_{0}\in\{\pm 1\}
, then
a_{0}
is a degenerate boundary fixed point, and Lemma
7.9
shows that the number of fixed points modulo
p^{k}
reducing to
a_{0}
modulo
p
is again exactly
p^{\min(k-1,s)}
.

Thus every degenerate residue class contributes exactly
p^{\min(k-1,s)}
fixed points modulo
p^{k}
. Each nondegenerate residue class contributes exactly one fixed point modulo
p^{k}
by Hensel’s lemma. Hence

N_{k}=(N_{1}-d)+d\,p^{\min(k-1,s)}=N_{1}+d\bigl(p^{\min(k-1,s)}-1\bigr).

It remains to rewrite this as a GCD formula. If
p\nmid(n^{2}-1)
, then
d=0
, and Lemma
7.10
shows that
G_{i,k}=G_{i}
for all
i
, so the formula is immediate. Suppose instead that
p^{s}\parallel(n-1)
. Applying Lemma
7.10
with
m=n-1
and
M=p-1
or
p+1
gives

G_{1,k}=p^{\min(k-1,s)}G_{1},\qquad G_{3,k}=p^{\min(k-1,s)}G_{3},

while
G_{2,k}=G_{2}
and
G_{4,k}=G_{4}
, because
p\nmid(n+1)
. Using Proposition
7.1
,
d=(G_{1}+G_{3})/2
, so

N_{k}=N_{1}+\frac{G_{1}+G_{3}}{2}\bigl(p^{\min(k-1,s)}-1\bigr)=\frac{G_{1,k}+G_{2}+G_{3,k}+G_{4}-2\delta}{2}.

The case
p^{s}\parallel(n+1)
is analogous, using
d=(G_{2}+G_{4})/2
and Lemma
7.10
to obtain

G_{2,k}=p^{\min(k-1,s)}G_{2},\qquad G_{4,k}=p^{\min(k-1,s)}G_{4}.

This proves the stated GCD formula for
N_{k}
.
∎

The following lemma is the local first-lift consequence extracted from the proof of Theorem
7.3
. It is stated separately because Section
10
uses it for the iterates
T_{n^{j}}
.

Lemma 7.11

(Local first lift)
.

Let
p
be an odd prime, let
m\geq 2
, and put
g_{m}(x)=T_{m}(x)-x
. Let
a_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
be a fixed point of
T_{m}
. If
a_{0}
is nondegenerate, then exactly one lift of
a_{0}
to
\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z}
is fixed by
T_{m}
. If
a_{0}
is degenerate, then all
p
lifts of
a_{0}
to
\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z}
are fixed by
T_{m}
.

Proof.

Choose an integer representative
A_{0}
of
a_{0}
. For a lift
A_{0}+tp
with
t\in\{0,\dots,p-1\}
, Taylor expansion gives

g_{m}(A_{0}+tp)\equiv g_{m}(A_{0})+tp\,g_{m}^{\prime}(A_{0})\pmod{p^{2}}.

If
a_{0}
is nondegenerate, then
g_{m}^{\prime}(A_{0})=T_{m}^{\prime}(A_{0})-1
is a unit modulo
p
, so this linear congruence has a unique solution for
t
modulo
p
; this is the usual first Hensel lift.

Assume now that
a_{0}
is degenerate, so
g_{m}^{\prime}(A_{0})\equiv 0\pmod{p}
. It remains to show that
p^{2}\mid g_{m}(A_{0})
. If
a_{0}=\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
, write
A_{0}=\varepsilon+pu
in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Since
a_{0}
is a fixed point,
g_{m}(\varepsilon)=0
exactly, and degeneracy gives
g_{m}^{\prime}(\varepsilon)\equiv 0\pmod{p}
. Taylor expansion at
\varepsilon
gives

g_{m}(A_{0})\equiv g_{m}(\varepsilon)+pu\,g_{m}^{\prime}(\varepsilon)\equiv 0\pmod{p^{2}}.

If
a_{0}\notin\{\pm 1\}
, choose a source parameter
\zeta_{0}
for
a_{0}
and let
\tilde{\zeta}
be its Teichmüller lift in the corresponding source group. Put

\alpha=\frac{\tilde{\zeta}+\tilde{\zeta}^{-1}}{2}.

In the split case,
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
immediately. In the nonsplit case, if
\varphi
denotes the Frobenius automorphism of the unramified quadratic extension, then
\varphi(\tilde{\zeta})=\tilde{\zeta}^{-1}
. Hence
\alpha
is fixed by
\varphi
; since
\alpha
is integral,
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.
Because
a_{0}
is fixed by
T_{m}
, there is a sign
\sigma\in\{\pm 1\}
with
\zeta_{0}^{m-\sigma}=1
; hence
\tilde{\zeta}^{\,m-\sigma}=1
and
g_{m}(\alpha)=0
. Since
\alpha\equiv a_{0}\pmod{p}
and
a_{0}
is degenerate,
g_{m}^{\prime}(\alpha)\equiv g_{m}^{\prime}(a_{0})\equiv 0\pmod{p}
. Taylor expansion at
\alpha
then gives

g_{m}(A_{0})\equiv g_{m}(\alpha)+(A_{0}-\alpha)g_{m}^{\prime}(\alpha)\equiv 0\pmod{p^{2}},

because
A_{0}-\alpha\in p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Thus
p^{2}\mid g_{m}(A_{0})
in all degenerate cases, and the displayed congruence vanishes for every
t
. Hence all
p
lifts are fixed modulo
p^{2}
.
∎

Lemma 7.12

(Local form of the fixed-point count)
.

Assume
p\geq 5
, and let
a_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
be a fixed point of
T_{n}
. For every
k\geq 1
, the number of lifts of
a_{0}
to
\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}
which are fixed by
T_{n}
modulo
p^{k}
is:

(a)

1
, if
a_{0}
is nondegenerate;

(b)

p^{\min(k-1,\nu_{p}(n^{2}-1))}
, if
a_{0}
is degenerate.

Proof.

The nondegenerate case is Hensel’s lemma applied to
T_{n}(x)-x
. If
a_{0}
is degenerate and nonboundary, the count is Lemma
7.6
; if
a_{0}
is degenerate and lies in
\{\pm 1\}
, the count is Lemma
7.9
. These are exactly the two local cases used in the proof of Theorem
7.3
.
∎

Remark 7.13

(The hypothesis
p\geq 5
is essential)
.

The closed form
N_{k}=N_{1}+d(p^{\min(k-1,s)}-1)
for
k\geq 2

fails
at
p=3
. For
(p,n,k)=(3,2,3)
one has
N_{1}=1
(only
a=1\in\mathbb{F}_{3}
is fixed by
T_{2}
), the unique fixed point is degenerate so
d=1
, and
s=\nu_{3}(n^{2}-1)=\nu_{3}(3)=1
. The formula therefore predicts

N_{1}+d\bigl(3^{\min(2,1)}-1\bigr)=1+1\cdot 2=3,

whereas direct enumeration of the roots of
T_{2}(x)-x=2x^{2}-x-1=(2x+1)(x-1)
modulo
27
gives the six solutions
\{1,4,10,13,19,22\}
, i.e.
N_{3}=6
. Here the two factors are associates modulo
3
, so roots of the product modulo
27
are not obtained by solving the two linear factors separately. The discrepancy traces to Proposition
7.8
: at
p=3
the bound
\nu_{p}(w-1)\geq 1/2
from the square-root parametrization equals the convergence threshold
1/(p-1)=1/2
, so the
p
-adic logarithm and exponential estimates which give
\nu_{p}(A_{m}(w))=\nu_{p}(m)
no longer apply at the boundary of convergence. The nonboundary cyclic principal-unit argument does not use
p\geq 5
; the obstruction here is specifically the boundary valuation estimate in Proposition
7.8
. The unconditional first assertion (
N_{2}=N_{1}+d(p-1)
, valid for any odd
p
) is unaffected.

For the reader’s orientation, we record here the empirical behavior at
p=3
. Computing
N_{k}
directly for
1\leq k\leq 6
and a range of degrees
n
with
3\nmid n
shows that the eventual stabilized value of
N_{k}
at
p=3
is consistently larger than the value
N_{1}+d(p^{s}-1)
predicted by the
p\geq 5
formula, and the level at which stabilization occurs is shifted by one. For example, for
n=2
(
d=1
,
s=1
) the sequence
(N_{k})_{k\geq 1}
is
1,3,6,6,6,\ldots
, stabilizing at level
3
rather than at level
2
. For
n=8
(
d=1
,
s=2
) it is
1,3,9,18,18,\ldots
, stabilizing at level
4
. For
n=26
(
d=1
,
s=3
) it is
1,3,9,27,54,54,\ldots
, stabilizing at level
5
. In each case, the increments
N_{k+1}-N_{k}
initially follow the same pattern
(p-1)d,(p-1)pd,\ldots
as in the
p\geq 5
regime, but at the level where the
p\geq 5
formula would saturate, an additional increment occurs at
p=3
, of strictly smaller size than the next-stage extrapolation would predict. A precise all-level closed form for
p=3
would require a separate boundary analysis at the convergence boundary
\nu_{p}(w-1)=1/(p-1)
; we do not pursue it here.

The case
p=2
has further dyadic features (the factor
2
is not invertible, so the parametrization
a=(\zeta+\zeta^{-1})/2
requires modification), and lies outside the scope of the present paper. We refer to Fan and Liao
[
4
]
for a complete description of the dynamical structure of Chebyshev polynomials on
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.

Corollary 7.14

(Chebyshev–Fermat lifting)
.

Let
p
be an odd prime. For
n=p
and every
k\geq 1
, one has
N_{k}=p
.

Proof.

By Corollary
6.1
, every residue class modulo
p
is fixed. For
g(x)=T_{p}(x)-x
we have

g^{\prime}(x)=p\,U_{p-1}(x)-1\equiv-1\pmod{p},

so every fixed residue class is nondegenerate. Hensel’s lemma therefore gives a unique lift of each class in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
to a fixed point modulo
p^{k}
for every
k\geq 1
. Since there are
p
residue classes modulo
p
, it follows that
N_{k}=p
for all
k
.
∎

When no fixed point is degenerate, the lifting picture collapses: every fixed point modulo
p
has a unique Hensel lift, and the count is independent of
k
.

Corollary 7.15

(Trivial-lifting case)
.

Let
p\geq 5
and
n\geq 2
. If
p\nmid n^{2}-1
, then

N_{k}=N_{1}\qquad\text{for every }k\geq 1.

Proof.

The hypothesis
p\nmid n^{2}-1
gives
d=0
in Proposition
7.1
, so Theorem
7.3
reduces to
N_{k}=N_{1}
.
∎

The trivial-lifting hypothesis
p\nmid n^{2}-1
is independent of the permutation hypothesis
\gcd(n,p^{3}-p)=1
used in
[
8
]
: neither implies the other. For example,
(p,n)=(5,11)
satisfies
\gcd(11,120)=1
, so
T_{11}
is a permutation of
\mathbb{Z}/5^{k}\mathbb{Z}
, yet
p\mid n-1=10
and consequently
N_{1}=5
while
N_{2}=N_{3}=\cdots=13
. Conversely,
(p,n)=(5,2)
satisfies
5\nmid n^{2}-1=3
, so lifting is trivial and
N_{k}=N_{1}
for all
k
, yet
\gcd(2,120)=2
, so
T_{2}
is not a permutation of
\mathbb{Z}/5^{k}\mathbb{Z}
. Thus, even within the permutation setting, the level-
p^{k}
count can strictly exceed
N_{1}
, and conversely trivial lifting can occur outside the permutation setting.

In dynamical language, Theorem
7.3
also yields a clean increment formula: it identifies precisely how many new fixed points appear at each lifting level, and the level beyond which no new fixed points are created.

Corollary 7.16

(Stabilization and increments)
.

Let
p\geq 5
,
n\geq 2
, and
s=\nu_{p}(n^{2}-1)
. Then
(N_{k})_{k\geq 1}
is nondecreasing and stabilizes at level
s+1
:

N_{k+1}-N_{k}=\begin{cases}d(p-1)p^{k-1},&1\leq k\leq s,\\
0,&k>s.\end{cases}

In particular,
N_{k}=N_{s+1}
for every
k\geq s+1
.

Proof.

By Theorem
7.3
,
N_{k}=N_{1}+d(p^{\min(k-1,s)}-1)
, so

N_{k+1}-N_{k}=d\bigl(p^{\min(k,s)}-p^{\min(k-1,s)}\bigr).

For
1\leq k\leq s
, this equals
d(p^{k}-p^{k-1})=d(p-1)p^{k-1}
. For
k>s
, both exponents equal
s
and the difference vanishes.
∎

Example 7.17
.

8.
The Chebyshev order

By the composition law,
T_{n}^{\circ j}=T_{n^{j}}
. The points of exact period
r
for
T_{n}
on
\mathbb{F}_{p}
are the fixed points of
T_{n^{r}}
that are not fixed by
T_{n^{j}}
for any proper divisor
j\mid r
,
j<r
. The global cycle structure of Chebyshev maps over finite fields is described in
[
5
,
15
]
. Here we isolate an orbitwise invariant that yields explicit period formulas and is especially well suited to the quotient relation
\zeta\sim\zeta^{-1}
.

The key observation is this. The parametrization
a=(\zeta+\zeta^{-1})/2
identifies the two source points
\zeta
and
\zeta^{-1}
. Consequently, an iterate
T_{n^{j}}
fixes
a
not only when
n^{j}\equiv 1\pmod{e}
, but already when
n^{j}\equiv-1\pmod{e}
, since both congruences send
\zeta
to one of the two representatives of the same
a
-value. The relevant order invariant for Chebyshev dynamics is therefore the first occurrence of
\pm 1
, not just of
1
.

Definition 8.1
.

For integers
n\geq 2
and
e\geq 1
with
\gcd(n,e)=1
, the
Chebyshev order
of
n
modulo
e
is

\operatorname{cord}_{e}(n)=\min\{r\geq 1:n^{r}\equiv\pm 1\pmod{e}\}.

Equivalently, for
e\geq 2
, this is the order of the class of
n
in the quotient of
(\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
by the involution generated by
-1
. In the notation of Qureshi–Panario
[
15
]
, this is the quotient order
\widetilde{o}_{e}(n)
in
(\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z})^{\times}/\{\pm 1\}
; we use the notation
\operatorname{cord}_{e}(n)
to emphasize its role as the Chebyshev source-order period in the prime-power lifting arguments below. We set
\operatorname{cord}_{1}(n)=1
for all
n
; congruences modulo
1
are, of course, vacuous. For
e=2
and
\gcd(n,2)=1
, the definition gives
\operatorname{cord}_{2}(n)=1
; when
n
is even, the boundary point
a=-1
is not periodic and this convention is not used to assign it a period.

The Chebyshev order is determined by, but is generally different from, the usual multiplicative order
\operatorname{ord}_{e}(n)
.

Lemma 8.2
.

Let
e\geq 2
and assume
\gcd(n,e)=1
. Let
\rho=\operatorname{ord}_{e}(n)
. Then
\operatorname{cord}_{e}(n)=\rho/2
if
\rho
is even and
n^{\rho/2}\equiv-1\pmod{e}
; otherwise
\operatorname{cord}_{e}(n)=\rho
. For
e=1
, the convention
\operatorname{cord}_{1}(n)=1
of Definition
8.1
applies, and every positive integer
r
satisfies
n^{r}\equiv\pm 1\pmod{1}
.

Proof.

For
e=2
, the assumption
\gcd(n,2)=1
gives
\rho=1
and
\operatorname{cord}_{2}(n)=1
, so the stated formula holds. Hence assume
e\geq 3
. Then
1
and
-1
are distinct modulo
e
. The condition
n^{r}\equiv 1\pmod{e}
holds iff
\rho\mid r
, while the condition
n^{r}\equiv-1\pmod{e}
holds iff
\rho
is even and
r\equiv\rho/2\pmod{\rho}
. Thus
n^{r}\equiv\pm 1\pmod{e}
iff
r
is a multiple of
\rho/2
when
n^{\rho/2}\equiv-1\pmod{e}
, and iff
r
is a multiple of
\rho
otherwise.

The assertion for
e=1
is exactly the convention in Definition
8.1
.
∎

Remark 8.3
.

The name reflects the role: the Chebyshev equation
T_{n}(a)=a
factors as
(\zeta^{n-1}-1)(\zeta^{n+1}-1)=0
, and the period structure is governed by
n^{r}\equiv\pm 1
rather than
n^{r}\equiv 1
alone. In the language of Dickson polynomials,
\operatorname{cord}_{e}(n)
records the length of the
\zeta
-orbit under the map
\zeta\mapsto\zeta^{n}
after passing to the dihedral quotient induced by
\zeta\leftrightarrow\zeta^{-1}
; compare
[
17
]
.

9.
Periodic points over
\mathbb{F}_{p}

The next theorem records the orbitwise period formula over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
in the same two-source language. While the resulting periodic-point data can be recovered from the global functional-graph descriptions of Gassert
[
5
, Theorems 2.3–2.4]
, Qureshi–Panario
[
15
, Corollaries 1–2 and Theorems 4–5]
, and Hutz–Patel
[
6
, Sec. 3, Thm. 3.6]
, the invariant
\operatorname{cord}_{e}(n)
packages them in a short divisor-order form suited to the later lifting arguments. Thus the point of the formulation below is not a new finite-field graph classification, but the identification of the quotient order that controls the first-lift and tower statements in Sections
10
and
11
. Throughout this and the following period-count sections,
P_{r}
counts elements of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
of exact period
r
, not cycles. We write
\varphi
for Euler’s totient function and
\mu_{\mathrm{Mob}}
for the classical Möbius function; the notation
\mu_{p+1}
continues to denote the subgroup of
(p+1)
st roots of unity. When the Chebyshev degree varies, we write

N_{k,p}(m):=|\operatorname{Fix}(T_{m},\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z})|,\qquad d_{p}(m):=\#\{\text{degenerate fixed points of }T_{m}\text{ over }\mathbb{F}_{p}\}.

Here
d_{p}(m)
is computed by Proposition
7.1
with
n
replaced by
m
. In particular,
N_{1,p}(m)=|\operatorname{Fix}(T_{m},\mathbb{F}_{p})|
.

Theorem 9.1
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
with
a=(\zeta+\zeta^{-1})/2
, where
\zeta\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
or
\zeta\in\mu_{p+1}
, and let
e=\operatorname{ord}(\zeta)
. For the boundary points we use the convention
\zeta=1
for
a=1
, hence
e=1
, and
\zeta=-1
for
a=-1
, hence
e=2
. For
a\neq\pm 1
, the two possible choices of
\zeta
are inverse to one another, so
e=\operatorname{ord}(\zeta)
is well defined. Then
a
is periodic under
T_{n}
on
\mathbb{F}_{p}
if and only if
\gcd(n,e)=1
. When this holds,

\operatorname{per}(a)=\operatorname{cord}_{e}(n).

In particular,
a=1
is always fixed, while
a=-1
is fixed if and only if
n
is odd.

Proof.

If
a=1
, then
e=1
, so
\gcd(n,e)=1
and
\operatorname{per}(a)=1=\operatorname{cord}_{1}(n)
.

If
a=-1
, then
e=2
. The point
-1
is periodic exactly when
T_{n}(-1)=(-1)^{n}=-1
, i.e. when
n
is odd, equivalently
\gcd(n,2)=1
; in that case
\operatorname{per}(-1)=1=\operatorname{cord}_{2}(n)
.

Assume now that
a\notin\{\pm 1\}
.

Suppose first that
a
is periodic of period
t
. Then
T_{n^{t}}(a)=a
, so Lemma
3.3
applied to
T_{n^{t}}
gives

e\mid(n^{t}-1)\qquad\text{or}\qquad e\mid(n^{t}+1).

Hence
\gcd(n,e)=1
.

Conversely, assume
\gcd(n,e)=1
and set
t=\operatorname{cord}_{e}(n)
. Then
n^{t}\equiv\pm 1\pmod{e}
, so Lemma
3.3
shows that
T_{n^{t}}(a)=a
. If
0<j<t
and
T_{n^{j}}(a)=a
, then again Lemma
3.3
gives
n^{j}\equiv\pm 1\pmod{e}
, contradicting the minimality of
t
. Therefore
\operatorname{per}(a)=t
.
∎

Corollary 9.2

(Direct periodic-point count)
.

The boundary source orders
e=1
and
e=2
are separated because their parameters
\zeta=1
and
\zeta=-1
are fixed by the involution
\zeta\mapsto\zeta^{-1}
; equivalently, the signs
+1
and
-1
are not distinguished modulo
e=1
or
e=2
. For
e\geq 3
, the source parameters with order
e
occur in inverse pairs, and the quotient
\zeta\sim\zeta^{-1}
is uniformly two-to-one. The point count for exact period
r
is

(5)

P_{r}=[r=1]\cdot(1+[n\text{ odd}])+\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\mid(p-1),\;e\geq 3\\
\gcd(n,e)=1,\;\operatorname{cord}_{e}(n)=r\end{subarray}}\!\!\frac{\varphi(e)}{2}+\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\mid(p+1),\;e\geq 3\\
\gcd(n,e)=1,\;\operatorname{cord}_{e}(n)=r\end{subarray}}\!\!\frac{\varphi(e)}{2},

where
[P]
denotes the Iverson bracket
(
[P]=1
if
P
is true and
0
otherwise
)
. There is no double-counting between the two sums for
e\geq 3
, because
\gcd(p-1,p+1)=2
.

Proof.

For
e\geq 3
dividing
p-1
(resp.
p+1
), the elements
\zeta\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
(resp.
\mu_{p+1}
) of order
e
number
\varphi(e)
. The map
\zeta\mapsto a=(\zeta+\zeta^{-1})/2
is
2
-to-
1
, giving
\varphi(e)/2
distinct
a
. By Theorem
9.1
, each such
a
has period
\operatorname{cord}_{e}(n)
. The boundary cases
e=1
(
a=1
) and
e=2
(
a=-1
) are encoded by the Iverson-bracket term
[r=1](1+[n\text{ odd}])
.
∎

Corollary 9.3

(Consistency with Möbius formula)
.

The formula (
5
) is equivalent to

P_{r}=\sum_{j\mid r}\mu_{\mathrm{Mob}}(r/j)\cdot N_{1,p}(n^{j}).

Proof.

For each
j\geq 1
, the fixed points of
T_{n^{j}}
are exactly the points whose period under
T_{n}
divides
j
. Hence
N_{1,p}(n^{j})=\sum_{d\mid j}P_{d}
, and Möbius inversion yields the stated formula.
∎

Corollary 9.4
.

P_{r}=0
unless there exists a divisor
e
of
p-1
or
p+1
with
\gcd(n,e)=1
and
\operatorname{cord}_{e}(n)=r
. In particular,

P_{r}=0\qquad\text{for all }r>\max\{\operatorname{cord}_{e}(n):e\mid(p-1)\text{ or }e\mid(p+1),\ \gcd(n,e)=1\}.

The set in the maximum is nonempty because
e=1
is included and
\operatorname{cord}_{1}(n)=1
.

10.
Ghost periodic points over
\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z}

We next lift the period analysis to
\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z}
. The global graph-theoretic literature over higher prime-power levels, especially
[
8
, Thm. 1, Thm. 2, and Prop. 7]
, describes cycle counts and path structure, while Yoshioka
[
22
]
studies period growth for Chebyshev sequences modulo
p^{k}
. Nara’s main theorem
[
13
, main theorem]
separates one-step lifts of a cycle according to the multiplier alternatives
\lambda=0
,
\lambda=1
, and
\lambda\notin\{0,1\}
. The phenomenon isolated here is more local and Chebyshev-specific: a periodic residue class modulo
p
may acquire lifts whose period already increases at the first prime-power level, and the new periods are expressed by the source order
\operatorname{cord}_{ep}(n)
. Compared with Nara’s general cycle-lifting framework, Theorem
10.4
identifies which multiplier alternative occurs from the signed source congruence, gives the Chebyshev-specific first lifted period
\operatorname{cord}_{ep}(n)
, and gives the exact split of the
p
lifts above each periodic residue. We call such lifted points
ghost periodic points
; the cycles formed by them are ghost orbits. The orbitwise theorem in this section assumes
p\nmid n
; without that hypothesis, the point-count formula below still follows from fixed-point counts by Möbius inversion. From the viewpoint of arithmetic dynamics, the ghost phenomenon is a first-lift periodic-point bifurcation governed by the interaction between the base period and the extra modulus
p
.

Definition 10.1
.

A periodic point
\tilde{a}\in\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z}
of
T_{n}
is a
ghost
if its period modulo
p^{2}
is strictly greater than the period of
a_{0}=\tilde{a}\bmod p
. Thus a ghost point is not visible as a new periodic residue modulo
p
; it appears only after lifting to
p^{2}
. A
ghost orbit
is a cycle consisting of ghost points.

Lemma 10.2

(First-lift sign matching)
.

Let
p
be odd, let
p\nmid n
, and let
e\geq 1
with
\gcd(n,e)=\gcd(e,p)=1
. Set
t=\operatorname{cord}_{e}(n)
and
\kappa=\operatorname{cord}_{ep}(n)
.

If
e\geq 3
, let
\sigma\in\{\pm 1\}
be the unique sign such that
n^{t}\equiv\sigma\pmod{e}
. Then

\kappa=t\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad n^{t}\equiv\sigma\pmod{p}.

If
e=1
or
e=2
, then
t=1
, and

\kappa=t\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad n^{2}\equiv 1\pmod{p}.

Proof.

For
e\geq 3
, the signs
+1
and
-1
are distinct modulo
e
. Since no positive integer
j<t
satisfies
n^{j}\equiv\pm 1\pmod{e}
, the equality
\kappa=t
is equivalent to
n^{t}\equiv\pm 1\pmod{ep}
. The sign modulo
e
is already fixed as
\sigma
, so the Chinese remainder theorem gives the criterion
n^{t}\equiv\sigma\pmod{p}
.

For
e=1
or
e=2
, the boundary source-order period is
t=1
. Since
p
is odd and
p\nmid n
, one has
\operatorname{cord}_{ep}(n)=1
exactly when
n\equiv\pm 1\pmod{p}
, equivalently
n^{2}\equiv 1\pmod{p}
.
∎

Lemma 10.3

(Degeneracy for iterates modulo
ep
)
.

Assume
p\nmid n
, and let
a_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
be periodic under
T_{n}
with source order
e
and base period
t=\operatorname{cord}_{e}(n)
. Let
j\geq 1
with
t\mid j
. Then
a_{0}
is a degenerate fixed point of
T_{n^{j}}
modulo
p
if and only if

n^{j}\equiv\pm 1\pmod{ep},

equivalently, if and only if
\operatorname{cord}_{ep}(n)\mid j
.

Proof.

First suppose
a_{0}\notin\{\pm 1\}
. Since
t\mid j
, the point
a_{0}
is fixed by
T_{n^{j}}
modulo
p
, so exactly one sign
\sigma\in\{\pm 1\}
satisfies
n^{j}\equiv\sigma\pmod{e}
. By Proposition
7.1
, applied to the degree
n^{j}
, the fixed point
a_{0}
is degenerate for
T_{n^{j}}
if and only if
n^{j}\equiv\sigma\pmod{p}
. This is equivalent, by the Chinese remainder theorem, to
n^{j}\equiv\sigma\pmod{ep}
, hence to
n^{j}\equiv\pm 1\pmod{ep}
.

For
a_{0}=1
, one has
e=1
and
t=1
. The boundary derivative for
T_{n^{j}}
is
(n^{j})^{2}
, so Proposition
7.1
gives degeneracy if and only if
(n^{j})^{2}\equiv 1\pmod{p}
, equivalently
n^{j}\equiv\pm 1\pmod{p}
. Since
e=1
, the modulus
ep
equals
p
, so this is the asserted criterion. For
a_{0}=-1
, periodicity forces
n
odd, so
n^{j}
is odd and
a_{0}
is fixed by
T_{n^{j}}
; the same boundary derivative argument gives degeneracy if and only if
n^{j}\equiv\pm 1\pmod{p}
. When
e=2
, the congruence modulo
2
imposes no additional sign condition, because
n^{j}
and both signs are odd modulo
2
. The sign is therefore determined entirely by the congruence modulo
p
, and the Chinese remainder theorem gives the stated equivalence modulo
2p=ep
.

The final equivalence is the definition of
\operatorname{cord}_{ep}(n)
.
∎

Theorem 10.4

(Ghost period theorem)
.

Assume
p\nmid n
. Let
a_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
be periodic under
T_{n}
, and define

e=\begin{cases}1,&a_{0}=1,\\
2,&a_{0}=-1,\\
\operatorname{ord}(\zeta),&a_{0}\notin\{\pm 1\}\text{ and }a_{0}=(\zeta+\zeta^{-1})/2.\end{cases}

For
a_{0}=-1
, periodicity already forces
n
to be odd. Because
p\nmid n
and
\gcd(n,e)=1
, the integer
n
is invertible modulo both
e
and
ep
, so the Chebyshev orders below are well defined. Let

t=\operatorname{per}(a_{0})=\operatorname{cord}_{e}(n),\qquad\kappa=\operatorname{cord}_{ep}(n).

For the boundary source orders
e=1
and
e=2
, Lemma
10.2
says that
\kappa=t=1
is equivalent to
n^{2}\equiv 1\pmod{p}
.
Since
n^{\kappa}\equiv\pm 1\pmod{ep}
, reduction modulo
e
gives
n^{\kappa}\equiv\pm 1\pmod{e}
, and hence
t\mid\kappa
.
Then each lift of
a_{0}
to
\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z}
has period either
t
or
\kappa
, and exactly one of the following occurs:

(a)

If
\kappa=t
, then all
p
lifts of
a_{0}
have period
t
modulo
p^{2}
.

(b)

If
\kappa>t
, then exactly one lift of
a_{0}
has period
t
modulo
p^{2}
, and the remaining
p-1
lifts are ghost periodic points of period
\kappa
.

Proof.

Let
m=n^{t}
. Then
a_{0}
is a fixed point of
T_{m}
modulo
p
.

First suppose
a_{0}\notin\{\pm 1\}
. Since
t=\operatorname{cord}_{e}(n)
, exactly one of the congruences
m\equiv 1\pmod{e}
or
m\equiv-1\pmod{e}
holds. Let
\sigma\in\{\pm 1\}
denote this sign, so
a_{0}
lies on the branch
\zeta^{m-\sigma}=1
. By Proposition
7.1
(a),
a_{0}
is degenerate for
T_{m}
if and only if
m\equiv\sigma\pmod{p}
. Thus

a_{0}\text{ is degenerate for }T_{m}\iff m\equiv\sigma\pmod{p}.

By Lemma
10.2
, this is equivalent to
\kappa=t
.
Thus, for nonboundary points,

a_{0}\text{ is degenerate for }T_{m}\iff\operatorname{cord}_{ep}(n)=t.

The point here is subtle and worth stating: it is not enough that
n^{t}\equiv\pm 1\pmod{p}
on its own; the sign modulo
p
must agree with the already determined sign
\sigma=n^{t}\bmod e
. This sign-matching condition is exactly what distinguishes Hensel retention from ghost period growth.

For the boundary points, if
a_{0}=1
then
e=1
and
t=1
, while if
a_{0}=-1
then
e=2
and
t=1
. Hence in both cases
m=n^{t}=n
, and Proposition
7.1
(b) gives
T_{m}^{\prime}(a_{0})=T_{n}^{\prime}(a_{0})=n^{2}
. Therefore
a_{0}
is degenerate for
T_{m}
iff
n^{2}\equiv 1\pmod{p}
, again equivalent to
\kappa=t
by Lemma
10.2
. Thus the same dichotomy holds for all periodic residues.

If
\kappa=t
, then

11.
Higher-level exact-period counts and the cord tower

Möbius inversion gives exact-period point counts on
\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}
once the fixed-point counts for the iterates
T_{n^{j}}
are known. This identity is valid for every odd prime. The closed substitution from Theorem
7.3
, however, is available at all levels only for
p\geq 5
. Under the additional hypotheses
p\geq 5
and
p\nmid n
, the orbitwise dichotomy of Theorem
10.4
extends to a sign-aware tower governed by the chain
c_{q}=\operatorname{cord}_{ep^{q}}(n)
.
Here and below,
\mu_{\mathrm{Mob}}
denotes the classical Möbius function.

Throughout this section,

P_{r}^{(k)}:=\#\{x\in\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}:\operatorname{per}_{p^{k}}(x)=r\},

and we continue to use the notation
N_{k,p}(m)
and
d_{p}(m)
for fixed-point and degeneracy counts when the Chebyshev degree varies.

Corollary 11.1

(Arbitrary-level periodic-point count)
.

For every odd prime
p
, every
n\geq 2
, every
k\geq 1
, and every
r\geq 1
, the following Möbius identity counts points, not cycles:

(8)

P_{r}^{(k)}=\sum_{j\mid r}\mu_{\mathrm{Mob}}(r/j)\,N_{k,p}(n^{j}).

Only periodic residues are counted by the quantities
P_{r}^{(k)}
; preperiodic but nonperiodic residues are not included in any
P_{r}^{(k)}
.
If in addition
p\geq 5
, then substituting the closed fixed-point formula from Theorem
7.3
gives

(9)

P_{r}^{(k)}=\sum_{j\mid r}\mu_{\mathrm{Mob}}(r/j)\,\Bigl(N_{1,p}(n^{j})+d_{p}(n^{j})\bigl(p^{\min(k-1,\,\nu_{p}(n^{2j}-1))}-1\bigr)\Bigr).

Proof.

The composition law
T_{n^{j}}=T_{n}^{\circ j}
shows that, for any
j\geq 1
, a residue fixed by
T_{n^{j}}
is periodic under
T_{n}
on
\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}
, and its exact period divides
j
. Indeed, on the finite set
\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}
, the equality
T_{n}^{\circ j}(x)=x
places
x
on a cycle whose length divides
j
. Conversely, every point whose exact period divides
j
is fixed by
T_{n^{j}}
. Hence

N_{k,p}(n^{j})=\sum_{\ell\mid j}P_{\ell}^{(k)},

and Möbius inversion gives (
8
). For
p\geq 5
, substituting Theorem
7.3
with base
n
replaced by
n^{j}
into each summand yields (
9
). For
p=3
and
k\geq 3
, the identity (
8
) remains valid, but no all-level closed formula for the quantities
N_{k,p}(n^{j})
is asserted here.
∎

.

. At

Corollary 11.4

(Eventual stabilization of period-

.

Let

,

, and

,

Remark 11.5

(Comparison with cycle-count stabilization in
[

]
)
.

Under the additional hypothesis

, where

, set
c_{q}=\operatorname{cord}_{ep^{q}}(n)
, so that
c_{0}\mid c_{1}\mid c_{2}\mid\cdots
. Fix
k\geq 1
, and list the distinct values among
c_{0},c_{1},\dots,c_{k-1}
in their divisibility order, equivalently in strictly increasing numerical order:

\tau_{1}<\tau_{2}<\cdots<\tau_{u}

Define

m_{i}:=\max\{q\in\{0,1,\dots,k-1\}:c_{q}=\tau_{i}\}\qquad(1\leq i\leq u).

Thus
m_{i}
is the last level at which the tower value remains
\tau_{i}
.
When
k=1
, the list consists only of
\tau_{1}=c_{0}
and
m_{1}=0
.
Set

M_{0}=0,\qquad M_{i}=p^{m_{i}}\quad(1\leq i\leq u).

Since the largest distinct tower value comes from some
c_{q}
with maximal index
q=k-1
, one has
m_{u}=k-1
. Thus the terminal fixed-lift set in the proof has cardinality
p^{k-1}
, the total number of lifts above
a_{0}
.
Then the
p^{k-1}
lifts
\tilde{a}\in\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}
that reduce to
a_{0}
modulo
p
are partitioned by their period under
T_{n}
as follows:

(10)

\#\bigl\{\tilde{a}\equiv a_{0}\!\!\pmod{p}:\operatorname{per}_{p^{k}}(\tilde{a})=\tau_{i}\bigr\}=M_{i}-M_{i-1}\qquad(1\leq i\leq u).

Proof.

For
j\geq 1
with
c_{0}\mid j
, let
A_{j}
and
\lambda_{k}(j)
be as in Lemma
11.8
. Since
c_{q}\mid c_{q+1}
by Lemma
11.6
, the distinct values
\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{u}
form a divisibility chain. Equal values occur in consecutive blocks: if
a<b
and
c_{a}=c_{b}
, then
c_{a}\mid c_{q}\mid c_{b}
for every
a\leq q\leq b
, and hence
c_{q}=c_{a}
. The lemma gives

|A_{j}|=p^{\lambda_{k}(j)}.

Step 1.
We show
\lambda_{k}(\tau_{i})=m_{i}
. For
q\leq m_{i}
, Lemma
11.6
gives
c_{q}\mid c_{m_{i}}=\tau_{i}
. If
m_{i}+1\leq k-1
, then
c_{m_{i}+1}>\tau_{i}
, so
c_{m_{i}+1}\nmid\tau_{i}
, giving
\lambda_{k}(\tau_{i})=m_{i}
. If
m_{i}=k-1
, the same conclusion is immediate. Therefore
|A_{\tau_{i}}|=p^{m_{i}}
.

Step 2.
For any
j
with
c_{0}\mid j
, let
i_{*}
be the largest index with
\tau_{i_{*}}\mid j
. Because the sequence
(c_{q})
is divisibility-monotone, the distinct values
\tau_{i}
occur in consecutive blocks. Hence the largest
q
for which
c_{q}\mid j
is precisely the terminal index
m_{i_{*}}
attached to the largest distinct value
\tau_{i_{*}}
dividing
j
. Therefore
\lambda_{k}(j)=m_{i_{*}}
. Since
\tau_{i_{*}}\mid j
, the Chebyshev composition law gives

T_{n^{j}}=\bigl(T_{n^{\tau_{i_{*}}}}\bigr)^{\circ j/\tau_{i_{*}}}.

Therefore
A_{\tau_{i_{*}}}\subseteq A_{j}
. By Lemma
11.8
and the identity
\lambda_{k}(j)=m_{i_{*}}
,

|A_{j}|=p^{\lambda_{k}(j)}=p^{m_{i_{*}}}=|A_{\tau_{i_{*}}}|.

The inclusion is therefore an equality:

(11)

A_{j}=A_{\tau_{i_{*}}}\qquad\text{whenever }c_{0}\mid j.

For notational uniformity, set
A_{\tau_{0}}:=\emptyset
.

Step 3.
Let
\tilde{a}\in A_{\tau_{i}}\setminus A_{\tau_{i-1}}
, and let
r=\operatorname{per}_{p^{k}}(\tilde{a})
. Since
\tilde{a}\in A_{\tau_{i}}
, it is fixed by
T_{n^{\tau_{i}}}
; the exact period
r
therefore divides the return time
\tau_{i}
. Also
\tilde{a}\in A_{r}
. Reducing
T_{n^{r}}(\tilde{a})=\tilde{a}
modulo
p
gives
T_{n^{r}}(a_{0})=a_{0}
. Since
a_{0}
has base period
c_{0}
, this implies
c_{0}\mid r
, so (
11
) applies with
j=r
. Hence
A_{r}=A_{\tau_{i_{*}}}
for the largest
i_{*}
with
\tau_{i_{*}}\mid r
. If
i_{*}<i
, then
\tilde{a}\in A_{\tau_{i-1}}
, a contradiction. Hence
i_{*}=i
, so
\tau_{i}\mid r
. Since also
r\mid\tau_{i}
, we get
r=\tau_{i}
.

Step 4.
The chain
A_{\tau_{1}}\subseteq A_{\tau_{2}}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq A_{\tau_{u}}
has
|A_{\tau_{i}}|=p^{m_{i}}=M_{i}
. Step 3 identifies
A_{\tau_{i}}\setminus A_{\tau_{i-1}}
with the lifts of period exactly
\tau_{i}
, proving (
10
). The total count is
p^{k-1}
, as required.
∎

Remark 11.10

(Recovery of Theorem
10.4
at
k=2
)
.

For
k=2
, the chain
c_{0},c_{1}
has either one or two distinct values. If
c_{0}=c_{1}
, then Theorem
11.9
gives
p
lifts of period
c_{0}
, recovering case (a) of Theorem
10.4
. If
c_{0}<c_{1}
, then it gives one lift of period
c_{0}
and
p-1
lifts of period
c_{1}
, recovering case (b).

Example 11.11

(Multi-layer ghost tower at
k=3
and
k=4
)
.

p=5
,
n=2
,
a_{0}=1
. Then
e=1
and

c_{0}=\operatorname{cord}_{1}(2)=1,\qquad c_{1}=\operatorname{cord}_{5}(2)=2,\qquad c_{2}=\operatorname{cord}_{25}(2)=10,\qquad c_{3}=\operatorname{cord}_{125}(2)=50.

For
k=3
we obtain three distinct values
\tau_{1}=1<\tau_{2}=2<\tau_{3}=10
, with
m_{1}=0
,
m_{2}=1
, and
m_{3}=2
. Theorem
11.9
predicts that the
25
lifts of
a_{0}=1
in
\mathbb{Z}/125\mathbb{Z}
split as

1\text{ lift of period }1,\qquad 4\text{ lifts of period }2,\qquad 20\text{ lifts of period }10.

At
k=4
a fourth layer appears: the four distinct values are
\tau_{1}=1<\tau_{2}=2<\tau_{3}=10<\tau_{4}=50
, with
m_{1}=0
,
m_{2}=1
,
m_{3}=2
,
m_{4}=3
, so
M_{i}=5^{m_{i}}
and Theorem
11.9
predicts that the
125
lifts of
a_{0}=1
in
\mathbb{Z}/625\mathbb{Z}
split as

1\text{ lift of period }1,\qquad 4\text{ lifts of period }2,\qquad 20\text{ lifts of period }10,\qquad 100\text{ lifts of period }50.

Direct enumeration confirms each layer count, illustrating how each prime-power level can introduce a fresh ghost layer above the previous tower.

Theorem
11.9
counts lifted points above one residue class. The corresponding count of lifted cycles above an entire base cycle follows immediately, and connects the local picture to the cycle-distribution language used in the graph-theoretic literature
[
8
,
15
]
.

Corollary 11.12

(Cycle counts above a base cycle)
.

Assume the hypotheses of Theorem
11.9
. Let
\mathcal{C}
be a
T_{n}
-cycle of length
\ell
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, and let
\tau_{1}<\cdots<\tau_{u}
and
m_{1}<\cdots<m_{u}
be the cord-tower data attached to any point of
\mathcal{C}
. Then the lifts of
\mathcal{C}
to
\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}
contain

\frac{\ell\bigl(p^{m_{i}}-p^{m_{i-1}}\bigr)}{\tau_{i}}\qquad(1\leq i\leq u)

T_{n}
-cycles of length
\tau_{i}
, with the convention
p^{m_{0}}:=0
.

Proof.

The base cycle has length
\ell=c_{0}=\operatorname{cord}_{e}(n)
by Theorem
9.1
, so
\ell\mid\tau_{i}
for every
i
by Lemma
11.6
. By Theorem
11.9
, above each point of
\mathcal{C}
there are
p^{m_{i}}-p^{m_{i-1}}
lifts of period
\tau_{i}
. Summing over the
\ell
base points gives a total of
\ell(p^{m_{i}}-p^{m_{i-1}})
lifted points of period
\tau_{i}
. Each such lifted cycle has length
\tau_{i}
and projects to
\mathcal{C}
via reduction modulo
p
, visiting each of the
\ell
base points exactly
\tau_{i}/\ell
times; in particular, distinct lifted cycles deposit disjoint
\tau_{i}/\ell
-element fibers above each base point. The number of cycles is therefore the lifted point count divided by
\tau_{i}
.
∎

12.
The Chebyshev–Fermat case

The case
n=p
is exceptional from the periodic-point viewpoint: nontrivial periodic lifting disappears completely at every prime-power level. Thus no cord-tower interpretation is needed in this non-permutation case. The periodic-point statement below holds for every odd prime
p
; it does not invoke the all-level fixed-point formula of Theorem
7.3
, whose closed form is restricted to
p\geq 5
. This is compatible with the fixed-point lifting statement in Corollary
7.14
and with the
p
-adic behavior of
T_{p}
studied by Diarra and Sylla
[
2
]
.

Corollary 12.1
.

Let
p
be an odd prime. When
n=p
, for every
k\geq 1
every periodic point of
T_{p}
on
\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}
is fixed. In particular,

P_{1}^{(k)}=p,\qquad P_{r}^{(k)}=0\quad(r\geq 2).

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}
be periodic under
T_{p}
, say of period
r
. Then
x
is a fixed point of
T_{p^{r}}
modulo
p^{k}
. Write
a_{0}=x\bmod p
. Since
T_{p}\equiv\operatorname{id}\pmod{p}
, the residue
a_{0}
is fixed by
T_{p^{r}}
modulo
p
.

Set
g_{r}(x)=T_{p^{r}}(x)-x
. Then

g_{r}^{\prime}(x)=p^{r}U_{p^{r}-1}(x)-1\equiv-1\pmod{p}.

Thus
a_{0}
is a nondegenerate fixed point of
T_{p^{r}}
modulo
p
, so Hensel’s lemma gives a unique lift of
a_{0}
fixed by
T_{p^{r}}
modulo
p^{k}
.

On the other hand, the fixed lift of
a_{0}
for
T_{p}
is also fixed by
T_{p^{r}}=T_{p}^{\circ r}
. By uniqueness,
x
must equal that fixed lift. Hence every periodic point modulo
p^{k}
is already fixed by
T_{p}
. Since each residue class modulo
p
contributes exactly one fixed lift by Corollary
7.14
, the count is
P_{1}^{(k)}=p
and
P_{r}^{(k)}=0
for all
r\geq 2
.
∎
