Tunable and Predictable Bimodal Metathesis Polymers Enabled by Fusion Catalysis-Controlled Polymerization of Norbornene
Authors: Chao Wang, Tian-Tian Wang, Zheng-Hong Luo, Yin-Ning Zhou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The diversity of molecular weight distribution (MWD) is a cornerstone in materials property control within the polymer community. Modulating the shape of the MWD profile through an established polymerization process is challenging, as it requires overcoming inherent kinetic limitations. In this work, we report a dual-catalysis strategy to construct a tunable bimodal distribution via one-pot ring-opening metathesis polymerization (ROMP) of norbornene. A composite catalytic system, comprising two metathesis catalysts with distinct reactivities, is designed, and the catalysts interact cooperatively to mediate chain growth rather than proceeding in an isolated manner. Three-fold regulation of peak spacing (23.8–170.0 kg/mol), relative peak height (0.2–1.0), and molecular weight span is readily achieved by adjusting the catalyst composition ratio, polymerization time, and total catalyst loading. The rheological measurements of representative unimodal and bimodal samples show that the bimodal polymer has reduced storage modulus G′ and complex viscosity (η*) compared with its unimodal counterpart. Additionally, an equation is derived to predict the contributions of the two catalytic populations to the resulting distribution by quantifying the effect of catalyst composition linked to the kinetic differences, which serves as a predictive framework integrated into the developed strategy. Kinetic experiments and theoretical computations reveal a fusion-catalysis mechanism behind bimodal distribution formation, in which propagating chains dynamically shuttle between two kinetic behaviors through the switching of ligand association states. This work establishes a robust strategy enabling previously inaccessible control over distribution shape via one-pot ROMP, advancing MWD engineering toward broader polymerization platforms.

Introduction

Ring-opening metathesis polymerization (ROMP) represents a robust platform for the synthesis of functional polymers from cyclic olefins, affording well-defined polymer microstructures under mild conditions. − A unique feature of the synthesized polymers is their diverse microstructures, among which molecular weight distribution (MWD) is one of the most important structural characteristics. − The ability to control polymer MWD demonstrates mastery over the polymerization process, which, in turn, allows for the fine-tuning of the resulting polymer properties. − Beyond the conventional focus on narrow MWD control, the development of robust and scalable methods for synthesizing polymers with variable distribution profiles (including breadth, skewness, and modality) has been a longstanding active focus in MWD engineering. − Although numerous elegant strategies for manipulating the distribution have been developed in various polymerization platforms, − approaches for manipulating MWD profiles in ROMP remain underdeveloped, which restricts its potential for producing polymers with optimized performance.

For certain specialized industrial applications, broad, bimodal, or even multimodal distributions are highly desirable for polyolefins, as they endow polymers with improved mechanical properties while maintaining good processability, thereby overcoming the limitations inherent to monomodal systems, in which polymer chains are concentrated within a narrow molecular weight range. − Polymer blending is a conventional way of tailoring the modality of MWD by combining discrete presynthesized polymer samples with different degrees of polymerization. However, it requires precise control over the blending ratios and the tedious preparation of the polymer components. Besides, polymer blending is fundamentally a postpolymerization physical method for tuning MWD, which may suffer from compositional heterogeneity and nonuniform entanglement. In contrast, chemical regulation during the polymerization process provides a more effective and direct route for controlling MWDs in situ, as it enables distribution shaping at the molecular level with improved uniformity and synthetic efficiency without requiring separate polymer synthesis and subsequent blending.

So far, one of the most accessible strategies for tailoring the polymer MWD in ROMP relies on flow-mediated platforms. One notable advance was reported by the Guironnet group, who achieved control over distribution shape in ROMP by programmable regulation of the flow rate and the residence time, generating narrowly-distributed polymer fractions with different molecular weights that were blended (Figure ). , Parallel to polymerization process design based on flow chemistry, accessing analogous modulation via conventional flasks or one-pot systems is of critical importance because of the advantages in operational simplicity, scalability, and integration with existing polymerization workflows. , However, there are no reports on the construction of shape-varied MWDs in ROMP in a single-batch reactor.

Strategies for manipulating the shape of MWD profiles in ROMP.

One effective approach is to construct a system that integrates two dynamically switchable catalytic events. − The Do group achieved bimodal distributions of polyethylene by dynamic ionic interplay between Li+-nickel and Na+-nickel complexes, and the relative fraction of the peaks can be precisely controlled by adjusting the molar ratio of the two complexes. Motivated by this precedent, it is hypothesized that a dynamic ligand-regulation strategy implemented through ROMP, with the coexistence of two catalysts, may induce differentiated catalyst reactivity states, allowing the generation of two polymer populations that evolve concurrently under the same reaction conditions. It should be noted that polymerization with two catalysts does not necessarily guarantee the formation of a bimodal distribution. Without proper interaction, the ligands associated with different catalysts fail to operate jointly, leading to poorly defined distributions, which give rise either to two fully separated peaks or to merged peaks with low resolution. Previous studies have mainly focused on single-catalyst-driven ROMP systems, and this conceptual inertia has limited the understanding of catalytic behavior in more complex environments. − Therefore, deciphering the governing principles linking polymer chain growth and the resulting polymer MWD in complex catalytic environments is also crucial for advancing ROMP systems.

Herein, we report a dual-catalysis strategy enabling one-pot access to bimodal MWDs in the ROMP of norbornene (NB) by leveraging the nature of the metathesis catalysts, as demonstrated in Figure . Transformation from an originally monomodal to a bimodal distribution by combining two metathesis catalysts with distinct catalytic activities is demonstrated. Guided by established regulatory principles, metathesis polymers (i.e., polynorbornene, PNB) exhibiting diverse bimodal MWD profiles are successfully synthesized. The relative peak distance, height, and molecular weight span are modulated through the fine-tuning of catalyst composition, polymerization time, and total catalyst loading. Furthermore, the dynamic interplay between the two catalysts and their cooperative fusion behavior on the polymerization kinetics and the evolution of MWD is elucidated through combined experimental and theoretical studies, providing new mechanistic insights into dual-catalysis ROMP systems. The developed strategy offers a straightforward yet effective platform for engineering MWDs in ROMP-derived polymers. To the best of our awareness, this work represents the first demonstration of tunable bimodal MWDs via one-pot ROMP, expanding the potential dimensions of property modulation.

Results and Discussion

Proof of Concept of Dual-Catalysis Strategy

Typically, ROMP is mediated by a metal-carbene complex, which serves as an initiator to trigger the polymerization. Following initiation, the metal-carbene remains at the polymer chain end, continuously providing reactive sites for successive monomer insertion. Owing to the characteristics of the ROMP mechanism, the metal-carbene complex governs both initiation and propagation kinetics. Grubbs-type catalysts are widely used metal-carbene complexes, distinguished by the ligands coordinated to the ruthenium (Ru) center. Grubbs’ second-generation (G2) and Grubbs’ third-generation (G3) catalysts are the most prevalent catalysts in ROMP. Although the Ru centers share an identical core structure, their distinct coordination environments, induced by different ligands, lead to markedly different catalytic performance. The strongly coordinating tricyclohexylphosphine (PCy3) ligand leads to a slower initiation rate of G2 compared to that of the system mediated by G3, with the more labile 3-bromopyridine (Brpy) ligand, which dissociates more rapidly from the Ru center. G2 and G3 generate identical active intermediates upon initiation, and the dissociated ligands can randomly recoordinate with active species to form dormant complexes (Figure A). With this insight, it is hypothesized that implementing dual catalysis within a one-pot polymerization system may enable deliberate control over the MWD.

To examine this assumption, we first compared the kinetics of ROMP of NB catalyzed by G2 and G3, respectively. Norbornene was chosen as a model monomer, as it is a prototypical parent monomer in cyclic olefin polymerization. CH2Cl2 was deliberately selected as a relatively noncoordinating, weakly interacting solvent that allows both G2 and G3 catalysts to operate under well-defined and comparable conditions. The polymerizations were carried out under −20 °C to minimize undesirable side reactions. As shown in Figure B, the polymerization kinetics of G3-catalyzed ROMP exhibit a higher apparent polymerization rate (k obs = 0.45 min–1) than the G2-catalyzed system (k obs = 0.11 min–1). Meanwhile, both G2- and G3-catalyzed polymerizations of NB follow pseudo-first-order kinetics, with monomer conversions exceeding 99%. Figure C illustrates that the MWDs in both catalytic systems gradually shift toward a higher molecular weight region with increasing monomer conversion and remain monomodal throughout the polymerization, indicating uniform chain propagation without secondary initiation and secondary metathesis. Polymers obtained from G3-catalyzed polymerization exhibit narrow MWDs (Đ < 1.1), and the number-average molecular weights are in agreement with theoretical values (Table S1), whereas those produced by G2-catalyzed ROMP show comparatively broader distributions (Đ > 1.7) with higher molecular weights. The slower polymerization kinetics and broader MWDs in the G2-mediated system originate from its slow initiation and limited active sites for monomer propagation, highlighting the kinetic differences between the two catalytic systems.

Subsequently, we conducted polymerizations of NB using a mixed-catalyst system composed of G2 and G3 to investigate their potential for constructing bimodal distributions. The initial monomer concentration ([M]0) and the total catalyst concentration ([Ru]total = [G2]0 + [G3]0) were fixed, whereas the fraction of G2 in the mixed-catalyst system was varied at 25%, 50%, 75%, and 87.5%. The MWDs of the polymers obtained after 5 min of polymerization with four different [G2]0/[G3]0 ratios are compared (Figure D and Table S2). As the proportion of G2 increases, the initially monomodal distribution gradually evolves into a bimodal one, accompanied by a progressive enhancement of the broad low-molecular-weight peak, which corresponds to the polymers propagated by G2-catalyzed species. The high-molecular-weight peak remains relatively narrow and shifts toward higher molecular weight with decreasing G3 content, suggesting that this fraction corresponds to polymers generated from the G3-catalyzed propagating species. This observation provides preliminary validation of the catalyst-driven kinetic mismatch strategy, demonstrating its capability to generate tunable bimodal MWDs through a dual-catalysis system.

Manipulation of Bimodal MWD by Dual-Catalysis ROMP

Following this initial validation, we systematically investigated the effects of catalyst composition and polymerization time on the MWD to identify a regulatory principle for controlling the polymer MWD. Polymerizations were performed with initial G2 fractions of 25%, 50%, 75%, and 87.5% for identical reaction durations, and the evolution of MWDs in single-catalyst ROMP is used as a benchmark for comparison. The SEC traces are shown in Figure A. At the G2/G3 blended catalysts with G2 contents of 25%, the low-molecular-weight region exhibits a weak bimodal tendency. At a G2 content of 50%, the bimodal feature of the distribution becomes more evident, indicating the cooperative contributions of the two catalysts. As G2 content increases from 50% to 87.5%, the promotion of the contribution from the G2-derived polymer population enhances the intensity of the low-molecular-weight peak. On the other hand, both the relative distance between the two peaks and the relative peak heights vary with the polymerization time. Under G2 contents of 50–87.5%, bimodality emerges within 1 min, suggesting that the presence of G3 does not inhibit the triggering of G2-mediated polymerization, despite the slower initiation kinetics of G2 relative to those of G3. As the polymerization proceeds, the distribution remains bimodal but progressively diverges, indicating that the chain growth of the two polymer populations is driven by different kinetics. The trends identified above highlight the effects of polymerization time and the dual-catalysis composition on the coordinated control over peak spacing and intensity of the bimodal distribution, which are quantitatively described as shown in Figure B and C. The relative distance between the two peaks is determined by the difference value of the peak molecular weights in the high- and low-molecular-weight regions of the SEC distributions, which is defined as M p1 – M p2 (Figure B and Tables S7–S10). The relative peak distance becomes larger with increasing G2 content and continuously increases with prolonged polymerization time, spanning from 23.8 kg/mol (50% G2, 1 min) to 170 kg/mol (87.5% G2, 9 min). The relative peak intensity is calculated by the ratio of the low- to high-molecular-weight peak heights (H p2/H p1) (Figure C). The relative peak height ratio decreases with prolonged polymerization time, whereas increasing the G2 content leads to the promotion of this ratio. Taking an H p2/H p1 value of 0.37 as a reference, this value is attained at 1 min for 50% G2 but is progressively delayed to 5 and 9 min as the G2 content increases to 75% and 87.5%, respectively.

Such a methodology not only enables to produce polymers with bimodal distributions of different peak heights in one pot but also allows for the widening of the peak separation without altering the relative peak-height ratio. For example, two MWDs having identical relative peak heights (50% G2, 1 min vs 75% G2, 5 min) exhibit different peak separations, with one pair of peaks separated by 23.8 kg/mol and the other by 68.8 kg/mol (Tables S8 and S9), respectively. Therefore, although the individual G2- and G3-catalyzed ROMP exhibit markedly different kinetic behaviors, their combination acts cooperatively to mediate a bimodal polymerization behavior and results in a single bimodal rather than two discrete monomodal distributions, even though the theoretical polymerization degrees of the individual components differ substantially.

Beyond validating the effectiveness of this strategy in regulating bimodal distributions, the effects of different distribution shapes on polymer properties were also discussed. The polynorbornene samples with monomodal and bimodal molecular weight distribution shapes were synthesized. As shown in Figure S24, the SEC curves show that they basically overlap in the high molecular weight region, which avoids the potential influence caused by differences in molecular weights. The small-amplitude oscillatory shear (SAOS) rheological measurements on these two PNB samples were conducted, including frequency sweep tests to obtain the storage modulus (G′), loss modulus (G″), loss tangent (tan δ), and complex viscosity (η*) over an angular frequency range of 0.1–100 rad/s at 25 and 40 °C (Figure S25). Across the measured frequency range, the bimodal sample consistently exhibited lower G′ and η* than the monomodal sample, with the difference being most pronounced at low frequency. For example, at 0.1 rad/s and 25 °C, G′ and η* of the bimodal sample were reduced by approximately 52% and 50%, respectively. The results suggest that the added low-molecular-weight population in the bimodal sample broadens the relaxation behavior and promotes viscous dissipation, while reducing the overall elastic strength and flow resistance of the material. The enhanced processing performance of the bimodal metathesis polymer further highlights the promising potential of the MWD regulation strategy for tailoring material properties, and enables the establishment of structure–property relationships in polymeric systems through precise MWD engineering. The detailed results and corresponding discussion are provided in Section 7 of Supporting Information.

Ligand Exchange in Dual-Catalyst System

During the design of the dual-catalyst system, we observed that catalyst solutions obtained by stoichiometric mixing experience an equilibration process prior to polymerization, which is essential for achieving reproducible distribution regulation. It has been demonstrated that the G3 catalyst releases an equivalent quantity of free 3-bromopyridine (Brpy) ligand in solution. , Therefore, we first probed the effect of free Brpy ligands on the G2 catalyst prior to polymerization. 1H NMR was employed to track the chemical shifts of the coordination environment around the Ru centers (Figures A and Figure S22). The resonance observed at δ = 19.13 ppm represents the Ru benzylidene peak of G2. Upon adding the Brpy ligand, a new resonance at δ = 19.09 ppm appears, which corresponds to the Ru benzylidene peak of in situ generated G3. At low Brpy loadings, two distinct resonances are detected, indicative of slow exchange between PCy3 and Brpy ligands. Upon the addition of 8 equivalents, they exhibit an incipient coalescence, indicating that the ligand exchange becomes fast on the NMR time scale. Increasing the amount of Brpy leads to a higher proportion of G3 generated in situ, and the composition of the species in the dual-catalysis system is displayed in Figure B. Once the relative proportions of species become constant, the ligand exchange reaches equilibrium. Polymerization Kinetic Clues for Bimodal MWD Formation

We next focus on the interactions in dual-catalyst systems that occur during the polymerization stage. The bimodal distributions illustrated in Figure imply that the two catalysts are kinetically coupled rather than operating independently, leading to an integrated polymerization process. Such a coupling is assumed to arise from their complementary functions in governing initiation and propagation kinetics throughout the polymerization process. To elucidate the factors governing chain growth kinetics and their impact on the molecular weight distribution, this section determines the apparent reaction orders with respect to both the catalyst and free ligand concentrations (Figure ). It should be noted that norbornene is explicitly defined as the model system in this study. Accordingly, we continued to employ this monomer, along with its corresponding temperature conditions, to investigate the underlying mechanisms and kinetic regulation principles.

As displayed in Figure A, the ln–ln plot of k obs versus [G2]0 reveals that the polymerization rate follows a 2.15-order dependence on [G2]0 at −20 °C, arising from the combined effects of the activation/deactivation equilibrium between free PCy3 and active propagating Ru species, as well as diffusion limitations. Besides, the monomer consumption exhibits pseudo-first-order kinetics under different [G2]0, suggesting a fixed number of initiated polymeric chains throughout the polymerization. A detailed discussion of the unique kinetic order of G2-catalyzed ROMP is provided in Section 5 of Supporting Information. In contrast, under the same temperature and in the absence of an additional ligand, the G3-catalyzed polymerization exhibits a zero-order dependence on [G3]0 and the number of active sites remains constant under different [G3]0. , It should be noted that this apparent zero-order behavior arises from the combination of the first-order dependence on the G3 catalyst concentration and the negative-first-order dependence on the free Brpy ligand concentration, leading to an overall zero-order rate in the isolated G3-catalyzed system. These results suggest that variations in G2 concentration exert a much stronger influence on the polymerization rate than equivalent changes in G3.

Fusion-Catalysis Mechanism of Dual-Catalyst-Driven ROMP

To further elucidate the polymerization pathway involved in the dual-catalysis strategy, DFT calculations were carried out. Figure shows the calculated energy profiles for the polymerization processes involved in the two catalysts. For the G3 catalyst, the mono-Brpy-coordinated Ru species (A3) is calculated to be more stable (−3.47 kcal/mol) than the bi-Brpy-coordinated complex (0.96 kcal/mol), in agreement with experimental results indicating that G3 exists predominantly as a monopyridine complex in solution. , The higher dissociation barrier of PCy3 (−10.04 kcal/mol versus −3.47 kcal/mol for Brpy) causes it to dissociate more slowly during initiation, resulting in a broader polymer distribution for the G2-derived fraction. The difference in primary ligand dissociation energy between the two catalysts indicates that two kinetically differentiated propagating species are generated at the initiation stage. The transition state formed by metal cyclobutane (TS) was calculated to have the highest energy (9.18 kcal/mol), which is consistent with previous research results. Upon insertion of one monomer, the resulting 14-electron intermediate oligomer (E) exhibits an energy of −6.16 kcal/mol, which can potentially react with one monomer to proceed with chain growth (E-F3) or alternatively with either of the two ligands, leading to distinct kinetic pathways. The energy difference from E to F1 (−3.02 kcal/mol) is close to that from E to F2 (−3.44 kcal/mol), indicating that the two pathways are kinetically competitive and can coexist during the polymerization process, rather than one ligand-dominated pathway.

Free energy profile of the species involved in dual-catalysis ROMP of norbornene.

On the basis of the experimental and computational results, we reveal a fusion-catalytic process for ROMP (Scheme ). Once mixed with the monomer, G2 and G3 act as independent initiators and undergo primary ligand dissociation to trigger the polymerization without mutual interference. Upon initiation, G2 and G3 converge to the same 14-electron active intermediate for monomer insertion, leading to the preliminary emergence of a cocatalyst ensemble. During chain propagation, the identical active species share the same propagation mechanism. Polymer chains originally initiated by G2 may subsequently turn into a G3-mediated propagation pathway through dynamic ligand exchange in a common active metal-carbene pool, and vice versa. The continuous region bridging the two peaks reflects the coexistence of stalled G3-initiated chains caused by PCy3 association and accelerated G2-initiated chains caused by Brpy association. This interplay between the two ligand-associated species underlies their mutual influence on the apparent rate as the catalyst ratio varies. Consequently, the chain growth of the polymer chains shuttles between G2- and G3-mediated kinetics, highlighting a fusion-catalytic effect, which ultimately gives rise to the formation of tunable MWDs.

Proposed Mechanism of Fusion Catalysis-ROMP of Norbornene Mediated by the Mixtures of G2 and G3.

It should be stressed that the underlying mechanism of the as-developed strategy is distinct from the previous studies based on the external ligand competition strategy, in which the amount of added external ligand typically exceeds that of the catalyst, establishing an external-ligand-dominated coordination environment. − Those approaches enable the modulation of metathesis reactivity, suppress secondary metathesis, and prolong the lifetime of active propagating species, thereby enhancing polymerization control and affording polymers with narrow molecular weight distributions. Differently, the strategy in this work is designed to break the intrinsic kinetic characteristics of a single-catalysis ROMP system, involving fusion catalysis to regulate the kinetics throughout the entire polymerization process.

Predictive Framework for Regulating Bimodal MWD

Having achieved an explicit mechanistic understanding through experimental and computational investigations, we next leverage these insights to formulate a practical framework that is applied to guide MWD regulation in the dual-catalysis strategy. Each step of the process is detailed in the left panel of Figure A. At a fixed total Ru concentration, stoichiometric mixing of the two catalysts is performed (step 1), followed by the calculation of the effective equilibrium concentrations of the relevant species prior to polymerization using eq (step 2). The R p (G2)/R p (G3) can be determined by eq to predict the polymerization behavior (step 3), which subsequently guides polymerization experiments and the evaluation of the resulting distribution characteristics (step 4).

Two representative catalyst ratios (25% and 50% of G2) were chosen based on the observation of Figure B to examine how kinetic differences vary with the total Ru concentration. The 25% of G2 was selected because it represents a condition under which a bimodal distribution cannot be achieved at a low total Ru concentration of 1.4 mM. The 50% ratio of G2 was chosen because bimodal distributions emerge when the G2 content reaches or exceeds 50%. The corresponding R p (G2)/R p (G3) values vary with the [Ru]total and are shown in the right panel of Figure A. The R p (G2)/R p (G3) values increase monotonically with increasing [Ru]total for both ratios. The R p (G2)/R p (G3) under 50% G2 conditions consistently shows a higher value than the corresponding 25% G2 condition at the same [Ru]total.

To validate the impact of kinetic differences on bimodal distribution regulation, [Ru]total values of 1.4, 2.0, and 3.0 mM were selected for comparison, as indicated by the circled data points in the right panel of Figure A and Table S13. When the G2 fraction is 25%, R p (G2)/R p (G3) is 0.10 at 2 mM and increases to 0.53 at 3 mM. When the G2 fraction was increased to 50%, the corresponding ratios at 2 mM and 3 mM were 0.21 and 1.11, respectively. Guided by these results, the corresponding polymerizations were performed. The resulting SEC traces obtained under the above conditions are shown in Figure B, and their characteristics are illustrated in Figure C. At a G2 fraction of 25%, the bimodal feature becomes pronounced with total Ru concentrations of 2 mM and 3 mM. The distance between the two peaks (M p1-M p2) is reduced from 13.5 kg/mol to 8.2 kg/mol (Tables S14 and S15), while the relative height differences H p2/H p1 increase to 0.18 and 0.44, respectively. These results indicate that bimodal distribution regulation can be achieved at low G2 fractions by increasing the [Ru]total. A higher [Ru]total leads to an effective balance of kinetic differences, enabling the construction of a bimodal distribution with a tunable molecular weight span covered by the distribution. At a fixed G2 fraction of 50%, increasing [Ru]total from 2 mM to 3 mM enhances the relative peak heights H p2/H p1 from 0.43 to 0.59 and reduces M p1-M p2 from 22.8 kg/mol to 15.5 kg/mol, leading to a corresponding change in the molecular weight span of the distributions simultaneously.

Conclusions

This work introduces a brand-new distribution-oriented catalytic system design in the one-pot ROMP of norbornene, where a robust dual-catalysis strategy constructed from a blended G2/G3 catalyst enables the formation of polymers with highly resolved bimodal MWDs. A manipulation principle for controlling the distribution profiles was established, in which the relative position and intensity of the two peaks in an SEC trace were governed by polymerization time and catalyst composition. Increasing the G2 content enhances the relative peak intensity, shifting it from 0.37 to unity, and prolonging polymerization time further expands the distribution spacing from a narrow gap of 23.8 kg/mol to a wide gap of 170 kg/mol. Interestingly, comparative rheological studies between unimodal and bimodal samples show that the bimodal PNB exhibits markedly reduced storage modulus G’ and complex viscosity η* compared to its counterpart, indicating that the added low-molecular-weight population in the bimodal sample promotes viscous dissipation, while reducing the overall elastic strength and flow resistance of the material. Furthermore, an equation determining the relative apparent propagation rate of G2- to G3-catalyzed polymer chains was derived to quantify the dual-catalysis-controlled kinetics. Bimodal distributions with tunable molecular weight spans were successfully achieved by changing total catalyst loading as guided by the prediction of the derived equation, confirming the utility and robustness of the as-developed strategy. A fusion-catalysis mechanism behind the dual-catalysis-controlled kinetics was revealed, in which polymer chains initiated by the two Grubbs-type catalysts converge into an identical active intermediate, allowing chains with different lengths to shuttle between two ligand association states and dynamically switch their relative propagation rates throughout polymerization, leading to harmony in the formation of bimodal MWDs.

Overall, this contribution demonstrates the feasibility of the dual-catalyst strategy by ROMP and unravels the underlying mechanism for controlling metathesis polymer chain growth behavior to shape bimodal distributions, expanding the diversity of accessible polymer distribution profiles. It, however, should be pointed out that norbornene was used as a model system in this work, and the associated kinetic features may not be simply generalized to other monomer types. Future work will focus on extending this strategy to other strained cyclic monomers to systematically evaluate its broader applicability and its potential to enhance the performance and functional versatility of cyclic olefin polymer materials.
