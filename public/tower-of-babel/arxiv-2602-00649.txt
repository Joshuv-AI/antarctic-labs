Linear Magnetohydrodynamic Waves in a Magneto-Lattice: A Unified Theoretical Framework and Numerical Validation
Shiyu Sun, Peifeng Fan, Yulei Wang, Qiang Chen, Xingkai Li, Weihua Wang

Abstract
We present a systematic theoretical and numerical investigation of the propagation properties of linear magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) waves in a spatially periodic magnetic field, referred to as a magneto-lattice. Two types of central equations, expressed in terms of ( ρ , 𝑩 , 𝒗 ) \left(\rho,\bm{B},\bm{v}\right) (where ρ \rho is perturbed mass density, 𝑩 \bm{B} is perturbed magnetic field, and 𝒗 \bm{v} is perturbed velocity) and the perturbation displacement 𝝃 \bm{\xi} , are established using the plane wave expansion (PWE) method. The validity of both equations is demonstrated through two numerical examples. This framework enables the identification of intrinsic frequency bandgaps and cutoff phenomena within the system. Our numerical results show that the bandgap width increases with the magnetic modulation ratio B m B_{m} , leading to the suppression of specific MHD wave modes. Furthermore, the periodicity of the magnetic field induces the splitting of Alfvén waves into multiple branches—a phenomenon absent in uniform plasmas. These findings provide new insights for manipulating MHD waves in a crystalline lattice framework of structured plasmas.

INTRODUCTION

The control of wave propagation using spatially

and materials science

;

;

)

In particular, periodic mesoscale structures—such as photonic

;

.
Phononic crystals have extended this idea to the field of elastic

to achieve suppression and mode selection of acoustic/elastic wave
propagation
(
Badreddine Assouar and Oudich 2011
;
Hussein and Leamy 2014
;
Ash et al. 2017
;

)
.
They have shown great potential in fields such as vibration reduction

, noise reduction
(

;

;

;

;
Zaky et al. 2025
)
,

(

;

;
Qiu et al. 2005
;
Ma et al. 2022
;
Beoletto et al. 2024
)
.
These studies collectively reveal a universal law: spatial periodicity
reshapes wave dispersion relations, and the emergence of band gaps
enables novel strategies for active wave modulation.

Magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) investigates the macroscopic
behavior of electrically conducting fluids in magnetic fields and
is widely applied in space physics
(
Zhou et al. 2004
)
, controlled
nuclear fusion
(
Freidberg 1982
;
Freidberg 2014
;

)
,

;

)
.
As the fundamental disturbance modes in magnetized plasmas, MHD waves
primarily include fast waves (FWs), slow waves (SWs), and Alfvén
waves (AWs). Advances in the understanding of periodic structures
have revealed that wave propagation can be profoundly modified by
spatial periodicity. In particular, studies of photonic and phononic
crystals have shown that periodic structures can give rise to characteristic
band gaps (i.e., frequency gaps), within which wave propagation is
forbidden. Motivated by this concept, this study explores the propagation
behavior of MHD waves in spatially periodic magnetic field structures,
referred to as magneto-lattices. This magneto-lattice configuration
can be realized either by periodically arranging permanent magnets
or by periodically arranging externally energized coils. For instance,
Fig.
1
(d) illustrates a 2D
magneto-lattice formed by a spatially periodic arrangement of magnetic
dipoles, analogous to an atomic crystal lattice and conceptually resembling
a magnetic crystal.

Figure 1:
Schematic illustration
of periodic lattice structures in different physical systems. (a)
Atomic crystal lattice in condensed matter physics. (b) Periodic elastic

In this study, we establish a systematic theoretical framework for
studying the propagation of linear MHD waves in magneto-lattice. Starting
from the ideal MHD equations, we employ a perturbative approach to
derive the equilibrium configuration and the corresponding linearized
MHD equations. The magneto-lattice induced equilibrium provides a
stable background for MHD wave evolution, analogous to the controlled
environments realized in laboratory photonic and phononic crystals.
Starting from the linearized MHD equations and applying Bloch’s
theorem together with the plane wave expansion (PWE) method
(
Hsue et al. 2005
;
Poggetto and Serpa 2020
)
,
we systematically derive the central equations in two equivalent representations:
one expressed in terms of the perturbed physical fields
\left(\rho,\bm{B},\bm{v}\right)

(perturbed mass density, magnetic field, and velocity), and the other
formulated in terms of the perturbed displacement field
\bm{\xi}
.
To validate the two models, we consider a magnetic field that is uniform
in direction (along the
y
-axis) and sinusoidally modulated in magnitude
along the
x
-direction. The normalized magnetic field is given by

\bm{B}_{0}\left(x\right)=\left[1+B_{m}\mathrm{sin}\left(x\right)\right]\bm{e}_{y}
.
Two values of the modulation amplitude are considered:
B_{m}=0

corresponding to the empty-lattice (uniform-field) case, and
B_{m}=0.1
.
The results show excellent agreement between the two theoretical formulations.
In addition, full MHD simulations are performed using the Athena++
code
(
Stone et al. 2020
;
Athena++ development team 2021
)
, with spectral properties extracted
via fast Fourier transform (FFT). The simulation results closely match
the band-gap locations and widths predicted by the central equations,
confirming the effectiveness of periodic magnetic fields in generating
band-gaps. Moreover, the numerical results show that periodic magnetic
fields can produce substantial frequency gaps, with the band-gap width
increasing as the modulation amplitude
B_{m}
grows. Notably, Alfvén
waves split into multiple branches in a periodic magnetic field—a
phenomenon absent in uniform plasma.

Section
V

II
MHD equilibrium and linearized equations

In electronic materials, crystals are formed by the periodic arrangement
of atoms or molecules, which in most cases remains stable and provides
a foundational environment for electron motion. Analogously, in MHD
systems, the evolution of waves also requires a stable configuration,
termed MHD equilibrium. Perturbations propagating atop this equilibrium
constitute MHD waves. By extending the concept of a crystal lattice
to MHD systems, we aim to establish a spatially periodic equilibrium
configuration within a magneto-lattice. Just as electron behavior
is described by matter waves or wavefunctions in crystalline structures,
MHD waves serve as their counterparts in magnetofluids. Thus, studying
MHD waves fundamentally relies on first constructing such equilibrium
states, mirroring how investigating "electron waves"
necessitates a preexisting crystalline lattice framework.

We begin with the conservative ideal MHD equations
(
Visconti and Ruggieri 2020
;
Freidberg 2014
)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}=-\nabla\cdot\left(\rho\bm{v}\right),

(1)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\bm{B}}{\partial t}=-\nabla\cdot\left(\bm{v}\bm{B}-\bm{B}\bm{v}\right),

(2)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\rho\bm{v}\right)=-\nabla\cdot\left[\rho\bm{v}\bm{v}+\left(P+\frac{\bm{B}^{2}}{8\pi}\right)\bm{I}-\frac{\bm{B}\bm{B}}{4\pi}\right],

(3)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{P}{\rho^{\gamma}}\right)=0,

(4)

where
\bm{B}
is the magnetic field,
\gamma
is the adiabatic
index, and
\rho
,
P
and
\bm{v}
represent the mass
density, thermal pressure, and velocity of the magnetofluid, respectively.
Let each physical field be generically denoted as
Q
, where
Q

represents quantities such as
\rho
,
P
,
\bm{v}
, and
others. For a magnetofluid with perturbed fluctuations, each field

Q
can be decomposed into a stationary equilibrium component
Q_{0}

and a perturbed component
Q_{1}
, i.e.,
Q=Q_{0}+Q_{1}
. Since
the equilibrium state is time-independent and lacks background flow,
(i.e.,
\partial Q_{0}/\partial t=0
and
\bm{v}_{0}=0
),
the MHD equilibrium equation reduces to

\nabla\cdot\left[\left(P_{0}+\frac{\bm{B}_{0}^{2}}{8\pi}\right)\bm{I}-\frac{\bm{B}_{0}\bm{B}_{0}}{4\pi}\right]=0,

(5)

which is equivalent to

\nabla P_{0}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\left(\nabla\times\bm{B}_{0}\right)\times\bm{B}_{0}.

(6)

Substituting
Q=Q_{0}+Q_{1}
into Eqs. (
1
)-(
4
),
neglecting higher-order terms, and simplifying the resulting expressions
yields the linearized MHD equations governing the perturbations

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\rho_{1}}{\partial t}=-\nabla\cdot\left(\rho_{0}\bm{v}_{1}\right),

(7)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\bm{B}_{1}}{\partial t}=-\nabla\cdot\left(\bm{v}_{1}\bm{B}_{0}-\bm{B}_{0}\bm{v}_{1}\right),

(8)

\displaystyle\rho_{0}\frac{\partial\bm{v}_{1}}{\partial t}=-\nabla\cdot\left[\left(C_{s}^{2}\rho_{1}+\frac{\bm{B}_{0}\cdot\bm{B}_{1}}{4\pi}\right)\bm{I}-\frac{\bm{B}_{0}\bm{B}_{1}+\bm{B}_{1}\bm{B}_{0}}{4\pi}\right],

(9)

where
C_{s}^{2}=\gamma P_{0}/\rho_{0}
is the sound speed squared.

The linearized MHD equations formulated in terms of the perturbed
variables
\left(\rho_{1},\bm{B}_{1},\bm{v}_{1}\right)

involve seven dynamical variables and constitute a system of seven
coupled first-order partial differential equations. Alternatively,
by introducing the perturbation displacement field
\bm{\xi}\left(t,\bm{x}\right)
,
which satisfies
\bm{v}_{1}=\partial\bm{\xi}/\partial t
,
the system can be reformulated as three coupled second-order partial
differential equations for the components of
\bm{\xi}
.
In this formulation, the linearized MHD equations reduce to a unified
governing equation,

\displaystyle\rho_{0}\frac{\partial^{2}\bm{\xi}}{\partial t^{2}}=\nabla\cdot\left\{\left[\left(\gamma P_{0}\nabla\cdot\bm{\xi}+\bm{\xi}\cdot\nabla P_{0}\right)-\frac{\bm{B}_{0}}{4\pi}\cdot\left[\nabla\times\left(\bm{\xi}\times\bm{B}_{0}\right)\right]\right]\bm{I}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\vphantom{\frac{\bm{B}_{0}\cdot\left[\right]}{4\pi}}+\frac{\bm{B}_{0}\left[\nabla\times\left(\bm{\xi}\times\bm{B}_{0}\right)\right]+\left[\nabla\times\left(\bm{\xi}\times\bm{B}_{0}\right)\right]\bm{B}_{0}}{4\pi}\right\},

(10)

where the perturbed fields
\rho_{1}
,
P_{1}
and
\bm{B}_{1}

are expressed in terms of
\bm{\xi}
as

\displaystyle\rho_{1}=-\nabla\cdot\left(\rho_{0}\bm{\xi}\right),

(11)

\displaystyle P_{1}=-\gamma P_{0}\nabla\cdot\bm{\xi}-\bm{\xi}\cdot\nabla P_{0},

(12)

\displaystyle\bm{B}_{1}=\nabla\times\left(\bm{\xi}\times\bm{B}_{0}\right).

(13)

Such a displacement-based formulation is widely employed in investigations
of MHD instabilities. This displacement-based formulation provides
an alternative yet fully equivalent description of linear magnetohydrodynamics
and is mathematically equivalent to the original seven-variable first-order
system. Accordingly, either Eq. (
7
)-(
9
)
or equation (
10
) may be employed to determine
the linear perturbations of MHD waves.

Suppose the equilibrium magnetic field
\bm{B}_{0}
consists
of a uniform background field
\bm{B}_{0b}
and a spatially
periodic magneto-lattice component
\bm{B}_{0L}
, so that

\bm{B}_{0}\left(\bm{x}\right)=B_{0b}\bm{e}_{y}+\bm{B}_{0L}\left(\bm{x}\right),

(14)

The magneto-lattice field
\bm{B}_{0L}
satisfies the periodicity
condition
\bm{B}_{0L}\left(\bm{x}+\bm{R}_{n}\right)=\bm{B}_{0L}\left(\bm{x}\right)
,
where
\bm{R}_{n}
denotes the lattice vectors. In the absence
of
\bm{B}_{0L}
, the equilibrium density and pressure are
uniform constants,
\rho_{0b}
and
P_{0b}
, respectively. After
introducing the magneto-lattice field, the system relaxes to a new
equilibrium characterized by spatially periodic mass density
\rho_{0L}

and pressure
P_{0L}
which satisfy the equilibrium condition in
(
5
). This establishes a periodic equilibrium.
We further assume that the relaxation is isothermal,

\frac{P_{0b}}{\rho_{0b}}=\frac{P_{0}\left(\bm{x}\right)}{\rho_{0}\left(\bm{x}\right)},

(15)

so that the sound speed remains uniform throughout the system, i.e.,

C_{s}^{2}=\gamma P_{0b}/\rho_{0b}\equiv C_{sb}^{2}
, where
C_{sb}

is the constant background sound speed.

III
central equations

In condensed matter physics, the central equation plays a fundamental
role in the analysis of wave phenomena in periodic media
(
Kittel 1979
)
.
By exploiting the discrete translational symmetry of a crystal lattice
and combining Bloch’s theorem with a PWE, the governing
equations can be recast into an eigenvalue problem that explicitly
couples different reciprocal lattice vectors. This formulation provides
a unified and systematic framework for determining band structures,
identifying band gaps, and understanding mode hybridization induced
by periodicity. Owing to its generality and transparency, the central
equation has become a cornerstone in the theoretical treatment of
electrons, phonons, and photonic excitations in crystalline solids.

Motivated by its success in condensed matter systems, we extend this
formalism to magnetohydrodynamics in periodic magnetic equilibria.
In this section, we derive the central equations for linearized MHD
waves in a magneto-lattice, thereby establishing a direct analogy
between wave propagation in periodic magnetofluids and that in conventional
crystalline materials.

Before deriving the central equations, we nondimensionalize the linearized
magnetohydrodynamic equations to obtain a unified, dimensionless formulation.
All physical quantities are normalized by the uniform background density

\rho_{0b}
and magnetic field
B_{0b}
, and the nondimensional
variables are defined as

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}_{1}=\frac{\rho_{1}}{\rho_{0b}},\thinspace\tilde{\rho}_{0L}=\frac{\rho_{0L}}{\rho_{0b}},\thinspace\tilde{\rho}_{0}=\frac{\rho_{0}}{\rho_{0b}}

(16)

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{B}}_{1}=\frac{\bm{B}_{1}}{\left|B_{0b}\right|},\thinspace\tilde{\bm{B}}_{0L}=\frac{\bm{B}_{0L}}{\left|B_{0b}\right|},\thinspace\tilde{\bm{B}}_{0}=\frac{\bm{B}_{0}}{\left|B_{0b}\right|}=\sigma\bm{e}_{z}+\tilde{\bm{B}}_{0L},

(17)

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{v}}_{1}=\frac{\bm{v}_{1}}{V_{Ab}},

(18)

where
V_{Ab}=\sqrt{B_{0b}^{2}/4\pi\rho_{0b}}
is the Alfvén
speed and
\sigma=B_{0b}/\left|B_{0b}\right|=\pm 1
. Let

s_{L}=\sqrt[D]{V_{\mathrm{cell}}}/2\pi
denote the characteristic
length of a unit cell, where
D
is the dimension of the lattice
and
V_{\mathrm{cell}}
is the volume of the unit cell. Using
s_{L}

and
V_{Ab}
, the characteristic time is given by
t_{0}=s_{L}/V_{Ab}
,
which is referred to as the Alfvén time. The space and time coordinates
are nondimensionalized as

\tilde{\bm{x}}=\frac{\bm{x}}{s_{L}},\quad\tilde{t}=\frac{t}{t_{0}},

(19)

with the corresponding nondimensional derivatives given by

\tilde{\bm{\nabla}}=s_{L}\bm{\nabla},\quad\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{t}}=t_{0}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}.

(20)

Substituting the nondimensional variables from Eqs. (
16
)
and (
20
) into Eqs. (
7
)
and (
9
) and omitting tildes “~”
and perturbation subscripts “1” for simplicity, yields the nondimensionalized
equations

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}=-\nabla\cdot\left(\rho_{0}\bm{v}\right),

(21)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\bm{B}}{\partial t}=-\nabla\cdot\left(\bm{v}\bm{B}_{0}-\bm{B}_{0}\bm{v}\right),

(22)

\displaystyle\rho_{0}\frac{\partial\bm{v}}{\partial t}=-\nabla\cdot\left[\left(\beta\rho+\bm{B}_{0}\cdot\bm{B}\right)\bm{I}-\left(\bm{B}_{0}\bm{B}_{1}+\bm{B}\bm{B}_{0}\right)\right],

(23)

where
\beta=C_{s}^{2}/V_{Ab}^{2}
and
\bm{I}
is the identity
tensor. The equilibrium condition become

\nabla P_{0}=\frac{\gamma}{\beta}\left(\nabla\times\bm{B}_{0}\right)\times\bm{B}_{0}.

(24)

Introducing the nondimensional displacement
\tilde{\bm{\xi}}=\bm{\xi}/s_{L}

and applying the normalization schemes from Eqs. (
19
)
and (
20
) into Eq. (
10
),
while omitting tildes for brevity, results in

\displaystyle\rho_{0}\frac{\partial^{2}\bm{\xi}}{\partial t^{2}}=\nabla\cdot\left\{\left[\beta\left(\rho_{0}\nabla\cdot\bm{\xi}+\frac{1}{\gamma}\bm{\xi}\cdot\nabla\rho_{0}\right)-\bm{B}_{0}\cdot\nabla\times\left(\bm{\xi}\times\bm{B}_{0}\right)\right]\bm{I}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\vphantom{\frac{\bm{B}_{0}\left[\right]}{4\pi}}+\bm{B}_{0}\nabla\times\left(\bm{\xi}\times\bm{B}_{0}\right)+\nabla\times\left(\bm{\xi}\times\bm{B}_{0}\right)\bm{B}_{0}\right\}.

(25)

III.1
Central equation in terms of
\left(\rho,\bm{B},\bm{v}\right)

We next using the PWE methond to derive the central equation in terms
of
\left(\rho,\bm{B},\bm{v}\right)
. Let
\bm{\psi}\left(t,\bm{x}\right)=\left(\rho,\bm{B},\bm{v}\right)
.
Assuming time-harmonic solutions, we write
\bm{\psi}\left(t,\bm{x}\right)=\bm{\psi}\left(\bm{x}\right)e^{-i\omega t}
,
where
\omega
is the dimensionless frequency (normalized by
t_{0}
,
i.e.,
\omega\rightarrow\omega t_{0}
). Substituting this ansatz
into (
21
)-(
23
) yields
the corresponding eigenvalue equations

\displaystyle-i\omega\rho=-\nabla\cdot\left(\rho_{0}\bm{v}\right),

(26)

\displaystyle-i\omega\bm{B}=-\nabla\cdot\left(\bm{v}\bm{B}_{0}-\bm{B}_{0}\bm{v}\right),

(27)

\displaystyle-i\omega\rho_{0}\bm{v}=-\nabla\cdot\left[\left(\beta\rho+\bm{B}_{0}\cdot\bm{B}\right)\bm{I}-\left(\bm{B}_{0}\bm{B}_{1}+\bm{B}_{1}\bm{B}_{0}\right)\right].

(28)

The equilibrium fields
\left(\rho_{0},\bm{B}_{0}\right)
,
which are spatially periodic with respect to the normalized lattice
vectors
\bm{R}_{n}
(made dimensionless via
\bm{R}_{n}\rightarrow\bm{R}_{n}/s_{L}
),
can be expanded in a Fourier series as

\left(\rho_{0},\bm{B}_{0}\right)=\sum_{\bm{G}}\left(\rho_{0\bm{G}},\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}}\right)e^{i\bm{G}\cdot\bm{x}},

(29)

where
\bm{G}
denotes reciprocal lattice vectors.
The corresponding Fourier coefficients are given by

\displaystyle\left(\rho_{0\bm{G}},\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}}\right)=\frac{1}{V_{\mathrm{cell}}}\int_{V_{\mathrm{cell}}}\left(\rho_{0},\bm{B}_{0}\right)e^{-i\bm{G}\cdot\bm{x}}d\bm{x}.

(30)

Here,
V_{\mathrm{cell}}
denotes the dimensionless unit-cell volume
normalized by
s_{L}^{3}
, and thus satisfies
V_{\mathrm{cell}}=\left(2\pi\right)^{3}
.
Assuming the perturbed fields
\bm{\psi}
satisfy periodic
boundary conditions throughout the magnetofluid, they can be expanded
in a Fourier series of the form:

\bm{\psi}\left(\bm{x}\right)=\sum_{\bm{k}}\bm{\psi}_{\bm{k}}e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x}},

(31)

where the expansion coefficients
\bm{\psi}_{\bm{k}}

are given by

\displaystyle\bm{\psi}_{\bm{k}}=\frac{1}{V_{\mathrm{cry}}}\int_{V_{\mathrm{cry}}}\bm{\psi}\left(\bm{x}\right)e^{-i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x}}d\bm{x}.

(32)

Here,
V_{\mathrm{cry}}
represents the dimensionless volume of the
whole magnetofluid (normalized by
\left(s_{L}/2\pi\right)^{3}
),
and
\bm{k}
is the discrete wave vector quantized by the
periodic boundary conditions.

Substituting Eqs.(
29
)-Eq.(
32
)
into Eqs.(
26
)-(
28
),
the continuity equation Eq. (
26
) becomes

\sum_{\bm{k}}\omega\rho_{\bm{k}}e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x}}=\sum_{\bm{k}}\sum_{\bm{G}}\rho_{0\bm{G}}\left(\bm{k}+\bm{G}\right)\cdot\bm{v}_{\bm{k}}e^{i\left(\bm{k}+\bm{G}\right)\cdot\bm{x}}=\sum_{\bm{k}}\sum_{\bm{G}}\rho_{0\bm{G}}\bm{k}\cdot\bm{v}_{\bm{k}-\bm{G}}e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x}},

(33)

where we have redefined the summation index
\bm{k}\rightarrow\bm{k}-\bm{G}

for each
\bm{G}
in the last step. This is permitted because
the sum runs over all wave vectors in the Brillouin zone. Similarly,
equation (
27
) transforms into

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{k}}\omega\bm{B}_{\bm{k}}e^{i\bm{k}\cdot x}=\sum_{\bm{k}}\sum_{\bm{G}}\left(\bm{k}+\bm{G}\right)\cdot\left(\bm{v}_{\bm{k}}\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}}-\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}}\bm{v}_{\bm{k}}\right)e^{i\left(\bm{k}+\bm{G}\right)\cdot\bm{x}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{k}}\sum_{\bm{G}}\bm{k}\cdot\left(\bm{v}_{\bm{k}-\bm{G}}\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}}-\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}}\bm{v}_{\bm{k}-\bm{G}}\right)e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x}}.

(34)

By manipulating the left-hand side (LHS) of the momentum equation
(
28
) into

\sum_{\bm{k}}\sum_{\bm{G}}\omega\rho_{0\bm{G}}\bm{v}_{\bm{k}}e^{i\left(\bm{k}+\bm{G}\right)\cdot\bm{x}}=\sum_{\bm{k}}\sum_{\bm{G}}\omega\rho_{0\bm{G}}\bm{v}_{\bm{k}-\bm{G}}e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x}},

(35)

equation (
28
) is then transformed into

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{k}}\sum_{\bm{G}}\omega\rho_{0\bm{G}}\bm{v}_{\bm{k}-\bm{G}}e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x}}=\sum_{\bm{k}}\sum_{\bm{G}}\left[\beta\bm{k}\rho_{\bm{k}}\delta_{0\bm{G}}+\left(\bm{k}+\bm{G}\right)\left(\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}}\cdot\bm{B}_{\bm{k}}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.\vphantom{e^{i\left(\bm{k}+\bm{G}\right)\cdot\bm{x}}}-\left(\bm{k}+\bm{G}\right)\cdot\left(\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}}\bm{B}_{\bm{k}}+\bm{B}_{\bm{k}}\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}}\right)\right]e^{i\left(\bm{k}+\bm{G}\right)\cdot\bm{x}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{k}}\sum_{\bm{G}}\left[\beta\rho_{\bm{k}-\bm{G}}\delta_{0\bm{G}}\bm{k}+\bm{k}\left(\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}}\cdot\bm{B}_{\bm{k}-\bm{G}}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.\vphantom{e^{i\left(\bm{k}+\bm{G}\right)\cdot\bm{x}}}-\bm{k}\cdot\left(\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}}\bm{B}_{\bm{k}-\bm{G}}+\bm{B}_{\bm{k}-\bm{G}}\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}}\right)\right]e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x}},

(36)

where

\delta_{0\bm{G}}=\begin{cases}1&\bm{G}=0,\\
0&\bm{G}\neq 0.\end{cases}

(37)

By invoking the uniqueness of Fourier decompositions in Eqs. (
33
),
(
34
) and (
36
), we
derive the central equations

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{G}}\left(-\omega\delta_{0\bm{G}}\rho_{\bm{k}-\bm{G}}+\rho_{0\bm{G}}\bm{k}\cdot\bm{v}_{\bm{k}-\bm{G}}\right)=0,

(38)

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{G}}\left[-\omega\delta_{0\bm{G}}\bm{B}_{\bm{k}-\bm{G}}+\bm{k}\cdot\left(\bm{v}_{\bm{k}-\bm{G}}\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}}-\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}}\bm{v}_{\bm{k}-\bm{G}}\right)\right]=0,

(39)

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{G}}\left[-\omega\rho_{0\bm{G}}\bm{v}_{\bm{k}-\bm{G}}+\beta\delta_{0\bm{G}}\bm{k}\rho_{\bm{k}-\bm{G}}+\bm{k}\left(\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}}\cdot\bm{B}_{\bm{k}-\bm{G}}\right)-\bm{k}\cdot\left(\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}}\bm{B}_{\bm{k}-\bm{G}}+\bm{B}_{\bm{k}-\bm{G}}\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}}\right)\right]=0.

(40)

These equations can be compactly expressed in matrix form

\sum_{\bm{G}}\mathcal{\bm{N}}\left(\omega,\bm{k},\bm{G}\right)\cdot\bm{\psi_{k-G}}=0,

(42)

which constitutes a

\mathcal{\bm{N}}\left(\omega,\bm{k},\bm{G}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}-\omega\delta_{0\bm{G}}&0&0&0&\rho_{0\bm{G}}k_{x}&\rho_{0\bm{G}}k_{y}&\rho_{0\bm{G}}k_{z}\\

\beta\delta_{0\bm{G}}k_{z}&0&B_{0\bm{G}}k_{z}&-B_{0\bm{G}}k_{y}&0&0&-\omega\rho_{0\bm{G}}\end{array}\right).

(43)

(46)

Accordingly, equation (

III.2
Central equation in terms of
\bm{\xi}

Given the utility of formulating MHD equations in terms of
\bm{\xi}
,
and to benchmark this formulation against the previously derived central
equations for
\left(\rho,\bm{B},\bm{v}\right)
,
we now derive the central equation in terms of
\bm{\xi}
.
This derivation builds upon the theoretical framework established
in Subsec.
III.1
, thereby ensuring
the equivalence and complementarity of the two central equation expressions.
We begin with Eq. (
25
). Building on the methodology
employed in the preceding subsection, we assume a time-harmonic form
for the perturbed displacement field, i.e.,
\bm{\xi}\left(t,\bm{x}\right)=\bm{\xi}\left(\bm{x}\right)e^{-i\omega t}
.
Substituting this ansatz into Eq. (
25
), yields
the eigenvalue equation

\displaystyle-\omega^{2}\rho\bm{\xi}=\nabla\cdot\left\{\left[\beta\left(\rho_{0}\nabla\cdot\bm{\xi}+\frac{1}{\gamma}\bm{\xi}\cdot\nabla\rho_{0}\right)-\bm{B}_{0}\cdot\nabla\times\left(\bm{\xi}\times\bm{B}_{0}\right)\right]\bm{I}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\vphantom{\frac{\bm{B}_{0}\cdot\left[\right]}{4\pi}}+\bm{B}_{0}\nabla\times\left(\bm{\xi}\times\bm{B}_{0}\right)+\nabla\times\left(\bm{\xi}\times\bm{B}_{0}\right)\bm{B}_{0}\right\}.

(53)

Assuming the perturbed displacement field
\bm{\xi}
satisfies
periodic boundary conditions over the magnetofluid domain, it can
be expanded in a Fourier series as

\displaystyle\bm{\xi}\left(\bm{x}\right)=\sum_{\bm{k}}\bm{\xi}_{\bm{k}}e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x}},

(54)

where the wave vector
\bm{k}
is quantized by the periodicity,
and the expansion coefficients are given by the inverse transform

\bm{\xi}_{\bm{k}}=\frac{1}{V_{\mathrm{cry}}}\int_{V_{\mathrm{cry}}}\bm{\xi}\left(\bm{x}\right)e^{-i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x}}d\bm{x}.

(55)

By substituting Eqs. (
29
) and (
54
)
into Eq. (
53
), and following a procedure
analogous to that in Subsec.
III.1
,
we obtain the central equation for
\bm{\xi}
. This
requires expanding each term in Eq. (
53
)
using the PWE method

\displaystyle-\omega^{2}\rho_{0}\bm{\xi}=-\omega^{2}\sum_{\bm{k}}\sum_{\bm{G}}\rho_{0\bm{G}}\bm{\xi}_{\bm{k}-\bm{G}}e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x}},

(56)

\displaystyle\nabla\times\left(\bm{\xi}\times\bm{B}_{0}\right)=\sum_{\bm{k}}\sum_{\bm{G}}i\bm{k}\times\left(\bm{\xi}_{\bm{k}-\bm{G}}\times\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}}\right)e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x}},

(57)

\displaystyle\bm{B}_{0}\nabla\times\left(\bm{\xi}\times\bm{B}_{0}\right)=\sum_{\bm{k}}\sum_{\bm{G}}\sum_{\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}}i\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}}\left\{\left[\left(\bm{k}-\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}\right)\cdot\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}}\right]\bm{\xi}_{\bm{k}-\bm{G}-\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\vphantom{\frac{\bm{B}_{0}\cdot\left[\right]}{4\pi}}-\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}}\left(\bm{k}-\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}\right)\cdot\bm{\xi}_{\bm{k}-\bm{G}-\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}}\right\}e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x}},

(58)

\displaystyle\bm{B}_{0}\cdot\nabla\times\left(\bm{\xi}\times\bm{B}_{0}\right)=\sum_{\bm{k}}\sum_{\bm{G}}\sum_{\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}}i\left\{\left[\left(\bm{k}-\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}\right)\cdot\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}}\right]\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\vphantom{\frac{\bm{B}_{0}\cdot\left[\right]}{4\pi}}-\left(\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}}\cdot\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}}\right)\left(\bm{k}-\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}\right)\right\}\cdot\bm{\xi}_{\bm{k}-\bm{G}-\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}}e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x}},

(59)

\displaystyle\nabla\times\left(\bm{\xi}\times\bm{B}_{0}\right)\bm{B}_{0}=\sum_{\bm{k}}\sum_{\bm{G}}\sum_{\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}}i\left\{\left[\left(\bm{k}-\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}\right)\cdot\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}}\right]\bm{\xi}_{\bm{k}-\bm{G}-\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\vphantom{\frac{\bm{B}_{0}\cdot\left[\right]}{4\pi}}-\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}}\left(\bm{k}-\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}\right)\cdot\bm{\xi}_{\bm{k}-\bm{G}-\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}}\right\}\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}}e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x}},

(60)

\displaystyle\beta\left(\rho_{0}\nabla\cdot\bm{\xi}+\frac{1}{\gamma}\bm{\xi}\cdot\nabla\rho_{0}\right)=\sum_{\bm{k}}\sum_{\bm{G}}\sum_{\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}}i\beta\delta_{0\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}}\rho_{0\bm{G}}\left(\bm{k}-\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}\bm{G}\right)\cdot\bm{\xi}_{\bm{k}-\bm{G}-\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}}e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x}}.

(61)

Substituting Eq. (
56
)-Eq. (
61
) into Eq. (
53
)
yields the following eigenvalue equation

\displaystyle-\omega^{2}\sum_{\bm{k}}\sum_{\bm{G}}\rho_{0\bm{G}}\bm{\xi}_{\bm{k}-\bm{G}}e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x}}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\bm{k}}\sum_{\bm{G}}\sum_{\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}}\left\{\beta\rho_{0\bm{G}}\delta_{0\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}}\bm{k}\left(\bm{k}-\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}\bm{G}\right)\right.

\displaystyle-\left[\left(\bm{k}-\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}\right)\cdot\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}}\right]\bm{k}\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}}+\left(\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}}\cdot\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}}\right)\bm{k}\left(\bm{k}-\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle+\left[\left(\bm{k}\cdot\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}}\right)\left(\bm{k}-\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}\right)\cdot\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}}\right]\bm{I}-\left(\bm{k}\cdot\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}}\right)\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}}\left(\bm{k}-\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle\left.\vphantom{\frac{\bm{B}_{0}\cdot\left[\right]}{4\pi}}+\left[\left(\bm{k}-\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}\right)\cdot\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}}\right]\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}}\bm{k}-\left(\bm{k}\cdot\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}}\right)\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}}\left(\bm{k}-\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}\right)\right\}\cdot\bm{\xi}_{\bm{k}-\bm{G}-\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}}e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x}}.

(62)

By invoking the uniqueness of Fourier decompositions in Eq. (
62
),
we derive the central equations

\sum_{\bm{G}}\sum_{\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}}\mathcal{\bm{M}}\left(\omega,\bm{k},\bm{G},\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}\right)\cdot\bm{\xi}_{\bm{k}-\bm{G}-\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}}=0,

(63)

where
\mathcal{\bm{M}}\left(\omega,\bm{k},\bm{G},\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}\right)

is defined as

\mathcal{\bm{M}}\left(\omega,\bm{k},\bm{G},\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}\right)=\mathscr{\mathcal{\bm{H}}}\left(\bm{k},\bm{G},\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}\right)-\omega^{2}\rho_{0\bm{G}}\delta_{0\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}}\bm{I},

(64)

and where
\mathscr{\mathcal{\bm{H}}}\left(\bm{k},\bm{G},\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}\right)

is given by

\displaystyle\mathscr{\mathcal{\bm{H}}}\left(\bm{k},\bm{G},\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}\right)=\beta\rho_{0\bm{G}}\delta_{0\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}}\bm{k}\left(\bm{k}-\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}\bm{G}\right)-\left[\left(\bm{k}-\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}\right)\cdot\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}}\right]\bm{k}\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle+\left(\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}}\cdot\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}}\right)\bm{k}\left(\bm{k}-\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}\right)+\left[\left(\bm{k}\cdot\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}}\right)\left(\bm{k}-\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}\right)\cdot\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}}\right]\bm{I}

\displaystyle-\left(\bm{k}\cdot\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}}\right)\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}}\left(\bm{k}-\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}\right)+\left[\left(\bm{k}-\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}\right)\cdot\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}}\right]\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}}\bm{k}-\left(\bm{k}\cdot\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}}\right)\bm{B}_{0\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}}\left(\bm{k}-\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}\right).

(65)

Similar to Subsec. (
III.1
), we
consider “empty lattice” case. Under this condition, Eqs (
63
)
becomes

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\ddots&\bm{0}&\bm{0}&\bm{0}&\bm{0}\\
\bm{0}&\mathcal{\bm{M}}\left(\omega,\bm{k}+\bm{G}_{0},0,0\right)&\bm{0}&\bm{0}&\bm{0}\\
\bm{0}&\bm{0}&\mathcal{\bm{M}}\left(\omega,\bm{k},0,0\right)&\bm{0}&\bm{0}\\
\bm{0}&\bm{0}&\bm{0}&\mathcal{\bm{M}}\left(\omega,\bm{k}-\bm{G}_{0},0,0\right)&\bm{0}\\
\bm{0}&\bm{0}&\bm{0}&\bm{0}&\ddots\end{array}\right)\cdot\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\vdots\\
\bm{\xi}_{\bm{k}+\bm{G}_{0}}\\
\bm{\xi}_{\bm{k}}\\
\bm{\xi}_{\bm{k}-\bm{G}_{0}}\\
\vdots\end{array}\right)=0,\quad\bm{k}\bell\in 1_{st}BZ,

(66)

For nontrivial solutions, this further leads to

\cdots\left(\det\mathcal{\bm{M}}\left(\omega,\bm{k}+\bm{G}_{0},0,0\right)\right)\cdot\left(\det\mathcal{\mathcal{\bm{M}}}\left(\omega,\bm{k},0,0\right)\right)\cdot\left(\det\mathcal{\mathcal{\bm{M}}}\left(\omega,\bm{k}-\bm{G}_{0},0,0\right)\right)\cdots=0,\quad\bm{k}\in 1_{st}BZ.

(67)

Equation (

\det\mathcal{\mathcal{\bm{M}}}\left(\omega,\bm{k},0,0\right)=0,\quad\bm{k}\in BZ.

(68)

Evaluating this determinant yields

\left(\omega^{2}-k_{y}^{2}\right)\left[\omega^{4}-\left(1+\beta\right)k^{2}\omega^{2}+\beta k_{y}^{2}k^{2}\right]=0.

(69)

The result of Eq. (

Although the central Eq. (

theoretically describe the propagation behavior of linear MHD waves

involve the coupling of all reciprocal lattice vectors
\bm{G}
,
making direct numerical solution infeasible. To convert this into
a computable finite dimensional matrix eigenvalue problem, truncation
of the reciprocal lattice vector set is necessary. We proceed to calculate
the dispersion equation and determine the band structure of this 1D
magneto-lattice using the central equation formulated in terms of

\left(\rho,\bm{B},\bm{v}\right)
(see Eq. (
41
)).
Focusing on the region covered by the first Brillouin zone, we specifically
limit the reciprocal lattice vectors

\bm{G}

to
0,\pm 1,\pm 2
, thereby truncating the infinite sum in the central
equation into a numerically tractable finite form.

\sum_{G=-2}^{2}\mathcal{\bm{N}}\left(\omega,k_{x},k_{y},k_{z},G\right)\cdot\bm{\psi}_{k_{x}-G}=0,

(76)

where fixed values are adopted for
k_{y}
and
k_{z}
. To solve
for all possible
\bm{\psi}_{k_{x}}
, we first fold all energy
bands into the first Brillouin zone, such that
k_{x}\in 1_{st}BZ
.
This folding, combined with the truncation introduced earlier, transforms
the central equation into a system of three coupled equations

\displaystyle\mathcal{\bm{N}}\left(\omega,k_{x}-1,0\right)\cdot\bm{\psi}_{k_{x}-1}+\mathcal{\bm{N}}\left(\omega,k_{x}-1,-1\right)\cdot\bm{\psi}_{k_{x}}+\mathcal{\bm{N}}\left(\omega,k_{x}-1,-2\right)\cdot\bm{\psi}_{k_{x}+1}=0,

\displaystyle\mathcal{\bm{N}}\left(\omega,k_{x},1\right)\cdot\bm{\psi}_{k_{x}-1}+\mathcal{\bm{N}}\left(\omega,k_{x},0\right)\cdot\bm{\psi}_{k_{x}}+\mathcal{\bm{N}}\left(\omega,k_{x},-1\right)\cdot\bm{\psi}_{k_{x}+1}=0,

\displaystyle\mathcal{\bm{N}}\left(\omega,k_{x}+1,2\right)\cdot\bm{\psi}_{k_{x}-1}+\mathcal{\bm{N}}\left(\omega,k_{x}+1,1\right)\cdot\bm{\psi}_{k_{x}}+\mathcal{\bm{N}}\left(\omega,k_{x}+1,0\right)\cdot\bm{\psi}_{k_{x}+1}=0.

(77)

To determine the dispersion relation, we require the system of equations
to have a non-trivial solution, which imposes the condition that the
determinant of the coefficient matrix must be zero. This leads to
the following dispersion equation

\det\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\mathcal{\bm{N}}\left(\omega,k_{x}-1,0\right)&\mathcal{\bm{N}}\left(\omega,k_{x}-1,-1\right)&\mathcal{\bm{N}}\left(\omega,k_{x}-1,-2\right)\\
\mathcal{\bm{N}}\left(\omega,k_{x},1\right)&\mathcal{\bm{N}}\left(\omega,k_{x},0\right)&\mathcal{\bm{N}}\left(\omega,k_{x},-1\right)\\
\mathcal{\bm{N}}\left(\omega,k_{x}+1,2\right)&\mathcal{\bm{N}}\left(\omega,k_{x}+1,1\right)&\mathcal{\bm{N}}\left(\omega,k_{x}+1,0\right)\end{array}\right)=0.

(78)

\bm{\xi}

\left(\rho,\bm{B},\bm{v}\right)
based formulation

\bm{\xi}
dependent system.
Consistent with the truncation method employed for the
\left(\rho,\bm{B},\bm{v}\right)

\bm{G}

and
\bm{G}^{{}^{\prime}}
to
0,\pm 1,\pm 2
. The central equation

(
63
) can thus be truncated as

\sum_{G=-2}^{2}\sum_{G^{{}^{\prime}}=-2}^{-2}\mathcal{\bm{M}}\left(\omega,k_{x},k_{y},k_{z},G,G^{{}^{\prime}}\right)\cdot\bm{\xi}_{k_{x}-G-G^{{}^{\prime}}}=0.

(79)

\left(\rho,\bm{B},\bm{v}\right)

\bm{\xi}_{k_{x}}

) can be transformed into three
coupled equations

\displaystyle\bm{\Theta}\left(\omega,k_{x}-1,0\right)\cdot\bm{\xi}_{k_{x}-1}+\bm{\Theta}\left(\omega,k_{x}-1,-1\right)\cdot\bm{\xi}_{k_{x}}+\bm{\Theta}\left(\omega,k_{x}-1,-2\right)\cdot\bm{\xi}_{k_{x}+1}=0,

\displaystyle\bm{\Theta}\left(\omega,k_{x},1\right)\cdot\bm{\xi}_{k_{x}-1}+\bm{\Theta}\left(\omega,k_{x},0\right)\cdot\bm{\xi}_{k_{x}}+\bm{\Theta}\left(\omega,k_{x},-1\right)\cdot\bm{\xi}_{k_{x}+1}=0,

\displaystyle\bm{\Theta}\left(\omega,k_{x}+1,2\right)\cdot\bm{\xi}_{k_{x}-1}+\bm{\Theta}\left(\omega,k_{x}+1,1\right)\cdot\bm{\xi}_{k_{x}}+\bm{\Theta}\left(\omega,k_{x}+1,0\right)\cdot\bm{\xi}_{k_{x}+1}=0,

(80)

where
\bm{\Theta}
are defined as

\displaystyle\bm{\Theta}\left(\omega,k_{x},k_{y},k_{z},0\right)=\mathcal{\bm{M}}\left(\omega,k_{x},k_{y},k_{z},1,-1\right)+\mathcal{\bm{M}}\left(\omega,k_{x},k_{y},k_{z},-1,1\right)+\mathcal{\bm{M}}\left(\omega,k_{x},k_{y},k_{z},0,0\right)

\displaystyle\bm{\Theta}\left(\omega,k_{x},k_{y},k_{z},1\right)=\mathcal{\bm{M}}\left(\omega,k_{x},k_{y},k_{z},0,1\right)+\mathcal{\bm{M}}\left(\omega,k_{x},k_{y},k_{z},1,0\right)

\displaystyle\bm{\Theta}\left(\omega,k_{x},k_{y},k_{z},-1\right)=\mathcal{\bm{M}}\left(\omega,k_{x},k_{y},k_{z},0,-1\right)+\mathcal{\bm{M}}\left(\omega,k_{x},k_{y},k_{z},-1,0\right)

\displaystyle\bm{\Theta}\left(\omega,k_{x},k_{y},k_{z},2\right)=\mathcal{\bm{M}}\left(\omega,k_{x},k_{y},k_{z},2,0\right)+\mathcal{\bm{M}}\left(\omega,k_{x},k_{y},k_{z},0,2\right)+\mathcal{\bm{M}}\left(\omega,k_{x},k_{y},k_{z},1,1\right)

\displaystyle\bm{\Theta}\left(\omega,k_{x},k_{y},k_{z},-2\right)=\mathcal{\bm{M}}\left(\omega,k_{x},k_{y},k_{z},-2,0\right)+\mathcal{\bm{M}}\left(\omega,k_{x},k_{y},k_{z},0,-2\right)+\mathcal{\bm{M}}\left(\omega,k_{x},k_{y},k_{z},-1,-1\right)

(81)

The corresponding dispersion equation thus can be obtained by setting
the determinant of the coefficient matrix to zero:

\det\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\bm{\Theta}\left(\omega,k_{x}+1,0\right)&\bm{\Theta}\left(\omega,k_{x}+1,1\right)&\bm{\Theta}\left(\omega,k_{x}+1,2\right)\\
\bm{\Theta}\left(\omega,k_{x},-1\right)&\bm{\Theta}\left(\omega,k_{x},0\right)&\bm{\Theta}\left(\omega,k_{x},1\right)\\
\bm{\Theta}\left(\omega,k_{x}-1,-2\right)&\bm{\Theta}\left(\omega,k_{x}-1,-1\right)&\bm{\Theta}\left(\omega,k_{x}-1,0\right)\end{array}\right)=0.

(82)

The band structure, or equivalently the dispersion relations, can
be calculated using Eq. (
78
) or Eq. (
82
),
respectively. As a benchmark of the two truncated models, we consider
two cases,
B_{m}=0
and
B_{m}=0.1
, and solve Eqs. (
78
)
and (
82
) numerically using Python. The results
are shown in Fig.
2
, demonstrating excellent
agreement between the two central equation models. Results from the
two formulations are plotted on the same graphs and distinguished
by solid dots and hollow circles. As seen in Fig. (
2
),
the solid dots are nearly coincident with the hollow circles, indicating
strong consistency between the two dispersion relations. To further
quantify the agreement, we analyze the error distribution between
the two central equations. The maximum discrepancy remains on the
order of
10^{-3}
for different values of
B_{m}
. This confirms
that the differences between the two formulations are well within
an acceptable range.

V
full MHD simulations

The direct numerical simulations were performed using the Athena++
code
(
Stone et al. 2020
)
. The computational domain was set to
x\in\left[-280\pi,280\pi\right]
,

y\in\left[-\pi,\pi\right]
, and
z\in\left[-1,1\right]
, discretized
with a mesh of
8192\times 64\times 1
cells. This configuration prioritizes
high resolution along the direction of magnetic field modulation
x

while maintaining computational efficiency. The plasma was modeled
with the adiabatic index
\gamma=5/3
and the parameter of
\beta=5/6
.
The computational domain is designed with periodic boundary conditions
applied globally. To excite a broad spectrum of linear waves, initial
velocity perturbations were imposed: for
v_{x}
, 500 random disturbance
points were seeded in the
x
-direction and 30 in the
y
-direction;
for
v_{z}
, 30 points were seeded in
x
and 3 in
y
, all within
the amplitude range of
\left(-0.001,0.001\right)
. Each simulation
ran for a total of 500 Alfvén time. For spectral analysis, we
selected wavenumbers with
k_{x}
in
\left[-1.5,1.5\right]
,
k_{y}

near
1
and
k_{z}
near
0
with the resulting bands folded into
the first Brillouin zone for direct analysis.

VI
CONCLUSION AND DISSCUSSION

This study establishes a systematic theoretical and numerical framework
for analyzing linear MHD wave propagation in magneto-lattices. Starting
from the ideal MHD equations, we review the perturbative approach
to obtain the equilibrium configuration and linearized MHD equations.
The MHD equilibrium holds fundamental importance in this research,
and its role is equivalent to that of crystalline lattices in condensed
matter physics and periodic structures in photonic and phononic crystals.
The linearized MHD equations are used in two distinct yet equivalent
forms, one is a set of first order partial differential equations
expressed in terms of
\left(\rho,\bm{B},\bm{v}\right)
,
and the other is a system of second-order partial differential equations
formulated on the basis of the perturbation displacement
\bm{\xi}
.
The presence of a periodic magnetic field induces a periodic equilibrium
state, and this equilibrium plays a pivotal role in modulating the
propagation of plasma waves. By analogy with theoretical models in
condensed matter physics, we generally derive two equivalent central
equations based on the
\left(\rho,\bm{B},\bm{v}\right)

and the perturbation displacement
\bm{\xi}
,
through
the PWE method, and these two central equations serve as the fundamental
equations for calculating the dispersion relations and band structures
of MHD waves.

As a special scenario, we conduct several verification studies under
a 1D sinusoidally modulated magnetic field to demonstrate the reliability
of the model. First, we conduct comparative verification of the two
models represented by
\left(\rho,\bm{B},\bm{v}\right)

and
\bm{\xi}
, truncate the central equations using the
same truncation method to obtain their truncated versions, adopt the
empty lattice approximation, and calculate the band structures with
the two truncated central equations, which show good agreement between
them as well as with theoretical analytical solutions, a consistency
that is also maintained when setting
B_{m}=0.1
. We further verify
the validity of the truncation scheme by conducting full MHD simulations,
where we introduce random values of
v_{x}
and
v_{z}
at the initial
moment, evolve the system for 500 Alfvén times, perform FFT analysis
on the obtained physical quantities, and the results are in good agreement
with those from the truncated central equations, which further confirms
the effectiveness of our model. These two central equations successfully
reveal intrinsic frequency bandgaps and cutoff phenomena in the system,
with the bandgap width increasing with the magnetic modulation amplitude

B_{m}
, and the presence of the magneto-lattice induces
the splitting of AWs modes into multiple branches, an effect that
is absent in uniform plasmas.

These findings provide new theoretical insights for controlling MHD
waves in structured plasmas, and in future work, we will apply this
model to study the band structures and propagation characteristics
of MHD waves in 2D or 3D magneto-lattices, such as spatially periodic
magnetic islands, and analyze the nonlinear behaviors arising when
the magnetic field intensity of the magneto-lattice is comparable
to that of the background magnetic field.
