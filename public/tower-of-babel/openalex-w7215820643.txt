PLAYFULNESS AND TEACHING CHILDREN WITH ADHD: A SYSTEMATIC
REVIEW OF SCIENTIFIC EVIDENCE AND INCLUSIVE PEDAGOGICAL
PRACTICES
A LUDICIDADE E O ENSINO DE CRIANÇAS COM TDAH: UMA REVISÃO
SISTEMÁTICA DE EVIDÊNCIAS CIENTÍFICAS E PRÁTICAS PEDAGÓGICAS
INCLUSIVAS
Ana Lúcia de Souza Almeida1

RESUMO: O Transtorno do Déficit de Atenção e Hiperatividade (TDAH) representa um dos
desafios mais recorrentes no cotidiano escolar contemporâneo, impactando diretamente o
desempenho acadêmico, a regulação atencional e a socialização das crianças. Este artigo tem
como objetivo analisar de forma sistemática as evidências científicas recentes acerca do impacto
e da eficácia do uso da ludicidade e de práticas pedagógicas baseadas em evidências no ensino
de alunos com TDAH. Metodologicamente, estruturou-se uma revisão integrativa
fundamentada em bases acadêmicas e ancorada em cinco produções de referência publicadas no
período de 2021 a 2026. A amostragem contemplou investigações sobre percepções docentes na
escola pública, o uso de jogos no ensino de matemática, estratégias comportamentais e
panoramas gerais das pesquisas acadêmicas sobre o transtorno. Os resultados evidenciam que a
incorporação de recursos lúdicos reduz a desatenção motora, promove a motivação intrínseca e
melhora o clima em sala de aula, embora ainda existam barreiras estruturais significativas e
lacunas na formação continuada dos professores. Conclui-se que a ludicidade não deve ser
encarada como mero passatempo, mas como uma ferramenta neuropedagógica indispensável
para a efetivação da inclusão escolar.
Palavras-chave: TDAH. Ludicidade. Jogos Educativos. Inclusão Educacional. Práticas
Pedagógicas.

ABSTRACT: Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) represents one of the most
recurrent challenges in contemporary school life, directly impacting academic performance,
attentional regulation, and children's socialization. This article aims to systematically analyze
recent scientific evidence regarding the impact and effectiveness of using playfulness and
evidence-based pedagogical practices in teaching students with ADHD. Methodologically, an
integrative review was structured based on academic databases and anchored in five benchmark
productions published between 2021 and 2026. The sampling included investigations into
teacher perceptions in public schools, the use of games in mathematics teaching, behavioral
strategies, and general overviews of academic research on the disorder. The results show that
the incorporation of playful resources reduces motor inattention, promotes intrinsic
motivation, and improves classroom climate, although significant structural barriers and gaps
in ongoing teacher training still persist. It is concluded that playfulness should not be viewed
as a mere pastime, but as an indispensable neuro-pedagogical tool for the realization of school
inclusion.
Keywords: ADHD. Playfulness. Educational Games. Educational Inclusion. Pedagogical
Practices.
1. INTRODUCTION
Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) is a neurodevelopmental disorder
characterized by persistent levels of inattention, hyperactivity, and impulsivity that interfere
with the child's school and social functioning or development. In the Brazilian educational
context, the insertion and permanence of students diagnosed with ADHD are one of the
greatest tests for the adaptive capacity of educational institutions, especially the public
network.
The traditional teaching model — strongly based on prolonged oral exposure, student
passivity and the requirement of strict motor control — clashes head-on with the
neurobiological profile of students with ADHD. In view of this mismatch, the search for active
methodologies and, in particular, playfulness has gained central prominence in recent scientific
discussions. Games, interactive dynamics, manipulable materials and playful approaches
emerge not only as recreational elements, but as neuropedagogical tools capable of modulating

1.1 Research Problem
In view of this panorama, the present study revolves around the following research
problem: How do playfulness and evidence-based pedagogical strategies contribute to the
improvement of the teaching-learning process and to the educational inclusion of children with
ADHD, according to recent scientific literature?
1.2 Hypothesis
It is formulated as a central hypothesis that the structured integration of playful
practices and educational games in the school environment significantly attenuates the
attentional and behavioral deficits of students with ADHD, promoting superior cognitive and
social engagement compared to traditional methods, as long as it is supported by adequate
teacher training and institutional support.
1.3 Justification
The relevance of this research is justified by the urgency of transposing theoretical
academic knowledge to the practical daily lives of teachers. Although there are numerous legal
guidelines for inclusion, the teaching staff often reports pedagogical loneliness and a lack of
applicable methodological guidelines. Synthesizing recent scientific evidence through a
rigorous systematic review provides practical and theoretical subsidies for public policy makers,
school administrators, and educators who seek to transform the classroom into a truly inclusive
and stimulating environment.
1.4 Objectives
1.4.1 General Objective
To critically analyze, through a systematic review of recent literature (2021-2026), the
impact and effectiveness of playfulness and evidence-based pedagogical practices in the
teaching of children with ADHD.

2. THEORETICAL FOUNDATION: PLAYFULNESS AND NEUROPEDAGOGICAL
MANAGEMENT IN ADHD
Introduction to neurodiversity and the concept of adhd in school
The contemporary understanding of Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder
(ADHD) has overcome the purely deficit and reductionist view, embracing the paradigm of
neurodiversity. From this perspective, the atypical brain is not understood as defective, but as
the bearer of natural variations in neurological development that imply different ways of
processing stimuli, regulating attention, and modulating motor response.
In the school context, however, the traditional teaching structure — inherited from
nineteenth-century industrial models — continues to require prolonged physical immobility,
passive listening, and maintenance of sustained attentional focus for excessive periods. For
students diagnosed with ADHD, whose executive functions associated with the prefrontal
cortex present atypical maturation, these requirements configure insurmountable barriers. As
pointed out by Macêdo et al. (2026), recent academic research demonstrates that the mismatch
between the student's neurobiology and the rigidity of the school curriculum is the main root
of academic failure and school dropout of atypical children.

Executive functions and attentional dysregulation
From a neuropsychological point of view, ADHD is primarily understood as a disorder
of executive functions (EFs). EFs encompass the set of higher cognitive processes responsible
for planning, inhibitory control, working memory, cognitive flexibility, and emotional
regulation.
Children with ADHD experience severe difficulties in inhibitory control (the ability to
rein in impulses and resist distractions) and working memory (retaining and manipulating
information for short periods). In the classroom, this translates into the inability to stay
focused on tasks that are considered monotonous or repetitive.
It is exactly at this point that the theoretical foundation meets playfulness. While
traditional lectures require top-down attentional effort (voluntary and exhausting for the brain
with ADHD), playful activities and games activate the dopaminergic reward system in a
bottom-up way (through pleasurable and immediate external stimulus), engaging the student
spontaneously and drastically reducing motor hyperactivity behaviors (Silva, 2023).
Playfulness as a neuropedagogical tool
Playfulness should not be reduced to a mere recreational resource or pastime to fill
vacant schedules. From the perspective of modern neuropedagogy, the act of playing and
interacting through structured games constitutes a universal language of cognitive and social
development.
The Game as a Mediator of Learning
Based on the socio-interactionist theory, play acts on the Zone of Proximal
Development (ZPD) described by Vygotsky, allowing the child to reach levels of problemsolving in cooperation and interaction that he would not reach alone. In the case of students
with ADHD, the pedagogical game offers:
•

Structuring of Clear Rules: The regulated format of the game trains inhibitory control in a
playful way, teaching tolerance to frustration and waiting for one's turn.

•

Immediate Feedback: Unlike a traditional test whose results take days, the game provides
instant feedback on the action taken, keeping the brain engaged and curious.

Playfulness in Mathematics Teaching
One of the fields where ADHD generates the most anxiety and academic blocks is
logical-mathematical reasoning. Dantas, Costa, and Carvalho (2023) demonstrate that the
insertion of manipulable materials and mathematical board games transforms the abstract
concept into something concrete and tangible. By manipulating pieces, counting points, and
solving spatial challenges in a group, the student with ADHD channels motor energy into the
cognitive task, dissolving the barrier of inattention.
The daily life of public schools: teachers' perceptions and challenges of inclusion
The implementation of playful and evidence-based practices comes up against a
complex structural scenario, especially in public educational institutions. Miranda and Silva
(2021) reveal in their research that public school teachers feel lonely and unprepared to deal with
the behavioral management of students with ADHD.
Teaching Overload and Lack of Multidisciplinary Support
The main bottlenecks pointed out by the teaching literature include:
1. Overcrowding of classrooms: Makes it impossible to have individualized monitoring and
constant mediation required by atypical students.
2. Gap in initial and continuing education: Undergraduate courses rarely offer in-depth
courses on neurodiversity and neurodevelopmental disorders.
3. Scarcity of pedagogical resources: The absence of adapted materials and support from
multiprofessional teams (psychologists, psychopedagogues, speech therapists) overwhelms
the teacher, who often resorts to purely intuitive and informal adaptations.
Evidence-based pedagogical strategies (ebps)
In view of the challenges mentioned above, the integrative review literature
systematized by João Batista da Silva (2023) and corroborated by Brito and Vale (2024) points
to a set of interventions based on scientific evidence that obtain a high success rate in the school
management of ADHD:

Environmental and Visual Modifications: Reduction of excessive visual stimuli on the
walls (visual pollution), positioning of the student near the teacher's desk and removal of
windows with large external flow.

•

Chunking: Breaking down long written activities into short, manageable steps, providing
positive reinforcement with each completed step.

•

Use of Assistive Technology and Multimedia Resources: Application of interactive
educational software and tablets, which capture atypical attention through dynamic visual
and auditory stimuli.

•

Token Economy and Positive Reinforcement Systems: Setting short-term behavioral goals
rewarded for playful privileges or points in the activity, reinforcing desired behavior at the
expense of punishment.
An in-depth analysis of the recent literature (2021–2026) shows that the success of school

inclusion of children with ADHD is not a pedagogical utopia, but a requirement that demands
the articulation between neuroscientific knowledge and teaching practice. Playfulness emerges
as the central axis of this transformation, humanizing the teaching process and respecting the
singularities of child neurodevelopment. However, for playfulness to cease to be the exception
and become the rule, it is imperative to invest in school infrastructure, multiprofessional
support, and continuing teacher training.
3. METHODOLOGY
The present study is characterized as a systematic and integrative review of the
literature, structured to gather, analyze and critically synthesize findings of scientific research
published on the theme of playfulness and ADHD.
3.1 Sampling and Eligibility Criteria
The sampling was intentional and based on fundamental references from the recent
academic literature (2021–2026). Five main axes of study published by authors of relevance in
the Brazilian educational and neuroscientific area were selected:
1. Miranda & Silva (2021): Focus on teacher perceptions and challenges of inclusion in public
schools.

Exclusion Criteria:

Publications prior to 2021.

Studies focused exclusively on pharmacological treatment without a pedagogical

Opinion pieces, editorials or articles without explicit methodological rigor.

3.1 Analysis of Methodological Dimensions and Approaches
As shown in Table 1, the qualitative or qualitative-quantitative approach predominates
in recent studies, reflecting the need to understand the complexity of the educational and
behavioral phenomenon beyond purely quantitative metrics.
•

The Teaching Factor (Miranda & Silva, 2021; Brito & Vale, 2024): Research shows that
public school teachers face a double challenge: overcrowding in classrooms and the absence
of specialized multidisciplinary support. Inclusive practices often emerge from the
individual and intuitive effort of teachers, evidencing a structural gap in initial training.

•

The Effectiveness of Play in Specific Areas (Dantas, Costa & Carvalho, 2023): In the
teaching of abstract subjects such as Mathematics, the introduction of games and
manipulable materials transforms the axis of the class. Play acts as a mediator of sustained
attention, allowing the student with ADHD to process abstract concepts through concrete
experimentation.

•

Scientific Evidence and Behavioral Management (Silva, 2023; Macêdo et al., 2026): The
most recent integrative reviews reinforce that successful pedagogical interventions combine
positive reinforcement, visual structuring of the environment, and breaking down complex
tasks into smaller, rewarding steps.

5. FINAL CONSIDERATIONS
The present systematic review showed that playfulness plays a fundamental
neuropedagogical role in the teaching of children with ADHD. The initial hypothesis was fully
confirmed: when the school environment replaces traditional rigidity with playful strategies,

It is recommended that future research investigate the longitudinal application of
