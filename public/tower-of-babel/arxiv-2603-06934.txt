Abstract
We extend the single-flavon B B -lattice Froggatt–Nielsen (FN) framework—previously successful for quark masses and Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa (CKM) mixing—to the lepton sector. The same B B -lattice power structure ( ϵ ≡ 1 / B ≃ 0.19 \epsilon\equiv 1/B\simeq 0.19 ) generates charged-lepton mass hierarchies and a normal-ordered neutrino spectrum; large neutrino mixing angles require an additional approximate mu–tau symmetry, broken at 𝒪 ⁡ ( ϵ ) \mathcal{O}(\epsilon) to generate a nonzero reactor angle and CP-violating phase. The Pontecorvo-Maki-Nakagawa-Sakata (PMNS) matrix factorizes as U PMNS = U e † ​ U ν U_{\rm PMNS}=U_{e}^{\dagger}U_{\nu} , with near-tribimaximal U ν U_{\nu} corrected by small charged-lepton rotations whose phases are naturally aligned by the single-flavon origin of the Yukawa textures. This alignment produces a two-branch prediction in the ( θ 23 , δ ) (\theta_{23},\delta) plane: a lower-octant solution with θ 23 ≈ 43 ∘ \theta_{23}\approx 43^{\circ} , δ ≈ 286 ∘ \delta\approx 286^{\circ} , and an upper-octant solution with θ 23 ≈ 46 ∘ \theta_{23}\approx 46^{\circ} , δ ≈ 304 ∘ \delta\approx 304^{\circ} . The lower octant is favored by a ∼ 4 : 1 \sim\!4{:}1 theoretical prior. The Jarlskog invariant J CP ≃ − 0.027 J_{\rm CP}\simeq-0.027 is nearly branch-independent; only precision measurements of the atmospheric octant and Dirac phase at DUNE, Hyper-Kamiokande, IceCube, and JUNO can distinguish the two solutions.

XI.1
Phase alignment from the single-flavon structure

The scan assumes approximate alignment of the charged-lepton

This assumption follows from the single-flavon origin of all Yukawa

In a Froggatt–Nielsen model with a single flavon
\Phi

each Yukawa entry arises from a messenger chain carrying

p_{ij}

\langle\Phi\rangle/\Lambda

If the

Leading-order octant–
\delta
theorem.

To leading nontrivial order in the small charged-lepton angles, the two-branch
structure in the

The full reactor element receives contributions from both the

U_{e3}\simeq s_{13}^{\nu}e^{-i\delta_{\nu}}-\theta_{12}^{e}\,s_{23}^{\nu}\,e^{i\phi_{12}^{e}}-\theta_{13}^{e}\,c_{23}^{\nu}\,e^{i\phi_{13}^{e}}.

In the
B

\theta_{13}^{e}c_{23}^{\nu}/(\theta_{12}^{e}s_{23}^{\nu})=\epsilon^{1/3}\approx 0.57

Define

(52)

U_{e3}\simeq s_{13}^{\nu}e^{-i\delta_{\nu}}\left(1-r\,e^{-i\Phi_{e}}\right).

(53)

\theta_{12}^{e}s_{23}^{\nu}e^{i\phi_{12}^{e}}

the ratio

interfering amplitudes, and the effective phase difference

\Phi_{e}=\phi_{12}^{e}-\delta_{\nu}

of the shift in the complex plane.

We have extended the single-flavon
B

B

\epsilon=1/B
), which fixes charged-lepton Yukawa suppressions and neutrino mass eigenvalue ratios, and (ii) an approximate mu–tau symmetry (
Z_{2}^{\mu\tau}

HarrisonScott

MuTauReview

AltarelliFeruglio

23

The central result is a two-branch prediction in the

•

\theta_{23}\approx 43^{\circ}

•

NO-II

\theta_{23}\approx 46^{\circ}

The two branches arise because the same charged-lepton–neutrino interference that generates the reactor element

\sim\!\phi_{0}/9

The framework makes a falsifiable prediction: if future data establish the upper octant,

Appendix A
Analytic PMNS matrix in terms of model parameters

We collect here the explicit construction of the PMNS matrix
used throughout this paper.

A.1
Model parameters

The framework has two sectors, each specified by a small number of parameters.

Neutrino sector (4 parameters).

The neutrino diagonalization matrix
U_{\nu}
is parameterized in
the PDG convention,

U_{\nu}=R_{23}(\theta_{23}^{\nu})\,R_{13}(\theta_{13}^{\nu},\,\delta_{\nu})\,R_{12}(\theta_{12}^{\nu}),

(56)

with
\theta_{23}^{\nu}\simeq 45^{\circ}
(near-maximal, from mu–tau symmetry),

\theta_{12}^{\nu}\simeq 35^{\circ}
(near-tribimaximal),

\theta_{13}^{\nu}\sim\epsilon\simeq 0.15
rad (from
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon)
mu–tau breaking),
and
\delta_{\nu}\sim 290^{\circ}
(neutrino CP phase).

Charged-lepton sector (6 parameters).

The charged-lepton diagonalization matrix
U_{e}
is built from
three small rotations with phases,

U_{e}=R_{23}(\theta_{23}^{e},\,\phi_{23}^{e})\,R_{13}(\theta_{13}^{e},\,\phi_{13}^{e})\,R_{12}(\theta_{12}^{e},\,\phi_{12}^{e}),

(57)

where the rotation angles are fixed by the
B
-lattice exponent
differences up to
\mathcal{O}(1)
coefficients:

\theta_{12}^{e}=c_{12}^{e}\,\epsilon^{17/9},\quad\theta_{23}^{e}=c_{23}^{e}\,\epsilon^{2},\quad\theta_{13}^{e}=c_{13}^{e}\,\epsilon^{20/9},

(58)

with
c_{ij}^{e}=\mathcal{O}(1)
.
The phases
\phi_{ij}^{e}
arise from the complex flavon VEV
as described in Sec.
XI.1
; the single-flavon
structure enforces the approximate alignment

\phi_{12}^{e}\approx\phi_{23}^{e}
with misalignment

\sim\!\phi_{0}/9
.

Numerical base values.

With
\epsilon=1/B\simeq 0.1867
:

\displaystyle\theta_{12}^{e}\big|_{c=1}

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{17/9}\simeq 0.042~\text{rad}\simeq 2.4^{\circ},

(59)

\displaystyle\theta_{23}^{e}\big|_{c=1}

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{2}\phantom{/9}\simeq 0.035~\text{rad}\simeq 2.0^{\circ},

\displaystyle\theta_{13}^{e}\big|_{c=1}

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{20/9}\simeq 0.024~\text{rad}\simeq 1.4^{\circ}.

A.2
Rotation matrix conventions

Each rotation
R_{ij}(\theta,\phi)
acts in the
ij
plane with
a phase
\phi
attached to the off-diagonal sine entries.
Explicitly,

R_{12}(\theta,\phi)=\begin{pmatrix}c&s\,e^{i\phi}&0\\
-s\,e^{-i\phi}&c&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}\!,

(60)

R_{13}(\theta,\phi)=\begin{pmatrix}c&0&s\,e^{i\phi}\\
0&1&0\\
-s\,e^{-i\phi}&0&c\end{pmatrix}\!,

(61)

R_{23}(\theta,\phi)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&c&s\,e^{i\phi}\\
0&-s\,e^{-i\phi}&c\end{pmatrix}\!,

(62)

where
c\equiv\cos\theta
,
s\equiv\sin\theta
.
When the phase argument is omitted,
\phi=0
.

A.3
Leading-order PMNS matrix

Since all charged-lepton angles satisfy

\theta_{ij}^{e}\ll 1
,
U_{e}
may be expanded to first order:

U_{e}\simeq\mathbb{1}+\begin{pmatrix}0&\theta_{12}^{e}\,e^{i\phi_{12}^{e}}&\theta_{13}^{e}\,e^{i\phi_{13}^{e}}\\[4.0pt]
-\theta_{12}^{e}\,e^{-i\phi_{12}^{e}}&0&\theta_{23}^{e}\,e^{i\phi_{23}^{e}}\\[4.0pt]
-\theta_{13}^{e}\,e^{-i\phi_{13}^{e}}&-\theta_{23}^{e}\,e^{-i\phi_{23}^{e}}&0\end{pmatrix}\!+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{34/9}).

(63)

The Hermitian conjugate is

U_{e}^{\dagger}\simeq\mathbb{1}+\begin{pmatrix}0&-\theta_{12}^{e}\,e^{i\phi_{12}^{e}}&-\theta_{13}^{e}\,e^{i\phi_{13}^{e}}\\[4.0pt]
\theta_{12}^{e}\,e^{-i\phi_{12}^{e}}&0&-\theta_{23}^{e}\,e^{i\phi_{23}^{e}}\\[4.0pt]
\theta_{13}^{e}\,e^{-i\phi_{13}^{e}}&\theta_{23}^{e}\,e^{-i\phi_{23}^{e}}&0\end{pmatrix}\!.

(64)

The PMNS matrix
U_{\mathrm{PMNS}}=U_{e}^{\dagger}U_{\nu}
then takes the form

(U_{\mathrm{PMNS}})_{\alpha i}\;=\;(U_{\nu})_{\alpha i}+\sum_{\beta\neq\alpha}(\Delta_{e}^{\dagger})_{\alpha\beta}\,(U_{\nu})_{\beta i}+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{34/9}),

(65)

where
\Delta_{e}^{\dagger}\equiv U_{e}^{\dagger}-\mathbb{1}
is the
off-diagonal perturbation in Eq. (
64
).

A.4
Explicit PMNS elements

Using the shorthand
(U_{\nu})_{\alpha i}\equiv U^{\nu}_{\alpha i}
and
suppressing the superscript
e
on the charged-lepton angles and phases,
the nine elements are:

\displaystyle U_{e1}

\displaystyle=U^{\nu}_{e1}-\theta_{12}\,e^{i\phi_{12}}\,U^{\nu}_{\mu 1}-\theta_{13}\,e^{i\phi_{13}}\,U^{\nu}_{\tau 1},

(66)

\displaystyle U_{e2}

\displaystyle=U^{\nu}_{e2}-\theta_{12}\,e^{i\phi_{12}}\,U^{\nu}_{\mu 2}-\theta_{13}\,e^{i\phi_{13}}\,U^{\nu}_{\tau 2},

(67)

\displaystyle U_{e3}

\displaystyle=U^{\nu}_{e3}-\theta_{12}\,e^{i\phi_{12}}\,U^{\nu}_{\mu 3}-\theta_{13}\,e^{i\phi_{13}}\,U^{\nu}_{\tau 3}

\displaystyle=s_{13}^{\nu}\,e^{-i\delta_{\nu}}-\theta_{12}\,s_{23}^{\nu}c_{13}^{\nu}\,e^{i\phi_{12}}

\displaystyle\quad-\theta_{13}\,c_{23}^{\nu}c_{13}^{\nu}\,e^{i\phi_{13}},

(68)

\displaystyle U_{\mu 1}

\displaystyle=U^{\nu}_{\mu 1}+\theta_{12}\,e^{-i\phi_{12}}\,U^{\nu}_{e1}-\theta_{23}\,e^{i\phi_{23}}\,U^{\nu}_{\tau 1},

(69)

\displaystyle U_{\mu 2}

\displaystyle=U^{\nu}_{\mu 2}+\theta_{12}\,e^{-i\phi_{12}}\,U^{\nu}_{e2}-\theta_{23}\,e^{i\phi_{23}}\,U^{\nu}_{\tau 2},

(70)

\displaystyle U_{\mu 3}

\displaystyle=U^{\nu}_{\mu 3}+\theta_{12}\,e^{-i\phi_{12}}\,U^{\nu}_{e3}-\theta_{23}\,e^{i\phi_{23}}\,U^{\nu}_{\tau 3}

\displaystyle=s_{23}^{\nu}c_{13}^{\nu}+\theta_{12}\,s_{13}^{\nu}\,e^{-i(\phi_{12}+\delta_{\nu})}

\displaystyle\quad-\theta_{23}\,c_{23}^{\nu}c_{13}^{\nu}\,e^{i\phi_{23}},

(71)

\displaystyle U_{\tau 1}

\displaystyle=U^{\nu}_{\tau 1}+\theta_{13}\,e^{-i\phi_{13}}\,U^{\nu}_{e1}+\theta_{23}\,e^{-i\phi_{23}}\,U^{\nu}_{\mu 1},

(72)

\displaystyle U_{\tau 2}

\displaystyle=U^{\nu}_{\tau 2}+\theta_{13}\,e^{-i\phi_{13}}\,U^{\nu}_{e2}+\theta_{23}\,e^{-i\phi_{23}}\,U^{\nu}_{\mu 2},

(73)

\displaystyle U_{\tau 3}

\displaystyle=U^{\nu}_{\tau 3}+\theta_{13}\,e^{-i\phi_{13}}\,U^{\nu}_{e3}+\theta_{23}\,e^{-i\phi_{23}}\,U^{\nu}_{\mu 3}

\displaystyle=c_{23}^{\nu}c_{13}^{\nu}+\theta_{13}\,s_{13}^{\nu}\,e^{-i(\phi_{13}+\delta_{\nu})}

\displaystyle\quad+\theta_{23}\,s_{23}^{\nu}c_{13}^{\nu}\,e^{-i\phi_{23}}.

(74)

In Eqs. (
68
), (
71
), and (
74
)
we have substituted the explicit PDG forms of the third-column
neutrino elements (
U^{\nu}_{e3}=s_{13}^{\nu}e^{-i\delta_{\nu}}
,

U^{\nu}_{\mu 3}=s_{23}^{\nu}c_{13}^{\nu}
,

U^{\nu}_{\tau 3}=c_{23}^{\nu}c_{13}^{\nu}
).

Appendix B
Relation to the companion-paper neutrino texture

The companion paper
Barger2025bfnb
derives a neutrino exponent
matrix from the strict Weinberg-operator additive rule

p^{\nu}_{ij}=Q(L_{i})+Q(L_{j})
with lepton-doublet charges

Q(L_{i})=(1,\tfrac{1}{2},0)
, giving

p^{\nu}_{\text{comp.}}=\begin{pmatrix}2&\tfrac{3}{2}&1\\[2.0pt]
\tfrac{3}{2}&1&\tfrac{1}{2}\\[2.0pt]
1&\tfrac{1}{2}&0\end{pmatrix}.

(79)

This hierarchical texture reproduces the correct mass eigenvalue
scaling
m_{1}:m_{2}:m_{3}\sim\epsilon^{2}:\epsilon:1
, which is the only neutrino
observable the companion paper addresses.
The present paper adopts instead the flat texture of
Eq. (
28
), in which

p^{\nu}_{L_{2}}=p^{\nu}_{L_{3}}=0
, so that the entire
23
block is

\mathcal{O}(m_{0})
. This flattening is necessary to accommodate
the large atmospheric and solar mixing angles through an approximate
mu–tau symmetry in the
\mathcal{O}(1)
coefficients.
The mass eigenvalue hierarchy is preserved: it arises from the

\mathcal{O}(\epsilon)
suppression of the first-generation couplings
rather than from a hierarchical
23
block.

The two assignments share common features:
(i) the same expansion parameter
\epsilon=1/B
;
(ii) the same first-generation suppression

p^{\nu}_{1j}\geq 1
;
(iii) identical mass-eigenvalue ratios

m_{1}:m_{2}:m_{3}\sim\epsilon^{2}:\epsilon:1
; and
(iv) identical charged-lepton mass scaling

m_{e}:m_{\mu}:m_{\tau}\propto\epsilon^{29/6}:\epsilon^{5/3}:1
.
They differ only in the
23
sub-block of the neutrino
texture—precisely the sector where the additional mu–tau symmetry
input of the present paper acts.
In this sense the two treatments are complementary: the companion
paper establishes the
B
-lattice mass framework, while the present
paper introduces the symmetry structure required for the PMNS
mixing pattern.

In earlier two-over-two constructions the Majorana nature of the
neutrino operator required a charge normalization denominator of 18,
reflecting the bilinear structure of the effective dimension-five
operator. In the present effective-texture formulation this doubling
is absorbed into the definition of
p^{\nu}_{ij}
, allowing a minimal
denominator of 9 without altering the predicted
B
-scaling relations
for masses or mixing.
