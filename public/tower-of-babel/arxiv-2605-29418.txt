Abstract
. In this paper, we give a description of the cohomology groups of the symmetric powers of the tautological bundle associated with a sufficiently positive line bundle on the Hilbert scheme of 2 or 3 points on a smooth projective complex variety. The dimensions of these cohomology groups are expressed in terms of the Hilbert polynomials of the first and second secant varieties of the embedding given by the sufficiently positive line bundle. We also compute these Hilbert polynomials completely.



1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a smooth projective complex variety of dimension
n
, and
L
be a line bundle on
X
. Denote by
X^{[k+1]}
the Hilbert scheme of
k+1
points on
X
, and consider the tautological bundle
E_{k+1,L}
on
X^{[k+1]}
associated to
L
(see Section
2
for the definitions and notations). The cohomology groups
H^{i}(X^{[k+1]},S^{\ell}E_{k+1,L})
of symmetric powers of the tautological bundle
E_{k+1,L}
has attracted considerable attention (see e.g.,
[
7
,
8
,
10
,
13
,
17
]
). This has been studied mainly for the case where
n
and
\ell
are small and
k
is arbitrary. In this paper, we deal with the case where
n
and
\ell
are arbitrary and
k=1
or
2
under the assumption that
L
is sufficiently positive (see Remark
2.3
for the discussion on the required positivity condition for
L
). Our result may provide a complement to the existing literature.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
L
is sufficiently positive. For an integer
\ell\geq 1
, we have the following:

(1)

H^{i}(X^{[2]},S^{\ell}E_{2,L})=\begin{cases}H^{0}(\Sigma_{1},\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma_{1}}(\ell))&\text{for $i=0$}\\
H^{i}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})\otimes H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(\ell))&\text{for $1\leq i\leq n$}\\
0&\text{for $i\geq n+1$}.\end{cases}

(2)

H^{i}(X^{[3]},S^{\ell}E_{3,L})=\begin{cases}H^{0}(\Sigma_{2},\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma_{2}}(\ell))&\text{for $i=0$}\\
\begin{aligned} &\big(H^{i}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})\otimes H^{0}(\Sigma_{1},\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma_{1}}(\ell))\big)\\[-7.0pt]
&\oplus\big(H^{i}(X^{[2]},\mathcal{O}_{X^{[2]}})/H^{i}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})\otimes H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(\ell))\big)\end{aligned}&\text{for $1\leq i\leq n$}\\
H^{i}(X^{[2]},\mathcal{O}_{X^{[2]}})\otimes H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(\ell))&\text{for $n+1\leq i\leq 2n$}\\
0&\text{for $i\geq 2n+1$}.\end{cases}

This theorem is a higher dimensional generalization of
[
10
, Theorem 4.2 (2)]
, which deals with the case where
X
is a curve. Since the cohomology groups
H^{i}(X^{[2]},\mathcal{O}_{X^{[2]}})
can be explicitly determined (Lemma
2.1
), the problem is reduced to computing the Hilbert function
H_{\Sigma_{k}}(\ell)=h^{0}(\Sigma_{k},\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma_{k}}(\ell))
of the
k
-th secant variety
\Sigma_{k}
of
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}H^{0}(X,L)=\mathbb{P}^{r}
for
k=1
and
2
, which is defined as

\Sigma_{k}=\Sigma_{k}(X,L):=\overline{\bigcup_{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k+1}\in X}\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{k+1}\rangle}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}.

Here as
L
is sufficiently positive,
L
is very ample giving an embedding of
X
into
\mathbb{P}^{r}
. There has been a great deal of work on secant varieties in various flavors (see e.g.,
[
1
,
3
,
4
,
6
,
9
,
10
,
16
,
18
]
). Recently, many people are interested in the connection to the tensor decomposition problem and computational complexity theory (see
[
14
,
15
]
).
In the main cases of this paper, namely,
k=1
or
2
, it is known that
H^{i}(\Sigma_{k},\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma_{k}}(\ell))=0
for
i>0
and
\ell>0
(see Theorem
2.2
). Thus
h^{0}(\Sigma_{k},\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma_{k}}(\ell))=\chi(\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma_{k}}(\ell))
for
\ell\geq 1
. In other words, the Hilbert function
H_{\Sigma_{k}}(\ell)
coincides with the Hilbert polynomial
P_{\Sigma_{k}}(\ell):=\chi(\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma_{k}}(\ell))
. In this paper, we express
P_{\Sigma_{k}}(\ell)
in terms of
\Gamma:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{n}h^{p}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
and the following functions

l(a):=\chi(L^{a}),~s_{1}(a,b):=\chi(S^{a}\Omega_{X}\otimes L^{b}),~s_{2}(a,b,c):=\chi(S^{a}\Omega_{X}\otimes S^{b}\Omega_{X}\otimes L^{c})

depending only on the cotangent bundle
\Omega_{X}
and the embedding line bundle
L
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that
L
is sufficiently positive. For an integer
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have the following:

(1)

\displaystyle P_{\Sigma_{1}}(\ell)=\sum_{m=0}^{2n+1}\left(l(2m+1)+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\Big(l(2m+1-i)l(i)-\sum_{j=0}^{2i-1}s_{1}(j,2m+1)\Big)\right)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}t=0\\
t\neq m\end{subarray}}^{2n+1}\frac{\ell-2t-1}{2m-2t}
.

(2)

\displaystyle P_{\Sigma_{2}}(\ell)=\sum_{m=0}^{3n+2}P_{\Sigma_{2}}(3m+2)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}t=0\\
t\neq m\end{subarray}}^{3n+2}\frac{\ell-3t-2}{3m-3t}
, where

\displaystyle P_{\Sigma_{2}}(3m+2)=P_{\Sigma_{1}}(3m+2)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\Bigg(P_{\Sigma_{1}}(3m-i+2)+\Gamma\cdot l(3m-i+2)-\sum_{p=1}^{i+1}\Big(l(i+1-p)\cdot l(3m-2i+1+p)-\sum_{q=0}^{2i-2p+1}s_{1}(q,3m-i+2)\Big)\Bigg)\cdot l(i)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\Bigg(P_{\Sigma_{1}}(2i)+\Gamma\cdot l(2i)-\sum_{p=1}^{i}\Big(l(i-p)\cdot l(i+p)-\sum_{q=0}^{2i-2p-1}s_{1}(q,2i)\Big)\Bigg)\cdot l(3m-2i+2)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=0}^{2i-1}\sum_{p=1}^{2i}s_{2}(j,2i-p,3m+2)+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=0}^{2i-1}\sum_{p=1}^{j}s_{2}(j-p,2i+2p-1,3m+2)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=0}^{2i-1}\sum_{k=0}^{3m-3i}\Bigg(\sum_{p=1}^{k+2i+2}s_{2}(j,k+2i-p+2,3m+2)+\sum_{p=1}^{j}s_{2}(j-p,k+2i+2p+1,3m+2)\Bigg)~\hskip 8.50012pt~

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=0}^{2i-1}s_{1}(j,3m-i+2)\cdot l(i)-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=0}^{2i-1}\sum_{k=0}^{3m-3i}s_{1}(j,3m-i-k+1)\cdot l(k+i+1).

This theorem is a higher dimensional generalization of
[
10
, Theorem 4.4]
, which deals with the case where
X
is a curve.
As in the theorem, assume that
k=1
or
2
and
L
is sufficiently positive. Let
B^{k+1}=B^{k+1}(L):=\mathbb{P}(E_{k+1,L})
. Note that
H^{i}(X^{[k+1]},S^{\ell}E_{k+1,L})=H^{i}(B^{k+1},\mathcal{O}_{B^{k+1}}(\ell))
for
i\geq 0
and
\ell\geq 1
. The complete linear system
\lvert\mathcal{O}_{B^{k+1}}(1)\rvert
induces a birational morphism
\alpha_{k+1}\colon B^{k+1}\to\Sigma_{k}
, which is a resolution of singularities. We review basic facts on secant varieties in Section
2
. If one can determine the higher direct images
R^{i}\alpha_{k+1,*}\mathcal{O}_{B^{k+1}}
explicitly, then Theorem
1.1
follows from the Leray spectral sequence for
\alpha_{k+1}
together with known results for the cohomology of secant varieties. This is precisely what we do in Section
3
. We also briefly discuss in Remark
3.2
how to extend Theorem
1.1
when
X
is a surface and
k
is arbitrary. The idea of computing the cohomology groups of symmetric powers of the tautological bundles via secant varieties was already explored in
[
10
, Section 4]
in the case where
X
is a curve. In that case, since
\deg P_{\Sigma_{k}}(\ell)=\dim\Sigma_{k}=2k+1
, the result is obtained by applying Lagrange interpolation after computing
P_{\Sigma_{k}}(\ell)
for
-k\leq\ell\leq k+1
. These values can be derived rather easily from the descriptions of
R^{i}\alpha_{k+1,*}\mathcal{O}_{B^{k+1}}
. However, in the higher dimensional cases, we need a new strategy (we instead compute
P_{\Sigma_{k}}(km+k-1)
for all
m\geq 0
), and the arguments become substantially more involved. See Section
4
for the details. Finally, we note that the degrees of
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}
were known before (
[
11
, Corollary 8.2.9]
and
[
4
, Theorem 1.2]
, respectively). Our result may be regarded as a far-reaching generalization of those work.

2.
Preliminaries

We begin with collecting necessary facts on Hilbert schemes of points and secant varieties. We closely follow the notations in
[
3
]
, and we refer to that paper and the references therein for further details. Throughout the paper,
X
is a smooth projective complex variety of dimension
n
, and
L
is a sufficiently positive line bundle on
X
giving an embedding
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}H^{0}(X,L)=\mathbb{P}^{r}
.

2.1.
Hilbert schemes of points

The
Hilbert scheme

X^{[k]}:=\{\xi\subseteq X\mid\dim\xi=0,\operatorname{length}(\xi)=k\}
of
k
points on
X
is smooth if and only if
n\leq 2
or
k\leq 3
(see
[
2
]
). In this paper, we always assume that
k\leq 3
so that
X^{[k]}
is a smooth projective complex variety of dimension
kn
. For a zero-dimensional subscheme
\xi\subseteq X
of length
k
, we denote by
[\xi]
the point of
X^{[k]}
corresponding to
\xi
. The universal family

\mathcal{Z}_{k}:=\{(x,[\xi])\in X\times X^{[k]}\mid x\in\xi\}\subseteq X\times X^{[k]}

over
X^{[k]}
has rational singularities (see
[
3
, §1.2.4]
). Let

\textrm{pr}_{1}\colon\mathcal{Z}_{k}\to X
and
\textrm{pr}_{2}\colon\mathcal{Z}_{k}\to X^{[k]}
be the projection maps, and
E_{k,L}:=\textrm{pr}_{2,*}\textrm{pr}_{1}^{*}L
be the
tautological bundle
associated to
L
, which is a rank
k
vector bundle on
X^{[k]}
since
\textrm{pr}_{2}
is a finite flat morphism of degree
k
.
Note that
H^{0}(X^{[k]},E_{k,L})=H^{0}(X,L)
and the fiber of
E_{k,L}
over
[\xi]\in X^{[k]}
can be naturally identified with
H^{0}(\xi,L|_{\xi})
. In particular,
E_{k,L}
is globally generated whenever
L
is
(k-1)
-very ample, i.e.,
H^{0}(X,L)\to H^{0}(\xi,L|_{\xi})
is surjective for every
[\xi]\in X^{[k]}
.

Next, we consider the
nested Hilbert scheme

X^{[k-1,k]}:=\{([\eta],[\xi])\in X^{[k-1]}\times X^{[k]}\mid\eta\subseteq\xi\}\subseteq X^{[k-1]}\times X^{[k]}

with projection maps
\tau\colon X^{[k-1,k]}\to X^{[k-1]}
and
\rho\colon X^{[k-1,k]}\to X^{[k]}
. There is a residual morphism
\mathrm{res}\colon X^{[k-1,k]}\to X
sending
([\eta],[\xi])
to
(\mathscr{I}_{\xi}:\mathscr{I}_{\eta})
. When
n\leq 2
or
k\leq 3
, it is also known that
X^{[k-1,k]}
is a smooth projective complex variety of dimension
kn
(see
[
2
]
). The morhpism
\rho\colon X^{[k-1,k]}\to X^{[k]}
is generically finite and factors through the universal family
\mathcal{Z}_{k}
. The morphism
X^{[k-1,k]}\to\mathcal{Z}_{k}
is a resolution of singularities. On the other hand,
X^{[k-1,k]}
can be obtained by the blow-up
\textrm{bl}\colon X^{[k-1,k]}\to X\times X^{[k-1]}
along the universal family
\mathcal{Z}_{k-1}
with exceptional divisor
F_{k-1}
. Then
\mathrm{res}=\textrm{pr}_{1}\circ\textrm{bl}
and
\tau=\textrm{pr}_{2}\circ\textrm{bl}
, where
\textrm{pr}_{1}\colon X\times X^{[k-1]}\to X
and
\textrm{pr}_{2}\colon X\times X^{[k-1]}\to X^{[k-1]}
are projection maps. Notice that
\mathcal{Z}_{2}=X^{[1,2]}
is obtained by the blow-up of
X\times X
along the diagonal
\Delta\subseteq X\times X
. We have a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathrm{res}^{*}L(-F_{k-1})\longrightarrow\rho^{*}E_{k,L}\longrightarrow\tau^{*}E_{k-1,L}\longrightarrow 0.

Now, we consider the
Hilbert–Chow morphism

h_{k}\colon X^{[k]}\to X^{(k)}
. The
k
-th
symmetric product

X^{(k)}
is obtained by the quotient of the ordinary
k
-th product
X^{k}
by the symmetric group action
\mathfrak{S}_{k}
permuting the components. The line bundle
L^{\boxtimes k}
on
X^{k}
descends to a line bundle
S_{k,L}
on
X^{(k)}
. We denote by
T_{k,L}:=h_{k}^{*}S_{k,L}
. Then there exists a divisor
\delta_{k}
on
X^{[k]}
such that
N_{k,L}:=\det E_{k,L}=T_{k,L}(-\delta_{k})
. Notice that
2\delta_{k}
is the exceptional divisor of
h_{k}
when
n\geq 2
. We set
A_{k,L}:=T_{k,L}(-2\delta_{k})
. When
k=1
, we have
\delta_{1}=0
and
E_{1,L}=T_{1,L}=N_{1,L}=A_{1,L}=L
. We refer to
[
3
, §2.3.2]
for basic properties of these line bundles on
X^{[k]}
. Here we recall that
\rho^{*}T_{k,L}=\tau^{*}T_{k-1,L}\otimes\mathrm{res}^{*}L
and
\rho^{*}\delta_{k}=\tau^{*}\delta_{k-1}+F_{k-1}
. We can also compute the canonical line bundles
\omega_{X^{[k]}}=T_{k,\omega_{X}}((n-2)\delta_{k})
and
\omega_{X^{[k-1,k]}}=(\tau^{*}\omega_{X^{[k-1]}}\otimes\mathrm{res}^{*}\omega_{X})((n-1)F_{k-1})
(see
[
3
, §1.1.7 and §1.2.3]
). Notice that
F_{k-1}
is the ramification divisor of
\rho
and
\omega_{X^{[k-1,k]}}=\rho^{*}\omega_{X^{[k]}}(F_{k-1})
.

Finally, we show the following lemma, which is certainly known to experts.

Lemma 2.1
.

We have

H^{i}(X^{[2]},\mathcal{O}_{X^{[2]}})=\Bigg(\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}p+q=i\\
p<q\end{subarray}}H^{p}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})\otimes H^{q}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})\Bigg)\oplus\begin{cases}S^{2}H^{i/2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})&\text{if $i$ is even and $i/2$ is even}\\
\wedge^{2}H^{i/2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})&\text{if $i$ is even and $i/2$ is odd}\\
0&\text{$i$ is odd}.\end{cases}

Proof.

As
X^{(2)}=X^{2}/\mathfrak{S}_{2}
has rational singularities and
h_{2}\colon X^{[2]}\to X^{(2)}
is a resolution of singularities,
H^{i}(X^{[2]},\mathcal{O}_{X^{[2]}})=H^{i}(X^{(2)},\mathcal{O}_{X^{(2)}})
for
i\geq 0
. Note that
\mathcal{O}_{X^{(2)}}=S_{2,\mathcal{O}_{X}}
is obtained by descending
\mathcal{O}_{X^{2}}
. As in
[
3
, Lemma 5.25]
,
H^{i}(X^{(2)},\mathcal{O}_{X^{(2)}})
can be computed by analyzing
\mathfrak{S}_{2}
-action on the Künneth decomposition of
H^{i}(X^{2},\mathcal{O}_{X^{2}})=\bigoplus_{p+q=i}H^{p}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})\oplus H^{q}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
.
∎

2.2.
Secant varieties

We keep assuming
k=2
or
3
so that
X^{[k]}
and
X^{[k-1,k]}
are smooth. Most of the results still holds when
n\leq 2
or
k\leq 3
. Let
B^{k}=B^{k}(L):=\mathbb{P}(E_{k+1,L})
with canonical projection
\pi_{k}\colon B^{k}\to X^{[k]}
, and
H_{k}
be the tautological divisor on
B^{k}
so that
\mathcal{O}_{B^{k}}(H_{k})=\mathcal{O}_{B^{k}}(1)
. As
E_{k,L}
is globally generated, the tautological line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{B^{k}}(1)
is base point free. Note that
H^{0}(B^{k},\mathcal{O}_{B^{k}}(1))=H^{0}(X^{[k]},E_{k,L})=H^{0}(X,L)
. Thus the complete linear system
\lvert\mathcal{O}_{B^{k}}(1)\rvert
gives a morhpism
\alpha_{k}\colon B^{k}\to\mathbb{P}H^{0}(X,L)=\mathbb{P}^{r}
whose image is the
k
-secant variety

\sigma_{k}=\sigma_{k}(X,L):=\overline{\bigcup_{[\xi]\in X^{[k]}}\langle\xi\rangle}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r},

which is nothing but the
(k-1)
-th secant variety
\Sigma_{k-1}=\Sigma_{k-1}(X,L)
. Two different conventions for secant varieties are both widely used in the literature. From now on, following
[
3
]
, we use the notation of
k
-secant variety
\sigma_{k}
simply because it is more convenient for maintaining consistent indexing. Note that
\alpha_{k}\colon B^{k}\to\sigma_{k}
is a birational morphism hence a resolution of singularities and
\sigma_{k}
is a projective variety with
\dim\sigma_{k}=kn+k-1
. We have
\operatorname{Sing}(\sigma_{k})=\sigma_{k-1}
(see
[
3
, Corollary E]
). Here, recall some of the main results of
[
3
]
(see also
[
6
]
and
[
18
]
for the case of
k=2
).

Theorem 2.2

(
[
3
, Theorems 5.3 and 5.4]
)
.

For
k=2
or
3
, we have the following:

(1)

\sigma_{k}
has normal singularities, and
\sigma_{k}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
is projectively normal.

(2)

H^{i}(\sigma_{k},\mathscr{I}_{\sigma_{k-1}/\sigma_{k}}(\ell))=H^{i}(\sigma_{k},\mathcal{O}_{\sigma_{k}}(\ell))=0
for
i>0
and
\ell>0
.

(3)

\alpha_{k,*}\mathcal{O}_{B^{k}}(-Z_{k-1}^{k})=\mathscr{I}_{\sigma_{k-1}/\sigma_{k}}
and
R^{i}\alpha_{k,*}\mathcal{O}_{B^{k}}(-Z_{k-1}^{k})=0
for
i>0
.

Remark 2.3
.

In this paper, the assumption that the embedding line bundle
L
is sufficiently positive is needed only when we apply Theorem
2.2
. Apart from this, most of the results introduced in this section only require that
L
is
(2k-1)
-very ample. Recently, the authors of the present paper have proved that this theorem holds when
L=\omega_{X}\otimes A^{m}\otimes B
with
m\geq 2n+2
for
k=2
and
m\geq 3n+3
for
k=3
, where
A
is very ample and
B
is nef (see
[
5
]
). Therefore, the main results of this paper (Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
) remain valid under this assumption.

B^{k-1,k}=B^{k-1,k}(L):=\mathbb{P}(\tau^{*}E_{k-1,L})
with canonical projection
\pi_{k-1,k}\colon B^{k-1,k}\to X^{[k]}
. We have a commutative diagram

\textstyle{B^{k-1,k}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

On the other hand, from the surjection
\rho^{*}E_{k,L}\to\tau^{*}E_{k-1,L}
, we get a morphism

\alpha_{k-1,k}\colon B^{k-1,k}\to B^{k}
whose image is denoted by

. Note that
\alpha_{k-1,k}\colon B^{k-1,k}\to Z_{k-1}^{k}
is a birational morphism hence a resolution of singularities and

. By

\mathcal{O}_{B^{3}}(-Z_{k-1}^{k})=\mathcal{O}_{B^{k}}(-k)\otimes\pi_{k}^{*}A_{k,L}

\textstyle{B^{k-1,k}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Note that
\alpha_{k}\circ\alpha_{k-1,k}=\alpha_{k-1}\circ\widetilde{\tau}
and
\alpha_{k}(\alpha_{k-1,k}(B^{k-1,k}))=\alpha_{k}(Z_{k-1}^{k})=\sigma_{k-1}
.
When
k=2
, we have
\mathcal{Z}_{2}=X^{[1,2]}=B^{1,2}=Z_{1}^{2}
, which is the universal family over
X^{[2]}
via
\pi_{2}
, and the restricted morphism
\alpha_{2}\colon Z_{1}^{2}\to X
coincides with the residual morhpism
\mathrm{res}\colon X^{[1,2]}\to X
. For
k=3
, consider the divisor
\widetilde{\tau}^{*}Z_{1}^{2}
on
B^{2,3}
. Let
Z_{1}^{3}:=\alpha_{2,3}(\widetilde{\tau}^{*}Z_{1}^{2})
. Then
\alpha_{3}(Z_{1}^{3})=X
and
Z_{1}^{3}=\alpha_{3}^{-1}(X)
. Note that
Z_{1}^{3}=\mathcal{Z}_{3}
is the universal family over
X^{[3]}
via
\pi_{3}
and the restricted morphism
\alpha_{3}\colon Z_{1}^{3}\to X
coincides with the the projection map
\textrm{pr}_{1}\colon\mathcal{Z}_{3}\to X
. Since
Z_{2}^{3}
is a prime divisor on a smooth variety
B^{3}
, it is Gorenstein. We have
\omega_{B^{2,3}}(\widetilde{\tau}^{*}Z_{1}^{2})=\alpha_{2,3}^{*}\omega_{Z_{2}^{3}}
. Observe that
\alpha_{2,3}\colon B^{2,3}\setminus\widetilde{\tau}^{*}Z_{1}^{2}\to Z_{2}^{3}\setminus Z_{1}^{3}
is an isomorphism and
\alpha_{2,3}\colon\widetilde{\tau}^{*}Z_{1}^{2}\to Z_{1}^{3}
is a generically 2-to-1 map. It is easy to show that
\alpha_{2,3,*}\mathcal{O}_{B^{2,3}}(-\widetilde{\tau}^{*}Z_{1}^{2})\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{Z_{2}^{3}}
is injective and the subscheme of
Z_{2}^{3}
defined by
\alpha_{2,3,*}\mathcal{O}_{B^{2,3}}(-\widetilde{\tau}^{*}Z_{1}^{2})
is generically reduced. But
\alpha_{2,3,*}\mathcal{O}_{B^{2,3}}(-\widetilde{\tau}^{*}Z_{1}^{2})
and
\mathcal{O}_{Z_{2}^{3}}
are Cohen–Macaulay, so
\alpha_{2,3,*}\mathcal{O}_{B^{2,3}}(-\widetilde{\tau}^{*}Z_{1}^{2})=\mathscr{I}_{Z_{1}^{3}/Z_{2}^{3}}
since the cosupport of

Using Kawamata–Viehweg vanishing theorem, one can easily check

. We refer to

for more details on

and

Proof.

(1)

(2)

4.
Hilbert polynomials of secant varieties

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.2

L

X

4.1.
Hilbert polynomial of
\sigma_{2}

We want to compute
P_{\sigma_{2}}(\ell)=\chi(\mathcal{O}_{\sigma_{2}}(\ell))
for
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
. We begin with the short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathscr{I}_{X/\sigma_{2}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\sigma_{2}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}\longrightarrow 0.

We find

\chi(\mathcal{O}_{\sigma_{2}}(\ell))=\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X}(\ell))+\chi(\mathscr{I}_{X/\sigma_{2}}(\ell)).

By Theorem
2.2
(3), we have

\chi(\mathscr{I}_{X/\sigma_{2}}(\ell))=\chi(\mathcal{O}_{B^{2}}(\ell H_{2}-Z_{1}^{2})).

Recall that
\mathcal{O}_{B^{2}}(-Z_{1}^{2})=\mathcal{O}_{B^{2}}(-2)\otimes\pi_{2}^{*}A_{2,L}
. For an integer
i\geq 1
, we have a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{B^{2}}(\ell H_{2}-(i+1)Z_{1}^{2})\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{B^{2}}(\ell H_{2}-iZ_{1}^{2})\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{Z_{1}^{2}}(\ell H_{2}-iZ_{1}^{2}).\longrightarrow 0.

Now, assume
\ell=2m+1
for some integer
m\geq 0
. As
\mathcal{O}_{B^{2}}(\ell H_{2}-(m+1)Z_{1}^{2})=\mathcal{O}_{B^{3}}(-1)\otimes\pi_{2}^{*}A_{2,L}^{m+1}
, we get
\chi(\mathcal{O}_{B^{2}}(\ell H_{2}-(m+1)Z_{1}^{2}))=0
. Considering the above short exact sequences for
i=1,\ldots,m
, we see that

\chi(\mathcal{O}_{B^{2}}(\ell H_{2}-Z_{1}^{2}))=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\chi(\mathcal{O}_{Z_{1}^{2}}(\ell H_{2}-iZ_{1}^{2})).

Recall that
Z_{1}^{2}=X^{[1,2]}
. Then we may write

\mathcal{O}_{Z_{1}^{2}}(\ell H_{2}-iZ_{1}^{2})=\rho^{*}A_{2,L}^{i}\otimes\mathrm{res}^{*}L^{\ell-2i}=(\tau^{*}L^{i}\otimes\mathrm{res}^{*}L^{\ell-i})(-2iF_{1}).

By Lemma
4.1
shown below, we have

\chi\big((\tau^{*}L^{i}\otimes\mathrm{res}^{*}L^{\ell-i})(-2iF_{1})\big)=\chi(L^{\ell-i})\cdot\chi(L^{i})-\sum_{j=0}^{2i-1}\chi(S^{j}\Omega_{X}\otimes L^{\ell}).

Combining all together, we obtain

\chi(\mathcal{O}_{\sigma_{2}}(2m+1))=\chi(L^{2m+1})+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(\chi(L^{2m-i+1})\cdot\chi(L^{i})-\sum_{j=0}^{2i-1}\chi(S^{j}\Omega_{X}\otimes L^{2m+1})\right)

for
m\geq 0
. Note that
P_{\sigma_{2}}(\ell)=\chi(\mathcal{O}_{\sigma_{2}}(\ell))
is a polynomial in
\ell
of degree
2n+1=\dim\sigma_{2}
. By Lagrange interpolation,

P_{\sigma_{2}}(\ell)=\sum_{m=0}^{2n+1}P_{\sigma_{2}}(2m+1)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}t=0\\
t\neq m\end{subarray}}^{2n+1}\frac{\ell-2t-1}{2m-2t}

for any integer
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
. To finish the proof of Theorem
1.2

(1)
, it only remain to show the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
F,G
be vector bundles on
X
.
For integers
a,b,c\geq 0
, we have

\chi\big((\tau^{*}(L^{a}\otimes F)\otimes\mathrm{res}^{*}(L^{b}\otimes G))(-cF_{1})\big)=\chi(L^{a}\otimes F)\cdot\chi(L^{b}\otimes G)-\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\chi(S^{i}\Omega_{X}\otimes F\otimes G\otimes L^{a+b}).

Proof.

Recall that
X^{[1,2]}
is obtained by the blow-up
\textrm{bl}\colon X^{[1,2]}\to X\times X
along the diagonal
\Delta\subseteq X\times X
with exceptional divisor
F_{1}
. We have

(\tau^{*}(L^{a}\otimes F)\otimes\mathrm{res}^{*}(L^{b}\otimes G))(-cF_{1})=\textrm{bl}^{*}((L^{b}\otimes F)\boxtimes(L^{a}\otimes G))(-cF_{1}).

For an integer
i\geq 0
, we have a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X^{[1,2]}}(-(i+1)F_{1})\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X^{[1,2]}}(-iF_{1})\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{F_{1}}(-iF_{i})\longrightarrow 0.

Notice that
\textrm{bl}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{[1,2]}}(-iF_{1})=\mathscr{I}_{\Delta/X^{2}}^{i}
and
\textrm{bl}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{F_{1}}(-iF_{1})=S^{i}N^{\vee}_{\Delta/X^{2}}=S^{i}\Omega_{X}
. Clearly,
R^{j}\textrm{bl}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{[1,2]}}(-iF_{1})=0
and
R^{j}\textrm{bl}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{F_{1}}(-iF_{1})=0
for
j>0
and
i\geq 0
. Considering the above short exact sequences for
i=0,\ldots,c-1
after tensoring by
\textrm{bl}^{*}((L^{b}\otimes F)\boxtimes(L^{a}\otimes G))
, we see that

\chi\big(\textrm{bl}^{*}((L^{b}\otimes G)\boxtimes(L^{a}\otimes F))(-cF_{1})\big)=\chi((L^{b}\otimes G)\boxtimes(L^{a}\otimes F))-\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\chi(S^{i}\Omega_{X}\otimes F\otimes G\otimes L^{a+b}).

Finally, notice that
\chi((L^{b}\otimes G)\boxtimes(L^{a}\otimes F))=\chi(L^{b}\otimes G)\cdot\chi(L^{a}\otimes F)
by the Künneth formula.
∎

4.2.
Hilbert polynomial of
\sigma_{3}

We want to compute
P_{\sigma_{3}}(\ell)=\chi(\mathcal{O}_{\sigma_{3}}(\ell))
for
\ell\in\mathbf{Z}
. The basic strategy is the same as in the case of
\sigma_{2}
, but the computation becomes considerably more complicated. Rather than presenting every detailed step of the calculation, we focus on the essential ideas and key steps of the argument. The omitted part should be manageable for the reader. As before, we start by considering the short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{I}_{\sigma_{2}/\sigma_{3}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\sigma_{3}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\sigma_{2}}\longrightarrow 0.

We find

\chi(\mathcal{O}_{\sigma_{3}}(\ell))=\chi(\mathcal{O}_{\sigma_{2}}(\ell))+\chi(\mathscr{I}_{\sigma_{2}/\sigma_{3}}(\ell))=P_{\sigma_{2}}(\ell)+\chi(\mathscr{I}_{\sigma_{2}/\sigma_{3}}(\ell)).

By Theorem
2.2
(3), we have

\chi(\mathscr{I}_{\sigma_{2}/\sigma_{3}}(\ell))=\chi(\mathcal{O}_{B^{3}}(\ell H_{3}-Z_{2}^{3})).

Recall that
\mathcal{O}_{B^{3}}(-Z_{2}^{3})=\mathcal{O}_{B^{3}}(-3)\otimes\pi_{3}^{*}A_{3,L}
. For an integer
i\geq 1
, we have a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{B^{3}}(\ell H_{3}-(i+1)Z_{2}^{3})\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{B^{3}}(\ell H_{3}-iZ_{2}^{3})\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{Z_{2}^{3}}(\ell H_{3}-iZ_{2}^{3})\longrightarrow 0.

Now, assume
\ell=3m+2
for some integer
m\geq 0
. As
\mathcal{O}_{B^{3}}(\ell H_{3}-(m+1)Z_{2}^{3})=\mathcal{O}_{B^{3}}(-1)\otimes\pi_{3}^{*}A_{3,L}^{m+1}
, we get
\chi(\mathcal{O}_{B^{3}}(\ell H_{3}-(m+1)Z_{2}^{3}))=0
. Considering the above short exact sequences for

\chi(\mathcal{O}_{B^{3}}(\ell H_{3}-Z_{2}^{3}))=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\chi(\mathcal{O}_{Z_{2}^{3}}(\ell H_{3}-iZ_{2}^{3})).

Next, from the short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\alpha_{2,3,*}\mathcal{O}_{B^{2,3}}(-\tilde{\tau}^{*}Z_{1}^{2})\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{Z_{2}^{3}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{Z_{1}^{3}}\longrightarrow 0,

we get

\chi(\mathcal{O}_{Z_{2}^{3}}(\ell H_{3}-iZ_{2}^{3}))=\chi(\alpha_{2,3,*}\mathcal{O}_{B^{2,3}}(-\tilde{\tau}^{*}Z_{1}^{2})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Z_{2}^{3}}(\ell H_{3}-iZ_{2}^{3}))+\chi(\mathcal{O}_{Z_{1}^{3}}(\ell H_{3}-iZ_{2}^{3})).

Note that

\displaystyle\chi(\alpha_{2,3,*}\mathcal{O}_{B^{2,3}}(-\tilde{\tau}^{*}Z_{1}^{2})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Z_{2}^{3}}(\ell H_{3}-iZ_{2}^{3})))=\chi\big((\tau^{*}(S^{\ell-3i-2}E_{2,L}\otimes A_{2,L}^{i+1})\otimes\mathrm{res}^{*}L^{i})(-2iF_{2})\big);

\displaystyle\chi(\mathcal{O}_{Z_{1}^{3}}(\ell H_{3}-iZ_{2}^{3}))=\chi\big((\tau^{*}A_{2,L}^{i}\otimes\mathrm{res}^{*}L^{\ell-2i})(-2iF_{2})\big).

The Euler characteristics on the right-hand side are defined on
X^{[2,3]}
. For an integer
j\geq 0
, we have a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X^{[2,3]}}(-(j+1)F_{2})\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X^{[2,3]}}(-jF_{2})\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{F_{2}}(-jF_{2})\longrightarrow 0.

Recall that there is the blow-up
\textrm{bl}\colon X^{[2,3]}\to X\times X^{[2]}
along
\mathcal{Z}_{2}=X^{[1,2]}
with exceptional divisor
F_{2}
. Let
N:=N_{\mathcal{Z}_{2}/X\times X^{[2]}}
be the normal bundle of
\mathcal{Z}_{2}
in
X\times X^{[2]}
.
Considering the above short exact sequence for
j=0,\ldots,2i-1
after tesnsoring by relevant vector bundles on
X^{[2,3]}
, we see that

\displaystyle\chi\big((\tau^{*}(S^{\ell-3i-2}E_{2,L}\otimes A_{2,L}^{i+1})\otimes\mathrm{res}^{*}L^{i})(-2iF_{2})\big)

\displaystyle=\chi(S^{\ell-3i-2}E_{2,L}\otimes A_{2,L}^{i+1})\cdot\chi(L^{i})-\sum_{j=0}^{2i-1}\chi(S^{j}N^{\vee}\otimes\rho^{*}(S^{\ell-3i-2}E_{2,L}\otimes A_{2,L}^{i+1})\otimes\mathrm{res}^{*}L^{i});

\displaystyle\chi\big((\tau^{*}A_{2,L}^{i}\otimes\mathrm{res}^{*}L^{\ell-2i})(-2iF_{2})\big)=\chi(A_{2,L}^{i})\cdot\chi(L^{\ell-2i})-\sum_{j=0}^{2i-1}\chi(S^{j}N^{\vee}\otimes(\tau^{*}L^{i}\otimes\mathrm{res}^{*}L^{\ell-i})(-2iF_{1})).

The Euler characteristics on the right-end terms are defined on
X^{[1,2]}
.
From the suitable symmetric powers of the short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\tau^{*}L(-F_{1})\longrightarrow\rho^{*}E_{2,L}\longrightarrow\mathrm{res}^{*}L\longrightarrow 0,

we deduce that

\chi(S^{j}N^{\vee}\otimes\rho^{*}(S^{\ell-3i-2}E_{2,L}\otimes A_{2,L}^{i+1})\otimes\mathrm{res}^{*}L^{i})=\sum_{k=0}^{\ell-3i-2}\chi\big(S^{j}N^{\vee}\otimes(\tau^{*}L^{k+i+1}\otimes\mathrm{res}^{*}L^{\ell-i-k-1})(-(k+2i+2)F_{1})\big).

Putting together all the results obtained so far, we have

\displaystyle P_{\sigma_{3}}(3m+2)

\displaystyle=P_{\sigma_{2}}(3m+2)+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\Big(\chi(S^{3m-3i}E_{2,L}\otimes A_{2,L}^{i+1})\cdot\chi(L^{i})+\chi(A_{2,L}^{i})\cdot\chi(L^{3m-2i+2})\Big)

\displaystyle~-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=0}^{2i-1}\chi\big(S^{j}N^{\vee}\otimes(\tau^{*}L^{i}\otimes\mathrm{res}^{*}L^{3m-i+2})(-2iF_{1})\big)

\displaystyle~-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=0}^{2i-1}\sum_{k=0}^{3m-3i}\chi\big(S^{j}N^{\vee}\otimes(\tau^{*}L^{k+i+1}\otimes\mathrm{res}^{*}L^{3m-i-k+1})(-(k+2i+2)F_{1})\big).

for
m\geq 0
.
In the end, it remains to compute
\chi(S^{a}E_{2,L}\otimes A_{2,L}^{b})
and
\chi(S^{a}N^{\vee}\otimes(\tau^{*}L^{b}\otimes\mathrm{res}^{*}L^{c})(-dF_{1}))
. Finally, Theorem
1.2

(2)
follows from the next two lemmas, which complete the remaining tasks, combined with Lagrange interpolation.

Lemma 4.2
.

For integers
a,b\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\chi(S^{a}E_{2,L}\otimes A_{2,L}^{b})

\displaystyle=P_{\sigma_{2}}(a+2b)+\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k}h^{k}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})\cdot\chi(L^{a+2b})-\sum_{i=1}^{b}\Bigg(\chi(L^{b-i})\cdot\chi(L^{a+b+i})-\sum_{j=0}^{2(b-i)-1}\chi(S^{j}\Omega_{X}\otimes L^{a+2b})\Bigg).

Proof.

Note that
\chi(S^{a}E_{2,L}\otimes A_{2,L}^{b})=\chi(\mathcal{O}_{B^{2}}(a)\otimes\pi_{2}^{*}A_{2,L}^{b})
. As
\mathcal{O}_{B^{2}}(-Z_{1}^{2})=\mathcal{O}_{B^{2}}(-2)\otimes\pi_{2}^{*}A_{2,L}
and
Z_{1}^{2}=X^{[1,2]}
, we have a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{B^{2}}(a+2i-2)\otimes\pi_{2}^{*}A_{2,L}^{b-i+1}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{B_{2}}(a+2i)\otimes\pi_{2}^{*}A_{2,L}^{b-i}\longrightarrow(\tau^{*}L^{b-i}\otimes\mathrm{res}^{*}L^{a+b+i})(-2(b-i)F_{1})\longrightarrow 0,

for
i=1,\ldots,b
. We find

\chi(\mathcal{O}_{B^{2}}(a)\otimes\pi_{2}^{*}A_{2,L}^{b})=\chi(\mathcal{O}_{B^{2}}(a+2b))-\sum_{i=1}^{b}\chi\big((\tau^{*}L^{b-i}\otimes\mathrm{res}^{*}L^{a+b+i})(-2(b-i)F_{1})\big).

Now, by Theorem
1.1

(1)
,

\chi(\mathcal{O}_{B^{2}}(a+2b))=\chi(S^{a+2b}E_{2,L})=P_{\sigma_{2}}(a+2b)+\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k}h^{k}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})\cdot\chi(L^{a+2b}),

and by Lemma
4.1
,

\chi\big((\tau^{*}L^{b-i}\otimes\mathrm{res}^{*}L^{a+b+i})(-2(b-i)F_{1})\big)=\chi(L^{b-i})\cdot\chi(L^{a+b+i})-\sum_{j=0}^{2(b-i)-1}\chi(S^{j}\Omega_{X}\otimes L^{a+2b}).\qed

Lemma 4.3
.

For integers
a,b,c,d\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\chi(S^{a}N^{\vee}\otimes(\tau^{*}L^{b}\otimes\mathrm{res}^{*}L^{c})(-dF_{1}))

\displaystyle=\chi(S^{a}\Omega_{X}\otimes L^{c})\cdot\chi(L^{b})-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\chi(S^{a}\Omega_{X}\otimes S^{d-i}\Omega_{X}\otimes L^{b+c})-\sum_{j=1}^{a}\chi(S^{a-j}\Omega_{X}\otimes S^{d+2j-1}\Omega_{X}\otimes L^{b+c}).

Proof.

Recall that
N^{\vee}=N_{X^{[1,2]}/X\times X^{[2]}}^{\vee}
is the conormal bundle of
X^{[1,2]}
in
X\times X^{[2]}
.
We have two short exact sequences

0\longrightarrow N^{\vee}\longrightarrow\mathrm{res}^{*}\Omega_{X}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{F_{1}}(-F_{1})\longrightarrow 0~\text{ and }~0\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X^{[1,2]}}(-2F_{1})\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X^{[1,2]}}(-F_{1})\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{F_{1}}(-F_{1})\longrightarrow 0.

The left short exact sequence can be derived from the fact that
F_{1}
is the ramification divisor of
\rho\colon X^{[1,2]}\to X^{[2]}
so that there is a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\rho^{*}\Omega_{X^{[2]}}\longrightarrow\Omega_{X^{[1,2]}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{F_{1}}(-F_{1})\longrightarrow 0.

We have

[\mathrm{res}^{*}\Omega_{X}]+[\mathcal{O}_{X^{[1,2]}}(-2F_{1})]=[N^{\vee}]+[\mathcal{O}_{X^{[1,2]}}(-F_{1})]

in the Grothendieck group
K_{0}(X^{[1,2]})
. Note that
K_{0}(X^{[1,2]})
is
\lambda
-ring (we refer to
[
12
, Chapter I]
for some details on
\lambda
-rings). Following
[
12
, Chapter I, §1]
, for a locally free sheaf
F
on
X^{[1,2]}
, we set

\lambda_{t}(F):=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}[\wedge^{i}F]t^{i}\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma_{t}(F):=\lambda_{-t}(F)^{-1}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}[S^{i}F]t^{i}.

The last equality on the right-hand side holds by
[
12
, Corollary 2.4 in Chapter V]
. Then

\sigma_{t}(\mathcal{O}_{X^{[1,2]}}(-F_{1}))=\frac{1}{1-[\mathcal{O}_{X^{[1,2]}}(-F_{1})]t}~~\text{ and }~~\sigma_{t}(\mathcal{O}_{X^{[1,2]}}(-2F_{1}))=\frac{1}{1-[\mathcal{O}_{X^{[1,2]}}(-2F_{1})]t}\;.

From the following relation

\sigma_{t}(N^{\vee})\cdot\sigma_{t}(\mathcal{O}_{X^{[1,2]}}(-F_{1}))=\sigma_{t}(\mathrm{res}^{*}\Omega_{X})\cdot\sigma_{t}(\mathcal{O}_{X^{[1,2]}}(-2F_{1})),

we deduce that

\sigma_{t}(N^{\vee})=\sigma_{t}(\mathrm{res}^{*}\Omega_{X})\cdot\frac{1-[\mathcal{O}_{X^{[1,2]}}(-F_{1})]t}{1-[\mathcal{O}_{X^{[1,2]}}(-2F_{1})]t}.

Then we find

[S^{a}N^{\vee}]=[S^{a}\mathrm{res}^{*}\Omega_{X}]+\sum_{i=1}^{a}\big([\mathcal{O}_{X^{[1,2]}}(-2iF_{1})]-[\mathcal{O}_{X^{[1,2]}}(-(2i-1)F_{1})]\big)\cdot[S^{a-i}\mathrm{res}^{*}\Omega_{X}].

Considering the Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\chi(S^{a}N^{\vee}\otimes(\tau^{*}L^{b}\otimes\mathrm{res}^{*}L^{c})(-dF_{1}))=\chi\big((\tau^{*}L^{b}\otimes\mathrm{res}^{*}(S^{a}\Omega_{X}\otimes L^{c}))(-dF_{1})\big)

\displaystyle~\quad~+\sum_{i=1}^{a}\chi\big((\tau^{*}L^{b}\otimes\mathrm{res}^{*}(S^{a-i}\Omega_{X}\otimes L^{c}))(-(d+2i)F_{1})\big)-\sum_{i=1}^{a}\chi\big((\tau^{*}L^{b}\otimes\mathrm{res}^{*}(S^{a-i}\Omega_{X}\otimes L^{c})(-(d+2i-1)F_{1})\big).

Applying Lemma
4.1
and performing some straightforward calculations for a lot of cancellations, we can derive the claimed formula.
∎
