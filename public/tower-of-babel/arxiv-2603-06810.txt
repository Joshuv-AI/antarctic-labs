Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning with Submodular Reward
Wenjing Chen, Chengyuan Qian, Shuo Xing, Yi Zhou, Victoria G. Crawford

Abstract
In this paper, we study cooperative multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) where the joint reward exhibits submodularity, which is a natural property capturing diminishing marginal returns when adding agents to a team. Unlike standard MARL with additive rewards, submodular rewards model realistic scenarios where agent contributions overlap (e.g., multi-drone surveillance, collaborative exploration). We provide the first formal framework for this setting and develop algorithms with provable guarantees on sample efficiency and regret bound. For known dynamics, our greedy policy optimization achieves a 1 / 2 1/2 -approximation with polynomial complexity in the number of agents K K , overcoming the exponential curse of dimensionality inherent in joint policy optimization. For unknown dynamics, we propose a UCB-based learning algorithm achieving a 1 / 2 1/2 -regret of O ⁡ ( H 2 ​ K ​ S ​ A ​ T ) O(H^{2}KS\sqrt{AT}) over T T episodes.

Introduction

Cooperative multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for coordinating teams of interactive agents in complex environments, with applications ranging from robotic systems
[
23
,
24
]
to network optimization
[
47
]
and resource allocation
[
35
,
41
]
. In cooperative MARL, agents coordinate their actions in a stochastic environment to maximize the expected cumulative reward, which is formalized as a multi-agent Markov decision process (MDP).

A common simplifying assumption is that the joint reward observed at each time step is a
linear
(additive) function of individual agent contributions
[
28
,
22
]
. While this assumption facilitates decomposition and analysis, it fundamentally limits the expressiveness of the reward model. In many real-world collaborative tasks, an agent’s contribution to the global objective depends critically on the actions of other agents, leading to interaction effects such as overlap, redundancy, or saturation that cannot be adequately captured by additive reward structures.

Such phenomena arise naturally in a variety of collaborative decision-making problems. In multi-robot map-exploration, for instance, a team of robots explores an unknown environment to build a comprehensive map with the goal of maximizing information gain
[
29
]
. The information gained by each robot may overlap with that of others and exhibits a diminishing returns property as more robots are deployed to the search task. Similarly, in multi-drone surveillance and tracking, a team of drones monitors a large geographic area with the objective of maximizing the number of distinct objects under surveillance
[
47
]
. Here, the coverage achieved by different drones often overlaps, again resulting in diminishing returns as more agents are assigned to the task. Ignoring such overlap effects can cause agents to converge to redundant behaviors, preventing efficient exploration and undermining effective cooperation (see Figure
1
).

To model such settings, we study a natural class of cooperative MARL problems where the joint reward exhibits
submodularity
, which is a fundamental property from combinatorial optimization that captures diminishing marginal returns
[
21
,
25
]
.
Submodular functions are ubiquitous in machine learning and optimization
10
,
45
, encompassing important cases such as set cover, entropy, mutual information, and facility location objectives. This property naturally describes cooperative MARL tasks where agents’ contributions overlap, the diversity of the agents’ behavior is important, or where coverage gains saturate, and the marginal benefit of adding an agent to a smaller team is at least as large as adding it to a larger team
[
28
]
. Notably, the coverage objective in multi-drone surveillance is monotone submodular
[
47
]
, and information gain in collaborative exploration can be quantified via entropy or mutual information, both of which are submodular functions
[
29
]
.

Motivated by these considerations, we introduce Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning with Submodular Rewards (MARLS), a novel cooperative MARL framework in which agents jointly influence a monotone submodular reward function at each time step. We consider an episodic setting with shared state and action spaces, where the goal is to maximize the total expected cumulative return. Despite the structural regularity imposed by monotonicity and submodularity, MARLS presents significant algorithmic and computational challenges. First, even with the monotonicity and submodularity assumptions, finding optimal policies in MARLS remains computationally intractable: As we illustrate in Section
4.1.1
, the single-step case reduces to the NP-hard problem of submodular maximization under partition matroid constraints. Second, although the classical Bellman optimality equation (Lemma
2
)
[
5
,
39
]
provides a recursive characterization of optimal policies, directly evaluating the value function of a given stochastic policy requires time and memory exponential in the number of agents
K
. Finally, while submodular maximization has been extensively studied in static settings, extending it to sequential decision-making settings introduces substantial difficulties stemming from stochastic transition dynamics and the dependence of each agent’s marginal reward contribution on the policies and randomness of previously optimized agents. This is further amplified when the transition dynamics are unknown.

Related Work

2.1
Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning

A fundamental challenge in cooperative MARL is the exponential growth of the joint state-action space with the number of agents
K
, which often renders traditional single-agent RL methods impractical. To address such complexity, decentralized policies are typically employed
[
37
,
18
,
11
]
, where each agent selects its action conditioned only on its local action-observation history. While policy execution is decentralized, training may be centralized. Decentralized approaches focus on communication efficiency and learning coordination protocols. Another line of work addresses this scalability challenge through value decomposition methods, which impose structural constraints on the joint action-value function.
[
38
,
34
,
30
,
14
]
address scalability by imposing structural constraints such that local optima on per-agent action-values correspond to the global optimum on the joint action-value.

Some existing work in multi-agent reinforcement learning has addressed the need to capture dependency structures in real-world collaborative tasks by encouraging diverse behavior between agents
[
31
,
42
,
28
,
26
]
.
28
proposed a novel information-theoretic objective based on the mutual information between agents’ identities and trajectories, which is a special case of ours.
However, most existing methods implicitly assume that agent contributions combine in simple ways, either additively or through monotonic mixing, and do not exploit the intrinsic structure of reward functions that exhibit submodularity.

Preliminaries

3.1

5.

Then at each time step

3.3
Submodular Rewards

The key characteristic of our model is that the global reward is not additive across agents, but instead reflects diminishing returns—a property captured by submodularity.
Formally, we assume the reward is determined by a monotone submodular set function
f:2^{\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}}\to[0,1]
evaluated on the set of current agent state-action pairs:

\displaystyle r(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})=f\bigl(\{(s^{1},a^{1}),(s^{2},a^{2}),\dots,(s^{K},a^{K})\}\bigr).

(1)

where
\mathbf{s}=(s^{1},s^{2},...,s^{K})
is the joint state, and
\mathbf{a}=(a^{1},a^{2},...,a^{K})
is the joint action. Here we use the notation
\{x^{1},x^{2},...x^{n}\}
to denote the set of the elements of
x^{1},x^{2},...,x^{n}
. The definition indicates that the reward is determined by the state-action pair of each agent. Furthermore, here we assume that there exists an oracle access
f
for any subset of state-action pairs. i.e., for any
S\in 2^{\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}}
, we have oracle access to
f(S)
.

Assumption 1

(Monotone Submodular Reward)
.

We assume that for any subsets of the state-action pairs
A\subseteq B\subseteq 2^{\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}}
, and state-action pair
x
such that
x\in 2^{\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}}/B
, it follows that

\displaystyle f(A\cup\{x\})-f(A)

\displaystyle\geq f(B\cup\{x\})-f(B)

\displaystyle f(A)

\displaystyle\leq f(B).

We define the marginal gain of adding a new state-action pair
x
to a state-action pair set
A
as
\Delta f(A,x)
, i.e.,
\Delta f(A,x)=f(A\cup x)-f(A)
.

Next, we define the objective of the problem, which is to maximize the total reward obtained in an episode. To do that, we define value function
V
and
Q
as follows.
V_{h}^{\pi}(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})
is defined as the expected total reward from step

\mathbf{s}_{h}=\mathbf{s}
, and
\mathbf{a}_{h}=\mathbf{a}
.

\displaystyle V_{h}^{\pi}(\mathbf{s})=\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\Big[\sum_{t=h}^{H}r(\mathbf{s}_{t},\mathbf{a}_{t})|\mathbf{s}_{h}=\mathbf{s}\Big].

The action-value function
Q_{h}^{\pi}:\mathcal{S}^{K}\times\mathcal{A}^{K}\to\mathbb{R}
is defined analogously conditioned on taking joint action
\mathbf{a}_{h}=\mathbf{a}

Q_{h}^{\pi}(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})\coloneqq\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\Bigl[\sum_{t=h}^{H}r(\mathbf{s}_{t},\mathbf{a}_{t})\,\Big|\,\mathbf{s}_{h}=\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a}_{h}=\mathbf{a}\Bigr].

The goal is to find an optimal policy
\pi^{*}
that maximizes the expected total reward from the initial state:

\pi^{*}\in\text{argmax}_{\pi}\;V_{1}^{\pi}(\bar{\mathbf{s}}_{1}).

Next, we illustrate the above problem setup in the example of multi-robot coordination and tracking

K

\mathcal{S}
is the spatial location of the drones, and the action space
\mathcal{A}
specifies the movement actions available to each drone. Therefore, the transition dynamics of different drones are independent. The reward at time
t
is the number of distinct objects covered by the drone team. If
M

r_{t}(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\mathbb{I}(\text{object }i\text{ is captured by the agents in state $\mathbf{s}$})
, where
\mathbb{I}(\text{object }i\text{ is captured by the agents in state $\mathbf{s}$})
is an indicator function about whether the object
i
is covered by the drones in the joint state
\mathbf{s}
. This is a submodular set-cover function bounded by
[0,M]
.

In this paper, we assume the function

[0,1]

4
Main Results

In this section, we present our main algorithmic and theoretical contributions for MARLS. We begin by establishing the computational barriers inherent in the problem, then propose tractable solutions based on sequential greedy policy optimization that leverage submodularity under two settings: the planning setting (known transition dynamics) and the online learning setting (unknown transition dynamics).

4.1
Computational Challenges and Value Function Decomposition

4.1.1
Computational Challenges

The classical Bellman optimality equation for multi-agent MDPs provides a recursive characterization of general and optimal policies (See Lemma
2
in Appendix
B.1
).
However, directly solving the Bellman equation and the optimal policy, as is formulated in Equation (
2
) and (
2
) from Lemma
2
,
are computationally infeasible:
evaluating the value functions
V
and
Q
of any given stochastic policy
\pi
as in (
2
) requires
O(|\mathcal{S}|^{2K}|\mathcal{A}|^{K}H)
time and
O(|\mathcal{S}|^{K}|\mathcal{A}|^{K}H)
memory, both exponential in the number of agents
K
. This exponential complexity arises from two fundamental challenges:

1.

A general joint policy
\pi=\{\pi_{h}(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})\}_{h\in[H]}
requires
O(|\mathcal{S}|^{K}|\mathcal{A}|^{K}H)
space to store, which is exponential in
K
.

2.

The Bellman equation update also requires computation and memory complexities that are exponential to the agent number
K
.

To circumvent this curse of dimensionality, we exploit the submodularity structure of the reward function. Our goal is to propose an algorithm that outputs a policy with high expected total reward that runs in computation complexity and memory complexity both polynomial in
K
.

However, below we explain that even with the submodularity assumption, finding optimal policies in MARLS with polynomial complexity in
K
remains computationally intractable. To illustrate this, consider the single-step case (
H=1
). The value function reduces to

\displaystyle Q_{1}^{\pi}(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[r(\mathbf{s}_{1},\mathbf{a}_{1})|\mathbf{s}_{1}=\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a}_{1}=\mathbf{a}]

\displaystyle=r(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a}).

By the Bellman optimality equation, the optimal policy satisfies

\displaystyle\pi^{*}_{1}(\bar{\mathbf{s}}_{1})

\displaystyle=\arg\max_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathcal{A}^{K}}r(\bar{\mathbf{s}}_{1},\mathbf{a})

\displaystyle=\arg\max_{a^{1},\ldots,a^{K}}f\bigl(\{(\bar{s}^{1},a^{1}),\ldots,(\bar{s}^{K},a^{K})\}\bigr).

This is equivalent to submodular maximization under a partition matroid constraint, where each agent’s action space forms a partition and the constraint requires selecting at most one action per partition (see Appendix
B.2
). Since partition matroids are a special case of general matroid constraints, and submodular maximization under matroid constraints is NP-hard unless
\text{NP}\subseteq n^{\mathcal{O}(\log\log n)}
-time
[
15
]
, finding optimal policies in MARLS is computationally intractable even for
H=1
.

Fortunately, the classical greedy algorithm for monotone submodular maximization achieves a
1/2
-approximation
[
43
]
. Motivated by this, we develop a greedy sequential policy optimization approach that leverages submodular structure to achieve polynomial complexity with provable approximation guarantees.

4.1.2
Joint Policies and Reward Decomposition

To overcome the challenge of the exponential memory complexity as illustrated in
1

\{\pi_{i,h}\}_{i\in[K],h\in[H]}

\pi_{h}(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})=\prod_{i}\pi_{i,h}(s^{i},a^{i}).

Here

Following the previous convention, we use
\pi_{i}:=\{\pi_{i,h}\}_{h\in[H]}
to denote the
i

3
3

3

Note that
\pi_{i}

The joint policies of the agents are indexed by

Notice that decomposable policies admit polynomial-size representations (requiring only

To tractably optimize the global reward, we decompose the submodular function
f

[i]

\mathbf{s}
and action
\mathbf{a}

r(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a};[i])\triangleq f(\{(s^{l},a^{l})\}_{l\in[i]}),

\Delta r_{i}(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})\triangleq r(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a};[i])-r(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a};[i-1]).

Then, by telescoping, the total reward decomposes as

r(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\Delta r_{i}(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a}).

4.1.3
Marginal Value and
Q
-Functions

Given fixed policies
\pi_{[i-1]}=\{\pi_{j,h}\}_{j\in[i-1],h\in[H]}

\displaystyle V_{h}^{\pi_{i}}(s;\pi_{[i-1]})=\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{i}}\Big[{\sum_{t=h}^{H}\Delta r_{i}(\mathbf{s}_{t},\mathbf{a}_{t})}|s_{h}^{i}=s,\pi_{[i-1]}\Big]

\displaystyle Q_{h}^{\pi_{i}}(s,a;\pi_{[i-1]})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{i}}\Big[{\sum_{t=h}^{H}\Delta r_{i}(\mathbf{s}_{t},\mathbf{a}_{t})}|s_{h}^{i}=s,a_{h}^{i}=a,\pi_{[i-1]}\Big].

Lemma 1

(Bellman Equation for Marginal Gains)
.

For any policy
\pi_{i}
,
h\in[H]
,
s\in\mathcal{S}
, and

\displaystyle V_{h}^{\pi_{i}}(s;\pi_{[i-1]})

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\pi_{i,h}(s,a)Q_{h}^{\pi_{i}}(s,a;\pi_{[i-1]})

\displaystyle Q_{h}^{\pi_{i}}(s,a;\pi_{[i-1]})

\displaystyle=R_{i,h}(s,a|\pi_{[i-1]})

\displaystyle+\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}P_{i,h}(s^{\prime}|s,a)V_{h+1}^{\pi_{i}}(s^{\prime};\pi_{[i-1]}).

Moreover, let
\widetilde{\pi}_{i}=\arg\max_{\pi_{i}}\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{i}}[\sum_{t=1}^{H}\Delta r_{i}(\mathbf{s}_{t},\mathbf{a}_{t})|s_{1}^{i}=\bar{s}_{1}^{i},\pi_{[i-1]}]
. Then

\displaystyle V_{h}^{\widetilde{\pi}_{i}}(s;\pi_{[i-1]})

\displaystyle=\max_{a}Q_{h}^{\widetilde{\pi}_{i}}(s,a;\pi_{[i-1]})

\displaystyle Q_{h}^{\widetilde{\pi}_{i}}(s,a;\pi_{[i-1]})

Technical Overview

The proof of our regret bound (Appendix
D
) requires fundamentally new techniques beyond standard UCB-type analyses for single-agent reinforcement learning
[
2
,
27
]
. While our approach builds on optimistic value estimation, the multi-agent and submodular structure of MARLS introduces several fundamental challenges that necessitate new analytical tools. In particular, our analysis is different from existing work along three dimensions:
(i) establishing approximation guarantees under empirical transition dynamics,
(ii) controlling multi-agent transition estimation errors without incurring exponential dependence on the number of agents
K
and
(iii) estimating marginal rewards through an additional sampling procedure within the learning loop.

Our analysis consists of two main components.

Part I: Approximation Ratio under Empirical Dynamics
. We prove that the greedy sequential policy construction achieves a
\frac{1}{2}
-approximation on the auxiliary MDP
\widehat{M}
with transition dynamics
\widehat{\mathbf{P}}^{k}
and augmented reward
r(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})+\sum_{i=1}^{K}b(N_{i,h}^{k}(s^{i},a^{i}))
(Lemma
8
). The central challenge is that the optimal policy
\pi^{*}
may be
non-decomposable
, which means that its value function cannot be expressed as a sum of marginal gains from individual agents. To overcome this, we introduce a novel proof technique comparing two hypothetical agent groups: one following the greedy policy
\pi^{k}
and another following the optimal policy
\pi^{*}
. We carefully define the quantity
G_{i}^{k}

which captures the marginal contribution of adding agent
i
from the
\pi^{*}
group (see the definition in (
14
)). By exploiting submodularity and monotonicity, we establish
\sum_{i=1}^{K}G_{i}^{k}\geq\bar{V}_{1}^{\pi^{*},k}(\bar{\mathbf{s}}_{1})-\bar{V}_{1}^{\pi^{k},k}(\bar{\mathbf{s}}_{1})
while simultaneously bounding each
G_{i}^{k}\leq\bar{V}_{1}^{\pi_{i}^{k},k}(\bar{s}_{1}^{i};\pi_{[i-1]}^{k})+O(\epsilon/K)
. Telescoping over agents then yields the desired approximation ratio.

Part II: Regret Decomposition via Multi-Agent Telescoping and Error Isolation
. We obtain the final regret bound by recursively expanding the value gap
\bar{V}_{h}^{\pi^{k},k}(\mathbf{s}_{h}^{k})-V_{h}^{\pi^{k}}(\mathbf{s}_{h}^{k})
backward from
h=H
to
h=1
. The critical technical challenge is bounding the difference between expected values under empirical versus true transitions for non-negative functions as given in Lemma
10
and Lemma
9
. Both bounds exploit the product structure
\mathbf{P}_{h}=\prod_{i=1}^{K}P_{i,h}
via telescoping decomposition, isolating individual agent errors to avoid exponential dependence on
K
. Lemma
9
employs a more complex and refined analysis: it first establishes a coarse recursive relation
Y_{i}-Y_{i-1}\leq\frac{Y_{i-1}}{2HK}+O(\frac{\iota}{N})
through calibrated concentration inequalities, then refines to
Y_{i}\leq(1+\frac{1}{K})Y_{i-1}+O(\frac{\iota}{N})
for the tight bound. The interplay between these two bounds—using Lemma
10
to control deterministic exploration costs and Lemma
9
to enable self-normalized recursive analysis—ultimately yields the
\tilde{O}(S^{2}AH^{3}K^{2}\sqrt{T})
regret rate.

The theoretical guarantee of
Greedy Policy Optimization
is provided in Theorem
2

\displaystyle F(X\cup\{e\})-F(X)

Next, we prove the following decomposition lemma.

Lemma 9
.

For any joint state-action pair
(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})\in\mathcal{S}^{K}\times\mathcal{A}^{K}
and any function
G:\mathcal{S}^{K}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfying
0\leq G(\mathbf{s})\leq H
for all
\mathbf{s}\in\mathcal{S}^{K}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}^{K}}[\widehat{\mathbf{P}}_{h}^{k}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime}|{\mathbf{s}},{\mathbf{a}})-\mathbf{P}_{h}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime}|{\mathbf{s}},{\mathbf{a}})]G(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})\leq\frac{1}{H}\sum_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}^{K}}\mathbf{P}_{h}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime}|{\mathbf{s}},{\mathbf{a}})G(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})+\sum_{i=1}^{K}\frac{10SH^{2}K\iota}{N_{i,h}^{k}(s^{i},a^{i})}.

Proof.

The proof exploits the product structure of multi-agent transition dynamics through a telescoping argument over agents, combined with carefully calibrated applications of concentration inequalities.

Step 1: Telescoping decomposition.

By the independence of agent transitions, the joint transition probability factors as:

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbf{P}}_{h}^{k}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime}|\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})=\prod_{i=1}^{K}\widehat{P}_{i,h}^{k}(s^{\prime i}|s^{i},a^{i}),\quad\mathbf{P}_{h}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime}|\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})=\prod_{i=1}^{K}P_{i,h}(s^{\prime i}|s^{i},a^{i}).

Our target quantity becomes:

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}^{K}}\widehat{\mathbf{P}}_{h}^{k}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime}|\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})G(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})-\sum_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}^{K}}{\mathbf{P}}_{h}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime}|\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})G(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}^{K}}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{K}\widehat{P}_{i,h}^{k}(s^{\prime i}|s^{i},a^{i})-\prod_{i=1}^{K}{P}_{i,h}(s^{\prime i}|s^{i},a^{i})\right]G(\mathbf{s}^{\prime}).

(18)

To decompose this difference of products, we introduce a sequence of intermediate terms that gradually transitions from using all true probabilities to using all empirical probabilities. For
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,K\}
, define:

\displaystyle Y_{i}:=\sum_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}^{K}}\left[\prod_{l=1}^{i}\widehat{P}_{l,h}^{k}(s^{\prime l}|s^{l},a^{l})\right]\left[\prod_{m=i+1}^{K}{P}_{m,h}(s^{\prime m}|s^{m},a^{m})\right]G(\mathbf{s}^{\prime}),

(19)

where we adopt the convention that empty products equal 1. This construction allows us to bridge the gap between empirical and true expectations by changing one agent’s transition at a time.
By telescoping, we can write the total difference as a sum of incremental changes:

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}^{K}}\widehat{\mathbf{P}}_{h}^{k}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime}|\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})G(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})-\sum_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}^{K}}{\mathbf{P}}_{h}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime}|\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})G(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})=Y_{K}-Y_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{K}(Y_{i}-Y_{i-1}).

(20)

Each difference
Y_{i}-Y_{i-1}
captures the effect of replacing agent
i
’s true transition probability with its empirical estimate, while keeping all other agents’ transitions fixed. This isolates individual agent errors and enables us to apply concentration inequalities separately for each agent.

Step 2: Bounding individual agent contributions.

By definition (
19
), the terms
Y_{i}
and
Y_{i-1}
differ only in the transition probability for agent
i
:

\displaystyle Y_{i}-Y_{i-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}^{K}}\prod_{l=1}^{i-1}\widehat{P}_{l,h}^{k}(s^{\prime l}|s^{l},a^{l})\prod_{m=i+1}^{K}{P}_{m,h}(s^{\prime m}|s^{m},a^{m})G(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})(\widehat{P}_{i,h}^{k}(s^{\prime i}|s^{i},a^{i})-{P}_{i,h}(s^{\prime i}|s^{i},a^{i}))

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}^{K}}\prod_{l=1}^{i-1}\widehat{P}_{l,h}^{k}(s^{\prime l}|s^{l},a^{l})\prod_{m=i+1}^{K}{P}_{m,h}(s^{\prime m}|s^{m},a^{m})G(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})|\widehat{P}_{i,h}^{k}(s^{\prime i}|s^{i},a^{i})-{P}_{i,h}(s^{\prime i}|s^{i},a^{i})|

\displaystyle=\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}|\widehat{P}_{i,h}^{k}(s^{\prime}|s^{i},a^{i})-{P}_{i,h}(s^{\prime}|s^{i},a^{i})|\sum_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}:s^{\prime i}=s^{\prime}}\prod_{l=1}^{i-1}\widehat{P}_{l,h}^{k}(s^{\prime l}|s^{l},a^{l})\prod_{m=i+1}^{K}{P}_{m,h}(s^{\prime m}|s^{m},a^{m})G(\mathbf{s}^{\prime}).

In addition, let us denote
Z_{i}(s^{\prime})=\sum_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}:s^{\prime i}=s^{\prime}}\prod_{l=1}^{i-1}\widehat{P}_{l,h}^{k}(s^{\prime l}|s^{l},a^{l})\prod_{m=i+1}^{K}{P}_{m,h}(s^{\prime m}|s^{m},a^{m})G(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})
. Then

\displaystyle Y_{i}-Y_{i-1}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}|\widehat{P}_{i,h}^{k}(s^{\prime}|s^{i},a^{i})-{P}_{i,h}(s^{\prime}|s^{i},a^{i})|Z_{i}(s^{\prime}).

By event
\mathcal{E}_{2}
in Lemma
6
, we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}|\widehat{P}_{i,h}^{k}(s^{\prime}|s^{i},a^{i})-{P}_{i,h}(s^{\prime}|s^{i},a^{i})|Z_{i}(s^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}\left(\sqrt{\frac{2P_{i,h}(s^{\prime}|s^{i},a^{i})\iota}{N_{i,h}^{k}(s^{i},a^{i})}}+\frac{3\iota}{N_{i,h}^{k}(s^{i},a^{i})}\right)Z_{i}(s^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{P_{i,h}(s^{\prime}|s^{i},a^{i})}{2HK}+\frac{HK\iota}{N_{i,h}^{k}(s^{i},a^{i})}+\frac{3\iota}{N_{i,h}^{k}(s^{i},a^{i})}\right)Z_{i}(s^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}P_{i,h}(s^{\prime}|s^{i},a^{i})Z_{i}(s^{\prime})}{2HK}+\frac{4HK\iota}{N_{i,h}^{k}(s^{i},a^{i})}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}Z_{i}(s^{\prime}),

(21)

where the second inequality follows from
\sqrt{ab}\leq\frac{a+b}{2}
with
a=\frac{P_{i,h}(s^{\prime}|s^{i},a^{i})}{2HK}
and
b=\frac{2HK\iota}{N_{i,h}^{k}(s^{i},a^{i})}
.

Since
G(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})\leq H
by assumption, we can bound the
Z_{i}(s^{\prime})
by factoring out
H
:

\displaystyle Z_{i}(s^{\prime})\leq H\sum_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}:s^{\prime i}=s^{\prime}}\prod_{l=1}^{i-1}\widehat{P}_{l,h}^{k}(s^{\prime l}|s^{l},a^{l})\prod_{m=i+1}^{K}{P}_{m,h}(s^{\prime m}|s^{m},a^{m})=H,

By definition, we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}P_{i,h}(s^{\prime}|s^{i},a^{i})Z_{i}(s^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}^{K}}\left[\prod_{l=1}^{i-1}\widehat{P}_{l,h}^{k}(s^{\prime l}|s^{l},a^{l})\right]\left[\sum_{s^{\prime i}\in\mathcal{S}}P_{i,h}(s^{\prime i}|s^{i},a^{i})\right]\left[\prod_{m=i+1}^{K}{P}_{m,h}(s^{\prime m}|s^{m},a^{m})\right]G(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}^{K}}\left[\prod_{l=1}^{i-1}\widehat{P}_{l,h}^{k}(s^{\prime l}|s^{l},a^{l})\right]\left[\prod_{m=i}^{K}{P}_{m,h}(s^{\prime m}|s^{m},a^{m})\right]G(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})=Y_{i-1},

(22)

Combining with (
D.2
), we can get

\displaystyle Y_{i}-Y_{i-1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Y_{i-1}}{2HK}+\frac{4HK\iota}{N_{i,h}^{k}(s^{i},a^{i})}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}Z_{i}(s^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Y_{i-1}}{2HK}+\frac{4H^{2}SK\iota}{N_{i,h}^{k}(s^{i},a^{i})}.

(23)

From (
20
) and (
D.2
), we get

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}\widehat{\mathbf{P}}_{h}^{k}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime}|\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})G(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})-\sum_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}{\mathbf{P}}_{h}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime}|\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})G(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2HK}\sum_{i=1}^{K}Y_{i-1}+\sum_{i=1}^{K}\frac{4SH^{2}K\iota}{N_{i,h}^{k}(s^{i},a^{i})}.

(24)

To bound
\sum_{i=1}^{K}Y_{i-1}
, we require an upper bound on each
Y_{i}
.

Step 3: Refined recursive bound on
Y_{i}
.

Next, we bound
Y_{i}
recursively. Using a similar argument with
a=\frac{P_{i,h}(s^{\prime}|s^{i},a^{i})}{K}
and
b=\frac{K\iota}{N_{i,h}^{k}(s^{i},a^{i})}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}|\widehat{P}_{i,h}^{k}(s^{\prime}|s^{i},a^{i})-{P}_{i,h}(s^{\prime}|s^{i},a^{i})|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}P_{i,h}(s^{\prime}|s^{i},a^{i})}{K}+\frac{4K\iota}{N_{i,h}^{k}(s^{i},a^{i})}.

Similarly as in Step 2, we can show

\displaystyle Y_{i}-Y_{i-1}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}|\widehat{P}_{i,h}^{k}(s^{\prime}|s^{i},a^{i})-{P}_{i,h}(s^{\prime}|s^{i},a^{i})|Z_{i}(s^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}P_{i,h}(s^{\prime}|s^{i},a^{i})Z_{i}(s^{\prime})}{K}+\frac{4HK\iota}{N_{i,h}^{k}(s^{i},a^{i})}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}Z_{i}(s^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{K}Y_{i-1}+\frac{4HSK\iota}{N_{i,h}^{k}(s^{i},a^{i})},

which gives

\displaystyle Y_{i}\leq(1+\frac{1}{K})Y_{i-1}+\frac{4HSK\iota}{N_{i,h}^{k}(s^{i},a^{i})}.

By recursion, we obtain

\displaystyle Y_{i}\leq(1+\frac{1}{K})^{i}Y_{0}+\sum_{m=1}^{i}\frac{4SHK\iota}{N_{m,h}^{k}(s^{m},a^{m})}(1+\frac{1}{K})^{i-m}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{K}Y_{i-1}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{K}(1+\frac{1}{K})^{i-1}Y_{0}+\sum_{i=2}^{K}\sum_{m=1}^{i-1}\frac{4SHK\iota}{N_{m,h}^{k}(s^{m},a^{m})}(1+\frac{1}{K})^{i-1-m}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(1+\frac{1}{K})^{K}-1}{\frac{1}{K}}Y_{0}+\sum_{m=1}^{K-1}\sum_{i=m+1}^{K}\frac{4SHK\iota}{N_{m,h}^{k}(s^{m},a^{m})}(1+\frac{1}{K})^{i-1-m}

\displaystyle\leq K[(1+\frac{1}{K})^{K}-1]Y_{0}+\sum_{m=1}^{K-1}\frac{4SHK\iota}{N_{m,h}^{k}(s^{m},a^{m})}\sum_{j=0}^{K-m-1}(1+\frac{1}{K})^{j}

\displaystyle\leq K(e-1)Y_{0}+\sum_{m=1}^{K-1}\frac{4SHK^{2}\iota}{N_{m,h}^{k}(s^{m},a^{m})}e,

(25)

where we used
(1+\frac{1}{K})^{K}\leq e
and
\sum_{j=0}^{K}(1+\frac{1}{K})^{j}\leq K\Big((1+\frac{1}{K})^{K}-1\Big)
.

Step 4: Final Bound.

Substituting (
D.2
) into (
24
):

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}\widehat{\mathbf{P}}_{h}^{k}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime}|\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})G(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})-\sum_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}{\mathbf{P}}_{h}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime}|\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})G(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{e-1}{2H}Y_{0}+2SKe\iota\sum_{m=1}^{K-1}\frac{1}{N_{m,h}^{k}(s^{m},a^{m})}+\sum_{i=1}^{K}\frac{4SH^{2}K\iota}{N_{i,h}^{k}(s^{i},a^{i})}.

Since
e<3
, we have
\frac{e-1}{2H}<\frac{1}{H}
and
2SKe\iota<6SK\iota
. Extending the first sum to include
i=K
and combining:

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}\widehat{\mathbf{P}}_{h}^{k}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime}|\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})G(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})-\sum_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}{\mathbf{P}}_{h}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime}|\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})G(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{H}Y_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{K}\frac{6SK\iota+4SH^{2}K\iota}{N_{i,h}^{k}(s^{i},a^{i})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{H}Y_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{K}\frac{10SH^{2}K\iota}{N_{i,h}^{k}(s^{i},a^{i})}.

Thus we complete the proof.
∎

D.3
Optimistic Estimate

Let us recall the definition of
\bar{V}_{h}^{\pi,k}(\mathbf{s})
, which is

\displaystyle\bar{V}_{h}^{\pi,k}(\mathbf{s})=\mathbb{E}_{\widehat{P}^{k},\pi}\left[\sum_{t=h}^{H}\left(r(\mathbf{s}_{t},\mathbf{a}_{t})+\sum_{i=1}^{K}b(N^{k}_{i,t}(s_{t}^{i},a_{t}^{i}))\right)\bigg|\mathbf{s}_{h}=\mathbf{s}\right].

In this section, we prove that
\bar{V}_{h}^{\pi,k}(\mathbf{s})
is indeed an optimistic estimate of the true value function
V_{h}^{\pi}
for any policy
\pi
. First of all, we prove the following lemma.

Lemma 10
.

Conditioned on event
\mathcal{E}
(Lemma
6
), for any
\mathbf{s}\in\mathcal{S}^{K}
,
\mathbf{a}\in\mathcal{A}^{K}
,
h\in[H]
,
k\in[T]
, and any function
V:\mathcal{S}^{K}\to\mathbb{R}
with
V(\mathbf{s})\in[0,H]
,

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}\widehat{\mathbf{P}}_{h}^{k}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime}|\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})V(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})-\sum_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}{\mathbf{P}}_{h}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime}|\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})V(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})\right|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{K}b(N_{i,h}^{k}(s^{i},a^{i})),

where
b(N)=H\sqrt{\frac{2S\iota}{N}}+\frac{3SH\iota}{N}
and
\iota=\log(6S^{2}ATHK/\delta)
.

Proof.

The proof of the lemma is similar to that of Lemma
9
. By independence of agent transitions,
\mathbf{P}_{h}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime}|\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})=\prod_{i=1}^{K}P_{i,h}(s^{\prime i}|s^{i},a^{i})
and similarly for
\widehat{\mathbf{P}}_{h}^{k}
. Define

\displaystyle X_{i}:=\sum_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}\prod_{l=1}^{i}\widehat{P}_{l,h}^{k}(s^{\prime l}|s^{l},a^{l})\prod_{m=i+1}^{K}P_{m,h}(s^{\prime m}|s^{m},a^{m})V(\mathbf{s}^{\prime}),\quad i\in\{0,\ldots,K\},

where empty products equal 1. Note
X_{0}
uses all true transitions and
X_{K}
uses all empirical transitions. By telescoping,

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}\widehat{\mathbf{P}}_{h}^{k}V(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})-\sum_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}{\mathbf{P}}_{h}V(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})\right|=|X_{K}-X_{0}|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{K}|X_{i}-X_{i-1}|.

(26)

For each
i\in[K]
,
X_{i}
and
X_{i-1}
differ only in agent
i
’s transition. Rearranging,

\displaystyle X_{i}-X_{i-1}=\sum_{s^{\prime i}}\left(\widehat{P}_{i,h}^{k}(s^{\prime i}|s^{i},a^{i})-P_{i,h}(s^{\prime i}|s^{i},a^{i})\right)\cdot W_{i}(s^{\prime i}),

where
W_{i}(s^{\prime i}):=\sum_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}:\,s^{\prime i}\text{ fixed}}\prod_{l\neq i}\mathbb{P}_{l,h}(s^{\prime l}|s^{l},a^{l})V(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})\in[0,H]
and
\mathbb{P}_{l,h}
denotes
\widehat{P}
for
l<i
and
P
for
l>i
. Since
|W_{i}(s^{\prime i})|\leq H
(probabilities sum to 1),

\displaystyle|X_{i}-X_{i-1}|\leq H\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}|\widehat{P}_{i,h}^{k}(s^{\prime}|s^{i},a^{i})-P_{i,h}(s^{\prime}|s^{i},a^{i})|.

(27)

By event
\mathcal{E}_{2}
(Lemma
6
),

\displaystyle|\widehat{P}_{i,h}^{k}(s^{\prime}|s^{i},a^{i})-P_{i,h}(s^{\prime}|s^{i},a^{i})|\leq\sqrt{\frac{2P_{i,h}(s^{\prime}|s^{i},a^{i})\iota}{N_{i,h}^{k}(s^{i},a^{i})}}+\frac{3\iota}{N_{i,h}^{k}(s^{i},a^{i})}.

Summing over
s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}
and applying Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle\sum_{s^{\prime}}|\widehat{P}_{i,h}^{k}(s^{\prime}|s^{i},a^{i})-P_{i,h}(s^{\prime}|s^{i},a^{i})|

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{2\iota}{N_{i,h}^{k}(s^{i},a^{i})}}\underbrace{\sum_{s^{\prime}}\sqrt{P_{i,h}(s^{\prime}|s^{i},a^{i})}}_{\leq\sqrt{S}}+\frac{3S\iota}{N_{i,h}^{k}(s^{i},a^{i})}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{2S\iota}{N_{i,h}^{k}(s^{i},a^{i})}}+\frac{3S\iota}{N_{i,h}^{k}(s^{i},a^{i})}.

Combining with (
27
) gives
|X_{i}-X_{i-1}|\leq b(N_{i,h}^{k}(s^{i},a^{i}))
. The result follows from (
26
).
∎

Next, we prove the following lemma.

Lemma 11
.

Conditioned on the event
\mathcal{E}
in Lemma
6
, we have that throughout the Algorithm
2
, it holds that for any
\mathbf{s}\in\mathcal{S}
,
\mathbf{a}\in\mathcal{A}^{K}
, and any policy
\pi
, it holds that

\displaystyle\bar{V}_{h}^{\pi,k}(\mathbf{s})\geq V_{h}^{\pi}(\mathbf{s})

and

\displaystyle\bar{Q}_{h}^{\pi,k}(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})\geq Q_{h}^{\pi}(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})

Proof.

First of all, consider the modified Markov Decision Process
\widehat{M}
under the transition dynamics
\widehat{\mathbf{P}}^{k}
with reward being
r(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})+\sum_{i=1}^{K}b(N_{i,h}^{k}(s^{i},a^{i}))
, where
b(\cdot)\geq 0
is the bonus function. By definition,
\bar{V}^{\pi,k}
and
\bar{Q}^{\pi,k}
are the value functions for policy
\pi
in this modified MDP. By apply the Bellman’s equation for
\widehat{M}
, we can get for any
\mathbf{s}=(s^{1},s^{2},...,s^{K})\in\mathcal{S}^{K}
and
\mathbf{a}=(a^{1},a^{2},...,a^{K})\in\mathcal{A}^{K}
,

\displaystyle\bar{V}_{h}^{\pi,k}(\mathbf{s})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathcal{A}^{K}}\pi(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})\bar{Q}_{h}^{\pi,k}(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})

\displaystyle\bar{Q}_{h}^{\pi,k}(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})

\displaystyle=r(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})+\sum_{i=1}^{K}b(N_{i,h}^{k}(s^{i},a^{i}))+\sum_{\mathbf{s^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}^{K}}\widehat{\mathbf{P}}_{h}^{k}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime}|\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})\bar{V}_{h+1}^{\pi,k}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})

(28)

where
\widehat{\mathbf{P}}_{h}^{k}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime}|\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})=\prod_{i=1}^{K}\widehat{P}_{i,h}^{k}(s^{\prime i}|s^{i},a^{i})
is the joint transition probability from
(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})
to
\mathbf{s}^{\prime}
.

Similarly, for the true value functions:

\displaystyle V_{h}^{\pi}(\mathbf{s})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathcal{A}^{K}}\pi(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})Q_{h}^{\pi}(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})

\displaystyle Q_{h}^{\pi}(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})

\displaystyle=r(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})+\sum_{\mathbf{s^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}^{K}}\mathbf{P}_{h}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime}|\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})V_{h+1}^{\pi}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})

(29)

where
\mathbf{P}_{h}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime}|\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})=\prod_{i=1}^{K}P_{i,h}(s^{\prime i}|s^{i},a^{i})
.

Next, we prove the result by induction. First of all, when
h=H
, we have that

\displaystyle\bar{Q}_{H}^{\pi,k}(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})

\displaystyle=r(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})+\sum_{i=1}^{K}b(N_{i,H}^{k}(s^{i},a^{i}))

\displaystyle\geq r(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})=Q_{H}^{\pi}(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a}).

Since
\bar{V}_{h}^{\pi,k}(\mathbf{s})=\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathcal{A}^{K}}\pi(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})\bar{Q}_{h}^{\pi,k}(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})

and
V_{h}^{\pi}(\mathbf{s})=\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathcal{A}^{K}}\pi(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})Q_{h}^{\pi}(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})
, we have that the results hold for
h=H
.

Next, suppose results hold for
h+1
.
From the Bellman’s equation in (
D.3
), we have that

\displaystyle\bar{Q}_{h}^{\pi,k}(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})

\displaystyle=r(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})+\sum_{\mathbf{s^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}^{K}}\widehat{\mathbf{P}}_{h}^{k}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime}|\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})\bar{V}_{h+1}^{\pi,k}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})+\sum_{i=1}^{K}b(N_{i,h}^{k}(s^{i},a^{i}))

\displaystyle\geq r(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})+\sum_{\mathbf{s^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}^{K}}\widehat{\mathbf{P}}_{h}^{k}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime}|\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})V_{h+1}^{\pi}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})+\sum_{i=1}^{K}b(N_{i,h}^{k}(s^{i},a^{i})).

From Lemma
10
, we can get that

\displaystyle\bar{Q}_{h}^{\pi,k}(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})

\displaystyle\geq r(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})+\sum_{\mathbf{s^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}^{K}}\mathbf{P}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime}|\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})V_{h+1}^{\pi}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\geq Q_{h}^{\pi}(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})

Since
\bar{V}_{h}^{\pi,k}(\mathbf{s})=\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathcal{A}^{K}}\pi(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})\bar{Q}_{h}^{\pi,k}(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})\geq\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathcal{A}^{K}}\pi(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})Q_{h}^{\pi}(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})=V_{h}^{\pi}(\mathbf{s})
, the result holds for the case of
h
. Thus, we can conclude the proof.
∎

D.4
Proof of Main Theorem

We now establish our main regret bound. The proof proceeds by relating the regret to the gap between optimistic value estimates and true values, decomposing this gap via a Bellman-style recursion, and bounding the resulting terms using concentration inequalities.

D.4.1
Relating Regret to Value Estimates

By definition,

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{T,\frac{1}{2}}=\sum_{k=1}^{T}\left[\frac{1}{2}V^{\pi^{*}}_{1}(\bar{\mathbf{s}}_{1})-V^{\pi^{k}}_{1}(\bar{\mathbf{s}}_{1})\right].

From Lemma
11
, we have
\bar{V}^{\pi^{*},k}_{1}(\bar{\mathbf{s}}_{1})\geq V^{\pi^{*}}_{1}(\bar{\mathbf{s}}_{1})
for all
k
, which gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{T,\frac{1}{2}}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{T}\left[\frac{1}{2}\bar{V}^{\pi^{*},k}_{1}(\bar{\mathbf{s}}_{1})-V^{\pi^{k}}_{1}(\bar{\mathbf{s}}_{1})\right].

(30)

From Lemma
8
, we have
\bar{V}_{1}^{\pi^{k},k}(\bar{\mathbf{s}}_{1})\geq\frac{\bar{V}_{1}^{\pi^{*},k}(\bar{\mathbf{s}}_{1})}{2}-\epsilon
, which rearranges to
\frac{1}{2}\bar{V}_{1}^{\pi^{*},k}(\bar{\mathbf{s}}_{1})\leq\bar{V}_{1}^{\pi^{k},k}(\bar{\mathbf{s}}_{1})+\epsilon
. Substituting into (
30
):

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{T,\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{T}\left[\bar{V}^{\pi^{k},k}_{1}(\bar{\mathbf{s}}_{1})-V^{\pi^{k}}_{1}(\bar{\mathbf{s}}_{1})+\epsilon\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{T}\left[\bar{V}^{\pi^{k},k}_{1}(\bar{\mathbf{s}}_{1})-V^{\pi^{k}}_{1}(\bar{\mathbf{s}}_{1})\right]+T\epsilon.

(31)

The remainder of the proof focuses on bounding the first term.

D.4.2
Recursive Decomposition of the Value Gap

For each step
h
in episode
k
, since policy
\pi^{k}
is deterministic and selects
{\mathbf{a}}_{h}^{k}
at state
{\mathbf{s}}_{h}^{k}
, we have

\displaystyle\bar{V}^{\pi^{k},k}_{h}({\mathbf{s}}_{h}^{k})-V^{\pi^{k}}_{h}({\mathbf{s}}_{h}^{k})=\bar{Q}^{\pi^{k},k}_{h}({\mathbf{s}}_{h}^{k},{\mathbf{a}}_{h}^{k})-Q^{\pi^{k}}_{h}({\mathbf{s}}_{h}^{k},{\mathbf{a}}_{h}^{k}).

(32)

Applying the Bellman equations (
D.3
) and (
D.3
), we have

\displaystyle\bar{Q}^{\pi^{k},k}_{h}(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})

\displaystyle=r(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})+\sum_{i=1}^{K}b(N_{i,h}^{k}(s^{i},a^{i}))+\sum_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}\widehat{\mathbf{P}}_{h}^{k}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime}|\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})\bar{V}^{\pi^{k},k}_{h+1}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime}),

\displaystyle Q^{\pi^{k}}_{h}(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})

\displaystyle=r(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})+\sum_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}\mathbf{P}_{h}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime}|\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})V_{h+1}^{\pi^{k}}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime}),

where
b(N)=H\sqrt{\frac{2S\iota}{N}}+\frac{3HS\iota}{N}
. Taking the difference at
({\mathbf{s}}_{h}^{k},{\mathbf{a}}_{h}^{k})
, we can get

\displaystyle\bar{Q}^{\pi^{k},k}_{h}({\mathbf{s}}_{h}^{k},{\mathbf{a}}_{h}^{k})-Q^{\pi^{k}}_{h}({\mathbf{s}}_{h}^{k},{\mathbf{a}}_{h}^{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}\widehat{\mathbf{P}}_{h}^{k}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime}|{\mathbf{s}}_{h}^{k},{\mathbf{a}}_{h}^{k})\bar{V}^{\pi^{k},k}_{h+1}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})-\sum_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}\mathbf{P}_{h}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime}|{\mathbf{s}}_{h}^{k},{\mathbf{a}}_{h}^{k})V_{h+1}^{\pi^{k}}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})+\sum_{i=1}^{K}b(N_{i,h}^{k}(s^{i,k}_{h},a^{i,k}_{h}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}[\widehat{\mathbf{P}}_{h}^{k}-\mathbf{P}_{h}](\mathbf{s}^{\prime}|{\mathbf{s}}_{h}^{k},{\mathbf{a}}_{h}^{k})\bar{V}^{\pi^{k},k}_{h+1}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})+\sum_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}\mathbf{P}_{h}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime}|{\mathbf{s}}_{h}^{k},{\mathbf{a}}_{h}^{k})[\bar{V}^{\pi^{k},k}_{h+1}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})-V_{h+1}^{\pi^{k}}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})]

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{i=1}^{K}b(N_{i,h}^{k}(s^{i,k}_{h},a^{i,k}_{h})).

(33)

We further refine the first term by adding and subtracting
\sum_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}[\widehat{\mathbf{P}}_{h}^{k}-\mathbf{P}_{h}](\mathbf{s}^{\prime}|{\mathbf{s}}_{h}^{k},{\mathbf{a}}_{h}^{k})V_{h+1}^{\pi^{k}}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})
:

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}[\widehat{\mathbf{P}}_{h}^{k}-\mathbf{P}_{h}](\mathbf{s}^{\prime}|{\mathbf{s}}_{h}^{k},{\mathbf{a}}_{h}^{k})\bar{V}^{\pi^{k},k}_{h+1}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}[\widehat{\mathbf{P}}_{h}^{k}-\mathbf{P}_{h}](\mathbf{s}^{\prime}|{\mathbf{s}}_{h}^{k},{\mathbf{a}}_{h}^{k})V_{h+1}^{\pi^{k}}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})+\sum_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}[\widehat{\mathbf{P}}_{h}^{k}-\mathbf{P}_{h}](\mathbf{s}^{\prime}|{\mathbf{s}}_{h}^{k},{\mathbf{a}}_{h}^{k})[\bar{V}^{\pi^{k},k}_{h+1}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})-V_{h+1}^{\pi^{k}}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})].

(34)

D.4.3
Bounding the Decomposed Terms

Since
V_{h+1}^{\pi^{k}}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})\in[0,H]
for all
\mathbf{s}^{\prime}
, by Lemma
10
:

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}[\widehat{\mathbf{P}}_{h}^{k}-\mathbf{P}_{h}](\mathbf{s}^{\prime}|{\mathbf{s}}_{h}^{k},{\mathbf{a}}_{h}^{k})V_{h+1}^{\pi^{k}}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})\right|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{K}b(N_{i,h}^{k}(s^{i,k}_{h},a^{i,k}_{h})).

(35)

From Lemma
11
, we have
\bar{V}^{\pi^{k},k}_{h+1}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})-V_{h+1}^{\pi^{k}}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})\geq 0
for all
\mathbf{s}^{\prime}
. Applying Lemma
9
:

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}[\widehat{\mathbf{P}}_{h}^{k}-\mathbf{P}_{h}](\mathbf{s}^{\prime}|{\mathbf{s}}_{h}^{k},{\mathbf{a}}_{h}^{k})[\bar{V}^{\pi^{k},k}_{h+1}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})-V_{h+1}^{\pi^{k}}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})]

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{1}{H}\sum_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}\mathbf{P}_{h}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime}|{\mathbf{s}}_{h}^{k},{\mathbf{a}}_{h}^{k})[\bar{V}^{\pi^{k},k}_{h+1}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})-V_{h+1}^{\pi^{k}}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})]+\sum_{i=1}^{K}\frac{10SH^{2}K\iota}{N_{i,h}^{k}(s^{i,k}_{h},a^{i,k}_{h})}.

(36)

Combining (
35
), (
D.4.3
), (
D.4.2
), and (
D.4.2
):

\displaystyle\bar{V}^{\pi^{k},k}_{h}({\mathbf{s}}_{h}^{k})-V^{\pi^{k}}_{h}({\mathbf{s}}_{h}^{k})

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\frac{1}{H}\right)\sum_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}\mathbf{P}_{h}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime}|{\mathbf{s}}_{h}^{k},{\mathbf{a}}_{h}^{k})[\bar{V}^{\pi^{k},k}_{h+1}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})-V_{h+1}^{\pi^{k}}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})]

\displaystyle\quad+2\sum_{i=1}^{K}b(N_{i,h}^{k}(s^{i,k}_{h},a^{i,k}_{h}))+\sum_{i=1}^{K}\frac{10SH^{2}K\iota}{N_{i,h}^{k}(s^{i,k}_{h},a^{i,k}_{h})}.

(37)

D.4.4
Introduction of Martingale Structure

Define the difference term
\xi_{h}^{k}
as

\displaystyle\xi_{h}^{k}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}\mathbf{P}_{h}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime}|{\mathbf{s}}_{h}^{k},{\mathbf{a}}_{h}^{k})[\bar{V}^{\pi^{k},k}_{h+1}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})-V_{h+1}^{\pi^{k}}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})]-[\bar{V}^{\pi^{k},k}_{h+1}(\bar{\mathbf{s}}_{h+1}^{k})-V^{\pi^{k}}_{h+1}({\mathbf{s}}_{h+1}^{k})],

and the deterministic error term:

\displaystyle\eta_{h}^{k}

\displaystyle:=2\sum_{i=1}^{K}b(N_{i,h}^{k}(s^{i,k}_{h},a^{i,k}_{h}))+\sum_{i=1}^{K}\frac{10SH^{2}K\iota}{N_{i,h}^{k}(s^{i,k}_{h},a^{i,k}_{h})}.

With these definitions, (
D.4.3
) becomes:

\displaystyle\bar{V}^{\pi^{k},k}_{h}({\mathbf{s}}_{h}^{k})-V^{\pi^{k}}_{h}({\mathbf{s}}_{h}^{k})

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\frac{1}{H}\right)[\bar{V}^{\pi^{k},k}_{h+1}(\bar{\mathbf{s}}_{h+1}^{k})-V^{\pi^{k}}_{h+1}({\mathbf{s}}_{h+1}^{k})]+\left(1+\frac{1}{H}\right)\xi_{h}^{k}+\eta_{h}^{k}.

(38)

D.4.5
Recursive Expansion

Applying (
38
) recursively from
h=1
to
h=H
:

\displaystyle\bar{V}^{\pi^{k},k}_{1}(\bar{\mathbf{s}}_{1}^{k})-V^{\pi^{k}}_{1}(\bar{\mathbf{s}}_{1}^{k})

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\frac{1}{H}\right)^{H}[\bar{V}^{\pi^{k},k}_{H+1}(\bar{\mathbf{s}}_{H+1}^{k})-V^{\pi^{k}}_{H+1}({\mathbf{s}}_{H+1}^{k})]+\sum_{h=1}^{H}\left(1+\frac{1}{H}\right)^{H-h+1}\xi_{h}^{k}+\sum_{h=1}^{H}\left(1+\frac{1}{H}\right)^{H-h}\eta_{h}^{k}.

(39)

Since
\bar{V}^{\pi^{k},k}_{H+1}(\bar{\mathbf{s}}_{H+1}^{k})=V^{\pi^{k}}_{H+1}({\mathbf{s}}_{H+1}^{k})=0
and
(1+\frac{1}{H})^{H}\leq e
:

\displaystyle\bar{V}^{\pi^{k},k}_{1}(\bar{\mathbf{s}}_{1}^{k})-V^{\pi^{k}}_{1}(\bar{\mathbf{s}}_{1}^{k})

\displaystyle\leq e\sum_{h=1}^{H}\xi_{h}^{k}+e\sum_{h=1}^{H}\eta_{h}^{k}.

(40)

Summing over all
T
episodes, we can get

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{T,\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{T}[\bar{V}^{\pi^{k},k}_{1}(\bar{\mathbf{s}}_{1})-V^{\pi^{k}}_{1}(\bar{\mathbf{s}}_{1})]+T\epsilon

\displaystyle\leq e\sum_{k=1}^{T}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\xi_{h}^{k}+e\sum_{k=1}^{T}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\eta_{h}^{k}+T\epsilon.

(41)

D.4.6
Bounding the Deterministic Exploration Cost

Expanding the definition of
\eta_{h}^{k}
with
b(N)=H\sqrt{\frac{2S\iota}{N}}+\frac{3HS\iota}{N}
:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{T}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\eta_{h}^{k}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{T}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{K}\frac{16SH^{2}K\iota}{N_{i,h}^{k}(s^{i,k}_{h},a^{i,k}_{h})}+2H\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sqrt{\frac{2S\iota}{N_{i,h}^{k}(s^{i,k}_{h},a^{i,k}_{h})}}\right),

where we used
10SH^{2}K\iota+2\cdot 3HS\iota\leq 16SH^{2}K\iota
.

For the first term, reordering summations and grouping by state-action pairs:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{T}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\frac{16SH^{2}K\iota}{N_{i,h}^{k}(s^{i,k}_{h},a^{i,k}_{h})}=16SH^{2}K\iota\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\sum_{(s,a)}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_{i,h}^{T}(s,a)}\frac{1}{\ell}

\displaystyle\leq 16SH^{2}K\iota\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\sum_{(s,a)}(1+\log N_{i,h}^{T}(s,a))\leq 32S^{2}AH^{3}K^{2}\iota\log T,

where we used
\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\ell}\leq 1+\log n
and
N_{i,h}^{T}(s,a)\leq T
.

For the second term, using
\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\ell}}\leq 1+2\sqrt{n}
:

\displaystyle 2H\sum_{k=1}^{T}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sqrt{\frac{2S\iota}{N_{i,h}^{k}(s^{i,k}_{h},a^{i,k}_{h})}}=2H\sqrt{2S\iota}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\sum_{(s,a)}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_{i,h}^{T}(s,a)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\ell}}

\displaystyle\leq 2H\sqrt{2S\iota}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\sum_{(s,a)}(1+2\sqrt{N_{i,h}^{T}(s,a)})

\displaystyle\leq 2\sqrt{2S\iota}H^{2}KSA+2H\sqrt{2S\iota}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\sum_{(s,a)}2\sqrt{N_{i,h}^{T}(s,a)}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sqrt{2S\iota}H^{2}KSA+4H^{2}KS\sqrt{2AT\iota}.

Combining:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{T}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\eta_{h}^{k}\leq 32S^{2}AH^{3}K^{2}\iota\log T+4H^{2}KS\sqrt{2AT\iota}+2\sqrt{2S\iota}H^{2}KSA.

(42)

D.4.7
Bounding the Martingale Terms

Let
\mathcal{F}_{h}^{k}
denote the
\sigma
-algebra generated by all randomness up to step
h
of episode
k
. The value gap
\bar{V}^{\pi^{k},k}_{h+1}(\mathbf{s})-V^{\pi^{k}}_{h+1}(\mathbf{s})
is
\mathcal{F}_{h}^{k}
-measurable for any
\mathbf{s}
(since it depends only on data through episode
k-1
), while
{\mathbf{s}}_{h+1}^{k}
is
\mathcal{F}_{h+1}^{k}
-measurable but not
\mathcal{F}_{h}^{k}
-measurable.

Computing the conditional expectation:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\xi_{h}^{k}|\mathcal{F}_{h}^{k}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}\mathbf{P}_{h}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime}|{\mathbf{s}}_{h}^{k},{\mathbf{a}}_{h}^{k})[\bar{V}^{\pi^{k},k}_{h+1}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})-V_{h+1}^{\pi^{k}}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})]-\mathbb{E}\left[\bar{V}^{\pi^{k},k}_{h+1}(\bar{\mathbf{s}}_{h+1}^{k})-V^{\pi^{k}}_{h+1}({\mathbf{s}}_{h+1}^{k})\bigg|\mathcal{F}_{h}^{k}\right].

Since
{\mathbf{s}}_{h+1}^{k}\sim\mathbf{P}_{h}(\cdot|{\mathbf{s}}_{h}^{k},{\mathbf{a}}_{h}^{k})
given
\mathcal{F}_{h}^{k}
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\bar{V}^{\pi^{k},k}_{h+1}(\bar{\mathbf{s}}_{h+1}^{k})-V^{\pi^{k}}_{h+1}({\mathbf{s}}_{h+1}^{k})\bigg|\mathcal{F}_{h}^{k}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}\mathbf{P}_{h}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime}|{\mathbf{s}}_{h}^{k},{\mathbf{a}}_{h}^{k})[\bar{V}^{\pi^{k},k}_{h+1}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})-V_{h+1}^{\pi^{k}}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})].

Therefore:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\xi_{h}^{k}|\mathcal{F}_{h}^{k}]=0,

(43)

confirming that
\{\xi_{h}^{k}\}
is a martingale difference sequence.

Since
0\leq\bar{V}^{\pi^{k},k}_{h+1}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime}),V_{h+1}^{\pi^{k}}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})\leq H
:

\displaystyle|\xi_{h}^{k}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}\mathbf{P}_{h}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime}|{\mathbf{s}}_{h}^{k},{\mathbf{a}}_{h}^{k})|\bar{V}^{\pi^{k},k}_{h+1}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})-V_{h+1}^{\pi^{k}}(\mathbf{s}^{\prime})|+|\bar{V}^{\pi^{k},k}_{h+1}(\bar{\mathbf{s}}_{h+1}^{k})-V^{\pi^{k}}_{h+1}({\mathbf{s}}_{h+1}^{k})|\leq 4H.

Applying Azuma-Hoeffding with
TH
martingale differences each bounded by
4H
, with probability at least
1-\frac{\delta}{3}
:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{T}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\xi_{h}^{k}\leq\sqrt{2TH\cdot(4H)^{2}\cdot\log(3/\delta)}=4H\sqrt{2TH\log(3/\delta)}.

Since
\iota=\log(10S^{2}AT/\delta)\geq\log(3/\delta)
:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{T}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\xi_{h}^{k}\leq 4H\sqrt{2TH\iota}.

(44)

D.4.8
Final Assembly

Combining (
D.4.5
), (
42
), and (
44
), and applying the union bound on the event
\mathcal{E}
in Lemma
6
and the Azuma’s inequality used to bound
\sum_{k=1}^{T}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\xi_{h}^{k}
, we have that with probability at least
1-\delta
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{T,\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 4eH\sqrt{2TH\iota}+e(32S^{2}AH^{3}K^{2}\iota\log T+4H^{2}KS\sqrt{2AT\iota}+2\sqrt{2S\iota}H^{2}KSA)+T\epsilon

\displaystyle\leq 4eH\sqrt{2TH\iota}+96S^{2}AH^{3}K^{2}\iota\log T+4eH^{2}KS\sqrt{2AT\iota}+T\epsilon.

Choosing
\epsilon=O(1/T)
and absorbing constants into
O
notation:

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{T,\frac{1}{2}}=O\left(S^{2}AH^{3}K^{2}\iota\log T+H^{2}KS\sqrt{AT\iota}\right).

Since
\iota:=\log(6S^{2}ATHK/\delta)
, we can get

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{T,\frac{1}{2}}=O\left(S^{2}AH^{3}K^{2}\log(SATHK/\delta)\log T+H^{2}KS\sqrt{AT\log(SATHK/\delta)}\right).

This establishes the desired
O(\sqrt{T})
regret rate, completing the proof.
