Tempered vs generic automorphic functions and the canonical filtration on automorphic functions
Dennis Gaitsgory, Vincent Lafforgue and Sam Raskin

Abstract
. We introduce and study the filtration on the space of automorphic functions (in the everywhere unramified situation for the function field case) obtained by transferring the filtration on the spectral side of the classical Langlands conjecture, induced by coherent singular support . We propose a number of conjectures that tie this filtration (which, by design, arises from the notion of cohomological support ) to a filtration on the space of ℂ {\mathbb{C}} -valued automorphic functions that arises by considering the analytic spectrum of Hecke operators.

1.1.1.

1.1.2.

By a

Note that a usual Weil sheaf is a similar data, where the map

1.1.3.

Assume that

to the data of

There are (at least) three ways to think about this construction.

1.1.4.

For

By adjunction,

Pulling back by means of

The value of

\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha^{\prime}_{y},{\mathcal{F}}_{y})\in\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\ell}.

This is Grothendieck’s original definition.

1.1.5.

The above definition can be rewritten (almost tautologically as follows):

The functor

Hence, by

The maps

\operatorname{LT}_{\mathcal{Y}}^{\operatorname{naive}}:\operatorname{Tr}((\operatorname{Frob}_{\mathcal{Y}})_{*},\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{Y}}))\to\mathsf{Funct}({\mathcal{F}}({\mathbb{F}}_{q}),\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\ell}).

Now, to

as above with

[

Unwinding, we obtain:

\mathsf{funct}({\mathcal{F}})=\operatorname{LT}_{\mathcal{Y}}^{\operatorname{naive}}({\mathcal{F}},\alpha).

1.1.6.

Finally, following

3.2.

In this subsection we continue to work in the analytic context.

We let

Let

discrete automorphic functions

11

3.3.10.

We now consider the interaction of the subspace of cuspidal functions with the filtration. Namely,
from Conjecture
3.3.3

.

Remark 3.3.12
.

Note that from Conjecture

\mathsf{Funct}_{\operatorname{cusp}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}({\mathbb{F}}_{q}),{\mathbb{C}})_{\mathbf{O}}\to\mathsf{Funct}_{c}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}({\mathbb{F}}_{q}),{\mathbb{C}})_{\overline{{\mathbf{O}}}}\twoheadrightarrow\\

(For example, for the

\Gamma({\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{coarse},(1)}_{\kappa},{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{coarse},(1)}_{\kappa}})\to\Gamma({\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{coarse},(1)}_{\kappa}\underset{{\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{coarse}}_{\kappa}}{\times}{\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{rigid}_{x},(n)}_{\kappa},{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{coarse},(1)}_{\kappa}\underset{{\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{coarse}}_{\kappa}}{\times}{\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{rigid}_{x},(n)}_{\kappa}})

is injective for
n
sufficiently large.

A.6.3.

Let
{\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{rigid}_{x},(\infty)}_{\kappa}
denote the formal completion of
{\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{rigid}_{x}}_{\kappa}
along
the preimage of
\operatorname{pt}/\operatorname{Aut}(\sigma)
.

Since
\Gamma({\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{coarse},(1)}_{\kappa},{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{coarse},(1)}_{\kappa}})
is finite-dimensional,
it suffices to show that the map

\Gamma({\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{coarse},(1)}_{\kappa},{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{coarse},(1)}_{\kappa}})\to\Gamma({\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{coarse},(1)}_{\kappa}\underset{{\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{coarse}}_{\kappa}}{\times}{\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{rigid}_{x},(\infty)}_{\kappa},{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{coarse},(1)}_{\kappa}\underset{{\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{coarse}}_{\kappa}}{\times}{\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{rigid}_{x},(\infty)}_{\kappa}})

is injective.

A.6.4.

Note that since
{\check{G}}
is reductive, the map

\Gamma({\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{coarse},(1)}_{\kappa},{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{coarse},(1)}_{\kappa}})\to\Gamma({\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{coarse},(1)}_{\kappa}\underset{{\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{coarse}}_{\kappa}}{\times}{\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{rigid}_{x}}_{\kappa},{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{coarse},(1)}_{\kappa}\underset{{\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{coarse}}_{\kappa}}{\times}{\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{rigid}_{x}}_{\kappa}})

is injective, with the image being the subspace of
{\mathsf{G}}
-invariants in the right-hand side.

Therefore, it remains to show that the restriction map

\Gamma({\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{coarse},(1)}_{\kappa}\underset{{\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{coarse}}_{\kappa}}{\times}{\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{rigid}_{x}}_{\kappa},{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{coarse},(1)}_{\kappa}\underset{{\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{coarse}}_{\kappa}}{\times}{\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{rigid}_{x}}_{\kappa}})\to\\
\to\Gamma({\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{coarse},(1)}_{\kappa}\underset{{\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{coarse}}_{\kappa}}{\times}{\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{rigid}_{x},(\infty)}_{\kappa},{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{coarse},(1)}_{\kappa}\underset{{\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{coarse}}_{\kappa}}{\times}{\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{rigid}_{x},(\infty)}_{\kappa}})

is injective
on
{\check{G}}
-invariant elements
.

A.6.5.

Since the map from a Noetherian local ring to its completion is injective,
it suffices to show that if
f
is a non-zero
{\check{G}}
-invariant element in

\Gamma({\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{coarse},(1)}_{\kappa}\underset{{\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{coarse}}_{\kappa}}{\times}{\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{rigid}_{x}}_{\kappa},{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{coarse},(1)}_{\kappa}\underset{{\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{coarse}}_{\kappa}}{\times}{\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{rigid}_{x}}_{\kappa}}),

then it does not vanish on any Zariski neighborhood of the preimage of
\operatorname{pt}/\operatorname{Aut}(\sigma)
.

However, this follows from the Hilbert-Mumford criterion: for any point

z\in{\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{coarse},(1)}_{\kappa}\underset{{\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{coarse}}_{\kappa}}{\times}{\mathcal{Z}}^{\operatorname{rigid}_{x}}_{\kappa},

we can find
a copy of
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\subset{\check{G}}
that contracts this point onto the preimage of
\operatorname{pt}/\operatorname{Aut}(\sigma)
.
