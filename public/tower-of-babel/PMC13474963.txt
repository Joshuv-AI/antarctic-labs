Vaginal natural orifice transluminal endoscopic surgery versus conventional multiport laparoscopy for hemorrhagic ovarian cyst rupture with hemoperitoneum: a multicenter retrospective cohort study
Authors: Emre Biton, Kemal Gungorduk, Berican Sahin Uyar, Serkan Ustuntas, Furkan Karaoz, Varol Gulseren
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo compare perioperative outcomes, postoperative pain, early recovery, and patient-reported sexual and cosmetic outcomes between vaginal natural orifice transluminal endoscopic surgery (vNOTES) and conventional multiport laparoscopy (CML) in hemodynamically stable women undergoing surgery for hemorrhagic ovarian cyst rupture with hemoperitoneum.MethodsDesign Multicenter retrospective cohort study (Canadian Task Force classification II-2).SettingDepartments of Obstetrics and Gynecology of two tertiary referral centers, January 2020 to December 2025.ParticipantsForty-two non-pregnant women aged ≥18 years with imaging-confirmed hemoperitoneum from a ruptured ovarian cyst and clinical evidence of ongoing bleeding undergoing minimally invasive surgery.InterventionsvNOTES via posterior colpotomy with a multichannel single-port device (n = 13) versus CML with a 10-mm umbilical and two or three 5-mm ancillary trocars (n = 29). Both arms used a standardized Enhanced Recovery After Surgery protocol.Results: Primary outcomes were operative time, estimated blood loss, visual analog scale (VAS) pain at 6, 12, and 24 hours, length of stay, and Quality of Recovery-15 score at discharge. Secondary outcomes included complications (Clavien-Dindo), conversion, transfusion, additional analgesia, and Female Sexual Function Index (FSFI) and Female Genital Self-Image Scale (FGSIS) scores at 6 months. Baseline characteristics were comparable between groups. Operative time (44.6 ± 17.1 vs 49.1 ± 16.5 min; p = .20), blood loss (124.0 ± 116.3 vs 132.8 ± 127.1 mL; p = .72), transfusion (7.7% vs 6.9%; p = .92), and intraoperative complications (7.7% vs 6.8%; p = .93) did not differ, and no conversion to laparotomy occurred. VAS pain at 12 hours was lower after vNOTES (1.9 ± 0.7 vs 3.2 ± 1.1; p = .002). FGSIS favored vNOTES (24.28 ± 1.80 vs 22.31 ± 3.47; p = .009), whereas FSFI total and domain scores, including lubrication, were comparable between groups. There were no conversions from vNOTES to conventional laparoscopy or to laparotomy. Exploratory adjusted and IPTW sensitivity analyses were consistent with the primary unadjusted findings. Because approach selection was non-random, the pain and genital self-image differences cannot be causally attributed to the technique.ConclusionIn hemodynamically stable women with hemorrhagic ovarian cyst rupture, vNOTES is feasible and safe, with hemostatic efficacy comparable to CML and advantages in 12-hour pain and genital self-image. Prospective studies are warranted to confirm these findings.

Introduction

Hemorrhagic rupture of an ovarian cyst, most commonly of the corpus luteum, is a frequent cause of acute lower abdominal pain in reproductive-aged women and can produce hemoperitoneum with, occasionally, hemodynamic compromise [1]. In hemodynamically stable patients, conservative management is appropriate, while surgery is reserved for women with evidence of ongoing or clinically significant bleeding, defined as hemodynamic instability, a marked or progressive hemoglobin decline, radiologic progression of intraperitoneal fluid, or severe persistent pain despite adequate analgesia. Clinical series of ruptured ovarian cysts and hemorrhagic corpus luteum confirm that these criteria identify patients at higher risk of continued bleeding, in whom timely operative management is recommended to control hemorrhage and evacuate hemoperitoneum [1–3].

When surgery is indicated, minimally invasive approaches are preferred because they provide diagnostic confirmation and hemostatic treatment with less postoperative pain, shorter hospital stay, faster recovery, and superior cosmesis compared with laparotomy. Conventional multiport laparoscopy (CML) is the established standard, but requires multiple abdominal incisions associated with visible scarring, port-site pain, and occasional port-related complications [4, 5].

Vaginal natural orifice transluminal endoscopic surgery (vNOTES) offers a scarless alternative by using a posterior colpotomy to access the pouch of Douglas, enabling evacuation of hemoperitoneum and treatment of the ruptured cyst without abdominal incisions. Randomized and observational data in elective benign adnexal surgery indicate that vNOTES is feasible and safe, with reductions in operative time, postoperative pain, and analgesic use, and improved cosmetic satisfaction compared with CML [6, 7]. However, evidence specifically addressing vNOTES for hemorrhagic ovarian cyst rupture with hemoperitoneum—an unscheduled, time-sensitive indication—is lacking.

We therefore conducted a multicenter retrospective cohort study comparing vNOTES with CML in hemodynamically stable women undergoing urgent surgery for hemoperitoneum after hemorrhagic ovarian cyst rupture, focusing on operative outcomes, postoperative pain, quality of recovery, and patient-reported sexual and cosmetic outcomes.

Materials and methods

Study design and ethics

This multicenter retrospective cohort study compared vNOTES and CML for the surgical management of hemorrhagic ovarian cyst rupture with hemoperitoneum between January 2020 and December 2025 in the Departments of Obstetrics and Gynecology of two participating tertiary centers. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the institutional review board (Protocol: 26003; Decision 1: 44) in February 2026. Written informed consent for surgery and for the use of anonymized clinical data was obtained from all participants.

Participants and eligibility

All non-pregnant women aged ≥ 18 years who underwent minimally invasive surgery for ruptured ovarian cyst with hemoperitoneum during the study period were screened. Surgery was indicated when imaging (ultrasonography and/or computed tomography [CT] or magnetic resonance imaging) demonstrated hemoperitoneum associated with a ruptured ovarian cyst together with evidence of clinically significant bleeding, defined as a decline in hemoglobin on serial measurements and/or hemodynamic alteration (tachycardia and/or hypotension), with or without severe persistent abdominal pain despite adequate analgesia. Exclusion criteria were suspected or confirmed invasive ovarian malignancy, concomitant adnexal torsion or other pathology requiring a different surgical strategy, prior pelvic radiotherapy, American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) physical status ≥ IV, and refusal/inability to complete postoperative questionnaires. After application of these criteria, 42 patients were included: 13 underwent vNOTES and 29 underwent CML.

Preoperative assessment and group assignment

Preoperative work-up included history, bimanual pelvic examination, and transvaginal and/or transabdominal ultrasonography; CT was performed at the clinician’s discretion when sonographic findings were equivocal or better quantification was required. On contrast-enhanced CT, hemoperitoneum was estimated using the Depth of Total Pelvic Fluid Collection (DTFC) method on axial images at the level of the tubal isthmus, summing the depth in the anterior cul-de-sac (anterior peritoneum to anterior uterine wall) and posterior cul-de-sac (posterior uterine wall to rectum) [8]. Hemoperitoneum was categorized as small (DTFC < 3 cm), moderate (3–6 cm), or large (> 6 cm). Both approaches were explained to the patient. Feasibility of vNOTES was judged by vaginal accessibility, suspected adhesions, and surgeon experience; when feasible, the final choice between vNOTES and CML was made jointly with the patient. Baseline variables recorded were age, body mass index (BMI), parity, comorbidities, smoking status, prior abdominal/pelvic surgery, presenting symptoms, preoperative hemoglobin, and extent of hemoperitoneum on imaging. Cyst diameter, cyst laterality, suspected rupture location, and the presence of active bleeding on imaging or intraoperative inspection were also recorded to evaluate whether disease severity influenced approach selection.

Surgical technique

All procedures were performed under general anesthesia by surgeons (KG andVG) and experienced in advanced laparoscopy vNOTES (≥ 50 prior cases of the respective approach). Perioperative care followed a standardized Enhanced Recovery After Surgery (ERAS) protocol in both groups. Local anesthesia was not applied to the incision sites.

In the vNOTES group, patients were placed in lithotomy with Trendelenburg tilt. After vaginal antiseptic preparation and, when appropriate, placement of a uterine manipulator, a posterior colpotomy was created at the posterior fornix to enter the pouch of Douglas. A multichannel single-port device was inserted, pneumoperitoneum was established at 8–10 mmHg, and a 10-mm 30° laparoscope with conventional rigid laparoscopic instruments was used to confirm hemoperitoneum and the ruptured cyst, evacuate blood and clots, and perform cystectomy or hemostatic repair as indicated, followed by copious irrigation. The posterior colpotomy was closed with absorbable sutures under direct vision.

In the CML group, pneumoperitoneum was established at the umbilicus by Veress needle or open (Hasson) technique, and intra-abdominal pressure was maintained at 10–12 mmHg. A 10-mm umbilical trocar and two or three ancillary 5-mm trocars were inserted under direct vision. After inspection and confirmation of hemoperitoneum and ruptured cyst, cystectomy or hemostatic suturing/coagulation was performed as required, followed by evacuation of blood and clots and irrigation. Trocars were removed under direct vision, and fascial and skin closure were completed routinely. In both groups, the choice between ovarian preservation and partial oophorectomy was individualized according to patient age, reproductive wishes, and cyst features.

Outcome measures

Total operative time was defined as the interval from initial skin or vaginal incision to completion of cutaneous or mucosal closure. Estimated intraoperative blood loss and perioperative blood transfusion (intraoperative or within 24 h) were extracted from anesthesia and operative records. Intraoperative complications were defined as any adverse event between incision and closure, including organ injury, major bleeding requiring transfusion or conversion to laparotomy, or equipment-related events. Postoperative complications within 30 days were graded according to the Clavien–Dindo classification [9]. Length of hospital stay was calculated from the end of surgery to discharge. For vNOTES cases, conversion was recorded in two steps: conversion from vNOTES to conventional laparoscopy and conversion from either minimally invasive approach to laparotomy.

Postoperative pain was assessed with a 10-point visual analog scale (VAS) [10] at 6, 12, and 24 h after surgery; the need for additional analgesia within the first 24 h, beyond the ERAS-based regimen, was recorded. Early recovery was assessed once on the day of discharge using the previously developed and validated Quality of Recovery-15 (QoR-15) questionnaire [11], where higher scores indicate better global recovery. At 6 months, patient-reported sexual function was assessed with the validated Female Sexual Function Index (FSFI) [12, 13] and genital self-image with the Female Genital Self-Image Scale (FGSIS) [14].

Primary outcomes were total operative time, estimated intraoperative blood loss, VAS pain scores at 6, 12, and 24 h, length of hospital stay, and QoR-15 score at discharge. Secondary outcomes included intraoperative and postoperative complications, conversion to open surgery, need for additional postoperative analgesics within 24 h, perioperative blood transfusion, and FSFI and FGSIS scores at 6-month follow-up.

Statistical analysis

Categorical variables are presented as frequencies and percentages and were compared using the χ² test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. Continuous variables are expressed as mean ± standard deviation or median (interquartile range, IQR) according to distribution, assessed with the Shapiro–Wilk test, and were compared using the independent-samples t test or Mann–Whitney U test. Given the relatively small and unequal group sizes (vNOTES n = 13; CML n = 29) and the exploratory nature of the study, propensity score matching was not undertaken; instead, baseline comparability was examined directly, and all eligible patients were included. No formal a priori sample-size calculation was performed; the cohort represents all consecutive eligible cases during the study period. A two-sided p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Analyses were performed with SPSS Statistics version 26 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY). Because of the small sample size and limited number of outcome events, formal propensity score matching was not performed. To partially address potential selection bias, exploratory multivariable linear regression models for key continuous outcomes were adjusted for age, BMI, prior pelvic or abdominal surgery, and hemoperitoneum extent; an inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) sensitivity analysis based on the same covariates was also performed. These analyses were interpreted as supportive rather than confirmatory.

Results

Baseline characteristics

Forty-two women met the inclusion criteria: 13 underwent vNOTES (31.0%) and 29 underwent CML (69.0%). Baseline demographic and clinical characteristics—age distribution, BMI category, parity, comorbidities, smoking status, prior abdominal or pelvic surgery, preoperative hemoglobin level, and the proportion of patients with moderate or large hemoperitoneum on imaging—were comparable between groups (all p > .05; Table 1). Cyst diameter, cyst laterality, suspected rupture location, and the presence of active bleeding were also balanced between groups (all p > .05; Table 2).

Intraoperative Outcomes

Intraoperative findings and perioperative outcomes are presented in Table 2. Mean total operative time did not differ significantly between vNOTES and CML (44.6 ± 17.1 vs. 49.1 ± 16.5 min; p = .20). Estimated blood loss was low and similar between groups (124.0 ± 116.3 vs. 132.8 ± 127.1 mL; p = .72), as were rates of perioperative blood transfusion (7.7% vs. 6.9%; p = .92) and intraoperative complications (7.7% vs. 6.8%; p = .93). No conversion to laparotomy occurred in either group. No vNOTES procedure required conversion to conventional laparoscopy, and no patient in either group required conversion to laparotomy. The similarity of cyst size, rupture characteristics, and active bleeding between groups suggests that measured disease-severity variables were unlikely to be the primary drivers of route selection, although unmeasured confounding cannot be excluded.

Postoperative Pain, Recovery, and Length of Stay

Early postoperative outcomes and patient-reported recovery are summarized in Table 3. VAS pain scores tended to be lower after vNOTES, with a statistically significant reduction at 12 h (1.9 ± 0.7 vs. 3.2 ± 1.1; p = .002); differences at 6 and 24 h did not reach significance (3.1 ± 1.0 vs. 3.9 ± 1.3, p = .06; and 1.8 ± 0.8 vs. 2.1 ± 0.9, p = .24, respectively). Additional analgesic use within the first 24 h was less frequent after vNOTES (15.4% vs. 34.5%; p = .06), and QoR-15 scores at discharge were numerically higher (127.4 ± 9.8 vs. 121.2 ± 11.3; p = .07). Median length of hospital stay was shorter after vNOTES (24 [24–30] vs. 28 [24–40] hours; p = .09), although this difference did not reach statistical significance. Exploratory adjusted regression and IPTW sensitivity analyses preserved the direction of the observed differences, including lower 12-hour VAS pain after vNOTES, while non-significant trends for analgesic use, QoR-15, and length of stay remained non-significant.

Sexual Function and Genital Self-Image at 6 Months

Sexual function and genital self-image outcomes at 6-month follow-up are summarized in Table 4. Mean FSFI total scores were comparable between women who underwent vNOTES and those who underwent CML (26.78 ± 2.53 vs. 27.30 ± 3.15; p = .38). No statistically significant differences were observed across the individual FSFI domains, including desire, arousal, lubrication, orgasm, satisfaction, and pain (all p > .05). In particular, lubrication scores were similar between the vNOTES and CML groups (4.62 ± 0.61 vs. 4.41 ± 0.81; p = .35). In contrast, mean FGSIS total scores were significantly higher in the vNOTES group than in the CML group (24.28 ± 1.80 vs. 22.31 ± 3.47; p = .009), indicating a more favorable postoperative genital self-image after the transvaginal approach.

Discussion

Principal Findings

In this multicenter retrospective cohort of 42 hemodynamically stable women undergoing urgent minimally invasive surgery for hemoperitoneum from a hemorrhagic ovarian cyst rupture, vNOTES achieved hemostatic and operative outcomes comparable to CML, with significantly lower pain scores at 12 h, a trend toward less analgesic use, and superior patient-reported genital self-image at 6 months. To our knowledge, this is the first comparative study of vNOTES versus CML for this acute indication.

Comparison with Prior Literature

Existing cohorts on hemoperitoneum from corpus luteum or hemorrhagic ovarian cyst rupture have clarified when surgery is indicated, but have not explored alternative minimally invasive access routes. Lee et al. [2] identified lower initial hemoglobin and greater cul-de-sac fluid depth as independent predictors of operative management. Kim et al. [8] reported that conservative management succeeded in most stable women, with surgery reserved for those with progressive bleeding. Balalau et al. [3] similarly advocated conservative management in stable patients and recommended laparoscopy whenever surgery is required, reserving laparotomy for circulatory collapse. Our findings extend this literature by adding a vaginal scarless option to the minimally invasive armamentarium for this specific indication.

Randomized data from the NOTABLE trial demonstrated that vNOTES adnexectomy is non-inferior to laparoscopic adnexectomy for benign pathology and is associated with shorter operative time, lower postoperative pain, and reduced analgesic use [6]. Observational studies comparing vNOTES with conventional laparoscopy for adnexal pathologies have confirmed feasibility and reported low pain scores and high cosmetic satisfaction [7]. However, those studies focus on elective benign indications, and patients with acute hemoperitoneum have been systematically excluded. Emerging case series describe vNOTES for emergencies such as ectopic pregnancy and adnexal torsion, but hemorrhagic ovarian cyst rupture has not been specifically examined. The present study addresses that gap with comparative data in a homogeneous population. Beyond adnexal surgery, accumulating experience with vNOTES hysterectomy has demonstrated feasibility across different uterine sizes and patient body habitus, and stepwise technical descriptions have helped standardize safe transvaginal access [15–18]. A recent systematic review and meta-analysis comparing vNOTES with laparoendoscopic single-site surgery in benign hysterectomy also reported favorable perioperative outcomes for the transvaginal approach [19]. Early gynecologic NOTES experience further supports the conceptual basis for scarless pelvic surgery, although emergency adnexal bleeding remains insufficiently studied [20]. A recently published national vNOTES guideline, developed by the PETKOZ Association combining a systematic review with expert consensus, now provides standardized recommendations on patient selection and perioperative management that may inform future application of the transvaginal approach to acute adnexal indications [21].

Mechanistic Considerations

The bleeding pattern in hemorrhagic ovarian cyst rupture offers a mechanistic explanation for the observed advantages of vNOTES. Hemoperitoneum typically accumulates in the pouch of Douglas, which is directly accessed through a posterior colpotomy. In our series, use of a multichannel transvaginal port, low-pressure pneumoperitoneum (8–10 mmHg), and standard rigid laparoscopic instruments allowed efficient evacuation of intraperitoneal blood and hemostatic treatment of the ruptured cyst without abdominal incisions. This direct caudal access likely contributes to operative times and blood loss comparable to those of CML while eliminating abdominal trocar–related morbidity. The absence of port-site nociception, in turn, may explain the lower 12-hour VAS scores and the trend toward reduced analgesic demand observed in the vNOTES group. Consistent with this, adjunctive paracervical block during vNOTES has been shown to further reduce early postoperative pain and intraoperative opioid consumption within enhanced-recovery pathways [22].

Patient-Reported and Cosmetic Outcomes

Patient-reported outcomes further support the cosmetic and psychosexual benefits of vNOTES. Overall sexual function (FSFI total) was preserved and similar between groups, with a small reduction in the lubrication domain after vNOTES that nevertheless remained within the range of preserved function and is unlikely to be clinically meaningful. By contrast, FGSIS scores at 6 months were significantly more favorable in the vNOTES group (24.28 ± 1.80 vs. 22.31 ± 3.47; p = .009), consistent with the absence of visible abdominal scars [14]. Genital self-image is an established correlate of sexual quality of life [14, 23], and these data underscore the relevance of cosmetic outcomes in young women requiring emergency surgery for a benign physiologic event.

Strengths and Limitations

Strengths of this study include the multicenter design, standardized ERAS protocol across both arms, prospective use of validated patient-reported outcome instruments (VAS, QoR-15, FSFI, FGSIS), and the homogeneity of the surgical indication. Limitations include the retrospective design, modest sample size—particularly in the vNOTES arm—and the potential for selection bias related to vaginal accessibility, suspected adhesions, and surgeon experience. Because the choice of vNOTES depended on vaginal accessibility, absence of suspected adhesions, and surgeon experience, the vNOTES group may represent an anatomically more favorable, positively selected subgroup; consequently, the lower 12-hour VAS pain scores and the more favorable FGSIS may be at least partly attributable to this selection rather than to the surgical technique itself, and these differences cannot be interpreted as causal effects of the approach. The absence of a formal sample-size calculation means that nominally non-significant differences (e.g., 6-hour pain, QoR-15, length of stay, additional analgesic use) may reflect type II error. Six-month follow-up may also underestimate later differences in cosmetic and sexual outcomes. The small sample size is the most important limitation; in particular, the vNOTES group included only 13 patients, limiting statistical power and increasing the risk of type II error. Therefore, clinically meaningful trends in QoR-15, additional analgesic requirement, and length of hospital stay should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating rather than evidence of equivalence or absence of effect. Although exploratory adjusted and IPTW analyses were performed, residual confounding and model instability cannot be excluded because of the limited number of patients and events. Six-month follow-up may be adequate for early recovery and cosmetic assessment, but longer-term evaluation of sexual function, dyspareunia, vaginal symptoms, and pelvic floor outcomes is needed to better define the safety profile of the transvaginal approach.

Conclusion

In hemodynamically stable women with hemoperitoneum from a hemorrhagic ovarian cyst rupture, vNOTES is a feasible and safe alternative to CML, with at least comparable hemostatic efficacy and potential advantages in early postoperative pain and patient-reported genital self-image. In centers with vNOTES expertise, this approach may be considered alongside CML in carefully selected patients, while conservative management remains appropriate for stable women without progression and laparotomy is reserved for circulatory collapse. Prospective multicenter, adequately powered studies are needed to validate these findings, refine selection criteria, and define the place of vNOTES in standardized algorithms for this acute gynecologic emergency. Future studies should include longer follow-up for sexual and pelvic floor outcomes and should use prospective designs with adequate power or robust propensity-based adjustment to minimize selection bias.
