Public access defibrillation by activated community first responders – the randomized HeartRunner trial
Authors: Fredrik Folke, Linn Andelius, Annette Kjær Ersbøll, Christian Torp-Pedersen, Gunnar Gislason, Lars Køber, Freddy Lippert, Mads Christian Tofte Gregers, Persia Shahriari, Victor Kjærulf, Carolina Malta Hansen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe HeartRunner trial was an investigator-initiated, randomized clinical controlled trial (RCT) examining whether community first responders (CFRs) activated through a smartphone application can improve survival after out-of-hospital cardiac arrest (OHCA).ObjectivesThe study compared standard care alone versus standard care with the addition of dispatched CFRs for patients with OHCA.MethodsThe trial was conducted in the Capital Region of Denmark, with a catchment area of approximately 1.9 million inhabitants. By May 2019, 15,401 citizens had registered to serve as CFRs (837/100,000 inhabitants) and roughly 6200 automated external defibrillators (344/100,000 inhabitants) were publicly accessible in the study area (2.4 defibrillators per km2).The primary outcome was 30-day survival. Secondary outcomes included bystander defibrillation, bystander cardiopulmonary resuscitation, return of spontaneous circulation before hospital arrival, favorable neurological outcome at hospital discharge, and 1-year survival. As safety outcomes, the trial examined the potential physical or psychological risk involved for the activated CFR. The trial was expected to randomize 4188 cases of suspected OHCAs over a period of 7 years and was terminated February 28, 2026, when inclusion was completed. The full study protocol is available at www.clinicaltrials.gov (identifier: NCT03835403).Keywords: Out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, Cardiopulmonary resuscitation, Bystander defibrillation, Community first responders, Automated external defibrillators

Introduction

Out-of-hospital cardiac arrest (OHCA) is a major health problem, with more than 300,000 OHCAs annually in Europe.1 Despite improvements in bystander cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) and survival, overall survival remains low around 8–10% in most communities.2 Survival rates above 50% have been reported when defibrillation is performed within five minutes.3, 4, 5, 6 In Denmark, efforts to promote bystander defibrillation including educating the public in CPR and use of automated external defibrillators (AEDs), establishing a national AED registry linked all emergency dispatch centers, as well as campaigns to increase AED accessibility have been associated with an 8-fold increase in bystander defibrillation in public locations (<2% in 2005 to approximately 16% in 2017).7 However, these efforts have not translated into the same increase for residential OHCAs, where the majority of OHCAs occur, for which a modest increase in bystander defibrillation (<1% in 2005 to approximately 4% in 2017) was observed.7 In most countries, bystander defibrillation in residential OHCAs is <2%.8, 9

To improve early bystander interventions, text message or smartphone alert systems have been implemented worldwide to dispatch community first responders (CFRs) to perform CPR and retrieve a nearby AED.10, 11 The only randomized controlled trial (RCT) assessing CFR activation in addition to standard care published in 2015 found an increase in bystander CPR, but the study was not powered to assess a potential survival gain.12 The pilot study of the CFR system found increased bystander defibrillation in cases where CFRs arrived prior to the Emergency Medical Services (EMS),13 but did not find association with increased survival. CFR activation has also been associated with reduced time from collapse to first defibrillation14 and increased survival.15 For this reason, the International Liaison Committee on Resuscitation (ILCOR) and the European Resuscitation Council (ERC) recommended the implementation of CFR programs while acknowledging the current very low level of evidence.16, 17 Importantly, activating CFRs carries a risk of inflicting psychological distress or physical injuries on those who have been dispatched, further underlining the need for accurately assessing the effect of such programs.16, 18

Study aims

The primary aim of the HeartRunner trial was to test whether activating CFRs can improve survival after OHCA compared to standard EMS response alone. Secondary outcomes included bystander defibrillation, bystander CPR, return of spontaneous circulation before hospital arrival, favorable neurological outcome at hospital discharge, and 1-year survival. The trial also investigated the risk of physical injuries and psychological distress among dispatched CFRs.

Methods

Protocol

Version 3, December 3, 2020, identifier NCT03835403

The protocol was developed in accordance with the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials (SPIRIT) statement.19 The trial was registered at ClinicalTrials.gov on February 8, 2019 (NCT03835403). The full protocol is available at ClinicalTrials.gov.

Important protocol modifications will be communicated to relevant parties.

Design

Investigator-initiated, investigator-blinded, superiority, RCT, comparing 30-day survival between OHCA patients receiving standard care to standard care with the addition of dispatched CFRs. No patients nor the public were involved in the design, conduct, interpretation of dissemination of the study.

Settings

The trial was conducted in the Capital Region of Denmark which covers 2560 km2, has a resident population of ≈1.9 million and an incidence of approximately 1400 OHCAs/year. One emergency dispatch center covers the entire study area with one single activation number (1–1-2). The EMS and health systems are public, and patients are treated free of charge. A detailed description of the study settings, including protocol for OHCA response by the dispatch center, activation of CFRs and the EMS, is provided in Supplements A.

Eligibility criteria

Inclusion criteria for CFR randomization were all suspected out-of-hospital cardiac arrests within the Capital Region of Denmark of non-traumatic origin, outside nursing homes, and ≥8 years of age. It can be challenging for emergency dispatchers to gather sufficient information about the patient within the first few minutes and CFRs may have been activated to OHCAs with one or more exclusion criteria. Since randomization occurred for all cases in which the CFR system was activated, cases with any of the exclusion criteria above will be secondarily excluded.20 Post randomization exclusion criteria were: (1) patients without cardiac arrest, (2) cardiac arrests witnessed by the emergency medical services, (3) cardiac arrests in nursing homes or with signs of trauma, suicide, drowning, or intoxication, (4) cardiac arrests not treated by the emergency medical services due to ethical reasons or obvious signs of death, and (5) cardiac arrest patients under eight years. These cases will be accounted for but not included in analyses for the primary outcome. Excluded cases are anticipated to be similar in the control and the intervention group. An analysis including all cases will also be reported. The pilot study showed that approximately 64% of suspected OHCAs for which the CFR system was activated were true OHCAs.13

The CFR system

The CFR system is based on smartphone app technology (Heartrunner app) which was implemented at the emergency dispatch center in the Capital Region of Denmark September 1, 2017. When the system is activated by the emergency dispatcher, the location of the incoming emergency call is compared to the last known positions of the CFRs connected to the app. A maximum of 20 CFRs ≤ 1.8 km from the suspected OHCA (corresponding to a response time of approximately 15 min with an estimated travel speed of 2 m/s [4.5 mph]) are alerted to either go directly to the OHCA location and start CPR or to retrieve an AED prior to going to the OHCA location. For every 5 CFRs alerted, the first 4 are directed to retrieve an AED and the last is directed to go directly to the OHCA location and assist with CPR. The app refers only to AEDs accessible at the time of the alarm using accessibility information from the AED Network.21, 22 By May 2019, roughly 6200 AEDs (344/100,000 inhabitants) were publicly accessible in the study area (2.4 defibrillators per km2). In November 2020, a cancellation feature was added to the CFR system, enabling dispatchers to abort the CFR alert at the discretion of the dispatchers. A detailed description of CFR activation is included in Supplements B. In May 2021, the activation radius was increased from 1.8 km to 5 km to ensure a sufficient number of available CFRs in less densely populated areas.

By May 2023, emergency dispatchers were able to make changes to the OHCA address, which was followed by notifications to CFR smartphone app.

CFR population

CFRs are individuals who have registered through the CFR app (HeartRunner app).13 The only requirement for registration is age ≥ 18 years. Prior training in CPR and AED use is highly recommended, but not mandatory. Approximately 98% of CFRs report previous CPR training.13, 18

By May 2019, 15,401 individuals had registered to serve as CFRs (837/100,000 inhabitants) in the study area.

Intervention

During the study, the OHCA response plan always included standard care as described in Supplements A. In the intervention arm, in addition to activating standard care, the activation of CFRs was carried out through the CFR system. In the control arm, CFRs were not alerted. The trial diagram is displayed in Fig. 1. As a strategy to improve adherence and to achieve adequate participant enrollment to reach target sample size, we implemented teaching sessions for the emergency dispatchers to increase awareness and utilization of the CFR system.

Randomization and allocation

Trial treatment has been allocated 1:1 in blocks of 10 as per a computer-generated randomisation schedule. The schedule was generated by HeartRunner and was kept securely at the HeartRunner mission server. The mission server used a variable block size of 4, 6 and 8, meaning that for each new block the next block was randomly selected to have size 4, 6 or 8. The intervention/control ratio matched every block size used. Randomisation ran in 3 independent strata, as described in detail in Supplements C. Strata 1, 2 and 3 were defined as OHCAs where ≥4 CFRs were estimated located <3 min (0–359 m), 3–9 min (360–1080 m), and ≥9 min (>1080 m) away, in a straight line from OHCA location, respectively. In all cases of suspected OHCA with no exclusion criteria fulfilled, the emergency dispatchers activated the standard response for OHCA including activation of CFRs. Thus, CFRs were only alerted in 50% of suspected OHCA cases.

Blinding

The allocated treatment was blinded to both the emergency dispatchers and the investigators. All cases of suspected OHCA for which the CFR system was activated were assigned a unique incident-ID. This number was the intervention key, and allocation was blinded until the randomization code was broken at final analysis. There were no circumstances where unblinding was permissible.

Survey and defusing

Ninety minutes after an alarm, all activated CFRs received a link to an electronic survey by text message (Supplements D).18, 23 Questions included information on whether CFRs arrived before EMS, whether they performed CPR, applied an AED, and whether the AED delivered a shock. They also reported whether they suffered physical injuries and/or degree of psychological distress. Psychological distress was reported on a 5-point Likert scale from not affected to severely affected.18, 23, 24 Those who reported severe distress were contacted and offered defusing by healthcare personnel. In case of missing survey response, a reminder was sent the following day.18 CFRs who did not respond to the reminder were contacted by telephone, e-mail or text-message. A detailed log was kept and sent to the Data and Safety and Monitoring Board (DSMB), as described in Supplements G.

Data collection

Detailed information on data sources is presented in Supplements E. All incoming 1-1-2 calls are registered at the emergency dispatch center. Seven data sources were used: the mission server, the dispatch system at Copenhagen EMS, the Danish Cardiac Arrest Registry,25 prehospital patient chart, patient hospital charts, CFR mission survey, and the Danish Civil Registration System.

The data including individual de-identified patient data was stored on a secure server located in the Capital Region.

Outcome measures

The primary outcome was 30-day survival obtained from the Danish Civil Registration System. Secondary outcomes were bystander defibrillation prior to EMS arrival, bystander CPR prior to EMS arrival, return of spontaneous circulation at hospital arrival, neurological intact survival at hospital discharge (cerebral performance category score of 1–2)26 and 1-year survival. For safety outcomes, the trial examined the risk of physical injuries and psychological distress among CFRs who have accepted an alarm.

Power calculations

This was a superiority trial and the sample size and power calculations were based on an assumed effect size using a binary outcome (30-day survival). Power calculations were estimated by Fisher’s Exact Test according to estimated time from CFR activation to arrival at the OHCA scene. Strata were (using a power of 80% and a significance level (two-sided test) of 5%): <3 min: 536 patients (increase from 15% to 25%) and 3–9 min (increase from 15% to 25%): 536 patients. Stratum 3 was expected to include very few patients and was therefore not expected have power to show a difference in outcomes. The full description is included in Supplements F.

Statistical analyses

A detailed description of power calculations and the statistical analysis plan is available on https://www.clinicaltrials.gov (identifier: NCT03835403).27 Analyses will be performed following an intention-to-treat principle (patients with true OHCA for whom the CFR system was activated vs. not activated). Baseline characteristics will be summarized as means and standard deviations, medians and interquartile ranges, or percentages.

The primary outcome will be reported as proportion of patients surviving 30 days for the intervention and control group. Fisher’s Exact Test will be used to test a statistically significant difference in 30-day survival between intervention and control group. Results will be presented as a relative risk (RR) with a corresponding 95% confidence interval (95% CI) and a risk difference (RD) with a corresponding 95% CI, Table 1. Survival during the first 30 days will be presented using cumulative survival.

Additional statistical analysis

In addition to the standard frequentist analysis, to aid interpretation of the results, we will also perform a Bayesian analysis to estimate the posterior distribution of the odds ratio and the corresponding 95% credible intervals for each outcome stratified by time stratum, as described in the statistical analysis plan.

Ethics and legal aspects

This study was presented to the Regional Committee on Health Research Ethics for the Capital Region of Denmark who concluded that the trial was not a notifiable health-science research project under Danish law and that it could be initiated without ethics committee approval (Journal nr.: 17018804). This determination was based on the nature of the trial: randomization occurred at the level of the emergency call for suspected OHCA, standard EMS response and dispatcher-assisted CPR instructions were provided in both groups, and activation of CFRs was an adjunct intended to shorten the time to CPR and AED retrieval rather than to provide treatment beyond standard resuscitation care. CFR provided consent to the app terms and to the survey, including geolocation while logged into the app and contact for research purposes after missions. The study was registered with the Danish Data Protection Agency through the Capital Region of Denmark and with the Danish Patient Safety Authority. An independent DSMB monitored trial safety and have access to unblinded grouped results during the trial. The study team have developed a plan to disseminate the study findings to relevant stakeholders and to the public.

To ensure confidentiality, all patient data is strictly held in trust by study investigators.

The study will not involve post-trial care, as defusing of CFRs was performed during the study.

Data and safety monitoring board

A DSMB with statistical, cardiological and prehospital expertise followed the study. See Supplements G for detailed description of the DSMB.

Trial period

The HeartRunner trial started May 15, 2019, and was planned to run for approximately 7 years until the planned inclusion number was reached or the independent DSMB recommended termination of the study due to significant difference in survival between control and intervention arm or due to increased risk of physical injuries as well as psychological distress among activated CFRs. The trial was terminated February 28, 2026, as the planned inclusion number was reached.

Discussion

Activating CFRs may, at first, seem unnecessary to test in an RCT. However, activating CFRs through an app-based alert system does not necessarily translate into increased survival, since many potential responders may not be able to respond and those who do may not arrive prior to EMS.13, 29 Further, it is unknown how many minutes before EMS arrival a CFR needs to arrive in order to improve OHCA survival.12 As underscored by the latest ERC guidelines, there is currently no evidence providing a causal relationship between dispatched CFRs and survival.16, 30 The most accurate assessment of a treatment effect is achieved through RCTs. Importantly, one RCT evaluated the activation of CFRs in OHCA and found a significant increase in the proportion of patients who received bystander CPR as compared to standard care.12 This study did not find a significant increase in survival, which may have been due to lack of power, since the study was not designed to detect a difference in survival.

By May 2019, during the pilot phase, the CFR program had already achieved substantial engagement from the population with more than 30,000 active CFRs in Denmark, 15,401 (837 CFRs/100,000 inhabitants) of whom lived in the study area.13 The majority of registered CFRs did not have a healthcare background thus likely limited exposure to critical illness.23, 31

CFRs volunteered to assist in case of a nearby OHCA which underscored the need to ensure their physical and psychological safety.16 During the pilot phase of this study, 1.0% of CFRs reported severe psychological stress and one case of serious physical injury was reported (an ankle fracture),13 in accordance with previous studies which have found mild psychological distress among CFRs.12, 26 Systematic follow-up of all CFRs who had been dispatched was conducted to monitor safety throughout the study period.13

Conclusions

The HeartRunner trial was RCT testing whether CFRs activated through a smartphone application in addition to standard prehospital OHCA care could improve 30-day survival from OHCA. The intervention was blinded to emergency dispatchers and investigators. The trial also evaluated whether it is safe (physically and psychologically) to activate CFRs to nearby OHCA. The trial was completed in February 2026.
