Endomyocardial Biopsy in Suspected Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor‐Myocarditis: Histologic Spectrum and Correlations With Clinical and Cardiac Magnetic Resonance Features
Authors: Francesco Cribari, Maya Khatoun, Soledad Henriquez, Marie‐Liesse Joulia, Raphael Colle, Delphine Loirat, Nicolas Girard, Cristophe Caussin, Delphine Colin, Mariana Mirabel
Published: 2026

Immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI)‐myocarditis is often suspected but diagnosis in asymptomatic patients, difficult. The reference standard, endomyocardial biopsy (EMB), is invasive and based on pre‐ICI era criteria. 1 Correlation between pathology findings and clinical or imaging data is scarce. 2 Cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR) could potentially present as an alternative to EMB but its accuracy in the setting of complex cancer therapies is unclear. We examined correlations between EMB, clinical, biological, and CMR findings in suspected ICI‐myocarditis from the FAST TRACK ICI (Fast‐Track Cardiovascular Assessment for Suspicion of Cardiovascular Events on Immunecheckpoint Inhibitors) prospective cohort (NCT05799898; IRB 2022‐08). 3 Data are available upon reasonable request.

EMB was performed in 48 of 175 prospective patients with suspected ICI‐myocarditis when diagnosis was uncertain or required for cancer therapy decisions. All patients provided informed consent and no complications occurred. All 3 left ventricular samples were read blinded to clinical data. Immunohistochemical staining characterized inflammatory cell populations (trichrome stain), including CD3 (clone 2GV6, Roche) for T lymphocytes, CD68 (clone PG‐M1, Dako) for macrophages, as per Dallas' recommended criteria, with no mechanistic CD4+, CD8+, or PD1, which may constitute a limitation of the study albeit reflecting real‐life practice. By counting CD3‐positive T cells and averaging counts per mm2 and hotspots, the threshold of >14 total leukocytes/mm2 (including ≥7 CD3+ T cells/mm2) was used. Definite myocarditis met the threshold with necrosis; otherwise, borderline.

CMR included T1 and T2 mapping, late gadolinium enhancement (LGE) acquired on a 1.5T GE scanner using the same software for interpretation (SuiteHeart) by a single reader blinded to clinical data. Variables were approximately normally distributed. Continuous variables were compared using the exact Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical variables were compared using Fisher's exact test. All tests were 2 sided, and P<0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Characteristics included 37 (77.1%) female, mean age 59 (SD 13.5) years old, 31 (64.6%) with breast cancer, 6 (12.5) on ICI alone, 20 (41.7%) had anthracyclines, 3 (6.3%) thymoma, and 16 (33.3%) metastatic cancer. Cardiovascular/muscle symptoms were present in 21 (43.8%), ECG was abnormal in 14 (29.2%), and 18 (37.5%) had extracardiovascular immune‐related adverse events. Definite myocarditis was found in 4/48 patients (8.3%), borderline myocarditis in 39/48, and no myocarditis in 5/48 patients (10.4%). Five of 48 had their CMR or EMB within 24 hours after starting steroids and 2 received steroids within 24 hours before EMB. T‐lymphocytic (CD3+T) infiltration was predominant with minimal scattered macrophages (CD68+), neutrophils or eosinophils. Median density of CD3+T cells was 16/mm2 (interquartile range [IQR], 11.0–22.7); median 114/mm2 (IQR, 28–200) in definite cases, versus 16/mm2 (IQR, 11–22) in borderline (P<0.001).

Half of the definite cases had thymoma (2/4, 50%). Breast cancer treated with anthracyclines was frequent in borderline cases (29/39, 74.4%), with similar cumulative doses (mean difference 1.8 mg/m2; Cohen's d=0.014; P=0.81), none had definite EMB. All patients with definite EMB had metastatic disease within the first 3 ICI cycles. ICI monotherapy was more common in definite cases (3/4, 75%) than borderline (6/37, 16.2%; P=0.001).

Median peak troponin rose across biopsy categories: 1.7×upper limit of normal (normal), 2.2×upper limit of normal (borderline), 4.5×upper limit of normal (definite; P=0.029), with lymphocytic density correlating with troponin (r=0.30, P=0.021), albeit weakly. Creatine phosphokinase was higher in definite (median 741 U/L) versus borderline (61 U/L; P=0.035).

In borderline or definite biopsies, T cells >22/mm2 (75th percentile) were associated with <7 ICI cycles (P=0.003), myocardial necrosis (P=0.004), cardiovascular/muscular symptoms (P=0.041), hospitalization (P=0.015), nonbreast/lung cancers (P=0.034), and Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events >2 (P=0.052). No patient died from cardiovascular death at 30 days. On CMR, modified Lake Louise criteria were met in 15/48 patients, including all definite (4/4) and 11/39 borderline cases: 3 elevation of native T1 and T2 values; 4 abnormal T1, T2, and LGE; 5 high T1 values and LGE; and 3 high T2 and LGE. Definite myocarditis more often showed systolic dysfunction (2/4 with left ventricular ejection fraction <50%; P=0.022). LGE was seen in 24/48 (75% definite, 54% borderline), consistently involving the inferior wall in definite cases (100% versus 28%; P=0.011–0.032); borderline cases had multifocal, heterogeneous LGE.

In borderline myocarditis, lymphocytic infiltrate >16/mm2 was prominent across troponin quartiles (41% versus 32%; Fisher's exact P=0.007), associated with higher native T1 globally (median 073 ms [IQR, 54] versus 1033 ms [IQR, 121], P=0.015) and regionally (median 1096 ms [IQR, 125] versus 1040 ms [IQR, 837], P=0.018).

In this cohort, definite myocarditis on EMB correlated with clinical, biomarker, and CMR features. Lymphocytic infiltration correlated with troponin and T1 values. Our findings support evidence of ICI‐cardiotoxicity severity, including troponin rise or underlying immune diseases like thymoma. 4 , 5 Diagnosing nonsevere ICI‐myocarditis is challenging, especially with multiple cardiotoxic cancer therapies. Limited CMR access and low LGE sensitivity remain issues. Higher T1 values corresponded to greater lymphocytic infiltration and myocardial loss. Combining troponin and T1 mapping with clinical features may improve ICI‐myocarditis diagnosis. 5 ICI‐myocarditis may be a continuum, with many benign mild cases.
