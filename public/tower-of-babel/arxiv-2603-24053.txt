Multi-filament coordination rescues active transport from inertia-induced spinning arrest
Anuradha Rajput, Arnab Bhattacharjee, Annwesha Dutta

Abstract
Active filaments driven by tangential forces can become trapped in a spinning state when attached to a heavy head, where activity and inertia drive persistent rotation rather than directed transport. Using three-dimensional Langevin dynamics of tangentially driven bead-spring chains anchored to a common heavy head, we demonstrate that increasing the filament number N f N_{\mathrm{f}} systematically rescues directed transport by sterically preventing the coiled conformations that underlie spinning. The rescue is established through three independent diagnostics: (i) the mean-square displacement recovers monotonic growth (transport rescue), (ii) the spatial tangent autocorrelation loses its negative dip signaling helical coiling (conformational rescue), and (iii) the tangent time autocorrelation ceases crossing zero (orientational rescue). At high bending stiffness ( κ b = 1000 \kappa_{\mathrm{b}}=1000 ), coiling is fully eliminated at a critical filament number N f ∗ ≈ 3 N_{\mathrm{f}}^{*}\approx 3 . At moderate stiffness ( κ b = 100 \kappa_{\mathrm{b}}=100 ), residual coiling persists ( min ⁡ C s ≈ − 0.13 \min C_{s}\approx-0.13 ) yet transport is still rescued—demonstrating that the destruction of spinning coherence , not coiling elimination, is the essential mechanism. The multi-filament architecture achieves up to five orders of magnitude transport enhancement. Two physically distinct rescue pathways emerge: at high stiffness, steric constraints force filaments into a coordinated bundle sustaining directed propulsion; at low stiffness, steric interactions destroy orientational coherence, producing enhanced active diffusion. These results demonstrate a purely mechanical, density-independent route to overcome inertia-induced motility arrest, with implications for synthetic microswimmer design, motor-driven filament assays, and multi-filament organization in biological systems.

II
Model and simulation

II.1
Active filament as a bead-spring chain

We model an active filament as a bead-spring chain of
N{+}1
monomers in three dimensions (Fig.
1
). A head bead (
i{=}1
) of diameter
\alpha\sigma
(
\alpha>1
) is followed by
N
body beads of diameter
\sigma
. The head bead serves as a proxy for a cargo, cell body, or other heavy structure to which the filament is anchored, and its larger mass and size relative to the body beads introduces the inertial asymmetry that drives the spinning transition.

The underdamped Langevin equation governing the motion of the
i
th bead is

m_{i}\,\ddot{\mathbf{r}}_{i}=-\gamma_{i}\,\dot{\mathbf{r}}_{i}-\nabla_{i}U+f_{a}^{(i)}\,\hat{\mathbf{t}}_{i}+\sqrt{2\gamma_{i}k_{\mathrm{B}}T}\;\bm{\eta}_{i}(t),

(1)

where
m_{i}
is the mass,
\gamma_{i}
is the friction coefficient, and
f_{a}^{(i)}=f_{a}
for body beads (
i=2,\ldots,N{+}1
) and
f_{a}^{(1)}=0
for the head bead. The total conservative potential
U=U_{\mathrm{bond}}+U_{\mathrm{bend}}+U_{\mathrm{WCA}}
comprises three contributions.

The
bond potential
enforces chain connectivity via a harmonic spring:

U_{\mathrm{bond}}=\frac{1}{2}\kappa_{\mathrm{s}}\sum_{i}\left(r_{i,i+1}-r_{0,i}\right)^{2},

(2)

where
\kappa_{\mathrm{s}}
is the spring constant,
r_{i,i+1}=|\mathbf{r}_{i+1}-\mathbf{r}_{i}|
is the distance between consecutive beads, and
r_{0,i}=\mathrm{rad}_{i}+\mathrm{rad}_{i+1}
is the equilibrium bond length (sum of bead radii). We use
\kappa_{\mathrm{s}}=1000\,k_{\mathrm{B}}T/\sigma^{2}
to enforce near-rigid bonds. The equilibrium bond length
r_{0,i}
equals the sum of the radii of the two bonded beads:
r_{0}=\sigma
for body–body bonds and
r_{0}=(1+\alpha)\sigma/2
for head–body bonds.

The
bending potential
imposes semiflexibility through a discrete bond-difference form:

U_{\mathrm{bend}}=\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm{b}}}{4}\sum_{i}\left|\hat{\mathbf{t}}_{i}-\hat{\mathbf{t}}_{i+1}\right|^{2}=\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm{b}}}{2}\sum_{i}\left(1-\cos\theta_{i}\right),

(3)

where
\hat{\mathbf{t}}_{i}
is the unit bond vector between beads
i
and
i{+}1
,
\theta_{i}
is the angle between consecutive bond vectors, and
\kappa_{\mathrm{b}}
is the bending modulus. The persistence length in the passive limit is
\ell_{p}=(\kappa_{\mathrm{b}}/2)\sigma/k_{\mathrm{B}}T
, reflecting the factor of
1/2
relative to the standard Kratky–Porod form
Doi1986
. We explore bending moduli ranging from
\kappa_{\mathrm{b}}=0
(fully flexible) to
\kappa_{\mathrm{b}}=1000\,k_{\mathrm{B}}T
.

The
excluded-volume interaction
is modeled by the Weeks–Chandler–Andersen (WCA) potential
Weeks1971
;
Kremer1990
:

U_{\mathrm{WCA}}(r_{ij})=\begin{cases}4\epsilon\!\left[\left(\frac{d_{ij}}{r_{ij}}\right)^{\!12}-\left(\frac{d_{ij}}{r_{ij}}\right)^{\!6}\right]+\epsilon,&r_{ij}<2^{1/6}d_{ij},\\[4.0pt]
0,&r_{ij}\geq 2^{1/6}d_{ij},\end{cases}

(4)

where
r_{ij}=|\mathbf{r}_{i}-\mathbf{r}_{j}|
,
d_{ij}=(\sigma_{i}+\sigma_{j})/2
is the interaction range set by the bead diameters, and
\epsilon=k_{\mathrm{B}}T
sets the energy scale. The WCA potential acts between all bead pairs separated by more than two bonds along the chain (i.e., bonded and 1–3 pairs are excluded), including beads on different filaments in the multi-filament model. This purely repulsive interaction is the sole source of inter-filament coupling and provides the steric frustration mechanism central to our results.

The active force on each body bead is
f_{a}\hat{\mathbf{t}}_{i}
, where
f_{a}
is the constant active force magnitude and
\hat{\mathbf{t}}_{i}=(\mathbf{r}_{i-1}-\mathbf{r}_{i})/|\mathbf{r}_{i-1}-\mathbf{r}_{i}|
is the unit tangent vector directed from bead
i
toward bead
i{-}1
(i.e., toward the head)
IseleHolder2015
;
Kolomeisky2007
. This tangential driving mimics the force generation by molecular motors walking along cytoskeletal filaments. The head bead (
i{=}1
) does not carry an active force; it is propelled passively by the cumulative thrust of the body beads.

The stochastic term
\bm{\eta}_{i}(t)
is a three-dimensional Gaussian white noise satisfying
\langle\eta_{i}^{\mu}(t)\rangle=0
and
\langle\eta_{i}^{\mu}(t)\,\eta_{j}^{\nu}(t^{\prime})\rangle=\delta_{ij}\delta_{\mu\nu}\delta(t-t^{\prime})
, where
\mu,\nu\in\{x,y,z\}
. The noise amplitude
\sqrt{2\gamma_{i}k_{\mathrm{B}}T}
ensures that the fluctuation-dissipation relation is satisfied in the absence of activity (
f_{a}=0
), recovering the correct equilibrium Boltzmann distribution.

II.2
Non-dimensionalization

The system dynamics are governed by three fundamental timescales: the inertial relaxation time
\tau_{\mathrm{i}}=m/\gamma
, the Brownian diffusion time
\tau_{\mathrm{d}}=\gamma\sigma^{2}/k_{\mathrm{B}}T
, and the active time
\tau_{\mathrm{a}}=\sigma/v_{0}
(where
v_{0}=f_{a}/\gamma
is the free-swimming speed of a body bead). We non-dimensionalize Eq. (
1
) using
\sigma
for length,
\tau_{\mathrm{d}}
for time, and
k_{\mathrm{B}}T
for energy. Defining
\tilde{\mathbf{r}}_{i}=\mathbf{r}_{i}/\sigma
and
\tilde{t}=t/\tau_{\mathrm{d}}
, and dividing through by
k_{\mathrm{B}}T/\sigma
, the dimensionless equation for body beads (
m_{i}=m
,
\gamma_{i}=\gamma
) becomes

\mathcal{M}\,\ddot{\tilde{\mathbf{r}}}_{i}=-\dot{\tilde{\mathbf{r}}}_{i}-\tilde{\nabla}_{i}\tilde{U}+\mathrm{Pe}\,\hat{\mathbf{t}}_{i}+\sqrt{2}\;\tilde{\bm{\eta}}_{i},

(5)

where the two key dimensionless parameters are

\mathcal{M}=\frac{mk_{\mathrm{B}}T}{\gamma^{2}\sigma^{2}}=\frac{\tau_{\mathrm{i}}}{\tau_{\mathrm{d}}},\qquad\mathrm{Pe}=\frac{\sigma f_{a}}{k_{\mathrm{B}}T}.

(6)

The dimensionless mass
\mathcal{M}
measures the ratio of inertial to diffusive timescales:
\mathcal{M}\ll 1
corresponds to the overdamped regime, while
\mathcal{M}\gtrsim 1
signals significant inertial effects. The Péclet number
\mathrm{Pe}
measures the ratio of active to thermal forces.

For the head bead, with mass
m_{\mathrm{h}}=\alpha^{3}m
(volume scaling at constant density) and friction
\gamma_{\mathrm{h}}=\alpha\gamma
(Stokes scaling with diameter), the dimensionless head mass is defined analogously as

M_{\mathrm{h}}=\frac{m_{\mathrm{h}}k_{\mathrm{B}}T}{\gamma_{\mathrm{h}}^{2}\sigma^{2}}=\frac{\alpha^{3}mk_{\mathrm{B}}T}{\alpha^{2}\gamma^{2}\sigma^{2}}=\alpha\mathcal{M}.

(7)

The linear scaling
M_{\mathrm{h}}=\alpha\mathcal{M}
arises because the Stokes friction (
\gamma_{\mathrm{h}}\propto\alpha
) partially compensates the volumetric mass (
m_{\mathrm{h}}\propto\alpha^{3}
). By contrast, non-dimensionalizing with the active time
\tau_{\mathrm{a}}
gives a head mass
\alpha^{2}\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
that couples geometry and inertia more strongly. Our choice of
\tau_{\mathrm{d}}
as the reference cleanly separates the inertial (
\mathcal{M}
), active (
\mathrm{Pe}
), and geometric (
\alpha
) control parameters (see Appendix
A
for the full derivation and comparison).

II.3
Multi-filament model

The multi-filament system consists of
N_{\mathrm{f}}
active filaments anchored to a common head bead (Fig.
1
). Each filament is an independent chain of
N
body beads obeying Eq. (
5
). The first body bead of each filament is bonded to the shared head bead, with its center placed at contact distance
r_{0}=(\alpha+1)\sigma/2
from the head center.

For
N_{\mathrm{f}}=1
the single filament is attached at the north pole of the head bead and initially extends along the
+z
axis. For
N_{\mathrm{f}}>1
the first filament retains this axial placement, while the remaining
N_{\mathrm{f}}-1
filaments are distributed on an equatorial ring around the head with equal angular spacing
\theta=2\pi/(N_{\mathrm{f}}-1)
. The anchor point of the
k
th equatorial filament (
k=1,\ldots,N_{\mathrm{f}}{-}1
) lies in the
xy
-plane at

\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{anchor},k}=\bigl(r_{0}\cos[(k{-}1)\theta],\;r_{0}\sin[(k{-}1)\theta],\;0\bigr).

(8)

All filaments are initialized as straight rods extending along the
+z
direction from their respective anchor points. The passive chain (
f_{a}=0
) is then equilibrated before switching on the active force, allowing the initial geometry to relax before data collection begins.

The head bead experiences the sum of all forces transmitted from all
N_{\mathrm{f}}
filaments, as well as its own friction and thermal noise. Using
m_{\mathrm{h}}=\alpha^{3}m
and
\gamma_{\mathrm{h}}=\alpha\gamma
, the dimensionless head equation is

\alpha\mathcal{M}\,\ddot{\tilde{\mathbf{r}}}_{\mathrm{h}}=-\alpha\,\dot{\tilde{\mathbf{r}}}_{\mathrm{h}}-\sum_{k=1}^{N_{\mathrm{f}}}\tilde{\nabla}_{\mathrm{h}}\tilde{U}_{k}+\sqrt{2\alpha}\;\tilde{\bm{\eta}}_{\mathrm{h}},

(9)

where the inertial prefactor
\alpha\mathcal{M}=M_{\mathrm{h}}
[Eq. (
7
)], the friction prefactor is
\gamma_{\mathrm{h}}/\gamma=\alpha
, the noise amplitude is
\sqrt{2\gamma_{\mathrm{h}}/\gamma}=\sqrt{2\alpha}
, and the sum runs over all filaments
k
. The head does not carry an active force.

Inter-filament interactions are
purely steric
: beads on different filaments interact only through the WCA excluded-volume potential, Eq. (
4
). No hydrodynamic coupling is included. This choice is deliberate: by excluding fluid-mediated interactions, we isolate the role of steric and elastic coordination mechanisms in the rescue
Reigh2012
;
Kim2003
. Hydrodynamic interactions would add an additional coupling channel that could enhance—but not diminish—the rescue effect; our results thus establish a lower bound on the multi-filament rescue.

II.4
Simulation details

We integrate the equations of motion using the Grønbech-Jensen–Farago (GJF) Langevin integrator
GJF2013
;
Allen2017
with a dimensionless timestep
\Delta\tilde{t}=10^{-3}
. The GJF scheme preserves the correct configurational temperature and diffusion coefficient for any timestep at which the trajectory remains stable, making it well suited for underdamped Langevin dynamics across a wide range of friction coefficients
GJF2013
. Each filament comprises
N=100
body beads, giving a total contour length of
L=100\sigma
. Simulations are run for
5\times 10^{6}
timesteps (corresponding to a total dimensionless time of
\tilde{t}_{\max}=5000
), and each parameter set is averaged over
50
independent runs with different random initial configurations and noise realizations.

We systematically explore the following parameter ranges: dimensionless head mass
M_{\mathrm{h}}=1
–
40
; bending stiffness
\kappa_{\mathrm{b}}=0
,
10
,
100
,
1000
; active force magnitude
F_{\mathrm{act}}=4
,
10
,
20
,
40
; and number of filaments
N_{\mathrm{f}}=1
–
7
. The head-to-body size ratio is
\alpha=3
throughout, giving
M_{\mathrm{h}}=3\mathcal{M}
. Initial configurations are generated by placing beads along a straight line with small random perturbations and equilibrating the passive chain (
f_{a}=0
) before switching on the active force.

III
Observables

We employ four complementary observables to characterize the spinning arrest and its rescue. The first (MSD) probes transport directly; the second (
\Phi
) provides a binary classification of the dynamical state; the third (SACF) reveals the spatial conformational structure; and the fourth (TTAF) captures the temporal orientational dynamics. Their quantitative agreement on the critical filament number
N_{\mathrm{f}}^{*}
provides a stringent self-consistency test.

III.1
Mean-square displacement and transport measure

The mean-square displacement of the head bead is computed as

\langle\delta^{2}(\tau)\rangle=\left\langle|\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{h}}(t_{0}+\tau)-\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{h}}(t_{0})|^{2}\right\rangle,

(10)

where the average
\langle\cdot\rangle
is taken over time origins
t_{0}
within each trajectory (standard time-averaging) and then over all
50
independent trajectories. We track the head bead displacement because the head represents the biologically relevant cargo-carrying element.

To quantify transport efficiency with a single number that avoids the ambiguities of fitting power-law exponents over an arbitrary time window, we define the reference MSD:

\mathrm{MSD}_{\mathrm{ref}}=\langle\delta^{2}(\tau)\rangle\big|_{\tau=\tau_{\mathrm{ref}}},

(11)

where
\tau_{\mathrm{ref}}
is chosen to be well beyond both the spinning period (
T_{\mathrm{spin}}\sim 60
–
90
) and the single-filament spinning coherence time (
\tau_{\mathrm{spin}}\sim 5000
–
15{,}000
), so that
\mathrm{MSD}_{\mathrm{ref}}
captures the long-time transport behavior.

III.2
Spinning order parameter
\Phi

The spinning state produces a distinctive non-monotonic MSD: after an initial ballistic increase, the MSD
decreases
at intermediate times as the filament completes half a rotation and the head returns toward its starting position, before increasing again as the slow radial drift accumulates over many cycles. To detect this signature quantitatively, we compute the local log-log slope of the MSD:

\beta(\tau)=\frac{d\ln\langle\delta^{2}(\tau)\rangle}{d\ln\tau},

(12)

evaluated via centered finite differences on the logarithmic MSD with Savitzky–Golay smoothing (window 31, polynomial order 2) to suppress statistical noise. Normal transport (
\langle\delta^{2}(\tau)\rangle\sim\tau^{\nu}
) gives
\beta=\nu>0
; the spinning state uniquely produces intervals where
\beta<0
(MSD decreasing).

The spinning order parameter is then defined as

\Phi=\frac{1}{\tau_{2}{-}\tau_{1}}\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}\Theta\!\bigl(-\beta(\tau)\bigr)\,d\tau,

(13)

where
\Theta
is the Heaviside step function. The integration window
[\tau_{1},\tau_{2}]=[10,10^{3}]
is chosen to bracket the intermediate-time regime:
\tau_{1}=10
excludes the short-time ballistic regime (where
\beta\approx 2
regardless of spinning), and
\tau_{2}=10^{3}
captures the MSD dips produced by coherent rotation while remaining below the coherence time where thermal drift dominates. Physically,
\Phi
measures the fraction of this window where the MSD is decreasing—a behavior unique to coherent periodic rotation. We classify states with
\Phi>0.1
as spinning (the threshold excludes minor statistical fluctuations around
\beta=0
) and
\Phi=0
as beating or propulsive.

III.3
Spatial tangent autocorrelation (SACF)

The bond tangent vector is defined consistently with the active force direction as
\hat{\mathbf{t}}_{i}=(\mathbf{r}_{i-1}-\mathbf{r}_{i})/|\mathbf{r}_{i-1}-\mathbf{r}_{i}|
, pointing from bead
i
toward the head. The spatial tangent autocorrelation function

C_{s}(s)=\langle\hat{\mathbf{t}}_{i}(t)\cdot\hat{\mathbf{t}}_{i+s}(t)\rangle

(14)

measures orientational correlations along the chain contour at separation
s
(in bond units). The average is taken over all body-bead indices
i
(excluding the head), over time, and over all independent trajectories. In the multi-filament case,
C_{s}(s)
is computed separately for each filament and then averaged over filaments.

For a passive worm-like chain in equilibrium,
C_{s}(s)
decays exponentially as
\exp(-s\sigma/\ell_{p})
, remaining positive for all
s
. The appearance of a
negative
region in
C_{s}(s)
at finite
s
is the structural hallmark of helical coiling: it indicates that bonds separated by
s
along the backbone are oriented in approximately opposite directions, meaning the chain has doubled back on itself. The depth of the negative minimum (denoted
\min C_{s}(s)
) quantifies the degree of coiling, while its position along
s
reflects the characteristic coiling wavelength (approximately half the coil circumference).

III.4
Tangent time autocorrelation (TTAF)

The tangent time autocorrelation function

C_{t}(\tau)=\langle\hat{\mathbf{t}}_{i}(t_{0})\cdot\hat{\mathbf{t}}_{i}(t_{0}+\tau)\rangle

(15)

measures how a given bond’s orientation decorrelates over lag time
\tau
. The average is taken over all body beads
i
, over time origins
t_{0}
, and over trajectories. In the multi-filament case, the TTAF is computed separately for each filament to reveal inter-filament differences in orientational dynamics (see Sec.
V.3
).

For a passive polymer,
C_{t}(\tau)
decays monotonically from unity to zero on the longest relaxation timescale. For a beating (non-spinning) active filament,
C_{t}(\tau)
also decays monotonically but with an activity-modified rate. For spinning filaments, the TTAF exhibits qualitatively distinct behavior: it oscillates through zero with a well-defined period
T_{\mathrm{spin}}
equal to the spinning rotation period, reflecting the periodic reversal of bond orientations as the coiled chain rotates. The decay envelope of these oscillations defines the spinning coherence time
\tau_{\mathrm{spin}}
—the duration over which spinning maintains phase coherence before thermal fluctuations randomize it. The TTAF thus provides both the spinning frequency (from
T_{\mathrm{spin}}
) and the spinning coherence time (from the envelope decay rate).

The multi-filament assembly exhibits distinct conformational regimes as a function of
\kappa_{\mathrm{b}}
and
F_{\mathrm{act}}
(Fig.

In the
disordered
regime [Fig.
10
(a)], the flexible filaments splay out from the head in random directions with significant local curvature and entanglement. Individual chains retain residual local coiling (
\min C_{s}(s)\approx-0.13
), visible as segments that double back on themselves, but the filaments are not organized into any coherent structure. This regime corresponds to the decorrelation rescue pathway: steric interactions destroy spinning coherence without imposing inter-filament alignment.

In the
bundled
regime [Fig.
10
(b)], the stiff filaments at low activity form a tight, nearly parallel bundle reminiscent of a bacterial flagellar bundle. The rigid backbones cannot coil around the head because neighboring filaments sterically prevent the required curvature. This regime produces the strongest rescue and corresponds to the coordination pathway, where the SACF remains uniformly high (
>0.7
) and the TTAF maintains long-lived positive correlations. All filaments contribute thrust along a common direction, sustaining directed propulsion along the bundle axis.

In the
helical
regime [Fig.
10

F_{\mathrm{act}}{=}40
, not shown), the braiding intensifies and the distal ends splay apart, reducing propulsion efficiency. The progression from bundle to helix to braid suggests an optimal activity range for multi-filament propulsion: strong enough to generate thrust but not so strong as to destabilize the aligned bundle.

VI
Discussion and conclusions

We have demonstrated that multi-filament architectures rescue active transport from inertia-induced spinning arrest through steric frustration of the coiled conformation. Three independent diagnostics—MSD, SACF, and TTAF—reveal two distinct rescue mechanisms: rescue by coordination (high stiffness, persistent bundled propulsion) and rescue by decorrelation (low stiffness, elimination of orientational coherence). At
\kappa_{\mathrm{b}}{=}1000
, conformational and transport rescue coincide at
N_{\mathrm{f}}^{*}\approx 3
: coiling is fully eliminated and replaced by a rigid bundle. At
\kappa_{\mathrm{b}}{=}100
, the conformational rescue is
incomplete
—residual coiling persists (
\min C_{s}(s)\approx-0.13
) even at
N_{\mathrm{f}}=7
—yet transport is still rescued by two to three orders of magnitude. This decoupling demonstrates that disrupting the
coherence
of spinning, not eliminating coiling per se, is sufficient for motility rescue.

Relation to prior work.
—The spinning arrest is consistent with spiral states reported by Karan
et al.

Karan2024
and Isele-Holder
et al.

IseleHolder2015
, extended here to three dimensions with an explicit heavy head. The closest precedent for the rescue is the density-dependent spiral destabilization by Janzen and Matoz-Fernandez
Janzen2024
; our mechanism is qualitatively different, operating through the architectural constraint of the star topology for a single isolated molecule. The active star polymer work of Buglakov
et al.

Buglakov2025
shares the multi-arm topology but did not address spinning suppression or inertia.

Steric frustration, not force accumulation.
—The rescue cannot be attributed to trivially having
N_{\mathrm{f}}
times more active force. Force scaling (
\mathrm{MSD}_{\mathrm{ref}}\propto N_{\mathrm{f}}^{2}
) accounts for at most
1.5
orders of magnitude (
N_{\mathrm{f}}^{2}=25
for
N_{\mathrm{f}}{=}5
), far short of the observed five orders. Moreover, the phase diagram (Fig.
3
) shows that increasing activity on a
single
filament
deepens
the spinning trap—the multi-filament rescue operates against the force trend. The conformational evidence (elimination of the SACF negative dip and cessation of TTAF zero crossings) confirms that the motility mode itself has changed, not merely the thrust magnitude.

Applicability and limitations.
—Our framework, excluding hydrodynamic interactions, is directly applicable to dry and quasi-dry settings:
in vitro
motility assays
Schaller2010
;
Sumino2012
;
Jiang2014
, macroscopic active chains
Fazelzadeh2023
;
Deblais2020
, gliding bacteria
Nan2014
, and the hydrodynamically screened intracellular environment
Brangwynne2008
. For multi-filament systems in fluid, HI would add coupling that mediates flagellar synchronization
Reigh2012
;
Brumley2012
and bundling
Kim2003
;
Flores2005
; our results establish the baseline that excluded-volume interactions alone achieve. Additional limitations include equal filament lengths and fixed anchor geometry.

Outlook.
—The two rescue mechanisms suggest design principles for synthetic microswimmers
Palagi2018
;
Tottori2012
;
Li2017
;
Ceylan2019
: stiff filaments for directed transport, flexible filaments for enhanced diffusion. Future work should incorporate HI, investigate the universality of the residual coiling saturation (
\min C_{s}(s)\approx-0.13
), and pursue experimental realization using motor-driven multi-filament assemblies or synthetic multi-flagellated swimmers.

Non-dimensionalization details

We provide the complete non-dimensionalization of Eq. (
1
). The characteristic scales are: length
\sigma
, time
\tau_{\mathrm{d}}=\gamma\sigma^{2}/k_{\mathrm{B}}T
, energy
k_{\mathrm{B}}T
, and force
k_{\mathrm{B}}T/\sigma
.

Substituting
\tilde{\mathbf{r}}_{i}=\mathbf{r}_{i}/\sigma
and
\tilde{t}=t/\tau_{\mathrm{d}}
into Eq. (
1
) gives

m_{i}\frac{\sigma}{\tau_{\mathrm{d}}^{2}}\,\ddot{\tilde{\mathbf{r}}}_{i}=-\gamma_{i}\frac{\sigma}{\tau_{\mathrm{d}}}\,\dot{\tilde{\mathbf{r}}}_{i}-\nabla_{i}U+f_{a}\,\hat{\mathbf{t}}_{i}+\sqrt{2\gamma_{i}k_{\mathrm{B}}T}\;\bm{\eta}_{i}(t).

(16)

Multiplying through by
\sigma/k_{\mathrm{B}}T
and using
\tau_{\mathrm{d}}=\gamma\sigma^{2}/k_{\mathrm{B}}T
, each prefactor simplifies:

Inertial:

\frac{m_{i}\sigma^{2}}{k_{\mathrm{B}}T\tau_{\mathrm{d}}^{2}}=\frac{m_{i}\sigma^{2}}{k_{\mathrm{B}}T}\cdot\frac{(k_{\mathrm{B}}T)^{2}}{\gamma^{2}\sigma^{4}}=\frac{m_{i}k_{\mathrm{B}}T}{\gamma^{2}\sigma^{2}}\equiv\mathcal{M}_{i}.

(17)

Friction:

\frac{\gamma_{i}\sigma^{2}}{k_{\mathrm{B}}T\tau_{\mathrm{d}}}=\frac{\gamma_{i}\sigma^{2}}{k_{\mathrm{B}}T}\cdot\frac{k_{\mathrm{B}}T}{\gamma\sigma^{2}}=\frac{\gamma_{i}}{\gamma}.

(18)

Active:

\sigma f_{a}/k_{\mathrm{B}}T=\mathrm{Pe}
.

Noise:
Defining the dimensionless noise
\tilde{\bm{\eta}}_{i}(\tilde{t})=\sqrt{\tau_{\mathrm{d}}}\;\bm{\eta}_{i}(t)
, which satisfies
\langle\tilde{\eta}_{i}^{\alpha}(\tilde{t})\,\tilde{\eta}_{j}^{\beta}(\tilde{t}^{\prime})\rangle=\delta_{ij}\delta_{\alpha\beta}\delta(\tilde{t}-\tilde{t}^{\prime})
, the noise prefactor becomes
\sqrt{2\gamma_{i}/\gamma}
.

For body beads (
m_{i}=m
,
\gamma_{i}=\gamma
):
\mathcal{M}_{i}=\mathcal{M}
,
\gamma_{i}/\gamma=1
, and the noise prefactor is
\sqrt{2}
, yielding Eq. (
5
).

For the head bead (
m_{\mathrm{h}}=\alpha^{3}m
,
\gamma_{\mathrm{h}}=\alpha\gamma
):

M_{\mathrm{h}}\equiv\frac{m_{\mathrm{h}}k_{\mathrm{B}}T}{\gamma_{\mathrm{h}}^{2}\sigma^{2}}=\frac{\alpha^{3}mk_{\mathrm{B}}T}{(\alpha\gamma)^{2}\sigma^{2}}=\frac{\alpha^{3}}{\alpha^{2}}\cdot\frac{mk_{\mathrm{B}}T}{\gamma^{2}\sigma^{2}}=\alpha\mathcal{M},

(19)

the friction prefactor is
\gamma_{\mathrm{h}}/\gamma=\alpha
, and the noise prefactor is
\sqrt{2\alpha}
. The dimensionless head equation is therefore

\alpha\mathcal{M}\,\ddot{\tilde{\mathbf{r}}}_{\mathrm{h}}=-\alpha\,\dot{\tilde{\mathbf{r}}}_{\mathrm{h}}-\tilde{\nabla}_{\mathrm{h}}\tilde{U}+\mathrm{Pe}\,\hat{\mathbf{t}}_{\mathrm{h}}+\sqrt{2\alpha}\;\tilde{\bm{\eta}}_{\mathrm{h}},

(20)

which is Eq. (
9
) of the main text (with forces summed over all filaments for the multi-filament case). The linear scaling
M_{\mathrm{h}}=\alpha\mathcal{M}
arises because the head mass scales as
\alpha^{3}
while
\gamma_{\mathrm{h}}^{2}
scales as
\alpha^{2}
, leaving a single power of
\alpha
.

Physical interpretation.
—The three dimensionless parameters separate cleanly:
\mathcal{M}=\tau_{\mathrm{i}}/\tau_{\mathrm{d}}
controls inertia (independently of activity),
\mathrm{Pe}=\sigma f_{a}/k_{\mathrm{B}}T
controls activity (independently of mass), and
\alpha
controls geometry. This allows systematic studies where, e.g.,
\mathrm{Pe}
is fixed while
\mathcal{M}
is varied to isolate inertial effects, or vice versa.
