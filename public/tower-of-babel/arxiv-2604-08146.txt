A Simpler Approach to a Descent Conjecture of Wittenberg
Yisheng TIAN

Abstract
A descent conjecture of Wittenberg [ Wit24 ] *Conjecture 3.7.4 predicts that if all the twists of a rationally connected torsor over a smooth base satisfy weak approximation with Brauer–Manin obstruction, then so does the base. We give an alternative proof of Wittenberg’s conjecture for certain torsors under connected linear groups via Cao’s descent formula.

Introduction

The inverse Galois problem, asking whether any finite group
G
is a quotient of
\gal(\overline{k}|k)
for some number field
k
,
is a fundamental open question in number theory.
It has a positive answer when
G
is symmetric or alternating
(Hilbert
1892
) and
G
is solvable (Shafarevich
1954
).
Other classical results for sporadic groups or non-abelian simple groups of Lie type are summarized in
[
Wit24
]
*Section 1.1.
As of today, there are many approaches to the inverse Galois problem as mentioned in
loc. cit.
.
In what follows, we shall proceed by a descent method developed by Colliot-Thélène–Sansuc, Harpaz–Wittenberg and others.

Throughout,
k
is a number field.
Let
\Omega
be the set of all places of
k
and let
k_{v}
be the completion of
k
at any
v\in\Omega
.
By a
k
-variety
X
, we always mean a separated
k
-scheme of finite type.
Manin
[
Man71
]
introduced a pairing (see
[
Sko01
]
*§5.2 for more information)

\textstyle\prod\limits_{v\in\Omega}X(k_{v})\times\Br_{{\mathrm{nr}}}(X)\to\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z},\ ((x_{v}),\alpha)\mapsto\textstyle\sum\limits_{v\in\Omega}j_{v}\circ x_{v}^{*}(\alpha),

where

\Br_{{\mathrm{nr}}}(X)
is the unramified Brauer group of
X
(see
2.1
below),

x_{v}^{*}:\Br_{{\mathrm{nr}}}(X)\to\Br(k_{v})
is the map induced by
x_{v}\in X(k_{v})
and

j_{v}:\Br(k_{v})\to\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
is the local invariant.
Let
X(k_{\Omega})^{\Br_{{\mathrm{nr}}}}
be its left kernel which is a closed subset of
X(k_{\Omega})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\prod_{v\in\Omega}X(k_{v})

with respect to the product of
v
-adic topologies.
A programmatic conjecture is the following

Conjecture 1.1

(Colliot-Thélène,
[
CT03
]
*p. 174)
.

Let
X
be a rationally connected smooth variety over
k
.
Then
X(k)
is dense in
X(k_{\Omega})^{\Br_{{\mathrm{nr}}}}
.

This conjecture is interesting in its own right as it predicts the geometry of
X
controls its arithmetic behavior.
Moreover, a significant consequence of this conjecture is the
inverse Galois problem
.
More precisely,
by embedding a finite group
G
into the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
for some
n\geq 1
,
we may let
G
act on
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}
via the
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-action.
Subsequently, taking the open subset

consisting of points with pairwise distinct coordinates,
we obtain a so-called
versal

-torsor

.
In applying

X\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}Y/G
,
we conclude a positive answer to the inverse Galois problem for

(see
[
Wit24
]
*Section 3.4 for a detailed discussion).
From this perspective, the investigation of the inverse Galois problem may be transferred into that of
1.1
,
in which the descent method plays a key role.

(1.2). Twisting of torsors.

Let
G
be a linear algebraic group over
k
and
let
Y\to X
be a left

-torsor.
For any
[\sigma]\in H^{1}(k,G)
, let
P{{}_{\sigma}}\to X
be a right

-torsor representing

We write

Then the induced morphism
{{}_{\sigma}}f:{{}_{\sigma}}Y\to X
is a
{{}_{\sigma}}G
-torsor.
Moreover, there is a bijection

of sets that
sends the neutral class of

After introducing the twisting technique, the descent method leads us to the following

Conjecture 1.3

(Wittenberg,
[
Wit24
]
*Conjecture 3.7.4)
.

Let
X
be a smooth
k
-variety and let
G
be a linear algebraic

-group.
Let

be a

-torsor with
Y

Assume that

If

[

,

]

In general, Linh
[

]
proved it for any connected linear group

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
G
be a connected linear group over
k
.
Let
X
be a smooth geometrically integral
k
-variety.
Let
f:Y\to X
be a
G
-torsor and let

be a smooth compactification of

Assume that
\pi_{1}(Y^{c}_{\overline{k}})^{\ab}=0
and
\Br(Y^{c})/\Image\Br(k)

Then we have

X(k_{\Omega})^{\Br_{{\mathrm{nr}}}(X)}=\overline{\textstyle\bigcup\limits_{[\sigma]\in H^{1}(k,G)}{{}_{\sigma}}f({{}_{\sigma}}Y(k_{\Omega})^{\Br_{{\mathrm{nr}}}({{}_{\sigma}}Y)})},

where

denotes the closure of

with respect to the product topology.

In particular, if

is rationally connected, then the assumption of

The main ingredients of the proof is the following descent formula of Cao

, where

In applying Harari’s formal lemma, we may identify

Let
k_{\Omega}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\prod_{v\in\Omega}k_{v}

Let

A

Definition 2.1
.

Let

-variety.

(1)

The Brauer group of

\Br(X)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}H_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}^{2}(X,\mathbb{G}_{m})

The arithmetic Brauer group of

\Br_{a}(X)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{\Ker\big(\Br(X)\to\Br(X\times_{k}\overline{k})\big)}{\Image\big(\Br(k)\to\Br(X)\big)}.

(2)

Suppose
X(k)\neq\varnothing

Take any

and let

be the induced homomorphism.
We put

\Br_{x}(X)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Ker\big(\Br(X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle x^{*}}}{{\to}}\Br(k)\big).

In particular, if we denote by

is defined.

(3)

Let

be a left

After
[

,
Cao20
]

the

of

is defined to be

\Br_{G}(X)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\big\{b\in\Br(X)\,|\,(\rho^{*}(b)-p_{2}^{*}(b))\in p_{1}^{*}\Br(G)\big\},

where

(4)

The Brauer group is a birational invariant for smooth proper

[

]
*Corollary 5.2.6.
So we are allowed to define
the unramified Brauer group

of

to be the Brauer group of
any smooth proper
k
-variety birationally equivalent to

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
X
be a smooth geometrically integral
k
-variety endowed with a left
G
-action.
If
\pi_{1}(X_{\overline{k}})^{\ab}=0
,
then
\Br_{G}(X)=\Br(X)
.

Proof.

Since
\pi_{1}(X_{\overline{k}})^{\ab}=0
,
we deduce
H^{1}(X_{\overline{k}},\mu_{n})=0
by
[
SGA1
]
*Exposé XI, §5, (
\ast
).
By
[
Cao23
]
*Théorème 2.1, we obtain a canonical isomorphism of abelian groups

(p_{1}^{*},p_{2}^{*}):H^{2}(G_{\overline{k}},\mu_{n})\ops H^{2}(X_{\overline{k}},\mu_{n})\to H^{2}(G_{\overline{k}}\times_{\overline{k}}X_{\overline{k}},\mu_{n}).

The Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence
H^{i}(k,H^{j}(-_{\overline{k}},\mu_{n}))\Rightarrow H^{i+j}(-,\mu_{n})

together with the
7
-term exact sequence in low degrees
yields an isomorphism of abelian groups

(p_{1}^{*},p_{2}^{*}):H^{2}_{e}(G,\mu_{n})\ops H^{2}(X,\mu_{n})\to H^{2}(G\times_{k}X,\mu_{n}).

The Kummer exact sequence
0\to\mu_{n}\to\mathbb{G}_{m}\to\mathbb{G}_{m}\to 0
then implies the surjectivity of the induced map

(p_{1}^{*},p_{2}^{*}):\Br_{e}(G)\ops\Br(X)\to\Br(G\times_{k}X).

Observe that
i=(e,\id):\e k\times_{k}X\to G\times_{k}X
induces a map

i^{*}:\Br(G\times_{k}X)\to\Br(X)
such that

i^{*}\circ p_{1}^{*}(\Br_{e}(G))=0
and
i^{*}\circ p_{2}^{*}=\id
.
So we have
\Image p_{1}^{*}\subset\Ker i^{*}
.
Conversely, take any
\alpha\in\Ker i^{*}\subset\Br(G\times_{k}X)
and
suppose
\alpha=p_{1}^{*}g+p_{2}^{*}x
for some
(g,x)\in\Br_{e}(G)\oplus\Br(X)
.
Then
x=i^{*}\circ p_{2}^{*}(x)=i^{*}\circ(p_{1}^{*},p_{2}^{*})(g,x)=i^{*}(\alpha)=0
implies

\alpha=p_{1}^{*}g
, i.e.,
\Ker i^{*}\subset\Image p_{1}^{*}
.

Now take any
b\in\Br(X)
.
Since
\rho\circ i=p_{2}\circ i=\id_{X}
,
we conclude
i^{*}(\rho^{*}b-p_{2}^{*}b)=0
which implies

\rho^{*}b-p_{2}^{*}b\in\Ker i^{*}=\Image p_{1}^{*}
.
This shows
b\in\Br_{G}(X)
, i.e.,
\Br(X)\subset\Br_{G}(X)
.
∎

Wittenberg’s descent conjecture

(4.1).

Throughout this section,
let
X
be a smooth geometrically integral
k
-variety.
Let
G
be a connected linear

-group and
let
f:Y\to X

G

The next lemma is probably well-known.
We still write it here for the lack of a reference.

Lemma 4.2
.

Keep the same notation as in Paragraph
(
4.1
)
.
The Picard group
\Pic(G)
and the kernel
\Ker(\Br(X)\to\Br(Y))
are finite.

Proof.

Let
\rad^{u}(G)
be the unipotent radical of
G
and
let
G^{\mathrm{red}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}G/\rad^{u}(G)
.
The exact sequence
1\to\rad^{u}(G)\to G\to G^{\mathrm{red}}\to 1

induces an exact sequence
\Pic(G^{\mathrm{red}})\to\Pic(G)\to\Pic(\rad^{u}(G))
by
[
San81
]
*Corollaire 6.11.
Since the underlying variety of
\rad^{u}(G)
is affine,
the map
\Pic(G^{\mathrm{red}})\to\Pic(G)
is surjective.
Thus it suffices to show that
\Pic(G^{\mathrm{red}})
is finite.

Let
G^{\mathrm{ss}}
be the derived subgroup of
G^{\mathrm{red}}
(which is semi-simple) and
let
G^{\mathrm{tor}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}G^{\mathrm{red}}/G^{\mathrm{ss}}
(which is a torus).
There is an exact sequence
\Pic(G^{\mathrm{tor}})\to\Pic(G^{\mathrm{red}})\to\Pic(G^{\mathrm{ss}})
by
loc. cit.
.
By
[
San81
]
*Lemme 6.9,
we see that
\Pic(G^{\mathrm{tor}})\simeq H^{1}(k,\mathbf{X}^{*}(G^{\mathrm{tor}}))

(where
\mathbf{X}^{*}(G^{\mathrm{tor}})
is the module of characters of
G^{\mathrm{tor}}
) and that
\Pic(G^{\mathrm{ss}})
is finite.
But
H^{1}(k,\mathbf{X}^{*}(G^{\mathrm{tor}}))
is a finitely generated torsion abelian group,
so it is finite.
Consequently,
\Pic(G^{\mathrm{red}})
is finite as well.

Finally, due to
[
San81
]
*(6.10.1) there is an exact sequence
\Pic(G)\to\Br(X)\to\Br(Y)
of abelian groups.
Thus the finiteness of
\Pic(G)
yields that of
\Ker(\Br(X)\to\Br(Y))
.
∎
