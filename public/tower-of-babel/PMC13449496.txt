Clinical and laboratory characteristics associated with liver failure in hospitalized dengue patients: insights from a non-endemic region
Authors: Si Yu Zhao, Ya Peng Zhang, Zhuan Zhou, Yong Wu, Jin Wei Hu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundDengue fever is an emerging public health issue expanding into non-endemic regions like Changsha, China. Liver involvement is a critical complication, and early identification of patients at risk of liver failure remains challenging. This study aimed to characterize hospitalized dengue patients, identify routine laboratory parameters significantly associated with liver failure, and utilize a machine learning model to corroborate the importance of these parameters in this non-endemic setting.MethodsA retrospective chart review was conducted on 86 laboratory-confirmed dengue patients hospitalized at the First Hospital of Changsha between 2023 and 2024. Routine demographic and laboratory data were collected. Statistical analyses were performed to identify parameters associated with liver failure within this cohort. Separately, a random forest model was developed using an independent dataset of dengue patients and healthy controls to rank feature importance for distinguishing dengue from a healthy baseline.ResultsAmong 86 dengue patients, mean age was 38.8 years, with 48.8% male. Cases peaked in summer and autumn. Common abnormalities included leukopenia (83.7%), thrombocytopenia (47.7%), elevated ALT (52.3%), AST (48.8%), and LDH (46.9%). After excluding patients with pre-existing liver disease or co-infections, 79 patients were analyzed for liver failure risk factors. Compared with patients without liver failure (n = 40), those with liver failure (n = 39) had significantly lower platelet counts (P = 0.003) and higher ALT (P = 0.005), AST (P = 0.045), and LDH (P = 0.041). A random forest model ranked AST, platelets, and ALT among its top features for distinguishing dengue from healthy status.ConclusionPlatelet count, ALT, AST, and LDH were identified as laboratory parameters associated with dengue-related liver failure in hospitalized patients. A complementary machine learning–based feature importance analysis showed consistent ranking of key variables in an independent dataset. These findings provide potential biomarkers for risk stratification and support future prospective validation studies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12879-026-13698-5.

Introduction

Dengue fever (DF), a mosquito-borne viral disease caused by any of the four serotypes of dengue virus (DENV), represents a substantial public health burden across tropical and subtropical regions worldwide [1–3]. In recent decades, the geographical distribution of dengue has expanded continuously, with outbreaks increasingly reported in previously non-endemic areas, including parts of Southern China [4, 5]. Changsha, the capital city of Hunan Province, has reported sporadic imported dengue cases; however, the clinical and laboratory characteristics of dengue patients in this region remain incompletely described.

The clinical spectrum of dengue ranges from asymptomatic infection to severe forms, including dengue hemorrhagic fever (DHF) and dengue shock syndrome (DSS) [6]. Liver involvement is a common extrapulmonary manifestation of dengue, with elevated liver enzymes reported in 60–80% of hospitalized patients. In a subset of cases, severe hepatitis or even acute liver failure can develop, significantly increasing morbidity and mortality [7, 8]. Early identification of patients at risk of liver failure remains challenging due to the non-specific nature of early clinical manifestations. The differential diagnosis of dengue includes other acute febrile illnesses such as chikungunya, Zika virus infection, leptospirosis, typhoid fever, influenza, and malaria [9, 10]. In resource-limited settings where laboratory confirmation (e.g., RT-PCR, NS1 antigen detection) is not readily available, clinicians often rely on clinical algorithms, such as the WHO 2009 guideline criteria [11]. However, these criteria demonstrate limited diagnostic accuracy, highlighting the potential value of routine laboratory parameters as supportive clinical indicators.Several studies have investigated laboratory-based approaches to characterize severe dengue, most commonly focusing on dengue hemorrhagic fever and dengue shock syndrome [12, 13]. However, comparatively fewer studies have specifically examined laboratory correlates of dengue-associated liver injury, particularly in non-endemic settings. In addition, many previous studies were conducted in highly endemic regions, which may limit their generalizability to areas with lower disease prevalence and less standardized clinical recognition [12, 14].

In this context, the present study aimed to characterize the clinical and laboratory features of hospitalized dengue patients in Changsha and to identify laboratory parameters associated with liver failure. To provide an additional exploratory perspective, we performed a feature importance analysis using a random forest algorithm on an independent dataset of dengue patients and healthy controls. This analysis was not intended for clinical prediction but rather to evaluate whether the laboratory parameters identified in the clinical cohort were consistently highlighted as informative features in an independent dataset.

Materials and methods

Retrospective data analysis

This retrospective study was conducted at the First Hospital of Changsha, Hunan Province, China, from January 1, 2023 to December 31, 2024. A total of 86 patients with laboratory-confirmed dengue fever were initially included. Dengue was confirmed by a positive NS1 antigen test (SD Biosensor) at admission. Clinical and demographic data were extracted from the hospital’s electronic medical record system. All laboratory parameters were obtained from the first blood draw within 24 h of admission.

For the analysis of liver failure, patients with pre-existing chronic liver disease (chronic hepatitis B, n = 5), co-infection with other acute febrile illnesses (malaria, n = 1), or malignancies affecting liver function (ileocecal malignancy, n = 1) were excluded. After exclusions, 79 patients remained, including 39 patients with liver failure and 40 patients without liver failure, as determined by the discharge diagnosis recorded by the attending infectious disease physician.

Random forest model for feature importance

A random forest model was developed to rank feature importance for distinguishing dengue patients from healthy controls. The machine learning dataset was derived from the same 86 dengue patients included in the clinical analysis. From each patient, multiple laboratory records were collected within the first 24 h of admission. After excluding records with missing values for the nine retained features, 171 complete observation-level records remained for model development. The healthy control group consisted of 757 individuals who underwent routine health check-ups at the same hospital during the study period.

Missing values were assessed for each variable. Four coagulation parameters (prothrombin time, activated partial thromboplastin time, thrombin time, and D-dimer) were excluded due to a high proportion of missing data. The remaining nine features were retained: platelet count, white blood cell count, hemoglobin, red blood cell count, lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), alanine aminotransferase (ALT), total protein, and albumin.

All nine features were normalized to the [0, 1] range using MinMaxScaler. A random forest classifier was implemented using scikit-learn (version 1.2.2) with the following parameters: n_estimators = 300, random_state = 42, class_weight = ‘balanced’, and n_jobs = -1. The dataset was divided into a training set (80%) and an independent test set (20%) using stratified sampling to maintain the original proportion of dengue cases. Five-fold stratified cross-validation was performed on the training set to evaluate model stability. Model performance was assessed using accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, and area under the ROC curve (AUC).

Feature importance was calculated using the mean decrease in impurity (MDI), also known as Gini importance. For each feature, the total reduction in Gini impurity across all splits in all trees was accumulated and then averaged over the number of trees. The resulting importance values were normalized to sum to 1. The importance ranking reflects the model’s reliance on each feature for distinguishing dengue patients from healthy controls.

Statistical analysis

Data was organized using Microsoft Excel software. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 24.0 software. Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation, and group comparisons were performed using independent samples t-tests. Categorical variables are presented as counts and percentages, and group comparisons were performed using chi-square tests. A p-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Epidemiological characteristics

This study included a total of 86 patients diagnosed with dengue fever. The number of cases was 38 in 2023 and 48 in 2024. In 2023, the majority of cases occurred between August and October, accounting for 94.7% (36/38) of the annual total. In 2024, a smaller peak occurred between May and July (14.5%, 7/48), followed by a larger peak from September to November (85.4%, 41/48). The mean age of the patients was 39.9 years (range: 6–84 years). There were 42 male patients (48.8%) and 44 female patients (51.2%). Among all patients, liver failure was observed in 42 cases (48.8%), and hypertension was present in 13 patients (15.1%). Electrolyte imbalances were identified in 18 patients, representing 20.9% (18/86) of the cohort. Detailed baseline characteristics of the patients are presented in Table 1.

Clinical manifestations

All 86 patients presented with fever, with maximum body temperatures reaching 40.1 °C. The fever pattern was primarily continuous or relapsing, with some patients experiencing chills or rigors. The majority of patients reported generalized myalgia, particularly affecting the lumbar region, back, and extremities. A subset of patients also experienced arthralgia or headache, with headaches often described as pressure or throbbing pain in the frontal or periorbital regions. Erythematous skin rashes were observed in some cases, while a minority of patients reported nausea or vomiting.

Abnormal laboratory values were prevalent in the study cohort. Leukopenia was the most frequently observed abnormality (83.7%, 72/86), with minimum values reaching 0.87 × 10⁹/L. Thrombocytopenia was present in 47.7% (41/86) of patients, with minimum platelet counts as low as 14 × 10⁹/L. Elevated ALT and AST, key indicators of hepatocellular injury, were observed in 52.3% (45/86) and 48.8% (42/86) of cases, respectively. Elevated LDH (> 250U/L) was observed in 45.6% (39/79) of patients. (Supplementary Table S1).

Risk factors for liver failure

After excluding patients with pre-existing chronic liver disease, co-infection, or malignancy (Fig. 1), 79 patients remained for the analysis of liver failure. Among these, 39 (49.4%) had a discharge diagnosis of liver failure. Laboratory parameters comparing patients with and without liver failure are summarized in Table 2. Patients with liver failure had significantly lower platelet counts (99.28 ± 58.25 vs. 143.65 ± 69.25 × 10⁹/L, P = 0.003) and significantly higher levels of ALT (83.79 ± 78.80 vs. 43.74 ± 39.57 U/L, P = 0.005), AST (68.86 ± 66.59 vs. 42.14 ± 49.08 U/L, P = 0.045), and LDH (305.97 ± 217.82 vs. 225.72 ± 78.44 U/L, P = 0.041).

Machine learning–based feature importance analysis

To further evaluate the biological relevance of laboratory parameters identified in the clinical analysis, a random forest–based feature importance analysis was performed using an independent dataset comprising 171 dengue records and 757 healthy controls.

Feature importance ranking derived from the random forest model is presented in Fig. 2. Platelet count, ALT, AST, and LDH were among the highest-ranked variables. Notably, these parameters were identical to those significantly associated with dengue-related liver failure in the clinical cohort (Table 2), demonstrating consistency between conventional statistical analysis and machine learning–based evaluation.

Correlation analysis was subsequently performed to explore relationships among laboratory variables (Fig. 3). Hemoglobin and red blood cell count showed strong positive correlation (r > 0.8), while AST and ALT demonstrated moderate positive correlation (r ≈ 0.6). Platelet count exhibited a negative association with dengue status, consistent with dengue-associated thrombocytopenia.

Discussion

This retrospective study characterized the clinical and laboratory profile of hospitalized dengue patients in Changsha, an inland region historically considered non-endemic for dengue transmission. The patient population was predominantly composed of young and middle-aged adults, with most infections imported from endemic areas and case occurrence peaking during summer and autumn. These findings reflect the evolving epidemiological pattern of dengue in inland China, where increased population mobility and climate change have contributed to expanding transmission risk [15, 16]. In this context, early identification of patients at risk for severe complications, particularly liver involvement, has become increasingly important for clinical management in newly affected regions.

Consistent with previous reports, common laboratory abnormalities included leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, and elevated liver enzymes [17]. Notably, thrombocytopenia, elevated ALT, elevated AST, and elevated LDH were significantly associated with the development of liver failure, suggesting that routine laboratory indicators obtained at admission may help identify patients at increased risk of hepatic complications. Patients with liver failure experienced longer hospitalization, supporting the clinical relevance of early risk stratification [18, 19]. These findings are biologically plausible given the multifactorial mechanisms of dengue-associated liver injury, including immune-mediated hepatocyte damage, systemic inflammation, and platelet consumption during acute infection.

To provide an independent analytical perspective, we performed a random forest–based feature importance analysis using an external dataset. Although the model was trained to distinguish dengue patients from healthy controls rather than to predict liver failure, the resulting feature importance ranking demonstrated substantial concordance with the clinical findings. Elevated ALT, elevated AST, thrombocytopenia, and elevated LDH consistently emerged as the most influential laboratory parameters across both conventional statistical analysis and machine learning evaluation. This convergence across two distinct analytical frameworks supports the robustness and biological relevance of these markers as indicators of hepatic involvement and potential progression toward liver failure in dengue infection.

Several considerations should be acknowledged when interpreting these findings. The random forest model was not designed as a diagnostic or prognostic tool; therefore, its high classification performance reflects separation between diseased and healthy populations and should not be interpreted as clinical diagnostic accuracy or liver failure prediction capability. The incomplete availability of certain coagulation parameters limited their inclusion in the primary statistical analysis. Since these tests were not ordered routinely for all patients, their exclusion may have introduced selection bias, as testing was more likely performed in patients with suspected bleeding risk or more severe illness. Liver failure was defined according to discharge diagnosis rather than prospectively applied standardized criteria, reflecting inherent limitations of retrospective data collection. A clinically meaningful diagnostic model would require comparison with other acute febrile illnesses (e.g., influenza, leptospirosis, chikungunya), which were not available in this retrospective dataset. In addition, hepatic encephalopathy could not be systematically assessed due to the lack of standardized neurological documentation for all patients. Furthermore, this single-center study included a relatively modest sample size from a non-endemic region, and information regarding dengue virus serotypes and infection status was unavailable. External validation in independent, multi-center cohorts is needed to confirm the generalizability of these findings.

Despite these limitations, the consistency observed between clinical statistical analysis, machine learning–derived feature importance, and correlation patterns strengthens the evidence that platelet count, ALT, AST, and LDH represent a coherent biomarker profile associated with dengue-related liver injury. These findings provide a clinically accessible basis for early risk stratification and warrant validation in larger prospective multicenter cohorts, particularly including patients with other acute febrile illnesses to further define clinical applicability.

Conclusions

This study characterizes dengue patients in a non-endemic region of China and identifies platelet count, ALT, AST, and LDH as laboratory parameters associated with dengue-related liver failure. A random forest feature importance analysis using an independent dataset also ranked these parameters among the top features. These findings are exploratory and require prospective validation in cohorts including other acute febrile illnesses.
