2.3
The distribution of stationary phase points

For large

(x,t)

\operatorname{Im}\theta(z)
. This sign structure is determined by the spectral parameter
\xi=x/t
through the branch structure of
\theta(z)
defined in (
2.10
).

The function
\theta(z)
possesses branch cuts along the contours
\Gamma_{k}
and satisfies the Schwarz reflection symmetry
\theta(z)=\overline{\theta(\bar{z})}
. Consequently, the endpoint values satisfy

\operatorname{Im}f(E_{k})=\operatorname{Im}g(E_{k})=0,\hskip 10.00002pt\operatorname{Im}\theta\left(E_{k}\right)=\operatorname{Im}\theta\left(\bar{E}_{k}\right)=0,

(2.31)

implying that
f(E_{k})
,
g(E_{k})
, and
\theta(E_{k})
are all real, and the boundary values on the cuts satisfy the jump relation
\operatorname{Im}\theta_{+}(z)=-\operatorname{Im}\theta_{-}(z)>0
for
z\in\Gamma
.

We give the asymtptotic as
z\to\infty
as follows

\displaystyle\theta(z)\sim 2z^{2}+\xi z+\theta_{c}(\infty,\xi)+\mathcal{O}(z^{-1}),

(2.32)

where

\theta_{c}(\infty,\xi)=\int_{\bar{E}_{0}}^{\infty}\left(\frac{h(z)}{w(z)}-4z-\xi\right)dz+2A_{0}^{2}-2B_{0}^{2}-(2B_{0}+f_{0})\xi-g_{0}

(2.33)

is a real function of
\xi
.
These properties imply that the level set
\operatorname{Im}\theta(z)=0
possesses two infinite branches: the real axis itself, and a secondary branch asymptotics to the vertical line
\operatorname{Re}z=-\frac{1}{4}\xi
. Henceforth, the term “infinite branch” refers exclusively to this latter component. The intersection points of the real axis with infinite branche is termed the real stationary phase point. Broadly speaking, this paper investigates how distinct configurations of the real stationary phase point give rise to qualitatively different asymptotic regimes.

Differentiating
\theta(z)
defined in (

hence, stationary phase points satisfy

(2.35)

Factoring

, we obtain

h(z;\xi)=\prod_{l=1}^{r}(z-\kappa_{l}^{\mathrm{R}}(\xi))\prod_{s=1}^{\frac{n+2-r}{2}}(z-\kappa_{s}^{\mathrm{C}}(\xi))(z-\bar{\kappa}_{s}^{\mathrm{C}}(\xi)),\hskip 10.00002ptr\leq n+2,

(2.36)

where
\kappa_{l}^{\mathrm{R}}(\xi)\in\mathbb{R}

(l\in\mathcal{L}=\{1,\dots,r\})
and
\kappa_{s}^{\mathrm{C}}(\xi)\in\mathbb{C}^{+}\setminus\mathbb{R}
denote the real and complex stationary phase points, respectively. Depending on the value of
\xi

1
,
2
, and

7

For generic

, the number

if

n+2

\hat{g}(z)=0

\xi
varies, complex conjugate pairs may collide on or separate from the real axis; hence the phase points can only appear or disappear in such pairs. The following lemma provides the coalescence condition.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
z_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
denote a collision point of two stationary phase points on the critical line
\xi=\xi_{j}
, which corresponds to the region
|\xi-\xi_{j}|t^{2/3}<C
for
C>0
in the
(x,t)
-plane, where two stationary phase points coalesce at
z_{j}
. Suppose the phase function

\theta(z;\xi)=\bigl(f(z)-f_{0}\bigr)\xi+\bigl(g(z)-g_{0}\bigr),

where
{f}(z)
and
{g}(z)
are given in (
2.6
)
satisfies the condition

\partial_{\xi}\theta(z_{j};\xi_{j})\neq 0.

(2.37)

Then the collision at
z_{j}
occurs if and only if the pair
(z_{j},\xi_{j})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
satisfies the system

\theta^{\prime}(z_{j};\xi_{j})=0,\hskip 20.00003pt\theta^{\prime\prime}(z_{j};\xi_{j})=0,\hskip 20.00003pt\theta^{\prime\prime\prime}(z_{j};\xi_{j})\neq 0,

(2.38)

where primes denote
z

\xi_{j}=-\frac{g^{\prime}(z_{j})}{f^{\prime}(z_{j})}=-\frac{\hat{g}(z_{j})}{\hat{f}(z_{j})}=-\frac{4z_{j}^{n+2}+\hat{g}_{n+1}z_{j}^{n+1}+\hat{g}_{n}z_{j}^{n}+\cdots+\hat{g}_{0}}{z_{j}^{n+1}+\hat{f}_{n}z_{j}^{n}+\hat{f}_{n-1}z_{j}^{n-1}+\cdots+\hat{f}_{0}},

(2.39)

where
\{\hat{f}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
and
\{\hat{g}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n+1}
are related to the finite-genus algebro-geometric solution given by (
2.8
), then eliminating
\xi_{j}
yields the Wronskian condition

\hat{f}^{\prime}(z_{j})\hat{g}(z_{j})-\hat{f}(z_{j})\hat{g}^{\prime}(z_{j})=0.

(2.40)

This algebraic equation is of degree
2n+2
; consequently, there exist exactly
2n+2
collision points
z_{j}
, indexed by
j\in\{1,\dots,2n+2\}
.

Proof.

First, for the given collision point
z_{j}
satisfying condition (
2.38
), we prove that there exist two first-order stationary phase points
\kappa_{1}(\xi),\kappa_{2}(\xi)
in its neighborhood.

At the critical line
\xi=\xi_{j}
, expanding
\theta(z;\xi_{j})
near
z=z_{j}
gives

\theta(z;\xi_{j})=\theta(z_{j};\xi_{j})+\frac{1}{6}\theta^{\prime\prime\prime}(z_{j};\xi_{j})(z-z_{j})^{3}+\frac{1}{24}\theta^{(4)}(z_{j};\xi_{j})(z-z_{j})^{4}+\mathcal{O}(z^{5}),

Since
\theta^{\prime}(z_{j};\xi_{j})=\theta^{\prime\prime}(z_{j};\xi_{j})=0
and
\theta^{\prime\prime\prime}(z_{j};\xi_{j})\neq 0
. Now consider a small perturbation
\xi=\xi_{j}+\delta_{\xi}
. Near

\theta^{\prime}(z;\xi)=\theta^{\prime}(z_{j};\xi_{j})+\theta^{\prime\prime}(z_{j};\xi_{j})(z-z_{j})+\frac{1}{2}\theta^{\prime\prime\prime}(z_{j};\xi_{j})(z-z_{j})^{2}+\partial_{\xi}\theta(z_{j};\xi_{j})\delta_{\xi}+\mathcal{O}(z^{3}),

which at leading order reduces to

\theta^{\prime}(z;\xi)\approx\frac{1}{2}\theta^{\prime\prime\prime}(z_{j};\xi_{j})(z-z_{j})^{2}+\partial_{\xi}\theta(z_{j};\xi_{j})(z_{j};\xi_{j})\delta_{\xi}.

For
\delta_{\xi}\neq 0
, let the equation
\theta^{\prime}(z;\xi)=0
yields

(z-z_{j})^{2}\approx-\frac{2}{\theta^{\prime\prime\prime}(z_{j};\xi_{j})}\partial_{\xi}\theta(z_{j};\xi_{j})(z_{j};\xi_{j})\delta_{\xi}.

(2.41)

Thus, there exist two simple stationary phase points

\kappa_{l}\approx z_{j}\pm\sqrt{-\frac{2}{\theta^{\prime\prime\prime}(z_{j};\xi_{j})}\partial_{\xi}\theta(z_{j};\xi_{j})\delta_{\xi}},\hskip 10.00002ptl=1,2,

(2.42)

which coalesce at
z_{j}
as
\delta_{\xi}\to 0
. Evaluating the second derivative (
2.41
) and using (
2.42
) at
\kappa_{l}
gives

\theta^{\prime\prime}\left(\kappa_{l};\xi\right)\approx\pm\theta^{\prime\prime\prime}\left(z_{j};\xi_{j}\right)\sqrt{-\frac{2}{\theta^{\prime\prime\prime}\left(z_{j};\xi_{j}\right)}\partial_{\xi}\theta(z_{j};\xi_{j})\delta_{\xi}}\neq 0,

(2.43)

Combined with (
2.37
), this confirms that
\kappa_{l},\,l=1,2
are nondegenerate stationary phase points.

Conversely, we prove that two stationary phase points
\kappa_{1}(\xi)
and
\kappa_{2}(\xi)
coalesce into a single point
z_{j}
, where
(z_{j},\xi_{j})
satisfies (
2.38
).

Given two simple stationary points
\kappa_{1}(\xi),\kappa_{2}(\xi)
coalesce at
z_{j}
as
\xi\to\xi_{j}
satisfying

\theta^{\prime}(\kappa_{l}(\xi);\xi)=0,\hskip 10.00002pt\theta^{\prime\prime}(\kappa_{l}(\xi);\xi)\neq 0,\hskip 10.00002ptl=1,2.

(2.44)

Since
\theta^{\prime\prime}(z,\xi)
is continuous, it remains nonzero in a neighborhood of
(z_{j},\xi_{j})
. The implicit function theorem then guarantees that each
\kappa_{l}(\xi)
extends uniquely as a smooth function with

\frac{d\kappa_{l}}{d\xi}=-\frac{\partial_{\xi}\theta^{\prime}(\kappa_{l}(\xi);\xi)}{\theta^{\prime\prime}(\kappa_{l}(\xi);\xi)},\hskip 10.00002ptl=1,2.

(2.45)

However, if
\theta^{\prime\prime}(z_{j};\xi_{j})\neq 0
, the implicit function theorem yields a unique smooth curve of zeros

passing through
(z_{j},\xi_{j})
. This contradicts the existence of two distinct stationary phase points
\kappa_{1}(\xi),\kappa_{2}(\xi)
both tending to
z_{j}
. Hence the second derivative must vanish at the coalescence point:

\theta^{\prime\prime}(z_{j};\xi_{j})=0.

(2.46)

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

The expansion order in the two variables is dictated by a dominant-balance argument. Since
\theta^{\prime\prime}(z_{j};\xi_{j})=0
by hypothesis, the linear term in
\delta_{z}:=z-z_{j}
vanishes, making
(\delta_{z})^{2}
the first nontrivial spatial contribution. In the critical line direction,
\delta_{\xi}
already provides the first nonzero correction. The balance relation
(\delta_{z})^{2}\sim\delta_{\xi}
(equivalently
\delta_{z}\sim\sqrt{\delta_{\xi}}
) then shows that keeping
(\delta_{z})^{2}
and
\delta_{\xi}
captures the leading-order behavior, while higher-order terms are negligible as
\delta_{\xi}\to 0
.

Appendix A
Riemann manifolds

In order to present an explicit solution of the RH problem to express
M^{alg}(z)
, we need several ingredients from the theory of the
n
genus of Riemann manifolds
\mathcal{X}
. Let a set of points
\left\{E_{k},\bar{E}_{k}\right\}_{k=0}^{n}
in the complex plane be given,

w^{2}=P(z)=\prod_{k=0}^{n}\left(z-E_{k}\right)\left(z-\bar{E}_{k}\right)

(A.1)

The upper and lower sheets of
\mathcal{X}
are denoted by
\mathcal{X}_{+}
and
\mathcal{X}_{-}
respectively; they are fixed by the relations

\sqrt{P(z)}=\pm z^{n+1}\left(1+\mathrm{O}\left(z^{-1}\right)\right),\hskip 10.00002ptz=\pi(\mathcal{P})\rightarrow\infty,\hskip 10.00002ptP\in\mathcal{X}_{\pm}

where
z=\pi(P)
is the standard projection of
P=(w,z)\in\mathcal{X}
on the Riemann sphere
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
. Thus each point on the
z
-plane has two preimages
P_{\pm}=\mathcal{X}_{\pm}
, except for the branch points. Denote the preimage of
z=\infty
on
\mathcal{X}_{\pm}
by, respectively,
\infty^{\pm}
. With the inclusion of two points
\left(\infty^{+},\infty^{-}\right),\mathcal{X}
becomes a compact Riemann surface of genus
n
. The square root
\sqrt{P(z)}
turns into a meromorphic function on its own compact Riemann surface
\mathcal{X}
, which have
2n+2
zeros at
E_{k}
and
\bar{E}_{k},k=0,1,\ldots,n
, and two poles at
\infty^{+}
and
\infty^{-}
, each of multiplicity
n+1
.

We next discuss the choice of branch cuts and the choice of a canonical homology basis
\{a_{j},b_{j}\}_{j}
for
\mathcal{X}
. One possibility is to let all branch cuts be vertical segments and adopt the standard choice of canonical homology basis
\{a_{j},b_{j}\}_{j}
. Here
a_{j}
is a closed curve on the upper sheet going conterclockwise around the interval
\bar{E}_{k},E_{k}
, and
b_{j}
start from
\bar{E}_{0},E_{0}
, goes on the upper sheet to

(A.2)

Here

We let

\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathcal{X})
due to the canonical homology basis

in the sense that
\zeta_{j}

Since

\zeta\left(z^{+}\right)=-\zeta\left(z^{-}\right).

(A.4)

\tau_{jl}:=\int_{b_{j}}\zeta_{l}.

(A.5)

\int_{a_{j}^{+}}\zeta=\frac{1}{2}\int_{a_{j}}\zeta=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{e}_{j}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ and }\hskip 10.00002pt\int_{b_{j}^{+}}\zeta=\frac{1}{2}\int_{b_{j}}\zeta=\frac{1}{2}\tau_{j},
