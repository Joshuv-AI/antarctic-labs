Axiomatic Justification in Constructive Morse Set Theory
Douglas S. Bridges

Abstract
Working within Constructive Morse Set Theory (CMST) [ 2 , 7 ] , we introduce axioms for a new notion, 𝗃𝗌𝗍 ​ P ​ p \mathsf{jst}\ Pp , intended to capture what it means for P P to prove, or justify, p p under the BHK interpretation of intuitionistic logic. Since it makes no distinction between terms and formulae—every term is also a formula, and vice versa—CMST is well suited to our axiomatic development of justification theory within set theory itself. After stating our axioms for 𝗃𝗌𝗍 ​ P ​ p \mathsf{jst}\ Pp , we derive many consequences thereof. In particular, we show that (with certain restrictions) our axioms for 𝗃𝗌𝗍 ​ P ​ p \mathsf{jst}\ Pp align with the intended BHK interpretations of the axioms of intuitionistic logic.

In view of this apparent drawback to our axiomatic constructive justification
theory, it is worth pointing out that in the practice of constructive
mathematics à la Bishop
[
4
,
6
]
, the domain of a quantification
is generally some well-constructed set (that is, a member of the universe).
Even in CMST, a formal foundation for Bishop-style constructive mathematics,
unrestricted quantifications are often of a trivial nature, like ‘
{\textstyle\bigwedge}x(x=x)
’, and can be replaced by free-variable statements (like ‘
(x=x)
’). An
obvious exception to this is the definition ‘
(0\equiv{\textstyle\bigwedge}xx)

make ‘
({\textstyle\bigwedge}xx\equiv 0)
’ an axiom. But we shall content ourselves with the situation
described in
[
2
,
7
]
and this paper; so our justification theory
does not incorporate unrestricted universal quantification.
