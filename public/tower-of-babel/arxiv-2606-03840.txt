On a system of semilinear damped σ \sigma -evolution equations with different damping types in the critical case Thanks: ∗ Corresponding author: Tuan Anh Dao


Abstract
. In this paper, we study the non-symmetric system of semilinear damped σ \sigma -evolution equations with different damping types, where two power exponents of nonlinearities belong to the critical curve, by using moduli of continuity in nonlinear terms. Our goal is to determine the sharp conditions on these moduli of continuity that guarantee the global (in time) existence of Sobolev solutions or, conversely, lead to finite-time blow-up. Furthermore, by employing the analysis introduced in the proof of our blow-up result, we provide a positive answer to an open problem for the symmetric models posed in the literature.

1.
Introduction

To get started, let us make a brief overview about the research perspectives of the following Cauchy problem for structurally damped
\sigma
-evolution equations:

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &u_{tt}+(-\Delta)^{\sigma}u+(-\Delta)^{\delta}u_{t}=|u|^{p},&&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t\geq 0,\\
&(u,u_{t})(0,x)=(u_{0},u_{1})(x),&&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{aligned}\right.

where
\sigma\geq 1
and
\delta\in[0,\sigma]
, which have attracted significant attention from the mathematical community (see, for example,
[
14
,
16
,
5
,
2
]
and references therein). The choice of
\delta
in the damping term plays a crucial role in determining the asymptotic behavior of solutions to the corresponding linear equation of (
1.1
). More precisely, in the case where
\delta\in[0,\sigma/2)
, the linear equation exhibits the “parabolic-like” behavior, as its solutions share the same asymptotic profile as those of the so-called anomalous diffusion equation (see
[
3
]
), which is exactly the heat equation when
\sigma=1
. Furthermore, the critical exponent for the semi-linear problem (
1.1
) under assuming additional
L^{1}
regularity for the initial data is determined by
p_{\mathrm{par}}(n,\sigma,\delta):=1+2\sigma/(n-2\delta)
. On the other hand, when
\delta\in(\sigma/2,\sigma]
, the diffusion phenomenon no longer occurs to the linear equation, and the behavior of solutions coincides with that of the free
\sigma
-evolution equations, which is exactly the classical wave equation when
\sigma=1
. This regime is often referred to the “
\sigma
-evolution-like” model. In this case, the critical exponent to (
1.1
), under assuming additional
L^{1}
regularity for the initial data, is given by
p_{\mathrm{evo}}(n,\sigma):=1+2\sigma/(n-\sigma)
. Here, the critical exponent
p_{\rm crit}
means that there exist global (in time) small data Sobolev solutions for some range of admissible exponent
p>p_{\rm crit}
, whereas one may find non-existence of global (in time) Sobolev solutions even for small data in the case
1<p<p_{\rm crit}
. In other words, we have only local (in time) Sobolev solutions in the latter case. From these observations, the value
\delta=\sigma/2
acts as the threshold to distinguish the two distinct models appearing in terms of the study of (
1.1
) and its corresponding linear equation. A natural question arising is whether one can find the so-called critical curve for a weakly coupled system in the non-symmetric setting, i.e. a system consists of both classes of equations mentioned above. This problem, which focuses heavily on the interaction between a “parabolic-like” equation and a “
\sigma
-evolution-like” equation, has been recently addressed in
[
10
]
for the following system:

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &u_{tt}+(-\Delta)^{\sigma}u+(-\Delta)^{\sigma}u_{t}&&=|v|^{p},&&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t\geq 0,\\
&v_{tt}+(-\Delta)^{\sigma}v+v_{t}&&=|u|^{q},&&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t\geq 0,\\
&(u,u_{t},v,v_{t})(0,x)&&=(0,u_{1},0,v_{1})(x),&&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{aligned}\right.

The authors proved that under the conditions
1<\sigma<n<2\sigma
and
p\geq 2
, if the inequality

\max\left\{\frac{2q+1}{pq+q-2},\frac{pq+p+1}{2pq-p-1}\right\}>\frac{n}{2\sigma}

holds, there exists a unique global (in time) Sobolev solution to (
1.2
). Additionally, they established a blow-up result if the relation

\max\left\{\frac{2q+1}{pq+q-2},\frac{pq+p+1}{2pq-p-1}\right\}<\frac{n}{2\sigma}

happens. Consequently, one realizes that the critical curve for (
1.2
) is defined by

(
*
)

\max\left\{\frac{2q+1}{pq+q-2},\frac{pq+p+1}{2pq-p-1}\right\}-\frac{n}{2\sigma}=0.

Among other perspectives, the very interesting topic is to study a single equation or a weakly coupled system with power nonlinearities whose exponents take on the critical value or belong to the critical curve, respectively. One recognizes that this perspective has recently attracted a growing interest from numerous mathematicians over the world. The main advantage of considering such problems is to determine the critical nonlinearity or the critical regularity of nonlinearities by adding modulus of continuity in nonlinear terms. For this approach, we assume a function
\mu:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
to be continuous, concave, and increasing which provides an additional regularity of nonlinearities in comparison with the power nonlinear terms. We refer the reader to a few papers (see more
[
13
,
7
,
1
,
18
,
19
]
) for recent results in this direction. One of the most remarkable contributions comes from Dao-Reissig in
[
7
]
, where the authors have investigated the weakly coupled system of semilinear damped wave equations in the critical case as follows:

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &u_{tt}-\Delta u+u_{t}=|v|^{p^{*}}\mu_{1}(|v|),&&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t\geq 0,\\
&v_{tt}-\Delta v+v_{t}=|u|^{q^{*}}\mu_{2}(|u|),&&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t\geq 0,\\
&(u,u_{t},v,v_{t})(0,x)=(u_{0},u_{1},v_{0},v_{1})(x),&&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{aligned}\right.

Here
(p^{*},q^{*})
belong to the critical curve described by

\frac{\max\{p,q\}+1}{pq-1}-\frac{n}{2}=0

and
\mu_{1}=\mu_{1}(s),\,\mu_{2}=\mu_{2}(s)
stand for moduli of continuity. The authors proved that (
1.3
) admits a global (in time) Sobolev solution if
I_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}<\infty
, otherwise, the solution blows up in finite time when
I_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}=\infty
, where the integral quantiy is defined by

I_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}:=\begin{cases}\vskip 5.69046pt\displaystyle\int_{0}^{c}\frac{1}{s}(\mu_{1}(s))^{\frac{1}{1+p^{*}}}(\mu_{2}(s))^{\frac{p^{*}}{1+p^{*}}}\,ds&\text{ if }p^{*}\geq q^{*},\\
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{c}\frac{1}{s}(\mu_{1}(s))^{\frac{q^{*}}{1+q^{*}}}(\mu_{2}(s))^{\frac{1}{1+q^{*}}}\,ds&\text{ if }q^{*}\geq p^{*},\\
\end{cases}

for a suitably small constant
c>0
. We can say that this paper gives a new interaction of additional regularities of power nonlinearities in comparison with other research results in terms of the study of weakly coupled systems. As strongly motivated from
[
10
,
7
]
, in the present work we would like to investigate the following weakly coupled system:

(1.4)

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &u_{tt}+(-\Delta)^{\sigma}u+(-\Delta)^{\sigma}u_{t}&&=|v|^{p^{*}}\mu_{1}(|v|),&&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t\geq 0,\\
&v_{tt}+(-\Delta)^{\sigma}v+v_{t}&&=|u|^{q^{*}}\mu_{2}(|u|),&&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t\geq 0,\\
&(u,u_{t},v,v_{t})(0,x)&&=(0,u_{1},0,v_{1})(x),&&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{aligned}\end{cases}

where
(p^{*},q^{*})
lies on the critical curve (

1
).
We aim to determine the sharp threshold for
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
that distinguishes between the global (in time) existence and the blow-up of Sobolev solutions to (
1.4

. Obviously, we can see that several key features distinguish our considered system from those previously studied in the literature. First, the system (
1.4

To overcome these difficulties, on the one hand the main strategy for the proof of global (in time) Sobolev solutions is based on using different weight functions of loss of decay in the solution space. On the other hand, we introduce a judiciously modified test function with non-compact support, which allows us to effectively estimate nonlocal operators in the proof of blow-up solutions. Finally, we also demonstrate that the test fucntion method developed in this paper can be successfully applied to provide a blow-up result to the symmetric models in Section

2.
Global existence of Sobolev solutions

In this section, we give the proof of Theorem
1.1
. We denote by
K_{0}=K_{0}(t,x)
and
K_{1}=K_{1}(t,x)
the fundamental solutions to the linear Cauchy problems (
2.3

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}u^{\mathrm{lin}}(t,x)=K_{0}(t,x)*_{x}u_{1}(x),\\

Thanks to Duhamel’s principle, the solutions to (
1.4

\left\{\begin{aligned} u(t,x)=u^{\mathrm{lin}}(t,x)+\int_{0}^{t}K_{0}(t-\tau,x)*_{x}\left(|v(\tau,x)|^{p^{*}}\mu_{1}(|v(\tau,x)|)\right)d\tau=:u^{\mathrm{lin}}(t,x)+u^{\mathrm{nl}}(t,x),\\

For all
t>0
, we define the operator in a solution space

\Psi:\quad(u,v)\in X(t)\mapsto\Psi(u,v)(t,x)=\left(u^{\mathrm{lin}}(t,x)+u^{\mathrm{nl}}(t,x),v^{\mathrm{lin}}(t,x)+v^{\mathrm{nl}}(t,x)\right).

Our aim is to apply Banach’s fixed point theorem in the proof of global (in time) existence of small data Sobolev solutions to (
1.4

(2.2)

\displaystyle\|\Psi(u,v)-\Psi(\tilde{u},\tilde{v})\|_{X(t)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|(u,v)-(\tilde{u},\tilde{v})\|_{X(t)}\left(\|(u,v)\|_{X(t)}^{p^{*}-1}+\|(\tilde{u},\tilde{v})\|_{X(t)}^{p^{*}-1}\right.

(n/\sigma)\left(1-1/k\right)-1\leq 1
, a similar argument leads to

\displaystyle\big\|u^{\mathrm{nl}}(t,\cdot)\big\|_{L^{k}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|(u,v)\|_{X(t)}^{p^{*}}\int_{0}^{t}(1+t-\tau)^{1-\frac{n}{\sigma}\left(1-\frac{1}{k}\right)}(1+\tau)^{-1}\big(\ell_{2}(\tau)\big)^{p^{*}}\mu_{1}\left(c(1+\tau)^{-\gamma}\right)d\tau

(2.29)

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+t)^{1-\frac{n}{\sigma}\left(1-\frac{1}{k}\right)}\|(u,v)\|_{X(t)}^{p^{*}}.

Notice that
(1+t)^{\varepsilon_{2}(q^{*})}\ell_{2}(t)
is an increasing function (see Lemma
2.5
), one has

\displaystyle\big\||D|^{\sigma}v^{\mathrm{nl}}(t,\cdot)\big\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|(u,v)\|_{X(t)}^{q^{*}}\int_{0}^{t}(1+t-\tau)^{-\frac{n}{4\sigma}-\frac{1}{2}}(1+\tau)^{-1+\varepsilon_{2}(q^{*})}\ell_{2}(\tau)\big(\ell_{2}(\tau)\big)^{-1}\mu_{2}\left(c(1+\tau)^{-\gamma}\right)d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+\tau)^{\varepsilon_{2}(q^{*})}\ell_{2}(t)\|(u,v)\|_{X(t)}^{q^{*}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\times\int_{0}^{t}(1+t-\tau)^{-\frac{n}{4\sigma}-\frac{1}{2}}(1+\tau)^{-1}\left(\mu_{1}\left(c(1+\tau)^{-\gamma}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}+1}}\left(\mu_{2}\left(c(1+\tau)^{-\gamma}\right)\right)^{\frac{p^{*}}{p^{*}+1}}d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{4\sigma}-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon_{2}(q^{*})}\|(u,v)\|_{X(t)}^{q^{*}}\int_{0}^{t}(1+\tau)^{-1}\left(\mu_{1}\left(c(1+\tau)^{-\gamma}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}+1}}\left(\mu_{2}\left(c(1+\tau)^{-\gamma}\right)\right)^{\frac{p^{*}}{p^{*}+1}}d\tau

(2.30)

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{4\sigma}-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon_{2}(q^{*})}\|(u,v)\|_{X(t)}^{q^{*}},

where we have employed Lemma
2.4
with

\alpha_{1}=\frac{n}{4\sigma}+\frac{1}{2},\quad\alpha_{2}=\gamma,\quad\beta_{1}=\frac{q^{*}}{q^{*}+1},\quad\beta_{2}=\frac{1}{q^{*}+1}.

By the same way, one achieves

(2.31)

\big\|v^{\mathrm{nl}}(t,\cdot)\big\|_{L^{k}}\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{2\sigma}\left(1-\frac{1}{k}\right)}\|(u,v)\|^{p^{*}}_{X(t)}\quad\text{ for }k\in\{2,\infty\}.

The link of all estimates (
2.28
)-(
2.31
) leads to the inequality (
2.5
).

Next, let us prove the inequality (
2.2
). For two elements
(u,v)
and
(\tilde{u},\tilde{v})
from
X(t)
, it is obvious to see that

\Psi(u,v)(t,x)-\Psi(\tilde{u},\tilde{v})(t,x)=\left(u^{\mathrm{nl}}(t,x)-\tilde{u}^{\mathrm{nl}}(t,x),v^{\mathrm{nl}}(t,x)-\tilde{v}^{\mathrm{nl}}(t,x)\right).

Then, we employ Proposition
2.1
to derive the following estimates for
k\in\{2,\infty\}
:

\displaystyle\big\|\big(u^{\mathrm{nl}}-\tilde{u}^{\mathrm{nl}}\big)(t,\cdot)\big\|_{L^{k}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}(1+t-\tau)^{-\frac{n}{\sigma}\left(1-\frac{1}{k}\right)+1}\big\||v(\tau,\cdot)|^{p^{*}}\mu_{1}\big(|v(\tau,\cdot)|\big)-|\tilde{v}(\tau,\cdot)|^{p^{*}}\mu_{1}(|\tilde{v}(\tau,\cdot)|)\big\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{2}}d\tau

and

\displaystyle\big\|\big(u^{\mathrm{nl}}-\tilde{u}^{\mathrm{nl}}\big)(t,\cdot)\big\|_{\dot{H}^{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}(1+t-\tau)^{-\frac{n}{4\sigma}}\big\||v(\tau,\cdot)|^{p^{*}}\mu_{1}\big(|v(\tau,\cdot)|\big)-|\tilde{v}(\tau,\cdot)|^{p^{*}}\mu_{1}(|\tilde{v}(\tau,\cdot)|)\big\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{2}}d\tau.

For
k\in\{q^{*},\infty\}
, the estimates from Proposition
2.2
imply

\displaystyle\big\|\big(v^{\mathrm{nl}}-\tilde{v}^{\mathrm{nl}}\big)(t,\cdot)\big\|_{L^{k}}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}(1+t-\tau)^{-\frac{n}{2\sigma}\left(1-\frac{1}{k}\right)}\big\||u(\tau,\cdot)|^{q^{*}}\mu_{2}(|u(\tau,\cdot)|)-|\tilde{u}(\tau,\cdot)|^{q^{*}}\mu_{2}(|\tilde{u}(\tau,\cdot)|)\big\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{2}}d\tau

and

\displaystyle\big\|\big(v^{\mathrm{nl}}-\tilde{v}^{\mathrm{nl}}\big)(t,\cdot)\big\|_{\dot{H}^{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}(1+t-\tau)^{-\frac{n}{4\sigma}-\frac{1}{2}}\big\||u(\tau,\cdot)|^{q^{*}}\mu_{2}\big(|u(\tau,\cdot)|\big)-|\tilde{u}(\tau,\cdot)|^{q^{*}}\mu_{2}(|\tilde{u}(\tau,\cdot)|)\big\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{2}}d\tau.

The application of the mean value theorem gives the following integral representation:

\displaystyle|v(\tau,x)|^{p^{*}}\mu_{1}(|v(\tau,x)|)-|\tilde{v}(\tau,x)|^{p^{*}}\mu_{1}(|\tilde{v}(\tau,x)|)

\displaystyle\qquad=(v(\tau,x)-\tilde{v}(\tau,x))\int_{0}^{1}d_{|v|}\Phi_{p}(\omega v(\tau,x)+(1-\omega)\tilde{v}(\tau,x))d\omega,

where
\Phi_{p}(s)
is defined as in (
1.12
). Since the condition (
1.5
) holds, one gets

d_{|v|}\Phi_{p}(v)=p^{*}|v(t,x)|^{p^{*}-1}\mu_{1}(|v(t,x)|)+|v(t,x)|^{p^{*}}d_{|v|}\mu_{1}(|v(t,x)|)\lesssim|v(t,x)|^{p^{*}-1}\mu_{1}(|v(t,x)|).

Thus, it follows that

\displaystyle\big||v(\tau,x)|^{p^{*}}\mu_{1}(|v(\tau,x)|)-|\tilde{v}(\tau,x)|^{p^{*}}\mu_{1}(|\tilde{v}(\tau,x)|)\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim|v(\tau,x)-\tilde{v}(\tau,x)|\int_{0}^{1}|\omega v(\tau,x)+(1-\omega)\tilde{v}(\tau,x)|^{p^{*}-1}\mu_{1}\big(|\omega v(\tau,x)+(1-\omega)\tilde{v}(\tau,x)|\big)d\omega.

Similarly, we also obtain

\displaystyle\big||u(\tau,x)|^{q^{*}}\mu_{2}(|u(\tau,x)|)-|\tilde{u}(\tau,x)|^{q^{*}}\mu_{2}(|\tilde{u}(\tau,x)|)\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim|u(\tau,x)-\tilde{u}(\tau,x)|\int^{1}_{0}|\omega u(\tau,x)+(1-\omega)\tilde{u}(\tau,x)|^{q^{*}-1}\mu_{2}\big(|\omega u(\tau,x)+(1-\omega)\tilde{u}(\tau,x)|\big)d\omega.

By the aid of Hölder’s inequality and applying the same arguments as in the proof of inequality (
2.5
), we arrive at the inequality (
2.2
). Hence, the proof of Theorem
1.1
is completed.

3.
Blow-up result

3.1.
Construction of test functions

We now introduce the test functions that will be used in the proof of Theorem
1.2
. We define the functions
\eta\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}([0,\infty))
and
\eta^{T}
fulfilling

\eta(t)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &1&&\text{ if }0\leq t\leq 1/2,\\
&\text{decreasing}&&\text{ if }1/2\leq t\leq 1,\\
&0&&\text{ if }t\geq 1,\end{aligned}\right.\text{ and }\eta^{T}(t)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &0&&\text{ if }0\leq t<1/2,\\
&\eta(t)&&\text{ if }t\geq 1/2.\end{aligned}\right.

Moreover, the functions
\varphi\in H^{2\sigma}
and
\varphi^{X}
are given by

\varphi(x):=\left\{\begin{aligned} &1&&\text{ for $|x|\leq 1$},\\
&\sqrt[4]{1+(|x|-1)^{4}}&&\text{ for $|x|\geq 1$},\end{aligned}\right.\text{ and }\varphi^{X}(x):=\left\{\begin{aligned} &0&&\text{ for }|x|\leq 1,\\
&\varphi(x)&&\text{ for }|x|\geq 1.\end{aligned}\right.

For a large parameter
R\in[0,\infty)
, we introduce the functions

\eta_{R}(t):=\eta\left(R^{-1}t\right)\text{ and }\eta^{T}_{R}(t):=\eta^{T}\left(R^{-1}t\right),

together with the following space-dependent functions possessing different scaling orders:

\varphi_{j,R}(x):=\varphi\left(R^{-\frac{1}{j\sigma}}x\right)\text{ and }\varphi^{X}_{j,R}(x):=\varphi^{X}\left(R^{-\frac{1}{j\sigma}}x\right)

for
j=1,2
. Let
\kappa=\sigma-\lfloor\sigma\rfloor
if
\sigma
is fractional and
\kappa=\sigma
if
\sigma
is an integer. For
j\in\{1,2\}
, we define the test functions

(3.1)

\displaystyle\phi_{j,R}(t,x):=\left[\varphi_{j,R}(x)\right]^{n+2\kappa}[\eta_{R}(t)]^{\nu+2},

\displaystyle\phi^{X}_{j,R}(t,x):=\left[\varphi^{X}_{j,R}(x)\right]^{n+2\kappa}[\eta_{R}(t)]^{\nu},

\displaystyle\phi^{T}_{j,R}(t,x):=\left[\varphi_{j,R}(x)\right]^{n+2\kappa}[\eta^{T}_{R}(t)]^{\nu},

where the parameter
\nu>0
will be fixed later, having the following properties:

(3.2)

\displaystyle\text{supp}\phi_{R}(t,x)\subset Q_{R}

\displaystyle:=\left\{(t,x):x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},t\in[0,R]\right\},

\displaystyle\text{supp}\phi^{X}_{j,R}(t,x)\subset Q^{1}_{j,R}

\displaystyle:=\left\{(t,x):|x|\geq R^{\frac{1}{j\sigma}},t\in[0,R]\right\},

\displaystyle\text{supp}\phi^{T}_{j,R}(t,x)\subset Q^{2}_{R}

\displaystyle:=\left\{(t,x):x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},t\in\left[R/2,R\right]\right\},

\displaystyle Q^{3}_{j,R}

\displaystyle:=\left\{(t,x):|x|\leq R^{\frac{1}{j\sigma}},t\leq R/2\right\}.

A straightforward calculation gives

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}\phi_{j,R}(t,x)|

\displaystyle=[\varphi_{j,R}(x)]^{n+2\kappa}\left|\partial_{t}[\eta_{R}(t)^{\nu+2}]\right|

(3.3)

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{-1}[\varphi_{j,R}(x)]^{n+2\kappa}[\eta^{T}_{R}(t)]^{\nu+1}\lesssim R^{-1}\phi^{T}_{j,R}(t,x),

\displaystyle\big|\partial^{2}_{t}\phi_{j,R}(t,x)\big|

\displaystyle=[\varphi_{j,R}(x)]^{n+2\kappa}\left|\partial^{2}_{t}[\eta_{R}(t)]^{\nu+2}\right|

\displaystyle=[\varphi_{j,R}(x)]^{n+2\kappa}\left|(\nu+2)(\nu+1)[\eta^{T}_{R}(t)]^{\nu}[\eta^{\prime}_{R}(t)]^{2}+(\nu+2)[\eta^{T}_{R}(t)]^{\nu+1}[\eta^{\prime\prime}_{R}(t)]\right|

(3.4)

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{-2}[\varphi_{j,R}(x)]^{n+2\kappa}[\eta^{T}_{R}(t)]^{\nu}\lesssim R^{-2}\phi^{T}_{j,R}(t,x)

for all
(t,x)\in Q^{2}_{R}
. Furthermore, if
\sigma
is an integer, then
\kappa=\sigma
. We use Lemma
A.1
to conclude that

\displaystyle\big|(-\Delta)^{\sigma}\phi_{j,R}(t,x)\big|=\left|\eta_{R}(t)R^{-1}((-\Delta)^{\sigma}[\varphi]^{n+2\kappa})\left(R^{-\frac{1}{j\sigma}}x\right)\right|

(3.5)

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim R^{-2/j}[\eta_{R}(t)]^{\nu+2}[\varphi^{X}_{j,R}(x)]^{n+2\kappa+2\sigma}\lesssim R^{-2/j}\phi^{X}_{j,R}(t,x),\;\text{ for a.e. }(t,x)\in Q_{R}.

If
\sigma
is fractional, then
\kappa=\sigma-\lfloor\sigma\rfloor
. Lemma
A.2
implies the estimate

(3.6)

\displaystyle\big|(-\Delta)^{\sigma}\phi_{j,R}(t,x)\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{-2/j}[\eta_{R}(t)]^{\nu+2}[\varphi_{j,R}(x)]^{n+2\kappa}\lesssim R^{-2/j}\phi_{j,R}(t,x)

for a.e.
(t,x)\in Q_{R}
. Similarly, we obtain the following estimate for a.e.
(t,x)\in Q^{2}_{R}
:

\displaystyle\big|(-\Delta)^{\sigma}\partial_{t}\phi_{1,R}(t,x)\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|R^{-1}\partial_{t}\eta^{T}_{R}(t)[\eta^{T}_{R}(t)]^{\nu+1}\big((-\Delta)^{\sigma}[\varphi]^{n+2\sigma}\big)\big(R^{-1/\sigma}x\big)\right|

(3.7)

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{-3}[\eta^{T}_{R}(t)]^{\nu+1}[\varphi_{1,R}(x)]^{n+2\kappa}\lesssim R^{-3}\phi^{T}_{1,R}(t,x).

In addition, the following version of Jensen’s inequality comes into play.

Lemma 3.1

(Generalized Jensen’s inequality, see
[
13
]
)
.

Let
\eta=\eta(x)
be a defined and nonnegative function almost everywhere on
\Omega
, provided that
\eta
is positive in a set of positive measure. Then, for each convex function
h
on
\mathbb{R}
the following inequality holds:

h\left(\frac{\int_{\Omega}f(x)\eta(x)dx}{\int_{\Omega}\eta(x)dx}\right)\leq\frac{\int_{\Omega}h\big(f(x)\big)\eta(x)dx}{\int_{\Omega}\eta(x)dx},

where
f
is any nonnegative function satisfying all the above integrals are meaningful.

Now, we are ready to present the proof of Theorem

in the next sequel.
