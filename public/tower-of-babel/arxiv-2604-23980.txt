SUDA-Muon: Structural Design Principles and Boundaries for Fully Decentralized Muon
Hengrui Zhang 2022141210007@stu.scu.edu.cn Sichuan University, Boao Kong kongboao@stu.pku.edu.cn Peking UniversityJiahe Geng gengcai02@gmail.com Peking UniversityZhengyang Huang huangzy20040420@163.com Beihang University

Abstract
Fully decentralized Muon is difficult because its nonlinear matrix-sign operator does not commute with linear gossip averaging. This makes decentralized Muon a structural design problem: in designing the algorithm, one must distinguish modular components from non-modular ones. We propose SUDA–Muon, which realizes this separation through a unified primal–dual communication template called SUDA; within this template, ED/D 2 , EXTRA, and gradient tracking become modular backbone choices. We prove a topology-separated non-asymptotic convergence guarantee in the nuclear-norm geometry: the dominant term scales as 𝒪 ( ( 1 + σ / N ) K − 1 / 4 ) \mathcal{O}((1+\sigma/\sqrt{N})K^{-1/4}) and does not explicitly involve graph quantities, identifying the communication backbone as the modular axis in the structure design. We then establish two complementary non-modular boundaries. Internally, tracking-before-polarization is necessary for this natural no-tracking variant to avoid non-stationary fixed points under heterogeneous objectives. Externally, in the absence of a central server, a fully decentralized method cannot perform the federated average-then-polarize update; we show that this non-modular local-polarize-then-average design is the essential reason why can fail to exhibit linear speedup. Experiments on CIFAR-100 and GPT-2 fine-tuning support the same picture: the unified template makes different communication algorithms directly comparable. In mild near-IID regimes, the resulting variants perform similarly, while in the more difficult long-horizon non-IID CIFAR-100 setting, SUDA–Muon achieves higher accuracy and lower loss than DeMuon .

Introduction

Decentralized optimization studies how a network of agents can cooperatively minimize a global objective using only local computation and neighbor-to-neighbor communication, without relying on a parameter server or any other central coordinator

,

A recurring theme in this literature is that statistical and network effects are inseparable: local objectives may be heterogeneous, stochastic gradients are noisy, and information propagates through repeated mixing over the communication graph, so the behavior of an algorithm depends not only on smoothness and noise but also on topology and on how consensus errors are corrected

,

,

Concretely, we consider the decentralized matrix optimization problem

\min_{X\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}}f(X)\;\triangleq\;\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}f_{i}(X),

Most existing decentralized optimization methods are built around classical vector-valued first-order updates, including decentralized SGD, correction-based variants such as EXTRA and D

, gradient-tracking methods, and more recent decentralized adaptive gradient methods inspired by AdaGrad/Adam

Once these two operations are combined, averaging and polarization need not agree, so the usual intuition from decentralized SGD no longer applies. As a result, decentralized Muon becomes a structural design problem: in designing the algorithm, one must distinguish modular components from non-modular ones.

Current decentralized Muon results do not yet provide a unified picture. DeMuon

7

combines Muon with gradient tracking and works over general graphs, but it is organized around one specific communication template. This leaves four questions open:

•

Q1:
Can decentralized Muon be organized so that the communication backbone can vary while the Muon geometry stays fixed?

•

Q2:
Under such a decomposition, can one obtain a topology-separated rate in the Muon-induced nuclear-norm geometry, rather than an analysis in which network effects and optimizer geometry are entangled from the start?

•

Q3:
Is tracking before polarization genuinely necessary, or merely one convenient design choice?

•

Q4:
Compared with server-based/federated Muon, why can a fully decentralized Muon update not exploit the usual linear-speedup effect from averaging stochastic gradients?

Related Work

2.1
Muon and Other Matrix-Aware Optimizers in Centralized Training

Recent centralized work on Muon has expanded quickly from the original optimizer proposal into a small but growing family of matrix-aware or LMO-based methods for deep learning. Unlike classical coordinatewise optimizers, and unlike matrix preconditioners such as Adafactor or Shampoo that still end with a vector-like first-order step, Muon-family methods operate directly at the level of each weight-matrix block and construct update directions through polarization, orthogonalization, or related norm-constrained LMO operations. Follow-up empirical papers study Muon’s behavior in large-scale language-model training, while related variants such as Gluon and other norm-constrained LMO formulations aim to improve its practical stability and clarify its relation to a broader optimizer family
[
21
,
6
,
8
,
13
,
20
,
18
]
. In parallel, recent theory interprets Muon-type updates through spectral- or operator-norm geometry and analyzes their convergence behavior
[
27
,
22
]
.

From an analysis viewpoint, however, Muon-type methods are substantially harder than standard SGD- or Adam-style optimizers for two related reasons. First, the search direction is generally not an unbiased estimator of the Euclidean gradient itself: after forming a gradient or momentum matrix, the algorithm applies a matrix polarization or orthogonalization map, so the final step is a biased transformed direction that is better understood through matrix geometry than through classical unbiased-gradient recursions. Second, this transformation is intrinsically nonlinear at the matrix level, which makes it difficult to transfer standard arguments based on linear averaging, coordinatewise decomposition, or diagonal preconditioning. Existing theory therefore has to work with operator- or spectral-norm geometry, LMO-style formulations, and more delicate progress measures than those used for conventional vector optimizers
[
8
,
18
,
20
,
22
]
.

2.2
Decentralized Optimization and Heterogeneity Correction

Classical decentralized optimization provides a large toolbox for coordinating first-order updates over graphs. Early diffusion and consensus-correction methods already showed that network mixing, local objective mismatch, and stochastic noise must be analyzed jointly rather than as separable effects
[
3
,
23
]
. In modern decentralized learning, DSGD is the basic stochastic baseline, while Exact Diffusion/D
2
and gradient-tracking methods introduce correction terms to mitigate the bias caused by heterogeneity, local drift, or imperfect consensus
[
12
,
25
,
19
,
31
]
. More recent unified analyses further characterize how topology, local updates, and communication schedules shape transient and asymptotic behavior across a common family of methods
[
10
,
1
,
32
,
11
]
. SUDA is especially relevant because it places several of these methods inside one primal–dual template parameterized by
(A,B,C)
, making it possible to compare communication mechanisms without redesigning the algorithm each time
[
1
,
32
,
11
]
.

Most of this literature, however, works in a Euclidean setting: gradients are communicated linearly, and the optimizer itself is vector-based or at most diagonally preconditioned. The resulting theory says a great deal about topology and bias correction, but it does not address what happens when the optimizer applies a nonlinear matrix transformation before the update. For the present paper, these methods should therefore be viewed as candidate communication backbones rather than as competing optimizer geometries.

2.3
Distributed Muon: Decentralized and Federated Extensions

Distributed extensions of Muon are only beginning to emerge, and most of the current literature is on the federated side rather than on fully peer-to-peer graphs. In federated learning, the standard star-topology setup already separates local client optimization from server aggregation, and the interaction between data heterogeneity and averaging is known to be a central difficulty
[
15
,
9
]
. Recent FedMuon-style methods bring matrix-aware or LMO-based updates into this setting and show that naively combining local Muon with FedAvg can be unstable or biased under heterogeneity, which motivates additional server-side correction and bias control
[
24
,
14
]
. These results are directly relevant because they show that Muon does not simply drop into a distributed pipeline as a black-box replacement for SGD or Adam.

DeMuon is, to our knowledge, the clearest fully decentralized Muon method on general graphs
[
7
]
. It demonstrates that combining Muon with gradient tracking is viable, but it is organized around one specific communication template. Our paper differs in emphasis along three axes. First, we separate Muon geometry from the communication backbone, rather than analyzing one fixed coupling from the outset. Second, we ask which backbone substitutions are modular inside a unified template, rather than studying only one admissible design. Third, we identify two complementary non-modular boundaries: an internal one, namely whether tracking must precede polarization, and an external one, namely whether federated Muon intuitions transfer to the fully decentralized setting.

3.2
Algorithm Description

Our design principle is to separate two roles that are often entangled in decentralized Muon variants. The first role is the
decentralized backbone
: the algorithm should use communication and, when needed, tracking to construct the signal to be polarized. The second role is
matrix geometry
: Muon’s nonlinear operator
\operatorname{msgn}
should act on that backbone-processed signal rather than on a purely local one. A unified primal–dual communication template is a natural way to realize this separation, because it lets the communication backbone vary while leaving the polarization rule unchanged.

In this paper, we instantiate that template using the stochastic unified decentralized algorithm (SUDA) formulation. SUDA is used only as a unified communication template. It is not the source of Muon’s geometry. The role of the matrices
(A,B,C)
is simply to parameterize how decentralized mixing and tracking are performed, while the Muon direction is always the matrix polarization
\operatorname{msgn}(\cdot)
. In the language of this paper, the unified template is used to study the modular axis of the design space; the two non-modular boundaries are established separately by structural counterexamples.

At a high level, each node maintains four coupled quantities: the local primal iterate
X_{i}^{k}
, an EMA state
M_{i}^{k}
for stochastic gradients, a tracked signal
H_{i}^{k}
that aggregates network-corrected gradient information, and a dual variable
Y_{i}^{k}
that accumulates consensus violations in the SUDA backbone. Each iteration first updates the EMA, then updates the tracked signal through communication, next applies the matrix-sign operator to obtain the Muon direction, and finally performs the SUDA primal–dual correction step.

Assumptions

We adopt standard assumptions from refined SUDA analyses and recent Muon/DeMuon convergence analyses, adapted to matrix-valued parameters.

Assumption 1

(Network and SUDA communication structure)
.

Let
W\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
be the (undirected) mixing matrix associated with the communication graph.
We assume:

1.

W
is symmetric, doubly stochastic, and primitive. Denote the mixing rate

\lambda\;\triangleq\;\rho\Big(W-\tfrac{1}{N}\mathbf{1}_{N}\mathbf{1}_{N}^{\top}\Big)\in(0,1).

2.

The SUDA matrices

satisfy:
(i)

and

are doubly stochastic; (ii)
BZ=0
iff
Z_{1}=\cdots=Z_{N}
for stacked variables;
(iii)

, and

are (low-degree) polynomial functions of

.

Assumption 2

(Matrix smoothness and lower boundedness)
.

Each local objective

is
L_{*}

X,Y\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
,

\|\nabla f_{i}(X)-\nabla f_{i}(Y)\|_{*}\;\leq\;L_{*}\|X-Y\|,\qquad\forall i\in[N],

where

and

Moreover, the global objective

.

Assumption 3

(Stochastic first-order oracle)
.

At each iteration
k
and node

\mathbb{E}\big[G_{i}^{k}\mid\mathcal{F}^{k}\big]=\nabla f_{i}(X_{i}^{k}),\qquad\mathbb{E}\big[\|G_{i}^{k}-\nabla f_{i}(X_{i}^{k})\|_{F}^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}^{k}\big]\leq\sigma^{2},

Stochastic gradients are conditionally independent across agents and time given

.

Assumption 4

(Muon polarization operator)
.

For any

with a reduced SVD

, define

\operatorname{msgn}(H)\triangleq UV^{\top},\qquad\operatorname{msgn}(0)\triangleq 0.

(4)

Each agent uses

We present the theoretical analysis of SUDA–Muon.
For the reader’s convenience, we first state the main convergence result and its big-

4.1
Statement of Main Results

The following proposition provides the explicit convergence rate for SUDA–Muon. Its role in the paper is not only to certify convergence, but also to expose the structural decomposition behind decentralized Muon: the leading stochastic term reflects the Muon geometry itself, whereas the network-dependent corrections isolate the effect of the communication backbone.

Proposition 1

(Main convergence rate — informal statement)
.

Under Assumptions
1
–
4
and standard SUDA stability conditions, choose the stepsize
\alpha=\alpha_{0}K^{-3/4}
and EMA parameter
1-\beta=b_{0}K^{-1/2}
.
Then the average nuclear-norm stationarity measure of SUDA–Muon satisfies

\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\nabla f(\bar{X}^{k})\|_{*}\big]\;=\;\mathcal{O}\!\left(\underbrace{\Bigl(1+\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{N}}\Bigr)K^{-1/4}}_{\text{topology-free}}+\underbrace{\frac{K^{-3/4}}{(1-\gamma)(1-\lambda)}}_{\text{network-dependent}}+\text{lower-order terms}\right),

(5)

where
\gamma\in(0,1)
is the SUDA contraction rate and
\lambda=\rho(W-J)\in(0,1)
is the mixing rate.

The precise five-term bound, together with all constants, is given in Proposition
4
and its big-
\mathcal{O}
simplification in Corollary
1
.
Three structural features of this result deserve emphasis:

1.

Topology separation.

The leading term
(1+\sigma/\sqrt{N})K^{-1/4}
is entirely free of any network quantity; the communication topology affects only the faster-decaying correction terms.

2.

Modular network dependence.

Network effects enter only through
(1-\gamma)
and
(1-\lambda)
, and different choices of
(A,B,C)
alter only
\gamma
, so one can compare communication strategies by simply substituting the corresponding SUDA contraction rate (see Subsection
4.4
).

3.

Muon-induced geometry.

The stationarity measure is the nuclear norm
\|\nabla f\|_{*}
, which is the natural dual norm for Muon’s operator-norm constraint set. This is fundamentally different from the Euclidean norm used in standard decentralized SGD analyses.

The proof proceeds in three stages: we first derive a one-step descent inequality in the nuclear-norm geometry, then establish the auxiliary bounds controlling EMA error, gradient tracking, SUDA disagreement, and forcing terms, and finally assemble these ingredients via telescoping and the chosen stepsize/EMA schedules.

4.2
Basic Properties of the Matrix Sign Operator

Lemma 1

(Basic properties of
\operatorname{msgn}
and a robust inner-product lower bound)
.

Let
H\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
and define
\operatorname{msgn}(H)
as in (
4
): if
H\neq 0
has reduced SVD
H=U\Sigma V^{\top}
with
U\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times r}
,
V\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times r}
,
\Sigma=\mathrm{diag}(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{r})
and
\sigma_{i}>0
, then
\operatorname{msgn}(H):=UV^{\top}
, and set
\operatorname{msgn}(0):=0
.
Then the following hold.

1.

Alignment with the nuclear norm.
One has

\langle H,\operatorname{msgn}(H)\rangle=\|H\|_{*}.

(6)

2.

Operator- and Frobenius-norm bounds.
One has

\|\operatorname{msgn}(H)\|\leq 1,\qquad\|\operatorname{msgn}(H)\|_{F}=\sqrt{\mathrm{rank}(H)}\leq\sqrt{r_{\max}},\qquad r_{\max}:=\min\{m,n\}.

(7)

Moreover, if
H\neq 0
then
\|\operatorname{msgn}(H)\|=1
.

3.

A key inequality (no Lipschitzness required).
For any
G,H\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
,

\langle G,\operatorname{msgn}(H)\rangle\geq\|G\|_{*}-2\|G-H\|_{*}.

(8)

The proof uses only the definition (
4
), basic linear algebra, and the standard nuclear/operator norm duality inequality

|\langle A,B\rangle|\leq\|A\|_{*}\,\|B\|,\qquad\forall A,B\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n},

(9)

where
\langle A,B\rangle:=\mathrm{tr}(A^{\top}B)
.

Proof.

If
H=0
, then
\operatorname{msgn}(H)=0
by definition and all claims are immediate. Hence assume
H\neq 0
and write its reduced SVD as
H=U\Sigma V^{\top}
with
U^{\top}U=I_{r}
and
V^{\top}V=I_{r}
.

Part (1):
\langle H,\operatorname{msgn}(H)\rangle=\|H\|_{*}
.

Using cyclicity of the trace and
U^{\top}U=I_{r}
,
V^{\top}V=I_{r}
,

\langle H,\operatorname{msgn}(H)\rangle=\mathrm{tr}\bigl((U\Sigma V^{\top})^{\top}(UV^{\top})\bigr)=\mathrm{tr}\bigl(V\Sigma U^{\top}UV^{\top}\bigr)=\mathrm{tr}(V\Sigma V^{\top})=\mathrm{tr}(\Sigma)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sigma_{i}=\|H\|_{*}.

Part (2): operator- and Frobenius-norm bounds.

Set
M:=\operatorname{msgn}(H)=UV^{\top}
. Then

M^{\top}M=VU^{\top}UV^{\top}=VV^{\top},

which is the orthogonal projector onto
\mathrm{col}(V)
and hence has eigenvalues in
\{0,1\}
. Therefore

\|M\|^{2}=\lambda_{\max}(M^{\top}M)=\lambda_{\max}(VV^{\top})=1,

so
\|\operatorname{msgn}(H)\|=1
for
H\neq 0
, and the bound
\|\operatorname{msgn}(H)\|\leq 1
holds in general (including
H=0
).
Moreover,

\|M\|_{F}^{2}=\mathrm{tr}(M^{\top}M)=\mathrm{tr}(VV^{\top})=\mathrm{rank}(VV^{\top})=r=\mathrm{rank}(H),

so
\|\operatorname{msgn}(H)\|_{F}=\sqrt{\mathrm{rank}(H)}\leq\sqrt{\min\{m,n\}}=:\sqrt{r_{\max}}
.

Part (3): robust inner-product lower bound.

Write

\langle G,\operatorname{msgn}(H)\rangle=\langle H,\operatorname{msgn}(H)\rangle+\langle G-H,\operatorname{msgn}(H)\rangle.

By Part (1),
\langle H,\operatorname{msgn}(H)\rangle=\|H\|_{*}
. For the second term, apply the duality inequality (
9
) with
A=G-H
and
B=\operatorname{msgn}(H)
, together with Part (2) (
\|\operatorname{msgn}(H)\|\leq 1
), to obtain

\langle G-H,\operatorname{msgn}(H)\rangle\geq-|\langle G-H,\operatorname{msgn}(H)\rangle|\geq-\|G-H\|_{*}\,\|\operatorname{msgn}(H)\|\geq-\|G-H\|_{*}.

Consequently,

\langle G,\operatorname{msgn}(H)\rangle\geq\|H\|_{*}-\|G-H\|_{*}.

(10)

Finally, by the triangle inequality for the nuclear norm,

\|G\|_{*}=\|H+(G-H)\|_{*}\leq\|H\|_{*}+\|G-H\|_{*},

which rearranges to
\|H\|_{*}\geq\|G\|_{*}-\|G-H\|_{*}
. Plugging this into (
10
) yields

\langle G,\operatorname{msgn}(H)\rangle\geq(\|G\|_{*}-\|G-H\|_{*})-\|G-H\|_{*}=\|G\|_{*}-2\|G-H\|_{*},

which is (
8
).
∎

4.3
Proof Ingredients and Main Proofs

This subsection develops the technical ingredients behind the master rate and then assembles them into the final convergence bound.

One-Step Descent Analysis

Lemma 2

(Averaged SUDA–Muon dynamics)
.

Suppose Assumption
1
holds and the iterates are generated by (
3
) with initialization
Y_{i}^{0}=0
for all
i\in[N]
.
Define the averages

\bar{X}^{k}\;\triangleq\;\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}^{k},\qquad\bar{S}^{k+1}\;\triangleq\;\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}S_{i}^{k+1},\quad S_{i}^{k+1}=\operatorname{msgn}(H_{i}^{k+1}).

Then, for all
k\geq 0
,

\bar{X}^{k+1}\,=\,\bar{X}^{k}-\alpha\,\bar{S}^{k+1}.

(11)

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
denote the all-ones vector and set

J\;\triangleq\;\frac{1}{N}\,\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}.

For a stacked variable
\mathbf{Z}=\mathrm{col}\{Z_{1},\dots,Z_{N}\}
(with matrix blocks
Z_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
),

J\mathbf{Z}
has all blocks equal to the average
\bar{Z}\triangleq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i}Z_{i}
.

From (
3
),

\mathbf{X}^{k+1}\,=\,A\Bigl(C\mathbf{X}^{k}-\alpha\,\operatorname{msgn}(\mathbf{H}^{k+1})\Bigr)-B\mathbf{Y}^{k}.

Left-multiplying by
J
and using that
A
and
C
are doubly stochastic (Assumption
1
(2)(i)), hence
JA=J
and
JC=J
, yields

J\mathbf{X}^{k+1}\,=\,J\mathbf{X}^{k}-\alpha\,J\operatorname{msgn}(\mathbf{H}^{k+1})-JB\mathbf{Y}^{k}.

(12)

We claim that
JB\mathbf{Y}^{k}=0
for all
k\geq 0
under
\mathbf{Y}^{0}=0
.
Indeed, define the row vector
b^{\top}\triangleq\tfrac{1}{N}\mathbf{1}^{\top}B
and the stacked linear functional

u^{k}\triangleq b^{\top}\mathbf{Y}^{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
.
From the dual update
\mathbf{Y}^{k+1}=\mathbf{Y}^{k}+B\mathbf{X}^{k+1}
,

u^{k+1}=u^{k}+b^{\top}B\mathbf{X}^{k+1}=u^{k}+\tfrac{1}{N}\mathbf{1}^{\top}B^{2}\mathbf{X}^{k+1}.

By Assumption
1
(2)(ii), for every
consensus
stacked variable

\mathbf{Z}=\mathrm{col}\{Z,\dots,Z\}
(with an arbitrary block
Z\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
), we have
B\mathbf{Z}=0
.
Writing the
i
-th block of
B\mathbf{Z}
as
(B\mathbf{Z})_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}b_{ij}Z
, we obtain

(\sum_{j=1}^{N}b_{ij})Z=0
for every
Z
.
Choosing any nonzero
Z
(e.g., a basis matrix) implies
\sum_{j=1}^{N}b_{ij}=0
for all
i
, i.e.,

B\mathbf{1}=0.

(13)

Consequently,
B^{2}\mathbf{1}=0
.

Next, by Assumption
1
(2)(iii), there exists a polynomial
p
such that
B^{2}=p(W)
.
Since
W
is doubly stochastic,
W\mathbf{1}=\mathbf{1}
and
\mathbf{1}^{\top}W=\mathbf{1}^{\top}
, hence

p(W)\mathbf{1}=p(1)\mathbf{1}
and
\mathbf{1}^{\top}p(W)=p(1)\mathbf{1}^{\top}
.
From
B^{2}\mathbf{1}=p(W)\mathbf{1}=0
we conclude
p(1)=0
, and therefore

\mathbf{1}^{\top}B^{2}=\mathbf{1}^{\top}p(W)=p(1)\mathbf{1}^{\top}=0.

(14)

Therefore
b^{\top}B=\tfrac{1}{N}\mathbf{1}^{\top}B^{2}=0
, and thus
u^{k+1}=u^{k}
.
Since
\mathbf{Y}^{0}=0
, we get
u^{0}=0
and hence
u^{k}\equiv 0
for all
k
.
Finally,

JB\mathbf{Y}^{k}=\frac{1}{N}\mathbf{1}\,(\mathbf{1}^{\top}B\mathbf{Y}^{k})=\mathbf{1}\,u^{k}=0.

Substituting
JB\mathbf{Y}^{k}=0
into (
12
) and taking the common block yields

\bar{X}^{k+1}=\bar{X}^{k}-\alpha\bar{S}^{k+1}
, which is (
11
).
∎

Lemma 3

(One-step descent of
f(\bar{X}^{k})
with Muon direction and tracking error)
.

Suppose Assumptions
1
and
2
hold, the iterates are generated by (
3
), and
Y_{i}^{0}=0
for all
i\in[N]
(as in Algorithm
1
).
Let
(\bar{X}^{k})_{k\geq 0}
be the averaged iterates defined in Lemma
2
.
Then, for all
k\geq 0
,

f(\bar{X}^{k+1})\,\leq\,f(\bar{X}^{k})-\alpha\,\|\nabla f(\bar{X}^{k})\|_{*}+\frac{L_{*}\alpha^{2}}{2}+\frac{2\alpha}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\nabla f(\bar{X}^{k})-H_{i}^{k+1}\|_{*}.

(15)

Here and throughout,
\langle A,B\rangle\!:=\!\mathrm{tr}(A^{\top}B)
denotes the matrix inner product.

Proof.

Step 1: a descent lemma under Assumption
2
.

Since
f(X)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}f_{i}(X)
, Assumption
2
implies that for all
X,Y
,

\|\nabla f(X)-\nabla f(Y)\|_{*}\;=\;\Big\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bigl(\nabla f_{i}(X)-\nabla f_{i}(Y)\bigr)\Big\|_{*}\;\leq\;\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\nabla f_{i}(X)-\nabla f_{i}(Y)\|_{*}\;\leq\;L_{*}\|X-Y\|.

(16)

Fix
X,Y\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
and let
D:=Y-X
.
Define
g(t):=f(X+tD)
for
t\in[0,1]
. Then
g
is differentiable and

g^{\prime}(t)=\langle\nabla f(X+tD),D\rangle.

Using the fundamental theorem of calculus,

f(Y)-f(X)=g(1)-g(0)=\int_{0}^{1}\langle\nabla f(X+tD),D\rangle\,dt.

(17)

Add and subtract
\nabla f(X)
inside the integrand to obtain

\displaystyle f(Y)-f(X)

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla f(X),D\rangle+\int_{0}^{1}\langle\nabla f(X+tD)-\nabla f(X),D\rangle\,dt.

By the nuclear/operator duality inequality
|\langle A,B\rangle|\leq\|A\|_{*}\,\|B\|
and (
16
), for each
t\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle\langle\nabla f(X+tD)-\nabla f(X),D\rangle

\displaystyle\leq|\langle\nabla f(X+tD)-\nabla f(X),D\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla f(X+tD)-\nabla f(X)\|_{*}\,\|D\|

\displaystyle\leq L_{*}\,\|tD\|\,\|D\|=L_{*}\,t\,\|D\|^{2}.

Plugging this into (
17
) gives the descent inequality

f(Y)\leq f(X)+\langle\nabla f(X),Y-X\rangle+\frac{L_{*}}{2}\|Y-X\|^{2}.

(18)

Step 2: apply (
18
) to the averaged recursion.

By Lemma
2
,

\bar{X}^{k+1}=\bar{X}^{k}-\alpha\bar{S}^{k+1}
with
\bar{S}^{k+1}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}S_{i}^{k+1}
and
S_{i}^{k+1}=\operatorname{msgn}(H_{i}^{k+1})
.
Applying (
18
) with
X=\bar{X}^{k}
and
Y=\bar{X}^{k+1}
yields

f(\bar{X}^{k+1})\leq f(\bar{X}^{k})-\alpha\,\langle\nabla f(\bar{X}^{k}),\bar{S}^{k+1}\rangle+\frac{L_{*}\alpha^{2}}{2}\,\|\bar{S}^{k+1}\|^{2}.

(19)

Moreover, by Lemma
1
(2),
\|S_{i}^{k+1}\|\leq 1
for all
i
, and by convexity of the operator norm,

\|\bar{S}^{k+1}\|=\Big\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}S_{i}^{k+1}\Big\|\leq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|S_{i}^{k+1}\|\leq 1.

(20)

Combining (
19
) and (
20
) gives

f(\bar{X}^{k+1})\leq f(\bar{X}^{k})-\alpha\,\langle\nabla f(\bar{X}^{k}),\bar{S}^{k+1}\rangle+\frac{L_{*}\alpha^{2}}{2}.

(21)

Step 3: lower bound
\langle\nabla f(\bar{X}^{k}),\bar{S}^{k+1}\rangle
via Lemma
1
.

Let
G:=\nabla f(\bar{X}^{k})
. For each agent
i
, Lemma
1
(3) with
H=H_{i}^{k+1}
gives

\langle G,S_{i}^{k+1}\rangle=\langle G,\operatorname{msgn}(H_{i}^{k+1})\rangle\geq\|G\|_{*}-2\,\|G-H_{i}^{k+1}\|_{*}.

Averaging over
i
and using
\langle G,\bar{S}^{k+1}\rangle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle G,S_{i}^{k+1}\rangle
yields

\langle\nabla f(\bar{X}^{k}),\bar{S}^{k+1}\rangle\geq\|\nabla f(\bar{X}^{k})\|_{*}-\frac{2}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\nabla f(\bar{X}^{k})-H_{i}^{k+1}\|_{*}.

(22)

Substituting (
22
) into (
21
) gives (
15
).
∎

Proposition 2

(Descent inequality with an explicit “tracking-error” remainder)
.

Suppose the assumptions of Lemma
3
hold.
Then for any integer
K\geq 1
,

\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\nabla f(\bar{X}^{k})\|_{*}\big]\;\leq\;\frac{\Delta_{0}}{\alpha K}+\frac{L_{*}\alpha}{2}+\frac{2}{NK}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\nabla f(\bar{X}^{k})-H_{i}^{k+1}\|_{*}\big],

(23)

where
\Delta_{0}:=\mathbb{E}[f(\bar{X}^{0})]-f_{\inf}
.

Proof.

Sum the one-step inequality (
15
) from Lemma
3
over
k=0,\dots,K-1
, take expectations, and use
\mathbb{E}[f(\bar{X}^{K})]\geq f_{\inf}
.
Divide by
\alpha K
.
∎

EMA and Gradient Tracking Foundations

Lemma 4

(EMA stochastic error (per-agent and averaged))
.

Suppose Assumption
3
holds and the iterates are generated by (
3
) (equivalently, Algorithm
1
).
Fix any agent
i\in[N]
.
Define
C_{i}^{0}:=\nabla f_{i}(X_{i}^{0})
and for
k\geq 0
,

C_{i}^{k+1}:=\beta C_{i}^{k}+(1-\beta)\nabla f_{i}(X_{i}^{k}),

and recall that
M_{i}^{0}=G_{i}^{0}
and
M_{i}^{k+1}=\beta M_{i}^{k}+(1-\beta)G_{i}^{k}
.
Then, for all
k\geq 0
,

\mathbb{E}\,\|C_{i}^{k}-M_{i}^{k}\|_{F}\;\leq\;\sqrt{\tfrac{1-\beta}{1+\beta}}\,\sigma\,+\,\beta^{k}\sigma.

(24)

Moreover, defining the averages
\bar{M}^{k}:=\tfrac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}M_{i}^{k}
and
\bar{C}^{k}:=\tfrac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}C_{i}^{k}
, we have for all
k\geq 0
,

\mathbb{E}\,\|\bar{C}^{k}-\bar{M}^{k}\|_{F}\;\leq\;\sqrt{\tfrac{1-\beta}{1+\beta}}\,\tfrac{\sigma}{\sqrt{N}}\,+\,\beta^{k}\tfrac{\sigma}{\sqrt{N}}.

(25)

Finally, letting
r_{\max}:=\min\{m,n\}
, for all
k\geq 0
,

\mathbb{E}\,\|C_{i}^{k}-M_{i}^{k}\|_{*}\;\leq\;\sqrt{r_{\max}}\,\mathbb{E}\,\|C_{i}^{k}-M_{i}^{k}\|_{F}.

(26)

Proof.

Throughout, define the stochastic gradient noise

\Delta_{i}^{k}\;:=\;G_{i}^{k}-\nabla f_{i}(X_{i}^{k}).

Then Assumption
3
implies

\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{i}^{k}\mid\mathcal{F}^{k}]=0,\qquad\mathbb{E}[\|\Delta_{i}^{k}\|_{F}^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}^{k}]\leq\sigma^{2}.

(27)

Step 1: per-agent EMA error.

Let
E_{i}^{k}:=C_{i}^{k}-M_{i}^{k}
.
By the definitions of
C_{i}^{k+1}
and
M_{i}^{k+1}
,

E_{i}^{k+1}=\beta E_{i}^{k}+(1-\beta)\bigl(\nabla f_{i}(X_{i}^{k})-G_{i}^{k}\bigr)=\beta E_{i}^{k}-(1-\beta)\Delta_{i}^{k}.

(28)

Unrolling (
28
) gives, for any
k\geq 0
,

E_{i}^{k}=(1-\beta)\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\beta^{k-1-t}\bigl(\nabla f_{i}(X_{i}^{t})-G_{i}^{t}\bigr)+\beta^{k}\bigl(\nabla f_{i}(X_{i}^{0})-G_{i}^{0}\bigr).

(29)

Taking Frobenius norms, using the triangle inequality and Cauchy–Schwarz,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|E_{i}^{k}\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\beta)\,\mathbb{E}\Big\|\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\beta^{k-1-t}\bigl(\nabla f_{i}(X_{i}^{t})-G_{i}^{t}\bigr)\Big\|_{F}+\beta^{k}\,\mathbb{E}\|\nabla f_{i}(X_{i}^{0})-G_{i}^{0}\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\beta)\,\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\Big\|\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\beta^{k-1-t}\bigl(\nabla f_{i}(X_{i}^{t})-G_{i}^{t}\bigr)\Big\|_{F}^{2}}+\beta^{k}\,\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\|\nabla f_{i}(X_{i}^{0})-G_{i}^{0}\|_{F}^{2}}.

(30)

The second term is bounded by
\beta^{k}\sigma
using (
27
).
For the first term, note that for
s<t
, by the tower property and (
27
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl[\bigl\langle\nabla f_{i}(X_{i}^{s})-G_{i}^{s},\;\nabla f_{i}(X_{i}^{t})-G_{i}^{t}\bigr\rangle\bigr]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\mathbb{E}\bigl[\bigl\langle\nabla f_{i}(X_{i}^{s})-G_{i}^{s},\;\nabla f_{i}(X_{i}^{t})-G_{i}^{t}\bigr\rangle\mid\mathcal{F}^{t}\bigr]\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\bigl\langle\nabla f_{i}(X_{i}^{s})-G_{i}^{s},\;\underbrace{\mathbb{E}[\nabla f_{i}(X_{i}^{t})-G_{i}^{t}\mid\mathcal{F}^{t}]}_{=\,0}\bigr\rangle\Bigr]=0.

Hence all cross terms vanish in the expansion of the squared norm, and therefore

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big\|\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\beta^{k-1-t}\bigl(\nabla f_{i}(X_{i}^{t})-G_{i}^{t}\bigr)\Big\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\beta^{2(k-1-t)}\,\mathbb{E}\|\nabla f_{i}(X_{i}^{t})-G_{i}^{t}\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sigma^{2}\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\beta^{2(k-1-t)}=\sigma^{2}\frac{1-\beta^{2k}}{1-\beta^{2}}\leq\frac{\sigma^{2}}{1-\beta^{2}}.

Plugging this into (
30
) yields

\mathbb{E}\|E_{i}^{k}\|_{F}\leq(1-\beta)\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{1-\beta^{2}}}+\beta^{k}\sigma=\sqrt{\tfrac{1-\beta}{1+\beta}}\,\sigma+\beta^{k}\sigma,

which proves (
24
).

Step 2: averaged EMA error and the
1/\sqrt{N}
factor.

Let
\bar{E}^{k}:=\bar{C}^{k}-\bar{M}^{k}=\tfrac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}E_{i}^{k}
and
\bar{\Delta}^{k}:=\tfrac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Delta_{i}^{k}
.
Averaging (
28
) over
i
gives

\bar{E}^{k+1}=\beta\bar{E}^{k}-(1-\beta)\bar{\Delta}^{k}.

(31)

We now bound the second moment of
\bar{\Delta}^{k}
.
By Assumption
3
,
conditional on
\mathcal{F}^{k}
the random matrices
\{\Delta_{i}^{k}\}_{i=1}^{N}
are independent across agents
and satisfy (
27
).
Therefore, conditional on
\mathcal{F}^{k}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\bar{\Delta}^{k}\|_{F}^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}^{k}\bigr]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Delta_{i}^{k}\Big\|_{F}^{2}\,\Big|\,\mathcal{F}^{k}\Big]=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\Delta_{i}^{k}\|_{F}^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}^{k}\bigr]

(32)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sigma^{2}}{N}.

Equality (
32
) is exactly where the conditional independence across agents is used: it removes the cross terms
\langle\Delta_{i}^{k},\Delta_{j}^{k}\rangle
for
i\neq j
.
Consequently, by Jensen,

\mathbb{E}\|\bar{\Delta}^{k}\|_{F}\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\|\bar{\Delta}^{k}\|_{F}^{2}}\leq\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{N}}.

(33)

Now apply the same argument as in Step 1 to the recursion (
31
), using (
32
) (in place of (
27
) with
\sigma^{2}
), to obtain

\mathbb{E}\|\bar{E}^{k}\|_{F}\leq\sqrt{\tfrac{1-\beta}{1+\beta}}\,\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{N}}+\beta^{k}\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{N}},

which is (
25
).

Step 3: nuclear norm conversion.

For any matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
with singular values
(s_{j})_{j=1}^{r_{\max}}
,

\|A\|_{*}=\sum_{j=1}^{r_{\max}}s_{j}\leq\sqrt{r_{\max}}\,\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{r_{\max}}s_{j}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}=\sqrt{r_{\max}}\,\|A\|_{F},

where the inequality is Cauchy–Schwarz.
Applying this pointwise with
A=E_{i}^{k}
and taking expectations gives (
26
).
∎

Lemma 5

(Gradient tracking preserves network averages)
.

Suppose Assumption
1
(1) holds (in particular,
W
is doubly stochastic) and the gradient-tracking variable is updated as in (
3
):

\mathbf{H}^{k+1}=W\bigl(\mathbf{H}^{k}+\mathbf{M}^{k+1}-\mathbf{M}^{k}\bigr),\qquad k\geq 0.

Assume the initialization satisfies
H_{i}^{0}=M_{i}^{0}
for all
i\in[N]
(equivalently,
\mathbf{H}^{0}=\mathbf{M}^{0}
).
Define the network averages

\bar{H}^{k}\;\triangleq\;\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}H_{i}^{k},\qquad\bar{M}^{k}\;\triangleq\;\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}M_{i}^{k}.

Then, for all
k\geq 0
,

\bar{H}^{k}\,=\,\bar{M}^{k}.

In particular, for all
k\geq 0
,

\bar{H}^{k+1}\,=\,\bar{H}^{k}+\bigl(\bar{M}^{k+1}-\bar{M}^{k}\bigr).

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
denote the all-ones vector and set

J\;\triangleq\;\frac{1}{N}\,\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}.

For any stacked variable
\mathbf{Z}=\mathrm{col}\{Z_{1},\dots,Z_{N}\}
(with matrix blocks
Z_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
), the stacked vector
J\mathbf{Z}
has all blocks equal to the average
\bar{Z}\triangleq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i}Z_{i}
.

Since
W
is doubly stochastic, we have
\mathbf{1}^{\top}W=\mathbf{1}^{\top}
, and thus

JW\,=\,\frac{1}{N}\,\mathbf{1}(\mathbf{1}^{\top}W)\,=\,\frac{1}{N}\,\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}\,=\,J.

Left-multiplying the gradient-tracking recursion by
J
and using
JW=J
yields

\displaystyle J\mathbf{H}^{k+1}

\displaystyle=JW\bigl(\mathbf{H}^{k}+\mathbf{M}^{k+1}-\mathbf{M}^{k}\bigr)\,=\,J\bigl(\mathbf{H}^{k}+\mathbf{M}^{k+1}-\mathbf{M}^{k}\bigr)

\displaystyle=J\mathbf{H}^{k}+\bigl(J\mathbf{M}^{k+1}-J\mathbf{M}^{k}\bigr),\qquad\forall k\geq 0.

Taking the common block of both sides (equivalently, premultiplying by
\tfrac{1}{N}\mathbf{1}^{\top}
) gives

\bar{H}^{k+1}\,=\,\bar{H}^{k}+\bigl(\bar{M}^{k+1}-\bar{M}^{k}\bigr),\qquad\forall k\geq 0.

By the initialization
\bar{H}^{0}=\bar{M}^{0}
, the above identity telescopes to

\bar{H}^{k}-\bar{M}^{k}\,=\,\bigl(\bar{H}^{0}-\bar{M}^{0}\bigr)\,=\,0,

for every
k\geq 0
. Hence
\bar{H}^{k}=\bar{M}^{k}
for all
k\geq 0
.
∎

Lemma 6

(Gradient-tracking disagreement recursion)
.

Suppose Assumption
1
(1) holds and the gradient-tracking variable obeys

\mathbf{H}^{k+1}=W\bigl(\mathbf{H}^{k}+\mathbf{M}^{k+1}-\mathbf{M}^{k}\bigr),\qquad k\geq 0.

Let
\mathbf{1}_{N}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
be the all-ones vector and define

J\;\triangleq\;\tfrac{1}{N}\,\mathbf{1}_{N}\mathbf{1}_{N}^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N},\qquad\tilde{\mathbf{Z}}^{k}\;\triangleq\;(I-J)\mathbf{Z}^{k}\;=\;\mathbf{Z}^{k}-J\mathbf{Z}^{k}.

Then, for all
k\geq 0
,

\|\tilde{\mathbf{H}}^{k+1}\|_{F}\;\leq\;\lambda\,\bigl\|\tilde{\mathbf{H}}^{k}+(\tilde{\mathbf{M}}^{k+1}-\tilde{\mathbf{M}}^{k})\bigr\|_{F},

where
\lambda\triangleq\rho\bigl(W-J\bigr)\in(0,1)
is the mixing rate from Assumption
1
(1).
Consequently, for any
\eta>0
,

\|\tilde{\mathbf{H}}^{k+1}\|_{F}^{2}\;\leq\;(1+\eta)\lambda^{2}\,\|\tilde{\mathbf{H}}^{k}\|_{F}^{2}+(1+1/\eta)\lambda^{2}\,\|\tilde{\mathbf{M}}^{k+1}-\tilde{\mathbf{M}}^{k}\|_{F}^{2}.

Proof.

Since
W
is doubly stochastic, we have
W\mathbf{1}_{N}=\mathbf{1}_{N}
and
\mathbf{1}_{N}^{\top}W=\mathbf{1}_{N}^{\top}
, which imply

WJ\,=\,\tfrac{1}{N}W\mathbf{1}_{N}\mathbf{1}_{N}^{\top}\,=\,\tfrac{1}{N}\mathbf{1}_{N}\mathbf{1}_{N}^{\top}\,=\,J,\qquad JW\,=\,\tfrac{1}{N}\mathbf{1}_{N}(\mathbf{1}_{N}^{\top}W)\,=\,\tfrac{1}{N}\mathbf{1}_{N}\mathbf{1}_{N}^{\top}\,=\,J.

Therefore,

(I-J)W\,=\,W-JW\,=\,W-J.

Left-multiplying the gradient-tracking recursion by
(I-J)
gives

\tilde{\mathbf{H}}^{k+1}\,=\,(I-J)\mathbf{H}^{k+1}\,=\,(I-J)W\bigl(\mathbf{H}^{k}+\mathbf{M}^{k+1}-\mathbf{M}^{k}\bigr)\,=\,(W-J)\bigl(\mathbf{H}^{k}+\mathbf{M}^{k+1}-\mathbf{M}^{k}\bigr).

Moreover, for any stacked variable
\mathbf{Z}
we can decompose
\mathbf{Z}=J\mathbf{Z}+(I-J)\mathbf{Z}
, and since
(W-J)J=WJ-J^{2}=J-J=0
, we have

(W-J)\mathbf{Z}=(W-J)(I-J)\mathbf{Z}=(W-J)\tilde{\mathbf{Z}}.

Applying this identity to
\mathbf{H}^{k}
and
\mathbf{M}^{k+1}-\mathbf{M}^{k}
yields

\tilde{\mathbf{H}}^{k+1}\,=\,(W-J)\bigl(\tilde{\mathbf{H}}^{k}+(\tilde{\mathbf{M}}^{k+1}-\tilde{\mathbf{M}}^{k})\bigr).

Next, view a stacked variable
\mathbf{Z}=\mathrm{col}\{Z_{1},\dots,Z_{N}\}
(with matrix blocks
Z_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
) as a vector by vectorization. Then the action of
W-J
on the agent index corresponds to the linear map
((W-J)\otimes I_{mn})
on
\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{Z})
, and

\|(W-J)\mathbf{Z}\|_{F}\,=\,\|((W-J)\otimes I_{mn})\,\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{Z})\|_{2}\,\leq\,\|(W-J)\otimes I_{mn}\|_{2}\,\|\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{Z})\|_{2}\,=\,\|W-J\|_{2}\,\|\mathbf{Z}\|_{F}.

Because
W
is symmetric,
W-J
is also symmetric. Hence its spectral norm equals its spectral radius:

\|W-J\|_{2}\,=\,\max_{i}|\lambda_{i}(W-J)|\,=\,\rho(W-J)\,=\,\lambda.

Combining the last two displays gives

\|\tilde{\mathbf{H}}^{k+1}\|_{F}\,\leq\,\lambda\,\bigl\|\tilde{\mathbf{H}}^{k}+(\tilde{\mathbf{M}}^{k+1}-\tilde{\mathbf{M}}^{k})\bigr\|_{F}.

Finally, squaring and using the standard inequality
\|U+V\|_{F}^{2}\leq(1+\eta)\|U\|_{F}^{2}+(1+1/\eta)\|V\|_{F}^{2}
(valid for any
\eta>0
) with

U=\tilde{\mathbf{H}}^{k}
and
V=\tilde{\mathbf{M}}^{k+1}-\tilde{\mathbf{M}}^{k}
yields

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\mathbf{H}}^{k+1}\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{2}\,\bigl\|\tilde{\mathbf{H}}^{k}+(\tilde{\mathbf{M}}^{k+1}-\tilde{\mathbf{M}}^{k})\bigr\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\eta)\lambda^{2}\,\|\tilde{\mathbf{H}}^{k}\|_{F}^{2}+(1+1/\eta)\lambda^{2}\,\|\tilde{\mathbf{M}}^{k+1}-\tilde{\mathbf{M}}^{k}\|_{F}^{2}.

This proves the claim.
∎

SUDA Backbone Analysis

Lemma 7

(SUDA backbone disagreement bound with bounded input directions)
.

Suppose Assumption
1
holds.
Consider the primal–dual recursion (stacked form)

\displaystyle\mathbf{X}^{k+1}

\displaystyle=A\bigl(C\mathbf{X}^{k}-\alpha\,\mathbf{U}^{k+1}\bigr)-B\mathbf{Y}^{k},

(34)

\displaystyle\mathbf{Y}^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{Y}^{k}+B\mathbf{X}^{k+1},

where
\alpha>0
and
\mathbf{U}^{k+1}=\mathrm{col}\{U_{1}^{k+1},\ldots,U_{N}^{k+1}\}
is an arbitrary (possibly time-varying) stacked input.
Let
J:=\tfrac{1}{N}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}
and
\tilde{\mathbf{Z}}:=(I-J)\mathbf{Z}
.
Assume
\mathbf{Y}^{0}=0
.

Assume additionally that the corresponding
SUDA deviation matrix
(defined in the proof) is stable, i.e., all of its eigenvalues are strictly inside the unit circle.
Then there exist network-dependent constants

\gamma\in(0,1),\qquad v_{1}\geq 1,\qquad v_{2}\geq 1,\qquad\lambda_{a}\geq 0,

(depending only on
A,B,C
and the network) such that for all
k\geq 0
,

\begin{split}\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{k}\|_{F}\;\leq\;&\;v_{1}v_{2}\,\gamma^{k}\Big(\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{0}\|_{F}+\|B^{2}\|\,\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{0}\|_{F}\Big)\\
&\;+\frac{\alpha\,v_{1}v_{2}\,\lambda_{a}}{1-\gamma}\,\sup_{0\leq t\leq k}\|\tilde{\mathbf{U}}^{t+1}\|_{F}.\end{split}

(35)

If, moreover,
\mathbf{U}^{k+1}=\operatorname{msgn}(\mathbf{H}^{k+1})
is the blockwise matrix-sign (Muon) direction from (
3
), then Lemma
1
implies

\|\tilde{\mathbf{U}}^{k+1}\|_{F}\leq 2\sqrt{Nr_{\max}},\qquad r_{\max}:=\min\{m,n\},

(36)

and thus

\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{k}\|_{F}\;\leq\;v_{1}v_{2}\,\gamma^{k}\bigl(1+\|B^{2}\|\bigr)\,\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{0}\|_{F}\;+\frac{2\alpha\,v_{1}v_{2}\,\lambda_{a}}{1-\gamma}\,\sqrt{Nr_{\max}}.

Proof.

Step 1: a non-incremental form involving
B^{2}
only.

Define

\mathbf{z}^{k}\triangleq\mathbf{Y}^{k}-B\mathbf{X}^{k},\qquad\mathbf{s}^{k}\triangleq B\mathbf{z}^{k}=B\mathbf{Y}^{k}-B^{2}\mathbf{X}^{k}.

Substituting
\mathbf{Y}^{k}=\mathbf{z}^{k}+B\mathbf{X}^{k}
into (
34
) yields

\displaystyle\mathbf{X}^{k+1}

\displaystyle=(AC-B^{2})\mathbf{X}^{k}-\alpha A\mathbf{U}^{k+1}-B\mathbf{z}^{k},

(37a)

\displaystyle\mathbf{z}^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{z}^{k}+B\mathbf{X}^{k}.

(37b)

Multiplying (
37b
) by
B
and using the definition of
\mathbf{s}^{k}
gives

\displaystyle\mathbf{X}^{k+1}

\displaystyle=(AC-B^{2})\mathbf{X}^{k}-\alpha A\mathbf{U}^{k+1}-\mathbf{s}^{k},

(38a)

\displaystyle\mathbf{s}^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{s}^{k}+B^{2}\mathbf{X}^{k}.

(38b)

Step 2: restriction to the disagreement subspace.

Let
\tilde{\mathbf{Z}}:=(I-J)\mathbf{Z}
.
Since
A,C,B^{2}
are polynomials in
W
(Assumption
1
(2)(iii)) and
W
is doubly stochastic,
these matrices commute with
J
and preserve the consensus/disagreement decomposition; in particular,

\widetilde{A\mathbf{Z}}=A\tilde{\mathbf{Z}}
,
\widetilde{C\mathbf{Z}}=C\tilde{\mathbf{Z}}
, and
\widetilde{B^{2}\mathbf{Z}}=B^{2}\tilde{\mathbf{Z}}
.
Applying
(I-J)
to (
38
) yields

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{k+1}

\displaystyle=(AC-B^{2})\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{k}-\alpha A\tilde{\mathbf{U}}^{k+1}-\tilde{\mathbf{s}}^{k},

(39a)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{s}}^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\tilde{\mathbf{s}}^{k}+B^{2}\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{k}.

(39b)

Step 3: diagonalization in the eigenbasis of
W
.

Since
W
is symmetric, it admits an orthogonal eigendecomposition

W=U\mathrm{diag}(1,\hat{\Lambda})U^{\top}
with
U=[\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\mathbf{1},\hat{U}]
and
\hat{U}\hat{U}^{\top}=I-J
.
Because
A,C,B^{2}
are polynomials in
W
, they share the eigenvectors of
W
and thus can be decomposed as

A=U\,\mathrm{diag}(1,\hat{\Lambda}_{a})\,U^{\top},\qquad C=U\,\mathrm{diag}(1,\hat{\Lambda}_{c})\,U^{\top},\qquad B^{2}=U\,\mathrm{diag}(0,\hat{\Lambda}_{b}^{2})\,U^{\top},

for some diagonal
\hat{\Lambda}_{a},\hat{\Lambda}_{c},\hat{\Lambda}_{b}^{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{(N-1)\times(N-1)}
.
Define the projected variables

\hat{\mathbf{X}}^{k}\triangleq\hat{U}^{\top}\mathbf{X}^{k},\qquad\hat{\mathbf{s}}^{k}\triangleq\hat{U}^{\top}\mathbf{s}^{k},\qquad\hat{\mathbf{U}}^{k+1}\triangleq\hat{U}^{\top}\tilde{\mathbf{U}}^{k+1}.

Then (
39
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{X}}^{k+1}

\displaystyle=(\hat{\Lambda}_{a}\hat{\Lambda}_{c}-\hat{\Lambda}_{b}^{2})\hat{\mathbf{X}}^{k}-\hat{\mathbf{s}}^{k}-\alpha\hat{\Lambda}_{a}\hat{\mathbf{U}}^{k+1},

(40a)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{s}}^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\hat{\mathbf{s}}^{k}+\hat{\Lambda}_{b}^{2}\hat{\mathbf{X}}^{k}.

(40b)

Introduce the augmented state
\hat{\xi}^{k}:=\bigl[\hat{\mathbf{X}}^{k};\hat{\mathbf{s}}^{k}\bigr]
and the deviation matrix

\mathbf{G}\triangleq\begin{bmatrix}\hat{\Lambda}_{a}\hat{\Lambda}_{c}-\hat{\Lambda}_{b}^{2}&-I\\
\hat{\Lambda}_{b}^{2}&I\end{bmatrix}.

Then

\hat{\xi}^{k+1}=\mathbf{G}\hat{\xi}^{k}-\alpha\begin{bmatrix}\hat{\Lambda}_{a}\hat{\mathbf{U}}^{k+1}\\
0\end{bmatrix}.

(41)

By the stability assumption on
\mathbf{G}
, there exist an invertible matrix
\hat{V}
and a matrix
\Gamma
such that

\mathbf{G}=\hat{V}\Gamma\hat{V}^{-1}
and
\|\Gamma\|=:\gamma<1
.
Define
v_{1}:=\|\hat{V}\|
,
v_{2}:=\|\hat{V}^{-1}\|
, and
\lambda_{a}:=\|\hat{\Lambda}_{a}\|
.

Step 4: contraction and unrolling.

Let
\hat{e}^{k}:=\hat{V}^{-1}\hat{\xi}^{k}
.
Multiplying (
41
) by
\hat{V}^{-1}
gives

\hat{e}^{k+1}=\Gamma\hat{e}^{k}-\alpha\hat{V}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}\hat{\Lambda}_{a}\hat{\mathbf{U}}^{k+1}\\
0\end{bmatrix}.

Taking Frobenius norms and using
\|\hat{\mathbf{U}}^{k+1}\|_{F}\leq\|\tilde{\mathbf{U}}^{k+1}\|_{F}
yields

\|\hat{e}^{k+1}\|_{F}\leq\gamma\|\hat{e}^{k}\|_{F}+\alpha v_{2}\lambda_{a}\|\tilde{\mathbf{U}}^{k+1}\|_{F}.

Unrolling gives

\|\hat{e}^{k}\|_{F}\leq\gamma^{k}\|\hat{e}^{0}\|_{F}+\frac{\alpha v_{2}\lambda_{a}}{1-\gamma}\sup_{0\leq t\leq k}\|\tilde{\mathbf{U}}^{t+1}\|_{F}.

Since
\hat{\xi}^{k}=\hat{V}\hat{e}^{k}
, we have
\|\hat{\mathbf{X}}^{k}\|_{F}\leq\|\hat{\xi}^{k}\|_{F}\leq v_{1}\|\hat{e}^{k}\|_{F}
and
\|\hat{e}^{0}\|_{F}\leq v_{2}\|\hat{\xi}^{0}\|_{F}
.
Moreover,
\mathbf{Y}^{0}=0
implies
\mathbf{s}^{0}=-B^{2}\mathbf{X}^{0}
and hence
\hat{\mathbf{s}}^{0}=-\hat{\Lambda}_{b}^{2}\hat{\mathbf{X}}^{0}
.
Therefore
\|\hat{\xi}^{0}\|_{F}\leq(1+\|\hat{\Lambda}_{b}^{2}\|)\|\hat{\mathbf{X}}^{0}\|_{F}\leq(1+\|B^{2}\|)\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{0}\|_{F}
.
Finally, since
\hat{U}\hat{U}^{\top}=I-J
we have
\|\hat{\mathbf{X}}^{k}\|_{F}=\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{k}\|_{F}
.
Combining these bounds yields (
35
).

Step 5: boundedness of the Muon directions.

If
\mathbf{U}^{k+1}=\operatorname{msgn}(\mathbf{H}^{k+1})
, then by Lemma
1
, each block satisfies
\|U_{i}^{k+1}\|_{F}\leq\sqrt{r_{\max}}
.
Thus

\|\tilde{\mathbf{U}}^{k+1}\|_{F}\leq\|\mathbf{U}^{k+1}\|_{F}+\|J\mathbf{U}^{k+1}\|_{F}\leq 2\|\mathbf{U}^{k+1}\|_{F}\leq 2\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|U_{i}^{k+1}\|_{F}^{2}}\leq 2\sqrt{Nr_{\max}},

which is (
36
).
∎

Drift, Mismatch, and Tracking Error Decomposition

Lemma 8

(EMA bias for averaged true gradients (drift term))
.

Suppose Assumption
2
holds.
Define the averaged true gradient at the local iterates

\overline{\nabla\mathbf{f}}(\mathbf{X}^{k})\;\triangleq\;\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\nabla f_{i}(X_{i}^{k}),

and the averaged EMA of true gradients by

\bar{C}^{0}\;:=\;\overline{\nabla\mathbf{f}}(\mathbf{X}^{0}),\qquad\bar{C}^{k+1}\;:=\;\beta\,\bar{C}^{k}+(1-\beta)\,\overline{\nabla\mathbf{f}}(\mathbf{X}^{k}),\qquad k\geq 0.

Define the (one-step-ahead) EMA bias

D^{k}\;\triangleq\;\overline{\nabla\mathbf{f}}(\mathbf{X}^{k})-\bar{C}^{k+1},\qquad k\geq 0.

Then, for all
k\geq 0
,
D^{k}
obeys the recursion

D^{k+1}\,=\,\beta D^{k}+\beta\bigl(\overline{\nabla\mathbf{f}}(\mathbf{X}^{k+1})-\overline{\nabla\mathbf{f}}(\mathbf{X}^{k})\bigr).

Moreover, for all
k\geq 0
,

\bigl\|\overline{\nabla\mathbf{f}}(\mathbf{X}^{k+1})-\overline{\nabla\mathbf{f}}(\mathbf{X}^{k})\bigr\|_{*}\;\leq\;\frac{L_{*}}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|X_{i}^{k+1}-X_{i}^{k}\|.

(42)

If, in addition, the iterates are generated by Algorithm
1
, then

\begin{split}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|X_{i}^{k+1}-X_{i}^{k}\|\;\leq\;&\;\|\bar{X}^{k+1}-\bar{X}^{k}\|+\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\,\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{k+1}\|_{F}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\,\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{k}\|_{F},\end{split}

(43)

where
\bar{X}^{k}:=\tfrac{1}{N}\sum_{i}X_{i}^{k}
and
\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{k}:=(I-J)\mathbf{X}^{k}
with
J:=\tfrac{1}{N}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}
.
Consequently, using Lemmas
2
and
3
(which imply
\|\bar{X}^{k+1}-\bar{X}^{k}\|\leq\alpha
), we have for all
k\geq 0
,

\|D^{k}\|_{*}\;\leq\;\frac{\beta L_{*}}{1-\beta}\Big(\alpha+\frac{2}{\sqrt{N}}\,\sup_{t\geq 0}\bigl(\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{t+1}\|_{F}+\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{t}\|_{F}\bigr)\Big).

(44)

In particular, if Lemma
7
holds (so that for all
t\geq 0
,

\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{t}\|_{F}\leq v_{1}v_{2}\gamma^{t}\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{0}\|_{F}+\tfrac{2\alpha v_{1}v_{2}\lambda_{a}}{1-\gamma}\sqrt{Nr_{\max}}
), then for all
k\geq 0
,

\|D^{k}\|_{*}\;\leq\;\frac{\beta L_{*}}{1-\beta}\Bigg[\alpha+\frac{4v_{1}v_{2}}{\sqrt{N}}\,\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{0}\|_{F}+\frac{8\alpha v_{1}v_{2}\lambda_{a}}{1-\gamma}\,\sqrt{r_{\max}}\Bigg].

(45)

Proof.

Throughout, abbreviate
g^{k}:=\overline{\nabla\mathbf{f}}(\mathbf{X}^{k})
.

Step 1: bias recursion.

By the definition of
D^{k+1}
and the EMA update,

D^{k+1}=g^{k+1}-\bar{C}^{k+2}=g^{k+1}-\bigl(\beta\bar{C}^{k+1}+(1-\beta)g^{k+1}\bigr)=\beta\bigl(g^{k+1}-\bar{C}^{k+1}\bigr).

Next, add and subtract
g^{k}
and use
D^{k}=g^{k}-\bar{C}^{k+1}
to obtain

g^{k+1}-\bar{C}^{k+1}=\bigl(g^{k+1}-g^{k}\bigr)+\bigl(g^{k}-\bar{C}^{k+1}\bigr)=\bigl(g^{k+1}-g^{k}\bigr)+D^{k}.

Combining the last two displays yields

D^{k+1}=\beta D^{k}+\beta\bigl(g^{k+1}-g^{k}\bigr),

as claimed.

Step 2: bound the averaged true-gradient drift by smoothness.

By convexity of
\|\cdot\|_{*}
and Assumption
2
,

\displaystyle\|g^{k+1}-g^{k}\|_{*}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bigl(\nabla f_{i}(X_{i}^{k+1})-\nabla f_{i}(X_{i}^{k})\bigr)\Big\|_{*}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\nabla f_{i}(X_{i}^{k+1})-\nabla f_{i}(X_{i}^{k})\|_{*}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{L_{*}}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|X_{i}^{k+1}-X_{i}^{k}\|,

which is (
42
).

Step 3: bound the average step size by the average motion and consensus errors.

For each
i
, insert and subtract the averages
\bar{X}^{k+1}
and
\bar{X}^{k}
and use triangle inequality:

\|X_{i}^{k+1}-X_{i}^{k}\|\leq\|X_{i}^{k+1}-\bar{X}^{k+1}\|+\|\bar{X}^{k+1}-\bar{X}^{k}\|+\|\bar{X}^{k}-X_{i}^{k}\|.

Averaging over
i
yields

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|X_{i}^{k+1}-X_{i}^{k}\|\leq\|\bar{X}^{k+1}-\bar{X}^{k}\|+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|X_{i}^{k+1}-\bar{X}^{k+1}\|+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|X_{i}^{k}-\bar{X}^{k}\|.

For the disagreement terms, use
\|A\|\leq\|A\|_{F}
and Cauchy–Schwarz:

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|X_{i}^{k}-\bar{X}^{k}\|\leq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|X_{i}^{k}-\bar{X}^{k}\|_{F}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|X_{i}^{k}-\bar{X}^{k}\|_{F}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\,\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{k}\|_{F}\leq\frac{2}{\sqrt{N}}\,\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{k}\|_{F}.

The same bound holds with
k
replaced by
k+1
, yielding (
43
).

Step 4: unroll the bias recursion.

Combine the recursion from Step 1, the drift bound (
42
), and the step-size bound (
43
) to get

\|D^{k+1}\|_{*}\leq\beta\|D^{k}\|_{*}+\beta L_{*}\Big(\|\bar{X}^{k+1}-\bar{X}^{k}\|+\tfrac{2}{\sqrt{N}}\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{k+1}\|_{F}+\tfrac{2}{\sqrt{N}}\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{k}\|_{F}\Big).

Under Algorithm
1
, Lemma
2
gives

\|\bar{X}^{k+1}-\bar{X}^{k}\|=\alpha\|\bar{S}^{k+1}\|
, and Lemma
3
(specifically (
20
) therein) implies
\|\bar{S}^{k+1}\|\leq 1
, hence
\|\bar{X}^{k+1}-\bar{X}^{k}\|\leq\alpha
.
Moreover,
D^{0}=g^{0}-\bar{C}^{1}=0
because
\bar{C}^{0}=g^{0}
.
Unrolling the above linear recursion and bounding the geometric series yields

\|D^{k}\|_{*}\leq\beta L_{*}\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\beta^{k-1-t}\Big(\alpha+\tfrac{2}{\sqrt{N}}\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{t+1}\|_{F}+\tfrac{2}{\sqrt{N}}\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{t}\|_{F}\Big)\leq\frac{\beta L_{*}}{1-\beta}\Big(\alpha+\tfrac{2}{\sqrt{N}}\sup_{t\geq 0}(\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{t+1}\|_{F}+\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{t}\|_{F})\Big),

which proves (
44
).

Step 5: plug in the SUDA consensus bound (Lemma
7
).

Lemma
7
implies for all
t\geq 0
,

\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{t}\|_{F}\leq v_{1}v_{2}\,\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{0}\|_{F}+\frac{2\alpha v_{1}v_{2}\lambda_{a}}{1-\gamma}\sqrt{Nr_{\max}},

since
\gamma^{t}\leq 1
.
Therefore,

\sup_{t\geq 0}(\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{t+1}\|_{F}+\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{t}\|_{F})\leq 2v_{1}v_{2}\,\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{0}\|_{F}+\frac{4\alpha v_{1}v_{2}\lambda_{a}}{1-\gamma}\sqrt{Nr_{\max}}.

Substituting this into (
44
) and simplifying gives (
45
).
∎

Lemma 9

(Consensus-induced gradient mismatch)
.

Assume Assumption
2
.
For any stacked primal iterate
\mathbf{X}^{k}=\mathrm{col}\{X_{1}^{k},\dots,X_{N}^{k}\}
, define

\bar{X}^{k}:=\tfrac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}^{k},\qquad\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{k}:=(I-J)\mathbf{X}^{k},\quad J:=\tfrac{1}{N}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}.

Then the mismatch between the gradient at the averaged point and the average of local gradients at local points satisfies

\Big\|\nabla f(\bar{X}^{k})-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\nabla f_{i}(X_{i}^{k})\Big\|_{*}\;\leq\;\frac{L_{*}}{\sqrt{N}}\,\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{k}\|_{F}.

(46)

Proof.

Using
\nabla f(\bar{X}^{k})=\tfrac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\nabla f_{i}(\bar{X}^{k})
, we write

\nabla f(\bar{X}^{k})-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\nabla f_{i}(X_{i}^{k})=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bigl(\nabla f_{i}(\bar{X}^{k})-\nabla f_{i}(X_{i}^{k})\bigr).

By convexity of the nuclear norm and the triangle inequality,

\Big\|\nabla f(\bar{X}^{k})-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\nabla f_{i}(X_{i}^{k})\Big\|_{*}\leq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\nabla f_{i}(\bar{X}^{k})-\nabla f_{i}(X_{i}^{k})\|_{*}.

By Assumption
2
,
\|\nabla f_{i}(\bar{X}^{k})-\nabla f_{i}(X_{i}^{k})\|_{*}\leq L_{*}\|\bar{X}^{k}-X_{i}^{k}\|
.
Moreover,
\|\bar{X}^{k}-X_{i}^{k}\|\leq\|\bar{X}^{k}-X_{i}^{k}\|_{F}
.
Therefore,

\Big\|\nabla f(\bar{X}^{k})-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\nabla f_{i}(X_{i}^{k})\Big\|_{*}\leq\frac{L_{*}}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|X_{i}^{k}-\bar{X}^{k}\|_{F}.

Finally, Cauchy–Schwarz gives

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|X_{i}^{k}-\bar{X}^{k}\|_{F}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|X_{i}^{k}-\bar{X}^{k}\|_{F}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\,\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{k}\|_{F}.

Combining the last two displays yields (
46
).
∎

Lemma 10

(Decomposing the tracking error
\|\nabla f(\bar{X}^{k})-H_{i}^{k+1}\|_{*}
)
.

Assume Assumptions
1
–
3
.
Let
(\mathbf{X}^{k},\mathbf{M}^{k},\mathbf{H}^{k})
be generated by (
3
) with initialization
H_{i}^{0}=M_{i}^{0}
for all
i\in[N]
.
Define

\bar{X}^{k}:=\tfrac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}^{k},\qquad\bar{M}^{k}:=\tfrac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}M_{i}^{k},\qquad\bar{H}^{k}:=\tfrac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}H_{i}^{k},

\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{k}:=(I-J)\mathbf{X}^{k},\qquad\tilde{\mathbf{H}}^{k}:=(I-J)\mathbf{H}^{k},\quad J:=\tfrac{1}{N}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}.

Moreover, define the averaged true-gradient EMA
(\bar{C}^{k})_{k\geq 0}
and the drift variable
(D^{k})_{k\geq 0}
as in Lemma
8
:

\bar{C}^{0}:=\overline{\nabla\mathbf{f}}(\mathbf{X}^{0}),\qquad\bar{C}^{k+1}:=\beta\bar{C}^{k}+(1-\beta)\,\overline{\nabla\mathbf{f}}(\mathbf{X}^{k}),\qquad D^{k}:=\overline{\nabla\mathbf{f}}(\mathbf{X}^{k})-\bar{C}^{k+1},

where
\overline{\nabla\mathbf{f}}(\mathbf{X}^{k}):=\tfrac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\nabla f_{i}(X_{i}^{k})
.
Then for every
k\geq 0
,

\begin{split}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\nabla f(\bar{X}^{k})-H_{i}^{k+1}\|_{*}\;\leq\;&\;\frac{L_{*}}{\sqrt{N}}\,\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{k}\|_{F}\;+\|D^{k}\|_{*}\\
&\;+\|\bar{C}^{k+1}-\bar{M}^{k+1}\|_{*}\;+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|H_{i}^{k+1}-\bar{H}^{k+1}\|_{*}.\end{split}

(47)

Furthermore, letting
r_{\max}:=\min\{m,n\}
, we have the conversion bound

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|H_{i}^{k+1}-\bar{H}^{k+1}\|_{*}\;\leq\;\sqrt{\frac{r_{\max}}{N}}\,\|\tilde{\mathbf{H}}^{k+1}\|_{F}.

(48)

Also,

\|\bar{C}^{k+1}-\bar{M}^{k+1}\|_{*}\;\leq\;\sqrt{r_{\max}}\,\|\bar{C}^{k+1}-\bar{M}^{k+1}\|_{F}.

(49)

Proof.

Step 1: a four-term decomposition.

Fix
k\geq 0
and an agent
i\in[N]
.
Using Lemma
5
, we have
\bar{H}^{k+1}=\bar{M}^{k+1}
.
Therefore, by repeated addition/subtraction and the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\|\nabla f(\bar{X}^{k})-H_{i}^{k+1}\|_{*}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|\nabla f(\bar{X}^{k})-\overline{\nabla\mathbf{f}}(\mathbf{X}^{k})\Big\|_{*}+\Big\|\overline{\nabla\mathbf{f}}(\mathbf{X}^{k})-\bar{C}^{k+1}\Big\|_{*}

\displaystyle\quad+\|\bar{C}^{k+1}-\bar{M}^{k+1}\|_{*}+\|\bar{H}^{k+1}-H_{i}^{k+1}\|_{*}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\nabla f(\bar{X}^{k})-\overline{\nabla\mathbf{f}}(\mathbf{X}^{k})\Big\|_{*}+\|D^{k}\|_{*}+\|\bar{C}^{k+1}-\bar{M}^{k+1}\|_{*}+\|H_{i}^{k+1}-\bar{H}^{k+1}\|_{*}.

Averaging over
i
yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\nabla f(\bar{X}^{k})-H_{i}^{k+1}\|_{*}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|\nabla f(\bar{X}^{k})-\overline{\nabla\mathbf{f}}(\mathbf{X}^{k})\Big\|_{*}+\|D^{k}\|_{*}

\displaystyle\quad+\|\bar{C}^{k+1}-\bar{M}^{k+1}\|_{*}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|H_{i}^{k+1}-\bar{H}^{k+1}\|_{*}.

Step 2: bound the first term by disagreement of
\mathbf{X}^{k}
.

Apply Lemma
9
to obtain

\Big\|\nabla f(\bar{X}^{k})-\overline{\nabla\mathbf{f}}(\mathbf{X}^{k})\Big\|_{*}\leq\frac{L_{*}}{\sqrt{N}}\,\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{k}\|_{F},

which proves (
47
).

Step 3: disagreement-to-average conversion for
\mathbf{H}^{k+1}
.

For each
i
, by Cauchy–Schwarz on singular values,
\|A\|_{*}\leq\sqrt{r_{\max}}\|A\|_{F}
.
Therefore,

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|H_{i}^{k+1}-\bar{H}^{k+1}\|_{*}\leq\frac{\sqrt{r_{\max}}}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|H_{i}^{k+1}-\bar{H}^{k+1}\|_{F}\leq\sqrt{\frac{r_{\max}}{N}}\,\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|H_{i}^{k+1}-\bar{H}^{k+1}\|_{F}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

Since
\tilde{\mathbf{H}}^{k+1}=(I-J)\mathbf{H}^{k+1}
has
i
-th block
H_{i}^{k+1}-\bar{H}^{k+1}
,

\|\tilde{\mathbf{H}}^{k+1}\|_{F}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|H_{i}^{k+1}-\bar{H}^{k+1}\|_{F}^{2}
, proving (
48
).

Step 4: nuclear-to-Frobenius conversion for the averaged EMA noise.

The same inequality
\|A\|_{*}\leq\sqrt{r_{\max}}\|A\|_{F}
yields (
49
).

Forcing Terms and Disagreement Bounds

Lemma 11

(A uniform bound on the stacked one-step motion)
.

Assume Assumptions
1
–
4
and that the iterates are generated by (
3
) with
Y_{i}^{0}=0
.
Define the agentwise average
\bar{X}^{k}:=\tfrac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}^{k}
, let
J:=\tfrac{1}{N}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}
, and define
\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{k}:=(I-J)\mathbf{X}^{k}
.
Then, for every
k\geq 0
,

\|\mathbf{X}^{k+1}-\mathbf{X}^{k}\|_{F}\;\leq\;\sqrt{N}\,\|\bar{X}^{k+1}-\bar{X}^{k}\|_{F}+\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{k+1}\|_{F}+\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{k}\|_{F}.

(50)

Consequently, using Lemma
2
and Lemma
1
(which imply
\|\bar{X}^{k+1}-\bar{X}^{k}\|_{F}\leq\alpha\sqrt{r_{\max}}
), we have

\|\mathbf{X}^{k+1}-\mathbf{X}^{k}\|_{F}\;\leq\;\alpha\sqrt{Nr_{\max}}+\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{k+1}\|_{F}+\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{k}\|_{F},\qquad r_{\max}:=\min\{m,n\}.

(51)

If, moreover, the assumptions and initialization of Lemma
7
hold with
\mathbf{U}^{k+1}=\operatorname{msgn}(\mathbf{H}^{k+1})
(so that
\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{t}\|_{F}\leq v_{1}v_{2}\gamma^{t}\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{0}\|_{F}+\tfrac{2\alpha v_{1}v_{2}\lambda_{a}}{1-\gamma}\sqrt{Nr_{\max}}
for all
t
), then

\sup_{k\geq 0}\|\mathbf{X}^{k+1}-\mathbf{X}^{k}\|_{F}\;\leq\;\alpha\sqrt{Nr_{\max}}+2v_{1}v_{2}\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{0}\|_{F}+\frac{4\alpha v_{1}v_{2}\lambda_{a}}{1-\gamma}\,\sqrt{Nr_{\max}}.

(52)

Proof.

Step 1: decompose into average and disagreement parts.

Write

\mathbf{X}^{k+1}-\mathbf{X}^{k}=J(\mathbf{X}^{k+1}-\mathbf{X}^{k})+(I-J)(\mathbf{X}^{k+1}-\mathbf{X}^{k}).

Take Frobenius norms and use the triangle inequality:

\|\mathbf{X}^{k+1}-\mathbf{X}^{k}\|_{F}\leq\|J(\mathbf{X}^{k+1}-\mathbf{X}^{k})\|_{F}+\|(I-J)(\mathbf{X}^{k+1}-\mathbf{X}^{k})\|_{F}.

Because
J\mathbf{Z}
has all blocks equal to
\bar{Z}:=\tfrac{1}{N}\sum_{i}Z_{i}
, we have

J(\mathbf{X}^{k+1}-\mathbf{X}^{k})=\mathrm{col}\{\bar{X}^{k+1}-\bar{X}^{k},\ldots,\bar{X}^{k+1}-\bar{X}^{k}\}
and hence

\|J(\mathbf{X}^{k+1}-\mathbf{X}^{k})\|_{F}=\sqrt{N}\,\|\bar{X}^{k+1}-\bar{X}^{k}\|_{F}.

Moreover,
(I-J)(\mathbf{X}^{k+1}-\mathbf{X}^{k})=\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{k+1}-\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{k}
, so

\|(I-J)(\mathbf{X}^{k+1}-\mathbf{X}^{k})\|_{F}\leq\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{k+1}\|_{F}+\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{k}\|_{F}.

Combining the last three displays yields (
50
).

Step 2: bound the average motion in Frobenius norm.

By Lemma
2
,

\bar{X}^{k+1}-\bar{X}^{k}=-\alpha\bar{S}^{k+1}
, where
\bar{S}^{k+1}=\tfrac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}S_{i}^{k+1}
and
S_{i}^{k+1}=\operatorname{msgn}(H_{i}^{k+1})
.
By Lemma
1
(2),
\|S_{i}^{k+1}\|_{F}\leq\sqrt{r_{\max}}
.
By convexity of the Frobenius norm,

\|\bar{S}^{k+1}\|_{F}\leq\tfrac{1}{N}\sum_{i}\|S_{i}^{k+1}\|_{F}\leq\sqrt{r_{\max}}
.
Hence
\|\bar{X}^{k+1}-\bar{X}^{k}\|_{F}\leq\alpha\sqrt{r_{\max}}
.
Substituting this into (
50
) yields (
51
).

Step 3: plug in the SUDA consensus bound.

Under Lemma
7
with Muon directions, for any
t\geq 0
,

\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{t}\|_{F}\leq v_{1}v_{2}\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{0}\|_{F}+\tfrac{2\alpha v_{1}v_{2}\lambda_{a}}{1-\gamma}\sqrt{Nr_{\max}}
since
\gamma^{t}\leq 1
.
Plugging this into (
51
) and taking the supremum over
k
yields (
52
).

Lemma 12

(Bounding the gradient-difference forcing term)
.

Assume Assumptions
2
and
3
.
Let
\mathbf{X}^{k}
be any (possibly random) stacked iterates and let

\mathbf{G}^{k}=\mathrm{col}\{G_{1}^{k},\ldots,G_{N}^{k}\}
be the stochastic gradients with

G_{i}^{k}=\nabla F_{i}(X_{i}^{k};\xi_{i}^{k})
.
Define the stacked noise
\Delta_{i}^{k}:=G_{i}^{k}-\nabla f_{i}(X_{i}^{k})
and
\mathbf{\Delta}^{k}:=\mathrm{col}\{\Delta_{1}^{k},\ldots,\Delta_{N}^{k}\}
.
Let
J:=\tfrac{1}{N}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}
and
\tilde{\mathbf{Z}}^{k}:=(I-J)\mathbf{Z}^{k}
.
Then for any
k\geq 1
,

\mathbb{E}\big[\|\tilde{\mathbf{G}}^{k}-\tilde{\mathbf{G}}^{k-1}\|_{F}^{2}\big]\;\leq\;2L_{*}^{2}\,\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathbf{X}^{k}-\mathbf{X}^{k-1}\|_{F}^{2}\big]+8N\sigma^{2}.

(53)

In particular, if Lemma
11
holds so that

\|\mathbf{X}^{t}-\mathbf{X}^{t-1}\|_{F}\leq B_{X}
for all
t\geq 1
, then

\sup_{k\geq 1}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\tilde{\mathbf{G}}^{k}-\tilde{\mathbf{G}}^{k-1}\|_{F}^{2}\big]\;\leq\;2L_{*}^{2}B_{X}^{2}+8N\sigma^{2}.

(54)

Proof.

Fix
k\geq 1
. Decompose

\mathbf{G}^{k}-\mathbf{G}^{k-1}=\bigl(\nabla\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{X}^{k})-\nabla\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{X}^{k-1})\bigr)+\bigl(\mathbf{\Delta}^{k}-\mathbf{\Delta}^{k-1}\bigr),

where
\nabla\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{X}^{k}):=\mathrm{col}\{\nabla f_{1}(X_{1}^{k}),\ldots,\nabla f_{N}(X_{N}^{k})\}
.
Since
I-J
is an orthogonal projection,
\|\tilde{\mathbf{Z}}\|_{F}\leq\|\mathbf{Z}\|_{F}
for all stacked
\mathbf{Z}
.
Therefore, using
\|U+V\|_{F}^{2}\leq 2\|U\|_{F}^{2}+2\|V\|_{F}^{2}
,

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\mathbf{G}}^{k}-\tilde{\mathbf{G}}^{k-1}\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|(I-J)(\mathbf{G}^{k}-\mathbf{G}^{k-1})\|_{F}^{2}\leq\|\mathbf{G}^{k}-\mathbf{G}^{k-1}\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\,\|\nabla\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{X}^{k})-\nabla\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{X}^{k-1})\|_{F}^{2}+2\,\|\mathbf{\Delta}^{k}-\mathbf{\Delta}^{k-1}\|_{F}^{2}.

Take expectations.

For the first term, by Assumption
2
, for each
i
we have

\|\nabla f_{i}(X_{i}^{k})-\nabla f_{i}(X_{i}^{k-1})\|_{F}\leq\|\nabla f_{i}(X_{i}^{k})-\nabla f_{i}(X_{i}^{k-1})\|_{*}\leq L_{*}\,\|X_{i}^{k}-X_{i}^{k-1}\|\leq L_{*}\,\|X_{i}^{k}-X_{i}^{k-1}\|_{F}.

Squaring and summing over
i
yields

\|\nabla\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{X}^{k})-\nabla\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{X}^{k-1})\|_{F}^{2}\leq L_{*}^{2}\,\|\mathbf{X}^{k}-\mathbf{X}^{k-1}\|_{F}^{2}
.

For the second term, use
\|A-B\|_{F}^{2}\leq 2\|A\|_{F}^{2}+2\|B\|_{F}^{2}
blockwise:

\|\mathbf{\Delta}^{k}-\mathbf{\Delta}^{k-1}\|_{F}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\Delta_{i}^{k}-\Delta_{i}^{k-1}\|_{F}^{2}\leq 2\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\Delta_{i}^{k}\|_{F}^{2}+2\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\Delta_{i}^{k-1}\|_{F}^{2}.

Taking expectations and using Assumption
3
(unconditional version of the conditional variance bound) gives

\mathbb{E}\|\Delta_{i}^{t}\|_{F}^{2}\leq\sigma^{2}
for all
i,t
, and hence

\mathbb{E}\|\mathbf{\Delta}^{k}-\mathbf{\Delta}^{k-1}\|_{F}^{2}\leq 4N\sigma^{2}
.

Combining the above bounds proves (
53
).
Finally, if
\|\mathbf{X}^{k}-\mathbf{X}^{k-1}\|_{F}\leq B_{X}
holds uniformly, then
(
54
) follows immediately.
∎

Lemma 13

(A bound on the EMA increment disagreement)
.

Assume Assumption
3
.
Let the iterates be generated by (
3
) with the EMA initialization
\mathbf{M}^{0}=\mathbf{G}^{0}
(as in Algorithm
1
).
Let
\beta\in[0,1)
.
Define
J:=\tfrac{1}{N}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}
and
\tilde{\mathbf{Z}}^{k}:=(I-J)\mathbf{Z}^{k}
.
For
k\geq 1
define the EMA increment
\mathbf{V}^{k}:=\mathbf{M}^{k}-\mathbf{M}^{k-1}
and its disagreement
\tilde{\mathbf{V}}^{k}:=(I-J)\mathbf{V}^{k}
.
Then
\mathbf{V}^{1}=0
and for all
k\geq 1
,

\tilde{\mathbf{V}}^{k+1}\;=\;\beta\,\tilde{\mathbf{V}}^{k}+(1-\beta)\,\bigl(\tilde{\mathbf{G}}^{k}-\tilde{\mathbf{G}}^{k-1}\bigr),\qquad\tilde{\mathbf{G}}^{k}:=(I-J)\mathbf{G}^{k}.

(55)

Consequently, for any
\eta>0
and all
k\geq 1
,

\|\tilde{\mathbf{V}}^{k+1}\|_{F}^{2}\;\leq\;(1+\eta)\beta^{2}\,\|\tilde{\mathbf{V}}^{k}\|_{F}^{2}+(1+1/\eta)(1-\beta)^{2}\,\|\tilde{\mathbf{G}}^{k}-\tilde{\mathbf{G}}^{k-1}\|_{F}^{2}.

(56)

In particular, if there exists a constant
B_{G}
such that

\sup_{k\geq 1}\mathbb{E}\|\tilde{\mathbf{G}}^{k}-\tilde{\mathbf{G}}^{k-1}\|_{F}^{2}\leq B_{G}^{2}
,
then

\sup_{k\geq 1}\mathbb{E}\|\tilde{\mathbf{M}}^{k}-\tilde{\mathbf{M}}^{k-1}\|_{F}^{2}\;=\;\sup_{k\geq 1}\mathbb{E}\|\tilde{\mathbf{V}}^{k}\|_{F}^{2}\;\leq\;\frac{2(1+\beta^{2})}{(1+\beta)^{2}}\,B_{G}^{2}\;\leq\;2\,B_{G}^{2}.

(57)

Proof.

Step 1: derive the increment recursion.

For
k\geq 1
, subtract the EMA updates at times
k
and
k-1
:

\mathbf{M}^{k+1}-\mathbf{M}^{k}=\beta(\mathbf{M}^{k}-\mathbf{M}^{k-1})+(1-\beta)(\mathbf{G}^{k}-\mathbf{G}^{k-1}).

This is exactly
\mathbf{V}^{k+1}=\beta\mathbf{V}^{k}+(1-\beta)(\mathbf{G}^{k}-\mathbf{G}^{k-1})
.
Applying
(I-J)
to both sides gives (
55
).
Moreover, since
\mathbf{M}^{0}=\mathbf{G}^{0}
, we have
\mathbf{M}^{1}=\beta\mathbf{M}^{0}+(1-\beta)\mathbf{G}^{0}=\mathbf{M}^{0}
and hence
\mathbf{V}^{1}=\mathbf{M}^{1}-\mathbf{M}^{0}=0
.

Step 2: square and apply Young’s inequality.

From (
55
) and
\|U+V\|_{F}^{2}\leq(1+\eta)\|U\|_{F}^{2}+(1+1/\eta)\|V\|_{F}^{2}
,
with
U=\beta\tilde{\mathbf{V}}^{k}
and
V=(1-\beta)(\tilde{\mathbf{G}}^{k}-\tilde{\mathbf{G}}^{k-1})
, we get (
56
).

Step 3: uniform bound under a uniform forcing bound.

Assume
\sup_{k\geq 1}\mathbb{E}\|\tilde{\mathbf{G}}^{k}-\tilde{\mathbf{G}}^{k-1}\|_{F}^{2}\leq B_{G}^{2}
.
For
\beta\in(0,1)
, pick
\eta:=\tfrac{1-\beta^{2}}{2\beta^{2}}
so that
(1+\eta)\beta^{2}=\tfrac{1+\beta^{2}}{2}=:q\in(0,1)
and
1+1/\eta=\tfrac{1+\beta^{2}}{1-\beta^{2}}
.
Taking expectations in (
56
) gives, for all
k\geq 1
,

\mathbb{E}\|\tilde{\mathbf{V}}^{k+1}\|_{F}^{2}\leq q\,\mathbb{E}\|\tilde{\mathbf{V}}^{k}\|_{F}^{2}+(1-\beta)^{2}\,\frac{1+\beta^{2}}{1-\beta^{2}}\,B_{G}^{2}.

Unrolling this recursion and using
\tilde{\mathbf{V}}^{1}=0
yields, for all
k\geq 1
,

\mathbb{E}\|\tilde{\mathbf{V}}^{k+1}\|_{F}^{2}\leq(1-\beta)^{2}\,\frac{1+\beta^{2}}{1-\beta^{2}}\,\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}q^{t}\,B_{G}^{2}\leq(1-\beta)^{2}\,\frac{1+\beta^{2}}{1-\beta^{2}}\,\frac{1}{1-q}\,B_{G}^{2}.

Since
1-q=\tfrac{1-\beta^{2}}{2}
, the last display becomes

\mathbb{E}\|\tilde{\mathbf{V}}^{k+1}\|_{F}^{2}\leq\frac{2(1+\beta^{2})(1-\beta)^{2}}{(1-\beta^{2})^{2}}\,B_{G}^{2}=\frac{2(1+\beta^{2})}{(1+\beta)^{2}}\,B_{G}^{2}.

Taking the supremum over
k
gives (
57
) for
\beta\in(0,1)
.
For
\beta=0
,
\mathbf{V}^{1}=0
and (
55
) reduces to
\tilde{\mathbf{V}}^{k+1}=\tilde{\mathbf{G}}^{k}-\tilde{\mathbf{G}}^{k-1}
, hence

\sup_{k\geq 1}\mathbb{E}\|\tilde{\mathbf{V}}^{k}\|_{F}^{2}\leq B_{G}^{2}\leq 2B_{G}^{2}
.
∎

Lemma 14

(A uniform bound on the gradient-tracking disagreement)

(1) holds.

\mathbb{E}\|\tilde{\mathbf{H}}^{k+1}\|_{F}^{2}\leq q^{k+1}\,\mathbb{E}\|\tilde{\mathbf{H}}^{0}\|_{F}^{2}+\lambda^{2}\,\frac{1+\lambda^{2}}{1-\lambda^{2}}\,\frac{2}{1-\lambda^{2}}\,B_{M}^{2}=q^{k+1}\,\mathbb{E}\|\tilde{\mathbf{H}}^{0}\|_{F}^{2}+\frac{2\lambda^{2}(1+\lambda^{2})}{(1-\lambda^{2})^{2}}\,B_{M}^{2},

which is (
59
).
Taking the supremum over
k

60

\begin{split}\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}\,\|E_{\mathrm{gt}}^{k+1}\|_{F}\;\leq\;&\;\underbrace{\sqrt{\tfrac{1-\beta}{1+\beta}}\,\tfrac{\sigma}{\sqrt{N}}}_{\text{stochastic noise }\sim\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{N}}\sqrt{1-\beta}}\;+\underbrace{\frac{L_{*}}{1-\beta}\cdot\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}\,\|\bar{X}^{k+1}-\bar{X}^{k}\|_{F}}_{\text{drift }\sim\frac{L_{*}\alpha}{1-\beta}}\\
&\;+\underbrace{\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}\,\|\bar{g}^{k}-\nabla f(\bar{X}^{k})\|_{F}}_{\text{consensus bias}}\;+\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\beta^{k+1}\,\mathbb{E}\,\|E_{\mathrm{gt}}^{0}\|_{F}.\end{split}

(70)

Moreover, under Assumption
2
, the consensus bias can be bounded by

\|\bar{g}^{k}-\nabla f(\bar{X}^{k})\|_{F}\;\leq\;\frac{L_{*}}{\sqrt{N}}\,\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{k}\|_{F},\qquad\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{k}:=(I-J)\mathbf{X}^{k},\;J:=\tfrac{1}{N}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}.

(71)

Proof.

By Lemma
5
,
\bar{H}^{k}=\bar{M}^{k}
for all
k\geq 0
.
Averaging the EMA update gives

\bar{M}^{k+1}=\beta\bar{M}^{k}+(1-\beta)\bar{G}^{k}.

(72)

Fix
k\geq 0
. Using
E_{\mathrm{gt}}^{k}=\bar{M}^{k}-\nabla f(\bar{X}^{k})
and (
72
),

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{gt}}^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\bar{M}^{k+1}-\nabla f(\bar{X}^{k+1})=\beta\bar{M}^{k}+(1-\beta)\bar{G}^{k}-\nabla f(\bar{X}^{k+1})

\displaystyle=\beta(\bar{M}^{k}-\nabla f(\bar{X}^{k}))+\bigl(\nabla f(\bar{X}^{k})-\nabla f(\bar{X}^{k+1})\bigr)+(1-\beta)\bigl(\bar{G}^{k}-\nabla f(\bar{X}^{k})\bigr)

\displaystyle=\beta E_{\mathrm{gt}}^{k}+\underbrace{(1-\beta)\bar{\Delta}^{k}}_{\text{noise}}+\underbrace{(1-\beta)(\bar{g}^{k}-\nabla f(\bar{X}^{k}))}_{\text{consensus bias}}+\underbrace{\bigl(\nabla f(\bar{X}^{k})-\nabla f(\bar{X}^{k+1})\bigr)}_{\text{drift}}.

(73)

Step 1: decompose
E_{\mathrm{gt}}^{k}=A^{k}+B^{k}
.

Define
A^{0}:=0
and
B^{0}:=E_{\mathrm{gt}}^{0}
, and for
k\geq 0
define

A^{k+1}:=\beta A^{k}+(1-\beta)\bigl(\bar{\Delta}^{k}+(\bar{g}^{k}-\nabla f(\bar{X}^{k}))\bigr),\qquad B^{k+1}:=\beta B^{k}+\bigl(\nabla f(\bar{X}^{k})-\nabla f(\bar{X}^{k+1})\bigr).

Then (
73
) implies
E_{\mathrm{gt}}^{k}=A^{k}+B^{k}
for all

.

Step 2: bound the stochastic part (noise).

Unrolling the
A
-recursion and keeping only the noise contribution yields

A_{\rm noise}^{k+1}=(1-\beta)\sum_{t=0}^{k}\beta^{k-t}\bar{\Delta}^{t}.

By Assumption
3
, conditional on
\mathcal{F}^{t}
the noises are independent across agents and satisfy

\mathbb{E}[\bar{\Delta}^{t}\mid\mathcal{F}^{t}]=0
and

\mathbb{E}[\|\bar{\Delta}^{t}\|_{F}^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}^{t}]\leq\sigma^{2}/N
.
Therefore cross terms vanish, and

\mathbb{E}\|A_{\rm noise}^{k+1}\|_{F}\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\|A_{\rm noise}^{k+1}\|_{F}^{2}}\leq(1-\beta)\sqrt{\sum_{t=0}^{k}\beta^{2(k-t)}\cdot\frac{\sigma^{2}}{N}}\leq(1-\beta)\sqrt{\frac{1}{1-\beta^{2}}}\cdot\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{N}}=\sqrt{\tfrac{1-\beta}{1+\beta}}\,\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{N}}.

Step 3: bound the drift part.

Unrolling
B^{k+1}=\beta B^{k}+d^{k}
with
d^{k}:=\nabla f(\bar{X}^{k})-\nabla f(\bar{X}^{k+1})
gives

B^{k+1}=\beta^{k+1}B^{0}+\sum_{t=0}^{k}\beta^{k-t}d^{t}.

Thus, by triangle inequality,

\mathbb{E}\|B^{k+1}\|_{F}\leq\beta^{k+1}\,\mathbb{E}\|B^{0}\|_{F}+\sum_{t=0}^{k}\beta^{k-t}\,\mathbb{E}\|d^{t}\|_{F}.

Now average over
k=0,\dots,K-1
and exchange the order of summation:

\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\sum_{t=0}^{k}\beta^{k-t}\,\mathbb{E}\|d^{t}\|_{F}=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{t=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}\|d^{t}\|_{F}\sum_{k=t}^{K-1}\beta^{k-t}\leq\frac{1}{1-\beta}\cdot\frac{1}{K}\sum_{t=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}\|d^{t}\|_{F}.

Therefore,

\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}\|B^{k+1}\|_{F}\leq\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\beta^{k+1}\,\mathbb{E}\|B^{0}\|_{F}+\frac{1}{1-\beta}\cdot\frac{1}{K}\sum_{t=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}\|d^{t}\|_{F}.

Under Assumption
2
,

\|d^{t}\|_{F}\leq\|d^{t}\|_{*}\leq L_{*}\|\bar{X}^{t+1}-\bar{X}^{t}\|
, and
\|\cdot\|\leq\|\cdot\|_{F}
.
This yields the drift term in (
70
).

Step 4: bound the consensus bias.

The consensus-bias contribution in
A^{k+1}
is

A_{\rm bias}^{k+1}=(1-\beta)\sum_{t=0}^{k}\beta^{k-t}\bigl(\bar{g}^{t}-\nabla f(\bar{X}^{t})\bigr).

By triangle inequality,

\mathbb{E}\|A_{\rm bias}^{k+1}\|_{F}\leq(1-\beta)\sum_{t=0}^{k}\beta^{k-t}\,\mathbb{E}\|\bar{g}^{t}-\nabla f(\bar{X}^{t})\|_{F}.

Now average over
k=0,\dots,K-1
and exchange summations:

\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}\|A_{\rm bias}^{k+1}\|_{F}\leq\frac{1}{K}\sum_{t=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}\|\bar{g}^{t}-\nabla f(\bar{X}^{t})\|_{F}\cdot(1-\beta)\sum_{k=t}^{K-1}\beta^{k-t}\leq\frac{1}{K}\sum_{t=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}\|\bar{g}^{t}-\nabla f(\bar{X}^{t})\|_{F},

where we used
(1-\beta)\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\beta^{s}=1
.
This yields the bias term in (
70
).

Combining Steps 2–4 and using
E_{\mathrm{gt}}^{k+1}=A^{k+1}+B^{k+1}
proves (
70
).
Finally, (
71
) follows from the same argument as Lemma
9
:

\|\bar{g}^{k}-\nabla f(\bar{X}^{k})\|_{F}=\Big\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bigl(\nabla f_{i}(X_{i}^{k})-\nabla f_{i}(\bar{X}^{k})\bigr)\Big\|_{F}\leq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}L_{*}\|X_{i}^{k}-\bar{X}^{k}\|_{F}\leq\frac{L_{*}}{\sqrt{N}}\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{k}\|_{F}.

∎

Lemma 17

(Gradient-tracking disagreement bound (no heterogeneity term))
.

(1),
2
, and

Proof.

Step 1: unroll the GT disagreement recursion.

(I-J)

(78)

Unrolling (
78

k=0,\dots,K-1

\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}\,\|\tilde{\mathbf{E}}^{k}\|_{F}\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}\,\|\tilde{\mathbf{E}}^{0}\|_{F}}{K(1-\beta)}+\frac{1}{K(1-\beta)}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}\,\|\widetilde{\nabla\mathbf{f}}(\mathbf{X}^{k+1})-\widetilde{\nabla\mathbf{f}}(\mathbf{X}^{k})\|_{F}+\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}\,\|\tilde{\mathbf{\Delta}}^{k}\|_{F},

.

\|\widetilde{\nabla\mathbf{f}}(\mathbf{X}^{k+1})-\widetilde{\nabla\mathbf{f}}(\mathbf{X}^{k})\|_{F}\leq L_{*}\,\|\mathbf{X}^{k+1}-\mathbf{X}^{k}\|_{F}.

\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}\,\|\tilde{\mathbf{E}}^{k}\|_{F}\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}\,\|\tilde{\mathbf{E}}^{0}\|_{F}}{K(1-\beta)}+\frac{L_{*}}{1-\beta}\cdot\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}\,\|\mathbf{X}^{k+1}-\mathbf{X}^{k}\|_{F}+\sigma\sqrt{N}.

\tilde{\mathbf{M}}^{k+1}-\tilde{\mathbf{M}}^{k}=(1-\beta)\big(\tilde{\mathbf{G}}^{k}-\tilde{\mathbf{M}}^{k}\big)=(1-\beta)\big(\tilde{\mathbf{\Delta}}^{k}+\tilde{\mathbf{E}}^{k}\big)

\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}\,\|\tilde{\mathbf{M}}^{k+1}-\tilde{\mathbf{M}}^{k}\|_{F}\leq(1-\beta)\cdot\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}\,\|\tilde{\mathbf{\Delta}}^{k}\|_{F}+(1-\beta)\cdot\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}\,\|\tilde{\mathbf{E}}^{k}\|_{F}

\leq(1-\beta)\sigma\sqrt{N}+\frac{\mathbb{E}\,\|\tilde{\mathbf{E}}^{0}\|_{F}}{K}+L_{*}\cdot\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}\,\|\mathbf{X}^{k+1}-\mathbf{X}^{k}\|_{F}+(1-\beta)\sigma\sqrt{N}.

It remains to bound the average primal increment.
Write

with
\bar{\mathbf{X}}^{k}:=J\mathbf{X}^{k}
.
Then

Since
\bar{\mathbf{X}}^{k}
is a stacked consensus variable with all blocks equal to
\bar{X}^{k}
, we have

\|\bar{\mathbf{X}}^{k+1}-\bar{\mathbf{X}}^{k}\|_{F}=\sqrt{N}\,\|\bar{X}^{k+1}-\bar{X}^{k}\|_{F}
.
By Lemma
2
,
\|\bar{X}^{k+1}-\bar{X}^{k}\|_{F}\leq\alpha\sqrt{r_{\max}}
.

\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{k+1}-\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{k}\|_{F}\leq\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{k+1}\|_{F}+\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{k}\|_{F}
.
Averaging over
k=0,\dots,K-1
yields

\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}\,\|\mathbf{X}^{k+1}-\mathbf{X}^{k}\|_{F}\leq\alpha\sqrt{Nr_{\max}}+\frac{2}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}\,\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{k}\|_{F}+\frac{1}{K}\,\mathbb{E}\,\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{K}\|_{F}.

Substituting into the previous display gives (
75
).

Step 3: plug into the GT bound and (optionally) use Lemma
7
.

Combining Step 1 and Step 2 yields (
76
).
If Lemma
7
holds with Muon directions, then

\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{k}\|_{F}\leq v_{1}v_{2}\gamma^{k}(1+\|B^{2}\|)\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{0}\|_{F}+\frac{2\alpha v_{1}v_{2}\lambda_{a}}{1-\gamma}\sqrt{Nr_{\max}}

\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}\,\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{k}\|_{F}\leq\frac{v_{1}v_{2}(1+\|B^{2}\|)}{K(1-\gamma)}\,\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{0}\|_{F}+\frac{2\alpha v_{1}v_{2}\lambda_{a}}{1-\gamma}\,\sqrt{Nr_{\max}},

Substituting the GT disagreement bound (

Step 4: substitute

(Big-O form of the SUDA–Muon convergence rate)

With the master bound established, we now draw two structural consequences. We first instantiate it for concrete SUDA communication backbones in Subsection

4.4
Modular Axis: Backbone Choices

The convergence theory implies the first structural conclusion of the paper:
within our decomposition, the choice of decentralized communication backbone is
the modular axis.
To compare concrete backbone choices, it suffices to inspect how
Corollary
1
depends on the SUDA
contraction rate
\gamma
. For convenience, we recall the big-
\mathcal{O}

master bound:

\begin{split}\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\nabla f(\bar{X}^{k})\|_{*}\big]\;\leq\;C\Bigg(&\Bigl(1+\tfrac{\sigma}{\sqrt{N}}\Bigr)K^{-1/4}+\frac{K^{-3/4}}{(1-\gamma)(1-\lambda)}\\
&+\Bigl(1+\frac{\sigma}{1-\lambda}\Bigr)K^{-1/2}+\frac{K^{-1}}{\sqrt{N}\,(1-\gamma)(1-\lambda)}\\
&+\frac{K^{-7/4}}{(1-\gamma)(1-\lambda)}\Bigg),\end{split}

(85)

The dominant term is the same for every SUDA instantiation and is completely
independent of the graph. Different communication backbones appear only through
the factor
(1-\gamma)^{-1}
in the lower-order network corrections, where

\gamma
is the SUDA contraction rate.

For the SUDA instantiations of theoretical interest here, the substitution is simple:

\gamma=\lambda^{2}\quad\text{for ED/D${}^{2}$ and EXTRA},\qquad\gamma=\lambda\quad\text{for ATC-GT}.

Therefore

\frac{1}{(1-\gamma)(1-\lambda)}=\mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{1}{(1-\lambda)^{2}}\right)

in all three cases. At the big-
\mathcal{O}
level, ED/D
2
, EXTRA, and
ATC-GT therefore all scale as
\mathcal{O}((1-\lambda)^{-2})
in the

K^{-3/4}
,
K^{-1}
, and
K^{-7/4}
network-dependent corrections.

The difference lies in constants rather than order. ED/D
2
and EXTRA use

1-\gamma=(1-\lambda)(1+\lambda)
, whereas ATC-GT uses
1-\gamma=1-\lambda
.
When
\lambda
is close to one, ATC-GT therefore carries a larger prefactor
in the subdominant network terms, even though the asymptotic order is the
same. This is precisely the modularity provided by the SUDA formulation:
among convergent SUDA instantiations, the leading stochastic term is graph-free,
and the communication backbone changes only the transient network corrections
through the substitution for
\gamma
.

In short, the theory does not predict different dominant rates for ED/D
2
,
EXTRA, and ATC-GT. It predicts the same leading order and different transient
behavior. This completes the modular part of the structural decomposition. The
next two subsections turn to the complementary non-modular part: first an
internal boundary concerning the order between tracking and polarization, and
then an external boundary comparing decentralized and federated Muon.

4.5
Internal Boundary: Tracking Before Polarization

The previous subsection identified the communication backbone as the modular
axis of decentralized Muon design. We now turn to the first non-modular
boundary, which is internal to the decentralized architecture: whether Muon
polarizes a tracked quantity or a purely local one. A key design choice in SUDA–Muon is that the Muon direction is computed from a

network-aggregated / tracked
quantity
H_{i}^{k+1}
rather than directly from a
local stochastic gradient (or its EMA). The following proposition shows that if we
remove this pre-polarization tracking step while keeping the rest of the algorithm
unchanged, then one cannot, in general, guarantee
exact
convergence to
stationarity for heterogeneous objectives. For clarity we present this internal obstruction in
the deterministic/noiseless subcase
\sigma=0
; this is
not
an additional
assumption, since the stochastic-oracle model in Assumption
3

explicitly allows
\sigma=0
(deterministic gradients), and any convergence guarantee
under that model must hold in particular for this simplest admissible case. In this
setting, we construct smooth and lower-bounded problems on which the averaged iterate

\bar{X}^{k}
gets stuck at a
non-stationary
point.

Proposition 5

(A non-stationary fixed point for the no-tracking variant (general matrix case))
.

For notational simplicity, consider the deterministic/noiseless setting
\sigma=0
and
the general matrix case
m,n\geq 1
.
Define the
no-tracking variant
of SUDA–Muon by keeping
Algorithm
1
unchanged except that the gradient-tracking update is
removed and replaced by

H_{i}^{k+1}\leftarrow M_{i}^{k+1}\qquad\text{for all }i\in[N],\ k\geq 0.

(86)

Assume the communication/SUDA matrices satisfy Assumption
1
,
and initialize as in Algorithm
1
with
Y_{i}^{0}=0
and
M_{i}^{0}=G_{i}^{0}
.

Then there exist
N=2
and local objectives
f_{1},f_{2}:\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}\to\mathbb{R}

that are
L_{*}
-smooth in the sense of Assumption
2
and are lower
bounded, such that for the consensus initialization
X_{1}^{0}=X_{2}^{0}=X^{\dagger}
, the
averaged iterate satisfies

\bar{X}^{k}\equiv X^{\dagger}\quad\text{for all }k\geq 0,

while the global objective
f(X)\triangleq\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(f_{1}(X)+f_{2}(X)\bigr)
has

\nabla f(X^{\dagger})\neq 0.

Consequently,

\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\bigl\|\nabla f(\bar{X}^{k})\bigr\|_{*}\equiv\bigl\|\nabla f(X^{\dagger})\bigr\|_{*}>0,

and hence the no-tracking variant (
86
) cannot guarantee exact
stationarity convergence (vanishing averaged gradient nuclear norm at
\bar{X}^{k}
)
over the class of smooth heterogeneous objectives.

Proof.

We first note that the averaging argument in the proof of
Lemma
2
uses only the
\mathbf{X}
- and
\mathbf{Y}
-updates
(and the doubly-stochasticity/polynomial properties of
A,B,C
), and does not rely on
the specific recursion defining
\mathbf{H}^{k+1}
. Hence the same argument applies to
the no-tracking variant as well and yields

\bar{X}^{k+1}=\bar{X}^{k}-\alpha\,\bar{S}^{k+1},\qquad\bar{S}^{k+1}\triangleq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{2}S_{i}^{k+1},\quad S_{i}^{k+1}=\operatorname{msgn}(H_{i}^{k+1}).

(87)

Under the no-tracking rule (
86
), we have
H_{i}^{k+1}=M_{i}^{k+1}
.
It remains to exhibit a smooth lower-bounded instance for which

\bar{S}^{k+1}\equiv 0
while
\nabla f(\bar{X}^{0})\neq 0
.

Construction of the counterexample.

Choose
f_{1},f_{2}
as in Example
1
below and
initialize at consensus
\bar{X}^{0}=X^{\dagger}
.
For these functions, for every
X\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
, the gradients satisfy

\nabla f_{1}(X)=c_{1}(X)\,U,\qquad\nabla f_{2}(X)=c_{2}(X)\,U,

(88)

where
U\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
is a fixed rank-one matrix and the scalar
coefficients obey
c_{1}(X)>0
and
c_{2}(X)<0
.
In particular, writing
U=uv^{\top}
with
\|u\|_{2}=\|v\|_{2}=1
, for any scalar
\mu\neq 0
we have

\operatorname{msgn}(\mu U)=\mathrm{sign}(\mu)\,U
because a reduced SVD of
\mu U
is

\mu U=u(|\mu|)(\mathrm{sign}(\mu)v)^{\top}
.
Therefore,
\operatorname{msgn}(\nabla f_{1}(X))=U
and
\operatorname{msgn}(\nabla f_{2}(X))=-U
for all
X
.

Inductive cancellation argument.

In the noiseless setting,
G_{i}^{k}=\nabla f_{i}(X_{i}^{k})
.
Since
M_{i}^{0}=G_{i}^{0}
and
M_{i}^{k+1}=\beta M_{i}^{k}+(1-\beta)G_{i}^{k}
, the alignment
(
88
) implies inductively that
M_{1}^{k}=\mu_{1}^{k}U
and

M_{2}^{k}=\mu_{2}^{k}U
for scalars
\mu_{1}^{k}>0
and
\mu_{2}^{k}<0
for all
k
.
Therefore, under (
86
), we have

S_{1}^{k+1}=\operatorname{msgn}(M_{1}^{k+1})=U
and

S_{2}^{k+1}=\operatorname{msgn}(M_{2}^{k+1})=-U
, and hence

\bar{S}^{k+1}=(U-U)/2=0
for all
k\geq 0
.
Plugging
\bar{S}^{k+1}=0
into (
87
) yields

\bar{X}^{k+1}=\bar{X}^{k}
for all
k
, so
\bar{X}^{k}\equiv\bar{X}^{0}=X^{\dagger}
.
Finally, Example
1
satisfies

\nabla f(X^{\dagger})\neq 0
, so the averaged stationarity measure stays bounded away
from zero.
∎

Example 1

(A concrete smooth counterexample in the general matrix setting)
.

Let
N=2
.
Pick any unit vectors
u\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with

\|u\|_{2}=\|v\|_{2}=1
and define the rank-one matrix

U\triangleq uv^{\top}.

Let
a>b>0
be constants and define, for
X\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
,

f_{1}(X)\triangleq a\,\log\bigl(1+e^{\langle U,X\rangle}\bigr),\qquad f_{2}(X)\triangleq b\,\log\bigl(1+e^{-\langle U,X\rangle}\bigr),

where
\langle U,X\rangle\triangleq\mathrm{trace}(U^{\top}X)
.
Then
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are lower bounded (both are nonnegative) and differentiable with

\nabla f_{1}(X)=a\,\frac{1}{1+e^{-\langle U,X\rangle}}\,U,\qquad\nabla f_{2}(X)=-b\,\frac{1}{1+e^{\langle U,X\rangle}}\,U.

In particular,
\nabla f_{1}(X)
and
\nabla f_{2}(X)
are always aligned with
U
but have
opposite signs in their scalar coefficients; hence

\operatorname{msgn}(\nabla f_{1}(X))=uv^{\top}
and
\operatorname{msgn}(\nabla f_{2}(X))=-uv^{\top}

for all
X
.

Moreover, the gradients are Lipschitz in the sense of Assumption
2
.
Indeed, let
\varphi(t)\triangleq(1+e^{-t})^{-1}
so that
\varphi^{\prime}(t)=\varphi(t)(1-\varphi(t))\leq 1/4
.
Since
\|U\|_{*}=1
(rank-one with unit singular value) and

|\langle U,X-Y\rangle|\leq\|U\|_{*}\,\|X-Y\|=\|X-Y\|
, we have

\|\nabla f_{1}(X)-\nabla f_{1}(Y)\|_{*}=a\,|\varphi(\langle U,X\rangle)-\varphi(\langle U,Y\rangle)|\,\|U\|_{*}\leq\frac{a}{4}\,|\langle U,X-Y\rangle|\leq\frac{a}{4}\,\|X-Y\|,

and similarly
\|\nabla f_{2}(X)-\nabla f_{2}(Y)\|_{*}\leq\frac{b}{4}\,\|X-Y\|
.
Thus Assumption
2
holds with
L_{*}\triangleq\max\{a,b\}/4
.

Finally, with
X^{\dagger}\triangleq 0
, for the global objective

f\triangleq\tfrac{1}{2}(f_{1}+f_{2})
we have

\nabla f(X^{\dagger})=\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\nabla f_{1}(0)+\nabla f_{2}(0)\bigr)=\tfrac{1}{2}\Bigl(\frac{a}{2}-\frac{b}{2}\Bigr)U=\frac{a-b}{4}\,U\neq 0.

Therefore, the hypotheses and conclusion of Proposition
5

are satisfied in the general matrix setting.

This structural result underscores the role of the gradient-tracking step in
Algorithm
1
: because
\operatorname{msgn}
is nonlinear,
replacing a tracked quantity by a purely local one can break exact convergence
under heterogeneous objectives. The next section asks a complementary question:
even when averaging is exact and topology is no obstacle, does decentralized
Muon retain the familiar federated linear-speedup effect?

4.6
External Boundary: Federated vs. Decentralized Linear Speedup

The previous subsection identified the first non-modular boundary, internal to
decentralized Muon itself: tracking-before-polarization is necessary for this natural no-tracking variant. We now turn
to a second, external structural boundary. Even if topology is made maximally
favorable, the fully decentralized update need not inherit the usual
linear-speedup intuition from server-based Muon. The reason is again the
ordering of operations. In a federated or server-based architecture, gradients
can be averaged first and polarized afterward. In a fully decentralized
architecture, each node polarizes a local noisy gradient and only then
communicates. The following toy example isolates this architectural difference
in the simplest possible setting.

We consider the vector case as a special instance of our matrix formulation,
namely
X^{k}=(x_{1}^{k},x_{2}^{k})^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 1}
. Let

f(X)\triangleq\frac{1}{2}x_{1}^{2},

(89)

so that
\nabla f(X)=(x_{1},0)^{\top}
. At iteration
k
, node
i\in[N]
observes
the stochastic gradient

G_{i}^{k}=\nabla f(X^{k})+\begin{bmatrix}0\\
\xi_{i}^{k}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}x_{1}^{k}\\
\xi_{i}^{k}\end{bmatrix},\qquad\xi_{i}^{k}\in\{+\sigma,-\sigma\},

(90)

where
\{\xi_{i}^{k}\}
are i.i.d. across
i
and
k
with

\mathbb{E}[\xi_{i}^{k}]=0
and
\mathbb{E}[(\xi_{i}^{k})^{2}]=\sigma^{2}
. The noise is transverse to the
signal: it acts only in the second coordinate.

Since
X^{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 1}
, the matrix-sign operator reduces to
Euclidean normalization,

\operatorname{msgn}(V)=\frac{V}{\left\|V\right\|_{2}},\qquad\operatorname{msgn}(0)=0.

(91)

To give the decentralized method its best possible chance, we assume a complete
graph with exact averaging and synchronized iterates. The only distinction is
the order of averaging and polarization.

\textbf{DSGD-Muon:}\qquad X^{k+1}=X^{k}-\alpha\cdot\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\operatorname{msgn}(G_{i}^{k}),

(92)

\textbf{Centralized-Muon:}\qquad X^{k+1}=X^{k}-\alpha\cdot\operatorname{msgn}\!\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}G_{i}^{k}\right).

(93)

Proposition 6

(Federated Muon can speed up, decentralized Muon cannot)
.

Consider (
89
)–(
91
) with any
\sigma>0
and
stepsize
\alpha\in(0,\sigma)
.

1.

DSGD-Muon is insensitive to
N
.

The first coordinate of (
92
) obeys the deterministic
recursion

x_{1}^{k+1}=x_{1}^{k}\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{(x_{1}^{k})^{2}+\sigma^{2}}}\right).

(94)

Hence

|x_{1}^{k+1}|\geq\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{\sigma}\right)|x_{1}^{k}|,

and reaching
|x_{1}^{T}|\leq\varepsilon|x_{1}^{0}|
requires

T=\Omega\!\left(\frac{\sigma}{\alpha}\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right),

which does not improve with
N
.

2.

Centralized-Muon enjoys variance reduction.

Let
\bar{\xi}^{k}\triangleq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\xi_{i}^{k}
, so that

\mathbb{E}[\bar{\xi}^{k}]=0
and
\mathbb{E}[(\bar{\xi}^{k})^{2}]=\sigma^{2}/N
.
Then the federated update (
93
) is driven by noise at scale

\sigma/\sqrt{N}
.
In the noise-dominated regime
|x_{1}^{k}|\lesssim\sigma/\sqrt{N}
, the
corresponding iteration complexity scales as

T=O\!\left(\frac{\sigma}{\alpha\sqrt{N}}\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right),

which is the usual linear-speedup behavior.

Proof sketch.

For decentralized Muon, note that

\left\|G_{i}^{k}\right\|_{2}=\sqrt{(x_{1}^{k})^{2}+(\xi_{i}^{k})^{2}}=\sqrt{(x_{1}^{k})^{2}+\sigma^{2}}.

Therefore the first coordinate of the local Muon direction equals

x_{1}^{k}/\sqrt{(x_{1}^{k})^{2}+\sigma^{2}}
, which is independent of both the node index

i
and the sign of the noise. Averaging over
i
yields
(
94
), and the lower bound follows from

\sqrt{(x_{1}^{k})^{2}+\sigma^{2}}\geq\sigma
.

For federated Muon, averaging occurs before normalization, so the second
coordinate becomes
\bar{\xi}^{k}
with variance
\sigma^{2}/N
. The effective noise
level is therefore reduced from
\sigma
to
\sigma/\sqrt{N}
, which gives the
stated complexity in the noise-dominated regime.

∎

This counterexample is useful precisely because topology plays no role in it:
the graph is complete and averaging is exact. The failure of linear speedup
comes solely from local polarization before averaging. In that sense, the
example isolates the external structural boundary of the paper: fully
decentralized Muon is not merely a communication-degraded version of federated
Muon, because local-polarize-then-average is structurally different from
average-then-polarize.

1

\sigma=50
and
N\in\{1,8,64,256\}
. The dashed federated curves accelerate as

N
grows, while the solid decentralized curves nearly overlap across all
choices of
N
.

Taken together, these experiments mainly support the
modular
axis of
the paper’s story. When the Muon geometry is held fixed and only the backbone
or topology is varied, the differences are real but strongly regime dependent:
they are small in short-horizon or near-IID settings and much larger in the
long-horizon, strongly heterogeneous CIFAR-100 run. This is consistent with the
theoretical picture that backbone choices alter transient communication effects
and lower-order corrections more than the dominant stochastic term. The
two non-modular boundaries about tracking-before-polarization and
federated-versus-decentralized ordering should therefore be read as structural
limits established theoretically, with the experiments providing practical
context rather than direct empirical proofs.

6
Conclusion

This paper studies one specific obstruction in fully decentralized Muon: the
polarization step is nonlinear, so it does not interact with gossip averaging
in the same way as an ordinary gradient step. Our main contribution is not
merely a new decentralized Muon variant, but a structural decomposition of
decentralized Muon design. In this decomposition, Muon supplies the matrix
geometry, while a unified primal–dual backbone handles communication
correction. SUDA–Muon realizes this decomposition through a SUDA-style
template that lets several decentralized correction mechanisms be compared
within one architecture.

The main theoretical message is organized around one modular axis and two
non-modular boundaries. The modular axis is the communication backbone: the
dominant stochastic term remains topology-free, while the graph enters only
through faster-decaying corrections, so changing ED/D
2
, EXTRA, or ATC-GT
affects transient network corrections but not the leading stochastic order. The
first non-modular boundary is internal: tracking before polarization is not
optional in general. Without it, one can construct smooth heterogeneous
problems on which the averaged iterate stays at a non-stationary point.

A second non-modular boundary is external and appears when comparing federated and fully
decentralized Muon. In our linear-speedup counterexample, server-side averaging
before polarization recovers the usual
\sigma/\sqrt{N}
variance reduction,
while fully decentralized local polarization before averaging does not, even on
a complete graph with exact averaging. Thus fully decentralized Muon is not
merely a communication-degraded version of federated Muon; it has its own
algorithmic structure.

Our experiments on CIFAR-100 and GPT-2/Wikitext-2 primarily support the
modular axis of this picture. Short-horizon and near-IID runs make the decentralized Muon variants look similar, but in the
20
-node,
100
-epoch non-IID CIFAR-100 setting both SUDA-based variants outperform
DeMuon
, with
SUDA–Muon-ED
performing best. When the partition is near IID, the
different SUDA–Muon instances behave much more similarly and the best
topology can shift. This regime dependence is consistent with the view that
backbone choices mainly change transient communication behavior, whereas the
two non-modular statements of the paper are established by the counterexamples
rather than by benchmark ranking alone.

Natural next steps include communication-efficient variants with compression,
sharper rates under additional structure such as PL-type conditions, online
tuning of the stepsize and EMA parameters, and larger-scale experiments that
test whether the same topology effects persist for bigger models and graphs.
