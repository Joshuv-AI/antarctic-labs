Quadratic congruences for half-integral weight cusp forms with the eta multiplier
Robert Dicks

Abstract
. Let ℓ ≥ 5 \ell\geq 5 be a prime, and let ν η \nu_{\eta} denote the Dedekind eta multiplier. For an odd integer r r , and a real Dirichlet character ψ \psi , recent work of Ahlgren, Andersen, and the author showed that quadratic congruences modulo ℓ \ell hold for a wide range of half-integral weight cusp forms with multiplier ψ ​ ν η r \psi\nu_{\eta}^{r} , vastly generalizing certain congruences discovered by Atkin for the partition function. In this paper, we show that such congruences hold when ψ \psi is an arbitrary character. Our methods rely on the theory of modular Galois representations. For primes ℓ ≥ 5 \ell\geq 5 , the core of our work is the study of modular Galois representations modulo ℓ \ell attached to integer-weight eigenforms with arbitrary Nebentypus whose images are large in a precise sense. Our key new result is that, given a finite set of such representations and γ ∈ SL 2 ​ ( 𝔽 ℓ ) \gamma\in{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell}) , there exists σ ∈ Gal ⁡ ( ℚ ¯ / ℚ ⁡ ( ζ ℓ ) ) \sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell})) whose images under the representations are in the conjugacy class of γ 2 \gamma^{2} .



1.
Introduction

For a positive integer
n
, the partition function
p(n)
counts the number of ways of writing
n
as a sum of a nonincreasing sequence of positive integers (where we agree by convention that
p(0)=1
and
p(n)=0
whenever
n\not\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
). The study of the arithmetic properties of
p(n)
has been deeply influential for the arithmetic of modular forms.
For example, the Ramanujan congruences
[
12
]

(1.1)

p\left(\frac{\ell n+1}{24}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell},\qquad\ell=5,7,11,

anticipated arithmetic phenomena which occur for the coefficients of many weakly holomorphic modular forms.

After the work of Ramanujan, many other congruences for
p(n)
were found, the most relevant of which for the present paper are Atkin’s congruences, which we now describe.
For several primes
\ell\leq 31
, Atkin
[
4
, eq.
(52)
]
found examples of congruences of the form

(1.2)

p\left(\frac{\ell Q^{2}n+\beta}{24}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}\quad\text{ if }\ \ \left(\frac{n}{Q}\right)=\varepsilon_{Q},

where
Q
is a prime different from
\ell
,
\beta
is an integer, and
\varepsilon_{Q}\in\{\pm 1\}
.
Recent work of Ahlgren, Allen, and Tang
[
1
]
used modular Galois representations to show that, for every prime
\ell\geq 5
, there exists a positive density set of primes
S
such that congruences of the form (
1.2
) hold for
Q\in S
.

The techniques of Ahlgren, Allen, and Tang are Galois theoretic and use work of Yang
[
15
]
on the usual Shimura correspondence for half-integral weight cusp forms on
{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. More precisely, let
f(z)
be such a form which transforms with multiplier
\nu^{r}_{\eta}
, where
\nu_{\eta}
is the multiplier for the Dedekind eta function (see (
2.1
) below), and

is an integer with
(r,6)=1
. Then
f(24z)
is a half-integral weight form in the sense of Shimura. Yang obtained precise information on the images of the Shimura lifts applied to
f(24z)
(he proves a similar result when
(r,6)=3
).

For many values
N\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
,
Ahlgren, Andersen, and the author
[
2
]
developed a precise Shimura correspondence for cusp forms of half-integral weight with eta multiplier of level
N
.
The arithmetic consequences of that work generalize congruences like (
1.2
) to a wide class of forms in
S_{\lambda+\frac{1}{2}}(N,\psi\nu_{\eta}^{r})
(see Section
2
for details on modular forms and related notation and terminology).
Here,
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
,
r
is an odd integer and
\psi
is a real Dirichlet character modulo
N
.
In this paper, we prove similar results when
\psi
is an
arbitrary
Dirichlet character modulo
N
.

The main new input in our work is a result which roughly speaking shows that one can find

for which the images of

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
\psi
be a Dirichlet character modulo
N
. Assume that
f_{1},...,f_{s}\in S_{k}(N,\psi)
are normalized eigenforms such that for each

,
\overline{\rho}_{f_{i}}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
contains a conjugate of
{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
.
Then for every
\gamma\in{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
, there exists
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell}))
such that, if

, then
\overline{\rho}_{f_{i}}(\sigma^{2})
is conjugate to
\gamma^{2}
.

Ahlgren, Allen, and Tang proved a more precise version of Proposition
1.3
when
\psi
is trivial (see
[
1
, Proposition
3.8
]
). At crucial steps in their argument, they are confronted with the problem of identifying several Galois characters modulo
\ell
. By virtue of working in spaces with trivial Dirichlet character, the Galois characters are easy to identify: they are either trivial or
\omega_{\ell}^{(\ell-1)/2}
, where
\omega_{\ell}
is the mod
\ell
cyclotomic character.

The main obstruction to generalizing
[
1
, Proposition
3.8
]
is that the corresponding Galois characters which come up in the analysis are difficult to identify. The crucial insight which allows us to prove Proposition
3.5
is that we can prove the result “up to a constant factor” without needing to know which Galois characters arise. We are then able to leverage the theory of modular Galois representations to show that the constant factors which occur are extremely well-behaved. In fact,
[
1
, Proposition 3.8]
is true “up to sign” in our context, which allows us to recover the result for matrices
\gamma^{2}
, where
\gamma\in{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
. This is sufficient for our arithmetic applications.

Most of our work is devoted to building tools to prove the main technical result needed for proving Theorem

In Section

to translate our congruences for newforms into congruences for half-integral weight forms.

of elements in
\text{Frob}_{p}
.
For representations
\rho:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow{\rm GL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})
which are unramified at
p
(which is to say that
I_{p}\subseteq\ker{\rho}
), the image of any
\sigma\in G_{p}
under
\rho
only depends on
\sigma I_{p}\in G_{p}/I_{p}
; for any such representation, we denote by
\rho(\text{Frob}_{p})
the image of any element in
\text{Frob}_{p}
under
\rho
.

We denote by
\chi_{\ell}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{\times}_{\ell}
and
\omega_{\ell}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}^{\times}_{\ell}
the
\ell
-adic and mod
\ell
cyclotomic characters, respectively.
We let
\omega_{2},\omega^{\prime}_{2}:I_{\ell}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}^{\times}_{\ell^{2}}
denote Serre’s fundamental characters of level
2
(see
[
13
,
\mathsection
2.1
]
).
Both characters have order
\ell^{2}-1
, and we have

\omega^{\ell+1}_{2}=\omega^{\prime\ell+1}_{2}=\omega_{\ell}
. Finally, we mention that if
\psi
is a Dirichlet character modulo
N
, we may regard it as a Galois character
\psi:\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}_{\ell}
in the standard way (see e.g.
[
6
, Chapter
9

\mathsection

3
]
).

The following theorem is due to Deligne, Fontaine, Langlands, Ribet, and Shimura (see
[
1
, Theorem 2.1]
).

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose that
\ell\geq 5
is prime and that
\psi
is a Dirichlet character modulo
N
with conductor
C_{\psi}
.
Let
f=\sum a(n)q^{n}\in S_{k}(N,\psi)
be a normalized eigenform. There is a continuous irreducible representation
\rho_{f}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow{\rm GL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})
with semisimple mod
\ell
reduction
\overline{\rho}_{f}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow{\rm GL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})
satisfying the following properties.

(1)

If
p\nmid\ell N
, then
\rho_{f}
is unramified at
p
and the characteristic polynomial of
\rho_{f}(\operatorname{Frob}_{p})
is
X^{2}-\iota_{\ell}(a(p))X+\psi(p)p^{k-1}
. This uniquely characterizes
\rho_{f}
.

(2)

If
f\in S^{\operatorname{new}Q}_{k}(N)
, where
Q
is a prime with
Q\mid\mid N
then we have

(2.12)

\rho_{f}|_{G_{Q}}\cong\left(\begin{matrix}\chi_{\ell}\psi_{Q}&*\\
0&\psi_{Q}\end{matrix}\right),

where
\psi_{Q}:G_{Q}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}_{\ell}
is the unramified character with
\psi_{Q}({\operatorname{Frob}}_{Q})=\iota_{\ell}(a(Q))
. If we also have
Q\nmid C_{\psi}
, then
\rho_{f}|_{I_{Q}}
is unipotent.
If
Q\mid C_{\psi}
, we have

(2.13)

\rho_{f}|_{I_{Q}}\cong\left(\begin{matrix}\psi&*\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right),

where we have regarded
\psi
as a Galois character.

(3)

Assume that
2\leq k\leq\ell+1
.

•

If
\iota_{\ell}(a(\ell))\in\overline{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}^{\times}
, then
\rho_{f}|_{G_{\ell}}
is reducible and we have

(2.14)

\rho_{f}|_{I_{\ell}}\cong\left(\begin{matrix}\chi_{\ell}^{k-1}&*\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right).

•

If
\iota_{\ell}(a(\ell))\not\in\overline{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}^{\times}
, then
\overline{\rho}_{f}|_{G_{\ell}}
is irreducible and
\overline{\rho}_{f}|_{I_{\ell}}\cong\omega^{k-1}_{2}\oplus\omega^{\prime(k-1)}_{2}
.

Remark
.

The Galois representations depend on the choice of embedding
\iota_{\ell}:\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
, but we have suppressed this from the notation.

We now introduce notation concerning group representations (see also
[
1
,
\mathsection

3
]
).
For
\tau\in\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}/\mathbb{F}_{\ell})

and a homomorphism
\overline{\rho}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow{\rm GL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})
, we write
{}^{\tau}\overline{\rho}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow{\rm GL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})
for the composite of
\overline{\rho}
with the automorphism of
{\rm GL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})
induced by
\tau
.
We make a similar definition for a homomorphism
r:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\text{PGL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})
.
For two homomorphisms
r_{1},r_{2}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\text{PGL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})
,
we write
r_{1}\cong r_{2}

if they are conjugate by an element of
\text{PGL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})
.
When matrices
A,B\in{\rm GL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})
are conjugate by an element of
{\rm GL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})
,
we write
A\sim B
.
For a normalized eigenform
f\in S_{k}(N,\psi)
, we let
r_{f}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\text{PGL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})
be the composite of
\overline{\rho}_{f}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow{\rm GL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})
with the natural projection
{\rm GL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})\rightarrow\text{PGL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})
.

Lastly, for a Galois field extension
L/K
and a subgroup
H\subseteq\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)
, we denote by
L^{H}
the subfield of
L
which is fixed by
H
.

3.
Results on Modular Galois representations

3.1.
Preliminary results

Throughout this section,
\ell\geq 5
is prime,
k
is an even, positive integer and
N
is a squarefree, positive integer.
We begin by recording two useful consequences of Theorem
2.1
which are useful for proving Proposition
~3.3
, which gives sufficient conditions for the condition of suitability mentioned in the introduction.

The following result, an analogue of
[
1
, Lemma
3.1
]
, gives precise information on how mod
\ell
modular Galois representations which are isomorphic up to a twist by a mod
\ell
Galois character are related. When the weight is low enough, one can constrain the weights of the normalized eigenforms under our consideration.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\psi
be a Dirichlet character modulo
N
which we regard as a Galois character.
Let
f,g\in S_{k}(N,\psi)
be normalized eigenforms such that
f=\sum a(n)q^{n}
and
\overline{\rho}_{g}\cong\overline{\rho}_{f}\otimes\varphi
, where
\varphi:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}_{\ell}
is a nontrivial continuous character.
Suppose further that
\ell\nmid\phi(N)
and that
\psi(\sigma)\neq-1
for all
\sigma\in G_{\mathbb{Q}}
. Then

(1)

\varphi=\omega_{\ell}^{\frac{\ell-1}{2}}
.

(2)

If
k\leq\ell+1
, then the following statements are true.

•

If
\iota_{\ell}(a(\ell))\in\overline{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}^{\times}
, then
k=\frac{\ell+1}{2}.

•

If
\iota_{\ell}(a(\ell))\not\in\overline{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}^{\times}
, then
k=\frac{\ell+3}{2}.

Proof.

A standard argument (see e.g. the discussion before
[
6
, Definition
9.6.10
]
) shows that the character
\varphi
has finite image with order coprime to
\ell
. If
\varphi
is unramified, it must also factor through
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell^{\infty}})/\mathbb{Q})
, where
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell^{\infty}})
is the
\ell
-adic cyclotomic extension.
These two observations show that
\varphi
factors through
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell})/\mathbb{Q})
.
From part
(1)
of Theorem
2.1
, we also have

\det\overline{\rho}_{f}=\omega^{k-1}_{\ell}=\det\overline{\rho}_{g}=\varphi^{2}\det\overline{\rho}_{f},

which means that
\varphi
is quadratic.

Thus, to prove that
\varphi=\omega^{\frac{\ell-1}{2}}_{\ell}
,
we only need to show that
\varphi
is unramified outside of
\ell
; this will show that
\varphi=\omega^{i}_{\ell}
where
1\leq i\leq\ell-2
, and we must then have
i=\frac{\ell-1}{2}
since
\varphi
is quadratic.
To this end, let
p\neq\ell
be prime.
When
Q\nmid C_{\psi}
, parts
(1)
and
(2)
of Theorem
2.1
show that

(3.1)

\overline{\rho}_{f}|_{I_{p}}\cong\left(\begin{matrix}1&*\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right).

The same conclusion holds for
\overline{\rho}_{g}|_{I_{p}}
, and it then follows from
\overline{\rho}_{g}\cong\overline{\rho}_{f}\otimes\varphi
that
\varphi
is unramified in this case.

Assume that
Q\mid C_{\psi}
and let
\sigma\in I_{p}
. Since we have
\overline{\rho}_{g}\cong\overline{\rho}_{f}\otimes\varphi

it follows from part
(2)
of
Theorem
2.1

that

\operatorname{tr}(\overline{\rho}_{g}(\sigma))=\operatorname{tr}(\overline{\rho}_{f}(\sigma))
and
\operatorname{tr}(\overline{\rho}_{g}(\sigma))=\varphi(\sigma)\operatorname{tr}(\overline{\rho}_{f}(\sigma))
. Since we have
\psi(\sigma)\neq-1
, part
(2)
of Theorem
2.1
also shows that
\varphi(\sigma)\neq-1
; the fact that
\varphi
is quadratic further implies that
\varphi(\sigma)=1
. This completes the proof that
\varphi
is unramified.

The proof of part
(2)
follows as in the proof of
[
1
, Lemma
3.1
]
by using part
(3)
of Theorem
2.1
in a similar manner.
∎

The next lemma is an analogue of
[
1
, Lemma
3.2
]
. Since the proof follows as in their work using Lemma
3.1
in a similar fashion, we omit the details.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\psi
be a Dirichlet character modulo
N
which we regard as a Galois character.
Let
f=\sum a(n)q^{n}\in S_{k}(N,\psi)
be a normalized eigenform. Suppose that
\ell\nmid\phi(N)
and that
\psi(\sigma)\neq-1
for all
\sigma\in G_{\mathbb{Q}}
. Assume that
2\leq k\leq\ell+1
and that there is a prime
Q
such that
Q\mid N
,
Q^{k-1}\not\equiv Q^{\pm 1}\pmod{\ell}
, and
f\in S_{k}^{\operatorname{new}Q}(N)
. Then the following statements hold.

(1)

\overline{\rho}_{f}
is irreducible.

(2)

Assume that there is a quadratic extension
K/\mathbb{Q}
such that
\overline{\rho}_{f}\big|_{G_{K}}
is reducible.
Then
\overline{\rho}_{f}\cong\overline{\rho}_{f}\otimes\omega_{\ell}^{\frac{\ell-1}{2}}
and

•

If
\iota_{\ell}(a(\ell))\in\overline{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}_{\ell}
, then
k=\frac{\ell+1}{2}
.

•

If
\iota_{\ell}(a(\ell))\not\in\overline{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}_{\ell}
, then
k=\frac{\ell+3}{2}
.

3.2.
Sufficient conditions for Suitability

Here we show that suitability holds for many spaces of forms. (see also
[
1
, Proposition
3.3
]
and
[
2
, Proposition
7.2
]
).

Proposition 3.3
.

Suppose that
r
is an odd integer.
Suppose that
N
is a squarefree, odd, positive integer with
\ell\nmid N
. Assume that
3\nmid N
if
3\nmid r
.
Let
\psi
be a Dirichlet character modulo
N
. Let
k
be an even, positive integer.
Then
(k,\ell)
is suitable for every triple
(N,\psi,r)
if the following conditions hold.

(1)

\psi(\sigma)\neq-1
for all
\sigma\in G_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

(2)

\ell\nmid\phi(N)
.

(3)

k\leq\ell+1
.

(4)

2^{k-1}\not\equiv 2^{\pm 1}\pmod{\ell}
.

(5)

k\neq\frac{\ell+1}{2},\frac{\ell+3}{2}
.

(6)

\frac{\ell+1}{(\ell+1,k-1)},\frac{\ell-1}{(\ell-1,k-1)}\geq 6
.

When
\ell>5k-4
, we always have conditions
(3)
,
(5)
and
(6)
.

Proof.

The final assertion is easy to check.
We also assume that
(r,6)=1
; the proof is similar when
(r,6)=3
. Suppose
that
f=\sum a(n)q^{n}\in S^{\new 2,3}_{k}(6N,\psi^{2},\varepsilon_{2,r,\psi},\varepsilon_{3,r,\psi})
is a newform.
From
[
5
, Theorem
2.47(b)
]
, we see that there are four possibilities for the image of
\overline{\rho}_{f}
:

(1)

\overline{\rho}_{f}
is reducible.

(2)

\overline{\rho}_{f}
is dihedral, i.e.
\overline{\rho}_{f}
is irreducible but
\overline{\rho}_{f}\big|_{G_{K}}
is reducible for some quadratic
K/\mathbb{Q}
.

(3)

\overline{\rho}_{f}
is exceptional, i.e. the projective image of
\overline{\rho}_{f}
is conjugate to one of
A_{4}
,
S_{4}
, or
A_{5}
.

(4)

The image of
\overline{\rho}_{f}
contains a conjugate of
{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
.

Our strategy is to rule out the first
3
cases.
By
conditions
(1)
,
(2)
, and
(4)
together with part
(1)
of Lemma
3.2
, we see that
\overline{\rho}_{f}
is irreducible.
By condition
(5)
and part
(2)
of Lemma
3.2
, we conclude that
\overline{\rho}_{f}
is not dihedral.

To rule out the exceptional case, it suffices to show that the projective image contains an element of order
\geq 6
.
Suppose that
\iota_{\ell}(a(\ell))\in\overline{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}_{\ell}
.
By part
(3)
of Theorem
2.1
, we know that

(3.2)

\rho_{f}\big|_{I_{\ell}}\cong\left(\begin{matrix}\chi_{\ell}^{k-1}&*\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right).

Since
\omega_{\ell}
has order
\ell-1
, the projective image of
\overline{\rho}_{f}
contains an element of order
\geq\frac{\ell-1}{(\ell-1,k-1)}\geq 6
.
If
\iota_{\ell}(a(\ell))\not\in\overline{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}_{\ell}
, then part
(3)
of Theorem
2.1
implies that

(3.3)

\overline{\rho}_{f}\cong\left(\begin{matrix}\omega^{k-1}_{2}&0\\
0&\omega^{\prime k-1}_{2}\end{matrix}\right).

Since
\omega_{2}/\omega^{\prime}_{2}
has order
\ell+1
, we conclude by condition
(6)
that the projective image of
\overline{\rho}_{f}
contains an element of order
\frac{\ell+1}{(\ell+1,k-1)}\geq 6
.
This completes the proof.
∎

3.3.
Crucial ingredients for proving Theorem
1.1

. Here we collect the results necessary for proving the main technical result which is essential for the proof of Theorem
1.1
. The most important result is Proposition
3.5
, which hinges on the next result, which roughly states that normalized eigenforms with sufficiently disjoint projective modulo
\ell
Galois representations cut out similarly disjoint field extensions; it also generalizes
[
1
, Lemma
3.7
]
to spaces of forms with arbitrary character.

Lemma 3.4
.

Assume that
N
is a positive integer and that
\psi
is a Dirichlet character modulo
N
with conductor
C_{\psi}
.
Let
f_{1},...,f_{s}\in S_{k}(N,\psi)
be normalized eigenforms. Assume for each
i\in\{1,...,s\}
that
\overline{\rho}_{f_{i}}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
contains a conjugate of
{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
, and let
M_{i}=\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{\operatorname{ker}(\overline{\rho}_{f_{i}})}
. Then the following statements are true.

(1)

For each
i\in\{1,...,s\}
, there exists an extension
K_{i}/\mathbb{Q}
of degree at most
2
in
M_{i}
and a finite extension
\mathbb{F}_{i}/\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
such that
\overline{\rho}_{f_{i}}(G_{K_{i}(\zeta_{C_{\psi}\ell})})
is conjugate to
{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{i})
.

(2)

Further assume for each
\tau\in\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}/\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
that
r_{f_{i}}\not\cong{}^{\tau}r_{f_{j}}
for each
i,j\in\{1,...,s\}
with
i\neq j
. Let
K_{i}
and
\mathbb{F}_{i}
be defined as in part (1), and set
M:=M_{1}...M_{s}
and
K:=K_{1}...K_{s}
. Then

\operatorname{Gal}(M(\zeta_{C_{\psi}\ell})/K(\zeta_{C_{\psi}\ell}))\cong\prod^{s}_{i=1}{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{i})
.

Proof.

For each
i\in\{1,\dots,s\}
, since
\overline{\rho}_{f_{i}}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
contains a conjugate of
{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
, we see from
[
5
, Theorem
2.47(b)
]
that there exists a finite extension
\mathbb{F}_{i}/\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
such that
r_{f_{i}}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
is conjugate to either
\text{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{i})
or
\text{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{i})
.
Upon replacing
\overline{\rho}_{f_{i}}
by a conjugate, we assume that
r_{f_{i}}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})=\text{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{i})
or
\text{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{i})
. Now define
K_{i}/\mathbb{Q}
to be the extension of degree at most
2
in
M_{i}
such that
r_{f_{i}}(G_{K_{i}})=\text{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{i})
. Note that
\text{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{i})
is a simple group since
\ell\geq 5
. Since
K_{i}(\zeta_{C_{\psi}\ell})/K_{i}
is an abelian extension, we see that
r_{f_{i}}(G_{K_{i}(\zeta_{C_{\psi}\ell})})={\rm PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{i})
. This fact and part
(1)
of Theorem
2.1
imply that
\overline{\rho}_{f_{i}}(G_{K_{i}(\zeta_{C_{\psi}\ell})})={\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{i})
.

The proof of part
(2)
is very similar to the proof of part
(2)
of
[
1
, Lemma 3.7]
, so we sketch the details. Let
L_{i}:=M_{i}^{\text{ker}(r_{f_{i}})}
. As in the proof of part
(2)
of
[
1
, Lemma
3.7
]
, the following three claims hold.

(a)

\operatorname{Gal}(L_{i}K(\zeta_{C_{\psi}\ell})/K(\zeta_{C_{\psi}\ell}))\cong\text{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{i})
.

(b)

\operatorname{Gal}(M_{i}K(\zeta_{C_{\psi}\ell})/K(\zeta_{C_{\psi}\ell}))\cong\text{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{i})
.

(c)

For
i,j\in\{1,\dots,s\}
with
i\neq j
, we have
L_{i}K(\zeta_{C_{\psi}\ell})\cap L_{j}K(\zeta_{C_{\psi}\ell})=K(\zeta_{C_{\psi}\ell})
.

The result follows by applying the above three claims and
[
1
, Lemma
3.6
]
as in the proof of
[
1
, Lemma
3.7
]
. More specifically, these tools allow us to prove by induction on
j\in\{1,\dots,s\}
that

\operatorname{Gal}(M_{1}\cdots M_{j}K(\zeta_{C_{\psi}\ell})/K(\zeta_{C_{\psi}\ell}))\cong\prod^{j}_{i=1}\operatorname{Gal}(M_{i}K(\zeta_{C_{\psi}\ell})/K(\zeta_{C_{\psi}\ell}))\cong\prod^{j}_{i=1}{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{i}).

∎

The next result, a generalization of
[
1
, Proposition
3.8
]
, is the crucial new ingredient which allows us to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Suppose that
N\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and that
\psi
is a Dirichlet character modulo
N
.
Let
f_{1},...,f_{s}\in S_{k}(N,\psi)
be normalized eigenforms such that for each
i\in\{1,\dots,s\}
,
\overline{\rho}_{f_{i}}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
contains a conjugate of
{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
.
Then for every
\gamma\in{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
, there exists
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell}))
such that for all
i\in\{1,...,s\}
, we have either
\overline{\rho}_{f_{i}}(\sigma)\thicksim\gamma
or
\overline{\rho}_{f_{i}}(\sigma)\thicksim-\gamma
. In particular, we have
\overline{\rho}_{f_{i}}(\sigma^{2})\thicksim\gamma^{2}
.

Proof.

Let
S=\{f_{1},\dots,f_{s}\}
.
For
f,g\in S
, we write
f\simeq g
if there exists
\tau\in\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}/\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
such that
r_{f_{i}}\cong{}^{\tau}r_{f_{j}}
.
Let
S^{\prime}=\{g_{1},\dots,g_{t}\}
be a complete set of representatives for
\simeq
.
It then follows from Lemma
3.4
that there exists
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{C_{\psi}\ell}))
such that
\overline{\rho}_{g}(\sigma)\thicksim\gamma
for all
g\in S^{\prime}
.

Now let
f\in S
.
By definition, there exists
g\in S^{\prime}
such that
f\simeq g
.
Thus, for some
\tau\in\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}/\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
we can define a character
\varphi:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}_{\ell}
via
\varphi(\sigma)=\overline{\rho}_{f}(\sigma){}^{\tau}\overline{\rho}_{g}(\sigma)^{-1}
.
It follows from
\overline{\rho}_{g}(\sigma)\thicksim\gamma
and part
(1)
of
Theorem
2.1
that
\det\overline{\rho}_{g}(\sigma)=\det\overline{\rho}_{f}(\sigma)=\varphi(\sigma)^{2}\det\overline{\rho}_{g}(\sigma)
.
Thus, we have
\varphi(\sigma)^{2}=1
and the result follows.
∎

3.4.
Main technical results

We now prove the main technical result which is used in the proof of Theorem

.

Theorem 3.6
.

Suppose that
\ell\geq 5
is prime and that
r
is an odd integer.
Suppose that
N
is a squarefree, odd, positive integer with
\ell\nmid N
. Assume that
3\nmid N
if
3\nmid r
. Let
\psi
be a Dirichlet character modulo
N
. Let
k
be an even positive integer such that
(k,\ell)
is suitable for
(N,\psi,r)
and
let
m\geq 1
be an integer.

If
(r,6)=1
, then there exists a positive density set
S
of primes such that if
p\in S
, then
p\equiv 1\pmod{\ell^{m}}
and
f\big|T_{p}\equiv-f\pmod{\ell^{m}}
for each newform

Proof.

We assume that
(r,6)=1

(r,6)=3
since the proof is nearly identical in that case.
Choose a number field
K
which contains all of the coefficients of all of the newforms
f\in S^{\new 2,3}_{k}(6N,\psi^{2},\varepsilon_{2,r,\psi},\varepsilon_{3,r,\psi})
.
Recall that we have fixed an embedding
\iota_{\ell}:\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
; if
\lambda
is the prime of
K
induced by
\iota_{\ell}
, then let
K_{\lambda}
be the completion of
K
at
\lambda
with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
and residue field
\mathbb{F}=\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}/\lambda
. Then for any newform
f\in S^{\new 2,3}_{k}(6N,\psi^{2},\varepsilon_{2,r,\psi},\varepsilon_{3,r,\psi})
, the Galois representations
\rho_{f}
and
\overline{\rho}_{f}
of Theorem
