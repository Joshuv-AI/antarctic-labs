Differentiable normal linearization of partially hyperbolic dynamical systems
Weijie Lu, Yonghui Xia, Weinian Zhang, Wenmeng Zhang

Abstract
. A result on C 0 C^{0} linearization which is differentiable at the hyperbolic fixed point is known. In this paper, we further investigate a partially hyperbolic diffeomorphism F F to find a local C 0 C^{0} conjugacy, which is C 1 C^{1} on the center manifold, to linearize the hyperbolic component (normal to the center direction) and obtain its Takens’ normal form. Our result is optimal, as it needs no non-resonant condition usually required for smooth conjugacy (e.g., as in the Takens’ theorem) and the C 1 , α C^{1,\alpha} ( α > 0 ) (\alpha>0) smoothness condition is sharp. For the proof, the center direction obstructs the decoupling of F F as the stable and unstable foliations do not intersect. We overcome this difficulty via a semi-decoupling method only with the unstable foliation, where a modified Lyapunov-Perron equation needs to be established along the center direction. Subsequent issues of cocycle reduction and differentiable linearization for an expansive fiber-preserving mapping are then addressed by the Whitney’s extension theory and a lifting technique, respectively. In the local context, our result improves the result of C 0 C^{0} normal linearization by [C. Pugh and M. Shub, Invent. Math., 10 (1970): 187-198] to a differentiable one. Keywords: Partial hyperbolicity; Takens’ normal form; center manifold; invariant foliation; Lyapunov-Perron equation MSC2020: 37C15; 37C86; 37D30



1.
Introduction

Linearization is one of the most important theories in the field of dynamical systems.
Given a smooth (analytic) diffeomorphism
F
near its fixed point
0
,
we expect to find a smooth (analytic) transformation such that
F
can be conjugated to its linear part
\Lambda:=DF(0)
.
In 1890, Poincaré (
[
28
]
) first studied the analytic linearization of an analytic diffeomorphism
F(x)=\Lambda x+O(\|x\|^{2})
on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
.
He proved that if all eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{p}
of
\Lambda
lie inside (or outside) the unit circle and satisfy the non-resonant conditions of all orders, i.e.,

\lambda_{i}\neq\lambda_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots\lambda_{d}^{n_{d}},\quad\forall i\in\{1,...,d\},

for all
n_{1},...,n_{d}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
with
\sum_{i=1}^{d}n_{i}\geq 2
, then there is an analytic diffeomorphism

\Phi(x)
such that
\Phi\circ F\circ\Phi^{-1}=\Lambda
.
Later, Siegel (
[
33
]
) considered the analytic linearization
under an assumption that the eigenvalues satisfy the Diophantine condition

|\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots\lambda_{d}^{n_{d}}|\geq\frac{C}{|n|^{\nu}},\quad\forall i\in\{1,...,d\},\,\sum_{i=1}^{d}n_{i}\geq 2,

for some constants
C,\nu>0
, proving that there exists an analytic diffeomorphism
\Phi(x)
linearizing
F
.
Brjuno (
[
4
]
) further weakened Siegel’s condition and proved the same result.
The proofs of Poincaré’s
and Siegel’s analytic linearization theorems, respectively,
can also be consulted in the works of Arnold (
[
1
]
),
Moser (
[
25
]
), Zehnder (
[
40
]
) and others.

In 1960s, Hartman (
[
19
]
) and Grobman (
[
16
]
) independently proved a
C^{0}
linearization of
C^{1}
hyperbolic diffeomorphisms near the fixed point.
The Hartman-Grobman theorem states that, for a
C^{1}
hyperbolic diffeomorphism
F
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
there exists a homeomorphism
\Phi
such that
F
can be
C^{0}
linearized.
Efforts were also made to generalize this result from a vicinity of a fixed point to a vicinity of an invariant manifold.
In 1970, Pugh and Shub (
[
29
]
) provided a normal linearization along a compact
C^{1}
invariant submanifold for a normally hyperbolic diffeomorphism of a Riemannian manifold.
Compared to the Hartman-Grobman theorem, Pugh-Shub’s
C^{0}
normal linearization theorem not only involves more techniques such as the fiber bundle theory and the
\lambda
-lemma, but also has more applications to problems such as the singular perturbation theory (
[
13
]
).

Regarding smooth linearization on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
Sternberg (
[
35
,
36
]
) obtained a local
C^{r}
linearization of
C^{N}
hyperbolic diffeomorphisms under the
N
-th order non-resonant condition, where
N=N(r)
is a large integer.
There is a a large number of literature to find better conditions for smooth linearization near the fixed point.
Some classical results can be found in Belickiĭ (
[
2
]
),
ElBialy (
[
11
]
),
Hartman (
[
18
]
),
Li and Lu (
[
22
]
),
Rodrigues and Solà-Morales (
[
31
]
),
Zhang, Zhang and Jarczyk (
[
41
]
).

On the other hand, extending Sternberg’s theorem, Takens (
[
37
]
) proved that under the
N
-th order strong non-resonant condition, i.e.,

|\lambda_{i}|\neq|\lambda_{1}|^{n_{1}}\cdots|\lambda_{d}|^{n_{d}},\quad\forall i\in\{1,...,d\},

with
2\leq\sum_{i=1}^{d}n_{i}\leq N
,
the hyperbolic part (i.e., the normal direction) of a
C^{N}
partially hyperbolic diffeomorphism
F
near its center manifold can be
C^{k}
linearized to obtain its Takens’ normal form (see (
2.5
) below).
For recent progresses in this regard, one can refer to (
[
6
,
23
]
).

Notice that among the results mentioned above,
C^{0}
linearization does not require the non-resonant condition, while analytic or smooth linearization needs it. Although some known results (e.g.,
[
30
,
38
]
) tell that
C^{0}
linearization can be improved to Hölder linearization without the non-resonant condition, the Hölder linearization cannot distinguish important properties such as the characteristic directions of dynamical systems.

Thus, one expects to enhance the regularity of the Hartman-Grobman
C^{0}
linearization as much as possible without the non-resonant condition. However, Hartman’s counterexample (
[
18
]
) shows that even a
C^{\infty}
diffeomorphism does not admit
C^{1}
linearization with resonance. Therefore, efforts are made to prove that the conjugacy is differentiable at the fixed point, i.e, the conjugacy
\Phi
has the form

\Phi(x)=x+o(\|x\|)\quad{\rm as}~x\to 0,

due to its important applications to the studies of semilinear elliptic equations (
[
15
, pp. 342]
), nonhyperbolic singularities (
[
3
]
), coarse-grained entropy (
[
27
]
), Onsager-Machlup functional (
[
9
]
) and so on.

The result on differentiable linearization without any non-resonant condition can be first found in
[
39
]
by van Strein in 1990 and later in
[
30
]
and
[
17
]
,
which stated that a smooth hyperbolic diffeomorphism on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
admits a local
C^{0}
linearization which is differentiable at the fixed point.
Recently, Zhang, Lu and Zhang (
[
42
]
) considered a
C^{1,\alpha}
(
\alpha>0
) diffeomorphism
F
and employed the method of decoupling via stable and unstable foliations to prove the differentiable linearization in a
Banach space under the spectral bandwidth condition

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}^{+}/\lambda_{i}^{-}<(\lambda_{k}^{+})^{-\alpha},\,\forall i=1,...,k,\quad\lambda_{j}^{+}/\lambda_{j}^{-}<(\lambda_{k+1}^{-})^{\alpha},\,\forall j=k+1,...,d,

where the spectrum of
\Lambda
satisfy

\lambda_{1}^{-}\leq\lambda_{1}^{+}<\cdots<\lambda_{k}^{-}\leq\lambda_{k}^{+}<1<\lambda_{k+1}^{-}\leq\lambda_{k+1}^{+}<\cdots<\lambda_{d}^{-}\leq\lambda_{d}^{+}.

Since the spectral condition can be automatically satisfied in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, this result generalizes the previous results of van Strein et al. by lowering the smoothness condition to
C^{1,\alpha}
, which is sharp via a counterexample (i.e., a
C^{1}
diffeomorphism) given in
[
42
]
. Moreover, the conjugacy was further proved to have the form

\Phi(x)=x+O(\|x\|^{1+\beta})\quad{\rm as}~x\to 0,

with
\beta>0
. This result was later applied to study the Onsager-Machlup functional (
[
9
]
),
so that the derivative of its minimizing sequence is uniformly bounded variation in the neighborhood of the critical point, which relies on the key estimate
O(\|x\|^{1+\beta})
of the nonlinearity of
\Phi
.

Recently,
Lu, Xia and Zhang (
[
24
]
) presented a differentiable linearization result in a Banach space
under a weaker spectral bandwidth condition

\lambda_{i}^{+}/\lambda_{i}^{-}<(\lambda_{i}^{+})^{-\alpha},\,\forall i=1,...,k,\quad\lambda_{j}^{+}/\lambda_{j}^{-}<(\lambda_{j}^{-})^{\alpha},\,\forall j=k+1,...,d,

which is shown to be (almost) sharp by a counter example given in
[
32
]
.
Moreover, Dragičević, Zhang and Zhang (
[
8
]
) proved that the differentiable linearization result holds in the presence of nonuniform hyperbolicity.

In this paper, we establish a differentiable normal linearzation, without any non-resonant condition, under the framework of partial hyperbolicity, to obtain the Takens’ normal form (see (
2.5
) below).
More specifically, we have the following result:

•

A

C^{1,\alpha}
(
\alpha\in(0,1]
)
partially hyperbolic diffeomorphism admits a
C^{0}
normal linearization, where the
C^{0}
conjugacy is differentiable on the center manifold with a continuous derivative.

The conjugacy exhibits
C^{1}
-smoothness on the center manifold, which usually requires some non-resonant conditions (for example, as in the Takens’ theorem), but we do not need.
Moreover, as mentioned before, the
C^{1,\alpha}
smoothness condition is sharp via a counterexample given in
[
42
]
, which will be elaborated in the next section (see Remark 4).
Since the center manifold can be regarded as a local version of the normally hyperbolic invariant manifold, in the local context, our result improves the result of Pugh and Shub’s
C^{0}
normal linearization in
[
29
]
to a differentiable one.

In comparison with the hyperbolic case, the center direction brings a crucial difficulty. Precisely, in the hyperbolic case, stable and unstable foliations intersect transversally, allowing to decouple the system into a contraction and an expansion; however, in the partially hyperbolic case, the center direction prevents intersection of foliations. In order to overcome the difficulty, inspired by
[
29
]
, we introduce the method of semi-decoupling. Namely, we straighten up only the unstable foliation to obtain an expansive fiber-preserving mapping and utilize the differentiable linearization result in
[
42
]
by a lifting technique. In the construction of the unstable foliation, in order to show its regularity on the center manifold, a modified Lyapunov-Perron equation needs to be established along the center direction.

For the subsequent difficulty in cocycle reduction posed by the semi-decoupling, we will address it by means of Whitney’s extension theory.
More precisely, for an expansive fiber-preserving mapping with base points on the center-stable subspace, its linear part is actually a linear cocycle depending on the center-stable variables. This linear cocycle needs to be reduced to a simpler one depending only on the center variable becasue the linear part in the Takens’ normal form (see (
2.5
) below) depends only on the center variable. For this purpose, we first establish a
\beta
-Hölder conjugacy with
\beta>0
between these two classes of cocycles, and then find a
C^{1,\beta}
transformation whose derivative at the center-stable subspace is just the

\beta
-Hölder conjugacy by using the Whitney extension theorem. Such a
C^{1,\beta}
transformation helps us achieve the reduction.

This paper will be organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we first state the main theorem, and then provide the block-diagonalization of linear cocycles generated by
DF
on the center manifold as preliminaries.
Section
3
is devoted to construct the unstable foliation and show its regularity on the center manifold. Subsequently, using the semi-decoupling method, we prove the main theorem in Section
4
by utilizing the reduction of a linear cocycle and the differentiable linearization of an expansive fiber-preserving mapping, which will be complementarily proved in Sections
5
and
6
, respectively.

2.
Main result and preliminaries

2.1.
Main result

In this subsection, we present our main result.
Consider a

C^{1,\alpha}
(
\alpha\in(0,1]
)
diffeomorphism

F:U\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d\in\mathbb{N}

(2.1)

\displaystyle\|Df(x)\|\leq\delta_{f},\quad\|Df(x)-Df(y)\|\leq\delta_{f}\|x-y\|^{\alpha/2},\quad\forall x,y\in U,

\delta_{f}>0

Then, by using a smooth cut-off function which is equal to

\displaystyle\|Df(x)\|\leq\delta_{f},\quad\|Df(x)-Df(y)\|\leq\delta_{f}\|x-y\|^{\alpha},\quad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Here, we still use
\delta_{f}
and
\alpha

Next, assume

The above block-diagonal form of

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{d}=X_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus X_{k}\oplus X_{c}\oplus X_{k+1}\oplus\cdots\oplus X_{p}.

In what follows, for

(

\displaystyle x_{su}:=x_{s}+x_{u},\quad x_{cs}:=x_{c}+x_{s},\quad x_{cu}:=x_{c}+x_{u},

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}x_{s}\\

.

From (

\tilde{x}_{c}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{c}

It implies that the conjugacy is

Remark 4
.

When the center direction does not exist (i.e.,

\mathcal{M}_{cs}=X_{cs}
and
\mathcal{M}_{cu}=X_{cu}
,
implying

\pi_{u}F(x_{c},x_{s},0)=0

and

\pi_{s}F(x_{c},0,x_{u})=0.

Hence,

(2.11)

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{s}}(\pi_{u}F)(x_{c})=0,\quad\partial_{x_{u}}(\pi_{s}F)(x_{c})=0,\quad\forall x_{c}\in X_{c},

because
\partial_{x_{cs}}(\pi_{u}F)(x_{c},x_{s},0)=0
and
\partial_{x_{cu}}(\pi_{s}F)(x_{c},0,x_{u})=0

by the fact stated below (
2.7
).

Combining (
2.7
), (
2.8
) with (
2.11
), we obtain that

\displaystyle DF(x_{c})=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\partial_{x_{c}}(\pi_{c}F)(x_{c})&0&0\\
0&\partial_{x_{s}}(\pi_{s}F)(x_{c})&0\\
0&0&\partial_{x_{u}}(\pi_{u}F)(x_{c})\end{array}\right),\quad\forall x_{c}\in X_{c}.

Now, we define
g(x_{c}):=F(x_{c})=\pi_{c}F(x_{c})
and
A(x_{c}):=DF(x_{c})
,
and consider the following linear cocycle

(2.15)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(m,n;x_{c}):=\begin{cases}A(g^{m-1}(x_{c}))\cdots A(g^{n}(x_{c})),\quad&m\geq n+1,\\
id,\quad&m=n,\\
A(g^{m}(x_{c}))^{-1}\cdots A(g^{n-1}(x_{c}))^{-1},\quad&m\leq n-1.\end{cases}

By (
2.10
) and (
2.2
), we see that

A(x_{c})
is a small perturbation of
A
, given in (
2.1
). Thus, applying
the roughness theory of exponential dichotomy (see e.g.
[
20
,
43
]
), the dichotomy spectrum
\Sigma(A(x_{c}))
of
\mathcal{A}(m,n;x_{c})
satisfies

(2.16)

\displaystyle\Sigma(A(x_{c}))\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{p}[\lambda_{i}-\varsigma,\lambda_{i}+\varsigma]\cup[1-\varsigma,1+\varsigma]\subset[\lambda_{s}^{-},\lambda_{s}^{+}]\cup[\lambda_{u}^{-},\lambda_{u}^{+}]\cup[1-\varsigma,1+\varsigma]

for a small constant
\varsigma\geq 0
, where
\lambda_{s}^{-},\lambda_{s}^{+},\lambda_{u}^{-},\lambda_{u}^{+}
are given in (
2.3
).
Then, by (
2.2
)-(
2.16
), there is a
K\geq 1
such that

(2.17)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\mathcal{A}(m,n;x_{c})\pi_{s}\|\leq K(\lambda_{s}^{+})^{m-n},\quad&\forall m\geq n,\\
\|\mathcal{A}(m,n;x_{c})\pi_{u}\|\leq K(\lambda_{u}^{-})^{m-n},\quad&\forall m\leq n,\end{split}

and

(2.18)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\mathcal{A}(m,n;x_{c})\pi_{s}\|\leq K(\lambda_{s}^{-})^{m-n},\quad&\forall m\leq n,\\
\|\mathcal{A}(m,n;x_{c})\pi_{u}\|\leq K(\lambda_{u}^{+})^{m-n},\quad&\forall m\geq n.\end{split}

Moreover, we see that

(2.19)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\mathcal{A}(m,n;x_{c})\pi_{c}\|\leq K(1+\varsigma)^{m-n},\quad&\forall m\geq n,\\
\|\mathcal{A}(m,n;x_{c})\pi_{c}\|\leq K(1-\varsigma)^{m-n},\quad&\forall m\leq n.\end{split}

Note that, in the above trichotomy (
2.17
)-(
2.19
), the projections
\pi_{s},\pi_{u},\pi_{c}
are independent of
x_{c}
, and this is because the cocycle
\mathcal{A}(m,n;x_{c})
has been block-diagonalized, as seen in (
2.2
).

Next, we give a result of the stable foliation of
g(x_{cs}):=\pi_{cs}F(x_{cs})
on the center-stable manifold

, which will be proved at the end of Section

.

Lemma 7
.

Suppose that

is a
C^{1,\alpha}
diffeomorphism

), (
2.2
), (

-smooth with a small constant

Remind that the

with small

.

Lemma

\displaystyle\phi(x_{cs}):=x_{cs}+\rho(x_{cs})(h_{s}(x_{c},x_{s})-x_{c}),\quad\forall x_{cs}=(x_{c},x_{s})\in X_{cs},

, for all

. Thus, the new diffeomorphism

-smoothness as both

) and (

). Moreover, since the transformation

\displaystyle h_{s}(x_{cs},z_{s})=x_{c},\quad\forall x_{cs},z_{s}\in\tilde{U}.

Finally, in view of Lemma
5
,
(
2.9
) and (
2.21
), we may define
\mathcal{T}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
by

(2.23)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(x):=(\phi+id_{u})\circ\mathcal{G}^{-1}\circ\Upsilon^{-1}(x)

and verify that

for any

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}(x)-\tilde{x}_{c}-D\mathcal{T}(\tilde{x}_{c})(x-\tilde{x}_{c})\|\leq L\|x-\tilde{x}_{c}\|^{1+\alpha},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

.
Putting

,

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(x)=\tilde{x}_{c}+\Delta(\tilde{x}_{c})(x-\tilde{x}_{c})+O(\|x-\tilde{x}_{c}\|^{1+\alpha}).

Similarly, one can obtain that

3.
Regularity of unstable foliation

In this section, we assume that
F
satisfies (
2.10
) and (
2.2
), while (
2.22
) is not needed. We investigate the regularity of the unstable foliation
\{\mathcal{W}_{u}(x)\}_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
of
F
,
whose leaves can be formulated by

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{u}(x)=\Big\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\sup_{n\leq 0}\{\varrho^{-n}\|F^{n}(z)-F^{n}(x)\|\}<\infty\Big\},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

for a constant
\varrho\in(1+\varsigma,\lambda_{u}^{-})
(see e.g.

[
5
]
).
Then we have the following result.

Proposition 8
.

Suppose that
F
is a
C^{1,\alpha}
diffeomorphism
such that (
2.17
)-(
2.19
) hold.
Then there exists an unstable foliation of
F
with leaves

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{u}(x)=\{z_{u}+h_{u}(x,z_{u}):z_{u}\in X_{u}\},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
h_{u}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times X_{u}\to X_{cs}
is continuous in both variables and is
C^{1,\alpha}
-smooth in
z_{u}
with a globally bounded derivative.
Furthermore,
h_{u}
satisfies that
h_{u}(x,\pi_{u}x)=\pi_{cs}x
and

(3.3)

\displaystyle\|h_{u}(x,z_{u})-\pi_{cs}x\|\leq L\|(\pi_{su}x,z_{u})\|^{1+\beta},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},~\forall z_{u}\in X_{u},

where
0<\beta<(\log(1+\varsigma)-\log\lambda_{u}^{-})/\log\lambda_{s}^{-}
.

In the proof, we use
C,K,L,M
to denote any positive constants that arise as upper bounds for certain expressions, rather than introducing a more precise indexing
C_{i},K_{i},L_{i},M_{i}
for distinct constants.
Moreover, we use
x_{*}
instead of
\pi_{*}x
with
*=c,s,u,cs,su
for simplification.

Proof of Proposition
8
.
.

To begin with,
we establish the Lyapunov-Perron equation for
F
along the center manifold.
Let
\mathfrak{U}_{\varrho}
consist of all sequences
(q_{n})_{n\leq 0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\sup_{n\leq 0}\varrho^{-n}\|q_{n}\|<\infty

for a constant

(3.4)

\displaystyle\varrho\in(1+\varsigma,\lambda_{u}^{-}(\lambda_{s}^{-})^{\beta})\subset(1+\varsigma,\lambda_{u}^{-}).

The choice of
\varrho
is possible because
\beta<(\log(1+\varsigma)-\log\lambda_{u}^{-})/\log\lambda_{s}^{-}
.
It is a Banach space equipped with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\varrho}
defined by

\|\mathbf{q}\|_{\varrho}:=\sup_{n\leq 0}\varrho^{-n}\|q_{n}\|,

where
\mathbf{q}:=(q_{n})_{n\leq 0}\in\mathfrak{U}_{\varrho}
. Then,
(
3.1
) can be rewritten as

(3.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{u}(x)=\Big\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:(F^{n}(z)-F^{n}(x))_{n\leq 0}\in\mathfrak{U}_{\varrho}\Big\}.

Next, recalling that
g
is defined above (
2.15
), we give the following lemma.

Lemma 9
.

Given any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
z_{u}\in X_{u}
, assume that
(q_{n})_{n\leq 0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a sequence such that
\pi_{u}q_{0}=z_{u}-x_{u}
and

(q_{n})_{n\leq 0}\in\mathfrak{U}_{\varrho}
. Then,
q_{n}=F^{n}(x+q_{0})-F^{n}(x)
for all
n\leq 0

if and only if
(q_{n})_{n\leq 0}
satisfies the Lyapunov-Perron equation along the center manifold, i.e.,

\displaystyle q_{n}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}(n,0;x_{c})(z_{u}-x_{u})

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k=n}^{-1}\mathcal{A}(n,k+1;x_{c})\pi_{u}\{f_{g^{k}(x_{c})}(F^{k}(x)+q_{k})-f_{g^{k}(x_{c})}(F^{k}(x))\}

(3.6)

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{k=-\infty}^{n-1}\mathcal{A}(n,k+1;x_{c})\pi_{cs}\{f_{g^{k}(x_{c})}(F^{k}(x)+q_{k})-f_{g^{k}(x_{c})}(F^{k}(x))\}

for all
n\leq 0
,
where
f_{g^{k}(x_{c})}(y):=f(y)-Df(g^{k}(x_{c}))y
for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and the term

\sum_{k=0}^{-1}

is defined to be
0
.

We will prove this lemma after the completion of this proof.
Subsequently, we claim that if equation (
9
) has a unique solution
(q_{n})_{n\leq 0}\in\mathfrak{U}_{\varrho}
, then

(3.7)

\displaystyle h_{u}(x,z_{u}):=x_{cs}+\pi_{cs}q_{0}

defines the unique unstable foliation of
F
via (
3.2
). In fact,
(q_{n})_{n\leq 0}\in\mathfrak{U}_{\varrho}
is a solution of (
9
) means that
\pi_{u}q_{0}=z_{u}-x_{u}
and, therefore,

(3.8)

\displaystyle z_{u}+h_{u}(x,z_{u})=\pi_{u}q_{0}+x_{u}+x_{cs}+\pi_{cs}q_{0}=x+q_{0}\in\mathcal{W}_{u}(x)

by (
3.5
) because Lemma
9
implies that

(F^{n}(x+q_{0})-F^{n}(x))_{n\leq 0}=(q_{n})_{n\leq 0}\in\mathfrak{U}_{\varrho}.

On the other hand, for a point
z\in\mathcal{W}_{u}(x)
such that
\pi_{u}z=z_{u}
, we define

\tilde{q}_{n}=F^{n}(z)-F^{n}(x),\quad\forall n\leq 0,

which implies that
(\tilde{q}_{n})_{n\leq 0}\in\mathfrak{U}_{\varrho}
by (
3.5
) and
\pi_{u}\tilde{q}_{0}=z_{u}-x_{u}
. Then, Lemma
9
indicates that
(\tilde{q}_{n})_{n\leq 0}
is the unique solution of equation (
9
) in
\mathfrak{U}_{\varrho}
, i.e.,
\tilde{q}_{n}=q_{n}
. It means that

\pi_{cs}z=x_{cs}+\pi_{cs}\tilde{q}_{0}=x_{cs}+\pi_{cs}q_{0}=h_{u}(x,z_{u}),

i.e., the point
z
has the unique form
z_{u}+h_{u}(x,z_{u})
. Combining this fact with (
3.8
), we prove the claimed result.

According to the above discussion, in what follows, we need to prove the uniqueness of the solution
\{q_{n}\}_{n\leq 0}
of equation (
9
), and show its regularity with respect to
x
and
z_{u}
. For the purpose, recalling that
f_{g^{k}(x_{c})}(y)=f(y)-Df(g^{k}(x_{c}))y
for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we obtain from
(
2.10
) that, for all
n\leq 0
,
Df_{g^{n}(x_{c})}(g^{n}(x_{c}))=0
and

(3.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|Df_{g^{n}(x_{c})}(y)\|=\|Df(y)-Df(g^{n}(x_{c}))\|\leq 2\delta_{f},\\
&\|Df_{g^{n}(x_{c})}(y)-Df_{g^{n}(x_{c})}(\tilde{y})\|=\|Df(y)-Df(\tilde{y})\|\leq M\|y-\tilde{y}\|^{\alpha}\end{split}

for all
y,\tilde{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Then, similarly to
[
5
, Theorems 2.1 and 2.2]
, we can prove that
equation (
9
) has a unique solution
(q_{n})_{n\leq 0}\in\mathfrak{U}_{\varrho}
, which enables us to regard all
q_{n}
’s as functions of
(x,z_{u})
, and that all
q_{n}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times X_{u}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
are continuous such that

q_{n}(x,x_{u})=0,\quad\forall n\leq 0,

and
q_{n}(x,\cdot):X_{u}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
are
C^{1,\alpha}
with globally bounded derivatives.

Next, in order to prove (
3.3
), it suffices to study the regularity of
q_{0}(x,z_{u})

due to (
3.7
). Then, we claim that

(3.10)

\displaystyle\sup_{(x,z_{u})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\{x_{c}\}}\frac{\|q_{0}(x,z_{u})-(z_{u}-x_{u})\|}{\|(x_{su},z_{u})\|^{1+\beta}}\leq L.

In fact, given a constant
\sigma\geq 0
, by (
2.17
), (
2.19
) and (
3.9
), we see from (
9
) that

\displaystyle\sup_{n\leq 0}\frac{\varrho^{-n}\|q_{n}(x,z_{u})-\mathcal{A}(n,0;x_{c})(z_{u}-x_{u})\|}{\|(x_{su},z_{u})\|^{1+\sigma}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\|(x_{su},z_{u})\|^{1+\sigma}}\sup_{n\leq 0}\Bigg\{\sum_{k=n}^{-1}K(\lambda_{u}^{-}/\varrho)^{n-k-1}\varrho^{-k-1}\big\|f_{g^{k}(x_{c})}(F^{k}(x)+q_{k}(x,z_{u}))-f_{g^{k}(x_{c})}(F^{k}(x))\big\|

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k=-\infty}^{n-1}K((1+\varsigma)/\varrho)^{n-k-1}\varrho^{-k-1}\big\|f_{g^{k}(x_{c})}(F^{k}(x)+q_{k}(x,z_{u}))-f_{g^{k}(x_{c})}(F^{k}(x))\big\|\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\|(x_{su},z_{u})\|^{1+\sigma}}\sup_{k\leq 0}\bigg\{\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\varrho^{-k}\|Df_{g^{k}(x_{c})}(F^{k}(x)+tq_{k}(x,z_{u}))\|\,\|q_{k}(x,z_{u})\|\bigg\}

\displaystyle=C\sup_{k\leq 0}\Bigg\{\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\varrho^{-k}\big\|Df_{g^{k}(x_{c})}(F^{k}(x)+tq_{k}(x,z_{u}))\big\|

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\bigg(\frac{\|q_{k}(x,z_{u})\!-\!\mathcal{A}(k,0;x_{c})(z_{u}\!-\!x_{u})\|}{\|(x_{su},z_{u})\|^{1+\sigma}}\!+\!\frac{\|\mathcal{A}(n,0;x_{c})(z_{u}\!-\!x_{u})\|}{\|(x_{su},z_{u})\|^{1+\sigma}}\bigg)\!\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq 2\delta_{f}C\sup_{k\leq 0}\frac{\varrho^{-k}\|q_{k}(x,z_{u})-\mathcal{A}(k,0;x_{c})(z_{u}-x_{u})\|}{\|(x_{su},z_{u})\|^{1+\sigma}}

(3.11)

\displaystyle\quad+C\sup_{k\leq 0}\frac{(\lambda_{u}^{-}/\varrho)^{k}\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\big\|Df_{g^{k}(x_{c})}(F^{n}(x)+tq_{n}(x,z_{u}))\big\|}{\|(x_{su},z_{u})\|^{\sigma}}

for all
(x,z_{u})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\{x_{c}\}
(i.e.,
\|(x_{su},z_{u})\|\neq 0
).
Then, regarding the case of
\sigma=0
, (
3
) gives

\displaystyle\sup_{n\leq 0}\frac{\varrho^{-n}\|q_{n}(x,z_{u})-\mathcal{A}(n,0;x_{c})(z_{u}-x_{u})\|}{\|(x_{su},z_{u})\|}\leq\frac{2\delta_{f}C}{1-2\delta_{f}C},

and thus,

\displaystyle\|q_{n}(x,z_{u})\|\leq

\displaystyle~L\varrho^{n}\|(x_{su},z_{u})\|+\|\mathcal{A}(n,0;x_{c})(z_{u}-x_{u})\|

(3.12)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~L\varrho^{n}\|(x_{su},z_{u})\|+K(\lambda_{u}^{-})^{n}\|(z_{u}-x_{u})\|\leq 2L\varrho^{n}\|(x_{su},z_{u})\|

for all
n\leq 0
.

Consider the case that
\sigma=\beta\in(0,\{\log(1+\varsigma)-\log\lambda_{u}^{-}\}/\log\lambda_{s}^{-})
. By (
3
) we get

\displaystyle\sup_{n\leq 0}\frac{\varrho^{-n}\|q_{n}(x,z_{u})-\mathcal{A}(n,0;x_{c})(z_{u}-x_{u})\|}{\|(x_{su},z_{u})\|^{1+\beta}}

(3.13)

\displaystyle\leq L\sup_{n\leq 0}\frac{(\lambda_{u}^{-}/\varrho)^{n}\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\big\|Df_{g^{n}(x_{c})}(F^{n}(x)+tq_{n})\big\|^{(1-\beta/\alpha)+\beta/\alpha}}{\|(x_{su},z_{u})\|^{\beta}}

for all
n\leq 0
.
Note that, similarly to (
2.18
), we have

\displaystyle\|F^{n}(x)-g^{n}(x_{c})\|

\displaystyle=\|F^{n}(x)-F^{n}(x_{c})\|\leq\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|DF^{n}(\xi)\|\|x_{su}\|

(3.14)

\displaystyle\leq(\lambda_{s}^{-})^{n}\|x_{su}\|,\quad\forall n\leq 0.

Then, by (
3.9
), (
3
) and (
3
), we see from (
3
) that

\displaystyle\sup_{n\leq 0}\frac{\varrho^{-n}\|q_{n}(x,z_{u})-\mathcal{A}(n,0;x_{c})(z_{u}-x_{u})\|}{\|(x_{su},z_{u})\|^{1+\beta}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\delta_{f}^{1-\beta/\alpha}L\sup_{n\leq 0}\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\Bigg\{(\lambda_{u}^{-}/\varrho)^{n}\frac{\big\|Df_{g^{n}(x_{c})}(F^{n}(x)+tq_{n}(x,z_{u}))-Df_{g^{n}(x_{c})}(g^{n}(x_{c}))\big\|^{\beta/\alpha}}{\big\|F^{n}(x)+tq_{n}(x,z_{u})-g^{n}(x_{c})\big\|^{\beta}}

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\frac{\big\|F^{n}(x)-g^{n}(x_{c})+tq_{n}(x,z_{u})\big\|^{\beta}}{\|(x_{su},z_{u})\|^{\beta}}\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq 2\delta_{f}^{1-\beta/\alpha}LM^{\beta/\alpha}\sup_{n\leq 0}\bigg\{(\lambda_{u}^{-}/\varrho)^{n}\bigg(\frac{\|F^{n}(x)-g^{n}(x_{c})\|+\|q_{n}(x,z_{u})\|}{\|(x_{su},z_{u})\|}\bigg)^{\beta}\bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq 2\delta_{f}^{1-\beta/\alpha}LM^{\beta/\alpha}\sup_{n\leq 0}\{(\lambda_{u}^{-}/\varrho)^{n}((\lambda_{s}^{-})^{n}+2L\varrho^{n})^{\beta}\}

\displaystyle\leq L\sup_{n\leq 0}\{(\lambda_{u}^{-}(\lambda_{s}^{-})^{\beta}/\varrho)^{n}\}\leq L,\quad\forall n\leq 0,

where
\lambda_{u}^{-}(\lambda_{s}^{-})^{\beta}/\varrho>1

due to (

). Hence, for any
(x_{cs},z_{u})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\{x_{c}\}
(i.e.,
\|(x_{su},z_{u})\|\neq 0

\displaystyle\frac{\|q_{0}(x,z_{u})-(z_{u}-x_{u})\|}{\|(x_{su},z_{u})\|^{1+\beta}}\leq\sup_{n\leq 0}\frac{\varrho^{-n}\|q_{n}(x,z_{u})-\mathcal{A}(n,0;x_{c})(z_{u}-x_{u})\|}{\|(x_{su},z_{u})\|^{1+\beta}}\leq L,

which proves the claimed result (

Finally, we obtain from (

) and (

\displaystyle\frac{\|h_{u}(x,z_{u})-x_{cs}\|}{\|(x_{su},z_{u})\|^{1+\beta}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\|q_{0}(x,z_{u})-\pi_{u}q_{0}(x,z_{u})\|}{\|(x_{su},z_{u})\|^{1+\beta}}

(3.16)

\displaystyle=\frac{\|q_{0}(x,z_{u})-(z_{u}-x_{u})\|}{\|(x_{su},z_{u})\|^{1+\beta}}\leq L.

The proof is completed.

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}A_{cu}^{n-k-1}\Big\{D(\pi_{cu}f)(F^{k}(x_{c}))\partial_{z_{s}}p_{k}(x_{c},0)-D(\pi_{cu}f)(F^{k}(\tilde{x}_{c}))\partial_{z_{s}}p_{k}(\tilde{x}_{c},0)\Big\}.

Choosing a
\gamma_{2}\in(\gamma_{1},1-\varsigma)
and by (
B.2
), we have

\sup_{n\geq 0}\{\gamma_{2}^{-n}\|\partial_{z_{s}}p_{n}(x_{c},0)-\partial_{z_{s}}p_{n}(\tilde{x}_{c},0)\|\}<\infty,

and note that

\|F^{k}(x_{c})-F^{k}(\tilde{x}_{c})\|\leq\sup_{\xi\in X_{c}}\|DF^{k}(\xi)\|\,\|x_{c}-\tilde{x}_{c}\|\leq(\lambda_{u}^{+}+\delta_{f})^{k}\|x_{c}-\tilde{x}_{c}\|,\quad\forall k\geq 0.

Therefore, (
B
) together with (
2.2
), (
2.17
)-(
2.19
) and (
B.2
) yields

\displaystyle\gamma_{2}^{-n}\|\partial_{z_{s}}p_{n}(x_{c},0)-\partial_{z_{s}}p_{n}(\tilde{x}_{c},0)\|

\displaystyle\leq\gamma_{2}^{-n}\Bigg\{\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\|A_{s}^{n-k-1}\{D(\pi_{s}f)(F^{k}(x_{c}))-D(\pi_{s}f)(F^{k}(\tilde{x}_{c}))\}\partial_{z_{s}}p_{k}(x_{c},0)\|

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\|A_{s}^{n-k-1}D(\pi_{s}f)(F^{k}(\tilde{x}_{c}))\{\partial_{z_{s}}p_{k}(x_{c},0)-\partial_{z_{s}}p_{k}(\tilde{x}_{c},0)\}\|

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\|A_{cu}^{n-k-1}\{D(\pi_{cu}f)(F^{k}(x_{c}))-D(\pi_{cu}f)(F^{k}(\tilde{x}_{c}))\}\partial_{z_{s}}p_{k}(x_{c},0)\|

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\|A_{cu}^{n-k-1}D(\pi_{cu}f)(F^{k}(\tilde{x}_{c}))\{\partial_{z_{s}}p_{k}(x_{c},0)-\partial_{z_{s}}p_{k}(\tilde{x}_{c},0)\}\|\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma_{2}^{-1}\Bigg\{\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}K\left(\frac{\lambda_{s}^{+}}{\gamma_{2}}\right)^{n-k-1}\delta_{f}\|F^{k}(x_{c})-F^{k}(\tilde{x}_{c})\|^{\alpha}(\gamma_{1}/\gamma_{2})^{k}\gamma_{1}^{-k}\|\partial_{z_{s}}p_{k}(x_{c},0)\|

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}K\left(\frac{\lambda_{s}^{+}}{\gamma_{2}}\right)^{n-k-1}\delta_{f}\gamma_{2}^{-k}\|\partial_{z_{s}}p_{k}(x_{c},0)-\partial_{z_{s}}p_{k}(\tilde{x}_{c},0)\|

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}K\left(\frac{1-\varsigma}{\gamma_{2}}\right)^{n-k-1}\delta_{f}\|F^{k}(x_{c})-F^{k}(\tilde{x}_{c})\|^{\alpha}(\gamma_{1}/\gamma_{2})^{k}\gamma_{1}^{-k}\|\partial_{z_{s}}p_{k}(x_{c},0)\|

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}K\left(\frac{1-\varsigma}{\gamma_{2}}\right)^{n-k-1}\delta_{f}\gamma_{2}^{-k}\|\partial_{z_{s}}p_{k}(x_{c},0)-\partial_{z_{s}}p_{k}(\tilde{x}_{c},0)\|\Bigg\}

(B.8)

\displaystyle\leq C\delta_{f}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{(\lambda_{u}^{+}+\delta_{f})^{\alpha}\gamma_{1}}{\gamma_{2}}\right)^{k}\|x_{c}-\tilde{x}_{c}\|^{\alpha}+C\delta_{f}\sup_{n\geq 0}\{\gamma_{2}^{-n}\|\partial_{z_{s}}p_{n}(x_{c},0)-\partial_{z_{s}}p_{n}(\tilde{x}_{c},0)\|\},

where
C>0
is a constant and
(\lambda_{u}^{+}+\delta_{f})^{\alpha}\gamma_{1}<\gamma_{2}
since
\gamma_{1}<\gamma_{2}
and

(\lambda_{u}^{+}+\delta_{f})^{\alpha}
is close to
1
as
\alpha>0
is small.
Therefore, (
B
) gives

\displaystyle\sup_{n\geq 0}\{\gamma_{2}^{-n}\|\partial_{z_{s}}p_{n}(x_{c},0)-\partial_{z_{s}}p_{n}(\tilde{x}_{c},0)\|\}\leq\delta_{p}\|x_{c}-\tilde{x}_{c}\|^{\alpha},\quad\forall n\geq 0,

which implies that

\displaystyle\|\partial_{z_{s}}p_{0}(x_{c},0)-\partial_{z_{s}}p_{0}(\tilde{x}_{c},0)\|\leq\delta_{p}\|x_{c}-\tilde{x}_{c}\|^{\alpha}.

This proves the second inequality of (
B.4
).

Step 2. Study the tangent spaces of leaves at
x_{c}\in X_{c}
.

Let
E_{s}(x_{c}):=\{y_{s}+\partial_{z_{s}}h_{s}(x_{c},0)y_{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:y_{s}\in X_{s}\}

be the tangent space of the stable foliation
W_{s}(x)
at
x_{c}\in X_{c}
.
From (
B
) and (
B.3
), we see that

id_{s}+\partial_{z_{s}}h_{s}(x_{c},0)=\partial_{z_{s}}(\pi_{s}p_{0})(x_{c},0)+\partial_{z_{s}}(\pi_{cu}p_{0})(x_{c},0)=\partial_{z_{s}}p_{0}(x_{c},0),

which together with (
B.4
) implies that
E_{s}(x_{c})
is uniformly close to
X_{s}
.
On the other hand, it is well known that the tangent spaces of leaves of the stable foliation is the fiber of the stable distribution (see e.g.
[
26
]
), which implies the invariance of
E_{s}(x_{c})
under the action of
DF(x_{c})
. Similarly, we can define the fiber of the unstable distribution
E_{u}(x_{c})
, which is uniformly close to
X_{u}
and is invariant under
DF(x_{c})
.

Then, there exists a transition matrix
P(x_{c})\in Gl(d,\mathbb{R})
, mapping
E_{s}(x_{c})
and
E_{u}(x_{c})
onto
X_{s}
and
X_{u}
, respectively, such that
P(0)=id
,

(B.9)

\displaystyle\|P(x_{c})-id\|\leq\delta_{E},\quad\|P(x_{c})-P(\tilde{x}_{c})\|\leq\delta_{E}\|x_{c}-\tilde{x}_{c}\|^{\alpha},\quad\forall x_{c},\tilde{x}_{c}\in X_{c},

where
\delta_{E}>0
is sufficiently small.
Moreover, we see that
X_{s}
and
X_{u}
are invariant under
P(F(x_{c}))DF(x_{c})P(x_{c})^{-1}
, which implies that

\displaystyle P(F(x_{c}))DF(x_{c})P(x_{c})^{-1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}*&0&0\\
&*&0\\
&0&*\end{array}\right),

where the three rows of the matrix correspond to the subspaces
X_{c}
,
X_{s}
and
X_{u}
respectively, and
*
denote elements that may be non-zero.

Step 3. Extend
P(x_{c})
via the Whitney’s extension theorem.

Similarly to Step 3 in Section
5
, we can apply the Whitney’s extension theorem (see Lemma
14
in Appendix
A
) to obtain a
C^{1,\alpha}
diffeomorphism
\Upsilon:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

(B.13)

\displaystyle\Upsilon(x_{c})=x_{c},\quad D\Upsilon(x_{c})=P(x_{c}),\quad\|D\Upsilon(x)-id\|\leq\delta_{\Upsilon}

for all
x_{c}\in X_{c}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
\delta_{\Upsilon}>0
is a small constant.

Finally, we only need to give some details to show the third inequality of (
B.13
). More precisely,
we see from (
B.9
) that the
C^{1,\alpha}
norm of
\Upsilon-id
on
X_{c}
is small.
Then, by applying
[
34
, p.177, Theorem 4]
(or
[
12
, Theorem 2]
), the extension of
\Upsilon-id
onto
\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfies that

\|\Upsilon-id\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\delta_{E},

where
C>0
is a constant and
\|\cdot\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
denotes the
C^{1,\alpha}
norm in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Therefore,
\Upsilon
is a
C^{1,\alpha}
diffeomorphism defined on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

(B.14)

\displaystyle\|\Upsilon(x)-x\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\delta_{E},\quad\quad\|\Upsilon^{-1}(x)-x\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\delta_{E},

by the Inverse Mapping Theorem. Hence, the new diffeomorphism
\Upsilon\circ F\circ\Upsilon^{-1}
satisfies that

\displaystyle D(\Upsilon\circ F\circ\Upsilon^{-1})(x_{c})

\displaystyle=D\Upsilon(F\circ\Upsilon^{-1}(x_{c}))DF(\Upsilon^{-1}(x_{c}))D\Upsilon^{-1}(x_{c})

\displaystyle=D\Upsilon(F(x_{c}))DF(x_{c})D\Upsilon^{-1}(x_{c})=P(F(x_{c}))DF(x_{c})P(x_{c})^{-1}

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}*&0&0\\
&*&0\\
&0&*\end{array}\right)

due to (
B.13
) and (
B
). Here, we still denoted
\Upsilon\circ F\circ\Upsilon^{-1}
by
F
, and see from the above equalities that (
2.8
) holds. Moreover,
(
B.14
) means that (
2.2
) is unchanged, and
X_{c}
is still the center manifold since
\Upsilon(x_{c})=x_{c}
. It follows that (
2.7
) is unchanged.
This completes the proof.
