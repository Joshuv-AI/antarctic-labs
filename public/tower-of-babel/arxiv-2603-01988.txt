Groups of generalized Moufang type and ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} -graded algebras.
Ilya Gorshkov

Abstract




Abstract.

A pair
(G,T)
is called a faithful odd transposition group if
T
is a normal set of involutions generating the group
G
and the product of any two distinct elements of
T
has odd order. We introduce a special subclass of such groups, a
generalized Moufang group of
p
-type
(or
GM(p)
-type), in which the product of any two distinct involutions from
T
has a fixed prime order
p
. For any such group
(G,T)
and a scalar parameter
\eta
in a field
\mathbb{F}
, we construct a non-associative, non-commutative algebra
A=A_{\mathbb{F}}(G,T,\eta)
.

We prove that every element of
T
considered as an element of the algebra
A
, is a primitive semisimple idempotent, defining a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading of
A
. The Miyamoto group of
A
with respect to
T
is isomorphic to
G/Z(G)
. The algebra
A
contains no nontrivial right ideals and, for a specific choice of the parameter
\eta
, admits a symmetric left Frobenius form.

When
G
is a free Burnside group of odd prime period
p
extended by an involutory automorphism, the finiteness of
G
is equivalent to the finite-dimensionality of
A_{\mathbb{F}}(G,T,\eta)
, providing a reformulation of the Burnside problem. For
p=5
and
\eta=-1/3
, the algebra generated by two idempotents from
T
is left-axial and satisfies the Monster-type fusion law
\mathcal{M}(4/3,-4/3)
. For a prime
p>5
, the two-generated algebra is also axial, but obeys a more general fusion law.

Although the algebra
A_{\mathbb{F}}(G,T,\eta)
is initially defined using a group
GM(p)
-type, we show that it admits an intrinsic, group-free characterization by axiomatizing a class of so-called
GM(p,\eta)
-type algebras. We prove that every algebra in this class is isomorphic to one arising from the construction above, establishing the equivalence of the two definitions.

Introduction

Let
G
be a group. If
G
is generated by a normal set of involutions
T
such that the order of the product of any two elements from
T
does not exceed
n
, then
(G,T)
is said to be an
n
-transposition group and elements of
T
are called
n
-involution.
Fischer
[
4
]
first studied
3
-transposition groups in the special case where the product of any two distinct
3
-transpositions has order
3
, such groups were called Moufang type groups. He showed that a finite group of Moufang type is soluble and has a
3
-group of index
2
. In the course of studying simple
3
-transposition groups, Fischer
[
5
]
found 3 simple sporadic groups
Fi_{22}
,
Fi_{23}
,
Fi_{24}
. Cuypers and Hall
[
6
]
explored the classification program and subsequently obtained a classification of finitely generated
3
-transposition groups. Their result implies that
3
-transposition groups are locally finite. It is known that the sporadic group Baby Monster is a
4
-transposition group. The largest sporadic group Monster is a
6
-transposition group and is generated by just three involutions.

Let
G
be a finite group and
\omega
be a set of positive integers. A pair
(G,T)
is called an
\omega
-transposition group if
T
is a normal set of involutions of
G
,
G=\langle T\rangle
, and
|uv|\in\omega
for any choice of distinct
u
and
v
in
T
.
Aschbacher
[
1
]
introduced the concept of odd transposition groups. If
\omega
is the set of all positive odd integers, then
\omega\cup\{2\}
-transposition group
(G,T)
is called an odd transposition group. Aschbacher described the odd transposition groups with trivial solvable radical
[
1
]
.

In this paper, we introduce the concept of faithful odd transposition groups. If
\omega
is the set of all positive odd integers, then
\omega
-transposition group
(G,T)
is called an faithful odd transposition group.
Let us define a special subclass of odd transposition groups. Faithful odd transposition group
(G,T)
is called a generalized Moufang group of type
n
(briefly
GM(n)
-type) if the order of the product of any two elements of
T
is
n
.

Using Glauberman’s
Z^{*}
-Theorem we get the following statement describing finite faithful odd transposition groups.

Theorem 1
.

Let
(G,T)
be a finite faithful odd transposition group. Then
G\simeq K(G)\rtimes\langle t\rangle
.

The free Burnside group of rank

and exponent

on
d
generators and

\{g^{n}\mid g\in F_{d}\}
in

. In 1902, Burnside
[
2
]
formulated the conjecture: the groups

are finite. He himself proved that the groups

and
B(d,3)
are finite for all
d
. In
[
7
]
, Sanov proved the finiteness of the group
B(d,4)
for all
d
. Hall
[
8
]
proved the finiteness of the group
B(d,6)
for all
d
.

Novikov and Adian
[
9
]
showed that the groups
B(d,n)
are infinite for all odd

and
d
greater than
1
. This result has been generalized many times. Atkarskaya, Rips and Tent
[
10
]
proved that
B(d,n)
is infinite for all odd
n\geq 557
. Lysenok
[
11
]
proved the infinity of groups
B(d,n)
for

where
k>8000
and

greater than
1
.

Let
G=\langle x_{1},....,x_{d}\rangle
be a free Burnside group of odd prime period
p
. Then
G
has an automorphism
t
of order two which inverts the free generators
x_{1},...,x_{d}
. Note that the group
G.\langle t\rangle
is generated by a set
T=\{t,x_{1}t,...,x_{d}t\}
of involutions. It is also easy to see that
t
and
x_{i}t
are conjugate in the dihedral group
\langle t,x_{i}t\rangle
. Thus
(G.\langle t\rangle,T)
is a group of

-type.

Theorem 2
.

Let
(G,T)
be an odd transposition group and let
G
have a bounded period. If
G/Z(G)
is a finite group and
T
is a finite set, then
G
is a finite group.

The theory of axial algebras represents a contemporary approach that reveals profound connections between group theory and non-associative algebras. Its origins lie in the construction of the Griess algebra, whose automorphism group is the sporadic Monster group.

Axial algebras were first introduced by
[
13
]
. An axial algebra is a commutative but non-associative algebra generated by a special set of idempotents called axes. For each axis
a
, the multiplication operator
\text{ad}_{a}
is semisimple, i.e. allowing the algebra to be decomposed into a direct sum of its eigenspaces.

The multiplication structure in the
\mathcal{F}
-axial algebra
A
is controlled by a fusion law
\mathcal{F}
. If the fusion law
\mathcal{F}
is
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded, then each axis
a
can be associated with
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading of
A
and an
A
automorphism
\tau_{a}
of order
2
, known as the Miyamoto automorphism. For a given generating set of axes
X
, the group generated by the corresponding Miyamoto automorphisms is called the Miyamoto group.

Rowen and Segev
[
12
]
study non-commutative axial algebras generated by primitive idempotents whose left and right multiplication operators are semisimple and possess at most three distinct eigenvalues.

By the term ”algebra” we mean a non-associative and non-commutative
\mathbb{F}
-algebra, where
\mathbb{F}
is a field of characteristic not
2
. In this paper, we construct algebras
A_{\mathbb{F}}(G,T,\eta)
associated with a group
GM(p)
-type
(G,T)
and parameter
\eta\in\mathbb{F}
.

Algebras constructed from groups of
GM(p)
-type
(G,T)
have dimension
|T|
and a Miyamoto group is isomorphic to
G/Z(G)
, where
Z(G)
is the center of
G
. The left multiplication operators by the generating idempotents have
4
eigenvalues and the entire algebra is the sum of eigenspaces. In particular, the following theorem is proved.

Theorem 3
.

Let
(G,T)
be a
GM(p)
-type group and
A=A_{\mathbb{F}}(G,T,\eta)
, where
\eta\in\mathbb{F}
be such that
1,\eta(p-2)+1,1-\eta
and
\eta-1
are distinct elements of
\mathbb{F}
. The following assertions hold:

1.

All elements of
T
are primitive left semisimple idempotents in
A
.

2.

If
\mathbb{F}
contains the
\frac{p-1}{2}
roots of
-1
, then any element
a\in T
is a right semisimple idempotent in
A
.

3.

A
contains no right ideals.

4.

For each idempotent
a\in T
, the decomposition
A=A^{+}_{a}\oplus A^{-}_{a}
defines a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading of
A
. With respect to these gradings
Miy(A,T)\simeq G/Z(G)
.

We say that the field
\mathbb{F}
has good characteristic relative to
(p,\eta)
if the elements
1,\eta(p-2)+1,\eta-1
and
1-\eta
are distinct.

We also show that for
\eta=-\frac{1}{3}
and a field
\mathbb{F}
of good characteristic relative to
(5,\eta)
, the two generated algebra
A_{\mathbb{F}}(G,T,\eta)
will be left axial with fusion law of Monster type.

Proposition 1
.

Let
(G,T)
be a two generated group of
GM(5)
-type,
\eta=-\frac{1}{3}
and
\mathbb{F}
is a field of good characteristic relative to
(5,\eta)
. Every idempotent in

is a left-axis of
\mathcal{M}(4/3,-4/3)
-type.

The next two propositions allow us to distinguish between algebras for group
GM(5)
-type and groups
GM(p)
-type, where

is a natural greater than

.

Proposition 2
.

Let
(G,T)
be a two generated group of
GM(p)
-type, where

,
\eta=-\frac{1}{p-2}
,
\mathbb{F}
is a field of characteristic good related to
(p,\eta)
, and

. Every idempotent in
T
is a left-axis of
\mathcal{GM}(\lambda,-\lambda)
-type.

Proposition 3
.

Let
(G,T)
be a group of
GM(p)
-type, where
p>5
,
\mathbb{F}
is a field,
\eta\in\mathbb{F}
, and characteristic of

is good related to
(p,\eta)
and

. All idempotents of

are not a left
\mathcal{M}(\alpha,-\alpha)
-axis for any value of the parameter

.

As a corollary of Theorems
2
and

, we obtain a connection between the dimension of the algebra
A_{\mathbb{F}}(B(n,p).\langle t\rangle,T,\eta)
and the order of the group

.

Corollary 1
.

Let
G=B(n,p).\langle t\rangle
, where
p
is a prime greater than
2
and
t
is an automorphism of order
2
of
B(n,p)
that inverts free generators,
T=t^{G}
,
\mathbb{F}
be a field of good characteristics relative to
(p,\eta)
. Then
Miy(A_{\mathbb{F}}(G,T,\eta),T)\simeq G/Z(G)
, where

is the center of

and

is finite-dimensional.

The obtained results allow us to reformulate Burnside’s conjecture on free Burnside groups of prime period
p
generated by
d
elements in terms of non-associative algebras.

Question 1
.

For what
d
and
p
does the algebra
A_{\mathbb{F}}(B(d,p).\langle t\rangle,T,\eta)
, where
t
and
T
are as in Corollary 1, have finite dimension?

The definition of algebras
A_{\mathbb{F}}(G,T,\eta)
-type involves a group of
GM(p)
-type, and in fact we define the structure of an algebra on the set of conjugate involutions. However, such a definition can be avoided. We define an algebra
A=A(T,\eta)
of
GM(\eta,p)
-type by defining multiplications on its basis
T
and defining some properties of the basis
T
. It follows directly from the definition that for any group
(G,T)
of
GM(p)
-type, the algebra
A_{\mathbb{F}}(G,T,\eta)
is an algebra of
GM(\eta,p)
-type. But we will also prove the converse.

Theorem 4
.

Let
A(T)
be an algebra of
GM(p,\eta)
-type. Then
(M,\{\tau_{t}|t\in T\})=Miy(A,T)
is a
GM(p)
-group and
A(T)\cong A_{\mathbb{F}}(M,\{\tau_{t}|t\in T\},\eta)
.

Thus, Burnside’s hypothesis on free Burnside groups of prime period
p
generated by
d
elements can be reformulated into a hypothesis that does not rely on the definition of a group.

Question 2
.

For which
p
does the algebra
GM(p,\eta)
-type generated by
d+1
elements, is finite dimensional?

A large number of methods have now been developed for finding the dimension of an algebra. This reformulation of Burnside’s conjecture opens up new approaches to its solution.

The paper is organized as follows. Section 1 presents preliminary results from group theory and proves Theorems 1 and 2. Section 2 provides basic definitions and preliminary results of non-associative algebras. In Section 3, Proposition 1, 2, and 3 are proved and useful results were obtained on
2

-type. In Section 4, Theorem 3 is proved. In Section 5 we define

-type algebras and prove Theorem 4.

Elements of Group Theory

Let
G
be a group and
a,b\in G
be involutions. We define the set
I(a,b)
of all involutions of the dihedral group
\langle a,b\rangle
generated by
a
and
b
.

Definition 1
.

A pair
(G,T)
is called a faithful odd transposition group if
G
is a periodic group and
T
is a normal subset of involutions of
G
such that
\langle T\rangle=G
, and
|ab|
for any
a,b\in T
is odd.

Let us introduce an important subclass of odd transposition groups.

Definition 2
.

A faithful odd transposition group
(G,T)
is called generalized Moufang type
n
, or
GM(n)
-type for short, if order of the product of any two distinct elements of
T
is
n
.

Note that if
n
is not a prime and the product of involutions
a
and
b
has order
n
, then in the set
I(a,b)
there exist two involutions
a^{\prime}
and
b^{\prime}
such that
|a^{\prime}b^{\prime}|<n
. Thus, if
(G,T)
is a
GM(n)
-type group, then
n
is a prime.

Definition 3
.

We say that
(G,T)
a group of
GM(n)
-type is generated by a set
D
if
D\subseteq T
and
\langle D\rangle=G
.

Let
G
be a finite group,
Z(G)
be the center of
G
. We define the core of
G
,
K(G)
, to be the largest normal subgroup of odd order in
G
. Define
Z^{*}(G)
to be the subgroup of
G
containing
K(G)
for which
Z^{*}(G)/K(G)=Z(G/K(G))
.

Lemma 1
.

[
3
, Corollary 1 (Glauberman’s
Z^{*}
-Theorem)]

Let
S
be a Sylow
2
-subgroup of a finite group
G
. Suppose
x\in S
. A necessary and sufficient condition for
x\in Z^{*}(G)
is that
C_{G}(x)\cap x^{G}=x
.

The following statement allows us to describe finite odd transposition groups.

Theorem 1.

Let
(G,T)
be a finite faithful odd transposition group. Then
G\simeq K(G)\rtimes\langle t\rangle
, where
|t|=2
.

Proof.

Suppose that there exists a Sylow
2
-subgroup of
G
containing two distinct commuting elements
a,b\in T
. But then
|ab|=2
; a contradiction with the fact that
(G,T)
is a faithful odd transposition group. It follows from Lemma
1
that
T\subseteq Z^{*}(G)
. Let
{}^{-}:G\rightarrow G/K(G)
be the natural homomorphism. Since
\langle T\rangle=G
, it follows that
\langle\overline{T}\rangle=\overline{G}
. Note that
\overline{T}
lies in
Z(\overline{G})
. Thus
\overline{G}
is an elementary abelian
2
-group. Suppose that
|\overline{G}|>2
. Let
a,b\in T
be such that
\overline{a}\neq\overline{b}
. We have
\overline{a}\overline{b}=\overline{ab}
, in particular
|\overline{ab}|=2
; a contradiction with the fact that
|ab|
is odd and divisible by
|\overline{ab}|
.

∎

Lemma 2

(Schreier’s Lemma
[
15
]
)
.

A finite index subgroup of a finitely generated group is finitely generated.

The following statement is a simple consequence of Schreier’s Lemma.

Theorem 2.

Let
(G,T)
be an odd transposition group and let
G
have a bounded period. If
G/Z(G)
is a finite group and
T
is a finite set, then
G
is a finite group.

Proof.

Lemma
2
implies that
Z(G)
is a finitely generated subgroup. A finitely generated abelian group of finite exponent is finite. Thus
|G|=|G/Z(G)||Z(G)|<\infty
.
∎
