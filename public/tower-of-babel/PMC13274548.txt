Phase 3 PERSPECTIVE study: Ibrutinib with rituximab for initial treatment of patients with follicular lymphoma
Authors: David Belada, Faithlore P. Gardner, Ana C. de Oliveira, Juan‐Manuel Sancho, Elza Lomaia, David Wright, Mehmet Turgut, Elena Martynova, Zsolt Lázár, Bertrand Anz, Ming‐Chung Wang, Wojciech Legiec, Stephen Opat, Junvie Pailden, Hillary M. Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractWe report primary analysis results from the Phase 3 PERSPECTIVE study comparing ibrutinib + rituximab versus placebo + rituximab in patients with previously untreated follicular lymphoma requiring treatment per Groupe d'Etude des Lymphomes Folliculaires criteria who were ineligible for chemoimmunotherapy due to age and/or comorbidities. The primary endpoint was investigator‐assessed progression‐free survival (PFS). Patients were randomly assigned 3:1 to receive ibrutinib (560 mg) or placebo once daily until progression, together with rituximab 375 mg/m2 weekly for 4 weeks, then every 8 weeks for 12 cycles. Overall, 445 patients were assigned to receive ibrutinib + rituximab (n = 334) or placebo + rituximab (n = 111). With a median follow‐up of 53.7 months, PFS was significantly improved with ibrutinib + rituximab versus placebo + rituximab (hazard ratio, 0.713 [95% CI, 0.532–0.955]; P = 0.0231; median 42.0 vs. 32.8 months), as was overall response rate (81% vs. 68%; rate ratio, 1.190 [95% CI, 1.039–1.364]; P = 0.004). Complete response rates also favored ibrutinib + rituximab (31% vs. 26%). Overall survival (OS) was immature and not significantly different between arms (hazard ratio 1.121 [95% CI, 0.771–1.631]; P = 0.5485). Grade ≥3 adverse events occurred in 78% versus 57% of patients with ibrutinib + rituximab versus placebo + rituximab, most commonly neutropenia (16% vs. 7%), pneumonia (9% vs. 5%), hypertension (8% vs. 5%), COVID‐19 (6% vs. 2%), COVID‐19 pneumonia (6% vs. 3%), and diarrhea (6% vs. 2%). In patients with previously untreated follicular lymphoma, adding ibrutinib to rituximab significantly improved PFS and response rates but did not improve OS. This trial was registered at www.clinicaltrials.gov, NCT02947347.

INTRODUCTION

Follicular lymphoma is the most common subtype of indolent non‐Hodgkin lymphoma and is often diagnosed at an advanced stage. 1 , 2 Standard first‐line treatment for advanced‐stage follicular lymphoma in fit patients with high tumor burden involves an anti‐CD20 antibody (rituximab or obinutuzumab) in combination with chemotherapy or lenalidomide.1, 2, 3 These regimens provide overall response rates (ORRs) of >80% and median progression‐free survival (PFS) of >5 years in patients with previously untreated follicular lymphoma, but are also associated with substantial toxicity and may be contraindicated in older patients and in those with comorbid conditions.4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 For patients who are older or patients with comorbidities that make them poor candidates for standard first‐line treatment with chemoimmunotherapy or lenalidomide + rituximab, treatment guidelines recommend single‐agent rituximab as first‐line treatment,1, 2, 3 yielding ORRs of 50%–70% and median event‐free survival of 1–3 years depending on the population studied.10, 11, 12 A chemotherapy‐free regimen with increased efficacy over single‐agent rituximab and with an acceptable safety profile, such as the combination of rituximab and ibrutinib, may be an attractive treatment option for such patients.

Since chronic activation of the B‐cell receptor signaling pathway has been implicated in the pathogenesis of follicular lymphoma, 13 , 14 downstream components of the B‐cell receptor signaling pathway, such as Bruton tyrosine kinase (BTK), represent rational therapeutic targets for novel treatment strategies. 15 Ibrutinib is a once‐daily BTK inhibitor approved in multiple regions for the treatment of various B‐cell malignancies and chronic graft‐versus‐host disease. Single‐agent ibrutinib has shown activity in patients with relapsed/refractory follicular lymphoma with ORRs of 21%–38%. 16 , 17 Additionally, the combination of ibrutinib with rituximab showed promising activity with durable responses in patients with previously untreated follicular lymphoma in Phase 2 studies. 18 , 19 With a median follow‐up of 34 months in the Phase 2 PCYC‐1125 study, ibrutinib + rituximab provided an ORR of 85% and a complete response rate of 40%. 18 PFS and overall survival (OS) rates at 30 months were 67% and 97%, respectively. 18 More recently, in the randomized Phase 2 SAKK 35/14 study, ibrutinib + rituximab showed no statistically significant improvement in the primary endpoint of complete remission rate at 24 months after randomization compared with placebo + rituximab, but PFS was significantly improved (3‐year PFS, 45% vs. 36%) and OS was favorable in both arms, supporting the ongoing use of rituximab as a therapeutic partner in clinical trials of novel combinations. 19 Promising activity was also recently reported with the BTK inhibitor acalabrutinib in combination with rituximab in a small number of patients with previously untreated follicular lymphoma in a Phase 1b/2 study. 20 Additionally, the BTK inhibitor zanubrutinib currently is approved in Europe and has US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) accelerated approval in combination with obinutuzumab for relapsed/refractory follicular lymphoma after ≥2 lines of systemic therapy based on results from the ROSEWOOD study. 21

Here, we report primary analysis results from the multinational, randomized, double‐blind, Phase 3 PERSPECTIVE study comparing ibrutinib + rituximab versus placebo + rituximab in patients with previously untreated follicular lymphoma requiring treatment per Groupe d'Etude des Lymphomes Folliculaires (GELF) criteria who were not eligible for chemoimmunotherapy due to age and/or comorbidities.

METHODS

Study design and treatment

In the Phase 3 PERSPECTIVE (PCYC‐1141) study, patients were randomly assigned in a 3:1 ratio (given the anticipated efficacy of BTK inhibition in this patient population) to receive oral ibrutinib (560 mg) or placebo once daily until progressive disease or unacceptable toxicity. All patients received rituximab 375 mg/m2 weekly for 4 weeks, then every 8 weeks for up to 12 cycles. 22 , 23 Dose modifications, including dose reductions (ibrutinib/placebo only) and treatment interruptions, were allowed for the management of adverse events (AEs). Randomization was stratified by age (60–69 vs. ≥70 years), Follicular Lymphoma International Prognostic Index (FLIPI)‐1 score (low vs. intermediate/high), and Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status score (0 or 1 vs. 2).

Patients

Eligible patients were aged ≥70 years, or 60–69 years with ≥1 comorbidity making them a candidate for single‐agent rituximab therapy (i.e., creatinine clearance 30–59 mL/min and/or ECOG performance status score of 2); had histologically documented CD20+ follicular lymphoma of Grade 1, 2, or 3a per World Health Organization (WHO) criteria and Ann Arbor Stage II, III, or IV disease; met ≥1 GELF criterion for treatment 24 ; and had not received prior treatment for follicular lymphoma (including systemic anticancer therapy or radiotherapy). Patients were also required to have ≥1 radiographically measurable lesion of ≥2.0 cm in the longest diameter and adequate hematologic and end‐organ function. Patients were excluded if they had transformed lymphoma, central nervous system involvement, or leptomeningeal disease.

Outcomes and assessments

The primary endpoint was PFS (time from randomization until disease progression or death from any cause, whichever occurred first), assessed by investigators according to Lugano 2014 criteria, 25 with an independent review committee (IRC) assessment for sensitivity analysis. Secondary endpoints were ORR (best response of complete or partial response by investigator assessment per Lugano 2014 criteria), OS (time from randomization until death from any cause), infusion‐related reaction (IRR) rates, duration of response (time from first documentation of response until disease progression or death, whichever occurs first), and safety (frequency, severity, seriousness, and relatedness of AEs). Patient‐reported lymphoma symptoms and health‐related quality of life as measured by the Functional Assessment of Cancer Therapy‐Lymphoma (FACT‐Lym) were exploratory endpoints. Time to next treatment (TTNT; time from randomization until initiation of subsequent anticancer treatment, censored at the last follow‐up date for subsequent anticancer treatment) was a post hoc exploratory endpoint.

Response and progression were assessed using computed tomography (with contrast, unless contraindicated) of the neck, chest, abdomen, and pelvis, and any other disease sites at baseline, every 16 weeks from cycles 5 through 37, and every 24 weeks thereafter until disease progression. Magnetic resonance imaging could be used for lesions not well visualized by computed tomography. Positron emission tomography (PET) was mandatory at baseline and was required at the time of maximal tumor reduction (as determined by the investigator based on sequential assessments) to confirm a complete response in patients with a positive PET scan at screening. Complete response also required a negative bone marrow biopsy if bone marrow involvement was positive or indeterminate at screening.

Safety was assessed by patient‐reported symptoms, laboratory tests (hematology and chemistry), ECOG performance status, physical examination, and vital signs at each study visit during and up to 30 days after the last dose of study treatment. AEs were graded according to Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events, version 4.03.

Statistical analysis

The sample size was calculated based on the primary endpoint of PFS. Enrollment of 440 patients was estimated to be necessary to accrue 200 PFS events, providing approximately 87% power to detect a hazard ratio (HR) of 0.600 at a 1‐sided significance level of 0.025 in a 3:1 randomization ratio, assuming a median PFS of 28 months for placebo + rituximab. 26 Primary analysis used censoring rules aligned with the US FDA, whereby patients without progression or death, with subsequent anticancer therapy, or missing ≥2 consecutive assessments, were censored at the last adequate disease assessment. Sensitivity analyses of PFS were performed by IRC assessment using FDA censoring rules, and by investigator and IRC assessment using global censoring rules. Per global censoring rules, patients without progression or death, or with subsequent anticancer therapy, were censored at the last adequate disease assessment. If the primary endpoint comparison of PFS demonstrated a statistically significant difference between arms, then secondary endpoints to be tested hierarchically were ORR, followed by OS, then IRR.

Follow‐up and time‐to‐event endpoints were estimated using Kaplan–Meier methodology. HRs were estimated using stratified Cox regression modeling, and 2‐sided P values were calculated by a stratified log‐rank test. Response rates were compared using rate ratios based on the Mantel–Haenszel estimator (pooled rate ratio across prespecified stratification factors), and 2‐sided P values were calculated using a stratified Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel chi‐square test. IRR‐related AEs were compared using the rate ratio, and the 2‐sided P value was calculated using the chi‐square test. Baseline characteristics and safety were summarized descriptively.

Efficacy was evaluated in the intent‐to‐treat (ITT) population, comprising all randomly assigned patients. Safety was evaluated in the safety population, including all patients who received ≥1 dose of study treatment. Prespecified exploratory subgroup analyses were performed according to age (<65 vs. ≥65 years, <70 vs. ≥70 years, and <75 vs. ≥75 years), sex (male vs. female), race (White vs. non‐White), geographic region (US vs. non‐US), FLIPI‐1 score (low/intermediate vs. high), ECOG performance status (0 or 1 vs. 2), WHO grade (1 vs. 2 vs. 3a), Ann Arbor stage (II vs. III vs. IV), and number of nodal areas involved (≤4 vs. ≥5).

RESULTS

Patients

Between February 2017 and July 2021, 445 patients in the ITT population were enrolled and randomly assigned (3:1 ratio) to receive ibrutinib + rituximab (n = 334) or placebo + rituximab (n = 111) (Figure 1). The safety population included all patients who received ≥1 dose of study drug in the ibrutinib + rituximab arm (n = 330) and in the placebo + rituximab arm (n = 111). Baseline characteristics were generally balanced across treatment arms (Table 1). In the ibrutinib + rituximab versus placebo + rituximab arms, respectively, median age was 74 versus 75 years, 66% (221/334) versus 67% (74/111) of patients had a FLIPI‐1 score of ≥3, and 75% (250/334) versus 76% (84/111) of patients had an ECOG performance status score of 0 or 1 (Table 1).

At data cutoff (February 21, 2024), median follow‐up was 53.7 months (range, 0.03–82.8). Treatment was ongoing with single‐agent ibrutinib in 67 of 334 patients (20%) in the ibrutinib + rituximab arm and with placebo in 24 of 111 patients (22%) in the placebo + rituximab arm (Figure 1). Ibrutinib had been discontinued in 263 of 334 patients (79%), and placebo had been discontinued in 87 of 111 patients (78%), respectively. Planned rituximab treatment had been completed in 166 of 334 patients (50%) in the ibrutinib + rituximab arm and in 55 of 111 patients (50%) in the placebo + rituximab arm. Rituximab treatment had been discontinued early in 164 of 334 patients (49%) and in 56 of 111 patients (50%), respectively. Four of 334 patients (1%) in the ibrutinib + rituximab arm did not receive any study treatment. The overall median duration of treatment was 22.1 months (range, 0.03–82.3) in the ibrutinib + rituximab arm and 22.1 months (range, 0.4–71.2) in the placebo + rituximab arm; the median duration of treatment with ibrutinib or placebo was 18.8 months (range, 0.03–82.3) and 21.5 months (range, 0.4–71.2), respectively, and the median duration of treatment with rituximab was 21.5 months (range, 0.03–24.0) and 20.5 months (range, 0.03–24.5), respectively.

Efficacy

At data cutoff, in the primary analysis of PFS using FDA censoring rules, disease progression or death was observed in 44% of patients (147/334) in the ibrutinib + rituximab arm and in 59% of patients (65/111 patients) in the placebo + rituximab arm; transformation to more aggressive histologies occurred in 5% (16/334) and 8% (9/111) of patients, respectively. PFS by investigator assessment was significantly improved with ibrutinib + rituximab (median 42.0 months; 95% CI, 33.3–50.8) compared with placebo + rituximab (median 32.8 months; 95% CI, 22.0–38.7), with an HR of 0.713 (95% CI, 0.532–0.955; P = 0.0231) (Figure 2A). Estimated PFS rates were 79% (95% CI, 74–84) versus 75% (95% CI, 65–82) at 1 year, 64% (95% CI, 58–70) versus 60% (95% CI, 50–69) at 2 years, 54% (95% CI, 48–60) versus 42% (32–52) at 3 years, and 45% (95% CI, 38–51) versus 32% (95% CI, 22–43) at 4 years. Consistent results were observed in sensitivity analyses of PFS by IRC assessment using FDA censoring rules (Figure 2B) and by investigator and IRC assessments using global censoring rules (Figure S1A,B). The PFS benefit observed overall was consistent across prespecified patient subgroups (Figure 2C) based on demographic and disease characteristics. Furthermore, in each prespecified subgroup, the observed PFS benefit was consistent between subgroup levels as indicated by the lack of “treatment‐by‐subgroup interaction” after multiplicity adjustments (Figure 2C).

At data cutoff, death had occurred in 34% of patients (114/334) in the ibrutinib + rituximab arm and in 32% of patients (36/111) in the placebo + rituximab arm. Median OS was not reached in either arm, and there was no statistically significant difference in OS between arms, with an HR of 1.121 (95% CI, 0.771–1.631; P = 0.5485) for ibrutinib + rituximab versus placebo + rituximab. Estimated 4‐year OS rates were 67% (95% CI, 61–72) with ibrutinib + rituximab and 71% (95% CI, 61–79) with placebo + rituximab (Figure 3B). Primary causes of death in the ibrutinib + rituximab versus placebo + rituximab arms included AEs (14% [47/334] vs. 7% [8/111]) and underlying disease (follicular lymphoma; 7% [23/334] vs. 9% [10/111]) (Table S1). In post hoc analysis, median TTNT was not reached in the ibrutinib + rituximab arm and was 43.1 months in the placebo + rituximab arm, with an HR of 0.510 (95% CI, 0.359–0.724; nominal P = 0.0001). Subsequent anticancer therapy after discontinuation of study treatment due to disease progression, AEs, or investigator/patient decision was reported in 34% of patients (78/231) in the ibrutinib + rituximab arm and in 61% of patients (51/83) in the placebo + rituximab arm (details of all subsequent anticancer therapies received on study are in Table S2).

In the ITT population, the prespecified secondary endpoint of IRRs (as assessed by the investigator) occurred in 21% of patients (71/334) in the ibrutinib + rituximab arm compared with 27% of patients (30/111) in the placebo + rituximab arm, with a rate ratio of 0.787 (95% CI, 0.544–1.137) that was not nominally significant (P = 0.2087). IRRs and IRR‐related AEs by preferred term and maximum grade in the safety population are reported in Table S3.

No statistically significant differences were observed between arms in FACT‐Lym total or subscale scores at any time point.

Safety

All other safety events were evaluated in the safety population. Treatment‐emergent AEs (TEAEs) of any grade occurred in 98% of patients (324/330) in the ibrutinib + rituximab arm and in 95% of patients (106/111) in the placebo + rituximab arm. The most common TEAEs (occurring in ≥15% of patients in either arm) are reported in Table 2. Grade ≥3 TEAEs occurred in 78% of patients (259/330) with ibrutinib + rituximab and in 57% of patients (63/111) with placebo + rituximab; the most common (occurring in ≥5% of patients in either arm) were neutropenia (16% [52/330] vs. 7% [8/111]), pneumonia (9% [30/330] vs. 5% [5/111]), hypertension (8% [27/330] vs. 5% [6/111]), COVID‐19 (6% [21/330] vs. 2% [2/111]), COVID‐19 pneumonia (6% [21/330] vs. 3% [3/111]), and diarrhea (6% [21/330] vs. 2% [2/111]). Grade ≥3 atrial fibrillation occurred in 5% of patients (15/330) with ibrutinib + rituximab and in 2% of patients (2/111) with placebo + rituximab. Concomitant growth factors were used in 12% of patients (38/330) in the ibrutinib + rituximab arm and in 7% of patients (8/111) in the placebo + rituximab arm.

COVID‐19 events and impact on efficacy endpoints

Enrollment occurred between February 2017 and July 2021, encompassing the start and height of the COVID‐19 pandemic. Overall, 73% of randomized patients (327/445) received doses of ≥1 study drug (rituximab and/or ibrutinib/placebo) during the pandemic, including 245 patients in the ibrutinib + rituximab arm and 82 patients in the placebo + rituximab arm. In the safety population (n = 441), ≥1 dose of a COVID‐19 vaccine was received by 39% of patients (129/330) in the ibrutinib + rituximab arm and 42% of patients (47/111) in the placebo + rituximab arm.

In the safety population, COVID‐19 AEs of any grade occurred in 28% of patients (92/330) in the ibrutinib + rituximab arm and in 23% of patients (26/111) in the placebo + rituximab arm; Grade ≥3 COVID‐19 AEs occurred in 12% (38/330) and 4% (4/111) of patients, respectively. Recommended antiviral and/or monoclonal antibody treatments were received by 33% (33/101) and 30% of patients (10/33), respectively, among patients diagnosed with COVID‐19 infection or who experienced COVID‐19–related death. In the ITT population, COVID‐19–related death occurred at any time during the study (including those reported after the treatment‐emergent safety period) in 9% (30/334) and 7% (8/111) of patients, respectively.

To evaluate the impact of COVID‐19 on PFS, sensitivity analysis of PFS in the ITT population was performed with censoring for COVID‐19 deaths and FDA censoring rules. Median PFS by investigator assessment was 50.8 months (95% CI, 42.0–NE) with ibrutinib + rituximab compared with 32.8 months (95% CI, 25.8–38.7) with placebo + rituximab, with an HR of 0.619 (95% CI, 0.458–0.837; P = 0.0017) (Figure S2A). In sensitivity analysis of OS with censoring for COVID‐19 deaths, the HR was 1.062 (95% CI, 0.692–1.629; P = 0.7811) with estimated 4‐year OS rates of 75% (95% CI, 70–80) with ibrutinib + rituximab versus 76% (95% CI, 67–84) with placebo + rituximab (Figure S2B).

DISCUSSION

The PERSPECTIVE study met its primary endpoint, with the addition of ibrutinib to rituximab demonstrating a statistically significant improvement in PFS in older or unfit patients with previously untreated follicular lymphoma for whom rituximab monotherapy is appropriate. The median PFS of 42.0 months achieved with ibrutinib + rituximab in this population was substantially increased compared with the median PFS of 32.8 months in the placebo + rituximab arm. Addition of ibrutinib to rituximab also conferred a significant improvement in ORR, but without improvement in OS, and increased the incidence of Grade ≥3 AEs.

To our knowledge, this is the first randomized, placebo‐controlled Phase 3 trial to show a significant improvement in PFS with a BTK inhibitor in the first‐line treatment of follicular lymphoma for an older, frail patient population with comorbidities rendering them ineligible for chemoimmunotherapy and for whom rituximab monotherapy is an appropriate treatment option. In the relapsed/refractory setting, addition of ibrutinib to chemoimmunotherapy resulted in a numerical but not statistically significant improvement in PFS in the Phase 3 SELENE study, possibly because of a variable treatment effect over time. 27 This trial also provides important data on rituximab monotherapy in this specific patient population (older adults with meaningful comorbidities) in that results observed in the placebo + rituximab arm are consistent with those seen in other trials using rituximab monotherapy in patients with previously untreated follicular lymphoma. Median PFS in the placebo + rituximab arm (32.8 months; 95% CI, 22.0–38.7) aligns with that reported in the rituximab monotherapy arm of the SAKK 35/14 study, which included a broader population of adult patients (≥18 years) with previously untreated follicular lymphoma (32.7 months; 95% CI, 25–55). 19 Efficacy results for the placebo + rituximab arm in this study are also consistent with previous studies reporting a median PFS of approximately 30 months with rituximab monotherapy in patients with previously untreated follicular lymphoma. 26 , 28 In patients with previously untreated follicular lymphoma, chemotherapy‐free regimens combining lenalidomide with rituximab or obinutuzumab, with or without a BTK inhibitor, have demonstrated high response rates and PFS outcomes comparable to those obtained with chemoimmunotherapy9, 29, 30, 31, 32; however, these regimens were evaluated in patient populations that were younger and fitter than the patient population in the current study, and the tolerability profile of lenalidomide‐based regimens may not be suitable for older patients or those with comorbidities.

The PFS results from PERSPECTIVE were consistent across sensitivity analyses, demonstrating the robustness of the ibrutinib treatment effect. The median TTNT was also longer in the ibrutinib + rituximab arm. OS was not significantly different between the ibrutinib + rituximab and placebo + rituximab arms in the current study, with a slightly higher frequency of deaths observed in the ibrutinib + rituximab arm. OS benefit is not typically observed in first‐line follicular lymphoma studies due to the indolent nature of the disease and the effect of subsequent lines of treatment.22, 33, 34, 35, 36 It is uncertain to what extent OS results may have been impacted by a higher percentage of patients in the placebo‐containing arm receiving subsequent treatment (34% in the ibrutinib + rituximab arm vs. 61% in the placebo + rituximab arm).

In a previous study of first‐line treatment for patients with chronic lymphocytic leukemia treated with an anti‐CD20 antibody (obinutuzumab), IRR rates were substantially reduced by concomitant treatment with ibrutinib compared with concomitant treatment with chlorambucil. 37 Although less pronounced, the non‐significant trend for reduced IRR rates (from 27% with placebo + rituximab to 21% with ibrutinib + rituximab) in the current study is consistent with this previously identified effect, which is thought to be due to ibrutinib‐mediated reduction in the release of inflammatory cytokines and chemokines. 37

Overall, the safety profile of ibrutinib + rituximab was consistent with the known safety profiles of each agent used individually, and no unexpected safety signals were reported. The incidence of Grade ≥3 AEs was higher with the ibrutinib + rituximab combination compared with rituximab alone (78% vs. 57%), largely due to a higher incidence of COVID‐19‐related AEs, as was the incidence of AEs leading to discontinuation of either or both study treatments (44% vs. 14%). This is consistent with previous results suggesting that older patients with a high comorbidity burden have higher rates of AEs leading to discontinuation of ibrutinib. 38 , 39 Importantly, the incidence of atrial fibrillation, one of the most frequent AEs leading to discontinuation of ibrutinib in this study, was in line with that previously reported with ibrutinib in older adults. 38 , 39 Additionally, a greater proportion of patients in the ibrutinib + rituximab arm had AEs leading to death compared with patients in the placebo + rituximab arm (15% vs. 5%). These risks need to be weighed against the statistically significant and clinically meaningful efficacy benefits observed with the ibrutinib + rituximab combination.

This study was impacted by the COVID‐19 pandemic, which may limit the generalizability of these results to nonpandemic conditions for these patients who are at increased risk for COVID‐19 infection due to their malignancy and these study treatments. The majority of patients were receiving study treatment during the COVID‐19 pandemic (73%). Importantly, the COVID‐19 vaccine was not widely available until Q2–Q3 2021 globally, and patients with hematologic malignancies are known to be at high risk for poor serologic responses to vaccination, due to both the malignancy and associated therapies, including anti‐CD20 agents and BTK inhibitors. Notably, modest fractions of patients received at least 1 dose of vaccine (39% in the ibrutinib + rituximab arm and 42% in the placebo + rituximab arm). Similarly, effective antiviral and/or monoclonal antibody treatments for COVID‐19 became available on a delayed basis during the pandemic and were administered to 33% and 30% of patients after diagnosis of COVID‐19 infection in the ibrutinib + rituximab and placebo + rituximab arms, respectively. Lastly, the age and the comorbidities of this enrolled patient population are associated with worse outcomes among patients with COVID‐19 infections. 40

Consistent with other studies conducted during the global pandemic that included a BTK inhibitor and an anti‐CD20 antibody, Grade ≥3 AEs related to COVID‐19 were more frequent in the BTK inhibitor–containing arm. 21 , 41 In the context of the above, in the ITT population, COVID‐19–related deaths were observed in 9% of patients in the ibrutinib + rituximab arm and 7% of those in the placebo + rituximab arm. Similar to findings from previous Phase 3 studies of BTK inhibitors (acalabrutinib and zanubrutinib) in lymphoid malignancies,41, 42, 43 censoring for COVID‐19 deaths in this study resulted in longer estimated median PFS in the ibrutinib + rituximab arm, a greater estimated PFS treatment effect, and similar effects favoring improved OS outcomes with ibrutinib.

Limitations of this study include the use of disease response assessments that were primarily based on computed tomography, with PET and bone marrow biopsies used for confirmation of complete response. Considering changes in the standard of care to emphasize metabolic response, the complete response rate may have been underestimated in the current study. Additionally, the trial enrolled an older patient population with comorbidities; although definitions were specified in the inclusion criteria, the trial relied on physician discretion to apply these criteria and determine that patients were not fit for chemoimmunotherapy.

In conclusion, primary analysis results from the Phase 3 PERSPECTIVE study show that the addition of ibrutinib to rituximab significantly extended PFS compared with rituximab alone, with a safety profile consistent with the known safety profiles of ibrutinib and rituximab. This study further supports the effectiveness of ibrutinib + rituximab for the treatment of follicular lymphoma, particularly among patients who are not eligible for aggressive chemoimmunotherapy regimens due to age and/or comorbidities. However, given the absence of an OS benefit and a high rate of Grade ≥3 AEs, the benefits must be weighed against the risks, and a strong recommendation cannot be made.
