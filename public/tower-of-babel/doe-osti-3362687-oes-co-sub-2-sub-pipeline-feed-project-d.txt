1 INTRODUCTION
This Design Basis Memorandum (DBM) establishes the basis for key functional and technical design
parameters and information for use in the design and engineering of the Trimeric One Earth Sequestration
(OES) CO2 Pipeline Project, specific to the Pipeline scope of design.
The DBM will be revised as required to include the latest pipeline design-related information as needed. In
addition to providing essential technical documentation for the project, this DBM shall guide the Company
and its project team in their work activities and production of engineering and design deliverables.
 1.1 PROJECT OVERVIEW
 OES plans to develop a new CO2 capture, transportation, and sequestration system. The Project will
 capture CO2 from 2 sources In Illinois Northwest of Gibson City and deliver the CO2 to three injection
 sites in Illinois Southeast of Anchor city for permanent geological sequestration.
 Initially the project will source CO2 from ethanol production facilities.
 CO2 from each source will be captured, dehydrated, and compressed to pipeline‐quality dense phase
 conditions for injection into the pipeline system. The mainline pipeline will run from Westest of Gibson
 City to the area Southeast of Anchor city, where the pipeline will deliver CO2 to multiple injection wells
 for permanent geologic sequestration.
 1.2 SCOPE OF WORK
 The scope of work for the Project addressed within this design basis is the engineering and design, right
 of way (ROW) services, civil survey services, and environmental services associated with the project
 objectives. The battery limits of this scope basis document are the outlet flange of the OES meter run
 into the PL and the inlet flange of the delivery meter at each sequestration well, if said meter exists.
 Otherwise, the scope ends at the wellhead wing valve. The current scope does not include recovery,
 treating, gathering, metering or compression of the CO2; the pipeline will receive pipeline‐quality dense
 phase CO2 from the OES capture facility.
 The battery limits which shall define scope delineation between Pipeline Scope and Other Scope are as
 follows (also see Error! Reference source not found.):
 • Launcher / Receiver Sites
 o A launcher will be installed at the inlet to the pipeline from the combined 4.5
 MMTPA stream at OES from One Earth Energy and others.
 o A receiver will be installed at the end of the PL at OES Injection Well #3.
 o Short laterals mostly on the well pads between the mainline and the injection wells
 will not require pigging.

6.1.2 Fracture Control
The pipelines will be designed to mitigate the risk of running ductile fracture. A ductile fracture
mitigation study will be performed during Detailed Design phase to determine minimum required
toughness of the line pipe material. The line pipe specification shall include the minimum
toughness values for each line size to protect the pipe from running ductile fracture. Crack
arrestors may be considered as mitigation to any changes, re‐use of existing off‐spec line pipe, or
for select areas identified as necessary where the pipe material does not meet the requirements
determined during the final fracture mechanics assessment.
6.1.3 Pipe Coating
External coatings will be provided in accordance with 49 CFR 195, Subpart H, Corrosion Control.
The exterior of the line pipe will be coated with a single layer of Fusion‐Bonded Epoxy (FBE) 14‐
16 mils in thickness. An additional layer of Abrasion Resistant Overcoat (ARO) will be applied
over the FBE coating for bores and horizontal directional drills. Thickness of the ARO is 30‐40
mils.
Field coating application, including girth weld areas, girth weld at directional drill or bore pipe, and
coating repairs shall be defined in project specifications to be developed during the Detailed
Design phase.

      6.1.4   Valves
 All valves shall meet the minimum requirements of API 6D. Flanges on valves shall be of raised
 face construction with surface quality and physical dimensions as specified in ASME B16.5 for
 ASME Class 900 service, suitable for -50OF ambient temperature
 Mainline valves shall be flanged both ends to facilitate removal and replacement if necessary,
 during the life of the system.
 Applications with a need for critical shut‐off will be an plug or ball valves. Currently these locations
 have been identified by OES as launcher outlet, receiver inlet, and trap bypass valves.
 Valve actuators will be suitable for the valve types chosen, whether quarter‐turn (e.g., ball valves)
 or gate valves. Depending upon valve size and power availability, actuators will be motor
 operated and supplied by three‐phase (e.g., 480 VAC, 60 Hz) or by split phase (e.g., 230 VAC, 60
 Hz). Actuator size/horsepower will be selected based upon closing and breakaway torque needs
 and required speed of valve closure.
 Special attention shall be given to valve material selection where high differential pressure may be
 generated, specifically for blowdown and equalization valves. Sacrificial valves may be
 considered for these applications with permanently installed isolation valves between any
 sacrificial components and the pipeline.
 Isolation valves of 10” and larger sizes will be equipped with actuators. All actuated valves shall
 be ordered with provision for remote actuation and position indication.
6.2 DEPTH OF COVER
 Minimum depth of cover shall be 6 feet throughout the pipeline system and increased to 5 feet in
 cultivated fields. Cover depths for specific crossings can be found in those respective sections.
 Depth of cover may be increased if determined to be necessary based on landowner input.

6.4 BENDING
 6.4.1 Field Bends
 Cold bends are to be field fabricated in accordance with the provisions outlined in 49 CFR, Part
 195, ASME B31.4, and Project Specification. Cold bends will not be bent more than 1.5 degrees
 per pipe diameter as measured along the pipe axis. All field bends (or PIs) over the selected
 maximum deflection angle of 30 degrees will be assumed as an induction bend or segmentable
 fitting.
 6.4.2 Induction Bends
 Induction bends shall be consistent with ASME B16.49 ‐ Factory–Made Wrought Steel
 Buttwelding Induction Bends for Transportation and Distribution Systems.
 Induction bends shall have a minimum bend radius of five (5) pipe diameters (5D) with two (2) foot
 tangents on each side of the bend.
 Segmentable fittings shall have a minimum bend radius of three (3) pipe diameters (3D) with two
 (2) foot tangents on each end.
6.5 WELDING
 All welding of the mainline, and associated Launchers, Receivers, and mainline valve station
 piping will be in accordance with API‐1104 and Project Specifications.
6.6 NON‐DESTRUCTIVE EXAMINATION (NDE)
 During construction, 100% of the field welds will be inspected. The preferred methods of NDE
 (i.e., ultrasonic, x‐ray, etc.) for the different types of welds will be evaluated and selected during

6.7.2 Water Bodies & Wetlands
Special construction procedures for working in water bodies and wetlands will be required to
minimize potential damage to these fragile ecosystem areas. Where open trench construction is
used, exterior weights may be used to provide negative buoyancy.
Water crossings are installed at a depth in order to provide the minimum depths of cover of 4 feet
at the lowest point of the stream or pond bed. Greater installation depth may be specified within
the crossing permit.
6.7.3 Highways & Roads
Road or highway crossings will be designed and installed per state and county permitting
requirements.
At road crossings, heavier wall pipe will be installed within the road easement limits and will run to
the property line or to the extreme edge of the road right‐of‐way. The selected heavy‐wall pipe
selection for roadway crossings will be checked in accordance with the API‐1102 Procedure.
 6.7.3.1 Open Cut Crossings
 For private, dirt, or unimproved roads (not major roadways), the pipeline will be installed with 4
 feet of cover (minimum) by open‐cut method or as required by the local County permit or
 landowner agreements.
 6.7.3.2 Bore Crossings
 For roads requiring bored crossing methods, the design and installation shall be in accordance
 with local County permit agreements.
6.7.4 Wetland Crossings
Wetland crossing pipe sections will be designed to the 0.72 design factor, where class location
factor permits, and will utilize buoyancy control measures. Refer to “Buoyancy Control” section for
the minimum negative buoyancy requirements. Minimum depth of cover in wetlands will be three
(3) feet if conventionally laid, and 15 feet when bored.
Buoyancy control shall be used when the pipeline crosses through the following wetland areas:
 • Wetlands typically containing saturated soils or standing water.
 • Dry wetlands (usually no standing water or saturated soil) with soils that are not
 dense enough when saturated to resist the buoyant force of the pipe.

                                     Page 14 of 20

 • Other areas found, for any combination of reasons, to be conducive of pipeline
 buoyancy, such as uplands with high water tables and low soil strength or density
 6.7.5 Waterbody Crossings
 A waterbody is defined as the full width, including the beds, banks, sides, shoreline, and any other
 part of a river, creek, stream, spring, brook, lake, pond, reservoir, canal, bayou, drain, or other
 natural channel open to the atmosphere, the primary function of which is the conveyance or
 containment of water, whether the flow be continuous or not.
 At conventional lay waterbody crossing locations the depth of cover shall be 5-feet below the
 lowest existing section of the crossing. Where concrete coating or weights are applied, the depth
 of cover shall be measured from the top of pipe to the lowest point on the river bottom or
 streambed.
 The HDD method is proposed for all major watercourses for this project.
 6.8 HYDROSTATIC TESTING
 Hydrostatic testing will follow the requirements of 49 CFR 195, Subpart E ‐ Pressure Testing and ASME
 B31.4.
 Above ground and shop fabricated piping shall be tested to the pressure designated on the included
 piping specifications for a minimum of four (4) hours. Below ground piping shall be tested for a minimum
 of eight (8) hours.
 Pipe sections welded together for HDD installation will be hydrostatically tested for a minimum of one (1)
 hour prior to the pull‐back operation to ensure the line segment meets all integrity requirements.
 A Hydrostatic Test Plan will be developed to define the test limits (which account for elevation
 differences), pressures, and procedures for each test segment. The testing pressures for the mainline
 will be at least 1.25 times the maximum design pressure. The maximum testing pressure at any point in
 the test section will be limited to the pressure that produces the hoop stress equal to 100% SMYS of
 pipe material.
 After completion of hydrostatic testing, the line shall be dewatered and dried until the dewpoint
 temperature of the line reaches a minimum of ‐40OF. Methods and limits for drying and cleaning shall be
 evaluated during detail design.

8 BUOYANCY CONTROL
 When designing buoyancy control, the Engineer shall consider the type of terrain, construction season
 and feasibility of site access, construction and equipment limitations, ease of handling during
 transport, and availability of materials for field fabrication of weights.
 Reference materials, including delineated wetlands from field environmental surveys and construction

 A minimum negative buoyancy design factor of 1.25 for fresh water shall be used for the pipeline when
 crossing saturated areas and flood plains. This will be accomplished by using concrete coated pipe or
 geotextile bag weights.

 10.2 SOIL EROSION AND SEDIMENT CONTROL
 A Soil Erosion and Sediment Control Plan should be developed with proper consideration of

 pipe as allowed by landowners and stakeholders. They will be clearly identified in advance on alignment
 sheets. Terms and duration will be stated in ROW lease agreements or amendments thereto.

 A topographic survey shall be performed by a Registered Professional Land Surveyor (RPLS) to
 determine natural and man‐made physical features and site drainage patterns of the property.
 Topographic survey shall include as a minimum property lines, contours, vegetation, existing structures
 above and below ground including power poles, power line, pipelines, utilities, roads, culvert, ditches,
 and fences and inverts of roadside ditches within the property. In addition, topographic survey should
 include culverts, roads, utilities, or any other features adjacent to the property that may impact the
 design of the facility. Topographic survey shall identify road names, North arrow and description of
 benchmarks used for the survey.
 The Pipeline Environmental Services contractor(s) shall perform all requisite surveys to support
 acquisition of required permits. Data from these surveys and permits including delineations, seasonal
 limitations, restoration/revegetation, shall be communicated to the engineer and incorporated in the
 construction drawings.
11 ELECTRICAL & INSTRUMENTATION
Electrical design will be based on NEC 70, NEMA, ANSI and IEEE requirements.
Each actuated MLV site with commercial utility service will have a pole mounted transformer (supplied by
the utility) with fused cut‐out to accommodate the electrical loads. The actuated and instrumented MLV
sites will utilize outdoor equipment, mounted on a switch‐rack.
Electrical requirements within the scope of the pipeline design will not exceed low voltage e.g., 480 VAC
three phase for larger valve actuators. Smaller actuators may be powered by single phase or split phase
AC. Standby power will be considered for critical (e.g., HCA) valve locations.
Local solar power generation and storage will be considered for MLV sites remote from available utility
supply. Solar shall be considered for facilities with monitoring instrumentation but no additional loads.
Rectifiers shall be located at planned pipeline facilities with available utility power where possible. In the
event rectifiers must be located separate from planned facilities, consideration shall be given to utility
power availability when determining locations.
 11.1 AREA CLASSIFICATION
 The product stream is not hazardous and therefore hazardous electrical area classification around the
 launchers, receivers, and mainline valves will not be required. If fuel for backup generation introduces
 hazardous area concerns, area classification will be evaluated at that time.
 11.2 POWER SYSTEMS STUDY
 During the Detailed Design phase, a study will be performed to set design requirements for mainline
 valve sites. Power required at each site shall be determined and appropriate source(s) for that power
 will be designed. At sites where the valve will be operated manually, solar power may be the best
 choice for the instrumentation and communication required. At sites where the valve will be operated by
 an actuator, each site will be evaluated for source requirements and designed accordingly.
 11.3 BACKUP POWER SUPPLY SYSTEM
 Utility Power at each facility (i.e., MLV sites) will be evaluated on a case‐by‐case basis. If utility power is
 not economically available, sites shall be designed with local solar generation and battery storage.

 11.4 INSTRUMENTATION DESIGN
 Pressure and temperature indication will be provided at sites, including pressure indication on both sides
 of all mainline valves and on all trap barrels. Where power and SCADA are available, indicating
 transmitters shall be installed for remote monitoring. Indicating transmitters may be installed at sites
 without utility power or actuation requirements if determined necessary for monitoring and control of the
 system; this determination shall be made in consultation with the OES Automation and Controls lead
 and in consideration of the control and leak detection system needs.
12 SCADA SYSTEM
 SCADA system design is beyond the scope of the Pipeline DBM. However, instrumentation specified
 for remotely monitored sites such as certain launchers, receivers, and RCV’s (remote controlled
 valves) shall be compatible with the overall SCADA system design and communications design.
 12.1 LEAK DETECTION
 49 CFR 195.134 requires leak detection that complies with the requirements in 195.444 for hazardous
 liquid pipelines transporting liquid in single phase (without gas in the liquid).
 New computational pipeline monitoring (CPM) leak detection system will be designed in accordance
 with the requirements in section 4.2 of API RP 1130.
 12.2 FIELD LOCATIONS
 12.2.1 CP Rectifiers
 Rectifiers shall be powered with utility power. Rectifiers shall be located and sized accordingly to
 align with existing utility power. Solar power will not be considered for rectifiers.
 12.2.2 Block Valves Control
 RCV’s (remote controlled valves) shall be compatible with the overall SCADA system design and
 communications design. They will be triggered by human action at the Control Center and or by
 human actions at the mainline valve site. Some valves (e.g., smaller valves in non‐HCA areas)
 may not be RCV’s and also may not have motorized actuators. Motorized valve actuation will be
 dictated by the HCA analysis and EFRD study.
13 SECURITY
 All above ground pipeline components shall be fenced at a minimum with locking gate access. Access
 control, intrusion detection, monitoring, and other physical security considerations shall be provided in
 the detailed design of each above ground site. Information security, including SCADA access control,
 is outside the scope of this document and shall be addressed by OES IT representative.
14 CORROSION CONTROL AND AC MITIGATION
 14.1 CORROSION CONTROL
 Corrosion control will be provided per 49 CFR Part 195, Subpart H –Corrosion Control and ASME
 B31.4, and Project specifications.
 An internal corrosion monitoring and mitigation plan will be developed according to fluid characteristics.
 The pipeline system will be cathodically protected with an impressed current system. The cathodic
 protection (CP) design efforts for the pipeline will include field measurements of soil resistivity,

 powerline to monitoring potential AC induced corrosion.
15 PIPELINE MARKERS
Line markers shall be installed in accordance with US Code of Federal Regulations, Title 49 Part 195.410
and OES Project standards. A cathodic protection test station marker may also serve as a pipeline marker.
Line markers shall be installed such that the location of the pipeline may be determined within the line of
sight at any point on the pipeline right of way.
When specified, buried warning ribbons are to be installed over each pipeline to mark the pipeline and alert
any digging operations to the presence of the pipeline.
