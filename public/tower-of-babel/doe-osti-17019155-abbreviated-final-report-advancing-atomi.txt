 ABBREVIATED FINAL REPORT

 Advancing Atomic Physics for Predictive Inertial Confinement Fusion Modeling
 William Farmer
 26-FS-023

Project Overview
Accurate atomic data for highly charged gold ions are needed to model high energy density
(HED) plasmas, including the x-ray emission produced by inertial confinement fusion (ICF)
experiments. Previous calculations overpredicted emission and electron-impact excitation for
important gold transitions in the 2 to 4 keV range, suggesting that the electronic-structure
models did not fully account for electron correlation.
This project used relativistic configuration-interaction calculations and second-order many-
body perturbation theory to study Ni-like, Cu-like, and Zn-like gold ions. The work examined
transitions from the 3d subshell to the 4f and 5f subshells and evaluated transition energies,
radiative decay rates, electron-impact excitation cross sections, spectral emission, and selected
autoionization rates. The results show that correlation effects are modest for Ni-like gold but
become substantially stronger for Cu-like and Zn-like ions, where configurations involving the
4s, 4p, and 4d subshells mix strongly. Including these effects reduces selected radiative rates
and excitation cross sections, improving agreement with electron beam ion trap (EBIT)
measurements. The results demonstrate that improved target-state electronic structure is
essential for reliable atomic data and plasma modeling of M-shell gold ions.

Mission Impact

This research supports Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory’s HED science and ICF missions
by improving the atomic data used to model x-ray emission and absorption in gold hohlraums.
More accurate transition rates and electron-impact excitation cross sections can improve
predictive simulations of radiation transport, plasma conditions, and energy coupling in
experiments at the National Ignition Facility. The work also advances Department of Energy and
National Nuclear Security Administration missions by providing validated science and
technology tools for national-security-relevant HED experiments, while establishing new
research directions for improved atomic-structure and collisional-radiative modeling.
