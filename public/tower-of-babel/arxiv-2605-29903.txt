1.
Introduction

We consider S. Ramanujan’s
partial theta function

\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}q^{j(j+1)/2}x^{j}
(see
[
1
]
and
[
32
]
) regarding

x\in\mathbb{C}
as a variable and
q\in\mathbb{D}_{1}
as a parameter;

\mathbb{D}_{r}\subset\mathbb{C}
stands for the open disk centered
at the origin and of radius
r
. The function’s name is chosen because of its
resemblance with the
Jacobi theta function

\Theta(q,x)=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{j^{2}}x^{j}
, as

\theta(q^{2},x/q)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}q^{j^{2}}x^{j}
; “partial” refers to the
summation being performed only for non-negative indices. We remind that

\Theta(q,x^{2})=\prod_{m=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{2m})(1+x^{2}q^{2m-1})(1+x^{-2}q^{2m-1})~~~\,{\rm(Jacobi~triple~product)}

which implies the equalities

(1.1)

\Theta^{*}(q,x):=\Theta(\sqrt{q},\sqrt{q}x)=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{j(j+1)/2}x^{j}=\prod_{m=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{m})(1+xq^{m})(1+q^{m-1}/x)~.

Thus one can write

(1.2)

\theta(q,x)=\Theta^{*}(q,x)-G(q,x)~,~~~\,G(q,x):=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{-1}q^{j(j+1)/2}x^{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}q^{j(j-1)/2}x^{-j}~.

The partial theta function is of interest in many domains: Ramanujan type

q
-series (
[
32
]
), the theory of (mock) modular forms (
[
4
]
),
statistical physics and combinatorics (
[
28
]
), asymptotic analysis
(
[
2
]
), asymptotics
and modularity of partial and false theta functions and their
interaction with representation theory and conformal field theory
(
[
3
]
,
[
5
]
), relationship between
Appell-Lerch sums and mock theta functions (
[
24
]
),
Artin-Tits monoids (
[
6
]
),
quantum many-body systems (
[
30
]
). Andrews-Warnaar identities for
the partial theta function can be found in
[
31
]
,
[
33
]

and
[
29
]
. The explicit combinatorial interpretation of the
coefficients of the leading root of
\theta
as a series in
q

is given in
[
27
]
. Padé
approximants of
\theta
are studied in
[
23
]
.

For each
q
fixed, the function
\theta
is an entire function of order
0
, see
[
12
, formula (5)]
. It has countably-many zeros (denoted by
\xi_{j}
) which are distinct for
|q|<c_{0}
, see Notation
1
and part (3) of Remarks
1
.

To continue the list of applications of
\theta
we define its
spectrum
as the set
of values of the parameter
q
for which
\theta(q,.)
has a multiple zero; its elements are called
spectral numbers
or
spectral values
.
Recently, the role played by
\theta
in the theory of
section-hyperbolic polynomials (i. e. real univariate polynomials with all
roots real negative and with all their truncations having only real
negative roots) was revealed in
[
25
]
,
[
9
]
and
[
22
]
,
based on the classical works of Hardy, Petrovitch and Hutchinson
(see
[
7
]
,
[
26
]
and
[
8
]
). The relationship with the
spectrum of
\theta
was mentioned in
[
22
]
. This stimulated the
study of the analytic properties of
\theta
, see

[
14
]
–
[
21
]
.

Remark 1
.

The positive spectral numbers

0<\tilde{q}_{1}=0.309249\ldots<\tilde{q}_{2}=0.516959\ldots<\tilde{q}_{3}=0.630628\ldots<\cdots

form an increasing sequence tending to

, see the list of the 6-digit truncations of the first

. The spectral number

is the closest to
0
.
The negative spectral numbers

Notation 1
.

For
q\in\mathbb{D}_{1}
, we denote by

\mathbb{A}_{a,b}(|q|)\subset\mathbb{C}

the open annulus
\{|q|^{-a}<|x|<|q|^{-b}\}
,
0<a<b
, and by

\mathbb{S}_{r;\alpha,\beta}\subset\mathbb{C}
the sector

\{0<|x|<r,{\rm arg}(x)\in[\alpha,\beta]\}
,

0\leq\alpha<\beta\leq 2\pi
. We denote by
c_{0}:=0.2078750206\ldots

the solution to the equation
2\sum_{\nu=1}^{\infty}|q|^{\nu^{2}/2}=1
. We set

\theta_{k}:=\sum_{j=0}^{k}q^{j(j+1)/2}x^{j}
(the
k
th
truncation
of
\theta
)
and
\theta_{k}^{\bullet}:=\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}q^{j(j+1)/2}x^{j}
.

Definition 1
.

We say that the zeros
\xi_{j}
of the partial theta function are
strongly
separated
in modulus
if

(1.3)

\xi_{1}\in(\mathbb{D}_{|q|^{-3/2}}\setminus\{0\})~~~\,{\rm and}~~~\,\xi_{k}\in\mathbb{A}_{k-1/2,k+1/2}(|q|)~,~~~\,k\geq 2~.

It is clear that if for
q=q_{*}
, the zeros of
\theta
are strongly separated in
modulus, then
q_{*}
is not a spectral number. We prove

the following theorem:

Theorem 1

(1) For
q\in\mathbb{S}_{0.6,\pi/4,7\pi/4}\cup\mathbb{D}_{c_{0}}
, the zeros of

\theta
are strongly separated in modulus.

(2) The only spectral number of

\theta
in the set
\mathbb{S}_{0.6,\pi/4,7\pi/4}\cup\mathbb{D}_{0.31}

is
\tilde{q}_{1}
.

Remark 3

To find the minimum of this function of
two variables numerically, we use the L-BFGS-B method provided by the
SciPy optimization library of Python. This is a Quasi-Newton method type
with boundary constraints. To garantee the stability, we use 20 random starts.
The precision is equal to
10^{-5}
. The code execution is fast (about two
seconds).

Taking square root we find
