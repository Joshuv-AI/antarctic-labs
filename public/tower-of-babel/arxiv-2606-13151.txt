Random Generation of k k -coloured Motzkin Paths
Elena Barcucci, Antonio Bernini, Stefano Bilotta, Renzo Pinzani

Abstract
We study k k -coloured Motzkin paths, namely Motzkin paths in which horizontal steps can be coloured in k k different ways, and investigate their connection with the number of prefixes ending at odd height from both an analytical and a combinatorial point of view. Moreover, the combinatorial approach provides a random generation algorithm for k k -coloured Motzkin paths in linear-time.

Introduction

A
Motzkin path
is a lattice path in the plane that starts at
(0,0)
, ends on the
x
-axis, never goes below it, and consists of three types of steps: an up step
(1,1)
, a down step
(1,-1)
, and a horizontal step
(1,0)
. Motzkin paths are classical objects in enumerative combinatorics enumerated by the famous Motzkin numbers, originally introduced by T. Motzkin
[
11
]
. They have been extensively investigated due to their
connections with Catalan structures, continued fractions, and
various combinatorial sequences
[
10
,
13
]
. Several
extensions have been considered, including coloured Motzkin paths
and weighted variations, which enrich the combinatorial structure
while preserving recursive decompositions
[
9
]
. In addition, we also recall their role in the generation of cross bifix-free words
[
4
]
, together with the study of their generating function under pattern-avoidance constraints
[
7
]
.

Many efficient algorithms to randomly generate Motzkin words of fixed
size are known. Several of them are based on rejection methods, as in
[
2
]
, while Barcucci et al.
[
5
,
6
]
proposed
an efficient algorithm based on an anticipated rejection method, known as the Florentine algorithm
[
3
]
.

In this paper we consider

k
-coloured Motzkin paths, namely Motzkin paths in which horizontal
steps can be coloured in
k
different ways, while rise and fall steps
remain uncoloured.
Let
m_{n}
denote the number of
k
-coloured Motzkin paths of length
n
.
We are interested in the enumeration of left factors (prefixes) of such
paths according to the parity of their final height.
More precisely, let
f_{n}^{(o)}
(resp.
f_{n}^{(e)}
) be the number of
prefixes of length
n
ending at odd (resp. even) height, and let

f_{n}
be the total number of prefixes of length
n
.

In this paper we consider a relation between

k
-coloured Motzkin paths and prefixes ending at odd height, namely

f_{n+1}^{(o)}=(n+1)m_{n},\qquad\text{for }n\geq 0.

We provide two different proofs of this identity.
The first one is analytic and is based on the functional equation
satisfied by the generating function of
k
-coloured Motzkin paths.
The second one is purely combinatorial and relies on a bijective
transformation together with Raney’s lemma
[
12
]
on cyclic shifts of
integer sequences.

Besides giving two independent proofs of the same enumerative
identity, the combinatorial approach also leads to a random
generation algorithm for
k
-coloured Motzkin paths and
a linear-time complexity analysis of it.

3
Enumeration of Motzkin prefixes

We give two different proofs of the following

Theorem 1
.

For
n\geq 0
,

f_{n+1}^{(o)}=(n+1)m_{n}.

Analytical proof (sketch)

It is not difficult to verify that

f_{n+1}^{(e)}=2f_{n}^{(o)}+kf_{n}^{(e)},

and

f_{n+1}^{(o)}=kf_{n}^{(o)}+2f_{n}^{(e)}-m_{n}.

with initial conditions

f_{0}^{(e)}=1,\ f_{0}^{(o)}=0,\ f_{1}^{(e)}=k,\ f_{1}^{(o)}=1.

We now define the generating functions

E(t)=\sum_{n\geq 0}f_{n}^{(e)}t^{n},\qquad O(t)=\sum_{n\geq 0}f_{n}^{(o)}t^{n},\qquad M(t)=\sum_{n\geq 0}m_{n}t^{n}.

From the above recurrence relations we obtain

\begin{cases}E(t)=2t\,O(t)+kt\,E(t)+1,\\
O(t)=kt\,O(t)+2t\,E(t)-t\,M(t),\end{cases}

whose solution is given by

E(t)=\frac{2tO(t)+1}{1-kt},

and

O(t)=\frac{(1-kt)tM(t)-2t}{4t^{2}-(1-kt)^{2}}.

(1)

Moreover, we recall the functional equation satisfied by
M(t)
:

M(t)=1+ktM(t)+t^{2}M(t)^{2}.

(2)

Setting

\overline{M}(t)=tM(t)

and performing some straightforward manipulations, we obtain

t\frac{d}{dt}\overline{M}(t)=\frac{\overline{M}(t)}{1-kt-2t\overline{M}(t)}.

We have
f_{n+1}^{(o)}=(n+1)m_{n}
if and only if
O(t)=t\frac{d}{dt}\overline{M}(t)
. This latter identity follows from equations (
1
) and (
2
).

P

k

If

f_{n+1}^{(0)}=(n+1)m_{n}.

4
The random generation algorithm

From the previous combinatorial proof we obtain an algorithm to randomly generate
a
k
-coloured Motzkin path:

1.

use the Florentine algorithm
[
5
]
to
randomly generate a
k
-coloured Motzkin prefix
F
of length
n+1
;

2.

while
F
has even final height, return to step
1
;

3.

perform the
\Gamma
transformation on
F
;

4.

perform the cyclic shifts of Lemma
1
and identify the unique Motzkin path of length
n
associated to the
n+1
cyclic shifts of length
n+1
.

4.1
The complexity

The expected number of extracted characters (or calls to
random
)
for generating a
k
-coloured Motzkin left factor of length
n
is
[
5
]

2n-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{(k+2)\pi(n+1)}+\frac{6+k}{4}+O(1).

Therefore, the first step can be performed in linear time.
For the second step, establishing that the final height of the newly generated prefix
F
is clearly performed in constant time. Nevertheless, the generation of
F
having final odd height depends on the ratio

\frac{f_{n}^{(0)}}{f_{n}}.

From Theorem
1
we have

\frac{f_{n}^{(0)}}{f_{n}}=\frac{nm_{n-1}}{f_{n}},

and from the recursive construction of
k
-coloured Motzkin left factors we have
m_{n}=(k+2)f_{n}-f_{n+1}
.
Thus,

\frac{f_{n}^{(0)}}{f_{n}}=\frac{n\big((k+2)f_{n-1}-f_{n}\big)}{f_{n}}.

Being

\displaystyle f(t)=\sum_{n\geq 0}f_{n}t^{n}=\frac{1}{2t}\left(\sqrt{\frac{1+t}{1-3t}}-1\right)
, see for example
[
5
]
, it is easy to prove (via the Darboux’s method
[
8
]
) that

f_{n}\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{k+2}}\frac{(k+2)^{n}}{\sqrt{\pi n}}.

Therefore,

\frac{f_{n}^{(0)}}{f_{n}}\sim\ldots\sim\frac{n}{\sqrt{n-1}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}+\sqrt{n-1}}\right)\sim\frac{1}{2}.

k=1

Therefore, the average computational cost of the first and second steps grows asymptotically as
4n
. The third and fourth steps can be performed in linear time. Then, the average complexity of the algorithm is linear in
n
.

5
Conclusions

The results herein contained are surely not up to date. Indeed better performing algorithms do exist in order to generate Motzkin prefixes
[
3
]
which could improve step 1 of our algorithm. Nevertheless the authors believe that these results give a new contribution to the field of the random generation of combinatorial objects.
