Stability Analysis of Monolithic Globally Divergence-Free ALE-HDG Methods for Fluid-Structure Interaction Journal: ?
Shuaijun Liu, Xiaoping Xie

Abstract
In this paper, we propose two monolithic fully discrete finite element methods for fluid-structure interaction (FSI) based on a novel Piola-type Arbitrary Lagrangian-Eulerian (ALE) mapping. For the temporal discretization, we apply the backward Euler method to both the non-conservative and conservative formulations. For the spatial discretization, we adopt arbitrary order hybridizable discontinuous Galerkin (HDG) methods for the incompressible Navier-Stokes and linear elasticity equations, and a continuous Galerkin (CG) method for the fluid mesh movement. We derive stability results for both the temporal semi-discretization and the fully discretization, and show that the velocity approximations of the fully discrete schemes are globally divergence-free. Several numerical experiments are performed to verify the performance of the proposed methods.

Introduction

Fluid-structure interaction (FSI) is a multi-physics phenomenon that describes the mutual interaction and interdependence between a movable or deformable structure and an internal or surrounding fluid flow, and has a broad range of applications in many engineering fields such as aerospace, civil and wind engineering, and biomedical engineering.
As a fundamental multi-physics discipline characterized by strong, highly nonlinear coupling between the fluid flow and the structure, its accurate numerical simulation has always been one of the most challenging frontiers in computational engineering; see
[
6
,
7
,
11
,
13
,
33
,
34
,
37
,
40
,
47
]
for some books and review articles.

FSI problems inherently involve moving domains, requiring accurate tracking or capturing of the dynamic interface between the fluid and structural components to ensure the accuracy and stability of numerical schemes.
To address this challenge, there are two types of numerical approaches in the literature: the interface-capturing approach and the interface-tracking approach.
The former, represented by the immersed boundary method
[
44
]
and the fictitious domain method
[
29
]
, uses body-unfitted meshes (not attaching to the interface) and captures the interface through additional transport equations.
The latter typically employs body-fitted meshes that adapt to the moving interface, ensuring precise representation of the evolving boundary. The most representative interface-tracking method is the Arbitrary Lagrangian-Eulerian (ALE) method
[
20
,
32
,
36
,
42
,
56
,
57
,
63
]
. The ALE method combines the advantages of two classical kinematical descriptions, the Lagrangian description and the Eulerian description, where the computational mesh of the fluid domain can be moved during the solution process in some arbitrarily specified way so as to avoid large distortions while preserving the clear description of interfaces.

Another classification of FSI numerical methods is based on the coupling strategy between the fluid and structural solvers: monolithic
[
2
,
5
,
28
,
48
,
52
]
and partitioned approaches
[
3
,
10
,
19
,
43
,
53
]
.
The monolithic approach implicitly enforces the coupling conditions and solves the entire coupled system as a single algebraic system, whereas the partitioned approach explicitly applies the coupling conditions to decouple the system into the fluid and structure components.
The former typically incurs higher computational cost, but has superior stability than the latter.
The latter allows flexible use of existing fluid and structure solvers, but may be stable only within specific physical parameter ranges of the model; for instance, the partitioned algorithm may suffer from numerical instabilities when the fluid and structure densities are comparable due to the so-called added-mass effect
[
12
]
.

The finite element method, as one of the most important numerical methods for solving partial differential equations, has been extensively applied to the spatial discretization of FSI problems
[
6
,
11
,
21
,
58
]
; see, for instance,
[
9
,
20
,
35
,
49
]
and
[
1
,
4
,
23
,
61
,
62
]
for some numerical schemes using continuous Galerkin (CG) finite elements and discontinuous Galerkin (DG) finite elements, respectively.
The hybridizable discontinuous Galerkin (HDG) framework, developed in
[
16
]
for diffusion problems, provides a unifying strategy for the hybridization of finite element methods.
On the one hand, the HDG framework preserves the advantages of the DG framework, e.g. the local conservation of physical quantities and the flexibility in meshing. On the other hand, it usually leads to a discrete system of significantly reduced sizes due to the local elimination property, i.e. the unknowns defined in the interior of elements can be locally eliminated by using the numerical traces defined on the interfaces of elements. We refer to
[
15
,
17
,
24
,
25
,
46
,
64
]
for some mass-conserving HDG discretizations of incompressible flow problems that yield divergence-free velocity approximations.

In the past decade, there have been several works applying HDG methods to the FSI problems.
In
[
54
,
55
]
Sheldon et al. proposed monolithic ALE-HDG schemes for modelling FSI, where the HDG method is applied to discretize the fluid and structure equations.
La Spina et al.
[
39
]
gave a monolithic ALE method for FSI problems with weakly compressible flows using an HDG scheme for the fluid and a standard CG scheme for the structure.
Later, Neunteufel and Schöberl
[
41
]
presented a monolithic ALE scheme in which the fluid equations are, for the first time, formulated using a Piola-type transformation and discretized with an
H(\mbox{div})
-conforming HDG approach to ensure an exactly divergence-free velocity approximation, while the structure equations are discretized using a standard CG method.
Subsequently, Fu
[
26
]
developed monolithic and strongly coupled partitioned schemes based on an ALE divergence-free HDG method for the Navier-Stokes equations, and a hybridized tangential-velocity-normal-normal-stress scheme for nonlinear hyperelasticity equations.
We note that the above contributions have primarily focused on the development of discretization techniques without rigorous stability analysis.

This paper develops two new monolithic Piola-type ALE HDG schemes for a FSI model where the fluid and the structure are described respectively by incompressible Navier-Stokes equations and linearly elasticity equations, and establishes energy stability results.
We apply the backward Euler method to both the non-conservative and conservative formulations so as to get two temporally semi-discrete schemes.
For the spatial discretization, we employ HDG methods for the fluid and structure equations, and a continuous Galerkin method for the fluid mesh movement equation.
More precisely, on the fluid domain the discontinuous
P_{k-1}
(
k\geq 1
) element is used for the fluid stress tensor approximation, the discontinuous
P_{k}/P_{k}
elements are adopted for the fluid velocity / its trace approximations, the discontinuous
P_{k-1}/P_{k}
elements are applied for the pressure / its trace approximations, and the continuous
P_{k}
element is utilized for the fluid mesh displacement approximation; On the structure domain, the discontinuous
P_{k-1}
element is used for the stress tensor approximation, and the discontinuous
P_{k}/P_{k}
elements are adopted for both the structural velocity/ its trace approximations and the displacement / its trace approximations.
The choice of the fluid velocity and pressure spaces, following
[
14
,
15
,
46
]
, ensures globally divergence-free velocity approximations of the two resulting fully discrete ALE-HDG schemes, as the pressure trace acts as a Lagrange multiplier enforcing continuity of the normal component of the fluid velocity across interelement boundaries.
We show the energy stability for both the temporally semi-discrete schemes and the fully discrete schemes.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section

3
applies the backward Euler method to construct two temporally semi-discrete schemes and analyzes their stability. Section
4

5

6
gives some concluding remarks.

2.2
Model description

In the following, we show the governing equations of the fluid and structure, as well as the fluid-structure coupling conditions.

2.2.1
Fluid equations

In the FSI model, the fluid dynamic behavior is described by incompressible Navier-Stokes equations in terms of the fluid velocity
\bm{u}^{f}
and the fluid pressure
p^{f}
:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \rho^{f}{\partial_{t}\bm{u}^{f}}+\rho^{f}(\bm{u}^{f}\cdot\nabla)\bm{u}^{f}&=\nabla\cdot\bm{\sigma}^{f}(\bm{u}^{f},p^{f})+\bm{g}^{f},&&\text{in}\ \Omega_{f}(t)\times(0,T],\\
\nabla\cdot\bm{u}^{f}&=0,&&\text{in}\ \Omega_{f}(t)\times(0,T],\\
\bm{u}^{f}&=\bm{0},&&\text{on}\ \Gamma_{f}(t)\times(0,T],\\
\bm{u}^{f}(\cdot,0)&=\bm{u}_{0}^{f},&&\text{on}\ \Omega_{f}(0),\end{aligned}\right.

(2.2)

where
\rho^{f}>0
denotes the fluid density (assumed constant),
\mathbf{g}^{f}
the fluid volume external force,
\bm{u}^{f}_{0}
the initial fluid velocity field, and
\Gamma_{f}(t):=\partial\Omega_{f}(t)\backslash\Gamma(t)
. The fluid Cauchy stress
\bm{\sigma}^{f}
reads

\displaystyle\bm{\sigma}^{f}(\bm{u}^{f},p^{f}):=2\rho^{f}\mu^{f}\bm{D}(\bm{u}^{f})-p^{f}\bm{I},

with the constant viscosity coefficient
\mu^{f}>0
, where
\bm{I}
is the identity tensor and
\bm{D}(\bm{u}^{f}):=\tfrac{1}{2}(\nabla\bm{u}^{f}+(\nabla\bm{u}^{f})^{\top})
is the fluid strain rate tensor.

2.2.2
Structure equations

As for the structural dynamic behavior, we consider the following linear elasticity system in terms of the structure velocity
\widehat{\bm{u}}^{s}
and the structure displacement
\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s}
in the Lagrangian description:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \rho^{s}{\partial_{t}\widehat{\bm{u}}^{s}}-\widehat{\nabla}\cdot\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}^{s}(\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s})&={\bm{0}},&&\text{in}\ \widehat{\Omega}_{s}\times(0,T],\\
\partial_{t}\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s}-\widehat{\bm{u}}^{s}&={\bm{0}},&&\text{in}\ \widehat{\Omega}_{s}\times(0,T],\\
\widehat{\bm{u}}^{s}&=\bm{0},&&\text{on}\ \widehat{\Gamma}_{s}\times(0,T],\\
\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s}(\cdot,0)=\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s}_{0},\ \widehat{\bm{u}}^{s}(\cdot,0)&=\widehat{\bm{u}}^{s}_{0},&&\text{on}\ \widehat{\Omega}_{s},\end{aligned}\right.

(2.3)

where
\widehat{\rho}^{s}>0
denotes the structure density (assumed constant),
\widehat{\bm{u}}^{s}_{0}
and
\mathbf{d}^{s}_{0}
the initial structure velocity and displacement, respectively, and
\widehat{\Gamma}_{s}:=\partial\widehat{\Omega}_{s}\backslash\widehat{\Gamma}
.
The structure Cauchy stress tensor
\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}^{s}
is given by

\displaystyle\bm{\widehat{\sigma}}^{s}(\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s}):=\lambda^{s}(\widehat{\nabla}\cdot\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s})\bm{I}+2\mu^{s}\bm{D}(\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s}),

where
\bm{D}(\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s}):=\tfrac{1}{2}(\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s}+(\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s})^{\top})
is the strain tensor and
\lambda^{s},\mu^{s}
are the Lamé constants such that
0<\lambda^{s}<\infty
and
0<\mu_{1}<\mu^{s}<\mu_{2}
for positive constants
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
. Here we mention that the gradient operator
\widehat{\nabla}
refers to differentiation with
respect to the Lagrangian coordinates
\widehat{\bm{x}}_{s}
.

2.2.3
Coupling conditions

For the FSI model, we introduce the following no-slip type interface conditions:

1.

Kinematic coupling condition:

\displaystyle\bm{u}^{f}=\widehat{\bm{u}}^{s}\circ(\widehat{\bm{X}}_{s})^{-1},\qquad\text{on}\ \Gamma(t),\ t\in(0,T].

(2.4)

2.

Dynamic coupling condition:

\displaystyle\widehat{J}_{b}\bm{\sigma}^{f}{\bm{n}}^{f}+(\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}^{s}\widehat{\bm{n}}^{s})\circ(\widehat{\bm{X}}_{s})^{-1}=\bm{0},\qquad\text{on}\ \Gamma(t),\ t\in(0,T].

(2.5)

Here,
\widehat{J}_{b}
is the surface Jacobian of the mapping from
\widehat{\Gamma}
to
\Gamma(t)
,
\bm{n}^{f}
and
\widehat{\bm{n}}^{s}
denote the fluid and structure outer normal vector on the fluid-structure interface
\Gamma(t)
and
\widehat{\Gamma}
, respectively. Notice that
\widehat{\bm{X}}_{s}
is given by (
2.1
) and it holds
\Gamma(t)=\widehat{\Gamma}\circ(\widehat{\bm{X}}_{s})^{-1}
.

2.3
The ALE mapping

In FSI problems, directly adopting the fluid material (Lagrangian) mapping
\widehat{\bm{X}}_{f}(\widehat{\bm{x}}_{f},t)
may lead to severe mesh distortion due to large displacements of the fluid material points.
To avoid this, we introduce for
t\in(0,T]
the classical Arbitrary Lagrangian-Eulerian (ALE) mapping
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}(\cdot,t)
, defined as a time-dependent bijection from the reference domain
\widehat{\Omega}_{f}
to the current domain
\Omega_{f}(t)
, with

\displaystyle\Omega_{f}(t)=\widehat{\mathcal{A}}(\widehat{\Omega}_{f},t)\text{ and }\Omega_{f}(t)\ni\bm{x}=\widehat{\mathcal{A}}(\widehat{\bm{x}},t)=\widehat{\bm{x}}+\widehat{\bm{\eta}}^{f}(\widehat{\bm{x}},t),\quad\forall\widehat{\bm{x}}\in\widehat{\Omega}_{f}.

(2.6)

Here,
\widehat{\bm{\eta}}^{f}
denotes the displacement field of the fluid domain, describing the motion of the computational grid in the fluid domain. It coincides with the structural displacement
\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s}(t)(\widehat{\bm{x}}):=\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s}(\widehat{\bm{x}},t)
on the fluid-structure interface
\widehat{\Gamma}
and is smoothly extended to the entire fluid domain
\widehat{\Omega}_{f}
. A common choice of the extension operator is the weighted harmonic extension:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} -\widehat{\nabla}\cdot(\kappa^{f}\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{\bm{\eta}}^{f})&=0,&&\text{in}\ \widehat{\Omega}_{f},\\
\widehat{\bm{\eta}}^{f}&=\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s}(t),&&\text{on}\ \widehat{\Gamma},\\
\widehat{\bm{\eta}}^{f}&=\bm{0},&&\text{on}\ \widehat{\Gamma}_{f}:=\partial\widehat{\Omega}_{f}\backslash\widehat{\Gamma}.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.7)

Here
\kappa^{f}=\kappa^{f}(\widehat{\bm{x}})
is a positive function. Note that
\kappa^{f}=1
is a simple and convenient choice of the weight function, but in some situations one may choose a more complex function to enable mesh adaptation.

Introduce the fluid domain velocity, the fluid deformation gradient and its Jacobian determinant respectively as follows:

\displaystyle\bm{w}^{f}(\bm{x},t)\circ\widehat{\mathcal{A}}=\widehat{\bm{w}}^{f}(\widehat{\bm{x}},t):={\partial_{t}\widehat{\mathcal{A}}(\widehat{\bm{x}},t)}=\partial_{t}\widehat{\bm{\eta}}^{f}(\widehat{\bm{x}},t),\quad\widehat{\bm{F}}_{f}:=\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{\mathcal{A}},\quad\widehat{J}_{f}=\det(\widehat{\bm{F}}_{f}).

(2.8)

Applying the chain rule gives

\displaystyle{\partial_{t}\widehat{J}_{f}}=\widehat{J}_{f}\widehat{\bm{F}}_{f}^{-\top}\bm{:}\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{\bm{w}}^{f}=\nabla\cdot\bm{w}^{f}\widehat{J}_{f},\qquad{\partial_{t}\widehat{\bm{F}}_{f}}=\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{\bm{w}}^{f}=\nabla\bm{w}^{f}\widehat{\bm{F}}_{f},

(2.9)

where ‘
\bm{:}
’ denote the Frobenius inner product of two
d\times d
martrices, i.e.
\mathbb{A}\bm{:}\mathbb{B}=\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{d}a_{ij}b_{ij}
for
\mathbb{A}=[a_{ij}]_{d\times d}
and
\mathbb{B}=[b_{ij}]_{d\times d}
.

Under the ALE mapping
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}
, we introduce the Piola transformation to ensure that a function
\widehat{\bm{v}}\in\bm{H}(\widehat{\mbox{div}},\widehat{\Omega}_{f})
remains in the space
\bm{H}(\mbox{div},\Omega_{f}(t))
after the domain deformation:

Definition 2.1
.

(The Piola-type ALE mapping of functions) Assume that
\widehat{J}_{f}
does not vanish at any point and time, then for each
\widehat{\bm{v}}(\widehat{\bm{x}},t)\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\bm{H}(\widehat{\mbox{div}},\widehat{\Omega}_{f}))
we define

\displaystyle\bm{v}(\bm{x},t):=\mathcal{P}(\widehat{\bm{v}})\circ{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}}^{-1}(\bm{x},t)\quad\text{with }\mathcal{P}(\widehat{\bm{v}}):=\frac{1}{\widehat{J}_{f}}\widehat{\bm{F}}_{f}\widehat{\bm{v}},\ \bm{x}=\widehat{\mathcal{A}}(\widehat{\bm{x}},t).

(2.10)

For the Piola transformation (
2.10
), it is standard that

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\bm{v}(\bm{x},t)=\frac{1}{\widehat{J}_{f}}\widehat{\nabla}\cdot\widehat{\bm{v}}(\widehat{\bm{x}},t).

(2.11)

Furthermore, we have
\bm{v}\in\bm{H}(\mbox{div},\Omega_{f}(t))
if
{1}/{\widehat{J}_{f}}\in L^{\infty}(\widehat{\Omega}_{f})
.

Let us introduce the following Sobolev space in view of the Piola-type ALE mapping:

\displaystyle\bm{V}_{\mbox{div}}^{f}(t)

\displaystyle:=\left\{\bm{v}:\Omega_{f}(t)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid\bm{v}=\mathcal{P}(\widehat{\bm{v}})\circ\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{-1},\ \widehat{\bm{v}}\in\bm{H}(\widehat{\mbox{div}},\widehat{\Omega}_{f})\right\},

where

\displaystyle\bm{H}(\widehat{\mbox{div}},\widehat{\Omega}_{f}):=\{\widehat{\bm{v}}\in\bm{L}^{2}(\widehat{\Omega}_{f})|\ \widehat{\nabla}\cdot\widehat{\bm{v}}\in L^{2}(\widehat{\Omega}_{f})\}.

The next lemma shows some fundamental properties of the Piola transform (cf.
[
8
, Lemma 2.1.6]
).

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{\rm div}^{f}(t)
and

q\in\widetilde{Q}^{f}(t):=\left\{q:\Omega_{f}(t)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\mid q=\widehat{q}\circ\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{-1},\ \widehat{q}\in L^{2}(\widehat{\Omega}_{f})\right\},

it holds that

\displaystyle(\bm{v},\nabla q)_{\Omega_{f}}=(\widehat{\bm{v}},\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{q})_{\widehat{\Omega}_{f}},\quad(q,\nabla\cdot\bm{v})_{\Omega_{f}}=(\widehat{q},\widehat{\nabla}\cdot\widehat{\bm{v}})_{\widehat{\Omega}_{f}},\quad(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n},q)_{\Omega_{f}}=(\widehat{\bm{v}}\cdot\widehat{\bm{n}},\widehat{q})_{\widehat{\Omega}_{f}}.

(2.12)

2.4
Weak formulations and stability

For any
\bm{v}(\bm{x},t)\in H^{1}(0,T;\bm{V}_{\rm{div}}^{f}(t))
, define its Piola-type ALE time material derivative as

\displaystyle\partial^{M}_{t}\bm{v}(\bm{x},t):=\mathcal{P}\big({\partial_{t}\widehat{\bm{v}}}\big)\circ\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{-1}(\bm{x},t)=\big(\frac{1}{\widehat{J}_{f}}\widehat{\bm{F}}_{f}{\partial_{t}\widehat{\bm{v}}}\big)\circ\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{-1}(\bm{x},t).

(2.13)

We easily get

\displaystyle\partial^{M}_{t}\bm{v}={\partial_{t}}\bm{v}+(\bm{w}^{f}\cdot\nabla)\bm{v}-\nabla\bm{w}^{f}\bm{v}+\nabla\cdot\bm{w}^{f}\bm{v},\quad\forall\bm{v}\in H^{1}(0,T;\bm{V}_{\rm{div}}^{f}(t)),

(2.14)

where
\bm{w}^{f}
is the fluid domain velocity given by (
2.8
). In fact, by Definition
2.1
and (
2.9
) we get

\displaystyle{\partial_{t}}(\bm{v}\circ\widehat{\mathcal{A}})={\partial_{t}}(\frac{1}{\widehat{J}_{f}}\widehat{\bm{F}}_{f}\widehat{\bm{v}})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\widehat{J}_{f}}(-\widehat{\bm{F}}_{f}^{-\top}\bm{:}\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{\bm{w}}^{f}\widehat{\bm{F}}_{f}+\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{\bm{w}}^{f})\widehat{\bm{v}}+\frac{1}{\widehat{J}_{f}}\widehat{\bm{F}}_{f}{\partial_{t}}\widehat{\bm{v}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\widehat{J}_{f}}(\nabla\bm{w}^{f}\widehat{\bm{F}}_{f}-\nabla\cdot\bm{w}^{f}\widehat{\bm{F}}_{f})\widehat{\bm{v}}+\frac{1}{\widehat{J}_{f}}\widehat{\bm{F}}_{f}{\partial_{t}}\widehat{\bm{v}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla\bm{w}^{f}(\bm{v}\circ\widehat{\mathcal{A}})-\nabla\cdot\bm{w}^{f}(\bm{v}\circ\widehat{\mathcal{A}})+\mathcal{P}({\partial_{t}}\widehat{\bm{v}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla\bm{w}^{f}\bm{v}-\nabla\cdot\bm{w}^{f}\bm{v}+\partial^{M}_{t}\bm{v}.

On the other hand, according to the chain rule we obtain

\displaystyle{\partial_{t}}(\bm{v}\circ\widehat{\mathcal{A}})={\partial_{t}}\bm{v}\circ\widehat{\mathcal{A}}+(\bm{w}^{f}\cdot\nabla)(\bm{v}\circ\widehat{\mathcal{A}})={\partial_{t}}\bm{v}+(\bm{w}^{f}\cdot\nabla)\bm{v}.

As a result, (
2.14
) follows.

In light of the relation (
2.14
) we immediately obtain the following Reynolds transport formulas under the Piola-type ALE frame:

Lemma 2.2
.

For
\bm{v},\bm{u}\in H^{1}(0,T;\bm{V}_{\rm{div}}^{f}(t))
it holds

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}(\bm{u},\bm{v})_{\Omega_{f}(t)}&=\left(\partial^{M}_{t}\bm{u}+\nabla\bm{w}^{f}\bm{u}-\nabla\cdot\bm{w}^{f}\bm{u}+(\nabla\bm{w}^{f})^{\top}\bm{u},\bm{v}\right)_{\Omega_{f}(t)}+\left(\bm{u},\partial^{M}_{t}\bm{v}\right)_{\Omega_{f}(t)}\\
&=\left({\partial_{t}}\bm{u}+(\bm{w}^{f}\cdot\nabla)\bm{u},\bm{v}\right)_{\Omega_{f}(t)}+\left(\bm{u},{\partial_{t}}\bm{v}+(\bm{w}^{f}\cdot\nabla)\bm{v}+\nabla\cdot\bm{w}^{f}\bm{v}\right)_{\Omega_{f}(t)}.\end{split}

(2.15)

In particular, if
\partial^{M}_{t}\bm{v}=0
, then

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}(\bm{u},\bm{v})_{\Omega_{f}(t)}&=\left(\partial^{M}_{t}\bm{u}+\nabla\bm{w}^{f}\bm{u}-\nabla\cdot\bm{w}^{f}\bm{u}+(\nabla\bm{w}^{f})^{\top}\bm{u},\bm{v}\right)_{\Omega_{f}(t)}\\
&=\left({\partial_{t}\bm{u}}+(\bm{w}^{f}\cdot\nabla)\bm{u}+(\nabla\bm{w}^{f})^{\top}\bm{u},\bm{v}\right)_{\Omega_{f}(t)}\end{split}

(2.16)

and

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\bm{v}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{f}(t))}=\left(2\nabla\bm{w}^{f}\bm{v}-\nabla\cdot\bm{w}^{f}\bm{v},\bm{v}\right)_{\Omega_{f}(t)}.

(2.17)

Proof.

In view of (
2.10
), (
2.9
) and (
2.13
) we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}(\bm{u},\bm{v})_{\Omega_{f}(t)}&=\frac{d}{dt}(\mathcal{P}(\widehat{\bm{u}}),\mathcal{P}(\widehat{\bm{v}})\widehat{J}_{f})_{\widehat{\Omega}_{f}}=\frac{d}{dt}(\mathcal{P}(\widehat{\bm{u}}),\widehat{\bm{F}}_{f}\widehat{\bm{v}})_{\widehat{\Omega}_{f}}\\
&=({\partial_{t}}(\bm{u}\circ\widehat{\mathcal{A}}),\widehat{\bm{F}}_{f}\widehat{\bm{v}})_{\widehat{\Omega}_{f}}+(\bm{u}\circ\widehat{\mathcal{A}},{\partial_{t}}\widehat{\bm{F}}_{f}\ \widehat{\bm{v}}+\widehat{\bm{F}}_{f}{\partial_{t}}\widehat{\bm{v}})_{\widehat{\Omega}_{f}}\\
&=({\partial_{t}}(\bm{u}\circ\widehat{\mathcal{A}}),\bm{v})_{\Omega_{f}(t)}+(\bm{u},\partial^{M}_{t}\bm{v}+\nabla\bm{w}^{f}\bm{v})_{\Omega_{f}(t)}.\end{split}

which, together with (
2.14

2.15
) and (
2.16
).
The identity (
2.17
) follows from (
2.16
) and the relation

\left(\nabla\bm{w}^{f}\bm{v},,\bm{v}\right)_{\Omega_{f}(t)}=\left((\nabla\bm{w}^{f})^{\top}\bm{v},\bm{v}\right)_{\Omega_{f}(t)}.

∎

Remark 2.1
.

By taking
\bm{u}=\bm{v}=\mathbf{e}_{i},\;i=1,\dots,d
in (
2.15
), where
\{\mathbf{e}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{d}
denotes the canonical basis of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we can obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}|\Omega_{f}(t)|=\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega_{f}(t)}d\bm{x}=\int_{\Omega_{f}(t)}\nabla\cdot\bm{w}^{f}d\bm{x}=\int_{\partial\Omega_{f}(t)}\bm{w}^{f}\cdot\bm{n}dS,

(2.18)

where
|\Omega_{f}(t)|
is the measure of the subdomain
\Omega_{f}(t)
. This relation is called the Geometric Conservation Law (GCL) for flow problems with a moving domain
[
22
,
30
]
.

For the time derivative and convective terms in the fluid system (
2.2
), we have the following results.

Lemma 2.3
.

For
\bm{u}\in\bm{H}^{1}(\Omega_{f}(t))
with
\nabla\cdot\bm{u}=0
,
\bm{w}^{f}\in\bm{H}^{1}(\Omega_{f}(t))
given by (
2.8
), and
\bm{v}\in\bm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{f}(t))
, it holds

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bigl(\partial_{t}\bm{u}+(\bm{u}\cdot\nabla)\bm{u},\bm{v}\bigr)_{\Omega_{f}(t)}={}&\Bigl(\partial_{t}^{M}\bm{u}+\nabla\bm{w}^{f}\ \bm{u}-\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot\bm{w}^{f}\,\bm{u},\bm{v}\Bigr)_{\Omega_{f}(t)}\\
&\quad+\tfrac{1}{2}\Bigl((\bm{v}\cdot\nabla)\bm{u}-(\bm{u}\cdot\nabla)\bm{v},\,\bm{u}-\bm{w}^{f}\Bigr)_{\Omega_{f}(t)}.\end{split}

(2.19)

Moreover, if
\partial_{t}^{M}\bm{v}^{f}=0
then

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bigl(\partial_{t}\bm{u}+(\bm{u}\cdot\nabla)\bm{u},\bm{v}\bigr)_{\Omega_{f}(t)}={}&\frac{d}{dt}\bigl(\bm{u},\bm{v}\bigr)_{\Omega_{f}(t)}-\Bigl((\nabla\bm{w}^{f})^{\top}\bm{u}-\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot\bm{w}^{f}\,\bm{u},\bm{v}\Bigr)_{\Omega_{f}(t)}\\
&\quad+\tfrac{1}{2}\Bigl((\bm{v}\cdot\nabla)\bm{u}-(\bm{u}\cdot\nabla)\bm{v},\,\bm{u}-\bm{w}^{f}\Bigr)_{\Omega_{f}(t)}.\end{split}

(2.20)

Proof.

First, applying integration by parts and (
2.14
) we obtain

\displaystyle\bigl(\partial_{t}\bm{u}+(\bm{u}\cdot\nabla)\bm{u},\bm{v}\bigr)_{\Omega_{f}(t)}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\Bigl(\partial_{t}^{M}\bm{u}+\nabla\bm{w}^{f}\bm{u}-\nabla\cdot\bm{w}^{f}\,\bm{u}+((\bm{u}-\bm{w}^{f})\cdot\nabla)\bm{u},\bm{v}\Bigr)_{\Omega_{f}(t)}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\Bigl(\partial_{t}^{M}\bm{u}+\nabla\bm{w}^{f}\bm{u}-\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot\bm{w}^{f}\,\bm{u},\bm{v}\Bigr)_{\Omega_{f}(t)}+\tfrac{1}{2}\Bigl((\bm{v}\cdot\nabla)\bm{u}-(\bm{u}\cdot\nabla)\bm{v},\,\bm{u}-\bm{w}^{f}\Bigr)_{\Omega_{f}(t)},

which proves (
2.19
).

Furthermore, if
\partial_{t}^{M}\bm{v}^{f}=0
, by the first equality of (
2.16
) we get

\displaystyle\Bigl(\partial_{t}^{M}\bm{u}+\nabla\bm{w}^{f}\bm{u}-\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot\bm{w}^{f}\,\bm{u},\bm{v}\Bigr)_{\Omega_{f}(t)}=\frac{d}{dt}(\bm{u},\bm{v})_{\Omega_{f}(t)}+\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot\bm{w}^{f}\,\bm{u}-(\nabla\bm{w}^{f})^{\top}\bm{u},\bm{v}\right)_{\Omega_{f}(t)}.

Substituting this relation into (
2.19
) yields
(
2.20
).
∎

Remark 2.2
.

Equations (
2.19
) and (
2.20
) are consistent in the continuous case, with the former often referred to as a non-conservative formulation and the latter as a conservative formulation for the time derivative and convective terms in the fluid equations. Based on them, we shall construct two types of temporal discretizations, a non-conservative scheme and a conservative one, and analyze their stability in Section
3
. We mention that the non-conservative formulation (
2.19
) has been adopted in
[
26
,
41
]
to construct the temporal discretizations of FSI problems, whereas the conservative form (
2.20
) is, to the best of our knowledge, introduced for the first time in our contribution.

To give the ALE weak formulation of the FSI model (
2.2
)-(
2.5
), we need to introduce, for all
t\in[0,T]
, the following function spaces:

\displaystyle\bm{V}^{f}(t)

\displaystyle:=\left\{\bm{v}^{f}:\Omega_{f}(t)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid\bm{v}^{f}=\mathcal{P}(\widehat{\bm{v}}^{f})\circ\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{-1},\ \widehat{\bm{v}}^{f}\in\bm{H}^{1}(\widehat{\Omega}_{f})\right\},

\displaystyle\bm{V}^{f}_{0}(t)

\displaystyle:=\left\{\bm{v}^{f}\in\bm{V}^{f}(t)\mid\bm{v}=\bm{0}\ \text{on}\ \Gamma_{f}(t)\right\},

\displaystyle\bm{W}^{f}(t)

\displaystyle\widehat{\bm{V}}_{0}^{s}

\displaystyle\widehat{\bm{V}}_{D}^{\mathcal{M}}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\widehat{\bm{d}}:\widehat{\Omega}_{f}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid\widehat{\bm{d}}\in\bm{H}^{1}(\widehat{\Omega}_{f}),\ \widehat{\bm{d}}=\bm{0}\ \text{on}\ \widehat{\Gamma}_{f}\right\},

\displaystyle\widehat{\bm{V}}_{0}^{\mathcal{M}}

\displaystyle:=\left\{(\bm{v}^{f},\widehat{\bm{v}}^{s})\in\bm{V}^{f}_{0}(t)\times\widehat{\bm{V}}^{s}_{0}\mid\bm{v}^{f}=\widehat{\bm{v}}^{s}\circ\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{-1}\ \text{on}\ \Gamma(t)\right\}.

Then, in view of (
2.19
), we describe the ALE weak formulation for FSI as follows:

Find
\left((\bm{u}^{f},\widehat{\bm{u}}^{s}),p^{f},\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s},\widehat{\bm{\eta}}^{f}\right)\in\bm{V}^{FSI}(t)\times Q^{f}(t)\times\widehat{\bm{V}}^{s}_{0}\times\widehat{\bm{V}}_{D}^{\mathcal{M}}
, with
\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s}=\widehat{\bm{\eta}}^{f}
on
\widehat{\Gamma}
and
\bm{w}^{f}=\partial_{t}\widehat{\bm{\eta}}^{f}\circ\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{-1}

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \rho^{f}(\partial_{t}^{M}\bm{u}^{f}+\nabla\bm{w}^{f}\bm{u}^{f}-\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot\bm{w}^{f}\bm{u}^{f},\bm{v}^{f})_{\Omega_{f}(t)}+\rho^{f}T^{f,t}(\bm{u}^{f}-\bm{w}^{f},\bm{u}^{f},\bm{v}^{f})\\

Q^{f,t}(q^{f},\bm{u}^{f})&=0,\\
(\partial_{t}\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s},\widehat{\bm{m}}^{s})_{\widehat{\Omega}_{s}}-(\widehat{\bm{u}}^{s},\widehat{\bm{m}}^{s})_{\widehat{\Omega}_{s}}&=0,\\
(\kappa^{f}\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{\bm{\eta}}^{f},\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{\bm{m}}^{f})_{\widehat{\Omega}_{f}}&=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(2.21)

for all
\left((\bm{v}^{f},\widehat{\bm{v}}^{s}),q^{f},\widehat{\bm{m}}^{s},\widehat{\bm{m}}^{f}\right)\in\bm{V}^{FSI}(t)\times Q^{f}(t)\times\widehat{\bm{V}}_{0}^{s}\times\widehat{\bm{V}}_{0}^{\mathcal{M}}
, where the bilinear/trilinear forms in (

\displaystyle B^{f,t}(\bm{u}^{f},\bm{v}^{f}):=\left(\bm{D}(\bm{u}^{f}),\bm{D}(\bm{v}^{f})\right)_{\Omega_{f}(t)},\qquad Q^{f,t}(q^{f},\bm{v}^{f}):=\left(q^{f},\nabla\cdot\bm{v}^{f}\right)_{\Omega_{f}(t)},

\displaystyle B^{s}(\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s},\widehat{\bm{v}}^{s}):=\left(\bm{D}(\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s}),\bm{D}(\widehat{\bm{v}}^{s})\right)_{\widehat{\Omega}_{s}},\qquad T^{f,t}(\bm{w}^{f},\bm{u}^{f},\bm{v}^{f}):=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\bm{v}^{f}\cdot(\nabla\bm{u}^{f})-\bm{u}^{f}\cdot(\nabla\bm{v}^{f}),\bm{w}^{f}\right)_{\Omega_{f}(t)}.

Remark 2.3
.

By means of a change of variables, the identity
((\bm{a}\cdot\nabla)\bm{b},\bm{c})=(\bm{c}\otimes\bm{a}\bm{:}\nabla\bm{b})
and Lemma
2.1
, we may reformulate the weak formulation (

) from the current domain
\Omega(t)
onto the reference domain

as follows:

Find
\left((\widehat{\bm{u}}^{f},\widehat{\bm{u}}^{s}),\widehat{p}^{f},\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s},\widehat{\bm{\eta}}^{f}\right)\in\widehat{\bm{V}}^{FSI}\times\widehat{Q}^{f}\times\widehat{\bm{V}}^{s}_{0}\times\widehat{\bm{V}}_{D}^{\mathcal{M}}
, with
\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s}=\widehat{\bm{\eta}}^{f}
on
\widehat{\Gamma}
, such that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \rho^{f}\left((\mathcal{P}(\partial_{t}^{M}\widehat{\bm{u}}^{f})+(\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{\bm{w}}\widehat{\bm{F}}_{f}^{-\top}-\tfrac{1}{2}\widehat{\bm{F}}_{f}^{-1}\bm{:}\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{\bm{w}}^{f})\mathcal{P}(\widehat{\bm{u}}),\widehat{J}_{f}\mathcal{P}(\widehat{\bm{v}}^{f})\right)_{\widehat{\Omega}_{f}}\\
+2\rho^{f}\mu^{f}\widehat{B}^{f}(\widehat{\bm{u}}^{f},\widehat{\bm{v}}^{f})+\rho^{f}\widehat{T}^{f}(\widehat{\bm{u}}^{f}-\widehat{\bm{w}}^{f},\widehat{\bm{u}}^{f},\widehat{\bm{v}}^{f})-\widehat{Q}^{f}(p^{f},\bm{v}^{f})+\rho^{s}(\partial_{t}\widehat{\bm{u}}^{s},\widehat{\bm{v}}^{s})_{\widehat{\Omega}_{s}}\\
+2\mu^{s}B^{s}(\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s},\widehat{\bm{v}}^{s})+\lambda^{s}(\widehat{\nabla}\cdot\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s},\widehat{\nabla}\cdot\widehat{\bm{v}}^{s})_{\widehat{\Omega}_{s}}-(\widehat{\bm{g}}^{f},J_{f}\mathcal{P}(\widehat{\bm{v}}^{f}))_{\widehat{\Omega}_{f}}&=0,\\
\widehat{Q}^{f}(\widehat{q}^{f},\widehat{\bm{u}}^{f})&=0,\\
(\partial_{t}\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s},\widehat{\bm{m}}^{s})_{\widehat{\Omega}_{s}}-(\widehat{\bm{u}}^{s},\widehat{\bm{m}}^{s})_{\widehat{\Omega}_{s}}&=0,\\
(\kappa^{f}\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{\bm{\eta}}^{f},\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{\bm{m}}^{f})_{\widehat{\Omega}_{f}}&=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(2.22)

for all
\left((\widehat{\bm{v}}^{f},\widehat{\bm{v}}^{s}),q^{f},\widehat{\bm{m}}^{s},\widehat{\bm{m}}^{f}\right)\in\widehat{\bm{V}}^{FSI}\times\widehat{Q}^{f}\times\widehat{\bm{V}}_{0}^{s}\times\widehat{\bm{V}}_{0}^{\mathcal{M}}
, where

\displaystyle\widehat{\bm{V}}^{FSI}:=\left\{(\widehat{\bm{v}}^{f},\widehat{\bm{v}}^{s})\in\bm{H}^{1}(\widehat{\Omega}_{f})\times\widehat{\bm{V}}^{s}_{0}\mid\widehat{\bm{v}}^{f}=\widehat{\bm{v}}^{s}\ \text{on}\ \widehat{\Gamma},\ \widehat{\bm{v}}^{f}=0\text{ on }\ \widehat{\Gamma}_{f}\right\},\quad\widehat{Q}^{f}:=L_{0}^{2}(\widehat{\Omega}_{f}),

\displaystyle\widehat{\bm{w}}^{f}=\partial_{t}\widehat{\bm{\eta}}^{f},\quad\bm{D}(\widehat{\bm{u}}^{f}):=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\widehat{\nabla}\mathcal{P}(\widehat{\bm{u}}^{f})\widehat{\bm{F}}_{f}^{-1}+\widehat{\bm{F}}_{f}^{-\top}(\widehat{\nabla}\mathcal{P}(\widehat{\bm{u}}^{f}))^{-\top}\right),

\displaystyle\widehat{B}^{f}(\widehat{\bm{u}}^{f},\widehat{\bm{v}}^{f}):=\left(\bm{D}(\widehat{\bm{u}}^{f}),\bm{D}(\widehat{\bm{v}}^{f})\widehat{J}_{f}\right)_{\widehat{\Omega}_{f}},\qquad\widehat{Q}^{f}(\widehat{p}^{f},\widehat{\bm{v}}^{f}):=\left(\widehat{p}^{f},\widehat{\nabla}\cdot\widehat{\bm{v}}^{f}\right)_{\widehat{\Omega}_{f}},

\displaystyle\widehat{T}^{f}(\widehat{\bm{u}}^{f}-\widehat{\bm{w}}^{f},\widehat{\bm{u}}^{f},\widehat{\bm{v}}^{f}):=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\big(\mathcal{P}(\widehat{\bm{v}}^{f})\otimes(\mathcal{P}(\widehat{\bm{u}}^{f})-\widehat{\bm{w}}^{f}),\widehat{\nabla}\mathcal{P}(\widehat{\bm{u}}^{f})\widehat{\bm{F}}_{f}^{-1}J_{f}\big)_{\widehat{\Omega}_{f}}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad-\left.\big(\mathcal{P}(\widehat{\bm{u}}^{f})\otimes(\mathcal{P}(\widehat{\bm{u}}^{f})-\widehat{\bm{w}}^{f}),\widehat{\nabla}\mathcal{P}(\widehat{\bm{v}}^{f})\widehat{\bm{F}}_{f}^{-1}\widehat{J}_{f}\big)_{\widehat{\Omega}_{f}}\right).

Let
E(t)
denote the sum of the kinetic energy of the fluid, the kinetic and elastic energy of the structure, i.e.,

\displaystyle E(t):=\frac{\rho^{f}}{2}\|\bm{u}^{f}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\Omega_{f}(t)})}+\frac{\rho^{s}}{2}\|\widehat{\bm{u}}^{s}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Omega}_{s})}+\left(\mu^{s}\|\widehat{\bm{D}}(\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s}(t))\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Omega}_{s})}+\frac{\lambda^{s}}{2}\|\widehat{\nabla}\cdot\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Omega}_{s})}\right).

Then we can follow a standard routine to get the energy stability of the weak solution.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\left((\bm{u}^{f},\widehat{\bm{u}}^{s}),p^{f},\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s},\widehat{\bm{\eta}}^{f}\right)\in\bm{V}^{FSI}(t)\times Q^{f}(t)\times\widehat{\bm{V}}^{s}_{0}\times\widehat{\bm{V}}_{D}^{\mathcal{M}}
be a solution to the weak problem (
2.21
), then for any
T>0
it holds the energy identity

\displaystyle\begin{split}E(T)+\int_{0}^{T}2\rho^{f}\mu^{f}\|\bm{D}(\bm{u}^{f})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{f}(t))}dt=\int_{0}^{T}(\bm{g}^{f},\bm{u}_{f})_{\Omega_{f}(t)}dt+E(0).\end{split}

(2.23)

In particular, if the source term
\bm{g}^{f}=\bm{0}
, then

\displaystyle E(T)+\int_{0}^{T}2\rho^{f}\mu^{f}\|\bm{D}(\bm{u}^{f})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{f}(t))}dt=E(0),

(2.24)

which means that the energy
E(t)
decreases monotonically over time
0<t\leq T
.

Proof.

Taking
(\bm{v}^{f},\widehat{\bm{v}}^{s},q^{f})=(\bm{u}^{f},\widehat{\bm{u}}^{s},p^{f})
in (
2.21
) and using
T^{f,t}(\bm{u}^{f}-\bm{w}^{f},\bm{u}^{f},\bm{u}^{f})=0
,
\partial_{t}\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s}=\widehat{\bm{u}}^{s}
and (
2.15
), we have

\displaystyle(\bm{g}^{f},\bm{u}^{f})_{\Omega_{f}(t)}=

\displaystyle\rho^{f}(\partial_{t}^{M}\bm{u}^{f}+\nabla\bm{w}^{f}\bm{u}-\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot\bm{w}^{f}\bm{u},\bm{u}^{f})_{\Omega_{f}(t)}+2\rho^{f}\mu^{f}\|\bm{D}(\bm{u}^{f})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{f}(t))}+\frac{\rho^{s}}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\widehat{\bm{u}}^{s}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Omega}_{s})}

\displaystyle\ \ +2\mu^{s}B^{s}(\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s},\partial_{t}\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s})_{\widehat{\Omega}_{s}}+\lambda^{s}(\widehat{\nabla}\cdot\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s},\widehat{\nabla}\cdot\partial_{t}\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s})_{\widehat{\Omega}_{s}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\rho^{f}}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\bm{u}^{f}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(_{\Omega_{f}(t)})}+\frac{\rho^{s}}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\widehat{\bm{u}}^{s}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Omega}_{s})}+2\rho^{f}\mu^{f}\|\bm{D}(\bm{u}^{f})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{f}(t))}

\displaystyle\ \ +\mu^{s}\frac{d}{dt}\|\bm{D}(\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Omega}_{s})}+\frac{\lambda^{s}}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\widehat{\nabla}\cdot\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Omega}_{s})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{dE(t)}{dt}+2\rho^{f}\mu^{f}\|\bm{D}(\bm{u}^{f})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{f}(t))}.

Integrating this relation from
0
to
T
gives the desired energy identity (
2.23
) immediately, and the result (
2.24
) follows. ∎

3
Temporal semi-discretization

In this section, we first introduce the time-discrete Piola-type ALE mapping. Based on this, we present two semi-discrete schemes corresponding to the non-conservative and conservative formulations, and establish the associated energy stability results.

3.1
The semi-discrete Piola-type ALE mapping

Let
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<...<t_{\mathrm{N}}=T
be a uniform division of the time interval
[0,T]
, with
t_{n}=n\tau
(
n=0,1,\cdots,N
) and the time step size
\tau=T/N
. We discretize in time the ALE mapping
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}(\widehat{\bm{x}},t)
in (
2.6
) by using a continuous piecewise linear interpolation. To this end, let

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{n}(\widehat{\bm{x}})=\widehat{\bm{x}}+\widehat{\bm{\eta}}^{f,n}(\widehat{\bm{x}}):\widehat{\Omega}_{f}\rightarrow\Omega_{f}^{n}:=\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{n}(\widehat{\Omega}_{f})

(3.1)

be an approximation of the ALE mapping
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}(\widehat{\bm{x}},t_{n})
for
n=1,\dots,N
. Then the linear interpolation of the mapping
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}(\widehat{\bm{x}},t)
for
t\in[t_{n-1},t_{n}]
, denoted by

\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{t}(\widehat{\bm{x}},t):\widehat{\Omega}_{f}\rightarrow\Omega_{f}^{t}:=\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{t}(\widehat{\Omega}_{f}),

is defined as

\displaystyle\bm{x}=\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{t}(\widehat{\bm{x}},t):=\frac{t-t_{n-1}}{\tau}\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{n}(\widehat{\bm{x}})+\frac{t_{n}-t}{\tau}\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{n-1}(\widehat{\bm{x}}),\ \ t\in[t_{n-1},t_{n}].

(3.2)

Notice that
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{t}(\widehat{\bm{x}},t_{n})=\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{n}(\widehat{\bm{x}})
for any
n
.

We define the fluid deformation gradient
\widehat{\bm{F}}_{f}^{t}
and its Jacobian determinant
\widehat{J}_{f}^{t}

as

\displaystyle\widehat{\bm{F}}_{f}^{t}:=\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{t}=\frac{t-t_{n-1}}{\tau}\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{n}+\frac{t_{n}-t}{\tau}\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{n-1},\quad\widehat{J}_{f}^{t}:=\det(\widehat{\bm{F}}_{f}^{t}),\quad t\in[t_{n-1},t_{n}]

(3.3)

and set

\displaystyle\widehat{\bm{F}}_{f}^{n}:=\widehat{\bm{F}}_{f}^{t}|_{t=t_{n}}=\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{n},\quad\widehat{J}_{f}^{n}:=\widehat{J}_{f}^{t}|_{t=t_{n}}=\det(\widehat{\bm{F}}_{f}^{n}).

Similar to (
2.9
), with (
3.1
) and (
3.3
) there hold for
t\in[t_{n-1},t_{n}]

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\widehat{J}_{f}^{t}=\widehat{J}_{f}^{t}(\widehat{\bm{F}}_{f}^{t})^{-\top}\bm{:}\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{\bm{w}}^{f,n}=\nabla\cdot{\bm{w}}_{t}^{f,n}\widehat{J}_{f}^{t},\qquad{\partial_{t}\widehat{\bm{F}}^{t}_{f}}=\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{\bm{w}}^{f,n}=\nabla{\bm{w}}_{t}^{f,n}\widehat{\bm{F}}^{t}_{f}.

(3.4)

With the temporally semi-discrete ALE mapping
(
3.2
), we set the fluid domain velocities
{\bm{w}}^{f,n}
on
\Omega_{f}^{n}

and
{\bm{w}}^{f,n}_{t}
on
\Omega_{f}^{t}
as follows:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}{\bm{w}}^{f,n}(\bm{x})={\bm{w}}^{f,n}\circ\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{n}(\widehat{\bm{x}})=\widehat{\bm{w}}^{f,n}(\widehat{\bm{x}}):=\left.{\partial_{t}\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{t}(\widehat{\bm{x}},t)}\right|_{t=t_{n}}=\frac{1}{\tau}\left(\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{n}(\widehat{\bm{x}})-\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{n-1}(\widehat{\bm{x}})\right),\\
{\bm{w}}^{f,n}_{t}(\bm{x},t):=\widehat{\bm{w}}^{f,n}\circ(\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{t})^{-1}(\bm{x},t),\quad t\in[t_{n-1},t_{n}].\end{array}\right.

For convenience, we write

\displaystyle{\bm{w}}_{j}^{f,n}:={\bm{w}}^{f,n}_{t}|_{t=t_{j}},\quad j=n,n-1

(3.7)

and easily see
{\bm{w}}^{f,n}_{n}={\bm{w}}^{f,n}.

Introduce the semi-discrete Piola-type ALE mapping

\displaystyle\bm{v}\circ\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{t}=\mathcal{P}^{t}(\widehat{\bm{v}}):=\frac{1}{\widehat{J}_{f}^{t}}\widehat{\bm{F}}_{f}^{t}\widehat{\bm{v}},\quad\text{for }\widehat{\bm{v}}\in\bm{H}(\widehat{\mbox{div}},\widehat{\Omega}_{f})

For simplicity we set

\displaystyle\bm{v}^{n}\circ\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{n}=\mathcal{P}^{n}(\widehat{\bm{v}}):=\mathcal{P}^{t}(\widehat{\bm{v}})|_{t=t_{n}}=\frac{1}{\widehat{J}_{f}^{n}}\widehat{\bm{F}}_{f}^{n}\widehat{\bm{v}}.

\mathcal{X}_{i}^{j}:=\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{j}\circ(\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{i})^{-1}

\displaystyle\bm{v}\circ\mathcal{X}_{i}^{j}

\displaystyle=(\frac{\widehat{\bm{F}}_{f}^{i}}{\widehat{J}_{f}^{i}}\widehat{\bm{v}})\circ(\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{i})^{-1}=\left(\frac{\widehat{J}_{f}^{j}}{\widehat{J}_{f}^{i}}\widehat{\bm{F}}_{f}^{i}(\widehat{\bm{F}}^{j}_{f})^{-1}\bm{v}\circ\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{j}\right)\circ(\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{i})^{-1},\quad\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{V}^{f}(t_{j}).

Remark 3.1
.

As the semi-discrete ALE mapping
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{t}

Hence, for
\bm{v}=\bm{u}
we have the following integral-form Reynolds transport formulas:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\bm{v}^{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{f}^{n})}-\|\bm{v}^{n-1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{f}^{n-1})}=&2\int^{t_{n}}_{t_{n-1}}\left(\partial^{M}_{t}\bm{v}+\nabla{\bm{w}_{t}^{f,n}}\bm{v}-\frac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot\bm{w}_{t}^{f,n}\bm{v},\bm{v}\right)_{\Omega_{f}(t)}dt\\
=&2\int^{t_{n}}_{t_{n-1}}\left(\partial_{t}\bm{v}+({\bm{w}_{t}^{f,n}}\cdot\nabla)\bm{v}+\frac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot\bm{w}_{t}^{f,n}\bm{v},\bm{v}\right)_{\Omega_{f}(t)}dt.\end{split}

(3.12)

which is an integral form of the GCL (
2.18
), and that

\displaystyle\|\bm{v}^{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{f}^{n})}-\|\bm{v}^{n-1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{f}^{n-1})}=

\displaystyle 2\int^{t_{n}}_{t_{n-1}}\left((\nabla{\bm{w}_{t}^{f,n}}-\frac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot\bm{w}_{t}^{f,n})\bm{v},\bm{v}\right)_{\Omega_{f}(t)}dt

(3.13)

if
\partial^{M}_{t}\bm{v}=0
or equivalently,
\partial_{t}\widehat{\bm{v}}=0
.

3.2
Temporally semi-discrete schemes

We first introduce the following temporally semi-discrete finite element spaces:

\displaystyle\bm{V}^{f,n}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\bm{v}^{f}:\Omega_{f}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid\bm{v}^{f}=\mathcal{P}^{n}(\widehat{\bm{v}}^{f})\circ(\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{n})^{-1},\ \widehat{\bm{v}}^{f}\in\bm{H}^{1}(\widehat{\Omega}_{f})\right\},

\displaystyle\bm{V}^{f,n}_{0}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\bm{v}^{f}\in\bm{V}^{f,n}\mid\bm{v}=\bm{0}\ \text{on}\ \Gamma_{f}^{n}\right\},\ \bm{W}^{f,n}:=\left\{\bm{v}^{f}\in\bm{V}^{f,n}_{0}\mid\nabla\cdot\bm{v}^{f}=0\right\},

\displaystyle Q^{f,n}

\displaystyle:=\left\{q^{f}:\Omega_{f}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\mid q^{f}=\widehat{q}^{f}\circ(\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{n})^{-1},\ \widehat{q}^{f}\in L_{0}^{2}(\widehat{\Omega}_{f})\right\},

\displaystyle\bm{V}^{FSI,n}

\displaystyle:=\left\{(\bm{v}^{f},\widehat{\bm{v}}^{s})\in\bm{V}^{f,n}_{0}\times\widehat{\bm{V}}^{s}_{0}\mid\bm{v}^{f}=\widehat{\bm{v}}^{s}\circ(\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{n})^{-1}\ \text{on}\ \Gamma^{n}\right\}.

For any time-dependent function
g
, denote by
g^{n}
an approximation of
g(t_{n})
(
n=1,2,\cdots,N
)
and introduce the following two backward difference operators:

\displaystyle\tilde{D}_{t}g^{n}:=\frac{g^{n}-g^{n-1}}{\tau},\qquad\tilde{D}_{t}^{M}g^{n}:=\frac{g^{n}-g^{n-1}\circ\mathcal{X}_{n}^{n-1}}{\tau}.

(3.14)

Given
(\bm{u}^{f,0},\widehat{\bm{u}}^{s,0},\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s,0})=(\bm{u}_{0}^{f},\widehat{\bm{u}}^{s}_{0},\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s}_{0})
, based on equations (
2.19
) and weak formulation (
2.21
) we give the following two types of implicit temporal semi-discretizations for the FSI model (
2.2
)-(
2.5
):

1.

Scheme-A
:

Find
\left((\bm{u}^{f,n},\widehat{\bm{u}}^{s,n}),p^{f,n},\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s,n},\widehat{\bm{\eta}}^{f,n}\right)\in\bm{V}^{FSI,n}\times Q^{f,n}\times\widehat{\bm{V}}^{s}_{0}\times\widehat{\bm{V}}_{D}^{\mathcal{M}}
for
n=1,2,\cdots,N
, with
\widehat{\bm{\eta}}^{f,n}=\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s,n}
on
\widehat{\Gamma}
and
\bm{w}^{f,n}=\tilde{D}_{t}\widehat{\bm{\eta}}^{f,n}\ \circ(\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{n})^{-1}
, such that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \rho^{f}(\tilde{D}_{t}^{M}\bm{u}^{f,n}+\nabla{\bm{w}^{f,n}}\bm{u}^{f,n}-\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot{\bm{w}^{f,n}}\bm{u}^{f,n},\bm{v}^{f})_{\Omega_{f}^{n}}\\
+\rho^{f}T^{f,n}(\bm{u}^{f,n}-{\bm{w}^{f,n}},\bm{u}^{f,n},\bm{v}^{f})+2\rho^{f}\mu^{f}B^{f,n}(\bm{u}^{f,n},\bm{v}^{f})\\
-Q^{f,n}(p^{f,n},\bm{v}^{f})+\rho^{s}(\tilde{D}_{t}\widehat{\bm{u}}^{s,n},\widehat{\bm{v}}^{s})_{\widehat{\Omega}_{s}}+2\mu^{s}B^{s}(\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s,n},\widehat{\bm{v}}^{s})\\
+\lambda^{s}(\widehat{\nabla}\cdot\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s,n},\widehat{\nabla}\cdot\widehat{\bm{v}}^{s})_{\widehat{\Omega}_{s}}-(\bm{g}^{f,n},\bm{v}^{f})_{\Omega_{f}^{n}}&=0,\\
Q^{f,n}(q^{f},\bm{u}^{f,n})&=0,\\
(\tilde{D}_{t}\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s,n},\widehat{\bm{m}}^{s})_{\widehat{\Omega}_{s}}-(\widehat{\bm{u}}^{s,n},\widehat{\bm{m}}^{s})_{\widehat{\Omega}_{s}}&=0,\\
(\kappa^{f}\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{\bm{\eta}}^{f,n},\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{\bm{m}}^{f})_{\widehat{\Omega}_{f}}&=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(3.15)

for all
\left((\bm{v}^{f},\widehat{\bm{v}}^{s}),q^{f},\widehat{\bm{m}}^{s},\widehat{\bm{m}}^{f}\right)\in\bm{V}^{FSI,n}\times Q^{f,n}\times\widehat{\bm{V}}_{0}^{s}\times\widehat{\bm{V}}_{0}^{\mathcal{M}}
.
Here and in what follows,

\displaystyle T^{f,n}(\bm{w}^{f,n},\bm{u}^{f,n},\bm{v}^{f}):=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\bm{v}^{f}\cdot(\nabla\bm{u}^{f,n})-\bm{u}^{f,n}\cdot(\nabla\bm{v}^{f}),\bm{w}^{f,n}\right)_{\Omega_{f}^{n}},

\displaystyle B^{f,n}(\bm{u}^{f,n},\bm{v}^{f}):=\left(\bm{D}(\bm{u}^{f,n}),\bm{D}(\bm{v}^{f})\right)_{\Omega_{f}^{n}},\qquad Q^{f,n}(q^{f},\bm{v}^{f}):=\left(q^{f},\nabla\cdot\bm{v}^{f}\right)_{\Omega_{f}^{n}}.

2.

Scheme-B
:

Find
\left((\bm{u}^{f,n},\widehat{\bm{u}}^{s,n}),p^{f,n},\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s,n},\widehat{\bm{\eta}}^{f,n}\right)\in\bm{V}^{FSI,n}\times Q^{f,n}\times\widehat{\bm{V}}^{s}_{0}\times\widehat{\bm{V}}_{D}^{\mathcal{M}}
for
n=1,2,\cdots,N
, with
\widehat{\bm{\eta}}^{f,n}=\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s,n}
on
\widehat{\Gamma}
and
\bm{w}^{f,n}=\tilde{D}_{t}\widehat{\bm{\eta}}^{f,n}\ \circ(\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{n})^{-1}
, such that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{\rho^{f}}{\tau}\left((\bm{u}^{f,n},\bm{v}^{f})_{\Omega_{f}^{n}}-(\bm{u}^{f,n-1},\bm{v}^{f}\circ\mathcal{X}_{n-1}^{n})_{\Omega_{f}^{n-1}}\right)\\
-\rho^{f}\left((\nabla{\bm{w}^{f,n}})^{\top}\bm{u}^{f,n}-\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot{\bm{w}^{f,n}}\bm{u}^{f,n},\bm{v}^{f}\right)_{\Omega_{f}^{n}}+\rho^{f}T^{f,n}(\bm{u}^{f,n}-{\bm{w}^{f,n}},\bm{u}^{f,n},\bm{v}^{f})\\
+2\rho^{f}\mu^{f}B^{f,n}(\bm{u}^{f,n},\bm{v}^{f})-Q^{f,n}(p^{f,n},\bm{v}^{f})+\rho^{s}(\tilde{D}_{t}\widehat{\bm{u}}^{s,n},\widehat{\bm{v}}^{s})_{\widehat{\Omega}_{s}}\\
+2\mu^{s}B^{s}(\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s,n},\widehat{\bm{v}}^{s})+\lambda^{s}(\widehat{\nabla}\cdot\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s,n},\widehat{\nabla}\cdot\widehat{\bm{v}}^{s})_{\widehat{\Omega}_{s}}-(\bm{g}^{f,n},\bm{v}^{f})_{\Omega_{f}^{n}}&=0,\\
Q^{f,n}(q^{f},\bm{u}^{f,n})&=0,\\
(\tilde{D}_{t}\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s,n},\widehat{\bm{m}}^{s})_{\widehat{\Omega}_{s}}-(\widehat{\bm{u}}^{s,n},\widehat{\bm{m}}^{s})_{\widehat{\Omega}_{s}}&=0,\\
(\kappa^{f}\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{\bm{\eta}}^{f,n},\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{\bm{m}}^{f})_{\widehat{\Omega}_{f}}&=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(3.16)

for all
\left((\bm{v}^{f},\widehat{\bm{v}}^{s}),q^{f},\widehat{\bm{m}}^{s},\widehat{\bm{m}}^{f}\right)\in\bm{V}^{FSI,n}\times Q^{f,n}\times\widehat{\bm{V}}_{0}^{s}\times\widehat{\bm{V}}_{0}^{\mathcal{M}}
.

Remark 3.2
.

Notice that Scheme-A is constructed directly from the weak formulation (
2.21
), where the term

(\partial_{t}^{M}\bm{u}^{f}+\nabla\bm{w}^{f}\bm{u}^{f}-\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot\bm{w}^{f}\bm{u}^{f},\bm{v}^{f})_{\Omega_{f}(t)}
is discretized as

(\tilde{D}_{t}^{M}\bm{u}^{f,n}+\nabla{\bm{w}^{f,n}}\bm{u}^{f,n}-\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot{\bm{w}^{f,n}}\bm{u}^{f,n},\bm{v}^{f})_{\Omega_{f}^{n}}.

Scheme-B is based on (
2.21
) and the identity

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}^{M}\bm{u}^{f}+\nabla\bm{w}^{f}\bm{u}^{f}-\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot\bm{w}^{f}\bm{u}^{f},\bm{v}^{f})_{\Omega_{f}(t)}=\frac{d}{dt}\bigl(\bm{u},\bm{v}\bigr)_{\Omega_{f}(t)}-\Bigl((\nabla\bm{w}^{f})^{\top}\bm{u}-\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot\bm{w}^{f}\,\bm{u},\bm{v}\Bigr)_{\Omega_{f}(t)}

for
\partial_{t}^{M}\bm{v}^{f}=0
(cf. Lemma
2.3
), where the right-hand side of the identity is discretized as

\frac{(\bm{u}^{f,n},\bm{v}^{f})_{\Omega_{f}^{n}}-(\bm{u}^{f,n-1},\bm{v}^{f}\circ\mathcal{X}_{n-1}^{n})_{\Omega_{f}^{n-1}}}{\tau}-\left((\nabla{\bm{w}^{f,n}})^{\top}\bm{u}^{f,n}-\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot{\bm{w}^{f,n}}\bm{u}^{f,n},\bm{v}^{f}\right)_{\Omega_{f}^{n}}.

In particular, Scheme-A and Scheme-B are equivalent if
\nabla{\bm{w}^{f,n}}=0
for any
n
, which means that the fluid domain is fixed all the time.

3.3
Energy stability analysis

We first introduce some notations.
For
n=1,2,\cdots,N
and
t\in[t_{n-1},t_{n}]
we define

\displaystyle\mathbb{F}_{n}(t):=\widehat{\bm{F}}^{t}_{f}(\widehat{\bm{F}}^{n}_{f})^{-1},\quad\mathbb{J}_{n}(t):=\frac{\widehat{J}_{f}^{n}}{\widehat{J}_{f}^{t}},\quad\mathbb{G}_{n}(t):=\mathbb{J}_{n}(t)\mathbb{F}_{n}(t),

(3.17)

and denote

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} G_{1,n}:=&\sup_{t\in[t_{n-1},t_{n}]}\Bigl(1+\tfrac{1}{2}\|(\mathbb{G}_{n-1}(t))^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}({\Omega_{f}^{n-1}})}\|\mathbb{G}_{n-1}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}({\Omega_{f}^{n-1}})}\Bigr)\|\mathbb{G}_{n-1}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}({\Omega_{f}^{n-1}})},\\
G_{2,n}:=&\sup_{t\in[t_{n-1},t_{n}]}\tfrac{1}{2}\Bigl(\|\mathbb{J}_{n}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{f}^{n})}+\bigl(2+\tfrac{1}{2}\|\mathbb{F}_{n}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{f}^{n})}\bigr)\|(\mathbb{F}_{n}(t))^{-\top}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{f}^{n})}\|\mathbb{G}_{n}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{f}^{n})}\\
&\ +\tfrac{1}{2}\|(\mathbb{F}_{n}(t))^{-\top}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{f}^{n})}\|\mathbb{F}_{n}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{f}^{n})}\|\mathbb{G}_{n}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{f}^{n})}\Bigr),\end{aligned}\right.

(3.18)

where
\|\mathbb{A}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{f}^{n})}:=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}\limits_{x\in\Omega_{f}^{n}}\left(\mathbb{A}\bm{:}\mathbb{A}\right)^{1/2}
for any
d\times d
matrix function
\mathbb{A}(\bm{x})
with ‘
\bm{:}
’ denoting the Frobenius inner product.

We have the following lemma for the stability analysis of the two semi-discrete schemes.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\widehat{\bm{v}}\in\bm{H}(\widehat{\rm{div}},\widehat{\Omega}_{f})
and
\bm{v}=\mathcal{P}^{t}(\widehat{\bm{v}})\circ(\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{t})^{-1}
. Then for
n=1,2,\cdots,N
there hold

\displaystyle\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\Bigl((\nabla\bm{w}_{t}^{f,n}-\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla\!\cdot\bm{w}_{t}^{f,n})\bm{v},\bm{v}\Bigr)_{\Omega_{f}(t)}dt\leq\tau G_{1,n}\|\nabla{\bm{w}}_{n-1}^{f,n}\|_{L^{\infty}({\Omega_{f}^{n-1}})}\|\bm{v}^{n-1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\Omega_{f}^{n-1}})}

(3.19)

and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\Bigl((\nabla\bm{w}_{t}^{f,n}-\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla\!\cdot\bm{w}_{t}^{f,n})\bm{v},\bm{v}\Bigr)_{\Omega_{f}(t)}dt\,-\tau\Bigl((\nabla\bm{w}^{f,n}-\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla\!\cdot\bm{w}^{f,n})\bm{v}^{n},\bm{v}^{n}\Bigr)_{\Omega_{f}^{n}}\\
\leq&\ \ \tau^{2}G_{2,n}\|\nabla\bm{w}^{f,n}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{f}^{n})}\|\bm{v}^{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\Omega_{f}^{n}})}.\end{aligned}

(3.20)

Proof.

We first show (
3.19
).
Apply (
3.1
), (
3.4
) and variable substitution to get

\displaystyle g(t):=

\displaystyle\Bigl((\nabla\bm{w}_{t}^{f,n}-\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla\!\cdot\bm{w}_{t}^{f,n})\bm{v},\bm{v}\Bigr)_{\Omega_{f}(t)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\left(\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{\bm{w}}^{f,n}(\widehat{\bm{F}}_{f}^{t})^{-1}-\tfrac{1}{2}((\widehat{\bm{F}}_{f}^{t})^{-\top}\bm{:}\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{\bm{w}}^{f,n})\,\bm{I}\right)\frac{\widehat{\bm{F}}^{t}_{f}}{\widehat{J}_{f}^{t}}\widehat{\bm{v}},\frac{\widehat{\bm{F}}^{t}_{f}}{\widehat{J}_{f}^{t}}\widehat{\bm{v}}\,\widehat{J}_{f}^{t}\right)_{\widehat{\Omega}_{f}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Bigl(\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{\bm{w}}^{f,n}\widehat{\bm{v}},\frac{\widehat{\bm{F}}^{t}_{f}}{\widehat{J}_{f}^{t}}\widehat{\bm{v}}\,\Bigr)_{\widehat{\Omega}_{f}}-\tfrac{1}{2}\Bigl(((\widehat{\bm{F}}_{f}^{t})^{-\top}\bm{:}\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{\bm{w}}^{f,n})\frac{\widehat{\bm{F}}^{t}_{f}}{\widehat{J}_{f}^{t}}\widehat{\bm{v}},\widehat{\bm{F}}^{t}_{f}\widehat{\bm{v}}\Bigr)_{\widehat{\Omega}_{f}}.

(3.21)

Using variable substitution again, in view of (
3.17
) and the identity
A\bm{:}(BC)=(AC^{\top})\bm{:}B
for
d\times d
matrices
A,B
and

\displaystyle\begin{split}g(t)=&\left(\nabla{\bm{w}}_{n-1}^{f,n}\widehat{\bm{F}}_{f}^{n-1}(\widehat{\bm{F}}_{f}^{t})^{-1}\mathbb{G}_{n-1}(t)\bm{v}^{n-1},\mathbb{G}_{n-1}(t)\bm{v}^{n-1}\frac{\widehat{J}_{f}^{t}}{\widehat{J}_{f}^{n-1}}\right)_{\Omega_{f}^{n-1}}\\

&\quad-\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\widehat{J}_{f}^{t}}{\widehat{J}_{f}^{n-1}}\Bigl(\bigl(\widehat{\bm{F}}_{f}^{{n-1}}\bigl(\widehat{\bm{F}}_{f}^{t})^{-1}\bigr)^{\top}\!\bm{:}\nabla{\bm{w}}_{n-1}^{f,{n}}\Bigr)\ \mathbb{G}_{{n-1}}(t)\bm{v}^{n-1},\mathbb{G}_{{n-1}}(t)\bm{v}^{n-1}\right)_{\Omega_{f}^{{n-1}}}\\

which plus the Hölder’s inequality gives

\displaystyle\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}g(t)dt\leq

\displaystyle\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\Bigl(1+\tfrac{1}{2}\|(\mathbb{G}_{n-1}(t))^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}({\Omega_{f}^{n-1}})}\|\mathbb{G}_{n-1}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}({\Omega_{f}^{n-1}})}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\|\mathbb{G}_{n-1}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}({\Omega_{f}^{n-1}})}\|\nabla{\bm{w}}_{n-1}^{f,n}\|_{L^{\infty}({\Omega_{f}^{n-1}})}\|\bm{v}^{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\Omega_{f}^{n-1}})}dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,\tau G_{1,n}\|\nabla{\bm{w}}_{n-1}^{f,n}\|_{L^{\infty}({\Omega_{f}^{n-1}})}\|\bm{v}^{n-1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\Omega_{f}^{n-1}})},

i.e. the desired estimate (

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Bigl(\widehat{J}_{f}^{n}\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{\bm{w}}^{f,n}(\widehat{\bm{F}}^{n}_{f})^{-1}\frac{\widehat{\bm{F}}^{n}_{f}}{\widehat{J}_{f}^{n}}\widehat{\bm{v}},\frac{\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{\bm{w}}^{f,n}(\widehat{\bm{F}}^{n}_{f})^{-1}}{\widehat{J}_{f}^{t}}\frac{\widehat{\bm{F}}^{n}_{f}}{\widehat{J}_{f}^{n}}\widehat{J}_{f}^{n}\widehat{\bm{v}}\,\Bigr)_{\widehat{\Omega}_{f}}

due to the Taylor expansion,

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
n
be a positive integer, and let
\tau
,
\rm B
and
a_{k},b_{k},c_{k},{\gamma}_{k}

(k=1,\cdots,n)
be nonnegative numbers such that

a_{n}+\tau\sum_{k=1}^{n}b_{k}\leq\tau\sum_{k=1}^{n}\gamma_{k}a_{k}+\tau\sum_{k=1}^{n}c_{k}+\rm B,\qquad for\ n\geq 1.

(3.23)

Suppose that
\tau\gamma_{k}<1
for all
k
, and set
\delta_{k}=(1-\tau\gamma_{k})^{-1}
. Then

a_{n}+\tau\sum_{k=1}^{n}b_{k}\leq\rm{exp}\big(\tau\sum_{k=1}^{n}\gamma_{k}\delta_{k}\big)\big(\tau\sum_{k=1}^{n}c_{k}+B\big).

(3.24)

We are now at a position to define the semi-discrete energy. Let
E^{n}
denote the sum of the semi-discrete kinetic energy of the fluid and the semi-discrete kinetic and elastic energy of the structure at time
t_{n}
, i.e.

\displaystyle E^{n}:=\frac{\rho^{f}}{2}\|\bm{u}^{f,n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\Omega_{f}^{n}})}+\frac{\rho^{s}}{2}\|\widehat{\bm{u}}^{s,n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Omega}_{s})}+\left(\mu^{s}\|\bm{D}(\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s,n})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Omega}_{s})}+\frac{\lambda^{s}}{2}\|\widehat{\nabla}\cdot{\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s,n}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Omega}_{s})}\right),

and let
\tilde{E}_{f}^{n}
and
\tilde{E}_{s,d}^{n}
denote respectively the fluid viscous dissipation and the structure numerical dissipation at time
t_{n}
with

\displaystyle\tilde{E}^{n}_{f}:=

\displaystyle\tau\rho^{f}\mu^{f}\|\bm{D}(\bm{u}^{f,n})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{f}^{n})},

\displaystyle\tilde{E}_{s,d}^{n}:=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\rho^{s}\|\widehat{\bm{u}}^{s,n}-\widehat{\bm{u}}^{s,n-1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\widehat{\Omega}_{s}})}+2\mu^{s}\|\bm{D}(\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s,n})-\bm{D}(\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s,n-1})\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\widehat{\Omega}_{s}})}+\lambda^{s}\|\widehat{\nabla}\cdot\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s,n}-\widehat{\nabla}\cdot\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s,n-1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\widehat{\Omega}_{s}})}\bigr).

Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
show energy estimates of the two semi-discrete schemes.

Theorem 3.1

(Stability of Scheme-A)
.

Let
(\bm{u}^{f,n},\widehat{\bm{u}}^{s,n},p^{f,n},\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s,n},\widehat{\bm{\eta}}^{f,n})
solve the semi-discrete system (
3.15
) for . If the time step
\tau
satisfies

\tau<\tau_{0}:=1/\max\limits_{n=1,\cdots,N}\Bigl(\tfrac{3}{2}\|\nabla{\bm{w}^{f,n}}\|_{L^{\infty}({\Omega_{f}^{n}})}+G_{1,n}\|\nabla{\bm{w}_{n-1}^{f,n}}\|_{L^{\infty}({\Omega_{f}^{n-1}})}\Bigr)

then the following energy estimate holds:

\displaystyle E^{N}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(\tilde{E}^{n}_{f}+\tilde{E}^{n}_{s,d}+\tfrac{\rho^{f}}{2}\|\bm{u}^{f,n-1}\circ\mathcal{X}^{n-1}_{n}-\bm{u}^{f,n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{f}^{n})}\right)\lesssim\tau\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|\bm{g}^{f,n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{f}^{n})}+E^{0}.

(3.25)

Proof.

Taking
(\bm{v}^{f},\widehat{\bm{v}}^{s},q^{f})=(\tau\bm{u}^{f,n},\tau\widehat{\bm{u}}^{s,n},p^{f,n})
in the first two equations of (
3.15
), using the skew-symmetry property of
T^{f,n}(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)
and the relation
\tilde{D}_{t}\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s,n}=\widehat{\bm{u}}^{s,n}
due to the third equation of (
3.15
), and applying the identity
2a(a-b)=a^{2}-b^{2}+(a-b)^{2}
for the terms
(\tilde{D}_{t}\widehat{\bm{u}}^{s,n},\widehat{\bm{u}}^{s,n})_{\widehat{\Omega}_{s}}
and
B^{s}(\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s,n},\widehat{\bm{v}}^{s})
, we get

\displaystyle\rho^{f}\tau(\tilde{D}_{t}^{M}\bm{u}^{f,n},\bm{u}^{f,n})_{\Omega_{f}^{n}}+\tau\rho^{f}(\nabla{\bm{w}^{f,n}}\bm{u}^{f,n}-\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot{\bm{w}^{f,n}}\bm{u}^{f,n},\bm{u}^{f,n})_{\Omega_{f}^{n}}

\displaystyle+2\tau\rho^{f}\mu^{f}\|\bm{D}(\bm{u}^{f,n})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{f}^{n})}+\tfrac{\rho^{s}}{2}\left(\|\widehat{\bm{u}}^{s,n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\widehat{\Omega}_{s}})}-\|\widehat{\bm{u}}^{s,n-1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\widehat{\Omega}_{s}})}+\|\widehat{\bm{u}}^{s,n}-\widehat{\bm{u}}^{s,n-1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\widehat{\Omega}_{s}})}\right)

\displaystyle+\mu^{s}(\|\bm{D}(\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s,n})\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\widehat{\Omega}_{s}})}-\|\bm{D}(\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s,n-1})\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\widehat{\Omega}_{s}})}+\|\bm{D}(\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s,n})-\bm{D}(\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s,n-1})\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\widehat{\Omega}_{s}})})

\displaystyle+\tfrac{\lambda^{s}}{2}(\|\widehat{\nabla}\cdot\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s,n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\widehat{\Omega}_{s}})}-\|\widehat{\nabla}\cdot\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s,n-1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\widehat{\Omega}_{s}})}+\|\widehat{\nabla}\cdot\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s,n}-\widehat{\nabla}\cdot\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s,n-1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\widehat{\Omega}_{s}})})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau(\bm{g}^{f,n},\bm{u}^{f,n})_{\Omega_{f}^{n}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\epsilon\tau\|\bm{g}^{f,n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{f}^{n})}+\tfrac{\tau}{4\epsilon}\|\bm{u}^{f,n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{f}^{n})},\quad\forall\epsilon>0.

(3.26)

For the first term of (
3.1
), by (
3.11
) and the identity
-2ab=-a^{2}-b^{2}+(a-b)^{2}
we get

\displaystyle\tau(\tilde{D}_{t}^{M}\bm{u}^{f,n},\bm{u}^{f,n})_{\Omega_{f}(t_{n})}=

\displaystyle(\bm{u}^{f,n},\bm{u}^{f,n})_{\Omega_{f}^{n}}-\rho^{f}(\bm{u}^{f,n-1}\circ\mathcal{X}_{n}^{n-1},\bm{u}^{f,n})_{\Omega_{f}^{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\|\bm{u}^{f,n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\Omega_{f}^{n}})}-\tfrac{1}{2}(|\mathcal{P}^{n}(\widehat{\bm{u}}^{f,n-1})|^{2},\widehat{J}_{f}^{n})_{\widehat{\Omega}_{f}}+\tfrac{1}{2}\|\bm{u}^{f,n-1}\circ\mathcal{X}^{n-1}_{n}-\bm{u}^{f,n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{f}^{n})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\Bigl(\|\bm{u}^{f,n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\Omega_{f}^{n}})}-\|\bm{u}^{f,n-1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{f}^{n-1})}\Bigr)+\tfrac{1}{2}\|\bm{u}^{f,n-1}\circ\mathcal{X}^{n-1}_{n}-\bm{u}^{f,n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{f}^{n})}

\displaystyle-\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\|\mathcal{P}^{n}(\widehat{\bm{u}}^{f,n-1})\circ(\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{n})^{-1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{f}^{n})}-\|\bm{u}^{f,n-1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{f}^{n-1})}\right).

(3.27)

For the last term of the above equation, we apply (
3.13
) and (
3.19
) with

\bm{v}=\mathcal{P}^{t}(\widehat{\bm{u}}^{f,n-1})\circ(\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{t})^{-1}
to obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|\mathcal{P}^{n}(\widehat{\bm{u}}^{f,n-1})\circ(\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{n})^{-1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{f}^{n})}-\|\bm{u}^{f,n-1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{f}^{n-1})}\\
=&\int^{t_{n}}_{t_{n-1}}\left((\nabla{\bm{w}_{t}^{f,n}}-\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot\bm{w}_{t}^{f,n})\mathcal{P}^{t}(\widehat{\bm{u}}^{f,n-1})\circ(\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{t})^{-1},\mathcal{P}^{t}(\widehat{\bm{u}}^{f,n-1})\circ(\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{t})^{-1}\right)_{\Omega_{f}(t)}dt\\
\leq&\,\tau G_{1,n}\|\nabla{\bm{w}_{n-1}^{f,n}}\|_{L^{\infty}({\Omega_{f}^{n-1}})}\|\bm{u}^{f,n-1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\Omega_{f}^{n-1}})}.\end{split}

(3.28)

Moreover, for the second term of (
3.1
) we have

\displaystyle-(\nabla{\bm{w}^{f,n}}\bm{u}^{f,n}-\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot{\bm{w}^{f,n}}\bm{u}^{f,n},\bm{u}^{f,n})_{\Omega_{f}^{n}}\leq\tfrac{3}{2}\|\nabla{\bm{w}^{f,n}}\|_{L^{\infty}({\Omega_{f}^{n}})}\|\bm{u}^{f,n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\Omega_{f}^{n}})}.

(3.29)

Combining (
3.1
)-(
3.29
), summing them from
n=1
to
N
, using the Poincaré inequality and Korn’s inequality to get
\|\bm{u}^{f,n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{f}^{n})}^{2}\leq C_{K}\|\bm{D}(\boldsymbol{u}^{f,n})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{f}^{n})}^{2}
, and taking
\epsilon=\tfrac{4C_{k}}{\rho^{f}\mu^{f}}
, we have

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\rho^{f}\|\bm{u}^{f,N}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\Omega_{f}^{N}})}+\rho^{s}\|\widehat{\bm{u}}^{s,N}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\widehat{\Omega}_{s}})}+2\mu^{s}\|\bm{D}(\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s,N})\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\widehat{\Omega}_{s}})}+\lambda^{s}\|\widehat{\nabla}\cdot\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s,N}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\widehat{\Omega}_{s}})}\right)

\displaystyle+\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\Bigr(4\tau\rho^{f}\mu^{f}\|\nabla\bm{u}^{f,n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{f}^{n})}+\rho^{f}\|\bm{u}^{f,n-1}\circ\mathcal{X}^{n-1}_{n}-\bm{u}^{f,n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{f}^{n})}+\rho^{s}\|\widehat{\bm{u}}^{s,n}-\widehat{\bm{u}}^{s,n-1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\widehat{\Omega}_{s}})}

\displaystyle+2\mu^{s}\|\bm{D}(\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s,n})-\bm{D}(\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s,n-1})\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\widehat{\Omega}_{s}})}+\lambda^{s}\|\widehat{\nabla}\cdot\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s,n}-\widehat{\nabla}\cdot\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s,n-1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\widehat{\Omega}_{s}})}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,\tau\rho^{f}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\Bigl(G_{1,n}\|\nabla{\bm{w}_{n-1}^{f,n}}\|_{L^{\infty}({\Omega_{f}^{n-1}})}\|\bm{u}^{f,n-1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\Omega_{f}^{n-1}})}+\tfrac{3}{2}\|\nabla{\bm{w}^{f,n}}\|_{L^{\infty}({\Omega_{f}^{n}})}\|\bm{u}^{f,n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\Omega_{f}^{n}})}\Bigr)

\displaystyle+\epsilon\tau\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|\bm{g}^{f,n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{f}^{n})}+\tfrac{1}{2}\Bigl(\rho^{f}\|\bm{u}^{f,0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\Omega_{f}^{N}})}+\rho^{s}\|\widehat{\bm{u}}^{s,0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\widehat{\Omega}_{s}})}+2\mu^{s}\|\bm{D}(\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s,0})\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\widehat{\Omega}_{s}})}+\lambda^{s}\|\widehat{\nabla}\cdot\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s,0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\widehat{\Omega}_{s}})}\Bigr).

Applying the discrete Grönwall’s inequality in Lemma
3.2
, we finally obtain

\displaystyle E^{N}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(\tilde{E}^{n}_{f}+\tilde{E}_{s,d}^{n}+\tfrac{\rho^{f}}{2}\|\bm{u}^{f,n-1}\circ\mathcal{X}^{n-1}_{n}-\bm{u}^{f,n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{f}^{n})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\left(\tau\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{\gamma_{n,1}}{1-\tau\gamma_{n,1}}\right)\left(\epsilon\tau\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|\bm{g}^{f,n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{f}^{n})}+E^{0}\right),

where

\displaystyle\gamma_{n,1}:=\tfrac{3}{2}\|\nabla{\bm{w}^{f,n}}\|_{L^{\infty}({\Omega_{f}^{n}})}+G_{1,n}\|\nabla{\bm{w}_{n-1}^{f,n}}\|_{L^{\infty}({\Omega_{f}^{n-1}})}.

Since
\tau_{0}=1/\max\limits_{n=1,\cdots,N}\gamma_{n,1}
, the desired stability result (
3.25
) follows when
\tau<\tau_{0}
.
∎

Theorem 3.2

(Stability of Scheme-B)
.

Let
(\bm{u}^{f,n},\widehat{\bm{u}}^{s,n},p^{f,n},\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s,n},\widehat{\bm{\eta}}^{f,n})
solve the semi-discrete system (
3.16
) for

then the following energy estimate holds:

\displaystyle E^{N}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(\tilde{E}^{n}_{f}+\tilde{E}_{s,d}^{n}+\tfrac{\rho^{f}}{2}\|\bm{u}^{f,n-1}-\bm{u}^{f,n}\circ\mathcal{X}_{n-1}^{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\Omega_{f}^{n-1}})}\right)\lesssim\tau\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|\bm{g}^{f,n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{f}^{n})}+E^{0}.

(3.30)

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Theorem
3.1
, and the main difference lies in the treatment of the ALE time derivative term. Using (

) and identity

\displaystyle(\bm{u}^{f,n},\bm{u}^{f,n})_{\Omega_{f}^{n}}-(\bm{u}^{f,n-1},\bm{u}^{f,n}\circ\mathcal{X}_{n-1}^{n})_{\Omega_{f}^{n-1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\|\bm{u}^{f,n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{f}^{n})}-\|\bm{u}^{f,n-1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{f}^{n-1})}\right)+\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\|\bm{u}^{f,n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{f}^{n})}-\|\mathcal{P}^{n-1}(\widehat{\bm{u}}^{f,n})\circ(\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{n-1})^{-1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{f}^{n-1})}\right)

\displaystyle+\tfrac{1}{2}\|\bm{u}^{f,n-1}-\bm{u}^{f,n}\circ\mathcal{X}_{n-1}^{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\Omega_{f}^{n-1}})}.

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\|\bm{u}^{f,n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{f}^{n})}-\|\mathcal{P}^{n-1}(\widehat{\bm{u}}^{f,n})\circ(\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{n-1})^{-1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{f}^{n-1})}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int^{t_{n}}_{t_{n-1}}\left((\nabla{\bm{w}_{t}^{f,n}}-\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot\bm{w}_{t}^{f,n})\mathcal{P}^{t}(\widehat{\bm{u}}^{f,n})\circ(\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{t})^{-1},\mathcal{P}^{t}(\widehat{\bm{u}}^{f,n})\circ(\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{t})^{-1}\right)_{\Omega_{f}(t)}dt,

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\|\bm{u}^{f,n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{f}^{n})}-\|\mathcal{P}^{n-1}(\widehat{\bm{u}}^{f,n})\circ(\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{n-1})^{-1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{f}^{n-1})}\right)-\tau\Bigl((\nabla{\bm{w}^{f,n}})^{\top}\bm{u}^{f,n}-\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot{\bm{w}^{f,n}}\bm{u}^{f,n},\bm{u}^{f,n}\Bigr)_{\Omega_{f}^{n}}

\displaystyle\,\tau^{2}\,G_{2,n}\|\nabla{\bm{w}^{f,n}}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}({\Omega_{f}^{n}})}\|\bm{u}^{f,n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{f}^{n})}.

Taking
(\bm{v}^{f},\widehat{\bm{v}}^{s},q^{f})=(\tau\bm{u}^{f,n},\tau\widehat{\bm{u}}^{s,n},p^{f,n})
in (

to

3.2

\displaystyle E^{N}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(\tilde{E}^{n}_{f}+\tilde{E}_{s,d}^{n}+\tfrac{\rho^{f}}{2}\|\bm{u}^{f,n-1}-\bm{u}^{f,n}\circ\mathcal{X}_{n-1}^{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\Omega_{f}^{n-1}})}\right)

Remark 3.3
.

Notice that the admissible time-step sizes for Scheme-A and Scheme-B are respectively

\tau_{0}=1/\max\limits_{n=1,\cdots,N}\Bigl(\tfrac{3}{2}\|\nabla{\bm{w}^{f,n}}\|_{L^{\infty}({\Omega_{f}^{n}})}+G_{1,n}\|\nabla{\bm{w}_{n-1}^{f,n}}\|_{L^{\infty}({\Omega_{f}^{n-1}})}\Bigr)

and

\tau_{0}^{\prime}=1/\max\limits_{n=1,\cdots,N}\Bigl(G_{2,n}\|\nabla{\bm{w}^{f,n}}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}({\Omega_{f}^{n}})}\Bigr),

which depend only on the temporally semi-discrete ALE mapping
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{t}
defined in (
3.2
). More precisely, the fluid domain velocities
{\bm{w}}^{f,n}

and
{\bm{w}}^{f,n}_{n-1}
are, due to (
3.1
), of the forms

\displaystyle{\bm{w}}^{f,n}(\bm{x})\circ(\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{t})=\widehat{\bm{w}}^{f,t}(\widehat{\bm{x}})=\left.{\partial_{t}\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{t}(\widehat{\bm{x}},t)}\right|_{t=t_{n}}\quad\text{and}\quad{\bm{w}}^{f,n}_{n-1}(\bm{x})=\widehat{\bm{w}}^{f,n}\circ(\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{t})^{-1}(\bm{x},t_{n-1})

respectively, and the numbers
G_{1,n}
and
G_{2,n}
, defined in (
3.18
), depend only on the fluid deformation gradient
\widehat{\bm{F}}_{f}^{t}=\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{t}
. Roughly speaking, the smaller
\max\limits_{n=1,\cdots,N}\|\nabla\bm{w}^{f,n}\|_{L^{\infty}({\Omega_{f}^{n}})}
is, the larger the admissible time steps will be. In particular, if
\nabla\bm{w}^{f,n}=0
, corresponding to a fixed fluid domain or a rigid translation with a constant velocity, then the time-step sizes can be chosen without restriction.

In addition, we note that the stability condition
\tau^{2}<\tau_{0}^{\prime}
(not
\tau<\tau_{0}^{\prime}
) for Scheme-B is much milder than the condition
\tau<\tau_{0}
for Scheme-A.

4
Full discretization

In this section we will construct two fully discrete schemes based on the two temporally semi-discrete schemes in Section
3.2
, and show the corresponding energy stability results.

4.1
Domain triangulation and fully discrete ALE mapping

We consider a family of shape regular,
conforming simplicial triangulations
\{\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}\}_{h>0}
of the reference configuration
\widehat{\Omega}
, consisting of closed triangles for
d=2
and of closed tetrahedra for
d=3
, which are interface-fitted in the sense that the interface is resolved by element edges (faces in 3D). More precisely,
\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
is assumed to be a matching mesh with respect to the
\widehat{\Omega}=\widehat{\Omega}_{f}\cup\widehat{\Omega}_{s}\cup\widehat{\Gamma}
in the sense that for every simplicial element
\widehat{K}\in\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
it holds

\widehat{K}\subset\widehat{\Omega}_{s}\quad\text{or}\quad\widehat{K}\subset\widehat{\Omega}_{f}.

Accordingly, we define the sub-triangulations

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{f,h}:=\{\widehat{K}\in\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}:\ \widehat{K}\subset\widehat{\Omega}_{f}\},\qquad\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{s,h}:=\{\widehat{K}\in\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}:\ \widehat{K}\subset\widehat{\Omega}_{s}\}.

(4.1)

Notice that
\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}=\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{f,h}\cup\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{s,h}.

Let
\widehat{h}_{\widehat{K}}
be the diameter of
\widehat{K}
and set

\widehat{h}:=\max_{\widehat{K}\in\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\widehat{h}_{\widehat{K}}
.
Denote by
\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{h}
the set of all edges/faces of the mesh skeleton,
and by
\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{f,h}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{s,h}
the corresponding subsets associated
with
\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{f,h}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{s,h}
, respectively. Since the triangulation is matching, the discrete interface
\widehat{\Gamma}_{h}
can be identified with a subset of
mesh edges/faces, namely

\widehat{\Gamma}_{h}:=\bigcup_{\widehat{e}\in\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{\Gamma,h}}{\widehat{e}}

with

\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{\Gamma,h}:=\bigl\{\widehat{e}\in\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{h}:\ \widehat{e}\subset\partial\widehat{K}_{f}\cap\partial\widehat{K}_{s}\text{ for some }\widehat{K}_{f}\in\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{f,h}\text{ and some }\widehat{K}_{s}\in\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{s,h}\bigr\}.

The discrete fluid and structure subdomains induced by the triangulation are defined as

\widehat{\Omega}_{f,h}:=\bigcup_{\widehat{K}\in\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{f,h}}\widehat{K},\qquad\widehat{\Omega}_{s,h}:=\bigcup_{\widehat{K}\in\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{s,h}}\widehat{K}.

For
k\geq 0
, let
P_{k}(\widehat{K})
and
P_{k}(\widehat{e})
denote the spaces of polynomials of
total degree at most
k
on element
\widehat{K}
and on edge/face
\widehat{e}
, respectively.

To give the spatially discrete ALE mapping, we use the conforming
P_{k}
-element to approximate the displacement
\widehat{\bm{\eta}}^{f}
of the fluid mesh and introduce the following continuous finite dimensional spaces:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \widehat{\bm{V}}_{h,k}^{\mathcal{M}}:=&\{\widehat{\bm{d}}_{h}^{m}\in\bm{C}^{0}(\overline{{\widehat{\Omega}}_{f,h}}):\widehat{\bm{d}}_{h}^{m}\mid_{\widehat{K}}\in[{P_{k}(\widehat{K})}]^{d},\forall{\widehat{K}}\in\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{f,h}\text{\ and\ }\widehat{{\bm{d}}}^{m}_{h}\mid_{\widehat{\Gamma}_{f,h}}=\bm{0}\},\\
{\widehat{\bm{V}}}_{0,h,k}^{\mathcal{M}}:=&\{\widehat{{\bm{d}}}_{h}^{m}\in\bm{C}^{0}(\overline{{\widehat{\Omega}}_{f,h}}):\widehat{{\bm{d}}}_{h}^{m}\mid_{\widehat{K}}\in[{P_{k}(\widehat{K})}]^{d},\forall\widehat{K}\in\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{f,h}\text{\ and\ }\widehat{{\bm{d}}}_{h}^{m}\mid_{\partial\widehat{\Omega}_{f,h}}=\bm{0}\},\end{aligned}\right.

(4.2)

where
\widehat{\Gamma}_{f,h}:=\partial\widehat{\Omega}_{f,h}\backslash\widehat{\Gamma}_{h}
.

Similar to the semi-discrete version (
3.1
), the fully discrete ALE mapping
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{h}^{n}
for the fluid domain at time
t_{n}
is constructed from the discrete fluid mesh displacement
\widehat{\bm{\eta}}^{f,n}_{h}\in\widehat{\bm{V}}_{h,k}^{\mathcal{M}}
with

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{h}^{n}(\widehat{\bm{x}})=\widehat{\bm{x}}+\widehat{\bm{\eta}}^{f,n}_{h}(\widehat{\bm{x}}):\widehat{\Omega}_{f,h}\rightarrow\Omega_{f,h}^{n}:=\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{h}^{n}(\widehat{\Omega}_{f,h}),\quad n=1,\dots,N.

(4.3)

Then
we introduce for any
t\in[0,T]
the fully discrete ALE mapping

(\kappa^{f}\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{\bm{\eta}}_{h}^{f,n},\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{\bm{m}}^{f}_{h})_{\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{f,h}}&=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(4.12)

for all
(\mathbb{M}^{f,n}_{h},\mathcal{V}^{f,n},\mathcal{Q}^{f,n},\widehat{\bm{m}}_{h}^{f})\in\mathbb{S}^{f,n}_{h,k-1}\times(\bm{V}^{f,n}_{h,k}\times\underline{\bm{V}}^{f,n}_{h,k})\times(Q^{f,n}_{h,k-1}\times\underline{Q}^{f,n}_{h,k})\times\widehat{\bm{V}}_{0,h,k}^{\mathcal{M}}
and
(\widehat{\mathbb{M}}^{s}_{h},\widehat{\mathcal{V}}^{s},\widehat{\mathcal{M}}^{s})\in\widehat{\mathbb{S}}^{s}_{h,k-1}\times(\widehat{\bm{V}}^{s}_{h,k}\times\underline{\widehat{\bm{V}}}^{s}_{h,k})\times(\widehat{\bm{V}}^{s}_{h,k}\times\underline{\widehat{\bm{V}}}^{s}_{h,k})
with
\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}^{f,n}=\underline{\widehat{\bm{v}}}_{h}^{s}\circ(\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{n}_{e})^{-1}
on
\Gamma_{h}^{n}
.

Remark 4.2
.

In Scheme-C as well as Scheme-D, the fluid, structural, and ALE mapping equations form a monolithic, strongly coupled nonlinear system of the following form: Find
\mathbf{X}_{h}^{n}:=(\bm{u}_{*}^{f,n},\widehat{\bm{u}}_{*}^{s,n},\widehat{\bm{\eta}}_{h}^{f,n})
, for
n=1,\cdots,N
, where the fluid unknown is given by
\bm{u}_{*}^{f,n}:=(\mathbb{L}^{f,n}_{h},\mathcal{U}^{f,n},\widetilde{P}^{f,n})
and the structural unknown is given by
\widehat{\bm{u}}_{*}^{s,n}:=(\widehat{\mathbb{L}}^{s,n}_{h},\widehat{\mathcal{U}}^{s,n},\widehat{\mathcal{D}}^{s,n})
, such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{h}^{n}(\mathbf{X}_{h}^{n})=0,

(4.13)

where the nonlinear residual
\mathcal{R}_{h}^{n}
involves the current computational mesh
{\mathcal{T}}_{f,h}^{n}
that depends on the fluid mesh displacement unknown
\widehat{\bm{\eta}}_{h}^{f,n}
.
The resulting system (
4.13
)
can be solved by a Newton-type iteration through the following steps:

Step 1: Initialization.

Given
\mathbf{X}_{h}^{n-1}
,
set the initial guess

\mathbf{X}_{h}^{n,0}=(\bm{u}_{*}^{f,n,0},\widehat{\bm{u}}_{*}^{s,n,0},\widehat{\bm{\eta}}_{h}^{f,n,0}):=\mathbf{X}_{h}^{n-1}

and the iteration number
k=0
.

Step 2: Domain definition.

With the current iteration
\mathbf{X}_{h}^{n,k}=(\bm{u}_{*}^{f,n,k},\widehat{\bm{u}}_{*}^{s,n,k},\widehat{\bm{\eta}}_{h}^{f,n,k})
, define the fluid computational mesh
{\mathcal{T}}_{f,h}^{n,k}
through the fluid mesh displacement
\widehat{\bm{\eta}}_{h}^{f,n,k}
.

Step 3: Residual and Jacobian assembly.

Assemble the nonlinear residual
\mathcal{R}_{h}^{n}(\mathbf{X}_{h}^{n,k})
on
{\mathcal{T}}_{f,h}^{n,k}
(and
\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{s,h}
for the structural part) and compute the Jacobian operator
D\mathcal{R}_{h}^{n}(\mathbf{X}_{h}^{n,k})
, i.e. the Fréchet derivative of the residual with respect to all unknowns.

Step 4: Linearly solve and update.

Determine the Newton increment
\delta\mathbf{X}_{h}^{n,k}
from

\displaystyle D\mathcal{R}_{h}^{n}(\mathbf{X}_{h}^{n,k})\bigl[\delta\mathbf{X}_{h}^{n,k}\bigr]=-\mathcal{R}_{h}^{n}(\mathbf{X}_{h}^{n,k}),

and compute

\mathbf{X}_{h}^{n,k+1}=\mathbf{X}_{h}^{n,k}+\delta\mathbf{X}_{h}^{n,k}.

Step 5: Convergence check.

Repeat Steps 2–5 with
k:=k+1
until
\|\mathcal{R}_{h}^{n}(\mathbf{X}_{h}^{n,k+1})\|_{\ell^{2}}\leq\varepsilon
or a prescribed maximum iteration number of
k
is reached.

Step 6: End of iteration.
Set

\mathbf{X}_{h}^{n}:=\mathbf{X}_{h}^{n,k+1},\quad{\mathcal{T}}_{f,h}^{n}:={\mathcal{T}}_{f,h}^{n,k+1}.

Remark 4.3
.

In the equation
C_{h,2}^{f,n}(\mathcal{U}^{f,n},\mathcal{Q}^{f,n})=0
in (
4.11
) and (
4.12
), setting
q_{h}^{f,n}=0
yields

\langle({\bm{u}}_{h}^{f,n}-\underline{\bm{u}}_{h}^{f,n})\cdot{\bm{n}^{f}},\,\underline{q}_{h}^{f,n}\rangle_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}^{n}_{f,h}}=0,

which shows that
\underline{p}_{h}^{f,n}
serves as a Lagrange multiplier enforcing the continuity of the normal component of
{\bm{u}}_{h}^{f,n}
across interelement boundaries (also cf. Theorem
4.1
).

Remark 4.4
.

Similar to the case of temporal semi-discretization (cf. Remark
3.2
), Scheme-C and Scheme-D are equivalent if
\nabla\bm{w}_{h}^{f,n}=0
for any
n
, which means that the fluid domain is fixed all the time.

The following result shows that the fluid velocity approximations obtained through the above two HDG fully discrete schemes are globally divergence-free, ensuring the mass-conserving property for the incompressible fluid.

Theorem 4.1
.

Scheme-C and Scheme-D both produce globally divergence-free velocity approximations, i.e. it holds for the discrete fluid velocity solutions
\bm{u}_{h}^{f,n}\in\bm{V}^{f,n}_{h,k}
(
n=1,\dots,N
) that

\displaystyle\bm{u}_{h}^{f,n}\in\bm{H}({\rm{div}},\Omega_{f,h}^{n}),\quad\nabla\cdot\bm{u}_{h}^{f,n}=0.

Proof.

Notice that from (
4.11
), as well as (
4.12
), it follows that the discrete solution
\mathcal{U}^{f,n}=(\bm{u}_{h}^{f,n},\underline{\bm{u}}_{h}^{f,n})\in\bm{V}^{f,n}_{h,k}\times\underline{\bm{V}}^{f,n}_{h,k}
satisfies

\displaystyle 0=C_{h,2}^{f,n}(\mathcal{U}^{f,n},\mathcal{Q}^{f,n})=(\nabla\cdot\bm{u}_{h}^{f,n},q^{f,n}_{h})_{{\mathcal{T}}^{n}_{f,h}}-\langle{\bm{u}}_{h}^{f,n}\cdot{\bm{n}^{f}},\underline{q}_{h}^{f,n}\rangle_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}^{n}_{f,h}}

(4.14)

for any
\mathcal{Q}^{f,n}=(q_{h}^{f,n},\underline{q}_{h}^{f,n})\in Q^{f,n}_{h,k-1}\times\underline{Q}^{f,n}_{h,k}
.
In view of (
4.9
), for any
K=\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{h}^{n}(\widehat{K})
and
e=\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{e}^{n}(\widehat{e})
with
\widehat{K}\in\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{f,h}
and
\widehat{e}\in\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{f,h},
we have

\displaystyle\bm{u}_{h}^{f,n}\circ\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{h}^{n}\mid_{K}=\tfrac{1}{\widehat{J}^{n}_{h,K}}\widehat{\bm{F}}^{n}_{h,K}\widehat{\bm{u}}_{h}^{f}\circ(\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{h}^{n})^{-1},\quad\widehat{\bm{u}}_{h}^{f}\in{P_{k}(\widehat{K})},

\displaystyle\underline{\bm{u}}^{f,n}_{h}\mid_{e}=\underline{\widehat{\bm{u}}}_{h}\circ(\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{n}_{e})^{-1},\quad\underline{\widehat{\bm{u}}}_{h}\in{P_{k}(\widehat{e})},

\displaystyle q^{f,n}_{h}\mid_{K}=\widehat{q}_{h}\circ(\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{h}^{n})^{-1},\quad\widehat{q}_{h}\in{P_{k-1}(\widehat{K})},

\displaystyle\underline{q}_{h}^{f,n}\mid_{e}=\underline{\widehat{q}}_{h}\circ(\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{n}_{e})^{-1},\quad\underline{\widehat{q}}_{h}\in{P_{k}(\widehat{e})},

which imply (cf. (
2.11
) and (
2.12
))
(\nabla\cdot\bm{u}_{h}^{f,n})\circ\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{h}^{n}=\frac{1}{\widehat{J}_{f,h}^{n}}\widehat{\nabla}\cdot\widehat{\bm{u}}_{h}^{f,n}
and

\displaystyle(\nabla\cdot\bm{u}_{h}^{f,n},q^{f,n}_{h})_{K}=(\widehat{\nabla}\cdot\widehat{\bm{u}}_{h}^{f,n},\widehat{q}_{h})_{\widehat{K}},\quad\langle\bm{u}_{h}^{f,n}\cdot\bm{n}^{f},q^{f,n}_{h}\rangle_{\partial K}=\langle\widehat{\bm{u}}_{h}^{f,n}\cdot\widehat{\bm{n}}^{f},\underline{\widehat{q}}_{h}\rangle_{\partial\widehat{K}}.

Then (
4.14
) leads to

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle(\nabla\cdot\bm{u}_{h}^{f,n},q^{f,n}_{h})_{{\mathcal{T}}^{n}_{f,h}}-\langle{\bm{u}}_{h}^{f,n}\cdot{\bm{n}^{f}},\underline{q}_{h}^{f,n}\rangle_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}^{n}_{f,h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\widehat{\nabla}\cdot\widehat{\bm{u}}_{h}^{f,n},\widehat{q}^{f}_{h})_{\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{f,h}}-\langle\widehat{\bm{u}}_{h}^{f,n}\cdot\widehat{\bm{n}^{f}},\underline{\widehat{q}}_{h}^{f}\rangle_{\partial\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{f,h}}.

Take
(\widehat{q}_{h}^{f},\underline{\widehat{q}}_{h}^{f})=(\widehat{\nabla}\cdot\widehat{\bm{u}}_{h}^{f,n}-\widehat{c},\widehat{r}_{b}-\widehat{c})
in this equation, with
\widehat{c}=\frac{1}{|\widehat{\Omega}_{f,h}|}\int_{\widehat{\Omega}_{f,h}}\widehat{\nabla}\cdot\widehat{\bm{u}}_{h}^{f,n}d\widehat{\bm{x}}
and

\displaystyle\widehat{r}_{b}|_{\widehat{e}}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &-(\widehat{\bm{u}}_{h}^{f,n}\cdot{\widehat{\bm{n}}}^{f})|_{\partial\widehat{K}_{1}\cap\widehat{e}}-(\widehat{\bm{u}}_{h}^{f,n}\cdot\widehat{\bm{n}}^{f})|_{\partial\widehat{K}_{2}\cap\widehat{e}},&\quad\text{ if }\widehat{e}=\partial\widehat{K}_{1}\cap\partial\widehat{K}_{2},\widehat{K}_{1},\widehat{K}_{2}\in\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{f,h},\\
&0,&\quad\text{ if }\widehat{e}\subset\partial\widehat{\Omega}_{f,h}\end{aligned}\right.

for any
\widehat{e}\in\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{f,h}
, then we get

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=(\widehat{\nabla}\cdot\widehat{\bm{u}}_{h}^{f,n},\widehat{\nabla}\cdot\widehat{\bm{u}}_{h}^{f,n}-\widehat{c})_{\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{f,h}}-\langle\widehat{\bm{u}}_{h}^{f,n}\cdot\widehat{\bm{n}^{f}},\widehat{r}_{b}-\widehat{c}\rangle_{\partial\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{f,h}}

\displaystyle=\|\widehat{\nabla}\cdot\widehat{\bm{u}}_{h}^{f,n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{f,h}})}-\langle\widehat{\bm{u}}_{h}^{f,n}\cdot\widehat{\bm{n}^{f}},\widehat{r}_{b}\rangle_{\partial\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{f,h}}

\displaystyle=\|\widehat{\nabla}\cdot\widehat{\bm{u}}_{h}^{f,n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{f,h}})}+\sum_{\widehat{e}\in\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{f,h}/\partial\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{f,h}}\|(\widehat{\bm{u}}_{h}^{f,n}\cdot{\widehat{\bm{n}}}^{f})|_{\partial\widehat{K}_{1}\cap\widehat{e}}+(\widehat{\bm{u}}_{h}^{f,n}\cdot\widehat{\bm{n}}^{f})|_{\partial\widehat{K}_{2}\cap\widehat{e}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\widehat{e})}.

This means that
\widehat{\nabla}\cdot\widehat{\bm{u}}_{h}^{f,n}|_{\widehat{K}}=0
and
\widehat{\bm{u}}_{h}^{f,n}\in H({\widehat{\rm{div}}},\widehat{\Omega}_{h}^{f})
, and the desired conclusion follows.
∎

To show the energy stability of the fully discrete schemes, we first introduce operators
\mathcal{K}_{h}^{f,n}:\bm{V}^{f,n}_{h,k}\times\underline{\bm{V}}^{f,n}_{h,k}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{f,n}_{h,k-1}
and
\mathcal{K}_{h}^{s,n}:\widehat{\bm{V}}^{s}_{h,k}\times\underline{\widehat{\bm{V}}}^{s}_{h,k}\rightarrow\widehat{\mathbb{S}}^{s}_{h,k-1}
defined by

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &(\mathcal{K}_{h}^{f,n}\mathcal{V}^{f,n},\mathbb{M}^{f,n}_{h})=-C_{h,1}^{f,n}(\mathcal{V}^{f,n},\mathbb{M}^{f,n}_{h}),\quad\forall\ \mathcal{V}^{f,n}\in\bm{V}^{f,n}_{h,k}\times\underline{\bm{V}}^{f,n}_{h,k},\ \mathbb{M}^{f,n}_{h}\in\mathbb{S}^{f,n}_{h,k-1},\\
&(\mathcal{K}_{h}^{s,n}\widehat{\mathcal{V}}^{s},\widehat{\mathbb{M}}^{s}_{h})=-C_{h}^{s}(\widehat{\mathcal{V}}^{s},\widehat{\mathbb{M}}^{s}_{h}),\quad\forall\ \widehat{\mathcal{V}}^{s}\in\widehat{\bm{V}}^{s}_{h,k}\times\underline{\widehat{\bm{V}}}^{s}_{h,k},\ \widehat{\mathbb{M}}^{s}_{h}\in\widehat{\mathbb{S}}^{s}_{h,k-1}.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.15)

It is evident that
\mathcal{K}_{h}^{f,n}
and
\mathcal{K}_{h}^{s,n}
are well-defined. From definitions and the first two equations in (
4.11
) or (
4.12
) , we immediately have

\displaystyle\mathbb{L}^{f,n}_{h}=2\rho^{f}\mu^{f}\mathcal{K}_{h}^{f,n}\mathcal{U}^{f,n},\quad\widehat{\mathbb{L}}^{s,n}_{h}=2\mu^{s}\mathcal{K}_{h}^{s,n}\widehat{\mathcal{U}}^{s,n}.

(4.16)

In addition, we introduce the following two norms: for any
\mathcal{V}^{f,n}=(\bm{v}_{h}^{f,n},\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}^{f,n})\in\bm{V}^{f,n}_{h,k}\times\underline{\bm{V}}^{f,n}_{h,k}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{V}}^{s}=(\widehat{\bm{v}}_{h}^{s},\underline{\widehat{\bm{v}}}_{h}^{s})\in\widehat{\bm{V}}^{s}_{h,k}\times\underline{\widehat{\bm{V}}}^{s}_{h,k}
,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{V}^{f,n}\|_{\bm{V}^{f,n}_{h,k}\times\underline{\bm{V}}^{f,n}_{h,k}}:=\|\mathcal{K}_{h}^{f,n}\mathcal{V}^{f,n}\|_{L^{2}({{\mathcal{T}}_{f,h}^{n}})}+\|s_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\bm{v}_{h}^{f,n}-\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}^{f,n})\|_{L^{2}(\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{f,h}^{n})},

\displaystyle\|\widehat{\mathcal{V}}^{s}\|_{\widehat{\bm{V}}^{s}_{h,k}\times\underline{\widehat{\bm{V}}}^{s}_{h,k}}:=\|\mathcal{K}_{h}^{s,n}\widehat{\mathcal{V}}^{s}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{s,h})}+\|s_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\widehat{\bm{v}}_{h}^{s}-\underline{\widehat{\bm{v}}}_{h}^{s})\|_{L^{2}(\partial\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{s,h})}.

The following HDG Sobolev embedding inequality holds (see
[
15
, Lemma 3.3]
):

\displaystyle\|\bm{v}^{f,n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{T}^{n}_{f,h})}\leq C\|\mathcal{V}^{f,n}\|_{\bm{V}^{f,n}_{h,k}\times\underline{\bm{V}}^{f,n}_{h,k}},\qquad\forall\ \mathcal{V}^{f,n}=(\bm{v}_{h}^{f,n},\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}^{f,n})\in\bm{V}^{f,n}_{h,k}\times\underline{\bm{V}}^{f,n}_{h,k}.

(4.17)

Similar to (
3.18
), for
n=1,2,\cdots,N
and
t\in[t_{n-1},t_{n}]
we define

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} G_{1,n,h}:=&\sup_{t\in[t_{n-1},t_{n}]}\Bigl(1+\tfrac{1}{2}\|(\mathbb{G}_{n-1,h}(t))^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathcal{T}}_{f,h}^{n-1})}\|\mathbb{G}_{n-1,h}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathcal{T}}_{f,h}^{n-1}))}\Bigr)\|\mathbb{G}_{n-1,h}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathcal{T}}_{f,h}^{n-1})},\\
G_{2,n,h}:=&\sup_{t\in[t_{n-1},t_{n}]}\Bigl(\|\mathbb{J}_{n,h}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathcal{T}}_{f,h}^{n})}+\bigl(2+\tfrac{1}{2}\|\mathbb{F}_{n,h}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}({\mathcal{T}}_{f,h}^{n})}\bigr)\|(\mathbb{F}_{n,h}(t))^{-\top}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathcal{T}}_{f,h}^{n})}\|\mathbb{G}_{n,h}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathcal{T}}_{f,h}^{n})}\\
&\ +\tfrac{1}{2}\|(\mathbb{F}_{n,h}(t))^{-\top}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}({\mathcal{T}}_{f,h}^{n})}\|\mathbb{F}_{n,h}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathcal{T}}_{f,h}^{n})}\|\mathbb{G}_{n,h}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathcal{T}}_{f,h}^{n})}\Bigr),\end{aligned}\right.

(4.18)

where

\mathbb{F}_{n,h}(t):=\widehat{\bm{F}}^{t}_{f,h}(\widehat{\bm{F}}^{n}_{f,h})^{-1},\quad\mathbb{J}_{n,h}(t):=\frac{\widehat{J}_{f,h}^{n}}{\widehat{J}_{f,h}^{t}},\quad\mathbb{G}_{n,h}(t):=\mathbb{J}_{n,h}(t)\mathbb{F}_{n,h}(t).

Take
\mathcal{X}_{i,h}^{j}:=\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{h}^{j}\circ(\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{h}^{i})^{-1}
for
i,j=n-1,n
, similar to (
3.11
) we have

\displaystyle\bm{v}_{h}\circ\mathcal{X}_{i,h}^{j}

\displaystyle=(\frac{\widehat{\bm{F}}_{f,h}^{i}}{\widehat{J}_{f,h}^{i}}\widehat{\bm{v}}_{h})\circ(\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{i}_{h})^{-1}=\left(\frac{\widehat{J}_{f,h}^{j}}{\widehat{J}_{f,h}^{i}}\widehat{\bm{F}}_{f,h}^{i}(\widehat{\bm{F}}^{j}_{f,h})^{-1}\bm{v}_{h}\circ\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{h}^{j}\right)\circ(\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{h}^{i})^{-1},\quad\forall\bm{v}_{h}\in\bm{V}^{f,j}_{h,k}.

We now define the fully discrete energy.
Let
E_{h}^{n}
denote the sum of the full discrete kinetic energy of the fluid and the discrete kinetic and discrete elastic energy of the structure at time
t_{n}
, i.e.

\displaystyle E^{n}_{h}:=\tfrac{\rho^{f}}{2}\|\bm{u}_{h}^{f,n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({{\mathcal{T}}_{f,h}^{n}})}+\tfrac{\rho^{s}}{2}\|\widehat{\bm{u}}_{h}^{s,n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{s,h})}+\left(\mu^{s}\|\widehat{\mathcal{D}}^{s,n}\|^{2}_{\widehat{\bm{V}}^{s}_{h,k}\times\underline{\widehat{\bm{V}}}^{s}_{h,k}}+\tfrac{\lambda^{s}}{2}\|\widehat{\nabla}\cdot{\widehat{\bm{d}}_{h}^{s,n}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{s,h})}\right).

(4.19)

And let
\tilde{E}_{f,h}^{n}
denote the fluid viscous dissipation and
\tilde{E}_{s,d,h}^{n}
the structural numerical dissipation, defined respectively by

\displaystyle\tilde{E}^{n}_{f,h}=

\displaystyle\tau\rho^{f}\mu^{f}\|\mathcal{U}^{f,n}\|^{2}_{\bm{V}^{f,n}_{h,k}\times\underline{\bm{V}}^{f,n}_{h,k}},

\displaystyle\tilde{E}_{s,d,h}^{n}=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}(\rho^{s}\|\widehat{\bm{u}}_{h}^{s,n}-\widehat{\bm{u}}_{h}^{s,n-1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{s,h}})}+2\mu^{s}\|\widehat{\mathcal{D}}^{s,n}-\widehat{\mathcal{D}}^{s,n-1}\|^{2}_{\widehat{\bm{V}}^{s}_{h,k}\times\underline{\widehat{\bm{V}}}^{s}_{h,k}}+\lambda^{s}\|\widehat{\nabla}\cdot\widehat{\bm{d}}_{h}^{s,n}-\widehat{\nabla}\cdot\widehat{\bm{d}}_{h}^{s,n-1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{s,h}})}).

By following the same routines as in the proofs of Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
, we can obtain energy stability results for the proposed fully discrete schemes.

Theorem 4.2

(Stability of Scheme-C)
.

Let
(\bm{u}_{h}^{f,n},\widehat{\bm{u}}_{h}^{s,n},p^{f,n},\widehat{\bm{d}}_{h}^{s,n},\widehat{\bm{\eta}}_{h}^{f,n})
solve the fully discrete system (
4.11
) for
n=1,2,\cdots,N
. If the time step
\tau
satisfies

\tau<\widetilde{\tau}_{0}:=1/\max\limits_{n=1,\cdots,N}\Bigl(\tfrac{3}{2}\|\nabla{\bm{w}_{h}^{f,n}}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathcal{T}_{f,h}^{n}})}+G_{1,n,h}\|\nabla{\bm{w}_{h,n-1}^{f,n}}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathcal{T}_{f,h}^{n-1}})}\Bigr),

then the following energy estimate holds:

\displaystyle E_{h}^{N}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(\tilde{E}^{n}_{f,h}+\tilde{E}_{s,d,h}^{n}+\frac{\rho^{f}}{2}\|\bm{u}_{h}^{f,n-1}\circ\mathcal{X}^{n-1}_{n,h}-\bm{u}_{h}^{f,n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{f,h}^{n})}\right)\lesssim\tau\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|\bm{g}^{f,n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{f,h}^{n})}+E_{h}^{0}.

(4.20)

Proof.

The fifth and sixth equations of (
4.11
) imply

\displaystyle\widehat{\bm{u}}_{h}^{s,n}|_{\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{s,h}}=\tilde{D}_{t}\widehat{\bm{d}}_{h}^{s,n}\quad\text{and}\quad\underline{\widehat{\bm{u}}}_{h}^{s,n}|_{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{s,h}}=\tilde{D}_{t}\underline{\widehat{\bm{d}}}_{h}^{s,n},

respectively.
Applying the Young’s inequality and the HDG Sobolev embedding inequality (
4.17
), we obtain

\displaystyle(\bm{g}^{f,n},\bm{v}_{h}^{f,n})_{{\mathcal{T}}_{f,h}^{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon\|\bm{g}^{f,n}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{f,h}^{n})}^{2}+\frac{1}{4\epsilon}\|\bm{v}_{h}^{f,n}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{f,h}^{n})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon\|\bm{g}^{f,n}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{f,h}^{n})}^{2}+\frac{C}{4\epsilon}\|\widehat{\mathcal{V}}^{s,n}\|_{\widehat{\bm{V}}^{s}_{h,k}\times\underline{\widehat{\bm{V}}}^{s}_{h,k}}^{2},\qquad\forall\,\epsilon>0.

By taking
(\mathbb{M}^{f,n}_{h},\mathcal{V}^{f,n},\mathcal{Q}^{f,n})=(\mathbb{L}^{f,n}_{h},\mathcal{U}^{f,n},\widetilde{P}^{f,n})
and
(\widehat{\mathbb{M}}^{s}_{h},\widehat{\mathcal{V}}^{s},\widehat{\mathcal{M}}^{s})=(\widehat{\mathbb{L}}^{s,n}_{h},\widehat{\mathcal{U}}^{s,n},\widehat{\mathcal{D}}^{s,n})
in (
4.11
), using (
4.16
) and the identity
T^{f,n}_{h}(\bm{w}_{h}^{f,n},\mathcal{U}^{f,n},\mathcal{U}^{f,n})=0
, and then following the same line as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we can get the desired estimate (
4.20
). Here for brevity we omit the details of proof.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

Note that the following spatially semi-discrete HDG method, corresponding to the fully discrete HDG methods (Scheme-C and Scheme-D), is energy conservative if the external forcing
\bm{g}^{f}=\bm{0}
.
For any

for all
(\mathbb{M}^{f}_{h},\mathcal{V}^{f},\mathcal{Q}^{f},\widehat{\bm{m}}_{h}^{f})\in\mathbb{S}^{f,t}_{h,k-1}\times(\bm{V}^{f,t}_{h,k}\times\underline{\bm{V}}^{f,t}_{h,k})\times(Q^{f,t}_{h,k-1}\times\underline{Q}^{f,t}_{h,k})\times\widehat{\bm{V}}_{0,h,k}^{\mathcal{M}}
and

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}^{f}:=(\bm{u}_{h}^{f},\underline{\bm{u}}_{h}^{f}),\ \mathcal{V}^{f}:=(\bm{v}_{h}^{f},\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}^{f}),\quad\widetilde{P}^{f}:=(p_{h}^{f},\underline{p}_{h}^{f}),\ \mathcal{Q}^{f}:=(q_{h}^{f},\underline{q}_{h}^{f}),

), (

) and the skew-symmetric property

, we can easily derive the following energy identity:

\displaystyle E_{h}(T)+\int_{0}^{T}\tau\rho^{f}\mu^{f}\|\mathcal{U}^{f}(t)\|^{2}_{\bm{V}^{f,t}_{h,k}\times\underline{\bm{V}}^{f,t}_{h,k}}dt=E_{h}(0),

where the spatially semi-discrete energy

\displaystyle E_{h}(t):=\tfrac{\rho^{f}}{2}\|\bm{u}_{h}^{f}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}({{\mathcal{T}}_{f,h}}(t))}+\tfrac{\rho^{s}}{2}\|\widehat{\bm{u}}_{h}^{s}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{s,h})}+\left(\mu^{s}\|\widehat{\mathcal{D}}^{s}(t)\|^{2}_{\widehat{\bm{V}}^{s}_{h,k}\times\underline{\widehat{\bm{V}}}^{s}_{h,k}}+\tfrac{\lambda^{s}}{2}\|\widehat{\nabla}\cdot{\widehat{\bm{d}}_{h}^{s}}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{s,h})}\right),

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{U}^{f}(t)\|^{2}_{\bm{V}^{f,t}_{h,k}\times\underline{\bm{V}}^{f,t}_{h,k}}:=\|\mathcal{K}_{h}^{f}\mathcal{U}^{f}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}({{\mathcal{T}}_{f,h}}(t))}+\|s_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\bm{u}_{h}^{f}(t)-\underline{\bm{u}}_{h}^{f}(t))\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{f,h}(t))},

and

and
\mathcal{K}_{h}^{s}
are defined in the same way as
\mathcal{K}_{h}^{f,n}
and
\mathcal{K}_{h}^{s,n}
in (
4.15

Numerical experiments

In this section, we present three numerical examples. The first two demonstrate the energy stability and convergence of the two fully discrete schemes (Scheme-C and Scheme-D), while the third serves as a modified Turek-Hron FSI benchmark test with linear elasticity. All simulations are carried out using the open-source finite element library NGSolve
[

[

]
(cf. Figure

4.2
), and the resulting nonsymmetric linearized systems are efficiently addressed by the direct solver UMFPACK
[

]
.

Example 5.1.
Energy stability test

In the FSI model (
2.2
)-(
2.5
), we take
\Omega=(0,1)\times(0,1)
, with the fluid subdomain
\widehat{\Omega}_{f}=(0,\tfrac{1}{2})\times(0,1)
and and the structure subdomain
\widehat{\Omega}_{s}=(\tfrac{1}{2},1)\times(0,1)
. The interface
\widehat{\Gamma}
is located at the vertical line
x=\tfrac{1}{2}
.
All the physical parameters are set as
1
, i.e.

\rho^{f}=\mu^{f}=\rho^{s}=\mu^{s}=\lambda^{s}=\kappa^{f}=1,

and the initial conditions are prescribed as follows:

\displaystyle\bm{u}^{f}_{0}

\displaystyle=\left(-1000x^{3}y^{2}(2y-1)(x-1)^{3}(y-1)^{2},1000x^{2}y^{3}(2x-1)(x-1)^{2}(y-1)^{3})\right)^{\top},

\displaystyle\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s}_{0}

\displaystyle=\widehat{\bm{u}}^{s}_{0}=\bm{0}.

The initial fluid motion
\bm{u}^{f}_{0}
generates a force on the structure via the interface
\widehat{\Gamma}
, causing structural deformation. The resulting displacement of the structure in turn alters the fluid domain, thereby establishing a fully two-way coupled FSI system.

We perform simulations with
k=2
and a fixed mesh size
h=1/20
(cf. Figure
2
) and consider a sequence of decreasing time steps:
\tau=0.02,0.005,0.001,0.0002
. Figure
3
plots the evolution of the discrete energy

\displaystyle E^{n}_{h}=\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{u}_{h}^{f,n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}_{f,h}^{n}})}+\frac{1}{2}\|\widehat{\bm{u}}_{h}^{s,n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{s,h})}+\left(\|\widehat{\mathcal{D}}^{s,n}\|^{2}_{\widehat{\bm{V}}^{s}_{h,k}\times\underline{\widehat{\bm{V}}}^{s}_{h,k}}+\frac{1}{2}\|\widehat{\nabla}\cdot{\widehat{\bm{d}}_{h}^{s,n}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{s,h})}\right)

for both Scheme-C and Scheme-D at time steps
n=0,\dots,N
. We can see that both of two schemes produce numerical solutions with monotonically decreasing energy in all cases.

Example 5.2.
Convergence test

In this example, we investigate the accuracy of Scheme-C and Scheme-D when applied to a moving domain FSI problem. The domains
\Omega
,
\widehat{\Omega}_{f}
and
\widehat{\Omega}_{s}
are taken as same as those in Example 5.1.

The exact solutions of the fluid velocity
\bm{u}^{f}
, the fluid pressure
p^{f}
, and the structure displacement
\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s}
are given as follows:

\displaystyle\bm{u}^{f}

\displaystyle=(-x^{3}y^{2}(2y-1)(x-1)^{3}(y-1)^{2}\sin(2t),x^{2}y^{3}(2x-1)(x-1)^{2}(y-1)^{3})\sin(2t))^{\top},

\displaystyle\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s}

\displaystyle=(-x^{3}y^{2}(2y-1)(x-1)^{3}(y-1)^{2}\sin^{2}(t),x^{2}y^{3}(2x-1)(x-1)^{2}(y-1)^{3}\sin^{2}(t))^{\top},

\displaystyle p^{f}

\displaystyle=(x-\tfrac{1}{2})^{4}(y-1)^{4}\sin(2t).

We further define the exact displacement in the fluid domain as
\widehat{\bm{\eta}}^{f}=\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s}
, and the exact fluid mesh velocity as
\widehat{\bm{w}}^{f}=\partial_{t}\widehat{\bm{\eta}}^{f}
. Based on these exact solutions, we compute the corresponding forcing terms
\mathbf{g}^{f}
,
\mathbf{g}^{s}
, and
\mathbf{g}^{D}
for the Navier-Stokes equations (
2.2
), the linear elastodynamics equations (
2.3
), and the fluid motion equations (
2.7
), respectively. Moreover, to ensure the continuity of normal stresses across the fluid-structure interface
\widehat{\Gamma}
, we additionally introduce a forcing term
\mathbf{g}^{\Gamma}=\widehat{J}_{f}\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}^{f}\widehat{\bm{F}}_{f}^{-\top}\widehat{\bm{n}}^{f}-\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}^{s}\widehat{\bm{n}}^{f}
on the interface
\widehat{\Gamma}
.

To investigate the convergence histories for materials with different physical parameters and
k
, we consider the following five cases:

\displaystyle\text{Case\ 1}:\rho^{f}=\mu^{f}=\rho^{s}=\mu^{s}=\lambda^{s}=\kappa^{f}=1,\quad k=1,2;

\displaystyle\text{Case\ 2}:\rho^{f}=\rho^{s}=10^{3},\mu^{f}=10^{-3},\mu^{s}=\lambda^{s}=\kappa^{f}=1,\quad k=1,2;

\displaystyle\text{Case\ 3}:\rho^{s}=10^{3},\mu^{s}=10^{2},\lambda^{s}=10^{6},\rho^{f}=\mu^{f}=\kappa^{f}=1,\quad k=1,2;

\displaystyle\text{Case\ 4}:\rho^{f}=\rho^{s}=\mu^{s}=\lambda^{s}=\kappa^{f}=1,\mu^{f}=10^{-6},10^{-8},\quad k=2.

\lambda^{s}=10^{6}

\mu^{f}=10^{-6},10^{-8}

Example 5.3.
Flow past a cylinder with a flexible bar

In our final example, we assess the performance of the two proposed numerical schemes with
k=3
on the classical benchmark problem introduced by Turek and Hron
[
59
,
60
]
, which is based on the well-known configuration of incompressible channel flow past a rigid cylinder. The original setup involves a two-dimensional incompressible flow interacting with a nonlinearly elastic bar attached to the downstream side of the cylinder. In our study, we retain the same geometric and flow configuration but replace the nonlinear elasticity with a linear elastic model, aiming to investigate whether oscillatory behavior persists under this simplified structural assumption. The benchmark geometry consists of a slightly asymmetric channel with a circular cylinder mounted near the inlet. For the FSI configuration, an elastic bar is affixed to the downstream side of the cylinder. The detailed geometric specifications are illustrated in Figure
4
.

Figure 4:

Geometric configuration of the benchmark problem.

The fluid domain is governed by the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations (
2.2
) with zero external forcing
\bm{g}^{f}=\mathbf{0}
, while the structure is modeled using the linear elasticity equations (
2.3
). The fluid-structure interaction is enforced via the coupling conditions (
2.4
)-(
2.5
) on the interface
\Gamma(t)
. The following boundary conditions are applied:

1.

A time-dependent parabolic inflow profile is prescribed at the inlet of the fluid channel:

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}^{f}(0,y,t)=\begin{cases}\mathbf{u}^{f}(0,y)\cdot\dfrac{1-\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2}t\right)}{2},&\text{if}\ t<2,\\
\mathbf{u}^{f}(0,y),&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where the steady inflow profile is given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}^{f}(0,y)=1.5\,\bar{U}\cdot\frac{4.0}{0.1681}\cdot y(0.41-y).

2.

A stress-free boundary condition
\bm{\sigma}^{f}\mathbf{n}=\mathbf{0}
is imposed at the outlet.

3.

A no-slip condition is enforced on all other fluid boundaries, i.e.,
\mathbf{u}^{f}=0
. On the structure, a clamped boundary condition
\widehat{\bm{d}}^{s}=\widehat{\bm{u}}^{s}=0
is applied on the fixed boundaries.

Two test cases with time-periodic solutions are considered, denoted as FSI2 and FSI3 in
[
59
]
. The associated material parameters are presented in Table
8
. One of the quantities used for comparison is the displacement of control point A, located at the free end of the elastic flag (see Figure
4
).
The computational mesh employed in the simulations is generated automatically from the geometric model using NETGEN
[
50
]
, and consists of 1,992 triangular elements (see Figure
5
). The time step size is taken as
\tau=0.001
.

To assess the performance of the two proposed schemes, we also compute, for comparison, the continuous Galerkin (CG) method where the fluid velocity-pressure pair is discretized using the
P_{3}-P_{2}
Taylor-Hood element and the structural displacement /velocity are approximated by the continuous
P_{3}
element.
The
P_{3}
curved element is adopted in the vicinity of the cylinder to better capture the curved boundary geometry. For the corresponding nonlinear systems, the Newton’s method is employed with a stopping criterion based on a residual tolerance of
10^{-6}
. At each Newton iteration, the resulting linear system is solved using a sparse direct solver.

6
Conclusions

We have developed two monolithic numerical methods for FSI based on the Piola-type ALE mapping, where the backward Euler scheme is used for the time discretization, and the HDG framework for the spatial discretization of both the fluid and structural equations. The methods lead to globally divergence-free fluid velocity approximations. Energy stability results have been established for both the semi-discrete and fully discrete schemes. The provided numerical results have confirmed the stability analysis and demonstrated the performance of the proposed methods.
