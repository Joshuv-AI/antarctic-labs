Sobolev-to-Lipschitz property of geodesically complete spaces with curvature bounded from above
Emanuele Caputo, Nicola Cavallucci, Toni Ikonen

Abstract
. We prove that every length space with curvature bounded from above that is geodesically complete has the Sobolev-to-Lipschitz property with exponent infinity. That is, every Sobolev map in the W 1 , ∞ W^{1,\infty} -space has a Lipschitz representative so that the Lipschitz constant coincides with the infinity energy of the map. The proof is geometric and relies on arbitrarily small perturbations of geodesics to a curve that has zero length on the singular set. The motivation is to develop the analytic theory of such spaces; in particular, our result implies that GCBA spaces satisfy the infinity Poincaré inequality and an essential assumption in the theory of Lipschitz–Volume rigidity.

1.
Introduction

Metric spaces with curvature bounded from above (
{\sf CBA}
) have been introduced by Alexandrov in
[
1
]
, in terms of comparison of triangles with the simply connected two-dimensional spaces of constant sectional curvature. The flexibility of this class prevents a comprehensive study of their local geometry, see (
[
24
, Example 4.2]
) and the discussion in their introduction.
For these reasons, Lytchak–Nagano studied in
[
24
]
the local geometry of a suitable subclass, namely locally compact, locally geodesically complete spaces with curvature bounded from above (
{\sf GCBA}
). Geodesically complete means that every local geodesic can be extended to a longer local geodesics from both ends. Under these assumptions, for every point
x
in a
{\sf GCBA}
space
({\rm X},{\sf d})
, there exists an integer
n_{x}\in\mathbb{N}
such that the Hausdorff dimension of every small enough ball around
x
is exactly
n_{x}
(
[
24
, Theorem 1.2]
). The quantity
n_{x}
is called the
dimension
of
x
. For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote by
{\rm X}^{k}
the set of points of dimension
k
. Then, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the set
{\rm X}^{k}
contains a dense and open subset
\mathcal{R}^{k}
which is locally biLipschitz to
\mathbb{R}^{k}
(
[
24
, Theorem 1.2]
). The singular set is
\mathcal{S}:=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}{\rm X}^{k}\setminus\mathcal{R}^{k}
. With this stratification of
{\rm X}
, we consider the canonical measure

\mu\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{H}^{k}\llcorner{{\rm X}^{k}}

that is positive on open sets and locally finite (
[
24
, Theorem 1.4]
). Moreover,
\mu(\mathcal{S})=0
, see
[
24
, Theorem 1.2]
. We also note that the measure is
infinitesimally doubling
:

\limsup_{r\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\mu(B(x,2r))}{\mu(B(x,r))}<\infty\quad\text{for almost every $x\in X$.}

We emphasize that for a connected
{\sf GCBA}
space
{\rm X}
, the measure
\mu
is (locally) doubling if and only if
{\rm X}
is purely dimensional; see
[
10
, Theorem 5.2]
.

The main goal of this paper is to initiate the study of analytical properties of a
{\sf GCBA}
space as a metric measure space
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mu)
. In general, several analytic properties are not well-understood. These include local properties such as local
(1,p)
-Poincaré inequalities (see
[
20
, Chapters 5-9]
), other potential-theoretic tools such as capacity estimates (see
[
19
,
5
]
), or other strong connectivity properties of the space (see
[
9
,
8
]
and references therein). Part of the difficulty in developing an analytic toolkit in this context, is the vastness of the class. However, it is clear that e.g. the validity of local Poincaré inequality depends highly on the local geometry of the
{\sf GCBA}
space of interest. For instance, the wedge sum of
n
-planes is a
{\sf GCBA}
space with a local
(1,p)
-Poincaré inequality valid only for
p>n
— see
[
21
, Section 6.12]
for related discussion. On the other hand, this leads to the natural question whether a local
(1,\infty)
-Poincaré inequality could be valid on the whole class (as raised in
[
18
, Remark 7.4]
).
To get an idea on some basic examples, consider the following.

(1)

{\sf GCBA}
is stable under gluings of
{\sf GCBA}
spaces along isometric locally complete convex subsets, see
[
6
, Theorem II.11.3]
. For example, a gluing of
n
- and
n^{\prime}
-dimensional planes along a
k
-plane is of non-constant dimension if
n\neq n^{\prime}
and there exists an
n
-dimensional singular set of Hausdorff dimension
k
.

(2)

The spherical suspension of the Poincaré three-sphere is a
{\sf GCBA}
space. Indeed, the Poincaré sphere has a canonical metric that has curvature bounded from above by one, and dilating the metric suitably and taking the spherical suspension gives the desired example. We also recall that a
{\sf CBA}
space that is a homology manifold is always
{\sf GCBA}
, see the proof of
[
6
, Proposition II.5.12]
.

(3)

Every finite simplicial complex has a metric that makes it a
\sf{CBA}
space, see
[
4
, Theorem 1]
. If it has no free faces, it is
{\sf GCBA}
by
[
6
, Proposition II.5.10]
.

(4)

There is a
{\sf GCBA}
space that admits no triangulations, see
[
28
, Example 2.7]
.

To formulate our main result, we recall that a metric measure space
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mu)
is an
\infty
-thick length space
if for every
x,y\in{\rm X}
and
N\subseteq{\rm X}
with
\mu(N)=0
, it holds that

{\sf d}(x,y)=\inf\left\{\ell(\gamma)\colon\gamma\textup{ joins }x\textup{ to }y,\,\ell(\gamma\cap N)=0\right\}.

Our main theorem shows that a length
{\sf GCBA}
space is, in fact, an
\infty
-thick length space.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d})
be a length
{\sf GCBA}
space. Then
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mu)
is an
\infty
-thick length space.
In particular, the following holds:

(i)

(Sobolev-to-Lipschitz property) for every
u\in W^{1,\infty}({\rm X})
, there exists a Lipschitz representative
\tilde{u}
such that the global Lipschitz constant
\textup{Lip}(\tilde{u})
is equal to the essential supremum
\|g_{u}\|_{\infty}
where
g_{u}
is the minimal
\infty
-weak upper gradient;

(ii)

(Poincaré inequality)
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mu)
satisfies a local
(1,\infty)
-Poincaré inequality.

We refer to Section
2
for the terminology. However, we emphasize that Theorem
1.1
(i) is equivalent to being an
\infty
-thick length space by
[
18
, Theorem 1.4]
while Theorem
1.1
(ii) follows from being an
\infty
-thick length space by
[
18
, Theorem 1.5]
. Hence, being an
\infty
-thick length space is the main content of Theorem
1.1
. It implies strong connectivity properties for weakly convex subsets of
{\sf GCBA}
spaces.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d})
be a
{\sf GCBA}
space. Then any open and weakly convex subset of
{\rm X}
is an
\infty
-thick length space.

Here a subset
C\subseteq{\rm X}
is
weakly convex
if every
x,y\in C
can be joined with a geodesic that lies in
C
and has length equal to that of
{\sf d}(x,y)
. In particular, the restriction
(C,{\sf d}|_{C\times C},\mu\llcorner{C})
is a geodesic length
{\sf GCBA}
space. Basic examples include small enough balls in any
{\sf GCBA}
space. It is also clear that the
{\sf GCBA}
property is stable when passing to the intrinsic distance on an open and connected subset. Thus we have the following corollary.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d})
be a
{\sf GCBA}
space. Then any connected and open subset of
{\rm X}
equipped with the intrinsic distance and the restriction of the reference measure is an
\infty
-thick length space.

The earliest constructions of
\infty
-thick length spaces were studied by De Cecco–Palmieri in the context of Lipschitz manifolds in
[
14
]
. In the setting of metric measure spaces, the notion has previously appeared in the work of Juutinen–Shanmugalingam
[
23
]
and Durand-Cartagena–Jaramillo–Shanmugalingam
[
15
,
17
,
16
]
when developing the theory of
\infty
-harmonic functions and
(1,\infty)
-Poincaré inequalities in metric measure spaces, respectively. Moreover, it was recently proved
[
18
, Theorem 1.4]
by the third author et al. that in a locally complete metric measure space equipped with an infinitesimally doubling measure, being
\infty
-thick length space is equivalent to Theorem
1.1
(i). Note that a closely related Sobolev-to-Lipschitz property is important in the local geometry of metric measure spaces satisfying synthetic Ricci curvature lower bounds (
[
2
,
3
]
); see also applications to the structure of minimal discs
[
13
]
. As far as the authors are aware, a version of the Sobolev-to-Lipschitz property was first introduced
[
29
, Theorem 1.5]
in the metric setting.

The local Poincaré inequalities have played a critical role on the analysis of metric measure spaces since the works of Heinonen–Koskela
[
21
]
and Cheeger
[
12
]
; see
[
20
]
for further discussion. As mentioned above, the
(1,\infty)
-Poincaré was introduced in
[
15
]
. This is the weakest of the local
(1,p)
-Poincaré inequalities, and thus Theorem
1.1
can be understood as a natural start for developing local analysis on
{\sf GCBA}
spaces.

The conclusion of Theorem
1.1
is also directly related to recent developments in the so-called
Lipschitz-volume rigidity problem
, see
[
30
,
27
]
for an overview of the topic.
The general setup is the following. Let
f\colon{\rm X}\to{\rm Y}
be a
1
-Lipschitz surjective map between two metric spaces such that
0<\mathcal{H}^{n}({\rm X})=\mathcal{H}^{n}({\rm Y})<\infty
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The Lipschitz-volume rigidity problem asks for conditions on
{\rm X}
and
{\rm Y}
under which
f
is an isometry. A necessary condition for the validity of the statement is that
({\rm Y},{\sf d}_{\rm Y},\mathcal{H}^{n})
is an
\infty
-thick length space, as observed in
[
30
, Example 4.1]
, even under very regular assumptions, namely when
{\rm Y}
is a smooth
n
-manifold equipped with a length distance locally biLipschitz to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Theorem
1.1
will be used in a forthcoming paper by the first two authors and Marti
[
7
]
in establishing Lipschitz-volume rigidity with certain
{\sf GCBA}
targets.

1.1.
Strategy of the proof

We first perform a simple reduction by proving a local-to-global property of the thick length space property in any length space. This allows us to reduce the analysis to a tiny ball in a
{\sf GCBA}
space, i.e. a sufficiently small ball which has compact closure and is
{\sf CAT}(\kappa)
for some
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
. We refer to Section
2.3
for more details.
Given a geodesic lying in a tiny ball, we define an iterative procedure to constructing a curve connecting the endpoints whose length is almost that of the geodesic and which have zero length in the singular set.
Then, we perturb the curve in the regular set by using the locally biLipschitz equivalence to the Euclidean geometry in such a way that the new curve spends zero length in a given
\mu
-negligible set. Since the resulting curve has zero length on the singular part, this results in the desired curve. Controlling the length of the perturbation is key in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

The following example illustrates the importance of the role of the structure theory of the singular set of a
{\sf GCBA}
spaces in Theorem
1.1
. Given a lower semicontinuous weight
\omega\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,1]
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we define the
weighted distance

{\sf d}_{\omega}(x,y)=\inf\{\int_{\gamma}\omega\,ds\colon\text{ $\gamma$ joins $x$ to $y$ }\}
. Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a compact Euclidean segment and
\omega=1
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus E
and
\omega=c
in
E
for
c\in(0,1)
. Then
(\mathbb{R}^{n},{\sf d}_{\omega})
is bi-Lipschitz equivalent to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
through the identity map, locally isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus E
, but it is not an
\infty
-thick length space when equipped with the
n
-dimensional Hausdorff measure. Indeed, if
\ell(E)
is the Euclidean length of
E
, its length under
{\sf d}_{\omega}
is
c\ell(E)
while any curve having the same end points and having zero length on
E
has length at least
\ell(E)
. A similar example was considered by Züst in
[
30
, Example 4.1]
(see
[
22
]
for analysis of more general weighted distances and applications to complex analysis and the uniformization problem, when
n=2
).

2.
Preliminaries

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d})
be a metric space,
x\in{\rm X}
and
r>0
. We denote by
B(x,r)
(resp.
\overline{B}(x,r)
) the
open
(resp.
closed
)
ball
centered at
x
and of radius
r
. A
curve
is a continuous map
\gamma\colon[a,b]\to{\rm X}
. The starting and end points of
\gamma
are denoted by
\alpha(\gamma):=\gamma(a)
and
\omega(\gamma):=\gamma(b)
, respectively. We say that
\gamma
joins
\alpha(\gamma)
to
\omega(\gamma)
. The
length
of
\gamma
is

\ell(\gamma):=\sup\left\{\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}{\sf d}(\gamma(t_{i}),\gamma(t_{i+1})),\,\{t_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}\subseteq[a,b],\,N\in\mathbb{N},\,a=t_{0}<t_{1}<\dots<t_{N}=b\right\}.

A continuous curve
\gamma\colon[a,b]\to{\rm X}
is said to be
rectifiable
if
\ell(\gamma)<\infty
. The curve
\gamma
has
constant-speed
\ell
if
\ell(\gamma|_{[s,t]})=\ell|t-s|
for every
a\leq s<t\leq b
. Every rectifiable curve has a constant-speed reparametrization. A rectifiable curve is a
length-minimizer
if
\ell(\gamma)={\sf d}(\alpha(\gamma),\omega(\gamma))
. We say that a curve
\gamma
is a
geodesic
if
{\sf d}(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))=|t-s|
for every
a\leq s<t\leq b
. Notice that every geodesic is a length-minimizer but not vice versa — however, a unit speed reparametrization of a length-minimizing curve is a geodesic. A rectifiable curve
\gamma
is a
local geodesic
if for every
t\in[a,b]
, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
{\sf d}(\gamma(t^{\prime}),\gamma(t^{\prime\prime}))=|t^{\prime\prime}-t^{\prime}|
for every
t^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime}\in[a,b]
and
|t^{\prime}-t|+|t^{\prime\prime}-t|\leq\varepsilon
. A local geodesic is not necessarily a length-minimizer.

The
concatenation
of two curves
\gamma\colon[a_{1},b_{1}]\to{\rm X}
and
\eta\colon[a_{2},b_{2}]\to{\rm X}
with
\eta(a_{2})=\gamma(b_{1})
is the curve
\gamma\star\eta\colon[a_{1},b_{1}+b_{2}-a_{2}]
defined as
(\gamma\star\eta)(t)=\gamma(t)
if
t\in[a_{1},b_{1}]
and
(\gamma\star\eta)(t)=\eta(t-(b_{1}-a_{1}))
if
t\in[b_{1},b_{1}+b_{2}-a_{2}]
. The concatenation is an associative operation and we will use this property below without further mention.
Let
(a,b)=\cup_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}[a_{i},b_{i}]
with
b_{i}=a_{i+1}
for every
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. Let
\gamma_{i}\colon[a_{i},b_{i}]\to{\rm X}
be a sequence of curves such that
\gamma_{i}(b_{i})=\gamma_{i+1}(a_{i+1})
for
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then the
infinite concatenation
of the family
\{\gamma_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
is given by the curve
\eta\colon(a,b)\to{\rm X}
defined by
\eta(t)=\gamma_{i}(t)
if
t\in[a_{i},b_{i}]
.

A metric space
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is a
length space
if
{\sf d}(x,y)
coincides with the infimum over the length of curves joining
x
to
y
, for every
x,y\in{\rm X}
. The space is
R
-geodesic
if for every
x,y\in{\rm X}
with
{\sf d}(x,y)\leq R
, there exists a geodesic joining
x
to
y
.
It is
geodesically complete
if every local geodesic
\gamma\colon[a,b]\to{\rm X}
can be extended to a local geodesic
\gamma\colon[a-\varepsilon,b+\varepsilon]\to{\rm X}
for some
\varepsilon>0
. A subset
U\subset{\rm X}
is
weakly
R
-convex
if, for every
x,y\in U
with
{\sf d}(x,y)\leq R
, there exists a geodesic in
U
joining
x
to
y
; in other words, it is weakly
R
-convex if
(U,{\sf d}\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{U\times U}$})
is
R
-geodesic. If also every such geodesic lies in
U
, then
U
is
R
-convex
. We omit
R
from the notation if
R=\infty
.

A
metric measure space
is a triple
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mu)
where
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is a metric space and
\mu
is a nonnegative Borel measure which is locally finite. Given
B\subseteq{\rm X}
Borel, we define
\mu\llcorner{B}(A):=\mu(A\cap B)
for every Borel
A\subseteq{\rm X}
, which is a nonnegative locally finite Borel measure on
B
.

We denote by
\mathcal{H}^{k}
the
k
-dimensional Hausdorff measure on a metric space
({\rm X},{\sf d})
.

2.1.
Analysis on metric spaces

Let
u\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
be a Lipschitz function. We denote by
{\rm Lip}(u)
the Lipschitz constant of
u
, defined as

{\rm Lip}(u):=\sup_{y\neq x}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{{\sf d}(x,y)}.

Given a function
u\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
, a Borel function
g\colon{\rm X}\to[0,\infty]
is an
upper gradient
of
u
if

|u(\alpha(\gamma))-u(\omega(\gamma))|\leq\int_{\gamma}g\,{\mathrm{d}}s

for any rectifiable curve
\gamma
. The integral on the right is the
path integral of
g
over
\gamma
. By definition, the value is set to be infinite if
\gamma
is not rectifiable and set to be zero if
\gamma
is a constant curve. For non-trivial rectifiable curves, the value is defined by integration over the
length measure
of
\gamma
on its domain; we refer to
[
20
, Chapter 5]
for details.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mu)
be a metric measure space.
For a function
u\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
, an
\infty
-weak upper gradient of
u
is an element in the
L^{\infty}(\mu)
-completion of the
L^{\infty}(\mu)
-classes of essentially bounded upper gradients of
u
. Standard arguments show that the set of
\infty
-weak upper gradients of
u
is a closed lattice, cf.
[
26
, Lemma 4.1 and Proposition 4.5]
, so there exists a unique element
g_{u}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, called the
minimal
\infty
-weak upper gradient of
u
, such that
g_{u}\leq g

\mu
-a.e. for any
\infty
-weak upper gradient
g
.

We say that an equivalence class
u\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
belongs to
W^{1,\infty}({\rm X})
if it has a representative with an
\infty
-weak upper gradient, and we review basic properties based on
[
26
]
. If there are two representatives of the same equivalence class in
L^{\infty}(\mu)
with an
\infty
-weak upper gradient, then the minimal
\infty
-weak upper gradient of the difference is zero and the minimal
\infty
-weak upper gradients coincide. This gives well-posedness for the definition of
W^{1,\infty}({\rm X})
and we may equip it with the norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{1,\infty}({\rm X})}=\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu)}+\|g_{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu)}.

Standard arguments show that
W^{1,\infty}({\rm X})
is a Banach space.

We say that a metric measure space
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mu)
satisfies the
Sobolev-to-Lipschitz property
if every
u\in W^{1,\infty}({\rm X})
has a Lipschitz representative
\tilde{u}
such that
{\rm Lip}(\tilde{u})=\|g_{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu)}
. We say that
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mu)
satisfies a local
(1,\infty)
-Poincaré inequality if there exist constants
C>0
,
\lambda\geq 1
such that

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B(x,r)}\Big|u(y)-\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B(x,r)}u\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu\Big|\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y)\leq Cr\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu\llcorner B(x,\lambda r))}

for any ball
B(x,r)\subseteq{\rm X}
with
\mu(B(x,r))<\infty
and every
u\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
integrable on
B(x,r)
with an
\infty
-upper gradient
g\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
. This coincides with the definition introduced in
[
15
, Definition 1.2]
.

2.2.
Very thick quasiconvexity

We recall very
\infty
-thick convexity that was introduced in
[
16
]
and its localization from
[
18
]
. To this end, let
({\rm X},{\sf d})
be a metric space,
N\subseteq{\rm X}
be any set and consider a rectifiable curve
\gamma\colon[a,b]\to{\rm X}
. We say that
\gamma
has
zero/negligible length in
N
if the preimage
\gamma^{-1}(N)
is negligible for the length measure of
\gamma
; we denote this by
\ell(\gamma\cap N)=0
. We recall that the length measure of
\gamma
on
[a,b]
is an outer measure obtained from a Carathéodory’s construction using the length functional
\nu([s,t]):=\ell(\gamma\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{[s,t]}$})
. Hence the definition is well-posed. When
N
is Borel, this corresponds to the path integral
\int_{\gamma}\chi_{N}\,{\mathrm{d}}s=0
.

Definition 2.1

(Very thick quasiconvexity)
.

Let
C\geq 1
and
r>0
. Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mu)
be a metric measure space. We say that
{\rm X}
is
very
\infty
-thick
(C,r)
-quasiconvex at
x
if for every
y,z\in B(x,r)
and every
\mu
-negligible set
N\subseteq{\rm X}
, there exists a rectifiable curve
\gamma
joining
y
to
z
such that
\ell(\gamma)\leq C{\sf d}(y,z)
and
\ell(\gamma\cap N)=0
. We say that
{\rm X}
is
very
\infty
-thick
C
-quasiconvex
if we may take
r=\infty
.

We recall that a metric measure space is an
\infty
-length space if it is very
\infty
-thick
(1+\varepsilon)
-quasiconvex for every
\varepsilon>0
.
We have the following local-to-global property of very
\infty
-thick quasiconvexity.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mu)
be a metric measure space and
\varepsilon>0
. Suppose that for every
x\in{\rm X}
, there exists
r_{x}>0
such that
{\rm X}
is very
\infty
-thick
(1+\varepsilon,r_{x})
-quasiconvex at
x
. Then
each connected component of
{\rm X}
, endowed with the length distance and the restriction of the measure, is very
\infty
-thick
(1+\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{\prime})
-quasiconvex for every
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
.
Moreover, if
U\subseteq{\rm X}
is an open weakly convex set, then
U
is very
\infty
-thick
(1+\varepsilon)
-quasiconvex.

Proof.

We will prove the following claim: for every rectifiable curve
\theta\colon[0,1]\to X
and every negligible set
N\subseteq{\rm X}
, there exists a curve
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to X
with the same endpoints, with
\ell(\gamma)\leq(1+\varepsilon)\ell(\theta)
, and
\gamma

Consider a non-constant rectifiable curve

k\in\mathbb{N}
such that if

, then
\theta([s,t])\subseteq B(x,r_{x})
for some
x\in K
. Let

be
\mu
-negligible and consider a partition
t_{j}=j/k
for
j=0,\dots,k
of
[0,1]
. For each
j=0,\dots,k-1
, we consider a rectifiable curve
\gamma_{j}\colon[t_{j},t_{j+1}]\to{\rm X}

to
\theta(t_{j+1})

Now, we show that each connected component with the length distance has the stated property. For this, fix

in a connected component and a
\mu

From the claim, it follows that, denoting by

The statement about the weakly convex open set follows by a similar concatenation argument as above.∎

Given
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}

A metric space

{\sf CAT}(\kappa_{x})

\overline{B}(x,10r)

\textup{CAT}(\kappa)
for some

\gamma\sim\gamma^{\prime}

between two geodesics, we refer to

6

(at

The space

t\in[0,{\sf d}(x,y)]
. By Proposition
2.4
.(i), we find points
w_{t}^{\pm}\in\mathcal{R}_{\delta}(\Sigma_{\gamma(t)}{\rm X})
such that
\angle_{\gamma(t)}(v_{t}^{\pm},w_{t}^{\pm})\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}
. Since
T_{z}{\rm X}
is a metric cone for each
z\in{\rm X}
, we also have

(2)

{\sf d}_{T}((r_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}(t),w_{t}^{\pm}),(r_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}(t),v_{t}^{\pm}))\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}r_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}(t).

We apply Proposition
2.4
.(ii) to the pair
w_{t}^{\pm}
and let
\rho(\gamma(t),w^{\pm}_{t})>0
denote the given parameters.
Finally, we define

r(t):=\min\{\rho(\gamma(t),w^{+}_{t}),\rho(\gamma(t),w^{-}_{t}),r_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}(t)\}>0.

Next, let
\eta^{\pm}_{t}
be a geodesic with starting point
\alpha(\eta^{\pm}_{t})=\gamma(t)
and initial direction
[\eta^{\pm}_{t}]=w_{t}^{\pm}
. By Proposition
2.4
.(ii) and the cone property of
T_{\gamma(t)}{\rm X}
, it holds that

\eta^{\pm}_{t}(s)\in\mathcal{R}_{\delta}(U)\text{ for all }0<s\leq r(t),

and

{\sf d}_{T}((\log_{\gamma(t)}(\eta^{\pm}_{t}(s)),\log_{\gamma(t)}(\gamma(t\pm s))))={\sf d}_{T}((s,w^{\pm}_{t}),(s,v^{\pm}_{t}))\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}s\quad\forall 0<s\leq r(t).

By Lemma
2.3
, we obtain that

(3)

{\sf d}(\eta^{\pm}_{t}(s),\gamma(t\pm s))\leq 2\varepsilon^{\prime}s\text{ for all }0<s\leq r(t).

By compactness, we find
N
balls
\{B(x_{i},r_{i})\}_{i}
such that
\textup{Im}(\gamma)\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}B(x_{i},r_{i})
, where
x_{i}=\gamma(t_{i})
for some
t_{i}
and
r_{i}=r(t_{i})
. We shorten the notation on the geodesics
\eta^{\pm}_{t_{i}}\colon[0,r_{i}]\to{\rm X}
to
\eta^{\pm}_{i}
. Up to passing to a subcollection and relabeling, we may suppose that the properties of Lemma
3.3
are satisfied. In particular,
t_{i}<t_{i+1}
,
r_{1}\geq{\sf d}(x,x_{1})
,
r_{N}\geq{\sf d}(y,x_{N})
and
r_{i}+r_{i+1}\geq{\sf d}(x_{i},x_{i+1})
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\}
.

Next, for
i=1
, we define
s_{1}^{-}:={\sf d}(x,x_{1})\leq r_{1}
.
Then, for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\}
, there exist
0<s^{+}_{i}\leq r_{i}
and
0<s^{-}_{i+1}\leq r_{i+1}
such that
s^{+}_{i}+s^{-}_{i+1}={\sf d}(x_{i},x_{i+1})
. For
i=N
, we define
s_{N}^{+}:={\sf d}(x_{N},y)\leq r_{N}
.

With this setup, for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
, we define the restriction
\tilde{\eta}^{\pm}_{i}:=\eta_{i}|_{[0,s^{\pm}_{i}]}
and we denote by
p^{\pm}_{i}=\omega(\tilde{\eta}^{\pm}_{i})
the corresponding endpoints; recall that the starting points are
x_{i}=\gamma(t_{i})
. Notice that
\tilde{\eta}_{i}^{\pm}(s)\in\mathcal{R}_{\delta}(U)
for every
s\neq 0
, hence
\ell(\tilde{\eta}_{i}^{\pm}\cap\mathcal{S}_{\delta}(U))=0
.
Moreover,

(4)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\ell(\tilde{\eta}^{-}_{i})+\ell(\tilde{\eta}^{+}_{i})\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left({\sf d}(p^{-}_{i},x_{i})+{\sf d}(x_{i},p_{i}^{+})\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:error_eta_regular}}}{{\leq}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\sf d}(\gamma(t_{i}-s^{-}_{i}),\gamma(t_{i}+s^{+}_{i}))+2\varepsilon^{\prime}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(s_{i}^{-}+s^{+}_{i})

\displaystyle=(1+2\varepsilon^{\prime})\sum_{i=1}^{N}(s_{i}^{-}+s^{+}_{i})=(1+2\varepsilon^{\prime}){\sf d}(x,y),

where the last equality follows by the construction of
s_{i}^{\pm}
.

We need additional geodesic segments to connect the endpoints
p^{\pm}_{i}
of the curves
\tilde{\eta}_{i}^{\pm}
. To this end, for
i\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\}
, we let
\theta_{i}
denote the geodesic joining
p^{+}_{i}
to
p^{-}_{i+1}
, with a suitably chosen domain of definition. Similarly, we consider a geodesic
\theta_{0}
joining
x
to
p^{-}_{1}
and by
\theta_{N}
a geodesic joining
p^{+}_{N}
to
y
. Now, we denote by
\beta^{-}_{i}
the composition of the inversion of the curve
\tilde{\eta}_{i}^{-}
and a suitable translation, and, by a slight abuse of notation, denote by
\tilde{\eta}_{i}^{+}
the geodesic obtained by a composition of
\tilde{\eta}_{i}^{+}
and a translation. The aforementioned translations and domains of
\theta_{j}
are chosen so that
\theta_{0}
is defined on
[0,{\sf d}(x,p^{-}_{1})]
and that the concatenation, up to suitable translations of the domains of definitions,

\gamma_{\varepsilon}:=\theta_{0}\star(\beta_{1}^{-}\star\tilde{\eta}_{1}^{+})\star\dots\star\theta_{i}\star(\beta_{i+1}^{-}\star\tilde{\eta}_{i+1}^{+})\star\dots\star\theta_{N-1}\star(\beta_{N}^{-}\star\tilde{\eta}_{N}^{+})\star\theta_{N}

is well-defined. We claim that
\gamma_{\varepsilon}
satisfies the thesis. To this end, we include the curves
\beta_{i}^{-}
,
\tilde{\eta}_{i}^{+}
, and those
\theta_{i}
for which
\ell(\theta_{i}\cap\mathcal{S}_{\delta}(U))=0
in the collection
\mathcal{G}
. The remaining segments
\theta_{i}
are included in
\mathcal{B}
. This gives the classification stated in (ii). To see that (iii) holds, we note that the sum of the length of the curves in
\mathcal{B}
is bounded from above by the following:

(5)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{N}\ell(\theta_{i})={\sf d}(x,p_{1}^{-})+\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}{\sf d}(p_{i}^{+},p_{i+1}^{-})+{\sf d}(p_{N}^{+},y)

\displaystyle\leq 2\varepsilon^{\prime}\left(s_{1}^{-}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}(s_{i}^{+}+s_{i+1}^{-})+s_{N}^{+}\right)

\displaystyle=2\varepsilon^{\prime}{\sf d}(x,y)\leq\varepsilon{\sf d}(x,y).

Moreover, combining (
4
) and (
5
), we deduce that

\ell(\gamma_{\varepsilon})=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\ell(\tilde{\eta}_{i}^{\pm})+\sum_{i=0}^{N}\ell(\theta_{i})\leq(1+4\varepsilon^{\prime}){\sf d}(x,y)\leq(1+\varepsilon){\sf d}(x,y).

Finally, (
3
) implies that
\gamma_{\varepsilon}
lies in the
\varepsilon{\sf d}(x,y)
-neighbourhood of
\gamma
.
This shows that (i) is satisfied. Since the elements of
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{G}
are geodesics, the proof is complete.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Let
x,y\in U
, let
\gamma_{0}\colon[0,1]\to U
be the geodesic segment joining
x
to
y
, and let
0<\delta\leq\delta_{0}
and
0<\varepsilon<{\sf d}(\gamma_{0},X\setminus U)
. We define
\xi:=\varepsilon/(2+\varepsilon)
, so that
\frac{1+\xi}{1-\xi}=\varepsilon
. We claim that there exists a recursively defined sequence of curves
(\gamma_{i}\colon[0,1]\to U)_{i}
with the following properties:

(i)

\alpha(\gamma_{i})=x
,
\omega(\gamma_{i})=y
,
\ell(\gamma_{i})\leq(\sum_{h=0}^{i-1}\xi^{h})(1+\xi){\sf d}(x,y)
and
\gamma_{i}
is contained in the
(\sum_{h=0}^{i-1}\xi^{h})(1+\xi){\sf d}(x,y)
-neighbourhood of
\gamma_{0}
;

(ii)

for every
i\geq 1
,
\gamma_{i}
is a concatenation of a finite number of length-minimizing curves
\{\gamma_{i}^{j}\}_{j\in J_{i}}
of two types:

\mathcal{G}_{i}:=\{j\in J_{i}:\,\ell(\gamma_{i}^{j}\cap\mathcal{S}_{\delta}(U))=0\}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{B}_{i}:=\{j\in J_{i}:\,\ell(\gamma_{i}^{j}\cap\mathcal{S}_{\delta}(U))>0\};

(iii)

the latter families satisfy
\sum_{j\in\mathcal{B}_{i}}\ell(\gamma_{i}^{j})\leq\xi^{i}{\sf d}(x,y)
;

(iv)

the curves from the former families are nested:
\{\gamma^{j}_{i}:j\in\mathcal{G}_{i}\}\supseteq\{\gamma^{j}_{i-1}:j\in\mathcal{G}_{i-1}\}
.

We define
\gamma_{1}
as the curve provided by Proposition
3.4
applied to
\gamma_{0}
, with parameter
\xi
. Then
\gamma_{1}
satisfies properties (i), (ii), (iii) and vacuously (iv). Suppose we have constructed
\gamma_{i}
. The new curve
\gamma_{i+1}
is obtained by replacing each geodesic segment in
\mathcal{B}_{i}
by a curve satisfying the conclusion of Proposition
3.4
with parameter
\xi
. This is a well defined curve since the new curves in Proposition
3.4
have the same endpoints as the original segment. The new curve
\gamma_{i+1}
satisfies
\alpha(\gamma_{i+1})=x
,
\omega(\gamma_{i+1})=y
, and is contained in
U
, since
U
is convex.
Moreover, by construction, it is a concatenation of a finite number of geodesic segments, so
\mathcal{G}_{i+1}
and
\mathcal{B}_{i+1}
are well-defined. On the one hand, condition (iv) is satisfied because the procedure does not modify any segments in
\mathcal{G}_{i}
. On the other hand, the segments in
\mathcal{B}_{i+1}
are necessarily obtained by the replacement procedure applied to a geodesic segment in
\mathcal{B}_{i}
, so

\sum_{j\in\mathcal{B}_{i+1}}\ell(\gamma_{i+1}^{j})\leq\xi\sum_{j\in\mathcal{B}_{i}}\ell(\gamma_{i}^{j})\leq\xi^{i+1}{\sf d}(x,y),

by property (iii) of
\gamma_{i}
and Proposition
3.4
.(iii). Since the elements of
\mathcal{G}_{i}
are unchanged, properties (i) and (iii) of
\gamma_{i}
and Proposition
3.4
.(i) imply the estimate

\displaystyle\ell(\gamma_{i+1})\leq\ell(\gamma_{i})+(1+\xi)\xi^{i}{\sf d}(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq(1+\xi)\left(\sum_{h=0}^{i-1}\xi^{h}\right){\sf d}(x,y)+(1+\xi)\xi^{i}{\sf d}(x,y)

\displaystyle=(1+\xi)\left(\sum_{h=0}^{i}\xi^{h}\right){\sf d}(x,y).

Similarly, the triangle inequality and Proposition
3.4
.(i) imply that
\gamma_{i+1}
is contained in the
(1+\xi)\left(\sum_{h=0}^{i}\xi^{h}\right){\sf d}(x,y)
-neighbourhood of
\gamma_{0}
. This is property (i) for
\gamma_{i+1}
.

We have
\ell(\gamma_{i})\leq\frac{1+\xi}{1-\xi}{\sf d}(x,y)
and
\alpha(\gamma_{i})=x
for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore, we can apply Arzela–Ascoli’s Theorem to the constant-speed reparametrization of
\gamma_{i}
in order to find a limit curve
\gamma_{\infty}
satisfying
\alpha(\gamma_{\infty})=x
,
\omega(\gamma_{\infty})=y
,
\ell(\gamma_{\infty})\leq\frac{1+\xi}{1-\xi}{\sf d}(x,y)=\varepsilon{\sf d}(x,y)
. Moreover,
\gamma_{\infty}
is contained in the
\frac{1+\xi}{1-\xi}{\sf d}(x,y)
-neighbourhood of
\gamma_{0}
, hence in the
\varepsilon
-neighbourhood of
\gamma_{0}
. The latter neighbourhood is contained in
U
by the choice of
\varepsilon
.
We claim that the limit curve satisfies
\ell(\gamma_{\infty}\cap\mathcal{S}_{\delta}(U))=0
. To this end, fix an error
\lambda>0
and find
i_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\sum_{j\in\mathcal{B}_{i_{0}}}\ell(\gamma_{i_{0}}^{j})<\lambda
(it suffices to take
i_{0}>\frac{\lambda}{{\sf d}(x,y)\log(1/\xi)}
). Because of (iv), the curve
\gamma_{\infty}
contains all the segments in
\mathcal{G}_{i_{0}}
. Moreover, the remaining part has length at most
\frac{1+\xi}{1-\xi}\lambda
. Therefore, it holds that

\ell(\gamma_{\infty}\cap\mathcal{S}_{\delta}(U))\leq\sum_{j\in\mathcal{G}_{i_{0}}}\ell(\gamma_{i_{0}}^{j}\cap\mathcal{S}_{\delta}(U))+\frac{1+\xi}{1-\xi}\lambda=\frac{1+\xi}{1-\xi}\lambda,

because of the definitions of
\mathcal{G}_{i_{0}}
and
\mathcal{B}_{i_{0}}
. By the arbitrariness of
\lambda
, we deduce that
\ell(\gamma_{\infty}\cap\mathcal{S}_{\delta}(U))=0
.
This shows the thesis.

3.2.
Avoiding negligible sets in the regular part

In this section, we prove Proposition

.

Proof of Proposition
3.2
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. By applying Proposition
2.4
.(iii), we find the function
\eta\colon(0,\delta_{0})\to(0,\infty)
and small enough
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)\leq\delta_{0}
such that
\eta(\delta)\leq(1+\varepsilon)^{1/3}-1

satisfies the thesis.

Let
x,y\in U
such that the geodesic

2.4
.(iii) applied to the connected set

and a
(1+\varepsilon)^{1/3}
-biLipschitz and open map

By compactness, we find
M

x_{i}=\gamma(t_{i})
for some
t_{i}

F_{x_{i}}
will be denoted by
F_{i}
. Up to passing to a subcollection and relabeling, we can suppose that the properties of Lemma
3.3
are satisfied. In particular,
t_{i}<t_{i+1}

r_{1}\geq{\sf d}(x,x_{1})
,
r_{M}\geq{\sf d}(y,x_{M})

r_{i}+r_{i+1}\geq{\sf d}(x_{i},x_{i+1})

and
y_{M}:=y
. Notice that for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,M\}

y_{i-1}
and
y_{i}
belong to the same ball
B(x_{i},r_{i})
. For every
i\in\{1,\ldots,M\}

I_{i}:=[\gamma^{-1}(y_{i-1}),\gamma^{-1}(y_{i})]=[a_{i},b_{i}]
and let
\gamma_{i}\colon I_{i}\to U
be the restriction of
\gamma
to
I_{i}

B(x_{i},r_{i})

\theta_{i}:=F_{i}\circ\gamma_{i}\colon I_{i}\to\mathbb{R}^{k}

\theta_{i}^{v}(s):=\theta_{i}(s)+v\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{|I_{i}|}(s-a_{i})\right),\quad s\in I_{i}.

We notice that
\alpha(\theta_{i}^{v})=F_{i}(y_{i-1})
and
\omega(\theta_{i}^{v})=F_{i}(y_{i})
for every

F_{i}(B(x_{i},r_{i}))=:W_{i}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{k}

Let
N\subseteq U
be
\mu

\mathcal{H}^{k}(N\cap B(x_{i},r_{i}))=0

E_{i}:=F_{i}(N\cap B(x_{i},r_{i}))\subseteq W_{i}

E^{\prime}_{i}:=\{(s,v)\in I_{i}\times\Omega_{i}\,:\,\theta^{v}_{i}(s)\in E_{i}\}
