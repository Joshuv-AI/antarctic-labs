On the binary relations defined using GD1 and 1GD inverses over infinite dimensional vector spaces Thanks: This work was supported by Agencia Estatal de Investigación (Spain) through grant PID2023-151823NB-I00.
Diego Alba Alonso* Javier Sánchez González**

Abstract
. The purpose of this article is to study certain binary relations of endomorphisms over infinite dimensional vector spaces defined by GD1 and 1GD generalized inverses. In order to do so, these generalized inverses are studied over arbitrary vector spaces (namely, infinite dimensional ones) using finite potent endomorphisms. We characterize them in terms of the AST decomposition of a finite potent endomorphism and we obtain algorithms for their respective computation. This theory is then used to characterize the GD1 and 1GD binary relations for finite potent endomorphisms in terms of the AST decomposition and to prove that they define partial orders in the set of finite potent endomorphisms, thus, completing the theory of these generalized inverses for matrices.

1.
Introduction.

Let
\operatorname{Mat}_{n\times s}(k)
denote the ring of
(n\times s)
matrices over an arbitrary field
k.
Given
A\in\operatorname{Mat}_{n\times s}(k),
the index of
A,

i(A),
is the smallest positive integer
m
such that
\mathrm{rk(A^{m})}=\mathrm{rk(A^{m+1})}.
We say that a matrix
X\in\operatorname{Mat}_{s\times n}(k)
is an inner inverse or
\{1\}-
inverse of
A
when
A\cdot X\cdot A=A.
These are a simple example of the so called generalized inverses of matrix
A.
In
[
17
]
, G-Drazin inverses of a matrix with entries in the complex numbers
A\in\operatorname{Mat}_{n\times n}(\mathbb{C})
were introduced as the matrices
X\in\operatorname{Mat}_{n\times n}(\mathbb{C})
such that

A\cdot X\cdot A=A\quad\&\quad A\cdot X\cdot A^{m+1}=A^{m}\quad\&\quad A^{m+1}\cdot X=A^{m},

where
m=i(A).
It is customary to denote the set of
\{1\}-
inverses of a matrix
A
by
A(1)
, as well as using the notation
A^{-}\in A(1)
for its elements (although they are not unique). Similarly,
A(GD)
denotes the set of G-Drazin inverses of a matrix
A
and any of its elements are denoted by
A^{GD}
(even though they are not unique). These generalized inverses have been generalized to the context of Banach spaces, see
[
8
]
. Further, in
[
6
]
, a new generalized inverse was studied combining
\{1\}-
inverses and G-Drazin inverses. Precisely, the composition
A^{GD}\cdot A\cdot A^{-}
for certain
A^{-}\in A(1)
and
A^{GD}\in A(GD)
was called as GD1 inverse of
A.
Different GD1 inverses appear varying the
\{1\}-
inverse and the G-Drazin inverse considered. Simultaneously, 1GD inverses were introduced as the “duals” of GD1 inverse, this is, as the composition
A^{-}\cdot A\cdot A^{GD}.
Again, these generalized inverses were studied in the framework of Banach space operators in
[
15
]
. It is worth pointing out that in this work, GD1 and 1GD inverses were investigated using the core-quasinilpotent decomposition as well as the closed range decomposition operator. Moreover, the interconnection with the Drazin inverse (in whatever way one could define it, as we will not use it in this paper) and other properties considering idempotent conditions and projectors were analyzed. Our treatment of this topic is completely different from this point of view.

In both works,
[
6
]
and
[
15
]
, a binary relation dealing with GD1 inverses (and 1GD inverses respectively) was introduced. In
[
6
, Theorem 2.14, Theorem 3.14]
, these relations were proved to be equivalent to other known relations, giving as an immediate corollary that they were partial orders in the set of matrices of index lesser or equal than 1.

In
[
16
]
, John Tate introduced the concept of finite potent endomorphisms. Let
V
denote an arbitrary
k-
vector space, in general, infinite-dimensional. The ring of endomorphisms over the vector space
V
will be denoted as
\operatorname{End}_{k}(V).
Given
\varphi\in\operatorname{End}_{k}(V)
we will say that
\varphi
is finite potent when
\varphi^{n}(V)
is a finite dimensional
k-
vector subspace of
V,
where
\varphi^{n}=\varphi\circ\dots\circ\varphi
composed
n
times. Further, in
[
4
]
, these endomorphisms were characterized in terms of a
\varphi-
invariant decomposition of the vector space which will be called AST decomposition.

This article is a contribution to the theory of Generalized Inverses and Matrix Partial Orders. Namely, we generalize the theory of GD1 inverses and 1GD inverses to arbitrary vector spaces, in general, infinite dimensional ones, using the theory of finite potent endomorphisms. We extend the studied binary relations defined by these generalized inverses and we prove that they are partial orders in the set of finite potent endomorphisms over arbitrary vector spaces.

The outline of this work, together with the results obtained, can be summarized as follows:

•

Generalization of GD1 (respectively 1GD) inverses to infinite dimensional vector spaces (without inner product, nor Banach or Hilbert spaces) in Section

(respectively Section

Application of the previous theory to Matrix Theory. Bearing in mind the well known relationship between endomorphisms over finite dimensional vector spaces and finite square matrices, the previous theory is used to study the set of GD1 inverses of a finite square matrix over an arbitrary field (respectively the set of 1GD inverses of a finite square matrix). Namely, if
A=A_{1}+A_{2}
is the core-nilpotent decomposition of
A
(Section
2.0.2
and
\mathrm{N_{u}(A)}
denotes the nullspace of

then by Theorem

\displaystyle A(GD1)\simeq k^{[\mathrm{dim}N_{u}(A)]\cdot(\mathrm{rk(A)}+\mathrm{rk(A_{2})})}

\displaystyle A(1GD)\simeq k^{[\mathrm{dim}N_{u}(A)]\cdot(\mathrm{rk(A)}+\mathrm{rk(A_{2})})}.

Moreover, the algorithms for the explicit calculation of these sets are offered in Section

and Section

•

In Section

the binary relations defined by GD1 and 1GD inverses in
[

]
are extended and studied over infinite dimensional vector spaces. After characterizing them using the AST decomposition of the operators involved, in Theorem
4.18
and Theorem
4.30
, we prove that they are both partial orders in the set of finite potent endomorphisms (Theorems
4.20
and
4.31
). This completes the theory of these binary relations, as in the matricial case presented in
[
6
]
, the authors prove that these relations are equivalent to other known ones defining partial orders (see
[

Moreover, we give a non trivial example (Example

•

As a side note we show that there is no relation between the binary relations involving GD1 and 1GD inverses and the subsets of GD1 and 1GD inverses of the linear operators (or matrices) defining this relation, Remark

•

To conclude, we present two “new” binary relations using compositions of GD1 and 1GD inverses (Definitions

4.34

We shall point out that as far as the authors know, these results and the approach here presented are not stated previously in the literature. Moreover, we highlight that every proof and result exposed can be specialized to finite square matrices over arbitrary ground fields. Finite potent endomorphisms do not form an ideal of the endomorphisms, namely, the sum and the composition of two finite potent endomorphisms is not, in general, a finite potent endomorphism; and therefore, the generalization presented here is not merely a generalization from finite dimensional vector spaces (finite square matrices) to infinite dimensional vector spaces, but one that also deals with additional problems derived from the impossibility to use the ordinary ring structure of endomorphisms.

2.0.1.
Finite Potent endomorphisms.

In
[
16
]
, John Tate introduced the concept of finite potent endomorphisms. Let
V
denote an arbitrary
k-
vector space, in general, an infinite dimensional one. The ring of endomorphisms over the vector space
V
will be denoted as
\operatorname{End}_{k}(V).
Given
\varphi\in\operatorname{End}_{k}(V)
we will say that
\varphi
is finite potent when
\varphi^{n}(V)
is a finite dimensional
k-
vector subspace of
V,
we shall note that
\varphi^{n}=\varphi\circ\dots\circ\varphi
n times.

In 2007, M. Argerami, F. Szechtman and R. Tifenbach showed in
[
4
]
that an endomorphism
\varphi
is
finite potent if and only if
V
admits a
\varphi
-invariant
decomposition
V=U_{\varphi}\oplus W_{\varphi}
such that

\varphi_{|_{U_{\varphi}}}
is nilpotent,
W_{\varphi}
is finite
dimensional and
\varphi_{|_{W_{\varphi}}}\colon W_{\varphi}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}W_{\varphi}
is an isomorphism.
This decomposition is unique and we shall call it
the
\varphi
-invariant AST-decomposition of
V
.

Moreover, we shall call “index of
\varphi
”,
i(\varphi)
, to the nilpotency order of
\varphi_{|_{U_{\varphi}}}
. One has that
i(\varphi)=0
if and only if
V
is a finite-dimensional vector space and
\varphi
is an automorphism.

We shall remark that the sum and the composition of finite potent endomorphism is not necessarily a finite potent endomorphism as can be seen by the following example. Let us consider the
k-
vector space
V=\underset{i\in\mathbb{N}}{\oplus}<v_{i}>.
Moreover, let us define the following endomorphisms:

\varphi(v_{i})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}v_{i+1}&\text{ if }&i\text{ is odd }\\
0&\text{ if }&i\text{ is even }\end{array}\right.\text{ and }\quad\psi(v_{i})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}0&\text{ if }&i\text{ is odd }\\
v_{i-1}&\text{ if }&i\text{ is even }\end{array}\right.

Notice that both of them are finite potent endomorphisms, as they are nilpotent. Then:

(\varphi+\psi)(v_{i})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}v_{i+1}&\text{if}&i\text{ is odd }\\
v_{i-1}&\text{ if }&i\text{ is even }\end{array}\right.\text{and }\quad(\varphi\circ\psi)(v_{i})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}0&\text{if}&i\text{ is odd }\\
v_{i}&\text{if}&i\text{ is even }\end{array}\right.,

from where we deduce that the sum and the composition of finite potent endomorphisms is not a finite potent endomorphism.

Basic examples of finite potent endomorphisms are all endomorphisms of a finite-dimensional vector space and finite rank or nilpotent endomorphisms of infinite-dimensional vector spaces.

For more details on the theory of finite potent endomorphisms, the reader is referred to
[
9
]
and
[
10
]
.

2.2.3.
Minus partial order of finite potent endomorphisms

Recently, one of the authors of this work has extended the minus partial order theory to arbitrary vector spaces, in general, infinite dimensional ones, using finite potent endomorphisms in

Theorem 2.12
.

[

The minus order of finite potent endomorphisms, “

Remark 2.13

[

The usual minus partial order restricted to the subset of finite potent endomorphisms coincides with the minus partial order for finite potent endomorphisms. This is, given two endomorphisms over an arbitrary k-vector space

[

, Theorem 4.5]

Let

II’.)

VI.)

3.

The purpose of the present section is to generalize the theory of

Precisely, the aim is to study these inverses by means of the AST decomposition of finite potent endomorphisms (which is intrinsic to the operator as it is unique and characterizes it). Furthermore, we will define several binary relations in the set of finite potent endomorphisms (which generalize the ones presented in

3.1.

Let

Definition 3.1
.

\displaystyle\varphi^{GD1}\circ\varphi\circ\varphi^{GD1}

\displaystyle\varphi^{GD1}\circ\varphi

\displaystyle=\varphi^{GD}\circ\varphi

\displaystyle\varphi\circ\varphi^{GD1}

\displaystyle=\varphi\circ\varphi^{-},

for certain
\varphi^{-}\in X_{\varphi}(1)
and
\varphi^{GD}\in X_{\varphi}(GD).

Theorem 3.2

Let
\varphi\in\operatorname{End}_{k}(V)
be a finite potent endomorphism. Then there exist GD1 inverses of

\varphi^{-}\in X_{\varphi}(1)
and

\displaystyle\psi\circ\circ\varphi\circ\psi

\displaystyle=\psi

\displaystyle\psi\circ\varphi

\displaystyle=\varphi^{GD}\circ\varphi

\displaystyle\varphi\circ\psi

\displaystyle=\varphi\circ\varphi^{-}

∎

Lemma 3.3

3.2.
1GD inverses of finite potent endomorphisms

The following section is devoted to the study of 1GD inverses, which can be understood as the dual generalized inverses of the GD1 inverses. Due to the analogy with their respective duals, we will only list the results concerning them without proofs unless we deem it necessary or clarifying.

Definition 3.13
.

Let

be a finite potent endomorphism. We will call “1 generalized Drazin inverse” of
\varphi,
or simply, 1GD inverse to any linear operator, denoted as

\displaystyle\varphi^{1GD}\circ\varphi\circ\varphi^{1GD}

\displaystyle=\varphi^{1GD}

\displaystyle\varphi^{1GD}\circ\varphi

\displaystyle=\varphi^{-}\circ\varphi

\displaystyle\varphi\circ\varphi^{1GD}

\displaystyle=\varphi\circ\varphi^{GD}.

Theorem 3.14
.

Let us consider a finite potent endomorphism
\varphi\in\operatorname{End}_{k}(V).
There exists 1GD inverses of
\varphi,

\displaystyle\psi\circ\varphi\circ\psi

\displaystyle=\psi

\displaystyle\psi\circ\varphi

\displaystyle=\varphi^{-}\circ\varphi

\displaystyle\varphi\circ\psi

\displaystyle=\varphi\circ\varphi^{GD}

4.
Binary relations with GD1 and 1GD inverses of finite potent endomorphisms

In the present section we will study the binary relations that were introduced in
[
6
, Section 2.1, Section 3.1]
for GD1 and 1GD inverses in the context of arbitrary vector spaces using finite potent endomorphisms.

Let us start by recalling
[
2
, Lemma 3.1]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\varphi\in\operatorname{End}_{k}(V)
be a linear operator over an arbitrary
k-
vector space. Then, the following morphism of sets

\begin{array}[]{rccl}\Gamma^{\varphi}\colon&X_{\varphi}(1)\times X_{\varphi}(1)&\rightarrow&X_{\varphi}(1,2)\\
&(\varphi_{1}^{-},\varphi_{2}^{-})&\mapsto&\varphi_{1}^{-}\circ\varphi\circ\varphi_{2}^{-}\end{array}

is surjective.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\varphi\in\operatorname{End}_{k}(V)
be a linear operator over an arbitrary
k-
vector space. Then, the following morphism of sets

\begin{array}[]{rccl}\hat{\Gamma}^{\varphi}\colon&X_{\varphi}(GD1)\times X_{\varphi}(GD1)&\rightarrow&X_{\varphi}(GD1)\\
&(\varphi^{GD1},\tilde{\varphi}^{GD1})&\mapsto&\varphi^{GD1}\circ\varphi\circ\tilde{\varphi}^{GD1}.\end{array}

is surjective.

Proof.

The map of sets is well defined. To wit, let us suppose that
\varphi^{GD1}=\varphi^{GD}\circ\varphi\circ\varphi^{-}
and that
\tilde{\varphi}^{GD1}=\tilde{\varphi}^{GD}\circ\varphi\circ\tilde{\varphi}^{-}
for certain
\varphi^{GD},\tilde{\varphi}^{GD}\in X_{\varphi}(GD)
and
\varphi^{-},\tilde{\varphi}^{-}\in X_{\varphi}(1).
Then

\varphi^{GD1}\circ\varphi\circ\tilde{\varphi}^{GD1}=(\varphi^{GD}\circ\varphi\circ\varphi^{-})\circ\varphi\circ(\tilde{\varphi}^{GD}\circ\varphi\circ\tilde{\varphi}^{-})=\varphi^{GD}\circ\varphi\circ\tilde{\varphi}^{-}

which is another
\widehat{\varphi}^{GD1}
in virtue of Proposition
3.5
. Moreover, the set of maps is surjective. Given any
\varphi^{GD1}\in X_{\varphi}(GD1),
it is clear that
\hat{\Gamma}^{\varphi}(\varphi^{GD1},\varphi^{GD1})=\varphi^{GD1}
and the claim is proved.
∎

The reader can notice that we can prove the analogous result for 1GD inverses.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\varphi\in\operatorname{End}_{k}(V)
be a linear operator over an arbitrary
k-
vector space. Then, the following map of sets

\begin{array}[]{rccl}\hat{\Gamma}^{\varphi}\colon&X_{\varphi}(1GD)\times X_{\varphi}(1GD)&\rightarrow&X_{\varphi}(1GD)\\
&(\varphi^{1GD},\tilde{\varphi}^{1GD})&\mapsto&\varphi^{1GD}\circ\varphi\circ\tilde{\varphi}^{1GD}.\end{array}

is surjective.

4.1.
GD1 and 1GD binary relations for finite potent operators

We shall define two binary relations that use GD1 inverses and 1GD inverses in their definitions in the context of arbitrary vector spaces, namely, infinite dimensional ones, in the framework of finite potent endomorphisms. Moreover, we shall prove that these relations are indeed partial orders in the set of finite potent endomorphisms over an arbitrary vector space.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\varphi\in\operatorname{End}_{k}(V)
be a finite potent endomorphism and let
V=W_{\varphi}\oplus U_{\varphi}
be the AST decomposition it induces. If
S\subseteq V
is a
\varphi-
invariant subspace then:

•

if
\varphi_{|_{S}}\in\operatorname{Aut}_{k}(S)
then
S
is finite dimensional and
S\subseteq W_{\varphi};

•

if
\varphi_{|_{S}}
is nilpotent then
S\subseteq U_{\varphi}.

Proof.

Let us suppose that
S
is a
\varphi-
invariant subspace such that
\varphi_{|_{S}}\in\operatorname{Aut}_{k}(S).
Then, for any
s\in S,
there exists some
s^{\prime}\in S
such that
s=\varphi^{m}(s^{\prime})
for
m=i(\varphi).
Hence,
s\in\operatorname{Im}(\varphi^{m})=W_{\varphi}.
Therefore,
S\subseteq W_{\varphi}
and
S
is finite dimensional. The equality holds precisely when
S+U_{\varphi}=V.
For the second statement, the reasoning is analogous.
∎

Definition 4.5
.

Let us consider two finite potent endomorphisms
\varphi,\psi\in\operatorname{End}_{k}(V).
We will say that
\varphi
is below
\psi
under the relation “
\,\leq^{GD1}\,
” if
\varphi\circ\hat{\varphi}^{GD1}=\psi\circ\hat{\varphi}^{GD1}
and
\tilde{\varphi}^{GD1}\circ\varphi=\tilde{\varphi}^{GD1}\circ\psi
for certain
\hat{\varphi}^{GD1},\tilde{\varphi}^{GD1}\in X_{\varphi}(GD1).

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\varphi,\psi\in\operatorname{End}_{k}(V)
be two finite potent endomorphisms. Then,
\varphi\,\leq^{GD1}\,\psi
(in the sense of Definition
4.5
) if and only if there exists a
\varphi^{GD1}\in X_{\varphi}(GD1)
such that
\varphi\circ\varphi^{GD1}=\psi\circ\varphi^{GD1}
and
\varphi^{GD1}\circ\varphi=\varphi^{GD1}\circ\psi.

Proof.

It is a direct consequence of Lemma
4.2
.
∎

Definition 4.7
.

Let us consider two finite potent endomorphisms
\varphi,\psi\in\operatorname{End}_{k}(V).
We will say that
\varphi
is below
\psi
under the relation “
\,\leq^{1GD}\,
” if
\varphi\circ\hat{\varphi}^{1GD}=\psi\circ\hat{\varphi}^{1GD}
and
\tilde{\varphi}^{1GD}\circ\varphi=\tilde{\varphi}^{1GD}\circ\psi
for certain
\hat{\varphi}^{GD1},\tilde{\varphi}^{1GD}\in X_{\varphi}(1GD).

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
\varphi,\psi\in\operatorname{End}_{k}(V)
be two finite potent endomorphisms. Then,
\varphi\,\leq^{1GD}\,\psi
(in the sense of Definition
4.7
) if and only if there exists a
\varphi^{1GD}\in X_{\varphi}(1GD)
such that
\varphi\circ\varphi^{1GD}=\psi\circ\varphi^{1GD}
and
\varphi^{1GD}\circ\varphi=\varphi^{1GD}\circ\psi.

Proof.

It follows directly from Lemma
4.3
.
∎

Remark 4.9
.

Lemma
4.6
and Lemma
4.8
state that the relations “
\,\leq^{GD1}\,
” and “
\,\leq^{1GD}\,
” are
\mathcal{G}-
based orders (recall Definition
2.8
).

Henceforth, whenever the relations “
\,\leq^{GD1}\,
” and “
\,\leq^{1GD}\,
” appear we will use its
\mathcal{G}-
based form.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
\varphi,\psi\in\operatorname{End}_{k}(V)
be two finite potent endomorphisms and let
\tau\in\mathrm{Aut}_{k}(V)
be any automorphism. Then:

•

\varphi\,\leq^{GD1}\,\psi
if and only if
\tau\circ\varphi\circ\tau^{-1}\,\leq^{GD1}\,\tau\circ\psi\circ\tau^{-1};

•

\varphi\,\leq^{1GD}\,\psi
if and only if
\tau\circ\varphi\circ\tau^{-1}\,\leq^{1GD}\,\tau\circ\psi\circ\tau^{-1};

Proof.

It is a direct consequence of Lemma
3.3
and Lemma
3.15
.
∎

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
\varphi,\psi\in\operatorname{End}_{k}(V)
be two finite potent endomorphisms. Then, the following statements are equivalent:

I.)

\varphi\,\leq^{GD1}\,\psi
;

II.)

\varphi=\varphi\circ\varphi^{-}\circ\psi=\psi\circ\varphi^{GD}\circ\varphi
for certain
\varphi^{-}\in X_{\varphi}(1)
and
\varphi^{GD}\in X_{\varphi}(GD);

III.)

\varphi=\varphi\circ\varphi^{GD1}\circ\psi=\psi\circ\varphi^{GD1}\circ\varphi
for certain
\varphi^{GD1}\in X_{\varphi}(GD1).

Proof.

I)\Longrightarrow II).
As
\varphi\circ\varphi^{GD1}=\psi\circ\varphi^{GD1}
and
\varphi^{GD1}\circ\varphi=\varphi^{GD1}\circ\psi
for certain
\varphi^{GD1}\in X_{\varphi}(GD1).
In virtue of the expression of any GD1 inverse obtained in Proposition
3.5
, we know that
\varphi\circ\varphi^{GD}\circ\varphi\circ\varphi^{-}=\psi\circ\varphi^{GD}\circ\varphi\circ\varphi^{-}
and therefore

(4.1)

\varphi\circ\varphi^{-}=\psi\circ\varphi^{GD}\circ\varphi\circ\varphi^{-}.

Similarly,
\varphi^{GD}\circ\varphi\circ\varphi^{-}\circ\varphi=\varphi^{GD}\circ\varphi\circ\varphi^{-}\circ\psi,
so

(4.2)

\varphi^{GD}\circ\varphi=\varphi^{GD}\circ\varphi\circ\varphi^{-}\circ\psi.

Composing in (
4.1
) with
\varphi
on the right hand side and in (
4.2
) with
\varphi
on the left hand side, one obtains that:
\varphi=\psi\circ\varphi^{GD}\circ\varphi
and
\varphi=\varphi\circ\varphi^{-}\circ\psi
respectively, so the first implication is proved.

II)\Longrightarrow III).
Let us suppose that
\varphi=\varphi\circ\varphi^{-}\circ\psi=\psi\circ\varphi^{GD}\circ\varphi
holds for certain
\varphi^{GD}\in X_{\varphi}(GD)
and
\varphi^{-}\in X_{\varphi}(1).
Take the composition:
\varphi^{GD}\circ\varphi\circ\varphi^{-}
which is a GD1 inverse of
\varphi.
By direct computation, one gets that:

\displaystyle\varphi\circ(\varphi^{GD}\circ\varphi\circ\varphi^{-})\circ\psi

\displaystyle=\varphi\circ\varphi^{-}\circ\psi=\varphi;

\displaystyle\psi\circ(\varphi^{GD}\circ\varphi\circ\varphi^{-})\circ\varphi

\displaystyle=\psi\circ\varphi^{GD}\circ\varphi=\varphi,

so the condition in the statement is deduced.

III)\Longrightarrow I).
Let us now suppose that
\varphi=\varphi\circ\varphi^{GD1}\circ\psi=\psi\circ\varphi^{GD1}\circ\varphi
for certain
\varphi^{GD1}.
To wit, right-composing with
\varphi^{GD1}
in the first equality one gets:

\varphi\circ\varphi^{GD1}=\psi\circ\varphi^{GD1}\circ\varphi\circ\varphi^{GD1}=\psi\circ\varphi^{GD1}

as GD1 inverses are, in particular,
\{2\}-
inverses (recall Lemma
3.6
). Reasoning on an analogous way, one obtains that:

\varphi^{GD1}\circ\varphi=\varphi^{GD1}\circ\varphi\circ\varphi^{GD1}\circ\psi=\varphi^{GD1}\circ\psi

so both conditions of the relation
\leq^{GD1}
hold and hence the claim is proved.
∎

As we shall see later in Theorem
4.20
, item
III)
of this theorem proves that the relation
\leq^{GD1}
is antisymmetric. However, transitivity is not directly granted. We shall devote the rest of this part of the article to seek a characterization of the aforementioned relation which serves to show that it is transitive and hence, a partial order in the set of finite potent endomorphisms.

The following result can be proven to be general for any
\mathcal{G}
-based relation defined using any subset of
\{1\}-
inverses on its definition, however, we adapt it for the binary relation we have introduced using
GD1-
inverses to maintain the coherence in exposition.

Corollary 4.12
.

Let
\varphi,\psi\in\operatorname{End}_{k}(V)
be two finite potent endomorphisms such that
\varphi\,\leq^{GD1}\,\psi,
then
\varphi\,\leq^{-}\,\psi
(Definition
2.11
). Moreover,
\varphi\,<^{s}\psi
(Definition
2.9
).

Proof.

Let us suppose that
\varphi\circ\varphi^{GD1}=\psi\circ\varphi^{GD1}
and
\varphi^{GD1}\circ\varphi=\varphi^{GD1}\circ\psi
for certain
\varphi^{GD1}\in X_{\varphi}(GD1).
As
X_{\varphi}(GD1)\subseteq X_{\varphi}(1)
(in fact
X_{\varphi}(GD1)\subseteq X_{\varphi}(1,2)
) then condition
I)
of Theorem
2.14
(and also II’) of Theorem
2.14
) are satisfied and hence
\varphi\,\leq^{-}\,\psi.
The statement dealing with the space pre-order (Definition
2.9
) is directly deduced from
II)
or
III)
of Theorem
4.11
. Namely, let us suppose that
\varphi\,\leq^{GD1}\,\psi
and hence
\varphi=\varphi\circ\varphi^{-}\circ\psi=\psi\circ\varphi^{GD}\circ\varphi
for certain
\varphi^{-}\in X_{\varphi}(1)
and
\varphi^{GD}\in X_{\varphi}(GD).
As
\varphi=\psi\circ\varphi^{GD}\circ\varphi
it is clear that
\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)\subseteq\operatorname{Im}(\psi)
and, similarly, using that
\varphi=\varphi\circ\varphi^{-}\circ\psi
one deduces that
\operatorname{Ker}(\psi)\subseteq\operatorname{Ker}(\varphi),
so both conditions of Definition
2.9
hold and the result is proved.
∎

Remark 4.13
.

Before continuing, let us use Theorem
4.11
to show that the relation given in Definition
4.5
for two finite potent operators
\varphi,\psi\in\operatorname{End}_{k}(V)
can not be characterized in terms of the sets of GD1 inverses of
\varphi
and
\psi.
Precisely, we shall remark that
\varphi\,\leq^{GD1}\,\psi
does not imply that
X_{\psi}(GD1)\subseteq X_{\varphi}(GD1).
Note that proving that a binary relation defined as the one in Definition
4.5
which is proven to be equivalent to an inclusion of certain subset of
\{1\}-
inverses is immediately transitive. In order to do so, let us consider the following two matrices:

A=\left(\begin{matrix}2&0\\
0&0\end{matrix}\right)\text{ and }B=\left(\begin{matrix}2&-6\\
0&3\end{matrix}\right).

Applying the algorithm described in Section
3.1.1
to
A,
one gets that the set of GD1 inverses of
A
is precisely:

A(GD1)=\bigg\{\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{2}&a\\
0&0\end{matrix}\right):a\in k\bigg\}.

Meanwhile,
B
is an invertible matrix and the set of GD1 inverses is precisely
B^{-1}
:

B(GD1)=\{B^{-1}\}=\bigg\{\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{2}&1\\
0&\frac{1}{3}\end{matrix}\right)\bigg\}.

Clearly,
B^{GD1}\notin A(GD1)
and, considering, for example, a fixed
A^{GD1}
such that
A^{GD1}=\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{2}&1\\
0&0\end{matrix}\right)\in A(GD1)
one has that the conditions of Theorem
4.11
are satisfied for
A^{GD1}.
Namely:

\displaystyle A\cdot A^{GD1}\cdot B

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{matrix}2&0\\
0&0\end{matrix}\right)\cdot\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{2}&1\\
0&0\end{matrix}\right)\cdot\left(\begin{matrix}2&-6\\
0&3\end{matrix}\right)=\left(\begin{matrix}2&0\\
0&0\end{matrix}\right).\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt

\displaystyle B\cdot A^{GD1}\cdot A

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{matrix}2&-6\\
0&3\end{matrix}\right)\cdot\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{2}&1\\
0&0\end{matrix}\right)\cdot\left(\begin{matrix}2&0\\

0&0\end{matrix}\right).

Therefore, we have shown that
A\leq^{GD1}B
but
B(GD1)\nsubseteq A(GD1).
Bearing in mind the well-known relationship between matrices and endomorphisms over finite dimensional vector spaces, which are an example of finite potent endomorphisms, we conclude.

Proposition 4.14
.

Let
\varphi,\psi\in\operatorname{End}_{k}(V)
be two finite potent endomorphisms and let
V=W_{\varphi}\oplus U_{\varphi}
be the AST decomposition
\varphi
induces. Then,

\varphi_{|_{W_{\varphi}}}=\psi_{|_{W_{\varphi}}}\text{ if and only if }(\varphi\circ\varphi^{GD1})_{|_{W_{\varphi}}}=(\psi\circ\varphi^{GD1})_{|_{W_{\varphi}}}

for any
\varphi^{GD1}\in X_{\varphi}(GD1).

Proof.

To prove the direct we must show that
(\psi\circ\varphi^{GD1})_{|_{W_{\varphi}}}=\operatorname{Id}_{|_{W_{\varphi}}},
because
(\varphi\circ\varphi^{GD1})_{|_{W_{\varphi}}}=(\varphi\circ\varphi^{-})_{|_{W_{\varphi}}}=\operatorname{Id}_{|_{W_{\varphi}}}
for any
\varphi^{GD1}\in X_{\varphi}(GD1).
Moreover, by Theorem
3.8
, one has that

(\psi\circ\varphi^{GD}\circ\varphi\circ\varphi^{-})_{|_{W_{\varphi}}}=(\psi\circ\varphi^{GD})_{|_{W_{\varphi}}}=(\psi\circ(\varphi_{|_{W_{\varphi}}})^{-1})_{|_{W_{\varphi}}}=(\psi\circ(\psi_{|_{W_{\varphi}}})^{-1})_{|_{W_{\varphi}}}

by hypothesis, so we conclude. For the converse,

(\varphi\circ\varphi^{GD1})_{|_{W_{\varphi}}}=\operatorname{Id}_{|_{W_{\varphi}}}=(\psi\circ\varphi^{GD1})_{|_{W_{\varphi}}}.

Therefore, one gets that

(\psi\circ(\varphi_{|_{W_{\varphi}}})^{-1})_{|_{W_{\varphi}}}=\operatorname{Id}_{|_{W_{\varphi}}}

and we conclude by the uniqueness of the inverse of a linear operator over a finite dimensional vector space.
∎

Corollary 4.15
.

Let us consider finite potent endomorphisms
\varphi,\psi\in\operatorname{End}_{k}(V)
with
i(\varphi)=m.
If
\varphi\,\leq^{GD1}\,\psi,
then

\varphi_{|_{W_{\varphi}}}=\psi_{|_{W_{\varphi}}}.
In particular,
\psi
leaves
W_{\varphi}

\psi-
invariant and
\varphi^{m+1}=\psi\circ\varphi^{m},
so
W_{\varphi}\subseteq\operatorname{Im}(\psi).

Proof.

It is deduced from Definition
4.5
and Proposition
4.14
. The rest of facts are a consequence of the construction and properties of the AST decomposition.
∎

Corollary 4.16
.

If
\varphi,\psi\in\operatorname{End}_{k}(V)
are two finite potent endomorphisms such that
\varphi\,\leq^{GD1}\,\psi
then
W_{\varphi}\subseteq W_{\psi}.
Moreover,

W_{\psi}=W_{\varphi}\oplus(U_{\varphi}\cap W_{\psi}).

Proof.

It is a direct consequence of Lemma
4.4
and Corollary
4.15
applied to the endomorphism
\psi
. To see the second statement, we shall prove the two following conditions
W_{\varphi}+(U_{\varphi}\cap W_{\psi})=W_{\psi}
and
W_{\varphi}\cap(U_{\varphi}\cap W_{\psi})=\{0\}.
Let
\bar{w}\in W_{\psi},
using the AST decomposition induced by
\varphi,
we write
\bar{w}=w+u
with
w\in W_{\varphi}
and
u\in U_{\varphi}.
Therefore,
u=\bar{w}-w\in U_{\varphi}\cap W_{\psi}
because
W_{\varphi}\subseteq W_{\psi}
by the recently proven result. Finally,
W_{\varphi}\cap(U_{\varphi}\cap W_{\psi})\subseteq W_{\varphi}\cap U_{\varphi}=\{0\},
and the claim is deduced.
∎

.

Let us consider two finite potent endomorphisms
\varphi,\psi\in\operatorname{End}_{k}(V)
such that
\varphi\,\leq^{GD1}\,\psi.
Then, the following morphism of sets

\begin{array}[]{rccl}\Gamma\colon&X_{\psi}(GD)\times X_{\varphi}(1)&\rightarrow&X_{\varphi}(GD1)\\
&(\psi^{GD},\varphi^{-})&\mapsto&\psi^{GD}\circ\varphi\circ\varphi^{-}\end{array}

is well defined for any
\psi^{GD}\in X_{\psi}(GD)
and

Proof.

We shall prove that the endomorphism

for any

and
\varphi^{-}\in X_{\varphi}(1),
is a reflexive generalized inverse of

that leaves

invariant, in virtue of the characterization of GD1-inverses given in Theorem

. If
\varphi\,\leq^{GD1}\,\psi,
then, by Corollaries

and

, we know that
\varphi_{|_{W_{\varphi}}}=\psi_{|_{W_{\varphi}}}
and

In virtue of the characterization of G-Drazin inverses of a finite potent endomorphism using the AST decomposition, recall Proposition
2.5
, we know that any
\psi^{GD}\in X_{\psi}(GD)
satisfies that
(\psi^{GD})_{|_{W_{\psi}}}=(\psi_{|_{W_{\psi}}})^{-1}.
In particular, we are only interested on the fact that, by Corollary
4.15

(4.3)

(\psi^{GD})_{|_{W_{\varphi}}}=(\varphi_{|_{W_{\varphi}}})^{-1}.

The claim is now that the endomorphism
\phi
is a reflexive generalized inverse of
\varphi.
To wit, as
\varphi\,\leq^{GD1}\,\psi
we know that
\varphi\,\leq^{-}\,\psi.
By Remark

invariant to conclude. This can be done by direct computation. To wit,

as it follows from the discussion showed in (

\phi
leaves
W_{\varphi}
invariant and both conditions of Theorem

Theorem 4.20

The binary relation “
\,\leq^{GD1}\,

k-

\displaystyle\psi

\displaystyle=\psi\circ\psi^{GD1}\circ\varphi=\varphi\circ\psi^{GD1}\circ\psi.

Lemma 4.25
.

Let
\varphi,\psi\in\operatorname{End}_{k}(V)
with
\varphi
being a finite potent endomorphism of index
i(\varphi)=m.
One has that
\varphi^{m+1}=\varphi^{m}\circ\psi
if and only if
\operatorname{Im}(\psi-\varphi)\subseteq U_{\varphi}.

As a consequence, under any one of both equivalent conditions,
\psi=\varphi+\phi
with
\phi\in\operatorname{End}_{k}(V)
satisfying that
\operatorname{Im}(\phi)\subseteq U_{\varphi}.

Proof.

Notice that one can rewrite
\varphi^{m+1}=\varphi^{m}\circ\psi
as
\varphi^{m}\circ\varphi=\varphi^{m}\circ\psi.
Hence
\varphi^{m}\circ(\psi-\varphi)=0
which is to say that
\operatorname{Im}(\psi-\varphi)\subseteq\operatorname{Ker}(\varphi^{m})=U_{\varphi}.
Conversely, if
\operatorname{Im}(\psi-\varphi)\subseteq U_{\varphi},
then, for any
v\in V,

\psi(v)-\varphi(v)\in U_{\varphi}.
Therefore,
\varphi^{m}(\psi(v)-\varphi(v))=0,
this is,
(\varphi^{m}\circ\psi)(v)=(\varphi^{m}\circ\varphi)(v).
This holds for any
v\in V,
so the converse is deduced.
∎

Proposition 4.26
.

Let
\varphi,\psi\in\operatorname{End}_{k}(V)
with
\varphi
being finite potent of index
i(\varphi)=m.
If
\varphi^{m+1}=\varphi^{m}\circ\psi
then:

I.)

\psi
leaves
U_{\varphi}
invariant.

II.)

\varphi^{m}\circ\varphi^{s}=\varphi^{m}\circ\psi^{s}
for any
s\geq 1.

Proof.

Let us begin proving
I.
Let us consider
u\in U_{\varphi}.
Then, one has that
0=\varphi^{m+1}(u)=\varphi^{m}(\psi(u))
and hence,
\psi(u)\in\operatorname{Ker}(\varphi^{m})=U_{\varphi}.
Let us continue by showing
II.
The proof is done by using the hypothesis recursively, precisely:

\varphi^{m}\circ\psi^{s}=\varphi^{m+1}\circ\psi^{s-1}=\dots=\varphi^{m+s},

and the statement is proved.
∎

Corollary 4.27
.

Let us consider two finite potent endomorphisms
\varphi,\psi\in\operatorname{End}_{k}(V),
with
i(\varphi)=m,i(\psi)=s
and such that they induce the following respective AST decompositions
V=W_{\varphi}\oplus U_{\varphi}=W_{\psi}\oplus U_{\psi}.
If
\varphi^{m+1}=\varphi^{m}\circ\psi
then
U_{\psi}\subseteq U_{\varphi}.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.26
we know that in this conditions
\varphi^{m}\circ\varphi^{s}=\varphi^{m}\circ\psi^{s}.
For any
u\in U_{\psi}
we have that

\varphi^{m+s}(u)=\varphi^{m}\circ\psi^{s}(u)=0

so
u\in\operatorname{Ker}(\varphi^{m+s})=\operatorname{Ker}(\varphi^{m})=U_{\varphi}
by definition of AST decomposition.
∎

Proposition 4.28
.

Let us consider two finite potent endomorphisms
\varphi,\psi\in\operatorname{End}_{k}(V),
such that they induce the following respective AST decompositions
V=W_{\varphi}\oplus U_{\varphi}=W_{\psi}\oplus U_{\psi}.
If
\varphi\,\leq^{1GD}\,\psi
then
U_{\psi}\subseteq U_{\varphi}.
Moreover,
U_{\varphi}=U_{\psi}\oplus(W_{\psi}\cap U_{\varphi}).

Proof.

If
\varphi\,\leq^{1GD}\,\psi
we know that
U_{\psi}\subseteq U_{\varphi}
by Lemma
4.24
and Corollary
4.27
.
We shall prove that
U_{\psi}+(U_{\varphi}\cap W_{\psi})=U_{\varphi}
and
U_{\psi}\cap(U_{\varphi}\cap W_{\psi})=\{0\}.
Let us express any
\bar{u}\in U_{\varphi}
using the AST decomposition induced by
\psi
as
\bar{u}=w+u
with
w\in W_{\psi}
and

Therefore, using that
U_{\psi}\subseteq U_{\varphi},
one has that

w=\bar{u}-u\in U_{\varphi}\cap W_{\psi},

and the first statement is deduced. Finally, it is clear from the construction of the AST decomposition that

U_{\psi}\cap(U_{\varphi}\cap W_{\psi})\subseteq U_{\psi}\cap W_{\psi}=\{0\},

so we conclude.
∎

Theorem 4.31
.

The binary relation “
\,\leq^{1GD}\,
” is a partial order on the set of finite potent endomorphisms over an arbitrary
k-
vector space.

Proof.

Reflexivity holds directly. In order to check antisymmetry, let us consider two finite potent endomorphisms
\varphi,\psi\in\operatorname{End}_{k}(V)
such that
\varphi\,\leq^{1GD}\,\psi
and
\psi\,\leq^{1GD}\,\varphi.
We shall use that
\varphi\circ\varphi^{1GD}=\varphi^{1GD}\circ\psi
for certain
\varphi^{1GD}\in X_{\varphi}(1GD)
as
\varphi\,\leq^{1GD}\,\psi
that
\varphi=\varphi\circ\varphi^{1GD}\circ\psi=\psi\circ\varphi^{1GD}\circ\varphi
for certain
\varphi^{1GD}\in X_{\varphi}(1GD)
and that

for some

in virtue of Theorem
4.21
. In these conditions, one has that

\displaystyle\varphi

\displaystyle=\varphi\circ\varphi^{1GD}\circ\psi=\varphi\circ\varphi^{1GD}\circ\psi\circ\psi^{1GD}\circ\psi=\varphi\circ\varphi^{1GD}\circ\varphi\circ\psi^{1GD}\circ\psi=

\varphi,\psi,\gamma\in\operatorname{End}_{k}(V)
such that
\varphi\,\leq^{1GD}\,\psi
and
\psi\,\leq^{1GD}\,\gamma.

4.30
we know that
\varphi\,\leq^{-}\,\psi
and

\operatorname{Im}(\psi-\varphi)\subseteq U_{\varphi}

\operatorname{Im}(\gamma-\psi)\subseteq U_{\psi}.

4.25

\operatorname{Im}(\beta)\subseteq U_{\psi}.

4.2.
On other binary relations with GD1 and 1GD inverses

This last section is devoted to define two binary relations using several compositions of GD1 and 1GD inverses and to show that they are partial orders. Indeed, we will prove that these relations are equivalent to two well known partial orders, the minus partial order and the G-Drazin partial order.

Proposition 4.32
.

Let us consider a finite potent endomorphism
\varphi\in\operatorname{End}_{k}(V).
Then, both maps of sets

\begin{array}[]{rccl}\tilde{\Gamma}^{\varphi}\colon&X_{\varphi}(GD1)\times X_{\varphi}(1GD)&\rightarrow&X_{\varphi}(GD)\cap X_{\varphi}(1,2)\\
&(\varphi^{GD1},\tilde{\varphi}^{1GD})&\mapsto&\varphi^{GD1}\circ\varphi\circ\tilde{\varphi}^{1GD}.\end{array}

and

\begin{array}[]{rccl}\Gamma^{+,\varphi}\colon&X_{\varphi}(1GD)\times X_{\varphi}(GD1)&\rightarrow&X_{\varphi}(1,2)\\
&(\tilde{\varphi}^{1GD},\varphi^{GD1})&\mapsto&\tilde{\varphi}^{1GD}\circ\varphi\circ\varphi^{GD1}.\end{array}

are surjective.

Proof.

Let us suppose that
\varphi^{GD1}=\varphi^{GD}\circ\varphi\circ\varphi^{-},

\tilde{\varphi}^{1GD}=\tilde{\varphi}^{-}\circ\varphi\circ\tilde{\varphi}^{GD}
for certain
\varphi^{GD},\tilde{\varphi}^{GD}\in X_{\varphi}(GD)
and
\varphi^{-},\tilde{\varphi}^{-}\in X_{\varphi}(1).
Let us study first the map of sets
\tilde{\Gamma}^{\varphi}.
The map is well defined. Clearly,

\displaystyle\varphi^{GD1}\circ\varphi\circ\tilde{\varphi}^{1GD}

\displaystyle=(\varphi^{GD}\circ\varphi\circ\varphi^{-})\circ\varphi\circ(\tilde{\varphi}^{-}\circ\varphi\circ\tilde{\varphi}^{GD})=\varphi^{GD}\circ\varphi\circ\tilde{\varphi}^{-}\circ\varphi\circ\tilde{\varphi}^{GD}=

\displaystyle=\varphi^{GD}\circ\varphi\circ\tilde{\varphi}^{GD};

which is a G-Drazin inverse in virtue of Lemma
2.6
. Indeed,

\displaystyle(\varphi^{GD1}\circ\varphi\circ\tilde{\varphi}^{1GD})

\displaystyle\circ\varphi\circ(\varphi^{GD1}\circ\varphi\circ\tilde{\varphi}^{1GD})=(\varphi^{GD}\circ\varphi\circ\tilde{\varphi}^{GD})\circ\varphi\circ(\varphi^{GD}\circ\varphi\circ\tilde{\varphi}^{GD})=

\displaystyle=\varphi^{GD}\circ\varphi\circ\varphi^{GD}\circ\varphi\circ\tilde{\varphi}^{GD}=\varphi^{GD}\circ\varphi\circ\tilde{\varphi}^{GD}.

Hence,
\tilde{\Gamma}^{\varphi}(\tilde{\varphi}^{1GD},\varphi^{GD1})
is a reflexive generalized inverse and a G-Drazin inverse. Moreover, if we consider a
\varphi^{+}\in X_{\varphi}(GD)\cap X_{\varphi}(1,2),
then, by Proposition
3.5
and Proposition
3.17
, we have that
\varphi^{+}\circ\varphi\circ\varphi^{+}\in X_{\varphi}(GD1)\cap X_{\varphi}(1GD).
In fact, one has that

\tilde{\Gamma}^{\varphi}(\varphi^{+},\varphi^{+})=\varphi^{+}\circ\varphi\circ\varphi^{+}=\varphi^{+}

so we conclude that
\tilde{\Gamma}^{\varphi}
is surjective. Let us now study the map
\Gamma^{+,\varphi}.
Firstly, note that

\Gamma^{+,\varphi}(\tilde{\varphi}^{1GD},\varphi^{GD1})=\tilde{\varphi}^{1GD}\circ\varphi\circ\varphi^{GD1}=(\tilde{\varphi}^{-}\circ\varphi\circ\tilde{\varphi}^{GD})\circ\varphi\circ(\varphi^{GD}\circ\varphi\circ\varphi^{-})=\tilde{\varphi}^{-}\circ\varphi\circ\varphi^{-}.

It is well defined.

\displaystyle(\tilde{\varphi}^{1GD}\circ\varphi\circ\varphi^{GD1})\circ\varphi\circ(\tilde{\varphi}^{1GD}\circ\varphi\circ\varphi^{GD1})

\displaystyle=(\tilde{\varphi}^{-}\circ\varphi\circ\varphi^{-})\circ\varphi\circ(\tilde{\varphi}^{-}\circ\varphi\circ\varphi^{-})=

\displaystyle=\tilde{\varphi}^{-}\circ\varphi\circ\varphi^{-}.

For the surjectivity, let us consider any
\hat{\varphi}^{+}\in X_{\varphi}(1,2).
Then, in virtue of Proposition
3.5
and Proposition
3.17
, it is clear that
\varphi^{GD}\circ\varphi\circ\hat{\varphi}^{+}\in X_{\varphi}(GD1)
and that
\hat{\varphi}^{+}\circ\varphi\circ\tilde{\varphi}^{GD}\in X_{\varphi}(1GD)
for whatever the G-Drazin inverses
\varphi^{GD},\tilde{\varphi}^{GD}\in X_{\varphi}(GD)
considered. Therefore:

\displaystyle(\hat{\varphi}^{+}\circ\varphi\circ\tilde{\varphi}^{GD})\circ\varphi\circ(\varphi^{GD}\circ\varphi\circ\hat{\varphi}^{+})

\displaystyle=\hat{\varphi}^{+}\circ\varphi\circ\varphi^{GD}\circ\varphi\circ\hat{\varphi}^{+}=

\displaystyle=\hat{\varphi}^{+}\circ\varphi\circ\hat{\varphi}^{+}=\hat{\varphi}^{+}

and thus we conclude the proof.
∎

Definition 4.33
.

Let
\varphi,\psi\in\operatorname{End}_{k}(V)
be two finite potent endomorphisms. We will say that
\varphi
is below
\psi
under the GD1-1GD relation, and it will be denoted as
\varphi\,\leq^{GD1-1GD}\,\psi,
when there exists some elements
\tilde{\varphi}_{1},\tilde{\varphi}_{2}\in\operatorname{Im}(\tilde{\Gamma}^{\varphi})
(Proposition
4.32
) such that

\displaystyle\varphi\circ\tilde{\varphi}_{1}=\psi\circ\tilde{\varphi}_{1}

\displaystyle\tilde{\varphi}_{2}\circ\varphi=\tilde{\varphi}_{2}\circ\psi.

Definition 4.34
.

Let
\varphi,\psi\in\operatorname{End}_{k}(V)
be two finite potent endomorphisms. We will say that
\varphi
is below
\psi
under the GD1-1GD relation, and it will be denoted as
\varphi\,\leq^{1GD-GD1}\,\psi,
when there exists some elements
\varphi^{+}_{1},\varphi^{+}_{2}\in\operatorname{Im}(\Gamma^{+,\varphi})
(Proposition
4.32
) such that

\displaystyle\varphi\circ\varphi^{+}_{1}=\psi\circ\varphi^{+}_{1}

\displaystyle\varphi^{+}_{2}\circ\varphi=\varphi^{+}_{2}\circ\psi.

Lemma 4.35
.

Let us consider two finite potent endomorphisms
\varphi,\psi\in\operatorname{End}_{k}(V).
Then

•

\varphi\,\leq^{GD1-1GD}\,\psi,
(in the sense of Definition
4.33
) if and only if there exists an element
\tilde{\varphi}\in\operatorname{Im}(\tilde{\Gamma}^{\varphi})
(Proposition
4.32
) such that

\displaystyle\varphi\circ\tilde{\varphi}

\displaystyle=\psi\circ\tilde{\varphi}

\displaystyle\tilde{\varphi}\circ\varphi

\displaystyle=\tilde{\varphi}\circ\psi.

•

\varphi\,\leq^{1GD-GD1}\,\psi,
(in the sense of Definition
4.34
) if and only if there exists an element
\varphi^{+}\in\operatorname{Im}(\Gamma^{+,\varphi})
(Proposition
4.32
) such that

\displaystyle\varphi\circ\varphi^{+}

\displaystyle=\psi\circ\varphi^{+}

\displaystyle\varphi^{+}\circ\varphi

\displaystyle=\varphi^{+}\circ\psi.

Proof.

It is a direct consequence of the surjectivity of both maps of sets presented in Proposition
4.32
.
∎

Remark 4.36
.

Notice that Lemma
4.35
states that the binary relations “
\leq^{GD1-1GD}
” and “
\leq^{1GD-GD1}
” are precisely
\mathcal{G}-
based orders (Definition
2.8
).

Theorem 4.37
.

Let
\varphi,\psi\in\operatorname{End}_{k}(V)
be two finite potent endomorphisms. Then:

I.)

\varphi\,\leq^{GD1-1GD}\,\psi,
if and only if
\varphi\,\leq^{GD}\,\psi
(Definition
2.10
).

II.)

\varphi\,\leq^{1GD-GD1}\,\psi,
if and only if
\varphi\,\leq^{-}\,\psi
(Definition
2.11
).

Proof.

Let us start by proving
I.
If
\varphi\,\leq^{GD1-1GD}\,\psi,
then, in particular, the
\tilde{\varphi}\in\operatorname{Im}(\tilde{\Gamma}^{\varphi})
is a G-Drazin inverse so Definition
2.10
is satisfied trivially. Conversely, let us suppose that
\varphi\,\leq^{GD}\,\psi
and therefore
\varphi\circ\varphi^{GD}=\psi\circ\varphi^{GD}
and
\varphi^{GD}\circ\varphi=\varphi^{GD}\circ\psi
for certain
\varphi^{GD}\in X_{\varphi}(GD).
Now, take the endomorphism
\tilde{\varphi}=\varphi^{GD}\circ\varphi\circ\varphi^{GD}
which is a G-Drazin inverse by Proposition
2.6
and a reflexive generalized inverse in virtue of Lemma
4.1
. Hence the constructed
\tilde{\varphi}\in\operatorname{Im}(\tilde{\Gamma}^{\varphi})
and clearly
\varphi\tilde{\varphi}=\psi\circ\tilde{\varphi}
and
\tilde{\varphi}\circ\varphi=\tilde{\varphi}\circ\psi
so
\varphi\,\leq^{GD1-1GD}\,\psi.

Let us now prove
II.
If
\varphi\,\leq^{1GD-GD1}\,\psi,
then, as we know that any element belonging to
\operatorname{Im}(\Gamma^{+,\varphi})
is, in particular, a
\{1\}-
inverse of
\varphi,
then
\varphi\,\leq^{-}\,\psi
in virtue of the characterization given in Theorem
2.14
. Conversely, if
\varphi\,\leq^{-}\,\psi,
then, for some
\varphi^{-}\in X_{\varphi}(1)
we know that
\varphi\circ\varphi^{-}=\psi\circ\varphi^{-}
and
\varphi^{-}\circ\varphi=\varphi^{-}\circ\psi.
Hence, for this same
\varphi^{-},
consider the composition
\varphi^{+}=\varphi^{-}\circ\varphi\circ\varphi^{+}.
By Lemma
4.1
we know
\varphi^{+}\in X_{\varphi}(1,2)=\operatorname{Im}(\Gamma^{+,\varphi}).
Again, it is direct that
\varphi\circ\varphi^{+}=\psi\circ\varphi^{+}
and
\varphi^{+}\circ\varphi=\varphi^{+}\circ\psi
(using the relation given by
\varphi\,\leq^{-}\,\psi
and the constructed
\varphi^{+}
). Therefore, we conclude that
\varphi\,\leq^{1GD-GD1}\,\psi
and the claim is proved.
∎

Corollary 4.38
.

The binary relations “
\leq^{GD1-1GD}
” and “
\leq^{1GD-GD1}
” are partial orders in the set of finite potent endomorphisms.

Proof.

It is deduced directly from the equivalences presented in Theorem
4.37
and Theorem
2.12
and Definition
2.10
.
