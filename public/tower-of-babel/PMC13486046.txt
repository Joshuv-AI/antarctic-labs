Interleukin-6 levels decrease following rituximab treatment but increase following tocilizumab treatment
Authors: Makoto Ide, Tetsuya Fukumoto, Hiroaki Ohnishi, Satoshi Okamura, Keitaro Kawano, Tomoko Yokoyama, Tomohiro Kameda
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIdiopathic multicentric Castleman disease (iMCD) is a rare, gradually progressive lymphoproliferative disorder thought to arise from chronic hypercytokinemia, with IL-6 playing a central role in disease manifestations. C-reactive protein (CRP) levels are widely used as a surrogate marker of IL-6 activity in clinical practice. Although anti-IL-6 therapies, such as tocilizumab, are established treatments for iMCD, spurious elevation of circulating IL-6 levels after IL-6 pathway blockade has been reported, complicating biomarker interpretation. In contrast, changes in IL-6 and CRP levels following rituximab therapy remain poorly characterized, and direct comparisons between these therapeutic approaches are limited. Therefore, this retrospective study measured serum IL-6 and CRP levels in 17 patients (12 men and five women; mean age, 45 years) with iMCD before and after treatment with rituximab (n = 8) and tocilizumab (n = 9). Paired t test were used to assess in-group differences. After rituximab therapy, CRP values decreased from 67.7 ± 55.5 (range: 1.0–174.9) to 25.8 ± 29.0 (range: 1.0–71.2) mg/L, while IL-6 values decreased from 25.6 ± 29.7 (range: 1.3–86.8) to 12.0 ± 11.2 (range: 0.8–31.0) pg/mL (normal range < 8.0), although these reductions were not statistically significant. In contrast, tocilizumab therapy resulted in a significant decrease in CRP values decreased from 68.9 ± 40.8 (range: 24.0–147.9) to 7.9 ± 9.8 (range: 0.1–30.5) mg/L (p < 0.05), while IL-6 levels increased from 24.2 ± 7.3 (range: 14.0–37.0) to 749.2 ± 497.7 (range: 26.5–1720.0) pg/mL (normal range < 8.0) (p < 0.05). These findings indicate distinct biomarker dynamics, in which tocilizumab therapy led to elevated IL-6 levels, while rituximab improved iMCD symptoms by reducing IL-6 levels.Graphical abstract Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1007/s00277-026-07130-w.Keywords: Castleman disease, TAFRO syndrome, IL-6, Rituximab, Tocilizumab

Introduction

Castleman disease (CD) is a benign lymphoproliferative disorder without any curative treatment. Clinically, CD is classified into unicentric Castleman disease (UCD), which involves a single lymph node station, and multicentric Castleman disease (MCD), which affects multiple lymph node regions and/or extranodal organs. MCD is a rare lymphoproliferative disorder characterised by gradual disease progression with fatal outcomes. While its aetiology is yet to be determined, the two aetiological categories include human herpes virus 8 (HHV-8)-associated MCD and HHV-8-negative (idiopathic) MCD (iMCD), with the latter demonstrating slowly progressive constitutional symptoms and a poor prognosis.

Histopathologically, CD has been classified into hyaline vascular, plasma cell, and mixed types. Among the various iMCD subtypes, TAFRO (thrombocytopenia, anasarca, fever, reticulin fibrosis, and organomegaly) syndrome is a rapidly progressive and fatal disease of unknown aetiology. Lymph nodes in TAFRO syndrome show hypervascular features resembling iMCD. Therefore, iMCD is divided into iMCD-not otherwise specified (NOS) and iMCD-TAFRO [1].

The aetiology of iMCD is thought to involve chronic hypercytokinemia involving various cytokine families, among which interleukin-6 (IL-6) plays a central role in mediating systemic inflammation and constitutional symptoms. Accordingly, IL-6 is considered a major potential therapeutic target. In clinical practice, the C-reactive protein (CRP) level is a well-known biomarker of IL-6 activity [2]. First-line therapies for iMCD include anti-IL-6 strategies: either IL-6 receptor blockade (tocilizumab) or IL-6 neutralization (siltuximab), as well as rituximab in selected cases [3].

Recent reports have described the paradoxical elevation of serum IL-6 values following anti-IL-6 therapy (siltuximab and tocilizumab) therapy for iMCD [2, 4, 5], complicating the interpretation of this biomarker during treatment. In contrast, changes in CRP and IL-6 levels following rituximab therapy remain poorly defined, while comparative data between B-cell–directed therapy and IL-6 pathway blockade are limited.

Therefore, in the present study, we compared the changes in serum IL-6 and CRP levels in patients with iMCD treated with rituximab or tocilizumab therapy to clarify treatment-specific biomarker dynamics and their implications for disease monitoring.

Patients and methods

This retrospective, single-centre observational study enrolled patients diagnosed with iMCD treated at Takamatsu Red Cross Hospital between 2001 and 2021. All included patients met the Japanese diagnostic criteria for CD [6, 7]. After receiving approval from Institutional Review Board (IRB) of Takamatsu Red Cross Hospital (No. 24 − 022), IL-6 and CRP levels were extracted from medical records.

Rituximab was administered at a standard dose of 375 mg/m2 weekly for 3–8 cycles without concomitant cytotoxic agents. Tocilizumab was administered at 8 mg/kg every 2–4 weeks during the treatment period. IL-6 and CRP levels were measured multiple times before and after treatment initiation. For rituximab-treated patients, serum IL-6 and CRP levels were measured both before and immediately after therapy completion.

For tocilizumab-treated patients, serum IL-6 and CRP levels were analysed immediately before treatment initiation and at the time of peak IL-6 concentration during repeated tocilizumab administration. These values were measured immediately prior to each tocilizumab administration. No complications without Castleman disease were recorded. IL-6 was measured using immunoassay in a commercial laboratory. Because the administration methods differed, IL-6 levels were measured continuously in the tocilizumab group, and the maximum value was detected and analysed.

Two patients with iMCD-TAFRO who were treated sequentially with rituximab followed by tocilizumab were excluded from the tocilizumab group analysis because their IL-6 levels had normalised after rituximab administration. Values below the limit of detection were imputed as one-half of the lower detection limit. Paired t tests were used to assess differences in IL-6 and CRP levels before and after therapy. Data were analysed using BellCurve for Excel®ฎ (Ver.3.21, Social Survey Research Information Co., Ltd., Japan), with p < 0.05 considered statistically significant.

Results

Seventeen patients with iMCD (12 men and 5 women; mean age: 45 years [range: 26–72 years]), including two with TAFRO syndrome, were analysed. Laboratory findings and chest and abdominal computed tomography excluded any other inflammatory diseases, such as infectious and collagen diseases. Histologically, four patients showed the hyaline vascular subtype, nine had the plasma cell subtype, and four were unclassified. Serological testing for hepatitis B, hepatitis C, human T-cell leukaemia, and human immunodeficiency viruses was negative in all patients. Polymerase chain reaction (PCR) testing for HHV-8 sequences in peripheral blood was negative in all 17 patients. Eight and nine patients received rituximab and tocilizumab, respectively.

Among eight patients treated with rituximab, CRP levels decreased from 67.7 ± 55.5 (range: 1.0–174.9) to 25.8 ± 29.0 (range: 1.0–71.2) mg/L, while IL-6 levels decreased from 25.6 ± 29.7 (range: 1.3–86.8) to 12.0 ± 11.2 (range: 0.8–31.0) pg/mL (normal range: <8.0). Although both biomarkers showed a downward trend, these changes did not reach statistical significance. Decreases in IL-6 and CRP were observed in both the iMCD-NOS and iMCD-TAFRO subgroups (Fig. 1A).

Similar changes were observed in patients with iMCD-NOS and iMCD-TAFRO. Dotted lines: iMCD-TAFRO cases. (B) IL-6 and CRP levels after tocilizumab treatment. Among the nine patients treated with tocilizumab, the median observation period was 27 months (range: 1–123 months). Peak IL-6 levels were observed at a median of 2 months following treatment initiation (range: 1–33 months). Among six cases with follow-up longer than 6 months, IL-6 levels decreased slightly from the peak levels but did not return to baseline. IL-6 levels increased more than 10-fold in all but one patient, and remained persistently elevated throughout therapy (Fig. 2).

Long-term interleukin-6 (IL-6) values following tocilizumab therapy in nine patients

In eight patients, the peak levels were 10-fold higher than baseline values, while one patient showed only a slight increase. The eight patients with high IL-6 values exhibited an early peak (median: 2 months after tocilizumab initiation), with slightly decreased IL-6 values.

After tocilizumab treatment, CRP levels decreased significantly from 68.9 ± 40.8 (range: 24.0–147.9) to 7.9 ± 9.8 (range: 0.1–30.5) mg/L, while IL-6 levels increased significantly from 24.2 ± 7.3 (range: 14.0–37.0) to 749.2 ± 497.7 (range: 26.5–1720.0) pg/mL (normal range < 8.0) (both p < 0.05) (Fig. 1B).

Discussion

Rituximab was first reported as a treatment for CD in 2001 in a case of HHV-8–associated MCD, in which its efficacy was attributed to reducing the viral load and reducing MCD symptoms [8]. The efficacy of rituximab for HIV-associated MCD was confirmed in a prospective open-label trial [9]. Thus, rituximab has been established as first-line therapy for HHV-8-associated MCD [10]. The use of rituximab therapy for iMCD was subsequently reported in an anecdotal case series in 2006 [11]. Although rituximab has been recommended as an alternative first-line therapy to anti-IL-6 therapy for non-severe iMCD [3], its precise role in iMCD currently remain uncertain. Combination regimens including rituximab are reportedly more effective than chemotherapy alone in iMCD [12]. A recent retrospective analysis demonstrated that only 23% of patients with iMCD achieved a durable response to rituximab therapy [10]; however, prospective studies are lacking. Therefore, the biological and clinical effects of rituximab in iMCD remain incompletely defined.

In the present study, CRP and IL-6 values decreased as clinical symptoms improved in patients with iMCD, including those with iMCD-TAFRO. Consistently, IL-6 levels decreased from the baseline following rituximab therapy. These findings indicate that rituximab was clinically effective in both iMCD-TAFRO and iMCD-NOS in our cohort. However, the mechanism of action of rituximab in CD remains incompletely understood.

IL-6 is produced in the germinal centres of lymph nodes and is positively correlated with anti-B-cell antibody responses [13]. Rituximab is thought to suppress B-cell activity, decreasing IL-6 production. Recent single-cell sequencing analyses in identical twin patients with iMCD revealed that IL-6 pathway genes more frequently co-localise on endothelial cells and fibroblastic reticular cells than on B cells. Notably, one patient treated with rituximab showed prompt symptom resolution and IL-6 decline. Together, these findings raise the possibility that rituximab may reduce IL-6 levels through mechanisms beyond direct B-cell depletion [14]. These results suggest that rituximab may attenuate iMCD activity, as evidenced by decreases in IL-6 and CRP levels.

This report included two patients with iMCD-TAFRO. Current treatment guidelines do not distinguish between IMCD-NOS and iMCD-TAFRO [3], although clinical features and laboratory findings differ between these subtypes. Whether iMCD-TAFRO is a subtype of iMCD remains debated [15]. Several agents, including rituximab, tocilizumab, and cyclosporine, are reportedly effective for the treatment of TAFRO syndrome. Notably, one study analysing the treatment outcomes for TAFRO syndrome observed a significantly longer time to the next treatment or death among patients receiving rituximab compared with those treated with tocilizumab or cyclosporine [16]. Although the present case series involved only two cases of iMCD-TAFRO, and did not permit subgroup comparison, both patients demonstrated biomarker improvement after rituximab therapy, supporting its potential utility in this subtype.

Tocilizumab is a humanised IgG antibody that selectively targets the IL-6 receptor to block downstream signalling and improving constitutional symptoms. In the present study, the repeated administration of tocilizumab every two weeks was required to maintain its efficacy. Tocilizumab use was first reported in 2000 in seven patients with CD [17], followed by a multicentre clinical trial [18]. Subsequently, tocilizumab was approved in Japan in 2005, and has since been used for the treatment of CD worldwide.

In the present study, CRP levels decreased, and clinical symptoms improved following tocilizumab therapy; however, IL-6 levels were markedly elevated, and did not correlate with post-treatment CRP levels. Similar IL-6 elevations following tocilizumab therapy have been reported in CD and rheumatoid arthritis [4, 16, 19]. This phenomenon is commonly attributed to receptor blockade: by saturating IL-6 receptors, tocilizumab prevents IL-6 binding and clearance, leading to the accumulation of circulating IL-6 [19]. A prior study reported that IL-6 levels increased dramatically in all patients receiving tocilizumab therapy, with 60% returning to baseline levels [5].

If impaired receptor-mediated clearance were the sole mechanism, IL-6 levels would be expected to remain persistently elevated throughout treatment. However, in our cohort, IL-6 levels exhibited an early peak following tocilizumab initiation, followed by a slight downward trend. Notably, spurious IL-6 elevation has been similarly reported following the administration of siltuximab, an anti-IL-6 antibody [2] with a mechanism of action distinct from that of tocilizumab, indicating that this phenomenon is not unique to tocilizumab and that an alternative mechanism may exist. Thus, the precise mechanism underlying IL-6 elevation following anti-IL-6 therapy remains unclear.

This study has several limitations. This was retrospective, single-centre, observational study without randomisation, control, or between-group case matching, with a small sample size because of the rarity of the disease. Consequently, the measurement intervals are not constant. There is also potential for bias and confounding factors. Moreover, although various cytokines are related to Castleman disease, we did not examine any cytokines other than IL-6. Therefore, cytokines not measured in this study may have influenced CRP levels. However, IL-6 was selected because it plays a major role in inflammation, and CRP levels indicate the severity of inflammation [2].

A strength of this study is that it presented the most extensive case data in this field and provided important knowledge of the cytokine profile of this rare and poorly understood disease. Therapeutic guidelines support the use of both anti-IL-6 agents and rituximab as first-line treatments [3]. Consistently, our findings revealed that tocilizumab increased IL-6 levels, whereas rituximab therapy was associated with a reduction in IL-6 levels. In our cohort, the observed decreases in IL-6 and CRP levels following rituximab administration were consistent with attenuation of iMCD activity; however, further research is needed to determine whether this effect reflects B-cell-dependent, B-cell-independent, or combined mechanisms.

Conclusions

Tocilizumab increased IL-6 levels with improved iMCD symptoms. Rituximab improved iMCD symptoms by reducing IL-6 levels. Therefore, rituximab treatment should be presumed to act via a different mechanism from tocilizumab in patients with iMCD.
