Sign-balance of random Laplace eigenfunctions
Stephen Muirhead 1, Igor Wigman 2

Abstract
. Motivated by the problem of the small-scale sign distribution of Laplace eigenfunctions, we introduce a strong notion of sign-balance for (eigen)functions, and prove that random eigenfunctions are sign-balanced above a precisely determined scale with almost full probability. The scale is proven to be optimal up to a logarithmic power of the energy. Our results include the important case of random spherical harmonics, as well as more general band-limited random waves on smooth Riemannian manifolds. Extending the notion of balance to arbitrary levels, we determine the precise optimum scale above which random eigenfunctions are volume-balanced with respect to non-zero levels. Beyond their intrinsic interest, our results serve as a model for a natural conjecture on the optimal scale at which deterministic Laplace eigenfunctions are sign-balanced.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation

Let
(\pazocal{M},g)
be a closed Riemannian
d
-dimensional manifold, and
\Delta
the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
\pazocal{M}
. It is well-known that the spectrum of
\Delta
is discrete, consisting of eigenvalues
\lambda_{j}\rightarrow\infty
corresponding to (real-valued) eigenfunctions
\varphi_{j}
satisfying the Helmholtz equation

(1.1)

\Delta\varphi_{j}+\lambda_{j}^{2}\varphi_{j}=0.

We are interested in the nodal geometry of
\varphi_{j}
for large
\lambda_{j}
. In the case of ‘generic’ chaotic manifolds
\pazocal{M}
, a heuristic argument due to M. Berry
[
3
]
suggests that, in the high energy limit
\lambda_{j}\rightarrow\infty
, the eigenfunctions
\varphi_{j}
exhibit, on geodesic balls
B_{r}(x)\subseteq\pazocal{M}
of radius at or above the
Planck scale

r=\frac{1}{\lambda_{j}}
, various statistical properties similar to those of random monochromatic waves. These are represented by the centred Gaussian isotropic random field
F:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with the covariance function

(1.2)

\mathbb{E}\left[F(x)\cdot F(y)\right]=\gamma_{d}(\|x-y\|),

where
\gamma_{d}(\|\cdot\|)
is the Fourier transform of the uniform measure on the sphere
\pazocal{S}^{d}
(e.g.
\gamma_{2}(\|\cdot\|)=J_{0}(\|\cdot\|)
). Further, it is suggested that the nodal geometry of
\varphi_{j}
and
F
should be similarly related.

In this paper we are interested in the
sign distribution
of the eigenfunctions
\varphi_{j}
. One may expect from Berry’s ansatz that
every
geodesic ball
B_{r}(x)\subseteq\pazocal{M}
of sufficiently large radius
r\gg\frac{1}{\lambda_{j}}
will contain a ‘balanced’ proportion of positive and negative values of
\varphi_{j}
. That is, in the appropriate regime of
r\gg\frac{1}{\lambda_{j}}
, as
j\rightarrow\infty
one might expect the
uniform
limit

(1.3)

\frac{\left|\varphi_{j}^{-1}(0,\infty)\cap B_{r}(x)\right|}{|B_{r}(x)|}\rightarrow\frac{1}{2},\quad x\in\pazocal{M},

where for a subset
\mathscr{D}\subseteq\pazocal{M}
,
|\mathscr{D}|
is the
d
-dimensional volume measure of
\mathscr{D}
.
Informally, we say that a sequence of eigenfunctions
\varphi_{j}
satisfying (
1.3
) is
sign-balanced
(see Definition
1.1
below). Our primary objective is to establish a positive result of this nature, thereby lending support to Berry’s ansatz.

There are only a few results pertaining to the sign distribution of Laplace eigenfunctions in the literature. A classical
quasi-symmetry
theorem due to Donnelly-Fefferman
[
7
]
asserts, under the extra assumption that
g
is analytic, that there exists a number
a>0
depending only on
g
, such that for every
fixed
geodesic ball
\mathscr{D}\subseteq\pazocal{M}
, and sufficiently large
j

(depending on
g
and the radius of
\mathscr{D}
), one has

\frac{\left|\varphi_{j}^{-1}(0,\infty)\cap\mathscr{D}\right|}{|\mathscr{D}|}\geq a.

Recently, Logunov-Nazarov
[
13
]
extended the quasi-symmetry to
smooth
closed surfaces.
For
d=2
, Nazarov-Sodin-Polterovich
[
20
, Theorem 1.4]
posed the question at what scales the quasi-symmetry persists, and proved a small-scale analogue of this result: there exists a number
a>0
depending only on
g
, so that the bound

(1.4)

\frac{|\varphi_{j}^{-1}(0,\infty)\cap\mathscr{D}|}{|\mathscr{D}|}\geq\frac{a}{\log{\lambda_{j}}\cdot\sqrt{\log\log{\lambda_{j}}}}

holds for every metric disk
\mathscr{D}=B_{r}(x)
that is ‘deeply intersected’ by
\varphi_{j}^{-1}(0,\infty)
(meaning that
\varphi_{j}^{-1}(0,\infty)\cap B_{r/2}(x)\neq\varnothing
). The lower bound in (
1.4
) is sharp up to the factor
\sqrt{\log\log{\lambda_{j}}}
, as there exists a number
C>0
, a sequence of spherical caps
\mathscr{D}_{j}
on the round
2
-sphere, and a sequence
\varphi_{j}
of spherical harmonics vanishing on the centre of
\mathscr{D}
, so that

\frac{|\varphi_{j}^{-1}(0,\infty)\cap\mathscr{D}_{j}|}{|\mathscr{D}_{j}|}\leq\frac{C}{\log{\lambda_{j}}}.

1.2.
Principal result I: Sign-balance for random spherical harmonics

In this paper we propose a new notion of
sign-balance
, finer than quasi-symmetry, and address whether an appropriately defined ‘generic’ Laplace eigenfunction satisfies this strong property.

1.2.1.
Sign-balanced sequences of Laplace eigenfunctions

Definition 1.1

(Sign-balanced sequences of functions)
.

i.

For
x\in\pazocal{M}
and
r>0
below the injectivity radius
\operatorname{Inj}(\pazocal{M})
of
\pazocal{M}
, the
defect
of a measurable function
f:\pazocal{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
on
B_{r}(x)
is

(1.5)

\pazocal{D}(x;r)=\pazocal{D}_{f}(x;r):=\frac{1}{|B_{r}(x)|}\int\limits_{B_{r}(x)}H(f(y))dy\in[-1,1],

where
H(\cdot)
is the sign function

(1.6)

H(t)=\begin{cases}1&t\geq 0\\
-1&t<0\end{cases}=\mathds{1}_{\cdot\geq 0}(t)-\mathds{1}_{\cdot<0}(t).

ii.

Let
\{r_{j}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{>0}
be a sequence of positive numbers, and
f_{j}:\pazocal{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
a sequence of measurable functions. The
sign-imbalance
of
f_{j}
at scale
r_{j}
is

(1.7)

\mathscr{B}(f_{j};r_{j}):=\sup\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\pazocal{M}\end{subarray}}|\pazocal{D}_{f_{j}}(x;r_{j})|\in[0,1].

We say that the
f_{j}(\cdot)
are
sign-balanced
above the scale
r_{j}
if

(1.8)

\lim\limits_{j\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{r\geq r_{j}}\mathscr{B}(f_{j};r)=0.

Our motivating question is whether, in a generic scenario, the Laplace eigenfunctions
\varphi_{j}
are sign-balanced in the sense of (
1.8
) above suitably chosen scales
r_{j}\gg\frac{1}{\lambda_{j}}
. Our main results assert that, in accordance with the discussion in §
1.1
, generic Laplace eigenfunctions are sign-balanced at sufficiently large scales
r\gg 1/\lambda_{j}
with almost full probability, in a sense to be made precise. Moreover we identify a second scale
r\gg 1/\lambda_{j}
below which the sign-balance fails decisively. Somewhat surprisingly, both these scales are
above
the Planck scale.

1.2.2.
Random spherical harmonics

Our first result concerns the Gaussian ensembles of random spherical harmonics on spheres
\pazocal{S}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
. Let us first define this ensemble in the classical setting of
d=2
. Recall that, for
\ell\geq 1
, the space
\pazocal{E}_{\ell}
of spherical harmonics of degree
\ell
, of dimension
\dim\pazocal{E}_{\ell}=2\ell+1
, are the functions
f
obeying the equation

\Delta f+\ell(\ell+1)f=0,

i.e. satisfy (
1.1
) with

\lambda=\lambda_{\ell}=\sqrt{\ell\cdot(\ell+1)}.

The space
\pazocal{E}_{\ell}
admits a canonical
L^{2}
-orthonormal basis of functions consisting of the Laplace spherical harmonics
\{Y_{\ell,m}\}_{-\ell\leq m\leq\ell}
. The
random spherical harmonics

H_{\ell}:\pazocal{S}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
of degree
\ell\geq 1
are the centred Gaussian random fields

(1.9)

H_{\ell}(x)=H_{2;\ell}(x)=\frac{\sqrt{4\pi}}{\sqrt{2\ell+1}}\sum\limits_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}a_{m}Y_{\ell,m}(x),\quad x\in\pazocal{S}^{2},

where the
a_{m}
are standard Gaussian i.i.d. random variables. Alternatively, the law of
H_{\ell}
is uniquely prescribed via its covariance kernel (also the reproducing kernel of
\pazocal{E}_{\ell}
as a Hilbert space)

(1.10)

K_{\ell}(x,y):=\mathbb{E}\left[H_{\ell}(x)\cdot H_{\ell}(y)\right]=P_{\ell}(\cos(d(x,y))),\quad x,y\in\pazocal{S}^{2},

where
d(\cdot,\cdot)
is the spherical distance and
P_{\ell}
is the Legendre polynomial of degree
\ell
. Since the law of
H_{\ell}
is invariant under rotations, the Laplace
spherical harmonics
\{Y_{\ell,m}\}_{-\ell\leq m\leq\ell}
in (
1.9
) could be replaced by an arbitrary
L^{2}
-orthonormal basis of
\pazocal{E}_{\ell}
.

The definition (
1.9
) can be extended to random (ultra-)spherical harmonics on spheres
\pazocal{S}^{d}
of arbitrary dimensions
d\geq 2
:

(1.11)

H_{\ell}(x)=H_{d;\ell}(x)=\frac{\big|\sqrt{\pazocal{S}^{d}}\big|}{\sqrt{n_{\ell}}}\sum\limits_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}a_{m}Y_{\ell,m}(x),\quad x\in\pazocal{S}^{d},

where the
a_{m}
are as in (
1.9
),
\{Y_{\ell,m}\}_{1\leq m\leq n_{\ell}}
are the Laplace spherical harmonics on
\pazocal{S}^{d}
, and

(1.12)

n_{\ell}=n_{d,\ell}=\dim{\pazocal{E}_{d;\ell}}={\ell+d-1\choose d-1}+{\ell+d-2\choose d-1}=\frac{2}{(d-1)!}\ell^{d-1}+O(\ell^{d-2}),

is the dimension of the space
\pazocal{E}_{d;\ell}
of spherical harmonics of degree
\ell
. Alternatively,
H_{\ell}
is the centred Gaussian random field on
\pazocal{S}^{d}
with the covariance (reproducing) kernel

\begin{split}K_{\ell}(x,y)=K_{d;\ell}(x,y):=\mathbb{E}\left[H_{d;\ell}(x)\cdot H_{d;\ell}(y)\right]&=G_{\alpha;\ell}(\cos(d(x,y)))=\frac{P_{\ell}^{\alpha-1/2,\alpha-1/2}(\cos(d(x,y)))}{{\ell+\frac{d}{2}-1\choose\ell}},\end{split}

where, as above,
d(\cdot,\cdot)
is the spherical distance,
\alpha=\frac{d-1}{2}
,
G_{d;\ell}
is the normalised Gegenbauer (ultra-spherical) polynomial of degree
\ell
, and
P_{\ell}^{\alpha,\beta}
is the Jacobi polynomial of degree
\ell
. The law of
H_{\ell}
is invariant with respect to the rotations of the sphere, and in particular

(1.13)

\operatorname{Var}(H_{\ell}(x))=K_{\ell}(x,x)=G_{\alpha;\ell}(1)\equiv 1

due to the implemented normalisation.

1.2.3.
Statement of principal result I: Sign-balance for random spherical harmonics

Recall the definition of sign-imbalance in (
1.7
). For every
\ell\geq 1
,
0<r<\pi
, the sign-imbalance of the random spherical harmonics
H_{\ell}
at scale
r
is the (real-valued) random variable

(1.14)

\mathscr{B}_{\ell}(r)=\mathscr{B}_{\ell;d}(r):=\mathscr{B}(H_{d;\ell};r).

Our first principal result determines a scale
\bar{r}=\bar{r}_{\ell}\gg\frac{1}{\ell}
, such that
\mathscr{B}_{\ell}(r)
vanishes in probability as
\ell\rightarrow\infty
, uniformly above
\bar{r}
. On the other hand, for a certain smaller scale
\frac{1}{\ell}\ll\underaccent{\bar}{r}_{\ell}\ll\bar{r}_{\ell}
, the random variables
\left\{\mathscr{B}_{\ell}(\underaccent{\bar}{r}_{\ell})\right\}
are shown to be bounded away from zero in the following strong sense:

Definition 1.2

(Random variables bounded away from zero)
.

A sequence of non-negative random variables
\left\{X_{n}\right\}

is
bounded away from zero in probability
if there exists a number
\epsilon_{0}>0
such that

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\pazocal{P}r(X_{n}>\epsilon_{0})=1.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
d\geq 2
. For
\ell\geq 1
and
0<r<\pi
define
\mathscr{B}_{\ell}(r)
as in (
1.14
), and set

(1.15)

\underaccent{\bar}{r}_{\ell}=\frac{(\log{\ell})^{\frac{1}{2(d-1)}}}{\ell}\quad\text{and}\quad\bar{r}_{\ell}=\frac{(\log{\ell})^{\frac{1}{d-1}}}{\ell}.

Then the following hold:

i.

There exists a number
\mu>0
sufficiently large, only depending on
d
, so that, as
\ell\rightarrow\infty
,

(1.16)

\sup_{r\geq\mu\cdot\bar{r}_{\ell}}\mathscr{B}_{\ell}(r)\xrightarrow{P}0.

ii.

There exists a number
\mu>0
sufficiently small, only depending on
d
, so that the sequence
of random variables

\left\{\mathscr{B}_{\ell}(\mu\cdot\underaccent{\bar}{r}_{\ell})\right\}_{\ell\geq 1}

is bounded away from zero in probability.

Theorem
1.3
(i.) asserts that the random spherical harmonics are sign-balanced, with almost full probability, above the scale
\bar{r}_{\ell}
in (
1.15
), and are
not
sign-balanced, with almost full probability, at and below the scale
\underaccent{\bar}{r}_{\ell}
; both scales are a logarithm power above the Planck scale. In fact, from a quantitative version of (
1.16
) obtained in the proof of Theorem
1.3
, and the Borel-Cantelli lemma, we can deduce that the same holds
almost surely
for sequences of random spherical harmonics, see Corollary
1.9
below for a (more general) result of this nature. As a concrete application, for
d=2
the relevant scales are:

\underaccent{\bar}{r}_{\ell}=\frac{\left(\log{\ell}\right)^{1/2}}{\ell}\quad\text{and}\quad\bar{r}_{\ell}=\frac{\log{\ell}}{\ell}.

See a further discussion on the significance of this result in §
2.1
below.

1.3.
Principal result II: Volume-balance for random waves on manifolds

Our second principal result generalises Theorem
1.3
in two directions: to a wider class of Gaussian ensemble of random waves defined on general smooth manifolds, and by extending the notion of sign-balance to the
volume-balance
at arbitrary levels. Remarkably, for non-zero levels, we identify the
precise
scale, coinciding with
\bar{r}_{\ell}
in (
1.15
), at which volume-balance occurs with high probability. This is in contrast to the sign-balance, where there is a slight lacuna between the upper and the lower scales (
1.15
).

1.3.1.
Random waves on smooth manifolds

Recall that
(\pazocal{M},g)
is a closed Riemannian
d
-manifold, and the
\varphi_{j}
are the Laplace eigenfunctions on
\pazocal{M}
corresponding to eigenvalues
\lambda_{j}
, satisfying the Helmholtz equation (
1.1
). Our results in Theorem
1.3
for the round sphere
\pazocal{M}=\pazocal{S}^{d}
benefited from the high spectral degeneracy of the Laplace operator on
\pazocal{S}^{d}
, which arises due to the rotational symmetry. Since this is not the case for generic manifolds
\pazocal{M}
, we will instead superimpose Laplace eigenfunctions corresponding to different eigenvalues lying in an
energy window
. If the energy window is suitably-chosen – narrow enough to contain comparable frequencies, but large enough for the superposition to be sufficiently chaotic – it is reasonable to expect the corresponding random linear combinations to represent ‘generic’ Laplace eigenfunctions on
\pazocal{M}
.

Let
T
be a (large) spectral parameter, and
\eta=\eta(T)\in(0,T]
a band-width. The
band-limited random Gaussian function (‘random wave’)
is the centred Gaussian random field

(1.17)

f_{T}(x)=f_{T,\eta}(x)=\frac{\sqrt{|\pazocal{M}|}}{\sqrt{N}}\sum\limits_{\lambda_{j}\in[T-\eta,T]}a_{j}\varphi_{j}(x),\quad x\in\pazocal{M},

where the
a_{j}
are standard Gaussian i.i.d. random variables, and

(1.18)

N=N(T,\eta)=\#\{\lambda_{j}\in[T-\eta,T]\}=N(T)-N(T-\eta)

is the number of energy levels inside the energy window
[T-\eta,T]
, with

(1.19)

N(T):=\#\{\lambda_{j}\leq T\}

the spectral function of
\pazocal{M}
. (We tacitly assume that
T-\eta
is not an energy level of
\pazocal{M}
.)
The normalising pre-factor in the definition (
1.17
) is chosen so that

(1.20)

\int\limits_{\pazocal{M}}\operatorname{Var}(f_{T}(x))dx=|\pazocal{M}|.

In the regime
\eta\to\infty
, this further ensures that, as
T\rightarrow\infty
,

\operatorname{Var}(f_{T}(x))\sim 1

uniformly in
x\in\pazocal{M}
(see Proposition
3.1
). We stress that spectral degeneracies of
\pazocal{M}
are allowed, in which case the law of
f_{T}
is invariant w.r.t. the choice of an orthonormal basis
\{\varphi_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
of
L^{2}(\pazocal{M})
. For the special case of the round sphere
\pazocal{M}=\pazocal{S}^{d}
we allow
\eta\equiv 1
, whence, by the conventions of §
3
, for the choice

T=\sqrt{\lambda_{\ell}}=\sqrt{\ell\cdot(\ell+d-1)}\sim\ell

(otherwise the summation on the r.h.s. of (
1.17
) is empty), the random waves in (
1.17
) coincides with the random spherical harmonics (
1.11
):

f_{\ell,1}(x)\equiv H_{\ell}(x).

More generally, for
\pazocal{M}=\pazocal{S}^{d}
the random waves (
1.17
) are a superposition of the random spherical harmonics
H_{\ell^{\prime}}
with
\ell^{\prime}
in the given
energy window (essentially
[\ell-\eta+1,\ell]
with
\ell\approx T
) with weights prescribed by the normalising factors
\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{\ell^{\prime}}}}

in (
1.11
), see (
3.4
) below.

1.3.2.
Volume-balance at arbitrary levels

Definition 1.4

(Volume-balanced sequences of functions)
.

Let
\pazocal{M}
be a smooth manifold,
f:\pazocal{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
a real-valued measurable function,
u\in\mathbb{R}
a (fixed) level, and recall that
H(\cdot)
is the sign function (
1.6
).

i.

For
x\in\pazocal{M}
and
0<r<\operatorname{Inj}(\pazocal{M})
, the
volume-bias
of
f
at level
u
, restricted to
B_{r}(x)
, is

(1.21)

\pazocal{D}_{u}(x;r)=\pazocal{D}_{f;u}(x;r):=\frac{1}{|B_{r}(x)|}\int\limits_{B_{r}(x)}\left(H(f(y)-u)-\tau(u)\right)dy\in[-2,2],

with
Z
a standard Gaussian random variable, and

(1.22)

\tau(u):=1-2\Phi(u)=\mathbb{E}[H(Z-u)],

where
\Phi
is the Gaussian cdf. We denote

(1.23)

\widetilde{\pazocal{D}}_{u}(x;r)=\widetilde{\pazocal{D}}_{f;u}(x;r):=\frac{1}{|B_{r}(x)|}\int\limits_{B_{r}(x)}H(f(y)-u)dy\in[-1,1],

the uncentred variant of
\pazocal{D}_{u}(x;r)
.

ii.

Let
r_{j}\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{>0}
be a sequence of positive numbers, and
f_{j}:\pazocal{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
a sequence of measurable functions. The
volume-imbalance
of
f_{j}
at scale
r_{j}
with respect to the level
u
is

(1.24)

\mathscr{B}_{u}(f_{j};r_{j}):=\sup\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\pazocal{M}\end{subarray}}|\pazocal{D}_{u}(x;r_{j})|\in[0,2].

We say that the
f_{j}(\cdot)
are
volume-balanced
above the scale
r_{j}
with respect to level
u
if

(1.25)

\lim\limits_{j\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{r\geq r_{j}}\mathscr{B}_{u}(f_{j};r)=0.

When applied to a centred Gaussian random field
f:\pazocal{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, the term
\tau(u)
in (
1.21
) is the natural centering, so that
\mathbb{E}[\pazocal{D}_{u}(x;r)]\equiv 0
. We observe that the volume-bias (
1.21
) (resp. volume-imbalance (
1.24
)) at level
u=0
coincides with the defect (
1.5
) (resp. sign-imbalance (
1.7
)).

1.3.3.
Statement of principal result II: Volume-balance for random waves on manifolds

For
T>0
,
\eta\in(0,T]
,
0<r<\operatorname{Inj}(\pazocal{M})
, and
u\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the
volume-imbalance
of the random wave
f_{T,\eta}
at level
u
to be

(1.26)

\mathscr{B}_{T,u}(r)=\mathscr{B}_{T,u;\eta}(r):=\mathscr{B}_{u}(f_{T,\eta};r),

cf. (
1.14
). As announced, our next result determines a scale
r=\bar{r}_{T}
above which the volume-imbalance
\mathscr{B}_{T,u}(r)
of
f_{T,\eta}
at arbitrary level vanishes in probability as
T\to\infty
, and shows that the scale is optimal for non-zero levels
u\neq 0
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
(\pazocal{M},g)
be a smooth Riemannian
d
-manifold, and
\eta=\eta(T)\in(0,T]
satisfying either (a)
\eta(T)\rightarrow\infty
or (b)
\pazocal{M}=\pazocal{S}^{d}
. Let
u\in\mathbb{R}
, and define
\mathscr{B}_{T,u}(r)
as in (
1.26
). Set

(1.27)

\bar{r}_{T}=\frac{\min\left\{(\log{T})^{\frac{1}{d-1}},\left(\frac{T\log{T}}{\eta}\right)^{\frac{1}{d}}\right\}}{T}\quad\quad\underaccent{\bar}{r}_{T}=\frac{\min\left\{(\log{T})^{\frac{1}{2(d-1)}},\left(\frac{T\log{T}}{\eta}\right)^{\frac{1}{2d-1}}\right\}}{T}.

Then, as
T\to\infty
, the following hold:

i.

There exists a number
\mu>0
sufficiently large, only depending on
\pazocal{M}
, so that

(1.28)

\sup_{r\geq\mu\cdot\bar{r}_{T}}\mathscr{B}_{T,u}(r)\xrightarrow{P}0.

ii.

Assume that either (a’) there exists a number
\delta_{0}>0
so that
\eta(T)>T^{\delta_{0}}
or (b’)
\pazocal{M}=\pazocal{S}^{d}
. Then there exists a number
\mu>0
sufficiently small, only depending on
\pazocal{M}
, so that:

•

If
u\neq 0
,
\left\{\mathscr{B}_{T,u}(\mu\cdot\bar{r}_{T})\right\}_{T\geq 1}
is bounded away from zero in probability.

•

If
u=0
,
\left\{\mathscr{B}_{T,u}(\mu\cdot\underaccent{\bar}{r}_{T})\right\}_{T\geq 1}
is bounded away from zero in probability.

As mentioned in §
1.3.1
, Theorem
1.5
allows for
\pazocal{M}=\pazocal{S}^{d}
and
\eta\equiv 1
, and so contains Theorem
1.3
as a particular case. See Corollary
1.9
below for an almost sure version of Theorem
1.5
(i.).

If
\eta
satisfies
\eta(T)=o(T)
, the random waves (
1.17
) are called
monochromatic
, closest to ‘pure’ eigenstates. Assuming in addition that
\eta(T)=o\big(T\cdot(\log T)^{-\frac{1}{d-1}}\big)

(for
\bar{r}_{T}
) or
\eta(T)=o\big(T\cdot(\log T)^{-\frac{1}{2(d-1)}}\big)
(for
\underaccent{\bar}{r}_{T}
), the scales (
1.27
) in the monochromatic regime are

(1.29)

\bar{r}_{T}=\frac{(\log{T})^{\frac{1}{d-1}}}{T}\quad\text{and}\quad\underaccent{\bar}{r}_{T}=\frac{(\log{T})^{\frac{1}{2(d-1)}}}{T},

coinciding with the scales (
1.15
) for random spherical harmonics (with
T\sim\ell
).

In general, the scales (
1.27
)
interpolate
between the monochromatic scales (
1.29
) and the scales

\bar{r}_{T}\asymp\frac{(\log{T})^{\frac{1}{d}}}{T}\quad\quad\underaccent{\bar}{r}_{T}\asymp\frac{(\log{T})^{\frac{1}{2d-1}}}{T}

which are relevant in the regime of positively-banded waves satisfying
\eta(T)=c_{0}\cdot T
with
0<c_{0}\leq 1
constant (alternatively
c_{0}=c_{0}(T)
bounded away from
0
). The
crossover
between these two regimes occurs for
barely monochromatic
waves for which
\eta(T)=o(T)
but
\frac{\eta(T)\cdot(\log{T})^{\gamma}}{T}\rightarrow\infty
for some
0<\gamma<\frac{1}{d-1}
, e.g. for

\eta\asymp\frac{T}{(\log{T})^{\gamma}}
with
0<\gamma<\frac{1}{2(d-1)}
, one has

\bar{r}_{T}\asymp\frac{(\log{T})^{\frac{\gamma+1}{d}}}{T}\quad\quad\underaccent{\bar}{r}_{T}\asymp\frac{(\log{T})^{\frac{\gamma+1}{2d-1}}}{T}.

Equivalently to (
1.27
), the scales
\bar{r}_{T}
and
\underaccent{\bar}{r}_{T}
may be defined implicitly as the solutions to the equations

(1.30)

(\bar{r}_{T}\eta)^{d-1}\max\{1,\bar{r}_{T}\eta_{T}\}=\log{T}\quad\quad(\underaccent{\bar}{r}_{T}\eta)^{2(d-1)}\max\{1,\underaccent{\bar}{r}_{T}\eta_{T}\}=\log{T},

more natural in the proofs (see, e.g., (
1.32
)).

1.4.
Volume-bias concentration

Theorem
1.5
(and Theorem
1.3
as a particular case of Theorem
1.5
) will follow from the concentration of the volume-bias of the restriction of
f_{T}(\cdot)

to geodesic balls centred at a
fixed
point
x\in\pazocal{M}
of arbitrary radius. We believe these concentration results to be of independent interest.

Recall that
(\pazocal{M},g)
is a closed Riemannian
d
-manifold, and
\pazocal{D}_{T,u}(x;r)
is the volume-bias defined in (
1.21
) for the random wave
f_{T,\eta}(\cdot)
. In particular
\pazocal{D}_{T}(x;r)=\pazocal{D}_{T,0}(x;r)
is the defect. First, we present an upper bound for the defect concentration that will be used to infer Theorem
1.5
(i.):

Theorem 1.6

(Volume-bias concentration upper bound)
.

There exists a number
C=C(\pazocal{M})>0
sufficiently large, and for every
\varepsilon>0
a number
c=c(\pazocal{M},\epsilon)>0
sufficiently small, such that the following holds. For all
T\geq 1
and
r\geq 1/T
such that either (a)
\eta\in[C,T]
or (b)
\pazocal{M}=\pazocal{S}^{d}
and
\eta\in[1,T]
,
one has:

(1.31)

\pazocal{P}r(|\pazocal{D}_{T,u}(x;r)|>\varepsilon)<\begin{cases}e^{-c(rT)^{d-1}}&1/T\leq r\leq 1/\eta,\\
e^{-c(rT)^{d-1}r\eta}&\frac{1}{\eta}<r<\operatorname{Inj}(\pazocal{M}),\end{cases}

uniformly w.r.t.
x\in\pazocal{M}
and
u\in\mathbb{R}
.

To infer Theorem
1.5
(ii.) we need the corresponding
lower
bound for the defect concentration. It will be more natural to work with the
uncentred
volume-bias (
1.23
),
one benefit of which is that
u\mapsto\widetilde{\pazocal{D}}_{f,u}(x;r)
is non-increasing. We infer Theorem
1.5
(ii.) in the case of non-zero level
u\neq 0
from the following bound:

Theorem 1.7

(Volume-bias concentration lower bound I)
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
there exist a number
C=C(\pazocal{M},\varepsilon)>0
sufficiently large such that, for all
T\geq 1
,
r\geq\frac{C}{T}
, such that either (a)
\min\left\{\eta,\frac{1}{r}\right\}>C\cdot(rT)^{(d-1)/2}
or (b)
\pazocal{M}=\pazocal{S}^{d}
and
r<1/C
, one has:

\pazocal{P}r(\widetilde{\pazocal{D}}_{T,u}(x;r)>-\varepsilon)>\begin{cases}e^{-C(1+u^{2})(rT)^{d-1}}&1/T\leq r\leq 1/\eta,\\
e^{-C(1+u^{2})(rT)^{d-1}r\eta}&\frac{1}{\eta}<r<\operatorname{Inj}(\pazocal{M}),\end{cases}

uniformly w.r.t.
x\in\pazocal{M}
and
u\in\mathbb{R}
.

Observe that, for
u>0
,
\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{\pazocal{D}}_{f;u}(x;r)]=\tau(u)>0
. Hence, one could choose
\varepsilon\in(0,\tau(u))
sufficiently small and apply Theorem
1.7
to obtain a lower bound on
\pazocal{P}r(|\pazocal{D}_{T,u}(x;r)|>\varepsilon)
that matches the order of the upper bound in Theorem
1.6
. By contrast, for the defect (
u=0
), one has
\tau(u)=0
, and thus Theorem
1.7
only asserts a lower bound on
\pazocal{P}r(|\pazocal{D}_{T,u}(x;r)|<\varepsilon)
, useless for the purpose of proving Theorem
1.5
(ii.). To handle this regime we establish the following bound which is of smaller order and applies to a narrower range of parameters:

Theorem 1.8

(Volume-bias concentration lower bound II)
.

There is an absolute constant
\varepsilon>0
and a number
C=C(\pazocal{M})>0
so that, for all
T\geq 1
,
r\geq\frac{C}{T}
, such that either (a)
\min\{\eta,1/r\}>C(rT)^{d-1}
or (b)
\pazocal{M}=\pazocal{S}^{d}
and
r<1/(C\sqrt{T})
, one has:

\pazocal{P}r(\widetilde{\pazocal{D}}_{T,u}(x;r)>\varepsilon)>\begin{cases}e^{-C(1+u^{2})(rT)^{2(d-1)}}&1/T\leq r\leq 1/\eta,\\
e^{-C(1+u^{2})(rT)^{2(d-1)}r\eta}&\frac{1}{\eta}<r<\operatorname{Inj}(\pazocal{M}),\end{cases}

uniformly w.r.t.
x\in\pazocal{M}
and
u\in\mathbb{R}
.

Although Theorem
1.8
is applied below for the purpose of studying the defect, the statement for
positive
levels
u>0
(chosen arbitrarily as
u=1
within the proof of Theorem
1.5
(ii.)) is a stronger result than the same with
u=0
. The stronger result is required, as the level
u
will be slightly adjusted within the proof of Theorem
1.5
(ii.) to exploit a certain ‘sprinkled decoupling’ technique.

Note that theorems
1.7
and
1.8
are only effective for general manifolds on
mesoscopic
scales: in the case of Theorem
1.7
, on scales smaller than

r=T^{-(d-1)/(d+1)}=T^{2/(d+1)}/T=o(1),

and for Theorem
1.8
, scales smaller than

r=T^{-d/(d+1)}=T^{1/(d+1)}/T=o(1).

For the sphere, Theorem
1.7
is effective on
all
mesoscopic scales
r=o(1)
, whereas Theorem
1.8
is only effective on
mesoscopic
scales
r=o(T^{1/2}/T)
.

While we are particularly interested in the defect, for the volume-bias at non-zero levels
u\neq 0
theorems
1.6
and
1.7
together show that, uniformly over geodesic balls at mesoscopic scales, the volume-bias has (upper) large deviations of order

(1.32)

-\log\pazocal{P}r(\pazocal{D}_{T,u}(x;r)>\varepsilon)\asymp(rT)^{d-1}\max\{1,r\eta\}.

On general manifolds we prove this only for sufficiently small mesoscopic scales, whereas on the sphere this holds at
all
mesoscopic scales.

For example, in the case of unit energy band
\eta\approx 1
(e.g. the random spherical harmonics), the deviations of the volume-bias (
u\neq 0
) are of minimal order

-\log\pazocal{P}r(\pazocal{D}_{T,u}(x;r))\asymp(rT)^{d-1},

whereas for the positively-banded case
\eta\asymp T
, the deviations are of maximal order

-\log\pazocal{P}r(\pazocal{D}_{T,u}(x;r)>\varepsilon)\asymp(rT)^{d}.

In the intermediate monochromatic case
\eta\rightarrow\infty
but
\eta=o(T)
, the deviations exhibit crossover for radii exceeding
1/\eta
.

1.4.1.
Almost sure sign-balance

The proof of Theorem
1.5
(i.) yields a rate of convergence for
\mathscr{B}_{T}\left(\mu\cdot r_{T}\right)
in (
1.28
) that is at least polynomial, of degree that can be made arbitrarily large by taking
\mu
sufficiently large. Therefore a straightforward application of the Borel-Cantelli lemma allows for upgrading the convergence mode to almost sure convergence, regardless of how the
f_{T}
are drawn for different
T
(i.e. independently or not):

Corollary 1.9

(Almost sure sign-balance)
.

Recall that for
u\in\mathbb{R}
, the level-imbalance
\mathscr{B}_{T,u}(r)
of the random waves is defined in (
1.26
). Assume that either (a)
\eta(T)\rightarrow\infty
or (b)
\pazocal{M}=\pazocal{S}^{d}
, and let
\bar{r}_{T}
be given by (
1.27
). Then if
\mu>0
is sufficiently large, as
T\to\infty
, one has

(1.33)

\sup\limits_{r\geq\mu\cdot\bar{r}_{T}}\mathscr{B}_{T,u}(r)\to 0\qquad\text{almost surely.}

In fact, by taking a sufficiently tight net
\{u_{j}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}
, and using the union bound, one could even take a supremum w.r.t. the level, i.e. the supremum on the l.h.s. of (
1.33
) could be replaced by

\sup\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\geq\mu\cdot\bar{r}_{T},\,u\in\mathbb{R}\end{subarray}}\mathscr{B}_{T,u}(r)
. The proof of this latter claim is left to the reader.

2.
Discussion

2.1.
Background and significance of the results

2.1.1.
Limit theory for the defect and volume-bias

The study of the defect and volume-bias of random Laplace eigenfunctions has previously focused mainly on their limit theory; see
[
15
,
16
,
17
]
for random spherical harmonics on
\pazocal{S}^{2}
,
[
14
]
for generalisations to
d\geq 2
,
[
10
]
for random toral eigenfunctions, and
[
4
]
for Euclidean waves.
To illustrate what is known, the defect of the random spherical harmonics
H_{\ell}
in (
1.11
) (here
\pazocal{M}=\pazocal{S}^{d}
with
d\geq 2
) on geodesic balls satisfies the following
[
15
,
16
,
14
]
:
for every fixed
u\in\mathbb{R}
,
x\in\pazocal{M}
, and any sequence of radii
r=r_{T}
above Planck scale (that is, satisfying
r\cdot T\to\infty
as
T\to\infty
), it holds that, as
T\to\infty

(2.1)

\textrm{Var}(\pazocal{D}_{\ell,u}(x;r))\sim\begin{cases}\frac{c_{d,u}}{(r\ell)^{d-1}}&u\neq 0,\\
\frac{c^{\prime}_{d,u}}{(r\ell)^{d}}&u=0,\end{cases}

for positive constants
c_{d,u},c^{\prime}_{d,u}>0
, and moreover

\frac{\pazocal{D}_{\ell,u}(x;r)}{\sqrt{\textrm{Var}(\pazocal{D}_{\ell,u}(x;r))}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\Longrightarrow}}\mathscr{N}(0,1).

One interesting feature of (
2.1
) is the phenomenon of
variance reduction
at the nodal level (
u=0
), which can be attributed to the (anti-)symmetry of the sign function, and is analogous to the
Berry cancellation
known to occur for other geometric functionals such as length of the nodal set
[
2
,
26
]
. From (
2.1
) one may infer that, with almost full probability, the volume-bias
\pazocal{D}_{\ell,u}(x;r)
is small around
almost
all points
x\in\pazocal{M}
at
any
radius
r
above Planck scale. More precisely, one can deduce that, for every
\epsilon>0
,

\lim\limits_{R\rightarrow\infty}\limsup\limits_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}\pazocal{P}r(|\{x\in\pazocal{S}^{2}:\>|\pazocal{D}_{\ell,u}(x;R\cdot\ell^{-1})|>\epsilon\}|>\varepsilon)=0.

However one cannot deduce from (
2.1
) that
\pazocal{D}_{\ell,u}(x;r)
is
uniformly
small over all
x\in\pazocal{M}
with high probability, as required by our definition of sign-balance. Indeed our results demonstrate that in order for the defect to be
uniformly
small, the radius
r=r_{\ell}
must be taken to be a logarithm power of the energy above Planck scale.

2.1.2.
Uniform
L^{2}
-mass equidistribution

In terms of
uniform
properties of random Laplace eigenfunctions comparable to our results, the only statistic that has been addressed so far in the literature is the
L^{2}
-mass equidistribution, motivated by the quantum ergodicity (QE) premise. Berry
[
3
]
suggested that mass equidistribution should hold for deterministic Laplace eigenfunctions on small scales, i.e. every geodesic ball
B_{r}(x)\subseteq\pazocal{M}
and sequence of radii
r=r_{\lambda_{j}}
satisfying
r\lambda_{j}\rightarrow\infty
should satisfy

\int\limits_{B_{r}(x)}\varphi_{j}(y)^{2}dy\rightarrow\frac{|B_{r}(x)|}{|\pazocal{M}|}

uniformly w.r.t.
x\in\pazocal{M}
. For
random
Laplace eigenfunctions, uniform mass equidistribution has been proven to hold with high probability on scales a logarithm power above Planck scale (see
[
5
]
for random spherical harmonics,
[
6
,
9
]
for generalisations to manifolds), which is analogous to our results.

Compared to
[
5
,
6
,
9
]
, the results presented in this manuscript are more general in terms of the permitted energy width
\eta
on general manifolds, and they also establish lower bounds on the radius at which the balance occurs. We believe that an adaptation of our techniques could refine the said results, likely yielding an optimal lower bound, in line with our result for the volume-balance at non-zero levels in Theorem
1.5
. We emphasise that the defect and level-balance have some extra challenging aspects compared to the
L^{2}
-mass distribution, not least since these functionals fail to be Lipschitz (in any appropriate function space) due to the discontinuity of the sign function at the origin.

2.1.3.
Deterministic sign-balance

Our results lead to a natural conjecture on the sign-balance of
deterministic
Laplace eigenfunctions
\varphi_{j}
at least in the context of a generic scenario (cf. the results in
[
20
,
13
]
explained in §
1.1
above):

Conjecture 2.1
.

Let
\pazocal{M}
be a chaotic smooth compact
d
-manifold, and
\{(\varphi_{j},\lambda_{j})\}_{j\geq 1}
the corresponding sequence of Laplace eigenfunctions and eigenvalues. Then, along a density-
1
subsequence, the
\varphi_{j}
are sign-balanced
above the scale

\frac{(\log{T})^{\frac{1}{d-1}+o(1)}}{T}.

Interestingly, it has been shown that ‘flat’ toral eigenfunctions satisfy the strongest notion of sign-balance at optimal scales. These are eigenfunctions on the standard flat
d
-torus
\pazocal{M}=\mathbb{R}^{d}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
whose Fourier coefficients are bounded, or at most grow slowly, a setting which benefits from certain
number theoretic
methods. Lester-Rudnick
[
11
]
showed that the deterministic toral eigenfunctions (in
2d
) satisfy the small-scale QE ansatz, all the way down to the Planck scale (up to a sub-polynomial factor), subsequently refined in
[
8
]
. In
[
10
]
it was proven that, for a density-
1
sequence of energies
\lambda_{j}\in E
, deterministic ‘flat’ eigenfunctions have small defect around
almost
all points at
any
radius above Planck scale, in the sense that, for every
\varepsilon>0
,

\lim\limits_{R\rightarrow\infty}\limsup\limits_{\lambda_{j}\in E,j\rightarrow\infty}|\{x\in\pazocal{S}^{2}:\>|\pazocal{D}_{\varphi_{j}}(x;R\cdot T^{-1})|>\epsilon\}|=0.

Sartori
[
21
]
showed how to modify the argument of
[
10
]
to upgrade this to
all
points, i.e. showing that flat eigenfunctions are sign-balanced above the Planck scale. Since random toral eigenfunctions (‘arithmetic random waves’) are ‘flat’ with almost full probability, this implies that, for ‘generic’ sequences of energies, one can establish the analogue of Theorem
1.3
for the arithmetic random waves without the extra logarithmic factor (i.e. with
\bar{r}_{T}
replaced by
R/\lambda_{j}
with
R\rightarrow\infty
).

2.2.
Further directions

We discuss directions for further research motivated by our results:

(a)

Subject 1: The phase transition for uniformity:

What is the true scale at which sign-balance occurs for random Laplace eigenfunctions? In other words, does there exist a scale
r_{T}
, so that, in the context of Theorem
1.5
at level
u=0
,

•

If
\mu
is sufficiently large,
\mathscr{B}_{T}\left(\mu\cdot r_{T}\right)\to 0
in probability,

•

If
\mu
is sufficiently small,
\mathscr{B}_{T}\left(\mu\cdot r_{T}\right)
is bounded away from zero in probability?

Our results show that such a scale
r_{T}
must satisfy
\underaccent{\bar}{r}_{T}\leq r_{T}\leq\bar{r}_{T}

with
\underaccent{\bar}{r}_{T},\bar{r}_{T}
given by (
1.27
). We believe that
r_{T}=o(\bar{r}_{T})
is plausible, and also that a stronger concentration than proven in Theorem
1.6
holds for the defect compared to non-zero levels, by analogy with the variance reduction that is known to occur for the sign defect (
2.1
).

Provided that the scale
r_{T}
is found, is there a
precise
phase transition at this scale, i.e. does there is a second scale
\omega_{T}=o(r_{T})
so that the above conclusions hold for the scale
\mu_{0}\cdot r_{T}+\gamma\cdot\omega_{T}
, with
\mu_{0}>0
a fixed constant, and
\gamma
sufficiently large or sufficiently small respectively?

(b)

Subject 2: Bridging the lacunas:
There are various technical conditions in our results which may not be optimal, most notably the statement of Theorem
1.5
does not apply in case
\eta
stays bounded, and Theorem
1.7
imposes a stronger condition on
r
than merely
r=o(1)
. It would be desirable to lift these, at least under some dynamical conditions on the manifold.

(c)

Subject 3: Concentration lower bounds for a larger deviation:
The concentration lower bound in Theorem
1.8
assumes that the deviation
\varepsilon>0
is taken sufficiently small. Does there exist a number
\varepsilon_{0}<1
such that the bounds in Theorem
1.7
no longer hold with
\varepsilon>\varepsilon_{0}
? If so, what is the correct order of deviations for such
\varepsilon
?

(d)

Subject 4: Generalised notion of balance:
Our techniques are robust, and applicable to a wider class of functionals of random waves (or even general Gaussian random fields). One may consider a generalised notion of balance, given by some function
G:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
(smooth or not) in place of the sign function as a building block of the defect (
1.5
), and in turn, the corresponding notion of (im)balance (
1.7
). As a concrete example, one may take
G(t)=t^{2}
, associated to the
L^{2}
-mass equidistribution. Broadening slightly to functions
G:\mathbb{R}^{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
k\geq 1
, one could also consider for instance the nodal volume of
f
restricted to
B_{r}(x)
(which can be expressed as an integral over a functional
G
of
(f,\nabla f)
). For a general class of
G
, what is the critical scale above which the relevant ensembles of random fields are balanced, and below which they are not balanced? What properties of
G
does this scale depend on?

2.3.
On the proofs

The proof of Theorem
1.5
(including Theorem
1.3
as a special case) divides cleanly into an upper bound (Theorem
1.5
(i.)) and a lower bound (Theorem
1.5
(ii.)), which proceed via disjoint routes.
For the
upper bound
we apply the upper concentration estimate (Theorem
1.6
) to a well-chosen dense net of geodesic balls. We complete the proof by combining the union bound with a certain stability property of the volume-bias.

The proof of Theorem
1.6
appeals to Lévy’s concentration of measure principle and the Gaussian isoperimetric inequality. It is inspired by the proof of the exponential concentration of the nodal domain count for random spherical harmonics
[
19
]
, but the present setting and the analysis are very different.

Let us point out a curious aspect of our use of the union bound in deducing Theorem
1.5
(i.) from Theorem
1.6
. One may be tempted to think that once the sign-balance of Theorem
1.6
(i.) has been established at some scale
\mu\cdot\bar{r}_{T}
, the same holds at all scales
r>\mu\cdot\bar{r}_{T}
, at least for
\mu
sufficiently large. A natural way to argue would be to employ the integral-geometric sandwich approach of Nazarov-Sodin
[
24
]
. However, such an argument would require an increase in the admissible scale, i.e. would only work for
r
so that
\frac{r}{\bar{r}_{T}}\rightarrow\infty
. Interestingly, it turns out that there is an unexpected (at least, to the authors) obstruction to this line of argument: for every
r>1
there exists a sequence of smooth functions on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
which are sign-balanced at radius
1
but
not
sign-balanced at radius
r
.

Proposition 2.2
.

For every
r>1
there exists a sequence of smooth functions
f_{j}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with the following properties as
j\to\infty
:

i.

Uniformly over
x
in compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

(2.2)

\frac{1}{\pi}\int\limits_{B_{1}(x)}H(f_{j}(y))dy\rightarrow 0.

ii.

There exists a number
z=z(r)>0
so that

(2.3)

\frac{1}{\pi r^{2}}\int\limits_{B_{r}(0)}H(f_{j}(y))dy\rightarrow z.

For completeness we give a proof of Proposition
2.2
in Appendix
A
(easy to generalise to arbitrary dimensions). While the proof constructs a sequence that satisfies the statement for
almost
every radius
r>1
(i.e. satisfies (
2.3
) with
r\in(1,\infty)\setminus S
, where
S
is a set of isolated points), it is conceivable that one could construct a single sequence that works for
every

r>1
, although we do not address this.

For the
lower bound
we apply the lower concentration estimates (theorems
1.7
and
1.8
) to a well-separated collection of geodesic balls, and combine with a certain ‘sprinkled decoupling’ technique that allows to establish the approximate independence of the defect (or volume-bias) on these balls.
The proof of theorems
1.7
and
1.8
proceed by constructing an exceptional event of sufficiently large probability. The construction in Theorem
1.8
is by far the more demanding, since it requires the existence of a ‘sign-barrier’: a sequence of functions in the reproducing kernel Hilbert space of the field that are
not
sign-balanced at the required scales. In fact, even constructing a
single
(deterministic) function that is not sign-balanced at scales above Planck’s scale is a major challenge, since the natural candidate (the reproducing kernel
K_{T}(\cdot,x)
) turns out to be
perfectly
sign-balanced at the theoretical minimum scale, decisively failing to serve as a sign-barrier.

Instead we construct a sign-barrier involving three superimposed copies of the reproducing kernel, associated to a certain modification of the field
f_{T}
with restricted energy levels, centred at three distant points; this is inspired by a similar Euclidean construction in
[
10
]
. This construction is by far the most subtle and difficult argument in the proof. The construction in Theorem
1.7
is relatively simple, involving only a single copy of the reproducing kernel
K_{T}(\cdot,x)
. This turns out to be sufficient, since the proof of Theorem
1.7
only requires the volume-imbalance of the reproducing kernel at some positive level
u>0
.

While the notion of sign-balance is most interesting for random waves, our results and methods actually apply to more general Gaussian ensembles than we consider in Theorem
1.5
. In fact, for many ensembles, namely those that have a non-trivial contribution from low eigenmodes, we could actually prove a stronger lower bound compared to Theorem
1.5
that replaces the lower scale
\underaccent{\bar}{r}_{T}
with the upper scale
\bar{r}_{T}
even
at
the nodal level
u=0
, matching our results for non-zero levels
u\neq 0
. Examples of such ensembles are the
fully-banded
random waves (i.e. with
\eta=T
), or the Kostlan ensemble of random polynomials on the sphere. For these ensembles one can easily construct an optimal sign-barrier using the low eigenmodes of the field.

3.
Asymptotics for the covariance kernel of random waves

In this section we study the asymptotics of the covariance kernel

(3.1)

K_{T}(x,y)=K_{T,\eta}(x,y)=\mathbb{E}\left[f_{T,\eta}(x)\cdot f_{T,\eta}(y)\right]=\frac{\left|\pazocal{M}\right|}{N(T,\eta)}\sum\limits_{\lambda_{j}\in[T-\eta,T]}\varphi_{j}(x)\cdot\varphi_{j}(y)

of the ensemble
\{f_{T,\eta}\}
of band-limited functions (
1.17
), central to our analysis. The kernel
K_{T}
coincides with the
spectral projector
in
L^{2}(\pazocal{M})
onto the space spanned by
\{\varphi_{j}\}_{\lambda_{j}\in[T-\eta,T]}
, and its asymptotic behaviour in various regimes has been extensively studied
in the microlocal analysis literature.

3.1.
Statement of the asymptotics

The results given in this section prescribe the leading asymptotics for
K_{T}(x,y)
in some regimes, both on and off the diagonal. We first discuss the general case of smooth manifolds, before giving some finer results for the round sphere
\pazocal{M}=\pazocal{S}^{d}
.

3.1.1.
General manifolds

Proposition 3.1
.

There exists a number

i.

Diagonal estimate:

For every

,

,

where the implicit constant in the ‘

ii.

Off-diagonal estimate:

Define

\gamma_{d}:=\frac{1}{4}(1-d)\pi\qquad\text{and}\qquad c_{d}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\cdot\frac{(2\pi)^{d/2}}{V_{d}},

K_{T,\eta}(x,y)=c_{d}\cdot(rT)^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}\Big(\cos\left(r\cdot T+\gamma_{d}\right)+O\Big(\eta r+\frac{1}{rT}+\frac{(rT)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}}{\eta}\Big)\Big),

where the implicit constant in the ‘

\pazocal{M}

Proposition

asserts that, in suitable regimes, the properly rescaled random waves are well approximated by the
d

[

Observe that the asymptotic formula (

for generic manifolds. However note that Theorem

does

to be monochromatic; this is since we shall apply Proposition

only after restricting the energies of

to lie in a monochromatic band.

3.1.2.
Round sphere

We next present stronger results for the round sphere
\pazocal{M}=\pazocal{S}^{d}
. In this case,

depends only on the spherical distance

In what follows it will be convenient to slightly abuse our notation. We assume that

In particular, the identity

One may express

(consistent to the new conventions)

Proposition 3.2

For every
\ell\geq 1
and
\eta\in[1,\ell]
one has

(3.11)

Further, for every
\ell\geq 1

A comparison between the respective error terms in (

Though we restrict

3.1.3.
Uniform off-diagonal decay

We shall also need the following rougher estimate, providing uniform power-law decay of correlations:

Corollary 3.3
.

Assume that either (a) there exists a number

In fact, it is possible to establish a stronger estimate of the form

is the number of energy levels (
3.5
) lying in
(\ell-\eta,\ell]

(under the same abuse of notation of §
3.1
). We invoke Lemma
3.4
(ii.) to write

(3.25)

\begin{split}&N(\ell,\eta)\cdot K_{\ell,\eta}(\theta)=c_{d}\kappa(\theta)\left(\ell^{d}\mu(\ell)J_{d/2}(M\theta)-(\ell-\eta)^{d}\mu(\ell-\eta)J_{d/2}((M-\eta)\theta)\right)+O\Big(\frac{\ell^{(d-3)/2}}{\theta^{(d-1)/2}}\Big),\end{split}

with

c_{d}=\frac{|\pazocal{S}^{d}|}{2^{d}\pi^{d/2}},\;\;\;M:=\ell+\frac{d}{2}.

Abbreviating
\delta=\frac{\eta}{M}
, we can rewrite (
3.25
) as

(3.26)

N(\ell,\eta)\cdot K_{\ell,\eta}(\theta)=c_{d}\kappa(\theta)\ell^{d}\cdot\left(\mu(\ell)J_{d/2}(M\theta)-(1-\delta)^{d}\mu(\ell-\eta)J_{d/2}(M(1-\delta)\theta)\right)+O\Big(\frac{\ell^{(d-3)/2}}{\theta^{(d-1)/2}}\Big),

and, further

(3.27)

\begin{split}&\mu(\ell)J_{d/2}(M\theta)-(1-\delta)^{d}\mu(\ell-\eta)J_{d/2}(M(1-\delta)\theta)\\
&\qquad=\mu(\ell)\cdot\left(J_{d/2}(M\theta)-(1-\delta)^{d/2}\cdot J_{d/2}(M(1-\delta)\theta)\right)\\
&\qquad\qquad+\left(\mu(\ell)(1-\delta)^{d/2}-(1-\delta)^{d}\mu(\ell-\eta)\right)\cdot J_{d/2}(M(1-\delta)\theta).\end{split}

Next, we approximate the former of the two terms on the r.h.s. of (
3.27
), and bound above the latter of these terms. Indeed, we denote
\alpha:=\frac{d-2}{2}
, and use Lemma
3.5
(iii.),
and, further, Lemma
3.5
(i.) with (
3.16
) for approximating
\mu(\ell)\approx\ell^{-d/2}
, to yield

(3.28)

\begin{split}&\mu(\ell)\cdot\left(J_{d/2}(M\theta)-(1-\delta)^{d/2}\cdot J_{d/2}(M(1-\delta)\theta)\right)=\mu(\ell)\cdot\Big(\eta\theta\cdot J_{\alpha}(M\theta)+O\Big(\frac{\eta^{2}\theta^{3/2}}{\ell^{1/2}}\Big)\Big)\\
&=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\cdot\frac{\eta\theta^{1/2}}{\ell^{(d+1)/2}}\Big(\cos\left(M\theta+\gamma_{d}\right)+O\Big(\frac{1}{\ell\theta}+\eta\theta\Big)\Big),\end{split}

for the former term of (
3.27
) (bearing in mind the obvious inequality
\frac{1}{\ell}\ll\frac{1}{\ell\theta}
).

For the latter term of (
3.27
), we write

(3.29)

\begin{split}&\left(\mu(\ell)(1-\delta)^{d/2}-(1-\delta)^{d}\mu(\ell-\eta)\right)\cdot J_{d/2}(M(1-\delta)\theta)\\
&\qquad=(1-\delta)^{d/2}J_{d/2}(M(1-\delta)\theta)\cdot\Big(\mu(\ell)-(1-\delta)^{d/2}\mu\Big(M\Big(\frac{\ell}{M}-\delta\Big)\Big)\Big),\end{split}

and observe that, by interpreting the expression
\frac{(\ell+d-1)!}{\ell!}
in (
3.16
) as a
(d-1)
-degree polynomial, the function
\mu(\cdot)
naturally extends to an elementary function of real variable.
Taylor expanding
the function

\delta\mapsto\mu\Big(M\Big(\frac{\ell}{M}-\delta\Big)\Big)

around
\delta=0
we obtain that

(3.30)

\Big||\mu(\ell)-(1-\delta)^{d/2}\mu\Big(M\Big(\frac{\ell}{M}-\delta\Big)\Big)\Big|\ll\frac{\delta M}{\ell^{d/2+1}}=\frac{\eta}{\ell^{d/2+1}},

valid for
\delta<\frac{1}{2}
(say), and trivial for
\delta>\frac{1}{2}
. We substitute (
3.30
) into (
3.29
), and use the decay at infinity of the Bessel function of Lemma
3.5
(i.)
to obtain the bound

(3.31)

\begin{split}&\left|\left(\mu(\ell)(1-\delta)^{d/2}-(1-\delta)^{d}\mu(\ell-\eta)\right)\cdot J_{d/2}(M(1-\delta)\theta)\right|\\
&\qquad\ll\frac{(1-\delta)^{d/2}}{(\ell(1-\delta)\theta)^{1/2}}\cdot\frac{\eta}{\ell^{d/2+1}}\ll\frac{\eta}{\ell^{(d+3)/2}\theta^{1/2}}\end{split}

for the second term of (
3.27
).

We consolidate the estimate (
3.28
) and the bound (
3.31
) (that, for
\theta>\frac{1}{\ell}
is majorised by the error term in (
3.28
)), and substitute into (
3.27
) to yield the estimate

(3.32)

\begin{split}&\mu(\ell)J_{d/2}(M\theta)-(1-\delta)^{d}\mu(\ell-\eta)J_{d/2}(M(1-\delta)\theta)\\
&\qquad\ll\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\cdot\frac{\eta\theta^{1/2}}{\ell^{(d+1)/2}}\Big(\cos\left(M\theta+\gamma_{d}\right)+O\Big(\frac{1}{\ell\theta}+\eta\theta\Big)\Big).\end{split}

It then remains to insert the estimate (
3.32
) into (
3.26
), and multiply it by
\frac{c_{d}\kappa(\theta)\ell^{d}}{N(\ell,\eta)},
with
\kappa(\theta)
as in (
3.15
),
and divide the error term in (
3.26
) by
N(\ell,\eta)
.
To this end, we mind that, using the explicit spectral multiplicities (
1.12
) of the sphere, we find the asymptotic expression

N(\ell,\eta)=\frac{2}{d!}\left(\ell^{d}-(\ell-\eta)^{d}\right)+O\left(\eta^{2}\ell^{d-2}\right)=\frac{2}{(d-1)!}\eta\ell^{d-1}\left(1+O\left(\frac{\eta}{\ell}\right)\right),

while
N(\ell,\eta)\asymp\eta\ell^{d-1}
, cf. (
3.24
). Hence its reciprocal is

\frac{1}{N(\ell,\eta)}=\frac{(d-1)!}{2\eta\ell^{d-1}}\left(1+O\left(\frac{\eta}{\ell}\right)\right).

Consolidating these estimates, we finally obtain:

\begin{split}&K_{\ell,\eta}(\theta)=c_{d}2^{d/2-1}(d-1)!\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\cdot\frac{1}{(\theta\ell)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}}\Big(\cos\left(M\theta+\gamma_{d}\right)+O\Big(\frac{1}{\ell\theta}+\eta\theta+\frac{\eta}{\ell}\Big)\Big)\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad+O\Big(\frac{1}{\eta\ell^{\frac{d+1}{2}}\theta^{\frac{d-1}{2}}}\Big)\\
&=c_{d}2^{d/2-1}(d-1)!\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\cdot\frac{1}{(\theta\ell)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}}\cdot\Big(\cos\left(M\theta+\gamma_{d}\right)+O\Big(\frac{1}{\ell\theta}+\eta\theta+\frac{\eta}{\ell}+\frac{1}{\ell\eta}\Big)\Big).\end{split}

Note that
\frac{1}{\ell\eta}\ll\frac{\eta}{\ell}
, and for
\theta\geq\frac{1}{\ell}
,
\frac{\eta}{\ell}\leq\eta\theta
, whereas for
\theta\leq\frac{1}{\ell}
,
\frac{1}{\ell\theta}\geq 1
, so that the estimate (
3.12
) is a tautology, due to the uniform boundedness of
K_{\ell,\eta}
, by Proposition
3.2
(i.) and Cauchy-Schwarz.
∎

Proof of Corollary
3.3
.

First, we assume case (a), i.e.
\pazocal{M}
is an arbitrary smooth manifold,
\eta(T)>T^{\delta_{0}}
. We apply Lemma
3.4
(i.), together with (
3.13
) and (

),
and using the standard decay

(3.33)

|J_{d/2}(t)|\ll\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}

of the Bessel function at infinity, to obtain

(3.34)

c_{d}^{\prime}K_{T,\eta}(x,y)=\frac{\frac{J_{d/2}(rT)}{(rT)^{d/2}}\cdot T^{d}-\frac{J_{d/2}(r(T-\eta))}{(r(T-\eta))^{d/2}}\cdot(T-\eta)^{d}}{\eta\cdot T^{d-1}}+O\Big(\frac{1}{\eta}\Big),

with

\frac{g(T)-g(T-\eta)}{\eta\cdot T^{d-1}}=\frac{\eta\cdot r^{1-\frac{d}{2}}S^{d/2}J_{(d-2)/2}(rS)}{\eta T^{d-1}}=\frac{r^{1-\frac{d}{2}}S^{d/2}J_{(d-2)/2}(rS)}{T^{d-1}},

\begin{split}\frac{|g(T)-g(T-\eta)|}{\eta\cdot T^{d-1}}&=O\Big(\frac{r^{1-\frac{d}{2}}S^{d/2}}{(rS)^{1/2}T^{d-1}}\Big)=O\Big(\frac{r^{1-\frac{d}{2}}S^{d/2}}{(rS)^{1/2}T^{d-1}}\Big)=O\Big(\frac{S^{\frac{d-1}{2}}}{r^{\frac{d-1}{2}}T^{d-1}}\Big)\\

4.
Upper bound for defect concentration: Proof of Theorem
1.6

4.1.
General upper bound for defect concentration

In this section we establish an upper bound for the defect concentration that is applicable to general random fields. It will be applied to the random waves in §
4.2
below. Ahead of stating the general result, we explain the abstract setting.

In this section we assume that
\pazocal{X}\subseteq\pazocal{M}
is a compact subdomain of
\pazocal{M}
of volume
|\pazocal{X}|
,
and
(f(x))_{x\in\pazocal{X}}
is a Gaussian random field on
\pazocal{X}
, a.s. continuous but not necessarily centred. Let
\pazocal{H}
be the reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS) associated to
f(\cdot)
, and
i:\pazocal{H}\rightarrow L^{2}(\pazocal{X})
the canonical inclusion map. We denote the usual operator norm
\|i\|_{\pazocal{H}\rightarrow L^{2}}
of
i
:

(4.1)

I:=\|i\|_{\pazocal{H}\rightarrow L^{2}}:=\sup\left\{\|h\|_{L^{2}(\pazocal{X})}:\>h\in\pazocal{H},\,\|h\|_{\pazocal{H}}=1\right\}.

Recall that
H(\cdot)
is the sign function (
1.6
), and define the volume-bias of
f(\cdot)
on
\pazocal{X}
:

(4.2)

\pazocal{F}=\pazocal{F}(f):=\frac{1}{|\pazocal{X}|}\int\limits_{\pazocal{X}}H(f(x))dx.

We are now ready to state the abstract upper bound for the defect concentration:

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\pazocal{X}
,
f(\cdot)
,
\pazocal{F}
, and
I
be as above, and further assume that
f(\cdot)
satisfies

\operatorname{Var}(f(x))\geq 1

for every
x\in\pazocal{X}
.
Then, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a number
\delta>0
, only depending on
\varepsilon
, so that

(4.3)

\pazocal{P}r(\pazocal{F}>\mathbb{E}[\pazocal{F}]+\varepsilon)<3\cdot e^{-\delta|\pazocal{X}|/I^{2}}.

Though in what follows we will only require the
existence
of a number
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)
with properties as in Proposition
4.1
, the proof of Proposition
4.1
yields
the quantitative dependency
\delta=c_{0}\cdot\varepsilon^{3}
, with some absolute
c_{0}>0
. By a suitable approximation, it would also be possible to prove the analogue of Proposition
4.1
with an arbitrary bounded measurable functional
\Xi(\cdot)
in place of the sign function
H(\cdot)
in the definition (
4.2
) of
\pazocal{F}
.

Proof.

First, we assume that

(4.4)

\varepsilon\geq c_{0}\cdot\frac{I^{2/3}}{|\pazocal{X}|^{1/3}}

with an arbitrary fixed number
c_{0}>0
. Indeed, if, otherwise,
\varepsilon<c_{0}\cdot\frac{I^{2/3}}{|\pazocal{X}|^{1/3}}
, then the inequality
(
4.3
) is a tautology with

\delta=\frac{\varepsilon^{3}}{c_{0}^{3}}
, since, in this case, the r.h.s. of (
4.3
) is
>3\cdot e^{-1}>1
.

Now we recall the Gaussian isoperimetric inequality (see e.g.
[
12
, Chapter 1]
). Let
(\pazocal{H},\mathscr{F},\pazocal{P}r)
be the probability space associated to
f(\cdot)
, and
\Phi
be the standard Gaussian cdf. Then, for every event
A\in\mathscr{F}
and
s>0
, one has

\pazocal{P}r(A^{+s})\geq\Phi\left(\Phi^{-1}(\pazocal{P}r(A))+s\right),

where
A^{+s}
is the
s
-neighbourhood in
\pazocal{H}
-norm (and
\pazocal{P}r(A):=\pazocal{P}r(f\in A)
). In particular, if for some
s>0
one has
\pazocal{P}r(A^{+s})\leq\frac{1}{2}
, then we may infer that

(4.5)

\pazocal{P}r(A)\leq\Phi(-s)\leq e^{-s^{2}/2}.

Similarly, one may show the ‘converse’ statement: if
\pazocal{P}r(A)\geq\frac{1}{4}
, then

(4.6)

\pazocal{P}r\left(A^{+s}\right)\geq 1-e^{-s^{2}/4}.

Let
B
be the ‘unstable’ event

B:=\left\{\operatorname{Vol}\left(\left\{x\in\pazocal{X}:\>|f(x)|<\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\right\}\right)>2\varepsilon\cdot|\pazocal{X}|\right\}.

We aim to bound the probability of
B
from above, by applying the inequality (
4.5
). We observe that, by the definition (
4.1
)
of
I
as the operator norm of
i
, one has for every
h\in\pazocal{H}
,

\|h\|_{L^{2}(\pazocal{X})}^{2}\leq I^{2}\cdot\|h\|^{2}_{\pazocal{H}},

and therefore,

(4.7)

\operatorname{Vol}\left(\left\{x\in\pazocal{X}:\>|h(x)|>\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\right\}\right)\leq\frac{\|h\|_{L^{2}(\pazocal{X})}^{2}}{(\varepsilon/4)^{2}}\leq\frac{I^{2}\cdot\|h\|^{2}_{\pazocal{H}}}{(\varepsilon/4)^{2}},

by Markov’s inequality. Therefore, if, for some
f\in B
and
h\in\pazocal{H}
one has
\|h\|_{\pazocal{H}}<s
with
s
given by

(4.8)

\frac{I^{2}\cdot s^{2}}{(\varepsilon/4)^{2}}=\varepsilon\cdot|\pazocal{X}|,

then it follows that

(4.9)

f+h\in C:=\left\{\operatorname{Vol}\left(\left\{x\in\pazocal{X}:\>|f(x)|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right\}\right)>\varepsilon\cdot|\pazocal{X}|\right\}.

To put it differently, with the choice of
s
as in (
4.8
), one has

(4.10)

B^{+s}\subseteq C.

However, since, by the assumptions of Proposition
4.1
, for all
x\in\pazocal{X}
we have
\operatorname{Var}\left(f(x)\right)\geq 1
, the expectation of the volume of the set within the definition of

C
satisfies

\mathbb{E}\left[\operatorname{Vol}\left(\left\{x\in\pazocal{X}:\>|f(x)|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right\}\right)\right]\leq\sup\limits_{x\in\pazocal{X}}\pazocal{P}r\left(|f(x)|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)\cdot|\pazocal{X}|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\cdot|\pazocal{X}|<\frac{\varepsilon\cdot|\pazocal{X}|}{2},

since the pdf of a centred Gaussian attains its maximum at the origin. Hence, by (
4.10
) and Markov’s inequality, we may bound the probability of
B^{+s}
(for the particular choice (
4.8
) for
s
) by

(4.11)

\pazocal{P}r(B^{+s})\leq\pazocal{P}r(C)\leq\frac{1}{2}.

Invoking (
4.5
) with (
4.11
) yields

(4.12)

\pazocal{P}r(B)\leq e^{-s^{2}/2}=e^{-\frac{1}{32}\varepsilon^{3}|\pazocal{X}|/I^{2}}<e^{-\frac{1}{64}\varepsilon^{3}|\pazocal{X}|/I^{2}},

on substituting the value of
s
as in (
4.8
).

Now let
\operatorname{Med}(\pazocal{F})
be the defect median, and define the event

D:=\{\pazocal{F}\leq\operatorname{Med}(\pazocal{F})\}\setminus B.

Recall the assumption (
4.4
) (where we have the freedom to choose the constant
c_{0}>0
),
so that (
4.12
) reads

\pazocal{P}r(B)\leq\frac{1}{4},
provided that
c_{0}
is sufficiently large. Therefore,

\pazocal{P}r(D)\geq\pazocal{P}r\left(\{\pazocal{F}\leq\operatorname{Med}(\pazocal{F})\}\right)-\pazocal{P}r(B)\geq\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{4}=\frac{1}{4},

meaning that
the underlying assumptions of the inequality (
4.6
), which we aim to apply with
A:=D
, are satisfied. An application of (
4.6
) then yields the inequality

(4.13)

\pazocal{P}r(D^{+s})\geq 1-e^{-s^{2}/4}.

Let
s
be given by (
4.8
), and assume that
f\in D
, and for some
h\in\pazocal{H}
one has
\|h\|_{\pazocal{H}}<s
, then, on reusing (
4.7
) with the given
s
, the defect of
f+h
on
\pazocal{X}
is

\begin{split}\pazocal{F}({f+h})&=\frac{1}{|\pazocal{X}|}\int\limits_{\pazocal{X}}H(f(x)+h(x))dx\\
&\leq\pazocal{F}+\frac{\operatorname{Vol}\left(\left\{x\in\pazocal{X}:\>|f(x)|<\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\right\}\right)}{|\pazocal{X}|}+\frac{\operatorname{Vol}\left(\left\{x\in\pazocal{X}:\>|h(x)|>\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\right\}\right)}{|\pazocal{X}|}\\
&\leq\operatorname{Med}(\pazocal{F})+2\varepsilon+\varepsilon=\operatorname{Med}(\pazocal{F})+3\varepsilon.\end{split}

Hence, in this case,

f+h\notin E:=\{\pazocal{F}>\operatorname{Med}(\pazocal{F})+3\varepsilon\}.

That is,
D^{+s}\cap E=\varnothing
, i.e.
D^{+s}
does not intersect
E
.
Thus,

(4.14)

\pazocal{P}r\left(\{\pazocal{F}>\operatorname{Med}(\pazocal{F})+3\varepsilon\}\right)=\pazocal{P}r(E)\leq 1-\pazocal{P}r(D^{+s})\leq e^{-s^{2}/4}<e^{-\frac{1}{128}\varepsilon^{3}|\pazocal{X}|/I^{2}},

by (
4.13
) and (
4.8
) (cf. (
4.12
)). Since (
4.14
) holds true for all
\varepsilon>c_{0}\cdot\frac{I^{2/3}}{|\pazocal{X}|^{1/3}}
in accordance to (
4.4
),
choosing
c_{0}
sufficiently large and integrating w.r.t.
\varepsilon
shows that

(4.15)

\mathbb{E}[\pazocal{F}]\leq\operatorname{Med}(\pazocal{F})+4\varepsilon.

At last, we consolidate the estimates (
4.14
) and (
4.15
), to obtain

\pazocal{P}r(\pazocal{F}\geq 7\varepsilon)\leq\pazocal{P}r\left(\{\pazocal{F}>\operatorname{Med}(\pazocal{F})+3\varepsilon\}\right)<e^{-\frac{1}{128}\varepsilon^{3}|\pazocal{X}|/I^{2}},

and the statement of Proposition
4.1
finally follows upon replacing
\varepsilon
by
\frac{\varepsilon}{7}
.
∎

4.2.
Application of the abstract upper bound to random waves

Let
f_{T,\eta}:\pazocal{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be the random waves (
1.17
),
K_{T,\eta}:\pazocal{M}\times\pazocal{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
the reproducing (covariance) kernel (
3.1
)
of
f_{T,\eta}
and
\pazocal{H}=\pazocal{H}(T,\eta)
the corresponding reproducing kernel Hilbert space. One has that

\left\|f_{T,\eta}\right\|_{\pazocal{H}}=\|{\bf a}\|_{2},

where
{\bf a}\in\mathbb{R}^{N(T,\eta)}
is the vector
{\bf a}=(a_{j})_{\lambda_{j}\in[T-\eta,T]}
with the
a_{j}
as in (
1.17
),
N(T,\eta)
is the number (
1.18
) of energy levels in
[T-\eta,T]
, and

\|{\bf a}\|_{2}
is the Euclidean norm of
{\bf a}
.

Definition 4.2

(Restriction of
\pazocal{H}
and the operator norm)
.

Let
B\subseteq\pazocal{M}
be a closed subdomain of
\pazocal{M}
.

i.

Denote the restriction of
\pazocal{H}
to
B
, i.e. an element of
\pazocal{H}_{B}
is a restriction

h|_{B}
of some
h\in\pazocal{H}
, and, by definition,

\left\|h|_{B}\right\|_{\pazocal{H}_{B}}:=\|h\|_{\pazocal{H}}.

ii.

Denote
I_{B}=I_{B}(T,\eta)
to be the operator norm (
4.1
) of the natural inclusion
i_{B}:\pazocal{H}_{B}\rightarrow L^{2}(B)
.

Note the slight ambiguity of the norm
\|p\|_{\pazocal{H}_{B}}
for
p\in\pazocal{H}_{B}
, in case
p=h|_{B}=\widetilde{h}|_{B}
for some distinct elements
h,\widetilde{h}\in\pazocal{H}
of the ambient RKHS. However, it is easy to see that this problem cannot occur in our settings, as a (finite) linear combination of Laplace eigenfunctions on
\pazocal{M}
vanishing on some domain
B
forces all the coefficients to vanish, a by-product of the Aronszajn unique continuation principle, hence we will ignore this possibility.

We will need to determine the dependence of
I_{B}(T,\eta)
on the parameters. The following result is contained, in essence, in
[
23
,
9
]
, which we explicate for the purpose of giving it a form suited to the subsequent applications.

Proposition 4.3

(Cf.
[
9
, Lemma 4.2]
)
.

There exists a constant
c=c(\pazocal{M})
, depending only on
\pazocal{M}
, such that, for every
T\geq 1
,
\eta\in[1,T]
, and a geodesic ball
B=B_{r}(x)
, one has

(4.16)

I_{B}^{2}\leq\zeta(T,\eta;r):=\begin{cases}c\cdot\frac{r\cdot\eta}{N(T,\eta)}&\frac{1}{T}\leq r\leq\frac{1}{\eta},\\
\frac{|\pazocal{M}|}{N(T,\eta)}&\frac{1}{\eta}<r<\operatorname{Inj}(\pazocal{M}).\end{cases}

Proof.

The operator norm
I_{B}
is given explicitly by

(4.17)

I_{B}^{2}=\sup\limits_{\|{\bf a}\|_{2}=1}\left\|\sqrt{\frac{|\pazocal{M}|}{N(T,\eta)}}\sum\limits_{\lambda_{j}\in[T-\eta,T]}a_{j}\varphi_{j}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B)}.

On the other hand, a straightforward application of
[
9
, Lemma 4.2]
(appealing to
[
23
, Eq. (4.1)]
and a Cauchy-Schwarz argument) on

u=\sum\limits_{\lambda_{j}\in[T-\eta,T]}a_{j}\varphi_{j},

of norm

In this section we prove the lower bounds for the volume-bias concentration in theorems

5.1.
Existence of Euclidean sign-barrier

For

,
v_{2}:=\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}
, and

(5.2)

The negative sign of

and

We recall that, given a lattice

The following lemma, whose proof is omitted,

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\Lambda

generated by the two complex numbers

.

i.

The lattice
\Lambda

by equilateral triangles. It acts by translations on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and its action has a fundamental domains that is a regular hexagon of area
\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}
, that tessellates
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

ii.

The dual lattice to
\Lambda
is the hexagonal lattice
\Lambda^{*}=\langle 2,1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}i\rangle
, and one has
2\Lambda\subsetneq\Lambda^{*}
.

iii.

For every
t>0
, the function
w_{t}
is invariant w.r.t. the action of
\Lambda^{*}
on
\mathbb{C}
by translations.

iv.

The group action of
\Lambda^{*}
has a fundamental domain
\Pi
that is a regular hexagon of side length
\frac{2}{3}
and area
\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}
, illustrated in Figure

Figure 1

The nodal set of

Proposition 5.2

(Euclidean sign-barrier)
.

Let
d\geq 2
, and for
t>0

be given by (

\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{0}>0
,

recalling that

Towards the proof of Proposition

Lemma 5.3

Let

has no critical zeros on

Then

D^{\prime}(0)=\frac{2}{|\Pi|}\int\limits_{\phi_{0}^{-1}(0)\cap\Pi}\frac{\psi(x)}{\|\nabla\phi_{0}(x)\|}dx

and

(5.4)

\begin{split}D^{\prime\prime}(0)&=\frac{2}{|\Pi|}\int\limits_{\phi_{0}^{-1}(0)\cap\Pi}\bigg(-2\frac{\psi(x)\cdot\left\langle\nabla\phi_{0}(x),\nabla\psi(x)\right\rangle}{\|\nabla\phi_{0}(x)\|^{3}}dx+\frac{\psi(x)^{2}}{\|\nabla\phi_{0}(x)\|^{4}}\cdot\partial_{\nabla\phi_{0}(x)}\left[\left\|\nabla\phi_{0}(x)\right\|\right]\\

where

The proof of Lemma

Existence of sign-barrier on

Recall that

is the RKHS corresponding to

Proposition 5.4

(Sign-barrier)
.

There exists a constant
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{1}>0
, and constants

,

sufficiently large, only depending on
\pazocal{M}
, with the following property. Let

,

Towards the proof of Proposition
5.4
we will require the following

Lemma 5.5
.

i.

Let

be two numbers so that
r<R
, and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
so that

. Then, uniformly for

(5.9)

where

is the standard Euclidean distance in

.

ii.

Let

, and

\pazocal{M}
, so that

in the tangent space, one has

(5.10)

d(\xi,\exp_{x}(w))=\widetilde{r}-\langle w,v\rangle+O\Big(\frac{r^{2}}{\widetilde{r}}\Big).

Proof of Lemma
5.5
.

First we prove Lemma
5.5
(i.). By rotating

if necessary, we may assume that

, whence (

with

. It is easy to derive the estimate (

) via a straightforward application of Pythagoras’s theorem.

Now we turn to proving Lemma

(ii.). By Lemma

(i.) we have that

(5.12)

on

. We then claim that

(5.13)

\left|d_{2}(\widetilde{r}\cdot v,w)-d(\xi,\exp_{x}(w))\right|=O\Big(\frac{r^{2}}{\widetilde{r}}\Big),

which, together with (

Indeed, let us consider the function

Evidently, for

, and, in addtion, it is easy to check that,

Further, an explicit computation shows that the Hessian of

on

\pazocal{M}
), hence (

5.4
.

Let
C>0
be a sufficiently large constant to be chosen later,

be the three roots of unity of

Given

Lemma 6.1

Proof.

The definition (

) of volume-bias clearly implies that

\begin{split}&\left|\pazocal{D}_{f;u}(y;r^{\prime})-\pazocal{D}_{f;u}(x;r)\right|\leq\max\limits_{x\in\pazocal{M}}\frac{\max\limits_{y\in B_{\delta\cdot r}(x)}|B_{r}(x)\triangle B_{r}(y)|}{\min\limits_{y\in B_{\delta\cdot r}(x)}|B_{r}(y)|}+\max\limits_{x\in\pazocal{M}}\frac{|B_{r}(x)\triangle B_{(1+\delta)r}(x)|}{|B_{r}(x)|}\\
&\leq\max\limits_{x\in\pazocal{M}}\frac{\max\limits_{y\in B_{\delta\cdot r}(x)}|B_{r}(x)\triangle B_{r}(y)|}{\min\limits_{y\in B_{\delta\cdot r}(x)}|B_{r}(y)|}+\max\limits_{x\in\pazocal{M}}\frac{|B_{(1+\delta)r}(x)\setminus B_{r}(x)|}{|B_{r}(x)|}.\end{split}

We now claim that the two terms on the r.h.s. of (

Lemma A.1

Comparing the coefficients of
\frac{1}{k}
, this forces
r=\frac{1}{r}=1
, a contradiction.
∎

Now fix
r>1
, and let
k=k_{r}
be such that
r\cdot z_{k}
is not contained in
\{z_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
(possible by Lemma
A.3
). Abbreviate
s=s_{r}:=z_{k}
and define

(A.7)

\xi(\rho)=\xi_{r}(\rho):=(-1)^{\mathds{1}_{J_{1}(rs)<0}}J_{0}(s\cdot\rho).

Lemma A.4
.

For every
r>1
, the function
\xi=\xi_{r}
defined in (
A.7
) satisfies (
A.1
).

Proof.

For
t>0
, we write

\psi_{t}(x)=\int\limits_{B_{t}(x)}\xi(|y|)dy=\left(\xi\star\chi_{t}\right)(x),

where
\star
denotes convolution,
\chi_{t}
is the characteristic function of the ball
B_{t}(0)
, and we abuse notation by writing
\xi(\cdot)
for the radial function
\xi(\|y\|)
. Formally, the Fourier transform of
\psi_{t}
is

\widehat{\psi_{t}}(\zeta)=\widehat{\xi}(\zeta)\cdot(t\widehat{\chi_{1}}(t\zeta)),

where (cf. (
3.19
))

\widehat{\chi_{1}}(\zeta)=2\pi\cdot\frac{J_{1}(\|\zeta\|)}{\|\zeta\|},

and, recalling the definition (
A.7
) of
\xi
,
\widehat{\xi}
is formally
(-1)^{\mathds{1}_{J_{1}(rs)<0}}\times\frac{1}{s}
times the uniform measure on
\partial B_{s}(0)
. Since
s
is a zero of
J_{1}(\cdot)
, it follows that
\widehat{\psi}_{1}(\zeta)\equiv 0
, which is the first statement in (
A.1
). Similarly for the second statement in (
A.1
), we formally have by Plancherel

\int\limits_{B_{r}(0)}\xi(|y|)dy=\psi_{r}(0)=\left\langle\xi,\,\chi_{1}\left(\frac{\cdot}{r}\right)\right\rangle=\left\langle\widehat{\xi},\,r\widehat{\chi_{1}}\left(r\cdot\right)\right\rangle=2\pi\cdot\frac{|J_{1}(rs)|}{s^{2}}>0.

To rigorously justify these arguments one can mollify
\xi
and pass to the limit, but we omit the details.
∎

We can now conclude the proof of Proposition
2.2
:

Proof of Proposition
2.2
.

Let
\xi
be the function in Lemma
A.4
. Applying Lemmas
A.1
and
A.2
we obtain a sequence
\{f_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
of smooth functions whose positive nodal domains

satisfy (
A.3
), and which therefore also satisfy (
A.2
). Recalling (
A.1
), this completes the proof of the pointwise convergence in Proposition
2.2
. It remains to argue that the limit in (
2.2
) is uniform w.r.t.
x
in compact sets. Indeed, this follows directly from the
equicontinuity
of the function
x\mapsto\int_{B_{1}(x)}H(f_{j}(y))dy
since the convergence of equicontinuous functions on a compact set to a continuous limit is
uniform
.
∎

Appendix B
Defect derivatives w.r.t. deformation: Proof of Lemma
5.3

For simplicity, we assume that the nodal line
\phi_{0}^{-1}(0)
does not intersect the boundary
\partial\Pi
, as otherwise we may apply a density argument to reduce to this case. The function
\phi_{0}
naturally gives rise to the partition

(B.1)

\Pi=\pazocal{C}_{1}^{+}\cup\ldots\cup\pazocal{C}^{+}_{m}\cup\pazocal{C}^{-}_{1}\cup\ldots\cup\pazocal{C}^{-}_{k}

of
\Pi
into the
nodal domains
of
\phi_{0}
intersecting
\Pi
, where
\pazocal{C}^{+}_{j}
,
1\leq j\leq m
(resp.
\pazocal{C}^{-}_{j}
,
1\leq j\leq k
) are the
positive
(resp. negative) nodal domains. Then,

(B.2)

D(0)=\frac{1}{|\Pi|}\Big(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}|\pazocal{C}_{j}^{+}|-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}|\pazocal{C}_{j}^{-}|\Big)=\frac{2}{|\Pi|}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}|\pazocal{C}_{j}^{+}|-1.

Since, by assumption,
\phi_{0}
does not have critical zeros, by Morse theory the partition of
\Pi
(
B.1
) into nodal domains of
\phi_{t}
is locally constant for
|t|
sufficiently small. Moreover, for every
1\leq j\leq m
there is a smooth bijective map

p:\partial\pazocal{C}^{+}_{j}\rightarrow\partial\pazocal{C}^{+}_{j}

between the corresponding boundaries, so that

(B.3)

p(x)=x+r(x;t)\cdot\vec{N}(x),

where
\vec{N}(x)=\frac{\nabla\phi_{0}(x)}{\|\nabla\phi_{0}(x)\|}
is a
inward
unit normal vector to
\partial\pazocal{C}_{j}^{+}
at
x\in\partial\pazocal{C}_{j}^{+}
, in accordance to Lemma
5.3
(ii.)
(with the same, suitably adjusted, holding for the negative nodal domains),
and

r(x;t):\partial\pazocal{C}_{j}^{+}\times[-\delta_{0},\delta_{0}]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

is a smooth
2
-variable function so that
r(x;0)\equiv 0
, cf.
[
1
, Proof of Lemma 4.7]
.
With the help of the implicit equation

\phi_{t}(p(x))=\phi_{0}\left(x+r(x;t)\cdot\vec{N}(x)\right)+t\psi\left(x+r(x;t)\cdot\vec{N}(x)\right)=0

w.r.t.
t
in a neighbourhood of
0
, we may recover

(B.4)

r(x;t)=-t\cdot\frac{\psi(x)}{\|\nabla\phi_{0}(x)\|}+O(t^{2}).

Therefore, for
j\leq m
,

|\pazocal{C}_{j}^{+}(t)|=|\pazocal{C}_{j}^{+}|+t\cdot\int\limits_{\partial\pazocal{C}_{j}^{+}}\frac{\psi(x)}{\|\nabla\phi_{0}(x)\|}dx+O(t^{2}),

with the correction term, stemming from the curvature of
\partial\pazocal{C}_{j}
, absorbed in the
O(t^{2})
error term. Inserting into (
B.2
), and comparing it to the analogous expression for
D(t)
, shows that

(B.5)

D^{\prime}(0)=\frac{2}{|\Pi|}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{m}\int\limits_{\partial\pazocal{C}_{j}^{+}}\frac{\psi(x)}{\|\nabla\phi_{0}(x)\|}dx=\frac{2}{|\Pi|}\int\limits_{\phi_{0}^{-1}(0)\cap\Pi}\frac{\psi(x)}{\|\nabla\phi_{0}(x)\|}dx.

To evaluate
D^{\prime\prime}(0)
, we observe that the same argument leading to (
B.5
) gives

(B.6)

\begin{split}D^{\prime}(t)&=\frac{2}{|\Pi|}\int\limits_{\phi_{t}^{-1}(0)\cap\Pi}\frac{\psi(x)}{\|\nabla\phi_{t}(x)\|}dx,\end{split}

provided
|t|
is sufficiently small. Let us denote for brevity

(B.7)

\Upsilon_{t}(x):=\frac{\psi(x)}{\|\nabla\phi_{t}(x)\|},

and
\gamma_{t}:=\phi_{t}^{-1}(0)\cap\Pi
. Writing

\int\limits_{\gamma_{t}}\Upsilon_{t}(x)dx=\int\limits_{\gamma_{0}}\Upsilon_{t}(x)dx+\Big(\int\limits_{\gamma_{t}}\Upsilon_{t}(x)dx-\int\limits_{\gamma_{0}}\Upsilon_{t}(x)dx\Big),

we have
D^{\prime\prime}(0)=\frac{2}{|\Pi|}\cdot\left(I_{1}(0)+I_{2}(0)\right)

where

(B.8)

I_{1}(t):=\int\limits_{\gamma_{0}}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Upsilon_{t}(x)dx\qquad\text{and}\qquad I_{2}(t):=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Big[\int\limits_{\gamma_{t}}\Upsilon_{t}(x)dx-\int\limits_{\gamma_{0}}\Upsilon_{t}(x)dx\Big].

Recalling (
B.7
),

\displaystyle I_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=-\int\limits_{\gamma_{0}}\frac{\psi(x)}{\|\nabla\phi_{t}(x)\|^{2}}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left[\|\nabla\phi_{t}(x)\|\right]dx=-\int\limits_{\gamma_{0}}\frac{\psi(x)}{\|\nabla\phi_{t}(x)\|^{3}}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left[\left\langle\nabla\phi_{t}(x),\nabla\phi_{t}(x)\right\rangle\right]dx

\displaystyle=-\int\limits_{\gamma_{0}}\frac{\psi(x)}{\|\nabla\phi_{t}(x)\|^{3}}\cdot\left\langle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left[\nabla\phi_{t}(x)\right],\nabla\phi_{t}(x)\right\rangle dx.

Since
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left[\nabla\phi_{t}(x)\right]=\nabla\psi(x)
, we obtain

(B.9)

I_{1}(0)=-\int\limits_{\gamma_{0}}\frac{\psi(x)}{\|\nabla\phi_{t}(x)\|^{3}}\cdot\left\langle\nabla\psi(x),\nabla\phi_{0}(x)\right\rangle dx.

Next, we evaluate
I_{2}(0)
. We have

\int\limits_{\gamma_{t}}\Upsilon_{t}(x)dx=\int\limits_{\gamma_{0}}\Upsilon_{t}\left(x+r(x;t)\cdot\vec{N}(x)\right)\cdot\left(1-\kappa(x)\cdot r(x;t)\right)dx,

where
r(x;t)
and
\vec{N}(x)

B.3

is as in Lemma

(ii.). Inserting into (

) gives

I_{2}(0)=\int\limits_{\gamma_{0}}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left[\Upsilon_{t}\left(x+r(x;t)\cdot\vec{N}(x)\right)\cdot\left(1-\kappa(x)\cdot r(x;t)\right)\right]\bigg|_{t=0}dy.

Since
\Upsilon_{t}(\cdot)
is given by (
B.7
), and

is given by (
B.4

(B.10)

I_{2}(0)=-\int\limits_{\gamma_{0}}\frac{\psi(x)}{\|\nabla\phi_{0}(x)\|}\cdot\partial_{\vec{N}(x)}\left[\frac{\psi(x)}{\|\nabla\phi_{0}(x)\|}\right]dx+\int\limits_{\gamma_{0}}\frac{\psi(x)}{\|\nabla\phi_{0}(x)\|}\cdot\kappa(x)\frac{\psi(x)}{\|\nabla\phi_{0}(x)\|}dx.

As above, we evaluate

\displaystyle\partial_{\vec{N}(x)}\Big[\frac{\psi(x)}{\|\nabla\phi_{0}(x)\|}\Big]

\displaystyle=\Big\langle\vec{N}(x),\nabla\Big[\frac{\psi(x)}{\|\nabla\phi_{0}(x)\|}\Big]\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\|\nabla\phi_{0}(x)\|^{3}}\left\langle\nabla\phi_{0}(x),\nabla\psi(x)\cdot\|\nabla\phi_{0}(x)\|-\psi(x)\cdot\nabla\|\nabla\phi_{0}(x)\|\right\rangle

&\qquad\qquad+\int\limits_{\gamma_{0}}\frac{\psi(x)^{2}}{\|\nabla\phi_{0}(x)\|^{2}}\cdot\kappa(x)dx.\end{split}

Consolidating (
