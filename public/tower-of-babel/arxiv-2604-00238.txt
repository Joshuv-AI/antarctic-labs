Stress Asymmetry in Hard Magnetic Soft Materials Preprint: To appear in Journal of Applied Mechanics ( DOI:10.1115/1.4071532 )
H. Gökçen Güner, Francois Barthelat, John D. Clayton, Carlos Mora-Corral, Noel Walkington, Kaushik Dayal

Abstract
Hard magnetic soft materials – soft polymers embedded with hard magnetic particles – are modeled using continuum magnetomechanical formulations in which the deformation and the magnetization field are the primary kinematic variables. A recent question in such formulations is whether the Cauchy stress is symmetric, which is directly related to frame invariance and angular momentum balance. This note discusses energetically equivalent formulations, related by a change of variables between referential and current descriptions of the magnetization, and shows that they generally yield different Cauchy stresses, including a change in their symmetry. Specifically, the formulation based on a referential magnetization produces a symmetric Cauchy stress, while that based on a current magnetization generally yields an asymmetric Cauchy stress. We highlight that when the internal variable (magnetization field) is at the energy-minimizing equilibrium configuration, the divergences of these stresses are the same, and both stresses are symmetric.

In this short note, we discuss that equivalent energetic formulations – which are related by an elementary change of variables – can generally lead to different Cauchy stresses, including changing whether they are symmetric.

The class of changes of variables that we consider are between referential and current descriptions of the internal variables; the magnetization field, in HMSM.
Here, we emphasize that we are
not
switching between Lagrangian and Eulerian descriptions; all quantities are Lagrangian.
What does change, however, is that the referential and current descriptions transform in different ways under a rigid rotation of the current configuration: the referential description is invariant, whereas the current description is generally not invariant.
A simple example is the temperature gradient: the gradient with respect to referential coordinates is invariant under a rigid rotation of the current configuration, whereas the gradient with respect to current coordinates is not invariant; however, the descriptions are equivalent in that it is possible to transform between them

An elementary example shows the main point of this note.

\displaystyle w(f,n)

\displaystyle w_{0}(f,n_{0}(f,n)):=w(f,n)

2.C
Equivalence of the Referential and Current Descriptions at Internal Variable Equilibrium

Consider a free energy functional
\displaystyle E[{\mathbfit x},{\mathbfit n}]
with general dependence on the arguments, including gradients of any order or nonlocal terms.
Here,
\displaystyle{\mathbfit n}({\mathbfit x}_{0})
is a general tensorial quantity that describes the internal variable in the current configuration.
Define the mapping between
\displaystyle{\mathbfit n}
and the reference description
\displaystyle{\mathbfit n}_{0}
by
\displaystyle{\mathbfit n}_{0}=\hat{\mathbfit n}_{0}({\mathbfit F},{\mathbfit n})
and
\displaystyle{\mathbfit n}=\hat{\mathbfit n}({\mathbfit F},{\mathbfit n}_{0})
, where the mappings satisfy the requirements discussed above.
We can then define the free energy functional
\displaystyle E_{0}[{\mathbfit x},{\mathbfit n}_{0}]
through:

E_{0}[{\mathbfit x},{\mathbfit n}_{0}]:=E[{\mathbfit x},{\mathbfit n}=\hat{\mathbfit n}({\mathbfit F},{\mathbfit n}_{0})]

(9)

Consider the arbitrary admissible variations
\displaystyle{\mathbfit x}\to{\mathbfit x}+\delta{\mathbfit x},{\mathbfit n}\to{\mathbfit n}+\delta{\mathbfit n}
.
Assuming that the variation of
\displaystyle{\mathbfit n}_{0}
is not arbitrary but is instead given by the mappings between reference and current descriptions, we have the following expression for
\displaystyle\delta{\mathbfit n}_{0}
to leading order:

{\mathbfit n}_{0}\to{\mathbfit n}_{0}+\delta{\mathbfit n}_{0}=\hat{\mathbfit n}_{0}({\mathbfit F}+\nabla_{0}\delta{\mathbfit x},{\mathbfit n}+\delta{\mathbfit n})\implies\delta{\mathbfit n}_{0}=\frac{\partial\hat{\mathbfit n}_{0}}{\partial{\mathbfit F}}\nabla_{0}\delta{\mathbfit x}+\frac{\partial\hat{\mathbfit n}_{0}}{\partial{\mathbfit n}}\delta{\mathbfit n}

(10)

Since the variation
\displaystyle\delta{\mathbfit n}_{0}
is not arbitrary, but is instead constrained by the reference-current mapping, it follows that the variations
\displaystyle\delta E_{0}
and
\displaystyle\delta E
are equal, giving the relation between the variational derivatives of
\displaystyle{\mathbfit E}
and
\displaystyle{\mathbfit E}_{0}
:

\updelta_{{\mathbfit x}}E_{0}\cdot\delta{\mathbfit x}+\updelta_{{\mathbfit n}_{0}}E_{0}\cdot\delta{\mathbfit n}_{0}=\updelta_{{\mathbfit x}}E\cdot\delta{\mathbfit x}+\updelta_{{\mathbfit n}}E\cdot\delta{\mathbfit n}

(11)

where the dot products are interpreted in this section as the Frobenius inner product appropriate for the tensorial order of
\displaystyle{\mathbfit n}
.

Using (
10
) to replace
\displaystyle\delta{\mathbfit n}_{0}
in the above equation gives:

\left(\updelta_{{\mathbfit x}}E_{0}-\updelta_{{\mathbfit x}}E\right)\cdot\delta{\mathbfit x}+\updelta_{{\mathbfit n}_{0}}E_{0}\cdot\frac{\partial\hat{\mathbfit n}_{0}}{\partial{\mathbfit F}}\nabla_{0}\delta{\mathbfit x}+\left(\updelta_{{\mathbfit n}_{0}}E_{0}\frac{\partial\hat{\mathbfit n}_{0}}{\partial{\mathbfit n}}-\updelta_{{\mathbfit n}}E\right)\cdot\delta{\mathbfit n}=0

(12)

Since
\displaystyle\delta{\mathbfit n}
is arbitrary, we conclude that
\displaystyle\updelta_{{\mathbfit n}_{0}}E_{0}\frac{\partial\hat{\mathbfit n}_{0}}{\partial{\mathbfit n}}=\updelta_{{\mathbfit n}}E
.
Further, since
\displaystyle\frac{\partial\hat{\mathbfit n}_{0}}{\partial{\mathbfit n}}
is invertible based on the assumptions made on the mapping, it follows that
\displaystyle\updelta_{{\mathbfit n}_{0}}E_{0}=\mathbf{0}\iff\updelta_{{\mathbfit n}}E=\mathbf{0}
.
In physical terms, the equilibrium conditions with respect to the reference and current descriptions are equivalent.

We next note that the arbitrariness of
\displaystyle\delta{\mathbfit x}
does not, in general, imply
\displaystyle\updelta_{{\mathbfit x}}E_{0}=\updelta_{{\mathbfit x}}E
.
However, if we further have
\displaystyle\updelta_{{\mathbfit n}_{0}}E_{0}=\mathbf{0}
, it then follows that
\displaystyle\updelta_{{\mathbfit x}}E_{0}=\updelta_{{\mathbfit x}}E
, where
\displaystyle\updelta_{{\mathbfit x}}E\equiv\divergence_{0}{\mathbfit P}
and
\displaystyle\updelta_{{\mathbfit x}}E_{0}\equiv\divergence_{0}{\mathbfit P}_{0}
represent the resultant forces in the balance of linear momentum.
Therefore, in general, the models predict the same forces in the balance of linear momentum only when the internal variable is allowed to relax, and not necessarily if the internal variable is not relaxed.

5
Concluding Remarks

We have shown that energetically equivalent continuum models with vectorial internal variables can give rise to different stresses, even permitting a change in symmetry of the Cauchy stress.
In the context of hard magnetic soft matter, we find that the magnetomechanical Maxwell stresses are symmetric in formulations based on referential descriptions of the magnetization but are not necessarily symmetric in formulations based on current descriptions of the magnetization.
The key issue is that the stresses are obtained from the energy as partial derivatives with respect to the deformation while keeping the internal variable fixed; since the internal variables are coupled to the deformation, a transformation of the internal variable naturally leads to a different value for the partial derivatives.
We note that we have not included external work terms in our discussion, but it is a simple extension that would not change our conclusions.

However, we also find that key aspects of the various formulations coincide when the internal variable is at equilibrium, i.e., the derivative of the energy with respect to the internal variable vanishes.
Under this condition, while the stress is not necessarily the same, the resultant divergence of the stress is the same and, in the case of hard magnetic soft matter, the stresses are symmetric.
However, when the internal variable is evolving away from equilibrium – e.g., through Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert dynamics for magnetism
Lak 11
, or through Ericksen-Leslie dynamics for liquid crystals
Cha 93
– the stresses and their resultants can generally be different.
