On a necessary condition for removing singularities of solutions of nonlinear elliptic inequalities
A.A. Kon’kov, A.E. Shishkov

Abstract
. We study solutions of the differential inequality Δ m / 2 ​ u ≥ f ⁡ ( x ) ​ g ​ ( u ) in ​ B 1 ∖ { 0 } , \Delta^{m/2}u\geq f(x)g(u)\quad\mbox{in }B_{1}\setminus\{0\}, where m ≥ 2 m\geq 2 is an even integer, f f and g g are some functions, and B 1 B_{1} is an open unit ball in R n R^{n} , n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 , centered at zero. Our aim is to obtain a necessary condition for a singularity at zero to be removable for any solution of this inequality.

1.
Introduction

We deal with differential inequalities of the form

(1.1)

\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\partial^{\alpha}a_{\alpha}(x,u)\geq f(x)g(|u|)\quad\mbox{in }B_{1}\setminus\{0\},

where
a_{\alpha}
are Caratheodory functions such that

|a_{\alpha}(x,\zeta)|\leq C|\zeta|,\quad|\alpha|=m,

with some constant
C>0
for almost all
x\in B_{1}

and for all
\zeta\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
In so doing,
f\in L_{loc,\infty}(\overline{B_{1}}\setminus\{0\})
and
g:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is a non-decreasing function such that
g(0)=0
and
g(\zeta)>0
for all
\zeta\in(0,\infty)
.
As is customary, by
B_{r}
we mean the open ball of radius
r>0
centered at zero.

A function
u
is a solution of (
1.1
) if

u\in L_{1}(B_{1}\setminus\{0\})

and

f(x){g(|u|)}\in L_{1}(B_{1}\setminus\{0\})

and, moreover,

(1.2)

\int_{B_{1}}\sum_{|\alpha|=m}(-1)^{m}a_{\alpha}(x,u)\partial^{\alpha}\varphi\,dx\geq\int_{B_{1}}f(x)g(|u|)\varphi\,dx

for all non-negative functions
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{1}\setminus\{0\})
.
We say that
u
has a removable singularity at zero if

u\in L_{1}(B_{1})
,

f(x){g(|u|)}\in L_{1}(B_{1})
,
and inequality (
1.2
) is valid for all non-negative functions
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{1})
.

In paper
[
1
]
, it is arisen the condition

(1.3)

\int_{1}^{\infty}g^{-1/m}(\zeta)\zeta^{1/m-1}\,d\zeta<\infty

which was required for singularity at zero to be removable for any solution of (
1.1
).
For
m=2
, it is easy to see that (
1.3
) is equivalent to the well-known Keller–Osserman blow-up condition
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
.
For
m=2
, relation (
1.3
) also appeared in
[
5
]
, where the differential inequality

(1.4)

\Delta u\geq a(x,u)\quad\mbox{in }B_{1}\setminus\{0\}

was considered. In this paper, it was assumed that
a
is a non-negative function such that

q(r)g(\zeta)\leq\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{x\in B_{2r}\setminus B_{r/2}}a(x,r^{2-n}\zeta)

with a measurable function
q:(0,1/2]\to[0,\infty)
and a non-decreasing continuous function
g:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
for all real numbers
0<r\leq 1/2
and
\zeta>1
.
In particular, it was shown that any positive solution of (
1.4
) has a removable singularity at zero if

(1.5)

\int_{1}^{\infty}(g(\zeta)\zeta)^{-1/2}\,d\zeta<\infty,

(1.6)

\int_{0}^{1/2}r^{n-1}q(r)dr=\infty,

and

(1.7)

\int_{0}^{1/2}r^{n-1}\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{x\in B_{2r}\setminus B_{r/2}}a(x,r^{2-n}\varepsilon)dr=\infty

for all real numbers
\varepsilon>0
.

In the case of the inequality

\Delta u\geq u^{\lambda}\quad\mbox{in }B_{1}\setminus\{0\},

where
B_{1}
is a unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
,
conditions (
1.5
)–(
1.7
) with
q(r)=r^{\lambda(2-n)}
and
g(\zeta)=\zeta^{\lambda}
yields

\lambda\geq\frac{n}{n-2}.

This coincides with the well-known Brezis–Véron condition
[
6
]
.

For the more general inequality

(1.8)

\Delta u\geq|x|^{s}u^{\lambda}\quad\mbox{in }B_{1}\setminus\{0\},

conditions (
1.5
)–(
1.7
) with
q(r)=r^{\lambda(2-n)+s}
and
g(\zeta)=\zeta^{\lambda}
take the form
\lambda>1
and
s\leq\lambda(n-2)-n
.

The results of
[
5
]
can also be applied to inequalities with non-power nonlinearities for an unknown function
u
.
For example, in the case of the inequality

\Delta u\geq u^{n/(n-2)}\log^{\mu}(1+u)\quad\mbox{in }B_{1}\setminus\{0\},

where
B_{1}
is a unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
,
from (
1.5
)–(
1.7
) with
q(r)=r^{-n}/\log(e^{3}+r^{2-n})
and
g(\zeta)=\zeta^{n/(n-2)}/\log(e^{3}+\zeta)
, it follows that any positive solution
has a removable singularity at zero if
\mu\geq-1
. This is in good agreement with the result of paper
[
7
]
.

Now, let us examine the critical exponent
\lambda=1
in the right-hand side of (
1.8
). Namely, consider the inequality

(1.9)

\Delta u\geq|x|^{s}u\log^{\mu}(1+u)\quad\mbox{in }B_{1}\setminus\{0\}.

Putting
q(r)=r^{2-n+s}
and
g(\zeta)=\zeta\log^{\mu}(1+\zeta)
,
we obtain from (
1.5
)–(
1.7
) that any positive solution of (
1.9
) has a removable singularity at zero if
s\leq-2
and
\mu>2
.

All the above conditions are exact.
It can easily be seen that (
1.5
) is equivalent to (
1.3
) with
m=2
.
Condition (
1.3
) is obviously not sufficient to remove a singularity since
we must also take into account the behavior of the function
f
in a neighborhood of zero.
Moreover, in the case of linear equations, we need to impose some restrictions on the growth of solutions
[
8
]
.
However, it can be shown that condition (
1.3
) is necessary.
The proof of this fact is the subject of Theorem
2.1
present to your attention.

2.
Main results

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
g:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
be a non-decreasing continuous function such that

(2.1)

\int_{1}^{\infty}g^{-1/m}(\zeta)\zeta^{1/m-1}\,d\zeta=\infty.

Then the inequality

(2.2)

\Delta^{m/2}u\geq f(x)g(u)\quad\mbox{in }B_{1}\setminus\{0\},

where
m\geq 2
is an even integer, has a positive solution with a non-removable singularity at zero for any
f\in L_{loc,\infty}(\overline{B_{1}}\setminus\{0\})
.

The proof of Theorem
2.1
relies on the following statement.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
g:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
be a non-decreasing continuous function satisfying condition (
2.1
). Then the problem

(2.3)

\left(\frac{1}{r^{n-1}}\frac{d}{dr}\left(r^{n-1}\frac{d}{dr}\right)\right)^{m/2}w\geq F(r)g(1+w),\quad w^{(i)}(1)=0,\;i=0,1,\ldots,m-1,

where
m
is a positive even integer,
has a solution on the whole interval
(0,1]
for any
F\in L_{loc,\infty}((0,1])
.
In so doing, this solution is a positive decreasing function on
(0,1)
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, it can be assumed that
F
is a positive non-increasing function on
(0,1]
; otherwise we replace
F
by

F_{max}(r)=\max\left\{\sup_{(r,1)}F,1\right\}.

We prove the theorem in several steps.

Step 1

Let us define mappings
A_{i}:C((0,1])\to C((0,1])
as follows:

A_{0}v(r)=F(r)g(1+|v(r)|)

and

A_{i}v(r)=\int_{r}^{1}\int_{\rho}^{1}\left(\frac{\xi}{\rho}\right)^{n-1}A_{i-1}v(\xi)d\xi d\rho,\quad i=1,\ldots,m/2,

For any
v\in C((0,1])
the functions
A_{i}v
are positive and decreasing on the interval
(0,1)
.
Let us extend
A_{i}v
on the whole set
(0,\infty)
by putting
A_{i}v(r)=0
for all
r\in(1,\infty)
,
i=0,\ldots,m/2
.

Assume that
r>0
and
h>0
are some real numbers.
We obviously have

A_{i}v(r+h)=\int_{r+h}^{\infty}\int_{\rho}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\xi}{\rho}\right)^{n-1}A_{i-1}v(\xi)d\xi d\rho\geq\int_{r+h}^{r+2h}\int_{r+2h}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\xi}{\rho}\right)^{n-1}A_{i-1}v(\xi)d\xi d\rho,

whence in accordance with the estimate

\int_{r+h}^{r+2h}\int_{r+2h}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\xi}{\rho}\right)^{n-1}A_{i-1}v(\xi)d\xi d\rho\geq\frac{h}{(r+2h)^{n-1}}\int_{r+2h}^{\infty}\xi^{n-1}A_{i-1}v(\xi)d\xi

it follows that

(2.4)

A_{i}v(r+h)\geq\frac{h}{(r+2h)^{n-1}}\int_{r+2h}^{\infty}\xi^{n-1}A_{i-1}v(\xi)d\xi,\quad i=1,\ldots,m/2,

for any
v\in C((0,1])
.
Since

\int_{r+2h}^{\infty}\xi^{n-1}A_{i-1}v(\xi)d\xi\geq\int_{r+2h}^{r+3h}\xi^{n-1}A_{i-1}v(\xi)d\xi\geq h(r+2h)^{n-1}A_{i-1}v(r+3h),

formula (
2.4
) yields

(2.5)

A_{i}v(r+h)\geq h^{2}A_{i-1}v(r+3h),\quad i=1,\ldots,m/2.

Iterating (
2.4
), we also obtain

(2.6)

A_{i}v(r+h)\geq\frac{h^{2j-1}}{(r+2jh)^{n-1}}\int_{r+2jh}^{\infty}\xi^{n-1}A_{i-j}v(\xi)d\xi,\quad 1\leq j\leq i,\quad i=1,\ldots,m/2,

for any
v\in C((0,1])
.
Analogously, (
2.5
) implies that

(2.7)

A_{i}v(r+h)\geq h^{2j}A_{i-j}v(r+(2j+1)h),\quad 1\leq j\leq i,\quad i=1,\ldots,m/2,

At the same time,

\displaystyle A_{i}v(r)-A_{i}v(r+jh)=\int_{r}^{r+jh}\int_{\rho}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\xi}{\rho}\right)^{n-1}A_{i-1}v(\xi)d\xi d\rho

\displaystyle\quad{}=\int_{r}^{r+jh}\int_{\rho}^{r+(j+1)h}\left(\frac{\xi}{\rho}\right)^{n-1}A_{i-1}v(\xi)d\xi d\rho

\displaystyle\quad\phantom{{}={}}{}+\int_{r}^{r+jh}\int_{r+(j+1)h}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\xi}{\rho}\right)^{n-1}A_{i-1}v(\xi)d\xi d\rho,\quad 1\leq j\leq i-1,\quad i=1,\ldots,m/2,

whence in accordance with the inequalities

\int_{r}^{r+jh}\int_{\rho}^{r+(j+1)h}\left(\frac{\xi}{\rho}\right)^{n-1}A_{i-1}v(\xi)d\xi d\rho\leq j(j+1)\left(\frac{r+(j+1)h}{r}\right)^{n-1}h^{2}A_{i-1}v(r)

and

\int_{r}^{r+jh}\int_{r+(j+1)h}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\xi}{\rho}\right)^{n-1}A_{i-1}v(\xi)d\xi d\rho\leq\frac{jh}{r^{n-1}}\int_{r+(j+1)h}^{\infty}\xi^{n-1}A_{i-1}v(\xi)d\xi

we have

\displaystyle A_{i}v(r)-A_{i}v(r+jh)\leq j(j+1)\left(\frac{r+(j+1)h}{r}\right)^{n-1}h^{2}A_{i-1}v(r)

(2.8)

\displaystyle\quad{}+\frac{jh}{r^{n-1}}\int_{r+(j+1)h}^{\infty}\xi^{n-1}A_{i-1}v(\xi)d\xi,\quad 1\leq j\leq i-1,\quad i=1,\ldots,m/2.

Now, assume that
0<h<2r/m
. In this case, (
2.8
) yields

\displaystyle A_{i}v(r)-A_{i}v(r+jh)\leq\lambda h^{2}A_{i-1}v(r)

(2.9)

\displaystyle\quad{}+\frac{\mu h}{r^{n-1}}\int_{r+(j+1)h}^{\infty}\xi^{n-1}A_{i-1}v(\xi)d\xi,\quad 1\leq j\leq i-1,\quad i=1,\ldots,m/2,

where

\lambda=\left(\frac{m}{2}\right)^{2}2^{n-1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mu=\frac{m}{2}.

In particular,

A_{m/2}v(r)-A_{m/2}v(r+h)\leq\lambda h^{2}A_{m/2-1}v(r)+\frac{\mu h}{r^{n-1}}\int_{r+2h}^{\infty}\xi^{n-1}A_{m/2-1}v(\xi)d\xi.

Combining this with the estimate

\displaystyle A_{m/2-1}v(r)\leq{}

\displaystyle A_{m/2-1}v(r+3h)+\lambda h^{2}A_{m/2-2}v(r)

\displaystyle{}+\frac{\mu h}{r^{n-1}}\int_{r+4h}^{\infty}\xi^{n-1}A_{m/2-2}v(\xi)d\xi

which also follows from (
2.9
), we obtain

\displaystyle A_{m/2}v(r)-A_{m/2}v(r+h)\leq\lambda h^{2}A_{m/2-1}v(r+3h)+\lambda^{2}h^{4}A_{m/2-2}v(r)

\displaystyle\quad{}+\frac{\mu h}{r^{n-1}}\int_{r+2h}^{\infty}\xi^{n-1}A_{m/2-1}v(\xi)d\xi+\frac{\lambda\mu h^{3}}{r^{n-1}}\int_{r+4h}^{\infty}\xi^{n-1}A_{m/2-2}v(\xi)d\xi.

Estimating
A_{m/2-i}v(r)
successively by (
2.9
) with
i=2,\ldots,m/2-1
and
j=2i+1
, we arrive at the inequality

\displaystyle A_{m/2}v(r)-A_{m/2}v(r+h)\leq{}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m/2-1}\lambda^{i}h^{2i}A_{m/2-i}v(r+(2i+1)h)+\lambda^{m/2}h^{m}A_{0}v(r)

(2.10)

\displaystyle\quad{}+\sum_{i=1}^{m/2}\frac{\lambda^{i-1}\mu h^{2i-1}}{r^{n-1}}\int_{r+2ih}^{\infty}\xi^{n-1}A_{m/2-i}v(\xi)d\xi.

From (
2.6
), it follows that

\displaystyle A_{m/2}v(r+h)

\displaystyle{}\geq\frac{h^{2i-1}}{(r+2ih)^{n-1}}\int_{r+2ih}^{\infty}\xi^{n-1}A_{m/2-i}v(\xi)d\xi

\displaystyle{}\geq\frac{h^{2i-1}}{2^{n-1}r^{n-1}}\int_{r+2ih}^{\infty}\xi^{n-1}A_{m/2-i}v(\xi)d\xi\quad 1\leq i\leq m/2,

Analogously, (
2.7
) yields

A_{m/2}v(r+h)\geq h^{2i}A_{m/2-i}v(r+(2i+1)h),\quad i=1,\ldots,m/2.

Thus, (
2.10
) allows us to assert that

\displaystyle A_{m/2}v(r)

\displaystyle{}\leq\left(1+\sum_{i=1}^{m/2-1}\lambda^{i}+2^{n-1}\mu\sum_{i=1}^{m/2}\lambda^{i-1}\right)A_{m/2}v(r+h)+\lambda^{m/2}h^{m}A_{0}v(r)

(2.11)

\displaystyle{}\leq\frac{\lambda^{m/2}-1}{\lambda-1}(1+2^{n-1}\mu)A_{m/2}v(r+h)+\lambda^{m/2}h^{m}A_{0}v(r).

Step 2

We take

(2.12)

\alpha=\max\left\{2^{(n-1)/2}m,2^{1/m}\lambda^{1/2}\right\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\beta=\frac{\lambda^{m/2}-1}{\lambda-1}(2+2^{n}\mu).

It can easily be seen that
\beta A_{m/2}
is a completely continuous nonlinear operator in the space

W=\{w\in C([r_{0},1]):\|w\|_{C([r_{0},1])}\leq 1\}

for some real number
1/2<r_{0}<1
.
Thus, by the Bohl–Brouwer fixed point theorem, there exists a function
v_{0}\in V
such that
w_{0}=\beta A_{m/2}w_{0}
or, in other words,

(2.13)

w_{0}(r)=\beta A_{m/2}w_{0}(r)

for all
r\in[r_{0},1]
.
Since
w_{0}
is a positive function on
(0,1]
, we have
w_{0}(r_{0})>0
.

Further, let
v_{0}
be a solution of the Cauchy problem

(2.14)

-\frac{d}{dr}v_{0}^{1/m}(r)=\frac{\alpha}{r^{(n+1)/2}}F^{1/m}\left(\frac{r}{2}\right)g^{1/m}(1+\beta v_{0})\quad\mbox{on }(0,r_{0}],\quad v_{0}(r_{0})=w_{0}(r_{0}).

To verify that (
2.14
) has a solution on the whole interval
(0,r_{0}]
, we denote
u_{0}=v_{0}^{1/n}
. Then (
2.14
) takes the form

-\frac{d}{dr}u_{0}(r)=\frac{\alpha}{r^{(n+1)/2}}F^{1/m}\left(\frac{r}{2}\right)g^{1/m}(1+\beta u_{0}^{m}),\quad u_{0}(r_{0})=v_{0}^{m}(r_{0}).

Integrating this, we obtain

\int_{u_{0}(r_{0})}^{u_{0}(r)}g^{-1/m}(1+\beta t^{m})dt=\alpha\int_{r}^{r_{0}}F^{1/m}\left(\frac{\xi}{2}\right)\frac{d\xi}{\xi^{(n+1)/2}},

whence after the changing of variables
\zeta=1+\beta t^{m}
, it follows that

\frac{1}{m\beta^{1/m}}\int_{1+\beta u_{0}^{m}(r_{0})}^{1+\beta u_{0}^{m}(r)}g^{-1/m}(\zeta)(\zeta-1)^{1/m-1}d\zeta=\alpha\int_{r}^{r_{0}}F^{1/m}\left(\frac{\xi}{2}\right)\frac{d\xi}{\xi^{(n+1)/2}}.

In view of (
2.1
), the left-hand side of the last equality tends to infinity as
u_{0}(r)\to\infty
. This, in turn, means that the solution
v_{0}
of Cauchy problem (
2.13
) is defined for all
r\in(0,r_{0}]
.
Moreover,
v_{0}
is a positive decreasing function on
(0,r_{0}]
.

Step 3

We put

v(r)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &v_{0}(r),&&r\in(0,r_{0}),\\
&w_{0}(r),&&r\in[r_{0},1].\end{aligned}\right.

Since
v
is a positive decreasing function on the interval
(0,1)
, by induction on
m
it is easy to show that

(2.15)

A_{m/2}v(r)\leq\frac{1}{r^{(n-1)m/2}}F(r)g(1+v(r)).

Let us prove that
v
satisfies the inequality

(2.16)

v(r)\geq A_{m/2}v(r)

for all
r\in(0,1]
.
If
r\in[r_{0},1]
, then (
2.16
) immediately follows from (
2.13
).

Assume that
r\in(0,r_{0})
and
k
is the minimal positive integer satisfying the relation
v(r)\leq\beta^{k}v(r_{0})
.
We prove (
2.16
) by induction on
k
.
At first, let
k=1
and
h=r_{0}-r\geq 2r/m
.
Integrating (
2.14
), we have

v_{0}^{1/m}(r)-v_{0}^{1/m}(r_{*})=\int_{r}^{r_{*}}\frac{\alpha}{\xi^{(n+1)/2}}F^{1/m}\left(\frac{\xi}{2}\right)g^{1/m}(1+\beta v_{0})d\xi,

where
r_{*}=\min\{r_{0},2r\}
,
whence it follows that

\displaystyle v(r)

\displaystyle{}\geq\left(\int_{r}^{r_{*}}\frac{\alpha}{\xi^{(n+1)/2}}F^{1/m}\left(\frac{\xi}{2}\right)g^{1/m}(1+\beta v)d\xi\right)^{m}

\displaystyle{}\geq\left(\frac{\alpha(r_{*}-r)}{r_{*}^{(n+1)/2}}F^{1/m}\left(\frac{r_{*}}{2}\right)g^{1/m}(1+\beta v(r_{*}))\right)^{m}.

Since
r_{*}-r\geq 2r/m
,
r_{*}\leq 2r
, and
\beta v(r_{*})\geq\beta v(r_{0})\geq v(r)
, this implies the inequality

(2.17)

v(r)\geq\left(\frac{\alpha}{2^{(n-1)/2}m}\right)^{m}\frac{1}{r^{(n-1)m/2}}F(r)g(1+v(r)).

In its turn, combining (
2.17
) with (
2.12
) and (
2.15
), we obtain (
2.16
).

Now, let
k=1
and
h=r_{0}-r<2r/m
.
From (
2.11
), it follows that

(2.18)

A_{m/2}v(r)\leq\frac{\lambda^{m/2}-1}{\lambda-1}(1+2^{n-1}\mu)A_{m/2}v(r_{0})+\lambda^{m/2}h^{m}A_{0}v(r).

In so doing, by (
2.13
), we have

v(r)\geq v(r_{0})=\beta A_{m/2}v(r_{0}).

In view of (
2.12
), this yields

(2.19)

\frac{1}{2}v(r)\geq\frac{\lambda^{m/2}-1}{\lambda-1}(1+2^{n-1}\mu)A_{m/2}v(r_{0}).

Integrating (
2.14
), we also obtain

v_{0}^{1/m}(r)-v_{0}^{1/m}(r_{0})=\int_{r}^{r_{0}}\frac{\alpha}{\xi^{(n+1)/2}}F^{1/m}\left(\frac{\xi}{2}\right)g^{1/m}(1+\beta v_{0})d\xi,

whence it follows that

\displaystyle v(r)

\displaystyle{}\geq\left(\int_{r}^{r_{0}}\frac{\alpha}{\xi^{(n+1)/2}}F^{1/m}\left(\frac{\xi}{2}\right)g^{1/m}(1+\beta v)d\xi\right)^{m}

\displaystyle{}\geq\left(\frac{\alpha h}{r_{0}^{(n+1)/2}}F^{1/m}\left(\frac{r_{0}}{2}\right)g^{1/m}(1+\beta v(r_{0}))\right)^{m}.

Since
0<r_{0}<1
,
r_{0}/2=(r+h)/2<r
, and
\beta v(r_{0})\geq v(r)
, this implies the estimate

v(r)\geq\alpha^{m}h^{m}F(r)g(1+v(r))=\alpha^{m}h^{m}A_{0}v(r);

therefore, taking into account (
2.12
), one can assert that

(2.20)

\frac{1}{2}v(r)\geq\lambda^{m/2}h^{m}A_{0}v(r).

Thus, combining the last inequality with (
2.18
) and (
2.19
), we arrive at (
2.16
).

Assume further that (
2.16
) is valid for all
k\leq l
, where
l
is a positive integer. Let us show that (
2.16
) is also valid for
k=l+1
. We take a real number
r<r_{1}<r_{0}
satisfying the condition
v(r)=\beta v(r_{1})
.
By the induction hypothesis, we obviously have

(2.21)

v(r_{1})\geq A_{m/2}v(r_{1}).

If
h=r-r_{1}<2r/m
, then repeating the above argument with
r_{0}
replaced by
r_{1}
, we obtain (
2.17
).
In view (
2.12
) and (
2.15
), this immediately implies (
2.16
).
Let
h=r-r_{1}\geq 2r/m
.
In this case, repeating the argument given in the proof of (
2.18
) with
r_{0}
replaced by
r_{1}
, we have

(2.22)

A_{m/2}v(r)\leq\frac{\lambda^{m/2}-1}{\lambda-1}(1+2^{n-1}\mu)A_{m/2}v(r_{1})+\lambda^{m/2}h^{m}A_{0}v(r).

At the same time, (
2.21
) allows us to assert that

v(r)=\beta v(r_{1})\geq\beta A_{m/2}v(r_{1}),

whence in accordance with (
