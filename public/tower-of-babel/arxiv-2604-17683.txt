1.4
Sketch of the proofs

We now give an outline of the proofs on Theorems
1.1
–
1.2
. When

(u_{0},u_{1})(x)
decay suitably fast at infinity, the global existence of smooth solution
u
to (
1.1
) with
d=3
and
m=1
has been established in
[
22
]
. The key points of the related analyses in
[
22
]
are based on the standard energy estimates for wave
equations, the smallness of
\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor+2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
and the following Klainerman-Sobolev inequality:

|\partial u(t,x)|\leq\frac{C}{(1+|t-r|)^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+t)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}}\sum_{|I|\leq\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor+1}\|Z^{I}\partial u(t,x)\|_{L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

(1.20)

where
Z\in\{\partial_{t},x_{i}\partial_{j}-x_{j}\partial_{i},x_{i}\partial_{t}+t\partial_{i},1\leq i,j\leq d,t\partial_{t}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{d}x_{k}\partial_{k}\}
and

r=|x|=\sqrt{(x_{1})^{2}+\cdots+(x_{d})^{2}}
.
In order to apply the vector field method and (
1.20
) to study problem (
1.1
), the weighted norm

\begin{split}\|(1+|x|)^{2}(\nabla u_{0},u_{1})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}<\infty\end{split}

(1.21)

should be satisfied. However, for the initial data
(u_{0},u_{1})(x)
in (
1.6
) with
\mu\in(0,1)
,
(
1.21
) may be violated in general. Meanwhile,
these vector fields in (
1.20
) are not suitable for the wave systems with different wave speeds.

On the other hand, the following KSS-type estimates (see
[
21
, Prop. 2.1]
or (1.7)–(1.8) on page 190 of
[
30
]
)

\begin{split}&(\ln(2+t))^{-1/2}\big(\|\langle{x}\rangle^{-1/2}\partial v\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{2}([0,t]\times\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}+\|\langle{x}\rangle^{-3/2}v\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{2}([0,t]\times\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}\big)\\
&\lesssim\|\partial v(0,x)\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\Box v(s,x)\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}ds\end{split}

(1.22)

or

\begin{split}&\|\langle{x}\rangle^{-1/2^{-}}\partial v\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{2}([0,t]\times\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}+\|\langle{x}\rangle^{-3/2^{-}}v\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{2}([0,t]\times\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}\\
&\lesssim\|\partial v(0,x)\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\Box v(s,x)\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}ds\end{split}

(1.23)

cannot be utilized to study problem (
1.1
) directly, where
\langle{x}\rangle=(1+|x|^{2})^{1/2}
.
Indeed, by virtue of (
1.23
) and Theorem
3.1
below, when the weight
\mu

in (
1.6
) is less than
1/2
or
\mu=0
,
note that the terms in the nonlinearity of (
1.1
)

\begin{split}\partial^{\leq 1}u\partial^{\leq 1}u\partial u=\underbrace{\langle{x}\rangle^{1/4^{+}}\partial^{\leq 1}u}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\notin L^{4}([0,t];L^{\infty})\\
\text{due to Theorem \ref {thm:Weighted Strichartz estimate}}\end{subarray}}\underbrace{\langle{x}\rangle^{1/4^{+}}\partial^{\leq 1}u}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\notin L^{4}([0,t];L^{\infty})\\
\text{due to Theorem \ref {thm:Weighted Strichartz estimate}}\end{subarray}}\underbrace{\langle{x}\rangle^{-1/2^{-}}\partial u}_{\in L^{2}([0,t];L^{2})}\notin L^{1}([0,t];L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}))\quad\text{hold},\end{split}

then it seems difficult for us to obtain the uniform energy estimate of the solution
u
to (
1.1
) when
\mu
in (
1.6
) is sufficiently small or
\mu=0
.

To overcome those difficulties mentioned above, instead of the usual
L^{\infty}-L^{2}
type estimate in (
1.20
) and the KSS estimates in (
1.22
)–(
1.23
), we establish a series of new
L^{\infty}-L^{2}
estimates (see Subsection
3.1
)
and weakly space-time weighted Strichartz estimates (see Theorem
3.1
)
based on the strong Huygens’ principle for the 3D linear wave equation.
Without loss of generality, in order to illustrate the proofs of Theorems
1.1
–
1.2
more explicitly, it suffices to
take the cubic semilinear form
\Box u=\partial u\cdot\partial u\cdot\partial u
in (
1.1
) for instance.
At first, we start with the proof of Theorem
1.1
. To obtain the long time
H^{N}
norm
of solution derivatives
\partial u
, by the standard energy estimates, the following condition is required

\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial u(s)\cdot\partial u(s)\cdot\partial u(s)\|_{H_{x}^{N}}ds<\infty.

(1.24)

In this case, one expects that

\underbrace{\partial u}_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{\infty}}\underbrace{\partial u}_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{\infty}}\underbrace{\partial u}_{L_{t}^{\infty}H_{x}^{N}}\in L_{t}^{1}H_{x}^{N}.

(1.25)

However, the endpoint
L^{2}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x}
Strichartz estimate fails for the 3D linear wave equation. This means that (
1.25
) may not hold for all
t\in[0,\infty)
, and only almost global solutions may be obtained for the initial data (
1.4
)
with small Sobolev norms.

Secondly, we turn to the proof of Theorem
1.2
. In this process, as mentioned above, it is essential to verify condition (
1.24
).
Although the classical endpoint
L^{2}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x}
Strichartz estimate is absent, inspired by
[
17
, Lemma 5.3]
,
one may derive some weighted Strichartz estimates instead (see Theorem
3.1
below).
In this case, condition (
1.24
) follows from

\begin{split}|\partial u\partial u\partial u|\leq\underbrace{|A_{\frac{1}{2}\mu^{-}}\partial u|}_{L^{2}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x}}\underbrace{|A_{\frac{1}{2}\mu^{-}}\partial u|}_{L^{2}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x}}\underbrace{|\partial u|}_{L^{\infty}_{t}H_{x}^{N}}\in L_{t}^{1}H_{x}^{N},\end{split}

where the space-time weight
A_{\nu}(t,x)=(1+t+|x|)^{\nu}
for
\nu>0
.
Therefore, the key step is to show that
A_{\frac{1}{2}\mu^{-}}\partial u\in L^{2}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x}
. In this procedure,
it is essential to establish (see the details in Sections
3
–
4
)

\int_{0}^{t}\|A_{\mu}(s,x)\partial u\cdot\partial u\cdot\partial u\|_{L_{x}^{2}}ds<\infty.

(1.26)

This will be achieved by

A_{\mu}\partial u\partial u\partial u=\underbrace{A_{\frac{1}{2}\mu^{-}}\partial u}_{\in L_{t}^{2^{+}}L_{x}^{\infty^{-}}}\underbrace{A_{\frac{1}{2}\mu^{-}}\partial u}_{\in L_{t}^{2^{+}}L_{x}^{\infty^{-}}}\underbrace{A_{0^{+}}\partial u}_{\in L_{t}^{\infty^{-}}L_{x}^{2^{+}}}\in L^{1}([0,t];L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))

and the conclusions of
A_{\frac{1}{2}\mu^{-}}\partial u\in L_{t}^{2^{+}}L_{x}^{\infty^{-}}
and
A_{0^{+}}\partial u\in L_{t}^{\infty^{-}}L_{x}^{2^{+}}

derived from Theorem
3.1
.

Finally, for the cubic quasilinear problem (
1.1
), we can obtain such estimates (see Section
4
):

\|\partial u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{N}_{x}}\lesssim\|\partial u(0)\|_{H^{N}_{x}}+\|\partial^{\leq 1}u\|_{A_{0}\tilde{L}^{2}_{t}B^{1+\delta}_{\infty,1}}^{2}\|\partial u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{N}_{x}},

(1.27)

\begin{split}\|\partial^{\leq 1}u\|_{A_{\frac{1}{2}\mu^{-}}\tilde{L}^{2}_{t}B^{1+\delta}_{\infty,1}}\lesssim\|\langle{x}\rangle^{\mu}\partial^{\leq 3}_{x}\partial u(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|\partial u\|_{A_{0^{+}}\tilde{L}^{\infty^{-}}_{t}B^{3+\mu^{-}}_{2^{+},1}}\|\partial^{\leq 1}u\|_{A_{\frac{1}{2}\mu^{-}}\tilde{L}^{2^{+}}_{t}B^{2+\mu^{-}}_{\infty^{-},1}}^{2},\end{split}

(1.28)

\begin{split}\|\partial u\|_{A_{0^{+}}\tilde{L}^{\infty^{-}}_{t}B^{3+\mu^{-}}_{2^{+},1}}\lesssim\|\langle{x}\rangle^{\mu}\partial^{\leq 4}_{x}\partial u(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|\partial^{\leq 1}u\|_{A_{0^{+}}\tilde{L}^{2}_{t}B^{1+\delta}_{\infty,1}}^{2}\|\partial u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{N}_{x}},\end{split}

(1.29)

\begin{split}\|\partial^{\leq 1}u\|_{A_{\frac{1}{2}\mu^{-}}\tilde{L}^{2^{+}}_{t}B^{2+\mu^{-}}_{\infty^{-},1}}\lesssim\|\langle{x}\rangle^{\mu}\partial^{\leq 5}_{x}\partial u(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|\partial^{\leq 1}u\|_{A_{\frac{1}{2}\mu^{-}}\tilde{L}^{2}_{t}B^{1+\delta}_{\infty,1}}^{2}\|\partial u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{N}_{x}},\end{split}

(1.30)

where
\mu\in(0,1)
,

,

, and

\|f\|_{A_{\nu}\tilde{L}^{p}_{t}B^{s}_{r,1}}=\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq-1}2^{sk}\|A_{\nu}(s,x)P_{k}f\|_{L^{p}([0,t];L^{r})}

1.6

the global uniform smallness of

From this, the global existence result in Theorem

can be shown by the continuity argument.

\end{split}

(2.2)

Lemma 2.4
.

For
\beta\in(-3/2,3/2)
, it holds

2^{j\beta}\|Q_{j}P_{k}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}\lesssim\|\langle{x}\rangle^{\beta}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}\lesssim\big\|2^{j\beta}\|Q_{j}P_{k}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}\big\|_{\ell^{1}_{k}\ell^{1}_{j}}.

(2.3)

Proof.

It is obvious that
P_{k}
is a bounded operator from
L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})
to
L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})

\|P_{k}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}

Since

belongs to the
A_{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})

A.2
),
one achieves

\|\langle{x}\rangle^{\beta}P_{k}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}\lesssim\|\langle{x}\rangle^{\beta}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}.

This yields

2^{j\beta}\|Q_{j}P_{k}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}\lesssim\|\langle{x}\rangle^{\beta}P_{k}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}\lesssim\|\langle{x}\rangle^{\beta}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}.

Thus, the first inequality in (

The second inequality in (
2.3

Proof.

2.3
The strong Huygens’ principle

Consider the Cauchy problem of the 3D linear wave equation

\left\{\begin{aligned} \square u=&F(t,x),\quad(t,x)\in[0,+\infty)\times\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3},\\
(u,\partial_{t}&u)(0,x)=(u_{0},u_{1})(x),\quad x\in\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.33)

It follows from the spatial Fourier transformation on (
2.33
) and Kirchhoff’s formula that

\displaystyle u(t,x)

\displaystyle=\cos(t|\nabla|)u_{0}(x)+\frac{\sin(t|\nabla|)}{|\nabla|}u_{1}(x)+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\sin\big((t-s)|\nabla|\big)}{|\nabla|}F(s,x)ds

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\big[\frac{1}{4\pi t}\int_{\left|x-y\right|=t}u_{0}(y)\mathrm{d}\sigma_{y}\big]+\frac{1}{4\pi t}\int_{\left|x-y\right|=t}u_{1}(y)\mathrm{d}\sigma_{y}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{4\pi(t-s)}\int_{\left|x-y\right|=t-s}F(s,y)\mathrm{d}\sigma_{y}ds.

Meanwhile,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u(t,x)

\displaystyle=-\sin(t|\nabla|)|\nabla|u_{0}(x)+\cos(t|\nabla|)u_{1}(x)+\int_{0}^{t}\cos\big((t-s)|\nabla|\big)F(s,x)ds

and

\displaystyle\nabla u(t,x)

\displaystyle=\cos(t|\nabla|)\nabla u_{0}(x)+\frac{\sin(t|\nabla|)}{|\nabla|}\nabla u_{1}(x)+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\sin\big((t-s)|\nabla|\big)}{|\nabla|}\nabla F(s,x)ds.

By virtue of the strong Huygens’ principle for the 3D linear wave equation, for any fixed point
(t_{0},x_{0})\in\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits_{+}\times\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}
,

\begin{split}\frac{\sin(t_{0}|\nabla|)}{|\nabla|}f(x_{0})=&\frac{1}{4\pi t_{0}}\int_{S(x_{0},t_{0})}f(y)\mathrm{d}\sigma_{y}\\
=&\frac{1}{4\pi t_{0}}\int_{S(x_{0},t_{0})}\Xi_{S(x_{0},t_{0})}(y)f(y)\mathrm{d}\sigma_{y}\\
=&\frac{\sin(t_{0}|\nabla|)}{|\nabla|}(\Xi_{S(x_{0},t_{0})}f)(x_{0}),\\
\end{split}

(2.34)

\begin{split}\cos(t_{0}|\nabla|)f(x_{0})=&\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\big[\frac{1}{4\pi t}\int_{\left|x-y\right|=t}f(y)\mathrm{d}\sigma_{y}\big]\big|_{(t,x)=(t_{0},x_{0})}\\
=&\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\big[\frac{t}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}f(x+tz)\mathrm{d}\sigma_{z}\big]\big|_{(t,x)=(t_{0},x_{0})}\\
=&\frac{1}{4\pi t_{0}^{2}}\int_{S(x_{0},t_{0})}f(y)\mathrm{d}\sigma_{y}+\frac{1}{4\pi t_{0}}\int_{S(x_{0},t_{0})}\frac{\partial f}{\partial n}(y)\mathrm{d}\sigma_{y}\\
=&\cos(t_{0}|\nabla|)(\Xi_{S(x_{0},t_{0})}f)(x_{0}),\\
\end{split}

(2.35)

\begin{split}-\sin(t_{0}|\nabla|)|\nabla|f(x_{0})=&-\sin(t_{0}|\nabla|)|\nabla|(\Xi_{S(x_{0},t_{0})}f)(x_{0}),\\
\end{split}

(2.36)

where for
t\geq 1
the cutoff function
\Xi_{S(x,t)}
is defined as

\Xi_{S(x,t)}(y)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &1,\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad t-0.01\leq|y-x|\leq t+0.01,\\
&\in[0,1],\quad\text{smooth, \quad otherwise},\\
&0,\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad|y-x|\leq t-0.02\quad\text{or}\ |y-x|\geq t+0.02,\end{aligned}\right.

and for
t<1
,

\Xi_{S(x,t)}(y)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &1,\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad|y-x|\leq t+0.01,\\
&\in[0,1],\quad\text{smooth, \quad otherwise},\\
&0,\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad|y-x|\geq t+0.02.\end{aligned}\right.

&\lesssim\sum_{j\geq-1}\sum_{|a+b|\leq 4}(1+t)^{-1}2^{j}\|\partial_{x}^{a}(\psi_{j})\partial_{x}^{b}\dot{P}_{0}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}\\
&\lesssim\sum_{j\geq-1}\sum_{|a+b|\leq 4}(1+t)^{-1}2^{(1-|a|)j}\|\partial_{x}^{b}\dot{P}_{0}f\|_{L^{2}(\supp\psi_{j})}\\
&\lesssim\sum_{j\geq-1}2^{-\delta j}\sum_{|b|\leq 4}(1+t)^{-1}\|\langle{x}\rangle^{1+\delta}\partial_{x}^{b}\dot{P}_{0}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}\\
&\lesssim\sum_{|b|\leq 4}(1+t)^{-1}\|\langle{x}\rangle^{1+\delta}\partial_{x}^{b}\dot{P}_{0}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}.\\
\end{split}

By Lemma
A.7
and
\langle{x}\rangle^{2+2\delta}\in A_{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})
, one has

\|\dot{P}_{0}\frac{\sin(t|\nabla|)}{|\nabla|}f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}\lesssim(1+t)^{-1}\|\langle{x}\rangle^{1+\delta}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}\lesssim(1+t)^{-1}\langle{R}\rangle^{1+\delta}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}.

Similarly, it holds that

\|\dot{P}_{0}\cos(t|\nabla|)f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}\lesssim(1+t)^{-1}\langle{R}\rangle^{1+\delta}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}.

Note that

\begin{split}\|\dot{P}_{k}\frac{\sin(t|\nabla|)}{|\nabla|}f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}&=\|\dot{P}_{0}\frac{\sin(2^{k}t|\nabla|)}{2^{k}|\nabla|}(f(2^{-k}\cdot))\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}\\
&\lesssim 2^{-k}(1+2^{k}t)^{-1}\langle{2^{k}R}\rangle^{1+\delta}\|f(2^{-k}\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}\\

Analogously, one can arrive at

Then the proof of Lemma
3.4
is completed.
∎

\begin{split}&(1+t)^{\mu^{-}}\|P_{k}\partial^{\leq 1}u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\\
&\lesssim 2^{(\frac{3}{2}+\mu)k}\big[\|\langle{x}\rangle^{\mu}P_{k}\partial u(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\int_{0}^{t}\big\|A_{\mu-20\delta}(\tau,x)P_{k}G(u,\partial u,\partial_{x}\partial u)(\tau)\big\|_{L^{2}_{x}}d\tau\big].\end{split}

(6.23)

By summing over
k
in (
6.23
), utilizing Lemma
A.7
, Lemma
4.4
and (
6.15
)–(
6.16
), one arrives at

\begin{split}&(1+t)^{\mu^{-}}\|\partial^{\leq 1}u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\\
&\lesssim\sum_{k\geq-1}2^{(\frac{3}{2}+\mu)k}\big[\|\langle{x}\rangle^{\mu}P_{k}\partial u(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\int_{0}^{t}\big\|A_{\mu-20\delta}(\tau,x)P_{k}G(u,\partial u,\partial_{x}\partial u)(\tau)\big\|_{L^{2}_{x}}d\tau\big]\\
&\lesssim\sum_{|a|\leq 3}\|\langle{x}\rangle^{\mu}\partial^{a}_{x}\partial u(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\varepsilon^{3}\\
&\lesssim\varepsilon.\end{split}

Then the proof of Theorem
1.2
is completed.

Appendix A
Introduction to
A_{2}
weights

For the reader’s convenience, in this section we give a brief introduction to some
A_{2}
weight inequalities.
For further properties of
A_{p}
weights, we refer the reader to
[
12
, Chapter 7]
and
[
36
, Chapter V]

Definition A.1
.

A non-negative weight function
w
is said to be of class
A_{2}
if

[w]_{A_{2}}=\sup_{\text{all cubes $Q$ in }\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w(x)dx\big)\big(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w(x)^{-1}dx\big)<\infty.

(A.1)

Lemma A.2
.

|x|^{\alpha},\ \langle{x}\rangle^{\alpha}\in A_{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})
if and only if
\alpha\in(-3,3)
.

Proof.

For
|x|^{\alpha}
, the related result is established in
[
36
, Chapter V, p. 218]
.

For
\langle x\rangle^{\alpha}
,
let
Q
be a cube with center
x_{0}
and diagonal length
2r
(so
Q\subseteq B(x_{0},r)
), we consider the following two cases.

Case 1.

r\leq|x_{0}|/2

For all
x\in Q
, we have
\frac{1}{2}|x_{0}|\leq|x|\leq\frac{3}{2}|x_{0}|
, hence
\langle x\rangle\approx\langle x_{0}\rangle
. Thus

\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}\langle x\rangle^{\alpha}\,dx\approx\langle x_{0}\rangle^{\alpha},\quad\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}\langle x\rangle^{-\alpha}\,dx\approx\langle x_{0}\rangle^{-\alpha},

and their product is bounded by a uniform constant, which fulfills the
A_{2}
condition.

Case 2.

r\geq|x_{0}|/2

In this case,
Q\subset B(x_{0},r)\subset B(0,3r)
holds.
Due to
\alpha\in(-3,3)
, one then has

\int_{Q}\langle x\rangle^{\alpha}\,dx\lesssim(3r)^{\alpha+3},\quad\int_{Q}\langle x\rangle^{-\alpha}\,dx\lesssim(3r)^{-\alpha+3}.

Multiplying these gives an upper bound
Cr^{6}
and subsequently being divided by
|Q|^{2}\approx r^{6}
yield the desired uniform bound.

If
\alpha\notin(-3,3)
, it is easy to verify
[\langle{x}\rangle^{\alpha}]_{A_{2}}=\infty
, which completes the proof of Lemma
A.2
.
∎

Definition A.3
.

Let
0<\delta,A<\infty
. A function
K(x,y)
defined for
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
x\neq y
is called a standard kernel (with constants
\delta
and
A
) if

|K(x,y)|\leq\frac{A}{|x-y|^{n}},\quad x\neq y,

(A.2)

and whenever
|x-x^{\prime}|\leq\frac{1}{2}\max\left(|x-y|,|x^{\prime}-y|\right)
, it holds

|K(x,y)-K(x^{\prime},y)|\leq\frac{A|x-x^{\prime}|^{\delta}}{\left(|x-y|+|x^{\prime}-y|\right)^{n+\delta}};

(A.3)

when
|y-y^{\prime}|\leq\frac{1}{2}\max\left(|x-y|,|x-y^{\prime}|\right)
, one has

|K(x,y)-K(x,y^{\prime})|\leq\frac{A|y-y^{\prime}|^{\delta}}{\left(|x-y|+|x-y^{\prime}|\right)^{n+\delta}}.

(A.4)

The class of all kernels that satisfy (
A.2
)-(
A.4
) is denoted by
SK(\delta,A)
.

Definition A.4
.

Let
0<\delta,A<\infty
and
K\in SK(\delta,A)
. A Calderón-Zygmund operator
T
associated with
K
is defined as

T(f)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}K(x,y)f(y)\,dy,

(A.5)

which fulfills

\|T(f)\|_{L^{2}}\leq B\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

(A.6)

All Calderón–Zygmund operators
T
form the space
CZO(\delta,A,B)
.

Lemma A.5
.

Let
A,B,\beta>0
and
T\in CZO(\beta,A,B)
. Then there is a constant
C=C(\beta,[w]_{A_{2}})
such that
for all
w\in A_{2}
and
f\in L^{2}(w)
,

\|T(f)\|_{L^{2}(w)}\leq C\,(A+B)\|f\|_{L^{2}(w)}.

Proof.

See
[
12
, Theorem 7.4.6]
.
∎

Lemma A.6
.

Let
w\in A_{2}
and
R_{j}
be the
j
-th Riesz transformation for
j=1,2,3
. There exists a constant
C=C([w]_{A_{2}})>0
such that
for any
f\in L^{2}(w)
,

\displaystyle\|R_{j}f\|_{L^{2}(w)}\leq C([w]_{A_{2}})\|f\|_{L^{2}(w)}.

(A.7)

Proof.

Since the Riesz transformation is a Calderón-Zygmund operator, (
A.7
) is shown by Lemma
A.5
.
∎

Lemma A.7
.

Let
w\in A_{2}
, and
k\in\mathop{\mathbb{Z}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits
. Then there exists a constant
C=C([w]_{A_{2}})>0
such that for any
f\in L^{2}(w)
,

\|\dot{P}_{k}f\|_{L^{2}(w)}\leq C([w]_{A_{2}})2^{-k}\|\dot{P}_{k}\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(w)}

and

\begin{split}\|\dot{P}_{k}\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(w)}\leq C([w]_{A_{2}})2^{k}\|\dot{P}_{k}f\|_{L^{2}(w)}.\end{split}

(A.8)

Proof.

When
k=0
, it follows from Bernstein’s inequality that

\|\dot{P}_{0}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}=\|\dot{P}_{0}|\nabla|^{-1}R\cdot\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}\lesssim\|\dot{P}_{0}\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}.

Since
\dot{P}_{0}|\nabla|^{-1}R
is a Calderón-Zygmund operator, then by Lemma
A.5
, one has

\|\dot{P}_{0}f\|_{L^{2}(w)}\leq C_{1}([w]_{A_{2}})\cdot\|\dot{P}_{0}\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(w)}.

In addition, due to
[w(2^{-k}\cdot)]_{A_{2}}=[w]_{A_{2}}
, then we arrive at

\begin{split}\|\dot{P}_{k}f\|_{L^{2}(w)}=&2^{-\frac{3}{2}k}\|\dot{P}_{0}(f(2^{-k}\cdot))\|_{L^{2}(w(2^{-k}\cdot))}\\
\leq&2^{-\frac{3}{2}k}C_{1}([w(2^{-k}\cdot)]_{A_{2}})\|\dot{P}_{0}\nabla(f(2^{-k}\cdot))\|_{L^{2}(w(2^{-k}\cdot))}\\
\leq&C([w]_{A_{2}})2^{-k}\|\dot{P}_{k}\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(w)}.\end{split}

(A.9)

Analogously, the proof of (
A.8
) can be completed as for (
A.9
).
∎
