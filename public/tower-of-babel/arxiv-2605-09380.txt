A reciprocity theorem of Robinson-Benson-Webb for finite-dimensional symmetric algebras
Shigeo Koshitani

Abstract
. We generalize the reciprocity theorem of G.R. Robinson, D. Benson and P. Webb between a finite group and its subgroup to the case of finite-dimensional symmetric algebras over a field which are connected by a bimodule for the two algebras.

20C20, 16D50

1Introduction

In his paper
[
Rob89
]
, G.R. Robinson presents a reciprocity theorem for
simple/projective modules over the group algebras
kG
and
kH
where

k
is a field and

H
is a subgroup of a finite group
G

(for the precise statement see Corollary
1.2
).

Our main theorem stated below claims that Robinson’s reciprocity theorem is extended for
finite-dimensional
k
-algebras
A
and
B
,
and for an
(A,B)
-bimodule
M
instead of
kG
and
kH
,
provided
A
and
B
both are symmetric algebras and
M
is projective as a left
A
-module
and also as a right
B
-module.
Actually the main theorem should work nicely when
we look for a kind of equivalence between the module categories of
A
-modules and
B
-modules
via the
(A,B)
-bimodule
M
. Now, our main result is:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field, and
let
A
and
B
be finite-dimensional
symmetric

k
-algebras.
Further, let
M
be an
(A,B)
-bimodule
such that
M
is projective as a left
A
-module and also as a right
B
-module.
Then, for any simple right
A
-module
S
and any simple right
B
-module
T
,

[P(T)\,|\,S\otimes_{A}M]^{B}=[P(S)\,|\,T\otimes_{B}M^{*}]^{A}

where
{M^{*}}\!\!:=\mathrm{Hom}_{k}(M,k)
is the
k
-dual of
M
,
P(S)
and
P(T)
are the
projective covers of
S
and
T
, respectively, and
[P\,|\,U]^{A}
for a projective
right
A
-module
P
and a right
A
-module
U
denotes the multiplicity of
P
as a direct
summand of
U
.

Then, we immediately get original Robinson’s reciprocity formula by applying
Theorem
1.1

to the case that

A:=kG
,
B:=kH
and
M:=(kG){\downarrow}^{G\times G}_{G\times H}

where the last term means the restriction of
kG
as a
k(G\times G)
-module
to
k(G\times H)
-module.

Corollary 1.2
.

[Theorem 3 in
[
Rob89
]
and Lemma 5.2 in
[
Gro02
]
]
Let
H
be a subgroup of a finite group
G
, and let
S
and
T
, respectively, be
a simple
kG
-module and a simple
kH
-module. Then,

[P(T)\,|\,S{\downarrow}_{H}]^{kH}=[P(S)\,|\,T{\uparrow}^{G}]^{kG}

where
{\downarrow}_{H}
and
{\uparrow}^{G}
, respectively, mean the restriction to
H

and the induction to
G
.

Remark 1.3
.

Footnote 1 on p.108 of
[
Rob89
]
says that
D. Benson and P. Webb have known the same result as Corollary
1.2
.

2The main theorem

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We can write

T\otimes_{B}M^{*}=\Omega^{0}(T\otimes_{B}M^{*})\oplus(\oplus_{S^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Irr}_{k}(A)}\,m_{S^{\prime}}\times P(S^{\prime}))\text{ for integers }m_{S^{\prime}}\geq 0

and

S\otimes_{A}M=\Omega^{0}(S\otimes_{A}M)\oplus(\oplus_{T^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Irr}_{k}(B)}\,n_{T^{\prime}}\times P(T^{\prime}))\text{ for integers }n_{T^{\prime}}\geq 0.

These imply that

(1)

\mathrm{dim}_{k}\,\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(S,T\otimes_{B}M^{*})=\mathrm{dim}_{k}\,\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(S,\Omega^{0}(T\otimes_{B}M^{*}))+m_{S}

and

(2)

\mathrm{dim}_{k}\,\mathrm{Hom}_{B}(S\otimes_{A}M,T)=\mathrm{dim}_{k}\,\mathrm{Hom}_{B}(\Omega^{0}(S\otimes_{A}M),T)+n_{T}\,.

Now, it follows that as
k
-spaces

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(S,\Omega^{0}(T\otimes_{B}M^{*}))

\displaystyle\cong\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{A}(S,\Omega^{0}(T\otimes_{B}M^{*}))\text{ by \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Lin18}{}{}, Corollary 4.13.4]} }

\displaystyle=\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{A}(S,T\otimes_{B}M^{*}))\text{ by the definitions of }\Omega^{0}\text{ and }\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}

\displaystyle\cong\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{B}(S\otimes_{A}M,T)\text{ by \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Lin18}{}{}, Proposition 2.15.5]} }

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{B}(\Omega^{0}(S\otimes_{A}M),T)\text{\ \ just as above,}

namely,

(3)

\quad\mathrm{dim}_{k}\,\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(S,\Omega^{0}(T\otimes_{B}M^{*}))=\mathrm{dim}_{k}\,\mathrm{Hom}_{B}(\Omega^{0}(S\otimes_{A}M),T).

On the other hand,
[
Lin18
, Theorem 2.12.7]
implies that

(4)

\mathrm{dim}_{k}\,\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(S,T\otimes_{B}M^{*})=\mathrm{dim}_{k}\,\mathrm{Hom}_{B}(S\otimes_{A}M,T).

Hence

\displaystyle m_{S}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{dim}_{k}\,\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(S,T\otimes_{B}M^{*})-\mathrm{dim}_{k}\,\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(S,\Omega^{0}(T\otimes_{B}M^{*}))\text{ \ \ by (\ref{m_S})}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{dim}_{k}\,\mathrm{Hom}_{B}(S\otimes_{A}M,T)-\mathrm{dim}_{k}\,\mathrm{Hom}_{B}(\Omega^{0}(S\otimes_{A}M),T)\text{ \ \ by (\ref{adjoint4}) and (\ref{adjoint3})}

\displaystyle=n_{T}\text{ \ \ by (\ref{n_T})}.

This completes the proof.
∎
