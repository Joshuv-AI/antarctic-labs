Hydraulic-Mechanical-Damage Multifield Coupling Control Mechanism and Gas Production Prediction of Fracturing in Tectonically Deformed Coal Seam
Authors: Zhihua Yan, Honggao Xie, Shuxun Sang, Xiaozhi Zhou, Shiqi Liu, Sijie Han, Fansheng Huang, Wenkai Wang, Jie Chen, Bingren Jiang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Efficient coalbed methane (CBM) development through hydraulic fracturing in tectonically deformed coal seams is governed by the multifield coupling of the hydraulic (H), mechanical (M), and damage (D), and it is urgent to build a data-driven gas production prediction model. Using multifield coupled numerical simulation, this study systematically analyzes the effects of elastic modulus (E), initial permeability (k 0), and fracturing displacement rate (H f) on the fracturing-induced damaged area (D A), maximum postfracturing permeability (k m‑max), and gas production. The applicability of three models of support vector machine (SVM), random forest (RF), and generalized regression neural network (GRNN) in gas production prediction is analyzed. The results show that the initial permeability and fracturing displacement rate have significant positive effects on gas production, increasing cumulative gas production by 341.44% and 48.47%, respectively. The elastic modulus exhibits a negative effect, reducing cumulative gas production by 22.21%. Postfracturing damaged area and permeability also show significant positive effects. The RF model demonstrates the best prediction performance on both the training and test sets, achieving R2 values of 0.9871 and 0.9551, respectively, with error metrics significantly lower than those of the SVM and GRNN models. The RF model is therefore more suitable for gas production prediction in CBM fracturing wells under multifeature parameter conditions. The HMD multifield coupling control mechanism+machine learning drive fusion gas production prediction method realized the technical path from physical mechanism revelation to engineering prediction of hydraulic fracturing coalbed methane development in structurally deformed coal seams.

Introduction

As a key component of clean energy, the efficient exploration, development, and utilization of coalbed methane (CBM) are of great significance to China’s energy structure adjustment and the achievement of its “dual carbon” goals. − However, over 60% of shallow CBM resources in China occur in tectonically deformed coal seams. As these coal seams are structurally fractured, have low strength and poor permeability, traditional development technologies struggle to achieve economically viable extraction. , As a key means to improve the permeability of coal-bearing gas reservoirs, hydraulic fracturing technology has promoted the rapid growth of unconventional oil and gas resources. − In hydraulic fracturing operations targeting tectonically deformed coal seams, through-layer fracturing via hard roof strata is commonly adopted. − However, when the water pressure is applied to the tectonically deformed coal seams, it faces the engineering dilemma of “unable to open, unable to support, and poor gas production effect”. At the heart of this challenge lies the fracture propagation behavior and permeability response mechanism governed by the multifield coupling of the hydraulic field (H), mechanical field (M), and damage field (D) during hydraulic fracturing, as well as the CBM productivity prediction models and methods under such multifield coupling. Therefore, it is essential to develop machine learning-based gas production prediction approaches that combine physical consistency with data adaptability.

Currently, exploration of coal seam fracturing engineering and CBM enhancement methods has evolved from an early focus on a single mechanical field to a stage that considers thermal-hydraulic-mechanical-chemical multifield coupling. − However, existing studies have mostly concentrated on relatively structurally intact high-rank coals, while insufficient attention has been paid to the unique characteristics of tectonically deformed coal seams, such as strong heterogeneity, significant plastic deformation, and complex fracture network generation. , The hydraulic-mechanical-damage (HMD) coupling process during hydraulic fracturing of shallow tectonically deformed coal seam represents a complex system full of uncertainties: the evolution of the seepage (hydraulic) field governs the pressure distribution within fractures and alters the pore structure and permeability of coal through gas adsorption–desorption, mechanical, and damage effects; the response of the mechanical field directly controls the morphology and effectiveness of the fracture network; and the damage field from microdefect initiation to macro-crack penetration is a “dark line” that runs through, reversely coupling and reshaping the state of the other two fields. Through a series of nonlinear constitutive relations, such as stress-permeability coupling and damage-permeability coupling, these three physical fields greatly amplify the small disturbance, resulting in complex behaviors that are difficult to accurately predict by traditional deterministic models. It is this complexity that provides an excellent opportunity and strong demand for the intervention of data-driven methods such as machine learning. For example, an interpretable machine learning model of the causal relationship between explicit parameters and coalbed methane well production during hydraulic fracturing. Furthermore, multimodal data neural networks have been successfully applied to predicting reservoir fracture propagation paths, while convolutional neural network models have been used to predict rock parameters such as elastic properties, permeability, and stress and damage. Traditional mechanistic models (based on physical equations) and emerging data science are not substitutes for each other; rather, they are inevitably moving toward integration. Machine learning models, for instance, can extract potential laws and associative features not yet described by explicit physical equations from massive amounts of experimental data, field monitoring data, and numerical simulation data.

In the realm of gas production prediction, an increasing number of scholars are considering the influence of reservoir parameters and postfracturing permeability evolution under multifield coupling on production performance. A series of discrete element, finite element, and combined discrete-finite element experimental or numerical simulation studies have been conducted. − Using software such as COMSOL Multiphysics, researchers have investigated the controlling effects of HMD evolution on production during hydraulic fracturing. , With the rapid advancement of artificial intelligence, models such as GA-BP neural networks and GRNN have been employed to predict CBM production under the influence of multiple geological parameters. , Various models have achieved considerable progress in predicting gas production from CBM wells. Furthermore, some scholars have integrated machine learning models such as SVM and MLP to accurately predict gas drainage rates. and have utilized RF and LSTM models to predict gas adsorption–desorption capacity of coal. , Postfracturing gas production efficiency depends not only on fracture geometry but is also governed by the complex fracture network formed under multifield coupling, gas adsorption-diffusion-seepage processes, and competitive adsorption and displacement between water and methane in micronanopores. However, both fracture propagation during hydraulic fracturing of tectonically deformed coal and postfracturing gas production prediction are difficult to accurately describe using traditional models. Therefore, integrating machine learning with HMD-coupled mechanism models holds the promise of unifying the interpretability of multifield coupling mechanisms with high-precision gas production prediction, potentially bringing a paradigm shift to gas production forecasting for hydraulic fracturing in tectonically deformed coal seams.

Reviewing previous research, solid foundations have been established in understanding the multifield coupling mechanisms of HMD during hydraulic fracturing and in data-driven CBM production prediction using multiple factors. This paper deepens the understanding of the HMD coupling control mechanisms and key gas production controlling factors in shallow, tectonically deformed coal seams through multifield coupled numerical simulations and machine learning methods. The rest of the paper is as follows: Section constructs a HMD coupling model and a machine learning model for a shallow, tectonically deformed coal seam, and preliminarily defines the numerical simulation scheme as well as the steps of the multifield coupling + machine learning prediction approach. Section presents numerical simulations and engineering gas production validation, generating multifactor coupled data sets for CBM extraction under different simulation cases. The physically constrained simulation results represented by the HMD governing equations (e.g., different Fracturing displacement, damage areas, initial permeability, postfracturing permeability) are embedded into machine learning models. Various machine learning algorithms (e.g., SVM, RF, and GRNN) are explored for their significance in predicting fracturing outcomes and production performance, and the optimal combination of features is selected to develop a prediction model integrating “physical mechanism interpretation + machine learning.” Section discusses and analyzes the engineering application value of multifield coupling theory and the data-driven hydraulic fracturing gas production prediction method. Section presents brief conclusions.

Methodology

HMD Multifield Coupling Theory and Gas Production Model Construction

HMD Multifield Coupling Theory of Broken Soft Coal Seam

In the hydraulic fracturing of a shallow broken soft coal seam and the drainage and drainage engineering of CBM, under the interaction between in situ stress and fluid pressure, the coal matrix undergoes elastic-plastic deformation, leading to fracture propagation or closure. This gradually evolves into damaged and undamaged zones, altering the seepage pathways and the space available for fluid migration. Figure illustrates a porous medium fluid flow model based on the damage evolution process in the coal. Based on this, the present study incorporates hydraulic fracture propagation in coal seams, gas–water migration during drainage, and methane adsorption/desorption, and proposes the following model assumptions: (1) The coal is treated as a poroelastic continuum composed of matrix pores and fractures. (2) The coal reservoir fractures are saturated with either water or CH4. (3) Methane in the coal matrix pores is predominantly in an adsorbed state, and the adsorption–desorption process follows the Langmuir eq . CH4 is considered an ideal gas; after desorption, it diffuses from the coal matrix into the fractures, following Fick’s law of diffusion. (5) Under differential pressure, CH4 and water flow through the fracture pathways in the reservoir, following Darcy’s law. (6) The coal reservoir temperature is assumed to be constant. (7) The deformation and failure of the coal under stress adhere to damage mechanics theory, obeying both the maximum tensile stress criterion and the Mohr-Coulomb criterion.

Geometric Model and Engineering Simulation Scheme

Affected by tectonic activity, the main coal-producing seams in the western Guizhou mining area are characterized as fragmented, soft, and low-permeability coals, with a firmness coefficient generally below 1.5, typically ranging between 0.39 and 1. The elastic modulus of Guizhou coals is generally less than 6 GPa, and the coal seam permeability ranges from 0.0001 to 1.56 mD, with the majority falling below 0.1 mD. The engineering background of this study is the TQD1 directional well in the Tengqing coal mine, located southwest of the Bide syncline in Guizhou. Directional well-perforation fracturing (with a fracturing displacement of 8 m3/min) was applied to enhance the permeability of the no. 32 coal seam. The measured permeability of the no. 32 coal seam is 0.0057 mD, and its elastic modulus is approximately 4 GPa. COMSOL Multiphysics software was used to investigate the effects of different elastic moduli, different fracturing displacements, different initial permeabilities of the coal seam, fracture damage zone sizes, and permeabilities after fracturing on gas production in directional vertical wells. Support Vector Machine (SVM) Model

SVM is a supervised learning model based on statistical learning theory, proposed by Vapnik et al. , Its core idea is to find an optimal hyperplane that not only correctly separates samples of different classes but also maximizes the margin between the closest points of each class (the support vectors) and the hyperplane. Random Forest (RF) Model

RF is an ensemble learning model proposed by Leo Breiman. It improves overall performance by constructing multiple decision trees and aggregating their predictions. The core ideas of RF are 2-fold: sample randomness (via bootstrap sampling) and feature randomness (random selection of a subset of features at each node split). This dual randomness endows RF with strong resistance to overfitting, robustness to noise and outliers, the ability to handle high-dimensional data, and the capability to evaluate feature importance. As a result, RF is widely used in both classification and regression tasks. Its core principles are as follows:

Through bootstrap sampling, N new subsets {s 1, s 2, ···, s n } are randomly formed, each with the same sample size as the original data set. During decision tree splitting, the feature selection is based on minimizing the mean squared error for the two child nodes R1 and R2 formed by splitting at any point s corresponding to any feature A. The minimum mean squared error (M se) is given by Mse=minA,s[minc1⁡∑xi∈R1(A,s)(yi−c1)2+minc2⁡∑xi∈R2(A,s)(yi−c2)2] 29 where A is the splitting feature; s is the splitting threshold; c 1 and c 2 are the mean output values of the data sets in R1 and R2, respectively; x i is the feature value; and y i is the regression value of the model.

During forest ensemble, N corresponding regression decision trees {c 1, c 2, ···, c n } are generated to complete the construction of the random forest. For prediction output, the forest produces N response prediction results from all decision trees, and the final output is the average of the prediction results from all trees: ŷ=1n∑i=1nTi(x) 30

GRNN is a feedforward neural network model based on nonlinear regression theory proposed by Specht. It is essentially a special case of the radial basis function (RBF) network and is particularly well-suited for function approximation, regression analysis, and pattern recognition. GRNN has the advantages of a simple structure, fast convergence speed, only a few samples are needed for training, and it can handle unstable data. Results and Analysis

HMD Multifield Coupling-Based Validation of Directional Well Gas Production Simulation

To verify the consistency between the simulated gas production of the TQD1 directional fracturing well and the actual field gas production, we compared the measured production data from the well with the numerically simulated gas production, as shown in Figure . The actual production curve fluctuates relatively sharply over time, while the simulated production curve is smoother; however, the overall increasing and decreasing trends of the two curves are similar. The fitting results show that the mean absolute percentage error (MAPE) of the simulated daily gas production of the CBM well is 19.86%, and the error of the cumulative gas production over 185 days is 8.42%, indicating good agreement with the field engineering data from the actual CBM well. Therefore, the HMD multifield coupled numerical model can serve as an appropriate governing model for describing the gas production behavior of the shallow, tectonically deformed coal seam in the TQD1 directional well.

Analysis of Fracturing Damage Zone Evolution and Gas Production Simulation Results under Different Parameters

Influence of Different Parameters on Reservoir Damage and Volumetric Strain

Based on the simulation results of scheme 2, Figures and illustrate the evolution of coal damage and volumetric strain over time during fracturing under different elastic moduli, respectively. To compare damage evolution across different elastic moduli, the fracturing damage at different time points was normalized. As fracturing time increases, damage gradually increases under all elastic modulus conditions. With fracturing time extending from 0.5 to 2 h, the damaged zone expands in reservoirs with different elastic moduli; however, the regulating effect of elastic modulus is critical. In high-elastic-modulus (5–6 GPa) reservoirs, fracture propagation within the damaged zone is more extensive. By 2 h, a distinct, clustered damaged zone extending along the fractures has formed, with a larger area of high-damage values. In contrast, for low-elastic-modulus (2–3 GPa) reservoirs, the damaged zone is shorter in morphology, the fracture propagation pathway is relatively narrow, and the stimulated reservoir volume is significantly limited. In loose sandstone fracturing, low elastic modulus similarly produces short, wide fractures. This indicates that coal with a high elastic modulus possesses greater stiffness, allowing hydraulic energy to be more concentrated in driving fracture propagation along preferential pathways, thereby more readily forming a complex damage-stimulation network. This finding is consistent with the results of Jiang et al. Considering volumetric strain during fracturing, it also increases with time. In the early fracturing stage (0.5 h), low-elastic-modulus (E = 2 GPa) coal exhibits significant positive (dilatational) volumetric strain (approximately 0.0063). In the late fracturing stage (2 h), the volumetric strain of coal near the injection point reaches 0.009, indicating thorough local fracture propagation and good reservoir enhancement near the wellbore; however, the volumetric strain curve along monitoring line AB is relatively narrow. For high-elastic-modulus (E = 6 GPa) coal, the volumetric strain is smaller (peak strain approximately 0.0042–0.007), but the volumetric strain curve along line AB is wider, and the zone over which volumetric strain occurs is broader than that for low elastic modulus. It is worth noting that for coal with an elastic modulus between 2 and 6 GPa, compressive strain (negative values) occurs locally in peripheral areas during fracturing. Pore pressure at isolated fractures is low, and some even exhibit compressive strain, reflecting that fracture opening is restricted in tectonically deformed coal, particularly when the elastic modulus is low.

Elastic modulus is a key parameter controlling damage propagation during hydraulic fracturing of tectonically deformed coal. A relatively low elastic modulus (2–3 GPa) is more favorable for forming a damage zone that extends along fractures and for generating significant volumetric dilation. However, an excessively low elastic modulus can inhibit both damage propagation and dilation effects. A high elastic modulus (5–6 GPa), while beneficial for wider fracture propagation and for forming a broader fracturing-induced damage zone, results in less significant volumetric dilation and requires a relatively higher fracture initiation pressure.

Based on the simulation results of scheme 3, Figures and reveal the key regulatory role of initial matrix permeability on the damage evolution and volumetric strain during hydraulic fracturing of coal reservoirs. As fracturing time extends from 0.5 to 2 h, the damaged zone under all permeability conditions shows a continuous expansion trend. At low initial matrix permeability (k 0 ≤ 0.01 mD), the damaged zone is narrow and elongated. Constrained by natural fractures, damage occurs only locally in a concentrated manner, resulting in a small overall damaged zone and slow evolution. At high permeability (k 0 ≥ 0.05 mD), the damaged zone is wider and expands more rapidly. Notably, at 2 h, the damaged zone has already formed a distinct clustered morphology, indicating that under high-permeability conditions, the fracturing fluid more easily penetrates the matrix, driving damage to expand uniformly in all directions. This finding is consistent with the results reported by Xu et al. The volumetric strain curves corroborate the damage evolution and further quantify the influence of initial reservoir permeability on fracturing. In the early fracturing stage (0.5 h), the strain for different permeabilities is concentrated near the fracturing point. Under low-permeability conditions, the peak strain is higher, indicating that energy is concentrated near the wellbore, readily generating high stress concentration. Under high-permeability conditions, the peak strain is lower, indicating that rapid seepage of the fracturing fluid dissipates the stress. In the middle to late fracturing stages (1–2 h): under low-permeability conditions, the strain curve exhibits a “sharp peak” morphology, indicating concentrated damage and a single fracture propagation pathway. Initial matrix permeability is a core factor determining the effectiveness of fracturing stimulation: in low-permeability reservoirs, fracturing tends to produce a “narrow and deep” fracture network with limited stimulated volume; in high-permeability reservoirs, it is more likely to form a “wide and uniform” damaged zone, which is beneficial for enhancing overall permeability. In engineering practice, differentiated fracturing parameters should be matched to reservoirs with different permeabilities in order to optimize fracture propagation morphology and stimulated reservoir volume.

A significant displacement rate effect is observed. Damage evolution characteristics (Figure ): As fracturing time extends from 0.5 to 2 h, the damaged zone expands continuously under all displacement rate conditions; however, the effect of displacement rate is critical. The fracturing displacement rate is a key parameter controlling the effectiveness of fracturing stimulation. Under low displacement rates, fracture propagation is limited, and the stimulated volume is constrained. High displacement rates significantly enhance hydraulic energy, promote multidirectional fracture propagation, and result in a larger stimulated volume and a more complex fracture network. In engineering practice, optimizing the fracturing displacement rate can achieve efficient stimulation under different reservoir conditions, balancing the stimulated volume with operational costs.

Figure reveals the coupled effects of elastic modulus and fracturing displacement rate on pore pressure and permeability evolution during coal reservoir fracturing (taking elastic moduli of 2 and 6 GPa and an initial matrix permeability of 0.0057 mD as examples), providing key quantitative support for the previous analysis of damage and volumetric strain. Pore pressure and permeability exhibit unimodal distributions near the fractured well with highly synchronized variation patterns. Increasing the fracturing displacement rate significantly enhances the peak pore pressure: in the 2 GPa reservoir, when the displacement rate increases from 5 to 10 m3/min, the peak pore pressure rises from approximately 45 to 70 MPa; in the 6 GPa reservoir, it rises from approximately 70 to 145 MPa. The peak permeability increases correspondingly, confirming the mechanism by which high displacement rates drive fracture propagation through increasing the near-wellbore pressure differential. The regulating effect of the elastic modulus on pressure and permeability is significant. Under the same displacement rate, the peak pore pressure in the 6 GPa reservoir is much higher than that in the 2 GPa reservoir, yet the peak permeability is significantly lower (1.1 mD < 2.8 mD). High-elastic-modulus reservoirs have greater stiffness, making it difficult for hydraulic energy to dissipate through matrix deformation; instead, it accumulates in the form of pore pressure, resulting in a sharp curve morphology, corresponding to fracture propagation along preferential pathways and a narrow, elongated damage zone. Low-elastic-modulus reservoirs are more prone to plastic yielding, exhibiting a broader and gentler pressure distribution, where the pore pressure is effectively converted into matrix damage and permeability enhancement, corresponding to a clustered stimulation pattern within a limited area (see Figure ). The coupling effect between the elastic modulus and fracturing displacement rate determines the fracturing stimulation mode: low-elastic-modulus reservoirs are characterized by overall matrix deformation and uniform stimulation, whereas high-elastic-modulus reservoirs are characterized by fracture-dominated extension. Meanwhile, the magnitude of matrix permeability governs the efficiency of fracturing fluid diffusion and modifies the fracturing stimulation outcome (Figures and ), providing direct guidance for optimizing fracturing parameters in reservoirs with different mechanical properties.

Gas Production Analysis of Coal Reservoir under HMD Multifield Coupling

To further investigate the effects of different fracturing displacement rates, permeabilities, and elastic moduli on gas production, fracturing simulations were conducted according to schemes 1–4, and 18 sets of 185-day cumulative gas production data were obtained, as shown in Figure . The stimulation effect of fracturing displacement rate shows a significant positive correlation (Figure a,b): as the fracturing displacement rate increases from 0 to 10 m3/min, the 185-day cumulative gas production increases from 1.09 × 104 to 10.23 × 104 m3, representing an increase of 834.66%. From a low displacement rate (5 m3/min) to a high displacement rate (10 m3/min), the cumulative gas production increase is 48.47%. An appropriate high fracturing displacement rate significantly enhances reservoir conductivity by intensifying fracture propagation and damage stimulation, representing a universally applicable and efficient stimulation method. The effect of elastic modulus shows a negative correlation (Figure c,d): as the elastic modulus increases from 2 to 6 GPa, the cumulative gas production decreases from 10.36 × 104 to 8.06 × 104 m3, a reduction of 22.21%. Under the same fracturing conditions, low-elastic-modulus coal is more prone to plastic yielding, exhibits greater volumetric strain in the near-wellbore region, and achieves better fracturing stimulation within the fractured damaged zone, resulting in a well-developed matrix-fracture network and stronger gas production capacity. High-elastic-modulus reservoirs have greater stiffness, higher resistance to fracture propagation, and higher fracture initiation pressure requirements, leading to limited volume fracturing stimulation and significantly reduced gas production. The amplification effect of initial permeability on gas production is the most prominent (Figure e,f): as the initial permeability increases from 0.001 mD to 0.2 mD, the cumulative gas production increases from 6.12 × 104 to 27.00 × 104 m3, an increase of 341.44%. High-permeability reservoirs inherently possess a higher conductivity baseline, and the synergistic effect between the fracture network and matrix pores further amplifies the gas production potential after fracturing, exhibiting a significant permeability-sensitive characteristic.

Cumulative gas production and gas production growth rate under (a, b) different fracturing displacements, (c, d) different elastic moduli, and (e, f) different initial permeabilities.

To further investigate the effects of permeability and elastic modulus on gas production under different fracturing displacement rates, 180 sets of 185-day cumulative gas production data were obtained from simulations based on scheme 5, as shown in Figure . Overall, under all working conditions, the cumulative gas production shows a significant linear increasing trend with increasing fracturing displacement rate. High flow rates enhance fracture propagation and matrix damage, forming a more complex flow conductivity network, and exhibit stimulation effects on reservoirs with different permeabilities and elastic moduli. The amplification effect of initial permeability is very significant: as k 0 increases from 0.001 to 0.2 mD, the overall gas production level increases substantially, and the stimulation effect of flow rate is significantly amplified. In low-permeability reservoirs, the baseline gas production is low, and the increase brought by higher flow rates is limited. In high-permeability reservoirs, which inherently possess strong flow conductivity, the synergistic effect between the fracture network and matrix pores after fracturing fully activates the gas production potential, making the stimulation effect of increasing the fracturing displacement rate more prominent. The effect of elastic modulus exhibits a clear permeability dependence. In low-permeability reservoirs, the cumulative gas production of low-elastic-modulus reservoirs is significantly higher, consistent with the mechanical characteristic that low-elastic-modulus coal is prone to plastic yielding and forms a uniform damaged zone. As permeability increases, the influence of elastic modulus on gas production gradually weakens; the gap in gas production between different elastic modulus conditions narrows significantly, and a trend even emerges where high-elastic-modulus reservoirs surpass low-elastic-modulus ones in gas production (Figure d–f). This indicates that under high-permeability conditions, the reservoir’s own flow conductivity is sufficient to support efficient gas production, and the influence of the fracture propagation mode is diminished. In summary, an appropriate fracturing displacement rate is a key factor for stimulation, while the coupling effect between initial permeability and elastic modulus determines the gas production potential of the reservoir.

Using the pre- and postfracturing gas production results from 181 simulations obtained from schemes 1 and 5, the Pearson correlation coefficients and SHAP values were calculated and plotted (see Figure ). These reveal the linear correlations between multiple pre- and postfracturing reservoir parameters under HMD multifield coupling control and gas production, as well as their contribution magnitudes in machine learning models. The two analyses show a high degree of consistency regarding the core influencing factors, while also demonstrating the complementarity of linear and nonlinear analyses. Correlation analysis indicates that initial reservoir permeability, damaged area, and maximum postfracturing permeability exhibit significant positive correlations with gas production, with correlation coefficients of 0.8888, 0.6817, and 0.8743, respectively, making them key linear influencing factors that govern gas production performance. Elastic modulus shows a weak negative correlation with gas production, with a relatively weak linear influence. The ranking of linear correlations with gas production is k 0 > k m‑max > D A > H f > E. SHAP interpretability analysis further quantifies the nonlinear contributions of each parameter in the SVM, RF, and GRNN models. The order of contribution of each parameter, as shown by the SHAP results for the SVM, RF, and GRNN models, is D A> k m‑max > E > H f > k 0, k m‑max>k 0>D A>E > H f, and k m‑max> D A > k 0> H f > E; The differing SHAP values across the three models validate their key driving roles within nonlinear models. The discrepancy between the aforementioned Pearson correlations and the SHAP ranking stems from the fact that correlation coefficients reflect only the linear relationship between two variables, whereas SHAP reflects the marginal predictive contribution of each feature within a specific nonlinear model (such as RF), while also accounting for interactions, multicollinearity, and nonlinear effects between features. However, parameters such as k m‑max and D A remain significant factors influencing gas production. The discrepancy between the Pearson coefficients and SHAP values highlights the nonlinear characteristics of the fracturing process, indicating that parameters such as fracturing volume and elastic modulus primarily exert a linear influence on the fracturing damage area and postfracturing permeability; however, their impact on gas production cannot be fully characterized by linear correlation alone and requires a comprehensive assessment incorporating the nonlinear interpretations provided by machine learning models. The combined analysis of Pearson correlation coefficients and SHAP values provides a comprehensive quantitative basis for understanding the influence mechanisms of fracturing parameters and further validates that permeability, damaged area, and fracturing displacement rate are the core factors affecting gas production.

Machine Learning Training and Error Analysis of Prediction Results

Figure compares the training effect and error index of SVM, RF, and GRNN and reveals the internal relationship between model performance and feature importance by combining with SHAP interpretable analysis. The training set fitting results of the three machine learning models show that all three can capture the overall distribution trend of the gas production data reasonably well. Among them, the RF model achieves the highest goodness of fit (R 2 = 0.9871). Its data points closely align with the fitted line, and the prediction errors are significantly lower than those of the other models: its MAE, MAPE, and RMSE values are 0.5547, 0.0428, and 0.8476, respectively, representing the best error metrics among the three. This demonstrates the RF model’s strong nonlinear fitting capability and indicates that it possesses good interpretability regarding the causal relationships between parameters and gas production in production prediction, which is highly consistent with previous studies. In contrast, the SVM and GRNN models achieve R 2 values of 0.9485 and 0.9463, respectively, with significantly higher error levels, indicating a weaker ability to capture complex nonlinear relationships compared to the RF model.

In contrast, the SVM and GRNN models have a relatively limited ability to characterize these core features, resulting in larger prediction errors. In summary, the RF model demonstrates the best prediction performance and generalization ability on both the training and test sets, making it the optimal model for fracturing gas production prediction in this scheme. Discussion

Multifield Coupling Mechanism and Data-Driven Gas Production Prediction System

In this study, a framework of “multifield coupling mechanism-data-driven prediction” integrated fracturing gas production analysis method was constructed (Figure ). This framework reveals the gas production control mechanism of HMD multifield coupling and elaborates on the mechanism-data fusion prediction methodology. At the multifield coupling mechanism level, hydraulic fracturing increases pore pressure, which, on one hand, directly drives fracture propagation and alters coal structure to form a damage field; on the other hand, it reduces the effective stress through fluid–solid coupling, inducing coal deformation. The evolution of the damage field, in turn, counteracts the mechanical parameters and fluid migration pathways, further intensifying the coupling effects among the multiple fields, collectively determining reservoir stimulation effectiveness and gas production potential. This forms a regulatory interaction among the hydraulic, mechanical, and damage fields. At the methodological level, this framework achieves a deep integration of the multifield coupling mechanism model and machine learning. First, through HMD multifield coupling numerical simulation, gas production data sets under various operating conditions are constructed, providing high-quality samples for machine learning. Then, through data set partitioning, model training, and optimization, a high-precision gas production prediction model considering the influence of multiple parameters is established, ultimately realizing a complete pathway from mechanism revelation to engineering prediction. This approach retains the accurate physical characterization of the multifield coupling mechanism while leveraging the advantages of machine learning in nonlinear mapping and rapid prediction, providing theoretical and methodological support for efficient gas production prediction and parameter optimization in fracturing.
