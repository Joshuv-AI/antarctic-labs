Fluctuations and correlations of conserved charges in the Polyakov chiral SU(3) quark mean field model
Dhananjay Singh, Arvind Kumar

Abstract
We compute generalized susceptibilities of conserved charges in the Polyakov chiral SU(3) quark mean field (PCQMF) model with the fermion vacuum term. At μ B = 0 \mu_{B}=0 MeV, the calculation covers the diagonal χ n B , Q , S \chi_{n}^{B,Q,S} through eighth order and all twelve independent fourth-order off-diagonal correlators. Extending to finite μ B \mu_{B} at μ Q = μ S = 0 \mu_{Q}=\mu_{S}=0 , we compute χ n B \chi_{n}^{B} through eighth order, χ n Q , S \chi_{n}^{Q,S} through fourth order, the second-order off-diagonals, all twelve fourth-order off-diagonal correlators, and the odd-order baryon susceptibilities χ 1 B \chi_{1}^{B} , χ 3 B \chi_{3}^{B} , χ 5 B \chi_{5}^{B} . The calculation includes the vacuum term (vac=1) and is repeated for an independently refitted no-sea variant (vac=0). At μ B = 0 \mu_{B}=0 MeV, the chiral pseudocritical temperature is T pc = 170.5 T_{\mathrm{pc}}=170.5 MeV (vac=1) and 166.4 166.4 MeV (vac=0), while the Polyakov-loop deconfinement temperature is T dec = 144.4 T_{\mathrm{dec}}=144.4 MeV (vac=1) and 146.6 146.6 MeV (vac=0). In vac=1, the derivative − d Δ l , s / d T -d\Delta_{l,s}/dT of the subtracted chiral condensate develops an inflection near T dec T_{\mathrm{dec}} . Higher derivative orders resolve the chiral-deconfinement splitting as twin maxima in χ 4 B \chi_{4}^{B} and χ 6 Q \chi_{6}^{Q} , twin minima in χ 8 B \chi_{8}^{B} and χ 8 Q \chi_{8}^{Q} , and multiple zero crossings in χ 6 B \chi_{6}^{B} . Among the fourth-order off-diagonal correlators, vac=1 amplitudes exceed vac=0 in the BQ channel across the chiral crossover. The BS, QS, and mixed BQS components peak near the strange-melting temperature, where vac=0 dominates. Along T pc ​ ( μ B ) T_{\mathrm{pc}}(\mu_{B}) , the kurtosis ratio R 42 B ≡ χ 4 B / χ 2 B R_{42}^{B}\equiv\chi_{4}^{B}/\chi_{2}^{B} of vac=1 crosses zero at μ B / T pc ≈ 2.15 \mu_{B}/T_{\mathrm{pc}}\approx 2.15 , while vac=0 stays positive across the full range. The higher-order ratios R 51 B ≡ χ 5 B / χ 1 B R_{51}^{B}\equiv\chi_{5}^{B}/\chi_{1}^{B} and R 62 B ≡ χ 6 B / χ 2 B R_{62}^{B}\equiv\chi_{6}^{B}/\chi_{2}^{B} start negative in vac=1 and grow more negative as μ B \mu_{B} increases.



I
Introduction

Quantum chromodynamics (QCD) at finite temperature
T
and baryon chemical potential
\mu_{B}
describes matter under conditions realized in the early universe and in relativistic heavy-ion collisions. At high temperature, asymptotic freedom implies deconfinement into a quark-gluon plasma, and chiral symmetry is expected to be approximately restored. Lattice simulations at
\mu_{B}=0
MeV have established that the two transitions (chiral and deconfinement) coincide as a smooth crossover
Aoki et al. 2006
;
Bazavov et al. 2019
;
Borsanyi et al. 2010
. The pseudocritical temperature at
\mu_{B}=0
is
T_{\mathrm{pc}}=158.0\pm 0.6
MeV
Borsanyi et al. 2020
. At finite
\mu_{B}
, effective models and universality arguments suggest a first-order phase boundary that terminates at a critical endpoint (CEP)
Halasz et al. 1998
;
Stephanov et al. 1998
;
Stephanov 2006
. The fermion sign problem prevents direct lattice simulations at finite
\mu_{B}
. The lattice equation of state (EoS) now extends to
\mu_{B}/T\approx 3.5

Abuali et al. 2025
, and recent Padé and Lee-Yang analyses find a CEP unlikely below that reach
Clarke et al. 2025
;
Başar 2024
. On the experimental side, the RHIC Beam Energy Scan program has measured net-proton cumulants at
\sqrt{s_{NN}}=7.7
-200 GeV, where a non-monotonic
\kappa\sigma^{2}
was first seen in BES-I
Adam et al. 2021
and confirmed in BES-II
Aboona et al. 2025a
, which also reported the first fifth- and sixth-order results
Aboona et al. 2025b
. Fixed-target measurements extend the reach to
\sqrt{s_{NN}}=3
GeV
Aboona et al. 2023
. Upcoming programs at FAIR (CBM) and NICA will extend the reach to higher
\mu_{B}
(lower
\sqrt{s_{NN}}
)
Bzdak et al. 2020
;
Pandav et al. 2022
. Recent reviews are given in
Luo and Xu 2017
;
Koch and Vovchenko 2025
. These data call for theoretical predictions for the full set of generalized baryon-charge-strangeness (BQS) susceptibilities, both diagonal and off-diagonal. Cumulants of net-proton, net-kaon, and net-charge distributions measured in heavy-ion collisions serve as experimental proxies for specific components of this set
Koch 2008
;
Kitazawa and Asakawa 2012
.

The generalized susceptibilities
\chi_{ijk}^{BQS}
are derivatives of the scaled pressure with respect to
\hat{\mu}_{B,Q,S}=\mu_{B,Q,S}/T
. They can be computed in both lattice QCD and effective models, and correspond directly to the cumulants of event-by-event net-particle distributions
Koch et al. 2005
;
Ejiri et al. 2006
;
Koch 2008
;
Hatta and Ikeda 2003
. At second order, the diagonal
\chi_{2}^{B,Q,S}
have been obtained on the lattice at
\mu_{B}=0
MeV
Bazavov et al. 2012
;
Borsanyi et al. 2012
;
Bellwied et al. 2015
;
Bollweg et al. 2021
, at finite
\mu_{B}
via Taylor expansion
Karsch et al. 2011
;
Bazavov et al. 2020
, and across the full
(T,\mu_{B},\mu_{Q},\mu_{S})
plane
Abuali et al. 2025
. In the Polyakov Nambu Jona Lasinio (PNJL) model
Fukushima 2004
;
Ratti et al. 2006
, diagonal susceptibilities were computed with multi-quark interactions
Ghosh et al. 2006
;
Bhattacharyya et al. 2010
, where eight-quark terms improved agreement with lattice data below
T_{\mathrm{pc}}
. Later PNJL studies covered net-baryon higher moments
Sarkar and Deb 2024
and net-strangeness fluctuations
Sarkar and Deb 2026
at BES energies. Off-diagonal correlators and the
C_{BS}
ratio at finite
\mu_{B}
have also been computed
Shao et al. 2018
;
Ferreira and Costa 2018
. In the Polyakov quark meson (PQM) model
Schaefer et al. 2007
;
Schaefer et al. 2010
, diagonal susceptibilities and their ratios were computed with and without the fermion vacuum term
Schaefer and Wagner 2012
;
Chatterjee and Mohan 2012a
. In the hadron resonance gas (HRG) model, repulsive mean-field interactions are essential for reproducing higher-order susceptibilities near
T_{\mathrm{pc}}

Pal et al. 2021
.

The second-order off-diagonal correlators
\chi_{11}^{BQ,BS,QS}
have received much less attention. They probe correlated fluctuations between conserved charges and are sensitive to the quantum numbers of the dominant carriers. For instance, the baryon-strangeness correlation coefficient
C_{BS}
distinguishes whether strangeness is carried by hadrons or by quarks
Koch et al. 2005
. It has been computed in the PNJL model and on the lattice along the pseudocritical line
Bollweg et al. 2024
;
Goswami and Karsch 2025
, and baryon-charge correlations have been examined in nuclear matter
Yang et al. 2025
. A persistent deficiency of these mean-field chiral models is the underestimation of
\chi_{2}^{Q}
below
T_{\mathrm{pc}}

Chatterjee and Mohan 2012a
;
Bhattacharyya et al. 2010
, since pions in mean-field PCQMF are frozen at their classical value and contribute no fluctuations.

Higher-order susceptibilities are directly sensitive to critical dynamics. The kurtosis
\chi_{4}^{B}/\chi_{2}^{B}
develops non-monotonic structure near the chiral crossover
Stephanov 2009
;
Stephanov 2011
, and
\chi_{6}^{B}
changes sign near
T_{\mathrm{pc}}
, a feature linked to
O(4)
universality
Ejiri et al. 2006
;
Friman et al. 2011
. Continuum-extrapolated lattice results for
\chi_{6}^{B}
and
\chi_{8}^{B}
now exist
Borsanyi et al. 2024
, and
\chi_{6}
through
\chi_{10}
have been measured on finite-volume lattices
Adam et al. 2025
. Lattice skewness and kurtosis ratios at finite
\mu_{B}
have been compared with STAR BES-I data
Bazavov et al. 2020
. More recently, higher-order cumulants have been evaluated along the pseudocritical line
Goswami and Karsch 2025
. In effective models, the scope of existing calculations varies considerably. In the PQM model, all twelve fourth-order off-diagonal correlators and diagonal susceptibilities through sixth order were computed with the vacuum term at
\mu_{B}=0
MeV
Chatterjee and Mohan 2012a
. A separate calculation extended baryon-number ratios to twelfth order at finite
\mu_{B}
along a single quark chemical potential
Schaefer and Wagner 2012
. In the NJL model, diagonal BQS susceptibilities were computed through fourth order
Fan et al. 2017
and baryon-number susceptibilities through eighth order
Fan et al. 2019
. Beyond mean field, functional renormalization group (fRG) calculations resum mesonic and fermionic quantum fluctuations and have produced baryon fluctuations through tenth order
Fu et al. 2021
;
Fu et al. 2020a
;
Fu et al. 2025
. The same approach has been applied to strangeness neutrality and baryon-strangeness correlations
Fu et al. 2020b
;
Fu and Pawlowski 2026
. Holographic QCD has produced baryon susceptibilities through twelfth order
Li and He 2023
. No existing effective-model study provides all twelve fourth-order off-diagonal BQS correlators together with diagonal susceptibilities through eighth order at
\mu_{B}=0
.

The vacuum (Dirac-sea) term, the zero-temperature one-loop quark contribution to the thermodynamic potential, significantly affects the crossover. In the two-flavor PQM model, the vacuum term restores second-order
O(4)
universality in the chiral limit
Skokov et al. 2010
. In the
(2+1)
-flavor extension, it smooths the crossover and produces oscillatory sign structure in
\chi_{6}^{B,Q,S}

Chatterjee and Mohan 2012a
;
Schaefer and Wagner 2012
, and improves agreement with lattice thermodynamics
Chatterjee and Mohan 2012b
. It also shifts the CEP to larger
\mu_{B}
and lower
T

Gupta and Tiwari 2012
;
Schaefer and Wagner 2012
, though the magnitude of the shift depends on the renormalization scheme
Rai and Tiwari 2024
. In the Polyakov chiral SU(3) quark mean field (PCQMF) model, the vacuum term has only been used for transport coefficients
Singh and Kumar 2025
and anisotropic quark matter
Singh and Kumar 2026
, while all existing thermodynamic and susceptibility studies
Kumari and Kumar 2021
;
Chahal et al. 2022
;
Chahal et al. 2023
omitted it. Its effect on the full set of generalized BQS susceptibilities has not been studied.

The PCQMF model includes the nonstrange and strange scalar fields
\sigma
and
\zeta
as order parameters for chiral symmetry breaking, and the Polyakov loop
\Phi
as an order parameter for deconfinement. Vector mesons
\omega
,
\rho
,
\phi
provide short-range repulsion, and a dilaton field
\chi
implements the QCD trace anomaly. An isovector scalar
\delta
is included for isospin-asymmetric matter. The only existing susceptibility calculation in this model
Chahal et al. 2022
omitted the vacuum term and computed only diagonal susceptibilities, through sixth order for charge and strangeness and eighth order for baryon. No off-diagonal correlators were reported. We report the first computation of the full set of generalized BQS susceptibilities in the PCQMF model with the fermion vacuum term. At
\mu_{B}=0
MeV, the calculation covers all diagonal
\chi_{n}^{B,Q,S}
for
n=2,4,6,8
and all twelve independent fourth-order off-diagonal correlators, namely the nine two-charge correlators
\chi_{ij}^{BQ,BS,QS}
with
i+j=4
and the three mixed correlators
\chi_{ijk}^{BQS}
with
i+j+k=4
. The extension to finite
\mu_{B}
up to 500 MeV at
\mu_{Q}=\mu_{S}=0
covers
\chi_{2}^{B,Q,S}
,
\chi_{4}^{B,Q,S}
,
\chi_{6}^{B}
,
\chi_{8}^{B}
, the second-order off-diagonals
\chi_{11}^{BQ,BS,QS}
, and the odd-order baryon susceptibilities
\chi_{1}^{B}
,
\chi_{3}^{B}
,
\chi_{5}^{B}
. The calculation is repeated with an independently refitted no-sea (vac=0) parameter set for comparison. We compare with continuum-extrapolated lattice data
Bellwied et al. 2015
;
Borsanyi et al. 2024
;
Bollweg et al. 2021
;
Abuali et al. 2025
where benchmarks exist.

The article is organized as follows. Section
II
introduces the chiral SU(3) Lagrangian, the PCQMF grand potential with the vacuum term, the coupled gap equations, and the susceptibility definitions. Section
III
presents results at
\mu_{B}=0
MeV, including order parameters, bulk thermodynamics, diagonal susceptibilities through eighth order, all twelve fourth-order off-diagonal correlators, and correlation ratios.
Section
IV
extends the analysis to finite
\mu_{B}
, covering diagonal and off-diagonal susceptibilities at second and fourth order, diagonal baryon susceptibilities through eighth order, and cumulant ratios along
T_{\mathrm{pc}}(\mu_{B})
. Section
V
discusses the vacuum-term effects and mean-field limitations. We conclude in Sec.
VI
with a summary and outlook. Stefan-Boltzmann limits for the quantities computed in this work are collected in Appendix
A
.

II
Model and formalism

II.1
Chiral SU(3) Lagrangian

We employ the Polyakov chiral SU(3) quark mean field (PCQMF) model. The chiral SU(3) Lagrangian
Papazoglou et al. 1998
;
Papazoglou et al. 1999
uses a nonlinear realization of chiral symmetry. Baryons couple to scalar mesons
\sigma
,
\zeta
,
\delta
and vector mesons
\omega
,
\rho
,
\phi
. The dilaton
\chi
accounts for broken scale invariance. A later reformulation at the quark level
Wang et al. 2001
;
Wang et al. 2003
replaced the baryons with constituent quarks, and the Polyakov-loop variables (
\Phi
,
\bar{\Phi}
)
Fukushima 2004
;
Ratti et al. 2006
were added in Ref.
Kumari and Kumar 2021
. The fermion vacuum (Dirac-sea) term, first included in the PCQMF model in Ref.
Singh and Kumar 2025
, is retained throughout the present work.

The Lagrangian comprises scalar and vector meson self-interactions, explicit chiral symmetry breaking, and a strange-quark mass term:

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{eff}}=\mathcal{L}_{\Sigma\Sigma}+\mathcal{L}_{VV}+\mathcal{L}_{SB}+\mathcal{L}_{\Delta m}.

(1)

The scalar sector reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\Sigma\Sigma}

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{2}k_{0}\chi^{2}(\sigma^{2}+\zeta^{2}+\delta^{2})+k_{1}(\sigma^{2}+\zeta^{2}+\delta^{2})^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+k_{2}\!\left(\frac{\sigma^{4}}{2}+\frac{\delta^{4}}{2}+3\sigma^{2}\delta^{2}+\zeta^{4}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+k_{3}\chi(\sigma^{2}-\delta^{2})\zeta-k_{4}\chi^{4}-\frac{1}{4}\chi^{4}\ln\frac{\chi^{4}}{\chi_{0}^{4}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{d}{3}\chi^{4}\ln\!\left[\frac{(\sigma^{2}-\delta^{2})\zeta}{\sigma_{0}^{2}\zeta_{0}}\cdot\frac{\chi^{3}}{\chi_{0}^{3}}\right],

(2)

where
\sigma_{0}
and
\zeta_{0}
are the vacuum expectation values of
\sigma
and
\zeta
and given by
\sigma_{0}=-f_{\pi}
and
\zeta_{0}=(f_{\pi}-2f_{K})/\sqrt{2}
. The dilaton vacuum value
\chi_{0}
is fitted to vacuum observables and listed in Table
1
. The pion and kaon decay constants are
f_{\pi}=93
MeV and
f_{K}=115
MeV. The logarithmic and
k_{4}
terms together implement broken scale invariance and generate the trace anomaly
Papazoglou et al. 1999
;
Wang et al. 2001
.

The vector meson self-interaction reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{VV}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\chi^{2}}{\chi_{0}^{2}}\bigl(m_{\omega}^{2}\omega^{2}+m_{\rho}^{2}\rho^{2}+m_{\phi}^{2}\phi^{2}\bigr)

\displaystyle\quad+g_{4}(\omega^{4}+6\omega^{2}\rho^{2}+\rho^{4}+2\phi^{4}),

(3)

with
m_{\omega}^{2}=m_{\rho}^{2}=m_{v}^{2}/(1-\tfrac{1}{2}\mu_{v}\sigma^{2})
and
m_{\phi}^{2}=m_{v}^{2}/(1-\mu_{v}\zeta^{2})
, where
m_{v}
and
\mu_{v}
are chosen to reproduce
m_{\omega}=783
MeV and
m_{\phi}=1020
MeV
Kumari and Kumar 2021
. Explicit chiral symmetry breaking enters through

\mathcal{L}_{SB}=-\frac{\chi^{2}}{\chi_{0}^{2}}\bigl(h_{x}\sigma+h_{y}\zeta\bigr),

(4)

where
h_{x}=m_{\pi}^{2}f_{\pi}
and
h_{y}=\sqrt{2}\,m_{K}^{2}f_{K}-m_{\pi}^{2}f_{\pi}/\sqrt{2}
are fixed by the physical pion and kaon masses. A strange-quark mass term

\mathcal{L}_{\Delta m}=-\Delta m_{s}\,\bar{\psi}\,S\,\psi,

(5)

with
S=\mathrm{diag}(0,0,1)
and
\Delta m_{s}=29
MeV, supplies a bare mass to the strange quark.

The quark-meson couplings follow from the SU(3) flavor structure,

g_{\sigma u}=g_{\sigma d}=g_{\delta u}=-g_{\delta d}=\frac{g_{s}}{\sqrt{2}},\quad g_{\zeta s}=g_{s},

(6)

with all other scalar couplings equal to zero and
g_{s}=4.76
as the single independent coupling. The vector coupling is set to
g_{v}=0
.

Numerical values of the parameters for both variants are collected in Table
1
. The fit imposes physical
\pi
and
K
masses, the combined
\eta
-
\eta^{\prime}
mass, the scalar
\sigma
-meson mass, and the vacuum stationarity conditions. The procedure was established for this model in Refs.
Wang et al. 2001
;
Kumari and Kumar 2021
. The PCAC relations fix
h_{x}
and
h_{y}
to common values in both variants. The resulting vacuum constituent quark masses are
m_{u}^{*}=m_{d}^{*}=313
MeV and
m_{s}^{*}=490
MeV.

II.2
Thermodynamic potential

In the mean-field approximation, the thermodynamic potential density reads

\Omega=\Omega_{\mathrm{mes}}+\Omega_{\bar{q}q}^{\mathrm{vac}}+\Omega_{\bar{q}q}^{\mathrm{th}}+\mathcal{U}(\Phi,\bar{\Phi};T),

(7)

with
\Omega_{\mathrm{mes}}
the mesonic potential,
\Omega_{\bar{q}q}^{\mathrm{vac}}
the vacuum contribution,
\Omega_{\bar{q}q}^{\mathrm{th}}
the thermal quark-antiquark contribution, and
\mathcal{U}(\Phi,\bar{\Phi};T)
the Polyakov-loop effective potential. The constituent quark masses entering
\Omega_{\bar{q}q}^{\mathrm{vac}}
and
\Omega_{\bar{q}q}^{\mathrm{th}}
are

m_{i}^{*}=-g_{\sigma i}\sigma-g_{\zeta i}\zeta-g_{\delta i}\delta+m_{i0},

(8)

with
m_{u0}=m_{d0}=0
and
m_{s0}=\Delta m_{s}
. The quark chemical potentials are

\mu_{i}=B_{i}\mu_{B}+Q_{i}\mu_{Q}+S_{i}\mu_{S},

(9)

with
B_{i}=1/3
for all flavors,
Q_{u}=2/3
,
Q_{d}=Q_{s}=-1/3
,
S_{s}=-1
, and
S_{u}=S_{d}=0
, giving

\displaystyle\mu_{u}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{3}\mu_{B}+\tfrac{2}{3}\mu_{Q},

\displaystyle\mu_{d}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{3}\mu_{B}-\tfrac{1}{3}\mu_{Q},

\displaystyle\mu_{s}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{3}\mu_{B}-\tfrac{1}{3}\mu_{Q}-\mu_{S}.

(10)

Coupling to the vector mean fields shifts the chemical potentials to

\mu_{i}^{*}=\mu_{i}-g_{\omega}^{i}\omega-g_{\rho}^{i}\rho-g_{\phi}^{i}\phi,

(11)

with the vector couplings
g_{\omega}^{u}=g_{\omega}^{d}=g_{v}/\sqrt{2}
,
g_{\rho}^{u}=-g_{\rho}^{d}=g_{v}/\sqrt{2}
, and
g_{\phi}^{s}=g_{v}
. All other vector couplings are set to zero.

The mesonic potential follows from Eq. (
1
) as

\Omega_{\mathrm{mes}}=-\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{eff}}-\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{vac}},

(12)

where
\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{vac}}
is the additive constant enforcing
\Omega=0
in vacuum.

The thermal quark-antiquark contribution in Eq. (
7
), dressed by the Polyakov-loop variables, reads

\Omega_{\bar{q}q}^{\mathrm{th}}=-\sum_{i=u,d,s}\frac{\gamma_{i}T}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{0}^{\infty}d^{3}k\bigl\{\ln\mathcal{N}_{i}^{-}+\ln\mathcal{N}_{i}^{+}\bigr\},

(13)

with spin degeneracy
\gamma_{i}=2
and

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{i}^{-}

\displaystyle=1+e^{-3E_{-}}+3\Phi\,e^{-E_{-}}+3\bar{\Phi}\,e^{-2E_{-}},

(14)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{i}^{+}

\displaystyle=1+e^{-3E_{+}}+3\bar{\Phi}\,e^{-E_{+}}+3\Phi\,e^{-2E_{+}},

(15)

where
E_{\pm}=(E_{i}^{*}(k)\pm\mu_{i}^{*})/T
and
E_{i}^{*}(k)=\sqrt{k^{2}+m_{i}^{*2}}
is the effective single-particle energy.

The Polyakov-loop effective potential in Eq. (
7
) is taken in the logarithmic form
Rössner et al. 2007

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{U}(\Phi,\bar{\Phi};T)}{T^{4}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{a(T)}{2}\Phi\bar{\Phi}+b(T)\ln\!\bigl[1-6\Phi\bar{\Phi}

\displaystyle\quad+4(\Phi^{3}+\bar{\Phi}^{3})-3(\Phi\bar{\Phi})^{2}\bigr],

(16)

with
a(T)=a_{0}+a_{1}(T_{0}/T)+a_{2}(T_{0}/T)^{2}
and
b(T)=b_{3}(T_{0}/T)^{3}
. The logarithm argument is proportional to the
\mathrm{SU}(3)
Haar measure, restricting
\Phi
and
\bar{\Phi}
to
|\Phi|,|\bar{\Phi}|\leq 1
. The parameters
a_{0}
,
a_{1}
,
a_{2}
,
b_{3}
are fitted to pure-gauge lattice thermodynamics
Rössner et al. 2007
. To account for the backreaction of dynamical quarks on the gluonic sector, the temperature argument of
a
and
b
is replaced by an effective Yang-Mills temperature
T_{\mathrm{YM}}
. The glue temperature of the full theory
T_{\mathrm{glue}}
is identified with the matter temperature
T
, and
T_{\mathrm{YM}}
follows from the mapping
Haas et al. 2013

\frac{T_{\mathrm{YM}}-T_{0}^{\mathrm{YM}}}{T_{0}^{\mathrm{YM}}}=0.57\left(\frac{T_{\mathrm{glue}}-T_{0}^{\mathrm{glue}}}{T_{0}^{\mathrm{glue}}}\right),

(17)

with
T_{0}^{\mathrm{YM}}=T_{0}^{\mathrm{glue}}=T_{0}=200
MeV.

II.3
Fermion vacuum contribution

The zero-temperature one-loop quark contribution is

\Omega_{\bar{q}q}^{\mathrm{vac}}=-2N_{c}\sum_{i=u,d,s}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,E_{i}^{*}(k),

(18)

which is ultraviolet divergent. Dimensional regularization of Eq. (
18
) yields
Skokov et al. 2010
;
Schaefer and Wagner 2012

\Omega_{\bar{q}q}^{\mathrm{vac}}=-\frac{N_{c}}{8\pi^{2}}\sum_{i=u,d,s}m_{i}^{*4}\ln\!\frac{m_{i}^{*}}{\Lambda},

(19)

where
\Lambda=600
MeV is the regularization scale. A change in
\Lambda
is absorbed by refitting the scalar-sector parameters to the same vacuum observables.

Because
m_{i}^{*}
depends on the scalar fields through Eq. (
8
),
\Omega_{\bar{q}q}^{\mathrm{vac}}
modifies the curvature of the effective potential in field space and contributes to the scalar gap equations derived in Sec.
II.4
. For comparison, we consider a no-sea variant (vac=0) in which
\Omega_{\bar{q}q}^{\mathrm{vac}}
is dropped, and the mesonic parameters are refitted to the same vacuum observables listed in Sec.
II.1
. The two parameter sets are listed in Table
1
.

II.4
Gap equations

The equilibrium mean fields at given
T
,
\mu_{B}
,
\mu_{Q}
,
\mu_{S}
are obtained by minimizing
\Omega
with respect to all dynamical variables,

\frac{\partial\Omega}{\partial\varphi_{a}}=0,\qquad\varphi_{a}\in\{\sigma,\zeta,\delta,\chi,\omega,\rho,\phi,\Phi,\bar{\Phi}\}.

(20)

The gap equations read

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\Omega}{\partial\sigma}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\Omega}{\partial\phi}

\displaystyle=\frac{\chi^{2}}{\chi_{0}^{2}}\,m_{\phi}^{2}\,\phi+8g_{4}\phi^{3}-g_{\phi}^{s}\,\rho_{s}=0,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\Omega}{\partial\Phi}

\displaystyle=-T^{4}\!\left[\frac{a(T)}{2}\bar{\Phi}+\frac{6b(T)\bigl(\bar{\Phi}-2\Phi^{2}+\bar{\Phi}^{2}\Phi\bigr)}{D}\right]

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{i=u,d,s}\frac{2TN_{c}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!d^{3}k\left[\frac{e^{-E_{-}}}{\mathcal{N}_{i}^{-}}+\frac{e^{-2E_{+}}}{\mathcal{N}_{i}^{+}}\right]=0,

II.5
Conserved-charge susceptibilities

The generalized susceptibilities of conserved charges are defined as derivatives of the scaled pressure with respect to the reduced chemical potentials
\hat{\mu}_{X}\equiv\mu_{X}/T

Bazavov et al. 2012
;
Borsanyi et al. 2012
,

\chi_{ijk}^{BQS}=\frac{\partial^{i+j+k}(p/T^{4})}{\partial\hat{\mu}_{B}^{\,i}\,\partial\hat{\mu}_{Q}^{\,j}\,\partial\hat{\mu}_{S}^{\,k}},

(35)

where
p=-\Omega
. Diagonal susceptibilities are denoted
\chi_{n}^{B}\equiv\chi_{n00}^{BQS}
,
\chi_{n}^{Q}\equiv\chi_{0n0}^{BQS}
, and
\chi_{n}^{S}\equiv\chi_{00n}^{BQS}
. Off-diagonal correlators between two charge sectors are denoted
\chi_{ij}^{XY}
, with
X,Y\in\{B,Q,S\}
and
i+j
equal to the total order. Mixed three-sector correlators retain the full
\chi_{ijk}^{BQS}
notation. At the stationary point of
\Omega
, first derivatives with respect to
\mu_{X}
reduce to partial derivatives at fixed fields
Chatterjee and Mohan 2012a
. At second and higher orders, the implicit response of the mean fields to chemical-potential variation contributes and must be tracked through Eqs. (
21
)-(
29
).

In the high-temperature limit, where all flavors become massless and noninteracting, the second-order susceptibilities approach their Stefan-Boltzmann values

\chi_{2}^{X}\big|_{\mathrm{SB}}=\sum_{i=u,d,s}X_{i}^{2},

(36)

yielding
\chi_{2}^{B}|_{\mathrm{SB}}=1/3
,
\chi_{2}^{Q}|_{\mathrm{SB}}=2/3
, and
\chi_{2}^{S}|_{\mathrm{SB}}=1
. Stefan-Boltzmann limits for the higher-order and off-diagonal quantities computed in this work are collected in Appendix
A
.

V
Discussion

The fermion vacuum term of Eq. (
19
) enters the PCQMF effective potential and modifies the gap-equation solutions. In vac=1, Fig.
1
(a) shows that the scalar field
\sigma
drops more steeply with temperature through the chiral crossover than in vac=0. This sharpening is opposite to the (2+1)-flavor PQM result, where the vacuum term smooths the crossover
Chatterjee and Mohan 2012a
;
Schaefer and Wagner 2012
. The light constituent quark masses follow, since they are set by the scalar fields through Eq. (
8
). In Fig.
3
(b),
-d\Delta_{l,s}/dT
develops an inflection near
T_{\mathrm{dec}}
in vac=1 and rises monotonically into the chiral peak in vac=0. The deconfinement-driven inflection in
\Delta_{l,s}(T)
near
T_{\mathrm{dec}}
becomes visible in vac=1 through the coupled gap equations. The chiral-deconfinement splitting is generated by the glue-to-Yang-Mills mapping of Eq. (
17
)
Haas et al. 2013
and is present in both variants. At higher derivative orders, Fig.
6
shows the chiral-deconfinement splitting resolved as a double-peak structure in
\chi_{4}^{B}
and as multiple zero crossings in
\chi_{6}^{B}
and
\chi_{8}^{B}
in both variants. The vac=1 peak-to-trough contrast exceeds that of vac=0.

The vac=1/vac=0 difference in the fourth-order off-diagonal tensor (Fig.
8
) depends on the temperature region of each peak. Chiral-crossover peaks are larger in vac=1, while strange-melting peaks are larger in vac=0. The BQ row sits in the first category, since light quarks dominate its correlators at the chiral crossover. The steeper
\sigma(T)
drop in vac=1 drives the vac=1 dominance throughout the chiral region of the tensor, where the chiral-deconfinement double-peak resolves at low
T
. The dominant peaks of the BS, QS, and BQS rows sit in the second category, where vac=0 produces the higher amplitudes despite the steeper
\zeta(T)
drop in vac=1 (Fig.
1
(b)). Both variants reproduce the same vacuum observables (Sec.
II.3
), so the strange-melting reversal lies in the high-
T
response of the two parameter sets rather than in the chiral-field response at low
T
alone.

The qualitative differences between vac=1 and vac=0 persist at finite
\mu_{B}
. The chiral-region vac=1 dominance and the strange-region vac=0 dominance both persist into the finite-density region (Sec.
IV.1
-
IV.3
). Along
T_{\mathrm{pc}}(\mu_{B})
,
R_{42}^{B}
changes sign in vac=1 but stays positive in vac=0, and
R_{51}^{B}
and
R_{62}^{B}
are persistently negative in vac=1 (Sec.
IV.5
).

PCQMF treats the scalar fields (
\sigma
,
\zeta
,
\delta
) and the Polyakov loop (
\Phi
,
\bar{\Phi}
) at the mean-field level, and fluctuations of these fields are absent. All three diagonal
\chi_{2}
underestimate the lattice (Sec.
III.2
), and the deficit is largest for
\chi_{2}^{Q}
. Pions are the lightest charged carriers and dominate
\chi_{2}^{Q}
in the hadronic phase, but in PCQMF they freeze at classical mean-field values and contribute no fluctuations. At low temperatures, PCQMF does not approach the HRG limit because hadrons are not realized as quasi-particle states. Beyond-mean-field methods such as the fRG incorporate both bosonic and fermionic fluctuations
Fu et al. 2021
.

VI
Summary and outlook

This work reports the first calculation of generalized susceptibilities of conserved charges in the PCQMF model with the fermion vacuum term. At
\mu_{B}=0
MeV, the calculation covers all diagonal
\chi_{n}^{B,Q,S}
through eighth order and all twelve independent fourth-order off-diagonal correlators. An independently refitted no-sea variant (vac=0) sets the comparison baseline. The chiral pseudocritical temperature is
T_{\mathrm{pc}}=170.5
MeV (vac=1) and
166.4
MeV (vac=0), and the deconfinement counterpart is
T_{\mathrm{dec}}=144.4
MeV and
146.6
MeV. The chiral-deconfinement splitting reaches
26.1
MeV in vac=1 and
19.8
MeV in vac=0. The inflection in
-d\Delta_{l,s}/dT
near
T_{\mathrm{dec}}
appears in vac=1 only. Higher derivative orders resolve the chiral-deconfinement splitting as twin maxima in
\chi_{4}^{B}
and
\chi_{6}^{Q}
, as twin minima in
\chi_{8}^{B}
and
\chi_{8}^{Q}
, and as multiple zero crossings in
\chi_{6}^{B}
. Among the fourth-order off-diagonal correlators, the BQ components carry vac=1 dominance across the chiral crossover. The dominant peaks of the BS, QS, and mixed BQS components occur at higher strange-melting temperatures, where vac=0 dominates. The model reproduces the qualitative crossover behavior seen on the lattice. Channel-by-channel magnitude differences are detailed in Sec.
III
.

The calculation extends to finite baryon chemical potential up to
\mu_{B}=500
MeV at
\mu_{Q}=\mu_{S}=0
. Along the pseudocritical line
T_{\mathrm{pc}}(\mu_{B})
,
R_{42}^{B}
starts at
\approx 0.42
in both variants and decreases monotonically with
\hat{\mu}_{B}=\mu_{B}/T_{\mathrm{pc}}
. The vac=1 curve crosses zero at
\hat{\mu}_{B}\approx 2.15
and continues to large negative values, while vac=0 stays positive across the full range.
R_{51}^{B}
and
R_{62}^{B}
start negative in vac=1 and grow more negative as
\hat{\mu}_{B}
increases. In vac=0, they start marginally positive and turn negative at
\hat{\mu}_{B}\approx 1.6
and
\hat{\mu}_{B}\approx 0.76
respectively. The vac=1 amplitudes far exceed vac=0 in both. The chiral and strange-melting dominance pattern observed at
\mu_{B}=0
persists into the finite-density region. The vac=1 curves dominate the chiral region, and the vac=0 curves dominate the strange-melting region.

In our calculations, we use
\mu_{Q}=\mu_{S}=0
throughout, while STAR BES-II data lie on the heavy-ion freeze-out trajectory of strangeness neutrality and
n_{Q}/n_{B}=0.4
(Sec.
IV.5
). A direct quantitative comparison requires model evaluation on this trajectory and is left for future work. Imposing these constraints self-consistently determines
\mu_{Q}(T,\mu_{B})
and
\mu_{S}(T,\mu_{B})
and enables direct comparison with the STAR cumulant ratios. Isentropes of constant entropy per net baryon density
s/n_{B}
trace the hydrodynamic cooling path at fixed
\sqrt{s_{NN}}
and connect model predictions to specific BES collision energies. Critical endpoint localization on the extended
(T,\mu_{B})
plane in both variants quantifies the vac=1/vac=0 shift. The earlier PCQMF endpoint estimate
Kumari and Kumar 2021
used neither the vacuum term nor the glue-to-Yang-Mills mapping.
