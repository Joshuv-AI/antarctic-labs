Elena Kopylova

Abstract
. We improve previous results on dispersive decay for 1D Klein-Gordon equation. We develop a novel approach, which allows us to establish the decay in more strong norms and weaken the assumption on the potential.

1.
Introduction

We are concerned with one-dimensional Klein–Gordon equation

\ddot{\psi}(x,t)=K\psi:=(\partial^{2}_{x}-m^{2}+V(x))\psi(x,t),\quad(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2},\quad m>0,

(1.1)

with real potential
V.
In vector form equation (
1.1
) reads

i\dot{\Psi}(t)={\mathcal{K}}\Psi(t),

(1.2)

where

\Psi(t)=\begin{pmatrix}\psi(t)\\
\dot{\psi}(t)\end{pmatrix},\quad{\mathcal{K}}=i\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
\Delta-m^{2}+V&0\end{pmatrix}.

(1.3)

We assume that
V(x)
is a continuous function, and

|V(x)|\leq C\langle x\rangle^{-\beta},\quad\quad\langle x\rangle=(1+|x|^{2})^{1/2},\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}

(1.4)

with some
\beta>0
.

Our goal is to prove dispersive decay estimates for these equations. This is a well-studied
area and our main contribution is to improve previous results.
To formulate them, we introduce the weighted Sobolev spaces
W^{\ell,p}_{\sigma}
,
\sigma\in{\mathbb{R}}
, associated with the norm

\|\psi\|_{W^{\ell,p}_{\sigma}}=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\ell}\|\langle x\rangle^{\sigma}\psi^{(k)}\|_{L^{p}},\quad 1\leq p\leq\infty,

where
L^{p}=L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})
. Denote
L^{p}_{\sigma}=W^{0,p}_{\sigma}
,

H^{\ell}_{\sigma}:=W^{\ell,2}_{\sigma}
, so
L^{2}_{\sigma}:=H^{0}_{\sigma}
.
Of course, the case
\sigma=0
corresponds to the usual
W^{\ell,p}
spaces without weight.
Denote

{{\mathcal{F}}}_{\sigma}=H^{1}_{\sigma}\oplus L^{2}_{\sigma},\qquad{\mathcal{L}}^{1}_{\sigma}=W^{1,1}_{\sigma}\oplus L^{1}_{\sigma},\qquad{\mathcal{L}}^{\infty}_{\sigma}=W^{1,\infty}_{\sigma}\oplus L^{\infty}_{\sigma}.

We recall (e.g.,
[
7
]
) that for
V
satisfies (
1.4
) with
\beta>1
, the spectrum of
{\mathcal{K}}
consists of
of a purely absolutely continuous part, covering
\Gamma:=(-\infty,-m)\cup(m,\infty)
, plus a finite number of eigenvalues located in
{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\Gamma
.
In addition, there could be resonances at the edges
\pm m
of the continuous spectrum.

Recall that the edges points
\lambda=\pm m
are resonances if the equation
K\psi=-m^{2}\psi
has nonzero solution

\psi\in L^{\infty}_{-1/2-}({\mathbb{R}})\setminus L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
(cf.
[
10
]
). In this case the operator
(K+m^{2})^{-1}
is unbounded.
In particular, the edges points
\lambda=\pm m
are resonances for free Klein-Gordon equation with
V(x)\equiv 0
.

Our first result read as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let (
1.4
) hold with
\beta>2
. Then the following decay holds

\|\mathrm{e}^{-it{\mathcal{K}}}P_{c}\|_{{{\mathcal{F}}}_{\sigma}\to{{\mathcal{F}}}_{-\sigma}}=\mathcal{O}(t^{-1/2}),\quad t\to\infty,\quad\sigma>1,

(1.5)

where
P_{c}=P_{c}({\mathcal{K}})
is the orthogonal projection in

L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
onto the continuous spectrum of
{\mathcal{K}}
.

Note that for the free Klein-Gordon equation
with
V=0
the estimate (
1.5
) follow from the explicit formula for the time evolution (see e.g.
[
4
]
).
Let us emphasize that we not require additional decay of
V
for (
1.5
) in the case when edges of the continuous spectrum are resonances.
In the remaining result we restrict ourselves to non-resonance case.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let (
1.4
) hold with
\beta>3
. Then, in the non-resonant case, the following decay holds

\|\mathrm{e}^{-it{\mathcal{K}}}P_{c}\|_{{{\mathcal{F}}}_{\sigma}\to{{\mathcal{F}}}_{-\sigma}}=\mathcal{O}(t^{-3/2}),\quad t\to\infty,\quad\sigma>3/2.

(1.6)

The decay (
1.6
) was obtained in
[
4
,
8
]

under more restrictive condition
\sigma>5/2
and
\beta>5
and under an additional assumption on the decay of
\nabla V(x)
.
Now we improve this result to
\sigma>3/2
assuming (
1.4
) with
\beta>3
only.

The low energy decay in
{\mathcal{L}}^{1}\to{\mathcal{L}}^{\infty}
norms was obtained in
[
3
]
(Theorem
4.1
below).
The approach of
[
3
]
depends on the fact that the scattering matrix is in the Wiener algebra (i.e. its Fourier transform
is integrable).
This result implies the decay (
1.5
) and (
1.6
) for the low energy part of solutions.
Unfortunately, the decay in
{\mathcal{L}}^{1}\to{\mathcal{L}}^{\infty}
norms for the operator

\mathrm{e}^{-it{\mathcal{K}}}P_{c}
does not hold (even in the case
V=0
), in contrast to the Schrödinger operator.
Namely, for the free Schrödinger operator the decay holds since its integral kernel is bounded for
|t|\geq 1
and decays uniformly:

\|\mathrm{e}^{-i\Delta t}(x,y)\|_{L^{1}\to L^{\infty}}=\sup\limits_{x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\Big|\frac{\mathrm{e}^{i|x-y|^{2}/4t}}{\sqrt{4\pi it}}\Big|\sim|t|^{-1/2},\quad|t|\geq 1.

(1.7)

On the other hand, the Green function
U(t,x,y)
of the free Klein-Gordon equation does not decay for
|x-y|\sim|t|
. Namely, for
t>0
,

U(t,x,y)=\frac{1}{2}\theta(t-|x-y|)J_{0}(m\sqrt{t^{2}-|x-y|^{2}})\sim\frac{1}{2},\quad|x-y|\sim t.

(1.8)

Here
J_{0}
is the Bessel function of order 0, and
\theta
is the Heavyside function.
This difference reflects the distinct character of the wave propagation
for the relativistic and nonrelativistic equations.
Namely, the singularities of solutions to the Schrödinger equations are concentrated at
t=0
and disappear at infinity for
t\neq 0
due to
infinite speed of propagation. On the other hand, in the case of Klein-Gordon equation, the singularities move
with bounded velocities, thus they are present forever in the space.

As shown in
[
3
]
, the decay of solution
\psi(t)
to (
1.1
) in
L^{\infty}
norm is possible only if
the initial data
(\psi(0),\dot{\psi}(0)
belong to generalized Sobolev space
W^{\frac{3}{2},1}\oplus W^{\frac{1}{2},1}
.

Our approach relies on the
Born expansion for high energy part of the dynamical group
{\mathcal{U}}(t)={\mathcal{U}}(\zeta,t)=\mathrm{e}^{-it{\mathcal{K}}}\zeta({\mathcal{K}}^{2})
, where

\zeta\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
,
\zeta(\omega)=0
for
\omega\leq m^{2}+1
, and

\zeta(\omega)=1
for
\omega\geq m^{2}+2
,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{U}}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{U}}_{0}(t)-i\int_{0}^{t}{\mathcal{U}}_{0}(t-s){\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{U}}_{0}(s)ds

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}{\mathcal{U}}_{0}(t-s){\mathcal{V}}\Big(\int_{0}^{s}{\mathcal{U}}_{0}(s-u){\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{U}}_{0}(u)du\Big)ds+{\mathcal{W}}(t),

where
{\mathcal{U}}_{0}(t)=\mathrm{e}^{-it{\mathcal{K}}_{0}}\zeta({\mathcal{K}}_{0}^{2})
, and

{\mathcal{V}}
is the matrix (
4.5
).

First we show that
\|{\mathcal{U}}_{0}(t)\|_{{\mathcal{F}}_{\sigma}\to{\mathcal{F}}_{-\sigma}}\leq C(1+|t|)^{-\sigma}
for any
\sigma>0
.
Then the decay of the next two terms (under appropriate values of
\beta
in (
1.4
))
follows by standard estimates for the convolutions.

Finally, we prove the decay in
{\mathcal{L}}^{1}\to{\mathcal{L}}^{\infty}
norms for remainder

{\mathcal{W}}(t)=\frac{i}{2\pi}\int\limits_{\Gamma}\!\mathrm{e}^{-i\omega t}\big(({\mathcal{R}}_{0}(\omega+\!i0){\mathcal{V}})^{3}{\mathcal{R}}(\omega+\!i0)\!-\!({\mathcal{R}}_{0}(\omega-\!i0){\mathcal{V}})^{3}{\mathcal{R}}(\omega-\!i0)\big)\zeta(\omega^{2})d\omega,

where
\Gamma=(-\infty,-m]\cup[m,\infty)
, and
{\mathcal{R}}
and

{\mathcal{R}}_{0}
stand for the resolvents of the operators
{\mathcal{K}}
and
{\mathcal{K}}_{0}
, respectively.
For the proof we use a representation of
{\mathcal{R}}
via Jost solutions and their properties
obtained in
[
3
]
.

Note that dispersion estimates of type (
1.5
)–(
1.6
) play an important role in proving asymptotic
stability of solitons and of scattering asymptotics in the associated one-dimensional nonlinear Klein-Gordon equations,
see for example
[
6
]
.

2.
Free Klein-Gordon equation

Here we consider the case
V=0
. Denote

{\mathcal{K}}_{0}=i\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
\Delta-m^{2}&0\end{pmatrix}

The resolvent
{\mathcal{R}}_{0}(\omega)=({\mathcal{K}}_{0}-\omega)^{-1}
of the operator
{\mathcal{K}}_{0}
can be expressed in terms of the resolvent

R_{0}(\omega)=(-\partial^{2}_{x}-\omega)^{-1}
of the Schrödinger operator as

{\mathcal{R}}_{0}(\omega)=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
-i&0\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}\omega&i\\
-i\omega^{2}&\omega\end{pmatrix}R_{0}(\omega^{2}-m^{2}).

For the dynamical group
\mathrm{e}^{-it{\mathcal{K}}_{0}}
of the free Klein-Gordon equation the spectral representation holds:

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\mathrm{e}^{-it{\mathcal{K}}_{0}}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{\Gamma}\mathrm{e}^{-it\omega}({\mathcal{R}}_{0}(\omega+i0)-{\mathcal{R}}_{0}(\omega-i0))\,d\omega

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{\Gamma}\mathrm{e}^{-i\omega t}\begin{pmatrix}\omega&i\\
-i\omega^{2}&\omega\end{pmatrix}\left(R_{0}((\omega+i0)^{2}\!-\!m^{2})-\!R_{0}((\omega-i0)^{2}\!-\!m^{2})\right)d\omega.

(2.1)

Recall, that the integral kernel of
R_{0}(\omega)
reads

R_{0}(\omega,x,y)=-\frac{e^{i\sqrt{\omega}|x-y|}}{2i\sqrt{\omega}}.

Hence,

[\mathrm{e}^{-it{\mathcal{K}}_{0}}](x,y)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\mathcal{M}}_{t}(k)\,\mathrm{e}^{i|y-x|k}\,dk,

(2.2)

where

{\mathcal{M}}_{t}(k)=\begin{pmatrix}\cos(t\sqrt{k^{2}+m^{2}})&\displaystyle\frac{\sin(t\sqrt{k^{2}+m^{2}})}{\sqrt{k^{2}+m^{2}}}\\
-\sqrt{k^{2}+m^{2}}\sin(t\sqrt{k^{2}+m^{2}})&\cos(t\sqrt{k^{2}+m^{2}})\end{pmatrix}.

(2.3)

Proposition 2.1
.

\|e^{-i{\mathcal{K}}_{0}t}\|_{{{\mathcal{F}}}_{\sigma}\to{{\mathcal{F}}}_{-\sigma}}\leq C(1+t)^{-1/2},\quad t\geq 0,\quad\sigma>1/2.

(2.4)

The proposition follows from two lemmas below.

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
\chi\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
the decay holds

\|e^{-i{\mathcal{K}}_{0}t}\chi({\mathcal{K}}_{0}^{2})\|_{{\mathcal{L}}^{1}\to{\mathcal{L}}^{\infty}}\leq{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}C}(1+t)^{-1/2},\quad t\geq 0.

(2.5)

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\zeta
be a smooth function such that
\zeta(\omega)=0
for
\omega\leq m^{2}+1
and

\zeta(\omega)=1
for
\omega\geq m^{2}+2
. Then the decay holds

\|e^{-i{\mathcal{K}}_{0}t}\zeta({\mathcal{K}}_{0}^{2})\|_{{{\mathcal{F}}}_{\sigma}\to{{\mathcal{F}}}_{-\sigma}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\sigma},\quad\sigma>0,\quad t\geq 0.

(2.6)

Proof of Lemma
2.2

Similarly to (
2.2
),

[\mathrm{e}^{-it{\mathcal{K}}_{0}}\chi({\mathcal{K}}_{0}^{2})](x,y)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\mathcal{M}}_{t}(k)\,\mathrm{e}^{i|y-x|k}\chi(k^{2}+m^{2})\,dk.

Note, that for an integral operator
T
,

\|T\|_{L^{1}\to L^{\infty}}=\sup\limits_{\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}=1,\|g\|_{L^{1}}=1}\langle f,Tg\rangle=\sup\limits_{x,y}|[T](x,y)|.

Hence, it suffices to prove that for
j=-1,0,1
and
p=0,1
,

\sup\limits_{x,y}|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{i\omega(k)t}\mathrm{e}^{i(y-x)k}\omega^{j}(k)k^{p}\chi(k^{2}+m^{2})\,dk|\leq Ct^{-1/2},\quad t\geq 1.

(2.7)

Here we denote
\omega(k):=\sqrt{k^{2}+m^{2}}
. The integral in (
2.7
) is
an oscillatory integral with the phase function
\phi(k)=\omega(k)+\frac{y-x}{t}k
, satisfying

|\phi^{\prime\prime}(k)|=\frac{m^{2}}{\omega^{3}(k)}\geq C(m,\chi),\quad(k^{2}+m^{2})\in{\rm supp}\,\chi.

Then (
2.5
) follows by the van der Corput lemma
[
11
, page 332]
.

Proof of Lemma
2.3

One has

[\mathrm{e}^{-it{\mathcal{K}}_{0}}\zeta({\mathcal{K}}_{0}^{2})](x,y)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\mathcal{M}}_{t}(k)\,\mathrm{e}^{i|y-x|k}\zeta(k^{2}+m^{2})\,dk.

(2.8)

Let
B_{j}(t)
,
j=0,1,-1
be integral operators with integral kernels

B_{j}(x,y,t)=B_{j}(x-y,t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\,\mathrm{e}^{i\omega(k)t}\mathrm{e}^{ik(x-y)}\zeta(k^{2}+m^{2})\omega^{j}(k)\,dk.

Thus, (
2.6
) is equivalent to

\|B_{0}(t)\|_{L^{2}_{\sigma}\to L^{2}_{-\sigma}}+\|B_{1}(t)\|_{H^{1}_{\sigma}\to L^{2}_{-\sigma}}+\|B_{-1}(t)\|_{L^{2}_{\sigma}\to H^{1}_{-\sigma}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\sigma}\!,~~\sigma>0

(2.9)

by (
2.3
) and (
2.8
). We also took into account that

\|B_{0}(t)\|_{H^{1}_{\sigma}\to H^{1}_{-\sigma}}=\|B_{0}(t)\|_{L^{2}_{\sigma}\to L^{2}_{-\sigma}}
, since

[B_{0}(t)f]^{\prime}(x)=[B_{0}(t)f^{\prime}](x)
.

First we prove (
2.9
) for integer values of
\sigma
. Namely, for
\ell=0,1,2,...
,

\|B_{0}(t)\|_{L^{2}_{\ell}\to L^{2}_{-\ell}}+\|B_{1}(t)\|_{H^{1}_{\ell}\to L^{2}_{-\ell}}+\|B_{-1}(t)\|_{L^{2}_{\ell}\to H^{1}_{-\ell}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\ell}\!,\quad t\geq 0.

(2.10)

Denote by
\hat{f}
the Fourier transform of
f
:

\hat{f}(k)=\int\mathrm{e}^{-iky}f(y)dy.

Then

\!\!\!\!\![B_{j}(t)f](x)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{i\omega(k)t}\,\mathrm{e}^{ikx}\zeta(k^{2}+m^{2})\hat{f}(k)\omega^{j}(k)\,dk.

(2.11)

In the case
\ell=0
, (
2.11
) implies

\|B_{0}(t)f\|_{L^{2}}=\|e^{i\omega(k)t}\zeta(k^{2}+m^{2})\hat{f}(k)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\hat{f}\|_{L^{2}}=C\|f\|_{L^{2}}

Further, using integration by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle[B_{0}(t)f](x)

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{i}{t}\int\mathrm{e}^{i\omega(k)t}\partial_{k}\Big(\mathrm{e}^{ikx}\,\frac{\omega(k)}{k}\zeta(k^{2}+m^{2})\hat{f}(k)\Big)\,dk

(2.12)

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle-\frac{x}{t}\int\mathrm{e}^{i\omega(k)t+ikx}\,\frac{\omega(k)}{k}\zeta(k^{2}+m^{2})\hat{f}(k)\,dk

\displaystyle\!\!+\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{i}{t}\int\mathrm{e}^{i\omega(k)t+ikx}\,\frac{\omega(k)}{k}\zeta(k^{2}+m^{2})\partial_{k}\hat{f}(k)\,dk

\displaystyle\!\!+\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{i}{t}\int\mathrm{e}^{i\omega(k)t+ikx}\hat{f}(k)\partial_{k}\Big(\frac{\omega(k)}{k}\zeta(k^{2}+m^{2})\Big)\,dk

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{1}(x,t)+g_{2}(x,t)+g_{3}(x,t),\quad t\geq 1.

Evidently,

\|g_{3}(t)\|_{L^{2}}=\frac{1}{t}\|\mathrm{e}^{i\omega(k)t}\hat{f}(k)\partial_{k}\Big(\frac{\omega(k)}{k}\zeta(k^{2}+m^{2})\Big)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{C}{t}\|\hat{f}\|_{L^{2}}=\frac{C}{t}\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

Similarly,

\|\langle x\rangle^{-1}g_{1}(x,t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{C}{t}\|f\|_{L^{2}},\quad\|g_{2}(x,t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{C}{t}\|\partial_{k}\hat{f}(k)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{C}{t}\|\langle x\rangle f(x)\|_{L^{2}}.

Hence, (
2.10
) with
\ell=1
for the first term follows. For
\ell=2,3,...
, (
2.10
) follows similarly by
\ell
- times integration by parts.
Note, that

[B_{-1}(t)f]^{\prime}(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{i\omega(k)t}\,\mathrm{e}^{ikx}\zeta(k^{2}+m^{2})\hat{f}(k)\frac{ik}{\omega(k)}\,dk.

[B_{1}(t)f]^{\prime}(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{i\omega(k)t}\,\mathrm{e}^{ikx}\zeta(k^{2}+m^{2})\widehat{f^{\prime}}(k)\frac{\omega(k)}{ik}\,dk.

Hence, the remaining terms in the left hand side of (
2.10
) are evaluated similarly.
Finally, (
2.10
) implies (
2.9
) by a suitable version of Stein-Weiss interpolation theorem:

Lemma 2.4
.

(cf.
[
1
, Theorem 5.4.1]
).
Let
\sigma_{0}\neq\sigma_{1}
, and let

T:L^{2}_{\sigma_{j}}\to L^{2}_{-\sigma_{j}}
with the norm
M_{j}=\sup\{\langle Tf,g\rangle:\|f\|_{L^{2}_{\sigma_{j}}}=\|g\|_{L^{2}_{\sigma_{j}}}=1\}
,

.
Then for
\sigma=\sigma(\theta)=(1-\theta)\sigma_{0}+\theta\sigma_{1}
,
\theta\in(0,1)
, one has

T:L^{2}_{\sigma}\to L^{2}_{-\sigma}~~{\rm with}~{\rm the}~{\rm norm}~~M\leq M_{0}^{1-\theta}M_{1}^{\theta}.

For the convenience of readers we give the proof in the Appendix A.

3.
Scattering properties of Schrödinger operator

Next we recall a few facts from scattering theory
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
of the Schrödinger operator
H=-(\partial^{2}_{x}+V)
.
Under the assumption

V\in L^{1}_{1}
there exist Jost solutions
f_{\pm}(x,k)=\mathrm{e}^{\pm ikx}h_{\pm}(x,k)
of

Hf=k^{2}f,\quad k\in\overline{{\mathbb{C}}_{+}},

normalized according to

h_{\pm}(x,k)\sim 1,\quad x\to\pm\infty.

Denote by
\mathcal{A}
the Banach algebra of Fourier transforms of integrable functions

\mathcal{A}=\left\{f(k):\,f(k)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{e}^{ikp}\hat{f}(p)dp,\,\hat{f}(\cdot)\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})\right\}

with the norm
\|f\|_{\mathcal{A}}=\|\hat{f}\|_{L^{1}}
, and by

\mathcal{A}_{1}
the corresponding unital Banach algebra

\mathcal{A}_{1}=\left\{f(k):\,f(k)=c+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{e}^{ikp}\hat{g}(p)dp,\,\hat{g}(\cdot)\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}),\,c\in{\mathbb{C}}\right\}

with the norm
\|f\|_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}=|c|+\|\hat{g}\|_{L^{1}}
.
Evidently,
\mathcal{A}
is a subalgebra of
\mathcal{A}_{1}
.
Then

h_{\pm}(x,\cdot)-1,~~h^{\prime}_{\pm}(x,\cdot)\in\mathcal{A},\quad\forall x\in{\mathbb{R}}.

(3.1)

Let

W(\varphi(x,k),\psi(x,k))=\varphi(x,k)\psi^{\prime}(x,k)-\varphi^{\prime}(x,k)\psi(x,k)

be the usual Wronskian, and set

W(k)=W(f_{-}(x,k),f_{+}(x,k)),\qquad W_{\pm}(k)=W(f_{\mp}(x,k),f_{\pm}(x,-k)).

T(k)=\frac{2ik}{W(k)},\quad R_{\pm}(k)=\mp\frac{W_{\pm}(k)}{W(k)},

Recall that
T
and
R_{\pm}
are the entries of the scattering matrix, that is, the transmission and reflection coefficients.
For
V\in L^{1}_{1}
,

T(k)-1\in\mathcal{A},\qquad R_{\pm}(k)\in\mathcal{A}

by
[
3
, Theorem 2.1]
. Introduce the function

\psi(x,y,k)=h_{+}(y,k)h_{-}(x,k)T(k),\qquad y\geq x,

(3.2)

and
\psi(x,y,k)=\psi(y,x,k)
for
y<x
.
Then the kernel
of the resolvent
R(\omega)=(H-\omega)^{-1}
for
\omega=k^{2}\pm i0
,
k>0
, can be expressed as

[R(k^{2}\pm i0)](x,y)=\mp\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\pm ik(y-x)}\psi(x,y,\pm k)}{2ik}.

(3.3)

Lemma 3.1
.

(cf.
[
3
, Lemma 2.3]
) Let
V\in L^{1}_{1}
.
Then
\psi(x,y,\cdot)\in\mathcal{A}_{1}

with some constant

, which does not depend on

Finally, in

where

4.

Theorem 4.2

Appendix A
Proof of Lemma
2.4

Suppose that
f
,
g
are simple functions, and for
0\leq{\rm Re}\,z\leq 1
define the functions

\varphi(z):=\varphi(x,z)=\langle x\rangle^{-\sigma+\sigma(z)}f(x),\quad\psi(z):=\psi(x,z)=\langle x\rangle^{\sigma-\sigma(z)}g(x),

where
\sigma(z):=(1-z)\sigma_{0}+z\sigma_{1}
. One has

\|\varphi(it)\|_{L^{2}_{-\sigma_{0}}}^{2}=\int\langle x\rangle^{-\sigma_{0}}|\langle x\rangle^{-\sigma+2(1-it)\sigma_{0}+it\sigma_{1})}f(x)|^{2}dx=\|f\|_{L^{2}_{-\sigma}}^{2}=1,

\|\varphi(1+it)\|_{L^{2}_{-\sigma_{1}}}^{2}=\int\langle x\rangle^{-\sigma_{1}}|\langle x\rangle^{-\sigma-2it\sigma_{0}+(1+it)\sigma_{1})}f(x)|^{2}dx=\|f\|_{L^{2}_{-\sigma}}^{2}=1.

Similarly,

\|\psi(it)\|_{L^{2}_{\sigma_{0}}}=\|\psi(1+it)\|_{L^{2}_{\sigma_{1}}}=1
.
The function

F(z)=\langle T\varphi(z),\psi(z)\rangle
is analytic in
0<{\rm Re}\,z<1
, and bounded and continuous in
0\leq{\rm Re}\,z\leq 1
.
Moreover,

\displaystyle|F(it)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|T(\varphi(it))\|_{L^{2}_{-\sigma_{0}}}\|\psi(it)\|_{L^{2}_{\sigma_{0}}}\leq M_{0},

\displaystyle|F(1+it)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|T(\varphi(1+it))\|_{L^{2}_{-\sigma_{1}}}\|\psi(1+it)\|_{L^{2}_{\sigma_{1}}}\leq M_{1}.

Besides,
\varphi(\theta)=f
,
\psi(\theta)=g
, and hence
F(\theta)=\langle Tf,g\rangle
.
Applying the Hadamard three-lines theorem (cf.
[
1
, Lemma 1.1.2]
), we obtain

M:=\sup\{\langle Tf,g\rangle:\|f\|_{L^{2}_{\sigma}}=\|g\|_{L^{2}_{\sigma}}=1\}\leq M_{0}^{1-\theta}M_{1}^{\theta}.
