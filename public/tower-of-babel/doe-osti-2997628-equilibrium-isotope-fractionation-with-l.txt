2 Introduction
Isotope exchange is a process where chemical species within a mixture exchange isotopes. For example, the 12 C ↔ 13 C ex-
change can occur in a mixture of carbon monoxide and dioxide. Granted enough time, this process reaches its equilibrium
where the forward and backward exchange rates become identical and concentrations of isotopologues - molecules that
differ only in their isotopic composition (e.g., 13 C16 O and 12 C16 O) - become time-independent. Fractionation of isotopes
between the species at such conditions is called equilibrium isotope fractionation. A thermochemical code is a computer
code that allows one to numerically equilibrate a mixture of species, i.e., to find their equilibrium concentrations subject
to various constraints. Since the isotope exchange is just a particular case of a chemical reaction, an arbitrary thermo-
chemical code should in principle allow one to study the equilibrium isotope fractionation once the complete description
of the thermodynamics of a mixture of chemical species and their relevant isotopologues is provided. Here, we report
on our progress in adopting the LANL thermochemical code magpie [1] to perform calculations of equilibrium isotope
fractionation.
 The report is organized as follows. General thermodynamics of equilibrium isotope fractionation and the approximation
of ideal mixing are discussed right before and in Sec. 3.1. How the thermodynamically complete equation of state (EOS)
for a species in isolation can be written using partition functions corresponding to separate classes of degrees of freedom
is described in Sec. 3.3. Sec. 3.4 shows how to use these partition functions to write down the equilibrium constant
of isotope fractionation. Sec. 3.5 introduces the so called β-factor, a quantity convenient in analysis of equilibrium
isotope fractionation, and discusses various definition of the β-factor in literature. Tab. 1 in the same section lists all the
approximations that lead from the most general postulate of Gibbs free energy minimization for the mixture of species to
Eqs. (42) and (3.5) that are very useful in practical calculations. Calculations of equilibrium isotope fraction for specific
examples of gaseous mixtures are demonstrated in Secs. 4 and 5. General theory and practical analysis of equilibrium
isotope fractionation in heterogeneous mixtures (gases and solids are present simultaneously) are discussed in Secs. 6 7.
Sec. 8 concludes.
 As mentioned above, the main focus of this report is to demonstrate how equilibrium isotope fractionation calculations
can be performed with magpie. Briefly, once thermodynamically complete EOSs for all the participating species are

G(P, T ; {νi }), this is as far as we can go.

8 Conclusion
In this work, we demonstrate how the LANL thermochemical code magpie, developed and maintained within the ASC-
PEM-HE program, can be used to perform analysis of equilibrium isotope fractionation. The required modifications to
the as-is magpie code and calculation procedures include:
 1. Adding EOS for isotopologues to the magpie EOS library.

 2. Adding a generalized ideal mixing, to be able to treat mixtures of gaseous and solid species.
 3. When required, tightening up numerical converge tolerances for magpie’s equilibrium solvers. This is because the
 accurate numerical analysis of equilibrium isotope fractionation often requires distinguishing relative deviations of
 species concentrations on the level of ∼ 10−3 -10−6 .

Using the modified code, we performed calculations for equilibrium isotope fractionation for pure gaseous and also gas-
solid mixtures. The obtained numerical results compare mostly favorably to the available literature data. The big part
of the present report is devoted to (i) providing the physical background for the equilibrium isotope fractionation, and
(ii) accurately listing the approximation that are used in derivations, see for example Tab. 1. We expect the developed
capability to become useful for multiple LANL programs.
