Invariant and periodic measures in classical spin systems on infinite lattices with highly degenerate noise
Tong Lu, Huaizhong Zhao

Abstract
In this paper, we consider the classical spin systems on unbounded lattices given by infinite-dimensional stochastic differential equations (SDEs). We assume that the stochastic forcing acts only on one particle. The other particles are not subject to stochastic forcing directly, but interact with their nearest neighbouring particles. Under the above highly degenerate noise setting, with some mild assumptions on the local interaction of each particle such as weak dissipation, we obtain the existence, uniqueness and the Markovian property of weak martingale solutions. We prove that the one-dimensional noise can propagate to any spin particle in the system in the sense that there exists a unique invariant/periodic measure and geometric ergodicity holds for the Markovian system when restricted to any finite volume. We then prove the finite-dimensional invariant measure and the average of lifted periodic measure are tight, and weak convergent subsequence gives an invariant and periodic measures of the infinite spin systems, respectively, in the time-homogeneous or time-periodic cases. Keywords: Spin systems; martingale problem; highly degenerate noise; weak dissipativity; invariant measures; periodic measures; Markovian processes

Introduction

Spin particle systems on lattices have a long and significant research history. Here, spin is the value of the configuration at each atom of the infinite lattice and represents the state of each of the infinite number of individual particles.
The dynamics in the continuous case are normally modelled by stochastic differential equations (SDEs) with solutions taking values in an infinite-dimensional space, while in the discrete case, it is normally described as a Markov chain, e.g. in the infinite ferromagnetic Ising model. Its invariant measure, if it exists, gives the equilibrium of configurations and is a Gibbs state if a certain consistency condition is satisfied when conditioned each finite subsystem (see Holley and Stroock
[
21
,
23
,
22
]
). It can be investigated as the long-time limit in the sense distributions and through weak approximations by a sequence of finite subsystems.
Therefore, studies of invariant measures of infinite-dimensional SDEs are of physical significance and have attracted considerable attention from mathematicians and physicists. Periodically driven spin systems have emerged as a fundamental paradigm in nonequilibrium statistical mechanics, exhibiting rich physical phenomena and important applications in Floquet engineering, time crystals, and topological phase transitions (see Abanin et al.
[
1
]
, Goldman and Dalibard
[
18
]
), but there is little understanding of the periodic measure or Gibbs state with time periodic pattern, which, though, is highly expected given the recent work on random periodicity that we will mention below.
In this paper, we take only the first step in this analysis to carry out the existence of periodic measures of spin systems on the infinite lattice.

To investigate the long-term behaviour of spin systems, two primary approaches have been developed. One is to interpret the classical Gibbs state as an invariant measure of a stochastic Langevin equation via “stochastic quantization method” (see Parisi and Wu
[
34
]
, Damgaard and Hüffel
[
12
]
, Albeverio et al.
[
3
,
2
,
4

); the other one is to describe the particle dynamics through the generator of a Markov process, thereby characterizing the long-time behaviour of the spin system via the ergodic properties of the process (see Holley and Stroock
[
22
]
, Zegarlinski
[
39
]
, Liggett
[
28
]
, Chen
[

, Xu and Zegarlinski
[

In particular, Da Prato and Zabczyk
[
10

employed the theory of dissipative operators in weighted Banach spaces to study the asymptotics of the associated transition semigroups. Albeverio et al.
[
3
]
and Zegarlinski
[

developed methodologies to construct a sequence of stochastic equations defined on finite volumes. In these works, convergence and ergodicity properties are established through the application of dissipative properties and the logarithmic Sobolev inequality, respectively. Won
[

developed an

[

used Galerkin approximation techniques combined with infinite-volume polymer measures to prove the existence and uniqueness of invariant measures in polymer dynamics. In a related development, Xu and Zegarlinski
[

examined the existence of invariant measures for an infinite-dimensional stochastic system driven by white

These works were all conducted under the consideration of non-degenerate noise. Žák
[

addressed the existence of equilibrium states for interacting particle systems linked to a specific class of degenerate elliptic operators using a probabilistic technique. However, it should be noted that the adopted noise structure is based on infinitely many noise sources and still falls short of achieving high degeneracy.

In this work, we focus on the second approach to investigate the long-time behaviour of a specific spin system. Its generator acts on any twice Fréchet differentiable function
h

\mathbb{Z}^{d}

\mathscr{L}h(x):=(a_{1,2}x_{2}+f(x_{1}))\partial_{1}h(x)+\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}(a_{i,i-1}x_{i-1}+a_{i,i+1}x_{i+1}+f(x_{i}))\partial_{i}h(x)+\frac{1}{2}{\partial_{1}}\partial_{1}h(x),

where

denotes the Gâteaux derivative along a family of orthogonal basis directions.
This generates an infinite-dimensional SDE

dX(t)=\left(AX(t)+F(X(t))\right)dt+BdW(t),\ t\geq 0,

(1.1)

on the chosen space, where

\begin{cases}A(x)=\left(a_{\gamma,\gamma-1}x_{\gamma-1}+a_{\gamma,\gamma+1}x_{\gamma+1}\right)_{\gamma},x=\left(x_{\gamma}\right)_{\gamma},\\
F(x)=\left(f_{\gamma}(x_{\gamma})\right)_{\gamma},x=\left(x_{\gamma}\right)_{\gamma},\\
B=(b_{\gamma,j})_{\gamma,j},\quad\text{with}\quad b_{1,1}=1,b_{\gamma,j}=0,\gamma,j\in\left\{\mathbb{Z}^{+}+1\right\}.\end{cases}

The noise term in this equation is highly degenerate, acting only on the first particle, with the stochasticity propagated to other particles through interactions with nearest neighbours. Since our conditions hold uniformly for all
f_{i}
and each local interaction term
f_{i}
plays a symmetric role, we denote them collectively as
f
for notational simplicity, so that
F(x)=\left(f(x_{\gamma})\right)_{\gamma}

For simplicity of presentation, we restrict our discussion to the results concerning
\mathbb{Z}^{+}

\mathbb{Z}^{d}
would require more elaborate formulations.

The first objective of this paper is to establish the existence and uniqueness of the solution to (
1.1
), and further to demonstrate the existence of invariant measures. We prove that the one-dimensional noise can propagate to any spin particle in the system in the sense that there exists a unique invariant/periodic measure and geometric ergodicity holds for the Markovian system when restricted to any finite volume. The solution is given in the sense of a weak martingale solution or the solution of a martingale problem. The weak solution framework makes it convenient to consider the approximation of the infinite lattice by a sequence of finite lattices. We first consider the spin system restricted to finite lattices and then study the limit when these bounded lattices increase to the infinite lattice. We prove that when on a finite lattice, when the volume is getting sufficiently large, the spin on any fixed atom becomes asymptotically the same. This result leads to the convergence in law and their limit is the unique solution to the martingale problem and is Markovian. Then the tightness of the invariant measures on the finite-volume lattices gives the result of a weak limit which is the invariant measure of the spin system on the infinite lattice. It is important to note that this paper does not address the uniqueness of the invariant measure.

The second aim of this paper is to study infinite-dimensional time-inhomogeneous stochastic differential equations as follows:

dX(t)=\left(A(t)X(t)+F(t,X(t))\right)dt+BdW(t),\ t\geq s,

(1.2)

where both
A(t)x=\left(a_{\gamma,\gamma-1}(t)x_{\gamma-1}+a_{\gamma,\gamma+1}(t)x_{\gamma+1}\right)_{\gamma}
and
F(t,x)=\left(f(t,x_{\gamma})\right)_{\gamma}
coefficients are
T
-periodic in the time variable.
Consequently, the interaction term in the corresponding generator becomes time-dependent.
Although the long-time behaviour of time-homogeneous systems is well-characterized by invariant measures, the corresponding theory for time-inhomogeneous systems like our periodically forced SDE (
1.2
) requires a more general framework of periodic measures and their ergodic theory that was first rigorously developed in Feng and Zhao
[
14
]
. Recent theoretical advances have significantly extended the understanding of such periodic measures. Notable progress includes breakthroughs in the existence and uniqueness of periodic measures of Markov processes (Feng et al.
[
15
]
), studies on quasi-periodic systems (Feng et al.
[
13
]
), and extensions to infinite-dimensional settings, as exemplified by results on the 2D stochastic Navier–Stokes equations (Liu and Lu
[
30
]
).

For our problem, we establish the existence of periodic measures for equation (
1.2
). Our approach proceeds by first considering finite-dimensional approximations and their associated periodic measures
\mu_{n,s}
. This is followed by proving the tightness of the lifted measures
\bar{\tilde{\mu}}_{n}:=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\tilde{\mu}_{n,s}ds
on an extended state space, from which we extract a subsequence
\mu_{n,s_{n}}
that converges to a measure
\nu_{s_{*}}
. The latter is invariant under the one-step transition probability kernel
\mathcal{P}(s_{*},s_{*}+T)
of (
1.2
), which ultimately yields the desired periodic measure. This construction not only provides a new result of the existence, but also clarifies the fundamental pattern of stochastic periodic systems on infinite lattice with the limiting distribution which is invariant with respect to the discrete time Markovorvian semigroup
\mathcal{P}(s_{*},s_{*}+nT),n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and varies periodically in time. The periodic measure serves as the periodic ”equilibria” in the nonequilibrium spin system.

Although the finite-volume approximation is a classical idea used also in the study of the time-homogeneous infinite-lattice spin system, the tightness of the average lifted invariant measures of the finite-volume lattice system and its disintegration procedure introduced in this paper for periodic measures are highly nontrivial and novel. In the absence of direct access to tightness results for finite-dimensional periodic measures, we proceed by first lifting the finite-dimensional truncation of the time-inhomogeneous equation to an time-homogeneous system. For the lifted finite-dimensional time-homogeneous system, we establish the existence of invariant measures and consequently derive their tightness. Using this tightness property, we then construct invariant measures for the one-step transition probabilities of the infinite-dimensional time-inhomogeneous equation. This approach allows us to establish the existence of periodic measures using the framework developed by Feng et al.
[
15
]
.

The organization of this paper is as follows. In the second section, we investigate the time-homogeneous finite-dimensional systems and verify, under certain conditions, that the transition semi-group corresponding to the finite-dimensional SDEs satisfies the criteria of the Harris-Meyn-Tweedie theorem, thus establishing the existence, uniqueness, and geometric ergodicity of invariant measures. In the third section, we approximate the solutions of time-homogeneous infinite-dimensional SDEs using the tightness of the solution’s distributions from time-homogeneous finite-dimensional equations, proving the existence, uniqueness, and Markov property of these solutions, which leads to the establishment of invariant measures. The fourth section is devoted to studying periodic measures for finite-dimensional equations with periodic coefficients and demonstrates the existence of periodic measures. In the last section, we give a proof of the main result in Section 2.

3.1
The concepts of solutions: weak martingale solutions and solutions to a martingale problem

The objective of this section is to introduce two distinct definitions of solutions corresponding to equation (

The definition of the martingale problem depends on the choice of test functions. Following the approach of Kallianpur and Xiong

\mathscr{L}h(x):=(a_{1,2}x_{2}+f(x_{1}))\partial_{1}h(x)+\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}(a_{i,i-1}x_{i-1}+a_{i,i+1}x_{i+1}+f(x_{i}))\partial_{i}h(x)+\frac{1}{2}{\partial_{1}}\partial_{1}h(x),

where
\partial_{i}
are given by that for Fréchet differentiable functions
h
with Fréchet derivative
h^{\prime}
,
\partial_{i}h:=<h^{\prime},e^{i}>_{2,\rho}
where
e^{i}=(e^{i}_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}}
with
e^{i}_{i}=1
and
e^{i}_{j}=0
for
j\neq i
.

Definition 3.5
.

(solution to the martingale problem). Given a probability measure
\mu
on
\ell_{\rho}^{2\theta}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})
, a probability measure
\mathbf{P}
on
(\Omega_{Spin},\mathscr{B}^{Spin})
is called a solution to the martingale problem associated to (
3.2
) with initial law
\mu
if

(i)

for every
T>0

\mathbf{P}\left(\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\|X(t)\|_{2\theta,\rho}<\infty\right)=1;

(ii)

for every
h\in C_{c}^{2,Cly}(\ell_{\rho}^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{+}))
(or
h\in C^{2,Cyl}(\ell_{\rho}^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{+}))
), the process
M^{h}(t)
defined
\mathbf{P}-a.s.
on
(\Omega_{Spin},\mathscr{B}^{Spin})
as

M^{h}(t):=h(X(t))-h(X(0))-\int_{0}^{t}\mathscr{L}h(X(s))ds,

is a continuous (local) martingale under
(\Omega_{Spin},\mathscr{B}^{Spin},\mathbf{P},\{\mathscr{B}^{Spin}_{t}\})
;

(iii)

\mu=\mathbf{P}\circ X(0)^{-1}
.

The boundedness of the operator
B
implies the equivalence between the two alternative conditions imposed on
h
in Definition
3.5
(ii). We now present the theorem connecting the notions of weak martingale solutions and solutions to the martingale problem.

Theorem 3.6
.

The existence of a solution
\mathbf{P}
to the martingale problem is equivalent to the existence of a weak martingale solution
(\tilde{X},\tilde{W})
,
(\tilde{\Omega},\tilde{\mathscr{F}},\tilde{\mathbb{P}})
,
\{\tilde{\mathscr{F}}_{t}\}
. The two solutions are related by
\mathbf{P}=\tilde{\mathbb{P}}\circ\tilde{X}^{-1}
.

Proof.

Step 1. Let
\mu
be a probability measure on
\ell_{\rho}^{2\theta}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})
and
(\tilde{X},\tilde{W})
,
(\tilde{\Omega},\tilde{\mathscr{F}},\tilde{\mathbb{P}})
,
\{\tilde{\mathscr{F}}_{t}\}
be a weak martingale solution of equation (
3.2
) with initial condition
\mu
. Let
\mathbf{P}:=\tilde{\mathbb{P}}\circ\tilde{X}^{-1}
be the law of the canonical process
X
on
\Omega_{Spin}
. Our objective is to prove that
\mathbf{P}
constitutes a solution to the martingale problem associated to (
3.2
) with initial law
\mu
. From conditions (ii) and (iii) of Definition

, we deduce

\mathbf{P}\left(\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\|X(t)\|_{2\theta,\rho}<\infty\right)=\tilde{\mathbb{P}}\left(\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\|\tilde{X}(t)\|_{2\theta,\rho}<\infty\right)=1

and

\mathbf{P}\circ(X(0))^{-1}=\tilde{\mathbb{P}}\circ(\tilde{X}(0))^{-1}=\mu.

Moreover, for a function

. By It

can be expressed as

\tilde{M}_{t}^{h}=\int_{0}^{t}<h^{\prime}(\tilde{X}(s)),BdW(s)>_{2,\rho}=\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{1}h(\tilde{X}(s))dW_{1}(s),

where

represents the inner product in
\ell_{\rho}^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})
.
Introducing the stopping time

condition (ii) of Definition

yields
\lim\limits_{m\rightarrow\infty}S_{m}=\infty

\tilde{\mathbb{P}}-a.s.
Therefore, the processes

is continuous local martingale. Based on Lemma 4.1 of Flandoli

is a local martingale under
(\Omega_{Spin},\mathscr{B}^{Spin},\mathbf{P},\{\mathscr{B}^{Spin}_{t}\})
. For

, analogous arguments apply, and thus we omit the details.

be a solution to the martingale problem associated to (

) with initial law

. The particular selection

. With

&\quad-\int_{0}^{t}\left(a_{j,j-1}X_{j-1}(s)+a_{j,j+1}X_{j+1}(s)+f(X_{j}(s))\right)X_{i}(s)ds,\quad\text{for}\quad(i,j)\neq(1,1),\end{split}

and

&\quad+a_{i,i+1}X_{i+1}(s)+f(X_{i}(s))ds\\

&\quad+\int_{0}^{t}(M_{s}^{(j)}-M_{t}^{(j)})\left(a_{i,i-1}X_{i-1}(s)+a_{i,i+1}X_{i+1}(s)+f(X_{i}(s))\right)ds\\

\begin{split}&[M^{(i)},M^{(j)}]_{t}=0,\quad\text{for}\quad(i,j)\neq(1,1),\\
&[M^{(1)},M^{(1)}]_{t}=t,\quad\text{for}\quad(i,j)=(1,1).\end{split}

By Lévy martingale characterization of Brownian motion it follows that

is a 1-dimensional Brownian motion on

. Thus, there is a cylindrical Wiener process

defined on an extension

of

with

such that
(\tilde{X}(t)=X(t),\tilde{W}_{t})
,
(\tilde{\Omega},\tilde{\mathscr{F}},\tilde{\mathbb{P}})
,
\{\tilde{\mathscr{F}}_{t}\}
is a weak martingale solution of equation (
3.2
) with initial condition
\mu
.
∎

Corollary 3.7
.

Given a probability measure
\mu
on
\ell_{\rho}^{2\theta}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})
, the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

Uniqueness holds for solutions of the martingale problem associated to (

(ii)

Lemma 3.10

Let

Proof.

The sufficiency of the result has been established by Žák

j=1,\cdots,l
and continuous linear functional
q_{m}(x)=\left(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{m},0_{i\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\backslash\Lambda_{m}}\right)

:\ell_{\rho}^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
. If
P(C)=Q(C)
for every
C\in\mathcal{C}
, then
P=Q
.

Proof.

Since
P(C)=Q(C)
for every
C\in\mathcal{C}
, and
\mathcal{C}
is a
\pi-
class, it follows from the basic uniqueness result in measure theory that
P(C)=Q(C)
for every
C
in the
\sigma
-algebra of
\mathcal{E}(\Omega_{Spin})
generated by
\mathcal{C}
. Thus, it remains to show that
\mathcal{E}(\Omega_{Spin})
coincides with the Borel
\sigma
-algebra of
\Omega_{Spin}
. As
\Omega_{Spin}
is a separable metric space under the topology of uniform convergence on compact sets, there exists a countable basis for its topology, which is of the form

B_{\Omega_{Spin}}(\omega^{0},\epsilon,n):=\{\omega\in\Omega_{Spin};\sup\limits_{0\leq t\leq n}\|\omega(t)-\omega^{0}(t)\|_{2,\rho}<\epsilon\}

with
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\omega^{0}
belongs to a dense subset of
\Omega_{Spin}
,
\epsilon
is a positive rational number. In fact, since every finite-dimensional closed set
C
is a Borel set, it suffices to show that all sets
B_{\Omega_{Spin}}(\omega^{0},\epsilon,n)
(and therefore all open and Borel) sets are contained in
\mathcal{E}(\Omega_{Spin})
.

For the set
\{x\in\ell_{\rho}^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{+});\|x\|=1\}
, there exists a dense countable subset
\{x^{j}\}
satisfying
x^{j}=(x^{j}_{1},x^{j}_{2},\cdots,x^{j}_{m(j)},0_{i\in\mathbb{Z}+\backslash\Lambda_{m(j)}})
for some
m(j)\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\{\zeta_{j}\}
be a sequence of continuous linear functions in
\ell_{\rho}^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})
such that
\zeta_{j}=(z_{1},\cdots,z_{m(j)},0_{i\in\mathbb{Z}+\backslash\Lambda_{m(j)}})
,
\|\zeta_{j}\|_{2,\rho}=1
and
\zeta_{j}(x^{j})=1
(existence follows from the Hahn-Banach extension theorem, see Brezis
[
8
]
). Let
\bar{B}(x^{0},a):=\{x\in\ell_{\rho}^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{+});\|x-x^{0}\|\leq a\}
be the closed ball of radius
\epsilon
centered at
x^{0}
in
\ell_{\rho}^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})
. We claim
\bar{B}(0,1)=K:=\bigcap_{j\geq 0}\{x\in\ell_{\rho}^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{+});|\zeta_{j}(x)|\leq 1\}
. Since obviously
\bar{B}(0,1)\subset K
, it suffices to show that the reverse inclusion holds. Let
y\in\ell_{\rho}^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})
with
\|y\|_{2,\rho}>1
be arbitrary and set
\hat{y}=y/\|y\|_{2,\rho}
. By the density of
x^{j}
’s, there exists a subsequence
x^{k_{j}}
such that
\|x^{k_{j}}-\hat{y}\|\leq\frac{1}{j}
. Since
1=\zeta_{k_{j}}(x^{k_{j}})=\zeta_{k_{j}}(x^{k_{j}}-\hat{y})+\zeta_{k_{j}}(\hat{y})
, we deduce that
\zeta_{k_{j}}(\hat{y})\geq 1-\frac{1}{j}
. By linearity, this implies that
\zeta_{k_{j}}(y)\geq\|y\|(1-\frac{1}{j})
, so that there exists a sufficiently large
j
such that
\zeta_{k_{j}}(y)>1
. This shows that
y\notin K
and we conclude that
K\subset\bar{B}(0,1)
as required.

Let
\{Q_{l}:=\{t_{1},\cdots,t_{l}\};l\in\mathbb{N}\}
be a sequence of subsets of
[0,n]\bigcap\mathbb{Q}
with finite elements such that
\bigcup_{l=1}^{\infty}Q_{l}=[0,n]\bigcap\mathbb{Q}
. Then we observe that

\displaystyle B_{\Omega_{Spin}}(\omega^{0},\epsilon,n)

\displaystyle=\{\omega\in\Omega_{Spin};\sup\limits_{0\leq t\leq n,t\in\mathbb{Q}}\|\omega(t)-\omega^{0}(t)\|<\epsilon\}

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{l=1}^{\infty}\{\omega\in\Omega_{Spin};\sup\limits_{0\leq t\leq n,t\in Q_{l}}\|\omega(t)-\omega^{0}(t)\|<\epsilon\}

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{l=1}^{\infty}\{\omega\in\Omega_{Spin};\|\omega(t_{1})-\omega^{0}(t_{1})\|<\epsilon,\cdots,\|\omega(t_{l})-\omega^{0}(t_{l})\|<\epsilon\}

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{l=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{u=1}^{\infty}\{\omega\in\Omega_{Spin};\|\omega(t_{1})-\omega^{0}(t_{1})\|\leq\epsilon-\frac{1}{u},\cdots,\|\omega(t_{l})-\omega^{0}(t_{l})\|\leq\epsilon-\frac{1}{u}\}

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{l=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{u=1}^{\infty}\{\omega\in\Omega_{Spin};\omega(t_{1})\in\bar{B}(\omega^{0}(t_{1}),\epsilon-\frac{1}{u}),\cdots,\omega(t_{l})\in\bar{B}(\omega^{0}(t_{l}),\epsilon-\frac{1}{u})\}

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{l=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{u=1}^{\infty}\bigcap_{j=1}^{\infty}\{\omega\in\Omega_{Spin};\omega(t_{1})\in K_{1,j},\cdots,\omega(t_{l})\in K_{l,j}\}

where
K_{i,j}=\{y=(\epsilon-\frac{1}{u})(x+\omega^{0}(t_{i}));x\in\ell_{\rho}^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{+}),|\zeta_{j}(x)|\leq 1\}
for
i=1,\cdots,l
. Therefore, it is clear that
B_{\Omega_{Spin}}(\omega^{0},\epsilon,n)\in\mathcal{E}(\Omega_{Spin})
and the lemma is established.
∎

Definition 3.20
.

A function
h:\Omega_{Spin}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is called a finite-dimensional cylinder function, if there exists
m,l\in\mathbb{N}
, a finite subset
\Lambda_{m}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
g:\mathbb{R}^{l\Lambda_{m}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that
h(\omega)=g(\Pi_{\Lambda_{m}}\omega(t_{1}),\cdots,\Pi_{\Lambda_{m}}\omega(t_{l}))
. Moreover, if
g
is bounded and Lipschitz i.e. there is a constant
L>0
such that

|g(z)-g(\tilde{z})|\leq L\|z-\tilde{z}\|_{\mathbb{R}^{l\Lambda_{m}}}\ \forall z,\tilde{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{l\Lambda_{m}},

we say that
h
is cylindrically bounded and Lipschitz, such functions
h
form a function space denoted as
C_{b,lip}^{Cly}(\Omega_{Spin})
.

Corollary 3.21
.

Let
P
and
Q
be two probability measures on
\Omega_{Spin}
. Then
P=Q
if and only if
Ph=Qh
for every
h\in C_{b,lip}^{Cly}(\Omega_{Spin})
.

Proof.

The necessity is evident, thus we only need to prove the sufficiency. According to Lemma
3.19
, it suffices to demonstrate that
P(C)=Q(C)
for every
C\in\mathcal{C}
. For the form of set

given in Lemma
3.19
, we define a sequence of functions on

\displaystyle h_{n}(\omega)

\displaystyle=[1-n\inf\limits_{z\in C}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{l}\|\omega(t_{j})-z(t_{j})\|_{2,\rho}]\vee 0

\displaystyle=[1-n\inf\limits_{z\in C}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{l}(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}|\omega_{i}(t_{j})-z_{i}(t_{j})|^{2}\rho(i))^{1/2}]\vee 0

\displaystyle=[1-n\inf\limits_{z\in C}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{l}(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}|\omega_{i}(t_{j})-z_{i}(t_{j})|^{2}\rho(i))^{1/2}]\vee 0

(3.32)

By (

), we observe that

. Hence, the assumptions yield

∎

3.5
Solution as a Markov process

This section is devoted to proving the Markov property for the probability measure
\{\mathbf{P}^{x}\}_{x\in\ell^{2\theta}_{\rho}(\mathbb{Z^{+}})}
solving the associated martingale problem. As preliminary results for the main theorem, we first present two fundamental lemmas that will support our arguments. The first of these lemmas provides a rigorous characterization of how the solutions continuously depend on their initial values.

Lemma 3.23
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
x\in\ell^{2\theta}_{\rho}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})
and
T\geq 0
be given. Then for all
\epsilon>0
, there exist
\delta>0
and
V\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying: whenever
n\geq V
and
y\in\{z\in\ell^{2\theta}_{\rho}(\mathbb{Z}^{+});\|z-x\|_{2,\rho}\leq\delta\}
, we have

\mathbb{E}\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\|X_{(k)}^{(n),x}(t)-X_{(k)}^{(n),y}(t)\|_{\mathbb{R}^{\Lambda_{k}}}^{2}\leq\epsilon.

Proof.

By employing the same notation and estimation techniques as in Lemma
3.18
, we can show that for arbitrary but fixed
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
x,y\in\ell^{2\theta}_{\rho}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})
,
0\leq t\leq T
and sufficiently large
n
, the following estimate holds:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\|X^{(n),x}_{(k)}(t)-X^{(n),y}\right\|_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}\|x_{(k)}-y_{(k)}\|^{2}_{k}+\frac{\tilde{C}t^{2}}{N^{\gamma}\rho^{2\theta+\gamma}(k)}+\tilde{C}(\tilde{M}+L^{2}(N))t\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left\|X^{(n),x}_{(k+1)}(t_{1})-X^{(n),y}_{(k+1)}(t_{1})\right\|_{k+1}^{2}dt_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\cdots

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{\mathbf{V}}_{1}(N)+\tilde{\mathbf{V}}_{2}(N)+\tilde{\mathbf{V}}_{3}(N).

Here

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{V}}_{1}(N)=\frac{\left[\tilde{C}(\tilde{M}+L^{2}(N))\right]^{l}t^{2l}}{(2l-1)!!\rho(k+l)},

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{V}}_{2}(N)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{l}\frac{\left[\tilde{C}(\tilde{M}+L^{2}(N))\right]^{i-1}t^{2i}}{(2i-1)!!N^{\gamma}\rho^{2\theta+\gamma}(k+i-1)},

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{V}}_{3}(N)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{l}\tilde{C}\left[\tilde{C}(\tilde{M}+L^{2}(N))\right]^{i-1}\|x_{(k+i-1)}-y_{(k+i-1)}\|^{2}_{k+i-1}\frac{t^{2(i-1)}}{(2i-3)!!},

where
\gamma
is a positive constant to be determined. Following arguments analogous to those in Lemma
3.18
and using Assumption
3
, we find that for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\tilde{N}
such that
\tilde{\mathbf{V}}_{2}(N)\leq\frac{\epsilon}{3}
holds for all
N\geq\tilde{N}
. Note that

\displaystyle\rho(k+i-1)\|x_{(k+i-1)}-y_{(k+i-1)}\|_{k+i-1}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k+i-1}|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{2}\rho(j)\frac{\rho(k+i-1)}{\inf\limits_{1\leq p\leq k+i-1}\rho(p)}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\sum\limits_{j=k+i}^{\infty}|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{2}\rho(j)\leq\tilde{C}\|x-y\|_{2,\rho}^{2}.

Consequently, for this particular
\tilde{N}
, through computations and rescaling
\tilde{c}
analogous to (
3.31
), we obtain the estimate

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{V}}_{3}(\tilde{N})

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{\left[\tilde{C}(\tilde{M}+L^{2}(\tilde{N}))\right]^{i-1}t^{2(i-1)}}{(2i-3)!!\rho(k+i-1)}\|x-y\|^{2}_{2,\rho}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{\left[\tilde{C}(\tilde{M}+L^{2}(\tilde{N}))\right]^{i-1}t^{2(i-1)}}{(2i-3)!!}e^{r(k+i-1)}\|x-y\|^{2}_{2,\rho}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{\left[\tilde{C}(1+L^{2}(\tilde{N}))\right]^{i-1}}{(2i-3)!!}\|x-y\|^{2}_{2,\rho}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}\|x-y\|_{2,\rho}^{2}\left(1+\sqrt{\tilde{C}(1+L^{2}(\tilde{N}))}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{\sqrt{\tilde{C}(1+L^{2}(\tilde{N}))}^{2i+1}}{(2i+1)!!}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}\|x-y\|_{2,\rho}^{2}\left(1+\sqrt{\tilde{C}(1+L^{2}(\tilde{N}))}\exp\{\frac{\tilde{C}(1+L^{2}(\tilde{N}))}{2}\}\right).

With

\delta=\frac{\epsilon}{3}\left[\tilde{C}\left(1+\sqrt{\tilde{C}(1+L^{2}(\tilde{N}))}\exp\{\frac{\tilde{C}(1+L^{2}(\tilde{N}))}{2}\}\right)\right]^{-1},

we have
\tilde{\mathbf{V}}_{3}(\tilde{N})\leq\frac{\epsilon}{3}
,
\forall y\in\ell^{2\theta}_{\rho}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})
with
\|x-y\|_{2,\rho}^{2}\leq\delta
. Following arguments analogous to those in the proof of Lemma
3.18
, we conclude that there exists
V
such that
\tilde{\mathbf{V}}_{1}(\tilde{N})\leq\frac{\epsilon}{3}
holds for all
l\geq V
. The assertion of the lemma follows by synthesizing the above analysis.
∎

Remark 3.24

Lemma 3.25

Let

The following is our main result on the Markov property of solutions to (

).

Theorem 3.26

Let

\mathscr{B}(\ell^{2\theta}_{\rho}(\mathbb{Z}^{+}))

\ \mathbf{P}^{x}\left(X(\cdot+t)\in C\mid\mathscr{B}^{Spin}_{t}\right)=\phi\left(X(t)\right),\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbf{P}^{x}-a.s.\hskip 10.00002pt\forall C\in\mathscr{B}^{Spin},\,t\geq 0,

3.2

3.25

, the canonical process
X(t)
admits a
\mathbf{P}^{x}
-modification on
\mathscr{B}^{Spin}_{t}

\mathscr{B}^{Spin}_{t}
-measurable with values in

It is obvious that (

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{x}(\mathbf{1}_{C}(X(\cdot+t))\mathbf{1}_{B})=\mathbf{E}^{x}(\phi\left(X(t)\right)\mathbf{1}_{B}).

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{x}[\mathbf{1}_{C}(X(\cdot+t))|\mathscr{B}^{Spin}_{t}]=\mathbf{E}^{X(t)}[\mathbf{1}_{C}(X(\cdot))].

\displaystyle\int_{B}h(X(\cdot+t))d\mathbf{P}^{x}=\int_{B}\varphi(X(t))d\mathbf{P}^{x}.

As the indicator function

4
Time-inhomogeneous systems

In this section, we address the case where the coefficients of the equation depend on time in a periodic manner. Under such circumstances, the resulting solutions become time-inhomogeneous, rendering traditional methods based on invariant measures inadequate for studying long-time behaviour. To characterize the asymptotic dynamics of the system, we thus employ the concept of periodic measures (Feng and Zhao
[
14
]
). This necessitates an investigation into the existence of periodic measures for the equation.

p>2

\hat{\text{o}}
’s formula to
e^{\beta t}\|X^{(n),s,x^{(n)}}(t)\|^{p}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
and invoking the conditions in Assumption
6
, we obtain

\displaystyle e^{\beta t}\|X^{(n),s,x^{(n)}}(t)\|^{p}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}

\displaystyle\leq e^{\beta s}\|x^{(n)}\|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{p}+\int_{s}^{t}e^{\beta r}\|X^{(n),s,x^{(n)}}(r)\|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{p-2}\left(\beta\|X^{(n),s,x^{(n)}}(r)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\hskip 10.00002pt+pX^{(n),s,x^{(n)}}(r)\cdot\left(A_{n}(r)X^{(n),s,x^{(n)}}(r)+F_{n}(r,X^{(n),s,x^{(n)}}(r))\right)+\frac{p(p-1)}{2}tr(B_{n}B_{n}^{*})\right)dr

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\int_{s}^{t}pe^{\beta r}\|X^{(n),s,x^{(n)}}(r)\|^{p-2}X^{(n),s,x^{(n)}}(r)B_{n}dW^{(n)}(r)

\displaystyle\leq e^{\beta s}\|x^{(n)}\|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{p}+\int_{s}^{t}e^{\beta r}\|X^{(n),s,x^{(n)}}(r)\|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{p-2}\left((\beta+p(2M-\lambda)\|X^{(n),s,x^{(n)}}(r)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}+p\eta\right.

\displaystyle\left.\hskip 10.00002pt+\frac{p(p-1)}{2}\right)dr+\int_{s}^{t}pe^{\beta r}\|X^{(n),s,x^{(n)}}(r)\|^{p-2}X^{(n),s,x^{(n)}}(r)B_{n}dW^{(n)}(r).

Since the condition
\lambda>2M+\frac{1}{2}
holds, we first set
2\epsilon:=p(\lambda-2M)>0
and choose
\beta=p(\lambda-2M)-\epsilon>0
, yielding

\displaystyle e^{\beta t}\|X^{(n),s,x^{(n)}}(t)\|^{p}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}

\displaystyle\leq e^{\beta s}\|x^{(n)}\|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{p}+\int_{s}^{t}e^{\beta r}\left(-\epsilon\|X^{(n),s,x^{(n)}}(r)\|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{p}+C(p,\eta)\|X^{(n),s,x^{(n)}}(r)\|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{p-2}\right)dr

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\int_{s}^{t}pe^{\beta r}\|X^{(n),s,x^{(n)}}(r)\|^{p-2}X^{(n),s,x^{(n)}}(r)B_{n}dW^{(n)}(r),

where
C(p,\eta)=p\eta+\frac{p(p-1)}{2}
. By an application of Young’s inequality
ab\leq\frac{\epsilon^{\prime}a^{p^{\prime}}}{p^{\prime}}+\frac{b^{p^{\prime}}}{q^{\prime}(\epsilon^{\prime})^{q^{\prime}/p^{\prime}}}
,
\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}=1
, we establish

\displaystyle C(p,\eta)\|X^{(n),s,x^{(n)}}(r)\|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{p-2}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon\|X^{(n),s,x^{(n)}}(r)\|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{p}+\frac{2}{p}C^{\frac{p}{2}}(p.\eta)(\frac{\epsilon p}{p-2})^{-\frac{p-2}{2}}

\displaystyle=\epsilon\|X^{(n),s,x^{(n)}}(r)\|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{p}+C(p,\eta,\lambda,M).

This immediately gives

\displaystyle e^{\beta t}\|X^{(n),s,x^{(n)}}(t)\|^{p}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}

\displaystyle\leq e^{\beta s}\|x^{(n)}\|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{p}+C(p,\eta,\lambda,M)\int_{s}^{t}e^{\beta r}dr+\int_{s}^{t}pe^{\beta r}\|X^{(n),s,x^{(n)}}(r)\|^{p-2}X^{(n),s,x^{(n)}}(r)B_{n}dW^{(n)}(r)

\displaystyle\leq e^{\beta s}\|x^{(n)}\|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{p}+C(p,\eta,\lambda,M)e^{\beta t}+\int_{s}^{t}pe^{\beta r}\|X^{(n),s,x^{(n)}}(r)\|^{p-2}X^{(n),s,x^{(n)}}(r)B_{n}dW^{(n)}(r),

where
C(p,\eta,\lambda,M)
denotes a generic positive constant whose value may change from line to line. Taking expectations on both sides, we obtain

e^{\beta t}\mathbb{E}\|X^{(n),s,x^{(n)}}(t)\|^{p}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\leq e^{\beta s}\|x^{(n)}\|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{p}+C(p,\eta,\lambda,M)e^{\beta t}.

Then

\mathbb{E}\|X^{(n),s,x^{(n)}}(t)\|^{p}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\leq\|x^{(n)}\|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{p}+C(p,\eta,\lambda,M),

thus proving the lemma.
∎

The finite-dimensional periodic measures are proved to be continuous in time under the weak topology, as precisely formulated in the following proposition.

Proposition 4.4
.

For any bounded cylindrically Lipschitz function
h\in C_{b,Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and arbitrary
s\geq 0
, there holds

\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow 0}\mu_{n,s+t}h=\mu_{n,s}h.

Proof.

Theorem
4.2
guarantees that the transition probability kernel
\mathcal{P}_{n}(s,t)
of equation (
4.1
) admits a unique periodic measure
\mu_{n,\cdot}
. By Theorem 3.6 of Feng, Zhao and Zhong
[
15
]
,
\mathcal{P}_{n}(s,s+mT,0,\cdot)
converges weakly to
\mu_{n,s}
as
m\rightarrow\infty
. Moreover, since
\mathcal{P}_{n}(s,t)
is strong Feller, it follows that for any bounded and Lipschitz continuous function
h\in C_{b,Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and any
s\geq 0
,
t>0
, there exists a constant
C
such that

\displaystyle|\mu_{n,s+t}h-\mu_{n,s}h|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h(x^{(n)})\mu_{n,s+t}(dx^{(n)})-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h(x^{(n)})\mu_{n,s}(dx^{(n)})\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h(x^{(n)})\mathcal{P}^{*}(s,s+t)\mu_{n,s}(dx^{(n)})-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h(x^{(n)})\mu_{n,s}(dx^{(n)})\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h(y^{(n)})\mathcal{P}(s,s+t,x^{(n)},dy^{(n)})\mu_{n,s}(dx^{(n)})-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h(x^{(n)})\mu_{n,s}(dx^{(n)})\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h(y^{(n)})\mathcal{P}(s,s+t,x^{(n)},dy^{(n)})-h(x^{(n)})\right]\mu_{n,s}(dx^{(n)})\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{m\rightarrow\infty}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h(y^{(n)})\mathcal{P}(s,s+t,x^{(n)},dy^{(n)})-h(x^{(n)})\right]\mathcal{P}_{n}(s,s+mT,0,dx^{(n)})\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\varliminf\limits_{m\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|h(X^{(n),s,x^{(n)}}(s+t))-h(x^{(n)})\right|^{2}\right]\mathcal{P}_{n}(s,s+mT,0,dx^{(n)})

\displaystyle\leq\varliminf\limits_{m\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}C\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|X^{(n),s,x^{(n)}}(s+t)-x^{(n)}\right\|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\right]\mathcal{P}_{n}(s,s+mT,0,dx^{(n)})

Following an analogous computation to that for (
3.8
), we obtain the estimate

\forall 0\leq s\leq t\leq T_{1}:\mathbb{E}\|X^{(n),s,x^{(n)}}(t)-x^{(n)}\|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{2}\leq C(T_{1})(1+\|x^{(n)}\|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{2s})|t-s|.

(4.3)

Then, under the condition
s+t<T_{1}
for some fixed
T_{1}>0
, the combined application of (
4.3
) and Lemma
4.3
yields

\displaystyle|\mu_{n,s+t}h-\mu_{n,s}h|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\varliminf\limits_{m\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}C(1+\|x^{(n)}\|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{2\theta})|t-s|\mathcal{P}_{n}(s,s+mT,0,dx^{(n)})

\displaystyle=\varliminf\limits_{m\rightarrow\infty}C|t-s|\mathbb{E}(1+\|X^{(n),s,0}(s+mT)\|^{2\theta}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}})

\displaystyle\leq C|t-s|,

which proves the right-continuity of
\mu_{n,\cdot}
. The left-continuity can be verified through analogous arguments by making appropriate modifications to the preceding analysis.
∎

4.2
Time-inhomogeneous infinite-dimensional systems: periodic measures

In this section, we consider infinite-dimensional equations with time-periodic coefficients. Under modified assumptions, arguments analogous to those in Section 3 yield the existence and uniqueness of weak solutions. Furthermore, we establish the periodicity of transition semi-group, from which we deduce the existence of periodic measures. The equations considered are:

\begin{cases}dX(t)=\left(A(t)X(t)+F(t,X(t))\right)dt+BdW(t),\ t\geq s,\\
X(s)=x\in\ell_{\rho}^{2s}(\mathbb{Z}^{+}),\end{cases}

(4.4)

where
W(t)
is a Wiener process on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
taking values in
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})
and with identity covariance,
B
is defined as in (
3.3
) and the operators
A(t)
,
F(t,x)
are given by the following formula

\begin{cases}A(t)x=\left(a_{\gamma,\gamma-1}(t)x_{\gamma-1}+a_{\gamma,\gamma+1}(t)x_{\gamma+1}\right)_{\gamma},x=\left(x_{\gamma}\right)_{\gamma},\\
F(t,x)=\left(f(t,x_{\gamma})\right)_{\gamma},x=\left(x_{\gamma}\right)_{\gamma}.\end{cases}

We impose the following assumptions on the coefficients of (
4.4
) and the solution space:

Assumption 6
.

(i)

The coefficients
\{a_{i,i-1}(t),a_{i-1,i}(t);i\geq 2\}
and
f(t,z)
are T-periodic in
t
for any fixed
z\in\mathbb{R}
.

(ii)

All the elements of the operator
A(t)
satisfy:
a_{i,i-1}(t)\neq 0,i\geq 2,t\geq 0
and there is a constant
M>0
such that

\max\left\{|a_{i-1,i}(t)|,|a_{i,i-1}(t)|\right\}\leq M,\hskip 10.00002pti\geq 2,\hskip 10.00002ptt\geq 0.

(iii)

There exist constants
R,N>0
such that

\left|\frac{\rho(\gamma)}{\rho(j)}\right|\leq N\ \text{if}\ |\gamma-j|\leq R,\hskip 10.00002pt\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}\rho(i)<+\infty,\hskip 10.00002pt\rho(i)>0.

(iii)

The function
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
is weakly dissipative, i.e. there exists constants
\eta>0
and

2M<\lambda-\frac{1}{2}

such that

f(t,z)z\leq\eta-\lambda|z|^{2},\hskip 10.00002pt(t,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}.

Moreover there exists some constants
\theta\geq 1
, and
\eta_{0}\geq 0
such that

\left|f(t,z)\right|\leq\eta_{0}\left(1+\left|z\right|^{\theta}\right),\hskip 10.00002pt(t,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}.

While Assumption
6
guarantees the existence of weak solutions, the following supplementary conditions ensure the uniqueness.

Assumption 7
.

(i)

There exist constants
r,M_{1},M_{2}>0
and
N_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that for any
n\geq N_{1}

\rho(n)\geq\frac{M_{1}}{e^{rn}},\hskip 10.00002pt\limsup\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\rho(n)}{\inf\limits_{1\leq i\leq n}\rho(i)}\leq M_{2}.

(ii)

There exists a function
L:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that for all
N>0
, the function
f
satisfies

|f(t,z)-f(t,\tilde{z})|\leq L(N)|z-\tilde{z}|,-N\leq z,\tilde{z}\leq N,t\geq 0.

where
L
further satisfies: for any constant
C>0
, there exists
\gamma=\gamma(C)>0
such that

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\exp\{CL^{2}(n)\}}{n^{\gamma}L(n)}=0.

(4.5)

Under Assumptions
6
and
7
, we obtain the existence and uniqueness of weak solutions. Following arguments similar to those in Section 3.5, one can verify that the solutions possess the Markov property. This allows us to define the transition probability kernel
\mathcal{P}(s,t,x,\Gamma)=\mathbf{P}^{s,x}[X^{s}(t)\in\Gamma]
, where
X^{s}(\cdot)
is the modified canonical process on
\Omega_{Spin}^{s}=C([s,\infty),\ell^{2}_{\rho}(\mathbb{Z}^{+}))
,
\Gamma\in\mathscr{B}(\ell^{2\theta}_{\rho}(\mathbb{Z}^{+}))
and
\mathbf{P}^{s,x}
is the solution to the martingale problem associated with (
4.4
) with the initial condition
x\in\ell^{2\theta}_{\rho}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})
. The following lemma establishes the periodicity of
\mathcal{P}(s,t)
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Assuming the validity of Assumptions
6
and
7
, the transition probability kernel
\mathcal{P}(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)
possesses T-periodicity, namely
\mathcal{P}(s,t,x,\cdot)=\mathcal{P}(s+T,t+T,x,\cdot)
, for all
x\in\ell^{2\theta}_{\rho}(\mathbb{Z}^{+}),s\leq t
.

Proof.

In order to prove the result of this lemma, application of Lemma
3.28
suggests we only need to verify that for any
h\in C_{b,lip}^{Cyl}(\ell_{\rho}^{2\theta}(\mathbb{Z}^{+}))
,

\int_{\ell_{\rho}^{2\theta}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})}h(a)\mathcal{P}(s,t,x,da)=\int_{\ell_{\rho}^{2\theta}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})}h(a)\mathcal{P}(s+T,t+T,x,da).

(4.6)

From the weak convergence property combined with the periodicity of transition probability kernels for finite-dimensional SDEs, we derive

\displaystyle\int_{\ell_{\rho}^{2\theta}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})}h(a)\mathcal{P}(s,t,x,da)

\displaystyle=\int_{\ell_{\rho}^{2\theta}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})}h(a)\mathbf{P}^{s,x}\circ X^{s}(t)=\int_{\Omega^{s}_{Spin}}h(X^{s}(t))d\mathbf{P}^{s,x}

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{n}\int_{\Omega^{s}_{Spin}}h(\omega(t))d\tilde{P}^{s,x}_{n}=\lim\limits_{n}\int_{\Omega}h(\tilde{X}^{(n),s,x}(t))d\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{n}\int_{\Omega}h(\tilde{X}^{(n),s+T,x}(t+T))d\mathbb{P}=\lim\limits_{n}\int_{\Omega^{s}_{Spin}}h(\omega(t+T))d\tilde{P}^{s+T,x}_{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega^{s}_{Spin}}h(\omega(t+T))d\mathbf{P}^{s+T,x}=\int_{\ell_{\rho}^{2\theta}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})}h(a)\mathcal{P}(s+T,t+T,x,da),

which yields (
4.6
).
∎

Before establishing the existence of periodic measures for
\mathcal{P}(s,t)
, we present an auxiliary lemma.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
(E,\mathscr{B}(E))
be a complete separable metric space. Given a family
\{\mu_{n,s}:0\leq s\leq T\}_{n}
of the
\mathscr{P}(E)
-valued functions that are measurable with respect to
s
. If a family of average measures
\mu_{n}:=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\mu_{n,s}ds,n\in\mathbb{N}
, forms a tight sequence in
\mathscr{P}(E)
, then there exists a sequence
s_{n}\in[0,T]
such that
\{\mu_{n,s_{n}}\}_{n}
is tight.

Proof.

Since
\{\mu_{n}\}_{n}
is tight, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a compact set
K_{k}
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mu_{n}(K_{k}^{c})\leq\frac{1}{k^{4}}
. For fixed
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define the sets
O_{n,k}=\{s\in[0,T];\mu_{n,s}(K_{k}^{c})<\frac{1}{k^{2}}\}
. By the definition of
\mu_{n}:=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\mu_{n,s}ds
and Fubini’s theorem, we obtain

\frac{1}{k^{4}}>\mu_{n}(K_{k}^{c})=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\mu_{n,s}(K_{k}^{c})ds\geq\frac{1}{Tk^{2}}m(O_{n,k}^{c}),

where
m
denotes the Lebesgue measure. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define
U_{n}=\bigcap\limits_{k=2}^{\infty}O_{n,k}
. Then we have the estimate

m(U_{n})=m([0,T]\backslash U_{n}^{c})=T-m(\bigcup\limits_{k=2}^{\infty}O_{n,k}^{c})\geq T-\sum\limits_{k=2}^{\infty}m(O_{n,k}^{c})\geq T-T\sum\limits_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{2}}>0,

which implies
U_{n}\neq\emptyset
. Choosing
s_{n}\in U_{n}
, we now prove that the sequence
\{\mu_{n,s_{n}}\},n\geq 0
, is tight. In fact, for any
\epsilon>0
, select
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\frac{1}{k^{2}}<\epsilon
. Then for the compact set
K_{k}
, we have for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mu_{n,s_{n}}(K_{k}^{c})<\frac{1}{k^{2}}<\epsilon
, completing the proof.
∎

We now investigate the existence of periodic measures for the transition semi-group
\mathcal{P}(s,t)
.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let Assumptions
6
,
7
and
4
hold. Then there exists a T-periodic measure
(\nu_{s})_{s\geq 0}
for the transition semi-group
\mathcal{P}(s,t)
.

Proof.

We begin by lifting the semi-flow of the finite-dimensional SDEs (
4.1
) to
\tilde{\mathbb{X}}=[0,T)\times\ell^{2\theta}_{\rho}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})
:

\begin{cases}dZ_{0}^{(n)}(t)=dt\quad\text{mod}\quad T,\\
dZ_{i}^{(n)}(t)=\left(A_{n}(Z_{0}^{(n)}(t))Z^{(n)}(t)+F_{n}(Z_{0}^{(n)}(t),Z^{(n)}(t))\right)dt+B_{n}dW^{(n)}(t),\\
t\geq 0,\quad 1\leq i\leq n,\\
Z_{0}^{(n)}(0)=0,\quad(Z_{i}^{(n)}(0))_{1\leq i\leq n}=\Pi_{n}x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{cases}

(4.7)

The corresponding transition probability kernel is defined by
\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{n}(t,(s,x),\tilde{\Gamma}):=\mathbb{P}[(Z^{(n)}_{0}(t),

Z^{(n)}(t))\in\tilde{\Gamma}]
,
\tilde{\Gamma}\in\mathscr{B}([s,T])\times\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. By adapting the proof methodology of Theorem 3.13 in Feng, Qu and Zhao
[
13
]
, we derive that
\tilde{\mu}_{n,s}(\tilde{\Gamma}):=[\delta_{s}\times\mu_{n,s}](\tilde{\Gamma})
is a periodic measure for
\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{n}
. It follows from Theorem 3.6 of Feng and Zhao
[
14
]
that
\bar{\tilde{\mu}}_{n}:=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\tilde{\mu}_{n,s}ds
is an invariant measure for
\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{n}
.

Noting that the generator of equation (
4.7
) is

\begin{split}\tilde{\mathscr{L}}_{n}\tilde{h}(z^{(n)})=&\partial_{t}\tilde{h}(z^{(n)})+(a_{1,2}(z^{(n)}_{0})z_{2}+f(z^{(n)}_{0},z^{(n)}_{1}))\partial_{z^{(n)}_{1}}\tilde{h}(z^{(n)})\\
&+\cdots+(a_{n,n-1}(z^{(n)}_{0})z^{(n)}_{n-1}+f(z^{(n)}_{0},z^{(n)}_{n}))\partial_{z^{(n)}_{n}}\tilde{h}(z^{(n)}),\quad z^{(n)}\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{split}

we adapt methods similar to Lemma
3.27
to show
\bar{\tilde{\nu}}_{n}:=\bar{\tilde{\mu}}_{n}\circ\tilde{\chi}_{n}^{-1}
is tight in
[0,T]\times\ell^{2\theta}_{\rho}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})
, where
\tilde{\chi}_{n}z^{(n)}=(z^{(n)},0_{i\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\backslash\Lambda_{n}})
. By Lemma
4.6
, there exists a sequence
\{t_{n}\}_{n}
such that
\{\tilde{\mu}_{n,t_{n}}\circ\tilde{\chi}_{n}^{-1}\}
is tight in space
[0,T]\times\ell^{2\theta}_{\rho}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})
. Consequently, the projected measures
\mu_{n,t_{n}}\circ\tilde{\chi}_{n}^{-1}(\cdot)=\tilde{\mu}_{n,t_{n}}\circ\tilde{\chi}_{n}^{-1}([0,T]\times\cdot)
form a tight family in the space
\ell^{2\theta}_{\rho}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})
. Since
[0,T]
is compact, we may extract a convergent subsequence (still denoted by
\{t_{n}\}_{n}
) with
t_{n}\rightarrow s_{*}\in[0,T]
. Furthermore, by Prokhorov’s theorem, there exists a further subsequence of
\{\mu_{n,t_{n}}\}_{n}
(which we still denote by
\{\mu_{n,t_{n}}\}_{n}
for simplicity) that converges weakly to some probability measure
\nu_{s_{*}}
. We now verify that
\nu_{s_{*}}
is an invariant measure for the one-step transition probability kernel
\mathcal{P}(s_{*},s_{*}+T)
. It suffices to show that for any
h\in C_{b,lip}^{Cyl}(\ell_{\rho}^{2\theta}(\mathbb{Z}^{+}))
,

\mathcal{P}^{*}(s_{*},s_{*}+T)\nu_{s_{*}}h=\int_{\ell^{2\theta}_{\rho}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})}\int_{\ell^{2\theta}_{\rho}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})}h(y)\mathcal{P}(s_{*},s_{*}+T,x,dy)\nu_{s_{*}}(dx)=\nu_{s_{*}}h.

(4.8)

Observing the periodicity of
\mu_{n,\cdot}
, we have
\mathcal{P}_{n}^{*}(s,s+T)\mu_{n,s}(h\circ\chi_{n})=\mu_{n,s}(h\circ\chi_{n})
for
s\geq 0
, which yields

\displaystyle\int_{\ell^{2\theta}_{\rho}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})}\int_{\ell^{2\theta}_{\rho}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})}h(y)\mathcal{P}_{n}(s_{n},s_{n}+T,x,dy)\mu_{n,s_{n}}\circ\chi_{n}^{-1}(dx)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h\circ\chi_{n}(y^{(n)})\mathcal{P}_{n}(s_{n},s_{n}+T,x^{(n)},dy^{(n)})\mu_{n,s_{n}}(dx^{(n)})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h\circ\chi_{n}(x^{(n)})\mu_{n,s_{n}}(dx^{(n)}).

(4.9)

Note that the limit of the right-hand side of Equation (
4.9
) is
\nu_{s_{*}}h
. To establish (
4.8
), we only need to prove that the limit of the left-hand side of (
4.9
) is
\int_{\ell^{2\theta}_{\rho}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})}\mathcal{P}^{*}(s_{*},s_{*}+T)\nu_{s_{*}}h
. For this purpose, we adopt the proof technique of Theorem
3.26
to demonstrate

x_{n},x\in\ell^{2\theta}_{\rho}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002ptx_{n}\rightarrow x,\hskip 10.00002pt\text{in}\hskip 10.00002pt\ell^{2}_{\rho}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})\hskip 10.00002pt\text{implies}\hskip 10.00002ptH_{n}(x_{n})\rightarrow H(x),\hskip 10.00002pt\text{as}\hskip 10.00002ptn\rightarrow\infty,

where
H_{n}(x):=\int_{\ell^{2\theta}_{\rho}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})}h(y)\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{n}(s_{n},s_{n}+T,x,dy)
and
H(x):=\int_{\ell^{2\theta}_{\rho}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})}h(y)\mathcal{P}(s_{*},s_{*}+T,x,dy)
. We observe that by an argument analogous to Lemma
3.23
, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exist
\delta_{1}>0
and
N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
n>N_{0}
and

, the following estimate holds:

\mathbb{E}\|X_{(k)}^{(n),s_{n},x}(s_{n}+T)-X_{(k)}^{(n),s_{n},y}(s_{n}+T)\|_{\mathbb{R}^{\Lambda_{k}}}^{2}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{64L^{2}},

(4.10)

where
L
is the Lipschitz constant for
g
, the finite-dimensional mapping corresponding to
h
. Furthermore, since
x_{n}\rightarrow x
, there exists
N_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
|x_{n}-x|<\delta_{1}
for all
n>N_{1}
. Using a method similar to Lemma
3.11
, we obtain the following estimate,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|\tilde{X}_{(k)}^{(n),s_{n},x}(t)-\tilde{X}_{(k)}^{(n),s_{n},x}(s)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\|\tilde{X}^{(n),s_{n},x}(t)-\tilde{X}^{(n),s_{n},x}(s)\|^{2}_{2,\rho}\frac{1}{\inf\limits_{1\leq i\leq k}\rho(i)}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+\|x\|^{2\theta}_{2\theta,\rho})|t-s|.

(4.11)

Consequently, there exists
\delta_{2}=\frac{\epsilon}{64LC(1+\|x\|^{2\theta}_{2\theta,\rho})}
such that whenever
|t-s|<\delta_{2}
, we have

\mathbb{E}\|\tilde{X}_{(k)}^{(n),s_{n},x}(t)-\tilde{X}_{(k)}^{(n),s_{n},x}(s)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{64L^{2}}.

(4.12)

\mathbb{E}\|\tilde{X}_{(k)}^{(n),s_{n},x}(s_{*}+T)-\tilde{X}_{(k)}^{(n),s_{*},x}(s_{*}+T)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{64L^{2}},

(4.14)

when

\displaystyle|H_{n}(x_{n})-H(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 16\left(L^{2}\mathbb{E}\|\tilde{X}_{(k)}^{(n),s_{n},x_{n}}(s_{n}+T)-\tilde{X}_{(k)}^{(n),s_{n},x}(s_{n}+T)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+L^{2}\mathbb{E}\|\tilde{X}_{(k)}^{(n),s_{n},x}(s_{n}+T)-\tilde{X}_{(k)}^{(n),s_{n},x}(s_{*}+T)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}

Proof of Theorem
2.2

In this section, our aim is to utilize the method proposed by Meyn and Tweedie to establish the geometric ergodicity of the SDE (
2.1
). Their theorem is stated as follows (see Meyn and Tweedie
[
32
]
and Mattingly, Stuart and Higham
[
31
, Theorem 2.5]
).

Theorem 5.1
.

(Harris-Meyn-Tweedie). Let
X(t)
be a time-homogeneous Markov process on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with transition probability kernel
\mathcal{P}_{t}
and infinitesimal generator
\mathscr{L}
. Suppose that following hypotheses are satisfied

The Markov process is irreducible, that is, there exists
t_{0}
(and then for all
t>t_{0}
) such that

\mathcal{P}_{t_{0}}(x,\Gamma)>0,

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and nonempty open sets
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

For any
t>0
the Markov semi-group
\mathcal{P}_{t}(x,dy)
has a density
p_{t}(x,y)
, which is a continuous function of
(x,y)
.

Assume there exists a Lyapunov function

V:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\left[0,\infty\right)\xrightarrow{\|x\|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rightarrow+\infty}+\infty

and constants
C,c>0
such that

\mathscr{L}V+cV\leq C.

Then there exists unique invariant measure
\mu
for the process
X(t)
and there exist constants
K,\alpha>0
such that

\sup\limits_{\{f:|f(x)|\leq V(x)\}}|\mathcal{P}_{t}f(a)-\mu(f)|\leq KV(a)e^{-\alpha t}

for any
a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

In the remainder part of this section, we will show that the transition probability kernel
\mathcal{P}^{(n)}_{t}(x,\cdot)
of the SDE (
2.1
) indeed satisfies the conditions of the above theorem. Thus, the ergodicity of the SDE in the finite-dimension case is obtained.

5.1
Strong Feller property

In this subsection, we prove that the transition probability kernel
\mathcal{P}^{(n)}_{t}(x,\cdot)
associated with SDE (
2.1
) admits a smooth density function, which is the immediate consequence of the Hörmander theorem in probabilistic settings (see Ichihara and Kunita
[
24
]
).

Theorem 5.2
.

(Hörmander probabilistic setting, Ichihara - Kunita). Assume
X(t)
is the strong unique solution to the Stratonovich SDE

dX_{i}(t)=b_{i}(t,X(t))dt+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sigma_{i,j}(t,X(t))\circ dW_{j}(t),\hskip 10.00002ptt\geq 0,\hskip 10.00002pti=1,\cdots,n

where
b_{i},\sigma_{i,j}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}),1\leq i,j\leq n,
. Suppose that following (Hörmander) condition is satisfied

dim(Lie\{A_{0},A_{1}...,A_{n}\})=n+1,\ \forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
A_{0}(t,x)=(1,b_{1}(t,x),\cdots,b_{n}(t,x))^{T}
and
A_{j}(t,x)=(0,\sigma_{1,j},\cdots,\sigma_{n,j})^{T}
for
1\leq j\leq n
.
Then the transition probability kernel
\mathcal{P}_{t}(x,\cdot)
associated with
X(t)
admits a density function
p_{t}(x,y)
such that

p_{t}(x,y)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}).

Since SDE (
2.1
) in the Stratonovich sense is actually the same as in the It
\hat{\text{o}}
form, we can calculate that the corresponding
A_{0},A_{i},1\leq i\leq d
in Theorem
5.2
can be represented as

A_{0}(x)=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
A_{n}x\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}0\\
f(x_{1})\\
f(x_{2})\\
\vdots\\
f(x_{n})\end{pmatrix},\hskip 10.00002ptA_{1}(x)=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\\
0\\
\vdots\\
0\end{pmatrix}.

Let

[V,W]\equiv DV(x)W(x)-DW(x)V(x)

denote the Lie bracket of two
C^{1}
vector fields
V
and
W
. We can calculate that

[A_{0},A_{1}]=DA_{0}(x)A_{1}(x)-DA_{1}(x)A_{0}(x)=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
b_{1,2}\\
a_{2,1}\\
0\\
\vdots\\
0\\
\end{pmatrix},[A_{0},[A_{0},A_{1}]]=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
b_{1,3}\\
b_{2,3}\\
a_{2,1}a_{3,2}\\
0\\
\vdots\\
0\\
\end{pmatrix},

where

b_{1,2}=f^{\prime}(x_{1}),b_{1,3}=\left(f^{\prime}(x_{1})\right)^{2}+a_{1,2}a_{2,1}-f^{\prime\prime}(x_{1})\left(a_{1,2}x_{2}+f(x_{1})\right),b_{2,3}=a_{2,1}\left(f^{\prime}(x_{1})+f^{\prime}(x_{2})\right).

By iterating, we can get

\underbrace{[A_{0},[A_{0},\cdots[A_{0},[A_{0},A_{1}]]]]}_{i}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
b_{1,i}\\
b_{2,i}\\
\vdots\\
b_{i-1,i}\\
a_{2,1}a_{3,2}\cdots a_{i,i-1}\\
0\\
\vdots\\
0\\
\end{pmatrix},1<i\leq n.

The form of

has no effect on verifying the Hörmander condition, so we will not give specific formulas here. According to Assumptions
1

(i)
, the above calculations show that SDE (
2.1
) satisfies the Hörmander condition, i.e.

dim(Lie\{A_{0}(x),A_{1}(x)\})=n+1,\ \forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

As a consequence of Ichihara-Kunita’s result, we conclude that the density function of the transition probability kernel corresponding to Equation (

5.2
Irreducibility

To investigate the irreducibility of
X^{(n),x}(t)
, we would like to use “approximate controllability” method. The question is whether we can solve the corresponding control problem,

\begin{cases}y^{\prime}(t)=A_{n}y(t)+F_{n}(y(t))+B_{n}u(t),\\
y(0)=x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
\end{cases}

(5.1)

where
A_{n},F_{n},B_{n}
is given by (
2.2
), and
u
is the control. We denote by

the solution of (
5.1
). We recall that the system (
5.1

Lemma 5.3

For arbitrary constants

Proof.

According to the setting of this lemma, we construct

m_{1}+\frac{t-t_{1}}{t_{2}-t_{1}}(m_{2}-m_{1}),t\in[t_{1},t_{2}],\\
\end{cases}

where

\begin{cases}\tilde{g}(0)=k_{3}=q_{1},\\

Solving the linear equation (

In the following, the function

5.2

5.3
Lyapunov condition

The existence of the Lyapunov function for the corresponding infinitesimal generator of the SDE (
2.1
) can be proved by structural calculation.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
\mathscr{L}_{n}
be the infinitesimal generator of SDE (
2.1
) under Assumption
1
. For the function
V_{n}(x)=1+x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+x_{n}^{2}
, there exist constants
C,c>0
such that

\mathscr{L}_{n}V_{n}(x)+cV_{n}(x)\leq C,\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(5.9)

Proof.

The corresponding infinitesimal generator of SDE (
2.1
) is of the following form

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{n}h(x)

\displaystyle=(a_{1,2}x_{2}+f(x_{1}))\partial_{x_{1}}h(x)+(a_{2,1}x_{1}+a_{2,3}x_{3}+f(x_{2}))\partial_{x_{2}}h(x)+\cdots

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+(a_{n,n-1}x_{n-1}+f(x_{n}))\partial_{x_{n}}h(x)+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial_{x_{1}^{2}}}h(x),\hskip 10.00002pth\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\hskip 10.00002ptx\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

We compute that

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{n}V_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=2(a_{1,2}x_{2}+f(x_{1}))x_{1}+\cdots+2(a_{n,n-1}x_{n-1}+f(x_{n}))x_{n}+1

\displaystyle\leq 4M\left(|x_{1}||x_{2}|+\cdots+|x_{n-1}||x_{n}|\right)+2f(x_{1})x_{1}+\cdots+2f(x_{n})x_{n}+1,

where
M=\max\left\{|a_{i-1,i}|,|a_{i,i+1}|;2\leq i\leq n\right\}
.
According to Assumption
1
(ii), we get

\begin{split}&\mathscr{L}_{n}V_{n}(x)+V_{n}(x)\\
&\leq 4M\left(|x_{1}||x_{2}|+\cdots+|x_{n-1}||x_{n}|\right)-(2\lambda-1)\left(|x_{1}|^{2}+\cdots+|x_{n}|^{2}\right)+2n\eta+2\\
&\leq 4M\left(|x_{1}|^{2}+\cdots+|x_{n}|^{2}\right)-(2\lambda-1)\left(|x_{1}|^{2}+\cdots+|x_{n}|^{2}\right)+2n\eta+2\\
&\leq 2n\eta+2.\end{split}

We take
c=1
and
C=2n\eta+2
, then the result of the lemma follows.
