The More the Merrier: Combining Properties for ABox Abduction under Repair Semantics in ELbot
Anselm Haak, Patrick Koopmann, Yasir Mahmood, Anni-Yasmin Turhan

Abstract
Abduction is a central approach to explain missing entailments from a knowledge base by providing a hypothesis, that would, if added to the knowledge base, make the missing entailment become true. Abduction under repair semantics has recently been investigated in detail, where several desirable properties and optimality criteria were considered, such as signature-restrictions and minimality in size and of introduced conflicts. Naturally, hypotheses that satisfy more than one of these properties or combine a property with an optimality criterion would be even more desirable for applications. So far, such hypotheses have not been investigated in the literature. In the present paper, we consider the ABox abduction problem for hypotheses satisfying more than one property or additional optimality criteria, for ℰ ​ ℒ ⊥ \mathcal{E\!L}_{\bot} under brave and AR semantics. Our main observation is that often requiring additional properties for hypotheses does not lead to an increase of complexity.

Introduction

Explainability remains a core feature of knowledge-based AI systems, including
those using description logic ontologies. Developing methods that can also
practically explain decisions made by such systems is crucial in order to
exploit this feature. While there is a lot of work on
explaining reasoning results under classical
semantics
20

investigated for non-classical semantics. In this paper, we consider

repair-based semantics

repairs

the chosen
semantics, we may then consider those entailments that hold in at least one of
the repairs (
brave
semantics), or only those entailments that hold in
all of the repairs (
AR

Since inconsistencies can easily
occur in realistic data, inconsistency-tolerant semantics are
often indispensable. Unfortunately, they also make it more
challenging to understand entailments from a user perspective. To explain why an

For explaining

Indeed, under repair-based semantics, two things can contribute to the

\alpha

2) assertions that are needed for the entailment are missing. While
explanations based on Situation 1 have been investigated in detail

17

on explanations based on Situation 2.
Interestingly, no one so far considered explanations that combine Situations 1) and 2), i.e., where assertions can both be added and removed.
We prefer to first fully understand abductive reasoning under repair-based semantics, and investigate more advanced notions of explanations in
the future.
Focusing on Situation 2, this corresponds to explaining missing
entailments using abductive reasoning, which is also a typical means of

2.1
Repair Semantics

If a knowledge base is inconsistent, repair semantics can
“restore”
consistent versions and admit meaningful reasoning again.
We focus here on ABox repairs and define these as well as two common kinds of repair semantics next.
We will often use the following notions of consistency and inconsistency w.r.t. a TBox
\mathcal{T}
.
An ABox
\mathcal{A}
is
\mathcal{T}
-consistent
, if
\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}\rangle\not\models\bot
, and
\mathcal{T}
-inconsistent
otherwise.
Further, we say that
\mathcal{A}
is a
\mathcal{T}
-support
of a concept assertion
\alpha
, if
\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}\rangle\models\alpha
.

Let
\mathcal{K}=\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}\rangle
be an inconsistent knowledge base and
\alpha
be a Boolean (conjunctive) query.
A
repair
of
\mathcal{K}
is a
\mathcal{T}
-consistent subset
\mathcal{R}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
and subset-maximal with this property, i.e., there is no
\mathcal{T}
-consistent subset
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
that is a strict superset of
\mathcal{R}
.
The somewhat dual notion is a
conflict
or
conflict set

\mathcal{C}
, which is a
\mathcal{T}
-inconsistent subset of the ABox and subset-minimal with this property.
We denote by
\Conf(\mathcal{K})
the set of conflicts of
\mathcal{K}
.
We recall entailment under Brave
5
and AR semantics
16
:

•

\mathcal{K}\models_{\textrm{Brave}}\alpha
iff there is a repair
\mathcal{R}
of
\mathcal{K}
s.t.
\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{R}\rangle\models\alpha
.

•

\mathcal{K}\models_{\textrm{AR}}\alpha
iff
\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{R}\rangle\models\alpha
for every repair
\mathcal{R}
of
\mathcal{K}
.

The complexity of query entailment under repair semantics is well understood
4
:
For
\mathcal{E\!L}_{\bot}
, checking entailment of a concept assertion under Brave semantics is
\mathsf{NP}
-complete in combined complexity, while it is
\mathsf{coNP}
-complete under AR semantics.

2.2
ABox Abduction for Inconsistent KBs

We now introduce the necessary notions for ABox abduction under repair semantics,
as introduced in
11
.
We begin with the central definitions of abduction problems and hypotheses in this setting.

Definition 2
.

Let
\mathcal{K}=\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}\rangle
be an inconsistent KB,
\alpha
a concept assertion
(called an
observation
) and
\mathcal{S}\in\{\textrm{Brave},\textrm{AR}\}
such that
\mathcal{K}\not\models_{\mathcal{S}}\alpha
.
Then, the pair
\langle\mathcal{K},\alpha\rangle
is called an
\mathcal{S}
-abduction problem
.
A solution for such a problem, called
\mathcal{S}
-hypothesis
, is an ABox
\mathcal{H}
using only individuals occurring in
\mathcal{K}
and
\alpha
s.t.
\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{H}\rangle\models_{\mathcal{S}}\alpha
.

Additionally, for a set
\Sigma
(signature) containing individual, concept and role names, we define the triple
\langle\mathcal{K},\alpha,\Sigma\rangle
to be a
\Sigma
-restricted
\mathcal{S}
-abduction problem
.
A solution for such a problem is an ABox
\mathcal{H}
using only symbols from
\Sigma
that is an
\mathcal{S}
-hypothesis for
\langle\mathcal{K},\alpha\rangle
.

Note also that we do not admit fresh individuals beyond the specified signature as
it is done for example in
14
.
Abduction with fresh individuals easily becomes
\mathsf{ExpTime}
-hard in
\mathcal{E\!L}_{\bot}

14
.
Because there are at most exponentially many repairs, the complexity is in this case dominated by the complexity of (classical) abduction, so such a setting might make
our complexity analysis less insightful.

To obtain hypotheses that are meaningful for explanation purposes, a number of additional properties and minimality criteria for hypotheses that yield
preferred hypotheses
have been introduced.
For two sets
S_{1},S_{2}
we may abbreviate
\lvert S_{1}\rvert\leq\lvert S_{2}\rvert
by
S_{1}\leq S_{2}
.
We next provide the definition of these properties and notions of minimality.

Definition 3
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}\in{\{\textrm{Brave},\textrm{AR}\}}
,
\langle\mathcal{K},\alpha\rangle
an
\mathcal{S}
-abduction problem, and

{\preceq}\in{\{\subseteq,\leq\}}
.
An ABox
\mathcal{H}
is

1.

conflict-confining for
\mathcal{K}=\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}\rangle
, provided that

\Conf(\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{H}\rangle)=\Conf(\mathcal{K})
.

If
\mathcal{H}
is an
\mathcal{S}
-hypothesis for
\langle\mathcal{K},\alpha\rangle

2.

non-trivial
, if
\alpha\not\in\mathcal{H}
,

3.

\preceq
-minimal
, if there is no
\mathcal{S}
-hypothesis
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
for
\langle\mathcal{K},\alpha\rangle
s.t.
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\prec\mathcal{H}
, and

4.

\preceq_{c}
-minimal
, if there is no
\mathcal{S}
-hypothesis
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
for
\langle\mathcal{K},\alpha\rangle
s.t.
\Conf(\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\rangle)\prec\Conf(\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{H}\rangle)
.

Conflict-confinement can be equivalently defined by requiring that

\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{R}\cup\mathcal{H}\rangle\not\models\bot
for every repair
\mathcal{R}
of
\mathcal{K}
.
Note that hypotheses introducing new conflicts can be desirable, as erroneous facts might need to implicitly be replaced by new facts, leading to conflicts when considering both together.

We use the terms
subset-minimal
for
\subseteq
-minimal and
cardinality-minimal
for
\leq
-minimal.
Further, we also consider minimal variants of hypotheses with additional properties:
For example, a
\subseteq
-minimal conflict-confining AR-hypothesis need only be
\subseteq
-minimal among all conflict-confining AR-hypotheses.

We investigate the following reasoning problems for a given (

-restricted)
\mathcal{S}
-abduction problem.

(Reasoning Problems)

(

1.

2.

(over

We restate the following two results that were established in

Given a KB
\mathcal{K}

\mathsf{coNP}

Let

3
Complexity Results for Combinations of Multiple Properties

We will now establish the complexity of existence and verification of hypotheses for combinations of two of the properties of being
\Sigma
-restricted, non-trivial and conflict-confining, as well as of one of these properties together with one of the minimality criteria.
Note that the combination of conflict-confining with one of the minimalities regarding the set of conflicts does not make sense, as the set of conflicts for a conflict-confining hypothesis is always minimal.

Regarding the combination of
\Sigma
-restriction with other properties or minimality criteria, we immediately obtain the following general observation:
any hardness result for the individual properties directly carries over to the case were
\Sigma
-restriction is required additionally.
To see this, observe that one can always set
\Sigma
to the (full) signature of the given abduction problem, ensuring that
\Sigma
does not actually restrict the solutions.

Observation 7
.

Let
P
be a property of hypotheses considered in this paper.
Then, the existence (resp., verification) of
\mathcal{S}
-hypotheses with property
P
can be reduced in polynomial time to the existence (resp., verification) of
\Sigma
-restricted
\mathcal{S}
-hypotheses with property
P
for
\mathcal{S}\in\{\textrm{Brave},\textrm{AR}\}
.

3.1
Existence

Here, we consider the existence problem for combinations of the three main properties of hypotheses.
Note that minimality criteria are not interesting for existence, as there is a minimal hypothesis with certain properties iff there is any such hypothesis.

First, we briefly motivate the combination of
\Sigma
-restriction and non-triviality.
One main motivation for using
\Sigma
-restriction can be to avoid trivial hypotheses.
However, a non-trivial hypothesis might still use all the symbols from the
observation, just in different combinations.
Consequently, we might lose relevant hypotheses if we use this trick.
The following example illustrates such a situation:

Example 8
.

Let
\mathcal{K}\coloneqq\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}\rangle
be a KB with
\mathcal{T}\coloneqq\{A\sqcap B\sqsubseteq C,D\sqcap\exists r.C\sqsubseteq A\}
and
\mathcal{A}\coloneqq\{B(m),r(m,n)\}
.
Moreover, let
\alpha\coloneqq C(m)
be the observation and
\Sigma\coloneqq\{C,D,m,n\}
be the signature.
Here, the trivial hypothesis
\{C(m)\}
is in fact also
\Sigma
-restricted.
The hypothesis
\mathcal{H}_{1}\coloneqq\{A(m)\}
is non-trivial but outside our signature.
Moreover,
\mathcal{H}_{2}\coloneqq\{C(n),D(m)\}
is non-trivial and uses both
C
and
m
, that is, both the concept name and the individual from the observation.

Turning to the complexity results, the combination of signature restriction and non-triviality does not affect the overall complexity, as the individual cases exhibit the same computational behavior.

Corollary 9
.

The existence problem for non-trivial
\Sigma
-restricted
\mathcal{S}
-hypotheses is

(i)

\mathsf{NP}
-complete, for
\mathcal{S}=\textrm{Brave}
, and

(ii)

\Sigma^{\mathsf{P}}_{2}
-complete, for
\mathcal{S}=\textrm{AR}
.

Proof.

We recall the membership for the non-trivial case (
Figure

1
).
For Brave semantics, existence of a non-trivial Brave-hypothesis for an instance
\langle\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}\rangle,\alpha\rangle

can be checked by guessing a candidate hypothesis
\mathcal{H}
over the signature of

\langle\mathcal{K},\alpha\rangle
, as well as a candidate repair

\mathcal{R}
of
\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{H}\rangle
and
verifying in polynomial time that
\mathcal{H}
is non-trivial,
\mathcal{R}
is a repair,
and
\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{R}\rangle\models\alpha
.
For AR semantics, we guess a hypothesis
\mathcal{H}
as before, and verify that
\mathcal{H}
is non-trivial and
\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{H}\rangle\models_{\textrm{AR}}\alpha
.
The latter is AR-entailment, which can be handled by querying an
\mathsf{NP}
oracle.

For the combination, note that both sketched approaches are guess-and-check algorithms, so we can simply include a check for
\Sigma
-restriction of
\mathcal{H}
.
Hence, we get the same complexity upper bounds as for the non-trivial setting.
The hardness in both cases follows from the lower bound for existence of non-trivial hypotheses combined with
7
.
∎

The existence problem for conflict-confining non-trivial

Proof idea.

For membership,

\mathcal{H}
over the signature of

and verify that

In particular,

For hardness, we reduce from the validity problem for
\exists\forall

Let

We construct a TBox

\mathcal{T}

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}

Intuitively, any non-trivial hypothesis corresponds to an assignment satisfying the formula
\forall Z\,\psi
, containing assertions for all literals over
Y
satisfied by the assignment.
It is not hard to see that adding such hypotheses to
\mathcal{K}
does not introduce new conflicts, and that there is no need to include additional assertions, allowing us to prove the following claim.

Claim 1
.

\Psi
is true iff
\langle\mathcal{K},\alpha\rangle
admits a non-trivial conflict-confining AR-hypothesis.

Hardness when adding
\Sigma
-restriction to either of the cases again follows from
7
.
∎

3.2
Verification

We now turn towards verification.
We first argue that the combinations of the main properties (without minimality criteria) can be handled similar as for existence.
Observe that the verification problem for either signature-restricted, or non-trivial
\mathcal{S}
-hypotheses admits the same complexity as
\mathcal{S}
-entailment for
\mathcal{S}\in\{\textrm{Brave},\textrm{AR}\}
.
Given an instance
\langle\mathcal{K},\alpha\rangle
of
\mathcal{S}
-abduction problem with
\mathcal{K}=\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}\rangle
,
verifying that an ABox
\mathcal{H}
is a non-trivial
\mathcal{S}
-hypothesis requires that

(1)

\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{H}\rangle\models_{\mathcal{S}}\alpha
, and

(2)

\mathcal{H}
is non-trivial.

Here, the complexity is dominated by the first check, as the second step can be performed in polynomial time.
Similarly, for
\Sigma
-restriction, step (2) above can be replaced by a check that
\mathcal{H}
is
\Sigma
-restricted, which again takes polynomial time.
Further, the same is still true when combining non-triviality with
\Sigma
-restriction.
The lower bound follows from hardness of verification for (general)
\mathcal{S}
-hypotheses and non-trivial
\mathcal{S}
-hypotheses combined with
7
, yielding the following corollary.

Corollary 11
.

Verification of non-trivial
\mathcal{S}
-hypotheses and of
\Sigma
-restricted
\mathcal{S}
-hypotheses is

(i)

\mathsf{NP}
-complete for
\mathcal{S}=\textrm{Brave}
, and

(ii)

\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP}
-complete for
\mathcal{S}=\textrm{AR}
.

Moreover, the same is true when combining both properties, i.e., for verification of non-trivial
\Sigma
-restricted
\mathcal{S}
-hypotheses.

It is already known that adding conflict-confinement increases the complexity for verification, but only for brave semantics (
Figure

1
).
We next prove that adding non-triviality or
\Sigma
-restriction does not increase the complexity beyond that of checking that a given ABox is a conflict-confining hypothesis.

Theorem 12
.

Verification of non-trivial conflict-confining
\mathcal{S}
-hypotheses is

(i)

\mathsf{DP}
-complete for
\mathcal{S}=\textrm{Brave}
, and

(ii)

\mathsf{co}\mathsf{NP}
-complete for
\mathcal{S}=\textrm{AR}
.

The complexity remains the same when non-triviality is replaced by
\Sigma
-restriction, and when requiring all three properties.

Proof idea.

The hardness applies due to the case of verification for conflict-confining hypotheses since the considered hypotheses in each case are indeed non-trivial (see appendix).
Hardness when adding
\Sigma
-restriction to either of the cases again follows from
7
.
We give a bit more detail for the case of conflict-confining non-trivial hypotheses.
For brevity, we only outline the proof for Brave semantics, which utilises a reduction from the combination of entailment and non-entailment under Brave semantics to verification of non-trivial conflict-confining Brave-hypotheses.
Given an instance
\langle\mathcal{K},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\rangle
for some inconsistent KB
\mathcal{K}
, the problem asks whether
\mathcal{K}\models_{\textrm{Brave}}\alpha_{1}
and
\mathcal{K}\not\models_{\textrm{Brave}}\alpha_{2}
.
This problem is
\mathsf{DP}
-complete because the first question is
\mathsf{NP}
-complete and the second question is
\mathsf{coNP}
-complete under Brave semantics:
Membership follows immediately, while only using a single KB in the problem is a slight obstacle to showing hardness.
To address this, reduce from the variant of the problem with two KBs by first ensuring that the first and the second part of the problem use disjoint sets of names, and then defining the new single KB as the disjoint union of both KBs.
For the reduction to the abduction problem at hand, assume w.l.o.g. that
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
both use the same individual, but different concept names, and let
\alpha_{1}=A(a)
,
\alpha_{2}=B(a)
, with
\mathcal{K}=\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}\rangle
.
We consider fresh concepts
C
and
D
to construct a KB
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\coloneqq\langle\mathcal{T}^{\prime},\mathcal{A}\rangle
, for

\mathcal{T}^{\prime}\coloneqq\mathcal{T}\cup\{C\sqcap A\sqsubseteq D,C\sqcap B\sqsubseteq\bot\},

an observation
\alpha\coloneqq D(a)
, and a hypothesis
\mathcal{H}\coloneqq\{C(a)\}
.
It can now be shown that
\mathcal{H}
is a Brave-hypothesis for
\langle\mathcal{K}^{\prime},\alpha\rangle
iff
\mathcal{K}\models_{\textrm{Brave}}A(a)
, and conflict-confining for
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}
iff
\mathcal{K}\not\models_{\textrm{Brave}}B(a)
.
Observing that
\alpha\not\in\mathcal{H}
, we obtain the following claim, proving correctness.

Claim 2
.

\mathcal{H}
is a non-trivial conflict-confining Brave-hypothesis for
\alpha
in
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}
iff
\mathcal{K}\models_{\textrm{Brave}}\alpha_{1}
and
\mathcal{K}\not\models_{\textrm{Brave}}\alpha_{2}
.

We next prove membership in each case.
Let
\langle\mathcal{K},\alpha\rangle
be an
\mathcal{S}
-abduction problem with
\mathcal{K}=\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}\rangle
and
\mathcal{H}
an ABox.

We begin with Brave semantics.
Observe that
\mathcal{H}
is a non-trivial conflict-confining
Brave
-hypothesis iff

(1)

\mathcal{H}
is non-trivial,

(2)

\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{H}\rangle\models_{\textrm{Brave}}\alpha
, and

(3)

\Conf(\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{H}\rangle)=\Conf(\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}\rangle)
.

Here, (1) can be easily checked in polynomial time.
Moreover, (2) is instance checking for
\mathcal{E\!L}_{\bot}
under brave semantics and hence in
\mathsf{NP}
, while (3) can be checked in
\mathsf{coNP}
, since the complement can be checked by guessing a conflict
\mathcal{C}
s.t.
\mathcal{C}\in\Conf(\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{H}\rangle)
and
\mathcal{C}\not\in\Conf(\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}\rangle)
.
Concretely, this amounts to guessing a
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{H}
such that:

(a)

\mathcal{C}\not\subseteq\mathcal{A}
,

(b)

\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{C}\rangle\models\bot
, and

(c)

\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{C}\setminus\{\gamma\}\rangle\not\models\bot
for any
\gamma\in C
.

Checking whether
\mathcal{C}
satisfies (a)–(c) can be done in polynomial time.
Altogether, (a) and (b) together are in
\mathsf{NP}
, while (c) is in
\mathsf{coNP}
.
Hence, the problem is contained in
\mathsf{DP}
.

The membership proof for brave semantics can be adapted to the cases where
\Sigma
-restriction is added as a requirement, or replaces non-triviality, by noting that checking
\Sigma
-restriction on top or instead of non-triviality can also be done in polynomial time.

We now turn to membership for AR-semantics.
First, check non-triviality of
\mathcal{H}
in polynomial time.
If this is the case,
\mathcal{H}
is a non-trivial conflict-confining
AR
-hypothesis iff

(1)

\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{H}\rangle\models_{\textrm{AR}}\alpha
, and

(2)

\mathcal{H}
is conflict-confining in
\mathcal{K}
, i.e.,
\Conf(\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{H}\rangle)=\Conf(\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}\rangle)
.

As a result, one can guess a candidate repair
\mathcal{R}
and a candidate conflict
\mathcal{C}
of
\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{H}\rangle
simultaneously, as a counter example to
\mathcal{H}
being an
AR
-hypothesis (
\mathcal{R}
) and conflict-confining (
\mathcal{C}
).
More precisely, one needs to check that
\mathcal{R}
is a subset-maximal
\mathcal{T}
-consistent subset of
\mathcal{A}
(hence a repair) and
\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{R}\rangle\not\models\alpha
, as well as that
\mathcal{C}\not\in\Conf(\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}\rangle)
, but
\mathcal{C}\in\Conf(\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{H}\rangle)
.
All these checks can be performed in polynomial time.
The claimed
\mathsf{coNP}
-membership follows by observing that
\mathcal{H}
is a conflict-confining hypothesis for
\alpha
in
\mathcal{K}
iff both these checks return false.
Membership when adding
\Sigma
-restriction to the conflict-confining and the conflict-confining non-trivial case again follows by noting that
\Sigma
-restriction can be checked separately in polynomial time.
∎

Combination of Properties with Minimality Criteria

We next turn towards combinations involving minimality criteria, beginning with
\subseteq
-minimality.
Here, the following general observation regarding our main properties is very helpful.

Observation 13
.

The properties of being conflict-confining, non-trivial, and
\Sigma
-restricted are downwards-closed.
More precisely, let
\langle\mathcal{K},\alpha\rangle
be an
\mathcal{S}
-abduction problem for
\mathcal{S}\in\{\textrm{Brave},\textrm{AR}\}
,
\mathcal{H}
an ABox and
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
.
If
\mathcal{H}
is conflict-confining for
\mathcal{K}
, then so is
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
.
Similarly, if
\alpha\not\in\mathcal{H}
, then
\alpha\not\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
, and if
\mathcal{H}
is an ABox over a signature
\Sigma
, then so is
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
.

This implies that an ABox
\mathcal{H}
being a
\subseteq
-minimal conflict-confining
\mathcal{S}
-hypotheses is equivalent to
\mathcal{H}
being a
\subseteq
-minimal
\mathcal{S}
-hypothesis and being conflict-confining (as a separate requirement). The same observation applies to the other two properties as well as combinations of properties.

Using the established results on verification of
\subseteq
-minimal hypotheses and the above observation, we obtain the following complexities when additionally requiring other properties.

Theorem 14
.

Verification of
\subseteq
-minimal non-trivial
\mathcal{S}
-hypotheses is

(i)

\mathsf{DP}
-complete, for
\mathcal{S}=\textrm{Brave}
, and

(ii)

\mathrm{\Pi}^{\mathsf{P}}_{2}
-complete, for
\mathcal{S}=\textrm{AR}
.

The complexity remains same when we replace non-triviality by
\Sigma
-restriction, and when combining both properties.

Proof idea.

The hardness in each case follows from the corresponding result on verification of
\subseteq
-minimal
\mathcal{S}
-hypotheses (see appendix).
Observe that the reduction in each case uses a non-trivial hypothesis,
thus the hardness applies to verification of non-trivial subset-minimal hypotheses.
Again, when adding
\Sigma
-restriction (either instead of or in combination with non-triviality), hardness is retained by
7
.
We outline the reduction for
\mathsf{DP}
-hardness with Brave semantics as it it also used in later results.
We reduce again from a combination of entailment and non-entailment under Brave semantics to verification of
\subseteq
-minimal Brave-hypotheses, similar to hardness under Brave semantics in the proof of
Theorem

12
.
Given an instance
\langle\mathcal{K},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\rangle
for some inconsistent KB
\mathcal{K}
, let
\alpha_{1}=A(a)
,
\alpha_{2}=B(a)
, and
\mathcal{K}=\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}\rangle
.
We now construct a KB
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}
, an observation
\alpha
, and an ABox
\mathcal{H}
as follows.
Let
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\coloneqq\langle\mathcal{T}^{\prime},\mathcal{A}\rangle
with

\mathcal{T}^{\prime}\coloneqq\mathcal{T}\cup\{B\sqsubseteq C,C\sqcap D\sqcap A\sqsubseteq Q\},

\alpha\coloneqq Q(a)
, and
\mathcal{H}\coloneqq\{C(a),D(a)\}
for fresh concepts
C,D,Q
.
Intuitively,
\mathcal{H}
is a Brave-hypothesis for
\langle\mathcal{K}^{\prime},\alpha\rangle
iff
\mathcal{K}\models_{\textrm{Brave}}A(a)
and
\mathcal{H}
is
\subseteq
-minimal iff
\mathcal{K}\not\models_{\textrm{Brave}}B(a)
.
To see the latter, note that
\subseteq
-minimality is violated iff
\{D(a)\}
is also a Brave-hypothesis for
\langle\mathcal{K}^{\prime},\alpha\rangle
, which is the case iff
\mathcal{K}\models_{\textrm{Brave}}B(a)
.
The following claim shows correctness.

Claim 3
.

\mathcal{H}
is a
\subseteq
-minimal non-trivial hypothesis for
\langle\mathcal{K}^{\prime},\alpha\rangle
iff
\mathcal{K}\models_{\textrm{Brave}}\alpha_{1}
and
\mathcal{K}\not\models_{\textrm{Brave}}\alpha_{2}
.

Membership follows from the the upper bound for verification of
\subseteq
-minimal
\mathcal{S}
-hypotheses together with
13
:
By the observation, it is sufficient to check that
\mathcal{H}
is a
\subseteq
-minimal
\mathcal{S}
-hypotheses, and separately checking that it is non-trivial or
\Sigma
-restricted.
Since the latter two properties can be checked in
\mathsf{P}
, we obtain the desired upper bounds.
For this, note that both
\mathsf{DP}
and
\mathrm{\Pi}^{\mathsf{P}}_{2}
are closed under intersection with languages from
\mathsf{P}
.

∎

Verification of
\subseteq
-minimal conflict-confining hypotheses enjoys the same complexity for Brave-hypotheses, but the precise complexity of AR-hypotheses remains open for now.

Theorem 15
.

Verification of
\subseteq

(i)

\mathsf{DP}
-complete for

Proof idea.

Membership for both semantics follows from the membership of verification of
\subseteq

\mathcal{S}
-hypotheses, again using

:

\mathcal{H}
is a
\subseteq
-minimal
\mathcal{S}

As the latter is in
\mathsf{coNP}
and both
\mathsf{DP}
as well as
\mathrm{\Pi}^{\mathsf{P}}_{2}
are closed under intersection with languages from
\mathsf{coNP}
, we obtain the desired upper bounds.
As both non-triviality and

, the same upper bound still holds when adding one or both of these properties.

\mathsf{DP}
-hardness for Brave semantics follows due to the same complexity of verification of

is actually conflict-confining. As a result,

Cardinality-minimality behaves similar to subset-minimality, if we consider either non-trivial or
\Sigma
-restricted hypotheses.

-minimal

Theorem 16
.

Verification of
\leq
-minimal non-trivial

\mathsf{DP}
-complete for
\mathcal{S}=\textrm{Brave}
, and

(ii)

\mathrm{\Pi}^{\mathsf{P}}_{2}
-complete for
\mathcal{S}=\textrm{AR}
.

The complexity remains the same when we replace non-triviality by
\Sigma
-restriction, and when combining both properties.

Proof.

Brave semantics:
For membership, one can guess a set
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
of assertions over the signature of
\mathcal{K}
such that
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
is a counter-witness to
\mathcal{H}
being a
\leq
-minimal
\mathcal{S}
-hypothesis for
\langle\mathcal{K},\alpha\rangle
.
Precisely,
\mathcal{H}
is a
\leq

(1)

(2)

there is no

\mathsf{coNP}
, yielding
\mathsf{DP}
-membership in total.

\Sigma
-restriction instead of (or in addition to) non-triviality, as verification of (non-trivial)

For hardness, we reconsider the reduction employed in the case of verification of
\subseteq

).
We observe that the considered hypothesis
\mathcal{H}
is
\subseteq
-minimal iff
\mathcal{H}
is
\leq
-minimal.
The reduction already applies for the non-trivial case as
\mathcal{H}
is non-trivial.
The case of
\Sigma
-restriction can again be handled using
7
.

AR semantics: The
\mathrm{\Pi}^{\mathsf{P}}_{2}
-membership can be shown similarly to
\mathsf{DP}
-membership for Brave semantics above.
Precisely,
\mathcal{H}
is a non-trivial

(1)

(2)

The complexity now readily follows from

We now turn to Brave semantics. For membership, we need to determine whether

\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{H}\rangle\models_{\textrm{Brave}}\alpha

(2)

\mathcal{H}
is conflict-confining, and

(3)

Here, one can guess the ABox
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
as a counter-witness, and handle all the remaining checks needed in (1)–(3) via oracle calls.
Consequently, we obtain membership in
\mathrm{\Pi}^{\mathsf{P}}_{2}

For hardness, we reduce from the complement of the existence problem for conflict-confining Brave-hypotheses.
To this aim, we extend and reuse the reduction for this case from our earlier work

.
The trick we employ here (details in the appendix) is to use candidate hypothesis
\mathcal{H}
of very large size following a similar idea as in the proof of
Theorem

16
for the case of AR semantics.
However, this trick cannot be applied as directly: We know that there might exist a conflict-confining hypothesis even though the observation itself is not conflict-confining.
In this case,
\mathcal{H}
would also not be conflict-confining, interfering with the rest of the construction.
Nevertheless, we invented a way to construct a hypothesis that avoids new conflicts

We apply the same strategy to our considered hypothesis
\mathcal{H}
here, which requires slightly changing the newly added TBox axiom in the proof of

16

This yields the mentioned
\mathrm{\Pi}^{\mathsf{P}}_{2}
-hardness.

Finally, we consider
\subseteq_{c}
-minimality. Recall that the combination of
\subseteq_{c}
-minimality and conflict-confining is not interesting, since conflict-confining hypotheses are always
\subseteq_{c}
-minimal.

4
Conclusion and Outlook

We observe that combining basic properties (excluding minimality criteria) and potentially subset-minimality does not increase the complexity for either existence or verification of
\mathcal{S}
-hypotheses in
\mathcal{E\!L}_{\bot}
:
The complexity for all considered cases is governed by the highest complexity among the individual properties involved.
In contrast, we often see an increase in the complexity, when combining cardinality-minimality or conflict-minimality with other properties.

Observe that the hardness proofs for some of our results (e.g., Theorems
10
,
14
and
15
) do not use role assertions.
Consequently, several of our results already hold for propositional Horn logic.
Nevertheless, we leave a detailed complexity characterization for this fragment as future work.

It will be interesting to complete the picture by also extending the study to conflict-minimality when considering the number of conflicts (instead of subset-minimality), and closing the gap for the case of
\subseteq
-minimal conflict-confining AR-hypotheses, which both remain open for now.

Further, we previously
also obtained complexity results for ABox abduction in the description logic

\mathrm{DL\text{-}Lite}

11
.
Preliminary work for combinations of properties in this setting shows that in some cases the complexity is again governed by the highest complexity among the individual properties, but there is a notable exception.
It turns out that in case of
\mathrm{DL\text{-}Lite}
, conflict-confining AR-hypotheses have a much simpler structure:
Every conflict-confining AR-hypothesis contains a
singleton
AR-hypothesis, which is of course again conflict-confining.
This often makes abduction problems
easier
when adding conflict-confinement in addition to some other property (or minimality condition) compared to the same problem without requiring conflict-confinement.
It will be interesting to fully understand the complexity landscape for combinations of properties in this setting.

It should be noted that in case of expressive DLs such as
\mathcal{ALC}
and its extensions, the complexity of the considered abduction problems seems significantly less interesting.
The reason is that for these DLs, entailment is often already
\mathsf{ExpTime}
-complete.
Consequently, the complexity of abduction tends to be dominated by the complexity of entailment.

Beside considering different DLs, another important direction for the future is to consider other repair semantics.
Most prominently, IAR semantics are currently missing from our study.
While there has been previous work on abduction under IAR semantics, it was more practical in nature, obtaining an upper bound for one specific case in
\mathrm{DL\text{-}Lite}
.
The complexity landscape for different (combinations of) properties remains widely unexplored.
