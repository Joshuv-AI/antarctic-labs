Going into a tailspin near the abyss: analytic solutions for spinning particles on near equatorial, plunging orbits in Kerr spacetime
Gabriel Andres Piovano 1

Abstract
This work presents, the first time, analytic solutions for the nearly equatorial, plunging motion of a spinning test-particle in Kerr spacetime. The equations of motion are solved at first-order in the small-body spin for all classes of plunging orbits with energy E < 1 E<1 . The solutions incorporate the small precession of the orbital plane caused by the precession of the particle’s spin. Additionally, we present the correction to the radius of the innermost bound circular orbit in closed form, and introduce a novel, Keplerian-like parametrization for generic plunging orbits. Our solutions will be useful in the modelling of inspiral-merger-ringdown waveforms with self-force methods and black hole perturbation theory.

Introduction

Gravitational wave (GW) astronomy is a blooming research field. Since the first landmark detection of GW in 2015
2
, the LIGO-Virgo-Kagra (LVK) collaboration has detected more than 200 events
1
, with even more detections expected by the end of the O4 run. Almost all of these events originated from astrophysical binaries of comparable masses, although few asymmetric mass-system have been observed
3

In the milliHertz frequency band, the Laser Interferometer Space Antenna (LISA)
4
;
14
will detect new type of sources like the Extreme Mass Ratio Inspirals (or EMRIs). EMRI binaries are constituted by a stellar mass compact object (the secondary) orbiting a supermassive black hole (the primary), the latter with mass
M\sim 10^{5}-10^{7}M_{\odot}

14
. Intermediate mass ratio coalescences (IMRAC), with mass ratio
\epsilon\sim 10^{-2}-10^{-4}
, represent another candidate sources observable by both LISA and next-generation ground based detector such as the Einstein Telescope
42
;
14

GW from asymmetric mass sources like EMRIs and IMRACs are best modeled within the self-force (SF) framework, which naturally accounts for the disparate scales involved in the dynamics. SF schemes relies on perturbative expansions in
\epsilon
to find approximate solutions of the Einstein-Field equations. We label with
n
SF the nth order SF term in the mass ratio. At zeroth order in
\epsilon
, the binary is treated as a point-particle with mass
\mu
free-falling in a Kerr spacetime generated by the primary. Corrections to the geodesic motion arise from radiation-reaction forces and finite-size effects of the of smaller companion. In turn, the secondary induces metric perturbations on the background spacetime, leading to generation of gravitational waves
55
;
6
.

Recently, Ref.
77
showed that SF waveform models incorporating 2SF corrections are in remarkable agreement with NR simulations for mass ratios of order
\mathcal{O}(1/10)
, with hybrid SF-Post-Newtonian (PN) waveforms achieving even better performance almost up-to merger
30
. An extensive program is underway to construct inspiral-merger-ringdown (IMR) waveform models for asymmetric mass-binaries based on SF theory, Post-Newtonian expansions and black hole perturbation theory
30
;
31
;
39
;
58
;
7
;

In the SF framework, the merger and ringdown phase can be modeled by considering a point-particle transitioning from the last stable orbit to the plunge phase. The leading order contribution come from the geodesic homoclininc motion and plunge, with radiation-reaction effects ensuring the smooth transition from the late inspiral into the plunge. After modeling the orbital motion, it is possible then to produce the merger part of the signal and gluing it with the inspiral part with a matched asymptotic expansion procedure
36
;
30
;
35
;
39
.

In addition to SF effects, complete IMR waveform models must include post-geodesic corrections due to the spin and higher order multipoles of the smaller object. Secondary spin corrections contribute to the inspiral waveform phase at first post-adiabatic order (or 1PA)
44
, therefore being on par with conservative 1SF
72
;
48
and dissipative 2SF effects
55
;
77
;
74
. In the transition-to-plunge phase, the field equations are expanded in powers of
\epsilon^{1/5}
in a post-leading transition-to-plunge expansion (or PLT). Such a perturbative expansion must be carried out up to 7 PLT order to obtain a complete IMR waveform in the SF program. Linear corrections due to the secondary spin and 1SF terms enters at third PLT order, and at first post-geodesic order in the plunging orbits

;
30

This work focus on the conservative effects due to the secondary spin, and neglect neglect radiation reaction and other SF effects. Specifically, we consider bound plunges,
that is, orbits with energy
E<1

By contrast, bound plunging orbits for a spinning particle has received little attention in the literature. Ref.
13

However, a complete analysis of bound (and unbound) plunges for a spinning particle is still missing, and there are no analytic solutions in Kerr-spacetime. Geodesic plunges has been studied in much greater detail. Refs.
38
;
47
;

Skoupý and Witzany found in Ref.
67

However, their solutions are given in terms of virtual geodesics parametrized in a ”deformed” Mino-time. As such, the orbits are not completely separated into physical geodesic plus spin-corrections. For this reason, it is not known yet how to map the shifts to the orbits given in Ref.

;
23
;
51
;

67

For the reasons mentioned above, this work focuses on nearly equatorial orbits in Kerr spacetime, for which the equations of motion are partially decoupled
22
;
69
. General solutions by quadrature for the nearly equatorial motion were given in Ref.
53

Here, we provide an in-depth study of bound plunging orbits for a spinning particle, including the precession of the particle’s spin and the ensuing precession of the orbital plane. We also introduce a convenient a Keplerian-like parametrization for plunging orbits with complex radial roots, valid for spinning and spinless particles. All solutions to the motion are given in analytic form as elliptic integrals and functions or elementary functions. Additionally, we derived the shift to the innermost bound circular orbit (IBCO) in closed form.

r_{2\rm g}=r_{3\rm g}
defines the location of the separatrix. In general, a separatrix is the boundary that divide regions of the phase space with distinct qualitative behaviors. In our case, the separatrix correspond to a homoclinic orbits

p^{*}_{\rm g}=p^{*}_{\rm g}(a,e^{*}_{\rm g})
for the separatrix is considered as function of
(a,e^{*}_{\rm g})
, with eccentricity
e^{*}_{\rm g}
a free parameter. The location of the separatrix
p^{*}_{\rm g}
can be found as root of a polynomial
68
.

Critical plunging orbits have the same constants of motion of homoclinic orbits, but different initial conditions, with
r_{\rm g}(0)\in[0,r_{2\rm g})
for
\lambda=0
. Moreover, the Mino time
\Lambda^{\mathsf{crit}}
require to reach the ring singularity
r_{\rm g}=0
from the double root
r_{2\rm g}
is infinite

\Lambda^{\mathsf{crit}}=\int^{r_{2\rm g}}_{0}\frac{\mathrm{d}r^{\prime}}{\sqrt{R_{\rm g}(r^{\prime})}}=\infty

Another characteristic of a critical plunge is the winding of the particle’s motion around the turning point
r_{2\rm g}
, since the azimuthal trajectory
\phi(r_{\rm g})
(and the coordinate time trajectory
t_{\rm g}(r_{\rm g})
as well) diverge logarithmically at the double root (see Appendices
B
,
G
and Fig.
3
).

II.1.2
ISCO plunges

A plunging orbit from the ISCO can be considered as a special case of a critical plunge with
(p^{*}_{\rm g},e^{*}_{\rm g})=(r_{\rm g,\text{isco}},0)
. ISCO plunges have the same constants of motion of the ISCO, but their orbital radius evolve from
r_{\rm g,\text{isco}}
to
r_{\rm g}=0

Since the radial potential has a triple root, the coordinate time trajectory
t_{\rm g}(r_{\rm g})
and azimuthal trajectory
\phi(r_{\rm g})
diverge as pole near the ISCO (see Appendices
B
,
G

II.1.3
Plunges related to bound orbits (PBO)

Plunges related to bound orbits (or PBO in short) have the same constants of motion of periodic orbits but initial condition
r_{\rm g}(0)\leq r_{3\rm g}
for
\lambda=0
(see
40
;
24
). Depending on the root structure of the radial potential, PBO are described by either elliptic integrals and functions, or by elementary functions. The latter case occur when the radial potential admit stable, circular orbits, that is
r_{1\rm g}=r_{2\rm g}
. PBO with a double root reduces to the circular motion at the ISCO when
r_{1\rm g}=r_{2\rm g}\to r_{3\rm g}
, while PBO with all distinct roots reduces to the critical plunges for
r_{2\rm}\to r_{3\rm g}
. Finally, there is a maximum value of the orbital angular momentum,
L^{+}_{z\rm g}
for which PBO are allowed
16
. Such a value correspond to the angular momentum for which
r_{3\rm g}=r_{+}
. When
L^{+}_{z\rm g}\leq L_{z\rm g}
, PBO is forbidden, but periodic orbits are still possible.

II.1.4
Generic plunges

Generic plunging orbits are characterized by the presence of complex conjugate roots
r_{2\rm g}=\overline{r_{3\rm g}}=\rho_{r\rm g}+i\rho_{i\rm g}
, with
\rho_{r\rm g}
and
\rho_{i\rm g}
the real and imaginary part, and the overbar denoting complex conjugation. We introduce here a novel parametrization, which relies on the same Keplerian-like orbital elements commonly used for periodic orbits. This parametrization is convenient for the derivation of the spin-corrections to the motion.
First, we define the real root
r_{1\rm g}
and real part
\rho_{r\rm g}
as

\displaystyle r_{1\rm g}=\frac{p_{\rm g}}{1-e_{\rm g}}\ ,\;\qquad\rho_{r\rm g}=\frac{p_{\rm g}}{1+e_{\rm g}}\ ,

(24)

with
p_{\rm g}\leq p^{*}_{\rm g}
and
e_{\rm g}\leq e^{*}_{\rm g}
. Moreover, we use Vieta’s formulas to find relations between the roots
r_{1\rm g},r_{2\rm g},r_{3\rm g}
of the polynomial (
16

and
\rho_{i\rm g}
in terms of

and
\rho_{r\rm g}

\displaystyle E_{\rm g}(p_{\rm g},{e_{\rm g}})

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{2(\rho_{\rm g}-1)+r_{1\rm g}}{r_{1\rm g}+2\rho_{\rm g}}}\ ,

(25)

\displaystyle L_{z\rm g}(p_{\rm g},{e_{\rm g}})

\displaystyle=\pm 2\sqrt{\frac{(r_{1\rm g}-2)r^{2}_{1\rm g}\rho_{\rm g}\mp 2\sqrt{a^{2}r_{1\rm g}\rho_{\rm g}\Delta(r_{1\rm g})\big(2(\rho_{\rm g}-1)+r_{1\rm g}\big)}+a^{2}\big(r_{1\rm g}\rho_{\rm g}+r_{1\rm g}+2(\rho_{\rm g}-1)\big)}{(r_{1\rm g}-2)^{2}(r_{1\rm g}+2\rho_{\rm g})}}\ ,

(26)

\displaystyle\rho_{i\rm g}(p_{\rm g},{e_{\rm g}})

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{a^{2}-\rho_{\rm g}(\rho_{\rm g}-4)+2\frac{4\rho_{\rm g}+a^{2}(\rho_{\rm g}+1)}{r_{1\rm g}-2}+4\frac{2a^{2}\rho_{\rm g}\mp\sqrt{a^{2}r_{1\rm g}\rho_{r\rm g}\Delta(r_{1\rm g})\big(2(\rho_{\rm g}-1)+r_{1\rm g}\big)}}{(r_{1\rm g}-2)^{2}}}\ ,

(27)

with the upper (lower) sign for prograde (retrograde) orbits, and
\Delta(r_{1\rm g})=r^{2}_{1\rm g}-2r_{1\rm g}+a^{2}

In the limit
(p_{\rm g},{e_{\rm g}})\to(p^{*}_{\rm g},{e^{*}_{\rm g}})
, the imaginary part
\rho_{i\rm g}\to 0
, while Eqs. (
25
)-(
26

However, the Jacobian matrix for Eqs. (
25

is different from the Jacobian matrix for the constants of motion of homoclinic orbits. In fact, the determinant of the Jacobian matrix of Eqs. (
25
)-(
26

(p_{\rm g},{e_{\rm g}})
is not vanishing at the geodesic separatrix.

II.1.5
Special plunges

Finally, a special type of plunge occurs when the condition
L_{z\rm g}=aE_{\rm g}
is satisfied. In such a case, the radial potential

simplifies to

II.2

We solve the system (

III.7
Special plunges

Special plunges satisfy the constraint
L_{z\rm g}=aE_{\rm g}
, which implies that

\delta L_{z}=a\delta E\ .

(103)

Thus, the radial potential
R_{5}(r)
reduce to

R_{5}(r)=\big[(E^{2}_{\rm g}-1+\epsilon\chi_{\parallel}2E_{\rm g}\delta E)r^{2}+2r-a(a-\epsilon\chi_{\parallel}2E^{2}_{\rm g})\big]r^{2}\ ,

(104)

The corrections to the radial turning points
\delta r_{1}
and
\delta r_{2}
are

\displaystyle\delta r_{1}=\frac{E_{\rm g}\big(aE_{\rm g}(1-E^{2}_{\rm g})-(a^{2}-2r_{1\rm g})\delta E\big)}{(1-E^{2}_{\rm g})\sqrt{1-a^{2}(1-E^{2}_{\rm g})}}\ ,

(105)

\displaystyle\delta r_{2}=-\frac{E_{\rm g}\big(aE_{\rm g}(1-E^{2}_{\rm g})-(a^{2}-2r_{1\rm g})\delta E\big)}{(1-E^{2}_{\rm g})\sqrt{1-a^{2}(1-E^{2}_{\rm g})}}

(106)

The FE gauge for special plunging orbits corresponds to the condition
\delta r_{1}=\delta r_{2}
, which fix the shift
\delta E
as

\delta E=-\frac{aE_{\rm g}(1-E^{2}_{\rm g})^{2}}{2-a^{2}(1-E^{2}_{\rm g})}

(107)

The correction
\delta r_{1}
in the FE gauge then simplifies to

\delta r_{1}=-\frac{2aE^{2}_{\rm g}}{2-a^{2}(1-E^{2}_{\rm g})}\ ,

(108)

while the function
\delta V_{r}(r_{\rm g})
is defined as

\displaystyle\delta V_{r}(r_{\rm g})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\bigg(\displaystyle\delta r_{1}\sum^{2}_{i=1}\frac{1}{r_{\rm g}-r_{i\rm g}}+\frac{2E_{\rm g}\delta E}{1-E^{2}_{\rm g}}\bigg)\,.

(109)

The solutions of Eqs. (
76
) for a special plunge reads

\displaystyle\delta r(r_{\rm g})

\displaystyle=\sqrt{R_{g}(r_{\rm g})}\bigg(\frac{E_{\rm g}\delta E}{1-E^{2}_{\rm g}}I^{\mathsf{spec}}(r_{\rm g})

\displaystyle+\frac{\delta r_{1}}{2}\displaystyle\sum^{2}_{i=1}I^{\mathsf{spec}}_{1/(r-r_{i})}(r_{\rm g})\bigg)\ ,

(110)

\displaystyle\delta t(r_{\rm g})

\displaystyle=\frac{T_{\rm g}(r_{\rm g})\delta r(r_{\rm g})}{\sqrt{R_{\rm g}(r_{\rm g})}}-\frac{\delta r_{1}}{2}\displaystyle\sum^{2}_{i=1}T_{\rm g}(r_{i\rm g})I^{\mathsf{spec}}_{1/(r-r_{i})}(r_{\rm g})

\displaystyle-\frac{\delta E}{1-E^{2}_{\rm g}}\Big(I^{\mathsf{spec}}_{r^{2}}(r_{\rm g})+2I^{\mathsf{spec}}_{r}(r_{\rm g})+4I^{\mathsf{spec}}(r_{\rm g})\Big)

\displaystyle-\frac{E_{\rm g}}{2}\delta r_{1}\Big[2I^{\mathsf{spec}}_{r}(r_{\rm g})+\big(4+r_{1\rm g}+r_{2\rm g}\big)I^{\mathsf{spec}}(r_{\rm g})\Big]\ ,

(111)

\displaystyle\delta\phi(r_{\rm g})

\displaystyle=\frac{\Phi_{\rm g}(r_{\rm g})\delta r(r_{\rm g})}{\sqrt{R_{\rm g}(r_{\rm g})}}+\frac{\delta r_{1}}{2}\displaystyle\sum^{2}_{i=1}\Phi_{\rm g}(r_{i\rm g})I^{\mathsf{spec}}_{1/(r-r_{i})}(r_{\rm g})

\displaystyle+\bigg(E_{\rm g}-\frac{a\delta E}{1-E^{2}_{\rm g}}\bigg)I^{\mathsf{spec}}(r_{\rm g})\ ,

(112)

where the integrals
I^{\mathsf{spec}}(r_{\rm g})

and

r_{\rm g}(0)=r_{1\rm g}
for
\lambda=0

r_{\rm g}=0

IV
Polar motion and spin-precession

The polar motion is governed by Eq. (
45
), which is the equation of motion of a forced harmonic oscillator driven by the spin-curvature force for
L_{z\text{rd}}\neq 0
. When
L_{z\text{rd}}=0
, Eq. (
45
) reduces to the equation of motion of a free harmonic oscillator. We impose that the referential geodesics is constrained on the equatorial plane through the initial conditions
(\delta z_{\text{h}}(0),\mathrm{d}\delta z_{\text{h}}(0)/\mathrm{d}\lambda)=(0,0)
for the homogeneous solutions
\delta z_{\text{h}}(\lambda)
of Eq. (
45
). As a result, the general solution of Eq. (
45
) is given by
78
;
51
;
53

\delta z(\lambda)=-\chi_{\perp}\frac{\cos\psi_{\rm p}\sqrt{r^{2}_{\rm g}+L_{z\text{rd}}^{2}}}{r_{\rm g}L_{z\text{rd}}}\ ,\quad\text{ for }L_{z\text{rd}}\neq 0

(113)

When
L_{z\text{rd}}=0
(i.e. for special plunges), the motion is constrained on the equatorial plane, which implies that the secondary spin is not precessing. Notice that the
shift
\delta z(\lambda)
is independent from the corrections to the constants of motion, therefore it is the same in any spin-gauge. Moreover, the polar motion orbits diverge at
r_{\rm g}=0
. Such behavior is expected, since the radial motion is also divergent at the ring singularity.

With the solution (
113
) at hand, we only need to find an analytic expression for the the spin-precession phase
\psi_{p}(\lambda)
, whose evolution satifies Eq. (
43
). The latter can be written as

\frac{\mathrm{d}\psi_{p}}{\mathrm{d}r_{\rm g}}=-\frac{\Psi_{r}(r_{\rm g})}{\sqrt{R_{\rm g}(r_{\rm g})}}\,,

(114)

Using partial fraction decomposition, the previous equation become

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\psi_{p}}{\mathrm{d}r_{\rm g}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(a+E_{\rm g}L_{z\text{rd}})L_{z\text{rd}}}{2i\sqrt{R_{\rm g}(r_{\rm g})}}\bigg(\frac{1}{r_{\rm g}-i|L_{z\text{rd}}|}-\frac{1}{r_{\rm g}+i|L_{z\text{rd}}|}\bigg)

\displaystyle-\frac{a\mathrm{sgn}(L_{z\text{rd}})+E_{\rm g}|L_{z\text{rd}}|}{\sqrt{R_{\rm g}(r_{\rm g})}}\ ,

(115)

which can be immediately solved in terms of elementary functions or elliptic integrals as

\psi^{\mathsf{y}}_{p}(r_{\rm g})=\psi^{\mathsf{y},\text{sgn}}_{p}(r_{\rm g})\ ,\qquad\mathsf{y}=\mathsf{crit},\mathsf{isco},\mathsf{pbo}

(116)

with

\displaystyle\psi^{\mathsf{y},\text{sgn}}_{p}(r_{\rm g})

\displaystyle=-\big(a\mathrm{sgn}(L_{z\text{rd}})+E_{\rm g}|L_{z\text{rd}}|\big)I^{\mathsf{y}}(r_{\rm g})

\displaystyle+\frac{L_{z\text{rd}}}{2}(a+E_{\rm g}L_{z\text{rd}})\mathrm{Im}\,\!\Big(I^{\mathsf{y}}_{1/(r-i|L_{z\text{rd}}|)}(r_{\rm g})

\displaystyle-I^{\mathsf{y}}_{1/(r+i|L_{z\text{rd}}|)}(r_{\rm g})\Big)\ ,

(117)

The integrals
I^{\mathsf{y}}(r_{\rm g}),I^{\mathsf{y}}_{1/(r\pm i|L_{z\text{rd}}|)}(r_{\rm g})
with
\mathsf{y}=\mathsf{crit},\mathsf{isco},\mathsf{pbo}
are given in Appendices
B
-
D
for critical plunges, ISCO plunges, and PBO, respectively. For generic plunges, the sign convention is different, and

\psi^{\mathsf{gen}}_{p}(r_{\rm g})=-\psi^{\mathsf{y},\text{sgn}}_{p}(r_{\rm g})\ ,

(118)

with
I^{\mathsf{gen}}(r_{\rm g}),I^{\mathsf{gen}}_{1/(r\pm i|L_{z\text{rd}}|)}(r_{\rm g})
given in Appendix
E
. The sign choice for
\psi^{\mathsf{gen}}_{p}
ensures that, in the limit
\rho_{i\rm g}\to 0
, generic plunging orbits continuously reduce to homoclinic orbits.

For all types of bound plunging motion, the Mino-Time spin precession frequency is simply given by
\Upsilon_{p}=\Psi_{r}(r_{\rm g})
. Finally, the bottom panels of Figs.
3
-
9
present the orthogonal projection onto a co-rotating polar plane for critical plunges, ISCO plunges, PBO and generic plunge, respectively, with
\delta z=\delta z(r_{\rm g})
parametrized using the geodesic orbital radius
r_{\rm g}
.

V
Discussion

In this work, we present the first, complete study of bound plunging motion of a spinning test-particle in Kerr spacetime. We solve the equations of motion in the near equatorial case, including the precession of the particle’s spin and orbital plane. The solutions are given in closed form as elliptic integrals and Jacobi elliptic functions or elementary functions. Additionally, we derive a closed form expression for the shift to the IBCO, which is useful to the characterization of both plunging and parabolic scattering. We also introduce a new, convenient parametrization for generic plunging orbits using Keplerian-like orbital elements.

Our analytic solutions can be employed to add conservative spin corrections to IMR waveform models constructed via self-force and black hole perturbation theory
36
;
25
;
18
;
17
;
8
;
7
;
20
;
35
;
39
;
18
;
17
.
Moreover, they represent an useful starting point to calculate quadratic corrections in the particle’s spin
19
;
56
;
57
, which are required in the modeling of the transition-to-plunge phase
31
;
30
;
36
.

In a follow up work
54
, we will extend the methods presented here to complete the analysis of the nearly equatorial motion for a spinning particle. The methods presented here can be readily extended to derive analytic solutions for plunging orbits related to scattering orbits
41
, unbound plunging orbits from infinity

;

, and scattering orbits

;

;

. Such solutions would find applications, for instance, in the calculation of gravitational radiation from hyperbolic and parabolic orbits
