Decoupled energy estimates for tensorial non-linear wave equations and applications
Sari Ghanem

Abstract
We prove energy estimates for solutions to a tensorial system of coupled non-linear wave equations, in a way that is suitable to deal with the structure of the non-linearity that arises from the Einstein-Yang-Mills system in the Lorenz gauge as well as with other new different non-linearities. We establish suitable bounds on the L 2 L^{2} -norm of each component in a frame decomposition of the tensorial solutions, in way that does not involve all the other components of the tensor, which would allow us to decouple the higher order energy estimates for certain components from the other components. We achieve this partly by exploiting the tensorial structure of the coupled non-linear wave equations, where the background metric that is à priori unknown, is a perturbation of the Minkowski space-time in a certain fixed system of coordinates, and by exploiting the structure of the commutator term for the Lie derivatives of the solutions. These decoupled energy estimates for each component of the tensor in a frame, are new and motivated by a problem that we address in a subsequent paper to prove the exterior non-linear stability of the ( 1 + 3 ) (1+3) -Minkowski space-time governed by a general class of perturbations, that includes the non-linearities that arise from the Einstein-Yang-Mills system in the Lorenz gauge as well as other new non-linearities, which have a different non-linear structure than the one treated by Lindblad-Rodnianski, for which their seminal L ∞ L^{\infty} -estimate does not work to the best of our knowledge. The decoupled energy bounds on each component in a frame derived here allow us to replace the celebrated L ∞ L^{\infty} -estimate of Lindblad-Rodnianski in a novel way that permits us to treat these new non-linear structures.

(1.12)

contains terms that involve other components different than
{\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{I}}\Phi_{V}
. We provide a suitable new commutator estimate in (
1.21
). Indeed, unlike the case of the Einstein vacuum equations, and also unlike the case of the Einstein-Maxwell system, in the case of the Einstein-Yang-Mills equations, we need to have a
decoupled
estimate for
each component
of the following commutator term

|{\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{I}}(g^{\lambda\mu}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\lambda}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\mu}A_{e_{a}})-g^{\lambda\mu}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\lambda}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\mu}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{I}}A_{e_{a}})|\;.

We solve the problem by establishing the following suitable estimate.

\displaystyle|g^{\lambda\mu}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\lambda}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\mu}{\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{I}}\Phi_{V}-{\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{I}}(g^{\lambda\mu}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\lambda}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\mu}\Phi_{V})|

(1.13)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{|K|<|I|}|g^{\lambda\mu}\cdot{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\lambda}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\mu}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{V})|

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{(1+t+|q|)}\cdot\sum_{|K|\leq|I|,}\,\,\sum_{|J|+(|K|-1)_{+}\leq|I|}\,\,\,|{\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{J}}H|\,\cdot|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi)|

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\frac{1}{(1+|q|)}}_{\text{bad factor}}\cdot\sum_{|K|\leq|I|,}\,\,\sum_{|J|+(|K|-1)_{+}\leq|I|}\,\,\,|{\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{J}}H_{LL}|\,\cdot\underbrace{\big(\sum_{V^{\prime}\in\cal T}|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{V^{\prime}})|\>\big)}_{\text{decoupled tangential components}}\;,

where
(|K|-1)_{+}=|K|-1
if
|K|\geq 1
and
(|K|-1)_{+}=0
if
|K|=0
.

With this
new
estimate (
1.21
), we notice that the terms with the bad factor
\frac{1}{(1+|q|)}
enter
only
with the tangential components. This is in fact the bad term in the estimate of the commutator term, that is behind an imposed troublesome Grönwall inequality in the proof in
[
29
]
, that needs to be dealt with separately. The estimate used previously in the literature contains all the components for the factor
\frac{1}{(1+|q|)}
, and does
not
allow us to decouple the higher order energy estimates.

Instead, we are able to establish this new estimate (
1.21
) thanks to the following inequality (see (
3
)),

\displaystyle|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}\Psi_{UV}|\lesssim\sum_{|I|\leq 1}\frac{1}{(1+t+|q|)}\cdot|{\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{I}}\Psi|+\sum_{U^{\prime}\in\cal U,V^{\prime}\in\cal T}\sum_{|I|\leq 1}\frac{1}{(1+|q|)}\cdot|{\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{I}}\Psi_{U^{\prime}V^{\prime}}|\;.

(1.14)

Whereas the terms that contain the full components in (
1.21
), have the good factor
\frac{1}{(1+t+|q|)}
.
Hence, we get an estimate on the commutator term of the higher order energy, where term with the weak factor is insensitive to the bad component
A_{{\underline{L}}}
that bothered us (as explained above).

Furthermore, we get a weighted conservation law (see Corollary
2.3.9
) that has also a quantity that controls the tangential derivatives of our unknown solution
{\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{I}}\Phi_{V}
(see Lemma
2.3.11
), thanks to the non-vanishing weight
\widehat{w}^{\prime}
(see Lemma
2.3.6
). In other words, thanks to the non-trivial weight
\widehat{w}
(see Definition
2.3.3
), we have control on the weighted space-time integral of the covariant tangential derivatives of
\Phi_{V}
, namely

\displaystyle\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Sigma^{ext}_{\tau}}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\Big(|({\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{t}+{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{r}){\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{I}}\Phi_{V}|^{2}+\delta^{ij}|({\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{i}-\frac{x_{i}}{r}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{r}){\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{I}}\Phi_{V}|^{2}\Big)\cdot d\tau\cdot\widehat{w}^{\prime}(q)d^{n}x\;.

However, for the energy estimate to be successful, it is of crucial importance that the bounds in the space-time integrand on the right hand side of the inequality are the correct bounds. It is the case in our bounds since we have the following

\displaystyle\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Sigma^{ext}_{\tau}}\overbrace{|H_{LL}|}^{\text{good component}}\cdot\overbrace{|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}{\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{I}}\Phi_{V}|^{2}}^{\text{decoupled bad derivatives}}\cdot d\tau\cdot\widetilde{w}^{\prime}(q)d^{3}x

\displaystyle+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Sigma^{ext}_{\tau}}\overbrace{|H|}^{\text{bad component}}\cdot\overbrace{|{\mbox{$\nabla\mkern-13.0mu/$\,}^{(\bf{m})}}{\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{I}}\Phi_{V}|}^{\text{good derivative}}\cdot\overbrace{|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}{\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{I}}\Phi_{V}|}^{\text{decoupled bad derivative}}\Big)\cdot d\tau\cdot\widetilde{w}^{\prime}(q)d^{3}x\;.

\displaystyle+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Sigma^{ext}_{\tau}}\Big(\overbrace{|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}H_{LL}|}^{\text{good component}}+\overbrace{|{\mbox{$\nabla\mkern-13.0mu/$\,}^{(\bf{m})}}H|}^{\text{good derivative}}\Big)\cdot\overbrace{|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}\Phi_{V}|^{2}}^{\text{decoupled bad derivatives}}\cdot d\tau\cdot\widetilde{w}(q)d^{3}x

\displaystyle+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Sigma^{ext}_{\tau}}\underbrace{|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}H|}_{\text{bad derivative}}\cdot\underbrace{|{\mbox{$\nabla\mkern-13.0mu/$\,}^{(\bf{m})}}{\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{I}}\Phi_{V}|}_{\text{good derivative}}\cdot\underbrace{|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}{\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{I}}\Phi_{V}|}_{\text{decoupled bad derivative}}\Big)\cdot d\tau\cdot\widetilde{w}(q)d^{3}x\;.

Also, in the commutator estimate (

Finally, we would have established the desired decoupled higher order energy estimates.

:

We have

\displaystyle{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\underline{L}}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{L}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{U})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{\underline{L}}\Big({\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{L}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{U})\Big)-{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\underline{L}}L}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{U})-{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{L}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\underline{L}}U})\;.

We have shown in the Appendix of
[
25
]
(by taking in
[
25
]
, a zero Schwarzschild mass, which gives a Minkowski metric), that for all
U\in{{\cal U}}
, we have
{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\underline{L}}U={\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{L}U=0\,.

Thus,

\displaystyle{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\underline{L}}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{L}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{U})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{\underline{L}}\Big(\partial_{L}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{U})\Big)\;.

As a result, we could write,

\displaystyle{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\underline{L}}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{L}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{U})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{L}\Big(\partial_{\underline{L}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{U})\Big)=\partial_{L}\Big({\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\underline{L}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{U})\Big)

(3.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{L}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\underline{L}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{U})\;.

Hence,

\displaystyle\sum_{|J|+|K|\leq|I|,\;|K|<|I|}|({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{J}}H)_{L\underline{L}}\cdot{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\underline{L}}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{L}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{U})|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{|J|+|K|\leq|I|,\;|K|<|I|}|({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{J}}H)_{L\underline{L}}|\cdot|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{L}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\underline{L}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{U})| \;.

The term
{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\underline{L}}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{e_{A}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{U})
:

We look at the region
t\geq 1
or
r\geq 1
and we distinguish the cases
q>0
and
q\leq 0
:

In the case where
q=r-t>0
:

We showed in Lemma
3.0.4
, that
\mbox{${\partial}\mkern-9.0mu/$}_{i}=\frac{x^{j}}{r^{2}}Z_{ij}\,,
and hence, we can write

\displaystyle e_{A}=\frac{1}{r}C^{ij}_{A}Z_{ij}\,,

(3.8)

where
C^{ij}_{A}
are in fact linear combinations of
\frac{x^{j}}{r}
. However,
\partial_{\underline{L}}\frac{x^{j}}{r}=-\frac{x^{i}}{r}(\frac{\delta_{i}^{\;\;j}}{r})+\frac{x^{i}}{r}\frac{x^{j}}{r^{2}}(\partial_{i}r)\;.

We have
\partial_{i}r=\frac{x_{i}}{r}\;.
Thus,

\displaystyle\partial_{r}(\frac{x^{j}}{r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{x^{i}}{r}(\frac{\delta_{i}^{\;\;j}}{r})-\frac{x^{i}}{r}\frac{x^{j}}{r^{2}}(\partial_{i}r)=\frac{x^{i}}{r}(\frac{\delta_{i}^{\;\;j}}{r})-\frac{x^{i}\cdot x_{i}}{r^{2}}\frac{x^{j}}{r^{2}}=0\;.

As a result

\displaystyle\partial_{\underline{L}}\frac{x^{j}}{r}=0\;,

(3.9)

and therefore,
\partial_{\underline{L}}C^{ij}_{A}=0\,.
We also have
\partial_{\underline{L}}\frac{1}{r}=-\partial_{r}(\frac{1}{r})=\frac{1}{r^{2}}\,.
Thus,

\displaystyle\partial_{\underline{L}}(\frac{1}{r}C^{ij}_{A})=\frac{1}{r^{2}}C^{ij}_{A}\;.

(3.10)

Since
{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{e_{A}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{U})=\frac{1}{r}C^{ij}_{A}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{Z_{ij}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{U})\;,
therefore,

\displaystyle{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\underline{L}}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{e_{A}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{U})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{\underline{L}}(\frac{1}{r}C^{ij}_{A})\cdot{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{Z_{ij}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{U})+\frac{1}{r}C^{ij}_{A}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\underline{L}}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{Z_{ij}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{U})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r^{2}}C^{ij}_{A}\cdot{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{Z_{ij}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{U})+\frac{1}{r}C^{ij}_{A}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\underline{L}}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{Z_{ij}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{U})\;.

However,
Z_{ij}=x_{j}\partial_{i}-x_{i}\partial_{j}
, and thus,

\displaystyle{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{Z_{ij}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi)=x_{j}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi)-x_{i}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi)\,.

Therefore,

\displaystyle|\frac{1}{r^{2}}C^{ij}_{A}\cdot{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{Z_{ij}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{U})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\frac{x_{j}}{r^{2}}C^{ij}_{A}|\cdot|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi)|+ |\frac{x_{i}}{r^{2}}C^{ij}_{A}|\cdot|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi)|\;.

Given that
C^{ij}_{A}
and
\frac{x_{i}}{r}
are bounded, we get

\displaystyle|\frac{1}{r^{2}}C^{ij}_{A}\cdot{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{Z_{ij}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{U})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r}\cdot|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi)|+ \frac{1}{r}\cdot|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi)|

(3.11)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r}\cdot|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi)|\;.

Whereas to the term
\frac{1}{r}C^{ij}_{A}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\underline{L}}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{Z_{ij}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{U})
, we first note that since it is a tensor, we have

\displaystyle|\frac{1}{r}C^{ij}_{A}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\underline{L}}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{Z_{ij}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{U})|\lesssim|\frac{1}{r}C^{ij}_{A}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\underline{L}}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{Z_{ij}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi)|\;.

Thus, we can compute, in our fixed system of coordinates, the right hand side of the above inequality in order to make an estimate. First, we compute for
\mu\in\{0,1,2,3\}
,

\displaystyle{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{Z_{ij}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{\mu})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x_{j}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{\mu})-x_{i}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{\mu})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x_{j}\partial_{i}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{\mu})-x_{i}\partial_{j}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{I_{2}}}\Phi_{\mu})=\partial_{Z_{ij}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{\mu})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{Z_{ij}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{\mu})+{\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi([Z_{ij},\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{\mu}}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{Z_{ij}}{\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{\mu}+\sum_{m}{\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{J_{m_{1}}}}{\mathcal{L}}_{[Z_{ij},Z^{m}]}{\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{J_{m_{2}}}}\Phi_{\mu}+{\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi([Z_{ij},\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{\mu}}])\;,

where by
Z^{m}
, we mean the
m
-th Minkowski vector field in the product
Z^{K}
, and the rest in the product is
Z^{J_{m_{1}}}
(what is before
Z^{m}
) and
Z^{J_{m_{2}}}
(what is after
Z^{m}
).

However, the commutation of two vector fields in
\cal Z
is a linear combination of vector fields in
\cal Z
, and the commutation of a vector field in
\cal Z
and of a vector
\partial_{\mu}
,
\mu\in\{t,x^{1},x^{2},x^{3}\}
, gives a linear combination of vectors of the form
\partial_{\mu}
. Using that fact, we get that for all
\mu\in(t,x^{1},x^{2},x^{3})
,

\displaystyle{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{Z_{ij}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{\mu})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}a_{i}{\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{I_{i}}}\Phi_{\mu}+\sum_{i}b_{i}\cdot{\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{\mu^{i}}\;,

for some
\mu^{i}\in\{t,x^{1},x^{2},x^{3}\}
, and
a_{i}\,,\,b_{i}
are constants, and
|I_{i}|\leq|K|+1
. Thus,

\displaystyle{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\underline{L}}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{Z_{ij}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{\mu})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}a_{i}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\underline{L}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{I_{i}}}\Phi_{\mu})+\sum_{i}b_{i}\cdot{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\underline{L}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{\mu^{i}})

and therefore, for all
\mu\in\{t,x^{1},x^{2},x^{3}\}
,

\displaystyle|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\underline{L}}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{Z_{ij}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{\mu})|\lesssim\sum_{|M|\leq|K|+1}|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\underline{L}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{M}}\Phi)|\;,

and as a result

\displaystyle|\frac{1}{r}C^{ij}_{A}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\underline{L}}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{Z_{ij}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{U})|\lesssim\sum_{|M|\leq|K|+1}\frac{1}{r}\cdot|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{M}}\Phi)|\;.

Finally, we obtain for
q>0
,

\displaystyle|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\underline{L}}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{e_{A}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{U})|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|\frac{1}{r^{2}}C^{ij}_{A}\cdot{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{Z_{ij}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{U})|+|\frac{1}{r}C^{ij}_{A}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\underline{L}}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{Z_{ij}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{U})|

(3.12)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{|M|\leq|K|+1}\frac{1}{r}\cdot|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{M}}\Phi)|\;.

Since we look in the region
t\geq 1
or
r\geq 1
and we are in the case where
q>0
, this imposes that
r\geq 1
and therefore, in that region, we have
\frac{1}{r}\lesssim\frac{1}{1+r}\;,
and as we have shown, for
q\geq 0
, we have
\frac{1}{1+r}=\frac{1}{1+t+|q|}\;.
Consequently,

\displaystyle|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\underline{L}}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{e_{A}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{U})|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{|M|\leq|K|+1}\frac{1}{1+r}\cdot|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{M}}\Phi)|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1+t+|q|)}\cdot\sum_{|M|\leq|K|+1}|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{M}}\Phi)|\;.

Thus, in the region
t\geq 1
or
r\geq 1
, for
q>0
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{|J|+|K|\leq|I|,\;|K|<|I|}|({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{J}}H)_{Le_{A}}\cdot{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\underline{L}}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{e_{A}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{U})|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{|J|+|K|\leq|I|,\;|K|<|I|}\frac{1}{(1+t+|q|)}\cdot|({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{J}}H)_{Le_{A}}|\cdot| \sum_{|M|\leq|K|+1}|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{M}}\Phi)|\;.

In the case where
q=r-t<0
:

We then recall that we showed in Lemma
3.0.4
, that we have also a different presentation for
\mbox{${\partial}\mkern-9.0mu/$}_{i}
, which is more suitable to make estimates in the region
q<0
, that is
\mbox{${\partial}\mkern-9.0mu/$}_{i}=\frac{-\frac{x_{i}}{r}\frac{x^{j}}{r}Z_{0j}+Z_{0i}}{t}\;,
and therefore, we can see
e_{A}
as linear combinations of the form

\displaystyle e_{A}=\frac{1}{t}G_{A}^{j}Z_{0j}+\frac{1}{t}B_{A}^{i}Z_{0i}\,,

(3.14)

where
G_{A}^{j}
is a linear combination of
\frac{x_{i}}{r}\frac{x^{j}}{r}
and
B_{A}^{i}
are constants. Based on what we have shown in (
3.9
), we have then
\partial_{\underline{L}}G_{A}^{j}=\partial_{\underline{L}}B_{A}^{j}=0\,.
Thus, we can write

\displaystyle e_{A}=\frac{1}{t}C_{A}^{j}Z_{0j}\,,

(3.15)

with
C_{A}^{j}
bounded and furthermore,
\partial_{\underline{L}}C_{A}^{j}=0\,.
We also have
\partial_{\underline{L}}\frac{1}{t}=\partial_{t}(\frac{1}{t})=-\frac{1}{t^{2}}\,.
Thus,

\displaystyle\partial_{\underline{L}}(\frac{1}{t}C_{A}^{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{t^{2}}C^{j}_{A}\;.

Since

\displaystyle{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{e_{A}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{U})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}C_{A}^{j}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{Z_{0j}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{U})\;,

therefore,

\displaystyle{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\underline{L}}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{e_{A}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{U})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{\underline{L}}(\frac{1}{t}C_{A}^{j})\cdot{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{Z_{0j}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{U})+\frac{1}{t}C_{A}^{j}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\underline{L}}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{Z_{0j}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{U})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{t^{2}}C_{A}^{j}\cdot{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{Z_{0j}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{U})+\frac{1}{t}C_{A}^{j}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\underline{L}}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{Z_{0j}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{U})\;.

However,
Z_{0j}=x_{j}\partial_{t}+t\partial_{j}
, and thus,

\displaystyle{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{Z_{0j}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi)=x_{j}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial t}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi)+t{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi)\,.

Therefore, on one hand

\displaystyle|\frac{1}{t^{2}}C^{j}_{A}\cdot{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{Z_{0j}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{U})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\frac{x_{j}}{t^{2}}C^{j}_{A}|\cdot|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial t}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi)|+ |\frac{t}{t^{2}}C^{j}_{A}|\cdot|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi)|\;.

Now, we recall that we are in the region
q=r-t<0
and therefore in that region,
\frac{r}{t}\leq 1\,,
and therefore, since also
C^{j}_{A}
is bounded, we get

\displaystyle|\frac{1}{t^{2}}C^{j}_{A}\cdot{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{Z_{0j}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{U})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}\cdot|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial t}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi)|+ \frac{1}{t}\cdot|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi)|

(3.16)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}\cdot|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi)|\;.

On the other hand, for the other term in (
3
), proceeding as earlier, we obtain

\displaystyle|\frac{1}{t}C_{A}^{j}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\underline{L}}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{Z_{0j}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{U})|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|\frac{1}{t}C_{A}^{j}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\underline{L}}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{Z_{0j}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi)|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{|M|\leq|K|+1}\frac{1}{t}\cdot|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{M}}\Phi)|\;.

Finally, we obtain for
q<0
,

\displaystyle|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\underline{L}}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{e_{A}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{U})|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{|M|\leq|K|+1}\frac{1}{t}\cdot|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{M}}\Phi)|\;.

Since we look in the region
t\geq 1
or
r\geq 1
and we are in the case where
q<0
, this imposes that
\frac{1}{t}\lesssim\frac{1}{1+t}\lesssim\frac{1}{1+t+|q|}\;,
and hence

\displaystyle|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\underline{L}}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{e_{A}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{U})|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1+t+|q|)}\cdot\sum_{|M|\leq|K|+1}|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{M}}\Phi)|\;.

Thus, in the region
t\geq 1
or
r\geq 1
, for
q<0
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{|J|+|K|\leq|I|,\;|K|<|I|}|({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{J}}H)_{Le_{A}}\cdot{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\underline{L}}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{e_{A}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{U})|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{|J|+|K|\leq|I|,\;|K|<|I|}\frac{1}{(1+t+|q|)}\cdot|({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{J}}H)_{Le_{A}}|\cdot| \sum_{|M|\leq|K|+1}|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{M}}\Phi)|\;.

Finally, putting all together and noting that the calculations are also true for a two-tensor, we obtain the result.

Lemma 3.0.6
.

We have the following well-known inequality for all
t\geq 0
and for all
q\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

\displaystyle(1+t+|q|)\cdot|{\mbox{$\nabla\mkern-13.0mu/$\,}^{(\bf{m})}}{\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{I}}\Phi|\lesssim\sum_{|J|\leq|I|+1}|{\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{J}}\Phi|\,.

Now, we are ready to prove our decoupled estimate on the commutator term.

Lemma 3.0.7
.

For any
V\in\cal T
, we have the decoupled commutator estimate in (
1.21
).

Proof.

In the result of Lemma
3.0.5
, we estimate

\displaystyle|({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{J}}H)_{L\underline{L}}|\cdot|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{L}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\underline{L}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{U})|

\displaystyle+|m^{\mu{\beta}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{J}}H)_{\underline{L}{\beta}}\cdot{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{L}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\mu}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{U})|+|m^{\mu{\beta}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{J}}H)_{e_{A}{\beta}}\cdot{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{e_{A}}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\mu}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{U})|\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{J}}H|\cdot|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{L}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi)| +|{\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{J}}H|\cdot|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{L}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi)|

\displaystyle+|{\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{J}}H|\cdot|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{e_{A}}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi)|\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{J}}H|\cdot|{\mbox{$\nabla\mkern-13.0mu/$\,}^{(\bf{m})}}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi)|\,.

In our fixed system of coordinates, for a given
\mu\,,\nu\in\{t,x^{1},x^{2},x^{3}\}
, we can look at each component and estimate, as we have shown in Lemma
3.0.6
, that

\displaystyle|{\mbox{$\nabla\mkern-13.0mu/$\,}^{(\bf{m})}}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\mu}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{\nu})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\mbox{${\partial}\mkern-9.0mu/$}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\mu}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{\nu})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1+t+|q|)}\cdot\sum_{|I|=1}|Z^{I}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\mu}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{\nu})|\,.

Using the fact that commutation of a Minkowski vector field
Z
and a coordinate vector field
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{\mu}}
, gives a linear combination of coordinates vector fields, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{|I|=1}|Z^{I}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\mu}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{\nu})|\lesssim\sum_{|I|\leq 1}|{\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{I}}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi)|\;.

Summing over all indices
\mu\,,\nu\in\{t,x^{1},x^{2},x^{3}\}
, we obtain

\displaystyle|{\mbox{$\nabla\mkern-13.0mu/$\,}^{(\bf{m})}}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi)|\lesssim\frac{1}{(1+t+|q|)}\cdot\sum_{|I|\leq 1}|{\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{I}}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi)|\;.

As a result, using the fact that the Lie derivative
{\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{I}}
commutes with
{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}
, we get

\displaystyle|({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{J}}H)_{L\underline{L}}|\cdot|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{L}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\underline{L}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{U})|

\displaystyle+|m^{\mu{\beta}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{J}}H)_{\underline{L}{\beta}}\cdot{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{L}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\mu}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{U})|+|m^{\mu{\beta}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{J}}H)_{e_{A}{\beta}}\cdot{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{e_{A}}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\mu}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{U})|\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{J}}H|\cdot\frac{1}{(1+t+|q|)}\cdot\sum_{|M|\leq|K|+1}|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{M}}\Phi)|\;.

Consequently, for any
V\in{\cal T}
,

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{I}}(g^{\lambda\mu}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\lambda}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\mu}\Phi_{V})-g^{\lambda\mu}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\lambda}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\mu}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{I}}\Phi_{V})|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{|K|<|I|}|g^{\lambda\mu}\cdot{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\lambda}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\mu}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{V})|

\displaystyle+\sum_{|J|+|K|\leq|I|,\;|K|<|I|}\Big(|({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{J}}H)_{LL}|\cdot\frac{1}{(1+t+|q|)}\cdot\sum_{|M|\leq|K|+1}|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{M}}\Phi)|

\displaystyle+|({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{J}}H)_{LL}|\cdot\frac{1}{(1+|q|)}\cdot\sum_{|M|\leq|K|+1}\sum_{V^{\prime}\in{\cal T}}|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{M}}\Phi_{V^{\prime}})|

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{(1+t+|q|)}\cdot|({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{J}}H)_{Le_{A}}|\cdot| \sum_{|M|\leq|K|+1}|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{M}}\Phi)|

\displaystyle+|{\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{J}}H|\cdot\frac{1}{(1+t+|q|)}\cdot\sum_{|M|\leq|K|+1}|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{M}}\Phi)|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{|K|<|I|}|g^{\lambda\mu}\cdot{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\lambda}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\mu}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi_{V})|

\displaystyle+\sum_{|J|+|K|\leq|I|,\;|K|<|I|}\Big(|{\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{J}}H|\cdot\frac{1}{(1+t+|q|)}\cdot\sum_{|M|\leq|K|+1}|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{M}}\Phi)|

\displaystyle+|({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{J}}H)_{LL}|\cdot\frac{1}{(1+|q|)}\cdot\sum_{|M|\leq|K|+1}\sum_{V^{\prime}\in{\cal T}}|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{M}}\Phi_{V^{\prime}})|\Big)\;.

Thus, we get the result.
