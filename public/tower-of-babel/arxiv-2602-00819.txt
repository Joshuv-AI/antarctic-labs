Spin alignment, tensor polarizabilities, and local equilibrium for spin-1 particles
Wojciech Florkowski, Sudip Kumar Kar, Valeriya Mykhaylova

Abstract
Different bases for the spin-1 density matrix are discussed to clarify the connection between its components and observables measured in heavy-ion collisions. The theoretical advantage of using the adjoint representation for spin matrices is emphasized. Next, the equilibrium spin density matrix and the corresponding Wigner function are introduced. With appropriate definitions of the energy-momentum and spin tensors, this framework allows for the formulation of perfect spin hydrodynamics in the same way as previously done for spin- 1 / 2 \nicefrac{{1}}{{2}} particles. Together, these results provide a unified description of spin- 1 / 2 \nicefrac{{1}}{{2}} and spin-1 particles.

The briefly sketched landscape of spin physics above highlights the importance of a deeper investigation of the relationships between different observables, as well as between theory and experiment. In this paper, we study fundamental relations among spin observables, aiming to clarify several issues discussed in recent literature.

We start our discussion with the spin-1 density matrix
\rho

\rho

{\cal P}^{\mu}
, while the symmetric part (after subtracting the Kronecker delta contribution) is given by the tensor polarizabilities
{\cal T}^{\mu\nu}

\epsilon^{\mu}
which are reduced to unit real vectors in the particle rest frame (PRF). The adjoint representation proves particularly useful in calculating the Wigner function for a relativistic gas of spin-1 particles, which can then be employed to obtain the energy-momentum and spin tensors.

The central part of this work is devoted to the analysis of
local equilibrium
. We argue that it can be defined analogously to the concept introduced for spin-
\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}
particles, i.e., as a state in which the spin part of the total angular momentum is conserved separately. The adopted form of the local-equilibrium spin density matrix uniquely determines the spin polarization vector and the tensor polarizabilities. This result is particularly important, since it allows one to establish a connection between the hyperon and vector-meson measurements. Previously, such a connection was found in the coalescence model
[

\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}

Finally, we turn to the discussion of tensor polarizabilities. Since they are measured in the basis in which the operator

Local equilibrium spin density

We have already introduced the concept of thermalization of particle momenta with the help of the function

\displaystyle\rho^{S}_{\rm eq}=\frac{\exp[{\bm{\alpha}}\cdot\bm{S}]}{{\rm tr_{3}}(\exp[{\bm{\alpha}}\cdot\bm{S}])}={\cal Z}^{-1}\exp[{\bm{\alpha}}\cdot\bm{S}],

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{P}}_{*}=\frac{2\sinh(\alpha)}{2\cosh\alpha+1}\,\hat{{\bm{\alpha}}},

Equation (
67
) implies that the energy-momentum tensor and the spin tensor can be obtained by differentiating the particle current
\mathcal{N}^{\mu}
with respect to the appropriate Lagrange multipliers, namely

\displaystyle T^{\mu\lambda}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\partial\mathcal{N}^{\mu}}{\partial\beta_{\lambda}},\quad S^{\mu,\rho\sigma}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{N}^{\mu}}{\partial\omega_{\rho\sigma}}.

(68)

With the conservation laws

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}T^{\mu\lambda}

\displaystyle=0,\quad\partial_{\mu}S^{\mu,\rho\sigma}=0,

(69)

our description agrees with the structure of the divergence-type theory
[
50
,
51
]
. The form of Eq. (
67
) also suggests that one can introduce the entropy current defined by the expression

\displaystyle S^{\mu}

\displaystyle=T^{\mu\lambda}\beta_{\lambda}-\frac{1}{2}S^{\mu,\rho\sigma}\omega_{\rho\sigma}+\mathcal{N}^{\mu}.

(70)

Equations (
67
) and (
70
) lead to the differential of the entropy current

\displaystyle dS^{\mu}

\displaystyle=\beta_{\lambda}dT^{\mu\lambda}-\frac{1}{2}\omega_{\rho\sigma}dS^{\mu,\rho\sigma}.

(71)

This expression indicates that the entropy is conserved as a direct consequence of the conservation of the energy, linear momentum, and spin. Following our previous studies, we interpret this case as the state of
local thermodynamic equilibrium
for spin-carrying particles. The dynamics of such a state is described by
perfect-fluid spin hydrodynamics
.

7.2
Pauli-Lubański four-vector

Following earlier studies
[
44
]
, we define the phase space density of the Pauli-Lubański four-vector by the formula

\displaystyle E_{p}\frac{d\Delta\Pi_{\mu}(x,p)}{d^{3}p}=-\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\Delta\Sigma_{\lambda}(x)E_{p}\frac{dJ^{\lambda,\nu\alpha}(x,p)}{d^{3}p}\frac{p^{\beta}}{m}.

(72)

The quantity
\Delta\Sigma_{\lambda}
represents a volume element over which particles with momentum
p
are considered. After integration over momentum and space we obtain the total conserved charges, for example

\displaystyle J^{\nu\alpha}_{\rm tot}=\int d\Sigma_{\lambda}(x)\int dP\,J^{\lambda,\nu\alpha}(x,p).

(73)

Splitting the angular momentum tensor into orbital and spin parts,
J^{\lambda,\nu\alpha}(x,p)=L^{\lambda,\nu\alpha}(x,p)+S^{\lambda,\nu\alpha}(x,p)
, we find that only the spin part contributes to (
72
) – the orbital part includes terms proportional to
p^{\nu}
and
p^{\alpha}
, which vanish upon contraction with the Levi-Civita symbol in (
72
). Thus, we arrive at the formula

\displaystyle E_{p}\frac{d\Delta\Pi_{\mu}}{d^{3}p}=-\frac{1}{2m^{2}}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\frac{\Delta\Sigma_{\lambda}(x)}{(2\pi)^{3}}p^{\lambda}\mathcal{Z}{f}_{0}(x,p)\epsilon^{\nu\alpha\rho\sigma}{\cal P}_{\rho}p_{\sigma}p^{\beta}.

(74)

Similarly, we obtain the number of particles in the volume
\Delta\Sigma_{\lambda}(x)
,

\displaystyle E_{p}\frac{d\Delta N}{d^{3}p}

\displaystyle=E_{p}\Delta\Sigma_{\lambda}\frac{d\mathcal{N}^{\lambda}}{d^{3}p}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\Delta\Sigma_{\lambda}p^{\lambda}\mathcal{Z}{f}_{0}(x,p).

(75)

This allows us to find the polarization per particle. Calculating the ratio of Eq. (
74
) and Eq. (
75
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d\Delta\Pi_{\mu}}{d\Delta N}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2m^{2}}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\epsilon^{\nu\alpha\rho\sigma}{\cal P}_{\rho}p_{\sigma}p^{\beta}={\cal P}_{\mu}-(\mathcal{P}\cdot p)p_{\mu}.

(76)

Since
\mathcal{P}\cdot p=0
, see Eq. (
38
), we find

\displaystyle\frac{d\Delta\Pi_{\mu}}{d\Delta N}={\cal P}_{\mu}.

(77)

This form supports our expression that relates the Wigner function to the spin density matrix (
46
).

It is interesting to compare our results with the expressions obtained before for spin-
\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}
. The formula derived in
[
48
]
gives

\displaystyle\frac{d\Delta\Pi_{\mu}^{(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})}}{d\Delta N}=\frac{1}{2}\tanh\sqrt{-a^{2}}\,\frac{a_{\mu}}{\sqrt{-a^{2}}},

(78)

which has the following limits for
\sqrt{-a^{2}}\ll 1
and
\sqrt{-a^{2}}\gg 1
, respectively:

\displaystyle\frac{d\Delta\Pi_{\mu}^{(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})}}{d\Delta N}=\frac{1}{2}\,a_{\mu},\qquad\frac{d\Delta\Pi_{\mu}^{(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})}}{d\Delta N}=\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{a_{\mu}}{\sqrt{-a^{2}}}.

(79)

Combining (
50
) and (
64
) we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d\Delta\Pi_{\mu}}{d\Delta N}=\frac{2\sinh(2 \sqrt{-a^2})}{2\cosh(2 \sqrt{-a^2})+1}\,\frac{a_{\mu}}{\sqrt{-a^{2}}},

(80)

with the corresponding limits (again for
\sqrt{-a^{2}}\ll 1
and
\sqrt{-a^{2}}\gg 1
):

\displaystyle\frac{d\Delta\Pi_{\mu}}{d\Delta N}=\frac{4}{3}a_{\mu},\qquad\frac{d\Delta\Pi_{\mu}}{d\Delta N}=\frac{a_{\mu}}{\sqrt{-a^{2}}}.

(81)

It is instructive to see that for
\sqrt{-a^{2}}\gg 1
, the quantity
d\Delta\Pi_{\mu}/d\Delta N
is bounded by 1/2 and 1, respectively, for spin 1/2 and 1. This indicates the correct normalization of our spin densities.

8
T representation and alignment measurements

Let us turn to the discussion of the T representation, which is used in measurements of the spin alignment. The
T^{i}
matrices are explicitly given by

\displaystyle T_{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&-1&0\\
1&0&-1\\
0&1&0\\
\end{array}\right),\quad T_{y}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&-1\\
\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912ptT_{z}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&1&0\\
1&0&1\\
0&1&0\\
\end{array}\right).

The unitary matrices that connect the J and S representations with the T representation are:

\displaystyle U_{JT}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}-\frac{1}{2}&-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}&-\frac{1}{2}\\[8.5359pt]
-\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}&0&\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\\[8.5359pt]
\frac{1}{2}&-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}&\frac{1}{2}\\[8.5359pt]
\end{array}\right),\quad U_{TS}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}&0&\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\\[8.5359pt]
0&-i&0\\[8.5359pt]
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}&0&-\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\\[8.5359pt]
\end{array}\right).

From this we can obtain the spin density matrix in the T representation. Since
\rho_{rs}^{T}
is Hermitian, its elements read:

\displaystyle\rho_{11}^{T}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{6}\left(2+3\mathcal{P}_{*}^{2}\!+\!\sqrt{6}\mathcal{T}_{*}^{22}\right),

(97)

\displaystyle\rho_{21}^{T}=\bar{\rho}_{12}^{T}

\displaystyle=\frac{3i{\mathcal{P}_{*}^{1}}+3{\mathcal{P}_{*}^{3}}+2\sqrt{6}({\mathcal{T}_{*}^{23}}+i{\mathcal{T}_{*}^{12}})}{6\sqrt{2}},

(98)

\displaystyle\rho_{13}^{T}=\bar{\rho}_{31}^{T}

\displaystyle=-\frac{{2{\mathcal{T}_{*}^{11}}+2i{\mathcal{T}_{*}^{13}}+{\mathcal{T}_{*}^{22}}}}{\sqrt{6}},

(99)

\displaystyle\rho_{22}^{T}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}-\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\mathcal{T}_{*}^{22},

(100)

\displaystyle\rho_{23}^{T}=\bar{\rho}_{32}^{T}

\displaystyle=\frac{-3i{\mathcal{P}_{*}^{1}}+3{\mathcal{P}_{*}^{3}}-2\sqrt{6}({\mathcal{T}_{*}^{23}}-i{\mathcal{T}_{*}^{12}})}{6\sqrt{2}},

(101)

\displaystyle\rho_{33}^{T}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{6}\left(2-3{\mathcal{P}_{*}^{2}}+\sqrt{6}{\mathcal{T}_{*}^{22}}\right),

(102)

where the overbar indicates complex conjugation.

In the spin literature, it is common to label the elements of the spin density matrix
\rho^{T}
with indices
-1,0,1
instead of
1,2,3
(to underline the connection between the matrix elements and the spin quantum numbers). Therefore, in this section, we adopt the traditional indexing scheme (e.g.,
\rho_{0-1}=\rho^{T}_{12}
,
\rho_{00}=\rho^{T}_{22}
,
\rho_{10}=\rho^{T}_{32}
,
\rho_{1-1}=\rho^{T}_{31}
, etc.) when presenting expressions for experimentally studied observables.

Of particular importance is the central element of the spin density matrix, which defines the alignment,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=\rho_{00}-\frac{1}{3}=-\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\mathcal{T}_{*}^{22}.

(103)

We observe that
\rho_{00}
is determined by
\mathcal{T}_{*}^{22}
, although it is measured in the
T
representation. In this respect, our approach differs from
[
21
]
, where the alignment is expressed by
\mathcal{T}_{*}^{33}
. Furthermore, in contrast to
[
21
]
, our analysis indicates that a non-trivial alignment can occur in local equilibrium – it does not require dissipation. Using the expression

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{22}_{*}=\frac{\left(3{{\hat{\alpha}}}_{2}^{2}\ -1\right)\left(\cosh\alpha-1\right)}{\sqrt{6}\,(2\cosh\alpha+1)},

(104)

we find that the alignment is given by the formula

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=-\frac{\left(3{{\hat{\alpha}}}_{2}^{2}\ -1\right)\left(\cosh\alpha-1\right)}{3\,(2\cosh\alpha+1)}.

(105)

For small values of
\alpha
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\approx-\frac{1}{18}\left(3{{\hat{b}}_{2\,*}}^{2}-1\right)|{\bm{b}}_{*}|^{2}.

(106)

where we have used the fact that
{\bm{\alpha}}=2{\bm{a}}_{*}=-{\bm{b}}_{*}
.
Other experimentally accessible observables include
[
18
]
:

\displaystyle-\sqrt{2}[{\rm Re}(\rho_{10})-{\rm Re}(\rho_{0-1})]

\displaystyle=\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}\mathcal{T}_{*}^{23},

(107)

\displaystyle\sqrt{2}[{\rm Im}(\rho_{10})-{\rm Im}(\rho_{0-1})]

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}\mathcal{T}_{*}^{12},

(108)

\displaystyle-\sqrt{2}{\rm Re}(\rho_{1-1})

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{T}_{*}^{11}-\mathcal{T}_{*}^{33}}{\sqrt{3}},

(109)

\displaystyle\sqrt{2}{\rm Im}(\rho_{1-1})

\displaystyle=\frac{2\mathcal{T}_{*}^{13}}{\sqrt{3}}.

(110)

We observe that in all these cases, the measured quantities can be expressed by the tensor polarizabilities rather than the spin polarization vector.

We conclude this section by the remark that knowledge of the spin polarization tensor
\omega^{\mu\nu}
on the freeze-out hypersurface
\Sigma^{\lambda}
allows us to obtain the alignment for particles with a given momentum,

{\cal A}(p)=\frac{\int d\Sigma_{\lambda}p^{\lambda}\mathcal{Z}{f}_{0}(x,p){\cal A}(x,p)}{\int d\Sigma_{\lambda}p^{\lambda}\mathcal{Z}{f}_{0}(x,p)}.

(111)

9
Summary

In this work, we have studied several issues related to the description of spin-1 particles in heavy-ion collisions. We have demonstrated that the adjoint representation is particularly convenient for analyzing spin observables and calculating the energy-momentum and spin tensors. The local equilibrium spin density matrix was introduced and its advantages emphasized: it leads to thermodynamically consistent relations and allows to construct a unified description of spin-1 and spin-
\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}
particles. The last property may soon allow a direct check of wether the spin polarization data for hyperons and vector-mesons are driven by the same physical mechanism. We have also shown that the KG definition of the spin tensor is consistent with the definition of the Pauli-Lubański four-vector, and that the obtained equations of spin hydrodynamics have the structure of a divergence-type theory.
