 Selective Deposition on 3D Ceramics for Electronics
 Ryan M. Hensleigh
 25-LW-038

Project Overview
Ceramic electronics are ubiquitous in aerospace and environments that require performance
across large temperature, vibrational, and other stress ranges. Their manufacturing, however,
has inherently limited them to bulky, blocks of metal and ceramic. Future needs in 6G and
security will continue to push towards more streamlined, lighter weight, and more flexible
designs of electronics, but there has been no innovation in ceramic/metal electronic
manufacturing, stymieing this new generation of devices.
In this project we developed a new light-based printing method that can cure thick (>1 mm)
well defined beams in conjunction with traditional layer-by-layer processing. This significantly
reducing print time and material waste during multi-material vat based printing. We also
demonstrated it is possible to use the difference in ceramic surface chemistries, to do selective
electroless metal deposition on pre-fired ceramic.
These are significant developments for 3D printing, demonstrating a new semi-volumetric
method that has benefits of fast printing but also well defined feature fidelity. This is also a new
method to manufacture ceramic/metal composites and can open up new device fabrication
modalities.

Mission Impact
This project has continued to build on LLNL’s excellence and expertise in additive
manufacturing. It has stood up a new multi-material AM capability that was not previously
available. One intern was trained and converted to staff during this project. We built upon the
collaboration between the UC schools and LLNL by our collaboration with University of
California Los Angeles. This project has enhanced our understanding of manufacturing
electronics for extreme environments which is critical for a variety of lab missions and national
security.
