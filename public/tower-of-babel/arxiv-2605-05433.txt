Superconductivity mediated by nematic fluctuations – the dispersion of collective modes
Kazi Ranjibul Islam, Andrey Chubukov*

Abstract
We analyze the spectrum of collective modes in a superconductor, in which pairing is mediated by long-range nematic fluctuations. Previous experimental and theoretical studies have found that a superconducting gap in such a system is highly anisotropic and at any finite T < T c T<T_{c} vanishes on four arcs of the Fermi surface even when the pairing symmetry is s − s- wave ( s + ⁣ − s^{+-} between hole and electron pockets). We derive the expression for the pair susceptibility χ ⁡ ( 𝐪 , Ω ) \chi(\mathbf{q},\Omega) at finite momentum 𝐪 \mathbf{q} and frequency Ω \Omega deep in the superconducting phase. We analyze the spectral function, Im ​ χ ​ ( 𝐪 , Ω ) \text{Im}\chi(\mathbf{q},\Omega) , and its pole structure in transverse (phase) and longitudinal (amplitude) channels and compare the results with those of a conventional s s -wave superconductor. We find that the analytic structure of the pair susceptibility in both channels is qualitatively distinct from that in a BCS superconductor. This gives rise to highly unconventional dispersion of phase and amplitude collective modes.

II
Model and Superconducting Gap Structure

We consider an effective one band model of 2D electrons with momentum-dependent short-range interaction in the Cooper channel.
The corresponding Hamiltonian is

\displaystyle H=\sum_{\mathbf{k},\sigma}\xi(\mathbf{k})c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k},\sigma}c_{\mathbf{k},\sigma}+\sum_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}}V(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{p})c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k},\uparrow}c^{\dagger}_{-\mathbf{k},\downarrow}c_{-\mathbf{p},\downarrow}c_{\mathbf{p},\uparrow},

(2)

where
c_{\mathbf{k},\sigma}
is a fermion annihilation operator at momentum
\mathbf{k}
and spin
\sigma=\uparrow,\downarrow
.
With FeSe in mind, we approximate the fermionic dispersion
\xi_{\mathbf{k}}
by isotropic and parabolic
\xi_{k}=\mathbf{k}^{2}/2m-\mu

\mu

fermions on the Fermi surface

is the angle between the direction of momentum
\mathbf{k}_{F}(\mathbf{p}_{F})
and
k_{x}
axis. We will use
\Lambda
for the ultra-violet cutoff for the theory.

The pairing interaction mediated by nematic fluctuations is attractive and generally has the form

V(\theta_{\mathbf{k}},\theta_{\mathbf{p}})=-f^{2}(\theta_{k},\theta_{p})\chi_{nem}(\theta_{k}-\theta_{p})
, where
\chi_{mem}
is the nematic susceptibility, and

is the nematic form factor. The form of

depends on the microscopic model and the origin of the nematic order. In many Fe-based superconductors, nematic transition occurs only a few Kelvin above a magnetic transition into a stripe-magnetic phase with momenta
(\pi,0)
or
(0,\pi)

and

For this case,

does not have strong angle-dependence and can be reasonably well approximated by a constant. As we said above, in doped FeSe magnetic and nematic transitions are well separated. We follow earlier works
Lederer et al. 2015
;
Schattner et al. 2016

;

\displaystyle V(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{p})\equiv V(\theta_{\mathbf{k}},\theta_{\mathbf{p}})=-V_{0}\cos^{2}{2\theta_{\mathbf{k}}}\delta(\theta_{\mathbf{k}}-\theta_{\mathbf{p}})

\displaystyle d\xi_{\mathbf{k}}\left(1+\dfrac{\xi_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}/2}\xi_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}/2}\pm\Delta^{2}(\theta_{\mathbf{k}})}{E_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}/2}E_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}/2}}\right)\times

\displaystyle\left[\dfrac{1}{E_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}/2}+E_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}/2}-i\,\Omega_{m}}+\dfrac{1}{E_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}/2}+E_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}/2}+i\,\Omega_{m}}\right],

\displaystyle\chi_{T}(q)=\dfrac{N_{0}}{2}\int\dfrac{d\theta_{\mathbf{k}}}{2\pi}\dfrac{\Pi_{T}(\theta_{\mathbf{k}},q)}{1-g\cos^{2}2\theta_{\mathbf{k}}\Pi_{T}(\theta_{\mathbf{k}},q)},\quad\chi_{L}(q)=\dfrac{N_{0}}{2}\int\dfrac{d\theta_{\mathbf{k}}}{2\pi}\dfrac{\Pi_{L}(\theta_{\mathbf{k}},q)}{1-g\cos^{2}2\theta_{\mathbf{k}}\Pi_{L}(\theta_{\mathbf{k}},q)},

III.3
Analytical results

Discontinuities and divergences of
\text{Im}\chi_{L}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)
displayed in

can be understood analytically by analyzing Eq. (
20
) and (
26
). We consider the cases
\mathbf{q}=0
and finite
\mathbf{q}
separately.

III.3.0.1
\mathbf{q}=0:

At zero momentum, the longitudinal polarization bubble
\delta\Pi_{L}(\theta_{k},0,\Omega)
still depends on
\theta_{k}
due to angle-dependence of the gap function. We find

\displaystyle\delta\Pi_{L}(\theta_{\mathbf{k}},0,\Omega)=-\dfrac{\sqrt{4\Delta^{2}(\theta_{\mathbf{k}})-(\Omega+i\delta)^{2}}}{\Omega}\sec^{-1}\left(\dfrac{2\Delta(\theta_{\mathbf{k}})}{\sqrt{4\Delta^{2}(\theta_{\mathbf{k}})-(\Omega+i\delta)^{2}}}\right),

(29)

where
\Delta(\theta_{\mathbf{k}})=\Delta_{0}\exp\{-\tan^{2}(2\theta_{\mathbf{k}})/g\}
. At low frequencies, the dominant contribution to
\delta\Pi_{L}
arises from the nodal regions where the gap is exponentially small. Expanding near a representative nodal point
\theta=\pi/4
as
\theta_{\mathbf{k}}=\pi/4+\delta\theta_{\mathbf{k}}
, and approximating
\Delta(\theta_{\mathbf{k}})\approx\Delta_{0}\exp\{-1/(4g\,\delta\theta_{\mathbf{k}}^{2})\}
,
\sqrt{4\Delta_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}-\Omega^{2}}\approx-i\sqrt{2\Omega}\,\sqrt{\Omega-2\Delta(\theta_{\mathbf{k}})}
, we find

\displaystyle\delta\Pi_{L}(\delta\theta_{\mathbf{k}},0,\Omega)\approx\log{\left(\dfrac{\Delta(\theta_\bk)}{\Omega}\right)}+i\dfrac{\pi}{2}=-\dfrac{1}{4\,g\,\delta\theta^{2}_{\mathbf{k}}}+\log\left(\dfrac{\Delta_{0}}{\Omega}\right)+i\dfrac{\pi}{2}

(30)

substituting this expression into Eq. (
20
) for
\chi_{L}(0,\Omega)
, approximating
\cos^{2}(2\theta_{\mathbf{k}})\approx 4\delta\theta_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}
, and evaluating the integral over
\delta\theta_{\mathbf{k}}
, we
obtain

\displaystyle\chi_{L}(0,\Omega)

\displaystyle\propto\int_{0}^{\delta\theta_{0}}d\delta\theta_{\mathbf{k}}\dfrac{1}{4g\delta\theta^{2}_{\mathbf{k}}}\left(\dfrac{1}{4g\delta\theta^{2}_{\mathbf{k}}\,\delta\Pi_{L}(\theta_{\mathbf{k}},0,\Omega)}+1\right)=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{4g}}\left(\sqrt{\log\dfrac{\Delta_{0}}{\Omega}}+i\dfrac{\pi}{2\sqrt{\log\dfrac{\Delta_{0}}{\Omega}}}\right).

(31)

This explains
(\log\dfrac{\Delta_{0}}{\Omega})^{1/2}
behavior of Re
\chi_{L}(0,\Omega)
and

1/({\log\dfrac{\Delta_{0}}{\Omega}})^{1/2}
behavior of Im
\chi_{L}(0,\Omega)
.

Near
\Omega=2\Delta_{0}
, we introduce

\Omega=2\Delta_{0}(1+\epsilon)
and evaluate
\delta\Pi_{L}(\delta\theta_{\mathbf{k}},0,\Omega)
at small
\epsilon
. We verified that for such
\Omega
, the largest contribution to
\delta\Pi_{L}(\theta_{\mathbf{k}},0,\Omega)
comes from the antinodal regions
\theta_{\mathrm{h}}\approx n\pi/2,n=0-3
. Expanding the gap function near an antinodal point

\theta_{\mathbf{k}}=0
as
\Delta_{\mathbf{k}}=\Delta_{0}(1-\alpha\theta^{2}_{\mathbf{k}})
, where
\alpha=4/g
,
we obtain
\delta\Pi_{L}(\theta_{\mathbf{k}},0,\Omega)\equiv\delta\Pi_{L}(\theta_{\mathbf{k}},\epsilon)
to the leading
order in
\epsilon
and
\theta_{k}
in the form

\displaystyle\delta\Pi_{L}(\theta_{\mathbf{k}},\epsilon)=\begin{cases}-\dfrac{\pi}{\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{|\epsilon|-\alpha\,\theta^{2}_{\mathbf{k}}}&\epsilon<0\\
i\,\dfrac{\pi}{\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{{\epsilon}+\alpha\,\theta^{2}_{\mathbf{k}}}&\epsilon>0,\end{cases}

(32)

Substituting this result into Eq. (
20
), and approximating
\cos^{2}(2\theta_{\mathbf{k}})\approx 1
, we find that the imaginary part of
\chi_{L}(\epsilon)
has a logarithmic singularity,

\displaystyle\text{Im}\chi_{L}(\epsilon)\propto\begin{cases}\dfrac{2\,\sqrt{2}N_{0}}{\pi^{2}\,g^{2}}\int_{0}^{W}d\delta\theta_{\mathbf{k}}\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha\,\delta\theta^{2}_{\mathbf{k}}+\epsilon}}=\dfrac{\sqrt{2}N_{0}}{\pi^{2}g^{2}\sqrt{\alpha}}\log\dfrac{4W^{2}\alpha}{\epsilon},&\epsilon>0\\
\dfrac{2\,\sqrt{2}N_{0}}{\pi^{2}\,g^{2}}\int_{\sqrt{|\bar{\epsilon}|/\alpha}}^{W}d\delta\theta_{\mathbf{k}}\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha\,\delta\theta^{2}_{\mathbf{k}}-|\bar{\epsilon}|}}=\dfrac{\sqrt{2}N_{0}}{\pi^{2}g^{2}\sqrt{\alpha}}\log\dfrac{32W^{2}\alpha}{|\bar{\epsilon}|},&\epsilon<0,\end{cases}

(33)

where
W
is the width of the hot region.
The real part of
\chi_{L}
has a discontinuity at
\Omega=2\Delta_{0}
:

\displaystyle\Delta\text{Re}\chi_{L}^{R}=\dfrac{2\,\sqrt{2}\,N_{0}}{\pi^{2}\,g^{2}}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{|{\epsilon}|/\alpha}}d\theta_{\mathbf{k}}\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{|{\epsilon}|-\alpha\theta^{2}_{\mathbf{k}}}}=\dfrac{N_{0}\,\sqrt{2}}{\pi g^{2}\sqrt{\alpha}}.

(34)

Eqs. (
34
)–(
33
) satisfy Kramers–Kronig relations (see Appendix
D
for details).

III.3.0.2
\mathbf{q}\neq 0:

We now analyze
\delta\Pi_{L}(\theta_{\mathbf{k}},\mathbf{q},\Omega)

26

\mathbf{q}
.
We find analytically that

\mathrm{Im}\chi_{\mathrm{L}}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)=0
below the threshold at

\Omega_{min}=\textrm{min}\left\{A(\theta_{\mathbf{k}},\mathbf{q})\right\}
, where

A(\theta_{\mathbf{k}},\mathbf{q})=\sqrt{4\Delta_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}+v^{2}_{F}|\mathbf{q}|^{2}\cos^{2}(\theta_{\mathbf{k}}-\theta_{\mathbf{q}})}.

(35)

Analyzing this condition, we find that
\Omega_{min}
vanishes for

\theta_{q}=\pi/4
, i.e., for
\mathbf{q}
along the nodal direction, but is non-zero for all other values of
\theta_{q}
. The largest
\Omega_{min}
is for
\mathbf{q}
along the antinodal directions (
\theta_{q}=0
, etc).

We next analyze the origin of discontinuities and divergences in
\mathrm{Im}\,\chi_{L}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)
at a finite
\mathbf{q}
. These singular features arise when
\Omega
matches the local extrema of

A(\theta_{\mathbf{k}},\mathbf{q})
w.r.t
\theta_{\mathbf{k}}
. Specifically, we show below that when
\Omega
matches a local minima of

,

\mathrm{Im}\,\chi_{L}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)
has a discontinuity, and when
\Omega
matches a local maximum,
\mathrm{Im}\,\chi_{L}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)
diverges logarithmically.
By the Kramers–Kronig relations, these correspond respectively to divergence and discontinuity of
\mathrm{Re}\,\chi_{L}
. To quantify this behavior, we expand
\delta\Pi_{L}(\theta_{\mathbf{k}},\mathbf{q},\Omega)
in Eq. (
26
) near an extremum of
A(\theta_{\mathbf{k}},\mathbf{q})
. We express

A(\theta_{\mathbf{k}},\mathbf{q})=A_{\mathrm{ext}}(\mathbf{q})+\beta\,\delta\theta_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}
, where

\delta\theta_{\mathbf{k}}=\theta_{\mathbf{k}}-\theta_{\mathrm{ext}}(\mathbf{q})
,
A_{\mathrm{ext}}(\mathbf{q})=A(\theta_{\mathrm{ext}}(\mathbf{q}),\mathbf{q})
, and
\beta>0
at a minimum and
\beta<0
at a maximum.
Evaluating
\delta\Pi_{L}(\theta_{\mathbf{k}},\mathbf{q},\Omega)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\delta\Pi_{L}(\delta\theta_{\mathbf{k}},\mathbf{q},\Omega)=\dfrac{\pi}{2\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{A_{\mathrm{ext}}(\mathbf{q})}\dfrac{\sqrt{-{\epsilon}+{\beta}\,\delta\theta_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}}}{\Delta(\theta_{\mathrm{ext}})},

(36)

where
\epsilon=\Omega-A_{\mathrm{ext}}(\mathbf{q})
. We first consider the case
\beta>0
and set
\Omega
to be near a
local minimum
A_{\mathrm{ext}}(\mathbf{q})\equiv A_{\text{Min}}(\mathbf{q})
. For
\epsilon<0
,
\delta\Pi_{L}
is purely real, and hence
\chi_{L}
remains real. For
\epsilon>0
,
\delta\Pi_{L}
acquires an imaginary part within the range
\delta\theta_{\mathbf{k}}<\sqrt{{\epsilon}/{\beta}}
. Substituting into Eq. (

V
Conclusions

In this work, we investigate the collective mode dynamics in a superconductor in which the pairing is driven by soft charge-nematic fluctuations. Such a pairing scenario is especially relevant to FeSe
1-x
S(Te)
x
systems, which host a nematic quantum critical point near
x_{c}\sim 0.17
(for S-doping) and
x_{c}\sim 0.5
(for Te-doping). Near
x_{x}
, strong nematic fluctuations, peaked at zero momentum, generate a highly anisotropic attractive pairing interaction between electrons in the form
V(\mathbf{k},-\mathbf{k};\mathbf{p},-\mathbf{p})\propto-\cos^{2}2\theta_{\mathbf{k}}\,\delta(\theta_{\mathbf{k}}-\theta\mathbf{p})
, where
\theta_{\mathbf{k}}
and
\theta_{\mathbf{p}}
are the angular position of the incoming/outgoing
fermions on the Fermi surface. The factor
\cos^{2}2\theta_{\mathbf{k}}
is the effect of coherent factors of the transformation from orbital to band basis (quantum geometry, in modern language).
This attraction gives rise to superconductivity with a highly anisotropic gap function
\Delta(\theta_{k})
, even in the
s-
wave channel, which we consider here:
\Delta(\theta_{\mathbf{k}})=\Delta_{0}\exp(-\tan^22\theta_\bk/g)
. The superconducting gap is maximized at the hot spots,
\theta_{\mathbf{k}}=n\pi/2,n=0,1,2,3
,and is significantly suppressed in the cold regions around
\theta_{\mathbf{k}}=(2n+1)\pi/4
. We analyze transverse and longitudinal collective excitations of such a superconductor. For this, we compute the pair-pair susceptibility
\chi(\mathbf{q},\Omega)
and decompose it into transverse (
\chi_{T}
) and longitudinal (
\chi_{L}
) components. We find that the structure of the pair-pair susceptibility in our case is qualitatively different from that in conventional
s-
wave BCS superconductor, reminiscent of the difference between series and parallel resistor networks.

In the transverse channel, we found, neglecting the long-range Coulomb interaction,that

\mathrm{Im}\,\chi_{T}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)
is non-zero in a finite range
0<\Omega<v_{F}|\mathbf{q}|
. Within this range, it has two maxima at
\Omega=v_{F}|\mathbf{q}|\cos\theta_{\mathbf{q}}
and
\Omega=v_{F}|\mathbf{q}|\sin\theta_{\mathbf{q}}
, where
\theta_{\mathbf{q}}
defines the direction of the momentum
\mathbf{q}
relative to
\hat{x}
towards a hot spot. These peaks correspond to anisotropic sound-like damped collective modes, whose velocities scale linearly with
\theta_{\mathbf{q}}
. We expect that in the presence of Coulomb interaction, two damped modes remain, but with dispersion
\Omega\propto\sqrt{q}
.

In the longitudinal channel, we found qualitatively different behavior at
q=0
and at a finite
q
.
At zero momentum,
\mathrm{Im}\,\chi_{L}(0,\Omega)
is nonzero for all
\Omega
. It vanishes at
\Omega=0
, but very rapidly increases at a non-zero
\Omega
, as
1/(\log(\Omega/\Delta_0)^{1/2}
. We related this behavior
to the unique nodal structure of our
\Delta(\theta_{k})
. Near
\Omega=2\Delta_{0}
, we found that

\mathrm{Im}\,\chi_{L}(0,\Omega)
diverges as
\log(|\Omega- 2\Delta_0|)
. This gives rise to a discontinuity in the real part of
\chi_{L}
at the same frequency.

At a finite
q
, we found that at small
\Omega\ll\Delta_{0}
,
\mathrm{Im}\,\chi_{L}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)
has one or two discontinuities, depending on the value of
\theta_{q}
. The real part of
\chi_{L}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)
diverges logarithmically at these frequencies. Near
\Omega=2\Delta_{0}
, we found two logarithmic divergencies of
\mathrm{Im}\,\chi_{L}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)
, at
\Omega=2\Delta_{0}+v_{F}^{2}|\mathbf{q}|^{2}\cos^{2}\theta_{\mathbf{q}}/4\Delta_{0}
and
\Omega=2\Delta_{0}+v_{F}^{2}|\mathbf{q}|^{2}\sin^{2}\theta_{\mathbf{q}}/4\Delta_{0}
, which we interpret as two dispersing longitudinal modes. The real part of
\chi_{L}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)
jumps by a finite value at each of these two frequencies. At
\theta_{q}
directed towards a hot spot (
\theta_{q}=n\pi/2,n=0-3
) one of these modes does not disperse with
q
and remains at
2\Delta_{0}
.

We hope that our findings open a new avenue in the studies of the interplay between electronic criticality and
the dynamics of a superconducting state mediated by strongly anisotropic attractive interaction emerging as the combination of quantum criticality and quantum geometry.

\displaystyle\left.+\dfrac{1}{E_{s,\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}/2}+E_{s,\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}/2}+(\Omega+i\delta)}\right]

\displaystyle\Pi^{s}_{L}(q)=\dfrac{N_{0}}{4}\int_{-\Lambda}^{\Lambda}d\xi_{\mathbf{k}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\dfrac{d\theta_{\mathbf{k}}}{2\pi}\left[1+\dfrac{\xi_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}/2}\xi_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}/2}-\Delta^{2}_{0}}{E_{s,\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}/2}E_{s,\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}/2}}\right]

A.1
Transverse Susceptibility

We call the transverse part of the pair-pair susceptibility,

\displaystyle\chi^{s}_{T}(q)=-\dfrac{1}{2\,V_{0}}\left[\dfrac{1}{V_{0}\,\delta\Pi^{s}_{T}(q)}+1\right],

(55)

where
\delta\Pi^{s}_{T}(q)=\Pi^{s}_{T}(q)-\Pi^{s}_{T}(0)

\displaystyle\delta\Pi^{s}_{T}(q)

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\dfrac{d\theta_{\mathbf{k}}}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\xi_{\mathbf{k}}\dfrac{E_{s,\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}/2}+E_{s,\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}/2}}{E_{s,\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}/2}\,E_{s,\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}/2}}\dfrac{\Omega^{2}-\left(\xi_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}/2}-\xi_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}/2}\right)^{2}}{(\Omega+i\delta)^{2}-\left(E_{s,\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}/2}+E_{s,\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}/2}\right)^{2}}.

and gives the dispersion of the Goldstone mode,

\displaystyle\Omega(\mathbf{q})=v_{F}\,|\mathbf{q}|/\sqrt{2}.

(59)

Appendix C
Width and height of the peaks of transverse susceptibility,
\chi_{T}

In this section we derive the height and width of the peaks of Im
\chi_{T}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)
presented in the Sec.
III.1
. We rewrite the expression for Im
\chi_{T}(\mathbf{q},\Omega)
,

\displaystyle\text{Im}\chi_{T}(|\mathbf{q}|,\theta_{\mathbf{q}},\Omega)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{N_{0}}{g^{2}}\dfrac{\Theta\left(1-\dfrac{|\Omega|}{v_{F}|\mathbf{q}|}\right)}{\Omega\sqrt{v^{2}_{F}|\mathbf{q}|^{2}-\Omega^{2}}}\left[\psi\left(\theta_{\mathbf{q}}+\cos^{-1}\dfrac{\Omega}{v_{F}|\mathbf{q}|}\right)+\psi\left(\theta_{\mathbf{q}}-\cos^{-1}\dfrac{\Omega}{v_{F}|\mathbf{q}|}\right)\right],

(72)

where
\psi(\theta)=\Delta^{2}(\theta)/\cos^{4}2\theta
and
\Delta(\theta)=\Delta_{0}\,\exp^{-\tan^{2}2\theta/g}
.
\psi(\theta)
is a highly peaked function of
\theta
, and its maxima occurs when
\theta=n\pi/2,.n=0-3
with a magnitude of
\Delta^{2}_{0}
. As a result, the peak of Im
\chi_{T}(|\mathbf{q}|,\theta_{\mathbf{q}},\Omega)
appears when

\displaystyle\theta_{\mathbf{q}}\pm\cos^{-1}\dfrac{\Omega}{v_{F}|\mathbf{q}|}=n\dfrac{\pi}{2},\quad n=0-3

(73)

This corresponds to two unique solution:
\Omega_{1}=v_{F}|\mathbf{q}|\,\cos\theta_{\mathbf{q}}
and
\Omega_{2}=v_{F}|\mathbf{q}|\,\sin\theta_{\mathbf{q}}
. The height of these peaks are equal to

\displaystyle h_{s}=\text{Im}\chi_{T}(|\mathbf{q}|,\theta_{\mathbf{q}},\Omega_{s})=\dfrac{2\,N_{0}\,\Delta^{2}_{0}}{g^{2}v^{2}_{F}|\mathbf{q}|^{2}\,\sin 2\theta_{\mathbf{q}}},\quad i=1,2

(74)

We define the width of these peaks as width at half maxima such that

\displaystyle\dfrac{\psi\left(\theta_{\mathbf{q}}\pm\cos^{-1}\dfrac{\Omega}{v_{F}|\mathbf{q}|}\right)}{\Omega\sqrt{v^{2}_{F}|\mathbf{q}|^{2}-\Omega^{2}}}=\dfrac{\Delta^{2}_{0}}{v^{2}_{F}|\mathbf{q}|^{2}\,\sin 2\theta_{\mathbf{q}}}.

(75)

We define
x=\Omega/v_{F}|\mathbf{q}|
and write Eq. (
75
) using the expression for
\psi
. This gives

\displaystyle\dfrac{\exp^{-2\tan^{2}(2\theta_{\mathbf{q}}\pm 2\cos^{-1}x)/g}}{\cos^{4}(2\theta_{\mathbf{q}}\pm 2\cos^{-1}x)}=\dfrac{x\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}{\sin 2\theta_{\mathbf{q}}}

(76)

We first focus on the peak at
x_{0}=\Omega_{1}/v_{F}|\mathbf{q}|\,=\cos\theta_{\mathbf{q}}
. For this peak, we write
x=x_{0}+\delta x
, and approximate
\cos^{-1}x\approx\cos^{-1}x_{0}-\delta x/\sin\theta_{\mathbf{q}}
,
\tan^{2}(2\theta_{\mathbf{q}}-2\cos^{-1}x)\approx\dfrac{4(\delta x)^{2}}{\sin^{2}\theta_{\mathbf{q}}}
, and
\cos^{4}(2\theta_{\mathbf{q}}-2\cos^{-1}x)\approx 1
and
x\sqrt{1-x^{2}}\approx\sin 2\theta_{\mathbf{q}}/2
. Combining all the factors we get,
\exp(-8 (\delta x)^2/ g \sin^2\theta_\bq)=1/2
. This gives the width proportional to

\displaystyle\delta x\propto|\sin\theta_{\mathbf{q}}|.

(77)

This shows that at small
\theta_{\mathbf{q}}
, the peak at
\Omega_{1}=v_{F}|\mathbf{q}|\,\cos\theta_{\mathbf{q}}
is very sharp.
On the other hand, for the peak at
\Omega_{2}=v_{f}|\mathbf{q}|\sin\theta_{\mathbf{q}}
, similar analysis will produce width
\delta x\propto\cos\theta_{\mathbf{q}}
.

Appendix D
Kramers Kronig Relation

Let
\chi(\omega)=\chi_{1}(\omega)+I\,\chi_{2}(\omega)
is a complex function of a complex variable
\omega
, where
\chi_{1,2}(\omega)
are real function. Suppose
\chi(\omega)
is analytic on the upper half plane of
\omega
and follow the Kramers-Kronig relation

\displaystyle\chi_{1}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{\pi}P\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\omega^{\prime}\dfrac{\chi_{2}(\omega^{\prime})}{\omega^{\prime}-\omega},

(78)

\displaystyle\chi_{2}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{1}{\pi}P\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\omega^{\prime}\dfrac{\chi_{1}(\omega^{\prime})}{\omega^{\prime}-\omega},

(79)

where
P
stands for the principle value of the integration. We prove that if one of the components of
\chi(\omega)
(either real or imaginary) has a logarithmic singularity at some
\omega=\omega_{0}
, the other component will show a discontinuity at
\omega_{0}
. Since Eq.(
79
) and (
78
) have the some functional structure (except the presence the minus sign), irrespective of which component of
\chi(\omega)
has the logarithmic singularity, the other will always have a discontinuity. Let’s consider

\displaystyle\chi_{2}(\omega)=A\log|\omega-\omega_{0}|.

(80)

Then using Eq. (
78
) we find,

\displaystyle\chi_{1}(\omega)=\dfrac{A}{\pi}P\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\omega^{\prime}\dfrac{\log|\omega^{\prime}-\omega_{0}|}{\omega^{\prime}-\omega}

(81)

. We shift the variable
x=\omega^{\prime}-\omega_{0}
, and define
\omega=\omega_{0}+\epsilon
, then Eq.(
81
) becomes

\displaystyle\chi_{1}(\epsilon)=\dfrac{A}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\,dx\dfrac{\log|x|}{x-\epsilon}=\dfrac{2\epsilon\,A}{\pi}P\int_{0}^{\infty}\,dx\dfrac{\log x}{x^{2}-\epsilon^{2}}=\dfrac{2A\pi}{sgn(\epsilon)}.

(82)

where
P\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\dfrac{\log x}{x^{2}-\epsilon^{2}}=\pi^{2}/4|\epsilon|
.
sgn(x)
is the signum function, it is
-1
when
x<0
and
1
when
x>0
. As a reuslt,
\chi_{1}(\epsilon)
undergoes a jump from
-2A\pi
to
2A\pi
across
\omega=\omega_{0}
.

On the other hand, if the real part has logarithmic singularity,
\chi_{1}(\omega)=A\,\log|\omega-\omega_{0}|
, using Eq. (
79
), one would find that
\chi_{2}(\omega)
undergoes a jump from
2A\pi
to
-2A\pi
across
\omega=\omega_{0}
.
