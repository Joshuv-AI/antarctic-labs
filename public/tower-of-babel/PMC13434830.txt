All-2D vertical metal-semiconductor field-effect transistor with sub-10 nm channel and contact lengths
Authors: Yi Zhang, Xionghui Jia, Jiamin Chen, Minglai Li, Yanping Li, Lun Dai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Two-dimensional (2D) materials are promising candidates for next-generation nanoelectronics in the post-Moore era. However, simultaneously scaling the channel length (Lch) and contact length (Lc) in transition metal dichalcogenide-based field-effect transistors remains a challenge. Here, we introduce an all-2D vertical metal–semiconductor field-effect transistor featuring an MoS2 channel that contacts the sidewall of a graphene (source)–hBN (insulator)–graphene (drain)–hBN (insulator) heterostructure. A self-aligned TiS2–MoS2 Schottky junction ensures full-gate control over the channel. The Lch and Lc are determined by the thicknesses of the bottom hBN and the graphene electrodes, respectively. Consequently, the Lch and Lc can be simultaneously scaled to sub-10 nm with a vertical pitch size of sub-30 nm. The typical device exhibits an on/off ratio > 107 at an operating voltage of ~0.5 V, a subthreshold swing of ~62 mV/Dec, a drain-induced barrier lowering of ~33 mV/V, and an on-state current density of ~144 μA/μm at 1 V. Sentaurus technology computer-aided design simulations further verify the good electrostatic control provided by the full-gate configuration and the bottleneck-free transport within the channel. These results highlight the potential of 2D materials for high-density and low-power integrated circuits.

Introduction

The field-effect transistor (FET) is a fundamental component of modern integrated circuits. Continuing miniaturization of FET has driven advancements in chip density and energy efficiency. However, as silicon-based FETs are approaching physical and technological limits, further scaling encounters significant challenges, including short-channel effects (SCE), increasing power consumption, and escalating fabrication costs1–3. Two-dimensional (2D) van der Waals (vdW) materials, such as transition metal dichalcogenides (TMDCs), graphene, and hexagonal boron nitride (hBN), etc., with atomic-scale thicknesses and dangling-bond-free surfaces4–7, have advantages in mitigating SCE, constituting clean interfaces, and eliminating lattice match requirement, providing a promising pathway for the post-Moore era2,3,8.

The fundamental metric for transistor density in conventional planar architectures is the contacted poly pitch (CPP), which is the sum of the gate length, contact length, and necessary lateral spacing9,10 (Supplementary Fig. 1a). Therefore, scaling certain critical parameters (such as the gate, channel, and contact length, Lg/Lch/Lc) is indispensable. Many efforts focused on scaling these parameters in TMDC-based metal–oxide–semiconductor FETs (MOSFETs) have been reported, in which high-cost and complex lithography processes, such as deep ultraviolet (DUV), extreme ultraviolet (EUV), or electron beam lithography (EBL), are employed11–13. Although alternative strategies, including nanowire masking14,15, self-assembly patterning16, crack formation17–19, shadow evaporation20,21, self-oxidation22, and selective etching23, etc., have been developed to create sub-100 nm channels, the related devices still have large lateral footprints. On the other hand, MoS2 FETs with 1 and sub-1-nm gate lengths have been realized using carbon nanotube (CNT)24 and the edge of monolayer graphene25 as the gates, respectively. However, in these two works, the physical Lch remains at the micrometer scale. Meanwhile, scaling contact electrodes also meets challenge: current crowding effect hinders the continuous reduction of the Lc26,27. The transfer length (LT) is a criterion for evaluating the scaling limit of the contacts. When the Lc, especially at the source, approaches this value, severe current crowding occurs, leading to a significant reduction in the on-current26,27.

It is inspiring that vertical FETs (VFETs) have brought forth a new concept, where the device density is governed by the vertical pitch size28 (the cumulative thickness of the functional stack, Supplementary Fig. 1b). In this architecture, Lch and Lc are determined by film thicknesses, enabling atomic-scale precision without relying on expensive lithography. Recently, VFETs based on an M (source)–TMDC–M (drain) architecture (M represents graphene, CNT, or metal) have been reported29,30, in which the Lch is determined by the thickness of the TMDC. However, in these devices, source–drain tunneling frequently occurs, resulting in a low on/off ratio and a large operating voltage. VFETs based on a source–insulator–drain (S–I–D) sidewall architecture have also been reported31–33, in which the TMDC channel is laid on the sidewall and the Lch is determined by the thickness of the insulator. These devices inhibit source–drain tunneling, but sacrifice the vertical pitch size.

Apart from the MOSFET, the metal–semiconductor FET (MESFET) is another fundamental transistor architecture, where the metal gate and semiconductor channel form a Schottky junction (SJ). In conventional TMDC-based MESFET fabrication processes, metal deposition typically introduces defects into the interface, causing Fermi-level pinning and deteriorating SJ quality34.

In this work, we design and fabricate an all-2D vertical MESFET based on vdW materials with a source–insulator–drain–insulator (S–I–D–I) configuration. Herein, few-layer graphene (FLG) flakes serve as the source and drain, while hBN flakes serve as the bottom and top insulators. Monolayer MoS2 serves as the channel, which contacts the sidewall of the S–I–D–I heterostructure. The channel length is determined by the thickness of the bottom hBN flake, and the contact lengths are determined by the thicknesses of the FLGs. We adopt TiS2, a vdW semimetal material with good conductivity and a high work function (~6.6 eV) to serve as the gate, which can form an SJ with MoS235. Our approach simultaneously scales the channel and contact lengths to sub-10 nm with a vertical pitch size of sub-30 nm. The fabrication process does not involve dielectric/metal deposition on the TMDC, thereby preserving a near-ideal SJ34. The typical device exhibits satisfying performance: on/off ratio > > 107 at an operating voltage of ~0.5 V, a subthreshold swing (SS) of ~62 mV/Dec, a drain-induced barrier lowering (DIBL) of ~33 mV/V, and an on-state current density (Ion) of ~144 μA/μm at 1 V. Sentaurus technology computer-aided design (TCAD) simulations further verify the good electrostatic control provided by the full-gate configuration and the bottleneck-free transport within the channel. Our work demonstrates a subtle and low-cost paradigm toward the extreme miniaturization of FET in the post-Moore era.

Results

Device fabrication and characterization

The device fabrication procedures are shown in Fig. 1a–d. First, an FLG (source)–hBN (bottom insulator)–FLG (drain)–hBN (top insulator) heterostructure was assembled on a p⁺-Si/SiO2 substrate by mechanically exfoliating the constituent materials. We chose a thicker FLG to serve as the source27. Subsequently, ultraviolet (UV) lithography and reactive ion etching (RIE) were employed to create a sloped sidewall region of the heterostructure. Chemical-vapor-deposition (CVD)-grown monolayer MoS2 (detailed characterization is provided in Supplementary Fig. 2) and mechanically exfoliated few-layer TiS2 were transferred in sequence onto this region. Next, with atop TiS2 as the mask, the uncovered part of the MoS2 was removed by a second RIE process. The self-aligned TiS2–MoS2 SJ ensures full-gate control over the channel, thereby enhancing the electrostatic modulation36. Detailed processes are provided in Supplementary Fig. 3 and the “Methods” section. The final MESFET device (Fig. 1e) features a vertical S–I–D–I sidewall architecture. The top hBN ensures that the MoS2 only contacts the sidewall of the top FLG to scale the contact length of the drain and mitigate interlayer tunneling between the gate and drain37. Electrical properties of the all-2D vertical MESFET

A representative device of ours has an Lch of ~8.1 nm and Lc of ~14.0 and 12.6 nm. The corresponding vertical pitch size is sub-30 nm, with the thicknesses of the s-FLG, b-hBN, d-FLG, and t-hBN measured before RIE (Supplementary Fig. 7). First, I–V curve of the SJ was measured, as shown in Fig. 3a. The device exhibits clear Schottky characteristics with a rectification ratio exceeding 104. From the I–V curve, we can extract the ideality factor (ηη) of ~1.21 by using the equation: \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\eta=\frac{q}{m{{\mathrm{ln}}}(10){k}_{{{\mathrm{B}}}}T}$$\end{document}η=qmln(10)kBT, where qq is the unit charge, m is the slope of the linear region, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${k}_{{{\mathrm{B}}}}$$\end{document}kB is the Boltzmann constant, and T is the absolute temperature34,38. The near-ideal value of ηη is attributed to the metal gate transfer process that preserves a clean interface between the TiS2 and MoS2, as well as a pristine MoS2 channel38,39. Subsequently, the IDS–VDS output curves of the MESFET were measured under various gate voltages (Fig. 3b). The Ion can reach up to ~144 μA/μm at VDS = 1 V. The distinct linear and saturation regions demonstrate the typical output characteristics of an FET with efficient carrier transport between the source/drain and the channel. Notably, the Ion decreases significantly when the source contact length is reduced below ~14 nm (Supplementary Fig. 8), suggesting that the LT is expected to be less than 14 nm. Compared to previous reports about short contacts (Supplementary Table 2), our result represents a state-of-the-art LT value obtained through the direct measurement of physically scaled contacts13,26,27,40–44. Figure 3c shows the IDS–VGS transfer curves at various VDS. The off-state currents (Ioff) are < 10 – 4 μA/μm, meeting the International Roadmap for Devices and Systems (IRDS) benchmarks for both high-performance (HP, Ioff < < 10−2 μA/μm) and high-density (HD, Ioff < < 10−4 μA/μm) applications9. The gate leakage current of the device (Supplementary Fig. 9) is consistent with SJ characteristics and meets the low-power limit requirement9 ( < < 1.5 × 10 – 2 A/cm2). Figure 3d illustrates the relationship between the on/off ratio and operating voltage, measured at VDS = 0.1 V. Herein, the operating voltage is defined as the VGS required for reaching a target on/off ratio45,46. The device achieves an on/off ratio exceeding 107 within a remarkably narrow operation window of ~1 V (i.e., the device can be turned from off-state to on-state within a small gate voltage range from −0.5 to 0.5 V). Notably, the operating voltage can be as low as 0.5 V, satisfying the 0.7 V limit of the complementary metal–oxide–semiconductor (CMOS) logic standard9, which decisively demonstrates its superior switching efficiency and low-power potential.

From Fig. 3c, we extracted the key parameters of the MESFET, including threshold voltage (Vth), on/off ratio, and SS, and summarized them in Fig. 3e. To quantitatively evaluate the electrostatic control limit of our vertical architecture, we extract the capacitance-equivalent thickness (CET). As a comprehensive metric, CET accounts for the equivalent thickness of the gate, vdW gap, and quantum capacitance effects, making it a critical indicator of the gate-coupling efficiency47. Capacitance–voltage (C–V) measurements were performed on the TiS2–MoS2 SJ with varying TiS2 thicknesses. As shown in Supplementary Fig. 10a, clear depletion and accumulation behaviors are observed. It is worth noting that the hysteresis of the IDS–VGS curves of the MESFET device can be as small as ~20 mV (Supplementary Fig. 12). We attribute this merit to our MESFET fabrication, which is free from the gate dielectric and metal deposition processes34,38.

Physical mechanism and TCAD simulation

To further elucidate the operating principle of the all-2D vertical MESFET, the band diagrams of the TiS2–MoS2 SJ and MESFET were analyzed under various gate voltages. As illustrated in Fig. 4a–c, TiS2 and MoS2 form a type-I band alignment. Owing to the large difference between the work function of TiS2 (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\varPhi}_{{{\mathrm{TiS}}}_{2}}$$\end{document}ΦTiS2) and the electron affinity of n-MoS2 (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\chi }_{{{{\mathrm{MoS}}}}_{2}}$$\end{document}χMoS2)13, the n-MoS2 bands bend upward, forming an SJ. When the SJ is forward/reverse biased, the Fermi level of n-MoS2 near the SJ moves toward/backward the conduction band edge, and the built-in potential energy (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\varPhi}_{{\mathrm{bi}}}$$\end{document}Φbi) decreases/increases. Accordingly, the depletion region width within the MoS2 channel of the MESFET narrows/widens, thereby effectively modulating the transport efficiency across the channel. Notably, our full-gate MESFET architecture offers a distinct advantage in electrostatic modulation due to the direct contact between the metal gate and the entire semiconductor channel. We preserve a robust Schottky barrier through pristine vdW integration and appropriate post-assembly thermal treatment. This approach maximizes capacitive coupling, facilitating efficient charge accumulation to support a high Ion and mitigate leakage current36,38,53. Reproducibility and benchmarks

To statistically validate device reproducibility and reliability, an additional batch of 15 devices with a physical channel length of ~8.7 ± 0.7 nm was fabricated under the identical optimized conditions. As illustrated by the cumulative distribution functions in Fig. 6a, the devices exhibit overall uniformity with narrow parameter distributions. Specifically, strong switching characteristics are demonstrated by an on/off ratio reaching ~7.8 × 108 (average ~1.4 × 108) and a steep minimum subthreshold swing (SSmin) down to ~62 mV/Dec (average ~81.5 mV/Dec). Furthermore, good immunity to SCE and low-power capability are evidenced by minimum values of DIBL and operating voltage of ~30 mV/V and ~0.5 V, respectively. Discussion

In summary, we have demonstrated an all-2D vertical MESFET architecture with sub-10 nm channel and contact lengths (vertical pitch size of sub-30 nm). The MESFET is constructed from vdW materials, including FLG, hBN, MoS2, and TiS2. The fabrication process is independent of lithographic precision and avoids direct dielectric/metal deposition onto the TMDC, thereby preserving a near-ideal SJ and pristine channel. The typical device exhibits favorable electrical performance, such as an on/off ratio > 107 at an operating voltage of ~0.5 V, SSmin ~62 mV/Dec, DIBL ~33 mV/V, and Ion ~144 μA/μm at 1 V. These metrics are attributed to the device configuration and precise nanoscale contact engineering. Our work provides a promising and low-cost paradigm for beyond-silicon and post-Moore nanoelectronics. It is anticipated that with the development of the high-quality wafer-scale growth of graphene, hBN, and their heterostructures4,5,64,65, alongside the successful synthesis of large-scale TiS266, our conceptual vertical MESFET devices will transition from the laboratory stage to industrial-scale production in the near future.

Methods

Synthesis of monolayer MoS2

The monolayer MoS2 films were grown on a p+-Si/SiO2 (285 nm) substrate using the CVD method in a three-temperature-zone tubular furnace. High-purity molybdenum trioxide (MoO3, 99.999%, 130 mg) and sulfur powder (S, 99.999%, excess) were used as precursors. Perylene-3,4,9,10-tetracarboxylic acid tetrapotassium salt (PTAS) served as the seeding promoter. The S powder was placed in the upstream zone at 150 °C. The MoO3 powder, PTAS, and substrate were sequentially placed in the downstream zone at 650 °C. The growth was carried out in a high-purity Ar environment under atmospheric pressure. The Ar flow rate was maintained at 15 sccm. The growth duration was 5 min, after which the furnace was cooled naturally to room temperature.

Transfer of monolayer MoS2 on the sidewall of the heterostructure

Monolayer MoS2 was transferred to the target substrate with the assistance of polymethyl methacrylate (PMMA) in deionized water. Once detached from the growth substrate, the PMMA/MoS2 film was subsequently collected onto the target substrate. After natural drying in a vacuum chamber for 12 h, the target substrate was placed on a hot plate and baked at 90 °C for 2 min to further improve the adhesion between the MoS2 and the sidewall of the heterostructure. The PMMA layer was removed from the target substrate using warm acetone.

Fabrication of the all-2D vertical MESFET

Mechanically exfoliated FLG and hBN flakes were transferred onto a p+-Si/SiO2 substrate by polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS) stamp under the help of a microscope attached with a micro-manipulator, to form the FLG–hBN–FLG–hBN vertical heterostructure. The FLGs were connected to two pre-fabricated metal electrodes for measurement purposes. The substrate was subsequently annealed in a tubular furnace at 200 °C with Ar/H2 (10 sccm/40 sccm) environment under low chamber pressure (0.45 Torr) for 2 h. Next, UV lithography (Suss MJB4) and RIE (SENTECH EtchLab200) were employed to create the sloped S–I–D–I sidewall region. Prior to RIE, the exposed lithography window region was cleaned with O2 plasma. The RIE was conducted in O2/CHF3 mixed gas (20 sccm/1 sccm) with a chamber pressure of 6 Pa and radio frequency power of 30 W. The etching duration was 3 min (more details are provided in Supplementary Note 1). CVD-grown monolayer MoS2 was transferred onto the sidewall region using the above-mentioned method. Subsequently, TiS2 was transferred onto the MoS2. With TiS2 as the mask, the uncovered part of MoS2 was removed by a second RIE process in O2 (30 sccm) environment for 1 min. The chamber pressure was 7 Pa, and the radio frequency power was 70 W. Finally, an additional FLG was transferred to connect the TiS2 to the third pre-fabricated metal electrode for measurement purposes. The completed device underwent a final low-pressure annealing treatment (the same condition as above) to remove transfer medium residues, reduce bubble density, and improve overall contact quality.

Characterizations and measurements

The Raman and photoluminescence (PL) spectra were collected by a micro-zone confocal Raman system (WITec alpha 300R) attached with a 532 nm laser. The thicknesses of 2D materials and surface potential of the TiS2–MoS2 SJ were measured by an atomic force microscope (Asylum Research, Cypher S). The HAADF-STEM and EDS elemental mapping images were taken by a spherical aberration-corrected transmission electron microscope (FEI Titan Cubed Themis G2 300). The sample for HAADF-STEM observation was prepared in a focused ion beam (FIB) system (ThermoFisher Helios G4 UX). The electrical measurements were conducted in a probe station (EB-6) connected to a semiconductor characterization system (Keithley 4200A-SCS). All the characterizations were conducted in the dark at room temperature.

TCAD Simulation

The electrical properties of the all-2D vertical MESFET were simulated using Sentaurus TCAD. All 2D materials were modeled by adapting the material parameters of bulk semiconductors, including permittivity, electron affinity, work function, and bandgap, to their respective layered 2D structures. A 0.2 nm vdW gap was intentionally introduced between adjacent 2D material interfaces to accurately reflect the physical nature of vdW integration. Carrier transport within the MoS2 channel was modeled using the drift-diffusion approximation, and field-effect mobility was assumed to be limited by phonon scattering. The Caughey–Thomas phenomenological model was used for velocity saturation in the high-lateral-field limit. Given the nanometer-scale of all the constituent layers in the device, the density-gradient quantum correction model was applied to account for quantum confinement effects on the carrier distribution. The contact between the FLG source/drain electrodes and MoS2 was modeled as a Schottky barrier with the thermionic emission model. Detailed parameters are provided in Supplementary Table 3.
