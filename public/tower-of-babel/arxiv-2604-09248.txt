Limit laws for longest edges in empty region graphs
Holger Sambale, Matthias Schulte, Christoph Thäle

Abstract
Empty region graphs are graphs whose vertices are points in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} and where two vertices are connected by an edge whenever some associated region does not contain any other vertices. We investigate the asymptotic behaviour of long edges in empty region graphs generated by a stationary Poisson process in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} . Letting the intensity of the underlying Poisson process tend to infinity, we consider the associated point process of edge midpoints, suitably transformed edge lengths, and directions of the edges. We prove that it converges in distribution to a Poisson process on ℝ d × ℝ × 𝕃 d \mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{L}^{d} , where 𝕃 d \mathbb{L}^{d} is the space of lines in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} through the origin, and that the suitably transformed length of the longest edge with midpoint in an observation window converges in distribution to a Gumbel distributed random variable. Our approach yields explicit error bounds in Kantorovich–Rubinstein distance for the point process convergence when restricting to an observation window and in Kolmogorov distance for the maximal edge length. The results apply uniformly to a broad class of empty region graphs, including the Gabriel graph, the relative neighbourhood graph, the beta-skeleton graph, the Mastercard graph, and the Pacman graph. Keywords: Empty region graph, geometric extreme value theory, Gumbel distribution, longest edge, Poisson approximation, Poisson process approximation, rates of convergence, stochastic geometry. MSC: 05C80, 60D05, 60F05, 60G55, 60G70.

Introduction and main results

1.1
Introductory notes

Geometric random graphs have been the subject of extensive study over the past decades as they provide a natural mathematical framework for describing spatial networks in stochastic geometry, computational geometry, and applied fields such as telecommunications and biological modelling. Given a random collection of points in Euclidean space, one typically connects two points according to some geometric rule, leading to a wide range of random graph models such as the Gilbert graph, the Delaunay graph, or the Gabriel graph. These models exhibit rich structural properties and have been widely investigated, for example, from the perspective of connectivity, cluster sizes, degree distributions, or total edge lengths.

A prominent class of such random geometric graphs are so-called
empty region graphs
, where two points are connected if a prescribed geometric region associated with the pair contains no further points
[
5
,
8
,
18
]
. These graphs belong to the broad family of proximity graphs, where the presence of edges is determined by local geometric constraints
[
17
]
. Examples include the Gabriel graph, the relative neighbourhood graph, and the beta-skeleton graph, see Section
1.2
below for detailed descriptions and illustrations. These and various other constructions arise in algorithmic and computational geometry, morphology, computer vision, geographic analysis, and pattern classification
[
1
,
12
,
14
]
. Despite their diverse definitions, empty region graphs share a common structural feature: the presence of long edges is governed by rare events, where unusually large empty regions occur. This makes them a natural setting in which to apply methods from extreme value theory and Poisson approximation to study extremal edge lengths.

In the present work, we consider the asymptotic behaviour of longest edges in empty region graphs generated by a stationary Poisson process in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Our focus lies on the distribution of the longest edges and on the Poissonian structure emerging in the limit when the edge midpoints and the suitably transformed edge lengths as well as the edge directions are recorded, where the latter are elements in
\mathbb{L}^{d}
, the space of all lines through the origin. More precisely, letting the intensity of the underlying Poisson process tend to infinity, we show that the associated point process of midpoints, transformed edge lengths, and edge directions converges in distribution to a Poisson process on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{L}^{d}
. When restricting to edges with midpoints in an observation window, we obtain explicit error bounds in the Kantorovich–Rubinstein distance and Gumbel limit laws for the length of the longest edge, together with associated rates of convergence for the Kolmogorov distance.

The study of longest edges in geometric random graphs and related models has a long tradition in stochastic geometry. Early work of Henze
[
11
]
established limit laws for maxima of nearest-neighbour distances, and Penrose
[
20
]
analysed the asymptotic behaviour of the longest edge in the random minimal spanning tree. More recently, extreme-value methods have been applied to other structures arising in stochastic geometry, such as Poisson–Voronoi or Delaunay tessellations (see, e.g.,
[
4
,
19
]
) and large
k
th-nearest neighbour balls (see
[
6
]
and the references therein). In parallel, limit laws for longest edges have also been studied in probabilistic network models, such as long-range percolation
[
22
]
and soft random geometric graphs
[
23
]
. Together, these contributions underline the role played by extremal edges in understanding the connectivity and geometry of spatial random graphs. They motivate the investigation of similar questions for empty region graphs, which are in the focus of the present paper. In contrast, cumulative edge length functionals, where all edges of a class of empty region graphs are taken into account, are studied in
[
10
]
. In that paper, the authors derive quantitative bounds for the normal approximation of such functionals employing stabilisation arguments.

On a technical level, the quantitative Poisson process limit theorem developed in this paper relies on a very general Poisson process approximation bound from
[
3
]
. In contrast, the Gumbel limit laws for the length of the longest edge are derived from a new Poisson approximation bound, which is of independent interest. This new bound is obtained by combining arguments from
[
3
]
with the classical Chen–Stein method for Poisson approximation.
The main part of the proofs then relies on delicate estimates for integrals and on lower bounds for intersection volumes of sets under translations or rigid motions. This task is technically demanding and requires arguments, going beyond those needed in the study of normal approximations for cumulative edge length functionals.

1.2
Empty region graphs

Before we introduce the class of graphs under consideration, let us fix some notation. The volume (i.e.,
d
-dimensional Lebesgue measure) of a set
W\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is denoted by
{\rm vol}(W)
. For two points
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we denote by
[xy]
the line segment connecting
x
with
y
and by
\|x-y\|
the Euclidean length of
[xy]
. Moreover, we write
\langle x,y\rangle
for the standard scalar product of
x
and
y
. For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
r>0
we let
B^{d}(x,r)
be the closed ball centred at
x
with radius
r
. Furthermore, we introduce the shorthand notation
\kappa_{d}:={\rm vol}(B^{d}(0,1))=\pi^{d/2}/\Gamma(d/2+1)
and put
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\|x\|=1\}
.

To introduce the class of random graphs we study in this article, let
\eta_{t}
be a stationary Poisson process with intensity
t\geq 2
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for some fixed space dimension
d\geq 1
. We remark that the choice of
2
as a lower bound for
t
is arbitrary, any number
>1
could be used instead. With any two distinct points
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we associate a compact set
S(x,y)\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
S(x,y)=S(y,x)
and

\mathrm{vol}(S(x,y))=\gamma\|x-y\|^{d}

(1.1)

for a constant
\gamma\in(0,\infty)
. Based on the sets
(S(x,y))_{x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
, we construct the
empty region graph

G_{S}(\eta_{t})
with vertex set
\eta_{t}
as follows. We include an edge
[xy]
between any two different points
x
and
y
of
\eta_{t}
if and only if the associated set
S(x,y)
contains no other point of
\eta_{t}
. Note that
x
and
y
may belong to
S(x,y)
but do not have to.

Example 1.1

(Gabriel graph)
.

Let
S(x,y):=B^{d}((x+y)/2,\|x-y\|/2)
be the
d
-dimensional closed ball whose diameter coincides with the line segment
[xy]
. Then the resulting empty region graph is the classical
Gabriel graph
. In this case, relation (
1.1
) is satisfied with
\gamma=\kappa_{d}/2^{d}
.

Example 1.2

(Strong nearest neighbour graph)
.

Let
S(x,y):=B^{d}(x,\|x-y\|)\cup B^{d}(y,\|x-y\|)
be the union of the two balls with radius
\|x-y\|
centred at
x
and
y
, respectively. Then
G_{S}(\eta_{t})
becomes the
strong nearest neighbour graph
. While in the nearest neighbour graph each vertex is connected to its nearest neighbour, in the strong nearest neighbour graph only edges between so-called mutual nearest neighbours (i.e., one point is the nearest neighbour of the other and vice versa) are present. Here, relation (
1.1
) is satisfied with
\gamma={\rm vol}(B^{d}(0,1)\cup B^{d}(e_{1},1))
, where
e_{1}:=(1,0,\ldots,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Example 1.3

(Relative neighbourhood graph)
.

Suppose that
S(x,y):=B^{d}(x,\|x-y\|)\cap B^{d}(y,\|x-y\|)
is the intersection of two balls with radius
\|x-y\|
centred at
x
and
y
, respectively. Then the empty region graph
G_{S}(\eta_{t})
is the
relative neighbourhood graph
and we have that (
1.1
) is satisfied with
\gamma={\rm vol}(B^{d}(0,1)\cap B^{d}(e_{1},1))
with
e_{1}
as in the previous example. In this graph, two points are connected by an edge if and only if there is no third point that is closer to both of them than they are to each other. The relative neighbourhood graph is almost surely connected as a consequence of
[
26
, Theorem 1]
and triangle free by
[
5
, Theorem 17]
.

Example 1.4

(Beta-skeleton graph)
.

Fix
\beta\in[1,\infty)
and put

S_{\beta}(x,y):=B^{d}\Big(\Big(1-{\beta\over 2}\Big)x+{\beta\over 2}y,{\beta\over 2}\|x-y\|\Big)\cap B^{d}\Big(\Big(1-{\beta\over 2}\Big)y+{\beta\over 2}x,{\beta\over 2}\|x-y\|\Big)\,.

The corresponding empty region graph
G_{S_{\beta}}(\eta_{t})
is the
beta-skeleton graph
from
[
14
]
. The family of these geometric random graphs have important applications to the analysis of interpoint linkages in empirical networks. Note that
G_{S_{\beta_{1}}}(\eta_{t})
is almost surely a subgraph of
G_{S_{\beta_{2}}}(\eta_{t})
, provided that
\beta_{1}\geq\beta_{2}\geq 1
. We also notice that
G_{S_{1}}(\eta_{t})
reduces to the Gabriel graph,
G_{S_{2}}(\eta_{t})
to the relative neighbourhood graph, and that for general
\beta\in[1,\infty)
relation (
1.1
) is satisfied with

\gamma={\rm vol}\Big(B^{d}\Big({\beta\over 2}e_{1},{\beta\over 2}\Big)\cap B^{d}\Big(\Big(1-{\beta\over 2}\Big)e_{1},{\beta\over 2}\Big)\Big)

and
e_{1}
as in the previous two examples.

Example 1.5

(Mastercard graph)
.

To construct the so-called
Mastercard graph
from
[
5
]
we take for

the convex hull
{\rm conv}\{B^{d}(x,\|x-y\|)\cup B^{d}(y,\|x-y\|)\}
. Equivalently,

is the Minkowski sum of the segment

Example 1.6

(Pacman graph)
.

Fix
\theta\in(0,2\pi]
and write
c_{\theta}:=\cos(\theta/2)
. Define the wedge with apex

, axis

For two distinct points

with parameter

arises by taking

). For

.

1.3
Main results

All results in this section are stated for the empty region graphs introduced in Examples
1.1
–
1.8
of the previous section. Nevertheless, our approach applies to a broader class of empty region graphs. Since the formulation of such general results requires several technical assumptions, we present the general versions of our results in Theorems
3.1
and

To formulate our first main result, we need some more concepts and notation. To introduce them, let
\mathbb{X}
be a locally finite second countable Hausdorff space with Borel
\sigma
-field
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{X})
and let
\mathbf{N}_{\mathbb{X}}
denote the space of locally finite counting measures on
\mathbb{X}
. In our paper,
\mathbb{X}
will be the product of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\mathbb{R}
and the space
\mathbb{L}^{d}
of
1
-dimensional linear subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. A random element in
\mathbf{N}_{\mathbb{X}}
is called a point process. In particular, by a Poisson process
\Pi
on
\mathbb{X}
with a locally finite intensity measure
\mathbf{M}
we understand a point process with the two properties that
\Pi(A)
follows a Poisson distribution with parameter
\mathbf{M}(A)
for all
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{X})
and that
\eta(A_{1}),\ldots,\eta(A_{m})
are independent for all pairwise disjoint
A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{X})
with
m\in\mathbb{N}
, see e.g.
[
15
,
24
]
.

To measure the distance between two point processes
\xi
and
\zeta
on
\mathbb{X}
such that
\mathbb{E}[\xi(\mathbb{X})],\mathbb{E}[\zeta(\mathbb{X})]<\infty
,
we use the
Kantorovich–Rubinstein distance
. It is defined as

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{KR}}(\xi,\zeta):=\sup\Bigl\{\,\bigl|\mathbb{E}[h(\xi)]-\mathbb{E}[h(\zeta)]\bigr|:\;h:\mathbf{N}_{\mathbb{X}}\to\mathbb{R},\;h\text{ is 1-Lipschitz w.r.t.\ }\mathrm{d}_{\rm TV}\Bigr\},

where
\mathrm{d}_{\rm TV}
denotes the
total variation distance
which for any two measures
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
on
\mathbb{X}
is given by

\mathrm{d}_{\rm TV}(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}):=\sup_{B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{X}),\varphi_{1}(B),\varphi_{2}(B)<\infty}\bigl|\varphi_{1}(B)-\varphi_{2}(B)\bigr|.

(1.2)

The Kantorovich–Rubinstein distance is also called Wasserstein distance, Monge–Kantorovich distance or Rubinstein distance, see the discussion in Section 2 of
[
7
]
. In particular, convergence in Kantorovich–Rubinstein distance implies distributional convergence of point process, a notion for which we refer to
[
13
, Chapter 23]
.

In what follows, we write
\mathrm{span}\,B
for the linear hull of a set
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. By
\nu
we denote the rotation-invariant Haar probability measure on
\mathbb{L}^{d}
. Recall that
\eta_{t}
stands for a Poisson process on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
whose intensity measure measure is
t\geq 2
times the Lebesgue measure. We write
\eta_{t,\neq}^{2}
for the set of pairs of distinct points of
\eta_{t}
and
\delta_{z}
for the Dirac measure concentrated at a point
z
. In each of the Examples
1.1
–
1.8
, one has an edge between
x
and
y
with
(x,y)\in\eta^{2}_{t,\neq}
if and only if the set
S(x,y)
does not contain any other points of
\eta_{t}
. The latter can be formulated as
(\eta_{t}-\delta_{x}-\delta_{y})(S(x,y))=0
since
\eta_{t}-\delta_{x}-\delta_{y}
is the Poisson process
\eta_{t}
without the points
x
and
y
. Recall that
\gamma
is the constant from (
1.1
). We consider the point process

\xi_{t}:=\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{(x,y)\in\eta^{2}_{t,\neq}}\mathbbm{1}\Big\{(\eta_{t}-\delta_{x}-\delta_{y})\big(S(x,y)\big)=0\Big\}\,\delta_{\big(\tfrac{x+y}{2},\,\gamma t\lVert x-y\rVert^{d}-\log(t)-\log(\kappa_{d}/(2\gamma)),\mathrm{span}\{x-y\}\big)},

which records the midpoints, the transformed lengths, and the directions of edges in the empty region graph. It is divided by two since each pair of points occurs twice in the sum. The following theorem shows that the distribution of
\xi_{t}
approaches that of a Poisson process on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{L}^{d}
with intensity measure
\mathrm{vol}\otimes\mu\otimes\nu
as
t\to\infty
, where, here and throughout this paper,
\mu
denotes the measure on
\mathbb{R}
defined by
\mu([a,\infty))=e^{-a}
for
a\in\mathbb{R}
. In particular, we obtain explicit dimension-dependent convergence rates in the Kantorovich–Rubinstein distance after restricting to an observation window. In what follows,
\varphi|_{A}
stands for the restriction of a point process
\varphi
to a set
A
(i.e., the point process containing only the points of
\varphi
that belong to
A
) and
\overset{d}{\longrightarrow}
indicates convergence in distribution.

. Here,

is a constant which only depends on

,

,

Our second main result describes and quantifies the (to some extent) universal asymptotic distributional behaviour of the longest edge with midpoint in an observation window of each of the empty region graphs in Examples
1.1
–
1.8
. To this end, let

L_{t,W}:=\max\Big\{\|x-y\|:(x,y)\in\eta_{t,\neq}^{2},(\eta_{t}-\delta_{x}-\delta_{y})(S(x,y))=0,{x+y\over 2}\in W\Big\}

be the length of the longest edge in such a graph with midpoint
\frac{x+y}{2}
in a set
W\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\mathrm{vol}(W)<\infty
. Here, we use the convention that
\max\varnothing=-\infty
. Furthermore, for two random variables
X
and
Y
we denote by

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{Kol}}(X,Y):=\sup_{s\in\mathbb{R}}|\mathbb{P}(X\leq s)-\mathbb{P}(Y\leq s)|

the
Kolmogorov distance
between
X
and
Y
, and note that convergence in Kolmogorov distance implies convergence in distribution.

Theorem 1.10
.

Assume the same set-up as in Theorem
1.9
. Then, for any
t\geq 2
,

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{Kol}}\big(\gamma tL_{t,W}^{d}-\log(t)-\log(\kappa_{d}/(2\gamma)),G_{W}\big)\leq\begin{cases}c\log^{-(d-2)}(t),&d\geq 3,\\
c\log^{-1/2}(t),&d=2,\end{cases}

where
G_{W}
is a Gumbel distributed random variable with
\mathbb{P}(G_{W}\leq s)=\exp(-\mathrm{vol}(W)e^{-s})
for
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Here,
c\in(0,\infty)
is a constant which only depends on
d
,
\mathrm{vol}(W)
, and the parameters of the particular example.

, the assumptions introduced in Section
3
.

2
Poisson process and Poisson approximation

For Poisson process approximations as in Theorem
1.9
we rely on a result from
[
3
]
, which we recall in this section. In order to provide a rate of convergence to the Gumbel distribution as in Theorem
1.10
, we employ a related new bound for Poisson approximation, which we show in this section and which we think is of independent interest.

Let
\mathbb{X}
and
\mathbb{Y}
be locally finite second countable Hausdorf spaces and, as above, let
\mathbf{N}_{\mathbb{X}}
be the space of locally finite counting measures on
\mathbb{X}
. For fixed
k\in\mathbb{N}
let
f:\mathbb{X}^{k}\times\mathbf{N}_{\mathbb{X}}\to\mathbb{Y}
and
g:\mathbb{X}^{k}\times\mathbf{N}_{\mathbb{X}}\to\{0,1\}
be measurable functions that are symmetric in the arguments from
\mathbb{X}^{k}
. Further, let
\eta
be a Poisson process on
\mathbb{X}
with a locally finite intensity measure
\mathbf{K}
. Denoting by
\eta^{k}_{\neq}
the collection of
k
-tuples of distinct points of
\eta
, we are interested in the point process

\displaystyle\xi:=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\eta^{k}_{\neq}}g(\mathbf{x},\eta)\delta_{f(\mathbf{x},\eta)},

(2.1)

whose intensity measure is denoted by
\mathbf{L}
. Since
\xi
depends on the underlying Poisson process
\eta
, we occasionally write
\xi(\,\cdot\,;\eta)
whenever we would like to emphasise the role of
\eta
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be the set of closed subsets of
\mathbb{Y}
equipped with the Borel
\sigma
-field generated by the Fell topology, see
[
24
, Chapter 2]
. Assume that there exists a measurable symmetric map
S:\mathbb{X}^{k}\to\mathcal{F}
such that, for all
\omega\in\mathbf{N}_{\mathbb{X}}
and
\mathbf{x}\in\omega^{k}_{\neq}
,

\mathbf{x}\in S_{\mathbf{x}}^{k},

(2.2)

g(\mathbf{x},\omega)=g(\mathbf{x},\omega|_{S_{\mathbf{x}}}),

(2.3)

and

f(\mathbf{x},\omega)=f(\mathbf{x},\omega|_{S_{\mathbf{x}}})\quad\text{if}\quad g(\mathbf{x},\omega)=1,

(2.4)

where
S_{\mathbf{x}}:=S(\mathbf{x})
and
\omega|_{S_{\mathbf{x}}}
denotes the restriction of
\omega
to
S_{\mathbf{x}}
. Here and in what follows, we write
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
for a
k
-tuple of points of
\mathbb{X}
and use the short hand notation

\delta_{\mathbf{x}}{:=}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\delta_{x_{i}}.

The following result is a simplified version of
[
3
, Theorem 5.1]
adapted to our situation.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\xi
be the point process defined by (
2.1
), and assume that
\mathbf{L}(\mathbb{Y})<\infty
, (
2.2
), (
2.3
), and (
2.4
) are satisfied. Let
\zeta
be a Poisson process on
\mathbb{Y}
with a finite intensity measure
\mathbf{M}
, and define

\displaystyle E_{2}

\displaystyle:=\frac{2}{(k!)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{k}}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{k}}\mathbbm{1}\{S_{\mathbf{x}}\cap S_{\mathbf{z}}\neq\varnothing\}\mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{x},\eta+\delta_{\mathbf{x}})]\mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{z},\eta+\delta_{\mathbf{z}})]\,\mathbf{K}^{k}(\textup{d}\mathbf{z})\,\mathbf{K}^{k}(\textup{d}\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle E_{3}

\displaystyle:=\frac{2}{(k!)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{k}}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{k}}\mathbbm{1}\{S_{\mathbf{x}}\cap S_{\mathbf{z}}\neq\varnothing\}\mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{x},\eta+\delta_{\mathbf{x}}+\delta_{\mathbf{z}})g(\mathbf{z},\eta+\delta_{\mathbf{x}}+\delta_{\mathbf{z}})]\,\mathbf{K}^{k}(\textup{d}\mathbf{z})\,\mathbf{K}^{k}(\textup{d}\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle E_{4}

\displaystyle:=\frac{2}{k!}\sum_{\varnothing\subsetneq I\subsetneq\{1,\ldots,k\}}\frac{1}{(k-|I|)!}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{k}}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{k-|I|}}\mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{x},\eta+\delta_{\mathbf{x}}+\delta_{\mathbf{z}})g((\mathbf{x}_{I},\mathbf{z}),\eta+\delta_{\mathbf{x}}+\delta_{\mathbf{z}})]\,\mathbf{K}^{k-|I|}(\textup{d}\mathbf{z})\,\mathbf{K}^{k}(\textup{d}\mathbf{x})

with
(\mathbf{x}_{I},\mathbf{z}):=(x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{m}},z_{1},\ldots,z_{k-m})
for
I=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}\}
.
Then,

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{KR}}(\xi,\zeta)\leq\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(\mathbf{L},\mathbf{M})+E_{2}+E_{3}+E_{4}.

Analogously to (
1.2
), we define for non-negative integer-valued random variables
X
and
Y
the
total variation distance

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(X,Y)=\sup_{A\subseteq\mathbb{N}_{0}}|\mathbb{P}(X\in A)-\mathbb{P}(Y\in A)|

with
\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. We will also use the following Poisson approximation result, whose proof is a combination of the arguments from the proof of Theorem
2.1
in
[
3
]
and the Chen–Stein method for Poisson approximation, for which we refer to
[
2
,
21
]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\xi
be the point process defined by (
2.1
), and assume that
\mathbf{L}(\mathbb{Y})<\infty
, (
2.2
), (
2.3
), and (
2.4
) are satisfied. Further, let
Y
be a Poisson random variable with parameter
a\in(0,\infty)
. Then,

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(\xi(\mathbb{Y}),Y)\leq\min\{1,1/\sqrt{a}\}|\mathbb{E}[\xi(\mathbb{Y})]-a|+\frac{\min\{1,1/a\}}{2}(E_{2}+E_{3}+E_{4})

with
E_{2}
,
E_{3}
, and
E_{4}
as in Theorem
2.1
.

Remark 2.3
.

The upper bound in Theorem
2.1
also dominates the total variation distance
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(\xi(\mathbb{Y}),Y)
considered in Theorem
2.2
. However, the bound in Theorem
2.2
is much stronger for large values of
a
due to the prefactors
\min\{1,1/\sqrt{a}\}
and
\min\{1,1/a\}/2
. Because of the counterintuitive behaviour to be smaller the larger the parameter
a
of the Poisson distribution is, such factors are called magic factors.

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

Our proof relies on the Chen–Stein method for Poisson approximation. For a function
h:\mathbb{N}_{0}\to\mathbb{R}
we put

\Delta h(k):=h(k+1)-h(k)\quad\text{for}\quad k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|h\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}|h(k)|,

and let
\mathcal{H}_{a}
be the set of all functions
h:\mathbb{N}_{0}\to\mathbb{R}
for which

\|h\|_{\infty}\leq\min\{1,1/\sqrt{a}\}\quad\text{and}\quad\|\Delta h\|_{\infty}\leq\min\{1,1/a\}.

(2.5)

Moreover, for
m,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
we have

|h(m)-h(n)|\leq\|\Delta h\|_{\infty}|m-n|.

(2.6)

From
[
21
, Theorem 4.5]
it follows that

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(\xi(\mathbb{Y}),Y)\leq\sup_{h\in\mathcal{H}_{a}}|\mathbb{E}[a\,h(\xi(\mathbb{Y})+1)-\xi(\mathbb{Y})h(\xi(\mathbb{Y}))]|.

(2.7)

In the following we consider the right-hand side for a fixed function
h\in\mathcal{H}_{a}
. First note that

\big|\mathbb{E}[a\,h(\xi(\mathbb{Y})+1)-\mathbb{E}[\xi(\mathbb{Y})]\mathbb{E}[h(\xi(\mathbb{Y})+1)]\big|\leq\|h\|_{\infty}|\mathbb{E}[\xi(\mathbb{Y})]-a|\leq\min\{1,1/\sqrt{a}\}|\mathbb{E}[\xi(\mathbb{Y})]-a|

(2.8)

by (
2.5
).
From the multivariate Mecke formula for Poisson processes
[
15
, Theorem 4.4]
we obtain

\mathbb{E}[\xi(\mathbb{Y})h(\xi(\mathbb{Y}))]=\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\eta^{k}_{\neq}}g(\mathbf{x},\eta)h(\xi(\mathbb{Y};\eta)=\frac{1}{k!}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{k}}\mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{x},\eta+\delta_{\mathbf{x}})h(\xi(\mathbb{Y};\eta+\delta_{\mathbf{x}}))]\,\mathbf{K}^{k}(\textup{d}\mathbf{x}),

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\xi(\mathbb{Y})]\mathbb{E}[h(\xi(\mathbb{Y})+1)]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\,\mathbb{E}\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\eta^{k}_{\neq}}g(\mathbf{x},\eta)\;\mathbb{E}[h(\xi(\mathbb{Y})+1)]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{k}}\mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{x},\eta+\delta_{\mathbf{x}})]\mathbb{E}[h(\xi(\mathbb{Y})+1)]\,\mathbf{K}^{k}(\textup{d}\mathbf{x}).

Combining this with (
2.8
) shows that

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}[a\,h(\xi(\mathbb{Y})+1)-\xi(\mathbb{Y})h(\xi(\mathbb{Y}))]|

\displaystyle\leq|\mathbb{E}[a\,h(\xi(\mathbb{Y})+1)-\mathbb{E}[\xi(\mathbb{Y})]\mathbb{E}[h(\xi(\mathbb{Y})+1)]|+|\mathbb{E}[\xi(\mathbb{Y})]\mathbb{E}[h(\xi(\mathbb{Y})+1)]-\mathbb{E}[\xi(\mathbb{Y})h(\xi(\mathbb{Y}))]|

\displaystyle\leq\min\{1,1/\sqrt{a}\}|\mathbb{E}[\xi(\mathbb{Y})]-a|

\displaystyle\quad+\bigg|\frac{1}{k!}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{k}}\mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{x},\eta+\delta_{\mathbf{x}})]\mathbb{E}[h(\xi(\mathbb{Y})+1)]-\mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{x},\eta+\delta_{\mathbf{x}})h(\xi(\mathbb{Y};\eta+\delta_{\mathbf{x}}))]\,\mathbf{K}^{k}(\textup{d}\mathbf{x})\bigg|.

(2.9)

Next, for
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{X}^{k}
we define

Z_{\mathbf{x}}:=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{\mathbf{z}\in\eta^{k}_{\neq}:S_{\mathbf{x}}\cap S_{\mathbf{z}}=\varnothing}g(\mathbf{z},\eta).

From (
2.6
) and the multivariate Mecke formula it follows that

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}[h(\xi(\mathbb{Y})+1)]-\mathbb{E}[h(Z_{\mathbf{x}}+1)]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|\Delta h\|_{\infty}\mathbb{E}[|\xi(\mathbb{Y})-Z_{\mathbf{x}}|]

\displaystyle\leq{\|\Delta h\|_{\infty}\over k!}\,\mathbb{E}\sum_{\mathbf{z}\in\eta^{k}_{\neq}:S_{\mathbf{x}}\cap S_{\mathbf{z}}\neq\varnothing}g(\mathbf{z},\eta)

\displaystyle={\|\Delta h\|_{\infty}\over k!}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{k}}\mathbbm{1}\{S_{\mathbf{x}}\cap S_{\mathbf{z}}\neq\varnothing\}\mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{z},\eta+\delta_{\mathbf{z}})]\,\mathbf{K}^{k}(\textup{d}\mathbf{z}).

This implies that

\displaystyle R_{1}

\displaystyle:=\bigg|{1\over k!}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{k}}\mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{x},\eta+\delta_{\mathbf{x}})]\,\big(\mathbb{E}[h(\xi(\mathbb{Y})+1)]-\mathbb{E}[h(Z_{\mathbf{x}}+1)]\big)\,\mathbf{K}^{k}(\textup{d}\mathbf{x})\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|\Delta h\|_{\infty}}{(k!)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{k}}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{k}}\mathbbm{1}\{S_{\mathbf{x}}\cap S_{\mathbf{z}}\neq\varnothing\}\mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{x},\eta+\delta_{\mathbf{x}})]\mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{z},\eta+\delta_{\mathbf{z}})]\,\mathbf{K}^{k}(\textup{d}\mathbf{z})\,\mathbf{K}^{k}(\textup{d}\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\min\{1,1/a\}}{2}E_{2}

(2.10)

by (
2.5
) and the definition of
E_{2}
.
For
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{X}^{k}
,
g(\mathbf{x},\eta+\delta_{\mathbf{x}})
and
Z_{\mathbf{x}}
depend only on
\eta|_{S_{\mathbf{x}}}
and
\eta|_{S_{\mathbf{x}}^{c}}
by (
2.3
), respectively. By the independence property of Poisson processes,
g(\mathbf{x},\eta+\delta_{\mathbf{x}})
and
Z_{\mathbf{x}}
are independent random variables.
It follows that
\mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{x},\eta+\delta_{\mathbf{x}})]\mathbb{E}[h(Z_{\mathbf{x}}+1)]=\mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{x},\eta+\delta_{\mathbf{x}})h(Z_{\mathbf{x}}+1)]
and hence

\frac{1}{k!}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{k}}\mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{x},\eta+\delta_{\mathbf{x}})]\mathbb{E}[h(Z_{\mathbf{x}}+1)]\,\mathbf{K}^{k}(\textup{d}\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{k!}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{k}}\mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{x},\eta+\delta_{\mathbf{x}})h(Z_{\mathbf{x}}+1)]\,\mathbf{K}^{k}(\textup{d}\mathbf{x}).

(2.11)

Moreover, we define

\displaystyle\widehat{Z}_{\mathbf{x}}:=

\displaystyle\xi(\mathbb{Y};\eta+\delta_{\mathbf{x}})-g(\mathbf{x},\eta+\delta_{\mathbf{x}})-\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{\mathbf{z}\in\eta^{k}_{\neq}}g(\mathbf{z},\eta+\delta_{\mathbf{x}})=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{\mathbf{z}\in(\eta+\delta_{\mathbf{x}})^{k}_{\neq}\setminus(\eta_{\neq}^{k}\cup(\delta_{\mathbf{x}})^{k}_{\neq})}g(\mathbf{z},\eta+\delta_{\mathbf{x}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\varnothing\subsetneq I\subsetneq\{1,\ldots,k\}}\frac{1}{(k-|I|)!}\sum_{\mathbf{z}\in\eta_{\neq}^{k-|I|}}g((\mathbf{x}_{I},\mathbf{z}),\eta+\delta_{\mathbf{x}})

for
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{X}^{k}
. Using this notation as well as the fact that, by (
2.2
) and (
2.3
),

\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{\mathbf{z}\in\eta^{k}_{\neq}:S_{\mathbf{x}}\cap S_{\mathbf{z}}=\varnothing}g(\mathbf{z},\eta)=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{\mathbf{z}\in\eta^{k}_{\neq}:S_{\mathbf{x}}\cap S_{\mathbf{z}}=\varnothing}g(\mathbf{z},\eta+\delta_{\mathbf{x}}),

we obtain

\displaystyle|\xi(\mathbb{Y};\eta+\delta_{\mathbf{x}})-(Z_{\mathbf{x}}+1)|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\xi(\mathbb{Y};\eta+\delta_{\mathbf{x}})-\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{\mathbf{z}\in\eta^{k}_{\neq}:S_{\mathbf{x}}\cap S_{\mathbf{z}}=\varnothing}g(\mathbf{z},\eta)-1\bigg|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\widehat{Z}_{\mathbf{x}}+g(\mathbf{x},\eta+\delta_{\mathbf{x}})+\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{\mathbf{z}\in\eta^{k}_{\neq}}g(\mathbf{z},\eta+\delta_{\mathbf{x}})-\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{\mathbf{z}\in\eta^{k}_{\neq}:S_{\mathbf{x}}\cap S_{\mathbf{z}}=\varnothing}g(\mathbf{z},\eta+\delta_{\mathbf{x}})-1\bigg|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\widehat{Z}_{\mathbf{x}}+\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{\mathbf{z}\in\eta^{k}_{\neq}:S_{\mathbf{x}}\cap S_{\mathbf{z}}\neq\varnothing}g(\mathbf{z},\eta+\delta_{\mathbf{x}})+g(\mathbf{x},\eta+\delta_{\mathbf{x}})-1\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\widehat{Z}_{\mathbf{x}}+\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{\mathbf{z}\in\eta^{k}_{\neq}:S_{\mathbf{x}}\cap S_{\mathbf{z}}\neq\varnothing}g(\mathbf{z},\eta+\delta_{\mathbf{x}})+|g(\mathbf{x},\eta+\delta_{\mathbf{x}})-1|.

It follows, using (
2.6
), that

\displaystyle R_{2}

\displaystyle:=\bigg|\frac{1}{k!}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{k}}\mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{x},\eta+\delta_{\mathbf{x}})h(\xi(\mathbb{Y};\eta+\delta_{\mathbf{x}}))]\,\mathbf{K}^{k}(\textup{d}\mathbf{x})-\frac{1}{k!}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{k}}\mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{x},\eta+\delta_{\mathbf{x}})h(Z_{\mathbf{x}}+1)]\,\mathbf{K}^{k}(\textup{d}\mathbf{x})\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|\Delta h\|_{\infty}}{k!}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{k}}\mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{x},\eta+\delta_{\mathbf{x}})|\xi(\mathbb{Y};\eta+\delta_{\mathbf{x}})-(Z_{\mathbf{x}}+1)|]\,\mathbf{K}^{k}(\textup{d}\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|\Delta h\|_{\infty}}{k!}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{k}}\mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{x},\eta+\delta_{\mathbf{x}})\widehat{Z}_{\mathbf{x}}]\,\mathbf{K}^{k}(\textup{d}\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\|\Delta h\|_{\infty}}{(k!)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{k}}\mathbb{E}\bigg[g(\mathbf{x},\eta+\delta_{\mathbf{x}})\sum_{\mathbf{z}\in\eta^{k}_{\neq}:S_{\mathbf{x}}\cap S_{\mathbf{z}}\neq\varnothing}g(\mathbf{z},\eta+\delta_{\mathbf{x}})\bigg]\,\mathbf{K}^{k}(\textup{d}\mathbf{x}).

In the last step, we used the fact that
g(\mathbf{x},\eta+\delta_{\mathbf{x}})|g(\mathbf{x},\eta+\delta_{\mathbf{x}})-1|=0
as the function
g
can only take the values
0
and
1
. Applying the multivariate Mecke formula once again, together with the definition of
\widehat{Z}_{\mathbf{x}}
, we derive

R_{2}\leq\frac{\min\{1,1/a\}}{2}(E_{3}+E_{4})

(2.12)

from (
2.5
) and the definitions of
E_{3}
and
E_{4}
. From (
2.9
) together with the definitions of
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
and (
2.11
) we derive

|\mathbb{E}[a\,h(\xi(\mathbb{Y})+1)-\xi(\mathbb{Y})h(\xi(\mathbb{Y}))]|\leq\min\{1,1/\sqrt{a}\}|\mathbb{E}[\xi(\mathbb{Y})]-a|+R_{1}+R_{2}.

Now combining (
2.7
) with (
2
) and (
2.12
) completes the proof.
∎

3
Longest edges in general empty region graphs

3.1
Results

The goal of this section is to derive general versions of Theorems
1.9
and
1.10
, which make some abstract assumptions on the sets
(S(x,y))_{x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
. In the next section, we will then verify these assumptions for Examples
1.1
–
1.8
, thus establishing Theorems
1.9
and
1.10
. Recall that we write
\mathbb{L}^{d}
for the space of lines in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mathrm{span}\,B
for the linear hull of a set
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, while
\nu
denotes the rotation-invariant Haar probability measure on
\mathbb{L}^{d}
and
\mu
is the measure on
\mathbb{R}
such that
\mu([a,\infty))=e^{-a}
for all
a\in\mathbb{R}
. We equip the closed subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with the Borel
\sigma
-field generated by the Fell topology as in Section
2
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\eta_{t}
be a stationary Poisson process on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with intensity
t\geq 2
. To each pair of distinct points
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we associate a compact set
S(x,y)
such that
(x,y)\mapsto S(x,y)
is measurable and the following assumptions for pairs of distinct points
(x,y),(u,v)\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}
are satisfied:

(i)

\mathrm{vol}(S(x,y))=\gamma\|x-y\|^{d}
for some
\gamma\in(0,\infty)
,

(ii)

S(x,y)\subseteq B^{d}({x+y\over 2},\alpha\|x-y\|)
for some
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},\infty)
,

(iii)

\mathrm{vol}(S(x,y)\setminus S(u,v))\geq\beta\|{x+y\over 2}-{u+v\over 2}\|\|x-y\|^{d-1}
for some
\beta\in(0,\infty)
if
\|x-y\|\geq\|u-v\|
and
S(x,y)\cap S(u,v)\neq\varnothing
,

(iv)

S(x,y)=S(y,x)
.

Define the point process

\xi_{t}:={1\over 2}\sum_{(x,y)\in\eta_{t,\neq}^{2}}\mathbbm{1}\{(\eta_{t}-\delta_{x}-\delta_{y})(S(x,y))=0\}\delta_{\big({x+y\over 2},\,\gamma t\|x-y\|^{d}-\log(t)-\log(\kappa_{d}/(2\gamma)),\,\mathrm{span}\{x-y\}\big)}

on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{L}^{d}
, denote by
\zeta
a Poisson process on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{L}^{d}
with intensity measure
\mathrm{vol}\otimes\mu\otimes\nu
, and let
W\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\mathrm{vol}(W)<\infty
and
b\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, for any
t\geq 2
,

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{KR}}(\xi_{t}|_{W\times[b,\infty)\times\mathbb{L}^{d}},\zeta|_{W\times[b,\infty)\times\mathbb{L}^{d}})\leq\begin{cases}c\log^{-(d-2)}(t),&d\geq 3,\\
c\log^{-1/2}(t),&d=2,\end{cases}

(3.1)

where
c\in(0,\infty)
is a constant only depending on
d
,
\mathrm{vol}(W)
,
b
,
\alpha
,
\beta
, and
\gamma
. Moreover, one has
\xi_{t}\overset{d}{\longrightarrow}\zeta
as
t\to\infty
.

Using the Poisson approximation result Theorem
2.2
, we can also derive the following quantitative bound for the Gumbel approximation of the length of the longest edge with midpoint in an observation window for an empty region graph based on sets
S(x,y)_{x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
satisfying the assumptions of the previous theorem.

Theorem 3.2
.

Assume the same set-up as in Theorem
3.1
and denote by
L_{t,W}
the length of the longest edge with midpoint in
W
, i.e.,

L_{t,W}:=\max\Big\{\|x-y\|:(x,y)\in\eta_{t,\neq}^{2},(\eta_{t}-\delta_{x}-\delta_{y})(S(x,y))=0,{x+y\over 2}\in W\Big\}.

Then, for any
t\geq 2
,

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{Kol}}\big(\gamma tL_{t,W}^{d}-\log(t)-\log(\kappa_{d}/(2\gamma)),G_{W}\big)\leq\begin{cases}c\log^{-(d-2)}(t),&d\geq 3,\\
c\log^{-1/2}(t),&d=2,\end{cases}

where
c
is a constant only depending on
d
,
\mathrm{vol}(W)
,
\alpha
,
\beta
, and
\gamma
, and
G_{W}
is a Gumbel distributed random variable with distribution function
\mathbb{P}(G_{W}\leq s)=\exp(-\mathrm{vol}(W)e^{-s})
for
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

The proofs of Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
3.2
will be given in Section
3.3
below after having developed some preparatory estimates, which are the content of the next section.

3.2
Preparatory estimates

The core of the proof of Theorem
3.1
is an application of Theorem
2.1
, while Theorem
3.2
will be obtained using Theorem
2.2
. In particular, we need to find suitable estimates for the quantities
E_{2}
,
E_{3}
, and
E_{4}
appearing therein. To start, we determine the intensity measure of the point process
\xi_{t}
. Throughout the proofs, we use the abbreviation
\tau:=\log(\kappa_{d}/(2\gamma))
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let the assumptions of Theorem
3.1
be satisfied. For all
t\geq 2
the intensity measure of
\xi_{t}
is given by

\mathbf{M}_{t}(U):=\mathbb{E}[\xi_{t}(U)]=(\mathrm{vol}\otimes\mu|_{[-\log(t)-\tau,\infty)}\otimes\nu)(U)

for
U\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{L}^{d})
.

Proof.

Let
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\mathrm{vol}(A)<\infty
, let
D\subseteq\mathbb{L}^{d}
be measurable, and let
a\in\mathbb{R}
. By the multivariate Mecke formula, the void probability of a stationary Poisson process, and assumption (i) of Theorem
3.1
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\xi_{t}(A\times[a,\infty)\times D)]

\displaystyle=\frac{t^{2}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{P}(\eta_{t}(S(x,y))=0)\mathbbm{1}\Big\{\frac{x+y}{2}\in A,\gamma t\lVert x-y\rVert^{d}-\log(t)-\tau\geq a,\mathrm{span}\{x-y\}\in D\Big\}\,\textup{d}x\,\textup{d}y

\displaystyle=\frac{t^{2}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-t\mathrm{vol}(S(x,y))}\mathbbm{1}\Big\{\frac{x+y}{2}\in A,\gamma t\lVert x-y\rVert^{d}-\log(t)-\tau\geq a,\mathrm{span}\{x-y\}\in D\Big\}\,\textup{d}x\,\textup{d}y

\displaystyle=\frac{t^{2}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\gamma t\|x-y\|^{d}}\mathbbm{1}\Big\{\frac{x+y}{2}\in A,\gamma t\lVert x-y\rVert^{d}-\log(t)-\tau\geq a,\mathrm{span}\{x-y\}\in D\Big\}\,\textup{d}x\,\textup{d}y.

Now the substitution
z=(x+y)/2
and
u=x-y
leads to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\xi_{t}(A\times[a,\infty)\times D)]

\displaystyle=\frac{t^{2}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\gamma t\|u\|^{d}}\mathbbm{1}\Big\{z\in A,\gamma t\lVert u\rVert^{d}-\log(t)-\tau\geq a,\mathrm{span}\{u\}\in D\Big\}\,\textup{d}z\,\textup{d}u

\displaystyle=\frac{t^{2}\mathrm{vol}(A)}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\gamma t\lVert u\rVert^{d}}\mathbbm{1}\bigg\{\lVert u\rVert^{d}\geq\max\bigg\{\frac{\log(t)+\tau+a}{\gamma t},0\bigg\},\mathrm{span}\{u\}\in D\bigg\}\,\textup{d}u.

Using spherical coordinates and the abbreviation

a_{t}:=\max\Big\{\frac{\log(t)+\tau+a}{\gamma t},0\Big\}^{1/d},

we obtain

\mathbb{E}[\xi_{t}(A\times[a,\infty)\times D)]=\frac{t^{2}\mathrm{vol}(A)}{2}d\kappa_{d}\nu(D)\int_{a_{t}}^{\infty}e^{-t\gamma r^{d}}r^{d-1}\,\textup{d}r=\frac{t\mathrm{vol}(A)\nu(D)\kappa_{d}}{2\gamma}e^{-t\gamma a_{t}^{d}}.

If
a_{t}>0
, we have

\frac{\kappa_{d}t}{2\gamma}e^{-t\gamma a_{t}^{d}}=\frac{\kappa_{d}t}{2\gamma}e^{-(\log(t)+\tau+a)}=\frac{\kappa_{d}}{2\gamma}\frac{2\gamma}{\kappa_{d}}e^{-a}=e^{-a}

so that

\mathbb{E}[\xi_{t}(A\times[a,\infty)\times D)]=\begin{cases}\mathrm{vol}(A)\nu(D)e^{-a},&a>-\log(t)-\tau,\\
\frac{\kappa_{d}\mathrm{vol}(A)\nu(D)t}{2\gamma},&a\leq-\log(t)-\tau.\end{cases}

This can be rewritten as

\mathbb{E}[\xi_{t}(A\times[a,\infty)\times D)]=\mathrm{vol}(A)\nu(D)\mu|_{[-\log(t)-\tau,\infty)}([a,\infty)).

As the measure
\mathbf{M}_{t}
is determined by the sets of the form
A\times[a,\infty)\times D
, this proves the formula for
\mathbf{M}_{t}
.
∎

so that

\displaystyle E_{4}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq 4t^{3}\mathrm{vol}(W)M_{d-1}(d\kappa_{d})^{2}\int_{[0,\infty)^{2}}\mathbbm{1}\{s\geq b_{t}\}e^{-t\gamma s^{d}}s^{d-1}e^{-t\beta rs^{d-1}/4}\frac{2^{d-1}}{(t\beta s^{d-1})^{d-1}}\,\textup{d}(r,s)

\displaystyle=4t^{3}\mathrm{vol}(W)M_{d-1}(d\kappa_{d})^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbbm{1}\{s\geq b_{t}\}e^{-t\gamma s^{d}}s^{d-1}\frac{2^{d+1}}{(t\beta s^{d-1})^{d}}\,\textup{d}s

\displaystyle{\leq\frac{2^{d+3}t^{3-d}\mathrm{vol}(W)M_{d-1}(d\kappa_{d})^{2}}{\beta^{d}b_{t}^{d(d-1)}}\int_{b_{t}}^{\infty}e^{-t\gamma s^{d}}s^{d-1}\,\textup{d}s.}

The last integral has already been computed in (
3.2
) and equals
\frac{2e^{-b}}{t^{2}d\kappa_{d}}
. Plugging this in yields

E_{4}^{\prime}\leq\frac{2^{d+3}t^{2}\mathrm{vol}(W)M_{d-1}(d\kappa_{d})^{2}}{\beta^{d}(tb_{t}^{d})^{(d-1)}}\cdot\frac{2e^{-b}}{t^{2}d\kappa_{d}}=\frac{2^{d+4}M_{d-1}d\kappa_{d}\mathrm{vol}(W)e^{-b}}{{\beta^{d}((b+\log(t)+\tau)/\gamma)^{d-1}}}=\frac{{C_{4,1}}}{e^{b}(b+\log(t)+\tau)^{d-1}}.

Using the same substitution as above, spherical coordinates, and (
3.2
) leads to

\displaystyle E_{4}^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle\leq 4t^{3}\mathrm{vol}(W)\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}}\mathbbm{1}\{\|u\|\geq\lVert v\rVert\geq b_{t}\}{e^{-t\gamma(\|u\|^{d}+\|v\|^{d})}}\,\textup{d}(u,v)

\displaystyle\leq 4t^{3}\mathrm{vol}(W)\bigg(d\kappa_{d}\int_{b_{t}}^{\infty}e^{\gamma ts^{d}}s^{d-1}\,\textup{d}s\bigg)^{2}=\frac{4t^{3}\mathrm{vol}(W)(d\kappa_{d})^{2}(2e^{-b})^{2}}{(d\kappa_{d}t^{2})^{2}}=\frac{16\mathrm{vol}(W)e^{-2b}}{t}{=\frac{C_{4,2}}{e^{2b}t}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

3.3
Proofs of Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
3.2

The proof of Theorem
3.1
now follows immediately by combining the bounds derived in the previous section with the Poisson process approximation result in Theorem
2.1
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Let

. We first assume that
t>\frac{2\gamma}{\kappa_{d}}e^{-b}
, which implies
b>{\log(\frac{2\gamma}{t\kappa_{d}})=-\log(t)}-\tau

\mathbf{M}_{t}|_{W\times[b,\infty)\times\mathbb{L}^{d}}=\mathrm{vol}\otimes\mu\otimes\nu|_{W\times[b,\infty)\times\mathbb{L}^{d}},

which follows from Lemma
3.3
, and the estimates provided in Lemma
3.4
into Theorem
2.2
yields

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{KR}}(\xi_{t}|_{W\times[b,\infty)\times\mathbb{L}^{d}},\zeta|_{W\times[b,\infty)\times\mathbb{L}^{d}})

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\frac{1+b+\log(t)+\tau}{e^{2b}t}+\frac{C_{3}}{e^{b}(b+\log(t)+\tau)^{\max\{d-2,1/2\}}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{C_{4,1}}{e^{b}(b+\log(t)+\tau)^{d-1}}+\frac{C_{4,2}}{e^{2b}t}

with the constants
C_{2}
,
C_{3}
,
C_{4,1}
, and

from Lemma

. The result of Theorem

thus follows for

. In case that

, which yields
b\leq-{\log(t)}-\tau
, the definition of the Kantorovich–Rubinstein distance and Lemma
3.3
lead to

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{KR}}(\xi_{t}|_{W\times[b,\infty)\times\mathbb{L}^{d}},\zeta|_{W\times[b,\infty)\times\mathbb{L}^{d}})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[\xi_{t}(W\times[b,\infty)\times\mathbb{L}^{d})]+\mathbb{E}[\zeta(W\times[b,\infty)\times\mathbb{L}^{d})]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{vol}(W)\mu([-\log(t)-\tau,\infty))+\mathrm{vol}(W)\mu([b,\infty))

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathrm{vol}(W)\mu([b,\infty))=2\mathrm{vol}(W)e^{-b}.

This allows to choose the constant in (
3.1
) such that the statement is valid for all

. Since convergence in Kantorovich–Rubinstein distance implies convergence in distribution (see

, Proposition 2.1]
), this yields
\xi_{t}|_{W\times[b,\infty)\times\mathbb{L}^{d}}\overset{d}{\longrightarrow}\zeta|_{W\times[b,\infty)\times\mathbb{L}^{d}}

, one has
\xi_{t}\overset{d}{\longrightarrow}\zeta
as

, which completes the proof.
∎

Similarly, Theorem
3.2
follows from Lemma
3.3
, Lemma
3.4
, and Theorem
2.2
.

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

We start by noting that, for any

,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\gamma tL_{t,W}^{d}-\log(t)-\tau\leq s)-\mathbb{P}(G_{W}\leq s)=\mathbb{P}(\xi_{t}(W\times{(s,\infty)}\times\mathbb{L}^{d})=0)-\mathbb{P}(Y=0),

where

is a Poisson distributed random variable with parameter
\mathrm{vol}(W)e^{-s}
. Thus, by Theorem
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle|\mathbb{P}(\gamma tL_{t,W}^{d}-\log(t)-\tau\leq s)-\mathbb{P}(G_{W}\leq s)|

\displaystyle\leq\min\bigg\{1,\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathrm{vol}(W)e^{-s}}}\bigg\}|\mathbb{E}[\xi_{t}(W\times{(s,\infty)}\times\mathbb{L}^{d})]-\mathrm{vol}(W)e^{-s}|

\displaystyle\quad+\min\bigg\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2\mathrm{vol}(W)e^{-s}}\bigg\}(E_{2}+E_{2}+E_{4})

\displaystyle=\min\bigg\{1,\frac{e^{s/2}}{\sqrt{\mathrm{vol}(W)}}\bigg\}|\mathbb{E}[\xi_{t}(W\times{(s,\infty)}\times\mathbb{L}^{d})]-\mathrm{vol}(W)e^{-s}|+\min\bigg\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{e^{s}}{2\mathrm{vol}(W)}\bigg\}(E_{2}+E_{2}+E_{4}).

For
s\geq-\log(t)/4-\tau
, we use Lemma
3.3
and Lemma
3.4
to conclude that

|\mathbb{E}[\xi_{t}(W\times{(s,\infty)}\times\mathbb{L}^{d})]-\mathrm{vol}(W)e^{-s}|=0

and

\displaystyle E_{2}\leq C^{\prime}_{2}\mathrm{vol}(W)\frac{1+s+\log(t)+\tau}{e^{2s}t},\quad E_{3}\leq\frac{C^{\prime}_{3}\mathrm{vol}(W)}{e^{s}(s+\log(t)+\tau)^{\max\{d-2,1/2\}}},

\displaystyle E_{4}\leq\frac{C^{\prime}_{4}\mathrm{vol}(W)}{e^{s}(s+\log(t)+\tau)^{d-1}}+\frac{16\mathrm{vol}(W)}{e^{2s}t}

with constants
C^{\prime}_{2},C^{\prime}_{3},C^{\prime}_{4}\in(0,\infty)
only depending on
d
,
\alpha
,
\beta
, and
\gamma
. Hence, one obtains

\displaystyle\frac{e^{s}\,E_{2}}{\mathrm{vol}(W)}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}_{2}\frac{1+s+\log(t)+\tau}{e^{s}t}\leq C^{\prime}_{2}\Big(\max_{u\geq 0}{u\over e^{u}}\Big){1\over t}+C^{\prime}_{2}{\log(t)+1+\tau\over e^{-\log(t)/4-\tau}\,t}\leq C^{\prime}_{5}\Big({1\over t}+{\log(t)\over t^{3/4}}\Big),

\displaystyle\frac{e^{s}\,E_{3}}{\mathrm{vol}(W)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C^{\prime}_{3}}{(s+\log(t)+\tau)^{\max\{d-2,1/2\}}}\leq{C^{\prime}_{3}\over({3\over 4}\log(t))^{\max\{d-2,1/2\}}},

\displaystyle\frac{e^{s}\,E_{4}}{\mathrm{vol}(W)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C^{\prime}_{4}}{(s+\log(t)+\tau)^{d-1}}+\frac{16}{e^{s}t}\leq{C^{\prime}_{4}\over({3\over 4}\log(t))^{d-1}}+\frac{16}{e^{-\tau}t^{3/4}}

with a suitable constant

depending on the same parameters as

. It follows that

\sup_{s\geq-\log(t)/4-\tau}|\mathbb{P}(\gamma tL_{t,W}^{d}-\log(t)-\tau\leq s)-\mathbb{P}(G_{W}\leq s)|\leq{C^{\prime}_{6}\over{\log^{\max\{d-2,1/2\}}(t)}}

(3.5)

for all

, where
C^{\prime}_{6}\in(0,\infty)
is a constant only depending on
d
,
\alpha
,
\beta
, and
\gamma
.

To deal with the complementary case

note that

\sup_{s<-\log(t)/4-\tau}\mathbb{P}(G_{W}\leq s)=\mathbb{P}(G_{W}\leq-\log(t)/4-\tau)=\exp(-\mathrm{vol}(W)e^{\log(t)/4+\tau})=\exp(-\mathrm{vol}(W)e^{\tau}\,t^{1/4})

and

\displaystyle\sup_{s<-\log(t)/4-\tau}\mathbb{P}(\gamma tL_{t,W}^{d}-\log(t)-\tau\leq s)=\mathbb{P}\big(\gamma tL_{t,W}^{d}-\log(t)-\tau\leq-\log(t)/4-\tau\big)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}(G_{W}\leq-\log(t)/4-\tau)+|\mathbb{P}\big(\gamma tL_{t,W}^{d}-\log(t)-\tau\leq-\log(t)/4-\tau\big)-\mathbb{P}(G_{W}\leq-\log(t)/4-\tau)|

\displaystyle\leq\exp(-\mathrm{vol}(W)e^{\tau}\,t^{1/4})+{C^{\prime}_{6}\over\log^{\max\{d-2,1/2\}}(t)}

\displaystyle\leq{C^{\prime}_{7}\over\log^{\max\{d-2,1/2\}}(t)}

with another constant

∎

4
Proofs of Theorem
1.9
and Theorem
1.10

To prove both Theorem
1.9
and Theorem
1.10
, we need to verify that assumptions (i)–(iv) of Theorem
3.1
are satisfied. The results then follow from Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
3.2
. The sets
(S(x,y))_{x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
used to construct the empty region graphs in Examples
1.1
–
1.8
fall into two classes:

(S1)

S(x,y)
is of the form

S(x,y)=\frac{x+y}{2}+\lVert x-y\rVert K

(4.1)

for a compact star-shaped set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. This is the case for Example
1.8
.

(S2)

S(x,y)
is of the form

S(x,y)=\frac{x+y}{2}+\lVert x-y\rVert\varrho_{x,y}K

with a compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
(satisfying a number of technical assumptions which will be specified later) and
\varrho_{x,y}
a rotation depending only on
x-y
in such a way that
\varrho_{x,y}=\varrho_{y,x}
. This is the case for Examples
1.1
–
1.7
.

4.1
Sets of class (S1)

If
S(x,y)
is of the form (
4.1
) for a compact star-shaped set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, assumptions (i), (ii), and (iv) are trivially satisfied. To verify assumption (iii), we rely on the for following geometric estimate taken from
[
25
]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
K\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be bounded and let
c>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be such that
\|x\|\leq c\,{\rm diam}(K)
, where
{\rm diam}(K):=\sup\{\|u-v\|:u,v\in K\}
is the diameter of
K
. Then,

\mathrm{vol}(K\setminus(K+x))\geq{1\over c+1}{\mathrm{vol}(K)\over{\rm diam}(K)}\,\|x\|.

Proof.

We start by writing
\mathrm{vol}((K+x)\triangle K)=\mathrm{vol}(K\setminus(K+x))+\mathrm{vol}((K+x)\setminus K)
for the symmetric difference
(K+x)\triangle K
. Moreover, we have
\mathrm{vol}(K\setminus(K+x))=\mathrm{vol}((K+x)\setminus K)
and

\mathrm{vol}((K+x)\triangle K)\geq{2\over c+1}{\mathrm{vol}(K)\over{\rm diam}(K)}\,\|x\|

from
[
25
, Lemma 3.12]
and a slight adaption of the argument there. This proves the claim.
∎

This estimate is now applied to the sets
(S(x,y))_{x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume that
S
is as in (
4.1
) with a compact and star-shaped set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let
c>0
. Let
x,y,u,v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be such that
\lVert x-y\rVert\geq\lVert u-v\rVert
. Then,

\mathrm{vol}(S(x,y)\setminus S(u,v))\geq{1\over c+1}{\mathrm{vol}(K)\over{\rm diam}(K)}\Big\lVert\frac{x+y}{2}-\frac{u+v}{2}\Big\rVert\lVert x-y\rVert^{d-1},

whenever
\lVert\frac{x+y}{2}-\frac{u+v}{2}\rVert\leq c\,{\rm diam}(K)\lVert x-y\rVert
.

Proof.

Using that
K
is star-shaped, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathrm{vol}(S(x,y)\setminus S(u,v))

\displaystyle\geq\mathrm{vol}\Big(\Big(\frac{x+y}{2}+\lVert x-y\rVert K\Big)\setminus\Big(\frac{u+v}{2}+\lVert x-y\rVert K\Big)\Big).

Defining
L:=\frac{x+y}{2}+\lVert x-y\rVert K
and
z:=\frac{u+v}{2}-\frac{x+y}{2}
, we can thus write

\mathrm{vol}(S(x,y)\setminus S(u,v))\geq\mathrm{vol}(L\setminus(L+z))\geq{1\over c+1}{\mathrm{vol}(L)\over{\rm diam}(L)}\,\|z\|

according to Lemma
4.1
, whenever
\|z\|\leq c\,{\rm diam}(L)
. Using that
{\rm diam}(L)=\|x-y\|{\rm diam}(K)
and
\mathrm{vol}(L)=\|x-y\|^{d}\,\mathrm{vol}(K)
, the result follows.
∎

Note that
S(x,y)\cap S(u,v)=\varnothing
whenever
\lVert\frac{x+y}{2}-\frac{u+v}{2}\rVert\geq{\rm diam}(K)\lVert x-y\rVert
and
\|x-y\|\geq\|u-v\|
because then the superset

\bigg(\frac{x+y}{2}+\|x-y\|K\bigg)\cap\bigg(\frac{u+v}{2}+\|x-y\|K\bigg)

of the intersection is empty. Thus, choosing
c=1
in the previous lemma verifies assumption (iii) of Theorem
3.1
for Example
1.8
, and eventually completes the proofs of Theorems
1.9
and
1.10
in that case.

4.2
Sets of class (S2)

Our goal is to verify assumptions (i)–(iv) of Theorem
3.1
for Examples
1.1
–
1.7
. Clearly, (i) and (ii) are satisfied as the involved sets
K
are bounded and have positive volume. Moreover, (iv) holds by assumption on the rotations
\varrho_{x,y}
. It remains to verify (iii). For that purpose, we need the following geometric result. Throughout this section, we denote the
(d-1)
-dimensional volume of a subset of a
(d-1)
-dimensional affine subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
also by
\mathrm{vol}
.

Proposition 4.3
.

For
d\in\mathbb{N}
with
d\geq 2
,
u\in(0,\infty)
, and a continuous symmetric function
R:[-u,u]\to[0,\infty)
let

K:=\Big\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:x_{1}\in[-u,u],\sum_{i=2}^{d}x_{i}^{2}\leq R(x_{1})^{2}\Big\}.

Assume that there exist
x_{K}\in K
and
r_{K}>0
such that
B^{d}(x_{K},r_{K})\subset K
. Then one can find a constant
c_{K}\in(0,\infty)
only depending on
K
and
d
such that

\mathrm{vol}(K\setminus(\varrho K+x))\geq c_{K}\|x\|

(4.2)

for all rotations
\varrho
and all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
K\cap(\varrho K+x)\neq\varnothing
.

Proof.

Let a rotation
\varrho
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be given and let
v_{\varrho}:=\varrho e_{1}
with
e_{1}:=(1,0,\ldots,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Moreover, we assume that
v_{\varrho}\neq\pm e_{1}
. We denote by
E_{\varrho}
the linear subspace generated by
e_{1}
and
v_{\varrho}
and define
a_{\varrho,1}:=\frac{e_{1}+v_{\varrho}}{\|e_{1}+v_{\varrho}\|}
and
a_{\varrho,2}:=\frac{e_{1}-v_{\varrho}}{\|e_{1}-v_{\varrho}\|}
, which are orthogonal. By the assumed symmetry properties of
K
,
\varrho K\cap E_{\varrho}
is the mirror image of
K\cap E_{\varrho}
with respect to the lines generated by
a_{1,\varrho}
and
a_{2,\varrho}
in
E_{\varrho}
, which implies that

\mathrm{vol}(K\cap(ta_{i,\varrho}+a_{i,\varrho}^{\perp}))=\mathrm{vol}(\varrho K\cap(ta_{i,\varrho}+a_{i,\varrho}^{\perp}))

(4.3)

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
i\in\{1,2\}
. For
d\geq 3
let
a_{3,\varrho},\ldots,a_{d,\varrho}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be such that
a_{1,\varrho},\ldots,a_{d,\varrho}
form an orthonormal base of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then we have

\mathrm{vol}(K\cap(ta_{i,\varrho}+a_{i,\varrho}^{\perp}))=\mathrm{vol}(\varrho K\cap(ta_{i,\varrho}+a_{i,\varrho}^{\perp}))

(4.4)

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
i\in\{3,\ldots,d\}
because
ta_{i,\varrho}+a_{i,\varrho}^{\perp}
is parallel to the lines generated by
e_{1}
and
v_{\varrho}
, and has the same distance to both of them.

Since
K
and
\varrho K+x
have the same volume, we obtain

\mathrm{vol}(K\setminus(\varrho K+x))=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{vol}(K\triangle(\varrho K+x)).

(4.5)

For
i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
let
H_{i}:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\langle a_{i,\varrho},y\rangle\leq\langle a_{i,\varrho},x_{K}\rangle\}
be the half-space through
x_{K}
whose bounding hyperplane has the normal vector
a_{i,\varrho}
pointing in the outward direction. Note that

\mathrm{vol}(K{\triangle}(\varrho K+x))\geq\big|\mathrm{vol}(K\cap H_{i})-\mathrm{vol}((\varrho K+x)\cap H_{i})\big|.

(4.6)

It follows from Cavalieri’s principle that

\displaystyle\mathrm{vol}(K\cap H_{i})-\mathrm{vol}((\varrho K+x)\cap H_{i})

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\langle a_{i,\varrho},x_{K}\rangle}\mathrm{vol}(K\cap(ta_{i,\varrho}+a_{i,\varrho}^{\perp}))\,\textup{d}t-\int_{-\infty}^{\langle a_{i,\varrho},x_{K}\rangle}\mathrm{vol}((\varrho K+x)\cap(ta_{i,\varrho}+a_{i,\varrho}^{\perp}))\,\textup{d}t.

For
t\in\mathbb{R}
we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{vol}((\varrho K+x)\cap(ta_{i,\varrho}+a_{i,\varrho}^{\perp}))

\displaystyle=\mathrm{vol}(\varrho K\cap(ta_{i,\varrho}-x+a_{i,\varrho}^{\perp}))=\mathrm{vol}(\varrho K\cap((t-\langle a_{i,\varrho},x\rangle)a_{i,\varrho}+a_{i,\varrho}^{\perp}))

\displaystyle=\mathrm{vol}(K\cap((t-\langle a_{i,\varrho},x\rangle)a_{i,\varrho}+a_{i,\varrho}^{\perp})),

where we used (
4.3
) for
i\in\{1,2\}
and (
4.4
) for
i\in\{3,\ldots,d\}
in the last step. This implies that

\displaystyle\mathrm{vol}(K\cap H_{i})-\mathrm{vol}((\varrho K+x)\cap H_{i})

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\langle a_{i,\varrho},x_{K}\rangle}\mathrm{vol}(K\cap(ta_{i,\varrho}+a_{i,\varrho}^{\perp}))\,\textup{d}t-\int_{-\infty}^{\langle a_{i,\varrho},x_{K}\rangle}\mathrm{vol}(K\cap((t-\langle a_{i,\varrho},x\rangle)a_{i,\varrho}+a_{i,\varrho}^{\perp}))\,\textup{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\langle a_{i,\varrho},x_{K}\rangle}\mathrm{vol}(K\cap(ta_{i,\varrho}+a_{i,\varrho}^{\perp}))\,\textup{d}t-\int_{-\infty}^{\langle a_{i,\varrho},x_{K}\rangle-\langle a_{i,\varrho},x\rangle}\mathrm{vol}(K\cap(ta_{i,\varrho}+a_{i,\varrho}^{\perp}))\,\textup{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{\langle a_{i,\varrho},x_{K}\rangle-\langle a_{i,\varrho},x\rangle}^{\langle a_{i,\varrho},x_{K}\rangle}\mathrm{vol}(K\cap(ta_{i,\varrho}+a_{i,\varrho}^{\perp}))\,\textup{d}t.

The absolute value of the right-hand side can be bounded from below by

\bigg|\int_{\langle a_{i,\varrho},x_{K}\rangle-\langle a_{i,\varrho},x\rangle}^{\langle a_{i,\varrho},x_{K}\rangle}\mathrm{vol}(B^{d}(x_{K},r_{K})\cap(ta_{i,\varrho}+a_{i,\varrho}^{\perp}))\,\textup{d}t\bigg|\geq\kappa_{d-1}(r_{K}/2)^{d-1}\min\{|\langle a_{i,\varrho},x\rangle|,r_{K}/2\},

where we used that
B^{d}(x_{K},r_{K})\cap(ta_{i,\varrho}+a_{i,\varrho}^{\perp})
contains a
(d-1)
-dimensional ball of radius
r_{K}/2
if
t\in[\langle a_{i,\varrho},x_{K}\rangle-r_{K}/2,\langle a_{i,\varrho},x_{K}\rangle+r_{K}/2]
. Combining this with (
4.5
) and (
4.6
) yields

\mathrm{vol}(K\setminus(\varrho K+x))\geq\frac{\kappa_{d-1}r_{K}^{d-1}}{2^{d}}\min\{|\langle a_{i,\varrho},x\rangle|,r_{K}/2\}.

Since
a_{1,\varrho},\ldots,a_{d,\varrho}
form an orthonormal base, we have
\sum_{i=1}^{d}\langle a_{i,\varrho},x\rangle^{2}=\|x\|^{2}
. Thus, there exists some
i_{0}\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
such that

\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\|x\|\leq|\langle a_{i_{0},\varrho},x\rangle|\leq\|x\|.

The two previous inequalities lead to

\mathrm{vol}(K\setminus(\varrho K+x))\geq\frac{\kappa_{d-1}r_{K}^{d-1}}{2^{d}}\min\{\|x\|/\sqrt{d},r_{K}/2\}.

For rotations
\varrho
such that
v_{\varrho}=\pm e_{1}
we have
\varrho K=K
so that we can apply Lemma
4.1
with
c=\sqrt{d}r_{K}/(2\operatorname{diam}(K))
. Thus, there exists a constant
c_{K}^{\prime}\in(0,\infty)
such that

\mathrm{vol}(K\setminus(\varrho K+x))\geq c_{K}^{\prime}\|x\|

(4.7)

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\|x\|\leq\sqrt{d}r_{K}/2
and all rotations
\varrho
.
So it remains to show the desired inequality for all rotations
\varrho
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\sqrt{d}r_{K}/2<\|x\|\leq r_{0}
, where
r_{0}
is given by

r_{0}:=\sup\{\|y\|:y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},(\tilde{\varrho}K+y)\cap K\neq\varnothing\text{ for some rotation }\tilde{\varrho}\}.

Next we note that (
4.7
) especially ensures that

\frac{\mathrm{vol}(K\setminus(\varrho K+x))}{\|x\|}>0

for all rotations
\varrho
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\|x\|\geq\sqrt{d}r_{K}/2
. Since, for a fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a fixed rotation
\varrho
, the function
(\tilde{x},\tilde{\varrho})\mapsto\mathbbm{1}\{y\in\tilde{\varrho}K+\tilde{x}\}
is continuous at
(x,\varrho)
for almost every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, rewriting the volume as integral and using the dominated convergence theorem shows that the function

(x,\varrho)\mapsto\frac{\mathrm{vol}(K\setminus(\varrho K+x))}{\|x\|}

is continuous for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
and rotations
\varrho
. Since a continuous function attains a minimum on a compact set, there exists a constant
\tilde{c}\in(0,\infty)
such that

\frac{\mathrm{vol}(K\setminus(\varrho K+x))}{\|x\|}\geq{\tilde{c}}

for all rotations
\varrho
and all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\sqrt{d}r_{K}/2\leq\|x\|\leq r_{0}
. Together with (
4.7
), this completes the proof.
∎

The sets
K
used in Examples
1.1
–
1.7
meet the requirements of Proposition
4.3
so that they satisfy (
4.2
). We observe that in all these cases, the set

What remains to verify is that for sets of class (S2) with star-shaped
K
, property (
4.2
) implies assumption (iii) of Theorem
3.1
. Using the star-shapedness of
K
together with (
4.2
), we obtain, for
x,y,u,v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\|x-y\|\geq\|u-v\|

\displaystyle\mathrm{vol}\Big(\Big({x+y\over 2}+\|x-y\|\varrho_{x,y}K\Big)\setminus\Big({u+v\over 2}+\|u-v\|\varrho_{u,v}K\Big)\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{vol}\Big(\|x-y\|\varrho_{x,y}K\setminus\Big({u+v\over 2}-{x+y\over 2}+\|u-v\|\varrho_{u,v}K\Big)\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\mathrm{vol}\Big(\|x-y\|\varrho_{x,y}K\setminus\Big({u+v\over 2}-{x+y\over 2}+\|x-y\|\varrho_{u,v}K\Big)\Big)

\displaystyle=\|x-y\|^{d}\,\mathrm{vol}\Big(K\setminus\Big(\|x-y\|^{-1}\varrho_{x,y}^{-1}\Big({u+v\over 2}-{x+y\over 2}\Big)+\varrho_{x,y}^{-1}\varrho_{u,v}K\Big)\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\|x-y\|^{d}\,c_{K}\,\|x-y\|^{-1}\Big\|{u+v\over 2}-{x+y\over 2}\Big\|

\displaystyle=c_{K}\,\|x-y\|^{d-1}\Big\|{u+v\over 2}-{x+y\over 2}\Big\|

with the constant
c_{K}
from (
4.2
). This completes the proof of Theorems
1.9

1.10
