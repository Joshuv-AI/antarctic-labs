Measured and supervised physical exercise for older individuals with asthma, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease and obstructive sleep apnoea (MIOLI): protocol of a randomised controlled trial
Authors: Marja Äijö, Pirjo Komulainen, Eija Lönnroos, Harri Lindholm, Saijamari Hiltunen, Heikki Tikkanen, Merja Rantakokko, Mika Venojärvi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionAsthma, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) and obstructive sleep apnoea (OSA) are prevalent chronic respiratory diseases associated with increased comorbidity, mortality and healthcare costs. Physical activity and exercise are widely recommended as part of treatment for these conditions, yet the specific effects of Nordic walking (NW) remain underexplored. The aims of this randomised controlled trial (RCT) are to improve physical fitness, functional capacity and respiratory health and increase regular physical activity and quality of life of older adults with asthma and/or COPD and/or OSA through a supervised 3-month group-based NW intervention combined with resistance, balance and mobility training.Methods and analysisThis single-blinded, parallel-group RCT will recruit 100 adults aged 55–80 years diagnosed with asthma and/or COPD and/or OSA in the Northern Savo region of Finland. Participants will be randomly allocated to either an intervention group or a control group.The intervention group will participate in a 12-week supervised exercise programme consisting of progressive NW sessions twice per week and resistance, balance and mobility training once per week. The primary outcome is a change in cardiorespiratory endurance. Secondary outcomes include functional capacity, physical activity level, spirometry parameters and quality of life. The control group will continue their usual physical activity and receive physical activity guidance after 12 weeks. Measurements were conducted at baseline, three and 9 months. Data will be analysed according to the intention-to-treat principle. Group differences over time will be examined using appropriate parametric or non-parametric methods depending on data distribution.Ethics and disseminationEthical approval was obtained from the Regional Medical Research Ethics Committee of Eastern Finland Collaborative Area (892/13.00/2023). Findings will be disseminated through publications in peer-reviewed journals and presentations at scientific conferences.Trial registration numberThe trial is registered at ISRCTN12097135, registration date: 7 June 2024.Keywords: Randomized Controlled Trial; Physical Fitness; Pulmonary Disease, Chronic Obstructive; Quality of Life; Aged

Introduction

Chronic pulmonary diseases constitute one of the world’s most prevalent non-communicable disease categories,1 2 affecting millions of people.3 Asthma, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) and obstructive sleep apnoea (OSA) are regarded as significant social and health concerns associated with a substantial economic burden due to lost workdays, medicine costs, healthcare visits and hospitalisations, increased comorbidity and mortality.4,9 COPD is usually caused by chronic exposure to noxious particles or gases, most often tobacco smoke and is characterised by a typically insidious progression, respiratory symptoms, including dyspnoea, cough and/or sputum production, airflow limitation and systemic complications.9 Similarly, asthma, characterised by the presence of airway obstruction, hyperresponsiveness and inflammation, presents with any combination of the following symptoms: wheezing, dyspnoea, cough, chest tightness and mucus hypersecretion.10 11 In OSA, repeated upper airway collapse during sleep causes intermittent hypoxia, sleep fragmentation, daytime sleepiness and cognitive impairment.12 Asthma, COPD and OSA share common risk factors such as obesity, smoking and male gender, contributing to overlapping pathophysiological mechanisms and frequent coexistence.13 14 Exercise intolerance is also prevalent across these conditions, often driven by dyspnoea, sleep disruption and muscle deconditioning, which further reduces physical activity and worsens disease outcomes.15

Physical activity (PA), defined as ‘any bodily movement produced by skeletal muscles that requires energy expenditure’, is a multifaceted behaviour.16 17 The global and Finnish COPD, asthma and OSA care guidelines911 18,21 enhance the role of regular PA and exercise in the treatment of these conditions. Despite clinical recommendations, individuals with asthma, COPD and OSA often experience reduced PA due to health-related limitations, such as dyspnoea, fatigue and environmental sensitivity, psychological factors (eg, fear and embarrassment), attitudinal challenges (eg, perceived control and ageing) and motivational issues may attenuate the PA.13 15

Nordic walking (NW), originating in Finland, is a full-body exercise modality that incorporates specially designed walking poles to engage the upper limb and enhance walking efficiency. NW is a suitable physical exercise mode for primary, secondary and tertiary prevention of a wide range of diseases. It has been shown to positively impact cardiovascular outcomes, physical performance and quality of life among older adults.22 23 NW has been found to be beneficial for individuals with coronary artery disease, yielding both short- and long-term improvements in respiratory and circulatory fitness.24 Compared with traditional walking, NW is effective in patients with acute coronary syndrome and peripheral arterial occlusive disease24 and in patients with Parkinson’s disease. Importantly, NW has shown promise in improving exercise tolerance and oxygen uptake in patients with chronic respiratory diseases such as COPD, without increasing the rate of perceived exertion (RPE).25 These physiological advantages are particularly relevant given the common barriers to PA in asthma, COPD and OSA, such as dyspnoea, fatigue and reduced functional capacity. In our previous study, a 12-week supervised NW intervention resulted in a 20% improvement in aerobic performance capacity among overweight and obese men aged 40–65 years compared with the control group.26 Additionally, NW enhanced body composition more effectively than resistance training in men with impaired glucose regulation.26

Previously, a 3-month NW intervention (1 hour, three times per week, at 75% of maximum effort) in COPD patients increased PA, reduced sedentary time and improved 6-min walking test (6MWT) scores compared with the control group.27 These results were maintained at the 6- and 9-month follow-up, with no change in outcomes observed in the control group.27 Another study demonstrated that group-based NW exercise of 60 min two times a week for 12 weeks improved 6MWT scores more than moderate aerobic exercise and high-intensity interval training in patients with coronary artery disease. All the above exercise regimes reduced depression and enhanced quality of life.28 In addition, among older adults, NW increased daily PA.27 Resistance training has been induced to the same beneficial effects as endurance training.29

Currently, there is no data on NW exercise for individuals with asthma or OSA, although a research protocol has been published focusing on NW in asthma.30 We found it important to investigate an exercise programme that is in line with the PA recommendations for older adults.31 Our Measured and Supervised Physical Exercise for Older Individuals (MIOLI) study combines aerobic training, NW, with resistance, balance and mobility training in older adults with COPD and/or asthma and/or OSA.

Aim and hypotheses

The aim of this randomised controlled trial (RCT) is to investigate the effects of a 12-week NW programme combined with resistance, balance and mobility training on cardiorespiratory endurance in older adults diagnosed with asthma and/or COPD and/or OSA.

More specifically, the study will examine changes in physical fitness, functional capacity and respiratory health, as well as increases in self-motivated PA and improvements in quality of life. These outcomes will be compared between the intervention group (IG) and control group (CG), measured at baseline, immediately after a 12-week intervention period and again 6 months after the intervention.

Hypothesis 1: It is hypothesised that individually progressive NW combined with resistance, balance and mobility training will enhance physical fitness, functional capacity and respiratory health in older adults diagnosed with asthma and/or COPD and/or OSA.

Hypothesis 2: It is hypothesised that individually progressive NW combined with resistance, balance and mobility training will increase self-motivated PA and improve quality of life among older adults with asthma and/or COPD and/or OSA.

Methods

Study design and setting

The MIOLI intervention is a randomised, controlled, parallel-group trial with a 1:1 allocation ratio. The parallel groups are an IG (n=50) and a CG (n=50). The RCT will include 36 intervention sessions over the 12-week intervention period for IG. Identical measurements for both groups were conducted at baseline, after the intervention period at 3 months and after the 6-month follow-up period at 9 months (figure 1).

The design and flow of the study.

Outcome measures

Primary outcome

The primary outcome of the MIOLI study is cardiorespiratory endurance measured with the walking distance (m) 6MWT at baseline, 3 months and 9 months. A detailed description of the 6MWT procedures is provided in the Data Collection section.

Secondary outcomes

The MIOLI study has four secondary outcomes: (1) balance and lower extremity performance measured by the Short Physical Performance Battery (SPPB); (2) self-reported physical activity assessed using the International Physical Activity Questionnaire Short Form (IPAQ-SF); (3) pulmonary function measured by the spirometry parameter forced expiratory volume in 1 second (FEV1) and (4) health-related quality of life assessed using the RAND 36-Item Health Survey. All secondary outcomes are measured at baseline, 3 months and 9 months. A detailed description of these measurement procedures is provided in the Data collection section.

Participants

Participants will be recruited through three channels. The first of them is the MIOLI mini-interventions, short physical activity events (2–4 hours) organised for members of the local respiratory association. An invitation letter with an information sheet about the MIOLI mini-interventions and RCT study will be posted to the members of the association. Those interested in joining the RCT provide their contact details in mini-intervention sessions or reply to the invitation letter with written consent to be contacted later by phone for checking whether they meet the initial inclusion criteria of the RCT. The second recruitment channel includes outpatient clinics treating patients with asthma, COPD or OSA in the Kuopio University Hospital and health centres in the Kuopio and Siilinjärvi regions. The third channel involves public outreach through newspaper advertisements, social media platforms such as Facebook and intranet postings in large institutions located in Kuopio. Screening and enrolment have started in January 2025 and will continue until the target number of participants is achieved. Planned start and end dates for this study are January 2025 and December 2027, respectively. The predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria for participants are detailed in table 1. The selection criteria were determined to enhance safe participation in the exercise intervention and to identify and exclude persons with conditions posing safety risks or hindering adherence to the intervention protocol. Standardised comprehensive medical examinations reinforced with cycle ergometer tests were applied for these purposes.

Procedure

Data collection

All assessments will take place at the Kuopio Research Institute of Exercise Medicine. Participants who were first contacted by phone and met the initial inclusion criteria are invited to baseline evaluations conducted on two separate days, including interviews, measurements and medical examinations. At the first study visit, participants will receive both oral and written information on the study and will be asked to sign a written consent form by the research nurse (online supplemental file 1). The study physician will then assess the participants’ health status and their suitability for safe participation in the study. End of the second baseline visit, after all baseline measurements, participants will be randomly assigned to IG or CG. All study outcomes will be re-evaluated at three and 9 months. Assessments will be performed by the trained research team, including a physician, research nurse, physiotherapist and sports and exercise medicine students.

The study will collect comprehensive data on participants, including personal information (name, date of birth, height, weight and age), sociodemographic factors, living conditions, perceived health status, diagnosed diseases, perceived quality of life and well-being, lifestyle habits (nutrition, smoking, alcohol use, sleep, physical activity and fear of movement) and use of social and health services and received assistance (self-assessment). Additionally, data on respiratory and cardiovascular function, physical fitness, and functional capacity will be collected using measurement devices. Lipid and glucose metabolism will be assessed through laboratory tests. In addition, concentrations of selected cytokines and myokines will be measured using ELISA.

Methods for measuring primary and secondary outcomes

6MWT is used to measure cardiorespiratory endurance.32 Before performing the 6MWT, the participant’s resting blood pressure is measured while seated, and the rate of RPE is assessed using the Borg RPE scale.33 If the systolic blood pressure is 180 mm Hg or the diastolic pressure is over 120 mm Hg or the pulse is over 120, the 6MWT is not performed. Before the test, oxygen saturation and pulse are measured. The walking test is performed on a 20-m track, where the participant walks back and forth. The tester records the total distance walked in metres. During the test, heart rate data from the heart rate monitor is collected at 2 and 4 min. At the end of the test, the following measurements are recorded: rate of RPE, heart rate from both devices, oxygen saturation and seated blood pressure. Three minutes after the test, blood pressure is measured again, along with heart rate from both devices, oxygen saturation and RPE. The participant is asked whether any symptoms occurred during the test that may have affected performance. If so, these are documented on the test form. In case the participant experiences symptoms or unusual sensations, they are recorded, and the participant is advised to contact their healthcare provider if necessary.34

SPPB35 is used to assess balance and lower extremity performance. The SPPB includes three subtests: static balance with feet in side-by-side, semitandem and tandem positions; 4-m walking time and five repetitions of sit-to-stand. All subtest results are recorded in seconds and scored from 0 to 4 points, leading to a maximum of 12 points. Higher SPPB test scores indicate better lower extremity performance.

Self-reported PA will be assessed using the IPAQ-SF. In the IPAQ-SF, participants report the number of days and amount of time when they engaged in vigorous and moderate PA and walking activity for at least 10 min per session for each activity, as well as sedentary behaviour (mainly sitting) during the last 7 days. Metabolic equivalents per minute per week will be calculated for each activity.36

FEV1 will be measured by spirometry. FEV1 shows a gradual, age-related decline even in healthy individuals without lung disease and reduced FEV1 also establishes the presence of and severity of COPD.37

Health-related quality of life will be assessed using the RAND 36-Item Health Survey 1.0, and data will be recoded on a 0–100 scale, higher values indicating better quality of life, including physical, mental and social well-being.38

Cardiorespiratory fitness and electrocardiography are assessed during a maximal symptom-limited exercise test to exhaustion on an electrically braked cycle ergometer (Ergoline, Bitz, Germany). The test will start with a 1-min sitting period in the saddle, followed by a 3-min warm-up with 0 WW; after that, the workload will increase gradually according to an individualised ramp protocol. The test will be supervised by a physician, and participants will be verbally encouraged to continue until exhaustion. Respiratory gas exchange and ventilation will be measured by the breath-by-breath method (Vyntus CPX, Vyaire Medical, USA), and ECG will be recorded before the test at rest and throughout the exercise test. ECG is measured in a lying position after a test at 3-min and 5-min points. Blood pressure is measured in a lying position before a test and after a test at 3-min and 5-min points. During the test, blood pressure is measured at 2-min intervals. Spirometry is carried out before and after the test using the participant’s own medication. Participants are instructed by written instructions to prepare for the test. Measurements of the MIOLI study other than primary and secondary outcomes described above are presented in table 2. The research nurse will assess the reasons for discontinuation among individuals who may withdraw from the study.

Randomisation and blinding

After the baseline measurements, the participants will be randomised by permuted block randomisation based on computer-generated random numbers. The block size will be fixed to contain four participants: two to control and two to exercise IGs. The participants will be reinformed of the study design and procedures at the time of randomisation. Study participants will be told that the goal of the study is to explore whether a 12-week exercise intervention will influence cardiorespiratory fitness. The randomisation procedure will involve participants choosing one of four identically sealed opaque envelopes, which will be in a mixed order containing the group assignment. The staff member who will generate the random numbers and fill the sealed envelopes will be independent of the individual who will run the randomisation meeting, ensuring that the allocation sequence will be concealed until a participant chooses an envelope.

The complete randomisation list will be stored electronically on a secure, access-restricted private server. Access will be limited to the independent researcher and the designated data manager, and all access events will be automatically logged. For allocation or other study-specific purposes, separate working files containing only the minimum necessary information will be generated.

All baseline data will be collected prior to the randomisation. After the randomisation, study participants will be advised not to discuss issues related to group allocation during the follow-up assessments. The personnel responsible for enrolling participants and those assigning participants to the interventions will not have access to the random allocation sequence. Follow-up assessors will be blinded to group allocation whenever possible.

Intervention

Intervention group

Participants randomised to the IG (n=50) will engage in a 12-week individually progressive training programme. The exercise programme includes three guided group sessions per week: two NW sessions and one classroom-based session targeting muscle strength, balance and flexibility. In addition to supporting independent PA, participants will be guided to self-directed exercise.

The NW programme includes warm-up exercises consisting of 5 min of walking and stretching of the main muscle groups, followed by walking with poles. The programmes were individually designed and began with learning sessions on how to walk with poles during the first 2 weeks. NW training sessions are conducted at increasing strain levels, starting from 50% to 60% of heart rate reserve in weeks 1–4, 60%–70% in weeks 5–8 and 60%–70% in weeks 9–12. Individual target heart rates are calculated using the formula: 208–(0.67× age in years), based on measured resting heart rate and estimated maximal heart rate with the Karvonen formula.39

Heart rate with heart rate average, distance (km), time (min), pace (min/km), calorie expenditure (kcal) and steps are monitored during training using a combination of the Polar Pacer Pro and the H10 heart rate sensor (Polar Electro Oy, Kempele, Finland). The ECG will be monitored using a Beat2Phone (Vital-Signum, Helsinki, Finland). The target heart rate range is progressively increased as mentioned. The training time increases progressively from 30 min to an hour during the intervention period. Walking speed is increased, or an uphill gradient is added starting from week 5, to achieve the intended target heart rate. During weeks 9–12, participants will perform five maximal walking sprints during each training session. After the NW session, the main muscle groups are stretched for 5 min as a cool-down.

Participants perform a once-a-week classroom-based session targeting muscle strength, balance and flexibility exercises. The training time increases progressively from 30 min to an hour during the intervention period. During the first 2 weeks, participants will familiarise themselves with the techniques for performing muscle strength, balance and flexibility exercises, as well as determine their individual load levels. From week 3 onwards, the duration, intensity and number of repetitions of the exercises will progressively increase. Each session will begin with a warm-up and end with a cool-down with flexibility exercises. The intensity of the exercises will be monitored using a combination of the Polar Pacer Pro and H10 heart rate monitor. Resistance exercise training will be performed for the main muscle groups (ie, knee extension and flexion, abdomen and back muscles, rotation, upper back and arm muscles). Progressive resistance exercise for the main muscle groups will be conducted by using body weight, elastic bands and dumbbells. Each session will consist of 8–15 repetitions per muscle group and 1–3 sets of exercises for each muscle group. The balance training will be carried out as functional training. The first week of training, flexibility exercises will be given to the subjects in written form to support independent stretching at home after all exercise sessions. During the last week of training, muscle-strength exercises for major muscle groups will be given to the subjects in written form to support independent training after the intervention. During the intervention, participants have the opportunity to consult the physiotherapist during the training sessions if needed.

Daily PA will be monitored using ActiGraphGT3X accelerometers (ActiGraphTM wGT3X-B; Pensacola, Florida, USA).40 Monitoring will take place 1 week before the intervention starts, during the first week of the intervention, in weeks 6 and 7, in week 12 and 1 week after the intervention. The monitor will be attached to the left side of the waist immediately upon waking up in the morning. It will be worn during all waking hours and removed only when going to bed or when showering, bathing or swimming. The time of waking up in the morning and the time of going to bed will be recorded in a diary. PA levels, as measured by the accelerometer, will be categorised into five distinct thresholds provided by the manufacturer: sedentary, light, moderate, vigorous and very vigorous PA.

The participation compliance with the PA training during the intervention will be monitored by means of a PA diary throughout the 3 months. Participants will be instructed to record all PAs, either independent or guided, during the 12 weeks. The diary will include recording fields for the type (eg, walking, swimming and rowing) and duration (in hours and minutes) of exercise, distance covered and type of activity, as well as perceived exertion, general well-being before and after the exercise and any abnormal symptoms or sensations experienced during or after the PA session. In addition, after each training session, the instructors will complete a structured session log documenting session implementation, progression and participant engagement.

Safety procedures and oversight

All intervention instructors were first-aid qualified and completed standardised training based on the official Suomen Latu Nordic Walking instructor curriculum, supplemented by structured training for the indoor exercise component. Both training modules were delivered by the same two qualified trainers to ensure consistency in instructional content and competence prior to intervention delivery.

Intervention fidelity was supported through scheduled supervision visits during the first intervention week and at mid-study, during which adherence to the predefined protocol and standardised operational procedures was evaluated. After each training session, instructors completed a structured session log documenting any deviations from the planned protocol. Instructors were also required to report unexpected events or abnormal participant responses immediately to the study team. These procedures formed a continuous quality-control framework designed to ensure protocol adherence and consistency across study sites. In the serious adverse event of an acute injury, instructors were instructed to call emergency services (tel. 112).

All exercise-related adverse events (ERAEs) and serious adverse events (SAEs) occurring during the exercise intervention will be systematically recorded in source documents and case report forms, regardless of whether they are suspected to be related to the intervention. ERAEs will be classified according to severity using a classification model appropriate for exercise interventions (mild, moderate and severe), which takes into account normal physiological responses to exercise and expected training-related symptoms. SAEs will be defined according to the International Council for Harmonisation Good Clinical Practice guidelines.

Control group

After baseline measurement, participants in the CG (n=50) are recommended to continue their daily activities, including PA, as they have done independently in the past. Daily PA will be monitored using similar accelerometers as in the IG. Monitoring will occur in three 2-week periods at corresponding time points and manners as in the IG.

Participants in CG will receive PA guidance and advice after the 12 weeks of measurements to support independent PA during the 6-month follow-up period. Their PA guidance includes written instructions, a guided NW session and a classroom-based session targeting muscle strength, balance and flexibility exercises.

Sample size

The power calculation is based on previous literature in chronic respiratory diseases, particularly studies using the 6MWT as a measure of functional exercise capacity. In patients with COPD, the minimal clinically important difference (MCID) for the 6 min walk distance is estimated to be 30–50 m, with an SD of approximately 60 m.41 Comparable reliability and measurement properties of the 6MWT have also been demonstrated in adults with asthma.42

In patients with OSA, the 6MWT is an established outcome measure, and studies have shown that adults with moderate to severe OSA exhibit reduced 6MWD compared with expected values. Reported SDs typically range from approximately 70–100 m, indicating both impaired functional capacity and acceptable variability of the test in OSA populations.43 44

Although a disease-specific MCID for 6MWD has not been formally defined in OSA, an improvement of 30–50 m is widely considered clinically meaningful across chronic respiratory diseases using the 6MWT. Therefore, a conservative between-group difference of 35 m was assumed for the present study. With an SD of 60 m, a two-sided significance level of 0.05 and a statistical power of 0.80, a total of 86 participants are required to detect this difference. To account for expected dropouts and other attrition during the study (approximately 10%), the final target sample size is set at 96 participants, with 48 participants allocated to each group.

Statistical analysis

Background variables will be expressed as mean and SD when normally distributed and as median and quartiles (Q1–Q3) when not normally distributed, with absolute numbers and relative frequencies with percentages (n, %). Normality will be assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test and visual inspection (Q–Q plots and histograms). Data will be analysed according to the intention-to-treat principle to compare outcomes between the IG and the CG. Depending on the normality of data distribution, either a two-way repeated measures analysis of variance (baseline, 3 and 9 months) or Kruskal-Wallis tests will be used. A p value <0.05 will be considered significant. Predefined subgroup analyses will be conducted based on, for example, age and disease, using interaction terms in the models, if such an analysis is feasible given the sample size. Missing data will be handled using multiple imputation under a missing-at-random assumption, and the primary analysis will follow the intention-to-treat principle. Sensitivity analyses using complete-case analysis and missing-not-at-random assumptions will be conducted to assess the robustness of the findings.

Data management

Data storage

The data are managed and registered in compliance with the Finnish Personal Data Act and the European Union Data Protection Act. All data are recorded and analysed without any direct personal identification information. The research data are carefully maintained, documented and stored on password-protected servers of the University of Eastern Finland. Access to the data will be restricted to the researchers conducting the study, protected by usernames and passwords.

Data anonymisation

Identifiable information will be pseudonymised at the data establishment stage, allowing re-identification using a code key. The code keys will be securely kept by the researcher and will not be disclosed to individuals outside the study. Research results will be reported at the group level to ensure that individual respondents cannot be identified.

Study duration

Screening and enrolment have started in January 2025 and will continue until the target number of participants is achieved. Planned start and end dates for this study are January 2025 and December 2027, respectively. Data collected through measurements will be archived without personal information at the end of the study and will be destroyed 10 years after the study’s conclusion. Thus, the total duration of the study is 15 years.

Data usage

The data will be used in scientific publications, conference presentations and possibly in theses. Potential thesis students will have access to pseudonymised data only.

Data sharing

Although the research data will not be made publicly available, it may be accessed by researchers outside the core research group, provided the use is based on an approved research plan. The data will not be shared outside the European Union, but international collaboration is possible, for example, in comparative studies, when data protection and usage conditions are met.

A data monitoring committee will not be established, as the trial involves a low-risk behavioural intervention and all safety and conduct monitoring will be managed internally without the need for an independent committee. No interim analyses are planned, and the trial will not include formal stopping guidelines. There will be no independent monitoring of the trial. This is justified because the intervention is low risk and data collection procedures are simple.

Other information

An unauthorised version of the Finnish MMSE was used by the study team without permission; however, this has now been rectified with PAR. The MMSE is a copyrighted instrument and may not be used or reproduced in whole or in part, in any form or language, or by any means without the written permission of PAR (www.parinc.com).

Ethical considerations

This study has been approved by the Regional Medical Research Ethics Committee of Eastern Finland Collaborative Area (Well-being Services County of North Savo, Kuopio, Finland) ref: 892/13.00/2023. The research will be conducted in accordance with good clinical research practice and the principles of the Helsinki Declaration. This study is registered on the ISRCTN registry with the register number ISRCTN12097135, registration date: 7 June 2024 and DOI: https://doi.org/10.1186/ISRCTN12097135. Detailed information about the research and experimental procedure will be provided to all participants. After reading the information sheet, they will sign the written informed consent before data collection. The results of the study will be submitted to relevant scientific journals and international conferences. The participants will enquire about our study findings after the study completion.

Protocol amendments

Modifications to the protocol which may impact the conduct of the study will require a formal amendment to the protocol. Such an amendment will be approved by the Regional Medical Research Ethics Committee of the Eastern Finland Collaborative Area prior to the implementation of the modifications.
