1 Executive Summary
1.1 Project Overview
The project titled Quantitative Strain Monitoring for Well and Seal Integrity through Wellbore Fiber
Optic Cables, or Q-Fibre in short, is a multi-national collaborative project under the Clean Energy
Transitional Partnership (CETP) program. The lead organization of the project is the Norwegian
Geotechnical Institute (NGI), and the LLNL team joins as a beneficiary member. The LLNL team
receives the funding from the U.S. Department of Energy with the amount of $1 million across three
years, and the budget is primarily allocated to Work Package 3 (WP3) of the project, which focuses
on Numerical Modeling of Fiber Optic Cable Data. Figure 1 presents all work packages involved
in the project and corresponding lead organization for each work package. The LLNL team leads
WP3.

 Figure 1: Work packages for Q-Fibre and all project participants.

1.2 Cumulative Progress
The LLNL team received the first-year funds on January 15, 2025, and officially began the technical
work at that time. The lead organization of Q-Fibre—Norwegian Geotechnical Institute (NGI),
organized a project kick-off meeting on February 5-6, 2025 in a hybrid way. The LLNL team joined
the kick-off meeting virtually. To ensure smooth communication, the LLNL team has set up two
regular WP3 meetings every month, one for internal progress updates, and the other for external
collaboration and discussion. These meetings were successfully held in January, February, and
March 2025 during the reporting period.
 During the first performance period (FY25), the LLNL team has started the technical work for
Task 1 of WP3, which is to conduct initial numerical modeling for near-well geomechanics and
seismic waves. More specifically, we have designed problem setups based on the representative well
configuration for the CO2 storage application. Material parameters and in situ conditions for the

simulations were obtained from publicly available datasets associated with the Northern Lights CCS
site in Europe Baseline simulations using the thermo-hydro-mechanical (THM) model in LLNL’s
GEOS simulator have been conducted to capture the response in each well component. Preliminary
parameter studies have been initiated to explore the effects of varying injection temperatures and rock
stiffness. Additionally, initial seismic modeling was performed using wave speeds obtained from
real log data of the Eos Well, a testing well in the Northern Lights CCS site, to model the seismic
response with a hypothetical earthquake source.

3.2 Actual Activities
The actual activities for this reporting period are provided below:
 • LLNL has designed several problem setups for the numerical simulation based on the represen-
 tative well configuration for the CO2 storage application. More specifically, a full 3D wellbore
 mesh is created, which includes a cased well section in the seal formation and an uncased

 tributions of important variables near the wellbore (e.g., pressure, temperature, strain) are
 plotted.

 the injection temperature or rock stiffness.
 • Initial seismic modeling has been conducted with the real log data of wave speeds obtained
 from a testing well in Northern Lights CCS site.

Baseline THM Simulation Results
The team has performed baseline THM simulations for all problem setups. Figure 3 presents the
plots of the axial strain distribution along the well. The results at different components of the well
are provided, i.e., inside casing, behind casing, and inside rock formation. These plots illustrate
the increasing deformation in all well components when it gets closer to the reservoir layer (depth
< 1 m). Also, it is expected that the deformation becomes larger as injection proceeds. These
preliminary results have demonstrated that the existing THM simulation can provide synthetic FOC
data (e.g., predicted strain distribution v.s. DSS data), thus will be helpful for the technical work in
Task 2.

Parameter Studies on 2D Uncased Wellbore
The team has conducted parameter studies using the THM model, starting from the 2D uncased
wellbore problem. Here, we focus on the geomechanical response and the tendency to reach failure
for the rock materials around the wellbore. Two parameter studies are considered: (a) the one
with varying injection temperature 𝑇inj but fixed rock stiffness 𝐸, (b) the other with varying rock
stiffness 𝐸 but fixed injection temperature 𝑇inj . Figure 4 presents the results of these two parameter
studies in the form of Mohr Circle, which represents the stress state of the most critical location
at the wellbore and its tendency to reach failure (denoted by the black solid line). We can observe
