Ground state energy of the Bose–Hubbard model with large coordination number with a polaron-type quantum de Finetti theorem
Shahnaz Farhat, Denis Périce, Sören Petrat

Abstract
We consider the ground state energy of the Bose–Hubbard model on a graph with large and homogeneous coordination number. In the limit of infinite coordination number, we prove convergence of the ground state energy to the minimizer of a mean-field energy functional. This functional is obtained by averaging the hopping term over the large number of connected sites, while the interaction energy is not averaged. Hence, the resulting mean-field description is in the strong coupling regime, and is expected to provide a qualitatively correct picture of the phase diagram of the Bose–Hubbard model for large enough coordination number. For our proof, we develop a new version of a de Finetti-type theorem, which we call the polaron-type quantum de Finetti theorem , and which we expect to be a more broadly useful extension of existing quantum de Finetti results. Our theorem covers the case where the Hilbert space is a tensor product of some Hilbert space with a bosonic Fock space. This theorem is applied to the convergence of the ground state energy of the Bose–Hubbard model after reducing it to a polaron-type model.

Introduction

1.1
Context

The Bose–Hubbard model
[
12
]
describes bosons on a lattice with on-site interactions and hopping between nearest neighbours. As a lattice model, it is amenable to rigorous analysis, while being rich in phenomenology. In particular, it is expected to exhibit a quantum phase transition between a Mott insulator and a superfluid state
[
11
]
. In the mathematical physics literature, there are only a few rigorous works on this type of phase transition. Without being complete, let us mention the following. The work
[
4
]
considers the Bose–Hubbard model on a complete graph, which considerably simplifies the problem as the model is then symmetry under permutation of lattice sites. For this model, rigorous proofs on the thermodynamic behaviour are given, and the corresponding phase diagram is analysed. In
[
1
]
, the authors prove Bose–Einstein condensation for the hard-core Bose–Hubbard model at half filling with periodic external potential in the regime of small external potential and low temperature, and the absence of it for large external potential or large temperature. In
[
10
]
, bounds near the critical line of the Mott insulator phase are proven for the hard-core Bose–Hubbard model using rigorous perturbation theory.

In our work, we do not directly attempt to prove the quantum phase transition. Instead, we prove that the ground state energy converges to that of a mean-field model in the limit of infinite coordination number of the lattice. The mean-field model is obtained from averaging the hopping term, and hence the mean-field theory is a one-lattice site description. The corresponding lattice-site product states are known as Gutzwiller product states
[
14
,
24
]
. In this mean-field theory one can compute the phase diagram with numerical and some analytical techniques, see Figure
1
. It is expected that this mean-field phase diagram is qualitatively correct already for not too large coordination numbers, e.g., already in three dimensions for a cubic lattice with nearest-neighbour interaction
[
11
]
. This justifies our interest in the mean-field theory, and the need for a rigorous justification of the validity of the mean-field limit. Note that for the averaging we have to scale down the hopping amplitude with the inverse coordination number, hence our limit could be described as a “weak-hopping limit”. The interaction is not scaled at all, and thus the mean-field theory is strongly coupled. This is in contrast to weak coupling mean-field limits for bosonic systems, which are not usually expected to exhibit phase transitions; see, e.g.,
[
3
,
13
]
for reviews and
[
23
]
for a result on the Bose–Hubbard model. Note that to our knowledge this is the first proof of convergence of the ground state energy in the limit of large coordination number. Recently, we proved a related result
[
9
]
for the dynamics. In the physics literature, our approximation is well-known as a simple instance of the celebrated
Dynamical Mean-field Theory
(DMFT), an extremely useful tool to study the dynamics of quantum lattice systems with large coordination number
[
11
,
15
]
.

A very versatile and general tool for rigorously justifying mean-field limits is the quantum de Finetti theorem
[
28
,
16
]
. For the justification of mean-field limits in quantum mechanics, it has for example been used in
[
18
]
to rigorously justify Hartree’s mean-field theory for continuum bosons in the weak coupling limit. For further references, see the remarks
[
19
]
, the reviews
[
25
,
26
]
, and
[
5
]
for a quantitative version. Many follow-up works used variants of the quantum de Finetti theorem in the derivation of mean-field-like limits in quantum mechanics, e.g.,
[
20
]
for a mixture of Bose gases,
[
21
]
for bosons in the Gross–Pitaevskii limit, and
[
22
]
for 2D fermions in strong magnetic fields. The quantum de Finetti theorem states that reduced
k
-body density matrices of a symmetric (i.e., bosonic) state converge in the limit of large particle number to a convex combination of product states. Such a theorem can then be used in lower bounds for ground state energies. For example, for pair interacting particles, the reduced two-body density matrix can be approximated by a convex combination of product states, and a lower bound is obtained by concentration of this convex combination on the minimizers of the corresponding mean-field functional.

The quantum de Finetti theorem as outlined above does not directly apply to our problem of the Bose–Hubbard model with large coordination number. This is because our approximation is for reduced lattice-site density matrices, and the Bose–Hubbard model is not generally symmetric under the exchange of lattice sites. However, we can generate the Bose–Hubbard Hamiltonian through translations of a reduced Hamiltonian of just one lattice site interacting with its neighbouring sites. This reduced Hamiltonian is symmetric in the neighbouring sites. Hence, we prove here a new version of a quantum de Finetti theorem that applies to a tensor product of a Hilbert space (to describe the “core” lattice site) with a large symmetric tensor product of another Hilbert space (to describe the neighbouring sites). Such a structure is also encountered when considering a tracer particle coupled to a bath of bosonic particles, e.g., the polaron
[
2
]
, hence we call the corresponding version of such a quantum de Finetti theorem a
polaron-type quantum de Finetti theorem
. Note that we cover the case of infinite dimensional Hilbert spaces here, hence going beyond related finite dimensional versions of such theorems
[
17
,
5
]
. We expect this theorem to be interesting in its own right, and regard it as the main technical novelty of the paper. We note that similar results for composite systems have recently been proven in
[
7
]
, where they have been applied to Nelson-type polaron models. This was based on the theorem from
[
8
]
, and our Theorem
7
is in the same spirit as such results. We remark that we explicitly construct the de Finetti measure in the finite dimensional case in Theorem
15
.

1.2
Model

We consider a sequence of graphs
(V_{z},E_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{N}}
with vertex set

We will consider the limit where

\displaystyle\left|E_{z}\right|=\frac{z}{2}\left|V_{z}\right|,\quad\left|V_{z}\right|>z.

(1)

The

The lattice

\displaystyle z\eqvl\displaylimits_{d\to\infty}\frac{(2d)^{n}}{n!}.

The one-lattice-site Hilbert space is
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{C})
, and we denote its canonical Hilbert basis by
(\left|n\right>)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. We define the standard annihilation and creation operators

satisfying the CCR by

\displaystyle a\left|0\right>=0,\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}^{*},\ a\left|n\right>\coloneq\sqrt{n}\,\left|n-1\right>,

\displaystyle\forall n\in\mathbb{N},\ a^{*}\left|n\right>\coloneq\sqrt{n+1}\,\left|n+1\right>.

The particle number operator is

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}\coloneq a^{*}a,

and the Fock space is

\mathcal{F}_{V_{z}}\coloneq\ell^{2}(\mathbb{C})^{\otimes\left|V_{z}\right|}\cong\mathcal{F}_{+}\left(L^{2}(V_{z},\mathbb{C})\right)\coloneq\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}}L^{2}(V_{z},\mathbb{C})^{\otimes_{+}n}.

(2)

In these notations,
\otimes_{+}
denotes the symmetric tensor product and
\mathcal{F}_{+}\left(L^{2}(V_{z},\mathbb{C})\right)
the bosonic Fock space constructed over the one-particle Hilbert space
L^{2}(V_{z},\mathbb{C})
. Equation (
2
) provides two equivalent representations of the same Fock space. The right-hand side is the standard “particle representation”, while we call the left-hand side the “lattice-site representation”. The latter will be the convenient setting for this paper, as we aim to control correlations between lattice sites rather than between particles.

We denote by
\mathcal{L}
the set of linear operators, by
\mathcal{L}_{+}
the set of positive linear operators, by
\mathcal{L}^{p}
the
p
-Schatten class for
p\in\left[1,\infty\right]
, and by
\mathcal{K}
the set of compact operators. Given an order
\leq
on
V_{z}
, we denote

\displaystyle\forall A\in\mathcal{L}\left(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{C})\right),\ x\in V_{z},\ A_{x}\coloneq\mathds{1}_{\ell^{2}}^{\otimes\left|\left\{y\in V\mid y<x\right\}\right|}\otimes A\otimes\mathds{1}_{\ell^{2}}^{\otimes\left|\left\{y\in V\mid x<y\right\}\right|}\in\mathcal{L}\left(\mathcal{F}_{z}\right).

(3)

For a hopping amplitude
J\in\mathbb{R}
, a chemical potential
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
, and a coupling constant
U\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the Bose–Hubbard Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H_{V_{z}}\coloneq-\frac{J}{z}\sum_{\left\{x,y\right\}\in E_{z}}\left(a_{x}^{*}a_{y}+a_{y}^{*}a_{x}\right)+(J-\mu)\sum_{x\in V_{z}}\mathcal{N}_{x}+\frac{U}{2}\sum_{x\in V_{z}}\mathcal{N}_{x}(\mathcal{N}_{x}-1).

(4)

In view of (
1
), we note that the kinetic energy is given by

\displaystyle d\Gamma(-\Delta)\coloneq

\displaystyle\ \sum_{\left\{x,y\right\}\in E_{z}}(a_{x}^{*}-a_{y}^{*})(a_{x}-a_{y})=\sum_{\left\{x,y\right\}\in E_{z}}\left(\mathcal{N}_{x}+\mathcal{N}_{y}\right)-\sum_{\left\{x,y\right\}\in E_{z}}\left(a_{x}^{*}a_{y}+a_{y}^{*}a_{x}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ z\sum_{x\in V_{z}}\mathcal{N}_{x}-\sum_{\left\{x,y\right\}\in E_{z}}\left(a_{x}^{*}a_{y}+a_{y}^{*}a_{x}\right),

with scaled amplitude
J/z
, so that the energy contribution is of the same order as that of the one-lattice-site terms when
z\to\infty
. We emphasize that the mean-field limit considered in this work amounts to averaging over the
y
variable in (
4
), i.e. over the neighbouring shell
V_{z}^{x}
around
x\in V_{z}
. This is the main difference from known results on mean-field limits, which average over particle interactions
[
3
,
13
,
23
]
. This is also the reason why the mean-field scaling
1/z
appears in front of the kinetic energy in (
4
), as opposed to the usual
1/N
rescaling of the interaction energy.

Note that, using (
1
), the Bose–Hubbard Hamiltonian (
4
) can be rewritten for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
as

\displaystyle H_{V_{z}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in V_{z}}\left(-J\left(\alpha a_{x}^{*}+\overline{\alpha}a_{x}-\left|\alpha\right|^{2}\right)+(J-\mu)\mathcal{N}_{x}+\frac{U}{2}\mathcal{N}_{x}(\mathcal{N}_{x}-1)\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{J}{z}\sum_{\left\{x,y\right\}\in E_{z}}\left((a_{x}^{*}-\overline{\alpha})(a_{y}-\alpha)+(a^{*}_{y}-\overline{\alpha})(a_{x}-\alpha)\right).

Then the mean-field theory is obtained by neglecting all terms quadratic in
a-\alpha
, and choosing
\alpha
as the averaged annihilation operator. Hence, we introduce the nonlinear mean-field operator

h_{\varphi}\coloneq-J\big(\alpha_{\varphi}a^{*}+\overline{\alpha_{\varphi}}a-\left|\alpha_{\varphi}\right|^{2}\big)+(J-\mu)\mathcal{N}+\frac{U}{2}\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{N}-1),

(5)

with the order parameter

\displaystyle\alpha_{\varphi}\coloneqq\langle\varphi,a\varphi\rangle

(6)

where
\varphi\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{C})
is a one-lattice-site state. The corresponding energy functional is

\left<\varphi,h_{\varphi}\varphi\right>=-J|\alpha_{\varphi}|^{2}+(J-\mu)\left<\varphi,\mathcal{N}\varphi\right>+\frac{U}{2}\left<\varphi,\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{N}-1)\varphi\right>.

(7)

1.3
Main results

Our main theorem is the convergence of the ground state energy of the Bose–Hubbard model (
4
) to the ground state energy of the corresponding mean-field energy functional (
7
).

Theorem 2
.

If
U>0
and
J\geq 0
, then

\displaystyle\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\psi\in\mathcal{F}_{V_{z}}\\
\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{\mathcal{F}_{V_{z}}}=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\left<\psi,H_{V_{z}}\psi\right>}{\left|V\right|}\limitoperator\displaylimits_{z\to\infty}\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varphi\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{C})\\
\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}}=1\end{subarray}}\left<\varphi,h_{\varphi}\varphi\right>.

The proof is given in Section
2
.

Remark 3
.

Let us gather a few simple facts about the ground state energy of the Bose–Hubbard model (
4
). Note that we do not fix the particle number (or density) as a constraint. Then the vacuum state is a possible trial state, hence the ground state energy is always non-positive. Furthermore, for example, for
U<0
, we can use a sequence of trial states with increasing density to see that in this case there is no ground state, i.e., the ground state energy is
-\infty
. Then there are only a few non-trivial regimes of parameters
J,\mu,U\in\mathbb{R}
, i.e., regimes where the ground state energy is neither
0
nor
-\infty
. The most physically relevant regime corresponds to
J\geq 0
,
\mu>0
, and
U>0
. The physically less relevant regimes, with non-trivial ground state, occur for negative hopping amplitude, i.e., when
J<0
, together with
U>0
and
\mu>J
.

Remark 4
.

In the regime where
J\geq 0
,
\mu>0
, and
U>0
, one expects a superfluid phase for
\frac{J}{U}\gg 1
. For small values of
\frac{J}{U}
, a Mott insulating phase can occur. Mean-field theory predicts a phase diagram as in Figure

(Multiple-species bosonic states and reduced density matrices)

\displaystyle\gamma_{N_{1},N_{2}}^{(k_{1},k_{2})}\coloneq\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{1}^{\otimes(N_{1}-k_{1})}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{2}^{\otimes(N_{2}-k_{2})}}\left(\gamma_{N_{1},N_{2}}\right)

We extend Definition

\displaystyle\gamma_{N_{1},\infty}\coloneq\left(\gamma_{N_{1},N_{2}}\right)_{N_{2}\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathcal{L}\left(\mathcal{H}_{1}^{\otimes_{+}N_{1}}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{+}\left(\mathcal{H}_{2}\right)\right)

2
Ground state energy convergence

2.1
Upper energy bound

The upper bound is trivial in the sense that it is sufficient to take a factorized state as a trial state. Indeed, the Bose–Hubbard energy per lattice site for a lattice-site-factorized state is equal to the mean-field energy.

Proposition 8

(Upper energy bound)
.

Let
\varphi\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{C})
such that
\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}}=1

\displaystyle\frac{\left<\varphi^{\otimes\left|V_{z}\right|},H_{V_{z}}\varphi^{\otimes\left|V_{z}\right|}\right>}{\left|V_{z}\right|}=\left<\varphi,h_{\varphi}\varphi\right>

(10)

and hence

\displaystyle\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\psi\in\mathcal{F}_{V_{z}}\\
\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{\mathcal{F}_{V_{z}}}=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\left<\psi,H_{V_{z}}\psi\right>}{\left|V\right|}\leq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varphi\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{C})\\
\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}}=1\end{subarray}}\left<\varphi,h_{\varphi}\varphi\right>.

(11)

Proof.

Using (

) and then (
1
), (

), and (

), we obtain

2.2
Translation invariance

Noting that (
4

\displaystyle H_{1,z}\coloneq\sum_{i=1}^{z}\left(-J\left(a_{0}^{*}a_{i}+a_{i}^{*}a_{0}\right)+(J-\mu)\left(\mathcal{N}_{0}+\mathcal{N}_{i}\right)+\frac{U}{2}\left(\mathcal{N}_{0}(\mathcal{N}_{0}-1)+\mathcal{N}_{i}(\mathcal{N}_{i}-1)\right)\right)

\displaystyle\forall x\in V_{z},\ T(V_{z}^{x})=V_{z}^{T(x)}.

For instance, in the examples we provide in Remark

The Hamiltonian (

\displaystyle\forall K\in\mathcal{K}\left(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{C})^{\otimes\left|W\right|}\right),\ \text{Tr}\left(\mathrm{Tr}_{V_{z}\backslash W}\left(\gamma\right)K\right)\coloneq\text{Tr}\left(\gamma K_{W}\right),

Proposition 9

(Energy reduction via translation invariance)
.

For

We find

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in V}(H_{1,z})_{x,V_{z}^{x}}=

3
Polaron-type quantum de Finetti theorems

3.1
Finite dimensional approximation

Following the previous subsection, we develop a de Finetti theorem for a system with one distinct core particle and a symmetric shell of
N\in\mathbb{N}
particles. We start by assuming that the shell Hilbert space has finite dimension, namely
\mathbb{C}^{m+1}
with
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\mathcal{H}_{0}
be a separable Hilbert space representing the core particle. This setting is similar to the one of the polaron model where one has an impurity in a bath of indistinguishable particles. This is why we expect that our methods might be applicable to proving mean-field limits for such models as well.

We define the symmetric projection

\displaystyle\Pi_{N}^{+}\coloneq\left(\frac{1}{N!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{N}}U_{\sigma}\right):\left(\mathbb{C}^{m+1}\right)^{\otimes N}\to\left(\mathbb{C}^{m+1}\right)^{\otimes_{+}N},

where
S_{N}
is the permutation group on
N

is the unitary defined by

\displaystyle\forall\psi\in\left(\mathbb{C}^{m+1}\right)^{\otimes N},\ x_{1:N}\in\left(\mathbb{C}^{m+1}\right)^{N},\ U_{\sigma}\psi(x_{1:N})\coloneq\psi\left(x_{\sigma(1)},\dots,x_{\sigma(N)}\right).

Denote by
S^{m}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{m+1}
the complex
m
-sphere, with
h_{m}
being the normalized Haar measure on
S^{m}
. We recall the Schur formula:

\displaystyle\Pi_{N}^{+}=\left(\begin{matrix}N+m\\
m\end{matrix}\right)\int\limits_{S^{m}}p_{u}^{\otimes N}\,dh_{m}(u),

(26)

where

is the orthogonal projection onto

. This is a consequence of Schur’s lemma applied to the following irreducible representation:

\displaystyle\begin{matrix}&\mathcal{U}_{m+1}(\mathbb{C})&\to&\textnormal{End}\left((\mathbb{C}^{m+1})^{\otimes_{+}N}\right)\\
&U&\mapsto&U^{\otimes N}.\end{matrix}

Our first result is the following.

(Polaron quantum de Finetti for finite

\displaystyle\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes p_{u}^{\otimes k}\ \gamma_{k}(u)\ \mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes p_{u}^{\otimes k}=\gamma_{0}(u)\otimes p_{u}^{\otimes k}.

Indeed, if
A\in\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H}_{0})
and
B\in\mathcal{K}\left(\left(\mathbb{C}^{m+1}\right)^{\otimes k}\right)
,

\displaystyle\text{Tr}\left(A\otimes B\ \mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes p_{u}^{\otimes k}\ \gamma_{k}(u)\ \mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes p_{u}^{\otimes k}\right)=

\displaystyle\ \text{Tr}\left(A\otimes p_{u}^{\otimes k}Bp_{u}^{\otimes k}\ \gamma_{k}(u)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \text{Tr}\left(A\otimes p_{u}^{\otimes k}\ \gamma_{k}(u)\right)\text{Tr}\left(p_{u}^{\otimes k}B\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \text{Tr}\left(A\otimes p_{u}^{\otimes N}\ \gamma_{1,N}\right)\text{Tr}\left(p_{u}^{\otimes k}B\right)=\text{Tr}\left(A\gamma_{0}(u)\right)\text{Tr}\left(p_{u}^{\otimes k}B\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \text{Tr}\left(A\otimes B\ \gamma_{0}(u)\otimes p_{u}^{\otimes k}\right).

Inserting (
31
) into (
27
),

\displaystyle\eta_{1,N}^{(1,k)}=

\displaystyle\ \left(\begin{matrix}N+m\\
m\end{matrix}\right)\int\limits_{S^{m}}\gamma_{0}(u)\otimes p_{u}^{\otimes k}dh_{m}(u)=\left(\begin{matrix}N+m\\
m\end{matrix}\right)\int\limits_{S^{m}}\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes p_{u}^{\otimes k}\ \gamma_{k}(u)\ \mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes p_{u}^{\otimes k}dh_{m}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \left(\begin{matrix}N-k+m\\
m\end{matrix}\right)\int\limits_{S^{m}}\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes p_{u}^{\otimes k}\ \gamma_{k}(u)\ \mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes p_{u}^{\otimes k}dh_{m}(u)+\left(1-\frac{\left(\begin{matrix}N-k+m\\
m\end{matrix}\right)}{\left(\begin{matrix}N+m\\
m\end{matrix}\right)}\right)\eta_{1,N}^{(1,k)}.

(32)

Moreover, using (
26
),

\displaystyle\left(\begin{matrix}N+m\\
m\end{matrix}\right)\int\limits_{S_{m}}\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes p_{u}^{\otimes k}\ \gamma_{k}(u)dh_{m}(u)=\left(\begin{matrix}N+m\\
m\end{matrix}\right)\int\limits_{S_{m}}\left(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes p_{u}^{\otimes N}\ \gamma_{1,N}\right)^{(1,k)}dh_{m}(u)=\gamma_{1,N}^{(1,k)}.

(33)

As
\gamma_{k}(u)\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\ \int\limits_{S^{m}}\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes(1-p_{u}^{\otimes k})\ \gamma_{k}(u)\ \mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes(1-p_{u}^{\otimes k})dh_{m}(u)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \text{Tr}\left(\ \int\limits_{S^{m}}\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes(1-p_{u}^{\otimes k})\ \gamma_{k}(u)\ \mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes(1-p_{u}^{\otimes k})dh_{m}(u)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \text{Tr}\left(\ \int\limits_{S^{m}}\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes(1-p_{u}^{\otimes k})\ \gamma_{k}(u)dh_{m}(u)\right)\leq\left\lVert\ \int\limits_{S^{m}}\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes(1-p_{u}^{\otimes k})\ \gamma_{k}(u)dh_{m}(u)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}.

Hence, combining

\displaystyle\gamma_{k}(u)-\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes p_{u}^{\otimes k}\ \gamma_{k}(u)\ \mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes p_{u}^{\otimes k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes(1-p_{u}^{\otimes k})\ \gamma_{k}(u)+\gamma_{k}(u)\ \mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes(1-p_{u}^{\otimes k})-\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes(1-p_{u}^{\otimes k})\ \gamma_{k}(u)\ \mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes(1-p_{u}^{\otimes k}),

with (
30
) and (
33
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left\lVert\int\limits_{S_{m}}\left(\gamma_{k}(u)-\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes p_{u}^{\otimes k}\ \gamma_{k}(u)\ \mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes p_{u}^{\otimes k}\right)dh_{m}(u)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}\leq 3\left\lVert\int\limits_{S^{m}}\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes(1-p_{u}^{\otimes k})\ \gamma_{k}(u)dh_{m}(u)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ 3\left\lVert\left(\left(\begin{matrix}N-k+m\\
m\end{matrix}\right)^{-1}-\left(\begin{matrix}N+m\\
m\end{matrix}\right)^{-1}\right)\gamma_{1,N}^{(1,k)}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}=3\left(\left(\begin{matrix}N-k+m\\
m\end{matrix}\right)^{-1}-\left(\begin{matrix}N+m\\
m\end{matrix}\right)^{-1}\right).

(34)

With (
30
) and (
32
),

\displaystyle\gamma_{1,N}^{(1,k)}-\eta_{1,N}^{(1,k)}=

\displaystyle\ \left(\begin{matrix}N-k+m\\
m\end{matrix}\right)\int\limits_{S_{m}}\left(\gamma_{k}(u)-\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes p_{u}^{\otimes k}\ \gamma_{k}(u)\ \mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes p_{u}^{\otimes k}\right)dh_{m}(u)

\displaystyle+\left(1-\frac{\left(\begin{matrix}N-k+m\\
m\end{matrix}\right)}{\left(\begin{matrix}N+m\\
m\end{matrix}\right)}\right)\eta_{1,N}^{(1,k)}.

Then, inserting (
34
) and (
29
) along with

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\left(\begin{matrix}N-k+m\\
m\end{matrix}\right)}{\left(\begin{matrix}N+m\\
m\end{matrix}\right)}=\frac{(N-k+m)!N!}{(N-k)!(N+m)!}=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{N-k+i}{N+i}\geq\left(\frac{N-k+1}{N+1}\right)^{m}=\left(1-\frac{k}{N+1}\right)^{m}

\displaystyle\geq 1-\frac{mk}{N+1},

we get

\displaystyle\left\lVert\gamma_{1,N}^{(1,k)}-\eta_{1,N}^{(1,k)}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}\leq 4\left(1-\frac{\left(\begin{matrix}N-k+m\\
m\end{matrix}\right)}{\left(\begin{matrix}N+m\\
m\end{matrix}\right)}\right)\leq 4\frac{mk}{N+1}.

(35)

∎

Remark 16
.

Some comments:

•

Our intuition is that (
31
) is the main novelty compared to the usual de Finetti argument.
A priori
, we only expect factorization to occur between the many symmetric variables, and not between the impurity and the rest. However, since we are able to project the symmetric part onto a rank-one projection
p_{u}^{\otimes k}
, the resulting
(1,k)
-variable operators exhibit a full factorization.

•

De Finetti theorems have already been used for multi-species systems
[
20
]
, with a large number of symmetric particles in each species. Our De Finetti theorem can also be generalized to this setting: one impurity and multiple species with a large number of particles in each species.

We now consider the limit
N\to\infty
while keeping the dimension
m+1
of the Hilbert space of the symmetric particles fixed. Note the following.

Definition 17

(
S^{1}
-invariance)
.

Let
\zeta
be a trace-class valued functions and
\mathbb{P}
a probability measure on a Hilbert space.
(\zeta,\mathbb{P})
is said to be
S^{1}
invariant if

\displaystyle\forall\theta\in\mathbb{R},\ e^{i\theta}_{*}\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}\text{ and }\mathbb{P}\text{-a.e.},\zeta=\zeta\circ e^{i\theta},

where the phase
e^{i\theta}
is set to act multiplicatively on every complex coordinates of the Hilbert space.

We denote by
\mathcal{M}
the set of Radon measures and
C^{0}
spaces of continuous functions.

Theorem 18

(Polaron quantum de Finetti for
N=\infty
)
.

Let
\gamma_{m}\in\mathcal{L}\left(\mathcal{H}_{0}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{+}\left(\mathbb{C}^{m+1}\right)\right)
be a
(1,\infty)
-bosonic state. Then there exist a unique
S^{1}
-invariant

•

probability measure
\mathbb{P}_{m}\in\mathcal{M}\left(S^{m},\mathbb{R}_{+}\right)
,

•

function
\zeta_{m}\in L^{1}\left(S^{m},\mathcal{L}^{1}_{+}(\mathcal{H}_{0})\right)
satisfying,
\mathbb{P}_{m}
-a.e.,
\text{Tr}\left(\zeta_{m}\right)=1
,

such that

\displaystyle\forall k\in\mathbb{N},\ \gamma_{m}^{(1,k)}=\int\limits_{S^{m}}\zeta_{m}(u)\otimes p_{u}^{\otimes k}\ d\mathbb{P}_{m}(u).

(36)

Moreover, up to a subsequence, we have the following approximation:

\displaystyle\left(\begin{matrix}N+m\\
m\end{matrix}\right)\left(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes p_{\bullet}^{\otimes N}\ \gamma_{m}^{(1,N)}\right)^{(1,0)}h_{m}\wslim\displaylimits_{N\to\infty}\zeta_{m}\mathbb{P}_{m}\text{ as trace-class valued Radon measures.}

(37)

Proof.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we define

\displaystyle\zeta_{N,m}:\begin{matrix}S^{m}&\to&\mathcal{L}_{+}^{1}\left(\mathcal{H}_{0}\right)\\
u&\mapsto&\left(\begin{matrix}N+m\\
m\end{matrix}\right)\left(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes p_{u}^{\otimes N}\ \gamma_{m}^{(1,N)}\right)^{(1,0)}\end{matrix}

(38)

and observe that, using (
26
),

\displaystyle\left\lVert\zeta_{N,m}\right\rVert_{L^{1}}=

\displaystyle\ \int\limits_{S^{m}}\left\lVert\zeta_{N,m}(u)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}dh_{m}(u)=\left(\begin{matrix}N+m\\
m\end{matrix}\right)\int\limits_{S^{m}}\text{Tr}\left(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes p_{u}^{\otimes N}\ \gamma_{m}^{(1,N)}\right)dh_{m}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \text{Tr}\left(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes\Pi_{N}^{+}\ \gamma_{m}^{(1,N)}\right)=\text{Tr}\left(\gamma_{m}^{(1,N)}\right)=1.

Thus

\displaystyle\left(\zeta_{N,m}h_{m}\right)_{N}\subseteq\mathcal{M}\left(S^{m},\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{H}_{0})\right)=C^{0}\left(S^{m},\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H}_{0})\right)^{*}

is a bounded sequence of trace-class-valued Radon measures. By the Banach–Alaoglu theorem, up to a subsequence,

\displaystyle\zeta_{N,m}h_{m}\wslim\displaylimits_{N\to\infty}Z_{m}\in\mathcal{M}\left(S^{m},\mathcal{L}_{+}^{1}(\mathcal{H}_{0})\right).

(39)

Moreover,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{m}\coloneq\left\lVert Z_{m}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}=\text{Tr}\left(Z_{m}\right)\in\mathcal{M}\left(S^{m},\mathbb{R}_{+}\right)

(40)

has bounded total variation since

\displaystyle\text{Tr}\left(Z_{m}(S^{m})\right)\leq\liminf_{N\to\infty}\left\lVert\zeta_{N,m}\right\rVert_{L^{1}}=1.

As
Z_{m}\ll\mathbb{P}_{m}
, by the Radon–Nikodym theorem,

\displaystyle\exists\zeta_{m}\in L^{1}\left(S^{m},\mathcal{L}^{1}_{+}(\mathcal{H}_{0})\right)\text{ such that }Z_{m}=\zeta_{m}\mathbb{P}_{m},

and (
39
) implies (
37
). Taking the trace, it follows from (
40
) that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{m}=\text{Tr}\left(\zeta_{m}\right)\mathbb{P}_{m},

meaning that
\mathbb{P}_{m}
-a.e.,
\text{Tr}\left(\zeta_{m}\right)=1
. Then (
39
) has the following meaning:

\displaystyle\forall\varphi\in C^{0}\left(S^{m},\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H}_{0})\right),\ \int\limits_{S^{m}}\text{Tr}\left(\zeta_{N,m}(u)\varphi(u)\right)dh_{m}(u)\limitoperator\displaylimits\displaylimits_{N\to\infty}\int\limits_{S^{m}}\text{Tr}\left(\zeta_{m}(u)\varphi(u)\right)d\mathbb{P}_{m}(u).

(41)

With the same notation as in Theorem
15
applied to
\gamma_{m}^{(1,N)}
, we define

\displaystyle\eta_{1,N}\coloneq\int\limits_{S^{m}}\zeta_{N,m}(u)\otimes p_{u}^{\otimes N}dh_{m}(u).

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
K\in\mathcal{K}\left(\mathcal{H}_{0}\otimes\left(\mathbb{C}^{m+1}\right)^{\otimes_{+}k}\right)
. Considering the test function

\displaystyle u\mapsto\left(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes p_{u}^{\otimes k}\ K\right)^{(1,0)}\in C^{0}\left(S^{m},\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H}_{0})\right),

in (
41
), we obtain

\displaystyle\text{Tr}\left(\eta_{1,N}^{(1,k)}K\right)=

\displaystyle\ \int\limits_{S^{m}}\text{Tr}\left(\zeta_{N,m}(u)\otimes p_{u}^{\otimes k}\ K\right)dh_{m}(u)=\int\limits_{S^{m}}\text{Tr}\left(\zeta_{N,m}(u)\left(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes p_{u}^{\otimes k}\ K\right)^{(1,0)}\right)dh_{m}(u)

\displaystyle\limitoperator\displaylimits\displaylimits_{N\to\infty}

\displaystyle\ \int\limits_{S^{m}}\text{Tr}\left(\zeta_{m}(u)\left(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes p_{u}^{\otimes k}\ K\right)^{(1,0)}\right)d\mathbb{P}_{m}(u)=\int\limits_{S^{m}}\text{Tr}\left(\zeta_{m}(u)\otimes p_{u}^{\otimes k}\ K\right)d\mathbb{P}_{m}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \text{Tr}\left(\left(\ \int\limits_{S^{m}}\zeta_{m}(u)\otimes p_{u}^{\otimes k}\ d\mathbb{P}_{m}(u)\right)K\right).

Hence,

\displaystyle\eta_{1,N}^{(1,k)}\wslim\displaylimits_{N\to\infty}\int\limits_{S^{m}}\zeta_{m}(u)\otimes p_{u}^{\otimes k}\ d\mathbb{P}_{m}(u).

Using (
28
), and observing that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\eta_{1,N}^{(1,k)}-\gamma_{m}^{(1,k)}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}=\left\lVert\eta_{1,N}^{(1,k)}-\left(\gamma_{m}^{(1,N)}\right)^{(1,k)}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}\limitoperator\displaylimits_{N\to\infty}0,

we conclude that

\displaystyle\gamma_{m}^{(1,k)}=\int\limits_{S^{m}}\zeta_{m}(u)\otimes p_{u}^{\otimes k}\ d\mathbb{P}_{m}(u).

Finally, taking the trace,

\displaystyle 1=\text{Tr}\left(\gamma_{m}^{(1,k)}\right)=\int\limits_{S^{m}}\text{Tr}\left(\zeta_{m}(u)\right)\text{Tr}\left(p_{u}\right)^{k}\,d\mathbb{P}_{m}(u)=\int\limits_{S^{m}}d\mathbb{P}_{m}(u)=\mathbb{P}_{m}(S^{m}),

so
\mathbb{P}_{m}
is a probability measure.

S^{1}
-Invariance.

From (
37
) we infer that
\zeta_{m}\mathbb{P}_{m}
is
S^{1}
-invariant as a trace-class-valued measure, being the limit of such measures. Taking the trace, this implies that
(\zeta_{m},\mathbb{P}_{m})
is
S^{1}
-invariant in the sense of Definition
17
.

Tracing out the first variable in (
36
), we reduce the situation to the known case where
\mathds{P}_{m}
is the unique
S^{1}
-invariant probability measure on
S^{m}
satisfying
(
36
)
. Then, if there exists another candidate
\widetilde{\zeta}_{m}\in L^{1}\left(S^{m},\mathcal{L}^{1}_{+}(\mathcal{H}_{0})\right)
, we find that

\displaystyle\forall k\in\mathbb{N},\ \int\limits_{S^{m}}\left(\zeta_{m}(u)-\widetilde{\zeta}_{m}(u)\right)\otimes p_{u}^{\otimes k}\ d\mathbb{P}_{m}(u)=0.

Using the standard arguments for the uniqueness of
\mathbb{P}_{m}
, i.e., testing the above against
\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes\bigotimes_{i=1}^{k}A_{i}
for
A_{1:k}\in\mathcal{L}\left(\mathbb{C}^{m+1}\right)
self-adjoint and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we find, by density (Stone–Weierstrass theorem) of the algebra generated by functions of the form

\displaystyle u\mapsto\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left<u,A_{i}u\right>\in C^{0}\left({{}^{S^{m}}}/{{}_{S^{1}}},\mathbb{R}\right),

that
\forall f\in C^{0}\left({{}^{S^{m}}}/{{}_{S^{1}}},\mathbb{R}\right)
,

\displaystyle\int\limits_{S^{m}}\left(\zeta_{m}(u)-\widetilde{\zeta}_{m}(u)\right)f(u)\ d\mathbb{P}_{m}(u)=0.

This implies that

\displaystyle\left(\zeta_{m}-\widetilde{\zeta}_{m}\right)\mathbb{P}_{m}=0

as a trace-class-valued measure, meaning that
\mathbb{P}_{m}
-a.e.,
\zeta_{m}=\widetilde{\zeta}_{m}
.
∎

3.2
Fock space localization

In order to deal with the finite-dimensional approximation in (
18
), and replace
\mathbb{C}^{m+1}
by a separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{s}
, we use the Fock space localization method, see
[
18
]
.

Proposition 19

(Fock space localization)
.

Let
\gamma_{1,N}\in\mathcal{L}\left(\mathcal{H}_{0}\otimes\left(\mathcal{H}_{s}\right)^{\otimes_{+}N}\right)
be a
(1,N)
-bosonic state,
P
be an orthonormal projection on
\mathcal{H}_{s}
, and
Q\coloneq\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}-P
. The trace-class valued measure

\displaystyle M_{N,k}\coloneq\sum_{n=k}^{N}\left(\begin{matrix}N\\
n\end{matrix}\right)\left(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes P^{\otimes n}\otimes Q^{\otimes(N-n)}\ \gamma_{1,N}\ \mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes P^{\otimes n}\otimes Q^{\otimes(N-n)}\right)^{(1,k)}\delta_{\frac{n}{N}}

satisfies

\displaystyle\forall k\in\mathbb{N},\ \left\lVert\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes P^{\otimes k}\ \gamma_{1,N}^{(1,k)}\ \mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes P^{\otimes k}-\int\limits_{0}^{1}\lambda^{k}\,dM_{N,k}(\lambda)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}\leq\frac{k(k-1)}{N}.

(42)

Proof.

Using the symmetry of
\gamma_{1,N}
with respect to its last
N
variables, we may apply the commutative binomial formula and observe that, for
k\in\left\llbracket 0,N\right\rrbracket
,

\displaystyle\gamma_{1,N}^{(1,k)}=

\displaystyle\ \left(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}^{\otimes k}\otimes(P+Q)^{\otimes(N-k)}\ \gamma_{1,N}\right)^{(1,k)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \sum_{n=0}^{N-k}\left(\begin{matrix}N-k\\
n\end{matrix}\right)\left(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}^{\otimes k}\otimes P^{\otimes n}\otimes Q^{\otimes(N-k-n)}\ \gamma_{1,N}\right)^{(1,k)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \sum_{n=k}^{N}\left(\begin{matrix}N-k\\
n-k\end{matrix}\right)\left(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}^{\otimes k}\otimes P^{\otimes(n-k)}\otimes Q^{\otimes(N-n)}\ \gamma_{1,N}\right)^{(1,k)}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes P^{\otimes k}\ \gamma_{1,N}^{(1,k)}\ \mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes P^{\otimes k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \sum_{n=k}^{N}\left(\begin{matrix}N-k\\
n-k\end{matrix}\right)\left(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes P^{\otimes n}\otimes Q^{\otimes(N-n)}\ \gamma_{1,N}\ \mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes P^{\otimes n}\otimes Q^{\otimes(N-n)}\right)^{(1,k)}.

(43)

Combining (
43
) with the definition of
M_{N,k}
yields

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes P^{\otimes k}\ \gamma_{1,N}^{(1,k)}\ \mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes P^{\otimes k}-\int\limits_{0}^{1}\lambda^{k}\,dM_{N,k}(\lambda)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \sum_{n=k}^{N}\left|\left(\begin{matrix}N-k\\
n-k\end{matrix}\right)-\left(\begin{matrix}N\\
n\end{matrix}\right)\left(\frac{n}{N}\right)^{k}\right|\text{Tr}\left(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes P^{\otimes n}\otimes Q^{\otimes(N-n)}\ \gamma_{1,N}\right).

(44)

Assume that
k\geq 1
and let
n\in\left\llbracket k,N\right\rrbracket
. We estimate

\displaystyle\left(\begin{matrix}N\\
n\end{matrix}\right)^{-1}\left(\begin{matrix}N-k\\
n-k\end{matrix}\right)=

\displaystyle\ \frac{n!(N-k)!}{N!(n-k)!}=\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{n-j}{N-j}\geq\left(\frac{n-(k-1)}{N}\right)^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \left(\frac{n}{N}\right)^{k}\left(1-\frac{k-1}{n}\right)^{k}\geq\left(\frac{n}{N}\right)^{k}\left(1-\frac{k(k-1)}{n}\right).

Hence,

\displaystyle 0\leq\left(\frac{n}{N}\right)^{k}-\left(\begin{matrix}N\\
n\end{matrix}\right)^{-1}\left(\begin{matrix}N-k\\
n-k\end{matrix}\right)\leq\left(\frac{n}{N}\right)^{k}\frac{k(k-1)}{n}\leq\frac{k(k-1)}{N}.

If
k=0
, the right-hand side in (
44
) vanishes. Otherwise, it is bounded by

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes P^{\otimes k}\ \gamma_{1,N}^{(1,k)}\ \mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes P^{\otimes k}-\int\limits_{0}^{1}\lambda^{k}\,dM_{N,k}(\lambda)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \frac{k(k-1)}{N}\sum_{n=k}^{N}\left(\begin{matrix}N\\
n\end{matrix}\right)\text{Tr}\left(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes P^{\otimes n}\otimes Q^{\otimes(N-n)}\ \gamma_{1,N}\right)\leq\frac{k(k-1)}{N}.

∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem
7
. Let us denote by
S_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}
the sphere in
\mathcal{H}_{s}
. The core idea is that
P_{m}
acts on the symmetric variables, thereby reducing the statement to the finite-dimensional case.

Proof of Theorem
7
.

Let
N,m\in\mathbb{N}
,
k\in\left\llbracket 0,N\right\rrbracket
, and with the usual notation,

\displaystyle Q_{m}\coloneq\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}-P_{m}.

In view of applying Proposition
19
to
\gamma^{(1,N)}
, we define

\displaystyle M_{N,k,m}\coloneq\sum_{n=k}^{N}\underbrace{\left(\begin{matrix}N\\
n\end{matrix}\right)\left(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes P_{m}^{\otimes n}\otimes Q_{m}^{\otimes(N-n)}\ \gamma^{(1,N)}\ \mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes P_{m}^{\otimes n}\otimes Q_{m}^{\otimes(N-n)}\right)^{(1,k)}}_{\coloneq\eta_{N,k}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}_{+}\left(\mathcal{H}_{0}\otimes(P_{m}\mathcal{H}_{s})^{\otimes_{+}k}\right)}\delta_{\frac{n}{N}}.

(45)

\bullet
Taking
N\to\infty
:

\left(M_{N,k,m}\right)_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in
\mathcal{M}\left(\left[0,1\right],\mathcal{L}^{1}_{+}\left(\mathcal{H}_{0}\otimes(P_{m}\mathcal{H}_{s})^{\otimes_{+}k}\right)\right)
:

\displaystyle\text{Tr}\left(M_{N,k,m}(\left[0,1\right])\right)=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{N}\text{Tr}\left(\eta_{N,k}\right)=\sum_{n=k}^{N}\left(\begin{matrix}N\\
n\end{matrix}\right)\text{Tr}\left(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes P^{\otimes n}\otimes Q^{\otimes(N-n)}\ \gamma^{(1,N)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \sum_{n=0}^{N}\left(\begin{matrix}N\\
n\end{matrix}\right)\text{Tr}\left(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes P^{\otimes n}\otimes Q^{\otimes(N-n)}\ \gamma^{(1,N)}\right)=\text{Tr}\left(\gamma^{(1,N)}\right)=1.

(46)

Hence, after extraction,

\displaystyle M_{N,k,m}\wslim\displaylimits_{N\to\infty}M_{k,m}\in\mathcal{M}\left(\left[0,1\right],\mathcal{L}^{1}_{+}\left(\mathcal{H}_{0}\otimes(P_{m}\mathcal{H}_{s})^{\otimes_{+}k}\right)\right).

(47)

Let
K\in\mathcal{K}\left(\mathcal{H}_{0}\otimes(P_{m}\mathcal{H}_{s})^{\otimes_{+}k}\right)
. Then
\lambda\mapsto\lambda^{k}K\in C^{0}\left(\left[0,1\right],\mathcal{K}\left(\mathcal{H}_{0}\otimes(P_{m}\mathcal{H}_{s})^{\otimes_{+}k}\right)\right)
, and thus

\displaystyle\text{Tr}\left(K\int\limits_{0}^{1}\lambda^{k}dM_{N,k,m}(\lambda)\right)\limitoperator\displaylimits_{N\to\infty}\text{Tr}\left(K\int\limits_{0}^{1}\lambda^{k}dM_{k,m}(\lambda)\right).

It follows that

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{1}\lambda^{k}dM_{N,k,m}(\lambda)\wslim\displaylimits_{N\to\infty}\int\limits_{0}^{1}\lambda^{k}dM_{k,m}(\lambda)\in\mathcal{L}^{1}\left(\mathcal{H}_{0}\otimes(P_{m}\mathcal{H}_{s})^{\otimes_{+}k}\right).

Together with (

\displaystyle\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes P_{m}^{\otimes k}\ \gamma^{(1,k)}\ \mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes P_{m}^{\otimes k}=\int\limits_{0}^{1}\lambda^{k}dM_{k,m}(\lambda),

(48)

since
\left(\gamma^{(1,N)}\right)^{(1,k)}=\gamma^{(1,k)}
is now independent of

\bullet
Consistency of

:

We can canonically extend Definitions
5
and
6
to trace-class valued measures. Since

is finite-dimensional, we can trace out one coordinate in (

) to obtain

\displaystyle M_{N,k+1,m}^{(1,k)}\wslim\displaylimits_{N\to\infty}M_{k+1,m}^{(1,k)}.

(49)

Following from (

),

\displaystyle M_{N,k+1,m}^{(1,k)}=M_{N,k,m}-\eta_{N,k}\delta_{\frac{k}{N}}.

(50)

The estimate (
46
) implies that
\left\lVert\eta_{N,k}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}=\text{Tr}\left(\eta_{N,k}\right)\leq 1
, so we can extract a limit

\displaystyle\eta_{N,k}\wslim\displaylimits_{N\to\infty}\eta_{k}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}_{+}\left(\mathcal{H}_{0}\otimes(P_{m}\mathcal{H}_{s})^{\otimes_{+}k}\right).

Let
\varphi\in C^{0}\left(\left[0,1\right],\mathcal{K}\left(\mathcal{H}_{0}\otimes(P_{m}\mathcal{H}_{s})^{\otimes_{+}k}\right)\right)
. Then

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{1}\text{Tr}\left(\eta_{N,k}\varphi(\lambda)\right)d\delta_{\frac{k}{N}}=

\displaystyle\ \text{Tr}\left(\eta_{N,k}\varphi\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right)=\text{Tr}\left(\eta_{N,k}\varphi(0)\right)+\text{Tr}\left(\eta_{N,k}\left(\varphi\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)-\varphi(0)\right)\right)

\displaystyle\limitoperator\displaylimits_{N\to\infty}

\displaystyle\ \text{Tr}\left(\eta_{k}\varphi(0)\right),

since
\varphi(0)
is compact and
\left\lVert\varphi\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)-\varphi(0)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}}\limitoperator\displaylimits\limits_{N\to\infty}0
by continuity of

. We deduce that

\displaystyle\eta_{N,k}\delta_{\frac{k}{N}}\wslim\displaylimits_{N\to\infty}\eta_{k}\delta_{0}\in\mathcal{M}\left(\left[0,1\right],\mathcal{L}^{1}_{+}\left(\mathcal{H}_{0}\otimes(P_{m}\mathcal{H}_{s})^{\otimes_{+}k}\right)\right).

Together with (
47
) and (

), taking

\displaystyle M_{k+1,m}^{(1,k)}=M_{k,m}-\eta_{k}\delta_{0}.

(51)

Keeping in mind that

will be integrated against

(see (

. Hence, let

\displaystyle\forall B\in\mathcal{B}(\left[0,1\right]),\ \widetilde{M}_{k,m}(B)=M_{k,m}\left(B\backslash\left\{0\right\}\right)+M_{0,m}\left(B\cap\left\{0\right\}\right)\otimes\left(\frac{\mathds{1}_{\mathbb{C}^{m+1}}}{m+1}\right)^{\otimes k},

so that
\widetilde{M}_{0,m}=M_{0,m}
and, with (

\displaystyle\ \text{Tr}\left(M_{0,m}\left([0,1]\right)\right)\leq\liminf_{N\to\infty}\text{Tr}\left(M_{N,k,m}(\left[0,1\right])\right)\leq 1,

(52)

and

is consistent in

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}_{k+1,m}^{(1,k)}=\widetilde{M}_{k,m}.

\bullet
Applying Theorem

:

By the Radon–Nikodym theorem, there exists
\gamma_{k,m}\in L^{1}\left([0,1],\mathcal{L}^{1}_{+}\left(\mathcal{H}_{0}\otimes(P_{m}\mathcal{H}_{s})^{\otimes_{+}k}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}_{k,m}=\gamma_{k,m}\text{Tr}\left(\widetilde{M}_{k,m}\right)=\gamma_{k,m}\text{Tr}\left(\widetilde{M}_{0,m}\right),

with
\text{Tr}\left(\widetilde{M}_{k,m}\right)
-a.e.
\text{Tr}\left(\gamma_{k,m}\right)=1
. Note that the consistency equation (

) implies that
\text{Tr}\left(\widetilde{M}_{k,m}\right)

. Hence,

\displaystyle\gamma_{k+1,m}^{(1,k)}\text{Tr}\left(\widetilde{M}_{0,m}\right)=\gamma_{k,m}\text{Tr}\left(\widetilde{M}_{0,m}\right),

and
(\gamma_{k,m})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is consistent
\text{Tr}\left(\widetilde{M}_{0,m}\right)
-a.e. Then, for
\text{Tr}\left(\widetilde{M}_{0,m}\right)
-a.e.
\lambda\in\left[0,1\right]
, we can apply Theorem
18
to
\left(\gamma_{k,m}(\lambda)\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
: there exist

•

a probability measure
\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,m}\in\mathcal{M}\left(S^{m},\mathbb{R}_{+}\right)
,

•

a function
\zeta_{m}(\lambda,\bullet)\in L^{1}\left(S^{m},\mathcal{L}^{1}_{+}(\mathcal{H}_{0})\right)
satisfying,
\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,m}
-a.e.,
\text{Tr}\left(\zeta_{m}(\lambda,\bullet)\right)=1
,

such that

\displaystyle\forall k\in\mathbb{N},\ \gamma_{k,m}(\lambda)=\int\limits_{S^{m}}\zeta_{m}(\lambda,u)\otimes p_{u}^{\otimes k}\ d\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,m}(u).

(55)

Here

stands for the

. From (

) and then (

\displaystyle\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes P_{m}^{\otimes k}\ \gamma^{(1,k)}\ \mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes P_{m}^{\otimes k}=

\displaystyle\ \int\limits_{0}^{1}\lambda^{k}dM_{k,m}(\lambda)=\int\limits_{0}^{1}\lambda^{k}d\widetilde{M}_{k,m}(\lambda)=\int\limits_{0}^{1}\lambda^{k}\gamma_{k,m}(\lambda)d\text{Tr}\left(\widetilde{M}_{0,m}\right)(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \int\limits_{0}^{1}\lambda^{k}\left(\ \int\limits_{S^{m}}\zeta_{m}(\lambda,u)\otimes p_{u}^{\otimes k}\ d\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,m}(u)\right)d\text{Tr}\left(\widetilde{M}_{0,m}\right)(\lambda).

(56)

\bullet
Construction of a measure on
\left[0,1\right]\times S^{m}
:

We observe that, by testing (
56
) against
\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes\bigotimes_{i=1}^{k}A_{i}
for

self-adjoint and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we find, by density (Stone–Weierstrass theorem) of the algebra generated by functions of the form

\displaystyle\lambda,u\mapsto\lambda^{k}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left<u,A_{i}u\right>\in C^{0}\left(\left[0,1\right]\times{{}^{S^{m}}}/{{}_{S^{1}}},\mathbb{R}\right),

that we can define
\mathbb{P}_{m}\coloneq\mathbb{P}_{\bullet,m}\otimes\text{Tr}\left(\widetilde{M}_{0,m}\right)
as a measure on
\left[0,1\right]\times S^{m}
, which is uniquely determined on measurable rectangles by

\displaystyle\forall A\in\mathcal{B}(\left[0,1\right]),\ B\in\mathcal{B}(S^{m}),\ \mathbb{P}_{m}(A\times B)\coloneq\int\limits_{\left[0,1\right]}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,m}(B)\,d\text{Tr}\left(\widetilde{M}_{0,m}\right)(\lambda).

This measure is finite, due to (

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{m}(\left[0,1\right]\times S^{m})=\int\limits_{\left[0,1\right]}d\text{Tr}\left(\widetilde{M}_{0,m}\right)(\lambda)=\text{Tr}\left(\widetilde{M}_{0,m}(\left[0,1\right])\right)\leq 1.

We can extend

by

since

. In particular,

\displaystyle\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes P_{m}^{\otimes k}\ \gamma^{(1,k)}\ \mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes P_{m}^{\otimes k}=\iint\limits_{\left[0,1\right]\times S_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}}\lambda^{k}\zeta_{m}(\lambda,u)\otimes p_{u}^{\otimes k}\,d\mathbb{P}_{m}(\lambda,u),

(58)

with
\mathbb{P}_{m}
-a.e.,
\text{Tr}\left(\zeta_{m}\right)=1
. As a consequence of the above density argument and the

-invariance of Theorem

,
\zeta_{m}\mathbb{P}_{m}
is the unique

-invariant trace-class-valued measure satisfying (
58
).

\bullet
Taking

:

It follows from this uniqueness property that
(\zeta_{m}\mathbb{P}_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
is consistent in the following sense

\displaystyle\forall m\in\mathbb{N},\ \zeta_{m}\mathbb{P}_{m}={P_{m}}_{*}\left(\zeta_{m+1}\mathbb{P}_{m+1}\right)

and a bounded sequence in
\mathcal{M}\left(\left[0,1\right]\times S_{\mathcal{H}_{s}},\mathcal{L}^{1}\left(\mathcal{H}_{0}\right)\right)
. Here the Kolmogorov extension theorem holds. For this we refer to
[
6
, Chapters V.1 and V.2]
, key ingredients are the Radon–Nikodym property of the trace-class and
\mathbb{P}_{m}
-a.e.,
\text{Tr}\left(\zeta_{m}\right)=1
. Therefore the Kolmogorov extension theorem constructs a trace-class valued measure
\zeta\mathbb{P}
, with

•

\mathbb{P}\in\mathcal{M}\left(\left[0,1\right]\times S_{\mathcal{H}_{s}},\mathbb{R}_{+}\right)
, satisfying
\mathbb{P}\left(\left[0,1\right]\times S_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}\right)\leq 1
,

•

\zeta\in L^{1}\left(\left[0,1\right]\times S_{\mathcal{H}_{s}},\mathcal{L}^{1}_{+}(\mathcal{H}_{0})\right)
satisfying,
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.,
\text{Tr}\left(\zeta\right)=1
, whose existence is guaranteed by the Radon–Nikodym theorem,

such that

\displaystyle\forall m\in\mathbb{N},\ \zeta_{m}\mathbb{P}_{m}={P_{m}}_{*}\left(\zeta\mathbb{P}\right).

Let

. Since

\displaystyle\varphi:(\lambda,u)\mapsto\lambda^{k}\left(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes p_{u}^{\otimes k}\ K\right)^{(1,0)}\in C^{0}\left(\left[0,1\right]\times S_{\mathcal{H}_{s}},\mathcal{K}\left(\mathcal{H}_{0}\right)\right),

\displaystyle\iint\limits_{\left[0,1\right]\times S_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}}\lambda^{k}\text{Tr}\left(\zeta_{m}(\lambda,u)\otimes p_{u}^{\otimes k}\ K\right)d\mathbb{P}_{m}(\lambda,u)=

\displaystyle\ \iint\limits_{\left[0,1\right]\times S_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}}\text{Tr}\left(\zeta_{m}(\lambda,u)\varphi(\lambda,u)\right)d\mathbb{P}_{m}(\lambda,u)

\displaystyle\limitoperator\displaylimits_{m\to\infty}

\displaystyle\ \iint\limits_{\left[0,1\right]\times S_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}}\text{Tr}\left(\zeta(\lambda,u)\varphi(\lambda,u)\right)d\mathbb{P}(\lambda,u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \iint\limits_{\left[0,1\right]\times S_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}}\lambda^{k}\text{Tr}\left(\zeta(\lambda,u)\otimes p_{u}^{\otimes k}\ K\right)d\mathbb{P}(\lambda,u),

from which we deduce that

\displaystyle\iint\limits_{\left[0,1\right]\times S_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}}\lambda^{k}\zeta_{m}(\lambda,u)\otimes p_{u}^{\otimes k}d\mathbb{P}_{m}(\lambda,u)\wslim\displaylimits_{m\to\infty}\iint\limits_{\left[0,1\right]\times S_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}}\lambda^{k}\zeta(\lambda,u)\otimes p_{u}^{\otimes k}d\mathbb{P}(\lambda,u).

\bullet
Estimating the tail of
\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes P_{m}^{\otimes k}\ \gamma^{(1,k)}\ \mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes P_{m}^{\otimes k}
:

By induction,

\displaystyle\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}^{\otimes k}-P_{m}^{\otimes k}=\sum_{n=1}^{k}P_{m}^{\otimes(n-1)}\otimes Q_{m}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}^{\otimes(k-n)}.

Indeed, the case
k=1
corresponds to the definition of
Q_{m}
, and assuming the above identity, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}^{\otimes(k+1)}-P_{m}^{\otimes(k+1)}

\displaystyle=P_{m}\otimes\left(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}^{\otimes k}-P_{m}^{\otimes k}\right)+Q_{m}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}^{\otimes k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{k}P_{m}^{\otimes n}\otimes Q_{m}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}^{\otimes(k-n)}+Q_{m}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}^{\otimes k}=\sum_{n=0}^{k}P_{m}^{\otimes n}\otimes Q_{m}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}^{\otimes(k-n)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{k+1}P_{m}^{\otimes(n-1)}\otimes Q_{m}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}^{\otimes(k+1-n)}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}^{\otimes k}-P_{m}^{\otimes k})\ \gamma^{(1,k)}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}\leq\sum_{n=1}^{k}\left\lVert\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes P_{m}^{\otimes(n-1)}\otimes Q_{m}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}^{\otimes(k-n)}\ \gamma^{(1,k)}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{k}\text{Tr}\left(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}^{\otimes(n-1)}\otimes Q_{m}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}^{\otimes(k-n)}\ \gamma_{1,k}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\text{Tr}\left(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes P_{m}^{\otimes(n-1)}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}^{\otimes(k+1-n)}\ \gamma_{1,k}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{k}\text{Tr}\left(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}^{\otimes(n-1)}\otimes Q_{m}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}^{\otimes(k-n)}\ \gamma_{1,k}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=k\text{Tr}\left(Q_{m}\gamma^{(0,1)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\limitoperator\displaylimits_{m\to\infty}0.

Developing the square, it follows from the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\gamma^{(1,k)}-\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes P_{m}^{\otimes k}\ \gamma^{(1,k)}\ \mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes P_{m}^{\otimes k}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}\leq

\displaystyle\ \left\lVert\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}^{\otimes k}-P_{m}^{\otimes k})\ \gamma^{(1,k)}\ \mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}^{\otimes k}-P_{m}^{\otimes k})\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}

\displaystyle+2\left\lVert\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}^{\otimes k}-P_{m}^{\otimes k})\ \gamma^{(1,k)}\ \mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\otimes P_{m}^{\otimes k}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}

\displaystyle\limitoperator\displaylimits_{m\to\infty}0.

\bullet
Conclusion:

Combining the above with (
58
) and (
59
), we obtain

\displaystyle\gamma^{(1,k)}=\iint\limits_{\left[0,1\right]\times S_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}}\lambda^{k}\zeta(\lambda,u)\otimes p_{u}^{\otimes k}d\mathbb{P}(\lambda,u).

Taking the trace yields

\displaystyle 1=\text{Tr}\left(\gamma^{(1,k)}\right)=\iint\limits_{\left[0,1\right]\times S_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}}\lambda^{k}d\mathbb{P}(\lambda,u).

Since with (
57

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left[0,1\right]\times S_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}\right)=\left\lVert\zeta\mathbb{P}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}\leq\liminf_{m\to\infty}\left\lVert\zeta_{m}\mathbb{P}_{m}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}=\liminf_{m\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{m}(\left[0,1\right]\times S^{m})\leq 1,

we infer that
\mathbb{P}
must be a probability measure supported on
\left\{1\right\}\times S_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}
, and the pair

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\left\{1\right\}\times\bullet),\zeta(1,\bullet)

is suitable to conclude, as
\mathbb{P}(\left\{1\right\}\times\bullet)
-a.e.
\text{Tr}\left(\zeta(1,\bullet)\right)=1
.
