A General Theory of Propositional Modal Bundled Modalities
Yifeng Ding Yuanzhe Yang

Abstract
In studies of bundled modalities, we encode a complex conceptual notion into the semantics of a single modal operator and study its logic. Although there is already a substantial body of work on various concrete bundled operators, we still lack a general understanding of them. In this paper, we provide a general theory of the expressivity and axiomatization of bundled modalities. We offer a uniform way to define bisimulations for arbitrary bundled modalities and justify our definition by the corresponding Hennessy-Milner property. We also define a special class of bundled modalities called convex bundles. This class covers most bundled modalities studied in the literature, and their axiomatizations can be done with the help of convex neighborhood semantics and corresponding representation results. As case studies, we axiomatize the “someone knows” bundle ⋁ a ∈ A □ a ​ ϕ \bigvee_{a\in A}\Box_{a}\phi over S ​ 5 S5 -models, the “disagreement in group” bundle ⋁ a , b ∈ A □ a ​ ϕ ∧ □ b ​ ¬ ϕ \bigvee_{a,b\in A}\Box_{a}\phi\wedge\Box_{b}\neg\phi over K ​ D ​ 45 KD45 -models, and the “belief without knowledge” bundle B ​ ϕ ∧ ¬ K ​ ϕ B\phi\wedge\neg K\phi over S ​ 4.2 S4.2 -models.

The idea of

The logics of bundled modalities,

Given the multitudinous papers on concrete bundled modalities,
one naturally wants to find a general theory subsuming them.
There are indeed a few attempts towards such a theory in the literature,
but none of them is completely satisfactory.
For example,

introduced a method to automatically define bisimulations for bundled modalities (which are called

but such a method applies only to a restricted class of modalities called
uniform connectives

introduced a uniform approach to axiomatizing logics of bundled modalities called
the method based on almost-definability schemas
,
but no uniform way to find such schemas for an arbitrary bundled modality is provided,
and it is also unclear to which kinds of bundled modalities this method applies.
As a result, in studies of concrete bundled modalities,
bisimulations are often left as open problems,
while radically different notions of canonical models are introduced to prove completeness theorems,
even though the logics in question often have a lot in common.

Our goal in this paper, then, is to provide a general theory for both expressivity and axiomatization of bundled modalities.
For expressivity,
we view the semantics of bundled modalities as a special kind of
neighborhood semantics

and uniformly define bisimulations for
all
bundled modalities.
As for axiomatizations, we focus on a particularly well-behaved class of bundled modalities called
convex bundles
.
This class covers most bundled modalities studied in the literature and yet a lot more.
We show that the fairly standard two-step strategy of proving completeness theorems, namely canonical model building followed by transforming to a semantically equivalent intended model, applies with a high degree of uniformity.
Specifically, canonical models can be constructed uniformly with a rich supply of canonical axioms, and model transformation does not require much action since, from our work on bisimulations, we know exactly what it takes to preserve truth values.

Language and Semantics

We now define the language and semantics for bundled modalities in general.
For simplicity, we focus on cases where there is only one unary bundled modality;
generalization to multiple bundled modalities with arbitrary arities is left for future work.
Formally, we introduce only
one
modal language with a unary modal operator,
and different bundled modalities are viewed as different
semantics
for the same modal operator.

Fix a countable set
\mathbf{P}
of propositional letters.

Definition 2.1

(Language)
.

Formulas in
\mathcal{L}_{\textnormal{\Circle}}
are defined recursively as follows:

\phi::=p\mid\top\mid\neg\phi\mid(\phi\wedge\phi)\mid{\textnormal{\Circle}}\phi\quad(\text{where }p\in\mathbf{P})
.

Let
{\textnormal{\CIRCLE}}\phi
be the abbreviation for
{\textnormal{\Circle}}\neg\phi
(
{\textnormal{\CIRCLE}}\phi
becomes useful when we offer axiomatizations).

Next, we define the
bundled semantics
for the above language.
We fix a countable set
\mathbf{A}
of indices,
together with an arity function
\rho
from
\mathbf{A}
to
\mathbb{N}

s.t. for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\rho^{-1}[n]
is infinite.
We also assume that for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
there is
{\mathtt{i}\!\mathtt{d}}_{n}\in\mathbf{A}
with
\rho({\mathtt{i}\!\mathtt{d}}_{n})=n
.
We consider Kripke models of the following kind.

Definition 2.2

(Kripke Models)
.

A
Kripke model
is a triple
\mathcal{M}=(W,\{R_{a}\}_{a\in\mathbf{A}},V)
, where

W\neq\emptyset
is the set of worlds;
for each
a\in\mathbf{A}
,
R_{a}
is a
(\rho(a)+1)
-ary relation on
W

and in particular,
R_{{\mathtt{i}\!\mathtt{d}}_{n}}=\bigcup_{w\in W}\{w\}^{n+1}
;
and
V
is a valuation function from
\mathbf{P}
to
\wp(W)
.
For
w\in W
and
a\in\mathbf{A}
, we use
R_{a}(w)
to denote
\{(v_{1},...,v_{n})\in W^{n}\mid(w,v_{1},...,v_{n})\in R_{a}\}
and do not distinguish
(x)
from
x
.

The structures of the bundles are expressed by the following
bundle terms
, which are essentially formulas of a positive modal language with only two propositional letters
(+)
and
(-)
.

Definition 2.3

(Bundle Terms)
.

Terms in
\mathcal{T}_{\mathbf{A}}
are defined recursively as follows:

\tau::=(+)\mid(-)\mid\nabla_{a}(\underbrace{\tau,...,\tau}_{\rho(a)\text{-many}})\mid\Delta_{a}(\underbrace{\tau,...,\tau}_{\rho(a)\text{-many}})\quad(\text{where }a\in\mathbf{A})
.

As usual, when
\rho(a)=1
, we may use
\Box_{a}\tau
and
\Diamond_{a}\tau
to denote
\nabla_{a}(\tau)
and
\Delta_{a}(\tau)
, respectively.

Then, for any bundle term
\tau
, we define the corresponding bundled semantics for
\mathcal{L}_{\textnormal{\Circle}}
as follows.

Definition 2.4

(Bundled Semantics)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=(W,\{R_{a}\}_{a\in\mathbf{A}},V)
be a Kripke model.

First, for any
w\in W
and
X\subseteq W
, we define the following satisfaction relation
\vDash
for terms in
\mathcal{T}_{\mathbf{A}}
:

\mathcal{M},w,X\vDash(+)

\iff

w\in X

\mathcal{M},w,X\vDash(-)

\iff

w\notin X

\mathcal{M},w,X\vDash\nabla_{a}(\tau_{1},...,\tau_{n})

\iff

\forall(v_{1},...,v_{n})\in R_{a}(w)\exists i\leq n\mathcal{M},v_{i},X\vDash\tau_{i}

\mathcal{M},w,X\vDash\Delta_{a}(\tau_{1},...,\tau_{n})

\iff

\exists(v_{1},...,v_{n})\in R_{a}(w)\forall i\leq n\mathcal{M},v_{i},X\vDash\tau_{i}
.

Then, for any
\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathbf{A}}
,
w\in W
and
\phi\in\mathcal{L}_{\textnormal{\Circle}}
,
the satisfaction relation
\vDash_{\tau}
for
\tau
is defined recursively as follows
(Boolean cases are omitted, and
|\phi|^{\tau}_{\mathcal{M}}=\{w\in W\mid\mathcal{M},w\vDash_{\tau}\phi\}
):

\mathcal{M},w\vDash_{\tau}p

\iff

w\in V(p)

\mathcal{M},w\vDash_{\tau}{\textnormal{\Circle}}\phi

\iff

\mathcal{M},w,|\phi|^{\tau}_{\mathcal{M}}\vDash\tau
.

Intuitively, we “unzip” the modal structure encoded by
\tau
when we compute the truth value of
{\textnormal{\Circle}}\phi
.
This formalizes the usual way in which semantics of bundled modalities are defined.

Note that Boolean connectives are not included as primitive components in our definition of bundle terms:
this is because they can be viewed as
modalities
corresponding to
{\mathtt{i}\!\mathtt{d}}_{n}
’s.
For example, binary disjunction and conjunction can be viewed as
\nabla_{{\mathtt{i}\!\mathtt{d}}_{2}}
and
\Delta_{{\mathtt{i}\!\mathtt{d}}_{2}}
respectively,
since
\mathcal{M},w,X\vDash\nabla_{{\mathtt{i}\!\mathtt{d}}_{2}}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})
iff
(\mathcal{M},w,X\vDash\tau_{1})\vee(\mathcal{M},w,X\vDash\tau_{2})
,
while
\mathcal{M},w,X\vDash\Delta_{{\mathtt{i}\!\mathtt{d}}_{2}}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})
iff
(\mathcal{M},w,X\vDash\tau_{1})\wedge(\mathcal{M},w,X\vDash\tau_{2})
.

As a concrete example, if we want to give

3
Bisimulations for Bundled Modalities

In this section, we offer a uniform way to define bisimulations for bundled modalities
and show that the bisimulations defined in this way correctly characterize the expressivity of the corresponding bundled modalities.
A key observation leading to these bisimulations is that
the bundled semantics can be viewed as a special kind of
neighborhood semantics
.
The neighborhood structure generated by a bundle term over a Kripke model is defined as follows.

Definition 3.1

(Generated Neighborhood)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=(W,\{R_{a}\}_{a\in\mathbf{A}},V)
be a Kripke model.

We define a function
\mathtt{Nbh}_{\mathcal{M}}
from
W\times\mathcal{T}_{\mathbf{A}}
to
\wp(\wp(W)\times\wp(W))
recursively as follows:
2
2

2

\prod_{x\in X}Y_{x}
denotes the set of functions from
X
to
\bigcup_{x\in X}Y_{x}

that map each
x\in X
to an element in
Y_{x}
.

\mathtt{Nbh}_{\mathcal{M}}(w,(+))

=

\{(\{w\},\emptyset)\}

\mathtt{Nbh}_{\mathcal{M}}(w,(-))

=

\{(\emptyset,\{w\})\}

\mathtt{Nbh}_{\mathcal{M}}(w,\nabla_{a}(\tau_{1},...,\tau_{n}))

=

\{\bigsqcup\text{ran}(f)\mid f\in\prod_{(v_{1},...,v_{n})\in R_{a}(w)}\bigcup_{i\leq n}\mathtt{Nbh}_{\mathcal{M}}(v_{i},\tau_{i})\}

\mathtt{Nbh}_{\mathcal{M}}(w,\Delta_{a}(\tau_{1},...,\tau_{n}))

=

\bigcup_{(v_{1},...,v_{n})\in R_{a}(w)}\{\bigsqcup\text{ran}(g)\mid g\in\prod_{i\leq n}\mathtt{Nbh}_{\mathcal{M}}(v_{i},\tau_{i})\}

(where
\bigsqcup\{(X_{i},Y_{i})\mid{i\in I}\}=(\bigcup_{i\in I}X_{i},\bigcup_{i\in I}Y_{i})
)

Intuitively, given a pointed model
\mathcal{M},w
,
each
(X,Y)\in\mathtt{Nbh}_{\mathcal{M}}(w,\tau)
represents a minimal way to satisfy and falsify
\phi
in the model
in order for
{\textnormal{\Circle}}\phi
to be true at
\mathcal{M},w
under the
\tau
-bundled semantics
(
X
and
Y
mark where
\phi
must be satisfied and falsified, respectively).
Following this intuition,
an element in
\mathtt{Nbh}_{\mathcal{M}}(w,\nabla_{a}(\tau_{1},...,\tau_{n}))
is obtained by choosing for each successor
(v_{1},...,v_{n})
of
w
one
i\leq\rho(a)
and one element from
\mathtt{Nbh}_{\mathcal{M}}(v_{i},\tau_{i})
, and then merging them together,
while an element in
\mathtt{Nbh}_{\mathcal{M}}(w,\Delta_{a}(\tau_{1},...,\tau_{n}))
is obtained by first choosing one successor
(v_{1},...,v_{n})
of
w
, then choosing for each
i\leq\rho(a)
one element from
\mathtt{Nbh}_{\mathcal{M}}(v_{i},\tau_{i})
, and merging them together.

Our intuition above is verified by the following proposition.
It is worth noting that the
Axiom of Choice
(AC) is indispensable for its proof.

For simplicity, we write
(X,Y)\sqsubseteq(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime})
for
X\subseteq X^{\prime}
and
Y\subseteq Y^{\prime}
.

Proposition 3.2
.

(AC)
For any model
\mathcal{M},w
, any
\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathbf{A}}
and
\phi\in\mathcal{L}_{\textnormal{\Circle}}
,

\mathcal{M},w\vDash_{\tau}{\textnormal{\Circle}}\phi\iff\exists(X,Y)\in\mathtt{Nbh}_{\mathcal{M}}(w,\tau)((X,Y)\sqsubseteq(|\phi|^{\tau}_{\mathcal{M}},W\setminus|\phi|^{\tau}_{\mathcal{M}}))
.

Proof.

Fix a model
\mathcal{M}=(W,\{R_{a}\}_{a\in\mathbf{A}},V)
.
We use induction on the structure of terms to show that for any
\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathbf{A}}
,
w\in W
and
Z\subseteq W
,

\mathcal{M},w,Z\vDash\tau
iff
\exists(X,Y)\in\mathtt{Nbh}_{\mathcal{M}}(w,\tau)((X,Y)\sqsubseteq(Z,W\setminus Z))
.

The base cases for
(+)
and
(-)
are obvious by definition, so we focus on the inductive steps.

For
\nabla_{a}(\tau_{1},...,\tau_{n})
, we have

\mathcal{M},w,Z\vDash\nabla_{a}(\tau_{1},...,\tau_{n})

\iff

\forall\vec{v}\in R_{a}(w)\exists i\leq n\mathcal{M},v_{i},Z\vDash\tau_{i}

\iff

\forall\vec{v}\in R_{a}(w)\exists i\leq n\exists(X,Y)\in\mathtt{Nbh}_{\mathcal{M}}(v_{i},\tau_{i})((X,Y)\sqsubseteq(Z,W\setminus Z))

(IH)

\iff

\exists f\in\prod_{\vec{v}\in R_{a}(w)}\bigcup_{i\leq n}\mathtt{Nbh}_{\mathcal{M}}(v_{i},\tau_{i})\forall\vec{v}\in R_{a}(w)(f(\vec{v})\sqsubseteq(Z,W\setminus Z))

(AC)

\iff

\exists f\in\prod_{\vec{v}\in R_{a}(w)}\bigcup_{i\leq n}\mathtt{Nbh}_{\mathcal{M}}(v_{i},\tau_{i})(\bigsqcup\text{ran}(f)\sqsubseteq(Z,W\setminus Z))

\iff

\exists(X,Y)\in\mathtt{Nbh}_{\mathcal{M}}(w,\nabla_{a}(\tau_{1},...,\tau_{n}))((X,Y)\sqsubseteq(Z,W\setminus Z))
.

For
\Delta_{a}(\tau_{1},...,\tau_{n})
, similarly, we have

\mathcal{M},w,Z\vDash\Delta_{a}(\tau_{1},...,\tau_{n})

\iff

\exists\vec{v}\in R_{a}(w)\forall i\leq n\mathcal{M},v_{i},Z\vDash\tau_{i}

\iff

\exists\vec{v}\in R_{a}(w)\forall i\leq n\exists(X,Y)\in\mathtt{Nbh}_{\mathcal{M}}(v_{i},\tau_{i})((X,Y)\sqsubseteq(Z,W\setminus Z))

(IH)

\iff

\exists\vec{v}\in R_{a}(w)\exists g\in\prod_{i\leq n}\mathtt{Nbh}_{\mathcal{M}}(v_{i},\tau_{i})\forall i\leq n(g(i)\sqsubseteq(Z,W\setminus Z))

\iff

\exists\vec{v}\in R_{a}(w)\exists g\in\prod_{i\leq n}\mathtt{Nbh}_{\mathcal{M}}(v_{i},\tau_{i})(\bigsqcup\text{ran}(g)\sqsubseteq(Z,W\setminus Z))

\iff

\exists(X,Y)\in\mathtt{Nbh}_{\mathcal{M}}(w,\Delta_{a}(\tau_{1},...,\tau_{n}))((X,Y)\sqsubseteq(Z,W\setminus Z))
.

Note that we do not need AC in the case for
\Delta_{a}(\tau_{1},...,\tau_{n})
since the
g
we need is a finite function.
∎

Before moving on to define the bisimulations,
as preparation,
we first introduce the following notions of
domains
and

Definition 3.3

(Domain and Completion)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=(W,\{R_{a}\}_{a\in\mathbf{A}},V)
be a model.

We define a function
\mathtt{Dom}_{\mathcal{M}}
from
W\times\mathcal{T}_{\mathbf{A}}
to
\wp(W)
recursively as follows:

\mathtt{Dom}_{\mathcal{M}}(w,(+))
,
\mathtt{Dom}_{\mathcal{M}}(w,(-))

=

\{w\}

\mathtt{Dom}_{\mathcal{M}}(w,\nabla_{a}(\tau_{1},...,\tau_{n}))
,
\mathtt{Dom}_{\mathcal{M}}(w,\Delta_{a}(\tau_{1},...,\tau_{n}))

=

\bigcup_{(v_{1},...,v_{n})\in R_{a}(w)}\bigcup_{i\leq n}\mathtt{Dom}_{\mathcal{M}}(v_{i},\tau_{i})

Then, for any term
\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathbf{A}}
, the

of
\mathtt{Nbh}_{\mathcal{M}}(w,\tau)

is defined as

\mathtt{Nbh}^{\mathtt{com}}_{\mathcal{M}}(w,\tau)=\{Z\subseteq\mathtt{Dom}_{\mathcal{M}}(w,\tau)\mid\exists(X,Y)\in\mathtt{Nbh}_{\mathcal{M}}(w,\tau)((X,Y)\sqsubseteq(Z,\mathtt{Dom}_{\mathcal{M}}(w,\tau)\setminus Z))\}
.

Intuitively, the truth value of
{\textnormal{\Circle}}\phi
at
\mathcal{M},w
under
\tau

\phi
in
\mathtt{Dom}_{\mathcal{M}}(w,\tau)
.
This is formally expressed by following proposition,
which can be checked via an easy induction on the structure of terms.

Proposition 3.4
.

For any model
\mathcal{M},w
and any
\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathbf{A}}
,
\mathtt{Dom}_{\mathcal{M}}(w,\tau)=\bigcup_{(X,Y)\in\mathtt{Nbh}_{\mathcal{M}}(w,\tau)}X\cup Y
.
Thus, by Proposition
3.2
, for any
\phi\in\mathcal{L}_{\textnormal{\Circle}}
,
\mathcal{M},w\vDash_{\tau}{\textnormal{\Circle}}\phi\iff|\phi|^{\tau}_{\mathcal{M}}\cap\mathtt{Dom}_{\mathcal{M}}(w,\tau)\in\mathtt{Nbh}^{\mathtt{com}}_{\mathcal{M}}(w,\tau)
.

(Bundled Bisimulation)

\mathcal{Z}[X\cup Y]\cap\mathtt{Dom}_{\mathcal{M}}(w,\tau)\subseteq X

Then, we say
\mathcal{Z}

\tau

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we have studied propositional bundled modalities at a general level.
We showed how to view bundled semantics as a kind of neighborhood semantics
and uniformly defined bisimulations for bundled modalities from this viewpoint.
We also studied the expressivity and axiomatizations of convex bundles
and offered axiomatizations for several concrete convex bundles using our general method.

There are several directions for future work.
One is to further study the bundled bisimulation we defined.
For example, a
characterization theorem
for these bisimulations is highly desirable.
A source of difficulty here is that
notions of modal saturation for ordinary neighborhood models appear too strong for the neighborhood structures generated by bundle terms,
so the usual ultrapower construction might not work here.

It should also be interesting to try to find more uniform ways to generate axiomatizations for bundled modalities.
For example, if we could prove Sahlqvist-style correspondence and completeness theorems for convex neighborhood logics,
and at the same time develop a method to automatically obtain representation results for certain bundles,
then we could immediately obtain a great amount of complete axiomatizations for these bundles.

Finally, we believe the class of convex neighborhood logics deserves a more fully developed theory.
On the one hand, some fine distinctions can only be made in this class of logics.
For example, the axioms
\mathtt{4}^{\prime}_{\textnormal{\Circle}}
and
\mathtt{4}^{\prime\prime}_{\textnormal{\Circle}}
we introduced above both collapse to
{\textnormal{\Circle}}\phi\to{\textnormal{\Circle}}(\phi\wedge{\textnormal{\Circle}}\phi)
in monotonic logics with
\mathtt{N}_{\textnormal{\Circle}}
,
and
\mathtt{4}^{\prime}_{\textnormal{\Circle}}
,
\mathtt{4}^{\prime\prime}_{\textnormal{\Circle}}
,
\mathtt{4}^{\prime\prime\prime}_{\textnormal{\Circle}}
all collapse to
{\textnormal{\Circle}}\phi\to{\textnormal{\Circle}}{\textnormal{\Circle}}\phi
in normal modal logics.
On the other hand, logics in this class admit adequate semantics based on core models, so we may hope for a more fruitful theory than that of all neighborhood modal logics.
Hence, it should be interesting to further study the general theory of convex modal logics.
