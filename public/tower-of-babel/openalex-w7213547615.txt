THE PROTECTION AND REHABILITATION OF HUMAN TRAFFICKING
VICTIMS IN THE CONTEXT OF SOCIAL VULNERABILITY: THE CASE OF
MARAJÓ ISLAND

Raquel Zapata Rosendo de Lima1

1
ABSTRACT: This research examines the concept of human trafficking and the measures taken
to combat it. It emphasizes the importance of promoting prevention, protection, and
punishment in order to achieve a lasting impact in the fight against human trafficking. The
study also underscores the need for rehabilitation measures for those who have suffered
violence within trafficking networks, highlighting the role of programs and projects that
support the social reintegration of survivors. Through the analysis of the context of Marajó
Island, the research demonstrates the persistence of these violations in contemporary society,
with particular attention to the sexual exploitation of women and children. The findings
reinforce the need to raise awareness and improve access to information for all members of
society.
Keywords: Human Trafficking. Sexual Exploitation. Victim Protection. Rehabilitation.
Marajó Island.

1.

INTRODUCTION
Victims of human trafficking are often confined not only physically but also

psychologically, trapped in a continuous cycle of abuse and exploitation. This study examines
this reality, identifying the complexities and challenges involved in combating human
trafficking, with particular attention to the role of protection and reintegration strategies for
victims.
Although the lack of visibility and discussion around this issue remains a crucial factor,
nations worldwide have joined efforts to develop treaties and agreements to protect and
rehabilitate these vulnerable individuals. According to the United Nations (UN), human
trafficking involves:
"The recruitment, transportation, transfer, harbouring or receipt of persons, by means of the
threat or use of force or other forms of coercion, abduction, fraud, deception, abuse of power or of a
position of vulnerability, or the giving or receiving of payments or benefits to achieve the consent of a
person having control over another person, for the purpose of exploitation" (UNITED NATIONS,
2000).
One important context examined in this study is exploitation reported on Marajó
Island, where geographic distance from urban centers and unequal access to public services may
contribute to social vulnerability. Limited access to information and awareness of civil rights
may increase some individuals' vulnerability to different forms of exploitation, which,
according to previous research, may also occur within family or community contexts (VIEIRA,
2011; MORAES, 2011; NUNES; FILARD, 2024).
Therefore, this study seeks not only to expose this concerning situation, but also to
highlight existing protection strategies and ensure rehabilitation for those whose lives have
been most deeply affected.

HUMAN TRAFFICKING FOR SEXUAL EXPLOITATION
Sexual exploitation, particularly of women and children, is a complex issue associated

with a combination of social, economic, cultural, and gender-related factors that may contribute
to increased vulnerability to this form of trafficking (GOMES; SANI, 2023).

Undeniably, gender inequality is one of the factors discussed in relation to vulnerability
to sexual exploitation. In many societies, women continue to experience discrimination and
remain subject to gender-based inequality, facing barriers to equal opportunities and rights.
Consequently, these inequalities may contribute to economic and social marginalization and
may increase vulnerability to different forms of exploitation (AGÊNCIA SENADO, 2023).
Anna Carolina da Conceição Aureliano (AGÊNCIA SENADO, 2023), a specialist in
International Relations, contextualizes and defines the issue:
"It is a crime with a gender perspective. Socially constructed, culturally accepted, and historically
reproduced gender inequalities are definitively reflected in the context of human trafficking, making it
one of the worst forms of gender-based violence," concludes the specialist.
Another significant factor in this discussion is the lack of education and opportunities
available to these individuals. Difficulties in accessing education and employment
opportunities may constitute contextual factors associated with increased social vulnerability
(VIEIRA, 2011; FAPESPA, 2024).
3.

THEORETICAL FRAMEWORK: INSIGHTS FROM PREVIOUS RESEARCH
Before presenting the case study on Marajó Island, it is important to consider the

findings of previous research on this topic. Three main themes from the existing literature
guide this paper: the intersection of culture and vulnerability on the island, the impact of stigma
on victims once their cases become public, and the crucial role of rehabilitation.
3.1. Studies Written About Marajó Itself
Some researchers have conducted field research in Marajó to study this problem. Vieira
(2011), in a master's thesis in Education at the Federal University of Pará, interviewed students
at a riverside school about how they understood the sexual exploitation happening on the
"balsas" (ferries that connect Marajó's towns) and how it affected their schooling. Her findings
showed that young people on the island already had their own ways of describing the
exploitation before any government program arrived to formally identify or address it.
More recently, Nunes and Filard (2024) examined the sexual exploitation of children
and adolescents on Marajó Island, discussing the relationship between the protection network
and the exploitation network. Their study also highlights the importance of understanding the

A second recurring finding concerns what happens to victims once a case becomes

3.3. Why Rehabilitation Cannot Be an Afterthought
García-Vázquez and Meneses-Falcón (2024), in a systematic review, examined the
impact of human trafficking on sexual, social, physical, and psychological health and
highlighted the importance of social health to the well-being of trafficking victims (GARCÍAVÁZQUEZ; MENESES-FALCÓN, 2024).
Therefore, rehabilitation should be considered an important component of victim
protection rather than only as a later stage following the end of exploitation.
4.

CASE STUDY – MARAJÓ ISLAND
Case Study Methodology: This case study was developed through qualitative

bibliographical and documentary research into the political, environmental, and socioeconomic
context of Marajó Island, seeking to understand the recurring factors associated with human
trafficking and the obstacles to implementing effective measures to combat the exploitation of
vulnerable groups living on the island.

Sources were selected for their direct relevance to human trafficking, sexual
exploitation, and social vulnerability in the Marajó region, using search terms such as "tráfico
de pessoas," "exploração sexual," "Ilha de Marajó," "vulnerabilidade social," and their
equivalents. Inclusion was prioritized for sources with identifiable academic or institutional
authorship, current publication dates, and direct thematic relevance to the study; sources
lacking clear authorship or institutional affiliation, or addressing unrelated subjects, were
excluded from the analysis.
The research consulted academic repositories and databases including SciELO and the
institutional repository of the Federal University of Pará (UFPA), as well as official channels
such as the Brazilian federal government's portal (gov.br), the Agência Senado news service,
and publications of the United Nations and the World Tourism Organization.
Peer-reviewed articles and official or institutional documents served as the primary
basis for conceptual and theoretical claims. Journalistic sources were consulted mainly to
document specific, verifiable events rather than as the primary basis for theoretical arguments.
The material gathered, spanning publications from 1995 to 2024, was analyzed
qualitatively. Recurring themes (namely vulnerability, stigma, and rehabilitation) were
identified through their repeated presence across the sources consulted, and were used to
organize the theoretical framework and interpret the specific case of Marajó Island.
4.1. Context and Challenges
Located in northern Brazil, in the State of Pará, Marajó Island is the largest fluvial and
maritime island in the world, surrounded by water on all sides. It is also an ecologically
significant ecosystem of importance for the Amazon region (MORIM, 2014).
The region has been the subject of academic and governmental discussions concerning
situations of social vulnerability and the sexual exploitation of children and adolescents
(NUNES; FILARD, 2024). However, these issues should not be understood as characteristics
of the population as a whole, but rather as specific social problems identified within the region.
Accordingly, the popular term “sex tourism” should be understood within the broader
discussion of commercial sexual exploitation associated with tourism. The World Tourism
Organization (UNWTO, 1995) describes sex tourism as travel involving the structures and
networks of the tourism sector with the purpose of facilitating commercial sexual relations

5.

REHABILITATION OF VICTIMS

5.1. The Problem of Victim Stigmatization
“Stigmatization” is defined as the act of negatively marking something or someone.
This attribution of characteristics associated with social disadvantage results in discrimination
and the marginalization of countless individuals, making them more susceptible to violations
of their most fundamental personal rights (BLANCHETTE; SILVA, 2018).
The stigma associated with trafficking may interfere with the recognition of individuals
as victims and may affect their access to protection and support. For this reason, approaches to
trafficking should prioritize protection and avoid narratives that place responsibility for
exploitation on the victim (BLANCHETTE; SILVA, 2018).
Also, the trauma experienced by victims of human trafficking may affect their social
relationships and sense of security. Recent systematic research has identified social,
psychological, physical, and sexual consequences associated with trafficking, reinforcing the
importance of comprehensive support after exploitation (GARCÍA-VÁZQUEZ; MENESESFALCÓN, 2024).
Furthermore, the interruption of education and professional activities during the period
of exploitation may affect victims' future opportunities, meaning that rehabilitation and social
reintegration require attention not only to immediate protection but also to education and

5.2. Rehabilitation and Social Reintegration Initiatives
It is widely recognized that exposure to human trafficking can result in significant
health consequences, affecting not only physical health but also mental and social health
(GARCÍA-VÁZQUEZ; MENESES-FALCÓN, 2024). From this perspective, it is important
to emphasize the role of psychological and social support in the rehabilitation process of these
individuals after the traumatic experience.
Recent research has identified psychological, physical, sexual, and social consequences
among trafficking survivors. However, the extent and nature of these consequences may vary
according to individual circumstances, forms of exploitation, and access to support services
(GARCÍA-VÁZQUEZ; MENESES-FALCÓN, 2024). Given this context, the need for
measures aimed at proposing rehabilitation and social reintegration initiatives for victims who
have suffered any form of trafficking becomes evident.
In Brazil, government initiatives also play an important role in prevention and
protection. In the Marajó region, the Federal Highway Police's Projeto Mapear represents an
example of institutional action directed toward identifying and monitoring locations
considered vulnerable to sexual exploitation of children and adolescents (POLÍCIA
RODOVIÁRIA FEDERAL, 2024).
6.

CONCLUSION
It is therefore concluded that Human Trafficking is a complex and multifaceted

phenomenon, composed of a variety of forms of exploitation.
When addressing victims' rights globally, the United Nations (UN) provides an
important international framework, along with its protocols and other international
instruments on the protection of victims of trafficking (UNITED NATIONS, 2000).
The search for better living conditions and survival may increase vulnerability in
certain circumstances. Marajó Island represents an important case for examining how social
and economic conditions, access to public services, geographic characteristics, and other
