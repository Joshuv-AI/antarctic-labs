Well-posedness of inhomogeneous nonlinear wave equations in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3}
Boyu Jiang, Jiawei Shen, Kexue Li*

Abstract
. This paper is devoted to the well-posedness of the inhomogeneous nonlinear wave equations. By combining Strichartz estimates with the contraction mapping principle, we establish local and global well-posedness in the function spaces H ˙ 1 ​ ( ℝ 3 ) × L 2 ​ ( ℝ 3 ) \dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}) and H ˙ s + 1 ​ ( ℝ 3 ) × H ˙ s ​ ( ℝ 3 ) \dot{H}^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3}) . The analysis is carried out in the energy-subcritical regime. As a consequence, our results extend and improve upon previous results in the literature for general nonlinear wave equations.



1.
Introduction

In this article, we primarily concentrate on the well-posedness for inhomogeneous nonlinear wave equations in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
\dot{H}^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. We begin by presenting the form of the equation,

(1.1)

\begin{cases}u_{tt}-\Delta u+|x|^{-b}|u|^{\alpha}u=0,&(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R},\\[4.0pt]
u(0)=\varphi(x),\quad u_{t}(0)=\psi(x),&x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\end{cases}.

This type of nonlinearity was first studied in
[
1
]
, where the well-posedness of the Schrödinger equation with this nonlinear term was investigated. Motivated by this article, we investigate the well-posedness theory of inhomogeneous nonlinear wave equations. When
b=0
, the equation reduces to the standard nonlinear wave equation. Research on this equation traces back to Jörgen, who investigated the Cauchy problem for the semilinear wave equation.

(1.2)

\begin{cases}u_{tt}-\Delta u+f(u)=0,&(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R},\\[4.0pt]
u(0)=\varphi(x),\quad u_{t}(0)=\psi(x),&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\end{cases}

where

(1.3)

f(u)=u|u|^{p-1},\quad 1<p\leqslant\frac{n+2}{n-2},\quad n\geqslant 3.

where
p_{c}=\frac{n+2}{n-2}
corresponds to the
H^{1}
critical exponent for (
1.2
).When
n\leq 2
, all
1<p<\infty
lie in the subcritical growth range. For
n=3
and
1<p<p_{c}=5
, Jörgen
[
2
]
established the global well-posedness of smooth solutions to (
1.2
), (
1.3
) in 1961. For higher dimensions
(3<n\leq 9)
, global well-posedness of smooth solutions was proved by Brenner and Wahl, as well as Pecher, in
[
3
,
4
]
. Furthermore, Ginibre and Velo
[
5
,
6
]
resolved the global well-posedness of energy solutions for (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) in the energy-subcritical regime. Additional well-posedness conclusions for the energy-critical regime exist, but we shall not discuss them in detail here. Recent years have witnessed further advances. For example, Shao et al.
[
7
]
demonstrated the existence of blow-up solutions for the defocusing wave equation in the supercritical setting—a phenomenon previously deemed impossible in the energy-critical and energy-subcritical regimes. Additionally, for dimensions
n\geq 4
, Jendrej
[
8
]
and coauthors demonstrated that radial ground state solutions exist for the nonlinear wave equation in the energy-critical setting.
As is well known, the wave equation admits a unique solution in
C([0,T],L^{2})\cap C^{1}([0,T],H^{-1})
if and only if it satisfies the following integral equation—Duhamel’s formula:

u(x,t)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\cos|\xi|t\,\mathcal{F}\varphi+\mathcal{F}^{-1}\frac{\sin|\xi|t}{|\xi|}\,\mathcal{F}\psi+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\frac{\sin|\xi|(t-\tau)}{|\xi|}\,\mathcal{F}h\,d\tau

\triangleq\dot{K}(t)\varphi+K(t)\psi+\int_{0}^{t}K(t-\tau)h(x,\tau)\,d\tau

\triangleq\dot{K}(t)\varphi+K(t)\psi+Gh\quad\left(K(t)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\frac{\sin|\xi|t}{|\xi|}\,\mathcal{F}\right)

which holds for all
t\in[0,T]
. With further regularity assumptions on the nonlinear term and initial data—for instance, in three dimensions,
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
and
f(0)=0
, along with
\phi,\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
—we have
u\in C([0,T],C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
.

Moreover, the nonlinear wave equation admits the familiar energy conservation law,

(1.4)

E(u(t))=\frac{1}{2}\int|u_{t}(t,x)|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int|\nabla u(t,x)|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{p+1}\int|u(t,x)|^{p+1}dx=E(u(0))

We now present our main result:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
0<\alpha<\frac{4-2b}{3}
and
0<b<2
. Then, for any initial data
(\phi,\psi)\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, equation (
1.1
) is locally well-posed in the space
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Under the conditions
0<\alpha<\frac{4-2b}{3}
and
0<b<2
, equation (
1.1
) is globally well-posed in
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for small initial data.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
0<\alpha<\frac{4-2b}{3-2s}
and
\frac{1}{2}<b<\frac{3}{2}
. If
0<s<b-\frac{1}{2}
. Then for any initial data
(\varphi,\psi)\in\dot{H}^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, equation (
1.1
) is locally well-posed in the space
\dot{H}^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

Remark 1.4
.

A global existence result for solutions under the conditions of Theorem
1.3
is not obtained in the present paper. We expect to improve this in future work.

3.
the proof of the main theory: part 1

Prior to the proof of the main theorem, we state a key lemma regarding the estimation of the nonlinearity.

Lemma 3.1
.

Given
0<\alpha<\frac{4-2b}{3}
and
0<b<2
, we have the following estimate for the nonlinear term:

(3.1)

\||x|^{-b}|u|^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{1}(I;L^{2})}\lesssim(T^{\theta_{1}}+T^{\theta_{2}})\|u\|^{\alpha+1}_{W(I;L^{2})}

where
I=[0,T]
, and
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}>0
.

Proof.

(3.2)

\displaystyle\||x|^{-b}|u|^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\||x|^{-b}|u|^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{2}(B)}+\||x|^{-b}|u|^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{2}(B^{c})}

\displaystyle\leq\||x|^{-b}|u|^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{2}(B)}+\||u|^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{2}}

\||u|^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{2}}=\|u\|_{2(\alpha+1)}^{\alpha+1}
, therefore, by Hölder’s inequality,

(3.3)

\|\|u\|_{2(\alpha+1)}^{\alpha+1}\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim T^{\frac{4-\alpha}{2}}\|u\|^{\alpha+1}_{L^{\frac{2(\alpha+1)}{\alpha-2}}(I,L^{2(\alpha+1)})}\leq T^{\theta_{1}}\|u\|_{W(L^{2};I)}^{\alpha+1},

where
\theta_{1}=\frac{4-\alpha}{2}.

One can readily verify that
(\frac{2(\alpha+1)}{\alpha-2},\frac{2(\alpha+1)}{3})
constitutes an optimal wave-admissible pair. Meanwhile

(3.4)

\||x|^{-b}|u|^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{2}(B)}\leq\||x|^{-b}\|_{L^{\gamma}(B)}\||u|^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma-2}}}\leq\||x|^{-b}\|_{L^{\gamma}(B)}\|u\|^{\alpha+1}_{L^{\frac{2\gamma(\alpha+1)}{\gamma-2}}}.

So

(3.5)

\|\|u\|^{\alpha+1}_{L^{\frac{2\gamma(\alpha+1)}{\gamma-2}}}\|_{L^{1}(I)}\lesssim T^{\frac{\alpha\gamma+4\gamma-6}{2\gamma(\alpha+1)}}\|u\|_{L^{\frac{2\gamma(\alpha+1)}{(\alpha-2)\gamma+6}}(I,L^{\frac{2\gamma(\alpha+1)}{\gamma-2}})}\leq T^{\theta_{2}}\|u\|_{W(L^{2};I)}

where
\theta_{2}=\frac{\alpha\gamma+4\gamma-6}{2\gamma(\alpha+1)}
, It is also easy to verify that
\left(\frac{2\gamma(\alpha+1)}{(\alpha-2)\gamma+6},\frac{2\gamma(\alpha+1)}{3(\gamma-2)}\right)
is an optimal wave-admissible pair. From (
3.2
), (
3.3
), (
3.4
), and (
3.5
), we derive (
3.1
), which concludes the proof of Lemma

.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By virtue of the time reversal symmetry of the wave equation, it suffices to consider positive time. We begin by defining

(3.7)

X=C([0,T];\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap C^{1}([0,T];L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap W([0,T];L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))

and we fix

B(a,T)=\{u\in X;\|u\|_{W([0,T];L^{2})}\leq a\},

Here,
a
and
T
are two undetermined positive constants. We define the mapping

(3.8)

H(u)(t)=\dot{K}(t)\varphi+K(t)\psi+\int_{0}^{t}K(t-\tau)[|x|^{-b}|u|^{\alpha}u](\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau

We now demonstrate that this map is a contraction mapping on
B(a,T)
.
By applying the Strichartz estimates (Theorem
2.6
) in conjunction with Lemma
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\|H(u)\|_{L^{q}(I;L^{3r})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\dot{K}(t)\varphi+K(t)\psi\|_{L^{q}(I;L^{3r})}+\|\int_{0}^{t}K(t-\tau)[|x|^{-b}|u|^{\alpha}u](\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau\|_{L^{q}(I;L^{3r})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\dot{K}(t)\varphi+K(t)\psi\|_{L^{q}(I;B^{\frac{2}{r}}_{r,2})}+\|\int_{0}^{t}K(t-\tau)[|x|^{-b}|u|^{\alpha}u](\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau\|_{L^{q}(I;B^{\frac{2}{r}}_{r,2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\varphi\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}+\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}+(T^{\theta_{1}}+T^{\theta_{2}})\|u\|_{W(I;L^{2})}^{\alpha+1}.

Taking the supremum over all optimal wave-admissible pairs on both sides yields,

\|H(u)\|_{W(I;L^{2})}\lesssim\|\varphi\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}+\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}+(T^{\theta_{1}}+T^{\theta_{2}})a^{\alpha+1}.

Choosing

and taking

we obtain that

.

Applying the Strichartz estimates once more, for any wave-admissible pair

\displaystyle\|H(u)-H(v)\|_{L^{q}(I;L^{3r})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\||x|^{-b}(|u|^{\alpha}u-|v|^{\alpha}v)\|_{L^{1}(I;L^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\||x|^{-b}|u|^{\alpha}|u-v||\|_{L^{1}(I;L^{2})}+\||x|^{-b}|v|^{\alpha}|u-v|\|_{L^{1}(I;L^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim(T^{\theta_{1}}+T^{\theta_{2}})(\|u\|^{\alpha}_{W(I;L^{2})}+\|v\|^{\alpha}_{W(I;L^{2})})\|u-v\|_{W(I;L^{2})}

where

. Similarly, we can choose

Thus we obtain

\|H(u)-H(v)\|_{W(I;L^{2})}\lesssim\frac{1}{2}\|u-v\|_{W(I;L^{2})},

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

From local well-posedness together with energy conservation, we deduce global well-posedness in the sense of small initial data. In fact, the Gagliardo–Nirenberg interpolation inequality yields

\displaystyle E(u(0))=

\displaystyle\|\psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|\nabla\varphi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\frac{1}{p+1}\|\varphi\|^{p+1}_{L^{p+1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

.
Therefore, we use the Hölder’s inequality to deduce that

\|\||x|^{-b}\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p_{2}}}}}\|u\|^{\alpha+1}_{\dot{H}^{s,\frac{1+\frac{1}{\alpha}}{\frac{1}{\alpha p_{2}}+\frac{s}{3}}}}\|_{L^{1}(I)}\lesssim T^{\theta_{1}}\|u\|_{L^{\frac{2(1+\frac{1}{\alpha})}{1+\frac{1}{\alpha}-\frac{6}{\alpha p_{2}}-2s}}(I,\dot{H}^{s,\frac{1+\frac{1}{\alpha}}{\frac{1}{\alpha p_{2}}+\frac{s}{3}}})}^{\alpha+1},

and it is easy to verify that
(\frac{2(1+\frac{1}{\alpha})}{1+\frac{1}{\alpha}-\frac{6}{\alpha p_{2}}-2s},\frac{1+\frac{3}{\alpha}}{\frac{1}{\alpha p_{2}}+s})
is the optimal admissible pair. Hence we have

(4.2)

\|\||x|^{-b}\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p_{2}}}}(B)}\|u\|^{\alpha+1}_{\dot{H}^{s,\frac{1+\frac{1}{\alpha}}{\frac{1}{\alpha p_{2}}+\frac{s}{3}}}}\|_{L^{1}(I)}\lesssim T^{\theta_{1}}\|u\|^{\alpha+1}_{W(I;\dot{H}^{s})}.

Similarly, we obtain

(4.3)

\|\|D^{s}|x|^{-b}\|_{L^{r_{1}}(B)}\|u\|_{L^{(\alpha+1)p_{1}}}^{\alpha+1}\|_{L^{1}(I)}\lesssim T^{\theta_{2}}\|u\|^{\alpha+1}_{W(I;\dot{H}^{s})}.

Combining (
4.2
) and (
4.3
), we arrive at the estimate (
4.1
), thus completing the proof of the lemma.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

Similarly, for the general nonlinear term
|x|^{-b}|u|^{\alpha}v
, the following estimate can also be made.

(4.4)

\||x|^{-b}|u|^{\alpha}v\|_{L^{1}(I,L^{2})}\lesssim(T^{\theta_{1}}+T^{\theta_{2}})\|u\|^{\alpha}_{W(I;\dot{H}^{s})}\|v\|_{W(I;L^{2})},

The proof is completely analogous.

We now begin the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

By using the time symmetry of the wave equation, we only need to consider forward time. We define
X=C([0,T];\dot{H}^{s+1})\cap C^{1}([0,T];\dot{H}^{s})\cap W([0,T];\dot{H}^{s})
, denote
I=[0,T]
, and define

\|u\|_{T}=\|u\|_{W(I;L^{2})}+\|u\|_{W(I;\dot{H}^{s})}.

Following the same notation as in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we define a map
H(u)
. Set
S(a,T)=\{u\in X:\|u\|_{T}\leq a\}
, where
a,T
are two positive constants to be determined, and endow
S(a,T)
with the metric.

d_{T}(u,v)=\|u-v\|_{W(I;L^{2})}.

We now prove that
S(a,T)
is a complete metric space. The proof is essentially the same as that in
[
11
]
(see Theorem 1.2.5 and the proof of Theorem 4.4.1 on page 94). Since
S(a,T)\subset X
, and
X
is complete, it suffices to show that
S(a,T)
is closed in
X
. Take a sequence
u_{n}\in S(a,T)
, with
u_{n}\rightarrow u
as
n\rightarrow\infty
, we need to verify that
u\in S(a,T)
. Because
u_{n}\in C([0,T];\dot{H}^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
, the set
{u_{n}(t)}
is bounded in
\dot{H}^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for each
t\in I
. By the reflexivity of
\dot{H}^{s+1}
, there exists a weakly convergent subsequence, still denoted by
u_{n}(t)
, such that
u_{n}(t)\rightharpoonup v(t)
in
\dot{H}^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, and

\|v(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{s+1}}\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}{\|u_{n}\|_{\dot{H}^{s+1}}}\leq a

On the other hand, by our assumption
d_{T}(u_{n},u)\to 0
, it follows that
u_{n}\to u
in
L^{q}(I;L^{3r})
, where
(q,r)
is any optimal wave admissible pair. Since
(\infty,2)
is also an optimal wave admissible pair, we obtain
u_{n}\to u
in
L^{\infty}(I;L^{6})
. Thus, for almost every
t\in I
, we have
u_{n}(t)\to u(t)
in
L^{6}
. Taking the space
\dot{H}^{s+1}\cap L^{6}
, by uniqueness of the limit in this space, we obtain
u(t)=v(t)
. Then, using a density argument and the properties of
v
itself, we obtain
\|u(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}_{x}}\leq a
. That is,
u\in C(I;\dot{H}^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
. By similar reasoning, we can also show that
u\in W(I;\dot{H}^{s})
and
C^{1}(I;\dot{H}^{s})
, and furthermore
\|u\|_{T}\leq a
, thereby completing the proof of closedness.

Next, applying the Strichartz estimates again, we have the following two estimates:

\displaystyle\|H(u)\|_{W(I;\dot{H}^{s})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\varphi\|_{\dot{H}^{s+1}}+\|\psi\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}+\|D^{s}[|x|^{-b}|u|^{\alpha}u]\|_{L^{1}(I;L^{2})},

\displaystyle\|H(u)\|_{W(I;L^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\varphi\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}+\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}+\|[|x|^{-b}|u|^{\alpha}u]\|_{L^{1}(I;L^{2})}.

Using Lemma
4.2
and Remark
4.3
, we have

\|H(u)\|_{T}\lesssim\|\varphi\|_{\dot{H}^{s+1}}+\|\psi\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}+(T^{\theta_{1}}+T^{\theta_{2}})a^{\alpha+1}.

Similarly, we choose
a=2\|(\varphi,\psi)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}}
and
T>0
such that

a^{\alpha}(T^{\theta_{1}}+T^{\theta_{2}})<\frac{1}{4},

then we obtain that
H
is a self-mapping on
S(a,T)
.

On the other hand, by an argument similar to the previous one, we have

(4.5)

d_{T}(H(u),H(v))\lesssim(T^{\theta_{1}}+T^{\theta_{2}})(\|u\|^{\alpha}_{T}+\|v\|^{\alpha}_{T})d_{T}(u,v).

Taking
u,v\in S(a,T)
, from (
4.5
) we obtain

d_{T}(H(u),H(v))\lesssim(T^{\theta_{1}}+T^{\theta_{2}})a^{\alpha}d_{T}(u,v)\leq\frac{1}{4}d_{T}(u,v),

which implies that
H
is a contraction mapping on
S(a,T)
. Applying the Banach fixed point theorem, there exists a unique fixed point in
S(a,T)
such that the Duhamel formula holds. This completes the proof.
∎
