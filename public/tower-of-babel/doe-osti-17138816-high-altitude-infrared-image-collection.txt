1.0 Summary
 The High-Altitude Infrared Image Collection (HAIIC) campaign was conducted at the U.S. Department
 of Energy Atmospheric Radiation Measurement (ARM) User Facility’s Bankhead National Forest (BNF)
 site in Alabama during August 12-15, 2025. The effort supports the development of a high-altitude
 atmospheric sensing platform based on an advanced tethered kite system designed for long-duration
 environmental observations.

 The primary purpose of this campaign was to collect a comprehensive data set of infrared (IR) imagery
 and synchronized attitude measurements to improve and validate a previously developed vision-based
 attitude estimation algorithm. This technology is being developed as a redundant attitude-sensing
 capability for Toyota's airborne kite platform, providing backup orientation information in the event of
 inertial measurement unit (IMU) degradation or failure. Ultimately, the broader research program aims to
 enable atmospheric data collection missions capable of measuring variables such as temperature,
 humidity, pressure, and wind conditions at various altitudes over extended periods.

 A custom data acquisition system was developed and deployed for the campaign. The system integrated a
 Boson+320 infrared camera, a VN-200 Global Navigation Satellite System (GNSS)-aided inertial
 navigation system, a Raspberry Pi 4B+ for onboard processing and storage, and a Gremsy T7 gimbal for
 precise camera orientation control. During data collection, infrared imagery was recorded alongside
 synchronized IMU and gimbal measurements. The primary data set was acquired across roll angles
 ranging from −40° to 40° and pitch angles from −20° to 40°, sampled at 1° increments. Additional data
 sets were collected at coarser 2.5° angular increments to supplement the primary measurements.

 The resulting data set includes infrared images stored in both JPG and NumPy (.npy) formats, RGB
 images in JPG format, and associated CSV files containing synchronized attitude and gimbal information.
 These data provide a valuable resource for evaluating the accuracy and robustness of optical attitude
 estimation methods under real outdoor conditions and at operationally relevant viewing angles.

 Several notable events occurred during the campaign. An electrical issue affected IR Camera 1, which
 was mounted along the pitch axis. As a result, data collection from that camera was inconsistent, and the
 sensor was ultimately removed from the system configuration. Consequently, portions of the data set
 contain only imagery from the forward-facing infrared camera (IR Camera 0). In addition, the data set
 collected on August 12, 2025, contains incorrect timestamp information. Users should note that the
 affected data were actually acquired after 20:00 EDT and should account for this discrepancy during
 analysis. Despite these issues, the data set is considered complete for its intended purpose and contains
 sufficient imagery and synchronized attitude measurements to support algorithm development and
 validation.

2.0 Results
 A significant outcome of this campaign is the creation of a unique, high-resolution infrared image data set
 paired with accurately synchronized attitude measurements. Such data sets are relatively uncommon in
 atmospheric sensing applications and are particularly valuable for advancing computer vision techniques
 for autonomous airborne systems operating in environments where conventional attitude sensors may be
 unreliable or unavailable. Please refer to the data set shown in https://iop.arm.gov/2025/bnf/haiic/deng-
 gnssins/.
