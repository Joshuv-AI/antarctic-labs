Predictive Value of Combined Serum NOX4, Gal-13, and Cyclophilin A for Prognosis in Severe Pneumonia
Authors: Qindi Liu, Huanghuang Meng, Hong Sun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo analyze the predictive value of the combined use of serum NADPH oxidase 4 (NOX4), galectin-13 (Gal-13), and cyclophilin A (CyPA) for poor prognosis in patients with severe pneumonia(SP).MethodsThis study was a retrospective study.A total of 172 patients with SP admitted to our hospital from September 2023 to September 2025 were enrolled. They were assigned into the good prognosis group (n=98) and the poor prognosis group (n=74) according to the prognosis at 28 days. The levels of NOX4, Gal-13 and CyPA in the serum of patients were detected by ELISA. The point-biserial method was conducted to analyze correlations. Logistic method was conducted to discuss the influencing factors.Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was used to discuss the predictive efficiency. In addition, decision curve analysis(DCA) was used to analyze their clinical application value.ResultsThe CPIS score and the levels of serum CRP, PCT, NOX4, Gal-13 and CyPA in the poor prognosis group were higher than those in the good prognosis group (P<0.05). The prognosis of SP patients 28 days after admission was positively correlated with the levels of serum NOX4 (rpb=0.517, P<0.001), Gal-13 (rpb=0.539, P<0.001), and CyPA (rpb=0.485, P<0.001) at admission. Higher CPIS score, CRP, PCT, NOX4, Gal-13 and CyPA levels were risk factors for poor prognosis (P<0.05). The area under the curve (AUC) of the joint prediction of serum NOX4, Gal-13 and CyPA levels for poor prognosis in SP patients was 0.939. Within the high-risk range of 0.07 to 0.94, the joint prediction of serum NOX4, Gal-13 and CyPA levels for the prognosis of SP patients had obvious positive clinical benefits.ConclusionDetecting the levels of serum NOX4, Gal-13 and CyPA in patients at admission can assist clinicians in evaluating the prognosis of patients with SP.

Introduction

Severe pneumonia (SP) is a critical pulmonary infection that is often complicated by respiratory failure and hemodynamic instability, and frequently requires life-support measures such as mechanical ventilation. Among these cases, community-acquired SP progresses rapidly, is associated with a high short-term mortality rate, and is prone to causing extrapulmonary complications.1 As a critical illness requiring rapid identification, timely antimicrobial therapy, and active supportive treatment,2 accurate assessment of SP patient’s prognostic status is of great significance for saving lives. NADPH oxidase 4 (NOX4) is is widely distributed in various tissues, including the cardiovascular system, kidneys, lungs, and liver, and is a continuously activated oxidase.3 During the pathological process of SP, NOX4 continuously produces H2O2, leading to elevated ROS levels, which promote oxidative damage to alveolar epithelial cells and vascular endothelial cells, thereby triggering apoptosis and disruption of barrier function, ultimately exacerbating pulmonary inflammation.4 Galectin-13 (Gal-13), a member of the β-galactoside-binding protein family, contains a typical galactose-binding domain capable of recognizing and binding β-galactoside glycans.5 Yi et al6 showed that serum Gal-13 levels are significantly elevated in patients with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease; Gal-13 has the characteristics of inducing T-cell apoptosis and regulating eosinophils, and influences immune-cell activation and migration during pulmonary inflammation. Cyclophilin A (CyPA) is a cytoplasmic protein with peptidyl-prolyl cis-trans isomerase activity, involved in protein folding, transport, and functional regulation.7 Studies have shown that serum CypA levels are significantly higher in patients with severe COVID-19 than in mild cases and are positively correlated with inflammatory markers such as IL-6 and CRP.8 Based on the above research background, NOX4, Gal-13, and CyPA may be involved in the pathological progression of SP patients through different molecular mechanisms and may have potential value in predicting the prognosis of SP patients. Although previous studies have identified a variety of biomarkers for predicting the prognosis of SP patients through microbiomic and transcriptomic analyses,9 these biomarkers have not yet been widely applied in clinical practice. Therefore, this study focused on patients with severe community-acquired pneumonia and analyzed the predictive value of NOX4, Gal-13, and CyPA for patient prognosis, which is of great significance for improving prognostic survival in these patients.

General Information and Methods

General Information

A total of 172 SP patients admitted to our hospital from September 2023 to September 2025 were retrospectively selected as study subjects. Inclusion criteria: (1) met the diagnostic criteria for SP,10 and had not received antibacterial treatment prior to admission; (2) age >18 years; (3) complete clinical data. Exclusion criteria: (1) presence of other respiratory-related diseases; (2) coexistence of malignant pulmonary tumors; (3) mental disorder resulting in inability to cooperate with the study; (4) presence of other autoimmune or infection-related diseases; (5) hematologic diseases. Methods

Collection of Baseline Data

Patient medical records were reviewed to obtain general information including age, sex, adverse lifestyle history, and underlying diseases. Complications associated with SP were recorded, and laboratory test results for CRP, PCT, and other indicators were collected.

Measurement of Serum NOX4, Gal-13, and CyPA Levels

On the day of admission, 5 mL of peripheral venous blood was collected and sent to the laboratory. After standing for 20 minutes, samples were centrifuged at 3000 r/min for 20 minutes to obtain the upper serum layer, which was stored at –80 °C. Serum NOX411(Cat. No.: CB11098-Hu; detection range: 0.1–100 mg/mL; purchased from Shanghai Cobe Biotechnology Co., Ltd.; inter-assay coefficient of variation <10%), Gal-136 (Cat. No.: NLH4179; detection range: 0.1–1000 ng/mL; purchased from Nulai Biotechnology Co., Ltd.; inter-assay coefficient of variation <10%), and CyPA12 (Cat. No.: CB11291-Hu; detection range: 0.1–1000 ng/mL; purchased from Shanghai Cobe Biotechnology Co., Ltd.; inter-assay coefficient of variation <10%) levels were measured using ELISA. The specific procedures were performed according to the instructions of each ELISA kit. First, the standards were diluted to gradient concentrations and added to the ELISA-coated plate together with the serum samples from each patient. After the plate was sealed with a sealing membrane, it was incubated in a 37 °C thermostatic water bath for 30 minutes. The liquid in the wells was discarded, and the wells were washed five times with washing buffer. Then, HRP-labeled detection antibody was added, followed by incubation at 37 °C for 30 minutes, and the wells were washed five times again with washing buffer. Finally, chromogenic solution was added to each well and incubated in the dark for 15 minutes. The absorbance was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader, and a standard curve was plotted. The serum levels of NOX4, Gal-13, and CyPA in each patient were calculated according to the standard curve.

Prognosis Assessment and Grouping

Prognosis was evaluated on day 28 of hospitalization. Based on recovery status, patients were divided into two groups: those who showed improvement or were cured were assigned to the good-prognosis group, while those who experienced disease progression (requiring mechanical ventilation, ICU admission, or presenting with organ dysfunction) or died were assigned to the poor-prognosis group.

Among the 172 SP patients, after 28 days of hospitalization, 74 patients experienced disease deterioration or death and were assigned to the poor-prognosis group, while 98 patients showed improvement or were cured and were assigned to the good-prognosis group.

Correlation Between Serum NOX4, Gal-13, CyPA Levels and Prognosis in SP Patients

The prognostic status of SP patients at 28 days after admission (poor prognosis = 1, good prognosis = 0) was positively correlated with serum NOX4, Gal-13, and CyPA levels at admission: NOX4 (rpb = 0.517, P<0.001), Gal-13 (rpb = 0.539, P<0.001), CyPA (rpb = 0.485, P<0.001) indicating that higher serum NOX4, Gal-13, and CyPA levels were associated with a greater risk of poor prognosis in SP patients.

Logistic Regression Analysis of Factors Influencing Poor Prognosis in SP Patients

The prognostic status of SP patients was used as the dependent variable (poor prognosis = 1, good prognosis = 0). CPIS score, and the measured values of CRP, PCT, NOX4, Gal-13, and CyPA levels—variables with significant differences in the baseline comparison—were selected as independent variables. A multivariate logistic regression analysis using the forward stepwise method showed that higher CPIS score, CRP, PCT, NOX4, Gal-13, and CyPA levels were all risk factors for poor prognosis in SP patients (P<0.05), suggesting that reducing CPIS score and serum CRP, PCT, NOX4, Gal-13, and CyPA levels is of great significance for improving the prognosis of SP patients. Discussion

As a common acute and critical condition in clinical practice, community-acquired severe pneumonia (SP) has led to a large number of hospitalizations and deaths worldwide,is characterized by rapid disease progression among respiratory infectious diseases, and has become a major challenge in global public health.13 With population aging, the increasing number of individuals with impaired immune function, and the spread of drug-resistant bacteria caused by antibiotic overuse, the incidence of SPhas shown an annual upward trend. This not only imposes a heavy financial burden on patients’ families but also consumes substantial medical resources.14 The pathophysiological process of SP is complex and involves multiple mechanisms, including pathogen invasion, dysregulated immune response, excessive activation of oxidative stress, and systemic inflammatory response syndrome.15 After pathogens invade the lungs, they may induce local inflammation by releasing toxins. Once the inflammatory response becomes uncontrolled, large amounts of inflammatory mediators such as CRP and PCT enter the bloodstream, leading to systemic inflammatory imbalance, which damages lung tissue and multiple organs throughout the body, ultimately affecting patient prognosis.16 Therefore, early identification of prognostic risk factors in SP and the search for sensitive and specific biomarkers hold significant clinical value for improving patient outcomes.

This study retrospectively analyzed the clinical data and serum biomarkers of 172 patients with SP, and the results showed that CPIS scores and serum CRP, PCT, NOX4, Gal-13, and CyPA levels were higher in the poor-prognosis group than in the good-prognosis group. CPIS, as a classic tool for assessing the severity of pulmonary infection, has been proven to effectively reflect the extent of pulmonary inflammatory infiltration, oxygenation status, and systemic infection. Higher CPIS scores often indicate more severe disease and poorer prognosis,17 which is consistent with the findings of this study. CRP and PCT, widely used in clinical practice as inflammatory markers, rise rapidly after infection. Their levels are closely related to the severity of infection and treatment response, making them important reference indicators for evaluating disease progression and prognosis in SP. The results of this study further confirm these conclusions.18 Correlation analysis revealed that the 28-day prognosis of SP patients was positively correlated with serum NOX4, Gal-13, and CyPA levels at admission, indicating that higher levels of these biomarkers correspond to a greater risk of poor prognosis. NOX4, an important member of the NADPH oxidase family, primarily functions to catalyze the production of ROS, which play a key role in oxidative stress responses.19 In the pathological process of SP, pathogen infection can activate NOX4 expression, leading to excessive ROS production. Excessive ROS damages pulmonary epithelial cells and vascular endothelial cells, exacerbating pulmonary inflammation and tissue damage. Meanwhile, It can activate signaling pathways such as NF-κB, promoting the release of inflammatory mediators and forming a vicious cycle of inflammation and oxidative stress, ultimately worsening the disease condition of SP patients.11 Gal-13, a member of the galectin family, has been increasingly recognized in recent years for its role in inflammation and immune regulation.20 By binding to glycoproteins on immune cell surfaces, Gal-13 modulates immune-cell activation, proliferation, and migration, participating in the initiation and progression of pulmonary inflammation.21 In SP, elevated Gal-13 may aggravate systemic inflammatory imbalance by promoting pro-inflammatory cytokine release and suppressing anti-inflammatory responses, thereby increasing the risk of poor prognosis. CyPA is a widely expressed protein in eukaryotic cells with peptidyl-prolyl cis-trans isomerase activity and participates in various biological processes including cell signaling, immune regulation, and inflammatory responses.22 Studies have shown that CyPA can activate MAPK and NF-κB signaling pathways, promoting the release of inflammatory mediators and aggravating pulmonary inflammatory injury. Additionally, CyPA contributes to increased vascular permeability and pulmonary edema, further impairing respiratory function and influencing prognosis.23

Logistic regression analysis indicated that higher CPIS scores, CRP, PCT, NOX4, Gal-13, and CyPA levels were independent risk factors for poor prognosis in SP, suggesting that these indicators can serve as important predictive factors. Among them, NOX4, Gal-13, and CyPA, as novel biomarkers, provide new references for clinical assessment. NOX4-mediated oxidative stress is one of the major mechanisms leading to pulmonary functional deterioration in patients of SP; thus, elevated NOX4 reflects the severity of oxidative injury in the lungs. Gal-13 and CyPA, on the other hand, influence disease progression and prognosis by modulating immune and inflammatory responses, and changes in their levels indirectly reflect the degree of immune dysregulation. Xu et al24 analyzed the longitudinal relationship between serum ferritin and the prognosis of SP patients. Their results showed that serum ferritin had a higher predictive ability for prognostic mortality in SP patients than the community-acquired pneumonia severity score, with a sensitivity of 85.70% and a specificity of 71.80%. Yang et al25 reported that the area under the curve (AUC) of metastasis-associated lung adenocarcinoma transcript 1 (MALAT1) for predicting prognostic mortality in children with severe community-acquired pneumonia was 0.927. In the present study, ROC curve analysis showed that the AUC of the combined detection of serum NOX4, Gal-13, and CyPA for predicting poor prognosis in SP patients was 0.939, with a sensitivity of 93.24% and a specificity of 78.57%, which were higher than the predictive values of serum ferritin and MALAT1 reported in previous studies. This may be because the combined detection of NOX4, Gal-13, and CyPA can cover multiple key pathological processes involved in SP progression, thereby allowing a more systematic and comprehensive assessment of patients’ prognostic risk.Furthermore, the DCA in this study showed that within the high-risk threshold range of 0.07–0.94, the combined model achieved significant clinical net benefit, confirming that this model has good clinical practical value. These findings suggest that serum NOX4, Gal-13, and CyPA may serve as supplements to existing clinical scoring systems and provide clinicians with a more scientific and reliable reference for formulating individualized treatment strategies, which is of great significance for improving prognostic survival in patients.

However, this study still has several limitations. First, it was a retrospective, single-center study with a limited sample size and no external validation data, which may have introduced certain bias into the results. In future studies, multicenter, large-sample prospective investigations will be conducted to further explore the dynamic changes in these biomarkers during disease progression and recovery, analyze their associations with different pathogen types, and further validate the clinical application value of this combined detection model in predicting the prognosis of SP patients.

In summary, serum NOX4, Gal-13, and CyPA levels at admission are closely associated with the prognosis of SP patients. The combination of these three indicators shows good prognostic predictive performance and high clinical application value. It may assist clinicians in evaluating patient prognosis and is of great significance for optimizing diagnosis and treatment, improving patient survival, and enhancing quality of life.
