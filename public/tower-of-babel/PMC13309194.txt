Hantavirus
Authors: Marie-Laure Oberweis, Ana C. Blanchard, Esther Vaugon
Published: 2026

Hantavirus is a nationally notifiable disease in Canada

On average, 4 to 5 cases of hantavirus are confirmed annually in Canada. Almost all cases are acquired in agricultural settings in Manitoba, Saskatchewan, Alberta, and British Columbia.1 Transmission usually occurs via contact with infected rodents or their excrements; however, the Andes strain is unique in that it can be transmitted person to person, typically following prolonged close contacts.2

Hantavirus causes 2 clinical syndromes

Strains endemic to the Americas, including the Andes virus, cause hantavirus cardiopulmonary syndrome (HCPS), whereas European and Asian strains cause hemorrhagic fever with renal syndrome (HFRS). The illness typically develops after an incubation of 2 to 4 weeks for both syndromes, with a median of 18 days for the Andes strain. Both HCPS and HFRS initially present with fever, headache, myalgia, and abdominal pain. Thereafter, HCPS progresses to pulmonary edema, respiratory failure, and cardiogenic shock, with a case fatality rate between 12% and 45%. In contrast, HFRS is characterized by acute kidney injury and hemorrhagic manifestations (e.g., epistaxis, hematuria, gastrointestinal bleeding), with a case fatality rate between 1% and 12%.2,3

Serology and polymerase chain reaction (PCR) testing is performed at the National Microbiology Laboratory

Hantavirus is confirmed via the presence of immunoglobulin (Ig) M antibodies, a fourfold or greater rise in IgG titres, or PCR detection of hantavirus in blood samples. Serology testing is the preferred diagnostic method, while PCR is typically useful in the acute phase of infection.1 Both forms of testing should be completed with guidance from a microbiologist.

Treatment is mainly supportive

No vaccine or specific antiviral therapy exists for hantavirus. Animal studies suggest that ribavirin and favipiravir may be useful in both treatment and postexposure prophylaxis.4,5 Patients with suspected HCPS should be transferred to centres with extracorporeal membrane oxygenation capabilities.3

Infection prevention and control measures are essential for patients suspected to be infected by Andes virus

Patients with suspected or confirmed Andes virus infection should be isolated with airborne, droplet, and contact precautions. An infectious diseases consultation should be obtained, and the local public health unit should be notified.6
