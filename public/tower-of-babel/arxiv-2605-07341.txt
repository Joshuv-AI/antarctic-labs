A Scaling Limit of Random Walks in the Rational Adeles
Rahul Rajkumar

Abstract
This paper shows the convergence of adele-valued random walks to an adelic Lévy process under scaling limits. We use random walks on the p p -adic numbers to construct random walks initially on the infinite product space, and use survival time analysis to prove that the random walks are almost surely adelic for all time. The adelic random walks are shown to be small perturbations of processes that are supported on a finite product of path spaces. Weak convergence to an adelic Lévy process is established in the J 1 J_{1} Skorokhod topology.

Introduction

One of the major results of 20th century probability theory is the establishment of the Wiener process as a scaling limit of random walks.
The general study of scaling limits of random walks is a core part of contemporary probability theory, replacing continuous-time continuous-space processes with far more tractable discrete-time discrete-space approximations in a principled manner.
Although the topological disconnectedness of the
p
-adic and adelic state spaces trivializes the space of continuous paths, Skorokhod space and Fourier analytic methods allow for the study of Lévy processes associated to
p
-adic and adelic (fractional) heat equations.
In this paper, we consider a natural adelic heat equation and its associated Lévy process.
We obtain an adele-valued random walk process from random walks on each
p
-adic component, and show that these adelic random walks converge weakly to the desired adelic Lévy process.

The initial motivation for
p
-adic mathematical physics was Volovich’s proposal in 1987 that spacetime at the Planck scale may be nonarchimedean
[
29
]
.
Researchers have since studied
p
-adic models as idealizations of a variety of systems displaying hierarchical and ultrametric structure
[
14
,
6
]
, including in the spreading of disease in the presence of social barriers
[
28
,
15
,
13
]
, neural networks and data science
[
35
,
5
]
, geophysics
[
16
,
12
]
, and spin glasses
[
18
,
3
]
.
Since it seems unlikely that Nature would distinguish any prime over all the others, adelic models furthermore aim to put all primes on equal footing
[
26
,
17
]
.
In addition,
p
-adic analogues of classical processes are an active area of research in number theory
[
24
,
20
]
, while
p
-adic constructions have recently contributed to studying long-range Bernoulli percolation by way of hierarchical lattices
[
1
,
10
,
8
,
9
]
.

An analogue of the fractional Laplace operator known as the Vladimirov operator, introduced in
[
27
]
, can be used to define a heat equation over the
p
-adic numbers or a general nonarchimedean local field.
In multiple dimensions and with a specified norm, the Vladimirov operator may be replaced with a Vladimirov-Taibleson operator
[
21
]
.
The fundamental solution determines a Lévy process
[
25
]
, which can also be obtained as scaling limits of random walks
[
34
,
31
,
19
]
.

Weisbart defined an Vladimirov-type operator on the (finite, rational) adeles, which acts as a
p
-adic Vladimirov operator in each component, and showed that the resulting adelic heat equation gives rise to an adelic process associated with the heat equation defined by this operator
[
30
]
.
Urban then extended the results to the setting of an adele ring over an algebraic number field
[
23
]
.
Separately, Yasuda investigated adelic Markov processes
[
33
]
, using the approach of Albeverio and Karwowski
[
2
]
to define processes on balls of shrinking radii and related exit times of the process with the Riemann zeta function.
Torba and Zúñiga-Galindo studied a different adelic process, with dependent components, by constructing an absolute value on the adeles and defining an associated Vladimirov-Taibleson operator
[
22
]
.
We use a metric derived from this absolute value in order to utilize standard characterizations of tightness in the Skorokhod topology.

This paper first summarizes some existing work on
p
-adic and adelic processes, including the convergence of certain
p
-adic random walks under a scaling limit.
Some technical details and notation for the
J_{1}
topology and weak convergence are presented and specialized to this setting.
A sequence of almost-surely adelic random walks is constructed, whose finite dimensional distributions converge to those of the adelic process.
The general case with a summable sequence of diffusion coefficients is shown to be a small perturbation of random walks with finitely many nonzero diffusion coefficients, and converges weakly to the adelic process.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
p
-Adic and Adelic Brownian Motion

A standard introduction to
p
-adic analysis is Gouvêa
[
7
]
.
For each prime
p
, denote by
\mathds{Q}_{p}
the topological field of
p
-adic numbers, equipped with the
p
-adic absolute value
|\cdot|_{p}
.
Under the metric induced by the
p
-adic absolute value,
\mathds{Q}_{p}
is a locally compact, totally disconnected, complete metric space.
The rationals are everywhere dense in
\mathds{Q}_{p}
, while the integers are a dense subset of the unit ball, which is the topological ring of
p
-adic integers
\mathds{Z}_{p}
.
Since the underlying additive group
(\mathds{Q}_{p},+)
is a locally compact abelian group, there is a unique Haar measure
\mu_{p}
with the normalization
\mu_{p}(\mathds{Z}_{p})=1
.

Since
\mathds{Q}_{p}
is Pontryagin self-dual, identify the Pontryagin dual
\widehat{\mathds{Q}_{p}}
with
\mathds{Q}_{p}
to define the Fourier and inverse Fourier transforms as integral operators on suitable complex-valued functions
f
on
\mathds{Q}_{p}
against the Haar measure
\mu_{p}
.
Denote the Fourier and inverse Fourier transforms of
f
by

\mathcal{F}_{p}f\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{p}f,

again complex-valued functions on
\mathds{Q}_{p}
.
For any positive real number
b>0
, let
M_{p,b}
denote the multiplication operator defined by

(M_{p,b}f)(\cdot)=|\cdot|_{p}^{b}f(\cdot).

Definition 1
.

The Vladimirov operator with exponent
b
is the pseudo-differential operator

\Delta_{p,b}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{p}M_{p,b}\mathcal{F}_{p},

a
p
-adic analogue of the fractional Laplace operator.

Definition 2
.

Let
\sigma_{p}
be a positive real number.
The
p
-adic heat equation with diffusion coefficient
\sigma_{p}
and exponent
b
is the pseudo-differential equation

\frac{d}{dt}u=-\sigma_{p}\Delta_{p,b}u,

where
u(t,x)
is a function of real time and
p
-adic space.

Theorem 1

(Varadarajan
[
25
]
)
.

The fundamental solution to the
p
-adic heat equation with diffusion coefficient
\sigma_{p}
and diffusion exponent
b
is the probability density function
f(t,x;p)
given by

f(t,x;p)=\left(\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{p}\exp\left(-\sigma_{p}t|\cdot|_{p}^{b}\right)\right)(x).

The family

\big(f(t,\cdot;p)\big)_{t\in[0,\infty)}

is a convolution semigroup of probability densities on
\mathds{Q}_{p}
.

Let
\mathds{Q}_{p}^{[0,\infty)}
be the space of all
\mathds{Q}_{p}
-valued functions on
[0,\infty)
.
Denote by
D(\mathds{Q}_{p})
the subset of
\mathds{Q}_{p}
-valued paths which are right continuous with left limits, the Skorokhod space of cadlag
\mathds{Q}_{p}
-valued paths, equipped with the standard
J_{1}
Skorokhod topology.
Let
Y^{(p)}
be the Kolmogorov coordinate function on
[0,\infty)\times\mathds{Q}_{p}^{[0,\infty)}
, given by

Y^{(p)}(t,\omega)=Y^{(p)}_{t}(\omega)=\omega(t).

Varadarajan uses the Kolmogorov existence theorem to obtain a probability measure on the space
\mathds{Q}_{p}^{[0,\infty)}
such that
Y^{(p)}_{t}
is a Lévy process with
Y^{(p)}_{0}=0
and with increments
Y^{(p)}_{t+u}-Y^{(p)}_{u}
having density
f(t,\cdot;p)
for any
t>0
and
u\geq 0
.
By an application of the Chentsov criterion, there is then a version of the stochastic process whose sample paths lie in
D(\mathds{Q}_{p})
.
Call such a process a
p
-adic Lévy process with diffusion coefficient
\sigma_{p}
and diffusion exponent
b
.

Theorem 2

(Varadarajan
[
25
]
)
.

There is a probability measure
\mathds{P}_{p}
on
D(\mathds{Q}_{p})
such that the stochastic process

(D(\mathds{Q}_{p}),\mathds{P}_{p},Y^{(p)})

is a
p
-adic Lévy process with diffusion coefficient
\sigma_{p}
and diffusion exponent
b
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
denote the collection of all primes.
The (finite, rational) adeles are the subring
\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}
of the product space
\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\mathds{Q}_{p}
given by

\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}=\left\{x=(x_{p})_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\in\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\mathds{Q}_{p}:x_{p}\in\mathds{Z}_{p}\text{ for almost every $p$}\right\}.

Let
\sigma=(\sigma_{p})_{p\in\mathcal{P}}
be a summable sequence of nonnegative diffusion coefficients, and let
b>0
be fixed.
For suitable functions
f
, define the multiplication operator
M_{\sigma}
by

M_{\sigma}f(x)=\left(\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\sigma_{p}|x_{p}|_{p}^{b}\right)f(x).

A Fourier and inverse Fourier transform on
\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}
are defined for suitable functions
f=(f_{p})_{p\in\mathcal{P}}
by

(\mathcal{F}_{\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}f)(x)=\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{F}_{p}f_{p})(x_{p})

and

(\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}f)(x)=\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{p}f_{p})(x_{p}).

The operator

\Delta_{\sigma,\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}M_{\sigma}\mathcal{F}_{\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}

defines an adelic heat equation

\frac{d}{dt}u=-\Delta_{\sigma,\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}u.

Note that the pseudo-Laplace operator
\Delta_{\sigma,\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}
encodes the diffusion coefficients directly, unlike in the
p
-adic case.

Let
\mathds{P}_{\sigma,\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}
denote the probability measure given by

\mathds{P}_{\sigma,\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}=\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\mathds{P}_{p},

where
\mathds{P}_{p}
is the probability measure on
D(\mathds{Q}_{p})
associated with a
p
-adic Brownian motion with diffusion coefficient
\sigma_{p}
and diffusion exponent
b
.
The finite dimensional distributions of
\mathds{P}_{\sigma,\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}
are given by integration against the fundamental solution of the adelic heat equation.
Let
D(\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}})
denote the subset of
\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}
-valued paths in the product Skorokhod space

D(\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}})\subset D\left(\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\mathds{Q}_{p}\right)=\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}}D(\mathds{Q}_{p}).

Theorem 3

(Weisbart
[
30
]
)
.

The probability measure
\mathds{P}_{\sigma,\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}
gives full measure to
D(\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}})
in the product Skorokhod space
D\left(\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\mathds{Q}_{p}\right)
.

Let
\mathbf{Y}=(Y^{(p)})_{p\in\mathcal{P}}
be the Kolmogorov coordinate function.
Refer to the stochastic process

(D(\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}),\mathds{P}_{\sigma,\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}},\mathbf{Y})

as an adelic Lévy process with diffusion coefficient (sequence)
\sigma
and diffusion exponent
b
.

2.2
Random Walks on
\mathds{Q}_{p}

Let
G_{p}
be the quotient group

G_{p}=\mathds{Q}_{p}/\mathds{Z}_{p},

and, for any
x
in
\mathds{Q}_{p}
, write
\{x\}
for the equivalence class
x+\mathds{Z}_{p}
.
The
p
-adic absolute value passes through the quotient as

|\{x\}|_{p}=\begin{cases}|x|_{p}&\{x\}\neq\{0\}\\
0&\{x\}=\{0\},\end{cases}

under which
G_{p}
is a discrete topological group.
For any
p
-adic number
x
, there is a unique coefficient function
a_{x}:\mathds{Z}\to\{0,\ldots,p-1\}
whose support is bounded below and satisfies

x=\sum_{k\in\mathds{Z}}a_{x}(k)p^{k}.

Identify
G_{p}
with its standard embedding in
\mathds{Q}_{p}
, given by

\{x\}\mapsto\sum_{k<0}a_{x}(k)p^{k},

to obtain a
p
-adic analogue of the standard integer lattice in
\mathds{R}
.

Define the normalization constant
C_{p,b}=p^{b}-1
.
Let
X^{(p)}
be a
G_{p}
-valued random variable with the following distribution:
for any positive integer
k
,

\Pr(|X^{(p)}|_{p}=p^{k})=\frac{C_{p,b}}{p^{kb}},

with
X^{(p)}
uniformly distributed on the level sets
|\{x\}|_{p}=p^{k}
. Let
X_{i}^{(p)}\overset{iid}{\sim}X^{(p)}
be iid copies of
X^{(p)}
, and let

S^{(p)}_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}^{(p)}

define the (discrete-time)
p
-adic random walk on the embedded
G_{p}
.
Let
D=\frac{p^{b}(p-1)}{p^{b+1}-1}\sigma_{p}
and, for each
m\geq 0
, define the random trajectory
\mathcal{S}^{(m)}:[0,\infty)\to\mathds{Q}_{p}
by

\mathcal{S}^{(m)}(t)=p^{m}S_{\lfloor Dp^{mb}t\rfloor}^{(p)}=p^{m}\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor Dp^{mb}t\rfloor}X_{i}^{(p)}.

The constant
D
is chosen so that all finite-dimensional distributions of the measures
\mathds{P}_{p}^{(m)}
converge to those of a
p
-adic Lévy process with diffusion coefficient
\sigma_{p}
and diffusion exponent
b
.

The following results are from
[
31
]
, or can be specialized from
[
19
]
.
Proposition
1
is a moment estimate which implies the uniform Chentsov criterion, and is Proposition 5.1 in
[
19
]
.
The uniform Chentsov estimate
[
4
, Theorem 13.5]
obtained in Proposition
1
is a tool used to establish weak convergence in the previous works
[
31
,
19
]
which we will be able to reuse to obtain Proposition
2
.

Theorem 4
.

The random trajectories
\mathcal{S}^{(m)}
induce probability measures
\mathds{P}_{p}^{(m)}
on
\mathds{Q}_{p}^{[0,\infty)}
which give full measure to
D(\mathds{Q}_{p})
.

Proposition 1
.

For any
r
in
(0,b)
, there is a constant
C
so that, for any
t
in
[0,\infty)
,

\mathds{E}_{\mathds{P}_{p}^{(m)}}(|Y_{t}|_{p}^{r})\leq Ct^{r/b},

where
\mathds{E}_{\mathds{P}_{p}^{(m)}}
denotes expectation with respect to
\mathds{P}_{p}^{(m)}
.

Theorem 5
.

The random walks
\mathcal{S}^{(m)}
converge weakly to a
p
-adic Lévy process with diffusion coefficient
\sigma_{p}
and diffusion exponent
b
.

2.3
Weak Convergence in the Strong
J_{1}
Topology

The product Skorokhod space
\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}}D(\mathds{Q}_{p})
is a Polish space with the product topology, the rarely-used “weak”
J_{1}
topology
[
11
]
. Torba and Zúñiga-Galindo showed
[
22
]
that the adelic absolute value
|\cdot|_{\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}
given by

|x|_{\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}=\max_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\frac{|x|_{p}}{p},\quad x=(x_{p})_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\in\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}

induces a complete metric on
\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}
, though neither the weak
J_{1}
topology nor the operator
\Delta_{\sigma,\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}
require a choice of metric on
\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}
.
The adelic metric gives rise to the standard “strong”
J_{1}
topology, under which
D(\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}})
is Polish.

Since both topologies are Polish, and hence sequential, and since a convergent sequence in the strong
J_{1}
topology is also convergent in the weak
J_{1}
topology, both define the same Borel sigma-algebra
[
32
, Theorem 11.5.3]
.
However, although they support the same collection of probability measures, weak convergence for the weak
J_{1}
topology is just weak convergence of the marginals on each factor
D(\mathds{Q}_{p})
, while weak convergence in the strong
J_{1}
topology is more involved.
Standard theorems for proving weak convergence in the strong
J_{1}
topology rely on the metric on the state space
[
4
, Chapter 16]
.

Instead of relying on a uniform Chentsov estimate for the adelic processes directly, we will use a characterization of uniform tightness for sequences of probability measures.
Let the state space
\Omega
be either
\mathds{Q}_{p}
or
\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}
, with norm denoted by
|\cdot|_{\Omega}
.
For an interval
I
and
\Omega
-valued function
x
, define
w(x,I;\Omega)
to be

w(x,I;\Omega)=\sup_{s,t\in I}|x(s)-x(t)|_{\Omega}.

Call a partition
0=t_{0}<\cdots<t_{v}=T
of an interval
[0,T)
essentially
\delta
-sparse if
t_{i}-t_{i-1}>\delta
for
1\leq i<v
, and let
\Delta(T,\delta)
denote the collection of all essentially
\delta
-sparse partitions of
[0,T)
.
Define the modified modulus of continuity

w_{T}^{\prime}(x,\delta;\Omega)=\inf_{\Delta(T,\delta)}\max_{1\leq i\leq v}w(x,[t_{i-1},t_{i});\Omega).

For an
\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}
-valued function
x=(x_{p})_{p\in\mathcal{P}}
, exchange suprema to obtain the relation

w_{T}^{\prime}(x,\delta;\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}})=\inf_{\Delta(T,\delta)}\max_{1\leq i\leq v}\max_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\frac{w(x_{p},[t_{i-1},t_{i});\mathds{Q}_{p})}{p}.

Tightness of a sequence of probability measures on
D(\Omega)
with respect to the strong
J_{1}
topology is characterized by the modified modulus of continuity and the sup norm for all times
T
.
Convergence of all finite-dimensional distributions then establishes weak convergence.

Theorem 6

(Theorem 16.8 of
[
4
]
)
.

A sequence of probability measures
(P_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is tight in the
J_{1}
topology if and only if:

1.

For each
T
,

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\limsup_{n}P_{n}(x:\sup_{s\leq T}|x(s)|_{\Omega}\geq\lambda)=0,

and

2.

for each
T
and
\lambda
,

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\limsup_{n}P_{n}(x:w_{T}^{\prime}(x,\delta;\Omega)\geq\lambda)=0.

3
Random Walks on the Adeles and Weak Convergence

Fix a diffusion exponent
b>0
throughout.
Let
\sigma=(\sigma_{p})_{p\in\mathcal{P}}
be a summable sequence of diffusion coefficients, and for each
m
let
\mathds{P}_{p}^{(m)}
be the random walk measure on
\mathds{Q}_{p}
with diffusion coefficient
\sigma_{p}
.
Initially define the random walks measure

\mathds{P}_{\sigma,\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}^{(m)}=\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\mathds{P}_{p}^{(m)}

on the product Skorokhod space
\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}}D(\mathds{Q}_{p})
.
Denote the associated random walk process by
\mathbf{S}^{(m)}
.
Let
D
denote the quantity

D\equiv D(p,\sigma,b)=\frac{p^{b}(p-1)}{p^{b+1}-1}\sigma_{p}.

For any time
t
, write the coordinates of the random walk
\mathbf{S}^{(m)}
as

\mathbf{S}^{(m)}(t)=\left(p^{m}S^{(p)}_{\lfloor Dp^{mb}t\rfloor}\right)_{p\in\mathcal{P}}.

For any prime
p
, denote the restriction of the adelic absolute value
|\cdot|_{\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}
to
\mathds{Q}_{p}
as

|\cdot|_{p,\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}=\frac{|\cdot|_{p}}{p}.

For any positive
\lambda
and prime
p
, write

[\lambda]_{p}=p^{\lceil\log_{p}(\lambda)\rceil-1}.

Note that, for any integer
k
,
p^{k}<\lambda
if and only if
p^{k}\leq[\lambda]_{p}
, and

-p^{b}\lambda^{-b}\leq-[\lambda]_{p}^{-b}<-\lambda^{-b}.

The following lemma computes survival probabilities for the adelic random walks in each component
\mathds{Q}_{p}
, which will be used to show that each random walk is almost surely valued in the adeles.

Lemma 1
.

For any
m,T
, and
p
, if
1\leq m+\lceil\log_{p}\lambda\rceil
, then

\mathds{P}_{p}^{(m)}\left(\sup_{s\leq T}|p^{m}S^{(p)}_{\lfloor Dp^{mb}s\rfloor}|_{p,\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}<\lambda\right)=\left(1-\frac{(p[\lambda]_{p})^{-b}}{p^{mb}}\right)^{\lfloor Dp^{mb}T\rfloor}.

Proof.

For any positive integer
k
, by the ultrametric property and since
X^{(p)}_{i}\overset{iid}{\sim}X^{(p)}
are iid,

\displaystyle\Pr\left(\sup_{j\leq n}|S^{(p)}_{j}|_{p}\leq p^{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\Pr\left(|X^{(p)}|_{p}\leq p^{k}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{p^{b}-1}{p^{jb}}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle=\left(1-p^{-{bk}}\right)^{n}.

The desired probability is then

\displaystyle\mathds{P}_{p}^{(m)}\left(\sup_{s\leq T}|p^{m}S^{(p)}_{\lfloor Dp^{mb}s\rfloor}|_{p,\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}<\lambda\right)

\displaystyle=\mathds{P}_{p}^{(m)}\left(\sup_{s\leq T}|p^{m}S^{(p)}_{\lfloor Dp^{mb}s\rfloor}|_{p,\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}\leq[\lambda]_{p}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathds{P}_{p}^{(m)}\left(\sup_{s\leq T}|p^{m}S^{(p)}_{\lfloor Dp^{mb}s\rfloor}|_{p}\leq p[\lambda]_{p}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathds{P}_{p}^{(m)}\left(\sup_{s\leq T}|S^{(p)}_{\lfloor Dp^{mb}s\rfloor}|_{p}\leq p^{m}p[\lambda]_{p}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathds{P}_{p}^{(m)}\left(\sup_{j\leq\lfloor Dp^{mb}T\rfloor}|S^{(p)}_{j}|_{p}\leq p^{m}p[\lambda]_{p}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{(p[\lambda]_{p})^{-b}}{p^{mb}}\right)^{\lfloor Dp^{mb}T\rfloor}.

∎

A path
x=(x_{p})_{p\in\mathcal{P}}
in the product Skorokhod space is valued in
\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}
up to time
T
if and only if, for all times
s\leq T
, all but finitely many components
x_{p}(s)
are in
\mathds{Z}_{p}
.
Under the adelic absolute value,

|\mathds{Z}_{p}|_{p,\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}=p^{-1}

for every prime
p
, so
x
is valued in
\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}
up to time
T
if and only if, for every
s\leq T
,

|x_{p}(s)|_{p,\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}\leq p^{-1}

for all but finitely many primes
p
.
Then a sufficient condition for
x
to be valued in
\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}
up to time
T
is that there exist a finite
M
such that

\sup_{s\leq T}|x_{p}(s)|_{p,\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}\leq p^{-1}\text{ for all $p\geq M$}.

Theorem 7
.

For any
m
, the random walks
\mathbf{S}^{(m)}
are almost surely valued in
\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}
for all time.

Proof.

Fix a constant
c>1
.
There is a
p_{c}
such that, if
p\geq p_{c}
, then

\exp(-cp^{-mb})\leq 1-\frac{1}{p^{mb}}\leq\exp(-p^{-mb}).

Let
A(T,M;m)
be the event that for every
p\geq M
the
p
-th component of
\mathbf{S}^{(m)}
has not left
\mathds{Z}_{p}
up to time
T
:

A(T,M;m)=\left\{\sup_{s\leq T}|p^{m}S^{(p)}_{\lfloor Dp^{mb}s\rfloor}|_{p,\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}\leq p^{-1}\text{ for all $p\geq M$}\right\}.

Let
M\geq p_{c}
.
Then with
\lambda=1
so that
[\lambda]_{p}=p^{-1}
, use Lemma
1
to obtain

\displaystyle\mathds{P}_{\sigma,\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}^{(m)}\left(A(T,M;m)\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{p\geq M}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{mb}}\right)^{\lfloor Dp^{mb}T\rfloor}

\displaystyle\geq\prod_{p\geq M}\exp\left(-cp^{-mb}Dp^{mb}T\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{p\geq M}\exp\left(-cT\frac{p^{b}(p-1)}{p^{b+1}-1}\sigma_{p}\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-cT\sum_{p\geq M}\frac{p^{b}(p-1)}{p^{b+1}-1}\sigma_{p}\right).

Summability of
\sigma_{p}
and the limit comparison test imply that
\frac{p^{b}(p-1)}{p^{b+1}-1}\sigma_{p}
is summable, and so for each
T
and
m
,

\lim_{M\to\infty}\mathds{P}_{\sigma,\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}^{(m)}\left(A(T,M;m)\right)=1.

Continuity from below (in
M
) and above (in
T
) imply
\mathds{P}_{\sigma,\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}^{(m)}\left(D(\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}})\right)=1
.
∎

The following proposition handles the special case of a finitely supported diffusion coefficient using the Chentsov criterion as in previous work.
When the diffusion coefficient sequence
\sigma=(\sigma_{p})_{p\in\mathcal{P}}
is positive for infinitely many
p
, the adelic absolute value will depend on infinitely many components.
However, the summability of
\sigma
will ensure that the random walks are small perturbations of the special case, circumventing this issue.

Proposition 2
.

Assume that
\sigma
is finitely supported, so that there is some
M
such that
\sigma_{p}>0
implies
p<M
.
Then the random walks
\mathbf{S}^{(m)}
converge weakly to an adelic Lévy process with diffusion coefficient
\sigma
.

Proof.

For each
p<M
and any
0<r<b
, by the uniform Chentsov estimate of Proposition
1
there is a constant
C_{p}
such that

\mathds{E}_{\mathds{P}_{p}^{(m)}}|Y^{(p)}_{t}|_{p}^{r}<C_{p}t^{r/b}.

Then, for some
C_{M}
,

\mathds{E}_{\mathds{P}_{\sigma,\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}^{(m)}}|\mathbf{Y}_{t}|^{r}_{\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}\leq M\max_{p<M}\mathds{E}_{\mathds{P}_{p}^{(m)}}\frac{|Y_{t}^{(p)}|_{p}^{r}}{p^{r}}<C_{M}t^{r/b},

and so the random walk measures
\mathds{P}_{\sigma,\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}^{(m)}
satisfy the uniform Chentsov criterion.
Convergence of the finite-dimensional distributions to those of
\mathds{P}_{\sigma,\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}
along with the uniform Chentsov condition then implies the weak convergence of the random walks to adelic Brownian motion.

∎

For a path

x(t)=(x_{p}(t))_{p\in\mathcal{P}},

write
x|_{p<M}
to denote the path

x|_{p<M}(t)=(x_{p}(t)\mathds{1}_{p<M})_{p\in\mathcal{P}}.

Similarly, write
\sigma|_{p<M}
to denote the finitely supported diffusion coefficient

\sigma|_{p<M}=(\sigma_{p}\mathds{1}_{p<M})_{p\in\mathcal{P}}.

Let
\Lambda(T,M,\lambda)
be the event

\Lambda(T,M,\lambda)=\left\{\forall p\geq M,\sup_{s\leq T}|x_{p}(s)|_{p,\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}<\lambda\right\}.

The following lemmas will be used to prove the second condition of Theorem
6
.

Lemma 2
.

For any
T,m,\lambda
, and
M
,

\mathds{P}_{\sigma,\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}^{(m)}\left(w_{T}^{\prime}(x,\delta;\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}})\geq\lambda\cap\Lambda(T,M,\lambda)\right)\leq\mathds{P}^{(m)}_{\sigma|_{p<M},\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}(w_{T}^{\prime}(x|_{p<M},\delta;\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}})\geq\lambda).

Proof.

If
w_{T}^{\prime}(x,\delta;\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}})\geq\lambda
, then for every essentially
\delta
-sparse
\{t_{i}\}
there exists an interval
[t_{i-1},t_{i})
such that

\max_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\frac{w(x_{p},[t_{i-1},t_{i});\mathds{Q}_{p})}{p}\geq\lambda.

If moreover
\sup_{s\leq T}|x_{p}(s)|_{p,\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}<\lambda
for all
p\geq M
, then

\frac{w(x_{p},[t_{i-1},t_{i});\mathds{Q}_{p})}{p}<\lambda

for all subintervals
[t_{i-1},t_{i})
and
p\geq M
.
Hence

\lambda\leq\max_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\frac{w(x_{p},[t_{i-1},t_{i});\mathds{Q}_{p})}{p}=\max_{p<M}\frac{w(x_{p},[t_{i-1},t_{i});\mathds{Q}_{p})}{p}=w({x|_{p<M}},[t_{i-1},t_{i});\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}),

and so, together,

w_{T}^{\prime}(x,\delta;\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}})\geq\lambda\quad\text{ and }\quad\sup_{s\leq T}|x_{p}(s)|_{p,\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}<\lambda\text{ for all $p\geq M$}

implies

w_{T}^{\prime}(x|_{p<M},\delta;\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}})\geq\lambda.

The result then follows from the equality

\mathds{P}_{\sigma,\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}^{(m)}(w_{T}^{\prime}(x|_{p<M},\delta;\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}})\geq\lambda)=\mathds{P}^{(m)}_{\sigma|_{p<M},\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}(w_{T}^{\prime}(x|_{p<M},\delta;\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}})\geq\lambda).

∎

Lemma 3
.

For any
T,\lambda
, and
\epsilon
, there is an
M=M(T,\lambda,\epsilon)
such that

\liminf_{m}\mathds{P}_{\sigma,\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}^{(m)}\left(\Lambda(T,M,\lambda)\right)>1-\epsilon.

Proof.

For any
T,\lambda,\epsilon
, and
M
,

\liminf_{m}\mathds{P}_{\sigma,\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}^{(m)}\left(\Lambda(T,M,\lambda)\right)=\lim_{m}\prod_{p\geq M}\left(1-\frac{(p[\lambda]_{p})^{-b}}{p^{mb}}\right)^{\lfloor Dp^{mb}T\rfloor}.

Apply the dominated convergence theorem for products to interchange the limits and obtain

\displaystyle\liminf_{m}\mathds{P}_{\sigma,\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}^{(m)}\left(\Lambda(T,M,\lambda)\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{p\geq M}\lim_{m}\left(1-\frac{(p[\lambda]_{p})^{-b}}{p^{mb}}\right)^{\lfloor Dp^{mb}T\rfloor}

\displaystyle=\prod_{p\geq M}\exp\left(-T[\lambda]_{p}^{-b}\frac{p-1}{p^{b+1}-1}\sigma_{p}\right).

Since

-p^{b}\lambda^{-b}\leq-[\lambda]_{p}^{-b}<-\lambda^{-b},

obtain

\displaystyle\liminf_{m}\mathds{P}_{\sigma,\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}^{(m)}\left(\Lambda(T,M,\lambda)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\prod_{p\geq M}\exp\left(-T\lambda^{-b}\frac{p^{b}(p-1)}{p^{b+1}-1}\sigma_{p}\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-T\lambda^{-b}\sum_{p\geq M}\frac{p^{b}(p-1)}{p^{b+1}-1}\sigma_{p}\right).

Since the sum is finite,

\lim_{M\to\infty}\exp\left(-T\lambda^{-b}\sum_{p\geq M}\frac{p^{b}(p-1)}{p^{b+1}-1}\sigma_{p}\right)=1,

so there is then a sufficiently large
M
such that

\liminf_{m}\mathds{P}_{\sigma,\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}^{(m)}\left(\Lambda(T,M,\lambda)\right)>1-\epsilon.

∎

The following propositions prove that the tightness conditions of Theorem
6
hold.

Proposition 3
.

For each
T
and
\lambda
,

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\limsup_{m}\mathds{P}_{\sigma,\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}^{(m)}(w_{T}^{\prime}(x,\delta;\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}})\geq\lambda)=0.

Proof.

Use monotonicity and the law of total probability with the events
\Lambda(T,M,\lambda)
and its complement to obtain

\displaystyle\mathds{P}_{\sigma,\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}^{(m)}(w_{T}^{\prime}(x,\delta;\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}})\geq\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq\mathds{P}_{\sigma,\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}^{(m)}(w_{T}^{\prime}(x,\delta;\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}})\geq\lambda\cap\Lambda(T,M,\lambda))

\displaystyle\qquad+\left(1-\mathds{P}_{\sigma,\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}^{(m)}(\Lambda(T,M,\lambda))\right).

By Lemma
2
,

\mathds{P}_{\sigma,\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}^{(m)}(w_{T}^{\prime}(x,\delta;\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}})\geq\lambda\cap\Lambda(T,M,\lambda))\leq\mathds{P}^{(m)}_{\sigma|_{p<M},\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}(w_{T}^{\prime}(x|_{p<M},\delta;\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}})\geq\lambda).

By Proposition
2
, the measures
\mathds{P}^{(m)}_{\sigma|_{p<M},\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}
converge weakly, and hence are tight.
Then by the conditions of Theorem
6
,

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\limsup_{m}\mathds{P}^{(m)}_{\sigma|_{p<M},\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}(w_{T}^{\prime}(x|_{p<M},\delta;\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}})\geq\lambda)=0.

By Lemma
3
, for any
\epsilon
there is an
M
large enough so that

\limsup_{m}\left(1-\mathds{P}_{\sigma,\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}^{(m)}(\Lambda(T,M,\lambda))\right)<\epsilon.

Hence

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\limsup_{m}\mathds{P}_{\sigma,\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}^{(m)}(w_{T}^{\prime}(x,\delta;\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}})\geq\lambda)<\epsilon

for any
\epsilon>0
.
∎

Proposition 4
.

For any
T
,

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\liminf_{m}\mathds{P}_{\sigma,\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}^{(m)}(\sup_{s\leq T}|x(s)|_{\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}<\lambda)=1.

Proof.

Since

-p^{b}\lambda^{-b}\leq-[\lambda]_{p}^{-b}<-\lambda^{-b},

apply the dominated convergence theorem for products to obtain

\displaystyle\liminf_{m}\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\left(1-\frac{(p[\lambda]_{p})^{-b}}{p^{mb}}\right)^{\lfloor Dp^{mb}T\rfloor}

\displaystyle=\prod_{p}\exp\left(-(p[\lambda]_{p})^{-b}\frac{p^{b}(p-1)}{p^{b+1}-1}\sigma_{p}T\right)

\displaystyle\geq\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\exp\left(-\lambda^{-b}\frac{p^{b}(p-1)}{p^{b+1}-1}\sigma_{p}T\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\lambda^{-b}T\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\frac{p^{b}(p-1)}{p^{b+1}-1}\sigma_{p}\right).

Since the sum is finite, take the limit as
\lambda
goes to infinity to conclude

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\liminf_{m}\mathds{P}_{\sigma,\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}^{(m)}(\sup_{s\leq T}|x(s)|_{\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}<\lambda)=1.

∎

Theorem 8
.

The random walks
\mathbf{S}^{(m)}
converge weakly to an adelic Lévy process with diffusion coefficient
\sigma
and diffusion exponent
b
.

Proof.

Propositions
3
and
4
prove that the measures
\mathds{P}_{\sigma,\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}}^{(m)}
corresponding to the adelic random walks
\mathbf{S}^{(m)}
satisfy the conditions of Theorem
6
, and therefore are tight.
Convergence of the finite-dimensional distributions then implies weak convergence.
∎
