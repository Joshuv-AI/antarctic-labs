1. Introduction
1.1 Overview
This report describes the critical configurations of the TEX4TerraPower project that were performed
in FY26Q4. Within this report, the configurations are presented along with physical measurements
performed in support of benchmarking. TEX4TerraPower builds upon the TEX-Chlorine1 experiments,
which in turn were based on the TEX-HEU2 experiments. Similar to TEX-Chlorine, the TEX4TerraPower
configurations iteratively layered HEU fuel with sodium chloride (NaCl) absorbers. However, the
main difference in the density achieved during the fabrication process of the sodium chloride
absorbers. Unlike TEX-Chlorine, which was able to achieve ~70% theoretical density, the
TEX4TerraPower salt plates achieved >90% theoretical density by hydraulically pressing the salt.
The design of the new configurations, described in detail in [1], build upon the TEX-HEU experiments3
and the Zeus copper reflected experiments4 to provide nuclear data validation in direct support of
the Molten Chloride Fast Reactor (MCFR) and Molten Chloride Reactor Experiment (MCRE) as part
of TerraPower’s molten salt reactor design. The assemblies were designed specifically to target
thermal and fast neutron capture reaction channels in 35Cl, as highlighted as discrepant by the
nuclear data community. Of particular interest are the 35Cl (n,ɣ), and 35Cl (n,p) reaction channels
which are considered drivers in the uncertainty criticality safety analyses and reactor design
validation.
These experiments were conducted in support of the Department of Energy (DOE)/Nuclear
Regulatory Commission (NRC) Collaboration for Criticality Safety Support for Commercial-Scale
HALEU for Fuel Cycles and Transportation (DNCSH). The experiments were performed at the
National Criticality Experiments Research Center (NCERC) at the Nevada National Security Site
(NNSS) between January 13, 2026 and January 21, 2026 for the polyethylene reflected configuration
and between February 9, 2026 and February 18, 2026 for the copper reflected configuration. The
experiments were conducted with NEN-2 staff including Theresa Culter, Travis Grove, J. Goda, Nick
Thompson, Trevor Omoto, and Alex McSpaden. LLNL staff supported the experiments including Eric
Aboud, Christopher Fitzgerald, Jacob Glesmann, and Jesse Norris.

2. Experiment Description
In this discussion, various sections and subsections are taken directly from the TEX-Chlorine
benchmark with minimal or no changes.

2.1 General Experiment Design
Two discrete experiment designs were used in this experiment to ensure adequate coverage to the
needs presented by TerraPower. The two designs are easily separated as polyethylene and copper
reﬂected conﬁgurations and are brieﬂy discussed within this section. For a more detailed description
of the conﬁguration designs, refer to the experimental design report [1].

2.1.1 Polyethylene Reﬂected Conﬁguration
The polyethylene reﬂected conﬁguration follows the structure of TEX-Chlorine. Layers were
iteratively assembled to create the core stack which was reﬂected by a 1” high-density polyethylene
(HDPE) bottom reﬂector, 1” HDPE annular reﬂector rings, and a variable thickness HDPE upper
reﬂector. The core stack layers alternated between HEU ‘Jemima’ fuel plates, HDPE moderator
plates, and 0.5 in. (1.27 cm) sodium chloride absorber plates. Coarse reactivity was controlled by
HEU mass, which was completed practically through swapping HEU plates with HEU plates with
varying inner annuli or by adding fuel layers to the core. Fine reactivity control was performed via
upper reﬂector thickness. A visual rendering of the polyethylene reﬂected conﬁguration is provided
in Figure 1.

 Figure 1: CAD rendering of the TEX4TerraPower polyethylene reflected configuration.

 9

2.1.2 Copper Reﬂected Conﬁguration
The copper reﬂected conﬁguration follows a mixed structure of TEX-Chlorine and Zeus. Layers were
iteratively assembled to create the core stack which was reﬂected by a 5.68 in. (14.43 cm) bottom
copper ‘penny’ reﬂector, various copper block side reﬂectors, and a 5.68 in. (14.43 cm) thick upper
reﬂector. The core stack layers alternated between 21 in. (53.34 cm) total diameter HEU fuel plates
and 1 in. (2.54 cm) sodium chloride absorber plates. Coarse reactivity was controlled by HEU mass,
which was completed practically through swapping HEU plates with HEU plates with varying inner
annuli or by adding fuel layers to the core. Fine reactivity control was performed via internal
aluminum spacer plates. A visual rendering of the polyethylene reﬂected conﬁguration is provided in
Figure 2.

2.2 Assembly Machine
2.2.1 Polyethylene Reﬂected Conﬁguration
The Comet General Purpose Critical Assembly Machine is a vertical lift machine (VLM) used to
remotely assemble a critical experiment. As shown in Figure 3, Comet consists of the surrounding
structure, stationary platform, and movable platen. For HEU-MET-MIXED-021 (TEX-HEU), HEU-MET-
INTER-013 (TEX-Hf), HEU-MET-THERM-038 (TEX-Chlorine), and the polyethylene configuration of
this experimental campaign, additional parts were affixed to Comet, including: an experiment
platform, which was affixed to the stationary platform, and a lower adapter, which extends the
movable platen.

 Figure 3: Comet General Purpose Critical Assembly Machine with the TEX4TerraPower polyethylene
 reflected configuration.

During assembly, roughly half of the experiment is built on the experiment platform with the other
half built on the lower adapter. During operation, the movable platen is extended vertically to bring
the two halves of the experiment into contact. The platen is then driven in further to lift the upper
stack, ensuring no gaps between the two halves of the assembly. Once fully assembled, the two
halves are only separated by a thin aluminum membrane.
The Godiva IV fast burst reactor is located in the same room as Comet but is located at greater than
10 ft (3 m) away from the machine.

2.2.1.1 Stationary Platform
The stationary platform, also called the experiment platform, was used in the polyethylene reflected
configuration to hold the upper half of the core stack. The experimental platform is shown in Figure
4. The platform consists of an interface plate and four standoffs, which connect the interface plate
to the stationary platform. The membrane is placed atop of the interface plate, covering the interface
plate hole, allowing the movable platen to lift the lower core stack to the point where it lifts the
membrane and the upper core stack. The interface plate includes four pegs which align and act as
rails for the membrane, allowing vertical movement without allowing horizontal displacement.

 Figure 4: Experiment platform, Alignment Plate, and Membrane on the Stationary Platform of Comet.

The interface plate is a 28 in. x 28 in. x 0.5 in. (71.12 cm x 71.12 cm x 1.27 cm) Al 6061-T6 plate with
a 19 in. (48.26 cm) diameter hole through its center. The standoffs are 12 in. (30.48 cm) long Al 6061-
T6 cylinders with a 1.25 in. (3.175 cm) diameter. These standoffs affix the experiment platform to the
stationary platform of Comet. The membrane is a 21 in x 21 in (53.34 cm x 53.34 cm) Al 6061-T6 plate
with a thickness of 0.125 in (0.3175 cm), reported in Table 1. The membrane features four holes, one
in each corner, ensuring consistent alignment when placed onto the interface plate using the four
pegs. The design allows the membrane to be lifted to 0.75 in (1.905 cm).

2.3 Highly Enriched Uranium Plates
The HEU fuel plates, known as the “Jemima” plates, are identiﬁed using a common nomenclature
indicative of their inner annulus diameters of 2.5 inch (6.35 cm), 6 in. (15.24 cm), 10 in. (25.4 cm), or
solid cylindrical plates. The plate types are identiﬁed by the inner annulus diameter and are labeled
15/0-HEU, 15/2.5-HEU, 15/6-HEU, and 15/10-HEU, respectively. These plates have nominal
thicknesses of 0.118 in. (0.29972 cm), nominal outer diameters of 15 in. (38.1 cm), and are nominally
enriched to 93%. Similarly, there are a matching set of HEU rings which have nominal inner diameters
of 15 in. (38.1 cm), nominal outer diameters of 21 in. (53.34 cm), and thicknesses of 0.118 in.
(0.29972 cm). These rings are referred to as 21/15-HEU.

The 15/0-HEU, 6/0-HEU, and 15/2.5-HEU plates were fabricated at Los Alamos Scientiﬁc Laboratory
(now LANL) in the mid-1950’s. The 15/10-HEU plates were modiﬁed from the existing stock of 15/0-
HEU plates. The 15/6-HEU plates and the 21/15-HEU rings were manufactured by Oak Ridge National
Laboratory at a later date but were designed to match and mate with the existing inventory. Figure 13
shows how the 15/X-HEU plates nest within the 21/15-HEU rings.

2.4.1 Polyethylene Components
The HDPE parts include moderator (MOD) plates, reﬂector (REF) plates, a bottom reﬂector
(BOTREFSRC), reﬂector rings (RING), and caps (CAP,BOTCAP). A representation of how the

conﬁguration were procured for the HEU-MET-MIXED-021 and HEU-MET-INTER-013 campaigns.

of 0.00635 cm, by LLNL’s Dimensional Inspection Laboratory prior to the experiment. The following
subsections describe the components used during this experimental campaign.

2.4.1.2 Annular Reﬂector Rings
The polyethylene reﬂector rings are annular cylinders with varying thicknesses and a nominal inner
and outer diameter of 15.1 in. (38.354 cm) and 17.1 in. (43.434 cm), respectively. The reﬂector rings
have four nominal thicknesses: 0.25 in. (0.635 cm), 0.5 in. (1.27 cm), 1 in. (2.54 cm), and 3 in. (7.62
cm). The reﬂector rings stack around the core stack to provide an edective 1 in. (2.54 cm) of side
reﬂection to the stack. The reﬂector rings interlock using step joints to maintain alignment as they
are stacked, while also reducing neutron streaming paths. The step joints have a nominal height of
0.125 in. (0.3175 cm). The rings around the lower core stack sit on the bottom reﬂector (BOTREF), as
described in Section 0. The top of the annular reﬂectors is capped with polyethylene caps (CAP or
BOTCAP), as described in Section 2.4.1.3. To simplify the description of the rings, the shape and
measurement references are provided in Figure 16. The mass and dimension measurements for the
reﬂector rings are provided in Table 17, Table 18, and Table 19.

2.6.2 Aluminum Encapsulations
Aluminum encapsulations were fabricated to encapsulate the sodium chloride disks. The
encapsulation plates were evenly split between top and bottom halves, as shown in Figure 26 which
shows a rendering of the 1 in. (2.54 cm) annular sodium chloride plate encapsulation. All of the plates

cm). The full, non-annular, plates have a nominal inner diameter of 14.030 in. (35.6362 cm) which
represented the joining lip between the top and bottom halves. The annular plate has a nominal inner
diameter of 14.15 in. (35.941 cm) but also features an inner annulus. To diderentiate between the
sets of diameters, annulus diameters are labeled as ‘minor’, and the edge diameters are labeled as
‘major’. The nominal minor outer diameter is 2.935 in. (7.4549 cm), and the nominal minor inner
diameter is 2.511 in. (6.3779 cm).

dimensions for both 1 in. (2.54 cm) plate types. The mass measurements were performed using an
Ohaus 35 kg scale (C522079580), the diameter measurements using Mitutoyo 24” Calipers (74337),
and height/thickness measurements using Mitutoyo 8” Calipers (B23313028), Starrett 12” Height
Gauge (201230025), and a surface plate (H-58-245). The distinction between height and thickness is
that height represents the total height of the plate while the thickness represents the wall thickness.

4. Conclusions

A set of two experiments were performed in January and February 2026 as part of the TEX-Chlorine
for TerraPower (TEX4TerraPower) collaboration between Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory,
Los Alamos National Laboratory, and TerraPower LLC as part of the Department of Energy
(DOE)/Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC) Collaboration for Criticality Safety Support for
Commercial-Scale HALEU for Fuel Cycles and Transportation (DNCSH). The experiments were
performed at the National Criticality Experiments Research Center (NCERC) in the Device Assembly
Facility (DAF) at the Nevada National Security Site (NNSS). TEX4TerraPower builds upon the TEX-
Chlorine, TEX-HEU, and Zeus benchmarks to address the nuclear data needs for TerraPower’s Molten
Salt Reactor Experiment (MCRE) and Molten Salt Fast Reactor (MCFR). The assemblies were
designed speciﬁcally to target thermal and fast neutron capture reaction channels in 35Cl, as
highlighted as discrepant by the nuclear data community. Of particular interest are the 35Cl (n,ɣ), and
35
 Cl (n,p) reaction channels which are considered drivers in the uncertainty criticality safety analyses
and reactor design validation.

This report serves to satisfy the experimental execution milestone of the TEX4TerraPower project. In
this document the final configurations that achieved criticality along with the necessary supporting
documentation/information regarding all of the components used within the experiments are given
in full detail. This document precedes the benchmark evaluation of this work.
