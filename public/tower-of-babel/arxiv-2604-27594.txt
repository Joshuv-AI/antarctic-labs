Structural description of (bull, house)-free graphs
Manoj Belavadi, Chính T. Hoàng

Abstract
The bull is a graph consisting of a triangle and two pendant edges. The P 5 P_{5} is the chordless path on five vertices. The house is the complement of a P 5 P_{5} . A graph is k k -critical if it is k k -chromatic but each of its proper induced subgraphs is ( k − 1 ) (k-1) -colorable. It is known that the number of k k -critical P 5 P_{5} -free graphs and bull-free graphs are infinite for large enough k k . We give a structural description of (bull, house)-free graphs and also (bull, P 5 P_{5} )-free graphs. Using these structural properties we prove that for any fixed k k , the number of k k -critical (bull, P 5 P_{5} )-free graphs is finite. This improves on a result of Huang, Li and Xia [Critical ( P 5 P_{5} , bull)-free graphs, Discrete Applied Mathematics 334 (2023) 15–25]. A graph G G is perfectly divisible if for each induced subgraph H H of G G with at least one edge, V ⁡ ( H ) V(H) can be partitioned into two sets V 1 , V 2 V_{1},V_{2} such that every largest clique of H H contains a vertex in V i V_{i} for i = 1 , 2 i=1,2 . Chudnovsky and Sivaraman proved that ( P 5 P_{5} , bull)-free graphs are perfectly divisible [Perfect divisibility and 2-divisibility, Journal of Graph Theory 90 (2019) 54–60]. Our structural result allows us to give a short proof of this theorem.

Introduction

Graph coloring is a well known problem in computer science and
discrete mathematics. A coloring of a graph is an assignment of “colors” to its vertices so that each vertex receives one color, and two vertices are assigned different colors if they are adjacent. If a graph
G
can be colored with
k
colors then we say that
G
is
k
-colorable. The Graph Coloring problem is the problem of coloring a graph with the smallest number
k
of color; this number
k
is the
chromatic number
of
G
and denoted by
\chi(G)
.
A graph
G
is
k
-critical if
\chi(G)=k
but each of its proper induced subgraphs is
(k-1)
-colorable. If a graph is not
k
-colorable then it contains an induced subgraph that is
k
-critical. Thus, the structure of
k
-critical graphs of a graph class
{\cal C}
may explain why certain graphs in
{\cal C}
are
k
-colorable. This line of research is encapsulated by the following problem. Following
[
19
]
, we call it the Finiteness Problem.

Problem 1.1

(Finiteness Problem)
.

Given a class
{\cal C}
of graph and an integer
k
, are there a finite number of
k
-critical graphs in
{\cal C}
?

The Finiteness Problem has an interesting consequence. If the answer to it is “yes” for a class
{\cal C}
and an integer
k
, then there is a polynomial-time algorithm to decide if a graph in
{\cal C}
is
(k-1)
-colorable. The purpose of this paper is to investigate the Finiteness Problem for
P_{5}
-free graphs. To do that, we will need to introduce a few definitions.

1.1
Definitions

Let

denotes the complement of

\overline{G}
as co-

. A graph is

-bipartite if its complement is bipartite.

co-connected
if
\overline{G}
is connected. If

denotes the subgraph of

to

is the number of edges in a shortest path from

to

(if such a path exists). A set

is

(respectively,

) to

if there are all (respectively, no) edges between

and

. A set

is

if

and every vertex in

is complete or anti-complete to

. A graph is

if it does not contain a homogeneous set. Note that prime graphs are connected and co-connected. For a vertex

in

. For a set

P_{k}
(respectively,

vertices. Let

be a set of graphs, we say that

-free if no induced subgraph of

is isomorphic to a graph in
{\cal F}
. A

is the graph

with

. A hole is

if it has an odd number of vertices.
If

and

are two disjoint graphs, then

denotes the union of
A
and
B
. A

(see Figure
1
) is the graph obtained from a triangle with two disjoint pendant edges. The bull has been much studied. A graph

is

of

can be partitioned into two sets

such that

. A graph

if each induced subgraph

of

. Note that the only

-critical perfect graph is the clique on
k

[

proved that a graph is perfect if and only if it does not contain an induced subgraph isomorphic to an odd hole or its complement.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
G
be a connected (bull, house)-free graph. Suppose

is a vertex of
G
, such that
G[N(v)]
has two components, then

is triangle-free or

Proof.

Assume the hypothesis. We will prove by contradiction. We may assume that

has no homogeneous set. Let

be two vertices of
G

to be the number of edges in a shortest path from

to

is not connected), then we let

.
Let
N_{0}:=\{v\}
. For

, let
N_{i}(v):=\{u\in V(G)|~dist(u,v)=i\}
. We are going to prove that for each

and for every

,
N(u)
is a stable set. The proof is by induction on
i
.

For
i=0
, suppose that
G[N(v)]
is not a stable set. Let
A
induce a component of
G[N(v)]
with an edge. By assumption on

,

cannot be a homogeneous set. Thus, there exist
x_{1}
,
x_{2}
in

such that
x_{1}x_{2}\in E(G)
and there exists

such that
y\sim x_{2}
but
y\not\sim x_{1}
. Since
G[N(v)]
has at least two components, there exists
z
in
N(v)\setminus A
.
Then
\{z,v,x_{1},x_{2},y\}
induces either a bull, or a house.

For
i>0
, consider a vertex

. Define

for

(if

exists). We know that

that contains this edge. Note that

be a neighbour of

∎

P_{5}
)-free graphs

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.2
. We will need to discuss the idea of modular decomposition.
A set
M\subseteq V(G)
is a
module
if every vertex in
V(G)\setminus M
is complete or anti-complete to
M
. A module
M
is
trivial
if
M=\emptyset,|M|=1
or
M=V(G)
. Thus, non-trivial modules are homogeneous sets. A module
M
is
strong
if it does not overlap another module. Module
M
is
maximal
if
M\not=V(G)
and there is no module
M^{\prime}\not=V(G)
with
M\subset M^{\prime}
. Gallai
[
10
]
showed that if both
G
and
\overline{G}
are connected then all maximal modules are strong (see
[
23
]
for an English translation of Gallai’s paper). In this case, the maximal modules of
G
form a partition of
V(G)
. The
join
of two graphs
A,B
is the graph obtained by taking
A
,
B
and join each vertex of
A
to each vertex of
B
by an edge (so
A
is complete to
B
and vice versa).

A graph is
prime
if it contains no non-trival modules (ie. no homogeneous sets). Let
G
be a graph such that both
G
and
\overline{G}
are connected. The
skeleton
of
G
, denoted
G_{s}
, is the graph obtained by contracting each maximal module to a single vertex (and removing any loops and multiple edges created). Note that
G_{s}
is a prime subgraph of
G
, induced by a set containing exactly one vertex from each maximal module in
G
.
A
blowup
of a graph
G
is a graph obtained by substituting non-empty cliques for some vertices of
G
.

For the remainder of this section, we use the following decomposition for graphs
G
that are connected and
co-connected. Let
S_{1}
,
S_{2}
,…,
S_{m}
be a partition of
V(G)
into maximal modules. Note that
S_{p}\cap S_{q}
=
\emptyset
for all
p\neq q
,
p
and
q\in[m]
. Let
\chi(G)=k
and let
\chi(G[S_{p}])=k_{p}
, for all

. We define

of
G
as the graph obtained from

by recursively substituting a clique

of size

for each maximal module

in
G
, where

. We use

to denote the clique skeleton of

. Note that the clique skeleton of a graph

, say

and

=

.

The result below can be obtained from Lemma 5 in
[

Lemma 3.1

Suppose

Assume the hypothesis. For a

∎

A

of a graph

Theorem 3.2

Suppose

Assume the hypotheses. The proof is by induction on

denote the maximum number of vertices in a

. Suppose

is a join of two graphs

and

, then clearly

is

is

-critical for some positive integers

such that

.

So we assume

,

From Theorem
