The horizontal Laplacian of a Riemannian submersion with totally geodesic fibers and an integrable horizontal distribution Thanks: The author is partially supported by JSPS KAKENHI Grant Number 23K25769, 24K06703.
Tatsuya Tate

Abstract
. The purpose of this note is to study spectral properties of the horizontal Laplacian of a Riemannian submersion with totally geodesic fibers and an integrable horizontal distribution. We show that the horizontal Laplacian is unitarily equivalent to a twisted Laplacian acting on the space of sections of a certain infinite-rank flat vector bundle over the base manifold of the Riemannian submersion. We give an application of this interpretation to the asymptotic behavior of the scaled first nonzero eigenvalue of the canonical variations introduced by Bérard-Bergery and Bourguignon. Our approach enables us to compare the horizontal Laplacian with the usual Laplacian on a Riemannian covering over the base manifold, and, when the holonomy group is infinite and amenable, we prove a coincidence of the essential spectrum, which strengthen, in our special setup, a result due to Kordyukov in the context of geometric analysis on foliated manifolds.



1.
Introduction

In 1982, Bérard-Bergery and Bourguignon
[
2
]
introduced the

horizontal Laplacian
on the total space of a Riemannian submersion
as a difference between the usual Laplacian and the
vertical Laplacian
,
and they used it to study spectral properties of the Laplacian.
One of their motivation was to systematically understand Urakawa’s example

[
21
]
of the metric on the
3
-sphere whose Laplacian has the first nonzero eigenvalue with multplicity 7.
This metric, which is often called the
Berger metric
,
is defined by a scaling along the fibers of the Hopf fibration.
Taking this into account, they introduced the so-called
canonical variations

of the given Riemannian submersion.
The horizontal Laplacian is well-behaved when the fibers of the submersion are totally geodesic,
and it becomes a useful tool to understand the behavior of the first nonzero eigenvalues of the canonical variations.
Since then, the study of spectral properties of Laplacians on Riemannian submersions
with totally geodesic fibers is one of major subjects in Spectral Geometry.
For example, Besson
[
4
]
obtained a characterization of the Riemannian product
in terms of the equality condition of an inequality on the heat kernel
Besson-Bordoni
[
3
]
studied the eigenvalues of the Laplacian
using a representation theory of the isometry group of the fiber of the Riemannian submersion.
Recently, Narita
[
14
]
gives a sufficient condition in terms of the Ricci curvatures
for the divergence of the scaled first nonzero eigenvalues of the canonical variations with the parameter goes to infinity.
The list of references in
[
14
]
will be helpful to find other works closely related to this vast topic.

When the horizontal distribution of the Riemannian submersion is integrable,
the Riemannian structure is very close to the Riemannian product.
However, as is shown in the example of the irrationally ‘tilted’ Riemannian metric on the torus,
which is mentioned in
[
2
]
, the spectral properties of the corresponding
horizontal Laplacian is still far from trivial.
Our purpose in this paper is to give several spectral properties of the horizontal Laplacian
under the strong assumption that, besides that the fibers are totally geodesic, the horizontal distribution is integrable.
The properties we obtain here comes from an interpretation of the horizontal Laplacian
as a twisted Laplacian on a certain flat vector bundle.
To explain our setup and results, let us prepare some notation.
Let
(M,g)
,
(B,j)
be compact connected
Riemannian manifolds of dimension
n
and
p
, respectively,
and let
\pi\colon M\to B
be a Riemannian submersion.
For each
x\in M
, set
\mathcal{V}_{x}=\ker d\pi_{x}
and write
\mathcal{H}_{x}
for the
orthogonal complement of
\mathcal{V}_{x}^{\perp}
in the tangent space
T_{x}M
.
The distributions
\mathcal{V}
and
\mathcal{H}
are called the
vertical
and
the
horizontal distributions
, respectively.
The Laplacian on
(M,g)
, denoted by
\Delta_{M}
, has a decomposition,

\Delta_{M}=\Delta_{H}+\Delta_{V},

where
\Delta_{V}
is the vertical Laplacian defined by

(\Delta_{V}f)(x)=(\Delta_{F_{\pi(x)}}f|_{\pi^{-1}(\pi(x))})(x),

with the Laplacian
\Delta_{F_{b}}
on the fiber
F_{b}=\pi^{-1}(b)
over a point
b\in B

equipped with the metric induced by
g
, and, by definition,
\Delta_{H}=\Delta_{M}-\Delta_{V}
.
The differential operator
\Delta_{H}
is called the horizontal Laplacian.
It was observed by Bérard-Bergery-Bourguignon
[
2
]
that
\Delta_{H}
and
\Delta_{V}

are commutative on
C^{\infty}(M)
if the Riemannian submersion has totally geodesic fibers.
Suppose now that, for any
b\in B
, the fiber
F_{b}
is a totally geodesic submanifold of
M
.
Under this assumption, Hermann
[
8
]
shows that every two fibers are isometric to each other,
and
\pi\colon M\to B
becomes a fiber bundle with structure group the isomorphism group,
\mathrm{Isom}(F)
,
of a typical fiber
F=F_{b_{o}}
over a fixed base-point
b_{o}\in B
.
Since
M
is compact and
\Delta_{M}
is elliptic, there exists an orthonormal basis
of
L^{2}(M)
consisting of smooth joint eigenfunctions of
\Delta_{M}
,
\Delta_{H}
and
\Delta_{V}
.
Thus the operators
\Delta_{M}
,
\Delta_{V}
,
\Delta_{H}
,
with the space of smooth functions on
M
as their domain, are essentially self-adjoint.
The closure of these operators are denoted by the same notation.
In general, let
\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits(S)
denote the spectrum of a self-adjoint operator
S
on a Hilbert space
and let
\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits_{\mathrm{ess}}(S)
denote the essential spectrum of
S
.
As a set, we have
\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits(\Delta_{V})=\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits(\Delta_{F})
, where
\Delta_{F}
denotes the
Laplacian of the metric on
F
induced by
g
, and
\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits(\Delta_{V})
consists of
eigenvalues, but each eigenvalue of
\Delta_{V}
could have an infinite multiplicity.
More subtle is the spectrum of the horizontal Laplacian
\Delta_{H}
,
which is our main object under the assumption that the horizontal distribution
\mathcal{H}
is integrable.

Now, suppose that each of the fiber of
\pi\colon M\to B
is connected and totally geodesic,
and furthermore, the horizontal distribution
\mathcal{H}
is integrable.
Under these assumptions, we have the holonomy representation (see Lemma
2.1
)

(1)

\mathfrak{h}\colon\pi_{1}(B,b_{o})\to\mathrm{Isom}(F)

of the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(B,b_{o})
with the base-point
b_{o}\in B
,
and its image is the holonomy group
\mathrm{Hol}(b_{o})
at
b_{o}

(see Section
2
for the definition).
Let
\widehat{p}\colon\widehat{B}\to B
be the Riemannian universal covering over
B
.
Later, we impose assumptions on
\mathrm{Hol}(b_{o})
instead of
\pi_{1}(B,b_{o})
.
Thus, we prepare a slightly different Riemannian covering.
Define
B_{H}=\widehat{B}/\mathrm{ker}(\mathfrak{h})
with a natural projection
\varpi_{H}\colon B_{H}\to B
.
Equip the pull-back metric
\varpi_{H}^{*}j
on

so that

regular Riemannian covering. The covering transformation group of

\mathrm{Hol}(b_{o})
.
Since
\mathrm{Hol}(b_{o})
is a subgroup of

, we have a unitary representation

(2)

\rho\colon\mathrm{Hol}(b_{o})\to U(L^{2}(F)),\quad(\rho(h)f)(p)=f(h^{-1}p)\ \ (h\in\mathrm{Hol}(b_{o}),f\in L^{2}(F),\ p\in F),

and the corresponding flat vector bundle

(3)

\pi_{\rho}\colon\mathcal{E}_{\rho}=B_{H}\times_{\rho}L^{2}(F)\to B,

whose fibers are isometrically isomorphic to
L^{2}(F)
.
As in
[
19
]
, all differential operators on
B
are extended to differential operators acting
on the space of smooth sections
C^{\infty}(\mathcal{E}_{\rho})
of
\mathcal{E}_{\rho}
.
Let
\Delta_{\rho}
denote the extension of the Laplacian
\Delta_{B}
on
B
to
C^{\infty}(\mathcal{E}_{\rho})
,
which is often called the
Laplacian twisted by the representation

\rho
.
It is well-known that the operator
\Delta_{\rho}
is essentially self-adjoint on
C^{\infty}(\mathcal{E}_{\rho})

(which is mentioned in
[
20
]
, and its proof using the heat kernel can be found in
[
19
]
).
For
s_{1},s_{2}\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{E}_{\rho})
, we define the inner product
\langle\,s_{1},s_{2}\,\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{E}_{\rho})}
by

\langle\,s_{1},s_{2}\,\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{E}_{\rho})}=\int_{B}\langle\,s_{1}(b),s_{2}(b)\,\rangle_{\pi_{\rho}^{-1}(b)}\,d\mu_{B}(b),

where
d\mu_{B}
is the Riemannian volume measure of
(B,j)

and
\langle\,\cdot,\cdot\,\rangle_{\pi_{\rho}^{-1}(b)}
is the Hilbert space inner product on the fiber
\pi_{\rho}^{-1}(b)
.
Let
L^{2}(\mathcal{E}_{\rho})
denote the Hilbert space obtained
by completing
C^{\infty}(\mathcal{E}_{\rho})
with respect to the norm defined by the above inner product.

When
M
is the Riemannian product
B\times F
, Fubini’s theorem shows
that
L^{2}(M)
is isomorphic to the space of
L^{2}
-sections of the trivial vector bundle
B\times L^{2}(F)

over
B
and the horizontal Laplacian on
B\times F
is unitarily equivalent to the Laplacian acting
on the space of sections of
B\times L^{2}(F)\to B
.
The following theorem generalizes this observation.

Theorem 1.1
.

Under the assumptions mentioned above,

\Delta_{\rho}
is unitarily equivalent to
\Delta_{H}
.

It is natural to expect the assertion in Theorem
1.1
because, as is proved in Section
2
,

M
is isometrically isomorphic, as a fiber bundle, to
B_{H}\times_{\mathrm{Hol}(b_{o})}F
.
However, this rather natural Theorem
1.1
has some applications.
For
a\geq 0
, we set

(4)

C^{\infty}(M,\mu)=\{f\in C^{\infty}(M)\mid\Delta_{V}f=\mu f\},\quad E(\mu,a)=\{f\in C^{\infty}(M,\mu)\mid\Delta_{H}f=af\}.

We denote
E(\mu)
the eigenspace of
\Delta_{F}
with the eigenvalue
\mu
.
Then the unitary operator intertwining
\Delta_{H}
with
\Delta_{\rho}

maps
C^{\infty}(M,\mu)
onto the space of smooth sections of the finite-dimensional flat vector bundle

(5)

\mathcal{E}_{\rho_{\mu}}=\widehat{B}\times_{\rho_{\mu}}E(\mu),

where
\rho_{\mu}
denotes the restriction of
\rho
on
E(\mu)
, and it intertwines
the restriction of
\Delta_{H}
on
C^{\infty}(M,\mu)
with the twisted Laplacian
\Delta_{\rho_{\mu}}
.
Therefore, the following is a direct consequence of Theorem
1.1
.

Corollary 1.2
.

Suppose that the assumptions mentioned above hold true.
For each
\mu\in\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits(\Delta_{F})
, there are countably infinitely many
a\geq 0
such that

E(\mu,a)\neq\{0\}
. Number such
a\geq 0
as

0\leq a_{0}(\mu)\leq a_{1}(\mu)\leq a_{2}(\mu)\leq\cdots\leq a_{k}(\mu)\leq\cdots,

where, a fixed number

is multiply counted with
\dim E(\mu,a)

Then, for each
\mu\in\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits(\Delta_{F})
, we have

a_{k}(\mu)\sim 4\pi C^{2/\dim(B)}k^{2/\dim B}\quad(k\to\infty),\quad C=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{p}{2}+1)}{m(\mu)\mathrm{vol}(B)},

where
m(\mu)
is the multiplicity of
\mu\in\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits(\Delta_{F})
.

The next application of Theorem
1.1
is about asymptotic behavior of
the scaled first nonzero eigenvalue of the canonical variations
\{g_{t}\}_{t>0}
of
the metric
g
on
M
, which is defined by (
[
2
]
)

(i)

g_{t}|_{\mathcal{V}_{x}\times\mathcal{H}_{x}}=0
;

(ii)

g_{t}|_{\mathcal{V}_{x}\times\mathcal{V}_{x}}=t^{2}g|_{\mathcal{V}_{x}\times\mathcal{V}_{x}}
;

(iii)

g_{t}|_{\mathcal{H}_{x}\times\mathcal{H}_{x}}=g|_{\mathcal{H}_{x}\times\mathcal{H}_{x}}
.

In general, for a Riemannian manifold
(N,h)
, the scaled first nonzero eigenvalue is defined by

(6)

\Lambda_{1}(N,h)=\lambda_{1}(N,h)\mathrm{vol}(N,h)^{2/\dim N},

where
\lambda_{1}(N,h)

.
The quantity
\Lambda_{1}(N,h)
is invariant under the scaling

by a positive constant

and is used to understand the

\lambda_{1}(N,h)
as a function of the metric

on
N
.
When
M
is the Riemannian product,
B\times F
, we have
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\Lambda_{1}(M,g_{t})=0
.
However, for the ‘irrational torus’
T(a)
with a quadratic irrational number

,
there exists a constant
c>0
such that
c\leq\Lambda_{1}(M,g_{t})\leq 8\pi
for

.
Due to the totally geodesic and the integrability assumptions,
our
(M,g)
is close to

with the Riemannian product metric.
But, we have the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose that
\mathrm{Hol}(b_{o})
satisfies Kazhdan’s property
\mathrm{(T)}
and
the action of
\mathrm{Hol}(b_{o})
on

As is observed by Sullivan
[
18

, there is an arithmetic lattice

The horizontal Laplacian in our setup is an example of tangentially elliptic

Theorem 1.4

Under the assumptions mentioned above, we have

\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits(\Delta_{B_{H}})\subset\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits(\Delta_{H})
.
If the holonomy group

\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits(\Delta_{B_{H}})=\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits_{\mathrm{ess}}(\Delta_{B_{H}})\subset\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits_{\mathrm{ess}}(\Delta_{H}).

Next, suppose that

is amenable.
Then we have
\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits(\Delta_{B_{H}})=\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits(\Delta_{H})
.
Furthermore, if

\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits_{\mathrm{ess}}(\Delta_{B_{H}})=\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits_{\mathrm{ess}}(\Delta_{H})=\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits(\Delta_{H}).

The fact that

does not have eigenvalues with finite multiplicities if

.
Note that, even if

is amenable and infinite,

, and hence

can have eigenvalues with finite multiplicities. According to Theorem

,
such eigenvalues are not isolated in the spectrum.

Let
L(p)
denote the leaf of the integrable horizontal distribution through

.
Consider the metric on

on

,
and denote

Corollary 1.5
.

In addition to the assumptions mentioned above,
we further assume that

If the stabilizer of

\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits(\Delta_{L(p)})=\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits(\Delta_{H})

2.
A flat vector bundle and a twisted Laplacian

In this section we give proofs of Theorem
1.1
and Corollary
1.2
.
We first show that Riemannian submersions with totally geodesic fibers
and integrable horizontal distributions can be written as fiber products.
The unitary intertwiner between the horizontal Laplacian and the twisted Laplacian
is naturally defined by using this interpretation.

2.1.
Fiber products

Let us begin by recalling the definition of the holonomy group of the Riemannian submersion.
Let
(M,g)
and
(B,j)
be complete connected Riemannian manifolds and let
\pi\colon M\to B

be a Riemannian submersion,
that is,
\pi
is surjective and, for each
x\in M
, the restriction of
d\pi_{x}
to the horizontal subspace
\mathcal{H}_{x}

is an isometric isomorphism onto
T_{\pi(x)}B
.
Let
c\colon[0,1]\to B
be a (piecewise smooth) path.
For each
p\in F_{c(0)}
there exists a unique path
\overline{c_{p}}\colon[0,1]\to M

such that
\overline{c_{p}}^{\prime}(t)\in\mathcal{H}_{\overline{c_{p}}(t)}
for each
t\in[0,1]
,
\pi{\scriptstyle\circ}\overline{c_{p}}=c
and

\overline{c_{p}}(0)=p
. Such a path
\overline{c_{p}}
is called the
horizontal lift
of
c
starting from the point
p
.
The horizontal lift always exists globally by the completeness assumption of
(M,g)
(
[
8
]
).
We set

(7)

k(c)p:=\overline{c_{p}}(1).

This gives a map
k(c)\colon F_{c(0)}\to F_{c(1)}
.
Assume that each of the fiber
F_{b}
is a totally geodesic submanifold in
M
.
As is proved in
[
8
]
,
k(c)
is an isometry from
F_{c(0)}
onto
F_{c(1)}
.
Fix a base-point
b_{o}\in B
.
Since
B
is assumed to be connected, each fiber of
\pi
is isomorphic to
F=F_{b_{o}}
.
Set

(8)

\mathrm{Hol}(b_{o})=\{k(c)\mid\mbox{$c$ is a closed path with the base-point $b_{o}$}\}.

Since
k(c_{1})k(c_{2})=k(c_{2}c_{1})
with the concatenation
c_{2}c_{1}
of
c_{2}
followed by
c_{1}
,

\mathrm{Hol}(b_{o})
is a subgroup in the group of isometries,
\mathrm{Isom}(F)
, of

,
which is called the

at

.
Since

is assumed to be connected,
the group structure of the holonomy group does not depend on the choice of the base-point.

Now assume further that the horizontal distribution
\mathcal{H}
is integrable.
Then,
\mathcal{H}
defines a foliation on
M
.
For
p\in M
, let
L(p)
denote the leaf of
\mathcal{H}
through

.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
c_{0},c_{1}
be paths in
B
with
c_{0}(0)=c_{1}(0)=b_{o}
and
c_{0}(1)=c_{1}(1)=b
.
Suppose that

and

are homotopic relative to
\{0,1\}
. Then
k(c_{0})=k(c_{1})
.

Proof:

Let
\gamma_{s}
(

) be a piecewise smooth homotopy

\gamma_{s}(0)=b_{o}
,
\gamma_{s}(1)=b
,
\gamma_{0}=c_{0}
,
\gamma_{1}=c_{1}
.
Take

.
Since
\overline{(\gamma_{s})_{p}}(t)

.
Thus

. Since
\overline{(\gamma_{s})_{p}}(t)

\overline{(\gamma_{s})_{p}}(t)\in L(p)

, and hence

.
Thus we have

Hence

, which shows the lemma.

\blacksquare

From Lemma
2.1
, we have a surjective homomorphism

\mathfrak{h}\colon\pi_{1}(B,b_{o})\to\mathrm{Isom}(F),\quad\mathfrak{h}(\langle\,c\,\rangle)=k(c)^{-1}\quad(\langle\,c\,\rangle\in\pi_{1}(B,b_{o})),

,

For

is smooth on

.
Since each

\rho(h)

commutes with

\begin{split}\phi_{\lambda}(hx)&=\sum_{\mu\in\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits(\Delta_{F}),\,\mu\leq\lambda}P_{\mu}\phi(hx)=\sum_{\mu\in\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits(\Delta_{F}),\,\mu\leq\lambda}P_{\mu}\rho(h)\phi(x)\\

\blacksquare

L^{2}(F)

Lemma 2.7
.

The unitary operator

Proof of Theorem

:

3.
Spectral problem for the canonical variation

This section is devoted to a proof of Theorem
1.3
.
Theorem
1.3
follows from Theorem
1.1
and Sunada’s inequality (
[
20
]
)
with an observation concerning about the spectral gap

(19)

\mbox{{sg}}=\inf_{0\neq u\in\mathbf{1}^{\perp}\cap C^{\infty}(M)}\frac{\langle\,\Delta_{H}u,u\,\rangle_{L^{2}(M)}}{\|u\|_{L^{2}(M)}^{2}}

of the horizontal Laplacian, where
\mathbf{1}^{\perp}
is the orthogonal complement
in
L^{2}(M)
of the space of constant functions.
Since
d\mu_{(M,g_{t})}=t^{n-p}d\mu_{(M,g)}
,
where

, the

-inner product
given by

.

3.1.
Spectral gap of horizontal Laplacian

It is straightforward to see that
\mbox{{sg}}>0
if and only if zero is a simple eigenvalue
of
\Delta_{H}
and an isolated point in
\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits(\Delta_{H})
.
Therefore, according to
[
2
]
, 7.7 Proposition,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\Lambda_{1}(M,g_{t})=+\infty
if
\mbox{{sg}}>0
.
The following is a direct relationship between
\Lambda_{1}(M,g_{t})
(or
more precisely
\lambda_{1}(g_{t})
) and
sg
.
Lemma
3.1
below holds without the assumption on the integrability of
the horizontal distribution
\mathcal{H}
but the following proof uses the assumption on the totally
geodesic fibration.

Lemma 3.1
.

We have

\lim_{t\to\infty}\lambda_{1}(g_{t})=\mbox{{sg}}.

Proof:

Let
u_{t}\in C^{\infty}(M)
be an eigenfunction of
\Delta_{t}

with the first non-zero eigenvalue
\lambda(t):=\lambda_{1}(g_{t})
of
g_{t}

such that
\|u_{t}\|_{L^{2}(M)}=1
. In particular,
u_{t}\in\mathbf{1}^{\perp}
.
Since
\Delta_{t}=t^{-2}\Delta_{V}+\Delta_{H}=t^{-2}\Delta_{M}+(1-t^{-2})\Delta_{H}

(5.3 Proposition in
[
2
]
)
and
\Delta_{V}
is non-negative, we see

\lambda(t)=\langle\,\Delta_{t}u_{t},u_{t}\,\rangle_{L^{2}(M)}\geq\langle\,\Delta_{H}u_{t},u_{t}\,\rangle_{L^{2}(M)}\geq\mbox{{sg}}.

Hence
\displaystyle\liminf_{t\to\infty}\lambda(t)\geq\mbox{{sg}}
.
The mini-max theorem shows that

\lambda(t)=\inf_{0\neq u\in\mathbf{1}^{\perp}\cap C^{\infty}(M)}\frac{\langle\,\Delta_{t}u,u\,\rangle_{L^{2}(M)}}{\|u\|_{L^{2}(M)}^{2}}.

Take
u\in\mathbf{1}^{\perp}\cap C^{\infty}(M)
. Then,

\lambda(t)\|u\|_{L^{2}(M)}^{2}\leq\langle\,\Delta_{t}u,u\,\rangle_{L^{2}(M)}=t^{-2}\langle\,\Delta_{M}u,u\,\rangle_{L^{2}(M)}+(1-t^{-2})\langle\,\Delta_{H}u,u\,\rangle_{L^{2}(M)}.

Thus we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to\infty}\lambda(t)\leq\frac{\langle\,\Delta_{H}u,u\,\rangle_{L^{2}(M)}}{\|u\|_{L^{2}(M)}^{2}}
.
Taking the infimum in
u\in\mathbf{1}^{\perp}\cap C^{\infty}(M)
,
u\neq 0
,
we get
\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to\infty}\lambda(t)\leq\mbox{{sg}}
.
Thus we obtain

\mbox{{sg}}\leq\liminf_{t\to\infty}\lambda_{1}(t)\leq\limsup_{t\to\infty}\lambda_{1}(t)\leq\mbox{{sg}}.

Therefore,
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\lambda_{1}(t)
exists and equals
sg
.

\blacksquare

3.2.
Sunada’s estimate and proof of Theorem
1.3

According to Lemma
3.1
, to prove Theorem
1.3

it is enough to show that the spectral gap

of
\Delta_{H}

is positive under the assumption in Theorem
1.3
.
The representation
\rho\colon\mathrm{Hol}(b_{o})\to U(L^{2}(F))
is decomposed as

\rho={\bf 1}\oplus\rho_{o}
, where
{\bf 1}={\bf 1}_{\mathrm{Hol}(b_{o})}

is the one-dimensional trivial sub-representation of

on the space of constant functions
E(0)=\mathbb{C}\mathbf{1}
,
and
\rho_{o}
is the orthogonal complement of
{\bf 1}
.
In the statement of Theorem

,

is assumed to be ergodic.
Thus
\rho_{o}
has no non-zero invariant vector.
Both of the representations

,

define

,
\mathcal{E}_{\rho_{o}}

and the twisted Laplacians,

These operators are restrictions of

on
C^{\infty}(\mathcal{E}_{{\bf 1}})

C^{\infty}(\mathcal{E}_{\rho_{o}})

Note that
\Delta_{{\bf 1}}
is unitarily equivalent to the Laplacian
\Delta_{B}
on

.
Since

is assumed to be connected, zero is a simple eigenvalue of
\Delta_{{\bf 1}}

As our aim is to bound the bottom of the spectrum of

away from zero,
one of important quantities is the first non-zero eigenvalue

of
\Delta_{B}

Likewise, for the bundle
\mathcal{E}_{\rho_{o}}
,
an important quantity is the bottom of the spectrum of

(20)

\lambda_{0}(\rho_{o})=\inf_{0\neq s\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{E}_{\rho_{o}})}\frac{\langle\,\Delta_{\rho_{o}}s,s\,\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{E}_{\rho_{o}})}}{\|s\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{E}_{\rho_{o}})}^{2}}.

that

satisfies

Let us review the definition of Kazhdan’s property (T) following
[
6

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated discrete group.
For any finite generating set

\Gamma
and

\kappa(\Gamma,Q,\pi)=\inf_{\xi\in H,\,\|\xi\|_{H}=1}\max_{x\in Q}\|\pi(x)\xi-\xi\|_{H},

Define a quantity

\kappa(\Gamma,Q)=\inf_{\pi}\kappa(\Gamma,Q,\pi),

Now, Sunada’s inequality

asserts that there exists a positive constant

Therefore, applying

Therefore, to prove Theorem

, it is enough to show the following.

Lemma 3.2

We have

\mbox{{sg}}=\min\{\lambda_{1}(B),\lambda_{0}(\rho_{o})\}.

\Delta_{\mathbf{1}}WPf\neq 0

\begin{split}\langle\,\Delta_{\rho}\phi,\phi\,\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{E}_{\rho})}&=\langle\,\Delta_{\mathbf{1}}WPf,WPf\,\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{1}})}+\langle\,\Delta_{\rho_{o}}\phi_{0},\phi_{0}\,\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{E}_{o})}\\
&\geq\|WPf\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{1}})}^{2}\lambda_{1}(B)+\|\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{E}_{\rho_{o}})}^{2}\lambda_{0}(\rho_{o})\\
&\geq(\|WPf\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{1}})}^{2}+\|\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{E}_{\rho_{o}})}^{2})\min\{\lambda_{1}(B),\lambda_{0}(\rho_{o})\}\\
&=\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{E}_{\rho})}^{2}\min\{\lambda_{1}(B),\lambda_{0}(\rho_{o})\}.\end{split}

Since this holds for any
\phi\in(W\mathbf{1})^{\perp}\cap C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{s}}(\mathcal{E}_{\rho})
,
we have
\mbox{{sg}}\geq\min\{\lambda_{1}(B),\lambda_{0}(\rho_{o})\}

This concludes the assertion.

\blacksquare

4.
Spectral coincidence for
\Delta_{H}

This final section is devoted to a proof of Theorem

To prove Theorem
1.4

\Delta_{B_{H}}
on
B_{H}

and the twisted Laplacian
\Delta_{\rho}
acting on

In the following, the unit element in
\mathrm{Hol}(b_{o})
is denoted by

4.1.
Periodification

For any
\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{H})
and
f\in L^{2}(F)
, define
the map
T(\psi,f)\colon B_{H}\to L^{2}(F)
by

T(\psi,f)(x)=\sum_{h\in\mathrm{Hol}(b_{o})}(h^{-1}\psi)(x)\rho(h)^{-1}f\quad(x\in B_{H}),

where, for
\psi\in C^{\infty}(B_{H})
,
h\in\mathrm{Hol}(b_{o})
and
x\in B_{H}
, we set
(h^{-1}\psi)(x)=\psi(hx)
.
Since
\mathrm{Hol}(b_{o})
acts on
B_{H}
properly discontinuously and since
{\operatorname{supp\,}}\psi
is compact,
the sum in the right-hand side is locally finite.
Since
T(\psi,f)
is
\rho
-equivariant,

T(\psi,f)
defines a smooth section of
\mathcal{E}_{\rho}
, which we tentatively write
s(\psi,f)
.
The
\rho
-equivariant map from
B_{H}
to
L^{2}(F)
corresponding to the section
\Delta_{\rho}s(\psi,f)

is
\Delta_{B_{H}}T(\psi,f)
. Since the sum defining
T(\psi,f)
is locally finite, we see that

\Delta_{B_{H}}T(\psi,f)=T(\Delta_{B_{H}}\psi,f)
. Thus, identifying
s(\psi,f)
and
T(\psi,f)
, we can write as

(24)

\Delta_{\rho}T(\psi,f)=T(\Delta_{B_{H}}\psi,f).

Lemma 4.1
.

For any
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{H})
and
f_{1},f_{2}\in L^{2}(F)
, we have

(25)

\langle\,T(\psi_{1},f_{1}),T(\psi_{2},f_{2})\,\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{E}_{\rho})}=\sum_{h\in\mathrm{Hol}(b_{o})}\langle\,\rho(h)f_{1},f_{2}\,\rangle_{L^{2}(F)}\langle\,h\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\,\rangle_{L^{2}(B_{H})}.

Proof:

Let
D\subset B_{H}
be a fundamental domain of
\varpi_{H}\colon B_{H}\to B
.
By definition, we have

\langle\,T(\psi_{1},f_{1}),T(\psi_{2},f_{2})\,\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{E}_{\rho})}=\int_{D}\langle\,T(\psi_{1},f_{1})(x),T(\psi_{2},f_{2})(x)\,\rangle_{L^{2}(F)}\,d\mu_{H}(x)

The integrand of the integral in the right-hand side is computed as

\langle\,T(\psi_{1},f_{1})(x),T(\psi_{2},f_{2})(x)\,\rangle_{L^{2}(F)}=\sum_{h,h^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Hol}(b_{o})}\psi_{1}(hx)\overline{\psi_{2}(h^{\prime}x)}\langle\,\rho(h)^{-1}f_{1},\rho(h^{\prime})^{-1}f_{2}\,\rangle_{L^{2}(F)}.

Using the fact that the covering transformation group
\mathrm{Hol}(b_{o})
acts isometrically on
B_{H}
,
we compute the inner product
\langle\,T(\psi_{1},f_{1}),T(\psi_{2},f_{2})\,\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{E}_{\rho})}
further as

\begin{split}\langle\,T(\psi_{1},f_{1}),T(\psi_{2},f_{2})\,\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{E}_{\rho})}&=\sum_{h,h^{\prime}}\langle\,\rho(h^{\prime}h^{-1})f_{1},f_{2}\,\rangle_{L^{2}(F)}\int_{D}\psi_{1}(hx)\overline{\psi_{2}(h^{\prime}x)}\,d\mu_{B_{H}}(x)\\
&=\sum_{h,h^{\prime}}\langle\,\rho(h^{\prime}h^{-1})f_{1},f_{2}\,\rangle_{L^{2}(F)}\int_{h^{\prime}D}\psi_{1}(hh^{\prime-1}y)\overline{\psi_{2}(y)}\,d\mu_{B_{H}}(y)\\
&=\sum_{g,h^{\prime}}\langle\,\rho(g)f_{1},f_{2}\,\rangle_{L^{2}(F)}\int_{h^{\prime}D}\psi_{1}(g^{-1}y)\overline{\psi_{2}(y)}\,d\mu_{B_{H}}(y)\\
&=\sum_{g}\langle\,\rho(g)f_{1},f_{2}\,\rangle_{L^{2}(F)}\int_{\bigcup_{h^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Hol}(b_{o})}h^{\prime}D}\psi_{1}(g^{-1}y)\overline{\psi_{2}(y)}\,d\mu_{B_{H}}(y)\\
&=\sum_{g}\langle\,\rho(g)f_{1},f_{2}\,\rangle_{L^{2}(F)}\int_{B_{H}}\psi_{1}(g^{-1}y)\overline{\psi_{2}(y)}\,d\mu_{B_{H}}(y),\\
\end{split}

which shows the lemma.

\blacksquare

The next lemma will be used later to handle the terms

with

in the sum
(
25

to compute the norm-square

Lemma 4.2

Let

\blacksquare

Proposition 4.3
.

We have

Proof:

Let

Proof of Theorem
1.4
:

We have proved the assertion that
\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits(\Delta_{B_{H}})\subset\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits(\Delta_{H})
.
Polymerakis shows in
[
17
]
that the Laplacian on the Riemannian covering over a compact Riemannian
manifold with infinite covering transformation group does not have an eigenvalue with finite multiplicity.
Hence Proposition
4.4
gives

\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits(\Delta_{B_{H}})=\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits_{\mathrm{ess}}(\Delta_{B_{H}})\subset\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits_{\mathrm{ess}}(\Delta_{\rho})=\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits_{\mathrm{ess}}(\Delta_{H}).

Suppose that
\mathrm{Hol}(b_{o})
is amenable. Then by Sunada’ theorem (
[
20
]
, Corollary to Theorem 2)

\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits(\Delta_{H})=\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits(\Delta_{\rho})\subset\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits(\Delta_{B_{H}})
. Hence we have
the equality
\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits(\Delta_{H})=\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits(\Delta_{B_{H}})
. Furthermore, if
\mathrm{Hol}(b_{o})
is infinite and amenable,
we have

\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits_{\mathrm{ess}}(\Delta_{B_{H}})\subset\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits_{\mathrm{ess}}(\Delta_{H})\subset\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits(\Delta_{H})\subset\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits(\Delta_{B_{H}})=\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits_{\mathrm{ess}}(\Delta_{B_{H}}),

from which the equality holds. Therefore, we conclude Theorem
1.4
.

\blacksquare
