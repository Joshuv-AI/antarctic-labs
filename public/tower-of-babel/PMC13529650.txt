Meaningful ciphertext encryption for color image via 2D-CICCM and stairwise compression
Authors: Shiwei Jing, Jianjun Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
With the widespread dissemination of digital media, the security of image data has attracted increasing attention. However, existing colour image meaningful ciphertext encryption schemes exhibit low ciphertext quality and poor reconstructed image quality. To address these issues, this paper proposes a colour image encryption scheme for meaningful ciphertext based on a two-dimensional coupled infinite collapsing chaotic mapping (2D-CICCM) and stairwise compression. First, the plaintext image is processed using stairwise compression followed by bit-level permutation to generate a noise-like ciphertext. The generated ciphertext is then embedded into a cover image to produce a meaningful ciphertext image. Unlike conventional compressive sensing methods, which usually neglect inter-channel redundancy in colour images, the proposed stairwise compression jointly exploits both spatial and inter-channel redundancies, thereby significantly improving the reconstruction quality of the decrypted image. Considering the Gaussian-like distribution of measurement values and the characteristics of GLSB-based embedding with correction, a histogram rearrangement and embedding-value adjustment strategy is further designed. This strategy minimises the distortion introduced to the cover image under given embedding constraints, leading to optimal meaningful ciphertext image quality. The pseudo-random sequences required in the encryption process are generated by the 2D-CICCM, which exhibits uniformly distributed attractors, relatively large Lyapunov exponents, and strong randomness, thus satisfying the requirements of cryptographic pseudo-random number generators. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed scheme achieves favourable performance in terms of ciphertext quality, decryption accuracy, and security, validating its feasibility and effectiveness. The code is available at https://github.com/jsw1995/MCCIE_2D-CICCM.

Introduction

With the continuous advancement of society and technology, individuals’ demand for collecting, sharing, and storing information has grown dramatically, while the convenience of related operations has also significantly improved. However, this also creates more opportunities for criminals to obtain private data, prompting extensive research on information security by many scholars. Due to their high information density and intuitive nature, images are widely used, and therefore their security is of particular importance. Common image encryption techniques include optical image encryption1,2, quantum image encryption3,4, digital permutation–diffusion encryption5,6, DNA coding7,8, RNA-based operations9,10, bit-plane encryption11,12, among others.

These methods convert the plaintext image into a noise-like ciphertext, effectively concealing the visual information. However, the noise characteristics make such ciphertext easier to detect and potentially intercept during transmission. To mitigate this problem, Bao13 introduced meaningful ciphertext encryption, wherein encrypted data are embedded into a cover image, making the ciphertext visually indistinguishable from the cover and thus significantly reducing the likelihood of being detected. Depending on the embedding domain, meaningful ciphertext encryption can be categorized into transform-domain and spatial-domain methods. Transform-domain embedding is commonly implemented in the wavelet transform domain14,15, discrete cosine transform domain16,17, fractional Fourier domain18, skew transform domain19, and U-transform domain20. Among these, integer wavelet transform-based methods are widely adopted due to their reversibility, as no quantization error is introduced during embedding. Typical spatial-domain schemes include least significant bit (LSB) embedding21,22, LSB+\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$2^K$$\end{document} correction15,23, and matrix coding24,25. Although spatial-domain methods generally produce ciphertext with higher visual quality than transform-domain methods, the embedding process is independent of the noise-like ciphertext generated through conventional encryption and fails to exploit the known statistical distribution of the ciphertext, limiting further improvements in visual quality26. To address this, Jing27,28 introduced histogram shifting to enhance ciphertext quality, achieving quality far exceeding the theoretical bounds of LSB+\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$2^K$$\end{document} correction.

Image ciphertexts are typically large in size, and meaningful ciphertext encryption further increases the ciphertext volume relative to noise-like encryption. Compressive sensing (CS) not only reduces plaintext data volume for efficient transmission but also inherently satisfies the confusion and diffusion principles required in cryptography29, leading to its widespread use in image encryption. Conventional CS-based compression typically involves three stages: sparsification, scrambling to uniformly distribute sparse coefficients, and measurement sampling. Common sparsification operators include wavelet transforms30–32, discrete cosine transform20, and singular value decomposition33. CS variants also include block-wise compression34–36, one-dimensional compression30,32, two-dimensional compression37,38, and parallel compression for acceleration25,39. Many optimization strategies have been proposed to further improve reconstruction fidelity, such as adaptive sparse thresholding to select suitable thresholds for different images28, adaptive sampling rate assignment for image blocks40, adaptive sparse basis selection33, and optimized measurement matrices41,42. However, most CS-based methods are designed for grayscale images; color images are typically treated as three independent grayscale channels without exploiting inter-channel correlations, resulting in suboptimal reconstruction quality.

Chaotic systems exhibit extreme sensitivity to initial conditions and control parameters, and their generated sequences possess ergodicity and pseudo-randomness. When used to produce random control sequences for image encryption, chaos-based mechanisms not only enhance security but also reduce key size, enabling efficient key distribution while improving key sensitivity43. As a result, a large number of chaotic systems have been adopted for image encryption. One-dimensional chaotic maps such as logistic and sine maps offer structural simplicity and fast computation but their low-dimensional trajectories and limited variables make it easier to deduce initial states44, compromising security. To enhance unpredictability while retaining structural simplicity, some researchers have constructed two-dimensional hyperchaotic systems by cascading or coupling one-dimensional chaotic maps. For example,45 formed a two-dimensional hyperchaos by multiplicatively coupling two infinite-collapse chaotic maps, while28 coupled infinite-collapse and logistic maps under a square-root structure. Multiplicative coupling schemes require carefully designed combinations of low-dimensional maps to ensure value-range constraints. Other works46–48 use additive coupling between low-dimensional maps or between a chaotic map and a simple function, with trigonometric functions applied for range control; however, trigonometric functions often lead to non-uniform value distributions. Similar studies49–51 adopt additive coupling and modular operations, while52 employs logarithmic functions to increase complexity. Additional efforts46,50 introduce non-cyclic infinite operations to further strengthen chaotic behavior.

Based on the above discussion and analysis, this study proposes a color image encryption scheme with meaningful ciphertext, which achieves visually secure ciphertext transmission by integrating the 2D-CICCM with a stairwise compression mechanism. Two-dimensional coupled infinite collapsing chaotic mapping

Traditional one-dimensional discrete chaotic maps have simple structural forms but their trajectories can be easily estimated and predicted using modern estimation techniques. In contrast, high-dimensional continuous hyperchaotic systems possess stronger chaotic characteristics but incur significantly higher computational cost27. This paper proposes a novel 2D-CICCM, which is constructed based on simple one-dimensional chaos and achieves two-dimensional hyperchaos through cascading coupling. As a result, it retains a simple structure while exhibiting desirable chaotic performance.

Lyapunov exponents

The Lyapunov exponent (LE) is a key indicator for determining whether a nonlinear system is in a periodic, chaotic, or hyperchaotic state. The number of LEs corresponds to the system dimension. A system with one or more positive LEs is classified as chaotic, while a system with multiple positive LEs is considered hyperchaotic. Furthermore, a larger LE generally implies greater chaotic sensitivity and unpredictability. Complexity analysis

Sample entropy (SE) is a nonlinear dynamic metric used to assess the complexity of a time series. A higher sample entropy indicates a greater likelihood of generating new patterns within the sequence, reflecting stronger sequence complexity. Permutation entropy (PE) evaluates the randomness of a dynamic system by analysing the ordinal patterns of the time series and constructing the corresponding probability distribution. PE values closer to 1 imply higher randomness of the underlying system. Figures 4 and 5 present the SE and PE results of the compared chaotic sequences. Three-channel stairwise compressed sensing

In existing visually meaningful ciphertext image encryption schemes, compressive sensing is predominantly applied to grayscale images, while colour images are usually processed by treating the three RGB channels as independent grayscale images. In such approaches, each channel is sparsified using a two-dimensional discrete cosine transform (2D-DCT) independently. This channel-wise strategy ignores the strong inter-channel correlation inherent in colour images, which results in inefficient utilisation of sampling resources and consequently limits reconstruction quality under a fixed sampling rate. To address this limitation, we propose a three-channel stairwise compressed sensing framework that jointly exploits spatial and inter-channel correlations. The proposed framework first performs joint sparsification in a three-dimensional transform domain using a 3D-DCT, followed by stairwise sampling with channel-dependent sampling ratios. By allocating sampling resources according to the information content of each channel, the reconstruction performance can be significantly improved. Ablation study

In this section, the effectiveness of the proposed 3D-DCT sparsification and stairwise sampling strategy is mainly evaluated. Two sampling schemes are selected as baselines. The first scheme consists of three steps: sparsification, permutation, and sampling28. The permutation step is designed to uniformly distribute sparse energy over the entire sparse matrix, thereby avoiding excessive concentration of low-frequency components in certain rows. Under the same sampling rate, this strategy reduces information loss and improves the quality of the reconstructed image. The second scheme follows a two-step process, including sparsification and sampling27. In this case, the sampling matrix assigns larger weights to the upper-left region of the sparse coefficient matrix, which preserves more low-frequency information. This design enables fast reconstruction while maintaining high reconstruction quality. To verify the effectiveness of the 3D sparsification and the stairwise sampling strategy, three experiments are conducted. In the first experiment, 3D sparsification is introduced into the first sampling scheme. In the second experiment, YCrCb colour space transformation and stairwise sampling are incorporated into the second sampling scheme. This experiment mainly serves as a supplementary validation of the effectiveness of 3D sparsification. Specifically, the luminance channel Y is treated as the primary channel with a higher sampling rate, while the chrominance channels Cr and Cb are treated as secondary channels with relatively lower sampling rates. The sampling ratios are optimally selected according to Sect. "Three-channel sampling rate", resulting in a ratio of (0.6533, 0.1733, 0.1733). This allocation principle is consistent with that of JPEG compression, as the human visual system is highly sensitive to variations in luminance Y but less sensitive to subtle changes in chrominance Cb and Cr, thereby motivating the use of stairwise sampling. The third experiment corresponds to the proposed method, in which both 3D sparsification and the stairwise sampling strategy are applied to the second sampling scheme. Furthermore, the correlation distribution of the decrypted image is almost identical to that of the plaintext, with the scatter points once again tightly clustered along the diagonal, demonstrating that the decryption process accurately restores the structural relationships and pixel dependencies of the original image, further validating the effective preservation of reconstruction quality from a statistical perspective.

Discussion

The above comparisons indicate that the proposed scheme achieves a well-balanced performance in terms of ciphertext imperceptibility, reconstruction quality, and computational efficiency. Benefiting from histogram shifting and embedding value adjustment, the generated ciphertext introduces minimal distortion to the cover image, resulting in superior PSNR and SSIM and ensuring strong visual imperceptibility. Moreover, the integration of 3D-DCT sparsification with stairwise compression effectively exploits inter-channel redundancy in color images, enabling more informative measurements to be preserved under the same sampling rate. This leads to consistently improved reconstruction quality compared with existing methods. In terms of efficiency, the proposed method avoids excessive iterative reconstruction by prioritizing low-frequency components and allowing early termination, yielding competitive encryption and decryption speed without relying on complex learning models.

Conclusion

This paper proposes a novel meaningful ciphertext encryption scheme. The proposed framework first employs a two-dimensional CICCM hyperchaotic system to generate control sequences for encryption, followed by stairwise compressive sensing applied to the plaintext image, and finally embeds the resulting ciphertext into a cover image. Compared with conventional two-dimensional chaotic systems, the proposed CICCM system exhibits superior chaotic characteristics, particularly in terms of the maximum Lyapunov exponent and sequence distribution, demonstrating its suitability for secure encryption applications. During the compression stage, a 3D-DCT is adopted to exploit the energy attenuation characteristics across RGB channels. Furthermore, a stairwise sampling strategy is introduced, where low-frequency components are assigned higher sampling rates. This strategy simultaneously reduces spatial redundancy and inter-channel redundancy, thereby improving the reconstruction quality of decrypted images. In addition, a histogram reorganisation and embedded-value adjustment strategy is designed to adapt to the approximately Gaussian distribution of compressive sensing measurements. This approach effectively reduces distortion to the cover image and improves ciphertext visual quality. Comparative experimental results demonstrate that the proposed scheme achieves high-quality ciphertext generation, superior reconstruction performance, fast encryption speed, and strong security robustness.
