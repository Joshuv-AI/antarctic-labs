ViewAdapt-Det: view-adaptive detection for soccer broadcasting videos
Authors: Chunwang Zhu, Yongli Zhang, Sheng Gao, Chenghao Wang, Chunyan Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObject detection in soccer videos plays an important role in intelligent broadcasting, tactical analysis, and player tracking. Frequent view switching driven by multi-camera coordination in soccer broadcasts introduces two distinct challenges for existing detection methods. First, in the temporal dimension, shot transitions cause abrupt visual changes between adjacent frames. Existing temporal aggregation methods forcibly fuse features from heterogeneous views, injecting cross-view noise that can degrade detection accuracy below single-frame baselines. Second, in the feature dimension, target appearance varies significantly across views: players in panoramic shots are low-resolution small objects characterized mainly by coarse contours and color, whereas the same players in close-up shots become high-resolution large objects with rich texture and fine-grained detail. A unified detection strategy struggles to handle both extremes simultaneously. We propose ViewAdapt-Det, a framework consisting of two complementary modules. The View-Aware Temporal Gating module (VATG) detects shot transitions and dynamically controls temporal aggregation intensity, suppressing historical frame contributions during view changes while preserving temporal enhancement during continuous sequences. The View-Conditioned Detection Modulation module (VCDM) infers the current view type and conditionally modulates detection features, allowing the detector to apply view-specific feature processing strategies that match the visual characteristics of each viewpoint. Experiments on SoccerNet-Tracking, SportsMOT, and our self-constructed BroadcastSwitch-Soccer dataset show that ViewAdapt-Det outperforms existing methods and demonstrates stronger robustness under abrupt shot transitions.Keywords: Object detection, Sports video analysis, Deep learning, Computer visionSubject terms: Computational biology and bioinformatics, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Object detection in soccer videos is a fundamental task in sports video intelligence, providing essential support for tactical review, player performance evaluation, automatic highlight generation, and real-time broadcasting assistance. A soccer pitch covers more than 7,000 square meters, far exceeding the playing area of indoor sports such as basketball and volleyball. Broadcasting such events requires coordinated multi-camera setups, with the director frequently switching among panoramic, medium, close-up, and replay views to capture different aspects of the match. This complex multi-view broadcasting paradigm makes soccer broadcast footage visually distinct from general video and imposes unique requirements on detection methods.

Existing sports-scene detection methods primarily address intra-frame visual degradation such as motion blur, similar appearance among teammates, and small object size, while overlooking the challenges introduced by frequent multi-view switching in soccer broadcasts. As illustrated in Fig. 1, these challenges manifest along two dimensions. In the temporal dimension, shot transitions cause abrupt changes in visual content between adjacent frames. For example, the view may jump from a full-pitch overview to a facial close-up, leaving virtually no visual correlation between consecutive frames. Existing video detection methods aggregate neighboring-frame features to enhance the current frame representation, implicitly assuming inter-frame visual continuity. Shot transitions directly violate this assumption; forced aggregation injects irrelevant features from a different viewpoint into the current frame, degrading detection accuracy to levels even below single-frame baselines. In the feature dimension, target appearance varies substantially across views. In panoramic shots, players occupy only tens of pixels and appear as low-resolution small objects whose features are dominated by coarse contours and color. In close-up shots, the same player may fill a large portion of the frame as a high-resolution object with rich texture, facial, and jersey-number details. These two types of features occupy very different regions in feature space, yet existing detectors apply a single detection head to all views, forcing the classifier to fit vastly different feature distributions simultaneously, which blurs decision boundaries and limits detection performance.

Core challenges of object detection in soccer broadcast videos. (a) Shot transitions produce entirely different visual content in adjacent frames; temporal aggregation methods introduce cross-view noise at transition frames, degrading detection performance below the single-frame baseline. (b) Target visual characteristics differ drastically across views: players in panoramic views are low-resolution small objects dominated by coarse contours and color, while players in close-up views are high-resolution large objects with rich texture and detail. A unified detection strategy cannot accommodate both feature distributions simultaneously.

To address these issues, we propose ViewAdapt-Det (View-Adaptive Detection), a framework that introduces view-aware adaptive mechanisms along both the temporal aggregation and feature representation dimensions, enabling the detector to perceive and adapt to multi-view changes in soccer broadcasts. ViewAdapt-Det contains two core modules. The first is the View-Aware Temporal Gating module (VATG), designed to solve the temporal aggregation failure caused by shot transitions. VATG models inter-frame view consistency as a continuous gating signal by jointly considering global semantic differences and local structural changes between the current frame and historical frames to estimate the degree of view transition. This signal is embedded as a soft gating factor into the temporal attention mechanism, performing channel-wise weighted modulation on the aggregation contribution of historical frame features. As a result, temporal complementary information is fully exploited when views are continuous, while cross-view noise propagation is automatically attenuated when views change abruptly. The second module is the View-Conditioned Detection Modulation module (VCDM), designed to address the inability of a unified detection strategy to accommodate multi-view feature differences. VCDM uses a view encoder to perceive the view type of the current frame and generates view-conditioned attention masks that selectively enhance detection features at both the spatial and semantic levels. Under panoramic views, the masks guide the detector to broadly attend to densely distributed small-object regions across the feature map; under close-up views, the masks guide the detector to focus on fine-grained feature regions of local large objects. Through this view-driven masking mechanism, the detector adaptively adjusts its feature attention pattern under different views without maintaining separate detection branches for each view type. Experiments on SoccerNet-Tracking, SportsMOT, and our self-constructed BroadcastSwitch-Soccer dataset demonstrate that ViewAdapt-Det achieves superior detection performance and exhibits stronger robustness in abrupt shot transition scenarios.

Related work

Object detection in sports scenes

Object detection in sports scenes has attracted growing attention in recent years. Early efforts directly applied general-purpose detectors to sports videos. Faster R-CNN1 and the YOLO series2 were adopted for player and ball detection, yet their performance remained limited due to challenges specific to sports footage, including motion blur, dense occlusion, and similar appearance among teammates. The establishment of large-scale sports video benchmarks such as SoccerNet3 and SportsMOT4 has spurred the development of specialized detection methods. Seweryn et al.5 employ super-resolution techniques to improve the detection accuracy of distant small targets in soccer broadcasts. Golovkin et al.6 combine detection and re-identification for end-to-end game state reconstruction in the SoccerNet-GSR task. On the architecture side, FootAndBall7 designs a joint player-ball detection network with dedicated optimization for the extremely small scale of the ball in soccer scenes. SoccerSynth-Detection8 leverages synthetic data augmentation to improve model robustness under diverse lighting and viewpoint conditions. More recently, DeCon-Net9 proposes a decoupled contrastive learning strategy to enhance individual discriminability against feature collapse among same-team players. However, all the above methods focus on intra-frame visual degradation (e.g., blur, occlusion, small objects) while overlooking the scene-level impact of frequent multi-view switching in soccer broadcasts on detection performance. This paper re-examines soccer broadcast video detection from the perspective of view changes, revealing how shot transitions and multi-view feature discrepancies constrain existing methods, and proposes targeted solutions.

Video object detection and temporal feature aggregation

Video object detection aims to leverage inter-frame temporal information to improve single-frame detection performance. Early methods focus on feature propagation and neighboring-frame alignment. Deep Feature Flow10 reduces redundant computation through keyframe feature propagation, while FGFA11 enhances moving-object detection via optical-flow-guided neighboring-frame feature aggregation. Subsequent research shifted from local alignment to long-range relation modeling. SELSA12 performs feature aggregation in a sequence-level semantic space, and MEGA13 combines global and local memory modules to jointly exploit long- and short-range temporal information. With the advent of Transformers, TransVOD 14 incorporates spatiotemporal interaction into a unified end-to-end framework. QueryProp15 efficiently propagates detection information across frames through an object query propagation mechanism. PTSEFormer16 progressively introduces temporal and spatial information to enhance DETR detection performance. FAQ17 proposes a feature aggregated query strategy to improve query quality in Transformer-based video detectors. More recently, MaskVD18 and Beyond Boxes19 advance the efficiency-accuracy trade-off in video detection from the perspectives of region-mask efficient computation and mask-guided spatiotemporal fusion, respectively. Temporal modeling has also been explored in infrared small target detection. Li et al.20 propose a direction-coded temporal U-shape module for multiframe infrared small target detection, where motion-to-data mapping and direction-coded convolution are used to encode target motion direction and suppress clutter-induced false alarms. Although these methods significantly improve temporal utilization, they all implicitly assume inter-frame visual continuity, i.e., that visual content changes smoothly between adjacent frames. This assumption is frequently violated in soccer broadcasts, where shot transitions produce entirely different visual content in consecutive frames, and forced aggregation instead injects noise. The VATG module proposed in this paper explicitly handles view discontinuity in temporal aggregation through view change detection and an adaptive gating mechanism, enabling the detector to dynamically control aggregation intensity based on inter-frame view consistency.

Conditional feature generation and adaptive detection

Conditional feature modulation aims to dynamically adjust the feature processing behavior of a network according to external signals. FiLM21 introduces feature-wise linear modulation as a general conditioning layer, performing affine transformations to enable conditional inputs to dynamically modulate network features. In object detection, Dynamic Head22 unifies detection head design through scale-aware, spatial-aware, and task-aware attention mechanisms, achieving input-adaptive feature processing. Conditional DETR23 introduces a conditional cross-attention mechanism that endows queries with stronger positional awareness. CondInst24 achieves conditioned instance segmentation by dynamically generating instance-level convolution kernels. On the adaptive attention front, the Dynamic Neural Networks survey25 systematically summarizes the application of dynamic convolution, adaptive routing, and related techniques across various tasks. Recent studies have further explored dynamic convolution for discriminative small-target representation learning. For example, DHiF26 introduces dynamic high-frequency convolution for single-frame infrared small target detection, where a dynamically generated local filter bank explicitly models high-frequency components and captures distinctive grayscale variation patterns of tiny targets and clutter regions. More recently, spatially-adaptive feature modulation27 demonstrates the effectiveness of dynamically adjusting feature responses based on local content in image super-resolution. AdaMAE28 proposes an adaptive masking strategy that dynamically allocates attention resources according to input content. In detection, adaptive masked distillation29 uses attention masks from a teacher model to guide the student model toward key regions. However, existing conditional modulation methods primarily use target category, instance identity, or image content as the conditioning signal; no prior work has introduced view type as a conditioning signal for detection feature modulation. The VCDM module proposed in this paper generates attention masks conditioned on view representations, guiding the detector to adaptively adjust its feature attention pattern under different views, extending conditional feature modulation to the new setting of view-adaptive detection. Recent advances in general detection and adaptive feature learning further motivate the need for robust view-adaptive representations. Recent DETR-family detectors, such as DINO and Co-DETR, improve query denoising and collaborative assignment, providing stronger single-frame detection baselines. Beyond visual detection, adaptive feature learning has also been explored in signal identification: Duan et al.30 introduce multi-channel complex residual networks with adaptive sphere-space decision boundaries for RF fingerprint interpretation, while Zhang et al.31 use diffusion-model-based augmentation to improve robustness under limited data. Although these works target different modalities, they are methodologically related to our goal of improving robustness under distribution variation through adaptive representation learning.

Architecture overview

The overall architecture of ViewAdapt-Det is shown in Fig. 2. Given a consecutive frame sequence , the framework consists of the following components: (1) a weight-shared CNN backbone that extracts multi-scale features from each frame; (2) the View-Aware Temporal Gating module (VATG), which evaluates inter-frame view consistency and dynamically controls temporal aggregation intensity, fully exploiting historical frame information when views are continuous while suppressing cross-view noise during shot transitions; (3) the View-Conditioned Detection Modulation module (VCDM), which perceives the view type of the current frame and generates view-conditioned attention masks to selectively enhance detection features; (4) a detection head that predicts target categories and bounding boxes based on the modulated features. The two core modules introduce view-aware mechanisms along the temporal aggregation and feature representation dimensions respectively, jointly improving detector robustness under multi-view broadcasting scenarios. View-aware temporal gating module (VATG)

Video object detection methods enhance the current frame representation by aggregating neighboring-frame features, implicitly assuming inter-frame visual continuity. However, frequent shot transitions in soccer broadcasts directly violate this assumption: when switching from a panoramic view to a close-up, the visual content of consecutive frames is entirely different, and forced aggregation injects irrelevant features from a different viewpoint into the current frame. The core idea of VATG is to model inter-frame view consistency as a continuous gating signal, fully exploiting temporal information when views are continuous while automatically suppressing historical frame contributions during shot transitions. This module contains three sub-modules: the view change detector, gated temporal attention, and adaptive aggregation fusion.

Different from standard temporal attention, which computes attention weights mainly from feature similarity and therefore performs generic content-aware reweighting, VATG explicitly estimates inter-frame view consistency before temporal aggregation. Standard attention cannot reliably distinguish useful similarity within the same view from coincidental similarity across different camera shots. VATG differs in three aspects: (1) it introduces a dedicated View Change Detector that combines global semantic difference and local structural change; (2) the resulting gating signal is multiplied with historical-frame attention scores before softmax normalization, allowing the contribution of frame t-k to be suppressed to near zero during shot transitions; and (3) it further uses the frame-level gate to reduce residual cross-view noise near transition boundaries.

View-Conditioned Detection Modulation Module (VCDM)

VATG addresses view discontinuity in the temporal dimension, but the visual characteristic differences of targets across views remain unhandled. In panoramic views, players are low-resolution small objects and the detector needs to broadly attend to densely distributed weak-response regions across the feature map. In close-up views, players are high-resolution large objects and the detector needs to focus on fine-grained texture features in local regions. The core idea of VCDM is to perceive the view type of the current frame and generate view-conditioned attention masks that selectively enhance detection features at both spatial and semantic levels, enabling the detector to adaptively adjust its feature attention pattern under different views. Different from generic conditional modulation methods such as FiLM, Dynamic Head, and CondInst, which condition feature processing on category, task, instance, or generic image content, VCDM uses view type as the conditioning signal. View type is particularly important in soccer broadcasts because it determines target scale distribution, spatial layout, and the effective feature channels needed for detection. Moreover, VCDM generates two complementary masks: a spatial mask that adapts where the detector should attend and a semantic mask that adapts which channels should be enhanced. The view query bank with soft selection enables continuous interpolation among view-conditioned processing strategies, making the modulation more suitable for transitional views than a fixed set of discrete branches.

Detection head

ViewAdapt-Det adopts the detection head of RT-DETR, which consists of a 6-layer Transformer decoder with 300 learnable object queries. Each query interacts with the modulated features through cross-attention and predicts a target category and bounding box. Label assignment follows the Hungarian matching strategy, where each ground-truth target is matched to the query with the lowest matching cost. The matching cost and training loss both include three terms: classification loss (focal loss), L1 regression loss, and GIoU loss.

Experiments

Datasets

We evaluate ViewAdapt-Det on two public datasets and one self-constructed soccer broadcast dataset.

SoccerNet-Tracking. SoccerNet-Tracking is currently the largest soccer video multi-object tracking benchmark. It contains 200 video clips of 30 seconds each, sourced from the main broadcast camera of 12 professional soccer matches, plus one complete 45-minute half for long-term tracking evaluation. The video resolution is 1920×1080 (Full HD) at 25 FPS, with each clip containing 750 frames, totaling approximately 150,000 frames and over 3.6 million bounding box annotations. All visible targets on the pitch are densely annotated, including players from both teams, goalkeepers, referees, assistant referees, and the ball, each assigned a unique tracking ID following the MOT20 format. The dataset is officially split into training (57 sequences), test (49 sequences), and challenge (58 sequences, with 37 reserved for future challenges) sets. Notably, this dataset consists primarily of single-camera continuous clips from the main broadcast view and does not contain abrupt camera switches or replay segments, making it more suitable for evaluating detection under relatively continuous viewpoints. Additionally, the highly similar appearance of same-team players, severe occlusions during tackles and corner kicks, and substantial camera motion from continuous panning and zooming collectively pose multi-dimensional detection challenges. We use the official training and test sets for model training and evaluation.

SportsMOT. SportsMOT is a large-scale multi-sport multi-object tracking dataset containing 240 video sequences with over 150,000 frames (approximately 15× MOT17), over 1.6 million bounding box annotations (approximately 3× MOT17), and about 3,401 tracking trajectories. The dataset covers three sports: basketball, volleyball, and soccer, with 80 sequences per sport. The video resolution is 1280×720 (720p) at 25 FPS, split into training/validation/test sets at a 45/45/150 ratio. This paper focuses on the soccer subset (80 sequences), which is the most challenging among the three sports: an average of 20.5 trajectories per sequence (vs. 10 for basketball and 12 for volleyball), 12.8 targets per frame (highest density), and an average trajectory gap length of 116.1 frames (far exceeding 68.7 for basketball and 38.2 for volleyball), reflecting more frequent occlusions and target disappearances in soccer. The SportsMOT soccer videos are predominantly overhead shots with relatively smooth view changes and no high-frequency abrupt transitions. This dataset is primarily used to evaluate model generalization under cross-scene and dense occlusion conditions.

BroadcastSwitch-Soccer (self-constructed). To address the insufficient coverage of abrupt shot transitions and replay segments in existing public datasets, we construct the BroadcastSwitch-Soccer dataset. The data is collected from real soccer broadcast streams, retaining director-initiated camera switches, replay insertions, and rapid panoramic/medium/close-up transitions. We annotate 120 video clips (20–45 seconds each), totaling approximately 96,000 frames at 1920×1080 resolution and 25 FPS. Annotations include bounding boxes and tracking IDs for players, goalkeepers, referees, assistant referees, and the ball, along with frame-level shot boundary labels and view type labels (panoramic/medium/close-up/replay). The dataset is split 70/10/20 into training, validation, and test sets, specifically designed to evaluate model robustness under real broadcast shot-switching scenarios.

Implementation details

We implement ViewAdapt-Det based on RT-DETR, using an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50 as the backbone, which outputs three-scale features fused through the Efficient Hybrid Encoder. In VATG, the gated temporal attention uses 8 attention heads and aggregates 3 consecutive frames (K=2). In VCDM, the view encoder uses multi-branch depth-wise separable convolutions with self-attention to extract view representations, and spatial and semantic modulation are injected into detection features via cross-attention and FiLM mechanisms. The detection head follows RT-DETR (6-layer decoder, 300 queries) with Hungarian matching for label assignment.

Training uses the AdamW optimizer with a backbone learning rate of and for other modules, weight decay of , for 150 epochs (10-epoch linear warmup, cosine annealing schedule). The total batch size is 16 (4 RTX 4090 GPUs, 4 per GPU). Input resolution is 1920× 1080 for SoccerNet-Tracking and BroadcastSwitch-Soccer, and 1280× 720 for SportsMOT. Data augmentation includes random horizontal flipping, 0.8–1.2 × random scaling, color jittering, and random cropping. Loss weights are set to , , , , . For shot boundary labels, all three datasets use a frame-level two-stage annotation pipeline. In the first stage, TransNetV2 generates transition probability scores for each frame, and frames with scores above 0.5 are selected as boundary candidates. In the second stage, two trained annotators independently review the candidates and refine the labels according to the following rules: (1) a shot boundary is marked only when consecutive frames correspond to different camera shots with substantially different visual content or camera position; ordinary panning, tilting, or zooming within the same shot is not marked as a boundary; (2) for gradual transitions or dissolve effects, the boundary is assigned to the first frame where the new view becomes visually dominant, defined as occupying more than 50% of the visible content; (3) the annotation granularity is frame-level, and no sub-frame boundary is used; (4) disagreements are resolved by a third review. The resulting inter-annotator agreement is Cohen’s kappa = 0.93. Manual review removes approximately 12% false-positive candidates and adds approximately 3.8% missed transitions. View type labels used by are assigned before training from ground-truth bounding boxes rather than model predictions. For each frame, we compute the average target area from annotated boxes. Frames with average target area below pixels are labeled as panoramic, frames above pixels are labeled as close-up, and the remaining frames are labeled as medium. The thresholds are selected from the average target-area histogram of 2,000 manually checked frames, where the distribution shows three modes corresponding to panoramic, medium, and close-up views; and lie near the valleys between adjacent modes. For SportsMOT, tracking boxes serve as detection supervision.

At test time, no multi-scale testing or model ensembling is used. Temporal aggregation is performed via a sliding window, with a confidence threshold of 0.3. We report mAP@0.5 and mAP@0.75 as primary metrics, along with per-class accuracy (Player, Ball) and per-view performance (Panoramic/Medium/Close-up). Inference speed is measured in FPS on a single RTX 4090.

Main results

We compare ViewAdapt-Det with quantitatively evaluated baselines in three groups: (1) single-frame detectors, including Deformable DETR33, RT-DETR32, and YOLO112; (2) video object detection methods, including QueryProp15, TransVOD14, and Beyond Boxes19; and (3) a soccer-scene specialized detector, DeCon-Net9. These baselines cover representative detection methods from 2021 to 2026 and span Transformer-based detection, real-time detection, temporal video detection, and soccer-specific detection. In the detector-backbone generalization study, we further evaluate DINO34 as a strong DETR-family baseline. All methods are trained and tested on the same dataset splits and evaluated using the same metrics. For methods with configurable CNN backbones, we use the ResNet-50 setting where applicable.

Results on BroadcastSwitch-Soccer

Table 3 presents the detection performance on the BroadcastSwitch-Soccer test set, which contains abundant abrupt shot transitions and replay segments and serves as the core benchmark for evaluating view robustness. ViewAdapt-Det achieves 70.4% mAP@0.5 (+2.9) and 45.6% mAP@0.75 (+3.3), the largest improvement among the three datasets. The most notable phenomenon on this dataset is the performance degradation of video detection methods: QueryProp15(65.3%) falls below the single-frame baseline RT-DETR (65.7%), TransVOD14(66.0%) only marginally exceeds it by 0.3 points, and Beyond Boxes (67.5%) improves over RT-DETR by only 1.8 points, far less than the 3.3-point gain on SoccerNet-Tracking. This directly validates the core observation of this paper: frequent shot transitions break the inter-frame continuity assumption, and forced temporal aggregation introduces cross-view noise. The metric further reveals the root cause: single-frame detectors have small values (3.5–3.8), video methods show significantly larger gaps (8.6–10.4), while ViewAdapt-Det achieves only 2.8, even lower than all single-frame detectors, demonstrating that the VATG gating mechanism effectively suppresses cross-view noise at transition frames. DeCon-Net has a of 4.1, close to single-frame levels (intra-frame contrastive learning is unaffected by temporal noise), but consequently cannot leverage temporal information, remaining 4.1 points below ViewAdapt-Det overall. Ball detection accuracy (59.6%) improves by 3.5 points over Beyond Boxes (56.1%), the largest Ball-class improvement across all three datasets, highlighting VCDM’s advantage under extreme scale variation. These results clarify why ViewAdapt-Det outperforms recent strong detection paradigms in broadcast-switching scenarios: stronger single-frame detectors improve per-frame representation but cannot exploit temporal cues, whereas generic video detectors exploit temporal cues but remain vulnerable when the visual-continuity assumption is violated. VATG bridges this gap by suppressing historical-frame contributions at shot boundaries while preserving temporal enhancement in continuous segments, and VCDM further adapts feature modulation to the current view type.

Limitations and future work

Limitations. Several aspects of this work remain open for improvement. It is worth noting that these limitations should be interpreted in the context of recent advances in video understanding. Large-scale video foundation models and video-language models have substantially improved general-purpose temporal modeling and high-level semantic understanding in complex videos. Self-supervised video representation learning has also shown strong potential for learning transferable spatiotemporal features, while promptable video models with memory mechanisms have advanced object-level understanding across video frames35–37. These developments suggest that modern video representations are becoming increasingly capable of capturing temporal context, long-range dependencies, and complex scene dynamics. Nevertheless, soccer broadcast detection still presents a specialized setting where director-induced shot transitions, replay insertions, and large cross-view scale variations can disrupt conventional temporal aggregation and single-view detection assumptions. Therefore, ViewAdapt-Det should be regarded as complementary to recent video understanding advances: instead of aiming to replace general-purpose video representations, it provides an explicit view-aware gating and view-conditioned modulation mechanism for real-time object detection under broadcast-switching scenarios.

First, although ViewAdapt-Det improves detection across multiple detector backbones on soccer broadcast data, the current evaluation is still soccer-centric. The view-adaptive mechanism is not inherently tied to soccer: VATG models generic inter-frame view consistency, and VCDM captures spatial layout and target-scale patterns that may also occur in other multi-camera scenarios such as basketball, tennis, American football broadcasts, multi-camera surveillance, or drone footage with altitude changes. However, transferability to these domains has not been empirically verified in this work. Applying the method to new domains may require recalibrating the view-type thresholds and because target scales and camera layouts differ. Second, the failure cases discussed in Sect. “Qualitative visualization” show that slow-motion replay boundaries, severe close-up occlusion, and extremely small ball instances in panoramic views remain challenging. Third, although the proposed modules maintain real-time inference on a high-end GPU, the model has not yet been compressed or quantized for edge deployment, so its performance on resource-constrained devices remains unverified. Finally, we have not yet explored whether recent video foundation representations can further improve the robustness of VATG and VCDM, or reduce the need for manually selected view-type thresholds.

Future Work. To address the above issues, we plan the following research directions. For lightweight deployment, we intend to compress the VATG and VCDM modules through knowledge distillation and structured pruning, combined with INT8 quantization for real-time inference on edge devices. For cross-domain transfer, we plan to validate the framework’s generality on surveillance video and drone footage with multi-view characteristics, and explore domain adaptation strategies to bridge feature distribution gaps across different data sources. For interpretability, we plan to introduce explicit view-type annotations as auxiliary supervision to enable the view encoder to learn semantically interpretable view representations, facilitating targeted fine-tuning for downstream tasks. In addition, inspired by recent progress in video foundation models, we plan to investigate whether pretrained video representations can be used to initialize the view encoder, guide temporal gating, or serve as auxiliary teachers for improving robustness under rare shot transitions and replay effects. Finally, we intend to propagate the VATG gating signal to downstream tracking modules, automatically resetting identity associations during shot transitions, achieving end-to-end view-adaptive joint optimization of detection and tracking.

Conclusion

This paper addresses the detection performance degradation caused by frequent multi-view switching in soccer broadcast videos and proposes the ViewAdapt-Det framework. The framework contains two core modules. The View-Aware Temporal Gating module (VATG) fuses global semantic similarity and local structural differences to generate a continuous gating signal, automatically suppressing cross-view noise propagation during shot transitions while fully exploiting inter-frame temporal complementary information when views are continuous. The View-Conditioned Detection Modulation module (VCDM) perceives the view type of the current frame and generates conditional attention masks, adaptively modulating detection features along both spatial attention patterns and semantic feature distributions, enabling the detector to adopt differentiated feature processing strategies under panoramic, medium, and close-up views. Experiments on SoccerNet-Tracking, the SportsMOT soccer subset, and the self-constructed BroadcastSwitch-Soccer dataset show that ViewAdapt-Det achieves 80.9%, 75.6%, and 70.4% mAP@0.5 respectively, outperforming all existing baselines. Ablation studies further confirm the complementarity of VATG and VCDM, with their combined gain exceeding the sum of their individual contributions. Qualitative visualizations intuitively demonstrate the precise gating response of VATG at shot boundaries and the dynamic spatial attention adjustment behavior of VCDM across view types. This work shows that explicitly modeling view changes and integrating them into the detection pipeline is an effective paradigm for multi-view broadcasting scenarios, offering new technical insights for intelligent sports video analysis.
