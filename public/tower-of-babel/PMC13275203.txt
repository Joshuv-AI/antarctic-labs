Public Perceptions of Ankyloglossia on Reddit: A Cross‐Sectional Thematic and Sentiment Analysis
Authors: Marcin Marciniak, Michael D Nguyen, Sean A Setzen, Katie L Holland, Arnav Shah, Allison Albers, Mihir K Bhayani, Krupa R Patel, Anatoli F Karas
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundThe incidence and treatment rates of ankyloglossia have been rising with significant variability in practice patterns. A 2020 clinical consensus statement by the American Academy of Otolaryngology—Head and Neck Surgery acknowledged these controversies. Reddit is a platform where patients and caregivers seek advice and share healthcare experiences. This study analyzed Reddit discussions referencing ankyloglossia to assess discussion themes, emotional sentiment, and diagnostic and practice patterns.MethodsReddit posts and comments were collected using a Python script from relevant subreddits between December 2018 and February 2025. Entries were labeled for themes, poster identity, symptoms, and diagnostic providers. Sentiment scores were generated using the VADER NLP algorithm. Descriptive statistics were calculated in R.ResultsA total of 2521 entries were analyzed. Most common themes were personal experiences (14.3%), offering advice (10.3%), and seeking advice (7.8%). Authors were typically adolescent/adult patients (35.9%) or parents (34.9%). Diagnosing providers were most often dentists (6.2%) and lactation consultants (5.9%). Overall sentiment skewed positive (56.4%), though 34.5% of entries were negative. Posts regarding financial concerns (−0.05) and postoperative symptoms (0.08) had the lowest sentiment scores. An average 44% increase in post frequency was observed per 6‐month interval across the study period (p < 0.001). Median time to first comment was 135 min.ConclusionReddit reveals significantly increasing engagement in ankyloglossia discussions. Varied sentiment and discussion themes highlight the need for standardized interdisciplinary guidelines and improved patient‐provider communication. Enhanced interdisciplinary counseling further mitigates misinformation and effectively supports caregivers. Future studies should explore interventions that address misinformation and emotional burden.Level of Evidence4.Keywords: ankyloglossia, frenotomy, misinformation, Reddit, sentiment, social media, tongue‐tie

Introduction

Ankyloglossia, commonly referred to as tongue‐tie, is a congenital condition characterized by a restrictive lingual frenulum that can impair tongue mobility. Though often considered benign, ankyloglossia remains clinically controversial due to ambiguous diagnostic criteria and inconsistent management strategies. While some clinicians argue that minor restrictions are over‐diagnosed and overtreated, others advocate for early intervention to prevent potential complications, such as breastfeeding difficulties, speech disorders, limited tongue mobility, and various mechanical and social issues [1, 2]. Currently, there is no universally accepted definition or standardized guideline for the diagnosis and management of ankyloglossia, resulting in significant variability in clinical practice and parental decision making [3, 4].

Various academy reports have strong consensus supporting a relationship between ankyloglossia and breastfeeding difficulties, particularly issues with latch and maternal nipple pain [1, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9]. However, evidence supporting long‐term effects on speech development, articulation, dental malocclusion, and other outcomes remains limited and lacks high‐quality longitudinal evidence [10, 11]. Despite the absence of strong long‐term evidence for benefit, frenotomy procedures have risen sharply worldwide over the past several decades [12, 13, 14]. For example, in Australia the rate increased from 1.22 to 6.35 per 1000 children aged 0–4 between 2006 and 2016, and in the United States the rate rose by ~866% from 1997 to 2012, even though birthrate remained stable during that time [3, 13, 14]. These trends raise concerns that non‐clinical influences, including provider marketing or social media, may contribute to increased diagnosis and intervention.

Additionally, fragmented care pathways may contribute to rising frenotomy rates. Patients with ankyloglossia are often evaluated by a wide range of professionals, including otolaryngologists, neonatologists, speech‐language pathologists, dentists, orthodontists, lactation consultants, pediatricians, and others, each with distinct training backgrounds and diagnostic thresholds [15]. The lack of interdisciplinary cooperation and standardized criteria leads to different standards of treatment, conflicting medical opinions, and increased patient frustration [15].

In the absence of clear clinical guidance, parents increasingly turn to online communities for support and information, often in the absence of well‐defined professional guidance. Reddit, a widely used discussion platform where users remain anonymous, hosts several subreddits where ankyloglossia is actively discussed, including r/TongueTies, r/ScienceBasedParenting, and r/NewParents. These discussions include personal experiences, peer‐to‐peer advice, and often unverified medical claims. Previous research has identified widespread misinformation about ankyloglossia on platforms such as Instagram, Twitter/X, and TikTok [11, 16, 17]. Booth et al. analyzed Instagram posts and found that nearly 90% contained clinically inaccurate claims, often shared by non‐physician providers [11, 16]. Despite Reddit's popularity and distinct structure that supports longer‐form, community‐moderated discourse, it remains understudied in the context of ankyloglossia.

Analyzing these online spaces adds insight to understanding how ankyloglossia is portrayed and interpreted by the public. Social media narratives can influence parental expectations, shape clinical encounters, and impact treatment choices. Therefore, discourse surrounding health conditions with unclear guidelines warrants careful examination. This study aims to systematically characterize Reddit discussions to (1) identify prevailing themes raised by Reddit users, (2) describe the emotional tone and sentiment surrounding these themes, (3) highlight common misconceptions or informational gaps, (4) assess how online discourse aligns with or diverges from current clinical evidence, and (5) examine discussion trends by determining change in posting frequency over time. By exploring the intersection of public perception and clinical evidence, we seek to inform patient education, patient‐provider communication, and interdisciplinary strategies to combat misinformation and misconceptions of ankyloglossia care.

Methods

Data Collection and Filtering

Reddit posts and comments referencing ankyloglossia were collected from multiple subreddits using a Python‐based data extraction script developed in Visual Studio Code (version 1.90.0 [Universal]; Microsoft Corporation, Redmond, WA). The script, built with the praw library (Python 3.9.16; Python Software Foundation), interfaced with the Reddit API to extract entries submitted between December 2018 and February 2025. Metadata collected for each entry included subreddit name, text content, entry date, entry type (post or comment), flair, number of upvotes, post ID, and anonymized author ID.

To identify relevant content, keyword filters were applied using terms such as “tongue tie,” “ankyloglossia,” “lip tie,” “cheek tie,” and other related variants outlined in Table 1. Keywords were processed with preference for longer and more specific phrases to maximize filtering accuracy and minimize false positives.

Manual Labeling and Classification

Entries were imported into Microsoft Excel (Microsoft Corporation, Redmond, WA) for manual review and classification. Entries with fewer than 10 words were excluded, while all others were included. Each entry was labeled by poster type (e.g., self‐identified patient, parent, clinician, unspecified), referenced patient (child, adult, or unspecified), and diagnosing provider (e.g., dentist, otolaryngologist, PCP). If mentioned, entries were manually coded for reported source of information, ankyloglossia symptoms, alternative treatments or symptom management attempts, and mentions of other oral ties. Posts were also assigned one of 20 predefined thematic categories developed through pre‐analysis planning and review of a 200‐entry sample. A full list of themes and definitions is shown in Table 2.

Thematic labeling was based on the overall context and intent of the entire post. When entries spanned multiple narrower categories, they were assigned to a broader theme that best captured the entry's comprehensive narrative. For example, an entry describing a patient's diagnostic journey, treatment experience, and postoperative reflections was categorized as “Personal Experiences,” whereas a post focusing specifically on one of those categories would be labeled accordingly. This logic ensured consistent categorization and reduced over‐segmentation of complex narratives.

Two reviewers (M.M. and M.D.N.) independently labeled all entries and resolved discrepancies through discussion and consensus.

Sentiment Analysis

Sentiment scores were calculated using the Valence Aware Dictionary for Sentiment Reasoning (VADER) algorithm, a rule‐based natural language processing (NLP) tool designed for social media text [18]. Each entry received a compound score between −1 (most negative) and +1 (most positive), with 0 indicating a neutral tone. Sentiment was classified as positive (≥ 0.05), negative (≤ −0.05), or neutral (between −0.05 and 0.05).

Statistical Analysis

Descriptive statistics were calculated to summarize dataset characteristics. Because sentiment scores and response times were non‐normally distributed, they were both reported using medians and interquartile ranges (IQRs). However, mean sentiment values were used to compare individual themes and poster identities for easier interpretation.

To assess posting trends over time, a Poisson regression was performed on the number of entries per 6‐month interval, with p values and incidence rate ratios reported. Sentiment differences across themes and poster identity were tested using Kruskal–Wallis rank‐sum tests. Response time to the first comment was analyzed by sentiment category. All statistical tests were two‐tailed, with significance defined as p < 0.05. Analyses were conducted using R (version 2024.12.1+563).

Ethical Considerations

This study received an institutional review board exemption due to the use of public, deidentified data. All data were publicly available and anonymized at the source. Data collection adhered to Reddit's API terms of use and no harm or adverse effects were encountered.

Declaration of Generative AI

The authors utilized ChatGPT 5.2 for the development of the Python script used for data extraction and the R code used for statistical analysis.

Information Source

Of the 2521 total entries, 1897 (75.2%) entries did not cite information or did not reference a specific source. Among the remaining 624 (24.8%) entries that shared information and cited their information source, the most cited were lactation consultants (n = 91), dentists (n = 72), online medical sources (n = 67), Google or general internet searches (n = 63), pediatricians or NICU providers (n = 46), and social media platforms (n = 34). Family members, friends, and other non‐clinical sources were occasionally cited.

Symptoms and Treatment Patterns

Symptom descriptions were present in 1458 (57.8%) entries. The most frequently cited symptoms included latch or breastfeeding challenges (n = 458, 18.2%), pain or discomfort (n = 192, 7.6%), limited tongue mobility or trouble with eating (n = 177, 7.0%), and speech or articulation concerns (n = 125, 5.0%). Other symptoms referenced craniofacial/MSK complaints (n = 113, 4.5%), sleep issues (n = 77, 3.1%), and altered oral sensation (n = 16, 0.6%). An additional 300 entries (11.9%) were “other,” which included posts describing multiple co‐occurring symptoms or less commonly reported issues that did not align with the predefined categories.

Alternative treatments or self‐management strategies were discussed in 17.6% of entries (n = 444). Myofunctional therapy was the most common (n = 281, 11.1%), followed by mewing (n = 39, 1.5%) and self‐release (n = 26, 1.0%). Posters less frequently discussed positional feeding adjustments for breastfeeding challenges, pain medications, and dietary adjustments.

Sentiment Analysis

The median sentiment score across all entries was 0.273 [−0.38, 0.78]. Overall, 56.4% of entries were classified as positive, 34.5% as negative, and 9.1% as neutral. Sentiment did not significantly differ by poster identity or by theme (p > 0.05, Kruskal–Wallis, η 2 < 0.001 for both comparisons). Mean sentiment scores across themes were generally clustered near neutral to mildly positive, with modest variability. Posts related to financial concerns (mean −0.05 (0.70)), postoperative symptoms (mean 0.08 (0.65)), and long‐term effects of frenotomy (mean 0.08 (0.68)) had the lowest average sentiment scores. Temporal Trends and Engagement

Posting frequency increased significantly across the study period. When modeled by 6‐month interval, a Poisson regression revealed a 44% increase in post count per interval (IRR 1.44, 95% CI 1.42–1.47, β = 0.37, p < 0.001). While post frequency showed a steady increase from 2018 to early 2023, a notable surge occurred in the second half of 2024 (Figure 1). Poisson regression confirmed a consistent upward trend across the entire study period (p < 0.001), indicating that engagement grew steadily rather than being driven exclusively by a single outlier interval. The first and last time intervals (2018 H2 and 2025 H1, respectively) represented partial periods rather than full 6‐month intervals but were analyzed as equal time blocks within the regression model.

Time to first comment was available for 330 posts (out of 641 total posts) and had a median value of 135.3 min [21.2, 566.8], showing users can receive consistently quick responses to their concerns. Difference in response times between positive (117 min), negative (128 min), and neutral (160 min) entries were not statistically significant (p > 0.05, Kruskal–Wallis).

Discussion

This study provides a characterization of public discourse surrounding ankyloglossia on Reddit over a six‐year period. Analysis of 2521 Reddit posts and comments revealed that conversations clustered around personal experiences, advice exchange, and care‐seeking behaviors, with additional discussion on diagnostic disagreement and evidence sharing. Sentiment across posts was mildly positive overall, though discussions concerning financial barriers and post‐operative symptoms carried the lowest average sentiment, reflecting increased distress involving procedural costs, discomfort, inconsistent recommendations, and challenges navigating care. Positive sentiment likely reflects generally favorable experiences or relief following diagnosis or resolution of prior uncertainty. Posting activity increased substantially across the study period, with a statistically significant 44% rise per six‐month interval, emphasizing growing public engagement and the increasing role of social media in healthcare discussion [19].

The near‐equal representation of adult patients and parents underscores the broad relevance of ankyloglossia discourse across age groups and clinical contexts, suggesting that online narratives encompass both infant feeding and adult functional concerns. Dentists and lactation consultants were most frequently cited as diagnosing providers, though the range of providers mentioned was highly variable. This finding is consistent across other social media platforms and highlights the fragmented and multidisciplinary nature of ankyloglossia care, which may contribute to diagnostic inconsistency and mixed patient guidance [11]. Notably, fewer than one in four entries cited any explicit information source, highlighting a reliance on peer exchange and anecdotal experience rather than formal, evidence‐based materials. Reported symptoms were most frequently related to breastfeeding challenges, pain, and speech concerns. Together, these findings suggest that Reddit functions not only as an information exchange but also as an emotional support network for patients and caregivers navigating uncertainty in ankyloglossia diagnosis and treatment.

These findings align with prior work documenting both increased frenotomy rates despite ongoing debate regarding diagnostic criteria and clinical indications [14, 16, 20]. While existing recommendations, such as the AAO‐HNSF clinical consensus statement, support frenotomy for breastfeeding‐related difficulties in select cases, long‐term benefits for speech, craniofacial development, and quality of life remain uncertain [1, 14, 16, 20]. In this context, social media platforms have emerged as an influential space for clinical discourse, allowing patients and caregivers to exchange experiences and fill informational gaps left by inconsistent guidance. The prominence of anecdotal and peer‐driven information in this dataset highlights both the supportive function and inherent risks of online health communities, where misinformation or oversimplified claims may circulate unchecked, even from self‐identified healthcare providers [16]. This concern is amplified by the increasing accessibility of artificial intelligence tools such as ChatGPT, which users often regard as trustworthy. However, emerging research shows these tools can generate and perpetuate clinically inaccurate information, further complicating the digital health information landscape [21].

While platforms such as Instagram, TikTok, and Twitter/X have been examined for accuracy and thematic content related to ankyloglossia, Reddit has been relatively understudied [11, 16, 17]. Booth et al. reported high rates of clinically inaccurate or unverified claims on Instagram, frequently disseminated by non‐physician accounts [11]. Our findings similarly indicate that non‐physician voices are prominent in diagnosis and advice threads. However, Reddit's long‐form, discussion‐based format distinguishes it from image or microblogging platforms by facilitating nuanced storytelling and sustained dialogue. Despite this, the uneven sentiment observed in financial and postoperative threads suggests persistent information gaps and mismatched expectations.

This study advances the current understanding of online discourse surrounding ankyloglossia in several ways. First, it provides the first platform‐specific characterization of Reddit discussions on the topic, mapping the thematic landscape and quantifying sentiment at scale. Identifying domains with the lowest sentiment (finances, postoperative symptoms) highlights areas where clinicians may focus anticipatory guidance and educational interventions. Second, the significant, sustained growth in posting volume suggests expanding public engagement and reliance on online forums for healthcare advice. This pattern may reflect greater digital health literacy, increased visibility of ankyloglossia across social media, and greater reliance on peer communities, especially following the COVID‐19 pandemic, when limited access to in‐person consultation led many patients to seek advice through online portals and social platforms [22]. Recognizing this trend may help clinicians anticipate the preconceptions patients bring to clinical encounters and develop more targeted, evidence‐based educational resources. Lastly, the prominence of various healthcare providers, such as dentists and lactation consultants, as diagnosing or advising sources reflects the diffuse entry points into the ankyloglossia care pathway. These findings reinforce the need for shared diagnostic terminology, consistent treatment guidelines, and coordinated counseling across disciplines to reduce confusion and conflicting advice.

Posts discussing financial concerns and postoperative symptoms suggest that clinicians may consider proactively addressing cost expectations, realistic recovery trajectories, and the role or limitations of adjunctive therapies such as myofunctional therapy during counseling. Because many patients forget or reinterpret counseling after discharge, clinicians could reinforce counseling through brief follow‐up communications, such as portal messages or timed postoperative resources, to address common concerns when they are most relevant. Additionally, providers may consider developing concise, easily accessible, verified Q&A forums where patients can seek clarification from trained healthcare professionals rather than relying on anecdotal advice online.

Future Directions

Future research should build on these findings by examining the linguistic and emotional framing of online discussions to better understand how misinformation and distress are conveyed. Qualitative analyses could be complemented by prospective studies linking social media data with patient interviews or clinical outcomes to contextualize online narratives in real‐world experiences. These findings may also inform quality improvement projects, such as targeted patient and family educational interventions to address misinformation, financial concerns, and postoperative recovery experiences to support ankyloglossia care. Cross‐platform comparisons may further clarify how message dissemination, misinformation spread, and sentiment differ across social media environments. Methodologically, continuous refinement of NLP models could enhance detection of nuanced content such as sarcasm, mixed emotional valence, or conflicting perspectives within a single post. Additionally, implementing multi‐label thematic classification would preserve post complexity and more accurately reflect the multidimensional nature of online discourse.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, sentiment analysis relied on the VADER algorithm, which, although well‐suited for social media text, cannot reliably detect sarcasm, mixed sentiment, or clinical nuance. Second, each post was assigned a single primary theme to avoid over‐segmentation, which may oversimplify complex, multitopic discussions. Third, thematic labeling was performed manually by two researchers, and although disagreements were discussed and resolved, some subjectivity may remain in how themes were applied. Fourth, selection and reporting biases are inherent to social media data, as users self‐select to post, and emotionally charged experiences, such as complications or financial strain, may be overrepresented relative to routine outcomes. Finally, while Reddit's anonymity and global reach enhance authentic discussion, they also prevent verification of user identity, demographic characteristics, or geographic distribution, limiting the generalizability of findings to specific populations.

Conclusion

Reddit reveals growing engagement in discussions about ankyloglossia, reflecting rising public interest and dependence on social media for health information. While overall sentiment was positive, recurring frustration surrounding cost and postoperative recovery points to ongoing informational and emotional gaps. These findings emphasize the need for standardized, interdisciplinary guidelines and more effective patient‐provider communication regarding ankyloglossia care. Strengthening collaboration among clinicians and reinforcing counseling through accessible, evidence‐based resources may help reduce misinformation and better support patients and caregivers navigating treatment decisions.
