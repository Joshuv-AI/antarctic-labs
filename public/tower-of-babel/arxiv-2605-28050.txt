Hadwiger’s Conjecture for { co-claw , co-gem } \{\text{co-claw},\text{co-gem}\} -free graphs and { fork , antifork } \{\text{fork},\text{antifork}\} -free graphs
Daniel Carter, Jung Hon Yip

Abstract
We prove Hadwiger’s Conjecture for { co-claw , co-gem } \{\text{co-claw},\text{co-gem}\} -free graphs and { fork , antifork } \{\text{fork},\text{antifork}\} -free graphs, where the co-claw is the disjoint union of a triangle and a vertex, the co-gem is the disjoint union of a 4-vertex path and a vertex, the fork is obtained from K 1 , 3 K_{1,3} by subdividing one of the edges, and the antifork is the complement of the fork. The { co-claw , co-gem } \{\text{co-claw},\text{co-gem}\} -free graphs include the complements of line graphs of triangle-free multigraphs, and thus our results imply Hadwiger’s Conjecture for these graphs. In fact, we prove a stronger result: every { co-claw , co-gem } \{\text{co-claw},\text{co-gem}\} -free graph G G has a K χ ⁡ ( G ) K_{\chi(G)} -model where each branch set has size at most 2, and every { fork , antifork } \{\text{fork},\text{antifork}\} -free graph G G has a K χ ⁡ ( G ) K_{\chi(G)} -model where at most one branch set has size greater than 2.

\overline{G}
is disconnected.

(iii), (iv)

G
or
\overline{G}
has a pair of adjacent simplicial twins.

(v), (vi)

G
or
\overline{G}
is candled.

(vii), (viii)

G
or
\overline{G}
is the line graph of a simple triangle-free graph.

Chudnovsky, Cook, and Seymour used
Theorem

3.1
to prove that
\chi(G)\leq 2\omega(G)
if
G
is
\{\text{fork},\text{antifork}\}
-free. We now explain the unfamiliar terms appearing in this structure theorem.

A vertex
v
in a graph
G
is
simplicial
if its neighborhood
N_{G}(v)
induces a clique. Two vertices
u,v\in V(G)
are
twins
if
N_{G}(u)\cap(V(G)\setminus\{u,v\})=N_{G}(v)\cap(V(G)\setminus\{u,v\})
.

Two sets of vertices are
complete
if every edge between them exists, and
anticomplete
if there are no edges between them. A
candelabrum
is a graph formed by
2k
non-empty disjoint sets of vertices
Y_{1},\dots,Y_{k}
and
Z_{1},\dots,Z_{k}
such that:

•

Each
Y_{i}
is a clique and each
Z_{i}
is a stable set.

•

The sets
Y_{i}
are pairwise anticomplete and the sets
Z_{i}
are pairwise complete.

•

Y_{i}
is complete to
Z_{j}
if
i=j
and anticomplete to
Z_{j}
if
i\neq j
.

We call the sets
Y_{i}
the
candle sets
and
Z_{i}
the
base sets
.

A graph
G
is
candled
if it contains a non-empty candelabrum with candle sets
Y_{1},\dots,Y_{k}
and base sets
Z_{1},\dots,Z_{k}
such that every vertex of
G
not in the candelabrum is adjacent to every vertex in
Z_{1},\dots,Z_{k}
and non-adjacent to every vertex in
Y_{1},\dots,Y_{k}
(see
Figure

3
).

Figure 3:
A candled graph with candelabrum
Y_{1}\cup\dots\cup Y_{5}\cup Z_{1}\cup\dots\cup Z_{5}
. Circles and the grey blob represent sets of vertices. Lines between two sets indicate complete pairs, the absence of a line indicates anticomplete pairs, and
K
and
S
mark cliques and stable sets, respectively. The grey blob represents all of the vertices that are not in the candelabrum. This figure is modified from
[
4
]
.

We now show that a minimal
\{\text{fork},\text{antifork}\}
-free counterexample to
Theorem

1.3
cannot satisfy any of the eight outcomes in
Theorem

3.1
, thus proving
Theorem

1.3
.

{proposition}

Let
G
be a minimal counterexample to
Theorem

1.3
. Then:

(i)

G
is not disconnected,

(ii)

\overline{G}
is not disconnected,

(iii)

G
has no simplicial vertex,

(iv)

G
has no pair of non-adjacent twins,

(v)

G
is not candled,

(vi)

\overline{G}
is not candled,

(vii)

G
is not a line graph of a simple graph, and

(viii)

\overline{G}
is not the line graph of a triangle-free (multi)graph.

Proof.

Note that
\chi(G)>\chi(H)
for all proper induced subgraphs
H
of
G
, as otherwise
\had_{2}^{+}(G)\geq\had_{2}^{+}(H)\geq\chi(H)=\chi(G)
.

(i)

If
G=A\sqcup B
for some non-empty graphs
A
and
B
, then

\had_{2}^{+}(G)=\max(\had_{2}^{+}(A),\had_{2}^{+}(B))\geq\max(\chi(A),\chi(B))=\chi(G).

(ii)

Suppose
\overline{G}=\overline{A}\sqcup\overline{B}
for some non-empty graphs
A
and
B
. Then
\chi(G)=\chi(A)+\chi(B)
. Let
M=\{S_{1},\dots,S_{\chi(A)}\}
be a semi-small
K_{\chi(A)}
-model in
A
with
|S_{i}|\leq 2
for
i\geq 2
and
N=\{T_{1},\dots,T_{\chi(B)}\}
be a semi-small
K_{\chi(B)}
-model in
B
with
|T_{i}|\leq 2
for
i\geq 2
. If
|S_{1}|\leq 1
or
|T_{1}|\leq 1
then
M\cup N
is a semi-small
K_{\chi(G)}
-model. Otherwise, let
s_{1},s_{2}\in S_{1}
and
t_{1},t_{2}\in T_{1}
be distinct; then

\{S_{2},\dots,S_{\chi(A)},T_{2},\dots,T_{\chi(B)},\{s_{1},t_{1}\},\{s_{2},t_{2}\}\}

is a small
K_{\chi(G)}
-model.

(iii)

Let
v\in V(G)
be simplicial. Since
G
is a minimal counterexample,
\chi(G\setminus v)=\chi(G)-1
, so
|N_{G}(v)|\geq\chi(G)-1
. Since
v
is simplicial,
N_{G}(v)\cup\{v\}
induces a clique of size
\chi(G)
in
G
, implying
\had_{2}^{+}(G)\geq\omega(G)=\chi(G)
.

(iv)

If
u,v\in V(G)
are non-adjacent twins, then
\chi(G\setminus u)=\chi(G)
, since
u
can be colored the color assigned to
v
in a coloring of
G\setminus u
. Thus
G
is not a minimal counterexample.

(v)

Suppose
G
is candled with base sets
Z_{1},\dots,Z_{k}
and candle sets
Y_{1},\dots,Y_{k}
. If
|Z_{i}|\geq 2
for some
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
, then for distinct
u,v\in Z_{i}
,
u
and
v
are non-adjacent twins in
G
, contradicting
(iv)
. Thus
|Z_{i}|=1
for each
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
. Therefore every vertex in each
Y_{i}
is simplicial in
G
, contradicting
(iii)
.

(vi)

Suppose
\overline{G}
is candled with base sets
Z_{1},\dots,Z_{k}
and candle sets
Y_{1},\dots,Y_{k}
. If
|Y_{i}|\geq 2
for some
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
, then for distinct
u,v\in Y_{i}
,
u
and
v
are non-adjacent twins in
G
, contradicting
(iv)
.
Thus,
|Y_{i}|=1
for each
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
. Therefore every vertex in each
Z_{i}
is simplicial in
G
(the neighborhood of such a vertex
z
in
G
consists of
Z_{i}\setminus\{z\}
plus the unique vertex in each
Y_{j}
with
j\neq i
; these vertices are all pairwise adjacent in
G
), contradicting
(iii)
.

(vii)

Recall Vizing’s theorem that
\chi(G)\leq\omega(G)+1
if
G
is a line graph
[
26
]
. If
\omega(G)=\chi(G)
, then we are done, so assume
\omega(G)=\chi(G)-1
. Let
C
be a clique of
G
of size
\omega(G)
, and let
A_{1},\dots,A_{k}
be non-empty connected components of
G\setminus C
. We will show that
k=1
. If
k\geq 2
, then since
G
is a minimal counterexample,
\chi(G[C\cup A_{i}])<\chi(G)
for each
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
. By relabeling colors, we can combine the colorings of
G[C\cup A_{1}],\dots,G[C\cup A_{k}]
to color
G
using
\max(\chi(G[C\cup A_{1}]),\dots,\chi(G[C\cup A_{k}]))
colors. Then
\chi(G)=\max(\chi(G[C\cup A_{1}]),\dots,\chi(G[C\cup A_{k}]))<\chi(G)
, a contradiction.

Thus
k=1
. If some vertex
c\in C
was not adjacent to any vertex in
A_{1}
, then
c
is simplicial, contradicting
(iii)
. Thus
\{\{c\}\}_{c\in C}\cup\{A_{1}\}
is a semi-small
K_{\chi(G)}
-model.

(viii)

This follows from
Section

1
. ∎

Note that
(vii)
is the only case where a branch set of size larger than 2 is introduced.
One might wonder if it is possible to replace
\had_{2}^{+}(G)
in
Theorem

1.3
with
\had_{2}(G)
. This cannot be done, because the cycles
C_{2k+1}
with
k\geq 3
are line graphs of triangle-free graphs and have
2=\had_{2}(G)<\chi(G)=3
, so branch sets of size larger than
2
are indeed required in
(vii)
. In fact, there is a more general counterexample:

{proposition}

Let
H
be a graph with girth at least 7, let
G
be the line graph of
H
, and assume
\chi(G)=\omega(G)+1
. Then
\had_{2}(G)=\chi(G)-1
.

Proof.

Note that connected subsets of
V(G)
of size 1 or 2 correspond to the edge sets of 1- or 2-edge paths in
H
respectively. Since
H
has girth at least 7, three disjoint connected subsets of
V(G)
of size at most 2 can only be pairwise adjacent if the corresponding edge sets in
H
meet at a common vertex. Therefore
\had_{2}(G)\leq\Delta(H)=\omega(G)
, where
\Delta(H)
is the maximum degree in
H
, and so
\had_{2}(G)=\chi(G)-1
.
∎

An example of such
H
is a
d
-regular graph of girth at least 7 with
|V(H)|
odd, which guarantees
\chi(G)=\omega(G)+1
(to see this, note that each color class of a coloring of
G
corresponds to a matching in
H
and therefore covers at most
(|V(H)|-1)/2
edges of
H
; there are
d|V(H)|/2
edges in
H
, so at least
d+1=\omega(G)+1
color classes are required).

4
Further results and open problems

One can easily improve the
\{\text{co-claw},\text{co-gem}\}
-free result to a slightly more general class. Note that if
\mathcal{C}
is a hereditary class such that
\had_{2}(G)\geq\chi(G)
for all
G\in\mathcal{C}
, then the closure of
\mathcal{C}
under disjoint unions is another class with the same property. The
\{\text{co-claw},\text{co-gem}\}
-free graphs are not closed under disjoint unions. Let
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
denote the closure of
\{\text{co-claw},\text{co-gem}\}
-free graphs under disjoint unions, which corresponds to graphs whose connected components are
\{\text{co-claw},\text{co-gem}\}
-free. We may obtain a forbidden induced subgraph characterization of
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
as follows. Note that the co-claw with the isolated vertex removed is
K_{3}
, and the co-gem with the isolated vertex removed is the
4
-vertex path
P_{4}
. Let
G
be a connected graph that contains the co-claw or co-gem as an induced subgraph, say on vertex set
Y
. Define
X\subseteq Y
as follows: if
Y
induces the co-claw, then
X
induces
K_{3}
, and if
Y
induces the co-gem, then
X
induces
P_{4}
. Then there is at least one vertex
v\in V(G)
anticomplete to
X
(for instance, the unique vertex in
Y\setminus X
). Choose such a
v
as close to
X
as possible; then
v
must be distance
2
from
X
. Let
w
be a neighbor of
v
that is adjacent to a vertex of
X
. By considering the neighborhood of
w
in
X
, we deduce that
G
induces one of the graphs in
Figure

4
. Hence:

{proposition}

Every connected component of
G
is
\{\text{co-claw},\text{co-gem}\}
-free if and only if
G
is
S
-free, where
S
is the set of graphs depicted in
Figure

4
. Therefore if
G
is
S
-free, then
\had_{2}(G)\geq\chi(G)
.

Figure 4:
A forbidden induced subgraph characterization of
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
, the class of graphs whose connected components are
\{\text{co-claw},\text{co-gem}\}
-free.

There are yet stronger results that can be easily obtained. For instance, let
S^{\prime}
be
S
minus the third and tenth graphs in
Figure

4
. Then one can prove that if
G
is
S^{\prime}
-free, then
\had_{2}(G)\geq\chi(G)
. This can be done by closing the class of
\{\text{co-claw},\text{co-gem}\}
graphs under both disjoint unions and complete joins (disjoint unions in the complement) simultaneously. Here, we note that
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is not closed complete joins; a complete join of two graphs in
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
might contain the third or tenth graph in
Figure

4
.

We conclude with some further problems.
Section

1
proves Hadwiger’s Conjecture for complements of line graphs of triangle-free multigraphs. The first author has extended this result to all complements of line graphs of multigraphs. The proof uses some similar techniques but with substantial computer assistance; the details will appear in a future paper.

Section

3
suggests the following broad problem:

{problem}

For which hereditary classes
\mathcal{C}
is it true that
\had_{2}(G)\geq\chi(G)
for every graph
G\in\mathcal{C}
?

Such a class
\mathcal{C}
must at least avoid the construction in
Section

3
, so for instance
\mathcal{C}
cannot contain odd cycles of length at least
7
. Perhaps the converse holds:

{problem}

Does every
\{C_{2k+1}\}_{k\geq 3}
-free graph
G
satisfy
\had_{2}(G)\geq\chi(G)
?

An affirmative answer would be a substantial strengthening of the conjecture of Seymour that
\had_{2}(G)\geq\chi(G)
if
G
is
\overline{K_{3}}
-free
[
22
]
. Here is an even stronger conjecture. For integer
m\geq 1
, let
\had_{m}(G)
be the largest
t
such that
G
contains a
K_{t}
-model using branch sets of size at most
m
. Let
\mathcal{C}_{m}
be the class of graphs
G
such that
\had_{m}(H)\geq\chi(H)
for all induced subgraphs
H
of
G
. Then
\mathcal{C}_{1}
is the class of perfect graphs, which we know is the class of
\{\text{odd hole},\text{odd antihole}\}
-free graphs. It is easy to see that
\mathcal{C}_{m}
is a subset of
\{C_{2k+1}\}_{k\geq 3m/2}
-free graphs, as
\had_{m}(C_{\ell})
is equal to 2 if
3m>\ell
and 3 if
3m\leq\ell
.

{problem}

Is
\mathcal{C}_{m}
the class of
\{C_{2k+1}\}_{k\geq 3m/2}
-free graphs for all
m\geq 2
?
