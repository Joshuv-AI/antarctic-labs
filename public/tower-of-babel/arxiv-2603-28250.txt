Resonant two-cluster scattering in a quasi-one-dimensional Bose gas
Tomohiro Tanaka, Yusuke Nishida

Abstract
We investigate two-cluster scattering in a quasi-one-dimensional Bose gas. We focus on the effective three-body interaction induced by transverse confinement, which is the leading term for breaking integrability in the quasi-one-dimensional setting. Exploiting the Lüscher formula and the integrability of the Lieb-Liniger Bose gas, we find a finite and positive scattering length for elastic two-cluster scattering. The resulting scattering lengths indicate the emergence of a resonance.

II
Preliminaries

We work with one-dimensional Bose gases with contact interactions,
whose Hamiltonian is given by

\displaystyle H=\int_{0}^{L}dx\Bigg[

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2m}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\phi^{\dagger}(x)\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\phi(x)+c\,\phi^{\dagger 2}(x)\phi^{2}(x)

\displaystyle+u\,\phi^{\dagger 3}(x)\phi^{3}(x)\Bigg].

(1)

Here,

m
is the particle mass,

\phi
is the bosonic field operator,

L
is the system size,
and

c
and
u
are the coupling constants of the two-body and three-body contact interactions, respectively.
Throughout this paper, we focus on the attractive regime
c<0
and
u<0
.
It is convenient to parametrize the two-body coupling as

\displaystyle c=-\frac{1}{ma},

(2)

where
a>0
is the one-dimensional two-body scattering length,
which serves as a typical length scale for the range of the effective interaction between clusters.
The corresponding Schrödinger equation is

\displaystyle\Bigg[

\displaystyle\sum_{j;\,1\leq j\leq N}\frac{-1}{2m}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{j}^{2}}+2\,c\,\sum_{j,l;\,1\leq j<l\leq N}\delta(x_{j}-x_{l})

\displaystyle+\,6\,u\,\sum_{j,l,n;\,1\leq j<l<n\leq N}\delta(x_{j}-x_{l})\delta(x_{l}-x_{n})\Bigg]\Psi(\bm{x})

\displaystyle=E\,\Psi(\bm{x}),

(3)

where
E
is the energy eigenvalue.
The quasi-one-dimensional Bose gas considered here can be realized by transversely confining three-dimensional bosons with a two-body contact interaction in a harmonic trap
8
;
25
.
In this realization, the one-dimensional coupling constants
c
and
u
are related to the three-dimensional two-body coupling constant
g_{3\mathrm{D}}
and the transverse confinement frequency
\omega_{\perp}
at leading order as
21
;
20

\displaystyle c=\frac{m\omega_{\perp}}{2\pi}g_{3\mathrm{D}}+\mathcal{O}(g_{3\mathrm{D}}^{2}),

(4)

\displaystyle u=-\frac{\log\frac{4}{3}}{2\pi^{2}}m^{2}\omega_{\perp}g_{3\mathrm{D}}^{2}+\mathcal{O}(g_{3\mathrm{D}}^{3}).

(5)

In the absence of the three-body interaction, the Bose gas in Eq. (
1
) reduces to the Lieb-Liniger model
16
;
17
.
In this model,
the exact
N
-body wave function is obtained via the Bethe ansatz
as

\displaystyle\Psi_{\mathrm{LL}}(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{N}|x_{1},\cdots,x_{N})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\sum_{\sigma;\,\sigma\in S_{N}}\prod_{j,l;\,1\leq j<l\leq N}\left[1+\frac{2mic\,\mathrm{sgn}(x_{j}-x_{l})}{\lambda_{\sigma_{j}}-\lambda_{\sigma_{l}}}\right]

\displaystyle\,\times\exp\left[i\sum_{n;\,1\leq n\leq N}\lambda_{\sigma_{n}}x_{n}\right],

(6)

where each
\lambda_{j}
is a so-called rapidity
16
;
17
;
15
.
Under the periodic boundary condition,
rapidities are quantized by the Bethe equations,

\displaystyle e^{i\lambda_{j}L}=-\prod_{l;\,1\leq l\leq N}\frac{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{l}+2mic}{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{l}-2mic},

(7)

and,
in the attractive regime,
arrange themselves into a regular pattern in the complex plane
22
;
32
.

The rapidities form groups that share a common real part and have equally spaced imaginary parts, and they are parameterized as

\displaystyle\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}=\bigcup_{\mu=1}^{N_{\mathrm{s}}}\{\lambda_{\mu,\nu}\}_{\nu=1}^{\alpha_{\mu}},

(8)

\displaystyle\lambda_{\mu,\nu}=\lambda_{\mu}^{\mathrm{s}}+imc\,(\alpha_{\mu}+1-2\nu)+i\delta_{\mu,\nu}^{\mathrm{s}}.

(9)

Here,
N=\sum_{\mu=1}^{N_{\mathrm{s}}}\alpha_{\mu}
.
Each group
\{\lambda_{\mu,\nu}\}_{\nu=1}^{\alpha_{\mu}}
is called a string and constitutes a bound cluster of
\alpha_{\mu}
particles.
The common real part
\lambda_{\mu}^{\mathrm{s}}
is referred to as the string center and is determined by the Bethe-Gaudin-Takahashi equations

;
5

\displaystyle\alpha_{\mu}\lambda_{\mu}^{\mathrm{s}}L-\sum_{\nu=1}^{N_{\mathrm{s}}}\Phi_{\mu,\nu}\!\left(\lambda_{\mu}^{\mathrm{s}}-\lambda_{\nu}^{\mathrm{s}}\right)=2\pi I_{\mu},

(10)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \Phi_{\mu,\nu}&(\lambda)=-2\Bigg[(1-\delta_{\mu,\nu})\arctan\!\left(\frac{\lambda}{mc|\mu-\nu|}\right)\\
&+\arctan\!\left(\frac{\lambda}{mc(\mu+\nu)}\right)\\
&+2\sum_{\alpha;\,1\leq\alpha\leq\min\{\mu,\nu\}-1}\arctan\!\left(\frac{\lambda}{mc(|\mu-\nu|+2\alpha)}\right)\Bigg]\end{aligned},

(11)

where
I_{\mu}
takes half-odd-integer (integer) values when the number of strings with length
\alpha_{\mu}
is even (odd).
From these rapidities, the total momentum and energy are given by

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu=1}^{N_{\mathrm{s}}}\alpha_{\mu}\lambda_{\mu}^{\mathrm{s}},

(12)

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu=1}^{N_{\mathrm{s}}}\left[\frac{\alpha_{\mu}}{2m}(\lambda_{\mu}^{\mathrm{s}})^{2}-\frac{1}{3}m^{2}c^{2}\alpha_{\mu}(\alpha_{\mu}-1)\right].

(13)

The Bethe ansatz associated with the parameterization in Eq. (
9
)
is often referred to as the string ansatz.

So far, our discussion has been restricted to the Lieb-Liniger Bose gas.
In this case, the wave function can be determined exactly, which allows the scattering phase shift to be extracted at arbitrary momentum, as shown below in Eq. (
24
).
However, this strategy is specific to integrable models and does not apply to generic systems.
Instead, one can use the Lüscher formula, which relates the finite-volume energy spectrum to the scattering phase shifts at the corresponding relative momentum
18
;
19

For two distinguishable particles in one dimension, it takes the form of
10

\displaystyle\cos\theta=\frac{\cos\left(\delta_{+}+\delta_{-}+qL\right)}{\cos\left(\delta_{+}-\delta_{-}\right)}.

(14)

Here,
q
is the relative momentum,
L
is the system size, and
\delta_{\pm}(q)
are the even- and odd-channel phase shifts.
In addition,
\theta
is defined by

\theta=-\alpha_{1}QL/(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})
,
where
Q
is the total momentum.
The equivalent choice
\theta=\alpha_{2}QL/(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})
leads to the same quantization condition for
q
,
since it leaves

When the effective interaction between clusters is parity invariant,
the phase shifts admit the effective-range expansions at low relative momentum
q\ll 1/a

.
In one dimension, they take the forms of

\displaystyle q\tan\delta_{+}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a_{+}}+\frac{1}{2}r_{+}q^{2}+\mathcal{O}(a^{4}q^{4}),

(15)

\displaystyle q\cot\delta_{-}

\displaystyle=\frac{-1}{a_{-}}+\frac{1}{2}r_{-}q^{2}+\mathcal{O}(a^{4}q^{4}).

(16)

Here,
a_{\pm}
and
r_{\pm}
are the scattering lengths and effective
ranges in the even- and odd-parity channels, respectively.

III
Two-cluster scattering

In this section, we study two-cluster scattering in the one-dimensional Bose gas described by Eq. (
1
), focusing on the effects of the three-body attraction.
We consider the regime in which two clusters form and analyze their cluster-cluster scattering within the elastic approximation, neglecting cluster breakup and recombination.
Exploiting the Lüscher formula together with the determinant formula for the three-body local correlation in the Lieb-Liniger Bose gas,
we aim to evaluate the scattering lengths.
The scattering phase shifts entering the Lüscher formula receive contributions from both the two-body and three-body interactions.
To isolate the effect of the three-body interaction on scattering, we first determine the phase shifts in the absence of the three-body interaction and then remove the corresponding two-body contribution from the Lüscher formula.
We therefore begin with two-cluster scattering in the attractive Lieb-Liniger Bose gas.

III.1
Attractive Lieb-Liniger Bose gas

We begin with the scattering of two strings.
The exact wave function is given by Eq. (
6
),
with the rapidities split into two sets:

\displaystyle\lambda_{1,\mu}=k+imc\,(\alpha_{1}+1-2\mu)+i\delta^{\mathrm{s}}_{1,\mu},

(17)

\displaystyle\lambda_{2,\mu}=p+imc\,(\alpha_{2}+1-2\mu)+i\delta^{\mathrm{s}}_{2,\mu}.

(18)

We denote the corresponding sets of rapidities by

\bm{\lambda}_{1}=\{\lambda_{1,\mu}\}_{\mu=1}^{\alpha_{1}}

and

\bm{\lambda}_{2}=\{\lambda_{2,\mu}\}_{\mu=1}^{\alpha_{2}}
.
Here,

\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
are the string lengths and

and
p
are the string centers of the two strings.
The string centers are related to each other via the following Bethe-Gaudin-Takahashi equation

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}kL=2\pi I_{1}+\Phi_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}}(k-p),

(19)

\displaystyle\alpha_{2}pL=2\pi I_{2}-\Phi_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}}(k-p),

(20)

where
I_{1}

From these equations, we have

\displaystyle Q=\frac{2\pi}{L}(I_{1}+I_{2})=\alpha_{1}k+\alpha_{2}p,

(21)

\displaystyle q_{\mathrm{LL}}L=2\pi\frac{\alpha_{2}I_{1}-\alpha_{1}I_{2}}{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}+\frac{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}\Phi_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}}\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}q_{\mathrm{LL}}\right),

(22)

where
Q
is the total momentum of the system
and
q_{\mathrm{LL}}
is the relative momentum between two strings,

\displaystyle q_{\mathrm{LL}}\coloneqq\frac{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}(k-p).

(23)

For scattering between two strings,
the asymptotic form of Eq. (
6

Consequently, the even- and odd-channel phase shifts agree modulo

and are explicitly given by

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathrm{LL},\pm}(q)\equiv\frac{\pi}{2}\delta_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}}-\Phi_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}}\!\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}q\right),\quad\mathrm{mod}\,\pi.

(24)

From this point onward,
we focus on the case where the string lengths are different.
In this case, according to Eqs. (
15
) and (
24

the inverse even-channel scattering length vanishes:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a_{\mathrm{LL},+}}=

\displaystyle\lim_{q\to 0}q\tan\delta_{\mathrm{LL},+}(q)=0.

(25)

On the other hand, the even-channel effective range (

\displaystyle r_{\mathrm{LL},+}=-2\,a_{\mathrm{LL},-},

\displaystyle r_{\mathrm{LL},+}=\lim_{q\to 0}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial q^{2}}q\tan\delta_{\mathrm{LL},+}(q),

\displaystyle\frac{-1}{a_{\mathrm{LL},-}}=\lim_{q\to 0}q\cot\delta_{\mathrm{LL},-}(q).

With these scattering parameters, Eq. (

\displaystyle 2\pi\frac{\alpha_{2}I_{1}-\alpha_{1}I_{2}}{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}=q_{\mathrm{LL}}(L+r_{\mathrm{LL},+})

It is worth noting that the equal-string-length case is qualitatively different in that the inverse even-channel scattering length does not vanish.

III.2
Quasi-one-dimensional Bose gas

Next, we turn to two-cluster scattering including the perturbative three-body interaction,
which constitutes the main part of this paper.
In what follows, we continue to assume unequal cluster sizes
and work with the Schrödinger equation in Eq. (
3

The energy eigenvalue up to first order in the three-body attraction is given by

\displaystyle E=

\displaystyle\,\frac{\alpha_{1}k^{2}}{2m}-\frac{m^{2}c^{2}}{3}\alpha_{1}(\alpha_{1}^{2}-1)+\frac{\alpha_{2}p^{2}}{2m}-\frac{m^{2}c^{2}}{3}\alpha_{2}(\alpha_{2}^{2}-1)

\displaystyle+u\,C_{3}\left(\bm{\lambda}_{1}\cup\bm{\lambda}_{2}\right)+\mathcal{O}(u^{2}).

(30)

Here, we introduced the local three-body correlation function

\displaystyle C_{3}\left(\bm{\lambda}_{1}\cup\bm{\lambda}_{2}\right)=\int_{0}^{L}dx\,\braket{\bm{\lambda}_{1}\cup\bm{\lambda}_{2}|\,\phi^{\dagger 3}(x)\phi^{3}(x)\,|\bm{\lambda}_{1}\cup\bm{\lambda}_{2}}.

The energy in Eq. (

By subtracting the binding and center-of-mass contributions, we obtain the relative-motion energy as

\displaystyle E_{\text{RM}}=

\displaystyle\,E+\frac{1}{3}m^{2}c^{2}\alpha_{1}(\alpha_{1}^{2}-1)+\frac{1}{3}m^{2}c^{2}\alpha_{2}(\alpha_{2}^{2}-1)

\displaystyle-E_{\text{3,bind}}-\frac{Q^{2}}{2m(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})}

(32)

\displaystyle\,\frac{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}{2m(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})}(k-p)^{2}+\delta E_{3}+\mathcal{O}(m^{2}u^{2})

(33)

Here,
Q
denotes the total momentum, which is unaffected by the three-body perturbation.
Therefore, the total momentum is still given by

\displaystyle Q=\alpha_{1}k+\alpha_{2}p,

(34)

with

and

being the centers of the two strings in Eqs. (
17

In addition,

is the first-order energy shift after subtracting the three-body binding contribution, given explicitly by

\displaystyle\delta E_{3}=u\,\delta C_{3},\quad\delta C_{3}\coloneqq C_{3}(\bm{\lambda}_{1}\cup\bm{\lambda}_{2})-C_{3,\text{const}}.

(35)

\displaystyle q=

\displaystyle\,\sqrt{2\mu_{r}E_{\text{RM}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\sqrt{\left[\frac{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}(k-p)\right]^{2}+2\mu_{r}\delta E_{3}}\,,

III.3
Numerical result

We now turn to the numerical evaluation of the even-channel scattering length.
Equation (
46
) shows that this evaluation requires the local three-body correlation function and its
\mathcal{O}(L^{0})
part.
The former admits the following determinant representation

;
29

\displaystyle C_{3}(\bm{\lambda}_{1}\cup\bm{\lambda}_{2})=

\displaystyle\,36\,L\,\sum_{B;\,B\subset\{1,\cdots,\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}\}\,\land\,|B|=3}\frac{\operatorname{det}\mathscr{H}_{B}}{\operatorname{det}\mathscr{G}_{B}}

\displaystyle\,\times\left[\prod_{j,l;\,3\geq j>l\geq 1}\frac{\lambda_{B,j}-\lambda_{B,l}}{(\lambda_{B,j}-\lambda_{B,l})^{2}+4m^{2}c^{2}}\right].

(47)

Here, we employed

\displaystyle\left[\mathscr{H}_{B}\right]_{jl}\coloneqq\begin{cases}\lambda_{B,j}^{l-1}\quad\text{for }l=1,\cdots,3,\\[8.61108pt]
\left[\mathscr{G}_{B}\right]_{jl}\quad\text{for }l=4,\cdots,\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2},\end{cases}

(48)

and the Gaudin matrix

\displaystyle\left[\mathscr{G}_{B}\right]_{jl}\coloneqq

\displaystyle\,\delta_{jl}\left[L+\sum_{a;\,1\leq a\leq\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}\varphi(\lambda_{B,j}-\lambda_{B,a})\right]

\displaystyle-\varphi(\lambda_{B,j}-\lambda_{B,l}),

(49)

with
\varphi
being

\displaystyle\varphi(\lambda)\coloneqq\frac{4mc}{\lambda^{2}+4m^{2}c^{2}}.

(50)

In this notation,
\lambda_{B}
denotes the ordered set of rapidities obtained by reordering
\bm{\lambda}_{1}\cup\bm{\lambda}_{2}
, with its first three entries given by

for

and the remaining entries arranged in ascending order.
It is worth noting that Eq. (

.
The divergence is resolved by taking into account the string deviations

;
5
.
Since the string deviations decay exponentially with the system size

all finite-size corrections in negative powers of

The
\mathcal{O}(L^{0})

by evaluating the expectation value of the local three-body operator in the coordinate-space representation of

\ket{\bm{\lambda}_{1}\cup\bm{\lambda}_{2}}

Then, we arrive at

\displaystyle C_{3,\text{const}}=

\displaystyle\,4\,m^{2}c^{2}

\displaystyle\,\times\Bigg[\sum_{j=2}^{\alpha_{1}-1}F_{j}^{\alpha_{1}}F_{j+1}^{\alpha_{1}}+\sum_{l=2}^{\alpha_{2}-1}F_{l}^{\alpha_{2}}F_{l+1}^{\alpha_{2}}\Bigg]+\mathcal{O}(L^{-1}),

(51)

where we employed

In the quasi-one-dimensional realization described by

This result has qualitatively different physical consequences for particle-cluster and cluster-cluster scatterings.
In the former case,

\displaystyle E_{\text{bind}}=-\frac{1}{2\mu_{r}a_{+}^{2}},

which is of the same order as the decay width of resonance.

IV
Summary

In this paper, we have investigated scattering between unequal-size clusters in a quasi-one-dimensional Bose gas.
When only the two-body contact attraction is present,
the even-channel scattering length diverges.
The situation changes in the quasi-one-dimensional setup considered here.
The weak three-body attraction induced by transverse confinement renders the even-channel scattering length finite and positive.
This result was obtained by exploiting the Lüscher formula in Eq. (
14
)
together with the determinant representation of the local three-body correlation function in Eq. (
47
).
The determinant representation allows us to go beyond the three- and four-body setting
up to the few-body regime of
N\sim 50
.

The relationship between the even-channel scattering length and the energy shift is summarized in Eq. (
45
),
and the resulting even-channel scattering length is presented in Fig.
1
.
Its positivity implies that the attractive effective interaction between clusters stems from the perturbative three-body attraction induced in the quasi-one-dimensional setup.
This result has different consequences for particle-cluster and cluster-cluster scatterings.
For scattering between a particle and a cluster,
a positive scattering length signals the formation of a bound state.
On the other hand, for scattering between unequal-size clusters,
the two-cluster threshold is embedded in an energy continuum,
so that a positive scattering length instead indicates the emergence of a resonance rather than a bound state.
In both cases,
the corresponding binding energy appears at second order in the three-body attraction,
and its explicit expression is given in Eq. (
52
).

To conclude,
the perturbative three-body attraction induced in the quasi-one-dimensional setup yields a finite and positive even-channel scattering length,
thereby generating a bound state or a resonance in scattering between unequal-size clusters.
These results suggest that even a perturbatively weak quasi-one-dimensionality can govern the dynamical properties of the system through integrability breaking rather than integrable interactions.
A natural extension of the present analysis is to go beyond first order in the perturbative three-body attraction.
In particular,
the physical consequences derived from splitting and recombination processes mediated by the resonance are of interest.
At second order,
cluster recombination and breakup processes are expected to enter,
so that the elastic two-cluster description and the corresponding two-body Lüscher formula are no longer sufficient.
Clarifying this regime will require a finite-volume quantization condition for coupled channels associated with different cluster partitions.
The extended analysis may reveal how quasi-one-dimensionality drives the relaxation dynamics.
